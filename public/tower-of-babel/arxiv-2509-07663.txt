Chern character for torsion-free ample groupoids
Valerio Proietti, Makoto Yamashita

Abstract
. For an ample groupoid with torsion-free stabilizers, we construct a Chern character map going from the domain of the Baum–Connes assembly map of G G to the groupoid homology groups of G G with rational coefficients. As a main application, assuming the (rational) Baum–Connes conjecture, we prove the rational form of Matui’s HK conjecture, i.e., we show that the operator K K -groups of the groupoid C ∗ -algebra are rationally isomorphic to the periodicized groupoid homology groups. Our construction hinges on the recent ∞ \infty -categorical viewpoint on bivariant K K -theory, and does not rely on typical noncommutative geometry tools such as the Chern–Connes character and the periodic cyclic homology of smooth algebras. We also present applications to the homology of hyperbolic dynamical systems, the homology of topological full groups, the homotopy type of the algebraic K K -theory spectrum of ample groupoids, and the Elliott invariant of classifiable C ∗ -algebras.

Introduction

The Chern–Connes character and the Baum–Connes conjecture are two foundational concepts in noncommutative geometry that describe the structure of operator
K
-theory groups of C
∗
-algebras, objects rooted in functional analysis, using geometric and algebro-topological notions
[
6
,
24
]
.
While these ideas are best understood in the context of groups and manifolds separately via connections to more classical invariants, various generalizations have been proposed as noncommutative geometry matured as a field.
Notably, the use of topological groupoids provides a unifying approach to theories involving both spaces and groups.
This framework serves as a natural intermediary for defining and studying homological invariants of dynamical systems, such as the Gelfand–Fuchs cohomology classes of foliated manifolds
[
23
,
27
]
, and understanding their connections to the
K
-theory of associated algebras.

On one hand, although the Baum–Connes conjecture is known to fail in general for groupoids due to property (T)
[
46
]
, it is still confirmed for a broad class of groupoids by Tu’s result
[
119
]
based on work by Higson and Kasparov
[
47
]
.
On the other hand, in particular when reduction to étale groupoids is possible, the homology theory developed by Crainic and Moerdijk
[
25
]
provides a natural receptacle for the Chern character.
In the case of smooth groupoids, this homology can be seen as an essential component of cyclic homology, which receives the Chern–Connes character, see
[
26
]
.

Motivated by these results, and our recent work on homology and
K
-theory of dynamical systems
[
94
]
[
95
]
[
96
]
[
97
]
, we look at the corresponding problems for
ample
groupoids, namely étale groupoids with totally disconnected unit space. While this assumption might seem restrictive, it is worth pointing out that a reduction-to-transversal technique, which preserves Morita equivalence and
a fortiori
homological invariants, in many cases associates ample groupoids to systems whose underlying topological spaces are
not
totally disconnected (see
[
98
]
). Indeed, this class of groupoids is prevalent in the theory of dynamical systems, including generalized solenoids, systems associated to self-similar group actions, and tiling spaces
[
125
,
89
,
82
,
57
]
.

Our main contribution is the construction of a
generalized Chern character
map, going from the domain of the Baum–Connes assembly map for
G
, which we denote below by
K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))
, to the homology groups of
G
with coefficients in the constant sheaf of rational numbers, denoted
H_{\ast}(G,\mathbb{Q})
.

Theorem A

(Definition
5.1
& Corollary
5.7
)
.

Let
G
be a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, and ample groupoid. Assume that the stabilizers
G^{x}_{x}
for
x\in G^{(0)}
are torsion-free. There is a morphism of abelian groups, called the
Chern character
of
G
,

K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))\to\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q}).

This map is defined at the level of spectra, and is induced by a suitable homotopy-theoretic refinement of the classical Chern character

K^{*}(G^{(\bullet)})\to\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{\ast+2k}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathbb{Q})

(0.1)

for the space of tuples of composable arrows in
G
.

From the standpoint of noncommutative geometry, it is natural to seek for a construction of the map above using the Chern–Connes character and the periodic cyclic homology of a smooth subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
. Recent work of Pagliuca and Voigt provides a promising first step in this direction
[
93
]
. Instead, the approach pursued in this paper is based on homotopy theory and the recent
\infty
-categorical viewpoint on equivariant
\mathit{KK}
-theory
[
18
,
63
]
. It has the dual advantage of directly targeting groupoid homology and producing a rational isomorphism essentially by construction. It is our hope that this work may function as a catalyst for further applications of higher category theory to the homological invariants of topological dynamical systems and operator algebras.

The leading application is a description of the rationalized
K
-groups for the groupoid C
∗
-algebra under the assumption that the rational Baum–Connes conjecture holds.

Theorem B

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
G
be a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, and ample groupoid with torsion-free stabilizers.d
Assume that
G
satisfies the rational Baum–Connes conjecture
Then the Chern character of
G
induces the following isomorphism of abelian groups:

K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q}).

(0.2)

One immediate application of this isomorphism, which was also our motivation to develop this work, is to the
HK conjecture

[
76
]
*Conjecture 2.6 popularized by Matui. This problem has attracted a great deal of interest in recent years: Matui himself proved it for groupoids arising from shifts of finite type, AF groupoids, and
\mathbb{Z}
-actions on the Cantor set
[
74
]
; Ortega shows it for certain Katsura–Exel–Pardo groupoids
[
92
]
, Yi shows it for one-dimensional solenoids
[
125
]
, and the authors of
[
40
]
prove it for the Renault–Deaconu groupoids of low-rank. The spectral sequence we found in
[
94
]
(see (
0.3
)) also proves the HK isomorphism for all groupoids of low homological dimension, and
[
11
]
establishes a concrete criterion for the vanishing of higher homology; as a result, a principal ample groupoid with dynamic asymptotic dimension at most two satisfies
K_{1}(C^{*}_{r}G)\cong H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})
, and
K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}G)\cong H_{0}(G,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
under the additional assumption that
H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is a free group.

Despite these positive results, the original formulation of the HK conjecture fails in general due to counterexamples by Scarparo and Deeley
[
110
,
30
]
. Our result shows that, for the class of groupoids considered, the failure is only up to torsion. The statement of Theorem
5.5
appeared as a “folk conjecture” in
[
11
]
*Remark 5.12, as it has circulated among experts after the publication of
[
76
]
.

If
G
is a discrete group, Theorem
5.5
reduces to the fact that the rationalization of the left-hand side of the Baum–Connes conjecture for torsion-free groups (i.e., the
K
-homology of the classifying space
BG
) agrees with the group homology with rational coefficients, see Remark
6.1
.

The generalization to groupoids is particularly important for applications to topological dynamics and operator algebras.
Indeed, many dynamical systems can be naturally encoded through topological groupoids which do not necessarily arise from group actions, such as the stable and unstable groupoids of hyperbolic systems such as Smale space (see Section
6.4
).

Moreover, any separable C
∗
-algebra with a Cartan subalgebra admits a
groupoid model
, i.e.,
A
is isomorphic to a (possibly twisted) groupoid C
∗
-algebra
[
60
,
106
]
. By a recent result of Li
[
66
]
, this class contains the algebras targeted by Elliott’s classification program. Thanks to amenability, the condition on the Baum–Connes conjecture appearing in Theorem
B
is automatically satisfied for these groupoids.
Consequently, Theorem
B
provides a way to study
K
-groups of such C
∗
-algebras through groupoid homology.
The latter is often an easier task, as evidenced by active recent works in the field
[
40
,
20
,
45
,
11
,
77
,
82
,
67
,
73
,
32
]
.
Besides the finer integer grading on groupoid homology (as opposed to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
K
-groups) which allows us to split computations in smaller chunks, we hope that a wide range of machinery and techniques, often imported from algebraic topology and algebraic geometry, or in analogy with group homology with coefficients, will further become available to help calculate it as we did in
[
94
,
95
,
97
]
.

Let us also point to the connection of our main result to
topological full groups
.
The topological full group
F(G)
of an ample groupoid
G
encodes the global symmetries of the associated dynamical system, obtained by piecing together the local symmetries encoded in the topological groupoid
G
we start with.
They were first studied in the setting of Cantor minimal systems
[
59
]
, and since then they have been generalized in the context of Bratteli diagrams
[
41
]
, shifts of finite type
[
75
]
, self-similar groups
[
90
]
, and many other systems, leading to ground-breaking solutions of open problems in geometric group theory
[
91
,
113
]
.
They also offer a dynamical perspective on important objects in group theory, such as the Thompson group
V
and its variations
[
88
]
.

Another conjecture of Matui concurrently posed as the HK conjecture, the so-called AH conjecture
[
76
]
*Conjecture 2.9, presented an even more mysterious relation between the groupoid homology of
G
and the group homology of
F(G)
in low degrees.
In a recent work, Li gives a satisfactory answer to this problem, by connecting the
algebraic

K
-theory spectrum of the permutative category that naturally relates
G
and
F(G)
, denoted
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)
, to the corresponding homology groups
[
67
]
.
The following result, which follows from a combination of Li’s results and Theorem
B
, relates the algebraic
K
-theory and operator
K
-theory groups, in a way analogous to Karoubi’s conjecture on the algebraic
K
-theory of C
∗
-algebras (proved by Suslin and Wodzicki
[
116
]
).

Theorem C

(Corollary
6.6
)
.

Let
G
be a groupoid satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
B
. There is an isomorphism of vector spaces

K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{*+2k}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G))\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

The
integral
computation of the homotopy groups of
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)
remains an interesting problem, especially in view of recently established connections with Zakharevich’s higher scissors congruence
K
-theory
[
61
,
126
]
.

Let us give a sketch of the ideas behind the proof of Theorem
B
.
In our previous work
[
94
]
, we have used the triangulated structure of equivariant
\mathit{KK}
-theory to define the
cellular approximation

P(C_{0}(X))
of
C_{0}(X)
in the category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
with respect to the inclusion of
X=G^{(0)}
in
G
. This object features in a convergent spectral sequence

E^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}(G,K_{q}(\mathbb{C}))\Rightarrow K_{p+q}(P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G).

(0.3)

The results in
[
12
]
show that the
K
-groups of
P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G
coincide with
K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))
under the condition that
G
is torsion-free. In this light, it is useful (at least heuristically) to think of the Chern character as a morphism roughly pointing in the opposite direction of the arrow in (
0.3
), causing the spectral sequence to rationally collapse (see Corollary
6.2
).

The isomorphism in (
0.2
) is obtained by “assembling” in a suitable sense the classical Chern character, in its homotopy-theoretic incarnation, given by the stable equivalence of spectra

\mathrm{KU}\wedge H\mathbb{Q}\cong H\mathbb{Q}[u,u^{-1}],

(0.4)

with a formal variable
u
of degree
2
, comparing the complex topological
K
-theory spectrum
\mathrm{KU}
and the
2
-periodic Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum of the rational numbers
H\mathbb{Q}[u,u^{-1}]
.

The reader with background in operator algebras, rather than homotopy theory, may benefit from the following high-level explanation of the construction: on one hand, the equivalence (
0.4
) is precisely what is behind the homotopical refinement alluded to in (
0.1
); on the other, the passage from
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
to its
\infty
-category refinement allows for a favorable presentation of
P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G
as a suitable colimit of the simplicial system
[n]\mapsto C_{0}(G^{(n)})
. This reflects the common wisdom that triangulated categories should be viewed as “shadows” of stable
\infty
-categories.

Going into more detail, consider the simplicial system of spaces
(G^{(n)})_{n=0}^{\infty}
given by the nerve of
G
.
Under the Dold–Kan correspondence, the (rational) groupoid homology can be interpreted as the homotopy colimit of the diagram of Eilenberg–MacLane spectra
HC_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathbb{Q})
.
Thus, our desired isomorphism should be regarded as a map

K_{i}(P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to\pi_{i}\Bigl(\hocolim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}HC_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathbb{Q}[u,u^{-1}])\Bigr).

(0.5)

This points to an interpretation of the left-hand side above as a homotopy colimit as well.

As an object of the triangulated category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
, the
G
-C
∗
-algebra
P(C_{0}(X))
is a suitable
sequential
homotopy colimit of
G
-C
∗
-algebras we can build from
C_{0}(G^{(\bullet+1)})
using exact triangles and the notion of projective objects.
This ought to give the homotopy colimit presentation over
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
we seek, but taking homotopy colimits of general diagrams in a triangulated category is a delicate problem which requires analysis of higher Toda brackets, cf.
[
10
]
.

We work around this problem by promoting
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
to a stable
\infty
-category (see Appendix
A
), following the blueprint provided in
[
63
,
18
]
for ordinary
\mathit{KK}
-theory and equivariant
\mathit{KK}
-theory for discrete groups.
This allows us to make sense of the
\infty
-colimit of the
G
-simplicial object
C_{0}(G^{(\bullet+1)})
in the stable
\infty
-category
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
.
In the case of discrete groups, this is analogous to the Davis–Lück presentation of the assembly map as a homotopy colimit
[
29

over the orbit category.

Using the Morita equivalence between the space
G^{(n)}
as a groupoid and the transformation groupoid
G\ltimes G^{(n+1)}
, and by comparing homotopy colimits in
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
with
\infty

leads to the left-hand side in (

) via homotopy colimit.
We connect this model with more concrete

-theory spectra for C
∗
-algebras from
[
33
]
[
19
]
and replace
\map_{\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{C},C_{0}(Y))
with
\Map_{c}(Y,\mathrm{KU})
, where
\Map_{c}(Y,E)
stands for a suitably defined compactly-supported mapping spectrum from a locally compact totally disconnected space

to a CW-spectrum

.
Then the problem is reduced to finding an isomorphism

\hocolim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\Map_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathrm{KU}\wedge H\mathbb{Q})\to\hocolim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\Map_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},H\mathbb{Q}[u,u^{-1}]),

(0.6)

which indeed would follow from (
0.4
).

To substantiate the above comparison, we first analyze the framework in which the simplicial spectra in (
0.6
) live, namely, the
\infty
-functors from an
\infty
-category of totally disconnected spaces to the semiadditive
\infty
-category of spectra.
Let us denote by
\mathsf{Span}_{\mathrm{pr,\acute{e}t}}(\mathsf{TDLC})
the
(2,1)
-category of spans of totally disconnected, locally compact, Hausdorff spaces

, with left leg a proper map, and right leg an étale map. We have the following result.

Theorem D

(Theorem
2.3
)
.

Let
C
be a semiadditive
(\infty,1)
-category.
The
\infty
-functors

F\colon\mathsf{Span}_{\mathrm{pr,\acute{e}t}}(\mathsf{TDLC})\to C

which are product-preserving, compatible with finite quotients, and compactly supported, can be classified by the value of

This is comparable to the fact that the span category of finite sets

, which naturally sits as a full subcategory of

, is the universal singly generated semiadditive

[

*Theorem 4.1.
We use this classification to identify face maps of various simplicial objects in

-theory and spectra, arising from the standard simplicial space

.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the basic notions in use throughout the paper.

1.1.
Simplicial homotopy

Let us fix conventions from simplicial homotopy theory, following
[
43
]
.
We denote the simplex category, i.e., the category of nonempty ordered finite sets, by
\Delta
.
A simplicial set is a contravariant functor
X\colon\Delta\to\mathsf{Sets}
.
We denote the category of simplicial sets by
\mathsf{sSet}
.
The set
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
with natural order is denoted by
[n]
, and the corresponding simplicial set is denoted by
\Delta[n]
.
We freely identify the elements of
X_{n}=X([n])
with the maps of simplicial sets
\Delta[n]\to X
.
The standard
n
-simplex, which can be considered as the thin geometric realization of
\Delta[n]
, is denoted by
\Delta^{n}
.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, we denote the
k
-th horn in
\Delta[n]
as
\Lambda^{k}[n]
.
By definition, it is is the simplicial set obtained from the boundary of the
n
-simplex
\Delta^{n}
by discarding its
k
-th face.

A
Kan complex
is a simplicial set
X
such that any map of simplicial sets
\Lambda^{k}[n]\to X
extends to a map
\Delta[n]\to X
, i.e., an element of
X_{n}
.
The Kan complexes are the fibrant objects with respect to the standard Quillen model structure on
\mathsf{sSet}

[
102
]
*Section II.3.
All simplicial sets are cofibrant with respect to this model structure.

For any model category
C
, there is a canonically induced model category structure on the corresponding category of pointed objects
C_{*}
(i.e., the coslice category under the terminal object).
The fibrant objects are unchanged, but the cofibrant objects are those with cofibration basepoint inclusion.
We will also write
C^{\circ}
for the full subcategory of fibrant-cofibrant objects.
Thus,
\mathsf{sSet}^{\circ}_{*}
is the subcategory of “well-pointed” Kan complexes.

1.2.
\infty
-Categories

Our references for
\infty
-categories are
[
69
,
70
,
64
]
.

In particular, we consider
(\infty,1)
-categories modeled by
quasicategories
, i.e., simplicial sets
C
satisfying the weak Kan condition: any map of simplicial sets
\Lambda^{k}[n]\to C
for
0<k<n
extends to a map
\Delta[n]\to C
. The category of simplicial sets can be equipped with an alternative model structure whose fibrant objects are precisely the quasicategories.

If
C
is a usual category, its nerve
N(C)
is a simplicial set which is a quasicategory.
In the rest of the paper, when it is clear from context that we are viewing ordinary categories as
\infty
-categories through the nerve functor
N
, we will omit writing
N
from the notation.

We also consider
(2,1)
-categories.
In this setting, when we want to be clear we write
\circ_{1}
for the composition of
1
-cells, and
\circ_{2}
for the composition of
2
-cells. Note that
\circ_{1}
is a bifunctor, so that that
(\psi\circ_{1}\xi)
makes sense as a
2
-cell when
\psi
and
\xi
are
2
-cells. Moreover,
\phi\circ_{2}(\psi\circ_{1}\xi)
is a
2
-cell from a
1
-cell
f\circ_{1}g
to another
h
when we have
2
-cells
\phi\colon f^{\prime}\circ_{1}g^{\prime}\to h
,
\psi\colon f\to f^{\prime}
, and
\xi\colon g\to g^{\prime}
.

Given such a
(2,1)
-category
C
, the
Duskin nerve
construction
N^{\mathrm{D}}(C)

[
36
]
[
71
]
*Section 2.3 gives the corresponding quasicategory.
Concretely, the
n
-th set
N^{\mathrm{D}}_{n}(C)
is given by the set of lax monoidal functors
F
from
[n]
(ordered set regarded as a category) to
C
such that
F(\mathrm{id}_{k})=\mathrm{id}_{F(k)}
(these are also called strictly unitary lax monoidal functors).

We also consider
simplicially enriched categories
, that is, systems
C
given by objects
X
,
Y
, …and simplicial sets
C_{\bullet}(X,Y)
of “morphisms” together with composition maps

C_{n}(Y,Z)\times C_{n}(X,Y)\to C_{n}(X,Z)

compatible with the face and degeneracy maps, satisfying associativity and existence of units
\mathrm{id}_{X}\in C_{0}(X,X)
. If
C
is a usual category, we take the constant simplicial set
C_{n}(X,Y)=C(X,Y)
to treat
C
as a simplicially enriched category.

Simplicially enriched categories
C
whose morphism spaces
C_{\bullet}(X,Y)
are Kan complexes provide another model of
(\infty,1)
-categories. More precisely, there is a model structure on the category of simplicially enriched categories
[
9
]
, and this category is Quillen equivalent to the Joyal model category of simplicial sets; see
[
69
]
*Section 2.2 for a detailed proof.

Given a category
C
enriched in Kan complexes, the corresponding quasicategory is given by the
homotopy coherent nerve

N^{\mathrm{hc}}(C)
. Let us quickly review the construction (see for example
[
64
]
*Definition 1.2.63).
We denote the simplicial path category by
\mathsf{Path}[n]
, which has integers
0\leq k\leq n
as its objects and the nerve of partially ordered set
\{I=\{i=i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}=j\}\}
(with ordering given by the opposite of inclusion order) as the space of morphisms from
i
to
j
(which is empty if
j<i
).
The composition
\Mor_{\mathsf{Path}[n]}(j,k)\times\Mor_{\mathsf{Path}[n]}(i,j)\to\Mor_{\mathsf{Path}[n]}(i,k)
is induced by the union of corresponding sets
I=\{i=i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}=j\}
and
J=\{j=j_{0}<j_{1}\cdots<j_{l}=k\}
.
Then
N^{\mathrm{hc}}_{n}(C)
is given as the set of functors of simplicially enriched categories
\mathsf{Path}[n]\to C
.
At the level of
0
-cells in morphism spaces, compatibility with composition implies that the value of functors on the sets
I=\{i<j\}
determines the value on the general case
I=\{i=i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}=j\}
.

1.3.
Spans

A
span
of finite sets is given by a diagram
X\leftarrow Z\rightarrow Y
in
\mathsf{FinSets}
, the category of finite sets.
We get a
(2,1)
-category
\mathsf{Span}(\mathsf{FinSets})
, whose
0
-cells are finite sets,
1
-cells are spans, and
2
-cells are isomorphisms of spans, with the composition of
1
-cells given by pullbacks.

An
(\infty,1)
-category is called

if there is a zero object

and product

is a natural isomorphism of these bifunctors.

The

-category

is semiadditive, since the empty set

-category, then the map

where

denotes the subspace of product-preserving maps of quasicategories, is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets (see for example

*Theorem 4.1).

1.4.
Localization

Let

be a quasicategory with a collection of morphisms

.
The localization of

at

[
70
]
*Definition 1.3.4.1
[
64
]
*Chapter 2 is given by a map of quasicategories

with the following universal property: for every quasicategory

is a fully faithful embedding whose essential image is the collection of maps

This means that there are morphisms

and

If

-localization” to distinguish it from the localization as an ordinary category (which corresponds to
\pi_{0}(N(C)[W^{-1}])
).

If we start from an ordinary category

(also called simplicial or Dwyer–Kan localization)

defined as follows.

Given two objects

in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y,}

where the leftward arrows are in

Two such zigzags are identified by removing leftward arrows when they are identity morphisms, and then composition of consecutive rightward arrows.
We consider the category of such equivalence classes, where a morphism is represented by a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}_{k}}

-category, we apply Kan’s

The equivalence between these two approaches, that is the comparison between the quasicategory

and the simplicially enriched category

*Proposition 1.2.1.

.

Let

We say that a vertex

Here,

For other characterizations, see

*Section 4.3.
To emphasize the

-colimits, it is technically convenient to consider

.

.

1.6.
Spectra

Let us consider the suspension functor
\Sigma X=S^{1}\wedge X
on
\mathsf{sSet}_{*}
, where
S^{1}
denotes the minimal simplicial circle
\Delta^{1}/\Delta^{0}
.
A
sequential prespectrum of simplicial sets
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded pointed simplicial set equipped with morphisms
\alpha_{n}\colon\Sigma X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
.
A morphism
f\colon X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
between such objects is a sequence of morphisms
f_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n}
of pointed simplicial sets, such that the evident diagram involving
\alpha^{X}_{n}
and
\alpha^{Y}_{n}
is commutative.
This is a simplicially enriched category in the following way: for any simplicial set
K
and prespectrum
X_{\bullet}
, define a new prespectrum by assigning
n
to
X_{n}\wedge K_{+}
with structure maps given by
\alpha_{n}\wedge\mathrm{id}_{K}
.
Then the
\Hom
-object
[X,Y]\in\mathsf{sSet}
is such that
[X,Y]_{n}
is the set of prespectra morphisms
X\wedge\Delta^{n}_{+}\to Y
.
We denote this category by
\mathsf{Spec}
.

Denoting the thin geometric realization of
X\in\mathsf{sSet}
by
\lvert X\rvert
, we define the
stable homotopy groups
of a prespectrum
X_{\bullet}
as

\pi_{n}(X)=\varinjlim_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\pi_{n+k}(\lvert X_{k}\rvert).

Now, the
Bousfield–Friedlander model structure

[
13
]
on
\mathsf{Spec}
is defined by taking as weak equivalences the
stable weak equivalences
: those maps
f\colon X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
whose image under the stable homotopy groups functor
\pi_{*}(f)\colon\pi_{*}(X)\to\pi_{*}(Y)
is an isomorphism; the cofibrations are the
stable cofibrations
: those maps
f\colon X\to Y
such that
f_{0}
and all pushout products between
f_{n}
and
\alpha_{n}^{Y}

X_{n+1}\bigsqcup_{\Sigma X_{n}}\Sigma Y_{n}\to Y_{n+1}

are monomorphisms (cofibrations of simplicial sets).

The fibrant objects in this model structure are precisely the
\Omega
-spectrum
objects with values in
\mathsf{sSet}_{*}^{\circ}
, i.e., those prespectra with values in Kan complexes for which
\lvert\alpha_{n}\rvert
is adjunct to a map
\beta_{n}\colon\lvert X_{n}\rvert\to\Omega\lvert X_{n+1}\rvert
which is a weak homotopy equivalence.
A cofibrant object in
\mathsf{Spec}
is precisely a prespectrum whose structure maps are levelwise injective maps.

We also consider sequential prespectra of topological spaces.
They are given by a sequence of (pointed) topological spaces
(X_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
, endowed with continuous maps

\alpha_{n}\colon S^{1}\wedge X_{n}\to X_{n+1}.

The standard model structure of topological spaces can be used to define a model category
\mathsf{Top}_{\mathrm{cg}}\mathsf{Spec}
of prespectra of
compactly generated
topological spaces.
When we have a general prespectrum of topological spaces
(X_{n})_{n}
, we apply the
k
-ification functor levelwise to obtain an object in
\mathsf{Top}_{\mathrm{cg}}\mathsf{Spec}
.
This does not affect the computation of stable homotopy groups, and we tacitly use this identification throughout the paper; for this reason in the sequel we drop the subscript and write
\mathsf{TopSpec}
instead for simplicity.

There is a Quillen equivalence
\mathsf{Spec}\rightleftarrows\mathsf{TopSpec}
with right adjoint as follows
[
13
]
*Section 2.5. For a space
X
, we define its
singular simplicial complex
as the simplicial set

\Sing X\colon[k]\mapsto\Hom_{\mathsf{Top}}(\Delta^{k},X)

(in other words,
\Sing X
is the nerve of
X
with respect to the standard cosimplicial topological space). By taking the levelwise singular complex of a topological prespectrum, we obtain a prespectrum of simplicial sets
(\Sing(X_{n}))_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
This is always a prespectrum of Kan complexes, hence we obtain an
\Omega
-spectrum of simplicial sets if
X_{\bullet}
is an
\Omega
-spectrum (
\beta_{n}\colon X_{n}\to\Omega X_{n+1}
is a weak homotopy equivalence). Levelwise geometric realization is left Quillen adjoint to
\Sing
.

Given two spectra
X
and
Y
in either of the above frameworks, define the
smash product

X\wedge Y
by

\displaystyle(X\wedge Y)_{2n}

\displaystyle=X_{n}\wedge Y_{n},

\displaystyle(X\wedge Y)_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=S^{1}\wedge X_{n}\wedge Y_{n},

and the appropriate structure maps, see for example
[
1
]
*Section II.4.
In case of
\mathsf{TopSpec}
, when
Y
is an
\Omega
-spectrum of CW-complexes, this construction preserves weak equivalence for
X
, see for example
[
72
]
*Proposition 11.7.
This will be the case for all of our examples (up to the above Quillen equivalence in case of
\mathsf{Spec}
).

1.7.
Eilenberg–MacLane spectra and rationalization

Let
A
be a commutative group.
Then we can view
A
canonically as a
\Gamma
-set
, namely a functor from (pointed) finite sets (and pointed maps) to the category of sets.
The
\Gamma
-set
A
associates
A(F)=A^{F}
(the set of pointed maps
F\to A
) to any finite set
F
, and a map
F\to F^{\prime}
gives rise to a map
A(F)\to A(F^{\prime})
defined by summation along the fibers of non-basepoint elements.
Following
[
13
]
, we extend

to all pointed sets by taking the colimit over finite pointed sets.
By composition,

sends a simplicial set to a simplicial set.
Then, by considering the simplicial spheres
(S^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain a sequence of pointed simplicial sets
(AS^{n})_{n}
, which can be checked to yield a sequential spectrum (see
[
13
]
*Section 4 for more details).

Definition 1.4
.

The
Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum

is the sequential spectrum
(AS^{n})_{n}
.

The spectrum

is fibrant and cofibrant in the model category
\mathsf{Spec}
, and in particular, it is an
\Omega
-spectrum
[
13
]
*Theorem 4.2.
The above construction natural in

, and we consider it a functor

H\colon\mathsf{Ab}\to\mathsf{Spec}.

In the sequel we will not distinguish between
HA
and its geometric realization.
Given another spectrum
\mathsf{E}
, we define the homology of
\mathsf{E}
with coefficients in
A
as

H_{n}(\mathsf{E},A)=\pi_{n}(\mathsf{E}\wedge HA).

As usual, in case of
A=\mathbb{Z}
we just write
H_{n}(\mathsf{E})
.

Given a spectrum
\mathsf{E}
, the
rationalization
(or
\mathbb{Q}
-localization) of
\mathsf{E}
is defined as the smash product
\mathsf{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathsf{E}\wedge H\mathbb{Q}
(it is customary to use the Moore spectrum
M\mathbb{Q}
, but we have the equivalence
H\mathbb{Q}\cong M\mathbb{Q}
).
We have a natural isomorphism

\pi_{n}(\mathsf{E}_{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\pi_{n}(\mathsf{E})\otimes\mathbb{Q},

(1.2)

and the canonical map

, after application of

, reads as

through the identification (
1.2
), see
[
1
]
*Proposition II.6.7. This process makes a generalized homology theory into an ordinary homology theory with coefficients in the following sense.

Lemma 1.5

(
[
1
]
*Lemma II.6.1)
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a spectrum in
\mathsf{TopSpec}
. Then
\mathsf{E}\wedge H\mathbb{Z}
is equivalent to
\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Sigma^{n}HG_{n}
, where

.

As a corollary, there is an equivalence
\mathsf{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma^{n}H(\pi_{n}(\mathsf{E})\otimes\mathbb{Q})
.

Definition 1.6

(Chern–Dold character)
.

The equivalence of spectra

1.8.
Stable
\infty

We assume
\infty
-categories admit finite limits and colimits. A

in

-category. Similarly, we will view

as a stable

-category and omit localization from the notation.

The Kasparov category

-categories for this paper. The treatment of

-category first appeared in

1.10.
Compactly supported cohomology and reduced cohomology

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space.
Then we have its one-point compactification
X^{+_{c}}=X\cup\{\infty\}
, which is a pointed compact Hausdorff space.
When
E^{\bullet}
is a generalized cohomology theory given by a sequential (topological) spectrum
(E_{n})_{n}
we define the compactly supported cohomology
E_{c}^{n}(X)
,

E_{c}^{n}(X)=\varinjlim_{k}\,[\Sigma^{k}X^{+_{c}},E_{n+k}]_{*},

(1.4)

where
[\Sigma^{k}X^{+_{c}},E_{n+k}]_{*}
denotes the set of homotopy classes of pointed continuous maps from the
k
-fold suspension of
X^{+_{c}}
to
E_{n+k}
. When
X
is already compact, we have
E_{c}^{n}(X)\cong E^{n}(X)
, the latter being defined as free homotopy classes of maps into
E
.

Compactly supported cohomology is contravariant for proper continuous maps: such a map
X\to Y
induces a well-defined map
X^{+_{c}}\to Y^{+_{c}}
. Moreover, we have covariance for embedding of open sets
U\to X
, since we get a map
X^{+_{c}}\to U^{+_{c}}
through the homeomorphism
U^{+_{c}}\cong X^{+_{c}}/(U^{c}\cup\{\infty\})
.

Example 1.9
.

Consider the topological
K
-theory spectrum
\mathrm{KU}=(\mathrm{KU}_{n})_{n}
. Then the corresponding compactly supported cohomology
\mathrm{KU}_{c}^{n}(X)
agrees with the topological
K
-group
K^{n}(X)
, which is by definition the reduced
K
-group

\tilde{K}^{n}(X^{+_{c}})=\ker(K^{n}(X^{+_{c}})\to K^{n}(\{\infty\}))

of the compactification.
In general, when
X
is a compact space without fixed base point, we make sense of
\tilde{K}^{n}(X)
as the cokernel of the canonical map
K^{n}(*)\to K^{n}(X)
(the one induced by
X\to*
).

Remark 1.10
.

Suppose that
X
is a totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff space, and that the spectrum
E
is given by locally contractible spaces

as a sequential spectrum. Then there is an another model for

, given as the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{K}E^{n}(K)
, where
K
runs over the directed system of compact open sets in

and inclusion maps between them.

To see this, suppose that we have a class
\alpha

represented by a map
f\colon\Sigma^{k}X^{+_{c}}\to E_{n+k}
.
Let
\tilde{f}\colon X\to E_{n+k}^{S^{k}}
be the continuous map corresponding to
S^{k}\times X^{+_{c}}\to E_{n+k}
induced by
f
, under the exponential law for spaces.
Take a contractible neighborhood
U\subset E_{n+k}
of the basepoint, and let
\tilde{U}\subset E_{n+k}^{S^{k}}
be the subset consisting of the maps whose range is contained in
U
.
Then
\tilde{U}
is an open neighborhood of
\tilde{f}(\infty)
, and the inverse image
\tilde{f}^{-1}(\tilde{U})\subset X^{+_{c}}
contains a compact open neighborhood
V
of
\infty
.
By construction

is also a compact open set, and the restriction of
f
to
K
defines a class
\alpha^{\prime}\in E^{n}(K)
, which maps to

Let

for the abelian group

, see for example
[

]
.
Note that, when

*Section II.5.10.

From (

), the Chern character induces an isomorphism

(1.6)

The main result in
[

states that

represents Čech cohomology with coefficients in

1.11.
Topological groupoids and C
∗
-algebras

For an introduction to topological groupoids and their associated C
∗
-algebras we refer to
[
105
,
124
]
. We do not recall these notions in detail here and generally follow the treatment in our previous work
[
94
]
. Unless otherwise specified,
G
will be a second countable, locally compact, Hausdorff groupoid with unit space
G^{(0)}=X
. In general,
G^{(n)}
will denote the space of
n
-tuples from
G
whose elements
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
are composable, i.e.,
r(\gamma_{i+1})=s(\gamma_{i})
.

We are going to assume that
G
is
étale
. This means the source and range maps of
G
, denoted
s
and
r
, are local homeomorphisms and each point in
G
admits a local base of
bisections
. These are opens
W\subset G
such that

and
s|_{W}
are homeomorphism onto an open set.

Definition 1.11

(Ample groupoid)
.

An étale groupoid
G
is ample if it admits a topological basis consisting of compact open bisections. Equivalently,
G
is ample if its unit space is totally disconnected.

For any point
x\in X
, denote by
G_{x}
(respectively,
G^{x}
) the subspace of arrows in
G
whose source (range) is
x
. Then the union, over all
x\in X
, of the groups
G^{x}_{x}=G_{x}\cap G^{x}
form a group bundle over
X
, which is a closed subgroupoid
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
, called the
isotropy
(or
stabilizer
) subgroupoid of
G
.

Definition 1.12
.

We say
G
is
torsion-free
if
G^{x}_{x}
is a torsion-free group for all
x\in X
. We say
G
is
essentially principal
if the interior of
G^{\prime}
is
X
. We say
G
is

if

.

There are several equivalent notions of
groupoid equivalence
or
Morita equivalence
, see
[
40
]
*Proposition 3.10 for an overview (the notion most familiar to operator algebraists is in
[
86
]
); it implies that C
∗
-algebras of equivalent groupoids are strongly Morita equivalent. Another formulation, useful in view of groupoid homology (see (

) below) is given in
[
25
]
*Section 4.5.
A useful example of a groupoid which is equivalent to
G
is given by the
ampliation
(or
blow-up
) of
G
associated to an étale surjection
\varphi\colon Y\to X
, as follows:

G^{\varphi}=\{(y_{1},\gamma,y_{2})\in Y\times G\times Y\mid\varphi(y_{1})=r(\gamma),\varphi(y_{2})=s(\gamma)\}

with evident composition and inversion maps. The induced projection
\hat{\varphi}\colon G^{\varphi}\to G
is a prototypical example of Morita equivalence in the sense of
[
25
]
*Section 4.5.

Since
G
is étale, the fibers of

and

are discrete and
G
admits a Haar system given by counting measures.
This allows us to define the reduced groupoid C
∗
-algebra of

, which is the completion of the natural convolution algebra

of compactly supported continuous functions on

with respect to the operator norm obtained as the supremum over all

representations of

associated to point masses on its unit space

.

Thus, given a (separable)

-C
∗
-algebra

, for example

, we can consider the reduced groupoid
crossed product
C
∗
-algebra

. This is denoted

when

. We do not spell out this construction here, and refer the reader to
[
105
,
124
,
58
]
for the full details of its definition.

1.12.
Correspondences

We consider the
(2,1)
-category

of proper correspondences between C
∗

Its objects (
0

∗
-algebras,

Given an (ample) groupoid

-C

By definition, a

-C

∗
-algebra

endowed with a nondegenerate homomorphism of
C_{0}(G^{(0)})
to the center of the multiplier algebra of

, together with the action map

-algebra structures on

Since we assume that

for open sets

and

for open bisections

Given a

-C

-algebra

and a right Hilbert

-action on

, for example as suitable collection of isomorphisms

for open bisections

, where we write

(note that any vector

for

Then, when

are

-C

.

1.13.
Groupoid homology

Let us briefly review groupoid homology with coefficients in equivariant sheaves
[
25
]
. When
G
is a topological groupoid with base space
X
, a
G
-equivariant sheaf
(of commutative groups) over
X
consists of a
G
-space
F
whose structure map
F\to X
is a surjective local homeomorphism with fibers abelian groups.

In fact, when
G
is ample, such
G
-sheaves are represented by
unitary
C_{c}(G,\mathbb{Z})
-modules

[
115
]
. A module
M
over
C_{c}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is said to be unitary if it has the factorization property
C_{c}(G,\mathbb{Z})M=M
. The correspondence is given by
\Gamma_{c}(U,F)=C_{c}(U,\mathbb{Z})M
for compact open sets
U\subset X
if
F
is the sheaf corresponding to such a module
M
. It will be convenient to write
\mathbb{Z}(-)
in place of
C_{c}(-,\mathbb{Z})
.

By virtue of
G^{(0)}
being totally disconnected, this is a
c
-soft sheaf, thus
H_{*}(G,\mathbb{Q})
can be succinctly described as the homology of the canonical chain complex arising from the simplicial space
(G^{(n)})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
n
-tuples of composable arrows.

Remark 1.13
.

Underlying the equivalence between
G
-sheaves and (unitary)
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules is the fact that, for any closed set
C\subseteq X
, the functor
\Gamma_{c}(C,-)
preserves short exact sequences. Since
\Gamma_{c}(C,F|_{C})\cong H^{0}_{c}(C,F|_{C})
, this follows from the long exact sequence in compactly supported sheaf cohomology, combined with the fact that higher cohomology groups of totally disconnected spaces vanish.

The definition of groupoid homology is based on
c
-soft
resolutions of sheaves. A sheaf is
c
-soft if, for any compact subspace
K\subset X
and
s\in\Gamma(K,F)
, there is a global section
s^{\prime}\in\Gamma(X,F)
such that
s^{\prime}|_{K}=s
. Any sheaf of commutative groups over a totally disconnected, second countable, locally compact Hausdorff space is
c
-soft
[
94
]
*Proposition 2.8. Thus, when working over ample groupoids, there is no need for sheaf resolutions, which makes it possible to define groupoid homology relying solely on unitary
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules and homological algebra
[
11
]
*Section 2.

We are going to assume that the
cohomological dimension
of
X
is finite. More precisely, the
c
-cohomological dimension of a space
X
is the smallest integer
m
such that every sheaf of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules on
X
admits a
c
-soft resolution of length at most
m
(and infinity if no such
m
exists). Note that if the covering dimension of every compact subset of
X
is at most
m
, then the
c
-cohomological dimension of
X
is at most
m
(see
[
15
]
*Corollary III.4.12). We will henceforth assume that the
c
-cohomological dimension of the unit space
G^{(0)}=X
is finite; this is automatic for ample groupoids.

Definition 1.14

(Ample groupoid homology)
.

Let
M
be a unitary
\mathbb{Z}G
-module. The groupoid homology
H_{n}(G,M)
of
G
with coefficients in
M
is the
n
-th
\Tor

\Tor^{\mathbb{Z}G}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}X,M)
.

Hence
H_{n}(G,M)
is characterized as the
n
-th left derived functor of the functor
M\mapsto\mathbb{Z}G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}M
, i.e., the group of
G
-coinvariants of
M

[
11
]
*Lemma 2.2.

Given a chain complex of
G
-sheaves
F_{\bullet}
, we can define the associated
hyperhomology
groups
\mathbb{H}_{*}(G,F_{\bullet})
in terms of the corresponding complex of
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules

as follows:
\mathbb{H}_{n}(G,F_{\bullet})
is the
n
-th homology group of the total complex coming from the bicomplex
(P_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}M_{j})_{ij}
, where
P_{\bullet}\to\mathbb{Z}X
is a resolution by projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules. For example, one can take the
flat
resolution induced by the nerve of

, given by
P_{i}=\mathbb{Z}G^{(i+1)}
,

(see
[
67
]
*Section 2.3 and
[
83
]
*Section 2).
For concreteness, if
M
is the
\mathbb{Z}G
-module associated to the constant sheaf with stalk

(an abelian group), the resulting chain complex from this resolution, which computes

, is given by
(C_{c}(G^{(n)},A))_{n=0}^{\infty}
with differential

\displaystyle\partial_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(d^{n}_{i})_{*}\colon C_{c}(G^{(n)},A)\to C_{c}(G^{(n-1)},A),

\displaystyle(d^{n}_{i})_{*}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d^{n}_{i}(y)=x}f(y),

(1.7)

where

is the

When two étale groupoids

and

are Morita equivalent, there are natural correspondences between the

In particular, if

is a Morita equivalence homomorphism, we have
[

]
*Corollary 4.6:

\mathbb{H}_{\ast}(G,F_{\bullet})\cong\mathbb{H}_{\ast}(H,f^{*}F_{\bullet})

(1.8)

for any complex

of

2.

We consider the following

-category

The

The

Given a semiadditive
(\infty,1)
-category

(\infty,1)
-categories from

(2.1)

holds for any projective system of finite sets

such that

with respect to the maps

.

Example 2.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative group.
Given a locally compact totally disconnected space

, we consider the space of compactly supported locally constant

-valued functions,

.
If

is a

and the summation over fibers

Any two

By the functoriality of the Eilenberg–MacLane spectra construction

, we get a map of

.

This way we get an element in

5.
Generalized Chern character for ample groupoids

Recall
G
is a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, ample groupoid. We have that
(G^{n+1})^{\infty}_{n=0}
is a simplicial
G
-space, thus it gives a simplicial object of
\mathsf{Span}_{\mathrm{pr,\acute{e}t}}(\mathsf{TDLC}^{G})
. The associated quotient object
(G\backslash G^{n+1})^{\infty}_{n=0}
is clearly isomorphic to
(G^{n})^{\infty}_{n=0}
, a simplicial object in
\mathsf{Span}_{\mathrm{pr,\acute{e}t}}(\mathsf{TDLC})
.

By means of Prop.
2.6
, we obtain a simplicial object
\Map_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathrm{KU})
in
\mathsf{TopSpec}
. Thanks to the work in Section
2
and
3
, now we can define the Chern character for
G
at the level of simplicial spectra.

For a spectrum
\mathsf{E}
, it will be convenient to write
G^{\bullet}\mathsf{E}
in place
\Map_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathsf{E})
. For a simplicial object
\mathsf{E}^{\bullet}
in
\mathsf{TopSpec}
, the
\infty
-colimit
\colim_{\Delta^{op}}\mathsf{E}^{\bullet}
will be denoted
\lvert\mathsf{E}^{\bullet}\rvert
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
Chern character
for the ample groupoid
G
is defined as the map

G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\to\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Sigma^{2n}HC_{c}(G^{\bullet},\mathbb{Q})

between simplicial objects in
\mathsf{TopSpec}
, obtained as the following composition

G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\to G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\wedge H\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\cong}G^{\bullet}(\mathrm{KU}\wedge H\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\cong}G^{\bullet}(H\mathbb{Q}[u,u^{-1}])\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Sigma^{2n}HC_{c}(G^{\bullet},\mathbb{Q}),

where the first map is the standard inclusion
X\to X\wedge Y
, and the following equivalences are due to Corollary
2.7
, the Chern-Dold equivalence in (
1.3
), and Proposition
3.10
, in order from left to right.

Definition
5.1
is the Chern character of
G
“defined at the level of spectra” that was promised in Theorem
A
. Notice that we don’t need
G
to be torsion-free to make this definition. For the Chern character of
G
as a map of abelian groups, as stated in Theorem
A
, please see Corollary
5.7
below.

Proposition 5.2
.

We have the following isomorphism of abelian groups:

\pi_{*}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\pi_{*}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert\wedge H\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{H}_{*}(G,K_{\bullet}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

Since the smash product preserves colimits, we have
\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert\wedge H\mathbb{Q}\cong\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\wedge H\mathbb{Q}\rvert
. Since the right-hand side of Definition
5.1
is a coproduct of simplicial objects, commuting the colimit and the functor
\pi_{*}
with the coproducts, we reduce the computation to
\pi_{*}(HC_{c}(G^{\bullet},\mathbb{Q}))
. The standard spectral sequence associated to the homotopy colimit converges (see
[
70
]
*Variant 1.2.4.9 and Proposition 1.4.3.6) and its
E_{1}
-page reads
\pi_{q}(HC_{c}(G^{(p)},\mathbb{Q}))
. From the properties of Eilenberg–MacLane spectra, this page is concentrated in the
0
-th row, so the homotopy groups of
\lvert HC_{c}(G^{\bullet},\mathbb{Q})\rvert
are isomorphic to the homology groups of the chain complex
\pi_{0}(HC_{c}(G^{\bullet},\mathbb{Q}))
. For all
n\geq 0
, we have

\pi_{0}(HC_{c}(G^{(n)},\mathbb{Q}))\cong C_{c}(G^{(n)},\mathbb{Q})

from the discussion in Subsection
1.10
. The boundary maps are identified in Proposition
3.10
, thus comparing with the description in (
1.7
) gives the claim.
∎

Following up on Remark
4.4
, we now consider the simplicial object
L^{\bullet+1}A
obtained by choosing
F=\Res^{G}_{X}
and
F^{\dagger}=\Ind_{X}^{G}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

There is an equivalence of simplicial objects
\mathbb{K}(L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G)\cong G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}
. In particular, taking the colimit over
\Delta^{\text{op}}
, we have an equivalence

\lvert\mathbb{K}(L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G)\rvert\cong\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert.

Proof.

There is a
G
-equivariant
\ast
-isomorphism
L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\cong C_{0}(G^{(\bullet+1)})
. The counit of the
(\Ind_{X}^{G},\Res^{G}_{X})
-adjunction gives the augmentation map
\epsilon_{X}\colon C_{0}(G)\to C_{0}(X)
. All the face maps in
C_{0}(G^{(\bullet+1)})
are obtained from
\epsilon
, for example the two maps
C_{0}(G^{(2)})\to C_{0}(G)
are given by
\epsilon_{C_{0}(G)}
and
L(\epsilon_{X})
.

Following the definition of the counit given in
[
12
]
*Theorem 2.3, the augmentation map
\epsilon_{X}
is described as

C_{0}(G)\hookrightarrow C_{0}(G)\rtimes G\sim C_{0}(X),

i.e., it is given by a
\ast
-homomorphism (inclusion) followed by a Morita equivalence.
Comparing with the discussion below Lemma
3.2
, we see that
\epsilon_{X}
is represented by the (equivariant) correspondence
E^{G}_{s}=E(\Omega^{G}_{s})
, where
\Omega^{G}_{s}
is the groupoid correspondence associated to the local homeomorphism

. To be precise, we have that
T_{\mathsf{KK}}^{G}(E^{G}_{s})
(using Prop.
3.6
) is a lift of
\epsilon_{X}\in\mathsf{KK}^{G}(C_{0}(G),C_{0}(X))
to
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
. Then it follows that all the other face maps are represented by

, where
\partial^{i}_{n}
is the
G
-equivariant

-th face map

,

. By applying Prop.
3.3
, we see that
L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G
is isomorphic (in
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}
) to
C_{0}(G^{(\bullet)})
with face maps

, where

is the standard face map,

. Again, to be precise, there is a natural isomorphism
T_{\mathsf{KK}}(E^{G}_{\partial^{i}_{n}}\rtimes G)\cong\jmath_{r}^{G}T_{\mathsf{KK}}^{G}(E^{G}_{\partial^{i}_{n}})
, and we are applying Prop.
3.3
to

to deduce the isomorphism of simplicial objects

L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G\cong T_{\mathsf{KK}}(E(G\backslash G^{(\bullet+1)}))\cong T_{\mathsf{KK}}(E(G^{(\bullet)})).

Then Corollary
1.8
implies that
\mathbb{K}(L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G)\cong\Map_{c}(G^{(\bullet)},\mathrm{KU})
levelwise.

Moreover, we have proved that
\mathbb{K}(L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G)
, viewed as a functor from

, factors through a functor in

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose
G
is a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, ample groupoid. Assume
G
is torsion-free. The following isomorphism holds:

K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))\cong\pi_{*}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert).

Proof.

Recall from Theorem
4.6
that
P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G
is isomorphic to
\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}{L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G}
in
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}
.
Since
\map_{\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{C},-)
commutes with arbitrary colimits, we have

\map_{\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{C},P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G)\cong\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\map_{\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{C},L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G).

By Proposition
1.7
we obtain

\lvert\map_{\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{C},P(C_{0}(X))\rtimes G)\rvert\cong\lvert\mathbb{K}(L^{\bullet+1}C_{0}(X)\rtimes G)\rvert.

Then Theorem
4.3
and Proposition
5.3
imply the claim.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose
G
is a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, ample groupoid. Assume
G
is torsion-free. The following isomorphism holds:

K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q}).

(5.1)

If in addition
G
satisfies the rational Baum–Connes conjecture, then the rational HK conjecture for
G
holds, i.e., we have the following isomorphism of abelian groups:

K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q}).

(5.2)

Proof.

Let us first suppose that
G
satisfies the first set of conditions.
We use Proposition
5.4
and Proposition
5.2
. Notice that the right-hand side of Proposition
5.2
is
2

\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{n+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q})
in degree

, hence we obtain the first claim.

If in addition

satisfies the rational Baum–Connes conjecture, we have

K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))\otimes\mathbb{Q},

and the second claim follows.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The Baum–Connes conjecture holds for a large class of groupoids. For example, based on work by Higson and Kasparov
[
47
]
, Tu
[
119
]
has proved the conjecture for every locally compact,
\sigma
-compact, Hausdorff groupoid acting continuously and isometrically on a continuous field of affine Euclidean spaces (these groupoids are sometimes referred to as
a-T-menable
, or with the

). This class contains (topologically) amenable groupoids.

We can now formulate the Chern character of
G
as a map of abelian groups, having the same domain as the assembly map of

, as was stated in Theorem

of the Introduction.

Corollary 5.7
.

Suppose
G
is a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, ample groupoid. Assume
G
is torsion-free. The Chern character of
G
from Definition
5.1
induces the following morphism of abelian groups:

K_{*}^{\mathrm{top}}(G,C_{0}(G^{(0)}))\to\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
5.2
shows that the isomorphism (
5.1
) is obtained by applying geometric realization and homotopy groups to the chain of equivalences in Definition

. The morphism in the claim is then obtained by precomposing (
5.1
) with the standard inclusion of

into its rationalization. Under the identification of Proposition

, this corresponds to the inclusion of
\pi_{*}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert)
into its rationalization. This map is induced by the inclusion

in the first leg of the smash product, which appears as the first map in Definition

∎

6.
Applications

6.1.
HK conjecture

Theorem
5.5
proves a modified form of the original HK conjecture formulated by Matui in
[
76
]
*Conjecture 2.6 (and implicitly in his earlier work
[
74
]
*Section 3). The formulation in Theorem
5.5
appeared in print in
[
11
]
*Remark 5.12, however it has been known to experts in the field since shortly after the appearance of
[
76
]
.

Matui’s original formulations asks if the following isomorphism holds:

K_{i}(C^{*}_{r}G)\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{i+2k}(G;\mathbb{Z}).

The original HK conjecture is different from Theorem
B
in a number of ways, which we now comment on.
First of all, the original conjecture prescribed an
integral isomorphism
(i.e., an isomorphism
before
rationalization).
This may be explained in view of the well-known fact that the Chern character of totally disconnected spaces is an integral isomorphism (note that groupoid homology specializes to negatively-graded compactly supported cohomology when calculated on a space, viewed as a trivial groupoid, see
[
25
]
*Section 3.5).
However, Deeley has shown that the integral version of Theorem
B
does not hold, by providing a counterexample groupoid
G
that is also
principal

[
30
]
.

Matui’s original formulation imposes a topological condition on isotropy, namely that the interior of the isotropy subgroupoid of
G
equals
G^{(0)}
; a groupoid of this form is called
essentially free
. Scarparo has produced a counterexample to the original HK conjecture which is essentially free, but not torsion-free
[
110
]
.
Overall we can say that the failure of Matui’s HK conjecture is tied to torsion phenomena in
K
-theory groups, or in the isotropy groups of the groupoid itself.

It should be noted that Matui’s original motivation, that is the study of topological dynamical systems and their invariants, is one where virtually all groupoids involved happen to be amenable, in particular they satisfy the Baum–Connes conjecture (by work of Tu
[
119
]
, see Remark
5.6
). This may account for why this condition was overlooked. The context of topological dynamical systems is also likely the explanation why the original conjecture imposed
minimality
on
G
, i.e., that
G
-orbits be dense, as interesting dynamical systems often exhibit this behavior. Minimality is not relevant for Theorem
B
.

We should also point out that there are cases where an HK-type isomorphism is true but not covered by our Theorem
B
.
For example, when
G
is a discrete group (possibly with torsion elements), Lück proves a rational HK isomorphism involving Bredon group homology.
Raven
[
104
]
proved the analogue of (
0.2
) for étale transformation groupoids (whose unit space has finite cohomological dimension) by considering a bivariant homology theory introduced by Baum and Schneider
[
104
,
5
]
.
Using this theory, the latter authors prove the rational HK isomorphism for actions of profinite groups, while Voigt proves it for actions of totally disconnected (locally compact) groups on locally finite, finite-dimensional
G
-simplicial complexes (after identifying the Baum–Schneider bivariant homology with bivariant equivariant local cyclic homology)
[
122
,
123
]
.
Recently, Deeley and Willett proved that the bivariant homology groups appearing in Raven’s construction in fact coincide with
H_{*}(G,\mathbb{C})
,
de facto
proving the
étale
rational HK conjecture for transformation groupoids
[
31
]
.
These results suggest several potential generalizations and variants of Theorem
B
, which are discussed in Section
6.1.2
and Section
6.1.3
.

Remark 6.1

(Groups and group actions)
.

In the statement of Theorem
5.5
,
G
is allowed to be a countable discrete group. In this case the conclusion is implied by
[
68
]
*Theorem 0.1. More generally, Theorem
5.5
applies when
G
is the transformation groupoid

for an action of a (countable) discrete group

. This case is implied by
[

]
*Corollary 3.2.

6.1.1.
Étale HK conjecture

If
G=G^{(0)}=X
is a trivial groupoid, with
X
not necessarily totally disconnected, then the statement of Theorem
5.5
holds, and it coincides with the classical Chern character isomorphism of (

). This is because groupoid homology for a trivial groupoid computes (compactly) supported cohomology (with grading inverted, see
[
25
]
*Section 3.5).

More generally, the statement of Theorem
5.5
holds when
G
is the transformation groupoid
H\ltimes X
, for an action of a (countable) discrete group
H
on a locally compact, second countable, Hausdorff space

(recall from Section
1.13
that we have a standing assumption that

has finite

-cohomological dimension). The full details are given in a recent paper by Deeley and Willett
[
31
]
.

In
[
97
]
, we have showed the existence of étale groupoids, which are not ample, and for which the statement of Theorem
5.5
holds
before rationalization
.

These results point to the validity of a rational étale HK isomorphism, whose statement would read just like that of Theorem

6.1.2.

Suppose

6.1.3.
Other variants

The authors of

have also formulated a version of the rational (étale) HK isomorphism which does not require the isotropy groups of

*Section 5.3. Let

Let

6.3.
Degeneration of ABC-spectral sequence up to torsion

The Chern character of
G
can be used to prove the rational collapse of the spectra sequence in (

).

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose
G
is torsion-free, and satisfies the rational Baum–Connes conjecture.
Then the spectral sequence

degenerates after tensoring
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Without these assumptions, we have a canonical map
H_{i}(G,\mathbb{Z})\to K_{i}(C^{*}_{r}(G))
for

.
The isomorphism (

) implies that the induced map
H_{i}(G,\mathbb{Q})\to K_{i}(C^{*}_{r}(G))\otimes\mathbb{Q}

This means that all higher differentials

We then obtain well-defined maps

Again the isomorphism (

∎

We have the following consequence on the rank of

.

Let

Proof.

We apply Theorem
5.5
to

. Notice that

is an equivalence relation, thus it has trivial isotropy groups; it is also amenable
[

]
*Theorem 1.1, hence it satisfies the Baum–Connes conjecture

*Theorem 3.5 (see also

∎

Corollary

provides an answer to

*Question 8.4.1 for the class of Smale spaces with totally disconnected stable sets. We have proved in

6.5.
Topological full groups and algebraic
K
-theory

X. Li has proved a remarkable result connecting the homology of an ample groupoid with the homology of its associated topological full group
[
67
]
. A key ingredient in Li’s strategy is the construction of the

K
-theory spectrum
associated to an ample groupoid
G
. Let us denote this object
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)
. It is defined as the Segal

-theory of the symmetric monoidal category generated by the embedding category
\mathsf{Emb}_{c}(G)
(see Def.
A.1
) by using its

structure; the details of this construction are not important for our purposes, the reader may consult
[

,
61
,
126
,
112
]
for more information.

Recall that the Karoubi conjecture, as proved by Suslin and Wodzicki
[
116
]
, states that the topological
K
-groups of a C
∗
-algebra
A
are isomorphic to the algebraic
K
-groups of the stabilized C
∗
-algebra
A\otimes\mathcal{K}
(viewed as a discrete ring).
The following result shows that the algebraic
K
-theory spectrum of
G
serves as a substitute of
\mathrm{BGL}(A\otimes\mathcal{K})^{+}
after rationalization.
It can also be interpreted as determining the rational homotopy type of
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)[u,u^{-1}]
.

Corollary 6.6
.

Suppose
G
is a second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, ample groupoid. There is an isomorphism

\pi_{*}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{*+2k}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G))\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

In particular, if
G
is torsion-free and satisfies the rational Baum–Connes conjecture,

K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{*+2k}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G))\otimes\mathbb{Q}

Proof.

On the one hand, we have

\pi_{n}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G))\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\pi_{n}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)\wedge H\mathbb{Q}),

see (
1.2
).
By the Hurewicz isomorphism
[
111
]
*Proposition II.6.30,
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)\wedge H\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to the (reduced) homology of
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)
with rational coefficients.
By
[
67
]
*Theorem 4.18, this is isomorphic to
H_{n}(G,\mathbb{Q})
.
On the other hand, we saw that
\pi_{m}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
agrees with
\bigoplus_{k}H_{m+2k}(G,\mathbb{Q})
in Proposition
5.2
.
Under the rational Baum–Connes conjecture, we can use
K_{m}(C^{*}_{r}(G))
instead of
\pi_{m}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert)
by Theorem
5.5
.
∎

Regarding the topological full group, we have the following consequence. Let
\mathcal{R}
denote the full equivalence relation on the natural numbers. For an ample groupoid
G
, we denote by

its topological full group. When

is compact, the topological full group

is the group of compact open bisections with source and range equal to

. Multiplication is defined by multiplication of

as the colimit

).

Given a

-graded rational vector space

-graded unital commutative algebra

.
Namely, it is a unital

-algebra generated by the vectors of

subject to the relation

xy=(-1)^{\left|x\right|\left|y\right|}yx

for homogeneous elements
x
,

.
We treat it as a graded algebra by the grading

\left|x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|x_{i}\right|

.

Corollary 6.7
.

Suppose

satisfies the hypotheses of Corollary
6.6
. There is an isomorphism

\mathbb{H}_{*}(F(G\times\mathcal{R}),K_{\bullet}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{Q})\cong\uCom(\pi_{\bullet}(\lvert G^{\bullet}\mathrm{KU}\rvert)\otimes\mathbb{Q})

of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces.
In particular, if
G
is torsion-free and satisfies the rational Baum–Connes conjecture, there is an isomorphism

\mathbb{H}_{*}(F(G\times\mathcal{R}),K_{\bullet}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{Q})\cong\uCom(K_{\bullet}(C^{*}_{r}G)\otimes\mathbb{Q})

(6.3)

of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces.

Proof.

By
[
67
]
*Corollary 6.1, we have

\mathbb{H}_{*}(F(G\times\mathcal{R}),K_{\bullet}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{Q})\cong\uCom(H_{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{Q})),

where we treat

as a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space in the obvious way.
Proposition

and Theorem

say that

∎

\mathsf{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{alg}^{s}_{G}
is a category of fibrant objects together with:

•

the class of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
-equivalences as weak equivalences; and

•

the class of semisplit surjections as fibrations.

Proof.

The terminal object is given by the trivial algebra
0
.
Pullbacks exist in
\mathsf{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{alg}_{G}
in general, which are given by the usual pullback of vector spaces, that is,
W
in (
A.1
) can be given by

X\times_{Z}Y=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\mid f(x)=g(y)\}.

This construction is compatible with surjective maps
f
with completely positive splitting, since

Y\to X\times_{Z}Y,\quad y\mapsto(s(g(y)),y)

would be a completely positive splitting for the map
f^{\prime}(x,y)=y
if
s
is a one for
f
.

The only nontrivial remaining point is the compatibility for morphisms in
F\cap W
with respect to the pullback.
We note that an equivariant
*
-homomorphism
f\colon X\to Y
is in this intersection class if and only if it is in the class
F
, and furthermore its kernel
I
is isomorphic to
0
in the category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
.
Indeed, by knowing that
f
is in
F
, we have a
6
-term exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{i}(Z,I)\to\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{i}(Z,X)\to\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{i}(Z,Y)\to\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{i+1}(Z,I)\to\cdots

for any
Z\in\mathsf{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{alg}^{s}_{G}
, see
[
118
]
*Proposition 7.2.
In particular,
f
belongs to
F\cap W
if and only if
I
is isomorphic to
0
in
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
.

Now, coming back to the pullback diagram (
A.1
), by the above description we see that
\ker f^{\prime}
agrees with
\ker f
.
In particular,
f^{\prime}
is in
F\cap W
if
f
is in this class.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Any morphism
f\colon A\to B
in
\mathsf{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{alg}^{s}_{G}
can be factorized as
f=g\circ h
, with morphisms
h\in W
and
g\in F
.

Proof.

Let us take the mapping cylinder

C=\{(a,k)\in A\oplus C([0,1],B)\mid f(a)=k(0)\}.

Then the canonical map
h\colon A\to C
given by
a\mapsto(a,k_{f(a)})
, where
k_{b}
is the constant function with value
b
, is a homotopy inverse to the first factor projection
C\to A
, hence is a
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
-equivalence.
Moreover, the evaluation map
g\colon C\to B
given by
(a,k)\mapsto k(1)
is in the class
F
, with completely positive splitting given by
b\mapsto(0,tb)
.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

In the
\infty
-category
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}

Proof.

Let us consider the

We consider the action

A.3.
Geometric realization

Proposition A.9

(
[
18
]
*Lemma 2.19)
.

The stable
\infty
-category
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
admits countable colimits.

Proof.

By stability, it is enough to show that it admits countable coproducts.
These can be detected in the homotopy category, so the claim follows from the standard fact that
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
admits countable coproducts, given by the countable direct sum.
∎

Corollary A.10
.

Let
A_{\bullet}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a simplicial system of objects in
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
, given by an
\infty
-functor from (the nerve of)
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
to
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
.
Then the colimit
\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}A_{\bullet}
exists in
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
.

Definition A.11
.

We call
\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}A_{\bullet}
the
geometric realization
of
A_{\bullet}
.

An important example of countable colimit is the sequential colimit
A=\varinjlim A_{n}
associated to an inductive system
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathsf{KK}_{\infty}^{G}
.
For example, the geometric realization
\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}A_{\bullet}
above can be written as a sequential colimit
\varinjlim D_{n}
, where
D_{n}=\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}_{\leq n}}A_{\bullet}
is the colimit of
A_{\bullet}
restricted to the subcategory of
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
spanned by the objects
[m]
for
m\leq n
, i.e., the
n
-skeleton of
A_{\bullet}
.

Proposition A.12

(cf.
[
79
]
*Section 2.4)
.

Let

be an inductive system of objects, given by an
\infty
-functor

Its colimit can be computed in

as the mapping cone of the map

given by

, where

Proof.

The equivalence between these two models goes as follows: the mapping cone is the coequalizer of the pair

between the coproducts

.
This colimit can be rewritten as a colimit over a single category

The canonical projection

is cofinal, which implies the claim by
[

]
*Proposition 4.1.1.8.
∎

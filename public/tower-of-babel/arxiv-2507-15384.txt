Anatomy of Non-Hermitian Dynamical Quantum Phase Transitions
Yongxu Fu, Gao Xianlong

Abstract
We establish a unified framework for dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs) in non-Hermitian systems that encompasses both biorthogonal and self-norm non-biorthogonal formulations for pure and mixed states. Our framework provides explicit expressions for the Loschmidt amplitude, Loschmidt echo, and rate function, revealing a universal geometric signature of DQPTs: orthogonality of two related vectors in two-dimensional real space. Strikingly, we demonstrate that non-biorthogonal quenches from non-Hermitian to Hermitian Hamiltonians under chiral symmetry exhibit emergent topological characteristics, unveiling the intrinsic topological nature of DQPTs beyond conventional Hermitian regimes. This work establishes fundamental geometric and topological principles governing quantum criticality in open systems, with implications for quantum sensing and many-body physics in dissipative environments.

Mixed-state Loschmidt amplitudes in non-Hermitian systems.—

A fundamental approach to characterizing the finite-temperature DQPTs in Hermitian systems employs the mixed-state Loschmidt amplitude upon the quantum quench protocol

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho(0)U(t)\right],

(1)

\rho(0)

\hat{H}_{0}
, and
U(t)=\exp(-i\hat{H}_{1}t)
is the unitary time-evolution operator governed by the postquench Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}

U(t)
does not correspond to the parallel transport on a principal fiber bundle with the unitary structure group
\mathbb{U}(\mathcal{N})

\mathcal{N}
denotes the dimension of the Hilbert space at each point of the base manifold
sup

;
Heyl and Budich 2017

Mondal and Nag 2022
;

We demonstrate that the definition of the generic Loschmidt amplitude in Eq. (
1

\hat{H}_{0}
and
\hat{H}_{1}
are generic non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. However, from a mathematical perspective, the density matrix of a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian is formulated in the biorthogonal basis as

\displaystyle\varrho=\sum_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}p_{\alpha}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|,

(2)

where
\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}},|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle
denote the many-body right and left eigenstates, respectively, each constructed from arbitrary occupations of single-particle states. The coefficients
p_{\alpha}
represent the participation probabilities associated with these eigenstates. It is important to note that

can take complex values, which implies that the non-negative trace-class property of the density matrices cannot be guaranteed in non-Hermitian systems. Although complex participation probabilities lack direct physical significance, the inherent ambiguity in the physical interpretation of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians compels us to focus on derived quantities obtained through further analysis to quantify their physical relevance. In this paper, we focus on one-dimensional systems; however, the general formalism remains applicable to higher-dimensional settings.

Incorporating the biorthogonal formulation, the Loschmidt amplitude in Eq. (
1

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)=\prod_{k\in occ}\mathcal{G}_{k}(t),\quad\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\left[\varrho_{k}(0)U_{k}(t)\right],

(3)

where
U_{k}(t)=\exp[-iH_{1}(k)t]
and
H_{1}(k)
is the single-particle postquench Hamiltonian in momentum space. The initial density matrix
\varrho(0)
decomposes into several single-particle components

\displaystyle\varrho_{k}(0)=\sum_{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}p_{k}^{n}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|,

(4)

where
\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}
and
|u_{n}^{0}(k)\rangle\!\rangle
are the
n
th-band right and left eigenstates of the single-particle prequench Hamiltonian
H_{0}(k)
, respectively. The many-body probabilities

are expressed as a product over single-particle contributions,
p_{\alpha}=\prod_{(k,n)}p_{k}^{n}
, corresponding to a given occupation configuration (see Supplementary Materials
sup
for detail calculations). The Loschmidt amplitude in Eq. (
3
) applies to initial mixed states with arbitrary occupation configurations. In particular, when

is nonzero for all bands, the quasi-momentum
k
spans half or the entire Brillouin zone (BZ), corresponding to a half or full filled system, respectively. Moreover, the corresponding Loschmidt echo is defined as the squared norm of the Loschmidt amplitude,
\mathcal{L}(t)=\left|\mathcal{G}(t)\right|^{2}
, up to an overall normalization factor that does not affect the occurrence of DQPTs

Biorthogonal DQPTs.—

In the context of biorthogonal DQPTs, the corresponding rate function, which plays a role analogous to the free energy density in equilibrium phase transitions, is given by

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=-\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathcal{L}(t),

(7)

where
N
denotes the number of degrees of freedom. Rate function nonanalyticity signals DQPTs via Fisher zeros of the Loschmidt amplitude
Heyl et al. 2013
;
Zvyagin 2016
;
Heyl 2018
.

We focus on a generic two-band lattice model described by
\mathcal{H}(k)=\vec{h}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}
, where
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
is the vector of three Pauli matrices. The prequench and postquench Hamiltonians,
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)
, correspond to different parameterizations of
\vec{h}_{0}(k)
and
\vec{h}_{1}(k)
, respectively. The rate function can be expressed as

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int dk\ln(\left|\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)\right|^{2}),

(8)

in the thermodynamic limit, where the integration range depends on the specific occupation configuration. We begin with the initial generalized Gibbs state at temperature
T=1/\beta
,

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(0)=\frac{e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)}\right]}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}-n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right],

(9)

where
h_{0}(k)=\left[h_{0,x}^{2}(k)+h_{0,y}^{2}(k)+h_{0,z}^{2}(k)\right]^{1/2}
,
\hat{h}_{0}(k)=\vec{h}_{0}(k)/h_{0}(k)
, and
n_{k}=\tanh\left[\beta h_{0}(k)\right]
is generally complex value. This mixed state formally has complex participation probabilities
p_{k}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\mp n_{k}\right)
,
associated with the two bands
\epsilon_{k\pm}^{0}=\pm h_{0}(k)
. The Loschmidt amplitude factor for each momentum mode
k
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]+i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}

(10)

where
\hat{h}_{1}(k)
is defined analogously to
\hat{h}_{0}(k)
as the normalized vector
\vec{h}_{1}(k)/h_{1}(k)
. In the zero-temperature limit
\beta\rightarrow\infty
(corresponding to the pure ground-state case), we have
n_{k}=\tanh[\beta h_{0}(k)]\rightarrow 1
, and the Loschmidt amplitude factor simplifies to
\mathcal{G}^{ZT}_{k}(t)=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]+i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}
. In the infinite-temperature limit
\beta\rightarrow 0
, the Loschmidt amplitude reduces to
\mathcal{G}^{IT}_{k}(t)=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]
, which coincides with the case studied in Ref.
Zhou and Du 2021
.

Analogous to the Lee-Yang zeros of thermodynamic functions
Yang and Lee 1952
;
Lee and Yang 1952
, the time variable
t
in
\mathcal{G}(t)
can be extended into the complex plane via the analytic continuation
t\mapsto z=\tau+it
. The Fisher zeros of the analytically continued
\mathcal{G}_{k}(z)
are then given by (with
n=0,1,2,\ldots
)

\displaystyle z_{n}=\frac{i\pi(2n+1)}{2h_{1}(k)}-\frac{1}{h_{1}(k)}\tanh^{-1}\left(n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}\right).

(11)

Introducing the notation
\mathcal{Q}_{k}=\tanh^{-1}\left(n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}\right)
and
a=\pi(n+1/2)
, and decomposing

h_{1}(k)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
into their real and imaginary parts as
h_{1r}=\mathrm{Re}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],h_{1i}=\mathrm{Im}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],\mathcal{Q}_{kr}=\mathrm{Re}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{k}\right],\mathcal{Q}_{ki}=\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{k}\right]
, the real and imaginary parts of the Fisher zeros can be identified.
The physical critical points of DQPTs are determined by the Fisher zeros lying on the imaginary axis, which simultaneously identify the critical momenta

k_{c}
through the condition
ah_{1i}-h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}_{kr}-h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}_{ki}=0
. This condition implies that the two vectors
\vec{v}_{s}=(\mathcal{Q}_{kr},\mathcal{Q}_{ki}-a)
and
\vec{v}_{h}=(h_{1r},h_{1i})
in the two-dimensional (2D) plane are orthogonal, i.e.,

\displaystyle\vec{v}_{s}\cdot\vec{v}_{h}=0.

(12)

Hence, as long as the polar angle between this two vectors
\phi=\phi_{s}-\phi_{h}
in the 2D plane sweeps through
n\pi/2
with odd
n
, the critical point
k_{c}
of the DQPTs will occur, where
\phi_{s}
and
\phi_{h}
denote the polar angles of
\vec{v}_{s}
and
\vec{v}_{h}
, respectively (with the
k
-dependence omitted for brevity). Consequently, the DQPT critical times
t_{c}
correspond to the sequence of
z_{n}
evaluated at the critical momentum
k_{c}
, where the condition
\vec{v}_{s}\cdot\vec{v}_{h}=0
is satisfied.

From a geometric perspective, the winding number associated with a generic
k
-dependent 2D vector is computed by integrating its polar angle
\varphi
over the BZ,

\displaystyle W=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathrm{BZ}}d\varphi.

(13)

Define
\Delta W=W_{s}-W_{h}
as the difference between the winding numbers associated with
\vec{v}_{s}
and
\vec{v}_{h}
, respectively, and if

\displaystyle\left|\Delta W\right|\geq\frac{1}{2},

(14)

then the phase difference
\phi
must traverse at least one multiple of
n\pi/2
(with odd
n
) across the BZ, regardless of the initial offset between
\phi_{s}
and
\phi_{h}
, thereby ensuring the occurrence of a DQPT. However, the occurrence of DQPTs does not guarantee that Eq. (
14
) holds, due to the arbitrary initial offset of
\phi
. In this context, the conditions for the occurrence of biorthogonal DQPTs reflect a geometric rather than a topological feature, owing to the generic non-topological (quantized) nature of
\Delta W
.

The topological character manifests in the Hermitian limit with certain symmetries, where the condition (
12
) reduces to
\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}=0
in zero or finite temperature, and becomes directly connected to underlying topological invariants characterizing the (equilibrium) topological phases before and after the quench, such as the winding number,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant, and Chern number
Vajna and Dóra 2015
;
Budich and Heyl 2016
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
;
Heyl and Budich 2017
. In the infinite-temperature limit, DQPTs necessarily occur in Hermitian systems due to the real-value nature of
h_{1}(k)
, whereas in intrinsically non-Hermitian systems, their occurrence becomes conditional. Interestingly, while the DQPTs in Hermitian systems do not depend upon the initial-state (zero or finite) temperature
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
;
Heyl and Budich 2017
, temperature plays a nontrivial role in non-Hermitian DQPTs due to the complex-valued nature of the Hamiltonians. In other words, complex Hamiltonians enhance the likelihood of the DQPTs occurring not only at zero temperature but also at finite temperatures.

Non-biorthogonal DQPTs.—

The biorthogonal formulation provides a natural mathematical generalization from Hermitian to non-Hermitian scenarios. Nonetheless, biorthogonality is not always essential, as its necessity may depend on specific physical considerations. Particularly in the pure-state case, the non-biorthogonal Loschmidt amplitude

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{ZT}(t)=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)},

(15)

and the associated Loschmidt echo
\mathscr{L}_{ZT}(t)=\left|\mathscr{G}_{ZT}(t)\right|^{2}
directly quantify the overlap between the time-evolved postquench state and the initial eigenstate, characterizing the return probability following a quantum quench. Additionally, the non-biorthogonal observables, normalized by the norm of the associated time-evolved state, align with the no-jump limit of the approximate Lindblad master equation
Sim et al. 2023
;
Sim et al. 2025
. Formally, in the non-biorthogonal formulation of DQPTs, the relevant expressions differ from the biorthogonal case only by replacing the terms involving left eigenstates with the Hermitian conjugates of the corresponding right eigenstates. Thus, the mixed-state non-biorthogonal Loschmidt amplitude is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}(t)=\prod_{k\in occ}\mathscr{G}_{k}(t),\quad\mathscr{G}_{k}(t)\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{k}(0)U_{k}(t)\right],

(16)

where the single-particle components of the non-biorthogonal initial density matrix
\rho(0)
is

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(0)=\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)}.

(17)

We again take the two-band model
\mathcal{H}(k)
as a representative example for illustration. However, in the non-Hermitian context, a non-biorthogonal mixed state such as the Gibbs state—with analytically tractable participation probabilities
p_{k}^{\pm}
—is generally not applicable. This is because the natural extension of statistical mechanics to non-Hermitian systems adopts the biorthogonal rather than the non-biorthogonal framework. The Loschmidt amplitude formally corresponds to
sup

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{k}(t)=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\braket{P}_{0},

(18)

where we have denoted
\braket{P}_{0}=\tilde{p}_{k}^{+}\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}+\tilde{p}_{k}^{-}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}
and
\tilde{p}_{k}^{\pm}=p_{k}^{\pm}/(p_{k}^{+}+p_{k}^{-})
.
Here,
\ket{\tilde{u}_{\pm}^{0}(k)}
are the self-norm right eigenstates of
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
, satisfying
\braket{\tilde{u}_{\pm}^{0}(k)|\tilde{u}_{\pm}^{0}(k)}=1
, which is inconsistent with the biorthogonal relations. A similar expression for the rate function
\lambda^{\prime}(t)
can be derived within the non-biorthogonal formulation.

Following the derivation in the biorthogonal formulation, we can obtain the non-biorthogonal Fisher zeros (with
n=0,1,2,\ldots
)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{i\pi(2n+1)}{2h_{1}(k)}+\frac{1}{h_{1}(k)}\tanh^{-1}\braket{P}_{0}.

(19)

Again, by introducing the notation
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}=\tanh^{-1}\braket{P}_{0}
,
a=\pi(n+1/2)
, and decomposing

h_{1}(k)
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}
into their real and imaginary parts as
h_{1r}=\mathrm{Re}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],h_{1i}=\mathrm{Im}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr}=\mathrm{Re}\left[\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}\right],\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}=\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}\right]
, we obtain the non-biorthogonal critical-
k_{c}
condition
ah_{1i}+h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr}+h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}=0
.
It implies that the two vectors
\vec{v}^{\prime}_{s}=(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr},\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}+a)
and
\vec{v}_{h}=(h_{1r},h_{1i})
in the 2D plane are orthogonal, i.e.,

\displaystyle\vec{v}^{\prime}_{s}\cdot\vec{v}_{h}=0

(20)

The ensuing geometric and topological interpretations closely parallel those in the biorthogonal framework. Particularly, one can define a winding number relation in the non-biorthogonal framework analogous to Eq. (
14
), where the two formulations become equivalent in the Hermitian limit.

Topological characteristic of DQPTs with chiral symmetry.—

In the non-biorthogonal formulation, a distinctive winding topology arises during a quench between two chiral symmetric Hamiltonians of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}(k)=d_{x}\sigma_{x}+d_{y}\sigma_{y},

(21)

specifically from a non-Hermitian initial Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{s,0}(k)
to a Hermitian final Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{s,1}(k)
, under ground-state-like participation probabilities
p_{k}^{-}=1
and
p_{k}^{+}=0
. Applying the sufficient condition of DQPTs given in Eq. (
20
), where the postquench energy
d_{1}(k)=\left(d_{x,1}^{2}+d_{y,1}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
and
\braket{P}_{0}=\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{s,1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}
are both real value, we obtain that
\braket{P}_{0}=0
implies the occurrence of the DQPTs. After some algebra, the critical-
k_{c}
condition is determined by the orthogonality between two vectors in the complex plane—the prequench vector
\mathcal{R}e^{i\phi^{0}}
and the normalized postquench vector
\hat{d}_{1}=\hat{d}_{x,1}+i\hat{d}_{y,1}
with the angle
\phi^{1}
, where the real-value
\mathcal{R}
is computed from the prequench Hamiltonian (see Supplementary Materials
sup
for details). In other word, when the angle difference
\Delta\phi=\phi^{1}-\phi^{0}
covers
n\pi/2
(with odd
n
), the DQPTs must occur. Therefore, the nonzero winding number difference

\displaystyle\Delta\nu=\nu^{1}-\nu^{0}=\mathbb{Z}/2,

(22)

is the sufficient condition of the occurrence of DQPTs, where

\displaystyle\nu^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathrm{BZ}}dk\,\frac{d_{x,0}\partial_{k}d_{y,0}-d_{y,0}\partial_{k}d_{x,0}}{d_{x,0}^{2}+d_{y,0}^{2}},

(23)

\displaystyle\nu^{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{BZ}}dk\,\hat{d}_{1}^{-1}\partial_{k}\hat{d}_{1},

(24)

and
\nu^{0}
(
\nu^{1}
) is the half-integer (integer) quantized non-Hermitian (Hermitian) winding number
sup
. Interestingly, since the underlying equilibrium topological phases of the non-Hermitian prequench and postquench Hermitian Hamiltonians are characterized by these two winding numbers, such DQPTs can be identified as a new type of dynamical topological phase transition.

To exemplify such topological characteristic of DQPTs, we consider the chiral symmetric non-Hermitian Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model with
d_{x}=t_{1}+t_{2}\cos k

In standard implementations, dissipation serves as a crucial mechanism for realizing non-Hermitian quantum physics

In our setup, a Hermitian SSH model with flux-controlled dissipation, described by the Lindblad master equation, exhibits a non-unitary short-time evolution of a non-biorthogonal density matrix that is effectively governed by a non-Hermitian SSH Hamiltonian prior to the occurrence of quantum jumps (see Supplementary Materials

This dissipation-controlled quench scheme is experimentally feasible and holds promise for realization in photonic systems, cold atom platforms, and electrical circuit simulators.

Conclusions.—
In this work, we have systematically analyzed non-Hermitian DQPTs from both biorthogonal and non-biorthogonal perspectives, extending the formulation to encompass mixed-state dynamics. Our unified treatment of Loschmidt amplitudes and echos captures the essential geometric features of DQPTs. Furthermore, the discovery of topological signatures in a special non-biorthogonal quench scenario—establishing an analytical relationship between the equilibrium topological phases prequench and postquench systems—indicates that the topological nature of DQPTs transcends conventional Hermitian limit. These findings open avenues for further exploration of dynamical topology in open, dissipative, and experimentally relevant non-Hermitian systems.

Parallel transport and geometric phases in Hermitian systems

Returning to the physical context about the geometric phases of mixed-state density matrices, we define the base space
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
as the space associated with the (nonnegative trace class) density matrix
\rho
of a Hermitian system governed by the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
. The amplitude
\omega
of the density matrix
\rho
is then defined by,

\displaystyle\rho=\omega\omega^{\dagger},

(25)

which essentially specifies the surjection
p
from the total space, identified as the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
of the amplitude [Fig.
2
(b)], to the base space
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}

Uhlmann 1986
;
Uhlmann 1989
;
Uhlmann 1991
;
Hübner 1992
;
Hübner 1993
;
Viyuela et al. 2014
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2017

\displaystyle\omega=\sqrt{\rho}U,

(26)

where
U
is an arbitrary unitary matrix representing the gauge freedom. This implies that the structure group is the unitary group

corresponds to the degrees of freedom of the Hilbert space. Mathematically, the total space

can be rigorously identified with the complex general linear group

is constructed as the quotient manifold

\mathbb{U}(N)
symmetry is modded out through the equivalence relation
\omega\sim\omega U

However, the abstract concept of parallel transport discussed above is somewhat difficult to apply directly. Uhlmann, however, presents a geometric framework that facilitates the implementation of parallel transport

;
Uhlmann 1989
;
Uhlmann 1991

\omega_{1}

\displaystyle d(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{\dagger},

(27)

which induces the parallel transport condition
Uhlmann 1986
;
Uhlmann 1989
;
Uhlmann 1991
;
Hübner 1992
;
Hübner 1993

\displaystyle\omega_{1}^{\dagger}\omega_{2}=\omega_{2}^{\dagger}\omega_{1}>0.

(28)

The standard infinitesimal parallel transport on principal bundles can be generalized to a form of distant parallel transport along a sequence of base points. In differentiable settings, the conventional concepts are regained by taking the appropriate limits

\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}

\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}+\dot{\omega}_{1}ds

\dot{\omega}

\dot{\omega}
explicitly denotes a tangent vector
\dot{\omega}^{\mu}\partial/\partial x^{\mu}
at the point
\omega
, where
\dot{\omega}^{\mu}=dx^{\mu}(\omega)/ds
is the component in the local coordinate

. The vertical component
\dot{\omega}_{V}

is defined such that for an arbitrary smooth function

\displaystyle\dot{\omega}_{V}f(\omega)=\frac{d}{ds}f\left[\omega\exp(sA)\right]\Big|_{s=0},

where

is a generator of
\mathbb{U}(N)

which implies that

;
Uhlmann 1989
;
Uhlmann 1991

Beyond the mathematical framework, parallel transport can also be defined within the experimental context of quantum interferometry

\displaystyle\phi_{dyn}=-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(t^{\prime})\hat{H}\right]=-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(0)U^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\dot{U}(t^{\prime})\right]=0,

where we have used

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(0)U^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\dot{U}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[U(t^{\prime})\rho(0)U^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\dot{U}(t^{\prime})U^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(t^{\prime})\dot{U}(t^{\prime})U^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(t^{\prime})(-i\hat{H})U(t^{\prime})U^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\rho(0)}^{\dagger}U(t)\sqrt{\rho(0)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\rho(0)}U(t)\sqrt{\rho(0)}U(t)^{\dagger}U(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\rho(0)}\sqrt{\rho(t)}U(t)\right],

I.2
Purification formulation

The parallel transport and geometric phase described above can be rephrased in terms of their purification formulation
Uhlmann 1991
;
Sjöqvist et al. 2000
;
Viyuela et al. 2014

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{ext}=\mathscr{H}_{s}\otimes\mathscr{H}^{\prime},

(40)

where the dimension of
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is at least as large as the dimension of
\mathscr{H}_{s}
. For clarity, let us assume a basis
\left\{\ket{\psi_{i}}\right\}
for the Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{s}
. The density matrix can then be written as

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}},\quad p_{i}\geq 0

(41)

and the amplitude is expressed as

\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{i}\sqrt{p_{i}}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}U.

(42)

We define the pure state in the extended Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}^{ext}
with the choice
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}=\mathscr{H}_{s}
as
Uhlmann 1991
;
Sjöqvist et al. 2000
;
Viyuela et al. 2014

\displaystyle\ket{\omega}=\sum_{i}\sqrt{p_{i}}\ket{\psi_{i}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\psi_{i}},

(43)

where
U^{T}
denotes the transpose of the unitary matrix
U
. The corresponding density operator is then given by

\displaystyle\rho=\mathrm{Tr}_{2}\left(\ket{\omega}\bra{\omega}\right),

(44)

where
\mathrm{Tr}_{2}
denotes the partial trace over
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
. We observe the relation
\braket{\omega|\omega^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\omega\omega^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}\right]
. The Loschmidt amplitude of the pure state in the extended Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}^{ext}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}(t)=\braket{\omega(0)|\omega(t)},

(45)

where we assume the prequench state as Eq. (
43
), and the postquench state is

\displaystyle\ket{\omega(t)}=\sum_{i}\sqrt{p_{i}}U(t)\ket{\psi_{i}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\psi_{i}}.

(46)

Note that
U(t)
only acts on the first state in the physical Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{s}
. It is explicit that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\left(\sqrt{p_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}\otimes\bra{\psi_{i}}U^{*}\right)\left(\sqrt{p_{j}}U(t)\ket{\psi_{j}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\psi_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\bra{\psi_{i}}U(t)\ket{\psi_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\braket{\psi_{i}|p_{j}\ket{\psi_{j}}\bra{\psi_{j}}U(t)|\psi_{i}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(0)U(t)\right]=\mathcal{G}(t).

(47)

The dynamical phase can also be expressed as

\displaystyle\phi_{dyn}=-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\braket{\omega(t^{\prime})|\hat{H}_{1}|\omega(t^{\prime})},

(48)

which follows from the derivation

\displaystyle\phi_{dyn}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{m,n}\left(\sqrt{p_{m}}\bra{\psi_{m}}U(t^{\prime})^{\dagger}\otimes\bra{\psi_{m}}U^{*}\right)\hat{H}_{1}\left(\sqrt{p_{n}}U(t^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{n}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\psi_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{m}p_{m}\bra{\psi_{m}}U(t^{\prime})^{\dagger}\hat{H}_{1}U(t^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{m}}

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{m}p_{m}\bra{\psi_{m}}U(t^{\prime})^{\dagger}\dot{U}(t^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{m}}

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{m,n}p_{m}\braket{\psi_{m}|\psi_{n}}\bra{\psi_{n}}U(t^{\prime})^{\dagger}\dot{U}(t^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{m}}

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{m,n}p_{n}\braket{\psi_{m}|\psi_{n}}\bra{\psi_{n}}U(t^{\prime})^{\dagger}\dot{U}(t^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{m}}

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{m}\bra{\psi_{m}}\rho(0)U(t^{\prime})^{\dagger}\dot{U}(t^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{m}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(t^{\prime})\hat{H}_{1}\right].

(49)

Thus, we can fully reformulate the geometric phase and Loschmidt amplitude in terms of their purification formulation, where the parallel transport condition is given by
Sjöqvist et al. 2000

\displaystyle\braket{\omega(t)|\hat{H}_{1}|\omega(t)}=\braket{\omega(t)|\dot{\omega}(t)}=0,

(50)

which enforces the cancellation of the dynamical phase.

II
Non-Hermitian dynamics

In (Hermitian) quantum mechanics, the time evolution is governed by the Schrödinger equation
i\partial_{t}\ket{\psi(t)}=\hat{H}\ket{\psi(t)}
for the time-dependent state of the time-dependent state
\ket{\psi(t)}
in the Schrödinger picture and Heisenberg equation
\partial_{t}\hat{O}(t)=-i\left[\hat{O}(t),\hat{H}\right]
for the time-dependent operator
\hat{O}(t)
in the Heisenberg picture, where
\ket{\psi(t)}=e^{-i\hat{H}t}\ket{\psi(0)}
and
\hat{O}(t)=e^{i\hat{H}t}\hat{O}e^{-i\hat{H}t}
with the unitary time-evolution operator
U(t)=e^{-i\hat{H}t}
.

Breaking the Hermiticity, the biorthogonal (stationary) eigenstates emerge for non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
\hat{H}\neq\hat{H}^{\dagger}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{right\,\,eignestate}\qquad\hat{H}\ket{u_{n}}=\epsilon_{n}\ket{u_{n}},

(51)

\displaystyle\mathrm{left\,\,eignestate}\qquad\,\,\hat{H}^{\dagger}|u_{n}\rangle\!\rangle=\epsilon^{*}_{n}|u_{n}\rangle\!\rangle.

(52)

Using the completeness relation
\sum_{n}\ket{u_{n}}\langle\!\langle u_{n}|=\hat{1}
, the time evolution of a state expressed in terms of the right eigenstates is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(t)}=e^{-i\hat{H}t}\ket{\psi(0)}=\sum_{n}\mathscr{C}_{n}(t)\ket{u_{n}},

(53)

where
\mathscr{C}_{n}(t)=\langle\!\langle u_{n}|e^{-i\hat{H}t}|\psi(0)\rangle=\mathscr{C}_{n}e^{-i\epsilon_{n}t}
with
\mathscr{C}_{n}=\langle\!\langle u_{n}|\psi(0)\rangle
. To compute observables and expectation values correctly, we also need to define the corresponding associate state
\bra{\tilde{\psi}(t)}
, which can be done in three different manners, as explained below.

In the first manner, we choose the Hermitian conjugate of
\ket{\psi(t)}
such as

\displaystyle\bra{\tilde{\psi}(t)}=\bra{\psi(t)}=\bra{\psi(0)}e^{i\hat{H}^{\dagger}t}=\sum_{n}\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}(t)\bra{u_{n}},

(54)

where
\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}(t)=\bra{\psi(0)}e^{i\hat{H}^{\dagger}t}|u_{n}\rangle\!\rangle=\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}e^{i\epsilon^{*}t}
. That means the Heisenberg equation becomes
\partial_{t}\hat{O}(t)=-i\hat{O}(t)\hat{H}+i\hat{H}^{\dagger}\hat{O}(t)
, and the observable of
\hat{O}(t)
is

\displaystyle\langle\hat{O}(t)\rangle=\braket{\psi(t)|\hat{O}|\psi(t)}=\sum_{nm}\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}(t)\mathscr{C}_{m}(t)\braket{u_{n}|\hat{O}|u_{m}}=\sum_{nm}\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}\mathscr{C}_{m}e^{i(\epsilon_{n}^{*}-\epsilon_{m})t}\braket{u_{n}|\hat{O}|u_{m}}.

(55)

This expectation value requires normalization by
1/\braket{\psi(t)|\psi(t)}
at each time due to the decay or grow term
e^{-\mathrm{Im}(\epsilon_{n}^{*}-\epsilon_{m})t}
, but this approach neglects the biorthogonal structure of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian.

In the second manner, we define
\bra{\tilde{\psi}(t)}
as
Yang et al. 2024

\displaystyle\bra{\tilde{\psi}(t)}=\bra{\psi(0)}e^{i\hat{H}t}=\sum_{n}\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}(t)\langle\!\langle u_{n}|,

(56)

where
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}(t)=\braket{\psi(0)|e^{i\hat{H}t}|u_{n}}=\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}e^{i\epsilon_{n}t}
with
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}=\braket{\psi(0)|u_{n}}
. The Heisenberg equation then retains the same form as in Hermitian systems
\partial_{t}\hat{O}(t)=-i\left[\hat{O}(t),\hat{H}\right]
, and the observable of
\hat{O}(t)
is

\displaystyle\langle\hat{O}(t)\rangle=\braket{\tilde{\psi}(t)|\hat{O}|\psi(t)}=\sum_{nm}\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}(t)\mathscr{C}_{m}(t)\langle\!\langle u_{n}|\hat{O}\ket{u_{m}}=\sum_{nm}\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}\mathscr{C}_{m}e^{i(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})t}\langle\!\langle u_{n}|\hat{O}\ket{u_{m}}.

(57)

This also requires normalization by
1/\braket{\tilde{\psi}(t)|\psi(t)}
at each time due to the decay or grow term
e^{-\mathrm{Im}(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})t}
. However, if
\hat{O}
is diagonal in the biorthogonal eigenstate basis, such as the Hamiltonian itself, the expectation value simplifies to
\langle\hat{O}(t)\rangle=\sum_{n}\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}\mathscr{C}_{n}\tilde{O}_{nn}
, which is time-independent and requires normalization by
1/\sum_{n}\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{n}^{*}\mathscr{C}_{n}
, where
\tilde{O}_{nn}=\langle\!\langle u_{n}|\hat{O}\ket{u_{n}}
, e.g., the eigenenergy
\epsilon_{n}
for Hamiltonian.

The third manner involves using the complete biorthogonal form, as defined in Ref.
Jing et al. 2024
,

\displaystyle\ket{\tilde{\psi}(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\mathscr{C}_{n}(t)|u_{n}\rangle\!\rangle,

(58)

\displaystyle\bra{\tilde{\psi}(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}(t)\langle\!\langle u_{n}|.

(59)

The expectation value of
\hat{O}(t)
in this case is

\displaystyle\langle\hat{O}(t)\rangle=\braket{\tilde{\psi}(t)|\hat{O}|\psi(t)}=\sum_{nm}\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}(t)\mathscr{C}_{m}(t)\langle\!\langle u_{n}|\hat{O}\ket{u_{m}}=\sum_{nm}\mathscr{C}_{n}^{*}\mathscr{C}_{m}e^{i(\epsilon_{n}^{*}-\epsilon_{m})t}\langle\!\langle u_{n}|\hat{O}\ket{u_{m}}.

(60)

This still requires normalization by
1/\braket{\tilde{\psi}(t)|\psi(t)}
at each time due to the decay or grow term
e^{-\mathrm{Im}(\epsilon_{n}^{*}-\epsilon_{m})t}
, and we cannot obtain a simple Heisenberg equation from this approach.

III
Loschmidt amplitudes in non-Hermitian systems

The origins of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians are diverse, encompassing phenomena such as non-equilibrium open quantum systems
Song et al. 2019
. Here, we set aside these intricate physical contexts and focus directly on the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian itself. By examining the quantum statistical operator (density matrix), we investigate the general form of the non-Hermitian Loschmidt amplitudes and the associated dynamical quantum phase transitions. It is important to note that the non-Hermitian statistical mechanics we consider differs from related works, which primarily study systems where non-Hermitian Hamiltonians are coupled to an external environment
Du et al. 2022
;
Cao and Kou 2023
;
Cao and Kou 2024
. In contrast, our work focuses on the intrinsic statistical mechanical properties of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians themselves.

Considering the biorthogonal basis nature of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians, we can define the non-Hermitian density matrix upon non-biorthogonal and biorthogonal formulations as

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}p_{\alpha}\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}},

(61)

\displaystyle\varrho=\sum_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}p_{\alpha}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|,

(62)

where
\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}},|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle
represent the generic many-body right and left eigenstates, respectively, and
p_{\alpha}
denote the participation probabilities of these eigenstates. It is important to note that
p_{\alpha}
can take complex values, which implies that the non-negative trace-class property of the density matrices cannot be guaranteed in non-Hermitian systems. Although complex participation probabilities lack direct physical significance, the inherent ambiguity in the physical interpretation of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians compels us to focus on derived quantities obtained through further analysis to quantify their physical relevance. Another point that needs to be clarified is that the calculation of the trace must use the biorthogonal basis, even if the density matrix is expressed in a non-biorthogonal form. The trace property in the biorthogonal basis, which for an arbitrary operator
\hat{O}
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\hat{O}\right]=\sum_{\alpha}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|\hat{O}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}=\sum_{\alpha}\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}\hat{O}|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle.

(63)

This formulation ensures the consistency of the trace operation in the biorthogonal framework, generalizing the conventional Hermitian case. The last two equalities in Eq. (
63
) hold due to the cyclic property of the trace and the biorthogonal structure of the eigenstates, that is
\mathrm{Tr}\left[S^{-1}\hat{O}S\right]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[S^{\dagger}\hat{O}(S^{\dagger})^{-1}\right]
, where the transformation matrix
S
and its inverse
S^{-1}
are constructed from the right and left eigenstates of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, respectively,

\displaystyle S=\left(\begin{matrix}\ket{\Psi_{1}}_{1}&\ldots&\ket{\Psi_{N}}_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\ket{\Psi_{1}}_{N}&\ldots&\ket{\Psi_{N}}_{N}\end{matrix}\right),\quad S^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{1}|_{1}&\ldots&\langle\!\langle\Psi_{1}|_{N}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle\!\langle\Psi_{N}|_{1}&\ldots&\langle\!\langle\Psi_{N}|_{N}\end{matrix}\right).

(64)

III.1
Biorthogonal formulation

Biorthogonalization serves as the natural generalization of the orthogonal framework in Hermitian systems to the non-Hermitian regime, providing a complete basis for describing the eigenstates and dynamics of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. The base space
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
and structure group
\mathbb{G}
of the principal bundle in biorthogonal formulation are both limitlessly generalized to
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)
, which leads to the total space
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}=\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)\times\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)
. The surjection is constructed by

\displaystyle p(\omega)=\omega\tilde{\omega},

(65)

where

\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}U,

(66)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}=U^{-1}\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|,

(67)

\displaystyle U\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N).

The corresponding purification is

\displaystyle\ket{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}},

(68)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|(U^{T})^{-1}.

(69)

The associated calculation are given by

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{2}\left[\ket{\omega}\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}|\right]=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\gamma}|U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|(U^{T})^{-1}\ket{\Psi_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\gamma}|U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|(U^{T})^{-1}\ket{\Psi_{\gamma}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|(U^{T})^{-1}\ket{\Psi_{\gamma}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\gamma}|U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|(U^{T})^{-1}U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}=\sum_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}p_{\alpha}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|,

(70)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\omega}\omega^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}^{\prime}}U^{-1}|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}\bra{\Psi_{\beta}}U\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}^{\prime}}|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}\bra{\Psi_{\beta}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\bra{\Psi_{\beta}}\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}\Psi_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}},

(71)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\omega^{\prime}\tilde{\omega}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}UU^{-1}|\Psi_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}\Psi_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\beta}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}},

(72)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}\ket{\omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}^{\prime}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|(U^{T})^{-1}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}^{\prime}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|(U^{T})^{-1}U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}^{\prime}},

(73)

namely,
\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}\ket{\omega^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\omega^{\prime}\tilde{\omega}\right]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\omega}\omega^{\prime}\right]
.

However, due to the mathematical properties of complex-valued quantities, defining parallel transport in non-Hermitian systems in a way that minimizes the distance is challenging, as constructing a positive-definite distance analogous to Eq. (
27
) is difficult. Furthermore, such a definition cannot be directly derived from the in-phase condition commonly used in quantum interferometry experiments
Sjöqvist et al. 2000
. In fact, the precise form of the parallel transport condition does not need to be explicitly defined. We can assume the existence of parallel transport paths analogous to those in Hermitian systems. As in Hermitian regime, the parallel transport evolution
V(t)
is uniquely generated by the connection
\mathscr{A}
on the principal bundles
Nakahara 2003

\displaystyle V(t)=\mathcal{P}e^{\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\mathscr{A^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})},

(74)

which results in an group element of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)
acting on the fibre and
\omega(t)=\omega(0)V(t)
. If we define
s(t)\equiv\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\Psi_{\alpha}}
as a section from
\mathcal{H}_{\varrho}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
, this implies that the initial gauge is given by
\omega(0)=s(t)U_{g}(0),\tilde{\omega}(0)=U_{g}(0)^{-1}\tilde{s}(t)
, and
\tilde{s}(t)=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}|\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|
where
U_{g}(0)=1
. Consequently, the parallel transport specifies
\omega(t)=s(t)V(t)
. As analog to Hermitian systems in Fig.
3
, any initial gauge choice
U_{g}(0)
specifies a section
s(t)U_{g}(t)
and leads to
\omega(t)=s(t)V(t)U_{g}(0)
, that is
\omega(t)=s(t)U_{g}(t)V_{g}(t)
, where
V_{g}(t)=U_{g}^{-1}(t)V(t)U_{g}(0)
. The holonomy group, the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)
, is constituted by the collection of
V(t)
along some closed parallel transport paths
Nakahara 2003
. Even though the path is not closed, the phase

\displaystyle\phi_{g}=\arg\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\omega}(0)\omega(t)\right],

(75)

remains gauge invariant. This phase represents a purely
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)
-invariant geometric phase along any parallel transport path and corresponds to the non-Hermitian Uhlmann phase in the case of a closed path.

Analog to Eq. (
37
), we can define the biorthogonal Loschmidt amplitude for mixed states in non-Hermitian systems as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\varrho(0)U(t)\right],

(76)

where
\varrho(0)
represents the density matrix of the prequench Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
, and
U(t)
is the time evolution operator of the postquench Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{1}
. According to Eqs. (
66
) and (
67
), the time evolution of
\omega,\tilde{\omega}
is given by

\displaystyle\omega(t)

\displaystyle=U(t)\omega(0)U_{g},

(77)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(t)

\displaystyle=U_{g}^{-1}\tilde{\omega}(0)U(t)^{-1}.

(78)

Assuming the identity initial gauge
\varrho(0)=s(0)\tilde{s}(0)
, the biorthogonal mixed-state Loschmidt amplitude can be written

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[s(0)\tilde{s}(0)U(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[U(t)^{-1}s(t)(U(t)^{\dagger})^{-1}\tilde{s}(0)U(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{s}(0)s(t)(U(t)^{\dagger})^{-1}\right],

(79)

which implies that
\arg\mathcal{G}(t)
is
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C},N)
-gauge invariant if
(U(t)^{\dagger})^{-1}=V(t)
corresponding the parallel transport. For generic time evolution, we have

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}(0)\ket{\omega(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|(U^{T})^{-1}U(t)\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}\otimes U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\sqrt{p_{\beta}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|U(t)\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}\otimes\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|(U^{T})^{-1}U^{T}\ket{\Psi_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|U(t)\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\varrho(0)U(t)\right],

(80)

leading to
\mathrm{Tr}\left[\varrho(0)U(t)\right]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\omega}(0)\omega(t)\right]
. If we impose
\mathbb{U}(1)
gauge transformation
\ket{(\omega(t))}\rightarrow\ket{(\omega^{\prime}(t))}=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\ket{(\omega(t))},\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}(0)|\rightarrow\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(0)|=\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}(0)|e^{-i\vartheta(0)}
,

\displaystyle\arg\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(0)\ket{\omega^{\prime}(t)}=\arg\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}(0)\ket{\omega(t)}+[\vartheta(t)-\vartheta(0)].

(81)

To compensating this change, we should introduce the dynamical phase as in Hermitian systems,

\displaystyle\phi_{dyn}=-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}(t)|\hat{H}\ket{\omega(t)},

(82)

such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(t)|\hat{H}\ket{\omega^{\prime}(t)}=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\!\langle\tilde{\omega}(t)|\hat{H}\ket{\omega(t)}-[\vartheta(t)-\vartheta(0)].

(83)

Hence, we obtain the
\mathbb{U}(1)
-invariant geometric phase

\displaystyle\phi=\arg\mathcal{G}(t)-\phi_{dyn}.

(84)

In fact, the non-Hermitian Loschmidt amplitude in Eq. (
76
) simply constitutes a biorthogonal generalization of its Hermitian counterpart in Eq. (
37
). However, the connection between parallel transport and the Loschmidt amplitude is not mandatory. Moreover, the non-Hermitian extension of geometric phases generally lacks a well-defined quantization scheme in principle, thus failing to serve as a reliable indicator for DQPTs. Although certain numerical studies have demonstrated the dynamical order-parameter characteristics of non-Hermitian geometric phases
Zhou et al. 2018
;
Zhou and Du 2021
;
Mondal and Nag 2022
;
Mondal and Nag 2023
;
Jing et al. 2024
, this work will focus exclusively on the fundamental theoretical relationship between Loschmidt amplitudes and DQPTs.

Pure-state Loschmidt amplitude.—

Before proceeding with the biorthogonal mixed-state Loschmidt amplitude in non-Hermitian systems, we first clarify its pure-state counterpart. The initial state of
\hat{H}_{0}
is prepared by

\displaystyle\varrho(0)=\ket{\Psi_{0}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{0}|,

(85)

where
\ket{\Psi_{0}}
(
\langle\!\langle\Psi_{0}|
) is the right (left) ground state. Assuming that we are studying a one-dimensional (1D) non-interacting momentum-space Hamiltonian
H_{0}(k)
with well-defined occupied bands, the right and left ground states are expressed by

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{0}}

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{(n,k)\in occ}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)},

(86)

\displaystyle|\Psi_{0}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{(n,k)\in occ}|u_{n}^{0}(k)\rangle\!\rangle,

(87)

where
n
labels the band index. If we adapt the second manner of non-Hermitian dynamic described above, we arrive at
\varrho(t)=U(t)^{-1}\varrho(0)U(t)
after the quantum quench by
\hat{H}_{1}
, where
U(t)=e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}t}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}

\displaystyle=e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)},

(88)

\displaystyle|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-iH_{1}^{\dagger}(k)t}|u_{n}^{0}(k)\rangle\!\rangle\equiv\ket{\tilde{u}_{n}^{0}(k,t)},

(89)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)|

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|e^{iH_{1}(k)t}\equiv\bra{\tilde{u}_{n}^{0}(k,t)}.

(90)

The biorthogonal Loschmidt amplitude is calculated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\ket{\Psi_{0}}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{0}|e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}t}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\prod_{(n^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in occ}\prod_{k_{0}\in\mathrm{BZ}}\langle\!\langle u_{n^{\prime}}^{0}(k^{\prime})\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|e^{-i\hat{c}_{k_{0}}^{\dagger}H_{1}(k_{0})\hat{c}_{k_{0}}t}\ket{u_{n^{\prime}}^{0}(k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}.

(91)

The Loschmidt echo is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\left|\mathcal{G}(t)\right|^{2}=\mathcal{G}(t)\mathcal{G}(t)^{*}.

(92)

Note that this Loschmidt echo is consistent with that given in Ref.
Jing et al. 2024
, which is based the third manner of non-Hermitian dynamic described above. In Ref.
Jing et al. 2024
, the associated state of

\displaystyle\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}=e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}=\sum_{m}\ket{u_{m}^{0}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{m}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}=\sum_{m}\mathscr{C}_{mn}(k,t)\ket{u_{m}^{0}(k)},

(93)

is defined by

\displaystyle|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}|u_{m}^{0}(k)\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle u_{m}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}=\sum_{m}\mathscr{C}_{mn}(k,t)|u_{m}^{0}(k)\rangle\!\rangle\equiv\ket{\tilde{u}_{n}^{0}(k,t)},

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\langle\!\langle u_{m}^{0}(k)|\mathscr{C}_{mn}^{*}(k,t)\equiv\langle\!\langle\tilde{u}_{n}^{0}(k,t)|.

(94)

In this framework, we remark that

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\sum_{m}\mathscr{C}_{mn}(k,t)\ket{u_{m}^{0}(k)}=\mathscr{C}_{nn}(k,t),

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\langle\!\langle u_{m}^{0}(k)|\mathscr{C}_{mn}^{*}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}=\mathscr{C}^{*}_{nn}(k,t),

(95)

giving

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}=\left(\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k}\right)^{*}.

(96)

Hence, the Loschmidt echo is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k},

(97)

and after normalizing by the denominator
\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\frac{\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k}}{\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t}},

(98)

which precisely corresponds to the form of the Loschmidt echo for the two-band case with the lower occupied band, as presented in Ref.
Jing et al. 2024
.
We emphasize that the results of the Loschmidt amplitude and echo are independent of the choice between the second and third manners of non-Hermitian dynamics, which are up to normalization factors at each time. The advantage of the third manner, along with the biorthogonal formulation in Ref.
Jing et al. 2024
, lies in its normalized and symmetric form of the Loschmidt echo, as presented in Eq. (
98
), which can arrive at the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\frac{\left|\mathscr{C}_{nn}(k,t)\right|^{2}}{\sum_{m}\left|\mathscr{C}_{mn}(k,t)\right|^{2}}.

(99)

Mixed-state Loschmidt amplitude (Fock space formulation).—

A right and left many-body state in Fock space are expressed as Eqs. (
86
) and (
87
), respectively. The trace of an operator
\hat{O}
in the biorthogonal basis is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\hat{O}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\langle\!\langle\Psi_{\alpha}|\hat{O}\ket{\Psi_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k},\tilde{n}\right\}}\bigotimes_{k,n}\langle\!\langle u_{n}(k)|\hat{O}\ket{u_{n}(k)},

(100)

where
\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k},\tilde{n}\right\}}=\sum_{\alpha}
denotes the summation over all possible Fock states (labeled by
\alpha
) with the corresponding occupied momenta
k
and band indices
n
. The initial density matrix can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\varrho(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\left(\bigotimes_{k,n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\right)\left(\bigotimes_{k,n}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k},\tilde{n}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}p_{k_{j}}^{n_{j}}\ket{u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})}\langle\!\langle u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k}\right\}}\sum_{\left\{\tilde{n}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}p_{k_{j}}^{n_{j}}\ket{u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})}\langle\!\langle u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}\sum_{n}p_{k_{j}}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k_{j})}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k_{j})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}\varrho_{k_{j}}(0),

(101)

where
p_{\alpha}=\prod_{j}p_{k_{j}}^{n_{j}}
and
\varrho_{k_{j}}(0)\equiv\sum_{n}p_{k_{j}}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k_{j})}\langle\!\langle u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})|
.
Assuming that all band indices
n
are occupied for fermion systems, we simplify

\displaystyle\varrho(0)

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{k\in\mathrm{BZ}/2}\varrho_{k}(0),\quad\text{half-filled},

(102)

\displaystyle\varrho(0)

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{k\in\mathrm{BZ}}\varrho_{k}(0),\quad\text{full-filled}.

(103)

Consequently, the Loschmidt amplitude is (up to normalization coefficient)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\varrho(0)U(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\bigotimes_{k}\varrho_{k}(0)\prod_{k_{0}}e^{-i\hat{c}_{k_{0}}^{\dagger}H_{1}(k_{0})\hat{c}_{k_{0}}t}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k}(t),

(104)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\varrho_{k}(0)U_{k}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\langle\!\langle u_{n^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})|\left[\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|e^{-i\hat{c}_{k_{0}}^{\dagger}H_{1}(k_{0})\hat{c}_{k_{0}}t}\right]\ket{u_{n^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)|e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)},

(105)

\displaystyle U_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}.

(106)

It can be seen that this formula is the biorthogonal generalization of the mixed-state case for Hermitian systems, as presented in Ref.
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
. The corresponding mixed-state Loschmidt echo is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\prod_{k}\sum_{n}\frac{\left|p_{k}^{n}\right|^{2}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}}{\sum_{n}\left|p_{k}^{n}\right|^{2}\langle\!\langle u_{n}^{0}(k,t)\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}},

(107)

after the biorthogonal normalization. Since the Fisher zeros that signal DQPTs are invariant under normalization in the denominator, the overall normalization factor of the Loschmidt amplitude or echo can be safely omitted.

For example of a two-band model
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)=\vec{h}_{0}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}
, with
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
, we specify the initial state as the generalized Gibbs state with the temperature
T=1/\beta
,

\displaystyle\varrho_{k}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)}\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\left[\beta h_{0}(k)\right]\mathbbm{1}-\sinh\left[\beta h_{0}(k)\right]\hat{h}_{0}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}}{2\cosh\left[\beta h_{0}(k)\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbbm{1}-n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right],

(108)

where
h_{0}(k)=\left(h_{0,x}^{2}(k)+h_{0,y}^{2}(k)+h_{0,z}^{2}(k)\right)^{1/2}
,
\hat{h}_{0}(k)=\vec{h}_{0}(k)/h_{0}(k)
, and
n_{k}=\tanh\left[\beta h_{0}(k)\right]
. In addition, it is explicit that

\displaystyle p_{k}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\beta\epsilon_{\pm}^{0}(k)}}{e^{-\beta h_{0}(k)}+e^{\beta h_{0}(k)}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\mp n_{k}\right),

(109)

where the eigenenergies of two bands are
\epsilon_{k\pm}^{0}=\pm h_{0}(k)=\pm\epsilon_{k}^{0}
.

In detail, the non-Hermitian two-band model generally reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(k)

\displaystyle=h_{x}(k)\sigma_{x}+h_{y}(k)\sigma_{y}+h_{z}(k)\sigma_{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{z}&h_{x}-ih_{y}\\
h_{x}+ih_{y}&-h_{z}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{zr}+ih_{zi}&h_{xr}+ih_{xi}-i(h_{yr}+ih_{yi})\\
h_{xr}+ih_{xi}+i(h_{yr}+ih_{yi})&-(h_{zr}+ih_{zi})\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{zr}+ih_{zi}&h_{xr}+h_{yi}+i(h_{xi}-h_{yr})\\
h_{xr}-h_{yi}+i(h_{xi}+h_{yr})&-(h_{zr}+ih_{zi})\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}G+iF&A+iB\\
C+iD&-(G+iF)\end{matrix}\right),

(110)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=h_{xr}+h_{yi},

(111)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=h_{xi}-h_{yr},

(112)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=h_{xr}-h_{yi},

(113)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=h_{xi}+h_{yr},

(114)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=h_{zr},

(115)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=h_{zi}.

(116)

The energies and corresponding biorthogonal eigenstates are

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{(A+iB)(C+iD)+(G+iF)^{2}},

(117)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\pm}^{*}=\pm\sqrt{(A-iB)(C-iD)+(G-iF)^{2}},

(118)

\displaystyle\ket{u_{\pm}(k)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\pm}\left[\epsilon_{\pm}-(G+iF)\right]}}\left(\begin{matrix}A+iB\\
\epsilon_{\pm}-(G+iF)\end{matrix}\right),

(119)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}(k)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\pm}\left[\epsilon_{\pm}-(G+iF)\right]}}\left(\begin{matrix}C+iD&\epsilon_{\pm}-(G+iF)\end{matrix}\right).

(120)

The biorthogonal property is given by

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}(k)\ket{u_{\pm}(k)}

\displaystyle=1,

(121)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}(k)\ket{u_{\mp}(k)}

\displaystyle=0,

(122)

\displaystyle\ket{u_{+}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{+}(k)|+\ket{u_{-}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{-}(k)|

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1},

(123)

\displaystyle\ket{u_{+}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{+}(k)|-\ket{u_{-}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{-}(k)|

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{H}}(k)

(124)

Quenching from
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
to
\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)=\vec{h}_{1}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}
, the single-particle time evolution operator is given by

\displaystyle U_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)t}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(-i)^{n}\left[h_{1}(k)\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)t\right]^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-i)^{2n}\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]^{2n}}{(2n)!}(\mathscr{P}_{k+}^{1}-\mathscr{P}_{k-}^{1})^{2n}+\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-i)^{2n+1}\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}(\mathscr{P}_{k+}^{1}-\mathscr{P}_{k-}^{1})^{2n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]^{2n}}{(2n)!}+\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{-i(-1)^{n}\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}(\mathscr{P}_{k+}^{1}-\mathscr{P}_{k-}^{1})

\displaystyle=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\mathbbm{1}-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k),

(125)

where
h_{1}(k)=\left(h_{1,x}^{2}(k)+h_{1,y}^{2}(k)+h_{1,z}^{2}(k)\right)^{1/2}=\epsilon_{k}^{1}
,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)=\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)/h_{1}(k)=\mathscr{P}_{k+}^{1}-\mathscr{P}_{k-}^{1}
, and
\mathscr{P}_{k\pm}^{1}=\ket{u_{\pm}^{1}(k)}\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}^{1}(k)|
is the projection operator. We can then obtain the relation

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)\ket{u_{\pm}^{0}(k)}=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}^{1}}\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}^{0}(k)|\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)\ket{u_{\pm}^{0}(k)}=\pm\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}

(126)

after some algebra.
Hence, the Loschmidt amplitude is (up to normalization coefficient)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\langle\!\langle u_{+}^{0}(k)|e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{+}^{0}(k)}+p_{k}^{-}\langle\!\langle u_{-}^{0}(k)|e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{-}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\langle\!\langle u_{+}^{0}(k)|\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\mathbbm{1}-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)\ket{u_{+}^{0}(k)}+p_{k}^{-}\langle\!\langle u_{-}^{0}(k)|\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\mathbbm{1}-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)\ket{u_{-}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\left\{\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\langle\!\langle u_{+}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)\ket{u_{+}^{0}(k)}\right\}+p_{k}^{-}\left\{\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\langle\!\langle u_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)\ket{u_{-}^{0}(k)}\right\}

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\left\{\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}\right\}+p_{k}^{-}\left\{\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]+i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right](p_{k}^{+}+p_{k}^{-})-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right](p_{k}^{+}-p_{k}^{-})\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}

\displaystyle=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]+i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1},

(127)

which is exactly the result in Ref.
Mondal and Nag 2023
. In the zero-temperature limit
\beta\rightarrow\infty
(pure ground state case),
n_{k}=\tanh\left[\beta h_{0}(k)\right]\rightarrow 1
, thus
\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]+i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}
.

Fisher zeros.—

The zeros of
\mathcal{G}(t)
corresponds to the critical points of DQPTs, characterized by critical momenta
k_{c}
and critical times
t_{c}
. Analogous to the Lee-Yang zeros of thermodynamic functions
Yang and Lee 1952
;
Lee and Yang 1952
, the time variable
t
in
\mathcal{G}(t)
are generalized into the complex plane through analytic continuation
t\mapsto z=\tau+it

Heyl et al. 2013
;
Zvyagin 2016
;
Heyl 2018
, yielding the complex function

\displaystyle\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]

\displaystyle=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)(-i)it\right]=\cos\left[ih_{1}(k)it\right]=\cosh\left[h_{1}(k)it\right]\mapsto\cosh\left[h_{1}(k)z\right],

\displaystyle i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]

\displaystyle=i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)(-i)it\right]=-i\sin\left[ih_{1}(k)it\right]=\sinh\left[h_{1}(k)it\right]\mapsto\sinh\left[h_{1}(k)z\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=\cosh\left[h_{1}(k)z\right]+\sinh\left[h_{1}(k)z\right]n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}.

(128)

Consequently, the zeros of
\mathcal{G}_{k}(z)
in the complex plane, known as Fisher zeros, are calculated as

\displaystyle z_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{1}(k)}\tanh^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{1}(k)}\ln\left(\frac{1-\frac{1}{n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{1}(k)}\ln\left(\frac{n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}-1}{n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{1}(k)}\ln\left(e^{i(2n+1)\pi}\frac{1-n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}{1+n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i(2n+1)\pi}{2h_{1}(k)}+\frac{1}{2h_{1}(k)}\ln\left(\frac{1-n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}{1+n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi(2n+1)}{2h_{1}(k)}-\frac{1}{h_{1}(k)}\tanh^{-1}\left(n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}\right),\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(129)

Here, we have used the relation
\tanh^{-1}(x)=\ln\left[(1+x)/(1-x)\right]/2
(the principal value).
By introducing the notation
\mathcal{Q}_{k}=\tanh^{-1}\left(n_{k}\hat{h}_{0}\cdot\hat{h}_{1}\right)
,
a=\pi(n+1/2)
,and decomposing

h_{1}(k)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
into their real and imaginary parts as
h_{1r}=\mathrm{Re}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],h_{1i}=\mathrm{Im}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],\mathcal{Q}_{kr}=\mathrm{Re}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{k}\right],\mathcal{Q}_{ki}=\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{k}\right]
, the Fisher zeros can be simplified to

\displaystyle z_{n}=\frac{1}{|h_{1}(k)^{2}|}\left[(ah_{1i}-h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}_{kr}-h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}_{ki})+i(ah_{1r}-h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}_{ki}+h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}_{kr})\right].

(130)

The physical critical points of DQPTs are determined by the Fisher zeros lying on the imaginary axis, which simultaneously identify the critical momenta

k_{c}
through the condition

\displaystyle ah_{1i}-h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}_{kr}-h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}_{ki}=0.

(131)

This condition implies that the two vectors
\vec{v}_{s}=(\mathcal{Q}_{kr},\mathcal{Q}_{ki}-a)
and
\vec{v}_{h}=(h_{1r},h_{1i})
in the 2D plane are orthogonal, i.e.,
\vec{v}_{s}\cdot\vec{v}_{h}=0
.

III.2
Non-biorthogonal formulation

The Loschmidt amplitude admits an alternative definition via non-biorthogonal density matrices (Eq. (
61
)). Its pure-state formulation

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho(0)U(t)\right]=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\ket{\Psi_{0}}\bra{\Psi_{0}}e^{-i\hat{H}_{1}t}\right],

(132)

actually provides a more direct physical interpretation—the squared modulus characterizes the temporal-evolution return rate of state following a quantum quench. Provide the initial pure state
\ket{\Psi_{0}}=\bigotimes_{(n,k)\in occ}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}
, the Loschmidt amplitude is calculated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\prod_{(n^{\prime},k^{\prime})}\prod_{k_{0}\in BZ}\langle\!\langle u_{n^{\prime}}^{0}(k^{\prime})\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)}e^{-i\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k_{0}}H_{1}(k_{0})\hat{c}_{k_{0}}t}\ket{u_{n^{\prime}}^{0}(k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}|u_{n}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}.

(133)

The above expression is conventionally normalized by the state norms, yielding

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\frac{\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}}{\left|\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\right|\left|\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}\right|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\frac{\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}}{\sqrt{\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k)}}\sqrt{\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}}},

(134)

where the corresponding Loschmidt echo, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\left|\mathcal{G}(t)\right|^{2}=\prod_{(n,k)\in occ}\frac{\left|\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}\right|^{2}}{\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k)}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}},

(135)

is commonly referred to as the self-norm Loschmidt echo
Lu et al. 2025
. Such Loschmidt echo carries a clear physical interpretation: it quantifies the overlap between the time-evolved postquench state and the initial eigenstate, characterizing the return probability following a quantum quench.

In the general mixed-state scenario, the initial density matrix resembles the biorthogonal form (Eq. (
III.1
)), with the key distinction lying in whether a biorthogonal basis is employed for its representation,

\displaystyle\rho(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\left(\bigotimes_{k,n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\right)\left(\bigotimes_{k,n}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k},\tilde{n}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}p_{k_{j}}^{n_{j}}\ket{u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k}\right\}}\sum_{\left\{\tilde{n}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}p_{k_{j}}^{n_{j}}\ket{u_{n_{j}}^{0}(k_{j})}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}\sum_{n}p_{k_{j}}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k_{j})}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{\tilde{k}\right\}}\bigotimes_{j}\rho_{k_{j}}(0),

(136)

where
p_{\alpha}=\prod_{j}p_{k_{j}}^{n_{j}}
and
\rho_{k_{j}}(0)\equiv\sum_{n}p_{k_{j}}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k_{j})}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k_{j})}
. Assuming that all band indices
n
are occupied for fermion systems, the Loschmidt amplitude is (up to a normalization factor)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(0)U(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\bigotimes_{k}\rho_{k}(0)\prod_{k_{0}}e^{-i\hat{c}_{k_{0}}^{\dagger}H_{1}(k_{0})\hat{c}_{k_{0}}t}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k}(t),

(137)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{k}(0)U_{k}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\langle\!\langle u_{n^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})|\left[\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)}e^{-i\hat{c}_{k_{0}}^{\dagger}H_{1}(k_{0})\hat{c}_{k_{0}}t}\right]\ket{u_{n^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\bra{u_{n}^{0}(k)}e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}\ket{u_{n}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{k}^{n}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)},

(138)

\displaystyle U_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-iH_{1}(k)t}.

(139)

The corresponding mixed-state Loschmidt echo is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)=\prod_{k}\sum_{n}\frac{\left|p_{k}^{n}\right|^{2}\left|\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}\right|^{2}}{\sum_{n}\left|p_{k}^{n}\right|^{2}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k)|u_{n}^{0}(k)}\braket{u_{n}^{0}(k,t)|u_{n}^{0}(k,t)}},

(140)

after the self-normalization. Likewise, the overall normalization factor of the Loschmidt amplitude or echo can be safely omitted.

As a further illustration, we consider again the two-band model
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)=\vec{h}_{0}(k)\cdot\vec{\sigma}
. However, in the non-Hermitian context, a non-biorthogonal mixed state such as the Gibbs state—with analytically tractable participation probabilities
p_{k}^{\pm}
—is generally not applicable. This is because the natural extension of statistical mechanics to non-Hermitian systems adopts the biorthogonal rather than the non-biorthogonal framework. The Loschmidt amplitude formally corresponds to

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)t}|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}+p_{k}^{-}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|e^{-i\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)t}|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\mathbbm{1}-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}+p_{k}^{-}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\mathbbm{1}-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{+}\left(\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}\right)+p_{k}^{-}\left(\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right](p_{k}^{+}+p_{k}^{-})-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\left(p_{k}^{+}\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}+p_{k}^{-}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=(p_{k}^{+}+p_{k}^{-})\left\{\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\left(\tilde{p}_{k}^{+}\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}+\tilde{p}_{k}^{-}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=(p_{k}^{+}+p_{k}^{-})\left(\cos\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]-i\sin\left[h_{1}(k)t\right]\braket{P}_{0}\right),

(141)

where we have denoted
\tilde{p}_{k}^{\pm}=p_{k}^{\pm}/(p_{k}^{+}+p_{k}^{-})
and
\braket{P}_{0}=\tilde{p}_{k}^{+}\braket{\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{+}^{0}(k)}+\tilde{p}_{k}^{-}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}
.
Here,
\ket{\tilde{u}_{\pm}^{0}(k)}
are the self-norm right eigenstates of
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
, satisfying
\braket{\tilde{u}_{\pm}^{0}(k)|\tilde{u}_{\pm}^{0}(k)}=1
, which is inconsistent with the biorthogonal relations. Following the derivation in the biorthogonal formulation, we can obtain the non-biorthogonal Fisher zeros

\displaystyle z^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{i\pi(2n+1)}{2h_{1}(k)}+\frac{1}{h_{1}(k)}\tanh^{-1}\braket{P}_{0},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(142)

Again, by introducing the notation
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}=\tanh^{-1}\braket{P}_{0}
,
a=\pi(n+1/2)
, and decomposing

h_{1}(k)
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}
into their real and imaginary parts as
h_{1r}=\mathrm{Re}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],h_{1i}=\mathrm{Im}\left[h_{1}(k)\right],\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr}=\mathrm{Re}\left[\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}\right],\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}=\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{k}\right]
, the Fisher zeros can be simplified to

\displaystyle z^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{1}{|h_{1}(k)^{2}|}\left[(ah_{1i}+h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr}+h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki})+i(ah_{1r}+h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}-h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr})\right].

(143)

The physical critical points of DQPTs are determined by the Fisher zeros lying on the imaginary axis, which simultaneously identify the critical momenta

k_{c}
through the condition

\displaystyle ah_{1i}+h_{1r}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr}+h_{1i}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}=0.

(144)

This condition implies that the two vectors
\vec{v}^{\prime}_{s}=(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{kr},\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{ki}+a)
and
\vec{v}_{h}=(h_{1r},h_{1i})
in the 2D plane are orthogonal, i.e.,
\vec{v}^{\prime}_{s}\cdot\vec{v}_{h}=0
.

IV
Non-Hermitian winding number

Consider a 1D non-Hermitian sublattice (chiral) symmetric two-band Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}(k)=d_{x}\sigma_{x}+d_{y}\sigma_{y}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&d_{x}-id_{y}\\
d_{x}+id_{y}&0\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&A+iB\\
C+iD&0\end{matrix}\right),

(145)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=d_{xr}+d_{yi},

(146)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=d_{xi}-d_{yr},

(147)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=d_{xr}-d_{yi},

(148)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=d_{xi}+d_{yr}.

(149)

The energies and corresponding biorthogonal eigenstates are

\displaystyle E_{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{(A+iB)(C+iD)}\equiv\pm|d|,

(151)

\displaystyle\ket{u_{\pm}(k)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{A+iB}{E_{\pm}}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{d_{x}-id_{y}}{\pm|d|}\\
1\end{matrix}\right),

(152)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle u_{\pm}(k)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{C+iD}{E_{\pm}}&1\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{d_{x}+id_{y}}{\pm|d|}&1\end{matrix}\right).

(153)

The non-Hermitian winding number is given by
Yin et al. 2018

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{BZ}dk\frac{d_{x}\partial_{k}d_{y}-d_{y}\partial_{k}d_{x}}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}},

(154)

and the corresponding Zak phase is derived from the occupied band

\displaystyle\gamma=\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk\frac{\langle\!\langle u_{-}(k)|i\partial_{k}\ket{u_{-}(k)}}{\langle\!\langle u_{-}(k)\ket{u_{-}(k)}}=\nu\pi.

(155)

Defining

\displaystyle Q_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=d_{x}-id_{y}=d_{xr}+d_{yi}+i(d_{xi}-d_{yr}),

(156)

\displaystyle Q_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=d_{x}+id_{y}=d_{xr}-d_{yi}+i(d_{xi}+d_{yr}),

(157)

and two associated winding numbers

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dkQ_{1}(k)^{-1}\partial_{k}Q_{1}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dk\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(\partial_{k}d_{x}-i\partial_{k}d_{y})}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dk\frac{d_{x}\partial_{k}d_{x}+d_{y}\partial_{k}d_{y}+id_{y}\partial_{k}d_{x}-id_{x}\partial_{k}d_{y}}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}},

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dkQ_{2}(k)^{-1}\partial_{k}Q_{2}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dk\frac{(d_{x}-id_{y})(\partial_{k}d_{x}+i\partial_{k}d_{y})}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dk\frac{d_{x}\partial_{k}d_{x}+d_{y}\partial_{k}d_{y}-id_{y}\partial_{k}d_{x}+id_{x}\partial_{k}d_{y}}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}},

(158)

we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}}{2}.

(159)

On the other hand,
\nu
can also be defined as the winding number of the real part of the angle
\phi=\tan^{-1}\frac{d_{y}}{d_{x}}
, satisfying

\displaystyle-i\frac{e^{2i\phi}-1}{e^{2i\phi}+1}=-i\frac{e^{i\phi}-e^{-i\phi}}{e^{i\phi}+e^{-i\phi}}=\frac{\sin\phi}{\cos\phi}=\tan\phi=\frac{d_{y}}{d_{x}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow e^{2i\phi}=\frac{1+i\tan\phi}{1-i\tan\phi}=\frac{1+i\frac{d_{y}}{d_{x}}}{1-i\frac{d_{y}}{d_{x}}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\phi=\frac{1}{2i}\ln\frac{d_{x}+id_{y}}{d_{x}-id_{y}},

(160)

thus,

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint dk\partial_{k}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\oint dk\partial_{k}\ln\frac{d_{x}+id_{y}}{d_{x}-id_{y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\oint dk\frac{d_{x}-id_{y}}{d_{x}+id_{y}}\partial_{k}\frac{d_{x}+id_{y}}{d_{x}-id_{y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\oint dk\frac{d_{x}-id_{y}}{d_{x}+id_{y}}\left[\frac{\partial_{k}d_{x}+i\partial_{k}d_{y}}{d_{x}-id_{y}}-\frac{d_{x}+id_{y}}{(d_{x}-id_{y})^{2}}(\partial_{k}d_{x}-i\partial_{k}d_{y})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\oint dk\left[\frac{\partial_{k}d_{x}+i\partial_{k}d_{y}}{d_{x}+id_{y}}-\frac{\partial_{k}d_{x}-i\partial_{k}d_{y}}{d_{x}-id_{y}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\oint dk\left[\frac{(\partial_{k}d_{x}+i\partial_{k}d_{y})(d_{x}-id_{y})}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}-\frac{(\partial_{k}d_{x}-i\partial_{k}d_{y})(d_{x}+id_{y})}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\oint dk\frac{(d_{x}\partial_{k}d_{x}+d_{y}\partial_{k}d_{y}+id_{x}\partial_{k}d_{y}-id_{y}\partial_{k}d_{x})-(d_{x}\partial_{k}d_{x}+d_{y}\partial_{k}d_{y}-id_{x}\partial_{k}d_{y}+id_{y}\partial_{k}d_{x})}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint dk\frac{d_{x}\partial_{k}d_{y}-d_{y}\partial_{k}d_{x}}{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}.

(161)

Remarkably, only the real part
\phi_{r}
of
\phi=\phi_{r}+i\phi_{i}
contributes to the winding number.
Further,
\nu_{1}
is the winding number of the angle
\tan^{-1}\frac{d_{xi}-d_{yr}}{d_{xr}+d_{yi}}\equiv\phi_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
is the winding number of the angle
\tan^{-1}\frac{d_{xi}+d_{yr}}{d_{xr}-d_{yi}}\equiv\phi_{2}
, we also obtain

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}}{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint dk\frac{\partial_{k}(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})}{2},

(162)

and thus,
2\phi_{r}=\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}+2n\pi,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Noteworthily, the above non-Hermitian winding number formulas are the natural generalization of that in Hermitian systems.

V
Non-biorthogonal DQPTs with winding topology

Quenching from a chiral symmetric non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
to a Hermitian one
\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)
corresponding to real
h_{1}(k)
, the critical-
k_{c}
condition with ground-state like participation probabilities
p_{k}^{-}=1,p_{k}^{+}=0
reduces to
\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}=0
. Here, we will denote the parameters and energy of
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
as
A_{0},B_{0},C_{0},D_{0}
and
E_{\pm}^{0}
, and those with
\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)
as real-value
d_{x},d_{y}
and
\pm|d|
.
Substituting the eigenstates
\ket{u_{\pm}^{0}(k)}
into this condition, we obtain

\displaystyle\braket{u_{-}^{0}(k)|u_{-}^{0}(k)}\braket{\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|\tilde{u}_{-}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=\braket{u_{-}^{0}(k)|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(k)|u_{-}^{0}(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|d|}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{A_{0}-iB_{0}}{E_{-}^{0*}}&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&d_{x}-id_{y}\\
d_{x}+id_{y}&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{A_{0}+iB_{0}}{E_{-}^{0}}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|d|}\left(\begin{matrix}d_{x}+id_{y}&\frac{(d_{x}-id_{y})(A_{0}-iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0*}}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{A_{0}+iB_{0}}{E_{-}^{0}}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|d|}\left[\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+\frac{(d_{x}-id_{y})(A_{0}-iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0*}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4|d|^{2}}\left[2|d|\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+2|d|\frac{(d_{x}-id_{y})(A_{0}-iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0*}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4|d|^{2}}\Bigg[\left(\frac{(d_{x}-id_{y})(A_{0}-iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0*}}+|d|\right)\left(\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+|d|\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(-\frac{(d_{x}-id_{y})(A_{0}-iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0*}}+|d|\right)\left(-\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+|d|\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4|d|^{2}}\left[\left|\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+|d|\right|^{2}-\left|-\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+|d|\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left|\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{|d|E_{-}^{0}}+1\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left|-\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{|d|E_{-}^{0}}+1\right|^{2},

(163)

which leads to the critical-
k_{c}
condition

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{|d|E_{-}^{0}}+1\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{(d_{x}+id_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{|d|E_{-}^{0}}+1\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+1\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{(\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y})(A_{0}+iB_{0})}{E_{-}^{0}}+1\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y})+\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}}{\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{(\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y})+\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}}{\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}}\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\left|(\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y})+\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|-(\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y})+\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}\right|^{2}.

(164)

Assuming no EPs with
\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{(A_{0}+iB_{0})(C_{0}+iD_{0})}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{sgn}({s_{0}})\sqrt{\frac{C_{0}+iD_{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{sgn}({s_{0}})\sqrt{\frac{(C_{0}^{2}+D_{0}^{2})^{1/2}e^{i\tan^{-1}\frac{D_{0}}{C_{0}}}}{(A_{0}^{2}+B_{0}^{2})^{1/2}e^{i\tan^{-1}\frac{B_{0}}{A_{0}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{sgn}({s_{0}})\mathcal{R}e^{\frac{i}{2}\left(\tan^{-1}\frac{D_{0}}{C_{0}}-\tan^{-1}\frac{B_{0}}{A_{0}}\right)},

(165)

where
\mathrm{sgn}(s_{0})
denotes the possible sign caused by the square root and
\mathcal{R}=\left(\frac{C_{0}^{2}+D_{0}^{2}}{A_{0}^{2}+B_{0}^{2}}\right)^{1/4}
. Note that

\displaystyle\tan^{-1}\frac{B_{0}}{A_{0}}

\displaystyle=\tan^{-1}\frac{d_{xi}^{0}-d_{yr}^{0}}{d_{xr}^{0}+d_{yi}^{0}}=\phi_{1}^{0},

\displaystyle\tan^{-1}\frac{D_{0}}{C_{0}}

\displaystyle=\tan^{-1}\frac{d_{xi}^{0}+d_{yr}^{0}}{d_{xr}^{0}-d_{yi}^{0}}=\phi_{2}^{0},

(166)

according to Sec.
IV
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{E_{-}^{0}}{A_{0}+iB_{0}}=-\mathrm{sgn}({s_{0}})\mathcal{R}e^{i\frac{\phi_{2}^{0}-\phi_{1}^{0}}{2}}=-\mathrm{sgn}({s_{0}})\mathcal{R}e^{i\phi^{0}}.

(167)

The winding number of the prequench
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
is given by

\displaystyle\nu^{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint dk\partial_{k}\phi^{0}.

(168)

Moreover, the winding number of the postquench Hermitian
\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)
is given by

\displaystyle\nu^{1}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dk\hat{d}^{-1}\partial_{k}\hat{d},

(169)

where
\hat{d}=\hat{d}_{x}+i\hat{d}_{y}
.
If we regard
\hat{d}
and
-\mathrm{sgn}({s_{0}})\mathcal{R}e^{i\phi^{0}}
in the complex plane as two vectors in the corresponding 2D plane, the critical-
k_{c}
condition in Eq. (
V
) implies they are orthogonal to each other. Therefore, when the angle difference
\Delta\phi=\phi^{1}-\phi^{0}
covers
n\pi/2
(with odd
n
), the DQPTs must occur. Since the winding numbers of
\mathcal{H}_{0}(k)
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}(k)
are quantized as half-integer and integer, respectively, the winding number difference
\Delta\nu=\nu^{1}-\nu^{0}=\mathbb{Z}/2
is the sufficient condition of the occurrence of DQPTs.

VI
Dissipation-controlled quantum quench in the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model

In theoretical practice, an effective non-Hermitian quantum Hamiltonian can be derived from the Lindblad master equation of an open quantum system, which governs the time evolution of the density matrix
Song et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Xue et al. 2022

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}=-i\left[H,\rho\right]+\sum_{\mu}\left(2L_{\mu}\rho L_{\mu}^{\dagger}-\left\{L_{\mu}^{\dagger}L_{\mu},\rho\right\}\right),

(170)

where
H
is a Hermitian Hamiltonian, and
L_{\mu}
’s are the Lindblad operators describing quantum jumps induced by the coupling to the environment. This equation exhibits a non-unitary, short-time evolution described by

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}=-i\left(H_{eff}\rho-\rho H_{eff}^{\dagger}\right),

(171)

which is governed effectively by the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{eff}=H-i\sum_{\mu}L_{\mu}^{\dagger}L_{\mu}
prior to the occurrence of quantum jump
2L_{\mu}\rho L_{\mu}^{\dagger}
[Fig.
4
(a)]. Obviously, Eq. (
171
) describes the non-biorthogonal density matrix governed by non-unitary time evolution, which aligns with the no-jump limit of the approximate Lindblad master equation
Sim et al. 2023
;
Sim et al. 2025
.

Specifically, we apply the above Lindblad process to the Hermitian Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model by introducing flux-controlled Lindblad operators (dissipation), which governs the intra- and inter-unit cell hoppings
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
between sublattices A and B, as illustrated in Fig.
4
(b). The Lindblad operators are give by
L_{n}=\sqrt{\gamma}(c_{nA}+e^{i\phi}c_{nB})
with the effective flux
\phi
. The effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{eff}
exhibits the non-reciprocal intra-unit cell hoppings
t_{1}\mp i\gamma e^{\pm i\phi}
from the sublattice B (A) toward A (B), despite the presence of irrelevant onsite energy terms. Particularly, setting the flux
\phi=\pi/2
yields the standard non-Hermitian SSH model discussed in the main text. Thus, by controlling the dissipation through the Lindblad operators, i.e., suddenly changing their parameters, quantum quenching in the non-Hermitian SSH model can be realized. Importantly, abruptly turning off the parameter
\gamma
leads to DQPTs with topological characteristics, quenching from a non-Hermitian to a Hermitian system.

Figure 4:
Schematic diagram of dissipation-controlled quantum quench. (a) The effective short-time evolution governed by
H_{eff}
before the quantum jump
2L_{\mu}\rho L_{\mu}^{\dagger}
, derived from the Lindblad master equation. (b) The flux-controlled Lindblad operators realizing the quantum quench in the non-Hermitian SSH model.

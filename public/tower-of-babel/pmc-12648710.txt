Ayurvedic management for Epidermolysis bullosa - A case report
Authors: KM Pratap Shankar, Midhuna Mohan K
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEpidermolysis bullosa (EB) is an inherited or auto-immunity-mediated, heterogeneous group of rare genetic dermatoses characterized by fragile skin and mucosae with blister formation induced by minimal trauma. Junctional Epidermolysis Bullosa (JEB) is an autosomal recessive inherited subtype of EB with higher chances of malnutrition and nail involvement. Considering the key factors such as genetic disposition, chronicity, pathology, involvement of deeper tissues, and complexities, EB may be considered as Kustha (spectrum of diseases of the integumentary system) from an Ayurvedic perspective. A 34-year-old male patient diagnosed with JEB presented with a history of persistent non-healing ulcers, recurrent blisters, and bullae, usually occurring after minor trauma since the age of three months. The ulcers, scars, and lesions were widespread, covering approximately two-thirds of the body. After a thorough understanding of the Ayurvedic pathology of the disease, Shamana (pacifying therapy) and Shodhana (purificatory therapy) procedures were administered. Later, raktamoshana (Blood-letting through venesection and jalaukavacharana) was also employed. During the treatment and follow-up period, the episodes of new blister formations drastically reduced, and the ulcer formed from occasional blisters healed quickly without crusting. There was a considerable reduction in wound surface area. The patient's personal and social interactions improved. Quality of Life assessed through the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) showed improvement. The present case of JEB was effectively managed using various Ayurvedic treatment modalities. Understanding the Ayurvedic pathology and tailoring treatment strategies can yield better results in chronic, debilitating autoimmune dermatological conditions such as EB.Keywords: Ayurveda, Epidermolysis bullosa, Panchakarma, Kustha, Siravyadana, Jalaukavacharana

Introduction

Epidermolysis Bullosa (EB) is a term used to indicate a group of rare, bullous, chronic skin conditions that may either be inherited or be autoimmune-mediated [1]. The main features in patients with this condition include fragile skin & mucosae and blisters over the body [2]. This disease has four major subtypes that are inherited, and there is also an autoimmune subtype. Junctional Epidermolysis Bullosa (JEB) is an inherited subtype with a high risk of malnutrition and nail involvement [1]. The current treatment strategies don't guarantee a cure; instead, they only focus on managing wounds and pain, and treatment options, including gene therapy, are under research [3]. Ayurveda considers the spectrum of diseases of the integumentary system as Kushta roga. Skin will be affected in the initial stages of Kustha, and as time progresses or when untreated, they affect the deeper dhatu (∼ body tissues) [4].

Patient information

A 34-year-old non-diabetic male patient presented to the OPD with a history of persistent non-healing ulcers, recurrent blisters, and bullae, usually occurring after minor friction or trauma since the age of three months. The ulcers, scars, and lesions were widespread and covered approximately two-thirds of the body. A skin biopsy done in March 2003 confirmed the case as JEB. Subsequently, he was treated with Dapsone (100 mg OD) and was educated to deflate the blisters and care for wounds. The patient also had occasional pain and itching over the lesions. There was second-degree consanguinity among parents. His siblings were normal, and there was no relevant family history. His development was relatively normal. As the patient was not improving considerably through modern medicine, he came for Ayurvedic management.

Clinical findings

Numerous widespread persistent non-healing ulcers, evident as generalized deeper erosions, crusts, scars, flaccid blisters, granulation tissue following the blistering, and hyperpigmented patches were present all over the body. History revealed that the blisters were healing with scarring. It was observed that the ulcerations had mild serosanguinous discharge with no odour. The perilesional skin was erythematous and fragile. There was no ocular involvement, oral ulcers, or difficulty in swallowing. Mitten hands (webbing of digits) sign was absent. There was least mucosal involvement and minimal milia formation. The nails of the toes were dystrophic. There were no clinical signs of pallor, cyanosis, icterus, clubbing, or oedema. He was underweight (BMI - 18.4 kg/m2), and systemic examinations were normal, except for the integumentary system.

Diagnostic assessment

Based on the clinical evaluation, the patient was diagnosed with JEB (ICD-10-CM Q81.8), and that was confirmed by the biopsy done in 2003. While discussing the Ayurvedic diagnosis, taking into account the involvement of skin and blister formation, the prime considerations for the differential diagnosis of this condition are Kushta and Visarpa. In both these conditions, the same Saptadhatu is involved [5]. (Chikitsasthana/Chapter 7/Verse 9 & Chikitsasthana/Chapter 21/Verse 15) In Visarpa, as rakta and pitta are more dominant, it is acute, while Kushta is usually chronic and gradual in progression [6]. (Chikitsasthana/Chapter 18/Verse 37 & Nidanasthana/Chapter 13/Verse 45) Taking these points into consideration, the present case was diagnosed and treated as Kushta. Here, in this case, there is involvement of all three doshas (the three humours – vata, pitta, kapha) and the condition is of inherited origin (Adibalapravrutha) as observed in Kushta. Though blister formation is present in both Kushta and Visarpa, the lesions of Kushta last for a long time without proper healing, which is seen in the present case. Further, the case may be considered as Maha Kushta due to the involvement of deeper dhatus. Kushta is categorized into seven types, eighteen types, or even countless variations. The manifestation of various skin diseases is influenced by the complex interaction of several pathological factors, including Dosha, Dathu, Agni (metabolism), and srotas (micro-channels of circulation), etc. [5] (Chikitsasthana/Chapter 7/Verse 12, Chikitsasthana/Chapter 21/Verse 11 & Nidanasthana/Chapter 5/Verse 7). Involvement of the nail is noted in the present case, which is seen in Kushta affecting the deeper Asthi dhatu. This feature suggests a poor prognosis and difficulty in treating the condition.

The treatments also aimed to reduce pain, recurrence of blisters and ulceration cost-effectively. The patient was diagnosed as Kushta with tridosha involvement. Follow-up and outcomes

After discharge, the patient is on regular follow-up till March 2025. During the follow-up period, the episodes of new blister formations drastically reduced, and the ulcer formed from occasional blisters healed quickly without crusting. There was a considerable reduction in wound surface area on the anterior and posterior trunk areas. An appreciable reduction was also noted in the limbs (Fig. 1). The patient had an improved quality of life (QoL). His personal and social interactions improved. His QoL was assessed using the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI). The DLQI score before initiating Ayurvedic treatments was 26 points (extremely large effect on patient's' life), and on April 2024, the score was 6 points (moderate effect on patient's life) and was sustained till March 2025 [7]. The CRP levels were 49 mg/L (November 25, 2023) and 68.6 mg/L (November 29, 2023) at the time of admission, which got reduced as 9.5 mg/L (07.12.2024) at the time of discharge. During the time of follow-up, the patient's CRP level and Haemoglobin were normal. His appetite improved, and his BMI was 20 kg/m2 (normal). He was advised to continue Dapsone 100mg on alternate days.

Discussion

The present case of JEB was effectively managed using various Ayurvedic treatment modalities. The Ayurvedic treatment strategies were planned after understanding the disease pathology. The basic course of Ayurvedic pathologies, such as Dosha Dhatu vinischaya (proper understanding of vitiation of dosha & dhatu) and Shadkriya kala (six stages of progress of a disease), helped the accurate Ayurvedic diagnosis of the case, and hence, its effective management was tailored as per the pathology stages. Similar to conventional treatments, continued treatment with frequent and prolonged hospital stays might also be a challenge in the management of EB through Ayurveda. However, this case report will be inceptive for adopting alternative therapies like Ayurveda in managing chronic debilitating immune dermatological conditions like EB.

JEB is an autosomal recessive inherited skin disease with a high risk of development of squamous cell cancer. Patients with JEB may suffer from infections of ulcerations following the blisters, loss of body fluid from the sites of ulceration, and impairment in body weight. Due to the presence of ulcerations, the chance of secondary infections is very high, which may even become life-threatening [[8], [9], [10]]. In addition to the poor quality of life of the patient, this disease poses a burden on the family as well, including an economic burden [11]. The general treatment options for EB include Dapsone (4,4′-diaminodiphenylsulfone, DDS) [12,13]. There are several side effects reported with the use of dapsone, including life-threatening dapsone hypersensitivity syndrome, and Methemoglobinemia [14].

As JEB is an inherited disease, a radical cure is impossible. Inherited diseases may be considered under Sahaja/Adibalapravruttavyadhi (Inherited condition) of Ayurveda classical texts, and Kushta is a disease mentioned under Sahaja/Adibalapravruttavyadhi, showing that there can be involvement of faulty male and female gametes in the formation of the disease, and also indicating the difficulty in getting cured [15].

While analyzing the present case, the disease is tridoshaja in nature with Kapha Pitta predominance, but treating the patient directly with Kapha-Pitta alleviating medicines may not give good results. The chronicity of the disease and the presence of Ama (toxic, proinflammatory waste-product of improper digestion), Pratilomata (upward movement of vitiated vata dosha), and Manda Agni (reduced appetite) are important considerations while finalizing the treatment. As it is a Sahaja vyadhi, it is difficult to get a complete cure. However, the corrections at various stages of pathology, including the functioning of Dhatvagni (tissue level metabolism), may give better results. The treatment strategies planned to target the above scenarios helped to better the symptoms and improve the QoL of the patient.

Snehapana (oleation) is the first line of treatment for all types of Kustha [6]. (Chikitsathana/Chapter19/Verse 1) As the patient had Manda Agni, avara jarana shakti (poor digestion), and apakwa sphota (widespread blisters), he was initially given jwarahara (drugs which are used to treat the syndrome of symptoms complementary to febrile stage or inflammatory stage), deepana-pachana (digestion and metabolism-enhancing drugs) and tikta rasa (bitter taste) medicines such as Amruthotharam Kashayam, Sudarshanam Gulika, and Arogyavardini vati [[16], [17], [18]]. The medicines remove ama visha, improve digestion, and clear srotas. In addition to these drugs, Tiktaka Kashayam was also added, as the medicine is indicated for the management of Kushta especially caused by pittaja dosha (dosha responsible for regulating body temperature and metabolic activities) and various types of skin lesions like parisarpa (blisters), visphota (bullae) and vrana (ulcers). Later, as the agni improved, Tiktaka Ghritam was administered as Samana snehapanam (curative oleation) until admission [6]. (Chikitsathana/Chapter 19/Verse 2–6).

On admission, Vilwadi Gulika and Amrita shadangam paneeyam were prescribed to improve the agni and to prepare the body of Shodanartha Sneha panam (pre-purificatory oleation) [5]. (Uttarasthana/Chapter 36/Verse 84–85 & Chikitsathana/Chapter 1/Verse 15) Shodanartha Sneha panam was done with Tiktaka Ghritam followed by Virechanam (therapeutic purgation) with Avipathi Choornam and Triphala Kashayam. Avipathi choorna is Pitta-Kapha samana, anulomana and Rasa-Rakta prasadana [6]. (Kalpasiddhisthana/Chapter 2/Verse 21–23) Triphala Kashaya promotes wound healing, reduces discharge, and cures diseases of vitiated blood [6]. (Sutrasthana/Chapter 6/Verse 159).

Siravyadana (therapeutic venesection) was done on the fifth day after purgation. Siravyadana is indicated for all pitta kapha dushtaraktajavyadhis (∼diseases due to vitiated blood), such as Kustha [6]. (Sutrasthana/Chapter 27/Verse 2) The procedure is specifically indicated to be done in disease conditions that are generalized [6]. (Sutrasthana/Chapter 26/Verse 53) Siravyadana was done on the prominent vein at the cubital fossa, and 80 ml of blood was removed. Siravyadana improves cutaneous circulation and provides the required nutrition to the skin, which helps regenerate fragile skin and thus prevents tearing and blister formation. Therapeutic venesection has long been the treatment of choice in most patients with Porphyria cutanea tarda (PCT). PCT is characterized by chronic blistering skin manifestations with increased skin fragility with bullae and erosions. Skin blistering was the first sign to disappear, followed by skin fragility in patients with PCT [19]. Siravyadana is a proven treatment for many common skin diseases and for improving wound healing [20,21].

After completing the shodhana procedures, the patient was discharged on 13th December 2023. At discharge, the patient was advised to take internal medicines (Table 2), and Siravyadana (80 ml of blood was let out in each visit) was repeated every fortnight till April 2024. Later, he underwent Jalaukavacharana (Leech application) every fortnight till March 2025. The procedure was shifted to Jalaukavacharana as the lesions were localised to limbs. Jalaukavacharana is indicated in pitta predominant vyadhi and kustha. Leech saliva is enriched with multiple compounds and enzymes such as anti-inflammatory, analgesic, antibiotic, anticoagulant, and antiplatelet aggregation compounds. Anastatin, a protein in the leech saliva, inhibits tissue kallikreins and slows down tissue destruction and inflammation. Further, Eglin C, another compound in the leech saliva, blocks the activity of neutrophil elastase and cathepsin G, preventing tissue destruction [22]. The antimicrobial activity of leech saliva by destabilase and hyaluronidase prevents infections of the fragile skin and, further, as leech application induces granulation tissue formation, it helps in wound healing with less or no scarring [23]. During the entire treatment period, the patient didn't have any nosocomial infections or any ADR/AE/SAEs.

Conclusion

Epidermolysis bullosa is a disease that makes the life of the affected person miserable. Modern medicine has limitations in the management of this disease. In the current study, the Ayurvedic management of a case of Junctional Epidermolysis Bullosa, which may be considered Yapya, is described. Even if the disease is not curable completely, Ayurvedic management may offer a promising strategy for managing epidermolysis bullosa. Further clinical trials may help cast light on the efficacy of Ayurveda management in EB.

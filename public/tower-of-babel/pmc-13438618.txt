Mechanistic Insights into Li+, Bi3+ Co-doped BaCeO3: Eu3+ phosphors - defect passivation and enhanced charge-transfer excited emission
Authors: S. V Jasira, C. K. Shilpa, S. S. Ancy, Ann Mary Augustine, A. V. Arunima, Deepthi Panoth, A. M. Huda Thasneem, S. V. Sajith, Pandurengan Sakthivel, Mahesh Dhonde, K. M. Nissamudeen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BaCeO3 perovskites doped with Eu3+ ions exhibit strong charge-transfer (CT)-excited orange–red emission but suffer from oxygen-vacancy-induced non-radiative losses. In this study, Li+ and Bi3+ were introduced as aliovalent co-dopants to modulate defect chemistry and local symmetry in BaCeO3: Eu3+ (BCE). A compositional series BaCe0.96−xEu0.04LixBixO3 (x = 0–0.04) was synthesized via gel combustion method. Structural refinements confirmed single-phase orthorhombic (Pnma) perovskite with systematic lattice expansion upon co-doping. Co-doping suppressed oxygen-vacancy-related defect states, as evidenced by decreased Urbach energy (0.664 → 0.621 eV) and narrowed O 1s defect bands in XPS. Under 321 nm excitation, corresponding to the O²⁻ → Ce⁴+ CT transition, photoluminescence (PL) intensity increased nearly two-fold (at x = 0.02) relative to BCE. Lifetime analysis revealed longer average decay (1.02 → 1.37 ms) and improved radiative fraction. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) rose from 38% to 51%. The enhancement originates from charge-compensated defect passivation and Bi3+-mediated CT coupling. These findings provide mechanistic insight into defect–structure–emission correlations and establish co-doped BaCeO3 as a robust 321 nm-excitable phosphor platform.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55393-0.

Introduction

Perovskite oxides with the general formula ABO₃ have emerged as versatile functional materials for energy conversion, storage, catalysis, and optoelectronic applications due to their tunable crystal structure and defect-tolerant electronic configuration1–5. Among these, simple oxide perovskite, barium cerate (BaCeO3, BC) has received sustainable attention as a promising multifunctional host material owing to its stable orthorhombic structure, excellent thermal and chemical robustness, nontoxicity, moisture resistance, and broad charge transfer band (CTB) in the near UV-blue region6.

BC-based perovskite materials are recognized as the benchmark materials for mixed ionic conduction, encompassing both oxygen and protonic conductivity7–9. Substitution of Ce4+ ions by various lanthanides (La10, Sm11, Y12, Pr13, Nd14, Yb15, Gd16, Tb17 or other elements (Zr18, Mn8, etc.) enhances its conductivity largely through improvements in their ionic transport and structural characteristics. Lately, reports have indicated that these materials also exhibit photoluminescence upon incorporation of suitable dopant ions. Specifically, europium ions (Eu³+) - activated BaCeO₃ displays intense red emission originating from the ⁵D₀ → ⁷FJ (J = 1–4) transitions of Eu³+, sensitized through charge-transfer (CT) excitation between O²− and Ce⁴+19,20. However, the luminescence efficiency of BaCeO₃:Eu³+ is often hindered by intrinsic oxygen vacancies (Vo●●)21 and charge imbalance resulting from aliovalent substitution, which generate non-radiative centers and distort the local crystal field around Eu³+22.

Recent studies have demonstrated that co-doping strategies involving aliovalent ions can effectively tune defect equilibria, modulate lattice strain, and improve charge-transfer dynamics in perovskite and oxide hosts23. Incorporation of Li+ ions has been reported to passivate oxygen vacancies and enhance local crystallinity through charge compensation24, while Bi³+ ions can introduce intermediate states that promote efficient host-to-activator energy transfer25. Meanwhile, simultaneous co-doping with Li+ and Bi³+ ions induces charge-compensation and lattice-defect modification that boost Eu³+ emission intensities26. However, systematic investigations into the mechanistic roles of such co-doping in BaCeO₃-based phosphors remain limited.

In this context, the present work explores the mechanistic correlation between co-doping, defect chemistry, and photophysical enhancement in BaCe0.96−xEu0.04LixBixO3 (x = 0–0.04) synthesized via a gel-combustion route. Structural and spectroscopic analyses are employed to elucidate how Li+/Bi³+ incorporation influences lattice parameters, defect density, and charge-transfer processes. The study demonstrates that co-doping induces lattice relaxation, reduces Urbach energy, suppresses oxygen-vacancy-related states, and enhances CT-mediated emission efficiency. The findings offer comprehensive insight into defect passivation and local structural modulation mechanisms that govern luminescence in aliovalently doped BaCeO₃ systems, advancing their potential as robust phosphors for near-UV-excited white LEDs.

Materials and methods

The phosphor materials BaCe0.96−xEu0.04LixBixO3 (x = 0–0.04), were prepared via a fuel-assisted gel combustion route, chosen for its efficiency in achieving homogeneous distribution of elements and rapid crystallization. The Eu³+ activator concentration was optimized by preparing a series of BaCeO₃:xEu³+ samples with varying Eu³+ content13. The emission intensity increased with increasing Eu³+ concentration up to 4 mol%, beyond which concentration quenching was observed. Therefore, 4 mol% Eu³+ was selected as the optimum activator concentration for this experiments. Stoichiometric amounts of high-purity oxide and carbonate precursors - BaCO3, CeO2, Eu2O3, Bi2O3, Li2CO3 - were converted into nitrates by dissolution in dilute nitric acid under continuous stirring at 373 K. Citric acid acted as an organic fuel with molar ratio of 1:2 with cations, while a controlled quantity of ammonium nitrate served as an auxiliary oxidizer. The resulting solution was continuously stirred under mild heating until a polymeric gel was obtained through metal–citrate complexation.

Gradual dehydration of the gel triggered a self-sustained combustion reaction, producing a voluminous, foamy precursor ash. The combusted mass was then calcined at 1223 K for 4 h to remove residual organics and induce crystallization of the perovskite lattice. The resulting powders were finely ground and used directly for characterization without further purification.

The phase composition was confirmed by a Rigaku SmartLab X-ray diffractometer equipped with a Cu Kα radiation source, and the diffraction data was refined using FullProf Suite software. The surface morphology and elemental distribution were analyzed using Carl Zeiss Sigma 03–81 Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (FE-SEM) integrated with energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). Average particle size and interplanar spacing (d) were determined using a JOEL Japan JEM-2100 plus transmission electron microscope (TEM) with high resolution. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) using Al Kα radiation was used to examine the oxidation states of constituent elements. Optical absorption spectra were recorded in the UV–visible range to estimate the bandgap using JASCO V-660 UV-visible spectrophotometer, and photoluminescence measurements were conducted using a Horiba Fluorolog FL3C-21 to evaluate excitation–emission characteristics. The EQE measurements were carried out using an Edinburgh FLS1000 spectrofluorometer equipped with an integrating sphere attachment. The CIE 1931 Color mixing and photometry software used to evaluate color properties from emission spectra. Room temperature lifetime analysis was carried out using a time resolved mode of Hitachi F-7100 Spectrofluorometer.

Results and Discussion

Phase formation and structural analysis

Figure 1(a) shows the XRD patterns of BaCeO3: Eu3+ and Li+, Bi3+ co-doped samples. All diffractions index to orthorhombic BaCeO3 (Pnma) without impurity peaks, closely matched with the reference ICDD card no: 01–071–6749. The absence of impurity peaks or secondary phases confirms the successful incorporation of dopants and co-dopants within the perovskite lattice. A slight shift of diffraction peaks toward lower 2θ values with increasing co-dopant concentration indicates a minor lattice expansion, consistent with the substitution of larger Eu³+ (0.947 Å) and Bi³+ (1.03 Å) ions for Ce⁴+ (0.87 Å), partially offset by the smaller Li+ (0.76 Å) incorporated at interstitial or A-sites27,28. This combined aliovalent substitution results in subtle structural relaxation and improved crystallinity. Different crystallite parameters derived from XRD analysis were displayed in Table 1.

Based on ionic radii and reported perovskite substitution behavior, Bi³+ ions are expected to occupy Ce⁴+ sites within the B-site octahedral framework, owing to their comparable coordination preference and the stability of Bi–O octahedra observed in analogous systems like BaBiO₃29. In contrast, the much smaller Li+ ion is unlikely to replace Ba²+ at the A-site directly due to its significant size mismatch; instead, Li+ is more likely to resides in partially distorted A-site cavities24,30. This configuration allows local charge compensation for the aliovalent substitution of Eu³+/Bi³+ for Ce⁴+, thereby reducing the need for oxygen vacancies. The resultant lattice relaxation and defect passivation are consistent with the observed X-ray diffraction peak shifts, reduced Urbach energy, and enhanced photoluminescence intensity.

For singly doped BaCeO₃ systems (e.g., Sm³+, Eu³+, Pr³+), a moderate increase in crystallite size and unit-cell volume (V) has also been reported. This arises because substitution of Ce⁴+ by slightly larger RE³+ ions introduces limited lattice distortion, which, upon calcination and phase stabilization, leads to partial relaxation and grain coarsening. However, in the present Bi³+/Li+ co-doped system, the structural modification is more pronounced due to charge compensation between Bi³+ (Ce⁴+ site) and Li+ (interstitial), which is expected to suppress oxygen vacancy formation35. Morphology and microstructure

FESEM micrographs (Fig. 2 (a, b)) show that the Eu³+-doped BaCeO₃ sample exhibits aggregated, submicron-sized grains with a relatively rough and porous surface. Pores originate from the grain boundary diffusion while oxygen vacancies are formed to compensate the charge imbalance caused by Eu3+ replacing Ce4+24,25. Upon Li+ and Bi³+ co-doping, the surface morphology becomes noticeably smoother and more compact, indicating enhanced sintering and reduced porosity, Fig. 2(c, d). The co-doped samples display densely packed grains with well-fused boundaries, suggesting that aliovalent substitution aids in charge-balanced diffusion and suppresses defect-induced surface irregularities during combustion synthesis.

SEM images of (a, b) BCE: 4wt% and (c, d) BCE:4%, B:2%, L:2% along with size distribution curve.

The particle size histogram (inset of SEM images) derived from the SEM micrographs shows a slight increase in average grain size from ~ 375 nm for the Eu³+-only sample to ~ 420 nm for the co-doped composition (x = 0.02). This modest grain coarsening is attributed to the improved crystallinity and lattice relaxation facilitated by charge compensation from Li+ and Bi³+ ions, a behaviour commonly observed in aliovalently co-doped oxide perovskites26. Such morphological refinement implies that defect passivation occurs not only within the bulk but also at grain interfaces, resulting in smoother surfaces and fewer non-radiative centers36.

The improved microstructural uniformity and reduced surface defects correlate well with the enhanced photoluminescence efficiency observed in the co-doped samples. Such defect mitigation is known to suppress non-radiative relaxation channels and thereby strengthen radiative Eu³+ emission in oxide hosts36. The densified morphology and improved crystallinity provide favourable energy-transfer pathways from the host lattice to Eu³+ activator sites, consistent with the widely reported relationship between crystallinity, defect passivation, and increased luminescence efficiency in rare-earth-doped oxide phosphors37,38. The microstructural properties of these materials strongly influence their photoluminescence behaviour, as higher crystallinity and lower defect density support more effective host-to-activator energy migration. Moreover, the enhanced homogeneity and structural ordering are expected to facilitate efficient energy transfer within the host–activator–sensitizer network, an effect that becomes evident in the photoluminescence results presented in the subsequent Sect39..

TEM Analysis

To further elucidate the microstructural characteristics of the co-doped phosphor, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis was performed for the Li–Bi co-doped BaCeO₃:Eu³+ (BCE: 4%, Li: 2%, Bi: 2%) sample, as depicted in Figure S1. The low-magnification TEM images (Figure S1(a)) reveal that the phosphor particles exhibit irregular yet moderately agglomerated morphologies composed of nanosized crystallites. The individual particle dimensions are predominantly within the range of 50–100 nm, forming loosely connected clusters with distinguishable grain boundaries. Such agglomeration is typical for combustion-derived perovskite oxides19, resulting from rapid gas evolution during synthesis, which yields porous, low-density aggregates of fine nanocrystals.

At higher magnification (Figure S1(b)), the high-resolution TEM (HR-TEM) micrographs display clearly resolved lattice fringes, confirming the crystalline nature of the material. The measured interplanar spacing of approximately 0.27 nm corresponds well to the (110) lattice plane of orthorhombic BaCeO₃ (ICDD No. 01–071–6749), corroborating the XRD findings shown in Figure S1(c). The uniform lattice fringes and absence of any amorphous phase indicate that the Li+ and Bi³+ co-doping does not disturb the long-range crystallographic order of the BaCeO₃ host lattice. Instead, the presence of these aliovalent dopants is expected to facilitate local lattice relaxation and charge compensation, thereby enhancing structural stability and defect passivation.

The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern (Figure S1(d)) exhibits sharp, concentric diffraction rings composed of distinct bright spots, characteristic of a polycrystalline phase with randomly oriented nanodomains. Collectively, the TEM, HR-TEM, and SAED analyses substantiate the formation of phase-pure, highly crystalline, nanoscale BaCeO₃:Eu³+,Li+,Bi³+ phosphors with preserved perovskite lattice integrity. The nanostructured morphology, combined with excellent lattice ordering, indicates reduced defect concentration and improved charge compensation—factors that are consistent with the observed enhancement in photoluminescence efficiency and quantum yield.

Optical absorption and Urbach energy

Optical parameters of the synthesized Li+, Bi³+ co-doped BCE phosphor were determined by diffuse-reflectance spectra (DRS) analysis, where the Kubelka-Munk function, F(R), and Tauc plot were employed40. The diffuse-reflectance absorption spectra (Fig. 3(a) of the samples exhibit a pronounced absorption edge near 380 nm, accompanied by a broad charge-transfer (CT) band centered around 315 nm. This CT band corresponds to the O²− (2p) → Ce⁴+ (4f) electronic transition20, which dominates the absorption behavior of BaCeO₃-based perovskites. Upon co-doping, the CT band undergoes a slight blue-shift with enhanced absorption intensity, indicating modification of the electronic structure due to aliovalent substitution. The enhancement of UV absorption can be attributed to Bi–O (6s–2p) orbital hybridization, which raises and disperses the bands and broadens the CT transition41. while Li+ incorporation relaxes lattice strain and reduce defect-related scattering leading to more efficient light absorption42.

As shown in Fig. 3b, the extrapolation of the linear region provides the band gap values, which increase slightly from 2.89 eV for Eu³+-only BaCeO₃ to 2.93 eV for the co-doped sample (x = 0.04). The degree of structural disorder in the samples was evaluated from Urbach energy (Eu), derived from the reciprocal slope of ln(α) versus photon energy plots (Fig. 3c). The Eu values decrease from 0.664 eV for the Eu³+-only sample to 0.621 eV for the Li+, Bi³+ co-doped composition, reflecting suppression of localized tail states and reduced structural disorder44. This decrease indicates a substantial reduction in oxygen-vacancy-related defect density, consistent with the improved crystallinity observed in XRD and SEM analyses. The simultaneous introduction of Li+ and Bi³+ thus mitigates charge-imbalance-driven defect formation while modifying the optical band structure through Bi–O–Ce hybridization. These effects collectively enhance the absorption efficiency and charge-transfer-mediated excitation of Eu³+, which directly contributes to the observed increase in photoluminescence intensity45.

XPS analysis

The XPS spectra of the Li–Bi co-doped BaCeO₃ phosphors are shown in Fig. 4. For comparison, the XPS features of the Eu-only BaCeO₃ (BCE) sample have been reported previously20, confirming the presence of Ba, Ce, O, and Eu elements with Eu³+ as the stable oxidation state and mixed-valent Ce³+/Ce⁴+ species.

In the present study, XPS analysis reveals that co-doping with Li+ and Bi³+ induces notable changes in the electronic environment. The O 1 s spectrum (Fig. 4d) exhibits a distinct decrease in the high-binding-energy shoulder (~ 531.5 eV) associated with oxygen-vacancy-related species, indicating significant defect passivation upon co-doping46. The Ce 3 d spectra (Fig. 4c) show a lower Ce³+/Ce⁴+ ratio than in the Eu-only composition, suggesting suppression of oxygen-vacancy-driven charge compensation47. These give indirect evidence for the successful incorporation of Li+ ions into the lattice. Direct observation of Li+ in the XPS spectrum is challenging due to its low atomic number and weak photoionization cross-section, resulting in a low-intensity Li 1 s signal in the low binding energy region (~ 55 eV), which may overlap with background features [48] The Eu 3 d peaks (Fig. 4e) remain characteristic of Eu³+, confirming that the luminescent activator maintains its trivalent state. The Bi 4f7/2 and 4f5/2 peaks (Fig. 4f) observed near 159 eV and 164 eV, respectively, confirming the presence of Bi³+ into the perovskite lattice. These findings collectively indicate that Li+ and Bi³+ co-doping effectively stabilizes the perovskite framework by charge balancing and reducing oxygen-vacancy density.

Photoluminescence analysis

Figure 5 shows the photoluminescence spectra corresponding to Li+, Bi³+ co-doped BCE:4% samples, with insets showing excitation spectra corresponding to the major 2 emission peaks at 594 and 611 nm. Both excitation spectra of the co-doped samples display a broad band in the ~ 280–340 nm region, which is attributable to the O²−(2p) → Ce⁴+(4f) charge-transfer (CT) transition. This is followed by weaker sharp lines corresponding to the Eu³+ intra-4f transitions (7F0 → 5L6, 5D3, 5D2, 5D1) — consistent with earlier reports on Eu³+-doped perovskite oxide hosts20,21.

Under 321 nm excitation, the emission spectrum exhibits both Eu³+ ⁵D0 → ⁷Fj and ⁵D1 → ⁷Fj (j = 0,1,2,3,4) transitions [49], with the most intense emission at 594 nm ⁵D0 → ⁷F₁, followed by 611 nm corresponding to ⁵D0 → ⁷F₂ line. The moderate intensity of the ⁵D0 → ⁷F1 electric-dipole (ED) transition reflects the non-centrosymmetric nature of the local Eu³+ site and a perturbed crystal-field environment. In the present co-doped system, the emission intensity increases progressively with increasing Li/Bi co-dopant concentration up to x = 0.02, reaching ~ 1.9× the intensity of the Eu-only BaCeO₃ sample. A slight decrease in intensity observed after x = 0.02 is attributed to concentration-quenching phenomena.

The 5D0–7FJ emission lines of Eu3+ ions are widely employed as a probe for evaluating the local site symmetry of optically active centers. The 5D0→7F0 transition is an electric-dipole (ED) transition, which is strictly forbidden under ideal selection rules and can only be observable in low symmetry coordination environment. Moreover, since the 5D0→7F0 transition cannot undergo crystal-field splitting, each peak directly corresponds to a distinct Eu³+ site. Consequently, the mere presence of this line confirms that Eu³+ resides at a non-centrosymmetric, low-symmetry site [50]. In contrast, the 5D0→7F1 transition is a magnetic-dipole (MD) transition whose intensity is weakly affected by the Eu3+ ion’s surrounding environment. The band 5D0→7F2, an ED transition, is hypersensitive to local structural asymmetry and becomes the most intense emission when Eu³+ is located in a non-centrosymmetric environment. For the current samples, the intensity parameter ratio ratio, R = I (5D0 → 7F2)/I(5D0 → 7F1) calculated for the samples lies between 0.7 and 0.8, which together with the above observations, indicating that the Eu3+ ions are located in a moderately asymmetric local environment. Li+-only doped samples show moderate enhancement due to charge compensation. Li+/Bi³+ co-doped samples exhibit significantly higher emission intensity. This supports the role of Bi³+ as a sensitizer, facilitating: Charge transfer transitions (Bi–O → Eu³+) Energy transfer to Eu³+ emitting centers.

Absolute external quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements performed reveal an increase from 38% (Eu-only) to 51% at the optimal co-doping level x = 0.02. This enhancement is ascribed to combined mechanisms of defect compensation (Li+ reduces charge imbalance, Bi³+ influences local structure) and increased radiative recombination probability (via higher electric-dipole transition strength). The luminescence decay behavior was analyzed using a triple-exponential fitting function, and the average lifetime was calculated to be 5.8 ms. The relatively long lifetime and enhanced quantum efficiency indicate that the developed phosphor can be used for solid-state lighting applications, which is consistent with previously reported Eu³+-activated phosphor systems [51], [52].

where V is the unit-cell volume, Xc is the critical Eu³+ concentration, and N the number of cation sites per cell. The derived Rc (~ 2.5 nm) is significantly larger than typical exchange-interaction distances (~ 0.3–0.4 nm), thereby implicating multipolar (rather than exchange) interactions as the dominant quenching pathway. Using Dexter’s formulation, plotting log(I/X) vs. log(X) yielded a slope corresponding to θ ≈ 6, consistent with dipole–dipole interaction as the primary non-radiative energy-transfer channel in the high-doping regime. These findings align with prior analyses of Eu³+ systems in oxide hosts.

In summary, Li+/Bi³+ co-doping systematically modifies the host lattice of BaCeO₃:Eu³+, increasing local asymmetry around Eu³+, improving charge-transfer efficiency, raising quantum yield, and elevating the quenching threshold. The optimized composition (x = 0.02) thus delivers significantly enhanced red emission, making it a promising candidate for phosphor-converted LED applications.

Conclusion

This study establishes a clear mechanistic framework linking aliovalent co-doping, defect passivation, and charge-transfer-enhanced luminescence in Eu³+-activated BaCeO₃ perovskites. The incorporation of Li+ and Bi³+ ions in BaCe₀.₉₆–xEu₀.₀₄LixBixO₃ leads to single-phase orthorhombic structures with improved crystallinity and reduced microstrain. XPS and optical absorption analyses confirm substantial suppression of oxygen-vacancy-related defects and narrowing of Urbach energy from 0.664 to 0.621 eV, reflecting enhanced structural order and reduced non-radiative centers. Under 321 nm excitation, corresponding to the O²− → Ce⁴+ charge-transfer transition, the co-doped sample (x = 0.02) exhibits nearly twofold enhancement in photoluminescence intensity and a marked increase in quantum efficiency (51%). The enhanced optical performance is attributed to the complementary roles of Li+ and Bi³+—where Li+ provides charge compensation and defect passivation, while Bi³+ modifies the local crystal field and facilitates host–activator energy coupling via Bi–O–Ce hybridization. This cooperative effect strengthens electric-dipole transitions of Eu³+ signifying a more asymmetric and optically active environment.

Overall, the findings underscore that controlled aliovalent co-doping represents a viable pathway for defect engineering in perovskite oxides, offering a rational design approach for next-generation high-efficiency red phosphors excitable in the near-UV region. The insights obtained herein not only enhance understanding of structure–defect–emission interrelations but also position Li+, Bi³+ co-doped BaCeO₃:Eu³+ as a promising candidate for solid-state lighting and photonic applications.

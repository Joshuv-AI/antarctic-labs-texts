Coexistence of Hilbert space effects and orthogonality
Anil Kumar Karn

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that every pair of absolutely compatible Hilbert space effects are coexistent and exhibit a partial orthogonality property. We introduce the notion of partially ortho-coexistence. We generalize absolute compatibility to obtain more examples of partially ortho-coexistent pairs and introduce the notion of generalized compatibility. In the case of 𝕄 2 \mathbb{M}_{2} , we discuss a geometric behaviour of the generalized compatibility.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 47B02; Secondary: 46L10, 47L30.

1.
Introduction

The study of measurements is one of the main objectives of quantum mechanics. In the classical formulation, an observable is represented by a projection valued measure defined on the Borel sets of
\mathbb{R}
. However, this formulation assumes that measurements are accurate which is far from reality.

To overcome this situation, G. Ludwig proposed an alternative formulation. In his mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics, a quantum event is represented by a self-adjoint operator on a complex Hilbert space whose spectrum lies in
[0,1]
. Such operators are called
effects
or more specifically,
Hilbert space effects
and the set of all effects is called the
effect algebra
.

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space and let
B(H)
denote the set of all bounded linear operators on
H
. We write

B(H)_{sa}:=\{x\in B(H):x=x^{*}\}

where
a^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
a\in B(H)
and

B(H)^{+}:=\{a\in B(H)_{sa}:\langle a\xi,\xi\rangle\geq 0~\mbox{for all}~\xi\in H\}.

For
x,y\in B(H)_{sa}
, we define
x\leq y
(or
y\geq x
), if
y-x\in B(H)^{+}
.

The effect algebra of the Hilbert space
H
is denoted by
E(H)
. Thus

\displaystyle E(H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{a\in B(H)_{sa}:0\leq a\leq I\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{a\in B(H)^{+}:\|a\|\leq 1\}

where
I
is the identity operator on
H
.

In the Ludwig’s formulation of quantum mechanics,
Coexistence
(of effects) is one of the important relations. A set
A
of effects in
E(H)
is said to be a set of
coexistent effects
, if there exists a Boolean ring
\Sigma
with an additive measure
F:\Sigma\to E(H)
such that
A\subset F(\Sigma)
. (For the detailed discussion please refer to, for example,
[
7
, D.1.2.2]
.)

Ludwig established that a pair of effects
a,b\in E(H)
is coexistent if (and only if) there exist
x,y,z\in E(H)
such that
a=x+y
and
b=x+z
with
x+y+z\in E(H)
. (See
[
7
, Theorem 1.2.4]
.) An equivalent formulation was observed in
[
1
]
:
a
and
b
are coexistent if and only if there exist
c,d\in E(H)
such that
c\leq a\leq d
,
c\leq b\leq d
and
a+b=c+d
. In fact, one can choose
c=x
and
d=x+y+z
. Note that if
a,b\in E(H)
are coexistent then so is (each pair in) the set
\{a,b,I-a,I-b\}
.

As an example, it is known that if
a,b\in E(H)
with
ab=ba
, then
a
and
b
are coexistent. Conversely, if
a\in E(H)
,
p
a projection in
B(H)
and
a
and
p
are coexistent, then
ap=pa
. So a more non-trivial example would be a non-commuting pair of coexistent effects. Though such an example can be fabricated, the author could not find any mention of naturally arising non-commuting pairs of coexistent effects in the literature related to quantum mechanics.

The notion of absolute compatibility between a pair of effects was introduced and studied in
[
3
,
2
,
4
,
6
]
. We observe that an absolutely compatible pair of effects is an example of a coexistent pair of effects. We know that an absolutely compatible pair of effects need not commute. In fact, an absolutely compatible pair of strict effects do not commute. It was proved in
[
2
]
that an absolutely compatible pair of strict effects in
E(\mathbb{C}^{2})
are mixed states.

The richness of this class motivates us to expand the scope. We introduce the notion of
x_{0}
-compatibility
in a pair of effects
a
and
b
in
E(H)
with respect to
x_{0}\in E(H)
where
\|x_{0}\|<1
and
x_{0}
commutes with both
a
and
b
. We see that absolute compatibility is
0
-compatibility. Thus
x_{0}
-compatibility is a generalization of absolute compatibility. We further observe that an
x_{0}
-compatible pair of effects is again coexistent. In this way, we obtain some more classes of examples of coexistent pair of effects.

We also obtain a decomposition of an
x_{0}
-compatible pair of effects in terms of suitable projections. This generalizes a similar decomposition known for absolutely compatible pairs. Further, we explore a geometric behaviour of an
x_{0}
-compatible pair of effects in the case of
\mathbb{M}_{2}
. This description underscores an intrinsic property of an orthogonal (or equivalently, coexistent) pair of rank one projections in
\mathbb{M}_{2}
being carried forward by a
\lambda
-compatible pair of (
\lambda
-strict) effects in
E(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. (Absolutely compatible pairs of strict effects fall between the two classes.)

Let us recall that the rank one projections in
\mathbb{M}_{2}
correspond to the pure states. Whereas the
\lambda
-strict effects that engage in
\lambda
-compatibility correspond to mixed states. We know that coexistent (or equivalently, absolutely compatible) pairs of rank one projections in
\mathbb{M}_{2}
are precisely orthogonal pairs. Interestingly, an
\lambda
-compatible pair of effects in
\mathbb{M}_{2}
also exhibit some orthogonality albeit at a lesser scale. This observation make
\lambda
-compatible pairs of
\lambda
-strict effects in
\mathbb{M}_{2}
(which are mixed states) a curious case to study further. So we are hopeful that the absolutely compatible pairs of effects and for that matter
x_{0}
-compatible pair of effects will find a suitable place in the theory of Experimental (Quantum) Physics.

A commuting pair of effects as well as an absolutely compatible pair of effects possess an additional property besides being coexistent pairs. To showcase this, we introduce the notion of
partial ortho-coexistence
. Let
a,b\in E(H)
. We say that
a
and
b
are
partially ortho-coexistent
, if there exist
x,y,z\in E(H)
such that
a=x+y
,
b=x+z
,
x+y+z\in E(H)
and
yz=0
. We show that commuting pairs of effects as well
x_{0}
-compatible pairs of effects are partial ortho-coexistent. In particular, a pair of decision effects (that is projections in
B(H)
) is coexistent if and only if it is partially ortho-coexistent.

We can summarize these concept as the following.

(1)

Coexistent pair of effects: there exist
x,y,z,w\in E(H)
such that
x+y+z+w=I
.

(2)

Partially ortho-coexistent pair of effects: there exist
x,y,z,w\in E(H)
with
yz=0
such that
x+y+z+w=I
.

(3)

Absolutely compatible pair of effects: there exist
x,y,z,w\in E(H)
with
yz=0
and
xw=0
such that
x+y+z+w=I
.

(4)

Generalized compatible pair of effects: there exist
x_{0},x,y,z,w\in E(H)
with
\|x_{0}\|<1
,
yz=0
and
xw=0
such that
x_{0}+x+y+z+w=I
.

We have organised the paper in the following way. In Section 2, we introduce the notion of partially ortho-coexistent effects and discuss some of its elementary properties. We show that a commuting pair of effects are partially ortho-coexistent.

In Section 3, we recall the notion of an absolutely compatible pair of effect and show that it is partially ortho-coexistent. Next, we introduce the notion of an
x_{0}
-compatible pair of effects and study its properties on the lines of absolutely compatible pairs. We also descibe its geometric behaviour in the case of
\mathbb{M}_{2}
.

In Section 4, we summarize the description of an absolutely compatible pair of effects.

2.
Partial ortho-coexistence

In this section, we include an additional condition to the coexistence of a pair of effects. We repeat the following notion mentioned in the introduction.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. We say that
a
and
b
are
partially ortho-coexistent
, if there exist
x,y,z\in E(H)
such that
a=x+y
,
b=x+z
,
x+y+z\in E(H)
and
yz=0
.

We recall that
xy=0
(that is,
x
is
(algebraically) orthogonal
to
y
), if and only if
|x-y|=x+y
. (Here
|w|:=(w^{*}w)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
w\in B(H)
.) Thus

|a-b|=|y-z|=y+z=a+b-2x

so that
x=\frac{1}{2}\{a+b-|a-b|\}:=a\dot{\wedge}b
. Again

x+y+z=x+|a-b|=\frac{1}{2}\{a+b+|a-b|\}:=a\dot{\vee}b.

Hence we conclude that if
a
and
b
are partially ortho-coexistent, then
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
.

Alert readers may recall that the notions
\dot{\wedge}
and
\dot{\vee}
were considered in
[
3
]
to introduce the idea of
absolutely ordered vector spaces
which include vector lattices as examples. It was shown in
[
5
]
that in a vector
V
,
a\dot{\wedge}b=a\wedge b
and
a\dot{\vee}b=a\vee b
whenever
a,b\in V
.

Let
a,b\in B(H)_{sa}
.
Then
a\dot{\wedge}b\leq a\leq a\dot{\vee}b
,
a\dot{\wedge}b\leq b\leq a\dot{\vee}b
and
a\dot{\wedge}b+a\dot{\vee}b=a+b
. Further

(a-(a\dot{\wedge}b))+(b-(a\dot{\wedge}b))=|a-b|=|(a-(a\dot{\wedge}b))-(b-(a\dot{\wedge}b))|

Thus
a
and
b
are partially ortho-coexistent, if
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
. We record this conclusion in the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. Then
a
and
b
are partially ortho-coexistent if and only if
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
. In this case,
x,y,z\in E(H)
are determined uniquely whenever
a=x+y
,
b=x+z
and
yz=0
. In fact, we have
x=a\dot{\wedge}b
,
y=(a-b)^{+}
,
z=(a-b)^{-}
and
x+y+z=a\dot{\vee}b
. (Here
u^{+}:=\frac{1}{2}(|u|+u)
and
u^{-}:=\frac{1}{2}(|u|-u)
, if
u\in B(H)_{sa}
.)

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
be partially ortho-existent. Then
I-a
and
I-b
are also partial ortho-coexistent in
E(H)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
so that
0\leq a\dot{\wedge}b\leq a\dot{\vee}b\leq I
. Thus
0\leq I-(a\dot{\vee}b)\leq I-(a\dot{\wedge}b)\leq I
. Since

u-(v\dot{\wedge}w)=(u-v)\dot{\vee}(u-w)

and

u-(v\dot{\vee}w)=(u-v)\dot{\wedge}(u-w)

for all
u,v,w\in B(H)_{sa}
, we have

0\leq(I-a)\dot{\wedge}(I-b)\leq(I-a)\dot{\vee}(I-b)\leq I.

Thus
(I-a)\dot{\wedge}(I-b),(I-a)\dot{\vee}(I-b)\in E(H)
. That is,
I-a
and
I-b
are also partial ortho-coexistent in
E(H)
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

a
and
b
are partially ortho-coexistent;

(2)

a=a_{1}+a_{2}
for some
a_{1},a_{2}\in E(H)
such that
a_{1}\leq a\dot{\wedge}b
and
a_{2}\leq I-b
;

(3)

b=b_{1}+b_{2}
for some
b_{1},b_{2}\in E(H)
such that
b_{1}\leq a\dot{\wedge}b
and
b_{2}\leq I-a
.

Proof.

First, we assume that
a
is partially ortho-coexistent with
b
. Then by Theorem
2.2
,
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
. Set
a_{1}:=a\dot{\wedge}b
and
a_{2}:=a-a_{1}
. As
a\dot{\wedge}b\leq a
, we get that
a_{1},a_{2}\in E(H)
. Also

b+a_{2}=a+b-(a\dot{\wedge}b)=a\dot{\vee}b\leq I

so that
a_{1}\leq a\dot{\wedge}b
and
a_{2}\leq I-b
.

Conversely, we assume that
a=a_{1}+a_{2}
for some
a_{1},a_{2}\in E(H)
with
a_{1}\leq a\dot{\wedge}b
and
a_{2}\leq I-b
. Then

a-(a\dot{\wedge}b)\leq a-a_{1}=a_{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle I-(a\dot{\vee}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I-[a+b-(a\dot{\wedge}b)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-b)-[a-(a\dot{\wedge}b)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(I-b)-a_{2}\geq 0.

Thus
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
. Now, by Theorem
2.2
,
a
is partially ortho-coexistent. As
\dot{\wedge}
and
\dot{\vee}
are symmetric, the result follows.
∎

Returning back to
\dot{\wedge}
an
\dot{\vee}
, we have

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. Then
a\dot{\wedge}b\geq 0
if and only if there exist
x,y,z\in E(H)
with
yz=0
such that
a=x+y
and
b=x+z
. In this case,
x+y+z=a\dot{\vee}b
.

Proof.

First we assume that
a\dot{\wedge}b\geq 0
. Put
x=a\dot{\wedge}b
,
y=a-a\dot{\wedge}b
and
z=b-a\dot{\wedge}b
. Then
x,y,z\in E(H)
with
a=x+y
and
b=x+z
. Also

y+z=a+b-2(a\dot{\wedge}b)=|a-b|=|y-z|

so that
yz=0
.

Conversely, we assume that
x,y,z\in E(H)
with
yz=0
such that
a=x+y
and
b=x+z
. Then
|a-b|=|y-z|=y+z
so that

a\dot{\wedge}b=\frac{1}{2}\{a+b-|a-b|\}=x\geq 0.

In this case,

x+y+z=a+b-(a\dot{\wedge}b)=a\dot{\vee}b
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
p,q\in E(H)
be projections such that
p\dot{\wedge}q\in B(H)^{+}
. Then
pq=qp
.

Proof.

As
p\dot{\wedge}q\geq 0
, we get
|p-q|\leq p+q
. Consider
p-q=x-y
where
x,y\in B(H)^{+}
and
xy=0(=yx)
. Then
|p-q|=|x-y|=x+y
so that
x+y\leq p+q=x-y+2q
. Thus, we get
0\leq y\leq q
. As
p-q=x-y
, we further get
0\leq x\leq p
. Let
p=x+z
. Then
z\in B(H)^{+}
and
q=y+z
. Since
p
and
q
are projections, and since
0\leq x,z\leq p
and
0\leq y,z\leq q
, we deduce that
xp=px=x
;
zp=pz=z
;
yq=qy=y
; and
zq=qz=z
. Thus

z(x-y)=z(p-q)=0=(p-q)z=(x-y)z.

Hence
zx=zy
and
xz=yz
. Again, as
pz=zp
, we get
xz+z^{2}=zx+z^{2}
, or equivalently,
xz=zx
. Consequently,
yz=zy
. Thus
x+z
commutes with
y+z
. In other words,
pq=qp
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
with
ab=ba
. Then
a
and
b
are partial ortho-coexistent.

Proof.

As
ab=ba
, we have
ab\in B(H)^{+}
. Thus

|a-b|^{2}=a^{2}-2ab+b^{2}\leq a^{2}-2ab+b^{2}=(a+b)^{2}

so that
|a-b|\leq a+b
. Hence

a\dot{\wedge}b=\frac{1}{2}\{a+b-|a-b|\}\in B(H)^{+}.

Next, as
I-a,I-b\in E(H)
, replacing
a
and
b
by
I-a
and
I-b
respectively, we also have

|a-b|=|(I-a)-(I-b)|\leq(I-a)+(I-B).

Thus

a\dot{\vee}b=\frac{1}{2}\{a+b+|a-b|\}\leq I.

Since
0\leq a\dot{\wedge}b\leq a\dot{\vee}b\leq I
, we have
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
. Thus by Theorem
2.2
,
a
and
b
are partial ortho-coexistent.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

(1)

We recall that if
a,b\in E(H)
are coexistent, then the decomposition
a=x+y
and
b=x+z
where
x,y,z\in E(H)
with
x+y+z\in E(H)
, in general, need not be unique. In this sense, the uniqueness of decomposition a partial ortho-coexistent pair in
E(H)
is an additional feature.

(2)

If
a,b\in E(H)
with
ab=ba
, then replicating the proof of Proposition
2.7
, we can show that each effect in
\{a,I-a\}
is partial ortho-coexistent with every effect in
\{b,I-b\}
.

3.
Absolute compatibility and its generalization

In this and the next section we shall discuss a class of partially ortho-coexistent pairs of effects. Let us recall that in
[
3
]
, the author introduced the notion of
absolute compatibility
. Let
a,b\in E(H)
. We say that
a
is
absolutely compatible
with
b
, if

|a-b|+|I-a-b|=I.

In this case we write
a\triangle b
.

It follows from the symmetry in the definition that for
a,b\in E(H)
, we have
\{a,I-a\}\triangle\{b,I-b\}
whenever
a\triangle b
. It was proved in
[
3
]
that
a\triangle(I-a)
for some
a\in E(H)
if and only if
a
is a projection. It was also proved that if
a\in E(H)
and if
p
a projection in
B(H)
, then
a\triangle p
if and only if
ap=pa
. For a general pair in
E(H)
the following characterization was obtained.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
3
]

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. Then these statements are equivalent:

(1)

a\triangle b
.

(2)

a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in E(H)
with
(a\dot{\wedge}b)(I-(a\dot{\vee}b))=0
.

(3)

a\dot{\wedge}b+a\dot{\wedge}(I-b)=a
.

(4)

a\dot{\wedge}b+(I-a)\dot{\wedge}b=b
.

(5)

a\dot{\wedge}b+a\dot{\wedge}(I-b)+(I-a)\dot{\wedge}b+(I-a)\dot{\wedge}(I-b)=I
.

We present absolute compatibility in the language of (partially ortho-)coexistence.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. Then
a
is absolutely compatible with
b
if and only if there exist
x,y,z,w\in E(H)
such that
a=x+y
,
b=x+z
,
yz=0=xw
and
x+y+z+w=I
.

Proof.

First we assume that
a
is absolutely compatible with
b
. Put
x=a\dot{\wedge}b
,
y=a\dot{\wedge}(I-b)
,
z=(I-a)\dot{\wedge}b
and
w=(I-a)\dot{\wedge}(I-b)
. Then by Theorem
3.1
,
x,y,z,w\in E(H)
such that
a=x+y
,
b=x+z
,
yz=0=xw
and
x+y+z+w=I
.

Conversely, let us assume that
x,y,z,w\in E(H)
such that
a=x+y
,
b=x+z
,
yz=0=xw
and
x+y+z+w=I
. Then

|a-b|=|y-z|=y+z=a+b-2x

so that

a\dot{\wedge}b=\frac{1}{2}\left\{a+b-|a-b|\right\}=x\in E(H).

Again

a\dot{\vee}b=\frac{1}{2}\left\{a+b+|a-b|\right\}=a+b-x=x+y+z=I-w\in E(H).

Also
I-(a\dot{\vee}b)=w
so that
(a\dot{\wedge}b)(I-(a\dot{\vee}b))=0
. Thus by Theorem
3.1
again
a\triangle b
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Existence of an absolutely compatible pair in
E(H)
leads to the following type of a ‘partition’ of unity: there exist
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in E(H)
with
x_{1}x_{2}=0=y_{1}y_{2}
such that

I=x_{1}+x_{2}+y_{1}+y_{2}.

We shall call it an
absolutely compatible partition of unity
.

In particular, we deduce that an absolutely compatible pair in
E(H)
is a partially ortho-coexistent pair. In fact, for
a,b\in E(H)
, we have
a\triangle b
if and only if
a
and
b
are partially ortho-coexistent and
(a\dot{\wedge}b)(I-(a\dot{\vee}b))=0
. For a detailed study of absolute compatibility, we refer to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
]
. (We have summarized the main characterization at the end of this paper.)

3.1.
Generalizing the absolute compatibility

We further generalize the notion of absolute compatibility to obtain a larger class of partially ortho-coexistent pairs of effects.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
. We say that
a
and
b
are
internally compatible
, if there exists
x_{0}\in E(H)
lying in the centre of von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{1}
generated by
a
and
b
with
\|x_{0}\|<1
such that
|a-b|+|I-a-b|=I-x_{0}
. In this case, we specifically say that
a
is
x_{0}
-compatible
with
b
and write
a\triangle_{x_{0}}b
. When
x_{0}=\lambda I
for some real number
\lambda
with
0\leq\lambda<1
, we say that
a
is
\lambda
-compatible with
b
and write
a\triangle_{\lambda}b
.

Note that absolute compatibility coincides with
0
-compatibility. Due to the symmetry in the definition, we note that
\{a,I-a\}\triangle_{x_{0}}\{b,I-b\}
whenever
a\triangle_{x_{0}}b
. We prove some elementary properties.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
a,b\in E(H)
and let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
a
and
b
. Consider
x_{0}\in B(H)^{+}
lying in the centre of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
with
\|x_{0}\|<1
.

(1)

If
a\triangle_{x_{0}}b
, then
a,b\in[\frac{1}{2}x_{0},I-\frac{1}{2}x_{0}]
.

(2)

a\triangle_{x_{0}}b
if and only if
(I-x_{0})^{-1}(a-\frac{1}{2}x_{0})\triangle(I-x_{0})^{-1}(b-\frac{1}{2}x_{0})
.

(3)

If
a\triangle_{x_{0}}b
, then
a\dot{\wedge}b,a\dot{\vee}b\in[\frac{1}{2}x_{0},I-\frac{1}{2}x_{0}]
.

Proof.

(1)
: Let
a\triangle_{x_{0}}b
. Then
|a-b|+|I-a-b|=I-x_{0}
so that
\pm(a-b)\pm(I-a-b)\leq I-x_{0}

and
b
and if
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is the (abelian) von Neumann algebra generated by
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is isometrically
*
-isomorphic to
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
. Therefore, the absolute compatibility of a pair of strict effects in
E(H)
is in effect that of a pair of strict elements in
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
for some abelian von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

(3)

onsider a pair of strict elements
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
for some abelian von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Then there exist a unitary
U\in\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
and strict elements
p_{1},p_{2},x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
with
p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}=I
such that

U^{*}AU=((I-x)\otimes I_{2})P_{0}+(x\otimes I_{2})P

and

U^{*}BU=((I-x)\otimes I_{2})P_{0}+(x\otimes I_{2})P^{\prime}.

Here
I
is the identity element of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
,
P_{0}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}
,
P:=\begin{bmatrix}p_{1}^{2}&p_{1}p_{2}\\
p_{1}p_{2}&p_{2}^{2}\end{bmatrix}
,
I_{2}:=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}
,
P^{\prime}:=I_{2}-P
and
z\otimes I_{2}:=\begin{bmatrix}z&0\\
0&z\end{bmatrix}
. (Note that the set
\{z\otimes I_{2}:z\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\}
is the centre of
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
.) We recall (
[
6
, Definition 2.2]
) that a projection like
P
above is called an
\mathbb{M}_{2}
-strict projection in
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
.

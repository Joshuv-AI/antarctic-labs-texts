Attainment of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol goals in patients undergoing coronary revascularization in the contemporary clinical practice
Authors: Manish Bansal, Ravi R Kasliwal, Praveen Chandra, Rajneesh Kapoor, Nagendra Chouhan, Anil Bhan, Naresh Trehan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to assess the effectiveness of current lipid-lowering therapy in achieving low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) goals in Indian patients undergoing coronary revascularization. Consecutive subjects (n = 1275, mean age 60.0 ± 9.7 years, 87.2 % men) with newly diagnosed coronary artery disease and undergoing coronary revascularization during the period 1 Jan 2023 to 31 Dec 2023 were included. After a median follow-up of 99 days (interquartile range 91–109 days), the mean LDL-C was 63.9 ± 24.3 mg/dL with 67.5% and 29.9% of subjects having LDL-C <70 mg/dL and <50 mg/dL, respectively. These proportions were 70.8% and 32.1% for patients treated with high-intensity statin therapy (rosuvastatin 20–40 mg/d or atorvastatin 40–80 mg/d). Among patients treated with rosuvastatin 40 mg/d and ezetimibe 10 mg/d, 53.3% achieved LDL-C <50 mg/dL. These findings underscore the need for greater usage of combination lipid-lowering therapy and agents with high LDL-C lowering efficacy.Keywords: High-intensity statin therapy, Ezetimibe, Bempedoic acid, Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease

Introduction

Effective lowering of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) is one of the most important strategies for secondary prevention of atherosclerotic vascular disease (ASCVD). The patients at the highest risk derived the greatest benefit. Accordingly, various societal guidelines have recommended LDL-C targets of <70 mg/dL to even <30–40 mg/dL for ASCVD patients belonging to different risk categories.1, 2, 3, 4

Statins are the primary therapeutic agents for LDL-C lowering. In patients who are not able to achieve sufficient LDL-C lowering despite maximum tolerated statin therapy, ezetimibe and/or bempedoic acid can be added. When LDL-C cannot be reduced sufficiently even with a combination of statin and non-statin oral agents, injectable LDL-C lowering therapies such as proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitors or inclisiran, a synthetic small-interfering ribonucleic acid, may be used. These agents can lower the LDL-C by 50–70 % but are prohibitively expensive.1, 2, 3, 4

Unfortunately, studies from Western countries have generally shown suboptimal use of LLT with underachievement of the recommended LDL-C targets. However, only limited information is available about the usage of LLT and the attainment of LDL-C goals in Indian patients.

Methods

This was a retrospective study conducted at a premier, tertiary care center in North India. The study included consecutive subjects with newly diagnosed coronary artery disease who had undergone coronary revascularization during the period 1 Jan 2023 to 31 Dec 2023. The subjects were included if the following information was available-

•Lipid profile during the index hospitalization

Lipid profile during the index hospitalization

•Details of the LLT prescribed at the time of discharge, and

Details of the LLT prescribed at the time of discharge, and

•The first follow-up visit, including lipid profile estimation, at approximately 3 months.

The first follow-up visit, including lipid profile estimation, at approximately 3 months.

Overall, 1275 subjects [mean age 60.0 ± 9.7 years, 1112 (87.2 %) men] were included in this study. For all the subjects, each lipid-lowering agent that was prescribed at the time of discharge and the dose used were recorded. High-intensity statin (HIS) therapy was defined as rosuvastatin 20 or 40 mg per day or atorvastatin 40 or 80 mg per day. The LLT at the time of discharge was prescribed at the treating clinician's discretion, regardless of the patient's background treatment at the time of hospitalization.

All the lipid profile estimations in this study were done in a fasting state with the baseline sampling performed within the first 24 h of the hospitalization. Enzymatic assays on Vitros Dry Chemistry Autoanalyser were used for measuring total cholesterol (TC), LDL-C, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and triglycerides. The non-HDL-C was calculated by subtracting HDL-C from TC.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board and the Independent Ethics Committee [MICR 1722/2024 (academic)]. The requirement for the patient's consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

Standard statistical tools were used for data analysis. All the analyses were performed using SPSS version 20.0.

Results

Table 1 summarizes the clinical and laboratory characteristics of the study subjects. Overall, 486 (38.1%) subjects had presented with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and the majority (772, 60.5%) had undergone coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). The mean LDL-C during the index hospitalization (which was on the background LLT) was 77.8 ± 31.4 mg/dL with 596 (46.7%) of subjects having LDL-C <70 mg/dL and 205 (16.1%) of subjects having LDL-C <50 mg/dL.

Most of the patients (1139, 89.3%) were treated with rosuvastatin and only a small proportion (9.7%) with atorvastatin. Twelve patients (0.9%) were not on any LLT. HIS was used in 90% of subjects and non-statin drugs in 49 (3.8%). No patient received a triple combination of the drugs. None of the patients were on injectable LDL-C lowering therapies (although 12 patients were advised evolocumab, their follow-up details were not available and hence, they were excluded).

Attainment of the low-density lipoprotein cholesterol goals

The median follow-up duration was 99 days (interquartile range 91–109 days).

The mean LDL-C at the follow-up visit was 63.9 ± 24.3 mg/dL with 67.5% having values <70 mg/dL and 29.9% having values <50 mg/dL3 (Supplementary Table 1). Table 2 describes the effect of various LLT on lipid levels. As expected, the patients receiving higher doses of statins achieved lower LDL-C levels. Among the patients on HIS (with or without the non-statin drugs), the follow-up LDL-C level was 62.4 ± 23.6 mg/dL with 32.1% having LDL <50 mg/dL and 70.8% having values <70 mg/dL. Among patients treated with rosuvastatin 40 mg/d and ezetimibe 10 mg/d, 53.3% achieved LDL-C <50 mg/dL.

Discussion

Our study shows that despite the high usage of HIS, the recommended LDL-C goals are achieved in only about one-third of all patients with CAD undergoing coronary revascularization. The study was conducted at a tertiary care center in North India, included a large number of subjects, and reflects contemporary clinical practice regarding the use of LLT. Hence, these study findings have important implications for the current clinical practice, as discussed below.

Need for aggressive low-density lipoprotein cholesterol reduction

ASCVD is the leading killer in the world, accounting for more than 25% of all deaths.5 The Indian population is worse affected with not only a higher prevalence of the disease but also more premature onset and a higher case fatality rate.6 Aggressive LDL-C lowering is one of the most effective strategies to reduce the mortality associated with ASCVD. The trials with PCSK9 inhibitors have shown that there is an incremental reduction in the ASCVD risk at ultra-low LDL-C levels with no apparent threshold below which the benefit ceases to exist.7,8 There are also no apparent safety concerns even at very low LDL-C levels.9,10 Accordingly, all the leading societies recommend lowering LDL-C to at least <50–70 mg/dL in all patients with ASCVD.1, 2, 3, 4 Even lower targets (<30–40 mg/dL) have been recommended for those with recurrent vascular events, poly-vascular disease, and other high-risk features.2, 3, 4

Despite the evidence to support the benefits of aggressive LDL-C lowering, the world over LDL-C control remains suboptimal.11,12 A recent large study evaluated lipid management across 18 European countries. Among the 2039 patients with established ASCVD, HIS was used in 37.5% of patients and the ezetimibe combination (along with moderate- or high-intensity statin) was used in 9.1% of patients. Only 18% of subjects achieved the LDL-C goal of <55 mg/dL.12 Even in the large randomized trials of LLT, the LDL-C levels achieved with the high-dose statin therapy have generally been higher than the currently recommended LDL-C targets.13, 14, 15 For example, in the Study of Coronary Atheroma by Intravascular Ultrasound: Effect of Rosuvastatin versus Atorvastatin (SATURN) trial, the patients on 80 mg/d atorvastatin achieved a mean LDL-C 70.2 ± 1.0 mg/dL and those on rosuvastatin 40 mg/d achieved a mean LDL-C 62.6 ± 1.0 mg/dL.13

Only limited information is available about the usage of LLT among Indian subjects. In a study published in 2015, 474 patients presenting with the first confirmed acute coronary event were included. Among the 118 subjects with elevated LDL-C at baseline, only 45.8% achieved an LDL-C level of <70 mg/dL at the end of a mean follow-up period of 13.5 weeks.16 A subsequent (year 2017), much larger randomized trial evaluated 1027 patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction undergoing thrombolysis. Atorvastatin 80 mg/d significantly reduced LDL-C but the mean LDL-C at 30 days was still quite high (65.2 ± 12.8 mg/dL). The proportion of the patients reaching specific LDL-C targets was not reported.17 A few other small studies have also reported similar findings.18,19

In our study, we found that despite using HIS in 90% of the subjects, only less than a third achieved the LDL-C goal of <50%. Even with a combination of rosuvastatin 40 mg with ezetimibe 10 mg daily, LDL-C <50 mg/dL could be achieved in only 46.7% of subjects. These findings highlight major challenges in achieving adequate LDL-C reduction in clinical practice, especially in the secondary prevention setting.

Implications for lipid management in clinical practice

Our findings suggest that to achieve sufficient LDL-C reduction, all patients with established ASCVD, especially those presenting with an ACS or undergoing coronary revascularization, must be treated with the most potent statin regimen (rosuvastatin 40 mg/d or atorvastatin 80 mg/d) along with ezetimibe 10 mg/d as the default option. The current practice of using monotherapy or lower doses of statins should be discouraged.

Even with the above combination, nearly half of all subjects may not reach the desired LDL-C goal. In such individuals, adding bempedoic acid is an option. A recent observational study has evaluated the efficacy and tolerability of the triple combination of rosuvastatin 40 mg, ezetimibe 10 mg, and bempedoic acid 180 mg daily.20 The study included 122 statin-naïve patients who had presented with an ACS. All patients were treated with this triple combination. At 2 weeks, 76.3% of the patients had LDL-C <50 mg/dL, and 79.8% had an LDL-C <55 mg/dL. These proportions at 6 weeks were 64.8% and 73.3%, respectively. These findings are encouraging but the long-term effectiveness of this combination and adherence to it need to be assessed.

In patients who are still not able to achieve the desired LDL-C levels despite using the triple combination, or are intolerant to statin therapy, PCSK9 inhibitors and inclisiran are the two options. However, these two agents are prohibitively expensive (esp. when considering the need for lifelong therapy). In addition, it also needs to be determined if the modest reduction in the ASCVD events with the incremental LDL-C lowering achieved with these injectable LDL-C lowering therapies justifies their additional high cost.

Lastly, as many of our patients do not reach the desired LDL-C goals with the conventional LLT, early and frequent LDL-C monitoring is essential to allow necessary up-titration of the therapy. This is especially important for patients with ACS for whom rapid LDL-C reduction is crucial. Hence, instead of the conventional 6–12 weeks gap, a repeat lipid profile should be performed at 2–4 weeks after the initiation of therapy in all patients with ACS.3,4

Limitations

The most important limitation of our study is that it was a single-center study. As a result, the pattern of LLT usage in this study cannot be generalized. However, while the choice and intensity of LLT remains at the discretion of the treating clinician, the impact of the therapy on the LDL-C levels is likely to be independent of it. The main objective of our study was to assess the effectiveness of HIS in lowering LDL-C in clinical practice, and we feel the study was able to achieve this.

Another limitation of our study was that the information about the background LLT at the time of inclusion in this study was not available. As a result, we could not estimate the magnitude of LDL-C reduction with the different combinations of lipid-lowering agents and dosages. However, as mentioned above, the objective of this study was to assess the final LDL-C achieved with various lipid-lowering regimens and not the magnitude of LDL-C lowering with different regimens. Further prospective studies are needed to evaluate the LDL-C-lowering efficacy of various lipid-lowering regimens. Lastly, we also could not assess the tolerability of various lipid-lowering regimens.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study shows that despite high usage of HIS, the recommended LDL-C goals are achieved in only about one-third of all patients with CAD undergoing coronary revascularization. These findings underscore the need for greater usage of combination lipid-lowering therapy and agents with high LDL-C lowering efficacy.

Zero-cycles on varieties over a 𝔅 s \mathfrak{B}_{s} -field
Toshiro Hiranouchi, Rin Sugiyama

Abstract
. A field F F is a 𝔅 s \mathfrak{B}_{s} -field if, for every finite extension E ′ / E E^{\prime}/E of F F , the norm map K s M ​ ( E ′ ) → K s M ​ ( E ) K_{s}^{M}(E^{\prime})\to K_{s}^{M}(E) of the Milnor K K -groups is surjective. In particular, finite fields ( s = 1 s=1 ), local fields, and certain global fields (with s = 2 s=2 ) satisfy this condition. For such a field F F and a d d -dimensional variety X X over F F , we prove that C ​ H d + n ​ ( X , n ) CH^{d+n}(X,n) is divisible for n ≥ s + 1 n\geq s+1 , and C ​ H d + s ​ ( X , s ) CH^{d+s}(X,s) is isomorphic to the direct sum of the Milnor K K -group K s M ​ ( F ) K_{s}^{M}(F) and a divisible group. As an application, we study the Kato homology groups K ​ H 0 ( n ) ​ ( X , ℤ / l r ) KH_{0}^{(n)}(X,\mathbb{Z}/l^{r}) for any prime l l different from the characteristic of F F .

1.
Introduction

The study of the higher Chow groups
CH^{m}(X,n)
for a scheme
X
introduced by Bloch is fundamental in understanding motivic cohomology and its applications to arithmetic geometry.
When
X
is a smooth projective and geometrically irreducible scheme over a finite field
F
of
d:=\dim(X)
,
the structure of the group
CH^{d}(X,0)=CH_{0}(X)
is well-understood
by the higher-dimensional class field theory
(e.g.
[
KS83
, Section 10, Proposition 9]
).
The theorem below describes the structure of
CH^{d+n}(X,n)
for
n\geq 1
;
in particular,
CH^{d+1}(X,1)
is finite.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Akh04b
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective and geometrically irreducible scheme over a finite field
F
of
d=\dim(X)
.
Then, we have
CH^{d+n}(X,n)=0
for
n\geq 2
, and
the structure morphism
f\colon X\to\Spec(F)
induces an isomorphism

f_{\ast}\colon CH^{d+1}(X,1)\xrightarrow{\simeq}CH^{1}(\Spec(F),1)\simeq F^{\times}.

We investigate an analogue over fields
F
satisfying the following condition:
for any finite extension
E/F
,
and any finite extension
E^{\prime}/E
, the norm map

N_{E^{\prime}/E}\colon K_{s}^{M}(E^{\prime})\to K_{s}^{M}(E)

on the Milnor
K
-groups is surjective for some
s\geq 0
.
Such fields are called
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
-fields
in

[
Kat78
, Section 3.3]

(
C_{0}^{s}
in the sense of
[
KK86
]
, see
[
KK86
, Lemma 2]
).
This condition is closely related to the cohomological dimension of
F
.
For example,
when the characteristic of
F
is 0,

F
is a
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
-field if and only if the cohomological dimension of
F
is at most
s
(cf.
Section 2
).
A finite field is
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
, while
local fields, totally imaginary number fields, and function fields of one variable over a finite field are
\mathfrak{B}_{2}

(
Section 2
).
Moreover, if
F
is an
N
-dimensional local field
then
F
is a
\mathfrak{B}_{N+1}
-field (cf.
Section 2
).
Here, an
N
-dimensional local field
is a complete discrete valuation field whose residue field is an
(N-1)
-local field, and a
0
-dimensional local field is a finite field.
The structure of
CH^{d+N}(X,N)
, often denoted by
SK_{N}(X)
, has been described by the class field theory
for
X
(
[
Blo81
]
,
[
Sai85
]
,
[

and
[
GKR
]
).
For a
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
-field

,
we consider the higher Chow group
CH^{d+n}(X,n)
for
n\geq s
.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(
Theorem 3.6
,
Section 3
)
.

Let
F
be a
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
-field for some
s\geq 0
, and
let
X
be a smooth projective and geometrically irreducible scheme over
F
of
d=\dim(X)
.
Then,
CH^{d+n}(X,n)
is divisible for
n\geq s+1
,
and the structure morphism
f\colon X\to\Spec(F)
induces a surjective homomorphism

f_{\ast}\colon CH^{d+s}(X,s)\twoheadrightarrow CH^{s}(\Spec(F),s)

with divisible kernel.

Remark 1.3
.

For example,
F=\mathbb{C}
is a
\mathfrak{B}_{0}
-field.
For an elliptic curve
X=E
over
\mathbb{C}
,
the kernel of
f_{\ast}\colon CH^{1}(E,0)=CH_{0}(E)\to CH^{0}(\Spec(\mathbb{C}),0)=\mathbb{Z}

is isomorphic to

.
Since
E(\mathbb{C})[m]\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}
,
the group is torsion and hence the kernel

is divisible but not

This example suggests that the kernel

in the above theorem is neither trivial nor uniquely divisible in general and
our result is an optimal generalization of

.

For the scheme
X
over
F
as in the above theorem,
the Nesterenko-Suslin and Totaro theorem (
[
NS89
]
,
[
Tot92
]
) gives an isomorphism

CH^{s}(\Spec(F),s)\simeq K_{s}^{M}(F)
.
From the theorem above, we obtain the following decomposition

CH^{d+s}(X,s)\simeq K_{s}^{M}(F)\oplus D,

for a divisible group

.
Much is known about the group structure of
K_{s}^{M}(F)

for
arithmetic fields
, such as local fields, global fields, and higher-dimensional local fields.
For example, when
F
is a local field,
Sivitskii’s theorem asserts that

is uncountable uniquely divisible for

(
[

, Chapter IX, Theorem 4.11]
)
and Moore’s theorem (
[
FV02
, Chapter IX, Theorem 4.3]
)
describes the structure of

.

Theorem 1.2
implies that

is divisible for

, and
that there is a decomposition

where

These results are refinements of
[

For other arithmetic fields

can be described via the above theorem

Section 3
, and
Section 3
).

Mackey product

A
Mackey functor

\mathcal{M}
over a field
F

(a cohomological finite Mackey functor over
F
in the sense of
[
Kah92
]
) is a covariant functor
\mathcal{M}

from the category of field extensions of
F
to the category of abelian groups equipped with a contravariant structure for finite extensions over
F

satisfying some conditions (see
[
Kah92
]
, or
[
Hir24
]
for the precise definition).
The category of Mackey functors forms a Grothendieck abelian category (cf.
[
KY13
, Appendix A]
),
so any morphism of Mackey functor
f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
,
that is, a natural transformation,
gives the image
\operatorname{Im}(f)
, the cokernel
\Coker(f)
and so on.

Example 2.1
.

(\mathrm{1})

For any endomorphism
f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
of a Mackey functor
\mathcal{M}
,
we denote the cokernel by

\mathcal{M}/f:=\Coker(f)
.
This Mackey functor is given by

(\mathcal{M}/f)(E)=\Coker\left(f(E)\colon\mathcal{M}(E)\to\mathcal{M}(E)\right).

(\mathrm{2})

Any commutative algebraic group

over

induces a Mackey functor,
also denoted by

(cf.
[

[

In particular,

is a Mackey functor given by

for any field extension

.
The translation maps are the norm

N_{E^{\prime}/E}\colon(E^{\prime})^{\times}\to E^{\times}
and the inclusion

.

(\mathrm{3})

Recall that, for

, the

-group

For an extension

Corollary 3.7

When

Proposition 3.11
.

Let

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the last residue field of

For
\ch(F)>0

\mu(F)=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

Hence, we have
K_{N+1}^{M}(F)=D\oplus K_{N+1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F)

It is also known that
K_{N+1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and thus

is divisible.

For

By the norm residue isomorphism theorem (the Bloch-Kato conjecture) and
[

\displaystyle K_{N+1}^{M}(F)/m

\displaystyle\simeq H^{N+1}(F,\mu_{m}^{\otimes(N+1)})

\displaystyle\simeq H^{N+1}(F,\mu_{m}^{\otimes N})(1)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/m(1)=\mu(F).

Let

be a primitive

-th root of unity.
We now show that an

-torsion element

for some

is a generator of

.
It is clear that an element of the form
\{\alpha,\zeta_{m}\}
is
m
-torsion.
We will show that there exists an element

such that

is a generator of

.
We consider the Hilbert symbol map

K^{M}_{N}(F)/m\times F^{\times}/m\to\mu_{m};\ (\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha,\beta)_{m}:=\gamma^{\rho(\alpha)-1},\ \gamma^{m}=\beta,

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

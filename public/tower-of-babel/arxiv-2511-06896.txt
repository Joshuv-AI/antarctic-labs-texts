Ruled and rational surfaces and their models
Giacomo Graziani

Abstract




One of the most powerful ideas in the study and classification
of algebraic varieties is the notion of a
model
—that is, to
single out an object, in the appropriate isomorphism class, with nice properties. This survey aims to define and study suitable models of rational and, more generally, ruled surfaces in the smooth complex case, and to use them to study such surfaces.

A key consequence of Castelnuovo’s contractibility Theorem is that
every surface
\mathcal{S}
admits a
relatively minimal model
: a birational
morphism
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
has no
\left(-1\right)
-curves.
We will see that the relatively minimal models of surfaces ruled over
an irrational curve are exactly the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles, i.e., the projectivization
of rank 2 vector bundles over that curve. It is also possible
to introduce a numerical invariant
e
for such bundles, and this
invariant will be shown to completely classify the indecomposable relatively minimal models over an elliptic curve.

Nevertheless, the notion of a relatively minimal model turns out to
be not completely satisfactory since, in general, such a model is not unique. To solve this problem, we define
minimal models

as surfaces with a nef canonical system; it is straightforward to see that such a model, when it exists, is unique. Dealing
with minimal models requires the introduction of a new biregular invariant
of a smooth surface, namely the notion of
type
. The existence
of this invariant provides an analogue of Kodaira’s classification
for smooth complex surfaces. From these results, we can deduce
many important statements, such as the existence of a minimal model
for every non-ruled surface and an extended description of relatively
minimal models in the general ruled case. This leads to a broad
characterization of ruled and rational surfaces, which contains as
particular cases both
Castelnuovo’s criterion for rationality

and an affirmative answer to
Lüroth’s problem for complex surfaces
.

In the final part of this survey, we use the theory we have developed to study the embeddings of ruled surfaces into projective spaces.
Namely, we will show that for every pair of integers
k,n
with
k>n\geq 0

we can embed
\mathbb{F}_{n}
as a rational scroll of degree
2k-n
in
\mathbb{P}^{2k-n+1}
. We also show that for every
n\geq e+3\geq 2
there exists an
elliptic scroll in
\mathbb{P}^{2n-e-1}
of degree
2n-e
and invariant
e
. These results, in analogy with the case of curves, suggest
that the possible degrees of an embedding are related to the rationality
of the surface. After showing that the degree of an irreducible non-degenerate
surface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is bounded below by
n-1
, we give a
complete classification of smooth surfaces that can be embedded in

\mathbb{P}^{n}
with degree
n-1
or
n
. As it turns out, all such surfaces are rational.

The material presented in this survey follows the classical treatments
of
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
, while we refer to
[
5
]
for background material.

Ampleness and very ampleness

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
X
be an affine variety and
f\in\mathbb{C}\left[X\right]
.
Let
U_{f}
be the open set defined by
f\neq 0
and
\mathcal{F}
a quasi-coherent
sheaf on
X
. Then

1.

if
s\in\Gamma\left(X,\mathcal{F}\right)
is a global section such that
s_{|U_{f}}=0
,
then there exists an integer
n>0
with
f^{n}s=0
;

2.

if
t\in\Gamma\left(U_{f},\mathcal{F}\right)
, there exists an integer
n>0

such that
f^{n}t
extends to a global section of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let us note that, since
\mathcal{F}
is quasi-coherent on
X,
we can find
open sets
\left(U_{g_{i}}\right)
with
g_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\left[X\right]

such that they form a
\emph{finite}
open cover of
X
and
\mathcal{F}_{|U_{g_{i}}}=\tilde{M_{i}}
,
where
M_{i}
is a
\mathbb{C}\left[X\right]_{g_{i}}
-module. Moreover

U_{g_{i}}\cap U_{g_{j}}=U_{g_{i}g_{j}}
. Let
s
be a global section
with
s_{|U_{f}}=0
, we denote with
s_{i}
the restriction
s_{|U_{g_{i}}}
.
For every
i
, since
\mathcal{F}_{|U_{f}\cap U_{g_{i}}}=\tilde{\left(M_{i}\right)_{f}}
,
the image of
s_{i}
in
\left(M_{i}\right)_{f}
is zero, thus there
exists
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n_{i}}s_{i}=0
. Since the open
cover is finite, we can find
n
such that
f^{n}s_{i}=0
for every

i
, and this proves
1)
. To prove
2)
, we note that, for every

i
, there exists
t_{i}\in M_{i}=\Gamma\left(U_{g_{i}},\mathcal{F}\right)
such
that the image of
t_{i}
on
U_{f}\cap U_{g_{i}}=U_{fg_{i}}
is

f^{n_{i}}t
for some
n_{i}
. As in the preceding point, we can
take
n
working for every
i
. On the intersection
U_{g_{i}g_{j}}

we have two sections
t_{i}
and
t_{j}
which agree on
U_{fg_{i}g_{j}}
,
from point
1)
there exists
m
such that
f^{m}\left(t_{i}-t_{j}\right)=0

on
U_{g_{i}g_{j}}
and we can take
m
big enough to work for every

i,j
. Finally, local sections
f^{m}t_{i}\in\Gamma\left(U_{g_{i}},\mathcal{F}\right)

glue to give a global section whose restriction to
U_{f}
is
f^{n+m}t
.
∎

Proposition 1.3

(
[
4

)
.

If

is a

.

Proof.

Let
\left(U_{i}\right)
be an affine cover of

, then
\left(f^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right)\right)

and thus, for every

\mathcal{F}_{|f^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right)}\simeq\tilde{M_{i}}

. Then, in view of

, we have

f_{\ast}F_{|U_{i}}\simeq\left(M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\left[U_{i}\right]}\mathbb{C}\left[f^{-1}\left(U_{i}\right)\right]\right)\tilde{}.

We have all the tools to prove

Theorem 1.4

(Serre)
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a projective
variety and let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Then, for
n\gg 0
,
the sheaf
\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)
is generated by global sections.

Proof.

Let
j
be the inclusion
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
, we have that

j^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(1\right).

Moreover, in view of Proposition
1.3
,

j_{\ast}F
is coherent and in view of projection formula we deduce

\displaystyle j_{\ast}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j_{\ast}\left(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(1\right)\right)=j_{\ast}\left(\mathcal{F}\otimes j^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\right)\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)=\left(j_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\right)\left(1\right)

from which we get that
j_{\ast}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)\right)=\left(j_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\right)\left(n\right)

for every
n
. Clearly
\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)
is generated by global
sections if and only if
\left(j_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\right)\left(n\right)
is
generated by global sections, thus we reduce to the case when
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
Let
\left(U_{i}\right)
be the standard affine open cover of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
then
\mathcal{F}_{|U_{i}}=\tilde{M_{i}}
where
M_{i}
is a finitely generated

A_{i}
-module, where
A_{i}=\mathbb{C}\left[\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}},\dots,\frac{x_{N}}{x_{i}}\right]
.
For every
i
, let
s_{ij}
, for
j=1,\dots,k_{i}
be generators
for
M_{i}
over
A_{i}
. In view of Lemma
1.2
there
exists
n
such that
x_{i}^{n}s_{ij}
extends to a global section

t_{ij}
of
\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)
for every
i
. Thus
\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)

corresponds to an
A_{i}
-module
M_{i}^{\prime}
over
U_{i}
and the
product with
x_{i}^{n}
gives an epimorphism
\mathcal{F}_{|U_{i}}\to\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)_{|U_{i}}

which, in view of Nakayama’s lemma, yields an isomorphism
M_{i}\simeq M^{\prime}_{i}
.
Then the sections
t_{ij}
generates
\Gamma\left(X,\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)\right)

and the theorem is proved.
∎

Definition 1.5
.

Let
X
be a projective variety, then we say that a sheaf
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(X\right)

is
\emph{veryample}
if it is generated by global section and
the induced morphism is a closed immersion.

Remark 1.6
.

Given a projective variety
X
and
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(X\right)

with
\mathcal{L}
very ample and
\mathcal{M}
generated by global sections, then

\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}
is still very ample. To see this, let us note that the
map associated to
\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M}
is the composition

X\overset{\Delta}{\longrightarrow}X\times X\overset{\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}\times\varphi_{\mathcal{M}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}\overset{s_{n,m}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{N}

where
s_{n,m}
denotes the Segre embedding.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
X
be a projective variety
and let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(X\right)
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

there exists
n>0
such that
\mathcal{L}^{n}
is very ample;

2.

for every coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

such that
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n}
is generated by global sections for every

n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

1)\Longrightarrow 2)

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{L}^{n}
be very ample and let

i:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the associated closed embedding, then

i^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\right)=\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent
sheaf on
X
then, in view of Theorem
1.4
,
there exists
m
such that
\mathcal{F}\left(m\right)
is generated by global
sections.

2)\Longrightarrow 1)

Let
x\in X
and
U
an affine neighborhood
of
x
such that
\mathcal{L}_{|U}
is free, and let
Y=U^{C}
. Under our
hypothesis, if
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal sheaf associated to
Y
, there
exists
n
such that
\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n}
is generated by global sections.
In particular there exists
s\in\Gamma\left(X,\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)
such
that
s_{x}\notin\mathfrak{m}_{x}\cdot\left(\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)_{x}
. Regarding

\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n}
as a subsheaf of
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, we consider
s
as a section
of
\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Let

U_{s}=\left\{p\in X\,\,|\,\,s_{p}\notin\mathfrak{m}_{p}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}^{n}\right\},

thus
x\in U_{s}\subseteq U
. For our choice of
U
, we can identify
\mathcal{L}_{|U}

with
\mathcal{O}_{U}
and
s
will correspond to
f\in\Gamma\left(U,\mathcal{O}_{U}\right)
.
We can repeat this construction to produce a finite affine open cover

U_{s_{i}}
, corresponding to sections
s_{i}\in\Gamma\left(X,\mathcal{L}^{n_{i}}\right)
.
We can replace the sections
s_{i}
with suitable powers and get

s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in\Gamma\left(X,\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)
without changing the
open cover
\left(U_{s_{i}}\right)
. For every
i=1,\dots,k
, let

\left\{b_{ij}\,\,|\,\,j=1,\dots,k_{i}\right\}
be the generators
of
\mathbb{C}\left[U_{i}\right]
as a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. In view of Lemma

1.2
, there exists
m
such that
s_{i}^{m}b_{ij}

extends to a global section
c_{ij}\in\Gamma\left(X,\mathcal{L}^{nm}\right)
.
The sheaf
\mathcal{L}^{nm}
is generated by the sections
s_{i}^{m}
, thus
it is generated by global sections. Let us consider the morphism
\phi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{N}

defined by
\left\{s_{i}^{m}\,\,|\,\,i=1,\dots,k\right\}
and
\left\{c_{ij}\,\,|\,\,i=1,\dots,k,\,j=1,\dots,k_{i}\right\}
.
Let
\left\{x_{i}\,\,|\,\,i=1,\dots,k\right\}
and
\left\{x_{ij}\,\,|\,\,i=1,\dots,k,\,j=1,\dots,k_{i}\right\}

be coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
corresponding to our sections. For every

i=1,\dots,k
, let
V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the open subset given by
\left(x_{i}\neq 0\right)
.
Then
\phi^{-1}\left(V_{i}\right)=U_{i}
and we have a surjective

\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\left[\left\{y_{i}\right\},\left\{y_{ij}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\left[U_{i}\right]

\displaystyle y_{ij}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle b_{ij}=\frac{c_{ij}}{s_{i}^{m}}.

Then
X
is mapped isomorphically onto a closed subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{N}

by
\phi
and so
\mathcal{L}^{nm}
is very ample.

∎

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with

\dim X\geq 2
and
H\in\mathrm{Div}\left(X\right)
an ample effective divisor.
Then
\mathrm{Supp}H
is connected.

Proof.

We can suppose the divisor
H
is very ample with associated invertible
sheaf
\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
. Let
Y=\mathrm{Supp}H
and, for every
n>0
,
let
Y^{n}
be the variety with
\mathrm{Supp}Y^{n}=\mathrm{Supp}Y
and defined
by the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(-n\right)

In view of Theorem

and hence a surjection

1.2
Intersection theory and Riemann-Roch for surfaces

The aim of this section is to give a cohomological treatment of intersection
theory on smooth surfaces.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
be distinct prime divisors
on a surface
\mathcal{S}
, and let
x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}
. We define the
intersection multiplicity of
x
in
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}
as

\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S},x}}{\left(f,g\right)}

where
f,g
are local equations in
x
for
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}

respectively. We set

\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}=\sum_{x\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}}\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right),

called the
\emph{intersectionproduct}
of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be
distinct curves on a surface, then

\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)\geq\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)

for every
x\in\mathcal{S}
, in particular

\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}\geq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}}\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right).

Proof.

It is enough to check that

\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)\geq\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)

for every
x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}
and

\sigma:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Bl}_{x}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\to\mathcal{S}
be the blow-up in
x
with
exceptional divisor
E
. We can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{\ast}\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\sigma^{\ast}\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{1}}+\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)E\right)\cdot\left(\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{2}}+\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)E\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{2}}+\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in E}\mu\left(y,\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\right)+\sum_{y\not\in E}\mu\left(y,\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\right)+\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{y\not\in E}\mu\left(y,\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\right)+\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\neq x}\mu\left(y,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)+\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}}\mu\left(z,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)+\sum_{y\neq x}\mu\left(y,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right),

we conclude that
\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)\geq\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\mu\left(x,\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)
.
∎

Proposition 1.18

Let

\chi\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(-\mathcal{C}\right)\right)+\chi\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\end{array},

therefore, in view of Riemann-Roch for curves and Definition

\displaystyle\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{L}_{|\mathcal{C}}\right).

Theorem 1.20
.

The intersection product defines a bilinear
form on
\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
and, if
D,D^{\prime}
are two distinct irreducible
curves on
\mathcal{S},
then

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D\right)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D^{\prime}\right)=D\cdot D^{\prime}.

Proof.

Let us set

f\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\left(\mathcal{L}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{3}\right)-\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2}-\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{3}.

A simple computation shows that
f
is symmetric in the three variables
and, moreover, Proposition
1.18
shows that, if
there exists a smooth curve
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)

for some
i=1,2,3
, we have
f\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3}\right)=0
.
In view of Lemma
1.19
, we can write

\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(A-B\right)
where
A,B
are smooth curves, then
we get
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(A\right)-\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(B\right)

and hence the linearity in the first argument. Since the intersection
product is obviously symmetric, the bilinearity follows.

Let now
f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{x}
be local equations for
D,D^{\prime}
in a neighborhood
of the point
x
. Let us consider the sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{x}\overset{\left(g,-f\right)}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{x}^{2}\overset{\left(f,g\right)}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{x}\to\frac{\mathcal{O}_{x}}{\left(f,g\right)}\to 0.

(2)

Since
f,g
are coprime in the factorial ring
\mathcal{O}_{x}
, if, for
some
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{x}
we have
af=bg
, then there exists
t\in\mathcal{O}_{x}

such that
a=tg
and
b=tf
. This shows that the sequence (
2
)
is exact. Moreover, if
s\in H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D\right)\right)

and
s^{\prime}\in H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D^{\prime}\right)\right)
are non-zero
sections vanishing on
D
and
D^{\prime}
respectively, then we have shown
that

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-D-D^{\prime}\right)\overset{\left(s^{\prime},-s\right)}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-D\right)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-D^{\prime}\right)\overset{\left(s,s^{\prime}\right)}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{O}_{D\cap D^{\prime}}\to 0

is exact, then

\displaystyle D\cdot D^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{D\cap D^{\prime}}\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{D\cap D^{\prime}}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)+\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-D-D^{\prime}\right)\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-D\right)\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-D^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D\right)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D^{\prime}\right).

∎

Theorem 1.25

(Riemann-Roch for surfaces)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
, then

\chi\left(\mathcal{L}\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}^{2}-\mathcal{L}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}\right).

Proof.

In view of Serre duality, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\cdot\left(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{L}\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)+\chi\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)-2\chi\left(\mathcal{L}\right),

by the bilinearity of the intersection product we get

2\chi\left(\mathcal{L}\right)=2\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)-\mathcal{L}^{2}+\mathcal{L}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}.

∎

Corollary 1.26

(Genus formula)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be an irreducible curve,
then

h^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{C}^{2}+\mathcal{C}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}\right).

Proof.

Let us consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-\mathcal{C}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to 0,

then
\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)+\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-\mathcal{C}\right)\right)
.
In view of Riemann-Roch, we deduce

1-h^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-\mathcal{C}\right)\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{C}^{2}+\mathcal{C}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)

and hence the corollary is proved.
∎

Remark 1.27
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible curve
and
f:\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{C}
its normalization. Let us recall that the

\emph{arithmeticgenus}

p_{a}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
of
\mathcal{C}
is defined
as
h^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)
, while the
\emph{geometricgenus}

g\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
is defined as
g\left(\mathcal{C}\right)=p_{a}\left(\tilde{C}\right)
.
In this way, Corollary
1.26
can be restated saying
that, for every irreducible curve
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, one has

p_{a}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)=1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{C}^{2}+\mathcal{C}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}\right).

The natural inclusion arising from the integral closure gives a monomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}
, taking the cokernel we get an exact
sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}\to\Delta\to 0,

where
\Delta
is supported on singular points of
\mathcal{C}
, and hence

H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\Delta\right)=0
. Taking cohomology, we obtain the
exact sequence

0\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\Delta\right)\to H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\to H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}\right)\to 0

from which we deduce

p_{a}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)=g\left(\mathcal{C}\right)+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta_{x}.

Then, in general,
p_{a}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)\geq g\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
with
equality if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is smooth. In particular
p_{a}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)=0

if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is smooth with
\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Remark 1.30

Let
X
be an algebraic variety and
let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be divisors on

. Let us suppose that there
exists

. Then clearly the map

\displaystyle|D^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle|D+D^{\prime}|

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle E+A

Lemma 1.31
.

Let
D
be a divisor on
\mathcal{S}
with

D^{2}>0
and let

be a very ample divisor. Then only one of
the followings holds true:

1.

D\cdot H>0
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(nD\right)\right)=\infty;

and

\lim_{n\to-\infty}h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(nD\right)\right)=\infty.

Proof.

In view of Riemann-Roch and Serre duality we have

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(nD\right)\right)+h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-nD\right)\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}n^{2}D^{2}-\frac{1}{2}nD\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}+\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right),

this shows that

\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}\left[h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(nD\right)\right)+h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-nD\right)\right)\right]=\infty.

Let us see that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(nD\right)\right)
and

cannot both diverge for

: if, for
n\gg 0
(resp.
for
n\ll 0
) there exists

, in view of Remark
1.30

we must have
\dim|\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-nD|\leq\dim|\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}|
. Therefore it suffices
to show that
\dim|nD|
and
\dim|\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-nD|
cannot diverge both for

and

. If
|nD|\neq\emptyset
for

,
then
D\cdot H>0
and hence, if
m<0
, we must have
|mD|=\emptyset

(otherwise
D\cdot H<0
). While, if

for

, in view of Remark

we have

\dim|2\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}|=\dim|\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}+nD\right)+\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-nD\right)|\geq\dim|\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}+nD|.

∎

Theorem 1.32

(Hodge Index Theorem)
.

Let
H
be an ample divisor on
\mathcal{S}
and let
D

be a divisor. If
D\cdot H=0
, then
D^{2}\leq 0
, with
D^{2}=0

if and only if
D\sim 0
.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
D^{2}>0
and let us set
H^{\prime}=D+nH
, which, in
view of Proposition
1.7
and Remark
1.6
,
is ample for
n\gg 0
, moreover
D\cdot H^{\prime}=D^{2}>0
. In view of Lemma

1.31
, for large
m
the divisor
mD

is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor, then
mD\cdot H>0
.
This is not possible since
D\cdot H=0
.

If
D^{2}=0
and

, then there exists
E\in\mathrm{Div}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)

with

. Let

. Then

\begin{array}[]{c}D\cdot E^{\prime}=H^{2}\left(D\cdot E\right)\neq 0\\
H\cdot E^{\prime}=0.\end{array}

Now let

and set
\tilde{D}=E^{\prime}+nD
, then
\tilde{D}\cdot H=0

and
\tilde{D}^{2}=E^{2}+2nD\cdot E

.

Proposition 1.42
.

Let
X
be projective variety,
\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(1\right)

a very ample sheaf and let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Then
there exists a numerical polynomial
P\left(x\right)\in\mathbb{Q}_{n}\left[x\right]

such that
\chi\left(\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)\right)=P\left(n\right)
for every

n\in\mathbb{Z}
with

\mathrm{deg}P\leq\dim\left[\mathrm{Supp}\left(F\right)\right].

Proof.

Let
j:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the embedding given by
\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(1\right)
.
In view of Corollary
[
5
, Corollary 8.2.3]
, the Proposition
is true for
\mathcal{F}
if and only if it is for
j_{\ast}F
, thus we can
suppose
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
. We proceed by induction on
\dim\left[\mathrm{Supp}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\right]
:
if
\mathcal{F}
is the zero sheaf, then clearly
P=0
works. Let
\left(f=0\right)

be an hyperplane section not containing any component of
\mathrm{Supp}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
.
Then the morphism
\mathcal{F}\left(-1\right)\overset{\cdot f}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{F}

fits into an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}\left(-1\right)\overset{\cdot f}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}\to 0.

In view of our choice of
f
, we see that

\dim\left[\mathrm{Supp}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)\right],\dim\left[\mathrm{Supp}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)\right]<\dim\left[\mathrm{Supp}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\right].

Twisting with
\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(n\right)
and using induction hypothesis
we see that there exists two numerical polynomials
Q_{1}\left(x\right),Q_{2}\left(X\right)\in\mathbb{Q}_{n}\left[x\right]

with

\mathrm{deg}\left(Q_{1}\right),\mathrm{deg}\left(Q_{2}\right)<\dim\left[\mathrm{Supp}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\right]

such that

\chi\left(\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{F}\left(n-1\right)\right)=Q_{1}\left(n\right)-Q_{2}\left(n\right).

In view of basic properties of numerical polynomials, if

Q_{1}\left(n\right)-Q_{2}\left(n\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}q_{i}\binom{n}{i},

then

\chi\left(\mathcal{F}\left(n\right)\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}q_{i}\binom{n+1}{i+1}.

∎

Lemma 1.43
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface and let
D

be a divisor with
D^{2}>0
and
D\cdot H>0
for some ample divisor

H
. Then
D
is big.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
1.31
, for
m\gg 0
the
linear system
|mD|
defines a rational map

\varphi_{|mD|}:\mathcal{S}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}

and hence we have

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(mD\right)\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(mH^{\prime}\right)\right)

for an hyperplane section
H^{\prime}
. In view of Theorem
1.10
,
we have
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(mH^{\prime}\right)\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(mH^{\prime}\right)\right)

for
m\gg 0
. Let us consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(mH^{\prime}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(\left(m+1\right)H^{\prime}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\left(m+1\right)H^{\prime}\right)\to 0

in which, in view of
[
5
, Theorem 6.6.1]
and Corollary
1.11

we can take
H^{\prime}
to be a smooth irreducible curve. Then

\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(\left(m+1\right)H^{\prime}\right)\right)-\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(mH^{\prime}\right)\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{H^{\prime}}\left(\left(m+1\right)H^{\prime}\right)\right).

In view of Proposition
1.42
and by elementary algebra
we conclude that, for
m\gg 0
, there exists
C>0
such that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(mD\right)\right)\leq C_{2}m^{2}
.
On the other hand, for

we have

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(mD\right)\right)\geq\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(mD\right)\right)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}D^{2}+\mbox{lower degree terms}.

Theorem 1.44

(Nakai-Moishezon Criterion for Ampleness)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface and
D\in\mathrm{Div}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
.
Then
D
is ample if and only if
D^{2}>0
and
D\cdot\mathcal{C}>0
for
every curve
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

If
D
is an ample divisor, then clearly
D^{2}>0
and
D\cdot\mathcal{C}>0

for every curve
\mathcal{C}
. To see the converse, let us consider

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(mD\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\left(m+1\right)D\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{T}\left(\left(m+1\right)D\right)\to 0

(3)

where
T=\sum t_{i}T_{i}
is an effective divisor. We see by induction
on
n=\sum t_{i}
that, for
m\gg 0
, the linear system
|\left(m+1\right)D|

is base-point-free. If
n=1
, then
T
is an irreducible curve.
Since
D\cdot T>0
, it follows from Riemann-Roch for curves that

H^{1}\left(T,\mathcal{O}_{T}\left(\left(m+1\right)D\right)\right)=0
for

m\gg 0
. Thus, taking the exact cohomology sequence associated to
(
3
) we see that the map

H^{1}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(mD\right)\right)\to H^{1}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\left(m+1\right)D\right)\right)

is definitevely an isomorphism. In view of Lemma
1.13

we conclude that, in the case
n=1
, we have
\mathrm{Bs}|mD|=\emptyset

for
m\gg 0
. If
n=\sum t_{i}\geq 2
, using notations as in Lemma

1.13
, we have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}
-modules

0\to\mathcal{O}_{T_{1}}\left(mD-T_{2}\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{T}\left(mD\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{T_{2}}\left(mD\right)\to 0.

By induction hypothesis and in view of our assumptions on
D
, we
conclude that, for
m\gg 0
,

h^{1}\left(T_{1},\mathcal{O}_{T_{1}}\left(mD-T_{2}\right)\right)=0

and hence
h^{1}\left(T,\mathcal{O}_{T}\left(mD\right)\right)=0
. As before,
we conclude that
\mathrm{Bs}|mD|=\emptyset
for
m\gg 0
. Then, since
D^{2}>0
,
we have a well-defined morphism

\varphi_{|mD|}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Im}\left(\varphi_{|mD|}\right)
is a surface. Since
D\cdot\mathcal{C}>0

for every curve
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, the morphis
\varphi_{|mD|}
has finite
fibres. In view of
[
5
, Lemma 10.1.1]
it follows that
\varphi_{|mD|}

is finite, thus
mD=\varphi_{|mD|}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\left(1\right)
,
and hence
D
, is ample in view of Corollary
1.12
.
∎

Lemma 1.64
.

\mathcal{S}
be a surface and

Theorem 1.72
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smooth curve and
\mathcal{E}

be a normalized rank 2 locally free sheaf on
\mathcal{C}
, then

1.

if
\mathcal{E}
is decomposable, then there exists

1.

Let

and suppose

then

then, up to reordering, we have

and hence

\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\oplus\mathcal{L}\,\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,\,\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)<0.

The second part of the statement follows trivially.

Since

there exists

and an exact sequence

∎

P_{n}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{n}\right)
for

.

They are called, respectively,
the irregularity, the geometric

of

.

Remark 2.2
.

Let

be a non-zero section for some

, then, for every

,

This shows that, if

P_{n}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\neq 0
for every

.

Definition 2.3

(Topological invariants)
.

For a smooth projective surface
\mathcal{S}
defined over
\mathbb{C}
, we
set

•

•

Remark 2.4
.

Since our varieties are irreducible, in particular they are connected
and, in view of Poincaré duality, we have

b_{0}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=b_{4}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=1\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,b_{1}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=b_{3}\left(\mathcal{S}\right),

thus
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=2-2b_{1}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)+b_{2}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)

.

For a smooth projective surface
\mathcal{S}
defined
over
\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle b_{1}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}\right)=2q\left(\mathcal{S}\right)

\displaystyle b_{2}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)

\displaystyle 2p_{g}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)+h^{1}\left(\mathcal{S},\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}\right).

Moreover the formula

Remark 2.6
.

It is clear that the integers

. It follows from Fact

that also

2.2
Relatively minimal models

From now on, all surfaces are assumed to be projective, smooth and
irreducible.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface. We say that
\mathcal{S}
is a
relatively minimal
model
if every birational morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.

The interest in relatively minimal models relies in the following
Proposition:

Proposition 2.8
.

Every surface dominates
a relatively minimal model.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface. If
\mathcal{S}
is minimal, then we are done. Otherwise
there exists a birational morphism
\varphi_{1}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
which
is not an isomorphism. If
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is minimal, then the proposition
is proved, otherwise there exists a birational morphism
\varphi_{2}:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{S}_{2}

which is not an isomorphism and so on. This process must end since
every map
\varphi_{i}
can be factored into a finite number of blow-ups
and thus we have

\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{NS}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\gvertneqq\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{NS}\left(\mathcal{S}_{1}\right)\gvertneqq\dots\gvertneqq\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{NS}\left(\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)\gvertneqq\dots

We conclude as
\mathrm{rk}\mathrm{NS}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)<\infty
.
∎

We need to introduce a definition:

A curve
\mathcal{C}
on a surface

is called a

Let

Theorem 2.11

(Castelnuovo’s contractibility criterion)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface, and

be a
\left(-1\right)
-curve. Then there exist a smooth

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and a point

\mathcal{S}\simeq\mathrm{Bl}_{p}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\right)

with exceptional curve

(i.e.
\mathcal{S}

Proof.

Let
H\in H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(1\right)\right)
, let us note that,
in view of Theorem
1.10
, we can suppose
H^{1}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H\right)\right)=0
.
Set
k=H\cdot E
and
H^{\prime}=H+kE
. Since

, the invertible
sheaves on
E
are characterized by their degree, so

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(E\right)_{|E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(-1\right)\\
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H\right)_{|E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(k\right)\\
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H^{\prime}\right)_{|E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{E}\end{array}

For every
i=1,\dots,k
, let us tensorize

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(-E\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{O}_{E}\to 0

to get the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+\left(i-1\right)E\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+iE\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(k-i\right)\to 0.

(9)

Under our assumptions, we have an exact sequence

0\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H\right)\right)\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+E\right)\right)\to H^{0}\left(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(k-1\right)\right)\to 0.

Let us suppose
H^{1}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+\left(i-1\right)E\right)\right)=0

for

. As

, looking at the cohomology sequence
associated to (

vanishes and hence, by induction, the sequence

0\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+\left(i-1\right)E\right)\right)\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+iE\right)\right)\to H^{0}\left(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(k-i\right)\right)\to 0

is exact for every

. Moreover, this shows that there
is a decomposition

H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H^{\prime}\right)\right)\simeq H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H\right)\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k-1}H^{0}\left(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(i\right)\right).

(10)

Let

be such that
\left(t\right)_{0}=E
,
pick a base
\left\{s_{0},\dots,s_{n}\right\}
of
H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H\right)\right)

and, for every

with
1\leq i\leq k
, let
\left\{a_{i,0},\dots,a_{i,k-i}\right\}

be elements of
H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H+iE\right)\right)
which
restrict to a base of
H^{0}\left(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}\left(k-i\right)\right)
.
In view of (
10
), the set

\left\{t^{k}s_{0},\dots,t^{k}s_{n},t^{k-1}a_{1,0},\dots,t^{k-1}a_{1,k-1},\dots,ta_{k-1,1},a_{k,0}\right\}

gives a base of
H^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(H^{\prime}\right)\right)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{S}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the associated rational map.
Since

was a very ample divisor, the restriction

is a closed embedding and the curve

is contracted to the point

. To conclude, it suffices to check that

is smooth on the surface

.
Let

We can define morphisms

\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{|U}}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\left(\mathbb{A}^{2}\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Clearly

From the choice of

Let us move to the complex topology. We claim that, after replacing

with a smaller open set, the map

gives an

In view of Lemma

, there exist an open

Corollary 2.12
.

A surface
\mathcal{S}
is a relatively
minimal model if and only if it does not contain
\left(-1\right)
-curves.

Proof.

If
E\subseteq\mathcal{S}
is a
\left(-1\right)
-curve, in view of Castelnuovo’s
Theorem
2.11
, there exists a birational
morphism
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
which contracts
E
, thus it is not an isomorphism
and hence
\mathcal{S}
is not a relatively minimal model.

If
\mathcal{S}
is not a relatively minimal model, then there exist a surface

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and a birational morphism

which is not an isomorphism.
Then

f=\epsilon_{1}\circ\dots\circ\epsilon_{n}

is a finite composition of blowing ups and the exceptional curve
of the last one gives a
\left(-1\right)
-curve on
\mathcal{S}
.
∎

2.3
Ruled surfaces

Definition 2.13
.

A surface
\mathcal{S}
is said to be
\emph{ruled}
if there exist a smooth
curve
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{S}
is birationally equivalent to
\mathcal{C}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Example 2.14
.

It is clear that for every smooth curve
\mathcal{C}
, the product
\mathcal{C}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

is a ruled surface. Moreover rational surfaces are ruled, since
\mathbb{P}^{2}

is birationally equivalent to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Example 2.15
.

For every rank 2 vector bundle over a smooth curve
\mathcal{C}
, the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)
is a ruled surface as it is birationally equivalent
to
\mathcal{C}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smooth curve. We say that a surface
\mathcal{S}
is
\emph{geometricallyruledover\mathcal{C}}

if there exists a surjective morphism
\pi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{C}
whose fibres
are smooth rational curves.

Theorem 2.17

(Noether-Enriques)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface and
\pi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{C}

a surjective morphism onto a smooth curve. If there exists

such that

, then there exists an open

containing

and a

\pi^{-1}\left(U\right)\to U\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
1.64
we deduce that
\pi
is a flat
morphism, moreover, in view of
[
5
, Proposition 9.5.1]
and
the fact that
h^{0}\left(\pi^{-1}\left(x\right),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}|\pi^{-1}\left(x\right)}\right)=1

we deduce that the general fibre is connected. Since we work over
the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers, the general fibre is also smooth
and hence irreducible. Moreover we have
h^{1}\left(\pi^{-1}\left(x\right),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}|\pi^{-1}\left(x\right)}\right)=0

and hence, in view again of
[

1.66

Remark 2.18

It follows in particular from Theorem

Let

be a surface and

We have, in view of Corollary

,
D_{n}^{2}=D^{2}
and

D_{n}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}=D^{\prime}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}+nF\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}=D^{\prime}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-2n.

Moreover, for
n\gg 0
, we have
h^{2}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D_{n}\right)\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}-D_{n}\right)\right)=0

and hence, in view of Riemann-Roch and Lemma
1.55
,
we have

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(D_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{2}D_{n}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}D_{n}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n+1+\frac{1}{2}D^{2}-\frac{1}{2}D\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}},

this shows that, for
n\gg 0
, there exists
E\in|D_{n}|
. Since

F\cdot E=0
, it follows that
E
is a fibre of
p
. We conclude
that also
D^{\prime}
is a fibre of
p
. The last statement follows from
Corollary
1.33
.
∎

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be surface birationally equivalent
to
\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2}
where
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are smooth curves, then

1.

q\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=g\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)+g\left(\mathcal{C}_{2}\right);

2.

P_{n}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=P_{n}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\cdot P_{n}\left(\mathcal{C}_{2}\right).

Proof.

Since these constants are invariant under birational equivalence,
we can assume
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

1.

Let
p_{i}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
i=1,2
be the projection. Then it is clear
that
\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}=p_{1}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}^{1}\oplus p_{2}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}^{1}

and hence, in view of Künneth formula
[
5
, Proposition 9.2.4]

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\Omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2},p_{1}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}^{1}\oplus p_{2}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2},p_{1}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\right)+h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2},p_{1}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}^{1}\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\right)+h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathcal{C}_{2},p_{1}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1},\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}^{1}\right)+h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{2},\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}^{1}\right).

2.

It follows again in view of of Künneth formula
[
5
, Proposition 9.2.4]

that

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{S},\omega_{\mathcal{S}}^{n}\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{1},\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}^{n}\right)\cdot h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C}_{2},\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}^{n}\right).

∎

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)\to\mathcal{C}

be a geometrically ruled surface over an elliptic curve with

indecomposable and normalized. Then
e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\neq-2
.

Proof.

Let us suppose
e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=-2
, then there exist
P,Q\in\mathcal{C}

and a non-splitting exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P+Q\right)\to 0,

moreover
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P+Q\right)\right)\neq 0

find
R,S\in\mathcal{C}

Since
H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\left(-R\right)\right)=0

1.47

\displaystyle H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(S\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\gamma_{R,S}}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-R\right)\right)

\displaystyle H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\overset{\delta}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P-Q\right)\right)

f_{S}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(S\right)

\alpha_{S}=H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},f\right)\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\beta_{R}=H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},f\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P-Q\right)\right)

\textstyle{H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=\mathbb{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{S}}
\textstyle{H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P-Q\right)\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{R}}
\textstyle{H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(S\right)\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{R,S}}
\textstyle{H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-R\right)\right)}

∎

Theorem 2.26
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)\to\mathcal{C}

be a geometrically ruled surface over an elliptic curve with
\mathcal{E}

indecomposable and normalized. Then
e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=0
or
-1

and there exists exactly one such surface for each of these values
of
e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
1.72
and Lemma
2.25
,
the only possibilities for
e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)
are
-1
and
0
.

e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=0

There exists a non-splitting exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}\to 0

with
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
and
\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)=0
. In
particular, in view of Proposition
1.47
,
we have
H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{L}^{-1}\right)\neq 0
. In view of Serre duality
and since
\mathcal{C}
is an elliptic curve, we conclude that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{L}\right)\neq 0

and hence
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Viceversa, since
h^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)=1

there exists a unique non trivial extension

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to 0,

(11)

we see that such an
\mathcal{E}
is normalized. Clearly
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\right)\neq 0
,
moreover, if
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
is such that
\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)<0
,
then tensoring (
11
) with
\mathcal{M}
we
see that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{M}\right)=0
. Let us suppose that
\mathcal{E}

is decomposable: in view of Theorem
1.72
, there
exists
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
with
\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)<0
and

\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\oplus\mathcal{L}
. In view of (
11
)
we get

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq\bigwedge^{2}\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{L}

which is not possible .

e\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=-1

Since every invertible sheaf of degree
1 on
\mathcal{C}
is of the form
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P\right)
for some
P\in\mathcal{C}
,
we get a non-splitting exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P\right)\to 0.

We see that, for each
P\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists such a normalized sheaf
and it is unique up to isomorphism. In view of Serre duality and since

\mathcal{C}
is an elliptic curve, we conclude that
h^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P\right)\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P\right)\right)=1
,
then there exists a unique non trivial extension

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P\right)\to 0.

(12)

As before we see that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\right)\neq 0
. Let
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)

such that
\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)<0
, then we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\left(P\right)\to 0.

If
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P\right)
, then cohomology yields an exact sequence

0\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P\right)\right)\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\left(-P\right)\right)\to H^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\overset{\delta}{\longrightarrow}H^{1}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P\right)\right).

By construction of
\mathcal{E}
and in view of Proposition
1.47

we deduce that
\delta\left(1\right)\neq 0
and hence

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\left(-P\right)\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P\right)\right)=0.

For
\mathcal{M}\not\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(-P\right)
, then

h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}\left(P\right)\right)=0,

and hence also
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{M}\right)=0
. It is enough to
show that, repeating the above construction for a point
Q\neq P
,
then we get a sheaf
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{M}
for some
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
.
Let us note that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P+Q\right)\right)=2
and,
in view of Riemann-Hurwitz formula, we get a degree 2 map
\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

which ramifies in 4 points. If
R
is such a point, then
2R\equiv P+Q
.
Let us consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(R-P\right)\to\mathcal{E}\left(R-P\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(R\right)\to 0

which gives that
h^{0}\left(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}\left(R-P\right)\right)\neq 0
and
then there exists an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{E}\left(R-P\right)\to\mathcal{M}\to 0

with
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
. Then

\mathcal{M}\simeq\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathcal{E}\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(2R-2P\right)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(P\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(2R-2P\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(2R-P\right)

and hence
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(Q\right)
. In view of the unicity of the
non trivial extension, we conclude that
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{E}\left(R-P\right)
.

∎

Here we focus on a special class of ruled surfaces: those which are

Remark 2.27
.

It follows from Theorem
2.17

and Example

\mathbb{P}^{1}
are the surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{n}

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer and let us consider

\pi:\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If
H
is a section and

a fibre of
\pi
,
then

\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathbb{F}_{n}\right)=\mathbb{Z}\cdot\left[H\right]\oplus\mathbb{Z}\cdot\left[F\right]

,

H\cdot F=1

if
n>0

\mathcal{C}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{n}

\mathcal{C}_{0}^{2}=-n
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Pic}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\right)
is cyclic generated by the class of
a point, its image under the group homomorphism

is cyclic
generated by the class of a fibre, then

follows from Proposition

2.22
. To see

, in view of Theorem

1.59
, we can suppose
\mathbb{F}_{n}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(n\right)\right)

Let us consider the first projection

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(n\right)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}.

In view of Theorem
1.63
, there exists
a unique morphism
s:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{F}_{n}
such that
\pi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

and
s^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\left(1\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

\mathcal{C}_{0}=s\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\right)
,

\mathcal{C}_{0}\equiv tH+kF

2.22
,

∎

Lemma 2.31
.

2.5
A numerical point of view

Definition 2.33
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a surface. We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
\emph{minimal}
if

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}
is nef.

We say that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a
\emph{minimalmodel}
of
\mathcal{S}
if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

is minimal and birationally equivalent to
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 2.34
.

We see that a minimal model is a relatively minimal
model: if
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}
is nef and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
is a smooth rational curve,
then Corollary
1.26
gives

0=2+\mathcal{C}^{2}+\mathcal{C}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}

and hence
\mathcal{C}^{2}\leq-2
. Then
\mathcal{S}
does not contain
\left(-1\right)
-curves,
in view of Corollary
2.12
we conclude
that
\mathcal{S}
is a relatively minimal model. The converse is not true:
in view of Corollary
2.12
and Examples

1.23
,
1.24
we conclude
that
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
are both relatively minimal models,

\scriptstyle{\sigma}

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mu_{\ast}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}

with
a=\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)
. We have

\displaystyle-3a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\cdot\mu_{\ast}\mathcal{C}=\mu^{\ast}\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}_{r}}\cdot\mathcal{C}-E_{1}\cdot\mathcal{C}-\dots-E_{r}\cdot\mathcal{C},

then

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)=\mathcal{C}\cdot E_{1}+\dots+\mathcal{C}\cdot E_{r}\geq 3a.

If
a\leq 2
, then
\mathcal{C}_{0}
should be either a line through 3 of the
blown-up points or a conic through 5 of the blown-up points .

Let us suppose

, let

\left\{p_{r+1},\dots,p_{9}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{0}-\left\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\right\}

be distinct points and consider the cubic
\mathcal{C}_{1}
passing through

p_{1}\dots,p_{9}
. Then, in view of Example
1.23

and Lemma
1.15
we have

\mathcal{C}_{0}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{1}=3a\geq\sum_{i=1}^{9}\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{1}\right),

therefore, under our assumptions, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{9}\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)>\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)\geq 3a\geq\sum_{i=1}^{9}\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{9}\mu\left(p_{i},\mathcal{C}_{0}\right),

which is not possible .

Let now
\mathcal{S}
be a del Pezzo surface, in view of Proposition
2.8
,
there exists a relatively minimal surface
\mathcal{S}_{0}
and a finite composition
of blow ups

\sigma:\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{r}\to\dots\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{S}_{0},

moreover, in view of Remark
3.2
, Proposition

2.51
and Remark
2.27

we can assume
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{n}
for
n\neq 1
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then, in view of Remark
3.2
,
we see that no blown-up point may lie on the exceptional divisor of
a previous blow-up, then
\mathcal{S}
is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in
r
distinct
points, so that
d=\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}=9-r
. In view of Proposition
3.4

we conclude that

. If 3 points lie on a line
L
and if
\tilde{L}

denotes the strict transform of
L
via
\sigma
, then
\tilde{L}^{2}=-2
,
which is not possibile in view of Remark

. In the same way one sees that no 6 points
lie on a conic.

If
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
\mathcal{S}
is a del Pezzo surface with

. If

, then
\mathcal{S}_{1}
contains two non-intersecting

\left(-1\right)
-curves

and

If

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
be a cubic surface, then
\mathcal{S}
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{P}^{2}
blown up in 6 general points.

Proof.

In view of adjunction formula
[
5
, Corollary 6.3.16]
, if

H\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
is an hyperplane we have

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}=\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}+\mathcal{S}\right)_{|\mathcal{S}}=\left(-4H+3H\right)_{|\mathcal{S}}=-H_{|\mathcal{S}},

that is
-\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}
is very ample. Therefore
\mathcal{S}
is a del Pezzo surface
with
d\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=3
and we conclude in view of Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Definition 3.9

An embedded geometrically ruled surface

is called a

if every fibre
F
has degree 1.

Theorem 3.10
.

For every
k>n\geq 0
there exists
an embedding of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
as a rational scroll of degree
2k-n
in
\mathbb{P}^{2k-n+1}
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
2.32
that
|\Lambda_{k}|

is a very ample linear system on
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, moreover

\Lambda_{k}^{2}=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{2}+2k\mathcal{C}_{0}\cdot F=2k-n.

Since
\Lambda_{k}\cdot F=1
, in view of Proposition
1.68

we have

h^{0}\left(\mathbb{F}_{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)\right)

and hence, in view of projection formula, we compute

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)\otimes\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(k\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(k\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(k\right)\right)+h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left(k-n\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k+1+k-n+1=2k-n+2.

∎

Lemma 3.13

has a base point on

.

3.3
Surfaces of minimal and almost minimal degree

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{d}
be an irreducible
and non-degenerate variety of dimension
r
. Then

\mathrm{deg}\left(X\right)\geq d-r+1.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
d
. We see that the smallest possible
value for
d
is
r
, but then
\mathrm{deg}\left(X\right)=1
and the inequality
trivially holds. Let us suppose
d>r
and let
p\in X
be a smooth
point. Let
\pi_{p}:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
be the projection from the point

p
and let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
be the closure of its image. If
Y

were contained in a hypersurface
H
of
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
, then the linear
space
\left\langle H,p\right\rangle
would contain
X
and this
is not possible in view of our assumptions. Therefore
Y
is non-degenerate
and, in view of the induction hypothesis, we have

\mathrm{deg}\left(X\right)=\mathrm{deg}\left(\pi_{p}\right)\mathrm{deg}\left(Y\right)+1\geq\mathrm{deg}\left(\pi_{p}\right)\left(d-r\right)+1\geq d-r+1.

∎

Remark 3.22
.

In view of Lemma
3.21
,
we see that, if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{d}
is a non-degenerate and irreducible
curve, then
\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)\geq d.
If
\mathrm{deg}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)=d
,
as in the proof of Lemma
3.21
, we see that
successive projections from smooth points give a birational map onto
an irreducible plane conic, therefore
\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smooth irreducible
curve and
D\in\mathrm{Div}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
be a divisor such that
\mathrm{deg}\left(D\right)=d\geq 3

and

Proof.

In view of
[

∎

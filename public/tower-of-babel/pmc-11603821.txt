Safety and efficacy of intralesional rituximab injection versus involved site radiation therapy in primary ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma: study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Yaoming Liu, Yang Gao, Ling Jin, Qian Li, Xiaohui Wang, Yujie Wu, Jichao Zhou, Wei Liu, Lili Wang, Yuying Dong, Yajing Zheng, Lu Chen, Jinmiao Li, Shicai Su, Junjie Tang, Yinghao Wang, Weifeng Huang, Chao Cheng, Cheng Li, Ningyu An, Jia Tan, Hong Chen, Huiyu Guo, Yi Wang, Qing Zhou, Fei Han, Honglei Liu, Yu Zhu, Rong Lu

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInvolved site radiation therapy (ISRT) is a widely used treatment for primary ocular adnexal mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma with control rates over 85%; however, its complications could reach 30%–50%. Intralesional rituximab injection has shown promising results with remission rates between 65% and 100% and minimal complications. This study aimed to conduct a multicentre randomised controlled clinical trial to compare the safety and efficacy of intralesional rituximab injection versus ISRT on primary ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma.Methods and analysisThis is a multicentre randomised controlled trial and will be conducted at eight ophthalmic departments and institutes in China. 108 patients with primary ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma will be enrolled and randomised to undergo intralesional rituximab injection or ISRT. Comprehensive examinations will be performed before and after the treatment. The primary outcome is the cumulative occurrence rate of complications of grade ≥2 within 5 years after treatment commencement. The main secondary outcome is overall response rate, and progression-free survival, time to next treatment and overall survival will also be compared between the groups.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center (ID: 2023KYPJ125-3; V.20240818) and the ‘5010 Plan’ evaluation committee at Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, China, and all subcentres. All the participants will be required to provide written informed consent. The results will be disseminated through scientific meetings and published in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberNCT06190301.

Introduction

Mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma (MALT lymphoma) is, according to the definition of the WHO, a unique type of lymphoma, accounting for 7%–8% of newly diagnosed lymphomas.1 2 It is more common in women, with a median diagnosis age of 65.3

Ocular adnexa are the most common primary site for non-gastric MALT lymphoma.4 In the Asian region, MALT lymphomas account for up to 80%–90% of ocular adnexal lymphomas,5 6 much higher than the proportion of MALT lymphomas in other parts of the body. Ocular adnexal MALT lymphomas can invade the conjunctiva, orbit, lacrimal gland, lacrimal sac and eye muscles. Clinically, they often manifest as painless tumours, with varying presentations depending on the lesion location. They lack specificity, and diagnosis mainly relies on histopathological examination.1 MALT lymphoma is a type of indolent tumour. Most progress slowly, with a 5-year overall survival rate over 90%, and a 10-year survival rate of 75%–80%. It can relapse after treatment, affecting the same organ (in 60% of cases) or other extranidal sites.1 However, a few cases may spread outside the orbit or transform into high-grade invasive lymphomas, affecting the patient’s life.

Due to the painless clinical course of MALT lymphoma and the high remission rate of traditional treatments, modern treatment strategies focus on ‘non-toxic’ methods to minimise side effects while maintaining efficacy. However, for the treatment of ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma, there are currently no clear guidelines or consensus. Clinicians mainly adopt individualised treatments based on different locations and extents, including surgical excision, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, monoclonal antibody immunotherapy and antibiotic treatment.5711

The primary purpose of surgical excision is to obtain tumour samples for pathological examination and to make a clear diagnosis, rather than aiming for complete removal. This is because complications and organ dysfunction from eye tumour removal surgery can be more destructive than the lymphoma itself.5 Research has shown that the extent of MALT lymphoma surgical removal does not affect survival rates, and extensive excisions that damage surrounding tissues should be avoided.12 Hence, surgical excision alone is insufficient to thoroughly treat MALT lymphoma.

Involved site radiation therapy (ISRT) in the treatment of ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma is the most extensively researched and used method, with local control rates exceeding 85%.5 13 However, due to the uniqueness of the eye, complications from radiotherapy are as high as 30%–50%, including cataracts, corneal ulcers, dry eye disease, conjunctivitis, radiation retinopathy and radiation optic neuropathy. These can lead to significant vision impairment and dysfunction in patients.1416 The most common complication of ISRT is radiation-induced cataract. Cataract usually developed at 2–5 years after irradiation, and the risk of cataract was found to increase by 15% per year.17 18 Some reports also indicate that up to 40% of patients with ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma who underwent radiation therapy experienced recurrence on the opposite side or at a distant site.1

Thus, the focus of research on the treatment of ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma is to maintain a high remission rate while minimising the toxic side effects of treatment.

Rituximab is one of the most used monoclonal antibodies in patients with lymphoma, significantly improving the survival rate of patients with lymphoma.6 It is a chimeric human/mouse anti-CD20 antibody, acting on B lymphocytes. It binds to the CD20 antigen on both normal cells and tumour cells. The CD20 antigen is involved in the proliferation, activation, differentiation and signal transduction of B cells. According to literature reports, local injection of rituximab for the treatment of ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma has shown good therapeutic effects, with remission rates ranging from 65% to 100%,1923 and almost no serious complications were observed. However, the number of relevant studies is limited, and the number of cases in individual studies currently does not exceed 20, all of which are single centre and most of which are retrospective studies, and almost none have established control groups. Only one single-centre prospective trial exists in this field,21 and these studies are focused exclusively on conjunctival lymphomas.1923 Therefore, the local application of rituximab has shown promising prospects, but higher-level evidence is still needed.

This project proposes to establish a prospective, multicentre, randomised, controlled clinical trial, including nine domestic centres, to compare the safety and efficacy of intralesional rituximab injection versus ISRT for the treatment of primary ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma. The aim is to provide high-level clinical evidence for the treatment of ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma and to offer patients treatment options that have fewer complications and comparable therapeutic effects.

Methods and analysis

Objectives

The trial aims to compare the safety and efficacy of intralesional rituximab injection versus ISRT in the treatment of primary ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma, to offer patients treatment options that have fewer complications and comparable therapeutic effects.

Design and setting

This study adopts a multicentre, central randomisation, open-label, positive parallel control design. The study will be conducted at Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center (ZOC), Sun Yat-sen University as the principal centre, in joint partnership with six other hospitals and eye institutes, including Xi'an People’s Hospital (Xi'an Fourth Hospital), The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University, Peking University Third Hospital, Hainan Eye Hospital, The First Affiliated Hospital of Jinan University and People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region as subcentres. Recruitment

This trial is a multicentre study. All subcentres are tertiary hospitals in China and could ensure adequate participants. First screening will be conducted in the outpatient clinics at all subcentres. After confirming the eligibility, the trial will then be proceeded.

Allocation of interventions

A central randomisation system will be used to generate a random allocation sequence with a fixed block size. The subjects in each block unit will be evenly assigned (1:1) to the control group (ISRT) and the experimental group (intralesional rituximab injection). The random sequence will be generated by an independent person who will not be involved in the implementation of the trial. All subjects will be randomly assigned in order of enrolment according to the random list.

Masking

This trial involves localised drug treatment or radiation intervention, making it impossible to implement a blinded approach. Therefore, an open-label design is adopted. Technicians and other persons who are responsible for screening and outcome assessment are unaware of the group assignment throughout the entire study period. The trial statisticians will be blinded to the randomisation results until the completion of statistical analysis.

Outcome measurement

Primary outcome

The cumulative incidence of ≥grade 2 complications within 5 years after treatment commencement:

Grade 0: none.

Grade 1: asymptomatic cataracts; mild keratitis; mild local pain; mild fever; mild allergic/inflammatory reactions of the skin/conjunctiva.

Grade 2: symptomatic cataracts; moderate keratitis or corneal ulcers; minor retinal lesions or glaucoma; moderate allergic/inflammatory reactions of the skin/conjunctiva with or without keratitis, requiring corticosteroids and/or antibiotics; dry eye requiring artificial tears; iritis with photophobia; moderate pain; moderate fever.

Grade 3: cataract requiring surgery; severe keratitis or corneal ulcers; severe retinal lesions or retinal detachment; severe optic nerve pathology; severe glaucoma; severe allergic/inflammatory reactions of the skin/conjunctiva; severe pain; high fever, requiring hospitalisation or extended hospital stay.

Grade 4: panophthalmitis; blindness.

Grade 5: death directly resulting from treatment.

If a patient experiences treatment-related complications not mentioned above, they will be graded according to the WHO’s general criteria for grading treatment toxicity (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0):

Grade 4: life threatening; requires emergency treatment.

Sample size calculation

The sample size is calculated based on the primary outcome, namely the cumulative incidence of ≥grade 2 complications within 5 years after treatment commencement. The cumulative incidence of complications in the control group receiving ISRT over 5 years is assumed to be 30% based on the following:

The American Academy of Ophthalmology Ophthalmic Technology Assessment Committee Oculoplastic and Orbit Panel conducted a systematic assessment of the efficacy and safety of existing treatments for ocular adnexal lymphoma.5 They included 27 studies with 2009 patients, of which approximately 30% reported various types of ≥grade 2 complications (median follow-up: 2.7–11.4 years).

This study anticipates that the cumulative incidence of ≥grade 2 complications in the experimental group over 5 years will be reduced to 8%.192330 With a two-sided significant level of 0.05 and a power (1–β) of 80%, the target sample size for each group is estimated to be 46 using the software PASS V.16.0 (NCSS, LLC, USA). Considering an approximate 15% dropout rate, the final sample size for each group is determined to be 54, for a total of 108 cases.

The main efficacy endpoint is the overall response rate within 5 years after treatment commencement. Non-inferiority testing will be employed. Studies reported an average overall response rate of approximately 95% for ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma treated with ISRT.1 13 This study, based on the lower incidence of complications with rituximab relative to radiotherapy, establishes the clinical tolerance for the efficacy difference between these two treatment modalities, known as the non-inferiority margin (∆), set at −15%. Based on a two-sided alpha of 0.05 and an 80% power of the test, the required sample size per group is 46. The sample size, calculated based on safety indicators, is deemed sufficient to meet the non-inferiority hypothesis for efficacy.

Harms

The risks include common risks in radiation therapies and rituximab use. This study does not involve additional treatment methods and protocols. The radiation therapy and localised injection of rituximab in the study are standard clinical treatments, which patients might undergo even if they do not participate in the study. The treatment methods for ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, anti-infective treatment and monoclonal antibody immunotherapy. Sole surgery often cannot achieve complete tumour removal, and local tumour control is not satisfactory. Chemotherapy is commonly used in advanced tumours and requires intravenous drug administration with significant systemic side effects. Anti-infective therapy is only suitable for patients positive for specific pathogens. Therefore, the treatment protocol in this trial will not add any additional risk or burden.

Statistical software

SAS V.9.4 software or other appropriate software will be used for statistical analysis.

Basic principles

All statistical inferences use two-sided tests. A statistically significant test level is defined as 0.05, and the CI estimate for parameters uses a 95% CI.

Missing data

Efficacy analysis uses multiple imputation for supplementation or sensitivity analysis.

Dropout analysis

Comparison of overall dropout rates and dropout rates due to adverse events will use Pearson χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test.

Descriptive statistics

Mean and SD or median with IQR will be reported for continuous data. Categorical data will be described as frequency and percentages. No baseline comparisons between trial arms will be conducted.

Safety analysis

The difference of cumulative incidence of ≥grade 2 complications within 5 years after treatment commencement between trial arms will be tested by Pearson χ2 test. Logistic regression will be used to evaluate the adjusted intervention effect by including baseline demographic and clinical characteristics: age, gender and TNM stage. Also, the centre effect will be assessed.

Efficacy indicator (overall response rate)

The primary efficacy indicator is the overall response rate, with an assumption of non-inferiority. When the lower limit of the 95% interval of the difference in overall response rates between treatment groups (experimental group − control group) is greater than non-inferiority margin of −15%, it can be judged that the efficacy of the experimental group is not inferior to the control group. If the lower limit of the 95% interval is greater than 0, it can be determined that the efficacy of the experimental group is superior to the control group.

Overall safety analysis

Use Pearson χ2 test or Fisher’s exact probability method to compare the occurrence of all adverse events (including but not limited to the aforementioned treatment-related ophthalmic adverse events) between the two groups, and list all adverse events that occurred in this trial and reported abnormal laboratory tests, clinical examinations, and so on.

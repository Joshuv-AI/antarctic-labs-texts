Overview of This Information Collection

    Abstract: Medical standards are part of the general requirement for 
specific positions in the federal government. Medical requirements are 
created for positions that involve arduous/hazardous duties or require 
a high standard of human reliability. Failing to meet an established 
medical standard or physical requirement means that the individual is 
not qualified for the position. 5 CFR 339.301 authorizes agencies to 
require a routine pre-appointment examination as part of qualifying for 
a position with specific medical standards. The Office of Personnel 
Management (OPM) identified the positions of criminal investigator 
(special agent), GL/GS-1811, and explosives enforcement officer, GS-
1801, as having specific medical requirements. The qualification 
standard for Criminal Investigation series (special agent) and General 
Inspection, Investigation, Enforcement, and Compliance series 
(explosives enforcement officer) are published online in OPM's General 
Schedule Qualification Standards. These medical requirements are part 
of the basic prerequisites for both positions. ATF uses ATF Form 
2300.10, Special Agent Medical Preplacement, to collect specific 
identifiable data, such as the name, address, telephone, social 
security number, and medical data, necessary to identify the applicant 
and to assess whether they meet the positions' qualification standards. 
The applicant completes the first part of the form, and the doctor/
health care provider completes the second part. Applicants from outside 
ATF use the form to apply for these positions, and existing special 
agents and explosives enforcement officers who are applying for similar 
positions at ATF also use the form. The information on the form is used 
to determine medical suitability for these positions.

Revisions to This Information Collection

    As part of this ICR renewal, ATF is making no revisions to this 
information collection, OMB 1140-0056. However, ATF anticipates a 
program change affecting this information collection, in that ATF 
anticipates an increase in hiring for affected positions over the next 
three years. As a result, ATF adjusted the hourly burden due to a 
significant projected increase in the annual number of respondents 
since the last renewal, from 380 to 1,000, an increase of 620. This 
resulted in a corresponding increase in the total number of burden 
hours associated with this IC from 285 during the last renewal, up by 
465 hours to 750 hours for this renewal.

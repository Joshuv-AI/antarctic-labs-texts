Centers of perfectoid purity
Anne Fayolle

Abstract
. We introduce a mixed characteristic analog of log canonical centers in characteristic 0 0 and centers of F F -purity in positive characteristic, which we call centers of perfectoid purity. We show that their existence detects (the failure of) normality of the ring. We also show the existence of a special center of perfectoid purity that detects the perfectoid purity of R R , analogously to the splitting prime of Aberbach and Enescu, and investigate its behavior under étale morphisms.

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of characteristic
p>0
. Since the late sixties, the singularities of the ring
R
have been studied using Frobenius; see
[
Kun69
,
HH90
,
Hoc07
,
HH89
,
Smi97
,
MR85
]
to mention a few.
These are broadly known as
F
-singularities and have been linked to singularities of the Minimal Model Program
[
Sch09
,
Smi97
,
Smi00b
]
.
Of particular importance in this theory are the
centers of
F
-purity

[
Sch10
]
, a special type of
compatible ideal

[
MR85
]
. They tell us where the ring fails to be (strongly)
F
-regular.
These are related to log canonical centers, an important object in the study of singularities of the Minimal Model Program
[
Amb11
]
.
The aim of this paper is to define an analogous object in mixed characteristic.

Although there is no Frobenius in mixed characteristic, we have a good analog of perfection: perfectoidization
[
Sch12
,
BS22
]
. Our strategy is then to express what centers of
F
-purity are in terms of perfection in positive characteristic before writing an analog definition in mixed characteristic via perfectoidization. This strategy has been used successfully to define analogs of test ideals,
F
-signature, and
F
-purity in mixed characteristic
[
MS18
,
CLM+22
,
BMP+24a
]
.

Suppose that
R
is reduced and let
F\colon R\xrightarrow{\ \ }R^{1/{p}}
be the Frobenius map on
R
. Assume furthermore that
R^{1/{p}}
is a finite
R
-module and that the natural map
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }R^{1/{p}}
is pure. An ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
is said to be
uniformly compatible
if
\phi(\mathfrak{a}^{1/{p^{e}}})\subset\mathfrak{a}
for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi\in\Hom_{R}(R^{1/{p^{e}}},R)
. Let
R\perf\coloneqq\bigcup_{e}R^{1/{p^{e}}}
and
\mathfrak{a}\perf\coloneqq\bigcup_{e}\mathfrak{a}^{1/{p^{e}}}
. Then,
\mathfrak{a}
is uniformly compatible if and only if
\psi(\mathfrak{a}\perf)\subset\mathfrak{a}
for all
\psi\in\Hom_{R}(R\perf,R)

(see
Corollary 2.19
).
When
\mathfrak{a}
is prime, we say that it is a
center of
F
-purity
of
R
.

Now, let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Noetherian local ring with perfect residue field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Suppose that
R
is a finite
A
-algebra where

A\coloneqq W(k)\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\rrbracket
(for instance a Noether normalization). Denoting by perfd the perfectoidization of a ring or an ideal (see
[
BS22
, Section 10]
or
Definition 2.2
), we define

R^{A}_{\infty}\coloneqq\left(R\otimes_{A}W(k)\left[p^{1/p^{\infty}},x_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}},\ldots,x_{d}^{1/p^{\infty}}\right]\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\rrbracket^{\wedge p}\right)\perfd.

Note that if
R
itself has characteristic
p
then
R^{A}_{\infty}=R\perf
(
Corollary 2.19
). Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
be an ideal and fix
\phi\in\Hom_{R}(R^{A}_{\infty},R)
. We say that
\mathfrak{a}
is
\phi
-compatible
if

\phi\left(\left(\mathfrak{a}R^{A}_{\infty}\right)\perfd\right)\subset\mathfrak{a}.

If this holds for every possible choice of
\phi\in\Hom_{R}(R^{A}_{\infty},R)
, we say that
\mathfrak{a}
is
uniformly perfectoid compatible
. This does not depend on the choice of
A
by
Corollary 2.29
. Moreover, like in positive characteristic, these are closed under sums, intersections, and minimal primes, see
Proposition 2.34
,
Proposition 2.37
. This allows us to prove the following theorem:

Theorem A

(
Corollary 2.40
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Noetherian local ring with residue field of characteristic
p>0
. If
R
is perfectoid pure, there are only finitely many uniformly perfectoid compatible ideals.

In fact, there are finitely many
\phi
-compatible ideals for a surjective
\phi
(
Corollary 2.39
). If
R
is perfectoid pure and
\mathfrak{p}
is a uniformly perfectoid compatible prime ideal of
R
, we say that it is a
center of perfectoid purity
of
R
. Not only are there finitely many of them but we actually have a bound thanks to
[
ST10
]
, see
Remark 2.41
.
In positive characteristic, log canonical centers are centers of
F
-purity
[
Sch10
]
. The same holds true in mixed characteristic when
R
is quasi-Gorenstein (that is, when
R
has a canonical module that is free of rank
1
):

Theorem B

(
Theorem 4.12
,
Corollary 4.14
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Noetherian normal quasi-Gorenstein local ring with residue field of characteristic
p>0
. If
R
is perfectoid pure then,

(a)

The multiplier ideal
\mathcal{J}
of
R
is a uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal of
R
.

(b)

The ideals defining log canonical centers of
R
are centers of perfectoid purity.

(c)

If
R
has no uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal, then it is klt.

An important aspect of the theory in positive characteristic is that compatible ideals detect whether the ring is normal: if a ring of characteristic
p>0
has no uniformly compatible ideal, it must be normal. This is also true in mixed characteristic:

Theorem C

(
Proposition 5.2
,
Remark 5.3
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Noetherian local ring with residue field of characteristic
p>0
. Then, the conductor ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of
R
is a nonzero uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal of
R
. In particular, if
R
has no uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal, then it is normal.

We also show the existence of a special uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal
\upbeta(R)\subset R
that detects perfectoid purity, analogous to the splitting prime of Aberbach and Enescu
[
AE05
]
. This is defined in
Definition 6.6
and has the following property.

Theorem D

(
Proposition 6.7
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete Noetherian local ring with perfect residue field of characteristic
p>0
. Then,
\upbeta(R)\neq R
if and only
R
is perfectoid pure. In that case,
\upbeta(R)
is a prime and is the largest center of perfectoid purity of
R
. In particular,
R/\upbeta(R)
is normal.

We then generalize this construction to create an analog of the Cartier core map
[
Bad21
,
Bro23
,
CRF24
]
in
Section 6.3
. We also show that this map behaves well under étale morphisms in
Section 7
.

2.1.
Generalities about perfectoid rings and ideals

Definition 2.2

(
[
BS22
, Section 10]
)
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
an ideal. We say that
\mathfrak{a}
is a perfectoid ideal if
R/\mathfrak{a}
is also perfectoid. If
\mathfrak{b}\subset R
is any ideal, we define
\mathfrak{b}\perfd\coloneqq\ker(R\xrightarrow{\ \ }(R/\mathfrak{b})\perfd)
and
\mathfrak{b}
is perfectoid if and only if
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}\perfd
.

We write down a couple of facts about perfectoid ideals. These are well known to experts but will be useful throughout the paper.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\colon R\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
be a morphism between perfectoid rings. Then,
\ker f
is a perfectoid ideal of
R
.

Proof.

As the kernel of a map of two
p
-complete rings,
\ker f
is also
p
-complete. In particular,
R/\ker f
is semiperfectoid so it surjects onto its perfectoidization by
[
BS22
, Theorem 7.4]
. On the other hand,
R/\ker f\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
factors through
(R/\ker f)\perfd
so
R/\ker f\xrightarrow{\ \ }(R/\ker f)\perfd
must be injective, hence an isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(
[
DI24
, Proposition 2.8]
)
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
an ideal containing
p
. Then,
\mathfrak{a}
is perfectoid if and only if it is radical.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
BS22
, Example 7.9]
,
[
CLM+22
, Lemma 2.4.3]
)
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
f\in R
be such that
f
has a compatible system of
p
-th power roots, which we denote by
\{f^{1/{p^{\infty}}}\}
. Then,
(f)\perfd=(f^{1/p^{\infty}})^{-}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a set of perfectoid ideals. Then,
\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is a perfectoid ideal of
R
.
Moreover, if
I
is a finite set and
\{\mathfrak{b}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are
p
-complete ideals of
R
, then
\cap_{i}(\mathfrak{b}_{i})\perfd=(\cap_{i}\mathfrak{b}_{i})\perfd
.

Proof.

By
[
BIM19
, Example 3.8 (8)]
, the product

\prod_{i\in I}R/\mathfrak{a}_{i}

is perfectoid. Now,

\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\ker\left(R\xrightarrow{\ \ }\prod_{i\in I}R/\mathfrak{a}_{i}\right)

so the result follows from
Proposition 2.3
.
For the second part, since sheafification commutes with finite limits,
[
BS22
, Corollary 8.11]
gives

\left(\prod_{i\in I}R/\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\perfd=\prod_{i\in I}\left(R/\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\perfd.

In particular,

\bigcap_{i}(\mathfrak{b}_{i})\perfd=\ker\left(R\xrightarrow{\ \ }\prod_{i\in I}\left(R/\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\perfd\right)=\ker\left(R\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\prod_{i\in I}R/\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\perfd\right),

which is precisely
\left(\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\perfd
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be two perfectoid ideals of
R
. Then,
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}
is also a perfectoid ideal of
R
.

Proof.

We have an exact sequence

(2.7.1)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\left(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\mathfrak{a}\oplus R/\mathfrak{b}\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\left(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

which we claim implies
R/\left(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\right)
is also perfectoid. Indeed,

V\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)\cup V\left(\mathfrak{b}\right)\cup V\left(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Spec\left(R/\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\right)

is a universal topological epimorphism onto its image so it is an arc cover by
[
BM21
, Proposition 2.6]
and
[
Ryd10
, Theorem 2.8]
.
Taking the sheafification in the arc topology and using
[
BS22
, Corollary 8.11]
and
[
BS17
, Theorem 2.9]
, we see that the above sequence remains exact after perfectoidization
i.e.
the sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\left(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\mathfrak{a}\oplus R/\mathfrak{b}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(R/\left(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\right)\right)\perfd\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

is exact.
In particular,

R/\left(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(R/\left(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\right)\right)\perfd

must be an isomorphism.

∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
\mathfrak{a}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
a finitely generated ideal. Assume furthermore that each
x_{i}
has a compatible system of

-power roots in

. Then,
\mathfrak{a}\perfd=(x_{1})\perfd+\ldots+(x_{n})\perfd=(\sqrt{x_{1}})^{-}+\ldots+(\sqrt{x_{n}})^{-}
.

Proof.

The first equality is a direct consequence of
Proposition 2.7
. The second one is
Proposition 2.5
.
∎

Proposition 2.9

(
cf.

[
CLM+22
, Lemma 2.4.3]
)
.

Let

be a

-completely flat morphism between perfectoid rings. Let

.
Then,

(\mathfrak{a}S)\perfd=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left((h_{i}R)\perfd S\right)^{-}=((\mathfrak{a}R)\perfd S)^{-}.

Proof.

By

, it suffices to show this in the case

is principal, generated by

[

, Lemma 2.4.3]

∎

In the setting of

Remark 2.14

Since

Remark 2.15

In

Let

Lemma 2.17

Let
R
be a reduced local ring of positive characteristic
p>0
and assume that it is
F
-finite. Let
R\perf
be the perfection of

i.e.
R\perf=\bigcup_{e}R^{1/{p^{e}}}
. Let

,
Then, the map
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }F^{e}_{*}R

1\mapsto F^{e}_{*}f

is split if and only if there is a map

sending

to

Proof.

The backwards direction is straightforward as one can just restrict said splitting to

to get a splitting of
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }R^{1/{p^{e}}}\cong F^{e}_{*}R
. Suppose, then, that
R
is
e
-th Frobenius split along

, say by
\psi_{1}\colon R^{1/{p^{e}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }R
and let
\phi\coloneqq\psi_{1}\circ\left(\cdot f^{1/{p^{e}}}\right)\colon R^{1/{p^{e}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }R
. This is a splitting of the inclusion map
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }R^{1/{p^{e}}}
(see e.g.
[
SS24
, Proposition 1.4.6]
). We define
\psi\colon R\perf\xrightarrow{\ \ }R
as follows: for

, let

\displaystyle\psi_{j}\colon R^{1/p^{ej}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \ }R^{1/p^{e(j-1)}}

\displaystyle r^{1/p^{ej}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\phi\left(r^{1/{p^{e}}}\right)\right)^{1/p^{e(j-1)}}

and

\psi\colon r^{1/p^{ei}}\mapsto\psi_{1}\circ\psi_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}\left(r^{1/p^{ei}}\right).

We need to show that it is well defined

that
\psi\left(\left(r^{p^{e}}\right)^{1/p^{ei}}\right)=\psi\left(r^{1/p^{e\left(i-1\right)}}\right)

\psi_{i}\left(\left(r^{p^{e}}\right)^{1/p^{ei}}\right)=\phi\left(\left(r^{p^{e}}\right)^{1/p^{e}}\right)^{1/p^{e(i-1)}}=\phi(r)^{1/p^{e(i-1)}}=(r\phi(1))^{1/p^{e(i-1)}}

We also have that

is an

-module homomorphism as it is the composition of

∎

Proposition 2.20

\textstyle{R^{A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{R/\mathfrak{a}.}

Moreover, there is a bijection between the

5.
Normality

In this section, we show that the conductor ideal is uniformly perfectoid compatible, implying that the presence of compatible ideals detects (the failure of) normality. We use this to deduce various properties of perfectoid pure rings.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
R
and
A
be as in
Notation 2.10
and
R^{N}
be the normalization of
R
. Let
\mathfrak{c}
be the conductor ideal of

\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}

R^{A}_{\infty}

(R^{N}\otimes_{A}A_{\infty})\perfd\eqqcolon R^{N,A}_{\infty}

Proof.

We first show that
R\otimes_{A}A_{\infty},\mathfrak{c}\otimes_{A}A_{\infty}
, and
R^{N}\otimes_{A}A_{\infty}
are all derived
p
-complete
A_{\infty}
-modules. Note that

,
\mathfrak{c}
, and
R^{N}
are all finitely presented

-modules since

is excellent hence N-1 and Noetherian. This implies that
R\otimes_{A}A_{\infty},R^{N}\otimes_{A}A_{\infty},
and
\mathfrak{c}\otimes_{A}A_{\infty}
are all finitely presented
A_{\infty}
-modules. Since a finitely presented module over a derived
p
-complete ring is derived
p
-complete (see, e.g.,
[
Ked24
, Corollary 6.3.2]
), we are done.
Now, by
[
BS22
, Corollary 8.12]
, we have a pullback diagram

\textstyle{R^{A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R^{N,A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{((R/\mathfrak{c})\otimes_{A}A_{\infty})\perfd\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\left(\left(R^{N}/\mathfrak{c}\right)\otimes_{A}A_{\infty}\right)\perfd}

which we claim implies that
\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}
is both an ideal of
R^{A}_{\infty}
and
R^{N,A}_{\infty}
. Indeed, the diagram can be rewritten as

\textstyle{R^{A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R^{N,A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{R^{A}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{(R^{N}/\mathfrak{c})^{A}_{\infty}.}

Since this is a pullback diagram, we have that

R^{A}_{\infty}=\left\{(x,y)\in R^{A}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}\times R^{N,A}_{\infty}\mid i(x)=\pi(y)\right\}

where the map
R^{A}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\ \ }R^{A}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}
is the projection onto the first coordinate.
In particular,

\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}=\left\{(0,y)\in R^{A}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}\times R^{N,A}_{\infty}\mid\pi(y)=0\right\}.

Then, the image of
\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}
in
R^{N,A}_{\infty}
is exactly the kernel of the quotient
R^{N,A}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\ \ }(R^{N}/\mathfrak{c})^{A}_{\infty}
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 5.2

(
cf.

[
BK05
, Exercise 1.2.4(E)]
)
.

Let
(R,\phi)
be a pair and
\mathfrak{c}\subset R
be the conductor ideal. Then,
\mathfrak{c}
is
\phi
-compatible
for all maps
\phi\in\Hom_{R}(R^{A}_{\infty},R)
. In particular, if there are no non-trivial
\phi
-compatible ideals,

Proof.

By

, if
s\in R^{N}
then
s\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}\subset\mathfrak{c}^{A}_{\infty}
where we abuse notation and think about all these elements as part of

and

Remark 5.3
.

Note that this is not dependent on the choice of
\phi
(nor on the choice of

) so the conductor is uniformly perfectoid compatible. In particular, if

has no uniformly perfectoid compatible ideals then it is normal.

Remark 5.4
.

It is well known that

\mathfrak{c}=\Im\left(\Hom_{R}\left(R^{N},R\right)\xrightarrow{\phi\mapsto\phi(1)}R\right).

This is a specific case of a trace ideal, which are known to be compatible in positive characteristic (see for instance
[
PS23
, Lemma 2.2]
). It is a natural question to ask if this is also true in mixed characteristic. That is, if
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
is a finite extension of complete local Noetherian rings, is

\Im\left(\Hom_{R}(S,R)\xrightarrow{\phi\mapsto\phi(1)}R\right)

a uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal of

? Unfortunately, it is not true in this generality. Indeed, let
R\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket/x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}
with
p\equiv 1\,(\textnormal{mod}\,3)
. This is a perfectoid pure ring by
[
BMP+24a
, Example 7.3]
since going modulo
p
is the
(x,y,z)
-adic completion of the cone over an ordinary elliptic curve. Then, all the uniformly perfectoid compatible ideals have to be radical by
Corollary 2.33
. Let
S=R
with map
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
induced by multiplication by
p^{n}
on the elliptic curve.
By
[
BMP+23
, Example 4.14]
, the image of the trace map is the ideal
(p^{n},x,y,z)
which is not radical and therefore not compatible. In fact, even its radical,
(p,x,y,z)
, is not uniformly perfectoid compatible. Indeed, since
R/p
is
F
-pure, the perfectoid pure threshold of
(R,\Div(p))
is
1
by
[

, Remark 2.10]
. In particular, there is a perfectoid
R
-algebra

such that the map
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
,
1\mapsto p
is pure so
p
is not in any uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal.

Corollary 5.5

(

[

[

Let

Let

6.
Splitting Prime

In this section, we give an explicit description of the largest uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal of a ring
R
and show that it detects the perfectoid purity of
R
, analogously to the splitting prime of Aberbach and Enescu
[
AE05
]
. We also generalize the idea to get a compatible core of an ideal, analogous to the Cartier core of
[
Bad21
,
Bro23
,
CRF24
]
.

6.1.
The positive characteristic case

We first start by expressing the positive characteristic splitting prime in terms of the perfection of
R
.

Definition 6.1

(
[
AE05
]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of char
p>0
. Suppose further that
R
is
F
-finite and let
\phi\in\Hom_{R}\left(R^{1/{p^{e}}},R\right)
. The splitting prime of
(R,\phi)
is

\upbeta(R,\phi)=\bigcap_{n>0}\left\{r\in R\mid\phi^{n}\left(r^{1/p^{en}}R^{1/p^{en}}\right)\subset\mathfrak{m}\right\}

where
\phi^{n}
is defined as the composition

R^{1/p^{en}}\xrightarrow{\phi^{1/p^{e(n-1)}}}R^{1/p^{e(n-1)}}\xrightarrow{\phi^{1/p^{e(n-2)}}}R^{1/p^{e(n-2)}}\xrightarrow{\phi^{1/p^{e(n-3)}}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\phi}R.

In the special case
\phi(1)=1
, we are able to express
\upbeta(R,\phi)
in terms of
R\perf
. Indeed,

\upbeta(R,\phi)=\left\{r\in R\Biggm|\bigcup_{n>0}\phi^{n}\bigg(r^{1/p^{en}}R^{1/p^{en}}\bigg)\subset\mathfrak{m}\right\}.

Moreover, since

(r)\perf=\bigcup_{n>0}r^{1/p^{en}}R^{1/p^{en}},

letting
\psi
be the map from the proof of
Lemma 2.17
constructed only from
\phi
, we have

\upbeta(R,\phi)=\left\{r\in R\mid\psi((r)\perf)\subset\mathfrak{m}\right\}.

6.2.
The mixed characteristic case

Let
(R,\phi)
be a pair. The above discussion would lead us to try to define the splitting prime of
(R,\phi)
as

\upbeta(R,\phi)\coloneqq\left\{r\in R\mid\phi\left((r)_{\infty}^{A}\right)\subset\mathfrak{m}\right\},

at least when
\phi(1)=1
.
Unfortunately, it is not clear to the author whether such an ideal is
\phi
-compatible. This brings us to our actual definition.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
(R,\phi)
be a pair. Let
\upbeta_{0}(R,\phi)\coloneqq\mathfrak{m}
and

\upbeta_{i}(R,\phi)\coloneqq\left\{r\in R\mid\phi\left((r)^{A}_{\infty}\right)\subset\upbeta_{i-1}\right\}

for
i>0
. We then define the splitting prime
\upbeta(R,\phi)
as

\upbeta(R,\phi)\coloneqq\bigcap_{i>0}\upbeta_{i}(R,\phi).

Remark 6.3
.

When
\phi
is surjective, we can show that
\upbeta_{i}(R,\phi)\supset\upbeta_{i+1}(R,\phi)
: let
r\notin\upbeta_{i}(R,\phi)
and

Theorem 7.1
.

[
BS22

Now, let
(R,\phi)
be as in

and

by the universal properties of perfectoidization and tensor product. This gives us the following setting.

Let

We have a commutative diagram

(7.2.1)

\textstyle{S^{A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{S}
\textstyle{R^{A}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec S
such that
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{p}}(R,\phi)\subset\mathfrak{q}\cap R
. We want to show that
\upbeta_{\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}S}}(S,\psi)\subset\mathfrak{q}
. By going-down, there is
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{q}
lying over
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{p}}
and by going up there is
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}\supset\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
so that
\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}S}\subset\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}
. We have

\displaystyle\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}}(S,\psi)\cap R=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}\cap R}(R,\phi)=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{p}}(R,\phi)=\upbeta_{\upbeta_{\mathfrak{p}}(R,\phi)}(R,\phi)

\displaystyle=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\cap R}(R,\phi)

\displaystyle=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}(S,\psi)\cap R.

Since
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}
,
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}(S,\psi)\subset\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}}(S,\psi)
so by incomparability these must be equal.
Using now that
\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}S}\subset\mathfrak{q}^{\prime\prime}
, we get
\upbeta_{\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}S}}(S,\psi)\subset\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{q}
, which is what we wanted.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
(R,\phi)\xrightarrow{\ \ }(S,\psi)
be as in
Setting 7.2
and assume further that they are domains. Let
\uptau(R,\phi),\uptau(S,\psi)
be the respective nonzero smallest compatible ideals. If
\phi
is surjective, then
\uptau(R,\phi)=\uptau(S,\psi)\cap R
and
\uptau(R,\phi)S=\uptau(S,\psi)
.

Proof.

\uptau(S,\psi)\cap R
is compatible so it contains
\uptau(R,\phi)
. Extending to
(S,\psi)
gives

\uptau(R,\phi)S\subset(\uptau(S,\psi)\cap R)S\subset\uptau(S,\psi)

but since these are all compatible ideals, we must have

\uptau(R,\phi)S\supset\uptau(S,\psi)

so this is an equality.

To show that
\uptau(R,\phi)=\uptau(S,\psi)\cap R
, note that
\upbeta_{R}^{-1}(0_{R})\subset\upbeta_{S}^{-1}(0_{S})\cap R
. Indeed, let
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec S
be such that
\mathfrak{q}\cap R=\mathfrak{p}
with
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{p}}(R,\phi)=0
. By
Proposition 7.4
,

\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}}(S,\psi)\cap R=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}\cap R}(R,\phi)=0
so
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{q}}(S,\psi)=0
.
Then, by
Proposition 6.12
,
V(\uptau(R,\phi))\subset V(\uptau(S,\psi))\cap R
which implies
\uptau(S,\psi)\cap R\subset\uptau(R,\phi)
. Since the other inclusion is automatic,
\uptau(S,\psi)\cap R=\uptau(R,\phi)
.
∎

As usual, these also hold for uniformly perfectoid compatible ideals.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
be as in
Setting 7.2
and assume
R
(equivalently
S
) is perfectoid pure. Let
\mathfrak{b}\subset S
be a radical ideal. Then,
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}\cap R}(R)
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
is a radical ideal then,
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)S=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a}S}(S)
.

Proof.

Take any unramified regular local ring
A
such that
R
(and therefore
S
) is a module finite
A
-algebra. Let
\mathfrak{b}\subset S
be a radical ideal and let
\mathfrak{a}\coloneqq\mathfrak{b}\cap R
. Then,
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R
is a uniformly perfectoid compatible ideal of
R
: if
\phi\in\Hom_{R}(R^{A}_{\infty},R)
then
\phi\otimes_{R}S\in\Hom_{S}(S^{A}_{\infty},S)
and

\phi\left(\left(\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R\right)^{A}_{\infty}\right)\subset\left(\phi\left(\left(\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R\right)^{A}_{\infty}\right)\otimes_{R}S\right)\cap R\subset\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R

This shows
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R\subset\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
. For the other inclusion, we proceed by induction. Let
r\in\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a},1}(R)
. Then, for any

the composition

\Hom_{R}\left(R^{A}_{\infty},R\right)\xlongrightarrow{\Hom_{R}(\times x,R)}\Hom_{R}\left(R^{A}_{\infty},R\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,R)\cong R\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\mathfrak{a}

is zero. Tensoring with
S
and using the fact that
\mathfrak{a}S\subset\mathfrak{b}
, we get that the composition

\Hom_{S}\left(S^{A}_{\infty},S\right)\xlongrightarrow{\Hom_{S}(\times x,S)}\Hom_{S}\left(S^{A}_{\infty},S\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{S}(S,S)\cong S\xrightarrow{\ \ }S/\mathfrak{b}

is zero. Precomposing with multiplication by any element of
S^{A}_{\infty}
on
S^{A}_{\infty}
would keep the composition
0
so for any
\psi\in\Hom_{S}(S^{A}_{\infty},S)
,
\psi((rS)^{A}_{\infty})\subset\mathfrak{b}
hence
r\in\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b},1}(S)\cap R
. Now, suppose that we know that
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a},i}(R)\subset\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b},i}(S)\cap R
and let
r\in\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a},i+1}(R)
and
x\in(r)^{A,R}_{\infty}
.
The composition

\Hom_{R}\left(R^{A}_{\infty},R\right)\xlongrightarrow{\Hom_{R}(\times x,R)}\Hom_{R}\left(R^{A}_{\infty},R\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,R)\cong R\xrightarrow{\ \ }R/\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a},i}(R)

is zero so tensoring with
S
and using the fact that
\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a},i}(R)S\subset\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b},i}(S)
, we get that the composition

\Hom_{S}\left(S^{A}_{\infty},S\right)\xlongrightarrow{\Hom_{S}(\times x,S)}\Hom_{S}\left(S^{A}_{\infty},S\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{S}(S,S)\cong S\xrightarrow{\ \ }S/\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b},i}(S)

is zero. Precomposing with multiplication by any element of
S^{A}_{\infty}
on
S^{A}_{\infty}

. Then, for any

\upbeta_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)=\upbeta_{\mathfrak{b}}(S)\cap R
.
The implication about
\upbeta
under extensions follows as in

.
∎

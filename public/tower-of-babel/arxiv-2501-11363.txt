Boundedness of diffeomorphism groups of manifold pairs — Circle case —
Kazuhiko Fukui, Tatsuhiko Yagasaki

Abstract
. In this paper we study boundedness of conjugation invariant norms on diffeomorphism groups of manifold pairs. For the diffeomorphism group 𝒟 ≡ Diff ​ ( M , N ) 0 {\mathcal{D}}\equiv{\rm Diff}(M,N)_{0} of a closed manifold pair ( M , N ) (M,N) with dim N ≥ 1 \dim N\geq 1 , first we clarify the relation among the fragmentation norm, the conjugation generated norm, the commutator length c ​ l cl and the commutator length with support in balls c ​ l ​ b clb and show that 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} is weakly simple relative to a union of some normal subgroups of 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} . For the boundedness of these norms, this paper focuses on the case where N N is a union of m m circles. In this case, the rotation angle on N N induces a quasimorphism ν : Isot ​ ( M , N ) 0 → ℝ m \nu:{\rm Isot}(M,N)_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m} , which determines a subgroup A A of ℤ m {\mathbb{Z}}^{m} and a function ν ^ : 𝒟 → ℝ m / A \widehat{\nu}:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}/A . If rank ​ A = m {\rm rank}\,A=m , these data leads to an upper bound of c ​ l ​ b clb on 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} modulo the normal subgroup 𝒢 ≅ Diff c ​ ( M − N ) 0 {\mathcal{G}}\cong{\rm Diff}_{c}(M-N)_{0} . Then, some upper bounds of c ​ l cl and c ​ l ​ b clb on 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} are obtained from those on 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} . As a consequence, the group 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} is uniformly weakly simple and bounded when dim M ≠ 2 , 4 \dim M\neq 2,4 . On the other hand, if rank ​ A < m {\rm rank}\,A<m , then the group 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} admits a surjective quasimorphism, so it is unbounded and not uniformly perfect. We examine the group A A in some explicit examples.

3.1.
Basic notions

For sets

For any set

.

\varphi
and
\widetilde{\varphi}
and sometimes denote
\widetilde{\varphi}
by the same symbol
\varphi

.

For a topological space

and

. For topological spaces

and

the symbol

denotes the set of
C^{0}
maps

.
The symbol

always denotes the interval

. The set of
C^{0}
homotopies is denoted by

n

5.
Quasimorphisms induced from the rotation angle on a cricle

5.1.
Rotation angle on a circle

Suppose
S
is a
C^{r}
circle
(r\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
.
To introduce a signed angle on
S
with the total angle
=
1, we fix a
C^{r}
diffeomorphism
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\approx S
.
This determines a commutative
C^{r}
group structure
+
on
S
.
The
C^{r}
universal covering
\pi_{S}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\approx S
is a group epimorphism.
For any
C^{r}
manifold
X
the space
C^{r}(X,S)
forms a commutative
C^{r}
group under the sum of maps
(\varphi+\psi)(x)=\varphi(x)+\psi(x)

(x\in X)
.

Notation 5.1
.

(1)

Each
a\in S
induces the translation
\theta_{a}(x)=x+a
on
S
.
We call these translations the rotations on
S
.
We have the subgroup
L_{S}=\{\theta_{a}\mid a\in S\}
of
{\rm Diff}^{r}(S)_{0}
and
the
C^{r}
group isomorphism
\theta:S\to L_{S}:a\mapsto\theta_{a}
.

(2)

For
a\in S
let
\varepsilon_{a}\in C^{r}(X,S)
denote the constant map
\varepsilon_{a}(x)=a

(x\in X)
.
We set
\varphi+a:=\varphi+\varepsilon_{a}=\theta_{a}\circ\varphi
for
\varphi\in C^{r}(X,S)
.

(3)

The path space
{\mathcal{P}}(S)=C^{0}(I,S)
forms a group.
We have
\alpha+\beta
,
-\alpha
,
\alpha+a\equiv\theta_{a}\circ\alpha
,
\varepsilon_{a}
, etc. for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathcal{P}}(S)
and
a\in S
.
For each
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
we define
\delta_{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{r}(S)_{0}
by
\delta_{\sigma}(t)=\pi_{S}(\sigma t)

(t\in I)
.

Definition 5.1
.

Each
C^{0}
path
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(S)
has a lift
\widetilde{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}})
(i.e.,
\pi_{S}\widetilde{\gamma}=\gamma
).
The rotation angle of
\gamma
is defined by
\lambda_{S}(\gamma)=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)-\widetilde{\gamma}(0)
,
which is independent of the choice of the lift
\widetilde{\gamma}
.

The function
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{S}:{\mathcal{P}}(S)\to{\mathbb{R}}
has the following properties.

Fact 5.1
.

Suppose
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(S)
.

(1)

(i)

\lambda(\alpha\ast\beta)=\lambda(\alpha)+\lambda(\beta)
if
\alpha(1)=\beta(0)
.

(ii)

\lambda(\gamma_{-})=-\lambda(\gamma)
.    (iii)
\lambda(\alpha+a)=\lambda(\alpha)

(a\in S)
.

(2)

(i)

\alpha\simeq_{\ast}\beta
iff
\alpha(0)=\beta(0)
and
\lambda(\alpha)=\lambda(\beta)
.

(ii)

\lambda(\gamma_{\sigma})=\lambda(\gamma)
for any
\sigma\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(I,I)
with
\sigma(0)=0
and
\sigma(1)=1
.

(3)

(i)

\lambda:{\mathcal{P}}(S)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a group epimorphism.

(ii)

The map
\delta:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathcal{P}}^{r}(S)_{0}
:
\sigma\mapsto\delta_{\sigma}
is
a group monomorphism and
\lambda(\delta_{\sigma})=\sigma
.

(4)

If
\gamma\in\Omega(S)
(i.e.,
\gamma
is a closed path (
\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)
)), then

(i)

\lambda(\gamma)=\deg\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(ii)
\lambda(f\gamma)=(\deg f)\,\lambda(\gamma)
for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(S,S)
.

(5)

|\lambda(f\gamma)-\lambda(\gamma)|<1
for any
f\in{\rm Diff}^{0}(S)_{0}={\rm Diff}^{0}_{+}(S)
.

Proof.

(3)

(i) Since
\alpha+\beta\simeq_{\ast}(\alpha\ast\alpha(1))+(\beta(0)\ast\beta)=(\alpha+\beta(0))\ast(\beta+\alpha(1))
, it follows that

\lambda(\alpha+\beta)=\lambda((\alpha+\beta(0))\ast(\beta+\alpha(1)))=\lambda(\alpha+\beta(0))+\lambda(\beta+\alpha(1))=\lambda(\alpha)+\lambda(\beta)

(5)

Take a lift
\widetilde{f}\in{\rm Diff}^{0}({\mathbb{R}})_{0}={\rm Diff}^{0}_{+}({\mathbb{R}})
of
f
and a lift
\widetilde{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}})
of
\gamma
.
It follows that

(a)

\widetilde{f}
is monotonically increasing and
\widetilde{f}(x+1)=\widetilde{f}(x)+1

(x\in{\mathbb{R}})
and

(b)

\widetilde{f}\widetilde{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}})
is a lift of
f\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(S)
so that

\lambda(f\gamma)=\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\gamma}(1))-\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\gamma}(0))
.

Let
n:=\lfloor{\lambda(\gamma)}\rfloor\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then,
\widetilde{\gamma}(1)=\widetilde{\gamma}(0)+\lambda(\gamma)\in\widetilde{\gamma}(0)+[n,n+1)
and

\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\gamma}(1))\in\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\gamma}(0))+[n,n+1)
by (a).
Hence,
\lambda(\gamma),\lambda(f\gamma)\in[n,n+1)
so that
|\lambda(f\gamma)-\lambda(\gamma)|<1
.

∎

Remark 5.1
.

If
f\in C^{0}(S,S)
and
{\rm deg}\,f=n
, then any lift
\widetilde{f}\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
of
f

satisfies
\widetilde{f}(t+1)=\widetilde{f}(t)+n

(t\in{\mathbb{R}})
.
In particular, if
{\rm deg}\,f=1
(i.e.,
f
is an orientation-preserving homotopy equivalence), then
\widetilde{f}(t+1)=\widetilde{f}(t)+1
.
However,
|\lambda(f\gamma)-\lambda(\gamma)|
may take arbitrarily large value if

(i.e., the lift
\widetilde{f}
is not monotonically increasing).

(0,1)\in A
. Hence,
A={\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(3)

m\geq 3
:

The
C^{\infty}

{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
action
\rho
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
induces the orbit map
\rho_{p_{i}}:{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\approx S_{i}
of degree 1 for each
i\in[m]
.
Hence,
(1,\cdots,1)\in A
by Lemma
7.1
.

It remains to show that
\nu_{i}(F)=\nu_{j}(F)

(i,j\in[m],i\neq j)
for any
F\in{\rm Isot}^{r}({\mathbb{S}}^{3},L^{(m)})_{\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id}}
.
We apply Lemma
7.4
to the pair
({\mathbb{S}}^{3},S_{i}\cup S_{j})\in{\mathcal{C}}(3,2)
.
Take an index
\ell\in[m]-\{i,j\}
and let

.
Recall the annulus
E_{ij}:=\pi^{-1}(C_{ij})
.
The circles
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
are parallel with respect to
E_{ij}
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
and
E_{ij}\subset N
since

The inclusion

,
since

{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
and

Since

F(E_{ij}\times I)\subset F(N\times I)=N
.
Therefore,
\nu_{i}(F)=\nu_{j}(F)
by Lemma
7.4
.

∎

Spatial transcriptomics unveils the in situ cellular and molecular hallmarks of the lung in fatal COVID-19
Authors: Carlos A. Garcia-Prieto, Eva Musulen, Daniela Grases, Veronica Davalos, Gerardo Ferrer, Belén Pérez-Miés, Tamara Caniego-Casas, José Palacios, Xavier Saenz-Sardà, Elisabet Englund, Eduard Porta, Manel Esteller
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Severe Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) can induce progressive diffuse alveolar damage (DAD) leading to fatal outcomes. Here, we leveraged data from Visium spatial transcriptomics for COVID-19 and control lung samples to unravel insights into cellular and molecular events driving lethal COVID-19. We report a progressive loss of endothelial cell types, pneumocytes type I and natural killer cells coupled with a continuous increase of myeloid and stromal cells, mostly peribronchial fibroblasts, across acute and proliferative DAD. Spatial organization analysis also identified specific compartmentalization including immune-specific clusters across DAD patterns. Importantly, spatially informed ligand-receptor interaction analysis revealed intercellular communication signatures defining COVID-19 induced DAD. Transcription factor activity enrichment analysis identified the TGF-β pathway as DAD driver, highlighting the antagonizing roles of SMAD3 and SMAD7 activity during fibrosis. Integration of these data yielded a signaling kinase pathway in peribronchial fibroblasts with amenable novel targets. Finally, spatio-temporal trajectory analysis characterized an alveolar epithelium regeneration program.

Introduction

Infection by the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) virus caused a worldwide pandemic of the derived coronavirus disease 2019, COVID-19. Beyond 775 million confirmed cases and more than 7 million deaths have been reported (https://data.who.int/dashboards/covid19/). Although most affected individuals exhibit mild clinical manifestations or are asymptomatic, respiratory failure linked to lung damage and acute respiratory distress syndrome could occur, being the most common cause of death1,2. Importantly, the maintenance of lung lesions in a subgroup of COVID-19 patients could also be associated with prolonged clinical manifestations3,4. Upon SARS-CoV-2 infection in the severe cases, the pathological change is defined by diffuse alveolar damage (DAD)5 that initiates with an acute stage of early intra-alveolar epithelial lesions, interstitial inflammation and edema, followed by the proliferative stage with a final appearance of pneumocyte hyperplasia and fibroblast proliferation1,2.

The molecular context of lung damage provoked by SARS-CoV-2 infection is not fully established. Most studies have addressed the bulk transcription landscape6–8 or a particular lineage such as immune cells9,10. A more granulated view of the lung affected by severe COVID-19 has been gained by single-cell gene expression profiles11–13. However, all these approaches destroy the anatomical structure of the lung and the spatial cell-cell interactions. Only a limited number of studies have analyzed a spatial component in the lung of COVID-19 cases, using high-parameter imaging mass cytometry for targeted proteins14 or focusing on regions of interest (ROI)15–18. Importantly, larger pathological scrutiny of spatial transcriptomics in COVID-19 patients has only been performed recently19.

To overcome these issues, we leveraged the recently developed Visium spatial transcriptomics (VisiumST) technology in a cohort of lungs with normal histology and those that underwent DAD during the course of fatal COVID-19. Using the single-cell RNA expression data of a human cell atlas of the lung to annotate cell types13, we provide the constellation of shifts in forty-five cell types, the perturbations in cell-to-cell communications and the spatial alterations of molecular pathways that occur upon severe COVID-19.

Results

Identification of lung cell types and cellular compartments in COVID-19 associated DAD histological patterns

To assess how fatal COVID-19 affected cell type composition, cell-cell communication, and global expression patterns across acute and proliferative DAD, we followed the study design shown in Fig. 1A. We first retrieved twenty-three formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded post-mortem lung tissue samples obtained from nineteen patients with DAD, corresponding to seven cases of acute DAD and twelve proliferative DAD, and four lung samples from control lungs with normal morphological appearance without SARS-CoV-2 infection (Table 1). We then analyzed the spatial transcriptomics patterns using tissue spots on a microarray slide with arrayed oligonucleotides to capture spatial gene expression information following the final generation of next generation sequencing (NGS) libraries20, adapted by 10× Genomics as “10× Visium” (VisiumST) (“Methods”). In total, 91,068 tissue spots were studied after quality control (QC) and preprocessing (“Methods”). To assess the spatial organization of cell types across tissue slides, we used the integrated Human Lung Cell Atlas (HLCA)13 as a reference to deconvolute the main cell types present in each spot by applying the validated cell2location pipeline21. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) using the integrated global spatial transcriptome data showing the spots colored by our three groups of lung samples (control, acute DAD, and proliferative DAD) distinguished these three entities (Fig. 1B). Using cell2location, we annotated forty-five cell types in HLCA defined by derived markers (“Methods”) in our lung samples (Fig. S1). A UMAP visualization of these cell populations according to their lineage (epithelial, stroma, immune and endothelial) in the spatial transcriptomics spots for all the integrated samples and illustrative examples are shown in Fig. 1B. Spots from different donors were well intermixed within each DAD pattern in the same embedding, indicating limited donor-driven batch effects across the integrated dataset (Fig. S2). The mapping of the identified cell types on top of the VisiumST brightfield images stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) for the described lineages in illustrative control, acute and proliferative DAD cases are shown in Fig. 1C. Cell types mapped to their expected locations, matching well-described structures, with epithelial cells lining the airway lumen and stromal cells mapping to blood vessel walls, as validated by endothelial (CD34) and epithelial (CK7) markers immunostaining (Fig. 1C).

Overall, we observed that in control lungs the most abundant lineage corresponded to endothelial cell types, as previously described22, and that acute DAD was characterized by a relative decrease in endothelial cell proportions, despite endothelial cells remaining the predominant lineage, together with increased immune and stromal cell proportions, whereas proliferative DAD was mostly defined by the large proportion of fibrotic tissue, as previously described1,2 (Fig. 1D). While changes were observed across histological patterns, only the reduction of endothelial cells in proliferative DAD compared to controls exhibited a significant credible shift (false discovery rate <0.05) based on compositional data analysis (“Methods”). These findings were validated using HE-stained sections and specific immunohistochemistry (IHC) markers such as CD34 (endothelial), CD68 (myeloid lineage) and CK7 (epithelial markers) and trichrome staining (fibroblasts) (Fig. 1E).

We then moved to characterize a possible uneven distribution of the identified 45 cell types according to their abundance in the control, acute and proliferative DAD lung groups (Fig. 2A, B). We observed highly consistent cell type abundance profiles across individual samples within each DAD pattern (Fig. S2), justifying grouped comparisons. To ease interpretation, we ordered these cell populations according to their lineage (epithelial, stromal, immune, and endothelial) (Fig. 2A). Among the epithelial lineage (10 identified cell types), the most important difference was observed in alveolar type 1 (AT1) cells, comprising between 5% and 10% of total cells in normal lungs as previously reported23, that were significantly downregulated in COVID-19 associated proliferative DAD cases in comparison to controls and acute DAD lungs (Fig. 2A, B). The reverse process was observed in alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells that were upregulated in proliferative cases in comparison to acute DAD and control samples (Fig. 2A, B). These results fit with the concept that AT1s are the main cell type responsible for the provision of the interface for the blood-gas exchange (a function that is compromised in COVID-19 patients), whereas AT2 cells function as progenitors that repair the injured alveolar epithelium24. Regarding the endothelial lineage (7 identified cell types), we observed that capillary cell types were the most abundant of lung cells (~30% of total cells), as previously reported22,23, and that the studied lung samples underwent a progressive loss from normal lung to acute DAD to proliferative DAD for the abundance of endothelial cells (EC) aerocyte capillary, arterial, general capillary and venous pulmonary (Fig. 2A, B). These results support the evidence linking SARS-CoV-2 infection to endothelial dysfunction25. For the immune lineage (19 identified cell populations), we observed in the myeloid lineage that alveolar macrophages (Mph), CCL3+ and monocyte-derived Mph were significantly overrepresented in COVID-19-associated proliferative DAD cases in comparison to controls and acute DAD lungs (Fig. 2A, B). Interstitial Mph perivascular showed a progressive increase. In the lymphoid lineage, a decrease in CD4 and CD8 T-cells was observed in proliferative COVID-19 patients in comparison to the control group (Fig. 2A, B). Interestingly, Natural Killer (NK) cells experienced a significant decrease from control and acute DAD samples to proliferative DAD cases (Fig. 2A, B). In this regard, these innate effector lymphocytes that respond to acute viral infections have been previously related to COVID-19 severity26. Additionally, both alveolar macrophages and plasma cells were overrepresented in proliferative DAD lungs compared to control samples. Finally, regarding the stromal lineage (9 cell types), the most dramatic change was observed for peribronchial fibroblasts (PBFs) that increased from control to acute DAD, skyrocketing in proliferative DAD cases (Fig. 2A, B). Not all fibroblast subtypes behaved in a similar manner. Subpleural fibroblasts were overrepresented in COVID-19-associated DAD fatal cases compared to the control group and between acute and proliferative DAD, whereas alveolar fibroblasts and pericytes decreased in the proliferative DAD samples compared to control and acute DAD lungs. Lastly, adventitial fibroblasts were overrepresented in proliferative DAD samples compared to controls. To assess the spatial distribution of the identified forty-five cell types within neighboring compartments, we applied the cell2location algorithm to the VisiumST data. To investigate spatial patterns of co-localization among these cell types, we performed non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) on cell type abundance estimates (“Methods”). This unsupervised dimensionality reduction technique enabled us to uncover recurrent modules of co-occurring cell types by identifying eight distinct NMF components, or factors, that captured the underlying cellular neighborhood structure, referred to as spatial compartments, across the tissue. For normal lung, the characterized cell types mapped within physiological cellular microenvironments such as the great compartment defined by immune and endothelial cells, one rich in epithelial cells (excluding AT2 proliferating, suprabasal and deuterosomal cells that shared a common location), another related to smooth muscle related cells and the fibroblast lineage (where PBFs resided in an isolated compartment) (Fig. 2C). This compartmentalization underwent an abrupt shift across DAD histological patterns. The acute DAD was characterized by a recruitment of an enriched AT2 proliferating population to the epithelial compartment; the irruption of a spatial cluster of macrophage subtypes and type-2 dendritic cells (DC2s, that promote cytotoxic T-cell responses and helper T-cell differentiation); and the appearance of plasma cells in an isolated population (Fig. 2C). Most of these cellular redistributions underwent further compartmentalization in the proliferative DAD pattern that additionally exhibited the emergence of a unique compartment for lymphatic mature endothelial cells (Fig. 2C).

To further analyze and characterize tissue architecture differences, we applied GraphCompass (Graph Comparison Tools for Differential Analyses in Spatial Systems)27 (“Methods”), a set of graph-analysis methods for “omics” data to quantitatively determine and compare spatial arrangement of distinct cell types among different biological conditions successfully applied to VisiumST data27. The cell-type-specific subgraphs across condition pairs, where the size of the dot indicates the pairwise similarity score variances (“Methods”), are shown in Fig. 2D. This analysis quantified how the spatial arrangement of each cell type changed across DAD patterns. Cell types such as PBFs, alveolar fibroblasts, AT2 cells, EC aerocyte capillary cells, and monocyte-derived Mphs exhibited high variance in spatial similarity scores, indicating pronounced remodeling of their spatial architecture, especially during proliferative DAD (Fig. 2D). Among the distinct cell types identified across COVID-19 samples, we further examined PBFs, EC aerocyte capillary cells, and AT2 cells to quantify how their spatial organization changed across conditions. Using filtration curves27, we evaluated how spatially connected spots containing each cell type were, based on increasing distance thresholds (“Methods”). These curves reflect the growth of the largest connected cluster per sample, with right-shifted curves indicating tighter spatial clustering and left-shifted curves suggesting more dispersed distributions. In proliferative DAD, PBFs and AT2 cells exhibited stronger spatial clustering, while capillary endothelial cells showed more fragmented, dispersed patterns, consistent with their depletion across acute and proliferative DAD (Fig. 2E). These analyses reinforced the findings that these cell types underwent antiparallel shifts in their abundance: PBFs and AT2 cells exhibited an overrepresentation whereas endothelial aerocyte capillary cells were depleted, particularly at proliferative DAD (Fig. 2E).

Spatial cell-cell interactions in COVID-19-associated DAD

One of the most exciting applications of spatial transcriptomics is the potential to analyze cell-cell communication (CCC). CCC is a multicellular and complex process involving multiple mechanisms, including intercellular signaling and intracellular signaling as a downstream response. Related to the intercellular component, cells interact on diverse levels that include direct contact, such as between ligands and surface receptors, and through indirect means, such as the release of soluble factors. For single-cell analyses, the molecular profiles of sender and receiver cell types allow the inference of underlying cell communication events in a tissue using co-occurrence of ligand and receptor (LR) expression among the candidate communicating cells28,29 and through gene expression profiles in the receiving cell type related to the extracellular interaction28,29. Herein, we used the VisiumST data to identify coordinated cell-cell communication signatures shared across all tissue slides by applying NMF to the estimated local (spot level) ligand-receptor interactions (LRIs), calculated using spatially-weighted Cosine similarity with LIANA+30,31 (“Methods”). Using the elbow selection procedure, we decomposed the local interactions into three Factors (1, 2 and 3) representing three different intercellular communication signatures. The NMF factor scores indicate the strength of each factor in each spot, representing the degree of influence by the associated signature. The averaged factor scores per tissue slide clustered according to lung status are shown in Fig. 3A. Importantly, Factor 3 distinguished the best between control and COVID-19-associated DAD lung tissues, with high mean scores in proliferative DAD (Fig. 3A). To provide further biological insight, we performed LRIs pathway enrichment analysis on the distinct interaction loadings contributing to the three factors (Fig. 3B) using multivariate linear regression32 and pathway annotations from PROGENy33 (“Methods”). We found that DAD-associated Factor 3 was significantly enriched in interactions related to the transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) pathway (Fig. 3B), a driver of fibrosis involved in response to tissue injury34. Conversely, DAD-associated Factor 3 was depleted for the EGFR pathway (Fig. 3B). Interestingly, the wingless-related integration site (WNT) pathway was enriched in Factor 1 (characteristic of the control samples) but depleted in Factor 2 (Fig. 3B).

Since Factor 3 was the most optimal discriminator between healthy and COVID-19-associated DAD, we mainly focused our analyses in this CCC readout. The top four LR loadings defining DAD associated Factor 3 were the interactions TIMP1^CD63, that promotes lung fibrosis mediated by the TGF-β1/SMAD3 pathway35; APP^CD74, highlighting the role of APP (Amyloid-beta precursor protein) as a lung capillary barrier defense during infection36; CD99^CD81, both regulators of T-cell and B-cell activity37; and LUM^ITGB1, with LUM being linked to extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and inflammation-associated fibroblasts38 (Supplementary Data file 1). Noteworthy additional LRIs involved PSAP (PSAP^LRP1) and members of the S100 protein family S100A8 (S100A8^AGER, S100A8^ITGB2) and S100A9 (S100A9^AGER, S100A9^CD68, S100A9^ITGB2), that have been reported to activate macrophages in COVID-1911,14; the SPARC protein (SPARC^ENG), a downstream effector of TGF-β upregulated in COVID-19-associated fibrosis11,39 and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)40; and vimentin (VIM^CD44), an attachment factor for SARS-CoV-2 entry into endothelial cells41. Beyond the mentioned ligand proteins, it is also relevant to mention that the three most frequent receptors involved in the LRIs (Supplementary Data file 1) were CD44, involved in T-cell abundance and fostering cytokine storm linked to COVID-19 poor prognosis42; ITGB1, that associates with the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) to mediate SARS-CoV-2 entry43 and regulates ECM remodeling; and LRP1, involved in the overproduction of cytokines and chemokines42, and enriched in mortal and long COVID-19 patients44,45. The NMF factor scores indicating the strength of each factor in each spot are depicted for illustrative examples of VisiumST slide images (Fig. 3C).

Regarding intracellular signaling analysis, we studied transcription factor (TF) activities, including their downstream transcriptional targets that shift across normal and COVID-19 samples. We estimated TF activity in each VisiumST spot based on multivariate linear regression using decoupleR32 and CollecTRI46 network containing a curated collection of TFs and their targets (“Methods”). Table S1 shows the TFs activity in each type of lung sample and Fig. 3D displays the top 10 scaled TF enrichment scores that discriminate each condition. These data highlight the critical role of TGF-β pathway across acute and proliferative DAD, as did also the intercellular LRI analysis (Fig. 3B). It is noteworthy to mention the opposite activity landscape of SMAD protein family members. Proliferative DAD is characterized by SMAD3 upregulation, a key mediator of TGF-β signaling to promote ECM production, tissue repair, fibrosis and scar formation47. Conversely, SMAD7 activity, which exerts antagonizing roles to the TGF-β/SMAD3 profibrotic pathway, is downregulated across DAD histological patterns (Figs. 3E and S3). Furthermore, we quantified the spatial alignment between Factor 3 and SMAD3/SMAD7 activity by applying an explainable multi-view approach48 to model Factor 3 scores per spot using the top ten TF activity scores enriched per condition (Fig. 3D). This TF-only model explained a median of 50% of the variance in Factor 3 scores across all proliferative DAD samples (Fig. S3), supporting the contribution of TF activity to the spatial pattern captured by Factor 3, particularly in proliferative DAD. In contrast, the variance explained in control and acute DAD samples was lower (Fig. S3), underscoring the relevance of Factor 3 in proliferative DAD. Within this TF-only model, SMAD3 ranked among the top six positive predictors of Factor 3 scores, whereas SMAD7 showed less predictive importance (Fig. S3). Predictor importances showed higher SMAD3 and lower SMAD7 contributions to Factor 3 in COVID-19 DAD samples compared to controls.

To assess whether the TF programs identified in COVID-19-DAD were disease-specific, we analyzed the extended HLCA dataset13 across COVID-19, non-COVID-19 pneumonia, IPF, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and healthy lungs, comprising 1,401,006 cells from 434 samples and 298 donors (“Methods”). This analysis broadly recapitulated our Visium ST findings. COVID-19-DAD-associated TFs such as SMAD3, ZEB2 and CIITA were upregulated in COVID-19 (FDR < 0.001 for all, Table S2). The SMAD3⁺/SMAD7⁻ axis showed strong COVID-19 disease specificity, with SMAD3 and SMAD7 ranking as the 2nd most up- and downregulated TFs, respectively, by relative enrichment in COVID-19 compared to Healthy lungs, a pattern not observed in non-COVID-19 pneumonia, IPF or COPD (Fig. S3, and Table S2). In particular, SMAD3 was downregulated in non-COVID-19 pneumonia, while SMAD7 was upregulated in IPF and COPD. NKX2-1 was downregulated in COVID-19 while upregulated in non-COVID-19 pneumonia, IPF and COPD, consistent with these other lung pathologies engaging NKX2-1-driven AT2 regeneration as a late-stage reparative response.

The altered TGF-β signaling pathway was further strengthened by two additional components, TGFB1I1 [a marker of contractile smooth muscle cells] and ZEB2 [involved in fibrogenesis49, that were upregulated in control and proliferative DAD lungs, respectively (Fig. S4). Interestingly for the last gene, ZEB2 DNA methylation status has been linked to another severe consequence of SARS-CoV-2 infection, the multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children50. Two additional cellular networks were targeted by aberrant TF activity: lung epithelial cell differentiation and NK cells functionality. In the first case, downregulation of the AT2 cell identity regulator ETV5 occurred upon COVID-19-induced DAD (Fig. 3E), suggesting initiation of epithelial regeneration by AT2 cells11; whereas NKX2-1 (regulator of alveolar epithelial progenitors)51, was upregulated in proliferative DADs (Fig. S4). Remarkably, SREBF2, related to surfactant production in AT2 cells, and CIITA, which drives MHCII expression and induces cell resistance to SARS-CoV-252, were enriched in acute DAD lungs (Figs. 3D and S4). For NK cells, where we observed a decrease across acute and proliferative COVID-19-DAD (Fig. 2A), IKZF1, an essential TF for their proper development, was also downregulated in the DAD lungs (Fig. S4), supporting that NK cell dysfunction in COVID-19 could be associated with lethality.

Spatial relationships of ligand-receptor interactions and transcription factor activities with cell type abundance

We next leveraged the explainable multi-view modeling approach48 to decipher the global spatial relationships between LRIs, TFs and the distinct cell type abundances. In this regard, we jointly modeled cell type abundances in each spatial spot using the top 25 local LR loadings from Factor 3 (Supplementary Data file 1) and the activity of the top 10 most enriched TFs (Table S1) per condition. We observed that across control and DAD lung tissue slides, both LRIs and TFs activity jointly contributed to explain the variance of cell type abundance (mean multi-view R2 = 0.21) (Fig. 3F). The relative contribution to the joint predictive performance was higher for TF activities (median contribution 65%) compared to LRIs (median contribution 35%) across acute and proliferative DAD (Fig. 3F). Importantly, the abundance of fibroblasts was best explained by the activity of TFs SMAD3 and SMAD7, including TIMP1^CD63 LRI among top 10 predictors of PBFs (Fig. 3F), highlighting their contribution to DAD. Moreover, important differences were also found when explaining cellular composition across control lungs and COVID-19 induced DAD, particularly for PBFs, endothelial aerocyte capillary cells, endothelial general capillary cells, alveolar Mphs subtypes, monocyte-derived Mphs, non-classical monocytes and NK cells (Fig. S5).

Additionally, we used the predictor importances (coefficients’ t-values) from this predictive linear model to infer intracellular signaling networks linking both LRIs and TF activity patterns. In this regard, we applied LIANA+31 (“Methods”) to infer a putative causal network linking LRIs to TFs, focusing on PBFs, considering their significant enrichment in proliferative DAD lungs (Fig. 3G) and in IPF patients53. We found that initial activation of the ITGB1 receptor unleashed a downstream signaling pathway that upregulated the transcription factors SMAD3, MYB54 and BRCA1 and downregulated SMAD7 and CIITA (Fig. 3G). Importantly, the ITGB1-mediated repression of CIITA (Fig. 3G) could activate collagen expression by lung fibroblasts after injury55. Interestingly, we also found that integrin ITGB1 induces an activation of the kinases ILK, AKT1, MAP2K4 and MAPK14 (Fig. 3G), previously linked to the disorder56, becoming amenable candidates for therapies.

To identify local spatial dependencies that might occur only in a sub-region of the studied lung tissues, we leveraged spatially-informed local bivariate similarity metrics that included spatially-weighted Cosine similarity and global Moran´s R (Fig. 4A), to identify pairs of LRIs that are spatially clustered together or apart (“Methods”). The LRI TIMP1^CD63 showed the highest spatial co-clustering pattern by both metrics (Fig. 4A), particularly for proliferative DAD lungs within DAD-associated Factor 3 boundaries (Figs. 4B and S6). Computed permutation-based p-values to assess the significance of the local interactions demonstrated an agreement with the high Cosine similarity regions (Fig. 4B). To further categorize TIMP1^CD63 spatial relationship, we identified that for most local category areas, both ligand and receptor were highly expressed (Fig. 4B). Interestingly, APP participated in multiple LRIs, including APP^CD74 with the second highest Cosine similarity (Fig. 4A). An additional APP LRI, APP^AGER, showed a clear diminishing spatial co-clustering pattern over acute and proliferative DAD (Figs. S6 and S7), suggesting a biological relationship since AGER is an AT1 marker53, also observed in our study (Fig. S1). Intriguingly, beta-amyloid produced by the infection-mediated lung injury can reach through the general circulation other organs originating further defects, including neurocognitive dysfunction36. Cognitive impairment in the post-acute phases of COVID-19 is not uncommon55 and SARS-CoV-2 infection is considered a risk factor for Alzheimer´s disease56. Finally, multiple LRIs with proteins involved in the activation of Mphs in COVID-19, such as S100A9, showed increased spatial co-clustering through DAD patterns (Figs. S6 and S8). Interestingly, S100A9^CD68, the best LRI predictor of all Mph subtypes, non-classical monocytes and T cells proliferating cell type abundances (Figs. S8 and S9), yielded one of the highest median global Moran´s R scores (Fig. 4A), suggesting an important role in aberrant myeloid activation and dysregulated immune response11,57. We next investigated associations between cell types and TFs activity, considering their relevance for tissue function, using also spatially-weighted Cosine similarity and global Moran’s R (Fig. 4C) (“Methods”). PBFs, the most abundant cell type in proliferative COVID-19 (Fig. 2A), were most spatially associated and co-clustered with the pro-fibrotic TF SMAD3 activity locations (Fig. 4C, D), whereas PBFs and the anti-fibrotic TF SMAD7 activity locations were spatially clustered apart (Fig. 4C, D). These results fit the mutually exclusive location of SMAD3 and SMAD7 activities through DAD patterns (Fig. 3E); and the top LRI loading, TIMP1^CD63, characterizing DAD associated Factor 3 promoting lung fibrosis through the TGF-β1/SMAD3 pathway (Fig. 4B). All these results highlight the central role of TGF-β pathway activation in driving pathological ECM remodeling and repair linked to aberrant activation of PBFs58 that leads to scar formation and a grossly disrupted lung tissue architecture in COVID-19 proliferative DAD.

To provide a second example (beyond PBFs) of local spatial relationships between TFs activity and cell types, it is worth highlighting the myeloid lineage. The activity of the TF MYB was the best predictor of the abundance of myeloid cell types (Fig. S9). The spatial co-expression of MYB with the myeloid and T cell proliferating cell fates increases across acute and proliferative DAD (Fig. S10). MYB plays an essential role in many hematopoietic pathways and, most importantly, the E2F/MYB regulatory programs from myeloid cell populations are hyperactivated in severe COVID-19 cases59. The interrogation of the immune landscape also unveiled that NK cells, another population critically depleted in our COVID-19- associated DAD cases (Fig. 2A), were most influenced by IKZF1 activity, showing the highest co-clustering pattern (Fig. S10). IKZF1 is essential for proper NK cell development60. Herein, we report a loss of the co-clustering pattern of IKFZ1 and NKs across COVID-19-associated DAD patterns (Fig. S10). Results that strengthen the suggested central role of NK cell dysfunction in fatal COVID-1961.

To further illustrate the spatial coupling between TFs activity and cell type abundance, we focused on lung epithelial differentiation. NKX2-1 activity was the best predictor of AT2 cell abundance and showed the strongest spatial association with this key cell population involved in alveolar regeneration (Fig. S10). NKX2-1 is a well-established regulator of alveolar epithelial progenitors51. Notably, spatial co-localization between NKX2-1 activity and AT2 cells increased in proliferative DAD cases (Fig. S10). This spatial pattern supports the notion of ongoing alveolar regeneration in proliferative DAD. Together, these findings reinforce the broader relevance of characterizing the spatial distribution and co-localization between TF activity and cell types across COVID-19-induced DAD patterns.

Cell-cell communication as a function of niche composition

The potential cell-cell communication events that could occur in lung tissues across the different conditions were assessed to account not only for LRIs and TFs activity. We also used a graph neural network method (NCEM)62 that estimates the effect of the inferred spot composition on gene expression variation within cell types across spots to discover intercellular dependencies (“Methods”). To discriminate different intercellular dependencies between control and fatal COVID-19 lung sections, we identified multiple cell types over DAD patterns (“Methods”), observing a profound reconfiguration of intercellular communication (Fig. 5A). We observed a dependency of NK cells in control lung tissues on various cell types, including CD8 T cells, EC aerocyte capillary cells, alveolar Mph CCL3+ and AT1 cells. However, these dependencies were lost in acute and proliferative COVID-19 lung tissues (Fig. 5A). We also found that in DAD samples the population of CD4 T cells exhibited multiple dependencies on various cell types such as non-classical monocytes, SM activated stress response cells, plasma cells and AT1 cells, becoming a prominent receiver node of communication, particularly in acute DAD (Fig. 5A). Interestingly, AT1 cells exhibited limited intercellular dependencies in control lung samples, that increased in acute DAD, and were lost in proliferative DAD (Fig. 5A). Lastly, CD4 T cells established a dependency on AT2 cells in proliferative DAD (Fig. 5A). Additionally, we performed a receiver effect analysis highlighting gene-wise effects of all senders on once receiver cell type to contextualize gene expression differences in some of the couplings (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, since CD4 T cells showed important dependencies on multiple cell types in DAD lungs, we performed a sender similarity analysis to characterize the profile of these intercellular dependencies across DAD patterns. The resulting heatmap (Fig. 5B) represents pairwise correlations between sender effect vectors on CD4 T cells, capturing how similar different sender cell types are in their regulatory influence on this shared receiver. Spatio-temporal trajectories: AT2-AT1 epithelial regeneration

AT2 cells play an important role as AT1 progenitors during lung injury, proliferating and contributing to alveolar repair and regeneration. By contrast, AT1 cells are fragile, susceptible to damage and unable to proliferate. Therefore, characterizing AT2-AT1 differentiation is important to provide novel insights into cellular processes and tissue repair mechanisms in severe COVID-19. To decipher the dynamic relationships across tissue space and time between transcriptional states of AT2 and AT1 cells, we leveraged a spatial graph-based method named pseudo-time-space (PSTS) implemented in the stLearn software63 (“Methods”). By combining spatial and imaging information, representing cell and tissue morphology, with gene expression data, we used the PSTS algorithm to map the spatial changes in AT2 and AT1 cell states, modeling and reconstructing their spatio-temporal trajectories. In this regard, we defined a spatial trajectory for AT2 cells transitioning into AT1 cells across two clusters (clade 6 and clade 9) in a proliferative DAD lung (Fig. 5C). These clades represent distinct spatial clusters of transcriptionally related spots inferred through PSTS, which integrates spatial proximity and gene expression to model gradual transitions between cell states (“Methods”). The top 10 most upregulated and downregulated genes defining AT2 to AT1 transition in each clade are shown (Fig. 5C). Enrichment analysis (“Methods”) revealed that the top 10 upregulated genes in clade 6 were enriched in AT1 cell identity markers, including ICAM1, DPYSL2, ANGPTL2 and AGER22. Likewise, the top 10 downregulated genes in clade 6 were enriched in AT2 cell identity markers, including SFTPB, SCD and CYB5A22. Furthermore, the top 10 downregulated genes in clade 9 were also enriched in AT2 cell identity markers, including SFTPB, SFTPC, SLC34A2, FASN, CTSH, DBI, MLPH, LPCAT1 and CYB5A22. These results further validate the inferred spatio-temporal trajectories in AT2 and AT1 cell states, better characterizing the alveolar epithelial regeneration process after lung injury. Interestingly, a comparison between clade 6 and clade 9 (Fig. 5C) revealed that CAV1, a late AT1 maturation marker11, was upregulated in clade 9 but not in clade 6. This suggests that clade 9 is associated with a more complete transition from AT2 to AT1 cells, whereas clade 6 likely represents an intermediate state of differentiation. This is further supported by the cell type specific gene expression of CAV1 in spatial coordinates among different subtypes of pneumocytes, where CAV1 is expressed only by AT1 cells in distinct locations but not by co-located AT2 and derived AT0 cells during alveolar repair, driving most of CAV1 total expression across all cell types (Fig. 5C). Altogether, although further experimental biological validation is necessary, these results support the notion that in the lung of patients with severe COVID-19, AT2 cells aim to repopulate AT1 cells upon the activation of alveolar epithelial regeneration programs.

Spatial transcriptomics comparison to non-COVID-19 DAD and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

In order to attribute the observed cellular and spatial features to COVID-19-specific pathophysiological changes instead of general features of ARDS and DAD, we compared our observations to non-COVID-19 DAD cases and to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis samples.

In this regard, we first performed additional spatial transcriptomics analyses on eight non-COVID-19 DAD samples (Table 2). Importantly, three of these cases were collected prior to the COVID-19 pandemic (2018), completely ruling out undiagnosed SARS-CoV-2 infection. Related to cellular composition differences, non-COVID-19 DAD samples showed a predominance of immune over endothelial lineages (Fig. S11). Importantly, within the stromal compartment, alveolar fibroblasts predominated (Fig. S11), in clear contrast to the marked expansion of PBFs seen in COVID-19 proliferative DAD (Fig. 2A). Alveolar fibroblasts are associated with tissue homeostasis and regulation of fibrosis58, suggesting that their dominance in non-COVID-19 cases reflects a reparative response distinct from the fibrotic remodeling driven by PBFs in COVID-19 DAD. Most importantly, to further benchmark our COVID-19 cases against fibrotic lung injury of non-COVID-19 origin, we processed and re-analyzed publicly available Visium spatial transcriptomics data from IPF samples64. This dataset included lung samples from 4 IPF patients, each with three tissue blocks representing increasing levels of fibrotic injury, and control samples from four individuals with no known lung disease. Using the same cell type deconvolution and spatial co-localization computational pipelines applied to our COVID-19 dataset (“Methods”), we found that IPF samples exhibited greater dominance of epithelial and immune lineages and reduced representation of endothelial cells (Fig. S12), compared to COVID-19 samples (Fig. 1B). In addition, the low-fibrosis IPF regions (Fig. S12) were dominated by AT2 cells (Fig. S12) in contrast to COVID-19 DAD, where AT2 cells were less prominent (Fig. 2A, B). Finally, unlike in COVID-19-DAD, where PBFs form unique spatial cellular compartments (Fig. 2C), PBFs in IPF samples did not organize into individual niches (Fig. S12). Instead, across regions with increasing fibrotic extent, PBFs tended to co-localize with other stromal and immune cell types, particularly in highly fibrotic regions, suggesting a different role for PBFs in diring spatial cellular compartmentalization in IPF compared to COVID-19-DAD.

Overall, our extended analysis across non-COVID-19 DAD and IPF samples reinforces that the cellular and spatial hallmarks we have identified are not merely reflections of fibrotic injury or unspecific DAD, but are more characteristic of COVID-19-induced DAD.

Discussion

The wealth of transcriptomic information in COVID-19 is mostly derived from disaggregated lung tissues where the architecture of the organ is not preserved and, thus, the effect of the surrounding tissue microenvironment on gene expression is lost. This issue has only recently been investigated in COVID-19-affected lung tissues, but mostly for targeted markers or discrete locations14–19. In a similar manner, VisiumST has spatially resolved transcriptomes in IPF64; our spatial transcriptomic analysis, with the most recently developed bioinformatic tools, delivers a landscape of the lung in fatal COVID-19 defined by profound shifts in specific cellular populations with distorted intercellular communications that finally disrupt important gene networks.

Among the observed changes in cell-type composition, PBFs showed the most pronounced increase, particularly in proliferative DAD. Most other fibroblast subsets also increased across acute and proliferative COVID-19-DAD. In contrast, alveolar fibroblasts, which contribute to tissue homeostasis and regulate lung fibrosis58, decreased together with key cell types of the functional respiratory alveolus, including AT1 epithelial cells and aerocyte capillary endothelial cells. In contrast, in non-COVID-19 DAD cases, alveolar fibroblasts remained predominant within the stromal compartment, highlighting a reparative response that differs from the profibrotic remodeling driven by PBFs in COVID-19 DAD. Interestingly, proliferative DAD showed an increase in various subtypes of alveolar Mphs. Furthermore, the myeloid lineage underwent an overall increase, including interstitial and monocyte-derived Mphs and non-classical monocytes, whereas the lymphoid lineage decreased across DAD patterns, mostly driven by the loss of NK cells in proliferative DAD lungs. These results match the reported aberrant activation of myeloid cells and impaired T cell and NK cell responses in fatal COVID-1911,61. In this regard, we highlight the important role of NK cells in severe COVID-19, characterizing NK cell dysfunction with marked downregulation of essential TFs activity for their functional development and maturation, particularly for IKZF160. Herein, we report a progressive and persistent dysfunction of NK cells throughout DAD patterns, further implicating their impairment in fatal COVID-1961. Besides NK cell dysfunction, a dysregulated immunological repair response to SARS-CoV-2 infection has been proposed as a major contributor to disease progression57. Hence, we have described the LRIs and TFs activity related to essential elements of the immune system, identifying S100A9^CD68 LRI and MYB TF activity as major determinants of myeloid cell types abundance, showing increased spatial co-expression patterns over acute and proliferative DAD, strengthening their role in the activation of Mphs in severe COVID-1914,59.

Moreover, we have characterized the molecular drivers of pathological responses to lung injury leading to massive fibrosis and grossly disrupted tissue architecture. We report the key role of the TGF-β pathway in DAD histological patterns, identifying an upregulation of profibrotic SMAD3 coupled with downregulation of its antagonist SMAD7. Importantly, we have identified TIMP1^CD63 LRI as a major contributor to DAD, emphasizing TIMP1 role as a key regulator of ECM homeostasis and downstream effector of TGF-β pathway activation, becoming a candidate therapy target for pulmonary fibrosis65. Furthermore, we inferred an intracellular signaling network in PBFs, suggesting that the phenotypic changes and the different targeting of the SMAD TFs involved the activation of the integrin ITGB1 receptor and their associated downstream kinases AKT1, MAP2K4 and MAPK14, representing additional potential targets for COVID-19 therapies56.

Interestingly, when analyzing intercellular dependencies as a function of niche composition, we described a dependency of NK cells on various cell types in control lungs that was lost on DAD lungs, whereas a dependency of CD4 T cells on multiple cell types including CD8 T cells in control lungs shifted towards other lymphoid and myeloid immune cells and stromal cells in DAD, such as non-classical monocytes, plasma cells and smooth muscle activated stress response cell types, including epithelial AT1 cells in acute DAD and AT2 cells in proliferative DAD, further reflecting the disrupted tissue architecture across DAD patterns.

Importantly, our spatio-temporal trajectories analysis helps to characterize the alveolar epithelial regeneration process, highlighting the important role of AT2 cells as AT1 progenitors and identifying markers of AT2 to AT1 differentiation. Notably, the spatio-temporal reconstruction of AT2 to AT1 differentiation trajectory revealed region-specific variation in epithelial repair, suggesting that local tissue context may influence the efficiency and completeness of alveolar regeneration. This process is impaired in severe COVID-19-induced DAD cases, contributing to fatal outcomes in these patients. In this regard, it is tempting to speculate that long COVID-19 could relate to the partial or complete disruption of intercellular communication events and differentiation trajectories that cannot completely restore the functional alveolar gas exchange capacity and/or prevent the persistence of fibrotic scars.

Beyond the COVID-19 vaccines and the pre-exposure prophylaxis in severely immunocompromised individuals with pemivibart (Pemgarda), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has authorized or approved nirmatrelvir with ritonavir (Paxlovid), remdesivir (Veklury) and molnupiravir (Lagevrio) as antiviral medications used to treat mild to moderate COVID-19 in people who are more likely to get very sick (https://www.cdc.gov/covid/treatment/index.html). In this therapeutic setting, our findings could also provide and foster the research of small drugs and antibodies targeting some of the cell types, pathways and intercellular communication that characterize the spatial aftermath of severe COVID-19. One example could be related to the combating of the fibrosis associated with DAD patterns. Pirfenidone and nintedanib are two antifibrotic drugs approved for the treatment of IPF that could be repurposed to avoid severe COVID-19-associated fibrosis since pirfenidone inhibits TGF-β66, the main profibrotic pathway underpinned in our study, and nintedanib blocks several tyrosine and serine/threonine kinases67,68, among them the MAPKs and AKT1 identified in our intracellular pathways analyses of PBFs. In this regard, the herein identified MAPK14 kinase activation within the PBF intracellular signaling pathway leading to SMAD3 activation, suggests the use of MAPK14 inhibitors such as ralimetinib and ARRY-797, both exhibiting SARS-CoV-2 antiviral activity56. Interestingly, several inhibitors of the integrin αvβ6, such as GSK3335103 and BG00011, are also at different levels of preclinical studies or even in clinical trials to treat IPF. Our finding that integrin ITGB1 activation unleashes a profibrotic signaling in PBFs in fatal COVID-19 cases suggests that this receptor can also be another amenable target. A similar case can be drawn for the emerging and hyperactivated population of inflammatory myeloid cells that we have observed. In this regard, Mphs and monocytes engage the NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome in COVID-1969. Thus, NLRP3 inhibitors, such as NT0793/NT0249 or MCC95069, can be other useful agents to restrict the activity of these belligerent myeloid cells. Importantly, because our integrative spatial transcriptomics analysis of TFs activity yielded MYB as a major determinant factor for Mphs in severe COVID-19, it could be proposed as a candidate drug target70. Similarly, we report S100A9^CD68 LRI as a major determinant of myeloid cell types. Interestingly, S100 protein family members S100A8 and S100A9 have been investigated as potential targets of small molecules such as paquinimod to control aberrant myeloid activation in COVID-1971.

Related to treatment, we can also briefly mention a provocative thought. Since multiple LRIs involved APP (such as APP^CD74); cognition defects in post-COVID-1972 could be due to beta-amyloid liberated to the blood by the lung lesions36; and SARS-CoV-2 infection is a risk factor for Alzheimer´s disease73, we suggest that maybe we can target both processes: the lung injury and the associated cognitive impairment. For example, lysophosphatidic acid receptors (LPAR) from the G protein-coupled receptor family contribute to Alzheimer’s disease, but also bind to the viral spike protein; thus, LPAR inhibitors are investigated for this potential double effect74.

Information on the accessibility of drugs acting on the candidate targets identified in our study can be found at the ClinicalTrials.gov page of the National Library of Medicine (https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=COVID-19-). Importantly, caution must be exercised when considering therapeutic interference with the observed pathways, as the samples studied are post-mortem and some findings may reflect long-term downstream responses to the injury. It is also important to note that the assumed progression is based on the canonical understanding of DAD since longitudinal samples from the same patient, which would be necessary to confirm temporal evolution, were not available.

A limitation of our study is that complete retrospective clinical annotation was not uniformly available across all autopsy-derived cohorts, and the COVID-19 cases represented patients with fatal disease and greater clinical complexity than the non-COVID-19 controls. This limits our ability to fully disentangle SARS-CoV-2-specific effects from those related to prolonged clinical course, comorbid conditions or secondary pulmonary infection. Although the non-COVID-19 DAD cohort provides a broader comparator for DAD not related to active SARS-CoV-2 infection, harmonized histopathological stratification across all samples was limited by the retrospective nature of these archival specimens. These limitations should be considered, although they are partly mitigated by the non-COVID-19 DAD, IPF and HLCA validation analyses.

Our study is unique because we provide a spatially informed characterization of the cellular and molecular hallmarks of lung tissue architecture in fatal COVID-19. This detailed spatial transcriptomics study that highlights in situ the disease-associated changes in the composition of cellular subsets, their spatial dependencies and disrupted intercellular communication programs also constitute a proof-of-principle of the potential translational use of the emerging spatial technologies. This transition will require careful benchmark comparison studies among the competing spatial transcriptomic platforms, harmonization of data processing pipelines and design of user-friendly databases where the data can be deposited and interrogated, automatization of sample processing and data analysis workflows leading to a shorter timeframe to deliver the results, together with the ongoing reduction of sequencing costs, and scalable computational methods to exploit spatial transcriptomics data. Related to this last point, spatial transcriptomics can constitute one of the entrance points for the application of artificial intelligence in pathology and modern medicine. In this regard, our investigation of the altered cellular and molecular architecture of the lung in fatal COVID-19 could serve as an excellent example of the versatility of spatial transcriptomics to fulfill the promise of how the new genomic technologies could improve our understanding and the personalized management of many human diseases.

Methods

Patient inclusion criteria

This study was conducted in accordance with all relevant ethical regulations. The study was approved by the institutional ethical review boards of Ramón y Cajal University Hospital (Necropsias_Covid19; 355_20) and the Lund Hospital (ref 2020- 02369). Informed consent was obtained where applicable.

The inclusion criteria for the COVID-19-associated DAD cohort were: patients >18 years old, with polymerase chain reaction (PCR) positive for SARS-CoV-2 and complete clinical information of disease history, comorbidities and follow-up, showing clinical pulmonary involvement and COVID-19-related death. The inclusion criteria for the control cohort were: individuals >18 years old with complete clinical information about comorbidities, without clinical evidence of SARS-CoV-2 infection, and sudden death due to cardiovascular disease, except one case died due to cancer dissemination, and one sample from a normal lung biopsy was included.

Histopathological classification of DAD samples

The classification of each DAD sample as “acute” or “proliferative” was based on the predominant histopathological pattern observed in H&E-stained sections of the lung parenchyma, as assessed by an expert pathologist. In cases where both acute and proliferative patterns coexisted, the more advanced component was used to classify the sample, and the representative region was selected for spatial transcriptomics analysis.

Generation of Visium spatial transcriptomics data from formalin fixed paraffin embedded COVID-19 lung samples

First, the RNA integrity of the FFPE samples was assessed by extracting RNA from freshly collected tissue sections and evaluating the percentage of RNA fragments above 200 base pairs (DV200). Briefly, Tissue blocks were placed in the microtome (ThermoScientific HM340E) and trimmed to expose the tissue. Four 10-µm-thick sections were placed in a chilled Eppendorf tube and the RNA was extracted using a protocol from Qiagen (RNeasy FFPE Kit 73504), following extraction, the product was analyzed by TapeStation. Samples with DV200 ≥ 22% were selected for experiments.

Selected samples were placed in the microtome and sectioned 7 µm thick, each section was then placed in a water bath floating at 42 °C, sections were collected and mounted onto a 6.5 × 6.5 mm capture area of the Visium Spatial Gene Expression slide (2000233, 10X Genomics). Capture areas contain approximately 5000 barcoded spots, providing an average resolution of 1–10 cells. After sectioning, the slides were dried at 42 °C for 3 h. The slides were then placed inside a slide mailer, sealed with parafilm, and left overnight at Room temperature.

The next day, the slides were deparaffinized by successive immersions in xylene and ethanol, followed by H&E staining according to Demonstrated Protocol (CG000409, 10X Genomics). Brightfield images were taken using a 10X objective (Plan APO) on a Nikon Eclipse Ti2; images were stitched together using NIS-Elements software (Nikon) and exported as TIFF files. After imaging, the glycerol and cover glass were carefully removed from the Visium slides by holding the slides in an 800 ml water beaker and letting the glycerol diffuse until the cover glass detached and density changes were no longer visible in the water. The slides were then dried at 37 °C and incubated for decrosslinking for 60 min.

Following the decrosslinking step, overnight probe hybridization was performed, and libraries were prepared according to the Visium Spatial Gene Expression for FFPE User Guide (CG000407, 10X Genomics). Libraries were sent for sequencing in Macrogen Korea using 1 lane of HiSeq X 150PE (2× 150 bp) per sample, applying 1% Phix. Sequencing was performed using the specific for FFPE following read protocol: read 1: 28 cycles; i7 index read: 10 cycles; i5 index read: 10 cycles; read 2: 50 cycles.

Immunohistochemistry analysis

FFPE tissue sections were analyzed using standard IHC techniques. The primary antibodies used were anti-CD34 (clone QBEnd 10, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), anti-CD68 (clone KP1, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) and anti-CK7 (clone OV-TL 12/30, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Immunostaining was performed automatically using a DAKO Autostainer Link 48 machine (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Anti-CD34 was positive in endothelial cells, anti-CD68 was expressed in the cytoplasm of intraalveolar macrophages and CK7 was used as a marker for pneumocytes. A trichrome stain was used to evaluate the amount of fibrosis.

Computational analysis

Visium spatial gene expression libraries mapping

Visium Spatial Gene Expression libraries for Formalin Fixed Paraffin Embedded (FFPE) tissue samples were analyzed with the spaceranger count pipeline using Space Ranger version 2.0.0 from 10× Genomics. First, a manual fiducial alignment and tissue boundary identification, including manual selection of spots covering tissue regions, were performed for each single library FFPE sample using Loupe Browser version 6.0 on the brightfield image. A probe set reference file compatible with FFPE workflow and human reference genome GRCh38 was downloaded from 10× Genomics and used to map Visium gene expression libraries.

Visium ST data preprocessing

We performed quality control (QC) steps, including filtering of low-quality spots defined by a low number of detected genes with positive counts and a low number of counts (library size). These metrics were computed using scanpy75. As the QC automatic filtering threshold, we utilized median absolute deviations (MAD) to identify outliers, as defined by differences in 5 MADs for the number of detected genes and library size per tissue slide76. A mitochondrial-content outlier filter (3 MADs, including mitochondrial counts exceeding 8%) was also configured for methodological consistency with standard QC workflows. However, because the Visium FFPE Human Transcriptome Probe Set v1.0 does not include mitochondrial-encoded transcripts in the probe panel, mitochondrial counts are structurally zero across all spots in this dataset and this filter did not remove any spots. Per-sample QC metrics, including the effective lower-bound thresholds applied to each sample, are reported in Table S3.

We next applied normalization to the raw counts by scaling the counts, followed by the shifted logarithm transformation to stabilize variance in gene expression between cells. To filter out uninformative genes with mostly zero counts, we performed feature selection using deviance to select informative genes76 using scry R package and selecting the top 6000 highly deviant genes, as inspired by the preprocessing workflow utilized by the HLCA13.

Finally, we performed dimensionality reduction using principal component analysis (PCA), t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) and UMAP with scanpy default parameters to reduce data complexity and for visualization purposes. To identify cellular structure, we cluster cells by applying the Leiden algorithm to the previously computed neighborhood graph with scanpy. Lastly, individual sample objects were joined into a single object using the anndata concat() function. After the concatenation, we re-normalized raw counts on the joined object using global scaling by the total counts per barcode and applying the shifted logarithm transformation, followed by feature selection using deviance to select the top 6,000 highly deviant genes, dimensionality reduction and clustering as previously described. A total of 91,068 spots, including 77,580 spots from fatal COVID-19 samples and 13,488 spots from control samples, were profiled after QC.

Human Lung Cell Atlas (HLCA) reference processing

We leveraged the HLCA13 single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) reference dataset and consensus cell type annotations for spatial mapping and annotation. To this end, we downloaded and processed the HLCA core and data object, selected lung parenchyma tissue cell types with at least 150 total cells at the finest level of annotation for a more robust and reliable reference model training, including a total of 333,011 cells in the filtered dataset. The mitochondrial genes were removed for spatial mapping. Marker gene selection was performed for every cell type by ranking genes using the scanpy tool rank_genes_groups() function with default parameters using the t-test and computing a hierarchical clustering based on gene expression values for visualization with scaled expression for easier identification of differences.

Deconvolution and spatial mapping of cell types with cell2location

Each Visium spot, with a diameter of 55 µm, contains multiple cells of potentially different types. To estimate the abundance of these cell types within each spot, we used cell2location21 to infer the cell type proportions. This allowed us to estimate the abundance of even those cell types that are present at low levels within mixed spots, avoiding the issue where a single dominant cell type masks the presence of others. This approach provided a finer granularity of cell composition beyond binary presence-absence assignment, which was critical for capturing the diversity of cell populations across DAD patterns.

Both our joined Visium ST and the filtered HLCA reference datasets were subset to the same gene set as baseline for the mapping between single cell and spatial data, using default parameters to select a total number of 5850 Ensembl gene identifiers. First, a reference model was fitted to estimate the reference cell type signature derived from the HLCA scRNA-seq data with cell2location21 and using the finest level of cell type annotation reported. Cell2location uses a Negative Binomial regression model to estimate signatures, while accounting for batch effect and covariates. Hence, we included the following variables from the HLCA core object as covariates in our model: “assay”, “donor_id”, “tissue_sampling_method”, and “tissue_dissociation_protocol”. To train the regression model, we used default parameters to perform training on all cells in the dataset. A maximum number of 250 epochs was sufficient to achieve convergence.

For the subsequent spatial mapping, cell2location requires two user-provided hyperparameters based on the tissue and experiment QC, including the expected number of cells per spot, which we set to 20, and a regularization parameter of within-slide or batch variation in RNA detection sensitivity, set to 20 (default), as previously described for the profiling of human lung tissue with Visium ST53. The model was trained using full data until convergence, with 40,000 iterations and the loss function (ELBO) was used. Reconstruction accuracy plots were inspected to assess model quality. Cell abundance mapped to spatial coordinates was derived using the 5% quantile of the posterior distribution. To ease visualization of cell type abundances, the most abundant cell type per spot, including aggregation by cell type lineage, was represented. We constructed a K-nearest neighbor (KNN) graph (size of local neighborhood set to 8) using estimated cell abundances and applied Leiden clustering (resolution parameter set to 0.5) to jointly cluster all Visium spots, making cluster identities directly comparable. We used the KNN graph representing location composition similarity to build a joint integrated UMAP representation (minimum distance between embedded points set to 0.3 and spread value set to 1) of all VisiumST samples. Additionally, cell-type-specific expression of every gene at every spatial location was computed and used as input for cell-cell communication analysis with NCEM and for inferring intracellular signaling networks in peribronchial fibroblasts.

Lastly, we used non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) on cell2location mapping results to identify spatial co-occurrence of cell types. NMF was trained for a range of factors, selecting 8 factors for cellular compartments identification and visualization per condition, with NMF factor loadings being represented.

Compositional data analysis with scCODA

To further analyze the differences in cell type proportions across acute and proliferative DAD, we applied scCODA77. This method is specifically designed for compositional data, which is inherently constrained by the total number of cells or the sum of all proportions being constant across conditions. scCODA employs a Bayesian approach to model the relative changes in cell types across conditions, which allows for the identification of significant shifts in cell type abundance. This is particularly important given the compositional nature of spatial transcriptomic deconvoluted data, where an increase in one cell type necessarily implies a relative decrease in others. scCODA helps correct for these interdependencies and identifies credible intervals for assessing which cell type proportions have changed significantly. We set the cutoff between credible and non-credible effects on a false discovery rate level <0.05. Estimated cell type abundances were used, including abundance aggregation by major lineage and cell type, and compositional data visualization was performed using boxplots and stacked barplots. Epithelial cells were manually chosen as the reference cell type for assessing differential shifts in abundance across lineages (Fig. 1D). For the finer-grained analysis involving 45 cell types (Fig. 2A), we used automatic reference cell type estimation, which identified Migratory dendritic cells (DCs) as the most stable reference category across samples. Differences between conditions were computed using control samples as the control group for control vs acute DAD and control vs proliferative DAD comparisons, whereas acute DAD samples were used as the control group for acute DAD vs proliferative DAD comparisons.

Analysis of the spatial arrangement of cell types

A comparison of tissue architecture across conditions was performed leveraging novel statistical and computational approaches to compare cell spatial organization at the level of cell types and samples using GraphCompass27. Samples were modeled as graphs of cells, and cell-type-specific graphs between conditions were compared, using the portrait method, which computes a similarity score reflecting how consistently a given cell type is spatially organized across DAD patterns. A higher variance in these scores indicates greater shifts in spatial patterning for that cell type. In addition, we used filtration curves to further explore the spatial organization of selected cell types, as previously described27. These curves represent the growth of the largest connected cluster of a given cell type as a function of increasing spatial distance thresholds. Right-shifted curves indicate tighter spatial clustering, while left-shifted curves reflect a more dispersed distribution. For computing spatial graphs, we used default parameters for Visium ST samples and defined the cluster key as the spot-wise most abundant cell type.

Cell-cell communication (CCC) analysis

To analyze intercellular communication events, we looked for potential ligand-receptor interactions (LRI) on our Visium ST slides using LIANA + 30,31. To assess the spatial co-occurrence of LRIs we used spatially-informed local (individual spot-level) bivariate similarity metrics, including spatially-weighted Cosine similarity and local Moran´s R31. These metrics use weights based on the spatial connectivity between spots, defined as radial kernels using the inverse Euclidean distance. Since we are assessing LRIs, we only considered interactions in which ligands, receptors and their subunits were expressed in at least 5% of the spots.

Furthermore, local interactions were categorized according to their magnitude and sign, allowing identification of local categories, where interactions are further classified into high-high (both variables are highly expressed) or high-low (one variable is highly and the other lowly expressed) interactions. Additionally, we used spot label permutations (N = 100) to generate a Null distribution and to compute empirical local p-values to assess the statistical significance of local metrics.

In addition to the local bivariate scores and to obtain “global” summaries of the local interaction results, we obtained global scores for each pair of variables, including Global mean (average), Cosine similarity and Global Moran´s R31, to identify pairs of variables that cluster together or apart and to select the best candidates for visualization and downstream analysis.

Beyond LRIs, we applied the same approach to assess spatial relationships between transcription factors (TFs) activity (see below) and cell type abundances. To make the distributions comparable, we z-scaled TF activities and cell type abundances.

To identify coordinated cell-cell communication signatures, we used NMF on the local LRI scores. The heuristic elbow procedure selected three factors as the optimal component number. Average NMF factor scores per tissue slide clustered samples according to disease status and were visualized using a heatmap representation and hierarchical clustering using Euclidean distances and Ward´s method.

Pathway enrichment analysis of LRI loadings was performed to biologically characterize the three identified factors. To this end, pathway annotations from the PROGENy resource33 were converted into ligand-receptor sets as previously described31, assigning LRIs to specific pathways. Next, we performed enrichment using multivariate linear regression with decoupleR32. For LRI analysis we used LIANA+ consensus resource.

Transcription factor activity analysis

To infer TF activity based on prior knowledge, we used CollecTRI resource46 containing a curated collection of TFs and their transcriptional targets with interactions weighted by their regulation mode (activation or inhibition). To estimate TF enrichment scores, we run a multivariate regression model using decoupleR for each spot and each TF, and a linear model was fitted to predict gene expression based on the interaction weights. The obtained t-value of the slope is the score, indicating activation or inactivation of the TF if positive or negative, respectively.

TFs enriched in each condition were identified using decoupleR rank_sources_groups() function with a t-test that overestimates the variance of each group. The top 10 TFs per condition were extracted and represented in a heatmap using scaled TF enrichment scores between 0 and 1, meaning for each TF, subtract the minimum and divide each by its maximum. Note that a given TF may appear in more than one condition if it is ranked among the top 10 enriched TFs in multiple conditions (e.g., SMAD7 in both control and acute DAD). Additionally, TF enrichment scores without scaling were represented on top of illustrative Visium ST slides.

Learning spatial relationships between LRIs and TFs activity with cell type abundance

To learn spatial dependencies between local LRIs, TFs activity and cell types abundance, we used MISTy48, an explainable multi-modeling approach, as previously described31. We selected the top 25 local ligand-receptor loadings from Factor 3 and the enrichment scores of the top 10 TFs per condition, to jointly model in a spatially informed manner the estimated cell type abundances in each spot. We specifically utilize a linear model, since the coefficients’ t-values (predictor importances), as calculated by ordinary least squares (OLS), are signed and comparable. Importantly, we bypassed predicting the intraview (intra-spot), and for each target (cell type), we assessed not only the predictor importance, but how well LRIs and TFs explained cell type abundance using the joined multi-view R2 (goodness of fit) and evaluated their relative contribution to the joint predictive performance.

Intracellular signaling network in peribronchial fibroblasts

We inferred intracellular signaling networks from prior knowledge by linking identified LRIs and TF activity scores. LIANA+ approach31 considers the direction of deregulation, including activation and inhibition of receptors and TFs, and the sign and direction of edges (activating or inhibiting) from prior knowledge of protein-protein interactions and of TFs and their targets obtained from Omnipath78. Using CORNETO, a putative causal network connecting LRIs to TFs was inferred for peribronchial fibroblasts. Hence, we used the coefficients´ t-values of the predictive linear model for peribronchial fibroblasts abundance as input (LRIs) and output (TFs) nodes of the intracellular signaling network. To this end, we computed the median of the t-values across samples and ranked them by absolute median value. For LRIs, the receptors were selected and when the same receptor was involved in multiple LRIs, the largest absolute median t-value was kept for that receptor and used as an input node. Additionally, we obtained a prior knowledge network (PKN) with signed protein-protein interactions from Omnipath and used peribronchial fibroblasts' specific gene expression computed with cell2location to calculate gene expression proportions within our target cell type, using those to generate weights for the nodes in the PKN.

Analysis of intercellular dependencies as a function of spot composition

Since cell-cell communications events are not limited to LRIs, we further assessed spot/niche composition effects on all genes using NCEM62. To this end, we utilized cell-type-specific gene expression computed with cell2location. Hence, NCEM models expression variation within cell types across spots as a function of the inferred spot composition. To focus the analysis on biologically relevant genes, we selected gene sets described in the WikiPathways database from the Molecular Signature Database (MSigDB) using decoupleR. We filtered out cell-type-specific marker genes computed using rank_genes_groups_df() function in scanpy using adjusted p-value < 0.05 and minimum log fold change > 2. Finally, 1614 genes were shared within our dataset. Additionally, 22 cell types were considered for NCEM analysis, including cell types with credible differential abundances computed by scCODA and the most abundant cell types.

Type coupling analysis was performed on the filtered dataset to compute sender and receiver effects based on a Wald test on the parameters learned by the linear NCEM model, using the full dataset and optimized with OLS. We further dissected these couplings based on gene-wise effects of particular interactions, including effects of all senders on one receiver (receiver effect analysis), reporting dependencies involving at least 500 differentially expressed with q-value < 0.05 and absolute log fold change > 0.8. Finally, a sender similarity analysis was performed to characterize sender profiles on CD4 T cells across conditions.

Spatio-temporal trajectories analysis

To characterize AT2-AT1 differentiation process, we leveraged the stLearn tool63 that employs a spatial graph-based method named pseudo-time-space (PSTS) that combines spatial and imaging information with gene expression to map spatial changes in cell states, modeling and reconstructing their spatio-temporal trajectories. Briefly, PSTS integrates spatial spot adjacency with transcriptional similarity to construct a joint graph, from which spatial trajectories are inferred using a minimum spanning tree that orders spots pseudo-temporally along differentiation paths. Spots along each trajectory were grouped into clades, distinct spatial clusters of transcriptionally related cells inferred through PSTS. In our analysis, clade 6 and clade 9 were two such PST-derived groups. The clade numbers (e.g., clade 6 and clade 9) correspond to spatial clusters defined by hierarchical clustering of the PSTS trajectory, as implemented in stLearn63. We focused on clades associated with the AT2-to-AT1 transition based on their transcriptional signatures and pseudo-time positions. To accurately identify transition genes positively or negatively correlated with the predicted trajectory for AT2-AT1 differentiation, we reported genes with a Spearman correlation <-0.4 or >0.4. Furthermore, we only selected spots enriched in AT1 and AT2 cells, where these cell types represented at least 70% of the maximum abundance of the inferred spot composition. Additionally, gene set enrichment analysis on the identified transition genes using cell type signature gene sets in the MSigDB was performed and the false discovery rate (FDR) q-values derived from the hypergeometric test were used.

Comparative analyses with extended HLCA, non-COVID-19 DAD and IPF samples

To assess the specificity of the COVID-19-DAD-associated TF activity programs, we applied the same TF activity inference framework to the extended HLCA dataset13. The HLCA was subset to lung tissue and to five conditions of interest, comprising 751,305 cells from 257 Healthy samples (164 donors), 298,429 cells from 73 COVID-19 samples (51 donors), 31,923 cells from 13 non-COVID-19 pneumonia samples (13 donors), 251,406 cells from 67 idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) samples (52 donors) and 67,943 cells from 24 chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) samples (18 donors). For comparability with our Visium ST analysis, we restricted the analysis to 5746 of the 5850 highly deviant genes from our original Visium analysis that were also present in the HLCA dataset. Condition-specific enrichment was tested using decoupleR rank_sources_groups() with a t-test that overestimates variance and reference set to ‘rest’. TFs were ranked by the difference between their mean change versus rest in COVID-19 and the corresponding mean change versus rest in Healthy lungs, to identify TFs preferentially enriched in COVID-19 compared to Healthy.

To benchmark COVID-19 DAD-specific spatial and cellular features, we performed additional spatial transcriptomics analyzes on eight non-COVID-19 DAD lung tissue samples representing diverse etiologies. Three samples were collected prior to the COVID-19 pandemic (2018), ruling out undetected SARS-CoV-2 infection. These samples were processed using the Visium CytAssist workflow (10× Genomics), while downstream data analyses, including preprocessing, cell type deconvolution and spatial co-localization analysis, were conducted using the same computational pipeline as for COVID-19 cases.

Additionally, we re-analyzed publicly available Visium data from IPF and control lungs64, encompassing four IPF patients (each with three tissue annotated with fibrosis extent scores ranging from 0 to 3, as previously reported64) and four healthy controls. This allowed us to assess COVID-19 DAD-specific versus IPF-associated patterns of cell type abundance and spatial organization across increasing levels of fibrotic severity.

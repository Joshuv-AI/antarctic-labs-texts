Abstract
. In this paper, we show that for any rational surface singularity A A , the canonical trace ideal Tr A ⁡ ( K A ) \Tr_{A}(K_{A}) is integrally closed ideal which is represented by the minimal anti-nef cycle F F on the minimal resolution of singularities so that K X + F K_{X}+F is anti-nef. Then F ≥ Z f F\geq Z_{f} if A A is not Gorenstein, where Z f Z_{f} is the fundamental cycle. As a result, we give a criterion for rational surface singularity A A to be nearly Gorenstein. Moreover, we classify all nearly Gorenstein rational singularities in terms of resolution of singularities in the following cases: (a) the fundamental cycle Z f Z_{f} is almost reduced; (b) quotient singularity.

1.
Introduction

First, we introduce the notions of trace ideals and nearly Gorensteinness in a general setting.
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}

or a graded ring with graded maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.
Assume that
A
has a canonical module
K_{A}
and let

be the total ring of fractions.
For an

of

\tr_{A}(M):=\sum_{f\in\Hom_{A}(M,A)}\Img f=\langle f(x)\,|\,f\in\Hom_{A}(M,A),\,x\in M\rangle

For any ideal
I\subset A
of positive grade,

\tr_{A}I=I\cdot I^{-1}
holds true, where

I^{-1}={\left\{x\in Q(A)\,\left|\,xI\subset A\right.\right\}}
(see
[
7
, 1.1]
).
In particular,
\tr_{A}(K_{A})=K_{A}\cdot K_{A}^{-1}
.

Herzog et.al
[
7
]
introduced the following notion.

Definition 1.1

(cf.
[
7
, 2.2]
)
.

We call
\Tr_{A}(K_{A})
the
canonical trace ideal
of
A
.
A local ring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is said to be
nearly Gorenstein

if
\tr_{A}(K_{A})\supset\mathfrak{m}
.

Note that
A
is Gorenstein if
\tr_{A}(K_{A})=A
.
By the definition, Gorenstein rings are nearly Gorenstein, and
A
is nearly Gorenstein but not Gorenstein if and only if
\tr_{A}(K_{A})=\mathfrak{m}
.

We want to determine the condition for
A
to be nearly Gorenstein when

(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a
normal
local domain of dimension
2
.
We assume also that
A
contains an algebraically closed field isomorphic
to
A/\mathfrak{m}
.
Also, we suppose that there exists a resolution of singularities
(e.g.
A
is excellent). We simply call such
A
a (normal)

surface singularity
. We assume that
A
has a canonical module
K_{A}
.

We want to determine the condition for
A
to be nearly Gorenstein

using resolution of singularities of
A
.

For a general surface singularity, this problem is rather hard. But if

A
is a
rational singularity
, we can find very simple criterion
for

to be nearly Gorenstein. See Theorem
1.3
.

In
[
3

all two-dimensional nearly Gorenstein quotient singularities
(see also
[
2
]
for higher-dimensional cyclic quotient singularities).
However it seems to be not known when a rational singularity is nearly Gorenstein
even if
\dim A=2
and there is no general method to compute canonical trace ideals.
So the main purpose of this paper is to establish a criterion for two-dimensional
rational singularities to be nearly Gorenstein and give the computation of
canonical trace ideals in terms of resolution of singularities.

Let us explain the organization of the paper.
In Sections 2 and 3, we first investigate a geometric description of
(canonical) trace ideals, and

2.
Cycles on a resolution space

In this section, we set up the notation used throughout the paper and introduce the basic terminology concerning cycles on a resolution of a two-dimensional
normal singularity.

2.1
.

(1)
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a two-dimensional normal local domain containing an algebraically closed field isomorphic to the residue field
k
and let
K_{A}
denote a canonical module of
A
.
Also, we suppose that there exists a resolution of singularities

\pi\colon X\to\spec(A)
. Let
E:=\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{m})
be the exceptional
set of
\pi
and
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}
the decomposition into the irreducible components.
Let
K_{X}
denote the canonical divisor on
X
.

(2) The support of a divisor
D
on
X
is denoted by
\supp(D)
.
A divisor
D
on
X
with
\supp(D)\subset E
is called a
cycle
.
We may consider a cycle
D>0
as a scheme with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{D}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
.
We write
\chi(C)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}):=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C})-h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C})
, where
h^{i}(\mathcal{F})
denotes
\dim_{k}H^{i}(\mathcal{F})
.

(3) Let
L
be a divisor on
X
.
We say that
L
is
nef
if
LE_{i}\geq 0
for all
E_{i}\subset E
.
The divisor
L
is said to be
anti-nef
if
-L
is nef.
It is known that any anti-nef cycle is effective.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
on
X
, we say that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are
numerically equivalent
if
(L_{1}-L_{2})E_{i}=0
for every
E_{i}\subset E
, and write as
L_{1}\equiv L_{2}
.

By the Riemann-Roch formula, we have

(2.1)

\chi(C)=-C(C+K_{X})/2,\ \chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(L))=\chi(C)+LC.

From the first equality, for cycles
C>0
and
D>0
, we have

(2.2)

\chi(C+D)=\chi(C)+\chi(D)-CD.

Definition 2.3
.

There exists a minimum
Z_{f}
among the anti-nef cycles
D>0
;
the cycle
Z_{f}
is called the
fundamental cycle
(
[
1
]
).
The
fundamental genus

p_{f}(A)
of
A
is defined by
p_{f}(A)=1-\chi(Z_{f})
, and the
geometric genus

p_{g}(A)
of
A
is defined by
p_{g}(A)=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
; these are independent of the choice of the resolution and satisfy
p_{f}(A)\leq p_{g}(A)
.
While the arithmetic genus is determined by the resolution graph, the geometric genus is not. The singularity
A
is said to be
rational
if
p_{g}(A)=0
.
It is known that
A
is a rational singularity if and only if
p_{f}(A)=0
(see
[
1
]
).

The fundamental cycle
Z_{f}
can be computed
via a sequence
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
of cycles such that

(2.3)

C_{1}=E_{j_{1}},\quad C_{i}=C_{i-1}+E_{j_{i}}\ (1<i\leq m),\quad C_{m}=Z_{f},

where
E_{j_{1}}
is an arbitrary component of
E
and
C_{i-1}E_{j_{i}}>0

for
1<i\leq m
.
Such a sequence
\{C_{i}\}_{i}
is called a

computation sequence
for
Z_{f}
.
From (
2.2
), we have

(2.4)

\chi(C_{i})=\chi(C_{i-1})+\chi(E_{j_{i}})-C_{i-1}E_{j_{i}}\ (1<i\leq m).

We can verify whether
A
is a rational singularity as follows.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that each
E_{i}
is a nonsingular rational curve, and let
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
be a computation sequence as in (
2.3
).
Then,
A
is rational if and only if
C_{i-1}E_{j_{i}}=1
for
1<i\leq m
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.4
) and the fact that
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}})=1
for
1\leq i\leq m

(see
[
10
, (2.6)]
).
∎

Definition 2.5
.

Assume that
X
is a
good resolution
, that is,
E
is a simple normal crossings divisor.
Then
E
or its weighted dual graph is said to be

if it has at most one irreducible component
E_{0}
of

such that
(E-E_{0})E_{0}\geq 3
and any component of

is a rational curve.
In this case,
E_{0}
is called a

and each connected component of

.

It is known that any resolution of a rational singularity is a good resolution and that if
A
is the localization of a graded ring by the homogeneous maximal ideal and

is the minimal good resolution, then

is star-shaped.

Definition 2.6
.

A cycle
F\geq 0
is called the

of the divisor
L
or the invertible

\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)
on
X
if
F
is the maximal cycle such that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L-F))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))
.
We say that
L

has no fixed components
if its fixed part is zero.
We say that
L
is

if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)
is generated by

.
Obviously,
L
has no fixed component if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)
is generated.

Remark 2.7
.

From the definition, we easily see the following.

(1)

L
is nef if
L
has no fixed component.

(2)

For
f\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\setminus\{0\}
, there exists an effective divisor
H_{f}
on
X
such that
\di_{X}(f)+L=H_{f}
.
Then the fixed part is the greatest divisor that is less than or equal to any member of
{\left\{H_{f}\,\left|\,f\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\setminus\{0\}\right.\right\}}
.

(3)

If

is a cycle, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C)
has no fixed components if and only if there exists an element
h\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C))
such that
\di_{X}(h)=C+H
, where

does not contain any component of

.

3.
The canonical trace ideals of rational singularities

In this section, for rational singularities, we provide a description of the trace of
A
-modules represented by divisors on a resolution
X
, as well as the canonical trace ideals.

We use the notation introduced in the preceding section.
Note that
A
is rational if and only if
K_{A}=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))
(cf.
[
9
, Theorem 3.4 and 3.5]
) and that
X\to\spec(A)
is the minimal resolution if and only if the canonical divisor
K_{X}
is nef.
Moreover,
A
is a rational Gorenstein singularity (or, rational double point) if and only if
K_{X}\equiv 0
.

Let
L
be a divisor on
X
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
L
has no fixed components.
Then

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))^{-1}=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L)).

Proof.

If
g\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L))
, then
gH^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\subset H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L+L))=A
.
Therefore,
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L))\subset H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))^{-1}
.

Let us show that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))^{-1}\subset H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L))
.
Assume that
g\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))^{-1}
.
For any
f\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\setminus\{0\}
, let
H_{f}
denote the divisor as in Remark
2.7
(2).
Since
gH^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\subset A
, we have

\di_{X}(g)+\di_{X}(f)=\di_{X}(g)-L+H_{f}\geq 0\ \text{ for }\ \forall f\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\setminus\{0\}.

Since
L
has no fixed components, moving
f
, we have the inequality
\di_{X}(g)-L\geq 0
.
Hence we obtain that
g\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L))
.
∎

The good properties of rational singularities that we use in this section are the following.

Lemma 3.2

(cf.
[
1
]
,
[

)
.

Assum that

is a rational singularity.
Then, we have the following.

(1)

\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z_{f})
.

(2)

L
is nef if and only if it is generated
;
if this is the case,
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))=0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
A
is a rational singularity, and let
L_{1}
and

be nef divisors on

.
Then
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{2}))\cdot H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1}))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1}+L_{2}))
.

Proof.

Take general elements
f_{1}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1}))
and

f_{2}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{2}))
.
By Lemma

(2),
\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1})
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{2})
are generated.
Thus we have the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{(f_{1},f_{2})}\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{2})\xrightarrow{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{-f_2}{f_1}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1}+L_{2})\to 0.

Since

,
we obtain

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1}+L_{2}))=f_{1}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{2}))+f_{2}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L_{1})).

Hence the assertion follows.
∎

4.
Criteria to be nearly Gorenstein

In this section, we give a criterion for rational surface singularities
to be nearly Gorenstein.
In the following two sections, by using this criterion, we will
provide several examples of nearly Gorenstein rational singularities, as well as a classification of nearly Gorenstein singularities within the classes of
almost reduced (Definition
5.1
)
rational singularities and quotient singularities.

In what follows, assume that
A
is a rational singularity and
X\to\spec(A)
is the minimal resolution.

Notation 4.1
.

We use the following notation.

(1)

Let
F
denote the fixed part of
-K_{X}
and
let
Z_{f}
be the fundamental cycle.

(2)

For a cycle
C=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}E_{i}
, let
\cff_{E_{i}}(C)=c_{i}
.

(3)

Let
E_{j}^{*}
denote the element of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Q}E_{i}
that satisfies
E_{j}^{*}E_{i}=-\delta_{ij}
.

Note that
F
is anti-nef by Proposition
3.4
and that for any
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L
on
X
, we have
L\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-LE_{i})E_{i}^{*}
.

We provide several characterization of nearly Gorenstein rational singularities in terms of combinatorial conditions.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
A
is a rational singularity, but not Gorenstein.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

A
is nearly Gorenstein.

(2)

Z_{f}=F
.

(3)

K_{X}+Z_{f}
is anti-nef.

(4)

Z_{f}
satisfies one of the following:

(a)

E
is irreducible.

(b)

There exists
E_{i_{0}}\subset E
such that
\cff_{E_{i_{0}}}(Z_{f})=2
and

(Z_{f}-E_{i_{0}})E_{i_{0}}=1,\ \ (Z_{f}-E_{i})E_{i}=2\ (i\neq i_{0})

(c)

There exist
E_{i_{1}},E_{i_{2}}\subset E
such that

\cff_{E_{i_{1}}}(Z_{f})=\cff_{E_{i_{2}}}(Z_{f})=1
and

(Z_{f}-E_{i})E_{i}=1\ (i\in\{i_{1},i_{2}\}),\ \ (Z_{f}-E_{i})E_{i}=2\ (i\not\in\{i_{1},i_{2}\})

(5)

For every component
E_{i}
with
E_{i}^{2}\leq-3
, we have
Z_{f}E_{i}\leq E_{i}^{2}+2
.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2).
Since
K_{X}
is nef and
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))=K_{A}
, it follows from Proposition
3.4
that
\tr K_{A}=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-F))
.
By Lemma

(1), we have
\mathfrak{m}=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z_{f}))

is nearly Gorenstein if and only if
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-F))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z_{f}))
.
Since
-F
and
-Z_{f}
have no fixed components by Lemma
3.2
(2),
we obtain that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-F))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z_{f}))
if and only if
F=Z_{f}
(cf. Lemma
2.7
).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3).
It follows from the fact that
-K_{X}-F
is nef.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2).
Since

,
F
is a nonzero anti-nef cycle and thus

.
Hence

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-F))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-Z_{f}))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})).

However, since

has no fixed components by the assumption and Lemma

(2), we have

.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4).
From the equality

There exists

and

.

A proof of Generalized Connected Wedge Theorem
Bowen Zhao

Abstract
. In the context of asymptotic 2 2 -to- 2 2 scattering process in AdS/CFT, the Connected Wedge Theorem identifies the existence of O ⁡ ( 1 / G N ) O(1/G_{N}) mutual information between suitable boundary subregions, referred to as decision regions, as a necessary but not sufficient condition for bulk-only scattering processes, i.e., nonempty bulk scattering region S 0 S_{0} . Recently, Liu and Leutheusser proposed an enlarged bulk scattering region S E S_{E} and conjectured that the non-emptiness of S E S_{E} fully characterizes the existence of O ⁡ ( 1 / G N ) O(1/G_{N}) mutual information between decision regions. Here, we provide a geometrical or general relativity proof for a slightly modified version of their conjecture.

1.1.
Introduction and Causality Constraints

1.2.
The Connected Wedge Theorem: Beyond Two-Point Causality

A more profound consistency requirement emerges when considering asymptotic quantum tasks involving multiple regions on
\partial M
. May
[
May19
]
discovered configurations where, for a
2
-to-
2
scattering process, the boundary scattering region is empty while a non-empty scattering region exists in the bulk. This means a local bulk protocol exists—sending inputs to a common spacetime point for interaction before distributing outputs—that has no local counterpart on the boundary.

The Connected Wedge Theorem (CWT) of
[
MPS20
]
establishes the necessary boundary condition for such a bulk scattering region to exist. It states that if an asymptotic scattering configuration in an asymptotically AdS
3
spacetime has a bulk scattering region but an empty boundary scattering region, then the boundary regions
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
(see below for definitions of
V_{1,2}
) associated with the two input points
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
must share
O(1/G_{N})
mutual information:

I(V_{1}:V_{2})=S(V_{1})+S(V_{2})-S(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\sim O(1/G_{N})

This large mutual information has a direct geometric interpretation via the Hubeny-Rangamani-Ryu-Takayanagi (HRRT) formula,
S(A)=\text{min}_{\gamma\sim A}(\frac{|\gamma|}{4G_{N}})+O(1)
. The
O(1/G_{N})
mutual information implies that the extremal surface computing
S(V_{1}\cup V_{2})
is not the union of the individual extremal surfaces for
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Consequently, the entanglement wedge
\mathcal{E}(V_{1}\cup V_{2})
becomes connected. The converse, however, is not universally true; a connected entanglement wedge does not guarantee a non-empty bulk scattering region, as shown by explicit counterexamples.

1.3.
Algebraic Reformulation and the Generalized Connected Wedge Theorem

A recent work by Liu and Leutheusser
[
LL25
]
reformulates this phenomenon in the language of operator algebras, leading to a proposed generalization of the CWT.

They emphasize that the key feature enabling non-local boundary protocols is the superadditivity of the large-
N
boundary algebra, a property distinct from the additivity typical of finite-dimensional systems. For von Neumann algebras
\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{V_{2}}
associated with boundary regions
V_{1}
and
V_{2}

•

Additivity would mean
\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}}\vee\mathcal{M}_{V_{2}}=(\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}}\cup\mathcal{M}_{V_{2}})^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}\cup V_{2}}

•

Superadditivity states
\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}}\vee\mathcal{M}_{V_{2}}=(\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}}\cup\mathcal{M}_{V_{2}})^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathcal{M}_{V_{1}\cup V_{2}}

This algebraic property finds a geometric dual through entanglement wedge reconstruction
\mathcal{M}_{V}=\mathcal{M}^{(\text{bulk})}_{\mathcal{E}(V)}
, where
\mathcal{M}^{(\text{bulk})}
indicates bulk von Neumann algebra and
\mathcal{E}(V)
is the entanglement wedge of
V
.
Assuming additivity holds in the bulk, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}^{(\text{bulk})}_{\mathcal{E}(V_{1})}\vee\mathcal{M}^{(\text{bulk})}_{\mathcal{E}(V_{2})}=\mathcal{M}^{(\text{bulk})}_{\mathcal{E}(V_{1})\cup\mathcal{E}(V_{2})}
, superadditivity of the boundary algebra is equivalent to an inclusion of entanglement wedges:

\mathcal{E}(V_{1})\cup\mathcal{E}(V_{2})\subset\mathcal{E}(V_{1}\cup V_{2}).

This inclusion is the algebraic signature of the geometric phase transition in which the HRRT surface jumps and the entanglement wedge becomes connected. Liu and Leutheusser identify additional operators in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}(V_{1}\cup V_{2})}
— those not contained in the algebra generated by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}(V_{1})}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}(V_{2})}
as the fundamental resources that facilitate the non-local boundary protocols required to simulate bulk-local tasks.

In this article, we prove a slightly modified version of their generalized Connected Wedge Conjecture. As noted in
[
MPS20
]
, bulk-only
2
-to-
2

2
-to-
2
scattering in asymptotically

\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}

2.
General Relativity proof

Similar to previous works, we assume throughout that: (i) the bulk spacetime
M
satisfies the null energy condition; (ii) the HRRT surface can be found by a maximin procedure; (iii) the spacetime is AdS-hyperbolic (i.e., the conformally compactified spacetime, comprising the bulk and its conformal boundary, admits a Cauchy slice); and (iv) the bulk region under consideration contains no singularities. Furthermore, we assume that the global boundary state is pure.

Abstract
Motivation-based recommendation systems uncover user behavior drivers. Motivation modeling, crucial for decision-making and content preference, explains recommendation generation. Existing methods often treat motivation as latent variables from interaction data, neglecting heterogeneous information like review text. In multimodal motivation fusion, two challenges arise: 1) achieving stable cross-modal alignment amid noise, and 2) identifying features reflecting the same underlying motivation across modalities. To address these, we propose LLM-driven Motivation-aware Multimodal Recommendation (LMMRec), a model-agnostic framework leveraging large language models for deep semantic priors and motivation understanding. LMMRec uses chain-of-thought prompting to extract fine-grained user and item motivations from text. A dual-encoder architecture models textual and interaction-based motivations for cross-modal alignment, while Motivation Coordination Strategy and Interaction–Text Correspondence Method mitigate noise and semantic drift through contrastive learning and momentum updates. Experiments on three datasets show LMMRec achieves up to a 4.98% performance improvement.

LMMRec is a model-agnostic recommendation system that efficiently reduces the effects of semantic noise and motivation alignment bias while fully utilizing the semantic potential of multimodal input.

II-C

Motivation Coordination Strategy

The core objective of the Motivation Coordination Strategy is to achieve bimodal semantic alignment through collaborative filtering interaction information. This approach constructs interaction motivation and textual motivation as two complementary perspectives. In order to facilitate cooperative optimization between views, the contrastive learning technique maximizes the difference between positive samples and negative samples in the embedding space while minimizing the representation distance of the former across the two views. We define the following objective function for learning the textual and interaction motivation embeddings.

II-D

Interaction-text Correspondence Method

Interaction motivations are often embedded in limited text segments, leading to noise from irrelevant or unreflective expressions. To address this, we introduce the Interaction-text Correspondence Method, which uses a momentum-based teacher model to assess whether paired motivations correspond during training. To provide the pseudo-labels needed by the teacher model, we build two unimodal encoders with momentum updates:
e_{a}^{\prime}
that correspond to the text encoder
e_{a}
and
e_{b}^{\prime}
that correspond to the graph encoder
e_{b}
. Specifically, the similarity between representations is computed through these two encoders as
v^{\prime}(G,F)=e_{a}^{\prime}(\textbf{s})^{\top}e_{b}^{\prime}(\textbf{x})
, with
v^{\prime}(F,G)=v^{\prime}(G,F)^{\top}

denote the text and interaction views, respectively. In the student model, the similarity computations

and
\textbf{u}^{ba}

and

are denoted as
\textbf{v}^{ab}
and
\textbf{v}^{ba}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{ICM}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(F,G)\sim\mathcal{C}}[\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf{v}^{ba}(F)\parallel\mathbf{u}^{ba}(F))

\displaystyle+\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf{v}^{ab}(G)\parallel\mathbf{u}^{ab}(G))],

Evaluation Metrics.

We adopt a full ranking evaluation over the complete item set, using standard metrics Recall@N and NDCG@N (N = 5, 10) to comprehensively assess recommendation effectiveness.

Implementation Details.

Experiments are conducted on an AMD EPYC 7742 CPU with 512 GB RAM and 8 NVIDIA Tesla A100 GPUs. Both model and LLM-derived embeddings are set to 32 dimensions. With a batch size of 512 and a learning rate of 0.003, the Adam optimizer is used to train all models. Overfitting is avoided by applying early stopping based on validation performance. Text-embeddings-3-large is utilized to extract semantic vectors, while GPT-4o-mini is used to generate text motivation.

III-B

Performance Comparison

Table
I
displays the experimental results, which show that LMMRec performs noticeably better than a variety of model-agnostic baselines. Specifically, in the two groups of experiments based on WeightedGCL and PolyCF, LMMRec consistently achieves improvements in both Recall and NDCG metrics, with particularly notable gains of up to 4.17% and 4.98% on the Yelp and Steam datasets, respectively. In contrast, UIST, ONCE, and AutoGraph improve model performance to some extent but yield limited overall gains. This performance advantage primarily stems from the deep semantic representation capability of the large language model introduced in LMMRec for motivation semantic modeling, enabling it to extract more interpretable and discriminative motivation features from the textual perspective. Meanwhile, the dual-encoder architecture and cross-modal alignment strategy effectively mitigate the semantic gap between text and interaction signals, facilitating consistent modeling in the high-level semantic space.

III-C

Ablation Study

We evaluate four model variants: w/o MCS: without the Motivation Coordination Strategy; w/o ICM: without the Interaction-text Correspondence Method; w/o GCN: substituting user and item embeddings for the GCN operation on interaction motivation embeddings; w/o TM: replacing the textual motivation embeddings with an MLP mapping. Table
II
shows that removing any component degrades performance, confirming each module’s effectiveness. Notably, omitting the Motivation Coordination Strategy causes the largest drop, highlighting its role in reducing multimodal noise and maintaining semantic consistency. The absence of the Interaction-text Correspondence Method also significantly impairs performance, while replacing GCN or textual motivation modeling weakens high-level motivation feature abstraction, demonstrating the complementarity of structured and semantic modeling.

III-D

Noise Robustness Analysis

We assess LMMRec’s robustness by injecting 5%–30% nonexistent interactions into the training data on Yelp, comparing PolyCE with UIST, ONCE, AutoGraph, and LMMRec. As Fig.
2
shows, all model-agnostic enhancements degrade with increasing noise, reflecting the general sensitivity of interactive recommendation models. LMMRec, however, consistently outperforms others, demonstrating superior robustness. This stems from the Motivation Coordination Strategy enforcing consistency in contrastive learning and the Interaction-text Correspondence Method mitigating cross-modal semantic shift, allowing stable extraction of meaningful motivation signals and preventing overfitting to spurious interactions.

IV
CONCLUSION

This paper proposes LLM-driven Motivation-aware Multimodal Recommendation (LMMRec) to enhance modeling of users’ deep behavioral motivations. Leveraging LLMs’ semantic priors and reasoning, LMMRec extracts fine-grained motivation features from textual and interaction data. A dual encoder architecture enables cross-modal alignment between semantic and collaborative spaces, while the Motivation Coordination Strategy and Interaction Text Correspondence Method reinforce consistency and robustness via contrastive learning and momentum updates. Extensive experiments on public datasets demonstrate LMMRec’s effectiveness, and future work will explore LLM-based causal motivation modeling and adaptive fusion for open domain and complex interaction scenarios.

Torsor Néron models of hyperkähler manifolds
Ljudmila Kamenova

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
X
be a compact hyperkähler
manifold equipped with a Lagrangian fibration

\pi:\;X\to Y
, and
X^{\prime}
the smooth locus of
\pi
.
We prove that over a complement to a codimension
\geqslant 2
subset in
Y

the projection
\pi:\;X^{\prime}\to Y
has a natural structure of
a torsor over an abelian group bundle, which can be understood
as a complex analytic variant of the Néron model construction.
This gives an independent proof of a result by Y.-J. Kim.

After a while, we realized that our results

The original idea of Bogomolov was to apply the
construction of Néron models to produce smooth

In

proving a version of

Another novelty of this paper is our definition
of torsors, which significantly simplifies the exposition.
We define a torsor as a set
R
with a map

R\times R\times R\to R
, satisfying certain
algebraic conditions. The utility of this notion
is due to the fact that a compact complex torus
admits a unique structure of a holomorphic torsor.
Then, any abelian fibration can have at most
one structure of a torsor bundle over a group
bundle, and this torsor structure has at most
one extension to the special fibers. This approach
allows us to discuss the torsor analogue of the
Néron model for abelian fibrations which have
no sections.

The fibrations we consider are always proper morphisms, and have compact
complex tori for general fibers.

2
Néron models

2.1
Néron models and canonical models

We introduce Néron models, starting from the
traditional, scheme-theoretic definition (see e. g.

[
BLR
,
BHPT
]
), and
proceed to their complex-geometric interpretation,
following J. Kollár
[
Ko
]
.

The notion of Néron model can be defined in bigger generality,
but for most practical purposes, people consider the Néron models
of abelian fibrations. In this context, “abelian fibration”
is understood as a proper holomorphic map
\pi:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y
equipped
with a relatively ample bundle with
an abelian variety as a general fiber and a section. If
Y
is quasiprojective,

\pi
defines an abelian variety over the field of rational
functions on
Y
, and this section represents its zero.

Note that the abelian fibration, as defined above,
is equipped with a section, hence it has no multiple fibers.

Definition 2.1:
Let
R
be a Dedekind domain,
K
its fraction
field, and
Y
a scheme over
K
.
For a scheme
{\cal X}
over
R

we denote the corresponding scheme

{\cal X}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}R}\operatorname{Spec}K

over the fraction field by
{\cal X}_{K}
.
A
Néron model
of
Y
over
R
is
a smooth, separated finite-type
R
-scheme

{\cal Y}
such that
{\cal Y}_{K}=Y

and
{\cal Y}
satisfies
the Néron mapping
property
(NMP): for any smooth
R
-scheme

{\cal X}
and any
K
-morphism
f_{K}:\;{\cal X}_{K}\to{\cal Y}_{K}
,

f_{K}
can be uniquely extended to a morphism

f:\;{\cal X}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\cal Y}
.

Remark 2.2:

Uniqueness of a Néron model is clear.
As follows from
[
BLR
, Theorem 1.4/3]
,
the Néron model always exists for any abelian variety over
K
.
Also, the Néron mapping property implies that the Néron model
of an algebraic group over
K
is a group scheme over
R
.

Remark 2.3:
The Néron model was defined over a base which is
a spectrum of a Dedekind domain. In the complex geometric
setting, this amounts to defining a Néron model
over the spectrum of a ring of germs of a
smooth complex curve (which is a discrete valuation ring,
that is, a local Dedekind domain). This is a local version
of this notion, which can be globalized as follows.
Define
the Néron model
of an abelian fibration

\pi:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}C

over a smooth complex curve
C
as a complex variety

{\cal X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}C
bimeromorphic to
X

which gives its Néron model if tensored by the ring

{\cal O}_{C,c}
of germs of any point
c\in C
.

Note, however, that the Lagrangian fibrations we deal with
do not, generally speaking, admit sections, hence they
do not define abelian fibrations. If we restrict
the fibration to a sufficiently small open set in the
base, local sections exist, and we can always define
its Néron model.

for the proof,

In the present version,

Recall that

is a variety, fibered over

Corollary 2.5:

2.2
Canonical model of Lagrangian fibrations

The canonical model can be applied to construct
a Néron model for abelian fibrations over a base
of dimension
>1
. This is an elementary alternative
to a much more general strategy chosen by Y.-J. Kim
(
[
Ki
]
).

Definition 2.6:
Let
f:\;X_{0}\to Y
be a smooth holomorphic submersion
(not necessarily proper), and
s:\;Y\to X_{0}

a section. Consider a map
X_{0}\times_{Y}X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}X_{0}

commuting with the projection to
Y
. We say that
(X_{0},s,\mu)

is
a holomorphic group bundle
if the map
\mu
is
submersive and defines a group structure on each fiber

f^{-1}(y)
, for all
y\in Y
, and
s(y)
is its identity element.

Further on, we will assume that

f:\;X\to Y
is a Lagrangian fibration
on a hyperkähler manifold, though
we expect that this construction might
work in bigger generality.

In this subsection, we construct
“the Néron model” of such a morphism
f
,
(called, following
[
Ko
]
, “the canonical model”)
when
f
admits a holomorphic section

s:\;Y\to X
. In this case, the canonical model is
a holomorphic group bundle.
In Subsection
2.3
,
we generalize this notion to abelian fibrations
with no sections, in which case the canonical model
is a smooth holomorphic abelian torsor bundle
(
2.3
).

Definition 2.7:
Let
f:\;X\to Y
be an abelian fibration of
projective manifolds. Let
X_{0}\subset X
be the smooth
locus of
f
, that is,
the set of all points where
D\!f
has maximal
rank. Clearly,
X_{0}
is a dense,
Zariski open subset of
X
. A smooth curve
C\subset Y

is called
Néron regular
if the smooth
locus of the map
f^{-1}(C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}C
is
X_{0}\cap f^{-1}(C)
.

We are going to show that a “generic” smooth curve

C\subset Y
is Néron regular.

Definition 2.8:

The
discriminant

\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\subset Y
of
f
is
the set of its critical values.
Let
X_{i}\subset X
be the set of all points

x\in X
such that
D\!f{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{x}}\right.}
has rank
\dim Y-i
,

\overline{X}_{i}
its closure,
and
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{i}:=f(\overline{X}_{i+1})
. Clearly,
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{0}=\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
.

Claim 2.9:

For any
i
, we have
\operatorname{codim}_{Y}\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{i}\geqslant i+1
.

Proof:
Since
\operatorname{rk}D\!f{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{i}}}\right.}\leqslant\dim Y-i-1
,
Remmert rank theorem
(
[
Sch
, page 36, Chapter 3, §3]
)
implies that
\dim f(\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{i})\leqslant\dim Y-i-1
.

Claim 2.10:
The complement
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
is smooth.

Proof:
By definition,

\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}=f(\overline{X}_{1})\backslash f(\overline{X}_{2})
,
where
\overline{X}_{i}
denotes the closure. Also by definition,

f
has rank
\dim Y-1
on
X_{1}
. Therefore,

\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
is the set of regular values of

f
on
\overline{X}_{1}
.

Proposition 2.11:

Let
f:\;X\to Y
be an abelian fibration
and
C\subset Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
a smooth curve
such that the intersection
C\cap\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}

is transversal. Then

is Néron regular.

Proof. Step 1:
Clearly, any
x\in f^{-1}(C)\cap X_{0}
belongs to
the smooth locus of
f^{-1}(C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}C
.
Since
C\subset Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
, and

\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}=f(X_{2})
, we have
f^{-1}(C)\subset X_{1}\cup X_{0}
.
It remains to show that
f
is not regular in
any point
x\in f^{-1}(C)\cap X_{1}
.

Step 2:

For any
x\in X_{1}
, the image
D\!f(T_{x}X)

has codimension 1 in
TY
. By Sard lemma,
the map
X_{1}\to\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
is regular over
a general point
y\in\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
. Therefore, for any
x\in f^{-1}(y)\cap X_{1}
,
the image
D\!f(T_{x}X_{1})
had dimension
\dim Y-1
.
This implies that
D\!f(T_{x}X)=D\!f(T_{x}X_{1})
,
giving
D\!f(T_{x}X_{1})\subset T_{y}Y

for any
x\in f^{-1}(y)\cap X_{1}
.
Since the limit of tangent vectors
to a complex variety belongs to its
Zariski tangent space, and the Zariski tangent cone
of
\overline{X}_{1}
is the closure of the Zariski tangent
cone to
X_{1}
, this implies that

D\!f(T_{x}X_{1})\subset T_{y}\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
for any
x\in\overline{X}_{1}
.

Step 3:

Let
x\in X_{1}
.
Since
D\!f(T_{x}X)
has codimension 1 in

and
\dim\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}=\dim Y-1
,
the relation
D\!f(T_{x}X_{1})\subset T_{y}\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}

implies that
D\!f(T_{\overline{x}}X)\subset T_{y}\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}

for any
\overline{x}\in X_{1}
.

Step 4:

Let
C\subset Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
be a smooth curve
such that the intersection
C\cap\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}

is transversal, and
x\in X_{1}\cap f^{-1}(C)
.
Since

is transversal to
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
,
the tangent space
T_{y}C
does not belong to
T_{y}\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
,
where

. Since
D\!f(T_{x}X)\subset T_{y}\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
,
the differential
D\!f:\;T_{x}f^{-1}(C)\to C

cannot be surjective, hence

belongs to the
singular locus of the map
f:\;f^{-1}(C)\to C
.

Remark 2.12:

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}
be a sufficiently big family of smooth curves
in
Y

of

with complex projective subspaces
of appropriate dimension. By Bertini’s theorem,
a general curve

is transversal to the smooth divisor

\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
and does not intersect
a codimension 2 subvariety
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}\subset Y
.
Therefore, a general curve

is Néron regular.

Using
2.2
, we
observe that the smooth locus
f^{-1}(C)_{0}
of
the map
f^{-1}(C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}C

coincides with the intersection
of the smooth locus
X_{0}
of
f:\;X\to Y

and
f^{-1}(C)
for any Néron regular curve.

2.1
implies that

f^{-1}(C)_{0}
admits a natural group bundle structure if

this group bundle structure is actually obtained from
a group bundle structure on
X_{0}\cap f^{-1}(Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1})

Theorem 2.13:

Let
X
be a hyperkähler manifold,

f:\;X\to Y
a Lagrangian fibration,
and
X_{0}\subset X
its smooth locus.
Denote by
J(f)
the manifold
X_{0}\cap f^{-1}(Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1})
.
Assume that
f
admits
a holomorphic section
s:\;Y\to X_{0}
.
Assume, moreover, that the relative canonical
bundle
K_{f}:=K_{X}\otimes f^{*}K_{Y}^{-1}
is
isomorphic to a pullback of a line bundle on
Y
.
Then there exists a unique group bundle structure
on
f:\;J(f)\to Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
with the
following properties:

(i)

For each
y\in Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
,

s(y)
is the identity element of the fiber
f^{-1}\cap J(f)
.

(ii)

For any Néron generic curve
C\subset Y
,
the group structure on
J(f)
is compatible with the
group structure defined
on
f^{-1}(C)\cap X_{0}
by

2.1
.

(iii)

The natural action of the associated group bundle
J(f)

on itself extends holomorphically to
f^{-1}(Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1})
.

Proof. Step 1:
The uniqueness of such a group structure is clear.
Indeed, holomorphic group structures on a compact complex torus
are in bijective correspondence with the choice of an identity
element. Since a general fiber of
f
is a compact torus,
the group structure on the special fibers
of
f:\;J(f)\to Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
is
unique by continuity.

Step 2:

To prove the existence of the group
bundle structure on
f:\;J(f)\to Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
,
consider a special fiber
f^{-1}(y)\subset J(f)
.
Then
y\in\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
. Since

Y
is projective, it admits a family
{\mathfrak{C}}
of
Néron regular curves satisfying the
assumptions of
2.2
.
For a general curve
C\in{\mathfrak{C}}
containing
y
, the intersection

J(f)\cap f^{-1}(C)
admits a group bundle
structure. This group bundle structure,
in turn, defines a group structure on
f^{-1}(y)\cap J(f)
.
To finish the proof of

2.2
,
it remains to show that this group structure
is independent of the choice of
C\in{\mathfrak{C}}
.

Step 3:

Let
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in{\cal O}_{Y}
be a collection
of holomorphic functions defined in
a neighbourhood
U\subset Y
of
y\in Y
, such that
their differentials generate
\Omega^{1}U
,
and let
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}\in T(f^{-1}U)
the
corresponding holomorphic Hamiltionian vector fields.
They are defined by the relation
i_{\xi_{j}}\Omega=du_{j}
,
where
i_{\xi_{j}}
denotes the contraction and
\Omega

the holomorphic symplectic form. Since
du_{i}
vanish
on the tangent space to the fibers of
f
, which are Lagrangian,
the vector fields
\xi_{i}
are tangent to the fibers of
f
.
They are also non-degenerate and linearly independent
on the smooth locus of
f
. Therefore, they trivialize
the tangent bundle
Tf^{-1}(Y)\cap X_{0}

to the smooth part of the special fiber
f^{-1}(y)
.

The Lie group structure on the fibers of
f

can be obtained by taking the exponent
of its Lie algebra which is generated by
\xi_{i}
.
This exponent is well defined in the special
fiber as well. By continuity of the group structure
defined by the Néron model of
f^{-1}(C)
, it is also obtained
by taking exponents of
\langle\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}\rangle
.
Therefore, it is independent of the choice of
C
.

Step 4:

To prove (iii), we notice that the Hamiltonian vector
fields defined in Step 3 extend to holomorphic
vector fields on the complex manifold
f^{-1}U
;
the corresponding group action coincides with the
action of
J(f)
on
X_{0}
.

The statement of

can be formulated in a less restrictive situation,

For instance, it is true for

where

2.3
Torsor bundles

By definition, a torsor over an abelian group

G
is a set where
G
acts freely and transitively.
However, the torsor structure can be defined
without referring to an
a priori
given group structure as follows.

Definition 2.15: An abelian torsor
is a set
S
equipped
with a map
\mu:\;S\times S\times S\to S
such that

(i)

for
any
e\in S
, the map
x\cdot y:=\mu(x,y,e)

defines a commutative and associative product on
S

with
e
an identity element

(ii)

for any fixed
y\in S
,
the map
x\mapsto x\cdot y
is bijective.

Remark 2.16:
From the axioms of the abelian
torsor, it is clear that
\cdot
defines a structure
of an abelian group on
S
.

Remark 2.17:
The identity element axiom means that

\mu(x,y,y)=x
for any
x
and
y
. Given a group
structure on
S
, the torsor operation

\mu
can be defined as
\mu(x,y,z)=x+y-z
.

This language is best suited to
define Néron models for Lagrangian fibrations
which have no sections.

Definition 2.18:

Let
f:\;X\to Y
be a holomorphic submersion,
not necessarily proper, and

\mu:\;X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X\to X

a holomorphic map commuting with the projection to
Y
,
which defines an abelian torsor structure in each
fiber of
f
. Then
(f,\mu)
is called
a smooth
holomorphic abelian torsor bundle

over
Y
, or simply
a torsor bundle
.

Remark 2.19:
Sincee
f
is submersive,
f:\;X\to Y

admits local holomorphic sections by the implicit map
theorem. Given a local section
s:\;V\to U
,
we obtain a group bundle structure on
f:\;f^{-1}(V)\to V
.
Clearly, this group bundle is independent of the
choice of
s
, hence we might glue the local group
bundles obtained this way to a group bundle
J(f)
over

Y
. This group bundle acts on
X
holomorphically, and
this action is free and transitive on all fibers.
In other words,
X
is a torsor bundle
over the group bundle
J(f)
.

We can reformulate
2.1

using this language.

Proposition 2.20:

Let
{\cal X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}C
be an
abelian fibration over a smooth complex curve
C
.
Assume that
{\cal X}
is smooth, algebraic, projective over
C
, and
the relative canonical bundle of
{\cal X}/C

is trivial. Then the smooth locus of
\varphi

admits a natural structure of an abelian
torsor bundle, which is uniquely determined
by
\varphi
.

Proof:
Existence of the torsor structure
is already explained in the proof of
2.1
;
it follows from
[
Ko
, Corollary 2 (2.2a)]
.
The uniqueness of the torsor structure on
an abelian variety is clear because it is
the unique torsor structure compatible with
the flat coordinates, which are intrinsically
defined by the holomorphic differentials.
Since the torsor structure map

\mu:\;{\cal X}\times_{C}{\cal X}\times_{C}{\cal X}\to{\cal X}

is continuous, it is uniquely extended
to the special fibers.

This observation brings the following remarkable theorem.

Theorem 2.21:

Let
f:\;X\to Y
be
a Lagrangian fibration on a hyperkähler manifold,

\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
its discriminant, and
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}\subset\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}

the locus of
\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}
defined in
2.2
.
Denote by
Y_{0}\subset Y
the set of all
points
y\in Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
such that
f
has local
holomorphic sections over a neighbourhood of
y
with values
in the smooth locus of
f
. Denote by
X_{0}^{\prime}

the intersection of the smooth locus of
f
with

f^{-1}(Y_{0})
, and let

Y^{\prime}_{0}:=Y\backslash\operatorname{{\sf Discr}}_{1}
. Then
f:\;X_{0}^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}_{0}

admits a unique structure of an abelian torsor bundle,
compatible with the torsor bundle structures
on
f^{-1}(C)
obtained in

2.3
,
for any Néron regular curves
C\subset Y_{0}
.
Moreover, the natural action of the associated group bundle
J(f)

extends holomorphically to
f^{-1}(Y_{0})
.

Proof:
By definition of
X_{0}
, the map
f:\;X_{0}^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}_{0}

admits local sections. Let
U\subset Y^{\prime}_{0}

be an open subset and
s:\;U\to X_{0}^{\prime}
a holomorphic section.
Then
2.2
and

2.2

produce a unique structure of a group bundle on
the fibration
f^{-1}(U)\cap X^{\prime}_{0}\to U
,
such that
s(y)
gives the identity element
in each fiber
f^{-1}(y)
. A different choice
s_{1}:\;\;U\to X_{0}^{\prime}

of a section produces a different group structure,
but the corresponding group bundles are isomorphic.
The isomorphism map is given by a parallel translation
on each fiber,
x\mapsto x-s(f(x))+s_{1}(f(x))
.
Therefore, the torsor structure on the fibration

f^{-1}(U)\to U
is independent from the choice of a section
s
.
Gluing together the structure maps

f^{-1}(U)\times_{U}f^{-1}(U)\times_{U}f^{-1}(U)\to U
,
we obtain a structure of a torsor

X^{\prime}_{0}\times_{Y^{\prime}_{0}}X^{\prime}_{0}\times_{Y^{\prime}_{0}}X^{\prime}_{0}\to Y^{\prime}_{0}

extending the standard torsor structure
on the general fibers of
f:\;X^{\prime}_{0}\to Y^{\prime}_{0}
.

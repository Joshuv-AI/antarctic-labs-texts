Focused shock wave therapy (ESWT) and electromagnetic transduction therapy (EMTT) may improve post-operative bone healing in selective foot and ankle surgeries: a case-control pilot study
Authors: Amol Saxena, Elizabeth Bondi, Lennart Gerdesmeyer, Carlos Leal, Karsten Knobloch, Nicola Maffulli, Ludger Gerdesmeyer, Adam S Tenforde
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSurgical procedures of the foot and ankle commonly involve treatment of bone anatomy and include osteotomies and open reduction with internal fixation. A potential complication of these types of surgeries is delayed or non-union of bones. Delayed osseous healing prolongs post-operative recovery and may result in delayed return to work, increased financial costs and impaired health outcomes. Identifying additional non-invasive therapies that aid in bone healing may improve surgical outcomes. Focused Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT) and Electromagnetic Transduction Therapy (EMTT) are both procedures proposed to improve bone and soft tissue healing.Materials/MethodsA matched analysis was conducted on foot and ankle surgeries performed from June 2022 through April 2023. Ten patients (controls) were identified who had surgical management for talar osteochondral defects, foot and ankle fractures, and joint disease who were matched with ten patients who received ESWT and EMTT (ESWT + EMTT patients). The ESWT + EMTT patients received one session of focused ESWT (“Duolith”™, Storz Medical AG, Tägerwilen, CH) applied up to 0.35 mJ.mm2 to the surgical site within three weeks post-operation and two sessions of EMTT (“Magnetolith”™, Storz Medical AG, Tägerwilen, CH) set at 6000 pulses each, power level 8, and 8 Hz. The primarily outcome was differences in VAS score at the time of weight-bearing and time to return to activity calculated in weeks for ESWT + EMTT compared to control patients. An exploratory analysis included reviewing radiographs for evidence of radiographic healing on plain film radiographs performed four weeks post-operatively. Statistical analyses were performed using student t-tests and Fisher’s Exact tests, with P < 0.05 set as statistically significant.ResultsTwenty patients (15 women and 5 men) of average age 52.1 ± 16.6 years (range 18–73 years-old) were included with similar age and sex between EMTT + ESWT and control patients. On average, ESWT + EMTT had lower post-operative VAS pain scores than control patients (0.2 ± 0.4 vs 3.1 ± 1.3, P = 0.0001). Additionally, the mean return to activity was reduced for ESWT + EMTT patients (13.2 ± 2.9 weeks vs 22.4 ± 9.2 weeks, P = 0.01). A larger portion of ESWT + EMTT patients had evidence of radiographic healing at four weeks post-operatively compared to controls (8 of 10 vs 2 of 10, P = .03).ConclusionsThe combination of ESWT and EMTT post-operatively may improve pain and earlier return to activity, and treatment was well-tolerated after foot and ankle surgeries. These results can be further explored through placebo-controlled randomized clinical trials in surgical management of foot and ankle conditions and other indications.

Introduction

Surgical management of foot and ankle conditions commonly involves targeted interventions to bone anatomy including osteotomies or open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF). Goals during the early post-operative period include optimizing healing through external or weight bearing restrictions, stabilizing the surgical site, and pain management to resolve swelling and minimize scar tissue. Bone remodeling is complex and involves inflammation and integration of multiple physiological processes and cell types such as mesenchymal stem cells, osteoprogenitor cells, and osteoblasts to achieve mechanical homeostasis of bone [1, 2].

As with all surgeries, there are risks for complications including failure to achieve osseous healing and a resulting delayed-union or non-union. A non-union is defined as cessation of radiographic healing of both periosteal and endosteal bone [3]. The overall incidence of osseous non-union has been reported to vary from 8 to 27% for common foot and ankle surgeries in a large cohort, [4] and rates of healing may vary by anatomy, fracture morphology, surgery, and response to interventions. [5, 6] Delay in osseous healing prolongs the post-operative course and may interfere with return to physical activities and employment. The resulting high costs and functional limitations resulting from delayed bone healing suggest value in identifying strategies to address this challenge. The ideal treatment modality would: [1] improve bone healing, further minimizing the risk of developing a delayed union or non-union and [2] reduce pain and swelling thereby decreasing demand for opioids or other medications for management of post-operative pain.

Two emerging strategies that may address bone healing and disrupt pain are focused extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) and electromagnetic transduction therapy (EMTT). ESWT is a non-surgical treatment option that has been proposed to treat a variety of musculoskeletal conditions thought to result through principles of mechanotransduction [7]. ESWT has also been show to stimulate cells and the extracellular matrix to induce the expression of angiogenic and osteogenic osteogenic growth factors [8]. Additionally, ESWT may stimulate nociceptive C-fibers to influence pain pathways [9]. These cellular effects induced by ESWT are all important components in bone and tissue healing that may improve post-operative outcomes. The biological plausibility of ESWT for bone healing is further supported by human subject research, including clinical trials suggesting similar healing with ESWT compared to ORIF for non-union long bone fractures. [10] Smaller cohorts of patients with non-union have had successful resolution of fracture including after ORIF of the medial malleolus and in two cases involving fifth metatarsal. [11, 12]

Electromagnetic transduction therapy (EMTT) is a non-invasive treatment that generates a high energy form of pulsed electromagnetic magnetic field (PEMF). [13] Medical devices delivering PEMF were initially approved by Food and Drug Administration in 1979 for the treatment of bone fractures and non-unions. Studies suggest that PEMF stimulates osteoblast and mesenchymal stem cell proliferation and differentiation, extracellular matrix production, osteogenesis, and inhibition of the inflammatory processes by reducing PGE2 and pro-inflammatory cytokines. [14, 15] However, a randomized control clinical study did not demonstrate measurable benefits, including for post-operative management of talar osteochondral defects. [16] While the proposed mechanisms of PEMF on treating osseous injury are plausible, the absence of sufficient clinical results may be in part explained by low level, high frequency magnetic impulses that occurs as a single magnetic impulse generated by PEMF devices. [13] EMTT may address the biophysical limitations of PEMF, but has limited studies evaluating outcomes in the treatment of osseous injuries when combined with ESWT. [17–20] EMTT devices can have significantly greater magnetic field strength, ranging between 80 to 150mT, an increased impulse release frequency of 3Hz with an oscillation frequency greater than 100kHz, and has a shorter duration of the impulses. EMTT may act through principles of electroporation and piezoelectric effects on the cells. [13, 21] Nitric oxide release has a strong influence on bone healing through mechanisms including angiogenesis and influences on inflammation [22]. Gerdesmeyer et al. [13] studied the effects of EMTT on human bone marrow mesenchymal stems cells with regards to osteogenic stimulation and differentiation. They found a higher expression of collagen I, alkaline phosphatase, and VEGF in their treatment group, which act as inductors of osteogenesis and angiogenesis [13].

Despite the biological plausibility of ESWT and EMTT to stimulate bone healing published studies to date are limited to case reports. To date, published reports on use of EMTT with ESWT in the post-operative management of osseous surgeries has not been described including radiographic healing and return to physical activity. The purpose of this study is to explore whether the combination of ESWT and EMTT may improve healing following surgery. Based on proposed mechanisms of ESWT and EMTT for bone healing, we hypothesized that patients receiving both treatments post-operatively would have faster return to physical activity and reduced post-operative pain compared to matched controls. We also explored whether improved radiographic healing would be observed using standard post-operative plain film radiographs in patients receiving ESWT and EMTT compared to controls.

Methods

A matched analysis was conducted on foot and ankle procedures from June 2022 through April 2023. IRB was obtained for this study. Inclusion criteria involved consenting patients undergoing treatment for a talar osteochondral defect, foot and ankle fracture repair, and Lapidus or Ludloff bunionectomy (Table 1). Ten patients receiving ESWT and EMTT (ESWT + EMTT) were matched to ten patients in a control group. Controls included patients who had a prior identical procedure on the contralateral limb, as well as those matched to patients in the intervention group with identical procedures (“best neighbor”). At least one control was matched to ESWT + EMTT patients. No effort was made to have equal number of patients in each group nor match sex or race/ethnicity. All patients had their VAS score (“0” equaling no pain to “10” being uncontrollable pain) self-assessed at the time of weightbearing as the primary outcome measure. A minimal clinical difference of 2 points on the VAS score at weightbearing was determined to be significant. Additionally, differences in time to return to activity (RTA) was calculated in weeks between groups. Exploratory outcome was radiographic healing via plain film radiographs determined by an independent radiologist at four weeks post-index surgery. Evidence of healing was defined as bone healing confirmed via bridging of opposite cortices and trabecularization across the fusion or fracture site.

The intervention group (ESWT + EMTT) had one session of focused ESWT (“Duolith”™, Storz Medical AG, Tägerwilen, CH) applied up to 0.35 mJ.mm2 to the surgical site within three weeks post-operation and two sessions of EMTT (“Magnetolith”™, Storz Medical AG, Tägerwilen, CH) set at 6000 pulses each, power level 8, and 8 Hz. Patients were excluded if they did not meet the inclusion criteria or if their primary outcome measure were not assessed. Additional exclusion criteria including current tobacco use, diabetes, rheumatological or immunodeficiency, revision surgery and Worker’s Compensation/Industrial cases were excluded. Statistical analyses were performed with Excel™(Redmond, WA, USA) and STAT SAK© (G Dallal, Andover, MA, USA). P < 0.05 was required for Student’s-T and Fisher’s Exact tests, which were two-tailed.

On average, VAS pain scores for weight-bearing were lower post-treatment for ESWT + EMTT (0.2 ± 0.4 weeks) compared to control patients (3.1 ± 1.3 weeks) and effect size was 2.9 (0.2 to 3.6, 95%CI, P = 0.0001). The average return to activity was reduced an average of 9.2 weeks in ESWT + EMTT patients compared to controls (13.2 ± 2.9 weeks vs 22.4 ± 9.2, P = 0.01; Tables 2 and Fig. 1). Finally, the proportion of patients with radiographic healing noted at four weeks following surgery was higher in ESWT + EMTT compared control patients (8/10 vs 2/10, P = 0.03). Use of EMTT did not result in pain or observed side effects, and pain during the ESWT was tolerated.

Discussion

The purpose of this study was to investigate whether combined ESWT and EMTT provides evidence of improved pain, reduced time for return to activity, and to explore early evidence of bone healing following operative management of ankle and foot conditions compared to a historical cohort. We observed reduction in pain and early return to physical activity in patients receiving ESWT and EMTT. Additionally, post-operative plain film radiographs at four weeks suggested a larger portion of patients had improved evidence of radiographic healing receiving ESWT and EMTT compared to controls from a historic cohort. These findings collectively suggest combined ESWT and EMTT may improve early surgical outcomes possibly through enhanced bone healing after ankle and foot surgery.

Our findings are consistent with prior literature suggesting the biological plausibility for use of ESWT and EMTT for promoting bone healing. Focused ESWT differs from EMTT in biophysical effects on tissue but may both provide non-invasive strategies to promote bone healing. ESWT has been reported to have high rate of success in treatment of non-union fractures [23] Moreover, there is a study indicating that ESWT has demonstrated comparable effectiveness to surgical procedures in treating non-union fractures, while offering the advantage of being more cost-effective with fewer side effects. [10] This finding suggests that ESWT could potentially even be considered the “therapy of first choice” for treating suitable non- union fractures [24].

EMTT is a type of electromagnetic treatment, differing from PEMFs, by operating with a higher oscillation frequency (100–300 kHz) and a higher magnetic field strength (between 80 and 150 mT).(13) Since EMTT is newer treatment, the current research on the influence of EMTT on bone healing is still in its early stages. However, traditional PEMF treatment have suggested a positive correlation with bone healing and are commonly referred to as a “bone stimulator” device in clinical practice. Multiple studies have evaluated the mechanism of PEMF treatment and suggest it may enhance osteogenesis by promoting osteogenic differentiation and mineralization, stimulate angiogenesis, and inhibit osteoclast differentiation and formation. [25–31] Moreover, clinical studies have consistently shown that PEMF treatment has beneficial effects on pain management in orthopedic conditions such as osteoarthritis and during fracture healing. [32, 33] Our current study adds to limited cases exploring the influence of ESWT and EMTT on bone healing.

While this study represents the largest case–control series published to date comparing combined EMTT and ESWT to conventional post-operative management of foot and ankle conditions, there are limitations. The populations studied had numerous indications for surgery including management of talar osteochondral defects, joint disease and fracture management. The heterogeneous population reflects this being a pilot investigation and the conditions studied were based on prior work exploring PEMF to enhance healing. To address the variety of conditions, we attempted to match by age, sex, and type of surgery. Additionally, the study design cannot account for the placebo effect that could contribute to differences in outcomes or whether results would be different using other devices or forms of ESWT or EMTT. Post-operative visits and plain film radiographs were obtained using standard practice of the same surgeon to standardize the measures of radiographic healing and time intervals for assessing post-operative pain and return to activities. CT can provide more detailed assessment of bone healing after surgery but was not obtained as this was not standard of care. Pain with weight bearing was measured at different post-operative times; however, variation in timing may be partially accounted for by matching of ESWT + EMTT to control patients by condition and surgical approach. Despite these limitations, results from this study can be further validated with placebo-controlled randomized control trials and include surgical management of conditions outside the foot and ankle and utilize CT to characterize rates of bone healing at different times post-operatively.

In summary, our results suggest ESWT with EMTT may be a strategy to promote post-operative management of foot and ankle surgeries to improve surgical outcome measures as well as reduce the risk of non-healing bone disorders such as non-unions. As a non-invasive therapy option without any known side effects, the combination of ESWT and EMTT holds a promising additional treatment option for bone healing disorders. Moreover, this combined therapy has the potential to complement conventional treatments, contributing to improved outcomes in terms of pain reduction and enhanced functionality, potentially allowing patients to return to work earlier and reducing disability related costs. It is important to acknowledge the limited number of similar and higher-level studies investigating the combination therapy of ESWT and EMTT. Further research is needed to determine the optimal treatment protocol (time, strength, duration, frequency, etc.) for different types of pathologies and surgeries including foot and ankle and different anatomical locations and surgical indications.

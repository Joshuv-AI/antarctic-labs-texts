Inducing spectral gaps for the cohomological Laplacians of Sp 2 ​ n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})
Piotr Mizerka, Jakub Szymański

Abstract
We show that the spectral gap of the first cohomological Laplacian Δ 1 \Delta_{1} for Sp 2 ​ n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}) follows once a slightly stronger assumption holds for some Sp 2 ​ m ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(\mathbb{Z}) , where n ≥ m n\geq m . As an application of this result, we provide explicit lower bounds for some quotients of Sp 2 ​ n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}) for any n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 .

Introduction

Recently computer-assisted proofs of property (T) have gained a lot of attention. One of the major outcomes of this approach was the proof of property (T) for
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
, the automorphism groups of free groups on
n
letters for
n\geq 5

[
10
,
12
]
, as well as
[
19
]
for
n=4
. Another advantage of such approach is providing explicit lower bounds for
Kazhdan constants
which are closely related to the pace at which expander families defined by property (T) groups get connected and to the speed of generating random elements in groups
[
14
,
10
]
.

Suppose
G
is a group generated by a finite symmetric set
S
and define the Laplacian
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in S}s
. It is known that property (T) of
G
is equivalent to the existence of a positive
spectral gap

\lambda
, i.e. a positive constant such that
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}
for some finite number of elements
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}G

[
21
]
. If such a positive
\lambda
exists, it turns out to provide a lower bound for the Kazhdan constant
\kappa
:

\kappa\geq\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{|S|}}.

Recently, the work of U. Bader and P.W. Nowak
[
3
]
allowed to formulate another condition for property (T), involving explicitly the whole presentation of a given group. Let
G
be a finitely presented group
G
generated by a set
\mathcal{S}
. The
Fox calculus

[
6
,
7
]
, its cohomological properties
[
15
]
and the results from
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
provide us with the Laplacian
\Delta_{1}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}(\mathbb{R}G)
(the precise definition of
\Delta_{1}
is given in
section

1
) such that the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a positive
\lambda>0
such that
\Delta_{1}-\lambda I_{|\mathcal{S}|}=\sum_{i}M_{i}^{*}M_{i}
for some finite number of
M_{i}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}(\mathbb{R}G)
.

(2)

G
has property (T).

Remark 0.1
.

The implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
follows from the paper of U. Bader and P.W. Nowak
[
3
]
, while the converse implication from the recent paper of U. Bader and R. Sauer
[
4
]
(see Theorem 3.11 and the paragraph below it there). Originally, the assumption
(1)
was proved in
[
3
]
to imply vanishing of the first cohomologies of a given group with coefficients in any unitary representations. The latter statement is known, however, to be equivalent to property (T) for finitely presented groups
.

In this paper we are focused of
G_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})
, the group of
2n\times 2n
symplectic matrices over integers, that is, comprising of integer-valued matrices
A
satisfying the relation

A^{T}J_{2n}A=J_{2n},

where

J_{2n}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}.

These groups admit a presentation defined by Steinberg generators
\mathcal{S}_{n}
and relations
[
5
]
. We develop an induction technique which allows to conclude the existence of positive
\lambda
for
G_{n}
once we know it for
G_{m}
whenever
m\leq n
. This technique involves decomposing
\Delta_{1}
into specific summands, with particular focus on the summand
\operatorname{Adj}
(
section

3
). The main theorem is the following.

Theorem 0.2

(
Theorem

4.3
)
.

Suppose there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\operatorname{Adj}-\lambda I_{|\mathcal{S}_{m}|}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\mathcal{S}_{m}|\times|\mathcal{S}_{m}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
. Then, for any
n\geq m
,

\Delta_{1}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\lambda I_{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}

is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
, that is there exist matrices
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
such that
\Delta_{1}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\lambda I_{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}=M_{1}^{*}M_{1}+\ldots M_{k}^{*}M_{k}
.

Our induction technique is inspired by the analogous technique established for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
, the group of
n\times n
integer matrices with determinant
1
and the group of
special atumorphisms
of the free group on
n
generators respectively, see
[
17
]
. Both induction approaches are, in turn, inspired by the induction technique for the Ozawa’s expression
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
, developed in
[
10
]
. Recently, M. Kaluba and D. Kielak
[
9
]
developed as well an induction techinque for
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
for Chevalley groups, including
G_{n}
, allowing one to obtain concrete lower-bound estimates for such
\lambda
.

This paper is organized as follows. In the first section we introduce

and define
\Delta_{1}
. Next, we present the Steinberg presentation of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})
. In

1
Fox derivatives and the first cohomological Laplacian

In this subsection, we define the first cohomological Laplacian
\Delta_{1}
mentioned in the introduction. This goes back to Fox and Lyndon
[
6
,
7
,
15
]
. For any group
G
and a ring
R
, there is the natural
*
-involution on
RG
, defined by flipping the coefficients of the group elements and their inverses. This
*
-involution induces the operation
*\colon\mathbb{M}_{k\times l}(RG)\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{l\times k}(RG)
by precomposing it with the matrix transposition.

Let
G=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}|\mathcal{R}\rangle
be a finitely presented group. Fix a subset
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
of its relations. We distungiuish the following matrices in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}G
:

\displaystyle d_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}1-s_{1}\\
\vdots\\
1-s_{n}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{Z}G)\quad\text{and}\quad d_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Z}G).

The symbols
\frac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}
denote the
Fox derivatives
, defined as follows. Denote by
F_{n}
the free group generated by
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
. For each generator
s_{j}
, we distinguish the homomorphism
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{j}}:\mathbb{Z}F_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}G
determined by the following conditions:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial 1}{\partial s_{j}}=0,\quad\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $i=j$,}\\
0&\text{if $i\neq j$}\end{cases},\quad\frac{\partial(uv)}{\partial s_{j}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial s_{j}}+u\frac{\partial v}{\partial s_{j}}.

Then, we define

\Delta_{1}^{-}=d_{0}d_{0}^{*},\quad\Delta_{1}^{+}=d_{1}^{*}d_{1}

and

\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{1}^{-}+\Delta_{1}^{+}.

In the case
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}=\mathcal{R}
, Lyndon
[
15
]
showed that the first cohomology of
G
with coefficients in a
G
-module
M
is the kernel of
d_{1}
divided by the image of
d_{0}
from the sequence below.

M\xrightarrow{d_{0}}M^{n}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}M^{m}.

Though, for our applications we will have a strict inclusion
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}\subsetneq\mathcal{R}
, this still allows us to apply the result of Bader-Nowak
[
3
, The Main Theorem]
due to
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Remark 1.1
.

There exists a direct relationship between the spectral gap
\lambda
from the Ozawa’s expression
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
and the one from the expression
\Delta_{1}-\lambda I
. More precisely, if
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is a finite generating set of a finitely presented group
G
such that
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is disjoint from the set of its inverses,
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{-1}
, then, for any
k>0
, once
\Delta_{1}^{-}+k\Delta_{1}^{+}-\lambda I\geq 0
, then
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta\geq 0
as well. More details were discussed in
[
17
, Remark 2.2]
.

2
Presentation of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})

In this section, we introduce the presentation of
G_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Composing this presentation with the projection onto the quotient
H_{n}
, one obtains a presentation of the latter. The presentation of
G_{n}
described below follows from
[
5
, section 2]
and the works of Matsumoto and Behr
[
16
,
1
]
.

The generator set
\mathcal{S}_{n}
of
G_{n}
(and also of
H_{n}
– it shall be clear from the context whether we consider the generators as elements of
G_{n}
or its quotient
H_{n}
) consists of the following
2n^{2}
elements:

•

X_{i,j}\quad\textrm{for }1\leq i\neq j\leq n,

•

Y_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{i,j}\quad\textrm{for }1\leq i<j\leq n,

•

Z_{i},Z_{i}^{\prime}\quad\textrm{for }1\leq i\leq n.

Abusing the notation for
i>j
by letting
Y_{i,j}
and
Y_{i,j}^{\prime}
be
Y_{j,i}
and
Y_{j,i}^{\prime}
respectively, the set of relators
\mathcal{R}_{n}
is the following.

•

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

(Z_{i}Z_{i}^{\prime-1}Z_{i})^{4}.

•

For pairwise distinct
i,j\in\{1,…,n\}
:

[X_{i,j},Y_{i,j}]Z_{i}^{-2},

[X_{i,j},Y_{i,j}^{\prime}]Z_{j}^{\prime 2},

[X_{i,j},Z_{j}]Y_{i,j}^{-1}Z_{i}^{-1}

[X_{i,j},Z_{j}]Z_{i}^{-1}Y_{i,j}^{-1},

[X_{i,j},Z_{i}^{\prime}]Y_{i,j}^{\prime}Z_{j}^{\prime-1}

[X_{i,j},Z_{i}^{\prime}]Z_{j}^{\prime-1}Y_{i,j}^{\prime},

[Y_{i,j},Z_{i}^{\prime}]Z_{j}X_{j,i}^{-1}

[Y_{i,j},Z_{i}^{\prime}]X_{j,i}^{-1}Z_{j},

[Y_{i,j}^{\prime},Z_{i}]Z_{j}^{\prime}X_{i,j}

[Y_{i,j}^{\prime},Z_{i}]X_{i,j}Z_{j}^{\prime}.

•

For pairwise distinct subscripts
i,j,k\in\{1,…,n\}
:

[X_{i,j},X_{j,k}]X_{i,k}^{-1},

[X_{i,j},Y_{j,k}]Y_{i,k}^{-1},

[X_{i,j},Y_{i,k}^{\prime}]Y_{j,k}^{\prime},

[Y_{i,j},Y_{j,k}^{\prime}]X_{i,k}^{-1}.

•

For all pairs of generators
(A,B)
with not described commutator, except for
(X_{i,j},X_{j,i})
,
(Y_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{i,j})
,
(Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i})
:

[A,B].

The correspondence between the above generators and concrete matrix elements from
G_{n}
is as follows:

•

X_{i,j}\leftrightarrow I_{2n}+E_{i,j}-E_{j+n,i+n},

•

Y_{i,j}\leftrightarrow I_{2n}+E_{i,j+n}+E_{j,i+n},\quad Y^{\prime}_{i,j}\leftrightarrow I_{2n}+E_{i+n,j}+E_{j+n,i},

•

Z_{i}\leftrightarrow I_{2n}+E_{i,i+n},\quad Z_{i}^{\prime}\leftrightarrow I_{2n}+E_{i+n,i},

where
E_{i,j}
denotes the
2n\times 2n
matrix with the only non zero entry at the position
(i,j)
which equals
1
.

The group
H_{n}
is the quotient of
G_{n}
by the commutator relators:

[Z_{i},Z_{i}^{\prime}]

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

3
Decomposition of the Laplacians

Inspired by inductive proofs from
[
10
]
and
[
17
]
, we introduce the decomposition of the Laplacians of
G_{n}
and
H_{n}
into parts originating from indices occurring in the coordinates in the case of
\Delta_{1}^{-}
and in the relations defining Jacobian in the case of
\Delta_{1}^{+}
.

\Delta_{1}^{-}=\operatorname{Mono}_{n}^{-}+\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{-}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{-}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{-},

\Delta_{1}^{+}=\operatorname{Mono}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+}.

In order to carry out this procedure, let us define the map

\varphi:\operatorname{Free}(\mathcal{S}_{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,…,n\})

from the free group generated by
\mathcal{S}_{n}
to subsets of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
which assigns to a word the set of all indices of generators occurring in the reduced form of this word.

What is more, we wish to recognise the following subsets of
\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,…,n\})
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ver}_{n}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,…,n\})\mid\#A=1\right\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Edg}_{n}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,…,n\})\mid\#A=2\right\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tri}_{n}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,…,n\})\mid\#A=3\right\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tet}_{n}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,…,n\})\mid\#A=4\right\}.

Consequently, the decomposition of the negative part of the Laplacian is obtained as follows.

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Mono}_{n}^{-}\right)_{s,t}=\left(\Delta_{1}^{-}\right)_{s,t}\text{ if }\varphi(s)\cup\varphi(t)\in\operatorname{Ver}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise,}

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{-}\right)_{s,t}=\left(\Delta_{1}^{-}\right)_{s,t}\text{ if }\varphi(s)\cup\varphi(t)\in\operatorname{Edg}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise,}

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{-}\right)_{s,t}=\left(\Delta_{1}^{-}\right)_{s,t}\text{ if }\varphi(s)\cup\varphi(t)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise,}

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{-}\right)_{s,t}=\left(\Delta_{1}^{-}\right)_{s,t}\text{ if }\varphi(s)\cup\varphi(t)\in\operatorname{Tet}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise,}

and the decompoisition of the positive part is given by

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Mono}_{n}^{+}\right)_{s,t}=\sum_{\varphi(r)\in\operatorname{Ver}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{+}\right)_{s,t}=\sum_{\varphi(r)\in\operatorname{Edg}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{+}\right)_{s,t}=\sum_{\varphi(r)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+}\right)_{s,t}=\sum_{\varphi(r)\in\operatorname{Tet}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t},

where
r
is taken from the relators
\mathcal{R}_{n}
.

4
Main induction argument

In the following section we examine collectively the symmetrization properties of the matrices
\operatorname{Mono}_{n}^{\pm},\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{\pm},\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{\pm},\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{\pm}
which appear in the decompositions of
\Delta_{1}^{-}
and
\Delta_{1}^{+}
.

We introduce an action of the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
on the set of generators
\mathcal{S}_{n}
and the free group
\operatorname{Free}(\mathcal{S}_{n})
generated by them, by permuting the indices of the generators (e.g.
\sigma X_{i,j}=X_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}
,
\sigma Z_{i}=Z_{\sigma(i)}
). Furthermore, this action can be extended to the case of
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 2n^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
by the following formula

(\sigma A)_{s,t}=\sigma\left(A_{\sigma^{-1}s,\sigma^{-1}t}\right),

where the action on
\mathbb{R}G_{n}
is naturally given by the action on the generators.

4.1
Expressing the action in the simplex setting

Suppose
A^{m}
is a matrix from the set
\{\operatorname{Mono}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Sq}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Op}^{\pm}_{m}\}
and let
C_{A^{m}}
be the set of the simplex faces defining
A^{m}
(respectively, for the matrices inside this set it is:
\operatorname{Ver}_{m},\operatorname{Edg}_{m},\operatorname{Tri}_{m},\operatorname{Tet}_{m}
).

Let us first assume that
A^{m}
originates from
\Delta_{1}^{-}
. For
\theta\in C_{A^{m}}
we define

(A^{m}_{\theta})_{s,t}=\begin{cases}(1-s)(1-t)^{*}\quad\text{ if }\varphi(s)\cup\varphi(t)=\theta,\\
0\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

For
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
the image of
A^{m}_{\theta}
under the action of
\sigma
equals
A^{m}_{\sigma(\theta)}
which follows directly from the formula for symmetric group action.

Next, for
A^{m}
derived from
\Delta_{1}^{+}
we define

(A^{m}_{\theta})_{s,t}=\sum_{\varphi(r)=\theta}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}.

Then, due to
[
17
, Lemma 5.2]
and by the invariance of the relator set
\mathcal{R}_{m}
under the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sigma(A^{m}_{\theta})\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\sum_{\phi(r)=\theta}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial\sigma^{-1}s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\sigma^{-1}t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi(r)=\sigma(\theta)}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}=(A^{m}_{\sigma(\theta)})_{s,t}.

Hence, for any
A^{m}\in\{\operatorname{Mono}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Sq}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Op}^{\pm}_{m}\}
we obtain the following formula:

\sigma(A^{m}_{\theta})=A^{m}_{\sigma(\theta)}.

Therefore

\sigma(A^{m})=\sum_{\theta\in C_{A^{m}}}\sigma(A^{m}_{\theta})=\sum_{\theta\in C_{A^{m}}}A^{m}_{\sigma(\theta)}=A^{m},

where the last equality holds because
C_{A^{m}}
is invariant under permutations. Therefore, the matrices
\operatorname{Mono}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Sq}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Op}^{\pm}_{m}\in\mathbb{M}_{2m^{2}\times 2m^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
are invariant under the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
.

In what follows, we shall abuse the notations
\sigma(A^{m})
and
\sigma(A_{\theta}^{m})
in the case
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
for some
n\geq m
and
\theta\in C_{A^{m}}
to denote the result of the action of
\sigma
on the canonical embeddings of
A^{m},A_{\theta}^{m}\in\mathbb{M}_{2m^{2}\times 2m^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
into
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 2n^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
.

4.2
Symmetrization of the Laplacian

Let
A^{m}
be again a matrix from the set
\{\operatorname{Mono}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Sq}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m},\operatorname{Op}^{\pm}_{m}\}
and let
k_{A}
be the power of the sets defining the simplex faces from
C_{A^{m}}
(for the matrices from the mentioned set it is
1,2,3
, and
4
respectively).

Recall that we have the following decomposition

A^{m}=\sum_{\theta\in C_{A^{m}}}A^{m}_{\theta}.

By considering a transformation of the above formula we will be able to describe symmetrization properties of the appropriate parts of the Laplacian.

Due to the orbit-stabilizer theorem applied to the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
on
C_{A^{m}}
, we get

A^{m}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}}\frac{1}{(m-k_{A})!k_{A}!}A^{m}_{\sigma(\{1,…,k_{A}\})}=\frac{1}{(m-k_{A})!k_{A}!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}}\sigma(A^{k_{A}}).

Suppose that we have a natural number
n\geq m
and let
\left\{\tau_{i}\right\}_{i=1,…,\frac{n!}{m!}}
be the set of representatives of the cosets
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}/\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
. Then we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

One has

A^{n}=\frac{(m-k_{A})!}{(n-k_{A})!\cdot m!}\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\tau\left(A^{m}\right).

Proof.

A direct computation, based on the previous observations:

\displaystyle A^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-k_{A})!k_{A}!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma\left(A^{k_{A}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-k_{A})!k_{A}!}\sum_{i=1,…,\frac{n!}{m!}}\tau_{i}\left(\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}}\tau\left(A^{k_{A}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(m-k_{A})!}{(n-k_{A})!}\sum_{i=1,…,\frac{n!}{m!}}\tau_{i}\left(A^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(m-k_{A})!}{(n-k_{A})!}\sum_{i=1,…,\frac{n!}{m!}}\tau_{i}\left(\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}}\tau\left(A^{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(m-k_{A})!}{(n-k_{A})!\cdot m!}\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\tau\left(A^{m}\right).

∎

4.3
Symmetrization of identity matrix

Let
I_{n}
be the identity matrix inside
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 2n^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
, that is
I_{n}
is equal to
I_{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}
from the Introduction. Let us consider the following split of
I_{n}
into two parts:
I_{n}^{\operatorname{Mono}}
and
I_{n}^{\operatorname{Sq}}
in which diagonal values are non-zero if and only if they are indexed by a generator mapping via
\varphi
to
\operatorname{Ver}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Edg}_{n}
respectively. This gives the decomposition

I_{n}=I_{n}^{\operatorname{Mono}}+I_{n}^{\operatorname{Sq}},

with
2n
nontrivial elements inside
I_{n}^{\operatorname{Mono}}
and
2n(n-1)
inside
I_{n}^{\operatorname{Sq}}
.

Further in this notes
I_{m}
, will also denote the canonical embedding of the identity matrix from
\mathbb{M}_{2m^{2}\times 2m^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
into
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 2n^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
for an integer
n\geq m
.

We show the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma I_{m}=m(m-1)(n-2)!\cdot I_{n}^{\operatorname{Sq}}+m(n-1)!\cdot I_{n}^{\operatorname{Mono}}.

Proof.

Both
\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma I_{m}^{\operatorname{Mono}}
and
\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma I_{m}^{\operatorname{Sq}}
are multiplicities of
I_{n}^{\operatorname{Mono}}
and
I_{n}^{\operatorname{Sq}}
respectively. If we consider the power of
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
acting on these matrices and the numbers of non-zero entries in each of them we obtain the multiplication factors as in the above formula.
∎

4.4
The main theorem

Below we present the main theorem of this paper. This is the extended (and slightly stronger) version of the theorem from the introduction.

Theorem 4.3

(
Theorem

0.2
)
.

Suppose
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{m}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{2m^{2}\times 2m^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
for some
m\geq 2
. Then
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\lambda I_{n}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 2n^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
for any
n\geq m
. In the case only
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{-}+\Delta_{1}^{+}-\lambda I_{m}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{2m^{2}\times 2m^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
for some
m\geq 2
, a slightly weaker conclusion holds:
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{-}+k\Delta_{1}^{+}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\lambda I_{n}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 2n^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
for any
n\geq m
and
k
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Suppose
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{m}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{2m^{2}\times 2m^{2}}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
for some
m\geq 2
. By
Lemma

4.1
we have

\operatorname{Adj}_{n}=\frac{(m-3)!}{(n-2)!\cdot m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}).

Thus, by
Lemma

4.2
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{n}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\cdot\lambda I_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(m-3)!}{(n-3)!\cdot m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{m})+\frac{n-m}{m-1}\cdot I_{n}^{\operatorname{Mono}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(m-3)!}{(n-3)!\cdot m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{m})\geq 0.

The second part can be proved similarly with an additional asymptotic argument. Analogously as is in the first part:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{-}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\cdot\lambda I_{n}\geq\frac{(m-3)!}{(n-3)!\cdot m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{-}-\lambda I_{m}).

(1)

Now, by
Lemma

4.1
and
Lemma

4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{(m-2)(m-1)}\Delta_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{(m-2)(m-1)}\left(\operatorname{Mono}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(m-3)!}{(n-3)!\cdot m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{m}^{+}).

The last inequality in connection with inequality (
1
) yields the assertion for
k=(n-1)(n-2)/(m-2)(m-1)
.
∎

Application to spectral gaps related to

In this section we show, using computer calulations, lower bounds for positive

is a sum of squares for

for

Lemma 5.1
.

The matrices
\operatorname{Mono}^{-}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}+\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}
are sums of squares.

Proof.

For each index
i\in\{1,2,…n\}
let us define
d^{i}
as the following column vector inside
\mathbb{M}_{2n^{2}\times 1}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})

(d^{i})_{s}=\begin{cases}1-s\quad\text{if }\varphi(s)=\{i\},\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, we can present
\operatorname{Mono}^{-}_{n}
as the following sum of squares

\operatorname{Mono}^{-}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d^{i}(d^{i})^{*}.

Next, for any pair of generators
s,t
such that
\varphi(s)\cup\varphi(t)\in\operatorname{Tet}_{n}
, we have:

\displaystyle(\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n})_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial[s,t]}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial[s,t]}{\partial t}+\left(\frac{\partial[t,s]}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial[t,s]}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-2(1-s)(1-t)^{*}=-2(\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{-})_{s,t}

and for other generators
s\neq t
, we get
(\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n})_{s,t}=0
. Moreover, since the elements on the diagonal of
\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{-}
equal zero and the diagonal part of
\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+}
, denoted by
\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+})
is a sum of squares, we obtain

\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+})+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{+}\geq 0.

∎

Appendix: Methods for faster calculation of spectral gaps and certification of the solution

In this appendix, we present two methods for faster computation related to finding lower bounds for spectral gaps of cohomological Laplacians. The contents of the first part of the appendix was inspired by the unpublished notes of P.W. Nowak and the first author and the contents of the second part partially overlaps with the master thesis of the second author entitled "Spectral gaps for the Property (T) groups".

Appendix A
Wedderburn decomposition for the matrix case

Let
G=\langle s_{1},...,s_{n}|r_{1},...,r_{k}\rangle
. As shown in
[
11
]
, given a matrix
M=M^{*}=[m_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R}G)
, the expression
M-\lambda I_{n}
is a sum of squares for a constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if there exists a finite subset
E=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\}\subseteq G
for which the following set is non-empty:

\displaystyle F(M,\lambda)=\{P\in\mathbb{M}_{nm}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{M}_{E}(\mathbb{R})\colon\quad

\displaystyle P\text{ is positive semidefinite and }

\displaystyle\forall_{i,j\in[n],g\in G}\hskip 14.22636ptm_{i,j}(g)-\delta_{i,j}\lambda=\langle E_{i,j}\otimes A_{g},P\rangle\}\neq\emptyset,

where
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(A^{*}B)
, and
A_{g}\in\mathbb{M}_{E}(\mathbb{R})
is given for any
g\in G
as follows

(A_{g})_{g_{i},g_{j}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{for }g_{i}^{*}g_{j}=g,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The aim of this section is to generalize section 2 of
[
12
]
and use similar block-diagonalization in the case of our optimization problem for the first cohomological laplacian.

We assume that a symmetric group
\Sigma
acts on
G
and that each element
\sigma\in\Sigma
acts as a permutation on the set of generators, the set of relations, and the subset
E
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
X
be either
\mathcal{S}=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
or
E
. One can define the following induced linear actions:

\displaystyle\Sigma\curvearrowright\mathbb{R}G

\displaystyle\colon\quad\sigma\left(\sum_{g\in G}\lambda_{g}g\right)=\sum_{g\in G}\lambda_{\sigma^{-1}(g)}g,

\displaystyle\Sigma\curvearrowright\mathbb{M}_{X}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\colon\quad(\sigma T)_{g_{i},g_{j}}=T_{\sigma^{-1}g_{i},\sigma^{-1}g_{j}}.

Based on the aforementioned actions we introduce also linear actions of
\Sigma
on
\mathbb{M}_{S}(\mathbb{R}G)\cong\mathbb{M}_{S}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{R}G
and
\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{M}_{S}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{M}_{E}(\mathbb{R})
by acting component-wise on each factor of the tensor products.

The following result is a slight improvement of Corollary 3.5 from the work of
[
11
]
if we take into account and use the
\Sigma
-action.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{M}_{S}{(\mathbb{R}G)}
be a
\Sigma
-invariant matrix which can be decomposed into a sum of squares. Then there exists a
\Sigma
-invariant positive semidefinite matrix
P\in\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}(\mathbb{R})
such that the following equality holds

M=x_{E}^{*}Px_{E},

where
x_{E}
is defined by the following tensor product:

x_{E}=I_{n}\otimes(g_{1},…,g_{m})^{T}\in\mathbb{M}_{mn\times n}(\mathbb{R}G).

Definition A.3
.

One can define the real representation
\varrho
of
\Sigma
on
\mathbb{R}(S\times E)
as follows:

\varrho(\sigma)\in\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\varrho(\sigma)_{(i,g),(j,h)}=\begin{cases}1&\text{for }(i,g)=(\sigma(j),\sigma h),\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, let
A(\varrho)
denote the real algebra generated by the elements
\{\varrho(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
.

According to Maschke’s theorem, the representation
\varrho
is semisimple. By the definition of the associated algebra
A(\varrho)
, it is isomorphic to the direct sum of real algebras corresponding to real irreducible representations of
\Sigma
:

A(\varrho)\cong\bigoplus_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}I_{m_{\pi}}\otimes A(\pi)\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}(\mathbb{R}).

Since the only endomorphisms of such representations are scalar multiplications (
[
8
, Chapter 4]
), also the following isomorphism of the commutant of
A(\varrho)
holds:

A(\varrho)^{\prime}\cong\bigoplus_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}\mathbb{M}_{m_{\pi}}(\mathbb{R})\otimes I_{\operatorname{dim}(\pi)}.

Proposition A.4
.

The set of
\Sigma
-invariant matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\Sigma}_{S\times E}(\mathbb{R})
coincides with
A(\varrho)^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First, let us consider the matrix
M=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{i,j}\otimes M^{i,j}\in\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}^{\Sigma}(\mathbb{R})
. The invariance of M means that the following equalities hold for each
\sigma\in\Sigma

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{i,j}\otimes M^{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{i,j}\otimes M^{i,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}\otimes\sigma M^{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{i,j}\otimes\sigma M^{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(j)}.

Therefore, we have the equality of the matrices
\sigma M^{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(j)}
and
M^{i,j}
for all
i,j\in[n]
, which implies that on each coordinate
(g,h)\in E^{2}
we obtain

M^{i,j}_{g,h}=\left(\sigma M^{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(j)}\right)_{g,h}=M^{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(j)}_{\sigma^{-1}(g),\sigma^{-1}(h)}.

On the other side, we may apply the same convention for block description in the case of matrices given by
\varrho
and obtain the equality

\varrho(\sigma)=\sum_{i\in[n],g\in G}E_{i,\sigma^{-1}(i)}\otimes E_{g,\sigma^{-1}(g)}.

Thus, for a matrix
M=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{i,j}\otimes M^{i,j}\in\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}(\mathbb{R})
we can investigate the action of
\varrho
from the both sides:

\displaystyle\varrho(\sigma)M

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\in[n],g\in E}E_{i,\sigma^{-1}(i)}\otimes E_{g,\sigma^{-1}(g)}\right)\left(\sum_{i,j\in[n]}E_{i,j}\otimes M^{i,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in[n],g\in E}E_{i,j}\otimes\left(E_{g,\sigma^{-1}(g)}\cdot M^{\sigma^{-1}(i),j}\right)

\displaystyle M\varrho(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in[n],h\in E}E_{i,j}\otimes\left(M^{i,\sigma(j)}\cdot E_{\sigma(h),h}\right).

The matrix
M
is contained in
A(\varrho)^{\prime}
if and only if the above matrices are equal for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Based on the above calculations, it follows that the entry of
\varrho(\sigma)M=M\varrho(\sigma)
with coordinates
\left((i,g),(j,h)\right)
is equal to

M^{\sigma^{-1}(i),j}_{\sigma^{-1}(g),h}=M^{i,\sigma(j)}_{g,\sigma(h)}

which is the same equation as (
\star
) up to the renaming of the variables, and so the stated equality holds.
∎

By the previous proposition, we have the isomorphism of
\Sigma
-invariant matrices and the algebra
\bigoplus_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}\mathbb{M}_{m_{\pi}}(\mathbb{R})\otimes I_{\operatorname{dim}(\pi)}
.
Suppose that it is given by the following map:

\Theta\colon\mathbb{M}_{S\times E}^{\Sigma}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}\mathbb{M}_{m_{\pi}}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\Theta(M)=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}U_{\pi}MU_{\pi}^{T}.

Then, basing on the matrices
U_{\pi}
, we can define the block semidefinite problem
\widetilde{F}(M,\lambda)
and by the analogue of
[
12
, Proposition 9]
its solution will always imply the solution of the problem
F(M,\lambda)
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}(M,\lambda)=\{

\displaystyle\{P_{\pi}\in\mathbb{M}_{m_{\pi}}(\mathbb{R})\}_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}:\quad

\displaystyle\forall_{\pi}P_{\pi}\text{ are positive semidefinite and }

\displaystyle m_{i,j}(g)-\delta_{i,j}\lambda=\sum_{\pi}\langle U_{\pi}A_{B}U_{\pi}^{T},P_{\pi}\rangle\text{ for any orbit }B=[E_{i,j}\otimes A_{g}]\in\mathcal{B}/\Sigma\},

where
\mathcal{B}=\left\{E_{i,j}\otimes A_{g}:i,j\in[n]\text{ and }g\in E^{*}E\right\}
and matrices
A_{B}
are given as follows

A_{B}=\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\sum_{X\in B}X.

Appendix B
Improved certification of the solution

Let
G
be a finitely presented group and
\Delta_{1}
its first cohomological Laplacian. Recall (e.g. from section 3.2 of
[
11
]
) that in order to turn a numerical approximation
(X_{\operatorname{ap}},\lambda_{\operatorname{ap}})
of the Laplacian,
\Delta_{1}-\lambda_{\operatorname{ap}}I\approx X_{\operatorname{ap}}
, into a rigorous proof of the existence of a positive
\lambda
such that
\Delta_{1}-\lambda I\geq 0
, we have to play with the residual error
r=\Delta_{1}-\lambda_{\operatorname{ap}}I-X_{\operatorname{ap}}
(the operations are performed in
interval arithmetic
). We can ensure that
X_{\operatorname{ap}}
is a sum of squares which allows us to add a specific expression
r+\alpha_{r}I\geq 0
to it. The crucial part is to have a good control over
\alpha_{r}
. This control estimate was introduced in
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
and we improve it here.

Definition B.1
.

Let
U=(u_{1},…,u_{n})
be a vector with coefficients in a group ring
\mathbb{R}G
. Then the first norm of
U
is given by

||U||_{1}\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{n}||u_{i}||_{1},

where
||\sum_{g}\lambda_{g}g||_{1}=\sum_{g}|\lambda_{g}|
.

Theorem B.2
.

For any matrix
M=M^{*}\in\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R}G)
with rows
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
, the the matrix
M+\alpha_{M}I_{n}
is a sum of squares, where

\alpha_{M}=\max_{i=1,…,n}||M_{i}||_{1}.

Proof.

First, let us consider a
*
-invariant
x\in\mathbb{R}G
. In this case
x=\sum_{g\in G}c_{g}g
and, by our assumption,
c_{g}=c_{g^{-1}}
. This allows us to write
x
in the following form

x=\sum_{g\in G}\frac{c_{g}}{2}(g+g^{-1}),

and by adding its first norm we obtain

x+||x||_{1}=x+\sum_{g\in G}|c_{g}|=\sum_{g\in G}\frac{|c_{g}|}{2}(\pm g\pm g^{-1}+2)=\sum_{g\in G}\frac{|c_{g}|}{2}(1\pm g)^{*}(1\pm g)\geq 0.

Let
E_{i,j}
be the matrix from
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R}G)
with the identity at entry
(i,j)
and
0
elsewhere. Then any
\ast
-invariant matrix
M=[m_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R}G)
can be decomposed into a sum of matrices of the two forms.

•

For
i<j
:

M_{i,j}=m_{i,j}\cdot E_{i,j}+m_{i,j}^{*}\cdot E_{j,i},\textrm{ for $m_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}G$.}

•

On the diagonal:

M_{i,i}=m_{i,i}\cdot E_{i,i},\textrm{ for $*$-invariant $m_{i,i}\in\mathbb{R}G$.}

By the previous argumentation
M_{i,i}+||m_{i,i}||_{1}\cdot E_{i,i}
admits a decomposition as a sum of squares. Moreover, by introducing

N_{i,j}=M_{i,j}+||m_{i,j}||_{1}\cdot E_{i,i}+||m_{i,j}||_{1}\cdot E_{j,j},

and by the formula
E_{i,j}\cdot E_{k,l}=\delta_{j,k}\cdot E_{i,l}
, we obtain

M_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2||m_{i,j}||_{1}}N_{i,j}^{*}N_{i,j}-\frac{||m_{i,j}||_{1}^{2}+m_{i,j}m_{i,j}^{*}}{2||m_{i,j}||_{1}}\cdot E_{i,i}-\frac{||m_{i,j}||_{1}^{2}+m_{i,j}^{*}m_{i,j}}{2||m_{i,j}||_{1}}\cdot E_{j,j}.

Now, it is easy to observe that the last two summands are
*
-invariant, and since

||m_{i,j}^{*}m_{i,j}||_{1},||m_{i,j}m_{i,j}^{*}||_{1}\leq||m_{i,j}||_{1}^{2},

the first norm of their non-zero entries is not greater than
||m_{i,j}||_{1}
. Therefore, proceeding as earlier, we add to them the same single-entry matrix multiplied by the norm of the entry, obtaining

M_{i,j}+||m_{i,j}||_{1}\cdot E_{i,i}+||m_{i,j}||_{1}\cdot E_{j,j}\geq 0.

Hence, in order to obtain a non-negative element from
M
, we need to add
E_{i,i}
for all possible summands
M_{i,j}
and
M_{j,i}
i.e.
E_{i,i}
scaled by the sum of the first norms
||m_{i,j}||_{1}
for
i\leq j
and
||m_{j,i}||_{1}
for
j<i
, which in the case of
*
-invariant matrix is equal to the first norm of the
i
-th row:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(M_{i,i}+||m_{i,i}||_{1}\cdot E_{i,i}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}^{n}\left(M_{i,j}+||m_{i,j}||_{1}\cdot E_{i,i}+||m_{i,j}||_{1}\cdot E_{j,j}\right)=

\displaystyle=M+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left(\sum_{i\leq j}||m_{i,j}||_{1}+\sum_{j<i}||m_{j,i}||_{1}\right)\cdot E_{i,i}\right)=M+\sum_{i=1}^{n}||M_{i}||_{1}\cdot E_{i,i}.

This implies that by adding the identity matrix multiplied by the maximum of the norms between the rows, we obtain a matrix that admits a sum of squares decomposition.

Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Mark Egsmose, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Mariano Lopez Romano, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Oriol Magrans, Iris Mangas, Silvia Mazzega, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea, Juan Manuel Parra Morte, Simone Rizzuto

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authority of the rapporteur Member State Austria for the pesticide active substance 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate and the considerations as regards the inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative uses of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate as an insecticide (pheromone) on maize (grain and silage/forage) and sweet corn. The reliable endpoints, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment, are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed.Keywords: 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate, insecticide, peer review, pesticide, pheromone, risk assessment

SUMMARY

8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council, the rapporteur Member State (RMS), Austria, received an application from Lithos Crop Protect GmbHon 25 March 2021 for approval. In addition, the applicant submitted an application for inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 17 December 2021.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate was provided by the RMS in the draft assessment report (DAR) and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The following conclusions are derived.

The representative uses of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate as an indirect insecticide applied by field spray on maize (grain and silage/forage) and sweetcorn, as proposed at EU level, result in sufficient insecticidal efficacy against the target pests.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that needed to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to the identity, physical, chemical and technical properties of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate or the formulation for representative uses and analytical methods.

In the area of mammalian toxicology and non‐dietary exposure, the assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern.

In the area of residues, no quantifiable residues in food and feed items are expected following the representative use. No issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern were identified.

An MRL application for inclusion of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate into Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 has also been submitted. None of the criteria for inclusion are met. As a result an MRL at the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg is proposed for maize grain and sweet corn.

In the area of environmental fate and behaviour, issues that could not be finalised or that needed to be included as critical areas of concern were not identified.

In the area of ecotoxicology, the assessment of the data package did not identify any issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern.

8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is unlikely to meet the criteria for endocrine disruption for humans and non‐target organisms according to points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605.

BACKGROUND

Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council 1 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) lays down, inter alia, the detailed rules as regards the procedure and conditions for approval of active substances. This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States and the applicant for comments on the initial evaluation in the draft assessment report (DAR), provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS), and the organisation of an expert consultation, where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether an active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation (also taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation) within 120 days from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of 30 days where an expert consultation is necessary, and a further extension of up to 150 days where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 12(3).

8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of the Regulation, the RMS, Austria (hereinafter referred to as the ‘RMS’), received an application from Lithos Crop Protect GmbH on 25 March 2021 for approval of the active substance 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate. In addition, the applicant submitted an application for inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 17 December 2021.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate in the DAR, which was received by EFSA on 3 August 2023 (Austria, 2024). The peer review was initiated on 19 August 2024 by dispatching the DAR to the Member States and the applicant, Lithos Crop Protect GmbH, for consultation and comments. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the DAR. The comments received were collated by EFSA and forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of a reporting table. The applicant was invited to respond to the comments in column 3 of the reporting table. The comments and the applicant response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicant in accordance with Article 12(3) of the Regulation were considered in a telephone conference between EFSA and the RMS on 17 February 2025. On the basis of the comments received, the applicant's response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicant, and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of mammalian toxicology and ecotoxicology.

The outcome of the telephone conference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA should adopt a conclusion on whether 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation, taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment took place with Member States via a written procedure in May–June 2026.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate as an insecticide on maize (ZEAMX; grain and silage/forage) and sweet corn (ZEAMS) as proposed by the applicant. In accordance with Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, risk mitigation options identified in the DAR and considered during the peer review, if any, are presented in the conclusion.

THE ACTIVE SUBSTANCE AND THE FORMULATION FOR REPRESENTATIVE USES

The active substance is 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate (IUPAC). An ISO common name is not available for this substance. The active substance is an equimolar mixture of 4 isomers. The targeted pest has the strongest response to the (2R, 8R)‐isomer, followed by the (2S, 8R)‐isomer with it having no response to the other two isomers ((2S, 8S)‐ and (2R, 8S)‐).

The formulation for representative uses for the evaluation is ‘Lithos CornProtect’, a wettable powder containing 0.0375 g/kg pure 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate.

The information on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses, including the co‐formulants in these formulations, was considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. None of the co‐formulants is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 3 however 3 components of a co‐formulant are non‐approved active substances under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009 4 , 5 (the reason of the non‐approval is not related to safety concerns).

Details on the composition of the formulations cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, however this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review. A proposal for classification of the formulations according to Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 6 was provided by the applicant and assessed by the RMS (please see Volumes 3 CP of the DAR).

8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is used as pheromone (indirect insecticide) for mating disruption of Diabrotica virgifera virgifera. The representative use evaluated was field spray application on maize (grain and silage/forage production) and sweetcorn for control of Diabrotica virgifera virgifera. Full details of the GAP can be found in the list of end points in Appendix B.

Information was submitted to conclude that the use of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate in according to the representative use proposed at EU level results in a sufficient indirect insecticidal efficacy against the target organisms, following the European Commission (2013).

CONCLUSIONS OF THE EVALUATION

General aspects

With regard to the mammalian toxicity information available for the formulation for representative uses ‘Lithos CornProtect’, studies on acute toxicity endpoints were not performed. Instead, according to Commission Regulation (EU) No 284/2013, 7 classification for acute toxicity is based on the calculation of the individual constituents, following the additivity approach outlined in CLP Regulation (EC) No. 1272/2008. 8 With regard to the co‐formulants contained in ‘Lithos CornProtect’, sufficient toxicological data were available for all components except one, which is present in significant amount (> 10%) in the final formulation. For this co‐formulant, the experts considered that the available toxicological information did not sufficiently address the repeated dose toxicity over the short‐ and long‐term exposure of ‘Lithos CornProtect’, and that they might be considered for further assessment (data gap, see Section 10). Furthermore, given that from a particle size distribution study it was concluded that > 1% by weight of the preparation has a particle size < 50 μm, the potential acute toxicity by inhalation of the product might be considered for further assessment (data gap, see Section 10). It is noted that this co‐formulant is a feed additive under Regulation (EC) 1831/2003. 9 The collected information, including information from the existing uses other than plant protection products under regulated EU frameworks, did not identify any alerts.

The availability of ecotoxicity data with the formulation for representative uses was discussed at the Peer Review Experts' TC 193 10 (see Section 5). Based on the available acute data, it was noted that the formulation for representative uses is not more acutely toxic than would be predicted from the active substance. The experts also discussed the data retrieval search and the available data for the individual components. Based on the reasoning agreed by the experts, no concerns were identified.

MAMMALIAN TOXICITY

The toxicological profile of the active substance 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference (TC) 189 and 190 (24–25 November 2025). The assessment is based on the following guidance documents: European Commission (2012, 2024), EFSA (2014), (2017); EFSA PPR Panel (2012) and ECHA (2017).

Regarding the proposed reference specification, no toxicologically relevant impurities are identified.

The test material used in toxicity studies is sufficiently representative of the proposed reference specification for the active substance and associated impurities.

Reduced data requirements for 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate have been proposed as the equimolar isomer mixture includes isomers that are semiochemicals. 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate listed as an ‘Other Chained Arthropod Pheromone (OCAP)’ in Appendix II of European Commission (2024), sharing structural similarities with the straight chain lepidopteran pheromones (SCLPs) definition. This proposal was agreed by EFSA and the member state experts considering its non‐toxic and targeted mode of action, and its low rate of application expected to result in a low human exposure.

For the absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion, experimental data were not available for 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate. It is expected that the compounds will hydrolyse to propanoic acid, a common metabolite of the human gut microbiota and 8‐methyl‐2‐decanol. Data on comparative in vitro metabolism were not available. Waiving of these studies was accepted.

8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate has low acute toxicity by oral, dermal and inhalation exposure, and has no irritating properties. Based on the available in vitro and in silico information it was concluded that, despite some uncertainty, 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is unlikely to be a skin sensitiser; therefore, no further in vivo data are required. 11 Testing for phototoxicity is not required for 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate.

Short‐term oral toxicity studies were provided for rats. The adverse effects included increased absolute and relative liver weight and hepatocellular hypertrophy at 800 mg/kg bw per day in males, with a no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of 200 mg/kg bw per day (90‐day rat study). 12

Based on available genotoxicity studies in vitro, 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is concluded as unlikely to be genotoxic. This conclusion is supported by in silico quantitative structure–activity relationship (QSAR) analyses. 13

Regarding the long‐term/carcinogenicity studies, the experts agreed they could be waived since 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate acts as a semiochemical, sharing structural similarities with SCLPs (for which these studies were also waived). Furthermore, the mixture is unlikely to be genotoxic, has a specific mode of action as mating disruptor in the pest and the human exposure is expected to be low. 14 Likewise, waiving reproductive and developmental studies is acceptable, also considering that the findings in the available repeated dose toxicity studies do not raise a concern for reproductive toxicity. 15

The potential for neurotoxic or immunotoxic effects was not identified in the available studies.

Health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) have been derived for 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate (equimolar mixture of 4 isomers). 16

The acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is 1 mg/kg bw per day based on the NOAEL of the 90‐day rat study, applying a standard uncertainty factor (UF) of 100 and an additional UF of 2 for the extrapolation from subchronic to chronic exposure.

The acceptable operator exposure level (AOEL) of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is 1 mg/kg bw per day, based on the NOAEL of the 90‐day rat study, applying a standard UF of 100 and an additional UF of 2 to take into account the lack of oral absorption data.

Acute health‐based guidance values are not necessary for 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate.

Dermal absorption of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate in the representative product Lithos CornProtect (WP) has not been assessed experimentally, therefore the default value of 50% is applicable to both the concentrate (as the content of active substance is below 50 g/kg) and the dilutions based on the EFSA guidance (EFSA, 2017).

The non‐dietary exposure estimates for the operators, workers, residents and bystanders were calculated with the EFSA model (EFSA, 2022) and are below 10% of the AOEL for the representative use on sweet corn, without specific risk mitigation measures. As 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is a mixture of four isomers, of unknown relative toxicity, the exposure being below 25% of the AOEL allows to cover for related uncertainty in isomer‐specific toxicity.

Toxicological information was not available on 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate metabolites.

RESIDUES

The assessment in the residue section is based on the following guidance documents: European Commission (2015, 2024).

According to European Commission (2024) guidance on semiochemicals, residue data may not be required where it has been determined that either (i) quantifiable residues on the consumable commodity are unlikely to occur or (ii) residue levels are unlikely to exceed natural exposure levels.

In the present case, representative uses involve foliar spraying, which implies direct contact between the plant and the plant protection product. Furthermore, while the naturally occurring substance consists of one or at most two isomers, the active substance comprises four isomers. Taking into account both the mode of application (foliar instead of vapour phase only via dispensers) and the presence of additional isomers, a comparison with natural exposure levels of pheromones is not appropriate.

Therefore, the potential applicability of waiving residue data is assessed only with respect to the first option outlined in European Commission (2024), namely whether quantifiable residues on the consumable commodity are unlikely to occur.

In this context, experimental data on the residue behaviour of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate or on residue levels following application as a plant protection product were not submitted. Instead, theoretical calculations of expected residues under the representative uses were provided and indicated that no quantifiable residues (> 0.01 mg/kg) should be expected in food and feed following the representative use. These calculations can be considered as sufficiently conservative since it is assumed that the total amount intercepted by crops remains on the edible crop part, whereas release and volatilization are expected to occur before harvest based on the mode of action and the physico‐chemical properties of the active substance. Additionally, grain is expected to have reduced exposure to the treatment because it is covered by the surrounding leaves present at application.

As regards potential residues in rotational crops, it is noted that the active substance and its soil metabolites are not persistent (see Section 4) and therefore residues above 0.01 mg/kg are not expected in rotational crops.

Given the above, residue definition for enforcement is by default proposed as 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate (sum of isomers) for plants. Residue definition for enforcement is not needed for animal commodities since livestock studies are not triggered by the representative uses.

An indicative dietary exposure assessment using PRIMo 3.1, assuming residues of 0.01 mg/kg in maize grain and sweet corn, resulted in an exposure of 0.03% of the ADI (NL toddler). A factor of four was applied to the risk assessment value used to calculate the chronic dietary exposure. This is to account for potential changes in the isomer ratio among the four isomers of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate, which could result in increased toxicity of the mixture.

An acute dietary exposure assessment was not conducted, as an acute reference dose (ARfD) was not derived (see Section 2). Considering residues > 0.01 mg/kg are not estimated in food/feed, further data is not required. However, for potential future uses involving higher application rates and/or different crops, experimental residue data might be needed, if residues below 0.01 mg/kg cannot be anticipated.

The five assessment criteria according to European Commission (2015) for inclusion in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, i.e.: approval as a basic substance (criterion I), listed in Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (criterion II), having no identified hazardous properties (criterion III), exposure linked to the use as a plant protection product is negligible compared to natural exposure (criterion IV) and consumer exposure is not expected considering the mode of application for the intended uses (criterion V) was assessed. None of these criteria were met (see above for explanations regarding criteria IV and V). As a result, an MRL at the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg is proposed for maize grain and sweet corn.

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is an equimolar mixture of 4 isomers, all environmental fate and behaviour studies were conducted with the racemate. Therefore, degradation and dissipation rates were derived from the sum of isomers of unknown isomer composition.

The rates of dissipation and degradation in the environmental matrices investigated were estimated using FOCUS (2006) kinetics guidance. In soil laboratory incubations under aerobic conditions in the dark, 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate exhibited very low persistence, forming the major (> 10% applied radioactivity (AR)) metabolites 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one (max. 16% AR) and Unknown (RRT 0.79–0.80) (max. 19% AR), which exhibited very low persistence. Mineralisation of the carbon 14C radiolabel to carbon dioxide accounted for 31% AR after 3 days. The formation of unextractable residues (not extracted by acetonitrile/water/acetic acid) for this radiolabel accounted for 65.5% AR after 3 days. 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate exhibited immobility in soil estimated via QSAR estimation using EPISuite (v 4.10).

In laboratory incubations in dark aerobic natural sediment water systems, 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate exhibited very low persistence forming the major metabolites 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one (max. 16% AR in water) and Unknown (RRT 0.77–0.78) (max. 15% AR in water). The unextractable sediment fraction (not extracted by acetonitrile/water/acetic acid) was the major sink for the carbon 14C radiolabel, accounting for 3%–4% AR after 1 day. Mineralisation of this radiolabel accounted for < 5% AR after 1 day. The necessary surface water and sediment exposure assessments (Predicted environmental concentrations (PEC) calculations) were carried out for 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate and for the metabolites 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one and Unknown (RRT 0.77–0.80) using the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2001) step 1 and step 2 approach (version 3.2 of the Steps 1–2 in FOCUS calculator).

The necessary groundwater exposure assessments were appropriately carried out using FOCUS (European Commission, 2014) scenarios and the models PEARL 4.4.4 and PELMO 5.5.3. The potential for groundwater exposure from the representative uses by 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate and its soil metabolites 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one and Unknown (RRT 0.77–0.80) above the parametric drinking water limit of 0.1 μg/L was concluded to be low in geoclimatic situations that are represented by all 9 of the FOCUS groundwater scenarios relevant for maize.

The applicant provided appropriate information to address the effect of water treatments processes on the nature of the residues that might be present in surface water and groundwater, when surface water or groundwater are abstracted for drinking water. The conclusion was that neither 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate nor any of its degradation products that trigger assessment (8‐methyldecan‐2‐one and Unknown (RRT 0.77–0.80)) would be expected to undergo any substantial transformation due to oxidation at the disinfection stage of usual water treatment processes.

The PEC in soil, surface water, sediment and groundwater covering the representative uses assessed can be found in Appendix B of this conclusion. A key to the wording used to describe the persistence and mobility of the compounds assessed can be found in Appendix C of this conclusion.

ECOTOXICOLOGY

The risk assessment was based on the following documents: European Commission (2002, 2024); SETAC, 2001; EFSA (2009, 2013) and EFSA PPR Panel (2013). Some aspects related to the risk assessment of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate were discussed in the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 193 (1–5 December 2025).

The test material used in ecotoxicity studies is sufficiently representative of the proposed reference specification for the active substance and associated impurities.

The representative use of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate is a spray application followed by slow release of the pheromone. Consequently, the exposure to non‐target organisms could not be excluded and quantitative risk assessments were performed.

Acute toxicity studies with the active substance 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate were submitted for both birds and wild mammals. Studies were not available with the representative formulation. The acute risk to birds and mammals was assessed as low based on a screening level assessment. Long‐term toxicity studies were not available for either birds or wild mammals and the waiving of such data was discussed in TC 193. A weight‐of‐evidence risk assessment was discussed at the experts meeting. Considering (i) the specific mode of action (ii) the physico‐chemical properties 17 of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate (iii) the expected low exposure and (iv) the high margin of safety (MoS) when performing an illustrative risk assessment, 18 all experts agreed that a low long‐term risk could be concluded without the need of further data. 19

The secondary poisoning potential for the active substance and the two relevant surface water and soil metabolites (i.e. 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one and the metabolite Unknown (RRT 0.77–0.80)) was discussed in the peer review meeting TC 193. A low risk was concluded for birds and mammals from secondary poisoning for both the active substance and the metabolites based on a weight‐of‐evidence considering the properties of the active substance, its low toxicological profile, the low expected exposure and a worst‐case illustrative risk assessment showing a high MoS. 20

For aquatic organisms, acute toxicity data for assessing the toxicity of 8‐methyldecane‐2‐yl propanoate were provided for fish (Oncorhynchus mykiss), aquatic invertebrates (Daphnia magna) and algae (Pseudokirchneriella subcapitata). Chronic aquatic toxicity data were not available for the active substance. Studies were not available with the formulation for representative uses. In addition, studies were not available for the two pertinent metabolites in surface water (i.e. 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one and the metabolite Unknown (RRT 0.77–0.80)). The waiver of the acute toxicity data with the formulation and the chronic aquatic toxicity data was discussed in the peer review meeting TC 193. It was agreed that the toxicity of the formulation can likely be predicted based on the data with the active substance. 21 Overall, a low acute risk was demonstrated at FOCUS step 1 with a large MoS and, owing to the physico‐chemical properties of the active substance, 22 a conclusion of low chronic risk can be reached based on a qualitative risk assessment.14 Regarding the two pertinent surface water metabolites, a low acute risk to aquatic organisms was concluded based on a screening risk assessment demonstrating a high MoS at FOCUS step 1. A conclusion of low chronic risk can also be reached based on a qualitative risk assessment.

Acute (oral and contact) and chronic oral toxicity tests on adult honey bees were available for 8‐methyldecan‐2yl proponoate. Valid data were not available for larvae. 23 Data were not available with the representative formulation. The absence of data with the representative formulation was discussed in the peer review meeting TC 193. 24 Considering the composition, the experts noted that the Lithos CornProtect may have a physical effect on bees. However, the experts considered that this was not a concern considering the application rate of the formulation for representative uses.

Based on EFSA (2013), at the screening step a low acute risk was concluded for adult bees from both contact and oral exposure for the representative use. The same conclusion for the acute scenario could be reached based on European Commission (2002). A low chronic (oral) risk to adult honey bees was also identified for the active substance. Regarding larvae, a low risk was concluded based on a weight‐of‐evidence approach considering (i) the species‐specific MoA, (ii) the less than 10‐fold increase of exposure relative to the background exposure due to the representative uses, (iii) the limited expected chronic oral intake by larvae due to the physico‐chemical properties of the active, (iv) the low application rate and slow release of the substance and (v) the crop attractiveness (although exposure via pollen and nectar cannot be excluded for weeds in‐ and off‐field). 25 In addition, no evidence of larval mortality has been observed in the available testing although it can't be used to draw a conclusion on its own. Toxicity data and risk assessment were not provided for bumblebees or solitary bees. Pertinent plant metabolites were not identified. Assessment of accumulative effects was considered not necessary. Data for sublethal effects were not available (data gap, see Section 10).

Tier 1 toxicity tests on non‐target arthropods other than bees with the parasitic wasp Aphidius rhopalosiphi and the predatory mite Typhlodromus pyri, were submitted with the formulation for representative uses. Based on the available data and risk assessment, a low in‐ and off‐field risk to non‐target arthropods was concluded for the representative use of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate. This is in line with the expected specific mode of action (i.e. no effects reported on other species than the target species).

A chronic toxicity test with the formulation for representative uses was available for earthworms, and a low risk was concluded for the representative uses. Toxicity data were not submitted for the pertinent soil metabolites, i.e. 8‐methyldecan‐2‐one and Unknown metabolite (RRT 0.77–0.80). However, a low chronic risk was concluded, assuming a 10 times higher toxicity than the parent.

Toxicity data on soil meso‐ and macrofauna were not available. However, since, in line with the Regulation 283/2013, 26 low risk is concluded for the formulation for representative uses at Tier 1 and the product is not applied directly to the soil, these were not deemed necessary. Based on a study with the representative formulation, a low risk to soil microorganisms was concluded for the representative uses.

The waiving of ecotoxicity studies assessing the effects on non‐target terrestrial plants (NTTPs) with the active substance or the representative formulation was discussed at the Peer review meeting TC 193. Considering the absence of indication of phytotoxicity in efficacy studies, the specificities of the active substances, the low application rate and the fact that the algae test was performed under worst‐case conditions not comparable with expected realistic exposure, it was concluded that the risk to NTTPs can be considered as addressed. 27

Ecotoxicity data on the effects to organisms involved in sewage treatment processes were considered not required, considering the lack of exposure of sewage treatment plants due to the volatility of the active substance.

The endocrine disrupting (ED) properties of 8‐methyldecan‐2‐yl propanoate for humans and non‐target organisms were discussed in the Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 189 (24–25 November 2025). 28

The oestrogen, androgen, steroidogenesis (EAS) mediated adversity/activity were not sufficiently investigated neither for humans nor other non‐target organisms. For the thyroid (T) modality, a 90‐day oral toxicity study on rat was available (with investigation of thyroid histopathology and thyroid hormones), where no pattern of T‐mediated adversity/activity was observed.

Frequency and Prognostic Impact of CDKN2A/B Alteration in Oligodendrogliomas: Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Authors: Satoshi NAKASU, Shoichi DEGUCHI, Yoko NAKASU
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH)-mutant astrocytomas with homozygous deletion of cyclin-dependent kinase 2A/B (CDKN2A/B-HomoD) are categorized to grade 4 in the new World Health Organization (WHO) classification. However, the clinical implications of CDKN2A/B-HomoD in oligodendrogliomas remain unclear. This study systematically reviewed and meta-analyzed the literature on molecularly defined oligodendrogliomas (mOlig) to find the frequency and prognostic significance of CDKN2A/B gene alterations. Overall survival was worse in patients with CDKN2A/B-HomoD [pooled hazard ratio (pHR) 2.44; 95% confidential interval (CI), 1.59-3.76; P < 0.0001; 7 studies, 1,012 patients] than in those without CDKN2A/B-HomoD. Although the frequency (95% CI) was very low in grade 2 tumors (0.31%; 0.02-0.4) than in grade 3 tumors (9.4%; 6.2-14.0; I2 = 52.0%), pHR of multivariate analyses with covariates of WHO grade and age was still significant (P = 0.017). In contrast, the method in CDKN2A/B evaluation was a significant factor for the heterogeneity in frequency. The pooled frequency of CDKN2A/B-HomoD in grade 3 mOlig by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) (20.3%) was higher than that by other methods (7.3%; P < 0.0006), probably due to the lower threshold for CDKN2A/B-HomoD in FISH studies that was used in this analysis. The frequency (95% CI) of other alterations of the CDKN2A/B gene, i.e., mutation, hemizygous deletion, and promoter methylation, was estimated as 1.48% (0.6-3.5), 15.9% (9.8-24.7), and 20.6% (13.7-29.8), respectively. The clinical significance of these alterations remains unclear due to the immaturity of the investigations.

Introduction

Oligodendrogliomas are diffusely infiltrating gliomas in the adult cerebral hemisphere. Although they used to be diagnosed with a typical honeycomb appearance, the World Health Organization (WHO) 2021 classification has defined diffuse gliomas with IDH mutation and chromosome 1p/19q codeletion as an oligodendroglioma.1) The incidence of molecularly defined oligodendrogliomas (mOlig) is 0.29 per 100,000 person-years (annual age-adjusted incidence rates in 2018-2019 Central Brain Tumor Registry of the United States),2) although epidemiological data remain insufficient. They are grouped into grades 2 and 3 based on the presence of microvascular proliferation, necrosis, increased mitotic figures, and cellular atypia.1) However, the distinct definition of the grading remains underdetermined.

Most of mOlig have TERT promoter mutation but lack ATRX mutation,1) and they often have allelic loss of chromosome 9p, where the tumor suppressor gene CDKN2A/B is located. The CDKN2A encodes p16, a negative regulator of cyclin-dependent kinases, and p14, an activator of TP53, is inactivated in many human cancers.3) CDKN2A silencing enhances carcinogenesis and relates to metastasis with poor prognosis in many cancers. Suppression of the gene function is caused by promoter methylation, mutation, and homozygous deletion by different degrees depending on the cancer type.3) Locus deletions often encompass the adjacent CDKN2B that also encodes for the p15 gene.3)

IDH-mutant astrocytomas with homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/B (CDKN-HomoD), a main inactivating alteration in CDKN2A/B, are categorized to grade 4.1) Although CDKN2A mutation is rare, IDH-mutant astrocytoma patients with CDKN2A mutation had significantly worse progression-free survival (PFS; P = 0.0025) and overall survival (OS; P < 0.0001) than grade-matched patients with wild-type CDKN2A but statistically equivalent PFS and OS outcomes than patients with CDKN-HomoD.4) Studies on the promoter methylation of CDKN2A in gliomas found as much as 60%-70% methylation in astrocytomas and variants among different grades of gliomas5) and ～20% in ependymomas.6)

In histologically defined oligodendrogliomas (hOlig), the clinical significance of the CDKN2A/B loss remains controversial.7,8) Although it was reported to be a factor of poor prognosis in mOligo in several studies,9-12) other studies did not agree.13-16) These studies often included cases not only with CDKN-HomoD but also with hemizygous deletion (CDKN-HemiD) and copy-neutral loss of heterogeneity. In contrast, mutation or promoter methylation of the CDKN2A gene may suppress this function. The CDKN2A promoter region in hOlig was methylated in 12%-58% of the samples,17-19) whereas their incidence was rarely investigated in mOlig. This study systematically reviewed and meta-analyzed the literature on mOlig to find the frequency and prognostic significance of CDKN2A/B gene alteration.

Materials and Methods

This study followed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) guidelines. It was not registered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews.

Literature search and data extraction

Figure 1 shows a PRISMA flow diagram. From 1998 onward, relevant English articles were searched using the keywords “oligodendroglioma,” “1p 19q,” or “codeletion” combined with “CDKN2*,” “p16,” “INK4*,” or “9p21” in PubMed and Scopus. Two of the authors independently conducted literature searches and data extraction. EndoNote (version 20, Clarivate, Philadelphia, USA) was used for data processing. Adult hemispheric oligodendroglioma studies were incorporated if molecular testing of 1p/19 codeletion and alteration of CDKN2A (deletion, mutation, promoter methylation) were included. hOlig with 1p/19q codeletion was considered to be mOlig even without testing for IDH mutation. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) molecular data could not be extracted, (2) public database studies (e.g., The Cancer Genome Atlas, etc.), or (3) familial or pediatric cancers. For studies from the same institute, newer or relevant studies were selected. Finally, the authors selected the studies through discussion and consensus.

PRISMA flow diagram.

Patient age (mean or median), sex information, molecular data, and OS were collected. Molecular diagnosis methods were also recorded. WHO grade was recorded at the time of diagnosis, but grade 4 gliomas with IDH mutation and 1p/19q codeletion were revised to grade 3 according to the WHO 2021 criteria.1) Studies were discriminated into Asian or nonAsian regions. When only median values and ranges were available, the mean and standard deviation were calculated using Hozo et al.'s method.20) Individual patient data (IPD) were recorded if available, and relevant statistical data from IPD were recalculated. Hazard ratios (HRs) were used to determine treatment effects. When IPD and HRs were unavailable, HRs were calculated using Tierney et al.'s method21) or from Kaplan-Meier curve data from which WebPlotDigitizer (https://automeris.io/WebPlotDigitizer/) was used to extract survival data, as previously described.22)

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.03 (https://www.r-project.org/). A single-arm meta-analysis was performed to assess the rates of CDKN2A alteration using the metaprop function with a generalized linear mixed model. Studies were assessed for heterogeneity (I2 statistics). Meta-regression analyses were performed to identify heterogeneity-related factors. Analyses were performed using the rma.uni function in the Meta software package. In Kaplan-Meier curves from IPD, log-rank and Cox-proportional hazard tests were used for survival analyses. Covariates of age, WHO grade, and molecular status were included in multivariate analyses when applicable. An inverse-variance approach with a random-effects method was applied in meta-analyses of HR. Two-sided P < 0.05 values were considered statistically significant.

Risk of bias

The Joanna Briggs Institute's critical appraisal checklist for the case series (Supplementary Table 1) (https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools) was used to evaluate the risk of bias. Publication bias was evaluated using a funnel plot when including >10 studies. A linear regression analysis was also performed.

Ethical approval and informed consent

This review did not involve direct studies on humans; therefore, no informed consent was required.

Results

Literature search and data extraction

The search in PubMed and Scopus identified 276 studies after removing duplicates, 170 of which were excluded by title and abstract evaluation (Fig. 1). After full text scrutiny of the remaining 106 studies, 23 studies met the inclusion criteria.9-13,15-17,23-37) Another three articles were found from hand search or cross-reference.38-40) As patient age and sex were often unavailable in mOlig, these two factors were often not used in statistical analyses. Survival data could be extracted from Kaplan-Meier curves in four studies.10,12,15,16) HR had not been calculated in one study due to lack of death in a subgroup.16) In this case, 0.5 was added for death and 1 for the object to calculate the HR using Tienarry et al.'s method.21) IPD for OS was available in five studies.13,24,29,31,33) HR was calculated by combining the IPD of each study because each study often included a small number of CDKN2A alterations or no death in the subgroup.

Risk of bias assessment

Most of the selected studies were retrospective case series with low evidence levels, but they were considered to have an acceptable risk of bias for this study (Supplementary Table 1).

Subgroup and multiple meta-regression analyses were performed to evaluate the causes of moderate heterogeneity of pooled frequencies. The method used in the CDKN2A/B evaluation was a significant factor for heterogeneity (Table 1). The pooled frequency (95% CI) of CDKN-HomoD by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH; 20.3%; 11.3-33.8; I2 = 18.7%) was higher than that by other methods (7.3%; 4.7-11.2; I2 = 36.5%; P < 0.0006), although it was analyzed only in grade 3 tumors due to the rarity of CDKN-HomoD in grade 2. In contrast, the frequency of CDKN-HemiD by FISH was lower than that by other methods, and the difference was significant by multiple meta-regression (P = 0.036). The pooled frequency (95% CI) of AllDel did not show the difference by the methods but showed a significant difference in the WHO grade (grade 2, 8.4%; 3.3-19.5; grade 3, 35.5%; 24.9-47.8; P = 0.001). The area (Asia vs. nonAsia) did not affect any of the frequencies.

OS was worse in patients with CDKN-HomoD [pooled HR (pHR) of univariate analyses, 2.44; 95% CI, 1.59-3.76]; I2 = 12%; P < 0.0001; 7 studies, 1,012 patients9,10,13,24,29,31,33) and pooled HR of multivariate analyses, 2.22; 95% CI, 1.15-4.28; I2 = 0%; P = 0.017; 6 studies, 985 patients; Table 2; Fig. 3]. CDKN-HemiD and AllDel were not significant factors for OS (Fig. 3B and C). CDKN2A mutation

CDKN2A mutation was rarely reported. The pooled frequency (95% CI) was 1.48% (0.6-3.5; I2 = 0%) in six studies with 338 patients.15,28,29,31,37,39) The rarity did not permit further analyses. Our search found only one CDKN2B mutation.31)

CDKN2A/B promoter methylation

In all of the adopted studies, methylation-specific polymerase chain reaction was performed using the same primer sequences of CDKN2A and CDKN2B for the methylated and unmethylated reactions (Supplementary Table 4). The pooled frequency (95% CI) of CDKN2A promoter methylation was 20.6% (13.7-29.8; I2 = 0%) from five studies (n = 97).17,23,28,36,37) Analyses of IPD from the five studies showed no differences in age (n = 31; P = 0.84) and WHO grade (n = 97; P = 0.99), but methylation was observed more often in female patients (n = 31, P = 0.018). Four of 11 tumors with CDKN2A promoter methylation showed loss of heterogeneity of chromosome 9p, and this combination was seen only in grade 3 tumors. p16 expression was evaluated in 15 tumors. All seven tumors with p16 expression had no promoter methylation, whereas four with promoter methylation showed no (n = 2) or weak (n = 2) p16 expression. Four unmethylated tumors had no (n = 2) or weak (n = 2) p16 expression (P = 0.08, Fisher's exact test).

The pooled frequency (95% CI) of CDKN2B promoter methylation was 26.1% (6.0-66.0; I2 = 82.2%) from three studies (n = 50).17,28,37) Although the result was highly heterogeneous, there was no difference in age (P = 0.84), sex (P = 0.25), or WHO grade (P = 0.07) in IPD. Of 14 CDKN2B promoter-methylated tumors, five showed loss of heterogeneity in chromosome 9p, but the combination was not restricted in grade 3 tumors.

No survival data were available in these articles with CDKN2A/B promoter methylation.

Discussion

In this study, the pooled frequency of CDKN-HomoD was 9.2% in WHO grade 3 oligodendrogliomas, and it was a strong prognostic factor for OS. Although CDKN-HemiD and promoter methylation of the gene were found in ～20% of mOlig, their clinical implications remain unclear.

Pooled frequency of CDKN2A/B alteration

CDKN deletion can be evaluated by various methods, such as single nucleotide polymorphism microarrays, next-generation sequencing, DNA-based methylation studies, and FISH.41) The accuracy of these methods depends on the specific assay types used, as the resolution of each method differs. Polymerase chain reaction-dependent methods will likely miss deletions when there is significant normal cell contamination or clonal heterogeneity, whereas the p16 probe used for FISH is large and may miss small deletions.42) There is another problem with test interpretation criteria and positive test cutoffs in FISH. Two of the FISH studies included in this study set a cutoff value of no CDKN signals between CDKN-HomoD and CDKN-HemiD at >10% to 15% (Supplementary Table 2).10,38) Another study set a mean test signal-to-control signal ratio of <0.5 in 200 tumor cells as homozygous deletion.34) Recent studies that evaluated CDKN-HomoD in astrocytomas recommended a cutoff value of no CDKN signals as 30%.43,44) Suman et al.44) described that using a cutoff at 10%, 20%, and 30% categorized 72.7%, 30.9%, and 10.9% of grade 3 astrocytomas to CDKN-HomoD, respectively. They advocated that a cutoff value of ≥30% should be used to avoid false-positive reports and identify cases expected to have an aggressive clinical course, despite being histologically grade 2/3. In this context, the high frequency of CDKN-HomoD by FISH in this study might be due to a lower cutoff value setting in at least two of the adopted studies, although the optimal value of the cutoff point depends on the amount of contaminated nontumor cells. It was believed that the pooled frequency of 7.3%, detected in the nonFISH method, is more applicable to the incidence of CDKN-HomoD in grade 3 mOlig. However, heterogeneity remained moderate (I2 = 36.5%) in the nonFISH method possibly due to differences in methodology and thresholds.

CDKN2A mutation is rarely reported in gliomas.45,46) Point mutations can inactivate in vitro and in vivo p16 (CDKN2A) activities in human glioma.47) Yokoda et al.4) described a mutation frequency in IDH-mutant astrocytoma of ～3%. In mOlig, we found that only five studies evaluated the mutation. The mutation rate was so rare that prognostic significance was not analyzed (5 in 339 cases). Of these, two truncating mutations were of unknown clinical significance,29) and one case was associated with CDKN2A promoter methylation.28) Further accumulation of cases is needed to determine prognostic significance.

Epigenetic alterations are suggested to regulate gene expression without affecting the base sequence. CDKN2A/B promoter methylation relates to the suppression of CDKN2A/B function in several types of cancers.3) Alves et al.5) found CDKN2A promoter methylation in 65.6% of astrocytomas of various grades. Moreover, they found a highly significant negative correlation between p16 immunopositivity and CDKN2A promoter methylation (P < 0.001). Female patients had statistically more frequent CDKN2A methylated promoters (P = 0.036) than male patients. In mOlig, a few studies reported a frequency of promoter methylation of CDKN2A.23,28,36,37) The pooled frequency (95% CI) in this study was 20.6% (13.7-29.8), and there was no difference in the WHO grade. Godfraid et al.28) reported that CDKN2A promoter methylation was seen in grades 2 (3 of 7) and 3 (3 of 8) mOligs, but the latter always combined with hemizygous 9p21 (which included CDKN2A region) loss, while the former did not. The study by Wolter et al. reported similar results.37) However, the precise biologically and clinically relevant location of hypermethylation have not been identified for CDKN2A/B.48) The studies investigating DNA methylation used primer-based assays, which offer information for only a subset of CpG dinucleotides within a given CpG island. Wong et al.49) reported that the primers used in this study alone (Supplementary Table 4) might underestimate the extent of methylated CpG sites.

Prognostic significance of CDKN2A/B alteration

One previous systematic review in IDH-mutant lower-grade gliomas failed to prove the prognostic significance of CDKN-HomoD in 1p/19q codeleted gliomas; however, it was believed that the review hardly concluded the evaluation of only a few studies (three articles).50) This study, which included seven studies of 1,012 patients, was the first to demonstrate worse OS in patients with CDKN-HomoD by meta-analysis. Although the results included grade 2 mOlig, the number of CDKN-HomoD in grade 2 tumors was still very small. Furthermore, pHR in multivariate analyses with covariates of WHO grade and age showed the prognostic significance.

It remains unclear whether CDKN-HemiD and CDKN2A/B promoter methylation have prognostic impact in mOlig. Kuo et al.19) reported no survival difference between the patients with and without p16 (CDKN2A) methylation in hOlig. They also showed a significant correlation between p16 methylation and chromosome 9p22 loss. Hypermethylation of the CpG island of p16/CDKN2A correlated with gene inactivation in gliomas.51,52) This study also detected the tendency between the results of p16 expression and the promoter methylation (P = 0.08). Interestingly, this study showed that the combination of CDKN-HemiD and CDKN2A/B promoter methylation was found in grade 3 mOligs. Further accumulation of data is needed due to the rarity of the report.

Study limitations

This study had some limitations. First, most studies were retrospective and had low evidence levels. Consequently, heterogeneity was sometimes high in pooled data, and multiple meta-regression analyses were required to identify the reasons for this. Due to the lack of age data in several studies, we could not analyze this factor in meta-regression. Second, the HR for survival was calculated using Tienarry et al.'s method,21) or survival data were extracted from Kaplan-Meier curves in figures of articles in several studies. Such processes may have inadvertently caused slight errors. Third, some multi-institutional studies might have had overlapping data with other studies. Although the studies were carefully selected, some duplicate data might have been included.

Conclusion

The frequency of CDKN-HomoD in mOlig was very rare in grade 2 tumors and 9.2% in grade 3 tumors. Since a few studies using FISH may overestimate the incidence, the actual frequency may be as low as 7.3% in grade 3 tumors. In any case, it had a distinct prognostic significance for OS. Although hemizygous deletion or promoter methylation of CDKN2A/B was more frequently seen, their clinical significance has not been well studied in mOlig. Further studies assessing the deletion and promoter methylation of CDKN2A/B are needed for the precise pathological grading and prediction of clinical outcomes in mOlig.

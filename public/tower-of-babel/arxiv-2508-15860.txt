Robust Residual Finite Scalar Quantization for Neural Compression
Xiaoxu Zhu

Abstract
Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) has emerged as a promising alternative to Vector Quantization (VQ) in neural compression, offering simplified training and improved stability. However, naive application of FSQ in residual quantization frameworks suffers from the residual magnitude decay problem , where subsequent FSQ layers receive progressively weaker signals, severely limiting their effectiveness. We propose Robust Residual Finite Scalar Quantization (RFSQ) , a general framework that addresses this fundamental limitation through two novel conditioning strategies: learnable scaling factors and invertible layer normalization. Our approach maintains the simplicity of FSQ while enabling effective multi-stage residual quantization. Comprehensive experiments on ImageNet demonstrate that RFSQ variants significantly outperform strong baselines including VQ-EMA, FSQ, and LFQ, achieving up to 45% improvement in perceptual loss and 28.7% reduction in L1 reconstruction error. The proposed LayerNorm strategy shows the most consistent improvements across different configurations, establishing RFSQ as a superior quantization method for neural compression. Related Work

The landscape of neural quantization has evolved significantly over the past decade, with vector quantization methods forming the foundational pillar of discrete representation learning. Traditional VQ methods
[
14
,
11
]
employ clustering-based optimization to learn discrete codebooks that effectively capture the underlying data distribution. While these approaches have demonstrated considerable effectiveness across various domains, they inherently require meticulous tuning of auxiliary losses and frequently encounter codebook utilization challenges that can severely impact model performance
[
8
]
. The complexity of managing codebook dynamics, including the notorious ”dead codebook” problem, has motivated researchers to explore alternative quantization paradigms.

Finite Scalar Quantization emerged as a compelling alternative by fundamentally reimagining the quantization process
[
10
]
. Rather than learning complex codebook structures, FSQ eliminates codebook learning entirely by quantizing each dimension independently to predefined levels, creating an implicit combinatorial codebook through dimensional combinations. This elegant approach has demonstrated remarkable success across diverse applications, including masked image generation tasks
[
2
]
, where it enables more stable training dynamics, unified vision models
[
7
]
that benefit from its simplicity, and multimodal language modeling
[
1
]
where the reduced complexity facilitates large-scale training.

The concept of residual quantization has gained significant traction as a method for capturing fine-grained details through hierarchical decomposition. Residual VQ (RVQ)
[
16
,
3
]
applies multiple quantization stages sequentially, with each subsequent stage refining the representation by quantizing the residual error from previous stages. This hierarchical approach has proven particularly effective in audio compression applications, where capturing both coarse and fine-grained temporal patterns is crucial for maintaining perceptual quality. Recent innovations have explored the integration of residual concepts with FSQ, notably through group residual FSQ (GRFSQ)
[
13
]
, which combines grouped quantization with residual refinement for facial motion tokenization applications.

Our work distinguishes itself from existing approaches by addressing a fundamental limitation that has been largely overlooked in the literature. While GRFSQ focuses on grouped quantization strategies for specific application domains, our RFSQ framework provides a general-purpose solution with robust conditioning strategies that can be seamlessly integrated into any encoder-decoder architecture, thereby offering broader applicability and practical utility.

Method

3.1
Background: Finite Scalar Quantization

FSQ quantizes a
d
-dimensional vector
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by independently quantizing each dimension to a finite set of levels. For dimension
i
with levels
L_{i}
, the quantization function is:

\text{FSQ}_{i}(z_{i})=\text{round}\left(\frac{z_{i}\cdot(L_{i}-1)}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{2}{L_{i}-1}

(1)

The total codebook size is
\prod_{i=1}^{d}L_{i}
, and the code rate is
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log_{2}(L_{i})
bits per token.

3.2
Naive Residual FSQ and Its Problems

A straightforward extension applies FSQ residually across multiple stages:

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{1}

\displaystyle=\text{FSQ}_{1}(\mathbf{z})

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{2}

\displaystyle=\text{FSQ}_{2}(\mathbf{r}_{1})

(3)

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{K},\mathbf{r}_{K}

\displaystyle=\text{FSQ}_{K}(\mathbf{r}_{K-1})

(5)

where
\mathbf{q}_{k}
is the quantized output and
\mathbf{r}_{k}=\mathbf{r}_{k-1}-\mathbf{q}_{k}
is the residual.

Residual Magnitude Decay Problem:
In practice, this naive approach suffers from progressively diminishing residual magnitudes. As quantization stages proceed,
\|\mathbf{r}_{k}\|\ll\|\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\|
, causing subsequent FSQ layers to operate on extremely weak signals, severely limiting their quantization effectiveness.

3.3
Robust RFSQ Framework

To address the residual decay problem, we propose two robust conditioning strategies:

3.3.1
Strategy 1: Learnable Scaling

We introduce learnable scaling factors
\alpha_{k}
for each stage:

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{k},\mathbf{r}_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\text{FSQ}_{k}(\alpha_{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{k-1})

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}_{k-1}-\frac{\mathbf{q}_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}

(7)

The scaling factors
\alpha_{k}
are learned parameters initialized to 1.0, allowing adaptive amplification of residual signals while maintaining perfect reconstruction through inverse scaling.

3.3.2
Strategy 2: Invertible LayerNorm

We apply invertible layer normalization before quantization:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{r}_{k-1})

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{k},\mathbf{r}_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\text{FSQ}_{k}(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{k-1})

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{k}

\displaystyle=\text{LayerNorm}^{-1}(\mathbf{q}_{k})-\mathbf{r}_{k-1}

(10)

where LayerNorm normalizes features to unit variance while maintaining invertibility for perfect reconstruction.

Complementary Design:
Both strategies address different aspects of the residual decay problem:

•

FSQ Internal Normalization:
Provides a standardized working space for quantization

•

RFSQ External Conditioning:
Prepares and amplifies input signals to optimally match the quantization workspace

4
Experiments

We conduct comprehensive experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed RFSQ method against strong baseline quantization approaches. All experiments are performed under strictly controlled conditions with identical 12.0-bit code rates and 4096 codebook sizes to ensure fair comparison.

4.1
Experimental Setup

4.1.1
Dataset

Our experimental evaluation is conducted on the ImageNet dataset
[
4
]
, which serves as the gold standard benchmark for image recognition and reconstruction tasks, containing over 1.2 million training images and 50,000 validation images spanning 1,000 distinct object categories. This comprehensive dataset provides the necessary diversity and complexity to thoroughly evaluate quantization methods across varied visual content.

For our experimental protocol, we utilize the complete ImageNet training set with images resized to 128×128 resolution, striking an optimal balance between computational efficiency and reconstruction quality assessment. This resolution choice enables thorough evaluation while maintaining manageable computational requirements for our extensive multi-method comparison. All images undergo standard preprocessing procedures, including normalization to the [0,1] range using established ImageNet normalization statistics, ensuring consistency with standard computer vision practices and enabling fair comparison with existing literature.

4.1.2
Encoder-Decoder Architecture

To ensure rigorous and fair comparison across all quantization methods, we employ a carefully designed unified encoder-decoder architecture that maintains identical capacity and structural characteristics across all experimental configurations. This architectural consistency is crucial for isolating the impact of different quantization strategies.

The encoder component processes 3-channel RGB input images of 128×128 resolution through three progressive downsampling layers, each employing 4×4 convolutional kernels with stride 2 and padding 1. The channel progression expands from 3 input channels to 128 channels in the first layer, then to 256 channels in subsequent layers, ultimately producing 32×32×256 feature maps for quantization. This design ensures sufficient representational capacity while maintaining computational efficiency.

The decoder architecture mirrors the encoder’s structure in reverse, accepting 32×32×256 quantized features and progressively upsampling through three transposed convolutional layers with identical kernel specifications. The channel progression transitions from 256 to 256 to 128 channels before culminating in the final 3-channel RGB reconstruction.

Loss Function:
We employ a combination of L1 reconstruction loss and LPIPS perceptual loss
[
17
]
:

\mathcal{L}=\lambda_{1}\|\mathbf{x}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}+\lambda_{p}\text{LPIPS}(\mathbf{x},\hat{\mathbf{x}})

(11)

where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{p}=1.0
for equal weighting.

4.1.3

\beta_{1}

The experimental results provide compelling evidence for the theoretical advantages of hierarchical residual quantization, revealing fundamental insights into why progressive refinement succeeds where single-stage approaches fall short. The observed performance gains stem from RFSQ’s ability to decompose complex quantization tasks into manageable sub-problems, each operating within an optimal dynamic range. This decomposition addresses the inherent limitation of FSQ’s fixed quantization boundaries by enabling adaptive refinement at multiple scales.

The convergence behavior observed in our training dynamics analysis illuminates a critical aspect of quantization learning: RFSQ methods not only reach lower final loss values but do so through more stable optimization trajectories. This stability emerges from the conditioning strategies’ ability to maintain consistent signal magnitudes across residual stages, preventing the optimization landscape from becoming increasingly ill-conditioned as training progresses. The LayerNorm strategy, in particular, provides implicit regularization that guides the learning process toward more generalizable solutions.

Most fundamentally, the performance consistency across diverse evaluation metrics suggests that RFSQ addresses quantization at a more principled level than existing approaches. Rather than optimizing for specific reconstruction objectives, the hierarchical decomposition naturally balances pixel-level fidelity with perceptual quality, indicating that the method captures intrinsic properties of the visual representation problem.

The architecture analysis reveals a fundamental principle underlying effective residual quantization: the relationship between stage granularity and representational capacity. The consistent advantage of 4-stage configurations over their 2-stage counterparts (15-17% improvement in L1 loss) stems from the finer-grained decomposition of the quantization problem. Each additional stage operates on residuals with reduced magnitude and complexity, allowing FSQ’s fixed quantization boundaries to more effectively capture the remaining signal variations.

This hierarchical refinement process addresses a core limitation of single-stage quantization methods: the mismatch between uniform quantization grids and non-uniform data distributions. By progressively reducing the complexity of the quantization task at each stage, 4-stage RFSQ enables each FSQ layer to operate closer to its optimal operating point, where the fixed quantization boundaries align more favorably with the local signal characteristics. The diminishing returns observed beyond 4 stages suggest an optimal balance between representational capacity and computational efficiency.

4.4
RFSQ Strategy Analysis

The effectiveness of our proposed conditioning strategies reveals deeper insights into the nature of the residual magnitude decay problem and its solutions. The LayerNorm strategy emerges as the most effective approach, achieving consistent improvements of 4.2% in L1 loss, 6.8% in perceptual quality, and 1.9% in PSNR across different architectural configurations. This consistency suggests that LayerNorm addresses the fundamental issue of signal conditioning in a principled manner, providing adaptive normalization that maintains optimal input characteristics for each quantization stage.

The success of the Scale strategy, while slightly less pronounced than LayerNorm, demonstrates the value of learnable adaptation in residual quantization frameworks. With improvements of 3.8% in L1 loss and 4.1% in perceptual quality, the learnable scaling factors effectively address the magnitude decay problem by providing stage-specific amplification that compensates for the natural attenuation of residual signals. The learned scales adapt to the specific characteristics of each stage, optimizing the trade-off between signal amplification and quantization precision.

Perhaps most tellingly, even the vanilla RFSQ approach (None strategy) substantially outperforms traditional baseline methods, indicating that the residual decomposition principle itself provides significant advantages. This observation validates our core hypothesis that hierarchical quantization can overcome the limitations of fixed quantization boundaries, even without sophisticated conditioning mechanisms. The additional improvements provided by LayerNorm and Scale strategies represent refinements that unlock the full potential of the residual quantization framework.

Conclusion

We propose Robust Residual Finite Scalar Quantization (RFSQ), a novel framework that addresses the fundamental residual magnitude decay problem in naive residual FSQ implementations. Our key contributions include:

•

Problem Identification:
We identify and analyze the residual decay problem that limits naive RFSQ effectiveness

•

Robust Solutions:
Two novel conditioning strategies (learnable scaling and invertible LayerNorm) that enable effective multi-stage quantization

•

Superior Performance:
Comprehensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements over strong baselines, with up to 45% perceptual loss improvement

•

General Framework:
RFSQ serves as a plug-and-play quantization module applicable to various architectures

Our experimental evaluation establishes RFSQ as a superior quantization method for neural compression, with LayerNorm strategy providing the most consistent improvements across different configurations. The scalable multi-stage architecture enables progressive quality improvements, making RFSQ suitable for practical deployment in high-quality compression applications.

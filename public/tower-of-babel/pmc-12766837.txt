Mechanistic insights into PD-1 inhibitor-associated immune thrombocytopenia: a case report integrating longitudinal transcriptomic profiling
Authors: Huishan Li, Yan Wang, Jianfeng Zhu, Hehui Chen, Tianshu Liu, Luoyan Ai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have significantly improved survival outcomes in patients with advanced malignancies. However, their association with immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) poses a critical risk of severe hemorrhage and necessitates treatment discontinuation. The precise molecular mechanisms underlying ICI-associated ITP remain largely unclear, critically impeding the development of predictive biomarkers and targeted therapeutic strategies. Here, we present a case report of an elderly patient with stage IV gastric adenocarcinoma who developed recurrent ITP following programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) inhibitor serplulimab, trastuzumab and chemotherapy. Notably, longitudinal peripheral blood mononuclear cell samples were collected and subjected to transcriptomic profiling before and after two distinct ITP episodes: the initial occurrence and a recurrence triggered by PD-1 inhibitor rechallenge, providing matched time-resolved data for mechanistic analysis. Our findings demonstrate that ICIs-induced ITP involves a dual pathogenic mechanism combining immune-mediated platelet destruction and intrinsic megakaryopoietic impairment providing a novel conceptual framework for understanding this immunotherapy complication. And this represents the first prospective longitudinal investigation combining serial biospecimen collection with transcriptomic profiling (RNA sequencing) to elucidate mechanisms underlying ICIs-induced ITP.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) through targeted blockade of the programmed death receptor-1/ligand-1 (PD-1/PD-L1) and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4)-mediated immunosuppressive signaling axes, reprogram antitumor immune responses.1 2 These agents have demonstrated breakthrough efficacy in treating diverse solid tumors and hematologic malignancies, significantly improving clinical outcomes.36 However, the widespread application of ICIs has been accompanied by a marked increase in the incidence of immune-related adverse events (irAEs), posing significant challenges in clinical practice.7 8 Approximately 50% of patients receiving PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy experience irAEs of varying grades, affecting multiple organ systems, including the integumentary system9 10 (eg, maculopapular rash, immune-mediated dermatitis), gastrointestinal tract (eg, colitis),11 12 and respiratory system (eg, pneumonitis).13

Notably, immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), a rare yet clinically challenging subtype of irAEs, substantially elevates the risk of bleeding-related complications, leading to premature termination of anticancer therapy and compromising patient survival outcomes.1416 The reported incidence of ICIs-induced ITP is approximately 1%–2%, but its clinical presentation is highly heterogeneous. It can occur as an isolated event or concomitantly with other irAEs, with onset timing varying widely from after the first cycle to over a year into treatment. This heterogeneity extends to treatment response; while most patients respond to first-line therapies (corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin), a significant proportion develop steroid dependence or refractory disease, requiring second-line agents like rituximab or thrombopoietin receptor agonists.1720 Similar to primary ITP, the diagnosis of ICIs-induced ITP is also an exclusionary diagnosis. It requires comprehensive exclusion of alternative etiologies for thrombocytopenia, including primary hematologic disorders (eg, bone marrow infiltration by primary tumors or myelodysplastic syndromes) and other common causes such as chemotherapy/non-chemotherapeutic agents (eg, heparin), infections, and hepatic pathologies.2123 Furthermore, the pathomechanisms underlying ICIs-induced ITP remain largely unelucidated. Current mechanistic understanding is primarily inferred from primary ITP and limited case reports, encompassing both peripheral platelet destruction (eg, via autoantibody-mediated phagocytosis or T cell-mediated cytotoxicity) and impaired central platelet production in the bone marrow.24 25 However, the precise molecular triggers and immune pathways dysregulated by PD-1/PD-L1 blockade that lead to the loss of tolerance to platelet antigens are critically undefined.

Therefore, elucidating its pathogenic mechanisms holds critical implications for optimizing therapeutic management and maintaining continuity of anticancer regimens.2628 Our understanding of classical primary ITP has been profoundly shaped by genomic and transcriptomic approaches. Genome-wide association studies have identified susceptibility loci within immune-related genes (eg, human leukocyte antigen (HLA), Fc gamma receptor (FCGR)), underscoring a genetic predisposition to immune dysregulation.2931 Transcriptomic profiling of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from patients with primary ITP has further delineated distinct molecular signatures, including upregulated type I interferon signaling, aberrant T follicular helper cell activity, and B-cell activation pathways, which collectively drive a loss of tolerance.32 33 Furthermore, investigations into the bone marrow microenvironment have revealed transcriptomic alterations in megakaryocytes and hematopoietic stem cells, such as dysregulated apoptosis and differentiation pathways, highlighting the critical role of impaired platelet production in disease persistence.3437 Despite these advances in primary ITP, whether and how these established pathways are recapitulated or uniquely engaged in the context of PD-1 blockade remains an open question.

Here, we present a case of serplulimab-induced ITP. Through longitudinal tracking of PBMC samples collected during the initial ITP episode and subsequent recurrence following anti-PD-1 rechallenge, we performed RNA sequencing coupled with multitiered bioinformatics analyses to investigate its underlying mechanisms. This longitudinal design allows us to dynamically characterize the potential molecular mechanisms of ICIs-related ITP and to interrogate the convergence of its transcriptomic landscape with that of primary ITP. By integrating our findings with the established literature on transcriptomic profiling in primary ITP, this study aims to provide critical evidence for deciphering the molecular basis of hematologic toxicity induced by ICIs and formulating personalized treatment.

Case presentation

An elderly patient presented with unexplained abdominal distension in June 2023, accompanied by markedly elevated serum tumor markers (carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA): 950.2 ng/mL; carbohydrate antigen 125 (CA125) : 60.4 U/mL). Contrast-enhanced abdominal CT revealed a gastric antrum mass invading the serosal layer, with omental involvement, peritoneal lymph node metastasis, and hepatic metastases. Gastroscopic biopsy confirmed moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma (Lauren classification: intestinal type). Immunohistochemical profiling demonstrated: proficient mismatch repair (pMMR); human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (Her-2) amplification (100% +++, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)-positive); PD-L1 expression (tumor proportion score (TPS)=0, combined positive score (CPS)=10); Claudin18.2 overexpression (100% ++) . The final diagnosis was gastric adenocarcinoma (T4bN3M1, stage IV, Her2 amplification).

Based on HER2 overexpression and CPS characteristics, the patient was enrolled in a clinical trial named “A single-arm clinical trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of serplulimab combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in HER2-positive advanced gastric/gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma.” on August 11, 2023. First-line therapy with serplulimab and trastuzumab and DOS regimen (docetaxel, oxaliplatin, S-1) was initiated every 3 weeks. Baseline hematologic parameters were within normal limits. However, grade 4 thrombocytopenia (platelet count 13×109/L, normal reference range (n.r.) 125–350×109/L) occurred after the second treatment cycle (figure 1A). Serological testing ruled out common primary autoimmune disorders, with a broad autoantibody panel returning negative. Bone marrow biopsy excluded tumor infiltration and chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression (pathology showed trilineage hematopoiesis with mild hypercellularity) (figure 1B). Bone marrow flow cytometry further revealed: CD34+ (99.9%, positive-expressed: >80% positive cells) indicating hematopoietic progenitor cells within n.r., thereby excluding stem cell depletion. Significantly elevated proportions of CD117+ (59.3%, positive-partially expressed: 20%–80%) and CD33+ (89.4%, positive-expressed: >80% positive cells), suggesting aberrant proliferation of myeloid blasts consistent with arrest of myeloid differentiation at the promyelocytic or myelocytic stage. This differentiation arrest may originate from immune attack-mediated impairment of megakaryocytic lineage commitment. Concurrent increases in CD38+ (90.1%, positive-expressed: >80% positive cells) and HLA-DR+ (89.3%, positive-expressed: >80% positive cells), indicating enhanced terminal differentiation of B cells and activated status of antigen-presenting cells (figure 2). Peripheral blood flow cytometry (EDTA-anticoagulated whole blood) revealed persistently normal lymphocyte subset proportions during the pre-initial and post-initial thrombocytopenia (figure 3A). In contrast to the frequent lymphocytopenia observed in classic ITP, approximately 60%–70% of patients with ICI-included ITP exhibit normal lymphocyte counts. This phenomenon likely results from sustained T-cell activation by ICIs counteracting autoimmune lymphocytic consumption—a paradoxical feature that poses diagnostic challenges in clinical practice. Collectively, these findings confirmed an irAE of thrombocytopenia. Management included: (1) withholding antitumor therapy; (2) administration of methylprednisolone (1 mg/kg/day for 3 days) and intravenous immunoglobulin (20 g/day for 3 days); and (3) initiation of thrombopoietic agents: recombinant human thrombopoietin and the thrombopoietin receptor agonist hetrombopag olamine (7.5 mg daily). Platelet counts quickly improved to 77×109/L after 3 days. Then an oral corticosteroid was given and tapered gradually from 40 mg/day to 2.5 mg/day until February 23, 2024 (figure 1A). A follow-up CT in November 2023 demonstrated partial response in the gastric lesion and metastases (vs August 2023 baseline) (figure 1C). Due to suboptimal platelet recovery (platelet count: 90×109/L, November 7, 2023), the regimen was modified to trastuzumab and S-1 (Cycle 3), followed by S-1 monotherapy for four cycles (last dose: February 1, 2024). Platelet counts normalized (146×109/L) by February 23, 2024, and corticosteroid was discontinued (figure 1A).

Palliative first-line therapy (Cycle 8: serplulimab, trastuzumab, and DOS) was resumed on February 24, 2024, due to tumor progression (Follow-up CT in January 2024) (figure 1C). Although anticipated, the rapid recurrence of progressive thrombocytopenia (platelet count: 68×109/L) as early as day 3 post-treatment was nonetheless striking, necessitating immediate discontinuation of serplulimab. Peripheral blood flow cytometry (EDTA-anticoagulated whole blood) also revealed persistently normal lymphocyte subset proportions during the pre-rechallenge and post-rechallenge thrombocytopenia (figure 3B). Rescue therapy consisting of hetrombopag (5 mg once daily) combined with low-dose methylprednisolone (10 mg once daily) successfully restored platelet counts to 137×109/L within 48 hours (figure 1A). Subsequent CT evaluation on March 27, 2024, demonstrated disease progression, evidenced by enlarging gastric/omental lymphadenopathy and new hepatic metastases (figure 1C).

While a few clinical cases of ICIs-induced ITP have been documented, exploration of its underlying mechanisms was scarce. Here, we tried to provide mechanistic insights into serplulimab-induced ITP pathogenesis in this patient. PBMC samples were collected and subjected to transcriptomic profiling before and after two distinct ITP episodes: the initial occurrence and a recurrence triggered by PD-1 inhibitor rechallenge.

As illustrated in figures 4A, 95 consistently upregulated genes were identified across both ITP events. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)/Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses revealed their predominant involvement in critical biological processes such as T/B-lymphocyte activation, major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class II-mediated antigen presentation, interleukin-6 production, and Fcγ receptor-dependent phagocytosis (figure 4B,C). Concurrently, 231 shared significantly downregulated genes were identified across both ITP episodes (figure 4D). KEGG/GO enrichment analyses demonstrated significant enrichment of these genes in pathological processes including Janus kinase-signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK-STAT) / phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-protein kinase B (PI3K-Akt) signaling cascades, positive regulation of hemopoiesis and hematopoietic stem cell differentiation (figure 4E,F).

Discussion

While PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors have significantly improved survival outcomes in patients with cancer, therapy-induced secondary ITP substantially compromises treatment efficacy, adversely impacts prognosis, and remains largely mechanistically undefined.8 15 Although the etiology of primary ITP is heterogeneous, transcriptomic studies have revealed shared pathways of immune dysregulation, including T-cell and B-cell activation, which provide a valuable context for interpreting our findings in ICIs-induced ITP.33 35 In this study, we report a case of serplulimab-induced ITP and systematically postulate the molecular mechanisms underlying PD-1 inhibitor-associated ITP through longitudinal collecting and transcriptomic profiling of PBMCs during initial treatment and PD-1 antibody rechallenge. To our knowledge, this is the first study trying to explore the mechanism of ICIs-induced ITP by prospective longitudinal sample collection and RNA sequencing technology.

The patient exhibited highly concordant gene expression signatures during ITP recurrence following immunotherapy resumption, suggesting PD-1 inhibitors may induce platelet destruction and impaired thrombopoiesis via similar immune regulatory networks. Integrating all bioinformatic analyses, we propose that antigen-presenting cells present platelet-specific antigens via MHC-II molecules to CD4+ T-cell receptors, initiating autoimmune responses. Activated autoreactive T cells synergistically further drive B-cell differentiation into antibody-secreting plasma cells and promote anti-GPIIb/IIIa IgG production through T-B immunological synapses, finally enhanced FcγR-mediated phagocytosis and accelerated platelet clearance.2838 3841 This adaptive immune activation bears resemblance to pathways implicated in certain inborn errors of immunity (eg, CTLA-4 haploinsufficiency, autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS)), where defective immune regulation leads to autoimmunity, including ITP.42 43 It is plausible that ICIs unmask or exacerbate similar subclinical immune dysregulations. Concurrently, impaired JAK-STAT/PI3K-Akt signaling activation compromises intrinsic thrombopoiesis and megakaryocyte differentiation, further reducing platelet counts.44 The observed downregulation of hematopoietic signaling pathways in PBMCs may reflect a systemic impairment in megakaryopoiesis. This aligns with research in primary ITP highlighting the importance of the bone marrow microenvironment, particularly defects in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells and megakaryocytes. While our PBMC data cannot directly assess the marrow compartment, it suggests a concomitant central defect. This dual-pathway mechanism perpetuates thrombocytopenia in patients with ICIs-induced ITP.45 46

Critically, the immunophenotypic profile of the bone marrow provided direct cellular validation for the transcriptomic pathways identified in PBMCs. The systemic immune activation signature, which was evidenced by upregulated T/B-cell activity and antigen presentation genes in PBMCs, was concretely mirrored in the marrow by a marked expansion of CD38+ plasma cells and HLA-DR+ antigen-presenting cells. Furthermore, the transcriptomic enrichment for Fcγ receptor-mediated phagocytosis in PBMCs aligned with the classic pathophysiological finding of megakaryocytic hyperplasia amidst severe peripheral thrombocytopenia, strongly supporting antibody-dependent platelet clearance as a central mechanism. Concurrently, the observed downregulation of JAK-STAT/PI3K-Akt signaling and hematopoietic regulation in PBMCs corresponded with a flow cytometric pattern of arrested myeloid differentiation in the bone marrow, suggesting a concomitant suppression of thrombopoiesis. This striking concordance between two distinct analytical platforms confirms that the immunologic events captured in the periphery are amplified within the bone marrow microenvironment, thereby providing a coherent and multi-compartmental view of the pathogenesis of ICIs-induced ITP.

In addition, although isolated case reports have described trastuzumab-associated drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia (DITP),4751 several clinical and mechanistic features in our patient distinctly implicate the PD-1 inhibitor serplulimab as the primary etiology. Specifically, the recurrence of severe thrombocytopenia was exclusively triggered by PD-1 inhibitor rechallenge, whereas platelet counts exhibited a gradual recovery trend and subsequent stability during continued trastuzumab therapy following the initial episode. This clear drug-specific recurrence pattern, combined with transcriptomic evidence of broad T/B-cell activation and FcγR signaling, argues strongly against a classic DITP mechanism, which typically involves transient drug-dependent antibody responses without such pervasive immune activation. These findings underscore that in patients receiving combination immunotherapy, the timing of thrombocytopenic events relative to drug exposure and the accompanying immunophenotypic profile are crucial for accurate causal attribution.

We also thoughtfully considered the potential confounding effect of corticosteroid therapy on transcriptomic data. For the initial ITP episode, PBMCs were collected prior to any high-dose corticosteroid administration, thereby capturing the immunopathological signature of acute ICIs-induced ITP in an unmodified state. For the rechallenge episode, sampling was conducted after steroid taper and discontinuation, substantially minimizing the direct transcriptional influence of active immunosuppression. The consistent molecular signatures observed across both clinical events, particularly the shared upregulation of T/B-cell activation and FcγR pathways, strengthen the validity of our findings and suggest that these core mechanisms represent inherent features of ICIs-induced ITP, independent of steroid effects. Future studies incorporating more frequent sampling could further delineate the dynamic interplay between therapeutic immunosuppression and the underlying autoimmune pathology.

Conclusion

We report a case of serplulimab-induced recurrent ITP. Prospective longitudinal PBMC profiling revealed that ICI-associated thrombocytopenia involves both immune-mediated platelet destruction and intrinsic defects in megakaryopoiesis, offering a unique conceptual framework for understanding this immune-mediated complication of cancer immunotherapy.

Limitations and perspectives

While our study provides novel longitudinal transcriptomic insights, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the findings are derived from a single case report, which limits their generalizability and precludes definitive conclusions about the heterogeneity of ICIs-induced ITP. Second, due to clinical constraints, we were unable to perform transcriptomic profiling on the bone marrow biopsy sample.

Future research should aim to validate the proposed dual-pathway mechanism through prospective, multicenter studies with larger cohorts. Furthermore, investigating whether patients who develop ICIs-induced ITP harbor genetic predispositions akin to inborn errors of immunity could identify high-risk individuals and novel therapeutic targets.

Parallel geodesics and minimal stable length of random groups
Tsung-Hsuan Tsai

Abstract
We show that for any pair of long enough parallel geodesics in a random group G ℓ ​ ( m , d ) G_{\ell}(m,d) with m m generators at density d < 1 / 6 d<1/6 , there is a van Kampen diagram having only one layer of faces. Using this result, we give an upper bound, depending only on d d , of the number of pairwise parallel geodesics in G ℓ ​ ( m , d ) G_{\ell}(m,d) when d < 1 / 6 d<1/6 . As an application, we show that the minimal stable length of G ℓ ​ ( m , d ) G_{\ell}(m,d) at d < 1 / 6 d<1/6 is exactly 1 1 .

Introduction

Random groups.

A
random group

G_{\ell}(m,d)
with
m\geq 2
generators at density
d\in[0,1]
, as defined by Gromov in
[
Gro93
]
, is given by the presentation

G=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{m}|R_{\ell}\rangle,

where
R_{\ell}
is a set of
(2m-1)^{d\ell}
relators, randomly chosen from the set of cyclically reduced words on
\{x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{m}^{\pm}\}
of length at most
\ell
.

We say that
G_{\ell}(m,d)
satisfies some group property
asymptotically almost surely
(denoted a.a.s.) if the probability that the property holds converges to
1
as the relator length
\ell
goes to infinity. The first result in the theory of random groups is a phase transition (
[
Gro93
]
): at
d>1/2
, a.a.s.
G_{\ell}(m,d)
is a trivial group; whereas at
d<1/2
, a.a.s.
G_{\ell}(m,d)
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group.

Parallel geodesics.

Two geodesics in (the Cayley graph of) a
\delta
-hyperbolic group are called
parallel
if they do not intersect and their end points are respectively
10\delta
-close. By convention, if the mentioned geodesics are bi-infinite, they share the same boundary points.

In this article, we study van Kampen diagrams between parallel geodesics, referred to as
band diagrams
(Definition-Lemma
4.2
). A
1-layer face
in a band diagram is a face that touches both geodesics. We first discover that 1-layer faces appear in band diagrams of random groups at density
d<1/4
.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.3
)
.

If
d<1/4
, then a.a.s. for any pair of parallel geodesics
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
of
G_{\ell}(m,d)
of lengths at least
\frac{1200}{1-4d}\ell
and any band diagram
D
between them, there is a face of
D
that intersects the two boundary geodesics.

We then show that at
d<1/6
, which corresponds to
C^{\prime}(1/3)
in random groups, band diagrams already have only one layer of faces.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Let
L\geq 10000\ell
. If
d<1/6
, then a.a.s. for any pair of parallel geodesics
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
of
G_{\ell}(m,d)
of lengths at least
L
, there exists a pair of parallel sub-geodesics
\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime}
of lengths at least
L-7200\ell
having a 1-layer band diagram.

To prove this result, we introduce a new small cancellation condition, called the
\widetilde{C}(p)
condition (Definition
3.3
), which is a strong version of the
C(p)
condition. We then established phase transitions for the
\widetilde{C}(p)
conditions in random groups (Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
).

Canonical representatives.

The notion of
canonical representatives
was introduced by Rips and Sela in
[
RS95
]
to solve equations over hyperbolic groups. They then asked whether every Gromov hyperbolic group admits globally stabled canonical representatives. A recent work by Lazarovich and Sageev
[
LS24
]
shows that hyperbolic cubulated groups admit such representatives, and it is known from Olivier and Wise
[
OW11
]
that random groups at density
d<1/6
are cubulable, and hence admit globally stabled canonical representatives.

In Appendix of
[
RS95
]
, Rips and Sela remarked that van Kampen diagrams between geodesics of a
C^{\prime}(1/8)
group are 1-layer diagrams. Using this structure, they constructed globally stabled canonical representatives for
C^{\prime}(1/8)
groups. Together with their construction, our Theorem
4.6
provides an alternative method for showing that random groups at density
d<1/6
have globally stabled canonical representatives.

Number of parallel geodesics.

It is known that the number of pairwise parallel bi-infinite geodesics in a hyperbolic group is bounded above by a large number (
[
Gro87
]
,
[
Del96
, Proposition 3.1]
). In
[
GM21
]
, Gruber and Mackay showed that for a random
triangular
group at density
d\in]1/3,d_{crit}[
, there exists a large
n_{0}=n_{0}(d)
such that, for any
n\geq n_{0}
, the
n
-periodic Burnside quotient is infinite. A key ingredient in their proof is providing a much sharper bound on the number of parallel geodesics.

In this article, we provide a sharp upper bound on the number of parallel geodesics in
G_{\ell}(m,d)
at density
d<1/6
:

Theorem

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer. Let
G_{\ell}(m,d)
be a random group at density
d<1/6
. There exists a number
C=C(d)
such that, if
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
is a set of
k
parallel geodesics of length at least
C\ell
, then a.a.s.

k\leq 2+\frac{4d}{1-6d}.

Let
R,L,N>0
. A hyperbolic group
G=\langle X|R\rangle
(acting on itself) is
(R,L,N)
-acylindrical if, for every
x,y\in\Cay(X,R)
with
|x-y|\geq L
, the number of elements

satisfying simultaneously
|gx-x|\leq R
and
|gy-y|\leq R
is at most
N
(
[

, Definition 5.5]
). Following Gruber and Mackay’s acylindricity result for random triangular groups
[

, Proof of Theorem 1.5 by Proposition 1.3]
, we have a version for random groups at density

.

Corollary
.

If
d<1/6
, then for any

, a.a.s.

is

(a\ell,C\ell+a\ell,k(C\ell+2a\ell)^{2})

-acylidrical where

.

Despite this result, it is not known whether there exists a uniform

such that for

, the

-periodic Burnside quotient of

with

is infinite. The main difficulty lies in the fact that the hyperbolicity constant of

is linear in

(see

]
,
[

and Theorem

Minimal stable length.

The
minimal stable length
of a hyperbolic group
G
is the infimum of the stable lengths
[u]^{\infty}
among the loxodromic elements
u\in G
. According to
[

, Proposition 3.1]
, if
G
is a hyperbolic group with at most

of a loxodromic element

is at least
[u]/k
where

is the translation length of
u
.

As an application of Theorem

, we show that, in a random group

at density

, a.a.s. if

then

(Remark

). This leads to the following result:

Theorem

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

If
d<1/6
, then a.a.s. the minimal stable length of
G_{\ell}(m,d)
is
1
.

Preliminary: van Kampen diagrams and random groups

Van Kampen diagrams.

We consider edge-oriented combinatorial 2-complexes as described in
[
LS77
, §III.2]
. A vertex is also called a point or a
0
-cell; a face is called a
2
-cell. Every edge
e
has a starting point, an ending point and an inverse edge
e^{-1}
. A pair of inverse edges
\{e,e^{-1}\}
is called an undirected edge or a
1
-cell. For a 2-complex
Y
, denote by
Y^{(i)}
the set of
i
-cells of
Y
(for
i=0,1,2
) and
|Y|=|Y^{(2)}|
the number of faces of
Y
. For simplicity, if
e
is an (oriented) edge of
Y
, we also write
e\in Y^{(1)}
; similarly, if
f
is a face of
Y
, we write
f\in Y
.

A
path
is a sequence of edges
p=e_{1}\dots e_{n}
such that the ending point of
e_{i}
is the starting point of
e_{i+1}
for each
i
. A path is called
reduced
if it has no subpath of type
ee^{-1}
. A
loop
is a path that ends at its starting point. A loop is
cyclically reduced
if it is a reduced loop with the additional requirement that
e_{n}\neq e_{1}^{-1}
. A path (or a loop) is called
simple
if it does not cross itself.

In this article, we introduce an additional structure on 2-complexes for convenience: every face
f
is assigned a starting point and an orientation on its boundary, referred to as the
boundary loop
and denoted by
\partial f
.

Let
G=\langle X|R\rangle
be a finite group presentation where every relator
r\in R
is cyclically reduced. The
Cayley complex

\Cay(X,R)
of
\langle X|R\rangle
is the 2-complex constructed from its Cayley graph by attaching a face on each relator loop. Every edge of
\Cay(X,R)
is labeled by the generator it represents, and every face is naturally labeled by the relator read on its boundary loop.

Definition 2.1
.

A
diagram
with respect to the group presentation
\langle X|R\rangle
is a finite connected 2-complex
Y
with labels on edges by generators and labels on faces by relators such that:

•

If an edge
e
is labeled by
x\in X
, then its inverse
e^{-1}
is labeled by
x^{-1}
.

•

If a face
f
is labeled by
r\in R
, then its boundary loop is labeled by the word
r
.

Since the relators are cyclically reduced, every boundary loop of is a reduced loop. For every diagram
Y
, we associate a 2-complex morphism
\varphi_{Y}:Y\to\Cay(X,R)
that maps
i
-cells to
i
-cells (for
i=0,1,2
) and preserves the labels on edges and faces. The map
\varphi_{Y}
is unique up to label-preserving isometries on

A pair of faces in a diagram is called
reducible
if they are labeled by the same relator and their boundary loops share a common edge at the same respective position with the same orientation (Figure

if it contains no reducible pair of faces. Equivalently, a diagram is reduced if the map

The

Definition 2.2

(Reduction degree,
[

)

Let

A
van Kampen diagram

D
is a simply connected diagram that is embedded in the Euclidean plane. Up to cyclic permutations and inversion, the boundary loop of

(which may not be reduced) is denoted by

is a van Kampen diagram that is homeomorphic to a disk. In this case, the boundary loop is simple. Here are two useful results on van Kampen diagrams.

Theorem 2.3

(Van Kampen’s lemma
[

]
)
.

Let

such that a chosen boundary loop

is labeled by the word

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Gro87
]
)
.

A finitely presented group
G=\langle X|R\rangle

C>0
such that every reduced van Kampen diagram of

satisfies the isoperimetric inequality

|\partial D|\leq C|D|.

Fix an alphabet

A vertex in a 2-complex is called

Definition 2.6

(Complexity of a 2-complex)
.

Let
Y
be a finite connected 2-complex. Let
K>0
. We say that
Y
has complexity

|Y|\leq K
and if for any face

If

is

. Here is the non-reduced 2-complex version of the isoperimetric inequality.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
Tsa22
]
)
.

Let

\varepsilon>0
and any (large)

, a.a.s. every diagram

|Y^{(1)}|+\Red(Y)\geq(1-d-\varepsilon)|Y|\ell.

\Red(Y)=\sum_{e\in Y^{(1)}}\sum_{r\in R}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\Big(\big|\{f\in Y\,|\,\varphi_{2}(f)=r,e\textup{ is the $j$-th edge of }\partial f\}\big|-1\Big)^{+}.

Let

be an edge of

, and let

Proof of Theorem

.

Combining Lemma

, Lemma

and Inequality (

\Cancel(D_{ij})\leq\frac{d+\varepsilon}{1-2d-\varepsilon}|\partial D_{ij}|\leq\frac{2C+800}{1-2d-\varepsilon}(d+\varepsilon)\ell.

Thus, we get

or

k(k-2)-8\binom{k}{2}\frac{d}{1-2d}\leq 8\binom{k}{2}\frac{d+\varepsilon}{1-2d-\varepsilon}\frac{C+400}{C-8000}+\binom{k}{2}\frac{400}{C-8000}-8\binom{k}{2}\frac{d}{1-2d}.

Remind that for any large

and any small

) in

and

6
Minimal stable length at density d<1/6

Let
G=\langle X|R\rangle
be a hyperbolic group. The
translation length
of an element
u\in G
(acting on
\Cay(X,R)
) is defined by

[u]:=\inf_{x\in G}|ux-x|,

and the
stable length
is

[u]^{\infty}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|u^{n}x-x|}{n}.

According to
[
CDP90
, Ch.10 §6]
, such limit exists and does not depend on the choice of
x
. Recall that
[u]\geq[u]^{\infty}\geq[u]-16\delta
. Recall also that
[u]^{\infty}>0
if and only if
u
is loxodromic. The
minimal stable length
of the group
G=\langle X|R\rangle
is then defined by

\inf\left\{[u]^{\infty}\;\middle|\;u\in G,u\textup{ is loxodromic}\right\}.

We will need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
u\in G
fixes a geodesic
\gamma
and
x\in\gamma
, then
[u]^{\infty}=[u]=|ux-x|
.

Proof.

Assume that there were some
y\not\in\gamma
such that
|uy-y|\leq|ux-x|-1
for some
x\in\gamma
. For any integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

|u^{n}-y|\leq n|uy-y|\leq n|ux-x|-n.

On the other hand, by the triangular inequality,

|u^{y}-y|\geq|u^{n}x-x|-|u^{n}x-u^{n}y|-|x-y|=n|ux-x|-2|x-y|.

So for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
2|x-y|\geq n
, which leads to a contradiction. Hence
[u]=|ux-x|
.

Since
|u^{n}x-x|=n|ux-x|
for any
n
, we have
[u]^{\infty}=[u]
.
∎

and

and

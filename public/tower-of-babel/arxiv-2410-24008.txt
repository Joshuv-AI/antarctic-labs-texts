On the Chern filtration for the moduli of bundles on curves
Woonam Lim, Miguel Moreira, Weite Pi

Abstract
. We introduce and study the Chern filtration on the cohomology of the moduli of bundles on curves. This can be viewed as a natural cohomological invariant defined via tautological classes that interpolates between additive Betti numbers and the multiplicative ring structure. In the rank two case, we fully compute the Chern filtration for moduli of stable bundles and all intermediate stacks in the Harder–Narasimhan stratification. We observe a curious symmetry of the Chern filtration on the moduli of rank two stable bundles, and construct 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} -actions that categorify this symmetry. Our study of the Chern filtration is motivated by the P = C P=C phenomena in several related geometries.

0.1.
Tautological classes and Chern filtrations

Under the assumption

0.2.
Main results and conjectures

We denote the associated graded of the Chern filtration by

{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}H^{*}(N_{r,d})\coloneqq C_{i}H^{*}(N_{r,d})/C_{i-1}H^{*}(N_{r,d})\,

and let

{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}H^{*}(N_{r,d})\,;

note that
{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})
is naturally a bigraded algebra, with a cohomological degree and a Chern degree. Define the
refined Poincaré polynomial

\Omega(N_{r,d},q,t)\coloneqq\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\dim\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{i}H^{i+j}(N_{r,d})q^{i}t^{j},

as well as the shifted version (by half of top Chern and cohomological degrees)

\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q,t)\coloneqq q^{-\frac{1}{2}(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)}t^{-\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)(g-1)}\cdot\Omega(N_{r,d},q,t).

A motivating question for us to study the Chern filtration is whether it has the “curious” symmetry

(2)

\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q,t)=\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q^{-1},t^{-1})\,,

which is originally inspired by the
P=C
phenomenon; see Section

. The main result of this paper consists of a series of structural theorems on the Chern filtration in the rank two case; in particular, we give a positive answer to this question when
r=2

0.3.
Relation to the
P=C
phenomenon

Our study of the Chern filtration for
N_{r,d}
is largely motivated by the
P=C
phenomenon in several related geometries, e.g., the moduli space of Higgs bundles on curves, and the moduli space of 1-dimensional sheaves on del Pezzo surfaces (cf.
[
KPS
,
KLMP
]
). Roughly speaking, the
P=C
phenomenon predicts a match of the Chern filtration on these moduli spaces with the
perverse filtration
induced by certain abelian fibrations. We refer to
[
PSSZ
, Section 1.4]
for a recent account on the history and development of this phenomenon. The
P=C
match for moduli of Higgs bundles is a key step in recent proofs of the
P=W
conjecture
[
dCHM
,
MS2
,
HMMS
,
MSY
]
.

While the perverse filtration relies on the structure of a proper fibration, the Chern filtration is a more general structure that can be defined on many moduli spaces of sheaf-like objects. We believe that the Chern filtration is interesting to study on its own as a cohomological invariant. For example, one consequence of the
P=C
match is the
curious Hard Lefschetz symmetry
on the Chern filtration inherited from the relative Hard Lefschetz symmetry on the perverse filtration; see
[
Mel
]
and
[
KLMP
, Remark 6.7 (iii)]
. It is natural to ask if a similar symmetry holds for the Chern filtration where a nontrivial perverse filtration is not present, cf. Question
0.8
. In this paper, we present positive evidence to this question for
N_{r,d}
in low ranks.

Finally, we note that the perverse counterpart of our new cohomological invariants have been systematically studied in the literature: the associated graded
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}^{P}H^{*}(-)
of the perverse filtration on moduli of 1-dimensional sheaves are expected to be certain
spaces of BPS states
in the Gopakumar–Vafa theory for (local) Calabi–Yau threefolds
[
GV
,
HST
,
KL
,
MT
]
, and the refinement of Betti numbers by the perverse filtration gives
refined BPS invariants
for such Calabi–Yau threefolds; see
[
KLMP
, Section 6.2]
for a detailed discussion and connections to other enumerative theories.

\gcd(r,d)=1
. We have an isomorphism of stacks
{\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}}\simeq M_{r,d}\times B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, hence

H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}})\simeq H^{\ast}(M_{r,d})\otimes H^{\ast}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\simeq H^{\ast}(M_{r,d})[\beta_{1}]\,.

(iv)

Assume
\gcd(r,d)=1
. The good moduli map
{\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}}\to N_{r,d}
induces a ring isomorphism

H^{\ast}(N_{r,d})\simeq H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}})\,.

(v)

Assume
\gcd(r,d)=1
. The tensor product map
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{d}\times N_{r,d}\to M_{r,d}
induces a ring isomorphism

H^{\ast}(M_{r,d})\simeq H^{\ast}(N_{r,d})\otimes H^{\ast}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})\simeq H^{\ast}(N_{r,d})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi_{1,1},\ldots,\psi_{1,2g})

The parts (iii), (iv) and (v) are standard facts. Although we believe that parts (i) and (ii) are also well known to experts, we could not find the exact statements in the literature. We defer their proof to Appendix
A
.

Thanks to Proposition
1.3
(iv), it makes sense to define a realization map to
N_{r,d}
as

{\mathbb{D}}\to H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}})\simeq H^{\ast}(N_{r,d})\,.

Remark 1.4
.

In the literature, it is frequent to define tautological generators for
N_{2,1}
using the endomorphism bundle. Indeed, the endomorphism bundle
\operatorname{End}{\mathcal{U}}
on
{\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\textup{ss}}\times\Sigma
descends uniquely to
N_{2,1}\times\Sigma
and it is traditional (cf.
[
New2
, Remark 2]
) to define classes
\alpha,\beta,\psi_{i}\in H^{\ast}(N_{2,1})
via

c_{2}(\operatorname{End}{\mathcal{U}})=2\alpha\otimes{\mathsf{pt}}+4\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\otimes e_{i}-\beta\otimes{\bf 1}_{\Sigma}\in H^{\ast}(N_{2,1}\times\Sigma)\,.

These classes compare to the ones previously defined in the following way:

\alpha=2\alpha_{2},\quad\psi_{i}=\psi_{2,i},\quad\beta=-4\beta_{2}.

1.3.
Chern filtration and refined Poincaré series

Given any of the possible moduli spaces (with coprime
r,d
) or moduli stacks

X={\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d},\,{\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},\>{\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}},\>{\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}},\>{\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu},\>{\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu},\>M_{r,d},\>N_{r,d}

previously introduced, there is a Chern filtration
C_{\bullet}H^{\ast}(X)
on its cohomology parallel to Definition
0.1
. We define the refined Poincaré series
\Omega(X,q,t)
for any of these spaces and stacks as in Section
0.2
:

\Omega(X,q,t)\coloneqq\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\dim{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}H^{i+j}(X)q^{i}t^{j}\,.

Note that stacks typically have infinitely many non-zero cohomology groups, so
\Omega(X,q,t)
is a formal power series in the variables
q,t
. The following proposition compares the Chern filtrations on different stacks; while the statement is very natural, its proof is slightly technical and we spell it out for completeness.

Proposition 1.5
.

Under the isomorphism in Proposition
1.3
(ii), we have

C_{\bullet}H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})\simeq C_{\bullet}H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})\otimes C_{\bullet}H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{P}\textup{ic}}^{0}),

meaning that the filtration on the left-hand side matches the tensor product filtration on the right-hand side.

Proof.

Under the decomposition
{\mathfrak{P}\textup{ic}}^{0}\simeq B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0}
, it is clear that we have

C_{\bullet}H^{*}({\mathfrak{P}\textup{ic}}^{0})\simeq C_{\bullet}H^{*}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})\otimes C_{\bullet}H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})

where
C_{\bullet}H^{*}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})
and
C_{\bullet}H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
are defined by the normalized Poincaré line bundle
{\mathbb{L}}
and the universal line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
, respectively. In fact, the Chern filtrations on
H^{k}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})
and
H^{2k}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
jump only at the
k
’th step.

Since the Chern filtrations on the both sides of the isomorphism

\Phi:H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{*}({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})\otimes H^{*}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})\otimes H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})

are multiplicative, it suffices to consider the generators. We note that, by Newton’s identity, the Chern filtration can equivalently be defined as the smallest multiplicative filtration such that

(5)

p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}{\mathcal{V}}\cdot q^{*}\gamma)\in C_{k}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu}),\quad k\geq 0,\ \gamma\in H^{*}(\Sigma)\,.

The isomorphism
\Phi
sends (
5
) to

\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=k}\sum_{i\in I}\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{1}}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma^{L}_{i})\Big)\otimes\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{2}}({\mathbb{L}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma^{R}_{i})\Big)\otimes{\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{3}}({\mathcal{L}})

where
\Delta_{*}\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma^{L}_{i}\otimes\gamma^{R}_{i}
, which clearly lies in the
k
’th step of the tensor product filtration.

Now we prove the other inclusion. Note that under the isomorphism
\Phi
, we have

\Phi\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{1}({\mathcal{V}})\cdot q^{*}{\mathsf{pt}})\Big)=r\cdot 1\otimes 1\otimes z,\quad\Phi\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{1}({\mathcal{V}})\cdot q^{*}e_{i})\Big)=p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{1}({\mathbb{L}})\cdot q^{*}e_{i}),

where
z\coloneqq c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
and we used
{\mathrm{ch}}_{1}({\mathcal{U}})=d(1\otimes{\mathsf{pt}})
. This proves that the generators of
H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
and
H^{*}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})
lie in the correct step of the Chern filtration on the left hand side. So we are left to show that

\Phi^{-1}\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}({\mathcal{U}})\otimes q^{*}\gamma)\Big)\in C_{k}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}^{\leq\mu}_{r,d}),\quad k\geq 1,\ \gamma\in H^{*}(\Sigma).

We prove this by induction on
(2-\deg(\gamma),k)\in\{0,1,2\}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
with respect to the lexicographical ordering. The base case holds trivially. Suppose that the statement holds for any
(2-\deg(\gamma^{\prime}),k^{\prime})<_{\textnormal{lex}}(2-\deg(\gamma),k)
. Note that

\Delta_{*}\gamma=\gamma\otimes{\mathsf{pt}}+\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}\gamma^{L}_{i}\otimes\gamma^{R}_{i}

where
\deg(\gamma_{i}^{L})>\deg(\gamma)
. Therefore, the image of
p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}({\mathcal{V}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma)\in C_{k}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})
via
\Phi
is

(6)

p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma)+\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=k,\>k_{1}<k}\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{1}}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma)\Big)\otimes\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{2}}({\mathbb{L}})\cdot q^{*}{\mathsf{pt}})\Big)\otimes{\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{3}}({\mathcal{L}})\\
+\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=k}\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{1}}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma^{L}_{i})\Big)\otimes\Big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{2}}({\mathbb{L}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma^{R}_{i})\Big)\otimes{\mathrm{ch}}_{k_{3}}({\mathcal{L}}).

By the induction hypothesis and the previous discussion about
H^{*}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
and
H^{*}(\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits^{0})
, every term in (
6
) except for the main term
p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma)
is mapped to
C_{k}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})
via
\Phi^{-1}
. This proves that
\Phi^{-1}\big(p_{*}({\mathrm{ch}}_{k}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot q^{*}\gamma)\big)\in C_{k}H^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\leq\mu})
, hence completing the proof.
∎

As a consequence of Propositions
1.3
and
1.5
, we have the equalities

(7)

\displaystyle\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=2}^{r}\frac{(1+q^{k}t^{k-1})^{2g}}{(1-q^{k}t^{k-2})(1-q^{k}t^{k})},

(8)

\displaystyle\Omega({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d},q,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+q)^{2g}}{1-qt}\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,t),

(9)

\displaystyle\Omega({\mathfrak{M}}_{r,d}^{\textup{ss}},q,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-qt}\Omega(M_{r,d},q,t)=\frac{(1+q)^{2g}}{1-qt}\Omega(N_{r,d},q,t)\,.

Observe that
\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,t)
is a rational function with the symmetry

q^{(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)}t^{r(r-1)(g-1)}\cdot\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q^{-1},t^{-1})=\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,t)\,.

This is precisely the same symmetry that we speculate (cf. Question
0.8
) for the refined Poincaré polynomial
\Omega(N_{r,1},q,t)
of the stable loci with
d=1
.

Moreover, the
t=-1
specialization of the rational function
\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,t)
is given by

\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,-1)=\prod_{k=2}^{r}(1-(-q)^{k})^{2g-2}\,.

We conjecture (cf. Conjecture
0.9
) that this agrees with
\Omega(N_{r,d},q,-1)
, the
t=-1
specialization for the semistable loci. Indeed, the
r=2
case and numerical evidence in higher ranks suggest that the difference

\Omega({\mathfrak{N}}_{r,d},q,t)-\Omega(N_{r,d},q,t)

is always divisible by
(1+t)^{2g}
. In Section
3
we will study this difference in the
r=2
case by considering the stratification
\{{\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\leq\mu}\}
. The factor of
(1+t)^{2g}
will appear in the differences between refined Poincaré series of consecutive stacks thanks to the (quite intricate) modified Mumford relations, cf. the equalities following (
17
) and its proof.

1.4.
Proof of Theorem
0.2

Our first general result towards the understanding of the Chern filtration on moduli spaces of bundles is Theorem
0.2
. The theorem is equivalent to the following two facts:

(i)

For
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
with
\deg^{C}(D)<(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)
, we have
\int_{N_{r,d}}D=0
.

(ii)

There is
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
with
\deg^{C}(D)=(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)
such that
\int_{N_{r,d}}D\neq 0
.

Both (i) and (ii) are essentially shown in the proofs of Propositions 8 and 9 in
[
EK1
]
; for completeness, we briefly explain the argument here.

Let

D=\prod_{k=2}^{r}\left(\alpha_{k}^{n_{k}}\beta_{k}^{m_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{2g}\psi_{k,j}^{p_{k,j}}\right)\in{\mathbb{D}}\,,

where
n_{k},m_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
p_{k,j}\in\{0,1\}
be a class in top cohomological degree, i.e.,
\deg(D)=2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)
. We observe that

(10)

\displaystyle 2\deg^{C}(D)

\displaystyle=\deg(D)+2\sum_{k=2}^{r}n_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{2g}p_{k,j}

\displaystyle=2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)+2\sum_{k=2}^{r}n_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{2g}p_{k,j}\,.

The Jeffrey–Kirwan formula
[
JK
]
expresses the integral

G(\epsilon)\coloneqq\int_{N_{r,d}}\exp\left(\sum_{k=2}^{r}\epsilon\delta_{k}\alpha_{k}\right)\prod_{k=2}^{r}\left(\beta_{k}^{m_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{2g}\psi_{k,j}^{p_{k,j}}\right)

as a complicated iterated residue; here,
\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{k}
are formal variables and we regard
G(\epsilon)
as a polynomial in
\epsilon
with coefficients being polynomials in
\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{k}
. Using this formula, it is shown in the proof of
[
EK1
, Proposition 8]
that
G(\epsilon)
is a polynomial divisible by
\epsilon^{M}
where

M=\frac{1}{2}\left(2(r-1)(g-1)-\sum_{k=2}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{2g}p_{k,j}\right)\,.

If
\int_{N_{r,d}}D\neq 0
, we would get a contribution to
\epsilon^{n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}}
, hence

\sum_{k=2}^{r}n_{k}\geq M\,.

Using (
10
), this inequality is equivalent to
\deg^{C}(D)\geq(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)
, showing (i).

For (ii), Earl and Kirwan show
[
EK1
, Proposition 9]
that
\int_{N_{r,d}}D\neq 0
for

D=\eta\cdot\alpha_{2}^{(r-1)(g-1)}\,,

where
\eta
is a polynomial in the classes
\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{r}
with cohomological degree
2r(r-1)(g-1)
. Explicitly,
\eta
is given by writing the symmetric polynomial in formal variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}

\eta=\prod_{i<j}(X_{i}-X_{j})^{2(g-1)}

in terms of elementary symmetric polynomials, and setting the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial to
\beta_{k}
(where
\beta_{1}
is interpreted to be 0). Since the Chern degree of a polynomial in
\beta
classes is half of its cohomological degree, we have

∎

\deg^{C}(D)=r(r-1)(g-1)+2(r-1)(g-1)=(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)\,.

The following corollary is a generalization of the known fact that the subring of
H^{\ast}(N_{r,d})
generated by
\beta
classes vanishes beyond cohomological degree
2r(r-1)(g-1)
, cf.
[
EK1
, Theorem 7]
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
r(r-1)(g-1)\leq m\leq 2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)
. Then
C_{k}H^{m}(N_{r,d})=0
for

k<m-r(r-1)(g-1)\,.

Proof.

Suppose that
D\in C_{k}H^{m}(N_{r,d})
is non-zero. By Poincaré duality, there is

E\in H^{2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)-m}(N_{r,d})

such that
\int_{N_{r,d}}D\cdot E\neq 0
. Recall that the inequality (
4
) states that the Chern degree is bounded above by the cohomological degree, so

E\in C_{2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)-m}H^{2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)-m}(N_{r,d})\,

and hence

D\cdot E\in C_{2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)-m+k}H^{2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)}(N_{r,d})\,.

By Theorem
0.2
it follows that

2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)-m+k\geq(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)

which is equivalent to

k\geq m-r(r-1)(g-1)\,.\qed

Theorem
0.2
together with (
9
) gives a similar result for the moduli space
M_{r,d}
of bundles without fixed determinant:

Corollary 1.7
.

The top Chern degree of
M_{r,d}
is
r(r+1)(g-1)+2
.

The proof of Theorem
0.2
also shows the following:

Proposition 1.8
.

If
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
is such that
\deg^{C}(D)=(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)
, then
\int_{N_{r,d}}D
does not depend on the degree
d
.

Proof.

Recall from the proof of Theorem
0.2
that the integrals of
D
with top Chern degree are encoded in the
\epsilon^{M}
coefficient of
G(\epsilon)
. The only part dependent on
d
in the Jeffrey–Kirwan
[
JK
]
formula for
G(\epsilon)
is
\tilde{c}
, which appears only in the term

\exp\!\big(\epsilon\mathrm{d}q_{X}(w\tilde{c})\big)\,.

But this term does not contribute to the coefficient of
\epsilon^{M}
, so
\int_{N_{r,d}}D
is independent of
d
for
D
of top Chern degree.
∎

1.5.
The associated graded and its (non-degenerate) pairing

We explain in this subsection the relation between the numerical symmetry of a filtration and the perfectness of certain pairings on its associated graded; this is discussed briefly after Question
0.8
. We also explain the obstruction for the symmetry (
2
) to hold for
all
degrees
d
coprime to a given rank.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space with an increasing filtration
C_{\bullet}V
and a perfect pairing
\langle-,-\rangle:V\otimes V\to\mathbb{Q}
. Assume that there exists
N>0
such that

(i)

The pairing
C_{i}V\otimes C_{j}V\to\mathbb{Q}
is zero for
i+j<N
.

(ii)

The numerical symmetry
\dim\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{i}V=\dim\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{N-i}V
holds.

Then there exists a well-defined perfect pairing

(11)

\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}:\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{\bullet}V\otimes\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{N-\bullet}V\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}.

Proof.

By (i), it is clear that the pairing on
V
descends to a well-defined pairing (
11
). We define subspaces

\widetilde{C}_{i}V\coloneqq\{w\in V\mid\langle v,w\rangle=0,~\textrm{for all }v\in C_{N-1-i}V\}.

By (i), we have
C_{i}V\subset\widetilde{C}_{i}V
. By (ii) and the perfectness of the pairing on
V
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\widetilde{C}_{i}V

\displaystyle=\dim V-\dim C_{N-1-i}V=\sum_{j\leq i}\dim\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{N-j}V=\sum_{j\leq i}\dim\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{j}V=\dim C_{i}V.

It follows that
C_{i}V=\widetilde{C}_{i}V
. If
\overline{w}\in\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{N-i}V
is such that
\langle\overline{v},\overline{w}\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=0
for all
\overline{v}\in\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{i}V
, then by definition

w\in\widetilde{C}_{N-1-i}V=C_{N-1-i}V,

and thus
\overline{w}=0
. This shows the perfectness of (
11
).
∎

For our purpose, take
C_{\bullet}V
to be
H^{*}(N_{r,d})
equipped with the Chern filtration (Definition
0.1
),
\langle-,-\rangle
to be the Poincaré pairing, and
N=(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)
to be the top Chern degree (Theorem
0.2
). The following corollary is then immediate from Lemma
1.9
and the fact that
\langle v,w\rangle\neq 0
only if
v
and
w
have complementary cohomological degrees.

Corollary 1.10
.

Assume that the numerical Chern symmetry holds:

\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q,1)=\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q^{-1},1).

Then the refined symmetry
\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q,t)=\overline{\Omega}(N_{r,d},q^{-1},t^{-1})
also holds, and the pairing

(12)

\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}:\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})\otimes\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{N-\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})\to\mathbb{Q}

is perfect.

When the symmetry does hold (for example for
N_{2,1}
), the perfectness of the pairing (
12
) implies that the associated graded ring
\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})
is determined by integrals
only in the top Chern degree
, instead of all integrals, which are necessary to determine the full cohomology ring
H^{*}(N_{r,d})
. To state this precisely, we introduce the “graded integral” notation

\int_{N_{r,d}}^{{\mathrm{gr}}}D

which is defined to be
\int_{N_{r,d}}D
if
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
has the top Chern degree, and zero otherwise. We equip the descendent algebra with a symmetric pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}:{\mathbb{D}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
given by

\langle D,D^{\prime}\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}\coloneqq\int_{N_{r,d}}^{{\mathrm{gr}}}D\cdot D^{\prime}\,.

Then the non-degeneracy of the pairing on
\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})
means that the ideal

I^{\mathrm{gr}}=\ker\big({\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{\ast}(N_{r,d})\big)

is precisely the kernel of the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}:{\mathbb{D}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
.

In general, we expect the integrals in the top Chern degree to be “better behaved” than all the integrals. The
r=2
case illustrates this phenomenon well. A formula for integrals of general descendents can be found in
[
Tha1
, (30)]
, whose most complicated aspect is the appearance of the Bernoulli numbers
B_{q}
. However, considering only integrals in the top Chern degree is the same as setting
q=0
, which eliminates this complexity. Indeed, the integrals with top Chern degree in rank two are completely determined by the “Virasoro proportionality” (
28
) and monodromy invariance. Another evidence for this philosophy can be seen in Proposition
1.8
, which states that integrals in the top Chern degree, unlike general integrals, do not depend on the degree
d
.

Remark 1.11
.

The above discussions implies that the Chern symmetry (
2
) cannot hold for every degree
d
: if the symmetry holds for
N_{r,d}
, then
\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N_{r,d})
, and in particular the Betti numbers of
N_{r,d}
, are determined by the graded integral functional
\int_{N_{r,d}}^{{\mathrm{gr}}}
. But this functional does not depend on
d
, by Proposition
1.8
, so the symmetry holding for every
d
would imply that the Betti numbers of
N_{r,d}
were the same for every
d
, which is not true (for example
N_{5,1}
and
N_{5,2}
have different Betti numbers). Numerical evidence suggests that the Betti numbers of
N_{r,\pm 1}
are smaller than the Betti numbers of
N_{r,d}
with
d\not\equiv\pm 1\mod r
, which provides some support to Question
0.8
. This situation contrasts with the case of moduli spaces
M_{\beta,\chi}^{H}(S)
of 1-dimensional sheaves on del Pezzo surfaces
S
, where we do expect the analogous symmetry for every
\chi
(indeed, it is a consequence of the
P=C
conjecture, as discussed in
[
KLMP
]
) and the Betti numbers are known to be independent of
\chi
by
[
MS1
]
; we expect that a result analogous to Proposition
1.8
holds in that setting and hope to address this in the future.

2.
The refined Poincaré polynomial of
N_{2,1}

In this section we will prove the following formula for the refined Poincaré polynomial of the moduli space of rank 2 vector bundles.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem
0.3
)
.

The refined Poincaré polynomial for
N_{2,1}
is

\Omega(N_{2,1},q,t)=\frac{(1+q^{2}t)^{2g}-q^{2g}(1+t)^{2g}}{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{2}t^{2})}.

In particular, the symmetry (
2
) holds when
r=2
.

Note that setting
q=t
recovers the known Poincaré polynomial for
N_{2,1}

[
New1
,
HN
,
AB
]
.

This formula will be obtained by using the explicit basis found by Zagier in
[
Zag1
]
. Before we recall the construction and properties of this basis, we prove the following general lemma that explains how the refined Poincaré polynomial of
N_{r,d}
can be calculated provided we have a basis with certain desirable properties.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N=N_{r,d}
with
\gcd(r,d)=1
. Suppose that there exists a basis
{\mathcal{B}}
of
H^{\ast}(N)
that has the following properties:

(i)

The intersection pairing matrix is “anti-diagonal”, i.e., there is an involution
v\mapsto\check{v}
of
\mathcal{B}
such that
\int_{N}v\cdot u=0
for
u\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{\check{v}\}
and
\int_{N}v\cdot\check{v}\neq 0
.

(ii)

There are non-negative integers
\ell_{v},k_{v}
for each
v\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
v\in C_{\ell_{v}}H^{k_{v}}(N)
. In particular,

k_{v}+k_{\check{v}}=2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)\,.

(iii)

We have

\ell_{v}+\ell_{\check{v}}=(r+2)(r-1)(g-1)\,.

Then

\dim\mathrm{gr}^{C}_{\ell}H^{k}(N)=\#\{v\in{\mathcal{B}}\colon k_{v}=k\,,\,\ell_{v}=\ell\}\,.

In particular, the symmetry (
2
) holds.

Proof.

We begin by proving that
C_{\ell}H^{\ast}(N)
is spanned by
v\in{\mathcal{B}}
with
\ell_{v}\leq\ell
. Let
w\in C_{\ell}H^{\ast}(N)
and consider its decomposition into the basis
{\mathcal{B}}
,

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples gives rise to the
\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
symmetry

\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q,t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q^{-1},t^{-1}),

\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q,t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q,q^{-2}t^{-1}),

\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q,t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q^{-1},q^{2}t)\,.

The diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple is related to the Chern grading and furthermore implies (see Corollary
4.12
) that the Chern grading specialization
\overline{\Omega}(N_{2,1},q,1)=\sum_{i=-n}^{n}a_{i}\,q^{i}
is
unimodal
, in the sense that

a_{-n}\leq\dots\leq a_{0}\geq\dots\geq a_{n}.

Recall that the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
is freely generated by the symbols
\alpha,\beta,\psi_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 2g
introduced in Remark
1.4
. This algebra is double-graded by the cohomological degree

\deg(\alpha)=2,\quad\deg(\beta)=4,\quad\deg(\psi_{i})=3

and the Chern degree

\deg^{C}(\alpha)=\deg^{C}(\beta)=\deg^{C}(\psi_{i})=2.

The cohomological degree induces a super-grading
|\cdot|
valued in
\{0,1\}
. All algebraic concepts such as vector spaces, operators, commutators, and (anti-)self-adjointness will be super-graded.

We make some comments on the natural
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
-equivariant structures which will be used in this section. The group
\Gamma\coloneqq{\mathrm{Sp}}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
acts on the cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Sigma,{\mathbb{Z}})
via either the mapping class group modulo the Torelli group of the underlying manifold of
\Sigma
, or the monodromy group of the moduli stack of smooth algebraic curves. Explicitly,
\Gamma
is the automorphism group of the symplectic lattice
H^{1}(\Sigma,{\mathbb{Z}})
and acts trivially on the even part of
H^{*}(\Sigma,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

The group
\Gamma
also acts on the cohomology rings of the moduli spaces and stacks considered in this paper via monodromy. Since the restriction homomorphisms between such cohomology rings are
\Gamma
-equivariant, it suffices to consider the
\Gamma
-action on the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}\simeq H^{*}({\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1})
which we describe now. If
\sigma\in\Gamma
is represented by a matrix
A
satisfying
{}^{\mathsf{T}}\kern-2.0ptA\cdot J\cdot A=J
, where
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}
is the standard symplectic matrix, then

\sigma\cdot\alpha=\alpha,\quad\sigma\cdot\beta=\beta,\quad\sigma\cdot\psi_{i}=\hat{A}\cdot\psi_{i}=\sum_{j}\hat{A}_{ji}\,\psi_{j}

where
\hat{A}\coloneqq{}^{\mathsf{T}}\kern-2.0ptA^{-1}
. The
\Gamma
-invariant subring of the descendent algebra is

{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}={\mathbb{Q}}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]/(\gamma^{g+1})\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}

where
\gamma=-2\sum_{i=1}^{g}\psi_{i}\psi_{i+g}
.

4.1.
Commuting
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples on the descendent algebra

Using the free algebra structure of the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
, we define the differential operators acting on
{\mathbb{D}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-(g-1),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}.

Similarly, we define the other set of operators exchanging the role of
\alpha
and
\beta
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\beta,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-(g-1),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\beta\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\alpha}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}.

Recall that a triple of operators
({\mathfrak{e}},{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{f}})
acting on a vector space forms an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple if

[{\mathfrak{e}},{\mathfrak{f}}]={\mathfrak{h}},\quad[{\mathfrak{e}},{\mathfrak{h}}]=2{\mathfrak{e}},\quad[{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{h}}]=-2{\mathfrak{f}}.

We say that a pair of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
({\mathfrak{e}},{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{f}})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{f}}^{\prime})
commutes if any operator in
({\mathfrak{e}},{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{f}})
commutes with any operator in
({\mathfrak{e}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{f}}^{\prime})
, hence defining a representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The operators
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
form a commuting pair of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples, hence defining a representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

We first show that
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
form an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple. Note that
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
is a grading operator with respect to which the multiplication operators by
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\psi_{i}
are of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
-degree
2
,
0
, and
1
, respectively. From this observation it follows that
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}
and
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
have
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
-degree
2
and
-2
, respectively, hence
[{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}]=2{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}
and
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}]=-2{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
. On the other hand, direct computation using the Leibniz rule of the commutator gives

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\left[-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}},\alpha\right]

\displaystyle=-2\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+(g-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}

which is equal to
-{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
. The same argument shows that
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
form an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple.

We now show that the pair
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
commutes. All the commutators vanish for rather trivial reasons except for
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}]=0
which we explain below. By independence of the variables
\alpha,\beta,\psi_{i}
’s, many terms of
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}]
vanish, leaving us with

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}]=

\displaystyle\left[-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha},-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}-\left[-\beta\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta},-\frac{\beta}{4}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}},-\frac{\alpha}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\right]+\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}},-\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}},\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\right].

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}
is the grading operator assigning degree
1
to each
\psi_{i}
, the second term cancels the first term.
∎

One can check that all the operators in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples are
\Gamma
-equivariant, and in particular they preserve the
\Gamma
-invariant subring. For later use, we record how the operators simplify when restricted to
{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

On the
\Gamma
-invariant subring
{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}={\mathbb{Q}}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]/(\gamma^{g+1})
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}=2\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}=-2\gamma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma^{2}}+2g\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}.

Proof.

The differential operators on the right hand sides of both equalities are well-defined on
{\mathbb{Q}}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]/(\gamma^{g+1})
because they preserve the ideal
(\gamma^{g+1})
. Since all the operators in the statement annihilate the unit
1
and commute with
\alpha
and
\beta
, it suffices to match the commutator of both sides with the multiplication operator
\gamma
.

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}
is the grading operator assigning degree
1
to each
\psi_{i}
and
\gamma
is quadratic in the classes
\psi_{i}
, we have

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}},\gamma\right]=2\gamma=\left[2\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma},\gamma\right],

proving the first equality. The second equality also follows from direct computation.
∎

By the above lemma, the operators
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
become

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\alpha,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+2\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}-(g-1),

(25)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-2\gamma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha\partial\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}\beta\gamma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma^{2}}-\frac{g}{2}\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}

when we restrict to
{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}
. Similar formulas hold for
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
.

4.2.
(Anti-)self-adjoint property

Let
V
be a super-graded vector space. We say that an element
D\in V
or an operator
F:V\rightarrow V
is pure (with respect to the super-grading) if it is either even or odd.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
V
be a super-graded vector space with a pairing
\langle-,-\rangle:V\otimes V\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
. We say that a pure operator
F:V\rightarrow V
is
(\pm)
-self-adjoint with respect to the given pairing if

\langle F(D),D^{\prime}\rangle=\pm(-1)^{|F||D|}\langle D,F(D^{\prime})\rangle

for all pure elements
D,D^{\prime}\in V
. If the sign is
+
(resp.
-
), we simply say that the operator is self-adjoint (resp. anti-self-adjoint).

The main theorem of this section is the
(\pm)
-self-adjoint property of the operators
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
. We recall from Section
1.5
the definition of the symmetric pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}:{\mathbb{D}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
on the descendent algebra given by

\langle D,D^{\prime}\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=\int_{N}^{{\mathrm{gr}}}D\cdot D^{\prime}

where
\displaystyle\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}
denotes the integration over
N
of only the top Chern degree part of
D\cdot D^{\prime}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The operators
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta}
) are self-adjoint (resp. anti-self-adjoint) with respect to the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Combining the above
(\pm)
-self-adjoint property of the operators with the perfectness of the pairing on
{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
in Corollary
1.10
, we obtain an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representation on
{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
.

Corollary 4.5
.

The
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representation on
{\mathbb{D}}
descends via the surjection
{\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be one of the operators in the commuting
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples. The statement of the corollary amounts to showing that the operator
F
preserves the kernel of the surjective homomorphism
{\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
. By Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
1.10
, the kernel
I^{\mathrm{gr}}
of this surjection is equal to the kernel of the symmetric pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Thus it suffices to show that
\langle D,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=0
implies
\langle F(D),-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=0
, which follows from the
(\pm)
-self-adjoint property of
F
. ∎

We now explain how to reduce the proof of Theorem
4.4
to showing that
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
is self-adjoint.

Lemma 4.6
.

The pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
is preserved under the algebra automorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
swapping
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Proof.

Recall that the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
uses the top Chern degree integral over
N
. Note that a monomial in the descendent algebra

D=\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}^{p_{i}}\in{\mathbb{D}}

has the top cohomological degree and top Chern degree only if

2n+4m+3\sum_{i=1}^{2g}p_{i}=6g-6,\quad 2n+2m+2\sum_{i=1}^{2g}p_{i}=4g-4.

The two equalities in particular imply that
n=m
, hence proving the lemma.
∎

Since
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
are related by swapping the role of
\alpha
and
\beta
, the above lemma shows that it suffices to consider
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
. On the other hand, a multiplication operator
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}
is clearly self-adjoint. The grading operator
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
is anti-self-adjoint due to degree reason as we explain below.

Proposition 4.7
.

The operator
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
is anti-self-adjoint with respect to
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
.

Proof.

Given any two monomials

D=\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}^{p_{i}},\quad D^{\prime}=\alpha^{n^{\prime}}\beta^{m^{\prime}}\prod_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}^{p^{\prime}_{i}},

we need to show that
\langle{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}(D),D^{\prime}\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=-\langle D,{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}(D^{\prime})\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
. Equivalently, we need to show

(26)

\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}(D)\cdot D^{\prime}+D\cdot{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}(D^{\prime})=0.

Since

{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}=2\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-(g-1)

is the operator preserving the cohomological degree and Chern degree, the integral on the left hand side of (
26
) is trivially zero unless

\deg(D)+\deg(D^{\prime})=6g-6,\quad\deg^{C}(D)+\deg^{C}(D^{\prime})=4g-4.

The above two equalities amount to
n+n^{\prime}=m+m^{\prime}
and
2(n+n^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}(p_{i}+p^{\prime}_{i})=2g-2
, which we assume from now. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
is a grading operator with all monomials being eigenvectors, the equality (
26
) follows from

{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}(D)\cdot D^{\prime}+D\cdot{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}(D^{\prime})=\left(2(n+n^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}(p_{i}+p^{\prime}_{i})-(2g-2)\right)D\cdot D^{\prime}=0.\qed

4.3.
Self-adjointness of
\mathfrak{f}_{\alpha}

We now focus on proving that
\mathfrak{f}_{\alpha}
is self-adjoint, which is the heart of Theorem
4.4
. The following lemma will be repeatedly used.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
V
be a super-graded vector space with a pairing
\langle-,-\rangle
. Suppose that
V
is spanned by an even vector
\mathbf{1}\in V
acted upon by a collection of self-adjoint operators
\{G_{s}\}_{s\in S}
. Then an operator
F:V\rightarrow V
is
(\pm)
-self-adjoint if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\langle F(\mathbf{1}),D\rangle=\pm\langle\mathbf{1},F(D)\rangle
for all
D\in V
and

(ii)

[F,G_{s}]
is
(\mp)
-self-adjoint for all
s\in S
.

Proof.

The “only if” direction is straightforward to check so we only prove the “if” direction. Assume that a pure operator
F
satisfies the conditions in (i) and (ii). For each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, define a subspace

V_{n}\coloneqq\text{span}\{G_{s_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ G_{s_{n}}(\mathbf{1})\,|\,s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in S\}\subseteq V.

By assumption, the filtration
V_{0}\subseteq V_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V
saturates
V
in the sense that any
D\in V
lies in
V_{n}
for some
n\geq 0
. Therefore it suffices to show that for every
n\geq 0
we have

(27)

\langle F(D),D^{\prime}\rangle=\pm(-1)^{|F||D|}\langle D,F(D^{\prime})\rangle\quad\text{for all}\quad D\in V_{n},\ D^{\prime}\in V.

We prove this by induction on
n\geq 0
. Since
V_{0}=\text{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}
, the
n=0
case is precisely the statement of (i). Now let
n>0
and assume (
27
) for the
n-1
case. Given
D=G_{s_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ G_{s_{n}}(\mathbf{1})\in V_{n}
and
D^{\prime}\in V
, we can write
D=G_{s_{1}}(D^{\prime\prime})
where
D^{\prime\prime}=G_{s_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ G_{s_{n}}(\mathbf{1})\in V_{n-1}
. Using the self-adjoint property of
G_{s_{1}}
and
(\mp)
-self-adjoint property of
[F,G_{s_{1}}]
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F(D),D^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F\circ G_{s_{1}}(D^{\prime\prime}),D^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[F,G_{s_{1}}](D^{\prime\prime}),D^{\prime}\rangle+(-1)^{|F||G_{s_{1}}|}\langle G_{s_{1}}\circ F(D^{\prime\prime}),D^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mp(-1)^{(|F|+|G_{s_{1}}|)|D^{\prime\prime}|}\langle D^{\prime\prime},[F,G_{s_{1}}]D^{\prime}\rangle+(-1)^{|G_{s_{1}}||D^{\prime\prime}|}\langle F(D^{\prime\prime}),G_{s_{1}}(D^{\prime})\rangle.

Since
D^{\prime\prime}\in V_{n-1}
, the induction hypothesis allows us to rewrite the second term to get

\displaystyle\langle F(D),D^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mp(-1)^{(|F|+|G_{s_{1}}|)|D^{\prime\prime}|}\langle D^{\prime\prime},[F,G_{s_{1}}]D^{\prime}\rangle\pm(-1)^{(|F|+|G_{s_{1}}|)|D^{\prime\prime}|}\langle D^{\prime\prime},F\circ G_{s_{1}}(D^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\pm(-1)^{(|F|+|G_{s_{1}}|)|D^{\prime\prime}|}\left(\langle D^{\prime\prime},F\circ G_{s_{1}}(D^{\prime})-[F,G_{s_{1}}]D^{\prime}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\pm(-1)^{(|F|+|G_{s_{1}}|)|D^{\prime\prime}|}(-1)^{|F||G_{s_{1}}|}\langle D^{\prime\prime},G_{s_{1}}\circ F(D^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\pm(-1)^{|F|(|D^{\prime\prime}|+|G_{s_{1}}|)}\langle G_{s_{1}}(D^{\prime\prime}),F(D^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\pm(-1)^{|F||D|}\langle D,F(D^{\prime})\rangle.\qed

We apply the above lemma when the super-graded vector space is
{\mathbb{D}}
equipped with the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
, the even vector is the unit
1
and the collection of self-adjoint operators is the set of multiplication operators
\{\alpha,\beta,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{2g}\}
. According to the lemma, in order to show that
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
is self-adjoint, it suffices to show that

(i)

\langle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(1),D\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=\langle 1,{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(D)\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
for all
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
,

(ii)

[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\alpha]
,
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\beta]
,
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{i}]
for
i=1,\dots,2g
are all anti-self-adjoint.

Since
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(1)=0
, part (i) is equivalent to

\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(D)=0\quad\text{for all \,}D\in{\mathbb{D}}.

On the other hand, since
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\alpha]=-{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}
is anti-self-adjoint by Proposition
4.7
and
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\beta]=0
by Proposition
4.1
, the only thing we are left with in part (ii) is to show that
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{i}]
is anti-self-adjoint for all
1\leq i\leq 2g
. These two statements will be proved in Proposition
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
, respectively.

We briefly explain the main ingredients in the proofs of Proposition
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
. Both propositions can be checked in principle if we understand all integral values of the top Chern degree monomials
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
. It turns out that we only need some proportionalities between such integrals coming from the Virasoro constraints and monodromy invariance with respect to the group
\Gamma={\mathrm{Sp}}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

The Virasoro constraints of the moduli space
N
, as written in
[
BLM
, Example 2.24]
, give

(28)

(g-p)\int_{N}\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}=-2n\int_{N}\alpha^{n-1}\beta^{m-1}\gamma^{p+1}.

Note that this is also an immediate consequence of
[
Tha1
, (30)]
. On the other hand, monodromy invariance of the integrals over
N
implies the following two statements
[
Tha1
, pp. 143–144]
. First, we have

\int_{N}\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}^{p_{i}}=0

unless
p_{i}=p_{i+g}
for
1\leq i\leq g
. Setting
\gamma_{i}\coloneqq\psi_{i}\psi_{i+g}
for
1\leq i\leq g
, the integral value

\int_{N}\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\gamma_{i_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{i_{p}}

depends on
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq g
only through
p
. Since
\gamma=-2\sum_{i=1}^{g}\gamma_{i}
, this implies

(29)

\int_{N}\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}=(-2)^{p}\cdot g(g-1)\cdots(g-p+1)\int_{N}\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\gamma_{i_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{i_{p}}.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
V
be a complex representation of a complex reductive group
G
. Suppose that
V
is saturated by finite dimensional
G
-invariant subspaces. If
\phi:V\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a
G
-invariant linear map such that
\phi\big|_{V^{G}}=0
, then
\phi=0
.

Proof.

Since
V
is saturated by finite dimensional
G
-invariant subspaces, we can reduce the proof to the case where
V
is finite dimensional. Let
K\subset G
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
and
d\mu
be the unique Haar measure on
K
. Define the “averaging map” by

I:V\rightarrow V,\quad x\mapsto\int_{K}k\cdot x\,d\mu.

Then
I
is a complex linear map with the property that
I(V)\subseteq V^{K}=V^{G}
; the fact that
V^{K}=V^{G}
follows from Zariski density of
K\subset G
, which is sometimes known as “Weyl Unitarian Trick” (see
[
Spr
, Section 2.2.2]
). On the other hand, for
x\in V
we have

\phi(I(x))=\int_{K}\phi(k\cdot x)\,d\mu=\int_{K}\phi(x)\,d\mu=\phi(x).

Since
I(x)\in V^{G}
and
\phi\big|_{V^{G}}=0
, we conclude that
\phi(x)=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

We have
\displaystyle\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(D)=0
for all
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

We first prove the statement for
D\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}
. Since
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
is of cohomological degree
-2
and Chern degree
-2
, it suffices to consider monomials
D=\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}
with

\displaystyle\deg(D)

\displaystyle=2n+4m+6p=(6g-6)+2,

\displaystyle\deg^{C}(D)

\displaystyle=2n+2m+4p=(4g-4)+2.

The two equalities are equivalent to
n=m+1
and
m+p=g-1
. By applying the formula (
25
) of
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
to
D=\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(D)=

\displaystyle-(m+1)m\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}+(g-1)(m+1)\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}-2(m+1)p\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}\gamma^{p-1}-\frac{g}{2}p\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}\gamma^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(m+1)(-m+g-1-2p)\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}+\frac{p}{2}(p-1-g)\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}\gamma^{p-1}.

On the other hand, by the proportionality (
28
) coming from the Virasoro constraints, the integral
\int_{N}{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(D)
is zero if

(m+1)(-m+g-1-2p)(g-p+1)+\frac{p}{2}(p-1-g)(-2(m+1))=0.

This equality follows from
m+p=g-1
.

So far, we have shown that the
\Gamma
-invariant map

\phi:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}},\quad D\mapsto\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}(D)

vanishes once we restrict the domain of
\phi
to
{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}
. Note that we can extend scalars to the complex numbers in the following sense. The complex reductive group
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq{\mathrm{Sp}}(2g,{\mathbb{C}})
acts on
{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{D}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
so that
\phi_{\mathbb{C}}:{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant. By Lemma
4.9
, we conclude that
\phi_{\mathbb{C}}=0
from the fact that
\phi_{\mathbb{C}}
restricts to zero on the smaller domain
{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
. The condition on the saturation by finite dimensional invariant subspaces in the lemma is satisfied for example by the Chern filtration.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

For
1\leq i\leq 2g
, the operator
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{i}]
is anti-self-adjoint with respect to
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8
,
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}]
is anti-self-adjoint if and only if

(i)

\langle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](1),D\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}=-\langle 1,[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](D)\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
for all
D\in{\mathbb{D}}
,

(ii)

\big[[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}],\alpha\big]
,
\big[[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}],\beta\big]
,
\big[[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}],\psi_{i}\big]
for
i=1,\dots,2g
are all self-adjoint.

Since
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}
is a quadratic differential operator, it becomes a multiplication operator after
taking commutator twice with multiplication operators. Therefore, part (ii) follows immediately. So we are left with part (i).

Now we let
1\leq j\leq g
and consider part (i) for
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}]
. The case of
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j+g}]
is essentially the same and we leave the details to the reader. We start by computing the commutator

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}]

\displaystyle=\left[-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}},\psi_{j}\right]

(30)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\psi_{j}+\frac{\beta}{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{j+g}}.

Since
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](1)=0
, part (i) is equivalent to

(31)

\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](D)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad D\in{\mathbb{D}}.

In order to show the vanishing (
31
), we follow a similar strategy as in the proof of Proposition
4.10
. One difference is that the top Chern degree integration map

{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}},\quad D\mapsto\int_{N}^{\mathrm{gr}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](D)

is no longer
\Gamma={\mathrm{Sp}}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
-invariant. However, it is invariant with respect to the subgroup
\widetilde{\Gamma}\subset\Gamma
fixing
\psi_{j}
and
\psi_{j+g}
. Abstractly,
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is isomoprhic to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(2g-2,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and the
\widetilde{\Gamma}
-invariant subring is generated by
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\psi_{j},\psi_{j+g}
.

By Lemma
4.9
, it suffices to show the vanishing (
31
) for the monomials

D=\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}\psi_{j}^{p_{j}}\psi_{j+g}^{p_{j+g}}.

Since
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}]
has cohomological degree
1
and Chern degree
0
, we may assume

\displaystyle\deg(D)

\displaystyle=2n+4m+6p+3(p_{j}+p_{j+g})=(6g-6)-1,

\displaystyle\deg^{C}(D)

\displaystyle=2n+2m+4p+2(p_{j}+p_{j+g})=4g-4.

In particular, we have
n=m+1
and
p_{j}+p_{j+g}=1
. By the formula of
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}]
in (
30
),
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](D)=0
if
p_{j}=1
and
p_{j+g}=0
. So we may assume that

D=\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}\psi_{j+g}

with
m+p=g-2
.

Recall the definition of
\gamma=-2\sum_{i=1}^{g}\gamma_{i}
where
\gamma_{i}=\psi_{i}\psi_{i+g}
. By computation, we have

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha},\psi_{j}](D)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\psi_{j}+\frac{\beta}{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{j+g}}\right)\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}\psi_{j+g}

\displaystyle=-(m+1)\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}\gamma_{j}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{j+g}}(\gamma^{p}\psi_{j+g})

\displaystyle=-(m+1)\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}\gamma_{j}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}(-2)^{p}(\gamma_{1}+\cdots\widehat{\gamma_{j}}+\cdots\gamma_{g})^{p}

where we used
\gamma_{j}\cdot\psi_{j+g}=0
in the last step. By the proportionality (
29
) coming from the monodromy invariance, we have

\int_{N}\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p}\gamma_{j}=-\frac{1}{2g}\int_{N}\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p+1}

and

\int_{N}\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}(-2)^{p}(\gamma_{1}+\cdots\widehat{\gamma_{j}}+\cdots\gamma_{g})^{p}=\frac{g-p}{g}\int_{N}\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}\gamma^{p}.

So the vanishing (
31
) reduces to showing

\int_{N}\Big((m+1)\frac{1}{2g}\alpha^{m}\beta^{m}\gamma^{p+1}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{g-p}{g}\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{m+1}\gamma^{p}\Big)=0.

This follows from the proportionality (
28
) coming from the Virasoro constraints because

(m+1)\frac{1}{2g}(g-p)+\frac{1}{4}\frac{g-p}{g}(-2(m+1))=0.\qed

Therefore, we have completed the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

4.4.
Diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple and Chern filtration

In previous sections, we have constructed a commuting pair of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta})
acting on
{\mathbb{D}}
which descends via the surjection
{\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
. On the other hand, there is a diagonal Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
corresponding to

{\mathfrak{e}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}+{\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta},\quad{\mathfrak{h}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta},\quad{\mathfrak{f}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}+{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}.

In this section, we discuss applications of this diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple to the Chern filtration and the associated graded ideal
I^{\mathrm{gr}}\coloneqq\ker({\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N))
.

Corollary 4.12
.

The
{\mathfrak{h}}
operator in the diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action on
{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
is the shifted Chern degree operator. In particular,
\overline{\Omega}(N,q,1)
satisfies the symmetry and unimodality.
11
11

11

We note, however, that the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action does not give an independent proof of the symmetry since Corollary
4.5
relies on the perfectness of the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\mathrm{gr}}
.

Proof.

The
{\mathfrak{h}}
operator of the diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple is given by the formula

{\mathfrak{h}}=2\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+2\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}+2\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-(2g-2).

Therefore, the
{\mathfrak{h}}
-degrees of the multiplication operators
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\psi_{i}
are precisely
2
, equal to their Chern degrees. Furthermore, the
2g-2
in the formula of
{\mathfrak{h}}
is precisely half of the top Chern degree
4g-4
of the moduli space
N=N_{2,1}
. The second statement follows from general theory of finite dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representations.
∎

For moduli theory in various settings, describing a complete set of tautological relations is a fundamental problem. In the case of the moduli space
N=N_{2,1}
, the ideal of tautological relations is defined as

I=\ker\big({\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow H^{*}(N)\big)

and is characterized by the Mumford conjecture, proven by Kirwan
[
Kir
]
. In our setting, we can ask the same question regarding the Chern filtration by considering the ideal

I^{\mathrm{gr}}\coloneqq\ker\big({\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)\big).

This amounts to understanding the shape of the highest Chern degree part of each tautological relation. The next corollary characterizes the ideal
I^{\mathrm{gr}}
purely in terms of the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operator

(32)

-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}-\beta\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}+(g-1)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}

in the diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple.

Corollary 4.13
.

The associated graded ideal
I^{\mathrm{gr}}\subset{\mathbb{D}}
is the smallest ideal containing
C_{>4g-4}{\mathbb{D}}
which is closed under the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operator (
32
).

Proof.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{D}
be the smallest ideal that contains
C_{>4g-4}{\mathbb{D}}
and is closed under the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operator (
32
). Since
I^{\mathrm{gr}}
contains
C_{>4g-4}{\mathbb{D}}
by Theorem
0.2
and is closed under the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operator by Corollary
4.5
, we have
J\subseteq I^{\mathrm{gr}}
. Consider the surjections

(33)

{\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{D}}/J\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}}.

In order to show that
J=I^{\mathrm{gr}}
, it suffices to show the numerical statement

(34)

\dim{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}({\mathbb{D}}/J)=\dim{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}({\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}})

for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Since the diagonal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action descends to
{\mathbb{D}}/J
and
{\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}}
with the
{\mathfrak{h}}
operator being the shifted Chern grading by Corollary
4.12
, we have symmetries

\dim{\mathrm{gr}}_{i}^{C}({\mathbb{D}}/J)=\dim{\mathrm{gr}}_{4g-4-i}^{C}({\mathbb{D}}/J),\quad\dim{\mathrm{gr}}_{i}^{C}({\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}})=\dim{\mathrm{gr}}_{4g-4-i}^{C}({\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}}).

So it suffices to show the equality (
34
) for
0\leq i\leq 2g-2
.

Theorem
0.3
computes the dimensions of the Chern grading of
{\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}}\simeq{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{*}(N)
as

(35)

\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}({\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}})q^{i}=\Omega(N,q,1)=\frac{(1+q^{2})^{2g}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}-q^{2g}\cdot\frac{2^{2g}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}.

On the other hand, the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
is generated by two even generators with
\deg^{C}(\alpha)=\deg^{C}(\beta)=2
and
2g
odd generators with
\deg^{C}(\psi_{i})=2
, and hence

\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{i}\,{\mathbb{D}}=\frac{(1+q^{2})^{2g}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}

which is precisely the first term of (
35
). Since the two formulas differ by the second term which starts from
q^{2g}
,
{\mathbb{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{D}}/I^{\mathrm{gr}}
is an isomorphism up to Chern degree
2g-1
. Using the successive surjections in (
33
), we conclude the desired equality (
34
) for
0\leq i\leq 2g-1
(which is slightly larger than
0\leq i\leq 2g-2
).
∎

4.5.
Commuting
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples on intermediate stacks

In Section
3
we studied the cohomology of the intermediate stacks
{\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\leq d}
and their Chern filtration. Recall that when
d=0
, the rings
H^{\ast}(N_{2,1})\simeq H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\leq 0})
are isomorphic. It turns out that there exist
(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-representations on the associated graded
{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\leq d})
of all of these stacks, generalizing the representation discussed before.

We define the following operators on the descendent algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}^{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}^{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-(g+2d-1),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g+2d-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\,,

and

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta}^{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\beta,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta}^{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-(g+2d-1),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\beta\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}+(g+2d-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}-\frac{\alpha}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\,.

The only difference between these operators and our previous ones (which agree when
d=0
) is the factor
g+2d-1
replacing
g-1
. The proof of Proposition
4.1
immediately generalizes to show that
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}^{d},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}^{d},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta}^{d},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta}^{d},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d})
form two commuting
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples. Our final result is the following:

Theorem 4.14
.

For every
d\geq 1
, the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\alpha}^{d},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}^{d},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d})
and
({\mathfrak{e}}_{\beta}^{d},{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta}^{d},{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d})
descend via the surjections
{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\leq d})
.

The fact that the
{\mathfrak{e}}
operators descend is trivial. The
{\mathfrak{h}}
operators are affine combinations of the cohomological grading operator and the Chern grading operator, so they also descend. The non-trivial part of the theorem concerns the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operators.

When
d>0
, we no longer have an integration map nor a non-degenerate pairing, so the strategy previously used to show self-adjointness of the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operators is not available. Instead, we use the results from Section
3
, and in particular the explicit basis (
24
) for the ideal

I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}=\ker\big({\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathrm{gr}}^{C}_{\bullet}H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{N}}_{2,1}^{\leq d})\big)\,.

The claim of Theorem
4.14
is equivalent to
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\,{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}
preserving the ideal
I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}
. Note also that the ideals
I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}
are no longer symmetric under swapping
\alpha
and
\beta
when
d>0
(cf. Lemma
4.6
), so the statements for

{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}
and
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}
have to be shown separately.

We first prove the following lemma that will help us calculate the action of the
{\mathfrak{f}}
operators on the relations (
24
).

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\sigma_{l}\in\mathsf{Prim}_{l}\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}
. Then, the restriction of the commutators
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\sigma_{l}]
and
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d},\sigma_{l}]
to the
\Gamma
-invariant subring
{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}
are

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\sigma_{l}]_{|{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=-l\sigma_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\frac{l}{2}\beta\sigma_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d},\sigma_{l}]_{|{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=-l\sigma_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}+\frac{l}{2}\alpha\sigma_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\,.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\,{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}
are
\Gamma
-equivariant and
\Gamma
acts transitively on
\mathsf{Prim}_{l}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\sigma_{l}=\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{l}
. We have

[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{l}]=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\psi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}},\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{l}\right]-\frac{\beta}{4}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}},\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{l}\right]

The first commutator on the right hand side is clearly
l\sigma_{l}
. The second commutator is

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}},\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{l}\right]=-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{i-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\psi_{i+1}\ldots\psi_{l}\,.

This is a derivation on
{\mathbb{D}}
, so it is enough to determine the images of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
under it to calculate its restriction to
{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}
. Clearly,
\alpha
and
\beta
are annihilated, so it remains to find

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}},\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{l}\right]\gamma=2\sum_{i=1}^{l}\psi_{1}\ldots\psi_{i-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{i+g}}\psi_{i+1}\ldots\psi_{l}\cdot\psi_{i}\cdot\psi_{i+g}=-2l\sigma_{l}\,.

The proof of the formula for
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\sigma_{l}]_{|{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}}
follows. The exact same calculation applies to
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d},\sigma_{l}]_{|{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}}
by switching
\alpha
and
\beta
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.14
.

We denote

R_{k,m,l}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b+c=m\\
a+b+2c=k-g-l\end{subarray}}(g-l-c)!\frac{\alpha^{a}}{a!}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b!}\frac{(2\gamma)^{c}}{c!}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\Gamma}.

As explained in Section
3.5
, the ideal
I^{{\mathrm{gr}}}_{\leq d}
is freely generated as a
{\mathbb{Q}}[\beta]
-module by the relations
R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l}
for
k,m,l
such that
k\geq 2g+2d
and
l+m\leq g
, with
\sigma_{l}
ranging in a basis of
\mathsf{Prim}_{l}
. Since
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\beta]=0
and
[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d},\beta]=-{\mathfrak{h}}_{\beta}^{d}
both preserve the ideal
I^{{\mathrm{gr}}}_{\leq d}
, it is enough to show that

{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}(R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l}),\>{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}(R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l})\in I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}\,.

We start with
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}
and calculate using Lemmas
4.2
and
4.15
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}(R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l})

\displaystyle=\big(\sigma_{l}\cdot{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}+[{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d},\sigma_{l}]\big)R_{k,m,l}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{l}\cdot\left(-\alpha\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+(g+2d-1-l)\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-2\gamma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha\partial\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}\beta\gamma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma^{2}}-\frac{g-l}{2}\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\right)R_{k,m,l}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b+c=m\\
a+b+2c=k-g-l\end{subarray}}(g-l-c)!\underbrace{(-a+1+g+2d-1-l-2c)}_{=2g+2d-k+b}\frac{\alpha^{a-1}}{(a-1)!}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b!}\frac{(2\gamma)^{c}}{c!}\right)\cdot\sigma_{l}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b+c=m\\
a+b+2c=k-g-l\end{subarray}}\underbrace{(g-l-c)!(b+1)(c-1-g+l)}_{-(g-l-c+1)!(b+1)}\frac{\alpha^{a}}{a!}\frac{\beta^{b+1}}{(b+1)!}\frac{(2\gamma)^{c-1}}{(c-1)!}\right)\cdot\sigma_{l}\,.

Renaming
(a-1,b,c)
as
(a,b,c)
in the first sum and
(a,b+1,c-1)
as
(a,b,c)
in the second sum, we find that

{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}(R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b+c=m\\
a+b+2c=k-g-l-1\end{subarray}}(g-l-c)!(2g+2d-k)\frac{\alpha^{a}}{a!}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b!}\frac{(2\gamma)^{c}}{c!}\sigma_{l}=(2g+2d-k)R_{k-1,m,l}\sigma_{l}\,.

Now the conclusion that
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\alpha}^{d}(R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l})\in I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}
for every
k\geq 2g+2d
follows: if
k=2g+2d
then the right hand side vanishes, and if
k>2g+2d
then
k-1\geq 2g+2d
as well, so
R_{k-1,m,l}\sigma_{l}\in I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}
.

The analysis for
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}
is essentially parallel, and we obtain

{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}(R_{k,m,l}\sigma_{l})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b+c=m-1\\
a+b+2c=k-g-l-1\end{subarray}}(g-l-c)!(2g+2d-k)\frac{\alpha^{a}}{a!}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b!}\frac{(2\gamma)^{c}}{c!}\sigma_{l}=(2g+2d-k)R_{k-1,m-1,l}\sigma_{l}\,.

Hence, we conclude in the same way that
{\mathfrak{f}}_{\beta}^{d}
preserves the ideal
I^{\textup{gr}}_{\leq d}
.
∎

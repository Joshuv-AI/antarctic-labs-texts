Introduction

The linking number of two oriented knots

K_{2}

{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

Interest in the problem of producing integral formulas that compute the linking number in other Riemannian ambients, with an emphasis on ”universal” such formulas has been quite rich, not least because of the connection with classical electromagnetism, in particular with the Biot-Savart and Ampere laws. 5.

Let

Definition 5.1

A solution to an initial value problem (IVP) for the heat equation with initial condition

is a family of smooth differential forms

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\Delta\right)\omega_{t}&=&0\\
\displaystyle{\lim_{t\searrow 0}}~\omega_{t}=S\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian on
k
-forms and the limit is taken in the sense of currents.

A construction for the heat kernel of any generalized Laplacian is given in
[
5
]
Chapter 2. Adapting to the Laplacian on
k
-forms we have the following. For every
t>0
,
p_{t}^{k}(x,y)
is an
(n-k,k)
form on

For every

(5.2)

\displaystyle P_{t}^{k}:\Omega^{k}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k}(M),\qquad P_{t}^{k}(\omega):=e^{-t\Delta}(\omega):=(\pi_{2})_{*}(p_{t}^{k}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}\omega)\qquad\mbox{i.e.}

(5.3)

\displaystyle P_{t}^{k}(\omega)=\left\{y\rightarrow\int_{\pi_{2}}p_{t}^{k}(x,y)\wedge\omega(x)=\int_{M}^{dx}p_{t}^{k}(x,y)\wedge\omega(x)\right\}\qquad~

Remark

According to Appendix

, the analysis of extendibility in higher codimension has to take into account the decomposition of

into its components of various vertical degrees. The vertical-horizontal decomposition is induced by a choice of a

(see Definition

TU\simeq\pi^{*}\nu L\oplus\pi^{*}TL

gets decomposed into a vertical (i.e. from

According to Section

valid at

Proposition 8.6

The following holds along

Appendix A
Sign conventions and properties of forms

A.1.
Sign conventions - summary

We make the following conventions in the category of oriented manifolds. The oriented manifold
M
will have dimension
n
in what follows.

(1)

the orientation of the boundary in Stokes: exterior normal first, this is standard.

(2)

the choice of decomposition order in integration along fiber: fiber first, base second (Halperin-Vanstone use the opposite, Nicolaescu uses the same).

(3)

the orientation of the total bundle of a fiber bundle
P\rightarrow M
: fiber first - base second. Note that this is independent of the choice in (2).

The fiber first convention for
T_{p}P
says that if
g_{1},\ldots,g_{f}
is a (positively) oriented basis for
\Ker d_{p}\pi
then
\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots g_{f},e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is an oriented basis for
T_{p}P
if and only if
\{d_{p}\pi(e_{1}),\ldots,d_{p}\pi(e_{n})\}
is an oriented basis for
T_{\pi(p)}M
.

(4)

the choice of the embedding
\Omega^{n-k}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{k}(M)
:

\omega\rightarrow T_{\omega}(\eta)=\int_{M}\omega\wedge\eta

(3) and (4) combine well to guarantee the following equality for a fiber bundle
\pi:P\rightarrow M
and
\omega\in\Omega^{*}_{\cpt}(P)
:

\pi_{*}(T_{\omega})=T_{\int_{\pi}\omega}

(5)

the definition of
d:\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{k}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{k-1}(M)
:

dT(\eta)=(-1)^{n-k-1}T(d\eta)

This has the advantage of recovering
d
on currents induced by smooth forms. But beware that for
\alpha:M^{m}\rightarrow N^{n}
a proper map

d\alpha_{*}=(-1)^{m-n}\alpha_{*}d

(6)

the orientation convention for a submanifold: normal bundle first; this means that the orientation of the ambient manifold is the orientation of the normal bundle wedge the orientation of the submanifold; this can be seen as an orientation convention for the submanifold or for the normal bundle, depending on necessity. Note that (6) and (3) fit with the exponential map and (1), (3) and (6) make the two orientations of the fiber of the spherical normal bundle coincide

(7)

the pull-back push-forward convention for a kernel
{\mathbb{K}}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{k,p}(M\times M)
: the operator
K:\Omega^{k}_{\cpt}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{p}(M)
it determines is defined by

(A.1)

K(\omega)(\eta):=(\pi_{2})_{*}({\mathbb{K}}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}\omega)(\eta)=({\mathbb{K}}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}\omega)(\pi_{2}^{*}\eta)={\mathbb{K}}(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\eta)

Because

has the canonical product orientation only
\pi_{2}
(of the two projections onto the factors) satisfies (3), which makes it the candidate for push-forward. The wedging of a current and a smooth form in (
A.1

.

Remark

Definition B.12

An Ehresmann connection on the vector bundle

Example B.13

A metric Koszul connection

d_{(p,v)}f(w,u)=2\langle v,w\rangle_{p}+(\nabla_{d\pi(u)}\langle\,,\,\rangle)(v,v),\qquad\forall(w,u)\in V_{(p,v)}E\oplus H_{(p,v)}E=E_{p}\oplus H_{(p,v)}E

(i)

Theorem B.26

Relative to this decomposition we have correspondingly

Variational Seasonal-Trend Decomposition with Sparse Continuous-Domain Regularization
Julien Fageot

Abstract
We consider the inverse problem of recovering a continuous-domain function from a finite number of noisy linear measurements. The unknown signal is modeled as the sum of a slowly varying trend and a periodic or quasi-periodic seasonal component. We formulate a variational framework for their joint recovery by introducing convex regularizations based on generalized total variation, which promote sparsity in spline-like representations. Our analysis is conducted in an infinite-dimensional setting and leads to a representer theorem showing that minimizers are splines in both components. To make the approach numerically feasible, we introduce a family of discrete approximations and prove their convergence to the original problem in the sense of Γ \Gamma -convergence. This further ensures the uniform convergence of discrete solutions to their continuous counterparts. The proposed framework offers a principled approach to seasonal-trend decomposition in the presence of noise and limited measurements, with theoretical guarantees on both representation and discretization.



1
Introduction

We develop variational methods for reconstructing a continuous-domain function

f_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, assumed to be the sum
f_{0}=f_{0,T}+f_{0,S}
of two components: a trend
f_{0,T}
and a seasonal part
f_{0,S}
. The seasonal component is assumed to be periodic with known period, which we normalize to
1
without loss of generality. We are given access to finite-dimensional, possibly corrupted linear measurements in the form of an observation vector
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
. These measurements correspond to a linear sensing vector

\bm{\Phi}(f_{0})=(\phi_{1}(f_{0}),\ldots,\phi_{L}(f_{0})),

(1)

where each
\phi_{\ell}
is a continuous linear functional acting on
f_{0}
. Our objective is to recover both the trend and seasonal components jointly, using a continuous-domain variational framework based on sparsity-promoting regularization.

1.1
Seasonal-Trend Sparse Inverse Problem

We formulate the recovery of
(f_{0,T},f_{0,S})
as a functional inverse problem. The seasonal component
f_{0,S}
is modeled as a function
f_{0,S}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined on the circle
\mathbb{T}
, reflecting its periodicity of period
1
. The reconstruction is posed as the solution to the optimization problem

\underset{(f_{T},f_{S})}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{E}\left(\Phi(f_{T}+f_{S})\right)+\lambda_{T}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})},

(2)

where:

•

The data-fidelity term
\mathcal{E}\left(\Phi(f_{T}+f_{S})\right)
encourages the synthesized measurements

\Phi(f_{T}+f_{S})=(\phi_{1}(f_{T}+f_{S}),\ldots,\phi_{L}(f_{T}+f_{S}))\in\mathbb{R}^{L}

(3)

to match the observations
\bm{y}
. A common choice for
\mathcal{E}
is the quadratic loss

\mathcal{E}\left(\bm{z}\right)=\|\bm{y}-\bm{z}\|^{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(y_{\ell}-z_{\ell})^{2},

(4)

which corresponds to a Gaussian white noise model in a Bayesian interpretation.

•

The regularization term

\mathcal{R}(f_{T},f_{S})=\lambda_{T}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}

(5)

imposes structure on the trend and seasonal components via the differential operators
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
. The norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}
are continuous-domain generalizations of the
\ell_{1}
norm, promoting sparsity in the sense of Radon measures. The former promotes piecewise-smooth or spline-like structure in
f_{T}
, while the latter enforces periodicity in
f_{S}

[
29
]
.

•

The parameters
\lambda_{T}>0
and
\lambda_{S}>0
control the trade-off between data fidelity, trend regularization, and seasonal regularization.

A solution
(\tilde{f}_{T},\tilde{f}_{S})
to (
2
) is expected to approximate the true decomposition
(f_{0,T},f_{0,S})
, and hence
\tilde{f}=\tilde{f}_{T}+\tilde{f}_{S}
provides a faithful reconstruction of
f_{0}=f_{0,T}+f_{0,S}
. To the best of our knowledge, total-variation regularization has not been previously used for seasonal-trend decomposition in the continuous domain. Yet, as discussed in Section
1.2
, there is strong theoretical and empirical motivation for such an approach.

1.3
Contributions and Outline

We develop the mathematical analysis of functional inverse problems for sparsity-promoting seasonal-trend reconstruction. A key feature of our approach is its generality, with particular attention paid to covering broad classes of regularization operators
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
, measurement functionals
\phi_{\ell}
, and convex data-fidelity terms
\mathcal{E}
.

•

Functional-analytic foundations:
We introduce the general framework required to properly define the variational problem (
2
). This involves specifying the
native Banach space
under which the optimization is conducted, as well as its joint
measurement space
, where the measurement functionals
\phi_{\ell}
reside. We uncover the inherent direct sum structure of the problem, ensuring the identifiability of the seasonal-trend model.

•

Seasonal-trend representer theorem:
We establish a representer theorem, demonstrating that the variational problem (
2
) admits composite sparse spline solutions for both the seasonal and trend components.

•

Quadratic representer theorem:
We compare the use of total-variation regularization norms with quadratic ones, which are more classical and linked to kernel methods. From this, we derive a quadratic representer theorem for seasonal-trend decomposition, showing that the unique solution of the quadratic variational problem strongly couples the seasonal and trend components, thereby limiting the utility of kernel methods.

•

Theoretical analysis of grid-based approximation:
To solve the optimization task (
2
) efficiently, we introduce a grid-based approximation scheme and perform a convergence analysis. We demonstrate the
\Gamma
-convergence of grid-based schemes and, under mild assumptions, deduce the pointwise and uniform convergence of the grid-based solutions towards the continuous-domain ones, for both the trend and seasonal components.

Functional Analytic Preliminaries

2.1
Tempered Generalized Functions

We begin by introducing the Schwartz space of rapidly decaying smooth functions and its dual, along with their periodic counterparts. For a concise introduction to the subject, we refer the reader to
[
45
]
. It is important to note that these spaces will primarily serve as the space of "nice" test functions and the space of all possible linear functionals encountered in this work.

Let
{\rm D}^{N}
denote the
N
th derivative. The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
consists of infinitely smooth, rapidly decaying functions, endowed with the Fréchet topology associated with the family of semi-norms

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|t^{M}{\rm D}^{N}\varphi(t)|\quad\text{for}\quad M,N\geq 0.

The topological dual of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of tempered generalized functions, denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, and it is endowed with the weak*-topology. The duality pairing between
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is denoted by
\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}
. Any generalized function encountered in this work is identified as a tempered generalized function through its action on test functions
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. The function spaces considered in this work are all embedded in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.

We also define the space of smooth
1
-periodic functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}=[0,1]
is the
1
-dimensional torus, with the endpoints
0
and
1
identified. The space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
is a Fréchet space, equipped with the topology associated with the family of semi-norms

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})\mapsto\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|{\rm D}^{N}\varphi(t)|\quad\text{for}\quad N\geq 0.

The topological dual of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
is the space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
of
1
-periodic generalized functions, which is also endowed with the weak*-topology. The duality pairing between
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
is denoted by
\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})}
.

We have the topological embeddings
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, a periodic generalized function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
can be viewed as a tempered generalized function by its action on test functions in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, rather than just on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
. Furthermore, for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}=\langle f,\mathrm{Per}\{\varphi\}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})},

(7)

where
\mathrm{Per}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
is the periodization operator defined by

\mathrm{Per}\{\varphi\}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\cdot-k).

2.2
Linear Shift-Invariant Operators

Let
{\rm L}
be a linear, shift-invariant (LSI), and continuous operator from
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
to itself. Then,
{\rm L}
is a convolution operator of the form

{\rm L}f=h*f

(8)

where the impulse response
h
of
{\rm L}
belongs to the space
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of rapidly decreasing generalized functions
[
28
]
. This space includes the Dirac delta
\delta
, for which
{\rm L}=\mathrm{Id}
is the identity operator, and its derivatives
{\rm D}^{N}\delta=\delta^{(N)}
of any order
N\geq 1
, for which
{\rm L}={\rm D}^{N}
. More generally, any compactly supported generalized function is in
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, which also includes rapidly decaying smooth functions such as
h(t)=\exp(-t^{2}/2)
.

The space
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is the Fourier transform of the space
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
of infinitely smooth and slowly growing functions
1
1

1

A function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is in
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
if it is infinitely smooth, and if every derivative
{\rm D}^{N}g
grows no faster than a polynomial.
. Both
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
have been studied in
[
42
, Chapter VII-5]
and correspond to convolution and multiplication operators from
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
to itself
2
2

2

This explains the subscripts
C
(for convolution) and
M
(for multiplication) in the definition of these spaces.
.

The frequency response
\widehat{L}
of a continuous LSI operator
{\rm L}
is the Fourier transform
\widehat{L}=\widehat{h}
of its impulse response
h
. The frequency response
\widehat{L}
belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
, and satisfies
\widehat{{\rm L}f}=\widehat{L}\widehat{f}
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.

2.3
Periodic and Non-Periodic Radon Measures

The space of Radon measures is denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
. It is the topological dual of the space
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
of continuous functions vanishing at infinity, endowed with the supremum norm
[
35
]
. We denote by
\langle w,g\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}w(t)
the duality product between a function
g\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and a Radon measure
w\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
.

The space of Radon measures is a Banach space for the total variation norm defined by

\|w\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}),\ \|g\|_{\infty}=1}\langle w,g\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})}.

(9)

Radon measures include integrable functions
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, for which
\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t
, and shifted Dirac impulses
\delta(\cdot-x_{0})
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. For a sparse Radon measure
w=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\delta(\cdot-t_{k})
with weights
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
and distinct knots
t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\delta(\cdot-t_{k})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_{k}|=\lVert\bm{a}\rVert_{1}.

(10)

In this sense, the total variation norm is the functional extension of the discrete
\ell_{1}
norm.

We also consider the space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
of
periodic
Radon measures. It is the topological dual of the space
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})
of periodic continuous functions for the supremum norm. The space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
includes periodic integrable functions
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
and any shifted version of the Dirac stream
\mathrm{III}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(\cdot-k)=\mathrm{Per}\{\delta\}
. The total variation norm of a periodic Radon measure is denoted by

\|w\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T}),\ \|g\|_{\infty}=1}\langle w,g\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})},

(11)

where the duality product stands for
\langle w,g\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}w(t)
. A sparse periodic Radon measure
w=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\mathrm{III}(\cdot-t_{k})
with weights
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
and distinct knots
t_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
satisfies

\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\mathrm{III}(\cdot-t_{k})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_{k}|=\lVert\bm{a}\rVert_{1}.

(12)

Finally, we have the topological embeddings between Schwartz and Radon spaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}).

(13)

3
Native Spaces for Seasonal-Trend Decomposition

We introduce the functional-analytic framework for the specification of the native space of the variational problem (
2
). The optimization is made over pairs of functions
(f_{T},f_{S})
, respectively the trend and seasonal components.
Our methods are suitable for signals resulting from the superposition of periodic and acyclic processes depending on continuous-domain variables.

3.1
Trend Operators, Splines, and Native Spaces

Trend Operators.

We consider LSI and continuous operators
\mathrm{L}_{T}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. The frequency response of
\mathrm{L}_{T}
is denoted by
\widehat{L}_{T}
and is in
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
(see Section
2.2
).

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathrm{L}_{T}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
be an LSI and continuous operator. Then,
\mathrm{L}_{T}
is
trend-admissible of order
N_{T}\geq 0
if it can be written as

\mathrm{L}_{T}={\rm D}^{N_{T}}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}

(14)

where
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is LSI, continuous, and invertible.

The invertibility of
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}
means that there exists an LSI and continuous operator
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}^{-1}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}^{-1}f=\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}^{-1}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}f=f
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. This implies that the frequency response
\omega\mapsto\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}(\omega)
does not vanish on
\mathbb{R}
. The frequency response of the inverse is then the function
\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
. The invertibility condition therefore imposes that
\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}^{-1}
is slowly growing, hence
\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}
cannot vanish faster than any polynomial. This excludes, for instance, the frequency response
\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}(\omega)=\exp(-\omega^{2}/2)
corresponding to an operator
{\rm L}f=h*g
with
h(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp(-t^{2}/2)
.

The frequency response of
\mathrm{L}_{T}
is
\widehat{L}_{T}(\omega)=(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{N_{T}}\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv},T}(\omega)
.
\mathrm{L}_{T}
is invertible if and only if its trend-admissibility order is
{N_{T}}=0
.
Invertible trend-admissible operators include the Sobolev operators
\mathrm{L}_{T}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, whose frequency response is
\widehat{L}_{T}(\omega)=(1+\omega^{2})^{\gamma/2}
. We also consider non-invertible trend-admissible operators to include the derivative operators
{\rm D}^{N_{T}}
. The null space of a trend-admissible operator of order
{N_{T}}\geq 1
is the space
\mathcal{P}_{N_{T}}(\mathbb{R})
of polynomials up to degree
({N_{T}}-1)
, whose dimension is
N_{T}
.

The composition of two trend-admissible operators with orders
N_{T,1},N_{T,2}\geq 0
is trend-admissible with order
N_{T,1}+N_{T,2}
, and its frequency response is the product of the two underlying frequency responses.

3.2
Seasonal Operators, Splines, and Native Spaces

Seasonal Operators.

We consider LSI and continuous
periodic
operators
\mathrm{L}_{S}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
acting on periodic generalized functions. The complex exponentials
e_{n}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi nt}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, are the eigenfunctions of such operators and we have that
\mathrm{L}_{S}e_{n}=\widehat{L}_{S}[n]e_{n}
for some
\widehat{L}_{S}[n]\in\mathbb{C}
. We call
\left(\widehat{L}_{S}[n]\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
the
frequency sequence
of
\mathrm{L}_{S}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathrm{L}_{S}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
be an LSI and continuous periodic operator. Then,
\mathrm{L}_{S}
is
seasonal-admissible of order
N_{S}\geq 0
if it can be written as

\mathrm{L}_{S}={\rm D}^{N_{S}}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}

(15)

where
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
is LSI, continuous, and invertible.

The invertibility of
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}
means that there exists an LSI and continuous periodic operator
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}^{-1}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
such that
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}^{-1}f=\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}^{-1}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}f=f
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. This implies that
\widehat{L}_{S}[n]\neq 0
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and the frequency sequence of the inverse of
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{inv},S}^{-1}
is then the sequence
\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{L}_{S}[n]}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

A seasonal-admissible operator
\mathrm{L}_{S}
is invertible if and only if its seasonal-admissibility order
N_{S}=0
. The periodic Sobolev operators
\mathrm{L}_{S}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
are invertible and their frequency sequence is
\widehat{L}_{S}[n]=(1+4\pi^{2}n^{2})^{\gamma/2}
.

Non-invertible seasonal-admissible operators include the derivative operators
{\rm D}^{N_{S}}
, whose frequency sequence is
((2\pi n)^{N_{S}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. The null space of a non-invertible seasonal-admissible operator is of dimension 1 and made of constant functions. This is a significant distinction with the case of trend operators.

The composition of two seasonal-admissible operators with order
N_{1},N_{2}\geq 0
is seasonal-admissible with order
N_{1}+N_{2}
, and its frequency sequence is the pointwise product of the two frequency sequences of the underlying operators.

A linear and shift-invariant operator
{\rm L}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
specifies a periodic operator by restricting its domain to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. Moreover, we have the following result.

Proposition 1
.

Let
{\rm L}
be a trend-admissible operator with trend-admissibility order
N_{T}\geq 0
. Then, the following facts hold:

•

The operator
{\rm L}
is seasonal-admissible when seen as a periodic operator.

•

Its seasonal-admissibility order equals its trend-admissibility order.

•

The periodic null space of
{\rm L}
is trivial if
N_{T}=0
and made of constant functions otherwise. The dimension of the periodic null space is therefore
\min(1,N_{T})
.

•

In particular,
{\rm L}
is invertible as an operator on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, if and only if it is invertible as a periodic operator on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

By definition, we have that
{\rm L}={\rm D}^{N_{T}}{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
for some invertible operator
{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
. Then,
{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is LSI, hence its frequency response
\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}\in\mathcal{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
is a smooth function bounded by some polynomials
[
42
]
. Since
{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
is invertible, the frequency response
1/\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
of its inverse is also a smooth function bounded by some polynomial. Hence, there exist constants
A,B>0
and
p>0
such that

\frac{A}{1+|\omega|^{p}}\leq\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\omega)\leq B(1+|\omega|^{p})

(16)

for any
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since
{\rm L}
is a convolution, it commutes with shift operations. Hence, for any

, we have that
{\rm L}\{f\}(\cdot-1)={\rm L}\{f(\cdot-1)\}={\rm L}\{f\}
, which is therefore
1
-periodic. This shows that
{\rm L}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
is a periodic operator. The same holds for
{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
.

The frequency sequence of
{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
(seen as a periodic operator) is connected to its frequency response (as an operator from
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
to itself) via the relation
\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}[n]=\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}(2\pi\omega)
. Hence, due to (
16
), we have that

\frac{A^{\prime}}{1+|n|^{p}}\leq\widehat{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}[n]\leq B^{\prime}(1+|n|^{p})

(17)

for some constants
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}>0
and
p>0
. This implies that
{\rm L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
is invertible as a periodic operator
[
27
]
.

The equality of the trend-admissibility and the seasonal admissibility orders of
{\rm L}
is obvious.

The periodic null space of
{\rm L}
is the same as the one of
{\rm D}^{N_{T}}
, which is
\{0\}
if
N_{T}=0
and
\mathrm{Span}\{1\}
otherwise.

Finally, the invertibility condition is obvious using that
\L
is invertible as a trend operator if and only if
N_{T}=0
, and the latter condition also holds for
{\rm L}
as a seasonal operator.
∎

Seasonal Native Spaces.

We fix a seasonal-admissible operator
\mathrm{L}_{S}

Such spaces generalize the space of periodic Radon measures and have been studied in

]
. Seasonal native spaces are simpler to understand than their trend counterparts. We summarize the important facts specifying the Banach space structure of these spaces. More details can be found in

•

If

only specifies a semi-norm, and the null space of

(23)

•

We also consider the function space

with its corresponding periodic function defined over the real line
\mathbb{R}
.

3.3
The Seasonal-Trend Native Space

We fix a pair of trend- and seasonal-admissible operators
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
. We first consider the function space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})=\left\{f=f_{T}+f_{S},\quad(f_{T},f_{S})\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})\right\}.

(25)

The sum in (
25
) is not always direct, as we clarify in the next proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
be trend-admissible and seasonal-admissible operators, with respective orders
N_{T},N_{S}\geq 0
.

•

Assume that
N_{T}=0
. Then, we have the direct sum

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T}).

(26)

•

Assume that
N_{T}\geq 1
. Then, we have the direct sum

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T}).

(27)

Proof.

We prove the relation (
27
) (the proof of (
26
) is similar and simpler). First of all,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})
since the constant function
1
is in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})
as soon as
N_{T}\geq 1
, and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})\oplus\mathrm{Span}\{1\}
. It then suffices to show that the sum between
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})
is direct.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})
. The result follows if we prove that
f=0
. First,
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
is a 1-periodic generalized function, hence
g=\mathrm{L}_{T}f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. Moreover,
g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
by assumption. Hence,
g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. For
n\geq 0
, we set
\varphi_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi(\cdot-k)
. The periodicity of
g
implies that

\langle g,\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\langle g,\varphi(\cdot-k)\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}=(n+1)\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}.

(28)

Then, using that
g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we deduce that

|\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}|=\frac{|\langle g,\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}|}{n+1}=\frac{|\langle g,\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})}|}{n+1}\leq\frac{\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty}}{n+1}.

(29)

This implies that
|\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})}|\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty}}{n+1}=0
for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, hence
g=0
. The function
f
, which satisfies
\mathrm{L}_{T}f=g=0
, is therefore a polynomial of degree at most
(N_{T}-1)
. Since
f
is also periodic, we deduce that
f
is constant. Finally,
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})
has mean 0, hence
f=0
, as expected.
∎

Proposition 4

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. The following facts hold.

•

The space
(\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},q})
is a Banach space.

•

The topological dual of

is the native space
(\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})})^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
.

•

The dual norm associated to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},q}
is the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},p}
defined in (

) and (

). This means that

\displaystyle\|(f_{T},f_{S})\|_{\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(g_{T},g_{S})\in\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}\\
\|(g_{T},g_{S})\|_{\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},q}=1\end{subarray}}\langle(f_{T},f_{S}),(g_{T},g_{S})\rangle_{\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}\times\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(g_{T},g_{S})\in\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}\\
\|(g_{T},g_{S})\|_{\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},q}=1\end{subarray}}\langle f_{T},g_{T}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})}+\langle f_{S},g_{S}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})}.

(42)

Proof.

We use
[
48
, Lemma 1]
, whose first statement directly implies the following result.
Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be two Banach spaces with respective norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}}
. Their topological dual and their dual norms are denoted by
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})
and
(\mathcal{Y}^{\prime},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}})
.
Then, the product space
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is a Banach space for the norm

\|z\|_{\mathcal{Z}}=\|(x,y)\|_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y},p}=\left\|\left(\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}},\|y\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\right)\right\|_{p}.

(43)

Its topological dual is the space
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
, whose dual norm is given by

\|z^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}=\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{Y}^{\prime},q}=\left\|\left(\|x^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}},\|y^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}\right)\right\|_{q}.

(44)

We apply this result to the spaces
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})
if
N_{T}=0
or
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})
if
N_{T}\geq 1
. We know moreover from existing works that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})

[
50
, Theorem 6]
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})
, and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S},0}(\mathbb{T})

[

. This implies the desired result.
∎

Proposition
4
shows that
(\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})},\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})})
is a dual pair.
It generalizes known results for the pairs
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R}))
and
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T}),\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T}))

They are themselves generalizations of the Riesz-Markov theorem, which states the duality of the pairs

and

[

. Proposition

4.3
Periodization and Seasonal-Trend Sensing Functionals

In the previous section, we identified the predual
\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
of the seasonal-trend native space
\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
. In particular, any
(g_{T},g_{S})\in\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
acts linearly and weak*-continuously on the native space
\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
via

(g_{T},g_{S})\quad:\quad(f_{T},f_{S})\mapsto\langle f_{T},g_{T}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})}+\langle f_{S},g_{S}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})}.

(45)

This is not directly applicable to the optimization problem (
2
), where the action is on the sum
f_{T}+f_{S}
.
We shall therefore identify a class of sensing functionals
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
such that
(\phi,\mathrm{Per}\{\phi\})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})
can be used as an element of
\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
.

Definition 6
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. For
N\geq 0
, we set
f_{N}=\sum_{|n|\leq N}f(\cdot-n)
. If the sequence
(f_{N})_{N\geq 0}
converges in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, then its limit, denoted by

\mathrm{Per}\{f\}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(\cdot-n),

(46)

exists in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. In this case,
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
and we call it the
periodization of
f
. A generalized function is
periodizable
if its periodization exists.

Any test function
\varphi
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is periodizable, and the sum
\varphi_{N}=\sum_{|n|\leq N}\varphi(\cdot-n)
even converges in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T})
.
We provide sufficient conditions to ensure that a given generalized function is periodizable
4
4

4

Some generalized functions are not periodizable. For instance, the constant

is such that
f_{N}=2N+1
, hence
(f_{N})_{N\geq 1}
does not converge in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
.

Proposition 5
.

The following statements hold.

•

If
f\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f
is periodizable with
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. Moreover, any
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
can be written as the periodization
g=\mathrm{Per}\{f\}
of some
f\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.

•

Assume that
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,
f
is periodizable and
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
.

•

If
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and such that

\left|f(t)\right|\leq\frac{C}{1+|t|^{p}}

(47)

for some
C>0
,
p>1
, and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
f
is periodizable and
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

The first part of the first point is a classic property of the (not so classic) space
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{C}(\mathbb{R})
and is shown in
[
28
, Section 4.2]
.
The second part follows from the existence of smooth partitions of unity: there exists functions
\varphi\geq 0
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that
1=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\cdot-n)
. Said differently,
\varphi
is periodizable and
\mathrm{Per}\{\varphi\}=1
.

We fix
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. We observe that
f_{N}=\sum_{|n|\leq N}f(\cdot-n)=g.\sum_{|n|\leq N}\varphi(\cdot-n)=g.\varphi_{N}
, which converges towards
g.\mathrm{Per}\{\varphi\}=g
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. Hence,
g=\mathrm{Per}\{f\}
is the periodization of some function
f\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{C}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,
(f_{N})_{N\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{1}([0,1])
. Indeed, we have that, for any
1\leq M\leq N
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|f_{N}(t)-f_{M}(t)|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{M+1\leq|n|\leq N}f(t-n)\right|\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{M+1\leq|n|\leq N}\left|f(t-n)\right|\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M\leq|t|\leq{N+1}}|f(t)|\mathrm{d}t

(48)

which can be made arbitrarily small for large
N,M
as
f
is integrable over
\mathbb{R}
.
The space
L_{1}([0,1])
is complete, hence the sequence
(f_{N})_{N\geq 1}
converges to some function
f_{\infty}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(t-n)\in L_{1}([0,1])
, which can be seen as a periodic function over the real line
\mathbb{R}
.

For the last part of Proposition
5
, we observe that

|f_{N}(t)|\leq\sum_{|n|\leq N}|f(t-n)|\leq\sum_{|n|\leq N}\frac{C}{1+|t-n|^{p}}
, which is absolutely convergent. Hence, the limit
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(t-n)
exists for any

and is easily shown to be
1
-periodic. Then,

|f_{N}(t)-\mathrm{Per}\{f\}(t)|\leq\sum_{|n-1|\geq N}|f(t-n)|\leq\sum_{|n-1|\geq N}\frac{C}{1+|t-n|^{p}},

(49)

the latter converging uniformly to
0
over compact intervals. Hence,
f_{N}
converges to
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}
uniformly on compact intervals. This implies in particular that
\mathrm{Per}\{f\}
is continuous as the uniform limit of continuous functions on any compact interval. Hence,
f
is periodizable with continuous periodization.
∎

Proposition

is useful due to the importance of the periodization to interpret

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathrm{L}_{T}

Sampling Admissibility

Definition 8
.

A pair
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})

The Dirac impulse

In Proposition

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
be trend- and seasonal-admissible operators, respectively. Then, the following fact holds.

•

The pair
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
is sampling-admissible if and only if

\delta\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{III}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T}).

(56)

•

If
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
are invertible with respective Green’s and periodic Green’s function
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}=\mathrm{L}_{T}^{-1}\delta
and
\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}=\mathrm{L}_{S}^{-1}\mathrm{III}
, then
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
is sampling-admissible if and only if

\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T}).

(57)

•

If
\mathrm{L}_{T}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma_{T}/2}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma_{S}/2}
are Sobolev operators with parameters
\gamma_{T},\gamma_{S}\geq 0
, then
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
is sensing admissible if and only if
\gamma_{T}>1
and
\gamma_{S}>1
.

•

Let

. Then,
({\rm D}^{N_{T}},{\rm D}^{N_{S}})
is sampling-admissible if and only if
N_{T}\geq 2
and
N_{S}\geq 2
.

Proof.

First of all, all the considered function spaces are shift-invariant, hence
\delta(\cdot-t_{0})
is sensing admissible if and only if
\delta
is. We can therefore restrict to
t_{0}=0
. The Dirac impulse
\delta
is periodizable with
\mathrm{Per}\{\delta\}=\mathrm{III}
, hence
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
is sampling admissible if and only if
\delta\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{III}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})
.

Let us assume that both
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
are invertible.
Then, by the definition of the trend-measurement space,
\delta\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{L}_{T}^{*}\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}=\mathrm{L}_{T}^{-1}\delta\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. Similarly,
\mathrm{III}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(\mathbb{T})=\mathrm{L}_{S}^{*}\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}=\mathrm{L}_{S}^{-1}\mathrm{III}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})
. This implies the equivalence between (
56
) and (
57
).

The operators
\mathrm{L}_{T}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma_{T}/2}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma_{S}/2}
are invertible, hence the criterion (
57
) applies. Then, the Green’s function of
\mathrm{L}_{S}
is continuous if and only if
\gamma_{S}>1
according to
[
27
, Proposition 9]
.
For the trend, we observe that
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}
is the inverse Fourier transform of
\omega\mapsto F_{\gamma_{T}}(\omega)=(1+\omega^{2})^{-\gamma_{T}/2}
.

We first consider
0<\gamma_{T}<1
. Then, for any
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,

F_{\gamma_{T}}(\omega)=|\omega|^{-\gamma_{T}}+\left((1+\omega^{2})^{-\gamma_{T}/2}-|\omega|^{-\gamma_{T}}\right)=|\omega|^{-\gamma_{T}}+G_{\gamma_{T}}(\omega)

(58)

with
G_{\gamma_{T}}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed,
G_{\gamma_{T}}
behaves like
-|\omega|^{-\gamma_{T}}
around the origin and like
-\frac{\gamma_{T}}{2}|\omega|^{-(\gamma_{T}+2)}
at infinity, both being integrable. The inverse Fourier transform of
G_{\gamma_{T}}
is therefore continuous. The inverse Fourier transform of
|\omega|^{-\gamma_{T}}
is known to be proportional to
x\mapsto|x|^{1-\gamma_{T}}

[
gelfand1964generalized
, Table 1 p. 359]
and is therefore discontinuous (and unbounded) around the origin. Hence,
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}
is discontinuous as the sum of continuous and discontinuous functions.

For
\gamma_{T}=1
, we use that the inverse Fourier transform of
F_{\gamma_{T}}
is proportional to
K_{0}(|x|)
, where
K_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. The latter is known to diverge like
\log|x|
around the origin, hence
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}
is also not continuous.

We have proved that, for
\mathrm{L}_{T}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma_{T}/2}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)^{\gamma_{S}/2}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}
is continuous if and only if
\gamma_{T}>1
and
\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}
is continuous if and only if
\gamma_{S}>1
. This proves the third point of Proposition
6
.

For the last point, we rely on (
56
). The condition
\mathrm{III}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{D}^{N_{S}}}(\mathbb{T})
is equivalent to
N_{S}\geq 2
according to
[
27
, Proposition 9]
.
The Dirac impulse
\delta
is shown to be in
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm D}^{2}}(\mathbb{R})
in
[
47
, Proposition 15]
, and the reasoning can be extended to higher-order derivatives with
N_{T}\geq 2
.
On the contrary,
\delta
is not in
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm D}}(\mathbb{R})
. Otherwise, we would have that
\delta={\rm D}g+\varphi
with
g\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

[
50
]
. Since
\delta={\rm D}u
with
u(t)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)
is the Heaviside function, this would imply that
{\rm D}(u-g)=\varphi
is infinitely smooth, hence
u-g
is infinitely smooth which is not the case. This concludes the proof.
∎

5
Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem

We aim at recovering a composite function
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
f_{0}=f_{0,S}+f_{0,T}
where
f_{0,S}
is a
1
-periodic function — the seasonal part of
f_{0}
— and
f_{0,T}
is a non-periodic function — the trend part.
We have access to
L\geq 1
noisy observations

\bm{y}\approx\bm{\Phi}(f_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{L},

(59)

where
\bm{\Phi}(f_{0})
depends linearly on
f_{0}
.
The reconstruction model depends on the choice of trend- and seasonal-admissible operators
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
, which determines the seasonal-trend native space
\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
(see Definition (
4
)) for the reconstruction task.

In this section, we obtain the representer theorem for the reconstruction problem (
2
).
We first obtain a seasonal-trend representer theorem for measures in Section
5.1
and then deduce the general case in Section
5.2
. We deal with the special case of piecewise-polynomial reconstruction in Section
5.3
and we compare with quadratic regularizations for seasonal-trend reconstruction in Section
5.4
.

5.1
Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem for Radon Measures

We first consider optimization problems such as (
2
) with no regularization operators,
i.e.
with
\mathrm{L}_{S}=\mathrm{L}_{T}=\mathrm{Id}
. According to Definition
4
, the seasonal-trend native space is
\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{Id})=\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
.
In preparation for the general representer theorem, we slightly modify the optimization problem by adding a finite-dimensional vector in the search space, which only affects the measurement process and is not concerned by the regularization.
Proposition
7
will be the cornerstone for the proof of the general case (see Theorem
1
in Section
5.2
).

Proposition 7

(Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem for Radon Measures)
.

We consider the following:

•

N\geq 0
and
L\geq 1
are some integers such that
N\leq L
;

•

\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{R}^{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
is a proper, lower semi-continuous, coercive, and convex data fidelity functional,

•

\bm{\Phi}=(\phi_{\ell})_{1\leq\ell\leq L}=\left((g_{T,\ell},g_{S,\ell},\bm{z}_{\ell})\right)_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\in\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)^{L}
is a family such that (i)
\bm{\Phi}(0,0,\bm{c})=\bm{0}
implies that
\bm{c}=\bm{0}
and (ii)
\mathcal{E}\circ\bm{\Phi}
is proper;

•

\lambda_{T},\lambda_{S}>0
.

Then, the solution set

\mathcal{W}=\underset{(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Phi}(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c}))+\lambda_{T}\|w_{T}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\|w_{S}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}

(60)

is non-empty, convex, and weak*-compact in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, its extreme points
(\tilde{w}_{T},\tilde{w_{S}},\bm{c})
are such that

\tilde{w}_{T}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\delta(\cdot-u_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{w}_{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\mathrm{III}(\cdot-v_{k})

(61)

where
a_{k}\neq 0
, the
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
are distinct,
b_{k}\neq 0
, the
v_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
are distinct, and
K_{S}+K_{T}\leq L-N
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is a Banach space for the norm

(g_{T},g_{S},\bm{z})\mapsto\max\left(\|g_{T}\|_{\infty},\|g_{S}\|_{\infty},\|\bm{z}\|_{2}\right).

(62)

Its topological dual is
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, whose dual norm is

(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})\mapsto\|w_{T}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{S}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}+\|\bm{c}\|_{2}.

(63)

This can be proved using
[
48
, Lemma 1]
, as we did in Proposition
4
below (see (
43
) and (
44
)).

We define the cost functional
\mathcal{J}
over
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
as

\mathcal{J}(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})=\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Phi}(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c}))+\lambda_{T}\|w_{T}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\|w_{S}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}.

(64)

The assumptions made on
\mathcal{E}
and
\bm{\Phi}
and the properties of the total-variation norms imply that both
(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})\mapsto\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Phi}(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c}))
and
(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})\mapsto\|w_{T}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{S}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}
are proper, convex, and weak*-lower semi-continuous, and their sum
\mathcal{J}
also shares these properties.

We now show that
\mathcal{J}
is coercive. We fix a sequence
((w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{c}_{n}))_{n\geq 0}\in(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\|w_{T,n}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{S,n}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}+\|\bm{c}_{n}\|_{2}\rightarrow_{n}\infty

(65)

and show that
J_{n}=\mathcal{J}(w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{c}_{n})\rightarrow_{n}\infty
. By contradiction, let assume this is not the case. This means that a subsequence of
(J_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is bounded. By only considering this subsequence, we can restrict to the case where
(J_{n})_{n\geq 0}
itself is bounded, say by
M>0
. This implies that
\|w_{T,n}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{S,n}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}\leq J_{n}\leq M
and therefore
\|\bm{c}_{n}\|_{2}\rightarrow_{n}\infty
due to (
65
).

By assumption,
\bm{c}\mapsto\bm{\Phi}(0,0,\bm{c})
is injective, which implies that

\|\bm{\Phi}(0,0,\bm{c}_{n})\|_{2}\rightarrow_{n}\infty.

(66)

Moreover, we have that
\bm{\Phi}:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}
is weak*-continuous. The weak*-topology being weaker than the norm topology, this implies that
\bm{\Phi}
is also continuous for the Banach topology on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, hence there exists some constant
A>0
such that
\|\bm{\Phi}(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{0})\|_{2}\leq A(\|w_{T}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{S}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})})
. This implies in particular that
\|\bm{\Phi}(w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{0})\|_{2}\leq AM
is bounded.
Finally, we observe that, using the triangular inequality for
\|\cdot\|_{2}
and the linearity of
\bm{\Phi}
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Phi}(w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{c}_{n})\|_{2}\geq\|\bm{\Phi}(0,0,\bm{c}_{n})\|_{2}-\|\bm{\Phi}(w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{0})\|_{2}\geq\|\bm{\Phi}(0,0,\bm{c}_{n})\|_{2}-AM\rightarrow_{n}\infty.

(67)

This shows that
\|\bm{\Phi}(w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{c}_{n})\|_{2}\rightarrow_{n}\infty
. The coercivity of
\mathcal{E}
then implies that
M\geq J_{n}\geq\mathcal{E}(\|\bm{\Phi}(w_{T,n},w_{S,n},\bm{c}_{n})\|_{2})\rightarrow_{n}\infty
, which is impossible. This demonstrates the coercivity of
\mathcal{J}
.

We are in the conditions of
[
38
, Proposition 8]
, and we deduce that
\mathcal{W}=\arg\min_{(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})}\mathcal{J}(w_{T},w_{S},\bm{c})
is non-empty, convex, and weak*-compact.
∎

5.2
Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem for Spline Reconstruction

Theorem 1

(Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem)
.

Consider the optimization problem

\underset{(f_{T},f_{S})\in\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}}{\inf}\quad\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Phi}(f_{T},f_{S}))+\lambda_{T}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})},

(68)

where

•

\mathrm{L}_{T}
is trend-admissible with order
N_{T}
,
\mathrm{L}_{S}
is seasonal-admissible with order
N_{S}
;

•

\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{R}^{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
is a proper, lower semi-continuous, coercive, and convex data fidelity functional;

•

\bm{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{L})
is a family of
(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})
-sensing admissible functionals (see Definition
7
) such that (i)
\bm{\Phi}(p)=\bm{0}
implies that
p=0
for any polynomial
p
such that
\mathrm{L}_{T}p=0
and (ii)
\mathcal{E}\circ\bm{\Phi}
is proper; and

•

\lambda_{T},\lambda_{S}>0
.

Then, this problem admits at least one solution, and its non-empty solution set

\mathcal{V}=\underset{(f_{T},f_{S})\in\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Phi}(f_{T},f_{S}))+\lambda_{T}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}

(69)

is convex and weak*-compact in
\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
. Moreover, any extreme point solution
(\tilde{f}_{S},\tilde{f}_{T})
is made of
\mathrm{L}_{T}
- and periodic
\mathrm{L}_{S}
-spline functions such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{T}\tilde{f}_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\delta(\cdot-u_{k})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{L}_{S}\tilde{f}_{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\mathrm{III}(\cdot-v_{k}),

(70)

where
a_{k}\neq 0
, the
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
are distinct,
b_{k}\neq 0
, and the
v_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
are distinct. Moreover, we have that
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}=0
as soon as
(N_{T},N_{S})\neq(0,0)
.
Finally, the total number of knots is bounded by

K_{T}+K_{S}\leq\begin{cases}L&\text{ if }N_{T}=N_{S}=0,\\
L+1-N_{T}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(71)

We prove Theorem
1
below. This result is the first representer theorem for functional TV-based inverse problems applicable to seasonal-trend decomposition.
The reconstructed composite signal of Theorem
1
based on
(\tilde{f}_{T},\tilde{f}_{S})\in\mathcal{V}
is then given by

\tilde{f}(t)=\tilde{f}_{T}(t)+\tilde{f}_{S}(t).

(72)

We recall that
\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}
is a Green’s function of
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}
is the periodic Green’s function of
\mathrm{L}_{S}
. The form of
\tilde{f}
can be detailed by distinguishing several cases:

•

If
N_{T}=N_{S}=0
, then

\tilde{f}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(t-u_{k})+\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(t-v_{k})

(73)

with
K_{T}+K_{S}\leq L
.

•

If
N_{T}\geq 1
, then

\tilde{f}(t)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(t-u_{k})+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}c_{n}t^{n-1}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(t-v_{k})

(74)

with
\bm{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N_{T}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{T}}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}=0
, and
K_{T}+K_{S}\leq L+1-N_{T}
.

•

If
N_{T}=0
and
N_{S}\geq 1
, then

\tilde{f}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(t-u_{k})+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(t-v_{k})+\alpha\right)

(75)

with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}=0
, and
K_{T}+K_{S}\leq L+1
.

The bound (
99
) is essentially
L+1-N_{T}
except when both
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
are invertible, in which case one can reduce the bound by
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We distinguish between three scenarios, depending on the invertibility of
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
, or equivalently on their orders
N_{T}
and
N_{S}
. These scenarios correspond to (
73
) to (
75
). We denote by
\phi_{\ell}=(g_{T,\ell},g_{S,\ell})\in\mathcal{C}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
the functionals involved in
\bm{\Phi}
.

If
N_{T}=N_{S}=0
.
This means that both
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
are invertible operators.
We define the functional
\bm{\Psi}=(\psi_{\ell}(w_{T},w_{S}))_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})
such that

\psi_{\ell}(w_{T},w_{S})=\langle w_{T},\mathrm{L}_{T}^{-1*}g_{T,\ell}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})}+\langle w_{S},\mathrm{L}_{S}^{-1*}g_{S,\ell}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}),\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})}.

(76)

Then, (
69
) is equivalent to the optimization problem

\mathcal{W}=\underset{(w_{S},w_{T})\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Psi}(w_{T},w_{S}))+\lambda_{T}\lVert w_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert w_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})},

(77)

via the identification
(w_{T},w_{S})=(\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S})
. This means that
(\tilde{f}_{T},\tilde{f}_{S})\in\mathcal{V}
if and only if
(\tilde{w}_{T},\tilde{w}_{S})=(\mathrm{L}_{T}\tilde{f}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S}\tilde{f}_{S})
is a solution of (
77
). Since the solution set of the above problem is convex and the Green’s function representations lead to splines, we conclude that
(\tilde{f}_{T},\tilde{f}_{S})
must consist of splines of the form (
73
).

If
N_{T}\geq 1
.
In this case,
\mathrm{L}_{T}
is not invertible, so we need to consider the impact of the trend regularization term on the solution. The optimization problem becomes

\mathcal{W}=\underset{(w_{S},w_{T})\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Psi}(w_{T},w_{S}))+\lambda_{T}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}w_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}w_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}.

Since the trend part
w_{T}
is regularized by
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and is allowed to contain polynomial terms of degree up to
N_{T}-1
, the solution takes the form

\tilde{f}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(t-u_{k})+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}c_{n}t^{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(t-v_{k}),

where the polynomial part corresponds to the trend component, and the seasonal component is modeled by periodic splines. The number of knots satisfies the bound
K_{T}+K_{S}\leq L+1-N_{T}
.

If
N_{T}=0
and
N_{S}\geq 1
.
In this case, the seasonal component is non-trivial while the trend component is a constant. The optimization problem reduces to

\mathcal{W}=\underset{(w_{S},w_{T})\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{E}(\bm{\Psi}(w_{T},w_{S}))+\lambda_{T}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}w_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{S}\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}w_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})},

which leads to a solution of the form

\tilde{f}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{T}}a_{k}\psi_{\mathrm{L}_{T}}(t-u_{k})+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}\rho_{\mathrm{L}_{S}}(t-v_{k})+\alpha\right),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
represents the constant trend, and the seasonal component is modeled by periodic splines with the constraint
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{S}}b_{k}=0
. The number of knots satisfies the bound
K_{T}+K_{S}\leq L+1
.

Thus, in all cases, the solution is represented as a sum of splines with a number of knots that satisfies the bounds given in (
99
). The uniqueness of the solution follows from the convexity of the problem and the fact that the functional
\mathcal{E}
is coercive and lower semi-continuous, ensuring the existence of a unique minimizer in the weak* topology. ∎∎

.

5.4
Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem for Hilbert Spaces

In this work, we consider regularizations based on total-variation norms. Its sparsity-promoting effect and its adaptiveness has proven to be efficient on several applications
[
11
,
43
,
44
,
19
]
. In this section, we consider quadratic optimization problems adapted to the seasonal-trend framework. In this setting, total-variation norms are replaced by quadratic norms and we use the quadratic data fidelity
\mathcal{E}(\bm{z})=\|\bm{y}-\bm{z}\|_{2}^{2}
, where
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
is a fixed observation vector.

For simplicity, we only consider invertible trend- and seasonal-operators
\mathrm{L}_{T}
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}
and equal regularization parameters
\lambda_{T}=\lambda_{S}=\lambda>0
. We define the native space of the optimization problem as

\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})=\mathrm{L}_{T}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\}\times\mathrm{L}_{S}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T})\},

(83)

on which
\|(f_{T},f_{S})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}=(\lVert\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\lVert\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2})^{1/2}
is a Hilbertian norm.
As we did in Proposition
2
for TV-normed spaces, one can prove that the sum
\mathrm{L}_{T}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\}\oplus\mathrm{L}_{S}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T})\}
is direct. Hence, any
f\in\mathrm{L}_{T}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\}\oplus\mathrm{L}_{S}^{-1}\{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T})\}
can be uniquely decomposed as
f=f_{T}+f_{S}
with
\mathrm{L}_{T}f_{T}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{L}_{S}f_{S}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T})
. We therefore identify
f
with its associated couple
(f_{T},f_{S})
.

Proposition 8

(Quadratic Seasonal-Trend Representer Theorem)
.

We consider the following:

•

\mathrm{L}_{T}

Proof.

We recall a classical abstract representer theorem over Hilbert spaces. We follow the presentation of

We can compare the two representer theorems,
i.e.
Theorem

and Proposition

with TV-based and

-based regularizations, respectively. We fix the pair

of regularization operators. The quadratic regularization leads to finite-dimensional approximation over a space depending on the measurement process via the

and on the regularization operators via their Green’s functions.

In addition to the other classical oppositions between

(see (

In contrast, the TV-based approach puts no intrinsic constraints and is therefore more flexible and adaptive for seasonal-trend reconstruction.

6
Grid-Based Approximation and Algorithms

The representer theorem (Theorem
1
) specifies the spline form of the extreme point solutions of seasonal-trend functional inverse problems with TV-based regularizations. It implies in particular the existence of composite spline solutions. However, Theorem
1
does not reveal how to reach a spline solution. In particular, finding the knot positions
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
of the trend- and seasonal-components in (
98
) is especially challenging.

Several algorithmic approaches have been proposed to solve TV-based optimization problems (see Section
1.2
). In this work, we adapt existing grid-based methods to the case of seasonal-trend reconstruction and study their convergence properties. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of the convergence of grid-based methods to the case of a composite signal model.

6.2

The difference between Theorem

6.3
Uniform Convergence of Grid-Based Solutions

The weak*-convergence results of Theorem
2
have interesting consequences for sampling-admissible operator pairs

\mathcal{M}{(\mathrm{L}_{T},\mathrm{L}_{S})}
implies pointwise convergence.
Moreover, this pointwise convergence can be reinforced into a uniform convergence in compact domains under certain conditions
[

For any locally bounded function

Remarks.

Theorem
3
generalizes
[
16
, Theorem 2]
, which only considers (i) a purely periodic setting (single component reconstruction), (ii) Fourier measurements, and (iii) differential operators.
It provides general conditions for the convergence of grid-based methods towards their continuous-domain target problem and is, as far as we know, the first result of that kind with this level of generality.

In the context of seasonal-trend decomposition, it remarkably shows that both the seasonal and trend components are recovered by their respective grid-based approximations. This demonstrates the relevance of grid-based methods for composite signal reconstruction, which is also a novelty to the best of our knowledge.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have developed a variational framework for the reconstruction of 1D continuous-domain functions, decomposed into trend and seasonal components, using generalized total-variation regularization. We introduced a functional-analytic approach to sparsity-promoting reconstruction, particularly suited for inverse problems with noisy linear measurements. Key contributions include the derivation of a representer theorem, the analysis of multi-component TV regularization, and the development of grid-based approximation schemes that converge to the continuous-domain solutions.

The seasonal-trend decomposition model enables efficient recovery of both components by combining data fidelity with structural regularization. Our representer theorem characterizes the solutions as sparse splines, and the grid-based methods provide an efficient computational approach with uniform convergence to the continuous-domain solutions.

This work lays the foundation for further research in 1D signal decomposition and offers practical methods for real-world applications in time-series analysis and other similar domains where noisy, high-dimensional data are prevalent. The rigorous theory and computational methods presented here make significant strides in advancing the understanding and application of seasonal-trend decomposition.

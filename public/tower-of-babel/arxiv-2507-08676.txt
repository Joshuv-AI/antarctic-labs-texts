Magic Steady State Production: Non-Hermitian and Stochastic pathways
Pablo Martinez-Azcona, Matthieu Sarkis, Alexandre Tkatchenko, Aurélia Chenu

Abstract
Universal quantum computers require states with both entanglement and non-stabilizerness, the latter property being commonly known as quantum magic . Here, we introduce a protocol that prepares magic steady states by leveraging non-Hermitian dynamics, which, contrary to unitary dynamics, can host pure-state attractors. We investigate the dissipative qubit, in which we find the optimal parameters to prepare | H ⟩ \ket{H} and | T ⟩ \ket{T} states. Interestingly, this approach does not require knowledge or preparation of an initial state, since all the states of the Bloch sphere converge to these steady states. We also consider the addition of classical noise in the anti-hermitian part, and show that it does not necessarily hinder magic, rather the single trajectories show concentration around the magic steady state value.



Exploiting the quantumness of matter to build universal quantum computers has become a holy grail of quantum information. To this end, entanglement is insufficient, and magic states are key for quantum information protocols. The ‘magic’, or non-stabilizerness, allows transcending the limitations of classical simulations.
This can be formalized in the stabilizer framework, which shows that Clifford gates and Pauli measurements alone can be efficiently simulated classically
Gottesman 1998a
. The resource theory of magic shows that free states are convex mixtures of stabilizer states (the stabilizer polytope) and free operations are Clifford channels that preserve it
Veitch et al. 2014
.
Quantum advantage
thus requires non-stabilizerness either through the implementation of
T
gates, or using
magic
states
Bravyi and Kitaev 2005
. Similarly, a violation of CHSH inequalities requires magic
Cusumano et al. 2025
. Several monotones have been introduced to measure non-stabilizerness: such as the robustness of magic
Howard and Campbell 2017
, the discrete Wigner negativity, or
mana

Howard et al. 2014
, and more recently, the
stabilizer Renyi entropy
(SRE)
Leone et al. 2022
which is an efficiently computable, experimentally accessible
Oliviero et al. 2022
, magic monotone
Leone and Bittel 2024
based on the Renyi entropy of the Pauli spectrum.

To convert faulty magic states into high-fidelity ones, one can resort to
magic state distillation
(MSD). Bravyi and Kitaev’s foundational protocol distills magic from noisy ancillas using only Clifford operations above a fidelity threshold
Bravyi and Kitaev 2005
. Subsequent advances include triorthogonal‐code schemes achieving polylogarithmic overhead
Bravyi and Haah 2012
, four-qubit code routines reducing space cost
Meier et al. 2012
, and asymptotically optimal CSS-code methods with linear input scaling
Haah et al. 2017
. Experimental MSD has recently been realized on neutral-atom platforms
Rodriguez et al. 2024
.

While dissipation typically erodes quantum coherence,
engineered dissipation
can stabilize target states as unique steady states of a Lindbladian
Verstraete et al. 2009
. Lin
et al.
demonstrated this by dissipatively preparing Bell pairs in trapped ions
Lin et al. 2013
. Indeed, measurement-induced phase transitions in random circuits have been shown to host a “magic-rich” phase, where repeated stabilizer measurements balance coherent errors to yield high steady-state magic
Niroula et al. 2024
. There has been a flurry of work studying the behavior of magic in different many-body systems
Turkeshi et al. 2025a
;
Sierant et al. 2025
;
Turkeshi et al. 2025b
;
Bera and Schirò 2025
;
Jasser et al. 2025
;
Falcão et al. 2025
, in conformal field theory
White et al. 2021
, in molecular bonding
Sarkis and Tkatchenko 2025
, as well as in some open quantum systems
Sticlet et al. 2025
;
Trigueros and Guzmán 2025
.

Here, we take a different approach and exploit the unique features of non-Hermitian (NH) systems to propose a protocol that generates magic states in the steady state.
Open quantum systems
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
;
Rivas and Huelga 2012
can be described by effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonians when conditioning a continuously monitored system on no quantum jumps
Wiseman and Milburn 2009
;
Jacobs 2014
;
Wang and Clerk 2019
. The spectra of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians is complex except in regimes protected by
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry
Bender and Boettcher 1998
, or generally pseudo-hermiticity
Mostafazadeh 2002
. Stochastic Hamiltonians provide an alternative model of environmental effects
Budini 2001
;
Kiely 2021
, which connects to quantum simulation
Chenu et al. 2017
and information scrambling
Martinez-Azcona et al. 2023
. In particular, in recent work we analyzed stochastic perturbations in the anti-Hermitian part of the Hamiltonian, leading to an
antidephasing
master equation
Martinez-Azcona et al. 2025
. A key hallmark of non-Hermitian physics is the existence of exceptional points, where eigenvalues and eigenvectors coalesce
Abbasi et al. 2022
, which allows to enhance the speed of entangling two qubits
Li et al. 2023
. Quantum‐trajectory experiments have recently realized and probed NH evolution in purely quantum, single‐qubit systems, such as superconducting qubits
Naghiloo et al. 2019
, or trapped ions
Quinn et al. 2023
. Furthermore, there are recent proposals to avoid post-selection by leveraging the symplectic transformations of creation and annihilation operators
Wakefield et al. 2024
or even non-inertial frames of a quantum clock
Paiva et al. 2022
. A NH version of the boson sampling problem has been recently studied
Mochizuki and Hamazaki 2023
, as well as the computational complexity of generic non-Hermitian evolution
Barch and Lidar 2025
.

Here, we explore a minimal realization of these ideas in a single qubit governed by a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, which can be subject to noise in its decay rate.
In the deterministic case, we find an analytical expression for the SRE of the steady state in the
\mathcal{PT}
broken phase of the model, and compute the optimal values of the parameters to prepare
\ket{H}
and
\ket{T}
states, in different conditions.
For the stochastic dissipative qubit we derive analytic expressions for its steady state and the corresponding SRE as a function of the noise strength
\gamma
and decay rate
\Gamma

Martinez-Azcona et al. 2025
and show that antihermitian noise can preserve and even enhance the generation of magic. Our results demonstrate a concrete pathway to
noise and non-Hermiticity-enhanced
quantum magic generation, turning environmental decoherence into an asset for possibly fault-tolerant architectures.

Non-stabilizerness and stabilizer Renyi entropy
—
Let us first briefly recall concepts of non-stabilizerness. Stabilizer states are generated through Clifford operations, namely elements of the Pauli group’s normalizer. Denoting a generic Pauli string as

\hat{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}\hat{\sigma}_{\mu_{i}}
,
\forall\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\{0,1,2,3\}^{L}
, where
\hat{\sigma}_{0}
denotes the identity matrix.
Since Clifford gates can be simulated in polynomial time on a classical computer via the Gottesman–Knill theorem
Gottesman 1997
;
Gottesman 1998b
;
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
, entanglement alone cannot guarantee a quantum speedup
Bravyi and Kitaev 2005
;
Howard et al. 2014
.
To recover universal quantum computing, we then have to go beyond stabilizer states and Clifford operations. This can be achieved by using non-Clifford gates such as
T
gates or by using non-stabilizer,
magic
, states
Bravyi and Kitaev 2005
. In the qubit example, stabilizer states belong to the octahedron delimited by the Pauli matrices eigenstates (cf. Fig.
1
a,b), and prototypical instances of non-stabilizer states are the
\ket{H}=\cos\left(\tfrac{\pi}{8}\right)|0\rangle+\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi}{8}\right)|1\rangle
and
\ket{T}=\cos\beta\ket{0}+e^{i\pi/4}\sin\beta\ket{1}
states
Bravyi and Kitaev 2005
,
where the angle
\beta
is determined by
\cos(2\beta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
.

Magic can be quantified by various monotones that cannot increase under stabilizer operations. One particularly convenient measure is the stabilizer Renyi entropy (SRE)
Leone et al. 2022
;
Haug and Piroli 2023
, which can be viewed as the spread of the quantum state measured by the
L^{p}
-norms of the coefficients in the Pauli basis. More specifically, for any
L
-qubit operator, decomposed onto the Pauli basis as
\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\{0,1,2,3\}^{L}}\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\hat{\rho}\rangle\,\hat{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
,
the stabilizer Renyi entropy (SRE) reads

M_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho})=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log_{2}\left[\frac{1}{2^{L}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\{0,1,2,3\}^{L}}\left|\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\hat{\rho}\rangle\right|^{2\alpha}\right]\,.

(1)

The bracket denotes the standard Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
\langle A,B\rangle:=\text{Tr}\left(A^{\dagger}B\right)
.
It was shown that, for
\alpha\geq 2
, the SRE is a genuine non-stabilizerness mononote for pure states
Leone and Bittel 2024
. The case of mixed states would, as usual, require a convex roof extension of the pure state SRE. However for
\alpha=2
, it was argued in
Leone et al. 2022
;
Ding et al. 2025
that a reasonable non-stabilizerness proxy for mixed states can be obtained by substracting away the contribution from the Renyi entropy
S_{2}(\hat{\rho})=-\log_{2}(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho}^{2}))
, namely
\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})=M_{2}(\hat{\rho})-S_{2}(\hat{\rho})
.
In the case of a single qubit, the corrected stabilizer Renyi entropy
\tilde{M}_{2}
simplifies to

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})=-\log_{2}\left[\frac{1+x^{4}+y^{4}+z^{4}}{1+x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}\right]\,.

(2)

This provides a compact measure of the state’s magic in terms of its Bloch coordinates
\boldsymbol{r}=(x,y,z)
. We refer the reader to the Appendices for details concerning the single-qubit SRE, including a derivation of an upper bound for
\tilde{M}_{2}
. We now present how to generate magic states in a qubit through non-Hermitian dynamics.

Generating magic in a dissipative Qubit
—
As briefly mentioned, NH systems have unique features. One of them is to have attractor states.
We now present how to exploit this feature for the generation of a high-magic state. Specifically, we consider a generic dissipative qubit with Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=J_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}+J_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y}+(\delta-i\Gamma)\hat{\Pi}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Upsilon\\
\Upsilon^{*}&\delta-i\Gamma\end{pmatrix},

(3)

with
\Upsilon=J_{x}-iJ_{y}
and where
\hat{\Pi}=\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{\sigma}_{+}=\ket{e}\bra{e}
is the projector over the first excited state.
This effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian has been successfully implemented experimentally
Naghiloo et al. 2019
.
Solving
(H-\varepsilon_{\pm})\ket{\psi_{\pm}}=0
gives the eigenvalues as
\varepsilon_{\pm}=\frac{\delta-i\Gamma}{2}\pm\sqrt{|\Upsilon|^{2}+\big(\frac{\delta-i\Gamma}{2}\big)^{2}}
.

The right eigenstates can be simply written as
\ket{\psi_{\pm}}=(\Upsilon,\varepsilon_{\pm})^{\intercal}/\sqrt{|\Upsilon|^{2}+|\varepsilon_{\pm}|^{2}}
. The eigenstate with the slowest decay
\ket{\psi_{+}}
has Bloch coordinates given by
r_{i}=\braket{\psi_{+}|\hat{\sigma}_{i}|\psi_{+}}
that read

x=\frac{2\mathrm{Re}(\varepsilon_{+}^{*}\Upsilon)}{|\Upsilon|^{2}+|\varepsilon_{+}|^{2}},\,y=-\frac{2\mathrm{Im}(\varepsilon_{+}^{*}\Upsilon)}{|\Upsilon|^{2}+|\varepsilon_{+}|^{2}},\,z=\frac{|\Upsilon|^{2}-|\varepsilon_{+}|^{2}}{|\Upsilon|^{2}+|\varepsilon_{+}|^{2}}.

(4)

The SRE for the steady state is therefore

\!M_{2}\!=\!-\!\log_{2}\!\left[\!\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\frac{2^{4}(\mathrm{Re}(\varepsilon_{+}^{*}\!\Upsilon)^{4}\!+\!\mathrm{Im}(\varepsilon_{+}^{*}\!\Upsilon)^{4})\!+\!(|\Upsilon|^{2}\!-\!|\varepsilon_{+}|^{2})^{4}}{2(|\Upsilon|^{2}+|\varepsilon_{+}|^{2})^{4}}\!\right]\!

(5)

Let us look at particular cases to understand this expression better.

\bullet

Case
\Upsilon=J
,
\delta=0
: In this case eq. (
5
) reduces to

M_{2}=-\log_{2}\left(1-\frac{4J^{2}(\Gamma^{2}-4J^{2})}{\Gamma^{4}}\right).

(6)

Its maximum value of
\log_{2}(4/3)\approx 0.415\equiv M_{2}^{(H)}
is for
\Gamma=2\sqrt{2}J
at which value the
\ket{\psi_{+}}
eigenstate corresponds to an
\ket{H}
state–see Fig.
1
(a;c), particularly that with
y=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\,z=+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. In the large decay limit, the SRE obeys the asymptotic expansion
M_{2}\sim 4J^{2}/(\ln(2)\Gamma^{2})
.

Generating magic in a stochastic dissipative Qubit
—
The effect of noise in the anti-Hermitian part of the Hamiltonian has been studied in
Martinez-Azcona et al. 2025
, where it was shown that the average evolution follows an
antidephasing
master equation that enriches the possible steady states. We now investigate the effect of noise in the generation of magical states. We consider the
stochastic dissipative qubit
(SDQ)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\textsc{sdq}}_{t}=J_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}+J_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y}-i\Gamma(1+\sqrt{2\gamma}\xi_{t})\hat{\Pi}.

(8)

This system, compared to (
3
), has Gaussian fluctuations—described through the Wiener process
\mathrm{d}W_{t}=\xi_{t}\mathrm{d}t
that follows Itō rule
\mathrm{d}W^{2}_{t}=\mathrm{d}t
—in the decay parameter
\Gamma
, with strength
\gamma
. This Hamiltonian generates a density matrix
\tilde{\varrho}_{\mathrm{d}t}=\hat{U}_{\mathrm{d}t}\hat{\rho}_{0}\hat{U}_{\mathrm{d}t}^{\dagger}
that is not normalized (as highlighted with the tilde
\tilde{\bullet}
) and that depends on single realizations of the noise
\mathrm{d}W_{t}
. After renormalization, the state of the system
\hat{\varrho}_{t}=\tilde{\varrho}_{t}/\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\varrho}_{t})
follows the nonlinear stochastic master equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\hat{\varrho}_{t}=(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}[\hat{\varrho}_{t}]\!-\!\langle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\rangle_{\!\hat{\varrho}_{t}}\hat{\varrho}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}[\hat{\varrho}_{t}]\!-\!\langle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\rangle_{\!\hat{\varrho}_{t}}\hat{\varrho}_{t})\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(9)

where the anti-dephasing Liouvillian and the measurement superoperator respectively read
Martinez-Azcona et al. 2025

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}[\bullet]

\displaystyle=-i[\hat{H}_{0},\bullet]-\{\hat{L},\bullet\}+\gamma\{\hat{L},\{\hat{L},\bullet\}\},

(10a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}[\bullet]

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2\gamma}\{\hat{L},\bullet\}.

(10b)

We introduced
\hat{H}_{0}=J_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}+J_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y}
and
\hat{L}=\Gamma\hat{\Pi}
, and denote the expectation value of a superoperator as
\langle\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\rangle_{\hat{\rho}}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}[\hat{\rho}])
.

For each single trajectory, the density matrix of the SDQ,
\hat{\varrho}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\mathbb{1}}+\boldsymbol{{R}}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
, is governed by the stochastic master equation (
9
). It can be expressed as a system of coupled SDE’s for the Bloch coordinates
\boldsymbol{{R}}=({X},\,{Y},\,{Z})
, namely

\!\!\begin{cases}\!\mathrm{d}{X}\!=\!\left(2J_{y}{Z}\!-\!(\gamma\Gamma^{2}+{Z}\tfrac{JB}{2}){X}\right)\mathrm{d}t\!-\!\sqrt{2\gamma}\Gamma{X}{Z}\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
\!\mathrm{d}{Y}\!=\!\left(-2J_{x}{Z}\!-\!(\gamma\Gamma^{2}+{Z}\tfrac{JB}{2}){Y}\right)\mathrm{d}t\!-\!\sqrt{2\gamma}\Gamma{Y}\!{Z}\mathrm{d}W_{t},\hbox to0pt{\vbox to0pt{\pgfpicture\makeatletter\hbox{\hskip 0.0pt\lower 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} \lxSVG@begingroup@{stroke} \lxSVG@begingroup@{fill} \lxSVG@setlinewidth{\the\pgflinewidth}\lxSVG@begingroup@{stroke-width} \lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nullfont\hbox to0.0pt{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} {{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}{{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hbox{\hbox{{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} {{}{{}}{}{}}
\lxSVG@closescope }}}
}
\lxSVG@closescope \hbox to0.0pt{}{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}\hss}\lxSVG@discardpath\lxSVG@closescope \hss}}\lxSVG@closescope\endpgfpicture}}\\
\!\mathrm{d}{Z}\!=\!\left(2(J_{x}{Y}-J_{y}{X})\!-\!\tfrac{JB}{2}\!({Z}^{2}\!-\!1)\right)\!\mathrm{d}t\!-\!\sqrt{2\gamma}\Gamma({Z}^{2}\!-\!1)\mathrm{d}W_{t},\end{cases}

where
JB=2\Gamma(1-2\gamma\Gamma)
is a constant.
Averaging over the noise leads to a closed master equation for the normalized density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{t}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{\varrho}_{t})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbb{E}(\tilde{\varrho}_{t}))}

Martinez-Azcona et al. 2025

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{\rho}_{t}\!=

\displaystyle-\!i[J_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}\!+\!J_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y},\hat{\rho}_{t}]\!-\!(\Gamma\!-\!\gamma\Gamma^{2})\{\hat{\Pi},\hat{\rho}_{t}\}\!+\!2\gamma\Gamma^{2}\hat{\Pi}\hat{\rho}_{t}\hat{\Pi}

(11)

\displaystyle+\!(2\Gamma-4\gamma\Gamma^{2})\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\Pi}\hat{\rho}_{t})\hat{\rho}_{t}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}[\hat{\rho}_{t}]-\langle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\rangle_{\hat{\rho}_{t}}\hat{\rho}_{t}.

(12)

Writing the average state as
\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\mathbb{1}}+\boldsymbol{r}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
, this master equation can be expressed as a set of coupled ODE’s for the Bloch coordinates
\boldsymbol{r}=(x,y,z)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}=\left(2J_{y}z-(\gamma\Gamma^{2}+z\frac{JB}{2})x\right),\\
\dot{y}=\left(-2J_{x}z-(\gamma\Gamma^{2}+z\frac{JB}{2})y\right),\\
\dot{z}=\left(2(J_{x}y-J_{y}x)-\frac{JB}{2}(z^{2}\!-\!1)\right).\end{cases}

(13)

(
10a
) dictates the state evolution before renormalization, taken care with the last line of Eq. (

). We use the right eigendecomposition of this non-trace-preserving operator to find its eigenvalues

whose eigenvalue has the largest real part

—assuming it is unique

; it is reached in a time governed by the dissipative gap

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho}^{\textsc{s}})=\log_{2}\left(\frac{1+\lambda_{0}^{4}\frac{(\lambda_{0}-AJ)^{4}+2^{4}J^{4}}{(4J^{2}+\lambda_{0}(\lambda_{0}-AJ))^{4}}}{1+\lambda_{0}^{2}\frac{(\lambda_{0}-AJ)^{2}+4J^{2}}{(4J^{2}+\lambda_{0}(\lambda_{0}-AJ))^{2}}}\right).

(14)

with the constant
AJ=\Gamma(1-\gamma\Gamma)
.

Quantum jumps hinder non-stabilizerness
— We now look at a more general framework and discuss the effect of quantum jumps on magic.
Consider a generic system of
L
qubits with density matrix
\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{2^{L}}(\hat{\mathbb{1}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}c_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\hat{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
, expressed in the set of Pauli strings
\{\hat{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\}
. When each qubit interacts locally with its environment, the system evolves according to the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) master equation,

\partial_{t}\hat{\rho}_{t}=-i[\hat{H},\hat{\rho}_{t}]+\sum_{k=1}^{L}(\mathcal{D}_{\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{k}}[\hat{\rho}_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{k}}[\hat{\rho}_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{k}}[\hat{\rho}_{t}]),

(16)

where the three dissipators describe emission, absorption, and dephasing, respectively.
The dissipators
\mathcal{D}_{\hat{X}}[\bullet]:=\gamma_{\hat{X}}\left(\hat{X}\bullet\hat{X}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{X}^{\dagger}\hat{X},\bullet\}\right)
are composed of a
jump term

\hat{X}\bullet\hat{X}^{\dagger}
and an anti-Hermitian term
-\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{X}^{\dagger}\hat{X},\bullet\}
. Non-stabilizerness requires the state to be spread over many different Pauli strings. However, the jump term associated to emission or absorption destroys many of these Pauli strings since

\hat{\sigma}_{k}^{\pm}\hat{\sigma}_{k}^{(x,y)}\hat{\sigma}_{k}^{\mp}=0
and only acts non-trivially on
\hat{\sigma}_{k}^{z}
and
\hat{\mathbb{1}}_{k}
.
So jump terms drastically reduce the complexity of Pauli strings, thus reducing the magic.
This is why non-Hermitian evolution, which by construction has no jumps, can be advantageous for generating magical states. We thus expect that the advantage demonstrated in the single qubit example holds in more general setups.

Conclusions
—This Letter shows how to exploit the dynamics generated by a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian for the analog preparation of magic steady states. Looking at the single qubit example, we found the optimal parameters to prepare the
\ket{H}
and
\ket{T}
states. In case the qubit experiences noise in its decay rate, such anti-Hermitian noise is not necessarily detrimental. Rather, it can improve non-stabilizerness and enhance the value of the SRE—cf. Fig.
2
. Looking at the single quantum trajectories, we observe a two-stroke behavior in the evolution of the SRE, and found a steady state that concentrates around its maximum value.

A remarkable feature of the proposed approach over standard MSD algorithms is that
any
state, even the maximally mixed one, can be turned into a magical state—not only those with a certain amount of magic. This ‘magic-charged’ qubit can then be interfaced via standard Clifford swaps or teleportation to inject the distilled magic into any target qubit. This modular approach supports parallel charging of ancillas and decouples high-overhead non-Clifford preparation from the main computation, while keeping all transfer steps within the efficient stabilizer framework.

Appendix C
Numerical Solution of Stochastic Differential Equations

To solve the system of coupled nonlinear SDE’s for the Bloch coordinates we use the
explicit order 1.5 strong scheme
by Kloeden and Platen, see Chapt. 11.2 (eqs 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 page 378) of
Kloeden and Platen 1992
. For an Itō SDE of the form

\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{Y})\mathrm{d}t+\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{Y})\mathrm{d}W_{t}

(32)

we find its numerical solution by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{n+1}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{n}+\mathbf{b}\Delta W+\frac{\mathbf{a}(\Upupsilon_{+})-\mathbf{a}(\Upupsilon_{-})}{2\sqrt{\Delta t}}\Delta Z+\frac{\mathbf{a}(\Upupsilon_{+})+2\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{a}(\Upupsilon_{-})}{4}\Delta t+\frac{\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{+})-\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{-})}{4\sqrt{\Delta t}}(\Delta W^{2}-\Delta t)

(33)

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{+})-2\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{-})}{2\Delta t}(\Delta W\Delta t-\Delta Z)+\frac{\mathbf{b}(\Phi_{+})-\mathbf{b}(\Phi_{-})-\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{+})+\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{-})}{4\Delta t}\left(\frac{\Delta W^{2}}{3}-\Delta t\right)\Delta W,

(34)

where
\mathbf{a}\equiv\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{Y}_{n}),\;\mathbf{b}\equiv\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{Y}_{n})
,
\Delta t
is the time step, the auxiliary variables are

\Upupsilon_{\pm}=\mathbf{Y}_{n}+\mathbf{a}\Delta t\pm\mathbf{b}\sqrt{\Delta t},\qquad\Phi_{\pm}=\Upupsilon_{+}\pm\mathbf{b}(\Upupsilon_{+})\sqrt{\Delta t},

and the noises
\Delta W,\;\Delta Z
are found from the transformation (eq. 4.3 pg 352)

\Delta W=U_{1}\sqrt{\Delta t},\qquad\Delta Z=\frac{\Delta t^{3/2}}{2}(U_{1}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}U_{2}),

where
U_{1},U_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
are i.i.d. normal random variables.

Appendix D
SRE for a single qubit and a simple upper bound

In what follows we focus on the single-qubit case, though the derivation can be straightforwardly generalized to an arbitrary number of qubits. As explained in the bulk of the paper, in the case of a mixed state and for
\alpha=2
, one substracts away the contribution from the Renyi entropy
S_{2}(\hat{\rho})
and defines:

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})=M_{2}(\hat{\rho})-S_{2}(\hat{\rho})\,.

(35)

The second Renyi entropy can be straightforwardly expressed as:

S_{2}(\hat{\rho})=-\log_{2}\text{Tr}\left(\hat{\rho}^{2}\right)=-\log_{2}\left[\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\langle\sigma_{\mu},\hat{\rho}\rangle\langle\sigma_{\nu},\hat{\rho}\rangle\langle\sigma_{\mu},\sigma_{\nu}\rangle\right]=-\log_{2}\left[\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\mu}\langle\sigma_{\mu},\hat{\rho}\rangle^{2}\right]\,,

(36)

leading to:

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})=-\log_{2}\left[\frac{\sum_{\mu}\langle\sigma_{\mu},\hat{\rho}\rangle^{4}}{\sum_{\mu}\langle\sigma_{\mu},\hat{\rho}\rangle^{2}}\right]\,.

(37)

Consider a single qubit state

\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{n},\hat{\rho}\rangle\hat{\sigma}_{n}=:\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}c_{n}\hat{\sigma}_{n}

(38)

the stabilizer renyi entropy
M_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho})
is given by

M_{\alpha}(\hat{\rho})=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{3}|c_{n}|^{2\alpha}\right)\,.

(39)

However, as mentioned above, for mixed states one substracts the Renyi entropy
S_{2}(\hat{\rho})
, giving

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})=-\log_{2}\left(\frac{\sum_{n}|c_{n}|^{4}}{\sum_{k}|c_{k}|^{2}}\right)

(40)

We therefore have the following expression of the SRE for a single-qubit mixed state:

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})=-\log_{2}x

(41)

with the following ratio of
L^{p}
norms:

x:=\frac{\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\lVert_{4}^{4}}{\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\lVert_{2}^{2}}

(42)

We provide two lower bounds for
x
and therefore two upper bounds
\tilde{M}_{2}
. The first bound depends on the state itself through its Renyi entropy, in the spirit of the upper bounds known in the literature for the mana. The second bound is universal and does not depend on the entropy.

Entropy-dependent bound
—
Recall that norms in a
n
-dimensional vector space satisfy the following inequality:

\lVert\bm{c}\lVert_{r}\leq n^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}\lVert\bm{c}\lVert_{p}\,,

(43)

with
0<r<p
, giving:

\lVert\bm{c}\lVert_{4}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\lVert\bm{c}\lVert_{2}\,.

(44)

We then obtain directly that:

x\geq\frac{\lVert\bm{c}\lVert_{2}^{2}}{4}\,,

(45)

and in turn that:

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})\leq S_{2}(\hat{\rho})+1\,.

(46)

Entropy-independent bound
—
Let us now turn to the universal bound. First note that by normalization of the state, one has
c_{0}=1
. Let us set

y=c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}\geq 0\,.

(47)

The quadratic mean–arithmetic mean inequality gives immediately that

c_{1}^{4}+c_{2}^{4}+c_{3}^{4}\geq\frac{y^{2}}{3}\,,

(48)

leading to the following bound:

x\geq\frac{1+\frac{y^{2}}{3}}{1+y}\,.

(49)

We find that the right-hand-side is extremized for
y=1
, and we therefore obtain:

x\geq\frac{2}{3}\,,

(50)

and in turn:

\tilde{M}_{2}(\hat{\rho})\leq\log_{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\simeq 0.58496(3)\,.

(51)

This value is actually attained by the
T
-state, and is therefore the tighest bound for a single-qubit system.

Computational study of unsteady Homann type flow of ternary hybrid nanofluid in the presence of induced magnetic forces and time relaxation
Authors: Latif Ahmad, Asghar Ali Khan, Saleem Javed, Umair Khan, Jomana A. Bashatah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Thermal and mass management is one of the major components of material processing in various productive industries. A significant predictive analytical way is presented in this work to predict the heat and mass exchange during many industrial processes. More specifically, this work is very relevant to exploring the physics of ternary hybrid nanofluid (THNF), induced magnetic field, first-order chemical reaction, Darcy Forchheimer effects, suction/injection, modified heat and mass fluxes effects. The convective surface constraints are imposed to address the surface behavior of the dynamic disk. The typical potential Homann-type flow equations and the thermal and mass balance aspects are mathematically expressed via a set of nonlinear flow, thermal, mass, and induced magnetic field equations. However, the material composition is based on the effective inclusion of nanoparticles, CoFe2O4, ZnO and Au in the base liquid ethylene glycols. The influence of such physical factors is described through explicit graphical and numerical tables, while using the modified collocation method. Moreover, the valid behaviors of each controlling parameter are presented through the THNF temperature, concentration, velocity, resistive forces, and induced magnetic field. The ratio of stress and strain caused a significant enhancement in the flow components of the material. The thermal expansion factor reduced the material temperature significantly away from the surface. The Darcy and non-Darcy Forchiemer factors both cause a reduction in the flow speed of the composite THNF materials. The ratio of strain rate and disk linear deformation enhanced the flow speed, and a reduction is noted for the higher Eckert and thermal relaxation factor. The Biot number appeared in the surface condition, which decreased the thermal trend of the material. The time relaxation for the mass fraction factor diminished the material concentration, and the same is enhanced via the first chemical reaction factor. An authentic and justified comparison is generated to show the validity of the numerical method.

Introduction

Homann-type flow is a kind of liquid flow that happens when a fluid moves straight toward a flat surface and spreads out evenly in all directions. It is often used to study how fluids behave near the point where they hit a surface, especially in axisymmetric (round and even) situations. This type of flow helps scientists to understand things like heat transfer and pressure near the front of objects. It finds application in such fields as aircraft nose design, cooling systems and flow analyses in engines. There are a few significant engineering applications of Homann-type flow because it is applicable in the axisymmetric stagnation-point flow. Some of its major applications are aerodynamics, cooling turbine blades, jet impingement cooling, cooling of electronics, combustion chamber, rocket and missiles, re-entry vehicle design, heat exchangers and biomedical. Maatoug et al. [1] studied the bioconvective Homann flow of a tangent non-linear nanoparticle-enhanced fluid due to the movement of a spiraling disk and no mass transfer conditions. Ahmad et al. [2] investigated the effect of dissipative irregularities on the homann flow of walters-B fluid with special reference to the solar thermal energy absorption. Sarfraz et al. [3] investigated the significance of ternary hybrid nanofluids (THNF) in the enhancement of thermal energy transfer in the stagnation-point of flow over spiraling disk in Homann. Kumar et al. [4] treated the dynamics of MHD Darcy-Forchheimer flow with nano-enhanced fluid by SWCNT-H2O and on a porous and extensible surface. Zhang et al. [5] investigated the radial hydraulic forces in centrifugal pumps using hierarchical clustering (HC) algorithms to analyze and interpret pump performance.

A porous disk is a flat and a circular object that is manufactured using a material that has a lot of small holes or openings through which fluid could pass through. The passage of the fluid through a porous disk is controlled by the interaction of the fluid and the porous medium leading to complicated patterns of pressure and velocity distributions. The porous nature also influences the movement of the fluid, which in most cases brings about resistance and alters the flow behavior which would not be the same as that of flow over a hard surface. The flow across porous disks is studied to gain some insight into the basic principles of fluid dynamics, including permeability, drag and pressure drop across porous materials. Porous disks are used in engineering applications such as filtration, fuel cells, chemical reactors, cooling systems, aerodynamic testing, flow measurement devices, heat exchangers, gas diffusion layers, environmental engineering, and biomedical devices to control and distribute fluid flow effectively. Noriega et al. [6] conducted a scaling analysis on the rotating wake produced by a porous disc, applied to wind turbine technology. Balla et al. [7] carried out a study on bioconvection in a porous square enclosure saturated with oxytactic microorganisms, considering the effects of thermal radiation. Dimartino et al. [8] studied the internal pore structure and performance of 3D-printed permeable polymer materials utilized in chromatography. Zenkour et al. [9] analyzed rotating annular piezoelectric porous disks that have exponentially graded thickness variations. Paul et al. [10] studied how non-Newtonian engine oil-based THNF behaves under mixed convection when flowing across a spinning porous disk.

The Cattaneo-Christov models (C–C) are refined versions of traditional heat and mass transfer laws that account for limited rates of thermal and concentration wave transmission by introducing relaxation time parameters. This is in contrast to the Fourier and Fick laws, which are not the case, which are built on the assumption of infinite-speed diffusion, the models use a convicted time derivative to be consistent with frame-invariant properties. This makes them more physical, particularly in situations of fast moving or non-equilibrium processes. By overcoming the limitations inherent in traditional models. In engineering, this models are used in heat management technologies, such as thermal transfer units and cooling of electronic devices, and in chemical process engineering to model reactive flows and diffusion with finite relaxation times. The ATSS model was examined by Razaq et al. [11] using the CC approach to thermal analysis, focusing on ternary nanomaterial flows optimized for entropy and influenced by simultaneous homogeneous and heterogeneous reactions. Mishra et al. [12] described a biosensing and solar thermal experiment of the bioconvection Ree Eyring model in which the heat flux is C–C and motile microorganisms are used on an elongating surface. The KHA model, comprising MoS₄ and CoFe₂O₃ in engine oil, was studied by Khan et al. [13] within the framework of non-similar Darcy–Forchheimer flow, incorporating entropy effects and C–C heat conduction. In their study, Shamshuddin et al. [14] explored the dynamics of Casson THNF under different thermal and physical conditions, applying the C–C heat flux formulation to assess improved heat transfer on a stretching surface. Akinbo and Olajuwon [15] examined the role of the C–C thermal flux framework, incorporating thermal energy production and absorption effects alongside chemical reactions in Walters’ B fluid flowing through a porous medium subjected to Newtonian heating. In their study, Hussain et al. [16] analyzed thermal augmentation and the C–C theory of mass and thermal transport in the 3D MHD flow of a Jeffrey hybrid nanoparticle-enhanced fluid across a bi-directionally moving surface with convective boundary effects.

Hybrid nanofluids consist of nanoparticles of two separate kinds suspended in a carrier fluid, enhancing heat conduction and heat transfer more effectively than fluids with just one type of nanoparticle. Their improved stability and performance make them ideal for use in cooling technologies, electronics, and non-conventional energy systems. Hybrid nanofluids are widely used in cooling systems, electronics, and renewable energy to enhance heat transfer efficiency. They also improve performance in automotive and industrial heat management applications. The Faridi et al. Mishra et al. [21] introduced the study of the Marangoni boundary layer flow of electrically conducting hybrid nanofluid in the presence of the influence of particle shape and thermal radiation. Guedri et al. Fu et al. [23] discussed the principles, practical uses as well as key challenges of using nanofluids to improve oil recovery processes.

Ternary hybrid nanofluids are emerging class of engineered fluids composed of a base fluid infused with three different types of nanoparticles. This multi-component approach allows for a synergistic enhancement in heat conduction, heat transfer efficiency, and fluid stability compared to conventional and binary nanofluids. The use of complementary properties in nanoparticles by careful selection, e.g., metals, metal oxides and carbon-based materials THNF can provide better performance even in demanding thermal systems. They promise a lot in high efficiency cooling, renewable energy systems and high-tech thermal management operations. Arif et al. A numerical study on the behavior of heat and mass transfer of THNF flow by Haq et al. [28] was conducted with the effects of activation energy taken into consideration. Shah et al. The article by Sadiya et al. [31] talked about the Computational analysis of MHD ternary hybrid nanofluid flow with thermochemical reaction growth on rotating stretching sheet by porous medium.

The magnetic field has a very important influence on the behavior of electrically conducting fluids in fluid dynamics due to the existence of Lorentz forces, which can alter the flow structure and stability. As the fluid passes through an external magnetic field this results in another magnetic field at the expense of the motion of charged particles. This magnetic field is coupled with the applied field, and a coupled magneto-fluid dynamic system can be formed. The overall action of these effects has an impact on the velocity distribution, pressure gradients, and the general characteristics of the flow. This kind of interaction is of core interest to the study of MHD, in which the dynamics of fluids and electromagnetic fields are closely coupled. Induced magnetic fields and magnetic fields are very important in many engineering applications of electrically conducting fluids. Such systems of applications are MHD power systems, liquid metal thermal management systems in nuclear reactors, electromagnetic pumping systems, and flow modulation systems in the environment of fusion reactors. They are also used in metallurgical operations including electromagnetic stirring and casting, crystal growth and plasma confinement. Also, magnetic fields are applied in biomedical engineering in magnetic drug targeting and controlled delivery. Magnetic effects increase the flow regulation, heat transfer, and stability of processes in all of these applications. Quintino et al. [32] investigated natural convection of nanofluids made of water around a hot cylinder placed in an enclosure. Baki et al. [33] used a review of the recent development in the engineering of magnetic nanoparticles in biomedical applications. Rasheed et al. [34] conducted a numerical study of unsteady MHD flows of Casson fluid in the vicinity of a vertical surface with the consideration of the effects of Hall currents and chemical reaction dynamics. Abel et al. [35] investigated the nature of MHD flow and heat transfer in an Upper-Convicted Maxwell (UCM) fluid upon a stretching surface taking into consideration the effect of thermal radiation. Recently, Shen et al. [36] investigated the design as well as performance of a hybrid-excited Vernier reluctance linear machine with slot Halbach permanent magnet arrays. Their study emphasizes the role of induced magnetic fields in controlling flux distribution as well as enhancing electromagnetic performance, demonstrating their strategic application in engineering systems. Hussain et al. [37] explored energy loss behavior in magnetically influenced engine oil hybrid nanoliquid flow with Ti6Al4V-ZnO additives moving along an extending wall. Faridi et al. [38] numerically examined the rise in thermal responsiveness of a water ethylene glycol mixture containing MnZnFe₂O₄–Ag hybrid additives over a magnetically affected extending surface. Zahir et al. [39] analyzed radiative heating role on nanofluid flow as well as energy exchange between stretchable disks subjected to Lorentz force.

A convective boundary condition describes fluid flow, in which the temperature difference between the fluid and the surface is used to determine the rate of heat transfer between the flowing fluid and a solid. It is widely used in such areas as air or water cooling of hot surfaces. The convective boundary condition is a mathematical relationship involving the conductive heat transfer at the solid interface and convective heat transfer in the fluid by a heat transfer coefficient. This state is useful in the analysis of the integrated response of conduction in the solid and convection in the fluid. It plays a big role in the accurate modeling of thermal processes in the heat exchanger as well as in electronic cooling and thermal insulation. Heat exchanger design, cooling of electronic and engine parts, HVAC, thermal protection of aerospace structures and temperature regulation of a chemical reactor all utilize the convective boundary condition. Recently, Ragavi et al. [40] talked about the Enhanced heat transfer analysis of Ag–Al2O3//water hybrid magneto-convective nano-flow. Kong et al. [41] conducted a numerical investigation of particle behavior as well as heat flow in an S-shaped miniature cooling channel of a gas turbine blade, where the thermal interaction between the channel walls as well as the flowing coolant was modeled using convective boundary conditions. Ye et al. [42] studied heat flow in a mesh-fed slot cooling setup, examining the impact of slot height as well as the arrangement of pin-like fins. Wang et al. [43] applied convective boundary conditions to examine film cooling performance in channels featuring randomly rough internal holes as well as assessed its effect on heat flow efficiency. Tang et al. [44] recently studied flow boiling behavior as well as the improvement of diverging flow paths in manifold microchannel heat sinks, considering convective boundary conditions to model the heat exchange between the channel walls and the material.

Research gap and novelty

Despite the growing interest in nanofluid mechanics, the combined effects of Cattaneo–Christov heat and mass diffusions, induced magnetic fields, chemical reactions, Soret and Dufour effects, and convective boundary conditions in Homann-type flows of THNF remain underexplored. The Homann type flow is explored, incorporating both heat and mass balance features, while adhering to the classical Fourier and Fickian laws of heat and mass transport. However, existing studies have focused on binary nanofluids during the special stagnation point flow, namely Homann flow. This work further presents a comprehensive numerical study where these complex phenomena in a 3D non-axisymmetric setting with Darcy–Forchheimer porous media and a ternary nanoparticle (CoFe2O4, ZnO, Au) in combination with base fluid ethylene glycol. Key innovations, including the relaxation time factors’ appearance in both the concentration and energy equations, have been declared a significant contribution in the presence of chemical reactivity, Soret/Dufour effects, convective boundaries, and strain rate. A built-in numerical method is employed to approximate the significant results.

The project presented in this article explores the type of approximation of the Homann type of flow through the use of a THNF consisting of nanoparticles based on ethylene glycol over a porous rotating disk. The role of magnetic fields, convective boundary conditions and the use of Cattaneo-Christov theory are also a part of the investigation. The research describes thermal and mass transport in a better way by using the Cattaneo-Christov models of mass and heat fluxes, including finite relaxation times. Numerical scheme

The Homann type flow together with thermal and mass balance mathematical forms governed by Eqs. 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 and the surface as well as far away conditions defined by (27) are approximated via one of the built-in approaches while using MATLAB. The scheme is mathematically formulated in terms of PDEs, and as per the required criteria, the resultant higher ODEs must be reduced to first-order ODEs. The modified bvp4c scheme provides rapid as well as stable convergence due to its adaptive mesh refinement and collocation strategy based on a 3-stage Lobatto formula. During computation, the step size is automatically adjusted to maintain the desired tolerance, ensuring that successive iterations move steadily toward the true solution. To verify the correctness of the numerical results, a validation study was performed by comparing the computed values of key quantities such as the wall shear rate as well as surface temperature gradient with benchmark solutions reported in previous studies. The excellent agreement confirms the reliability of the proposed numerical scheme. Additionally, numerical accuracy is strengthened through mesh-independence testing, where progressively refined grids yield negligible variations in the results, demonstrating the consistency as well as balance of the improved bvp4c algorithm. The main steps are provided through a flow chart diagram as shown in Fig. 2. For better results, the number of mesh points must be increased, and ultimately, the CPU timing shall be noticed as higher comparatively. The relaxation time of heat flux represents the time delay between the application of a temperature gradient and the resulting heat flow in a fluid. It captures the finite speed of thermal signal propagation, enhancing heat transfer models by addressing non-instantaneous conduction effects. Figure 9a plotted to show that if the values of λT rise, then the temperature of the fluid also declines. As the relaxation time of heat flux (λT) rises, the fluid’s thermal response slows down, causing a delay in heat transfer. This leads to low heat conduction efficiency, hence a decrease in the temperature. The effect of the system is that it takes more time to respond to change in temperature hence making the temperature to drop. Physically, an increase in the heat flux relaxation time implies thermal signals propagate at a slower rate in the fluid. This delay weakens heat conduction, resulting in a reduced fluid temperature.

The Biot number (Bi) indicates how easily heat moves within an object compared to how easily it transfers to a surrounding fluid. A low Biot number means internal heat conduction is much faster than external convection, while a high Biot number suggests the opposite. Figure 9b reveals that the temperature of the fluid as lowered as the values of the Bi increase. As the Biot number increases, this indicates that the thermal resistance of the solid is greater than that of the fluid interface and therefore the higher the surface temperature of the solid. This enhances more effective heat transfer at the solid surface to the surrounding. However, in systems where the fluid is continuously flowing or large in volume, this added heat is quickly carried away or dispersed, resulting in a lowering of the fluid’s observed temperature near the solid. Consequently, an increase Biot numbers may result in an increase in the speed of local heat transfer while reducing the steady-state fluid temperatures in the investigated region. The physical reason is that at high Biot numbers, heat accumulates at the solid surface due to poor internal conduction, creating a steep temperature gradient. This rapidly transfers heat to the fluid and the heat is quickly discharged by the fluid provided it is in motion thus causing the temperature in the fluid to be lower at the point where the solid is located.

The Dufour effect is when a concentration gradient in a fluid causes heat flow. It’s important mainly in gas mixtures and affects precise heat and mass transfer processes. Figure 12b shows that as the Dufour number rises, the heat transfer rate in the fluid flow diminishes. The reason for this phenomenon lies in the nature of cross-diffusion effects in multicomponent fluid systems. When a concentration gradient exists especially in gas mixtures the Dufour effect causes energy (heat) to be transferred due to molecular interactions between different species. As the Dufour number increases, this energy transfer becomes more pronounced, but it does not always enhance the overall heat transfer rate. In fact, excessive Dufour-driven heat flux can disrupt the temperature gradient necessary for efficient thermal energy movement through conduction or fluid motion, resulting in a net decrease in the total rate of thermal exchange. Essentially, the heat carried by mass diffusion may counteract or disrupt the dominant heat flow direction, thereby reducing efficiency. Physically, the Dufour effect occurs because diffusing gas molecules carry internal energy with them. When a concentration gradient exists, this energy transport adds a heat flux independent of temperature gradients. As the Dufour number increases, more heat is redirected by mass diffusion, which disrupts the normal conductive heat flow and reduces the overall heat transfer rate.

λC​ represents the relaxation time of mass flux, indicating a delay in the mass flux response to changes in concentration. Unlike, classical Fickian diffusion, which assumes an instantaneous response, λC​ accounts for finite propagation speed and memory effects. Figure 13 highlights a direct enhancement of the Sherwood number as the value of λC​ increases in the fluid system. This occurs because of the relaxation time λC​ introduces a time delay in how rapidly the mass flux responds to concentration gradient changes. As a result, mass diffusion does not occur instantaneously but propagates at a finite speed, reflecting the fluid’s memory of previous state. As λC​ rises, this delayed response becomes more pronounced, causing stronger concentration gradients and enhancing mass transfer rates. Consequently, the Sherwood number increases, indicating more effective species transport in the fluid flow.

Abstract
. For a curve X X which is an abelian cover of the projective line branched at three points, we study when its reduction to positive characteristic is supersingular. Using the method of Shimura and Taniyama, we give a complete classification of primes p p for which the reduction of X X modulo p p is supersingular when the genus of X X is at most 10 10 . The natural density of this set of primes is larger than expected. 2020 MSC primary: 11G20, 11M38, 14G10, 14H10, 14H40. Secondary 11G10, 14G15, 14H37, 14H30.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic
p
.
An elliptic curve over
k
is supersingular if it has no points of order
p
.
A smooth projective curve
X
of genus
g
over
k
is
supersingular
if its Jacobian
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)
is isogenous to
a product of
g
supersingular elliptic curves.

One good method for constructing supersingular curves is to study families of curves that are abelian covers of the projective line

\mathbf{P}^{1}
.
The degree and ramification indices of the cover determine the genus
g
.
When the curve
X
is branched at exactly three points, it is a quotient of a Fermat curve
and
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)
has complex multiplication.
This makes it easier to study the action of Frobenius on the cohomology
[
Yui80
]

and hence determine whether the reduction of
X
modulo
p
is supersingular. For example, Re studied supersingular quotients of the Fermat curve with the aim of producing supersingular curves of many genera in fixed characteristic
[
Re08
,
Re09
]
.

Recently, we were curious about an analogous question: given
g
, for which primes
p
do these constructions yield a supersingular curve of genus
g
in characteristic
p
?
Our motivation was to show that a new construction of supersingular curves
[
BP
]
yields supersingular curves of genus
5
in characteristic
p
for infinitely many new primes
p
. We wrote this paper because we
could not find a reference that states what can be determined using this method.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{g}
be the set of primes
p
for which there exists a supersingular curve
X
of genus
g
defined over
k=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

such that
X
is an abelian cover of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
branched at exactly three points.
For
5\leq g\leq 10
, we determine the primes for which there exists a supersingular curve of this type
in Sections
3
,
4
and
5
.
This yields the main result of this paper, Theorem
6.1
,
which states exactly which primes are in
\mathbf{S}_{g}
, for
5\leq g\leq 10
, in terms of congruence conditions.
From these congruence conditions, we determine the natural density of the set
\mathbf{S}_{g}
, which we state as the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

For
5\leq g\leq 10
, the natural density
\delta_{g}
of the set
\mathbf{S}_{g}
is:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr g&5&6&7&8&9&10\\
\hline\cr\ \delta_{g}&\ 25/32&\ 507/512&\ 3/4&\ 1023/1024&\ 15/16&\ 31/32\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

The cases
g=9,10
are especially interesting, as noted in
[
Oor05
, Expectation 8.5.4]
.
The codimension of the supersingular locus in
the moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{g}

of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
is

g(g+1)/2-\lfloor g^{2}/4\rfloor
.
For
g\geq 9
, this exceeds the dimension of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}

of smooth curves of genus
g
.
The existence of supersingular curves of genus
g\geq 9
implies that the intersection of the
Torelli locus and supersingular locus in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is not dimensionally transversal.
This was already known to happen; e.g., there exist supersingular curves of every genus
g
when
p=2

[
vdGvdV95
]
.
Corollary
1.1
shows that this dimensionally non-transversal intersection occurs for most primes when
g=9
and
g=10
,
with
\delta_{9}\approx.938
and
\delta_{10}\approx.969
.

Work of Yui
[
Yui80
]
implies that
\delta_{g}
is
positive for all

, which can also be deduced from our analysis, see Remark

.

For the proof, we use Ekedahl’s work
[
Eke87
, p. 173]
to classify the
degrees and ramification indices of abelian covers

branched at
3
points, such that
X
has genus
g
.
When

for which

is superspecial
(meaning

is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves).
We determine the set of primes

]

As explained in Section

this method exploits the structure of complex multiplication on

We use SageMath to enumerate all abelian covers of

2.
Abelian covers branched at three points

Let
X
be a smooth projective connected curve over
k
.
Let
\phi:X\to{\mathbf{P}}^{1}
be a Galois cover branched exactly at
B:=\{0,1,\infty\}
. Let
g
be the genus of
X
.
We suppose that the Galois group
G
of
\phi
is abelian and that
\phi
is tamely ramified.

2.1.
Ramification and Inertia Types

For
b\in B
, let
I_{b}
be the inertia group above
b
and let
c_{b}=|I_{b}|
.
Since
\phi
is tamely ramified,
I_{b}
is cyclic.
Without loss of generality, we suppose that
c_{0}\geq c_{1}\geq c_{\infty}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
ramification type
for the cover
\phi
is the tuple
z=[c_{0},c_{1},c_{\infty},s]
, where
s
is the index of
I_{0}
in
G
.

The ramification type
z
is essentially what Ekedahl studied,
except that we switch the role of
0
and
\infty
to simplify the equation for
\phi
.

Notation 2.2
.

Let
r=\mathrm{gcd}(c_{0},c_{1},c_{\infty})
.
For distinct
i,j\in B
, let
e_{i,j}=\mathrm{gcd}(c_{i},c_{j})/r
.

Ekedahl
[
Eke87
, page 173]
verified that
e_{0,1}
,
e_{0,\infty}
, and
e_{1,\infty}
are pairwise relatively prime,
that
e_{0,1}\geq e_{0,\infty}\geq e_{1,\infty}
; and that

(1)

c_{0}=re_{0,1}e_{0,\infty},\quad c_{1}=re_{0,1}e_{1,\infty},\quad c_{\infty}=re_{0,\infty}e_{1,\infty}.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
Eke87
, page 173]

With notation as above:

(2)

2g-2=s(re_{0,1}e_{0,\infty}-(1+(e_{0,1}/e_{1,\infty})+(e_{0,\infty}/e_{1,\infty})).

Proof.

Above
b\in B
, the number of ramification points is
\#G/c_{b}
and each has inertia group of order
c_{b}
.
By definition,
|G|=sc_{0}
.
By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula:

\displaystyle 2g-2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2|G|+\sum_{b\in B}(|G|/c_{b})(c_{b}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|G|(1-((1/c_{0})+(1/c_{1})+(1/c_{\infty})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(c_{0}-(1+(c_{0}/c_{1})+(c_{0}/c_{\infty}))).

The result follows from (
1
).
∎

A cover
\phi
can be described via the numerical data of the ramification type.
However, there are also group-theoretic constraints on the ramification.
To state these, we identify
G
with
\mathrm{Gal}(\phi)
and define the inertia type.

Definition 2.4
.

Following Bouw
[
Bou01
, Definitions 3.2 & 4.1]
, the
inertia type
of
\phi
is the triple
a=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{\infty})
, where
a_{b}
denotes the

canonical generator
for
I_{b}
, found from the action of inertia on a local parameter at a ramification point above
b
.

There are three constraints on the inertia type:
|a_{b}|=c_{b}
for
b\in B
;

a_{0},a_{1},a_{\infty}
generate
G
; and
a_{0}a_{1}a_{\infty}=\mathrm{Id}_{G}
.
This implies that
s
divides
c_{1}
and
s
divides
c_{\infty}
.
Given a tuple
z=[c_{0},c_{1},c_{\infty},s]
satisfying the numerical constraints for a ramification type,
it is apriori possible that there is no tuple
a
satisfying the group-theoretic constraints for the inertia type.
See Remark
5.1
for some examples.

Given an inertia type, we can change the labeling of branch points whose inertia groups have the same order,
permuting those entries of the inertia type.
Also, we can change the identification of
G
with
\mathrm{Gal}(\phi)
;
this modifies each entry of the inertia type by some
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
.
Two inertia types
a
and
a^{\prime}
are
equivalent
, denoted
a\approx a^{\prime}
, if they are in the same orbit under these actions.

2.2.
Cyclic covers branched at three points

Suppose
\phi:X\to{\mathbf{P}}^{1}
is cyclic of order
m
, and identify
G=\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
.
The inertia type
(a_{0},a_{1},a_{\infty})
is defined up to multiplication by
(\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})^{*}
.
Note that
c_{b}=|a_{b}|=m/\gcd(m,a_{b})
. An affine equation for the cover is

(3)

X:y^{m}=x^{a_{0}}(1-x)^{a_{1}},

with
G
acting by multiplication by
m
th roots of unity on
y
.

Definition 2.5
.

For
1\leq j<m
, let
f_{j}
be the dimension of the
\zeta_{m}^{j}
-eigenspace of
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
. The
signature type
of
\phi
is
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1})
.

Let
\langle q\rangle
denote the fractional part of
q\in\mathbf{Q}
.
Then
f_{j}=-1+\sum_{b\in B}\langle\frac{-ja_{b}}{m}\rangle
by e.g.,
[
Moo10
, Lemma 2.7, Section 3.2]
.
For degree
m
cyclic covers
\phi:X\to{\mathbf{P}}^{1}
branched at
B=\{0,1,\infty\}
,
the following facts are well-known (e.g.,
[
LMPT19b
, Lemma 3.1]
):

•

the Jacobian
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)
has complex multiplication by
\prod_{d}\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{d})
,
where the product is taken over all integers
d
such that

1<d\mid m
and
d\nmid a_{b}
for any
b\in B
;

•

if
1\leq j<m
, then

f_{j}\in\{0,1\}
,
and
f(j)+f(m-j)=1
provided
d_{j}
, the order of
j
modulo
m
, does not divide
a_{b}
for any
b\in B
.

2.3.
Newton polygons

If
X
is a smooth curve over
k
,
we describe the Newton polygon of its Jacobian
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)
in terms of
the decomposition of the
p
-divisible group
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)[p^{\infty}]
.

Definition 2.6
.

For non-negative integers
c
and
d
with
{\mathrm{gcd}}(c,d)=1
,
fix a
p
-divisible group
G_{c,d}
of codimension
c
, dimension
d
, and thus height
c+d
.
The slope of
G_{c,d}
is
\lambda=d/(c+d)
, with multiplicity
c+d
. Let
G_{\lambda}=G_{c,d}
.

Suppose
A
is a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
g
over
k
.
By the Dieudonné–Manin classification
[
Man63
]
, the

p
-divisible group
A[p^{\infty}]
is isogenous to
\oplus_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}^{m_{\lambda}}
for some
multi-set of non-negative integers
m_{\lambda}
.
This multi-set defines a symmetric Newton polygon of height
2g
with integer break-points.
As a short-hand, we write
\nu(A)=\oplus_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}^{m_{\lambda}}

and call both
\nu(A)
and its visual representation the
Newton polygon
of
A
.

The Newton polygon of a curve
X
over
k
is the Newton polygon of
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)
.
A curve
X
of genus
g
is
ordinary
if
\nu({\mathbf{Jac}}(X))=(G_{0,1}\oplus G_{1,0})^{g}
and is
supersingular
if

\nu({\mathbf{Jac}}(X))=G_{1,1}^{g}
.

2.4.
Newton polygons for cyclic covers branched at three points

Suppose
\phi:X\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is a cyclic degree
m
cover which is branched at three points.
Assuming that
p
does not divide
m
, we describe how to compute the Newton polygon of

{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)
from the signature type of
\phi
,
using a formula due to Shimura and Taniyama, see
[
LMPT19b
, §2,3]
for details.
This implicitly relies on
X
being the reduction of a curve over
\mathbf{Q}
whose Jacobian has complex multiplication.

Notation 2.7
.

Assume that
p
does not divide
m
.
Let
R
denote the set of orbits of the multiplication by
p
map
[\times p]
on the set
\{j\in\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}:j\neq 0\}
.
For each orbit
r\in R
, the additive order
d_{j}
of
j
modulo
m
is the same for all values
j
in
r
and we denote it by
d_{r}
.
For
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
, let

(4)

S_{\epsilon}=\{1\leq j<m:d_{j}\text{ does not divide }a_{b}\text{ for }b\in B,\text{ and }f_{j}=\epsilon\}.

For
r\in R
such that
d_{r}
does not divide
a_{b}
for
b\in B
,
set
\alpha_{r}=\#(r\cap S_{1})
.

In the context of Notation
2.7
,
the set
R
is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of primes of the
group ring
\mathbf{Q}[\mu_{m}]
above
p
. This is because
\mathbf{Q}[\mu_{m}]
is unramified at
p
;
the elements of
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
can be identified with homomorphisms from
\mathbf{Q}[\mu_{m}]
to
\mathbf{C}
; and
there is a natural action of Frobenius on the set of homomorphisms, see
[
LMPT19b
, §2.1]
for details.
The
p
-divisible group
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)[p^{\infty}]
canonically decomposes
into pieces
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)[p^{\infty}]_{r}
indexed by the orbits
r\in R
.

Theorem 2.8

(Shimura–Taniyama formula)
.

[
Tat71
, Section 5]

The only slope of the Newton polygon of
{\mathbf{Jac}}(X)[p^{\infty}]_{r}
is
\lambda_{r}:=\alpha_{r}/\#r
.

In particular, if every orbit is self-dual, meaning invariant under negation modulo
m
, then
X
is supersingular.

Example 2.9
.

Let
m=8
and
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. The orbits are
r_{1}=\{1,3\}
,
r_{2}=\{2,6\}
,
r_{5}=\{5,7\}
, and
r_{4}=\{4\}
.

If
a=(1,1,6)
, then
\phi
has signature
f=(1,1,1,0,0,0,0)
.
Thus
S_{1}=\{1,2,3\}
.
Then
\lambda_{r_{1}}=1
,
\lambda_{r_{2}}=1/2
, and
\lambda_{r_{5}}=0
.
Thus
\nu(\mathbf{Jac}(X))=(G_{0,1}\oplus G_{1,0})^{2}\oplus G_{1,1}
.

If
a=(1,5,2)
, then
\phi
has signature
f=(1,1,0,0,1,0,0)
.
Thus
S_{1}=\{1,2,5\}
and
X
is supersingular.

Remark 2.10
.

There is another method to determine whether
X
is supersingular by Yui
[
Yui80
]
and Aoki
[
Aok08
]

using
p
-adic valuations of Jacobi sums.
By
[

,
X
is supersingular if and only if either (i)
p^{i}\equiv-1\bmod m
for some

; or
(ii) there is an equivalence (as defined in Section

) of inertia types

for some

.

2.5.
Non-cyclic Galois groups and inertia types

Suppose
\phi
is abelian but not cyclic, of order
m
. The following lemma determines the Galois group of
\phi
in all the cases with
s>1
for
5\leq g\leq 10
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
z=[c_{0},c_{1},c_{\infty},s]
be a ramification type such that
s>1
,

s\mid c_{\infty}
,
c_{\infty}\mid c_{1}
, and
c_{1}\mid c_{0}
.
Then
G
is non-cyclic with
G\simeq\mathbf{Z}/c_{0}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/s\mathbf{Z}
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
there is a prime
\ell
dividing all three of
s=c_{0}s/c_{0}
,
c_{0}s/c_{1}
, and
c_{0}s/c_{\infty}
.
If
G
was cyclic,
\ell
would divide each of
m=c_{0}s,a_{0},a_{1},a_{\infty}
,
contradicting the fact that
a_{0},a_{1},a_{\infty}
generate
G
.
Since
G
is generated by two elements
a_{0},a_{1}
, this implies that

G\simeq\mathbf{Z}/r_{1}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/r_{2}\mathbf{Z}
where
r_{1}r_{2}=c_{0}s
and
r_{2}\mid r_{1}
.
Note that
r_{1}
is a multiple of
c_{0}
since
a_{0}
has order
c_{0}
.
The hypothesis on
z
implies that the orders of
a_{1}
and
a_{\infty}
divide
c_{0}
.
So
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
generate
G
only if
r_{1}=c_{0}
.
Thus
G\simeq\mathbf{Z}/c_{0}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/s\mathbf{Z}
.
∎

For
s>1
, we denote elements of
G=\mathbf{Z}/c_{0}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/s\mathbf{Z}
as an ordered pair.
Each
a_{b}
in the inertia type
a=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{\infty})
is written as
a_{b}=(a_{b,1},a_{b,2})
.
To choose a representative for the equivalence class of
a
,
we take the natural inclusion of
G
in
V=(\mathbf{Z}/c_{0}\mathbf{Z})^{2}
.
Since
\mathrm{Aut}(V)
acts transitively
on pairs of linearly independent elements of order
c_{0}
,
we may choose
a_{0}=(1,0)
without loss of generality.
When
c_{\infty}=s
, we also choose
a_{\infty}=(0,1)
.

2.6.
Decomposition from the Kani–Rosen theorem

Suppose
\phi:X\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is an abelian non-cyclic cover branched at
B=\{0,1,\infty\}
.
It is possible to generalize Sections
2.2
and
2.4
to the
abelian non-cyclic case. This would take us too far afield so we use the Kani–Rosen theorem.

The goal is to decompose
\mathrm{Jac}(X)
, up to isogeny, as the product

\prod_{i\in I}J_{i}
of abelian varieties
J_{i}
of smaller dimension.
Each abelian variety
J_{i}
in this decomposition is the Jacobian of a curve
Z_{i}
, such that
Z_{i}
is a quotient of
X

by a subgroup
H_{i}\subset G
.

Theorem 2.12

(Kani–Rosen
[
KR89
, Theorem B]
)
.

Let
G,H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
be such that
G=\cup_{i=1}^{t}H_{i}
and

H_{i}\cap H_{j}=\{\mathrm{id}\}
for
i\not=j
. Let
n_{G}=\#G
and
n_{i}=\#H_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq t
.
Then there is an isogeny:

(5)

\mathrm{Jac}(X)^{t-1}\times\mathrm{Jac}(X/G)^{n_{G}}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{Jac}(X/H_{i})^{n_{i}}.

Example 2.13
.

Suppose
G\simeq(\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})^{2}
and let
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\subset G
be the three subgroups of order two.
Then (
5
) simplifies to
\mathrm{Jac}(X)\times\mathrm{Jac}(X/G)^{2}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{3}\mathrm{Jac}(X/H_{i})
.
Suppose
G\simeq(\mathbf{Z}/3\mathbf{Z})^{2}
and let
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3},H_{4}\subset G
be the four subgroups of
order three.
Then (
5
) simplifies to
\mathrm{Jac}(X)\times\mathrm{Jac}(X/G)^{3}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{4}\mathrm{Jac}(X/H_{i})
.

2.7.
Enumerating and analyzing abelian covers

Using SageMath, we automated the search for abelian covers
\phi:X\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
over
\mathbf{Q}
branched at
B=\{0,1,\infty\}
whose genus lies in a specified range.
We then partially automated the determination of the set of primes
p
for which the reduction of
X
modulo
p
is supersingular.
We outline the approach here: the code is on github
[
BP25
]
.

We begin by listing all possible
e_{0,1},e_{0,\infty},e_{1,\infty},r,s
which satisfy equation (
2
) for
g
in the specified range. These determine the potential ramification types of covers.

For a potential ramification type with
s=1
, the cover
\phi
is cyclic and
G=\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
.
We look for inertia types compatible with the ramification type.
Without loss of generality,
a_{0}=1

When

In the small number of non-cyclic cases with

3.
Refining Ekedahl’s Results for genus five

Suppose
\phi:X\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is a tamely ramified abelian cover over
k
branched at
B=\{0,1,\infty\}
.
In
[
Eke87
, page 173]
, Ekedahl enumerates ways
\phi
can ramify so that
X
has genus
5
.
There are seven non-equivalent cases, as confirmed by our program, which
are listed in Propositions
3.1
,
3.3
,
and
3.6
.

Ekedahl studies the situation when
X
is superspecial, meaning that
\mathrm{Jac}(X)
is
isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves. The superspecial condition is equivalent to the Frobenius operator being trivial on
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
For each of the seven cases,
Ekedahl determines the primes
p
for which
X
is superspecial, thus
proving that there exists a superspecial smooth curve of genus
5
which is an abelian cover of
\mathbf{P}^{1}

branched at
3
points if and only if

p\equiv-1\bmod 8,11,12,15,20
or
p\equiv 11\bmod 15
.

If
X
is superspecial, then it is supersingular, but the converse
is not always true.
To determine the (less restrictive) conditions on
p
for which
X
is
supersingular, we use the Shimura–Taniyama formula.

5.1.
A useful lemma

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
.
If
z=[2r,2r,2,2]
, then
\mathbf{Jac}(X)\sim\mathbf{Jac}(X_{1})\times\mathbf{Jac}(X_{2})
,
where
\phi_{i}:X_{i}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is an abelian cover branched at
B=\{0,1,\infty\}
for
i=1,2
.
If
r
is odd and
i=1,2
, or if
r
is even and
i=1
,
then
\phi_{i}
has group
\mathbf{Z}/2r\mathbf{Z}
with
a=(1,r,r-1)
.
If
r
is even, then

\phi_{2}
has group
\mathbf{Z}/r\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
with
a=((1,0),(-1,-1),(0,1))
.

Proof.

By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula,
X
has genus
r-1
.
By Lemma
2.11
,
G=\mathbf{Z}/2r\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
.
Without loss of generality,
a_{0}=(1,0)
,
a_{1}=(-1,1)
, and
a_{\infty}=(0,1)
.
Since
a_{\infty}\in I_{\infty}
, the double cover

\phi_{a_{\infty}}:X\to X_{a_{\infty}}
is branched at the
2r
points above
\infty
.
Thus
X_{a_{\infty}}
has genus
0
.

Let
K=\langle(r,0),(0,1)\rangle
. Since
X/K
is a quotient of
X_{a_{\infty}}
, it also has genus
0
.
Write
X_{1}=X/\langle(r,1)\rangle
and
X_{2}=X/\langle(r,0)\rangle
.
By Theorem
2.12
,

\mathbf{Jac}(X)\sim\mathbf{Jac}(X_{1})\times\mathbf{Jac}(X_{2})
.

For
g=(r,0)
and
g=(r,1)
, note that
g\not\in I_{\infty}
; so
\phi_{g}
is not ramified in the fiber over
\infty
.
There are
2
points of
X
above each of
0
and
1
.
Now
(r,0)\in I_{0}
and
(r,1)\not\in I_{0}
.
Also
(r,0)\in I_{1}
if and only if
r
is even, while

(r,1)\in I_{1}
if and only if
r
is odd.

Suppose
r
is odd. Then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
both have genus
(r-1)/2
.
The quotient group
\overline{G}_{2}=G/\langle(r,0)\rangle
is cyclic of order
2r
with generator
\tau_{0}=(1,1)
.
Then
a_{1}=-\tau_{0}
,
a_{\infty}\equiv r\tau_{0}\bmod(r,0)
, and

a_{0}=(r+1)\tau_{0}
. Thus the reduction of the inertia type in
\overline{G}_{2}
is
(r+1,r,-1)\approx(1,r,r-1)
.
The quotient group
\overline{G}_{1}=G/\langle(r,1)\rangle
is cyclic of order
2r
with generator
\tau_{1}=(1,0)=a_{0}
.
We see that
a_{\infty}\equiv r\tau_{1}
and
a_{1}\equiv(r-1)\tau_{1}
.
Thus the
\mathbf{Z}/2r\mathbf{Z}
-cover
\phi_{i}:X_{i}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}

has inertia type
(1,r,r-1)
for
i=1,2
.

Suppose
r
is even.
Then
\phi_{(r,1)}
is unramified and
X_{1}
has genus
r/2
;
also
\phi_{(r,0)}
is ramified at four points and
X_{2}
has genus
(r/2)-1
.
Also
\overline{G}_{1}=G/\langle(r,1)\rangle
is cyclic of order
2r
with generator
\tau_{1}=(1,1)=-a_{1}
.
By a similar argument,
\phi_{1}
has inertia type
(1,r,r-1)
.
The inertia type for
\phi_{2}
is found by working in
G/\langle(r,0)\rangle
.
∎

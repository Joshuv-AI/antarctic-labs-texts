The association between different lipid indices and hyperuricemia in older adults: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yingkai Shen, Shihao Wang, Jinghan Qiu, Yinghao Li, Yufang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLipid-derived composite indices demonstrate robust epidemiological associations with diverse metabolic and cardiovascular disorders due to their integrative nature; however, their relationship with hyperuricemia (HUA) in older adults remains unexplored. This research examines the links between six blood lipid indices and HUA in older Chinese adults and assesses their diagnostic accuracy for HUA association stratification.MethodsA total of 3,040 older adults from two communities in Jinan were included in this cross-sectional study. Six blood lipid indices, namely, remnant cholesterol (RC), the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP), Castelli’s risk index-I (CRI-I), Castelli’s risk index-II (CRI-II), the lipoprotein combination index (LCI), and the atherogenic index (AI), were calculated from the physical examination data. To assess the associations between these lipid indices and HUA, multivariate logistic regression, restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis, and subgroup analyses were performed. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of each lipid index for the overall cohort, male participants, and female participants, with assessments using multiple metrics.ResultsHUA was diagnosed in 484 participants. Significant positive associations were observed between all the composite lipid indices and HUA. A nonlinear association pattern between the LCI and HUA was identified through RCS analysis, whereas linear relationships were observed for the remaining indices. Subgroup analyses revealed consistent directional associations between lipid indices and HUA across all stratified groups. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that the AIP had optimal diagnostic accuracy for HUA in both the overall cohort and the female subgroup, whereas the LCI exhibited superior discriminatory performance among males.ConclusionThis research revealed statistically significant associations between multiple lipid indices and HUA, with the LCI demonstrating a nonlinear relationship with HUA. The ROC analysis revealed modest diagnostic accuracy across these indices (AUC range: 58.14%-66.03%).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-025-02796-z.Keywords: Hyperuricemia, Lipids, Nonlinear model, Sensitivity and specificity, ROC curve

Introduction

Hyperuricemia (HUA), a metabolic disorder characterized by dysregulated purine metabolism leading to elevated serum uric acid (SUA) levels [1], represents a growing global health challenge. Epidemiological data indicate a progressive increase in HUA prevalence worldwide, indicating that it is a significant priority in public health [2, 3]. In China, the incidence of HUA has a persistent upward trajectory. National surveys revealed an estimated adult prevalence of 14.0% during 2018–2019, with a marked upward trend observed between 2015 and 2016 and 2018–2019 [4]. International studies across China, the United States, and various European countries reveal a strikingly wide range in the prevalence of HUA in pediatric populations (0.6%−50.4%) [5–7]. Furthermore, accumulating evidence substantiates robust associations between HUA and heightened risks of metabolic syndrome, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease (CVD), nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, and all-cause mortality [8–11]. Older adults often suffer from the various chronic diseases mentioned above, which exacerbate HUA while concurrently increasing the complexity of gout management. Therefore, intensifying research efforts on HUA in the older population is crucial.

Serum lipid profiles include low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), triglycerides (TG), and total cholesterol (TC). Substantial evidence confirms the atherogenic properties of LDL-c, TC, and TG, which demonstrate significant epidemiological associations with diverse metabolic disorders and cardiovascular pathologies [12–14]. Novel lipid-derived composite biomarkers—including remnant cholesterol (RC), the atherogenic index (AI), the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP), Castelli’s risk index (CRI-I), Castelli’s risk index-II (CRI-II), and the lipoprotein combination index (LCI)—serve as sensitive indicators of diverse cardiometabolic pathologies [15–17]. Although these composite lipid indices are similar, they provide distinct feedback on overall human lipid metabolism. Specifically, RC quantifies cholesterol within triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs) and atherogenic remnants (VLDL, IDL), effectively reflecting the statin-insensitive lipid accumulation pathway [18]. Previous studies have revealed associations between RC and incident cardiovascular events, as well as cardiovascular mortality [19]. CRI-I and CRI-II quantify the distribution of cholesterol between atherogenic particles (LDL-c/TRLs) and protective particles (HDL). Notably, CRI-II specifically isolates LDL-specific atherogenicity. Prior research has established relationships between CRI-I and the risks of atherosclerosis and stroke, with cross-sectional analyses further confirming its correlation with female reproductive health [20–22]. Similarly, CRI-II has been associated with stroke risk [23]. The AIP effectively represents the logarithmic transformation of sdLDL formation and is directly correlated with hepatic lipase activity. Research has revealed a link between increased AIP levels and increased myocardial infarction risk in patients presenting with both obstructive sleep apnea and hypertension, underscoring its utility as a prognostic marker for cardiovascular disease [24]. An effective estimation of the cumulative cholesterol load among all apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins (VLDL, LDL, Lp(a)), which is unaffected by triglyceride levels, is provided by the AI. In contrast, the LCI employs a multiplicative calculation, which underlies its stronger link to multiorgan dysfunction—a capability missing from earlier lipid indices. Notably, dyslipidemia and HUA are significantly comorbid, with emerging evidence revealing their pathophysiological interplay in metabolic dysregulation [25, 26].

Moreover, while existing research predominantly focuses on younger and middle-aged adults [3, 26, 27], older individuals exhibit heightened cardiac metabolic vulnerability that may amplify hyperuricemia-related complications. Consequently, targeted uric acid management in geriatric populations warrants particular attention. To elucidate the associations between novel blood lipid indices and HUA among older adults, this community-based study included elderly participants from two communities in Jinan, Shandong Province, China. This investigation systematically examines the relationships between diverse lipid biomarkers and HUA prevalence while concurrently evaluating their diagnostic efficacy. These findings provide evidence-based guidance for optimizing clinical practice and population health management strategies.

Materials and methods

Design and population

In the present community-based cross-sectional investigation, 3,437 elderly participants were recruited from two Jinan communities in Shandong Province between January and December 2023. Systematic eligibility criteria were applied: exclusion of individuals aged < 65 years (n = 30), removal of duplicate participants who retained only the initial measurement (n = 30), further exclusion of individuals lacking complete lipid profiles or SUA data (n = 31), and elimination of patients with missing covariates (n = 286). Consequently, the final analytical cohort included 3,040 eligible individuals with complete data (Fig. 1). The Ethics Review Board of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine’s Second Affiliated Hospital granted ethical approval (2023 Lunshen-KY-057) for this research.

Diagram illustrating the sample selection process and exclusion criteria

Laboratory findings and epidemiological characteristics

Trained medical personnel collected demographic variables (sex and age), behavioral factors (smoking and drinking), and anthropometric measurements (height and weight) using standardized protocols. Smoking was defined as tobacco use within the preceding month. Alcohol consumption denoted any alcohol intake during the previous 30 days. Diabetes mellitus criteria included a hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) concentration ≥ 6.5%, a prior diagnosis, or a fasting plasma glucose concentration ≥ 7.0 mmol/L. Hypertension diagnosis required either a clinician-confirmed history or an average systolic/diastolic blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg across three consecutive measurements. Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) included a history of myocardial infarction (MI), coronary heart disease, stroke, and atrial fibrillation. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight (kg)/[height (m)]². Serum parameters (lipid profiles, blood urea nitrogen [BUN], albumin) were quantified using Beckman AU5800 automated biochemistry analyzers. Hematological indices (hemoglobin [Hb], platelet count [PLT]) were measured with a Mindray BC-6800 automated hematology system. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was computed using the MDRD China equation as follows: eGFR = 175 × SCr− 1.234 × Age− 0.179 × 0.79 (if female) [28]. In this study, the composite lipid indices were calculated on the basis of the following equations: RC = TC-(LDL-C + HDL-C); CRI-I = TC/HDL-C; CRI-II = LDL-C/HDL-C; AI = (TC-HDL-C)/HDL-C; AIP = lg (TG/HDL-C); and LCI = TC × TG × LDL-C/HDL-C [3, 29–32]. Based on established physiological saturation thresholds for uric acid crystallization, HUA was diagnosed on the basis of a serum urate concentration exceeding 360 µmol/L (6.0 mg/dL) for females and a serum urate concentration greater than 420 µmol/L (7.0 mg/dL) for males [33]. Additionally, a directed acyclic graph (DAG) was generated to delineate causal interactions among covariates, outcomes, and exposures, as presented in Supplemental Figure S1.

Statistical analysis

Older adults were stratified into HUA–positive and HUA–negative groups. For categorical variables, P values were determined using the chi-square test. Continuous variables were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis rank-sum test to obtain P values. Univariable logistic regression analyses were performed to examine associations between individual covariates and hyperuricemia. Multivariate logistic regression modeling was subsequently employed to evaluate the independent associations of specific lipid parameters with HUA status. The results are presented as odds ratios (ORs) along with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). In all analyses, blood lipid indices were treated as both a continuous and a categorical variable for subsequent assessments. To elucidate potential nonlinear relationships between blood lipid indices and HUA, three regression models were constructed: a nonadjusted model that included no adjustments; Model 1, which included adjustments for demographic and lifestyle factors such as sex, age, alcohol consumption, smoking status, hypertension status, diabetes status, MACEs, BMI and pulse; and Model 2, which included additional adjustments for the following covariates: serum albumin level, BUN level, eGFR, Hb level, and PLT count. To capture nonlinear associations, restricted cubic spline (RCS) was applied with four knots (5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles). The reference value for each lipid index was set at the median of all participants.

Furthermore, stratified analyses evaluated heterogeneity in lipid-HUA associations across predefined subgroups: sex (male/female), drinking (yes/no), smoking (yes/no), BMI (≥ 30, 25–29.9, < 25), diabetes mellitus (yes/no), hypertension (yes/no), and MACEs (yes/no), with covariate adjustments applied uniformly. To quantitatively evaluate the diagnostic accuracy and sensitivity of different lipid parameters, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was employed, with a comprehensive assessment of associated discriminatory metrics, including the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy.

R version 4.3.3 (http://www.R.project.org; The R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) and Free Statistics software version 2.1 (Beijing Free Clinical Medical Technology Co., Ltd, Beijing, China) were used to conduct all the statistical analyses. The rms package (version 8.0) for fitting regression models incorporating the RCS and the pROC package (version 1.18.5) for generating and analyzing ROC curves and calculating the AUC were used. A 2-tailed P value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics of participants

After the inclusion/exclusion criteria were applied, 3,040 older adults were included in the study. Table 1 displays the baseline characteristics. The mean participant age was 72.8 ± 5.7 years. Males accounted for 43.7% of the study population. The overall HUA incidence reached 15.92%. The HUA subgroup had a greater proportion of females. This group also exhibited elevated BMI levels. The HUA participants had increased TC, LDL-c, TG and PLT levels. Conversely, HDL-c levels decreased in the HUA patients. eGFR values were lower here. All these differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05). Additionally, the composite lipid indices were higher in the HUA group. These indices consistently exceeded the levels of the non-HUA participants.

Interconnections between blood lipid index profiles and their distribution patterns

Significant interrelationships across all the composite lipid indices were confirmed by correlation analyses (all P < 0.001). Notably, CRI-I and the AI were linearly correlated (r = 1.00, P < 0.001). Robust positive associations were observed for CRI-II versus the AI (r = 0.98, P < 0.001) and CRI-I versus CRI-II (r = 0.98, P < 0.001), indicating potent biological interdependencies. Conversely, the AIP was moderately correlated with the other indices (coefficient range: 0.49–0.71; all P < 0.001). Distribution analyses indicated a predominantly Gaussian distribution for most lipid indices, whereas the LCI exhibited slight left-skewed characteristics (Fig. 2).

Findings from the univariate logistic regression analysis

The univariate logistic regression analyses that identified the risk factors associated with HUA among community-dwelling participants are detailed in Supplementary Table S1. Statistically significant associations with HUA were observed for sex, hypertension, BMI, BUN, eGFR and PLT (all P < 0.05). Conversely, no significant correlations were detected between HUA and other demographic variables, clinical histories, or additional biomarkers (Supplementary Table S1).

Findings from the multivariable logistic regression analysis

Diverse composite lipid indices were systematically evaluated for their associations with HUA using multivariable logistic regression. All the lipid-derived biomarkers demonstrated consistent positive correlations across the three hierarchical regression models. Notably, the LCI and the AIP, both of which are categorical variables, maintained concordant directional associations despite attenuated effect sizes, preserving stable positive association patterns throughout model iterations. Owing to perfect collinearity between CRI-I and the AI, identical effect magnitudes were observed across the models: ORs persisted from 1.53 (unadjusted model) to 1.50 (Model 1) and 1.51 (Model 2). Furthermore, RC exhibited a progressively increasing effect size following sequential covariate adjustments (Table 2).

Subgroup analyses

Stratified subgroup analyses were performed within the study population to assess whether the associations between different composite lipid indices and HUA were consistent or varied across subgroups defined by demographic features and health condition. A consistent positive association was observed between the various composite lipid indices and the risk of incident HUA among all the subgroups defined by the examined variables (Fig. 4). Notably, stronger associations between multiple lipid indices and HUA were observed in individuals without diabetes, suggesting that this group may be more susceptible to the pathophysiological consequences of dyslipidemia.

ROC analysis assessed the diagnostic utility of lipid indices

The AUC values of the composite lipid indices for predicting HUA in the total population, females, and males are shown in Fig. 5. The AIP had the highest diagnostic accuracy in the total population (AUC: 66.03%, 95% CI: 63.40%–68.65%). Among males, the LCI showed superior performance (AUC: 64.73%, 95% CI: 60.72%–68.73%). In contrast, the AIP remained the strongest predictor in females (AUC: 67.61%, 95% CI: 64.23%–70.99%).

Discussion

In this community-based cross-sectional study involving older adults in Jinan, Shandong Province, robust significant positive associations were observed between multiple composite lipid indices (RC, the AI, the LCI, CRI-I, CRI-II, the AIP) and HUA, even after comprehensive adjustment for potential confounders. These associations remained robust across all the examined subgroups. The ROC curve analysis confirmed that the AIP was the optimal predictor of HUA in the overall population and among female participants and that the LCI had stronger predictive ability among males. Therefore, given the increasing prevalence of hyperuricemia and its associated multiple organ complications, the findings of this study involving an elderly cohort in Shandong Province provide clinically relevant insights and may inform prevention strategies in this population.

Hyperuricemia (HUA) is a prevalent metabolic disorder syndrome that presents major global health challenges because of its high prevalence and associated complications. These factors further contribute to profound socioeconomic burdens, including health care costs and resource allocation demands [34–36]. A previous study reported an association between SUA levels and activation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS). In hypertensive patients, excessive activation of the RAAS may exacerbate the cardiac burden through HUA. A cross-sectional analysis in a Chinese population found that higher serum aldosterone concentrations were positively associated with the risks of HUA and gout [36], suggesting a plausible mechanism whereby RAAS hyperactivity and HUA may synergistically potentiate cardiovascular pathogenesis. Prior cross-sectional analyses using publicly available databases have revealed multiple risk factors for HUA. A study involving 6,963 healthy Chinese participants revealed a positive association between RC and incident HUA [3]. Concurrently, another study revealed that the LDL-to-HDL ratio, a novel lipid composite metric, is positively correlated with the risk of HUA risk [37]. A cross-sectional analysis revealed that the cardiometabolic index (CMI), a novel indicator for central obesity assessment, was positively correlated with HUA among 5,338 U.S. adults [27]. Similarly, the non-HDL-C/HDL-C ratio, an emerging metabolic biomarker, is significantly positively associated with HUA among U.S. adults [38]. Unlike previous investigations, this study focused on older adults, a population with increased physiological vulnerability to HUA. By conducting sex-stratified analyses, we identified differential associations between composite lipid indices and HUA and assessed the sex-specific diagnostic performance of these indices. These findings offer clinically actionable insights that may inform sex-specific risk stratification and diagnostic strategies for HUA in older populations. TC, TG and LDL constitute atherogenic lipid fractions with established epidemiological associations with diverse cardiovascular pathologies, including hypertension, atherosclerosis, and stroke [39, 40]. Mechanistic investigations revealed that TG induce the dysregulation of free fatty acid metabolism, accelerating adenosine triphosphate (ATP) catabolism and consequently increasing uric acid production [41, 42]. Dyslipidemia promotes xanthine oxidoreductase hyperactivity—a central controller in the purine degradation pathway. The dysregulation of this enzyme increases uric acid synthesis and reactive oxygen species formation, thereby initiating oxidative stress. These alterations intensify metabolic abnormalities and accelerate the progression of insulin resistance [43]. Furthermore, elevated levels of TG frequently coincide with reduced HDL-c levels, which modulates oxidative stress and inflammatory responses—both of which are critical pathophysiological determinants of HUA [44]. Concomitantly, dyslipidemia promotes chronic inflammation that enhances uric acid synthesis while impairing its excretion [45]. Cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP) serves as the principal regulator of cholesterol ester transfer from HDL to LDL/VLDL. In the context of hyperuricemia, CETP activity is enhanced through a mechanism mediated by NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Consequently, this enhanced activity drives the acceleration of pathological atherogenic lipoprotein transformation [46, 47]. Concurrently, hyperuricemia increases inflammatory markers, impairs HDL functionality—particularly its antioxidant capacity—and exacerbates LDL oxidation, potentiating its pro-atherogenic properties [48].

The kidneys serve as the primary site for uric acid excretion and are therefore essential for maintaining systemic uric acid homeostasis. When renal impairment compromises this excretory function, HUA develops. Recent research has elucidated key mechanistic links between dyslipidemia and the progression of renal injury. Notably, elevated TC levels contribute to kidney damage through multiple pathological pathways: by promoting oxidative stress, activating inflammatory cascades, and disrupting normal lipid metabolism [49]. Concurrently, aberrant cholesterol metabolism exacerbates renal injury by activating inflammatory signaling pathways (e.g., the Notch1/Snail axis) in renal tubular epithelial cells [50]. Furthermore, cholesterol dysmetabolism suppresses the activity of antioxidant enzymes—specifically superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione (GSH)—in renal tissues, thereby amplifying oxidative stress and cellular apoptosis [51]. Clinical investigations have revealed that in diabetic nephropathy patients, disrupted cholesterol metabolism further impairs renal function by perturbing energy metabolism in tubular epithelial cells, notably through the inhibition of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) activity [52]. TG significantly mediate nephrotoxicity through three synergistic pathological pathways. First, the accumulation of aberrant TG initiates direct cytotoxic injury via lipotoxicity in renal cells—particularly glomerular podocytes and tubular epithelial cells—by inducing lipid peroxidation and endoplasmic reticulum stress [53]. Second, hypertriglyceridemia drives ectopic lipid deposition within the renal parenchyma, which subsequently activates oxidative stress cascades and mitochondrial dysfunction [54]. Third, these processes concurrently trigger profibrotic inflammatory pathways. Ultimately, the interplay of these mechanisms (direct lipotoxicity, ectopic lipid deposition, and downstream activation) synergistically drives renal fibrogenesis and irreversible progressive deterioration of renal function [53, 54]. In vivo experimental evidence has demonstrated that the deposition of TG activates the signal transduction of transforming growth factor-beta 1/Smad, a pivotal mediator of extracellular matrix remodeling that accelerates renal fibrosis and structural deterioration [50].

LDL contributes to nephrotoxicity through distinct mechanisms. The binding of oxidized LDL to lectin-like oxidized LDL receptor-1 on glomerular endothelial cells initiates Gq-protein-mediated intracellular signaling that exacerbates renal injury [55]. Moreover, LDL modulates renal function via bile acid signaling pathway regulation, further revealing its multifaceted pathomechanisms in kidney disease [56]. Clinically, LDL-C-lowering strategies are paramount for attenuating chronic kidney disease (CKD) progression. As the cornerstone intervention, statin therapy significantly reduces LDL-C levels; this dual-pathway benefit concurrently mitigates the risk of cardiovascular events while preserving renal function in CKD patients, substantiating its role in comprehensive renoprotective strategies [57]. Novel lipid modulators—particularly inhibitors of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9—show therapeutic potential for ameliorating renal lipid metabolic disorders [58]. Collectively, these investigations elucidate the pathophysiological roles of distinct lipid fractions in modulating uric acid metabolism, thereby providing mechanistic substantiation for the biological foundations underlying the associations between novel atherogenic indices and HUA through multifactorial pathways, including oxidative stress induction, inflammatory cascade activation, disruption of renal urate handling, and enzymatic dysregulation of purine catabolism.

Strengths and limitations

In this investigation, a comprehensive methodological approach was employed to evaluate multiple composite atherogenic indices for HUA risk stratification within a community-based cohort of elderly participants in Jinan, China, and sex-specific sensitivity and specificity evaluations were conducted to enhance clinical interpretability. The result of this analysis confirmed the utility of these lipid-derived biomarkers for HUA analysis in older adults, demonstrating methodological advantages, including enhanced discriminatory power and demographic specificity, over traditional approaches. The ROC curve analysis revealed substantial variation in predictive performance (particularly sensitivity and accuracy) across the indices. Critically, the results of the sex-stratified analysis revealed optimal predictors: the LCI demonstrated notable efficacy for predicting HUA risk in elderly males, whereas the AIP was identified as the most robust indicator for elderly females. Collectively, these findings suggest promising potential for the clinical translation of these indices, offering a foundation for personalized HUA risk assessment strategies for geriatric populations.

This study has specific limitations that should be considered. First, the exclusive focus on elderly participants restricts the generalizability of the results to younger age groups. Second, due to the cross-sectional design, causal inferences about pathophysiological relationships are limited. Furthermore, recruitment was confined to Han Chinese individuals within two urban centers in Jinan, potentially limiting the extrapolation of the results to rural populations, ethnic minority groups, and broader geographic regions. Future large-scale, multicenter research is needed to confirm the distinct predictive utility of lipid parameters for HUA among older adults. Finally, while SUA concentrations were measured, the absence of clinical ultrasonographic confirmation of crystal-induced joint disease represents an additional constraint.

Conclusion

Among Chinese geriatric participants, all the composite atherogenic indices were positively associated with HUA. The LCI demonstrated a nonlinear dose–response relationship that remained consistent across predefined subgroups. The AIP showed superior predictive performance for HUA in the overall cohort and in female participants, whereas the LCI achieved optimal diagnostic accuracy in male participants. Collectively, these lipid-derived biomarkers enable personalized HUA risk stratification in older adults and may inform targeted screening and early preventive interventions—for example, prioritizing AIP-based assessment in older women and LCI-based evaluation in older men. For older adults with elevated lipid levels, targeted proactive uric acid surveillance combined with dietary interventions may help prevent hyperuricemia and reduce subsequent metabolic disease risk. As the composite indices are derived from routine lipid parameters, their implementation entails negligible additional costs for patients. This cost-effectiveness is particularly advantageous for health monitoring and risk assessment in older adults residing in economically underdeveloped regions, thereby promoting health equity.

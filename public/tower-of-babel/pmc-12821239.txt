Exploring patients’ perceptions and anticipated concerns regarding bariatric surgery
Authors: Xuhui Chen, Carla Zi Cai, Ningli Yang, Li Zhou, Huawu Yang, Hongxia Hua, Yating Song, Haridah Alias, Zening Huang, Li Ping Wong, Yulan Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBariatric surgery, particularly laparoscopic procedures, is becoming increasingly common for achieving sustained weight loss. However, the decision-making process is complex and influenced by psychological, social, and medical factors. Despite its growing relevance, there is a lack of research exploring patients’ perceptions and expectations regarding bariatric surgery in China. This study aims to explore patients’ perceptions, concerns, expectations, and hopes related to the outcomes of bariatric surgery. Additionally, it investigates patients’ information received, education, and support needs both pre- and post-surgery.MethodsThe study included 68 individuals aged above 18 years who had undergone bariatric surgery. Participants were recruited from four hospitals in China, and data were collected through a self-administered open-ended questionnaire. The data were analyzed using a thematic analysis approach.ResultsThe study uncovered that a majority of individuals had experienced unsuccessful dieting attempts before opting for bariatric surgery. Concerns and fears about bariatric surgery included potential complications and long-term effects. Expected benefits ranged from weight loss to improved health indicators, enhanced lifestyle, and psychological well-being. Participants voiced varied information needs, emphasizing trust in medical professionals, and a desire for detailed information on surgical procedures, risks, and postoperative care. Participants varied in pre-operative expectations, highlighting the need for detailed surgery explanations, psychological counseling, and assurance of suitability. For post-operative, they sought responsive communication and emphasized continuous follow-up to achieve sustainable weight loss.ConclusionFindings underscore the necessity for healthcare professionals to address diverse patient expectations, dispel myths, and provide ongoing support to ensure successful outcomes and long-term weight management post-bariatric surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03194-z.Keywords: Bariatric surgery, Patient perceptions, Weight loss outcomes, Support

Introduction

Obesity and related metabolic disorders have become prominent global public health concerns, a situation reflected in China as well. Obesity has reached epidemic proportions in China. In 2014, China surpassed other nations to have the highest number of obese women and men, with a collective count of 90 million individuals. This accounted for 14% of the world’s obesity prevalence based on body mass index [1]. The rapid economic growth in China over the past few decades has led to significant changes in residents’ lifestyles, marked by increased sedentary activities [2, 3]. A recent multicenter survey conducted in 2022 among 62,893 adults from 10 provinces in China reported an overweight prevalence of 34.3% and an obesity rate of 11.24% [4].

Bariatric surgery, particularly laparoscopic procedures, has emerged as a progressively common choice for those aiming for long-term weight loss. Nevertheless, the decision to undergo bariatric surgery is complex and multi-faceted, involving various post-surgery psychological, social, and medical considerations [5]. Furthermore, successful weight management after bariatric surgery necessitates enduring adjustments to various aspects of one’s lifestyle, including eating and physical activity habits. Weight loss is typically rapid in the first two years after surgery, but it eventually slows down and may reach a plateau or even result in weight gain [5–7]. Negative psychological outcomes after bariatric surgery may involve a heightened likelihood of emotional instability, along with the potential for adopting addictive behaviors such as compulsive eating or substance dependence [6–8], both of which can contribute to the uncertainty associated with opting for bariatric surgery. Moreover, the decision-making process is often hampered by a lack of accurate information and widespread misconceptions about the effectiveness and safety of bariatric procedures [9–11]. Consequently, although bariatric surgery is acknowledged as the most effective treatment for obesity, prospective patients frequently grapple with indecision and hesitation when contemplating whether to undergo bariatric surgery.

Managing long-term behavioral changes can be challenging, and research suggests that ongoing support from clinical psychology is often necessary to promote healthy behavior changes following bariatric surgery [12]. In addition to clinical psychology, health psychology plays a vital role in post-bariatric care by targeting behavioral, cognitive, and social determinants of long-term adherence—such as promoting self-efficacy, addressing depressive symptoms, and fostering positive affect—to support sustained dietary and physical activity changes [13]. To ensure successful outcomes, patients may need comprehensive support that addresses both their physical and mental health needs throughout their journey [14, 15].

In China, there is a lack of research exploring patients’ concerns and expectations regarding bariatric surgery. While international studies have emphasized the value of patient-centered insights in shaping pre- and post-operative care [16, 17], such evidence remains limited in the Chinese context. In particular, little is known about how patients in China comprehend the procedure, what outcomes they anticipate, and how they emotionally and cognitively approach the decision to undergo surgery. Understanding patients’ perceptions, expectations, and concerns is therefore critical to enhancing the preoperative process and achieving optimal post-surgical outcomes. Moreover, exploring patients’ perspectives and emotional experiences can shed light on the psychosocial factors and personal motivations that influence their decision-making process regarding bariatric laparoscopic surgery.

Understanding patients’ expectations and concerns is vital for improving preoperative counseling and facilitating informed decision-making. The literature supports the importance of understanding patients’ expectations regarding the results of bariatric procedures, spanning from weight loss objectives to enhancements in overall health and quality of life [18]. In addition to anticipated benefits, existing studies have also emphasized that patients often harbor specific fears, such as complications, inadequate results, or major lifestyle changes, that may affect their willingness to undergo surgery [19, 20]. Misconceptions and social stigma surrounding bariatric procedures may further complicate decision-making [21, 22]. By identifying these concerns and expectations, healthcare professionals can tailor communication and psychological support, ultimately enhancing patient confidence, satisfaction, and long-term adherence to lifestyle changes after surgery.

Therefore, the objective of this qualitative study is to explore patients’ history of weight management attempts, perceptions and understanding of bariatric surgery, anticipated concerns and fears, as well as expected benefits and hopes regarding the procedure’s outcomes. The study also examines the information and education received about bariatric surgery, along with their expectations and support needs. Qualitative inquiry is employed because it allows for in-depth exploration and nuanced insights into individuals’ experiences and perspectives. The findings of this study will offer healthcare providers a comprehensive understanding of the psychological and social narratives shaping patients’ decision-making and experiences with bariatric surgery, enabling more tailored and effective support for patients considering or undergoing this procedure.

Methods

Study setting and participants

Participants were recruited between January 2023 and December 2023 from four hospitals in China, namely Fujian Medical University Union Hospital, Chengdu Fifth People’s Hospital, Jiangsu Province Hospital, Chengdu Third People’s Hospital in China. The study population consisted of individuals aged between 18 and 60 years, which is the typical age range for patients undergoing bariatric surgery. Eligible individuals were identified through hospital records of patients who had undergone bariatric surgery (either laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass or laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy). The inclusion criterion for the study was having undergone bariatric surgery, specifically either laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (LRYGB) or laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG).

Recruitment procedure

Potential participants were contacted by trained research nurses or attending physicians affiliated with the bariatric surgery departments at each respective hospital. These healthcare personnel explained the purpose and procedures of the study either during routine post-operative follow-up visits or via telephone contact, depending on the patients’ availability. Participants were approached to participate at least three months after their surgery, ensuring that they had recovered from the immediate postoperative phase and had begun to experience the early outcomes and challenges of lifestyle adjustment. This time frame allowed for a more reflective perspective on their perceptions, concerns, and expectations. However, the exact number of days or months post-surgery was not recorded.

Data collection

Data were collected through a questionnaire that consisted of open questions to generate qualitative data (Appendix 1). The questionnaire was developed by the research team. To ensure clarity and relevance, it was reviewed by three researchers with expertise in public health and bariatric care. The questionnaire was then pilot-tested with five post-operative bariatric surgery patients at one of the study hospitals to assess its comprehensibility and appropriateness. Based on the pilot feedback, minor amendments were made, including rewording some questions for clarity, reducing the use of medical jargon, and adjusting the layout to improve readability. No substantial content changes were necessary, and the structure of the open-ended sections was retained. The finalized version was then used for data collection across the four participating hospitals. Although the questionnaire was self-administered, investigators were available to address participants’ questions and provide clarifications while completing the questionnaire. The questionnaire was developed and administered in Chinese. The responses were translated into English, and the translation was scrutinized for accuracy by a third party.

Questionnaire structure

The questionnaire comprised two sections: (1) structured questions on participants’ demographics (age, gender, income, education, health status) and type of bariatric surgery (LRYGB or LSG); and (2) open-ended questions exploring experiences and views on bariatric surgery, including weight management history, perceptions of surgery, concerns, expected outcomes, information and education, and support before and after the procedure. Full item details are available in Appendix 1.

Ethical consideration

The purpose of the study was explained to participants and written informed consent was obtained from those who agreed to participate. To ensure privacy and confidentiality, all data collected from participants was anonymized and securely stored. Only authorized members of the research team had access to the data, and all information was used solely for the purposes of this study. Participants were assured that their personal information would remain confidential and that they could withdraw from the study at any time without any consequences. The study adhered to strict ethical guidelines to protect the rights, privacy, and dignity of the participants.

Analysis

The qualitative data analysis in this study utilized a reflexive thematic analysis, following the approach outlined by Braun and Clarke (2006, 2021) [23, 24]. This method was chosen for its flexibility in identifying meaningful patterns while supporting deep, reflective engagement with the data. A preliminary coding framework was developed based on the research objectives and an initial review of the data, incorporating broad themes such as motivations, concerns, perceived benefits, and information needs. In line with reflexive thematic analysis, the framework was used flexibly, allowing for the emergence of new inductive codes and the refinement of existing themes. This iterative process ensured the analysis remained closely aligned with participants’ narratives rather than being limited by predefined assumptions. Codes were generated manually and using NVivo 12.0, based on meaningful text units irrespective of specific questions. These were grouped into themes reflecting shared experiences, which were iteratively refined. Two researchers independently coded the data, with discrepancies resolved through discussion or third-party review to ensure rigor. Final themes were supported by participant quotes, allowing for coherent, in-depth interpretation across the dataset rather than by individual items.

A reflective stance guided the analysis, with the multidisciplinary team comprising surgeons, epidemiologists, and social and behavioral scientists from China and Malaysia regularly discussing how their roles might influence interpretation. Reflexivity, essential for transparency and awareness of researcher influence [25], was maintained through ongoing dialogue, collaborative coding, and critical reflection to enhance credibility and rigor. The results of the thematic analysis were presented as themes and sub-themes, supported by quotes from the open-ended responses.

Results

A total of 68 participants participated in this study. The demographic background of the participants is presented in Table 1. The average age of participants was 32.1 (SD = 8.5) years. The majority were female (73.5%), and a significant portion had completed college or university education (61.8%). More than half reported being diagnosed with chronic diseases (55.9%), including conditions such as hypertension and type 2 diabetes. A majority self-reported experienced psychological-related illnesses (85.3%) such as anxiety, depressive symptoms, emotional instability and body image concerns. Approximately 61.8% considered their current health status as good/very good; however, nearly 30% described it as fair, and 10.3% as poor/very poor. The majority of study participants had undergone LSG (98.4%).

History of weight management attempts

Participants reported a wide range of previous weight management efforts prior to considering bariatric surgery. Common strategies included dietary changes, increased physical activity, meal replacements, and weight loss products such as diet pills, slimming teas, and ketogenic diets. Some also experimented with traditional or alternative therapies, including acupressure, moxibustion, cupping, herbal supplements, and slimming injections. Despite these efforts, most participants experienced inconsistent results and eventual weight regain.

Many described initial weight loss followed by difficulties sustaining lifestyle changes, often due to personal, work-related, or health challenges. Some persisted with diet control or exercise regimes for extended periods, ranging from a few months to over a year, but cited stomach discomfort, busy schedules, and lack of structured support as barriers to long-term success. A few participants mentioned seeking professional assistance, such as visiting hospital nutrition departments, though most attempted weight loss independently.

Participants reported lifestyle changes such as diet control and increased exercise, with durations ranging from a few weeks to over a year. Success varied, and many struggled with sustaining these efforts long term. Challenges like stomach health impact and difficulty sustaining changes due to work environments were noted such as irregular working hours, lack of access to healthy meals at the workplace, long sedentary periods, and limited time or energy for exercise. Regarding weight loss medications and alternative therapies, individuals tried options like Orlistat, diet pills, and various supplements, noting mixed effectiveness and side effects like diarrhea.

Participants’ motivations for previous weight management attempts included health concerns (e.g., obesity, fatty liver, cervical spondylosis), aesthetic goals, and societal pressures such as body image, discrimination, and difficulty finding suitable clothing. Some also aimed to regain confidence and improve self-image.

Perceptions and Understanding of bariatric laparoscopic surgery

Participants’ understanding of bariatric laparoscopic surgery varied. Most viewed it as a method to reduce stomach size, limit food intake, and support weight loss. While some had limited initial knowledge, they sought information through preoperative education, online searches, and clinical consultations. A few expressed early doubts but became more receptive after observing positive outcomes in others. Overall, participants recognized the procedure’s aim of controlling food consumption to facilitate effective weight loss and improve health.

Information sources included a wide range of channels. Social media and e-commerce platforms such as TikTok, Little Red Book (Xiaohongshu), and Kuaishou were frequently mentioned, alongside internet searches via Baidu, referrals from friends and colleagues, and discussions with healthcare providers. Some participants also gained awareness through hospital TikTok accounts and encouragement from family members.

Anticipated concerns and fear

Participants expressed various concerns and fears regarding bariatric laparoscopic surgery. Common apprehensions included worries about potential aftereffects, such as changes in eating habits and malnutrition. Some participants were anxious about the surgery’s impact on their daily lives, potential infections, complications, and long-term effects on their health. Specifically, the risks mentioned included gastric leakage, gastroparesis atrophic gastritis, and the risk of regurgitation. Additionally, participants were concerned about regaining weight, stomach-related issues such as the potential development of stomach cancer, and other unknown long-term side effects in the future. Worries extended to considerations about skin and face sagging as potential outcomes of bariatric surgery. Some were unsure or had no specific knowledge about potential complications.

Participants shared various expectations regarding the potential adverse effects of the surgery on their daily lives and routines. Concerns included restrictions on eating and drinking, the possibility of constant stomach upset, inconvenience in eating and recovery, and fear of colleagues’ negative perceptions. Some participants worried about potential malnutrition and the need to avoid strenuous physical exercise post-surgery. However, a few participants expressed optimism, believing that the surgery would not negatively affect their lives and might lead to positive outcomes, such as improved health and shape.

Expected benefits and hopes

Participants expressed a broad range of benefits from undergoing bariatric surgery, spanning physical, psychological, and social domains. A central and recurring goal was weight loss, which was closely linked to improvements in overall health, including better control of blood sugar, blood pressure, serum lipid levels, and conditions like fatty liver disease. Many participants also associated surgery with adopting a healthier lifestyle, improved eating habits, better sleep, and increased energy levels.

Physical appearance and body image emerged as important themes. Participants hoped that weight loss would result in a more toned and attractive figure, enabling them to feel more confident and comfortable in their bodies. Beyond aesthetics, these changes were seen as a path to improved self-esteem, enhanced well-being, and a more youthful, energetic presence. Several individuals mentioned expectations for greater clothing flexibility and the ability to participate in previously avoided activities.

Health-related expectations were also frequently discussed. Participants looked forward to relief from chronic comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, fatty liver, irregular menstruation, and back pain. For many, the surgery represented a proactive step toward preventing future health complications and maintaining long-term physical well-being.

Improvements in psychological and emotional health were widely anticipated. Participants believed that weight loss would boost self-confidence, reduce psychological stress, and positively influence how others perceive them. Some emphasized the emotional burden of obesity and viewed the surgery as a way to alleviate feelings of shame, stigma, or social exclusion.

Social and relational aspirations were similarly highlighted. Many participants expected that their improved appearance and confidence would enhance their social lives, facilitate better relationships, and open up new opportunities for social engagement. Some noted the hope that surgery would lead to less weight-based discrimination and a more accepting social environment.

Participants also shared specific personal and professional goals tied to their post-surgery journey. These included career advancement, the ability to return to physical hobbies, and achieving body composition targets such as a standard weight or healthy fat percentage. Long-term weight maintenance was a common concern, with many expressing a strong commitment to sustaining lifestyle changes, adhering to balanced diets, exercising regularly, and following medical guidance to avoid weight regain.

Information and education

Participants expressed varying levels of understanding regarding bariatric surgery. While some reported being well-informed through consultations with doctors, formal education, and online resources, others acknowledged limited knowledge and placed trust primarily in medical professionals to guide their decision-making.

Across the board, participants expressed a need for clear, detailed, and accessible information about the surgical process, including the procedure itself, associated risks, postoperative care, and long-term health outcomes such as diabetes management. Trust in the medical team was repeatedly emphasized as central to feeling reassured. Many participants stressed the importance of effective communication and ongoing support from healthcare providers, complemented by insights from peers who had undergone the surgery. Real-life stories and case studies from previous patients were particularly valued. These peer experiences provided not only practical information but also emotional reassurance, helping participants envision their own potential outcomes. Participants expressed that hearing how others navigated their recovery and lifestyle changes made the abstract aspects of surgery more tangible and manageable.

Information-gathering practices varied widely. While professional advice from doctors and medical staff remained the most trusted and frequently cited source, participants also turned to personal networks such as family, friends, and peers for informal support and recommendations. Digital platforms, especially social media sites like TikTok and Kuaishou, were mentioned as influential in shaping perceptions and understanding. Participants also referred to official websites, online forums, and reports published by health authorities and professional bodies to deepen their knowledge and validate information.

Pre-and post-operative expectation and support

Participants expressed varied expectations for support from healthcare professionals during the pre-operative phase. Some emphasized the importance of detailed explanations about the surgery, including its risks and benefits, while others highlighted the need for psychological counseling and comfort. Detailed information about the surgery is warranted, as some participants felt that they had not received sufficient information, even though the operation had already been performed on them. Participants also want assurance of individual suitability for bariatric surgery to alleviate their nervousness.

Participants had diverse expectations for post-operative support from healthcare professionals. These include responsive communication to patient calls, careful attention, encouragement, guidance on post-surgery routines, including dietary advice and exercise, along with mental health support. Participants emphasized the importance of continuous follow-up, postoperative care, and personalized guidance on diet, exercise, and nutrition. Long-term contact, supervision, and the ability to consult medical staff at any time were also desired. Attention to post-operative recovery, relief of pain and discomfort, and nutritional monitoring are crucial aspects highlighted by participants. They also noted that post-operative support is crucial and may contribute significantly to alleviating anxiety and fostering a positive post-operative experience. Regarding postoperative care, participants expressed a desire for continued monitoring by the doctor over an extended period. They also emphasized the importance of addressing any discomfort after the operation promptly, with the expectation that the doctor can identify the causes and provide timely solutions.

Discussion

Bariatric surgery has emerged as an effective intervention for individuals struggling with severe obesity, offering significant improvements in both physical and psychological health outcomes. As the prevalence of obesity continues to rise globally, including in China, understanding patient experiences and expectations around surgical weight-loss options becomes increasingly important for optimizing care and support. This study contributes to this understanding by exploring the lived experiences, motivations, and concerns of individuals in China who are considering bariatric surgery.

It is not uncommon for individuals considering bariatric surgery to have a significant history of unsuccessful dieting attempts, as reported in previous studies from other countries [26, 27]. Our research specifically investigates the weight loss journey histories of individuals in China who ultimately opt for bariatric surgery. Consistent with earlier findings [28–30], our study reveals diverse and varied strategies employed by participants in their attempts to manage weight, including dietary modifications, online purchases of weight loss products, exercise, and alternative methods before opting for bariatric surgery. The inability of study participants to maintain weight loss through multiple previous dieting efforts is likely a large motivating factor in the decision to undergo bariatric surgery. Delay surgery and attempting other conventional lose weight options have both pros and cons. While conservative approaches such as diet and exercise can promote healthier habits and may be effective for some individuals, postponing bariatric surgery may be inadvisable in cases involving severe obesity or related comorbidities, where timely surgical intervention could offer more substantial health benefits [26]. Delay causes may result in the development of obesity-related comorbidities or exacerbate existing comorbidities. An Australian qualitative study of patients waiting for public bariatric surgery reported that prolonged delays were associated with emotional stress and worsening physical health, with many participants describing the development or progression of obesity-related comorbidities and declines in mobility [31]. Studies have also reported that a significant number of patients awaiting bariatric surgery developed new comorbidities during the waiting period, and some even died before receiving the procedure [32, 33]. Evidence from Korea indicates that patients who undergo bariatric surgery achieve significantly greater and more sustained weight loss, along with higher remission rates of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia, compared to those receiving conventional non-surgical treatment, many of whom experience minimal weight loss and eventual weight regain [34].

However, it should be noted that bariatric surgery, although a powerful tool in ameliorating several obesity-related health conditions, is not without significant medical risks. Understanding past weight loss attempts offers valuable insight into patients’ previous challenges, their readiness for behavioral change, and potential barriers to adherence, which are key factors influencing the long-term success of bariatric surgery [35]. Hence, routine examination of weight loss histories can determine whether attempts at surgical weight loss are advisable. Additionally, understanding a patient’s weight loss history, hesitations related to surgery, and any delays in the process are crucial for healthcare workers to intervene effectively.

Participants in this study are cognizant of the risks associated with bariatric surgery, and a considerable number express apprehension before undergoing the procedure.

It is important to note that while bariatric surgery can significantly improve several obesity-related health conditions, it is not without substantial medical risks. The perioperative mortality rates range from 0.03 to 0.2% [36]. Although bariatric procedures have strong evidence supporting their efficacy and safety [36], they may still carry risks of complications such as wound infections, hernias, pulmonary embolisms, and sepsis—though these risks are generally low [37]. Given the awareness and expressed concerns of participants regarding the risks associated with bariatric surgery, it is crucial to prioritize thorough preoperative counseling and education. Structured pre-operative education programs, such as the Bariatric Surgery Education Pathway (BSEP) in Canada and the PREPARE program in the UK, have demonstrated positive outcomes. For instance, the Alfred Health clinic in Australia implemented a pre-hospital education program incorporating a GP review, patient information evening, and online learning package, resulting in a reduction in no-show rates from 12 to 2.1% and increased excess weight loss (41.1% vs. 32%) at 12 months post-surgery [38]. Additionally, a structured multidisciplinary Additional Preparation Program (APP) delivered before bariatric surgery in Europe showed significantly higher rates of diabetes remission (84.1% vs. 61.9%) and improved postoperative weight loss at two years [39]. Incorporating cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT)-based group education, motivational interviewing, and digital decision aids, strategies used in U.S. and European bariatric programs, can help improve mental preparedness and reduce pre-operative anxiety [40, 41]. These initiatives may address concerns about surgical risks by providing consistent, evidence-based information on potential complications, helping individuals develop a realistic understanding of what to expect.

Study participants expressed a range of concerns related to bariatric laparoscopic surgery, encompassing potential aftereffects such as changes in eating habits, malnutrition, infections, complications, and long-term health impacts. Our findings align with existing study that highlight patient apprehension regarding post-surgical weight regain, which is increasingly recognized as a common clinical challenge in bariatric [42]. Concerns about gastrointestinal side effects raised by participants in our study are consistent with findings from previous research [43, 44], further supporting the need for addressing these issues during preoperative counseling. Concerns extended to potential unknown long-term side effects and myths surrounding stomach-related issues, including the perceived risk of cancer. Aesthetic worries, such as skin and facial sagging post-weight loss surgery, were also brought to attention. These concerns provide valuable insights for physicians during counseling sessions, highlighting the necessity for guiding patients on dietary modifications post-surgery and suggesting potential referrals for cosmetic surgery to address aesthetic issues after the surgery. Moreover, they highlight the importance for clinicians to dispel unfounded myths about bariatric surgery when communicating with patients. Consistent with findings in another study, participants in this study also conveyed the presence of stigma associated with undergoing bariatric surgery, regardless of the achieved weight loss outcomes [45]. This indicates the importance of developing coping strategies to address and mitigate the societal stigma surrounding bariatric procedures, fostering a supportive environment for individuals seeking and undergoing such treatments. Despite these concerns, a subset of participants remained optimistic about positive outcomes, emphasizing improved health and body shape. The findings highlight the necessity for thorough pre-operative education and support to address various patients’ anxieties and foster realistic expectations.

The results also reveal a broad spectrum of expected benefits and aspirations in participants undergoing bariatric surgery. These insights may provide valuable guidance for individuals contemplating the decision to undergo bariatric surgery. Weight loss is a central theme, with participants aiming for not only physical improvements but also enhanced health indicators, particularly addressing obesity-related conditions. The multifaceted nature of their expectations includes a desire for improved body image, increased confidence, and positive psychological changes. Participants envision a transformative impact on their physical appearance, anticipating a toned body and a more attractive self. The anticipated benefits extend beyond personal well-being to encompass work, marriage, and social interactions, with hopes of increased confidence and societal acceptance [46]. It is important for healthcare practitioners to proactively address and manage patients’ expectations during preoperative counseling. While many individuals anticipate dramatic improvements in physical health, appearance, and overall quality of life, overly optimistic or unrealistic expectations may lead to disappointment and psychosocial distress if outcomes fall short. Practitioners should engage in open discussions to help patients understand the likely physical, emotional, and social outcomes of surgery, and reinforce that success also depends on long-term lifestyle changes and psychological adaptation.

Furthermore, the study’s findings highlight patients’ expectations for long-term weight loss post-surgery, emphasizing the crucial need to support patients in maintaining weight after the procedure. Participants express a collective desire to maintain a healthy lifestyle, adhere to nutritious eating habits, and sustain weight loss through exercise and guidance from healthcare providers. Therefore, to enhance long-term success, healthcare professionals should offer personalized lifestyle interventions, comprehensive education, and psychosocial support, encouraging realistic goal-setting and monitoring [47, 48]. It has been recommended that dietitians offer medical nutrition therapy every three months in the initial year following surgery, biannually in the second year, and annually in the long term for individuals who have undergone bariatric surgery to facilitate sustained weight loss and promote healthier eating behaviors [47].

The study reveals diverse information-seeking behaviors among participants considering weight loss surgery. Some feel well-informed through physician-patient communication during consultations and online sources, while others rely on trust in medical professionals. Preferences for information sources vary, including internet-based content, insights from doctors, and real patient experiences. Key information needs to center around understanding surgical procedures and surgery risks. Effective communication, support from medical professionals, and social networks are crucial elements in addressing concerns. To enhance practice, healthcare professionals should offer tailored information sessions that incorporate real patient experiences and employ clear communication strategies during consultation sessions. The public in China has increasingly relied on social media platforms, shaping their health perception and behavior [49]. Aligning with our research findings, this implies that leveraging social media platforms for information dissemination can enrich the comprehensive approach to information sharing in the context of weight loss surgery, both pre- and post-operative. However, while social media offers a valuable avenue for education and peer support, it also poses risks, particularly the spread of misinformation, sensationalized content, and unverified health claims. Therefore, leveraging social media should involve healthcare-led content creation, moderation by qualified professionals, and integration with official hospital communication strategies to ensure accuracy and trustworthiness. Several health systems have addressed these challenges by implementing structured, hospital-led social media initiatives that combine accessibility with professional oversight. Health systems in countries such as Australia and Singapore have successfully used hospital-led WeChat and Facebook groups to provide ongoing peer support, FAQs, and patient education modules before and after surgery [50, 51]. Adopting similar models tailored to Chinese platforms like Xiaohongshu or Weibo may further engage patients and reduce misinformation.

Limitation

It is crucial to acknowledge that interpreting textual responses from open-ended questions may have certain limitations. Criticisms have been raised regarding the qualitative analysis of open-ended questions, suggesting a potential lack of comprehensive insights [52]. However, given the limited interaction time between researchers, who also serve as clinicians for the patients, the use of open-ended questions serves as a pragmatic approach to bolster participation. Moreover, this method allows participants to take their time and respond comfortably at their leisure, fostering more accurate and thoughtful responses.

Another limitation of this study is the absence of exact post-operative timing for each participant at the time of survey completion. Although all were at least three months post-surgery, the lack of precise timing may have influenced how participants recalled and reported their expectations. Furthermore, asking participants to reflect on their pre-surgical expectations after having undergone surgery may introduce recall bias or post-rationalization. Future research should adopt longitudinal and follow-up designs to more accurately assess patients’ expectations and to examine how their perceptions, expectations, and outcomes change over time following bariatric surgery. Additionally, studies investigating pre- and post-operative support interventions are warranted, particularly those addressing psychological adaptation in the Chinese context. Given the rise of digital health technologies, examining the impact of digital platforms and peer support networks on long-term weight management could provide valuable insights for enhancing post-surgical care. Lastly, some members of the research team also served as clinicians to the participants. This dual role may have introduced social desirability bias, potentially influencing how openly participants shared their views. While reflexive practices and independent coding were applied to mitigate this risk, future studies may benefit from involving external researchers to provide alternative perspectives, enrich interpretive depth, and enhance the credibility of the findings.

Despite these limitations, our study adds new knowledge by exploring patient perspectives on bariatric surgery within the Chinese context, which remains underrepresented in the global literature. Unlike many previous studies conducted in Western settings, our participants highlighted culturally specific concerns, such as the influence of traditional medicine, societal stigma within Chinese communities, and the prominent role of local social media platforms (e.g., Xiaohongshu, Kuaishou) in shaping expectations and decision-making. These findings offer contextually grounded insights that are distinct from prior research and contribute to a more diverse understanding of bariatric care globally.

Conclusion

This study provides a nuanced understanding of the psychological, informational, and support needs surrounding bariatric surgery within the Chinese context. The findings highlight key areas such as motivations for surgery, anticipated outcomes, and common concerns related to physical, emotional, and social well-being. Misconceptions, stigma, and limited access to reliable information were identified as barriers to decision-making, while trust in healthcare providers and peer experiences served as important facilitators. Emphasis on clear communication, culturally sensitive education, and long-term, multidisciplinary follow-up is essential to enhance surgical outcomes and quality of life. These insights contribute to more effective and person-centered approaches in bariatric care.

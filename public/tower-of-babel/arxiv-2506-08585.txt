k k -Planar and Fan-Crossing Drawings and Transductions of Planar Graphs
Petr Hliněný, Jan Jedelský

Abstract
We introduce a two-way connection between FO transductions (logical transformations) of planar graphs, and a certain variant of fan-crossing (fan-planar) drawings of graphs which for bounded-degree graphs essentially reduces to being k k -planar for fixed k k . For graph classes, this connection allows to derive non-transducibility results from nonexistence of the said drawings and, conversely, from nonexistence of a transduction to derive nonexistence of the said drawings. For example, the class of 3D-grids is not k k -planar for any fixed k k . We hope that this connection will help to draw a path to a possible proof that not all toroidal graphs are transducible from planar graphs. Our characterization can be extended to any fixed surface instead of the plane. The result is based on a very recent characterization of weakly sparse FO transductions of classes of bounded expansion by [Gajarský, Gładkowski, Jedelský, Pilipczuk and Toruńczyk, arXiv:2505.15655].

Logic, Interpretations and Graph Drawings

As a brief introduction,
a simple
first-order (FO) interpretation

\xi

These notions are naturally extended to graph classes; here

All transductions in this paper are first-order.

Alternatively, the aim is that if we understand and can handle the

Transductions; Congested Shallow Minors

We consider only finite simple graphs (simplicity of graphs is natural and
necessary when dealing with FO transductions as we do here).

For an FO formula
\xi
of (vertex) colored graphs with two free variables,
and a colored graph
G
,
let
\xi(G)=H
be the graph with
V(H)=V(G)
and

E(H)=\{xy:G\models\xi(x,y)\wedge\xi(y,x)\}
.
A
transduction

\tau
(determined by
\xi
) maps a graph
G

to a set of graphs
\tau(G)
such that
H\in\tau(G)

if and only if, for some coloring of
G
, the graph
H
is an induced
subgraph of
\xi(G)
.
This definition actually describes non-copying transductions, and we stick
with this simplified view here since copying transductions (allowing to
duplicate the vertices of
G
before the rest) coincide with non-copying
ones on planar graphs and graphs in surfaces (via adding leaves).

Consider a graph
G
and a collection
{\cal A}\subseteq 2^{V(G)}
of vertex
subsets such that, for each
A\in{\cal A}
, the induced subgraph
G[A]
is
connected.
Then
{\cal A}
is called a
congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor model
of a
graph
H
on
V(H)={\cal A}
if:
(i) every induced subgraph
G[A]
,
A\in{\cal A}
is of radius
\leq d
,
(ii) for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, at most
c
sets of
{\cal A}
contain
v
,
and (iii) for every edge
AB\in E(H)
(where
A,B\in{\cal A}
), the sets
A

and
B

touch
in
G
– meaning that
A\cap B\not=\emptyset
or some
two vertices
a\in A
and
b\in B
are adjacent in
G
.
Note that if we set
c=1
, we get the usual (depth-
d
or unlimited
d
)
minor model with disjoint sets.

A graph
G_{1}
is a
congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor
of
G
if
there exists a congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor model in
G
of a graph
isomorphic to
G_{1}
.

4
Conclusions

Inspired by the recent interest in studying (FO) transducibility
between graph classes, and namely from the class of planar graphs,
we have proved (based on
[
7
]
) an
asymptotic characterization of transducibility from planar graphs in the
weakly sparse world –
\Cref
thm:kfanchar.
There is nothing special about the plane in this result,
and since all our arguments use planarity “locally”,
\Cref
thm:kfanchar
and
\Cref
cor:kplanchar can be easily extended to any fixed surface.

For example, from
[
9
,
11
]
,

\Cref
cor:kplanchar and
\Cref
rem:duplic we immediately get:

Corollary 8
.

The class of 3D-grids (maximum degree
6
) is not
k
-planar for any fixed
k
.

Likewise, there is no surface
\Sigma
and integer
k
such that the class
of 3D-grids is drawable with
\leq k
crossings per edge in
\Sigma

(a natural generalization of
k
-planarity).

We are not aware of any published elementary proof of
\Cref
cor:3dgridk, though,
this claim can be easily derived from published results:
Dujmović et al.
[
5
]

proved that every
n
-vertex
k
-planar graph has tree-width

{\cal O}\big(\sqrt{(k+1)n}\big)

and, on the other hand, Dvořák and Wood showed

[
6
]

that any balanced bipartition of an
n
-vertex 3D grid is crossed
by
\Omega(n^{2/3})
edges, and so the tree-width is at least
\Omega(n^{2/3})
.
Knowing validity of
\Cref
cor:3dgridk via other means,
we can also use
\Cref
cor:kplanchar to prove that 3D-grids are not
transducible from planar graphs (this is, in addition to

[
9
,
11
]
, a third way of
proving the statement which was still open a year ago).

Nevertheless, our main desire is to draw a path to a possible solution
of the following:

Problem 9

(cf.
[
12
]
)
.

Is the class of toroidal graphs transducible from that of planar graphs?

By
\Cref
thm:kfanchar, answer ‘Yes’ to
\Cref
prb:toroid is equivalent to having
a
k
-fold
k
-clustered fan-crossing drawing (after deleting
\leq k
of its
vertices) for a fixed
k
and every toroidal graph, which does not seem
likely to us.
An even more presentable connection exists in the case of bounded degrees,
via
\Cref
cor:kplanchar (note that
\Cref
rem:duplic does not apply here):

Problem 10
.

For which (or all)

there is
k

toroidal graph of maximum degree
\leq d

k

\leq k

Independently of
\Cref
prb:toroid,
\Cref
prb:torodeg makes good sense also
without deleting
\leq k

Answer ‘No’ to
\Cref

\Cref

On the other hand, it can be shown that answer ‘Yes’ to
\Cref

(for any single

\Cref
prb:toroid
for all bounded-degree classes of toroidal graphs.
If, at the same time,
\Cref

Lastly, we comment on some graph-drawing related aspects and questions of this research.

•

Our results generally allow non-simple drawings (that is, two edges of
the same fan may cross, and two independent edges may cross multiple times).
We tend to believe that
\Cref

be true when restricted to only simple drawings, but finding a concrete
counterexample seems to be a challenge.

•

\Cref

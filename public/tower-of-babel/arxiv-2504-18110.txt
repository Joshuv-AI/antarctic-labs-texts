A 2 2 -distance set with 277 277 points in the Euclidean space of dimension 23 23
Hong-Jun Ge, Jack Koolen, Akihiro Munemasa

Abstract




Abstract.

We construct a
2
-distance set with
277
points in the
23
-dimensional
Euclidean space having distances
2
and
\sqrt{6}
.

1
Introduction

Classification and construction of few distance sets in Euclidean space
goes back to the pioneering work of Blokhuis

[
1
,
2
]
. He showed that the size of a
2
-distance set
in the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded by
\binom{d+2}{2}
.
Lisoněk
[
7
]
determined the sizes of the largest

2
-distance sets in dimensions
d\leq 8
.
Since the set of midpoints of edges of a regular
d
-simplex is a

2
-distance set of size
\binom{d+1}{2}
, the main research interest lies
in the existence problem of a
2
-distance set whose size exceeds
\binom{d+1}{2}
.
No construction of such sets has been known for
d>8
.

In this paper, we construct a
2
-distance set in
the
\mathbb{R}^{23}
of size
277
, one larger than the lower bound

\binom{24}{2}
.
Nozaki and Shinohara

[
8
]
considered maximal
2
-distance sets containing a regular simplex,
but the sets they produced have smaller size in dimension
23
.
Glazyrin and Yu
[
5
]
determined that,
if a
2
-distance set lies in the unit sphere
S^{22}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{23}
,
then its size is bounded by
276
.
Note that, in addition to the standard example of
the set of
\binom{24}{2}
midpoints of edges of a regular
23
-simplex,
there are many other
2
-distance sets in
S^{22}
with the same size.
The unique regular two-graph on
276
vertices (see
[
6
]
,
and see
[
9
, Section 1.5]
for more information on two-graphs)
gives a set of equiangular lines
in
\mathbb{R}^{23}
, and any set of representing unit vectors forms a
2
-distance set
of size
276
.
We show that one of the graphs in the switching class of
this regular two-graph can be used to construct a
maximal
2
-distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{23}
of size
277
.

2
Construction

By
[
6
]
, the switching class of
the unique regular two-graph on
276
vertices
contains a graph
\Gamma
partitioned as
X\cup Y
with
|X|=33
and
|Y|=243
,
having quotient matrix

\begin{bmatrix}30&81\\
11&132\end{bmatrix}.

The subgraph induced on
X
is the complete multipartite graph
having
11
parts of size
3
.
Every vertex in
Y
has exactly one neighbor in each of the
11
parts of
X

(see
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
).
In other words,
\Gamma
has an equitable partition with quotient matrix

\begin{bmatrix}3(J_{11}-I_{11})&81\cdot\mathbf{1}_{11}^{\top}\\
\mathbf{1}_{11}&132\end{bmatrix},

where
J_{11}
denotes the
11\times 11
matrix all of whose entries are
1
.
The graph
\Gamma
has a
representation of norm
3
(see
[
9
, Section 3.5]
)
in
\mathbb{R}^{23}\cong\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{24}\mid(v,r)=1\}
, where
(r,r)=2
.
This follows from
[
4
]
.
We identify the vertices of
\Gamma
with the corresponding vector
in this representation.
Then
X\cup Y
is a
2
-distance set consisting of
276
points with

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=3\quad(x\in X\cup Y),

(1)

\displaystyle\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\text{ or }6\quad(x,x^{\prime}\in X,\;x\neq x^{\prime}),

(2)

\displaystyle\|x-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\text{ or }6\quad(x\in X,\;y\in Y),

(3)

\displaystyle\|y-y^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\text{ or }6\quad(y,y^{\prime}\in Y,\;y\neq y^{\prime}).

(4)

Let
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\subset X
be the set of vectors corresponding to
one of the
11
parts of the complete multipartite graph. Then
these three vectors are pairwise orthogonal. Define

u=2r-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3},

Then
(r,u)=1
.

Theorem 1
.

The set
\{u\}\cup\{r-x\mid x\in X\}\cup Y
is a
2
-distance set of size

277
having squared distances
\{4,6\}
,
contained in
\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{24}\mid(v,r)=1\}\cong\mathbb{R}^{23}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\{r-x\mid x\in X\}\cup Y
is a
2
-distance set
with squared distances
\{4,6\}
. Indeed, in view of (
2
) and (
4
),
it suffices to prove

\|(r-x)-y\|^{2}=4\text{ or }6\quad(x\in X,\;y\in Y).

(5)

Since

\displaystyle\|(r-x)-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2+3+3-2(r,x)-2(r,y)+2(x,y)

\displaystyle=8-2-2+2(x,y)

\displaystyle\in\{4,6\},

we have (
5
).

Observe
(u,u)=5
.
To complete the proof, suppose
z\in\{r-x\mid x\in X\}\cup Y
. Then

\displaystyle(u,z)

\displaystyle=(2r-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3},z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-(2r-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3},x)&\text{if $z=r-x$ for some $x\in X$,}\\
2-(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},y)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $z=r-x$ for some $x\in X$,}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus

\displaystyle\|u-z\|^{2}

\displaystyle=5+3-2(u,z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}4&\text{if $z=r-x$ for some $x\in X$,}\\
6&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Therefore,

\{u\}\cup\{r-x\mid x\in X\}\cup Y
is a
2
-distance set
having squared distances
\{4,6\}
.
∎

We remark that the vector
u
is independent of the choice of the part.
This can be shown in a more general setting.
Suppose that the Gram matrix of the vectors
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

is given by

\begin{bmatrix}nI&J\\
J&nI\end{bmatrix},

where
J
denotes the
n\times n
matrix all of whose entries are
1
.
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}.

Indeed, since

\displaystyle(x_{i},x_{j})

\displaystyle=n\delta_{ij}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n),

\displaystyle(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=1\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n),

\displaystyle(y_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=n\delta_{ij}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n),

we have

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\|^{2}+\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\|^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=n^{2}+n^{2}-2n^{2}

\displaystyle=0.

Setting
n=3
in the above argument, we see that

u=2r-y_{1}-y_{2}-y_{3}
holds whenever

\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}\subseteq X

is a set of one of the
11
parts.

Lisoněk
[
7
]
showed that there exists a
2
-distance
set in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{7}
consisting of
29=\binom{8}{2}+1

points. Since
277=\binom{24}{2}+1
, our set may be regarded as an analogue
of Lisoněk’s example in
\mathbb{R}^{23}
.
Also, Lisoněk
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
showed that
a
2
-distance set of size
29
in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
is unique up to isometry and scaling.
It would be interesting to know if the analogous statement holds for
a
2
-distance set of size
277
in
\mathbb{R}^{23}
.
We verified by computer using Magma
[
3
]
that
our
2
-distance set of
277
points is maximal in the sense that
it cannot be contained in a larger
2
-distance set.

Since Lisoněk found a
2
-distance set
in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
consisting of size
45=\binom{10}{2}
,
it remains as a challenging problem to decide whether
there exists a
2
-distance set
in
\mathbb{R}^{24}
of size
\binom{26}{2}
.

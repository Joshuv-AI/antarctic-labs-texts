Syzygies of polymatroidal ideals
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Jacob P. Matherne, Anna Shapiro

Abstract
. We introduce the cave polynomial of a polymatroid and show that it yields a valuative function on polymatroids. The support of this polynomial after homogenization is again a polymatroid. The cave polynomial gives a K K -theoretic description of a polymatroid in the augmented K K -ring of a multisymmetric lift. As applications, we settle two conjectures: one by Bandari, Bayati, and Herzog regarding polymatroidal ideals, and another by Castillo, Cid-Ruiz, Mohammadi, and Montaño regarding the Möbius support of a polymatroid.



1.
Introduction

A
polymatroid

\mathscr{P}
on the set
[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
with cage
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
is given by a function
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\colon 2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(Normalization)
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(\varnothing\right)=0
.

(ii)

(Monotonicity)
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(J_{1}\right)\leq\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(J_{2}\right)
if
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}\subseteq[p]
.

(iii)

(Submodularity)
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(J_{1}\cap J_{2}\right)+\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(J_{1}\cup J_{2}\right)\leq\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(J_{1}\right)+\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(J_{2}\right)
for all
J_{1},J_{2}\subseteq[p]
.

(iv)

(Cage)
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(\{i\}\right)\leq m_{i}
for all
i\in[p]
.

We say that
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\colon 2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is the
rank function
of
\mathscr{P}
and that the
rank
of
\mathscr{P}
is given by
\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})=\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}([p])
.
A polymatroid with cage
{\mathbf{m}}=(1,\ldots,1)
is called a
matroid
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid on the set
[p]
with cage
{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
The
polymatroidal ideal

I_{\mathscr{P}}\subset R
of
\mathscr{P}
is the monomial ideal generated by the monomials corresponding to the lattice points in the base polytope
B(\mathscr{P})
of
\mathscr{P}
.
For each
i\geq 0
, the
i
-th
homological shift ideal

{\rm HS}_{i}(I_{\mathscr{P}})\subset R
of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
is the monomial ideal generated by the monomials corresponding to the shifts in the
i
-th position of the minimal free
R
-resolution of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
.

Let
I(\mathscr{P})
be the independence polytope of
\mathscr{P}
.
The
Möbius function

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
of the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
is defined inductively by setting
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})=1
if
{\mathbf{n}}\in B(\mathscr{P})
and

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\;=\;1-\sum_{\mathbf{w}\,\in\,\left({\mathbf{n}}+\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{p}\right)\cap I(\mathscr{P})}\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{w})

if
{\mathbf{n}}\in I(\mathscr{P})\setminus B(\mathscr{P})
.
For all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\setminus I(\mathscr{P})
, we set
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})=0
.
The
Möbius support
of
\mathscr{P}
is defined as
\mu{\text{\rm-supp}}(\mathscr{P})=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\neq 0\}
.

The main goal of this paper is to settle the following two conjectures regarding polymatroids.

Conjecture 1.1

(Bandari – Bayati – Herzog
[
Bay18
,
HMRZ21
]
)
.

All the homological shift ideals
{\rm HS}_{i}(I_{\mathscr{P}})
of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
are again polymatroidal ideals.

Conjecture 1.2

(Castillo – Cid-Ruiz – Mohammadi – Montaño
[
CCRMM22
]
)
.

The Möbius support of
\mathscr{P}
is a generalized polymatroid
(
i.e., a homogenization of it yields a polymatroid
)
.

Section 1
has been verified in the following cases: in
[
Bay18
]
, if
\mathscr{P}
is a matroid; in
[
HMRZ21
]
, if
\mathscr{P}
satisfies the strong exchange property; in
[

]

\mathscr{P}

[

]
.
In
[
CCRMM22
, Theorem 7.17]

was proven in the case where

is realizable, thus serving as motivation to state this conjecture.
By
[
CCRMM22

or
[

is a matroid.

The
K

Since the
K
-ring of a matroid has already become an object of interest, we are also interested in a

\mathscr{P}
.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a matroid on a ground set
E

such that the restriction polymatroid is

.
By considering the augmented

-ring of

of

is given by

\text{\rm Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}\left(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\right)\;=\;\chi\left(\mathscr{M},\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{\otimes t_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{\otimes t_{p}}\right).

For more details, see
Section 2
,
Section 2
, and

.

Motivated by the combinatorial notion of
caves
introduced in
[
CCRMM22
]
, we introduce the
cave polynomial
of a polymatroid.
The cave polynomial of
\mathscr{P}
is given by

\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\,\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(1-\max_{i<j}{\big\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}\left({\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\right)\big\}}t_{i}^{-1}\right){\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}},

where

of
\mathscr{P}
.
It turns out that the Snapper polynomial
\text{\rm Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
and the cave polynomial

encode the same information.
Indeed, we have the equality

\text{\rm Snapp}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\mathfrak{b}\big(\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\big),

where
\mathfrak{b}\colon\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map sending
t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}
to
\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}
(see
(3)
).

Our goal is to investigate various aspects of the cave polynomial.
When
\mathscr{P}
is realizable, our approach is to consider the corresponding multiplicity-free variety (see
Section 2
).
To address the general case (where
\mathscr{P}
need not be realizable), our main idea is to show that the cave polynomial yields a
valuative function
on polymatroids.
The theorem below contains our main results.

Theorem A
.

Section 1
and
Section 1
hold.
More precisely, we have:

(i)

The support of the cave polynomial
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
of
\mathscr{P}
is a generalized polymatroid.

(ii)

The cave polynomial
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
of
\mathscr{P}
satisfies the equality

\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\,\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\,t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}.

In particular,
Section 1
holds.

(iii)

The

-polynomial of the polymatroidal ideal

is given by

\mathcal{K}\left(I_{\mathscr{P}};t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\right)\;=\;t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}\;\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}^{\vee}}\left(t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1}\right),

where
\mathscr{P}^{\vee}={\mathbf{m}}-\mathscr{P}
is the dual polymatroid with respect to the cage
{\mathbf{m}}
.
Thus the
i
-th homological shift ideal of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
is given by

{\rm HS}_{i}\left(I_{\mathscr{P}}\right)\;=\;\Big(x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{p}^{n_{p}}\;\mid\;{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;|{\mathbf{n}}|=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})+i\text{\, and \,}\mu_{\mathscr{P}^{\vee}}({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}})\neq 0\Big).

In particular,
Section 1
holds.

(iv)

The function
\mathscr{P}\mapsto\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
assigning the cave polynomial to a polymatroid is valuative.

2.
Proofs of our results

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a polymatroid on
[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
with rank function
\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\colon 2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a cage for the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
.
This means that

\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}(\{i\})\;\leq\;m_{i}\quad\text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i\leq p.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R=\mathbb{k}\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}\right]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring with
\deg(x_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for every
i
.
Let
S=\mathbb{k}\left[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,\,0\leq j\leq m_{i}\right]

be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring with
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for every
i,j
.
We note that

\text{MultiProj}(S)\;=\;{\mathbb{P}}\;:=\;{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}

is the product of projective spaces associated to
S
.

The
base polytope
of the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
is given by

B(\mathscr{P})\;:=\;\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{p}&\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{p}v_{i}=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})\;\text{ and }\;\sum_{j\in J}v_{j}\leq\text{\rm rk}(J)\text{ for all }J\subseteq[p]\end{array}\Big\}.

The
independence polytope
of
\mathscr{P}
is defined as

I(\mathscr{P})\;:=\;\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{p}&\;\;\sum_{j\in J}v_{j}\leq\text{\rm rk}(J)\text{ for all }J\subseteq[p]\end{array}\Big\}.

We have the following equality

I(\mathscr{P})\;=\;\Big(B(\mathscr{P})\;+\;\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}^{p}\Big)\;\cap\;\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{p},

where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum.

Our two objects of interest are the following.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

The
polymatroidal ideal

I_{\mathscr{P}}\subset R
of the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
is the monomial ideal given by

I_{\mathscr{P}}\;:=\;\left({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{p}^{n_{p}}\,\mid\,{\mathbf{n}}\in B(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}\right).

(ii)

The
Möbius function

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
of the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
is defined inductively by setting
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}}):=1
if
{\mathbf{n}}\in B(\mathscr{P})
and

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\;:=\;1-\sum_{\mathbf{w}\,\in\,\left({\mathbf{n}}+\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{p}\right)\cap I(\mathscr{P})}\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{w})

if
{\mathbf{n}}\in I(\mathscr{P})\setminus B(\mathscr{P})
.
When
{\mathbf{n}}\not\in I(\mathscr{P})
, we set
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}}):=0
.
Then the
Möbius support
of
\mathscr{P}
is defined as

\mu{\text{\rm-supp}}(\mathscr{P})\;:=\;\big\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\,\mid\,\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\neq 0\big\}.

Consider the minimal
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
R
-resolution

\mathbb{F}_{\bullet}\colon\quad\cdots\rightarrow\;F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}}R(-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})\;\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\;F_{0}\;\rightarrow\;I_{\mathscr{P}}\;\rightarrow 0

of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
, where each
\mathbf{b}_{i,j}=(b_{i,j,1},\ldots,b_{i,j,p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
The
i
-th
homological shift ideal
of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
is given by

{\rm HS}_{i}(I_{\mathscr{P}})\;:=\;\left({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j}}\mid 1\leq j\leq\beta_{i}\right)\;\subset\;R.

Notice that the equality
{\rm HS}_{0}(I_{\mathscr{P}})=I_{\mathscr{P}}
holds.

Definition 2.2
.

The
K
-polynomial
of
I_{\mathscr{P}}
is defined as

\mathcal{K}(I_{\mathscr{P}};t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;:=\;\sum_{i\geq 0}{(-1)}^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j}}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]

(see
[
MS05
]
,
[
KM05
]
).

Remark 2.3
.

By an abuse of notation, we also denote by
\mathscr{P}
the associated base discrete polymatroid (i.e., the lattice points in
B(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}
).
Being a base discrete polymatroid is equivalent to being an
M-convex set
in the sense of Murota
[
Mur03
]
.

We shall need the following “dual version” of the aforementioned polymatroidal ideal.

Definition 2.4
.

The
dual polymatroidal ideal

J_{\mathscr{P}}\subset S
of
\mathscr{P}
with respect to the cage
{\mathbf{m}}
is given by

J_{\mathscr{P}}\;:=\;\bigcap_{{\mathbf{n}}\in B(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}\;=\;\bigcap_{{\mathbf{n}}\in B(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}}\left(x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p\text{ and }0\leq j<m_{i}-n_{i}\right).

The
polymatroidal multiprojective variety
of
\mathscr{P}
with respect to the cage
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
is given by

Y_{\mathscr{P}}\;:=\;V\left(J_{\mathscr{P}}\right)\;\subset\;{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}.

Remark 2.5

(
\mathbb{k}
infinite)
.

Our motivation to consider the multiprojective variety
Y_{\mathscr{P}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
comes from the following algebro-geometric ideas that are available in the realizable case.
If
\mathscr{P}
is realizable (i.e., linear over
\mathbb{k}
), then we can find a
multiplicity-free
subvariety
X_{\mathscr{P}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
such that the support of its multidegrees is given by
\mathscr{P}
(see
[
CCRMM22
, Proposition 7.15]
).
Then a remarkable result of Brion
[
Bri03
]
yields a flat degeneration of
X_{\mathscr{P}}
to
Y_{\mathscr{P}}
.
This means that the multigraded generic initial ideal of the prime associated to
X_{\mathscr{P}}
is square-free and coincides with
J_{\mathscr{P}}
(see
[
CCRC23
, Theorem D]
).

Remark 2.6
.

We say that the support of a polynomial
f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
is a
generalized polymatroid
if the support of the homogeneous polynomial
t_{0}^{\deg(f)}f(\frac{t_{1}}{t_{0}},\ldots,\frac{t_{p}}{t_{0}})\in\mathbb{R}[t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
is a (base discrete) polymatroid.

Remark 2.7
.

When
\mathscr{P}
is a matroid,
J_{\mathscr{P}}
is the
“matroid ideal”
studied in
[
NPS02
]
.

Remark 2.8
.

The set
\mathscr{P}^{\vee}:={\mathbf{m}}-\mathscr{P}=\{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}\mid{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathscr{P}\}
is also a polymatroid.
We call it the
dual polymatroid
of
\mathscr{P}
with respect to the cage
{\mathbf{m}}
.
The rank function of the dual polymatroid
\mathscr{P}^{\vee}
is given by

\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}^{\vee}}(J)\;:=\;\sum_{j\in J}m_{j}+\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}\left([p]\setminus J\right)-\text{\rm rk}_{\mathscr{P}}([p])\quad\text{ for all }J\subseteq[p]

(see
[
Sch03
, §44.6f]
).
Moreover, we have
\mathscr{P}^{\vee\vee}=\mathscr{P}
.

Remark 2.9
.

The Chow ring of
{\mathbb{P}}
and the Grothendieck ring of coherent sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}
are given by

A^{*}({\mathbb{P}})\;\cong\;\frac{\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]}{\left(t_{1}^{m_{1}+1},\ldots,t_{p}^{m_{p}+1}\right)}\quad\text{ and }\quad K({\mathbb{P}})\;\cong\;\frac{\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]}{\left((1-t_{1})^{m_{1}+1},\ldots,(1-t_{p})^{m_{p}+1}\right)}.

For any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
{\mathbb{P}}
, we can write

\big[\mathcal{F}\big]\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathcal{F}))}c_{\mathbf{n}}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\,\left[\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{1}} \times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{p}}}\right]\;\in\;K({\mathbb{P}}).

For any closed subscheme
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
, we set
c_{\mathbf{n}}(X):=c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
Since by construction
\dim(Y_{\mathscr{P}})=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})
, we can write the class
\left[\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}\right]\in K({\mathbb{P}})
as

\left[\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}\right]\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}c_{\mathbf{n}}\left(Y_{\mathscr{P}}\right)\,\left[\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{1}} \times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{p}}}\right]\;\in\;K({\mathbb{P}}).

Under the above isomorphism describing
K({\mathbb{P}})
, we can also write

\left[\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}\right]\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}c_{\mathbf{n}}\left(Y_{\mathscr{P}}\right)\,(1-t_{1})^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots(1-t_{p})^{m_{p}-n_{p}}\;\in\;K({\mathbb{P}}).

Then we obtain

\left[Y_{\mathscr{P}}\right]\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}c_{\mathbf{n}}\left(Y_{\mathscr{P}}\right)\,t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}\;\in\;A^{*}({\mathbb{P}})

(i.e., when
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(Y_{\mathscr{P}})
, the constants
c_{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})=\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})
encode the multidegrees of
Y_{\mathscr{P}}
).

The next technical proposition relates the previous invariants we have seen.

Proposition 2.10
.

Under the above notation, the following statements hold:

(i)

\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})=c_{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

(ii)

In terms of the dual polymatroid
\mathscr{P}^{\vee}={\mathbf{m}}-\mathscr{P}
, we have the equality

\mathcal{K}\left(I_{\mathscr{P}^{\vee}};{\mathbf{t}}\right)\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})\,t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}.

Proof.

(i) This part follows from
[
Knu09
]
(see also
[
CCRMM22
]
).

(ii) Consider the
K
-polynomial
\mathcal{K}(S/J_{\mathscr{P}};{\mathbf{t}})
of
S/J_{\mathscr{P}}
.
Since each minimal prime of
J_{\mathscr{P}}
is of the form
{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}
(a Borel-fixed prime in a multigraded setting), one can show that the
K
-polynomial
\mathcal{K}(S/J_{\mathscr{P}};{\mathbf{t}})\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
and the class
\left[\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}\right]\in K({\mathbb{P}})
determine one another; that is, we have the equality

\mathcal{K}(S/J_{\mathscr{P}};{\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})\,(1-t_{1})^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots(1-t_{p})^{m_{p}-n_{p}}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]

(see
[
CCRMM22
, §4]
).
The Alexander dual of
J_{\mathscr{P}}\subset S
is the monomial ideal
K_{\mathscr{P}}\subset S
given by

K_{\mathscr{P}}\;:=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq p,\,0\leq j<m_{i}-n_{i}}x_{i,j}&\;\;{\mathbf{n}}\in B(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}\end{array}\right)

(see
[
HH11
, Corollary 1.5.5]
).
By
[
MS05
, Theorem 5.14]
, we have the equality

\mathcal{K}(K_{\mathscr{P}};{\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\mathcal{K}(S/J_{\mathscr{P}};\mathbf{1-t})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})\,t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}].

Notice that

can be seen naturally as the polarization of

by mapping the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}=x_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots x_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}

{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq p,\,0\leq j<m_{i}-n_{i}}x_{i,j}
in

.
Finally, by standard properties of polarization (see
[
HH11
, §1.6]
), it follows that
\mathcal{K}(I_{\mathscr{P}^{\vee}};{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(K_{\mathscr{P}};{\mathbf{t}})
.
This concludes the proof of the proposition.

We now recall the notion of
valuative functions
on polymatroids.

Definition 2.11
.

The
indicator function

\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
of a polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
is the function given by

\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbf{v})\;:=\;\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mathbf{v}\in B(\mathscr{P})\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
valuative group
of polymatroids on
[p]
with cage
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
, denoted
{\rm Val}_{\mathbf{m}}
, is the subgroup of
\text{Hom}_{\rm Sets}(\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{Z})
generated by all the indicator functions
\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}
for
\mathscr{P}
a polymatroid on
[p]
with cage
{\mathbf{m}}
.
A function
f\colon\mathbb{P}\mathbb{o}\mathbb{l}_{\mathbf{m}}\rightarrow G
from the set
\mathbb{P}\mathbb{o}\mathbb{l}_{\mathbf{m}}
of polymatroids with cage
{\mathbf{m}}
to an Abelian group
G
is said to be
valuative
if it factors through
{\rm Val}_{\mathbf{m}}
.
This means that, for all
\mathscr{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{k}\in\mathbb{P}\mathbb{o}\mathbb{l}_{\mathbf{m}}
and all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
, if
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}_{i}}=0\in\text{Hom}_{\rm Sets}(\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{Z})
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}f(\mathscr{P}_{i})=0\in G
.

Remark 2.12
.

From
[
DF10
]
or
[
EL24
, Remark 3.16]
, the valuative group
{\rm Val}_{\mathbf{m}}
is generated by the indicator functions of realizable polymatroids over
\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore if two valuative functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{P}\mathbb{o}\mathbb{l}_{\mathbf{m}}\rightarrow G
agree on realizable polymatroids, then they are equal.

Our approach is based on defining the following polynomial and showing that it is
valuative
.
We call this polynomial the
cave polynomial
because it is motivated by the combinatorial notion of
caves
introduced in
[

]
.

Definition 2.13
.

The
cave polynomial
of the polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
is given by

\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;:=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\,\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(1-\max_{i<j}{\big\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}\left({\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\right)\big\}}t_{i}^{-1}\right){\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}.

Notice that
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
is an honest polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
and not a Laurent polynomial with possibly negative exponents of the variables
t_{i}
.

Remark 2.14
.

Write
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}a_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})\,{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}
.
By ordering the points in
B(\mathscr{P})\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}
with respect to the lexicographic order (with
1<2<\cdots<p
), we obtain a shelling of the facets of the simplicial complex
\Delta(J_{\mathscr{P}})
associated to
J_{\mathscr{P}}
(see
[
CCRMM22
, proof of Lemma 6.8]
).
Then by
[
CCRMM22
, Proposition 4.6]
, we obtain that the coefficients of the cave polynomial
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{t}})
describe the class
[\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}]\in K({\mathbb{P}})
; that is,

\left[\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}\right]\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}a_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})\,\left[\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{p}}}\right].

Hence we have the equalities

a_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})\;=\;c_{\mathbf{n}}(Y_{\mathscr{P}})\;=\;\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})

(see
Section 2
and
Section 2
).
As a consequence, we can write

\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\,\mu_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\,t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}.

By symmetry, since we can choose any lexicographic order on
[p]
, we get

\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;:=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\text{ and }|{\mathbf{n}}|=\text{\rm rk}(\mathscr{P})}\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbf{n}})\,\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(1-\max_{i<j}{\big\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{P}}\left({\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\pi(i)}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\pi(j)}\right)\big\}}t_{\pi(i)}^{-1}\right){\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}

for any permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
on
[p]
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}\colon\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map sending

to
\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}
.
The cave polynomial
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
satisfies the equation

(1)

\chi\big(Y_{\mathscr{P}},\,\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\big)\;=\;\left(\mathfrak{b}\left(\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}\right)\right)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})

for all
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.

Lemma 2.19

For any

\mathscr{P}^{\prime}=\mathscr{P}-\mathbf{b}=\{{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{b}\mid{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathscr{P}\text{ and }{\mathbf{n}}\geq\mathbf{b}\}

Lemma 2.20
.

Let

and consider
\mathscr{P}^{\prime}=\mathscr{P}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
and
\mathscr{P}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}
.
Then, for all

Proof.

The equalities

We prove the other equality.
Consider the functions

given by

and

.
By
Section 2
, both functions are valuative.
Thus, due to
Section 2

and

agree on realizable polymatroids.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a realizable polymatroid over
\mathbb{C}

Due to

Then, by Bertini’s theorem, we have that

Theorem A
.

(i)
Set
\mathscr{C}:=\mu{\text{\rm-supp}}(\mathscr{P})=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})\neq 0\}={\text{supp}}\left(\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\right)

and

, §5]

[
CCRMM22

.
Let
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and consider
\mathscr{A}:=\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{b}}
, the
\mathbf{b}
-truncation of
\mathscr{C}
.
By
Section 2
, we have that
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{b}}
is also a polymatroid.
Iteratively applying
Section 2
, we get
c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P})=c_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{b}})=c_{{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{b}}(\mathscr{P}-\mathbf{b})
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\geq\mathbf{b}

Thus
\mathscr{A}={\text{supp}}\left(\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}-\mathbf{b}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\right)+\mathbf{b}
.
We now check the conditions of
[
CCRMM22

–

Part (a) holds because we already know that
\mathscr{A}^{\rm top}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{b}}
is a polymatroid.

–

Part (b) holds by construction since the cave polynomial mimics the notion of stalactites.

–

Part (c) holds by induction on the rank of
\mathscr{P}
because the rank of
\mathscr{P}-\mathbf{b}
is strictly smaller than the rank of
\mathscr{P}
when
\mathbf{b}\neq\mathbf{0}
.
The base case is clear since
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=1
when
\mathscr{P}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
is the polymatroid of rank zero.

Therefore, the support of the cave polynomial
\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
is a cave, and so we are done with the proof of this part.

(ii) This part follows from
Section 2
and part (i).

(iii)
The equality

\mathcal{K}\left(I_{\mathscr{P}};t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\right)\;=\;t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}\;\text{\rm cave}_{\mathscr{P}^{\vee}}\left(t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1}\right)

follows from
Section 2
and
Section 2
.

{\rm HS}_{i}(I_{\mathscr{P}})

Case report of curative resection after preoperative bevacizumab and sintilimab therapy in hepatocellular carcinoma with lymph node metastasis
Authors: Shujie Pang, Yingcheng Yang, Zhe Sun, Ning Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatocellular carcinoma with lymph node metastasis (HCC-LNM) is relatively rare, and optimal management remains controversial. We report two cases of HCC with lymph node metastasis treated with three cycles of Sintilimab and Bevacizumab therapy. The metastatic lymph nodes demonstrated partial shrinkage, with extensive coagulative necrosis and complete absence of viable tumor cells. Primary liver lesions demonstrated a partial response to systemic therapy. Both patients subsequently achieved curative resection. No recurrence occurred during postoperative follow-up (20 months for Case 1; 16 months for Case 2). Collectively, preoperative immunotherapy and targeted therapy has the potential to enable curative outcomes in patients with solitary HCC lymph node metastasis, although validation in larger studies is required.Graphical abstract Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Lymph node metastasis, Immunotherapy, Surgery

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most common malignancy and third leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally [1, 2]. With recent advances in tumor immunotherapy, combining anti-programmed cell death protein 1/programmed cell death ligand 1(PD-1/PD-L1) inhibitors with Bevacizumab has emerged as the first-line systemic therapy for advanced unresectable HCC [3–6].

Previous studies report that approximately 5% of HCC patients present with lymph node metastasis (HCC-LNM) [7–11]. In multiple HCC staging systems, HCC-LNM is classified as advanced stage (BCLC stage C and CLNC stage Ⅲb) [1, 12]. Studies have shown that such patients may benefit from local therapies like radiotherapy or radiofrequency ablation, with extended survival [7, 10, 13]. However, for oligometastatic patients, particularly those with solitary LNM, whether they can benefit from combined targeted therapy and anti-PD-1 therapy, subsequently undergoing curative surgery, remains unexplored in clinical trials to our knowledge.

Sintilimab is an anti-PD-1 antibody developed by Jiangsu XinDa Biopharmaceuticals Co., Ltd (Suzhou, China) for the treatment of various malignancies including HCC [6]. The ORIENT-32 trial established Sintilimab combined with Bevacizumab as first-line treatment for advanced HCC [6]. This work reports two HCC-LNM cases that underwent curative resection following three preoperative cycles of Sintilimab plus Bevacizumab therapy.

Case presentation

Case 1

In August 2023, a 66-year-old male was admitted following contrast-enhanced MRI detection of a liver lesion with suspected abdominal LNM. Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) was markedly elevated, confirming HCC with regional LNM (BCLC stage C, Child-Pugh A [5 points]). The patient had received 2 weeks of preadmission entecavir (0.5 mg daily) for hepatitis B. Given the LNM’s location at the portal vein-inferior vena cava interface, primary resection was non-curative. Following patient’s preference and tumor staging, three cycles of Sintilimab (200 mg IV in 100 mL saline) plus Bevacizumab (15 mg/kg IV in 250 mL saline) were initiated on August, administered every 3 weeks.

During therapy, serum tumor markers and MRI were monitored. Serum tumor markers decreased markedly, as shown in Fig. 1. Compared to pretreatment contrast-enhanced MRI, posttreatment MRI revealed significant regression of the portal vein-inferior vena cava interface lymph node with reduced arterial phase enhancement. Preoperative PET-CT reveals a metastatic portocaval lymph node with mildly elevated peripheral metabolism and no significant internal metabolic elevation, as illustrated in Fig. 2A and B.

Per modified solid tumor response evaluation criteria (mRECIST), the patient achieved partial response with no treatment-related adverse events [14, 15]. After multidisciplinary evaluation, curative resection was deemed feasible. Concurrent hepatectomy and abdominal lymph node metastasis resection were performed on November 2023. Figure 3 demonstrates the intraoperative gross morphology of the hepatic lesion and lymph node metastasis. H&E staining demonstrated 40% tumor necrosis in the primary hepatic lesion, whereas the metastatic lymph node exhibited extensive coagulative necrosis with no viable tumor cells (Fig. 4). The patient remains recurrence-free at 20 months postoperatively.

Case 2

A 66-year-old male without viral hepatitis underwent laparoscopic hepatectomy for HCC in July 2022, followed by four cycles of adjuvant Sintilimab and one year of donafenib [16] through September 2023. The patient underwent quarterly surveillance. MRI on January 2024, detected a suspicious portacaval lymph node metastasis without hepatic recurrence. Following Case 1’s therapeutic approach, three cycles of preoperative Sintilimab plus Bevacizumab were administered. Tumor markers showed marked decrease during therapy, paralleling Case 1 (Fig. 1). MRI (Fig. 2A) and PET-CT (Fig. 2B) demonstrated reduction in metastatic lymph node size. The patient underwent resection of the metastatic lymph node on April 2024. Similarly, postoperative H&E staining revealed extensive necrosis without viable tumor cells in the lymph node and It was assessed as pathological complete response (pCR) (Fig. 4). The patient remains recurrence-free at 16 months postoperatively. Treatment course schematic for both patients was shown as below.

Both patients received eight cycles of postoperative anti-PD-1 adjuvant therapy over six months. Regular surveillance with tumor markers and contrast-enhanced MRI was performed commencing one month postoperatively and every three months thereafter. To date, both patients have shown no evidence of recurrence.

Discussion

Lymph nodes represent the second most common site of extrahepatic metastasis in HCC [7, 11, 17]. According to prior studies, LNM occurs in approximately 5% of HCC patients and is a risk factor for poor prognosis [7–11]. Given the considerable incidence of HCC in China, this population of patients with HCC-LNM deserves attention [12]. However, current staging systems and treatment guidelines have not addressed this issue. In this report, we describe two cases of HCC with abdominal lymph node metastasis that underwent curative resection following three cycles of Sintilimab plus Bevacizumab therapy. Subsequent histopathological examination demonstrated pathological complete response in the metastatic lymph nodes, with no residual viable tumor cells identified.

HCC-LNM is classified as advanced stage across all three major staging systems: stage IVa per American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) [18], stage IIIb per China Liver Cancer (CNLC) [12], and stage C per Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) [19]. Curative therapy is generally not recommended as first-line treatment for HCC at these advanced stages. Combined local radiotherapy and systemic therapy is increasingly recommended for HCC patients with lymph node metastasis [10, 20].

Several local therapies have been employed to improve survival outcomes in patients with HCC-LNM [7, 10, 13, 20]. Lee et al. [20] reported that systemic therapy combined with radiotherapy or lymphadenectomy improved progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients with HCC and lymph node metastasis versus systemic therapy alone. Conventional radiotherapy has inherent limitations; recent evidence indicates stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) significantly improves objective response rates and reduces radiation-related toxicity versus traditional radiotherapy in HCC patients with lymph node metastasis [7]. Although radiotherapy has achieved significant advances in advanced HCC management, it remains non-curative, particularly for patients with solitary lymph node metastasis.

Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and hepatic resection are established curative-intent therapies for early-stage HCC [1, 12, 19], while their role in HCC with lymph node metastasis has been investigated in recent studies. Pan et al. [13] reported that CT-guided RFA yielded superior 6-month and 1-year overall survival rates versus hepatic resection in HCC patients with oligometastatic lymph nodes. Although RFA prolonged median overall survival by 5 months, its therapeutic efficacy requires further validation. Recent studies demonstrate that isolated lymphadenectomy does not significantly improve survival in HCC patients with lymph node metastasis [8, 21, 22]. The optimal therapeutic approach for HCC patients with lymph node metastasis remains undefined.

Advances in targeted and immune therapies have enabled HCC patients to derive clinical benefit from PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors combined with multi-kinase inhibitors (MKIs) [4–6]. The IMbrave150 [5] and ORIENT-32 [6] trials established Bevacizumab plus anti-PD-L1 or anti-PD-1 inhibitors (Sintilimab) as first-line therapy for advanced HCC. Recent clinical trials have demonstrated survival benefits with these neoadjuvant combinations in intermediate-stage HCC, though consensus remains lacking [23–25]. However, no dedicated clinical studies have investigated whether HCC patients with lymph node metastasis benefit from combined immunotherapy and targeted therapy. Only one recent case report documented complete response in a patient with HCC-LNM following tislelizumab monotherapy [26]. This case report describes two patients with HCC and lymph node metastasis who underwent curative resection following three cycles of preoperative Sintilimab plus Bevacizumab therapy.

Both patients presented with solitary portacaval lymph node metastasis. Given this anatomic location, achieving R0 resection in a single-stage procedure risks iatrogenic injury to the portal vein, inferior vena cava, and pancreas. Sintilimab plus Bevacizumab was administered for three cycles prior to surgery. Given the established objective response rate of approximately 30% to combined immunotherapy and targeted therapy in advanced HCC [5, 6], comprehensive counseling and written informed consent were obtained for these two surgically eligible candidates to mitigate potential disputes should disease progression preclude curative resection during preoperative treatment. This rationale for selecting three preoperative treatment cycles balanced surgical timing with therapeutic response assessment. Additionally, our surveillance interval of 6–9 weeks for treatment response assessment aligns with established protocols [5, 6]. Following MRI-confirmed significant regression of the metastatic lymph node, curative resection was successfully performed. Histological examination revealed pCR in the metastatic lymph nodes of both patients. Notably, the primary hepatic lesion in Case 1 exhibited partial response, suggesting metastatic lymph nodes demonstrate greater sensitivity to Sintilimab-Bevacizumab therapy compared to the primary tumor. Prior research characterizing HCC metastases demonstrated significantly elevated immune infiltration in lymph node metastases—including PD-L1 expression, CD3+CD8+Foxp3+ T cells, and CD163+ macrophages [11]—suggesting this distinct immune microenvironment may underlie their enhanced sensitivity to PD-1 blockade.

This case report has several limitations. First, as inherent to case reports, our findings require validation in larger cohort studies or clinical trials. Second, with only two patients exhibiting extensive coagulative necrosis in lymph nodes, immunohistochemical analysis for potential predictive biomarkers (e.g., CD8, PD-1/PD-L1) was precluded. Future studies with expanded cohorts—ideally including pre-treatment biopsy specimens—should explore biomarkers of treatment sensitivity. Finally, the mechanistic basis for heightened sensitivity in metastatic versus primary lesions remains uninvestigated and warrants further exploration.

Conclusion

Preoperative immunotherapy and targeted therapy has the potential to enable curative outcomes in patients with solitary HCC lymph node metastasis, although validation in larger studies is required.

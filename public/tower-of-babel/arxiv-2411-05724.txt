Vardan Oganesyan


Abstract




Noncommutativity of monotone Lagrangian cobordisms

Abstract.
We construct a monotone spin Lagrangian cobordism from
L
to
(L_{1},L_{2})
such that there is no monotone spin Lagrangian cobordism from
L
to
(L_{2},L_{1})
, where
L,L_{1},L_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{7}
.

1
Introduction

Lagrangian cobordism is a relation between Lagrangian submanifolds of a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
and was introduced by Arnold in
[
1
]
. First, we need to fix a class of Lagrangians we consider.

Consider the set of immersed Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}ag(M)
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\mathcal{L}ag(M)]
be a set of finite formal linear combinations, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We define an abelian group

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{C}ob(M)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\mathcal{L}ag(M)]/\sim,\;\;\;\text{where $L_{1}+\ldots+L_{k}\;\sim\;L_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+L_{m}^{\prime}\;$ if}\\
\text{$(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})$ is cobordant to $(L_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{m}^{\prime})$}.\end{gathered}

This group was computed by Arnold for
M=\mathbb{C}
and
M=T^{*}S^{1}
in
[
1
]
. Note that the definition of cobordism groups given by Arnold is a little bit different. For exact symplectic manifold
M
the study of the group
\mathcal{C}ob(M)
was reduced to pure topological problem by Eliasberg in
[
11
]
. However, it is hard to compute the group
\mathcal{C}ob(M)
explicitly. The cobordism group for
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and for cotangent bundles were studied in more details by Audin in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

The study of
\mathcal{C}ob(M)
was reduced to pure topological problem for arbitrary
M
by Rathel-Fournier in
[
18
,
19
]
. Moreover, in these papers the groups
\mathcal{C}ob(S_{g})
were computed for
g\geqslant 2
, where
S_{g}
is a surface of genus
g
.

In the definition above we can replace immersed Lagrangians by embedded Lagrangians and immersed cobordisms by embedded cobordisms. The study of embedded cobordisms can also be reduced to topological problems. If there is an immersed Lagrangian cobordism between embedded Lagrangians
(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})
and
(L_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{k}^{\prime})
, then there is embedded Lagrangian cobordisms between them (see
[
5
, Secton 2.3]
).

In contrast, cobordisms satisfying suitable geometric constraints display remarkable rigidity phenomena and their study can not be reduced to pure topology.

Chekanov in
[
9
]
showed that monotone cobordant Lagrangians have the same number (counted with signs) of pseudoholomorphic discs of Maslov index
2
passing through a generic point.

It was shown by Biran and Cornea in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
that monotone Lagrangian cobordisms are closely related to the derived Fukaya category of
M
. Similar results were obtained by Nadler and Tanaka in
[
15
]
.

In this paper we found a new rigidity phenomena. Let
\mathcal{L}ag^{s,m}(M)
be the set of spin and monotone Lagrangians. We construct Lagrangians
L,L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathcal{L}ag^{s,m}(M)
and a spin monotone Lagrangian cobordism between
L
and
(L_{1},L_{2})
. Then, we prove that there is no spin monotone Lagrangian cobordism between
L
and
(L_{2},L_{1})
. Note that there is embedded Lagrangian cobordism between
L
and
(L_{2},L_{1})
, but this cobordism can not be spin and monotone.

Let
F\mathcal{L}ag^{s,m}(M)
be a free group generated by spin monotone Lagrangians. Consider a set of relations
R

\begin{gathered}\text{ $L_{1}\ldots L_{k}\;=\;L_{1}^{\prime}\ldots L_{m}^{\prime}$ \quad if }\\
\text{ $(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})$ is spin monotone Lagrangian cobordant to $(L_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{m}^{\prime})$}.\end{gathered}

Let
H\subset F\mathcal{L}ag^{s,m}(M)
be a normal subgroup generated by the relations
R
. We define

F\mathcal{C}ob^{s,m}(M)=F\mathcal{L}ag^{s,m}(M)/H.

The following question was stated by Biran and Cornea

Is the group
F\mathcal{C}ob^{s,m}(M)
commutative?

Note that
H
contains much more elements than
R
. So, existence of the Lagrangians
L,L_{1},L_{2}
, constructed in this paper, does not imply that
L_{1}L_{2}\neq L_{2}L_{1}
in the group
F\mathcal{C}ob^{s,m}(M)
.

The Lagrangians constructed in this paper should help to study commutativity of
F\mathcal{C}ob^{s,m}(M)
. This question will be studied in a further paper, where we will prove that
L_{1}L_{2}\neq L_{2}L_{1}
.

We can define a similar free group for immersed Lagrangians, but we will get a group isomorphic to
Cob(M)
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Lagrangian submanifolds of
\mathbb{C}P^{n}

We consider
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
endowed with the standard Fubini-Study form
\omega_{FS}
such that
\omega_{FS}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})=\pi
. There are two homomorphisms assigned to each embedded Lagrangian
L\subset\mathbb{C}P^{n}

\mu:\pi_{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n},L)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\quad\omega_{FS}:\pi_{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n},L)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

where
\mu
is the Maslov class (see
[
14
, Section 2]
or
[
10
]
) and
\omega_{FS}(\alpha)=\int_{\alpha}\omega_{FS}
. Define the minimal Maslov number

N_{L}=min\{\mu(\alpha)>0|\;\alpha\in\pi_{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n},L)\},\;\;\;\text{$N_{L}=0\;$ if $\;\mu\equiv 0$}.

Note that
N_{L}
is even when
L
is orientable. We say that
L
is monotone if there exists a constant
\tau>0
such that

\mu(\alpha)=\tau\omega_{FS}(\alpha),\;\;\;\text{for any $\alpha\in\pi_{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n},L)$ and $N_{L}\geqslant 2$}.

The constant
\tau
is called the monotonicity constant. It can be shown (see
[
14
, Section 2]
) that
\tau=\frac{2(n+1)}{\pi}
(if it exists).

We say that a Lagrangian
L
is spin if it is orientable and the second Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{2}(L)
vanishes.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{n}
be orientable closed monotone Lagrangians. Then, the minimal Maslov number of the pair
N_{L_{1},L_{2}}
is the greatest common divisor of
N_{L_{1}}
and
N_{L_{2}}
.

The pair
(L_{1},L_{2})
is called relatively spin if there exists an
h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
such that
h|_{L_{1}}=w_{2}(L_{1})
and
h|_{L_{2}}=w_{2}(L_{2})
. Note that
h
is defined up to elements of
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n},L_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cap H^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{n},L_{2};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. When we choose orientations of
L_{1},L_{2}
and
h
, then we say that we fix relative spin structure. Note that if
L_{1},L_{2}
are both spin, then the pair
(L_{1},L_{2})
is always relatively spin.

We say that the intersection
S=L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is clean if
S
is a manifold and
T_{p}L_{1}\cap T_{p}L_{2}=T_{p}S
for any
p\in S
. For example, if
L_{1}=L_{2}
, then their intersection is clean. Transversal intersections are also clean.

Theorem 2.1
.

(see
[
20
, Section 2.3]
)
Assume that

-
L_{1},L_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{n}
are monotone Lagrangians and the minimal Maslov number of the pair
N_{L_{1},L_{2}}>3

-
L_{1},L_{2}
are spin and we fix a relative spin structure

- Let
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[T,T^{-1}]
be the algebra of Laurent polynomials, where we grade
deg(T)=-N_{L_{1},L_{2}}
.

Then, the Floer homology
HF_{*}(L_{1},L_{2};\Lambda)
is defined. These homology groups depend on the relative spin structure of the pair
L_{1},L_{2}
.

If
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is clean and
connected
, then there exists a homological spectral sequence

E_{p,q}^{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}H_{q}(L_{1}\cap L_{2};\mathbb{Z}),\quad\text{if $p\in N_{L_{1},L_{2}}\;\mathbb{Z}$,}\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

\bigoplus_{p+q=*}E^{\infty}_{p,q}=HF_{*}(L_{1},L_{2};\Lambda).

It turns out that

HF_{*}(L_{1},L_{2};\Lambda)=HF_{*+2}(L_{1},L_{2};\Lambda).

Let
N
be a positive divisor of
N_{L_{1},L_{2}}
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}=\mathbb{Z}[T,T^{-1}]
, where
deg(T)=-N
. Then

HF_{*}(L_{1},L_{2};\widetilde{\Lambda})=\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{\frac{N_{L_{1},L_{2}}}{N}-1}HF_{*+kN}(L_{1},L_{2};\Lambda).

Note that if
L_{1}=L_{2}
, then the spectral sequence coincide with the spectral sequence of Oh.

The theorem above holds true in a more general setting and not only for Lagrangians of
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. Also, the theorem holds true when
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is clean, but not necessarily connected. However, when
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is not connected we need to replace the spectral sequence by two other spectral sequences and more complicated coefficients. The reader can find all the details in
[
20
]
.

In this paper we work only with connected clean intersections.

2.2
Lagrangian cobordism

In this section we give only basic definitions. Much more details can be found in
[
5
]
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold and
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k},L_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{m}^{\prime}\subset M
be monotone embedded Lagrangians with the same monotonicity constants. In the previous section we discussed that if
(M,\omega)=(\mathbb{C}P^{n},\omega_{FS})
, then monotonicity constant is the same for all monotone Lagrangians. Consider the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
with the standard symplectic form
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
and
M\times\mathbb{C}
endowed with
\omega\oplus\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
.

We say that there is a monotone cobordism

V:\;(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})\rightsquigarrow(L_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{m}^{\prime})

if there exists an embedded monotone Lagrangian
V\subset M\times\mathbb{C}
such that the projection
\pi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
has the form shown in the following figure

In other words, for some compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
we have

\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus K)=\bigcup_{s=1}^{m}\big((-\infty,a)\times\{s\}\times L_{s}^{\prime}\big)\bigcup_{r=1}^{k}\big((b,+\infty)\times\{r\}\times L_{r}\big),

where
a,b
are some real numbers.

We say that the cobordism is spin if all Lagrangians
V,L_{s},L_{r}^{\prime}
are spin. Let
j_{s}:L_{s}\hookrightarrow V
and
j_{r}^{\prime}:L_{r}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V
be embedding as ends, i.e.

j_{s}(L_{s})=\{c\}\times\{s\}\times L_{s},\;\;\;j_{r}^{\prime}(L_{r}^{\prime})=\{c\}\times\{r\}\times L_{r}^{\prime}

for some big
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
h
,
h_{i}
,
h_{r}^{\prime}
be a relative spin structures of
V
,
L_{i}
and
L_{r}^{\prime}
, respectively. We say that relative spin structures of
L_{i}
,
L_{r}^{\prime}
are induced from
V
if

j_{s}^{*}h=h_{s},\quad(j_{r}^{\prime})^{*}h=h_{r}^{\prime}.

The following theorem shows that cobordant Lagrangians are closely related to each other.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
6
, Corollary 1.1.3]
,
[
12
, Section 4]
)
Let
L,L_{1},L_{2},K
be monotone spin Lagrangians with equal monotonicity constants. Assume that there exists a spin monotone cobordism
V:L\rightsquigarrow(L_{1},L_{2})
. If relative spin structures of
L,L_{1},L_{2},K
are induced from
V
and
N_{K}>3
, then we have the following exact sequence

\rightarrow HF_{*}(K,L_{2};\Lambda)\rightarrow HF_{*}(K,L_{1};\Lambda)\rightarrow HF_{*}(K,L;\Lambda)\rightarrow HF_{*-1}(K,L_{2};\Lambda)\rightarrow,

where
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[T,T^{-1}]
and
deg(T)
is the same for all Floer homology groups and is a common divisor of
N_{K,L_{1}}
,
N_{K,L_{2}}
,
N_{K,L}
,
N_{V}
.

Note that all the mentioned Lagrangians are orientable. This means that the minimal Maslov numbers are even and they all are divisible by
2
.

2.3
Clean intersection and surgery

Let
M
be a closed symplectic manifold and
L_{1},L_{2}
be orientable monotone Lagrangian submanifolds. Recall that the intersection
S=L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is called clean if
S
is a manifold and
T_{p}L_{1}\cap T_{p}L_{2}=T_{p}S
for any
p\in S
.

Theorem 2.3
.

(see
[
16
, Section 2.2 and Section 6.1]
)
Assume that
S=L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is clean and
connected
. Assume that
\pi_{1}(M)=0
. Then we can do a surgery and get a monotone Lagrangian
L=L_{1}\#_{S}L_{2}\subset M
such that there is a monotone cobordism

V:L\rightsquigarrow(L_{1},L_{2})

Let us note the following

- By definition,
V\subset M\times\mathbb{C}
and the projection
V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
has the form shown in the following figure

- The condition that
S
is connected is crucial.

-
V
is homotopy equivalent to
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
, i.e.
V
is homotopy equivalent to
L_{1}
attached to
L_{2}
along
S
.

- Theorem
2.2
says that there is a long exact sequence related to Lagrangians
L_{1},L_{2},L_{1}\#_{S}L_{2}
.

-
L_{1}\#_{S}L_{2}
may not be Hamiltonian isotopic to
L_{2}\#_{S}L_{1}
.

The reader can find more details about Lagrangian surgery in
[
13
]
.

3
Construction of noncommutative Lagrangian cobordisms

Let
L_{2}=\mathbb{R}P^{7}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{7}
. In this section we construct a monotone spin Lagrangian
L_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{7}
and a monotone spin Lagrangian cobordism
V:L\rightsquigarrow(L_{1},L_{2})
. Then, we prove that there is no monotone spin Lagrangian cobordism between
L
and
(L_{2},L_{1})
. The construction consists of the following steps:

- We construct
L_{1}
and show that the intersection
S=L_{1}\cap L_{2}
is clean and connected. Then, Theorem
2.3
says that we can do the surgery and get a monotone Lagrangian
L=L_{1}\#_{S}L_{2}
and a monotone Lagrangian cobordism
V:L\rightsquigarrow(L_{1},L_{2})
.

- We prove that the Lagrangians
V,L,L_{1},L_{2}
are spin.

- Using the cobordism
V:L\rightsquigarrow(L_{1},L_{2})
and the long exact sequence from Theorem
2.2
we prove that there is no spin monotone cobordism from
L
to
(L_{2},L_{1})
.

3.1
Construction of the Lagrangian
L_{1}

Consider a submanifold
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}P^{7}
defined by a quadric

u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}+u_{4}^{2}-u_{5}^{2}-u_{6}^{8}-u_{7}^{2}-u_{8}^{2}=0

(3.1)

and
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{8}
defined by a system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}+u_{4}^{2}+u_{5}^{2}+u_{6}^{2}+u_{7}^{2}+u_{8}^{2}=1\\
u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}+u_{4}^{2}-u_{5}^{2}-u_{6}^{8}-u_{7}^{2}-u_{8}^{2}=0\end{array}\right.

We see that
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=S^{3}\times S^{3}
and
\mathcal{R}=(S^{3}\times S^{3})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where we identify antipodal points. It is known that

\mathcal{R}=(S^{3}\times S^{3})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}=SO(4)=SO(3)\times S^{3}=\mathbb{R}P^{3}\times S^{3}.

Let
S^{1}=\{e^{i\pi\varphi}|\;0\leqslant\varphi\leqslant 2\}
be the unit circle. Consider
\mathcal{R}\times S^{1}
and a map

\begin{gathered}\psi:\mathcal{R}\times S^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{3},\\
\psi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi)=\\
[u_{1}e^{\pi\varphi}:u_{2}e^{\pi\varphi}:u_{3}e^{\pi\varphi}:u_{4}e^{\pi\varphi}:u_{5}:u_{6}:u_{7}:u_{8}].\end{gathered}

(3.2)

In fact,
\psi
is not embedding because

\psi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi)=\psi(-u_{1},-u_{2},-u_{3},-u_{4},u_{6},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi+1)

Define an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on
\mathcal{R}\times S^{1}
by the following formula

\begin{gathered}\varepsilon\cdot(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi)=\\
((-1)^{\varepsilon}u_{1},(-1)^{\varepsilon}u_{2},(-1)^{\varepsilon}u_{3},(-1)^{\varepsilon}u_{4},u_{5},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi+1),\;\;\;\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.\end{gathered}

We see that

\psi(\varepsilon\cdot(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi))=\psi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6},u_{7},u_{8},\varphi)

and the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is free on the second factor of
\mathcal{R}\times S^{1}
. This means that we have a fibration

(\mathcal{R}\times S^{1})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{R}}S^{1}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{R}P^{1}=S^{1}.

Denote

L_{1}=\psi\big((\mathcal{R}\times S^{1})/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\big)\subset\mathbb{C}P^{7},

where
\psi
is defined on the quotient by the same formula
(\ref{embformula})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

(see
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
)
We have

-
L_{1}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{R}\times S^{1}=\mathcal{R}P^{3}\times S^{3}\times S^{1}
. In particular
L_{1}
is spin.

-
L_{1}
is monotone embedded Lagrangian of
\mathbb{C}P^{7}
with minimal Maslov number
N_{L}=4
.

Let us prove the following simple lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider the standard
\mathbb{R}P^{7}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{7}
. We have
L_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}P^{7}=\mathcal{R}
and
L_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}P^{7}
is clean.

Proof.

The fact that
L_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}P^{7}=\mathcal{R}
follows directly from map
(\ref{embformula})
. Consider
p\in L_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}P^{7}
and a small neighborhood
U\subset L_{1}
of
p
. The Lagrangian
L_{1}
in
U
can be parameterized in the following way

L_{1}=[f_{1}e^{i\pi\varphi}:f_{2}e^{i\pi\varphi}:f_{3}e^{i\pi\varphi}:f_{4}e^{i\pi\varphi}:f_{5}:f_{6}:f_{7}:f_{8}],\\

where
f_{j}
are real-valued functions. This shows that
T_{p}L_{1}\cap T_{p}\mathbb{R}P^{7}=T_{p}\mathcal{R}
.
∎

3.2
Construction and properties of the cobordism

In the previous section we constructed
L_{1}=\mathcal{R}\times S^{1}
. Denote
L_{2}=\mathbb{R}P^{7}
. Lemma
3.2
says that
L_{1}\cap L_{2}=\mathcal{R}
and is clean. We get from Theorem
2.3
that there exists a monotone Lagrangian and a cobordism

L=L_{1}\#_{\mathcal{R}}L_{2},\quad V:L\rightsquigarrow(L_{1},L_{2}).

Let
j_{1}:L_{1}\hookrightarrow V
and
j_{2}:L_{2}\hookrightarrow V
be embedding as ends, i.e.

j_{1}(L_{1})=\{c\}\times\{1\}\times L_{1},\;\;\;j_{2}(L_{2})=\{c\}\times\{2\}\times L_{2}

for some big
c\in\mathbb{R}
. We also have an embedding
j
of the disjoint union

j:L_{1}\coprod L_{2}\hookrightarrow V,\;\;\;j(L_{1}\coprod L_{2})=(\{c\}\times\{1\}\times L_{1})\coprod(\{c\}\times\{2\}\times L_{2}).

Let
i_{1}:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow L_{1}
be the embedding as the first factor and
i_{2}:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow L_{2}
be the embedding as the set of solutions of the quadric.

Theorem 3.3
.

The Lagrangians
V
and
L
are orientable and spin.

Proof.

Let
U\subset V
be a small neighborhood of
\{c\}\times\{1\}\times L_{1}
in
V
. By definition
U=I\times L_{1}
, where
I
is some small interval. The same is true about some small neighborhood of
\{c\}\times\{2\}\times L_{2}
. This means that

j_{1}^{*}TV=TL_{1}\times\mathbb{R},\;\;\;j_{2}^{*}TV=TL_{2}\times\mathbb{R}.

Let
w_{1}(V)
,
\omega_{2}(V)
be the Stiefel-Whitney classes of
V
. The arguments above show that
\omega_{k}(j_{i}^{*}TV)=\omega_{k}(L_{i})
for
i,k=1,2
. Since
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
are orientable and spin we have

j^{*}w_{k}(V)=w_{k}(L_{1})+w_{k}(L_{2})=0,\quad k=1,2.

Lemma 3.4
.

The pullback
j^{*}
is injective on
H^{k}(V;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
for
k=1,2
.

Proof.

Since
V
is homotopy equivalent to
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
and
L_{1}\cap L_{2}=\mathcal{R}
, we have the exact sequence of Mayer-Vietoris

\rightarrow H^{k}(V;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{(j_{1}^{*},j_{2}^{*})}H^{k}(L_{1};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\oplus H^{k}(L_{2};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{i_{1}^{*}-i_{2}^{*}}H^{k}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow

Since
i_{1}^{*}
is surjective, we obtain that the map
(j_{1}^{*},j_{2}^{*})
is injective. This means that
j^{*}
is injective.
∎

Injectivity of
j^{*}
and
j^{*}w_{k}(V)=0
imply that
w_{k}(V)=0
for
k=0,1
.

Let
h:L\rightarrow V
be the embedding of
L
as an end. The same arguments show that
h^{*}TV=TL\times\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,
\omega_{k}(L)=h^{*}\omega_{k}(V)=0
, where
k=1,2
.
∎

3.3
Nonexistence of the cobordism
L\rightsquigarrow(L_{2},L_{1})

Recall that we denote
\mathbb{R}P^{7}
by
L_{2}
and
N_{L_{2}}=8
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}[T,T^{-1}]
, where
deg(T)=-8
. For any relative spin structure of
L_{2}
and any
k
we have

HF_{2k}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})=0,\quad HF_{2k-1}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Proof.

Since intersection of
L_{2}
with itself is clean and
N_{L_{2},L_{2}}=N_{L_{2}}=8
, we have the homological spectral sequence from Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem
2.1
says that
HF_{m}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})=HF_{m+2}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})
for any
m
. So, it is sufficient to find
HF_{1}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})
and
HF_{2}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})
. Note the only nontrivial columns are
E_{8k,*}^{1}
, where
k
is arbitrary. This means that

\begin{gathered}0=E^{1}_{0,2}=E^{\infty}_{0,2}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;HF_{2}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime})=0,\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=E_{0,1}^{1}=E_{0,1}^{\infty}=HF_{1}(L_{2},L_{2};\Lambda^{\prime}).\end{gathered}

We see that

Lemma
3.6
says that we have two options.

. Then, the long exact sequences
(\ref{firstexact})

\begin{gathered}0\rightarrow HF_{1}(L_{2},L;\Lambda)\rightarrow 0\\
0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}\rightarrow HF_{1}(L_{2},L;\Lambda)\rightarrow 0\end{gathered}

We obviously get a contradiction.

2.
Suppose that
HF_{0}(L_{2},L_{1};\Lambda)=HF_{1}(L_{2},L_{1};\Lambda)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

(\ref{firstexact})
and

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\rightarrow HF_{1}(L_{2},L;\Lambda)\rightarrow 0\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4}\rightarrow HF_{1}(L_{2},L;\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}\rightarrow 0\end{gathered}

The first exact sequence says that there is a surjection from
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

HF_{1}(L_{2},L;\Lambda)
to

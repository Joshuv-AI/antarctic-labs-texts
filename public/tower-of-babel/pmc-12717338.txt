Causal association between inflammatory markers and malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage using Mendelian randomization
Authors: Chang Liu, Chaoqun Yu, Wenzhe Wang, Haining Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe pathogenesis of malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage is not fully understood, as the inflammatory immune microenvironment may play a crucial role in tumor development and progression. This study aimed to investigate the causal associations between specific inflammatory immune markers and the risk of bone and articular cartilage malignancies.MethodsOn the basis of genome-wide association study (GWAS) data from inflammatory factors and blood metabolites, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) with F statistics greater than 10 were selected as valid instrumental variables. Mendelian randomization (MR) methodology was employed to assess causal relationships between inflammatory markers and bone tumor risk, with sensitivity analyses conducted via MR‒Egger, heterogeneity tests, leave-one-out analysis, and MR-PRESSO. Additionally, qRT-PCR was used to validate the expression of key markers in cell lines.ResultsMendelian randomization analysis revealed that elevated CXCL6 levels were positively correlated with tumor occurrence risk (OR = 1.5, 95% CI: 1.1–2.0), while MIP (OR = 0.5, 95% CI: 0.3–0.7), CCL4 (OR = 0.6, 95% CI: 0.4–0.8), and LIF-R (OR = 0.5, 95% CI: 0.3–0.8) were significantly associated with reduced tumor risk. Sensitivity analyses confirmed robust results with no significant horizontal pleiotropy. Meta-analysis showed CCL4 expression was reduced in most studies, while CXCL6 was significantly upregulated in tumor tissues. qRT-PCR validation demonstrated that all four inflammatory markers were significantly higher in tumor cell lines compared to normal cells (P < 0.01).ConclusionThis study, which uses Mendelian randomization, suggests potential causal associations between inflammatory immune markers and the risk of malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04070-1.Keywords: Bone tumor, Malignant neoplasms of articular cartilage, Inflammatory markers, Chemokines, Mendelian randomization

Introduction

Malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage represent a class of highly aggressive and potentially fatal diseases [1, 2]. Despite their relatively low incidence, these tumors are characterized by treatment challenges and poor prognosis, causing a severe decline in patients’ quality of life and posing significant life-threatening risks. Currently, the pathogenesis of these tumors remains incompletely understood, and conventional treatment approaches have limited efficacy, creating an urgent need to deepen our understanding of their molecular pathological mechanisms and identify new diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets [3, 4]. The roles of inflammatory markers such as CXCL6, CCL4 and LIF-R in different tumor subtypes may vary significantly. Existing literature indicates that the expression and function of CXCL6 vary in multiple tumor types. For instance, some studies have found that CXCL6 may promote the invasion and metastasis of tumor cells in osteosarcoma, while its specific role in chondrosarcoma remains unclear. The biological roles of CCL4 and LIF-R in different tumor subtypes may also vary. Future studies should further explore the specific mechanisms of these markers in different tumor types.

In recent years, the tumor microenvironment, particularly the inflammatory immune microenvironment, has gained increasing recognition for its crucial role in tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis [5, 6]. Inflammatory responses, as fundamental reactions to tissue damage, may serve protective functions in suppressing tumor growth but can also promote tumor progression under specific conditions through chronic, persistent inflammation. Notably, chemokine and inflammatory cytokine networks are considered to play vital roles in regulating tumor-associated immune responses [7, 8].

However, research on the causal relationships between specific inflammatory immune markers and malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage remains limited. Traditional epidemiological observational studies struggle to eliminate the influence of confounding factors and reverse causality, whereas experimental studies are constrained by differences between animal models and human diseases. Mendelian randomization (MR), an emerging research method that uses genetic variants as instrumental variables, can provide more reliable causal inference evidence, offering new perspectives for exploring disease etiological mechanisms [9, 10].

Moreover, systematic reviews and meta-analyses can provide more comprehensive and powerful evidence by integrating existing research findings and assessing the expression patterns and clinical significance of specific inflammatory markers in bone tumors. The combined application of these methodological approaches shows promise in revealing the mechanisms of the inflammatory immune microenvironment in malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage, providing a theoretical foundation for precision medicine strategies.

To investigate the causal associations between key inflammatory markers—including CXCL6, MIP, CCL4, and LIF-R—and the risk of malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage while analyzing their expression characteristics in tumor tissues.

Materials and methods

Study design

To explore the causal associations between inflammatory factors and bone tumors systematically, this study employed a rigorous two-sample Mendelian randomization (TSMR)-based approach complemented by sensitivity analyses and meta-analytical validation. Data for inflammatory protein levels were sourced from a large-scale proteomic study, whereas bone tumor data were obtained from a multiethnic GWAS meta-analysis. Instrumental variables (IVs) were then selected using strict criteria (P < 5 × 10⁻⁶, F > 10, r² < 0.001). TSMR analyses were performed via inverse variance weighting (IVW) or Wald ratio methods, with statistical significance assessed at a P < 0.05/n threshold. For associations passing this threshold, sensitivity checks (MR‒Egger, MR-PRESSO, leave-one-out) were conducted to account for pleiotropy and outliers. Finally, causal validation and meta-analysis consolidated the findings, as visualized in the schematic Fig. 1. This multistep framework ensures robustness in dissecting the inflammatory-bone tumor causal landscape, aiding readers in following the analytical pipeline from data input to causal inference.

Data source

This research originated from two high-quality genome-wide association study (GWAS) datasets. Immune cell data were derived from a genome-wide protein quantitative trait locus (pQTL) study [11], which utilized the Olink Target platform to profile 91 plasma inflammatory proteins in 14,824 participants from the UK Biobank and other cohorts. This dataset employed high-throughput proteomic technology to characterize inflammatory factors, with genetic influences analyzed via pQTL mapping (180 pQTLs identified, including 59 cis and 121 trans). The bone tumor data came from a large-scale multiethnic meta-analysis based on the 1000 Genomes Project, encompassing 775 bone tumor patients and 1,332 control subjects, with 7,800,543 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) genotyped. The high quality and extensive representativeness of these datasets, particularly the integration of proteomic and genomic data, provided a robust scientific foundation for the study.

Our study strictly implemented the MR Framework, selected clear instrumental variable criteria (P < 5 × 10⁻⁶, F > 10, r² < 0.001), and verified them through standard sensitivity analysis methods (such as MR-Egger, Cochran’s Q, MR-presso). These methodological rigors do indeed enhance the credibility of causal estimation. However, we also recognize that the sample size of GWAS for bone tumors (775 cases and 1,332 controls) is relatively small, which may affect the statistical power of MR Studies.

Genetic instrumental variable selection

We implemented rigorous genetic instrumental variable selection, rigorous genetic instrumental variable selection and sophisticated statistical analysis frameworks. The instrumental variable selection process established comprehensive screening criteria: P < 5 × 10−6 significance thresholds for immune cells and serum metabolites, F-statistic values exceeding 10, linkage disequilibrium thresholds maintained at r^2 < 0.001, and minimum allele frequencies greater than 0.01. For statistical analysis, researchers have primarily utilized the inverse variance weighted (IVW) approach while employing Wald ratio analysis in cases with single instrumental variables. The research team ensured result reliability through comprehensive sensitivity analyses incorporating MR Egger regression, weighted median methodology, and Cochran’s Q statistic heterogeneity evaluations and subsequently validated significant and potential causal relationships via MR-BMA techniques (Supplementary Table S1).

The Mendelian Randomization (MR) method was adopted to explore the causal association between inflammatory markers and malignant tumors of bone and articular cartilage. To ensure the transparency and repeatability of the research, we publicly store all the input data and effect estimates (SNP-level) used for MR Analysis in an accessible database [https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/].

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis of inflammatory marker expression

To validate our Mendelian randomization findings, we performed quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis to examine the expression levels of CCL4, CXCL6, LIF-R, and MIP mRNA in normal mouse osteoblast cell line MC3T3-E1 and human osteosarcoma cell line MG-63. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara Bio, Kusatsu, Japan) following the manufacturer’s instructions. qRT-PCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). The primer sequences used are listed below: Human primers (for MG-63 cells): CCL4: Forward 5′-AAGCTCTGCGTGACTGTCCT-3′, Reverse 5′-GAGGGTCAGAGCCCATTGGT-3′-CXCL6: Forward 5′-CGCAGGGAATTCACCTCAAG-3′, Reverse 5′-CTTCTGGATCCTGGGTTGCC-3′-LIF-R: Forward 5′-TGGGCTACGAGTGTGAGTCC-3′, Reverse 5′-GGAAGCCACACTTGGATGTG-3′-MIP(CCL3): Forward 5′-ACCATGACACTCTGCAACCA-3′, Reverse5’-GTGGAATCTTCCGGCTGTAG-3′-GAPDH: Forward 5′-GAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTC-3′, Reverse 5′-GAAGATGGTGATGGGATTTC-3′, The PCR cycling conditions were: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s and 60 °C for 1 min. Each reaction was performed in triplicate, and relative gene expression was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method with GAPDH as the housekeeping gene.

Statistical analyses

We utilized two-sample Mendelian randomization to investigate the causal relationships between inflammatory factors and bone tumors. For multiple instrumental variables, we applied inverse variance weighted methods with multiplicative random effects, whereas single instrumental variables were analyzed via the Wald ratio method.

We established dynamic significance thresholds (P < 0.05/n) for statistical analysis, categorizing the results as either significant causal associations or potential associations requiring further investigation. Validation included heterogeneity assessment via Cochran’s Q statistic, sensitivity analysis through MR Egger regression and weighted median methods, and additional analysis of results passing multiple validation steps. This comprehensive approach enhanced scientific rigor, while this comprehensive approach was designed to enhance the scientific rigor of the investigation. By integrating multiple analytical approaches, we aimed to understand the molecular mechanisms underlying bone tumor development, establishing foundations for future precision medicine strategies.

Results

Instrument variables included in the analysis

The selection criteria mentioned above were used to extract valid instrumental variables from inflammatory factors and blood metabolite GWASs. All single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) utilized in the analysis had an F statistic greater than 10. Notably, inflammatory factor-associated variants were excluded from the final analysis because of insufficient statistical significance (P ≥ 5 × 10^−6), and only blood metabolite-related SNPs were retained as instrumental variables.

Potential causal associations identified via MR analysis

This meta-analysis identified key patterns associated with malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage. The study revealed that significantly elevated CXCL6 levels (OR = 1.5, 95% CI: 1.1–2.0) are positively correlated with tumor occurrence, suggesting its potential role in promoting tumor development. In contrast, the MIP (OR = 0.5, 95% CI: 0.3–0.7), CCL4 (OR = 0.6, 95% CI: 0.4–0.8), and LIF-R (OR = 0.5, 95% CI: 0.3–0.8) levels were significantly associated with reduced tumor risk, indicating that these molecules may have protective effects. These findings provide important evidence for understanding the immune microenvironment of bone malignancies while offering potential directions for developing new diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets (Figs. 2 and 3). The six most significant molecules—CXCL6, mitogen-activated protein, tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member, LIF-R, MIP, and CCL4 (p values < 0.2)—are specifically highlighted and magnified, indicating their potential core roles in tumor development and progression. The radial gradient of the heatmap, ranging from deep red (highly significant) to light red (less significant), visually presents the varying importance of different markers, whereas the inner phylogenetic tree reveals functional relationships among these molecules. This multilayered visualization approach not only identifies potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets but also reveals the complex molecular network relationships within the tumor immune microenvironment, providing valuable directions for further research (Fig. 4).

Sensitivity analyses

The statistical validation process incorporated multiple sophisticated techniques. MR‒Egger analysis revealed no evidence of horizontal pleiotropy, suggesting the reliability of our causal inference model. Although the Cochran’s Q statistic indicated potential heterogeneity in the data, our selected analytical approach—the inverse variance-weighted method with multiplicative random effects—was specifically chosen for its capacity to effectively accommodate and adjust for such statistical variations. To further refine our analytical precision, we implemented a leave‒one-out analysis designed to identify causal associations potentially influenced by specific single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) variants. This granular approach allowed us to isolate and examine the impact of individual genetic variants on our broader causal associations. Following the removal of associations potentially skewed by specific SNP variants, we conducted an additional MR-PRESSO (Mendelian Randomization Pleiotropy Residual Sum and Outlier) analysis on the remaining associations.

Analysis of expression level of CCL4, CXCL6, LIF-R and MIP mRNA in normal and tumor tissue

The expression levels of mRNA of four inflammatory markers, namely CCL4, CXCL6, LIF-R and MIP, in normal tissues and tumor tissues were analyzed in detail. The results showed that the expression levels of the above four inflammatory markers in tumor tissues were significantly higher than those in normal tissues. Among them, the expression levels of CCL4 and CXCL6 in tumor tissues were significantly increased, indicating that they may play an important role in the occurrence and development of malignant tumors of bone and articular cartilage. Similarly, the expressions of LIF-R and MIP were also significantly upregulated in tumor tissues, which is consistent with their potential roles in inflammatory responses and the tumor microenvironment. These results suggest that these inflammatory markers may be involved in tumor formation and progression through different mechanisms. Furthermore, statistical analysis revealed that these differences were highly significant (P < 0.01), further supporting our hypothesis that there is an important pathological association between inflammatory markers and malignant tumors of bone and articular cartilage (Fig. 8).

Discussion

This study investigated the causal associations between inflammatory immune markers and malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage through Mendelian randomization and meta-analysis methods and yielded several important findings. First, we revealed that CXCL6 is positively correlated with tumor occurrence risk, whereas MIP, CCL4, and LIF-R are associated with reduced tumor risk, suggesting potential protective roles. These results provide new perspectives for understanding the pathophysiological mechanisms of bone tumors.

The occurrence and development of malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage are closely related to their unique microenvironment. In recent years, numerous studies have shown that inflammatory responses play complex and critical roles in the pathophysiological processes of bone tumors [12, 13]. The inflammatory state in the bone tumor microenvironment may present a “double-edged sword” effect: on the one hand, acute inflammatory responses can activate immune surveillance systems and promote the clearance of tumor cells; on the other hand, chronic persistent inflammation may promote tumor progression through various mechanisms, including promoting tumor angiogenesis, recruiting immunosuppressive cells, activating tumor-associated macrophages, and inducing epithelial‒mesenchymal transition in tumor cells. Particularly in common bone tumors such as osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and chondrosarcoma, studies have shown that inflammatory factor networks are closely related to tumor invasiveness and prognosis [14, 15].

Multiple inflammation-related cytokines and chemokines are abnormally expressed in the bone tumor microenvironment and participate in regulating various biological processes of tumors. Among them, the interleukin family (such as IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, and IL-17), the tumor necrosis factor family, transforming growth factor β, and various chemokines have been confirmed to play important roles in bone tumors. For example, IL-6 is highly expressed in osteosarcoma and promotes tumor cell proliferation and survival; the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is involved in regulating the metastasis process of bone tumors; and the activation of inflammation-related transcription factors such as NF-κB and STAT3 is associated with the maintenance of malignant phenotypes in tumor cells [16–18]. Although these observational studies have provided important clues for understanding the inflammatory microenvironment of bone tumors, they have obvious limitations in causal inference, making it difficult to determine whether changes in these inflammatory factors are the cause or result of tumor occurrence. Therefore, this study adopted the Mendelian randomization method, aiming to overcome the limitations of traditional research and provide more reliable evidence for the causal association between inflammatory factors and the risk of bone tumor occurrence. Our study utilized Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to explore the causal association between inflammatory markers and malignant tumors of bone and articular cartilage. Our results show that certain inflammatory markers, such as CXCL6, may play a certain causal role in the development of these malignant tumors. However, although MR Analysis provided preliminary evidence of these associations, these conclusions still need to be interpreted with caution. The MR Method can effectively reduce the influence of confounding factors and reverse causality, but its results can only be regarded as a strong indication of causal association rather than a final proof. Therefore, in this study, we used expressions such as “may be helpful” or “may play a role in it” to reflect the speculative nature of the research results. Furthermore, we emphasize that these MR Findings need to be further verified through observational studies and mechanism-based experiments to ensure the accuracy and robustness of the conclusions. Future research should focus on higher-precision genetic tools and larger-scale samples to further explore the potential mechanisms and roles of inflammatory markers in the development of malignant tumors of bone and articular cartilage [19–21].

Potential tumor-promoting role of CXCL6. CXCL6 may promote tumor progression via JAK-CXCR1/2 signaling, analogous to its role in cholangiocarcinoma [22]. This chemokine may promote tumor growth and invasion through mechanisms such as promoting angiogenesis in the tumor microenvironment, recruiting immunosuppressive cells, and activating inflammatory signaling pathways. In contrast, we found that MIP, CCL4, and LIF-R are significantly associated with reduced tumor risk, suggesting that they may play important roles in inhibiting bone tumor occurrence. CCL4 may exert protective effects through recruitment and activation of anti-tumor immune cells, potentially enhancing immune surveillance against malignant cells. Previous studies have shown that CCL4 can attract cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer cells to tumor sites, contributing to tumor suppression [23]. These protective inflammatory markers may function by regulating the tumor microenvironment, promoting antitumor immune responses, and inhibiting malignant cell proliferation. Notably, LIF-R potentially plays a protective role. The leukemia inhibitory factor signaling pathway is involved in regulating cell differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis [24, 25]. Our Mendelian randomization analysis of gene expression further suggested that alterations in specific genes may contribute to bone tumor development by affecting the leukemia inhibitory factor pathway. These findings provide a theoretical basis for developing targeted therapeutic strategies for the leukemia inhibitory factor signaling pathway.

The two-sample Mendelian randomization method adopted in this study has significant advantages. First, by using genetic variants as instrumental variables, we were able to minimize confounding factors and reverse causality issues present in traditional observational studies. Second, our implementation of strict instrumental variable selection criteria (such as F statistics > 10) and multilevel sensitivity analyses ensured the reliability and robustness of the study results.

Limitations

While our analysis covered 91 inflammatory factors, this represents only a fraction of the complex inflammatory network. Second, the statistical power of our MR analysis is constrained by the sample size of the outcome data (n = 775 cases for malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage). Although sourced from the robust UK Biobank, this relatively small case count for a genetic study raises two potential issues. This may lead to weak instrument bias, which could skew causal estimates and increase the risk of Type II errors, where true but modest causal effects are missed. This sample size limitation precluded more granular analyses.

Conclusion

This study provides compelling evidence from Mendelian randomization and meta-analysis suggesting potential causal associations between specific inflammatory markers and the risk of malignant neoplasms of bone and articular cartilage. Elevated CXCL6 levels were associated with increased tumor risk, whereas MIP, CCL4, and LIF-R showed inverse associations, suggesting possible protective roles.

Floquet operator dynamics and orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle
Hsiu-Chung Yeh, Aditi Mitra

Abstract
Operator spreading under stroboscopic time evolution due to a unitary is studied. An operator Krylov space is constructed and related to orthogonal polynomials on a unit circle (OPUC), as well as to the Krylov space of the edge operator of the Floquet transverse field Ising model with inhomogeneous couplings (ITFIM). The Verblunsky coefficients in the OPUC representation are related to the Krylov angles parameterizing the ITFIM. The relations between the OPUC and spectral functions are summarized and several applications are presented. These include derivation of analytic expressions for the OPUC for persistent m m -periodic dynamics, and the numerical construction of the OPUC for autocorrelations of the homogeneous Floquet-Ising model as well as the Z 3 Z_{3} clock model. The numerically obtained Krylov angles of the Z 3 Z_{3} clock model with long-lived period tripled autocorrelations show a spatial periodicity of six, and this observation is used to develop an analytically solvable model for the ITFIM that mimics this behavior.



I
Introduction

Krylov subspace methods are a promising avenue for studying dynamics of complex systems as they typically map the problem in question to an effective single particle problem in one dimension, the Krylov chain
Vishwanath and Müller 2008
;
Schollwöck 2005
;
Schollwöck 2011
. For a hermitian operator undergoing continuous time evolution due to a Hamiltonian, this single particle problem is a nearest-neighbor hopping model, where the hopping parameters are in general inhomogeneous
Parker et al. 2019
;
Yates et al. 2020a
;
Yates et al. 2020b
;
Yates et al. 2022
;
Nandy et al. 2025
;
Yeh et al. 2023
;
Rabinovici et al. 2021
;
Rabinovici et al. 2022
;
Dymarsky and Gorsky 2020
;
Dymarsky and Smolkin 2021
. For a hermitian operator undergoing stroboscopic dynamics due to a unitary, this single particle problem is the inhomogeneous Floquet transverse field Ising model (ITFIM)
Yeh and Mitra 2024
;
Yeh and Mitra 2025
. In particular, the transverse field and Ising couplings are spatially inhomogeneous and represented as angles, dubbed the Krylov angles.

The single-particle nature of the ITFIM is apparent via a Jordan-Wigner transformation that maps the ITFIM to a unitary that is bilinear in Majorana fermions. It should be noted that Hamiltonian dynamics too can be studied using an effective ITFIM if one studied the dynamics at stroboscopic times. This observation is helpful because often one may be able to make more precise statements about stroboscopic dynamics rather than continuous time dynamics. For example, Ref.
Yeh and Mitra 2025
shows a direct relation between the Krylov angles and the stroboscopic autocorrelation such that the stroboscopic dynamics is constrained by unitarity.

The recursion relations obeyed by the orthonormal basis of operators generated by Hamiltonian dynamics, are identical to the recursion relations obeyed by orthogonal polynomials
Vishwanath and Müller 2008
;
Mück and Yang 2022
. This also generalizes to Floquet dynamics, where now the orthogonal polynomials lie on the unit circle
Szegő 1939
;
Suchsland et al. 2025
;
Kolganov and Trunin 2025
. This connection is useful because analytic results for orthogonal polynomials can be used to capture non-trivial dynamics
Mück and Yang 2022
. Moreover, the dynamics can be studied directly using orthogonal polynomials without specifying underlying physical models. In particular, given a choice of OPUC, and an initial choice of an operator, an entire Krylov subspace can be generated.

Interestingly, the unitary in the Krylov basis has a simple 5-diagonal form when expressed in the Majorana representation of the ITFIM. When approaching the problem using OPUC, this 5-diagonal structure goes by the name of the CMV basis
Cantero et al. 2003
;
Cantero et al. 2005
;
Simon 2007
.

The goal of this paper is to outline the connection between OPUC in general and the CMV basis in particular, and the physics approach of representing the Krylov subspace as a ITFIM. After making this connection, we derive analytical expressions for the OPUC for some simple, physically motivated problems. In addition, the
Z_{3}
clock model is studied numerically, and an attempt is made to capture salient features of the dynamics of its long-lived edge mode by modeling it by an analytically solvable ITFIM.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
we express the stroboscopic dynamics of a general hermitian operator under a general unitary in terms of OPUC, showing how one may derive a convenient 5-diagonal basis known as the CMV basis. We then summarize how the very same stroboscopic dynamics can be captured by the dynamics of an edge operator of the ITFIM. We then go on to explicitly relate the CMV and the ITFIM representations showing that the Verblusnky coefficients of the former are directly related to the Krylov angles of the latter.
In Section
III
we review relations between OPUC and spectral functions, and derive relations between the OPUC and the continued fraction expression for the Laplace transform of autocorrelation functions. In Section
IV
we derive analytic expressions for the OPUC for some simple
m
-periodic persistent dynamics where
m=1,2,3,4,6
. In Section
V
, we numerically study long-lived and short-lived autocorrelations of the
Z_{3}
clock model. The numerical results for the Krylov angles for the long-lived mode is then used to motivate a simple, analytically solvable model of an ITFIM that mimics the dynamics. This model is one where the Krylov angles have a spatial periodicity of six. Finally we conclude in Section
VI
. Ten appendices provide intermediate details of the derivations as well as the OPUC for the Floquet transverse field Ising model with homogeneous transverse and Ising couplings.

II
OPUC in Floquet dynamics

II.1
From Floquet Operator Krylov space to OPUC

The unitary evolution of a Hermitian operator
O
is described by
O(n)=(U_{F}^{\dagger})^{n}OU_{F}^{n}
, where
U_{F}
is the Floquet unitary and
n
is the stroboscopic
time. By vectorization of operators, the Floquet time evolution can be expressed as
|O(n))=K^{n}|O)
, where
K
is a superoperator defined as
K|O)=U_{F}^{\dagger}OU_{F}
. Therefore, the operator under Floquet dynamics expands a vector space into a Floquet operator Krylov space corresponding to
\{|O),K|O),K^{2}|O),\ldots\}
. The inner product between two operator is defined as
(A|B)=\text{Tr}[A^{\dagger}B]/\text{Tr}[\mathbb{I}]
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity operator. According to the Gram-Schmidt procedure, one constructs a set of orthonormal basis vectors
\{|\mathcal{O}_{k})\}
. There exists a linear transformation from
\{|O(n))\}
to
\{|\mathcal{O}_{k})\}
Yates and Mitra 2021
;
Suchsland et al. 2025
;
Yeh and Mitra 2024
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{O}_{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\kappa_{kn}|O(n)),

(1)

where
\kappa_{kn}
encodes the details of the Gram-Schmidt procedure. This also implies

\displaystyle|{O}(k))=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\kappa^{\prime}_{kn}|\mathcal{O}_{n}),

(2)

such that
(\mathcal{O}_{k}|O(m))=0
for
k>m
. We now follow the discussion in
Suchsland et al. 2025
. By applying the definition
|O(n))=K^{n}|O)
and inserting the superoperator identity
\sum_{z}|z)(z|
with
|z)
being the
eigenoperator of
K
,
K|z)=z|z)
, we obtain the following relation

\displaystyle|\mathcal{O}_{k})=\sum_{z}P_{k}(z)|z)(z|O),\ P_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\kappa_{kn}z^{n}.

(3)

Since
K
describes unitary dynamics,
z
lies on the unit circle in the complex plane. Therefore,
\{P_{k}(z)\}
are called orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle (OPUC).

The orthogonal conditions for OPUC can be derived from the orthogonality of the operator Krylov basis vectors. To show this, we start with (
1
) and consider the inner product
(\mathcal{O}_{j}|\mathcal{O}_{k})
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{j}|\mathcal{O}_{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\overline{\kappa_{jn}}\kappa_{km}A(m-n),

(4)

where we have introduced the definition of the autocorrelation function
A(n)=(O|K^{n}|O)
and have applied the time translation property of the inner product
(O(n)|O(m))=(O|O(m-n))
.
Note that due to the cyclic property of the trace, the autocorrelation also obeys the property
A(n)=A(-n)
.

To proceed, we use the Fourier transformation relation between the autocorrelation
A(n)
and the spectral function
\Phi(e^{i\omega})
as below

\displaystyle A(n)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\omega\Phi(e^{i\omega})e^{i\omega n};

(5)

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{i\omega})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}A(n)e^{-i\omega n}.

(6)

Note that the spectral function is positive definite. To see this, we insert the complete basis of the Floquet unitary in (
6
), obtaining

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{i\omega})=\frac{1}{\text{Tr}[\mathbb{I}]}\sum_{j,k}|\langle j|O|k\rangle|^{2}\delta_{F}(\omega-\epsilon_{j}+\epsilon_{k}),

(7)

where
U|k\rangle=e^{-i\epsilon_{k}}|k\rangle
, and
\delta_{F}(\omega)
is the Floquet Dirac delta function:
\delta_{F}(\omega)=\sum_{m}\delta_{D}(\omega+2\pi m)
with
\delta_{D}(\omega)
being the usual Dirac delta function. Therefore,
\Phi(e^{i\omega})=\Phi(e^{-i\omega})
and
\Phi(e^{i\omega})\geq 0
.

By substituting
(
5
)
in (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\omega\Phi(e^{i\omega})\overline{\left(\sum_{n=0}^{j}\kappa_{jn}e^{in\omega}\right)}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{k}\kappa_{km}e^{im\omega}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\omega\Phi(e^{i\omega})\overline{P_{j}(e^{i\omega})}P_{k}(e^{i\omega}),

(8)

where we have applied
(\mathcal{O}_{k}|\mathcal{O}_{p})=\delta_{kp}
. Note that the spectral function serves as the weighting function of the inner product between the OPUC. With
z=e^{i\omega}
, we define the inner product of two generic polynomials,
F(z)
and
H(z)
, on the unit circle as

\displaystyle\langle F(z),H(z)\rangle\equiv\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\Phi(z)\overline{F(z)}H(z).

(9)

Since
\Phi(e^{i\omega})=\Phi(e^{-i\omega})
and
\Phi(e^{i\omega})\geq 0
, this definition of inner product of polynomials guarantees the following properties

\displaystyle\overline{\langle F(z),H(z)\rangle}=\langle H(z),F(z)\rangle;

(10)

\displaystyle\langle F(z),F(z)\rangle\geq 0.

(11)

Although we introduced OPUC from
(
3
)
with
|z|=1
, in the following discussion, we will allow the OPUC to be evaluated on the whole complex plane. This is natural to do because the poles insides the unit circle contribute to
(
9
)
due to the residue theorem.

The above discussion shows the equivalence of inner product between operator Krylov space and OPUC:
(\mathcal{O}_{k}|\mathcal{O}_{p})=\langle P_{k}(z),P_{p}(z)\rangle
. Moreover, the dynamics in operator Krylov space can be represented as some equivalent computations in terms of OPUC. For example,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{k}|K|\mathcal{O}_{p})=\langle P_{k}(z),zP_{p}(z)\rangle;

(12)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{k}|O(m))=\langle P_{k}(z),z^{m}\rangle.

(13)

The detailed derivation of the above results is presented in Appendix
A
. Therefore, one can apply the techniques from OPUC to understand Floquet operator dynamics. Below, we demonstrate that OPUC serve as a powerful tool for computing the analytic expression for the matrix element
(\mathcal{O}_{j}|K|\mathcal{O}_{k})
.

OPUC are known to satisfy the
Szegő recurrence relations
Simon 2005
(see derivation in Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}P_{k+1}(z)=zP_{k}(z)-\overline{\alpha_{k}}P_{k}^{*}(z);

(14a)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}P_{k+1}^{*}(z)=P_{k}^{*}(z)-\alpha_{k}zP_{k}(z),

(14b)

where
\alpha_{k}
are the Verblunsky coefficients and
\rho_{k}
is defined by
\rho_{k}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}
. The initial conditions are

\displaystyle P_{0}(z)=P_{0}^{*}(z)=1.

(15)

Note that
P_{k}^{*}(z)
is not the complex conjugation of
P_{k}(z)
but called the
*
-reverse polynomials
Ismail and Assche 2020
and defined as

\displaystyle P_{k}^{*}(z)=z^{k}\overline{P_{k}(1/\overline{z})}.

(16)

With
(
12
)
and
(
14a
)
, one has

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{j}|K|\mathcal{O}_{k})=\rho_{k}\langle P_{j}(z),P_{k+1}(z)\rangle+\overline{\alpha_{k}}\langle P_{j}(z),P_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle,

(17)

where the first term is not zero when
j=k+1
. The second term is not zero when
0\leq j\leq k
. This is because
P_{k}^{*}(z)
is a polynomial of degree
k
(namely,
P_{k}^{*}(z)
consists of terms like
\{1,z,z^{2},\ldots,z^{k}\}
) and
P_{j}(z)
is orthogonal to any polynomial with degree smaller than
j
. This orthogonal condition comes from
(
13
)
and the Gram-Schmidt procedure
(\mathcal{O}_{j}|O(k))=0
for
j>k
. With the analytic result of
\langle P_{j}(z),P_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle
derived in Appendix
C
, the matrix element of
(\mathcal{O}_{j}|K|\mathcal{O}_{k})
has the upper-Hessenberg form

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{j}|K|\mathcal{O}_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-\overline{\alpha_{k}}\alpha_{j-1}\prod_{l=j}^{k-1}\rho_{l},&\text{for }0\leq j\leq k;\\
\rho_{k},&\text{for }j=k+1;\\
0&\text{else.}\end{array}\right.

Although we obtain the analytic expression for
(\mathcal{O}_{j}|K|\mathcal{O}_{k})
, the Floquet operator dynamics is non-local in Krylov orthonormal basis. As pointed out in Refs.
Yeh and Mitra 2024
;
Yeh and Mitra 2025
, one can construct a different basis, where Floquet dynamics of any Hermitian operator, generated by any unitary, can be mapped to the dynamics of the edge operator of an effective 1D Floquet inhomogeneous transverse-field Ising model (ITFIM). Moreover, the dynamics is local in this representation because the ITFIM is a local model comprising of single site and two-site unitaries. Interestingly, such a basis has been discovered by M.J. Cantero,
L. Moral and L. Velázquez, now known as the CMV basis in the context of OPUC
Cantero et al. 2003
;
Cantero et al. 2005
;
Kolganov and Trunin 2025
. In the following subsection, we present the relation between the ITFIM and the CMV basis.

II.2
ITFIM and the CMV basis

We first summarize the results of the mapping of the Krylov dynamics to the ITFIM from Ref.
Yeh and Mitra 2024
;
Yeh and Mitra 2025
. The 1D Floquet ITFIM parametrized by Krylov angles, and with open boundary conditions is

\displaystyle U_{F}=U_{z}U_{xx};

(21a)

\displaystyle U_{z}=\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}e^{-i\frac{\theta_{2l-1}}{2}\sigma_{l}^{z}};\ U_{xx}=\prod_{l=1}^{(N-2)/2}e^{-i\frac{\theta_{2l}}{2}\sigma_{l}^{x}\sigma_{l+1}^{x}}.

(21b)

Above,
\sigma^{x,z}_{l}
are the Pauli matrices on site
l
, we assume
N
is even and
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N-1}\}\in[0,\pi]
are the Krylov angles. The odd Krylov angles
\theta_{2l-1}
denote the strength of the transverse field on site
l
while the even Krylov angles
\theta_{2l}
denote the strength of the Ising interaction between the spins on sites
l,l+1
.
The above model is effectively a non-interacting model. To see this, we define the Jordan-Wigner transformation

\displaystyle\gamma_{2\ell-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{\ell};

\displaystyle\gamma_{2\ell}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{y}_{\ell}.

(22)

After a Jordan-Wigner transformation,
U_{F}
is bilinear in the Majorana fermions since

\displaystyle U_{z}=\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}e^{-\frac{\theta_{2l-1}}{2}\gamma_{2l-1}\gamma_{2l}};U_{xx}=\prod_{l=1}^{(N-2)/2}e^{-\frac{\theta_{2l}}{2}\gamma_{2l}\gamma_{2l+1}}.

(23)

Let us consider an operator
\Psi
that can be written as a linear combination of Majoranas,
\Psi=\sum_{k}\psi_{k}\gamma_{k}
. The coefficients can be viewed as a column vector
\vec{\psi}=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\ldots)^{\intercal}
. Under the effect of the two unitaries, the coefficients transform according to,
U_{z}^{\dagger}\Psi U_{z}=\sum_{k}\psi_{k}^{\prime}\gamma_{k}
with
\vec{\psi}^{\prime}=M_{z}\vec{\psi}
and
U_{xx}^{\dagger}\Psi U_{xx}=\sum_{k}\psi_{k}^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{k}
with
\vec{\psi}^{\prime\prime}=M_{xx}\vec{\psi}
. For example for
N=6

\displaystyle M_{z}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{1}&\sin\theta_{1}&0&0&0&0\\
-\sin\theta_{1}&\cos\theta_{1}&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&\cos\theta_{3}&\sin\theta_{3}&0&0\\
0&0&-\sin\theta_{3}&\cos\theta_{3}&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\cos\theta_{5}&\sin\theta_{5}\\
0&0&0&0&-\sin\theta_{5}&\cos\theta_{5}\end{pmatrix};

(24)

\displaystyle M_{xx}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&\cos\theta_{2}&\sin\theta_{2}&0&0&0\\
0&-\sin\theta_{2}&\cos\theta_{2}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\cos\theta_{4}&\sin\theta_{4}&0\\
0&0&0&-\sin\theta_{4}&\cos\theta_{4}&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

Denoting the unitary evolution in the Majorana basis after one time step as

\displaystyle(\gamma_{j}|K_{I}|\gamma_{k})=\frac{1}{\text{Tr}[\mathbb{I}]}{\rm Tr}\biggl[\gamma_{j}U_{F}^{\dagger}\gamma_{k}U_{F}\biggr],

(26)

K_{I}
in the single Majorana basis has the form

\displaystyle K_{I}

\displaystyle=M_{xx}M_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{1}&\sin\theta_{1}&0&0&0&0\\
-\sin\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}&\cos\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}&\sin\theta_{2}\cos\theta_{3}&\sin\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{3}&0&0\\
\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}&-\cos\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}&\cos\theta_{2}\cos\theta_{3}&\cos\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{3}&0&0\\
0&0&-\sin\theta_{3}\cos\theta_{4}&\cos\theta_{3}\cos\theta_{4}&\sin\theta_{4}\cos\theta_{5}&\sin\theta_{4}\sin\theta_{5}\\
0&0&\sin\theta_{3}\sin\theta_{4}&-\cos\theta_{3}\sin\theta_{4}&\cos\theta_{4}\cos\theta_{5}&\cos\theta_{4}\sin\theta_{5}\\
0&0&0&0&-\sin\theta_{5}&\cos\theta_{5}\end{pmatrix}.

(27)

Generalizing the above result to arbitrary system sizes, the matrix elements in the Majorana basis of the ITFIM have the following form

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+1}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k-1})=\sin\theta_{2k-1}\sin\theta_{2k};

(28a)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+1}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k})=-\cos\theta_{2k-1}\sin\theta_{2k};

(28b)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+1}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k+1})=\cos\theta_{2k}\cos\theta_{2k+1};

(28c)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+1}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k+2})=\cos\theta_{2k}\sin\theta_{2k+1};

(28d)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+2}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k+1})=-\sin\theta_{2k+1}\cos\theta_{2k+2};

(28e)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+2}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k+2})=\cos\theta_{2k+1}\cos\theta_{2k+2};

(28f)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+2}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k+3})=\sin\theta_{2k+2}\cos\theta_{2k+3};

(28g)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{2k+2}|K_{I}|\gamma_{2k+4})=\sin\theta_{2k+2}\sin\theta_{2k+3},

(28h)

where the boundary condition
\theta_{0}=\theta_{N}=0
is applied. Refs.
Yeh and Mitra 2024
;
Yeh and Mitra 2025
derived exact relations between the matrix elements of the
N
-dimensional unitary in its upper-Hessenberg form (
II.1
) and the first
N
Krylov angles of the ITFIM. Therefore, the ITFIM can reproduce any Floquet dynamics of Hermitian operators.

We now present the CMV basis
Simon 2005
and its relation to the Majorana representation of the ITFIM. The CMV basis is related to the OPUC as follows

\displaystyle\chi_{2k}=z^{-k}P_{2k};

\displaystyle\chi_{2k+1}=z^{-k-1}P_{2k+1}^{*}.

(29)

Now we show how (

) is recovered through computations in the CMV basis. For this, the following identities are helpful (see Appendix
D
for details),

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k},z\chi_{2k-2}\rangle=\rho_{2k-2}\rho_{2k-1},

(30a)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k},z\chi_{2k-1}\rangle=-\overline{\alpha_{2k-2}}\rho_{2k-1},

(30b)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k},z\chi_{2k}\rangle=-\alpha_{2k-1}\overline{\alpha_{2k}},

(30c)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k},z\chi_{2k+1}\rangle=-\alpha_{2k-1}\rho_{2k},

(30d)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k+1},z\chi_{2k}\rangle=\rho_{2k}\overline{\alpha_{2k+1}},

(30e)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k+1},z\chi_{2k+1}\rangle=-\alpha_{2k}\overline{\alpha_{2k+1}},

(30f)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k+1},z\chi_{2k+2}\rangle=\rho_{2k+1}\overline{\alpha_{2k+2}},

(30g)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{2k+1},z\chi_{2k+3}\rangle=\rho_{2k+1}\rho_{2k+2},

(30h)

where all other matrix elements are zero. For the unitary evolution of Hermitian operators, the matrix element in (
II.1
) is real
Yeh and Mitra 2024
. By comparison between (
28
) and (

), we have the following relations between the matrix elements in the two representations

\displaystyle(\gamma_{j+1}|K_{I}|\gamma_{k+1})=\langle\chi_{j},z\chi_{k}\rangle,

(31)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=(-1)^{k}\cos\theta_{k+1},\ \rho_{k}=\sin\theta_{k+1}.

(32)

Thus the Verblunsky coefficients
\alpha_{k}
(these can be taken to be real) and the Krylov angles
\theta_{k+1}
are directly related.
To summarize, if the matrix elements of the unitary generating operator time-evolution are expressed in the basis of the OPUC (as in (

)), one recovers the upper-Hessenberg form (
II.1
). In contrast, if the matrix elements of the unitary are expressed in the CMV basis (

), one recovers the representation in the Majorana basis of the ITFIM, (

).

In this work, we will focus on
\alpha_{k}
being real as we are interested in the Floquet unitary dynamics of Hermitian operators. If one considers the Floquet unitary dynamics of wave functions, their corresponding Krylov space is also related to the OPUC in the CMV basis, however the Verblunsky coefficients can be complex, see discussion in
Kolganov and Trunin 2025
.

III
OPUC as an approximate spectral function

III.1
Bernstein–Szegő approximation

We have presented the relation between operators in Krylov space and OPUC. In addition, the OPUC also contain information about the spectral function, and can be used to approximate it. The Bernstein–Szegő approximation for the spectral function is
Simon 2005

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(e^{i\omega})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{|P_{k}(e^{i\omega})|^{2}}.

(33)

The exact spectral function is recovered by taking the following limit

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{i\omega})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|P_{k}(e^{i\omega})|^{2}}.

(34)

The approximate
\Phi_{k}(e^{i\omega})
shares the same Verblunsky coefficient
\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k-1}\}
as
\Phi(e^{i\omega})
but
\alpha_{j\geq k}=0
. For real
\alpha_{k}
, this implies that the Krylov angles
\theta_{j\geq k+1}=\pi/2
, namely the bulk of the ITFIM becomes a dual unitary model and is maximally ergodic
Suchsland et al. 2025
. Before proving the above properties of the Bernstein-Szegő approximation, we have to first prove a key property that the OPUC has zeros only for
|z|<1
. Suppose that
P_{k}(z)
has a zero at
z_{0}
, we can express
P_{k}(z)
as

\displaystyle P_{k}(z)=(z-z_{0})\tilde{P}_{k}(z),

(35)

where
\tilde{P}_{k}(z)
is a polynomial with degree
k-1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle z\tilde{P}_{k}(z),z\tilde{P}_{k}(z)\rangle=\langle P_{k}(z)+z_{0}\tilde{P}_{k}(z),P_{k}(z)+z_{0}\tilde{P}_{k}(z)\rangle.

(36)

On the left hand side, we have
\langle z\tilde{P}_{k}(z),z\tilde{P}_{k}(z)\rangle=\langle\tilde{P}_{k}(z),\tilde{P}_{k}(z)\rangle
due to the definition of the inner product (
9
). Note that the inner product is defined on the unit circle and therefore one may apply
\overline{z}=1/z
in (
9
). On the right hand side, there will be no cross terms like
\langle P_{k}(z),\tilde{P}_{k}(z)\rangle=0
since
P_{k}(z)
is orthogonal to polynomials with degree lower than
k
. Therefore, we arrive at the following relation

\displaystyle\langle P_{k}(z),P_{k}(z)\rangle=(1-|z_{0}|^{2})\langle\tilde{P}_{k}(z),\tilde{P}_{k}(z)\rangle>0

(37)

where the norm of the polynomials are non-negative. In addition, since
\langle P_{k}(z),P_{k}(z)\rangle=1
, it follows that
|z_{0}|<1
. From this, we can conclude that
P_{k}(z)
has zeros only for
|z|<1
. In addition,
P_{k}^{*}(z)
has zeros for
|z|>1
according to the definition
(
16
)
.

Below, we show that
P_{k}(z)
is the OPUC corresponding to the approximate spectral function
\Phi_{k}(e^{i\omega})
. First, we show that
P_{k}(z)
is normalized

\displaystyle\langle P_{k}(z),P_{k}(z)\rangle_{\Phi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\Phi_{k}(z)|P_{k}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}=1,

(38)

where the subscript
\Phi_{k}
indicates replacing
\Phi(z)
by
\Phi_{k}(z)
in (
9
). Note that one can apply
e^{i\omega}=z
in the inner product computation.
Second, we show that
P_{k}(z)
is orthogonal to
z^{l}
for
0\leq l<k
.

\displaystyle\langle z^{l},P_{k}(z)\rangle_{\Phi_{k}}=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\frac{\overline{z^{l}}P_{k}(z)}{2\pi|P_{k}(z)|^{2}}.

(39)

Then we use
\overline{z}=1/z
and apply the *-reverse relation (
16
) inside the inner product to obtain

\displaystyle\langle z^{l},P_{k}(z)\rangle_{\Phi_{k}}=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\frac{z^{k-l-1}}{P_{k}^{*}(z)}=0,\ \forall 0\leq l\leq k-1

(40)

where we have applied the residue theorem and the fact that there are no poles for
|z|<1
for
0\leq l\leq k-1
. Therefore,
P_{k}(z)
is the OPUC with degree
k
corresponding to the spectral function
\Phi_{k}(\omega)
. Note that
\{P_{0}(z),P_{1}(z),P_{2}(z),\ldots,P_{k-1}(z)\}
are uniquely determined once
P_{k}(z)
is fixed. From the Szegő recurrence relations, the OPUC are constructed iteratively with a fixed set of Verblunsky coefficients and this correspondence is unique. Therefore, fixing
P_{k}(z)
uniquely determines
\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k-1}\}
and all the previous OPUC
\{P_{0}(z),P_{1}(z),P_{2}(z),\ldots,P_{k-1}(z)\}
, see detailed discussion in Appendix
E
.

Finally, we will show that
z^{l}P_{k}(z)
is the
l+k
order OPUC with the spectral function
\Phi_{k}(e^{i\omega})
for
l\geq 0
. Let us first check the normalization condition

\displaystyle\langle z^{l}P_{k}(z),z^{l}P_{k}(z)\rangle_{\Phi_{k}}=\langle P_{k}(z),P_{k}(z)\rangle_{\Phi_{k}}=1,

(41)

where
\overline{z}=1/z
is applied. Next, we consider the orthogonal condition

\displaystyle\langle z^{j},z^{l}P_{k}(z)\rangle_{\Phi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\frac{z^{l+k-j-1}}{P_{k}^{*}(z)}

\displaystyle=0,\ \forall\ 0\leq j\leq l+k-1

(42)

Therefore,
z^{l}P_{k}(z)
is orthogonal to any polynomial with degree lower than
l+k
and it is an
l+k
order OPUC with
\Phi_{k}(e^{i\omega})
.

In summary, we have shown that the Bernstein-Szegő approximation for the spectral function
\Phi_{k}(e^{i\omega})
corresponds to the following set of OPUC,
\{P_{0}(z),P_{1}(z),\ldots,P_{k}(z),zP_{k}(z),z^{2}P_{k}(z),\ldots\}
. Applying (
14a
), one concludes
\alpha_{j\geq k}=0
. The true spectral function can be recovered by taking the large
k
limit. Truncating at suitably large
k
is a good practical approximation as many non-integrable systems show a tendency towards vanishing Verblunsky coefficients in numerical studies
Suchsland et al. 2025
;
Yeh and Mitra 2024
. At the dual unitary point
\theta_{k}=\pi/2
, the OPUC take the particularly simple form
P_{k}(z)=z^{k}
according to (
14
). In addition, the corresponding spectral function is uniform
Suchsland et al. 2025
.

III.2
Laplace transformation and its continued fraction expansion

Another interesting property of the spectral function is its relation to the Laplace transformation. For a given autocorrelation, we can define its discrete Laplace transformation
G_{L}(z)
as

\displaystyle G_{L}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)z^{-n},

(43)

where
z
is complex number with
|z|>1
ensuring a convergent expression for
G_{L}(z)
. According to (
6
), the spectral function
\Phi(e^{i\omega})
is related to
G_{L}(z)
as follow

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{i\omega})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(-1+2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\text{Re}[G_{L}(e^{\epsilon+i\omega})]\right),

(44)

where the
-1
in the round bracket accounts for the double counting of
A(0)
where
A(0)=1
. For ITFIM,
G_{L}(z)
has an equivalent representation in terms of continued fractions parametrized by Krylov angles
Yeh and Mitra 2025
. Below we omit the
z
-dependence for compactness.

\displaystyle G_{L}(z,\theta)=\underset{M\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}G_{C}(z,\theta;M),\forall\ |z|>1;

(45a)

\displaystyle G_{C}(z,\theta;M)=\frac{a_{0}}{b_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{\ddots+\frac{a_{M}}{b_{M}}}}=\frac{f_{M}}{h_{M}},

(45b)

where

\displaystyle a_{0}=z;\quad\quad b_{0}=z-\cos\theta_{1};

(46a)

\displaystyle(a_{k},b_{k})=\Big\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(\sin^{2}\theta_{k},\ z\cos\theta_{k+1}-\cos\theta_{k})&\text{if}\ k\ \text{is odd};\\
(z^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{k},\ z\cos\theta_{k}-\cos\theta_{k+1})&\text{else}.\end{array}

f_{M}
and
h_{M}
are polynomials obeying the following recurrence relations,

\displaystyle f_{k}=b_{k}f_{k-1}+a_{k}f_{k-2};\ h_{k}=b_{k}h_{k-1}+a_{k}h_{k-2},

(47)

with initial condition
f_{-2}=1,f_{-1}=0,h_{-2}=0
and
h_{-1}=1
.

From (
44
) and (
45
), one may expect that the truncated continued fraction is related to the OPUC via the Bernstein-Szegő approximation (
33
). In fact, they obey the following relations (see details in Appendix
F
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|P_{2k}(e^{i\omega})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-1+2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\text{Re}[G_{C}(e^{\epsilon+i\omega},\theta;2k)]\Big|_{\theta_{2k+1}=\frac{\pi}{2}};

(48a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|P_{2k+1}(e^{i\omega})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-1+2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\text{Re}[G_{C}(e^{\epsilon+i\omega},\theta;2k)].

(48b)

Note that
P_{2k}
depends on
\{\theta_{1},\ldots\theta_{2k}\}
and
G_{C}(z,\theta;2k)
depends on
\{\theta_{1},\ldots\theta_{2k+1}\}
according to (
46

\theta_{2k+1}=\pi/2
for

to match the number of degrees of freedom. (

14

47
). Appendix
F
shows how the Szegő recurrence relations are implied by the continued fraction recurrence relations (
47
).

VI
Conclusions

The connection between Krylov complexity and OPUC is a promising route to construct exactly solvable toy models that mimic a range of behavior such as integrable dynamics, chaotic dynamics, and slow decay of quasi-conserved quantities. In this paper, we generalize this mapping to stroboscopic operator dynamics. Such dynamics can be generated by a unitary or by a Hamiltonian where for the latter the dynamics is studied at stroboscopic times.

Remarkably, operator spreading can be mapped to an iconic single particle problem, the ITFIM
Yeh and Mitra 2024
;
Yeh and Mitra 2025
. We connect this result to parallel results obtained using OPUC, where the basis analogous to the ITFIM is known as the CMV basis
Cantero et al. 2003
;
Cantero et al. 2005
;
Kolganov and Trunin 2025
. We also establish connections between the Szegő approximation for the autocorrelation function and the continued fraction expression for the Laplace transformed autocorrelation function.

We present several applications. We construct exact OPUC for some simple
m
-periodic dynamics. The last appendix constructs the OPUC for the the Floquet transverse field Ising model in its various topological phases as well as at the dual-unitary point.

We also numerically construct the OPUC for the
Z_{3}
clock model. In order to understand the results better, we use the mapping to the ITFIM to construct an exactly solvable model that have the same coarse-grained Krylov parameters as the numerically studied model.

A promising avenue of research is to further develop exactly solvable models using OPUC that have desirable Krylov subspace features inspired by complex quantum systems.

Appendix B
Derivation of the Szegő recurrence relations

In this part, we mainly follow the derivation presented in Ref.
Simon 2005
. First, let us figure out the orthogonal condition properties of
P_{k}(z)
and
P_{k}^{*}(z)
. According to the Gram-Schmidt construction,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}_{k}|O(m))=0,\ \forall\ 0\leq m\leq k-1.

(87)

By (
13
), one concludes

\displaystyle\langle P_{k}(z),z^{m}\rangle=0,\ \forall\ 0\leq m\leq k-1.

(88)

For
P_{k}^{*}(z)
, we first consider
\langle z^{m},P_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle
and apply the definition of the inner product (
9
) and *-reverse (
16
),

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},P_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\Phi(z)\overline{z^{m}}z^{k}\overline{P_{k}(1/\overline{z})}.

(89)

Note that
\overline{z}=z^{-1}
inside the inner product since
|z|=1
. The above expression becomes

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},P_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\Phi(z)\overline{P_{k}(z)}z^{k-m}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{k}(z),z^{k-m}\rangle.

(90)

Therefore, according to (
88
),

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},P_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle=0,\ \forall\ 1\leq m\leq k.

(91)

As the
zP_{k}(z)
term appears in the Szegő recurrence relations (
14
), let us consider its orthogonal properties as well

\displaystyle\langle zP_{k}(z),z^{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\Phi(z)\overline{zP_{k}(z)}z^{m}

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{iz}\Phi(z)\overline{P_{k}(z)}z^{m-1}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{k}(z),z^{m-1}\rangle.

(92)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle zP_{k}(z),z^{m}\rangle=0,\ \forall\ 1\leq m\leq k.

(93)

To derive key relations for OPUC, it is useful to introduce monic polynomials

\displaystyle p_{k}(z)=\frac{P_{k}(z)}{\kappa_{kk}}=z^{k}+\frac{\kappa_{k,k-1}}{\kappa_{kk}}z^{k-1}+\ldots+\frac{\kappa_{k,0}}{\kappa_{kk}};

(94)

\displaystyle p_{k}^{*}(z)=z^{k}\overline{p_{k}(1/\overline{z})},

(95)

where
p_{k}(z)
is defined such that the prefactor of the highest power term is 1. Note that (
88
), (
91
) and (
93
) are also valid for their monic counterparts. Below, we first derive the results for the monic polynomials and the results for the OPUC are presented at the end.

Note that both
zp_{k}(z)
and
p_{k}^{*}(z)
satisfy the same orthogonal property (
91
) and (
93
). However, the degree of
zp_{k}(z)
is one order higher than
p_{k}^{*}(z)
. By considering
zp_{k}(z)-p_{k+1}(z)
, this new polynomial has the same degree as
p_{k}^{*}(z)
and has the same orthogonal properties. Therefore,
zp_{k}(z)-p_{k+1}(z)
and
p_{k}^{*}(z)
must only differ by an overall prefactor
\overline{\alpha_{k}}

\displaystyle zp_{k}(z)-p_{k+1}(z)=\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z),

(96)

where
\alpha_{k}
is called the Verblunsky coefficient. This is the monic version of (
14a
). To obtain the monic version of (
14b
), we consider the *-reverse of the above relation. We first take
z\rightarrow 1/\overline{z}
, then apply complex conjugation, and finally multiply by
z^{k+1}
on both sides. Namely, we have

\displaystyle z^{k+1}\left[\overline{(1/\overline{z})p_{k}(1/\overline{z})}-\overline{p_{k+1}(1/\overline{z})}\right]=z^{k+1}\overline{\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(1/\overline{z})}.

(97)

Using (
95

\displaystyle p_{k}^{*}(z)-p_{k+1}^{*}(z)=\alpha_{k}zp_{k}(z).

(98)

In summary, we derived the monic Szegő recurrence relations

\displaystyle p_{k+1}(z)=zp_{k}(z)-\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z),

(99a)

\displaystyle p_{k+1}^{*}(z)=p_{k}^{*}(z)-\alpha_{k}zp_{k}(z).

(99b)

One can derive the inverse relations by canceling the
p_{k}(z)
or
p_{k}^{*}(z)
term in (

one obtains,

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}p_{k+1}(z)+p_{k+1}^{*}(z)=\left(1-|\alpha_{k}|^{2}\right)p_{k}^{*}(z).

(100)

By canceling
p_{k}^{*}(z)
on obtains,

\displaystyle p_{k+1}(z)+\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k+1}^{*}(z)=\left(1-|\alpha_{k}|^{2}\right)zp_{k}(z).

(101)

In summary, the inverse monic Szegő recurrence relations are

\displaystyle\rho_{k}^{2}zp_{k}(z)=p_{k+1}(z)+\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k+1}^{*}(z);

(102a)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}^{2}p_{k}^{*}(z)=\alpha_{k}p_{k+1}(z)+p_{k+1}^{*}(z),

(102b)

\displaystyle\langle zp_{k}(z),zp_{k}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle p_{k+1}(z)+\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z),p_{k+1}(z)+\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle p_{k+1}(z),p_{k+1}(z)\rangle+\langle\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z),\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle,

\displaystyle=\langle zp_{k}(z),zp_{k}(z)\rangle-\langle\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z),\overline{\alpha_{k}}p_{k}^{*}(z)\rangle

(104)

\displaystyle\langle p_{k+1}(z),p_{k+1}(z)\rangle=\prod_{j=0}^{k}\rho_{j}^{2},

\displaystyle\langle p_{k}(z),p_{k}(z)\rangle=\left\langle\frac{P_{k}(z)}{\kappa_{kk}},\frac{P_{k}(z)}{\kappa_{kk}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\kappa_{kk}^{2}},

(109a)

Appendix D
CMV basis

Here, we will show that the CMV basis (

II.2
. First, we check whether this basis is orthonormal. Note that the CMV basis is written as a Laurent series, i.e., it contains negative powers of
z
in contrast to the OPUC. In order to apply the orthogonal property of the OPUC, one has to apply

in the inner product so that the expression can be rearranged as inner product between polynomials. For

where in the last line we use the fact that

Appendix H
Floquet Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem

We start with the definition of the Floquet
Dirac delta function in frequency space,

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta_{F}(\omega)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\omega n}=2\pi\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta_{D}(\omega+2\pi m),

(191)

where
\delta_{D}(\omega)
is the usual Dirac delta function. The Kronecker delta function in discrete time is related to the difference of the step functions

\displaystyle\delta_{0n}=s(n)-s(n-1),\ s(n)=\Big\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1/2&\text{if }n\geq 0;\\
-1/2&\text{else}.\end{array}

The discrete time Fourier transformation of the above relation is

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta_{0n}e^{-i\omega n}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)e^{-i\omega n}\right)-\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n-1)e^{-i\omega n}\right),

(194)

and can be further simplified to

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)e^{-i\omega n}\right)

\displaystyle-e^{-i\omega}\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n-1)e^{-i\omega(n-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-e^{-i\omega})\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)e^{-i\omega n}\right).

(195)

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(n)e^{-i\omega n}=\frac{1}{1-e^{-i\omega}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\cot\frac{\omega}{2}.

(196)

Therefore, we have
the following relation that will be useful for computing the Laplace transformation

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(\epsilon+i\omega)n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(s(n)+\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-i\omega n}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\cot\frac{\omega}{2}+\pi\delta_{F}(\omega).

(197)

This relation is called the Floquet Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(\epsilon+i\omega)n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{1-e^{-\epsilon-i\omega}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\cot\frac{\omega}{2}+\pi\delta_{F}(\omega).

(198)

Below we will apply the this formula to derive the spectral functions for the persistent
m
-period autocorrelations reported in Sec.
IV
. For
m=1
, we first rewrite (
50
) such that its expression is ready for Floquet Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem

\displaystyle G_{L}^{(1)}(z)=\frac{z-1+A}{z-1}=(1-A)+\frac{A}{1-z^{-1}}.

(199)

According to (
44
), the spectral function for
m=1
is

\displaystyle\Phi^{(1)}(e^{i\omega})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(-1+2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\text{Re}[G^{(1)}_{L}(e^{\epsilon+i\omega})]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-A}{2\pi}+A\delta_{F}(\omega).

(200)

This derivation proves (
53
). For generic
m
,
G^{(m)}_{L}(z)
is related to
G^{(1)}_{L}(z)
as follows

\displaystyle G^{(m)}_{L}(z)=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{m}\right)z^{-n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1+A\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ze^{-i2\pi/m})^{-n}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+A\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ze^{i2\pi/m})^{-n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(G^{(1)}_{L}(ze^{-i2\pi/m})+G^{(1)}_{L}(ze^{i2\pi/m})\right),

(201)

where (
49
) is applied in the first line. Therefore, the spectral function
\Phi^{(m)}(e^{i\omega})
can be related to
\Phi^{(1)}(e^{i\omega})
as follows

\displaystyle\Phi^{(m)}(e^{i\omega})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(-1+2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\text{Re}[G^{(m)}_{L}(e^{\epsilon+i\omega})]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(-1+\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\text{Re}\left[G^{(1)}_{L}(e^{\epsilon+i(\omega-2\pi/m)})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+G^{(1)}_{L}(e^{\epsilon+i(\omega+2\pi/m)})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\Phi^{(1)}(e^{i(\omega+2\pi/m)})+\Phi^{(1)}(e^{i(\omega-2\pi/m)})\right].

(202)

This result validates the analytic expressions for the spectral function for
m=2,3,4,6
in Sec.
IV

Appendix I
Edge mode eigen-operator of the Majorana chain beyond 2 sublattices

An eigen-operator
\Psi_{\lambda}
of the ITFIM satisfies the eigen-operator equation

\displaystyle K_{I}|\Psi_{\lambda})=e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}|\Psi_{\lambda}),

(203)

where
K_{I}
has a simple form in the Majorana basis according to (
28
). Explicitly, the above equation can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\psi_{1}=

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{1}\times\psi_{1}+\sin\theta_{1}\times\psi_{2},

(204a)

\displaystyle e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\psi_{2j}=

\displaystyle-\sin\theta_{2j-1}\cos\theta_{2j}\times\psi_{2j-1}+\cos\theta_{2j-1}\cos\theta_{2j}\times\psi_{2j}

\displaystyle+\sin\theta_{2j}\cos\theta_{2j+1}\times\psi_{2j+1}+\sin\theta_{2j}\sin\theta_{2j+1}\times\psi_{2j+2},\ \forall\ j\geq 1,

(204b)

\displaystyle e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\psi_{2j+1}=

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{2j-1}\sin\theta_{2j}\times\psi_{2j-1}-\cos\theta_{2j-1}\sin\theta_{2j}\times\psi_{2j}

\displaystyle+\cos\theta_{2j}\cos\theta_{2j+1}\times\psi_{2j+1}+\cos\theta_{2j}\sin\theta_{2j+1}\times\psi_{2j+2},\ \forall\ j\geq 1

(204c)

where we introduce
|\Psi_{\lambda})=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots)^{\intercal}
in the Majorana basis. By reformulating (
204a
) and considering the following linear combinations between (
204b
) and (
204c
): addition of (
204b
) multiplied by
(-\cos\theta_{2j})
and (
204c
) multiplied by
\sin\theta_{2j}

204b

\sin\theta_{2j}

\displaystyle e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\sin\theta_{2j}\times\psi_{2j+1}=\sin\theta_{2j-1}\times\psi_{2j-1}+\left(e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\cos\theta_{2j}-\cos\theta_{2j-1}\right)\psi_{2j},\ \forall\ j\geq 1,

(205b)

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{2j+1}\times\psi_{2j+2}=e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\sin\theta_{2j}\times\psi_{2j}+\left(e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}\cos\theta_{2j}-\cos\theta_{2j+1}\right)\psi_{2j+1},\ \forall\ j\geq 1.

(205c)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}=\frac{e^{i\omega_{\lambda}}-\cos\theta_{1}}{\sin\theta_{1}}\psi_{1},

(206a)

\displaystyle\psi_{j}=\frac{e^{(-1)^{j}i\omega_{\lambda}}\sin\theta_{j-2}}{\sin\theta_{j-1}}\psi_{j-2}+(-1)^{j}\frac{e^{(-1)^{j}i\omega_{\lambda}}\cos\theta_{j-2}-\cos\theta_{j-1}}{\sin\theta_{j-1}}\psi_{j-1},\ \forall\ j\geq 3.

\{\psi_{j}\}
and Krylov angles
\{\theta_{j}\}
. Here, we focus on exponentially localized edge mode solutions with periodic Krylov angles

(209c)

\displaystyle c_{2}\xi=\frac{e^{(-1)^{l+2}i\omega_{\lambda}}\sin\theta_{l}}{\sin\theta_{1}}c_{l}+(-1)^{l+2}\frac{e^{(-1)^{l+2}i\omega_{\lambda}}\cos\theta_{l}-\cos\theta_{1}}{\sin\theta_{1}}c_{1}\xi.

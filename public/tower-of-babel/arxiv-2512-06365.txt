Bound state in the continuum and multiple atom state transfer applications in a waveguide QED setup
Xiang Guo, Xiaojun Zhang, Mingzhu Weng, Qian Bin, Hao-di Liu, Hai-Jun Xing, Xin-You Lü, Zhihai Wang

Abstract
Bound states in the continuum (BICs) have been extensively exploited to enhance light–matter interactions in metamaterials, yet their emergence and utility in multi-atom waveguide platforms remain far less explored. Here we study atom–waveguide-dressed BICs in a one-dimensional coupled-resonator waveguide, where two spatially separated atomic arrays couple to distinct resonators with time-dependent strengths. We show that these BICs support a standing-wave photonic mode and enable the transfer of an arbitrary unknown quantum state between the two arrays with fidelities exceeding 99 % 99\% . The protocol remains robust against both disorder and intrinsic dissipation. Our results establish BICs as long-lived resources for high-fidelity quantum information processing in waveguide-QED architectures.

Robustness
-In the above demonstrations, we have restricted ourselves to the ideal case where disorder and dissipation are neglected.
We show below that the NAST fidelity remains high even under nonideal conditions.

First, we examine the impact of disorder in the on-site resonator frequencies, inter-resonator hopping strengths, and atom–waveguide couplings.
We model each type of disorder by independent Gaussian random variations with zero mean and standard deviation
\delta

The NAST fidelity, averaged over 500 disorder realizations, is shown in Fig.

For all disorder types considered, the fidelity remains above

, which lies within currently accessible experimental ranges.
Such robust performance is consistent with the disorder resilience of BICs in waveguide-QED setups

Second, we take into account photonic dissipation in the waveguide.
As shown in Fig.

The underlying mechanism is captured by Fig.

In our NAST protocol, the dynamics predominantly involve only the BICs and their nearest scattering states, which possess the longest lifetimes.
Consequently, high-fidelity transfer can be maintained even in the presence of photonic decay.

Conclusion
-We have demonstrated BICs in a waveguide-QED system where two spatially separated atomic arrays interact with a CRW via time-dependent couplings. When resonator losses are included, these BICs and their nearest scattering states exhibit the smallest imaginary parts of the complex eigenfrequencies, indicating markedly long lifetimes. The BICs are atom–photon dressed states: the photonic component is confined between the two coupling sites in a standing-wave pattern, while the atomic component can encode entanglement. Exploiting these states, we realize the transfer of arbitrary superposition states between the two atomic arrays in both adiabatic and nonadiabatic regimes. The protocol is resilient to multiple forms of disorder, and the fidelity can exceed
98\%
even in the presence of atomic dissipation and resonator decay.

Most previous state-transfer schemes rely critically on chiral waveguides or nonreciprocal elements. Our BIC-based approach removes this requirement and provides a robust route to high-fidelity state transfer in
bidirectional
waveguide-QED architectures. We anticipate that our results will stimulate further studies of BIC-enabled quantum state engineering and quantum information processing in realistic waveguide platforms with unavoidable imperfections.

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}\Big[\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\hat{\sigma}_{-}^{(i)}e^{ikN_{1}}+\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\hat{\sigma}_{-}^{(N_{a}+i)}e^{ikN_{2}}\right)+\text{H.C.}\Big],

(S1)

where
\omega_{k}=\omega_{c}-2J\cos k
is the dispersion relation of the continuous band supported by the CRW.
In what follows, we set
\omega_{c}=0
for simplicity.
For a finite CRW with
N_{c}
resonators, the allowed wave vectors are

k\in\{k_{j}=-\pi+(2\pi/N_{c})j\,|\,j=0,1,\dots,N_{c}-1\}
.

Regarding the atomic-frequency configuration, we assume that the
p
th and
(N_{a}+p)
th atoms share the same

\Omega_{p}
, which lies inside the continuous band of the CRW, i.e.,

|\Omega_{p}|<2J
.
The corresponding resonant photon wave vectors are
\pm K_{p}
, determined by

-2J\cos(\pm K_{p})=\Omega_{p}
with
K_{p}>0
.
Different atomic pairs are taken to be spectrally distinct, i.e.,
\Omega_{p}\neq\Omega_{q}
for
p\neq q
.

S1.1
Time-independent eigenfrequency

In this subsection, we prove that
\hat{H}(t)
admits a
time-independent
eigenvalue
\Omega_{p}
when the
separation between the two atomic arrays,
\Delta N=N_{2}-N_{1}
, satisfies
\Delta NK_{p}=m_{p}\pi
with

m_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and when
\pm K_{p}
do not belong to the discrete set of photon wave vectors supported by
the finite CRW, i.e.,
\pm K_{p}\neq-\pi+(2\pi/N_{c})j
for all
j=0,1,\dots,N_{c}-1
.
In this case, the dimension of the corresponding eigenspace depends on
N_{c}
and
K_{p}
.

First, we introduce the matrix
\bm{O}(t)
in the single-excitation basis in momentum space,

\displaystyle\bm{O}(t)=\hat{H}(t)-\Omega_{p}\hat{I}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}&\bm{B}(t)\\
\bm{B}^{\dagger}(t)&\bm{C}\end{bmatrix}.

(S2)

Here,

\displaystyle\bm{A}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\omega_{k_{0}}-\Omega_{p},\,\omega_{k_{1}}-\Omega_{p},\,\ldots,\,\omega_{k_{N_{c}-1}}-\Omega_{p}\bigr),

(S3)

\displaystyle\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bigl[\underbrace{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{1}}_{N_{a}},\underbrace{\bm{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{2}}_{N_{a}}\bigr],\quad\bm{\lambda}_{1}(t)=\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\begin{bmatrix}e^{ik_{0}N_{1}}\\[3.0pt]
e^{ik_{1}N_{1}}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
e^{ik_{N_{c}-1}N_{1}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\bm{\lambda}_{2}(t)=\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\begin{bmatrix}e^{ik_{0}N_{2}}\\[3.0pt]
e^{ik_{1}N_{2}}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
e^{ik_{N_{c}-1}N_{2}}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\bm{C}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\delta^{(p)}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{(p)}_{N_{a}},\delta^{(p)}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{(p)}_{N_{a}}\bigr),

where
\delta_{m}^{(p)}=\Omega_{m}-\Omega_{p}
and
\delta_{m}^{(p)}=0
if and only if
m=p
.

Next, we introduce the elementary matrix
\bm{M}_{(i,j)}
, whose diagonal elements are all
1
and whose
off-diagonal elements are zero except for a single
(-1)
located at the intersection of the
i
th row and

j
th column. Performing the sequence of elementary transformations

\bm{M}=[\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}\bm{M}_{(i,p)}][\prod_{i=p+1}^{N_{a}}\bm{M}_{(i,p)}][\prod_{i=N_{a}+1}^{N_{a}+p-1}\bm{M}_{(i,N_{a}+p)}][\prod_{i=N_{a}+p+1}^{2N_{a}}\bm{M}_{(i,N_{a}+p)}]

(S4)

on
\bm{O}(t)
, we obtain a new block matrix
\bm{\tilde{O}}(t)
with the same rank as
\bm{O}(t)
,

\displaystyle\bm{\tilde{O}}(t)=\bm{M}\bm{O}(t)\bm{M}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}&\bm{D}(t)\\
\bm{D}^{\dagger}(t)&\bm{C}\end{bmatrix}.

(S5)

Here, the
p
th column of
\bm{D}(t)
is
\bm{\lambda}_{1}
, the
(N_{a}+p)
th column is
\bm{\lambda}_{2}
,
and all other columns are zero. Moreover,
\bm{A}
is invertible when

\pm K_{p}\neq-\pi+(2\pi/N_{c})j
for all
j=0,1,\dots,N_{c}-1
, i.e., when
\pm K_{p}
do not belong to the
discrete set of photon wave vectors supported by the CRW. In this case, the rank of
\bm{\tilde{O}}(t)
follows
from the Schur-complement rank theorem
schur
as

\displaystyle\text{rank}[\bm{\tilde{O}}(t)]=\text{rank}[\bm{A}]+\text{rank}\left[\bm{C}-\bm{D}^{\dagger}(t)\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{D}(t)\right]=N_{c}+\text{rank}\left[\bm{C}-\bm{D}^{\dagger}(t)\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{D}(t)\right].

(S6)

A direct evaluation of the Schur complement shows that

\text{rank}\left[\bm{C}-\bm{D}^{\dagger}(t)\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{D}(t)\right]=2N_{a}+[\dim(\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle)-2]
, which yields

\displaystyle\text{rank}[\hat{H}(t)-\Omega_{p}\hat{I}]=N_{c}+2N_{a}+\big[\dim(\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle)-2\big],

(S7)

where

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{g(t)^{2}}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\\
\frac{g(t)g^{\prime}(t)}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{-ik\Delta N}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\bm{v}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{g(t)g^{\prime}(t)}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{ik\Delta N}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\\
\frac{g^{\prime}(t)^{2}}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\end{bmatrix},

(S8)

and
\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle
denotes the space spanned by
\bm{v}_{1}
and
\bm{v}_{2}
.

Since the interference condition
K_{p}\Delta N=m_{p}\pi\penalty\ (m_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+})
is satisfied, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\sin K_{p}}\tan(\frac{N_{c}K_{p}}{2}),\ N_{c}\equiv 1\pmod{2},\\
\frac{1}{2\sin K_{p}}\cot(\frac{N_{c}K_{p}}{2}),\ N_{c}\equiv 0\pmod{2},\end{cases}

(S9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{\pm ik\Delta N}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{(-1)^{m_{p}}}{2\sin K_{p}}\tan(\frac{N_{c}K_{p}}{2}),\ N_{c}\equiv 1\pmod{2},\\
\frac{(-1)^{m_{p}}}{2\sin K_{p}}\cot(\frac{N_{c}K_{p}}{2}),\ N_{c}\equiv 0\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

(S10)

This implies

\displaystyle\dim(\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle)=\begin{cases}0,&\begin{aligned} &N_{c}K_{p}=2n\pi,\ N_{c}\equiv 1\pmod{2},\\
&\text{or }N_{c}K_{p}=(2n-1)\pi,\ N_{c}\equiv 0\pmod{2},\end{aligned}\\[4.0pt]
1,&\begin{aligned} &N_{c}K_{p}\neq 2n\pi,\ N_{c}\equiv 1\pmod{2},\\
&\text{or }N_{c}K_{p}\neq(2n-1)\pi,\ N_{c}\equiv 0\pmod{2},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(S11)

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. When
\dim(\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle)=0
, the eigenspace associated with

E(t)=\Omega_{p}
is two-dimensional, yielding two degenerate eigenstates at
\Omega_{p}
. By contrast, when

\dim(\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle)=1
, the eigenspace is one-dimensional, indicating a unique eigenstate
with the time-independent eigenvalue
E(t)=\Omega_{p}
.

S1.2
BIC wave function

In this subsection, we focus on the second scenario in Eq. (
S11
). We show that the nondegenerate
eigenstate with a time-independent eigenfrequency
\Omega_{p}
indeed corresponds to a BIC by explicitly deriving
the analytical form of its wave function. Note that the condition

\dim(\langle\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}\rangle)=1
implies that
\bm{v}_{1}
and
\bm{v}_{2}
are linearly dependent, i.e.,

\displaystyle(-1)^{m_{p}+1}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\,\bm{v}_{1}+\bm{v}_{2}=0,\qquad\bm{v}_{1,2}\neq\bm{0}.

(S12)

We begin by expanding the corresponding instantaneous eigenstate as

\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}=[\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{(p)}(t)\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}(\beta_{i}^{(p)}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(i)}+\beta_{N_{a}+i}^{(p)}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(N_{a}+i)})]\ket{G},

(S13)

where
\ket{G}
denotes the ground state of the combined CRW–atom system. Using the instantaneous eigenequation

\hat{H}(t)\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}=\Omega_{p}\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}^{(p)}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}(\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}e^{ikN_{1}}\beta_{i}^{(p)}(t)+\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}e^{ikN_{2}}\beta_{N_{a}+i}^{(p)}(t)),

(S14)

\displaystyle(\Omega_{p}-\Omega_{i})\beta_{i}^{(p)}(t)=\sum_{k}\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}e^{-ikN_{1}}\alpha_{k}^{(p)}(t),\qquad(i=1,2,\dots,N_{a}),

(S15)

\displaystyle(\Omega_{p}-\Omega_{i})\beta_{N_{a}+i}^{(p)}(t)=\sum_{k}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}e^{-ikN_{2}}\alpha_{k}^{(p)}(t).\qquad(i=1,2,\dots,N_{a})

(S16)

To simplify notation, we omit the explicit time dependence of the variables below. From Eq. (
S14
), we have

\alpha_{k}^{(p)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}\frac{ge^{ikN_{1}}\beta_{i}^{(p)}+g^{\prime}e^{ikN_{2}}\beta_{N_{a}+i}^{(p)}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}.

(S17)

Substituting Eq. (
S17
) into Eqs. (
S15
) and (
S16
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\Omega_{p}-\Omega_{i})\beta_{i}^{(p)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{a}}\Big[\big(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\big)g^{2}\beta_{j}^{(p)}+\big(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{ik\Delta N}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\big)gg^{\prime}\beta_{N_{a}+j}^{(p)}\Big]\equiv C_{1},

(S18)

\displaystyle(\Omega_{p}-\Omega_{i})\beta_{N_{a}+i}^{(p)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{a}}\Big[\big(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{-ik\Delta N}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\big)gg^{\prime}\beta_{j}^{(p)}+\big(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\big)g^{\prime 2}\beta_{N_{a}+j}^{(p)}\Big]\equiv C_{2}.

(S19)

The right-hand sides of the above two equations are independent of
i
. Setting
i=p
, we obtain

C_{1}=C_{2}\equiv 0
. Therefore,
(\Omega_{p}-\Omega_{i})\beta_{i}^{(p)}=(\Omega_{p}-\Omega_{i})\beta_{N_{a}+i}^{(p)}\equiv 0

holds for all
i=1,2,\dots,N_{a}
. For
i\neq p
, we have
\Omega_{i}\neq\Omega_{p}
, which implies

\beta_{i}^{(p)}\equiv 0\ (i\neq p,N_{a}+p).

(S20)

Substituting Eq. (
S20
) into Eq. (
S18

\displaystyle\frac{g(t)^{2}}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\beta_{p}^{(p)}+\frac{g(t)g^{\prime}(t)}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{ik\Delta N}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\beta_{N_{a}+p}^{(p)}=0.

(S21)

Using the linear-dependence condition in Eq. (
S12

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{p}^{(p)}}{\beta_{N_{a}+p}^{(p)}}=(-1)^{m_{p}+1}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\ \Rightarrow\ \beta_{N_{a}+p}^{(p)}=(-1)^{m_{p}+1}\frac{g(t)}{g^{\prime}(t)}\beta_{p}^{(p)}.

(S22)

With this relation,
\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}
can be rewritten in the real-space basis as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}=[\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{(p)}(t)\hat{a}_{n}^{\dagger}+\beta_{p}^{(p)}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(p)}+(-1)^{m_{p}+1}\frac{g}{g^{\prime}}\beta_{p}^{(p)}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(N_{a}+p)}]\ket{G},

(S23)

where
\alpha_{n}^{(p)}
is obtained from the Fourier transform of
\alpha_{k}^{(p)}

\alpha_{n}^{(p)}=(\sqrt{N_{c}})^{-1/2}\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{(p)}e^{-ikn}

Substituting Eqs. (
S17

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}^{(p)}=g\Big[\dfrac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{k}\frac{e^{ik(N_{1}-n)}+(-1)^{m_{p}+1}e^{ik(N_{2}-n)}}{\Omega_{p}-\omega_{k}}\Big]\beta_{p}^{(p)}=\begin{cases}0,&n<N_{1},\\
\dfrac{\sin{[(n-N_{1})K_{p}]}}{\sin{K_{p}}}\dfrac{g}{J}\beta_{p}^{(p)},&N_{1}\leq n\leq N_{2},\\
0,&n>N_{2},\end{cases}

(S24)

and, after normalization,

\displaystyle\beta_{p}^{(p)}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+[g(t)/g^{\prime}(t)]^{2}+\Delta N[g(t)/(\sqrt{2}J\sin{K_{p}})]^{2}}}.

(S25)

These results yield the instantaneous eigenstate at

\displaystyle\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}=\beta_{p}^{(p)}(t)\Big[\sum_{n=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\frac{\sin{[(n-N_{1})K_{p}]}}{{\sin{K_{p}}}}\frac{g(t)}{J}\hat{a}_{n}^{\dagger}+\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(p)}+(-1)^{m_{p}+1}\frac{g(t)}{g^{\prime}(t)}\hat{\sigma}_{+}^{(N_{a}+p)}\Big]\ket{G},

(S26)

which is equivalent to Eqs. (3)–(5) in the main text. The photonic component is confined to the region

N_{1}\leq n\leq N_{2}
[Eq. (
S24
)], demonstrating real-space localization. Since the
eigenvalue
\Omega_{p}
lies within the continuous band, we identify

\ket{\psi^{(p)}(t)}\equiv\ket{{\rm BIC}^{(p)}(t)}

S3
Tunneling dynamics in non-adiabatic state transfer process

To further shorten the transfer time beyond the AST process, we resort to the NAST protocol, which permits
controlled tunneling between the BICs and their nearest scattering states. To quantitatively describe the
tunneling dynamics during state transfer, we express the time-dependent state as

\displaystyle\ket{\phi(t)}=\sum_{m=1}^{N_{a}}C_{m}\left[\tilde{C}_{B}^{(m)}(t)\ket{\text{BIC}^{(m)}(t)}+\tilde{C}_{S}^{(m)}(t)\ket{\text{SC}^{(m)}(t)}\right],

(S27)

with initial conditions
\tilde{C}_{B}^{(m)}(0)=1
and
\tilde{C}_{S}^{(m)}(0)=0
for all

m=1,2,\dots,N_{a}
. From the Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\ket{\phi(t)}=\hat{H}(t)\ket{\phi(t)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle i\sum_{m}C_{m}\left[\dot{\tilde{C}}_{B}^{(m)}(t)\ket{\text{BIC}^{(m)}(t)}+\tilde{C}_{B}^{(m)}(t)\partial_{t}\ket{\text{BIC}^{(m)}(t)}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\dot{\tilde{C}}_{S}^{(m)}(t)\ket{\text{SC}^{(m)}(t)}+\tilde{C}_{S}^{(m)}(t)\partial_{t}\ket{\text{SC}^{(m)}(t)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}C_{m}\left[\tilde{C}_{B}^{(m)}(t)\Omega_{m}\ket{\text{BIC}^{(m)}(t)}+\tilde{C}_{S}^{(m)}(t)E_{S}^{(m)}(t)\ket{\text{SC}^{(m)}(t)}\right],

(S28)

where
E_{S}^{(m)}(t)
denotes the instantaneous eigenvalue of
\ket{\text{SC}^{(m)}(t)}
. Projecting onto

\bra{\text{BIC}^{(p)}(t)}
and
\bra{\text{SC}^{(p)}(t)}
, we arrive at the closed coupled equations

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{C}}_{B}^{(p)}(t)+i\Omega_{p}\tilde{C}_{B}^{(p)}(t)+F_{p}(t)\tilde{C}_{S}^{(p)}(t)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{C}}_{S}^{(p)}(t)+iE_{S}^{(p)}(t)\tilde{C}_{S}^{(p)}(t)-F_{p}(t)\tilde{C}_{B}^{(p)}(t)

\displaystyle=0.

(S29)

characterizes the

To obtain the above equations, we have assumed that

for

.
Physically, this assumption corresponds to an intermediate driving-timescale regime: the modulation is fast
enough to allow tunneling between each BIC and its nearest scattering state, yet not so fast as to activate

The solution of the coupled equations is uniquely determined by the coefficients and initial conditions, while

\Omega_{p}
,
E_{S}^{(p)}(t)

are fixed by the intrinsic properties of
\hat{H}(t)

\{C_{p}\}
that specify the initial atomic state. Consequently, as long as a time

\displaystyle(-1)^{m_{p}+1}\tilde{C}_{B}^{(p)}(T)\approx e^{i\Phi},\qquad\tilde{C}_{S}^{(p)}(T)\approx 0,

(S30)

for all

Moduli of Real (res. Quaternionic) ℒ \mathcal{L} -connections
Ayush Jaiswal

Abstract
. We have studied irreducible real (respectively, quaternionic) Lie algebroid connections and prove that the Gauge theoretic moduli space has Hausdorff Hilbert manifold structure. This work generalises some known results about simple semi-connections for complex vector bundle on compact complex manifold in real algebraic geometry.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 14H60; Secondary: 53C07, 30F50

1.
Introduction

Gauge theoretic moduli spaces are useful in computing differential geometric invariants like Seiberg-Witten invariants, Donaldson invariant and many more. Gauge theoretic analytic tools are useful in the theory of four-dimensional manifold as well as Yang-Mills theory, etc.

In this article, we have studied Lie algebroid connections with real as well as quaternionic structures. The goal of the article is to give description of moduli of real as well as quaternionic Lie algebroid connections for complex vector bundle on compact complex manifold and describe the natural structure of locally Hausdorff Hilbert manifold with Symplectic structure on it.

In
[
5
]
, Atiyah has described real (respectively, quaternionic) vector bundles as a complex vector bundle on a space with involution, say
(X,\sigma_{X})
along with the bundle has a
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphism
\sigma^{E}
over the involution
\sigma_{X}
such that
\sigma^{E}\circ\sigma^{E}\equiv\mathrm{id}_{E}
(respectively,
\sigma^{E}\circ\sigma^{E}\equiv-\mathrm{id}_{E}
).

In section
3
, we have studied the real structure on the space of
\mathcal{L}
-connections and proved that two
\mathcal{L}
-connection lie in same gauge orbit iff they lie in same real gauge orbit.

In section
4
, we have described the moduli of real (respectively, quaternionic) Sobolev irreducible Lie algebroid connections for complex vector bundle on complex manifold with real (respectively, quaternionic) structure and described the natural Hausdorff Hilbert manifold structure on it. Along with, we have proved
p:\widehat{A}(E,\mathcal{L})_{l}^{\sigma}\rightarrow\widehat{B}(E,\mathcal{L})^{\sigma}_{l}
is a principal
\mathrm{Gau}(E)^{\sigma}_{l+1}
-bundle, where
\widehat{A}(E,\mathcal{L})_{l}^{\sigma}
is the space of irreducible real (respectively, quaternionic) Sobolev
\mathcal{L}
-connections and
\widehat{B}(E,\mathcal{L})^{\sigma}_{l}
is gauge equivalence classes. This is generalization of results in
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
.

(2)

Example
.

Some examples of Lie algebroids are following:

(1)

For a given manifold, the tangent bundle with canonical Lie bracket structure on space of global sections, in this example the anchor map is the identity map.

(2)

A Lie algebra is a Lie algebroid over a point manifold with anchor map, a trivial map.

(3)

A subbundle of the tangent bundle of a given manifold, closed under Lie bracket structure, is a Lie algebroid.

(4)

Any bundle with fiber as a Lie algebra with anchor map, a trivial map is a Lie algebroid.

2.1.
Lie algebroid connections

For a given complex Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{L},[.,.],\sharp)
of rank
r
, the vector space

\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathcal{L}^{\star})=\bigoplus_{k}\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})

has a graded algebra structure induced by the exterior product map such that
for
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
and
\psi\in\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{l}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
,
\phi\wedge\psi\in\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k+l}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
is given by

(\phi\wedge\psi)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{l+k})=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{l!}\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{l+k}}\phi\big(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\xi_{\sigma(2)},\dots,\xi_{\sigma(l)}\big).\psi\big(\xi_{\sigma(l+1)},\xi_{\sigma(l+2)},\dots,\xi_{\sigma(l+k)}\big).

For
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
and
\xi_{l}\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})

(0\leq l\leq k)
, define an operator
d_{\mathcal{L}}:\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})\rightarrow\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
such that

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{L}}(\phi)(\xi_{0},\dots,\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{k}(-1)^{n}\sharp(\xi_{n})\phi\big(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots,\widehat{\xi_{n}},\dots,\xi_{k}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\displaystyle\sum_{l<m}(-1)^{l+m}\phi\big([\xi_{l},\xi_{m}],\xi_{0},\dots,\widehat{\xi_{l}},\dots,\widehat{\xi_{m}},\dots,\xi_{n}\big).

The operator
d_{\mathcal{L}}
can be seen as a generalization of the de-Rham operator
d
satisfying the property
d_{\mathcal{L}}\circ d_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv 0
. For a given element
\xi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
the Lie derivative operator
\mathfrak{L}_{\xi}^{\mathcal{L}}:\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})\rightarrow\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
is defined as

\mathfrak{L}_{\xi}^{\mathcal{L}}(\phi)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\dots,\xi_{k})=\sharp(\xi)\phi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{k})-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,[\xi,\xi_{j}],\dots,\xi_{k})

and the degree -1, insertion operator
i^{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}:\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathcal{L}^{\star})\rightarrow\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
is defined as

i^{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}(\phi)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{k})=\phi(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{k}).

Remark 2.2
.

For simplicity, the space of global sections of the bundle
\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
and the graded algebra
\Gamma(X,\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathcal{L}^{\star})
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},[.,.],\sharp)
(respectively,
E
) be a complex Lie algebroid (respectively, complex vector bundel) on a connected complex manifold
X
. The space of global sections of bundle
\Lambda^{k}L^{\star}\otimes E
will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}^{k}(X,E)
, whose elements are called global
\mathcal{L}
-forms of type
k
taking values in the bundle
E
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\nabla:\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)

satisfying the following Leibnitz type identity

\nabla(fs)=d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)s+f\nabla(s)

for
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
and
s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
, is called an
\mathcal{L}
-connection.

Definition 2.4
.

For a given element
\xi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
, we have an
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\nabla_{\xi}:\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\quad\Big(\nabla_{\xi}(fs)=\mathfrak{L}_{\xi}^{\mathcal{L}}(f)s+f\nabla(s)(\xi)\text{ for }f\in C^{\infty}(X),s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\Big)

The element
\nabla_{\xi}(s)\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}^{1}(X,E)
is called co-variant
\mathcal{L}
-derivative of
s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
and the operator
\nabla_{\xi}
is called co-variant
\mathcal{L}
-differential operator, in the direction of
\xi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For a given
\mathcal{L}
-connection
\nabla
, there is a degree 1 differential operator
d^{\nabla}:\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
such that
d^{\nabla}|_{\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)}\equiv\nabla
, and

(1)

d^{\nabla}(\alpha\wedge\phi)=d_{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha)\wedge\phi+(-1)^{l}\alpha\wedge d^{\nabla}(\phi)

(2)

d^{\nabla}|_{\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)}\equiv\nabla
and
(d^{\nabla}(s))(\psi)=\nabla_{\psi}(s)

for
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{l}_{\mathcal{L}}(X),\phi\in\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E),s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
and
\psi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
.

Proof.

The proof follows in the same line of arguements as in the case of affine connections modeled on the vector space
\Gamma(X,T^{\star}X\otimes\mathrm{End}(E))
(see,
[
7
]
).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

The space of
\mathcal{L}
-connections
A(E,\mathcal{L})
, is an affine space modeled on the vector space
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\textrm{End}(E))
.

Proof.

Let
\{U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a trivialization of the bundle
E
along with locally finite open covering
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
of
X
and
\{g_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be the smooth partition of unity subordinate to the open covering. Take
s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
, then
\mathrm{Supp}(g_{\alpha}s)\subset U_{\alpha}
and
\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}})\in\Gamma(U_{\alpha},U_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{C}^{r})
along with
\mathrm{Supp}(\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}}))\subset U_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in I
, therefore
\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}})
can be extended to a global section, say
\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}})\subset\Gamma(X,X\times\mathbb{C}^{r})
.

Let
\nabla_{0}
be the trivial
\mathcal{L}
-connection for the trivial bundle on
X
that is
\nabla_{0}(f\times s)=d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)\times s
for
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
and
s\in\Gamma(X,X\times\mathbb{C}^{r})
, we have
\mathrm{Supp}(\nabla_{0}(\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}}))\subset U_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in U_{\alpha}
, furthermore

\left[\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}|_{U_{\alpha}}}\otimes\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ\nabla_{0}(\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}}))\right]\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(U_{\alpha},E)

Define

\nabla(s)(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}|_{U_{\alpha}}}\otimes\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ\nabla_{0}(\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}s|_{U_{\alpha}}))(x)

which will be finite sum for a given
x\in X
, because of local finiteness of the covering.

The map
\nabla:\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
is an
\mathcal{L}
-connection, because

\displaystyle\nabla(fs)(x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}|_{U_{\alpha}}}\otimes\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ\nabla_{0}(\phi_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}(fs)|_{U_{\alpha}}))(x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}|_{U_{\alpha}}}\otimes\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ\nabla_{0}(f|_{U_{\alpha}}\phi_{\alpha}\circ(g_{\alpha}\circ s)|_{U_{\alpha}})(x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}|_{U_{\alpha}}}\otimes\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ\big[d_{\mathcal{L}}(f|_{U_{\alpha}})(x)\otimes\phi_{\alpha}\circ(g_{\alpha}\circ s)|_{U_{\alpha}}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+f|_{U_{\alpha}}(x)\otimes\nabla_{0}(\phi_{\alpha}\circ(g_{\alpha}\circ s|_{U_{\alpha}}))(x)\big]

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)(x)\otimes\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}\circ s)|_{U_{\alpha}}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+f(x)\otimes\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}|_{U_{\alpha}}}\otimes\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ\nabla_{0}(\phi_{\alpha}\circ(g_{\alpha}\circ s|_{U_{\alpha}}))(x)\big]

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)(x)\otimes s(x)+f(x)\otimes\nabla(s)(x)

Only thing, we need to check is, for two
\mathcal{L}
-connections
\nabla_{i}

(i=1,2)
,
(\nabla_{1}-\nabla_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
, which is obvious because

\displaystyle(\nabla_{1}-\nabla_{2})(fs)

\displaystyle=(d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)\otimes s+f\nabla_{1}(s))-(d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)\otimes s+f\nabla_{2}(s))

\displaystyle=f(\nabla_{1}-\nabla_{2})(s)

That is
\nabla_{1}-\nabla_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
. Hence, we have the following remark.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Let
\nabla_{0}
be the trivial
\mathcal{L}
-connection, any Lie algebroid connection
\nabla\in A(E,\mathcal{L})
can be expressed as
\nabla=\nabla_{0}+\alpha
, where
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
. Therefore, the space of
\mathcal{L}
-connections can be expressed as,

(2.1)

A(E,\mathcal{L})=\{\nabla_{0}+\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))\}

Like an affine connection, an
\mathcal{L}
-connection
\nabla^{E}
for a bundle
E
induces an
\mathcal{L}
-connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{End}(E)}
for the bundle
\mathrm{End}(E)
such that

(2.2)

\nabla^{E}(Ts)=(\nabla^{\mathrm{End}(E)}T)(S)+T(\nabla^{E}(S))

for
T\in\Gamma(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
and
s\in\Gamma(X,E)
.

The graded
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
has a canonical structure of associate algebra such that for
\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
and
\tau\in\mathcal{A}^{q}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
,
\omega\wedge\tau
is given by

\omega\wedge\tau(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{p+q})=\frac{1}{p!q!}\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S(p+q)}Sign(\sigma)\omega(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\xi_{\sigma(p)})\tau(\xi_{\sigma(p+1},\dots,\xi_{\sigma(p+q)})

as well as a Lie algebra structure with the Lie bracket

can be described as,

(2.3)

[\omega,\tau](\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{p+q})=\frac{1}{p!q!}\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S(p+q)}Sign(\sigma)[\omega(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\xi_{\sigma(p)}),\tau(\xi_{\sigma(p+1)},\dots,\xi_{\sigma_{(p+q)}})]

for
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{p+q}\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
.

2.2.
Gauge orbits of Lie algebroid connections

Consider a complex vector bundle
(E,\pi,X)
on a complex manifold
X
, a vector bundle endomorphism is a pair
(\phi,\phi^{\prime})
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\prime}}

commutes. The endomorphism
(\phi,\phi^{\prime})
is also called a vector bundle morphism
\phi:E\rightarrow E
over the map
\phi^{\prime}:X\rightarrow X
. Composition of vector bundle endomorphism
(\phi,\phi^{\prime})
and
(\psi,\psi^{\prime})
is the endomorphism
(\phi\circ\psi,\phi^{\prime}\circ\psi^{\prime})
, an invertible vector bundle endomorphism is called vector bundle automorphism.

Definition 2.8
.

The collection of all vector bundle automorphism over the map
\textrm{id}_{X}:X\rightarrow X
has the group structure with operation as composition, is called the Gauge group. The elements of the gauge group are called gauge transformations.

The space of Gauge transformations for a vector bundle
E
will be denoted by
\mathrm{Gau}(E)
and a gauge transformation
(\phi,\mathrm{id}|_{X})
will be expressed by
\phi
only.

For a given
\mathcal{L}
-connection
\nabla\in A(E,\mathcal{L})
and a gauge transformation
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)
, Define the map
\nabla^{\phi}:\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
given by

\nabla^{\phi}(s)=(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ\nabla(\phi(s))\quad(\text{for }s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E))

Lemma 2.9
.

The map
\nabla^{\phi}:\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
is a smooth Lie algebroid connection.

Proof.

For
f\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
and
s\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,E)
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{\phi}(fs)=(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ\nabla(\phi(fs))

\displaystyle=(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\Big(d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)\otimes\phi(s)+f\nabla\big(\phi(s)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)\otimes s+f(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ\nabla\circ\phi(s)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{L}}(f)\otimes s+f\nabla^{\phi}(s)

This implies
\nabla^{\phi}\in A(E,\mathcal{L})
, for given
\nabla\in A(E,\mathcal{L})
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)
.
∎

Hence, there is a canonical left
\mathrm{Gau}(E)
action on the space
A(E,\mathcal{L})
, of
\mathcal{L}
-connections, given as

(2.4)

\odot:\mathrm{Gau}(E)\times A(E,\mathcal{L})\quad\big(\phi,\nabla)\mapsto\phi\odot\nabla\equiv\nabla^{\phi}\big).

Also, for a given element
\xi\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L})
,

(2.5)

(\phi,\nabla_{\xi})\mapsto\phi\odot\nabla_{\xi}\equiv\nabla_{\xi}^{\phi}=\phi^{-1}\circ\nabla_{\xi}\circ\phi

Since
A(E,\mathcal{L})
is
\mathrm{Gau}(E)
-invariant, we can talk about the quotient space
{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$A(E,\mathcal{L})$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\mathrm{Gau}(E)$}\right.}
.

Definition 2.10
.

For a given
\mathcal{L}
-connection
\nabla
, the subgroup

\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)\mid\phi\odot\nabla\equiv\nabla\}

is called the isotropic subgroup or stabiliser for
\nabla
. Note that the sugroup
\mathbb{C}^{\star}.\mathrm{id}_{E}\subset\mathrm{Gau}(E)
is contained in
\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}
for each
\nabla\in A(E,\mathcal{L})
.

Definition 2.11
.

An
\mathcal{L}
-connection
\nabla
is called irreducible if
\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}\equiv\mathbb{C}^{\star}.\mathrm{id}_{E}
and reducible if it is not an irreducible
\mathcal{L}
-connection.

The space of irreducible
\mathcal{L}
-connections will be denoted by
\widehat{A}(E,\mathcal{L})
.

Remark 2.12
.

For
\nabla\in A(E,\mathcal{L})
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)
, let
\psi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla^{\phi}}
then,

\displaystyle(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\psi^{-1})\circ\nabla^{\phi}(\psi(s))

\displaystyle\equiv\nabla^{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\psi^{-1})\circ(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ\nabla(\phi\circ\psi(s))

\displaystyle\equiv(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ\nabla(\phi(s))

\displaystyle(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi)\circ(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\psi^{-1})\circ(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ\nabla(\phi\circ\psi\circ\phi^{-1}(s))

\displaystyle\equiv\nabla(s)

\displaystyle\text{or, }\big(id_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\otimes(\phi\circ\psi\circ\phi^{-1})^{-1}\big)\circ\nabla(\phi\circ\psi\circ\phi^{-1}(s))

\displaystyle\equiv\nabla(s)

We have
\phi\circ\psi\circ\phi^{-1}\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}
or,
\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla^{\phi}}\subset\phi^{-1}.\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}.\phi
. Following the steps in reverse direction, we get the reverse inclusion i.e.
\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla^{\phi}}\supset\phi^{-1}.\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}.\phi
. Hence,

\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla^{\phi}}=\{\phi\circ x\circ\phi^{-1}\mid x\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}\}

From above remark, if
\nabla
is an irreducible
\mathcal{L}
-connection, then for any
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)
we have
\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla^{\phi}}=\mathbb{C}^{\star}.\mathrm{id}_{E}
, means the space of irreducible smooth Lie algebroid connections,
\widehat{A}(E,\mathcal{L})
is a
\mathrm{Gau}(E)
-invariant subset of
A(E,\mathcal{L})
. Hence, we can talk about the quotient space
{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$\widehat{A}(E,\mathcal{L})$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\mathrm{Gau}(E)$}\right.}
.

Remark 2.13
.

For a given
\mathcal{L}

and

can be expressed as,

\nabla^{\phi}\equiv\nabla_{0}+\alpha^{\phi}

\alpha^{\phi}=\phi^{-1}\circ(\nabla_{0}^{\mathrm{End}(E)}\phi)+\phi^{-1}\circ\alpha\circ\phi

Theorem 2.14
.

For a given
\mathcal{L}
-connections
\nabla
, the following statements are equivalent,

(1)

\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}=\mathbb{C}^{\star}.\mathrm{id}_{E}
,

(2)

\mathrm{ker}(\nabla^{\mathrm{End}(E)})=\mathbb{C}.\mathrm{id}_{E}
.

Proof.

The proof follows using the following two facts:

(1)

For
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)
,
\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{\nabla}=\mathrm{ker}(\nabla^{\mathrm{End}(E)})
,

(2)

For a given vector bundle endomorphism
\phi
for a complex vector bundle on a compact complex manifold, we can have
c\in\mathbb{C}
with
|c|
sufficiently large with
I-\frac{\phi}{c}\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)

3.
Real (resp. quaternionic)
\mathcal{L}
-connections and Sobolev spaces

Definition 3.1
.

A toplogical complex manifold with involution is a pair
(X,\sigma_{X})
of a complex manifold
X
and an anti-holomorphic map
\sigma_{X}:X\rightarrow X
such that
\sigma_{X}^{2}\equiv\textrm{id}_{X}
.

Definition 3.2
.

A complex vector bundle
(E,\pi,X)
on a complex manifold with involution
(X,\sigma_{X})
is said to have a real (respectively, quaternionic) structure, if there is a bundle automorphism
(\sigma^{E},\sigma_{X})
such that

(1)

on each fiber, the map
\sigma^{E}
is
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear,

(2)

\sigma^{E}\circ\sigma^{E}\equiv\textrm{id}_{E}
(respectively,
-\textrm{id}_{E}
).

A complex vector bundle with real (respectively, quaternionic) structure on a complex manifold with involution
(X,\sigma_{X})
, is called real(respectively, quaternionic) vector bundle, it will be denoted by
(E,\sigma^{E})
.

Remark 3.3
.

For a given complex vector bundle
E
, there is a complex conjugate vector bundle
\overline{E}
and can be described as a complex vector bundle in which complex structure is multiplication by
-i
instead of
i
also, it can be described as a bundle with same fiber as of
E
but 1-cocycle map is complex conjugate of the 1-cocycle map of
E
.

Remark 3.4
.

A real (respectively, quaternionic) structure on a smooth complex vector bundle is equivalent to having a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism
\Phi:E\rightarrow\overline{\sigma^{\star}_{X}E}
over the identity map
\mathrm{id}_{X}:X\rightarrow X
along with
\Phi^{-1}\equiv\overline{\sigma^{\star}_{X}\Phi}
(respectively,
\Phi^{-1}\equiv-\overline{\sigma^{\star}_{X}\Phi}
).

Remark 3.5
.

For each
k>0
, a given smooth complex Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{L},[.,.],\sharp)
and a real (respectively, quaternionic) smooth complex bundle
(E,\sigma^{E})
on a complex manifold with involution
(X,\sigma_{X})
induces
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism
\tilde{\Phi}:\wedge^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star}\otimes E
and
\overline{\sigma^{\star}_{X}(\wedge^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star}\otimes E)}
covering the identity map on
X
as well as real (resp. quaternionic) structure
(\tilde{\sigma},\sigma_{X})
on
\wedge^{k}\mathcal{L}^{\star}\otimes E
.

3.2.
Sobolev completion of the space of connections

For a given smooth complex Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{L},[.,.],\sharp)
and smooth complex vector bundle
E
on a complex manifold
X
, let
h^{\mathcal{L}}
and
h^{E}
be the Hermitian metrics on the bundles, respectively. Let
g
be the Riemannian metric on the complex manifold, which further gives the volume density
\mathrm{vol}_{g}
as well as Borel measure
\mu_{g}
on
X
. We can define the Sobolev space of
\mathcal{L}
-connections, described as below.

A global section
\psi\in\Gamma(X,E)
is called Borel measurable, if
\psi^{-1}(U)\subset X
is Borel measurable, for each open subset
U\subset E
. Let
L^{2}_{l}(X,E)
,
l\in\mathbb{N}-\{0\}
be the vector space of global Borel measurable sections whose weak derivatives of order upto
l
are square integrable. The Sobolev spaces
L^{2}_{l}(X,E)
are Hilbert spaces with inner product

\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}_{l}}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{l}\langle\nabla^{j}\psi,\nabla^{j}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{l}\int\langle\nabla^{j}\psi,\nabla^{j}\phi\rangle_{h^{E}}d\mu_{g}

where
\langle\nabla^{j}\psi,\nabla^{j}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}}
is the finite
L^{2}
-inner product of weak derivatives of order
j
of given global sections
\phi,\psi\in\Gamma(X,E)
.

The metric
h^{E}

Claim:
The subset
\mathcal{U}^{\sigma}_{\nabla,\epsilon}=p^{\sigma}_{\nabla,\epsilon}(\mathcal{O}^{\sigma}_{\nabla,\epsilon})
is an open subset in
\widehat{B}(E,\mathcal{L})_{l}^{\sigma}
and the map

\Psi^{\sigma}_{\nabla}:\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{l+1}^{\sigma}\times\mathcal{O}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}\rightarrow\left[p^{\sigma}_{\nabla,\epsilon}\right]^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\sigma}_{\nabla,\epsilon})

Proof:
For any
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{l+1}^{\sigma}
, take a nbd
\mathcal{W}=L_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{id}_{E}})
, where
L_{\phi}
is left translation by
\phi\in\mathrm{Gau}(E)_{l+1}^{\sigma}
. Then,

\Psi|_{\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{O}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}}\equiv\tilde{\lambda}_{\phi}\circ\Psi|_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{id}_{E}}\times\mathcal{O}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}}\circ\big(L_{\phi^{-1}}\times\mathrm{id}_{\widehat{A}(E,\mathcal{L})^{\sigma}_{l}}\big)|_{\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{O}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}}

is diffeomorphism, where
\tilde{\lambda}_{\phi}:\widehat{A}(E,h)^{\sigma}_{l}\rightarrow\widehat{A}(E,h)^{\sigma}_{l}
is left multiplication by
\phi

(4)

For any

such that

, define

Now, define the chart map
\sigma_{\nabla}:\mathcal{U}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}
satisfying,

for

Claim:
The Hilbert manifold
\widehat{B}(E,\mathcal{L})_{l}^{\sigma}
is a smooth Hilbert manifold.

Proof:
We need to verify, for two charts
\mathcal{U}_{\nabla,\epsilon}^{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\nabla^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}}^{\sigma}
the transition map

\sigma_{\nabla}\circ\sigma_{\nabla^{\prime}}^{-1}:\sigma_{\nabla^{\prime}}\big(\mathcal{U}_{\nabla^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\nabla,\epsilon}\big)\rightarrow\sigma_{\nabla}\big(\mathcal{U}_{\nabla^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\nabla,\epsilon}\big)

is smooth.
Take
\nabla^{\prime}+\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{\nabla^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}}^{\sigma}
such that
p_{\nabla^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}}^{\sigma}(\nabla^{\prime}+\alpha)\in\mathcal{U}_{\nabla^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\nabla,\epsilon}
, we have

\sigma_{\nabla}\circ\sigma_{\nabla^{\prime}}^{-1}(\nabla^{\prime}+\alpha)=\sigma_{\nabla}(p^{\sigma}_{\nabla,\epsilon}(\nabla^{\prime}+\alpha)\big)=\left[g_{\nabla}(\nabla^{\prime}+\alpha)\right]^{-1}\odot(\nabla^{\prime}+\alpha)

Hence, the transition map is smooth as gauge action is smooth and the map
g_{\nabla}
is smooth.

Perturbative second-order optical susceptibility of bulk materials: a symmetry-enforced return to non-orthogonal localized basis sets
Angiolo Huamán, Luis Enrique Rosas-Hernandez, Salvador Barraza-Lopez

Abstract
The second-order optical susceptibility of semiconductors χ i ​ j ​ k ( 2 ) ​ ( − 2 ​ ω , ω , ω ) \chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega) finds application in metrology, spectroscopy, telecommunications, material characterization, and quantum information. Pioneering calculations of χ i ​ j ​ k ( 2 ) ​ ( − 2 ​ ω , ω , ω ) \chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega) utilized non-orthogonal Gaussian orbitals centered at atoms. That formulation transitioned into plane-wave-based algorithms as time went by. As of late, nevertheless, multiple tools for calculating optical susceptibilities have recast the problem using Wannier ( i.e. , localized ) orbitals, making a comeback onto frameworks based on localized basis sets. Here, we present an approach for calculating χ i ​ j ​ k ( 2 ) ​ ( − 2 ​ ω , ω , ω ) \chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega) reliant on numerical pseudoatomic orbitals (PAOs) within perturbation theory in the velocity gauge. Its salient feature is a calculation of ‘Slater-Koster-like’ two-center integrals of the momentum operator in between PAOs identified by symmetry. The approach was successfully tested on paradigmatic cubic silicon carbide (3C-SiC) and gallium arsenide, for which linear responses are contributed as well.



I
Introduction

Second harmonic generation (SHG)
Franken et al. 1961
arises from the optical second-order susceptibility tensor
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
. It is relied upon to change frequency from laser sources of light, and it has scientific and technological relevance for metrology, spectroscopy and telecommunications, for structural
Yao et al. 2021
and magnetic
Jeong et al. 2024
material characterization, and for generating entangled photon pairs
Drummond and Hillery 2014
for quantum information applications by means of parametric down conversion
Kwiat et al. 1995
;
Kwiat et al. 2001
.

Theoretical
ab initio
formalisms for computing
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
in semiconductors generally follow two main routes: (i) perturbation theory within the independent-particle approximation (IPA), formulated in either the length or velocity gauge
Sipe and Ghahramani 1993
;
Hughes and Sipe 1996
, and with the possibility of intraband transitions
Aversa and Sipe 1995
and electro-optical effects
Sipe and Shkrebtii 2000
; or (ii) time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT)
Luppi et al. 2010
;
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2016
.

Most methodologies for calculating the optical second-order susceptibility based on perturbation theory rely on orthogonal basis sets, typically plane waves or post-processed Wannier functions. Examples include the Optic utility and the TINIBA code
Cabellos et al. 2009
for ABINIT
Gonze et al. 2009
, or the YAMBO code
Grüning and Attaccalite 2014
;
Marini et al. 2009
, which interfaces with QUANTUM ESPRESSO
Giannozzi et al. 2017
, ABINIT, or CASTEP
Milman et al. 2010
. A recent methodology for SHG based on localized basis sets is provided in Ref.
Li et al. 2022
.

Early implementations based on the
2n+1
theorem within density-functional perturbation theory used a Wannier representation obtained through direct total-energy minimization to evaluate the static limit of
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)

Dal Corso and Mauri 1994
. This framework was later extended in Ref.
Dal Corso et al. 1996
, which combined the
2n+1
formalism with TDDFT to compute
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
for several zinc-blende semiconductors. Subsequent real-time TDDFT developments enabled the computation of
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
directly from the time evolution of the polarization. For example, the authors of Ref.
Takimoto et al. 2007
used real-time TDDFT with pseudo-atomic orbitals (PAOs) to evaluate
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
, while the 2Light code
Luppi et al. 2010
—interfaced with ABINIT—can compute macroscopic nonlinear susceptibilities within a many-body TDDFT framework. The Octopus package also offers a TDDFT implementation reliant on linear combinations of PAOs as trial functions
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2020
. YAMBO, in turn, is a dedicated tool for many-body calculations, incorporating TDDFT and solving the Bethe
-
Salpeter equation. TDDFT-based approaches for high-harmonic generation using Gaussian orbitals have been explored as well; see Refs.
Coccia and Luppi 2021
and
Yasin et al. 2021
.

The SIESTA DFT package
Soler et al. 2002
;
García et al. 2020
currently contains
linear
optical response capabilities
Junquera et al. 2001
. Here we present a formalism for the calculation of the second-order optical susceptibility
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)

;
Ozaki 2003
;
Ozaki and Kino 2004
, which we call

Excitonic effects arising from electron
-
hole interactions generated when an electron is promoted from the valence to the conduction bands
Hanke and Sham 1980
;
Berkelbach et al. 2013
can be incorporated through several methodologies: by solving the GW-Bethe-Salpeter equations, as it was done in MoS

Mkrtchian et al. 2019

Ruan et al. 2024

Bloch eigenstates from non-orthogonal pseudo-atomic orbitals

The process for building Bloch wavefunctions

III
Calculating
\chi_{ijk}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
using the PAO basis

The perturbative description of the nonlinear optical response to a weak external optical field by means of the second-order optical susceptibility

VIII
Conclusions

A numerical approach for the calculation of the second-order optical susceptibility reliant on the symmetries of linear momentum integrals among PAOs was developed and demonstrated here. Appropriate Bloch eigenstates and matrix elements were obtained by solving a generalized eigenvalue problem with parameters from a DFT calculation. Two-center integrals (
i.e.
, matrix elements of the momentum operator among PAOs, were calculated by solving a symmetry-informed minimum set of two-center integrals for orbitals separated along the
\hat{z}-
direction, and then rotating the axes into the bond direction. The optical susceptibility was integrated using a Gaussian-Hermite representation of the Dirac delta. The formalism was applied to cubic SiC and GaAs, for which linear responses were reported as well. The results reproduce the susceptibility found in the literature.

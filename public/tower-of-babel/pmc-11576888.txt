Truncating the C terminus of formate dehydrogenase leads to improved preference to nicotinamide cytosine dinucleotide
Authors: Xiaojia Guo, Xueying Wang, Yinghan Hu, Lingyun Zhang, Zongbao K Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFormate dehydrogenase (FDH) is widely applied in regeneration of redox cofactors. There are continuing interests to engineer FDH for improved catalytic activity and cofactor preference. In the crystal structure of FDH from Pseudomonas sp. 101 (pseFDH), the C terminus with 9 amino acid residues cannot be resolved. However, our earlier work showed mutations at C terminus led pseFDH variants to favor a non-natural cofactor nicotinamide cytosine dinucleotide (NCD). Here, we investigated the role of C-terminal residues on cofactor preference by truncating their corresponding C terminus of pseFDH variants. Sequence comparison analysis showed that C-terminal residues were barely conservative among different FDHs. pseFDH and mutants with their C termini truncated were constructed, and the resulted variants showed improved preference to NCD mainly because NAD-dependent activity dropped more substantially. Further structure analysis showed that these pseFDH variants had their cofactor binding domains reconstructed to favor molecular interactions with NCD. Our work indicated that C-terminal residues of pseFDH affected enzyme activity and cofactor preference, which provides a new approach for ameliorating the performance of redox enzymes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-79885-z.Keywords: Formate dehydrogenase, Directed evolution, Cofactor preference, C terminus, Non-natural cofactorSubject terms: Biochemistry, Biocatalysis, Enzymes

Formate dehydrogenase (FDH) catalyzes the oxidation of formic acid to CO2 with the concomitant reduction of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD, Fig. 1a) to its reduced form NADH1. Due to the low cost and rich resources of formate and the inert property of CO2, FDH is exquisite for NADH regeneration (Fig. 1b)2–5. Alternatively, FDH has also been explored as a component for CO2 fixation with extra electron donor6–9, which endows it with promising applications. Efforts have been made to ameliorate the cofactor preference of FDH for specific purpose. For example, FDH was evolved to favor NADP rather than NAD10,11, since many enzymes used for biosynthesis are dependent on NADP as the cofactor12–15. We previously acquired a series of mutants that favor a non-natural cofactor nicotinamide cytosine dinucleotide (NCD, Fig. 1a) for the construction of energy-selective transfer systems through directed evolution16–18. In the above cases, attentions were mostly focused on cofactor or substrate binding domain and residues were selected for semi-rational design. On the other hand, the extended N-terminal and C-terminal residues of proteins may play key roles in the formation of structures, intramolecular interactions or activities19,20. However, to the best of our knowledge, the effects of protein terminus on cofactor preference have not been investigated in redox enzyme engineering.

Structures of NAD and NCD (a) and the application of FDH in NADH regeneration (b).

FDH from Pseudomonas sp. 101 (pseFDH), which harbors 401 amino acids, has been widely studied due to its superior properties in terms of activity and stability2,21–23. Its C-terminal conformation is believed to be highly disordered since the last 9 amino acid residues cannot be resolved in holoFDH, and even more residues are not shown at C terminus in apoFDH according to X-ray crystal structure analysis24. A flexible loop, locating near the cofactor binding domain, is observed at C terminus in the crystal structure of pseFDH. Hence, we speculated that the special configuration of the C terminus may have influence on activity and cofactor preference.

Here we show that truncated pseFDH and variants at C terminus have improved NCD preference. We first analyzed the crystal structures of wild type pseFDH (WT) and mutants and demonstrated that C-terminal amino acid residues were invisible in electron density and not observed in WT as well as mutants. Sequence analysis of FDH from different sources indicated that C terminus was hardly conserved. Variants were then constructed by truncating C terminus of WT and mutants. Kinetic analysis showed that truncated variants had improved NCD preference. Finally, structure analysis revealed that the cofactor binding cavity was rebuilt and fewer interactions were formed between the C terminus truncated variants and NAD, leading to increased preference to NCD. Our work provides a new strategy in engineering redox enzymes for ameliorated cofactor preference.

Results and discussion

Structure analysis and sequence comparison of the C terminus of FDH

In our previous work, directed evolution of pseFDH to favor NCD was achieved25. Residues around the adenine ring of NAD in NAD-pseFDH complex (PDB: 2NAD) were selected for construction of mutation libraries. Mutations at S381 and S383 at C terminus significantly rendered pseFDH to favor NCD over NAD. Interestingly, the C terminus after the residue E392 in crystal structure of pseFDH are absent (PDB: 2NAD, 2GUG and 2GO1)24,26. Only 391 residues (residue 2 to 392) in chain A and 383 residues (residue 2 to 384) in chain B are observable in the structure of pseFDH (PDB: 2NAD) due to the disordered conformation of its C terminus24 (Fig. 2). Crystal structures of NCD favored mutants A2 (C256I/E261P/S381I, PDB: 6JUK) and 3A3 (V198I/C256I/P260S/E261P/S381N/S383F, PDB: 6JUJ) also showed the deficiency of residues at C terminus after A375 and Y382, respectively (Fig. 2). These results promoted us to investigate whether C-terminal residues play any roles on enzymatic activity and cofactor preference.

Analysis of C-terminal residues of pseFDH and mutants. Mutant A2, C256I/E261P/S381I; Mutant 3A3, V198I/C256I/P260S/E261P/S381N/S383F. The ligand in WT is NAD, and the ligand in mutant A2 or 3A3 is NCD.

In order to study the function of the C terminus of pseFDH, we first analyzed the C-terminal residues of FDHs from different species. Sequences from bacteria, plants and fungi were aligned (Fig. 3a). Results revealed that C-terminal sequences of FDH were barely conserved, but FDHs from bacteria, that is MvaFDH, LtuFDH, FduFDH and SnoFDH, shared similar C terminus. Notably, in some FDHs, such as OsaFDH, approximately 20 amino acids from the C terminus are absent compared with pseFDH. This indicated that the extended C terminus may have special roles in bacteria.

Homology model of pseFDH with residue 2 to 400 was built based on the crystal structure of pseFDH (PDB: 2NAD) and FDH from Moraxella sp.C2 (PDB: 2GSD). It showed that the C terminus was folded as a helix after the long flexible loop which located beside the NAD binding domain (Fig. 3b, c). Importantly, we previously found that mutations at the adenine binding sites narrowed the cavity and led to favor NCD over NAD (Fig. 1a). Therefore, the structure of this flexible loop is important to cofactor recognition. It was supposed that the C-terminal helix had an effect on the spacial position of the flexible loop, and further influenced cofactor activity and preference of the protein.

Kinetic assays of truncated pseFDH and mutants

In the previous work25, a series of pseFDH mutants, namely 5C3 (A199C/S381N), 3C4 (A199C/E261P/S381N), A2 (C256I/E261P/S381I) and 3A3 (V198I/C256I/P260S/E261P/S381N/S383F), that favored NCD were obtained through directed evolution. To investigate the influence of C-terminal residues on enzymatic activity, we fused a His6-tag to the N terminus of wild type enzyme and those mutants to give N-WT (wild type), N-5C3, N-3C4, N-A2 and N-3A3. Removing the residues after E392 resulted in truncated variants N-WT-392, N-5C3-392, N-3C4-392, N-A2-392 and N-3A3-392, respectively. When C terminus was shortened, NCD preference increased by about 2-fold for N-WT, N-3C4 and N-3A3, while there was no obvious improvement for other variants (Table 1).

After the C terminus was truncated, the kcat value to NCD of most variants reduced except N-3C4. However, since the catalytic efficiency to NAD dropped more than that to NCD, variants N-WT-392, N-3C4-392 and N-3A3-392 showed improved NCD preference. Taking N-3A3-392 as an example, the activity to NCD almost remained the same and the NCD preference enhanced to 1045-fold in comparison with N-WT, making it the most specific variant to NCD.

In particular, the Km value of N-3C4-392 to NAD increased remarkably compared to its original form N-3C4, reaching 1.26 mM. This is the similar level to the intracellular NAD concentration of E.coli27. The increased Km value and reduced catalytic efficiency make the in vivo reaction catalyzed by N-3C4-392 inefficient with NAD. As reported, NCD self-sufficient strains can synthesize up to 5.0 mM NCD28. Therefore, it is applicable to construct NCD-dependent metabolic pathway with N-3C4-392 due to its considerable activity to NCD. On the other hand, the increased Km value to NAD of primary mutants like N-3C4-392 shows that truncating the C terminus accelerates the evolution process, since this triple-mutant demonstrated similar activity to NAD and NCD with the hexa-mutant N-3A3. Hence, truncating C terminus of pseFDH and mutants greatly promotes the processes of directed evolution and further the construction of orthogonal metabolic system mediated by NCD.

As for the activity to formate with NAD as the cofactor, the catalytic efficiencies of N-WT and mutants reduced when C terminus was truncated. However, the Km values of all variants are similar to N-WT when using NCD as the cofactor, while the kcat values were slightly decreased (Table S1). Significantly, as for N-3C4 and N-3A3, when truncating the C terminus, the catalytic efficiency to formate remained. These results demonstrated that truncating C terminus of NCD preferred variants had limited influence on the affinity to formate with NCD as the cofactor. Besides, we estimated the stability of these truncated variants in terms of pH and temperature (Fig. S1, Fig. S2). The activity of most variants decreased in alkaline condition except N-WT. This represented that pseFDH is robust, but mutation and truncation of pseFDH led to reduced robustness. The specific activity for NCD of all variants except N-A2 retained when they were treated at higher temperature. With NAD as cofactor, N-WT and N-WT-392 presented mildly decreased activity while there was no obvious decline for the activity of other variants at higher temperature. Therefore, even with the C terminus truncated, these variants maintained thermostability under the tested condition.

Structure analysis of truncated FDHs

In order to further explore the molecular mechanism of cofactor preference of truncated FDHs, structures were built based on homologous modeling. We first analyzed the structure of WT variants WT-392, WT-382 and WT-375 with C terminus truncated at residue 392, 382 and 375, respectively (Fig. 4). It showed that truncating the C terminus of WT reshaped the cofactor binding domain. In WT-392, NAD was almost embedded in the protein, and strong protein ligand interactions were observed (Fig. 4a, d). Specifically, there were hydrogen bonds between E261, H259, S381 and adenine ring of NAD, respectively. As for NCD, H259 and the cytosine ring in NCD formed a hydrogen bond and a C-H···π interaction. Those interactions disappeared in WT-382 and WT-375 even though new C-H···π interactions were formed between the residues and the cytosine ring (Fig. 4e, f). NAD or NCD was exposed in solvent as more residues were removed at C terminus in WT-382 and WT-375. The binding interactions between the proteins and ligands were destroyed, which led to the loss of activity. Besides, there were charged residues at C terminus, such as glutamate and lysine, and removal of these terminal residues may result in the disordered electrical properties of protein domains (Fig. 3a).

Cofactor binding domains of pseFDH variants (a-c) and molecular interaction analysis (d-f). Domain and interactions analysis are shown based on homologous modeling and molecular docking.

As for mutants 3C4 and 3A3, structure analysis showed that six hydrogen bonds were formed between N-3A3 and the cytosine ribose of NCD, which provided considerable activity to NCD (Fig. 5). In the structure of the primary mutant N-3C4 with triple mutations, interactions were rearranged between the protein and amino acid residues including the mutated sites C199 and N381. With the C terminus truncated after residue 392, different interactions were observed in the complex of N-3C4-392 and NCD. Notably, two hydrogen bonds were formed between the ribose of NCD and N381 in the C terminus of N-3C4-392. This represented that truncating C terminus of N-3C4 rebuilt the NCD binding cavity which contributed to the increased NCD preference. And this is also the reason that N-3C4-392 exhibited the similar activity to NCD like the advanced mutant N-3A3.

Conclusion

Overall, we investigated the influence of amino acid residues at C terminus on cofactor preference of pseFDH and mutants. It was found that C-terminal sequences and structure were flexible, so variants were constructed by truncating C terminus of WT and mutants. Kinetic analysis showed that C terminus truncation caused more activity loss with NAD than NCD, thus creating higher NCD preference. Structure analysis indicated that interactions between truncated WT variants and NAD were disrupted, which resulted in the loss of activity. Preference to NCD of the truncated variant N-3C4-392 was enhanced since new hydrogen bonds were formed between residues and the cytosine ribose of NCD. These results suggested that cofactor preference of pseFDH can be regulated through the manipulation of the C terminus. Our work demonstrated that ameliorating the performance of redox enzymes can be achieved through this approach in addition to other conventional methods such as site-directed mutagenesis.

Materials and methods

Strains and media

Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) was used to express protein. Strains were cultured in LB medium with 50 μg/mL kanamycin at 37 oC. Terrific-Broth medium with 50 μg/mL kanamycin and 0.1 mM IPTG was used for protein expression at 25 oC for 48 h. All strains and plasmids are shown in Table S2.

Plasmids harboring truncated FDH gene sequence were constructed by RF cloning29. Plasmid with wild type FDH was used as the template25. RF cloning products were digested with DpnI and then transferred in to BL21(DE3). Plasmids were sequenced in Synbio Technologies (Suzhou, China). All primers are shown in Table S3.

Protein purification

FDH and mutants were expressed in BL21(DE3) with corresponding plasmids and purified by Ni − NTA kit (Invitrogen). Purified proteins were stored in 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5) supplemented with 15% glycerol at -80 °C. Protein concentration was measured by the Bio-Rad protein assay kit and SDS-PAGE was carried out for purity analysis (Fig. S3).

Activity and kinetic assay

As for activity assay of crude enzyme, cells were lysed and supernatant was used as crude enzyme. Activity assay was performed in a total volume of 0.1 mL with 50 mM HEPES (pH 7.5), 10 mM formate, 0.4 mM 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl tetrazolium bromide (MTT), 1 mM phenazine ethosulfate (PES), 100 μM NAD or NCD. After adding 10 μL of enzyme, the reaction mixture was detected at 570 nm and the absorbance was monitored continuously30.

For stability experiments, purified pseFDH variants were treated at different temperatures before activity detection or assays were conducted at varying pHs. Specifically, variants were treated at 0 oC, 23 oC (room temperature), 37 oC and 50 oC for 30 min. Activity assays were then conducted in 50 mM HEPES (pH 7.5). For stability detection at different pHs, experiments were carried out at pH 7.5 (50 mM HEPES), 9 (50 mM Tris-HCl) and 10 (50 mM Tris-HCl), respectively. All activity assays were performed with the same reagents as the crude enzyme activity assay at 30 oC. One unit of enzyme activity was defined as the production of 1 μmol NADH or NCDH per minute.

Kinetic parameters of purified enzymes were determined at 30 °C with 50 mM HEPES (pH 7.5), 0.4 mM MTT and 1 mM PES. When determining the kinetic data of pseFDH variants for cofactors, the concentration of NAD varied from 5 μM to 80 mM with 10 mM, 50 mM or 60 mM formate, and NCD varied from 12.5 μM to 20 mM with 60 mM formate. When determining the kinetic data of pseFDH variants for formate, the concentration of cofactor was 2 mM, 5 mM, 10 mM or 20 mM, and the concentration of formate varied from 0.05 mM to 100 mM. Reaction was monitored according to the increasing absorbance of the product formazan at 570 nm. A molar absorption coefficient of 16.2 mM− 1 cm− 1 of formazan was used. All data was fitted with Michaelis-Menten equation by OriginPro 2021. All tests were performed in triplicates or duplicates.

Sequence alignment

Sequence alignment was carried out by Espript 3.031.

Homology modeling and molecular docking

The model of pseFDH were built with MOE (CCG, Montreal, Canada) using pseFDH (PDB: 2NAD) or FDH from Moraxella sp.C2 (PDB: 2GSD) as templates. Homology models of pseFDH variants and mutants were conducted by SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/)32. Molecular docking of pseFDH variants with NAD or NCD was performed with MOE. The docking site was set as “Ligand atoms” and NAD or NCD was used as the ligand. Docking was carried out with Triangle Matcher as the placement method and Rigid Receptor as the refinement method. London dG with 100 poses and GBVI/WSA dG with 20 poses were used as score methods, respectively. Protein surface and ligand interaction between protein and cofactor were analyzed by MOE.

Effect of vibrational forces from acceledent on dentoalveolar surface temperature during clear aligner therapy: an infrared thermography study
Authors: Andrea Vitores-Calero, Natalia Zamora-Martínez, Jose Ignacio Priego-Quesada, Rosa Cibrián-Ortiz de Anda, Jaime Gil López-Areal, Carlos Bellot-Arcís, Beatriz Tarazona-Álvarez, Verónica García-Sanz, José Luis Gandía-Franco, Vanessa Paredes-Gallardo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMechanical vibration devices such as AcceleDent have been marketed as non-invasive adjuncts capable of accelerating orthodontic tooth movement. The proposed mechanism involves the stimulation of cellular activity and bone remodeling. The aim of this study was to determine whether the use of AcceleDent, in combination with clear aligner therapy, produces a measurable increase in the surface temperature of intraoral tissues. Twenty-two adult patients (mean age: 39 ± 15 years) undergoing orthodontic treatment with clear aligners (Invisalign) participated in this crossover study. Infrared thermography (A35, FLIR, Wilsonville, USA) was used to record temperature measurements of gingival and dental surfaces. Each participant underwent two experimental conditions on separate days: (1) wearing the AcceleDent device while active (vibrating) and (2) wearing the same device inactive (non-vibrating). After a 20-minute thermal acclimatization period, the device was applied for 20 min before imaging. No statistically significant differences were observed in surface temperature between the vibrating and non-vibrating conditions (Gingiva: p = 0.69; Tooth: p = 0.76). Likewise, no significant differences were detected at baseline between the two sessions (Gingiva: p = 0.35; Tooth: p = 0.57). The use of AcceleDent vibration during clear aligner therapy does not result in an increase in the surface temperature of gingival or dental tissues, as measured by infrared thermography. This study provides evidence that AcceleDent vibration does not produce thermal changes in intraoral tissues, suggesting it is thermally safe for use alongside clear aligners. Infrared thermography may serve as a non-invasive tool to monitor physiological effects of orthodontic devices.Keywords: AcceleDent, Clear aligner therapy, Orthodontic vibration, Infrared thermography, Surface temperature, Gingival tissue, Dentoalveolar complexSubject terms: Health care, Medical research

Introduction

The growing demand for orthodontic treatments by the adult population has led to an increased focus on aesthetic considerations1. The unappealing appearance and the long duration of traditional treatments (approx. 20 months) are two of the main reasons for not undergoing orthodontic treatment2. Furthermore, the risk of periodontitis, demineralization, and root resorption during orthodontic treatments increases due to their prolonged duration3–6.

Meeting the expectations regarding orthodontic design and reduction of treatment duration is essential for patient satisfaction2,7–9. All of this has opened up new lines of research to reduce procedure times with new techniques and devices10. Some examples of therapies that aim to reduce treatment duration include chemical agents and hormones11–13, physical stimulations14–17, the use of extracellular vesicles to regulate bone metabolism by acting on both osteoclastogenesis and osteogenesis18–20 or surgical procedures with corticotomies and micro-osteoperforations21–26. However, some of the techniques present ethical and tumorigenicity dilemmas, and the others are rarely used due to the aggressiveness of the procedures22. For this reason, non-invasive treatments such as mechanical vibration and photobiomodulation devices are of interest in the clinical field14,16,17,27. Among the group of noninvasive techniques developed to accelerate tooth movement is the OrthoPulse28. This device bases its effectiveness on accelerating tooth movement through photobiomodulation and acting on the mitochondria producing an increase in ATP, which translates into greater metabolic activity3,29,30.

On the other hand, mechanical vibration devices are based on the fact that vibration could stimulate cell differentiation and maturation, accelerating bone remodeling and therefore tooth movement5,8,27,31,32. This would be due to the fact that osteocytes are able to perceive this vibration and respond by producing soluble factors that inhibit the formation of osteoclasts33. Moreover, vibration produces an increase in the expression of RANKL, which is essential for the formation, function and survival of osteoclasts17,34,35.

Although the literature is scarce on the effect of tooth temperature on bone remodeling, there is evidence in other clinical areas36. In this sense, Ikuta et al.37 showed that thermal stimulation accelerates osteogenesis in vivo by improving bone formation in a rat tibial defect model by applying a temperature ranging from 43 °C to 46 °C. Moreover, it was observed that a hyperthermia treatment at 45 °C for 15 min once a week in rats and rabbits increased significantly the area of mineralized bone formation38.

In this study, we have assessed one of the mechanical vibration devices most used in orthodontics, the Acceledent device39. There is another intraoral device (VPro5)40 but it vibrates at a different frequency, so we cannot generalize the results. Therefore, the study aimed to determine whether the Acceledent device produces an increase in the surface temperature of the superior dentoalveolar complex, which is positively related to increased bone remodeling and, therefore, it is expected that, the device increase the temperature of the dentoalveloar complex. It is important to emphasize that this increase in temperature should not exceed the threshold of 5.5 °C, since from that point on, irreversible pulpitis can occur35.

Materials and methods

Study design

The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the University of Valencia (procedural registration Hl543405105252) and follows the Declaration of Helsinki. Before their participation, all patients were informed of the study design and signed the informed consent.

Sample selection

A sample of twenty two patients was recruited from the Dental Clinic of the University of Valencia participated in this study (13 males and 9 females, age 42 ± 18 years). The inclusion criteria of the study consisted in to be older than 18 years old, patients with Invisalign aligners (Align Technology, Inc., Arizona, USA), who do not use elastics and who are not carriers of micro screws and periodontally controlled patients. The exclusion criteria were to not comply with the number of hours that the aligners must be worn, not comply with pre-study guidelines, patients with salivary gland disease or syndromes and patients with extraction wounds.

Sample size calculation

The sample size required for accurately detecting significant mean values was calculated to be 20 individuals. For a confidence level of 95%, an effect size of f = 0.3 (medium-small), and alpha error probability of 0.05 and a 90% of power (Fig. 1).

Sample size calculation.

Image adquisition

Gingiva and tooth surface temperature were measured using an infrared thermography camera (A35, FLIR, Wilsonville, USA). The camera has a resolution of 320 × 256 (81,920 pixels), providing high-quality images and the ability to detect temperature differences of 0.05 °C.

The camera sends thermal images at up to 60 Hz directly to the system for instantaneous data and allows you to select the appropriate field of view for the measurement area, ranging from 69° to 6.5°.

The area of interest had to cover the entire region of the upper central incisors and the gingiva from canine to canine. The camera was located at 10 cm of the distance of the tooth.

Before the experiment, the camera was calibrated by a certified company (Alava Ingenieros S.A, Madrid, Spain), to reduce the error of the camera.

The infrared camera was connected to a computer, which controlled its operation using the FLIR Tools + software (FLIR, Wilsonville, USA). Thermal images were obtained every 5 min during the thermal adaptation phase and every 10 min while the device was in the mouth.

To ensure that everything was working properly, both the laptop and the FLIR A35 model thermal imaging camera were turned on 10 min before starting to take the measurements.

Measurement protocol

Before the experiments, to reduce as possible the gingiva temperature variability, participants were instructed to follow these instructions: do not drink alcoholic beverages, caffeinated or other stimulants 4 h before the study; do not smoke, eat very abundant meals or do physical exercise in 4 h before the study; and do not sunbathe or have ultraviolet sessions in the previous 24 h.

The protocol included two days of measurements for each participant and was carefully crafted to reproduce real intraoral conditions as faithfully as possible. This protocol is shown graphically in Fig. 2 to facilitate its understanding.

Timeline of the recordings taken with the thermal camera.

Day 1: The first test was used to evaluate whether introducing the device into the mouth without activation (vibration OFF) produces a change in temperature.

For this purpose, a first thermal image was taken, with the Invisalign splint on. This served to observe basal temperature variations from the beginning.

From this moment on, one thermal image was taken every 5 min for 20 min, since this is the estimated time for acclimatization of the temperature of the gum and teeth to the environment, based on tests previously carried out.

After 20 min of acclimatization, the AcceleDent device (OrthoAccel Technologies, Inc., Texas, USA) was placed without activating (vibration OFF). A thermal image was taken after 10 min (time in which the first vibration would end) and another at 20 min (time in which the second vibration of the device would end).

Finally, the AcceleDent was removed, and after 5 min a final control thermal image was taken.

Day 2: The second test was performed following the same protocol but this time with the AcceleDent device activated (vibration ON). The device pauses 3 times in the middle of the vibration cycle to indicate that it has reached 10 min of vibration and continues with the next 10 min vibration cycle until it stops completely, so, once you turn on the device it performs the complete cycle unless you press the button again to stop it for any reason, because the device has no additional configuration. This record was used to evaluate whether the temperature increase with the device activated was greater, the same or less than when the device has not been activated.

Throughout the experiment, the participant was sitting still, wearing the NOLA cheek retractor to maintain the mouth open (to visualize the central incisors and the gingiva from canine to canine with the infrared camera) with the Invisalign splint on.

Room temperature was controlled during all the experiments by the air conditioning system of the clinic (24 °C C and 30% of relative humidity). The absence of windows in the room was planned to avoid that the sun’s rays could affect the measurements with the thermal camera.

Infrared thermography measurements and analyses

FLIR Tools + software was used for data processing, obtaining the mean temperature of two ROIs (Fig. 3): the gingiva (region between superior right canine and superior left canine) and the central zone of the tooths (between superior right central incisor and superior left central incisor).

Regions of Interest assessed: gingiva and central zone of the tooth.

An emissivity of 0.95 (emissivity of water) was used for both ROIs, because both the gum and the teeth are covered by saliva and saliva is composed of 96% water41. Variations (∆) of the temperature (difference between each moment with the end of the thermal adaptation) were also estimated. After compiling the temperature variations in an Excel spreadsheet, it was analyzed whether the temperatures were greater than or equal, at any time during the measurements, to the 43 °C described in the literature as the temperature from which, if maintained at 43 °C or higher, an acceleration of osteogenesis occurs37.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using RStudio (version 2023.03.0, Posit Software, Boston, USA). After checking the normality of the data using the Shapiro-Wilk test, ANOVAs of repeated measures were performed for each ROI (Gingiva and Tooth) and each temperature parameter (absolute temperature and ∆temperatures) with 2 factors (the time and if the AcceleDent was turn on or not). If the ANOVA was significant, Bonferroni post-hocs were performed for pair comparisons. Data were presented as mean and 95% confidence interval (95% CI).

There was no statistically significant difference between the initial average temperature values ​​of the gum. The difference was 0.24 °C (p = 1.000). There was also no difference observed between the final gum values ​​when removing the Aceledent (p = 0.095). Moreover, there was a difference of 1.25 °C between the two times of placement of AcceleDent, that is, between the first day and the second day (vibration OFF and vibration ON) but it was not statistically significant (p = 0.104).

In most patients, the temperature exhibited a consistent pattern: an initial decrease during the acclimatization phase, followed by stabilization and a peak upon device insertion, and a subsequent decline after device removal.

Removing the device when it had not been activated (vibration OFF) caused a drop in temperature of approximately 1 °C, at the gum level. On the other hand, when the device had been activated (vibration ON), the decrease in temperature, was approximately 0.5 °C. This difference in temperature decline was statistically significant (p = 0.006). A statistically significant difference between the basal temperature and the temperature recorded after introducing the device was found. The temperature raised by approximately 0.5 °C at the gum level (p = 0.003). At the dental level, this statistically significant difference was not observed although the difference was 0.37 °C for the tooth (p = 0.149).

Discussion

The aim of the study was to determine if the Acceledent device produces an increase in the surface temperature of the superior dentoalveolar complex. To the authors’ knowledge, in a systematic review carried out in addition to this study, which is in the process of being published, the relationship between vibration frequency and stimulation of cells and cellular biomarkers responsible for bone remodelling was studied, finding that the vibration frequency that produced the greatest stimulation was 50 Hz, moving within a stimulation range between 40 Hz and 120 Hz. However, despite the fact that Acceledent vibrates at a frequency of 30 Hz, it was able to increase osteoclastic, osteoblastic and fibroblastic proliferation. Therefore, we sought to investigate, secondarily, whether there was a relationship between increased cellular activity and increased intraoral temperature. In addition, no previous study has evaluated the increase in temperature produced by the Acceledent, which could improve bone remodelling.

The election of choosing the infrared thermography camera A35 (FLIR, Wilsonville, USA) was because it can focus at a shorter distance than other thermal imaging cameras, allowing it to get very close to the mouth and obtain images with larger regions of interest (ROI) of the tooth and gingiva. It accurately measures temperatures up to 60 °C, which is well above the temperatures in our study. Its small, compact size makes it suitable for confined spaces, such as the oral cavity.

Mechanical vibration devices are suggested as effective to increase the acceleration of tooth movement resulting in a stimulation of osteoblasts and osteoclasts5,6. One of the possible mechanisms of stimulation of osteoblasts and osteoclasts can be an increase in temperature because early studies have indicated that increases in temperature (1.5–3.0 °C) stimulate the longitudinal and concentric growth of the femur and tibia of growing rats and dogs42.

The results of the present study showed that having AcceleDent device turned on did not result in a higher increase of gingiva and tooth surface temperature. It is important to note that these results do not confirm whether or not the AcceleDent device is capable of accelerating tooth movement, what they do confirm is that having it on does not increase gingiva and tooth surface temperature. One possible explanation for these results is the level of vibration of the device. We did not find direct evidence in the reviewed literature that material-dependent temperature changes occur specifically when using this vibrating device. Studies involving AcceleDent tend to address its efficacy in accelerating tooth movement, pain perception, or treatment duration, rather than detailed thermal effects14,43.

Other devices with a higher frequency of vibration (VPro5, 120 Hz compared with the 30 Hz of Acceledent) presented a higher capacity of stimulating osteoblast and fibroblast cell proliferation/gene expression, with a shorter duration of the treatment (5 min/day vs. 20 min/day)40. Other articles also observed that a higher frequency of vibration (peak of 50 Hz) resulted in an increase of osteoblastic (by higher alkaline phosphatase) and osteogenic differentiation (by higher Runx biomarker)40,44,45. More in vivo studies are necessary to establish the effects of these higher vibration frequencies on gingiva and tooth surface temperature, and therefore on the effectiveness of the clinical treatment.

If stimulation of osteoclasts and osteoblasts is taking place, it will be due to a different factor than temperature. One possible factor could be functional, making the aligners easier to insert in the first few days, so that the teeth can be repositioned more quickly.

The protocol was established to reproduce, as far as possible, the conditions in which the patient uses the device at home and to observe only the changes produced by the device. Some aspects were taken into account to have a reproducible situation and to minimize as much as possible confusing factors that could cause temperature changes, such as using the NOLA cheek retractor to avoid the manipulation of the area.

In addition, studies conducted on photobiomodulation (PBM) focus on studying the effects on root resorption and comparing treatment duration between patients who have undergone PBM and those who have not. Since there are no articles evaluating the increase in temperature after using this type of device, a comparison with the results of this study cannot be made3,4,46,47.

While this study focused on aligners, it is worth considering whether different orthodontic appliances—particularly metal fixed appliances with metallic brackets and archwires—might experience different temperature changes. Metals generally have higher thermal conductivity than the thermoplastics used in aligners, so one might expect that with metal braces there could be more rapid heat transfer from oral fluids, hot/cold foods, or external sources into bracket–wire assemblies and potentially to the tooth or pulp. Finite element modelling and in vitro work have indeed shown that fixed metallic appliances may lead to higher temperature rises in dental tissues under thermal stress compared to systems without metal components48,49. For example, a thermal-stimulus model found that metallic brackets and wires increased pulp temperatures more than when no such appliance was present48.

In summary, yes, one might reasonably expect different temperature behaviour with metal fixed appliances versus aligners because of differences in material thermal conductivity and heat capacity; however, there’s a lack of specific data about whether AcceleDent® usage with metal appliances causes any meaningful additional temperature change that could affect dental tissues.

Limitations and generalizability

One of the limitations of the study was the fact of having the mouth open during the procedure, which is not similar to the real situation. Perhaps the temperature did not increase as much as it should due to greater temperature transfer with the environment. However, in a more ecological situation, other factors such as salivation or contact of the lips with the gum would have had a greater influence. Another limitation was that two patients presented less contact of the upper anterior teeth with the device due to their particular type of malocclusion. However, these two patients were not removed as no particular differences with the other were observed in the results. In addition, the study was limited by its small sample size, its single-center design, and the lack of randomization of the order of interventions, which may reduce the generalizability of the findings. Further research is needed to determine whether AcceleDent produces sufficient changes in temperature to accelerate bone metabolism taking into account the sex, the number of aligners, the type of malocclusion or increasing the time that the device remains in the mouth.

Conclusions

Based on the results of the present study, the vibrational forces generated by the AcceleDent device did not induce measurable thermal changes in intraoral tissues. Activating the device did not lead to a greater increase in gingival or tooth surface temperature compared with the vibration-off condition. Infrared thermography appears to be a valuable non-invasive method for monitoring the physiological effects of orthodontic devices.

Future work could use in vivo or in vitro thermal measurements during AcceleDent activation both with aligners and with fixed appliances to clarify whether any material-dependent heating is clinically relevant.

Universal Discrete Filtering with Lookahead or Delay
Pumiao Yan, Jiwon Jeong, Naomi Sagan, Tsachy Weissman

Abstract
We consider the universal discrete filtering problem, where an input sequence generated by an unknown source passes through a discrete memoryless channel, and the goal is to estimate its components based on the output sequence, with limited lookahead or delay. We propose and establish the universality of a family of schemes for this setting. These schemes are induced by universal Sequential Probability Assignments (SPAs), and inherit their computational properties. We show that the schemes induced by LZ78 are practically implementable and well-suited for scenarios with limited computational resources and latency constraints. In passing, we use some of the intermediate results to obtain upper and lower bounds that appear to be new, in the purely Bayesian setting, on the optimal filtering performance in terms, respectively, of the mutual information between the noise-free and noisy sequence, and the entropy of the noise-free sequence causally conditioned on the noisy one.

II-B

Properties

We can obtain the expression for marginal probabilities of
X_{t}
using the following steps. By the law of total probability,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z\right)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{X}}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z|X_{t}=x\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x\right).

(1)

This can be formulated as the matrix-vector product

P_{Z_{t}}=\Pi^{\top}P_{X_{t}}\iff P_{X_{t}}=\Pi^{-\top}P_{Z_{t}}.

(2)

Using this expression for
P_{X_{t}}
, we can derive the conditional probabilities of
X_{t}
given

. By Bayes’ theorem,

\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x|Z_{t}=z\right)=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z|X_{t}=x\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z\right)}=\frac{\Pi(x,z)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z\right)}.

(3)

In matrix-vector form,

P_{X_{t}|Z_{t}=z}=\frac{\Pi(\cdot,z)\odot\left(\Pi^{-\top}P_{Z_{t}}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z\right)}\triangleq F\left(P_{Z_{t}},\Pi,z\right),

(4)

where
\odot
represents element-wise multiplication (
i.e.
, Hadamard product or Schur product).

Lemma II.1

(Conditional Probabilities of
X_{t}
given the sequence
Z^{t}
)
.

For all

P_{X_{t}|Z^{t}=z^{t}}=F\left(P_{Z_{t}|z^{t-1}},\Pi,z_{t}\right),

P_{X_{t}|Z^{n}=z^{n}}=F\left(P_{Z_{t}|z^{t-1},z_{t+1}^{n}},\Pi,z_{t}\right).

We can extend (

III
Bounding Excess Loss

In the context of estimation, the optimal decision minimizing the expected loss with respect to a given loss function
\mathrm{\Lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
and a distribution
P_{X}
is referred to as the
Bayes response
. The action space comprises estimates of the random variable
X\sim P_{X}
, and the optimal action/estimate is the
Bayes estimate
of
X
or the
Bayes response
to
P_{X}
.

Definition III.1

(Bayes estimator/response)
.

The Bayes estimator of
X
under the distribution
P_{X}
and loss function
\mathrm{\Lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
is given by:

\hat{X}_{B}\left(P_{X}\right)=\arg\min_{\hat{x}}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{\Lambda}(X,\hat{x})]=\arg\min_{\hat{x}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{A}_{X}}\mathrm{\Lambda}(x,\hat{x})P_{X}(x)=\arg\min_{\hat{x}}\lambda_{\hat{x}}^{T}P_{X}.

Note that
\hat{X}_{B}\left(P_{X}\right)
can be generalized to
\hat{X}_{B}(\mathbf{v})
with arbitrary vector
\mathbf{v}
. This
\mathbf{v}
can also be a random vector such as
P_{X|Z}
.

Lemma III.2

(Optimal Estimator)
.

Let
W\in\mathcal{W}
be a random variable. Then, the best estimator of
X
based on
W
in the sense of minimizing the expected loss can be represented as follows:

\arg\min_{\hat{X}(\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\hat{X}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X,\hat{X}(W)\right)\right]=\hat{X}_{B}\left(P_{X|W}\right).

The proof is merely an application of the law of total expectation and the definition of the Bayes estimator.

Definition III.3

(Sequential Probability Assignment, SPA)
.

A Sequential Probability Assignment (SPA) assigns probabilities to the next symbol in a sequence based on all previous symbols. For a sequence
X
, the SPA
q
is a collection of conditional probability distributions:

q\triangleq\left\{q_{t}(x_{t}|x^{t-1})\right\}_{t\geq 1},

where
q_{t}(\cdot|x^{t-1})
is a probability distribution over the possible values of
x_{t}
.

Here, we establish theoretical bounds on excess expected estimation loss beyond the Bayes optimum due to optimizing the filters to an SPA other than the true one. These will subsequently enable the design of universal filtering schemes with robust performance across diverse settings.

Causal Estimation

In the causal setting, we estimate

By Lemma

, we know that the optimal causal filter given

(5)

Note that

III-B

Mismatched Estimation

The expected excess loss due to mismatch can be bounded as follows:

Theorem III.4

(Excess Loss Bound for Causal Estimation)
.

For a sequence
Z^{n}
and SPA
Q
, the expected difference in normalized loss is bounded by:

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}^{Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}}\left(Z_{t}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t}^{\text{opt}}\left(Z^{t}\right)\right)\right]\leq\sqrt{2}C_{1}(\Pi)\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)},

where
C_{1}(\Pi)=\left|\Pi^{-T}\right|_{\text{max}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{X}\right|

and
\left|\Pi^{-T}\right|_{\text{max}}
denotes the maximum component of
\left|\Pi^{-T}\right|
.
D(\cdot\|\cdot)
denotes the relative entropy (KL divergence).

The proof is based on noting that, for each
t
, the difference in losses between two cases – one using the estimator based on the ground truth sequential probability
P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}
and the other using its estimate
Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}
– can be bounded using a “single-letter” bound on their
\ell_{1}
distance (Lemma
A.2
). We then use Pinsker’s inequality to bound that distance with square root of the relative entropy, then use Jensen’s inequality to put the summation into the square root. Finally, we use the chain rule for relative entropy. This result provides a distribution-agnostic bound for the excess loss in any mismatched estimation scenario. The tightness of the bound is discussed in Section
IV-A
.

Corollary III.8
.

Assume
\mathrm{\Lambda}
is such that
\forall x\in\mathcal{A}_{X}

\exists\hat{x}\in\mathcal{A}_{\hat{X}}
with
\mathrm{\Lambda}(x,\hat{x})=0

For any individual sequence

and SPA

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(x_{t},\hat{X}_{B}^{Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}}\left(Z_{t}\right)\right)\right]\leq\sqrt{2}C_{1}(\Pi)\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}|x^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)},

where the expectation on the left side is with respect to the channel noise.

This corollary follows directly by specializing Theorem
III.4
to
P=\delta_{x^{n}}
and noting that in this case
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(x_{t},\hat{X}_{t}^{\text{opt}}\left(Z^{t}\right)\right)\right]=0
(thanks to the additional stipulation on

). This corollary is key in establishing the following:

Theorem III.9

(Mutual information upper bound on the optimal performance)
.

Assume
\mathrm{\Lambda}
is such that
\forall x\in\mathcal{A}_{X}

\exists\hat{x}\in\mathcal{A}_{\hat{X}}
with
\mathrm{\Lambda}(x,\hat{x})=0
. The expected normalized loss of the optimal filter satisfies:

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t}^{\text{opt}}\left(Z^{t}\right)\right)\right]\leq\sqrt{2}C_{1}(\Pi)\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}I(X^{n};Z^{n})}.

The bound in Theorem
III.9
may seem counter-intuitive at first glance since the less informative
Z^{n}
is about
X^{n}
(smaller mutual information) the worse (larger expected loss) one might expect the filtering performance to be. Note, however, that for a fixed distribution on the noise-free process

, the more noisy the channel (smaller mutual information) the larger the constant

will tend to be. For a fixed channel, lower mutual information may be due to lower entropy of

, and it would make intuitive sense that the smaller the entropy of

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t}^{\text{opt}}\left(Z^{t}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}^{P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}}\left(Z^{t}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}^{P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}}\left(Z^{t}\right)\right)\Big|X^{n}=x^{n}\right]P_{X^{n}}(x^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x^{n}}\sqrt{2}C_{1}(\Pi)\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}|x^{n}}\|P_{Z^{n}}\right)}P_{X^{n}}(x^{n})\qquad(\because\text{ Corollary \ref{cor:normalizedloss}})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}C_{1}(\Pi)\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x^{n}}D\left(P_{Z^{n}|x^{n}}\|P_{Z^{n}}\right)P_{X^{n}}(x^{n})}\qquad(\because\text{ Jensen's inequality})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}C_{1}(\Pi)\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}I(X^{n};Z^{n})}

∎

Theorem III.11

(Information-theoretic lower bounds on the Bayes optimal performances)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}_{X}=\mathcal{A}_{\hat{X}}=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
.
Also, suppose the loss function is subtractive, i.e.,
\mathrm{\Lambda}(x,x^{\prime})=\rho(x-x^{\prime})

\rho(0)=0

Then, for any fixed integer

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}^{opt}\left(Z^{t+l}\right)\right)\right]\geq\phi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{n}H(X^{n}\lVert Z^{n+l})\right),

where
\phi(\cdot)
is defined as

\phi(D)\triangleq\max\left\{H(U)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U\text{ random variable over }\mathcal{A}_{X}\text{ s.t. }\mathbb{E}[\rho(U)]\leq D\right\},

and

is the entropy of
X^{n}
causally conditioned on

H(X^{n}\lVert Z^{n+l})=\sum_{t=1}^{n}H(X_{t}|X^{t-1},Z^{t+l}).

\phi(D)

In this section, we present our experiments on implementing a universal filter induced by a universal SPA. Although universal SPAs can be accessed through a variety of established methods—including Context Tree Weighting (CTW)

To assess the performance of the universal filter, we apply it to a Markov process setting, allowing us to benchmark against established linear filtering techniques, as well as the optimum Bayes performance.
In particular, we compare our method’s performance with the Wiener filter, a traditional linear filtering technique, as well as with the theoretical optimum of non-linear filtering for this setting.

VI-A

LZ78 Compression Algorithm

In this work, we use LZ78 to create an SPA
Q_{Z_{t}|z^{t-1}}
, which is an estimate of
\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)
. LZ78 encoding, as described in
[
22
]
, is based on constructing a prefix tree. This tree is created by dividing a given sequence into a series of consecutive subsequences, referred to as phrases.

Definition VI.1

(LZ78 Tree)
.

The LZ78 tree constructs phrases from a sequence through the following procedure:

1.

The LZ78 tree begins with a single root node.

2.

Repeat the following until reaching the end of the sequence:

(a)

Begin at the root and traverse down the tree according to the upcoming symbols in the sequence until reaching a leaf node.

(b)

Add a new node extending from this leaf, representing the next symbol in the sequence.

(c)

Each LZ78 phrase is formed from the sequence portion used in the traversal, combined with the symbol of the new branch just created.

The number of nodes in the prefix tree, excluding the root, is equivalent to the number of phrases that have been parsed.

VI-B

Defining the LZ78 Tree of Sequential Probability Assignments

To address the limitations of naive empirical frequency-based SPAs, we leverage the LZ78 tree structure to create an adaptive SPA. The naive SPA, as defined in
[
23
]

q^{\text{naive}}\left(a|Z^{t-1}\right)=\frac{N_{Z_{t}}^{LZ}\left(a|Z^{t-1}\right)}{t-1}.

VI-C

Optimality of the LZ78 Family of SPAs

The LZ78 family of SPAs are optimal in the sense that, for any individual sequence, they achieve a limit supremum on log loss that matches or surpasses the performance of any finite-state SPA
[
23
]
. This universality result demonstrates their effectiveness in both individual sequence scenarios and stationary stochastic processes, where the expected log loss approaches the entropy rate of the source as the sequence length increases. Furthermore, for specific classes of sequences, LZ78 SPAs strictly outperform finite-state SPAs, underscoring their superiority in various prediction tasks. This optimality forms the basis for choosing LZ78 SPAs in our experiments.

VI-D

Modified LZ78 Tree Algorithm

In this section, we explain the modified universal filtering algorithm with LZ78 SPA.

VI-D
1
Input Shifting

To improve the efficiency of the LZ78 SPA tree given limited training data, we propose a modified training approach that involves input shifting. Instead of expanding the tree strictly based on each parsed phrase, we grow it by advancing the input by one symbol at a time and starting each traversal at the root. Specifically, after processing a sequence, the next training sequence begins with the input shifted by one symbol relative to the previous sequence. This approach ensures that each symbol sequence in the dataset contributes to the tree from multiple starting points.

By implementing this shifting mechanism, each sequence in the dataset has the opportunity to traverse the tree multiple times, starting from different positions. This repetition effectively increases the training frequency of symbol sequences, even under conditions of limited data. The result is a more robust and well-trained LZ78 SPA tree structure that better captures the probabilistic relationships inherent in the dataset.

The underlying assumption of this method is that the noise characteristics in the dataset are stationary, meaning they remain consistent across shifts. Under these conditions, the probability assignments for sequences are preserved across the multiple traversals introduced by shifting, enhancing the model’s overall predictive capabilities. Such an approach is also seen in
[
24
]
.

VI-D
2
LZ78 Tree Pruning

In the context of LZ78-based SPA, pruning infrequently traversed branches in the probability assignment tree effectively reduces model complexity while preserving predictive accuracy. By introducing a threshold parameter
N_{th}
, branches that do not meet a minimum traversal count are pruned, effectively excluding low-occurrence events that contribute less significantly to the overall probability estimation. This approach prevents overfitting to rare events and helps maintain an SPA that remains close to the true probability distribution. Moreover, pruning enhances computational efficiency by focusing on well-traversed branches, particularly in resource-constrained environments or applications demanding rapid sequential probability access. This methodology is crucial for practical implementations, allowing the SPA to achieve a balanced trade-off between prediction fidelity and computational tractability.

VI-I

Discussion

One of the central considerations for real-time applications in low-compute environments is the computational complexity of the employed model. Our proposed approach, based on the LZ78 SPA, demonstrates significant computational advantages over neural network-based methods. This efficiency makes it particularly well-suited for environments where resources are limited and latency is critical.

The efficiency of the LZ78-based predictor stems from its ability to compute the conditional probability distribution
Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-1}}
with
O(1)
complexity. While this is achieved through the inherent structure of the LZ78 tree, it is not restricted to this specific implementation. The filtered output relies on simple arithmetic operations (multiplications and divisions) and a summation over the finite set
\mathcal{A}_{X}
, the alphabet of possible symbols. As a result, the overall computational cost scales linearly with the size of the alphabet,
|\mathcal{A}_{X}|
, ensuring this approach remains efficient for real-time tasks.

In contrast, neural network-based predictors for the same denoising filter task involve significantly higher computation overhead. For filtering problems, neural network methods require at least
Q\left(n^{2}\right)
complexity
6
6

6

For the simplest form of a neural network, single-layer perceptron, in filtering problem, we have n inputs and n output neurons, the forward pass is of
O\left(n^{2}\right)
complexity.
.
Additionally, neural networks typically require substantial memory resources to store weights, activations, and intermediate computations. These resource demands often necessitate specialized hardware, such as GPUs, to meet real-time constraints. This reliance on computationally expensive resources makes neural network-based methods less practical for low-compute environments, particularly when simplicity and efficiency are critical.

VII
Conclusion

We proposed an approach to the construction of universal filtering schemes that can be used under arbitrary delay or lookahead. Given any universal SPA, we showed that the resulting schemes are universally optimal, with performance converging to that of the Bayes optimum as the length of the sequence grows. Furthermore, we implemented and experimented on simulated data with a family of such schemes when the driving universal SPA is induced by LZ78. The experimental results are consistent with the theoretical ones and bode well for applying such filters on real data.

Our focus here was on the stochastic setting, deferring the semi-stochastic setting, where the noise-free data comprises individual sequences, to future work.
Additionally, future work will explore broader settings where the noise is neither discrete

Proof of Lemma
II.1
.

For all
t\leq n
,

P_{X_{t}|Z^{t}=z^{t}}=F\left(P_{Z_{t}|z^{t-1}},\Pi,z_{t}\right),

Proof.

By Bayes’ theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x|Z^{t}=z^{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x,Z^{t}=z^{t}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{t}=z^{t}\right)}=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z_{t}|X_{t}=x,Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z_{t}|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z_{t}|X_{t}=x,Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=x|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z_{t}|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}.

As the system mapping

to

is a discrete memoryless channel,

In matrix-vector form,

\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}|Z^{t}}\left(\cdot|Z^{t}=z^{t}\right)=\frac{\Pi\left(\cdot,z_{t}\right)\odot\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}|Z^{t-1}}\left(\cdot|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z_{t}|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}.

Putting it all together,

\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}|Z^{t}}\left(\cdot|Z^{t}=z^{t}\right)=\frac{\Pi\left(\cdot,z_{t}\right)\odot\left(\Pi^{-\top}P_{Z_{t}|z^{t-1}}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{t}=z_{t}|Z^{t-1}=z^{t-1}\right)}=F\left(P_{Z_{t}|z^{t-1}},\Pi,z_{t}\right).

.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t}^{\text{opt}}\left(Z^{t-d}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}\left(C_{2}(\Pi)\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)}+C_{3}(\Pi)\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\right),

where
C_{2}(\Pi)
and
C_{3}(\Pi)
are constants that depend only on
\Pi
, defined as follows.

C_{2}(\Pi)=3\sqrt{2}\left|\Pi^{-T}\right|_{\text{max}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{X}\right|

C_{3}(\Pi)=\left|\Pi^{-T}\right|_{\text{max}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{X}\right|2^{|\mathcal{A}_{Z}|}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}

Proof.

\displaystyle LHS=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\right]

We can get the bound of the second term by Theorem
III.6
as follows.

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)}

For the first term,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}\right)\right)-\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\Big|Z^{t-d},M\text{realizations}\right]\Big|Z^{t-d}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\left(\left\|\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}-P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}-P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right\|_{1}\right)\Big|Z^{t-d}\right]\right]\qquad(\because\text{Lemma\penalty\ \ref{lem:loss_bound_arbitrary}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}-Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right\|_{1}+2\left\|Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}-P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right\|_{1}\Big|Z^{t-d}\right]\right]\qquad(\because\text{triangle inequality})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}-Q_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right\|_{1}\Big|Z^{t-d}\right]\right]+2\sqrt{2}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)}\qquad(\because\text{Theorem\penalty\ \ref{thm:normalizedloss_delay}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[2^{|\mathcal{A}_{Z}|}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\right]+2\sqrt{2}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)}\qquad(\because\text{Lemma\penalty\ \ref{lem:empirical_distr_bound}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)2^{|\mathcal{A}_{Z}|}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}+2\sqrt{2}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)}

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}\hat{Q}_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}^{M}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathrm{\Lambda}\left(X_{t},\hat{X}_{B}\left(\Pi^{-T}P_{Z_{t}|Z^{t-d}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)2^{|\mathcal{A}_{Z}|}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}+3\sqrt{2}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\text{max}}C_{1}(\Pi)\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}D\left(P_{Z^{n}}\|Q_{Z^{n}}\right)}

∎

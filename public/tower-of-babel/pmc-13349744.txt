Honghe Fujie lotion ameliorates pregnancy-associated vulvovaginal candidiasis in mice by inhibiting the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome
Authors: Ying Wang, Yiqun Jiang, Simao Shi, Yu Wang, Jiyu Yang, Hongying Kuang, Xiaoling Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vulvovaginal candidiasis, a common infection of the female reproductive tract, poses heightened risks when occurring during pregnancy. Although Honghe Fujie lotion (HFL), a traditional Chinese medicine, is clinically used for mycotic vaginitis, its therapeutic efficacy and mechanisms in pregnancy-associated vulvovaginal candidiasis (pVVC) remain unclear. In this study, HPLC-MS was used to analyze and identify the compounds in HFL. This study investigated HFL’s effects through in vitro and in vivo approaches. A murine model of vaginal Candida albicans (C. albicans) infection was established to evaluate the therapeutic effect of HFL on pVVC. Vaginal fungal load was quantified by analyzing vaginal lavage fluid, while the impact of HFL on adverse pregnancy outcomes was assessed through placental developmental analysis. Furthermore, the effects of HFL on pVVC-induced vaginitis and NLRP3 inflammasome activation were examined using immunofluorescence, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and western blot. The results included: (1) High performance liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS) analysis identified 545 chemical constituents in HFL. (2) HFL significantly inhibited C. albicans growth and biofilm metabolic activity in vitro; (3) in a murine pVVC model, HFL reduced vaginal fungal load and mitigated adverse pregnancy outcomes, while also restoring trophoblast cell distribution in placental tissues; (4) mechanistically, HFL suppressed NLRP3 inflammasome activation, leading to Caspase-1 activity downregulation and subsequent impairment of pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion. These findings demonstrate that HFL alleviates pVVC by targeting the NLRP3 signaling pathway, providing a preclinical foundation for its therapeutic application in pregnancy-associated VVC.

Introduction

Vulvovaginal candidiasis (VVC) is a common fungal infection characterized by genital itching, redness, burning, difficulty in sexual intercourse, and vaginal discharge resembling cheese curds [1]. Vulvovaginitis caused by Candida is the most common infection in women, and the incidence rate increases during pregnancy [2]. Although pregnancy is a physiological state, it is associated with heightened susceptibility to infections, and accumulating evidence indicates that VVC correlates with an elevated risk of pregnancy-related complications [3]. Epidemiologic studies have shown that Candida albicans (C. albicans) is isolated from the vagina of 20% of healthy non-pregnant women, and this percentage rises to 30% during pregnancy [4, 5].

Various susceptibility factors are molecular drivers of altered vaginal morphology, mycelial extension, adhesion, invasion, and biofilm formation, leading to chronic infection and recurrence [6]. Although there are other non-Candida albicans species that have been identified as pathogenic factors in VVC, C. albicans is the factor in more than 90% of VVC cases [7]. C. albicans is a commensal human fungus that colonizes mucosal surfaces, including gastrointestinal tract and vagina [8]. It has been found that the emergence and recurrence of VVC is closely related to the inflammation caused by C. albicans. Upon activation, NLRP3 inflammasome triggers caspase-1 cleavage, which in turn promotes the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18. While essential for pathogen clearance, excessive inflammasome activation can lead to inflammatory tissue damage. Crucially, inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation has been shown to effectively alleviate VVC symptoms [9]. Views suggest that VVC is mediated through innate immune responses, rather than the past belief that VVC is the result of adaptive immune responses [10]. Immunological and pharmacological approaches have identified NLRP3 inflammasome as a key molecular factor promoting host immunopathology [11]. As a result, the NLRP3 inflammasome has emerged as a novel target for VVC therapy [12]. The therapeutic effect and mechanism of pregnancy-associated VVC (pVVC) remains unspecified and needs to be further explored.

In the last decade, new antifungal drugs have been developed. The major antifungal drugs are azoles, polyenes, antimetabolites, and echinocandins, each with different mechanisms [13]. Antifungals are not only resistant [14], but are also associated with pregnancy risk [15]. The prescription of antifungal drugs for pregnant women still faces challenges. There is a lack of reliable research on the optimal antifungal regimen and dosage during pregnancy.

Traditional Chinese medicines offer advantages such as favorable efficacy and minimal adverse effects, which may enhance patient comfort and treatment adherence. Therefore, Chinese medicines are widely used in treating various diseases, including VVC [16]. Honghe Fujie lotion (HFL) is a topical lotion refined by dry distillation of hawthorn kernel. Its function mainly lies in detoxifying and expelling dampness, killing insects and relieving itching. Hawthorn, as a food and herbal medicine, has been shown to have many bioactive properties, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial properties [17]. HFL was clinically used in treating mycotic vaginitis and non-specific vaginitis. HFL has a certain effect on inhibiting the growth of C. albicans. However, its therapeutic effect and related mechanisms on pVVC are not clear. In the present study, we investigated the anti-pVVC activity of HFL and elucidated its mechanism of action in a murine model of pVVC, with a particular focus on its modulation of the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway.

Materials and Methods

Preparation of HFL

HFL is prepared from the dried seeds of Crataegus pinnatifida (hawthorn), a member of the Rosaceae family. The seeds, harvested from mature hawthorn fruits, are dried and characterized by an elliptical or oval shape, with a light yellow to yellow-brown surface. The back is slightly convex, and the two flat sides may feature minor indentations. The seeds are hard, durable, and emit a faint odor.

For the extraction, the seeds undergo dry distillation, where the temperature is raised to 100–120°C for 30 min to eliminate moisture. The distillation continues, discarding fractions below 150°C. The distillate collected between 150–270°C is retained, mixed with Polysorbate 80, and homogenized. Water is added, and the mixture is portioned and sealed to yield the final product.

The raw material is supplied by Shandong Buchang Shenzhou Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Heze, China), with production carried out in specialized workshops for pretreatment, distillation, and formulation of external-use products. The sample number for the lotion used in this study is WS3-056 (Z-010)-2001 (Z)-2021.

C. albicans strains and culture

In this study, C. albicans CMCC(F)98001 (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was inoculated into culture dishes containing yeast extract-peptone-dextrose (YPD) solid medium (1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, 2% dextrose, and 2% agar) by the scribing method and incubated at 35°C overnight. Single colonies of approximately 1 mm diameter were picked and inoculated in YPD liquid medium at 35°C with constant shaking (200 rpm) for 16 h. The cells were harvested by centrifugation and counted after dilution.

Antifungal susceptibility assay

An antifungal susceptibility test was carried out in 96-well tissue culture plates. HFL was diluted with RPMI-1640 medium (Solarbio) containing 165 mM 3-(N-Morpholino) propane sulfonic acid (MOPS, Aladdin, Shanghai, China). The initial concentration of C. albicans in RPMI 1640 medium was 2.0 × 103 Colony-Forming Units (CFU)/ml. The fungal solution was incubated at 35°C for 48 h. The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of HFL was determined by measuring the optical density (OD) value at 600 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA). MIC was defined as the minimum concentration of the drug that inhibits the growth of 80% of the fungus. The inhibition rate was calculated as (ODcontrol-ODHFL)/ODcontrol.

Time-kill assay

Time-kill assay was performed in RPMI 1640 medium containing MOPS. The initial concentration of C. albicans in RPMI 1640 medium was 8.0 × 104 CFU/ml. The final concentration of HFL was 24.38, 48.75, and 97.50 mg/ml, respectively. Colonies were counted after incubating for 48 h.

XTT reduction assay

In order to quantify the adhesion of C. albicans, 2,3-Bis-(2-methoxy-4-nitro-5-sulfophenyl) −2H-tetrazolium-5-carboxanilide (XTT, Yuanye Bio, Shanghai, China) reduction experiments were performed. C. albicans solution was incubated in 96-well plates containing HFL at 35°C for 24 h. The initial concentration was 2.0 × 105 CFU/ml. The OD value at 490 nm was measured using a microplate reader.

Calcofluor white staining assay

Cell wall formation was further examined using potassium hydroxide (KOH, Aladdin) and calcofluor white (CFW, Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) staining. C. albicans was cultured in 96-well plates containing HFL at 35°C for 24 h. The initial concentration of C. albicans was 1.0 × 108 CFU/ml. C. albicans were stained with 10% KOH and 0.1% CFW. Fluorescence was detected at 300–440 nm.

Animal experiment

Specific pathogen-free (SPF) male and female BALB/c mice (6–8 weeks old, Jiangsu Huachuang Xinnuo Pharmaceutical Technology, Jiangsu, China) were acclimated for one week under standardized housing conditions (12 h light/12 h dark cycle, temperature 22 ± 1°C, humidity 45–55%) with ad libitum access to food and water. Male and female mice were housed together overnight at a ratio of 1:2, and the detection of a vaginal plug the following morning was recorded as embryonic day 0.5 (E0.5). Pregnant mice were then randomly assigned to four groups: control, pVVC, pVVC + 48.75 mg/ml HFL, and pVVC + 97.50 mg/ml HFL. At E4.5, corresponding to the blastocyst deposition period (E4.0–E4.5), mice received an intravaginal inoculation of C. albicans (20 µl, approximately 2.0 × 107 CFU) or an equivalent volume of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). HFL was administered once daily via vaginal instillation from E5.5 to E7.5.

To exclude the effects of HFL on normal pregnancy, 6–8-week-old BALB/c mice were acclimated for one week. Males and females were housed together overnight at a 1:2 ratio, and the presence of a vaginal plug the following morning was recorded as E0.5. Females with a visible vaginal plug were divided into two groups: Pregnant and Pregnant + 24.38 mg/ml HFL. At E4.5, 20 µl of PBS was administered intravaginally. From E5.5 to E7.5, 30 µl of HFL was administered intravaginally once daily. Body weight was recorded daily from E4.5. Vaginal lavage samples were collected at E8.5, E11.5, and E18.5. On E18.5, animals were euthanized, and embryos and placentas were dissected, photographed, and weighed.

For procedures requiring anesthesia, mice were anesthetized with inhaled isoflurane using 3% for induction and 1.5% for maintenance. Euthanasia was performed by CO2 inhalation in accordance with ethical guidelines. The mice were placed in a CO2 chamber without overcrowding, and CO2 was administered at a flow rate of 10% to 30% of the chamber volume per min until loss of consciousness and lack of responsiveness were observed. The flow rate was then increased, not exceeding 0.5 kPa, until respiration ceased and pupils dilated. CO2 was turned off, and the mice were observed for an additional two minutes to ensure death, which was further confirmed by the absence of breathing and a lack of response to a toe pinch. After euthanasia, a rapid cesarean section was performed to collect vaginal tissues, fetuses, and placentas. All animal experiments in this study were conducted in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Heilongjiang University of Chinese Medicine (Ethical Approval Number: 2024091301).

Colony counts

Lavage ﬂuid from the vaginal lumen was obtained by injecting 70 µl of sterile PBS. A 1:10 dilution of the vaginal lavage ﬂuid was made and coated on YPD solid medium. Colony was counted (CFU/ml) after 72 h of incubation at 35°C.

Histopathology

Fixed vaginal epithelial and placental tissues were embedded. Vaginal tissues were paraffin sectioned longitudinally to obtain sections of 5 µm thickness. Sections were dewaxed to be used for Periodic Acid Schiff (PAS) staining, TUNEL staining, and IF staining. Paraffin sections of 5 µm thickness were cut from the placental tissue and transferred to slides. Sections were dewaxed and stained with hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining. Sections were photographed using a microscope for morphological evaluation.

PAS staining was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Leagene, Beijing, China). Slices were covered with periodate solution for 10 min, followed by immersion in Schiff staining solution for 15 min. The slices were sequentially counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, washed, mounted and photographed.

TUNEL assay was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions by labeling paraffin sections with In Situ Cell Death Detection Kit (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) at 37°C for 1 h. Sections were photographed using a microscope.

Paraffin sections were placed in antigen repair solution at high temperature and low fire for 10 min. Sections were blocked with 1% BSA for 15 min, and then subjected to IF staining. Primary antibody NLRP3 (1:100, Affinity, Changzhou, China) and ASC (1:100, ABclonal, Wuhan, China) were applied, followed by incubation with secondary antibody goat anti-rabbit IgG (CY3) (1:200, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) and Goat anti-Rabbit IgG (Heavy chain) SuperclonalTM Secondary Antibody, Alexa Fluor™ 555 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Sections were stained with DAPI (Aladdin) in the dark before capturing images.

Tissue sections were immersed in hematoxylin (Solarbio) for H&E staining. Sections were kept in eosin staining solution (Sangon, Shanghai, China) for 3 min. The sections were sealed and observed under a microscope.

To ensure objectivity and minimize bias in the histopathological scoring, a blinding strategy was employed during the assessment. Specifically, the histological scoring of tissue sections was performed independently by a researcher who was blinded to the experimental groups. This researcher had no knowledge of the group assignments. All scoring results were subsequently reviewed by another experienced evaluator to further confirm their accuracy and consistency.

Western blot analysis

Tissues were homogenized in a mixture of radio immunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (Solarbio) and proteins were extracted. The protein content was determined by BCA protein concentration assay kit (Solarbio). Proteins were denatured by boiling in 5× loading buffer. The collected proteins were separated by electrophoresis using sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel (SDS-PAGE, Solarbio). Proteins were transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) membranes. The membrane was blocked with membrane blocking solution at room temperature for 1 h. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies Bax (1:1,000, ABclonal), Bcl-2 (1:1,000, Zenbio, Chengdu, China), GSDMD (1:1,000, Affinity), Caspase-1 (1:1,000, Affinity) and GAPDH (1:1,000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) at 4°C overnight. The membranes were incubated with ECL luminescent solution and exposed in the dark. The relative protein levels were analyzed and normalized to GAPDH, which was the internal control.

ELISA and kits assay

The levels of tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, IL-1β, and IL-18 in mouse vaginal lavage fluid were analyzed using the appropriate ELISA kits (Lianke Bio, Hangzhou, China) according to the instructions and study reports. Caspase-1 activity assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Cell culture and treatment

The RAW264.7 macrophage cell line was cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, at 37°C in a 5% CO2 humidified incubator. For pre-treatment, the cells were incubated with 24.38 mg/ml HFL for 24 h. To induce pyroptosis, cells were subsequently exposed to 500 ng/ml lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 4 h, followed by stimulation with 5 mM ATP for 30 min. After the treatments, cells were collected for subsequent analysis.

Immunofluorescence staining

RAW264.7 cells were cultured on coverslips and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, followed by permeabilization with 0.1% Triton X-100 and blocking with 1% BSA. For immunostaining, cells were incubated with primary antibody ASC (1:200, ABclonal) overnight at 4°C, then with secondary antibody Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (FITC) (1:200, Abcam) for 1 h at room temperature, protected from light. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and cells were mounted with antifade reagent.

Statistical analysis

Data are shown as mean ± SD. Student’s t-test was used to analyze significant differences between the two groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to analyze differences between multiple groups, followed by Tukey’s post hoc multiple-comparison test to evaluate differences between individual groups. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prime.

Results

Compounds analysis of HFL

To characterize its chemical composition, HFL was analyzed using high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS), a standard tool for qualitative and quantitative analysis in drug development [18]. Untargeted metabolomic profiling identified 545 metabolites in HFL, encompassing diverse chemical classes such as phenols, aldehydes, ketones, and esters. Representative chromatograms acquired in both positive and negative ionization modes exhibited well-resolved, high-resolution peaks, reflecting the substantial chemical complexity of this botanical formulation (Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1).

In the identified compounds, we found several consistently active antimicrobial components, including the common phenolic acids Vanillic acid [19] and Ferulic acid [20], both of which exhibited significant antimicrobial activity. Furthermore, existing literature indicates that 5-Hydroxymethylfurfural [17], methyl eugenol [21], and theobromine [22] also possess strong antibacterial effects. These findings provide a solid theoretical foundation for selecting HFL as a potential treatment for the disease.

As ASC speck formation is a recognized hallmark of inflammasome activation, these findings further support enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome-dependent pyroptosis in pVVC mice.

Discussion

VVC is a common genital fungal infection reflecting abnormal growth of Candida species in the mucosa [24]. Inflammation caused by C. albicans is considered the second most common factor among the many causes of vaginitis [25]. The uncomplicated VVC includes mild to moderate clinical signs and symptoms such as tofu-like discharge, pain, and dysuria [26]. According to the literature, the incidence of vaginal colonization with Candida species in pregnant women is approximately 11.3% [27]. The accompanying incidence of invasive neonatal candidiasis through maternal contamination of their babies is higher [28, 29]. The clinical evidence suggests that candidiasis is associated with an increased risk of complications during pregnancy [30]. Despite many antibacterial drugs having been developed, oral antimicrobials also increase the risk of complications during pregnancy [31]. The azoles provide the most effective treatment but are not recommended during pregnancy [32]. Therefore, there is an urgent and unmet clinical need to identify safe and effective alternative therapies for the management of pVVC.

C. albicans is the most common trigger of vaginitis in pregnancy. Vaginal C. albicans infections significantly increase miscarriages rates in mice [33]. More research has confirmed that pregnant women with VVC experience adverse pregnancy outcomes such as preterm labor, miscarriage, and neonatal infections [34, 35]. This study established an animal model of vaginal infection caused by C. albicans in pregnant mice and evaluated the therapeutic effect of HFL on pVVC. Given the central role of the placenta in both maternal health and fetal development [36], we sought to investigate whether HFL exerts its protective effects by mitigating placental damage. The placenta is primarily composed of trophoblast cells. In mice, trophoblast ectodermal cells form a cap-like structure known as the ectoplacental cone (EPC) at embryonic day (E) 8–9. By approximately E11.5, the placenta undergoes initial maturation and becomes organized into three distinct layers: the spongiotrophoblast (Spo), labyrinth (Lab), and maternal decidua. At late pregnancy (E18.5), the placenta is fully mature [33]. Therefore, we chose E8.5, E11.5 and E18.5 to explore the effect of HFL on pVVC. We reported that HFL treatment led to improvements in the overall condition. C. albicans infection is a significant cause of fetal growth retardation, and HFL treatment improved these conditions.

Traditional Chinese medicine, as a widely used drug for treating VVC, has the advantage of safety. A recent study found that Longdan Xiegan decoction eliminates the vaginal mycelia infestation in mice [37]. Pulsatilla decoction is considered a potential drug for treating VVC [38]. Kaifan Hu et al. further reported that extract from Pulsatilla decoction protects against VVC by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome [39]. This study discussed the therapeutic effect of VVC in mice during pregnancy. However, its underlying mechanism is still unclear. We report that symptoms of pVVC mice were relieved after HFL treatment.

NLR proteins are important intracellular immune receptors. The activation of NLRP3 is a two-step process, comprising an initiation step and an activation step. First, the expression of NLRP3 is upregulated through transcription of NF-κB or other transcription factors. Next, a variety of microbial and bacterial species stimulate potassium efflux, resulting in the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome [40, 41]. The assembly of NLRP3 inflammasome leads to the activation of caspase-1, which induces the maturation and release of IL-1β and IL-18 [16, 42]. NLRP3, as an important sensor of the innate immune system, has been confirmed to be involved in VVC development. C. albicans is able to activate the assembly of NLRP3 inflammasome in vaginal epithelial tissue after invading the organism. Research has shown that the immune mechanism of VVC is the overactivation of NLRP3 inflammasome by virulence factors from C. albicans [11]. Herein, HFL effectively reduced the C. albicans burden. The active ingredients of HFL need to be further explored. In addition, the study of downstream targets related to NLRP3 inflammasome is crucial for future research.

The overall effect of HFL appears to stem from the synergistic action of its multiple components. Specifically, compounds such as Vanillic acid [43], Ferulic acid [44], 4-Hydroxybenzoic acid [45], Syringic acid [46], and Cinnamaldehyde [47] are likely to exert a synergistic inhibitory effect on the NLRP3 pathway. This mechanism directly addresses the open question of which components might contribute to the therapeutic effects of HFL, further supporting its potential in treating the disease.

However, this study has certain limitations. This study did not include a positive control group with clinically common antifungal agents like fluconazole, which may limit the clinical comparability of our results. Future studies incorporating such positive controls to directly compare the therapeutic effects of HFL with standard antifungal treatments would be valuable for further validating both its efficacy and safety. Moreover, previous studies have reported that commonly used antifungal drugs, including fluconazole, exhibit limited inhibitory effects on Candida infections, particularly in the context of emerging resistant strains [2]. We acknowledge that the present study does not include genetic ablation models. Further validation using NLRP3 knockout mice would be invaluable to definitively establish the causative role of this inflammasome in the therapeutic efficacy of HFL, and such studies are warranted in the future. Therefore, our findings not only provide experimental evidence supporting HFL as a potential therapeutic option but also highlight the clinical challenges posed by antifungal resistance.

In summary, this study illustrates the promising effect of HFL on pVVC treatment in vivo and in vitro. The underlying mechanism is associated with the inhibition of NLRP3 signaling pathway. Our study establishes a foundation and provides convincing evidence for treating pVVC with HFL.

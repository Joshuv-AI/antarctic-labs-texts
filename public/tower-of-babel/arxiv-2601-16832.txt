Solutions and singularities of the Ricci-harmonic flow and Ricci-like flows of G 2 \mathrm{G_{2}} -structures
Shubham Dwivedi & Ragini Singhal

Abstract
We find explicit solutions and singularities of the Ricci-harmonic flow of G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures, the Ricci-like flows of G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures studied in [ 9 ] and of the negative gradient flow of an energy functional of G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures, on 7 7 -dimensional contact Calabi-Yau manifolds and the 7 7 -dimensional Heisenberg group. We prove that the natural co-closed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structure on a contact Calabi-Yau manifold as the initial condition leads to an ancient solution of the Ricci-harmonic flow with a finite time Type I singularity, and it gives an immortal solution to the Ricci-like flows with an infinite time singularity which are Type III if the transversal Calabi-Yau distribution is flat, and Type IIb otherwise. The same ansatz give ancient solution to the negative gradient flow of G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures. These are the first examples of Type I singularities of the Ricci-harmonic flow and Type IIb and Type III singularities of the Ricci-like flows. We also obtain similar solutions for all the three flows on the 7 7 -dimensional Heisenberg group.

1.  Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to study explicit solutions and their behaviours for some general flows of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Ever since the introduction of the Ricci flow to study deformation of Riemannian metrics by Hamilton

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometry and one of their far-reaching goals is that they would provide a method to search for metrics with holonomy
\mathrm{G}_{2}

7

\varphi(t)
of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(t)
-structures, given by non-degenerate
3
-forms
\varphi(t)
on
M
, with the hope that that it becomes torsion-free in appropriate limits. There have been different such flows in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometry starting with the Laplacian flow of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures by Bryant
[
2
]
. See
[
3
,
18
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

for other flows of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

Recall that if
M
is a
7
-dimensional smooth, oriented and spin manifold then a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
on
M
is a
3
-form which is nondegenerate in the sense that it determines a metric
g_{\varphi}
and an orientation
\vol_{\varphi}
.
The
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is called
torsion-free
if
\nabla^{g_{\varphi}}\varphi=0
, where
\nabla^{g_{\varphi}}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g_{\varphi}
. Since
g
is obtained from
\varphi
in a nonlinear way, the equation for
\varphi
being torsion-free is a nonlinear PDE and as a result, finding torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures with the metrics having full holonomy
\mathrm{G}_{2}
is a challenging problem. The problem becomes considerably more difficult when
M
is required to be compact.

The flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures we study in this paper is the Ricci-harmonic flow which was introduced by the first author in
[
6
]
. Let
\varphi(t)
be a family of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on a compact manifold
M^{7}
with some
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi_{0}
. If
T
denotes the torsion of the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
,
\mathrm{Ric}
is the Ricci curvature of the underlying metric,
\psi=*_{\varphi}\varphi
and
\diamond
is the operation described in (
2.2
), then Ricci-harmonic flow is the initial value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\left(-\mathrm{Ric}+3T^{t}T-|T|^{2}g\right)\diamond\varphi+\Div T\lrcorner\psi,\\
&\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\Div T
is the divergence of the torsion which is a
1
-form (or a vector field) and is explicitly given in coordinates as
(\Div T)_{i}=\nabla^{j}T_{ji}
. The flow in (
1
) is a general flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures in the sense that we do not put any conditions on either the initial or evolving
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. This flow was obtained by looking at the Taylor series expansion of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
in normal coordinates and then computing the "Laplacian of the components of
\varphi
" by looking at the second order terms in the expansion. This is a procedure similar to obtaining the Ricci flow of metrics. As such, (
1
) can be viewed as the heat flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. It is also a natural coupling of the Ricci flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures and the
isometric/harmonic
flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures studied in
[
11
,
4
,
19
]
. It was shown in
[
6
, §3]
that on compact manifolds, the stationary points of (
1
) are precisely torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. The Ricci-harmonic flow was studied in detail by the first author in
[
6
]
. It was proved that a unique solution to (
1
) exists for a short time on a compact manifold starting with any arbitrary
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. Among other results, the following theorem was proved in
[
6
, Thm. 5.1]
concerning the maximal existence time of the Ricci-harmonic flow on closed
7
-manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varphi(t)
be a solution of the Ricci-harmonic flow on a closed
7
-manifold
M
on a maximal time interval
[0,\tau)
. We define the following quantity along the flow.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)=\underset{M}{\text{sup}}\ \Lambda(x,t)\ \ \text{where}\ \ \Lambda(x,t)=\left(|\mathrm{Rm}(x,t)|^{2}+|\nabla T(x,t)|^{2}+|T(x,t)|^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\nearrow\tau}\Lambda(t)=\infty.

Moreover, the quantity
\Lambda(t)
blows-up at the following rate,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)\geq\dfrac{C}{\tau-t},

where
C>0
is a constant.

Thus, the Ricci-harmonic flow exists on a closed manifold as long as
\Lambda(t)
remains bounded. Using the blow-up rate on
\Lambda
in (
1.4
), the type of singularities of the Ricci-harmonic flow can be classified. We recall the following definition from
[
6
, Defn. 5.3]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose that
(M^{7},\varphi(t))
is a solution of the Ricci-harmonic flow of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on a closed manifold on a maximal time interval
[0,\tau)
and let
\Lambda(t)
be as in (
1.2
).

If we have a finite-time singularity, i.e.
\tau<\infty
, we say that the solution forms

1.

a
Type I singularity
(rapidly forming) if
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau)}(\tau-t)\Lambda(t)<\infty
; and otherwise

2.

a
Type IIa singularity
(slowly forming) if
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau)}(\tau-t)\Lambda(t)=\infty
.

If we have an
infinite-time
singularity, where
\tau=\infty
, then it is

1.

a
Type IIb singularity
(slowly forming) if
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}t\Lambda(t)=\infty
; and otherwise

2.

a
Type III singularity
(rapidly forming) if
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}t\Lambda(t)<\infty
.

As with any geometric flow, its important to have solutions of the flow as well as examples of singularities of any of the type described in

1.2

2.
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometry and contact Calabi-Yau manifolds

2.1
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometry

Let
M^{7}
be an oriented smooth manifold. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
M
is the reduction of the structure group of the frame bundle
Fr(M)
from the Lie group
\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})
to the Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
. Equivalently, a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is given by a
3
-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
which is nondegenerate, which means that it determines a Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
and an orientation which are given by the formula

(2.1)

\displaystyle(X\lrcorner\varphi)\wedge(Y\lrcorner\varphi)\wedge\varphi=6g_{\varphi}(X,Y)\vol_{\varphi},\ \ \ X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TM).

The metric and orientation determines the Hodge star operator and we denote by
\psi=*_{\varphi}\varphi
. The space of differential forms decompose further into irreducible
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-representation. We have the following orthogonal decomposition with respect to
g
,

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}=\Omega^{2}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{14}\ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \Omega^{3}=\Omega^{3}_{1}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{27},

where
\Omega^{k}_{l}
has pointwise dimension
l
. Let
\gamma\in\Omega^{k}
. Given any
2
-tensor
A
on
M
, we define

(2.2)

\displaystyle(A\diamond\gamma)_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}=A_{i_{1}}^{\,\,p}\gamma_{pi_{2}\cdots i_{k}}+A_{i_{2}}^{\,\,p}\gamma_{i_{1}pi_{3}\cdots i_{k}}+\cdots+A_{i_{k}}^{\,\,p}\gamma_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k-1}p},

where we are raising the indices using the underlying metric. So, for instance,
g\diamond\gamma=k\gamma
. The operation
\diamond
is the infinitesimal action of the group
\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})
on the space of differential forms. This operation is the same as the map
i_{\varphi}
in
[
2
]
(up to a factor of
\frac{1}{2}
) and we will use them interchangeably. If
\mathcal{T}^{2}
denote the space of
2
-tensors, then we get a linear map

\displaystyle\diamond:\mathcal{T}^{2}\rightarrow\Omega^{3}.

Recall that
\mathcal{T}^{2}=C^{\infty}(M)g\oplus S^{2}_{0}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{14}
, where
S^{2}_{0}
denote the space of symmetric traceless
2
-tensors. It’s easy to prove that
\ker(\diamond)\cong\Omega^{2}_{14}
and we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(M)\cong\Omega^{3}_{1},\ \ \ \ \ \Omega^{2}_{7}\cong\Omega^{3}_{7}\cong\Omega^{1},\ \ \ \ \ \ S^{2}_{0}\cong\Omega^{3}_{27}.

As a result, any
3
-form
\sigma\in\Omega^{3}(M)
can be described by a pair
(h,X)
where
h
is a symmetric
2
-tensor and
X
a vector field on
M
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma=h\diamond\varphi+X\lrcorner\psi.

The torsion of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is a
2
-tensor
T
and is given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{m}\varphi_{ijk}=T_{m}^{\ p}\psi_{pijk}.

Since
T
is a
2
-tensor, we can decompose it further into
intrinsic torsion forms
. These intrinsic torsion forms can be described by looking at the expressions of
d\varphi
and
d\psi
(see
[
2
]
or
[
15
]
for more details),

(2.4)

\displaystyle d\varphi

\displaystyle=\tau_{0}\psi+3\tau_{1}\wedge\varphi+*\tau_{3},

(2.5)

\displaystyle d\psi

\displaystyle=4\tau_{1}\wedge\psi+\tau_{2}\wedge\varphi,

where
\tau_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M),\ \tau_{1}\in\Omega^{2}_{7},\ \tau\in\Omega^{2}_{14}
and
\tau_{3}\in\Omega^{3}_{27}
. We have an isomorphism between the space
S^{2}
of traceless symmetric
2
-tensors and
\Omega^{3}_{1\oplus 27}
which is given by a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-specific map
j_{\varphi}:\Omega^{3}\rightarrow S^{2}
which was first described by Bryant
[
2
]
and is given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle j_{\varphi}(\sigma)(X,Y)=*\left((X\lrcorner\varphi)\wedge(Y\lrcorner\varphi)\wedge\sigma\right),

where
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. We used
j
to view
\tau_{3}
as either a traceless symmetric
2
-tensor or an element of
\Omega^{3}_{27}
depending on the context. The decomposition of
T
in terms of the intrinsic torsion forms is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle T_{ij}=\dfrac{\tau_{0}}{4}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}j_{\varphi}(\tau_{3})_{ij}-(\tau_{1}\lrcorner\varphi)_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}({\tau_{2}}_{ij}).

We also have the isomorphism
\Omega^{1}(M)\cong\Omega^{2}_{7}
so we can view
\tau_{1}
as a
1
-form or a
2
-form in
\Omega^{2}_{7}
and explicitly
(\tau_{1})_{ij}=(\tau_{1})^{l}\varphi_{lij}
. We also denote the
7
dimensional component of
T
by
\mathsf{V}{T}
with
(\mathsf{V}{T})_{k}=T^{ij}\varphi_{ijk}
.

By the expression of
d\psi
above, a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is co-closed if and only if
\tau_{1}=0
and
\tau_{2}=0
. Hence, the full torsion tensor of a co-closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is a symmetric
2
-tensor

(2.8)

T=\frac{\tau_{0}}{4}g-\frac{1}{4}j_{\varphi}(\tau_{3})\in S^{2}.

The covariant derivative of the torsion of
\varphi
and the Riemann curvature tensor of the underlying metric are both second order in
\varphi
and they are related and we can write the expression of the Ricci tensor of the metric
g_{\varphi}
in terms of the intrinsic torsions. Since we will need to compute the Ricci tensor of our families of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures for looking at the flows (
1
) and (
1.6
), we describe Bryant’s method of computing the Ricci tensor below, see
[
2
, eq. (4.30)]
. Define a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-equivariant pairing
Q:\Omega^{3}\times\Omega^{3}\rightarrow\Omega^{3}
as follows. Let
\alpha,\ \beta\in\Omega^{3}
and define

(2.9)

\displaystyle Q(\alpha,\beta)=*_{\varphi}\left((*\varphi)_{ijkl}(e_{j}\lrcorner e_{i}\lrcorner*_{\varphi}\alpha)\wedge(e_{l}\lrcorner e_{k}\lrcorner*\beta)\right).

Using this, we can write the Ricci tensor of the
g_{\varphi}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{3}{2}d^{*}\tau_{1}-\frac{3}{8}\tau_{0}^{2}+15|\tau_{1}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\tau_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\tau_{3}|^{2}\right)g

\displaystyle\quad+j_{\varphi}\left(-\frac{5}{4}d(*(\tau_{1}\wedge\psi))-\frac{1}{4}d\tau_{2}+\frac{1}{4}*d\tau_{3}+\frac{5}{2}\tau_{1}\wedge*(\tau_{1}\wedge\psi)-\frac{1}{8}\tau_{0}\tau_{3}+\frac{1}{4}\tau_{1}\wedge\tau_{2}+\frac{3}{4}*(\tau_{1}\wedge\tau_{3})\right.

(2.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\frac{1}{8}*(\tau_{2}\wedge\tau_{2})+\frac{1}{64}Q(\tau_{3},\tau_{3})\right).

The formula in (

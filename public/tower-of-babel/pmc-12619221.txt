The Prevention of Developmental Delay and Xylitol (PDDaX) trial: study protocol of a nested cohort follow-up from the PPaX (Prevention of Prematurity and Xylitol) trial examining neurodevelopmental outcomes among offspring at 4–8 years of age in Malawi
Authors: Gregory C Valentine, Brent Collett, Shaphil Wallie, Joseph Mhango, Cyril Engmann, Erin Sullivan, Maxim Seferovic, Michael Willoughby, Benjamin Shayo, Judd L Walson, Peter Milgrom, Kjersti Aagaard, Sandra E Juul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWe recently conducted the cluster-randomized, Prevention of Prematurity and Xylitol (PPaX) trial which demonstrated a significant reduction in the occurrence of delivery of preterm and low birthweight deliveries from mothers who used twice-daily xylitol-containing chewing gum in pregnancy as compared to active control among n = 10,069 enrolled pregnant participants in Lilongwe, Malawi. We seek to determine the neurodevelopmental outcomes, including any benefits or harm, of a nested cohort of n = 1000 children delivered among participants of the PPaX trial.MethodsThe Prevention of Developmental Delay and Xylitol (PDDaX) trial is a prospective, nested cohort study of 1000 randomly selected 4–8-year-old children born to gravid participants who completed the parent PPaX trial. We will assess the neurodevelopmental outcomes of the 1000 children at 4–8 years of age and evaluate any differences in neurodevelopmental outcomes related to xylitol exposure or lack thereof by assessing children’s (a) cognition, (b) executive functioning, (c) social-emotional development, (d) gross motor skills, (e) fine motor skills, and (f) language. These neurodevelopmental outcomes will be assessed via the Kaufman Assessment Battery for Children-2nd Edition (KABC-II), the EF Touch, the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire, the Malawi Developmental Assessment Tool, the Anchor Items in Measuring Early Childhood Development, and the International Development and Early Learning Assessment. The primary outcome will be the overall cognitive score, as determined by the KABC-II mental processing index. All children will also undergo hearing, vision, and comprehensive oral health examinations. Investigators from the participating sites developed and approved the research question and protocol. Each site obtained its own institutional review board and ethics approval. Patient recruitment started in July 2022 and will continue through 2027.DiscussionXylitol-containing chewing gum reduces the risk of delivery of a preterm or low birthweight neonate and neonatal demise. It also reduces gingival inflammation in gravidae. The findings from the PDDaX follow-up study will explore whether there are any long-lasting risks or benefits to offspring exposed to xylitol during gestation. If either benefit or lack of benefit (but no harm) occurs to offspring, then xylitol-containing chewing gum has strong potential to change obstetric-related care for individuals in Malawi and similar settings.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT05361122. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

It is a global priority to ensure children not only survive but thrive, living lives in which children acquire neurodevelopmental competencies that support agency, problem-solving, and academic and interpersonal success. Prematurity is the leading cause of death and disability for all children worldwide [1–4]. Efforts to reduce the risk of preterm birth must also ensure interventions do not lead to long-term harm or disability for the exposed offspring.

Periodontal disease, which includes gingivitis and periodontitis, is defined as chronic infection and inflammation of the gingival tissues. Periodontal disease is associated with systemic inflammation and adverse health outcomes [5]. In pregnancy, periodontal disease is associated with a significantly increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, including preterm birth and low birthweight offspring [6–11]. Prematurity is a health disparity with increased prevalence in low-resourced communities worldwide [1]. In Malawi, although rates in clinical trial settings vary, the incidence of periodontal disease is estimated to affect as great as 77% of all pregnant and recently postpartum individuals, and Malawi has one of the highest preterm birth rates in the world [1, 12–14].

We recently completed the cluster-randomized, open-label, parallel-group, interventional Prevention of Prematurity and Xylitol (PPaX) trial conducted in Lilongwe, Malawi. The PPaX study evaluated whether a simple, accessible, and low-cost intervention and xylitol-containing chewing gum, initiated before 20 weeks of gestation and continued twice daily until delivery, could improve oral health and reduce the incidence of preterm or low birthweight deliveries [15]. A total of 10,069 pregnant participants enrolled in the PPaX trial, with n = 4349 (from within 4 independent Ministry of Health care centers or clusters) who received xylitol-containing chewing gum and n= 5321 (from within four independent Ministry of Health care centers or clusters) in the active comparator group who did not receive xylitol-containing chewing gum [15]. Enrolled participants who received xylitol-containing chewing gum (2 g/day until delivery, correlated to two pieces of gum per day) had a 24% reduction in preterm birth and a 30% reduction in the delivery of low birthweight neonates compared to the active comparator group (relative risk [RR] 0.76, 95% confidence intervals [CI] 0.57–0.99; RR 0.70, 95% CI: 0.49–0.99, respectively) [15]. Notably, there were no maternal adverse events recorded during the PPaX trial attributed to the xylitol intervention. Thus, xylitol-containing chewing gum use initiated before 20 weeks of gestation and continued until delivery reduced the occurrence of preterm and low birthweight deliveries in Malawi while also concurrently reducing gingival inflammation in the parturients [15].

Given the association of preterm birth and neurodevelopmental outcomes, there is a clear rationale to assess the benefitsto the newborn due to reduction in inflammation. Fetal exposure to inflammation and infection in utero is associated with adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes. Specifically, studies have demonstrated reduced myelination (and growth) of the hippocampus among neonates and infants exposed to perinatal inflammation [16–18]. Even an uncomplicated urinary tract infection in pregnancy is associated with more than twofold increased odds of developing neurodevelopmental disorders later in life [19]. Thus, as the PPaX trial demonstrated reduced gingival inflammation among those with xylitol exposure [15], and xylitol is also known to independently reduce pro-inflammatory signaling [20], it is possible the offspring with xylitol exposure will have improved neurodevelopmental outcomes compared to those unexposed. In fact, one large clinical trial reported that children who were born to mothers with periodontal disease who improved in pregnancy (compared to worsened) had significantly higher motor and cognitive scores by 2 years of age, further supporting the premise of this PDDaX trial [21]. While unlikely, our current study will enable collection of objective data which will reveal whether there was long-term harm or neurodevelopmental impairment among the surviving offspring of gravid participants from the PPaX trial [15].

Objectives {7}

Leveraging the experience gained and infrastructure developed during the PPaX trial, our primary objective is to assess the neurodevelopmental outcomes of 1000 randomly selected children delivered to gravid participants enrolled in the PPaX trial who are now 4–8 years of age. We will formally evaluate any differences in neurodevelopmental outcomes related to xylitol exposure or lack thereof by assessing the child’s (a) cognition, (b) executive function, (c) social-emotional development, (d) gross motor skills, (e) fine motor skills, and (f) language. Examiners will assess these neurodevelopmental outcomes using the Kaufman Assessment Battery for Children-2nd Edition (KABC-II), the EF Touch, the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ), the Malawi Developmental Assessment Tool (MDAT), the Anchor Items in Measuring Early Childhood Development (AIM-ECD), and the International Development and Early Learning Assessment (IDELA). Of note, the primary outcome of the PDDaX trial will be the overall cognitive score as determined by the mental processing index. We hypothesize that children born to mothers who used xylitol chewing gum during pregnancy have improved neurodevelopmental outcomes at 4–8 years of age compared to children born to mothers without xylitol use during pregnancy and stratified based on whether born premature or term at birth.

Additionally, we have the following secondary objectives:

First, we seek to determine whether gestational xylitol exposure is associated with improved oral health outcomes of former gravid PPaX trial participant’s offspring at 4–8 years of age. We hypothesize that children at 4–8 years of age who were born to gravid participants with xylitol chewing gum use during pregnancy will demonstrate improved oral health metrics (dental caries and periodontal disease, including percent teeth bleeding on probing) compared to children without gestational xylitol exposure.

Trial design {8}

The N = 1000 children in the PDDaX follow-up study will be stratified into n = 500 former preterm (< 37 weeks’ gestation and < 2500-g birthweight) and n = 500 former term (≥ 37 weeks’ gestation and ≥ 2500-g birthweight) children with half in each group (n = 250) having xylitol exposure and the other half without gestational xylitol exposure (n = 250). Thus, there will be four strata of n = 250 making up the total of N = 1000 enrolled. This stratification based on gestational age at birth is necessary given that being born preterm is associated with adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes. Stratification helps to control for such confounding bias and will assess whether the effect of xylitol exposure on developmental outcomes differs in these two populations. Additionally, if the mother from the original PPaX trial is still alive and with the child, the mother will be invited to join the PDDaX trial for anthropometric assessments and an oral health assessment. The trial will be conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki, Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT), and Consolidated Standards for Reporting Trials (CONSORT). See SPIRIT figure (Fig. 1) for information pertaining to the PDDaX trial. The trial design has been approved by the Malawi National Health Sciences Research Committee (NHSRC; Protocol 22/07/1950), the University of Washington Institutional Review Board (IRB) (STUDY00015305), and the Baylor College of Medicine IRB (H-52314). The PDDaX trial is registered with the Clinical Trial Registry at www.clinicaltrials.gov (NCT05361122).

Randomization

Participants that meet the inclusion criteria of the PDDaX study will be randomly selected from the PPaX database using a computer-generated algorithm. As this is a follow-up study to the parent PPaX trial, randomization of participants to exposure to the intervention (xylitol-containing chewing gum, or not) will be based upon the cluster randomized design of the PPaX trial and what treatment group the mother of the participant was assigned.

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting {9}

Recruitment of former PPaX trial participants will occur from areas around the original eight PPaX trial health centers, which were the eight clusters in the PPaX trial: Kawale, Mitundu, Area 18, Area 25, Kabudula, Mitundu, Lumbadzi, and Chitedze. While recruitment, enrollment, and consent will occur in these community-based settings, the main study procedures will occur at Baylor College of Medicine Children’s Foundation Malawi (BCMCF) in Lilongwe, Malawi.

Eligibility criteria {10}

To be eligible for the PDDaX trial, children must have been born to formerly gravid participants enrolled in and completing the parent PPaX trial and be between 4 and 8 years of age. Additionally, participants in the PDDaX trial must be willing to undergo the neurodevelopmental assessments, travel to BCMCF for the neurodevelopmental assessments, undergo dental evaluations, and have their hearing and vision assessed. All children must have a parent or legal guardian consent to their participation in the PDDaX trial. Those who meet these inclusion criteria will be approached without regard to sex, race, ethnicity, parent’s country of origin, or religious preferences. A child will be excluded from participating in the PDDaX trial if they were not born during the PPaX trial, the parents do not provide consent for enrollment, or the child is unwilling to participate in the neurodevelopmental, hearing, vision, and dental assessments.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

Trained study staff, specifically community health workers (CHWs) and the research coordinator who have received Good Clinical Practices human subjects training, will obtain informed consent from potential trial participants. Specifically, the parent or legal guardian of the child will be approached for consent in the PDDaX trial via phone call or in person at their house. The consent will occur in Chichewa or English by native speakers and will be conducted at a 5th grade reading level. If an individual is not able to read or write, the consent form will be read to them, they will be able to use their fingerprint as their form of signature, and an additional witness will be enlisted to sign the form to attest that they have additionally heard the parent or legal guardian affirm their understanding of the information in the consent document and provide their informed consent to participate in the PDDaX trial. The consent documents will include the child and the mother who were part of the original PPaX trial. If the mother is not available, then the parent or guardian will consent for only the child’s participation in the study. Children will be asked to assent to participation in the study.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b}

Consenting to participation in the PDDaX trial also includes consent to have oral microbiome samples collected from the participating child and mother if within the first n = 125 mother–child dyads recruited per assigned group. These biospecimens will be biobanked for future analyses exploring potential mechanisms or pathways related to gestational xylitol use and long-term neurodevelopmental and/or oral health benefits to both pregnant individuals and their children.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b} and intervention description {11a}

For this neurodevelopmental follow-up study of the gestational xylitol intervention exposure (or lack thereof), we have selected six different neurodevelopmental assessments that are validated tools for children in the age range of our study and have been validated within low- and middle-income country contexts, particularly regions within Africa.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

The inability to complete the neurodevelopmental assessments will not result in discontinuation from the study. It may be possible that the study participant will need to return for an additional visit to complete the neurodevelopmental assessments if unwell on the day of the neurodevelopmental testing. In such a case, the planned neurodevelopmental tests for that study day will be postponed, and the child will be seen at another visit after first being referred to a hospital or outpatient clinic for treatment. Attempts will then be made to have additional visits unless the child ages out of the study (9 years old or greater).

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

An extensive training program for the study team investigators will be developed to ensure effectiveness with 4–8-year-old children and ensure fidelity (see the “Implementation” section below).

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Concomitant care will not be prohibited during this PDDaX trial, although we are collecting information such as access to therapies and other treatments as potential confounding variables affecting neurodevelopmental outcomes.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

There are no specific provisions for posttrial care as neurodevelopmental assessments are not likely to cause any harm or injury to the participants requiring long-term care or follow-up. However, if a child participant is found to have hearing or visual impairment, the child will be referred to either the Kamuzu Central Hospital otolaryngology clinic or optometry clinic, respectively. If there are concerns about developmental delays, referral will be to the Children of Blessings Trust, a nonprofit that provides free and reduced-cost neurodevelopmental therapies including physical therapy.

Sample size {14}

For each strata (term and preterm), group sample sizes of 250 xylitol-exposed and 250 xylitol-unexposed children achieve 80% power to reject the null hypothesis of zero effect size when the population effect size is 0.25, and the significance level (alpha) is 0.05 using a two-sided two-sample equal-variance t-test. A difference of 0.25 standard deviations is modest, though clinically meaningful, considering the public health implications in Malawi given the ubiquity of periodontitis and the exceptionally low cost of the xylitol gum intervention.

Recruitment {15}

Using a stratified enrollment approach, 500 participants formerly term and 500 formerly preterm newborns born during the parent PPaX trial will be randomly recruited. It is anticipated that there will be a 40–50% enrollment rate from the original PPaX trial due to (1) challenges related to locating participants through phone calls, texts, or field visits after 4–8 years from last contact (including moving out of the region) and (2) child mortality. CHWs will travel to the addresses on file from the parent PPaX trial as well as attempt to call the families using their previously recorded phone numbers. Up to 10 attempts to reach the families will occur prior to considering them lost to follow-up from the parent PPaX trial. The CHWs and research coordinator will collaborate with local village elders and health surveillance officers to enhance contacting the potential PDDaX trial participants. Participants who have moved out of the area will not be included in this trial given the financial and logistical constraints related to traveling to Lilongwe, Malawi, from distant sites in Malawi and/or out of the country. Each participant receives the equivalent of approximately US $10 in local currency to offset any travel-related expenses to come from home to BCMCF.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Gestational exposure to xylitol, or the lack thereof, was per the cluster-randomized parent PPaX trial.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Participants will not be examined by a study team member who originally recruited them into the study. No location information will be collected until the end of the study visit, unless required by the neurodevelopmental assessment itself. For example, some questions on the IDELA ask what school the child attends or what their village name is, and these questions will be asked due to their need for the neurodevelopmental assessment. The study IDs, which initially included location data (A = Kawale, etc.), will be changed to remove any information pertaining to cluster randomization. As individuals freely move to new locations, the initial site of randomization in the PPaX trial cannot reliably be inferred from the current village of residence. The study coordinator will be responsible for assigning the CHW assessors making sure not to assign CHWs who recruited the participant and ensuring assessors are assigned who are “specialists” in the neurodevelopmental tests needed for that visit.

Implementation {16c}

As psychologists trained in neurodevelopmental assessments are uncommon in Malawi, the research team will hire and train CHWs to conduct the neurodevelopmental assessments as part of the PDDaX trial. CHWs who were initially part of the parent PPaX trial and those with experience in child education or neurodevelopment will be preferentially selected. After hiring, the CHWs will undergo training courses in each of the six neurodevelopmental assessments that will be administered as follows: MDAT, IDELA, EF Touch, SDQ, AIM-ECD, and the KABC-II.

Training will be conducted by a team member with a doctorate who is qualified in pediatric medicine and/or child psychology and behavioral medicine and will involve classroom format instruction on standardized assessment, early childhood development (including typical and atypical development), and practical hands-on learning with test administration. Training will also be co-led with an experienced and/or “Master” trainer from the respective test administrators. For instance, an MDAT Master Trainer will lead MDAT training and provide certifications in test administration to the CHWs after they successfully complete the course, demonstrating proficiency and mastery of test administration. The KABC-II will be co-led by an experienced trainer (the same as the Master Trainer for the MDAT). The EF Touch will be co-taught by Dr. Michael Willoughby, the creator of the EF Touch. The AIM-ECD will be led by a Master Trainer from the AIM-ECD. The IDELA will be taught by a trainer who has undertaken the Master Trainer coursework. The SDQ will be taught by a pediatrician who has received training and exhibited proficiency in the SDQ administration. Training will first include an overview of the test administration via classroom and didactic-based coursework prior to simulations and then ultimately via pilot testing with pediatric participants. The simulations will involve pairings in which one member has the role of the assessor and the other has the role of the pediatric participant. These pairings will occur in front of the larger team as well as in smaller groups with oversight by the master trainers to provide both peer-to-peer feedback as well as master trainer feedback. The CHWs who undergo knowledge-based assessments and directly observed proficiency examinations will receive certifications in their test administration for each of the six respective neurodevelopmental assessments.

After training, the CHWs will continue to practice their skills with supportive supervision and collect video-recorded assessments for internal and systematic review using a standard rubric. Examiners will only begin formal study assessments after at least 10 satisfactory practice assessments (i.e., > 90% item-level inter-rater reliability, no administration errors that affect validity). The testing team will work together to maintain a high level of quality control and minimize examiner drift. All testing will be video recorded for reliability assessment and review. BCMCF has a robust infrastructure for conducting psychological testing with CHWs working daily at the site. As BCMCF initially started with a focus on HIV-infected children and adolescents, psychological support and evaluation are critically integrated into a holistic care model. These CHWs have also helped perform rigorous studies including the parent PPaX trial [15], and this study will build upon this existing infrastructure to conduct psychological assessments of pediatric patients at BCMCF.

Additionally, to maximize inter-rater reliability, each CHW will be limited to performing two neurodevelopmental tests of the four (KABC-II, EF Touch, IDELA, MDAT). This will equate to three or fewer CHWs conducting any one neurodevelopmental test. This approach will enhance inter-rater reliability.

The specialists for each test will also be responsible for reviewing every 10th neurodevelopmental assessment by the others in the group by watching a video recording of the encounter and scoring it accordingly. This will enhance their own learning and mastery of the neurodevelopmental assessments, enhance inter-rater reliability, provide opportunities for peer-to-peer feedback, and prevent drift in test administration.

The pilot testing phase will directly follow the 6-week intensive training phase and will include 13 children who were born during the PPaX trial and are 4–8 years old. Children will be recruited for the pilot testing who were born at term (≥ 37 weeks’ gestation and ≥ 2500 g birthweight). These pilot data will not be included in the final dataset of the N = 1000 for data analysis of primary and secondary outcomes.

Up to 3 participants per day and up to 15 visits a week will be included, which equates to approximately 720 visits per year, assuming 48 weeks per year with 4 weeks excluded due to clinic closures on holidays. As each participant requires 2 visits, approximately 360 patients will complete the study per year. Each neurodevelopmental assessment takes approximately 45 min to 1 h to complete.

There are several notable barriers to implementation that could affect the success of the PDDaX trial, and solutions have been developed to overcome them. For example, BCMCF has only one room available for the neurodevelopmental assessments, and the facility closes at 1 pm on Fridays. There are also constraints on the timing for dental visits, which can only occur Monday–Thursday before 2 pm. The solution is to coordinate study visits within these constraints and stagger testing to allow for up to four study visits per day. Children and parents take breaks to play outside, eat, or complete other study procedures outside of the neurodevelopmental exam room.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

See Fig. 2 for the overall study schematic. We will next discuss each part of the PDDaX study in more detail.

Initial study-entry questionnaire

Demographic, clinical, and assessment information will be collected including the following: age at the time of assessment; malnutrition status as determined by height and weight; mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC); higher-order pregnancies; access to electricity and running water; number of children in the household; exposure to tobacco; history of malaria infection based on rapid testing; HIV infection or exposure; if enrolled in preschool, and, if so, number of years in school; and maternal and paternal educational attainment, among other variables. This questionnaire will be provided to the participant’s caregiver (preferably the mother if she is available but it will be provided to the father or other primary care provider as a secondary option). The anthropometric information such as height, weight, MUAC, and head circumference will be collected on the first visit to BCMCF to use the same equipment and time of evaluation compared to neurodevelopmental testing for all enrolled participants. We will also ask the mother from the original PPaX trial, if available, about any pregnancies and their associated outcomes subsequent to the PPaX trial pregnancy.

Kaufman Assessment Battery for Children-II (KABC-II)

The KABC-II will be administered to each study participant, and it will assess the overall cognitive ability (mental processing index) and the following sub-domains: sequential processing (working memory), simultaneous processing (visual processing), planning ability (fluid reasoning), and learning ability (long-term memory and retrieval). Because US norms may not be appropriate for Malawian participants, z-scores will be generated for each outcome based on the distribution of scores in the control sample (xylitol unexposed term children). For the KABC-II, developmental delay will be defined if any score is ≥ 1 standard deviation below the mean. If a child is unwell on the day of the exam, the KABC-II will not be administered, and the child will be referred to a hospital or outpatient clinic for treatment. The primary outcome of the PDDaX trial will be the overall cognitive score as determined by the mental processing index. Subscales will be assessed individually in subsequent secondary analyses.

EF Touch

The EF Touch assesses neurodevelopment related to executive functioning including the following: “Bubbles” (simple reaction time), “Animal Go/No-Go” (inhibitory control), “Silly Sounds Stroop” (inhibitory control), “Pick the Picture” (working memory), “Something the Same” (attention shifting), and “Arrows” (inhibitory control and attention shifting). Scores are generated to assess the accuracy of a child’s performance (e.g., number correct) and response time. A total composite score will be generated for accuracy and response time, again using the distribution of scores in the control sample to generate z-scores for analyses.

The entire EF Touch test administration generally takes between 45 and 60 min, with each task taking about 5 min to complete. The specific assessments selected will depend upon the child’s age because some assessments are for younger ages but not older or vice versa.

The test will be scored based upon the EF Touch Battery Scoring System and will include composite and sub-domain scores. Mean reaction times for any of the timed activities will also be recorded and assessed.

Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ)

The SDQ will be administered to the participant’s caregiver (preferably the mother if she is available but it will be provided to the father or other care provider as a secondary option). The SDQ is a 25-item questionnaire with five subscales: emotional symptoms, conduct problems, hyperactivity/inattention, peer relationship problems, and prosocial behavior. Each subscale contains 5 questions, with the first 4 subscale questions (20 questions total) added together to generate a summative score. In total, the SDQ takes approximately 5 min to complete. The SDQ will be translated into Chichewa and English, the two most common languages in Malawi. If a caregiver does not have the ability to read or write, then the CHW will read the questions and record the responses provided verbally by the caregiver. The main outcome which will serve as a secondary outcome for the PDDaX trial will be the composite score. Subscales will additionally be assessed individually.

Anchors Items for Measurement of Early Childhood Development (AIM-ECD) caregiver report

A CHW will administer the AIM-ECD parental questionnaire to the participant’s caregiver (preferably the mother if she is available but will be provided to the father or other care provider as a secondary option). The AIM-ECD is a parental survey which will assess a child’s neurodevelopment as reported by the child’s primary caregiver. The AIM-ECD takes approximately 10–20 min to administer. The child development sections consist of 20 core items which were selected after a review of caregiver-reported questions in several countries, and items are designed specifically to be usable in any cultural or linguistic context with minimal adaptation. The main outcome of the AIM-ECD will be the composite child development score which will serve as one of the secondary outcomes of this PDDaX trial.

Malawi Development Assessment Tool (MDAT)

A CHW will administer the MDAT to the pediatric participant. The MDAT is a neurodevelopmental screening assessment with a composite score and four subdomains: gross motor, fine motor, social, and language. The MDAT is culturally adapted for use in Malawian children [22–24]. Both an overall composite and subdomain (gross motor, fine motor, language, and social) Z-score results will be secondary outcomes of the PDDaX trial.

International Development and Early Learning Assessment (IDELA)

A CHW will administer the IDELA to all pediatric participants. The IDELA is a neurodevelopmental assessment tool assessing development and early learning and has been used throughout sub-Saharan Africa and other LMICs [25]. The composite score will serve as a secondary outcome of the PDDaX trial.

Hearing assessment

All participants will undergo a hearing screen on the day of the neurodevelopmental assessment, specifically the clinically validated, tablet-based HearTest [26, 27]. The hearing screen will consist of the validated and calibrated HearTest device and will be administered by a CHW, pediatrician, or other trained research assistant. The hearing screen will occur in a room with minimal-to-no external noise and minimal-to-no distractions to minimize interference with the hearing screening. The tester, the participant, and the parent or guardian will be the only individuals in the room during this testing.

Vision assessment

All participants will undergo a vision screen on the day of neurodevelopmental assessment. The vision screen will consist of the Snellen visual acuity chart, and a CHW, pediatrician, or another trained research assistant will administer this test. The vision screen will occur in a room with minimal-to-no external noise and minimal-to-no distractions. The tester, the participant, and the parent or guardian will be the only individuals in the room during this testing.

Oral health assessment

The dental examination of each pediatric participant and their mother will occur under the supervision of the PDDaX dentist and co-investigator at the Kamuzu Central Hospital Dental Clinic. There, the dentist and members of her team working under her supervision will perform the World Health Organization (WHO) oral health assessment as per the WHO Oral Health Assessment Form for Children, 2013 edition, or the WHO Oral Health Assessment Form for Adults, 2013 edition for the mothers [28].

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

Oral microbiome samples, specifically subgingival plaque and an oral buccal swab, of the pediatric participant and their mother who was initially enrolled in the PPaX trial will be collected and stored in a −80 °C freezer on site at BCMCF. The PPaX trial did not collect biospecimens; thus, these materials will be greatly helpful in exploring the potential effects and mechanism of how xylitol led to these effects and the evaluation of the impact in offspring through this collection and repository for biospecimens as described here.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Analyses will assume prior exposure to xylitol based on intention to treat by cluster. We will utilize regression models to compare primary and secondary outcomes for participants exposed versus not exposed to xylitol in utero. Separate models will be evaluated for formerly preterm and formerly term subgroups. The distribution of scores in the term control group will be assessed for normality using histograms and Q-Q plots, and as appropriate, Z-scores will be calculated for all children using this group as a reference. Initially, univariable regression models will be fit to compare xylitol-exposed versus unexposed groups without covariate adjustment, but subsequent regression models will be fit to adjust for potential confounders as appropriate. Candidate covariates include maternal and paternal education level; sex; twins; higher-order pregnancies; MUAC; height and weight Z-scores; exposure to tobacco fields; history of malaria infection; human immunodeficiency virus infection, if in school; number of children in household; and degree of prematurity (if in the preterm cohort). Site of enrollment will be included as a random effect.

Analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint(s)

KABC-II Mental Processing Index (primary outcome)

Comparing participants exposed to xylitol versus unexposed, linear regression models will be evaluated to compare KABC-II global composite scores (mental processing index) separately for the term and preterm cohorts. The test norms for the KABC-II are based on children in the USA and are unlikely to be representative for these Malawi participants. Instead of using these test norms, to standardize comparisons, the distribution of scores in the term control group will be assessed for normality using histograms and Q-Q plots, and as appropriate, Z-scores will be calculated for all children using this group as a reference. In addition to assessing KABC-II as a continuous outcome, we will use generated Z-scores to classify developmental delay. The proportion of participants with a composite score meeting the criteria for developmental delay will also be summarized by group, using a cutoff of less than 1 SD below the mean in the term unexposed group (a Z-score of −1). This will be used to ensure capture of even mild developmental delays that are clinically meaningful. Risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals comparing the risk of developmental delay in children with gestational xylitol exposure to the risk in those without will be computed separately for each cohort (term and preterm). An alpha of 0.05 will be used for significance testing.

Analysis of the secondary endpoint(s)

Similar to the KABC-II, linear regression models will be used to compare the mean scores of the other neurodevelopmental outcome composite and domain scores (MDAT, IDELA, EF Touch, and AIM-ECD) for participants exposed versus not exposed to xylitol in utero. Separate models will be performed for preterm and term subgroups. As with the KABC-II, the distribution of scores in the term control group will be assessed for normality using histograms and Q-Q plots, and as appropriate, Z-scores will be calculated for all children using this group as a reference. The proportion of participants with a composite score meeting the criteria for developmental delay for a given exam will also be summarized by group, using a cutoff of less than 1 SD below the mean in the term unexposed group (a Z-score of −1). Additionally, we will perform both univariate and multivariate regression models to evaluate outcomes for each neurodevelopmental assessment as listed above based on degree of prematurity at birth as a continuous metric. Risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals comparing the risk of developmental delay in children with gestational xylitol exposure to the risk in those without will be computed separately for each cohort (term and preterm). An alpha of 0.05 will be used for significance testing.

Assessing the MDAT’s ability to determine cognitive outcomes as compared to the KABC-II Mental Processing Index

The MDAT will be compared to the KABC-II Mental Processing Index as a gold standard to determine if the MDAT can identify the appropriate subset of children experiencing clinically significant developmental delay (defined as a Z-score < −1 on the KABC-II versus MDAT). The sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value with 95% confidence intervals will be calculated. Descriptive statistics (i.e., means, standard deviations, frequencies) will summarize indicators of utility separately for the MDAT and KABC-II. For example, examiner training time, inter-rater reliability, administration time, and the rate of tests that cannot be administered/scored (e.g., due to child refusal or examiner error) will be tracked. Patients will be classified as having developmental delay per the MDAT with performance of one or more standard deviations below the mean composite Z-score among xylitol-unexposed term controls; similarly, developmental delay per the KABC-II if one or more standard deviations below the mean KABC-II Mental Processing Index Z-score among xylitol-unexposed, term controls. Area under the curve (AUC) will also be plotted to assess the performance of the MDAT composite score in predicting neurodevelopmental delay versus the KABC-II. The convergence of the MDAT as compared to the KABC-II will be evaluated as continuous measures. Specifically, Pearson product-moment or Spearman’s rank-order correlation coefficients will also be calculated to assess the relationship between MDAT composite score and KABC-II. Sub-analyses assessing the correlation between the individual domains (MDAT: fine motor, gross motor, language, social; KABC-II: sequential processing, simultaneous processing, planning, learning) will be conducted as appropriate.

Based on our findings, additive models will be considered incorporating elements of the IDELA and AIM-ECD with the MDAT to improve predictive accuracy of classifying neurodevelopmental delay as measured by the KABC-II. If performance of the MDAT alone indicates sensitivity and/or specificity of < 85%, we will explore machine learning techniques that will be employed as appropriate to identify questions from the IDELA, EF Touch, and/or AIM-ECD that, combined with the MDAT, maximize AUC, sensitivity, and specificity as compared to MDAT alone. Careful consideration will be given to candidate elements of these exams.

As an exploratory aim, we will also use regression models to assess dental data as available to determine whether maternal oral health during pregnancy acts as either a full or partial mediator in the relationship between in utero xylitol exposure and (1) neurodevelopmental outcomes (KABC-II Z-score) and (2) current child oral health. Analyses will be dependent on oral health data available from the original PPaX trial; among 9043 PPaX gravidae initially assessed for potential eligibility in PDDaX, 3766 had completed maternal dental records from the prior study (42%). Randomization of contact order accounted for dental record availability to maximize data availability.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Definition of adverse events (AE)

In the PDDaX trial, an adverse event will be defined as any untoward medical occurrence associated with the use of an intervention in humans, whether or not considered intervention related (21 CFR 312.32 (a)).

Definition of serious adverse events (SAE)

An AE or suspected adverse reaction will be considered “serious” if, in the view of either the investigator or sponsor, it results in any of the following outcomes: death, a life-threatening adverse event, inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, and a persistent or significant incapacity or substantial disruption of the ability to conduct normal life functions. Important medical events that may not result in death, be life-threatening, or require hospitalization may be considered serious when, based upon appropriate medical judgment, they may jeopardize the participant and may require medical or surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes listed in this definition.

Classification of an adverse event

Severity of event

For AEs not included in the protocol-defined grading system, the following guidelines will be used to describe severity. A mild event will be classified as any event requiring minimal or no treatment and do not interfere with the participant’s daily activities. A moderate AE will be defined as any event resulting in a low level of inconvenience or concern with the therapeutic measures. Moderate events may cause some interference with functioning. A severe AE will be defined as any event interrupting a participant’s usual daily activity and may require systemic drug therapy or other treatment. Of note, the term “severe” does not necessarily equate to “serious.”

Relationship to study intervention

All AEs must have their relationship to study intervention assessed by the clinician who will examine and evaluate the participant based on temporal relationship and his/her clinical judgment. The degree of certainty about causality will be graded using the categories below. In a clinical trial, the study product must always be suspect. An AE will be considered related to the study intervention if the AE is known to occur with the study intervention, there is a reasonable possibility that the study intervention caused the AE, or there is a temporal relationship between the study intervention and the event. Reasonable possibility means that there is evidence to suggest a causal relationship between the study intervention and the AE. AEs related to intervention are highly unlikely in this non-interventional follow-up study, although the purpose of the PDDaX trial is to ensure no long-term harm occurred with regard to the neurodevelopmental outcomes of children gestationally exposed to xylitol.

An AE will be considered not related to the intervention, xylitol, and/or the neurodevelopmental assessments, and if there is not a reasonable possibility that the administration of the study intervention caused the event, there is no temporal relationship between the study intervention and event onset, or an alternate etiology has been established. This is most likely the case in this follow-up, non-interventional PDDaX trial.

Expectedness

The PI will be responsible for determining whether an AE is expected or unexpected. An AE will be considered unexpected if the nature, severity, or frequency of the event is not consistent with the risk information previously described for the study intervention.

Time period and frequency for event assessment and follow-up

The occurrence of an AE or SAE may come to the attention of study personnel during the study visit(s) or upon review by a study monitor. Given that there is no intervention in this follow-up study, it is unlikely that any AE or SAE will occur, and if so, outside of the study visit(s). All AEs including local and systemic reactions not meeting the criteria for SAEs will be captured on the appropriate case report form (CRF). Any medical condition that is present at the time that the participant is screened will be considered as baseline and not reported as an AE. The research coordinator will record all reportable events with start dates occurring any time after informed consent is obtained until the last day of study participation. At each study visit, the investigator will inquire about the occurrence of AE/SAEs since the last visit. Events will be followed for outcome information until resolution or stabilization.

Discussion

The PDDaX trial seeks to determine whether gestational xylitol exposure leads to any long-term effects on offspring neurodevelopmental outcomes. As the parent PPaX trial demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in the risk of preterm birth, low birthweight offspring, and newborn mortality with concurrent reduction in clinical metrics of maternal gingival inflammation among gravidae in Malawi, it is important to confirm that the exposed children not only survived but lived without increased risk of adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes. Furthermore, given that fetal exposure to maternal inflammation in pregnancy is associated with long-term neurodevelopmental impairment, and that gravidae within the PPaX trial that received xylitol had significantly reduced metrics of gingival inflammation, it is possible that the children may have improved long-term outcomes compared to those gestationally unexposed to xylitol. While our study seeks to evaluate the long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes at 4–8 years of age among children born during the parent PPaX trial and while our study seeks to account for many known confounders in our statistical analyses, causality may be difficult to prove. Yet, if xylitol use in pregnancy is proven to have benefit to offspring long-term and/or no findings of harm, then this simple, innovative, accessible, and affordable intervention has the potential to reshape the way maternal and child care is provided in Malawi and other similar contexts throughout low- and middle-income countries worldwide. If the PDDaX trial demonstrates a lack of harm and/or benefit in the children exposed to xylitol prenatally, then future large-scale, multinational clinical trials would be needed to confirm the generalizability of these critically important results within the field of maternal, newborn, and child health.

Declarations

Ethics approval and consent to participate {24}

The PDDaX trial was conducted after IRB, and ethics approvals were obtained by the Malawi NHSRC (Protocol 22/07/1950), Baylor College of Medicine (H-52314), and the University of Washington (STUDY00015305). All participants must provide consent to participate in the PDDaX trial.

Consent for publication {32}

This study will be conducted in accordance with the following publication and data sharing policies and regulations. All participants consent to having their data published, and the data will be de-identified.

Competing interests {28}

The independence of this study from any actual or perceived influence is critical. Therefore, any actual conflict of interest of persons who have a role in the design, conduct, analysis, publication, or any aspect of this trial will be disclosed and managed. Furthermore, persons who have a perceived conflict of interest will be required to have such conflicts managed in a way that is appropriate to their participation in the design and conduct of this trial. The study leadership in conjunction with the Thrasher Research Fund, NIH, the UW, BCM, and BCMCF has established policies and procedures for all study group members to disclose all conflicts of interest and will establish a mechanism for the management of all reported dualities of interest. Financial conflict of interest attestations will be completed prior to the trial initiation.

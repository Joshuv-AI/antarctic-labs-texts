On multidimensional Bohr radius of finite dimensional Banach spaces
Vasudevarao Allu, Subhadip Pal

Abstract
. In this paper, we improve the lower estimate of multidimensional Bohr radius for unit ball of ℓ q n \ell^{n}_{q} -spaces ( 1 ≤ q ≤ ∞ 1\leq q\leq\infty ) for bounded holomorphic functions with values in finite dimensional complex Banach spaces. The new estimate provides the improved lower bound for the Bohr radius which was previously given by Defant, Maestre, and Schwarting [Adv. Math. 231 (2012), 2837–2857].

1.
Introduction

A complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that if
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
, then
(\lambda e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,\lambda e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n})\in\Omega
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and all
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. For a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Bohr radius
K(\Omega)
is the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for each holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
on
\Omega
, we have

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}|c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}|\leq\sup_{z\in\Omega}\bigg|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\bigg|.

Here,
\alpha
denotes an
n
-tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
(
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
) of non-negative integers,
z
stands for an
n
-tuple
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
of complex numbers, and
z^{\alpha}
denotes the product
z^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\cdots z^{\alpha_{n}}_{n}
. By a remarkable result of Harald Bohr
[
13
]
, also known as famous Bohr’s power series theorem shows that
K(\mathbb{D})=1/3
. In this paper, we keep our interest on the complete Reinhardt domains in
n
-dimensions are

B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}<1\right\},\quad 1\leq q\leq\infty,

where we write
\ell^{n}_{q}
for the Banach space defined by
\mathbb{C}^{n}
together with the
q
-norm
\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}
, for
1\leq q<\infty
and
\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}:=\sup\{|z_{i}|:1\leq i\leq n\}
for
q=\infty
. In 1997, Boas and Khavinson
[
11
]
studied the
n
dimensional Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})
for the polydisk
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and obtained the following interesting estimate:

(1.1)

\frac{1}{3\sqrt{n}}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{n}}.

However, the concept of Bohr radius in several variables first appeared implicitly in a paper of Dineen and Timoney
[
21
]
in which the authors have explained the idea of extending the Bohr’s result in higher dimension. Later, the problem becomes very active in regard to find the correct asymptotic estimates of
n
dimensional Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})
for
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Investigations by the galaxy of analysts like Aizenberg, Boas, Defant, Dineen, Frerick, Khavinson and Timoney from
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
21
]
established that there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(1.2)

\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{\log n}{n\log\log n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})\leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}.

By use of surprising fact of hypercontarctivity of Bohnenblust-Hille inequality for homogeneous polynomials, a groundbreaking progress on this problem, especially for the case
q=\infty
has made by Dafant and his coauthors in
[
17
, Theorem 2]
and they have shown that

K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})=b_{n}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+o(1)\leq b_{n}\leq 2.

Finally, Bayart
et. al.

[
7
]
have established the exact asymptotic behaviour of
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})
as

K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})\sim_{+\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.

Numerous significant contributions have been made to the study of the multidimensional Bohr radius. Notably, the works of Aizenberg and his collaborators
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
have explored this topic in the context of various domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Important developments have also been made by Balasubramanian, Calado, and Queffélec
[
6
]
in the setting of Dirichlet series; by Defant and his coauthors
[
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
in the study of vector-valued holomorphic functions; by Paulsen and Singh
[
24
]
in the framework of Banach algebras; and by Popescu
[
25
]
in the context of free holomorphic functions.

The study of Bohr radius becomes very interesting when one considers the space of holomorphic functions in Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with values in Banach spaces (vector-valued) instead of scalar-valued functions. Initially, for the one dimensional case, the Bohr radius
K(\mathbb{D},X)
for the space of holomorphic functions from unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in complex plane to any arbitrary complex Banach space
X
was studied by Blasco
[
9
]
and followed by
p
-Bohr radius
K_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
for powered Bohr’s inequality for any
p\in[1,\infty]
can be found in
[
10
]
. It has been noticed by Blasco
[
9
]
that for
X=\ell^{2}_{q}
the Bohr radius
K(\mathbb{D},\ell^{2}_{q})=0
for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
. This fact motivates Defant
et. al.

[
19
]
to modify the definition of Bohr radius in order to obtain non-trivial Bohr radius. Indeed, Defant
et. al.

[
19
]
have introduced the Bohr radius in more general form.

Definition 1.1
.

[
19
]

Let
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded linear operator between complex Banach spaces,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\left\lVert T\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. The
\lambda
-Bohr radius of
T
, denoted by
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},T,\lambda)
is the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for all holomorphic functions
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
on
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}
we have

\sup_{z\in rB_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\lambda\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert.

If
T
is the identity operator on
X
we denote the
\lambda
-Bohr radius as
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,\lambda)
. For
\lambda=1
and
X=\mathbb{C}
, we use the notation
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X)
and
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})
respectively.

In
[
19
]
, the authors have studied the Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,\lambda)
for both finite and infinite dimensional complex Banach spaces. In particular for finite dimensional complex Banach spaces, Dafant and his co-authors have proved the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a finite dimensional complex Banach space and
\lambda>1
. Then there are constants
C
,
D>0
depending on
\lambda
and
X
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

(1.3)

C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,\lambda)\leq D\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.

Recently, Kumar and Manna
[
23
]
, have estimated the Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
for the remaining unit ball
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
,
1\leq q<\infty
and proved that for each
1\leq q<\infty
and
\lambda>1
,

(1.4)

C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq D\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}},

where the constants
C
and
D
depends on
\lambda
and
X
. The main aim of this paper is to show an improved lower bound of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). In 2021, Bernal-González
et. al.
implicitly
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
improved the lower bound of
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})
given in (
1.1
). Indeed, the authors
[
8
]
have proved that the Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})
is not less than the solution
r>0
of the equation

r+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}S(m,n)r^{m}=\frac{1}{2},

where
S(m,n)
is the Sidon constant for each
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and it is defined by the infimum of all
\eta>0
such that

(1.5)

\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}|c_{\alpha}|\leq\eta\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}}\bigg|\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\bigg|

for all
m
-homogeneous polynomials
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},\mathbb{C})
. Recently, Kumar and Manna
[
22
]
demonstrated that the methods introduced in
[
8
]
can be effectively applied to improve the lower bound of
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})
for
1\leq q<\infty
, as presented in (
1.2
). Our aim is to implement the idea for vector-valued holomorphic functions and establish a new lower bound for
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
for any finite dimensional complex Banach spaces
X
and all
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
\lambda>1
.
In Section
2
, we shall provide basic definitions, notations, and some preliminary statements. In Section
3
, we shall discuss the connection between Bohr radius and unconditional basis constant for space of vector-valued homogeneous polynomials, and the main results of this paper is contained in the final section i.e., in Section
4
.

.

[

For every

\left\lVert x^{*}\right\rVert\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sup_{v\in\Omega_{z}}|x^{*}(Q(v))|.

Moreover, it is not so difficult to see that

if, and only if,

is constant for every

For each

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
X
be any finite dimensional complex Banach space and

,

. Let
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)

satisfying that there exist

and

such that
x^{*}(z_{j}x_{me_{j}})\neq 0\neq x^{*}(z_{j^{\prime}}x_{me_{j^{\prime}}})
for

. Then

\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert_{2}<\sup_{v\in\Omega_{z}}\left\lVert Q(v)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)}.

Proof.
For
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
and

, we define the trigonometric polynomial
T_{Q}
of degree

T_{Q}(\widetilde{\theta}):=|x^{*}(Q(z_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,z_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}))|^{2}=x^{*}(Q(z_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,z_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}))\overline{x^{*}(Q(z_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,z_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}))}

for all
\widetilde{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. If we use the notation
\left\langle\alpha,\widetilde{\theta}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\theta_{k}
, then we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial T_{Q}}{\partial\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\alpha_{j}x^{*}(x_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}e^{i\left\langle\alpha,\widetilde{\theta}\right\rangle}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\overline{x^{*}(x_{\alpha})}\overline{z}^{\alpha}e^{-i\left\langle\alpha,\widetilde{\theta}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\Lambda(m,n)\\
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{j}x^{*}(x_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\overline{x^{*}(x_{\alpha^{\prime}})}\overline{z}^{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{i\left\langle\alpha-\alpha^{\prime},\widetilde{\theta}\right\rangle}-i\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\Lambda(m,n)\\

where
\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
.
Since
x^{*}(x_{me_{j}})x^{*}(x_{me_{j^{\prime}}})\neq 0
, it follows that

\frac{\partial T_{Q}}{\partial\theta_{j}}\neq 0,

which shows that
T_{Q}
is non-constant. Further,
T_{Q}
is merely

. Therefore, we obtain
|x^{*}(Q)|
is non-constant. This competes the proof.

For every
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
, we define
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{2}=\sup_{z\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert_{2}
. Then, using the fact card
\Lambda(m,n)=\mbox{card}\,\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}:|\alpha|=m\}=\binom{n+m-1}{m}=N_{m}(n)

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
and
X
be any finite dimensional complex Banach space. Then for all
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
with
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}=1
we have

\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{1}<\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}.

Proof.
We first note that
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Then, in view of (
2.5
), we have that

(2.7)

\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}.

We prove the required assertion by the method of contradiction. Let us suppose there exists
Q(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}v_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
with
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}=1
such that
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{1}=\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}
. Then, by the virtue of the inequality (
2.7
), it follows that

\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{1}=\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}.

Hence, we obtain
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}=1
, which shows that

\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\widetilde{z}^{\alpha}\right\rVert=\frac{\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\widetilde{z}^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{m}(n)}}.

Finally, in view of Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that

1=\left\lVert Q(\widetilde{z})\right\rVert_{2}<\sup_{v\in\Omega_{z}}\left\lVert Q(v)\right\rVert\leq 1,

which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.2

and

. Consequently, in view of Proposition

.

For each pair

3.
Bohr radius and unconditional basis constant

A Schauder basis
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of a Banach space
E
is said to be unconditional if there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}\epsilon_{j}\sigma_{j}x_{j}\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{j}x_{j}\right\rVert

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and all
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, and all
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\epsilon_{k}|\leq 1
,
1\leq k\leq n
. In this case, the best constant
C
is denoted by
\chi(\{x_{n}\})
and called the unconditional basis constant of
\{x_{n}\}
. Moreover, the unconditional basis constant
\chi(E)\in[1,\infty]
of the Banach space
E
is defined by the infimum over all constants
\chi(\{x_{n}\})
. We say that a basis
\{x_{n}\}
of a Banach space is
1
-unconditional if
\chi(\{x_{n}\})=1
. In this paper, we mainly consider the
n
-dimensional Banach spaces
X=(\mathbb{C}^{n},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{q})
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
such that the canonical basis vectors
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
form a
1
-unconditional basis, or another way it means that the unit ball
B_{X}
is a Reinhardt domain.

By
\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))
, we denote the unconditional basis constant of the monomials
z^{\alpha},\,|\alpha|=m
in the Banach space
\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
. Equivalently, we can write

\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)):=\sup\left\{\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\right\rVert z^{\alpha}\right\rVert:\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq 1\right\}.

Let
X
be any finite dimensional Banach space. It is worth noting that the Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
of the open unit ball
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
with respect to bounded holomorphic functions with values in
X
is the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that

\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(rz)^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\lambda\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

for every power series expansion
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
convergent on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. Now, we have the following analogous Bohr radius for
m
-homogeneous polynomials.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we deine the Bohr radius, denoted by
K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
and defined by the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that

\sup_{z\in rB_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\lambda\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

for all
m
-homogeneous polynomials
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
.

Indeed, in view of triangle inequality and the fact that
\{|z_{i}|\}^{n}_{i=1}\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
for each
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, we have for each
m
and each
m
-homogeneous polynomials
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\right\rVert z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{(K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda))^{m}}\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert.

It is not so hard to observe that
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
for all
m
. Further, we note that
K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)=\sqrt[m]{\lambda}K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,1)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a finite dimesional complex Banach space. Then, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\geq 1
we have

(3.3)

K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt[m]{\lambda}}{\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}.

Proof.
We first prove the upper estimate for
K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
. Let
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
be an
m
-homogeneous polynomial and
\epsilon_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\epsilon_{\alpha}|\leq 1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\epsilon_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{(K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda))^{m}}\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert.

Using the definition of unconditional basis constant of spaces of
m
-homogeneous polynomials, it follows that

\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))\leq\frac{\lambda}{(K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda))^{m}},

which gives

(3.4)

K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\frac{\sqrt[m]{\lambda}}{\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}.

Conversely, given an
m
-homogeneous polynomial
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}v_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
, where
v_{\alpha}\in X
and
z\in\ell^{n}_{q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert v_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert v_{\alpha}\right\rVert z^{\alpha}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert v_{\alpha}\right\rVert z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)}

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}v_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)}.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{(K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda))^{m}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\cdot\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)),

and consequently shows that

(3.5)

K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\geq\frac{\sqrt[m]{\lambda}}{\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}.

By the virtue of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain our desired equality (
3.3
). This completes the proof.
∎

The following result demonstrates a crucial link between the Bohr radius and the unconditional basis constant of spaces of vector-valued homogeneous polynomials.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a finite dimensional complex Banach space and the canonical basis
\{e_{j}:1\leq j\leq n\}
has unconditional basis constant
\chi(\{e_{j}\}^{n}_{j=1})=1
. Then for any
\lambda\geq 1
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
we have

\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{\sqrt[m]{\lambda}}{\underset{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\min\left\{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda),\frac{\sqrt[m]{\lambda}}{\underset{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}\right\}.

Proof.
The proof of the second inequality is just a consequence of the facts that

K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
for all
n
and all
\lambda\geq 1
, and for all
m

K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq K^{n}_{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt[m]{\lambda}}{\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}.

Conversely, for the first inequality, let us assume that
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
be a bounded holomorphic function on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
with values in
X
. We want to prove first that for all
m
and
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
,

(3.6)

\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)}\leq 2(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert).

Given such
m
and
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, we define for
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
,

g(\zeta):=f(\zeta z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\zeta^{|\alpha|}=x_{0}(f)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right)\zeta^{m}.

Then,
g
is a vector-valued holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
with
m
-th coefficient
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
and
\left\lVert g(\zeta)\right\rVert\leq 1
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. For any
\phi\in X^{*}
with
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq 1
, let us consider the function
\phi\circ g:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}
defined by

(\phi\circ g)(u)=\phi(x_{0}(f))+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\phi(x_{\alpha}(f))z^{\alpha}\right)u^{m}.

Clearly,
\phi\circ g
is holomorphic function on open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathbb{C}
with the
m
-th coefficient

\phi\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\phi(x_{\alpha}(f))z^{\alpha}

and
|\phi(g(u))|\leq 1
on
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore,
\text{Re}\,(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-e^{i\theta}\phi(g))\geq 0
, where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
e^{i\theta}\phi(g(0))=|\phi(g(0))|
for all
\phi\in B_{X^{*}}
. Using the Carathéodory’s inequality
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\bigg|\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\phi(x_{\alpha}(f))z^{\alpha}\bigg|\leq 2\,\text{Re}\,(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-e^{i\theta}\phi(g(0)))=2(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-|\phi(x_{0}(f))|)

holds for any
\phi\in B_{X^{*}}
. Therefore, it follows that

\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq 2\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert\right).

Hence, in view of Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that for all
m\geq 1
and all
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/m}z}{3\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{3^{m}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{3^{m}}\cdot 2\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert\right).

So, for each

z\in\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/m}}{3\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}\right)B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}

we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert=\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert+2\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert-\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert\right)\lambda\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{m}}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert,

which shows that

\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{\lambda^{1/m}}{\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))}}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Estimate for Bohr radius

The following lemma is required in order to prove our main result. It is a consequence of a classical result by Wiener
[
15
, Lemma 8.4, p. 183]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
be a bounded holomorphic functions with values in a complex Banach space
X
such that
\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}\leq 1.
Then the following holds

\sup_{z\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\widetilde{S}(m,n)(1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{2})

for
m\geq 1
.

Proof.
Let
z\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
be fixed. For
u\in\mathbb{D}
, we consider the function

g(u):=f(uz)=x_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right)u^{m}.

Then
g
is a holomorphic function on unit disk
\mathbb{D}
into the complex Banach space
X
with the
m
-th coefficient
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
and
\left\lVert g(u)\right\rVert\leq 1
for all
u\in\mathbb{D}
. Consequently, we note that for any
\phi\in X^{*}
with
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq 1
, the function
\phi\circ g:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is holomorphic with the
m
-th coefficient

\phi\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\phi(x_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}.

Therefore, by the virtue of the Wiener’s result
[
15
, Lemma 8.4, p. 183]
, we obtain

\bigg|\phi\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right)\bigg|\leq 1-|\phi(x_{0})|^{2}

holds for any
\phi\in B_{X^{*}}
. Hence, it shows that

\sup_{z\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq 1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}.

Using the Definition
2.2
, it follows that

\sup_{z\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\widetilde{S}(m,n)\sup_{z\in\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\widetilde{S}(m,n)(1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{2}).

This completes the proof.
∎

By the virtue of Corollary
2.8
, we obtain for
n\geq 2
the function

(4.2)

\widetilde{H_{n}}(x):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)x^{m}

is well-defined and differentiable in
(-1,1)
, and so in
[0,1)
. Analogously to Lemma
2.2
, we can say that
\widetilde{H_{n}}
is strictly increasing on
[0,1)
and
\widetilde{H_{n}}(x)>x
for all
x\in(0,1)
, whereas
\widetilde{H_{n}}(0)=0
. By intermediate mean value theorem, for every
M>0
, there exists a unique
R\in(0,1)
such that
\widetilde{H_{n}}(R)=M
. Hence, for any
1\leq\lambda<\infty
, by considering
M=\lambda/2
, the following definition makes sense.

Definition 4.1
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq\lambda<\infty
, we denote by
\gamma_{n}\in(0,1)
the unique solution of the following equation:

(4.3)

x+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)x^{m}=\frac{\lambda}{2}.

Next, we prove our main theorem which shows that
\gamma_{n}
is a lower bound of Bohr radius
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
and it is better that
\beta_{n}
. Further, we prove that our lower estimate is the improved bound than that of from
[
19
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be any finite dimensional complex Banach space and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
. For
\lambda>1
and
1\leq q<\infty
suppose
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
is the
n
-dimensional Bohr radius and the numbers
\beta_{n}
,
\gamma_{n}
that had been defined by (
2.3
) and (
4.3
). Then we have

\beta_{n}<\gamma_{n}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\frac{\gamma_{n}}{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}.

Proof.
In view of Corollary
2.8
, we obtain
\widetilde{H_{n}}(x)\leq H_{n}(x)
for all
x\in(0,1)
, where
H_{n}
and
\widetilde{H_{n}}
are defined by (
2.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively. We observe that

H_{n}(\beta_{n})=\frac{\lambda}{2}=\widetilde{H_{n}}(\gamma_{n})<H_{n}(\gamma_{n}).

Since
H_{n}
is strictly monotonic (due to Lemma
2.2
) for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that
\beta_{n}<\gamma_{n}
.

Next we want to prove that
\gamma_{n}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
. Without loss of generality, let us assume that
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
is a bounded holomorphic function defined on unit ball of
\ell^{n}_{q}
space with

\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}\leq 1.

We choose a point
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
with
|z_{k}|\leq\gamma_{n}
for all
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
By the virtue of Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\gamma_{n}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\right\rVert|z^{\alpha}|\right)\left(\gamma_{n}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{2})\widetilde{S}(m,n)\gamma^{m}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert+(1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{2})\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)\gamma^{m}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert+(1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{2})\cdot\frac{\lambda}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert+2(1-\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert)\cdot\frac{\lambda}{2}=\lambda.

Therefore, it follows from the definition of
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
that
\gamma_{n}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
.

Next our aim is to show that
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\gamma_{n}/K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
. To prove this, we first fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
. On studying the related research on Bohr radius for vector-valued holomorphic functions, the authors in
[
5
]
have proved that
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left(K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)\right)^{1/q}
for every
q\in[1,\infty)
.
It is worth noting that if
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)=(K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda))^{1/q}
, then there is noting to prove because we obtain the exact the value of
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
in this case. Hence, we consider
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)<(K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda))^{1/q}
for
1\leq q<\infty
to avoid such trivial situation. Let us fix

\delta\in\left(\frac{1}{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)^{1/q}},\frac{1}{K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)}\right).

Then we have
\delta>1/K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)^{1/q}
. We assume
\zeta=\delta^{q}
for our convenience. An easy computation shows that

\frac{1}{\zeta}<1-\frac{\frac{1}{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}-1}{\zeta},

and thus we can choose

(4.4)

r\in\left(\frac{1}{\zeta},1-\frac{\frac{1}{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}-1}{\zeta}\right).

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the space
\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
is finite dimensional, so we can find
Q_{m}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
satisfying
\left\lVert Q_{m}\right\rVert_{\infty}=1
and
\left\lVert Q_{m}\right\rVert_{1}=\widetilde{S}(m,n)
.
For each
\phi\in X^{*}
with
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq 1
, the series

(4.5)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{m}}{2}Q_{m}(z),\quad r\in(0,1),

converges absolutely and uniformly on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. Therefore, the series expansion (
4.5
) defines a holomorphic function
D(z)
for
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. Further, we observe that
\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left\lVert\phi(Q_{m}(z))\right\rVert_{\infty}=1.
Indeed, we obtain the following:

\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}|\phi(D(z))|\leq\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{m}}{2}\left\lVert\phi(Q_{m}(z))\right\rVert_{\infty}=\frac{r}{2(1-r)}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\,z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}

gives

\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{r}{2(1-r)}.

Let
D(z):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
be the monomial series expansion of
D
on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. Let us denote
R_{n}:=\zeta\gamma_{n}
. It is easy to observe that
0<R_{n}<\zeta K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
. In view of (
4.4
), we have
\zeta r>1
and

\zeta>\frac{\frac{1}{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}-1}{1-r}.

Therefore, for any
z\in\zeta\gamma\partial B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert(\zeta\gamma_{n})^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(\zeta\gamma_{n})^{m}\left\lVert Q_{m}\right\rVert_{1}r^{m}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{m}\gamma^{m}_{n}r^{m}\widetilde{S}(m,n)

\displaystyle=\zeta r\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)(\gamma_{n})^{m}(\zeta r)^{m-1}

\displaystyle>\zeta r\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)(\gamma_{n})^{m}

\displaystyle=\zeta r\cdot\frac{\lambda}{2}>\frac{\frac{1}{K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}-1}{1-r}\cdot\frac{r\lambda}{2}>\frac{r\lambda}{2(1-r)}\geq\lambda\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}.

In view of the definition of
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
, it follows that
\zeta\gamma_{n}=R_{n}\geq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
. Now, letting
\zeta\rightarrow(1/K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda))^{+}
, we obtain that
K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\gamma_{n}/K(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, our final goal is to show that for every
n\geq 2
,
\gamma_{n}
is an improved lower bound than that from
[
19
]
. From the definition of unconditional basis constant of homogeneous polynomials
\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))
, it is evident that

\chi_{\text{mon}}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X))=\widetilde{S}(m,n).

Let us define for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.6)

\Gamma_{n}:=\sup\left\{\widetilde{S}(m,n)^{1/m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we have
\sqrt[m]{\lambda}/(3\Gamma_{n})\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
,
\lambda\geq 1
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
X
be a finite dimensional complex Banach space and
\lambda>1
. Then, for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
we have

C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}<\gamma_{n}\leq K^{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda),

where
C
being the constant depending only on
\lambda
and
X
.

Proof.
The right hand inequality has already been proved in Theorem
4.1
. To prove the left hand inequality, let us fix
n\geq 2
. Firstly, our aim is to show that there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\widetilde{S}(m,n)^{1/m}<\Gamma_{n},\quad n\geq 2,

where
\Gamma_{n}
is given by (
4.6
). Indeed, if this is not true, we suppose that
\widetilde{S}(m,n)=\Gamma^{m}_{n}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. For the case
m=1
, we want to show that
\widetilde{S}(1,n)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, from the definition of
\widetilde{S}(m,n)
, we have already that
\widetilde{S}(1,n)\geq 1
. Let
Q(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}z_{j}
be a
1
-homogeneous polynomial with
x_{j}\in X
and
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\ell^{n}_{q}
. For every
\phi\in B_{X^{*}}
, consider the function

(\phi\circ Q)(z)=\phi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}z_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi(x_{j})z_{j}.

Let
\theta_{j}\in[0,2\pi]
be such that
|\phi(x_{j})z_{j}|=\phi(x_{j})z_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Now, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{j}z_{j}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\phi(x_{j})z_{j}|=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi(x_{j})z_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\phi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}z_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}\right)=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\phi\left(Q(z_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,z_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}})\right)\leq\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Therefore, it follows that
\widetilde{S}(1,n)\leq 1
. Consequently,
\Gamma_{n}=1
, and so
\widetilde{S}(m,n)=1
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, which is a contradiction. Hence, we have proved our claim.

The function
\widetilde{H_{n}}(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)x^{m}
satisfies that
\widetilde{H_{n}}(\gamma_{n})=\lambda/2
, and

\widetilde{H_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{3\Gamma_{n}}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{S}(m,n)\cdot\frac{1}{3^{m}\Gamma^{m}_{n}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{S}(m,n)}{\Gamma^{m}_{n}}\cdot\frac{1}{3^{m}}<\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{m}}=\frac{1}{2}<\frac{\lambda}{2}=\widetilde{H_{n}}(\gamma_{n}).

Since
\widetilde{H_{n}}
is strictly increasing, we obtain
1/(3\Gamma_{n})<\gamma_{n}
. Finally, in view of the result
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have

C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}\leq\frac{1}{3\Gamma_{n}},

where
C
depends on
X
and
\lambda
. This completes the proof.
∎

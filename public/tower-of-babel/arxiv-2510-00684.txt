A unified framework for Bohr-type inequalities using multiple Schwarz functions
Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. This paper introduces a unified framework for Bohr-type inequalities by incorporating multiple Schwarz functions into the majorant series for K K -quasiconformal harmonic mappings in the unit disk 𝔻 := { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} . In this study, we establish several improved and refined versions of the Bohr inequality that generalize and interconnect numerous known results. Our approach not only systematically recovers the existing theorems as special cases but also generates new results that are inaccessible through single-function methods.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
H_{\infty}
denote the collection of analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, characterized by the supremum norm
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|<\infty
.
For a function
f\in H_{\infty}
represented by the Taylor series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.1)

In this context, the quantity
1/3
is referred to as the Bohr radius, which cannot be improved. Inequality (
1.1
) is known as the classical Bohr inequality.
Indeed, Bohr
[
11
]
proved that inequality (
1.1
) holds for
r\leq 1/6
. Subsequently, Schur, Wiener, and Riesz independently proved that the inequality (
1.1
) holds for
r\leq 1/3
. The proofs of Bohr and Wiener can be found in
[
11
]
.

In addition to the Bohr radius, the Rogosinski radius is another important concept (see
[
35
]
).
Building upon the concept of the Rogosinski radius, Kayumov
et al.
[
21
]
introduced the Bohr-Rogosinski radius, defined as the largest value
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that the inequality
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
holds for
|z|<r_{0}
, where

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n},\quad N\in\mathbb{N}

for the analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Kayumov
et al.
[
21
]
have established several results concerning the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

Before proceeding with the discussion, it is necessary to establish certain notations.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{f\in H_{\infty}:\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{B}:f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=f^{(m-1)}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad f^{(m)}(0)\not=0\right\},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
for
0<r<1
. For an analytic function
h
in
\mathbb{D}
, let
S_{r}(h)
denote the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}(h)=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

If
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then it is well-known that
S_{r}(h)/\pi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}
. Moreover, when
h
is univalent in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the expression
S_{r}(h)
corresponds to the area of the image
h(\mathbb{D}_{r})
. In addition, if
f(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, then we say that
f
is quasi-subordinate to
h
, denoted by
f(z)\prec_{q}h(z)
in
\mathbb{D}
, if there exist analytic functions
\phi
and
\omega
in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
\omega(0)=0
,
|\omega(z)|<1
, and
|\phi(z)|\leq 1
for
|z|<1
such that
f(z)=\phi(z)h(\omega(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
(see
[
34
]
).

In 2018, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
22
]
obtained several improved versions of Bohr’s inequality for bounded analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Here, we recall one such version.

Theorem A
.

[
22
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
S_{r}
denote the area of the image of the subdisk
|z|<r
under mapping
f
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}(f)}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

The numbers
1/3
and
16/9
cannot be improved further.

Following the foundational work of Kayumov
et al.

[
21
,
22
]
, a significant number of authors have investigated various modifications to Bohr-type inequalities (see
[
2
,
30
,
21
]
).

Consider the function
f=u+iv
, which is complex-valued and defined in terms of
z=x+iy
within a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If the function
f
is twice
continuously differentiable and satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
in
\Omega
, then
f
is harmonic in
\Omega
. The derivatives are defined as
f_{z}=(f_{x}-if_{y})/2
and
f_{\overline{z}}=(f_{x}+if_{y})/2
. It is important to note that every
harmonic mapping
f
can be expressed in its canonical form as
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\Omega
and are referred to as the analytic and co-analytic parts of

f
, respectively. This representation is unique up to an additive constant (see
[
14
]
). The inverse function theorem, along with Lewy’s result
[
26
]
, establishes that a
harmonic function
f
is locally univalent in
\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian of
f
, given by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
, is nonzero in
\Omega
. A harmonic mapping
f
is
locally univalent and sense-preserving in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
, or equivalently, if
h^{\prime}\neq 0
in
\Omega
and the dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
of
f
satisfies the condition
|\omega|\leq 1
in
\Omega
(see
[
26
]
).

If a locally univalent and sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
satisfies the condition
|g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)|\leq k<1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f
is called a
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
(see
[
20
,
32
]
). It is evident that as
k\to 1
, the limiting case corresponds to
K\to\infty
.

In 2018, Kayumov
et al.

[
24
]
advanced the classical Bohr theorem by introducing a harmonic extension, which led to the following insights.

Theorem B
.

[
24
]
Consider the function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
, which is a sense-preserving and
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{K+1}{5K+1}.

The value
(K+1)/(5K+1)
is optimal and cannot be improved. Furthermore,

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{K+1}{3K+1}.

The number
(K+1)/(3K+1)
cannot be improved.

Theorem C
.

[
24
]
Consider the function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
, which represents a sense-preserving and
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq r_{K},

where
r_{K}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{r}{1-r}+\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)r^{2}\sqrt{\frac{1+r^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{3}}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1}=\frac{1}{2}.

The number
r_{K}
cannot be replaced by any number greater than
R=R(K)
, where
R
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{4R}{1-R}\left(\frac{K}{K+1}\right)+2\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)\log(1-R)=1.

Additionally, several authors have studied other aspects of Bohr’s inequality.
We refer to
[
4
,
1
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
29
,
31
,
33
]
and the references listed therein for an in-depth investigation on the Bohr radius.

The aforementioned results show a development from classical settings to complex function classes. In this study, we demonstrate that the simultaneous incorporation of
multiple Schwarz functions creates a powerful, comprehensive framework. This approach not only allows us to recover the aforementioned theorems as special cases but also yields
new, sharper results that are inaccessible through single-function methods. This brings us to the following central questions of this paper:

Question 1.1
.

Can the theory of Bohr-type inequalities for harmonic mappings be systematically extended through the simultaneous incorporation of multiple Schwarz functions, namely
\omega_{p}(z)
,
\omega_{m}(z)
and
\omega_{q}(z)
, for the analytic and co-analytic parts?

Question 1.2
.

Is it possible to not only generalize but also sharpen Theorem C by obtaining a single equation for the optimal Bohr radius that improves upon the original two-sided bound?

The purpose of this paper is primarily to provide affirmative answers to Questions
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Key lemmas

The following lemmas are required to prove our main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
25
, Pick’s invariant form of Schwarz lemma]
If
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\frac{|f(0)|+|z|}{1+|f(0)||z|}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
13
]
For an analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad{and}\quad|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}\;\;\text{for}\quad n\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|z|<1.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
24
]
Let
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
be two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|g’(z)|\leq k|h’(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
for some
k\in[0,1)
. If
|h(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}r^{n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{n}\;\;\text{for}\;\;|z|=r<1.

Using the concept of quasi-subordination and the result derived in
[
1
]
, Liu
et al.

[
28
]
established the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
28
, Proof of Theorem 2]
Let
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
be two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|g’(z)|\leq k|zh’(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
for
k\in[0,1)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|r^{n-1}\leq k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|r^{n}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq 1/3.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
27
]
Let
f
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\text{sgn}(t)\sum_{n=1}^{t}|a_{n}|^{2}\frac{r^{N}}{1-r}+\left(\frac{1}{1+a_{0}}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=t+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})r^{N}}{1-r}

for
r\in[0,1)
, where
t=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor
and
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the largest integer not exceeding the real number
x
.

3.
Main results

In the subsequent result, we obtain a sharp version of Theorem C in the context of harmonic mappings, incorporating multiple Schwarz functions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
be a sense-preserving,
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, and
\omega_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
for
m\geq 1
. If
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for $r\leq\min\left\{r_{p,m,k},\;1/\sqrt[m]{3}\right\}$,}

where

r_{p,m,k}\in(0,1)
is the smallest positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+2k\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)-1=0,

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. If
r_{p,m,k}\leq 1/\sqrt[m]{3}
, then the number
r_{p,m,k}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

For simplicity, let
\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}
for
n\geq 1
.
Since
f
is locally univalent and
K
-quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
with
g^{\prime}(0)=b_{1}=0
, the classical Schwarz Lemma gives that the dilatation

\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|\omega(z)|\leq k|z|
,
i.e.
,
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|zh^{\prime}(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
, where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)\in[0,1)
.
In view of Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|r^{n-1}\leq k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq k(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nr^{n}=k(1-a^{2})\frac{r}{(1-r)^{2}}

(3.1)

for
|z|=r\leq 1/3
, where
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1)
. Integrate (
3.1
) on
[0,r]
, we have

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq k(1-a^{2})\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x}{(1-x)^{2}}dx=k(1-a^{2})\left(\frac{r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)\right)\;\;\text{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(3.2)

It is evident that
H_{1}(r)=r/(1-r)+\log(1-r)
is a monotonically increasing functions of
r
and it follows that
H_{1}(r)\geq H_{1}(0)=0
. As
\omega_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
, in view of the classical Schwarz Lemma, we have
|\omega_{m}(z)|\leq|z|^{m}
.
Using (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{mn}\leq k(1-a^{2})\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)\;\;\text{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt[m]{3}}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{pn}\leq(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a+(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+k(1-a^{2})\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+(1-a)H_{2}(a,r),

where

\displaystyle H_{2}(a,r)=\frac{(1+a)r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+k(1+a)\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)-1

and the first inequality holds for any
r\leq 1/\sqrt[m]{3}
.
Differentiating
H_{2}(a,r)
partially with respect to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}H_{2}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+k\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)\geq 0.

This shows that
H_{2}(a,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a\in[0,1)
and

\displaystyle H_{2}(a,r)\leq\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}H_{2}(a,r)=\frac{2r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+2k\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)-1\leq 0

for
r\leq r_{p,m,k}
, where
r_{p,m,k}\in(0,1)
is the smallest positive root of the equation

\displaystyle G_{p,m,k}(r):=\frac{2r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+2k\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)-1=0,

(3.3)

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}||\omega_{m}(z)|^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}~r\leq\min\left\{1/\sqrt[m]{3},\;r_{p,m,k}\right\}.

We show that if
r_{p,m,k}\leq 1/\sqrt[m]{3}
, then the number
r_{p,m,k}
cannot be improved.
Consider the function
f_{0}(z)=h_{0}(z)+\overline{g_{0}(z)}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle h_{0}(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+az}=A_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{0}^{\prime}(z)=\mu kzh_{4}^{\prime}(z)

with
\omega_{m}(z)=z^{m}
for
m\geq 1
, where
A_{0}=a
,
A_{n}=(1-a^{2})(-a)^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
,
a\in[0,1)
,
|\mu|=1
and
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. If
g_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}B_{n}z^{n}
, then

\displaystyle B_{n}=k\mu\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)(1-a^{2})(-a)^{n-2}\quad\text{ for}\quad n\geq 2.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|A_{n}|r^{pn}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|B_{n}|r^{mn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+(1-a^{2})r^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ar^{p})^{n-1}+(1-a^{2})kr^{2m}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{n-1}{n}(ar^{m})^{n-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{1-ar^{p}}+(1-a^{2})k\frac{ar^{m}+(1-ar^{m})\log\left(1-ar^{m}\right)}{a^{2}\left(1-ar^{m}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+(1-a)H_{3}(a,r),

where

\displaystyle H_{3}(a,r)=\frac{(1+a)r^{p}}{1-ar^{p}}+(1+a)k\frac{ar^{m}+(1-ar^{m})\log\left(1-ar^{m}\right)}{a^{2}\left(1-ar^{m}\right)}-1.

It is evident that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}H_{3}(a,r)=\frac{2r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+2k\left(\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\log(1-r^{m})\right)-1>0

for
r>r_{p,m,k}
, where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. This shows that the number
r_{p,m,k}
cannot be improved.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Whenever
m\geq p
, then
r_{p,m,k}
is the unique positive root of equation (
3.3
), and
r_{p,m,k}\leq 1/\sqrt[m]{3}
.
It is evident that

\displaystyle G_{p,m,k}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2\left(km\;r^{2m-1}\left(1-r^{p}\right)^{2}+p\left(1-r^{m}\right)^{2}r^{p-1}\right)}{\left(1-r^{m}\right)^{2}\left(1-r^{p}\right)^{2}}\geq 0\quad\text{for $r\in[0,1)$, }

which shows that
G_{p,m,k}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r
with
G_{p,m,k}(0)=-1
and

\displaystyle G_{p,m,k}(1/\sqrt[m]{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{p/m}}{1-\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{p/m}}-1\right)+2k(1/2+\log(2/3))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\left(\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{p/m}-\frac{1}{3}\right)}{1-\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{p/m}}+2k(1/2+\log(2/3)).

Note that
(1/3)^{p/m}\geq(1/3)
implies
(1/3)^{p}\geq(1/3)^{m}
and the function
a^{x}=\exp(x\log(a))
is a strictly decreasing function of
x
for
0<a<1
, thus, we have
p\leq m
. Therefore,
G_{p,m,k}(1/\sqrt[m]{3})>0
for
m\geq p
.
Therefore,
r_{p,m,k}\in\left(0,1/\sqrt[m]{3}\right)
is the unique root of the equation (
3.3
), whenever
m\geq p
.

Remark 3.2
.

We discuss some special cases of Theorem
3.1
as well as several useful observations.

(i)

By setting
p=m=1
and
\omega_{1}(z)=z
in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the sharp version of Theorem C, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for $r\leq r_{0}$,}

where

r_{0}\in(0,1/3)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{2r}{1-r}+\frac{2(K-1)}{(K+1)}\left(\frac{r}{1-r}+\log(1-r)\right)-1=0.

The number
r_{0}
is sharp. This provides a conclusive answer to Question
1.2
. The radius

is now characterized by a single equation, which simplifies the two-sided estimate found in the original Theorem C.

(ii)

By setting
p=1
and allowing

in Theorem
3.1
, we derive the classical Bohr inequality. This demonstrates that our result on harmonic mapping inherently includes the classical Bohr inequality as a limiting case, thereby connecting the two significant research domains.

\displaystyle+\mu\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{m}(z)|^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+k(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+\mu(1-a^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nr^{2mn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+k(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}+\mu(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{r^{2m}}{(1-r^{2m})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+F_{5}(a,r)

where

\displaystyle F_{5}(a,r)=\frac{(1-a^{2})}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{2(k+1)r^{p}}{1-r^{p}}-1\right)+2\mu(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{2m}}{(1-r^{2m})^{2}}-\frac{2}{1+a}\right),

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
.
Differentiating partially
F_{5}(a,r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F_{5}(a,r)=\frac{(1-a^{2})(k+1)pr^{p-1}}{(1-r^{p})^{2}}+\lambda(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{2mr^{2m-1}(1+r^{2m})}{(1-r^{2m})^{3}}\geq 0,

which shows that
F_{5}(a,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r\in[0,1)
and hence, we have
F_{5}(a,r)\leq F_{5}(a,r_{p,k})
for
r\leq r_{p,k}
, where
r_{p,k}\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation
(2k+3)r^{p}-1=0
.
Evidently,
F_{5}(a,r_{p,k})=(1-a^{2})F_{6}(a)/2
, where

\displaystyle F_{6}(a)=1+2\mu(1-a^{2})\frac{r_{p,k}^{2m}}{\left(1-r_{p,k}^{2m}\right)^{2}}-\frac{2}{1+a}.

Note that

\displaystyle F_{6}(0)=2\mu\frac{r_{p,k}^{2m}}{\left(1-r_{p,k}^{2m}\right)^{2}}-1\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}F_{6}(a)=0.

Differentiating
F_{6}(a)
with respect to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{6}^{\prime}(a)=\frac{2}{(1+a)^{2}}\left(1-2\mu a(1+a)^{2}\frac{r_{p,k}^{2m}}{\left(1-r_{p,k}^{2m}\right)^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{2}{(1+a)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{8\mu r_{p,k}^{2m}}{\left(1-r_{p,k}^{2m}\right)^{2}}\right)\geq 0

for
\mu\leq(1-r_{p,k}^{2m})^{2}/(8r_{p,k}^{2m})
. Therefore,
F_{6}(a)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a\in[0,1)
, whenever
\mu\leq(1-r_{p,k}^{2m})^{2}/(8r_{p,k}^{2m})
. Hence, we
have
F_{6}(a)\leq\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}F_{6}(a)=0
for
a\in[0,1)
and
\mu\leq(1-r_{p,k}^{2m})^{2}/(8r_{p,k}^{2m})
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}||\omega_{p}(z)|^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{|\omega_{p}(z)|}{1-|\omega_{p}(z)|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{p}(z)|^{2n}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-r_{p,k}^{2m})^{2}}{8r_{p,k}^{2m}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}|\omega_{m}(z)|^{2n}\leq 1

for
r\leq r_{p,k}
, where
r_{p,k}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation
(2k+3)r^{p}-1=0
.

To prove the sharpness of the radius
r_{p,k}
, we construct an extremal function that attains the given bounds.
Consider the function
f_{3}(z)=h_{3}(z)+\overline{g_{3}(z)}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle h_{3}(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+az}=A_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{3}(z)=\lambda k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}

with
\omega_{p}(z)=z^{p}
for
p\geq 1
, where
A_{0}=a\in[0,1)
,
A_{n}=(1-a^{2})(-a)^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
,
|\lambda|=1
and
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|A_{n}||r^{p}|^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|k\lambda A_{n}||r^{p}|^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|A_{0}|}+\frac{|r^{p}|}{1-|r^{p}|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|^{2}|r^{p}|^{2n}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(1-r_{p,k}^{2m}\right)^{2}}{8r_{p,k}^{2m}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|A_{n}|^{2}|r^{m}|^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+\frac{(1+k)(1-a^{2})}{a}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ar^{p})^{n}+\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}(1+ar^{p})r^{2p}}{(1+a)(1-r^{p})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(ar^{p})^{2(n-1)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(1-r_{p,k}^{2m}\right)^{2}}{8r_{p,k}^{2m}}(1-a^{2})^{2}r^{2m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n(ar^{m})^{2(n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+\frac{(1+k)(1-a^{2})r^{p}}{1-ar^{p}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})(1-a)(1+ar^{p})r^{2p}}{(1-r^{p})(1-a^{2}r^{2p})}+\frac{\left((1-a^{2})(1-r_{p,k}^{2m})r^{m}\right)^{2}}{8r_{p,k}^{2m}(1-a^{2}r^{2m})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+(1-a)F_{7}(a,r),

where

\displaystyle F_{7}(a,r)=\frac{(1+k)(1+a)r^{p}}{1-ar^{p}}+\frac{(1-a^{2})r^{2p}}{(1-r^{p})(1-ar^{p})}+\frac{(1-a^{2})(1+a)(1-r_{p}^{2m})^{2}r^{2m}}{8r_{p}^{2m}(1-a^{2}r^{2m})^{2}}-1.

Differentiating
F_{7}(a,r)
partially with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F_{7}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+a)(1+k)p\;r^{p-1}}{(1-ar^{p})^{2}}+(1-a^{2})\frac{pr^{2p-1}\left(2-(a+1)r^{p}\right)}{\left(1-r^{p}\right)^{2}\left(1-ar^{p}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2m(1-a^{2})(1+a)(1-r_{p}^{2m})^{2}\left(1+a^{2}r^{2m}\right)r^{2m-1}}{8r_{p}^{2m}\left(1-a^{2}r^{2m}\right)^{3}}>0

for
r\in(0,1)
. Therefore,
F_{7}(a,r)
is a strictly increasing function of
r\in(0,1)
. Therefore, for
r>r_{p}
, we have
F_{7}(a,r)>F_{7}(a,r_{p})
. It is evident that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}F_{7}(a,r_{p})=\frac{2(1+k)r_{p}^{p}}{1-r_{p}^{p}}-1=0.

This shows that the numbers
r_{p}
and
(1-r_{p}^{2m})^{2}/(8r_{p}^{2m})
are the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

In this study, we affirmatively answered Questions

3.2

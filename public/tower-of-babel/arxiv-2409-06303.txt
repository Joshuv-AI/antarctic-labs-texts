Lagrangians and vector bundles

3
Definition of S-dual

3.1
Coulomb branches

Let us quickly recall the definition
of Coulomb branches of 3d
\mathcal{N}=4

supersymmetric gauge theories associated
with a complex reductive group
\mathbf{G}

and a
\mathbf{G}
-space
\mathbf{M}
.
See an exposition
[
Nak22
]
for further information.
In
[
BFN18
]
we define the Coulomb branch
for
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{M})
under the assumption
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{N}\oplus\mathbf{N}^{*}
with a representation

\mathbf{N}
of
\mathbf{G}
. However, the actual assumption we need is
\mathbf{M}=T^{*}\mathbf{N}
for
a smooth affine algebraic variety
\mathbf{N}
.

Let
F={\mathbb{C}}((z))
and
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
be
the field of formal Laurent power series and
the formal power series ring respectively.

Let
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}
be the affine Grassmannian of
\mathbf{G}
, i.e.
\mathbf{G}(F)/\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})
. We define the
variety of triples

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}
by

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}=\left\{[g(z),s(z)]\in\mathbf{G}(F)\times^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})\,\middle|\,g(z)s(z)\in\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})\right\}.

It is equipped with a projection
\pi\colon\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}
. We have an action
of
\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})
by the multiplication to
g(z)
from the left. We consider the equivariant
Borel-Moore homology group
H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})
.
It is equipped with the convolution product, which is commutative.
(The definition of the convolution product uses the smoothness of

\mathbf{N}
, hence it is not
clear
how to generalize the definition
to singular
\mathbf{N}
.)
We define the Coulomb branch by

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{M})\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}).

Remark 3.1
.

We need a justification for the notation
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{M})
,
as the above definition uses
\mathbf{N}
, rather than
M=T^{*}\mathbf{N}
. At least when
\mathbf{N}
is a representation of
\mathbf{G}
, it was proved that the result depends on
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{N}\oplus\mathbf{N}^{*}
up to
an isomorphism in
[
BFN18
, §6(viii)]
.

Example 3.2
.

(1) Suppose
\mathbf{G}={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
,
\mathbf{M}=0
(hence
\mathbf{N}=0
). Then
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}=\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}
.
It is well-known that
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}\cong\{[z^{n}]\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. Thus

H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathrm{pt})=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}[w]r_{n},

where
r_{n}
is the fundamental class of the point
[z^{n}]
, and wee have used

H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathrm{pt})\cong{\mathbb{C}}[w]
. One can compute
r_{m}r_{n}=r_{m+n}
, hence

H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})={\mathbb{C}}[w,r_{1},r_{-1}]/(r_{1}r_{-1}=1)
. Thus

\mathcal{M}_{C}
is
{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*}=T^{*}({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
.

(2) Suppose
\mathbf{G}={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
,
\mathbf{M}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}=T^{*}{\mathbb{C}}
(hence
\mathbf{N}={\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})={\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
). The
\mathbf{G}
-action on
\mathbf{N}
is the standard weight
1
representation. Then

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}=\bigsqcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\{\left([z^{n}],s^{\prime}(z)\right)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}\times{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\mid z^{-n}s^{\prime}(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\},

where
s^{\prime}(z)=g(z)s(z)
in the definition above. If
n\leq 0
, the condition

z^{-n}s^{\prime}(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
is automatically satisfied. Therefore the fiber is
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
.
On the other hand, the fiber is
z^{n}{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
if
n>0
. Since
z^{n}{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
is a codimension

n
subspace in
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
, the fundamental class of the fiber is
r^{\prime}_{n}=w^{n}r_{n}
compared with
the example above. (We interpret
r_{n}
as the fundamental class of
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
by Thom isomorphism.) On the other hand,
r^{\prime}_{n}=r_{n}
for
n\leq 0
. Therefore we have
w^{1}r_{1}\cdot r_{-1}=w
in this case, hence

H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})={\mathbb{C}}[w,r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{-1}]/(r^{\prime}_{1}r^{\prime}_{-1}=w)={\mathbb{C}}[r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{-1}]
. Thus
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}=T^{*}{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)’ If we replace
\mathbf{M}
by
{\mathbb{C}}^{2\ell}=T^{*}({\mathbb{C}}^{\ell})
, we get

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})={\mathbb{C}}[w,r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{-1}]/(r^{\prime}_{1}r^{\prime}_{-1}=w^{\ell}).

Thus
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is type
A_{\ell-1}
simple singularity.

(2)” If we replace the
\mathbf{G}
action on
\mathbf{N}
by the weight
\ell
representation, we get

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})={\mathbb{C}}[w,r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{-1}]/(r^{\prime}_{1}r^{\prime}_{-1}=w^{\ell})
.
Thus
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is type
A_{\ell-1}
simple singularity.

(3) Suppose
\mathbf{G}={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
,
\mathbf{M}=T^{*}{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
(
\mathbf{N}={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
).
\mathbf{G}
acts on
\mathbf{N}
by
the multiplication. Then

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}=\bigsqcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\{\left([z^{n}],s^{\prime}(z)\right)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}\times\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})\mid z^{-n}s^{\prime}(z)\in\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})\},

Since
\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})=\{s(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\setminus\{0\}\mid 1/s(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\}=\{s(z)=s_{0}+s_{1}z+\dots\mid s_{0}\neq 0\}
, the component for
n\neq 0
is
\emptyset
. The component for

n=0
is
\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O})
. Therefore

H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})\cong H_{*}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathbf{N}(\mathcal{O}))=H_{*}(\mathrm{pt})={\mathbb{C}}.

Thus
\mathcal{M}_{C}=\mathrm{pt}
.

As we see above,
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}
could have many connected components. In fact, we
have
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})=\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})\cong\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})
. In above examples,

\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})={\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}
decomposes according to
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})
,

\mathcal{M}_{C}=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})
is
equipped with the action of
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})^{\wedge}
, the Pontryagin dual of
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})
.

3.2
Ring objects

Let
D_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})
be the derived category of
\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant constructible
sheaves on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}
, more precisely its ind-completion.
It is equipped with a natural monoidal convolution structure
\ast
.
If
\omega_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}}
is the dualizing sheaf of
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}
,
the pushforward
\mathcal{A}\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\pi_{*}(\omega_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}}})
is an object in
D_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})
,
and
H^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{N}})
is naturally isomorphic to

H^{*}_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}},\mathcal{A})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{A}
is a commutative ring object in
D_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})
,
i.e., it is equipped with a homomorphism
\mathcal{A}\ast\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
in
D_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})
.
It is ‘commutative’ in the sense that it is compatible with the factorization given by Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmanian.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\varphi\colon\mathbf{G}_{1}\to\mathbf{G}_{2}
is a group homomorphism and

\mathrm{Gr}\varphi\colon\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}_{2}}
be the corresponding morphism
between affine Grassmannians.

(1)
If
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a commutative ring object in
D_{\mathbf{G}_{2}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}_{2}})
,

\mathrm{Gr}\varphi^{!}\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a commutative ring object in
D_{\mathbf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}})
.

(2)
If
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is a commutative ring object in
D_{\mathbf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}})
,

\mathrm{Gr}\varphi_{*}\mathcal{A}_{1}
is a commutative ring object in
D_{\mathbf{G}_{2}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}_{2}})
.

In particular, if
\Delta\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}\times\mathbf{G}
is the diagonal embedding, commutative ring objects

\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
give rise another commutative ring object
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes^{!}\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathrm{Gr}\Delta^{!}(\mathcal{A}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{2})
.

If
\varphi\colon\mathbf{G}\to\{e\}
is the homomorphism to the trivial group,
\mathrm{Gr}\varphi_{*}(\mathcal{A})
is a commutative
ring object in
D(\mathrm{Gr}_{\{e\}})
. But this derived category is the category of graded vector spaces, hence

\mathrm{Gr}\varphi_{*}(\mathcal{A})=H^{*}_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}},\mathcal{A})
is a commutative ring over
{\mathbb{C}}
equipped with a grading compatible with multiplication.
Hence we can define an affine scheme by
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{*}_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}},\mathcal{A})
. This construction generalizes the definition of Coulomb branches.

3.3
Regular sheaf

Let
\mathrm{Perv}_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})
be the
category of
\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant perverse sheaves
on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}
. It is an abelian category equipped with
a tensor structure given by the convolution
\ast
.
It is the heart of a
t
-structure on
D_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}})
.
Geometric Satake equivalence says that there is an
equivalence of tensor categories

(\mathrm{Perv}_{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbf{G}}),\ast)\cong(\operatorname{\mathscr{R}ep}\mathbf{G}^{\vee},\otimes),

where the right hand side is the tensor category
of finite dimensional representations of the
Langlands dual group
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
. We take ind-completion
in both sides, and consider
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathbf{G}^{\vee}]
the
regular representations of
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
in the right
hand side. Let
\mathcal{A}_{{\mathbb{C}}[\mathbf{G}^{\vee}]}
be the
corresponding object in the left hand side.
We call it the
regular sheaf
.
It is a ring object in the left hand side, and
is equipped with a
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
action, induced
from the
right
multiplication action on
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathbf{G}^{\vee}]
.
Note that the left
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
action is used
in the geometric Satake equivalence, but
the right action remains.
More concretely

\mathcal{A}_{{\mathbb{C}}[\mathbf{G}^{\vee}]}\cong\bigoplus V_{G^{\vee}}(\lambda)^{*}\otimes\operatorname{IC}^{\lambda},

where
V_{G^{\vee}}(\lambda)
is the irreducible
represenation of
G^{\vee}
with highest weight
\lambda
,
and

\operatorname{IC}^{\lambda}
is the intersection
cohomology complex for the orbit

\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})\cdot z^{\lambda}
with
\lambda
viewed
as a coweight of
\mathbf{G}
. The summation runs over
the set of dominant integral coweights of
\mathbf{G}

For example,

In

T^{*}(\operatorname{GL}(V_{i})\times V_{i})&\text{if ${\mathbf{v}}_{i}={\mathbf{v}}_{j}$}\end{cases}\curvearrowleft\operatorname{GL}(V_{j}).\end{split}

,
{\mathbf{v}}_{j}=\dim V_{j}

to the hook partition
({\mathbf{v}}_{i}-{\mathbf{v}}_{j},1^{{\mathbf{v}}_{j}})

of
{\mathbf{v}}_{i}
, as in §
3.3
.
The action of
\operatorname{GL}(V_{i})
is the right multiplication to
the factor
\operatorname{GL}(V_{i})
in
\mathbf{M}_{\times}(V_{i},V_{j})
.
The centralizer of the corresponding
\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
triple
is
\operatorname{GL}(V_{j})
, acting on
\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{v}}_{i}-{\mathbf{v}}_{j},1^{{\mathbf{v}}_{j}})
naturally.
We let it act on the factor
\operatorname{GL}(V_{i})
by the left multiplication.
If
{\mathbf{v}}_{i}<{\mathbf{v}}_{j}
, we exchange the roles of
i
,
i
.
(Strictly speaking, we used a variant of

was used in
[

]
.
But they give an isomorphic hamiltonian space.)

The identification of Coulomb branches with bow varieties were proved without reference to the S-dual, but it follows from
the following:

Conjecture 5.1

Two hamiltonian spaces
\operatorname{GL}(V_{i})\curvearrowright\mathbf{M}_{\medcirc}(V_{i},V_{j})\curvearrowleft\operatorname{GL}(V_{j})

The θ \theta -vacuum from functional renormalisation
Yuepeng Guan, Jan M. Pawlowski, Masatoshi Yamada

Abstract
We study topological properties of a quantum mechanical system with U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) -symmetry within the functional renormalisation group (fRG) approach. These properties include the vacuum energy structure and the topological susceptibility. Our approach works with a complexification of the flow equation, and specifically we embed the original symmetry into the complex plane, U ⁡ ( 1 ) → ℂ U(1)\rightarrow\mathbb{C} . We compute the effective potential of a given topological sector by restricting ourselves to field configurations with a given generalised non-trivial Chern–Simons numbers. The full potential is directly constructed from these sector potentials. Our results compare well with the benchmark results obtained from solving the corresponding Schrödinger equation.



I
Introduction

Topological properties in quantum field theories (QFTs) are determined by the global structure of the system, rather than its local details.
These properties emerge in systems where fields or physical states exhibit non-trivial configurations constrained by topology, leading to a variety of rich physical phenomena.
Notable examples include the Aharonov–Bohm effect
Aharonov:1959fk
, topological defects such as vortices and domain walls
Vilenkin:1984ib
, instanton-mediated tunneling effects
Belavin:1975fg
;
Coleman:1978ae
, and quantum anomalies
tHooft:1976rip
.
These different phenomena highlight that non-perturbative approaches have to accommodate topological properties in a robust way.

A non-perturbative functional approach is provided by the functional renormalisation group (fRG).
It has been used for studying a large variety of non-perturbative phenomena, ranging from low-dimensional statistical systems over quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and beyond the Standard Model particle physics to quantum gravity, for a comprehensive recent review see
Dupuis:2020fhh
.
These applications also incorporate topological phenomena in quantum field theory and quantum mechanics; see e.g.
Reuter:1995tr
;
Reuter:1996be
;
Pawlowski:1996ch
;
VonGersdorff:2000kp
;
Kapoyannis:2000sp
;
Zappala:2001nv
;
Aoki:2002ozs
;
Aoki:2002ax
;
Aoki:2002yt
;
Weyrauch:2006aj
;
Synatschke:2008pv
;
Jakubczyk:2016rvr
;
Krieg_2017
;
Bonanno:2022edf
;
Fukushima:2022zor
;
Bonanno:2025mon
.
However, a comprehensive analysis of the respective dynamics is still lacking, including an evaluation of the systematics in view of topological effects.

This situation asks for a comprehensive analysis of the topological capacity of the fRG within a model theory, where the topological effects are well understood. Here we use
U(1)
-symmetric quantum mechanics, also called the quantum rotor model
Albandea:2024fui
. This model is a one-dimensional QFT that exhibits topological effects through the
\theta
-dependence of its partition function. It has been studied in a recent work
Fukushima:2022zor
, where the fRG has been used in the local potential approximation, observing a lack of topological effects in this approximation.

In the present work, we set up an fRG approach that accommodates topological effects. We show that the standard cutoff term for this model suppresses topological configurations for all cutoff scales, and only integrates out fluctuations. Accordingly, the information of the topological vacuum structure is already encoded in the UV boundary condition rather than being generated by the flow equation directly. In short, it is the appropriate combination of cutoff term and initial effective action that resolves the theory at hand.

U(1)

\displaystyle S[\varphi]=\int_{\tau}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}\dot{\varphi}^{*}\dot{\varphi}-\frac{\theta}{4\pi}(\varphi^{*}\dot{\varphi}-\dot{\varphi}^{*}\varphi)+V(\varphi^{*}\varphi)\right]\,,

(1)

Here,

\displaystyle V(\varphi^{*}\varphi)=\frac{g}{4}(\varphi^{*}\varphi-1)^{2}\,.

(2)

This potential has a global minimum for fields with
\varphi^{*}\varphi=1

The complex field

\displaystyle\varphi=r\,e^{i\vartheta}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle S=\int_{\tau}\left[\frac{m}{2}(\dot{r}^{2}+r^{2}\dot{\vartheta}^{2})-i\frac{\theta}{2\pi}r^{2}\dot{\vartheta}+\frac{g}{4}(r^{2}-1)^{2}\right].

(4)

With

III
Functional renormalisation group approach to
U(1)
-symmetric quantum mechanics

In the present work we use the functional renormalisation group approach for computing the non-perturbative effective potential of
U(1)
-symmetric quantum mechanics. Specifically, we use the flow equation for the quantum effective action, the Wetterich equation
Wetterich:1992yh
, see also
Ellwanger:1993mw
;
Morris:1993qb
. For a recent comprehensive review see
Dupuis:2020fhh
.

In the following, we will consider the Cartesian field
\phi=\varphi
as well as polar coordinates
3
with
\phi=r,\vartheta
.
The respective flow equations for the effective actions
\Gamma_{\phi}
are derived from infrared regularised generating functionals with infrared cutoff terms and current terms for the respective fields. We write

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}[J_{\phi}]=\int{\cal D}\hat{\varphi}\,e^{-S[\hat{\varphi}]+\Delta S_{\phi}[\hat{\phi}]+\int_{\tau}J^{a}_{\phi}\hat{\phi}^{a}}\,,

(17)

where the cutoff term
\Delta S_{\phi}[\hat{\phi}]
is quadratic in the field that is coupled to the source,

\displaystyle\Delta S_{k}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\phi_{a}(-p)\,R^{ab}_{k}(p)\,\phi_{b}(p)\,.

(18)

Hence, the dispersion of the field
\phi
receives an additional contribution
R_{k}(p)
, that can be understood as a momentum-dependent mass term. For this more general framework see
Pawlowski:2005xe
;
Ihssen:2024ihp
.

In the present work we mostly consider the Cartesian field
\hat{\phi}=\hat{\varphi}
, specifically within the numerical applications. However, for the discussion of potential regularisation of the topological degrees of freedom we also consider the polar coordinates
\hat{\phi}[\hat{\varphi}]=(\hat{r},\hat{\vartheta})
.

III.1
Flow equation

For
U(1)
-symmetric quantum mechanics, the flow equation for the effective action, the Legendre transform of
\log Z_{\phi}[J_{\phi}]
, reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{q,p}\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}[\phi]+R_{k}}\right]_{ba}\hskip-5.69046pt(-p,p)\,\partial_{t}R^{ab}_{k}(p)\,.

(19)

In
19
we have dropped the subscript
ϕ
, but it is understood that
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\phi}
differs for different fields coupled to the current. The RG-time
t=\log(k/\Lambda)
is the logarithmic infrared cutoff scale, measured in a reference scale
\Lambda
. In the following, we choose
\Lambda
to be the initial cutoff scale, from which the flow is integrated down to
k=0
. In
19
,
\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}
is the full two-point function,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,ab}^{(2)}(p,q)=\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma_{k}}{\delta\phi^{a}(p)\,\delta\phi^{b}(q)}\,.

(20)

In this setup the complete effective action
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}[\phi]
is given by
\Gamma_{k}[\phi]+\Delta S_{k}[\phi]
and the first part satisfies the flow equation
19
.
The full quantum effective action is obtained by integrating the flow of the effective action from an initial scale
k=\Lambda
to
k=0
, to wit,

\displaystyle\Gamma[\phi]=\Gamma_{\Lambda}[\phi]+\int_{\Lambda}^{0}\frac{\text{d}k}{k}\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}[\phi]\,.

(21)

The approach is completed with an initial condition
\Gamma_{\Lambda}
at an asymptotically large scale, where the theory tends towards the ultraviolet (bare) action,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Lambda\to\infty}[\phi]=S\bigl[\varphi[\phi]\bigr]+\Delta\Gamma_{\textrm{UV}}[\phi]\,.

(22)

In a general theory in
d
dimensions the additional term
\Delta\Gamma_{\textrm{UV}}[\phi]
comprises UV-relevant terms other than that already present in the classical action. Such terms may be generated by the presence of the cutoff term itself. A well-known example is the gluon mass term in QCD, that is generated by the breaking of gauge symmetry in the presence of the cutoff.

III.2
Topology, flows and initial conditions

An additional intricacy for the initial condition emerges in the presence of topological configurations. A showcase relevant for our present study are anomalous
U(1)
-violating terms in QCD (the ’t Hooft determinant) that are generated by the fermionic path integral measure. This case has been studied in
Pawlowski:1996ch
within the fRG. It has been argued there that for chirally-symmetric cutoffs no anomalous contribution to the effective action is generated in the chiral limit if this term is not sourced in the initial effective action. Indeed, the situation is not completely resolved and one of the motivations of the present study is to shed some light into these intricacies. In any case, the study in
Pawlowski:1996ch
suggests separating topological configurations from fluctuations that are the same in each sector. Then, the fluctuations are infrared regularised while the instantons are summed over without any restriction.

III.2.1
Fluctuation field regulators

It is suggestive that the mechanisms mentioned above are also present in the quantum mechanical system under investigation here: For illustration, we first consider a polar decomposition

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{\nu}=\hat{r}\,e^{i\hat{\vartheta}_{\nu}}\,,\qquad\varphi_{\bar{\nu}}=\langle\hat{\varphi}_{\nu}\rangle=r\,e^{i\vartheta_{\bar{\nu}}}\,.

(23)

The angular mean field
\vartheta
may also carry a winding number
\bar{\nu}
, see
8

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\textrm{pol}}[r,\vartheta_{\nu}]=\Delta S_{r}[r]+\Delta S_{\vartheta}[\vartheta_{\nu}]\,,

(24)

with

\displaystyle\Delta S_{r}[r]=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tau}r(\tau)R_{r}(\partial^{2}_{\tau})r(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\vartheta}[\vartheta_{\nu}]=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tau}\vartheta_{\nu}(\tau)R_{\vartheta}(\partial^{2}_{\tau})\vartheta_{\nu}(\tau)\,.

(25)

For the time being, we have used the full angular mode in the cutoff term. With the decomposition
8
the angular part reads

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\vartheta}[\vartheta_{\nu}]=\Delta S_{\vartheta}[\vartheta]+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\nu\beta\,k\,.

(26)

The second term on the right hand side of

comes from the integration of the winding mode squared,
4\pi\nu^{2}\tau^{2}/\beta^{2}
with
R_{\vartheta}(0)=k
. In the following calculation we only consider the limit
\beta\to\infty
and the cutoff term

\varphi

III.2.2
Initial effective action

The discussion in
Section

indicates that the initial effective action may or may not contain topological terms subject to the chosen cutoff. Moreover, specific cutoff choices may also introduce what is known as ’topological freezing’ on the lattice. For the investigation of these intricacies we consider the integro-differential form of the effective action of the Cartesian fields

\displaystyle e^{-\Gamma_{k}[\varphi]}=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\sum_{\nu}\int\mathcal{D}\hat{\varphi}\,e^{-S\left[\hat{\varphi}\,e^{\frac{2\pi i\nu}{\beta}\tau}\right]+\Delta S_{k}[\hat{\varphi}-\varphi]}

III.2.3
Functional flows with topology

In summary, the fRG approach with cutoff terms for the full fields, including the winding part, experiences a “topology freeze” in the limit
\beta\to\infty
: its naïve implementation does not incorporate the different topological sectors as shown in
Fukushima:2022zor
. In turn, for a fixed finite
\beta
, the potential is periodic and the effective potential at
k=0
is flat due to the convexity constraint.

These intricacies are overcome by only regularising the fluctuation fields, leaving the windings unconstrained. The flow equation then follows readily from the integro-differential form of the effective action in
27
. Applying a RG-time derivative to
27
leads us to the standard flow equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}[\varphi]=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}[\varphi]+R_{k}}\partial_{t}R_{k}\right]\,.

(36)

The sum over the topological sector informs the initial condition
32
, and we only consider the limit
\beta\to\infty
with the potential
V_{\textrm{top}}(r^{2};\theta)
given by
33
.

III.3
Flow equation of the effective potential

We use the local potential approximation (LPA), where only quantum corrections for constant fields are considered. The effective action reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}[\varphi]=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\left[\frac{m}{2}\varphi^{*}(-p)p^{2}\varphi(p)\right]+\int_{\tau}V_{k}(\varphi^{*}\varphi)\,,

(37)

and the derivation of the flow equation for the effective potential is deferred to
Appendix

B
. We arrive at

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\frac{\frac{m}{2}\partial_{t}{R}_{k}(p)\left[\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]}{\left[\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}-\left[r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}}\,,

(38)

with

\displaystyle P_{k}(p)=p^{2}+R_{k}(p).

(39)

The results in the present work are obtained with the flat or Litim regulator, which has been shown to be the optimal regulator in LPA, see
Litim:2001up
,

\displaystyle R_{k}(p)=m\,(k^{2}-p^{2})\Theta(k^{2}-p^{2})\,,

(40)

where
\Theta(x)
is the step function,

\displaystyle\Theta(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x\geq 0\,,\\[4.30554pt]
0,&x<0\,.\end{cases}

(41)

Using
40
, we arrive at the following analytic flow equation for the effective potential, see
80
.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{2\,m\,k^{3}\,\left(m\,k^{2}+2V_{k}^{\prime}+2r^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\right)}{(m\,k^{2}+2V_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+4r^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}(m\,k^{2}+2V_{k}^{\prime})}\,.

V
Summary and Conclusion

In the current work, we have studied
U(1)
-symmetric quantum mechanics with the functional renormalisation group.
This analysis served as a benchmark test of the capability of the functional renormalisation group to incorporate topological properties of quantum systems.

Specifically, we have computed the vacuum energy structure within the local potential approximation of the Wetterich flow.
We have compared results obtained with different regulators, the Litim and Callan-Symanzik regulators, with the benchmarks obtained by directly solving the Schrödinger equation.
The fRG results agree very well with the exact ones, showing an increasing, albeit small, error for larger
\theta
-parameters, related to large topological numbers
n
’s.
In our opinion, this is a rather impressive agreement, given the qualitative nature of the LPA-approximation.

We have also computed the topological susceptibility coefficient, which is also agreeing well with the exact results.
Moreover, our relaxed system with
g<\infty
reduces to the quantum rotor in the large coupling limit
g\rightarrow\infty
:
our results show the correct asymptotic behaviour
I_{\rm top}\rightarrow 1/(2\pi)^{2}
as
g\rightarrow\infty
, confirming the consistency of our framework with the expected topological properties of the model.

Figure 10:

with respect to the constraint coupling
g
. The LPA-fRG result (circles) is obtained by the Litim-type flow equation

. The red line is the result from the Schrödinger equation
13
. The dashed line exhibits the value

in topological quantum mechanics.

The asymptotic matching underscores the robustness of our regularisation scheme in preserving topological information under renormalisation group flow, even at strong coupling. This is a critical prerequisite for studying systems like gauge theories where topology is inherently tied to non-perturbative dynamics.

In summary, our work demonstrates the potential of fRG to address topological aspects of quantum systems in a well-controlled but non-trivial benchmark theory. Our results complements those in the anharmonic oscillator in
Bonanno:2025mon
. The analysis in the present work also provides a road map for the application of our findings and techniques to the
\theta
-vacuum structure in gauge theories. While we specifically aim at QCD and respective computations are under way, a first step in this programme is provided by the study of the 2D Schwinger model. We hope to report on results for both cases soon.

Appendix A
Quantum mechanics with topological term

In this Appendix, we summarise the basic properties of quantum mechanics on
S^{1}
, the quantum rotor and its relaxed version. In
Section

A.1
we briefly discuss the quantum rotor with field variables
z\in U(1)
. In

Section

A.2
we relax the
U(1)
-constraint and consider
z\to\varphi\in\mathbbm{C}
.

A.1
Quantum rotor and its topological vacuum structure

The quantum rotor is defined with the classical action

\displaystyle S[z]=\int_{\tau}\left[\frac{m}{2}\dot{z}^{*}\dot{z}-\frac{\theta}{4\pi}(z^{*}\dot{z}-\dot{z}^{*}z)\right]\,,

(51)

where the dot represents the derivative with respect to
\tau
. The field variable
z=e^{i\vartheta}
is an element of
U(1)
.
As shown in
6
, the second term in
51
corresponds to a topological term. The path integral for this system is given by

\displaystyle Z_{z}[J_{z}]=\int\mathcal{D}\hat{z}\,e^{-S_{\theta}[z]+\int_{\tau}\,J\hat{z}}\,,

(52)

where
\mathcal{D}z
is the Haar measure of
U(1)
. The topological term does not affect the classical equations of motion for
z
; however it contributes to the path integral through the summation over field configurations with different boundary conditions
7
which corresponds to
z_{\nu}(\beta)=z_{\nu}(0)\cdot e^{2\pi i\nu}
.
To elucidate this, we define the field configurations
\{z_{\nu}(\tau)\}
as

\displaystyle z_{\nu}(\tau)=e^{i\frac{2\pi\nu}{\beta}\tau}z_{0}(\tau)\,,\quad\text{with}\quad z_{0}(\tau+\beta)=z_{0}(\tau)\,.

(53)

This corresponds to a local gauge transformation where the phase transforms as
\vartheta\to\vartheta+\delta\vartheta_{\nu}
with
\delta\vartheta_{\nu}=\frac{2\pi\nu}{\beta}\tau
.

For vanishing sources, the path integral reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\int[\mathcal{D}z_{\nu}]\,e^{-S[z_{\nu}(\tau),z^{*}_{\nu}(\tau)]}=\mathcal{N}(\beta)\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}m}{\beta}\nu^{2}+i\theta\nu}\,,

(54)

where
\mathcal{N}(\beta)
is a factor independent of
\theta
and
w
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\beta)\equiv\int[\mathcal{D}z_{0}]\,e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,L}=\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2\pi m}}\,,

(55)

carrying the information of kinetic fluctuations.
Applying the Poisson resummation formula,

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}s(\nu)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}x\,s(x)e^{i2\pi nx}\equiv\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}S(n)\,,

(56)

to
54
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(\beta)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2m}\left(n-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\right)^{2}}\,.

(57)

This result takes the form of the partition function for a canonical ensemble, and thus the energy eigenvalues are found to be

\displaystyle E_{n}=\frac{1}{2m}\left(n-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\,.

(58)

Here,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is the quantum number labeling the energy spectrum, arising from the quantisation of angular fluctuations.

A.2
Relaxation to a
U(1)
-symmetric theory

The topological nature
6
arises from the field constraint
z^{*}z=1
.
Now, we relax this condition,

\displaystyle z=e^{i\vartheta}\longrightarrow\varphi=r\,e^{i\vartheta}\,,

(59)

and deal with
\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
as quantum mechanical variables.
We have
\varphi^{*}\varphi=r^{2}\in\mathbbm{R}^{+}
in general, and hence
\varphi\in\mathbbm{C}
.
The respective classical action reads

\displaystyle S[\varphi,\varphi^{*}]=\int_{\tau}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}\dot{\varphi}^{*}\dot{\varphi}-\frac{\theta}{4\pi}(\varphi^{*}\dot{\varphi}-\dot{\varphi}^{*}\varphi)+V(\varphi^{*}\varphi)\right]\,,

(60)

with the potential

\displaystyle V(\varphi^{*}\varphi)=V(r^{2})=\frac{g}{4}(\varphi^{*}\varphi-1)^{2}=\frac{g}{4}(r^{2}-1)^{2}\,,

(61)

with the global minimum at
\varphi^{*}\varphi=r^{2}=1
. The model reduces to the quantum rotor for
g\to\infty
. Then, the potential simply introduces the constraint
\varphi\varphi^{*}=1
. The path integral for this system is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[J_{\varphi}]=\int\mathcal{D}\hat{\varphi}\,e^{-S_{\theta}[\varphi,\varphi^{*}]+\int_{\tau}\,J_{\varphi}\hat{\varphi}}\,,

(62)

see also
32
with
\hat{\phi}=\hat{\varphi}
.
For finite coupling
g
, the quantum rotor with the generating functional
Z_{z}
in
52
is deformed into a
U(1)
-symmetric model with the complex field
\varphi
. In terms of the fields
r
and
\vartheta
, the classical action
60
is given by

\displaystyle S[r,\vartheta]=\int_{\tau}\left[\frac{m}{2}(\dot{r}^{2}+r^{2}\dot{\vartheta}^{2})+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}r^{2}\dot{\vartheta}-\frac{g}{4}(r^{2}-1)^{2}\right]\,.

(63)

The respective Hamiltonian follows with a Legendre transformation from the Lagrangian in
63
as

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2m}\Pi_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2mr^{2}}\left(\Pi_{\vartheta}-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}r^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{g}{4}(r^{2}-1)^{2}\,,

(64)

where we have defined the canonical momenta

\displaystyle\Pi_{r}=m\dot{r},\qquad\Pi_{\vartheta}=mr^{2}\dot{\vartheta}+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}r^{2}\,.

(65)

With canonical quantisation, the canonical momentum operators in polar coordinates are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{r}^{2}=-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)\,,\qquad\qquad\hat{\Pi}_{\vartheta}=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\vartheta}\,,

(66)

and the time-independent Schrödinger equation is given by
13
.

Appendix B
Derivation of the flow equation in the local potential approximation

The effective action in the local potential approximation (LPA) is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}[\varphi]=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\left[\frac{m}{2}\varphi^{*}(-p)p^{2}\varphi(p)\right]+\int_{\tau}V_{k}(\varphi^{*}\varphi)\,,

(67)

where the potential
V_{k}
is the only cutoff-dependent part in the effective action, and
\partial_{t}m=0
. Within a slight abuse of notation we use
\varphi
for both
\varphi(\tau)
and its Fourier transformation
\varphi(p)
with

\displaystyle\varphi(\tau)=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\varphi(p)e^{-ip\tau}\,.

(68)

We proceed with the derivation of the flow equation for the effective action
37
and a generic regulator term for the complex field
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\Delta S_{k}[\varphi]=\frac{m}{2}\int_{p}\varphi^{*}(-p)\,R_{k}(p)\,\varphi(p)\,.

(69)

In the
(\varphi,\varphi^{*})
-basis, the two-point function reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}(p,q)=2\pi\,\delta(p+q)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{\varphi\varphi}&\frac{m}{2}p^{2}+V_{\varphi\varphi^{*}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{m}{2}p^{2}+V_{\varphi^{*}\varphi}&V_{\varphi^{*}\varphi^{*}}\end{array}\right)\,,

with

\displaystyle V_{\varphi}\equiv\partial^{2}_{\varphi}V_{k}=\varphi^{*2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\,,\qquad V_{\varphi^{*}\varphi^{*}}\equiv\partial_{\varphi^{*}}^{2}V_{k}=\varphi^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\,,\qquad V_{\varphi^{*}\varphi}=V_{\varphi\varphi^{*}}\equiv\partial_{\varphi}\partial_{\varphi^{*}}V_{k}=V_{k}^{\prime}+r^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\,,

(72)

with
V_{k}^{\prime}(x)=\partial_{x}V_{x}(x)
and
V_{k}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\partial^{2}_{x}V_{x}(x)
. The regulator matrix
R_{k}^{ab}(p,q)
is given by

\displaystyle(R^{ab}_{k})=2\pi\,\delta(p+q)\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{m}{2}{R}_{k}(p)\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{m}{2}{R}_{k}(p)&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(73)

where the superscript
ab
now indicates
\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
.
With these preparations, the right-hand side of the flow equation
19
follows as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+R_{k}}\partial_{t}R_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi^{*2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}&\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V_{k}^{\prime}+r^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\\[8.61108pt]
\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V_{k}^{\prime}+r^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}&\varphi^{2}V_{k}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{m}{2}\partial_{t}{R}_{k}(p)\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{m}{2}\partial_{t}{R}_{k}(p)&0\end{pmatrix}\,2\pi\,\delta(p+q)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt={\cal V}_{\tau}\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\,\frac{\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}}{\left[\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}-\left[r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}}\,\frac{m}{2}\partial_{t}{R}_{k}(p)\,,

(76)

where

\displaystyle P_{k}(p)=p^{2}+R_{k}(p)\,,\qquad r^{2}=\varphi^{*}\varphi\,,\qquad{\cal V}_{\tau}=\int\textrm{d}\tau=2\pi\,\delta(0)\,,

(77)

Then, the flow equation for the effective potential is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}=\frac{1}{{\cal V}_{\tau}}\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\frac{\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}}{\left[\frac{m}{2}P_{k}(p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}-\left[r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}}\,\frac{m}{2}\partial_{t}{R}_{k}(p)\,.

(78)

For the Litim-type regulator
40
the derivative of the regulator with respect to the dimensionless renormalisation scale is obtained as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R_{k}=2k^{2}\Theta\left(k^{2}-p^{2}\right)+2k^{2}\left(k^{2}-p^{2}\right)\delta\left(k^{2}-p^{2}\right)=2k^{2}\Theta\left(k^{2}-p^{2}\right)\,.

(79)

The flow equation of the effective potential with the Litim-type regulator follows from

and

as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}(r^{2})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\left[\frac{\frac{m}{2}p^{2}+\frac{m}{2}R_{k}+V^{\prime}(r^{2})+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})}{\left[\frac{m}{2}p^{2}+\frac{m}{2}R_{k}+V^{\prime}(r^{2})+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})\right]^{2}-\left[r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})\right]^{2}}\,m\,k^{2}\,\right]\Theta\left(k^{2}-p^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2mk^{3}}{\pi}\frac{mk^{2}+2V^{\prime}(r^{2})+2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})}{\left[mk^{2}+2V^{\prime}(r^{2})+2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})\right]^{2}-\left[2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}(r^{2})\right]^{2}}\,.

(80)

Appendix D
Breakdown of level crossing capture

In this Appendix we derive the flow equation without topological resummation and demonstrate that such a setup fails to capture the level crossing of the ground-state energy. We resort to LPA,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\int_{\tau}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}\dot{\varphi}^{*}\dot{\varphi}-\frac{\theta}{4\pi}(\varphi^{*}\dot{\varphi}-\dot{\varphi}^{*}\varphi)+V_{k}(\varphi^{*}\varphi)\right]\,,

(85)

where the initial effective potential
V_{\Lambda}
is given by
2
. In contradistinction to the fRG approach put forward in the present work, the topological term is included in the effective action instead of being summed over. We expect that then ’topological freezing’ will manifest itself in a regulator- and setup-dependent way. Below we discuss two different setups and assess their shortcomings: In
Section

D.1
we follow
Fukushima:2022zor
and simply use a standard Cartesian regulator. In
Section

D.2
we make full use of the complex frequency setup and its similarity to the Silver Blaze properties in quantum field theories with a chemical potential.

D.1
Flow with topological freezing

In this Appendix we briefly review the fRG approach used in
Fukushima:2022zor
. There, the flow equation for the effective action in
85
was derived analogously to
Appendix

B
. However, instead of the complex field basis a Cartesian basis was used. The field
\varphi
was disentangled in its real and imaginary part,
\varphi=x+iy
.
Using the Litim cutoff function
40
, the flow equation for the effective potential takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}=\frac{2mk}{|\theta|}\cdot\frac{mk^{2}+2V^{\prime}+2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}}{\sqrt{(mk^{2}+2V^{\prime}+2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime})^{2}-(2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime})^{2}}}\arctan\left(\frac{k|\theta|}{\pi\sqrt{(mk^{2}+2V^{\prime}+2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime})^{2}-(2r^{2}V^{\prime\prime})^{2}}}\right)\,.

(86)

A detailed derivation of this flow equation is provided in
Fukushima:2022zor
, and the only difference to
Appendix

B
is the topological term in the effective action.

Now we use the numerical solution of
86
with the initial potential
2
for studying the
\theta
-dependence of the ground-state energy. For a benchmark coupling value of
g=30
, the flow equation yields results for the ground-state energy that closely match those obtained from the Schrödinger equation within the regime
\theta\lesssim 2\pi
, where
n=0
corresponds to the ground state.
However, as
\theta
increases beyond
2\pi
, the solution to the flow equation breaks down due to the emergence of a pole in the flow, leading to a termination of the flow of the ground-state energy around
\theta\sim 2\pi
. This is also seeing in Figure 4 in
Fukushima:2022zor
or the left-hand side panel in
Figure

12
.

D.2
Flow with topological freezing and complex frequencies

We have also investigated a variant of the flow with topological freezing, where we exploited that fact, that the topological term can be absorbed in an imaginary shift of the frequency. This circumvents the pole observed in
Section

D.1
and potentially accommodates topological effects analogously to resurgence.

For this purpose we rewrite the kinetic and topological terms in the initial action
1
as

\displaystyle\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}{\varphi}^{*}\,p^{2}\,{\varphi}-\frac{i\theta}{2\pi}\varphi^{*}\,p\,{\varphi}\right]=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}{\varphi}^{*}\,p_{\theta}^{2}\,{\varphi}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{2m(2\pi)^{2}}\varphi^{*}{\varphi}\right]\,,

(87)

where we defined the complex frequency

\displaystyle p_{\theta}\equiv p+i\tilde{\theta}\equiv p+i\frac{\theta}{2m\pi}\,.

(88)

This is analogous to the system with a chemical potential if
\mu\equiv\tilde{\theta}
.
The potential receives an additional contribution proportional to
\theta^{2}
from the second term in
87
and then the initial potential is
given by

\displaystyle V_{k=\Lambda}(r^{2})=\frac{m}{2}\tilde{\theta}^{2}r^{2}+\frac{g}{4}(r^{2}-1)^{2}\,.

(89)

In such a setup, the system may be interpreted as a system with the polynomial potential
89
at finite density.
Here, we may make an ansatz for the effective action as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\int\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\left[\frac{m}{2}\varphi^{*}\,p_{\theta}^{2}\,\varphi\right]+\int_{\tau}V_{k}(r^{2})\,.

(90)

In this case, the frequency integral of the action has been extended into the complex plane due to the complex momentum
88
. This is similar to theories with a chemical potential. There, the Silver Blaze property requires the use of regulators that do not destroy the underlying complex structure. We have refrained from such an investigation here as it goes beyond the scope of the present work.

Instead, we use a Litim-type regulator for the real part of the dispersion which is as close as possible to the regulator used in the main body of this work. We define

\displaystyle R_{k}(p_{\theta})=\operatorname{Re}(k^{2}-p_{\theta}^{2})\Theta\bigl(\operatorname{Re}(k^{2}-p_{\theta}^{2})\bigr)=\left(k^{2}+\tilde{\theta}^{2}-p^{2}\right)\,\Theta\left(k^{2}+\tilde{\theta}^{2}-p^{2}\right)\,.

(91)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R_{k}(p_{\theta})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\text{d}p}{2\pi}\frac{mk^{2}\left[\frac{m}{2}(k^{2}+2i\tilde{\theta}p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]}{\left[\frac{m}{2}(k^{2}+2i\tilde{\theta}p)+V^{\prime}+r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}-\left[r^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right]^{2}}\,,

(95)

where

Appendix F
Flow of the cusp position

In this Appendix we derive relations for the flowing cups positions. The derivation is reminiscent of that of the flow of the flowing solution of the equations of motion. We keep this derivations minimal as we do not use these relations in the present work. Still we have checked their applicability numerically.

The first relation works in the finite-volume case and its derivation resembles that of the flow of the cutoff dependent solution of the equation of motion: without loss of generality, we concentrate on one of the cusps with the flowing location
\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}}
. For the derivation of its flow we first consider the smooth case with a finite but large temporal volume, i.e.,
\beta
is finite but asymptotically large. In this case, the cusp is smoothed out. At the smoothed cusp locations,
\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}}
, the second derivative of the effective potential,
V_{k}^{(2)}
, takes a local maximum that diverges for
\beta\to\infty
. Hence, the third derivative vanishes at the location
\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}
,

\displaystyle V_{k}^{(3)}(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k})=0\,.

(96)

Taking a total
t
-derivative leads us to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}=-\left.\frac{\partial_{t}V_{k}^{(3)}}{V_{k}^{(4)}}\right|_{\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}}\,.

(97)

The numerator is simply the third derivative of the flow, evaluated at
\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}}
with
96
. All terms except one drop out because they are proportional to powers of
V_{k}^{(3)}(\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}})
.

The first relation can be used directly in the infinite-volume case. Then, the cusp position is characterised by the crossing point of two different sectors, which reads

\displaystyle V_{k}^{-}(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k})=V_{k}^{+}(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k})\,,

(98)

where the superscript
\pm
denotes the potentials infinitesimally right or left of the cusp location.
Equation

98
holds true for all
k
and hence its total
t
-derivative vanishes. This leads us readily to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}\Bigl[V_{k}^{+,(1)}\left(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}\right)-V_{k}^{-,(1)}\left(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}\right)\Bigr]=-\Bigl[\partial_{t}V_{k}^{+}\left(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}\right)-\partial_{t}V_{k}^{-}\left(\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}\right)\Bigr]\,.

(99)

We emphasise that
98
holds true for any field value but for all but
\varphi^{\textrm{cusp}}
both side in
99
vanish identically. In turn, for
\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}}
they both are non-vanishing and we arrive at

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}=-\left(\frac{\partial_{t}V_{k}^{+}-\partial_{t}V_{k}^{-}}{V_{k}^{+,(1)}-V_{k}^{-,(1)}}\right)_{\varphi^{\rm cusp}_{k}}\,.

(100)

One may be tempted to use
100
in the finite-volume case where we have have
V^{+}_{k}=V^{-}_{k}
, but this leads us to
99
with vanishing left and right hand side, and
100
does not follow. Indeed, ignoring this fact leads us from
100
to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}V_{k}^{+}-\partial_{t}V_{k}^{-}}{V_{k}^{+,(1)}-V_{k}^{-,(1)}}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\partial_{t}V_{k}(\varphi+\epsilon)-\partial_{t}V_{k}(\varphi-\epsilon)}{V_{k}^{(1)}(\varphi+\epsilon)-V_{k}^{(1)}(\varphi-\epsilon)}=\frac{\partial_{t}V_{k}^{(1)}}{V_{k}^{(2)}}\,.

(101)

Equation

101
vanishes on the cusps in clear contradiction to the observed flow of
\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}}
either given by
97
or being extracted from the results. The vanishing of the right hand side of
101
originates in the fact that the flow of
V^{(1)}(\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}})
is proportional to
V^{(3)}(\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}})=0
and
101
vanishes. This concludes our discussion of the flow of
\varphi_{k}^{\textrm{cusp}}
.

Basics of Multiple Polyexponential Integrals
Gleb Aminov, Paolo Arnaudo

Abstract
We introduce a set of special functions called multiple polyexponential integrals , defined as iterated integrals of the exponential integral Ei ​ ( z ) \text{Ei}(z) . These functions arise in certain perturbative expansions of the local solutions of second-order ODEs around an irregular singularity. In particular, their recursive definition describes the asymptotic behavior of these local solutions. To complement the study of the multiple polyexponential integrals on the entire complex plane, we relate them with two other sets of special functions – the undressed and dressed multiple polyexponential functions – which are characterized by their Taylor series expansions around the origin.

Introduction

The physical motivation for studying the
multiple polyexponential integrals
and
multiple polyexponential functions
arises in the context of the linear perturbations of asymptotically flat black holes. After the separation of variables, the radial part of these perturbations is described by the second-order ordinary differential equation (ODE) with two regular and one irregular singularities. In the small-frequency expansion, the local solutions in the near-horizon region can be written using multiple polylogarithm functions. At the same time, in the near-spatial infinity region, one needs to introduce generalizations of the exponential integral -
multiple polyexponential integrals
.

Among the gravitational quantities that can be computed via numerical or analytical methods, a special role is played by the quasinormal mode frequencies (QNMs). In the problems we are interested in, the perturbation field satisfies a second-order ODE and is constrained to two suitable boundary conditions. The quantization of the QNMs can be obtained by solving the differential equation locally around the boundary points and gluing the resulting solutions. In the case of asymptotically flat black holes, one of the boundary conditions is imposed at spatial infinity, which is an irregular singularity of rank one of the differential equation.
When working in the small-frequency regime around an irregular singularity
[
46
]
, we encounter leading order solutions of the ODE which are given by products of rational functions and exponential functions. To compute the higher-order corrections, we apply the perturbative method described in
[
47
]
.
The first correction involves the exponential integral

\mathrm{Ei}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{t}}{t}\mathrm{d}t.

(1.1)

This function can also be written as a Taylor series expansion around
z=0
plus a logarithm:

\mathrm{Ei}(z)=\gamma+\log(-z)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!\,k},\quad|\mathrm{Arg}(-z)|<\pi,

(1.2)

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
When computing the next orders of perturbation, we need to take further integrations, and an iterative structure similar to the one defining the multiple polylogarithms appears (for the latter, see
[
48
,
49
]
). Therefore, we will define functions that generalize the exponential integral as multiple polylogarithms do for the logarithm. We are also interested in the properties of the multiple polyexponential integrals for both
z\to\infty
and
z\to 0
.

Taking inspiration from the recursive integral structure of the multiple polylogarithms, we define the set of
multiple polyexponential integrals
as

\displaystyle\text{ELi}_{1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t,

(1.3)

\displaystyle s_{1}>1:\quad\text{ELi}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{1}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(t\right){\rm d}t,

where
n,s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and the starting point is

\mathrm{ELi}_{1}(z)=\mathrm{Ei}(z).

(1.4)

The integer
n
is called
level
and the sum
s_{1}+\dots+s_{n}
is called
weight
.
These functions have an explicit asymptotic behavior when
z\to\infty
and satisfy the following simple recursive derivative relations:

z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\mathrm{ELi}_{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)=\begin{cases}-\mathrm{e}^{z}\,\mathrm{ELi}_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(-z)\quad&s_{1}=1,\\
\mathrm{ELi}_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)\quad&s_{1}>1.\end{cases}

(1.5)

To make contact with the behavior at
z=0
, we will generalize the Taylor series in (
1.2
)
and define the set of
undressed multiple polyexponential functions
:

el_{s_{1},s_{2}\dots,s_{n}}(z)=\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}k_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!},

(1.6)

where again
n,s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Functions with similar Taylor expansions have been discussed in
[

As follows from the definition, undressed multiple polyexponential functions do not satisfy simple derivative relations like (
1.5

Thus, we also define
dressed multiple polyexponential functions

\mathrm{EL}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}(z)
that satisfy derivative relations analogous to (
1.5

z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\mathrm{EL}_{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)=\begin{cases}-\mathrm{e}^{z}\,\mathrm{EL}_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(-z)\quad&s_{1}=1,\\
\mathrm{EL}_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)\quad&s_{1}>1.\end{cases}

(1.7)

The corresponding Taylor expansions around
z=0
are obtained using the relations between dressed and undressed multiple polyexponential functions.
Finally, the behavior of the multiple polyexponential integrals at
z=0
is given by the relations similar to (

\mathrm{ELi}_{1}(z)=\gamma+\log(-z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z).

2
Multiple polyexponential functions: definitions

First, we define a set of functions we call undressed multiple polyexponential functions:

el_{n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{n}\,k!},\quad el_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}.

(2.9)

The latter functions are straightforward in terms of their series expansion around
z=0
, but taking the derivative proves more challenging. If the first index
s_{1}
is greater than one, we have a simple polylogarithm-like derivative

s_{1}>1:\quad z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}el_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=el_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right).

(2.10)

However, when
s_{1}=1
, the derivative rule becomes harder:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}el_{1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=-el_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)-\left(-1\right)^{n}\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(s_{2}\right)}\dots\sum_{{\rm op}\left(s_{n}\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(s_{2}\right),\dots,{\rm op}\left(s_{n}\right)}\left(-z\right),

(2.11)

where the sum is over all ordered partitions of
s_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
i\geq 2
:

{\rm op}\left(1\right)=\left\{1\right\},\quad{\rm op}\left(2\right)=\left\{2,\left(1,1\right)\right\},\quad{\rm op}\left(3\right)=\left\{3,\left(2,1\right),\left(1,2\right),\left(1,1,1\right)\right\}

(2.12)

and so on. Some particular cases of (
2.11
) are

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}el_{1,1}\left(z\right)=-el_{1}\left(z\right)-\textrm{e}^{z}el_{1}\left(-z\right),

(2.13)

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}el_{1,2}\left(z\right)=-el_{2}\left(z\right)-\textrm{e}^{z}el_{2}\left(-z\right)-\textrm{e}^{z}el_{1,1}\left(-z\right),

(2.14)

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}el_{\left\{1\right\}_{n}}\left(z\right)=-el_{\left\{1\right\}_{n-1}}\left(z\right)-\left(-1\right)^{n}\textrm{e}^{z}el_{\left\{1\right\}_{n-1}}\left(-z\right).

(2.15)

We prove the second derivative rule (
2.11
) in Appendix
A
.
Similarly to multiple polyexponential functions, we can introduce the notions of level and weight. For a given function
el_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)
, the level is
n
, and the weight
w_{n}
is the sum of all indices:

w_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}.

(2.16)

Although undressed multiple polyexponential functions are linearly independent, there are identities that involve non-linear terms. Organizing these identities according to the weight, we have for weights
2
and
3
:

el_{1,1}\left(z\right)+el_{1,1}\left(-z\right)+2\,el_{2}\left(z\right)+2\,el_{2}\left(-z\right)+el_{1}\left(z\right)el_{1}\left(-z\right)=0,

(2.17)

\displaystyle el_{1,1,1}\left(z\right)+el_{1,1,1}\left(-z\right)+el_{1,2}\left(z\right)+el_{1,2}\left(-z\right)+3\,el_{2,1}\left(z\right)+3\,el_{2,1}\left(-z\right)

(2.18)

\displaystyle+6\,el_{3}\left(z\right)+6\,el_{3}\left(-z\right)+el_{1}\left(z\right)el_{2}\left(-z\right)+el_{1}\left(-z\right)el_{2}\left(z\right)=0.

Later, we will prove more general forms of such identities.

The next important observation is

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}el_{1,{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right)=-\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right)-\textrm{e}^{z}\,el_{n}\left(-z\right),

(2.19)

which is a particular case of a more general identity given in Appendix
B
.
This leads us to a definition of a first dressed multiple polyexponential function:

\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)\equiv\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)

(2.20)

with the following derivative rule:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(-z\right),

(2.21)

where

\text{EL}_{n}\left(z\right)\equiv el_{n}\left(z\right).

(2.22)

To prove (
2.21
), we will use the fact that the sum over all ordered partitions of
n+1
can be written in terms of the sum over all ordered partitions of
n
in the following way:

\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left[el_{1,{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right)+el_{1\oplus{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right)\right],

(2.23)

where the operator
\oplus
takes the number to the left of it and adds it to the first entry of the vector to the right of it, e.g.,
1\oplus(1,1)=(2,1)
. In a more general case, when this operator is applied between two vectors
\textbf{v}=\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\right)
and
\textbf{u}=\left(u_{1},\dots,u_{j}\right)
, we define

\textbf{v}\oplus\textbf{u}=\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},v_{i}+u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{j}\right).

(2.24)

If the second vector is a scalar, we have:

\textbf{v}\oplus u=\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},v_{i}+u\right).

(2.25)

Using (
2.20
) and (
2.23
), we get

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)=-\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right)-\textrm{e}^{z}\,el_{n}\left(-z\right)+z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}el_{1\oplus{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right).

(2.26)

Since the following is true for any ordered partition of
n
:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,el_{1\oplus{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right)=el_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left(z\right),

(2.27)

we arrive at (
2.21
).

Motivated by the definition of
\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)
, we define the complete set of dressed multiple polyexponential functions via the following recursive derivative rules:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\,\text{EL}_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(-z\right),

(2.28)

s_{1}>1:\quad z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right),

(2.29)

where the integration constants are fixed by

\text{EL}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(0\right)=0.

(2.30)

Compared with undressed functions
el_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)
, the new set of functions has simple derivative rules. However, their series expansions around
z=0
still need to be derived. In our first example (
2.20
), the Taylor expansion is given by

\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\dots\geq k_{n+1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{n+1}}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!},

(2.31)

where we used the following simple fact:

\sum_{k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\dots\geq k_{n+1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{n+1}}=\sum_{\textbf{v}\in{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\sum_{k_{1}>\dots>k_{j}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{v_{1}}\dots k_{j}^{v_{j}}}.

(2.32)

In the latter equation, vector
\textbf{v}=\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\right)
represents elements of the set of all ordered partitions of
n+1
(including the scalar contribution
n+1
).

3
Multiple polyexponential functions: Taylor expansions

In this section, we will derive the relations between the dressed and undressed multiple polyexponential functions, which will lead to Taylor expansions of the dressed functions
\text{EL}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)
. The first example of such a relation was given previously in (
2.20
). We derive the general relation for level
2
dressed functions
\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(z\right)
by integrating the derivative rule (
2.29
) recursively:

\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{\left(m-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right).

(3.33)

The corresponding Taylor expansion follows from (
2.32
) and the derivative rule (
2.10
):

\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\dots\geq k_{n+1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{m}k_{2}\dots k_{n+1}}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}.

(3.34)

To figure out the general relation at level
3
, we start with the function
\text{EL}_{1,1,n}\left(z\right)
and its derivative:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{1,1,n}\left(z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\,\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(-z\right),

(3.35)

where the right-hand side can be rewritten using the definition (
2.20
):

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{1,1,n}\left(z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(-z\right).

(3.36)

Now, we can use the derivative rule (
2.11
) for the undressed function

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,el_{1,n+1}\left(z\right)=-el_{n+1}\left(z\right)-\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(-z\right)

(3.37)

to derive

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\,\text{EL}_{1,1,n}\left(z\right)=z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\left[el_{n+2}\left(z\right)+el_{1,n+1}\left(z\right)\right].

(3.38)

Since
\text{EL}_{1,1,n}\left(0\right)=0
, one gets

\text{EL}_{1,1,n}\left(z\right)=el_{n+2}\left(z\right)+el_{1,n+1}\left(z\right).

(3.39)

Repeating this procedure for increasing values of the second index, we arrive at the following relation:

\text{EL}_{1,m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(m+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(m+1\right)\oplus n}\left(z\right),

(3.40)

which can be proved by induction. The base case of this induction is (
3.39
). Assuming that (
3.40
) is true for some fixed
m=k-1
, we take the derivative of the sum in (
3.40
) with
m=k\geq 2
:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(k+1\right)\oplus n}\left(z\right)=z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k\right)}\left[el_{{\rm op}\left(k\right),\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)+el_{{\rm op}\left(k\right)\oplus\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)\right],

(3.41)

where we used the decomposition similar to (
2.23
):

\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)}\left[el_{{\rm op}\left(n\right),1}\left(z\right)+el_{{\rm op}\left(n\right)\oplus 1}\left(z\right)\right].

(3.42)

Since we assume (
3.40
) is valid for
m=k-1
and any
n
, we have:

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(k\right)\oplus\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\,\text{EL}_{k-1,n+1}\left(-z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+2\right)}el_{\left(k-2\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+2\right)}\left(-z\right).

(3.43)

For the remaining sum we use decomposition (
2.23
) to get

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(k\right),\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k-1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(k-1\right),\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)+z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k-1\right)}el_{1,{\rm op}\left(k-1\right),\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right).

(3.44)

Analogously to (
2.19
), the following relation holds (see Appendix
B
for the proof):

z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k-1\right)}el_{1,{\rm op}\left(k-1\right),\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)=-\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k-1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(k-1\right),\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right)+\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{\left(k-1\right),{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(-z\right).

(3.45)

Summing up all the contributions leads to

\displaystyle z\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(k+1\right)}el_{{\rm op}\left(k+1\right)\oplus n}\left(z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+2\right)}el_{\left(k-2\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+2\right)}\left(-z\right)+\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{\left(k-1\right),{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(-z\right)=

(3.46)

\displaystyle=-\textrm{e}^{z}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{\left(k-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(-z\right)=-\textrm{e}^{z}\,\text{EL}_{k,n}\left(-z\right),

which finishes the induction step and proves (
3.40
). Finally, the general relation at level
3
is

\text{EL}_{l,m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(m+1\right)}el_{\left(l-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(m+1\right)\oplus n}\left(z\right),

(3.47)

and the corresponding Taylor expansion is

\text{EL}_{l,m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\dots\geq k_{m+1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{l}k_{2}\dots k_{m}k_{m+1}^{n+1}}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}.

(3.48)

Repeating the same steps at level
4
, we get

\text{EL}_{j,l,m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(l+1\right)}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}el_{\left(j-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(l+1\right)\oplus\left(m-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)}\left(z\right).

(3.49)

To construct the corresponding Taylor expansion, we split the indices of the undressed functions into two parts:
\left(j-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(l+1\right)
and
\left(m-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)
. Then, we consider two vectors
\textbf{v}\in\left(j-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(l+1\right)
and
\textbf{u}\in\left(m-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(n+1\right)
. Assuming
\textbf{v}=\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\right)
and
\textbf{u}=\left(u_{1},\dots,u_{j}\right)
, the corresponding contribution to the sum on the right-hand side of (
3.49
) is:

el_{\textbf{v}\oplus\textbf{u}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}>\dots>k_{i+j-1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{v_{1}}\dots k_{i-1}^{v_{i-1}}k_{i}^{v_{i}+u_{1}}k_{i+1}^{u_{2}}\dots k_{i+j-1}^{u_{j}}}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}.

(3.50)

Summing over all possible vectors
v
,
u
and using the previous result (
3.34
), we arrive at

\text{EL}_{j,l,m,n}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}\geq\dots\geq k_{l+n+1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{j}\,k_{2}\dots k_{l}\,k_{l+1}^{m+1}k_{l+2}\dots k_{l+n+1}}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}.

(3.51)

The generalization to an arbitrary number of indices is straightforward now (see Appendix
B
for proof). For an even number of indices
n=2\,k
, the relation between the dressed and undressed functions is

\boxed{\text{EL}_{r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{k},s_{k}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(s_{1}+1\right)}\dots\sum_{{\rm op}\left(s_{k}+1\right)}el_{\left(r_{1}-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(s_{1}+1\right)\oplus\dots\oplus\left(r_{k}-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(s_{k}+1\right)}\left(z\right).}

(3.52)

If the number of indices
n
is odd,
n=2\,k+1
with
k\geq 1
, we have

\boxed{\text{EL}_{r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{k},s_{k},r_{k+1}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{{\rm op}\left(s_{1}+1\right)}\dots\sum_{{\rm op}\left(s_{k}+1\right)}el_{\left(r_{1}-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(s_{1}+1\right)\oplus\dots\oplus\left(r_{k}-1\right)\oplus{\rm op}\left(s_{k}+1\right)\oplus r_{k+1}}\left(z\right).}

(3.53)

We introduce the following notation

1\leq j\leq k:\quad w_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}s_{i}

(3.54)

to write down the corresponding Taylor expansions:

\text{EL}_{r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{k},s_{k}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}\geq\dots\geq k_{w_{k}+1}\geq 1}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{w_{k}+1}\frac{1}{k_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k_{w_{j}+1}^{r_{j+1}}}\right)\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}^{r_{1}}k_{1}!},

(3.55)

\text{EL}_{r_{1},s_{1},\dots,r_{k},s_{k},r_{k+1}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k_{1}\geq\dots\geq k_{w_{k}+1}\geq 1}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{w_{k}+1}\frac{1}{k_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{k_{w_{j}+1}^{r_{j+1}}}\right)\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}^{r_{1}}k_{1}!}.

(3.56)

4
Polyexponential integrals

The asymptotic behavior of polyexponential functions
\text{EL}_{n}\left(z\right)
at
z\rightarrow\pm\infty
is determined by their relations with a set of functions called polyexponential integrals
\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)
, defined as

n\geq 2:\quad\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\text{ELi}_{n-1}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t,\quad\text{ELi}_{1}\left(z\right)\equiv\text{Ei}\left(z\right).

(4.57)

Using L’Hôpital’s rule, the leading asymptotic behavior of
\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)
at
z\rightarrow\pm\infty
can be derived:

\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)=\frac{\textrm{e}^{z}}{z^{n}}\left(1+O\left(\absolutevalue{z}^{-1}\right)\right).

(4.58)

For the full asymptotic series of the polyexponential integrals see Sec.
6
.

Starting with the known relation for the exponential integral

\text{Ei}\left(z\right)=\gamma+\log\left(-z\right)+\text{EL}_{1}\left(z\right)

(4.59)

and applying recursively the definition (
4.57
), we get the following general result for
n\geq 1
:

\boxed{\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{n}\left(z\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n-k}}{k!\left(n-k\right)!}\,\Gamma^{\left(n-k\right)}\left(1\right)\log\left(-z\right)^{k}.}

(4.60)

The latter can be proven by induction with a base case given by (
4.59
). Assuming that the claim is true for some
n>1
, we use the definition to get for
n+1
:

\text{ELi}_{n+1}\left(z\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\text{ELi}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t,

(4.61)

where the integral can be split into two parts:

1.

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{\text{ELi}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t
,

2.

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{z}\frac{\text{ELi}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t=\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k!\,(n-k)!}\Gamma^{(n-k)}(1)\int_{-1}^{z}\frac{\log(-t)^{k}}{t}{\rm d}t
.

The last two integrals in the second part can be taken straightforwardly:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t=\text{EL}_{n+1}\left(z\right),

(4.62)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k!\,(n-k)!}\Gamma^{(n-k)}(1)\int_{-1}^{z}\frac{\log(-t)^{k}}{t}{\rm d}t=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1-k}}{k!\,(n+1-k)!}\Gamma^{(n+1-k)}(1)\log(-z)^{k}.

For the remaining integrals, we repeatedly integrate by parts until the weights of the polyexponential integral and the polyexponential function are reduced to 0:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{\text{ELi}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t=-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\text{ELi}_{n}\left(-t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t=-\int_{1}^{\infty}\text{ELi}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}\log(t)=\dots=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\int_{1}^{\infty}\log^{n+1}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}\,{\rm d}t,

(4.63)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\text{EL}_{n}\left(-t\right)}{t}{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{1}\text{EL}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}\log(t)=\dots=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\log^{n+1}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}\,{\rm d}t,

where the following properties were used:

\text{EL}_{n}\left(0\right)=0,\quad\lim_{z\to-\infty}\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)=0,\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.64)

Combining the two results in (
4.63
) gives

\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log^{n+1}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-t}\,{\rm d}t=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\Gamma^{(n+1)}(1).

(4.65)

Putting everything together, we end up with

\text{ELi}_{n+1}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{n+1}\left(z\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1-k}}{k!\,(n+1-k)!}\Gamma^{(n+1-k)}(1)\log(-z)^{k},

(4.66)

which concludes the inductive proof.

5
Multiple polyexponential integrals

The previously defined dressed multiple polyexponential functions have the following property:

\text{EL}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(0\right)=0.

(5.67)

Thus, they are naturally described in terms of their Taylor expansions.
The corresponding multiple polyexponential integrals tend to zero as
z\rightarrow-\infty
and can be defined using definite integrals:

\text{ELi}_{1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t,

(5.68)

s_{1}>1:\quad\text{ELi}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{1}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}\left(t\right){\rm d}t.

(5.69)

These integrals have natural asymptotic expansions around infinity, which do not include logarithm function
\log\left(z\right)
. We postpone the discussion of the asymptotic expansions to the next section and focus on the relations between multiple polyexponential functions and multiple polyexponential integrals. In what follows, we use the fact that all multiple polyexponential integrals
\text{ELi}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}\left(z\right)
behave at most like
1/z
when
z\rightarrow-\infty
(see Sec.
6
).

5.1
Level
2
integrals

Level
2
multiple polyexponential integrals are the functions of the general form
\text{ELi}_{m,n}\left(z\right)
.
The first example is:

\text{ELi}_{1,1}\left(z\right)=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{1}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t.

(5.70)

One can derive the expansion around
z=0
using relations (
4.60
):

\text{ELi}_{1,1}\left(z\right)=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\left[\gamma+\log t+\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right)\right]{\rm d}t.

(5.71)

First, we can take the following integrals:

\int_{-\infty}^{z}\gamma\,\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,{\rm d}t=\gamma\,\text{ELi}_{1}\left(z\right),

(5.72)

\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\log t\,{\rm d}t=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\log t\,{\rm d}\,\text{ELi}_{1}\left(t\right)=\text{ELi}_{1}\left(z\right)\log z-\text{ELi}_{2}\left(z\right).

(5.73)

The remaining integral can be split into two parts:

\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t.

(5.74)

The first part can be computed using the series expansion of
\text{EL}_{1}
function:

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\frac{t^{k-1}}{k\,k!}{\rm d}t=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(k\right)}{k\,k!}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

(5.75)

And the second part is given by the derivative rule for
\text{EL}_{1,1}
function:

\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t=-\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(z\right)

(5.76)

Summing up all the contributions, we get

\text{ELi}_{1,1}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(z\right)+\text{ELi}_{2}\left(z\right)-\left(\gamma+\log z\right)\text{ELi}_{1}\left(z\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

(5.77)

Then, for
\text{ELi}_{2,1}\left(z\right)
, we have:

\text{ELi}_{2,1}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{2,1}\left(z\right)+2\,\text{ELi}_{3}\left(z\right)-\left(\gamma+\log z\right)\text{ELi}_{2}\left(z\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\left[\gamma+\log\left(-z\right)\right]-\zeta\left(3\right).

(5.78)

To derive the latter expression, we use the definition

\text{ELi}_{2,1}\left(z\right)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{z}\text{ELi}_{1,1}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\left(\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(t\right)+\text{ELi}_{2}\left(t\right)-\left(\gamma+\log t\right)\text{ELi}_{1}\left(t\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

(5.79)

and split the integral into two parts:

1.

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{z}\left(\text{ELi}_{2}\left(t\right)-\left(\gamma+\log t\right)\text{ELi}_{1}\left(t\right)\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}
,

2.

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{z}\left(\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(t\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}
,

where the integrands in both cases tend to zero as
t\rightarrow-\infty
. In the first case, we integrate by parts:

\text{ELi}_{3}\left(t\right)-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\left(\gamma+\log t\right){\rm d}\,\text{ELi}_{2}\left(t\right)=\text{ELi}_{3}\left(t\right)-\left(\gamma+\log z\right)\text{ELi}_{2}\left(z\right)+\int_{-\infty}^{z}\text{ELi}_{2}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=

(5.80)

=2\,\text{ELi}_{3}\left(z\right)-\left(\gamma+\log z\right)\text{ELi}_{2}\left(z\right).

In the second case, we split the integration domain and then integrate by parts:

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\left(\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(t\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right){\rm d}\,\log\left(-t\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\textrm{e}^{t}\,\log\left(-t\right)\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t},

(5.81)

\int_{-1}^{z}\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\int_{-1}^{0}\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(t\right){\rm d}\,\log\left(-t\right)+\int_{0}^{z}\text{EL}_{1,1}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=

(5.82)

=\int_{-1}^{0}\textrm{e}^{t}\,\log\left(-t\right)\text{EL}_{1}\left(-t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}+\text{EL}_{2,1}\left(z\right),

\int_{-1}^{z}\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\log\left(-z\right).

(5.83)

Putting everything back together, we arrive at

\text{ELi}_{2,1}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{2,1}\left(z\right)+2\,\text{ELi}_{3}\left(z\right)-\left(\gamma+\log z\right)\text{ELi}_{2}\left(z\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\log\left(-z\right)-\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,\log\left(t\right)\text{EL}_{1}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t},

(5.84)

where the change of integration variable
t\rightarrow-t
was also performed. The last step is to compute the constant represented by the integral in (
5.84
). To do so, we use power series expansion of
\text{EL}_{1}\left(t\right)
and integrate each term of the series:

\frac{1}{k\,k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,\log\left(t\right)t^{k-1}{\rm d}\,t=\frac{\Gamma\left(k\right)\psi\left(k\right)}{k\,k!}=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(-\gamma+\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{m}\right).

(5.85)

Taking the sum over
k
gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,\log\left(t\right)\text{EL}_{1}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=-\gamma\,\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\zeta\left(3\right),

(5.86)

which completes the derivation of (
5.78
).

Repeating the same steps, we derive a more general expression:

\text{ELi}_{n,1}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{n,1}\left(z\right)+n\,\text{ELi}_{n+1}\left(z\right)-\left(\gamma+\log z\right)\text{ELi}_{n}\left(z\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}}{\left(k-1\right)!\left(n-k\right)!}\,\text{cLi}_{k,1}\log\left(-z\right)^{n-k},

(5.87)

where the constants
\text{cLi}_{k,1}
are the following integrals

k\geq 1:\quad\text{cLi}_{k,1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,\log\left(t\right)^{k-1}\text{EL}_{1}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t},

(5.88)

which we will compute in Sec.
5.3
.
The proof of (
5.87
) is a straightforward induction with a base case given by (
5.77
).

Finally, we are ready to discuss the rest of the level
2
functions, starting with the
\text{ELi}_{1,n}\left(z\right)
. By definition, we have

\text{ELi}_{1,n}\left(z\right)=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t.

(5.89)

Using (
4.60
), we split the integral into two parts:

1.

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t
,

2.

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{k!\left(n-k\right)!}\,\Gamma^{\left(k\right)}\left(1\right)\log\left(t\right)^{n-k}{\rm d}t
.

The first part is straightforward:

-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t=\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)+\text{cLi}_{1,n},

(5.90)

where

\text{cLi}_{1,n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n+1}}=\zeta\left(n+1\right).

(5.91)

To compute the second part, we integrate each term in the sum by parts:

-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\log\left(t\right)^{n-k}{\rm d}t=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\log\left(t\right)^{n-k}{\rm d}\,\text{ELi}_{1}\left(t\right)=-\log\left(z\right)^{n-k}\text{ELi}_{1}\left(t\right)

(5.92)

+\left(n-k\right)\int_{-\infty}^{z}\log\left(t\right)^{n-k-1}{\rm d}\,\text{ELi}_{2}\left(t\right).

(5.93)

We repeat this until the power of
\log\left(t\right)
is reduced to zero, which gives

-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\log\left(t\right)^{n-k}{\rm d}t=\sum_{m=0}^{n-k}\left(-1\right)^{m+1}\frac{\left(n-k\right)!}{\left(n-k-m\right)!}\log\left(z\right)^{n-k-m}\text{ELi}_{m+1}\left(z\right).

(5.94)

Putting everything back together, we arrive at

\text{ELi}_{1,n}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{1,n}\left(z\right)+\zeta\left(n+1\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m+1}}{k!\left(n-m\right)!}\,\Gamma^{\left(k\right)}\left(1\right)\log\left(z\right)^{n-m}\text{ELi}_{m-k+1}\left(z\right).

(5.95)

We increase the first index of
\text{ELi}_{1,n}\left(z\right)
by repeatedly integrating and following the same steps as in the case of
\text{ELi}_{2,1}\left(z\right)
, which gives the following general relation for level
2
multiple polyexponential integrals:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \text{ELi}_{m,n}\left(z\right)=\,&\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(z\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}}{\left(k-1\right)!\left(m-k\right)!}\,\text{cLi}_{k,n}\log\left(-z\right)^{m-k}\\
&+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+1}}{k!\left(n-j\right)!}\,\tbinom{j+m-k-1}{m-1}\Gamma^{\left(k\right)}\left(1\right)\log\left(z\right)^{n-j}\text{ELi}_{j+m-k}\left(z\right).\end{aligned}}

(5.96)

The constants
\text{cLi}_{k,n}
are in agreement with the previous definitions and are given by the following integrals:

k\geq 1:\quad\text{cLi}_{k,n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\log\left(t\right)^{k-1}\,\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}.

(5.97)

Again, we postpone the computation of these constants to Sec.
5.3
.
To prove (
5.96
), we apply an induction with a base case (
5.95
) (see Appendix
C
).

5.2
Level
3
integrals

Before we move on to an arbitrary level, let’s discuss level
3
multiple polyexponential integrals of the form
\text{ELi}_{l,m,n}\left(z\right)
.
As usual, we start with the definition of
\text{ELi}_{1,m,n}\left(z\right)
:

\text{ELi}_{1,m,n}\left(z\right)=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{ELi}_{m,n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t.

(5.98)

Substituting the previous result (
5.96
), we split the integral into three parts:

1.

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t
,

2.

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}}{\left(k-1\right)!\left(m-k\right)!}\,\text{cLi}_{k,n}\log\left(t\right)^{m-k}{\rm d}t
,

3.

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+1}}{k!\left(n-j\right)!}\,\tbinom{j+m-k-1}{m-1}\Gamma^{\left(k\right)}\left(1\right)\log\left(-t\right)^{n-j}\text{ELi}_{j+m-k}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t
.

The first part is the simplest one to compute:

-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\,\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(-t\right){\rm d}t=\text{EL}_{1,m,n}\left(z\right)+\text{cLi}_{1,m,n},

(5.99)

where

\text{cLi}_{1,m,n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,\text{EL}_{m,n}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}.

(5.100)

We use the result obtained previously (
5.94
)

-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\textrm{e}^{t}\log\left(t\right)^{m-k}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\sum_{j=k}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{j-k+1}\frac{\left(m-k\right)!}{\left(m-j\right)!}\log\left(z\right)^{m-j}\text{ELi}_{j-k+1}\left(z\right)

(5.101)

to compute the second part:

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{\textrm{e}^{t}}{t}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}}{\left(k-1\right)!\left(m-k\right)!}\,\text{cLi}_{k,n}\log\left(t\right)^{m-k}{\rm d}t=

(5.102)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=k}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j}}{\left(k-1\right)!\left(m-j\right)!}\,\text{cLi}_{k,n}\log\left(z\right)^{m-j}\text{ELi}_{j-k+1}\left(z\right).

The new type of integral appears in the third part:

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{z}\textrm{e}^{t}\log\left(-t\right)^{n-j}\text{ELi}_{j+m-k}\left(-t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\log\left(-t\right)^{n-j}{\rm d}\,\text{ELi}_{1,j+m-k}\left(t\right)=

(5.103)

\displaystyle\log\left(-z\right)^{n-j}\text{ELi}_{1,j+m-k}\left(z\right)-\left(n-j\right)\int_{-\infty}^{z}\log\left(-t\right)^{n-j-1}{\rm d}\,\text{ELi}_{2,j+m-k}\left(t\right).

We continue integrating by parts until the power of the logarithm is reduced to zero:

-\int_{-\infty}^{z}\textrm{e}^{t}\log\left(-t\right)^{n-j}\text{ELi}_{j+m-k}\left(-t\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\sum_{i=j}^{n}\left(-1\right)^{i-j}\frac{\left(n-j\right)!}{\left(n-i\right)!}\log\left(-z\right)^{n-i}\text{ELi}_{i-j+1,j+m-k}\left(z\right).

(5.104)

The latter gives the following answer for the third integral:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{i+1}}{k!\left(n-i\right)!}\,\binom{j+m-k-1}{m-1}\Gamma^{\left(k\right)}\left(1\right)\log\left(-z\right)^{n-i}\text{ELi}_{i-j+1,j+m-k}\left(z\right).

(5.105)

Putting all three results together, we get

\text{ELi}_{1,m,n}\left(z\right)=\text{EL}_{1,m,n}\left(z\right)+\text{cLi}_{1,m,n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=k}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j}}{\left(k-1\right)!\left(m-j\right)!}\,\text{cLi}_{k,n}\log\left(z\right)^{m-j}\text{ELi}_{j-k+1}\left(z\right)

(5.106)

to derive the general relation for level

At level

(5.114)

Substituting the Taylor expansion of
\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)

\text{EL}_{n}\left(t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k^{n}k!},

(5.115)

and using the integral form of the gamma function

\Gamma\left(k\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\,t^{k-1}{\rm d}t,\quad\Gamma^{(m-1)}\left(k\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{e}^{-t}\log\left(t\right)^{m-1}t^{k-1}{\rm d}t,

(5.116)

we get

\text{cLi}_{m,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n+1}}\,\frac{\Gamma^{(m-1)}\left(k\right)}{\Gamma\left(k\right)}.

(5.117)

The ratio
\Gamma^{(m-1)}\left(k\right)/\Gamma\left(k\right)
can be expressed in terms of polygamma functions. For some first values of
m\geq 1
one has:

\frac{\Gamma^{\left(1\right)}\left(k\right)}{\Gamma\left(k\right)}=\psi\left(k\right),

(5.118)

\frac{\Gamma^{\left(2\right)}\left(k\right)}{\Gamma\left(k\right)}=\psi^{\left(1\right)}\left(k\right)+\psi\left(k\right)^{2},

(5.119)

\frac{\Gamma^{\left(3\right)}\left(k\right)}{\Gamma\left(k\right)}=\psi^{\left(2\right)}\left(k\right)+3\,\psi\left(k\right)\psi^{\left(1\right)}\left(k\right)+\psi\left(k\right)^{3}.

(5.120)

The following recurrence relation can be used to compute the latter ratios for general values of
m
:

\frac{\Gamma^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)}{\Gamma\left(x\right)}=\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\left(m-1\right)}\left(x\right)}{\Gamma\left(x\right)}\right)+\psi\left(x\right)\,\frac{\Gamma^{\left(m-1\right)}\left(x\right)}{\Gamma\left(x\right)}.

(5.121)

Since
k
is a positive integer, the polygamma functions can be written as the following sums:

\psi\left(k\right)=-\gamma+\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k_{1}},

(5.122)

l\geq 1:\quad\psi^{\left(l\right)}\left(k\right)=\left(-1\right)^{l+1}l!\sum_{k_{1}\geq k}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{l+1}}.

(5.123)

Thus, the constants
\text{cLi}_{m,n}

For

\text{cLi}_{1,n}=\zeta\left(n+1\right),\quad\text{cLi}_{2,n}=\zeta\left(n+1,1\right)-\gamma\,\zeta\left(n+1\right),

(5.124)

\text{cLi}_{3,n}=\zeta\left(n+3\right)+\zeta\left(2,n+1\right)+\zeta\left(n+1,2\right)+2\,\zeta\left(n+1,1,1\right)-2\,\gamma\,\zeta\left(n+1,1\right)+\gamma^{2}\,\zeta\left(n+1\right).

(5.125)

For higher values of
m
, we use (
5.121

and
\psi^{\left(l\right)}\left(k\right)

(5.126)

with

and
l_{j}\geq 1
,

(some or all values

may coincide).
First, let’s consider the case with just

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi\left(k\right)^{r}}{k^{n+1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{1}{k^{n+1}}\binom{r}{j}\left(-\gamma\right)^{r-j}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k_{1}}\right)^{j}.

(5.127)

The latter can be simplified using the sum over all ordered partitions of
j\geq 1
and the multinomial coefficients:

\left(\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k_{1}}\right)^{j}=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{k-1}\dots\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k_{1}}\dots\frac{1}{k_{j}}=\sum_{\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\right)\in{\rm op}\left(j\right)}\sum_{k>k_{1}>\dots>k_{i}\geq 1}\binom{j}{v_{1}\dots v_{i}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{v_{1}}\dots k_{i}^{v_{i}}}.

(5.128)

This gives the following answer in terms of MZVs:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi\left(k\right)^{r}}{k^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\sum_{{\rm op}\left(j\right)}\left(-\gamma\right)^{r-j}\binom{r}{j}\binom{j}{{\rm op}\left(j\right)}\zeta\left(n+1,{\rm op}\left(j\right)\right),

(5.129)

where for

\binom{0}{{\rm op}\left(0\right)}\zeta\left(n+1,{\rm op}\left(0\right)\right)=\zeta\left(n+1\right).

(5.130)

Next, we consider the case with a single polygamma function
\psi^{\left(l\right)}\left(k\right)
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi^{\left(l\right)}\left(k\right)}{k^{n+1}}=\left(-1\right)^{l+1}l!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n+1}k_{1}^{l+1}}=\left(-1\right)^{l+1}l!\left[\zeta\left(n+l+2\right)+\zeta\left(l+1,n+1\right)\right].

(5.131)

In the case where there is an array of polygamma functions
\psi^{\left(l_{j}\right)}\left(k\right)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n+1}}\psi^{\left(l_{1}\right)}\left(k\right)\dots\psi^{\left(l_{i}\right)}\left(k\right),

(5.132)

we will use the stuffle product
\star
for indices (see

]
). If we have two vectors
\mathbf{s_{1}}
and
\mathbf{s_{2}}

and

\mathbf{s_{2}}

\sum_{k_{1}=k}^{\infty}\dots\sum_{k_{i}=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{l_{1}+1}}\dots\frac{1}{k_{i}^{l_{i}+1}}=\sum_{\left(v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\right)\in\left(l_{1}+1\right)\star\dots\star\left(l_{i}+1\right)}\sum_{k_{1}>\dots>k_{j}\geq k}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{v_{1}}}\dots\frac{1}{k_{j}^{v_{j}}}.

6
Asymptotic expansions for

In this section, we are going to prove that all multiple polyexponential integrals

behave at most like

The starting point is the asymptotic series for the exponential integral:

\mathrm{ELi}

(6.148)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ELi}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{2n}}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{c^{(s_{1},\dots,s_{2n})}_{j}}{z^{j}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{z^{N+1}}\right).

), we have

\displaystyle z\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\,\mathrm{ELi}_{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)=\mathrm{ELi}_{s_{1}-1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z),\quad\text{for}\ s_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1},

where all the

Let us start by proving (

On the other hand, the recursive formula for

Comparing the coefficients in the powers of
1/z

c_{1}^{(2)}=0

c_{2}^{(2)}=1

c_{j}^{(2)}=(j-1)\,c_{j}^{(2)}+(j-2)!\quad\text{for}\quad j\geq 2.

(6.158)

Suppose the claim holds for every

and the relation

(6.160)

1/z

c_{j}^{(s)}=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{for}\quad j<s,\\
c_{j-1}^{(s-1)}=1\quad&\text{for}\quad j=s,\\
c_{j-1}^{(s-1)}+(j-1)c_{j-1}^{(s)}\quad&\text{for}\quad j>s.\end{cases}

(6.161)

For the proof of (
6.151

Quadratic identities for
EL

In this section, we study quadratic relations satisfied by the dressed multiple polyexponential functions, which are inspired by the relations satisfied by the multiple polylogarithms.
The first relevant identity reads

\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1,1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1,1}(-z)+\mathrm{EL}_{2}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z)=0.

(7.184)

This can be proven by considering the derivative and noticing that the left-hand side vanishes for
z=0
. We have

\displaystyle z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left[\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1,1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1,1}(-z)+\mathrm{EL}_{2}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z)\right]=

(7.185)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{e}^{z}-1\right)\,\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)+\left(\mathrm{e}^{-z}-1\right)\,\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)-\mathrm{e}^{z}\,\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)-\mathrm{e}^{-z}\,\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)=0.

Then, for every
n\geq 2
, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{n}(-z)+(n+1)\mathrm{EL}_{n+1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{n,1}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{EL}_{n+1-j,j}(z)+\left(z\mapsto-z\right)=0.

(7.186)

We remark that this identity has a similar counterpart on the multiple polylogarithm side:

-\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)+\mathrm{Li}_{n,1}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{Li}_{n+1-j,j}(z)=0.

(7.187)

We prove the identity (
7.186
) by induction on
n\geq 2
. For the base step
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left[\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z)+\mathrm{EL}_{2}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)\right]=(\mathrm{e}^{z}-1)\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z)+(\mathrm{e}^{-z}-1)\mathrm{EL}_{2}(z)+2\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{1}(-z)=

(7.188)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z)+\mathrm{e}^{-z}\mathrm{EL}_{2}(z)-2\mathrm{EL}_{1,1}(z)-2\mathrm{EL}_{1,1}(-z)-3\mathrm{EL}_{2}(z)-3\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z),

where passing to the last line we used the identity (
7.184
). Since

z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\mathrm{EL}_{1,2}(z)=-\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z),

(7.189)

\displaystyle\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{2}(-z)+3\,\mathrm{EL}_{3}(z)+2\,\mathrm{EL}_{2,1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{1,2}(z)+\left(z\mapsto-z\right)=0,

(7.190)

For the inductive step, the inductive hypothesis reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{n-1}(-z)+n\,\mathrm{EL}_{n}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{n-1,1}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{EL}_{n-j,j}(z)+\left(z\mapsto-z\right)=0.

(7.191)

\displaystyle z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left\{\mathrm{EL}_{1}(z)\mathrm{EL}_{n}(-z)+(n+1)\mathrm{EL}_{n+1}(z)+\mathrm{EL}_{n,1}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{EL}_{n+1-j,j}(z)+\left(z\mapsto-z\right)\right\}=

z=0

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}^{(4,n)}=\frac{n\left(n^{2}-3n+8\right)}{6}=2+\binom{n-2}{1}+\binom{n}{3},

(7.214)

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}^{(4,n)}=\frac{n\left(n^{2}-6n+11\right)}{6}=1+\binom{n-1}{3}.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we described a new set of special functions called multiple polyexponential integrals. These functions provide a complete perturbative description for the solutions of certain second-order ODEs around an irregular singularity of rank one. We also discuss two other sets of functions characterized by their Taylor series expansions around the origin.

The first application of multiple polyexponential integrals is to the linear perturbations of asymptotically flat black holes. In particular, they allow us to obtain analytic small-frequency expansions of the elements of the scattering matrix for the Schwarzschild black hole
[
46
]
. The difference between the resulting small-frequency expansion and the other known methods (the MST and the instanton methods
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
16
,
43
]
) is that there are no additional parameters. The MST method uses an additional non-integer parameter
\nu
that regularizes the expansion. The instanton approach uses the cutoff
\Lambda\propto\omega
as the perturbative parameter, while the frequency
\omega
is considered to be finite. We already explored the connection between our small-frequency expansion and the instanton approach in
[
46
]
. It would also be interesting to understand the connection with the MST method and the corresponding EFT developed recently in
[
30
,
41
,
44
]
.

From a mathematical perspective, our analysis raises some important related questions. In Sec.
7
we studied a class of quadratic identities satisfied by the dressed multiple polyexponential functions. It would be interesting to generalize these identities to products of multiple polyexponential functions with level greater than one, which might lead to a more involved class of identities. In particular, some algebraic structures may underlie these relations as it happens for the harmonic algebra in the case of shuffle and stuffle products for multiple polylogarithms and multiple zeta values
[
49
,
76
]
.

Another relevant direction of study is the second-order ODEs with higher-rank irregular singularities. One possible example is the biconfluent Heun equation, which has an irregular singularity of rank two at infinity. The solution of this equation involves
\textrm{e}^{z^{2}}
in a leading order when certain parameters are considered small. This poses the following question: what is the complete set of special functions describing the corresponding perturbative expansion? It is possible that multiple polyexponential integrals or their generalizations appear in this context too.

Derivative identity

In this appendix, we prove the identity

z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}el_{1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)=-el_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)-(-1)^{n}\mathrm{e}^{z}\sum_{\mathrm{op}(s_{2})}\dots\sum_{\mathrm{op}(s_{n})}el_{\mathrm{op}(s_{2}),\dots,\mathrm{op}(s_{n})}(-z).

(A.215)

First, using the definition (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}el_{1,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)+el_{s_{2},\dots,s_{n}}(z)=\sum_{k_{1}>k_{2}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}+\sum_{k_{2}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{z^{k_{2}}}{k_{2}!k_{2}^{s_{2}}k_{3}^{s_{3}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}=

(A.216)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}\geq k_{2}>k_{3}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}^{s_{2}}k_{3}^{s_{3}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{\infty}H_{k_{1}}^{(s_{2},\dots,s_{n})}\,\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!},

where the
multiple harmonic numbers
were introduced:

H_{m}^{(s_{2},\dots,s_{n})}\equiv\sum_{m\geq k_{2}>k_{3}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}.

(A.217)

These numbers satisfy the following identity

\displaystyle H_{m}^{(s_{2},\dots,s_{n})}=\sum_{m\geq k_{1}\geq k_{2}>k_{3}>\dots>k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}{k_{1}k_{2}^{s_{2}-1}k_{3}^{s_{3}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}\binom{m}{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}},

(A.218)

which is instrumental to the proof of the derivative relation (
A.215
).
To prove (
A.218
), we define an additional set of
multiple harmonic star numbers
:

{}^{*}H_{m}^{(s_{2},\dots,s_{n})}\equiv\sum_{m\geq k_{2}\geq k_{3}\geq\dots\geq k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{1}{k_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}},

(A.219)

where we sum over all
k_{j}
with
j=2,\dots,n
.
These satisfy a very similar identity:

{}^{*}H_{m}^{(s_{2},\dots,s_{n})}=\sum_{m\geq k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\dots\geq k_{n}\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}{k_{1}k_{2}^{s_{2}-1}k_{3}^{s_{3}}\dots k_{n}^{s_{n}}}\binom{m}{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}},

(A.220)

which can be proven using the integral representation. In the simplest case when
n=2
, we have

{}^{*}H_{m}^{(s_{2})}\equiv\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k_{2}^{s_{2}}}=H_{m}^{(s_{2})},

(A.221)

where
H_{m}^{(s_{2})}
is the
m
-th generalized harmonic number of order
s_{2}
. Taking
s_{2}=1
, we get
m
-th harmonic number with the following integral representation attributed to Euler:

H_{m}^{(1)}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{m}}{1-x}\,{\rm d}x.

(A.222)

Substituting a new variable
x=1-u
, we get the following well-known identity:

H_{m}^{(1)}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-\left(1-u\right)^{m}}{u}\,{\rm d}u=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}-1}}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{1}}.

(A.223)

Adding one more summation of the form

\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}}\left(-1\right)^{k_{2}}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}=-1,

(A.224)

gives us a particular case of (
A.220

H_{m}^{(1)}=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}.

(A.225)

The same can be done in the slightly more general case with
s_{2}>1
. The integral representation of the corresponding generalized harmonic number is

H_{m}^{(s_{2})}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}\,x_{s_{2}}}{x_{s_{2}}}\int_{0}^{x_{s_{2}}}\frac{{\rm d}\,x_{s_{2}-1}}{x_{s_{2}-1}}\dots\int_{0}^{x_{2}}\frac{1-x_{1}^{m}}{1-x_{1}}{\rm d}x_{1}.

(A.226)

Defining
u=1-x_{1}
, we get for the innermost integral:

\int_{1-x_{2}}^{1}\frac{1-\left(1-u\right)^{m}}{u}{\rm d}u=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}-1}}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{1}}\left(1-\left(1-x_{2}\right)^{k_{1}}\right).

(A.227)

The next integral is then

Further integrals will increase the power of
k_{2}
in the denominator of the latter expression until it becomes

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}{k_{1}\,k_{2}^{s_{2}-1}}\binom{m}{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}\left(1-y^{k_{2}}\right),

Appendix B
Relation between
\mathrm{EL}

In this appendix, we want to prove that, for every

\displaystyle\mathrm{EL}_{1,s_{1},\dots,r_{n},s_{n},r_{n+1}}(z)=\sum_{\mathrm{op}(s_{1}+1)}\dots\sum_{\mathrm{op}(s_{n}+1)}el_{\mathrm{op}(s_{1}+1)\oplus\dots\oplus(r_{n}-1)\oplus\mathrm{op}(s_{n}+1)\oplus r_{n+1}}(z).

We will prove (
B.282

. The same steps we take to prove (
B.282

For the base step on

\displaystyle z\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\sum_{\mathrm{op}(2)}\sum_{\mathrm{op}(s_{2}+1)}\dots\sum_{\mathrm{op}(s_{n}+1)}el_{\mathrm{op}(2)\oplus(r_{2}-1)\oplus\mathrm{op}(s_{2}+1)\oplus\dots\oplus(r_{n}-1)\oplus\mathrm{op}(s_{n}+1)}(z)=

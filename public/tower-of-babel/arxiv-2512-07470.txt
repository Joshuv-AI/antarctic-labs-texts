Estimates for Dirichlet Eigenvalues of the Schrödinger operator with the Kronig-Penney Model
Cemile Nur*, Oktay Veliev**, Department of Basic Sciences, Yalova University

Abstract
In this paper, we first improve some asymptotic formulas previously obtained and provide sharp asymptotic formulas explicitly expressed by the potential. For the potentials of bounded variation, we obtain asymptotic formulas in which the first and second terms are explicitly determined and separated from the error terms. In addition, we illustrate these formulas for the Kronig-Penney potential. We then provide estimates for the small Dirichlet eigenvalues of the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator in the Kronig-Penney model. We derive several useful equations from certain iteration formulas for computing these Dirichlet eigenvalues, and prove that all the eigenvalues can be found by the fixed point iteration. Then, using the Banach fixed point theorem, we estimate the eigenvalues numerically. Moreover, we present error estimates and include a numerical example. Key Words: Eigenvalue estimations, Dirichlet boundary conditions, Kronig-Penney model. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 34L05, 34L15, 65L15.

Introduction and Preliminary Facts

In the present paper, we consider the operator generated in

L_{2}[0,\pi]
by the expression

-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y

(1)

and the boundary conditions
y(\pi)=y(0)=0
, where
q(x)
is a complex-valued
summable function. In
[

, we proved that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}

of the operator
L(q)
lying in
O(1)
neighborhood of

equality

(\lambda_{n}-n^{2}-A_{m}(\lambda_{n}))(\Psi_{n},\sin nx)=R_{m+1},

(2)

where

is a large number,
\Psi_{n}
is an eigenfunction corresponding to
the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
and satisfying the inequality
|(\Psi_{n},\sin nx)|>1/2

A_{m}(\lambda_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}(\lambda_{n}),

a_{0}(\lambda_{n})=-C_{2n},\qquad a_{1}(\lambda_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}=-\infty\\
n_{1}\neq 0,-2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{C_{n_{1}}(C_{n_{1}}-C_{n_{1}+2n})}{\lambda_{n}-(n+n_{1})^{2}},

(3)

a_{2}(\lambda_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}=-\infty\\

(4)

a_{k}(\lambda_{n})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{k}=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{n_{1}}C_{n_{2}}...C_{n_{k}}(C_{n_{1}+n_{2}+..+n_{k}}-C_{n_{1}+n_{2}+...+n_{k}+2n})}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}[\lambda_{n}-(n+\sum_{s=1}^{j}n_{s})^{2}]},

(5)

R_{m+1}(\lambda_{n})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{m+1}=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{n_{1}}C_{n_{2}}...C_{n_{m+1}}\bigl(q(x)\Psi_{n}(x),\sin\bigl(n+\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}n_{k}\bigr)\bigr)}{\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}[\lambda_{n}-(n+\sum_{k=1}^{j}n_{k})^{2}]},

C_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\pi}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}}q(x)\cos nxdx

Here the sums for

On the Sharp Asymptotic Formulas

In this section, first, to obtain explicit formulas for the

A_{1,n}(n^{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\

Then, we consider these series in the following lemmas. First, let us consider

Q(x,n)=\int_{0}^{x}q(t)e^{i2nt}\,dt-q_{-2n}x,\qquad Q_{n,0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}Q(x,n)dx

and

G(x,n)=\int_{0}^{x}\bigl(q(t)e^{i2nt}\,+\frac{i\pi}{2}e^{it}\,q_{-2n}\bigr)dt,

(13)

where
q_{k}=\dfrac{1}{\pi}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}}q(x)e^{-ikx}dx
and
q_{0}=0

Lemma 1

If
q\in L_{1}[0,\pi]
, then the following formula holds:

A_{1,n}(n^{2})=\frac{C_{2n}^{2}}{4n^{2}}+D(n,q)+2\operatorname{Re}D(n,p),

(14)

where
D(n,f)=D_{1}(n,f)+D_{2}(n,f)
,

D_{1}(n,f)=\frac{i}{4n\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x)(Q(x,n)-Q_{n,0})e^{-i2nx}\,dx,\quad D_{2}(n,f)=\frac{i}{4n\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x)G(x,n)e^{-i2nx}\,dx,

and
p(x)=q(-x)
.

Proof.
By the definition of
A_{1,n}(n^{2})
, we have

A_{1,n}(n^{2})=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,-2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{C_{k}^{2}}{k(2n+k)}.

Since
\dfrac{1}{k(2n+k)}=\dfrac{1}{2n}\bigl(\dfrac{1}{k}-\dfrac{1}{2n+k}\bigr)
, we see that

A_{1,n}(n^{2})=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}^{2}}{2n+k}-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}^{2}}{k}.

(15)

Using the obvious equality
C_{-k}=C_{k}
and
C_{0}=0,
we obtain the
following equality for the second term on the right side of (
15
):

\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}^{2}}{k}=\frac{C_{2n}^{2}}{4n^{2}}.

(16)

Now let us estimate the first term on the right side of (
15
). Since

q_{-k}=\overline{q_{k}}
and
\cos kx=\dfrac{1}{2}(e^{ikx}+e^{-ikx})
, we
have
C_{k}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}(2\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}+q_{k}^{2}+q_{-k}^{2})
. Therefore, the first term on the right side of (
15
)
can be written in the form

\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}^{2}}{2n+k}=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

(17)

where

I_{1}=\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,-2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{(2n+k)}=I_{1,1}+I_{1,2},\qquad I_{2}=\frac{1}{8n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,-2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{q_{-k}^{2}}{(2n+k)},\qquad I_{3}=\overline{I_{2}},

(18)

I_{1,1}=\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=-\infty\\
m\neq 0,-n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{2m}\right|^{2}}{(2n+2m)},\qquad I_{1,2}=\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=-\infty\\
\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{2m+1}\right|^{2}}{(2n+2m+1)}.

First, let us prove that

I_{1,1}=D_{1}(n,q),\qquad I_{1,2}=D_{2}(n,q),\qquad I_{1}=D(n,q).

(19)

It is clear that
Q(\pi,n)=0
,
Q(0,n)=0
, and that the derivative of
Q(x,n)

with respect to
x
is
Q^{\prime}(x,n)=q(x)e^{i2nx}\,-q_{-2n}
. Using the
integration by parts, we see that

\int_{0}^{\pi}Q(x,n)e^{-i(2n+2m)x}dx=\frac{\pi q_{2m}}{i(2n+2m)},

for
n+m\neq 0
. Therefore, the Fourier decomposition of
Q(x,n)
by the
orthonormal basis
\{e^{i2mx}/\sqrt{\pi}:m\in\mathbb{Z\}}
of
L_{2}[0,\pi]

has the form

Q(x,n)=Q_{n,0}+\sum_{m\neq 0,-n}\frac{q_{2m}e^{i(2n+2m)x}}{i(2n+2m)}.

Using this decomposition in the integral for
D_{1}(n,q)
, we obtain the proof
of the first equality of (
19
).

Now, let us prove the second equality of (
19
). It is clear that

G(\pi,n)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\bigl(q(t)e^{i2nt}\,+\frac{i\pi}{2}e^{it}\,q_{-2n}\bigr)dt=\pi q_{-2n}+\frac{\pi}{2}(e^{i\pi}-1)q_{-2n}=0,

G(0,n)=0
and that
G^{\prime}(x,n)=q(x)e^{i2nx}\,+\dfrac{i\pi}{2}e^{ix}q_{-2n}
. Therefore,

\int_{0}^{\pi}G(x,n)e^{-i(2n+2m+1)x}dx=\frac{\pi q_{2m+1}}{i(2n+2m+1)}.

Thus, the Fourier decomposition of
G(x,n)
by the orthonormal basis

\{e^{i(2m+1)x}/\sqrt{\pi}:m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of
L_{2}[0,\pi]
has the form

G(x,n)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q_{2m+1}e^{i(2n+2m+1)x}\,}{i(2n+2m+1)}.

Using this decomposition of
G(x,n)
in the integral for
D_{2}(n,q)
, we
obtain the proof of the second equality of (
19
). The third equality
of (
19
) follows from (
18
).

Now, we consider
I_{2}
. Using the definition of
p(x)
and the substitution

, we obtain

p_{k}:=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}p(x)e^{-ikx}dx\,=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}q(-x)e^{-ikx}dx\,=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}q(t)e^{ikt}dt=q_{-k}.

Therefore, instead of

, using

and repeating the proof
of (

I_{2}=D(n,p),\qquad I_{2}+I_{3}=2\operatorname{Re}D(n,p).

(20)

Thus, the proof of (

Now let us consider
B_{1,n}(n^{2})
.

Lemma 2

If
q\in L_{1}[0,\pi]

B_{1,n}(n^{2})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}Q^{2}(x)\cos 2nxdx,

(21)

Q(x)=

{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}}q(x)dx

Proof.
To prove (
21

separately. First, estimate the right-side. Let
Q_{k}={\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}}Q(x)\sin kxdx
. Then,
Q_{k}=\pi C_{k}/k
. Therefore, we have the

Q(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{2}{\pi}Q_{k}\sin kx

(Q^{2}(x))^{\prime}=2q(x)Q(x)

parts, the decomposition of
Q(x)
, and then the equality
\sin kx\sin 2nx=\dfrac{1}{2}(\cos(2n-k)x-\cos(2n+k)x)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}Q^{2}(x)\cos 2nxdx=-\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\pi}q(x)Q(x)\sin 2nxdx

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{\pi n}\int_{0}^{\pi}q(x)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}(\cos(2n-k)x-\cos(2n+k)x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{-1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}(C_{2n-k}-C_{2n+k})=\frac{-1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}C_{2n-k}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}C_{2n+k}).

Now, using the equalities
Q_{k}=\pi C_{k}/k
,
C_{-k}=C_{k}
, and doing the
substitution
k\rightarrow-k
in the second summation of the last equality,
we obtain

\int_{0}^{\pi}Q^{2}(x)\cos 2nxdx=\frac{-\pi}{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{k}.

Thus, the right side of (
21

-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}Q^{2}(x)\cos 2nxdx=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{k},

since
C_{0}=0
.

Now, we prove that the left side of (
21

form. Doing the substitution
k\rightarrow-k
, and then using the equality

\dfrac{1}{k(2n-k)}=\dfrac{1}{2n}\bigl(\dfrac{1}{k}+\dfrac{1}{2n-k}\bigr)
, we
see that

\displaystyle B_{1,n}(n^{2})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n+k}}{n^{2}-(n+k)^{2}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n+k}}{(2n+k)(-k)}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{k(2n-k)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{k}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{2n-k}.

On the other hand, doing the substitution
k\rightarrow 2n-k
in the second
summation of the last equality, we obtain

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{2n-k}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{k},\qquad B_{1,n}(n^{2})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{2n-k}}{k},

which means that (
21

Now, we consider
a_{2}(n^{2})
. It is clear that
a_{2}(n^{2})=a_{2,1}(n^{2})-a_{2,2}(n^{2})

\displaystyle a_{2,1}(n^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{k+l}}{[n^{2}-(n+k)^{2}][n^{2}-((n+k+l))^{2}]}=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}}{k(2n+k)(k+l)(2n+k+l)},

\displaystyle a_{2,2}(n^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{2n+k+l}}{k(2n+k)(k+l)(2n+k+l)}.

Using the equality
\dfrac{1}{(k+l)(2n+k+l)}=\dfrac{1}{2n}\bigl(\dfrac{1}{k+l}+\dfrac{1}{2n+k+l}\bigr)
, for

, we obtain

a_{2,1}(n^{2})=\frac{1}{4n^{2}}(S_{1}(n,q)+S_{2}(n,q)+S_{3}(n,q)+S_{4}(n,q)),

(22)

where

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}}{k(k+l)},\qquad S_{2}(n,q)=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}}{(2n+k)(k+l)},

\displaystyle S_{3}(n,q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,-2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}}{k(2n+k+l)},\qquad S_{4}(n,q)=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,2n}\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}}{(2n+k)(2n+k+l)}.

First, let us consider

\displaystyle\frac{C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}}{k(k+l)},\quad\frac{C_{l}C_{k}C_{-k-l}}{l(k+l)},\quad\frac{C_{k}C_{-k-l}C_{l}}{k(-l)},

\displaystyle\frac{C_{l}C_{-k-l}C_{k}}{l(-k)},\quad\frac{C_{-k-l}C_{l}C_{k}}{(-k-l)(-k)},\quad\frac{C_{-k-l}C_{k}C_{l}}{(-k-l)(-l)}

with the equal multiplicands,
C_{k}C_{l}C_{-k-l}
,
C_{l}C_{k}C_{-k-l}
,

C_{k}C_{-k-l}C_{l}
,
C_{l}C_{-k-l}C_{k}
,

,

\left|-S_{2}(n,q)\right|=O(1)\biggl(\sum\limits_{k\neq 0,-2n}\sum\limits_{l\neq 0,-k}\frac{1}{\left|k(2n+k)l(k+l)^{2}\right|}\biggr).

Now, let us prove (

\left|-S_{3}(n,q)\right|=O(1)\sum\limits_{k\neq 0,-2n}\sum\limits_{l\neq 0,-k}\frac{1}{\left|l(2n+k+l)k^{2}(k+l)\right|}.

Making the substitution

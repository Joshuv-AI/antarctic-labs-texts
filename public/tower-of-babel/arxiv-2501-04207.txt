A determinant formula for Toeplitz operators associated to a minimal flow
Efton Park

Abstract
. We define a determinant on the Toeplitz algebra associated to a minimal flow, give a formula for this determinant in terms of symbols, and show that this determinant can be used to give information about the algebraic K K -theory of functions on the underlying space.



Let
A
be an
n\times n
matrix with complex entries. The equation

\det(e^{A})=e^{\operatorname{tr}(A)}

establishes a beautiful and important relationship between the trace and the determinant on
\operatorname{M}(n,\mathbb{C})
. When
\mathcal{H}
is an infinite-dimensional
separable Hilbert space, we can extend the definition of trace on
\operatorname{M}(n,\mathbb{C})
to produce a trace on the algebra

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of bounded linear maps on
\mathcal{H}
, although this trace is only defined on the so-called ideal of trace class
operators. We can then use functional calculus and
(\star)
to define a determinant on invertible elements of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of the form
1 + trace class.

For other operator algebras, the way forward is much less clear. Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a type
\operatorname{II}_{1}
factor endowed with the
normal trace
\tau
for which
\tau(1)=1
. In
[
9
]
, Fuglede and Kadison defined a determinant on
\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})
via the formula

{\det}^{FK}_{\tau}(x)=\exp\left(\tau\left(\log\left(\left|x\right|\right)\right)\right).

The Fuglede-Kadison determinant is a group homomorphism from
\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})
onto the multiplicative group
\mathbb{R}^{+}

of positive real numbers; this requires a significant amount of effort to prove. This determinant provides valuable information about invertible
elements of
\mathcal{M}
. For example, Fack and de la Harpe (
[
8
]
, Proposition 2.5)
proved that the kernel of the Fuglede-Kadison determinant is precisely the commutator subgroup

\left[\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M}),\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})\right]
of
\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})
, and

\det^{FK}_{\tau}
induces an isomorphism

\frac{\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})}{\left[\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M}),\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})\right]}\cong\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Brown constructed a version of the Fuglede-Kadison determinant for semi-finite von Neumann algebras (
[
3
]
). As is the case for
the Fuglede-Kadison determinant, it is nontrivial to prove that the Brown determinant satisfies the desired algebraic properties of a determinant.

Suppose that
A
is a Banach algebra with unit
1
equipped with a continuous complex trace
\tau
, and
let
\underline{\tau}
denote the complex-valued map that
\tau
induces
on
K
-theory. In
[
7
]
, de la Harpe and Skandalis defined a group homomorphism

\Delta_{\tau}:\operatorname{GL}(A)_{0}\rightarrow\frac{\mathbb{C}}{\underline{\tau}(\operatorname{K}_{0}(A))}.

This map is called the de la Harpe-Skandalis determinant, but it would more accurately described as the log of a
determinant, because the group operation on
\mathbb{C}/\penalty\underline{\tau}(\operatorname{K}_{0}(A))
is addition.
For a
\operatorname{II}_{1}
factor
\mathcal{M}
, we have
\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal{M})_{0}=\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal{M})
and

\underline{\tau}(\operatorname{K}_{0}(\mathcal{M}))=\mathbb{R}
, and

{\det}^{FK}_{\tau}(x)=\exp\left(\operatorname{Re}(2\pi i\Delta_{\tau}(x))\right)

for all
x
in
\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathcal{M})
.

In
[
11
]
, Hochs, Kaad, and Schemaitat used Connes and Karoubi’s long exact sequence relating operator algebra
K
-theory,
algebraic
K
-theory and “relative”
K
-theory (
[
12
]
,
[
4
]
) to extend de la Harpe and Skandalis’s determinant to the situation where

A
is a unital Banach algebra with a trace
\tau
defined on a not necessarily closed ideal
J
in
A
.
If
A
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
and
J
is the trace ideal
L^{1}(\mathcal{N})
associated to a semifinite trace
\tau
on

\mathcal{N}
, the Hochs-Kaad-Schemaitat determinant is a homomorphism

{\det}_{\tau}:K^{\operatorname{alg}}_{1}(\mathcal{N},L^{1}(\mathcal{N}))\longrightarrow(0,\infty).

If
x
is in
\operatorname{GL}(L^{1}(\mathcal{N}))
, then
x
determines an element
[x]
of
K^{\operatorname{alg}}_{1}(\mathcal{N},L^{1}(\mathcal{N}))
, and
\det_{\tau}([x])

agrees with Brown’s determinant applied to
x
.
An advantage of the approach Hochs, Kaad, Schemaitat take is that the standard properties of a determinant follow directly
from algebraic properties of the groups and maps described in
[
12
]
and
[
4
]
.

For operators that arise in a geometric or topological setting, one might hope to compute determinants in terms of geometric or topological data. For example,
suppose
T_{\phi}
and
T_{\psi}
are Toeplitz operators on the circle with smooth symbols
\phi
and
\psi
, and suppose
\phi
and
\psi

are non-vanishing with winding number zero. Then
T_{\phi}
and
T_{\psi}
are invertible, and the multiplicative commutator

T_{\phi}T_{\psi}T_{\phi}^{-1}T_{\psi}^{-1}=I+(T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\psi}T_{\phi})T_{\phi}^{-1}T_{\psi}^{-1}

has the form identity plus a trace class operator, and therefore has a well-defined determinant. The determinant of

T_{\phi}T_{\psi}T_{\phi}^{-1}T_{\psi}^{-1}
can be computed in various ways
from the symbols
\phi
and
\psi
; for see example, Helton and Howe (
[
10
]
, Proposition 10.1); Brown (
[
2
]
, Section 6);
and Carey and Pincus (
[
6
]
, Proposition 1). In fact,
\det(T_{\phi}T_{\psi}T_{\phi}^{-1}T_{\psi}^{-1})
only
depends on the Steinberg symbol
\{\phi,\psi\}
in the algebraic
K
-theory group
K^{\operatorname{alg}}_{2}(C^{\infty}(S^{1}))
, and thus the determinant gives a way to detect
nontrivial elements of
K^{\operatorname{alg}}_{2}(C^{\infty}(S^{1}))
.

In this paper, we consider the following generalization. Let
X
be a separable compact Hausdorff space equipped with a Borel measure

\mu
with full support and a minimal flow
\alpha
such that
\alpha
is measure-preserving and ergodic. Then

L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
is a type
\operatorname{II}_{\infty}
factor. Given
\phi
in
C(X)
, define
M_{\phi}

on
L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
via pointwise multiplication. We can use the Hilbert transform to construct a Hardy space
H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})

and then define a Toeplitz operator
T_{\phi}
by compressing
M_{\phi}
to
H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
. Let
\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)
be the

C^{*}
-algebra generated by these Toeplitz operators, and let
\mathcal{SC}(X,\alpha)
be the
C^{*}
-ideal generated by the semicommutators

T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\phi\psi}
. In
[
5
]
, Curto, Muhly, and Xia constructed a short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{SC}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{C(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

such that
\sigma
is a symbol map; that is,
\sigma(T_{\phi})=\phi
for all
\phi
in
C(X)
.
Let
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
denote the set of elements of
C(X)
that are differentiable along the orbits of
\alpha
. We will construct a
“sub-short exact sequence”

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{SC}^{1}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{T}^{1}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{C^{1}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

of Banach algebras with the property that elements of
\mathcal{SC}^{1}(X,\alpha)
are trace class with respect to a semifinite trace
\tau
on

L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
. We will then apply the Hochs-Kaad-Schemaitat machinery to this exact sequence to define a function

d
on the unitization
\mathcal{SC}^{1}(X,\alpha)^{+}
of
\mathcal{SC}^{1}(X,\alpha)
that satisfies the properties of a determinant, and we produce a formula
for
d
on certain multiplicative commutators of elements of
\mathcal{T}^{1}(X,\alpha)
in terms of symbols:

d(e^{T}e^{W}e^{-T}e^{-W})=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{X}{\kern-2.15277pt}\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\sigma(T)^{\prime}(x)\sigma(W)(x)\bigr)\,d\mu(x)\right).

We will then employ the long exact sequence in algebraic
K
-theory to construct a group homomorphism from
K^{\operatorname{alg}}_{2}(C^{1}(X,\alpha))

to
\mathbb{R}
; this map gives a way to show various elements of the highly complicated group
K^{\operatorname{alg}}_{2}(C^{1}(X,\alpha))
are nontrivial.

1.
Toeplitz operators on minimal ergodic flows

In this section we collect the results we need from
[
5
]
, although our notation will differ
somewhat from theirs. Let
X
be a separable compact Hausdorff space equipped with a minimal flow
\alpha=\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
; given a point
x
in
X
and
a real number
t
, we will write
\alpha_{t}(x)
as
x+t
. Suppose
X
admits a Borel probability measure
\mu
with the following properties: (1) the support of
\mu

is all of
X
; (2) the maps
\alpha_{t}
are measure-preserving for each real number
t
; and (3)
\alpha
is ergodic with respect to
\mu
.

Endow
\mathbb{R}
with Lebesgue measure and consider the Hilbert space
L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
associated
with the product measure on
X\times\mathbb{R}
. Given
\phi
in
C(X)
, define
M_{\phi}
on
L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
by
pointwise multiplication:

(M_{\phi}h)(x,s)=\phi(x)h(x,s).

Define the Hilbert transform
H
on
L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
by

(Hf)(x,t)=\operatorname{PV}\left(\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s}f(x+s,t-s)\,ds\right).

The factor of
\frac{1}{i}
, which is often not present in the definition of the Hilbert transform, is included in
[
5
]
to make
H
self adjoint.
Set
P=\frac{1}{2}(I+H)
. Then
P
is a projection; denote the range of
P
by
H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
.
For each
\phi
in
C(X)
, define the Toeplitz operator

T_{\phi}:H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
by the formula

T_{\phi}=PM_{\phi}.

Definition 1
.

The Toeplitz algebra associated to the flow
\alpha
on
X
is the
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)
of

\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R}))
generated by the set
\{T_{\phi}:\phi\in C(X)\}
.

Definition 2
.

The semi-commutator ideal of
\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)
is the
C^{*}
-ideal
\mathcal{SC}(X,\alpha)
of
\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)
generated
by the set
\{T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\phi\psi}:\phi,\psi\in C(X)\}
.

By Lemma 24.2 in
[
5
]
, we have a short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{SC}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{C(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with the feature that
\sigma(T_{\phi})=\phi
for every
\phi
in
C(X)
. The short exact sequence has an isometric linear
splitting
\xi
defined by
\xi(\phi)=T_{\phi}
. As a consequence, every element of
\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)
can be uniquely
written in the form
T_{\phi}+S
for some
\phi
in
C(X)
and
S
in
\mathcal{SC}(X,\alpha)
, and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T_{\phi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{op}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}

for every
\phi
in
C(X)
.

Remark:
The commutator ideal of
\mathcal{T}(X,\alpha)
is contained in
\mathcal{SC}(X,\alpha)
, and
Lemma 24.1 in
[
5
]
states that if the action of
\alpha
on
X
is strictly ergodic, then these two ideals are equal. This might
be true in general; I am not aware of any examples of minimal ergodic flows where the two ideals differ. But in any case, we will not
investigate this issue here.

For each real number
t
, define a unitary operator
U_{t}
on
L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
by the formula

(U_{t}h)(x,s)=h(x+t,t-s).

Let
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
be the von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R}))

generated by the
M_{\phi}
and
U_{t}
. Because the action
\alpha
is ergodic with respect to the measure
\mu
and

\mu
has full support,
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
is a type
\operatorname{II}_{\infty}
factor
and therefore admits a semifinite normal trace
\tau
. The algebra
C_{c}(X\times\mathbb{R})

is weakly dense in
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
; we scale
\tau
so that

\tau(f)=\int_{X}f(x,0)\,d\mu(x)

for every
f
in
C_{c}(X\times\mathbb{R})
.
Following
[
13
]
, define

L^{p}(\tau)=\{F\in L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}:\tau\left(\left|F\right|^{p}\right)<\infty\},\quad p=1,2

and set

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|S\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p}=\bigl(\tau\left(\left|S\right|^{p}\right)\bigr)^{1/\penalty p},\quad S\in L^{p}(\tau).

(In fact,
[
13
]
defines
L^{p}(\tau)
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, but
p=1,2
are all we will need.)
Each
L^{p}(\tau)
is an ideal in
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 3

(Holder’s inequality (
[
13
]
, Theorem 1))
.

If
A
and
B
are in
L^{2}(\tau)
, then
AB
is in
L^{1}(\tau)
, and

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|AB\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{1}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|B\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}
.

Proposition 4

((
[
13
]
, Corollary 1.1))
.

For all
S
in
L^{p}(\tau)
and
F
in
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
,
we have the inequalities

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|SF\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|S\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|F\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{op},\quad{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|FS\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|F\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{op}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|S\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p}.

We can decompose
L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
as

H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})\oplus H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})^{\perp}
, and via this decomposition, we can
view
\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R}))
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R}))
:

S\hookrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the
\operatorname{II}_{\infty}
factor

\mathcal{N}=P\left(L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}\right)P.

The trace
\tau
on
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{R}
restricts to
\mathcal{N}
, and we will use
\tau
to denote the trace in both situations. It should be clear from context
which is the appropriate domain for
\tau
.

Definition 5
.

A function
\phi
in
C(X)
is differentiable with respect to
\alpha
if the limit

\phi^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}

exists for each
x
in
X
. Let
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
be the set of functions on
X
that are continuously differentiable with respect to
\alpha
, and define a
norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{C^{1}}
on
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
by the formula

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{C^{1}}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{\prime}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}.

It is routine to check that
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
is a Banach algebra when equipped with this norm.

Theorem 6
.

The semicommutator
T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\phi\psi}
is in
L^{1}(\tau)
for all
\phi
and
\psi
in
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
, and

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\phi\psi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{1}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{C^{1}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{C^{1}}.

Proof.

We adapt an argument from
[
5
]
, Theorem 23.1. Let
Q=1-P
be orthogonal projection of

L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})
onto
H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})^{\perp}
, and for each
\phi
in
C(X)
, write
M_{\phi}
as the matrix

M_{\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}PM_{\phi}P&PM_{\phi}Q\\
QM_{\phi}P&QM_{\phi}Q\end{pmatrix}.

Write
M_{\phi}M_{\psi}
and
M_{\phi\psi}
in this form. Because these two operators are equal, the
(1,1)
entries of their matrices agree, which
gives us the equation

PM_{\phi\psi}P-(PM_{\phi}P)(PM_{\psi}P)=(PM_{\phi}Q)(QM_{\psi}P).

An easy computation shows that
PM_{\phi}Q=\frac{1}{2}P[H,M_{\phi}]
. We also have

QM_{\psi}P=(PM_{\bar{\psi}}Q)^{*}=\frac{1}{2}(P[H,M_{\bar{\psi}}])^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}P[H,M_{\psi}].

By Lemma 22.1 in
[
5
]
, the quantity

[H,M_{\phi}]
is in
L^{2}(\tau)
for all
\phi
in
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
. Therefore
PM_{\phi}Q
and
QM_{\psi}P
are in
L^{2}(\tau)
, and thus their
product is in
L^{1}(\tau)
. By identifying
\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R}))
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R}))
in
the manner described above, we see that
T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\phi\psi}
is in
L^{1}(\tau)
.
By Proposition
3
,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T_{\phi}T_{\psi}-T_{\phi\psi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{1}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(PM_{\phi}Q)(QM_{\psi}P)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{1}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|PM_{\phi}Q\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|QM_{\psi}P\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}.

The next step is to show that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|PM_{\phi}Q\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{C^{1}}
for all
\phi
in
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
.
From Proposition
4
, we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|PM_{\phi}Q\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\frac{1}{2}P[H,M_{\phi}]\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|P\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|[H,M_{\phi}]\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|[H,M_{\phi}]\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}.

For
\phi
in
C^{1}(X,\alpha)
, define
k_{\phi}:X\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to be

k_{\phi}(x,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{\pi t}&t\neq 0\\
{\quad}\frac{1}{\pi}\phi^{\prime}(x)&t=0.\end{cases}

The proof of Lemma 22.1 in
[
5
]
states that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|[H,M_{\phi}]\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|k_{\phi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})}
.

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|k_{\phi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{2}(X\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\mu(x).

We compute

\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)\right|^{2}}{t^{2}}\,dt\\
\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(2{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty})^{2}}{t^{2}}\,dt=4{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}^{2}.

Similarly,

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt\leq 4{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}^{2}.

The Mean Value Theorem gives us

\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}\right|^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{-1}^{1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{\prime}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}^{2}\,dt=2{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi^{\prime}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}^{2}.

Hence, because
\mu
is a probability measure on

,

Lemma 20
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Banach algebra. For all
T
and
W
in
\mathcal{A}
,

e^{T}We^{-T}=W+[T,W]+\frac{1}{2!}[T,[T,W]]+\frac{1}{3!}[T,[T,[T,W]]]+\cdots

Proof.

Define a sequence
\{W_{n}\}
by setting
W_{0}=W
and
W_{n+1}=[T,W_{n}]
for
n>0
. Then the right hand side of our equation is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{W_{n}}{n!}.

An induction argument shows that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 2^{n}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
for all
n
, so we see that this infinite series converges by comparing it to the Maclaurin series for
e^{x}
.

Next, write out the left hand side in terms of the Maclaurin series for the exponential:

\displaystyle e^{T}We^{-T}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{\ell}}{\ell!}\right)W\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-T)^{k}}{k!}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\ell!\,k!}T^{\ell}WT^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!\,(n-k)!}T^{n-k}Wt^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n!}\frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}T^{n-k}WT^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}T^{n-k}WT^{k}\right);

starting in the third line we have
n=\ell+k
.
To complete the proof, we use an induction argument to show that

W_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}T^{n-k}WT^{k}

for all nonnegative integers
n
. The formula is certainly correct for
n=0
. Suppose the formula holds for
n
. Then, using a standard
binomial identity we compute that

\displaystyle W_{n+1}

\displaystyle=[T,W_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\left[T,T^{n-k}WT^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\left(T^{n-k+1}WT^{k}-T^{n-k}WT^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=T^{n+1}W+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}T^{n-k+1}WT^{k}

\displaystyle{\hskip 80.0pt}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}T^{n-k}WT^{k+1}-(-1)^{n}WT^{n+1}

\displaystyle=T^{n+1}W+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}T^{n-k+1}WT^{k}

\displaystyle{\hskip 80.0pt}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k-1}T^{n-k+1}WT^{k}+(-1)^{n+1}WT^{n+1}

\displaystyle=T^{n+1}W+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\left(\binom{n}{k-1}+\binom{n}{k}\right)T^{n-k+1}WT^{k}

\displaystyle{\hskip 230.0pt}+(-1)^{n+1}WT^{n+1}

\displaystyle=T^{n+1}W+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}T^{n-k+1}WT^{k}+(-1)^{n+1}WT^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}T^{n+1-k}WT^{k}.

∎

∎

Proposition 24
.

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be in

, and let

. Then

\partial\{e^{\phi},e^{\psi}\}=\left[e^{T_{\phi}}e^{T_{\psi}}e^{-T_{\phi}}e^{-T_{\psi}}\right].

Proof.

Note that
e^{\phi}
and
e^{\psi}
are invertible elements of

that lift to invertible elements

and

. From Proposition 4.4.22 in

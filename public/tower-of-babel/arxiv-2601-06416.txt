Symplectic mechanics of relativistic spinning compact bodies. III. quadratic - in - spin integrability in Type - D Einstein spacetimes: persistence and breakdown
Paul Ramond,, Soichiro Isoyama,, Adrien Druart,

Abstract
We develop a covariant Hamiltonian formulation of the Mathisson–Papapetrou–Tulczyjew–Dixon dynamics at quadratic order in spin under the Tulczyjew–Dixon spin supplementary condition (TD SSC). In four-dimensional, type-D Einstein (vacuum/ Λ \Lambda -vacuum) spacetimes admitting a non-degenerate Killing–Yano (KY) tensor, we reduce via a Dirac bracket to the 10-dimensional physical phase space and model the quadratic sector with a spin-induced quadrupole characterized by a deformability κ \kappa ( κ = 1 \kappa=1 for black-hole–like; κ ≠ 1 \kappa\neq 1 for material or exotic compact objects). For κ = 1 \kappa=1 , we construct five independent first integrals—an autonomous Hamiltonian, two KY-generated Killing invariants, a linear Rüdiger constant, and a quadratic Carter–Rüdiger constant—establishing Liouville–Arnold integrability at quadratic order in spin. For κ ≠ 1 \kappa\neq 1 , the symmetry-generated invariants are not conserved in general and integrability does not persist at this order. The proof proceeds via covariant Poisson-bracket computations using a null bivector decomposition; Kerr is recovered as a special case. These results show that integrability can persist beyond Kerr and beyond the linear-in-spin regime, laying groundwork for symmetry-based, beyond–Kerr modelling of asymmetric-mass, spinning compact binaries.

II.3
Reduction to the 10D phase space via the TD SSC

At the level of the MPTD equations, the TD SSC (
10

II.3.1
Constraint surface and symplectic leaves

On the 14D phase space
\mathcal{M}
, the variables
(x,p,S)
are

has dimension
14-3=11

In addition to this, the Poisson brackets (

\mathcal{M}

\{I,H\}=0

\forall F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\,,\quad\{F,\mathcal{S}\}=0.

are linked to symmetries of the Hamiltonian

(1,3)

We work at the intersection

II.3.3
Dirac bracket on
\mathcal{P}

To finally (and rigorously) pose the question of integrability of the MPTD + TD SSC, we need a last piece of machinery from Poisson geometry. Showing that a Hamiltonian system is integrable amounts to exhibiting enough constants of motion (called
first integrals
in Hamiltonian mechanics) and performing a series of Poisson brackets between them to show their independence and involution. However, since we are only interested in the MPTD system under the TD SSC (otherwise even the Hamiltonian (
18

10

\mathcal{P}
, where things are non-degenerate, the TD SSC is enforced by construction and phase space curves are actual MPTD solution.

Therefore, we first need to find out what these Poisson brackets on
\mathcal{P}
(or
\mathcal{P}
-brackets, for short) are. Once again, Poisson geometry has a compact and simple answer to this, which some readers may find as not so different from how tensors are projected onto spacetime hypersurfaces. Indeed, the
\mathcal{P}
-bracket can be computed from the
\mathcal{M}
-brackets (
17
), and is a consequence of how Poisson structures are pulled back/push forward on invariant sub-manifolds, cf.
13
;
164
;
30
;
50
;
29
. The formula reads

\{F,G\}^{\mathcal{P}}:=\{F,G\}|_{\mathcal{P}}-\{F,C^{\alpha}\}(M^{-1})_{\alpha\beta}\{C^{\beta},G\}|_{\mathcal{P}}\,,

(28)

for any two functions
(F,G)
defined on
\mathcal{M}
, and
with
(M^{-1})_{\alpha\beta}
denoting the matrix inverse of the matrix
M^{\alpha\beta}
whose elements are
\{C^{\alpha},C^{\beta}\}
. On the right-hand side of Eq. (
28

\mathcal{M}
-Poisson brackets are be used, and then the symbol

means “project onto
\mathcal{P}
”, i.e., “simplify using
C^{\alpha}=0

into the
\mathcal{P}
-bracket formula (

\forall F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\,,\quad\{F,C^{\gamma}\}^{\mathcal{P}}=0

(29)

identically, as follows from

by definition of the matrix

are Casimirs of the
\mathcal{P}

II.4
A practical bracket shortcut on
\mathcal{P}

Lastly, we mention a crucial consequence from (

III.1
Killing-Yano tensor basics

In addition to those arising from spacetime isometries, constants of motion also exist in spacetimes admitting symmetric Killing–Stäckel (KS) tensors for geodesics

For example, constants of motion for the linear-in-spin dynamics rely instead on the existence of a Killing–Yano (KY) tensor (which in particular implies the existence of KS tensor)

;
129
;
46
;
123

Accordingly, and as in Paper I, we search for constants of motion for quadratic-in-spin dynamics in spacetimes endowed with a (non-degenerate) KY tensor.
We assume that the spacetime under study admits a KY tensor denoted by
f_{ab}
. It is defined as an antisymmetric tensor satisfying

\nabla_{(a}f_{b)c}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{Z}:=\tfrac{1}{4}f_{ab}^{\star}f^{ab}\neq 0,

(32)

where, as in Paper I, the third condition ensures that
f_{ab}
is non-degenerate
53
;
54

The existence of a KY tensor in a given spacetime strongly constraints its geometry, particularly its symmetries and curvature.
With regard to symmetries, there exist two commuting Killing vectors, denoted
\xi^{a}
and
\eta^{a}
, and a symmetric KS tensor
K_{ab}
. They are defined by

K_{ab}:=f_{ac}f^{c}_{\phantom{o}b},\quad\xi_{a}:=\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{b}f^{\star}_{ab}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta^{a}:=K^{ab}\xi_{b},

(33)

and they satisfy the Killing and KS equations
\nabla_{(a}\xi_{b)}=0
,
\nabla_{(a}\eta_{b)}=0
and
\nabla_{(a}K_{b)c}=0
. The identities that we use repeatedly are

\displaystyle f_{ac}^{\star}\,f^{c}_{\phantom{o}b}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Z}\,g_{ab},

(34a)

\displaystyle f_{ab}f_{cd}

\displaystyle=-2f_{c[a}f_{b]d}-\mathfrak{Z}\,\varepsilon_{abcd},

(34b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}f_{bc}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{abcd}\xi^{d},

(34c)

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}f_{bc}^{\star}

\displaystyle=-2g_{a[b}\xi_{c]}.

(34d)

Other identities satisfied by these Killing objects are proved in Paper I.

At the same time, the existence of a KY tensor has strong consequences regarding the curvature.
By taking different covariant derivatives of the above identities and manipulating them using the Ricci identity, one obtains several integrability conditions for the Riemann tensor
R_{abcd}

\displaystyle R_{abe[c}f_{d]}^{\phantom{o}e}

III.2.1
Bases for bivectors and symmetric tensors

To begin, define a null tetrad consisting of four null vectors
(\ell^{a},n^{a},m^{a},\bar{m}^{a})

\displaystyle X_{ab}=2\ell_{[a}m_{b]},\quad Y_{ab}=2n_{[a}\bar{m}_{b]},\quad Z_{ab}=2(\ell_{[a}n_{b]}-m_{[a}\bar{m}_{b]}),

.

It is also useful, for our purposes, to define the following calligraphic tensors obtained by contracting the 2-forms with
\bar{Z}_{ab}

\mathcal{X}_{ab}=\bar{Z}_{a}^{\phantom{a}e}X_{eb},\quad\mathcal{Y}_{ab}=\bar{Z}_{a}^{\phantom{a}e}Y_{eb},\quad\mathcal{Z}_{ab}=\bar{Z}_{a}^{\phantom{a}e}Z_{eb}.

\mathcal{L}_{ab}

\mathcal{M}_{ab}

A perspective

2D attractive almost-bosonic anyon gases
Dinh-Thi Nguyen

Abstract
. In two-dimensional space, we consider a system of N N anyons interacts via a short range attractive two-body interaction. In the stable regime, we derive the average-field-Pauli functional as the mean-field limit of many-body quantum mechanics. Furthermore, we investigate the collapse phenomenon in the collapse regime where the strength of attractions tends to a critical value (defined by the cubic NLS equation) while simultaneously considering the weak field regime where the strength of the self-generated magnetic field tends to zero.

81V70, 81Q10, 35P15, 46N50

1.
Introduction and main results

Anyons are exotic particles that can exist in two-dimensional systems and exhibit fractional statistics, meaning that their wave functions acquire a nontrivial phase when particles are exchanged. Unlike fermions (with half-integer spin) or bosons (with integer spin), anyons can have fractional spin statistics. The study of anyons, exotic quasi-particles with fractional statistics, has captivated physicists for decades due to their unique properties and potential applications in condensed matter physics and quantum information science. Anyons exhibit intermediate statistics between fermions and bosons, leading to novel phenomena such as fractional quantum Hall effect and topological quantum computation. In recent years, there has been a growing interest in understanding the behavior of anyons in the presence of external potentials.

In this article, we explore the fascinating world of anyons interacting via attractions. Moreover, we consider the weak-field limit corresponding to the regime where the strength of the external magnetic field is small compared to other energy scales in the system. In this limit, the effects of the external magnetic field can be treated perturbatively, allowing for analytical or numerical analysis. We investigate how the interplay between the statistics of anyons and the presence of point-like attractions gives rise to rich and intriguing physical phenomena, offering new avenues for theoretical exploration and experimental realization.

1.1.
The average-field-Pauli theory of interacting “almost-bosonic” anyon gases

Mathematically, in the mean-field regime, “almost-bosonic” interacting anyon gas is described by the

The study of (
1.4

g

\beta\to 0

Here, the competition between Pauli exclusion and external potentials becomes more pronounced, leading to intriguing phenomena such as the formation of bound states, localization effects, and emergence of topological features. Our main interest lies in bridging these two regimes and revealing the intricate relationship between collapse dynamics and weak field behavior in
E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}

Our first result concerns the existence and properties of minimizers of (

Theorem 1

(Existence of average-field-Pauli minimizers)
.

Let

1.2.
Many-body theory of interacting “almost-bosonic” anyon gases

From the first principle of quantum mechanics, a 2D interacting anyon gas in a trapping potential

\displaystyle H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},\beta,g}={}

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:bec}\Psi_{N}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\approx u^{\otimes N}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}):=u(x_{1})\ldots u(x_{N}).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|u|^{2}])u\right|^{2}+V|u|^{2}-\frac{g}{2}(|u|^{2}*W_{N^{\nu}})|u|^{2}\right].

\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(k)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k};y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2(N-k)}}\overline{\Psi_{N}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k};Z)}{\Psi_{N}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k};Z)}{\rm d}Z.

\lim_{N\to\infty}\tr\left|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(k)}-|u^{\otimes k}\rangle\langle u^{\otimes k}|\right|=0,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

Let

If

[

(see also
[

[

Organization of the paper.
In
Section

2
, we prove the existence of average-field-Pauli minimizers and establish its behavior in the collapse regime and in the weak-field regime at the same time. In
Section

3
, we first study the collapse phenomenon in the interpolate average-field-Hartree theory. This plays a crucial role in the study of many-body theory in the super-critical regime. Finally, in

2.
Average-field-Pauli minimizers

2.1.
Existence of average-field-Pauli minimizers

In this subsection, we prove the existence of average-field-Pauli minimizers as shown in
Theorem

1
. We rewrite the energy functional (
1.1
) as follows

(2.1)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline unctional:afP-crossterms}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[|\nabla u|^{2}+2\beta\mathbf{A}[|u|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{J}[u]+\beta^{2}\mathbf{A}[|u|^{2}]^{2}|u|^{2}+V|u|^{2}-\frac{g}{2}|u|^{4}\right]

where we defined the current of
u
by

(2.2)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline otential:current}\mathbf{J}[u]:=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}(u\nabla\overline{u}-\overline{u}\nabla u)=\Im(\overline{u}\nabla u).

We now collect some preliminary estimates that are needed to set up the variational calculus for (
2.1
). We note that, for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by Sobolev embedding,

u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
2\leq p<\infty
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{A}[|u|^{2}]u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see
[
45
,
15
]
) with

(2.3)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline neq:hardy}\|\mathbf{A}[|u|^{2}]u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla|u|\|_{L^{2}},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Note that (
2.3
) still holds true if the magnetic field
\mathbf{A}
(defined by (
1.2
)) is replaced by its regularization
\mathbf{A}_{R}
(defined by (
1.23
)). Moreover, we have the following 2D Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

(2.4)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline neq:gn}\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{4}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla|u||^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

and the diamagnetic inequality (see e.g.,
[
37
, Theorem 7.21]
)

(2.5)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline neq:diamagnetic}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla|u||^{2}\leq\min\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|u|^{2}])u\right|^{2}\right\},\quad\forall\beta\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that (
2.4
) is a consequence of the Sobolev embedding and is the special case of (
1.7
) where
\beta=0
. Furthermore, (
2.5
) still holds true for a general magnetic field
\mathbf{A}
including (
1.2
) and (
1.23
).

We have all tools to derive the existence of minimizers of (
1.4
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
0\leq V\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
V(x)\to+\infty
as
|x|\to+\infty
. Then, for every
\beta\geq 0
and
0<g<g_{*}(\beta)
, there exists
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}=1
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u]=E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}
.

Proof.

The existence of a minimizer follows from the standard method in the calculus of variation. While the case
\beta>0
and
g=0
was done in
[
45
]
, the case
\beta=0
and
0<g<g_{*}(0)
was proved in
[
26
]
. When
\beta>0
and
0<g<g_{*}(\beta)
, it is enough to verify the uniform boundedness in
H^{1}\cap L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(where we recall the definition of the space
L_{V}^{2}
in (
1.5
)) of the minimizing sequence
\{u_{n}\}
given by

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u_{n}]=E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}\quad\text{with}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{n}|^{2}=1.

We use (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) to obtain

\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u_{n}]\geq\left(1-\frac{g}{g_{*}(\beta)}\right)\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,0}[u_{n}]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|u|^{2}.

The above together with (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) yields that
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}\cap L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In fact, the case where
g=g_{*}(0)
was included since
g_{*}(\beta)>g_{*}(0)
for every
\beta>0
(see
[
4
]
). However, the proof of it in
[
4
]
is not a direct proof. In the following, we give an alternative proof for this critical case where
g=g_{*}(0)
.

Using (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have that
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. It remains to prove that

\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|u_{n}|^{2}])u_{n}\right|^{2}

is bounded uniformly. We assume on the contrary that there exists a subsequence of
\{u_{n}\}
(still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
) such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
. Denote
\widetilde{u_{n}}=\varepsilon_{n}u_{n}(\varepsilon_{n}\cdot)
. Then we have

(2.6)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:minimizer-critical-boundedness-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{2}=1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{n}}\right|^{2}

and, by dropping the nonnegative trapping potential,

\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}[u_{n}]\geq\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{n}}\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{4}\right].

Multiplying both side by
\varepsilon_{n}^{2}
, dropping the nonnegative trapping term and using (
2.5
) we have

(2.7)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:minimizer-critical-boundedness-1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{n}}\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{4}\right]=0.

Using (
2.3
), (
2.5
), we deduce from (
2.6
) that
\{\widetilde{u_{n}}\}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Up to translation and extracting a subsequence, we have
\widetilde{u_{n}}\to\widetilde{u_{0}}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We prove that this is actually the strong convergence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We first note that
\widetilde{u_{0}}\not\equiv 0
. Otherwise, we must have that
u_{n}\to 0
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
2<p<\infty
(see
[
42
]
). This yields a contradiction, by (
2.7
) and (
2.6
). Next, we eliminate the case where
0<\|\widetilde{u_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<1
. We assume on the contrary that this is the case. It is worth noting that
\{|\widetilde{u_{n}}|\}
is also bounded uniformly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by the diamagnetic inequality (
2.5
). Then
|\widetilde{u_{n}}|\to|\widetilde{u_{0}}|
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We deduce from (
2.7
) and (
2.5
) and Brezis–Lieb lemma
[
8
]
that

\displaystyle 0\geq{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|\nabla|\widetilde{u_{n}}|\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|\nabla|\widetilde{u_{0}}|\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{4}\right]+\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|\nabla(|\widetilde{u_{n}}|-|\widetilde{u_{0}}|)|\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\left||\widetilde{u_{n}}|-|\widetilde{u_{0}}|\right|^{4}\right]

(2.8)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

On one hand, it follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) that

(2.9)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:minimizer-critical-dichotomy-2}I_{1}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}}-1\right)\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{4}>0,

On the other hand, by again (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),

(2.10)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:minimizer-critical-dichotomy-3}I_{2}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{n}}-\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}}-1\right)\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{n}}-\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{4}\geq 0.

Combining (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) we obtain a contraction. Therefore, we must have that
\|\widetilde{u_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=1
. This yields that, by Brezis–Lieb lemma
[
8
]
, up to translation and extracting a subsequence,
\widetilde{u_{n}}\to\widetilde{u_{0}}
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In fact, this strong convergence holds in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
2\leq p<\infty
, by interpolation. We then deduce from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{4}=\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{n}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{n}}\right|^{2}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{0}}\right|^{2}.

Here we have used Fatou’s lemma (see e.g.,
[
45
, Proposition 3.8]
) in the last inequality. Then (
2.4
) yields that we must have equality in the above. This, however, is not possible. Indeed, assume on the contrary that the equality in the above holds true. Then, by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),
|\widetilde{u_{0}}|=Q_{0}
where
Q_{0}
is the normalized unique solution (up to translation and dilation) of the equation (
1.8
). On the other hand, we can write
\widetilde{u_{0}}=|\widetilde{u_{0}}|e^{{\rm i}\varphi}
where
\varphi
is a real-valued function. Then we have

\displaystyle\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{0}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|-{\rm i}\nabla|\widetilde{u_{0}}|+|\widetilde{u_{0}}|(\nabla\varphi+\beta\mathbf{A}[\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}])\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla|\widetilde{u_{0}}|\right|^{2}+|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}\left|\nabla\varphi+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{0}}|^{2}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla Q_{0}\right|^{2}+Q_{0}^{2}\left|\nabla\varphi+\beta\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}^{2}]\right|^{2}

Therefore, we deduce from the equality in (
2.11
) that we must have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q_{0}^{2}\left|\nabla\varphi+\beta\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}^{2}]\right|^{2}=0,

which yields that

\nabla\varphi+\beta\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}^{2}]=0

almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By taking skew gradient both side in the above and using (
1.3
), we obtain that

2\pi\beta Q_{0}^{2}=0.

This is impossible whence
\beta>0
. Therefore, the equality in (
2.11
) cannot occur and we must have that
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. At this stage, the proof of existence of average-field-Pauli minimizers in the critical case where
g=g_{*}(0)
follows from the compactness of
\{u_{n}\}
in
H^{1}\cap L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

By using (

) with

2.2.
Collapse of average-field-Pauli minimizers in the weak field regime

In this section, we establish the collapse phenomenon in the average-field-Pauli theory. We prove the asymptotic formula of the average-field-Pauli energy and it minimizers as shown in
Theorem

2
. We recall that, for simplicity, we take in particular
V(x)=|x|^{s}
for
s>0
.

2.2.1.
The sub-critical case

In this subsection, we establish the behavior of
E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}
given by (
1.1
) as well as its minimizers when
\beta\searrow 0
slower than
g=g_{\beta}\nearrow g_{*}(0)
. Essentially, this is similar to the case where
\beta=0
which was done in
[
26
]
. For reader’s convenience, we give a detail proof in the following.

By the variational principle, we have

(2.12)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:energy-afP-subcritical-upper-bound}E_{\beta,g_{\beta}}^{\rm afP}\leq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{\beta}}[\ell^{-1}Q_{0}(\ell^{-1}\cdot)]=\ell^{-2}\left(1-\frac{g_{\beta}}{g_{*}(0)}\right)+\ell^{s}\frac{2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}+\ell^{-2}\beta^{2}\mathcal{A}_{0}

for any
\ell>0
. Since
\beta^{2}\ll g_{*}(0)-g_{\beta}
, the last term on the right hand side of (
2.12
) is of small order to the leading order term (the first term on the right hand side of (
2.12
)). By optimizing over all
\ell>0
the first two terms on the right hand side of (
2.12
), we obtain the energy upper bound in (
1.12
). We prove the energy lower bound by justifying the convergence of minimizers in (
1.11
).

Let’s denote
w_{\beta}=\ell_{\beta}u_{\beta}(\ell_{\beta}\cdot)
where
\ell_{\beta}
is given by (
1.13
). Then
\|w_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}=1
and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{\beta}}[u_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=\ell_{\beta}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}-g_{\beta}|w_{\beta}|^{4}\right]+\ell_{\beta}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\geq\ell_{\beta}^{-2}\left(1-\frac{g_{\beta}}{g_{*}(0)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}+\ell_{\beta}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}

where we have used (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). It follows from (
2.14
) and the energy upper bound in (
1.12
) that

(2.15)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:minimizer-afP-subcritical-boundedness}\frac{s+2}{s}\left(1+o(1)_{\beta\to 0}\right)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}+\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}.

We deduce from (
2.15
), (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) that
\{w_{\beta}\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}\cap L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. After extracting a subsequence, we have
w_{\beta}\to w_{0}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
2\leq p<\infty
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\sup_{\|v\|_{L^{2}}=1}|\langle\nabla w_{0},v\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|v\|_{L^{2}}=1}\lim_{\beta\to 0}\left|\left\langle(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta},v\right\rangle\right|

(2.16)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\beta\to 0}\left\|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right\|_{L^{2}}

Multiplying both side of (
2.13
) by
\ell_{\beta}^{2}
, dropping the nonnegative external term, using again the energy upper bound in (
1.12
) and taking the limit
\beta\to 0
, we have

0\geq\lim_{\beta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}-g_{\beta}|w_{\beta}|^{4}\right]\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|\nabla v_{0}\right|^{2}-g_{*}(0)|v_{0}|^{4}\right]\geq 0.

Here we have used (
2.16
) in the next-to-last inequality and (
2.5
) in the last inequality. Therefore, we must have equality in the above and it yields that
v_{0}
is an optimizer of (
2.5
). Hence

w_{0}(x)=t_{0}Q_{0}(t_{0}x+x_{0})

for some
t_{0}>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
Q_{0}
being the (unique) real-valued solution of (
1.8
). We prove that
v_{0}\equiv Q_{0}
by verifying that
t_{0}=1
and
x_{0}\equiv 0
. Indeed, taking the limit
\beta\to 0
in (
2.15
) and using (
2.16
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{s+2}{s}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla w_{0}|^{2}+\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{s}|w_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle=t_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla Q_{0}|^{2}+t_{0}^{-s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{s}|Q_{0}(x-x_{0})|^{2}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\geq t_{0}^{2}+\frac{2}{s}t_{0}^{-s}.

Here we have used the rearrangement inequality as
Q_{0}
is symmetric decreasing and

is strictly symmetric increasing. It is elementary to check that

\inf_{r>0}\left(r^{2}+\frac{2}{s}r^{-s}\right)=\frac{s+2}{s}

and the equality is attained at

. Therefore, we must have equality in (

) and it yields that

and

. This conclude the energy lower bound in (

) as well as the convergence of blow-up sequence in (

).

2.2.2.
The critical case

We now establish the behavior of
E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}
given by (

) as well as its minimizers at the critical value

. In which case, the existence of minimizers can be obtained easily since the critical interaction strength

is strictly larger than

. However, the analysis on the collapse of minimizers seems complicated due to the lack of its compactness in the collapse regime. The strategy of proof follows from

By the variational principle, we have

(2.18)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:energy-afP-upper-bound-critical}E_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}^{\rm afP}\leq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}[\ell^{-1}Q_{0}(\ell^{-1}\cdot)]=\ell^{s}\frac{2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}+\ell^{-2}\beta^{2}\mathcal{A}_{0}

for any
\ell>0
. By optimizing (
2.18
) over all
\ell>0
, we obtain the energy upper bound in (
1.12
). We prove the energy lower bound by justifying the convergence of minimizers in (
1.11
). The proof is divided into several steps as in
[
18
]
with a few simplified arguments and is processed as follows.

Step 1 (Blow-up property).
The sequence
\{u_{\beta}\}_{\beta}
blows up in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
in the sense that

(2.19)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline lowup:property-critical}\lim_{\beta\to 0}\|\nabla u_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}=+\infty.

Indeed, assume for contradiction that
\{u_{\beta}\}_{\beta}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Using (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have that
\{u_{\beta}\}_{\beta}
is also uniformly bounded in
L_{V}^{2}
. Therefore, up to extraction a subsequence,
u_{\beta}\to u_{0}
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
2\leq p<\infty
. It then follows from the upper bound in (
1.12
) that

0\geq\lim_{\beta\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}[u_{\beta}]\geq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{0,g_{*}(0)}[u_{0}]\geq E_{0,g_{*}(0)}^{\rm afP}=0.

This yields that
u_{0}
is a minimizer for
E_{0,g_{*}(0)}^{\rm afP}=0
which, however, is impossible. Therefore, we must have (
2.19
).

Step 2 (Convergence of the blow-up sequence).
In this step, we show that there exists a sequence
\{x_{\beta}\}_{\beta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\varepsilon_{\beta}u_{\beta}(\varepsilon_{\beta}\cdot+x_{\beta})=Q_{0}

strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
where, by (
2.19
),

(2.20)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline ength:blowup-critical}\varepsilon_{\beta}:=\|\nabla u_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}\xrightarrow{\beta\to 0}0.

Let’s denote
w_{\beta}(x):=\varepsilon_{\beta}u_{\beta}(\varepsilon_{\beta}x)
. Then we have

(2.21)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:blow-up-critical-0}\|w_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}=1=\|\nabla w_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}.

We rewrite the average-field-Pauli energy as follows

(2.22)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline nergy:blow-up-critical}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}=\varepsilon_{\beta}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}-g_{*}(0)|w_{\beta}|^{4}\right]+\varepsilon_{\beta}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}.

Multiplying both side of (
2.22
) by
\varepsilon_{\beta}^{2}
, dropping the nonnegative trapping term and using the energy upper bound in (
1.12
), we get

(2.23)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:blow-up-critical-1}\lim_{\beta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}-g_{*}(0)|w_{\beta}|^{4}\right]=0.

Since, by (
2.3
),

(1-C\beta)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla w_{\beta}|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}])w_{\beta}\right|^{2}\leq(1+C\beta)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla w_{\beta}|^{2},

we deduce from (
2.23
) that

(2.24)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:blow-up-critical-2}\lim_{\beta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|\nabla w_{\beta}\right|^{2}-g_{*}(0)|w_{\beta}|^{4}\right]=0.

Looking back at (
2.21
), since
\{w_{\beta}\}_{\beta}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have, up to translation and extracting a subsequence,
w_{\beta}\to w_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We show that this is actually strong convergence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

We first note that
w_{0}\not\equiv 0
. Otherwise, we must have that
w_{\beta}\to 0
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
2<p<\infty
(see
[
42
]
). This yields a contradiction, by (
2.24
) and (
2.21
). On the other hand, if
0<\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<1
then it follows from (
2.24
) and Brezis–Lieb lemma
[
8
]
that

(2.25)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:blow-up-critical-dichotomy-0}0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[|\nabla w_{0}|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}|w_{0}|^{4}\right]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[|\nabla(w_{\beta}-w_{0})|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}|w_{\beta}-w_{0}|^{4}\right]=\tilde{I}_{1}+\tilde{I}_{2}.

By (
2.4
), we have

(2.26)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:blow-up-critical-dichotomy-1}\tilde{I}_{1}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{0}|^{2}}-1\right)\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{0}|^{4}>0.

Furthermore,

(2.27)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:blow-up-critical-dichotomy-2}\tilde{I}_{2}\geq\lim_{\beta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{\beta}-w_{0}|^{2}}-1\right)\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{\beta}-w_{0}|^{4}\geq 0.

Combining (
2.25
), (
2.26
) and (
2.27
) we obtain a contraction. Therefore, we must have
\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=1
. This yields that, by Brezis–Lieb lemma
[
8
]
, up to translation and extracting a subsequence,
w_{\beta}\to w_{0}
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In fact, this strong convergence holds in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
2\leq p<\infty
, by interpolation. We then deduce from Fatou’s lemma and (
2.24
) that

\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{0}|^{4}=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{\beta}|^{4}=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\nabla w_{\beta}\right|^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\nabla w_{0}\right|^{2}.

The above yields that we must have
w_{\beta}\to w_{0}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by (
2.4
). Moreover,
w_{0}
is an optimizer of the standard Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality (
2.4
). By the uniqueness (up to translations and dilations) of optimizers for (
2.4
), there exist
\lambda>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
w_{0}(x)=\lambda Q_{0}(\lambda(x+x_{0}))
. Since

\|\nabla w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=1
, we must have
\lambda=1
. Again, by the uniqueness of
Q_{0}
, we conclude that passing to a subsequence is unnecessary.

Step 3. Smallness of the imaginary part.
We write

w_{\beta}(x)=q_{\beta}(x)+{\rm i}r_{\beta}(x)

with
q_{\beta}
and
r_{\beta}
the real and imaginary parts of
w_{\beta}
, respectively. Then
q_{\beta}\to Q_{0}
and
r_{\beta}\to 0
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
since
w_{\beta}\to Q_{0}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Furthermore, we choose the phase of
w_{\beta}
such that
w_{\beta}
is the closest to its limit

\left\|w_{\beta}-Q_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\left\|e^{{\rm i}\theta}v_{\theta}-Q_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives the following orthogonality condition

(2.28)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline rtho-cond}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q_{0}r_{\beta}=0.

We observe that, by using the integration by part and the fact that
\nabla\cdot\nabla^{\perp}=0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{J}[w_{\beta}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]\left(q_{\beta}\nabla r_{\beta}-r_{\beta}\nabla q_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}\nabla r_{\beta}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]r_{\beta}\nabla q_{\beta}.

This yields that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{J}[w_{\beta}]\right|\leq 2\|\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}.

Here we have used the fact that
\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}
is bounded uniformly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
since
w_{\beta}\to Q_{0}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We deduce from the above that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\beta}^{2}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla q_{\beta}|^{2}+|\nabla r_{\beta}|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}(q_{\beta}^{4}+r_{\beta}^{4}+2q_{\beta}^{2}r_{\beta}^{2})-C\beta\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}.

One one hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(r_{\beta}^{4}+2q_{\beta}^{2}r_{\beta}^{2})\leq g_{*}(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{\beta}|^{2}r_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q^{2}r_{\beta}^{2}+g_{*}(0)\int(|w_{\beta}|^{2}-Q_{0}^{2})r_{\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q^{2}r_{\beta}^{2}+o(1)\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

Here we have used Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|w_{\beta}|^{2}-Q_{0}^{2})r_{\beta}^{2}\right|\leq\||w_{\beta}|^{2}-Q_{0}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\||w_{\beta}|^{2}-Q_{0}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\||w_{\beta}|+Q_{0}\|_{L^{4}}\||w_{\beta}|-Q_{0}\|_{L^{4}}\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

as well as the strong convergence
|w_{\beta}|\to Q_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. On the other hand, by the standard Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality (
2.4
), the strong convergence
q_{\beta}\to Q_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the fact that
\|\nabla Q_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=1
as well as
\|q_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\|w_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla q_{\beta}|^{2}-\frac{g_{*}(0)}{2}q_{\beta}^{4}\geq\|\nabla q_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(1-\|q_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}})=(1+o(1)_{\beta\to 0})\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Collecting all the above estimates, we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\beta}^{2}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[|\nabla r_{\beta}|^{2}-Q^{2}r_{\beta}^{2}+r_{\beta}^{2}\right]+o(1)\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-C\beta\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\langle r_{\beta},\mathcal{L}r_{\beta}\rangle+\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}o(1)_{\beta\to 0}-C\beta\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}},

where we denoted
\mathcal{L}:=-\Delta-Q^{2}+1
.

We now use the non-degeneracy property of
Q
to deal with the term of
\mathcal{L}
in the above. It is well-known (see
[
37
, Theorem 11.8 and Corrollary 11.9]
) that
Q
is the first eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
and the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=0
is non-degenerate. In particular, we have

\langle\mathcal{L}u,u\rangle\geq\lambda_{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

for all
u
orthogonal to
Q
, where
\lambda_{2}>0
is the second eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
. This together with the fact that

\langle\mathcal{L}u,u\rangle\geq\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

yield the estimate

(2.30)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline st-rn-0}\langle\mathcal{L}u,u\rangle\geq C\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

for some constant
C>0
and all
u
orthogonal to
Q
. It follows from (
2.29
), (
2.30
) and the orthogonality condition (
2.28
) that

\varepsilon_{\beta}^{2}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}\geq C\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-C\beta\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}.

This implies that

(2.31)

\displaystyle C\|r_{\beta}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon_{\beta}^{2}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}+\beta^{2}.

On the other hand, from (
2.4
) and the energy upper bound in (
1.12
) we have

C\beta^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}\geq E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}=\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g_{*}(0)}[u_{\beta}]\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|u_{\beta}|^{2}=\varepsilon_{\beta}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}.

We deduce from the above and the strong convergence
w_{\beta}\to Q_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
that

(2.32)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\beta}\leq C\beta^{\frac{2}{s+2}}.

Putting together (
2.31
), (
2.32
) and the energy upper bound in (
1.12
), we obtain

(2.33)

\displaystyle\|r_{\beta}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\beta.

Step 4. Identifying the blow-up limit.
By the definition of
\mathbf{A}
in (
1.2
) and of
\mathbf{J}
in (
2.2
), we have the following decomposition

(2.34)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline st:cross-term-0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{J}[w_{\beta}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|q_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}\nabla r_{\beta}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|r_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}\nabla r_{\beta}.

It is worth noting that
w_{0}
,
Q_{0}
as well as
w_{0}*Q_{0}
are radial symmetric. Using integration by part, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}|^{2}]Q_{0}\nabla r_{\beta}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}|^{2}]r_{\beta}\nabla Q_{0}=0.

Therefore, one can estimate the first term on the right hand side of (
2.34
) as follow

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|q_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}\nabla r_{\beta}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\mathbf{A}[|q_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}-\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}^{2}]Q_{0}\right)\nabla r_{\beta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{A}[|q_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}-\mathbf{A}[Q_{0}^{2}]Q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}

(2.35)

\displaystyle=o(\beta)_{\beta\to 0}

where we have used (
2.33
) and the strong convergence
q_{\beta}\to Q_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For the second term on the right hand side of (
2.34
), we use again (
2.33
) and (
2.3
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|r_{\beta}|^{2}]q_{\beta}\nabla r_{\beta}\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|r_{\beta}|^{2}]r_{\beta}\nabla q_{\beta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{A}[|r_{\beta}|^{2}]r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla r_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}

(2.36)

\displaystyle\leq C\beta^{3}.

Combining (
2.34
), (
2.35
) and (
2.36
), we arrive at

(2.37)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline st:cross-term}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{J}[w_{\beta}]=o(\beta)_{\beta\to 0}.

We now denote

\kappa_{\beta}=\frac{\varepsilon_{\beta}}{\beta^{\frac{2}{s+2}}}

which is bounded uniformly from above, by (
2.34
). Furthermore, it follows from (
2.22
), (
2.4
) and the energy upper bound in (
1.12
) that

(2.38)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline ower-bound:energy-critical}\frac{s+2}{s}\beta^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{\frac{2}{s+2}}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\frac{s}{s+2}}\geq\varepsilon_{\beta}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[2\beta\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]\cdot\mathbf{J}[w_{\beta}]+\beta^{2}|\mathbf{A}[|w_{\beta}|^{2}]|^{2}|w_{\beta}|^{2}\right]+\varepsilon_{\beta}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}.

Multiplying both sides of (
2.38
) by
\varepsilon_{\beta}^{2}{\beta}^{-2}
, dropping the nonnegative trapping term and using (
2.37
), we must have that
\kappa_{\beta}
is bounded away from zero in the limit
\beta\to 0
. Hence

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\kappa_{\beta}=\kappa_{0}>0.

Next, we determine
\kappa_{0}
by calculating the matching energy lower bound in (
1.12
). Multiplying both sides of (
2.38
) by
\beta^{-\frac{2s}{s+2}}
, using (
2.37
) and taking the limit
\beta\to 0
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{s+2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{\frac{2}{s+2}}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\frac{s}{s+2}}

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{0}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{A}[|w_{0}|^{2}]|^{2}|w_{0}|^{2}+\kappa_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{s}|w_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{0}^{-2}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x-x_{0}|^{2}|Q_{0}|^{2}

(2.39)

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{0}^{-2}\mathcal{A}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{s}\frac{2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}.

Here we have used the rearrangement inequality as
Q_{0}
is symmetric decreasing and
|x|^{s}
is strictly symmetric increasing. It is elementary to check that

\inf_{r>0}\left(r^{-2}\mathcal{A}_{0}+r^{s}\frac{2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}\right)=\frac{s+2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{\frac{2}{s+2}}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\frac{s}{s+2}}

and the equality is attained at

r_{0}=\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{-\frac{1}{s+2}}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\frac{1}{s+2}}.

Therefore, we must have equality in (
2.39
) and it yields that
\kappa_{0}=r_{0}
and
x_{0}=0
. This conclude the energy lower bound in (
1.12
) as well as the convergence of blow-up sequence in (
1.11
).

The proof of

). As usual, we prove the energy lower bound by justifying the convergence of minimizers in (

).

Let’s denote
w_{\beta}=\ell_{\beta}u_{\beta}(\ell_{\beta}\cdot)

is given by (

\|w_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}=1

\displaystyle\frac{s+2}{s}\left(1+o(1)_{\beta\to 0}\right)\geq{}

(2.43)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{Q}_{s}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{\beta}|^{2}.

We deduce from (
2.43
), (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) that
\{w_{\beta}\}
is uniformly bounded in

. Similarly to the arguments in

3.
Condensation and collapse in the many-body theory

The goal of this section is to prove
Theorem

3
. It is worth noting that the (regularized) average-field-Hartree theory interpolates between the many-body and the average-field-Pauli theories. The strategy of the proof is to compare the (regularized) quantum energy
E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}
in (
1.19
) and the (regularized) average-field-Hartree energy
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}
in (
1.22
). Therefore, it is necessary to make a relation between the average-field-Hartree and the average-field-Pauli theories. This might not difficult in this sub-critical regime where
0<g<g_{*}(0)
since the (regularized) magnetic potential can be neglected. However, this is non-trivial in the critical and super-critical.

3.1.
Condensation and collapse in the average-field-Hartree theory

In order to study the collapse phenomenon in the average-field-Hartree theory, we need the following lemma which relies on the convergence (rate) of the two-body interactions and the (regularized) kinetic energy with magnetic field.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\beta,R,\nu>0
and
W
be satisfying (
1.18
). Then for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

(3.1)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v-ineq:afH-afP-2body}0\leq\|u\|_{L^{4}}^{4}-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}W_{N^{\nu}}(x-y)|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\leq\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{4}o(1)_{N\to\infty}.

Assume in addition that
xW\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
then

(3.2)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v-rate:afH-afP-2body}0\leq\|u\|_{L^{4}}^{4}-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}W_{N^{\nu}}(x-y)|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\leq CN^{-\nu}\|xW\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

Furthermore, the non-interacting functional
\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{R,\beta,0}
converges pointwise to
\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,0}
as
R\to 0
and we have

(3.3)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:afH-afP-curvature}\left|\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{R,\beta,0}[u]-\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,0}[u]\right|\leq C\beta R\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

Proof.

The proof of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) can be found in
[
35
,
34
]
. On the other hand, (
3.3
) follows from
[
45
, Proposition 3.9]
.
∎

Theorem 4

(Condensation and collapse of the average-field-Hartree ground states)
.

Let
\eta,\nu>0
,
R=R_{N}\sim N^{-\eta}
and
W
be satisfying (
1.18
).

(i)

Let
\beta,g>0
be fixed with

. Assume that
\nu<\eta
if

. Then we have

(3.4)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:afH-afP-energy}\lim_{N\to\infty}E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}=E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}.

Moreover, if
\{u_{N}\}_{N}
is a sequence of (approximate) ground states of
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u_{N}]=E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}(1+o(1)_{N\to+\infty})
, then there exists a minimizer
u_{0}
of
E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}
such that, up to extracting a subsequence,

(3.5)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:afH-afP-ground-state}\lim_{N\to\infty}\|u_{N}-u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}=0.

(ii)

Let
Q_{0}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{s}
,
\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
\{\beta_{N}\}_{N}
with
\beta_{N}\searrow 0
,
\{g_{N}\}_{N}
with
g_{N}\to g_{*}(0)
, and
\{\ell_{N}\}_{N}
be as in
Theorem

2
. Assume that
xW\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\ell_{N}\sim N^{-\sigma}
with

(3.6)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline ollapse:speed-hartree}0<\sigma<\min\left\{\frac{\nu}{s+3},\frac{2\eta}{s+4}\right\}.

Assume further that
\nu<\eta
in the critical case and additionally
\sigma<\frac{2(\eta-\nu)}{s+2}
in the super-critical case. Then we have

(3.7)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline lowup:energy-afH}E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}=E^{\rm afP}_{\beta_{N},g_{N}}(1+o(1)_{N\to\infty})=\ell_{N}^{s}\left(\frac{s+2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}+o(1)_{N\to\infty}\right).

Moreover, if
\{u_{N}\}_{N}
is a sequence of (approximate) ground states of
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u_{N}]=E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}(1+o(1)_{N\to+\infty})
, then we have

(3.8)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline lowup:minimizer-afH}\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\ell_{N}u_{N}(\ell_{N}\cdot)-Q_{0}\|_{H^{1}}=0.

Proof.

First, we prove the convergence of Hartree energy in (
3.4
) as well as of its ground states in (
3.5
). Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a minimizer of
E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}
. It follows immediately from the variational principle together with (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) that

\lim_{N\to\infty}E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u_{0}]\leq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u_{0}]=\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u_{0}]=E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}.

This is the desired energy upper bound in (
3.4
).

We prove the matching lower bound (
3.4
) by verifying the convergence of ground states in (
3.5
). Let
\{u_{N}\}_{N}
be a sequence of (approximate) ground state of
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}
. In the stable regime where
0<g<g_{*}(\beta)
, we claim that
\{u_{N}\}_{N}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}\cap L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In fact, this is obvious in the case
0<g<g_{*}(0)
, by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). We prove that this still holds true when
g\geq g_{*}(0)
. We assume on the contrary that
\|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}}[|u_{N}|^{2}])u_{N}\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. We define
\widetilde{u_{N}}=\varepsilon_{N}u_{N}(\varepsilon_{N}\cdot)
with

(3.9)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:afH-ground-state-length}\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}}[|u_{N}|^{2}])u_{N}\right|^{2}.

Noticing that

(3.10)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:afH-ground-state-renormalized}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{2}=1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}[|\widetilde{u_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{2}.

Using (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
3.10
) with
\mathbf{A}
replaced by
\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}
, we deduce that
\widetilde{u_{N}}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We then use the first inequality in (
3.1
) to obtain that

(3.11)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:afH-ground-state}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u_{N}]\geq\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}[|\widetilde{u_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{2}-\frac{g}{2}\left|\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{4}\right]+\varepsilon_{N}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|\widetilde{u_{N}}|^{2}.

Multiplying both side of (
3.11
) with
\varepsilon_{N}^{2}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, dropping the nonnegative external potential, using (
3.10
) and the energy upper bound in (
3.4
), we deduce that

(3.12)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline onv:afH-afP-ground-state-0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{4}\geq\frac{2}{g}>0.

This, however, is impossible. Indeed, we first note that
\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\leq CN^{\nu}
, by (
1.18
) and (
3.9
). This yields that, under the assumption
\nu<\eta
,

R_{N}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\leq N^{-\eta+\nu}\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0.

We then use (
1.7
), (
3.3
), (
3.10
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{g_{*}(\beta)}\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{u_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{g_{*}(\beta)}\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}[|\widetilde{u_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{u_{N}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{g_{*}(\beta)}<\frac{2}{g}.

Here we have used the fact that
g<g_{*}(\beta)
. The above contradicts (
3.12
). Therefore, we must have that
\|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}}[|u_{N}|^{2}])u_{N}\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded uniformly. This in turn implies that
\{u_{N}\}_{N}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as well, by using again (
3.10
), (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) with
\mathbf{A}
replaced by
\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}}
. Then we use again (
3.3
) and the first inequality in (
3.1
) to obtain that

o(1)_{N\to\infty}+\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u_{N}]\geq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,0}[u_{N}]-\frac{g}{2}\|u_{N}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|u_{N}|^{2}.

It then follows from (
1.7
) and the energy upper bound in (
3.4
) that
\{u_{N}\}_{N}
is bounded uniformly in
L_{V}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Therefore, up to a subsequence,
u_{N}\to u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and strongly in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
2\leq r<\infty
. In particular, we have
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=1
and

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}[u_{N}]\geq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u_{0}]\geq E^{\rm afP}_{N^{\nu},\beta,g},

by (
3.1
), (
3.3
) and the weak lower semicontinuity (see
[
45
, Proposition 3.7]
). The above yields the desired energy lower bound in (
3.4
) as well as the convergence of ground states in (
3.5
).

Now we complete the proof of
Theorem

4
by establishing the asymptotic behavior of the (regularized) average-field-Hartree energy and its ground states in the collapse regime. By the variational principle and the second inequality in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[\ell_{N}^{-1}Q_{0}(\ell_{N}^{-1}\cdot)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{\beta_{N},g_{N}}[\ell_{N}^{-1}Q_{0}(\ell_{N}^{-1}\cdot)]+CN^{-\nu}\ell_{N}^{-3}+C\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-3}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\ell_{N}^{s}\left(\frac{s+2}{s}\mathcal{Q}_{s}+CN^{-\nu}\ell_{N}^{-s-3}+C\beta_{N}N^{-\eta}\ell_{N}^{-s-3}+o(1)_{N\to\infty}\right).

Since
\ell_{N}\sim N^{-\sigma}
with
\sigma>0
, the error term
N^{-\nu}\ell_{N}^{-s-3}
is negligible when
\sigma<\frac{\nu}{s+3}
. Furthermore, the error term
\beta_{N}N^{-\eta}\ell_{N}^{-s-3}
is also negligible when
\sigma<\frac{2\eta}{s+4}
in the all three cases of
Theorem

2
. This gives the energy upper bound in (
3.7
).

We establish the matching energy lower bound in (
3.7
) by verifying the convergence of the blowup sequence in (
3.8
). Let
u_{N}
be a sequence of (approximate) ground state of
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}
and
w_{N}:=\ell_{N}u_{N}(\ell_{N}\cdot)
. Then,
\|w_{N}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{N}\|_{L^{2}}=1
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u_{N}]={}

\displaystyle\ell_{N}^{-2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta_{N}\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}}[|w_{N}|^{2}])w_{N}\right|^{2}\right.

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{g_{N}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}W_{N^{\nu}\ell_{N}}|w_{N}(x)|^{2}|w_{N}(y)|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\right]+\ell_{N}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{N}|^{2}.

First, in the sub-critical regime where
g_{N}\nearrow g_{*}(0)
faster than
\beta_{N}^{2}\to 0
, the magnetic potential
\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}}[|w_{N}|^{2}]
in (

) have no contribution to the leading order term in the collapse regime. By using (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
3.1
), (
3.14
), the proof of (

) is exactly the same as (

) in
Section

2.2.1
.

Next, in the critical regime where
g_{N}\equiv g_{*}(0)
, by using again (
2.3
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
3.1
), (
3.14
) with
\mathbf{A}
replaced by
\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}}
and
\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}}
, the proof of (
3.8
) is similar to (
1.11
) in
Section

2.2.2
. The only thing we need to verify is the convergence (

) where
\mathbf{A}
replaced by

This holds true, by (

Finally, in the super-critical regime where

in addition to the assumption

\|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta_{N}\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}}[|w_{N}|^{2}])w_{N}\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. We define
\widetilde{w_{N}}=\varepsilon_{N}w_{N}(\varepsilon_{N}\cdot)
with

(3.15)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:afH-blowup-length}\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}}[|w_{N}|^{2}])w_{N}\right|^{2}.

Noticing that

(3.16)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:afH-blowup-renormalized}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\widetilde{w_{N}}\right|^{2}=1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}[|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{w_{N}}\right|^{2}.

Using (

), (

) and (

replaced by

. By using (

), (
3.3
), (
3.1
), (
1.7

\displaystyle E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}\geq{}

\displaystyle\ell_{N}^{-2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta_{N}\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}[|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{w_{N}}\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{4}\right]+\ell_{N}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V|w_{N}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\ell_{N}^{-2}\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta_{N}\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{w_{N}}\right|^{2}-\frac{g_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{4}-C\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\ell_{N}^{-2}\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}\left[\left(1-\frac{g_{N}}{g_{*}(\beta_{N})}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta_{N}\mathbf{A}[|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{w_{N}}\right|^{2}-C\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\ell_{N}^{-2}\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}\left[\left(1-\frac{g_{N}}{g_{*}(\beta)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta_{N}\mathbf{A}_{R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}}[|\widetilde{w_{N}}|^{2}])\widetilde{w_{N}}\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-C\left(1-\frac{g_{N}}{g_{*}(\beta)}\right)\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}-C\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-1}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\right].

3.16
), the definition of the blowup length
\ell_{N}

(3.17)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:afH-blowup}E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}\geq\varepsilon_{N}^{-2}\ell_{N}^{s}\left[\Gamma-C\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-s-3}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\right].

). It is worth noting that, by (

\beta_{N}R_{N}\ell_{N}^{-s-3}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}\leq CN^{\sigma\frac{s+2}{2}-\eta+\nu}\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0.

\ell_{N}

w_{N}

3.2.
Condensation of the quantum energy and its ground states in the mean-field regime

In the mean-field regime, we recall that
\beta>0
and
0<g<g_{*}(\beta)
are fixed. The rigorous derivation of the average-field-Pauli theory (
1.1
) in the mean-field limit regime can be obtained by combining the well-known results for non-interacting anyon gases
[
45
]
and for interacting Bose gases
[
33
]
. In the mentioned articles, the method of proof is the (quantitative) quantum de Finetti theory
[
52
,
54
]
. The idea is to use the orthogonal projector

P:=\mathbbm{1}(h\leq L)\quad\text{with}\quad h=-\Delta+V

to project the many-body ground state into finite dimensional space and then bound the full (regularized) energy from below in terms of projected state of
H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}
. In particular, if
V(x)=|x|^{s}
then the projected Hilbert space
PL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is of finite dimensional and we have (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Lemma 3.3]
)

(3.18)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline st:CLR}\dim(PL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\leq CL^{1+\frac{2}{s}}.

Let
\Psi_{N}
be a many-body ground state of
H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}
. It follows from
[
45
,
33
]
that, with
P^{\perp}=\mathbbm{1}-P
and for every
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Psi_{N},H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}\Psi_{N}\rangle}{N}\geq{}

\displaystyle\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}[u]{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)+CL\tr\left[P^{\perp}\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(1)}\right]

\displaystyle-C\frac{L^{2+\frac{2}{s}}}{N}\left(1+|\log R|\right)-\frac{C_{\beta}}{N}-\left(\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{1+\varepsilon}}+\frac{C}{LR^{2}}+\frac{CN^{\frac{3\nu}{2}}}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\tr\left[h\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{{(1)}}\right].

Here it is worth noting that the last error term in the above came from the estimate of the two-body interactions when projecting onto the finite dimensional space. For this purpose, we made use of
[
35
, Equation (44)]
as well as the facts that
W_{N^{\nu}}\leq CN^{2\nu}
and that
Q\leq L^{-1}h
. We do not detail the proof of this since a similar argument has been detailed in
[
50
]
. It is worth noting that the authors in
[
35
]
used a refined operator bound for two-body interactions and a second moment estimate in order to obtain BECs for a wide range of
\nu>0
(see also
[
47
]
). This, however, is not available for three-body problem in high dimensional space such as interacting anyon gases in the present article.

We next optimize the error in (
3.19
) by choosing optimally
L>0
. Assuming that
R>0
behaves at worst as
R\sim N^{-\eta}
with
\eta>0
. We might ignore the
|\log R|
and
R^{\varepsilon}
factors, by changing a little bit
\eta
. Roughly speaking, by a bootstrap argument as used in
[
35
,
47
]
, we can prove that
\tr\left[h\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{{(1)}}\right]
is bounded uniformly, provided that

0<\nu<\frac{s}{6s+6}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\eta<\frac{s}{4s+4}.

To see this, we consider the modified Hamiltonian by a small perturbation of non-interacting Hamiltonian, i.e., with
0<\varepsilon<1
,

H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g,\varepsilon}:=H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}-\varepsilon H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,0}.

Let
E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g,\varepsilon}
be the corresponding ground state energy. We then make use of the Schrödinger equation satisfying by the ground state
\Psi_{N}
of
H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}
and deduce that

(3.20)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:qm-ground-state}\frac{\langle\Psi_{N}|H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,0}|\Psi_{N}\rangle}{N}\leq\frac{E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}-E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g,\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\leq C\frac{1+\left|E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g,\varepsilon}\right|}{\varepsilon}.

The uniform boundedness of the above follows from (
3.19
) and a bootstrap argument as in
[
35
,
47
]
. This also yields that
\tr\left[h\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{{(1)}}\right]
is bounded uniformly as well (see
[
45
, Proposition 2.6]
). Looking back at (
3.19
), it is necessary that
L\gg N^{3\nu}
and
L\gg N^{2\eta}
in order to obtain the convergence of energy. We thus have to optimize

\frac{L^{2+\frac{2}{s}}}{N}+\frac{N^{\frac{3\nu}{2}}+N^{\eta}}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The optimal choice of
L>0
is

(3.21)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:L}L\sim\left(N^{\frac{3\nu}{2}+1}+N^{\eta+1}\right)^{\frac{2s}{5s+4}}\quad\text{with}\quad\nu<\frac{s}{6s+6}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\eta<\frac{s}{4s+4},

which yields that the de Finetti measure
\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}
is tight as
N\to\infty
. Indeed, this follows from the above choice of
L
and the estimates

(3.22)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline st:de-Finetti-measure}1\geq\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)=\tr\left[P^{\otimes 3}\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}P^{\otimes 3}\right]\geq 1-3\tr\left[P^{\perp}\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(1)}\right]\geq 1-3L^{-1}\tr\left[h\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(1)}\right].

Looking back at (
3.19
), by collecting all above estimates, we arrive at the final estimate

(3.23)

\displaystyle E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}\geq E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u]{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)-CN^{\frac{\nu(6s+6)-s}{5s+4}}-CN^{\frac{\eta(4s+4)-s}{5s+4}}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\geq E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)-CN^{\frac{\nu(6s+6)-s}{5s+4}}-CN^{\frac{\eta(4s+4)-s}{5s+4}}.

Here we have used the fact that, for
N
large enough,
\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u]\geq 0
(see
Theorem

4
). It is worth noting that it was required
\nu<\eta
in the case where
g_{*}(0)\leq g<g_{*}(\beta)
. In the stable regime where
0\leq g<g_{*}(\beta)
, we deduce the desired convergence (
1.25
) of the average-field quantum energy to the average-field-Pauli energy from (
3.24
), (
3.22
) and (
3.4
).

Next, we prove the convergence of many-body ground states in (
1.25
). Let
\Psi_{N}
be a sequence of ground states of
H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g}
. Since
\tr\left[h\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(1)}\right]
is bounded uniformly, modulo a subsequence, we may assume that (see
[
32
]
)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\tr\left|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(k)}-\gamma^{(k)}\right|=0,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, by the quantitative quantum de Finetti theorem (see e.g.,
[
33
]
) and (
3.18
), (
3.21
), we have

(3.25)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:qm-ground-state-finite}\tr\left|P^{\otimes 3}\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}P^{\otimes 3}-\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}|u^{\otimes 3}\rangle\langle u^{\otimes 3}|{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\right|\leq C\frac{\dim(PL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}{N}\leq C\frac{L^{1+\frac{2}{s}}}{N}\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0.

It is worth noting that
\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\to 1
as
N\to\infty
, by (
3.22
). Then it follows from (
3.25
) that

(3.26)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline oundedness:qm-ground-state-Finetti}\lim_{N\to\infty}\tr\left|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}-\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}|u^{\otimes 3}\rangle\langle u^{\otimes 3}|{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\right|=0.

Testing the above with a sequence of finite rank orthogonal projectors
P_{K}\to 0
as
K\to\infty
, and using the strong convergence of
\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}
, we obtain that the measure
\{\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}\}_{N}
is tight on the set of one-body pure states. Therefore, there exists a limit measure
\mu
supported on the unit ball of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We then deduce that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\tr\left|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}-\int_{SL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}|u^{\otimes 3}\rangle\langle u^{\otimes 3}|{\rm d}\mu(u)\right|=0

which in turn implies that

(3.27)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:quantum-groundstate}\lim_{N\to\infty}\tr\left|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(1)}-\int_{SL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}|u\rangle\langle u|{\rm d}\mu(u)\right|=0.

To complete the proof of (
1.26
), it remains to prove that
\mu
in (
3.27
) is supported on the set of average-field-Pauli ground states
\mathcal{M}^{\rm afP}
.

Looking back at (
3.23
), we split the integral into two disjoint parts, i.e.,
SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\mathrm{K}_{-}\cup\mathrm{K}_{+}
where

\mathrm{K}_{-}=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{R_{N},\beta,g}[u]\leq C_{\mathrm{kin}}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{K}_{+}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\mathrm{K}_{-}.

with
C_{\mathrm{kin}}>0
a large constant independent of
N
. For the high kinetic energy part
\mathrm{K}_{+}
, we consider the perturbed average-field-Hartree functional

\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g,\varepsilon}[u]=\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u]-\varepsilon\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{R_{N},\beta,0}[u]

with the corresponding modified Hartree energy
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g,\varepsilon}
. Here
\lambda
is fixed such that either
0<\varepsilon<1-\frac{g}{g_{*}(0)}
if
g<g_{*}(0)
or
0<\varepsilon<1
if
g\geq g_{*}(0)
and
\nu<\eta
. We may return to
Theorem

4
and derive a similar energy lower bound (
3.4
) with
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}
replaced by
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g,\varepsilon}
. In particular, we find that
E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g,\varepsilon}\geq-C_{\varepsilon}
and deduce that

(3.28)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:ground-states-split-1}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u]\geq\varepsilon\mathcal{E}^{\rm afP}_{R_{N},\beta,0}[u]-C_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{\varepsilon C_{\mathrm{kin}}}{2},\quad\forall u\in\mathrm{K}_{+}

for a large enough
C_{\rm kin}>0
. Plugin (
3.28
) into (
3.23
), we deduce that

\displaystyle E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}\geq E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}\geq{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{K}_{+}}\frac{\varepsilon C_{\mathrm{Kin}}}{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)+\int_{\mathrm{K}_{-}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u]{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)-o(1)_{N\to\infty}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon C_{\mathrm{Kin}}}{2},\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}[u]\right\}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)-o(1)_{N\to\infty}.

Passing to the limit
N\to\infty
, using the convergences of energy (
3.4
), of ground states (
3.5
) and of measures
\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}\to\mu
, and taking finally
C_{\rm kin}\to\infty
, we arrive at

(3.29)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:ground-states-last-step}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}\geq\lim_{N\to\infty}E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta,g}\geq\int_{SL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}[u]{\rm d}\mu(u)\geq E^{\rm afP}_{\beta,g}.

The above shows that
\mu
must be supported on
\mathcal{M}^{\rm afP}
. This proved (
1.26
).

3.3.
Collapse of the quantum energy and its ground states in the weak field regime

We complete the proof of
Theorem

3
by establishing the collapse phenomenon. The collapse of average-field quantum energy in (
1.28
) follows from (
3.24
), (
3.22
) and
Theorem

4
. It remains to prove the collapse of average-field many-body ground states in (
1.29
).

Looking back at (
3.20
), we choose
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N}
equaling to either
C\left(1-\frac{g_{N}}{g_{*}(0)}\right)
in the sub-critical case, or
C\beta_{N}^{2}
in the critical and super-critical cases. Here
C>0
is a universal small constant. We may return to
Theorem

3
and derive a similar asymptotic formula for
E^{\rm afQM}_{N^{\nu},R,\beta,g,\varepsilon_{N}}
. We then deduce from (
3.20
) and arguments in
[
45
, Proposition 2.6]
that

\tr\left[h\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{{(1)}}\right]\leq C\ell_{N}^{-2}.

Plugin this into (
3.19
) and (
3.22
), we obtain the estimate of energy

(3.30)

\displaystyle E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\langle\Psi_{N},H^{\rm af}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}\Psi_{N}\rangle}{N}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u]{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)-C\frac{L^{2+\frac{2}{s}}}{N}-C\frac{N^{\frac{3\nu}{2}}+N^{\eta}}{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ell_{N}^{-2}

and the estimate of the de Finetti measure

(3.31)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline st:de-Finetti-measure-collapse}1\geq\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\geq 1-3L^{-1}\ell_{N}^{-2}.

It is straightforward that, if additionally to (
1.27
) we assume further that

\sigma<\min\left\{\frac{s-\nu(6s+6)}{5s^{2}+12s+8},\frac{s-\eta(4s+4)}{5s^{2}+12s+8}\right\}

then we can choose
L>0
optimally in (
3.30
) such that

(3.32)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:qm-energy-measure}\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u]}{E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)=1=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u).

Here the second equality in (
3.32
) follows from (
3.31
), (
1.24
) and (
1.27
).

Next, by using (
3.32
) and (
3.25
), we then obtain again (
3.26
) which in turn implies that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\tr\left|\gamma_{\Psi_{N}}^{(1)}-\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}|u\rangle\langle u|{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\right|=0.

The convergence of the one-particle density matrix then reduces to the following

(3.33)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline lowup:qm-ground-state-completed}\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left|\langle u,Q_{N}\rangle\right|{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)=1,

where
Q_{N}=\ell_{N}^{-1}Q_{0}(\ell_{N}^{-1}\cdot)
. To prove this, we come back to (
3.32
). We split the integral into two disjoint parts: one is of small de Finetti measure and one contains “approximate” average-field-Hartree ground states. For this purpose, we define the nonnegative sequence of numbers

(3.34)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline umber:approximate-afP}\delta_{N}:=\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u]}{E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}}-1\right){\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)

which converges to
0
as
N\to\infty
, by (
3.32
). Furthermore, we introduce the set

(3.35)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline et:approximate-afP}\mathcal{T}_{N}:=\left\{u\in SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):0\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u]}{E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}}-1\leq\sqrt{\delta_{N}},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}=1\right\}.

For any sequence
\{u_{N}\}_{N}\subset\mathcal{T}_{N}
, by the same arguments as in the proof of
Theorem

4
, we can prove that
\ell_{N}u_{N}(\ell_{N}\cdot)
converges strongly to
Q_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Equivalently, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left|\langle u_{N},Q_{N}\rangle\right|=1

which in turn yields that

(3.36)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline v:approximate-afP}\lim_{N\to\infty}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left|\langle u,Q_{N}\rangle\right|=1.

On the other hand, by the definition of
\delta_{N}
in (
3.34
) and of
\mathcal{T}_{N}
in (
3.35
), we have

\delta_{N}\geq\int_{\mathcal{T}_{N}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}[u]}{E^{\rm afH}_{N^{\nu},R_{N},\beta_{N},g_{N}}}-1\right){\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\geq\sqrt{\delta_{N}}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(\mathcal{T}_{N}^{\prime}).

Since
\delta_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, the above yields that
\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(\mathcal{T}_{N}^{\prime})\to 0
and hence
\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(\mathcal{T}_{N})\to 1
, by (
3.32
). We then deduce from (
3.36
) that

\int_{SPL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left|\langle u,Q_{N}\rangle\right|{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\geq\int_{\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left|\langle u,Q_{N}\rangle\right|{\rm d}\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(u)\geq\mu_{\Psi_{N}}^{(3)}(\mathcal{T}_{N})\inf_{u\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left|\langle u,Q_{N}\rangle\right|\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}1.

This is the desired convergence (
3.33
). The proof of (
1.29
) is finished.

Appendix A
Magnetic Gagliardo–Nirenberg optimizers in the weak-field limit

Proposition 2
.

Let
u_{0}
be the (unique) minimizer for
g_{*}(0)
and
u_{\beta}
be a sequence of minimizers of
g_{*}(\beta)
, given by (
1.6
), in the limit
\beta\to 0
. Then we have, for the whole sequence,
u_{\beta}\to u_{0}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, up to translation, dilation and phase transition. Furthermore,
g_{*}(\beta)\to g_{*}(0)
.

Proof.

The convergence
g_{*}(\beta)\to g_{*}(0)
follows immediately from the estimates (
1.16
). On the other hand, the proof of convergence of minimizers is based on concentration compactness method
[
41
,
42
]
. By scaling, we assume without loss of generality that
\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}=1
. Then
\{u_{\beta}\}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hence, up to translation, dilation and phase transition,
u_{\beta}
converges to
u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=1
then, by Brezis–Lieb lemma
[
8
]
,
u_{\beta}
converges to
u_{0}
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In fact, this strong convergence holds true in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for every
2\leq p<\infty
. This yields that, by Fatou’s lemma (see (
2.16
)),

g_{*}(0)=\lim_{\beta\to 0}g_{*}(\beta)=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|u_{\beta}|^{2}])u_{\beta}\right|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{\beta}|^{4}}\geq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{0}|^{4}}.

This proves that
u_{0}
is the minimizer for
g_{*}(0)
, by (
2.4
). Since this minimizer
u_{0}
is unique (up to translation and dilation), the convergence holds for the whole sequence.

It remains to eliminate the case where
0\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<1
. Indeed, if
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0
then we must have that
u_{\beta}\to 0
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for every
2<p<\infty
(see
[
42
]
). However, this contradicts our assumption that
\|u_{\beta}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}=1
. Finally, we now assume that
0<\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<1
. Then it follows from (
2.3
) and Brezis–Lieb lemma
[
8
]
that

\displaystyle g_{*}(0)=\lim_{\beta\to 0}g_{*}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|(-{\rm i}\nabla+\beta\mathbf{A}[|u_{\beta}|^{2}])u_{\beta}\right|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{\beta}|^{4}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u_{\beta}|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{\beta}|^{4}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla(u_{\beta}-u_{0})|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{0}|^{4}+|u_{\beta}-u_{0}|^{4}}

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{0}|^{4}},\liminf_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla(u_{\beta}-u_{0})|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{\beta}-u_{0}|^{4}}\right\}.

However, this is impossible. Indeed, by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{0}|^{4}}\geq\frac{g_{*}(0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{0}|^{2}}>g_{*}(0).

Furthermore, by again (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),

\liminf_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla(u_{\beta}-u_{0})|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{\beta}-u_{0}|^{4}}\geq\liminf_{\beta\to 0}\frac{g_{*}(0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{\beta}-u_{0}|^{2}}=\frac{g_{*}(0)}{1-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{0}|^{2}}>g_{*}(0).

Collecting all the above estimates, we obtain a contradiction. The proof is completed.
∎

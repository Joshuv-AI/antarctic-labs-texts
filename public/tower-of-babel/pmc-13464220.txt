Genetic susceptibility of Alzheimer's disease associated with APOE polymorphism: systematic review and bibliometric study
Authors: Danang Prasetyaning Amukti, Nurul Kusumawardani, Ria Indah Pratami, Barkah Djaka Purwanto, Rafi Achmad Rukhama, Imam Akbar, Raden Jaka Sarwadhamana, Riko Anugrah Aditya, Sapto Yuliani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAlzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that is the leading cause of dementia worldwide. Genetic factors, particularly Apolipoprotein E (APOE) polymorphisms, are known to play a significant role in increasing susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease. The increasing number of publications related to APOE and Alzheimer's disease has prompted the need for bibliometric analysis and systematic reviews to comprehensively understand research developments and their genetic relationships. This study aims to analyze the global development of research on APOE polymorphisms in Alzheimer's disease through a bibliometric study and systematic review approach.MethodsThe study used a combined bibliometric and systematic review design. Data were obtained from the Scopus database for the period 2000–2025 using keywords Title-Abs-Key ((“APOE” Or “APOE E4” Or “Apolipoprotein E4”) And (“Alzheimer's disease”And (“Genetic Susceptibility”Or”Risk Factor”)) And (Limit-To (Doctype,”Ar”)) And (Limit-To(Language,”English”)) And Pubyear > 1999 And Pubyear < 2026. Bibliometric analysis was performed using VOSviewer and Microsoft Excel to evaluate publication trends, countries, institutions, journals, author collaborations, and co-occurrence keywords . A systematic review was conducted on 26 articles that met the inclusion criteria to evaluate the association of APOE polymorphisms with Alzheimer's risk.ResultsA total of 3824 articles from 718 publication sources were identified with an average citation of 51.51 per document. The United States was the country with the highest publication contribution. Keyword co-occurrence analysis showed that APOE4, genetics, and biomarkers were the main research themes. The results of the systematic review showed that APOE polymorphisms, especially rs429358 and rs7412, had a significant association with an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease in various ethnic populations with a very low p-value of up to 1E-320.ConclusionAPOE polymorphisms play an important role in the genetic susceptibility of Alzheimer's disease and have the potential to be key biomarkers in early detection and the development of precision medicine in neurodegenerative diseases.Keywords: Alzheimer's disease, Apolipoprotein E, Single nucleotide polymorphism, Precision medicine, Bibliometric analysis

Introduction

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is the most common neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive cognitive decline, memory impairment, behavioral changes, and a gradual decline in the ability to perform daily activities.1, 2 This disease is becoming an increasingly serious global health challenge as life expectancy increases and the population ages in various countries.3 According to the World Health Organization, dementia affects millions of people worldwide, with approximately 60–70% of dementia cases being caused by Alzheimer's disease.4, 5 Pathologically, Alzheimer's is characterized by the accumulation of extracellular amyloid-beta plaques, the formation of neurofibrillary tangles due to hyperphosphorylation of tau protein, neuroinflammation, synaptic damage, and progressive neuronal death.6, 7 Although age is the primary determinant, various studies have shown that genetic factors play a significant role in an individual's susceptibility to Alzheimer's. Advances in genomic and neurogenetic technologies over the past two decades have opened up new insights into the molecular mechanisms of this disease, particularly regarding genetic polymorphisms that play a role in increasing the risk of neurodegeneration.8 (See Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5.) One of the most extensively studied genetic factors in Alzheimer's disease is the Apolipoprotein E (APOE) gene, specifically the ε4 allele, which is known to be strongly associated with an increased risk of late-onset Alzheimer's disease.9, 10 The APOE gene, located on chromosome 19q13.2, functions in lipid metabolism, cholesterol transport, neuronal membrane repair, and the clearance of amyloid-beta in the brain. There are three main isoforms of APOE: ε2, ε3, and ε4, with APOE ε4 consistently reported as the most significant genetic risk factor for sporadic Alzheimer's disease.11, 12 Individuals carrying one ε4 allele have a higher risk of developing Alzheimer's disease than non-carriers, while individuals with two ε4 alleles show a significantly greater risk and earlier disease onset. Furthermore, APOE is also associated with increased amyloid-beta deposition, impaired mitochondrial function, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and accelerated brain atrophy. This complex relationship makes APOE a major focus in biomarker research, genetic risk prediction, and the development of targeted therapies in Alzheimer's disease.13

In recent years, the number of scientific publications on the relationship between APOE and genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's has increased significantly. Research focuses not only on molecular and clinical aspects but also encompasses epidemiological approaches, neuroimaging biomarkers, precision medicine, bioinformatics, and even artificial intelligence in disease risk prediction.14 This rapid growth in the literature necessitates a bibliometric approach to comprehensively map research developments. Bibliometric analysis allows for the identification of publication trends, the most productive countries and institutions, international collaboration patterns, core journals, influential authors, and the development of research topics based on co-occurrence keywords and citation networks.15, 16 This approach is crucial for describing the knowledge structure and direction of research development related to APOE and Alzheimer's globally. Furthermore, bibliometrics can also help researchers identify research gaps and opportunities for future multidisciplinary research development.17, 18

Although numerous studies have investigated the role of Apolipoprotein E in Alzheimer's disease, existing evidence remains fragmented across genetic association studies and bibliometric investigations. To date, only a limited number of studies have integrated quantitative knowledge mapping with a systematic synthesis of genetic evidence. Such integration is essential for identifying research hotspots while simultaneously evaluating the consistency of genetic associations across diverse populations. Furthermore, combining bibliometric analysis with a systematic review provides a more comprehensive understanding of both the evolution of scientific research and the robustness of the available evidence regarding the role of Apolipoprotein E polymorphisms in Alzheimer's disease. This integrated approach may facilitate the identification of emerging research directions, knowledge gaps, and potential translational applications in precision medicine.19 Most previous studies have focused solely on publication trends without conducting an in-depth synthesis of the scientific evidence regarding the association between APOE and genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's. Yet, systematic reviews play a crucial role in evaluating the consistency of research findings, comparing population characteristics, assessing research methodology, and identifying the biological mechanisms underlying the relationship between APOE and Alzheimer's disease progression.20 Integrating bibliometric studies and systematic reviews can yield a more comprehensive understanding, not only of the quantitative development of research but also of the quality and substance of published scientific findings. This combined approach is becoming increasingly relevant in the era of evidence- based medicine and translational neuroscience, which demands systematic synthesis of multidimensional data.21, 22

Based on this background, this study aims to analyze the global development of research on genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease related to APOE polymorphisms through a bibliometric study and systematic review approach.23 Bibliometric analysis was conducted to map publication trends, contributions by countries, institutions, authors, journals, and the evolution of research themes.24, 25 While the systematic review was used to evaluate scientific evidence related to the association of APOE with increased Alzheimer's risk, its pathogenesis mechanisms, and its clinical implications. This study is expected to provide a comprehensive overview of the APOE research landscape in Alzheimer's, identify future research directions, and provide a scientific basis for the development of early diagnosis strategies, genetic risk prediction, and precision therapy in neurodegenerative diseases.

Methods

This study followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines and employed a combined bibliometric analysis and systematic review design to comprehensively investigate the association between Apolipoprotein E (APOE) polymorphisms and genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease. The bibliometric analysis was conducted to map global scientific publication trends, collaboration networks, research productivity, and the evolution of research themes, whereas the systematic review synthesized and critically evaluated the available evidence regarding the association between APOE polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease risk across different populations.26

Data sources and search strategy

The research data were obtained from the Scopus database because it has a wide coverage of international journals and provides complete metadata for bibliometric analysis.27, 28 The literature search process was conducted in May 2026 using keywords compiled based on a combination of terms related to APOE, Alzheimer's disease, and genetic susceptibility. The search strategy used was as follows: Title-Abs-Key((“APOE” Or “APOE E4” Or “Apolipoprotein E4”)And(“Alzheimer's disease”)And (“Genetic Susceptibility” Or “Risk Factor”)) And (Limit-To (Doctype,”Ar”)) And (Limit-To(Language,”English”))And Pubyear > 1999 And Pubyear < 2026. The search was limited to research articles (articles), in English, and published between 2000 and 2025. This limitation was made to obtain articles with adequate scientific quality and relevance to the development of modern research related to APOE and Alzheimer's.29, 30

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for this study included: (1) original research articles; (2) articles discussing the relationship between APOE and Alzheimer's disease; (3) articles addressing genetic susceptibility or risk factors; (4) articles in English; and (5) articles indexed in the Scopus database. The exclusion criteria included: (1) review articles, conference papers, editorials, notes, and letters; (2) duplicate articles; (3) articles irrelevant to the research topic; and (4) articles with incomplete metadata.

Literature selection process

The article selection process was conducted systematically following the PRISMA guidelines. The selection stages included identification, screening, eligibility, and inclusion. All searched articles were exported in CSV and Bibtex formats from the Scopus database.31, 32 A duplication check was then performed using reference management software. The screening process was based on the title and abstract to determine the article's relevance to the research topic. Articles meeting the inclusion criteria were then further analyzed in a full-text review.

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometric analysis was performed using Bibliometrix (R package), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and Microsoft Excel to comprehensively evaluate the global research landscape on Apolipoprotein E polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease. Bibliometrix was used to calculate bibliometric performance indicators, including annual scientific production, citation analysis, the most productive authors, countries, institutions, journals, and thematic evolution. VOSviewer was employed to construct and visualize co-authorship networks, keyword co-occurrence networks, citation networks, country collaboration networks, and thematic mapping. Network visualizations were generated as network, overlay, and density maps to illustrate the intellectual structure, collaborative patterns, emerging research hotspots, and the temporal evolution of scientific knowledge in this field.16, 33

Systematic review

In the systematic review phase, articles that met the inclusion criteria were analyzed narratively to evaluate the association between APOE polymorphisms and genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease. Extracted data included PubMed ID, disease type, initial sample size, studied gene, single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) type, variant category, and statistical significance (p-value). Furthermore, information regarding study characteristics, study design, study population, genotyping method, primary outcome, and the association between APOE and Alzheimer's risk was analyzed. Data synthesis was performed descriptively to identify the consistency of findings between studies, the biological mechanisms underlying the association between APOE and neurodegeneration, and its clinical implications for early diagnosis and the development of precision medicine in Alzheimer's disease.26

Research procedures and data extraction

Article selection was conducted independently by two reviewers through title screening, abstract screening, and full-text eligibility assessment based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Data extraction was performed using a standardized form that included study characteristics such as authors, year of publication, country or ethnicity of the study population, study design, sample size, genotyping methods, APOE variants or single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), variant category, statistical significance (p-value), odds ratios, confidence intervals, and the main findings related to the association between APOE polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease susceptibility. The methodological quality and risk of bias of included studies were independently assessed by two reviewers using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool, which evaluates bias arising from the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of outcomes, and selection of the reported results. Any disagreements between reviewers were resolved through discussion and consensus.

Functional annotation of APOE variants

To evaluate the potential functional significance of the identified APOE variants, functional annotation was performed using HaploReg v4.2. The selected single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) identified from the systematic review were queried to obtain genomic location, functional annotation, sequence conservation (GERP and SiPhy), and allele frequency data from the 1000 Genomes Phase 1 populations (African, American, East Asian, and European). These annotations were used to characterize the biological relevance and population distribution of APOE variants associated with Alzheimer's disease.

Risk of bias assessment, protocol, and registration

The methodological quality and risk of bias of the included genetic association studies were independently assessed by two reviewers using the Quality of Genetic Association Studies (Q-Genie) instrument, which is specifically developed for evaluating genetic association studies. Any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion until consensus was achieved. The Q-Genie instrument evaluates study quality across 11 methodological domains, including: (1) scientific rationale, (2) selection and definition of outcomes, (3) selection and comparability of comparison groups, (4) technical classification of exposure, (5) non-technical classification of exposure, (6) other potential sources of bias, (7) sample size and statistical power, (8) a priori planning of analyses, (9) statistical methods and control for confounding, (10) appropriateness of the inferences drawn, and (11) appropriateness of the interpretation of results. Each domain was scored on a seven-point Likert scale, yielding a maximum possible score of 77. Based on the total score, studies were classified as good quality (≥55 points), moderate quality (36–54 points), or poor quality (≤35 points). Among the 26 studies included in the systematic review, all achieved total Q-Genie scores ranging from 75 to 76, indicating good methodological quality and an overall low risk of bias. Most studies received the highest ratings for scientific rationale, outcome definition, exposure assessment, statistical analysis, and interpretation of findings, while only minor reductions in score were observed in domains related to other potential sources of bias and sample size justification. The systematic review was conducted and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement. The review protocol was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under registration number CRD420261399405 before the study was initiated.

Results

Main information about data

The results of the bibliometric analysis indicate that research on the relationship between APOE polymorphisms and genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease experienced significant development during the period 2000–2025. A total of 3824 articles from 718 publication sources were identified with an annual growth rate of 1.79% and an average of 51.51 citations per document. The analysis also shows high scientific productivity with 19,584 authors involved and an average of 10.7 authors per article, indicating the dominance of multidisciplinary and international collaboration with a percentage of international co-authorship of 30.31%. In addition, 12,282 Keywords Plus and 5335 Author's Keywords were found, reflecting the broad scope of research topics related to APOE and Alzheimer's disease.

Most productive affiliations

Analysis of institutional affiliations shows that the University of California has the highest contribution to publications in research related to APOE polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease, with a total of 589 articles. Columbia University follows with 226 articles and Washington University School of Medicine with 215 articles. Furthermore, the University of Washington, Harvard Medical School, and the Mayo Clinic also contribute significant publications. The dominance of US institutions demonstrates the country's leading role in the development of genetic and neurodegenerative research, particularly regarding the relationship between APOE and Alzheimer's disease.

Most productive countries

Analysis of country contributions shows that the United States has the highest number of publications in research related to APOE polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease, with a total of 1464 articles (38.28%). Next in line are China with 411 articles (10.74%) and the United Kingdom with 189 articles (4.94%). Furthermore, Italy, Sweden, and Germany also show significant publication contributions. Sweden has the highest multiple country publication (MCP) rate at 58.53%, indicating a high level of international collaboration in research. Overall, these results demonstrate that developed countries play a dominant role in the development of genetic and neurodegenerative research related to Alzheimer's disease.

Most productive sources

Analysis of publication sources shows that the Journal of Alzheimer's Disease has the highest number of publications related to APOE polymorphism and Alzheimer's disease research, with a total of 358 articles. Next, Alzheimer's and Dementia contributed 198 articles, followed by Neurobiology of Aging with 159 articles and Neurology with 135 articles. Several other journals, such as Neuroscience Letters, Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience, and Alzheimer's Research and Therapy, also showed significant contributions. These results indicate that research on APOE and Alzheimer's disease is largely published in journals focused on neurology, neurodegeneration, geriatrics, and neuroscience.

Co-occurrence keywords analysis

Analysis of co-occurrence keywords using VOSviewer shows that the keyword “Alzheimer's disease” is the main focus of the research network with the highest level of connectivity to other keywords such as “polymorphism”, “APOE4”, “genetics”, “late-onset Alzheimer's disease”, and “biomarker.” Visualization results indicate that global research on Alzheimer's disease is dominated by studies of the association of the APOE ε4 polymorphism with genetic susceptibility, neurodegeneration mechanisms, neuroinflammation, and early diagnostic biomarkers. Furthermore, keywords such as “microglia”, “oxidative stress”, “functional connectivity”, and “Machine learning” represents the development of multidisciplinary research that integrates molecular genetics, neuroimaging, bioinformatics, and artificial intelligence in understanding Alzheimer's pathogenesis and the future development of precision medicine.

Co-authorship authors analysis

Analysis of author co-authorship using VOSviewer shows the existence of a broad and complex network of scientific collaborations in research related to Alzheimer's disease and APOE polymorphisms. The network visualization shows several key authors with high levels of connectivity, such as Bennett DA, Holtzman DM, Michaelson DM, Blennow K, and Tan L which serves as a collaborative hub in various research clusters. These results indicate that Alzheimer's research is dominated by multidisciplinary and international collaborations involving genetics, neurology, biomarkers, neuroimaging, and neuroscience. Furthermore, the formation of different colored clusters indicates the presence of specific research groups with specific study focuses, such as molecular genetics, cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers, neuroinflammation, and Alzheimer's epidemiology. The high level of interconnectedness among authors reflects the growing global collaboration in efforts to understand the mechanisms of pathogenesis and develop precision diagnosis and therapy for Alzheimer's disease.

Country collaboration network analysis

Country collaboration network analysis using VOSviewer shows that the United States is the main center of global collaboration in research related to APOE polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease, characterized by the largest node size and high connectivity with various countries such as China, Germany, the United Kingdom, Sweden, Italy, and Australia. Network visualization shows the formation of several international collaboration clusters reflecting different research focuses, ranging from molecular genetics, neurodegenerative biomarkers, genetic epidemiology, to precision medicine. In addition to European and North American countries, the contribution of Asian countries such as China, South Korea, and India is also seen to be increasing, indicating the development of Alzheimer's research as a multidisciplinary global health issue. The high connectivity between countries illustrates the importance of international collaboration in accelerating the development of understanding the mechanisms of Alzheimer's pathogenesis, identifying genetic risk factors for APOE, and innovation in early diagnosis and precision therapy in neurodegenerative diseases.

Systematic review of APOE polymorphism in Alzheimer's disease

A systematic review of 26 articles showed that the APOE gene, specifically the rs429358 and rs7412 variants, has a highly significant association with an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease in various ethnic populations. Most studies reported that missense variants in APOE ε4 are strongly associated with increased genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's with very high significance values, even reaching p-values of up to 1.00E-300. The rs429358 variant is the most predominant SNP found in European, African-American, Hispanic, East Asian, and Puerto Rican populations, indicating that APOE is a major genetic risk factor that is consistent across populations. In addition, several studies also reported the involvement of the rs7412 variant which contributes to the risk of neurodegeneration through mechanisms such as impaired lipid metabolism, increased amyloid-beta deposition, neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress. The analysis also showed that most studies used very large sample sizes, ranging from hundreds to over a million participants, thus strengthening the validity of the association between APOE polymorphisms and Alzheimer's disease. Populations with European ancestry dominated the research, but the increasing number of studies in African-American, Hispanic, and Asian populations demonstrates the growing interest in cross-ethnic genetic variation in Alzheimer's pathogenesis. These findings indicate that APOE ε4 not only serves as a key genetic biomarker in Alzheimer's risk prediction but also has significant potential in the development of precision medicine, early detection, and genetic-based therapeutic strategies for neurodegenerative diseases.

Functional annotation of APOE variants

Functional annotation using HaploReg v4.2 identified three APOE variants (rs7412, rs429358, and rs7259620), all located on chromosome 19 and classified as missense variants. These variants exhibited sequence conservation based on both GERP and SiPhy scores, indicating potential functional importance. Population allele frequency data from the 1000 Genomes Project showed that rs429358 and rs7259620 had the highest alternate allele frequency in African populations (AFR = 0.26), followed by European (EUR = 0.14), American (AMR = 0.11), and East Asian (ASN = 0.09) populations, whereas rs7412 showed lower frequencies across all populations (AFR = 0.09, AMR = 0.06, ASN = 0.08, and EUR = 0.07). These findings suggest that conserved APOE missense variants may contribute to the genetic susceptibility of Alzheimer's disease and support their functional relevance in disease pathogenesis.

Discussion

APOE and its relationship to Alzheimer's disease

Apolipoprotein E is one of the genes most involved in genetic susceptibility to Alzheimer's disease, particularly late-onset Alzheimer's disease (LOAD).34, 35 This gene is located on chromosome 19q13.2 and functions in lipid metabolism, cholesterol transport, neuronal membrane repair, and the clearance of amyloid-beta in the brain.36, 37 In the central nervous system, APOE is produced primarily by astrocytes and plays a crucial role in maintaining neuronal homeostasis. Several studies have shown that altered APOE function can lead to impaired amyloid-beta elimination, leading to amyloid plaque accumulation, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, synaptic dysfunction, and progressive neuronal degeneration, the hallmarks of Alzheimer's disease.38, 39 A systematic review of this study demonstrated that APOE consistently has a significant association with an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease across various ethnic populations, including Europeans, Asians, African Americans, and Hispanics.40 The high statistical significance across these studies reinforces the role of APOE as a major genetic risk factor in sporadic Alzheimer's disease. In addition to contributing to the formation of amyloid-beta plaques, APOE also affects tau protein hyperphosphorylation, mitochondrial function, blood-brain barrier permeability, and microglia activation, which triggers chronic neuroinflammatory processes. Therefore, APOE is currently used not only as a biomarker of genetic risk but also as an important target in the development of precision therapies and early detection strategies for Alzheimer's disease.41, 42

Polymorphism rs429358 and its relationship with Alzheimer's disease

rs429358 polymorphism is one of the key SNPs in the APOE gene most frequently associated with an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease.43 This SNP plays a role in the formation of the APOE allele, known to be the strongest genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's disease. Based on the results of a systematic review, most studies indicate that rs429358 has a highly significant association with Alzheimer's, with a very small p-value of up to 1E-320.44 These findings indicate that the presence of the APOE ε4 allele due to the rs429358 variant consistently increases the risk of neurodegeneration in various population groups. Biologically, the rs429358 variant causes changes in the structure of the APOE protein, thereby reducing the protein's ability to clear amyloid-beta from the brain. This condition leads to increased amyloid plaque deposition that triggers neuronal damage and progressive cognitive impairment.45, 46 Furthermore, APOE is also known to be associated with increased neuroinflammation, impaired neuronal lipid metabolism, decreased synaptic function, and more rapid hippocampal atrophy compared to non-carriers of the ε4 allele. Neuroimaging studies have also shown that individuals with rs429358 have higher amyloid accumulation even before the onset of clinical Alzheimer's symptoms. Thus, rs429358 not only serves as a genetic biomarker of Alzheimer's risk but also holds significant potential in disease prediction approaches and the development of genetic-based therapies.47

The rs7412 polymorphism and its relationship to Alzheimer's disease

In addition to rs429358, the rs7412 polymorphism is also an important SNP in the APOE gene that plays a role in determining the APOE isoform.48 Variation in rs7412 is associated with the formation of APOE ε2 and ε3 alleles, which have different biological effects on the risk of Alzheimer's disease.49 A systematic review showed that rs7412 has a significant association with Alzheimer's in various populations, with a very low p-value reaching 4.00E-123.50 Although the effect of rs7412 on Alzheimer's risk is not as strong as rs429358, this SNP still has an important contribution in modulating genetic susceptibility to neurodegeneration. Mechanistically, rs7412 affects the structure and function of the APOE protein in lipid metabolism and cholesterol transport in the central nervous system.51 The APOE ε2 allele associated with rs7412 is generally considered to have a protective effect against Alzheimer's because it increases the clearance of amyloid-beta and reduces amyloid plaque deposition. Conversely, certain combinations of rs7412 and rs429358 may increase disease risk through disruption of neuronal lipid homeostasis and chronic inflammation. Furthermore, recent research suggests that rs7412 is also associated with altered microglial immune responses, synaptic function, and susceptibility to oxidative stress. Therefore, the rs7412 polymorphism plays a crucial role in understanding individual variations in Alzheimer's risk and supporting the development of precision medicine strategies based on patients' genetic profiles.52

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that Apolipoprotein E (APOE) polymorphisms, particularly rs429358 and rs7412, are significantly associated with an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease across diverse ethnic populations, reinforcing their role as major genetic determinants of disease susceptibility. Bibliometric analysis revealed a continuous growth in global research on APOE and Alzheimer's disease, characterized by extensive multidisciplinary and international collaborations, particularly in the fields of genetics, biomarkers, neurodegeneration, and precision medicine. Furthermore, the systematic review confirmed that APOE variants contribute substantially to the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease through mechanisms involving amyloid-beta accumulation, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and impaired lipid metabolism, highlighting their potential as valuable biomarkers for early diagnosis and personalized therapeutic strategies. Future research should integrate multi-omics approaches, including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, together with artificial intelligence and advanced bioinformatics methodologies to improve genetic risk prediction, uncover novel molecular mechanisms, and accelerate the development of precision medicine for Alzheimer's disease.

Global, Regional, and National Burden of Acute Abdominal Conditions in Females—Appendicitis, Ectopic Pregnancy, Paralytic Ileus and Intestinal Obstruction, and Urolithiasis, 1990–2021: A Systematic Analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 with ARIMA Projections to 2030
Authors: Xiaohui Dong, Zhijun Huang, Hui Li, Xiaojie Fu, Xiaoling Liang, Bin Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute abdominal conditions--appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, and urolithiasis--are surgical emergencies carrying substantial risks of morbidity, mortality, and long-term disability. Introduction

Acute abdominal conditions constitute a leading global surgical emergency, responsible for 5–10% of all emergency-department presentations and characterised by the potential for rapid progression to life-threatening complications when diagnosis or intervention is delayed. Non-specific abdominal pain accounts for the largest share of presentations (30–41%), yet the predominant organic aetiologies--acute appendicitis (8–30%), ectopic pregnancy, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction (4–5%), and urolithiasis--drive the majority of time-critical surgical workloads and resource utilisation.1 These four disorders share the requirement for expeditious recognition and frequently operative or interventional management; any delay substantially increases the risk of perforation, haemorrhage, sepsis, bowel ischaemia, or maternal death.

Appendicitis remains the most common abdominal surgical emergency worldwide, predominantly affecting adolescents and young adults, with a global age-standardised incidence historically in the range of 96–100 cases per 100,000 adults and classic migratory right-lower-quadrant pain accompanied by anorexia. Ectopic pregnancy, defined as implantation of a fertilised ovum outside the uterine cavity, is a leading cause of first-trimester maternal mortality and comprises approximately 6.4% of acute gynaecological emergencies. Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, most often adhesion-related following prior abdominal surgery, is associated with high recurrence rates and prolonged hospitalisation. Urolithiasis, although rarely fatal, produces recurrent severe pain and obstructive uropathy that can complicate congenital or acquired genitourinary anomalies and frequently necessitates complex endourological procedures.

Despite their distinct pathophysiologies, these four conditions share several clinically and epidemiologically relevant characteristics: they all present as acute surgical emergencies with a high risk of rapid deterioration, they collectively account for a substantial proportion of emergency-department surgical workloads, they require timely access to diagnostic imaging and operative or interventional facilities, and their outcomes are demonstrably sensitive to the quality and accessibility of emergency health systems. These shared features mean that their combined burden provides a high-level indicator of the overall resilience of a health system’s emergency surgical capacity. Addressing them together--rather than in isolation--allows a unified perspective on shared systemic vulnerabilities and the optimisation of scarce acute-care resources. In resource-limited settings, inadequate access to basic surgical services results in persistently elevated rates of preventable mortality from otherwise curable diseases such as appendicitis. Beyond immediate threats, chronic sequelae--including persistent pain, infertility after ectopic pregnancy, short-bowel syndrome after extensive resection, and recurrent renal colic--lead to long-term disability, lost productivity, and substantial healthcare expenditure. Comprehensive, up-to-date epidemiological characterisation is therefore essential for prioritising health-system investments and designing context-specific interventions.

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study provides a standardised, internally consistent, and comparable framework for quantifying incidence, prevalence, mortality, years of life lost (YLLs), years lived with disability (YLDs), and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) across 371 diseases and injuries in 204 countries and territories.2 Annual iterations incorporate newly identified data sources, refined modelling algorithms, and improved covariate integration, thereby enabling robust cross-national and temporal comparisons even in data-sparse regions. Although the GBD collaborative network has published cause-specific estimates for each of these four conditions independently,3–6 no prior study has jointly analysed all four conditions together within the most current GBD 2021 framework, nor has any prior work combined integrated estimates with forward-looking ARIMA projections to 2030. Conducting a comparative analysis of these conditions together enables a unified quantification of the overarching burden on global emergency surgical systems, identification of shared systemic vulnerabilities, and cross-condition contrasts that would be invisible when each condition is examined in isolation.

This study presents an integrated assessment of the global, regional, and national burden of appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, and urolithiasis from 1990 to 2021 using GBD 2021 data. We provide stratified estimates by age, sex, 21 GBD regions, and five socio-demographic index (SDI) quintiles, perform decomposition analyses, quantify trends with the estimated annual percentage change (EAPC), and apply ARIMA modelling to project selected indicators to 2030. By synthesising and contrasting these four time-sensitive surgical conditions, we highlight shared and divergent epidemiological patterns and provide a forward-looking evidence base for emergency surgical capacity planning.

Evidence Before This Study

Separate GBD analyses have described the burden of appendicitis,3 ectopic pregnancy,4 paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction,5 and urolithiasis.6 Earlier iterations documented rising incidence of appendicitis and urolithiasis in many low-income and middle-income countries alongside stable or declining trends in high-income settings, persistently high mortality from ectopic pregnancy in low-SDI regions, and fragmented data on paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction. No prior study has jointly analysed all four conditions using the complete GBD 2021 framework or included forward projections.

Added Value of This Study

This study provides the logically integrated analysis of all four conditions within the most recent GBD 2021 dataset, enabling a direct comparison of their shifting proportional contributions to the total acute abdominal burden and their divergent disease trajectories. The complementary use of EAPC for historical trend description and ARIMA modelling for forward projections to 2030 offers a preliminary indication of possible future trajectories and associated health-system implications. Furthermore, the integration of outpatient and claims data improves the capture of the non-fatal burden, particularly for day-case appendicectomy and urolithiasis management. Together, these metrics offer a comparative evidence base for emergency surgical capacity planning and public health prioritisation.

The available evidence indicates that stable or declining age-standardised rates co-exist with a large absolute burden increase driven by demographic shifts. Pronounced socio-economic gradients and middle-SDI incidence peaks are consistent with an epidemiological transition pattern. Further investigation is warranted into the health-system, lifestyle, and demographic factors that may underlie these observed disparities.

Methods

Overview

We analysed data from the Global Burden of DiseasesInjuriesand Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2021. The overall GBD methodology, including data sources, processing pipelines, and modelling tools, has been described in detail elsewhere.2 Cause-specific estimates for appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, and urolithiasis were extracted from their respective GBD 2021 publications (appendicitis3 ectopic pregnancy4 Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction5 urolithiasis6) and harmonised for integrated comparative analysis. All procedures adhered to the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER).7

Data Sources and Case Definitions

Mortality estimates were derived from vital registration systems, verbal autopsy records, and surveillance datasets encompassing more than 870 sources across 134 countries and territories. Incidence and prevalence data were obtained from health insurance claims (United States, Poland, and Taiwan province of China), inpatient discharge records from 50 countries, and supplementary population-based studies. Where available, adjustments were made to incorporate outpatient and same-day care encounter. Condition-specific International Classification of Diseases (ICD-9 and ICD-10) codes were applied uniformly across all sources, with garbage-code redistribution and misclassification corrections performed centrally within the GBD framework.

Mortality Estimation

Cause-specific mortality rates were modelled using the Cause of Death Ensemble model (CODEm), incorporating predictive covariates such as the Socio-demographic Index (SDI), Healthcare Access and Quality Index, dietary factors, and lag-distributed income per capita. Final estimates were scaled to all-cause mortality envelopes through the CoDCorrect procedure.

Incidence, prevalence, and non-fatal burden estimation

Incidence was estimated with DisMod-MR 2.1, a Bayesian meta-regression tool that enforces internal consistency among incidence, prevalence, remission, and mortality while borrowing strength from data-rich regions via covariates including the Healthcare Access and Quality Index, fibre consumption, and urbanisation metric. Excess mortality rates were derived by matching cause-specific mortality to incidence data point. Years lived with disability (YLDs) were calculated by multiplying incident cases by condition-specific average durations and corresponding disability weight. Years of life lost (YLLs) were computed by multiplying death counts by reference standard life expectancy at the age of death. Disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) were the simple sum of YLLs and YLDs.

Socio-Demographic Stratification and Decomposition

The Socio-demographic Index (SDI) is a composite indicator that integrates income per capita, mean years of schooling, and total fertility rate to reflect overall levels of development. Values range from 0 to 1, with higher scores indicating greater socio-demographic advancement. All 204 countries and territories included in GBD 2021 were categorised into five SDI quintiles (low, low-middle, middle, high-middle, and high. This stratification enables direct comparison of disease burden among countries at similar development stages, facilitates identification of high- and low-performing settings, and supports policy learning from successful intervention. All estimates and decompositions were stratified by SDI quintile, GBD region, age, and sex, following the standardised methodological framework of the most recent GBD cycle.

Trend Analysis

Age-standardised rates (ASRs) per 100000 population were calculated using the GBD world standard population. Trends were quantified as the estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) derived from linear regression of the natural logarithm of the ASR against calendar year, with 95% confidence intervals.

Projections

Autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models were applied to generate exploratory projections of DALYs, deaths, incidence, and prevalence to 2030. ARIMA captures autocorrelation and non-stationarity through its three components: autoregressive (p), differencing (d), and moving-average (q) terms. Models were fitted to national-level GBD time series (1990–2021) using the auto_arima() function in Python (pmdarima package), with optimal orders selected by minimising the Akaike information criterion (AIC). Stationarity was achieved after first-order differencing (d=1) in most series; dominant orders were (0,1,0) or (1,1,0). Residual diagnostics confirmed white noise. Compared with traditional EAPC (log-linear regression), ARIMA distinguishes historical from future trends and provides explicit 95% confidence intervals for projections. Historical EAPC and ARIMA in-sample values matched closely (within 0.01%), confirming that the models reasonably reproduce known data; ARIMA additionally reveals non-linear accelerations or oscillations that are not captured by linear methods alone. All statistical analyses and visualisations were performed in R (version 4.3.1), with ARIMA projections implemented in Python (version 3.1.1).

Uncertainty and Sensitivity

Ninety-five per cent uncertainty intervals (UIs) were generated by propagating 1000 posterior draws through every modelling ste. Final point estimates are reported to three significant figures.

Statistical Software and Visualisations

All statistical analyses and visualisations—including global maps of DALYs and incidence, SDI scatter plots, EAPC bar charts, stacked-bar decompositions, and age-specific line graphs—were performed in R(version 4.3.1). ARIMA projections were implemented in python (version 3.1.1).

Role of the Funding Source

The funder had no role in study design, data collection, analysis, interpretation, or manuscript preparation.

Results

Global Burden and Temporal Trends

Overall Incidence Trends Between 1990 and 2021, the combined age-standardised incidence rate (ASR) of the four acute abdominal conditions--appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, and urolithiasis--remained essentially stable at 1,200 (95% UI 1,150–1,250) per 100,000 population (EAPC −0.05%, 95% CI −0.10 to 0.00) (Figure 1). In contrast, the absolute number of incident cases rose by 85% (80–90%), from 50 million (48–52 million) in 1990 to 92 million (88–96 million) in 2021. This rise coincided with global population growth and demographic ageing.

Overall Mortality and DALY Trends

The combined age-standardised DALY rate declined (EAPC −1.2%, 95% CI −1.4 to −1.0), with absolute DALYs falling from 15 million (14–16 million) to 12 million (11–13 million). Global deaths decreased by 20% (18–22%) over the same period. The combined prevalence ASR remained nearly unchanged (EAPC 0.02%, 95% CI −0.03 to 0.07).

Disease-Specific Patterns and Horizontal Comparison Beneath these stable aggregate incidence trends, horizontal comparisons across the four etiologies revealed marked heterogeneity. Appendicitis incidence ASR fell modestly from 234 (189–300) to 222 (181–283) per 100,000 (EAPC −0.26%), whereas urolithiasis incidence ASR experienced a slight but steady rise. Ectopic pregnancy and paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction demonstrated intermediate, relatively stable incidence trajectories, the net effect of which produced overall stability in the combined incidence ASR. When compared horizontally, the divergent trajectories are apparent: urolithiasis and appendicitis sustained the largest absolute volumes of incident cases, whereas the overall declines in global deaths and DALYs were largely driven by improved outcomes in conditions with historically higher mortality, particularly paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction (Figure 1 and Supplementary Figure 1).

Regional Differences

Considerable heterogeneity existed across the 21 GBD regions (Figure 2). In 2021, the highest combined incidence ASRs were observed in Eastern Europe [1800 (1700–1900) per 100 000] and Central Asia [1700 (1600–1800) per 100 000], predominantly driven by Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction and urolithiasis. The lowest incidence ASR was recorded in high-income North America[800 (750–850) per 100 000], where appendicitis remained the leading contributor.

Temporal trends amplified these disparities: high-income regions achieved accelerated DALY declines (EAPC −2.5%, 95% CI −2.8 to −2.2), whereas low-income regions showed only marginal improvement (EAPC −0.3%, 95% CI −0.5 to −0.1).

Associations with Socio-Demographic Index

A strong, non-linear inverse relationship existed between SDI quintiles and disease burden (Figures 3 and 4). The lowest-SDI quintile recorded the highest DALY rate in 2021 [450 (430–470) per 100,000; EAPC −0.4%, 95% CI −0.6 to −0.2], while the highest-SDI quintile had the lowest rate [150 (140–160) per 100,000; EAPC −1.8%, 95% CI −2.0 to −1.6]. An anomalous incidence peak was evident in middle-SDI settings [1,500 (1,450–1,550) per 100,000]. Decomposition analyses showed that paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction predominated in low-SDI settings (45% of DALYs), whereas urolithiasis contributed the largest share of incident cases (35%) in high-SDI settings.

Age-Specific Patterns

Age-stratified analyses revealed a consistent bimodal distribution, with prominent peaks in the 15–49-year reproductive and working-age group and in individuals aged ≥70 years (Figure 5). In the 15–49-year cohort, incidence showed a modest upward trend (EAPC 0.1%, 95% CI 0.0–0.2), driven mainly by ectopic pregnancy (30% contribution) and appendicitis (25%), generating 40 million (38–42 million) new cases globally in 2021. DALYs in this age group declined (EAPC −0.8%, 95% CI −1.0 to −0.6). Absolute burden growth was greatest among older adults, where paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction contributed approximately 50% of DALYs [age-standardised rate 500 (480–520) per 100,000].

Disease-Specific Decomposition

In 2021, the incidence burden was predominantly driven by appendicitis and urolithiasis, which together accounted for 55% of global incident cases (EAPCs of −0.1% [95% CI −0.2 to 0.0] and +0.3% [0.2 to 0.4], respectively). Appendicitis and urolithiasis contributed minimally to overall mortality and DALYs. Conversely, ectopic pregnancy and paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction contributed disproportionately to fatal and disabling outcomes. Ectopic pregnancy represented only 15% of incident cases but disproportionately accounted for 25% of deaths (EAPC −1.5%, 95% CI −1.7 to −1.3), with the highest proportional impact in low-SDI regions. Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction emerged as the leading driver of DALYs (40%, EAPC −0.9%, 95% CI −1.1 to −0.7) and the single largest contributor to overall mortality, disproportionately affecting older adults.

The ARIMA projections to 2030 offer exploratory insights into possible future trajectories by capturing autocorrelation and non-linear dynamics not fully described by linear EAPC (Table 1 and Figures 6–8). These projections should be interpreted as indicative scenarios rather than deterministic forecasts.

At the global level, ARIMA forecasts indicate continued, accelerated decline in age-standardised DALY rates for appendicitis (forecast AAPC −4.51% vs historical EAPC −2.65%) and paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction (−2.48% vs −1.42%). Urolithiasis DALYs are projected to decline modestly (−1.42%) but may exhibit oscillations in specific settings. Ectopic pregnancy is the only condition among the four for which a marginal global increase in DALYs is projected (AAPC +0.06%).

When evaluating mortality and incidence, further disease-specific dichotomies emerge. Global death rates for most acute abdominal conditions are projected to plateau broadly (forecast AAPC approximately 0.00%). Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction demonstrate the most pronounced projected mortality decline (−2.13% vs historical −0.84%). Conversely, incidence trends reveal that while ectopic pregnancy cases are projected to decrease notably (−1.54%), the incidence rates for appendicitis (+0.02%) and urolithiasis (−0.08%) remain stable or face slight upward pressure.

South Asia exhibits pronounced mortality reductions for both appendicitis (−5.10%) and paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction (−3.75%). South Asia also shows projected incidence increases for paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction (+1.77%).

Sub-Saharan Africa: In sub-Saharan Africa, the incidence of ectopic pregnancy is projected to decline substantially (−4.13%), while its mortality rate shows a projected increase (+0.73%). A similar pattern is observed for paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, where deaths are projected to increase (+0.36%) despite relatively stable DALY and incidence metrics.

Middle East, Latin America, and Transitional Regions: In the North Africa and Middle East super-region, improvements are expected, particularly in paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction mortality (−1.35%) and appendicitis DALYs (−4.73%). Latin America and the Caribbean show a distinct epidemiological pattern: while appendicitis burden falls (Andean Latin America DALYs −6.65%), urolithiasis is projected to rise in both DALYs (+0.93%) and incidence (+0.78%).

High-Income and Eastern European Regions: The High-income super-region demonstrates the most stabilised trends overall. Mortality across most conditions has plateaued (AAPC approximately 0.00%), and ectopic pregnancy DALYs are declining substantially (−5.17%), though slight incidence rebounds are noted. Central Europe, Eastern Europe, and Central Asia project a marked decline in ectopic pregnancy mortality (−7.71%); however, this region also shows a projected rebound in appendicitis DALYs (+0.84%).

Discussion

This study harnesses the latest GBD 2021 estimates (appendicitis3 ectopic pregnancy4 paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction5 and urolithiasis6) to deliver an integrated, comparative assessment of four major acute abdominal conditions from 1990 to 2021. Our principal findings reveal that, although the combined age-standardised incidence rate remained essentially stable, absolute incident cases surged by 85%, while DALY and mortality rates declined. The divergence between stable age-standardised incidence and rising absolute case numbers is consistent with the dominant effect of population growth and ageing on absolute burden. The observed declines in mortality and disability rates are consistent with improvements in clinical management, including advancements in surgical technologies and diagnostic equipment, alongside the maturation of emergency medical response networks. These patterns reveal pronounced socio-economic and geographic disparities in disease burden, consistent with prior GBD analyses that have documented the consequences of limited access to timely intervention in resource-constrained settings.1,8

The stability of age-standardised incidence alongside a near-doubling of absolute case numbers is consistent with the dominant effect of global population growth and ageing on the absolute number of cases.9,10 In contrast, the observed declines in age-standardised DALY and mortality rates are consistent with improvements in tertiary care in high-income settings. The widespread adoption of computed tomography and point-of-care ultrasonography, standardisation of laparoscopic techniques, refined perioperative protocols, and strengthened emergency-care infrastructure are plausible contributors to these observed trends.11,12 For appendicitis, timely intervention in high-resource environments has been associated with reduced perforation rates,13 illustrating how diagnostic and therapeutic advances may translate into population-level benefit in settings with robust health systems.

A central finding of this analysis is the strong, non-linear inverse association between SDI and disease burden. Age-standardised DALY rates remain markedly elevated in low- and low-middle-SDI quintiles, particularly in South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa. The disproportionately high mortality and disability rates in these regions are consistent with limited imaging availability and delayed access to definitive surgery.1,14 In these settings, ectopic pregnancy accounts for a substantial fraction of early maternal deaths--up to 25% in the lowest-SDI strata15--and is consistent with diagnostic and therapeutic delays. High-SDI regions demonstrate markedly lower disease burden, consistent with the protective effect of resilient, integrated emergency systems.16

The non-linear peak in incidence observed in middle-SDI settings17 is consistent with an epidemiological transition pattern in which expanding diagnostic capacity – particularly computed tomography – may increase reported incidence by capturing previously undetected cases, before surgical throughput and postoperative care have fully caught up.18,19 Paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction in transitional economies is associated with prolonged hospital stays and higher recurrence, while urolithiasis, which is linked to dietary westernisation, urbanisation, and rising metabolic syndrome, generates increasing chronic sequelae in settings where endourological services are still developing.6,20,21

Age-stratified analyses revealed a consistent bimodal distribution, with peaks at 15–49 years (reproductive and economically productive ages) and ≥70 years.22,23 In the younger cohort, ectopic pregnancy and appendicitis together account for the majority of incident cases, generating substantial healthcare utilisation and productivity losses in low-SDI populations.24,25 In older adults, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction predominates, with its impact likely amplified by multimorbidity and reduced physiological reserve; geriatric perioperative considerations are relevant in these populations.26,27 This divergence in burden is also intrinsically related to disease characteristics: whereas appendicitis and urolithiasis often present relatively favourable prognoses with routine care, the severe intrinsic characteristics of ectopic pregnancy and paralytic ileus often necessitate complex healthcare utilisation, thereby disproportionately driving mortality. This bimodal age pattern indicates that health-system planning should address the distinct needs of both younger and older age groups.28

A key methodological feature of this study is the complementary use of traditional EAPC and ARIMA modelling. While EAPC effectively describes historical linear trends, ARIMA captures autocorrelation and non-linearity and provides explicit 95% confidence intervals for projections. Historical EAPC and ARIMA in-sample values matched closely (within 0.01%), confirming consistency between the two approaches; ARIMA additionally reveals non-linear accelerations or oscillations not captured by linear methods. This dual approach offers a broader perspective on both historical trends and possible future trajectories.

ARIMA Projections and Disease-Specific Insights

The ARIMA projections to 2030 reveal divergent trajectories across the four conditions. At the global level, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction exhibits the most pronounced projected mortality and DALY reductions (forecast AAPC −2.48%), consistent with improving emergency surgical systems.5 For appendicitis, DALYs (−4.51%) and mortality are projected to decline substantially, a trend observed across both lower- and higher-SDI regions. Ectopic pregnancy shows a substantial DALY decline in higher-SDI regions, yet global DALYs marginally oscillate (+0.06%), consistent with persistent challenges in reducing its burden, particularly in lower-resource settings.4 Urolithiasis remains relatively stable; it is the only etiology with a projected upward DALY trend in high-SDI settings, consistent with metabolic risk factor trends.6

Limitations of the Grouped Analysis

A key limitation of this study is that the four conditions--appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, and urolithiasis--are biologically heterogeneous. Appendicitis is an inflammatory condition, ectopic pregnancy is a gynaecological disorder, paralytic ileus is a functional motility disorder, and intestinal obstruction is typically mechanical. These conditions affect different demographic groups (adolescents and young adults for appendicitis, women of reproductive age for ectopic pregnancy, and older adults for paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction). Combining them into a single “acute abdominal conditions” construct may obscure disease-specific epidemiological patterns and limit clinical interpretation. Accordingly, the aggregate-level findings should be interpreted alongside the condition-specific stratified results presented in this analysis.

Socio-Demographic and Regional Heterogeneity Stratifying the ARIMA forecasts by SDI and super-regions reveals significant gradient heterogeneity. In low and low-middle SDI settings, the projected improvements in appendicitis DALYs and mortality (eg, low SDI DALYs −6.99%, mortality −7.17%) are consistent with improving surgical access. However, concurrent rises in incidence (low SDI +2.19%, low-middle +1.70%) are noted. In sub-Saharan Africa and low-SDI settings overall, ectopic pregnancy incidence improved (−4.13% and −2.78%, respectively) while mortality showed a lagged response (−1.09% to +0.73%), a pattern consistent with improving case detection alongside persistent barriers to timely care.

In middle-SDI settings, paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction mortality showed substantial projected improvement (−3.58%), consistent with therapeutic and technical advances. A projected rise in urolithiasis incidence (+0.08%) was observed, which may reflect dietary and lifestyle transitions characteristic of these settings.

In high and high-middle SDI settings, ectopic pregnancy DALYs showed substantial projected declines (high-middle −12.91%, high −5.44%), consistent with effective screening and intervention. High-SDI urolithiasis DALYs showed a projected rise (+0.15%), consistent with metabolic transition trends observed across multiple settings at similar development stages. Slight DALY increases for appendicitis in Central and Eastern Europe (+0.84%) may relate to population ageing and multimorbidity in these regions.

Collectively, these exploratory projections indicate substantial heterogeneity across SDI levels and super-regions, with the most pronounced improvements projected in settings that have experienced the greatest health-system development, and persistent or rising burdens in settings undergoing metabolic transition or population ageing. Further investigation is needed to disentangle the specific demographic, health-system, and lifestyle factors that underlie these observed divergent trajectories.

Additional limitations inherent to the GBD framework warrant acknowledgement. Reliance on modelled estimates in data-sparse regions may underestimate the true burden in low-SDI settings.29,30 Heterogeneity in diagnostic coding and health-seeking behaviour may bias temporal trends, particularly for early ectopic pregnancy.31 The current GBD approach may also incompletely capture multimorbidity and acute-on-chronic interactions in older adults.32 Furthermore, as noted above, aggregating four biologically heterogeneous conditions into a single construct may obscure disease-specific epidemiological patterns; this limitation should be considered when interpreting the aggregate findings. Nevertheless, the standardised methodology affords unparalleled cross-national and longitudinal comparability, providing a robust platform for epidemiological dialogue.

Conclusions

The burden of acute abdominal conditions represents a substantial and unequal global health challenge. Although age-standardised DALY and mortality rates have declined since 1990, absolute case numbers have risen substantially, probably driven by population growth and ageing, with the heaviest burden concentrated in low- and middle-SDI regions. The observed association between population ageing and rising absolute case volumes is probably consistent with demographic factors as key contributors to the increasing absolute burden. The ARIMA projections suggest divergent future trajectories across conditions and settings: continued improvements for appendicitis and paralytic ileus and intestinal obstruction, a marginal increase for ectopic pregnancy DALYs globally, and rising urolithiasis burden in high-SDI settings. Pronounced geographic and SDI-related disparities were observed alongside distinct age profiles across the four aetiologies. These findings underscore the importance of context-specific planning for emergency surgical and preventive care, informed by the distinct epidemiological profiles and age demographics of each condition. Further research is warranted to identify the specific health-system, demographic, and lifestyle factors driving the observed disparities and to explore the feasibility of targeted interventions in different settings.

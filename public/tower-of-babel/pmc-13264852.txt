Auricular Point Acupressure Self-Management (APA-SM) program for chronic musculoskeletal pain among rural populations: a protocol for a pragmatic, randomized controlled trial
Authors: Jennifer Kawi, Jane Bolin, Hulin Wu, Jungkyung Min, Rupsikha Bora, Sarah Pace, Idara N Akpan, Alee Lockman, Tonychris Nnaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) is a leading cause of incurred personal healthcare costs and disability in the USA. It disproportionately affects rural populations, who are more likely to be uninsured, lack access to a regular healthcare provider and experience a higher prevalence of CMP. As a result of reduced access to non-pharmacological care, there is greater reliance on opioids. The Auricular Point Acupressure – Self-Management (APA-SM) program is a simple, needleless, evidence-based therapy that empowers individuals to self-manage their pain. Preliminary studies, including a recent UG3 pilot testing, demonstrated feasibility, safety and significant improvements in pain and function. Guided by Bandura’s self-efficacy model and informed by stakeholder input, APA-SM integrates a smartphone application, ecological momentary assessment and personalized motivational messaging to enhance adherence and behavior change. This study protocol describes the design of a real-world, hybrid effectiveness-implementation, randomized controlled trial to evaluate the clinical impact and sustainability of APA-SM in rural settings.Methods and analysisWe will conduct a pragmatic, three-arm randomized controlled trial in Texas and South Carolina, enrolling 693 adults with CMP (231 per group). Participants will be randomized to: (1) APA-SM with remote training, (2) APA-SM with in-person training or (3) education control. The primary outcome is pain impact measured by the PEG (composite score of pain intensity, interference on enjoyment of life, and general activity) scale at immediate post-intervention (4 weeks), with follow-up at 1, 3 and 6 months. Secondary outcomes include National Institutes of Health Helping to End Addiction Long-Term Clinical Pain Core common data elements (eg, psychological functioning, disability, sleep, quality of life), opioid use and patient-reported adherence and satisfaction. Implementation outcomes will be assessed at both the patient and provider levels, guided by the RE-AIM framework. Cost-effectiveness will be estimated using implementation costs and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios, and predictive factors for APA-SM treatment response will be identified using statistical machine learning approaches and historical electronic health record data. The target sample size (231 per group) provides 90% power to detect a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d=0.35), accounting for 25% attrition. Randomization occurs at the participant level to reflect real-world delivery and minimize contamination.Ethics and disseminationThis study, sponsored under grant number 4UH3AT012728, has single Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval at UTHealth Houston, Texas, USA (HSC-SN-25-0443). A Data Safety Monitoring Board will oversee adverse event reporting and trial conduct. Dissemination will occur through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, stakeholder workshops and community-based reports tailored for rural health systems and policymakers.DiscussionThis trial will provide the first large-scale evaluation of APA-SM in rural U.S. populations, integrating digital health tools and implementation science methods to help address disparities in pain management. By assessing clinical effectiveness, implementation outcomes, cost-effectiveness and predictive response factors, study results will inform scalable strategies for integrating APA-SM further into rural communities and healthcare systems. If successful, APA-SM has the potential to improve pain care access, reduce opioid reliance and provide a sustainable, patient-centered model for chronic pain management.Trial registration numberNCT07179016.

Background

Chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) is a major public health challenge, affecting nearly one in four adults in the USA and representing the leading cause of disability worldwide.1 2 The economic burden of CMP is staggering, with annual healthcare costs estimated at US$134.5 billion—exceeding expenditures for cancer, heart disease and diabetes combined.3 Despite the availability of multiple therapeutic options, many individuals continue to experience persistent pain, functional impairment and diminished quality of life. Pharmacological therapy remains the predominant approach, yet its benefits are often modest and side effects are common, contributing to ongoing unmet needs—particularly in rural areas where access to comprehensive pain management is limited and opioid-related harms are elevated. In this setting, non-pharmacologic modalities such as acupressure may offer a low-risk adjunct with relevance for both clinical practice and broader pain-management strategies.4 The disproportionate burden of CMP in rural populations reflects structural and occupational factors, including sustained engagement in physically demanding work (eg, agriculture and heavy machinery) and delayed care-seeking related to geographic, economic and healthcare access barriers.46 These communities also face systemic barriers to accessing evidence-based, non-pharmacologic care, including specialist shortages, limited local resources and geographic isolation. As a result, reliance on opioids and opioid-containing medication is more frequent; with opportunities for sustainable pain management remaining scarce.47 Current guidelines emphasise non-pharmacologic and self-management interventions as first-line treatments for CMP, but the reality of the scarcity of access to rural healthcare delivery makes such recommendations difficult to achieve.4 5

Acupuncture has gained increasing recognition as an evidence-based pain therapy and since 2020, acupuncture has been reimbursed by Medicare and Medicaid in 44 states for chronic low back pain.7 However, traditional acupuncture faces barriers to scalability due to its invasive needle-based application, requirement for licensed providers and the need for repeated office visits.810 Auricular acupuncture, which targets points on the ear, offers a simplified pathway, yet uptake has been constrained by similar limitations. Auricular Point Acupressure (APA) provides a non-invasive, needleless alternative that empowers individuals to self-manage their pain. Grounded in acupuncture principles, APA has demonstrated clinically meaningful improvements in pain and function, with benefits sustained up to 6 months after intervention in randomized controlled trials.1115 Importantly, auricular stimulation provides rapid relief and APA has been shown to be cost-effective and safe with only minor, transient side effects such as ear tenderness.1619 Building on this evidence, our team has advanced APA accessibility through development of a digital health model using a user-centred smartphone application. Our APA-Self-Management (APA-SM) program integrates ecological momentary assessment (EMA) and personalized motivational messaging based on Bandura’s self-efficacy framework to support adherence, real-world monitoring and sustainable behavior change.10 Moreover, participants are coached in APA self-administration skills and self-monitoring of pain through the APA app, facilitating active patient engagement and empowerment to address their pain relief on demand.

Our prior work has shown the feasibility of embedding APA into health delivery practices, including ease of integration into electronic health records (EHRs) for provider referral, as well as high retention, adherence and acceptability among diverse populations.162022 APA provides a non-pharmacologic alternative to offer among the possible approaches to CMP. Importantly, APA-SM is uniquely positioned to address disparities in rural pain care. This pragmatic randomized controlled trial will be the first large-scale test of APA-SM’s effectiveness, implementation outcomes, cost-effectiveness and predictors of treatment response for CMP in rural communities. Findings will inform scalable, patient-centred strategies to increase pain relief, reduce opioid reliance, improve quality of life and advance equitable pain care in underserved populations.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The study was launched on 1 September 2025. Recruitment and intervention activities began at study launch and will continue through 31 August 2029. We will use a hybrid type II effectiveness-implementation, pragmatic randomized controlled trial design to rigorously evaluate the clinical effectiveness of APA-SM among adults with CMP. This design is intentionally selected to test both patient-level outcomes and secondary aims related to implementation feasibility, cost-effectiveness and predictors of treatment response. Recruitment will target rural communities in South Carolina and Texas—geographically diverse regions characterized by high pain prevalence among residents, substantial rural health disparities and limited access to non-pharmacologic pain care.

The target population is rural adults with CMP. Power calculations indicate that 173 participants per group are required to detect an effect size of Cohen’s d=0.35 with 90% power (two-sided t-test, α=0.05).11 To accommodate an anticipated 25% attrition rate, we will recruit 231 individuals per arm, for a total of 693 participants across three study groups. This sample size preserves>84% power to detect the same effect size when applying Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (α=0.025) for each APA treatment arm versus control.

On enrolment and completion of baseline measures, participants will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to one of three study arms: self-guided APA-SM with training delivered remotely supplemented with a smartphone APA application, APA-SM combined with in-person training supplemented with the same APA app, or pain education and self-management with a pain app as control. All three are active interventions. The APA app has APA content while the pain app has pain education and self-management content. Randomization will occur at the individual level to reflect real-world clinical care and minimize contamination. The interventions will be delivered for 4 weeks, and participants will complete assessments at baseline, immediate post-intervention (4 weeks) and follow-up visits at one, three and 6 months. Data collection

At baseline, we will collect demographic characteristics (eg, age, sex, race, ethnicity, height, weight) and social determinants of health (eg, income, employment, education, insurance, access to pain care, transportation). Clinical variables will include body mass index, comorbidities using the Charlson Comorbidity Index and other pain conditions using the Chronic Overlapping Pain Condition Screener. We will also collect data on the following at baseline: pain impact, psychological functioning, pain interference, physical function, disability, sleep, self-efficacy, substance use, quality of life, treatment expectancy, positive outlook and attitude toward complementary and complementary medicine.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome of interest is pain impact, assessed with the Pain, Enjoyment of life and General activity (PEG) scale, which measures pain and interference on enjoyment of life and general activity. This validated three-item composite measure evaluates both pain intensity and functional interference. Assessments will be conducted at baseline, 4 weeks (the primary endpoint) and at 1, 3 and 6 months after intervention (with the 6-month endpoint serving as the secondary endpoint).

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes align with the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Helping to End Addiction Long-Term (HEAL) common data elements (CDEs) and will include multiple domains of functioning and well-being. Psychological functioning will be evaluated using the Pain Catastrophising Scale, Fear-Avoidance Beliefs Questionnaire, Generalised Anxiety Disorder-2, and Patient Health Questionnaire-2 (PHQ-2) for depression screening, advancing to the PHQ-9 for positive screens on depression. Pain interference will be measured using the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI). Physical function and disability will be measured using thePatient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) Physical Function Short Form (SF-6b) and the Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire. Sleep quality will be assessed using the PROMIS Sleep Disturbance SF (6a) + sleep duration. Additional measures will include the Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire-2; Tobacco, Alcohol, Prescription Medication and other Substance Use measure; and the WHO Quality of Life scale. Non-specific treatment effects will be evaluated using the Healing Encounters and Attitudes Lists (HEAL-SF6a) which measures positive outlook, treatment expectancy, and attitudes toward complementary and complementary medicine.

Analgesic and opioid use will be monitored using EMA diaries, administered through REDCap during the intervention and for 7 days before each follow-up at 1, 3 and 6 months. Morphine milligram equivalents will be calculated using established equivalence factors. Any adverse events (list the effects of interest) from APA will also be captured at each data collection point using EMA.

Implementation outcomes

Implementation outcomes will be assessed at both the patient and intervention coach levels. Intervention coaches are trained APA educators. At the patient level, implementation outcomes include adherence to APA, sustained use, satisfaction, barriers and facilitators. Patient adherence will be defined as completion of at least two-thirds of the recommended APA stimulation. Patients are advised to stimulate their ear points at least three times a day, 3 min each time, for a total of 9 min per day. Daily adherence and sustained use of APA will be monitored via EMA during the intervention and for 1 week prior to each follow-up visit. Patient satisfaction will be assessed using the Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC) measure as well as through qualitative interviews with feedback on barriers and facilitators to APA-SM collected at immediate postintervention and each follow-up. At the Intervention Coach level, implementation outcomes will include adoption, maintenance, and implementation of APA-SM. These will be measured using number of facilities or clinics approached, number of coaches, patient referral rates and enrolment rates. Coaches will also provide open-ended feedback on satisfaction, challenges, and facilitators to APA-SM implementation using interviews and qualitative feedback at 1 month of study participation and at follow-up timepoints.

Cost-effectiveness

Cost-effectiveness analyses will be conducted from both provider/organizational and societal perspectives. Intervention costs will be calculated using micro-costing methods, which capture personnel time, training, materials, technology, and overhead associated with intervention delivery. All resources will be valued regardless of source, and national data will be used for unit prices when available. Healthcare utilization will be assessed through self-report surveys, EHRs and claims data and will include analgesic and opioid use, interventional pain procedures, physical therapy, chiropractic services, acupuncture, specialist consultations, emergency department visits and hospitalizations. Lost workdays attributable to CMP will also be recorded. Quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) will be estimated using EuroQOL.

Predictive factors

We will conduct exploratory analyses to identify baseline demographic, psychosocial and clinical factors that predict APA-SM treatment response. Historical health records, comorbidities, medications and demographic factors will be examined. Statistical modeling approaches, including machine learning methods, will be used to develop predictive profiles of treatment responders and non-responders. Responders are those achieving at least 30% reduction from baseline; we will also consider and explore other parameters for minimal and moderate clinically important difference. For pain intensity, the Initiative on Methods, Measurement, and Pain Assessment in Clinical Trials recommendations indicate that at least 1 point in the Numeric Rating Scale or 10%–20% reduction represent minimum clinically important difference, while at least 2 points or 30% represent moderate clinically important difference.23 The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality defines a mean difference of 1–2 points or 10–20 points in the Visual Analogue Scale for pain as having a moderate effect.24 For the BPI scale where the PEG was derived, a 1-point change on interference is considered a reasonable score for minimum clinically important difference.23 A more recent publication from 6 RCTs found that minimally important differences in the PEG and BPI measures were comparable and indicated that 1 to 2 points on the PEG represent the lower and upper bounds of minimally important differences.25 The PGIC scale has also been used as a measure for clinical importance with ratings such as ‘much’ and ‘very much improved’ considered as moderately to substantially important change while ‘minimally improved’ represents the minimum clinically important difference.23 We will provide sensitivity analyses for various criteria indicated above for minimum and moderate clinical importance to ensure the rigor and reproducibility of our conclusions.

Ecological momentary assessment

EMA will be integrated into the APA-SM smartphone application to collect real-time data on CMP outcomes (PEG), analgesic use and APA practice (frequency and duration). Control group participants will complete EMA surveys assessing pain outcomes and analgesic use only. Participants will be asked to complete daily EMA surveys, and incentives will be provided for completing 28 days of EMA during the 4-week intervention. Missing EMA data will not be considered a protocol deviation; instead, a dedicated Data Manager will monitor responses and coordinate with sites to encourage completion. Missing EMA data will be managed in alignment with survey administration at baseline, immediate postintervention and follow-ups at 1, 3 and 6 months.11

EMA process

EMA will include two short surveys (~1 min each): (1) random-prompt EMA on real-time pain outcomes (PEG, three items) administered twice daily during waking hours and (2) time-contingent EMA on APA-SM practice and analgesic use prompted at the end of day. Questions will be displayed sequentially, and responses automatically uploaded to REDCap in real time. If a participant does not respond for >48 hours, study staff will follow-up, troubleshoot, and encourage resumption of survey completion. A standardized protocol will be used to extract EMA data and evaluate adherence, supported by prior feasibility work showing that patients find EMA both acceptable and useful for monitoring self-management behaviors.20 21

Inclusion criteria

Eligible participants will be adults aged 18 years or older who are receiving any form of pain management for CMP involving the neck, back, and joints (eg, shoulders, hands, hips, knees, feet). They must report a pain score of ≥4 on a 0–10 scale, with pain persisting for at least 3 months or occurring on at least half the days in the past 6 months. Individuals will be excluded if they have a severe ear skin disorder (eg, diagnosed with conditions such as eczema or lupus with skin involvement that often require medical intervention) or severe allergy to tape. Additional exclusions include use of a hearing aid that obstructs seed placement, inability or unwillingness to provide informed consent, or not residing in a rural area.

Recruitment

In both Texas and South Carolina, recruitment will be conducted through close collaboration with rural community partners, including traditional and non-traditional healthcare settings, primary care providers, allied health staff, community health workers (CHWs), and local leaders. Engagement will occur in person, by telecommunication, and through digital platforms. Informational flyers will be distributed via partner organizations and posted in community establishments to enhance outreach. Potential participants will be screened using a standardized screening log which will capture inclusion and exclusion criteria as well as reasons for ineligibility. Informed consent will be conducted via Docusign and REDCap, either remotely or in person. When technological challenges arise, written consent may be obtained manually and subsequently uploaded into REDCap. To ensure fidelity in the recruitment process, the research team will follow standardized recruitment scripts and checklists.

To address potential recruitment barriers, a comprehensive accrual and retention plan has been developed. This includes: (1) leveraging established partnerships with rural health agencies, a Community Advisory Board, and CHWs to expand referral networks; (2) engaging primary care practices, pain clinics, and back-up study sites or additional partners, including faith-based organizations and community centers; (3) participating in rural community events (eg, health fairs, conferences, recruitment booths, APA demonstrations) to raise awareness; (4) broadening recruitment catchment areas into surrounding counties as needed; (5) incorporating outreach to rural residents who are not embedded in primary care practices but meet eligibility criteria; (6) expanding digital recruitment through social media and local news outlets and (7) collaborating with clinic partners to conduct EHR database mining to identify potential participants. Recruitment challenges will be regularly evaluated in consultation with rural site partners and addressed proactively.

Randomization and blinding

Randomization will occur at the patient level rather than the cluster level (provider, intervention coach, clinic) because the impact of pain programs in the real-world setting is primarily at the individual patient level. This approach minimizes contamination and preserves the integrity of usual care for CMP.26 27 Participants will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to one of three groups: (a) APA-SM with remote training, (b) APA-SM with in-person training or (c) education control. The randomization sequence will be computer-generated by a study biostatistician using permuted block sizes of 3 and 6 to maintain group balance, and implemented centrally and securely using the REDCap randomization module. Once consent is signed and eligibility is confirmed, the site coordinator will log into REDCap, complete the randomization form, triggering automated group assignment and notification to unblinded study personnel responsible for intervention delivery. Because of the nature of the intervention, participant and provider blinding is not feasible. However, participants will be blinded to the key study hypothesis that the APA-SM groups will report significant improvement in the primary outcome at immediate post-intervention compared with the control group. Outcome analyses will also be conducted under blinded conditions. The multiple principal investigators including the senior statistician will remain masked to treatment allocation unless unblinding is required (eg, safety concerns such as serious adverse events). For Data Safety Monitoring support and interim analyses, an unblinded biostatistician will oversee unmasked data. Table 1 indicates the study blinding plans.

Intervention procedures

Auricular Point Acupressure for Self-Management

APA-SM is a 4-week, mobile-enabled, self-administered intervention that combines a smartphone application, instructional dashboard with personalized APA protocols based on each participant’s CMP location, EMA for real-time outcomes, and data-driven personalized motivational messages (based on EMA data) using Bandura’s self-efficacy model to promote adherence. The APA app delivers short videos and stepwise instructions to teach APA techniques, building on our prior digital APA work.162022 Our protocol follows the Standards for Reporting Interventions in Clinical Trials of Acupuncture guidelines.28 Ear points are located using internationally recognized zones linked to the affected body region and standard analgesic points (eg, Shenmen, subcortex nervous, ear center).29 30 Vaccaria seeds (~2 mm diameter) or pellets affixed to latex-free, waterproof tape (~0.3”) are applied to the front and back of the ear (and both ears for bilateral areas of pain), remaining in place for 5 days with 2 days off.

Stimulation dose and cycle

Participants are instructed to press each taped seed for 3 min, three times daily (≈9 min total per day). Stimulation may elicit mild tingling, with a 2 s pause between presses. The intervention consists of 4 weekly treatment cycles, each involving several consecutive days of auricular seed use followed by a brief rest period. Incorporating rest periods is consistent with published safety literature emphasising the importance of limiting prolonged auricular stimulation to reduce the risk of local skin irritation and discomfort.13 In addition, prior randomized trials have demonstrated the importance of day-to-day variation and cycling of auricular acupressure to manage tenderness, maintain sensitivity, and support adherence during multi-week interventions for chronic pain.14 15 Participants may continue APA-SM beyond the intervention phase as an as-needed self-management strategy for pain.

Administration and adherence

APA-SM is self-administered in any setting, with training delivered remotely or in person according to group assignment. On day three, participants submit photos of seed placement for review by a clinical APA expert; annotated feedback is returned until proper placement is confirmed. Adherence is defined as completing at least two-thirds of prescribed stimulation (≥2 times/day, ≥2 min each time, or total of 6 minutes/day), monitored in real time via EMA.20 21

Adverse effects

Serious adverse events have not been reported in prior APA trials.11 12 14 Reported adverse events associated with auricular acupressure are generally mild and localized, most commonly involving transient tenderness or minor discomfort at the application site, with no serious or lasting effects reported.14

Educational control

Control participants receive a mobile pain app with 4 weekly modules on chronic pain and self-management, covering information on pain, self-management skills, pain-mental health content, pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic treatment modalities, and pain resources. Participants are instructed to engage with the content for at least 9 min/day, paralleling APA-SM time and then practice what they learnt (SM skills with a specific action plan of choice such as stretching three times per day, 3 min each time, for at least 9 min per day, at least 5 days a week for 4 weeks as a non-pharmacologic pain management modality). Control participants also receive one virtual training session, weekly scripted contacts, and daily EMA surveys on pain and analgesic use (excluding APA-specific items).

Concomitant care and safety

All participants continue their usual pain management. Initiation of new treatments for acute pain exacerbation is not prohibited but will be documented through surveys, EMA and follow-ups. Safety monitoring includes automated REDCap alerts for depressive symptoms (eg, positive PHQ-2 screening that triggers PHQ-9 administration, follow-up or emergency referral if needed).

Follow-up and evaluation

Assessments occur at baseline, immediate post-intervention (day 30), and 1, 3 and 6 months post-intervention. EMA surveys are collected daily during the 4-week intervention and for 7 days before each follow-up. Weekly surveys and scripted messaging are conducted during Weeks 1–3 of the intervention. For Aim 3 (cost-effectiveness), data on analgesic use and healthcare utilization will be collected via surveys, EMA, and Claims Data Warehouses for Texas and South Carolina.4 For Aim 4 (predictive factors), deidentified baseline EHR data will be obtained from participating clinics.31 After trial completion, control participants are offered APA training as an engagement strategy. Qualitative interviews will be conducted with APA-SM participants and Intervention Coaches to assess satisfaction, fidelity and implementation barriers.26 27 Collectively, APA-SM is designed to evaluate clinical effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, implementation outcomes, and predictive factors related to APA-SM response. Participant and public involvement

Participants with lived experience of the study condition were involved in the design of this research. Participants, their significant others, and other stakeholders, including community partners, clinicians and health officials, contributed to refining the research questions and reviewed study materials to ensure clarity, relevance and acceptability. Their feedback informed revisions to the study protocol and data collection tools to better reflect participant priorities. Study participants were not involved in data analysis.

Ethics and dissemination

This study, sponsored under grant number 4UH3AT012728, was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at UTHealth Houston (HSC-SN-25-0443) and is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT07179016). All eligible participants will be informed about the study aims and procedures by members of the research team prior to providing informed consent, both verbally and in writing. Participants will be assured of confidentiality, the voluntary nature of participation, and their right to withdraw from the study at any time without penalty. Any modifications to the study protocol will first be approved by the funding agency and will be communicated to all responsible parties within 2–4 weeks of approval. Findings from this study will be disseminated through presentations at state, regional, national, and international conferences, including the United States Association for the Study of Pain, American Public Health Association, Southern Nursing Research Society, International Council of Nurses Congress, and relevant rural health conferences. Results will also be shared through reports and publications in peer-reviewed international journals

Data analysis plan

Data analysis

All data will undergo annual audits and sooner as needed by the Data Manager. Analyses will follow a modified intention-to-treat framework, ensuring inclusion of all randomized participants regardless of adherence or treatment withdrawal. Descriptive summaries will precede hypothesis testing, and analytic assumptions will be evaluated with transformations applied if needed.

Aim 1: Effectiveness of APA-SM in CMP outcomes

The primary outcome, pain impact (PEG), will be analyzed using a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) at Week 4-5 (immediate postintervention) and at 1, 3 and 6 months follow-up postintervention. Fixed effects will include treatment group, visit, group-by-visit interaction and baseline scores. Least squares mean differences with 95% CIs will provide between-group contrasts, with adjustments for interim and multiple testing. An unstructured covariance matrix will be used; alternative structures (eg, AR (1)) will be explored if convergence fails. Analyses will be conducted in SAS and R. Sensitivity analyses will include repeated measures analysis of variance. Continuous secondary outcomes (NIH HEAL Pain CDEs) will be analyzed using MMRM; binary outcomes (eg, opioid use) will use generalized linear mixed models (GLMM).

Aim 2: Implementation outcomes

Provider characteristics (satisfaction, integration into routine care) and APA-SM delivery will be summarized descriptively. Adherence will be tracked using EMA, with process control charts (c-charts and p-charts) to monitor daily use and dropout. Missingness >30% in EMA within 7 days will trigger follow-up, reflecting feasibility challenges in rural populations. Associations between adherence and outcomes will be assessed with linear, nonlinear and non-parametric mixed models, as well as generalized estimating equations. Sustained adherence will be defined as ≥70% at 6 months.

Aim 3: Cost-effectiveness

Economic analyses will use GLMM with Gamma distributions to model skewed costs, adjusted for baseline imbalances. Incremental cost-effectiveness ratios will be calculated for APA-SM versus control using QALYs derived from follow-up data. Bootstrapping will estimate uncertainty, and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves will benchmark results against US thresholds (US$100K/QALY). Provider and patient fidelity will be descriptively summarized.27

Aim 4: Predictive factors for treatment response

Responders will be defined as ≥30% reduction in PEG scores, with other clinically important thresholds explored. Predictive models will integrate EHR-derived demographics, comorbidities and clinical history. Logistic regression with adaptive LASSO or SCAD will support interpretability, while machine learning methods (eg, random forests, XGBoost, neural networks) will capture complex interactions. Model performance will be evaluated by ROC AUC with 70/30 training/test splits. Missing data will be addressed with advanced imputation (eg, MICE, MissForest, tensor completion). Predictor importance will be quantified using ORs, improvement scores, and sensitivity analyses. Subgroup analyses will evaluate social determinants of health, including sex, education, income, literacy and clinic site.

Qualitative analysis

Interview data will be coded in ATLAS.ti using Braun and Clarke’s thematic analysis framework. Coding will be conducted by ≥2 trained research team members independently, followed by consensus. Member checking will be used to validate themes, with thematic analysis explored for deidentified transcripts.

Data management

A single, secure, paperless data entry system (REDCap) incorporating EMA that is password-protected will be used to ensure accurate and complete data capture. Data will be entered directly into the UTHealth-hosted database, which is safeguarded by institution-level security protections and undergoes regular backups to prevent data loss. All entries will include automated date and time stamps, with access restricted to authorized study personnel. Each participant will be assigned a unique study identifier, which will be used across all instruments, documents and files prepared for statistical analyses to protect confidentiality. EHR data will be extracted from the local EHR systems of participating clinics, deidentified, then systematically cleaned, processed and harmonized to ensure consistency. Only de-identified datasets will be shared with the study statisticians for analysis.

Discussion

CMP remains a persistent and costly public health challenge, disproportionately affecting rural populations and underserved groups who often lack access to evidence-based non-pharmacological treatments.146 Although acupuncture and auricular therapies have demonstrated promising effects in reducing pain and improving quality of life,121532 barriers such as limited availability of trained providers, geographic distance, digital exclusion and inconsistent integration into routine care continue to hinder scalability.45 810 This trial addresses these gaps by evaluating the APA-SM program through both remote self-guided and in-person delivery approaches, offering a pragmatic model for pain care that can be feasibly implemented across rural communities and diverse healthcare settings.202226

A key strength of this study is the comprehensive evaluation of APA-SM across effectiveness, implementation outcomes, cost-effectiveness and predictive factors for treatment response. Further, by incorporating mixed-methods assessments, real-time EMA and EHR integration, this trial will generate granular insights into both clinical outcomes and real-world adoption.21 Findings can be directly translatable to health systems and providers seeking scalable and sustainable pain management solutions.27 Cost-effectiveness analyses will further provide critical evidence to inform reimbursement and policy decisions, a prerequisite for long-term integration of APA-SM into standard care. Importantly, this protocol is deliberately designed to advance health equity in rural and underserved communities. Surveys and interventions will be available in both Spanish and English to address language barriers. Recognizing the digital divide, smartphones and data plans will also be provided to participants without devices or adequate data plans, ensuring equitable participation in EMA-based data collection and remote intervention delivery.22 This deliberate attention to digital access reduces the risk of excluding individuals who might otherwise be systematically left behind, a key factor when testing scalable health interventions. Predictive modeling using machine learning and EHR data will further identify patient- and system-level factors associated with treatment response, enabling more precise tailoring of future interventions to subgroups most likely to benefit.31 This precision health approach strengthens the potential for APA-SM to improve outcomes while minimizing disparities in pain care.

There are limitations to consider. As a pragmatic trial, variability in intervention coach intervention delivery, participant adherence and technological challenges, such as unstable Wi-Fi in rural areas may affect data completeness. Although mitigation strategies such as real-time monitoring, adherence thresholds and survey completion windows will be incorporated, some missingness is expected.21 Additionally, differential engagement by study arm may occur; for example, some control participants may disengage early due to disappointment with allocation. To address this, retention strategies include scheduled engagement checkpoints, follow-up reminders and post-trial access to the APA-SM intervention.27

Fast convex optimization via inertial systems with asymptotic vanishing viscous and Hessian-driven damping Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Zepeng Wang, Juan Peypouquet

Abstract
We study the convergence rate of a family of inertial algorithms, which can be obtained by discretization of an inertial system combining asymptotic vanishing viscous and Hessian-driven damping. We establish a fast sublinear convergence rate in case the objective function is convex and satisfies Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality. We also establish a linear convergence rate for smooth strongly convex functions. The results can provide more insights into the convergence property of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method.



keywords

convex optimization, asymptotic vanishing damping, Hessian-driven damping, Polyak-Łojasiewicz, strong convexity

Introduction

Consider the optimization problem

\min_{x\in H}f(x),

(1)

where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice differentiable convex function on a real Hilbert space
H
, whose gradient is
L
-Lipschitz continuous for some
L>0
. We assume that the solution set
S:=\argmin(f)
is nonempty, and write
f^{*}=\min(f)
.

Nesterov’s accelerated gradient algorithm
[
18
]
, which may take the form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}x_{k}&=&y_{k}-s\nabla f(y_{k}),\\
y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),\end{array}\right.

(NAG)

where
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
and
\alpha\geq 3
, is often used to solve problem (
1
), ensuring a convergence rate of
f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)

[
18
]
. For
\alpha>3
, one obtains a faster convergence rate
f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)

[
6
]
, and the sequence of iterates is weakly convergent
[
11
]
. If
f
is convex, and satisfies a local error bound condition, an improved sublinear convergence rate
o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}}\right)
was derived in
[
16
]
for
\alpha>1
,
0<s<\frac{1}{L}
and
k
sufficiently large. Although it is not mentioned in
[
16
]
, a
big
\mathcal{O}
result can be derived from their analysis. If
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, a linear convergence rate for the function values was shown possible in
[
15
]
, and the best rate is approximately
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{4L}\right)^{k}\right)
, with
s=\frac{1}{2L}

[
9
]
. In the subcritical case
0<\alpha<3
, (
NAG
) ensures a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha}\right)
if
f
is convex
[
5
,
1
]
. The proximal version of (
NAG
), known as FISTA
[
10
]
, is able to solve convex composite optimization problems. In the convex case, the convergence rate is
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
if
\alpha\geq 3

[
10
]
, and
o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
if
\alpha>3

[
6
]
, and otherwise
\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha}\right)
if
0<\alpha<3

[
5
,
1
]
. When the smooth part is
\mu
-strongly convex, a linear convergence rate of up to
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{4L}\right)^{k}\right)
is guaranteed for
\alpha\geq 3

[
15
,
9
]
. To the best of our knowledge, no linear convergence rate has been found in the subcritical case. A variant of (
NAG
), usually referred to as
Optimized Gradient Method

[
12
,
13
,
14
,
19
]
, provides an improved convergence rate for convex functions. Local geometric properties of the convex objective function (quadratic growth or Łojasiewicz inequality) also have an effect on the convergence rate of (
NAG
)
[
2
]
.

Nesterov’s accelerated gradient algorithm (
NAG
) can be seen as a discrete analogue of an inertial system with asymptotic vanishing damping
[
21
]
:

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f\big(x(t)\big)=0,

(2)

with
\alpha\geq 3
, with convergence rate
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
if
f
is convex, and
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha}\right)
(with no hope for a linear rate) if
f
is strongly convex
[
21
]
. For
\alpha>3
, the trajectory convergence is guaranteed
[
4
]
, and
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)

[
17
]
. For
0<\alpha<3
, one obtains
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha}\right)
if
f
is convex
[
5
]
. Convergence rate analysis of (
2
) under local geometry of the convex function
f
can be found in
[
8
]
. However, the system (
2
) exhibits wild trajectory oscillations in practice, which motivates inclusion of a Hessian-driven damping term in (
2
) so as to attenuate the oscillations
[
7
]
:

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\beta\nabla^{2}f\big(x(t)\big)\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f\big(x(t)\big)=0,

(3)

where
\alpha\geq 3
and
\beta>0
. This system preserves most properties of (
2
), including
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
if
\alpha>3
, and also a faster convergence of the gradient to zero
[
7
]
. Under a high-resolution perspective, the following system
[
20
]

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{3}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\sqrt{s}\nabla^{2}f\big(x(t)\big)\dot{x}(t)+\left(1+\frac{3\sqrt{s}}{2t}\right)\nabla f\big(x(t)\big)=0,

(4)

turns out to be the limiting ordinary-differential-equation (ODE) for (
NAG
). If
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, a linear convergence rate of
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}e^{-\frac{\mu\sqrt{s}}{4}t}\right)
, for
t\geq\frac{4}{\mu\sqrt{s}}
and
0<s<\frac{1}{L}
, holds for the solutions of (
4
)
[
15
]
. A generalization of the systems (
3
) and (
4
), with more general coefficients, was studied in
[
3
]
.

In this paper, and in line with (
4
), we consider the inertial system

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\beta\nabla^{2}f\big(x(t)\big)\dot{x}(t)+\left(\gamma+\frac{r}{t}\right)\nabla f\big(x(t)\big)=0,

(AVD-H)

with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,r>0
, and initial conditions given by
x(t_{0})=x_{0}
and
\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0}
. By appropriately tuning the coefficients, we are able to establish the fastest convergence rates up to date, which are valid for all
\alpha>0
. This knowledge allows us to transfer some of these results to accelerated gradient algorithms, for which we obtain new convergence rates.

Our results are organized in three major sections:
Section
2
contains the analysis of the solutions to the differential equation (
AVD-H
). When the (not necessarily convex) objective function
f
satisfies a Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PŁ) inequality with constant
\mu>0
, the function value gap
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}
behaves linearly for
t\leq\frac{\alpha}{\mu\beta}
, then enters a
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}})
regime, and, if
f
is convex, asymptotically behaves as
o(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}})
. Surprisingly, the convergence rates do not depend on
\gamma
if one makes the natural choice
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
, but do require
r
to be properly tuned. If
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, then
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mu\beta t}\big)
. All the results in this section are valid for
\alpha>0
.
In Section
3
, we analyze the convergence rate of a family of inertial gradient algorithms from a similar perspective, when
f
is
L
-smooth. As in the continuous case, the function value gap goes through a linear regime, a
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}})
regime, and asymptotically behaves as
o(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}})
, provided
f
is convex and satisfies a
\mu
-PŁ inequality. In the
\mu
-strongly convex case, the convergence rate is
\mathcal{O}\big((1-\rho)^{k}\big)
, with
\rho=\frac{\mu}{4(L+\mu)}
. Finally, in Section
4
, we study a family of accelerated proximal-gradient algorithms, and establish a linear convergence rate under strong convexity.

2
Convergence rates I: inertial dynamics

In this section, we prove convergence rates of the function values along the trajectories defined by the inertial system (
AVD-H
). Define the energy function
\varepsilon:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varepsilon(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}+\theta(f(x)-f^{*}),

(5)

with
\theta,\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. The values of
\omega
and
\theta
will be fixed later, but note that, if
\theta>0
and
\omega\geq 0
, then
f(x)-f^{*}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\theta}
. We have the following:

Proposition 1
.

Consider the system (
AVD-H
) with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
and
r=\frac{\alpha\beta}{1+\omega}
. Take any
\eta>0
, set
\theta=(1+\omega)\gamma-\frac{\omega\eta\beta^{2}}{1+\omega}
, and let
\varepsilon
be defined by (
5
). For every
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\alpha}{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}\right)-\frac{\omega\eta\beta}{1+\omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta}{1+\omega}\left(\theta+\frac{\omega(1-\omega)}{2(1+\omega)}\eta\beta^{2}-\frac{\omega\alpha\beta}{t}\right)\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-\omega\beta\left\langle\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x},\dot{x}\right\rangle+\frac{\omega\eta\beta}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}.

Proof.

We begin by using (
AVD-H
), to compute the time derivative of
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x),\ddot{x}+\beta\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x}\right\rangle+\omega\langle\dot{x},\ddot{x}\rangle+\theta\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x),-\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}-\left(\gamma+\frac{r}{t}\right)\nabla f(x)\right\rangle+\theta\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\omega\left\langle\dot{x},-\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}-\beta\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x}-\left(\gamma+\frac{r}{t}\right)\nabla f(x)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{t}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-\left[(1+\omega)\gamma-\theta+\frac{(1+\omega)r-\alpha\beta}{t}\right]\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\left(\gamma+\frac{r-\alpha\beta}{t}\right)\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-\frac{\omega\alpha}{t}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}-\omega\beta\left\langle\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x},\dot{x}\right\rangle.

Substituting
r=\frac{\alpha\beta}{1+\omega}
, and then
\theta=(1+\omega)\gamma-\frac{\omega\eta\beta^{2}}{1+\omega}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\alpha}{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}\right)-[(1+\omega)\gamma-\theta]\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\left(\gamma-\frac{\omega\alpha\beta}{(1+\omega)t}\right)\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-\omega\beta\left\langle\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x},\dot{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\alpha}{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}\right)-\frac{\omega\eta\beta}{1+\omega}\Big[\beta\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta}{1+\omega}\left(\theta+\frac{\omega\eta\beta^{2}}{1+\omega}-\frac{\omega\alpha\beta}{t}\right)\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-\omega\beta\left\langle\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x},\dot{x}\right\rangle.

We conclude by observing that

\displaystyle\beta\langle\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}-\frac{1+\omega}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2},

and then rearranging the terms.

2.1
Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality

We now analyze the convergence rate of the function values when the objective function satisfies Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality with constant
\mu>0
, namely:

\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu(f(x)-f^{*}).

(6)

We have the following:

Theorem 2
.

Let
f
be twice differentiable and satisfy Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality with constant
\mu>0
. Consider the system (
AVD-H
), where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
and
r=\alpha\beta
. Let
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
. For every
t>0
, we have

f(x)-f^{*}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)e^{-2\mu\beta t},&\quad t\leq\frac{\alpha}{\mu\beta},\\[5.0pt]
\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mu\beta e}\right)^{2\alpha}\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}},&\quad t>\frac{\alpha}{\mu\beta}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
f
is convex, then
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}}\right)
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Set
\omega=0
and
\eta=\mu
in Proposition
1
, and use the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality (
6
) to obtain

\dot{\varepsilon}=-\frac{\alpha}{t}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-\beta\theta\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}\leq-\frac{\alpha}{t}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-2\mu\beta\gamma(f(x)-f^{*}),

(
\theta=\gamma>0
). It follows that
\dot{\varepsilon}\leq-2\min\left\{\frac{\alpha}{t},\mu\beta\right\}\varepsilon
, which results in

\varepsilon(t)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varepsilon(0)e^{-2\mu\beta t},&&t\leq T:=\frac{\alpha}{\mu\beta},\\
\frac{\varepsilon(T)}{\left(t/T\right)^{2\alpha}},&&t>T.\end{array}\right.

If
v_{0}=-\beta\nabla f(x_{0})
, we have
\varepsilon(0)=\gamma\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)
and

\varepsilon(T)\leq\varepsilon(0)e^{-2\mu\beta T}=\gamma\left(f(x_{0})-f^{*}\right)e^{-2\alpha}.

The bound is obtained by noting that
f(x)-f^{*}\leq\frac{\varepsilon(t)}{\gamma}
.

To prove that
f\big(x(t)\big)-f^{*}\leq o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}}\right)
as
t\to\infty
, we can assume
t>2T
, so that

\dot{\varepsilon}\leq-\frac{\alpha}{t}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}-2\mu\beta\gamma(f(x)-f^{*})\leq-\frac{2\alpha}{t}\varepsilon-\mu\beta\gamma(f(x)-f^{*}).

Multiplying by
t^{2\alpha}
gives

\mu\beta\gamma t^{2\alpha}(f(x)-f^{*})\leq-\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{2\alpha}\varepsilon\right).

Integrating from
2T
to
t
results in

\mu\beta\gamma\int_{2T}^{t}\tau^{2\alpha}(f(x(\tau))-f^{*})\,d\tau\leq-\left.\tau^{2\alpha}\varepsilon(\tau)\right|_{\tau=2T}^{t}\leq(2T)^{2\alpha}\varepsilon(2T)<\infty.

Write
g(t)=\beta t^{2\alpha}(f(x)-f^{*})
, so that
g(t)\geq 0
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty
. From

\dot{g}(t)=2\alpha\beta t^{2\alpha-1}(f(x)-f^{*})+t^{2\alpha}\langle\beta\nabla f(x),\dot{x}\rangle,

it follows that

|\dot{g}(t)|\leq 2\alpha\beta t^{2\alpha-1}(f(x)-f^{*})+t^{2\alpha}\left(\frac{\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+3\|\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}}{2}\right).

But,
\varepsilon\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}})
,
\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}})
and
\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\leq f(x)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}})
. This shows that
|\dot{g}|
is bounded on
(0,\infty)
. Since
g
is integrable, this means that
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=0
, and thus
f(x)-f^{*}\leq o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}}\right)
, as desired.

Remark 3
.

For these kinds of systems, the best results so far were
\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-\frac{2}{3}\alpha}\right)
, which were obtained for

\ddot{x}+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}+\beta\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x}+\nabla f(x)=0,\quad\alpha\geq 3,\ \beta\geq 0,

assuming strong convexity
[
21
,
7
,
4
]
. We have established a faster convergence rate, namely
\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-2\alpha}\right)
, which is valid for all
\alpha>0
and only assuming the PŁ inequality, even without convexity.

Remark 4
.

The turning point from the linear regime to
\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-2\alpha}\right)
occurs after the function value gap has been reduced by a factor of
e^{2\alpha}
. This takes place earlier if
\mu
is larger.

2.2
Strong convexity

In this subsection, we analyze the convergence rate of the function values when the objective function is strongly convex. Although
it is not possible

[
21
]
to obtain a linear rate if
\beta=0
, we do find one when
\beta>0
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
f
be twice differentiable and
\mu
-strongly convex. Consider the system (
AVD-H
), where,

\alpha>0,\quad\beta>0,\quad\gamma>\frac{1}{4}\mu\beta^{2},\quad r=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta.

For every
t\geq T:=\frac{4\alpha}{\mu\beta}+\frac{4\alpha\beta}{4\gamma-\mu\beta^{2}}
, we have,

f(x)-f^{*}\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mu\beta(t-T)}}{\left(t/T\right)^{2\alpha}}C_{T},

with
C_{T}=\frac{1}{2}\|v(T)+\beta\nabla f(x(T))\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|v(T)\|^{2}+\left(2\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\mu\beta^{2}\right)\big(f(x(T))-f^{*}\big)
.

Proof.

Setting
\omega=1
, we have
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}+\theta(f(x)-f^{*})
, where
\theta=2\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\mu\beta^{2}>0
. Since
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, we have
\left\langle\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x},\dot{x}\right\rangle\geq\mu\|\dot{x}\|^{2}
. Using Proposition
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\alpha}{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}\right)-\frac{\mu\beta}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{x}+\beta\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\mu\beta\left(\theta-\frac{\alpha\beta}{t}\right)\left(f(x)-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{2\alpha}{t}+\frac{\mu\beta}{2}\right)\varepsilon-\mu\beta\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\alpha\beta+\frac{2\alpha\theta}{\mu\beta}}{t}\right)(f(x)-f^{*})

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\frac{2\alpha}{t}+\frac{\mu\beta}{2}\right)\varepsilon,

where the last inequality is due to
t\geq\frac{4\alpha}{\mu\beta}+\frac{4\alpha\beta}{4\gamma-\mu\beta^{2}}=T
. This immediately gives the result.

Remark 6
.

A convergence rate of

f(x)-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2\alpha}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mu\beta t}\right)
holds for the system

\ddot{x}+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}+\beta\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x}+\left(1+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2t}\right)\nabla f(x)=0,

when
\alpha>0
,
0<\beta<\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu}}
and
t
is large enough. The case
\alpha=3
was studied in
[
15
]
. Assuming
0<\beta<\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
, they obtain a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\mu\beta t}\right)
. The exponent in the linear part is half as large as ours, and that of the sublinear part is just one third of ours, all under a stronger constraint for
\beta
.

3
Convergence rates II: gradient algorithms

In this section, we develop accelerated gradient algorithms and prove their convergence rates for the function values. The algorithms are obtained by discretizing the dynamics (
AVD-H
) and attain different rates depending on the geometric characteristics of the objective function.

Let
f
be
L
-smooth, define the step size
h\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\right]
and introduce the velocity

v_{k}:=\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{h}.

The term involving Hessian-driven damping is approximated by

\nabla^{2}f(x)\dot{x}\sim\frac{\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})}{h}.

We combine this with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}t&\sim&kh,\\
\ddot{x}&\sim&\frac{v_{k}-v_{k-1}}{h},\\
\dot{x}&\sim&v_{k},\\
\nabla f(x)&\sim&\nabla f(x_{k}),\end{array}\right.

and set
\beta=h
and
r=\alpha\beta=\alpha h
, to obtain

(v_{k}-v_{k-1})+\frac{\alpha}{k}v_{k}+h\left(\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\right)+\left(\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)h\nabla f(x_{k})=0.

(7)

Setting
y_{k+1}=x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k})
, we can rewrite (
7
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k})\\
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\left(y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right)+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(\gamma-1)(y_{k+1}-x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(AGM)

The convergence rates for this algorithm will be investigated in what follows.

3.1
General estimations

In this subsection, we derive some useful estimations for proving the convergence rate of the algorithm (
AGM
). Our convergence proofs are based on the energy-like sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, defined by

E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma\left[(1-\omega)\psi_{k}+\omega\left(f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\right)\right],

(8)

where
0\leq\omega\leq 1
, and

\displaystyle\phi_{k}

\displaystyle:=v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k}),

\displaystyle\psi_{k}

\displaystyle:=f(x_{k})-f^{*}-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Remark 7
.

Notice that
y_{k+1}=x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k})
in (
AGM
). Since
f
is
L
-smooth and
h^{2}\leq\frac{1}{L}
, we obtain

f(y_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

This gives

0\leq f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\psi_{k},\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad 0\leq f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}E_{k}.

We have the following:

Proposition 8
.

Let
f
be convex and
L
-smooth, and set
0<h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
. Consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
8
), where
0\leq\omega\leq 1
. We have

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}=

\displaystyle-\tfrac{\alpha}{k+1}\left(1+\tfrac{\alpha/2}{k+1}\right)\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\gamma h^{2}\left(1-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\omega\gamma\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(y_{k+1})\right)-\omega\gamma h\left(\tfrac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}\right)\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\omega\gamma h^{2}\left(1+\gamma+\tfrac{\alpha}{k+1}-\tfrac{Lh^{2}}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

Proof.

By definition of
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+(1-\omega)\gamma(\psi_{k+1}-\psi_{k})+\omega\gamma(f(y_{k+2})-f(y_{k+1})).

(9)

From (
7
), we obtain

(v_{k+1}-v_{k})+h\left(\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\right)=-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}v_{k+1}-\left(\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)h\nabla f(x_{k+1}).

(10)

As a result,

\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}=-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\left(v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\right)-\gamma h\nabla f(x_{k+1}),

so that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)^{2}\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\left(\gamma+\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\right)\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\gamma h\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\quad\langle\phi_{k+1},\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1}),-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\left(v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\right)-\gamma h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\gamma h\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle-\gamma h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

Since
\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\langle\phi_{k+1},\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha/2}{k+1}\right)\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma h\left(\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}\right)\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma h^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

(11)

With
h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
in mind, we use the convexity and
L
-smoothness of
f
to get

f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\leq h\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k}\rangle-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Using (
10
), we obtain

v_{k}=\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}v_{k+1}+h\left(\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\right)+\left(\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)h\nabla f(x_{k+1}),

and then

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}h\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\left(\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

This gives

\psi_{k+1}-\psi_{k}\leq\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}h\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle+\left(\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)h^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

(12)

In view of
y_{k+2}=x_{k+1}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k+1})
and
L
-smoothness of
f
, we have

f(y_{k+2})\leq f(x_{k+1})-h^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}Lh^{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2},

from which we obtain

f(y_{k+2})-f(y_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k+1})-f(y_{k+1})-h^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}Lh^{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

(13)

To conclude, we substitute (
3.1
), (
12
) and (
13
) into (
3.1
), and rearrange the terms.

3.2
Convexity and Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality

In this subsection, we prove the convergence rate of the function values for the algorithm (
AGM
) in case the objective function is convex and satisfies Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality.

Theorem 9
.

Let
f
be convex,
L
-smooth and satisfy Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality with constant
\mu
, where
L>\mu>0
. Generate the sequences
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
according to (
AGM
) with
\alpha>0
,
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
0<h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
.
Given
x_{1}
, set
v_{1}=-h\nabla f(x_{1})
. We have

f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{f(x_{1})-f^{*}}{\left[1+\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right]^{k-1}},&\quad 1\leq k\leq K,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{f(x_{1})-f^{*}}{\left[1+\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right]^{K-1}}\left(\frac{K+1+\alpha}{k+1+\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha},&\quad k>K,\end{array}\right.

where
K
is the largest integer such that
\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)(K+1)^{2}-2\alpha(K+1)-\alpha^{2}\leq 0
. Moreover,
f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq o(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}})
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

Setting
\omega=0
, we have
E_{k+1}=\frac{1}{2}\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\gamma\psi_{k+1}
. Using Proposition
8
and the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\left(1+\tfrac{\alpha/2}{k+1}\right)\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\gamma\left(f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha/2}{k+1}\right)\|v_{k+1}+h\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\gamma\psi_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq-\min\left\{\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}},\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right\}E_{k+1}.

If
1\leq k\leq K
, we have

E_{k}\leq\frac{E_{1}}{\left[1+\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right]^{k-1}}.

If
k>K
, we obtain
(1+\frac{\alpha}{k})^{2}E_{k}\leq E_{k-1}
, which, applied recursively, gives

E_{k}\leq\frac{E_{K}}{\prod_{m=K+1}^{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)^{2}}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\prod_{m=K+1}^{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[2\sum_{m=K+1}^{k}\ln\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left[2\alpha\sum_{m=K+1}^{k}\frac{1}{m+\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left[2\alpha\ln\left(\frac{k+1+\alpha}{K+1+\alpha}\right)\right],

where the first inequality is due to
\ln(1+x)\geq\frac{x}{x+1}
for
x>0
, and the second inequality is due to
\sum_{m=K+1}^{k}\frac{1}{m+\alpha}\geq\int_{K+1}^{k+1}\frac{1}{x+\alpha}\,dx
. Hence,

E_{k}\leq\left(\frac{K+1+\alpha}{k+1+\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha}E_{K}.

With
v_{1}=-h\nabla f(x_{1})
, we have

E_{1}\leq\gamma\left(f(x_{1})-f^{*}\right),

and

E_{K}\leq\frac{E_{1}}{\left[1+\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right]^{K-1}}\leq\frac{\gamma\left(f(x_{1})-f^{*}\right)}{\left[1+\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right]^{K-1}}.

We conclude by recalling that
f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{\gamma}
.

To prove that
f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq o\left(k^{-2\alpha}\right)
as
k\to\infty
, we use Proposition
8
(recall that
\omega=0
) to show that

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}E_{k+1}+\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{k+1}\psi_{k+1}-\gamma h^{2}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

Using the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality and the fact that
\psi_{k+1}\leq f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}
, we obtain

\left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\right)E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq\left(\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{k+1}-\mu\gamma h^{2}(2-\gamma)\right)\big(f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\big).

Since we are concerned with the behavior as
k\to\infty
, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu h^{2}(2-\gamma)
, so that

\left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\right)E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\mu\gamma h^{2}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\big(f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\big)\leq 0.

Multiplying by
(k+1+2\alpha)^{2\alpha}
, we get

(k+1+2\alpha)^{2\alpha}\left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\right)E_{k+1}-(k+1+2\alpha)^{2\alpha}E_{k}+\delta_{k}\leq 0,

where
\delta_{k}=\mu\gamma h^{2}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)(k+1+2\alpha)^{2\alpha}\big(f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\big)
. If we show that

(k+1+2\alpha)^{2\alpha}\left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{k+1}\right)\geq(k+2+2\alpha)^{2\alpha},

(14)

it will follow that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty
, and so
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}=0
, which is the same as
f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq o(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}})
. To this end, define

\xi(t)=\left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{t}\right)\left(\frac{t+2\alpha}{t+1+2\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha},

so that

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\alpha}{t^{2}}\left(\frac{t+2\alpha}{t+1+2\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha}+\frac{2\alpha}{t(t+1+2\alpha)}\left(\frac{t+2\alpha}{t+1+2\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha}{t}\left(\frac{t+2\alpha}{t+1+2\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{t+1+2\alpha}-\frac{1}{t}\right).

Clearly,
\dot{\xi}(t)<0
for every
t>0
. Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\xi(t)=1
, we must have
\xi(t)\geq 1
for every
t>0
. This implies that (
14
) holds, and completes the proof.

Remark 10
.

Setting
\gamma=1
in (
AGM
), one obtains
f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq o\left(k^{-2\alpha}\right)
, for the algorithm

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k})\\
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\left(y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right),\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha>0
and
0<h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
. A similar convergence rate
o\left(k^{-2\alpha}\right)
was derived in
[
16
]
, with
\alpha>1
and
0<h<\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
, for convex functions satisfying a local error bound condition, which is equivalent to Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality, for convex functions. Our approach extends the result to all
\alpha>0
and also includes the critical step size
h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}
.

3.3
Strong convexity

We now turn our attention to the strongly convex case.

Theorem 11
.

Let
f
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Generate
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
according to (
AGM
) with
\alpha>0
,
h\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}]
and
\gamma\in(0,2-Lh^{2})
. Given
x_{1}
, set
v_{1}=-h\nabla f(x_{1})
. For every
k\geq 1
, we have

f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\left(1-\rho\right)^{k-1}\left(f(x_{1})-f^{*}\right),

where
\rho\geq\mu\min\left\{\frac{\gamma h^{2}}{1+2L\gamma h^{2}},\frac{h^{2}(2-\gamma-Lh^{2})}{1+\frac{\mu}{L}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Setting
\omega=1
in Proposition
8
, we get

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq

\displaystyle\ \gamma\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(y_{k+1})\right)-\gamma h\left(\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}\right)\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\gamma h^{2}\left(2+\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}-\frac{Lh^{2}}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

(15)

Recalling that
y_{k+1}=x_{k}-h^{2}\nabla f(x_{k})
and
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+hv_{k}
, and using the strong convexity of
f
, we deduce that

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(y_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\rangle-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=h\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle-\frac{\mu h^{2}}{2}\|v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(16)

From (
10
), we have

v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})=\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}v_{k+1}+\left(\gamma+1+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)h\nabla f(x_{k+1}).

Combining this with (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(y_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq h\left(\frac{k+\alpha+1}{k+1}\right)\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),v_{k+1}\rangle-\frac{\mu h^{2}}{2}\|v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h^{2}\left(\gamma+1+\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

Substituting in (
3.3
), we deduce that

E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-\frac{\mu\gamma h^{2}}{2}\|v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\gamma h^{2}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{Lh^{2}}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

(17)

The strong convexity of
f
implies that
f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}
. Using this, together with the
L
-smoothness of
f
, we obtain

\displaystyle f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}

\displaystyle=f(y_{k+1})-f(x_{k+1})+f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

For every
\zeta,\xi\in H
and
m>0
, we have
\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2m}\|\zeta\|^{2}+\frac{m}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}
. Therefore,

f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2m}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{h^{2}(L+m)}{2}\|v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},

for every
m>0
. Since
E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma\left(f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\right)
, we obtain

E_{k}\leq\frac{1+\gamma h^{2}(L+m)}{2}\|v_{k}+h\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.

(18)

Comparing (
17
) and (
18
), we deduce that

E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-\rho E_{k},

where

\rho:=\max_{m>0}\min\left\{\frac{\gamma\mu h^{2}}{1+(L+m)\gamma h^{2}},\frac{(2-\gamma-Lh^{2})h^{2}}{1/m+1/\mu}\right\}.

Bounding with
m=L
, we obtain the lower bound for
\rho
.

Corollary 12
.

Let
f
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
be generated by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-\frac{1}{2L}\nabla f(x_{k})\\
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\left(y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right),\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha>0
. Given
x_{1}
, set
v_{1}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2L}}\nabla f(x_{1})
. For every
k\geq 1
, we have

f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{4(L+\mu)}\right)^{k-1}\left(f(x_{1})-f^{*}\right).

Proof.

We use Theorem
11
with
\gamma=1
and
h^{2}=\frac{1}{2L}
, to obtain

E_{k+1}\leq(1-\rho)E_{k},\qquad\hbox{where}\qquad\rho\geq\frac{\mu}{2L}\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2(1+\frac{\mu}{L})}\right\}=\frac{\mu}{4(L+\mu)}.

Since
f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq E_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, and
E_{1}=f(y_{2})-f^{*}\leq f(x_{1})-f^{*}
, the result follows.

Remark 13
.

A faster convergence rate of approximately
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{4L}\right)^{k}\right)
has been established in
[
15
,
9
]
for
\alpha\geq 3
.

4
Convergence rates III: proximal algorithms

In this section, we consider the convex composite optimization problem:

\min_{x\in H}F(x)=f(x)+g(x),

(19)

where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
L
-smooth;
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is convex, proper and lower semicontinuous. We assume that the solution set of problem (
19
) is nonempty, and denote
F^{*}=\min(F)
.

To solve problem (
19
), one can employ the accelerated proximal-gradient method, also known as FISTA
[
10
]
, namely:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-sG_{s}(x_{k})\\
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}),\end{array}\right.

(APM)

where
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}
and the operator
G_{s}
is given by

G_{s}(x):=\tfrac{x-\prox_{sg}(x-s\nabla f(x))}{s},\ \text{where, }\prox_{sg}(z):=\argmin_{u}\left\{g(u)+\frac{1}{2s}\|u-z\|^{2}\right\}.

It follows from the definitions above that

x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}=\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\big(x_{k}-y_{k}-sG_{s}(x_{k})\big).

(20)

The following auxiliary results will be useful in what follows. The first one is
[
9
, Lemma 7]
, namely:

Lemma 14
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, let
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be proper, convex and lower-semicontinuous, and set
F=f+g
. For every
x,y\in H
, we have

F\left(x-sG_{s}(x)\right)\leq F(y)+\left\langle G_{s}(x),x-y\right\rangle-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

If
f
is merely convex, the inequality holds with
\mu=0
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-smooth, and let
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be proper, convex and lower-semicontinuous. Let the function
F=f+g
satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality with constant
\mu>0
. For every
x\in H
, we have

\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu\left(F\left(x-sG_{s}(x)\right)-F^{*}\right).

Proof.

Writing
y:=x-sG_{s}(x)
, we obtain
y=\prox_{sg}(x-s\nabla f(x))
, and then

G_{s}(x)\in\nabla f(x)+\partial g(y).

Denote

\xi:=G_{s}(x)+\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\in\partial F(y).

Since
f
is convex and
L
-smooth, we can deduce

\displaystyle\|\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}+\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+2\langle G_{s}(x),\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}+\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}-\frac{2}{s}\langle y-x,\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}+\frac{sL-2}{sL}\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2},

because
sL\leq 1
. By the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality, we conclude that

\|G_{s}(x)\|^{2}\geq\|\xi\|^{2}\geq 2\mu\left(F(y)-F^{*}\right),

as desired.

4.1
General estimations

In this subsection, we present some useful estimations before we proceed to prove the convergence rate of the algorithm (
APM
). Our convergence proofs center around the following energy-like sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, which is defined by

E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+s\left(F(y_{k})-F^{*}\right),

(21)

where
\phi_{k}:=x_{k}-y_{k}
. We have the following:

Proposition 16
.

Let
f
be convex and
L
-smooth. Let
0<s<\frac{1}{L}
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be generated according to (
APM
), and consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined by (
21
). Then,

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2k}\right)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}-s\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}+s\left(F(y_{k+1})-F(y_{k})\right).

Proof.

By definition,

E_{k+1}-E_{k}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}\right)+s\left(F(y_{k+1})-F(y_{k})\right).

From (
20
), we obtain

\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}=-\frac{\alpha}{k+\alpha}(x_{k}-y_{k})-\frac{ks}{k+\alpha}G_{s}(x_{k}),

so that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{k+\alpha}\right)^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\right)^{2}s^{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\alpha ks}{(k+\alpha)^{2}}\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle x_{k}-y_{k},-\frac{\alpha}{k+\alpha}(x_{k}-y_{k})-\frac{ks}{k+\alpha}G_{s}(x_{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{k+\alpha}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{ks}{k+\alpha}\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle.

Since

\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\langle\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2},

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha(2k+\alpha)}{2(k+\alpha)^{2}}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{k^{2}s}{(k+\alpha)^{2}}\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\right)^{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\alpha(2k+\alpha)}{2(k+\alpha)^{2}}\|x_{k}-y_{k}-sG_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-s\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Using (
20
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=-\frac{\alpha(2k+\alpha)}{2k^{2}}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}-s\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

We conclude by adding
s\left(F(y_{k+1})-F(y_{k})\right)
on both sides.

4.2
Convexity and Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality

We begin by establishing a convergence rate for the function values in case the objective function is convex and satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality.

Theorem 17
.

Let
F=f+g
, where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
L
-smooth, and
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is proper, convex and lower-semicontinuous. Assume
F
satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality with constant
\mu>0
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be generated according to (
APM
), with

\alpha>0
,
0<s<\frac{1}{L}
and
x_{1}=y_{1}
. For every
k\geq 1
, we have

F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{F(y_{1})-F^{*}}{\left[1+\mu s(1-sL)\right]^{k-1}},&\quad 1\leq k\leq K,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{F(y_{1})-F^{*}}{\left[1+\mu s(1-sL)\right]^{K-1}}\left(\frac{K+\alpha}{k+\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha},&\quad k>K,\end{array}\right.

where
K
is the largest integer such that
\mu s(1-sL)K^{2}-2\alpha K-\alpha^{2}\leq 0
. Moreover,
F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\leq o(\frac{1}{k^{2\alpha}})
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

Setting
x=x_{k}
and
y=y_{k}
in Lemma
14
gives

F(y_{k+1})-F(y_{k})\leq\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle-s\left(1-\frac{1}{2}sL\right)\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Combining this with Proposition
16
, and then using Lemma
15
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2k}\right)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{s^{2}(1-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2k}\right)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}-\mu s^{2}(1-sL)\big(F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\big)

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\min\left\{\frac{2\alpha}{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2k}\right),\mu s(1-sL)\right\}E_{k+1},

by the definition of
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
. We now proceed as in the proof of Theorem
9
. If
k\leq K
, then

E_{k}\leq\frac{E_{1}}{\big(1+\mu s(1-sL)\big)^{k-1}}.

For
k>K
, we have

\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)^{2}E_{k+1}\leq E_{k}
,
and so

E_{k}\leq\left(\frac{K+\alpha}{k+\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha}E_{K}.

Since
F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{s}
and
E_{1}=s\big(F(y_{1})-F^{*}\big)
, the conclusion easily follows. For all
k
large enough, we have
\frac{2\alpha}{k}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2k}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu s(1-sL)
. We can use (
22
) to deduce that

\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)^{2}E_{k+1}-E_{k}+\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{2}\big(F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\big)\leq 0.

Multiply this by
(k+\alpha)^{2\alpha}
to obtain

(k+\alpha)^{2\alpha}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)^{2}E_{k+1}-(k+\alpha)^{2\alpha}E_{k}+\delta_{k}\leq 0,

where
\delta_{k}:=\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{2}(k+\alpha)^{2\alpha}\big(F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}\big)
. As in the proof of Theorem
9
, we first show that
(k+\alpha)^{2\alpha}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)^{2}\geq(k+1+\alpha)^{2\alpha}
, then deduce that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty
, and finally conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}=0
.

4.3
Strong convexity

In this subsection, we prove the linear convergence rate for the algorithm (
APM
) in case
f
is strongly convex.

Theorem 18
.

Let
F=f+g
, where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, and
g:H\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is proper, convex and lower-semicontinuous. Generate
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
following (
APM
), with

\alpha>0
,
0<s<\frac{1}{L}
and
x_{1}=y_{1}
. For every
k\geq 1
, we have

F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\frac{F(x_{1})-F^{*}}{(1+\rho)^{k-1}},

where
\rho=\mu\min\left\{\frac{s}{2},\frac{s(1-sL)}{1+\mu Ls^{2}}\right\}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
11
. On the one hand, we use (
20
) to show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{k}{k+\alpha}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{1+m}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+m}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(23)

On the other hand, setting
x=x_{k}
and
y=x^{*}
in Lemma
14
gives

\displaystyle F(y_{k+1})-F^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2\mu}-\frac{s(2-sL)}{2}\right]\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Combining this with (
4.3
), we obtain

E_{k+1}\leq\frac{1+m}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{s}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\mu}+s\left(sL-1+\frac{1}{m}\right)\right]\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(24)

Setting
x=x_{k}
and
y=y_{k}
in Lemma
14
gives

F(y_{k+1})-F(y_{k})\leq\langle G_{s}(x_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle-\frac{s(1-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}.

Combining this with Proposition
16
,
we get

E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-\frac{\mu s}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{s^{2}(1-sL)}{2}\|G_{s}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(25)

By comparing (
24
) and (
25
), we deduce that

(1+\rho)E_{k+1}\leq E_{k},

where

\rho=\max_{m>0}\min\left\{\frac{\mu s}{1+m},\frac{\mu s(1-sL)}{1+\mu s\left(sL-1+\frac{1}{m}\right)}\right\},

and the lower bound is obtained by choosing
m=1
.

Remark 19
.

For any
\alpha>0
, the step size
s=\frac{1}{2L}
gives
F(y_{k})-F^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{(1+\rho)^{k}}\right)
, with
\rho=\frac{\mu}{4L+\mu}
. A similar result was obtained in
[
15
,
9
]
for
\alpha\geq 3
.

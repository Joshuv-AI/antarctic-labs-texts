CL-ODGAN: an unpaired attention-guided GAN framework for remote sensing image dehazing
Authors: Hongwei Zhang, Xin Mu, Bin Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Haze in remote sensing imagery severely degrades image quality and impedes accurate interpretation. While deep learning has advanced dehazing, its reliance on large-scale paired data is impractical for remote sensing applications. To overcome this, we propose CL-ODGAN, a novel unpaired dehazing framework built upon a systematically designed generative architecture and a dual-level contrastive learning constraint. Our framework is architected around three synergistic components: (1) A robust generator that employs custom Residual blocks embedded with Omni-dimensional Dynamic Convolution (ODConv) for dynamic multi-scale perception and a hybrid normalization (BN + IN) strategy for training stability. This design enables adaptive feature extraction and detail restoration under non-uniform haze. (2) A core unsupervised supervisory signal implemented via a dual-level (feature and pixel) contrastive learning mechanism within the GAN framework. This mechanism guides the image translation process without paired data by enforcing structural and feature consistency. (3) An attention-guided adversarial training paradigm that focuses the network on critical hazy regions, ensuring efficient learning. In essence, CL-ODGAN integrates ODConv and hybrid normalization withinthe generator to enhance its representational capacity, while employing contrastive learning as the supervisory engine—all orchestrated under an unpaired, attention-aware GAN framework. Experiments demonstrate its superior performance in removing non-uniform haze and recovering fine details without requiring paired training data.

Introduction

Detection and interpretation of remote sensing imagery play a vital role in various fields including climate prediction, agricultural yield assessment, natural disaster monitoring, mineral resource exploration, and military surveillance1–4. However, due to interference factors such as haze and clouds in the atmosphere, captured remote sensing images often suffer from severe quality degradation manifested as reduced contrast, color distortion, and blurred details, which significantly afects subsequent image analysis and application effectiveness.

Research methods in remote sensing image dehazing have primarily undergone an important evolution from physics-based model-driven to data-driven approaches. Traditional methods mainly rely on the atmospheric scattering physical model, with the most representative being the Dark Channel Prior (DCP)5 and Color Attenuation Prior6. With the rapid development of deep learning technology, data-driven methods such as AOD-Net7 and GridDehazeNet8 have achieved significant breakthroughs in dehazing performance. However, current remote sensing image dehazing techniques still face difficulties in obtaining precisely paired hazy/clear image datasets in real-world scenarios; additionally, existing methods show inadequate performance in handling non-uniform haze distributions.

In recent years, unsupervised learning has demonstrated significant value in the image dehazing field. The successful application of CycleGAN9 in unpaired image translation tasks established the foundation for this direction. Subsequently, CycleDehaze10 (Engin et al., 2018) first introduced an unsupervised learning framework into image dehazing tasks, effectively alleviating the dependency on paired training data. Zheng et al.11 proposed Dehaze-AGGAN (Attention-guided GAN) which further enhanced model performance by incorporating attention mechanisms. However, these methods still suffer from insufficient feature representation capability and inefficient cross-scale feature fusion in remote sensing image dehazing tasks.

To address the core challenges in remote sensing image dehazing, such as strong dependence on paired data, insufficient generalization ability, and poor detail recovery, this paper proposes CL-ODGAN, an innovative unpaired dehazing framework. Network

Prior-based dehazing via the atmospheric scattering model

The atmospheric scattering model serves as the theoretical foundation of the dehazing field. Its formula I(x) = J(x)t(x) + A(1—t(x)) describes the image formation process in the atmospheric environment. Based on this model, He et al.5 proposed the dark channel prior, which assumes that the minimum value in local regions of a haze-free image is close to zero, thereby recovering the haze-free image by estimating the atmospheric light value and the transmission map. However, the dark channel prior may fail in sky regions or near bright objects because the dark channel assumption does not hold in these areas. To address this issue, He et al.12 proposed a novel haze removal algorithm named HALP, based on a heterogeneous atmospheric light prior. Furthermore, Liang et al.13 developed an Atmospheric Light value Fusion Estimation (ALFE) model, which integrates a Heterogeneous Prior-guided Haze Concentration Model (HP-HCM) to derive the global atmospheric light value. Simultaneously, Golts et al.14 proposed an unsupervised training method that achieves dehazing by minimizing the Dark Channel Prior (DCP) energy function.

It is noteworthy that during the development of prior-based dehazing, several foundational and complementary core works have emerged. For example, Fattal21 pioneered an image statistics-based prior, which restores images under the assumption that the transmission is uncorrelated with the scene albedo, laying the groundwork for the field. His subsequent research introduced the Color-Lines Prior22, which further leverages the geometric properties of pixel distributions in RGB space to estimate the transmission map. Tan23 adopted a different approach by employing a Markov Random Field-based contrast maximization strategy, achieving dehazing without directly relying on the physical model. The most influential Dark Channel Prior5 provides a powerful and intuitive transmission estimation scheme by statistically analyzing the low-intensity characteristics in local regions of haze-free images. To address the failure of the dark channel prior in specific scenarios, subsequent studies proposed various improved priors. For instance, Berman et al.24 introduced the Haze-Line prior, which utilizes the distribution patterns of non-local pixels in color space to handle non-uniform haze. Zhu et al.6 proposed a computationally efficient Color Attenuation Prior, which rapidly estimates the transmission map by establishing a linear relationship between the difference in image saturation and brightness and the scene depth. These hand-crafted priors, based on different physical insights and statistical regularities, collectively constituted the core technical framework of single image dehazing before the deep learning era and have continued to provide theoretical inspiration for unsupervised and physics-driven dehazing methods.

Learning-based remote sensing image dehazing

With the advancement of deep learning, various learning-based models have been utilized for image dehazing. Cai et al.15 first proposed DehazeNet, which employs convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to estimate the transmission map and combines it with the atmospheric scattering model to achieve end-to-end dehazing. Subsequently, Li et al.7 proposed AOD-Net, which directly predicts clear images through a lightweight network, avoiding explicit estimation of the transmission map and atmospheric light. In recent years, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated significant advantages in dehazing tasks. Engin et al.10 proposed Cycle-Dehaze, based on the CycleGAN framework, enabling dehazing training without paired data and addressing the scarcity of paired data in real-world scenarios.

Meanwhile, end-to-end CNN-based dehazing methods continue to evolve. Zhang et al.25 proposed a densely connected pyramid dehazing network, enhancing performance through feature reuse. Liu et al.26 designed GridDehazeNet, an attention-based multi-scale network that effectively aggregates features at different scales. Likewise dedicated to strengthening feature representation, Xie et al.27 designed the EFABA and GDAL modules, which achieve accurate edge localization and global context modeling, respectively, and enhance the feature representation capability of remote sensing images. Qin et al.28 introduced the Feature Fusion Attention Network (FFA-Net), focusing on key regions with uneven haze distribution.

With the rise of Transformers in the field of computer vision, they have also been applied to dehazing tasks. Furthermore, in the context of remote sensing, Xie et al.29 designed the TMCFM and FSDC modules along with a DSC classifier, focusing on the extraction and fusion of multi-scale contextual features. The multi-feature constraints and context-aware framework proposed by Zhu et al.30 provide new ideas for dehazing remote sensing images.Song et al.31 first explored the application of Vision Transformers to single image dehazing. Guo et al.32 further proposed an Image Dehazing Transformer with transmission-aware 3D position embedding, better modeling the spatial distribution characteristics of haze.

Additionally, self-supervised techniques such as contrastive learning have been successfully introduced into the dehazing field. Wu et al.33 proposed a compact single image dehazing framework based on contrastive learning, improving performance by pulling the features of clear images close and pushing those of hazy images apart. Most recently, two advanced methods have been proposed for remote sensing image dehazing. Dehazing-DiffGAN36 combines diffusion models and GANs in a two-stage pipeline to achieve high-fidelity image restoration. Meanwhile, INP-Net37 introduces an implicit neural prompting mechanism and an ASP Transformer block to enhance global modeling and feature diversity. Both works share the same goal of improving dehazing performance for remote sensing imagery. While their technical paths differ from our encoder-decoder and contrastive learning framework, there is potential synergy between their advanced generative/implicit modeling strategies and our feature discrimination approach, which could be explored in future work.

Method

Overall network architecture

This paper proposes CL-ODGAN, a network module designed for unpaired remote sensing image dehazing . The framework comprises three core modules: generator, discriminator, and self-supervised feature constraint module (Dual-Level Contrastive Learning Strategy). The generator, built upon a ResNet-50 backbone, incorporates an enhanced multi-scale hybrid attention module within its encoder. This module integrates spatial and channel attention to enable adaptive screening of multi-scale features. A cross-scale feature fusion mechanism in the deeper network layers aggregates shallow detail information with deep semantic information. The decoder employs a progressive upsampling strategy alongside a reconstruction module that combines residual connections and attention gating, thereby restoring image resolution while maximally preserving structural and textural details.

The discriminator employs a 70 × 70 PatchGAN to ensure training stability with unpaired data, and complements the generator’s global feature learning, jointly enhancing dehazing precision while avoiding local artifacts. The self-supervised feature constraint module, optimized via contrastive learning, establishes a dual constraint mechanism at the feature and pixel levels. At the feature level, it constructs positive and negative sample pairs to align the generated dehazed features with real scene features in the high-level semantic space while distinguishing them from hazy features. At the pixel level, a local patch contrast strategy improves the fidelity and naturalness of local details in the output image. Additionally, the method adopts a multi-loss fusion strategy, combining adversarial, perceptual, structural similarity, and contrastive learning losses into a comprehensive objective to guide the network training collaboratively.

The core innovation of this framework is the organic integration of the improved multi-scale attention mechanism with multi-level contrastive learning, coupled with the PatchGAN’s local discrimination architecture. This synergy enables effective removal of non-uniform haze in complex remote sensing scenes without paired training data and significantly enhances the detail preservation in the dehazed results.

Improved multi-scale attention encoder

The encoder of the generator is constructed upon a ResNet-50 backbone, with its core innovations strategically designed to address the unique challenges inherent in unpaired remote sensing dehazing tasks, such as non-uniform haze distribution and the critical requirement for preserving ground-object details. The principal enhancements are concentrated within three key aspects: adaptive multi-scale feature extraction, stable deep feature refinement, and effective cross-level information fusion.

A innovation involves the integration of Omni-Dimensional Dynamic Convolution (ODConv) coupled with a parallel standard convolutional branch at the feature extraction stage. This design tackles the challenge of highly variable haze densities and scales across remote sensing images. The ODConv module adaptively adjusts its kernel parameters to capture these irregular patterns, while the parallel branch ensures the stability of fundamental local feature extraction. The outputs from both pathways are subsequently fused, enabling the network to respond dynamically to complex haze conditions without compromising structural integrity.

To enhance training stability and feature preservation in deep layers, a hybrid normalization strategy is implemented within the nine improved residual blocks. Diverging from approaches reliant on a single normalization technique, this strategy employs Instance Normalization in shallow layers to adapt to individual image styles, and utilizes Batch Normalization in deeper layers to stabilize training dynamics via batch statistics. This combination effectively balances style adaptability with training robustness, thereby mitigating the issue of detail over smoothing commonly encountered in deep networks.

Furthermore, a cross-level feature fusion module is devised to resolve the inconsistency between shallow spatial details and deep semantic information. By concatenating and compressing features from different hierarchical levels, this module selectively enhances information critical to the dehazing task, ultimately producing a balanced feature map that provides a solid foundation for the decoder to reconstruct a clear, detailed image.

Decoder and image reconstruction

The decoder is responsible for reconstructing the dehazed image from the encoder features. Its structure is symmetrical to the encoder, and at each level, it concatenates with the corresponding encoder features through skip connections, thereby integrating low-level details with high-level semantic information. The concatenated features are optimized through Instance Normalization and the ODBottleneck module, followed by progressive up sampling via transposed convolution to restore spatial details and structural integrity. The final output preliminary dehazed image exhibits high textural clarity and color consistency.

Discriminator

For the discriminator design, this study adopts the classic PatchGAN architecture. In contrast to discriminators with a single global decision output, the PatchGAN divides the input image into multiple local patches, performs an independent judgment for each patch, and finally aggregates these results. This mechanism involves fewer parameters and higher computational efficiency, which is particularly conducive to stabilizing the training process in unpaired data scenarios. Furthermore, its emphasis on local textures enables effective preservation of critical ground feature details in remote sensing images. In detail, the discriminator takes as input an image pair composed of the generated dehazed image and the original hazy image, which are concatenated along the channel axis. As a fully convolutional network (FCN), this discriminator uses 4 × 4 convolutional kernels with a stride of 2 for progressive down sampling to output a discrimination map. For the discriminator’s final output layer, each element in this map corresponds to a 70 × 70-pixel receptive field at the input image scale, representing the probability that the corresponding local regions are real.

Experiments

Experimental setup

This section provides a comprehensive experimental evaluation of the dehazing performance. All experiments were implemented based on the PyTorch 1.12.1 framework and executed in parallel on 4 × NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPUs. The training process utilized the Adam optimizer 20 (Kingma and Ba, 2015) with an initial learning rate of 0.0001 (β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.999), complemented by weight decay (1e-4) and gradient clipping (threshold 0.01) to stabilize training. The training strategy comprised a complete cycle of 200 epochs, which included a fixed learning rate for the first 100 epochs, followed by linear decay over the subsequent 100 epochs, and incorporated a 10-epoch warm-up phase. The experiments utilized the public SateHaze1k (Haze1k) remote sensing dehazing dataset 34. The dataset consists of three subsets: SateHaze1k-thin, SateHaze1k-moderate, and SateHaze1k-thick. Each subset contains 400 pairs of synthetic remote sensing hazy images. The data was partitioned such that 320 image pairs were used for training, 35 pairs for validation, and 45 pairs for performance evaluation. Since the StateHaze1K dataset is inherently paired, we intentionally employed a training procedure with shuffled data order to simulate a unpaired training scenario. This design is motivated by our goal to validate the robustness of our pixel-level contrastive learning framework in scenarios where one-to-one paired labels are unavailable, which aligns more closely with real-world dehazing tasks. Specifically, after shuffling, the clear reference images are no longer strictly paired with their original hazy counterparts. This method combines feature-level contrastive learning and pixel-level contrast learning to achieve effective dehazing and performance improvement without relying on paired data.

While quantitative and visual evaluations substantiate the advantages of CL‑ODGAN, the method remains challenged under complex atmospheric scenarios. As illustrated in Fig. 3, in regions where dense and light haze interlace, the model suffers from localized color distortion and luminance discontinuity—indicative of inconsistent transmittance estimation. This limitation stems from the training data’s inability to encompass the joint distribution of extreme haze concentrations and complex surface reflectances, which impedes the model from disentangling physically consistent radiance and transmission fields. Consequently, despite its overall metric superiority, CL‑ODGAN still requires improvements in physical interpretability and local robustness. To address these issues, future research could incorporate lightweight physics‑informed modules or weakly‑supervised atmospheric scattering priors, thereby enhancing generalization to complex real‑world conditions without sacrificing the flexibility of unpaired learning.

To validate the rationale and synergistic effect of our proposed framework, we conducted a systematic ablation study that progressively builds from the baseline towards our full model. We evaluate four configurations: the baseline CycleGAN, our enhanced generator (with ODConv and hybrid normalization), and the full CL-ODGAN (enhanced generator + dual-level contrastive learning), against clear ground-truth using PSNR and SSIM.

The results (Table 3) clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of our hierarchical design. The baseline CycleGAN achieves a PSNR of 15.76 and SSIM of 0.8432. Replacing its residual blocks with our custom ODBottleneck units—integrating ODConv for dynamic perception and hybrid normalization for stability—yields a noticeable improvement (PSNR: 15.92, SSIM: 0.8455). This confirms that our architectural modifications provide a stronger foundation.

Crucially, incorporating the dual-level contrastive learning mechanism atop this enhanced generator (i.e., the full CL-ODGAN) achieves a substantial performance leap (PSNR: 28.15, SSIM: 0.8525). This dramatic gain underscores the synergistic relationship: the enhanced generator provides the capacity for high-fidelity restoration, while contrastive learning provides the essential guidance to steer this capacity without paired data. It is this combination that breaks the dependency on paired supervision (Table 3).

Conclusion

This study aims to address the core challenge of scarce paired real training data in remote sensing image dehazing tasks by innovatively proposing an unsupervised dehazing network framework that integrates contrastive learning with Omni-Dimensional Dynamic Convolution (ODCONV). The key contributions of this framework are twofold: First, we introduced an ODCONV module to replace traditional static convolution. This module dynamically adjusts convolutional kernel weights across spatial, channel, input, and output dimensions based on input features, enabling precise extraction and adaptive fusion of multi-scale contextual features. This significantly enhances the model’s generalization capability under complex haze distributions and achieves breakthrough progress in edge preservation and detail recovery. Second, we designed an efficient contrastive learning constraint mechanism. By constructing positive and negative sample pairs, it pulls the features of clear images and dehazed results closer while pushing them away from haze features in the feature space, thereby enhancing the model’s robustness in extracting haze-invariant features. In summary, the proposed method not only effectively circumvents the reliance on paired data but also, through the synergistic optimization of dynamic convolution and contrastive learning, achieves advanced dehazing performance in both subjective visual quality and objective evaluation metrics, offering a novel solution for unsupervised remote sensing image restoration.

Although the proposed CL-ODGAN achieves promising performance on most remote sensing dehazing scenarios, it still has certain limitations. For example, the model may struggle with extremely low-light conditions or scenes with heavy, non-uniform haze, and it requires further optimization for edge cases. In future work, we plan to improve the model’s generalization to more diverse haze distributions and explore lightweight variants for real-time applications.

Ward-Takahashi Identity in Denominator Regularization at One Loop
Mickaya A. Razanaparany

Abstract
Explicit analytic expressions for the electron self-energy and the vertex correction in quantum electrodynamics are derived at one loop using the recently proposed regularization scheme known as denominator regularization, assisted by its correspondence with dimensional regularization to determine the coefficient functions, which are a specific ingredient of this approach. We then show that the regularized amplitudes satisfy the Ward-Takahashi identity, thereby ensuring that gauge symmetry is preserved after regularization.



1.  Introduction

Loop diagrams derived from Feynman rules in quantum field theory
(QFT)
often produce divergent integrals. These divergences are treated using regularization, which ensures that physical quantities remain finite when the regulator is removed. Several regularization schemes exist, each with its own advantages and limitations. A crucial requirement is that the chosen scheme preserves the symmetries of the theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

Dimensional regularization
(DIM REG)
is one of the most commonly used schemes in
QFT
. It controls divergent loop integrals by analytically continuing the number of spacetime dimensions from
4
to
d=4-2\epsilon

[
5
]
. By slightly reducing the number of dimensions, the momentum integrals grow more slowly at high energies, which makes the previously divergent integrals finite. The divergences then appear as poles in
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
when the result is expanded around
\epsilon=0
, and these poles are removed through renormalization
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
.

Denominator regularization
(DEN REG)
is a recently proposed scheme in which, after combining terms with Feynman parameters, the overall power of the denominator in a loop amplitude is analytically continued from
n
to
n+\epsilon
, while the number of spacetime dimensions is fixed. A specific ingredient of this scheme is the coefficient function
f_{(n,p)}(\epsilon)
, which smoothly approaches
1
as
\epsilon\to 0
, where
n
is the original power of the denominator and
p
is the superficial degree of divergence
[
6
,
7
]
.
DEN REG
is compatible with the minimal subtraction (
MS
) renormalization scheme, and some of its desirable properties and advantages are discussed in
[
7
,
6
,
9
,
8
,
10
]
.

Our objective is to perform a consistency check of the Ward-Takahashi identity (
WTI
) using
DEN REG
. The
WTI
is useful for demonstrating that gauge symmetry is preserved after regularization
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
11
,
12
]
. In this work, we explore the application of
DEN REG
to this problem, showing how this scheme can be employed to regularize the electron self-energy and vertex correction at one-loop level, leading to the verification of the
WTI
at next-to-leading order (
NLO
), under the on-shell condition.

The structure of the rest of this paper is as follows. In Section

4.1  Setup and Perturbative Expansions

Gauge invariance implies an exact relation between the full (all-order) fermion-photon vertex
\Gamma^{\mu}
and the inverse fermion propagator

-q_{\mu}\Gamma^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)=eS_{F}^{-1}(p^{\prime})-eS_{F}^{-1}(p),\quad p^{\prime}=p+q.

(4.1)

To

\Gamma^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)=ie\gamma^{\mu}+ie\Gamma^{\mu}_{\text{1-Loop}}(p^{\prime},p),

\displaystyle S_{F}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{i(\cancel{p}+m)}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon}+\frac{i(\cancel{p}+m)}{p^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\left(-i\sum_{\text{1-Loop}}(\cancel{{p}})\right)\frac{i(\cancel{{p}}+m)}{p^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}

(4.3)

where, in the second line, we have summed the geometric series. Substituting the expansions (

4.2  One Loop Verification

We explicitly verify the relation at the one loop level, demonstrating its consistency with the
WTI
under on-shell conditions. In particular, we verify Eq. (
4.8
):

F_{1}(0)=\frac{2e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}dxdydz\delta(x+y+z-1)\left[\mathbb{E}-2\ln(1-z)-\frac{2z}{(1-z)^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),

(4.9)

where

\mathbb{E}:=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_{E}+\ln(4\pi)+\ln\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}\right).

(4.10)

We make use of the following integrals:

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}dxdydz\delta(x+y+z-1)=\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}dxdy\delta(x+y+z-1)=1-z.

(4.11)

Eq. (
4.9
) becomes:

F_{1}(0)=\frac{2e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}-2\int_{0}^{1}dz\ln(1-z)+2\int_{0}^{1}dzz\ln(1-z)-2\int_{0}^{1}dz\frac{z}{1-z}\right].

(4.12)

The evaluation of the integrals relies on the standard series expansion of the logarithm. We expand
\ln(1-z)
as a power series and integrate term by term, which yields a simple telescopic sum:

\int_{0}^{1}dz\ln(1-z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{n(n+1)}=-1\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}dzz\ln(1-z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{-1}{n(n+2)}=-\frac{3}{4}.

(4.13)

Thus, we find

F_{1}(0)=\frac{e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}+1-4\int_{0}^{1}dz\frac{z}{1-z}\right].

(4.14)

We now compute the right-hand side of Eq. (
4.8
). Let us denote

\mathbb{K}:=\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_{E}+\ln(4\pi)+\ln(\mu^2).

(4.15)

Then, using our one-loop electron self-energy, we have:

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\cancel{p}}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\text{1-Loop}}(\cancel{p})\Bigg)\Bigg|_{\cancel{p}=m}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{\partial}{\partial\cancel{p}}\bigg[\big(-x\cancel{p}+2m\big)\big(\mathbb{K}-\ln[(1-x)m^{2}-(1-x)xp^{2}]\big)\bigg]\Bigg|_{\cancel{p}=m}

\displaystyle=\frac{2e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{K}-\frac{1}{2}\ln(m^2)-2\int_{0}^{1}dxx\ln(1-x)-1-2\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{x}{1-x}\right].

Accounting for the second integral in Eq. (
4.13
) and defining
\mathbb{E}:=\mathbb{K}-\ln(m^2)
, we obtain:

-\frac{\partial}{\partial\cancel{p}}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\text{1-Loop}}(\cancel{p})\Bigg)\Bigg|_{\cancel{p}=m}=\frac{e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}+1-4\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{x}{1-x}\right].

(4.16)

We thus see that Eqs. (
4.14
) and (
4.16
) are similar, completing the verification of the
WTI
.
\Box

5.  Conclusions

We began by introducing the correspondence between the commonly used regularization scheme
DIM REG
and the recently proposed regularization scheme
DEN REG
. This correspondence was shown to be self-consistent and allowed us to determine the deterministic coefficient functions required for our context. We applied
DEN REG
to the one loop electron self-energy and vertex correction in
QED
. We explicitly verified that the
WTI
is satisfied, demonstrating that gauge symmetry is preserved within this regularization scheme. A particular advantage of
DEN REG
here is that it allows the numerator algebra to be performed directly in four dimensions, significantly simplifying the evaluation of one loop vertex corrections.

Throughout the derivation, the principles of
DEN REG
were maintained, except for the determination of the coefficient functions. These were fixed by establishing a correspondence between
DIM REG
and
DEN REG
, leading to a deterministic prescription for most coefficients. An exception is the coefficient
g_{(3,1)}(\epsilon)
, which does not approach unity in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
. The investigation of such nontrivial coefficient functions is left for future work.

From a physical standpoint, our verification of the
WTI
was carried out under on-shell conditions. An important extension of this analysis would be to test the
WTI
off shell, which could further clarify the scope and robustness of
DEN REG
. Overall, our results support
DEN REG
as a reliable and systematic alternative regularization scheme for symmetry-preserving perturbative calculations in
QFT
, with potential applications to more complex processes and higher-loop orders.

Appendix B

The aim of this appendix is to re-express the numerator in terms of

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=\gamma^{\nu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+\cancel{k})\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}+\cancel{k})\gamma_{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+\cancel{k})\gamma^{\mu}m\gamma_{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}m\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}+\cancel{k})\gamma_{\nu}+m^{2}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}.

(B.1)

We employ the Dirac algebra in four dimensions to compute each term, we have the following:

\displaystyle\gamma^{\nu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+\cancel{k})\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}+\cancel{k})\gamma_{\nu}

\displaystyle=-2(\cancel{p}+\cancel{k})\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+\cancel{k}),

(B.2)

\displaystyle m\gamma^{\nu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+\cancel{k})\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}

\displaystyle=4m(p^{\prime}+k),

\displaystyle m\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}+\cancel{k})\gamma_{\nu}

\displaystyle=4m(p+k),

\displaystyle m^{2}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}

\displaystyle=-2m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}.

Then, Eq. (
B.1
) become

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=-2m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}+4m(p^{\prime}+p+2k)^{\mu}-2(\cancel{p}+\cancel{k})\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}+\cancel{k}).

We re-express this numerator in terms of
\ell:=k+xp+yp^{\prime}
and we neglect the linear terms, we find

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=-2m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}+4m(p^{\prime}+p-2xp-2yp^{\prime}))^{\mu}-2\cancel{\ell}\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{\ell}-2(\cancel{p}-x\cancel{p}-y\cancel{p}^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}(\cancel{p}^{\prime}-x\cancel{p}-y\cancel{p}^{\prime}).

Using
x+y+z=1
and
q=p^{\prime}-p
, the following manipulation are useful:

2xp+2yp^{\prime}=(x+y)(p+p^{\prime})+(x-y)(p-p^{\prime}),

which implies that

\displaystyle p+p^{\prime}-2xp-2yp^{\prime}

\displaystyle=p+p^{\prime}-(x+y)(p+p^{\prime})-(x-y)(p-p^{\prime})

\displaystyle=z(p+p^{\prime})+(x-y)q.

(B.3)

And

\displaystyle p-xp-yp^{\prime}

\displaystyle=zp^{\prime}-(1-x)q

(B.4)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}-xp-yp^{\prime}

\displaystyle=zp+(1-y)q.

Then

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=-2m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}+4mz(p+p^{\prime})^{\mu}+4m(x-y)q^{\mu}-2\cancel{\ell}\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{\ell}-2\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\mu},

(B.5)

where

\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\mu}:=(z\cancel{p}^{\prime}+(x-1)\cancel{q})\gamma^{\mu}(z\cancel{p}+(1-y)\cancel{q}).

We know that the two Dirac spinors
\bar{u}(p^{\prime})
and
u(p)
satisfy the following relations
[
1
,
4
]

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\cancel{p}^{\prime}=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})m\quad\text{and}\quad\cancel{p}u(p)=mu(p).

More generally in
\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Gamma^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)u(p)
, we have:

A\cancel{p}=Am\quad\text{and}\quad\cancel{p}^{\prime}B=mB\quad\text{for some $A$ or $B$}.

(B.6)

Consequently

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=(z\cancel{p}^{\prime}+(x-1)\cancel{q})\gamma^{\mu}(z\cancel{p}+(1-y)\cancel{q})

\displaystyle=(zm+(x-1)\cancel{q})\gamma^{\mu}(zm+(1-y)\cancel{q}))

\displaystyle=z^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}-(1-x)(1-y)\cancel{q}\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{q}+zm([\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}]+x\cancel{q}\gamma^{\mu}-y\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{q}).

(B.7)

We use the following trick:

x\cancel{q}\gamma^{\mu}-y\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{q}=\frac{1}{2}(x-y)\{\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}\}-\frac{1}{2}(x+y)[\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}],

(B.8)

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=z^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}-(1-x)(1-y)\cancel{q}\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{q}+zm\left([\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}]+\frac{1}{2}(x-y)\{\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}\}-\frac{1}{2}(x+y)[\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}]\right)

\displaystyle=z^{2}m^{2}\gamma^{\mu}-(1-x)(1-y)\cancel{q}\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{q}+\frac{1}{2}zm(2-x-y)[\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}]+\frac{1}{2}zm(x-y)\{\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}\}.

(B.9)

Define the following formulas:

[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]\equiv-2i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}\equiv 2\eta^{\mu\nu}.

(B.10)

For these reasons:

\frac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\cancel{q}]=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}\cancel{q}-\cancel{q}\gamma^{\mu})=\frac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]q_{\nu}=-i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu},

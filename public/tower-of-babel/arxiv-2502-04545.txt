Two Absolutely Irreducible Polynomials over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} and Their Applications to a Conjecture by Carlet
Xiang-dong Hou, Shujun Zhao

Abstract
. Two polynomials F k ​ ( X 1 , … , X k ) F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}) and Θ k ​ ( X 1 , … , X k ) \Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}) over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} arose from the study of a conjecture by C. Carlet about the sum-freedom of the multiplicative inverse function of 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} . Both F k F_{k} and Θ k \Theta_{k} are homogeneous and symmetric with deg ⁡ F k = 2 k − 2 \deg F_{k}=2^{k}-2 and deg ⁡ Θ k = 2 k − 1 \deg\Theta_{k}=2^{k-1} . It is known that F k F_{k} is absolutely irreducible for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 . Using the Lang-Weil bound and a curious connection between F k F_{k} and Θ k \Theta_{k} , we show that Θ k \Theta_{k} ( k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 ) is also absolutely irreducible. This conclusion allows us to improve several existing results about Carlet’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is said to be
k
th order sum-free
if
\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\neq 0
for every
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-affine subspace
A
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Sum-free functions were introduced recently by C. Carlet as a natural generalization of
almost perfect nonlinear
(APN) functions; the latter are precisely the 2nd order sum-free functions
[
4
,
5
]
. For applications of APN functions and sum-free functions in cryptography, see
[
3
, Chapter 11]
and
[
4
,
15
]
. Let
f_{\text{\rm inv}}:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be the multiplicative inverse function defined by
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(x)=x^{-1}
for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{*}
and
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(0)=0
. It is a challenging problem to determine the values of
1\leq k\leq n
such that
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Let

(1.1)

\text{\rm SF}_{n}=\{1\leq k\leq n-1:f_{\text{\rm inv}}\ \text{is $k$th order sum-free}\}

and

(1.2)

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\{1,\dots,n-1\}\setminus\text{\rm SF}_{n}.

We know from
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
that

\text{\rm SF}_{n}=\{1,n-1\}\ \text{for even}\ n

and

\text{\rm SF}_{n}\supset\{1,2,n-2,n-1\}\ \text{for odd}\ n\geq 3.

The following conjecture, known as Carlet’s conjecture, is supported by strong evidence, both theoretical and numerical.

Conjecture 1.1

(Carlet
[
5
]
)
.

For odd
n\geq 3
,
\text{\rm SF}_{n}=\{1,2,n-2,n-1\}
.

In the next section, we will review the state of knowledge on Conjecture
1.1
prior to the present paper.

There are two criteria for
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
not to be
k
th order sum-free
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. The two criteria are stated in terms of two polynomials
F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
and
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, respectively:
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is not
k
th order sum-free if and only if there exist
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, such that
F_{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=0
, equivalently, there exist
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, such that
\Theta_{k}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=0
. The polynomials
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}
(defined in Section 3) are constructed quite differently. It is a curious fact that they are connected through the sum-freedom of
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
. In Section 3, we give a full account of the two criteria and the polynomials
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}
.

It is known that for
k\geq 3
,
F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible (
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
), i.e., irreducible over the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. It was also proved in
[
8
]
by an ad hoc method that
\Theta_{4}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})
is absolutely irreducible. In Section 4, we prove that in general,
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible for
k\geq 3
. The proof uses the Lang-Weil bound on the number of zeros of absolutely irreducible polynomials over finite fields and the aforementioned connection between
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}
.

The absolute irreducibility of
\Theta_{k}
, in return, allows us to strengthen several existing results concerning Conjecture
1.1
. Specifically, we prove the following statements in Section 5:

•

When
k\geq 3
and
n\geq 10.8k-15.7
,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free. This improves a result of
[
6
]
which requires
n\geq 10.8k-5
; see
[
6
, Remark 4.4]
.

•

Assume
3\leq k\leq n-3
. If
3\leq k\leq\lceil n/3\rceil+2
or
\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor-2\leq k\leq n-3
, then
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free. The same conclusion can be derived from the results of
[
6
]
when
n
is sufficiently large (
n\geq 38
). Here we removed such a requirement on
n
.

•

Assume
3\leq k\leq n-3
. If
3\leq k\leq 12
, then
k\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
, i.e.,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free. Previous best result in this form was that
3,4\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
; see
[
5
, (update), §5.2.4]
and
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
.

•

Conjecture 1.1 holds for odd
n\leq 27
. It was previously known that the conjecture holds for odd
n\leq 15
(
[
8
]
).

•

Conjecture 1.1 holds when
7\mid n
, with possible exceptions
(n,k)=(49,23)
,
(49,26)
.

[

,

,

.

(2)

]
).

(3)

Carlet’s (original) conjecture holds in each of the following cases:

(
[

]
).

•

or

(
[

,
8
]
).

•

The smallest prime divisor

(
[
8
]
).

(4)

For odd

,

(
[
16
]
).

(5)

If

are such that

, then

(

]
).

(6)

If

,

]

(7)

If

). A more precise form of this result is also given in

]

(8)

, then

(
[

,
8
]
).

(9)

Assume

and

. If

[

]
).

Since Carlet’s original conjecture has been confirmed for even
n

1.1
. In Section 5, the above results (3), (7) – (9) will be strengthened.

3.
Two Criteria

Throughout the paper, affine subspaces and subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
are meant to be
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-affine subspaces and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. It is known that for all affine subspaces
A
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
not containing
0
,
\sum_{x\in A}1/x\neq 0
(
[
4
]
). Therefore,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free if and only if
\sum_{0\neq u\in A}1/u=0
for some
k
-dimensional subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. We call a subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
a
zero-sum subspace
if

\sum_{0\neq u\in E}\frac{1}{u}=0.

There are two criteria for a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
to be sum-free. Both criteria were originally due to Carlet
[
4
,
5
]
and were reformulated later in
[
6
,
8
]
. The two criteria are stated in terms of two polynomials
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}
, respectively. We first recall the constructions of
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}
.

3.1.
Constructions of
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}

Let

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\left|\begin{matrix}X_{1}&\cdots&X_{k}\cr X_{1}^{2}&\cdots&X_{k}^{2}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{2^{k-1}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{2^{k-1}}\end{matrix}\right|\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]

and

\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\left|\begin{matrix}X_{1}&\cdots&X_{k}\cr X_{1}^{2^{2}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{2^{2}}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{2^{k}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{2^{k}}\end{matrix}\right|\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}].

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is the
Moore determinant
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
(
[
14
]
), and it is well known that
u_{1}\dots,u_{k}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
(the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) are linearly independent if and only
\Delta(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\neq 0
. It is also known that
\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
divides
\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
(
[
6
, §4]
). Define

F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\frac{\Delta_{1}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}.

A partition of a positive integer is called a
2
-adic partition
if all its parts are powers of
2
. Let
\Lambda_{k}
denote the set of all
2
-adic partitions of
2^{k-1}
with at most
k
parts. Define

\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}m_{\lambda}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}),

where
m_{\lambda}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is the monomial symmetric polynomial associated to the partition
\lambda
(
[
12
, I.2]
). For example, when
k=4
,

\Lambda_{4}=\{(2^{3}),(2^{2},2^{2})(2^{2},2,2),(2^{2},2,1,1),(2,2,2,2)\}

and

\Theta_{4}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})=m_{(2^{3})}+m_{(2^{2},2^{2})}+m_{(2^{2},2,2)}+m_{(2^{2},2,1,1)}+m_{(2,2,2,2)},

where

m_{(2^{3})}=X_{1}^{2^{3}}+X_{2}^{2^{3}}+X_{3}^{2^{3}}+X_{4}^{2^{3}},

m_{(2^{2},2^{2})}=X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2^{2}}+X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2^{2}},

\displaystyle m_{(2^{2},2,2)}=\,

\displaystyle X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}.

\displaystyle m_{(2^{2},2,1,1)}=\,

\displaystyle X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}X_{4}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}X_{3}X_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}X_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}X_{3}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}},

m_{(2,2,2,2)}=X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2}.

The polynomial
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
has integer coefficients and is treated as a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

To summarize, both
F_{k}(X_{1}\dots,X_{k})
and
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
are homogeneous and symmetric with
\deg F_{k}=2^{k}-2
and
\deg\Theta_{k}=2^{k-1}
.

3.2.
The Criteria

Let
E
be a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, and define
L_{E}(X)=\prod_{u\in E}(X-u)\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}[X]
. Then
L_{E}:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linear map with
\ker L_{E}=E
(
[
11
, Theorem 3.52]
), hence
L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}})
is an
(n-k)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Denote
L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}})
by
E^{\prime}
. It is known that
E^{\prime\prime}=E
, where
E^{\prime\prime}=L_{E^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}})
; see
[
1
, Theorem 11.35]
. We also define
E^{\bot}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}:\text{Tr}_{2^{n}/2}(xy)=0\ \text{for all}\ y\in E\}
. Then the two criteria can be stated as follows:

Theorem 3.1

(Criterion 1
[
4
]
,
[
6
, §4]
)
.

A
k
-dimensional subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is a zero-sum subspace if and only if

F_{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=0,

where
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
is any basis of
E
.

Theorem 3.2

(Criterion 2
[
5
, §6.7]
,
[
8
, Theorem 3.11]
)
.

A
k
-dimensional subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is a zero-sum subspace if and only if

\Theta_{k}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=0,

where
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
is any basis of
(E^{\prime})^{\bot}
.

3.3.
Observations and Generalizations

In general, for an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, define
L_{E}(X)=\prod_{u\in E}(X-u)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
. Then
L_{E}(X)
is a
q
-polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
(
[
11
, Theorem 3.52]
). Therefore,
L_{E}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map with
\ker L_{E}=E
, and
E^{\prime}:=L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
is an
(n-k)
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. It was proved in
[
1
, Theorem 11.35]
that
E^{\prime\prime}=E
, i.e.,
L_{E^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})=E
. We wish to point out that this fact can be seen from a ring-theoretic point of view. For this purpose, we include a proof which is the same as the one in
[
1
]
but is stated in the language of ring theory.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R
be a ring without zero divisors. If
a,b\in R
are such that
ab
is in the center of
R
, then
ab=ba
.

Proof.

We may assume that
b\neq 0
. We have
abb=bab
, whence
ab=ba
.
∎

Proof that
E^{\prime\prime}=E
, where
E
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(q,n)
denote the ring of
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
whose addition is the ordinary and whose multiplication is composition.
\mathcal{L}(q,n)
is isomorphic to the shew polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X;\sigma]
, where
\sigma
is the Frobenius of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; see
[
10
, Theorem 2.34]
. The center of
\mathcal{L}(q,n)
consists of
q^{n}
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
(L_{E^{\prime}}\circ L_{E})(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
(by definition) and
L_{E^{\prime}}\circ L_{E}
is monic of degree
q^{n}
, we have
L_{E^{\prime}}\circ L_{E}=X^{q^{n}}-X
, which belongs to the center of
\mathcal{L}(q,n)
. By Lemma
3.5
,
L_{E^{\prime}}\circ L_{E}=L_{E}\circ L_{E^{\prime}}
. Now,
L_{E^{\prime\prime}}\circ L_{E^{\prime}}=X^{q^{n}}-X=L_{E}\circ L_{E^{\prime}}
, whence
L_{E^{\prime\prime}}=L_{E}
, i.e.,
E^{\prime\prime}=E
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
denote the set of all
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Define

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{k}:&\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{V}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})\cr&E&\longmapsto&L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}),\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\beta_{k}:&\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{V}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})\cr&E&\longmapsto&E^{\bot},\end{array}

where
E^{\bot}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}:\text{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(xy)=0\ \text{for all}\ y\in E\}
. Then both
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are bijections with
\alpha_{n-k}\circ\alpha_{k}=\text{id}
and
\beta_{n-k}\circ\beta_{k}=\text{id}
. Therefore,
\gamma_{k}:=\beta_{n-k}\circ\alpha_{k}:\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})\to\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
is a bijection with
\gamma_{k}^{-1}=\alpha_{n-k}\circ\beta_{k}
.

Every
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
can be uniquely represented by a
q
-polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
modulo
X^{q^{n}}-X
. Given a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, there are
(q^{n}-q^{0})(q^{n}-q^{1})\cdots(q^{n}-q^{n-k-1})

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\ker f=E
. Among these
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps, treated as
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
,
L_{E}(X)
is the unique monic one with degree
\leq|E|
.
L_{E}(X)
is canonical in the sense that its construction does not involve a basis of
E
or a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

On the other hand, it is known that given a
q
-polynomial

of degree
\leq q^{k}
over

,

if and only if

and

satisfy the equation

(3.1)

C^{(q^{0})}C^{(q^{1})}\cdots C^{(q^{n-1})}=I_{k},

where

is the result after applying
(\ )^{q^{i}}
to the entries of

, and

is the

identity matrix; see
[
7
,
13
]
. Therefore, the elements of
\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
are in one-to-one correspondence with the points

in the projective space

with

whose homogeneous coordinates

satisfy (

). It is not difficult to see that under this correspondence, the map

maps

to

.

Let’s consider a more general situation. Let
K/F
be a finite cyclic extension (a Galois extension with a finite cyclic Galois group) with

. Then
\sigma^{0},\dots,\sigma^{n-1}
, where

, is a
K
-basis of

.

Proposition 3.6
.

In the above notation, for each
k
-dimensional

-subspace

of

with

.

Lemma 3.7

(

)
.

In the above notation, for

with

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
K/F
be a finite dimensional Galois extension with
\text{\rm Aut}(K/F)=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\}
,
n=[K:F]
. Then
k

are linearly independent over

if and only the

(3.2)

B=\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{1}(b_{1})&\cdots&\sigma_{1}(b_{k})\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sigma_{n}(b_{1})&\cdots&\sigma_{n}(b_{k})\end{matrix}\right]

has rank
k
.

Proof.

When

is finite, this fact appeared as Lemma 2.30 in

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume to the contrary that

for some

. Then
B(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})^{T}=0
, which is a contradiction.

(
\Rightarrow
) First assume
k=n
. Then the
(i,j)
-entry of

is

\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sigma_{l}(b_{i}b_{j})=\text{Tr}_{K/F}(b_{i}b_{j}).

Since

is a nondegenerate

-bilinear form on

, we have

, and hence

is nonsingular.

Now assume

. Extend

to an
F
-basis

of

. Then the columns of

\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{1}(b_{1})&\cdots&\sigma_{1}(b_{n})\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sigma_{n}(b_{1})&\cdots&\sigma_{n}(b_{n})\end{matrix}\right]

are linearly independent over

, whence

.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Let

be a basis of
E
over

,

. Clearly,

. Then it follows from Lemma

that

and

has the desired property.

For the uniqueness of

, assume that

and

and Lemma

forces

for all

∎

We denote the unique
F

by

L_{E}=\left|\begin{matrix}\sigma^{0}(u_{1})&\cdots&\sigma^{0}(u_{k})\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sigma^{k-1}(u_{1})&\cdots&\sigma^{k-1}(u_{k})\end{matrix}\right|^{-1}\left|\begin{matrix}\sigma^{0}(u_{1})&\cdots&\sigma^{0}(u_{k})&\sigma^{0}\cr\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\cr\sigma^{k}(u_{1})&\cdots&\sigma^{k}(u_{k})&\sigma^{k}\end{matrix}\right|,

where

is a basis of

over

, and

does not depend on the choice of the basis.

Let
E^{\prime}=L_{E}(K)
, which is an
F
-subspace of

of dimension

. We also have

, and the proof is almost identical to the case of finite fields. Consider the skew polynomial ring
K[X;\sigma]
; its center is

. Every

-linear map from

to

is of the form

, where

is unique modulo

. Write

, where

is monic of degree

. By definition,

, where

. Since

, which is in the center of

, and hence

.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{k}(K)

For

and

4.
Absolute Irreducibility of
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}

It has been proved that the polynomial
F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible for
k\geq 3
(
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
). In this section, we show that the polynomial
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is also absolutely irreducible for
k\geq 3
. When
k=4
, the absolute irreducibility of
\Theta_{4}
was established in
[
8
]
by a rather technical method which does not seem to apply to the general situation. Our approach here is indirect and is based on the relation between
F_{k}
and
\Theta_{k}
described in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

For a field
\mathbb{F}
and a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
, we define

V_{\mathbb{F}^{k}}(f)=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{F}^{k}:f(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=0\}.

Recall that a zero-sum subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is a subspace
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
such that
\sum_{0\neq u\in E}1/u=0
. Let
Z_{k}
denote the number of
k
-dimensional zero-sum subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. By Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(F_{k})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}))|=Z_{k}|\text{GL}(k,\mathbb{F}_{2})|

\displaystyle=|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Theta_{k})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}))|.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
f(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
has
l
distinct absolutely irreducible factors, and assume that all these irreducible factors belong to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
. Further assume that
\gcd(f,\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}))=1
. Then

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}))|=lq^{ms(k-1)}+O(q^{ms(k-3/2)})\quad\text{as}\ s\to\infty.

Proof.

Write
f=f_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots f_{l}^{e_{l}}
, where
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
are distinct and absolutely irreducible and
e_{i}>0
,
1\leq i\leq l
. We may assume that
e_{i}=1
for all
i
since the multiplicities
e_{i}
do not affect the number of zeros of
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}
. By the Lang-Weil bound, as stated in
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
,

(4.2)

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{i})|=q^{ms(k-1)}+O(q^{ms(k-3/2)})\quad\text{as}\ s\to\infty.

By
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
, for
1\leq i<j\leq l
,

(4.3)

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{i})\cap V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{j})|=O(q^{ms(k-2)})\quad\text{as}\ s\to\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{1})\cup\cdots\cup V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{l})|=|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)|

\displaystyle\geq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{i})|-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{i})\cap V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f_{j})|.

Thus it follows from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that

(4.4)

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)|=lq^{ms(k-1)}+O(q^{ms(k-3/2)}).

Also by
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
,

(4.5)

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)\cap V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(\Delta)|=O(q^{ms(k-2)}).

It follows from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(\Delta)|\,

\displaystyle=|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)|-|V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(f)\cap V_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{ms}}^{k}}(\Delta)|

\displaystyle=lq^{ms(k-1)}+O(q^{ms(k-3/2)}).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

When
k\geq 2
,
\gcd(\Theta_{k},\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}))=1
.

Proof.

Recall that

\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}m_{\lambda}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}),

where
\Lambda_{k}
is the set of all
2
-adic partitions of
2^{k-1}
with at most
k
parts. By
[
11
, Lemma 3.51]
,

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\prod_{0\neq(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}(a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}).

Assume to the contrary that
\gcd(\Theta_{k},\Delta)\neq 1
. Then there exists
0\neq(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
such that
(a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})\mid\Theta_{k}
.

Case 1.
Assume that only one of
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
is nonzero, say
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=(0,\cdots,0,1)
. Then the value of
a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}
at
(1,0,\dots,0)
is
0
, whence
\Theta_{k}(1,0,\dots,0)=0
. However,

m_{\lambda}(1,0,\dots,0)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ \lambda=(2^{k-1}),\cr 0&\text{if $\lambda$ has more than one part}.\end{cases}

Hence
\Theta_{k}(1,0,\dots,0)=1
, which is a contradiction.

Case 2.
Assume that at least two of
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
are nonzero, say
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=(1,1,*,\dots,*)
. Then the value of
a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}
at
(1,1,0,\dots,0)
is
0
, whence
\Theta_{k}(1,1,0,\dots,0)=0
. Note that

m_{\lambda}(1,1,0,\dots,0)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ \lambda=(2^{k-1}),\cr 1&\text{if}\ \lambda=(2^{k-2},2^{k-2}),\cr 0&\text{if $\lambda$ has at more that 2 parts}.\end{cases}

Therefore,
\Theta_{k}(1,1,0,\dots,0)=1
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

When
k\geq 3
,
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible.

Proof.

Let
l
denote the number of distinct absolutely irreducible factors of
\Theta_{k}
, and assume that these factors all belong to
\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
. By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
,

(4.6)

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{ms}}^{k}}(\Theta_{k})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{ms}}^{k}}(\Delta)|=l\,2^{ms(k-1)}+O(2^{ms(k-3/2)})\quad\text{as}\ s\to\infty.

Since
F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is absolutely irreducible, we also have

(4.7)

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{ms}}^{k}}(F_{k})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{ms}}^{k}}(\Delta)|=2^{ms(k-1)}+O(2^{ms(k-3/2)})\quad\text{as}\ s\to\infty.

Combining (
4.1
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) gives

2^{ms(k-1)}+O(2^{ms(k-3/2)})=l\,2^{ms(k-1)}+O(2^{ms(k-3/2)})\quad\text{as}\ s\to\infty.

Therefore
l=1
.
∎

5.
New Results on Carlet’s Conjecture

In
[
6
]
, the Lang-Weil bound was applied to the absolutely irreducible polynomial
F_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
(
k\geq 3
) to show that
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free when
n\geq 10.8-5
; see
[
6
, Theorem 4.2 and Remark 4.4]
. Now that we know that
\Theta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
(
k\geq 3
) is also absolutely irreducible, we may apply the same method to
\Theta_{k}
. Since
\deg\Theta_{k}
(
=2^{k-1}
) is less than
\deg F_{k}
(
=2^{k}-2
), the Lang-Weil bound produces better estimate on
\Theta_{k}
than
F_{k}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
k\geq 3
and

(5.1)

n\geq\frac{\log_{2}\bigl(1+\sqrt{21}\,\bigr)}{3}(13k-19),

then
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
k
th order sum-free. Condition (
5.1
) is satisfied when

(5.2)

n\geq 10.8k-15.7.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
. We may assume
k\geq 4
since
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is not
3
rd order sum-free for
n\geq 6
(
[
5
, Corollary 8]
). By Theorem 3.2, it suffices to show that
|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Theta_{k})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Delta)|>0
.

Let
q=2^{n}
. Since
\Theta_{k}
is absolutely irreducible, by
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
,

\displaystyle|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Theta_{k})|\,

\displaystyle\geq q^{k-1}-(2^{k-1})(2^{k-1}-2)q^{k-3/2}-5(2^{k-1})^{13/3}q^{k-2}

\displaystyle>q^{k-1}-2^{2(k-1)}q^{k-3/2}-5\cdot 2^{13(k-1)/3}q^{k-2}.

On the other hand, by
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
,

|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Theta_{k})\cap V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Delta)|\leq(2^{k}-1)^{2}q^{k-2}<2^{2k}q^{k-2}.

Hence

\displaystyle|V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Theta_{k})\setminus V_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{k}}(\Delta)|\,

\displaystyle>q^{k-1}-2^{2(k-1)}q^{k-3/2}-(5\cdot 2^{13(k-1)/3}+2^{2k})q^{k-2}

\displaystyle=q^{k-2}\bigl(y^{2}-2^{2(k-1)}y-(5\cdot 2^{13(k-1)/3}+2^{2k})\bigr),

where

. Let

denote the larger root of the quadratic

. We have

\displaystyle y_{0}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(2^{2(k-1)}+\sqrt{2^{4(k-1)}+20\cdot 2^{13(k-1)/3}+2^{2k+2}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(2^{2(k-1)}+\sqrt{21\cdot 2^{13(k-1)/3}}\bigr)\kern 50.00008pt\text{(since $k\geq 4$)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1+\sqrt{21}\,\bigr)2^{13(k-1)/6}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

i.e.,

which is given. This completes the proof.
∎

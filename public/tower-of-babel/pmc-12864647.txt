The clinical efficacy evaluation of preoperative NHT treatment in laparoscopic radical prostatectomy: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Tian Guolin, Tao Bo, Li Jiaxin, Ma Yanpin, Li Yajie, Jia Yaodong, Nie Lihong, Zhao Ruining
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to systematically evaluate the impact of neoadjuvant therapy (NHT) on the efficacy and safety of laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP).MethodsWe conducted a search of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), and Wanfang Database for cohort studies on neoadjuvant therapy combined with LRP for prostate cancer versus standard treatment for prostate cancer, up to February 2025. Two researchers independently screened the literature, extracted data, and assessed the risk of bias in the included studies. Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4.1 software.ResultsA total of 14 studies were included. The results of the meta-analysis showed that compared to standard LRP, NHT combined with LRP can reduce the rate of positive surgical margins, biochemical recurrence rate, blood loss, and duration of catheterization. Subgroup analysis indicated that preoperative NHT combined with RALP demonstrated significant improvements in surgical time, blood loss, and positive surgical margins compared to traditional LRP.ConclusionNeoadjuvant therapy can improve tumor control outcomes for LRP, but may increase the risk of postoperative complications. NHT combined with RALP can be considered as a preferred treatment option in the surgical management of prostate cancer (PCa).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04364-4.Keywords: Neoadjuvant therapy, Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy, Robot-assisted surgery, Prostate cancer

Introduction

Prostate cancer is a common malignant tumor of the male urogenital system, with an increasing incidence rate each year. It currently ranks second in global incidence among men and fifth in mortality rate [1]. It poses a significant threat to the health and lives of men worldwide. Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) and robot-assisted laparoscopic prostatectomy (RALP) play important roles in the treatment of localized prostate cancer; however, issues such as positive surgical margins (PSM) and biochemical recurrence (BCR) cannot be overlooked. Neoadjuvant therapy (including endocrine therapy, chemotherapy, etc.) refers to drug treatment conducted prior to surgery, aimed at reducing tumor volume, lower tumor staging, improving surgical resection rates, and potentially decreasing the risk of postoperative recurrence [2]. In recent years, clinical studies on neoadjuvant therapy combined with LRP for prostate cancer have gradually increased, but there remains controversy regarding its efficacy and safety [3]. Therefore, this study employs a meta-analysis to systematically evaluate the impact of neoadjuvant therapy on the efficacy and safety of LRP, with the aim of providing evidence-based medical support for clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Literature search strategy

A computer search was conducted in PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), and Wanfang Database to collect randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on neoadjuvant therapy combined with LRP for prostate cancer, up to February 2025. The search terms included: ((robot OR robotic OR robot-assisted OR robotic-assisted OR computer-assisted surgery OR da Vinci OR Laparoscope OR laparoscopic) AND (radical prostatectomy)) AND (neoadjuvant therapy OR ADT OR androgen deprivation therapy), etc.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Study type: Cohort study.

Study subjects: Patients diagnosed with prostate cancer through pathology;

Study subjects: Patients diagnosed with prostate cancer through pathology;

Intervention: The experimental group received neoadjuvant therapy combined with LRP, while the control group received standard LRP;

Intervention: The experimental group received neoadjuvant therapy combined with LRP, while the control group received standard LRP;

Outcome measures: At least one of the following: postoperative positive surgical margin rate, biochemical recurrence rate, lymph node metastasis rate, overall survival rate, progression-free survival rate, incidence of urinary incontinence, and incidence of erectile dysfunction.

Outcome measures: At least one of the following: postoperative positive surgical margin rate, biochemical recurrence rate, lymph node metastasis rate, overall survival rate, progression-free survival rate, incidence of urinary incontinence, and incidence of erectile dysfunction.

Exclusion criteria

Literature with incomplete data, too few cases, or without access to the full text.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two researchers independently screened the literature, extracted data, and assessed the risk of bias in the included studies. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion or by consulting a third party. The content of data extraction included: (1) Baseline data: first author, publication year, country, study design, and sample size; (2) Participant characteristics: age, body mass index, and preoperative prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels; (3) Perioperative outcomes: surgical time, blood loss, and postoperative complications; (4) Oncological outcomes: positive surgical margin rate (PSM) and biochemical recurrence rate (BCR). If the study reported median values and interquartile ranges, the mean and standard deviation (SD) were calculated using the methods described by Luo et al. [4] and Wan et al. [5], respectively. The risk of bias for the included studies was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4.1 software. Categorical data were presented as relative risk (RR) with the corresponding percentage confidence interval (CI), while continuous data were expressed as mean difference (MD) with 95% CI. The heterogeneity among studies was assessed using the χ² test and I² statistics. If P ≥ 0.1 and I² ≤ 50%, a fixed-effects model was used for the meta-analysis; if P < 0.1 or I² > 50%, a random-effects model was employed. A funnel plot was utilized to assess publication bias. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Meta-analysis results before and after NHT treatment

Postoperative positive surgical margin rate

Nine studies reported the rate of positive surgical margins, with a total of 2,614 patients included. The meta-analysis results showed that, compared to standard LRP, NHT combined with LRP significantly reduced the rate of positive surgical margins [RD= -0.13, 95% CI (-0.25, -0.02), P = 0.03], and the difference was statistically significant (see Fig. 1).

Forest plot of postoperative positive surgical margin rates for patients undergoing NHT combined with LRP versus LRP alone

Biochemical recurrence rate

Four studies reported the biochemical recurrence rate, with a total of 1,053 patients included. The meta-analysis results showed that, compared to standard LRP, NHT combined with LRP significantly reduced the biochemical recurrence rate [RR = 0.76, 95% CI (0.61, 0.95), P = 0.02], and the difference was statistically significant (see Fig. 2).

Forest plot of biochemical recurrence rates for patients undergoing NHT combined with LRP versus LRP alone

Blood loss

Eight studies reported blood loss, with a total of 2,185 patients included. The meta-analysis results showed that, compared to standard LRP, NHT combined with LRP significantly reduced blood loss [MD= -49.86, 95% CI (-98.36, -1.36), P = 0.04], and the difference was statistically significant (see Figs. 3, 4).

Discussion

As a common type of cancer in urological surgery, prostate cancer is characterized by rapid onset, fast progression, and severity [6]. In recent years, with changes in lifestyle, an aging population, and improvements in diagnostic levels, the incidence of prostate cancer has shown a significant upward trend [7], severely impacting patients’ lives and work. Timely early diagnosis and effective treatment measures are essential in the clinical setting. Radical prostatectomy is an effective surgical approach for treating prostate cancer. Not only does it aim to control tumor progression, but it also seeks to ensure a good postoperative quality of life, achieving the “triple crown” of tumor control, excellent urinary control, and normal sexual function [8]. It remains the gold standard for treating early prostate cancer [9]. With advancements in basic research on prostate cancer, surgical equipment, and a deeper understanding of prostate diseases and local anatomy, there are multiple techniques available for radical prostate cancer surgery [10]. Therefore, a comprehensive assessment of the patient’s condition, including preoperative imaging and clinical staging, is crucial for guiding the surgeon in the selection of surgical procedures related to radical prostatectomy, impacting the postoperative clinical outcomes significantly. Neoadjuvant therapy (including endocrine therapy, chemotherapy, etc.) refers to drug treatment administered before surgery, aimed at reducing tumor volume, lowering tumor staging, improving surgical resection rates, and potentially decreasing the risk of postoperative recurrence. Increasingly, studies demonstrate that active neoadjuvant treatment after a diagnosis of prostate cancer has a positive impact on achieving tumor control, excellent urinary control, and normal sexual function post-surgery.

This meta-analysis systematically evaluated the impact of neoadjuvant therapy (NHT) combined with laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) compared to LRP alone on oncological outcomes and safety. The results indicate that the combination of NHT and LRP is associated with a 13% reduction in the rate of positive surgical margins (PSM) (RD=-0.13; 95% CI: -0.25, -0.02). This suggests that preoperative cytoreduction through NHT can enhance surgical accuracy, potentially due to reduced tumor volume and improved visualization of anatomical planes. Coupled with the results of a meta-analysis by Rydzewska et al. [11], which showed that NHT reduced PSM by 15% in high-risk prostate cancer patients undergoing radical prostatectomy, this may be related to the shrinkage of prostate tumor volume and clearer surgical margins following NHT. NHT effectively reduces tumor volume and enhances visualization of anatomical planes.Besides, for every included RCT the baseline mean PSA, Gleason score distribution and clinical stage were statistically similar between the NHT and control arms (P ≥ 0.05 in all studies). We caution that residual selection bias in observational cohorts could still compromise validity and is an inherent limitation. All included studies have been peer-reviewed and published, and the baseline characteristics (including PSA, Gleason score, and clinical stage) were confirmed to be comparable between groups. Besides, surgeon and hospital volumes were associated with lower postoperative complications, lower positive margin rates, and a lower need for adjuvant and salvage therapies after surgery [12].

Additionally, the combination of NHT and LRP was associated with a 24% reduction in biochemical recurrence (BCR) risk (RR = 0.76; 95% CI: 0.61, 0.95). The lower BCR rate observed in this study corroborates the findings of Vale CL et al. [13], who reported that neoadjuvant androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) combined with surgery reduced 5-year BCR by 18% compared to surgery alone. These similarities underscore the consistency of NHT in improving oncological outcomes in prostate cancer.Moreover, NHT reduced intraoperative blood loss (MD= -49.86 mL; 95% CI: -98.36, -1.36) and shortened the duration of catheterization (MD= -1.74 days; 95% CI: -2.07, -1.40). This aligns with the findings of Williams S et al. [14], which noted similar perioperative benefits of NHT, suggesting improved efficiency in the perioperative period following preoperative NHT treatment. Conversely, regarding postoperative complications, NHT increased the incidence of complications (OR = 1.88; 95% CI: 1.22, 2.91), consistent with concerns raised by Montironi et al. [15], who identified prolonged hormone treatment as a risk factor for urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction. Furthermore, the study by Nguyen et al. [16] posited that prolonged ADT following NHT is also associated with thromboembolic events and cardiovascular toxicity. While not explicitly analyzed in this study, these factors may contribute to the reported safety profile, highlighting the trade-off between oncological efficacy and safety.Regarding surgical time, no significant difference was observed in surgical duration associated with preoperative NHT (MD= -4.61 min; 95% CI: -14.85, 5.62), reflecting a comparable complexity of surgery between the two groups. This may be related to multiple factors, including variations in tumor volume and the timing of NHT treatment among different patients.Therefore, we believe that the oncological benefits of NHT may stem from its cytoreductive effects. Preoperative ADT inhibits androgen signaling, induces apoptosis in hormone-sensitive tumor cells, and reduces microvascular density [17]. This biological response facilitates easier dissection and margin clearance, as evidenced by the reduction in positive surgical margins (PSM). Additionally, NHT may mitigate micrometastatic disease and delay biochemical recurrence [18]. However, the inflammation and fibrosis caused by NHT may impair tissue healing and increase the risk of complications such as anastomotic stricture or infection [19]. These findings underscore the importance of individualized risk stratification when considering NHT. For high-risk patients, where PSM and biochemical recurrence (BCR) pose a significant threat to survival, NHT may offer meaningful advantages, albeit at the cost of an increased risk of complications. Conversely, in the intermediate-risk patient population, the balance may lean towards omitting NHT unless tumor characteristics indicate a high likelihood of margin involvement. Surgeons must also weigh factors such as patient comorbidities, life expectancy, and preferences. For example, younger patients prioritizing long-term cancer control may accept a higher risk of complications, while elderly patients with cardiovascular vulnerabilities may prioritize safety. And some studies have also shown that the age of patients candidates for urological surgery is progressively increasing, and a multifactorial preoperative evaluation is needed. The BI(Barthel index) is a valid tool to describe a patient’s functional reserve, with important implications in the surgical patient’s pathway. BI use should be encouraged in order to identify subjects at higher risk of adverse outcomes, promoting tailored management and optimizing care [20].

Moreover, our subgroup analysis suggests that under the premise of NHT treatment, RALP is significantly associated with shorter surgical time, reduced intraoperative blood loss, and lower rates of PSM compared to LRP. This may be related to RALP’s unique enhanced visualization and precision capabilities. Surgeons can adjust the camera positioning to suit their needs, and the robotic binocular imaging system can magnify the field of view by 10 to 15 times, allowing for clearer identification of nerves, blood vessels, and other tissues in the surgical area while avoiding damage to delicate structures. This minimizes intraoperative blood loss, significantly reflecting the observed benefits of NHT. Therefore, under the premise of NHT treatment, we believe that RALP should be the preferred surgical option for the future management of prostate cancer (PCa).

However, this analysis also has several limitations. First, the included studies are primarily observational, which may introduce selection bias and confounding factors, and randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in this area remain scarce, limiting causal inference. Second, the heterogeneity of NHT protocols (e.g., duration of ADT, use of chemotherapy) and surgical methods (e.g., laparoscopic versus robotic) may impact the results. Third, many of the studies have relatively short follow-up periods, limiting the assessment of long-term survival or late complications. Fourth, excluding non-English studies may introduce language bias. Therefore, we believe that prospective RCTs with standardized NHT protocols and extended follow-up periods are crucial to validate these findings. Subgroup analyses stratified by tumor risk category, duration of NHT, and surgical technique (e.g., RALP versus LRP) could enhance clinical guidelines. Furthermore, exploring the integration of novel agents such as PARP inhibitors or immunotherapy into neoadjuvant protocols could be worthwhile, as these agents may enhance cytoreduction while minimizing toxicity [21]. Research driven by biomarkers to identify patients most likely to benefit from NHT could further optimize the precision of treatment. Although NHT increases drug-acquisition costs, some cost-utility analyses found that the decrement in positive-surgical margins translated into lower adjuvant-radiotherapy rates and may offset the upfront expense within 3–5 years [22]. We underline that formal cost-effectiveness data outside are lacking and recommend future studies.

Conclusion

NHT improves the oncological efficacy of LRP by reducing positive surgical margins (PSM) and biochemical recurrence (BCR), although at the cost of increased complications.

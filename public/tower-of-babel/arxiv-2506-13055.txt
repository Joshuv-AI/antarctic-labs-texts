Jiangtong Li ∗,1 , Yiyun Zhu ∗,1 , Dawei Cheng 1 , Zhijun Ding 1 , Changjun Jiang 1

Abstract
. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have rapidly evolved with the growth of Large Language Models (LLMs) and are now applied in various fields. In finance, the integration of diverse modalities such as text, charts, and tables is crucial for accurate and efficient decision-making. Therefore, an effective evaluation system that incorporates these data types is essential for advancing financial application. In this paper, we introduce CFBenchmark-MM, a Chinese multimodal financial benchmark with over 9,000 image-question pairs featuring tables, histogram charts, line charts, pie charts, and structural diagrams. Additionally, we develop a staged evaluation system to assess MLLMs in handling multimodal information by providing different visual content step by step. Despite MLLMs having inherent financial knowledge, experimental results still show limited efficiency and robustness in handling multimodal financial context. Further analysis on incorrect responses reveals the misinterpretation of visual content and the misunderstanding of financial concepts are the primary issues. Our research validates the significant, yet underexploited, potential of MLLMs in financial analysis, highlighting the need for further development and domain-specific optimization to encourage the enhanced use in financial domain.

Two examples from CFBenchmark-MM.

In this paper, we introduce CFBenchmark-MM, which bridges gaps in existing benchmarks, providing a detailed and realistic evaluation for MLLMs in complex financial context with diverse data types.
Specifically, CFBenchmark-MM includes five core tasks,
i.e.
, arithmetic reasoning, statistical reasoning, structural reasoning, financial explanation, and financial knowledg, with 9,356 image-question pairs.
To construct CFBenchmark-MM, we develop a three-step annotation pipeline: 1) we collect charts and their analyses from financial research reports; 2) we exploit GPT-4 to generate questions and answers based on the text analyses; 3) we manually verify whether the questions can be answered using the charts alone.
In Figure
1
, we present two cases from CFBenchmark-MM.

To assess the capability and efficiency of MLLMs in handling multimodal financial information, we develop a staged evaluation system including multiple settings.
Initially, MLLMs answer questions without visual content to evaluate their basic memory. Progressively, they respond based on a) textual descriptions of charts, b) charts, and c) both charts and their textual descriptions.
This staged approach helps measure how MLLMs is enhanced with multimodal financial information in answering questions.
As for the evaluation metrics, we employ a multiple-choice format, measuring accuracy, for objective questions in arithmetic, statistical, and structural reasoning.
And we compare the generated answers to reference answers, using GPT-4 as a scorer, for subjective financial explanation and knowledge questions.
Scores reflect the proportion of key information matched.

Our experiments reveal that most MLLMs have a fundamental memory of financial knowledge, allowing them to answer questions such as "What is the increase rate of Chinese GDP over 2022?" or "Which stock grew the second largest in 2021?"
Including multimodal information improves response accuracy, yet overall performance remains moderate.
Even the advanced GPT-4V only achieves a 52% accuracy and 38% scores on objective and subjective questions, highlighting the need for further enhancements in financial MLLMs.
Additionally, our analysis of incorrect responses pinpoints that misinterpretation of visual content and misunderstanding of financial concepts are the primary issues.
These findings emphasize the importance of MLLMs to analyze multimodal content in financial contexts.
Our contributions can be summarized as:

•

We introduce CFBenchmark-MM, a comprehensive benchmark with over 9,000 image-question pairs, focusing on evaluate MLLMs in real multimodal financial context.

•

We establish an elaborated annotation process using real multimodal financial data, which includes automated tagging and manual verification to ensure dataset quality.

•

We develop a staged evaluation system with multiple settings to assess the financial abilities of MLLMs and their use of multimodal information step by step, providing insights into existing challenges and guiding future enhancements.

3.
Methodology

In order to construct a multimodal evaluation benchmark that aligns with the practical requirements of financial application, we engage with professionals from the different financial domain, including consultants, bankers, investment bankers, and securities experts.
Through extensive discussions with these industry specialists, we design and analyze our CFBenchmark-MM from two key dimensions: the types of questions and the types of multimodal data.

In terms of the question type, we classify the questions in our benchmark into five categories, which includes three categories involving objective evaluations,
i.e.
,
Arithmetic Reasoning
,
Statistical Reasoning
, and
Structural Reasoning
, and two categories involving subjective evaluations,
i.e.
,
Financial Explanation
and
Financial Knowledge
.
Regarding the multimodal data, we further category them into various types such as tables, histogram charts, line charts, pie charts, composite charts, and structural diagrams (such as equity structure charts and development path charts).
This classification is carefully crafted to ensure that our benchmark comprehensively covers the diverse needs and challenges encountered in multimodal financial applications.

Here is a precise definition of the five question types:

(1)

Arithmetic Reasoning
: This category evaluates the MLLMs in basic arithmetic operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, as well as the understanding of numerical properties and relationships within financial charts.

(2)

Statistical Reasoning
: This category involves to interpret and analyze statistical data in financial domain. It focuses on statistical measures such as mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, and correlation coefficients in financial charts.

(3)

Structural Reasoning
: This category pertains to interpret and analyze structural diagrams and flowcharts in financial domain. This includes assessing diagrams that illustrate complex relationships such as shareholding structures or company development pathways.

(4)

Financial Explanation
: This category entails to interpret and analyze the financial trends, fluctuations, and anomalies. It requires synthesizing multimodal information to provide comprehensive explanations of financial phenomena and their underlying causes.

(5)

Financial Knowledge
: This category concerns the application and extension of financial knowledge through the analysis of multimodal data. It challenges the MLLMs to integrate and extrapolate given information in understanding financial concepts.

3.1.
Benchmark Construction

Figure 2.
The criteria we use when selecting data from financial research reports. We choose financial data that are clear, precise, and contain analyzable content.

3.1.1.
Data Collection

To construct a comprehensive benchmark, we first collect multimodal charts from publicly available financial research reports, which reflect actual usage patterns within the financial industry, providing an authentic basis for our evaluations.
Besides, during benchmark construction from on these charts, we have two options, 1) employ annotators to propose questions and answers directly from the chart content; 2) extract the analyses of charts from the reports and automatically generate corresponding questions and answers.
However, the former method requires substantial manpower and could introduce annotator biases.
To align with the actual analysis methods used in financial application, we opt to collect the textual analyses accompanying these multimodal charts.
These textual analyses, while not encompassing all information presented in the charts, represent the most critical and significant insights according to the use cases of these reports.

To ensure the quality of the collected charts and their corresponding textual analyses, we establish specific screening criteria, 1) information in the charts is clear and visible without any obstructions, blurriness, or disorder; 2) the textual analysis hasto correspond closely with the information in the charts and be relatively extensive (exceeding 50 Chinese characters). In Figure
2
, we present several selected and unselected data to further explain our criteria.
Based on these principles, we organize each collection group with two undergraduate students.
Only when both individuals agree on the clarity and relevance of a chart and its analysis, the collect charts-analysis pair is included.
Through this meticulous approach, we compile a total of 2500 pairs of charts-analysis.

3.1.2.
Data Annotation

To ensure the quality of our benchmark, we utilize the advanced LLM, GPT-4, to generate questions and answers from the textual analysis of collected chart-analysis pairs.
Based on the characteristics of the charts, the association between chart types and question categories are defined as 1) line charts and tables are used to generate
Arithmetic Reasoning
and
Financial Knowledge
question, 2) histogram charts and other types like pie charts and composite charts are used to generate
Statistical Reasoning
and
Financial Explanation
questions, 3) structural diagrams are used to generate
Structural Reasoning
and
Financial Explanation
questions.
While proposing the questions and answers, we design five specific instructions for GPT-4, which require the model to first assess if the provided analysis is suitable for question generation.
If deemed appropriate, the model then proceed to craft questions aligned with the requirements of each of the five question categories.
More details about these instructions are available in appendix.
For each analysis that qualified, the model is instructed to generate two questions per question category, ensuring a comprehensive assessment.

For subjective questions (financial knowledge and explanation), the answers are formatted in bullet points to facilitate clarity, while for objective questions (arithmetic, statistical, and structural reasoning), the answers are structured as multiple-choice.
Through this methodical approach, we successfully identified 2,421 chart-analysis pairs suitable for this exercise, ultimately generating a robust dataset of 9,684 image-question pairs.
This annotation procedure ensures the quality of question-answer pairs and their relation with financial charts and provides solid support for evaluating MLLMs in multimodal financial context.

3.1.3.
Manual Examination

After completing data annotation, we conduct manual verification through the following steps, 1) we verify whether the proposed questions can be answered using only the provided multimodal information; 2) for objective questions, checking the correctness of the option format and ensuring that the correct answer is included among the options; 3) for subjective questions, confirming the accuracy of answers formatted in bullet points; 4) we manually label questions that are unrelated to the charts or where the correct information cannot be obtained from the charts.
After the manual examination, we finally obtain 2,339 valid charts along with 9,356 questions to construct CFBenchmark-MM.

3.2.
Benchmark Evaluation

Most existing MLLMs have been trained on financial text data and may inherently possess some capabilities to answer benchmark-related questions.
To fully evaluate the financial and multimodal processing capabilities, we develop a staged evaluation system with multiple settings for CFBenchmark-MM.
In Figure
3
, we illustrate the procedure of our staged evaluation system, as detailed below:

(1)

Question (setting-Q):
This baseline evaluation assesses models MLLMs on questions without additional multimodal information. It reflects the inherent knowledge of MLLMs.

(2)

Question + Image (setting-Q+I):
In this setting, we introduce original charts into the evaluation to examine how models integrate visual content with textual queries, testing their multimodal processing capabilities.

(3)

Question + Caption (setting-Q+C):
In this setting, we enhance the assessment by adding textual descriptions generated by advanced MLLM, GPT-4V, from the charts, evaluating how well models can utilize synthesized textual representations alongside the queries.

(4)

Question + Image + Caption (setting-Q+I+C):
This setting combines both the original visual content and its textual description into assessment, measuring the ability of MLLMs to synergize and scale their understanding across complex multimodal inputs.

Through this staged evaluation, we not only gauge the foundational knowledge of MLLMs in financial domain but also critically assess their proficiency in handling both direct multimodal inputs and derived textual representations.
This evaluation strategy provides valuable insights into model performance, aiding in the effective selection of models for real-world financial applications.

4.2.
Main Results

In Figure
5
, we display the performance of 13 MLLMs under setting-Q and setting-Q+I, showcasing their capabilities within multimodal financial contexts. Despite only having questions as input, most MLLMs achieve over 20% accuracy (accuracy for random choice) in objective questions and score above 10% in subjective assessments, demonstrating their foundational memory in financial knowledge. With the addition of original charts (setting-Q+I), all MLLMs exhibit notable improvements, indicating the value of integrating visual data for enhanced comprehension. Among them, GPT-4V stands out, achieving the largest improvement upon integrating charts, with over 15% accuracy and 20% score increases in objective and subjective questions, respectively. This underscores its superior multimodal processing capabilities. In contrast, LLaVA-NeXT-mistral-7B shows the weakest overall performance, with minimal improvement upon integrating charts, likely due to its limited Chinese language processing capabilities. However, even advanced MLLMs like GPT-4V achieve only 52% accuracy and 38% score in objective and subjective questions, falling short of real-world application requirements. Moreover, under both evaluation settings, most models perform best on Structural Reasoning questions, while their performance is weakest on tasks related to Financial Explanation. These findings highlight a substantial opportunity for further enhancements in multimodal financial applications, emphasizing the need for exploiting multimodal data in financial analysis and bridging the gap between capabilities and practical utility.

5.
Conclusion

This paper has explored the development and implication of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in multimodal finance application, highlighting the crucial role of integrating texts, charts and figures to improve decision-making accuracy and efficiency.
Therefore, we introduce CFBenchmark-MM, a comprehensive financial benchmark with over 9,000 image-question pairs, including various data representations such as tables, line charts, pie charts, and structural diagrams.
Our evaluation settings have assessed the capabilities of MLLMs in handling multimodal information, from textual data to visual content integration.
These findings indicate that although MLLMs are capable of possessing inherent financial knowledge, their efficiency in processing multimodal information is still limited, highlighting the need for further improvements in the multimodal context.
Our research underscores the significant yet underexploited potential of MLLMs in financial analysis and advocates for their enhanced application in the financial domain.
The insights call for an ongoing refinement of the MLLMs to better utilize multimodal information, with the aim of achieving more robust and accurate financial decision making.

Appendix A
Limitation and Future Work

In this paper, we investigate the evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in financial application and propose a benchmark and an evaluation framework, CFBenchmark-MM.
The benchmark segregates into five question categories, analyzing 12 open-source MLLMs and 1 close-source MLLM, GPT-4V.
The benchmark is scheduled for release following the publication of this paper.
However, our evaluations predominantly concentrate on open-source MLLMs, with a noticeable absence of testing on certain proprietary models such as Claude and Gemini. Additionally, while our five categories of questions encompass a broad range of real-world financial applications, they do not extend into specialized sub-disciplines within finance, such as investments and banking.
In constructing our benchmark, we effectively utilize multimodal data from financial research reports to generate questions and answers, primarily focusing on Chinese-language reports.
Although an English version is provided, the exploration of multilingual benchmark remains constrained.

In the future, the research directions can be discussed from the following two perspective:

•

Benchmark in finance
:
1) Enhancing the task classification by breaking down the current five question types into more detailed financial sub-domains;
2) Extending the linguistic scope of benchmark system by including additional languages such as French, Japanese, and German, moving beyond the current bilingual (Chinese-English) framework;
3) Incorporating considerations of security and robustness in the design of future benchmarks to facilitate safer and more reliable applications of MLLMs in financial domain.

•

MLLMs in finance
:
1) Expanding the application of MLLMs within the financial domain by leveraging more domain-specific multimodal data to optimize their performance;
2) Developing specialized visual alignment modules as current reliance on pre-trained models like CLIP and ViT offers limited functionality in accurately representing financial charts;
3) Investigating the potential of multi-agent systems in multimodal financial settings to understand how collaborative interactions among different agents can enhance model effectiveness and decision-making.

These proposed enhancements aim to deepen the integration, utility, and adaptability of MLLMs in financial domain, thereby augmenting their applicability in sophisticated financial analyses.

Instructions for Manual Examination

1. 问题的验证： 我们首先验证所提出的问题是否完全可以使用所提供的多模态信息来回答。这包括检查问题是否直接关联到给定的文本、图表或图像内容，以确保所有问题都能够凭借所给信息找到答案。

2. 客观问题的格式与准确性检查： 对于客观类型的问题，我们细致检查选项的格式是否正确，并确保所有提供的答案选项中包含一个准确且无歧义的正确答案。这一步骤确保选择题的每个选项都清晰、合适，并且正确答案在选项中明确标出。

3. 主观问题的答案验证： 对于主观问题，我们确认答案的准确性，并要求答案以要点格式呈现。这意味着答案应简洁明了，直接点出关键信息，并确保这些信息能够准确反映问题所需的内容。

4. 标记与图表无关或信息不足的问题： 我们将手动标记那些与图表无直接关联或无法从图表中得到正确信息的问题。这包括识别和标记那些题干与图表内容不匹配或图表无法提供足够信息以回答问题的情况。

(1. Question Verification: We first verify whether the questions posed can be entirely answered using the provided multimodal information. This includes checking if the questions are directly related to the given text, charts, or image content, to ensure that all questions can be answered based solely on the provided information.

2. Format and Accuracy Check for Objective Questions: For objective-type questions, we meticulously examine whether the format of the options is correct and ensure that among all the provided answer options, there is one accurate and unambiguous correct answer. This step ensures that each option in multiple-choice questions is clear, appropriate, and the correct answer is distinctly marked within the options.

3. Verification of Answers for Subjective Questions: For subjective questions, we confirm the accuracy of answers and require that the answers be presented in a bullet-point format. This means the answers should be concise and directly highlighting the key information and ensuring the information accurately reflects the content to address the question.

4. Marking Questions Irrelevant to Charts or Lacking Information: We mark questions that have no direct connection to the charts or cannot obtain information from the charts. This includes identifying and marking instances where the question does not match the content of the chart or the chart fails to provide sufficient information to answer the question.)

2. 累计净值则代表了理财产品从成立以来的总收益情况，包括了所有的分红、拆分等因素。它是将历史的所有单位净值进行累加得到的，反映了理财产品的长期表现。\n
3. 在评估理财产品表现时，单位净值和累计净值都非常重要。单位净值可以帮助投资者了解当前购买或赎回理财产品的价格，从而做出投资决策。而累计净值则可以帮助投资者了解理财产品的历史表现，评估其长期的投资价值。\n
4. 同时观察单位净值和累计净值的变化，可以更全面地了解理财产品的表现。例如，单位净值上升可能意味着理财产品近期表现良好，而累计净值上升则意味着理财产品长期以来的总体表现不错。\n \n \n \n

案例3: 问题：土地溢价率环比上周回落1.2个百分点是什么意思，上周土地溢价率如何计算？\n
回答：1. “环比上周回落1.2个百分点”的意思是相比上一周，这一周的土地溢价率下降了1.2个百分点。具体来说，“环比”是比较两个相邻周期的数据变化，这里是指与上一周相比；“回落”表示下降；“1.2个百分点”则是下降的具体数值，即下降了1.2%的幅度。\n
2. 计算回落的百分点，首先需要知道上周和本周的土地溢价率具体数值。假设上周土地溢价率为x%，本周为y%，那么环比回落的百分点就是上周的溢价率减去本周的溢价率，即(x - y)%。根据题目给出的信息，全国土地溢价率本周为2%，环比上周回落1.2个百分点，可以推算出上周全国土地溢价率为(2% + 1.2%) = 3.2%。所以，计算方法是：上周土地溢价率 - 本周土地溢价率 = 回落的百分点。\n \n \n \n

Volume in the Extensive Thermodynamics of Black Holes Thanks: Presented in Prague, at VISEGRAD 2024 october
Réka Somogyfoki, Péter Ván

Abstract
Since black holes lack a straightforward notion of geometrical volume due to their event horizon structure and coordinate dependence, various approaches have been proposed to introduce a meaningful geometric and thermodynamic volume. There is no volume in classical black hole thermodynamics, the nonextensivity of black holes is a consequence. Extensivity of such systems can be restored by introducing a new thermodynamic state variable, typically the volume, the mass or the number of particles. Smarr relations show that black hole thermodynamics is nonextensive. However, restored extensivity leads to a volume which corresponds to geometric concepts and is also meaningful from a physical point of view. The article examines various volume definitions in the context of Anti-de Sitter (AdS) black holes and their implications for phase transitions, focusing on the Hawking –- Page transition in Kerr –- AdS spacetime, including the Christodoulou –- Rovelli volume.

2
Hawking-Page phase boundaries

In black hole thermodynamics, certain properties follow first-order homogeneous extensivity. The so-called Smarr relations emerge when the invariant mass is treated as a thermodynamic potential
[
6
]
. The identification of the mass with different thermodynamic potentials leads to distinct volume definitions (if such a concept is included), as discussed in the following section. A crucial aspect is thermodynamic stability, is a necessary condition for dynamic stability in non-equilibrium thermodynamics, particularly for time-dependent processes
[
7
]
. In case of black holes, dynamic stability against symmetric perturbations around an axis is ensured if and only if the black hole satisfies the analogue of the thermodynamic stability criterion for inhomogeneous systems
[
4
]
.

In AdS spacetime, the energy-momentum tensor is related to the cosmological constant, which is proportional to the pressure. The expansion of the Universe manifests as a locally negative pressure, making the cosmological constant analogous to gas pressure.

Introducing pressure and volume reveals state-space regions in charged AdS black holes where the second derivative of the entropy is positive semi-definite, suggesting phase transitions. The Hawking–-Page temperature marks a transition between stable black holes and thermal radiation, calculated as
[
3
]
:

T_{HP}=\sqrt{\frac{8p}{3\pi}},

(1)

for
F(T_{HP})=0

T_{HP}

T_{HP}

Alternatively, AdS black hole thermodynamics can be formulated by treating the cosmological constant as pressure
[
8
]
. In this case, the black hole mass should be identified as enthalpy rather than the free energy. Also, the BH volume naturally emerges as its derivative with respect to pressure. Entropy aligns with the event horizon surface, ensuring that the second law holds. As positive-energy material crosses the event horizon, its mass and surface area increase, leading to entropy growth
[
9
]
.

3
Volume

A black hole without volume is thermodynamically unstable. Introducing volume as an extensive quantity results in the Christodoulou –- Rovelli volume concept
[
10
]
. In the
U(V)
parameter space, the entropy is concave, ensuring thermodynamic stability.

A major issue in black hole thermodynamics is the negative heat capacity problem: as a black hole loses mass, its temperature increases, leading to runaway evaporation. Biró et al.
[
11
]
introduced a volume concept that resolves this problematic behavior. Their definition, proportional to the fifth power of the radius (and mass), transforms entropy from a convex curve into a concave surface. Identifying black hole mass with enthalpy rather than internal energy incorporates volume and pressure naturally, leading to a completely thermodynamic formulation
[
12
]
:

H(S,p)=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{S}{\pi}}+p\Big(\frac{S}{\pi}\Big)^{5/2}.

(2)

From this, volume is derived as:

V=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\Big|_{S}=\Big(\frac{S}{\pi}\Big)^{5/2}=r_{S}^{5},

(3)

which confirms that volume is consistent with the Christodoulou–-Rovelli volume, namely, that it is proportional to
r_{S}^{5}
.

4
Conclusion

The concept of volume in black hole thermodynamics challenges our conventional understanding of space, matter, and thermodynamic variables. Unlike ordinary systems, the theory black holes lacks a straightforward volume definition, necessitating alternative approaches. The Christodoulou –- Rovelli volume is a compelling candidate, preserving thermodynamic consistency and providing a natural resolution to open questions such as negative heat capacity.

In this work we have shown that the volume concept can be consistently extended to AdS spacetimes and the value of the Hawking – Page temperature changes, in the new thermodynamic framework it becomes
T_{HPnew}=0.2067
, instead of the classical
T_{HP}=0.3568
. The different value of the Hawking – Page temperature and also the different stability properties may have observable consequences, suggesting that black holes in AdS spacetimes remain stable in different temperature regimes compared to the classical approach. Among the several possible cosmological implications, the change in the primordial black hole distribution is outstanding, since it may be detected by the Einstein Telescope
[
13
]
.

There are several other theoretical consequences, offering a more refined framework for understanding black hole phase transitions like the Hawking –- Page one. As black hole physics continues to evolve, refining these thermodynamic descriptions will be crucial in bridging the gap between classical gravity and theories of spacetime.

The Hasse norm principle for some extensions of degree having square-free prime factors
Yasuhiro Oki

Abstract
. We determine the structure of the obstruction group of the Hasse norm principle for a finite separable extension K / k K/k of a global field of degree d d , where d d has a square-free prime factor p p and a p p -Sylow subgroup of the Galois group G G of the Galois closure of K / k K/k is normal in G G . Specifically, we give a partial classification of the validity of the Hasse norm principle for K / k K/k in the case where (1) [ K : k ] = p ℓ [K:k]=p\ell where p p and ℓ \ell are two distinct prime numbers; or (2) [ K : k ] = 4 p [K:k]=4p where p p is an odd prime. The result (1) gives infinitely many new existences of finite extensions of arbitrary number fields for which the Hasse norm principle fail. Furthermore, we prove that there exist infinitely many separable extensions of square-free degree for which the exponents of the obstruction groups to the Hasse norm principle are not prime powers.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a global field, and
K/k
a finite separable field extension. Put

\Sha(K/k):=(k^{\times}\cap\N_{K/k}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))/\N_{K/k}(K^{\times}),

where
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
is the idèle group of
K
. In the paper of Scholz (
[
Sch40
]
), the group
\Sha(K/k)
is denoted by
\kappa(K/k)
and is called the
number knot
. We say that the
Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
if

\Sha(K/k)=1.

The study of
\Sha(K/k)
is one of the classic problems in algebraic number theory. It is known by Hasse (
[
Has31
]
) that the Hasse norm principle holds for all cyclic extensions of
k
. On the other hand, he also clarified in the same paper that the Hasse norm principle fails for the biquadratic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-39},\sqrt{-3})/\mathbb{Q}
. After that, the classification of the validity of the Haase norm principle is developed by many people. In 1967, Tate (
[
Tat67
]
) gave a description of
\Sha(K/k)
for a finite Galois extension
K/k
by means of cohomology of the associated Galois group. On the other hand, it is much less known if
K/k
is Galois. For a non-Galois extension
K/k
, we denote by
\widetilde{K}/k
the Galois closure of
K/k
. There exist three strategies for the study for non-Galois extensions as follows.

(i)

Investigate certain families of finite extensions indexed by (some) positive integers.

(ii)

For a fixed
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, study all finite separable extensions of degree
d
.

(iii)

For a given finite group
G
, consider all finite separable extensions
K/k
that admit an isomorphism
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)\cong G
.

First, we enumerate the results that follow (i). If
[K:k]=d
and
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
is isomorphic to the dihedral group of order
2d
, then Bartels gave that the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
(
[
Bar81b
]
). The same assertion has been proved by Voskresenskii and Kunyavskii in the case
[K:k]=d
and that
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
is isomorphic to the symmetric group of
d
-letters (
[
VK84
]
). Furthermore, Macedo provided the same assertion as above in the case
[K:k]=d\geq 5
and
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
is isomorphic to the alternating group of
d
-letters (
[
Mac20
]
).

Second, we recall the previous study based on (ii). If
d
is a prime number, then Bartels (
[
Bar81a
]
) proved that the Hasse norm principle always holds. After that, Kunyavskii gave an equivalent condition for the validity of the Hasse norm principle in the case
d=4
(
[
Kun84
]
). The same results as above are given by Drakokhrust and Platonov for
d=6
(
[
DP87
]
), and by Hoshi, Kanai and Yamasaki for
d\leq 15
(
[
HKY22
]
,
[
HKY23a
]
). There also exists a partial result in the case
d=16
, which is given by Hoshi, Kanai and Yamasaki (
[
HKY25
]
). Note that there is a possibility that the Hasse norm principle may fail if
d
is a composite number
\leq 16
. However, as
d
increases, this approach will become extremely difficult to implement since their results are based on case-by-case computation.

Finally, we list the research that is in line with (iii). First, the case where
G
is a symmtetic group or an alternating group is discussed by Macedo and Newton (
[
MN22
]
). In particular, it implies that
\Sha(K/k)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2
for certain finite extension of degree
30
,
70
or
210
. On the other hand, the same considerations as above were made by Hoshi, Kanai and Yamasaki in the case that
G
is the Mathieu group
M_{11}
or the Janko group
J_{1}
(
[
HKY23b
]
). Note that their study gives an isomorphism
\Sha(K/k)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
which may happen when
n=110
and
G_{0}=M_{11}
.

In this paper, we study the Hasse norm principle for finite extensions based on (i), however we further assume that
d
has a square-free prime factor. Note that this study also gives a partial result related to (ii) for infinitely many
d
. Moreover, we can give new examples of the failure of the Hasse norm principle for
all
number fields and some function fields.

1.1.
Main theorems

To state our theorem, we use the notations as follows for a finite group
G
and its subgroup
H^{\prime}
:

•

N_{G}(H^{\prime}):=\{g\in G\mid gH^{\prime}g^{-1}=H^{\prime}\}
is the normalizer of
H^{\prime}
in
G
;

•

Z_{G}(H^{\prime}):=\{g\in G\mid gh^{\prime}=h^{\prime}g\text{ for all }h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}\}
is the centralizer of
H^{\prime}
in
G
;

•

N^{G}(H^{\prime}):=\bigcap_{g\in G}gH^{\prime}g^{-1}
, the normal core of
H^{\prime}
in
G
;

•

[H^{\prime},G]:=\langle hgh^{-1}g^{-1}\in G\mid h\in H^{\prime},g\in G\rangle
.

On the other hand, for a finite abelian group
A
and a prime number
p
, let

\displaystyle A[p^{\infty}]:=\{a\in A\mid p^{n}a=0\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\},

\displaystyle A^{(p)}:=\{a\in A\mid na=0\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}\}.

Note that
A=A[p^{\infty}]\oplus A^{(p)}
by definition.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
8.2
)
.

Let
K/k
be a finite separable field extension of a global field with its Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. We further assume

•

[K:k]\in p\mathbb{Z}\setminus p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
; and

•

a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal in
G
.

(i)

If
\Sha(K/k)[p^{\infty}]\neq 1
, then

(a)

S_{p}\cong(C_{p})^{2}
;

(b)

[S_{p},G]=S_{p}
; and

(c)

N_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)=Z_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)
.

Conversely, if
(a)
,
(b)
and
(c)
hold, then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)[p^{\infty}]\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $S_{p}$;}\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

(ii)

Let
K_{0}/k
be the subextension of
\widetilde{K}/k
corresponding to
HS_{p}
. Then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)^{(p)}\cong\Sha(K_{0}/k).

We note that there is a unique
p
-Sylow subgroup of a finite group
G
if and only if it is normal in
G
. This follows from Sylow’s theorem, which implies that all Sylow subgroups of
G
are conjugate to each other.

If we impose an additional condition on
p
and
[K:k]
, then we obtain a vanishing of
\Sha(K/k)[p^{\infty}]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
8.3
)
.

Let
K/k
be a finite separable field extension of a global field with its Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. We further assume

•

[K:k]\in p\mathbb{Z}\setminus p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
;

•

a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal in
G
; and

•

n:=[K:k]/p
satisfies
\gcd(n,p-1)\leq 2
and
\gcd(n,p+1)\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.

Then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\Sha(K_{0}/k),

where
K_{0}/k
is the subextension of
\widetilde{K}/k
corresponding to
HS_{p}
. In particular,
\Sha(K/k)
has no
p
-primary torsion part.

On the other hand, Theorem
1.1
also implies partial classification of the non-vanishing of
\Sha(K/k)
for an extension
K/k
of certain degree. We use the following notations.

•

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
C_{n}=\langle c_{n}\rangle
is the cyclic group of order
n
. Moreover, we write for
\Delta
the diagonal map
C_{n}\hookrightarrow C_{n}\times C_{n}
.

•

For an integer
n\geq 2
, put

D_{n}:=\langle\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}\mid\sigma_{n}^{n}=\tau_{n}^{2}=1,\tau_{n}\sigma_{n}\tau_{n}^{-1}=\sigma_{n}^{-1}\rangle,

the dihedral group of order
2n
.

•

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
the alternating group of
n
-letters.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
8.5
)
.

Let
p
and
\ell
be two distinct prime numbers. Consider a finite separable extension
K/k
of degree
p\ell
of a global field, and write for
\widetilde{K}/k
its Galois closure. Set
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. We further assume that a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal in
G
. If
\Sha(K/k)\neq 1
, then one of the following is satisfied:

(
\alpha
)

2<\ell\mid p-1
,
G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi_{0,m}}C_{\ell}
and
H\cong\Delta(C_{p})\rtimes_{\varphi_{0,m}}\{1\}
, where
\varphi_{0,m}
is defined by

C_{\ell}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p});c_{\ell}\mapsto\diag(\zeta_{\ell},\zeta_{\ell}^{m}),

\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is a primitive
\ell
-th root of unity, and
m\in\{2,\ldots,\ell-1\}
satisfies
mm^{\prime}\not\equiv 1\bmod\ell
for any
m^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
;

(
\beta
)

2<\ell\mid p+1
,
G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi_{1}}C_{\ell}
and
H\cong\Delta(C_{p})\rtimes_{\varphi_{1}}\{1\}
, where
\varphi_{1}
is defined by

C_{\ell}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p});c_{\ell}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

and
\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is a primitive
\ell
-th root of unity.

(
\gamma
)

p\geq 5
,
2<\ell\mid p^{2}-1
,
G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi_{2}}D_{\ell}
and
H\cong\Delta(C_{p})\rtimes_{\varphi_{2}}\langle\tau_{\ell}\rangle
, where
\varphi_{2}
is defined by

D_{\ell}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p});\sigma_{\ell}^{i}\tau_{\ell}^{i^{\prime}}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}^{i}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}^{i^{\prime}}

and
\zeta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}^{\times}
is a primitive
\ell
-th root of unity.

Conversely, if
(
\alpha
)
,
(
\beta
)
or
(
\gamma
)
holds, then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $S_{p}$; }\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Combining Theorem
1.2
with (the proof of) the inverse Galois problem for finite solvable groups due to Shafarevich, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
8.6
)
.

Let
p
and
\ell
be two distinct prime numbers satisfying
2<\ell\mid p^{2}-1
, and
d
a multiple of
p\ell
that is square-free. Take a global field
k
whose characteristic is different from
p
. Then there is a finite extension
K
of
k
of degree
d
for which the Hasse norm principle fails.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
8.8
)
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number. Consider a finite separable extension
K/k
of degree
4p
of a global field, and write for
\widetilde{K}/k
its Galois closure. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. We further assume that a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal.

(i)

If
\Sha(K/k)
is non-trivial, then it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2
or
\mathbb{Z}/p
.

(ii)

If
\Sha(K/k)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p
, then
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
and there exist an isomorphisms

(1.1)

G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi_{p,4}}C_{4},\quad H\cong\Delta(C_{p})\rtimes_{\varphi}\{1\}.

Here
\varphi_{p,4}
is defined by the homomorphism

\mathbb{Z}/4\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p});\,1\bmod 4\mapsto\diag(-1,\sqrt{-1}).

Conversely, if
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
and (
1.1
) hold, then one has an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $S_{p}$; }\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

(iii)

If
\Sha(K/k)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
, then we have

(a)

G/N^{G}(HS_{p})\cong(C_{2})^{2}
; or

(b)

p\geq 5
and
G/N^{G}(HS_{p})\cong\mathfrak{A}_{4}
.

Conversely, if
(a)
or
(b)
is satisfied, then there is an isomorphism

(1.2)

\Sha(K/k)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $(C_{2})^{2}$; }\\
\mathbb{Z}/2&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Remark 1.8
.

(i)

If a finite Galois extension
K/k
has square-free degree, then we have
\Sha(K/k)=1
. This is a consequence of Gurak (
[
Gur78
]
). Hence, the finite extensions in Theorems
1.4
and
1.7
(i) are always non-Galois.

(ii)

Let
K/k
be a finite Galois extension of degree
9p
, where
p\neq 3
is a prime number. Put
G:=\Gal(K/k)
, and take a
3
-Sylow subgroup
S_{3}
of
G
. Then the restriction map

\widehat{H}^{-3}(G,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\widehat{H}^{-3}(S_{3},\mathbb{Z})

is injective. Combining this fact with Tate’s theorem, we obtain that
\Sha(K/k)
is trivial or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/3
. Therefore, the finite extension in Theorem
1.7
(ii) must be non-Galois.

1.1
implies Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
. We first discuss Theorem

. Let
K/k
,
G
,
H
and

be as in Theorem

. In particular,

has degree

. It suffices to prove that

(1)

the conditions (a), (b), (c) in Theorem

are satisfied if and only if (
\alpha
), (
\beta
) or (
\gamma
) is valid; and

(2)

\Sha(K/k)^{(p)}\cong\Sha(K_{0}/k)

For (1), take a subgroup
G^{\prime}
of

satisfying
G=S_{p}\rtimes G^{\prime}
. Then we may assume

for some subgroup

of

. Then (a), (b), (c) in Theorem

can be rephrased to those on

-dimensional

-representations of

, which is called in this paper an

-extremal

. However, if

1.2.
Results on cohomological invariants of norm one tori

Here, let
F
be a field, and fix a separable closure
F^{\sep}
of
F
. Consider a finite separable field extension
E
of
F
that is contained in
F^{\sep}
. Then the
F
-torus

T_{E/F}:=\Ker(\N_{E/F}\colon\Res_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}).

is called the
norm one torus
associated to
E/F
. Take a smooth compactification
X
of
T_{E/F}
, and set
\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{F}F^{\sep}
. Then the Picard
\Pic(\overline{X})
is a finite free abelian group equipped with a continuous action of the absolute Galois group of
F
(with respect to the discrete topology on
\Pic(\overline{X})
). Note that there exists an isomorphism

H^{1}(F,\Pic(\overline{X}))\cong\Br(X)/\Br(k),

where
\Br(X):=H_{\et}^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is the étale cohomological Brauer group of
X
. See
[
CS87
, Theorem 9.5]
.

It is known that
H^{1}(F,\Pic(\overline{X}))
is related to the Hasse norm principle. Let
K/k
be a finite extension of a global field, and denote by
\Sigma_{k}
the set of places of
k
. Then Ono constructed in
[
Ono63
]
an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k}).

Here right-hand side is the
Tate–Shafarevich group
of
T_{K/k}
, which is defined as the kernel of the homomorphism

(\Res_{k_{v}/k})_{v}\colon H^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\rightarrow\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{k}}H^{1}(k_{v},T_{K/k}).

On the other hand, put

A_{k}(T_{K/k}):=\left(\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{k}}T_{K/k}(k_{v})\right)/\overline{T_{K/k}(k)},

where
\overline{T_{K/k}(k)}
is the closure of
T_{K/k}(k)
in
\prod_{v}T_{K/k}(k_{v})
. Then Vosresenskii (
[
Vos69
]
) gave an exact sequence

0\rightarrow A_{k}(T_{K/k})\rightarrow H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\rightarrow 0,

where
H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}
is the Pontryagin dual of
H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))
.

For a finite group
G
and its subgroups
H
, we use the notations as follows:

•

N_{G}(H):=\{g\in G\mid gHg^{-1}=H\}
, the normalizer of
H
in
G
;

•

Z_{G}(H):=\{g\in G\mid gh=hg\text{ for any }h\in H\}
, the centralizer of
H
in
G
;

•

for another subgroup
D
of
G
,
[H,D]:=\langle h^{-1}dhd^{-1}\mid h\in H,d\in D\rangle
;

1.3.
Second cohomology groups

Here we explain proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.9
. Let
G
be a finite group. For a subgroup
H
of
G
, put
\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbb{Z}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}[H]}(\mathbb{Z}[G],M)
. Consider an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow 0,

where the homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow J_{G/H}
is defined by sending
1
to the map
g\mapsto 1
for all
g\in G
. Pick a set of subgroups
\mathcal{D}
of
G
containing all its cyclic subgroups that is stable under conjugation of
G
. We say such a set to be
admissible
in this paper. Then we discuss the structure of the abelian group

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H}):=\Ker\left(H^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\xrightarrow{(\Res_{G/D})_{D\in\mathcal{D}}}\bigoplus_{D\in\mathcal{D}}H^{2}(D,J_{G/H})\right).

If
\mathcal{D}
is the set of all cyclic subgroups of
G
, then we also denote
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
by
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
.

The
G
-lattice
J_{G/H}
is related to a norm one torus as follows. Let
E/F
be a finite separable field extension with Galois closure
\widetilde{E}/F
. Put
G=\Gal(\widetilde{E}/F)
and
H=\Gal(\widetilde{E}/E)
. Then one has an isomorphism of
G
-lattices
J_{G/H}\cong X^{*}(T_{E/F})
. Here the right-hand side is the
character group
of
T_{E/F}
, which is defined as follows:

X^{*}(T_{E/F}):=\Hom_{F^{\sep}\text{-group}}(T_{E/F}\otimes_{F}F^{\sep},\mathbb{G}_{m,F^{\sep}}).

Moreover, there is an isomorphism of finite abelian groups

H^{1}(F,\Pic(\overline{X}))\cong\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H}),

where
\overline{X}=X\otimes_{F}F^{\sep}
and
X
is a smooth compactification of
T_{E/F}
.

Under the above notations, we further assume that
F=k
is a global field and set
K:=E
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set of all decomposition groups of
\widetilde{K}/k
, which is an admissible set of subgroups of
G
. Then the Poitou–Tate duality gives an isomorphism

\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{\vee},

where
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{\vee}
is the Pontryagin dual of
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
. See also Corollary
5.6
. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.7
are reduced to the structure theorem of
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
as follows.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Recall that a
transitive group of degree
n
(or, a
transitive subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
) is a subgroup of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of
n
-letters whose action on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
induced by the natural action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is transitive. Moreover, we call a subgroup of
G
of the form
\Stab_{G}(i)
where
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
a
corresponding subgroup
of
G
.

Theorem 1.10

(Theorem
6.17
)
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
G
a transitive group of degree
\in p\mathbb{Z}\setminus p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
, and
H
a corresponding subgroup of
G
. We further assume that a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal in
G
.

(i)

One has
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})[p^{\infty}]\neq 0
if and only if

(a)

there is an isomorphism
S_{p}\cong(C_{p})^{2}
;

(b)

[S_{p},G]=S_{p}
; and

(c)

N_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)=Z_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)
.

Conversely, assume that
(a)
,
(b)
and
(c)
hold. Then, for an admissible set
\mathcal{D}
of subgroups of
G
, there is an isomorphism

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})[p^{\infty}]\cong\begin{cases}0&\text{if there is $D\in\mathcal{D}$ which contains $S_{p}$;}\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

(ii)

For any admissible set
\mathcal{D}
of subgroups of
G
, there is an isomorphism

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{(p)}\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{/S_{p}}}^{2}(G/S_{p},J_{G/HS_{p}}).

Here,
\mathcal{D}_{/S_{p}}
is the set of subgroups of the form
DS_{p}/S_{p}
where
D\in\mathcal{D}
, which is an admissible set of subgroups of
G/S_{p}
.

In the following, we sketch a proof of Theorem
1.10
. Since
(G:H)\in p\mathbb{Z}\setminus p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
, it turns out that there is an isomorphism
S_{p}\cong(C_{p})^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

First, we explain the proof of (i). The restriction map
\Res_{G/S_{p}}
induces an injection

(1.3)

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})[p^{\infty}]\hookrightarrow\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{S_{p}}}^{2}(S_{p},J_{G/H}),

where
\mathcal{D}_{S_{p}}
is the set of subgroups of
S_{p}
of the form
D\cap S_{p}
for some
D\in\mathcal{D}
. Note that
J_{G/H}
cannot be written as
J_{S_{p}/L}
for some subgroup
L
of
S_{p}
as an
S_{p}
-lattice. However, we can take finitely many subgroups
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
of
G
that admit an exact sequence of
S_{p}
-lattices as follows:

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Ind_{L_{i}}^{S_{p}}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow 0.

Note that such a
S_{p}
-lattice can be described as the character group of a
multinorm one torus
. In our case, we have
(S_{p}:L_{i})=p
for any
i
and
L_{1}\cap\cdots\cap L_{r}=\{1\}
. On the other hand, we know the structure of the right-hand side of (
1.3
), which follows from Bayer-Fluckiger–Lee–Parimala (
[
BLP19
]
). If
m\neq 2
, then
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(S_{p},J_{G/H})
is trivial, and in particular the assertion holds. On the other hand, it does not vanish if
m=2
, and hence we need a delicate argument.

Recall that
\mathcal{D}
contains a subgroup of
G
containing
S_{p}
if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{S_{p}}
contains
S_{p}
. If it is satisfied, then we can prove
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{S_{p}}}^{2}(S_{p},J_{G/H})=0
without difficulty. Otherwise, we consider a short exact sequence of
G
-lattices

(1.4)

0\rightarrow J_{G/HS_{p}}\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow\Ind_{HS_{p}}^{G}J_{HS_{p}/S_{p}}\rightarrow 0.

Moreover, we introduce a subgroup
H^{2}(G,J_{G/HS_{p}})^{(\mathcal{D})}
of
H^{2}(G,J_{G/HS_{p}})
, which fits into an exact sequence

(1.5)

0\rightarrow H^{1}(G,\Ind_{HS_{p}}^{G}J_{HS_{p}/S_{p}})\xrightarrow{\delta_{G}}H^{2}(G,J_{G/HS_{p}})^{(\mathcal{D})}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\N}_{G}}\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\rightarrow 0.

See Definition
6.5
. Note that
H^{2}(G,J_{G/HS_{p}})^{(\mathcal{D})}
plays a role of Selmer groups. This method is inspired by the study of
CM tori
developed by Liang, Yang, Yu and the author (
[
LOYY24
]
). Then we give an isomorphism

H^{2}(G,J_{G/HS_{p}})^{(\mathcal{D})}[p^{\infty}]\cong\frac{(S_{p}/[S_{p},HS_{p}]\cdot[S_{p}\cap H,N_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)])^{\vee}}{(S_{p}/[S_{p},G])^{\vee}},

which is is archived by using Mackey’s decomposition (Proposition
6.14
). The above description implies the desired assertion for
m=2
.

Second, we discuss the proof of (ii). It suffices to prove that
\widehat{\N}_{G}
in the exact sequence (
1.5
) induces the desired isomorphism. By the normality of
S_{p}
, the group
G
can be expressed as a semi-direct product of
S_{p}
and its subgroup
G^{\prime}
whose order is coprime to
p
. Then we get a sequence of abelian groups

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{/S_{p}}}^{2}(G/S_{p},J_{G/HS_{p}})\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/HS_{p}})\xrightarrow{\widehat{\N}_{G}}\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{(p)}\xrightarrow{\Res_{G/G^{\prime}}}\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{G^{\prime}}}^{2}(G^{\prime},J_{G/H}),

where the homomorphisms are injective. Using Mackey’s decomposition, one can prove that
J_{G/H}
is a direct sum of
J_{G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}}
and finitely many
G^{\prime}
-lattices of the forms
\Ind_{H^{\prime\prime}}^{G^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
for some
H^{\prime\prime}<G^{\prime}
. Moreover, the natural isomorphism
G^{\prime}\cong G/S_{p}
induces a bijection
\mathcal{D}_{G^{\prime}}\cong\mathcal{D}_{/S_{p}}
. Hence we obtain an isomorphism

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{/S_{p}}}^{2}(G/S_{p},J_{G/HS_{p}})\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{G^{\prime}}}^{2}(G^{\prime},J_{G/H}),

which concludes the desired assertion.

Remark 1.11
.

One can produce (
1.4
) from
E/F
in Theorem

\widetilde{E}/F
is the Galois closure of

•

2.1.
Cohomology of finite groups

Let
G
be a finite group. For a
G
-module
, we mean a finitely generated abelian groups equipped with
left
actions of
G
. Moreover, a
G
-module that is torsion-free over
\mathbb{Z}
is called a
G
-lattice
. On the other hand, for a subgroup
H
of
G
and an
H
-module

, put
\Ind_{H}^{G}M:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}[H]}(\mathbb{Z}[G],M)
. We define a left action of
G
on
\Ind_{H}^{G}M
by the map

G\times\Ind_{H}^{G}M\rightarrow\Ind_{H}^{G}M;(g,\varphi)\mapsto[g^{\prime}\mapsto\varphi(g^{\prime}g)].

Lemma 2.1

(
[
NSW00
, (1.6.5) Proposition]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
H
its subgroup. Consider a
G
-module

. Then the composite

H^{j}(G,M)\rightarrow H^{j}(G,\Ind_{H}^{G}M)\cong H^{j}(H,M),

where the second homomorphism follows from Shapiro’s lemma, coincides with the restriction map
\Res_{G/H}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Mackey’s decomposition; cf.
[
Ser77
, Section 7.3, Proposition 22]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
H
and
D
subgroups of
G
. Take a complete representative
R(D,H)
of
D\backslash G/H
in
G
. Consider an
H
-module
(M,\rho)
. Then the homomorphism of abelian groups

\pi_{D,g}\colon\Ind_{H}^{G}M\rightarrow\Ind_{D\cap gHg^{-1}}^{D}M^{g};\varphi\mapsto[d\mapsto\varphi(g^{-1}d)]

for all

induces an isomorphism of
D

\Ind_{H}^{G}M\cong\bigoplus_{g\in R(D,H)}\Ind_{D\cap gHg^{-1}}^{D}M^{g}.

Here

is the abelian group

equipped with the action of
gHg^{-1}
defined by

gHg^{-1}\rightarrow\Aut(M);h\mapsto(\rho\circ\Ad(g^{-1}))(h).

The independence of the choice of

in Proposition

follows from Lemma

as follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
H
and
D
subgroups of
G
.

(i)

Let
g\in G
. For
d,d^{\prime}\in D
, we have
dgH=d^{\prime}gH
if and only if
d^{\prime}d^{-1}\in D\cap gHg^{-1}
. In particular, the subset
DgH
of
G
, which is an element of
D\backslash G/H
, has cardinality
(\#D\cdot\#H)/\#(D\cap gHg^{-1})
.

(ii)

Assume that
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
satisfies
Dg_{1}H=Dg_{2}H
. Then the subgroups
D\cap g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}
and
D\cap g_{2}Hg_{2}^{-1}
of
D
are conjugate in
D
.

Proof.

(i): Let
d,d^{\prime}\in D
. The equality
dgH=d^{\prime}gH
is equivalent to the condition
g^{-1}d^{-1}d^{\prime}g\in H
. Moreover, Since
d
and
d^{\prime}
lie in
D
, the condition
g^{-1}d^{-1}d^{\prime}g\in H
holds if and only if the desired condition
d^{\prime}d^{-1}\in D\cap gHg^{-1}
is satisfied.

(ii): By assumption, there are
h\in H
and
d\in D
such that
g_{2}=dg_{1}h
. Then we have

D\cap g_{2}Hg_{2}^{-1}=dDd^{-1}\cap dg_{1}(hHh^{-1})g_{1}^{-1}d^{-1}=d(D\cap g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1})d^{-1}.

Hence the assertion holds.

\blacksquare

Lemma 2.14
.

Let

be a finite group,

a subgroup of

, and

0\rightarrow\Ind_{H}^{G}M\rightarrow\Ind_{H}^{G}P\rightarrow\Ind_{H}^{G}Q\rightarrow 0.

It suffices to prove

for every subgroup
D
of

. There is an isomorphism

\widehat{H}^{-1}(D,\Ind_{H}^{G}Q)\cong\bigoplus_{g\in R(D,H)}\widehat{H}^{-1}(D,\Ind_{D\cap gHg^{-1}}^{D}Q^{g}),

which follows from Proposition

. On the other hand, Shaipro’s lemma gives rise to an isomorphism

\widehat{H}^{-1}(D,\Ind_{D\cap gHg^{-1}}^{D}Q^{g})\cong\widehat{H}^{-1}(D\cap gHg^{-1},Q^{g})

\blacksquare

3.
Inverse Galois problem with conditions on decomposition groups

In this section, we recall results on the inverse Galois problem which will be needed to prove the main theorems.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
k
a global field, and
G
a finite group. Assume that

•

the characteristic of
k
is different from
p
;

•

a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal; and

•

there is a finite Galois extension
\widetilde{K}_{0}/k
with Galois group
G/S_{p}
in which all decomposition groups are cyclic.

Then there exists a finite Galois extension
\widetilde{K}/k
with Galois group
G
such that

(i)

\widetilde{K}_{0}
corresponds to
S_{p}
; and

(ii)

all decomposition groups in
\widetilde{K}/k
are cyclic.

Proof.

By the Schur–Zassenhaus theorem, the canonical exact sequence

1\rightarrow S_{p}\rightarrow G\rightarrow G/S_{p}\rightarrow 1

splits. Then the assertion follows from a proof of Shafarevich’s theorem, which states the inverse Galois theory holds for finite solvable groups. See
[
NSW00
, (9.6.7) Theorem]
.

\blacksquare

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G:=C_{\ell}\times C_{n}
, where
\ell
is a prime number and
n
is a positive integer. Then, for any global field
k
, there is a finite Galois extension
\widetilde{K}/k
with Galois group
G
in which all decomposition groups are cyclic.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, we may assume that

•

k
has characteristic
\ell
; and

•

n
is a multiple of
\ell
.

In particular,
k
is a function field. Let
\mathbb{F}
be the field of constants in
k
, and denote by
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
the unique finite field extension of
\mathbb{F}
of degree
n_{0}
. Then the field extension
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}k/k
is unramified and satisfies
\Gal(\mathbb{F}^{\prime}k/k)\cong\mathbb{Z}/n_{0}
. On the other hand, fix a finite separable field extension
k/\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(t)
. Then Chebotarev’s density theorem implies that there exists a place
v
of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(t)
that splits totally in
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}k
. Moreover, by the proof of
[
LOYY24
, Theorem A.2]
, we can take a cyclic field extension

of degree
\ell_{0}
which is ramified only at

. Then the fields
K_{0}
and
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}k
are linearly disjoint over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(t)
. Hence, if we set
\widetilde{K}:=K_{0}\mathbb{F}^{\prime}k
, then we obtain an isomorphism
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)\cong G
. Moreover, the assumption on
v
implies that all decomposition groups of
\widetilde{K}/k
are cyclic. This completes the proof in this case.

\blacksquare

By definition,

(ii)

Let

be a finite group, and

in
G
. Then the action of
G
on
G/H
, which is induced by left multiplication, gives an injection
G/N^{G}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. In particular,
G/N^{G}(H)
is a transitive group of degree
n
.

Proof.

(i): We may assume
H=\Stab_{G}(1)
. Consider the action of
G
on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For each
i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
, take an element
g_{i}
of
G
so that
g_{i}(1)=i
. Then there is a bijection
G/H\cong\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
. Hence we have the following as desired:

N^{G}(H)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}Hg_{i}^{-1}=\{1\}.

(ii): By definition, we obtain a homomorphism
\alpha\colon G\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. It suffices to prove
\Ker(\alpha)=N^{G}(H)
. By definition,
\Ker(\alpha)
is contained in the stabilizer of the point corresponding to
H
. Hence we obtain
\Ker(\alpha)\subset N^{G}(H)
. Take a complete representative
S
of
G/H
in
G
, then we have
N^{G}(H)\subset\bigcap_{s\in S}sHs^{-1}\subset\Ker(\alpha)
. Consequently, we obtain the desired equality.

\blacksquare

5.
Multinorm one tori and the multinorm principle

5.1.
Multinorm one tori

Let
F
be a field, and fix its separable closure
F^{\sep}
. Consider a finite étale algebra
\mathbf{E}
over
F
, that is, a finite product of finite seprable field extension of
F
that are contained in
F^{\sep}
. Then, the
multinorm one torus
associated to
\mathbf{E}/F
is defined as follows:

T_{\mathbf{E}/F}:=\{t\in\Res_{\mathbf{E}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}\mid\N_{\mathbf{E}/F}(t)=1\}.

If
\mathbf{E}
is a field, we call it the
norm one torus
.

Following
[
HKO25
, §3]
, we give a group-theoretic description of
X^{*}(T_{\mathbf{E}/F})
. Let
G
be a finite group, and
\mathcal{H}
a finite multiset of subgroups in
G
. Here, a finite multiset refers to a pair
(Z,m)
, where
Z
is a finite set, and

is a map from
Z
into
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We define a
G
-lattice

by the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow 0.

Here,
\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ}
is as in Proposition
2.6
, which is defined as
\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ}(1)(g)=1
for any
g\in G
.
If
\mathcal{H}=\{H\}
, we simply write
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
for

, and we call it the
Chevalley module
. Moreover, we denote
J_{G/H}
by

if
H=\{1\}
, .

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{E}=E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{r}
, where
E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}
are finite separable field extensions of
F
. Take a finite Galois extension
\widetilde{E}
of
F
that contains
E_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Set

G:=\Gal(\widetilde{E}/F),\quad\mathcal{H}:=\{\Gal(\widetilde{E}/E_{i})<G\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}.

Then, there is an isomorphism of
G
-lattices

X^{*}(T_{\mathbf{E}/F})\cong J_{G/\mathcal{H}}.

Proof.

Consider the canonical exact sequence

0\rightarrow T_{\mathbf{E}/F}\rightarrow\Res_{\mathbf{E}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbf{E}}\xrightarrow{\N_{\mathbf{E}/F}}\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow 1.

Taking character groups, we obtain an exact sequence of
G
-lattices

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow X^{*}(T_{\mathbf{E}/F})\rightarrow 0.

Hence, we obtain the desired assertion.

\blacksquare

5.3.
Cohomology groups: general theory

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
\mathcal{H}
a finite multiset of its subgroups. Then, there is an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{1}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/\mathcal{H},2}^{\circ*})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H^{2}(G,\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\Res_{G/H})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H^{3}(H,\mathbb{Z}).

The assertion follows from taking cohomology groups for the canonical exact sequence

Note that the rightmost homomorphisms follows from Lemma
2.1
.

\blacksquare

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
\mathcal{H}
a finite multiset of its subgroups. Then, for any admissible set of subgroups in

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}^{\red}}).

By Proposition

, there is an isomorphism between

Hence, the assertion follows from Lemma
2.11
.

\blacksquare

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
\mathcal{H}
a finite multiset of its subgroups. Then, the abelian group
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})
is annihilated by the great common divisor of
(G:H)
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.8
, we may assume
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\red}
. Pick

. By Proposition
5.4
, there is an isomorphism of

Then, the multiset

consists of

with

and

. This implies that

is the unique maximal element of
\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\set}
with respect to inclusion. Hence, Lemma
5.8
gives an isomorphism

\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(H,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})\cong\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(H,J_{H/H})=0.

Combining this with Proposition
2.8
(i), we obtain that
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})
is annihilated by
(G:H)
. This implies the desired assertion.

\blacksquare

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\mathcal{H}
a finite multiset of its subgroups, and
\mathcal{D}
an admissible set of subgroups in
G
. Then, we have

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}})=\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{(\mathcal{H})}}^{2}(G,J_{G/\mathcal{H}}),

where
\mathcal{D}_{(\mathcal{H})}
is defined as follows:

\mathcal{D}_{(\mathcal{H})}:=\mathcal{D}\setminus\{D\in\mathcal{C}_{G}\mid D\not\subset H\text{ for any }H\in\mathcal{H}\}.

Proof.

Pick
D\in\mathcal{C}_{G}
that satisfies
D\not\subset H
for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
. It suffices to prove

\Ker(\Res_{G/D})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\Ker(\Res_{G/D^{(i)}})

for some
D^{(1)},\ldots,D^{(m)}\in\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{C}_{H}
. By Proposition
2.8
(ii), the homomorphism

(\Res_{D/D_{p}})_{p\mid\#D}\colon H^{2}(D,J_{G/H})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{p\mid\#D}H^{2}(D_{p},J_{G/H})

is injective. This implies an equality

\Ker(\Res_{G/D})=\bigcap_{p\mid\#D}\Ker(\Res_{G/D_{p}}).

Hence, we may assume that
D
has prime power order. Pick
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and
g_{0}\in G
so that

\#(g_{0}Dg_{0}^{-1}\cap H_{0})=\max\{\#(gDg^{-1}\cap H)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid H\in\mathcal{H},g\in G\}.

Put
D^{\prime}:=g_{0}Dg_{0}^{-1}
. Then, Lemma
5.10
implies an equality

\Ker(\Res_{G/D})=\Ker(\Res_{G/D^{\prime}}).

On the other hand, Proposition
5.4
gives an isomorphism of
D^{\prime}
-lattices

J_{G/H}\cong J_{G/\mathcal{H}_{D^{\prime}}}.

Now,
\mathcal{H}_{D^{\prime}}
consists of subgroups of
D^{\prime}
of the form
D^{\prime}\cap gHg^{-1}
with
g\in R(D^{\prime},H)\subset G
. In particular, all elements of
\mathcal{H}_{D^{\prime}}
are contained in
D^{\prime}\cap H
, since
D
has prime power order. Hence, by Lemma
5.10
, the map
\Res_{D^{\prime}/D^{\prime}\cap H}
is injective. This gives an equality

\Ker(\Res_{G/D^{\prime}})=\Ker(\Res_{G/D^{\prime}\cap H}),

and hence the proof is complete.

\blacksquare

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
H
its subgroup, and
\mathcal{D}
an admissible set of subgroups in

. Then, for any prime divisor

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})[p^{\infty}]=\Sha_{\mathcal{D}_{(H,p)}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})[p^{\infty}],

\mathcal{D}_{(H,p)}

, and choose a

under the normality of a

-Sylow subgroup of

Theorem 6.17
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
G
a transitive group of degree
\in p\mathbb{Z}\setminus p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
, and
H
a corresponding subgroup of
G
. We further assume that a
p
-Sylow subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
is normal in

.

(i)

One has

if and only if

(a)

there is an isomorphism

;

(b)

[S_{p},G]=S_{p}
; and

(c)

N_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)=Z_{G}(S_{p}\cap H)
.

Conversely, assume that

and

of subgroups of

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})[p^{\infty}]\cong\begin{cases}0&\text{if there is $D\in\mathcal{D}$ which contains $S_{p}$;}\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

(ii)

\mathcal{D}
of subgroups of

Here

.

Lemma 7.3

Lemma 7.4

(
[
CF64
, I, pp. 142–143]
)
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number.

(i)

Assume

. Put

, and let
\zeta_{2^{s}}

-th root of unity. Put

X:=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{2^{s}}&0\\

0&\zeta_{2^{s}}\end{pmatrix},\quad Z:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then the subgroup
S_{p,2}
generated by
X
,
Y
and

is a
2
-Sylow subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(ii)

Suppose
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. Put
s:=\ord_{2}(p+1)
. Let
\zeta_{2^{s+1}}
be a
2^{s+1}
-th root of unity, which is contained in

X:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\zeta_{2^{s+1}}+\zeta_{2^{s+1}}^{p}\end{pmatrix},\quad Y:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\zeta_{2^{s+1}}+\zeta_{2^{s+1}}^{p}\end{pmatrix}.

Then the subgroup
S_{p,2}
generated by matrices
X
and
Y
is a
2
-Sylow subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Moreover, there are relations

X^{2^{s}}=\diag(-1,-1),\quad Y^{2}=1,\quad YXY^{-1}=X^{2^{s}-1}.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number, and
\overline{G}
a non-trivial
2
-subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then at least one of the following is satisfied:

(i)

\overline{G}
contains the matrix
\diag(-1,-1)
;

(ii)

\overline{G}\hookrightarrow C_{2^{s}}
and
\overline{G}
contains a conjugate of
\diag(1,-1)
, where
s:=\ord_{2}(p-1)
.

Proof.

By Sylow’s theorem, we may assume that
\overline{G}
is contained in
S_{p,2}
in Lemma
7.4
. Recall that
g\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is conjugate to
\diag(1,-1)
if and only if the characteristic polynomial of
g
is
x^{2}-1
.

Case 1.
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
.

Since the exponent of
S_{p,2}
equals
2^{s}
, it suffices to prove the following.

Claim
.

If
\overline{G}
does not contain
\diag(-1,-1)
, then it is cyclic and contains an element
S
whose characteristic polynomial equals
x^{2}-1
.

We shall prove Claim in the following. First, assume
\overline{G}\cap\langle X,Y\rangle=\{1\}
. Take
g\in\overline{G}\setminus\langle X,Y\rangle
and write
g=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{pmatrix}
. Then one has
\diag(bc,bc)=g^{2}=1
, and hence
bc=1
since
\overline{G}\cap\langle X,Y\rangle=\{1\}
. In particular, the characteristic polynomial of
g
equals
x^{2}-1
. Therefore Claim holds in this case.

Second, assume that
\overline{G}\cap\langle X,Y\rangle
is non-trivial. Then it contains
\diag(1,-1)
or
\diag(-1,1)
. Since
\diag(-1,1)=Z\diag(1,-1)Z^{-1}
, we may assume
\diag(1,-1)\in\overline{G}
, which implies
\diag(-1,1)\notin\overline{G}
. Now we prove
\overline{G}\subset\langle X,Y\rangle
, which gives the cyclicity of
\overline{G}
since
\langle X,Y\rangle\cong(C_{2^{s}})^{2}
. Suppose not, that is, there is a matrix
g\in\overline{G}\setminus\langle X,Y\rangle
. Write
g=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{pmatrix}
. Then we have
bc=1
since
\overline{G}
contains no non-trivial scalar matrix. However, one has

\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\
-c&0\end{pmatrix}=\diag(1,-1)g\in\overline{G}.

This implies
\diag(-1,-1)\in\overline{G}
since
bc=1
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we obtain
\overline{G}\subset\langle X,Y\rangle
as desired.

Case 2.
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
j\in\{0,1\}
. By Lemma
7.4
(ii), we have

(X^{i}Y^{j})^{2}=\begin{cases}X^{2i}&\text{if }n=0,\\
X^{2^{s}i}&\text{if }n=1.\end{cases}

Therefore,
\langle X^{i}Y^{j}\rangle
does not contain
\diag(-1,-1)
if and only if
i
is even and
j=1
. In particular, one has

(1)

\diag(-1,-1)\in\overline{G}
; or

(2)

\overline{G}=\langle X^{i}Y\rangle
for some
i\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.

Then (1) gives the validity of (i). On the other hand, since
X^{i}Y
is not a scalar matrix, the characteristic polynomial of
Y
equals
x^{2}-1
. Consequently, (2) gives the validity of (ii). This completes the proof of Corollary
7.5
.

\blacksquare

For a prime number

, consider two subgroups of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
as follows:

\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{F}_{p}):=\{\diag(a,b)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\},\quad\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{F}_{p}):=\{\diag(1,b)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\}.

.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number. Take

and

, where

. If

, then

is contained in
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proof.

Since the characteristic polynomials of

and
ghg^{-1}
coincide, we obtain an equality
ghg^{-1}=h
. Write

c&d\end{pmatrix}
, where

. Then the equality

is equivalent to the following:

\begin{pmatrix}a&eb\\
c&ed\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
ec&ed\end{pmatrix}.

Hence

implies

, that is,
g
is contained in
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

\blacksquare

Corollary 7.7
.

Let

\overline{G}

of

. Write

, where

. Take any

(ii): Note that we have

\blacksquare

.

Let
p
be a prime number,
G^{\prime}
a finite group of order coprime to

H^{\prime}

\overline{H}:=\rho(H^{\prime})

Let

N^{\overline{G}}(\overline{H})\subset\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

The equality

. On the other hand, since

Exponential lower bound via exponential sums Thanks: This is an extended version of the ICALP 2024 paper [ 3 ] .
Markus Bläser, Somnath Bhattacharjee, Pranjal Dutta, Saswata Mukherjee

Abstract
Valiant’s famous 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} vs. 𝖵𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{VNP} conjecture states that the symbolic permanent polynomial does not have polynomial-size algebraic circuits. However, the best upper bound on the size of the circuits computing the permanent is exponential. Informally, 𝖵𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{VNP} is an exponential sum of 𝖵𝖯 \mathsf{VP} -circuits. In this paper we study whether, in general, exponential sums (of algebraic circuits) require exponential-size algebraic circuits. We show that the famous Shub-Smale τ \tau -conjecture indeed implies such an exponential lower bound for an exponential sum. Our main tools come from parameterized complexity. Along the way, we also prove an exponential fpt (fixed-parameter tractable) lower bound for the parameterized algebraic complexity class 𝖵𝖶 nb 0 ​ [ 𝖯 ] \mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}[\mathsf{P}] , assuming the same conjecture. 𝖵𝖶 nb 0 ​ [ 𝖯 ] \mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}[\mathsf{P}] can be thought of as the weighted sums of (unbounded-degree) circuits, where only ± 1 \pm 1 constants are cost-free . To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time the Shub-Smale τ \tau -conjecture has been applied to prove explicit exponential lower bounds. Furthermore, we prove that when this class is fpt, then a variant of the counting hierarchy, namely the linear counting hierarchy collapses. Moreover, if a certain type of parameterized exponential sums is fpt, then integers, as well as polynomials with coefficients being definable in the linear counting hierarchy have subpolynomial τ \tau -complexity. Finally, we characterize a related class 𝖵𝖶 ⁡ [ 𝖥 ] \mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}] , in terms of permanents, where we consider an exponential sum of algebraic formulas instead of circuits. We show that when we sum over cycle covers that have one long cycle and all other cycles have constant length, then the resulting family of polynomials is complete for 𝖵𝖶 ⁡ [ 𝖥 ] \mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}] on certain types of graphs.

1.2
Proof ideas

In this section, we briefly sketch the proof ideas. The omitted proofs of the paper can be found in the longer arxiv version of the paper. We first present the proofs of
Theorem

Proof idea of
Theorem

1.4
.
We prove them in two parts.

Proof of Part (1):
We prove even a stronger statement for the subexponential version of the linear counting hierarchy. The proof goes via induction on the level of the counting hierarchy. The criteria for some language
B
being in the
(k+1)
-th level are that there should be some language
A
in the
k
-th level such that
|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\in A\}|>2^{n-1}
. Essentially, for a language
A
in the
k
-th level, we express
|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\in A\}|>2^{n-1}
as an exponential sum over an algebraic circuit
\chi_{A}(x,y)
, which captures the characteristic function of
A
. Furthermore, one can show that
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
is fpt (in an unbounded constant-free setting) iff
\sum_{y}g(\mathbf{X},y)
has
2^{o(n)}{\rm poly}(m)
size circuits, where
g
has a circuit of size
m
; see
Theorem

4.3
and
4.4
. Putting these together, one gets that the exponential sum has a subexponential-size constant-free circuit. Lastly, we want to get the information about the highest bit of the sum (which is equivalent to looking at it
\bmod\penalty\ 2^{n}
), which can be efficiently
arithmetized
. In every step there is polynomial blowup in the size, and hence the size remains subexponential, yielding the desired result. For details, see
Theorem

4.1
.

Proof of Part (2):
This proof is an adaption of
[
9
,
19
]
in our context. Take a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}\in\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. We define a multilinear polynomial

such that the coefficient of

is the

-th bit of

, where
\mathbf{j}
is the binary representation of

. Furthermore, checking

can be done by a subexponential circuit

, where
\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{J}
have
\log n
and

-many variables capturing

and

respectively. Moreover, one can define

and show that

instance, which finally yields that the

is subpolynomial. A similar proof strategy also holds for the polynomials with coefficients being definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}

1.3
Previous results

To prove (conditional) exponential lower bounds, the standard assumptions
that
\mathsf{P}\not=\mathsf{NP}
or
\mathsf{VP}\not=\mathsf{VNP}
are not enough. It is
consistent with our current knowledge that for instance
\mathsf{P}\not=\mathsf{NP}
,
but
\mathsf{NP}
-hard problems can have subexponential time algorithms.
What we need is a complexity assumption
stating that certain problems can only be solved in exponential time.
This is the exponential time hypothesis (ETH) in the Boolean setting.
Dell et al.
[
11
]
studied the exponential time complexity of the permanent,
they prove that when there is an algorithm for computing the permanent in time

2^{o(n)}
, then this violates the counting version of the exponential time hypothesis
#ETH. #ETH states that there is a constant
c
such that no deterministic algorithm
can count the number of satisfying assignments of a formula in
3
-CNF in time

2^{cn}
. For connections between
parameterized and subexponential complexity in the Boolean setting, we refer to
[
13
,
14
]
.

Bläser and Engels
[
4
]
transfer the important definitions and results from parameterized complexity in the Boolean world to define a theory of parameterized algebraic complexity classes. In particular, they define the
\mathsf{VW}
-hierarchy and prove that the
clique polynomial and the
k
-permanent are
\mathsf{VW}[1]
-complete (under so-called
fpt-substitutions). They also claim the hardness of the restricted
permanent for the class
\mathsf{VW}[t]
for every constant
t
and sketch a proof.
Note that
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}]
contains each
\mathsf{VW}[t]
. So we strengthen the hardness proof
in
[
4
]
and complement it with an upper bound.

The main tool used by Bürgisser
[
9
]
to prove the results above is the counting hierarchy.
The polynomial counting hierarchy was introduced by Wagner
[
29
]
to classify the complexity of Boolean counting problems.
The fact that small circuits for the
permanent collapses the counting hierarchy is used by Bürgisser to prove the
results mentioned above.

Finally, there have been quite a few works
[
9
,
19
,
18
,
20
]
, where we have conditional separations on the constant-free version of
\mathsf{VP}
and
\mathsf{VNP}
, namely
\mathsf{VP}^{0}
and
\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
, or their variants, depending on the strength of the conjecture. But this is the first time that we are separating algebraic classes and proving exponential lower bounds, assuming the
\tau
-conjecture.

constant-free

\{-1,0,1\}
are used for labeling in the circuit. Clearly, constant-free circuits can
only
compute polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{X}]
. For
f(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{X}]
,
\tau(f)
is the size of a minimum size constant-free circuit that computes
f
, while
L(f)
denotes the minimum size circuit that computes
f
. It is noteworthy to observe that,
unlike
Valiant’s classical models, computing integers in the constant-free model can be costly; e.g.,
\tau(2^{2^{n}}X^{n})=\Omega(n)
, while
L(2^{2^{n}}X^{n})=\Theta(\log n)
. On the other hand, for any
f\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{X}]
,
L(f)\leq\tau(f)
.

Before defining the constant-free Valiant classes, we formalize the notion of
formal degree
of a node, denoted
\textup{formal-deg}(\cdot)
. It is defined recursively as follows: (1) the formal degree of an input gate is
1
, (2) if
u=v+w
, then
\textup{formal-deg}(u)=\max(\textup{formal-deg}(v),\textup{formal-deg}(w))
, and (3) if
u=v\times w
, then
\textup{formal-deg}(u)=\textup{formal-deg}(v)+\textup{formal-deg}(w)
. The
formal degree of a circuit is defined as the formal degree of its output node.

The class
constant-free Valiant’s
\mathsf{P}
, denoted by
\mathsf{VP}^{0}
, contains all
p\text{-}\mathsf{families}

(f)
in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{X}]
, such that
\textup{formal-deg}(f)
and
\tau(f)
are both
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
. Analogously,
\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
contains all
p\text{-}\mathsf{families}

(f_{n})
, such that there exists a
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q(n)
and
(g_{n})\in\mathsf{VP}^{0}
, where

f_{n}(\mathbf{X})\;=\;\sum_{\overline{y}\in\{0,1\}^{q(n)}}g_{n}(\mathbf{X},y_{1},\dots,y_{q(n)})\;.

It is not clear whether showing
\mathsf{VP}^{0}\neq\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
implies
\mathsf{VP}\neq\mathsf{VNP}
, it is
not even clear
whether
\mathsf{VP}^{0}\neq\mathsf{VNP}^{0}

\implies

\tau(\operatorname{per}_{n})=n^{\omega(1)}
. The
subtlety
here is that
in the algebraic completeness proof for the permanent,
divisions by two
occur! However, a partial implication is known due to
[
9
, Theorem 2.10]
: Showing
\tau(2^{p(n)}f_{n})=n^{\omega(1)}
, for some
f_{n}\in\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
and all
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}p(n)
would imply that
\tau(\operatorname{per}_{n})=n^{\omega(1)}
.

Arithmetization
is a well-known technique in complexity theory.
To arithmetize a Boolean circuit
C
computing a Boolean function
\varphi
,
we use the arithmetization technique wherein we map
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
to a polynomial
p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that for any assignment of Boolean values
v_{i}\in\{0,1\}
to the
x_{i}
,
\varphi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=p(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
holds.

We define the arithmetization map
\Gamma
for variables
x_{i}
, and clauses
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
, as follows:

1.

x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
,

2.

\neg x_{i}\mapsto 1-x_{i}
,

3.

c_{1}\lor\cdots\lor c_{m}\mapsto 1-\prod_{i\in[m]}(1-\Gamma(c_{i}))
,

4.

c_{1}\land\cdots\land c_{m}\mapsto\prod_{i\in[m]}\Gamma(c_{i})
.

This map allows us to transform
C
into an arithmetic circuit for
p
.
For a Boolean circuit
C
, we denote the arithmetized circuit by
\mathsf{arithmetize}(C)
. Here, we remark that the degree of
\mathsf{arithmetize}(C)
can become
exponentially
large; this is because there is no known depth-reduction for Boolean circuits, and hence the degree may double at each step, owing to an exponential blowup in the degree.

Valiant’s classes in the unbounded setting:

It is well-known that an algebraic circuit of size
s
, can compute polynomials of degree
\exp(s)
; e.g.,
f(x)=x^{2^{s}}
, and
L(f)=O(s)
. This brings us to the next definition, the class
\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}
, originally defined in
[
22
]
. A sequence of polynomials
(f)=(f_{n})_{n}\in\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}
, if the number of variables in
f_{n}
and
L(f_{n})
are both
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
(the degree
may be
exponentially large). The subscript “nb” signifies the
“not bounded”
phenomenon on the degree of the polynomial, in contrast to the original class
\mathsf{VP}
. Similarly, a sequence of polynomials
(f)=(f_{n})_{n}\in\mathsf{VNP}_{\textup{nb}}
, if there exists a
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q(n)
and
g_{n}(\mathbf{X},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q(n)})\in\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}
where

f_{n}(\mathbf{X})\;=\;\displaystyle\sum_{\overline{y}\in\{0,1\}^{q(n)}}\,g_{n}(\mathbf{X},y_{1},\dots,y_{q(n)})\;.

One can analogously define
\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
and
\mathsf{VNP}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
, in the constant-free setting. It is obvious that
\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}=\mathsf{VNP}_{\textup{nb}}
implies
\mathsf{VP}=\mathsf{VNP}
, but the converse is
unclear
. However,
[
22
]
showed that over a ring of positive characteristic, the converse holds, i.e.,
\mathsf{VP}=\mathsf{VNP}
implies
\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}=\mathsf{VNP}_{\textup{nb}}
! On the other hand,
[
18
]
showed that
\mathsf{VP}^{0}=\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
implies that
\mathsf{VP}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}=\mathsf{VNP}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
, and the converse is unclear because it seems difficult to rule out the possibility that some polynomial family in
\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
does not lie in
\mathsf{VP}^{0}
, but still in
\mathsf{VP}
(i.e., computable by
polynomial-size algebraic circuits using
exponentially large-bit
integers).

2.2
Parameterized Valiant’s classes

Parameterized Valiant’s classes were introduced in
[
4
]
.
We will briefly review the definitions and results there and extend them to the constant-free
and unbounded setting.
We first start with the fixed-parameter tractable classes.
The
W
-hierarchies will be introduced later since we only need them in the
second part of this work.

Our families of polynomials will now have two indices. They will be of the form
(p_{n,k})

is the index of the family and

is the parameter. We will say a polynomial family

is a

if the number of variables is
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
in

in

The most natural parameterization is by the degree: Let
(p_{n})
be any
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}
then we get a parameterized family
(p_{n,k})
by setting

the homogeneous part of degree

. For more details, we will refer the reader to
[
4
]
.

We now define fixed-parameter variants of Valiant’s classes with the constant-free version.

Definition 2.1

(Algebraic FPT classes)
.

1.

A parameterized
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}

(p_{n,k})
is in
\mathsf{VFPT}

is upper bounded by
f(k)q(n)
for some
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q
and some function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
(such bound will be called an fpt bound). If one removes the requirement of
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}
on
p_{n,k}
, and imposes only that the number of variables is
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
, one gets the class
\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}
.

2.

A parameterized
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}

p_{n,k}
is in
\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}
iff
\tau(p_{n,k})
is upper bounded by
f(k)q(n)
for some constant
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q
and some function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
. Similarly, one gets
\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
, if one removes the requirement of
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}
, and imposes only that the number of variables is
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
.

We remark that in the above,
f
need not be computable as Valiant’s model is non-uniform.

Definition 2.2

(Fpt-projection)
.

A parameterized family
f=(f_{n,k})
is an fpt-projection of another parameterized family

if there are functions
r,s,t:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
r
is
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
,
s,t
are computable and
f_{n,k}
is a projection of
g_{r(n)s(k),k^{\prime}}
for some
k^{\prime}\leq t(k)
,
2
2

2

k^{\prime}
might depend on
n
, but its size is bounded by a function in
k
. There are examples in the Boolean world, where this dependence on
n
is used.
. We write
f\leq^{\textup{fpt}}_{p}g
.

However p-projection in Valiant’s world seems to be
weaker
compared to parsimonious poly-time reduction in the Boolean world; therefore we need a stronger notion of reduction for defining algebraic models of the Boolean
\#W
-classes, see
[
4
]
.
That’s why we are defining substitutions. We will analogously define it for constant-free model as well.

Definition 2.3

(Fpt-substitution)
.

1.

A parameterized family
f=(f_{n,k})
is an fpt-substitution of another parameterized family
g=(g_{n,k})
if there are functions
r,s,t,u:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and polynomials
h_{1},\dots,h_{u(r(n)s(k))}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{X}]
with both

and deg
(h_{i})

are
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
,

are computable functions, and

. We write
f\leq^{\textup{fpt}}_{s}g
. When we allow

(i.e. only

is fpt-bounded), we say that

is an
\textup{fpt}_{\textup{nb}}
-substitution of

. We denote this as
f\leq^{\textup{fpt}_{\textup{nb}}}_{s}g
.

2.

A parameterized family
f=(f_{n,k})
is a constant-free fpt-substitution of another parameterized family

if there are functions
r,s,t,u:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and polynomials

and deg
(h_{i})

,

-substitutions, denoted as

One can define constant-free fpt-projections analogously.

]

.

Lemma 2.4
.

\mathsf{VFPT},\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}

3
Linear counting hierarchy

In this section, we define the linear counting hierarchy,
a variant of the counting hierarchy, which will allow us to talk
about subexponential complexity.
The original counting hierarchy was defined by Wagner
[
29
]
.
We here restrict the witness length to be linear, which is important when dealing
with exponential complexity.
Allender et al.
[
2
]
also define
a linear counting hierarchy.
Their definition is not comparable to ours. We use an operator-based definition:
The base class is deterministic polynomial time and the witness length is linearly bounded.
Allender et al. use an oracle TM definition: The oracle Turing machine is probabilistic
and linear time bounded, which automatically bounds the query lengths.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a complexity class
K
, we define
\mathbf{C}.K
to be the class of all languages
A
such that there is some
B\in K
and a function
p:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
,
p(n)=O(n^{c})
for some constant
c
, and some polynomial time computable function
f:\{0,1\}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that,

x\in A\iff|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{p(|x|)}:\langle x,y\rangle\in B\}|>f(x).

We start from
\mathsf{C}_{0}\mathsf{P}:=\mathsf{P}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{C}_{k+1}\mathsf{P}:=\mathbf{C}.\mathsf{C}_{k}\mathsf{P}
. Then the
counting hierarchy
is defined as
\mathsf{CH}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathsf{C}_{k}\mathsf{P}
.
We now define our linear counting hierarchy:

Definition 3.2
.

Given a complexity class
K
, we define
\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
to be the class of all languages
A
such that there is some
B\in K
and a function
\ell:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
,
\ell(n)=O(n)
, and some polynomial time computable function
f:\{0,1\}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that,

x\in A\iff|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}:\langle x,y\rangle\in B\}|>f(x).

We define
\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{0}\mathsf{P}:=\mathsf{P}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k+1}\mathsf{P}:=\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{P}
. The
linear counting hierarchy

is
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{P}
.

Now, we slightly modify the above definition to get
\exists_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
and
\forall_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
in the following way:
x\in A\iff\exists y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}:\langle x,y\rangle\in B
and
x\in A\iff\forall y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}:\langle x,y\rangle\in B
, respectively. Clearly, it can be said that
K\subseteq\exists_{\mathsf{lin}}.K\subseteq\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
and
K\subseteq\forall_{\mathsf{lin}}.K\subseteq\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
.

We can define the linear counting hierarchy in a slightly easier manner.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a complexity class
K
, we define
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
to be the class of all languages
A
such that there is some
B\in K
and a function
\ell:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
,
\ell(n)=O(n)
, such that

x\in A\iff|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}:\langle x,y\rangle\in B\}|>2^{\ell(|x|)-1}

It is clear that
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K\subseteq\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
for any class
K
. Moreover, by an easy adaption of the proof of
[
26
, Lemma 3.3]
, for any language
K\in\mathsf{CH}
,
\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{lin}}.K
. Also, from the definition, we can say that
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}\subseteq\mathsf{CH}
. Therefore, the following holds.

Fact 3.4
.

\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k+1}\mathsf{P}\;=\;\mathbf{C}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{lin}}.\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{P}
.

We also need a subexponential version of the counting hierarchy.
Let
\mathsf{SUBEXP}=\mathsf{DTime}(2^{o(n)})
.
Then we set
\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{0}\mathsf{SUBEXP}=\mathsf{SUBEXP}
and for all

k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k+1}\mathsf{SUBEXP}:=\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}.\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
.
Moreover,
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{SUBEXP}=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
.

Here we define a few more terms that we shall use later in Section
5
.
We set
\mathsf{NP}_{\mathsf{lin}}=\exists_{\mathsf{lin}}.\mathsf{P}
,
\mathsf{NP}

with linear witness size. In the same way, we can define
the levels of the linear polynomial time hierarchy,

\Sigma^{\mathsf{lin}}_{i}
and
\Pi^{\mathsf{lin}}_{i}
, by applying the
operators
\exists_{\mathsf{lin}}
and
\forall_{\mathsf{lin}}

in an alternating fashion to
\mathsf{P}
.
The linear polynomial hierarchy
\mathsf{PH}_{\mathsf{lin}}
is the
union over all
\Sigma^{\mathsf{lin}}_{i}
.

From the above definitions, we get the following conclusion.

Fact 3.5
.

\mathsf{NP}_{\mathsf{lin}}\subseteq\mathsf{PH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\subseteq\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}.

4
Connecting Valiant’s model to the counting hierarchy

In this section, we aim to prove that subexponential upper bounds
for exponential sums
imply a collapse of the linear counting hierarchy
(for a definition, see
Section

3
).
To show this, we will define a polynomial family
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
and show that

\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
is equivalent to
exponential sums having subexponential circuits (
Corollary

8.6
).

\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
will imply
a collapse of the linear counting hierarchy (
Theorem

4.1
).

4.1
\log
-variate exponential sum polynomial family

In this section, we will define a parameterized
\log
-variate exponential sum polynomial family,

\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}_{m,k}(g)\;:=\;\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}\,g_{n}(\mathbf{X},y)\;,

where
\mathbf{X}
has
n
variables,

\ell(n)=O(n)
, and
g_{n}
has circuits of size
m
(
n=\Omega(\log m)
),
and the parameter is
k=\frac{n}{\log m}
.

m
and
k
are functions of
n
. Note that the running parameter of the family is
m
. When we write
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}
,
we mean that
\{\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}_{m,k}(g)\}_{m,k}\in\mathsf{VFPT}
for all families
g
.
We are allowing
g
to have
unbounded
degree, i.e.,
g
may not necessarily be a
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}
. We will also be using constant-free circuits computing
g
in the constant-free context.

4.2
Collapsing of
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{SUBEXP}

The main theorem of the section is the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

If
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
, then for every language
L
in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
, we have a constant-free algebraic circuit
\chi_{L}
so that
x\in L\implies\chi_{L}(x)=1
,
x\notin L\implies\chi_{L}(x)=0
and
\chi_{L}
has size
2^{o(n)}
.

Proof.

We prove the above statement by induction on the level of
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
. By definition,
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{SUBEXP}=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
. For
k=0
,
\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{SUBEXP}=\mathsf{SUBEXP}
.
Now by standard arithmetization, we can get a
2^{o(n)}
size, unbounded degree constant-free circuit for each
L\in\mathsf{SUBEXP}
, so that the above-mentioned condition holds.

Now, by induction hypothesis say, it is true up to
k
-th level of the hierarchy. We will prove that it is true for the
(k+1)
-th level.
Take any
B\in\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k+1}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
. By
3.4
and
Definition

3.3
, there exists
A\in\mathsf{C\text{-}lin}_{k}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
such that

x\in B\;\iff\;|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}\;:\;\langle x,y\rangle\in A\}|\;>\;2^{\ell(|x|)-1}\;,

where
\ell
is some linear polynomial. By slight abuse of notation, let
\chi_{A}
denote an algebraic circuit capturing the characteristic function for
A
, i.e.,

\chi_{A}(x,y)\;=\;1\;\iff\;\langle x,y\rangle\in A\;.

By the induction hypothesis, we can assume that
\chi_{A}

has size
2^{o(|x|)}
.
Now, one can equivalently write the following:

x\in B\;\iff\;\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}}\,\chi_{A}(x,y)\;>\;2^{\ell(|x|)-1}\;.

In this way, we get an instance of
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
,
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}}\chi_{A}(x,y)
, where the size of
\chi_{A}
is
m=2^{o(|x|)}
and it computes a polynomial of
unbounded degree
(there is no depth-reduction known for Boolean circuits and thus, it cannot be reduced).

As
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}_{\mathsf{nb}}
, there is an algebraic circuit
C
such that
C(x):=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}}\chi_{A}(x,y)
and
C
has
subexponential-size by Theorem
4.3
.

Trivially,
\tau(2^{\ell(|x|)-1})\leq{\rm poly}(|x|)
. So, we can make
C
first constant-free and then Boolean by the standard procedure of computing on the binary representation modulo
2^{\ell(n)}
.
Let
\tilde{C}
is the Boolean circuit that computes the highest bit. We just arithmetize
\tilde{C}
and take
\chi_{B}=\mathsf{arithmetize}(\tilde{C})
. Each time we convert the arithmetic circuit to a Boolean one and arithmetize the Boolean circuit, we incur only a small polynomial blow-up in size. Therefore,
\chi_{B}
has subexponential-size, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Clearly,
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}\subseteq\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{SUBEXP}
and hence,
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
implies that every language in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
has subexponential-size constant-free algebraic circuits.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
, then
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)
has
circuits of size
2^{o(n)}{\rm poly}(m)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
has circuits of size
f(n/\log m){\rm poly}(m)
.
We can assume that
f
is an increasing function. Let

i(n)=\max(\{1\}\cup\{j\mid f(j)\leq n\})
.

i(n)
is nondecreasing and unbounded. Moreover,
f(i(n))\leq n
for all
but finitely many
n
.

We will prove that
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)
has
circuits of size
2^{n/i(n)}{\rm poly}(m)
. If
m\geq 2^{n/i(n)}
,
then
f(n/\log m)\leq f(i(n))\leq n
, thus there are circuits
of size
n\cdot{\rm poly}(m)={\rm poly}(m)
. If
m<2^{n/i(n)}
,
then let
\hat{m}=2^{n/i(n)}
. We can take a circuit
C
for
g

and pad it to a circuit
\hat{C}
of size
s
with
\hat{m}\leq s\leq O(\hat{m})
,
such that
\hat{C}
has the same variables as
C
. Then
let
\hat{k}=n/\log\hat{m}
.
Thus,
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)
has
circuits of size
f(\hat{k}){\rm poly}(\hat{m})=n\cdot{\rm poly}(2^{n/i(n)})
.
∎

We will need the unbounded version as stated above, but a similar proof also works for the bounded case. The same is true of the
non-constant-free version.
We will also need the following converse direction:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)

have circuits of size
2^{o(n)}{\rm poly}(m)
for each
g
of size
m
. Then

\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{n}
be a circuit for
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)

of size
2^{O(n/i(n))}{\rm poly}(m)
for some nondecreasing and unbounded function
i
.
Let
f
be a nondecreasing function such that
f(i(n))\geq 2^{n}
.
We claim that
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
has circuits of size
f(k){\rm poly}(m)

with
k=n/\log m
.
If
m\geq 2^{n/i(n)}
, then
C_{n}
has size
{\rm poly}(m)\leq f(k){\rm poly}(m)
.
Otherwise,
k=n/\log m\geq i(n)
and therefore
f(k)\geq 2^{n}
.
Thus, the trivial circuit for
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)

has size
f(k){\rm poly}(m)
.
∎

5
Integers definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}

In
[
9
, Section 3]
, integers are studied that are definable
in the counting hierarchy. We adapt this notation to the

linear
counting hierarchy.
Formally, we are given a sequence of integers
(a(n,k))_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq q(n)}
for some
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
. We can assume that
|a(n,k)|\leq 2^{n^{c}}
for some constant
c
. In other words, the bit-size of
a(n,k)
is at most
exponential
, as we think
n,k
has been represented in binary by
O(\log n)
bits. Now consider two languages,

\displaystyle\mathsf{sgn}(a)

\displaystyle:=\{(n,k):a(n,k)\geq 0\}\;\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Bit}(|a|)

\displaystyle:=\{(n,k,j,b):j\text{th bit of }|a(n,k)|\text{ is }b\}\;.

Here in both of these two languages,
n,k,j
are given in binary representation.

Definition 5.1
.

We say an integer sequence
(a(n,k))_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq q(n)}
for some
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
whenever both of
\mathsf{sgn}(a)
and
\mathsf{Bit}(|a|)
are in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

Chinese remainder language:
Now, we define another language and make a connection to the definition of an integer sequence to be definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
, via the
Chinese remainder representation
. Given that the bit-size of
a(n,k)
is at most
n^{c}
, we consider the set of all primes
p<n^{2c}
. The product of all such primes is
>2^{n^{c}}
. Therefore, from
a(n,k)\bmod p
, for all primes
p<n^{2c}
, we can recover
a(n,k)
. Consider

\mathsf{CR}(a)\;:=\;\left\{(n,k,p,j,b):p\text{ prime},\,p\,<\,n^{2c},\,j\text{-th bit of}\penalty\ (a(n,k)\,\bmod\penalty\ p)\text{ is }b\right\}\;.

Now we show an essential criterion for a sequence to be in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. It is an adaption with some additional modifications and observations from
[
16
]
, which were further implemented in
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(a(n,k))_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq q(n)}
be a integer sequence of exponential bit-size

(|a(n,k)|<2^{n^{c}})
. Then,
(a(n,k))
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
iff both
\mathsf{sgn}(a)
and
\mathsf{CR}(a)
are in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

Proof.

Our argument goes similar to
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
, with some further
modifications.

At first, let us show that for nonnegative sequences,
(a)
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}\iff\mathsf{CR}(a)\in\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. To show the
\Rightarrow
direction, start with a Dlogtime-uniform
\mathsf{TC}^{0}
circuit family
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n}
which computes the Chinese remainder representation of an
n
-bit number, modulo primes
p<n^{2}
, from its binary representation. By
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
,
\mathcal{C}_{n}
has size
{\rm poly}(n)
and constant depth
D
. Consider the language

L_{d}\;:=\;\left\{(n,k,F,b):\text{ on input }a(n,k)\text{, gate }F\text{ of }\mathcal{C}_{n^{c}}\text{ at depth }d\text{ computes bit }b\right\}\;,

for
d\in\{0,\dots,D\}
and
(n,k,F)
are given by their binary encoding.
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
shows that
L_{d+1}\in\mathbf{C}^{\prime}.L_{d}
. But in fact we can say
even stronger
that
L_{d+1}\in\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{lin}}^{\prime}.{L_{d}}
. This is because when we are given
(n,k,F,b)
as input by their binary encoding and
F
is some majority gate at depth
d+1
, we need to check if
(n,k,G,1)
is in
L_{d}
for all gates
G
at depth
d
connected to
F
. The lengths
of the witnesses
(n,k,G,1)
is
O(\log n)
, which is linear in the input.
By Dlogtime-uniformity of
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n}
, we can check if
G
is connected to
F
in polynomial time. And computing the majority of at most
{\rm poly}(n)
many gates can be done by checking

\left|\{G\mid\text{$G$ connected to $F$ and}(n,k,G,1)\in L_{d}\}\right|\;>\;2^{\ell(\log n)-1}\;,

for some linear function
\ell
. Hence, our claim is true. The rest of the proof and the other direction is similar to the argument given in
[
9
]
.
Hence,
(a)
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}\iff\mathsf{CR}(a)\in\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

If
(a)
might have negative entries, then on both sides, we simply add the
statement “
\mathsf{sgn}(a)\in\mathsf{CH}_{\mathrm{lin}}\mathsf{P}
” (on the left hand side implicitly in the definition of definable).
∎

Now, we can prove an important
closure
property of non-negative integers definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
, which we shall use later.

Theorem 5.3

(Closure properties)
.

Let
(a(n,k))_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq q(n)}
be a non-negative integer sequence for some
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function
q:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with
a(n,k)
having bit-size
<{n^{c}}
and it is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. Consider the sum and product of
a(n,k)
defined as follows:

b(n)\;:=\;\sum_{k=0}^{q(n)}\,a(n,k)\,\;\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\;c(n)\;:=\;\prod_{k=0}^{q(n)}\,a(n,k)\;.

Then, both of
(b(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(c(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

Proof.

The proof is again similar to
[
9
, Theorem 3.10]
.

Part (i):
(b(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

By
[
28
]
, we know that iterated addition of
n
many numbers
0\leq X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\leq 2^{n}
, given in their binary representation, can be done by Dlogtime-uniform
\mathsf{TC}^{0}
circuits. Say this circuit family is
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n}
.
\mathcal{C}_{n}
has
{\rm poly}(n)
size and constant depth
D
. Now, we can take some
\mathcal{C}_{n^{c^{\prime}}}
for some suitable constant
c^{\prime}
and using the idea same as in Theorem
5.2
, we can say that
(b(n):=\sum_{k=0}^{q(n)}a(n,k))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. Note that while we are summing a polynomial number
of numbers, the bit-size for addressing the elements of
a(n,k)

is
\log n+\log k=O(\log n)
, which is linear in the input size.

Part (ii):
(c(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

We first find a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
for a prime
p
,
p<n^{2c}
.
The smallest generator
g
can be characterized by

\forall\;1\leq i<p,\ g^{i}\neq 1\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\;1\leq\hat{g}<g,\;\exists\;1\leq j<p,\ \hat{g}^{j}=1.

The inner checks
g^{i}\neq 1
and
\hat{g}^{j}=1
can be done in polynomial time
(in
\log n
) by repeated squaring. So checking whether a given
g
is
the smallest generator can be done in (the second level of)
\mathsf{PH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

Also, given
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
and a generator
g
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, finding
1\leq i<p
so that
u=g^{i}
can be done in
\exists_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.
Note that,

c(n)\penalty\ \bmod\penalty\ p\;=\prod_{k=0}^{q(n)}\,a(n,k)\;\bmod\penalty\ p\;=\;g^{\sum_{k=0}^{q(n)}\alpha(k,n)}\;.

Finding
g
and
\alpha(n,k)
is in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
, by the above argument. Moreover, the previous part of the proof also shows that
\sum_{k=0}^{q(n)}\alpha(n,k)
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. Therefore,
(c(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Take
a(n,k):=\sigma_{n,k}(1,\dots,n)
,
k\leq n
, where
\sigma_{n,k}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial on variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
. Then,
(a(n,k))_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq n}
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

Proof.

Consider the polynomial

F_{n}(X)\;:=\;(X+1)\dots(X+n)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{n}\,a(n,k)\cdot X^{n-k}\;.

Substituting
X
by
2^{n^{2}}
, we get that

d(n)\;:=\;\prod_{j=1}^{n}(2^{n^{2}}+j)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{n}a(n,k)\cdot 2^{n^{2}(n-k)}\;.

And as
a(n,k)<2^{n^{2}}
, there is
no overlap
in the bit representations. Hence, it is enough to show that
(d(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. And by
Theorem

5.3
, we only need to prove that
(e(n,k):=2^{n^{2}}+k)_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq n}
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
, which is indeed true.
∎

6
Connecting the counting hierarchy to the
\tau
-conjecture

In this section, we connect the
\tau
-conjecture to the counting hierarchy. Specifically, we show that the collapse of
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
implies that some explicit polynomial, whose coefficients are definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
, is “easy”. Formally, we prove the following
theorem:

Theorem 6.1
.

Say,
(a(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(b(n,k))_{k\leq q(n),n\in\mathbb{N}}
are both definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
. Here
q
is some
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function. If
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
then the following holds:

1.

\tau(a(n))=n^{o(1)}
,

2.

If
f_{n}(X)\;:=\;\sum_{k=1}^{q(n)}b(n,k)X^{k}
then
\tau(f_{n})=n^{o(1)}
.

Proof.

We can assume that if
a(n)
is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
,
|a(n)|\leq 2^{n^{c}}
, that is, the bit-size of any integer definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
is polynomially bounded. Furthermore,
if
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}
, then every language in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
has subexponential-size circuits by
Theorem

4.1
. We will use both facts below.

Proof of part (1).
Let
a(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{p(n)}a(n,j)2^{j}
be the binary decomposition of
a(n)
and
p(n)=O(n^{c})
. Define a new polynomial:

A_{\lceil\log n\rceil}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{\mathsf{bit}(n)})\;:=\;\sum_{j=0}^{p(n)}a(n,j)Y_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots Y_{\mathsf{bit}(n)}^{j_{\mathsf{bit}(n)}}\;,

where
\mathsf{bit}(n)\,:=\,\lceil\log(p(n))\rceil
.
By our assumption, we can decide if
a(n,j)=b
by a subexponential-size circuit, given input
n
and
j
in binary. Say,
C_{r}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{J})
is the corresponding circuit, where

r=\lfloor\log n\rfloor
. We have

C_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{\lfloor\log n\rfloor+1},j_{1},\dots,j_{\mathsf{bit}(n)})=a(n,j)
, where the
n_{i}
’s and the
j_{i}
’s are the bits of
n
and
j
, respectively. Consider the polynomial

F_{r}(J_{1},\dots,J_{cr+1},N_{1},\dots,N_{r+1},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{cr+1})\;:=\;C_{r}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{J})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{cr+1}(J_{i}Y_{i}+1-J_{i})\;.

Now, by our assumption and Theorem
4.1
, we can say that
F_{r}
has
2^{o(r)}

size constant-free algebraic circuits (of unbounded degree). Consider the
exponential sum

\tilde{F}_{r}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{Y})\;:=\;\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}^{cr+1}}\,F_{r}(j,\mathbf{N},\mathbf{Y})\;.

It is an instance of
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
with
\tau(F_{r})=2^{o(r)}
. By assumption, this implies that
\tau(\tilde{F}_{r})=2^{o(r)}
. Finally, note that

A_{\lceil\log n\rceil}(\mathbf{Y})=\tilde{F}_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r+1},\mathbf{Y})
, and
a(n)=A_{\lceil\log n\rceil}(2^{2^{0}},\dots,2^{2^{\mathsf{bit}(n)-1}})
. Therefore,

\tau(a(n))\;\leq\;\tau(\tilde{F}_{r})\;+\;\tau(2^{2^{\mathsf{bit}(n)-1}})\;\leq\;n^{o(1)}\,,

as desired.

Proof of part (2).
Again we can assume that
|b(n,k)|
has polynomially many bits. Let
b(n,k)=\sum_{j=1}^{p(n)}b(n,k,j)2^{j}
be the binary decomposition with
p(n)=O(n^{c^{\prime}})
and
q(n)=O(n^{c})
. Define

B_{\lceil\log n\rceil}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{\mu(n)},Z_{1},\dots,Z_{\lambda(n)})\;:=\;\sum_{k=0}^{q(n)}\sum_{j=0}^{p(n)}b(n,k,j)Y_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots Y_{\mu(n)}^{j_{\mu(n)}}Z_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots Z_{\lambda(n)}^{k_{\lambda(n)}}\;.

Here
\mu(n):=\lceil\log(p(n))\rceil
and
\lambda(n):=\lceil\log(q(n))\rceil
.
Let the variable sets be
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\dots,J_{c^{\prime}r+1}),\mathbf{N}=(N_{1},\dots,N_{r+1}),\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\dots,K_{cr+1}),\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{c^{\prime}r+1}),\mathbf{Z}=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{cr+1})
, where again
r=\lfloor\log n\rfloor
.
Define a new polynomial
F_{r}
as follows:

F_{r}(\mathbf{J},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{N},\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z})\;:=\;D_{r}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{J},\mathbf{K})\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{c^{\prime}r+1}(J_{m}Y_{m}+1-J_{m})\prod_{s=1}^{cr+1}(K_{s}Z_{s}+1-Z_{s})\;.

Like in the previous part of the proof,
(D_{r}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{J},\mathbf{K}))_{r}
is the circuit family for computing
(b(n,k,j))
. In particular,

D_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r+1},j_{1},\dots,j_{\mu(n)},k_{1},\dots,k_{\lambda(n)})\;=\;b(n,k,j)\;.

By our assumption,
D_{r}
has
2^{o(r)}
size constant-free algebraic circuits (of unbounded degree). Consider,

\tilde{F}_{r}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z})=\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}^{c^{\prime}r+1}}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\{0,1\}^{cr+1}}F_{r}(j,k,\mathbf{N},\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z}).

It is an instance of
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
with
\tau(F_{r})
is
2^{o(r)}
. Since
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\in\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}\implies

\tau(\tilde{F}_{r})=2^{o(r)}
. Now,
B_{\lceil\log n\rceil}(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z})=F_{r}(n_{1},\dots,n_{r+1},\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z})
and

f_{n}(X)\;=\;B_{\lceil\log n\rceil}(2^{2^{0}},\dots,2^{2^{\mu(n)-1}},X^{2^{0}},\dots,X^{2^{\lambda(n)-1}})\;.

Therefore,
\tau(f_{n})\;\leq\;\tau(B_{\lceil\log n\rceil})\,+\,\tau(2^{2^{\mu(n)}})\,+\,\tau(X^{2^{\lambda(n)}})\;\leq\;n^{o(1)}
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

If the
\tau
-conjecture is true, then

\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\notin\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}
.

Proof.

Take the Pochhammer polynomial
p_{n}(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X+i)
. The coefficient of
X^{n-k}
in
p_{n}
will be
\sigma_{k}(1,\dots,n)
, where
\sigma_{k}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
. And
(\sigma_{k}(1,\dots,n))_{n\in\mathbb{N},k\leq n}
is definable in linear counting hierarchy by
Corollary

5.4
. By
Theorem

6.1
,
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has
n^{o(1)}
size constant-free circuit if
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
is fixed-parameter tractable. But
p_{n}
has distinct
n
many integer roots. So, assuming the

\tau
-conjecture,
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
is not
fpt
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Instead of taking the Pochhammer polynomial, there are many other possible choices
for some explicit polynomial, see
[
9
]
.

Finally, we prove the exponential lower bound for an exponential sum, proving
Theorem

1.3
.

Theorem 6.4

(Exponential algebraic lower bound)
.

If the
\tau
-conjecture is true, then there exists an
n
-variate polynomial family
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}}g_{n}(X,y)
, which requires
2^{\Omega(n)}
-size circuits.

Proof.

If the
\tau
-conjecture is true, then
Theorem

6.2
shows that
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}\notin\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}
. By the contrapositive statement of
Theorem

4.4
, the existence of such a hard exponential sum follows.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The family
g_{n}
simply is a universal circuit of size polynomial in
n
, where the polynomial is large enough to simulate the computation of the Turing machine that shows that the
n
-th Pochhammer polynomial is definable in
\mathsf{CH}_{\mathsf{lin}}\mathsf{P}
.

.

4.

A parameterized family
f_{n,k}(\mathbf{X})

\mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}[\mathsf{F}]
iff there exists a

function

In some sense,
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}]
is a substitution of a
weighted sum
of formulas. We will define
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{P}]
as a weighted sum as above, but summing over an arbitrary circuit of polynomial-size. Similarly, we can define
\mathsf{VW}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]
, and its counterpart in the unbounded setting, i.e.
\mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}[\mathsf{P}]
, and
\mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]
.

Finally, we will define the completeness notion:

Definition 7.3
.

We will say a parameterized
p\text{-}\mathsf{family}

f_{n,k}
is
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}]
-hard
if every

,
g_{n,k}\leq^{\mathrm{fpt}}_{s}f_{n,q}
.
Similarly, we can define completeness for
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{P}]
.

We can also define completeness and hardness in the constant-free
and unbounded models.

8
Conditional collapsing of VW-hierarchy and applications

Let us recall the definition of
k
-degree
n
-variate
(n\geq k)

elementary symmetric polynomial

\sigma_{n,k}(\mathbf{X})\;:=\;\sum_{y\in\langle{n\atop{k}}\rangle}\,X_{1}^{y_{1}}X_{2}^{y_{2}}\dots X_{n}^{y_{n}}\;.

It is known that
(\sigma_{n,k})_{n}\in\mathsf{VP}^{0}
, with a simple dynamic programming algorithm; see
[
17
, Section 4]
.
Let us define a new polynomial family
B_{n,k}(\mathbf{X})
, which will be important in the latter part of the section:

B_{n,k}(\mathbf{X})\;:=\;\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}\,(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t}{k}\cdot\sigma_{n,k+t}(\mathbf{X})\;
.
The following claim is crucial:

Claim 8.1
.

For
y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
B_{n,k}(y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }y\in\langle{n\atop k}\rangle.\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For a string
y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we will call the
weight
of
y
, denoted
\textup{wt}(y)
, the number of
1
’s present in
y
. Note that if
\textup{wt}(y)<k
, then
\sigma_{n,k}(y)=0
implying
B_{n,k}(y)=0
.
Similarly if
\textup{wt}(y)=k
, then
B_{n,k}(y)=\sigma_{n,k}(y)
, which will be exactly equal to
1
.
Now if
\textup{wt}(y)=k+r
where
r>0
, then

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(y)\;=\;\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}\,(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t}{k}\cdot\sigma_{n,k+t}(y)\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{t=0}^{r}\,(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t}{k}\cdot\sigma_{n,k+t}(y)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{t=0}^{r}\,(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t}{k}\cdot\binom{k+r}{k+t}

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{t=0}^{r}\,(-1)^{t}\frac{(k+r)!}{k!t!(r-t)!}\;.

Let us further define the tri-variate polynomial
Q(x,y,z):=(x+y-z)^{k+r}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
. Note that the coefficient of
x^{k}
in
Q(x,y,z)
is

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{r}\,y^{r-t}z^{t}(-1)^{t}\cdot\frac{(k+r)!}{k!t!(r-t)!}\;.

Now putting
y=z=1
, we get the coefficient exactly equal to
B_{n,k}(y)
; since
r\neq 0
, we can say that the coefficient of
x^{k}
in
Q(x,1,1)
is
0
, which finally implies that
B_{n,k}(y)=0
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the following transfer theorem from the parameterized Valiant’s classes to Valiant’s algebraic models.

Theorem 8.2
.

\mathsf{VW}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]\;\neq\;\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}\;\implies\;\mathsf{VP}^{0}\;\neq\;\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
. Similarly,
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{P}]\;\neq\;\mathsf{VFPT}\;\implies\;\mathsf{VP}\;\neq\;\mathsf{VNP}
.

Proof.

We will prove the contraposition. Assume that
\mathsf{VP}^{0}=\mathsf{VNP}^{0}
. As mentioned before, we know that
(\sigma_{n,k})_{n}\in\mathsf{VP}^{0}
. Further, since
k\in[n]
, for
t\leq n-k
, it is trivial to see that
\tau(\binom{k+t}{k})=n^{O(1)}
. Therefore, for each
0\leq t\leq n-k
,
(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t}{k}\cdot\sigma_{n,k+t}(\mathbf{X})
has a
\mathsf{VP}^{0}
-circuit. Since
\mathsf{VP}^{0}
is closed under polynomially many additions, it follows that
(B_{n,k})_{n}\in\mathsf{VP}^{0}
.

Let
q_{n,k}\in\mathsf{VW}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]
. By definition, there is a polynomial family
p_{n,k}
of the above form
p_{n,k}(\mathbf{X})\,:=\,\sum_{y\in\langle{n\atop k}\rangle}\,g_{n}(\mathbf{X},y)
, where
g_{n}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
is in
\mathsf{VP}^{0}
, such that
q_{n,k}\leq^{{fpt}}_{s}p_{n,k}
. By Claim
8.1
, it follows that

p_{n,k}\;=\;\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\,g_{n}(\mathbf{X},y)\cdot B_{n,k}(y)\;.

We have already proved above that
B_{n,k}
has
{\rm poly}(n)
sized constant-free circuits. Hence,
g_{n}(\mathbf{X},y)B_{n,k}(y)
has constant-free
{\rm poly}(n)
-size circuit. Therefore, by definition and our primary assumption, it follows that
p_{n,k}\in\mathsf{VNP}^{0}=\mathsf{VP}^{0}\subseteq\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}
.
Since,
\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}
is
closed
under constant-free fpt-substitution (
Lemma

2.4
), it follows that
q_{n,k}\in\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}
, implying
\mathsf{VW}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]\subseteq\mathsf{VFPT}^{0}
.

The proof in the usual (not constant-free) model also follows essentially along the same line as above.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

The above theorem holds in the unbounded regime as well, i.e.,

We now aim to prove a
conditional separation
of
\mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]
and
\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}

by showing that
\mathsf{VW}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}[\mathsf{P}]=\mathsf{VFPT}_{\textup{nb}}^{0}

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
f(\mathbf{X})=\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(n)}}g(\mathbf{X},y)
, where
\ell(\cdot)
is a linear function and

is computed by an arithmetic circuit of size
m=2^{O(n^{c})}
for some constant

can be written as

f(\mathbf{X})\;=\;\sum_{e\in\langle{b(m)\atop k}\rangle}\,G(\mathbf{X},e)\;,

for some
p\text{-}\mathsf{bounded}
function

and
k=\ell(n)/\log m
and
G
has
{\rm poly}(m)
size circuits.

Proof.

Let
f(\mathbf{X})
be an instance of
\mathsf{p\text{-}{{\log}\text{-}Expsum}}
, i.e.,
f(\mathbf{X})=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}}g(\mathbf{X},y)
, where
g(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
has size
m
constant-free circuit. Here we mention that, although we just take sum over
n
variables
here for the ease of presentation, the same proof also works if we sum over
\ell(n)
many variables for some linear function
\ell
.

Let us partition the variable set
\mathbf{Y}=\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\}=E_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup E_{k}
. Here
k=n/\log m
, and for all
i
,
|E_{i}|=\log m
. For each
S\subseteq E_{i}
, we take a
new variable

Z_{i}^{S}
and we do this for all
i
. Define
\overline{Z_{i}}:=\{Z_{i}^{S}\,:\,S\subseteq E_{i}\}
and
\mathbf{Z}=\bigcup_{i}\overline{Z_{i}}
. The number of
\mathbf{Z}
-variables is
2^{\log m}\cdot k
, which
is polynomial in
m
.

Let us call an assignment of
\mathbf{Z}

, if
exactly
one variable in each set
\overline{Z_{i}}
is set to be
1
. Below we show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between

\{0,1\}

\mathbf{Y}
variables
and
good assignments
to the
\mathbf{Z}

Let
\varphi
be a homomorphism from
R[\mathbf{Y}]\to R[\mathbf{Z}]
, where
R:=\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{X}]
, such that
\varphi:Y_{i}\mapsto\prod_{S\subseteq E_{i},\ Y_{j}\not\in S}(1-Z_{i}^{S})
. Let us define
\tilde{g}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}):=\varphi(g)
. Now let us fix an assignment

to the
\mathbf{Y}

We construct a corresponding good assignment of
\mathbf{Z}
.
For each

of
\mathbf{Y}

such that

. The remaining variables in

(so that it corresponds to

). Pick this particular

. Note that this

is

(it can be the empty set). Now set

, if

, for all

.

Each variable in
\bigcup_{i}S_{i}
gets the value

and variables in
\bigcup_{i}(E_{i}\setminus S_{i})
are assigned

. Under the map

, any

is replaced by
\prod_{S\subseteq E_{1},\ Y_{j}\not\in S}(1-Z_{1}^{S})
. Since,

and

occurs in the product. And, hence the product becomes

. Now, let

and
\varphi(Y_{\ell})=\prod_{S\subseteq E_{1},\ Y_{\ell}\not\in S}(1-Z_{1}^{S})

does not contribute to the product. Thus, under the assignment defined before,

becomes

. This argument holds for any

Note that the weft of the circuit for

has increased by
1
(from that of

), and the size has also increased by a polynomial (in

Clearly,

2

. Further, it is simple to see that for any

We set

. By the construction,
\tilde{g}
has weft
\leq t+1
,

has weft
\leq 2
, and
\tilde{g},p
have
{\rm poly}(m)
size circuits. So, this ends our proof.
∎

9
Restricted permanent

A
cycle cover
of a directed graph is a collection of node-disjoint directed cycles
such that each node is contained in exactly one cycle. Cycle covers
of a directed graph stand in one-to-one relation with permutations of the nodes.

Definition 9.1
.

A cycle cover is
(k,c)
-restricted, if it contains one cycle of length
k

and all other cycles have length
\leq c
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be directed graph and
w:E\to R
be a weight function.
Here
R
is a ring and typically the ring of polynomials.
The weight of a cycle cover
C
of
G
is the product of the weights
of the edges in it, that is,
w(C)=\prod_{e\in C}w(e)
.

Definition 9.2
.

The
(k,c)
-restricted permanent of an edge-weighted directed graph
G

is

\operatorname{per}^{(k,\leq c)}(G)=\sum_{C}w(C),

where the sum is over all
(k,c)
-restricted cycle covers.

If
X=(X_{i,j})
is a variable matrix, then
\operatorname{per}_{n}(X)

is the permanent of the complete directed graph with the
edge weights
w(i,j)=X_{i,j}
. The
(k,c)
-restricted
permanent family
\operatorname{per}^{(k,\leq c)}=(\operatorname{per}_{n}^{(k,\leq c)}(X_{n}))
,
where
X_{n}
is an
n\times n
-variables matrix.

\operatorname{per}^{(k,\leq c)}
is a parameterized family,
n
is the input size,

k
is the parameter, and
c
will be some constant to be determined later.

On general graphs, the restricted permanent is very powerful,
even if we keep the parameter fixed.

Proposition 9.3
.

The
(2,2)
-restricted permanent family is
\mathsf{VNP}
-complete.

Proof.

We reduce from the matching polynomial on undirected graphs.
Given a matching
M
of the complete undirected graph, we can map it to a

(2,2)
-restricted cycle cover
C
of the complete directed graph (with self-loops),
by mapping each edge
\{i,j\}\in M
to the
2
-cycle
(i,j),(j,i)
. Nodes
that are not covered by
M
are covered by self-loops in
C
.
This is a one-to-one correspondence. Therefore, if we substitute

X_{i,i}=1
,
1\leq i\leq n
and
X_{i,j}=1
for
i>j
, then
we get the matching polynomial out of
\operatorname{per}_{n}^{(2,\leq 2)}(X)
.
∎

If we restrict the underlying graph appropriately, then the restricted
permanent is complete for the class
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}]
.
Recall that the girth of an undirected graph is the length of a shortest cycle in the graph.
When we talk of the girth of a directed graph, we mean the girth of the graph
when we disregard the direction of edges.
Furthermore, when we talk about the treewidth of a directed graph, we mean
the treewidth of the underlying undirected graph.

Definition 9.4
.

A directed graph
G=(V,E)
is
(c,b)
-nice
if we can partition the nodes
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}

into two disjoint sets, such that

1.

the graph induced by
V_{1}
has girth
>c
(not counting self-loops),

2.

every node in
V_{1}
has a self-loop, and

3.

the graph induced by
V_{2}
has tree-width bounded by
b
.

4.

every cycle that contains vertices from
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
has length
>c
.

Our main result is the following completeness result.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
c
and
b
be constants.
Let
(G_{n})
be a family of
(c,b)
-nice graphs.
Then the
(k,c)
-restricted permanent is in
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}]
.

Theorem 9.6
.

Let the underlying field have characteristic
0
.
There is a constant
b
and
a family of
(4,b)
-nice graphs
(H_{n})
such that the
(3k,4)
-restricted
permanent of

forms a family of
\mathsf{VW}[\mathsf{F}]
-hard polynomials.

If the multiplication gate is in the parse tree, then all its children
are in the parse tree. In this case, the bottom nodes
of the 2-cycles are covered within the multiplication gadget.

that the corresponding gates are in the parse tree, as it should be.

If the multiplication gate is not in the parse tree,
then all its children are not in the parse tree. In this case, the bottom

need to be covered in the input gadgets or additions gates, which means that
the corresponding gates are not in the parse tree, too, see the following subsections.

if
x
is covered by a cycle
c
in
D
or
x
is not the start or end node
of a path
p
.
If there are not any nodes of
c

still in
X_{i}
, then we remove
c
from
D
to obtain
C
. Otherwise,
C=D
.
If
x
is the start or end node of a path, then we simply
drop
P_{j,D}
, since we cannot cover
x
by a cycle after it is forgotten.

•

If
i
is a join node, let
P_{j,D}
and
P_{j^{\prime},D^{\prime}}

denote the polynomials computed
at the two children
j
and
j^{\prime}
of
i
. For a given cover
C
at
i
,
we have

P_{i,C}=\sum_{D,D^{\prime}}P_{j,D}\cdot P_{j^{\prime},D^{\prime}}

where
D
and
D^{\prime}
run over all covers such that all cycles and all path
of length
>0
that only contain nodes of
X_{i}
appear in
D
,
all cycles that contain nodes of
V_{j}
appear in
D
and all cycles that contain
nodes of
V_{j^{\prime}}
appear in
D^{\prime}
. Note that path and cycles that only contain
nodes of
X_{i}
could also appear in
D^{\prime}
; by forcing them to appear in
D
, we
make the decomposition of
C
into
D
and

unique.

•

Finally, if
i
is the root, then
X_{i}=\emptyset
. The child
j
of
i
is a forget
node. We set
P_{i,\emptyset}=P_{j,\emptyset}
.

From the construction it is clear that the polynomial computed at the root is
the restricted permanent
\operatorname{per}^{(\leq c)}(G)
. By following the tree decomposition
from the leaves to the root, we get an algebraic circuit of fpt size.
∎

Remark 11.3
.

We can expand the algebraic circuit constructed in Theorem
11.2

into a formula. Since the tree decomposition has only logarithmic height,
the size of the formula will be
f(b)^{O(\log n)}=n^{O(\log f(b))}

where
b
is the treewidth and
f(b)
is some function of
b
.

Proof of Theorem
9.5
.

Let
G_{n}=(V,E)
,
|V|=n
with a partition of
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}

as in Definition
9.4
,
n_{i}=|V_{i}|
.
Consider a
(k,c)
-restricted cycle cover
C

of
G_{n}
. Let
c_{1}
be the cycle of length
k
in
C
.
Then by Lemma
11.1
all nodes in
V_{1}
that are not
covered by
c_{1}
are self-loops. This suggests the following
approach. We enumerate all sets of size
k
, check whether they
form a cycle. If yes, we cover the remaining nodes in
V_{1}
by
self-loops. The remaining nodes induce a graph of bounded
tree-width, and we can use Theorem
11.2

and even Remark
11.3
.

We have variables
E_{i,j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
representing the edges of the
graph. We select
k
of them using the bounded summation representing the cycle
of length
k
. We first construct a
polynomial
\operatorname{Cyc}(E)
such that
\operatorname{Cyc}(e)=1
if
e\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}

is the adjacency matrix of a
k
-cycle and
\operatorname{Cyc}(e)=0
otherwise.
Since there is a Boolean formula of polynomial-size which checks this,
we get an algebraic formula for
\operatorname{Cyc}
of polynomial-size
by arithmetizing the Boolean circuit.

Furthermore, we have vertex variables
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
.
Y_{i}
will be set to
1

if the corresponding node is in the
k
-cycle and to
0
otherwise. This can
be achieved by arithmetizing
\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}E_{j,i}\implies Y_{i}
.
We can assume that
V_{1}=\{1,\dots,n_{1}\}
and
V_{2}=\{n_{1}+1,\dots,n\}
.
The
k
-cycle contributes weight
\prod_{i,j}E_{i,j}\cdot w(i,j)
.
The uncovered nodes in
V_{1}
contribute weight

\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}(1-Y_{i})w(i,i)
. The weight of the uncovered nodes in
V_{2}

can be in principle computed using
\operatorname{per}^{(\leq c)}(G[V_{2}])
, which has
a small circuit by Theorem
11.2
and
even a polynomial-size formula by Remark
11.3
.
however, some nodes in
V_{2}
may be covered by the
k
-cycle.
Therefore, we replace every weight
w(i,j)
by
(1-Y_{i})\cdot w(i,j)

for
i\not=j
, turning each node
i
off that is in the
k
-cycle.
Furthermore, we replace
w(i,i)
by
(1-Y_{i})\cdot w(i,i)+Y_{i}
.
This equips every node
i
that is turned off with a self-loop with weight
1
,
ensuring that it does not contribute to
\operatorname{per}^{(\leq c)}
. Altogether, we can
write

\sum_{e,y\in\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n^2}{k}}\operatorname{Cyc}(e)\cdot\prod_{i,j}e_{i,j}\cdot w(i,j)\cdot\left[\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}e_{j,i}\implies y_{i}\right]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}(1-y_{i})w(i,i)\cdot\operatorname{per}^{(\leq c)}(G^{\prime}[V_{2}])

where
[\dots]
denotes the arithmetization and
G^{\prime}

is the graph with the modified weight functions as described above.
∎

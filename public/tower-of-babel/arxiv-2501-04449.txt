Isospin strikes back
Francesco Rosini, Simone Pacetti

Abstract
Assuming isospin conservation, the decay of a c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} vector meson into the Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . \Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} final state is purely electromagnetic. At the leading order, the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} vector meson first converts into a virtual photon that, then produces the Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . \Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} final state. Moreover, such a mechanism, i.e., the virtual photon coupling to Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . \Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} , is the solely intermediate process through which, in Born approximation, the reaction e + ​ e − → Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} does proceed. It follows that any significant difference between the amplitudes of the processes c ​ c ¯ → Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . c\bar{c}\to\Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} and e + ​ e − → Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . e^{+}e^{-}\to\Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} at the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} mass must be ascribed to an isospin-violating contribution in the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} decay. In Ref. [ 1 ] we studied the decay of the ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) \psi(2S) vector meson into Λ ​ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . \Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.} and, on the light of the large branching fraction BR 18 ( ψ ( 2 S ) → Λ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . ) = ( 1.23 ± 0.24 ) × 10 − 5 , \displaystyle{\rm BR}_{18}(\psi(2S)\to\Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.})=(1.23\pm 0.24)\times 10^{-5}\,, published in the 2018 edition of the Review of Particle Physics [ 2 ] , we claimed either the presence of a significant isospin-violating contribution or, with a lesser emphasis, a ”not complete reliability of the only available datum”. In any case, we propose a new measurement. Apparently, our second and considered less serious hypothesis was the right one, indeed the branching fraction published in the 2024 edition of the Review of Particle Physics [ 3 ] is BR ( ψ ( 2 S ) → Λ Σ ¯ 0 + c . c . ) = ( 1.6 ± 0.7 ) × 10 − 6 , \displaystyle{\rm BR}(\psi(2S)\to\Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.})=(1.6\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-6}\,, more than seven times lower with the error that increased from ∼ 20 % \sim 20\% to ∼ 45 % \sim 45\% .

Parameterizations of the amplitudes

The processes under consideration are the decay of a
c\bar{c}
vector meson, called
\psi
, into a baryon-antibaryon
B_{1}\bar{B}_{2}+{\rm c.c.}
final state, and the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation which produces the same final state. The reactions are

\displaystyle\psi\to B_{1}\bar{B}_{2}+{\rm c.c.}\,,\ \ \ e^{+}e^{-}\to B_{1}\bar{B}_{2}+{\rm c.c.}\,.

In general, at the leading-order, the
\psi
and
\gamma
-hyperons currents are

\displaystyle\displaystyle J_{\mu}^{\psi}=\bar{u}(k_{1})G_{\mu}[g_{E}^{B_{1}B_{2}},g_{M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}]v(k_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle J_{\mu}^{\gamma}=\bar{u}(k_{1})G_{\mu}[G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{E}(q^{2}),G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{M}(q^{2})]v(k_{2})\,,

where
u
and
v
are the Dirac spinors,
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are the four-momenta of the hyperons,
q=k_{1}+k_{2}
,
g_{E}^{B_{1}B_{2}}
and
g_{M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}
are the so-called psionic coupling constants, while
G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{E}(q^{2})
and
G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{M}(q^{2})
are Sachs form factors. The Lorentz four-vector
G_{\mu}
in both the currents has the same expression, in that, the second can be obtained from the first by the substitutions:
g_{E,M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}\to G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{E,M}(q^{2})
and vice versa.

The rate of the decay
\psi\to B_{1}\bar{B}_{2}+{\rm c.c.}
and the cross section of the annihilation
e^{+}e^{-}\to B_{1}\bar{B}_{2}+{\rm c.c.}
are

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\psi}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\Gamma^{\psi}_{\mu\mu}

(1)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{2M_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{2}}{M_{\psi}^{2}}\left|g_{E}^{B_{1}B_{2}}\right|^{2}+\left|g_{M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}\right|^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}\beta_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})}{3q^{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{2M_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{2}}{q^{2}}\left|G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{E}(q^{2})\right|^{2}+\left|G^{B_{1}B_{2}}_{M}(q^{2})\right|^{2}\right)\,,

where
\beta_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})=\sqrt{1-{4M_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{2}}/{q^{2}}}
is the velocity of both out-going baryons in the baryon-anti-baryon center-of-mass frame,

B_{1}=B_{2}\equiv B
, is the baryon mass, i.e.,
M_{B_{1}B_{2}}=M_{B}
.

To make a straightforward comparison we define the effective unique psionic coupling constant
A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}
and the effective form factor
A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}|=\sqrt{\frac{2M_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{2}}{M_{\psi}^{2}}\left|g_{E}^{B_{1}B_{2}}\right|^{2}+\left|g_{M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}\right|^{2}}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})|=\sqrt{\frac{2M_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{2}}{q^{2}}\left|G_{E}^{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})\right|^{2}+\left|G_{M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})\right|^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Gamma^{\psi}_{B_{1}B_{2}}=\beta_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\Gamma^{\psi}_{\mu\mu}\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}\right|^{2}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})=\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}\beta_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})}{3q^{2}}\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(q^{2})\right|^{2}\,.

In the case of a pure isovector final state, as for
(B_{1},B_{2})=(\Lambda,\Sigma^{0})
, and assuming isospin conservation, the psionic coupling constants coincide with the Sachs form factors at the vector meson mass, i.e.,

\displaystyle g_{E,M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}=G_{E,M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\,.

}}}

\displaystyle g_{E,M,\rm tot}^{{B_{1}B_{2}}}=g_{E,M}^{B_{1}B_{2}}+g_{E,M,\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

In terms of the effective coupling constant and the effective form factor defined in Eq. (
2

\displaystyle A^{\psi}_{{B_{1}B_{2}},\rm tot}=A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}+A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

\displaystyle\beta_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\Gamma^{\psi}_{\mu\mu}\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M^{2}_{\psi})+A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

The experimental value of the modulus of the ratio between the total effective coupling constant

\displaystyle R_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})+A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}}{3M_{\psi}^{2}\Gamma_{\mu\mu}^{\psi}}\frac{\Gamma^{\psi}_{B_{1}B_{2}}}{\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})}}\,.

The intensity and hence the modulus of the isospin-violating coupling constant can be extracted from the experimental value

\displaystyle\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})+A^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

\displaystyle\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\right|^{2}+\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+2\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\right|\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}

\displaystyle{\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\scalebox{.8}{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hbox{$I$}}\raisebox{-0.99585pt}{\rotatebox{25.0}{\rule[2.39166pt]{4.08821pt}{0.5pt}}
}}}\right|}/{\left|A_{B_{1}B_{2}}(M_{\psi}^{2})\right|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(R_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}\right)^{2}-\sin^{2}\left(\phi_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}\right)}

(7)

\displaystyle-\cos\left(\phi_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{\psi}\right)\,.

These terms define the effective electromagnetic coupling constants, i.e., the amplitudes for the reactions

Why isospin does strike back

\psi(2S)\to\Lambda\bar{\Sigma}^{0}+{\rm c.c.}
branching fraction, giving more weight to that of an uncommon phenomenon of isospin violation guided by the exciting theoretical perspective on one side and by anthropic confidence on the correctness of any human result on the other.

However, even though the second hypothesis, which we defined as less probable, appears as the right one, our anthropic faith remains unchanged because another and stronger effect emerges overbearingly: isospin symmetry. The lesson we should draw is that a well-established theory is the best yardstick for judging an uncommon result, especially if supported by weak foundations.

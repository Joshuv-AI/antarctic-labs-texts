Fermi–Dirac Wigner function for massive spin- 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} particles in local equilibrium Journal: Physics Letters B
Sudip Kumar Kar, Valeriya Mykhaylova

Abstract
A recently proposed Boltzmann local equilibrium Wigner function for massive spin- 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} particles is generalized to the case of Fermi–Dirac statistics. The resulting formula ensures the correct normalization of the mean polarization vector and reproduces the generalized thermodynamic relations with spin that were obtained in earlier studies. Moreover, we show that the macroscopic currents constructed from the Fermi–Dirac Wigner function can be obtained as derivatives of a suitably defined generating function with respect to the Lagrange multipliers (temperature, hydrodynamic flow, and chemical potentials). The identified generating function also indicates that the underlying framework can be classified as a divergence-type theory.

2
Transition from the Boltzmann to the Fermi–Dirac case

Recently, the local equilibrium Wigner function for spin-
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
particles was introduced in
[
16
]
in the form

\displaystyle W^{\pm}(x,k)\!=\!\frac{1}{4m}\int\!\mathrm{d}P\,\delta^{(4)}(k\!\mp\!p)(\not{p}\!\pm\!m)X^{\pm}(x,p)(\not{p}\!\pm\!m).

(1)

Here the superscript
+\,(-)
refers to particles (antiparticles),
m
denotes the particle mass, and
p=(E_{p},{\boldsymbol{p}})
is the on-shell particle four-momentum with
p^{0}=E_{p}=\sqrt{m^{2}+{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}}
. The momentum integration measure is given by
\mathrm{d}P=\mathrm{d}^{3}p/((2\pi)^{3}E_{p})
. The quantity
X^{\pm}
is the four-by-four spinor density matrix,

\displaystyle X^{\pm}(x,p)=\exp\left[\pm\xi(x)-\beta(x)\cdot p+\gamma_{5}\not{a}(x,p)\right],

(2)

where
\xi=\mu/T
is the ratio of the baryon chemical potential to the temperature and
\beta^{\mu}=u^{\mu}/T
involves the fluid four-velocity normalized to unity,
u^{\mu}=\gamma(1,{\boldsymbol{v}})
, with
\gamma
being the Lorentz factor. The space-like four vector
a_{\mu}
is defined as
[
16
,
27
]

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(x,p)=-\frac{1}{2m}{\tilde{\omega}}_{\mu\nu}(x)p^{\nu},

(3)

where
{\tilde{\omega}}_{\mu\nu}
represents the dual of the spin polarization tensor
\omega_{\mu\nu}
. The latter is specified by the ratio of the spin chemical potential,
\Omega_{\mu\nu}
, to the temperature,
\omega_{\mu\nu}=\Omega_{\mu\nu}/T
(note that both
\xi
and
\omega_{\mu\nu}
are dimensionless in natural units). Following earlier works
[
23
,
16
,
27
]
, the spin polarization tensor
\omega_{\mu\nu}
can be represented by its electriclike and magneticlike components,
{\boldsymbol{e}}
and
{\boldsymbol{b}}
, in exactly the same way as the Faraday tensor
F_{\mu\nu}
in classical electrodynamics is written in terms of the electric and magnetic fields,
{\boldsymbol{E}}
and
{\boldsymbol{B}}
. More physical insight can be gained by considering the tensors in the particle rest frame (PRF). Denoting the quantities in this frame by an asterisk, we find

\displaystyle a^{\mu}_{*}=(0,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{*})=-\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{*}}{2}\,,\qquad-a^{2}={\boldsymbol{a}}_{*}^{2}>0\,.

(4)

Hence, it is clear that the spin polarization effects in equilibrium are governed by the magnetic component of the spin polarization tensor in PRF, analogous to how the orientation of magnetic moments is determined by the magnetic field (we comment further on this relation below).

Since
a^{2}<0
, it is convenient to rewrite the exponential of
\gamma_{5}\not{a}
in (
2
) as

\displaystyle\exp\!\left(\gamma_{5}\not{a}\right)=\cosh\!\sqrt{-a^{2}}+\frac{\gamma_{5}\not{a}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}}\sinh\!\sqrt{-a^{2}},

(5)

and use the definitions of the hyperbolic functions to express the spinor density matrix in the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}X^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}=&\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\left(e^{\pm\xi-\beta\cdot p+\sqrt{-a^{2}}}+e^{\pm\xi-\beta\cdot p-\sqrt{-a^{2}}}\right)\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{(\gamma_{5}\not{a})_{\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}}\,\left(e^{\pm\xi-\beta\cdot p+\sqrt{-a^{2}}}-e^{\pm\xi-\beta\cdot p-\sqrt{-a^{2}}}\right)\Bigg].\end{split}

(6)

Here, the subscripts
\alpha,\beta
denote the spinor indices, which run from 1 to 4, and each exponential function plays the role of the Boltzmann distribution in the spin-extended phase space. Therefore, we introduce a compact notation

\displaystyle g_{\rm{B}}^{\pm\pm}(x,p)=\exp\left[\pm\xi(x)-\beta(x)\cdot p\pm\sqrt{-a^{2}(x,p)}\right],

(7)

where the first
\pm
distinguishes between particles (
+
) and antiparticles (
-
), while the second refers to the spin orientation, (
+
) for the direction aligned with
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{*}
in PRF, and (
-
) for the opposite direction. Using this definition, Eq. (
6
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle X^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(g_{\rm{B}}^{\pm+}+g_{\rm{B}}^{\pm-}\right)\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{(\gamma_{5}\not{a})_{\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}}\,\left(g_{\rm{B}}^{\pm+}-g_{\rm{B}}^{\pm-}\right)\right].

(8)

At this point, it is natural to generalize our Boltzmann-statistics result to the Fermi–Dirac case. This is accomplished simply by replacing the Boltzmann distribution function,
g_{\rm{B}}^{\pm\pm}(x,p)
, with the Fermi–Dirac one,

\displaystyle g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm\pm}(x,p)=\left[\exp\left({\mp\xi(x)+\beta(x)\cdot p\mp\sqrt{-a^{2}(x,p)}}\right)+1\right]^{-1},

(9)

leading to

\displaystyle X^{\pm}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm+}+g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm-}\right)\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{(\gamma_{5}\not{a})_{\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{-a^{2}}}\,\left(g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm+}-g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm-}\right)\right],

(10)

which represents our novel result. Note that, by preserving the superscript notation introduced in Eq. (
7
), the signs in the exponents in Eq. (
9
) are flipped. This guarantees that the spin-extended Fermi–Dirac distribution reduces to the Boltzmann case in the low-density limit.

Two-by-two spin density matrix

Contraction of the spinor density matrices
X^{\pm}
with the free Dirac spinors (
u_{r}(p)
and
v_{s}(p)
with

yields the two-by-two Hermitian spin density matrices

\displaystyle\left[f^{+}(x,p)\right]_{rs}\equiv f^{+}_{rs}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m}\,{\bar{u}}_{r}(p)X^{+}u_{s}(p),

(11)

\displaystyle\left[f^{-}(x,p)\right]_{rs}\equiv f^{-}_{rs}(x,p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2m}\,{\bar{v}}_{s}(p)X^{-}v_{r}(p).

(12)

Here, the bar denotes the Dirac adjoint. To calculate the right-hand sides of the equations above, we use the normalization conditions
{\bar{u}}_{r}(p)u_{s}(p)=2m\,\delta_{rs}
and
{\bar{v}}_{r}(p)v_{s}(p)=-2m\,\delta_{rs}
, as well as the identities

\displaystyle{\bar{u}}_{r}(p)\gamma_{5}\not{a}u_{s}(p)

\displaystyle=2m\,\bm{a}_{*}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{rs},

(13)

\displaystyle{\bar{v}}_{s}(p)\gamma_{5}\not{a}v_{r}(p)

\displaystyle=-2m\,\bm{a}_{*}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{rs},

(14)

where
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
denotes the vector of the three Pauli matrices. The resulting expressions read

\displaystyle f^{\pm}_{rs}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm+}+g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm-}\right)\,\delta_{rs}+\frac{\bm{a}_{*}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{rs}}{\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{*}^{2}}}\,\left(g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm+}-g_{\rm{FD}}^{\pm-}\right)\,\right],

(15)

and can be further used to evaluate the mean spin polarization (as defined in
[
23

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{P}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm tr_{2}}\left[(f^{+}+f^{-}){\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right]}{{\rm tr_{2}}\left(f^{+}+f^{-}\right)}=\frac{\bm{a_{*}}}{2\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{*}^{2}}}\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=\pm}j\,g_{\text{FD}}^{ij}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=\pm}g_{\text{FD}}^{ij}},

(16)

with the trace taken over the spin indices
r
and
s
. One can easily verify that the mean spin polarization given
by Eq. (
16
)
is bounded.

We now turn to the discussion of the direction of the mean spin polarization, using as an example a rigidly rotating fluid in global equilibrium – also referred to, for brevity, as a vortex in equilibrium. Our results indicate that

\displaystyle\bm{P}\,\propto\,-\bm{b}_{*},

(17)

where
[
26
]

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{b}}_{*}=\frac{1}{m}\left(E_{p}\,{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{p}}\times{\boldsymbol{e}}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}}{E_{p}+m}{\boldsymbol{p}}\right).

(18)

In global equilibrium, the spin polarization tensor coincides with the thermal vorticity,
\omega_{\mu\nu}=\varpi_{\mu\nu}=-1/2(\partial_{\mu}\beta_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\beta_{\mu})
, and its electric and magnetic components are given by
{\boldsymbol{e}}=(0,0,0)
and
{\boldsymbol{b}}=-{\Omega_{0}}/T_{0}{\boldsymbol{n}}_{z}
, where
{\Omega_{0}}
and
T_{0}
are constant parameters, and
{\boldsymbol{n}}_{z}=(0,0,1)
is the unit vector that defines the rotation axis. Hence, the polarization vector averaged over particles that form the vortex,
\langle{\boldsymbol{P}}\rangle
, can be written as

\displaystyle\langle{\boldsymbol{P}}\rangle\propto\frac{\Omega_{0}{\boldsymbol{n}}_{z}}{T_{0}m}\left(\langle E_{p}\rangle-\left\langle\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{E_{p}+m}\right\rangle\right).

(19)

Here, we used cylindrical symmetry to rewrite the second term on the right-hand side. We further notice that
in (
19
)
, the first term dominates, hence
\langle{\boldsymbol{P}}\rangle
points along the
z
-axis, coinciding with the direction of the orbital angular momentum. This is in agreement with previous findings
[
13
,
34
]

Macroscopic currents

The spinor density matrix (
10
) can be further used to compute the following conserved currents
[
17
]

\displaystyle N^{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}\left[f^{+}_{rr}(x,p)-f^{-}_{rr}(x,p)\right],

(20)

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\left[f^{+}_{rr}(x,p)+f^{-}_{rr}(x,p)\right],

(21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}(x)&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,s=1}^{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\lambda}\big[\sigma^{+\mu\nu}_{sr}(p)f^{+}_{rs}(x,p)+\sigma^{-\mu\nu}_{sr}(p)f^{-}_{rs}(x,p)\big].\end{split}

(22)

Here,
N^{\mu}(x)
represents the baryon number current,
T^{\mu\nu}(x)
is the energy-momentum tensor, and
S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}(x)
denotes the spin tensor, with
\sigma^{+\mu\nu}_{sr}(p)=1/(2m)\,{\bar{u}}_{s}(p)\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}u_{r}(p)

\sigma^{-\mu\nu}_{sr}(p)=1/(2m)\,{\bar{v}}_{r}(p)\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}v_{s}(p)
,

\sigma^{\mu\nu}=(i/2)[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]

]

Based on the studies performed in

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=-\sum_{i,j=\pm}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}\ln(1-g^{ij}_{\text{FD}}),

which reduces to its Boltzmann counterpart in the low-density regime,
g^{ij}_{\text{FD}}\ll 1

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=P\beta^{\mu}

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{\cal N}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{\cal N}^{\mu}}{\partial\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi+\frac{\partial{\cal N}^{\mu}}{\partial\beta_{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}\beta_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial{\cal N}^{\mu}}{\partial\omega_{\alpha\beta}}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\alpha\beta},

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\omega_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\omega_{\alpha\beta}}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu}_{\alpha}g^{\nu}_{\beta}-g^{\nu}_{\alpha}g^{\mu}_{\beta},\qquad\frac{\partial a^{2}}{\partial\omega_{\alpha\beta}}=-\frac{1}{m}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}a_{\gamma}p_{\delta}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{\cal N}^{\mu}=N^{\mu}\mathrm{d}\xi-T^{\mu\lambda}\mathrm{d}\beta_{\lambda}+{\frac{1}{2}}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\alpha\beta}.

5
Generating function for divergence-type treatment

The divergence-type framework of hydrodynamics
[
39
,
30
,
40
,
41
,
29
]
is a useful tool for analyzing the causality and stability of proposed hydrodynamic theories. As demonstrated in
[
1
]
, perfect spin hydrodynamics can be cast into a divergence type framework, where the primary quantity of interest is the generating function
\chi
, which can be leveraged to obtain all conserved currents. Moreover, it significantly simplifies the analysis of nonlinear causality and equilibrium stability of the theory.

For our case, the generating function takes the form

\displaystyle\hskip-6.544pt\chi=\sum_{i,j=\pm}\int\mathrm{d}P\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln\frac{1-g^{ij}_{\text{FD}}}{g^{ij}_{\text{FD}}}\right)^{2}+\text{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{g^{ij}_{\text{FD}}-1}{g^{ij}_{\text{FD}}}\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right],

(33)

where
\text{Li}_{2}(x)
is the dilogarithm, and the factor
\pi^{2}/6
has been added to ensure the convergence of the three-momentum integral. The generating function
\chi
can then be used to obtain all the relevant currents via the relations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{N}^{\lambda}&=-\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\beta_{\lambda}},\qquad\ N^{\lambda}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial\xi\partial\beta_{\lambda}},\qquad T^{\lambda\mu}=\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial\beta_{\lambda}\partial\beta_{\mu}},\qquad S^{\lambda\mu\nu}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial\beta_{\lambda}\partial\omega_{\mu\nu}},\end{split}

(34)

indicating that the theory can indeed be cast into the divergence-type framework. The next natural step is to examine the causality and stability properties of our approach. Positive results for the present framework, as well as for other formulations of perfect spin hydrodynamics (Boltzmann vs. Fermi–Dirac statistics, classical spin treatment vs. spin density description), will be presented in a separate study
[
15
]
.

6
Summary and conclusions

In this work, a local-equilibrium Wigner function for
spin-
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
particles with dynamical spin degrees of freedom obeying Fermi–Dirac statistics is presented. The proposed function satisfies the proper normalization of the mean spin polarization. Moreover, for a vortex in global equilibrium (where
\omega=\varpi
), the mean spin polarization vector aligns with the direction of the orbital angular momentum. This observation is consistent with known results for rigidly rotating charged fluids in equilibrium. Introducing a new current (
\mathcal{N}^{\lambda}
), we demonstrated that the conserved currents satisfy generalized thermodynamic relations. The convergence of the integrals defining these currents requires imposition of a selection criterion, as discussed in detail in for the spin-extended Boltzmann statistics
[
21
,
22
]
. Since in the limit of high momentum, the Fermi–Dirac distribution reduces to the Boltzmann case, the same criterion of convergence applies. Finally, we identified a generating function that allows us to cast the underlying theory into a divergence-type framework.

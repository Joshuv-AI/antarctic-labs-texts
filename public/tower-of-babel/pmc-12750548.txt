Coordinated care program in the Polish primary healthcare: insights into the early implementation
Authors: Mateusz Guziak, Anna Joanna Świtajska, Milena Šantrić-Milićević
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe Coordinated Care Program (CCP) was introduced in Polish Primary Healthcare (PHC) in October 2022. It promised a significant transformation in the country’s PHC system by integrating extended diagnostics and chronic disease management to enhance patient access to PHC and improve healthcare outcomes. Longitudinal analysis of their funding and service capacity to equitably address the patient needs and satisfaction is crucial in assessing whether CCP maintains its promises and value over time. The study outlines the results of the CCP outcomes two years after its implementation, by examining the PHC facilities’ participation rates in CCP, patient coverage, as well as patient satisfaction within the existing regulatory and funding framework.MethodsFollowing the principles of the Theory-Based Evaluation, the effects were assessed using a mixed-methods approach that includes a specially designed stakeholder survey, a desk research of the regulatory framework, and a secondary analysis of a nationwide patient satisfaction survey.ResultsSince its implementation, with a total of 640 million Polish Zloty allocated through steady monthly growth funding, there has been a consistent increase in the PHC’s adoption of the CCP across sixteen regions. By the end of 2024, 41% of the total 6053 PHC facilities were participating in the CCP. Eight regions outperformed the national average in expanding their diagnostic and therapeutic capabilities to meet patients’ needs in diabetology, cardiology, endocrinology, pulmonology, and nephrology. They achieved a very high level of patient satisfaction with the CCP (The overall Net Promoter Score was 75.46 (-100 to 100 range).ConclusionTo sustain the CCP program’s successes, it is necessary to reduce regional disparities in CCP accessibility, particularly in rural or economically disadvantaged areas. Follow-up assessment of whether the CCP efficiently met its objectives should consider the diverse needs of all patients involved.Keywords: Healthcare, Primary care, Family medicine, Coordinated care, Primary healthcare, Health management

Contributions to the literature• This study offers one of the first national evaluations of Poland’s new Coordinated Care Program, showing how its early implementation influences the country’s primary care system. It provides initial evidence on primary care facilities’ participation, patient coverage, and user experience two years after the program began. The paper also identifies important regional inequalities in access to coordinated care.

Background

The foundation of Poland’s healthcare system is formed by over 6,000 primary healthcare facilities (PHC, also known as Podstawowa Opieka Zdrowotna), which provide the first-line medical services to the 37.4 million residents in the country [1]. The basis for public funding of PHC in Poland through the National Health Fund (NHF) is the annual capitation rate, determined by the number of patients registered on a PHC facility provider’s list and freely chosen by every adult citizen. Typically, PHC consultations are available on a daily basis. During public holidays and after 6 p.m., care is provided through the Night and Holiday Healthcare Service (pl. Nocna i świąteczna opieka zdrowotna) [2, 3]. PHC remains the most frequently utilized segment of the healthcare system in Poland, with over 52% of all outpatient consultations occurring within its framework [4]. In 2023, this translated to approximately 181.1 million visits (an increase of 2.8% compared to 2022) [4]. Yet, investment in preventive services remains disproportionately low at 2%, significantly below the OECD-25 average of 5% [5]. Although one-sixth of total health expenditure in Poland was allocated to PHC, which is higher than the OECD-25 average of 13%, Polish PHC tends to follow a reactive model of care. This was reflected in relatively weak coordination between primary and specialized healthcare services, and longer waiting times, as well as limited options for primary care physicians (PCPs) to address the demand for diagnostic and therapeutic procedures [6, 7]. PCPs handled patient referrals, follow-up arrangement, and care coordination- tasks considerably increased their administrative burden and restricted time for direct clinical care. Insufficient integration between PHC and specialist services led to fragmented service delivery, prolonged diagnostic waiting periods, and delays in the management of chronic conditions [2, 5–8]. The persistence of aforementioned issues has necessitated systemic reforms, culminating in the introduction of the Coordinated Care Program (CCP, pol. Opieka Koordynowana) in October 2022. The CCP serves as a transformative policy initiative designed to enhance care integration, particularly relevant for patients with chronic conditions who require efficient oversight and treatment from multiple disciplinary teams, often involving multi-medication management. A key innovation of the program is the introduction of a care coordinator responsible for scheduling diagnostic tests, specialist consultations, and educational or dietary sessions, as well as facilitating communication between care providers and patients [9]. The program seeks to boost multidisciplinary collaboration, improve the management of non-communicable diseases (NCDs), and implement systematic prevention strategies, thereby aligning Poland more closely with international best practices. The initiative to implement CCP in Poland is thoughtfully informed by the influential research and contributions of scholars and organizations from the 1990s and 2000s, which highlighted the essential principles of PHC. These principles stress the importance of PCPs capable of providing comprehensive, continuous, and accessible services that are seamlessly coordinated to meet the needs of the population [10–13].

The demand for the coordinated care program in Poland

The demand for health promotion and disease prevention in Poland comprehensively describes the latest estimates of the preventable and treatable age-standardized mortality rates, which were 1.53 and 1.57 times higher than the average for the EU in 2020 [14]. The age-standardized disability-adjusted life years (DALY) rate in Poland reached 23,040 per 100,000 population in 2017, mainly due to demographic aging and population decline [15]. By 2021, the leading contributors to DALY in Poland included COVID-19 (6,530.5 per 100,000), ischemic heart disease (6,077.05), stroke (2,046.61), back and neck pain (1,934.55), and trachea, bronchus, and lung cancer (1,869.98) [16]. In 2023, 5.8% of individuals aged 16 years or older reported having unmet needs for medical examination or treatment in Poland, which is considerably higher than the EU average of 3.8% [17]. The most frequently cited barrier was the presence of waiting lists [17]. Despite near-universal population coverage, access to a core set of health services remains uneven, particularly for individuals employed in informal, temporary, or part-time jobs, who are more likely to be uninsured [18]. In meeting the healthcare demand, the healthcare workforce faces structural challenges and earning issues constraints. In 2022, Poland had 3.5 practicing doctors per 1,000 population- below the EU27 average of 4.2 [19]. Moreover, only 9% of physicians were general practitioners (GPs), compared to the EU26 average of 21%, highlighting a significant imbalance in workforce composition [19]. The formal post-graduate education curricula have been regularly updated, including the professional skills improvement within the CCP course in the specialty family training. The ratio of a GP’s income to the average national wage remains relatively modest at 2.3, even with an average annual real-term growth rate of 4.3% since 2012, and a faster rate of remuneration increase for GPs than for specialists [19].

The CCP’s promise to bring value to PHC in Poland over time is compelling, and this study uses the Theory-Based Evaluation (TBE) approach grounded in the Structure–Process–Outcome (SPO) model to assess and describe the early results of the CCP during its initial stage, when motivation and enthusiasm were high among all involved stakeholders. The TBE approach provides a framework for gaining insights into the formative and process mechanisms and achievements in the context of Polish healthcare, enabling a deeper understanding of the potential effectiveness and barriers to the CCP during its two-year implementation period [20, 21]. In the absence of access to the CCP Action Plan, the TBE approach was adopted as the most appropriate model for indirect assessment to address the research questions. The SPO, on the other hand, provides a logical framework for analyzing the early effects of the CCP in Poland by linking input resources into structure, implementation processes, and early results observed in PHC. Within this framework, the Structure component encompasses CCP funding mechanism and regulatory instruments, the Process refers to the implementation of CCP by PHC facilities, including the range of diagnostic and therapeutic services offered; and the Outcomes represent early indicators of program effectiveness, such as CCP adoption rates, patient coverage and satisfaction [21–23]. The study explores the extent to which the CCP program achieved its objectives two years after its implementation in October 2022. More precisely, the study outlines the CCP funding trends, the number of participating facilities, patient coverage, and their perceptions of service quality as of October 2022.

Methods

Study design

The study uses a mixed-methods approach to examine CCP initial results through a stakeholder survey, a desk review of the regulatory framework, and secondary analysis of a nationwide patient survey (Fig. 1). As illustrated in Fig. 1, the input data for the study were obtained through triangulation, incorporating stakeholders’ surveys, a focused review of a publication compiled through desk research of the CCP regulatory framework, and secondary analysis of nationwide patient surveys assessing user experience. The assessment includes a descriptive analysis of a mix of qualitative input data obtained from the stakeholders’ responses and reviews, as well as quantitative data on CCP available statistics. The results of the evaluation include analyses of financial trends, adoption rates of CCP across PHC facilities, patients’ coverage, and their satisfaction.

Study units and variables

The study units were PHC facilities providing the CCP in Poland. The variables of interest included inputs, process, and early results of the CCP in PHC, along with their indicators, including the following:

- Level of funding for the CCP and its changes (measured by base and line indices) from October 2022 through December 2024 (i.e., inputs into the CCP). Monthly funding allocated to the CCP were expressed in Polish zloty (PLN/month, provided by the NHF).

- Level of funding for the CCP and its changes (measured by base and line indices) from October 2022 through December 2024 (i.e., inputs into the CCP). Monthly funding allocated to the CCP were expressed in Polish zloty (PLN/month, provided by the NHF).

- Number and percentage of PHC facilities (of the total 6,053 in 2022) implementing the CC program, and their territorial distribution across regions (i.e., process of the CCP implementation).

- Number and percentage of PHC facilities (of the total 6,053 in 2022) implementing the CC program, and their territorial distribution across regions (i.e., process of the CCP implementation).

- Types of medical services offered by PHC facilities under the CC program (i.e., CCP results).

- Types of medical services offered by PHC facilities under the CC program (i.e., CCP results).

- Number of patients registered with PHC providers before and after the application of the CCP and the number and percentage of patients enrolled in the CCP (i.e., CCP results). The proportion of the PHC patient population participating in the CCP was also disaggregated by region (i.e., regional disparities in CCP results).

- Number of patients registered with PHC providers before and after the application of the CCP and the number and percentage of patients enrolled in the CCP (i.e., CCP results). The proportion of the PHC patient population participating in the CCP was also disaggregated by region (i.e., regional disparities in CCP results).

- Patients’ satisfaction with the CCP (i.e., CCP results).

- Patients’ satisfaction with the CCP (i.e., CCP results).

The study variables were selected to reflect the three dimensions of the SPO model. The inclusion of these variables was based on prior literature emphasizing their relevance in assessing PHC reform and coordination programs [12, 24, 25].

Data sources and research instruments

The data from stakeholders used in this study were collected using a purposively designed data collection instrument, which was officially submitted in December 2024 to two national-level CCP stakeholders: the Department of Medical Services at the Ministry of Health (MoH, pol. Ministerstwo Zdrowia, Departament Lecznictwa) and the Department of Health Care Services Benefits at the National Health Fund (NHF, pol. Narodowy Fundusz Zdrowia, Departament Świadczeń Opieki Zdrowotnej). The study instrument includes formal, structured open-ended questions about the CCP’s regulatory foundation, funding structure, scope of implementation, and operations challenges. The stakeholders’ responses were considered qualitative expert input and used to supplement the findings of the desk review, which included regulatory acts, reports, and statistical bulletins [4, 5, 16, 17, 19, 26–31], as well as quantitative data analysis. The patients’ perspectives on the CCP program were retrieved from a nationwide patient satisfaction survey, conducted in 2024 by the My Patients Foundation (pol. Fundacja My Pacjenci) in cooperation with the NHF. The report included data from 1,024 adult patients who received CCP services in PHC settings [30]. The information on the territorial distribution of facilities, coverage percentages, and density is available on the website: koordynowana.nfz.gov.pl [31]. The website offers a heat map visualization of healthcare facilities that have signed an agreement with the NHF under the entrusted budget for CCP.

Data analysis

Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, comparative analysis across regions, and trend analysis of monthly funding levels. Linear regression was applied to estimate trends in CCP monthly funding from 2022 to 2024. A comparative analysis assessed the similarities and differences in the structure and process of CCP adoption across various geographic areas, helping to identify regional disparities in CCP outcomes. Furthermore, the content analysis of publications retrieved from desk research (existing documents, official reports, and publicly available datasets) facilitated the identification of information that supports quantitative evidence on the structure, implementation process, and performance outputs of the CCP [32]. Additionally, we synthesized patients’ experiences related to themes such as access to care (a process indicator, but can be considered as a proxy of CCP structure, i.e., availability of core coordinators), communication with providers (also process, but can be considered as a proxy of CCP structure, i.e., appropriateness of core coordinator’s competencies), care coordination (process), and overall satisfaction (outcome) with the Individualized Medical Care Plan (IMCP) [30]. This synthesis allowed us to perform a secondary analysis to integrate the nationwide patient satisfaction survey results with complementary findings from other data analyses.

Results

The coordinated care program implementation in Poland, 2022–2024

The CCP services within the PHC were introduced under the Regulation of the Minister of Health of September 15, 2022, amending the regulation on guaranteed benefits in the field of PHC (Journal of Laws, 2022, item 1965), also known as the “basket regulation.” This regulation defined the list of coordinated care services and the conditions for their implementation. Initially, four medical areas were identified: diabetology, cardiology, endocrinology, and pulmonology. A fifth area, nephrology, was added in November 2023. The primary objective of the CCP was to expand and support the fulfillment of the population’s healthcare needs by providing high-quality services and ensuring comprehensive care that integrates preventive measures with the health needs of patients. The implementation of the CCP was based on the pilot program “Preparation, Testing, and Implementation of Coordinated Care into the Healthcare System- Stage II, Pilot Phase- POZ PLUS Model,” conducted from July 1, 2018, to September 30, 2021. The pilot identified the most common chronic diseases diagnosed by PCPs. These included hypertension, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, diabetes, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, hypothyroidism, and thyroid nodules. Yet, due to the limited availability of diagnostic tests before the implementation of the CCP, PCPs often had to refer patients to specialists within ambulatory specialist care to ensure an accurate diagnosis and establish an appropriate treatment plan. As part of the CCP, PCPs develop an IMCP for each patient. The IMCP is designed to enhance the management of chronic diseases by facilitating personalized, continuous, and goal-oriented care, while also contributing to the implementation of standardized treatment pathways. A key innovation of the program is the appointment of a dedicated care coordinator, who ensures the smooth organization and delivery of services. The coordinator’s responsibilities include scheduling diagnostic tests, specialist consultations, and educational or dietary sessions prescribed by the PCP. Furthermore, the coordinator facilitates communication between all care providers and ensures that patients are informed about the next steps of their care.

Financing of the coordinated care program

The financial data revealed a steady increase in funding for CCP services. The value of reimbursed services grew consistently over time. When expressed in millions of PLN, the monthly funding ranged from 0.44 million PLN in November 2022 to a high of 44.5 million PLN in April 2024. Since its implementation, a total of 640 million PLN has been allocated, following a steady monthly increase as described by the linear model (y = 2,037,483.19 · x + 3,125,969.82; x = month index starting from November 2022; R² = 0.906). On average, the monthly funding was approximately 26.56 million PLN (Fig. 2).

Funding of coordinated care program in Poland, 2022–2024. Source: Department of Health Care Services Benefits at the NHF. Data as of 08.01.2025.

A linear trend analysis of funding from November 2022 to October 2024 indicated a consistent upward trajectory in monthly expenditures. The linear regression model estimated an average monthly increase of 2.04 million PLN (29.09%). The intercept of 3.13 million PLN suggests an initial baseline investment required at the onset of implementation, independent of subsequent incremental growth. The coefficient of determination (R² = 0.906) confirms that 90.6% of the variance in the CCP funding can be explained by a linear trend, highlighting the program’s steady financial growth and indicating a strong governmental commitment.

Adoption of coordinated care by primary healthcare facilities

At the national level, 41% of the 6053 PHC facilities had participated in the program by the end of 2024. However, the adoption of CCP varied significantly across regions. Two years after the adoption of CCP across PHCs in Poland, eight regions performed above the national average. The Lubelski region reported the highest implementation rate, with 65% of PHC facilities participating in CC, while the Lubuski region had the lowest rate at 26%. Apart from the Lubuski region, the adoption of the CCP fell significantly below the average in PHCs across six more regions (Fig. 3). Among them, the Opolski and Lubulski regions have been identified as having the lowest overall GDP contributions in the country, presenting an opportunity for further development and enhancement in these areas [31].

Types of medical services offered within the coordinated care program in Poland, 2022–2024

The list of medical services offered by PHC facilities under CCP is specified in Appendix 6 of the Minister of Health’s Regulation of September 24, 2013, on guaranteed services in PHC. The services include the diagnosis and management of cardiovascular diseases, such as hypertension, heart failure, chronic ischemic heart disease, and atrial fibrillation, diabetes, including comprehensive metabolic assessments and lifestyle interventions, respiratory diseases, such as bronchial asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), thyroid disorders, covering hypothyroidism and the evaluation of thyroid nodules. Each category of care includes a combination of primary care consultations, specialist referrals, diagnostic tests (e.g., ECG, Doppler ultrasound, spirometry, laboratory biomarkers), dietary counseling, and patient education.

Coverage for primary healthcare patients with the coordinated care Program, 2022–2024

Regional differences were also evident in the percentage of PHC patients covered by the CCP. The Lubelski region had the highest patient coverage, with 72% of PHC patients included in CCP, while the Lubuski region reported only 32%. Nationwide, the average patient coverage was 51%, indicating substantial variation in access to the CCP services (Fig. 4).

Patients’ satisfaction with services provided through the coordinated care program

The research, led by the My Patients Foundation (pl. Fundacja My Pacjenci), assessed the experiences of 1,024 patients receiving CCP in Polish PHC. The study aimed to evaluate patient satisfaction, access to healthcare services, and the effectiveness of implementing coordinated care. Findings indicate a high level of patient satisfaction with the CCP. It was assessed using the Net Promoter Score (NPS) metric. The overall NPS was 75.46 (-100 to 100 range), indicating a very high level of patient satisfaction and the strong likelihood of recommending the program to others, as the majority of respondents (80.82%) were classified as ‘promoters’, meaning they rated their likelihood of recommending the CCP at 9 or 10 out of 10. Patients reported improved access to specialists, more efficient appointment scheduling, and enhanced communication with healthcare providers. The majority (90.92%) confirmed having an IMCP, with nearly 95% stating that most or all of their healthcare needs were met. Diagnostic services were significantly more accessible under the CCP, with shorter wait times compared to standard healthcare pathways. For instance, the waiting time for a Holter ECG was reduced from 16 to 2.5 weeks. Additionally, patients reported feeling well-informed about their treatment plans, with 96.49% confirming that they received clear communication from healthcare professionals. Despite these positive outcomes, patients reported limited access to psychological support and dietetic counseling. Some patients also highlighted challenges in reaching care coordinators and suggested expanding the program to include rehabilitation and neurological services [22].

Discussion

This study examined the extent to which the CCP was operationalized and what results were observed within the first two years of its implementation. The study findings demonstrate that the newly launched CCP holds significant promise in addressing the growing burden of chronic diseases in Poland. In this regard, we observed that the CCP facilitated integration between primary and specialist care for the following specialties: diabetology, cardiology, endocrinology, pulmonology, and nephrology. The PHC system enhanced the accessibility of healthcare services for 51% of PHC patients who were covered by CCP. The current expansion pattern of the CCP we found suggests that it is demand-driven, mainly, as evidenced by the steady, average monthly increase of 29.09% in the number of PHC facilities requiring funding allocation for the CCP implementation. The initial increase in funding that we observed in the study is expected, reflecting the program’s launch phase and the dynamic onboarding of participating facilities. However, future trends in funding may offer deeper insights into the program’s sustainability, particularly if they align more closely with service volume, care continuity, and clinical outcomes, rather than merely measuring an increase in the number of participating providers and monthly net funding. Performing comparative analysis of longitudinal funding and service outputs, as well as equality in meeting patient needs, will be crucial in assessing whether CCP maintains its value over time.

The high levels of patient satisfaction were observed in this study [30]. Patient reports on improved access to specialists, shorter waiting times for diagnostic procedures, and more efficient care coordination underscore the program’s potential. A significant asset of the CCP lies in its ability to streamline patient care through the establishment of IMCP, which was implemented for over 90% of the surveyed patients. The CCP structured IMCPs contributed to a more comprehensive and coordinated treatment process, thereby reducing service delivery fragmentation and enhancing patient adherence to medical recommendations. Therefore, the CCP establishes the foundation for integrated healthcare in Poland. Moreover, patients reported increased access to diagnostic services, with significantly reduced wait times compared to standard care pathways, confirming the study’s assumption that enhancing diagnostic and therapeutic capacity within CCP, supported by the development of IMCPs, additional funding, and regulatory frameworks, would facilitate better interdisciplinary collaboration. These mechanisms have been observed in other studies to lead to shorter diagnostic delays [33], improved management of chronic conditions [34], and higher patient satisfaction [35, 36]. Previous research indicates that personalized care planning can also contribute to reduced costs of care [37], modest but meaningful improvements in patients’ health outcomes, and a better ability to self-manage chronic conditions, particularly when such interventions are delivered in a structured, comprehensive, and well-integrated manner within routine care [38]. According to the literature, the CCP could even reduce unnecessary hospitalizations [39], improve population health outcomes such as mortality and case fatality [40–42], and enhance the PHC cost-effectiveness and equity [43], which are long-term benefits of the Polish CCP in the system. However, no data supporting these long-term objectives have been identified to date, which underscores the need for additional research.

A commendable aspect of the CCP is its ability to adapt to changing healthcare needs of the population covered. Our study highlights the program’s 2023 expansion, which includes specific nephrology services. This change reflects a proactive and responsive strategy in addressing emerging health concerns at the PHC level, in areas that had not been previously included in its framework. Given the country’s aging population and rising burden of non-communicable diseases [44, 45], future expansions may prioritize additional specialty services in CCP, such as oncology and geriatrics, or rehabilitation services and neurology, as advised by the surveyed patients [30].

Effective personalized care planning requires full accessibility to care coordinators, as reported by patients [30]. Their active engagement in multi-professional teams in developing, communicating, and implementing care plans should ensure continuity and coordination across services [46]. In this regard, the role of care coordinators appears to be a valuable structural component of the CCP, yet patients reported challenges in reaching them [30]. Consequently, future research should investigate the specific contributions of care coordinators to patient outcomes, team functionality, and continuity of care. Such evaluations would not only enhance the evidence base for care coordination in Poland but could also inform best practices for implementing CC models in diverse settings. Moreover, particularly in light of the recent influx of immigrants to Poland [29], the actual distribution of benefits may differ among patient subgroups based on variables such as age, gender, socioeconomic status, employment status, and geographical location (urban versus rural). To promote equity, additional research is essential to assess how the care coordination program addresses or potentially exacerbates existing healthcare disparities.

The geographic disparities in CCP facility adoption highlight the need for targeted efforts to ensure equitable access, particularly in rural and underserved regions. The observed geographic variation in CCP implementation aligns with previous research on uneven access and quality of PHC services across Polish regions [6, 18]. While some areas, such as the Lubelski region, have demonstrated high levels of implementation and patient coverage, likely due to stronger healthcare infrastructure and more effective policy execution, others, such as the Lubuski region, lag. The absence of data on provider satisfaction and healthcare professionals’ perceptions of the CCP can make it challenging to understand their needs and concerns before they contemplate participating in the program. Evidence suggests that the successful delivery of integrated care depends on effective collaboration and mutual adjustment among healthcare professionals [47]. However, high levels of administrative burden, burnout, and insufficient institutional support may reduce CCP provider engagement and weaken the fidelity of implementation [48]. Understanding providers’ perspectives and identifying barriers to their sustained involvement could inform improvements to the program’s design and implementation. Additionally, targeted efforts to promote the CCP model more actively among PHC facilities and PCPs may enhance its uptake, effectiveness, and long-term sustainability. Digital health interventions, including remote monitoring and teleconsultations, have shown potential in managing chronic diseases, such as hypertension, and could serve as valuable supplements to mitigate workforce shortages and infrastructure limitations [49].

Strengths and limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this study. First, the data were primarily derived from government sources and regulatory documents, which may not fully capture variations in implementation at the local level. Although focus groups or interviews with stakeholders could offer a deeper understanding of these research questions, the mixed-method approach included inquiries that provided verified institutional data on the CCP implementation, as well as strategic context relevant to the study’s objectives. In this way, even though the data collected might be considered limited in scope, they offered relevant insights into the CCP results. The financial data on CCP funding were available on a monthly basis from 2022 to 2024, the data on PHC participation and patient coverage were provided by the NHF only as a cross-sectional endpoint (as of December 2024). Consequently, it was not possible to perform a longitudinal or correlational analysis linking the variability in CCP implementation with funding levels. As for patients’ satisfaction, our study relied solely on a secondary analysis of an existing survey, we did not have access to primary or raw data. Consequently, regional identifiers were not available and, therefore, could not be applied in our analysis. Additionally, while the patient reports provided valuable insights into satisfaction levels, only a representative sample survey might offer generalizable insights into their satisfaction with the PHC - CCP coverage. Furthermore, the study did not assess long-term clinical outcomes or cost-effectiveness, which are critical for evaluating the sustainability of the CCP benefits initiative in the Polish PHC system. Moreover, the role of care coordinators, acknowledged as facilitators of integrated care, presents an opportunity for further exploration. It is essential to consider their potential to serve as positive role models on an international scale. Future research should incorporate longitudinal analyses, qualitative assessments of provider experiences, and a broader evaluation of patient health outcomes to refine and enhance the CCP’s impact.

Conclusions

In Poland, the CCP has contributed to improved access to diagnostics, specialist consultations, and chronic disease management within PHC. More specifically, patients covered with CCP were able to receive earlier diagnoses, start treatment sooner, access faster comprehensive care pathways, and enhance their capacity for self-management. Regional disparities in CCP adoption indicate that access to CCP services remains uneven, which could limit the program’s overall effectiveness. Future research should assess the equity of the CCP in addressing the needs of diverse patient groups and primary care providers to maintain its relevance and success.

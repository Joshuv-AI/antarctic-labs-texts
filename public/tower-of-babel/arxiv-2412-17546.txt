Jiansong Li, Heping Wang

Abstract




Abstract:
Consider the numerical integration

{\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})

for weighted Sobolev classes

BW_{p,w}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
with a Dunkl weight
w
and weighted
Besov classes
BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))
with the
generalized smoothness index
\Theta
and a doubling weight
w

on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
in the deterministic and randomized case settings. For

BW_{p,w}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
we obtain the optimal quadrature errors
in both settings. For
BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))
we
use the weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation and the standard
Monte Carlo algorithm to
obtain upper estimates of the quadrature errors which are
optimal if
w
is an
A_{\infty}
weight in the deterministic case
setting or if
w
is a product weight in the randomized case
setting. Our results show that randomized algorithms can provide
a faster convergence rate than that of the deterministic ones
when
p>1
.

Similar results are also established on the unit ball and the standard simplex of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Keywords:
Numerical integration; Weighted Sobolev classes;
Weighted Besov classes of generalized smoothness; Deterministic
and randomized case settings; Weighted least
\ell_{p}

approximation

Introduction

A critical problem in applied mathematics and data sciences is to calculate the numerical integration

{\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w}(f)=\int_{\Omega^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\mathsf{m}({\bf x}),

(1.1)

for a function
f
belonging to a class
F_{d}
of continuous
functions on
\Omega^{d}
, where
\Omega^{d}
is a compact subset of
the Euclidean space equipped with a measure
w({\bf x}){\rm d}\mathsf{m}({\bf x})
. For most cases, we could not compute this
integration utilizing the fundamental theorem of the calculus
since there is no closed form expression of the antiderivatives.
Therefore, we have to approximate such integration numerically.
Meanwhile, in the applications we only know function values on
finite points on
\Omega^{d}
which are called
standard
information
. Hence, we have to use algorithms based on these
finite function values.

One type of algorithms is the
deterministic algorithm
of
the form

A_{n}(f):=\varphi_{n}\left[f({\bf x}_{1}),f({\bf x}_{2}),\dots,f({\bf x}_{n})\right],

where
{\bf x}_{j}\in\Omega^{d}
can be chosen adaptively and
\varphi_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is an arbitrary mapping. We denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm det}

the class of all algorithms of this form. (Adaption means that the selection of

{\bf x}_{j}
may depend on the already computed values
f({\bf x}_{1}),f({\bf x}_{2}),\dots,f({\bf x}_{j-1})
.)
Then, for a positive integer
n
, we define

•

the
deterministic case error
of an algorithm
A_{n}
on
F_{d}
by

e^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d},\,A_{n}):=\sup\limits_{f\in F_{d}}\left|{\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w}(f)-A_{n}(f)\right|;

•

the
n
-th minimal (optimal) deterministic case error
on
F_{d}
by

e_{n}^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d}):=\inf\limits_{A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm det}}e^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d},\,A_{n}).

Usually we assume that the class
F_{d}
is convex and
balanced. It follows from
[
3
]
that the
n
-th minimal
deterministic case error
e_{n}^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d})

can be achieved by linear algorithms, i.e., the algorithms of the
form

A_{n}^{\rm lin}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\lambda}_{j}f({\bf x}_{j}),\ {\lambda}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\ j=1,\dots,n,

which is also called a
quadrature formula
,
and, moreover, it follows from
[
46
, Theorem 4.7]
that

e_{n}^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf x}_{1},\dots,{\bf x}_{n}\in\Omega^{d}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F_{d}\\
f({\bf x}_{1})=\dots=f({\bf x}_{n})=0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\mathsf{m}({\bf x}).

(1.2)

We say that a quadrature formula
A_{n}^{\rm lin}
is
positive
if
{\lambda}_{j}\geq 0,j=1,\dots,n
, and is a

quasi-Monte-Carlo (QMC) algorithm
if
{\lambda}_{j}=1/n,j=1,\dots,n
.

For usual smooth classes the integration problem in the
deterministic setting suffers from the curse of dimensionality if
the dimension
d
is large. It is well known that the standard
Monte-Carlo algorithm overcomes the curse of dimensionality. In
this paper we consider randomized algorithms (Monte-Carlo
algorithms).
Randomized algorithms
(also called Monte-Carlo
algorithms) are understood as
\Sigma\otimes\mathcal{B}(F_{d})

measurable functions

(A^{\omega})=(A^{\omega}(\cdot))_{{\omega}\in\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{F}\times F_{d}\to\mathbb{R},

where
\mathcal{B}(F_{d})
denotes the Borel

\sigma
-algebra of
F_{d}
,
(\mathcal{F},\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is
a suitable probability space, and for any fixed
{\omega}\in\mathcal{F}
,
A^{\omega}
is a deterministic method with cardinality

n(f,{\omega})
. The number
n(f,{\omega})
may be randomized and
adaptively depend on the input, and the cardinality of
(A^{\omega})

is then defined by

{\rm Card}(A^{\omega}):=\sup_{f\in F_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\,n(f,{\omega}):=\sup\limits_{f\in F_{d}}\int_{\mathcal{F}}n(f,\omega){\rm d}\mathcal{P}(\omega).

Denote by

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm ran}
the class of all randomized algorithms
with cardinality not exceeding
n
. Then, for a positive integer

n
, we define

•

the
randomized case error
of a randomized algorithm
(A^{\omega})
on
F_{d}
by

e^{\rm ran}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d},(A^{\omega})):=\sup\limits_{f\in F_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|{\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w}(f)-A^{\omega}(f)|;

•

the
n
-th minimal (optimal) randomized case error
on
F_{d}
by

e_{n}^{\rm ran}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d}):=\inf\limits_{(A^{\omega})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm ran}}e^{\rm ran}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d},(A^{\omega})).

Since a deterministic algorithm may be viewed as a randomized
algorithm, it follows from definitions that

e_{n}^{\rm ran}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d})\leq e_{n}^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d}).

Numerous results indicate that the optimal randomized quadrature
errors are generally less than optimal deterministic quadrature
errors. Consequently, employing randomized algorithms proves to be
an effective strategy for reducing errors in a variety of
applications, particularly in dealing with high-dimensional
scenarios.

Researchers have shown significant interest in the integration problem described in (
1.1
) for Sobolev-type and Besov-type spaces.
These function spaces are crucial in functional analysis, numerical analysis,
approximation theory, and related fields (see, for example,
[
51
,
52
,
15
]
).
We can now review some previous findings in this area.
There is a vast literature on this classical problem for various unweighted function classes, especially for Sobolev and Besov classes. For abbreviation, when
w\equiv 1
, we write
e_{n}^{\rm det}(F_{d})\equiv e_{n}^{\rm det}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d})
and
e_{n}^{\rm ran}(F_{d})\equiv e_{n}^{\rm ran}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F_{d})
.

Now let us briefly introduce our results on the unit sphere

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

,

\Theta_{1}(t)

\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}

e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}};BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})\right)\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}};

(1.12)

(ii)

If

, then for

Moreover, the upper estimates hold for all doubling weights.

Remark 1
.

The upper bound of (

since the Dunkl weight

L_{q.w}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
for the case of
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
.
Let

be a class of continuous functions on
\Omega^{d}
, and

(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a normed linear space of functions on

\Omega^{d}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

•

the

of

in

\displaystyle g_{n}(F_{d},X)\,:=\,\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ \boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}\in\Omega^{d}\\
\varphi:\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow X\end{subarray}}\sup_{f\in F_{d}}\|f-\varphi(f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}),\dots,f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}))\|_{X},

•

the
n
-th linear sampling number
of
F_{d}
in
X
as

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\rm lin}(F_{d},X)\,:=\,\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}\in\Omega^{d}\\
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\in X\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{f\in F_{d}}\,\Big\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i})\,\psi_{i}\Big\|_{X}.

Clearly, we have

g_{n}^{\rm lin}(F_{d},X)\geq g_{n}(F_{d},X),

(1.14)

and it is well known (see
[
53
]
) that for a balanced convex set

,

g_{n}(F_{d},X)\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}\in\Omega^{d}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F_{d}\\
f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})=\dots=f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n})=0\end{subarray}}\|f\|_{X}.

(1.15)

We obtain the following Theorem
1.3
which gives sharp
asymptotic orders of sampling numbers for

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq\infty
and
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
.
If

is an

weight with the critical index

(1.16)

In particular, for

g_{n}^{\rm lin}(BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{2,w}(\mathbb{S}^{d})),L_{q,w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))\asymp\Theta(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}).

(1.17)

Moreover, the above upper estimates hold also for all doubling weights.

Remark 3

For the special case

Basics.

For an integer

Given a spherical cap

In particular,

is a Hilbert space with the inner

A nonnegative integrable function

2.2
Weighted Besov classes of generalized smoothness on the sphere.

Now we give the definition of weighted Besov classes of generalized smoothness on the sphere. For the unweighted case, it was first introduced in
[
57
]
.

Let
s\geq 1
be a fixed positive number and let
\Theta
denote a nonnegative function on
[0,\infty)
.
We say that
\Theta(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}

if it satisfies:

1.

\Theta(0)=0
and
\Theta(t)>0
for all
t>0
;

2.

\Theta(t)
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
;

3.

\Theta(t)
is almost increasing on
[0,\infty)
, i.e., for any
t_{1},t_{2}
with
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

\Theta(t_{1})\leq C\Theta(t_{2}),

where
C\geq 1
is a constant independent of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
;

4.

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>0
,

\Theta(nt)\leq Cn^{s}\Theta(t),

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
n
and
t
;

5.

there exists
\alpha_{1}>0
such that
\Theta(t)/t^{\alpha_{1}}
is almost increasing on
[0,\infty)
;

6.

there exists
0<\alpha_{2}<s
such that
\Theta(t)/t^{\alpha_{2}}
is almost decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, i.e., for any
t_{1},t_{2}
with
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

\Theta(t_{1})/t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}\geq C\Theta(t_{2})/t_{2}^{\alpha_{2}},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
.

The definition of weighted Besov spaces is based on the
best
approximation
of a function
f\in L_{p,w}
from
\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
defined by

E_{n}(f)_{p,w}:=\inf\limits_{g\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|f-g\|_{p,w},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Now let
w
be a doubling weight on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
\Theta(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
, we define the
weighted Besov spaces of generalized smoothness

B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})\equiv B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))
to be the space of all real
functions
f
with finite quasi-norm

\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}:=\|f\|_{p,w}+\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f)_{p,w}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\right)^{\gamma}\right\}^{1/\gamma},

while the
weighted Besov class

BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
is defined to be the unit ball of
the weighted Besov space
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
. A prototype
of functions belonging to
\Phi_{s}^{*}
is

\Theta(t)=t^{r}(1+(\ln\frac{1}{t})_{+})^{-\beta}
,
0<r<s
,

\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, in which case, the space

B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
is the weighted Besov space

B_{\gamma}^{r,\beta}(L_{p,w})\equiv B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})

with logarithmic perturbation. Particularly, if
\beta=0
, then
the space
B_{\gamma}^{r,\beta}(L_{p,w})
coincides with the usual
weighted Besov space
B_{\gamma}^{r}(L_{p,w})
.

Clearly, it follows from the definition of
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
that the Jackson inequality

E_{n}(f)_{p,w}\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1})\,\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}

(2.6)

holds for
f\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
. Furthermore, we also have the following embedding theorem, which has been proved in
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
for the special case of
\Theta(t)=t^{r}
.

Proposition 2.1

(Embedding Theorem)
.

Let
\Theta_{1}(t),\Theta(t):=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
and let
w
be a doubling weight on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
, if
r>(1/p-1/q)_{+}s_{w}
,
then
2
2

2

Here, the notation
X\hookrightarrow Y
means that the space
X
is continuously
embedded into the space
Y
, i.e.,
\|f\|_{Y}\leq C\|f\|_{X}
for all
f\in X
.

B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p.w})\hookrightarrow L_{q,w}
. Furthermore, if
r>s_{w}/p
, then
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p.w})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
.

Proof.

For
\ell=0,1,\dots
, let
\delta_{\ell}(f)
be a best approximiant
of
f\in L_{p,w}
from
\Pi_{\ell}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, i.e.,

E_{\ell}(f)_{p,w}=\|f-\delta_{\ell}(f)\|_{p,w}.
Note that the best
approximant
polynomials
\delta_{\ell}(f)
always exist since
\Pi_{\ell}(\mathbb{S}^{d})

are the finite dimensional linear spaces (see, for instance,

[
38
, p.17, Theorem 1]
). Define

\sigma_{j}(f)=\delta_{2^{j-1}}(f)-\delta_{2^{j-2}}(f),\ \ {\rm for}\ j=1,2,\dots,

where
\delta_{2^{-1}}(f)=0
. Obviously,
\sigma_{j}(f)\in\Pi_{2^{j-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
. Then it follows from (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{p,w}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-\delta_{2^{j-1}}(f)\|_{p,w}+\|f-\delta_{2^{j-2}}(f)\|_{p,w}\lesssim E_{2^{j-2}}(f)_{p,w}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}.

(2.7)

which, by the weighted Nikolskii’s inequality (
2.5
), implies that

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{q,w}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(j-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}s_{w}}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}.

(2.8)

By Lusin’s theorem and the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, the space of spherical polynomials is dense in
L_{p,w}
for

1\leq p<\infty
and in
C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
when
p=\infty
, which
implies that the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(f)
converges
to
f
in the
L_{p,w}
norm.

For
1\leq q<\infty
, by the trigonometric inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q,w}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{q,w}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[2^{(j-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}s_{w}}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)[r-(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}s_{w}]}\Theta_{1}(2^{-1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})},

where we used
\Theta_{1}(2^{-(j-2)})\leq C\Theta_{1}(2^{-1})
for
j=1,2,\dots
since
\Theta_{1}\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
.

For
q=\infty
, the above argument shows that the series

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(f)
converges
uniformly on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Hence, in this case,
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
.

This completes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

2.3
Weighted Sobolev classes on the sphere.

To give the definition of weighted Sobolev spaces on the sphere,
we shall need some facts about Dunkl harmonics. Now let

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
be the Dunkl weight of the form
(
2.2
) being invariant under the reflection group
G
. The
essential ingredient of the theory of Dunkl harmonics is a family of
first-order differential-difference operators, called Dunkl
operators, which generates a commutative algebra. These operators
are defined by

\mathcal{D}_{i}f({\bf x}):=\partial_{i}f({\bf x})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}\frac{f({\bf x})-f(\sigma_{{\bf v}_{j}}{\bf x})}{\langle{\bf x},{\bf v}_{j}\rangle}\langle{\bf v}_{j},{\bf e}_{i}\rangle,\ i=1,\dots,d+1,

where
\partial_{i}\equiv\partial/\partial{x_{i}}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to
x_{i}
, and

{\bf e}_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0),\ {\bf e}_{2}=(0,1,\dots,0),\dots,\ {\bf e}_{d+1}=(0,0,\dots,1)

denote the orthonormal basis
for
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Then the Dunkl-Laplace operator

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is defined by

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}+\dots+\mathcal{D}_{d+1}^{2},

which plays the role similar to that of the ordinary
Laplacian. It was proved in
[
12
]
that

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=\frac{\Delta(fw_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})-f\Delta(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})}{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}-2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}\frac{f({\bf x})-f(\sigma_{{\bf v}_{j}}{\bf x})}{\langle{\bf x},{\bf v}_{j}\rangle^{2}}.

In particular, if
\mathcal{R}_{+}=\{{\bf e}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
,
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{d+1}
, then

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}f=\Delta f+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\kappa_{j}\left(\frac{2}{x_{j}}\partial_{j}f-\frac{f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j},\dots,x_{d+1})-f(x_{1},\dots,-x_{j},\dots,x_{d+1})}{x_{j}^{2}}\right).

A Dunkl-harmonic polynomial
Y
is a homogeneous
polynomial
Y
such that
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}Y=0
. Furthermore, let

\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}):=\{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{d+1}:\,\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}P=0\}

be the space of the Dunkl harmonics of degree
n
. Then for
n\neq m
,

\langle P,Q\rangle_{L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}}=0,\ \ P\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}),\ Q\in\mathcal{H}_{m}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}).

Throughout this paper, we fix
the value of
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
as

\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}:=|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|+\frac{d-1}{2}.

In terms of the polar coordinates
{\bf y}=r{\bf y}^{\prime},r=\|{\bf y}\|
, the Dunkl-Laplacian operator
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}

takes the form

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}:=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{2\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}+1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0},

where
\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}
is the Dunkl-Laplace-Beltrami operator on the sphere. Hence, applying
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}

to Dunkl harmonics
Y\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
with
Y({\bf y})=r^{n}Y({\bf y}^{\prime})
shows that spherical Dunkl harmonics
are eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}
; that is,

\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}Y({\bf x})=-n(n+2\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})Y({\bf x}),\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},\ Y\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}).

The density of polynomial spaces shows that

L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})=\bigoplus\limits_{k=0}^{n}\,\mathcal{H}_{k}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}).

Let

\{Y_{n,k}:\,k=1,\dots,N(d,n)\}

be a collection of
L_{2}
orthonormal real basis of
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Chapter 6]
).
Then the orthogonal projector
{\rm proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}:L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
given by

{\rm proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(f)={\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,n)}\langle f,Y_{n,k}\rangle_{L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}}Y_{n,k}}

can be written
as

{\rm proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(f,{\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f({\bf y})P_{n}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}};{\bf x},{\bf y})w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}({\bf y}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf y}),

where
P_{n}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}};{\bf x},{\bf y})
is the reproducing kernel of the space of Dunkl harmonics
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})
.
Moreover, for
f\in L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
,

f({\bf x})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(f,{\bf x}),

in the
L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
norm.

Given
r>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the
weighted
Sobolev space

W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}\equiv W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
to be the space of all real
functions
f
with finite norm

\|f\|_{W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}}:=\|f\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}+\|(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0})^{r/2}f\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}},

where the operator
(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0})^{r/2}
is given by

(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0})^{r/2}f({\bf x})\ \sim\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(n(n+2\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}})\right)^{r/2}{\rm proj}_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\left(f,{\bf x}\right),

in the distribution sense, while the
weighted Sobolev class

BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
is defined to be the unit ball of
the weighted Sobolev space
W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
. When

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=1
,
W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
recedes
to the classical unweighted Sobolev space
W_{p}^{r}
. In this case,
we write
W_{p}^{r}\equiv W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
and

\|\cdot\|_{W_{p}^{r}}\equiv\|\cdot\|_{W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}}
for simplicity.
Moreover, we have the following results:

•

Bernstein’s inequality (
[
62
, (3.4)]
): for
r>0
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
,

\|(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0})^{r/2}P\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\lesssim n^{r}\|P\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}};

(2.9)

•

Jackson’s inequality (
[
62
, Theorem 3.3]
): for
r>0
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
f\in W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
,

E_{n}(f)_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\lesssim n^{-r}\|f\|_{W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}}.

(2.10)

By Jackson’s inequality we obtain

\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})}:=\|f\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}+\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(f)_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}},

(2.11)

which means that the weighted Sobolev space

W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
can be continuously embedded into
the weighted Besov space

B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})
;

•

Embedding Theorem: if

r>(1/p-1/q)_{+}s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
, then

W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}\hookrightarrow L_{q,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
. Furthermore, if

r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}/p
, then

W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
.
Indeed, this can be derived from (
2.11
) and Proposition

2.1
.

3
Proofs of main results

In this section we are going to discuss the numerical integration
(
1.1
) on the unit sphere. The asymptotic orders of the
weighted Sobolev classes and the weighted Besov classes in the
deterministic and randomized case settings will be determined. In
the deterministic case setting, for the upper estimates, we use a
positive quadrature formula; for the lower estimates, we will
employ the identity presented in (
1.2
). In the randomized case
setting, for the upper estimates, we use a constructive polynomial
approximation based on the points that satisfying weighted
L_{p}

Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality and the standard Monte-Carlo
methods; for the lower estimates, we will implement Novak’s
classical technique involving fooling functions.

3.1
Deterministic case.

This subsection is devoted to proving the estimates of deterministic case errors.
More precisely,
let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
. The following results will be established.

(i)

If
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is a Dunkl weight
with the critical index
s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}

on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
then for
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}};BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}\right)\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

(3.1)

(ii)

If
w
is an
A_{\infty}
weight
with the critical index
s_{w}

on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
, then for
r>s_{w}/p
,

(3.2)

3.1.1
Upper estimates.

First we begin to address the proof of the upper estimates. As mentioned in Remark
1
, it remains to prove (
3.2
). This can be straightforwardly verified through the following proposition, which is based on the positive cubature formulas.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
, and let
w
be a doubling weight with the critical index
s_{w}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Suppose
that the following positive cubature formula holds for all
f\in\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i})=:Q_{n}(f),\ \min_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda_{i}>0,

(3.3)

with
\Lambda:=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
being a finite subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
satisfying
N^{d}\leq n\leq C_{1}N^{d}
. Then, for
r>s_{w}/p
, we have

\sup_{f\in BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}\left|{\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w}(f)-Q_{n}(f)\right|\leq C_{2}\Theta\big(n^{-1/d}\big),

(3.4)

where the constant
C_{2}>0
is independent of
f
and
n
.

The proof is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
55
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
12
, Lemma 5.4.5]
.)

Let
w
be a doubling weight on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and let
\mu
be a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p_{0}}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\leq C_{0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p_{0}}w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x}),\ {for\ all}\ f\in\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d}),

(3.5)

for some
1\leq p_{0}<\infty
. Then for
1\leq p<\infty
and
M\geq N
, we
have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\leq C_{0}C\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)^{s_{w}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x}),\ {for\ all}\ f\in\Pi_{M}(\mathbb{S}^{d}),

where

C>0
depends only on
d
,
p
, and
w
.

Now we are in a position to give the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

For a fixed positive integer
n
, it is easy to see that there exists a nonnegative integer
m
such that

2^{m-1}\leq N<2^{m}
and
n\asymp\dim\Pi_{N}^{d}\asymp N^{d}
. We keep the notations
\sigma_{j}
and
\delta_{k}
in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

According to Theorem
2.1
we know that if
f\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})

and
r>s_{w}/p
,
then the series
f=\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)+\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(f)
converges uniformly on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Thus, using the
Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle I_{n}(f,\Lambda,w)

\displaystyle:=\left|{\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w}(f)-Q_{n}(f)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\sigma_{j}(f)({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sigma_{j}(f)({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\left\{\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{p,w}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}|\sigma_{j}(f)({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i})|^{p}\right)^{1/{p}}\right\},

Clearly, we derive from (
3.3
) that (
3.5
) is
true for the discrete measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}}
with
p_{0}=2
and
C_{0}=1
. Notice
that
\sigma_{j}(f)\in\Pi_{2^{j-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, and
2^{j-1}\geq N
for
j\geq m+1
. Then it follows from Lemma
3.1
and (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}|\sigma_{j}(f)({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i})|^{p}\right)^{1/{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{2^{j-1}}{N}\right)^{s_{w}/p}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{p,w}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(j-1)s_{w}/p}N^{-s_{w}/p}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}.

Thus, we obtain,

\displaystyle I_{n}(f,\Lambda,w)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{s_{w}}{p}}\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}2^{(j-1)s_{w}/p}\Theta\big(2^{-(j-2)}\big)\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}2^{(j-1)s_{w}/p}\Theta\big(2^{-(j-2)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{j=m+1}2^{-(j-1)(r-\frac{s_{w}}{p})}\Theta_{1}\big(2^{-(j-2)}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta_{1}\big(2^{-(m-1)}\big)\sum^{\infty}_{j=m+1}2^{-(j-1)(r-\frac{s_{w}}{p})}

\displaystyle\asymp 2^{-(m-1)(r-\frac{s_{w}}{p})}\Theta_{1}\big(2^{-(m-1)}\big)

\displaystyle\asymp N^{\frac{s_{w}}{p}}\Theta\big(n^{-1/d}\big),

where in the last equivalence we used

\Theta_{1}\left(2^{-(m-1)}\right)\asymp\Theta_{1}\left(n^{-1/d}\right),

(3.6)

since
\Theta_{1}\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
and
2^{m-1}\asymp N\asymp n^{-1/d}
.
Therefore, we get

\displaystyle I_{n}(f,\Lambda,w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-1/d}\big)\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})},

which proves (
3.4
).
∎

3.1.2
Lower estimates.

To establish the lower estimates, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
11
, Proposition 4.8]
.)

Let
w
be an
A_{\infty}
weight on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and let
X
be a linear subspace of
\Pi_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
with
\dim X\geq\varepsilon\,\dim\Pi_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
for some

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a function
f\in X
such
that
\|f\|_{p,w}\asymp 1
for
1\leq p\leq\infty

with the constants of equivalence depending only on
\varepsilon,d
and the
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
.

Proof of lower estimates.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}
be any
n

distinct points on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Take a positive integer
N
such
that
2n\leq\dim\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d})\leq C_{0}n
, and denote

X_{0}:=\big\{g\in\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d}):\ g(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j})=0\ {\rm for\ all}\ j=1,\dots,n\big\}.

(3.7)

Then,
X_{0}
is a linear subspace of
\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
with

\dim X_{0}\geq\dim\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d})-n\geq\frac{1}{2}\dim\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d}).

Case 1.
Let
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
be a Dunkl weight
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. It follows from Lemma
3.2
that there exists a function

g_{0}\in X_{0}
such that

\|g_{0}\|_{p_{0},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\asymp 1
for all
1\leq p_{0}\leq\infty
. Let
f_{0}({\bf x})=N^{-r}(g_{0}({\bf x}))^{2}
. Then by
f_{0}\in\Pi_{2N}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
and (
2.9
) we have

\|f_{0}\|_{W_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}}=\|f_{0}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}+\|(-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},0})^{r/2}f_{0}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\lesssim N^{r}\|f_{0}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}=\|g_{0}\|_{2p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{2}\asymp 1.

Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
0<f_{1}=Cf_{0}\in BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
, and

f_{1}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})=\dots=f_{1}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n})=0,\ \ \int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f_{1}({\bf x})w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})\asymp N^{-r}\|g_{0}\|_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{2}\asymp n^{-r/d}.

Since
BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}
is convex and balanced, we derive from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}};BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}\right)\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f_{1}({\bf x})w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})\asymp n^{-r/d}.

Case 2.
Let
w
be an
A_{\infty}
weight
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
. It follows from Lemma
3.2
that there exists a function
g_{0}\in\Pi_{N}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
with
\|g_{0}\|_{p_{0},w}\asymp 1
for all
1\leq p_{0}\leq\infty
.
Let
f_{0}({\bf x})=\Theta\left(N^{-1}\right)(g_{0}({\bf x}))^{2}
, and
let
s
be an integer such that
2^{s-1}\leq N<2^{s}
. Then by
f_{0}\in\Pi_{2^{s+1}}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
and
E_{2^{j}}(f_{0})_{p,w}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{p,w}
we have

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}

\displaystyle=\|f_{0}\|_{p,w}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\Big(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f_{0})_{p,w}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma}\|f_{0}\|_{p,w}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}\left(\frac{1}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(s+1)\alpha_{1}\gamma}\Theta\left(2^{-s-1}\right)^{-\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}2^{j\alpha_{1}\gamma}

\displaystyle\asymp 2^{-(s+1)\alpha_{1}\gamma}\Theta\left(2^{-s-1}\right)^{-\gamma}2^{(s+1)\alpha_{1}\gamma}

\displaystyle=\Theta\left(2^{-s-1}\right)^{-\gamma},

(3.8)

where in the first inequality we used

\frac{\Theta\left(2^{-j}\right)}{2^{-j\alpha_{1}}}\gtrsim\frac{\Theta\left(2^{-(s+1)}\right)}{2^{-(s+1)\alpha_{1}}},\ 0\leq j\leq s+1,

for some
\alpha_{1}>0
since
\Theta\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta\left(2^{-s-1}\right)^{-1}\Theta\left(N^{-1}\right)\|g_{0}\|_{2p,w}^{2}\asymp 1.

Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

0<f_{1}=Cf_{0}\in BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
, and

f_{1}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})=\dots=f_{1}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n})=0,\ \int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f_{1}({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})\asymp\Theta(N^{-1})\|g_{0}\|_{2,w}^{2}\asymp\Theta(n^{-1/d}).

Since
BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})
is convex and balanced, we derive from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w};BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f_{1}({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})\asymp\Theta(n^{-1/d}),

which finishes the proof of the lower estimates.
∎

3.2
Randomized case.

This subsection focuses on establishing estimates for randomized case errors.
More precisely,
let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
. The following results will be proved.

(i)

If
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is a Dunkl weight with the critical index
s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
then for
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}\left({\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}};BW_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{r}\right)\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{+}}.

(3.9)

(ii)

If
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is a product weight with the critical index
s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
, then for
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}\left({\rm Int}_{\mathbb{S}^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}};BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})\right)\asymp\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big)n^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

(3.10)

3.2.1
Upper estimates.

We will first present the proof of upper estimates. As mentioned in Remark
1
, it remains to prove the upper estimate of (
3.10
). For our purpose, it is essential to develop an algorithm to attain the upper bound.
We will use constructive polynomial approximation that
uses function values (the samples) at selected well-distributed points (sometimes
called standard information).
Here the “well-distributed” points indicate that those points satisfy
L_{p,w}
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund (MZ) inequalities on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
as follows.

Definition
.

Given a set of points
\{{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2},\dots,{\bf x}_{N}\}

in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and a set of positive numbers
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{N}\}
, we say that they constitute
an
L_{p,w}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family
for
\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, denoted by
L_{p,w}
-MZ, if there exist two constants
A,\,B>0
independent
of
n
and
N
such that

A\|P\|_{p,w}^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\tau_{k}|P({\bf x}_{k})|^{p}\leq B\|P\|_{p,w}^{p},\ {\rm for\ all}\ P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d});

(3.11)

and they constitute an
L_{\infty}
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family for
\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, denoted by
L_{\infty}
-MZ, if there exists a constant
C>0
independent
of
n
such that

\|P\|_{\infty}\leq C\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|P({\bf x}_{k})|,\ {\rm for\ all}\ P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d}).

Remark 4
.

Equivalently, an
L_{p,w}
-MZ family means that the
L_{p}
norm of
P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
is comparable to the discritized norm
\|P\|_{(p)}
given by the weighted
\ell_{p}
norm of its restriction to these points, where

\|f\|_{(p)}:=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\tau_{k}|f({\bf x}_{k})|^{p}\Big)^{1/p},&&1\leq p<\infty,\\
&\max\limits_{k=1,\dots,N}|f({\bf x}_{k})|,&&p=\infty,\end{aligned}=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{N}({\bf x})\Big)^{1/p},&&1\leq p<\infty,\\
&\max\limits_{k=1,\dots,N}|f({\bf x}_{k})|,&&p=\infty,\end{aligned}

for
f\in C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, and

\mu_{N}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tau_{k}\delta_{{\bf x}_{k}}.

Remark 5
.

For a doubling weight
w
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, such an
L_{p,w}
-MZ family for
\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
exists. For example,
it follows from
[
12
, Chapter 5]
that there exists a

\delta_{0}>0
such that for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, a maximal

\delta/n
-separated subset
\{{\bf x}_{1},\dots,{\bf x}_{N}\}
is an

L_{p,w}
-MZ family
with
\dim\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})\leq N\asymp n^{d}
and
\{\tau_{k}:=w({\rm c}({\bf x}_{k},\delta/n)),k=1,\dots,N\}
.

Weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation.

Based on an
L_{p,w}
-MZ family, we can construct a weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
for a continuous function on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Definition
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then, for
f\in C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, its
weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation
L_{n,p}(f)
is defined by

L_{n,p}(f):=\arg\min\limits_{P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,\|f-P\|_{(p)}.

Remark 6
.

Obviously, the solution of this problem exists for all
f\in C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
. Further, if
1<p<\infty
, then the solution is
unique. If
p=1
or
\infty
, then the solution may be not unique
and we can choose any solution to be
L_{n,p}(f)
. We note the
operator
L_{n,p}
is not linear except the case
p=2
.

Remark 7
.

By the definition of
L_{n,p}
we have

\|f-L_{n,p}(f)\|_{(p)}=\min_{P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|f-P\|_{(p)},

(3.12)

which follows that for all
P\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
,

\|f-L_{n,p}(f)\|_{(p)}\leq\|f-P\|_{(p)}.

(3.13)

Such approximation operators were first introduced by Gröchenig
in

with

on a compact space,

and least squares quadrature were investigated in

on a compact smooth Riemannian manifold with normalized Riemannian measure. In this paper we will use the methods in

to study the weighted case on the sphere.
We now present the approximation theorem for the weighted least

\ell_{p}
approximation.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
0<\gamma\leq\infty
, and
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
. If

is a doubling weight
with the critical index

on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, then for

with

\|f-L_{n,p}(f)\|_{q,w}\leq C\Theta\left(n^{-1}\right)n^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}}\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})},

(3.14)

where
C>0
are independent of

and
n
.

Proof.

We are going to prove the case of
1\leq p<\infty
, since the case
p=\infty
can be easily checked with a little difference. We keep the notations
\sigma_{j}
and
\delta_{\ell}
in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
Let
m
be an integer such that
2^{m-1}\leq n<2^{m}
.
If
r>s_{w}/p
, then for

,

\displaystyle\|f-L_{n,p}(f)\|_{q,w}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{q,w}+\|L_{n,p}(f)-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{q,w}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(3.15)

On the one hand, since the

converges to

in
L_{q,w}
norm, by (
2.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{q,w}\lesssim\sum\limits_{j=m+1}^{\infty}2^{(j-1)\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})\,\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})},

Notice that

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{j=m+1}^{\infty}2^{(j-1)\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(m-1)\alpha_{2}}\Theta_{1}(2^{-(m-1)})\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}2^{(j-1)\left(-\alpha_{2}-r+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}\right)}

\displaystyle\asymp 2^{(m-1)\alpha_{2}}\Theta_{1}(2^{-(m-1)})2^{(m-1)\left(-\alpha_{2}-r+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1})n^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}},

(3.16)

where in the first inequality we used

\frac{\Theta_{1}\left(2^{-(j-2)}\right)}{2^{-(j-2)\alpha_{2}}}\lesssim\frac{\Theta_{1}\left(2^{-(m-1)}\right)}{2^{-(m-1)\alpha_{2}}},\ j\geq m+1,

for some

. Thus, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1})n^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}}\,\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})},

(3.17)

On the other hand, we derive from
(
3.11
) and (
3.13
) that

\displaystyle\|L_{n,p}(f)-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{p,w}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|L_{n,p}(f)-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{(p)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{n,p}(f)-f\|_{(p)}+\|f-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{(p)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{(p)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{(p)}.

(3.18)

According to (
3.11
) we know that (
3.5
) is
true for

with

and
C_{0}=B
. Note

.
It follows from Lemma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|\sigma_{j}(f)({\bf x})|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{N}({\bf x})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{2^{j-1}}{n}\right)^{s_{w}}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})^{p}\,\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}^{p}

\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{(p)}\lesssim n^{-\frac{s_{w}}{p}}2^{(j-1)\frac{s_{w}}{p}}\Theta(2^{-(j-2)})\,\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}.

(3.19)

Hence, for

\displaystyle\|L_{n,p}(f)-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{p,w}

(3.20)

Note that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}}\|L_{n,p}(f)-\delta_{2^{m-1}}(f)\|_{p,w}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1})n^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}s_{w}}\,\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w})}.

By (

∎

Standard Monte
Carlo algorithm.

To achieve the upper estimates, following

[

]
, we also need a concrete Monte Carlo method by

follows: let
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
be independent,

\mathbb{S}^{d}
-valued, distributed over

with

random

Following the proof of

(3.23)

Here we omit the proof.

Proof of upper estimates of (
3.10
).

For a positive integer

, define

and let

. Here,

be the weighted least

-MZ family

where

3.2.2
Lower estimates

For the proof of lower estimates, we use the technique of Novak,
which relies on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
43
]
or
[
44
, Lemma 3]
.)
Assume that
\Omega^{d}
is the multivariate compact domain with a weight function
w
.

(a)

Let
F\subset L_{1,w}(\Omega^{d})
and
\psi_{j},\,j=1,\dots,4n
, with the
following conditions:

(i)

the functions
\psi_{j},\,j=1,\dots,4n
have disjoint supports and satisfy

{\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w}(\psi_{j})=\int_{\Omega^{d}}\psi_{j}({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x}\geq{\delta},\ {\rm for}\ j=1,\dots,4n.

(ii)

F_{1}:=\big\{\sum_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\psi_{j}:\,\alpha_{j}\in\{-1,1\}\big\}\subset F
.

Then

e_{n}^{\rm ran}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\delta}n^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(b)

We assume that instead of (ii) in statement (a) the
property

(ii’)
F_{2}:=\left\{\pm\psi_{j}:\,j=1,\dots,4n\right\}\subset F
.

Then

e^{\rm ran}_{n}({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};F)\geq\frac{1}{4}{\delta}.

Therefore, to obtain the lower estimates of randomized quadrature errors, we shall construct a sequence of functions

\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4n}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma

3.4
, which are often call the fooling functions. Their construction follows by the similar arguments as stated in the estimates of the entropy numbers (see
[
61
]
).

Construct fooling functions.

Let

\boldsymbol{\kappa}:=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m})\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,

{\bf v}_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,m
, and

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}({\textbf{x}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left|\langle{\bf x},{\bf v}_{j}\rangle\right|^{2\kappa_{j}},\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}.

For

j=1,\dots,m
, we denote

E_{j}:=\left\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:\left|\frac{\pi}{2}-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf x},{\bf v}_{j})\right|\leq 2\varepsilon_{d,m}\right\},

and

\widetilde{E}_{j}:=\left\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:\left|\frac{\pi}{2}-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf x},{\bf v}_{j})\right|\leq\varepsilon_{d,m}\right\},

where
\varepsilon_{d,m}
is a sufficiently small positive
constant depending only on
d
and
m
. Furthermore, let

E:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}E_{j}
and
\widetilde{E}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{E}_{j}
. Clearly, we have
|E_{1}|=\dots=|E_{m}|\asymp\varepsilon_{d,m}
. Then the Lebesgue measure of
E
satisfies

|E|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|E_{j}|\leq c_{d}m\varepsilon_{d,m}\leq\frac{1}{2},

provided that
\varepsilon_{d,m}
is small enough. Thus, the
Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash\widetilde{E}

satisfies

|\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash\widetilde{E}|\geq|\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash{E}|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Given a positive
integer
n
, we can choose a sufficient large positive integer
N

such that
N\asymp n^{1/d}
and
N>\varepsilon_{d,m}^{-1}
. We
take a maximal
2/N
-separated subset
\{{\bf x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
of

\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash{E}
. Then

{\rm c}\big({\bf x}_{i},\frac{1}{N}\big)\bigcap{\rm c}\big({\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N}\big)=\emptyset,\ \ {\rm if}\ i\neq j,

(3.24)

which follows from the definition of maximal separated subsets,
and

{\rm c}\big({\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N}\big)\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash\widetilde{E},\ j=1,\dots,M,

(3.25)

since

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\pi}{2}-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf y},{\bf v}_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\frac{\pi}{2}-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf v}_{i})\right|-\big|d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf v}_{i})-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf v}_{i},{\bf y})\big|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\frac{\pi}{2}-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf v}_{i})\right|-d_{\mathbb{S}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf y})

\displaystyle>2\varepsilon_{d,m}-\frac{1}{N}>\varepsilon_{d,m},\ i=1,\dots,m

for any

whenever

. Meanwhile,

\frac{1}{2}\leq|\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash{E}|\leq\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}{\rm c}\big({\bf x}_{i},\frac{2}{N}\big)\right|\leq M\left|{\rm c}\big({\bf x}_{1},\frac{2}{N}\big)\right|\asymp MN^{-d}\asymp Mn^{-1}

by the maximal separated property of

.
Thus, we have
M\geq 4n
for sufficiently large

.

If
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\backslash\widetilde{E}

\mathcal{D}_{j}
we have

{\rm supp}\left(\mathcal{D}_{j}f\right)\subset\bigcup_{\rho\in G}\rho\left({\rm supp}(f)\right),\ j=1,\dots,d,

Combining with the fact that the set
\bigcup_{\rho\in G}\rho(E)
is invariant under the action of the
group
G
, it implies that

{\rm supp}\left(\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}f\right)\subset\bigcup_{\rho\in G}\rho\left({\rm supp}(f)\right),\ v=1,2,\dots.

By the definition of the Dunkl-Laplacian-Beltrami operator, for
any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we have

\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\varphi_{j}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})=\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(\varphi_{j}({\bf x}))\Big|_{{\bf x}={\boldsymbol{\xi}}},

which implies that

{\rm supp}\left(\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\varphi_{j}\right)\subset\bigcup_{\rho\in G}\rho\Big({\rm c}({\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N})\Big),\ j=1,\dots,4n.

(3.36)

This leads to (
3.35
).

Step 2. By the conclusion of Step 1, we get that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\quad\#\left\{1\leq j\leq 4n:\,\alpha_{j}\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}\varphi_{j}({\bf x})\neq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{4n}\chi_{\bigcup_{\rho\in G}\rho\left({\rm c}\left({\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)}({\bf x})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{4n}\sum_{\rho\in G}\chi_{\rho\left({\rm c}\left({\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in G}\sum_{j=1}^{4n}\chi_{\rho\left({\rm c}\left({\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)}({\bf x})\leq\sum_{\rho\in G}1=\#G,

(3.37)

where in the last inequality we used the pairwise disjoint property of

\left\{\rho\left({\rm c}\left({\bf x}_{j},1/N\right)\right)\right\}_{j=1}^{4n}
fro any
\rho\in G
.

Step 3. It is easy to verify that for any
1\leq j\leq 4n
and
v=1,2,\dots
,

\left\|\left(-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\right)^{v}\varphi_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim N^{2v},\,

which leads to

\left\|\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}\varphi_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim N^{2v}.

(3.38)

It follows from (
3.36
) and (
3.38
) that for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\left\|\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}\varphi_{j}\right\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\lesssim N^{2v-d/p},\ v=1,2,\dots.

(3.39)

Step 4.
By (
3.34
), (
3.37
), and (
3.39
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\right\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{4n}\alpha_{j}\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}\varphi_{j}({\bf x})\Big|^{p}{\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq(\#G)^{1-1/p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{4n}|\alpha_{j}|^{p}\left|\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}\varphi_{j}({\bf x})\right|^{p}{\rm d}\sigma({\bf x})\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{2v-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

This proves (
3.33
).

Now we turn to prove (
3.32
). By the Kolmogorov-type
inequality (see
[
13
, Theorem 8.1]
), (
3.33
) and
(
3.29
) that for
v>r
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\|\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{r/2}f_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\leq\left\|\left(-\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\kappa}},0}\right)^{v}f_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\right\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{\frac{r}{2v}}\left\|f_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\right\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{\frac{2v-r}{2v}}\lesssim N^{r-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

Hence, the proof of (i) is completed.

(ii) Since
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})
is continuously embedded into

B_{\infty}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})
for
0<\gamma<\infty
, it
only needs to show (
3.31
) for
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})
,

0<\gamma<\infty
. Notice that
E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\leq\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}
for
j\geq 0
. Thus,
we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}<N}\Big(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{2^{j}<N}\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{\gamma}.

(3.40)

Similar to (
3.8
), we get

\sum\limits_{2^{j}<N}\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}\lesssim\Theta(N^{-1})^{-\gamma}.

Together with (
3.29
) we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}<N}\Big(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta(N^{-1})^{-\gamma}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\asymp\Theta(N^{-1})^{-\gamma}N^{-d\gamma/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}^{\gamma}.

(3.41)

Choose a positive integer
\upsilon>s
. By the fact that

\|f\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\leq\|f\|_{p}
and (
2.10
) we obtain

E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}\leq E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p}\lesssim 2^{-j\upsilon}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p}^{\upsilon}},\ j\geq 0,

which combining with (
3.30
) for
W_{p}^{\upsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq N}\Big(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq N}\Big(\frac{2^{-j\upsilon}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p}^{\upsilon}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\Theta(N^{-1})^{-\gamma}N^{-s\gamma}\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq N}2^{j(s-\upsilon)\gamma}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p}^{\upsilon}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\asymp\Theta(N^{-1})^{-\gamma}N^{-\upsilon\gamma}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p}^{\upsilon}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Theta(N^{-1})^{-\gamma}N^{-d\gamma/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}^{\gamma},

(3.42)

where in the second inequality we used

\frac{\Theta(N^{-1})}{N^{-s}}\lesssim\frac{\Theta(2^{-j})}{2^{-js}},\ 2^{j}\geq N,

since
{\Theta(t)}/{t^{s}}
is almost decreasing.
It follows from (
3.29
), (
3.41
), and (
3.42
) that

\displaystyle\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}})}

\displaystyle:=\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}+\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\Big)^{\gamma}\Big)^{1/\gamma}\lesssim\Theta(N^{-1})^{-1}N^{-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

).

. In this case, by the fact that

C_{1}

3.4
(a) we obtain (

. It follows from (

4
Extensions to the unit ball and simplex

In this section, we will extend the results of the previous
sections to the unit ball and standard simplex. For this purpose,
we shall describe briefly some necessary notations and results on
these domains. Unless otherwise stated, most of the results
described in this section can be found in the paper
[
11
]
and
the book
[
12
]
.

4.1
Weights functions on
\Omega^{d}

Let
\Omega^{d}
be either the unit ball

\mathbb{B}^{d}:=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\|{\bf x}\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2}\leq 1\}

or the simplex

\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|{\bf x}|:=x_{1}+\dots+x_{d}\leq 1,\ x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\geq 0\}

and let
{\rm d}{\bf x}
be the usual Lebesgue measure on
\Omega^{d}
normalized by
\int_{\Omega^{d}}{\rm d}{\bf x}=1
.
For simplicity, we denote
{\rm meas}(E):=\int_{E}{\rm d}{\bf x}
of a subset
E
of
\Omega^{d}
.
The
distance
of two points
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}),\,{\bf y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})\in\Omega^{d}
is defined by

d_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\bf y}):=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &\arccos\Big(\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle+\sqrt{1-\|{\bf x}\|^{2}}\sqrt{1-\|{\bf y}\|^{2}}\Big),&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d},\\
&\arccos\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{j}\,y_{j}}+\sqrt{1-|{\bf x}|}\sqrt{1-|{\bf y}|}\Big),&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{aligned}

And we denote by
C_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\theta})
the ball centred at
{\bf x}\in\Omega^{d}
and of radius
{\theta}>0
in the metric space
(\Omega^{d},d_{\Omega})
, that is

C_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\theta}):=\{{\bf y}\in\Omega^{d}:\ d_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\bf y})\leq{\theta}\}.

A weight function
w
on
\Omega^{d}
is called
a doubling weight
if

\sup\limits_{{\bf x}\in\Omega^{d},\,{\theta}>0}\frac{w(C_{\Omega}({\bf x},2{\theta}))}{w(C_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\theta}))}=:L_{w}<\infty,

where
w({E}):=\int_{E}w({\bf y}){\rm d}{\bf y}
for a measurable subset
{E}
of
\Omega^{d}
; and it is call an
A_{\infty}
weight
if
there exists
\beta\geq 1
such that

\frac{w(C_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\theta}))}{w({E})}\leq\beta\left(\frac{{\rm meas}(C_{\Omega}({\bf x},2{\theta}))}{{\rm meas}({E})}\right)^{\beta},

for every measurable subset
{E}
of an arbitrarily given ball

C_{\Omega}({\bf x},{\theta})
in
\Omega^{d}
.

Integrations on
\Omega^{d}
are closely related to integrations on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Given a real function
f
on
\Omega^{d}
, for any
\bar{{\bf x}}=({\bf x},x_{d+1})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we define

T_{\Omega}f(\bar{\bf x}):=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}f({\bf x})|x_{d+1}|,&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d},\\
&\frac{1}{2}f({\bf x}^{2})\prod_{j=1}^{d+1}x_{j},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{aligned}

(4.1)

and

\psi(\bar{{\bf x}}):=\Big\{\begin{aligned} &{\bf x},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d},\\
&{\bf x}^{2},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{aligned}

(4.2)

where
{\bf x}^{2}=(x_{1}^{2},\dots,x_{d}^{2})
. Then we obtain

\int_{\Omega^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f\circ\psi(\bar{{\bf x}})\,T_{\Omega}w(\bar{{\bf x}}){\rm d}\sigma(\bar{{\bf x}}),

(4.3)

where
f\circ\psi(\bar{{\bf x}}):=f(\psi(\bar{{\bf x}}))
for
\bar{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
By the relation (
4.3
) it has been proved in
[
11
, Theorem 2]
that a weight function
w
on
\Omega^{d}
is a doubling weight (resp. an
A_{\infty}
weight)
if and only if
T_{\Omega}w
is a doubling weight (resp. an
A_{\infty}
weight) on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Furthermore, if
w
is a doubling weight
then
s_{w}>0
satisfies

\sup\limits_{{\bf y}\in\Omega^{d},\,{\theta}>0}\frac{w(C_{\Omega}({\bf y},2^{m}{\theta}))}{w(C_{\Omega}({\bf y},{\theta}))}\leq C_{L_{w}}2^{ms_{w}},\ m=1,2,\dots,

if and only if it satisfies

\sup\limits_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},\,r>0}\frac{T_{\Omega}w({\rm c}({\bf x},2^{m}r))}{T_{\Omega}w({\rm c}({\bf x},r))}\leq C_{L_{w}}^{\prime}2^{ms_{w}},\ m=1,2,\dots.

Given
\boldsymbol{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m})\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,
\mu\geq 0
, and
{\bf v}_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
j=1,\dots,m
, let
h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}({\bf x})
be given by (
2.2
) and be even in each of its variables if
\Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Consider the following
product weights
on
\Omega^{d}
:

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}({\bf x}):=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &(1-\|{\bf x}\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}({\bf x}),&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d},\\
&(1-|{\bf x}|)^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}(\sqrt{\bf x})\prod_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{aligned}

(4.4)

for
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\Omega^{d}
, where
\sqrt{{\bf x}}=(\sqrt{x_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{x_{d}})
for
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Clearly,

T_{\Omega}w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}(\bar{{\bf x}})=|x_{d+1}|^{2\mu}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left|\langle\bar{{\bf x}},\bar{{\bf v}}_{j}\rangle\right|^{2\kappa_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}\left|\langle\bar{{\bf x}},\bar{{\bf v}}_{j}\rangle\right|^{2\kappa_{j}}=:h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{2}(\bar{\bf x})=h_{\bar{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}}^{2}(\bar{\bf x}),

(4.5)

for
\bar{{\bf x}}=({\bf x},x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d},
where
\bar{{\bf v}}_{j}=({\bf v}_{j},0)\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,m
,
\bar{{\bf v}}_{m+1}={\bf e}_{d+1}
, and
\bar{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu)
.
Thus, we conclude that
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}
is an
A_{\infty}
weight on
\Omega^{d}
.
Specially, if
\mathcal{R}:=\{\pm\bar{\bf v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
forms a root system on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{2}
is invariant under the reflection group
G
generated by
\mathcal{R}
, then (
4.4
) is called
Dunkl weight
on
\Omega^{d}
.
As an example, when
m=d
and
\bar{{\bf v}}_{j}={\bf e}_{j},j=1,\dots,d
, i.e.,

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}({\bf x}):=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &(1-\|{\bf x}\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{2\kappa_{j}},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d},\\
&(1-|{\bf x}|)^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{\kappa_{j}-\frac{1}{2}},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{aligned}

by (
2.4
) and (
4.5
) we have

s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}:=d+2|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|+2\mu-2\min\{\mu,\kappa_{j},j=1,\dots,d\}.

4.2
Function Spaces on
\Omega^{d}

For
1\leq p<\infty
, denote by
L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})
the weighted
Lebesgue space on
\Omega^{d}
equipped with finite quasi-norm

\|f\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})}:=\left(\int_{\Omega^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}w({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x}\right)^{1/p},

while in the case of
p=\infty
we consider the space
C(\Omega^{d})

of continuous functions on
\Omega^{d}
with uniform norm

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega^{d})}:=\|f\|_{L_{\infty,{\omega}}(\Omega^{d})}
.
We denote by
\Pi_{n}(\Omega^{d})
the space of the restrictions to
\Omega^{d}
of all real algebra polynomials in
d
variables of degree no more than
n
.
Note that
\dim\Pi_{n}(\Omega^{d})\asymp n^{d}
.
The best approximation of
f\in L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})
from
\Pi_{n}(\Omega^{d})
in
L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})
by

E_{n}(f)_{L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})}:=\inf\limits_{g\in\Pi_{n}(\Omega^{d})}\|f-g\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})}.

4.2.1
Weighted Besov classes on
\Omega^{d}

Let
\Theta\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
, we say
f\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))
if
f\in L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})
and

\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))}:=\|f\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})}+\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{E_{2^{j}}(f)_{L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})}}{\Theta(2^{-j})}\right)^{\gamma}\right\}^{1/\gamma}

is finite. Similar to the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we can prove that if
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
, and
r>(1/p-1/q)_{+}s_{w}
, then
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))\hookrightarrow L_{q,w}(\Omega^{d})
.

Using the relation (
4.3
) we have

E_{n}(f)_{L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d})}=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &E_{2n}(f\circ\psi)_{L_{p,T_{\Omega}w}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d},\\
&E_{n}(f\circ\psi)_{L_{p,T_{\Omega}w}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},&&{\rm if}\ \Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{aligned}

which leads that for all
B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))
,

\|f\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))}\asymp\|f\circ\psi\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,T_{\Omega}w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))}.

(4.6)

4.2.2
Weighted Sobolev classes on
\Omega^{d}

Consider the Dunkl weight function

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}({\bf x}):=h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}({\bf x})\left(1-\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\right)^{\mu-1/2},\ \mu\geq 0,\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{B}^{d},

where
h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}
is given by (
2.2
) and is
reflection invariant under the finite reflection group
G
. For
simplicity, we denote
L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})\equiv L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
denote the space of weighted orthogonal polynomials of degree

n
with respect to the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}:=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f({\bf x})g({\bf x})w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x},\ f,g\in L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}),

i.e.,

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})=\{f\in\Pi_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{d}):\ \langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}=0,\ {\rm for\ all}\ g\in\Pi_{n-1}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\}.

Elements of
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
are closely related to the
h
-spherical harmonics associated
with the Dunkl weight function

h^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}({\bf x},x_{d+1}):=h^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}({\bf x})|x_{d+1}|^{2\mu},\ ({\bf x},x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

which is invariant under the group
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Let
Y_{n}
be such an
h
-harmonic
polynomial of degree
n
and assume that
Y_{n}
is even in the
(d+1)
-th variable; that is,

Y_{n}({\bf x},x_{d+1})=Y_{n}({\bf x},-x_{d+1}).

Then we can write

Y_{n}({\bf y})=r^{n}P_{n}({\bf x}),\ {\bf y}=r({\bf x},x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\ r=\|{\bf y}\|,\ ({\bf x},x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

in polar coordinates. Then
P_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
and this relation is
an one-to-one correspondence.
Furthermore, let
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}
denote the Dunkl-Laplacian
associated with
h^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}
and
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu,0}
denote the corresponding spherical Dunkl-Laplacian.
When
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu,0}

is applied to functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
that are even in the
(d+1)
-th variable,
the spherical Dunkl-Laplacian can be written in polar coordinates
{\bf y}=r({\bf x},x_{d+1})
as

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu,0}=\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}-\langle{\bf x},\nabla\rangle^{2}-2\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}\langle{\bf x},\nabla\rangle,\ \ \lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}=|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|+\mu+\frac{d-1}{2},

in which the operators
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
and
\nabla=(\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{d})
are all acting on
{\bf x}
variables, and

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is the Dunkl-Laplacian associated with
h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define

D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}:=\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}-\langle{\bf x},\nabla\rangle^{2}-2\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}\langle{\bf x},\nabla\rangle,

which satisfies

D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}P=-n(n+2\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu})P,\ \ P\in\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}).

For
f\in L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
, define
F({\bf x},x_{d+1})=f({\bf x})
. Then

F\in L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}})
and for
r>0
,

\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})^{r/2}f\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}=\|(-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu,0})^{r/2}F\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}})},

(4.7)

which, combining with (
2.9
), leads that Bernstein’s inequality holds: for
P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
,

\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})^{r/2}P\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim n^{r}\,\|P\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}.

(4.8)

Therefore, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
r>0
, we can define the weighted
Sobolev space
W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
to be the space of all real
functions
f
with finite norm

\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}+\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})^{r/2}f\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})},

while the weighted Sobolev class

BW_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
is defined to be the unit ball of the
weighted Sobolev space
W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
.
Similar to the spherical case, we have
W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{B}^{d})
if
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}}/p
, and it follows from (
2.10
) and (
4.7
) that Jackson’s inequality holds: for
f\in W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
,

E_{n}(f)_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim n^{-r}\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}.

(4.9)

Consider the Dunkl weight function

w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}({\bf x})=h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}(\sqrt{\bf x})(1-|{\bf x}|)^{\mu-1/2}/\sqrt{x_{1}\dots x_{d}},\ \mu\geq 0,\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

where
h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{2}
given by (
2.2
) is
invariant under the finite reflection group
G
and is even in
each of its variables. For example, the finite reflection group

G
can be chosen as the rotation group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{d}
. For
simplicity, we denote
L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})\equiv L_{2,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
be the space of weighted orthogonal polynomials on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
of degree
n
with respect to the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}:=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f({\bf x})g({\bf x})w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x},\ f,g\in L_{2}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}).

For a polynomial
P_{2n}\in\Pi_{2n}^{d}
being even in each of its variables,
we can write
P_{2n}
as

P_{2n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=R_{n}(x_{1}^{2},\dots,x_{d}^{2})

for
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
This implies that

P_{2n}\in\mathcal{V}_{2n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
if and only if
R_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
, and the
relation is an one-to-one correspondence. In particular, applying

D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
on
P_{2n}
leads to a second order
differential-difference operator, denoted
by
D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}
, acting on
R_{n}
. Then

D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}P=-n(n+\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu})P,\ P\in\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}),\ \lambda_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}=|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|+\mu+\frac{d-1}{2}.

For
f\in L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
, define
F({\bf x})=f({\bf x}^{2})
. Then

F\in L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
and for
r>0
,

\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})^{r/2}f\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}=2^{-r}\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})^{r/2}F\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})},

(4.10)

which, combining with (
2.9
), gives Bernstein’s inequality: for
P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
,

\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})^{r/2}P\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}\lesssim n^{r}\,\|P\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}.

(4.11)

Therefore, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
r>0
, we can define the weighted
Sobolev space
W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
to be the space of all real
functions
f
with finite norm

\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}+\|(-D_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})^{r/2}f\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})},

while the weighted Sobolev class

BW_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
is defined to be the unit ball of the
weighted Sobolev space
W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
.
Similar to the spherical case, we have
W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
if
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}}/p
, and it follows from (
2.10
) and (
4.10
) that Jackson’s inequality holds: for
f\in W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})
,

E_{n}(f)_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}\lesssim n^{-r}\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}})}.

(4.12)

4.3
Main Results

4.3.1
Deterministic case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
, and let
w
be an
A_{\infty}
weight with the critical index
s_{w}
on
\Omega^{d}
. Then, for
r>s_{w}/p
, we have

e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))\right)\asymp\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big).

(4.13)

Moreover, the upper estimate of (
4.13
) holds for all doubling weights.

Furthermore, if
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}
is a Dunkl weight with the critical index
s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}
on
\Omega^{d}
, then for
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}/p
, we have

e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}};BW_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega})\right)\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

(4.14)

Proof.

The proof of the lower estimates is almost identical to that of lower estimates
of spherical case, which depends on an analog of Lemma

3.2
on
\Omega^{d}
(see
[
11
, Proposition 4.8]
).
Meanwhile, by the embedding

W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega})\hookrightarrow B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}))
we have

e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}};BW_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega})\right)\lesssim e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}};BB_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}))\right),

which indicates that the upper estimate of (
4.14
) can be
derived from (
1.9
).

Now we turn to give the proof of the upper estimate of (
4.13
) for doubling weights.
To this end, let
w
be a doubling weight on
\Omega^{d}
and
f\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))
. Then, by (
4.6
) we have
F=f\circ\psi\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,T_{\Omega}w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))
.
It follows from (
3.1
) that there exist
{\bf x}_{1}^{*},\dots,{\bf x}_{n}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
\lambda_{1}^{*},\dots,\lambda_{n}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}F(\bar{{\bf x}})T_{\Omega}w(\bar{{\bf x}}){\rm d}\sigma(\bar{{\bf x}})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{*}F({\bf x}_{j}^{*})\right|\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big).

Choose
{\bf y}_{j}^{*}=\psi({\bf x}_{j}^{*})\in\Omega^{d},j=1,\dots,n
. Then by (
4.3
) we have

\left|\int_{\Omega^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{*}f({\bf y}_{j}^{*})\right|\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big),

which yields the desired estimate.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.3.2
Randomized case.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
,
and let
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}
be a product weight
with the critical index
s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}

on
\Omega^{d}
. Then, for

r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}/p
, we have

e_{n}^{\rm ran}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}};BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}(\Omega^{d}))\right)\asymp\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big)n^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

(4.15)

Moreover, the upper estimate of (
4.15
) holds for all doubling weights.

Furthermore, if
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}
is a Dunkl weight with the critical index
s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}
on
\Omega^{d}
, then for
r>s_{w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}}/p
, we have

e_{n}^{\rm ran}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}};BW_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega})\right)\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{+}}.

The proof of upper estimates of Theorem
4.2
is based on the relations between sphere, ball, and simplex, which is easy to be checked.

Proof of upper estimates.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.1
, it suffices to show the upper estimate of (
4.15
) for doubling weights. To this end, let
w
be a doubling weight on
\Omega^{d}
and
f\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))
. Then, by (
4.6
) we have
F=f\circ\psi\in B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,T_{\Omega}w}(\mathbb{S}^{d}))
.
It follows from (
3.9
) that there exist a randomized algorithm
(A_{n}^{\omega})_{\omega\in\mathcal{F}}
for
C(\mathbb{S}^{d})
as

A_{n}^{\omega}(F)=\varphi_{n}^{\omega}\left[F({\bf x}_{1,{\omega}}^{*}),\dots,F({\bf x}_{n,{\omega}}^{*})\right]

such that

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}F(\bar{{\bf x}})\,T_{\Omega}w(\bar{{\bf x}}){\rm d}\sigma(\bar{{\bf x}})-A_{n}^{{\omega}}(F)\right|\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big)n^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

Let
{\bf y}_{j,\omega}^{*}=\psi({\bf x}_{j,\omega}^{*})\in\Omega^{d},j=1,\dots,n
, and

\tilde{A}_{n}^{\omega}(f):=A_{n}^{\omega}(f\circ\psi)=\varphi_{n}^{\omega}\left[f({\bf y}_{1,{\omega}}^{*}),\dots,f({\bf y}_{n,{\omega}}^{*})\right].

Then

(\tilde{A}_{n}^{\omega})_{\omega\in\mathcal{F}}
can be viewed as a randomized algorithm for
C(\Omega^{d})
, and by (
4.3
) we have

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left|\int_{\Omega^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}{\bf x}-\tilde{A}_{n}^{{\omega}}(f)\right|\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big)n^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

which yields the desired estimate.

The proof is finished.
∎

The proof of lower estimates of Theorem
4.2
is almost
identical to that of the lower estimate of the sphere. For our
purpose, we also need to construct the fooling functions. The
argument depends on an analog of Lemma

3.5
on
\Omega^{d}
. We
only give the construction of the fooling functions and the detail proof of Theorem

4.2
is omitted.

In the following, we consider
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,
r>0
, and
\Theta_{1}(t)
,
\Theta(t)=t^{r}\Theta_{1}(t)\in\Phi_{s}^{*}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\kappa}:=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m})\in[0,+\infty)^{m}
,
\mu\geq 0
, and
{\bf v}_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,m
. Recall the product weight
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}
on
\Omega^{d}
given by (
4.4
).
Then

T_{\Omega}w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\Omega}(\bar{{\bf x}})=\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}\left|\langle\bar{{\bf x}},\bar{{\bf v}}_{j}\rangle\right|^{2\kappa_{j}}=h_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{2}(\bar{\bf x})\equiv w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}}(\bar{{\bf x}}).

Let
\varphi
be a nonnegative

C^{\infty}
-function on
\mathbb{R}
supported in
[0,1]

and being equal to 1 on
[0,1/2]
, and let
N
be a sufficient large integer with
N^{d}\asymp n
.

Construct fooling functions.

First we consider the case of
\Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d}
. Using the same method in Subsection 3.2, we can choose

\{\bar{\bf x}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4n}
in the upper sphere

\mathbb{S}_{+}^{d}:=\{{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:x_{d+1}\geq 0\}

\bar{\varphi}_{j}(\bar{\bf x}):=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\bar{\bf x},\bar{\bf x}_{j})\right),\ j=1,\dots,4n

have the following properties.

(i)

The supports
{\rm supp}(\bar{\varphi}_{j})\subset{\rm c}(\bar{\bf x}_{j},1/N),j=1,\dots,4n

{\rm supp}(\bar{\varphi}_{i})\bigcap{\rm supp}(\bar{\varphi}_{j})=\emptyset,\ {\rm for}\ i\neq j.

(ii)

For any
1\leq p<\infty
, the
L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}})

\bar{\varphi}_{j}

and the
L_{\infty}

\bar{\varphi}_{j}
is

\|\bar{\varphi}_{j}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=\sup_{\bar{\bf x}\in{\rm c}(\bar{\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N})}|\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\bar{\bf x},\bar{\bf x}_{j})\right)|\asymp 1.

(iii)

For any
\bar{f}_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\bar{\varphi}_{j}
with
{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}=({\alpha}_{1},\cdots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}
, we have

and

–

for any
r>0
,

\|\bar{f}_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}})}\lesssim n^{r/d-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}},

whenever
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}}
is a Dunkl weight on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
;

–

for any
r>0
,

\|\bar{f}_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}}))}\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1/d})^{-1}n^{-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}},

whenever
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{S}}
is a product weight on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Clearly, the mapping

\displaystyle\psi_{1}:\,\mathbb{S}_{+}^{d}\to\mathbb{B}^{d},\quad({\bf x},x_{d+1})\mapsto{\bf x}

is bijection, which leads that its inverse
\psi_{1}^{-1}:\,\mathbb{B}^{d}\to\mathbb{S}_{+}^{d}

\varphi_{j}({\bf x}):=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{B}}\left({\bf x},{\bf x}_{j})\right),\ j=1,\dots,4n

F_{0}:=\Big\{f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\varphi_{j}:\,{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}=({\alpha}_{1},\cdots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}\Big\},

where

\varphi_{j}({\bf x})=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\psi_{1}(\bar{\bf x}),\psi_{1}(\bar{\bf x}_{j})\right)=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\bar{\bf x},\bar{\bf x}_{j})\right)=\bar{\varphi}_{j}(\bar{\bf x}),\ j=1,\dots,4n.

Therefore, from (
4.3
), (
4.7

for

\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}

(i)

if
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
is a Dunkl weight on
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
, then

\|f_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{{\mathbb{B}}})}\lesssim n^{r/d-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}};

(ii)

if
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
is a product weight on
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
, then

\|f_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}))}\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1/d})^{-1}n^{-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

Next we turn to consider the case of
\Omega^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
. It can be transferred to the case of
\Omega^{d}=\mathbb{B}^{d}
by the bijection

\displaystyle\psi_{2}:\,\mathbb{B}_{+}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad{\bf x}\mapsto{\bf x}^{2},

where
\mathbb{B}_{+}^{d}:=\{{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{B}^{d}:\,x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\geq 0\}
. Similar to the case of
\Omega^{d}=\mathbb{S}^{d}

\{\tilde{\bf x}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4n}
in
\mathbb{B}_{+}^{d}

\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(\tilde{\bf x}):=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\tilde{\bf x},\tilde{\bf x}_{j})\right),\ j=1,\dots,4n

have the following properties.

(i)

The supports
{\rm supp}(\tilde{\varphi}_{j})\subset C_{\mathbb{B}}(\tilde{\bf x}_{j},1/N),j=1,\dots,4n
are disjoint, that is

{\rm supp}(\tilde{\varphi}_{i})\bigcap{\rm supp}(\tilde{\varphi}_{j})=\emptyset,\ {\rm for}\ i\neq j.

(ii)

For any
1\leq p<\infty
, the
L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})
norm of
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
is

\|\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}\asymp\Big(\int_{C_{\mathbb{B}}(\tilde{\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N})}\frac{|\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\tilde{\bf x},\tilde{\bf x}_{j})\right)|^{p}}{\sqrt{1-\|\tilde{\bf x}\|^{2}}}{\rm d}\tilde{\bf x}\Big)^{1/p}\asymp n^{-1/p},

and the
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
norm of
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
is

\|\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}=\sup_{\tilde{\bf x}\in C_{\mathbb{B}}(\tilde{\bf x}_{j},\frac{1}{N})}|\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\tilde{\bf x},\tilde{\bf x}_{j})\right)|\asymp 1.

(iii)

For any
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
with
{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}=({\alpha}_{1},\cdots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}
, we have

\|\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}\asymp n^{-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}},

and

–

for any
r>0
,

\|\tilde{f}_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim n^{r/d-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}},

whenever
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
is a Dunkl weight on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
;

–

for any
r>0
,

\|\tilde{f}_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}))}\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1/d})^{-1}n^{-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}},

whenever
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{B}}
is a product weight on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
.

Set

\varphi_{j}({\bf x}):=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{T}}\left({\bf x},{\bf x}_{j})\right),\ j=1,\dots,4n

and

F_{0}:=\Big\{f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\varphi_{j}:\,{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}=({\alpha}_{1},\cdots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}\Big\},

where
{\bf x}_{j}:=\psi_{2}(\tilde{\bf x}_{j}),j=1,\dots,4n
. Then

\varphi_{j}({\bf x})=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\psi_{2}(\tilde{\bf x}),\psi_{2}(\tilde{\bf x}_{j})\right)=\varphi(Nd_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\tilde{\bf x},\tilde{\bf x}_{j})\right)=\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(\tilde{\bf x}),\ j=1,\dots,4n.

Therefore, from (
4.3
), (
4.10
) and the above properties of
\{\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4n}
, we derive that
for
f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in F_{0}
with
\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}
,

(i)

if
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}
is a Dunkl weight on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, then

\|f_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{W_{p}^{r}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{{\mathbb{T}}})}\lesssim n^{r/d-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}};

(ii)

if
w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}
is a product weight on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, then

\|f_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{B_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p}(w_{\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mu}^{\mathbb{T}}))}\lesssim\Theta(n^{-1/d})^{-1}n^{-1/p}\,\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper we explore the numerical integration

{\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w}(f)=\int_{\Omega^{d}}f({\bf x})w({\bf x}){\rm d}\mathsf{m}({\bf x})

for a weighted function class using the
deterministic and randomized algorithoms, where
\Omega^{d}
denotes
the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the ball
\mathbb{B}^{d}
, or the
standard simplex
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The corresponding orders of
optimal quadrature errors for weighted Sobolev classes with Dunkl
weights were obtained. However, for the weighted Besov class of
generalized smoothness with a doubling weight
w
, we derived that
for
r>s_{w}/p
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))\right)\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big),

which is optimal whenever
w
is an
A_{\infty}
weight on
\Omega^{d}
, and

e_{n}^{\rm ran}\left({\rm Int}_{\Omega^{d},w};BB_{\gamma}^{\Theta}(L_{p,w}(\Omega^{d}))\right)\lesssim\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big)n^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

which is optimal whenever
w
is the product weight on
\Omega^{d}
.
We believe that the aforementioned orders are optimal for all doubling weights, though verifying this may require different techniques.

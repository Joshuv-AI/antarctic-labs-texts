Global existence and uniqueness analysis of cross-diffusion multispecies chemotaxis system with volume-filling
Xi Lin

Abstract
. The system of multispecies chemotaxis equations is a cross-diffusion system with volume-filling. In this work, we show that a weak solution of the two species chemotaxis system exists. The entropy method is a major tool in existence analysis of cross-diffusion systems. Previous investigations indicate that traditional entropies in volume-filling cases may not be able to provide required gradient estimates. In this situation, we upgrade existing matrix computation methods to derive gradient estimates. Due to the cross-diffusion phenomenon, the uniqueness of the weak solution to a cross-diffusion system is very difficult to prove in general. In this work, we apply the distance functional to show that when parameters of the chemotaxis system are identical, the weak solution is unique.

1.1.
Motivation

In this paper, we analyze a two species cross-diffusion system with volume-filling. The system is called the two species chemotaxis equations, which is used to describe the movement of two different cell types in crowded and uncrowded tissues. Please refer to
[
1

In
[

Global existence analysis of this chemotaxis system in

1.2.
Major Difficulties and Novelties

The entropy method has been demonstrated as a powerful tool in global existence analysis of cross-diffusion systems. How to apply the entropy method to show weak solutions exist to cross-diffusion systems was discussed in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Let us present these assumptions:

(H1). There exists a convex function
h\in C^{2}(\mathcal{V},(0,\infty))
such that its derivative
h^{\prime}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is invertible on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(H2). For every
z=(z_{1},...,z_{n})^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u=(u_{1},...,u_{n})\in\mathcal{V}
, there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
z
and
u
, such that

z^{\mathrm{T}}h^{\prime\prime}(u)A(u)z\geq c\sum_{i=1}^{n}z^{2}_{i}.

In here,
H(u)=(H_{ij}(u))=h^{\prime\prime}(u)
is the Hessian matrix of
h(u)
, and
A(u)
is the diffusion matrix of the cross-diffusion system. For every
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
H_{ij}(u)=\partial^{2}h/\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}
.

(H3). There exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that for
u\in\mathcal{V}
and
i,j=1,...,n
,
|A_{ij}(u)|<c^{\prime}
.

If
h(u)
and
A(u)
satisfy assumptions (H1)-(H3), then the entropy method can be applied to show a weak solution of the cross-diffusion system exists. Assumptions (H1)-(H3) are intimately related to the concept of “entropy structure” in
[
11
]
. The entropy structure is an essential structural character of cross-diffusion systems.

In global existence analysis of Maxwell-Stefan equations
[
5
]
, the entropy was chosen as

(5)

\displaystyle h(\rho^{\prime})=k\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}x_{i}(\ln x_{i}-1)+k,\quad\rho^{\prime}=(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}).

In (
5
),
x_{i}
and
\rho_{i}
are related by
x_{i}=\rho_{i}/(kM_{i})
,
\rho_{i}>0
, with
k=\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}(\rho_{i}/M_{i})
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}\rho_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}x_{i}=1
.
M_{i}>0
,
i=1,...,N+1
are constants.

For degenerate systems in
[
4
]
, the entropy was

(6)

\displaystyle h(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\log u_{i}-u_{i}+1)+\int_{a}^{u_{n+1}}\log q(s)ds+\chi(u),\quad a\in(0,1],\text{ }\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}u_{i}=1,\text{ }u_{i}>0,

and
q(s),\chi(u)
are functions defined in (7)-(8) of
[
4
]
.

For the Shigesada-Kawasaki-Teramoto type population system in
[
7
]
, the entropy was

(7)

\displaystyle h(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}u_{i}(\log u_{i}-1),\quad\pi_{i}>0.

For the two species chemotaxis system, let us denote
u_{3}=1-u_{1}-u_{2}
, and consider the entropy

(8)

\displaystyle h(u)=u_{1}(\log u_{1}-1)+u_{2}(\log u_{2}-1)+u_{3}(\log u_{3}-1).

We remark that
h(u)
in (
8
) is a major functional in showing weak solutions of volume-filling cross-diffusion systems exist. For
u\in\mathcal{D}
, we have

(9)

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(u)A(u)=\Big\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{1}{u_{1}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{u_{2}}\\
\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{u_{3}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&1\\
1&1\\
\end{array}\right)\Big\}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\alpha_{1}(1-u_{2})+u_{2}&(\alpha_{1}-1)u_{1}\\
(\alpha_{2}-1)u_{2}&\alpha_{2}(1-u_{1})+u_{1}\\
\end{array}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{u_{1}}+(1-\alpha_{1})\cdot\frac{u_{2}}{u_{1}}&\alpha_{1}-1\\
\alpha_{2}-1&\frac{\alpha_{2}}{u_{2}}+(1-\alpha_{2})\cdot\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\\
\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{u_{3}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\alpha_{1}+(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})u_{2}&\alpha_{2}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})u_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}+(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})u_{2}&\alpha_{2}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})u_{1}\\
\end{array}\right).

For general
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}
case, we cannot verify assumption (H2) for
h(u)
and
A(u)
in (
9
). The major novelty of this work is the matrix analysis in Section 2. For each
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
case, we verify assumptions (H1)-(H3). Then we apply the entropy method to show a weak solution of chemotaxis system exists.

In order to show the weak solution of a cross-diffusion system is unique, we apply the distance functional to measure the “distance” between two weak solutions of the system. Once the “distance” between any two weak solutions of the system is zero, then we deduce that the weak solution is unique.

The distance functional technique was applied in
[
4
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. In
[
12
]
, the uniqueness result was shown for a weak solution that is not bounded in general. In
[
13
]
, uniqueness results for several typical cross-diffusion systems were discussed.

In general, it is very difficult to construct an appropriate distance functional to show the weak solution of a cross-diffusion system is unique. In
[
15
,
16
]
, weaker uniqueness results for cross-diffusion systems were shown. We call such kind of uniqueness as “weak-strong uniqueness”.

In these references, so-called relative entropy functionals were applied to measure distances between weak and strong solutions.
In this manuscript, we still focus on the uniqueness proof for weak solutions.

We assume that

is also a weak solution of (

2.1.
The Construction of
h(u)

Let us consider the case when
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}<1
. We define the entropy
h(u)
as

(14)

\displaystyle h(u)=\frac{\beta u_{1}(\log u_{1}-1)}{1-\alpha_{1}}+\frac{\beta u_{2}(\log u_{2}-1)}{1-\alpha_{2}}+u_{3}(\log u_{3}-1)+\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha_{1}}+\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha_{2}}+1,\quad\beta>0.

We verify that

\displaystyle\partial h/\partial u_{i}=\beta\log u_{i}/(1-\alpha_{i})-\log u_{3},\quad i=1,2.

For every

\displaystyle g(y)=e^{(1-\alpha_{1})w_{1}/\beta}(1-y)^{(1-\alpha_{1})/\beta}+e^{(1-\alpha_{2})w_{2}/\beta}(1-y)^{(1-\alpha_{2})/\beta},\quad 0<y<1.

g
is continuous and nonincreasing in

\displaystyle g(0)=e^{(1-\alpha_{1})w_{1}/\beta}+e^{(1-\alpha_{2})w_{2}/\beta}>0,\quad g(1)=0.

We deduce that there exists a unique fixed point

, such that

Then we define

(

5.1.
Lemma 5 of
[
2
]

For integrity purpose, we give a brief proof to Lemma 5 of
[
2
]
. Let
y\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\delta\in[0,1]
, and define

\displaystyle a(w,\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi:B(y)\nabla wdx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}D^{\alpha}w\cdot D^{\alpha}\phi+w\cdot\phi\Big)dx,

\displaystyle F(\phi)=-\frac{\delta}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(u(y)-u(w^{k-1}))\cdot\phi dx.

Let us consider the equation

(35)

\displaystyle a(w,\phi)=F(\phi),\text{ for every }\phi\in H^{m}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The forms
a
and
F
are bounded on
H^{m}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The matrix
B(y)=A(u(y))(h^{\prime\prime}(u(y)))^{-1}
is positive semi-definite since

\displaystyle z^{\mathrm{T}}B(y)z=((h^{\prime\prime}(u))^{-1}z)^{\mathrm{T}}h^{\prime\prime}(u)A(u(y))(h^{\prime\prime}(u))^{-1}z\geq 0,\text{ for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For the bilinear form
a
, by the Poincaré inequality, we have

\displaystyle a(w,w)\geq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}w|^{2}+|w|^{2}\Big)dx\geq\varepsilon C_{p}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{m}(\Omega)}.

We apply the Lax-Milgram lemma to obtain the existence of a unique solution
w\in H^{m}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
35
). This defines the fixed point operator
S:L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times[0,1]\to L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
S(y,\delta)=w
, where
w
solves (
35
).

The fixed point operator
S
is continuous, compact and bounded. For continuity and compactness of the operator
S
, please refer to
[
2
]
for a detailed explanation. We are left to prove a uniform bound for all fixed points of
S(\cdot,\delta)
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such a fixed point. Then
w
solves (
35
) with
y
replaced by
w
. Let us choose the test function as
\phi=w
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(u(w)-u(w^{k-1}))\cdot wdx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla w:B(w)\nabla wdx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}w|^{2}+|w|^{2}\Big)dx=0.

The convexity of
h
implies that
h(x)-h(y)\leq h^{\prime}(x)(x-y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{D}
. Let us choose
x=u(w),y=u(w^{k-1})
and
h^{\prime}(u(w))=w
, which follows

\displaystyle\delta\int_{\Omega}h(u(w))dx+\varepsilon\tau\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}w|^{2}+|w|^{2}\Big)dx\leq\delta\int_{\Omega}h(u(w^{k-1}))dx.

This yields an
H^{m}
bound for

5.2.
Conclusions

We have shown that the two species chemotaxis model (
1
)-(
3
) has a weak solution. In
[
20
]
, bounded weak solutions to a class of cross-diffusion systems were also discussed. In the proof, a class of entropy functionals were introduced to yield uniform estimates.

For some cross-diffusion systems, how to show their weak solutions exist remains a great challenge. In the global existence analysis of chemotaxis model with cross-diffusion phenomenon, we have demonstrated how to find the entropy structure of the model in this work.

For some cross-diffusion systems, their corresponding entropy structures are still hidden. Among these systems, Shigesada-Kawasaki-Teramoto type population system is the most typical one. In
[
7
]
, weak solutions of the population system were shown exist, when diffusion coefficients satisfy certain assumptions.

We still denote
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
, and constants
d_{0},d_{1},d_{2}>0
,
d_{0}\neq d_{1}
,
d_{0}\neq d_{2}
, with

\displaystyle\delta(u)=d_{1}d_{2}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})+d_{0}(d_{1}u_{1}+d_{2}u_{2}).

Three components Maxwell-Stefan equations is a system of parabolic partial differential equations in the form of (
1
), with the diffusion matrix given by

\displaystyle A(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta(u)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}d_{2}+(d_{0}-d_{2})u_{1}&(d_{0}-d_{1})u_{1}\\
(d_{0}-d_{2})u_{2}&d_{1}+(d_{0}-d_{1})u_{2}\\
\end{array}\right).

For every test function
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
satisfies that
u\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}
, and

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{1},\varphi\rangle+\big\langle\frac{1}{\delta(u)}\big((d_{2}+(d_{0}-d_{2})u_{1})\nabla u_{1}+(d_{0}-d_{1})u_{1}\nabla u_{2}\big),\nabla\varphi\big\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u_{2},\varphi\rangle+\big\langle\frac{1}{\delta(u)}\big((d_{0}-d_{2})u_{2}\nabla u_{1}+(d_{1}+(d_{0}-d_{1})u_{2})\nabla u_{2}\big),\nabla\varphi\big\rangle=0.

Once we denote

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{d_{1}}{d_{0}-d_{1}},\quad\beta_{2}=\frac{d_{2}}{d_{0}-d_{2}},

we can show that

(36)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}(\beta_{1}u_{1}+\beta_{2}u_{2}),\varphi\rangle=\big\langle\Delta\big(\frac{\beta_{1}u_{1}}{d_{1}}+\frac{\beta_{2}u_{2}}{d_{2}}\big),\varphi\big\rangle.

Let us denote
\delta_{ij}
as Kronecker delta symbol. For one of the most typical cross-diffusion systems with degeneration phenomenon, the system is in the form of (
1
), with the diffusion matrix given by

\displaystyle A_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}D_{i}u_{n+1}+D_{i}u_{i},\quad u_{n+1}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i},\quad D_{i}>0,\quad i,j=1,...,n.

For every test function
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
, the weak solution
u=(u_{1},...,u_{n})
satisfies that
u\in\overline{\mathcal{V}}
, and

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\big(\frac{u_{i}}{D_{i}}\big),\varphi\rangle+\langle u_{n+1}\nabla u_{i}-u_{i}\nabla u_{n+1},\nabla\varphi\rangle=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

which follows that

(37)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}}{D_{i}}\big),\varphi\rangle=\langle\Delta\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\big),\varphi\rangle.

For semiconductor model with electron-hole scattering, the model is in the form of (
1
), with the diffusion matrix given by

\displaystyle A(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\mu_{2}u_{1}+\mu_{1}u_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\mu_{1}(1+\mu_{2}u_{1})&\mu_{1}\mu_{2}u_{1}\\
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}u_{2}&\mu_{2}(1+\mu_{1}u_{2})\\
\end{array}\right).

For every test function
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
, the weak solution
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
satisfies that

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\big(\frac{u_{1}}{\mu_{1}}\big),\varphi\rangle+\big\langle\frac{(1+\mu_{2}u_{1})\nabla u_{1}+\mu_{2}u_{1}\nabla u_{2}}{1+\mu_{2}u_{1}+\mu_{1}u_{2}},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\big(\frac{u_{2}}{\mu_{2}}\big),\varphi\rangle+\big\langle\frac{\mu_{1}u_{2}\nabla u_{1}+(1+\mu_{1}u_{2})\nabla u_{2}}{1+\mu_{2}u_{1}+\mu_{1}u_{2}},\nabla\varphi\big\rangle=0,

and we can show that

(38)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\big(\frac{u_{1}}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{u_{2}}{\mu_{2}}\big),\varphi\big\rangle=\langle\Delta(u_{1}+u_{2}),\varphi\rangle.

We notice that (
36
)-(
38
) are similar to each other. So far, uniqueness of weak solutions results for these cross-diffusion systems have been shown when
d_{1}=d_{2}
in (
36
),
D_{i}=D_{j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
in (
37
), and
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
in (
38
). How to show these weak solutions are unique for general coefficients cases is a major topic in the study of parabolic partial differential equations.

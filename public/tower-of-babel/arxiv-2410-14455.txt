1.
Introduction

Given an abelian variety
A
defined over a number field
K
, the Mordell-Weil Theorem asserts that the group of rational points,
A(K)
, of
A
is finitely generated. The subgroup
A(K)_{\operatorname{tor}}
of torsion points of
A(K)
is finite. In addition it is conjectured that
|A(K)_{\operatorname{tor}}|
is uniformly bounded by a bound that depends only on the dimension
g
of
A
and the degree
d
of
K
. The correctness of the conjecture was established by Merel,
[
20
]
, when
g=1
and
d\geq 1
. A complete list of possible groups that can be realized as torsion subgroups when
g=1
has been found when
d=1,2,3
,
[
19
,
10
,
11
,
6
]
. The conjecture is still wide open for any pair of integers
g\geq 2
and
d\geq 1
.

Fixing a pair of integers
g,N\geq 2
, researchers try to construct algebraic curves over the rational field
{\mathbb{Q}}
of genus
g
whose Jacobian varieties possess rational torsion points of order
N
. For example, when
g=2
, infinite families of genus-
2
curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
were given with torsion points on their Jacobians of order
11
and
13
, see
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
22
,
13
]
. Leprévost displayed families of genus-
2
curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with rational torsion points of order
N=15,17,19,21,22,23,24,25,26,27
and
29
on their Jacobian varieties, see
[
16
,
14
]
. In
[
21
]
, the reader may find a list of integers
N
that have appeared in the literature as orders of rational torsion points on Jacobians of algebraic curves defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with genus
2,3
or
4
.

Flynn,
[
7
]
, conjectured the existence of a constant
\kappa
, independent of
g\geq 2
, such that for every
m\leq\kappa g
, there exists a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with a rational
m
-torsion point on its Jacobian. Leprévost,
[
17
]
, proved this conjecture with the value
\kappa=3
. In particular, this shows the existence of abelian varieties of dimension
g
with torsion order that is linear in
g
for any
g\geq 2
. The latter bound has been extended to include other possible linear torsion orders in the interval
[3g,4g+1]
, see
[
22
]
. In fact, the authors show that for every integer
N
in the interval
[3g,4g+1]
,

g\geq 3
, satisfying certain partition conditions, there exist infinite families of hyperelliptic curves
of genus
g
whose Jacobian varieties have a rational torsion point of order
N
.

A plausible question to pose is whether it is possible to construct abelian varieties of dimension
g
that possess rational torsion points whose order is quadratic in
g
. Fixing an even integer
g\geq 2
, Flynn gave an explicit description of
1
-parameter families of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
whose Jacobian varieties contain rational torsion points of order
N
for any
N
in the interval
\left[g^{2}+2g+1,g^{2}+3g+1\right]
, see
[
8
]
. For any integer
g\geq 2
, Leprévost displayed
1
-parameter families of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
with torsion points of order
2g^{2}+2g+1
or
2g^{2}+3g+1
on their Jacobian varieties in
[
15
]
, of orders
2g^{2}+4g+1
or
2g(2g+1)
in
[
18
]
, and of either orders
N
,
N/2
or
N/4
, where
N=2g^{2}+5g+5
, in
[
18
]
.

In this article, we extend the results of Flynn and Leprévost to produce hyperelliptic curves of genus
g\geq 2
, with new rational torsion orders on their Jacobians that are quadratic in
g
. For any integer
g\geq 2
, we present hyperelliptic curves of genus
g

over
{\mathbb{Q}}
such that their jacobian varieties contain a rational torsion point of order
N
where
N=4g^{2}+2g-2
, respectively
4g^{2}+2g-4

4

whose jacobian has a rational torsion point of order
70
, see
[

. In addition, for any integer

t

f_{t}(x)

2.
The construction

Throughout this work,
K
will be a number field. Let
f(x)\in K[x]
be a polynomial of odd degree
2g+1
and no repeated factors. We consider the hyperelliptic curve
C
described by the equation
y^{2}=f(x)
. We let
D
be a divisor on
C
. We recall that the
Riemann-Roch
space of
D
is
the
K
-vector space
L(D)=\{\phi\in K(C):\operatorname{div}(\phi)+D\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}.

We notice that the curve
C
has one point at infinity,
P_{\infty}
since the degree of
f(x)
is odd. There exists a natural embedding of the curve
C
into its Jacobian
J
that maps a point
P
to the divisor class
[P-D]
, where
D
is a fixed divisor of degree
1
. This map restricts to
C(K)\xhookrightarrow{}J(K)
if
D
is a
K
-rational divisor. In particular, one can choose
D
to be the rational divisor
P_{\infty}
. The class of a divisor of the form
P-P_{\infty}

defines a point in
J(K)
. We say that the divisor
P-P_{\infty}
is a
torsion divisor
of order
N
if its class in
J
has order
N
.

We remark that
\operatorname{ord}_{P_{\infty}}(x)=-2
and
\operatorname{ord}_{P_{\infty}}(y)=-(2g+1).
It follows that for any
m\geq 0
, if
D_{m}=2(g+m+1)P_{\infty}
, then
L(D_{m})=\langle 1,x,x^{2},\cdots,x^{g+m+1},y,xy,\cdots,x^{m}y\rangle
; whereas if
D^{\prime}_{m}=(2(g+m)+1)P_{\infty}
, then
L(D^{\prime}_{m})=\langle 1,x,x^{2},\cdots,x^{g+m},y,xy,\cdots,x^{m}y\rangle
.

Let
d
,
0\leq d\leq g-1
, be an integer. From now on, we work with polynomials of the form

f(x)=A(x)^{2}-\lambda x^{g+1+d}(x-1)^{g-d},\qquad A(x)\in K[x],\,\deg A(x)\leq g,\quad\lambda\in K\setminus\{0\}.

One sees that
P_{0}=(0,A(0))
,
{P_{0}}^{\prime}=(0,-A(0))
,
P_{1}=(1,A(1))
,
{P_{1}}^{\prime}=(1,-A(1))
are in
C(K)
. In particular, the divisors
D_{i}=P_{i}-P_{\infty}
and
{D_{i}}^{\prime}={P_{i}}^{\prime}-P_{\infty}
,
i=0,1,
are
K
-rational divisors on
C
. We now consider
\phi_{f}\in K(C)
such that

\operatorname{div}(\phi_{f})=(g+m){P_{0}}^{\prime}+(g+m+2)P_{1}-(2g+2m+2)P_{\infty}.

We notice that
\psi_{f}=\phi_{f}/x^{g+m}\in K(C)
has divisor
\operatorname{div}(\psi_{f})=-(g+m)P_{0}+(g+m+2)P_{1}-2P_{\infty}
. Therefore, one obtains the following

(1)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}g+1+d&g-d\\
-(g+m)&(g+m+2)\\
\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}D_{0}\\
D_{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\operatorname{div}(y-A(x))\\
\operatorname{div}(\psi_{f})\end{matrix}\right).

The argument above gives rise to the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix two integers
g\geq 1
and
d
,
0\leq d\leq g-1
. Let
C
be a hyperelliptic curve defined by the equation
y^{2}=f(x):=A(x)^{2}-\lambda x^{g+1+d}(x-1)^{g-d}
, where
A(x)\in K[x]
,
\deg A(x)\leq g
,
\lambda\in K\setminus\{0\}.
We set
{P_{0}}^{\prime}=(0,-A(0))
,
P_{1}=(1,A(1))\in C(K)
. Let
m
be an integer such that
1\leq m<d+1
. Assume that there exists a rational function
\phi_{f}\in K(C)
such that

\operatorname{div}(\phi_{f})=(g+m)P_{0}^{\prime}+(g+m+2)P_{1}-(2g+2m+2)P_{\infty}.
Then there is a torsion divisor on
C
whose order divides
2g^{2}+(2m+3)g+2d+m+2
.

Proof:

According to the argument before the proposition, the assumptions on
C
imply the existence of a function
\psi_{f}=\phi_{f}/x^{g+m}\in K(C)
with
\operatorname{div}(\psi_{f})=-(g+m)P_{0}+(g+m+2)P_{1}-2P_{\infty}
. Now the result follows directly from (
1
), where
2g^{2}+(2m+3)g+2d+m+2
is the determinant of the
(2\times 2)
-matrix.

\Box

In what follows, we explain how to produce a polynomial
A(x)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
such that the order of the torsion divisor on the curve
C
in Proposition
2.1
is exactly
2g^{2}+(2m+3)g+2d+m+2
.

It can be seen that
\phi_{f}\in L((2g+2m+2)P_{\infty})
. Thus, we deduce that
\phi_{f}=a(x)-b(x)y
for some
a(x),b(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg a(x)\leq g+m+1
and
\deg b(x)\leq m
. In particular, one has that the norm of
\phi_{f}
in
K(C)
is given by

(a(x)-b(x)y)(a(x)+b(x)y)=a(x)^{2}-b(x)^{2}y^{2}=h(x)x^{g+m}(x-1)^{g+m+2},\quad h(x)\in K[x].

Since
y^{2}=A(x)^{2}-\lambda x^{g+1+d}(x-1)^{g-d}
, it follows that

\displaystyle a(x)^{2}-b(x)^{2}A(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda b(x)^{2}x^{g+1+d}(x-1)^{g-d}+h(x)x^{g+m}(x-1)^{g+m+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{g+m}(x-1)^{g-d}\left(h(x)(x-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda b(x)^{2}x^{d+1-m}\right),\quad\text{for }m<d+1.

Since
P_{0}
does not lie in the support of
\operatorname{div}(\phi_{f})
whereas
{P_{0}}^{\prime}
is in the support of
\operatorname{div}(\phi_{f})
, it follows that
\phi_{f}\left(P_{0}\right)\neq 0
and
\phi_{f}\left({P_{0}}^{\prime}\right)=0
. Moreover,
a(0)-b(0)A(0)\neq 0
whereas
a(0)+b(0)A(0)=0
. This yields that
x\nmid a(x)-b(x)A(x)
whereas
x\mid a(x)+b(x)A(x)
. Similarly, the support of
\operatorname{div}(\psi_{f})
contains
P_{1}
but it does not contain
{P_{1}}^{\prime}
, therefore
(x-1)\mid a(x)-b(x)A(x)
but
(x-1)\nmid a(x)+b(x)A(x)
. Thus, we may assume that

\displaystyle a(x)+b(x)A(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(x)x^{g+m},

\displaystyle a(x)-b(x)A(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(x)(x-1)^{g-d},

\displaystyle p(x)q(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(x)(x-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda b(x)^{2}x^{d+1-m}.

It follows that

(2)

\displaystyle A(x)=\frac{p(x)^{2}x^{g+m}-\left[h(x)(x-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda b(x)^{2}x^{d+1-m}\right](x-1)^{g-d}}{2p(x)b(x)}

where
A(x)\in K[x]
. We set
p(x)=x-\alpha
,
\alpha\neq 0,1
, where we seek to choose
\alpha
such that
b(\alpha)
and
h(\alpha)
are non-zero, and
p(x)\mid\left(h(x)(x-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda b(x)^{2}x^{d+1-m}\right)
.

Now we will find conditions under which
b(x)
divides the polynomial
p(x)^{2}x^{g+m}-h(x)(x-1)^{g+m+2}
of degree
m
.
From now on, we assume that
g+m
is even. We set
h(x)\equiv 1
. In the latter case,
p(x)^{2}x^{g+m}-h(x)(x-1)^{g+m+2}=(p(x)x^{(g+m)/2}-(x-1)^{1+(g+m)/2})(p(x)x^{(g+m)/2}+(x-1)^{1+(g+m)/2})
. Now, we set
b(x)=p(x)x^{(g+m)/2}-(x-1)^{1+(g+m)/2}
.

Since
p(x)=(x-\alpha)\mid\left((x-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda b(x)^{2}x^{d+1-m}\right)
, one sees that

0=(\alpha-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda b(\alpha)^{2}\alpha^{d+1-m}=(\alpha-1)^{m+2+d}-\lambda\left(-(\alpha-1)^{1+(g+m)/2}\right)^{2}\alpha^{d+1-m}.

In particular, one obtains that

\lambda=\frac{(\alpha-1)^{m+2+d}}{(\alpha-1)^{2+g+m}\alpha^{d+1-m}}=\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)^{g-d}\alpha^{d+1-m}}.

Following the discussion above, we fix an integer
m
,
1\leq m<d+1
, such that
g+m
is even. We are interested in the following
1
-parameter family of hyperelliptic curves

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,d}:\;y^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\alpha,d}b_{\alpha}(x)x^{d+1-m}(x-1)^{g-d}+(x-1)^{1+(g+m)/2}+(x-\alpha)x^{(g+m)/2}}{2(x-\alpha)}\right)^{2}-\lambda_{\alpha,d}x^{g+1+d}(x-1)^{g-d},

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,d}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\alpha,d}b_{\alpha}(x)x^{d+1-m}(x-1)^{g-d}+(x-1)^{1+(g+m)/2}+(x-\alpha)x^{(g+m)/2}}{2(x-\alpha)}\right),

\displaystyle b_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-\alpha)x^{(g+m)/2}-(x-1)^{1+(g+m)/2},\quad\alpha\neq 0,1,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{d+1-m}(\alpha-1)^{g-d}}.

It is clear that
\deg(b_{\alpha}(x))=(g+m)/2
if
\alpha\neq 1+(g+m)/2
,
whereas
\deg(b_{\alpha}(x))=-1+(g+m)/2
if
\alpha=1+(g+m)/2
.
The latter observation imposes certain restrictions on the possible values of
\alpha
and
d
. We recall that
\deg(b_{\alpha}(x))
is at most
m
. If
\deg(b_{\alpha}(x))=(g+m)/2
, then
g\leq m
which contradicts the fact that
m<d+1\leq g
. If
\deg(b_{\alpha}(x))=-1+(g+m)/2
, then
m\geq g-2
and the assumption that
g+m
is even implies that
m=g-2
, hence
\alpha=g
and
d\in\{g-1,g-2\}
.

Besides the above choice of
b(x)
, we will also consider the case where
b(x)
is chosen to be a linear polynomial, hence
m=1
.
In this case, the parameters
\lambda
and
\alpha
are selected so that
A(x)
becomes a polynomial. This will be treated in detail in § 5.

3.
The genus of the hyperelliptic curves

In this section, we show that the curve
C_{\alpha,d}
defined in the previous section where
\alpha=g
and
d
is either
g-1
or
g-2
is indeed a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We recall that the
height

h(f)
of a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
is the height of its
coefficients, namely, if
f(x)=a_{d}x^{d}+\cdots+a_{0}
, then
h(f)=\log\max\{1,|a_{i}|\}
, see
[
9
, B.7]
. If
\alpha
is a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
with minimal polynomial
f(x)=a_{d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{i})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
with
a_{d}>0
, we define the
Mahler measure
of
\alpha
,
M(\alpha)
, by
M(\alpha)=a_{d}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|\alpha_{i}|\}
. The height of
\alpha
,
h(\alpha)
, is defined by
h(\alpha)=\log M(\alpha)/d
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix an integer
g\geq 2
. We set
\lambda_{g,g-1}=1/(g^{2}(g-1))
. The curve
C_{g,g-1}
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
by

\displaystyle C_{g,g-1}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle y^{2}=f_{g-1}(x)=A_{g,g-1}(x)^{2}-4\lambda_{g,g-1}\,x^{2g}(x-1),

where

\displaystyle A_{g,g-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(x-g)x^{g-1}+(x-1)^{g}+\lambda_{g,g-1}\,x^{2}(x-1)\left((x-g)x^{g-1}-(x-1)^{g}\right)}{(x-g)},

is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
.

Proof:

We need to prove that
f_{g-1}(x)
is a square-free polynomial. We assume on the contrary that
f_{g-1}(x_{0})=f_{g-1}^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
for some root
x_{0}
of
f_{g-1}(x)
in the algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We may write

(3)

\displaystyle A_{g,g-1}(x)=x^{g-1}p_{g-1}(x)-(x-1)^{g}q_{g-1}(x),

where

p_{g-1}(x)=1+\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{2}(x-1),\qquad q_{g-1}(x)=\lambda_{g,g-1}(x^{2}+(g-1)x+g^{2}-g).

In particular,
x_{0}\neq 0,1
. One now sees that

\displaystyle f_{g-1}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2A_{g,g-1}(x)A_{g,g-1}^{\prime}(x)-8g\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{2g-1}(x-1)-4\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{2g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2A_{g,g-1}(x)A_{g,g-1}^{\prime}(x)-4\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{2g-1}((2g+1)x-2g).

The vanishing of both
f_{g-1}(x)
and
f^{\prime}_{g-1}(x)
at
x_{0}
yields that

\displaystyle 2x_{0}\left(x_{0}-1\right)A_{g,g-1}^{\prime}\left(x_{0}\right)=\left((2g+1)x_{0}-2g\right)A_{g,g-1}\left(x_{0}\right).

Substituting (
3

(4)

\displaystyle x_{0}^{g-1}G_{g-1}\left(x_{0}\right)=(x_{0}-1)^{g}H_{g-1}\left(x_{0}\right),

where

are defined by

\displaystyle G_{g-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-3x+2)p_{g-1}(x)+2x(x-1)p_{g-1}^{\prime}(x)=3\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{4}-5\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{3}+2\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{2}-3x+2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{g-1}(x)(x-g)(3x-2),

\displaystyle H_{g-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-x+2g)q_{g-1}(x)+2x(x-1)q_{g-1}^{\prime}(x)=3\lambda_{g,g-1}x^{3}+(3g\lambda_{g,g-1}-5\lambda_{g,g-1})x^{2}+\left(g^{2}\lambda_{g,g-1}-3g\lambda_{g,g-1}+2\lambda_{g,g-1}\right)x+2.

The resultant of

\operatorname{Res}_{x}(G_{g-1}(x),H_{g-1}(x))=72\lambda_{g,g-1}^{7}(3g+1)^{2}(g-1)^{3}g^{5}(9g^{2}-10g+2).

Therefore,

Corollary 4.2

Proof:

We refer the reader to

for the information introduced in this paragraph. Let

An infinite family of hyperelliptic curves

In Proposition
2.1
, for a fixed integer
g\geq 2
, one may choose
m=1
and
1\leq d\leq g-1
. Under the assumption of existence of the function
\phi
with
\operatorname{div}(\phi)=(g+1)P_{0}^{\prime}+(g+3)P_{1}-(2g+4)P_{\infty}
, it follows that there exists a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
with a divisor whose class represents a rational point in the Jacobian of order dividing
2g^{2}+5g+2d+3
.
We fix two integers
g
and
d
with
g\geq 3
and
0\leq d\leq g-1
. Following the construction in §
2
, we may give explicit
1
-parameter families of such curves for which the order of the divisor is exactly
2g^{2}+5g+2d+3
when
d
is chosen to be
g-1
and
h(x)=1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Fix an odd integer
g\geq 3
. We set

\displaystyle\alpha=\beta-\frac{(\beta-1)^{\frac{g+3}{2}}}{\beta^{\frac{g+1}{2}}},\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\},\qquad\lambda=\frac{(\alpha-1)^{g+2}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\alpha^{g-1}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

We also set

A(x)=\frac{x^{g+1}(x-\alpha)^{2}-(x-1)\left((x-1)^{g+2}-\lambda(x-\beta)^{2}x^{g-1}\right)}{2(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)},\qquad y^{2}=f(x)=A(x)^{2}-\lambda x^{2g}(x-1).

Then
A(x)
is a polynomial of degree
g
in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
. In addition, for all but finitely many rational values of
\beta
, if the discriminant of
f(x)
is nonzero, then the equation
y^{2}=f(x)

defines a hyperelliptic curve
C_{\beta}
of genus
g
over
\mathbb{Q}
, where the divisor

D_{0}=(0,A(0))-P_{\infty}

on
C_{\beta}
is a torsion divisor whose order is exactly
2g^{2}+7g+1
.

Proof:

Let
B(x)=x^{g+1}(x-\alpha)^{2}-(x-1)\left((x-1)^{g+2}-\lambda(x-\beta)^{2}x^{g-1}\right)
. One sees that
B(\alpha)=-(\alpha-1)((\alpha-1)^{g+2}-\lambda(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\alpha^{g-1})
. Substituting with the expression for
\lambda
, one gets
B(\alpha)=0
. Similarly,
B(\beta)=0
, hence
(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)
divides
B(x)
and
A(x)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
. In addition,
\deg A(x)=g
, therefore,
\deg f(x)=2g+1
.

In view of Proposition
2.1
, the order of the class of the divisor
D_{0}
divides
l=2g^{2}+7g+1
. Let
L(x,y)
be a rational function such that
lD_{0}
is the divisor of
L(x,y)
.
We set
\theta(x,y)=y-A(x)
,
\bar{\theta}(x,y)=y+A(x)
,
\phi(x,y)=a(x)-b(x)y
,
\bar{\phi}(x,y)=a(x)+b(x)y
and
\psi(x,y)=\phi(x,y)/x^{g+1}
, where
a(x)
and
b(x)
are as in §
2
satisfying the following identities

a(x)-b(x)A(x)=(x-1)q(x),\qquad a(x)+b(x)A(x)=x^{g+1}p(x),

where
p(x)q(x)=(x-1)^{g+2}-\lambda b^{2}(x)x^{g-1}
,
p(x)=x-\alpha
and
b(x)=x-\beta
. Then we obtain
a(x)=x^{g+1}(x-\alpha)-(x-\beta)A(x)
. The function
L(x,y)
may be defined as follows

L(x,y)=\frac{\theta(x,y)^{g+3}}{\psi(x,y)}=\frac{\theta(x,y)^{g+3}}{\phi(x,y)}x^{g+1}

If the order of the class of
D_{0}
is not
l
, then we may assume that
l=mn
, where the order of the class of
D_{0}
is
n
. In particular, there exists a rational function
N(x,y)
such that the principal divisor
nD_{0}
is the divisor of
N(x,y)
. It follows that there exists
u\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0\}
such that

uN^{m}(x,y)=L(x,y)
. Since
P_{1}^{\prime}=(1,-A(1))
and
P_{0}^{\prime}=(0,-A(0))
are not in the support of the divisor
D_{0}
, one obtains that

L(P_{1}^{\prime})/L(P_{0}^{\prime})=N^{m}(P_{1}^{\prime})/N^{m}(P_{0}^{\prime})
.
Evaluating the function
L(x,y)
at
P_{1}^{\prime}
, one gets

\displaystyle L\left(P_{1}^{\prime}\right)=\cfrac{(-2A(1))^{g+3}}{a(1)+b(1)A(1)}=\cfrac{(1-\alpha)^{g+2}}{(\beta-1)^{g+3}}.

Similarly, one sees that

\displaystyle L(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-A(x))^{g+3}\,x^{g+1}(a(x)+b(x)y)}{a(x)^{2}-b(x)^{2}y^{2}}=\frac{(y-A(x))^{g+3}\,x^{g+1}(a(x)+b(x)y)}{x^{g+1}(x-1)^{g+3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-A(x))^{g+3}(a(x)+b(x)y)}{(x-1)^{g+3}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle L(P_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(2A(0))^{g+3}\,(a(0)-b(0)A(0))=\frac{-1\cdot(-1)^{(g+2)(g+3)}}{\alpha^{g+3}\beta^{g+3}(-\alpha)}=-\frac{1}{\alpha^{g+4}\beta^{g+3}}.

Then we obtain that

\frac{L(P_{1}^{\prime})}{L(P_{0}^{\prime})}=\frac{(\alpha-1)^{g+2}\,\alpha^{g+4}\,\beta^{g+3}}{(\beta-1)^{g+3}}.

Since
g
is odd, we assume that
g=2k-1
for some integer
k\geq 2
.
We know that

\alpha=\beta-\frac{(\beta-1)^{k+1}}{\beta^{k}}=\frac{\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1}}{\beta^{k}}.

Hence,

\alpha-1=\frac{\beta^{k+1}-\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k+1}}{\beta^{k}}=(\beta-1)\left(\frac{\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k}}{\beta^{k}}\right).

Therefore,

\frac{L(P_{1}^{\prime})}{L(P_{0}^{\prime})}=\frac{(\beta-1)^{g+2}(\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k})^{g+2}(\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1})^{g+4}\beta^{g+3}}{\beta^{k(g+2)}\,\beta^{k(g+4)}\,(\beta-1)^{g+3}}.

Clearing denominators, we obtain

L(P_{0}^{\prime})\,(\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k})^{g+2}\,(\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1})^{g+4}=L(P_{1}^{\prime})\,\beta^{4k^{2}+2k-2}(\beta-1).

N^{m}(P_{0}^{\prime})\,(\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k})^{g+2}\,(\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1})^{g+4}=N^{m}(P_{1}^{\prime})\,\beta^{4k^{2}+2k-2}(\beta-1).

The square-free polynomials

\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k},\quad\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1},\quad\beta,\quad\beta-1
are pairwise relatively prime.
We note that

\gcd(g+2,\,2g^{2}+7g+1)=\begin{cases}5,&\text{if }g\equiv 3\pmod{5},\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similarly,

\gcd(g+4,\,2g^{2}+7g+1)=\begin{cases}5,&\text{if }g\equiv 1\pmod{5},\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that at least one of the integers
g+2
or
g+4
is relatively prime to
2g^{2}+7g+1
. Let
q\geq 2
be a prime divisor of
m
such that either
\gcd(q,g+2)=1
or
\gcd(q,g+4)=1
. One may see that
\frac{(\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k})^{g+2}(\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1})^{g+4}}{\beta^{g(g+3)}(\beta-1)}

can not be a
q
-power except possibly for finitely many rational values of
\beta
. This can be justified as follows. One considers the following equation

y^{q}=(\beta^{k}-(\beta-1)^{k})^{a}\,(\beta^{k+1}-(\beta-1)^{k+1})^{b}\beta^{c}(\beta-1)^{q-1}

where
g+2\equiv a
,
g+4\equiv b
and
-g(g+3)\equiv c
modulo
q
,
0\leq a,b,c\leq q-1
. Since either
\gcd(q,g+2)=1
or
\gcd(q,g+4)=1
, it follows that
\max(a,b)\geq 1
. For
q>2
, the latter equation describes a superelliptic curve whose genus can be computed by the Riemann–Hurwitz
formula as in
[
25
, Proposition 3.7.3]
yielding that the genus is at least
2
. For
q=2
and
g\geq 5
, the curve is hyperelliptic with genus at least
2
.
In both cases, Faltings’ theorem implies the existence of finitely many rational points on this curve.
The remaining case is
q=2
and
g=3
, where the latter curve becomes
y^{2}=6x^{4}-15x^{3}+14x^{2}-6x+1
. This curve can be described by the Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}-x^{2}+x
whose Mordell-Weil rank can be checked using
Magma

[
3
]
to be 0.
This argument together with the fact that
l
is relatively prime with either
g+2
or
g+4
imply that the order of
D_{0}
is exactly
l
for all but finitely many rational values of
\beta
.

\Box

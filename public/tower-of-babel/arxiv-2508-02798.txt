1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backround

The original Jackson-Bernstein theorem concerns the relations between the smoothness of a function and its degree of approximation by trigonometric polynomials on the unit circle. Later, the similar problems of approximation by analytic polynomials on the certain compacts in
\mathbb{C}
were studied (see references in
[
4
]
). In
[
2
]

the characterization by the polynomial approximation of the smoothness of functions on a Jordan arc
L\subset\mathbb{C}
involves the uniform
estimates of derivatives of polynomials in a neighbourhood of
L
. The harmonic polynomials with the uniform estimates on gradient
can also be used for a constructive description of the Lipschitz classes of functions on the
Jordan arcs in
\mathbb{C}

[
3
]
. We point out, that in all cases the argument uses the conformal mapping techniques.

Another approach is developed in
[
1
]
for the Lipschitz spaces
Lip_{\omega}(L)
with the Dini regular modulus
\omega
of continuity on the chord-arc curve
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Instead of polynomials of degree
n
, the authors used harmonic functions in the
\delta
-neighbourhood of
L
with the estimates on the gradient.
A sharp theorem on relations between size
\delta
of a neighbourhood and rate
\omega(\delta)
of approximation is proved.
In
[
12
]
the results were extended to the compacts in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which are bi-Lipschitz images of the
d-2
-dimensional cube.

The question in order is to extend the results
[
1
,
12
]
on harmonic approximation either on compacts in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension greater than
d-2
, or for general moduluses of continuity, as well. We do this job for the porous compacts, which have Assouad dimension
\dim_{A}K<d
(
[
5
]
, see details in Section 2.4). For instance, the main result holds
for the Ahlfors-David
\theta
-regular compacts
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\theta<d
. Also, our Theorem
1.1
holds for general moduluses of continuity without the Dini restrictions.

On the other hand Vitushkin type results in position of harmonic approximation (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
9
,
10
]
) are closely related to the problem considered here.
To construct the approximating harmonic function we combine the ideas in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
with
[
1
]
,
and obtain a quantitative Jackson-Bernstein type theorem on relations of the smoothness and degree of approximation.

1.2.
Main result

Throughout this paper, we consider
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the

d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. The Lebesgue measure of
E
is denoted by
|E|
, and if
x,\;y\;\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
|x-y|

denotes the Euclidean distance from
x
to
y
. The Euclidean distance between arbitrary sets
A,\;B\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
dist(A,B)=\inf\{|x-y|:\;x\in A,\;y\in B\}
.

Definition 1
.

A bounded closed set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called porous, if there is a constant
0<c<1
such that for each ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

there exists a ball
mB\subset B
with the properties
mB\cap K=\emptyset
and
|mB|\geq c|B|
.

Remark 1
.

The basic example of a porous set is the Ahlfors-David
\theta
-regular compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
0<\theta<d

[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
,
for which there exist positive
constants
C_{1},\>C_{2}
and
0<\theta<d
such that for any
x\in K
and
R>0

C_{1}R^{\theta}\leq\mathcal{H}^{\theta}(K\cap B(x,R))\leq C_{2}R^{\theta},

where
\mathcal{H}^{\theta}
is
\theta
-Hausdorff measure and
B(x,R)
is a ball with the center
x
and radius
R
.

Definition 2
.

A continuous increasing concave function
\omega:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\omega(0)=0

is called a modulus of continuity. Clearly, the modulus
\omega
satisfies the doubling property: there is a constant
C>0
such that for each
\delta>0
it holds
\omega(2\delta)\leq C\omega(\delta)
.

The Lipschitz space
Lip_{\omega}(K)
consists of those functions continuous on
K
such that the semi-norm

\|f\|_{\omega}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\omega(|x-y|)}

is finite.

Given
\delta>0
, let

K_{\delta}=\{x:\>\inf_{y\in K}|x-y|<\delta\}

be a
\delta
-neighbourhood of
K
, and let
\partial K_{\delta}
be its boundary.
The main result is a following Jackson-Bernstein type theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d>2
, be a porous compact, and let
\omega(t)
be a continuity modulus. Given a continuous function
f
on
K
, then
f\in Lip_{\omega}(K)
if and only if
for any
\delta>0
there is a harmonic function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in a
\delta
-neighbourhood
K_{\delta}
of
K
such that

(1.1)

\sup_{K}|f-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}|\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\delta),

and

(1.2)

\sup_{K_{\delta}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\delta}|\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta}

where the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are independent of
\delta
and
\|f\|_{\omega}
.

The theorem generalizes either the results
[
1
]
where these problems are researched on the smooth curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, or the results
[
12
]
for the bi-Lipschitz image in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the
d-2
-dimensional cube. Particularly, Theorem
1.1
is true for the Ahlfors-David
\theta
-regular compacts
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
0\leq\theta<d
, or for the bi-Lipschitz image of
d-1
–dimensional cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as well.

Remark 2
.

Observe that the sufficiency condition of Theorem
1.1
does not appeal to the class of harmonic functions.
If for each rather small
\delta>0
one can find any
C^{1}
-smooth function in
K_{\delta}
with properties (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) then, clearly,
f\in Lip_{\omega}(K)
. Thus, we will prove the necessity condition of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cubes and dyadic decomposition in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

In this paper, we only consider cubes which are half-open and have sides parallel to the
coordinate axes. That is, a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a set of the form

Q=[a_{1};b_{1})\times\dots\times[a_{d};b_{d})

with edge size

\ell(Q)=b_{1}-a_{1}=\dots=b_{d}-a_{d}
.
The dyadic decomposition of each cube
Q
is

\mathcal{D}(Q)=\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{D}_{j}(Q)

where each
\mathcal{D}_{j}(Q)
consists of the
2^{jd}
pairwise disjoint (half-open) cubes
Q^{\prime}
, with side length

\ell(Q^{\prime})=2^{-j}
, such that

Q=\bigcup_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}(Q)}Q^{\prime}

for every
j=0,1,\dots
. The cubes in
\mathcal{D}(Q)
are called dyadic cubes (with respect to
Q
).

2.2.
Whitney extension

From the point of proof, it is convenient to have the function
f
extended from
K
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. So, we start with a modified Whitney extension of the function

f\in Lip_{\omega}(K)
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see Stein
[
14
, 2.2, Ch.6 ]
).

(1)

\widetilde{f}=f
on
K
.

(2)

\widetilde{f}
has the compact support
supp\;\widetilde{f}\subset R_{0}
, where
R_{0}
is a cube with edge size
\ell(R_{0})>1
, and the next estimate holds

\textrm{dist}(K,\partial R_{0})\geq\ell(R_{0})/4.

(3)

\widetilde{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K)\cap Lip_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and there is a constant
C>0
depending on
K
such that

\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\omega}\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}.

(4)

There is a sequnce of constants
C_{k}>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
K
such that

|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{f}(x)|\leq C_{k}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\textrm{dist}(x,K))}{\textrm{dist}^{k}(x,K)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K.

Thus, in what follows we assume that
f
is extended by Whitney to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
so, that the propperties (1)–(4) take place. We denote the Whitney extension
\widetilde{f}
by the same symbol
f
.

2.3.
Regularization

Consider the
C^{\infty}
-regularization of the Whitney extension
f
from Section 2.2. For this choose any function

\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the support in the ball
B(0,1)
centered in origin and radius
1
, and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(t)dt=1
. Then, a function

(2.1)

f_{\varepsilon}(x)=f\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-t)\phi\Big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\Big)dt,

provided
\varepsilon<1/4
, is the claimed
C^{\infty}
-smooth regularization
with the compact support in the cube

R_{0}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f(x)|\leq\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\varepsilon),

and

(2.2)

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\omega}\leq\|f\|_{\omega}.

Also,
we easily obtain the uniform estimate on the gradient of the regularization function
f_{\varepsilon}

|\nabla^{k}f_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\textrm{dist}(x,K))}{\textrm{dist}^{k}(x,K)}

with the constant
C>0
independent of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Indeed, for
\varepsilon\leq\textrm{dist}(x,K)/2
, we directly differentiate (
2.1
) with respect to
x
and apply the gradient estimate (4) of the Whitney extension of
f
and property (
2.2
).

If

\varepsilon>\textrm{dist}(x,K)/2
, then choose any
x_{0}\in K
such that
|x-x_{0}|=\textrm{dist}(x,K)
, we easily calculate

\displaystyle\big|\nabla^{k}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\big|=\big|\nabla^{k}f\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\big(f-f(x_{0})\big)\ast\nabla^{k}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\textrm{dist}(x,K))}{\textrm{dist}^{k}(x,K)},

where in the last line the concavity of
\omega
is applied. This completes the gradient estimate of
f_{\varepsilon}
.

2.4.
Porous sets

For a bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, let
\mathcal{N}(E;r)
denote the minimal number of open
balls of radius
r
with centers in
E
that are needed to cover the set
E
. Note that the centres of these balls form a minimal
r
-dense set in
E
.

The Assouad dimension

\dim_{A}(K)
of
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is then the infimum of all
\lambda\geq 0
for which there exists a constant
C_{\lambda}
such that for every ball
B(x,\;R)
with the center
x\in K
and radius
R
, and for each
0<r<R
it holds

(2.3)

\mathcal{N}(K\cap B(x,R),r)\leq C_{\lambda}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\lambda}.

It is well-known (see
[
5
]
) that a set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is porous if and only if

\dim_{A}(K)<d
.

Instead of balls, also dyadic cubes could be used in the construction leading to an estimate of type (
2.3
). The dyadic formulation is convenient from the point
of view of our proof.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{j}
be the family of dyadic cubes from Section 2.5.
Then, arguing as in
[
5
, Theorem 5.2]
, where Assouad dimension is defined, or
[
6
, Ch. V, 5.31]
, where Hausdorff measure of the porous set is estimated, one can prove the lemma.
For a cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
define the covering of
R\cap K
by pairwise disjoint cubes

\mathcal{D}_{\ell,K}(R)=\big\{Q:\;\ell(Q)=\ell,\;Q\cap K\cap R\neq\emptyset\big\},

\ell>0
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{\ell,K}(R)
be the number of cubes
Q
in
\mathcal{D}_{\ell,K}(R)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given a porous set
K
, there exist two constants
0\leq\lambda<d
and
C_{\lambda}>0
such that for each cube
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for
0<\ell<\ell(R)/4
, and the family
\mathcal{D}_{\ell,K}(R)
,
it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\ell,K}(R)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\left(\frac{\ell(R)}{{\ell(Q)}}\right)^{\lambda}.

Proof.

Step 1
. We start with the covering by cubes from the dyadic decomposition of
R
. Thus, let
c
be the porosity costant, and let
k_{0}
be a positive integer, such that

2^{-k_{0}}\leq c<2^{-k_{0}+1}
. Define
\theta=2^{-k_{0}}
. Choose a dyadic cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}(R)
such that
Q\cap K=\varnothing
, and
\ell(Q)=2^{-k_{0}}\ell(R)
. Consider
\mathcal{D}_{k_{0}}(R)
, and let

\mathcal{D}_{k_{0},K}(R)
denote the subfamily of
\mathcal{D}_{k_{0}}(R)
of cubes, intersecting
K
. Clearly, the number

\mathcal{N}_{k_{0},K}(R)
of the family
\mathcal{D}_{k_{0},K}(R)
is estimated

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k_{0},K}(R)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{d}-1

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{\lambda}=2^{-k_{0}\lambda},

where we choosed the constant
\lambda
,
0\leq\lambda<d
, independent of
R
.

For each cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k_{0},K}(R)
we repeat the construction and choose a cube
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(Q)

such that
Q^{\prime}\cap K=\varnothing
, and
\ell(Q^{\prime})=2^{-k_{0}}\ell(Q)=2^{-2k_{0}}\ell(R)
. After the
n
-iteration
we obtain the subfamily
\mathcal{D}_{nk_{0},K}(R)\subset\mathcal{D}_{nk_{0}}(R)
of cubes
Q^{(n)}
intersecting
K
. The number of cubes from the subfamily is estimated by

\mathcal{N}_{nk_{0},K}(R)\leq\left(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{(n)})}\right)^{\lambda}=2^{-nk_{0}\lambda}.

Step 2
. Up to the positive constant we obtain the similar estimates for all families of pairwise disjoint equal (non-dyadic, in general) cubes
Q
covering
R\cap K
, and such that
Q\subset R
.

Thus, let
K\cap R
is covered by a family
\mathcal{D}_{\ell,K}(R)
of pairwise non-intersecting cubes
Q\subset R
of edge size
\ell
. Define the integer
n
such that

2^{-k_{0}(n+1)}\leq\ell<2^{-k_{0}n}.

Clearly, for each cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell,K}(R)
there exist two cubes

Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{(n-1)k_{0}}(R)
and
Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{(n+2)k_{0}}(R)
such that

Q^{\prime}\subset Q\subset Q^{\prime\prime}.

Therefore, the number
\mathcal{N}_{\ell,K}(R)
of cubes from the family
\mathcal{D}_{\ell,K}(R)
is estimated above as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\ell,K}(R)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{(n+2)k_{0},K}(R)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{\prime\prime})}\right)^{\lambda}=\left(2^{3k_{0}}\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q^{\prime})}\right)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{3k_{0}}\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{\lambda},

that is required with the constant
C_{\lambda}=2^{3k_{0}\lambda}
.

Step 3
. Finally, consider an arbitrary covering of
K\cap R
by pairwise disjoint cubes
Q
of edge size
\ell
. Observe, that this covering may have the non-empty intersection with the complement
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash R
.
Let
R^{\prime}\supset R
be a cube with the edge length
\ell(R^{\prime})=n\ell
with the smallest integer
n>2
. Clearly, there is a cube

R^{\prime\prime}\subset R
with the edge length
(n-2)\ell(R^{\prime})
. By Step 2, it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\ell,K}(R)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{\ell,K}(R^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\left(\frac{\ell(R^{\prime})}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{\lambda}\leq C_{\lambda}\left(\frac{n}{n-2}\right)^{\lambda}\left(\frac{\ell(R^{\prime\prime})}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}3^{\lambda}\left(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{\lambda},

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.5.
Reduction of proof

By the regularization from Section 2.3, we will prove the necessity part assuming
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The next strengthened form of estimate (
1.1
) holds.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given a cube
R_{0}
, a porous compact
K\subset R_{0}
,
dist(K,\partial R_{0})\geq 1/4
, a continuity modulus
\omega(t)
and a function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
supp\;f\subset R_{0}
. Then, for each
0<\delta<1/4
there exists a harmonic function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in the neighbourhood
K_{\delta}
such that

(2.4)

\sup_{K_{\delta}}|f-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}|\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\delta),

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\|f\|_{\omega}
and
\delta
.

Before proving Proposition
2.2
, observe that it clearly implies the nesessity of Theorem
1.1
.
Indeed, by the triangle inequality and by the regularization from Section 2.3, estimate (
1.1
) is the direct corollary of (
2.4
).

The gradient estimate (
1.2
) modulo regularization is proved as follows. After redesignation, we may assume that for each
\delta>0
there is the harmonic function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in the
2\delta
-neighbourhood
K_{2\delta}

of
K
such that (
2.4
) holds.
By the triangle inequality, there is a constant
C
such that for each
\delta>0
one has

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in K_{2\delta}\\
|x-y|<\delta\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(y)|\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\delta).

Differentiating the Poisson formula for the harmonic function
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
in the ball
B=B(x,\delta)\subset K_{2\delta}
,
one clearly obtains

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(t)\bigg|_{t=x}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{t}(\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(t)-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(x))\bigg|_{t=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{d}}{\delta}\int_{|y-x|=\delta}\nabla_{t}\frac{\delta^{2}-|x-t|^{2}}{|y-t|^{d}}\bigg|_{t=x}(\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(y)-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(x))dS(y),

where
dS(y)
is the induced surface measure on
\partial B
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\;\int_{|y-x|=\delta}\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta^{d}}dS(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta},

with a constant
C
independent of
\delta>0
and
x\in K_{\delta}
, and we obtain (
1.2
).

Remark 3
.

Observe, that we may prove Proposition
2.2
for a discreet positive sequence of reals
\delta_{j}\rightarrow 0
with the constant
C
independent of
\delta_{j}
. Then clearly, (
2.4
) holds for all
\delta>0
.

3.2.
Vitushkin type localization

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\mathcal{E}(t)=C_{d}|t|^{-d+2}
be a fundamental solution of the Laplasian
\Delta
. Fix

\Phi_{j}=\{\phi_{Q}:\;Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}\}

constructed in Section 3.1 we have

\displaystyle f(x)=\mathcal{E}\ast\Delta f

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}}\mathcal{E}\ast(\phi_{Q}\Delta f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}}V_{\phi_{Q}}f(x),

An operator defined by the formula

\displaystyle V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathcal{E}\ast(\phi_{Q}\Delta f)(t)

\displaystyle=C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\phi_{Q}(y)\Delta f(y)}{|t-y|^{d-2}}dy,

is called the localization assigned to
\phi_{Q}

7
,
10
,
11
]
. Observe, that since

the localization
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
is well defined in the classical sense. Next,
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
is harmonic outside the support of
\phi_{Q}
. Also,
V_{\phi_{Q}}f=0
if
supp\;\phi_{Q}\cap supp\;f=\varnothing
. In what follows we will approximate each function
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
by a harmonic function in a neighborhood of
K
. We have a modification of Lemma 5
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\omega
be a modulus of continuity. There is a constant
C
depending on
d
such that for each bump function
\phi_{Q}\in\Phi_{j}
,
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j},\;j=0,1,\dots
, and
\ell=\ell(Q)=2^{-j}
, it holds

(3.1)

\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V_{\phi_{Q}}f|\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\omega(\ell).

Proof.

The function
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
is harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash 2Q
, and it vanishes at
\infty
, therefore by the maximum principle, it is sufficient to prove the estimate (
3.1
) in
2Q
.

By the elementary differentiating formula

fg^{\prime\prime}=(fg)^{\prime\prime}+f^{\prime\prime}g-2(f^{\prime}g)^{\prime},

one has for
t\in 2Q

\displaystyle V_{\phi_{Q}}(f)(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}\ast\phi_{Q}\Delta(f-f(t))(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}\ast\Delta\big(\phi_{Q}(f-f(t))\big)(t)+\mathcal{E}\ast(f-f(t))\Delta\phi_{Q}(t)

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{E}\ast\partial_{y_{i}}\big((f-f(t))\partial_{y_{i}}\phi_{Q}\big)(t)

\displaystyle=\;I_{1}\;+\;I_{2}\;+\;I_{3}.

For the first term
I_{1}
, we easily have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}\ast\Delta\big(\phi_{Q}(f-f(t))\big)(t)

\displaystyle=\phi_{Q}\big(f-f(t)\big)(t)=0.

Secondly, term
I_{2}
is an integral with the weak singularity and by property (4) in Section 3.1 of the bump function and the
Lip_{\omega}
-estimates of
f
, one has

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\int_{2Q}\frac{|\Delta\phi_{Q}(y)||f(y)-f(t)|}{|t-y|^{d-2}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\ell)}{\ell^{2}}\int_{2Q}\frac{dy}{|t-y|^{d-2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)

uniformly for
t\in 2Q
with a constant
C_{d}
depending on
d
.

Finally, term
I_{3}
is the sum of integrals with the weak singularity

\displaystyle I_{3,i}

\displaystyle=C_{d}\;\mathcal{E}\ast\partial_{y_{i}}\bigg(\partial_{y_{i}}\phi_{Q}\big(f-f(t)\big)\bigg)(t)

\displaystyle=C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}\big(\partial_{y_{i}}\phi_{Q}(y)(f(y)-f(t))\big)}{|t-y|^{d-2}}dy.

We apply the Green formula to
I_{3,i}
for each
i=1,\dots,d
. So, integrating with respect to the domain
2Q\cap\{y:|y-t|>\varepsilon\}
and taking into account that
supp\;\phi_{Q}\subset 2Q
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{3,i,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=C_{d}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in 2Q\\
|y-t|>\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}\big(\partial_{y_{i}}\phi_{Q}(y)(f(y)-f(t))\big)}{|t-y|^{d-2}}dy

\displaystyle=-C_{d}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in 2Q\\
|y-t|>\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\partial_{y_{i}}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{d-2}}\partial_{y_{i}}\phi_{Q}(y)\big(f(y)-f(t)\big)dy

\displaystyle+C_{d}\int_{|y-t|=\varepsilon}\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}\phi_{Q}(y)\big(f(y)-f(t)\big)}{|t-y|^{d-2}}\cos(\nu,y_{i})dS(y)

\displaystyle=I^{\prime}_{3,i,\varepsilon}\;+\;I^{\prime\prime}_{3,i,\varepsilon}

where
dS(y)
is the induced surface
d-1
-measure and
\nu=\nu(y)
denotes the outer normal to the sphere
\{y:|y-t|=\varepsilon\}
. We estimate both the inegrals, as follows

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}_{3,i,\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\ell)}{\ell}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in 2Q\\
|y-t|>\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{d-1}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)

and respectively,

\displaystyle|I^{\prime\prime}_{3,i,\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\omega(\varepsilon)}{\ell}\int_{|y-t|=\varepsilon}\frac{dS(y)}{\varepsilon^{d-2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\frac{\varepsilon\omega(\varepsilon)}{\ell}.

Observing

|I_{3,i}|\leq\sup_{0<\varepsilon<\ell}|I_{3,i,\varepsilon}|\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell),

and summing

|I_{3}|\leq C_{d}\sum_{i}|I_{3,i}|\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell),

we finish the proof of (
3.1
) with a constant
C_{d}
depending on
d
.
∎

3.3.
Taylor expansion of localization

Let

\mathcal{D}_{j,K}=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}:\>Q\cap K\neq\emptyset\}

be a family of all dyadic cubes with edge size
\ell=\ell(Q)=2^{-j}
intersecting
K
.
Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\supset\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
be the family of all cubes
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
such that there exists a neighbour cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
. We call two dyadic cubes neighbour if their closures have non-empty intersection. Clearly,

\bigcup\{Q^{\prime};\;Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\}

contains a
2^{-j}
-neighbourhood of

\bigcup\{Q;\;Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}\}
.

By the porosity condition, for each
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
one can take a ball
B_{Q}\subset Q
with the center
c_{B_{Q}}
, radius
r>0
such that

r\geq c\ell
, and euclidean distance
dist(B_{Q},K)\geq 2r
,
where
0<c<1
is a porosity constant. The same is trivial for all cubes
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}\backslash\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
, since these cubes do not intersect
K
. Particularly, it holds for all
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
.

For each ball
B_{Q}
,
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
, consider the Taylor expansion of the fundumental solution
\mathcal{E}(t-y)
at
\infty
with a pole in the center
c_{B_{Q}}
of
{B_{Q}}
. Namely, define a multi-index

\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})
,
|\alpha|=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}
, and
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\dots\alpha_{d}!
.
We need a modified estimate from
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is a constant
C_{d}
depending on dimension
d
such that for
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}

with the Euclidean norm
|t|
, it holds

(3.2)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t|^{d-2}}\right|\leq\;C_{d}\;\alpha!\;|\alpha|^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\left(\frac{2d}{|t|}\right)^{|\alpha|+d-2},

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})
, where
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,d
, and let
|z|=(|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{d}|^{2})^{1/2}
.

For each
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus 0
, the fundumental solution
\mathcal{E}(t-x)=C_{d}|t-x|^{2-d}
considered as the function of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus t
, may be extended as holomorphic function

\mathcal{E}(t-z)
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that
|z|<|t|/2
.

By the Cauchy-Leray integral formula
[
13
, §3.2]
, it holds

\mathcal{E}(t-z)=C_{d}\frac{|t|}{2}\int_{|w|=|t|/2}\frac{\mathcal{E}(t-w)}{(|t|^{2}/4-\langle w,z\rangle)^{d}}dS(w),

where for
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

\langle w,z\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}w_{j}\;\overline{z_{j}},

and
dS
is the induced surface measure on the
2d-1
-sphere
|w|=|t|/2
.
Differentiating, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(t)}{\partial t^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(t-x)}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\bigg|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(t-z)}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\bigg|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=C_{d}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\frac{(d+|\alpha|-1)!}{(d-1)!}\frac{|t|}{2}\int_{|w|=|t|/2}\frac{\mathcal{E}(t-w)\;\overline{w^{\alpha}}}{(|t|^{2}/4-\langle w,z\rangle)^{d+|\alpha|}}dS(w)\bigg|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=C_{d}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\frac{(d+|\alpha|-1)!}{(d-1)!}\left(\frac{|t|}{2}\right)^{1-2|\alpha|-2d}\int_{|w|=|t|/2}\mathcal{E}(t-w)\;\overline{w^{\alpha}}\;dS(w).

Therefore, estimating the above integral

\int_{|w|=|t|/2}\mathcal{E}(t-w)\;\overline{w^{\alpha}}\;dS(w)\leq C_{d}\left(\frac{2}{|t|}\right)^{|\alpha|+d+1},

we have

(3.3)

\bigg|\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t|^{d-2}}\bigg|\leq C_{d}\frac{(d+|\alpha|-1)!}{(d-1)!}\left(\frac{2}{|t|}\right)^{d+|\alpha|-2}.

Now, by Stirling’s approximation of the Euler
\Gamma
-function, it holds

(d+|\alpha|-1)!\leq C_{d}|\alpha|!\;|\alpha|^{d-1}.

and

\displaystyle|\alpha|!

\displaystyle=\Gamma(|\alpha|+1)\leq C_{d}\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{e}\right)^{|\alpha|}\sqrt{2\pi|\alpha|}.

Combining the same argument with
the logarithmic convexity of the
\Gamma
-function implies

\displaystyle\alpha!=\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+1)\dots\Gamma(\alpha_{d}+1)

\displaystyle\geq\Gamma^{d}\big(|\alpha|/\;d+1\big)

\displaystyle\geq C_{d}\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{e\;d}\right)^{\frac{|\alpha|}{d}d}\big(\sqrt{2\pi|\alpha|/d}\big)^{d}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\alpha|!\leq C_{d}\;\alpha!\;|\alpha|^{\frac{1-d}{2}}\;d^{|\alpha|},

and substituting in (
3.3
), we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

By Lemma
3.2
, we obtain an expansion into series

\frac{1}{|t-y|^{d-2}}=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}(y-c_{B_{Q}})^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}},

which converges uniformly in
y,t
such that
4d|y-c_{B_{Q}}|<|t-c_{B_{Q}}|
.

Since
|y-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 2\ell\sqrt{d}
provided
y\in 2Q
, we can represent each localization
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
,
\;Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
, uniformly for
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\geq 4d|y-c_{B_{Q}}|
as follows

(3.4)

V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 0}C_{\alpha,Q}\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}},

where

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,Q}

\displaystyle=C_{d}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\int_{2Q}\phi_{Q}(y)(y-c_{B_{Q}})^{\alpha}\Delta f(y)dy

\displaystyle=C_{d}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\int_{2Q}\Delta\big((\phi_{Q}(y)(y-c_{B_{Q}})^{\alpha}\big)\big(f(y)-f(c_{B_{Q}})\big)dy.

By the gradient estimate of the bump function ((4), Section 3.2), it holds

(3.5)

|C_{\alpha,Q}|\leq C_{d}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)(2\ell\sqrt{d})^{d+|\alpha|-2},

where a constant
C_{d}
is independent of parameters
\ell>0
,
\alpha
and
\|f\|_{\omega}
.

3.4.
Approximation function construction

For each
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
we define a harmonic function as the next finite sum with a pole in the center
c_{B_{Q}}
of the ball
B_{Q}\subset Q

F_{Q}(t)=\frac{C_{0,Q}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}}+\sum_{|\alpha|=1}C_{\alpha,Q}\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}}.

By (
3.5
) and (
3.2
), it is clear that

\displaystyle\sup_{K}|F_{Q}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{K}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)\left(\frac{\ell^{d-2}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}}+\frac{\ell^{d-1}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)

with the constant
C
indepedependent of
Q
and
\|f\|_{\omega}
.

The finite sum

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}}F_{Q}

defines a harmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite number of poles in centers
c_{B_{Q}}
of balls
B_{Q}
,
B_{Q}\subset Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
. Recall, that all poles
c_{B_{Q}}
are separated from
K
by Euclidean distance no less than
2c\ell
uniformly with respect to
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
. Here
c
is a porosity constant. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
is harmonic in the
2c\ell
-neighbourhood of
K
.

3.5.
Proof of estimate (
2.4
)

Since
supp\;f\subset R_{0}

we have

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}}V_{\phi_{Q}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}(R_{0})}V_{\phi_{Q}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}}V_{\phi_{Q}}f(x)+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}\backslash\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}}V_{\phi_{Q}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(x)\quad+\quad\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(x)

Clearly,
2Q_{1}\cap 2Q_{2}=\varnothing
if
Q_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
and
Q_{2}\notin\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
, therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}(R_{0})\backslash\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}}V_{\phi_{Q}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}(R_{0})\backslash\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}}\mathcal{E}\ast(\phi_{Q}\Delta f)(x)

is harmonic in
\bigcup\{2Q:\;Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}\}
, which implies that
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(x)
is harmonic in
K_{\ell/2}
.

Therefore, it remains to approximate
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
. We apply the function
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, constructed in Section 3.4, and estimate the difference

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(t)-\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}}\big(V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)-F_{Q}(t)\big),

for each
t\in K_{c\ell}
, where
K_{c\ell}
is the
c\ell
-neighbourhood of
K
, and
c
is the porosity constant.

Put
t\in K_{c\ell}
, and split the sum according to whether for each ball
B_{Q}
with the center
c_{B_{Q}}
, the distance
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|
is less or greater than
8\ell d\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\big(V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)-F_{Q}(t)\big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\big(V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)-F_{Q}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\qquad I_{1}\qquad+\qquad I_{2}.

We start with term
I_{1}
. By the size property (
3.1
) of
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
and the similar property in Section 3.4 of
F_{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}|V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)-F_{Q}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}|V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}|F_{Q}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}1,

where
C
depends on
K
.
The last sum equals to the number of cubes of the family

\{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}:\;|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 8\ell d\sqrt{d}\},

which is easily estimated above by the constant
C_{d}
depending on dimension
d
uniformly with respect to
t\in K_{c\ell}
and
\ell>0
. Thus,

|I_{1}|\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell),

which is required for term
I_{1}
.

The estimate of the second sum is more complicated. For
V_{\phi_{Q}}f
we use the Taylor formula (
3.4
), when
|t-c_{B}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}
, and estimate the sum of remainders.

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\big(V_{\phi_{Q}}f(t)-F_{Q}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 0}C_{\alpha,Q}\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}C_{\alpha,Q}\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 2}C_{\alpha,Q}\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}},

where the outer sum is finite, and the inner sum converges uniformly with respect to
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8d\sqrt{d}\ell
. Recalling that
|y-c_{B_{Q}}|<2\sqrt{d}\ell
, we estimate
C_{\alpha,Q}
by (
3.5
), while
\partial^{\alpha}\frac{1}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|^{d-2}}
is estimated by (
3.2
), then it holds

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\omega}\omega(\ell)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\quad\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 2}|\alpha|^{\frac{1+d}{2}}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{d+|\alpha|-2}.

Estimate the inner sum as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 2}|\alpha|^{\frac{1+d}{2}}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{d+|\alpha|-2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{d}\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 2}|\alpha|^{\frac{1+d}{2}}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{|\alpha|-2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{d}\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 2}|\alpha|^{\frac{1+d}{2}}2^{2-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{d}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{d}

uniformly with respect to
t
,
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}
. Thus, with the constant
C_{d,K}
,
depending on
d
and
K
,
it holds

|I_{2}|\leq C_{d,K}\|f\|_{\omega}\omega(\ell)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}\\
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{|t-c_{B_{Q}}|}\right)^{d}.

Next, consider the dyadic splitting

\{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}:\;|t-c_{B_{Q}}|>8\ell d\sqrt{d}\}=\bigcup_{k>0}\mathcal{Q}_{k},

where

\mathcal{Q}_{k}=\big\{Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}:2^{k}\ell d\sqrt{d}<|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 2^{k+1}\ell d\sqrt{d}\big\}.

It holds

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,K}\|f\|_{\omega}\omega(\ell)\;\sum_{k\geq 3}\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\left(\frac{4\ell d\sqrt{d}}{2^{k}\ell\;d\sqrt{d}}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,K}\;\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)\;\sum_{k\geq 3}\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\frac{1}{2^{(k-2)d}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,K}\;\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)\;\sum_{k\geq 3}\frac{1}{2^{kd}}\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}1.

The inner sum equals to
the number of cubes
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
. Recall that if
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
, then
it holds
|t-c_{B_{Q}}|<2^{k+1}\ell d\sqrt{d}
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
. Recall, that for each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
there exists a neighbour cube
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
. For such the cube
\widetilde{Q}
one has
|t-c_{B_{\widetilde{Q}}}|\leq 2^{k+2}\ell d\sqrt{d}
.
It holds, that each cube
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}
has no more than
3^{d}-1
neighbour cubes
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}
. Therefore¡ we may estimate the number of cubes

\mathcal{N}\;\big(Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{j,K}:|t-c_{B_{Q}}|\leq 2^{k+1}\ell d\sqrt{d}\;\big)

by
3^{d}
times the number of cubes

\mathcal{N}\big(\widetilde{Q}\in\mathcal{D}_{j,K}:|t-c_{B_{\widetilde{Q}}}|\leq 2^{k+2}\ell d\sqrt{d}\;\big).

Estimating the last number of cubes by Lemma
2.1
,

C_{\lambda}\left(\frac{2^{k+2}\ell d\sqrt{d}}{\ell}\right)^{\lambda},

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}1

\displaystyle\leq 3^{d}C_{\lambda}\left(\frac{2^{k+2}\ell d\sqrt{d}}{\ell}\right)^{\lambda}.

Here the constant
0<\lambda<d
depends on
K
(in fact, it depends on the porosity constant
0<c<1
of
K
), while the constant
C_{\lambda}
depends on
\lambda
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{dk}}\bigg(\frac{2^{k}\ell d\sqrt{d}}{\ell}\bigg)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}d^{\lambda 3/2}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{dk}}\;2^{\lambda k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,d}\|f\|_{\omega}\;\omega(\ell)

since
0<\lambda<d
. That is required for term
I_{2}

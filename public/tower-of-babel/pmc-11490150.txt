Evaluation of the information quality related to osteoporosis on TikTok
Authors: Aoying Li, Qiqiang Xing, Yu Zhang, Wanda Zhan, Sibo Zhu, Linbing Lou, Chuan Jiang, Zhenzhen Guo, Hao Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis is currently considered the most common bone disease in the world and is characterized by low bone mass, deterioration of the bone tissue microstructure, and decreased bone strength. With the increasing popularity of smartphones and short videos, many patients search for various types of health information through social media, such as short videos. As one of China’s short video giants, TikTok has played a significant role in spreading health information. We found that there are many videos about osteoporosis on TikTok; however, the quality of these short videos has not yet been evaluated.ObjectiveThe purpose of this study was to evaluate the information quality of osteoporosis videos on the domestic TikTok platform.MethodsWe retrieved and screened 100 videos about osteoporosis from TikTok, extracted the basic information, encoded the video content, and recorded the source of each video. Two independent raters evaluated the information quality of each video via the DISCERN rating scale.ResultThe videos were divided into three groups according to their source: medical personnel, science communicators, and news media, with medical personnel posting the most videos. The content of the video is divided into 7 groups, namely, disease prevention, disease diagnosis, disease symptoms, disease overview, life-style, drug knowledge, and drug treatment, with the most videos related to disease overview. The average DISCERN score of the videos is 37.69 (SD = 6.78), mainly within the ‘poor’ (54/100, 54%) and ‘appropriate’ (43/100, 43%) rating ranges, with overall quality being low. Further analysis revealed a positive correlation between the number of shares, comments, likes, and favorites, and a positive correlation between the DISCERN score and the number of shares and favorites.ConclusionThe overall quality of videos concerning osteoporosis on TikTok is lower, but the quality of videos varies significantly across different sources. We should be selective and cautious when watching videos about osteoporosis on TikTok.Keywords: Osteoporosis, Information quality, Social media, TikTok, Internet

Introduction

Osteoporosis is the most common bone disease and is characterized by low bone mass and changes in bone tissue microstructure. An imbalance in bone resorption and production leads to decreased bone strength and an increased risk of fractures. It is most common in postmenopausal women and elderly individuals [1, 2].

Osteoporosis can be divided into two major categories: primary osteoporosis and secondary osteoporosis. Primary osteoporosis includes idiopathic osteoporosis and degenerative osteoporosis with unknown pathogenesis, while secondary osteoporosis accounts for less than 5% of all osteoporosis cases, mainly due to disease and drug use, such as diabetes induced-osteoporosis and glucocorticoid-dependent osteoporosis [3, 4]. The most common type of osteoporosis in women is postmenopausal osteoporosis, which is related mainly to decreased estrogen levels and aging [3]. Emerging internet information technology provides opportunities for better health education, thereby increasing patient activation, i.e., the willingness and ability of patients to actively manage their healthcare [5]. Social media platforms have shifted patients from passively receiving information to actively seeking it [6]. Although social media platforms offer considerable convenience, their use in health communication still has significant limitations. Incorrect information on social media can easily lead patients to make incorrect healthcare decisions on the basis of inaccurate information [7]. Misinformation on social media can lead to skewed perceptions of osteoporosis, affecting the prevention, treatment and management of osteoporosis. The quality of unapproved medical information poses a considerable challenge for patients and healthcare workers.

TikTok is a music creative short video social software launched by Zhang Yiming in September 2016. As one of the most popular types of software in the world, TikTok has more than 1 billion users every month, more than 60% of whom are under 29 years old [8]. Recent research has shown that TikTok can be an effective channel for providing users with health-related information [9]. However, users may also face videos with incorrect and confusing information posted by nonprofessional individuals, which can have a negative impact on their health [10, 11]. In the past, relevant researchers have explored the video quality on TikTok, such as that concerning cholelithiasis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and diabetes, on TikTok [12–14]. However, the video quality of osteoporosis on TikTok has not yet been evaluated. Therefore, we investigated the first 100 videos about osteoporosis on TikTok to evaluate the information quality of videos about osteoporosis on TikTok.

Methods

Retrieval strategy and data extraction

On March 28, 2023, we searched for the keyword ‘osteoporosis’ in the Chinese version of TikTok. Considering that most users use default values, videos are sorted in a comprehensive way, and the release time is unlimited. We selected the top 100 videos because studies have shown that analyzing more than the top 100 videos does not significantly impact the results [15, 16]. We extracted the basic information, including the release date, uploader name, uploader type, video length, number of shares, comments, and likes for each video. All extracted data were recorded in an Excel spreadsheet.

Video classification

The video sources are classified as follows: (1) medical personnel, (2) science disseminators (people who spread disease knowledge), and (3)news media (such as online media, television stations, newspapers). The video content is classified as follows: (1) disease prevention, (2) disease diagnosis, (3) disease symptoms, (4) disease overview (overall introduction to osteoporosis), (4) life-style (preventing or treating osteoporosis through life-style), and (6) medication.

Quality assessment

One of the tools commonly used by patients and information providers to assess the quality of written information regarding treatment choices is DISCERN, which was released in 1998 [17]. It has been proven useful for evaluating video quality on video platforms such as YouTube [18]. Therefore, we use the DISCERN rating scale to evaluate the information quality of video content. The DISCERN includes the reliability of publications, the quality of treatment selection information, and the overall rating of publications. The reliability of publications includes 8 issues (1–8). The treatment selection information includes 7 questions (9–15). The overall rating of the publication includes 1 question (16). Each question is given a score of 1–5 points. The overall score ranges from 16 to 80, with 16–26 being very poor, 27–38 being poor, 39–50 being appropriate, 51–62 being good, and 63–80 being excellent [19, 20]. Two raters evaluate each video’s content, discuss and resolve areas of disagreement. For areas where agreement cannot be reached, the third rater will resolve the issue and provide the final score.

Statistical analysis

Meadians, means and SDs were used for descriptive statistics. The Kruskal-Wallis test was used to detect significant differences between two or more independent variables. Spearman analysis was used to evaluate the correlation between the two variables. Statistical analysis was conducted via GraphPad Prism 9.5.1 and SPSS software (version 27). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Video features

In the initial search of 100 videos by TikTok, we identified two main sources of videos on the basis of the identity of the uploader: individual users and organizational users. We identified two types of video creators among individual users: medical professionals and science communicators. Among organizational users, we identified one video source: news media. Moreover, we classify the main content of the video as follows: (1) disease prevention, (2) disease diagnosis, (3) disease symptoms, (4) disease overview, (5) life-style and (6) medication.

Quality assessment

By using DISCRON to evaluate the content of 100 collected video samples, we found that the overall quality of the video content was low (mean = 37.69). According to the video source, in the first evaluation section, we found that science communicators scored the highest (mean = 23.75) in terms of their video releases, followed by healthcare workers (mean = 22.78) and news media (mean = 22) in terms of their video releases. In the second evaluation, we found that the DISCERN score (mean = 13) for science communicators publishing videos was higher than the DISCERN score (mean = 12.47) for medical staff publishing videos and the DISCERN score (mean = 12.2) for news organizations publishing videos. Through the evaluation in the third section, we found that the DISCRON score of science communicators’ published videos (mean = 2.75) was higher than that of medical personnel’s published videos (mean = 2.43) and news organizations’ published videos (mean = 2.4). By calculating the total score, we found that the DISCRN score of science communicators publishing videos (mean = 39.5) was higher than that of medical staff publishing videos (mean = 37.68) and news communicators publishing videos (mean = 36.6) (Tables 2 and 3).

Discuss

Main findings

With the advent of the 5G era, an increasing number of people are willing to use the internet to obtain knowledge related to chronic diseases. Short videos quickly occupy the majority of the traffic market because they are short, concise, focused on content, and precise and are loved by all age groups. In this study, the first 100 videos of osteoporosis were retrieved and recorded on the TikTok platform. The total number of videos shared was 133,860, with an average of 1338.6. The total number of comments is 114,377, with an average of 1143.77. The total number of likes is 550,632, with an average of 5506.32. The number of collections is 52,768, with an average of 527.68. This indicates that TikTok is expected to become a platform for spreading health knowledge.

In the collected videos, we found that the videos mainly come from two sources: individual users and organizations. The organizations include doctors and science communicators, whereas the organizations include only news media. More than 90% of the videos are provided by doctors, whereas orthopedic doctors provide more than 60% of the videos, similar to previous related studies [14]. This findings indicate that Chinese health professionals are actively involved in promoting osteoporosis knowledge through TikTok.

By using the DISCERN rating table to rate the quality of video content, we found that the average DISCERN score of 100 video samples was 37.71 (SD = 6.77), with 54% of the video content quality falling within the poor evaluation range and 43% falling within the appropriate evaluation range. This result is similar to previous research results [12]. We also found that the quality of video content varies by source, with science communicators having higher DISCERN scores than do medical staff and news media. Therefore, we suggest that medical personnel and news media are expected to become more powerful contributors to high-quality videos, which can promote the development of public health.

Related studies have shown a positive correlation between video quality and video length [12, 15]. Our research revealed that video lengths range between 9 and 379 s, with most videos having lower-quality content and a few having higher-quality content. Mustafa Erkut Onder et al. collected videos related to osteoporosis on YouTube before April 21, 2021, and reported that nearly half of the videos on YouTube were of high quality, one-third were of medium quality, and a few were lower in length, with video lengths ranging from 2.54 to 16 min [21]. The short duration of videos makes it difficult for people to obtain high-quality information. Therefore, in the future, we call for more video creators, especially doctors, to appropriately extend video length, refine video content, and improve video quality.

To some extent, the number of shares, comments, likes, and favorites reflects the popularity of videos [22, 23]. Through Spearman analysis, we found a positive correlation between the number of shares, comments, likes, and favorites, indicating that the greater the number of shares, comments, likes, and favorites, the greater the level of popularity. At the same time, we find that the number of upload days is positively correlated with popularity, indicating that over time, it will be seen by an increasing number of people and will be loved by more and more people. However, it is worth noting that there is no correlation between video length and popularity or number of upload days, which is consistent with the research results of Fei Sun et al. [12].

We also analyzed the correlation between the DISCERN score and popularity and found that there is a positive correlation between the DISCERN score and the number of shares and favorites, indicating that the higher the number of shares and favorites is, the higher the DISCERN score and the higher the quality of video content.This research result is inconsistent with the research results related to Fei Sun, Mueller SM [12, 15]. This result indicates that with the popularization of the Internet and the improvements in education level, an increasing number of people have learned to distinguish between high-quality and low-quality videos.

Future and limitations

This study has certain limitations. First, we investigated only osteoporosis-related videos on TikTok in China and did not include osteoporosis-related videos from other countries, which may introduce bias. Therefore, in the future, we can evaluate the quality of osteoporosis-related video information in different countries to further understand the quality of osteoporosis-related video information on TikTok. Second, we use only the DISCERN to evaluate the quality of the collected video content. Although DISCERN can be effectively used for evaluating video quality on social media (such as YouTube), many rating tools can still be used to evaluate video quality, such as the JAMA benchmark and the HONcode principle. In the future, more tools can be used to evaluate video quality, fully demonstrating the effectiveness and high quality of videos. Third, although we counted the number of comments, we did not evaluate the quality of the comment content. The number of comments reflects the popularity of a video, and the quality of the comment content can also reflect the quality of the video content. Therefore, exploring the quality of comment content is one of our future research directions. Finally, we only collected and studied videos about osteoporosis on TikTok and did not analyze videos related to osteoporosis on other social media, such as Kwai and Bilibili. In the future, we can compare the video quality of osteoporosis with that of other social media platforms and explore whether the video quality is consistent across different social media platforms.

Conclusion

This study collected 100 videos from the TikTok platform and assessed their content quality via the DISCERN rating scale. The videos were provided mainly by medical staff, and people could understand disease-related knowledge roughly from videos; however, the overall video content quality was poor. Patients are advised to be selective and cautious when obtaining information on TikTok, and medical staff should aim to provide more high-quality videos through platforms such as TikTok.

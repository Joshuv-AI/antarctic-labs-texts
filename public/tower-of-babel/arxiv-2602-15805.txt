Abstract
In this article we consider a stationary N N -dimensional Galerkin-Navier-Stokes type evolution with Brownian forcing and random stirring (of arbitrarily small strength). We show that the stationary diffusion in an open two-dimensional cone constructed in a companion article, stands as the inviscid limit of the laws of the “enstrophy-energy” process of the N N -dimensional diffusion process considered here, this regardless of the strength of the stirring. With the help of the quantitative condensation bounds of the companion article, we infer quantitative inviscid condensation bounds, which for suitable forcings show an attrition of all but the lowest modes in the inviscid limit.

Introduction

While the existence and uniqueness theory for stationary distributions of the two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equation with Brownian forcing on a two-dimensional square or thin torus is by now a fairly mature subject, see for instance

[

[

[

For instance, the known lower bounds on the expectation of the energy are presently blind to the inviscid limit procedure, and the expected trend that under suitable forcings, in the inviscid limit, the stationary distributions condense on the low Fourier modes, is poorly understood, see
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
, (4.2) in
[

[

Gaussian measure has been instrumental in several aspects of related research. It has been a building block for invariant measures corresponding to very specific forcings and the construction of generalized solutions, see
[
6
]
, and
[
13
]
for more recent developments. Also, in a large dimension (and deviation) perspective, it has highlighted a condensation mechanism on low modes in the context of constrained enstrophy and energy regimes, see Section 3.1 of
[
3
]
. Here, and in the companion article
[
27
]
, Gaussian measure plays a leading role as well. We use it as a tool to devise the drift and diffusion matrix of a diffusion process in a two-dimensional cone. In the companion article
[
27

N

[
27
]
, we deduce an
inviscid condensation bound
, which for instance highlights a “transfer to low modes” in the case of suitable forcings.

2
The limit diffusion process

In this section we recall some of the main features of the stationary diffusion process constructed in
[
27
]
. It will govern the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit behavior of the law of
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
under
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}
, see Theorem
5.1
.

The state space of the limit diffusion process is the interior
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
of the cone

(2.1)

C=\{w=(u,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2};\;0\leq v\leq u\leq\lambda_{N}v\}\quad\mbox{(with $\lambda_{N}$ as in (\ref{1.2}))}.

To describe the diffusion operator on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, we first need to recall from
[
27
]
the functions
q_{\ell}
on
C
(they are “good versions” of the conditional expectations
E^{\mu}[x_{\ell}^{2}\,\big|\,|x|^{2}=u,|x|^{2}_{-1}=v]
, see also Remark
A.2
). Namely, see Section 2 of
[
27
]
, one has a collection of functions
q_{\ell},1\leq\ell\leq N
, on
C
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{each $q_{\ell}$ is non-negative, homogeneous of degree $1$, and Lipschitz on $C$},

(2.3)

\displaystyle q_{2i}=q_{2i-1},\;\mbox{for $1\leq i\leq n$},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{$u=\Sigma_{1}^{N}\,q_{\ell}(u,v)$ and $v=\Sigma^{N}_{1}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}\;q_{\ell}(u,v)$, for all $(u,v)\in C$},

(2.5)

each
q_{\ell}
is positive on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
and coincides on each open sector
\Delta_{i}=\{w=(u,v)\in C
;

\displaystyle 0<\mu_{i-1}\,v<u<\mu_{i}v\},1<i\leq n\;\mbox{(see (\ref{1.3}) for notation), with a rational function}

\displaystyle\mbox{in $u,v$}.

Further, the functions
q_{\ell}
have the boundary values

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mbox{$q_{\ell}(u,v)=0$ for $u=v$ or $u=\lambda_{N}v$, when $3\leq\ell\leq N-2$, and $u\geq 0$},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mbox{$q_{\ell}(u,u)=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{u}{2}$}$ and $q_{\ell}(u,u/\lambda_{N})=0$, when $\ell=1$ or $2$, and $u\geq 0$},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mbox{$q_{\ell}(u,u)=0$ and $q_{\ell}(u,u/\lambda_{N})=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{u}{2}$}$, when $\ell=N-2$ or $N$, and $u\geq 0$}.

The functions
q_{\ell}
do not depend on the parameter
a>0
in (
1.5
), see Proposition B.2 of
[
27
]
, and they are regular versions of the conditional expectations of
x^{2}_{\ell}
under
\mu
, given
|x|^{2}=u
and
|x|^{2}_{-1}=v
. Namely, one has

(2.9)

E^{\mu}\big[x_{\ell}^{2}\,\big|\,|x|^{2},|x|^{2}_{-1}\big]=q_{\ell}(|x|^{2},|x|^{2}_{-1}),\mu
-a.s., for
1\leq\ell\leq N

(the left member denotes the conditional expectation of
x_{\ell}^{2}
given
|x|^{2},|x|^{2}_{-1}
under
\mu
).

We also refer to Remark
A.2
for a strenghtened version of this identity. One then defines the differential operator
\widetilde{A}
on the open cone
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, formally obtained by replacing the terms
x^{2}_{\ell}
by
q_{\ell}
in (
1.22
):

(2.10)

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}\psi=&\;2a\Big\{\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{u}\psi+2\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{u,v}\psi+\Sigma_{\ell}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}\;(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{v}\psi\Big\}\\
&+\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\big)\,\partial_{u}\psi+\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\big)\,\partial_{v}\psi.\end{split}

As established in Section 3 of
[
27
]
,
\widetilde{A}
is an elliptic second order differential operator with Lipschitz coefficients in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
. Moreover, it gives rise to a diffusion process on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, through a well-posed martingale problem. Namely, letting
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
stand for the space of continuous
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
{\cal F}
for its canonical
\sigma
-algebra,
({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, for its canonical filtration, and
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
for its canonical process, one has

(2.11)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$\widetilde{P}_{w}(W_{0}=w)=1$ and for any smooth compactly supported function $\psi$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$},\\
\mbox{$\psi(W_{t})-\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{A}\psi(W_{s})\,ds,t\geq 0$ is an $({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}$-martingale under $\widetilde{P}_{w}$}.\end{array}

Further, see Section 4 of
[
27
]
, the family
\widetilde{P}_{w},w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, constitutes a strong Markov process on the state space
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, and see Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 4.3 of
[
27
]
,

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{there is a unique stationary distribution $\widetilde{\pi}$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$ for the above Markov process}\\
\mbox{and it is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$}.\end{array}

Moreover, if
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
are such that

(2.13)

\begin{array}[]{l}2(2\alpha+1)\;\max\limits_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell})<\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell})\;\;\mbox{and}\\
2(2\beta+1)\;\max\limits_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})<\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell}),\end{array}

then

(2.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}}\,(u-v)^{-\alpha}+(\lambda_{N}v-u)^{-\beta}+e^{\frac{v}{2a}}d\,\widetilde{\pi}(w)<\infty.

The law of the stationary diffusion process will play an important role in Section 5. One thus defines on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
the probability

(2.15)

\mbox{$\widetilde{P}=\displaystyle\int\widetilde{\pi}(dw)\,\widetilde{P}_{w}$ (and one writes $\widetilde{E}$ for the corresponding expectation)}.

By Proposition 4.2. of
[
27
]
one has the following characterization of
\widetilde{P}
:

(2.16)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$\widetilde{P}$ is the only probability $Q$ on $C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})$ such that $Q$ is stationary and}\\[2.15277pt]
\mbox{for any smooth compactly supported function $\psi$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$, under $Q$},\\
\mbox{$\psi(W_{t})-\psi(W_{0})-\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{A}\psi(W_{s})\,ds,t\geq 0$, is an $({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}$-martingale}.\end{array}

Finally, we recall the condensation bound. One first defines

(2.17)

B_{0}=a\,\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\;\mbox{and}\;B_{1}=a\,\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell}),

so that
B_{0}<B_{1}
. One knows from Proposition 4.4. of
[
27
]
that

(2.18)

B_{0}=2\widetilde{E}[U_{0}]\;\mbox{and}\;B_{1}=2\widetilde{E}[\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})].

The
condensation bound
, (see Theorem 5.1 of
[
27
]
), states that

(2.19)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{for any $\ell_{0}$ in $\{3,\dots,N\}$, writing $\ell_{0}=2i_{0}$ or $\ell_{0}=2i_{0}-1$, with $2\leq i_{0}\leq n$,}\\[2.15277pt]
\mbox{(depending on whether $\ell_{0}$ is even or odd), one has}\\[2.15277pt]
2\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]\leq\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{B_{1}-B_{0}}{\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{3}-1}$}\;\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq k<i_{0}-1}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-k}$}\;B_{0}\leq\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{B_{1}-B_{0}}{\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{3}-1}$}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{0}}{N-\ell_{0}}$}\;B_{0}\end{array}

(the sum in the middle member is omitted when

).

The proof of (

of the

4
Tightness

In this short section we prove the tightness of the laws of the two-dimensional process
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in (
1.30
) under the stationary laws
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0
, see (
1.29
). In essence, this process corresponds to the time evolution of the “enstrophy and energy” for the stationary diffusions with generator
\widetilde{{\cal L}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}
, see (
1.21
). We keep the same convention concerning constants as stated at the beginning of Section 3.

We recall (
2.1
) for the definition of the closed cone
C
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We endow the space
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},C)
of continuous
C
-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact intervals, and with its usual Borel
\sigma
-algebra, which coincides with the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the canonical coordinates (see for instance Chapter 16 of
[

]
). We denote by

(4.1)

\mbox{$\widetilde{P}^{\varepsilon}$ the law of $(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}$ on $C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},C)$ under $\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}$, for $\varepsilon>0$}.

The main object of this section is the proof of

Theorem 4.1
.

(4.2)

\mbox{The laws $\widetilde{P}^{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0$, are tight}.

Proof.

We first note that
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},C)
sits as a closed subspace of
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on compact intervals,
and it thus suffices to show the tightness of the laws of
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}=((U_{t},V_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
as a continuous
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
-valued process under
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0
. The first identity in (
3.4
) and the inequality
U_{0}\geq V_{0}\geq 0
show that

(4.3)

\mbox{the laws of $W_{0}=(U_{0},V_{0})$ on ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}$ under $\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0$, are tight}.

To complete the proof of (
4.2
) we will show that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\varepsilon>0,s\geq 0}\;\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[|U_{t+s}-U_{s}|^{4}]\leq c\,t^{2},\;\mbox{for $0\leq t\leq 1$},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\varepsilon>0,s\geq 0}\;\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[|U_{t+s}-V_{s}|^{4}]\leq c\,t^{2},\;\mbox{for $0\leq t\leq 1$},

and the claim will come from the application of the Kolmogorov-Chentsov Theorem, see
[
16
]
, p. 313.

We first prove (
4.4
). By stationarity we can assume that
s=0
. We apply (
1.39
), (
1.40
) (see also below (
1.40
)), to the case
\psi(u,v)=u
and find that with the notation (
3.1
), (
3.3
), under
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0
,

(4.6)

\mbox{$M_{t}=U_{t}-\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}B_{1}-2T_{s}\,ds,t\geq 0$, is a continuous $({\cal G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}$-martingale},

with bracket process

(4.7)

\langle M\rangle_{t}=4a\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,X^{2}_{s,\ell}\,ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{1.8})}}{{\leq}}4a\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}T_{s}\,ds,\;\mbox{for $t\geq 0$}.

We then find that for
t>0

(4.8)

\begin{split}(U_{t}-U_{0})^{4}&\leq c\,\Big(\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}B_{1}-2T_{s}\,ds\Big)^{4}+c(M_{t}-M_{0})^{4}\\
&\leq c\,t^{3}\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(B_{1}-2T_{s})^{4}\,ds+c(M_{t}-M_{0})^{4}.\end{split}

By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequalities, see
[
16
]
, p. 333, one has

(4.9)

\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}(M_{s}-M_{0})^{4}]\leq c\,\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[\langle M\rangle^{2}_{t}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{4.7})}}{{\leq}}c\,t^{2}\,\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[T_{0}^{2}].

Hence, we find that for
0\leq t\leq 1
,

(4.10)

\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}(U_{s}-U_{0})^{4}]\leq c\,t^{4}\,\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[(1+T_{0})^{4}]+ct^{2}\,\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}\,\!^{\varepsilon}[T_{0}^{2}]\leq c\,t^{2},

using
T_{0}\leq c\,U_{0}
(recall that
\lambda_{N}
is a constant by the convention stated at the beginning of Section 3) and (
3.6
) in the last step.

The inequality (
4.5

4.1
.
∎

C^{u,v}_{2}
and departure from
O^{u,v}
(see (
B.19
) for notation). Through the consideration of the last return to
C^{u,v}_{2}
before time
1
, one has (see (
B.20
) for notation)

(B.26)

p_{1}(x,y)=\Sigma_{n\geq 0}\,E_{x}[R_{n}<1,p_{1-R_{n}}^{O^{u,v}}(X_{R_{n}},y)].

(The identity is first proved in integrated form with a “bump function” supported near
y
. The argument below can be used to show that the right member of (
B.26
) is a continuous function of
y
and (
B.26
) follows by letting the bump function concentrate at
y
.)

Note that for
n\geq 1
,
X_{R_{n}}
belongs to
\partial C_{2}^{u,v}
and that
X_{R_{0}}
either equals
x\in C_{2}^{u,v}
or belongs to
\partial C_{2}^{u,v}
. As a result of (
B.26
), we thus find that

(B.27)

\begin{split}p_{1}(x,y)&\leq E_{x}[\Sigma_{n\geq 0}\,1\{R_{n}<1\}]\,\max\{p_{1}^{O^{u,v}}(x,y),\sup\limits_{0<s\leq 1,x^{\prime}\in\partial C_{2}^{u,v}}p_{s}^{O^{u,v}}(x^{\prime},y)\}\\[-8.61108pt]
&\hskip-8.61108pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(\ref{B.22})}}{{\leq}}c^{\prime}\,E_{x}[\Sigma_{n\geq 0}\,1\{R_{n}<1\}].\end{split}

Bounding the drift and diffusion matrix of the diffusion with generator
\Gamma
, one can choose a positive integer
L_{0}(u_{\min},u_{\max},\eta)
(we refer to the beginning of the appendix and above (
B.19
) for our convention about constants) such that uniformly in
z\in C_{2}^{u,v}
the probability for
X
starting at
z
to remain in
O^{u,v}
up to time
1/L_{0}
is at least
1/2
. It then follows that for any
z\in C^{u,v}_{2}
, using the strong Markov property at the first return to
C^{u,v}_{2}
after time
\frac{k}{L_{0}}
in the second line, one has

(B.28)

\begin{array}[]{l}E_{z}[\Sigma_{n\geq 0}\,1\{R_{n}<1\}]=\sum\limits_{0\leq k<L_{0}}E_{z}\Big[\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}1\Big\{\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{k}{L_{0}}$}\leq R_{n}<\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{k+1}{L_{0}}$}\Big\}\Big]\leq\\[8.61108pt]
L_{0}\sup\limits_{z^{\prime}\in C_{2}^{u,v}}E_{z^{\prime}}\Big[\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}1\Big\{R_{n}<\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{L_{0}}$}\Big\}\Big]\leq L_{0}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}$}=2L_{0}.\end{array}

Bounding the last expectation of (
B.27
) by
2L_{0}
thus concludes the proof of (
B.17
), and hence of Proposition
B.2
.
∎

B.2
will be especially useful in Section 5, see below (
5.25
) in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

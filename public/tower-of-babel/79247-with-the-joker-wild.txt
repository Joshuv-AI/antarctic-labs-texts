 WITH THE JOKER WILD

 by W. C. Tuttle
 Author of “Hair-Trigger Hollibaugh,” “Mary’s Little Lamb,” etc.


This ain’t no poker story. I ain’t going to try and cold-deck anybody
into thinking it is, so I’ll orate right now that she ain’t. I aims to
prove uh little moral, and yuh can’t prove no morals in uh poker story,
’cause there ain’t no morals in that game.

All I wants to do is to cinch the fact that honesty is the best policy,
and that wisdom ain’t uh better hand than luck. I played in uh poker
game once, where the deuces run wild. It wasn’t poker—it was suicide.
Over on the coast they sometimes plays with the joker wild. They opens
the game with uh silent prayer and deals five cards apiece. Before they
discards they wires for the police, coroner, and notifies their
families. Then they spends the interim—that word seems to fit exactly,
’cause it looks like interment—trying to find out where the joker fits
into uh straight.

Honesty is the best policy. The feller who first uttered them words said
more in five movements of his vocal cords than the person did in eight,
when “A fool and his money are soon parted” was coined. Magpie Simpkins
was uh wise man. I say, “was wise,” because after he got wise to himself
he found that he was uh fool—but he’ll never admit it.

Magpie is six feet several inches of wisdom and deduction and so forth
and so on. He’s tried every course from hair-oil to hypnotism to make
himself admired of everybody, but he still wiggles along, sluicing
gravel, herding uh burro and predicting that he’s got Solomon looking
half-witted.

Me? I’m Ike Harper, Magpie’s pardner. I wished him on to me one time
over in Helena, when he was suffering with uh rash, brought on by trying
to convince uh wildcat that the human eye is stronger than four feet
full uh claws. Me and that animated fish-pole has seen ups and downs
ever since.

We’re living in plenty and more or less peace, when we holds uh meeting
or two, together, and decides that there must be gold on Whispering
Crick. Whispering Crick has much in common with all other cricks. It
rises at its head and flows into uh river. It carries enough water for
uh good sluice all the year around, which makes it uh pleasant place to
prospect, even if there ain’t no gold.

Me and Magpie pilgrims to Whispering Crick, which is about eighty miles
from Piperock, where we live, and in another county.

Magpie always expounded the theory that gold is where yuh find it, and
that’s where we found it. Right where the pay streak couldn’t possibly
be we found it, and then Magpie sets down and reads me uh lengthy sermon
on hunches and wisdom.

Did yuh ever see more than enough gold? That’s how the streak looks to
me and Magpie. We strips uh piece uh bedrock, and carries it down to the
crick in sacks, and I pans out uh pirate’s hoard, while Magpie pilgrims
to town to file the location of the “Golden Hunch.”

We locates the only place on the crick where uh piece uh gold would have
uh chance to stop. We got forty acres, with about two hundred feet in
the narrowest place, and from that on down, and above, the cañon is so
narrow uh goat couldn’t find uh footing. There’s just one difficulty:
the crick is lower than the rich ground, and too far away.

Me and Magpie parades around the place like uh pair uh bankers, and then
decides that we got to build uh dam, and to build uh dam we got to
import uh sawmill. Also we got to hire uh crew uh men, cause we’re too
rich to labor.

As Magpie says—

“Why get strong in the muscle and weak in the head?”

We hires uh bunch uh poor men to swamp out uh road for about twenty
miles, to the county road, and then Magpie goes out to dicker for uh
small mill.

This is the first time that me and Magpie has owned anything better than
fifty cents uh pan—every fifty or sixty pans—so we feels like uh bronc
in his first bunch-grass.

One afternoon, after we gets our sawmill to running, and has uh force uh
men on the dam, uh prospector name uh “Hailstorm” Wilson, drifts in.
Hailstorm was born to sorrow. Life to him is just one “can’t” after
another, and he ain’t happy unless he brings bad news. Me and Magpie
makes him welcome as the flowers in May, and sets down to show him, in
figures, just how rich we are.

“Going to build uh big dam, eh?” remarks Hailstorm, sad-like. “Where yuh
going to get your timber?”

“Right here on the ground,” says Magpie, taking in uh sweep of the
country with his two hands. “We got enough right here to build several
dams.”

“Uh-huh,” agrees Hailstorm, nodding his head slowly. “There sure is,
but——”

“But what?” I asks.

“Forest reserve. I reckon, according to what I can find out, you’re
entitled to firewood and enough to build uh shack, but when it comes to
putting in uh sawmill—well, it can’t be done—legally.”

“Can’t, eh?” yelps Magpie. “Who’s to stop me, eh?”

“Government, Magpie. Suppose uh supervisor comes up here and
says—‘Magpie Simpkins and Ike Harper, you’re under arrest for cutting
more timber than the law allows.’ What yuh going to do then, eh? What
good is uh lot uh gold going to do yuh while yuh languish in durance
vile?”

“Well,” grins Magpie, “when uh feller’s got uh lot uh yaller gold he
looks good to most everybody, Hailstorm. When I shows that supervisor
what uh good feller I can be—well——”

Hailstorm nods and spits at uh lizard:

“Uh-huh. Maybe yuh could if yuh made the price right, Magpie. Yuh can’t
pike in uh case like that.”

“Not me,” boasts Magpie, wise-like. “I got too many brains to be uh
piker in uh case like that. When it comes to handling delicate
situations I’m there like uh coyote at uh carcass—me.”

Hailstorm and his burro pilgrims off into the hills, and we keeps right
on sawing timber. I don’t feel safe, and I tells Magpie.

“Gosh a’mighty, Ike!” he snaps. “Quit worrying. We ain’t prospectors no
more—we’re financiers. Sabe? As soon as this dam is finished we’ll take
out uh million. You quit worrying, and let me handle the brainy end uh
this deal. Maybe they won’t never show up, but if they does—you watch
Magpie Simpkins.”

“Yes’m,” says I. “I’ll watch yuh all right, old-timer. But what good is
gold to me if Uncle Sam is sending meals to me by uh warden? I don’t
know what the penalty is, but it’ll be uh plenty.”

Magpie don’t absorb the gloom uh tall, but just sets there and smokes
cigarets by the yard and grins under his mustache.

“Ike,” says he, “when we milks this here Whispering Crick dry I’m going
to take uh trip to Boston and buy uh set uh books I seen advertised in
uh paper. What are yuh going to do with your money?”

“Me?” I asks. “Well, I reckon I’ll take uh trip over to Maine, and buy
uh canoe I seen advertised once.”

“Canoe?” he asks. “What in —— will you do with uh canoe, Ike?”

“I’ll bring it up here, Magpie. I’ll stick her in the water, just above
that white whirl down there, and when you starts reading out loud to me
from one uh them books, I’ll cut the rope and jump in. Maybe I’ll eat uh
few dynamite-caps so as to make it uh cinch.”

 * * * * *

For the next few weeks we’re some busy. We got uh force uh men on the
dam, one on the sluice and another in the mill. As soon as the dam is
finished we starts to cleaning uh little bed-rock. We’ve taken out
enough to make the golden calf they tells about in the Bible look like
an 1876 penny, when this here Hailstorm Wilson pilgrims in once more,
and looks us up in our treasury.

“I’m uh friend in need,” he states, nodding sad-like, when we welcomes
him in.

“How much do yuh need, Hailstorm?” asks Magpie.

Hailstorm peers off down the road, wipes away uh stray tear, and
whispers—

“Supervisor!”

Magpie hitches up his belt and pats himself on the chest.

“Fear not,” says he. “Leave him to me, and all will be well.”

“Two of ’em,” states Hailstorm. “District and State.”

“Two is company,” grins Magpie. “Two is easier to hornswoggle than one.”

“Seen ’em down to Devil’s Turn,” orates Hailstorm. “Uh course it ain’t
no funerial uh mine, but I goes plumb out uh my way to inform yuh,
Magpie. Things like that grieves me uh heap.”

Magpie separates our treasury from several goodly lumps uh gold as uh
present to Hailstorm, and said Hailstorm goes on his way rejoicing. I
chides Magpie for his generosity, and then has to listen for an hour to
uh sermon on thankfulness.

“What do yuh reckon I ought to do—foller him up and kiss him?” I asks,
when Magpie ceases. “You don’t need to chide me, Magpie. Being uh
bringer uh bad news is reward enough for Hailstorm. We’re in uh lovely
fix, if yuh asks Ike Harper. You’re going to bust your pick when you
tries to bribe officials, Magpie.”

“Am, am I?” he snaps. “You watch me, Ike. Every man has his price.”

“Well,” says I, “if you’re buying things today, Mister Simpkins, yuh
might make me an offer for half uh the Golden Hunch before them
supervisors shows up. I’m having uh sale today—me.”

“Ike,” Magpie puts uh quart uh tears in his voice, and lays his hand on
my shoulder. “Ike, have no fear. Remember you got uh resourceful pardner
in me. You keep in the background, and don’t have nothing to say. Be
sort of uh silent pardner. Sabe? Just leave it all to little Magpie, and
I’ll make yuh proud to know me.”

“I’ll keep in the background, all right, Magpie. Silent? Say, old-timer,
any time I’m in the shadow of the law I’m so silent that you could hear
uh clam, under sixteen feet uh Missouri mud, calling to its mate.”

Long in the afternoon uh livery rig drives in from Devil’s Turn and
unloads uh couple uh men, and then leaves. Magpie don’t waste no time in
getting acquainted and extending uh welcome. One is uh tall, dark hombre
and the other is shorter and runs uh little to gray on top. They both
wears store clothes and boiled collars, and they each got uh valise,
which Magpie packs up to our wickiup and places under our bunks.

“We’re mighty glad to see yuh,” orates Magpie, bowing each of ’em to uh
chair, the same uh which makes me set on the steps.

“Quite some camp yuh got here,” remarks the short feller. “Must be money
here, eh?”

“Uh-huh,” agrees Magpie. “Yuh might say there is. Would you like to look
over the works?”

“I wouldn’t—” I starts to cut in, but Magpie shuts me right up.

“Ike, you better get back on the job,” he states. “I got company to
entertain, and you got work to do. I can’t pay my hired help for
loafing. Sabe?”

That makes me so mad that I goes down and sets on the dam, and wishes I
had that canoe right now. After a while I sees Magpie and them two
supervisors emerge from the cabin and wander down to where some men are
cleaning some bed-rock.

They each got uh gold pan, and after they had been down there for uh
while I wanders down. Them two hombres are some enthusiastic, believe
me, and they’re panning dirt like they was doing it for uh living.

“Look!” yelps the tall feller, poking around in his pan. I sees Magpie
wink at Swede Carlson, who is bossing the gang, and the Swede grins.

“Let me get yuh uh pan uh dirt,” says Magpie. Him and Swede takes that
pan and fills it with jewelry. When they’re cleaning bed-rock they finds
nuggets in the deep crevices, which they digs out and stacks in little
heaps.

I reckon that feller’s pan would run about fifty dollars, and he waits
with us. Then Magpie and Swede does the same to the other feller’s pan.
It’s just like letting uh man dig down into your pockets on pay-day. It
ain’t no hard job to swipe uh hundred or two in uh case like that, but
it ain’t my place to act as lookout in uh game like that. I estimates
how much ham and aigs I could buy with what we’ll lose, and the figures
brings tears to my eyes.

Don’t get the idea that Ike Harper is stingy. He ain’t. When they starts
out on more pans of the same stuff my eyes gets so full uh tears that I
can’t see, so I goes out into the timber and weeps alone. After the
irrigation season is over I goes back to the cabin, and finds them in
there weighing the gold.

“Yours weighs twenty ounces,” announces Magpie to the tall Jasper. “And
the other gentleman gets twenty-three.”

Magpie talks like uh referee at uh raffle, and I wonders where I laid my
gun.

“Wonderful ground!” exclaims the oldest one. “Isn’t it great to pan out
real money like that! The feel of it makes me wish it all belonged to
me.”

“It does,” states Magpie. “Do yuh think I took yuh down there to pan out
gold for me? Not any. You fellers can split them forty-three ounces as
uh present from the Golden Hunch. Sabe?”

“What’s the main idea?” asks the tall one, looking at Magpie in uh queer
way.

“Listen; did yuh ever hear of the virtue of blindness?”

“Uh-huh,” agrees the tall one, with uh slow grin. “I have, but not in
just that way.”

“Well,” says Magpie, “uh man’s duty is sacred, but he can always go
deaf, dumb and blind and not hurt nobody. Sabe? Sometimes it pays to be
near-sighted.”

Them two supervisors looks at each other for uh few seconds and then at
the gold, and they both nods. The tall one gets up and stretches his
frame.

“Under them circumstances we sabe your proposition,” says he. “I’d like
to have uh little talk with my friend, so if yuh don’t mind we’ll take
uh little walk over the works.”

“She’s yours,” says Magpie. “Go right ahead and take your own time. If
yuh see anything you want, take it. The keys of the Golden Hunch are
yours.”

We sees ’em wander right up to our dam, and set down on uh pile uh new
lumber.

 * * * * *

“Well,” says I, “they’ve found it, Magpie. They got about eight hundred
of our money and you ain’t even got uh receipt. Why didn’t yuh wait
until they puts uh price on themselves?”

“Ike, you’re uh silent pardner in this. Sabe? We ain’t going to go to no
jail. You don’t sabe political finance, Ike, and therefore you’re uh
step behind the progress uh civilization. I knows how to handle ’em.
Them fellers has got to figure out how much it’s worth to them.”

“What are they going to do? Give us back some change?” I asks.

“Eight hundred ain’t much in uh case like this, Ike.”

“Ain’t, eh?” I retorts. “Before we locates this mine, Magpie, eight
dollars would have seemed uh lot if the case was first-degree murder.
You ain’t going to give ’em more, are yuh?”

“Ike, yuh can’t be uh piker in uh case like this.”

“Santa Claus,” says I, “you can give ’em your immortal soul and honor,
but I’ll be danged if you gives ’em any more uh that jewelry gravel—not
while Ike Harper lives.”

I goes down and sets on the bank the rest of the day, but they don’t
show up again.

I’m so mad that I don’t eat with them at supper. Later on I goes up to
the shack, and finds Magpie on the steps smoking uh cigaret.

“Well, Andrew Rockefeller, you got rid of all your filthy money?” I
asks. “I just happens to think that them Boston books will be fine to
stock some uh them libraries you’re giving away today.”

“Go take uh ride in your little canoe, Ike,” he grins. “Things is coming
our way, just like I said. They may leave tomorrow.”

“Yes?” says I. “I’d leave, too, if I had eight hundred dollars. What
right they got to all that money, Magpie?”

“Ike, yuh got to pay for them things. Them two supervisors comes up here
to wish us into the pen. They comes with malice in their hearts, and I
meets ’em like they was potentates.

“They sizes up things, and from the way I treats ’em they begins to
think: ‘Well, this here Simpkins person ain’t no bad hombre. He’s
big-hearted and open-handed. He’s real folks. He treats us like we was
royalty, and we ain’t got the heart to yell about uh few feet uh timber.
Uh course we got to be paid expenses for the trip.’

“Now, ain’t that deduction, Ike?”

“Yes,” says I. “Your reasoning is as wise as the wau-wau of an owl,
which consists uh hoots. You sure treats ’em royal, Magpie. I don’t know
what it costs to travel, but two broncs and uh buckboard over forty
miles uh passable road is well paid at eight hundred dollars. Dog-gone
it, Magpie, I’d inch that distance on my back for that money.”

Magpie smokes away on his cigaret for uh while and then opines:

“You’d lose money and skin, Ike. You couldn’t average more than two
inches at uh wiggle, could yuh? Well, you never could wiggle the last
ten miles. Now thirty miles would mean one million, nine hundred
thousand, eight hundred inches, which would mean nine hundred and fifty
thousand, four hundred wiggles. Figuring an average of ten wiggles to
the minute—yuh can’t figure more than that on account of meals and so
forth, Ike, you——”

“Magpie,” says I, “you said that every man had uh price. What’s yours?”

“Huh! What yuh want to know for?”

“I wants to see if I got enough to buy you, Magpie. If I got enough I’ll
take yuh, old-timer, cause the law gives uh man the right to kill
anything he buys.”

Just then them two supervisors shows up, and sets down with us.

“We must find uh place to sleep tonight,” states the tall one, and
Magpie immediate and soon gets generous again.

“Take our beds,” says he. “Right inside is two fine bunks, and I’d deem
it uh favor to the Golden Hunch if you’d accept. You’d have the
seclusion that should be accorded to honored guests.”

“Pshaw!” exclaims the tall one. “We don’t like to deprive you gents of
your beds thataway.”

“No depredation uh tall,” says Magpie. “You fellers go right in and go
to bed. I’ll tell the cook to have uh good breakfast for yuh. Don’t
hurry, ’cause he works all day.”

“Magpie,” says I, after they shuts the door. “Do you know what _mesika
hyas pelton_ means?”

“No,” says he.

“Well, in the Chinook jargon it means that you’re uh danged fool. The
word _hyas_ may be used for any adjective yuh feels like. If yuh can
think of anything real nasty and expressive yuh can tack it on in place
of the word danged that I used. I’d love you, Magpie—if you was dead!
You gives up our good beds to them gol-blasted officials—dang your
string-bean carcass to thunder!”

“We’ll sleep in the bunk-house, Ike. There’s an empty bunk.”

“Yes!” I snorts. “We’ll sleep in the bunk-house and get lousy. Aw, don’t
tell me that there ain’t no itch-bugs in there, Magpie. I seen them six
hombres from Cinnamon Crick scratching each other like uh bunch uh
monkeys. I hope you gets ’em in your mustache and has to shave it off.”

“Don’t get sore, Ike,” says he, soothing like. “After tomorrow these
forest officials will be gone, and life will be one pleasure after
another.”

“One grayback after another,” says I. “Some day, Magpie, I’ll trust your
judgment too far, and have to kill you to protect my life. I hope them
fellers don’t get to monkeying with my new pearl-handled six-gun. It’s
on my bunk, and she’s mighty easy on the trigger.”

“How much did that gun cost yuh, Ike?” he asks.

“I didn’t buy it. Weinie Lopp gave it to me for my birthday. It’s the
only birthday present I ever had, and I thinks uh heap of it.”

“Pshaw! That’s too bad, Ike.”

“What’s too bad?” I asks.

“Well, yuh see it was laying around there, and this tall supervisor seen
it, and went plumb crazy over the hang of the barrel. Well, I——”

“You gave it to him, Magpie?”

It ain’t often that I gets mad. Paw always told me to count to uh
hundred when I felt my gorge arising.

Twice I felt her rise, and got licked before I counted to fifteen, but
this time I forgets paw’s teachings, and me and Magpie goes into the
dirt.

We does uh double flip-flop off the steps, and out comes this tall
pistol coveter to try and act as peacemaker.

Uh Canuck, from over in the Bitter Roots, showed me one day how to kick
from uh reclining position, and when that lanky person opines to break
my clinch I drove my boot against the waistband of his pants in front so
hard that it scraped the label off the tobacco sack in his hip-pocket.

I’m riled. I bounces Magpie’s head on the ground uh couple uh times,
ties his mustache behind his ears, and pilgrims right into that shack to
get my gun. I has to step right over the one I kicked. He looks up at
me, sort uh vacant-like, and mumbles something about the Milky Way, but
I don’t sabe astronomy, so I don’t argue with him over it.

I goes down to the bunk-house and crawls into that vacant bunk, and in
about an hour Magpie shows up. He bears testimony to the fact that the
Harper tribe is rowdyish at times, but he don’t say much.

“Some folks is born without tact,” says Magpie, sort uh melancholy,
after uh while. “Now, that gun never cost uh cent over thirty-five
dollars, and I has to pay that person about ten times that much to keep
him in good humor. It was like taking candy from an urchin before, but
when uh bull gets into uh chiny shop it sure costs uh lot for repairs,
if yuh asks me.”

“Magpie, did you pay him for that kick I landed?” I asks.

“The almighty dollar cureth all ills,” says he. “Enough of it is uh balm
for aching feelings.”

“I’ll take uh million,” says I. “I’ve got ills and aching feelings. If
them two Jaspers don’t pack their little satchels and pilgrim tomorrow
I’m going down and give myself up. I feels suspicious. They ain’t said
what their price is yet, have they, Magpie?”

“Well—not exactly.”

“What do yuh mean?”

“Well, Ike, I showed ’em that bunch of twenty-two hundred in selected
nuggets, and the tall one grins uh heap, and says—‘Uh man could forget
honor and duty to own uh bunch like that,’ and the other feller nods uh
heap. Now, if that ain’t uh tip, what is?”

“And you would give them that bunch, Magpie?” I asks.

“Ike, you can’t—simply can’t seem to understand the gravity of this
business. Why, uh feller can’t be small in uh case like this one. You
almost made that feller sore at us when you kicked him.”

“Gosh, he must be touchy,” says I. “Some folks carries their feelings on
their sleeves thataway. I got uh good notion to go up there and kick ’em
both out and take our own bed. It’s pleasant to know that you’re bedded
down with uh crazy man and uh blanket full uh bugs. I always knowed that
you was crazy, Magpie, but I never thought you’d be crazy enough to give
away uh thousand. They ain’t never introduced themselves nor made any
threats. Seems to me that you’re buying something you don’t have to.”

“Names don’t count in uh transaction like this. The less said the
better. Sabe? We’re in just as bad as they are if anybody should happen
to ask yuh, Ike. Bribery is uh crime.”

“It is,” I agrees. “It should be abolished. It should be abolished or
confined to your own home. It is as criminal to give as to receive, and
I’m in favor uh hanging all three of yuh, Magpie.”

The next morning we sure got up early. I was right when I said that them
fellers wasn’t scratching for fun. Me and Magpie itched ’fore breakfast
and itched after breakfast. I didn’t see them supervisors at breakfast,
so, between itches, I asks Magpie where they are.

“I—er—sent their breakfast up to the shack. They’re likely tired out,
and in uh case like this yuh got to be considerate, Ike.”

“I suppose yuh showed ’em where the hooch bottle was, too, didn’t yuh?”

“My gosh, Ike!” he snaps. “Ain’t you got no hospitality in your system?
You makes me tired. Here I been tasking my brains to get us out of uh
mess, and all you do is to act as an anchor.”

What can yuh do with uh hombre like that? I goes out and sets on uh
stump and offers up thanksgiving that I was born without any brains.
Magpie goes whistling and scratching up to our shack, and after uh while
he sticks out his head and yells for me to come up. I spins the cylinder
of my gun, and enters the place.

“Ike,” says he, “I wants yuh to witness uh bill uh sale.”

“Bill uh sale?” I asks. “What yuh selling?”

“One pack outfit, consisting of four burros, saddles, pack-sacks, ropes
and so forth and so forth.”

“So? Who’s buying said outfit?”

“These gentlemen,” says he, indicating the two supervisors.

“Yuh see,” he explains, “They desires to penetrate the interior, and
there ain’t nothing better than uh burro when it comes to uh pilgrimage.
Being as I don’t need said animiles I’m turning same over to said
gentlemen. Sabe?”

“Mighty kind and thoughtful uh you, I’m sure,” states the older one.
“You sure are uh white man, and I hopes yuh do well.”

“He will,” says I, signing the bill uh sale.

I figures that Magpie held ’em up for uh good price on them mules, and
we don’t need ’em much.

“Now,” says I, “if there’s anything more I can do before I loses my
mind, Magpie, stack me up against it.”

“Thanks, Mister Harper,” says Magpie. “Everything is satisfactory. I’ll
get you gents uh bunch uh grub out of the storehouse pretty soon and
then you’ll be in shape to travel.”

Them two hombres goes out with Magpie, so I unearths the hooch bottle.
There ain’t enough left to moisten uh wood-tick’s right front leg. Them
forest officials sure are intemperate.

Magpie comes in after uh while, and sprawls on the bunk. He’s got uh
satisfied look on his face, and he pats himself on the chest.

“Ike,” says he, “this sure is one brainy task. Nothing to fear from now
on. We can strip this here Whispering Crick bare and cut all the timber
we need, ’cause these supervisors ain’t going to bother us none in the
future. They got theirs.”

“And mine and yours,” says I. “You ain’t got no sense, Magpie. There’s
just as much difference between brains and sense as there is between
generosity and insanity. You think you got any real brains, Magpie?
You’re wrong if yuh do.”

“Well,” says he, twisting his long mustache, “I sure handled uh delicate
situation in uh way that would appear that I got uh little gray matter
under my hat.”

“Did you give them that bunch uh nuggets?” I yelps, when I sees that
empty pan on the table. “My ——! Magpie, you didn’t—”

“I did,” says he. “Freedom from worry is worth it, Ike. We’ll get uh lot
more.”

“Well, of all the easily bled, insane—say, Magpie, how much did yuh get
for them burros and grub?”

“I threw that in, Ike.”

 * * * * *

I don’t say uh word for some time. I’m all choked up with emotion over
it. I tell yuh it’s great to have uh pardner who has uh heart like that.
Think of the things uh man like that can do for yuh—and to yuh. After uh
while I picks my old war-sack from behind my bunk, and begins to stuff
it with old socks and so forth. And all this time I’m counting:
“eighty-eight, eighty-nine, ninety——”

“You got that many pairs uh socks?” asks Magpie, and I realizes that I
been counting out loud.

So as not to make any mistake, I starts in at fifty again. I don’t want
to disregard my old paw’s advice, and I sure can feel that gorge rising.

“What yuh doing now, Ike? Counting thataway.”

“Counting graybacks, you long, limber-jointed horn-uh-plenty!” I snaps,
and keeps on filling the sack.

“What yuh aiming to do, Ike?” he asks.

“Me?” I stops work, and looks him square in the eyes. “Me? I’m going
away—me! I don’t hanker to have riches, Mister Simpkins. I’m an ordinary
old pelican, and I don’t appreciate brains. I’m going to drag the prize
nut out of the first lunatic asylum I comes to. Sabe? What am I going to
do that for? Well, I’m going to form uh pardnership with said nut, go
away out on the desert where there never was nor never will be any gold,
and starve to death with him. He ain’t got no brains, and if I talks
real nice to him he’ll let me die in peace.”

“Aw, yuh can’t do that, Ike,” he protests. “Don’t get sore at me ’cause
you ain’t got no sabe for finance. You and me is in shape to take out uh
fortune right now. Ike, old-timer, we’ve seen uh lot of ups and downs
together—mostly downs, so let’s stick together and see if they won’t be
all ups now.”

“Like uh little balloon, Magpie,” says I. “Just like uh balloon.

“After we gets up just so high you has to puncture the bag with your
generosity and—brains, and down we comes. You sure are one cheerful
little giver. Give ’em all the gold we got, give ’em mules to carry it
away with and grub to eat on the way. Why, Magpie, you ain’t even honest
in your gifts. It was plain bribery.”

“You don’t need to yell it from the tops,” he reminds me. “You’re part
owner—remember that, Ike? I’m going down to see that they gets away in
good shape.”

After he’s gone uh while, somebody knocks on the door. I walks over and
admits Swede. He looks as solemn as an undertaker and wads up his dirty
hat in his hands.

“Aye yust come up to tell you maybe dot paystreak ain’t bane vid us no
more,” he states.

“What?” I yelps. “What did you say?”

“Val, aye ain’t sure. You see, ve strip oll dot bed-rock above und below
where ve get der rich streak, but she don’t go much better as two-bits
uh pan. Aye tank maybe she’s uh pocket. But den of course ve maybe find
anodder pocket—maybe.”

“Yes, in your pants!” I yowls. “Do you mean to tell me that the
paystreak is only twenty feet long?”

“Yust fine color above und below. Maybe if ve cut furder up——”

“My ——!” says I. “The balloon is leaking already.”

I sets there on uh box and tries to remember how it feels to be broke
again, when I happens to see the end of uh valise under my bunk.

“Well,” says I to myself. “I wonder if them supervisors is forgetting
their property?”

I hauls out the two valises, and looks out the window. Them two hombres
are each on a burro, and they got the other two plumb loaded down with
stuff that cost us five cents uh pound to get hauled in.

I grabs them valises and pilgrims right down toward them two departing
pirates. Magpie sees me coming and meets me half way.

“Where yuh going, Ike?” he asks, but I don’t heed him none, and goes
straight for the supervisors.

“Hey! Ike,” yells Magpie, from my rear. “Take them valises back to the
shack. They’re going to leave ’em with us until they returns. Sabe?”

“I will like ——!” I yells. “Since when did the Golden Hunch turn into uh
storage warehouse? There ain’t going to be no reason for them two to
ever come back here, Magpie.”

“Have uh little sense, Ike,” wails Magpie, in my ear. “Don’t do or say
anything to disturb the serenity of their departure.”

He grabs me by the arm and swings me around.

“_Bing!_”

I hits him square in the head with uh valise, as I pivots on one heel,
and I puts uh lot uh animation in the punch. I hears one of them
supervisors yip as I enters their midst over Magpie’s recumbent form,
and then I’m among them.

The tall one makes uh swipe at me with the rope, as his burro crow-hops
past me, but he misses and I comes up with uh pickhandle in my paws. Uh
burro ain’t exactly what you’d designate as uh getaway animile, being of
an inquiring disposition, and this one is no exception. It whirls after
uh couple uh bucks and comes right back past me.

I don’t propose to be the pivot to no merry-go-round, so I simply sticks
that pick-handle between its front legs as it goes past, and that tall
supervisor skates off down the hard trail on his face, with the burro
setting on his legs.

The other supervisor is having one hy-iu time trying to get his beast uh
burden into uh lope, so I gallops right along in his wake, and catches
him inside two hundred yards. I gets that burro by the tail long enough
to slam it in the ribs a couple uh times with that pick-handle, and then
I embraces uh tree to keep from going on down hill.

Yuh can’t fool uh burro. That dun-colored, overgrown jack-rabbit knowed
just exactly what I wanted done, and went right ahead and did it. The
trail ain’t wide enough for classy work, so it leaves the trail and
carves uh future for itself right down through the brush and over
slide-rock.

That supervisor rode well, I’ll say that much for him, but flesh and
blood can only stand so much. As they fades out of the picture, I sees
the supervisor’s heels go up and forward and there comes daylight
between man and beast. I removes my hat and orates to whom it may
concern—

“Well done thou good and faithful jackass.”

I’m feeling vivacious and whistles like uh medder lark as I pilgrims
back toward the bunk-house, and meets some more of the parade.

Here comes the tall feller, walking like uh man in uh dream. He’s got
his arm twined lovingly around the neck of one uh them pack-burros, and
the other is poking along in the rear, and here they comes, man weaving
and burros wondering.

I passes the time uh day with them all, but the burros seems to be the
only ones to pay any attention. I steps up on the uphill side and walks
along with ’em for uh few steps, but that tall hombre don’t seem to even
see me.

I reckon that when the burro turns up flip-flop that supervisor must uh
sprained his whole system from his heels to his hair, ’cause he walks
like uh man in uh dream. He don’t even bat an eye when I speaks—just
walks along with them burros, like uh toy what has been wound up and has
got to run down.

“Well,” says I out loud, “Far be it from Ike Harper to interrupt uh
loving scene like that, but—”

So I takes my pocket-knife out and cuts swing-rope and cinch, and they
pilgrims right along down the trail minus the packs. The supervisor
didn’t seem to mind and I know the other two jacks didn’t.

I watches ’em off down to the bottom of the trail, and then I pilgrims
on up to the bunk-house, where I finds Magpie setting on the ground with
one foot inside uh valise, and uh mad look on his face.

“Now look what you’ve done!” he wails. “Dog-gone, I’ll bet you’ve hurt
their feelings again, Ike Harper. You ain’t got uh trace uh finess in
your system.”

“Finess?”

“Yes, finess!” he snaps. “If you had any brains you’d know what that
means.”

“If I had any brains I wouldn’t be your pardner,” I retorts. “You’ve
give away about all the money we got and Swede just informs me that our
paystreak was only uh pocket.”

He looks at me, with his mouth working like uh fish out uh water.

“My ——!” he howls. “We ain’t got nothing left.”

“Nothing,” I agrees. “Not even our honor.”

Just then Swede comes around the corner.

“Hey! Mister Simpkins. Two yentlemen comes to see you. Aye’ll bring dem
up.”

“Who are they, Swede?” I asks, thinking maybe them two is back.

“Val, von is de sheriff, and aye ain’t sure about de odder. Aye tank he
bane forest ranger.”

Magpie and me looks at each other, sort uh sad like. I feels like the
feller did who fought fire for an hour to keep from burning to death,
and then got drowned when his hose busted.

“What will we do?” wails Magpie.

“Finess,” says I. “Give ’em the mill.”

 * * * * *

Around the corner comes two men, and me and Magpie shrinks like uh
woolen sock in boiling water.

“Howdy,” says the tall, hairy one. “Are you the owner uh this property?”

Magpie swallers hard and looks at me, but I says:

“He is. I’m just uh hired hindrance.”

Magpie looks at me, with his mouth open like uh winded duck, and his
Adam’s apple hops up and down his long neck.

“I’m Bill Blewett, sheriff uh this here county,” states the person who
opened the conversation. “This here gent with me is Mister Walden, the
forest ranger. We’re looking for information. Sabe?”

“Yes’m,” says I. “That’s about all that’s left. How’ll yuh have it?”

“I’m looking for uh tall hombre, dark complected, and his pardner, who
is short and uh bit gray on the hair. Couple uh tin-horns. Sabe?
Specialize on cleaning up camps after payday. Got uh little roulette
wheel.

“The tall one got previous with uh gun down at Devil’s Turn, and we
wants to ask him uh few questions. Just dropped in, thinking they might
uh stopped, being as your payday is about due.”

“You’re uh forest ranger?” I asks, looking at the sheriff’s companion,
and he nods.

“Uh-huh,” says he. “I just rode in with the sheriff. Uh course this
ain’t reserve land, but I likes to keep posted on mining operations this
near my district.”

“You ever have visits from supervisors?” I asks, offhand like.

“Sure. Two of ’em visited me this week. Left yesterday.”

They thanks us and rides away.

“Magpie,” says I. “It’s uh wonder you wouldn’t stand up when yuh
receives company.”

“I—I—I know it ain’t dignified, Ike, but every time I moves it hurts me
like blazes.”

He struggles to uh standing position, and something unhooks from the
seat of his pants and spins on the ground.

“Roulette wheel!” he yelps. “Baby roulette wheel, and I impales my
carcass on the spindle—dang it!” He takes it and throws it as far as he
can.

“All is lost!” he wails, rubbing his rear exposure. “They comes as
strangers——”

“And they took you in, Magpie,” I finishes. “If I’d uh had any brains
and them burros had any sense we’d uh been broke. Down the trail uh ways
you’ll find them two packs with our money in ’em. Sabe? The money you
been trying to give away. Next time you wants to impersonate uh wise man
bringing gifts try and get the right address. Sabe? You tries to plate
uh couple uh tin-horns with gold, and it fills you with wonderment to
think they didn’t look uh gift jackass in the face, you long-nosed,
animated Christmas tree—you!”

“My soul wallers in the depths uh remorse, Ike,” he sobs. “It’s uh good
thing that we wasn’t both born with brains. I was wise, but——”

“Ike Harper was uh danged fool,” I finishes for him again. “Uh danged
fool and his money is sometimes hard to part, Magpie. Them four burros
didn’t—say, Magpie, it was uh case uh three of uh kind trying to beat
four jacks and the joker.”

“Five jacks, Ike,” corrects Magpie. “The joker was wild.”

All of which shows—well, why preach?

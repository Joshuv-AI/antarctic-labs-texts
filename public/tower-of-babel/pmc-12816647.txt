Federated learning-based trust and energy-aware routing in Fog–Cloud computing environments for the Internet of Things
Authors: Fengchai Wang, Kun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid convergence of Fog, Cloud, and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies has introduced a new era of distributed intelligence and real-time data processing. However, ensuring secure, reliable, and energy-efficient communication across heterogeneous and resource-constrained nodes remains a fundamental challenge. This paper introduces a novel framework entitled Federated Learning-Based Trust and Energy-Aware Routing (FL-TEAR), designed to enhance routing performance in hybrid Fog–Cloud–IoT environments through collaborative intelligence, adaptive trust management, and dynamic energy optimization. The FL-TEAR system replaces static trust evaluation with a federated learning paradigm, allowing IoT and fog nodes to cooperatively train a global trust–energy model without exposing raw data. Trust scores are continuously refined based on behavioral patterns, communication reliability, and residual energy, while routing paths are selected using a composite fitness function integrating trustworthiness, energy availability, latency, and link stability. The hierarchical architecture, spanning IoT, fog, and cloud layers, reduces communication overhead, supports scalability, and preserves privacy. Simulation results confirm that FL-TEAR significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines such as E-ODMA (Energy-Efficient On-Demand Multipath Adaptive) + AOMDV (Ad hoc On-Demand Multipath Distance Vector), TAGA (Trust-Aware Geographic Routing Algorithm), and EigenTrust, achieving approximately 23% higher trust accuracy, 23% lower energy consumption, approximately 13% greater packet delivery ratio, and 37% lower delay. These findings demonstrate that federated learning can effectively balance security, sustainability, and quality of service (QoS) in large-scale IoT ecosystems, establishing FL-TEAR as a viable pathway toward intelligent, secure, and energy-efficient next-generation networks.Keywords: Fog–Cloud collaboration, Trust management, Energy-aware routing, Context-aware computing, Hybrid IoT networks, Reinforcement learning, Secure data transmissionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) has transformed the digital world fundamentally by connecting billions of different gadgets that can sense, analyze, and communicate in real time1,2. People often say that the huge amounts of data that these networked systems create are dynamic, distributed, and sensitive to delays3. Traditional cloud computing infrastructures have a lot of processing and storage power, but they aren’t good at handling applications that are spread out and sensitive to delay4. The natural distance between cloud data centers and IoT edge devices causes longer transmission delays, more congestion, and higher energy utilization. To solve these difficulties, fog computing has become a complementary paradigm that brings intelligence, storage, and processing closer to the sources of data5. Fog–Cloud Computing Environments (FCCE) are the outcome of combining fog and cloud computing. They try to make IoT systems more scalable, responsive, and energy-efficient.

In order to lower latency and energy overhead, hybrid Fog-Cloud and MEC designs have been adopted as a result of the Internet of Things’ (IoT) rapid proliferation, which has produced a significant amount of latency-sensitive data6,7. However, secure routing, energy efficiency, trustworthiness, and privacy present major issues for resource-constrained and heterogeneous nodes8–10. Single-point failures, excessive overhead, and susceptibility to malevolent activity plague traditional trust and centralized routing solutions10,11. Recent developments demonstrate that these problems can be successfully resolved by combining Byzantine-resilient federated learning12 with privacy-preserving cryptographic techniques11 and trust-aware AI-based decision-making models10.

Traditional trust management frameworks, such as EigenTrust13, TAGA14,15, and EEOIT16, typically rely on static or centralized structures that derive trust values from direct or indirect observations. Although these methods improve the assessment of node reliability, they remain highly sensitive to environmental dynamics and often incur substantial computational overhead. Moreover, centralized trust computation introduces critical limitations, including communication bottlenecks, privacy vulnerabilities, and single points of failure.

Similarly, existing energy-aware routing schemes generally emphasize resource utilization without adequately addressing the intrinsic interdependence between trust reliability and energy efficiency. Consequently, their applicability in large-scale and heterogeneous IoT deployments is constrained by the absence of a unified trust–energy coordination mechanism2,17. These challenges highlight the pressing need for a collaborative, privacy-preserving, and intelligent routing framework capable of dynamically balancing network resilience, energy conservation, and trust accuracy.

To provide relevant performance baselines, two representative multipath routing protocols are also considered. The AOMDV protocol extends AODV by establishing multiple loop-free, link-disjoint routes, thereby enhancing fault tolerance and reducing route discovery frequency in dynamic networks18. Meanwhile, the E-ODMA protocol optimizes route selection based on energy consumption and network lifetime, adapting multipath decisions to residual node energy and link quality19. The proposed FL-TEAR method presented in this paper is evaluated against these established models to demonstrate its superiority in trust accuracy, energy efficiency, and routing stability.

Recently, Federated Learning (FL) has become a game-changing option for distributed IoT networks in order to overcome these difficulties. Instead of transmitting raw data, FL enables several devices or fog nodes to work together to build a common global model, in contrast to traditional machine learning techniques that call for centralized data aggregation. Only model parameters are sent to a central aggregator, protecting data privacy and cutting down on communication cost. Each participant updates the model locally using its own private dataset. Since data privacy, bandwidth optimization, and local decision-making are critical in decentralized Fog–Cloud–IoT ecosystems, this distributed learning paradigm is ideal.

In the context of routing and trust management, Federated Learning-Based Trust and Energy-Aware Routing (FL-TEAR) introduces an adaptive and secure mechanism capable of learning from diverse network experiences while maintaining system autonomy and privacy. Instead of computing trust values in isolation or relying on direct neighbor feedback, FL enables fog and IoT nodes to collaboratively learn global trust–energy models that reflect the dynamic state of the entire network. Through iterative local training and global aggregation, nodes enhance their ability to identify malicious behaviors, optimize energy consumption, and select secure routing paths. Furthermore, FL facilitates continual learning, enabling the routing framework to evolve with changes in node behavior, mobility patterns, and energy levels over time.

Despite its potential, implementing FL-based trust and energy-aware routing introduces several research challenges. First, the heterogeneity of IoT nodes in terms of computational power, energy resources, and communication capabilities can result in imbalanced participation during federated training. Second, communication inefficiency and synchronization overhead between fog and cloud layers can degrade convergence speed. Third, the presence of Byzantine or malicious participants poses threats to model integrity, as compromised nodes might inject false updates to manipulate the global model. Overcoming these challenges requires the integration of robust aggregation mechanisms, adaptive node selection strategies, and anomaly detection modules that preserve both the accuracy and security of the federated learning process.

Motivated by these challenges, this paper proposes a novel Federated Learning-Based Trust and Energy-Aware Routing (FL-TEAR) framework for hybrid Fog–Cloud–IoT environments. The proposed model establishes a hierarchical collaborative learning system in which IoT devices compute local trust values based on direct interactions, such as packet delivery ratio, latency, and residual energy, and transmit encrypted model parameters to fog nodes for intermediate aggregation. The fog layer performs localized federated updates, while the cloud acts as a global coordinator to refine the overall trust–energy model. This multi-tiered structure reduces communication costs, enhances scalability, and maintains privacy across all layers. Through this approach, the system dynamically adapts to network fluctuations, minimizes energy wastage, and resists trust-related attacks such as self-advertisement, collusion, and on–off behavior.

The main novelty of FL-TEAR is the integration of federated intelligence with energy-aware trust routing, which produces a self-evolving architecture that optimizes security and efficiency at the same time. FL’s distributed architecture removes centralized dependency, enabling collective intelligence. Furthermore, the energy-efficient architecture ensures that routes are selected for reliability and sustainability. Consequently, the system achieves suitable trade-offs between routing efficiency, energy conservation, and trust accuracy, all crucial elements for the next generation of intelligent IoT infrastructures.

Related works

Paper20 suggests an Energy-aware Two-Way Trust Routing (ETWTR) system specifically designed for fog computing settings. This system may dynamically route data to optimize transmission while taking into account dependability and energy availability. ETWTR can take into account security, dependability, quality of service, and energy use all at once, which is better than fog computing. For better security, the ETWTR-based trust management system lets both requesters and service providers assess each other’s reliability at the same time. To solve the problem of optimizing energy use, a Deep Q-Network (DQN) is used to anticipate how much energy is available in nodes and how to use it most effectively.

Study21 presents an Energy-aware safe Routing strategy using Two-Way Trust evaluation (ERTWT) for IoT networks. This plan makes the network safer from several types of attacks by figuring out trust values based on energy trust, direct trust, and indirect trust. The goal of the strategy is to make data transmission more efficient by choosing routes based on the energy availability and trustworthiness parameters of fog nodes. With ERTWT, both the service requester and the service provider can check each other’s safety and trustworthiness at the same time. This study also uses Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) to guess the energy levels of nodes so that they can be used in the best way possible.

Article22 proposes a multidimensional trust fusion preference (MTFP) technique to identify malicious nodes, leveraging several characteristics that influence trust assessment. Initially, the discrepancies in service ratings between self-evaluations and external evaluations within a historical sliding window are computed and utilized differentially to mitigate the issue of sparse behavior in direct trust evaluation. Furthermore, a multiperspective weight method has been developed to enhance the rationality of recommendation trust.

Research23 proposes a bi-directional trust management system (TMS) for secure offloading and fog-to-fog collaboration, enabling a service requester (SR) to assess the trustworthiness of a service provider (SP) and vice versa, prior to establishing a connection. The suggested TMS employs quality of service (QoS), quality of security (QoSec), and social ties for direct trust computation via self-observation, with past reputation and recommendations to ascertain indirect trust. Paper24 proposes a Gaussian distribution-based comprehensive trust management system (GDTMS) for F-IWSN. In addition, the grey decision-making process is used to find the right balance between security, transmission performance, and energy use in the trust choice. The suggested trade-off can help choose the secure and reliable relay node, which is a secure routing strategy based on trust management. The proposed approaches are also useful for protecting against attacks that involve poor mouthing.

In Work25, an Efficient and Secure Fog Based Routing Mechanism (ESFRM) is presented to safeguard the network from both internal and external assaults. Before sending the data, every node that is part of IoT communications determines the overall trust value of the next intermediate node by adding up the direct trust, indirect trust, and energy trust values. Then, this full trust value is compared to the full threshold trust value to see if the node is a rogue node or a legitimate node. Additionally, an improved RSA (Rivest, Shamir, Adleman) method is used to protect data at three levels: from the Cluster Head (CH) to the fog node, from the fog node to the cloud server, and directly from the CH to the cloud server.

Research26 intends to offer a trust management-based edge caching system that utilizes blockchain technology to enhance the security of the caching process by fostering trust between content producers and edge servers. The suggested trust management system builds trust in both sides of the relationship, which gives the caching actors more confidence in each other.

In27, a new Two-way IoT data & Fog Trust management (TwI-FTM) technique is suggested to find bad data. It uses the Trusted Data Measurement (TDM) and Fog Access Trust Evaluation (FATE) methods to find bad fog access nodes in a fog network. The threshold level is used to keep an eye on fog control nodes all the time to find these strange data and bad fog access nodes. Once it is recognized, they will be automatically eliminated from the IoT-FoG network.

Study28 introduces the Unsupervised Federated Hypernetwork Method for Distributed Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection and Diagnosis (uFedHy-DisMTSADD). A federated hypernetwork architecture is presented that efficiently addresses the heterogeneity and variability in remote situations while safeguarding client data privacy. Next, we use the Series Conversion Normalization Transformer (SC Nor-Transformer) to fix the time bias that happens when models are combined through series conversion. Research29 suggests a lightweight solution for Industrial IoT nodes that can’t keep their security up to date in order to deal with security problems, latency, and the possibility of bad behavior. LightTrust (Lightweight Trust Management) uses a central trust agent to create and administer trust certificates that let nodes talk to each other for a set amount of time without having to do trust computations.

Proposed method

This section introduces the proposed Federated Learning-Based Trust and Energy-Aware Routing (FL-TEAR) framework designed for hybrid Fog–Cloud computing environments in large-scale IoT networks. The FL-TEAR model integrates federated intelligence, trust management, and energy-aware routing within a multi-layer architecture to achieve adaptive, secure, and efficient communication between heterogeneous devices. The overall framework operates in three hierarchical layers, IoT, Fog, and Cloud, which collaboratively manage data processing, model training, and trust–energy optimization without exposing sensitive data.

System architecture

Three functional levels make up the system:

IoT layer: This layer consists of edge devices with energy constraints, like embedded systems, cameras, and sensors. To keep tabs on nearby nodes based on factors including packet delivery ratio (PDR), latency, residual energy, and network dependability, each node keeps a local trust evaluation module. The nodes engage in the federated learning process as local learners as well.

IoT layer: This layer consists of edge devices with energy constraints, like embedded systems, cameras, and sensors. To keep tabs on nearby nodes based on factors including packet delivery ratio (PDR), latency, residual energy, and network dependability, each node keeps a local trust evaluation module. The nodes engage in the federated learning process as local learners as well.

Fog layer: Local model updates from Internet of Things devices are compiled by fog nodes, which serve as intermediary gateways. They carry out regional trust normalization, anomaly detection on updates, and intermediate federated aggregation. On the basis of the global trust-energy index, they also manage multi-path routing decisions between clusters.

Fog layer: Local model updates from Internet of Things devices are compiled by fog nodes, which serve as intermediary gateways. They carry out regional trust normalization, anomaly detection on updates, and intermediate federated aggregation. On the basis of the global trust-energy index, they also manage multi-path routing decisions between clusters.

The cloud layer serves as the global federated coordinator, spreading updated trust-energy models to all fog nodes, improving global routing protocols, and maintaining the overall model parameters. Only model weights and gradients, not raw data, are transferred between layers to guarantee scalability and privacy.

The fog and IoT layers get the most up-to-date model parameters and routing policies from the cloud. This helps the network adapt to the changing conditions of the environment and the energy supply. The FL-TEAR architecture gets rid of centralized bottlenecks and speeds up decision-making by spreading intelligence across the three layers.

The hierarchical form also makes it easier to grow because new IoT nodes or fog clusters can join the network without affecting the overall structure. In the heterogeneous Fog–Cloud–IoT ecosystem, our method lets people learn in real time and route energy-aware traffic while keeping trust and privacy safe.

Restrictions and dangers to validity

Using a simulated fog-cloud-IoT environment with up to 200 nodes, random waypoint mobility (speed 0–5 m/s), and up to 200 federated rounds, the suggested FL-TEAR framework was assessed in MATLAB R2024b. The following simplifying assumptions may affect generalization to real deployments, even though these settings capture important dynamics of large-scale IoT networks: (i) uniform initial energy distribution and idealized radio propagation; (ii) artificial malicious behavior (on–off, bad-mouthing, and poisoning attacks) instead of real-world adversaries; (iii) the absence of real hardware constraints (such as STM32-level clock drift and flash wear); (iv) constant traffic patterns instead of bursty real IoT workloads; and (v) perfect synchronization among layers without clock skew or packet loss during aggregation. Instead of using actual data that had been labeled by humans, ground-truth trust values were created by controlled assault injection. Despite being commonplace in the literature, these simulation-specific decisions pose the most risks to external validity. To further verify practical performance, more research on hardware testbeds and actual traffic traces is planned.

Discussion

Real IoT deployments inevitably suffer from non-IID data and concept drift due to heterogeneous sensing conditions and node-specific patterns. As comprehensively surveyed in41, common mitigation strategies include clustering, personalised models, and regularised objectives. In FL-TEAR, fog-level intermediate aggregation naturally creates implicit clusters of similar nodes, restricting accuracy degradation to only 4.2% under severe non-IID scenarios (70% of nodes having highly skewed local datasets). Continuous federated rounds enable adaptation to drift, while energy-aware weighting (Eq. 20) ensures fairness by preventing systematic exclusion of low-energy nodes.

The framework simultaneously addresses security, privacy, and resource efficiency, aligning with the integration perspective in42. Cosine-similarity filtering combined with differential-privacy noise (σ = 0.1) and lightweight Paillier encryption protects against poisoning and eavesdropping, while energy-aware participant selection and fog pre-aggregation keep per-round overhead below 4% of local training time.

Regarding the privacy–performance–efficiency see-saw described in43, the trust module effectively counters realistic poisoning and free-rider attacks (94% malicious influence reduction), with privacy noise causing only ~ 2.8% accuracy loss—a favourable trade-off.

Mutable blockchains, as explored in44, offer promising extensions for audited rollback and safe unlearning of compromised updates.

Finally, model size and update frequency heavily impact battery life, as analyzed in45. FL-TEAR mitigates this through fog-level pre-aggregation (reducing wide-area uploads by 78%) and selective scheduling based on residual energy, achieving 23% lower consumption than full-participation baselines while preserving convergence.

Conclusion

A federated, trust-energy-aware routing architecture that promotes safe, low-latency communication over hybrid fog-cloud-IoT networks was presented in this work. The approach offers three main advantages by combining hierarchical federated learning with fitness-based multipath selection, energy modeling, and multidimensional trust estimation. By combining robust, privacy-preserving aggregation with direct observations, it first increases trust reliability by reducing on–off behaviors, ballot stuffing, and poisoning without disclosing raw data. Second, by avoiding energy gaps and lowering rerouting churn, it maintains network lifespan through energy-aware route construction and balanced load distribution. Third, by choosing routes that simultaneously maximize trust, residual energy, connection stability, latency, and congestion, it stabilizes quality of service, resulting in faster convergence and greater delivery ratios.

The suggested method regularly achieves higher trust accuracy, better packet delivery, lower delay, decreased routing overhead, and better convergence behavior, according to a comparative analysis against commonly used baselines. Crucially, these benefits endure in the face of changing circumstances and diverse resource profiles, demonstrating the flexibility and robustness of the framework (see Fig. 13). Future research will extend to hardware-in-the-loop testbeds, measure communication/compute overheads each federated round across device classes, and investigate defenses against sophisticated collusion and Byzantine updates, even as simulations confirm efficacy at scale. Additionally, we foresee auditability using lightweight cryptographic proofs and cross-layer integration (e.g., MAC/radio hints). All things considered, the findings show that federated, trust-energy-aware routing is a workable route to high-performance, safe, and sustainable fog-enabled IoT systems.

Bosonization of Noise Effects in Nonlocal Quantum Dynamics
Michele Fantechi, Marco Merkli

Abstract
Quantum systems that interact non-locally with an environment are paradigms for exploring collective phenomena. They naturally emerge in various physical contexts involving long-range, many-body interactions. We consider a general class of such open systems characterized by a coupling to the environment which is inversely proportional to the square root of the environment size. We show that the induced system dynamics has a universal bosonic nature: the same evolution arises from coupling the system to a collection of noninteracting bosonic modes, independently of the microscopic structure of the original environment. This emergent ‘bosonization’ of the environment’s influence results from the scaling of the coupling in the thermodynamic limit and is a manifestation of the quantum central limit theorem. While the effect has been observed in specific models before, we show that it is, in fact, a universal feature.

Discussion and examples.

Summary.
— We study quantum systems
{\rm S}
coupled to reservoirs
{\rm R}
of size
M
through non-local interactions, scaled mesoscopically, proportional to
1/\sqrt{M}
, in the limit
M\to\infty
. We show that the reduced density matrix of the system
{\rm S}
evolves exactly as if it were interacting with a different reservoir
{\rm F}
, consisting of independent bosonic modes initially in their ground states, and coupled through a simple, explicit, linear interaction. Our findings reveal that free bosonic reservoirs universally emerge from mesoscopically scaled, non-local couplings. We plan to use this framework to analyze the interplay of mean field and fluctuation effects in systems far from equilibrium (transport) and describe thermodynamic effects at arbitrary coupling strengths.

take pairwise equal values and different values among pairs.

To list all these terms we proceed as follows. For each such term,
m_{1}=m_{\pi(1)}
equals some other index
m_{\pi(2)}
with
\pi(1)<\pi(2)
. The next ‘free’
m
-index in the product
w_{1}^{[m_{1}]}\cdots w_{n}^{[m_{n}]}
(not equal to
m_{\pi(1)}
nor
m_{\pi(2)}
) is
m_{\pi(3)}
for some
\pi(1)<\pi(3)
. That one is paired up with an
m_{\pi(4)}
with
\pi(3)<\pi(4)
. Continuing this way we obtain the pairing

\big(w_{\pi(1)},w_{\pi(2)}\big),\big(w_{\pi(3)},w_{\pi(4)}\big),\ldots,\big(w_{\pi(n-1)},w_{\pi(n)}\big).

(S.44)

Each pair is ‘sitting’ on a different factor (value of
m
) and so the average in the state
\rho_{{\rm R},M}
of the pair (
S.44
) is the product of the single-site averages,

\omega_{\rm R}\Big(w_{\pi(1)}w_{\pi(2)}\Big)\cdots\omega_{\rm R}\Big(w_{\pi(n-1)}w_{\pi(n)}\Big).

(S.45)

As discussed above, each term (
S.45
) has a multiplicity
M(M-1)\cdots(M-k_{2}+1)
with
k_{2}=n/2
, which is compensated by the prefactor
M^{-n/2}
,

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M(M-1)\cdots(M-n/2+1)}{M^{n/2}}=1.

(S.46)

The formula (
S.40
) follows. This completes the proof of Proposition
2
.
\square

S.2
Equivalent Gaussian reservoir: Proof of the theorem

Let
\rho_{\rm F}
be a Gaussian state of a number
N
(to be determined) of independent quantum harmonic oscillators with creation and annihilation operators
a^{\dagger}_{j}
,
a_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,N
. For
{\mathbf{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
we set

\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(a^{\dagger}({\mathbf{z}})+a({\mathbf{z}})\big),\qquad a^{\dagger}({\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger},\quad a({\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{z}_{j}a_{j}.

Here,
\bar{z}
is the complex conjugate of
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Wick’s theorem gives

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{\rm F}\Big(\rho_{\rm F}\,\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}}_{1})\cdots\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}}_{n})\Big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}{\rm tr}_{\rm F}\Big(\rho_{\rm F}\,\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}}_{\pi(2j-1)})\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}}_{\pi(2j)})\Big)&\mbox{for $n$ even}\\
\displaystyle 0&\mbox{for $n$ odd}\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is defined after (
S.40
). Consider a (‘Bogoliubov’) dynamics generated by a Hamiltonian
H_{\rm F}
such that

e^{itH_{\rm F}}\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}})e^{-itH_{\rm F}}=\Phi_{\rm F}({\mathbf{z}}(t)),

(S.49)

where
{\mathbf{z}}(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
for all
t
. We let the system
{\rm S}
interact with the bosonic reservoir according to the total Hamiltonian,

H_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}=H_{\rm S}+H_{\rm F}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}G_{q}\otimes X_{q}+G_{q}^{\dagger}\otimes X_{q}^{\dagger}

(S.50)

where we take
X_{q}
to be

X_{q}=a({\mathbf{z}}_{q})+a^{\dagger}({\mathbf{w}}_{q})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{z}_{q,j}a_{j}+w_{q,j}a^{\dagger}_{j},

(S.51)

for some
{\mathbf{z}}_{q}=(z_{q,1},\ldots,z_{q,N})
and
{\mathbf{w}}_{q}=(w_{q,1},\ldots,w_{q,N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
to be determined.

Our strategy to prove the theorem is this: We show that the Dyson series obtained for
{\rm tr}_{\rm F}(e^{itH_{\rm S}}e^{-itH_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}}(\rho_{\rm S}\otimes\rho_{\rm F})e^{itH_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}}e^{-itH_{\rm S}})
(see (
S.55
) below) is the same as the Dyson series (
S.28
) in the limit
M\rightarrow\infty
. For the latter Dyson series, we use Proposition
2
. Namely, as explained in the proof of Proposition
1
, expanding the multiple commutator in the series in (
S.28
) gives an operator shown on the right hand side of (
S.30
), and using (
S.40
) we obtain for this term,

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{\rm R}\Big(V^{\sigma_{\ell_{1}}}_{{\rm R},M,r_{\ell_{1}}}(t_{\ell_{1}})\cdots V^{\sigma_{\ell_{L}}}_{{\rm R},M,r_{\ell_{L}}}(t_{\ell_{L}})\ \rho_{{\rm R},M}\ V^{\sigma_{\ell_{L+1}}}_{{\rm R},M,r_{\ell_{L+1}}}(t_{\ell_{L}+1})\cdots V^{\sigma_{\ell_{n}}}_{{\rm R},M,r_{\ell_{n}}}(t_{\ell_{n}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}{\rm tr}_{\rm R}\Big(\rho_{{\rm R},M}\ W_{\ell_{L+1}}\cdots W_{\ell_{n}}W_{\ell_{1}}\cdots W_{\ell_{L}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}\omega_{\rm R}\Big(w_{\pi(2j-1)}w_{\pi(2j)}\Big)&\mbox{for $n$ even}\\
\displaystyle 0&\mbox{for $n$ odd}\end{array}\right.

where the notation is as in Proposition
2
and the
w_{j}
are operators
v_{r}^{\sigma}(t)
.

We now consider the Dyson series resulting from the coupling of
{\rm S}
to the fluctuation reservoir
{\rm F}
with total Hamiltonian
H_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}
, (
S.50
). We write for the free evolution of
X_{q}
,

X_{q}(t)=e^{itH_{\rm F}}X_{q}e^{-itH_{\rm F}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{z}_{q,j}e^{-it\omega_{j}}a_{j}+w_{q,j}e^{i\omega_{j}t}a^{\dagger}_{j}.

(S.54)

Analogously to (
S.29
), we obtain the Dyson series expansion,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{\rm F}

\displaystyle\Big(e^{itH_{\rm S}}e^{-itH_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}}(\rho_{\rm S}\otimes\rho_{\rm F})e^{itH_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}}e^{-itH_{\rm S}}\Big)=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-i)^{n}\sum_{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}}\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}}\int_{0\leq t_{n}\leq\cdots\leq t_{1}\leq t}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{n}

\displaystyle\times\ {\rm tr}_{\rm F}\Big(\big[G_{q_{1}}^{\sigma_{1}}(t_{1})\otimes X_{q_{1}}^{\sigma_{1}}(t_{1}),[\cdots[G_{q_{n}}^{\sigma_{n}}(t_{n})\otimes X_{q_{n}}^{\sigma_{n}}(t_{n}),\rho_{\rm S}\otimes\rho_{\rm F}]\ldots]\big]\Big).

(S.55)

Consider fixed values of
q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}
and
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
. Expanding the multiple commutator in (
S.55
) yields
2^{n}
terms, each of the form

\displaystyle G_{q_{\ell_{1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{1}}}(t_{\ell_{1}})\cdots

\displaystyle\,G_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L}}}({t_{\ell_{L}}})\ \rho_{\rm S}\ G_{q_{\ell_{L+1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L+1}}}(t_{\ell_{L}+1})\cdots G_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{n}}}(t_{\ell_{n}})

\displaystyle\otimes X_{q_{\ell_{1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{1}}}(t_{\ell_{1}})\cdots X_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L}}}(t_{\ell_{L}})\ \rho_{\rm F}\ X_{q_{\ell_{L+1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L+1}}}(t_{\ell_{L}+1})\cdots X_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{n}}}(t_{\ell_{n}}),

(S.56)

just as in (
S.30
). The trace over the reservoir operators in (
S.56
) is

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{\rm F}

\displaystyle\Big(X_{q_{\ell_{1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{1}}}(t_{\ell_{1}})\cdots X_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L}}}(t_{\ell_{L}})\ \rho_{\rm F}\ X_{q_{\ell_{L+1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L+1}}}(t_{\ell_{L}+1})\cdots X_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{n}}}(t_{\ell_{n}})\Big)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}_{\rm F}\Big(\rho_{\rm F}\ X_{q_{\ell_{L+1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L+1}}}(t_{\ell_{L}+1})\cdots X_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{n}}}(t_{\ell_{n}})X_{q_{\ell_{1}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{1}}}(t_{\ell_{1}})\cdots X_{q_{\ell_{L}}}^{\sigma_{\ell_{L}}}(t_{\ell_{L}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}\omega_{\rm F}\Big(x_{\pi(2j-1)}x_{\pi(2j)}\Big)&\mbox{for $n$ even}\\
\displaystyle 0&\mbox{for $n$ odd}\end{array}\right.

Here we set
x_{j}
to stand for the
X_{q}^{\sigma}(t)
, analogously to the
w_{j}
in (
S.2
). In the last equality in (

) we used Wick’s theorem which holds for any linear combination of field operators (hence for the

in the supplemental material.

Comparing (
S.2
) and (
S.2
) we conclude that

{\rm tr}_{\rm F}\Big(e^{itH_{\rm S}}e^{-itH_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}}(\rho_{\rm S}\otimes\rho_{\rm F})e^{itH_{{\rm S}{\rm F}}}e^{-itH_{\rm S}}\Big)=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}{\rm tr}_{{\rm R},M}\Big(e^{itH_{M}^{0}}e^{-itH_{{\rm S}{\rm R},M}}\rho_{{\rm S}{\rm R},M}\,e^{itH_{{\rm S}{\rm R},M}}e^{-itH_{M}^{0}}\Big)

provided that the following two-point functions coincide for each

,

\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}^{\sigma}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)=\omega_{\rm F}\big(X_{q}^{\sigma}(t)X_{q^{\prime}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big).

(S.59)

Therefore, the theorem is proven provided we can show (
S.59
), which is what we do now.

For a general state
\omega
and operator
A
we have
\omega(A)=\overline{\omega(A^{\dagger})}
. Using this and that
v_{q}^{\dagger}(t)=(v_{q}(t))^{\dagger}
and
X_{q}^{\dagger}(t)=(X_{q}(t))^{\dagger}
shows that (
S.59
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm F}\big(X_{q}(t)X_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)

(S.60)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}^{\dagger}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm F}\big(X_{q}^{\dagger}(t)X_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)

(S.61)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm F}\big(X_{q}(t)X_{q^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\big).

(S.62)

We now identify
{\mathbf{z}}_{q},{\mathbf{w}}_{q}
in (
S.51
) such that (
S.60
)-(
S.62
) hold. Denote the Bohr energies of
h_{\rm R}
by
E_{kl}=E_{k}-E_{l}

and write
[v_{q}]_{kl}=\langle\chi_{k}|v_{q}|\chi_{l}\rangle
for the matrix elements of
v_{q}
in the eigenbasis

\{\chi_{j}\}
of
h_{\rm R}
(see (
14
)). Using (
14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}p_{k}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})E_{kl}}[v_{q}]_{kl}[v_{q^{\prime}}]_{lk}

(S.63)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}^{\dagger}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}p_{k}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})E_{kl}}\overline{[v_{q}]_{lk}}[v_{q^{\prime}}]_{lk}

(S.64)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm R}\big(v_{q}(t)v_{q^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}p_{k}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})E_{kl}}[v_{q}]_{kl}\overline{[v_{q^{\prime}}]_{kl}}.

(S.65)

We can restrict the sums to indices

for which the summands are nonzero, see (
17
).
Next we calculate the right hand sides of (
S.60
)-(
S.62
), with
X_{q}(t)
given in (
S.54

\omega_{\rm F}
is the product state of the vacua of each mode. We have
\omega_{\rm F}(a_{j}a^{\dagger}_{m})=\delta_{j,m}

\omega_{\rm F}\big(X_{q}(t)X_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)=\sum_{j,m}\overline{z}_{q,j}w_{q^{\prime},m}e^{-it\omega_{j}+it^{\prime}\omega_{m}}\omega_{\rm F}\big(a_{j}a^{\dagger}_{m}\big)=\sum_{j}\overline{z}_{q,j}w_{q^{\prime},j}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})(-\omega_{j})}.

(S.66)

We thus take one fluctuation mode for each value of the pair
(k,l)
. The total number of fluctuation modes is (maximally)

— if

for some

or some of the matrix elements vanish, then the corresponding terms in the sums (

\omega_{\rm F}\big(X_{q}(t)X_{q^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big)=\sum_{k,l}\overline{z}_{q,kl}w_{q^{\prime},kl}\,e^{i(t-t^{\prime})(-\omega_{kl})}.

T
Emergence of the ground state for
{\rm F}

The purpose of this section is to illustrate how the ground state emerges as the standard representation of
{\rm F}
, regardless of the details of the
{\rm S}{\rm R}
interaction or the properties of
{\rm R}
. We also explain that in certain special cases (of
{\rm S}{\rm R}
interactions and properties of
{\rm R}
), other representations than the ground state can be used for
{\rm F}
.

To explain the emergence of the ground state of
{\rm F}
, we first consider the case (
5
) with
Q=1
and
G=G^{\dagger}
,
v=v^{\dagger}
. As explained in the main text, we need to find a Gaussian state
\rho_{\rm F}
of
N
oscillators to match the two-point function (
15
). Denote the ‘field operator’ of
N
bosonic modes by
\Phi({\mathbf{z}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(a^{\dagger}({\mathbf{z}})+a({\mathbf{z}}))
,
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
(compare also with (
S.51
)). A Gaussian state is determined uniquely by its covariance matrix
\mathcal{C}
via the expression
{\rm tr}_{\rm F}(\rho_{\rm F}\,e^{i\Phi({\mathbf{z}})})=e^{-\frac{1}{4}\langle{\mathbf{z}},\mathcal{C}{\mathbf{z}}\rangle}
, where
\langle{\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{z_{j}}w_{j}
is the Euclidean inner product of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\mathcal{C}
is an
N\times N
complex matrix satisfying
\mathcal{C}\geq\mathbf{1}
. The two-point function is,

{\rm tr}\big(\rho_{\rm F}\Phi({\mathbf{z}})\Phi({\mathbf{w}})\big)=\frac{1}{4}\langle{\mathbf{z}},(\mathcal{C}+\mathbf{1}){\mathbf{w}}\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\langle{\mathbf{w}},(\mathcal{C}-\mathbf{1}){\mathbf{z}}\rangle.

(T.69)

As (
15
) depends on the time difference
\tau=t-t^{\prime}
only, which encodes the stationarity of
\rho_{\rm R}
, the same must be true for (
T.69
) when
{\mathbf{z}}
and
{\mathbf{w}}
are replaced by
{\mathbf{z}}(t)
and
{\mathbf{w}}(t^{\prime})
(the
j
-th component of
{\mathbf{z}}(t)
is
e^{it\omega_{j}}z_{j}
). This means that the covariance matrix is diagonal,
\mathcal{C}={\rm diag}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{N})
,
c_{j}\geq 1
, and the time-dependent two-point function (
T.69
) becomes,

{\rm tr}\big(\rho_{\rm F}\Phi({\mathbf{z}}(t))\Phi({\mathbf{w}}(t^{\prime}))\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\alpha_{j}\frac{c_{j}+1}{4}e^{-i\omega_{j}\tau}+\overline{\alpha}_{j}\frac{c_{j}-1}{4}e^{i\omega_{j}\tau}\Big),\qquad\alpha_{j}=w_{j}\overline{z}_{j},\quad\tau=t-t^{\prime}.

(T.70)

Setting
x_{j}={\rm Re}\alpha_{j}
,
y_{j}={\rm Im}\alpha_{j}
and comparing the real and imaginary parts of (
T.70
) and (
15
) gives,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{c_{j}}{2}\Big[x_{j}\cos(\omega_{j}\tau)+y_{j}\sin(\omega_{j}\tau)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d_{\rm R}}p_{k}|v_{kl}|^{2}\cos\big((E_{l}-E_{k})\tau\big),

(T.71)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\Big[-x_{j}\sin(\omega_{j}\tau)+y_{j}\cos(\omega_{j}\tau)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d_{\rm R}}-p_{k}|v_{kl}|^{2}\sin\big((E_{l}-E_{k})\tau\big).

(T.72)

In order to satisfy (
T.71
) and (
T.72
) we can take one oscillator
j
for each ordered pair
(k,l)
, so that
x_{j}\rightarrow x_{kl}
and so on.
Then
\omega_{kl}=E_{l}-E_{k}
,
y_{kl}=0
,
\frac{c_{kl}}{2}x_{kl}=p_{k}|v_{kl}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}x_{kl}
and therefore
c_{kl}=1
, which means that
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{1}
and
\rho_{\rm F}
is the vacuum state. The same analysis shows that
c_{j}=1
in the general case (
Q>1
,
G_{q}
and
v_{q}
not hermitian).

Other representations for special cases.
Assume that
Q=1
in the interaction (
5
) and
G=G^{\dagger}
,
v=v^{\dagger}
, with
v_{kk}=0
. Assume also that all the populations
p_{j}
are distinct. By a possible relabeling of the energies
E_{1},\ldots,E_{d_{\rm R}}
we achieve that

p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{d_{\rm R}}.

We now show that we can satisfy (
T.71
) and (
T.72
) by associating to each pair
(k,l)
with
k<l
a mode
a_{kl}
in a thermal state, accounting for transitions
E_{k}\rightarrow E_{l}
and
E_{l}\rightarrow E_{k}
jointly. Namely, the right hand sides of (
T.71
) and (
T.72
) can be written respectively as (use
v_{kk}=0
and
|v_{kl}|=|v_{lk}|
)

\sum_{k<l}(p_{k}+p_{l})|v_{kl}|^{2}\cos\big((E_{k}-E_{l})\tau\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{k<l}(p_{k}-p_{l})|v_{kl}|^{2}\sin\big((E_{k}-E_{l})\tau\big).

(T.73)

We identify (choose modes
j
with)
j\leftrightarrow\{(k,l)\mbox{\ with\ }k<l\}
and we have

y_{kl}=0,\quad\omega_{kl}=E_{l}-E_{k},\quad c_{kl}x_{kl}=2(p_{l}+p_{k})\,|v_{kl}|^{2},\quad x_{kl}=2(p_{l}-p_{k})\,|v_{kl}|^{2}.

The resulting covariance is
c_{kl}=(p_{l}+p_{k})/(p_{l}-p_{k})
(which is
\geq 1
as it has to be). This covariance defines the state

\rho_{kl}=Z^{-1}_{kl}\ e^{-\beta_{kl}\omega_{kl}a^{\dagger}_{kl}a_{kl}},\qquad\text{with}\qquad\beta_{kl}\omega_{kl}=\ln\big(\frac{p_{l}}{p_{k}}\big).

With this identification of the fluctuation modes, the reservoir state is
\rho_{\rm F}=\otimes_{k<l}\,\rho_{kl}
, the product of single mode equilibrium states at different temperatures. However, contrary to the standard representation of
{\rm F}
by the ground state, this ‘thermal representation’ cannot be done in general, as shows the following simple example.

Let
{\rm S}
be arbitrary and let the components of
{\rm R}
be two-level systems with basis states
\{|1\rangle,|2\rangle\}
and
h_{\rm R}=E_{1}|1\rangle\langle 1|+E_{2}|2\rangle\langle 2|
, some
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. The the reservoir initial state
\rho_{\rm R}=p|1\rangle\langle 1|+(1-p)|2\rangle\langle 2|
for some
p\in[0,1]
and take the interaction (
5
) with
Q=1
,
G
arbitrary and
v=v_{12}|1\rangle\langle 2|+v_{21}|2\rangle\langle 1|
off-diagonal, some
v_{12},v_{21}\in\mathbb{C}
(
v
not necessarily hermitian). The reservoir
{\rm R}
two-point function (with
\tau=t-t^{\prime}
,
E_{12}=E_{1}-E_{2}
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\big(\rho_{\rm R}v(t)v(t^{\prime})\big)=pv_{12}v_{21}e^{i\tau E_{12}}+(1-p)v_{12}v_{21}e^{-i\tau E_{12}}

(T.74)

has to be matched by the reservoir
{\rm F}
two-point correlation function of a Gaussian
\rho_{\rm F}
with covariance
\mathcal{C}
(see before (
T.69
)), which for
X({\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(a^{\dagger}({\mathbf{z}})+a({\mathbf{w}}))
,
{\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, is given by (similarly to (
T.70
), with
{\mathbf{z}}(t)
having components
e^{i\omega_{j}t}z_{j}
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\Big(\rho_{\rm F}X\big({\mathbf{z}}(t),{\mathbf{w}}(t)\big)X\big({\mathbf{z}}(t^{\prime}),{\mathbf{w}}(t^{\prime})\big)\Big)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{c_{j}+1}{4}\bar{w}_{j}z_{j}e^{-i\tau\omega_{j}}+\frac{c_{j}-1}{4}\bar{z}_{j}w_{j}e^{i\tau\omega_{j}}\Big).

(T.75)

Suppose we could represent
{\rm F}
by a single oscillator (in a thermal state, as outlined above), then
N=1
in (
T.75
).
Equating (
T.74
) and (
T.75
) implies that
\omega
is either
E_{12}
or
-E_{12}
. If
\omega=-E_{12}
then
(c+1)\bar{w}z=4pv_{12}v_{21}
and
(c-1)\bar{z}w=4(1-p)v_{12}v_{21}
. Take
p\neq 0,1
. The last two equalities imply that
v_{12}v_{21}=\frac{1}{4p}(c+1)\bar{w}z=\frac{1}{4(1-p)}(c-1)\bar{z}w
. Therefore, as
c\geq 1
and
p
are real, we must have
\bar{z}w\in\mathbb{R}
. This forces
v_{12}v_{21}\in\mathbb{R}
. The analysis for
\omega=E_{12}
yields the same result. Consequently, a simple case where we cannot realize
{\rm F}
by a thermal mode is when the phases of
v_{12}
and
v_{21}
are not opposite.

U
Wick’s theorem

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space and let
W(f)
be Weyl operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
, for
f\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and where
{\mathfrak{h}}
is another Hilbert space, the so-called single-particle Hilbert space. (For
N
bosonic modes we have
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathbb{C}^{N}
, for a ‘usual’ scalar field in three space dimensions,
{\mathfrak{h}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},d^{3}x)
.) The operators
W(f)
are unitary and satisfy

W(-f)=W(f)^{*}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad W(f)W(g)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}{\rm Im}\langle f,g\rangle}W(f+g),

(U.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the inner product of
{\mathfrak{h}}
. We assume that

W(f)=e^{i\Phi(f)}

for some self-adjoint operator
\Phi(f)
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, which is called the field operator (the field operators exist for so-called ‘regular representation’ of the canonical commutation relations
[
46
]
).
\Phi
is real linear, that is,
\Phi(tf)=t\Phi(f)
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\rho
be a density matrix on
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfying

{\rm tr}\big(\rho W(f)\big)=e^{-\frac{1}{4}\langle f,\mathcal{C}f\rangle},

(U.2)

where
\mathcal{C}\geq\mathbf{1}
is an operator called the covariance operator. The state
\rho
is called centered and Gaussian (or, quasi-free). We want to find an expression for the correlation function
{\rm tr}\big(\rho\Phi(f_{1})\cdots\Phi(f_{n})\big)
, where
n\geq 1
and
f_{j}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
are given. As
\Phi(f)=(-i)\partial_{t}|_{0}W(tf)
and due to (
U.1
), (
U.2
) we have

{\rm tr}\big(\rho\Phi(f_{1})\cdots\Phi(f_{n})\big)=(-i)^{n}\partial^{n}_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\big|_{0}\ e^{-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k<l}t_{k}t_{l}{\rm Im}\langle f_{k},f_{l}\rangle}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k,l}t_{k}t_{l}\langle f_{k},\mathcal{C}f_{l}\rangle}.

The term in the second exponent when
k=l
yields squares
t_{k}^{2}
which do not contribute as we take the derivatives and set all
t_{k}
equal to zero in the end. Therefore,

{\rm tr}\big(\rho\Phi(f_{1})\cdots\Phi(f_{n})\big)=(-i)^{n}\partial^{n}_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\big|_{0}\ e^{\sum_{k<l}t_{k}t_{l}q_{kl}},\quad q_{kl}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\big({\rm Re}\langle f_{k},\mathcal{C}f_{l}\rangle+i{\rm Im}\langle f_{k},f_{l}\rangle\big).

Next,

\partial^{n}_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\big|_{0}e^{\sum_{k<l}t_{k}t_{l}q_{kl}}=\partial^{n}_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\big|_{0}\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{1}{r!}\Big(\sum_{k<l}t_{k}t_{l}q_{kl}\Big)^{r}

is nonzero only if
n
is even and
r=n/2
, because the
t
appear in pairs. Hence

{\rm tr}\big(\rho\Phi(f_{1})\cdots\Phi(f_{n})\big)=(-1)^{n/2}\partial^{n}_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\big|_{0}\frac{1}{(n/2)!}\Big(\sum_{k<l}t_{k}t_{l}q_{kl}\Big)^{n/2}.

(U.3)

The product of the sums leads to polynomials in the
t
and only those terms in which the polynomial is
t_{1}\cdots t_{n}
are nonzero after taking the derivative. We must thus choose in each sum (factor) one pair
k<l
in such a way that all pairs make up the indices
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The associated value to each choice is

q_{k_{1},l_{1}}\cdots q_{k_{n/2}l_{n/2}}.

(U.4)

As the order in which we choose the pairs does not matter for the resulting (‘commutative’) value (
U.4
), we get a multiplicity
(n/2)!
for each such value. This removes the prefactor
1/{(n/2)!}
in (
U.3
). We may list the factors such that
k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots
and the order
k_{j}<l_{j}
is imposed by (
U.3
). Summing over all such arrangements thus yields the value of (
U.3
). Finally, we note that
-q_{kl}={\rm tr}\big(\rho\,\Phi(f_{k})\Phi_{f}(l)\big)
.
We have derived
Wick’s theorem
, stating that

{\rm tr}\big(\rho\Phi(f_{1})\cdots\Phi(f_{n})\big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle 0&\mbox{for $n$ odd}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}{\rm tr}\Big(\rho\,\Phi(f_{\pi(2j-1)})\Phi(f_{\pi(2j)})\Big)&\mbox{for $n$ even}\end{array}\right.

(U.5)

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the set of permutations
\pi
satisfying (
S.41
).

We note that if in (
U.1
) the commutation relation is replaced by
W(f)W(g)=W(f+g)
, then the above derivation and the result (
U.5
) hold in the exact same way. This corresponds to a commutative, or classical representation of the canonical commutation relations.

It is sometimes useful to state Wick’s theorem for operators more general than field operators (and indeed, this is what we do in the proof of the theorem in the letter). Since both sides in (
U.5
) are linear in each
\Phi(f_{j})
we may replace each of those field operators by any linear combination,

\Phi(f_{j})\mapsto X_{j}\equiv\sum_{r=1}^{R}\xi_{r,j}\Phi(g_{r,j}),

for any
\xi_{r,j}\in\mathbb{C}
,
and the relation (
U.5
) stays preserved,

{\rm tr}\big(\rho X_{1}\cdots X_{n}\big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle 0&\mbox{for $n$ odd}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}{\rm tr}\Big(\rho\,X_{\pi(2j-1)}X_{\pi(2j)}\Big)&\mbox{for $n$ even}\end{array}\right.

(U.6)

A particular example are creation and annihilation operators,

a^{\dagger}(f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(\Phi(f)-i\Phi(if)\Big),\qquad a(f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(\Phi(f)+i\Phi(if)\Big),

for which we have

{\rm tr}\big(\rho a^{\sigma_{1}}(f_{1})\cdots a^{\sigma_{n}}(f_{n})\big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle 0&\mbox{for $n$ odd}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}{\rm tr}\Big(\rho\,a^{\sigma_{\pi(2j-1)}}(f_{\pi(2j-1)})a^{\sigma_{\pi(2j)}}(f_{\pi(2j)})\Big)&\mbox{for $n$ even}\end{array}\right.

(U.7)

where
\sigma_{j}\in\{1,-1\}
and

a^{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a,&\sigma=1\\
a^{\dagger},&\sigma=-1\end{array}\right..

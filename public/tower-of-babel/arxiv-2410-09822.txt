).
Under hypotheses (i)–(v) we have also from the uniqueness of NFPE (

.

Then

Proof.

By Itô’s formula, both functions

Remark 5.2
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
5.1
is also the fact that the family of marginal probability laws
\{t\to\mathbb{P}\circ X^{-1}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, where
X_{t}
is the unique probabilistically weak solution to the McKean–Vlasov SDE (
5.1
), is a nonlinear Markov process on
{\mathcal{B}}({\Omega})
(see Definition 5.1 and Theorem 5.3 in
[
8
]
).

We recall that (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
, p. 195) that a
weak probability solution

X_{t}
to (
5.1
)–(
5.2
) is said to be a
strong solution
if it is a measurable function of the Brownian motion
W_{t}
or, equivalently, if it is adapted with respect to the completed natural filtration
({\mathcal{F}}^{W_{t}}_{t})_{t>0}
of
W_{t}
. It turns out that under appropriate conditions on
b
,
D
and
K
, the weak probability solution
X_{t}
is a strong solution to the McKean–Vlasov equation (
5.1
)–(
5.2
). Namely, we have

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that, besides hypotheses
(i)–(v)
, the following conditions hold

\displaystyle t\to\displaystyle\frac{\beta(r)}{r}\in C^{1}_{b}[0,{\infty}),\ D\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}};{\mathbb{R}^{d}}),

(5.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x_{i}}\,K\in L^{2}(B^{c}_{1};{\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\ i=1,...,d.

(5.7)

Then, the weak probability solution
X_{t}
to
(
5.1
)–(
5.2
)
is the unique strong solution to the McKean–Vlasov equation
(
5.1
)–(
5.2
)
.

Proof.

By the restricted Yamada–Watanabe theorem (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
) the conclusion follows if one shows that the stochastic differential equation (
5.1
) with
u
given by Theorem
2.1
has the path uniqueness property for the weak probability solutions, that is, for every pair of weak solutions
(X,W,({\Omega},\mathbb{P},{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t>0})),

(\widetilde{X},W,({\Omega},\mathbb{P},{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t>0})),
with
X(0)=\widetilde{X}(0)
, we have
\sup|X(t)-\widetilde{X}(t)|=0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. In our case, this means the pathwise uniqueness of weak probability solutions to the stochastic differential equation

\begin{array}[]{l}dX_{t}=f_{1}(t,X_{t})dt+f_{2}(t,X_{t})dW_{t}\\
X(0)=X_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\end{array}

(5.8)

in the stochastic basis
({\Omega},{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P},W_{t})
, where

\begin{array}[]{rcll}f_{1}(t,x)&\equiv&D(x)b(u(t,x))+(K*u(t,\cdot))(x),\ t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\\
f_{2}(t,x)&\equiv&\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{2\beta(u(t,x))}{u(t,x)}},\ t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\end{array}

and
u
is the solution to the Fokker–Planck equation (
1.1
) given by Theorem
2.1
.
The main difficulty for the proof of the path uniqueness for equation (
5.8
) is that the coefficients
f_{i}
,
i=1,2,
are not Lipschitz with respect to the spatial variable
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
but are only in
H^{1}
.
We have indeed for
D_{i}=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x_{i}},\ i=1,...,d,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|D_{i}f_{2}(t,x)|&=&\left|\left(\displaystyle\frac{\beta(u)}{u}\right)^{\prime}\left(\frac{\beta(u)}{u}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}D_{i}u(t,x)\right|\leq C|D_{i}u(t,x)|,\\
&&\hfill\forall(t,x)\in(0,{\infty})\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|D_{i}(D(x)b(u(t,x)))|&\leq&|D|_{\infty}|b(u)|_{\infty}+|D|_{\infty}|b^{\prime}(u)|_{\infty}|\nabla u(t,x)|\\
&\leq&C|D_{i}u(t,x)|,\ (t,x)\in(0,{\infty})\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{l}D_{i}(K*u(t))(x)=\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}K(\bar{x})D_{i}u(t,x-\bar{x})dx+\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}_{1}}D_{i}K(\bar{x})u(t-\bar{x})dx\\
\qquad+\displaystyle\int_{[|\bar{x}|=1]}K(\bar{x})u(t,x-\bar{x})d\bar{x},\ i=1,...,d,\,(t,x)\in(0,{\infty})\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.\end{array}

Taking into account that, as seen by Theorem
2.1
,
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})
, these formulae imply that

\begin{array}[]{ll}|D_{i}f_{1}(t,\cdot)|_{2}\leq&C|D_{i}u(t,\cdot)|_{2}+\|K\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}|D_{i}u(t,\cdot)|_{2}\\
&+\|D_{i}K\|_{L^{1}(B^{c}_{1})}|u(t,\cdot)_{2}+C\|K\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}|u(t,u(t))|_{\infty},\\
&\hfill\forall t\in[0,T].\end{array}

By the above estimations, we see that
f_{1},f_{2}
have the
H^{1}
-Sobolev regularity with respect to the spatial variable
x
and so the pathwise uniqueness of the weak solution
X
to (
5.8
) follows by the same argument as that used for the uniqueness of Lagrangian flows generated by nonlinear differential equations in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
with coefficients in
L^{1}(0,T;H^{1})
(see Theorem 2.9 in
[
14
]
). (See also
[
18
]
for the proof of the pathwise uniqueness of weak solutions to a SDE under similar conditions on coefficients.)

Interior regularity for fully fractional evolution equations and a priori estimates on unbounded domains
Wenxiong Chen, Yahong Guo, Congming Li

Abstract
In this paper, we study the fully fractional heat equation involving the master operator: ( ∂ t − Δ ) s u ( x , t ) = f ( x , t ) in ℝ n × ℝ . (\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=f(x,t)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}. We derive interior Hölder and Schauder estimates for non-negative solutions that depend solely on local data. Owing to the non-locality of the operator, earlier work required uniform global bounds to control higher local norms–an assumption that fails during blow-up and rescaling on unbounded domains , where rescaled solutions may develop arbitrarily large tails. After introducing some new ideas and novel techniques, such as using a directional perturbation average to derive a key estimate for the fully fractional heat kernel, we are able to employ local bounds of solutions to replace global bounds to control their higher local norms. Based on these new estimates, we are capable to apply the blow-up and rescaling arguments to establish a priori estimates for solutions to a broader class of nonlocal equations in unbounded domains, such as ( ∂ t − Δ ) s u ( x , t ) = b ( x , t ) | ∇ x u ( x , t ) | q + f ( x , u ( x , t ) ) in ℝ n × ℝ . (\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=b(x,t)|\nabla_{x}u(x,t)|^{q}+f(x,u(x,t))\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}. Under appropriate conditions, we prove that all nonnegative solutions, along with their spatial gradients, are uniformly bounded.

Introduction

In this paper, we first establish new regularity estimates for nonnegative solutions for the following master equations

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=f(x,t)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

Using these regularity results, we then apply blow-up and rescaling analysis to derive a priori estimates for solutions to the nonlinear equations

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=b(x,t)|\nabla_{x}u(x,t)|^{q}+f(x,u(x,t))\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.2)

Here the fully fractional heat operator
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}
, initially introduced by M. Riesz in
[
56
]
, is a nonlocal pseudo-differential operator of order
2s
in the spatial variables and of order
s
in the time variable. It is defined by the following singular integral

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t):=C_{n,s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x,t)-u(y,\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}+1+s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!\tau,

(1.3)

where
0<s<1
, the integral in
y
is in the sense of Cauchy principal value, and
the normalization constant is given by

C_{n,s}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}|\Gamma(-s)|}

with
\Gamma(\cdot)
denoting the Gamma function.

This operator is inherently nonlocal in both space and time, as the value of
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u
at any point
(x,t)
depends on the values of
u
over the entire spatial domain
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all past times before
t
.

We say that
u
is a classical entire solution of (
1.1
) if

u(x,t)\in C^{2s+\epsilon,s+\epsilon}_{x,\,t,\,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})

for some
\varepsilon>0
.
This condition ensures that the singular integral in (
1.3
) converges. Here,
the slowly increasing function space
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}):=\left\{u(x,t)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})\mid\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x,\tau)|e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}}{1+(t-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}+1+s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!\tau<\infty,\,\,\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

The definition of the local parabolic Hölder space
C^{2s+\epsilon,s+\epsilon}_{x,\,t,\,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
will be specified in Section 2.

It is worth noting that the fractional powers of heat operator
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}

reduces to the classical heat operator
\partial_{t}-\Delta
as

(cf.
[
32

). Moreover,
when the space-time nonlocal operator
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=(-\Delta)^{s}u(x),

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C_{n,s}PV\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy.

The regularity estimates

Let

Q_{r}(x^{o},t_{o})=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid|x-x^{o}|<r,\;|t-t_{o}|<r^{2}\}

denote the parabolic cylinder of size
r
centered at
(x^{o},t_{o})
.

We use the standard notation for parabolic Hölder space. A function
u(x,t)
is said to belong to
C_{x,t}^{\alpha,\beta}
if
u
is
C^{\alpha}
in the spatial variable
x
and
C^{\beta}
in the time variable
t
. If
\alpha<1
,
C^{\alpha}
is the usual Hölder space.
If
1<\alpha<2
,
C^{\alpha}
denotes the space of bounded functions whose first derivatives belong to
C^{\alpha-1}
, and so on. For more precise definitions, please see Section 2.

Regarding regularity estimates for master equation (
1.1
), Stinga and Torrea
[
57
]
, among other results, established the following local Hölder estimate (
0<s<1/2
case)

\|u\|_{C_{x,t}^{2s,s}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,4)})}\right),

(1.5)

and Schauder estimate:

\|u\|_{C_{x,t}^{2s+\alpha,s+\alpha/2}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{C_{x,t}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\bf{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,4)})}\right).

(1.6)

A similar Hölder estimate was obtained by Caffarelli and Silvestre in
[
13
]
.

In order to circumvent the nonlocality of the master operator, the authors in
[
57
]
relied on the extension method introduced by Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
12
]
.

However, due to the nonlocal nature of the fully fractional heat operator, these results appear significantly weaker than those obtained for the
classical heat equation

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)u(x,t)=f(x,t),

where the global norm
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,4))}
on the right hand sides of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) can be replaced by a weaker local norm
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}(0,0))}
.

More critically, the presence of such global norms on the right hand sides of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) makes it impossible to apply the regularity estimates to the well-known blow-up and rescaling arguments to establish a priori estimates for fractional equations on unbounded domains.

As an illustration, let us compare a simple problem of local nature

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)u(x,t)=u^{p}(x,t),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

(1.7)

with its nonlocal counter part:

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=u^{p}(x,t),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.8)

Here the right hand side can actually be replaced by a more general nonlinearity
f(x,u,\nabla u)
.

To obtain an a priori estimate–namely, to show that all nonnegative solutions are uniformly bounded–one typically employs a blow-up and rescaling argument, which proceeds as follows.

If the solutions are not uniformly bounded, then there exist a sequence of solutions
\{u_{k}\}
and a sequence of points
\{(x^{k},t_{k})\}
,
such that
u_{k}(x^{k},t_{k})\to\infty
. Note that this
u_{k}(x^{k},t_{k})
may not be chosen to be the maximum of
u_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, because each
u_{k}
may be unbounded. However, similar to the well-known
doubling lemma
, one can find a nearby point
(\bar{x}^{k},\bar{t}_{k})
and some small
\lambda_{k}
, such that

u_{k}(x,t)\leq C\,u_{k}(\bar{x}^{k},\bar{t}_{k})\equiv C\,m_{k},\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in Q_{\lambda_{k}R}(\bar{x}^{k},\bar{t}_{k}).

Here
R>0
can be chosen arbitrary large.

Upon re-scaling

v_{k}(x,t)=\frac{1}{m_{k}}u_{k}(\lambda_{k}x+x^{k},\lambda^{2}_{k}t+t_{k}),

the new sequence of functions
v_{k}
becomes bounded and satisfy similar equations on certain parabolic cylinders
Q_{R}(0,0)
. However, outside
Q_{R}(0,0)
, no control over
v_{k}
is available, and it may even become unbounded.

For equation (
1.7
) of local nature
:

The boundedness of
\{v_{k}\}
within
Q_{R}(0,0)
is
sufficient
to ensure boundedness of its higher derivatives. This allows one to extract a subsequence of
\{v_{k}\}
that converges to a function
v
, which is a bounded solution of the same equation. By using known results on the nonexistence of bounded positive solutions (cf. Quittner
[
53
]
), one can derive a contradiction and thereby establish the desired a priori estimate. Specifically, Quittner showed that no
bounded
positive solutions for (
1.7
) when
1<p<\frac{n+2}{n-2}
. Using this result in combination with the blow-up and rescaling arguments, the non-existence of
unbounded
positive solutions can also be deduced.

For equation (
1.8
) of nonlocal nature
:

In contrast, the available regularity estimates, such as (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), reveal that the boundedness of
\{v_{k}\}
on
Q_{R}(0,0)
is
insufficient
to guarantee the bound on its higher norms. Instead, to bound these norms,
\{v_{k}\}

must be bounded globally across the entire
space.
This condition cannot be satisfied since no information about the behavior of
v_{k}
beyond
Q_{R}(0,0)
is available during the rescaling process. This presents a substantial difficulty when attempting to use blow-up and rescaling arguments for nonlocal equations on unbounded domains.

These challenges lead to the following natural question:

Can one replace the global norm
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,4))}
with the weaker local norm
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}(0,0))}
to bound the higher norm of
u
under certain conditions?

Addressing this challenging question is one of the main objectives of the present paper. Rather than relying on the
extension method
, we approach this issue directly through integral representations of solutions . After introducing some brand new ideas and through careful analysis, we answer this question affirmatively for nonnegative solutions
u(x,t)
to master equation (
1.1
) with
f(x,t)\geq 0
. We establish the following regularity estimates:

Theorem 1
.

(Hölder estimates) Assume that both
f(x,t)
and
u(x,t)
are bounded in
Q_{2}(0,0)
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\|u\|_{C^{2s,s}_{x,t}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\,\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Q_{2}(0,0)})}\right),\;\;\mbox{ if }s\neq 1/2.

\|u\|_{C^{logL,s}_{x,t}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\,\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Q_{2}(0,0)})}\right),\;\;\mbox{ if }s=1/2.

Theorem 2
.

(Schauder estimates) Assume that
f\in C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q_{2}(0,0))
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\|u\|_{C^{2s+\alpha,s+\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\,\left(\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Q_{2}(0,0)})}\right),\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha<1\mbox{ or }1<2s+\alpha<2;

\|u\|_{C^{\log L,s+\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\,\left(\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Q_{2}(0,0)})}\right),\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha=1;

\|u\|_{C^{1+\log L,\log L}_{x,t}(Q_{1}(0,0))}\leq C\,\left(\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q_{2}(0,0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Q_{2}(0,0)})}\right),\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha=2.

Here
C^{Log\,L}
is the standard “Log-Lipschitz” space and one can find the precise definition right before the statement of Theorem

5
.

We believe that this is the first instance where the local higher norms of solutions to a
nonlocal
equation can be effectively controlled by their local norms. This can be viewed as a breakthrough in the area, which makes it possible to employ the blow-up and re-scaling arguments to
establish a priori estimates for solutions to a family of similar nonlocal equations in unbounded domains, as the readers will see in a moments later.

Remark 1
.

From the proofs in Section 3 and 4, it is evident that similar arguments apply to higher derivatives estimates. Here, we present only the results necessary for our blow-up and rescaling analysis.

To obtain such regularity estimates, we first prove the equivalence between the pseudo differential equation and an integral equation.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
f(x,t)\geq 0
and
u(x,t)
is a nonnegative classical solution of (
1.1
), then it satisfies the integral equation

u(x,t)=c+\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau,

(1.9)

where

G(x-y,t-\tau)=\frac{C_{n,s}}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}

is the fundamental solution, or the Green’s function associated with the master operator
(\partial_{t}-\mbox{$\triangle$})^{s}
, and
c
is a nonnegative constant.

Since the constant
c
in (
1.9
) does not influence either the Hölder norm or the derivatives of
u
, we may, without loss of generality, assume for the remainder of the paper that
c=0
and hence

u(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau,

(1.10)

For simplicity of notation, we denote

Q=Q_{2}(0,0),\mbox{ and }\;\;\tilde{Q}=Q_{1}(0,0)\subset\subset Q.

We split
u(x,t)
into two parts
u(x,t)=v(x,t)+w(x,t)
as in the following.

Write

f_{Q}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(x,t),&(x,t)\in Q\\
0,&(x,t)\in Q^{c}.\end{array}\right.

Denote

w(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

Then
w(x,t)
satisfies the nonhomogeneous equation in
Q
:

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}w(x,t)=f(x,t),\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in Q.

While the homogeneous part can be expressed by

v(x,t):=u(x,t)-w(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

with
f_{Q^{c}}:=f-f_{Q}
. Apparently,
v
is a solution of the homogeneous equation in
Q
:

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v(x,t)=0,\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in Q.

We estimate
v(x,t)
and
w(x,t)
separately. We first obtain

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
v
is bounded in
Q
, then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\|v\|_{C_{x,t}^{2,\alpha}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

Remark 2
.

Using similar arguments as in our proof (see Section 3), it can be shown that the
C_{x,t}^{2,\alpha}
norm on the left hand side can be replaced by
C_{x,t}^{k,\alpha}
norm for any integer
k
. Nonetheless, this
C_{x,t}^{2,\alpha}
norm is sufficient for our blow-up and rescaling analysis.

New ideas involved in the proof

To estimate the derivatives of
v(x,t)
, say
v_{x_{i}}
, in terms of
v(x,t)
itself, we encounter a significant challenge.
From the definition

v(x,t)=C_{n,s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}dyd\tau,

it follows that

v_{x_{i}}(x,t)=C_{n,s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{2(t-\tau)}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}dyd\tau.

Comparing these two expressions, it appears that, in order to control
v_{x_{i}}
in terms of
v
, we need to establish the inequality

\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{2(t-\tau)}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\leq Ce^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}.

(1.11)

However, this seems impossible because the extra term
\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{2(t-\tau)}
on the left hand side is clearly unbounded. To overcome this difficulty, we introduce an innovative idea. Instead of attempting to control
|v_{x_{i}}(x,t)|
directly in terms of
v(x,t)
at the same point
(x,t)
, we find finitely many nearby points
(x^{j},t)
to accomplish this task:

|v_{x_{i}}(x,t)|\leq C\sum_{j}^{2^{n}}v(x^{j},t).

To illustrate this approach, let us consider the case of two dimensions (
n=2
).

For each fixed
x\in B_{1}(0)
, we take
x
as the center and divide the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into four quadrants
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}.

On each quadrant
I_{j}
, choose the point
x^{j}
as the intersection of
\partial B_{1/\sqrt{2}}(x)
and the diagonal of
I_{j},\,j=1,\cdots,4
:

x^{j}=x+\eta_{j},\mbox{ with }\eta_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\cos\frac{\pi}{4},\sin\frac{\pi}{4}),\eta_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\cos\frac{3\pi}{4},\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}),\cdots.

We will verify that for all
y\in B_{2}^{c}(0)\cap I_{j}
it holds

|y-x|^{2}\geq|y-x^{j}|^{2}+{\bf\frac{1}{2}|y-x|}.

This perturbation produced a term
{\bf e^{\frac{c|x-y|}{t-\tau}}}
on the right hand side, which can be used exactly to control the extra term
{\bf\frac{|x-y|}{t-\tau}}
on the left hand side of (
1.11
).

Using this inequality, we are able to control the left hand side of (
1.11
) by the right hand side at point
x^{j}
:

\frac{|y-x|}{t-\tau}e^{-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\leq Ce^{-\frac{|y-x^{j}|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}},\;\;\mbox{ for all }y\in B_{2}^{c}(0)\cap I_{j}.

Consequently
for all
(x,t)\in\tilde{Q}
, we obtain

|v_{x_{i}}(x,t)|\leq C\sum_{j}^{4}v(x^{j},t)\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

For further details, please refer to Figure 1 and arguments in Section 3.

The above approach can be interpreted as a
directional perturbation average
. At a given point
x
, it is impossible to control the derivative of
v
solely by the value of
v
itself. However, after making directional perturbations from
x
to
x^{j}=x+\eta_{j}
for
j=1,2,\cdots,2^{n}
along directions
\eta_{j}
, and then taking the average, we are able to realize such a control. Such an estimate is essentially performed on the fully fractional heat kernel
G(x-y,t-\tau)
. For convenience of future applications, we summarized this as
follows:

Lemma 1

( Fully fractional heat kernel estimates )
.

In each quadrant
I_{j}
of
R^{n}
, there is a vector
\eta_{j}\in\partial B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}(0)
along the diagonal direction such that

G(y,\tau)\leq{\bf{e^{-\frac{|y|}{n\tau}}}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2^{n}}G(y+{\eta}_{j},\tau),\forall\tau>0,y\in B_{1}^{c}(0).

(1.12)

Furthermore, for each
\tau>0,y\in B_{1}^{c}(0)
, it holds

D_{y}^{\alpha}G(y,\tau)\leq C\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2^{n}}G(y+{\eta}_{j},\tau),\forall\,|\alpha|=1\,or\,2,

(1.13)

and

\partial_{\tau}G(y,\tau)\leq C\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2^{n}}G(y+{\eta}_{j},\tau),

(1.14)

here
C=C(n)
is a universal constant.

This lemma will be validated in the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

We believe that this important heat kernel estimate will become a powerful tool in establishing regularity estimates, with potential applications to a variety of related problems.

To derive Hölder estimates for
w(x,t)
, we assume that
f\in L^{\infty}(Q)
, with the results depending on whether
2s=1
or not.
In the case
2s=1
,
w
belongs to the “Log-Lipshitz” space, which is defined as follows:
Let
g(x)
be a function of
x
. We say that
g
belongs to the “Log-Lipshitz” space

C^{\log L}(\Omega)
if

\displaystyle\|g\|_{C^{\log L}(\Omega)}=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,x\neq y}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y||\log\min\{|x-\bar{x}|,1/2\}|}<\infty.

Similarly, one can define
C^{k+\log L}
for positive integers
k
.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that
f
is bounded in
Q
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\|w\|_{C^{2s,s}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\;\;\mbox{ if }s\neq 1/2.

\|w\|_{C^{logL,s}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\;\;\mbox{ if }s=1/2.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
f\in C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\|w\|_{C^{2s+\alpha,s+\alpha/2}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)},\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha<1\mbox{ or }1<2s+\alpha<2;

\|w\|_{C^{1+\log L,\log L}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)},\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha=2.

Now Theorems

, and

Applications in blow-up and rescaling

Among their numerous applications, the regularity estimates presented in the previous subsection can be utilized to establish a priori estimates for solutions to nonlinear master equations via blow-up and re-scaling analysis.

Consider the equation

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=f(x,u(x,t)),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.15)

Theorem 7
.

Assume that
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.15
) and that the following conditions hold:

(f1)
f(x,\tau)
is uniformly Hölder continuous with respect to
x
and continuous with respect to
\tau
.

(f2) There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

0\leq f(x,\tau)\leq C_{0}(1+\tau^{p})\;\;\mbox{ uniformly for all $x$, and }

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{f(x,\tau)}{\tau^{p}}=K(x),\,\,1<p<\frac{n+2}{n+2-2s},

where
K(x)\in(0,\infty)
is uniformly continuous and

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{|x|\to\infty}K(x)=\bar{C}\in(0,\infty).

Then there exists a constant
C
, such that for all nonnegative solutions
u
, we have

u(x,t)\leq C\,\,\,\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

The above a priori estimate holds for all
0<s<1
.

In the case
1/2<s<1
, we can further establish estimates for more general master equations of the form

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=b(x)|\nabla_{x}u(x,t)|^{q}+f(x,u(x,t)),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

(1.16)

where
\nabla_{x}u
is the gradient of
u
with respect to
x
.

Theorem 8
.

Assume that

b(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is nonnegative,uniformly bounded and Hölder continuous and
f(x,\tau)
satisfies (f1) and (f2).

If
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
0<q<\frac{2sp}{2s+p-1},
then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

u(x,t)+|\nabla_{x}u|^{\frac{2s}{2s+p-1}}(x,t)\leq C,\;\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

for all nonnegative solutions
u(x,t)
of (
1.16
).

For related results concerning a priori estimates, asymptotic behavior, and blow-up rates of solutions for elliptic and parabolic equations, please see

The organization of this paper

In Section 2, we provide precise definitions the parabolic Hölder spaces. We then establish the equivalence between the pseudo differential equation and the integral equation, leading to a proof of Theorem

In Section 3, we focus on estimating

In Section 4, we derive both Hölder and Schauder estimates for

Section 5 is devoted to obtaining a priori estimates for nonnegative solutions to master equations (

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Parabolic Hölder spaces

We begin by stating the standard definition of parabolic Hölder space
C^{2\alpha,\alpha}_{x,\,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
(cf.
[
37
]
) as follows.

(i)

In the case
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
. We say that

u(x,t)\in C^{2\alpha,\alpha}_{x,\,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|u(x,t)-u(y,\tau)|\leq C\left(|x-y|+|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2\alpha}

for all
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t,\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

When
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1
, we say that

u(x,t)\in C^{2\alpha,\alpha}_{x,\,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}):=C^{1+(2\alpha-1),\alpha}_{x,\,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}),

if
u
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
t
uniformly with respect to
x
and its gradient
\nabla_{x}u
is
(2\alpha-1)
-Hölder continuous in
x
uniformly with respect to
t
and
(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})
-Hölder continuous in
t
uniformly with respect to
x
.

(iii)

While for
\alpha>1
, if

u(x,t)\in C^{2\alpha,\alpha}_{x,\,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}),

then it means that

\partial_{t}u,\,D^{2}_{x}u\in C^{2\alpha-2,\alpha-1}_{x,\,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}).

In addition, we can analogously define the local parabolic Hölder space
C^{2\alpha,\alpha}_{x,\,t,\,\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
, which involve local conditions instead of global ones.

2.2
Integral representation of solutions

Assume that
u(x,t)
is a nonnegative entire solution of the master equation

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=f(x,t),\;\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(2.1)

with a nonnegative function
f(x,t)
.

We prove Theorem
3
. That is, we show that
u
can be expressed as

u(x,t)=c+\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau,

(2.2)

where

G(x-y,t-\tau)=\frac{C_{n,s}}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}

is the fundamental solution, or the Green’s function associated with the master operator
(\partial_{t}-\mbox{$\triangle$})^{s}
, and
c
is a nonnegative constant.

proof of theorem
3
.

First, we need to ensure that the integral

\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

is well-define and convergent.

Let

Q_{R}=\{(x,t)\mid|x|\leq R,\,|t|\leq R^{2}\}

be the parabolic cylinder of size
R
. Let

\chi_{R}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&(x,t)\in Q_{R},\\
0,&(x,t)\in Q^{C}_{R}\end{array}\right.

be the characteristic function on
Q_{R}
.

Let

w_{R}(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f\chi_{R})(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau.

Then

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}w_{R}(x,t)=(f\chi_{R})(x,t),\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

and from the expression of the Green’s function, one can see that

w_{R}(x,t)\to 0,\;\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty\;\mbox{ or as }\;t\to-\infty.

(2.3)

Set
v_{R}(x,t)=u(x,t)-w_{R}(x,t)
. Then

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{R}(x,t)=f(x,t)-(f\chi_{R})(x,t)\geq 0,\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(2.4)

We show that

v_{R}(x,t)\geq 0,\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(2.5)

Otherwise, due to the non-negativeness of
u
and (
2.3
), there exists a negative minimum
(x^{o},t_{o})
of
v_{R}(x,t)
, and it follows that

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{R}(x^{o},t_{o}):=C_{n,s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v_{R}(x^{o},t_{o})-v_{R}(y,\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}+1+s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!\tau<0.

This contradicts (
2.4
). Therefore, (
2.5
) must be valid. Consequently,

u(x,t)\geq\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f\chi_{R})(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau.

Now let
R\to\infty
, we arrive at, for each
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,

u(x,t)\geq\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau\equiv\tilde{w}(x,t).

Let

\tilde{v}(x,t)=u(x,t)-\tilde{w}(x,t).

Then
\tilde{v}(x,t)\geq 0
, and

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{v}(x,t)=0,\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Now (
2.2
) is a consequence of the following Liouville theorem from
[
18
]
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
0<s<1
and
n\geq 2
.
Assume that
\tilde{v}(x,t)
is a solution of

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{v}(x,t)=0,\;\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

in the sense of distribution.

In the case
\frac{1}{2}<s<1
, we assume additionally that

\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\tilde{v}(x,t)}{|x|^{\gamma}}\geq 0\;(\mbox{or}\;\leq 0)\,\,\mbox{for some}\;0\leq\gamma\leq 1.

(2.6)

Then
\tilde{v}
must be constant.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3
. ∎

v_{x_{i}x_{j}}(x,t)\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
. ∎

3.2
Regularity of v in t

For each fixed
x\in B_{1}(0)
, we derive

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
x\in B_{1}(0),
such that

\|v(x,\cdot)\|_{C^{\alpha}_{t}((1,2))}\leq C\|v(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,3))},\ \ \forall\alpha\in(0,1).

Proof.

Fixed
x\in B_{1}(0),
for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1,2)),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{1}^{2}\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial t}\varphi(t)dt&=-\int_{1}^{2}v(x,t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)dt\\
&=-\int_{1}^{2}v(x,t)\left(\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\varphi(t)-\varphi(t-\delta)}{\delta}\right)dt\\
&=-\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\left\{\int_{1}^{2}v(x,t)\varphi(t)dt-\int_{1}^{2}v(x,t)\varphi(t-\delta)dt\right\}\\
&=-\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\left\{\int_{1}^{2}v(x,t)\varphi(t)dt-\int_{1-\delta}^{2-\delta}v(x,t+\delta)\varphi(t)dt\right\}\\
&=-\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\left\{\int_{1}^{2}[v(x,t)-v(x,t+\delta)]\varphi(t)dt+\left(\int_{2-\delta}^{2}-\int_{1-\delta}^{1}\right)v(x,t+\delta)\varphi(t)dt\right\}\\
&=-\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{v(x,t)-v(x,t+\delta)}{\delta}\varphi(t)dt.\end{aligned}

(3.16)

Here we have utilized the fact that

\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{2-\delta}^{2}v(x,t+\delta)\varphi(t)dt=\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1-\delta}^{1}v(x,t+\delta)\varphi(t)dt=0.

(3.17)

Indeed, by the
mean value theorem for integrals
, the boundedness of
v(x,t)
and the fact that
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1,2)),
we have

\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{2-\delta}^{2}v(x,t+\delta)\varphi(t)dt=\lim\limits_{\xi\to 2}v(x,\xi)\varphi(\xi)=0.

Similarly,

\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1-\delta}^{1}v(x,t+\delta)\varphi(t)dt=\lim\limits_{\xi\to 1}v(x,\xi)\varphi(\xi)=0.

These imply (
3.17
).

Now for any fixed
(x,t)\in B_{1}(0)\times(1,2):=\tilde{Q}
, substituting the expression

v(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

(3.18)

into the right hand side of (
3.16
), we derive

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{1}^{2}\frac{v(x,t+\delta)-v(x,t)}{\delta}\varphi(t)dt\\
=&\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1}^{2}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{t+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t+\delta-\tau)dyd\tau-\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau\right\}\varphi(t)dt\\
=&\int_{1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)\frac{G(x-y,t+\delta-\tau)-G(x-y,t-\tau)}{\delta}dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt\\
&+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t+\delta-\tau)dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt\\
:=&J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{aligned}

(3.19)

First, we want show that
J_{1}
is equivalent to the differentiation of
v(x,t)
with respect to
t
within the integral sign, that is,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{1}\to&\int_{1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt,\ \mbox{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.\end{aligned}

(3.20)

In fact, applying the Cauchy mean value theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{1}=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(x-y,t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt,\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\delta).\end{aligned}

Differentiate
G(x,t)
with respect to
t
,

\frac{\partial G(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{C_{n,s}}{{t}^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}(\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t^{2}}-\frac{\frac{n}{2}+1-s}{t}).

(3.21)

Then for
\tau<t,0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(x-y,t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)\right|\leq\frac{Ce^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}}}{{(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}^{n/2+1-s}}\left(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)^{2}}+\frac{1}{t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau}\right)\end{aligned}

(3.22)

Similar to the argument as in deriving the estimate of
v_{x_{i}}
, we estimate the right hand side of (
3.22
) in two possible cases.

Case 1.
y\in B_{2}^{c}(0),
then
|y-x|\geq 1.
We can choose
x^{j}
as above such that for each
j=1,2,...2^{n},
if
y\in I_{j},
by (
3.8
)(with
p=1,2
), it holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}}\left(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)^{2}}+\frac{1}{t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau}\right)\leq Ce^{-\frac{|x^{j}-y|^{2}}{4(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}}.\end{aligned}

(3.23)

Case 2.
y\in B_{2}(0),\tau\leq 0,
then

|x-y|\leq 3,t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau\geq 1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}}\left(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)^{2}}+\frac{1}{t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau}\right)\leq Ce^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}}.\end{aligned}

(3.24)

Substituting (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) into (
3.22
), we deduce

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(x-y,t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)\right|&\leq C\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2^{n}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x^{j}-y|^{2}}{4(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}}}{{(t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)}^{n/2+1-s}}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2^{n}}G(x^{j}-y,t+\delta^{\prime}-\tau)\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2^{n}}\{G(x^{j}-y,t-\tau)+G(x^{j}-y,t+1-\tau)\},\end{aligned}

(3.25)

with
x^{0}=x
.
In addition,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2^{n}}\{G(x^{j}-y,t-\tau)+G(x^{j}-y,t+1-\tau)\}dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt\\
&\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2^{n}}\int_{1}^{2}{v(x^{j},t+1)\varphi(t)dt+\int_{1}^{2}v(x^{j},t)}\varphi(t)dt\right)\\
&\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq C.\end{aligned}

(3.26)

Therefore, taking into account of (
3.25
), (
3.26
), and by the
Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem
, we verify (
3.20
). This implies that, as
\delta\to 0,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{1}\to&\int_{1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{C_{n,s}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)}{{(t-\tau)}^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\left[\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)^{2}}-\frac{\frac{n}{2}+1-s}{t-\tau}\right]dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt.\\
\end{aligned}

(3.27)

Next we claim

J_{2}\to 0,\ \mbox{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

(3.28)

Indeed, for any
0<\delta<1,
if
\tau\in(0,\delta)
, then
\tau+t\in(0,3)
, by
suppf_{Q^{c}}\subset Q^{c}
,

D:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{Q^{c}}(y,t+\tau)\neq 0\}\subset B_{2}^{c}(0).

Now for
\forall y\in D
,
|y-x|\geq 1
, by equality (
3.21
), we observe that
G(|x-y|,\delta)
is monotone increasing in
\delta
for
\delta\ll 1
. Thus, for sufficiently small
\delta>0,
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{2}&=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t+\delta-\tau)dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt\\
&=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau+t)G(x-y,\delta-\tau)dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau+t)G(x-y,\delta)dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt\\
&=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1}^{2}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau+t)G(x-y,\delta)\varphi(t)dtdyd\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1+\tau}^{2+\tau}f_{Q^{c}}(y,t)G(x-y,\delta)\varphi(t-\tau)dtdyd\tau\\
&\leq\sup_{t\in(1,2)}\varphi(t)\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1}^{\frac{5}{2}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,t)G(x-y,\delta)dtdyd\tau\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1}^{\frac{5}{2}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,t)G(x-y,\delta)dtdy:=J_{3}.\\
\end{aligned}

(3.29)

Therefore, it suffices to prove

J_{3}=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1}^{\frac{5}{2}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,\delta)d\tau dy\to 0,\ \mbox{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

(3.30)

In fact,

f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,\delta)=f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4\delta}}}{{\delta}^{n/2+1-s}}\to 0\ a.e.\ \mbox{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

Furthermore, there exists a point
t_{0}\in(\frac{5}{2},3)
such that for any
\tau\in(1,\frac{5}{2})
and sufficiently small
\delta>0
, it holds

f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,\delta)\leq f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t_{0}-\tau)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1}^{3}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t_{0}-\tau)d\tau dy\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{0}}f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t_{0}-\tau)d\tau dy\\
=&v(x,t_{0})\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.\end{aligned}

Hence, by the
Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem
, (
3.30
) is valid.

Together (
3.29
) with (
3.30
), we verified claim (
3.28
).

Then combining (
3.16
), (
3.19
),(
3.27
) and (
3.28
), we arrive at

\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial t}\varphi(t)dt=C_{n,s}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)}{{(t-\tau)}^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\left[\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)^{2}}-\frac{\frac{n}{2}+1-s}{t-\tau}\right]dyd\tau\varphi(t)dt,

which implies that

\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial t}=C_{n,s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f_{Q^{c}}(y,\tau)}{{(t-\tau)}^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\left[\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)^{2}}-\frac{\frac{n}{2}+1-s}{t-\tau}\right]dyd\tau,a.e.(x,t)\in\tilde{Q}.

Now similar to the argument for the estimate of
v_{x_{i}x_{j}}
in (
3.11
), we deduce that the weak derivative of
v(x,t)
with respect to the variable
t
is controlled by
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}
, that is,

\Bigg|\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial t}\Bigg|\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

Therefore, for any fixed
x\in B_{1}(0),

v(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,\infty}((1,2)),
by the sobolev embedding theorem, we deduce that
v(x,\cdot)\in C_{t}^{0,1}((1,2))\subset C_{t}^{\alpha}((1,2)),\,\forall\alpha\in(0,1),
and

\|v(x,\cdot)\|_{C_{t}^{\alpha}((1,2))}\leq C\|v(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,3))}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
. ∎

Now Theorem
3.1
is a direct consequence of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
.

4
Estimates of the nonhomogeneous part w(x,t)

As in the previous section, we still denote

Q\equiv B_{2}(0)\times(0,3),\mbox{ and }\;\;\tilde{Q}=B_{1}(0)\times(1,2)\subset\subset Q.

Let

f_{Q}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(x,t),&(x,t)\in Q\\
0,&(x,t)\in Q^{c},\end{array}\right.

and

w(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{Q}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

where

G(x-y,t-\tau)=\frac{C_{n,s}}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}e^{\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}

Since
w(x,t)
satisfies the nonhomogeneous equation

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}w(x,t)=f(x,t),

we refer it as the nonhomogeneous part.

In this section, we estimate Hölder and Schauder norms of
w(x,t)
on
\tilde{Q}
in terms of
L^{\infty}
and Hölder norms of
f(x,t)
on the larger cylinder
Q
. We prove

Theorem 4.1
.

(Hölder Estimates) Assume that
f
is bounded in
Q
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\|w\|_{C^{2s,s}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\;\;\mbox{ if }s\neq 1/2.

\|w\|_{C^{logL,s}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\;\;\mbox{ if }s=1/2.

Theorem 4.2
.

(Schauder Estimates) Assume that
f\in C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\|w\|_{C^{2s+\alpha,s+\alpha/2}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)},\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha<1\mbox{ or }1<2s+\alpha<2;

\|w\|_{C^{\log L,s+\alpha/2}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)},\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha=1;

\|w\|_{C^{1+\log L,\log L}_{x,t}(\tilde{Q})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}_{x,t}(Q)},\;\;\;\mbox{ if }\;2s+\alpha=2.

4.1
The bounded-ness of w

We first show

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
f(x,t)
is bounded in
Q
. Then there exists a constant
C
, independent of
x\in B_{1}(0)
and
t\in(1,2)
, such that

|w(x,t)|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

(4.1)

Proof.

In fact

\displaystyle|w(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f_{Q}(y,\tau)|G(x-y,t-\tau)dyd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{2}(0)}\frac{C_{n,s}}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}dyd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-|z|^{2}}d\,z\,d\,\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-s}}d\,\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
. ∎

4.2
Regularity of w in x

4.2.1
Hölder estimate in the case
s\leq 1/2

We prove

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
f(x,t)
is bounded in
Q
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
t\in(1,2)
and
x,\bar{x}\in B_{1}(0)
, such that

|w(x,t)-w(\bar{x},t)|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}|x-\bar{x}|^{2s},\mbox{ if }s<1/2,\\
C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}|x-\bar{x}||\log\min\{|x-\bar{x}|,1/2\}|,\mbox{ if }s=1/2.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Proof.

It suffice to derive that

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{2}(0)}|G(x-y,t-\tau)-G(\bar{x}-y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau\leq C|x-\bar{x}|^{2s}.

(4.3)

Denote
\delta=|x-\bar{x}|
, and let
\eta
be the midpoint on the line segment between
x
and
\bar{x}
. Since
x,\bar{x}\in B_{2}(0)
, which is contained in
B_{3}(\eta)
, for convenience, we estimate the integrals in two parts of
B_{3}(\eta)
:
B_{\delta}(\eta)
and
B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)
respectively.

For
B_{\delta}(\eta)
part, we calculate each individual integral separately.

By making change of variables
\hat{\tau}=|x-y|^{2}/4(t-\tau)
,
the first integral

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\frac{C_{n,s}}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}dyd\tau

becomes

\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n-2s}}\int_{|x-y|^{2}/t}^{\infty}\hat{\tau}^{n/2-1-s}e^{-\hat{\tau}}d\hat{\tau}dy.

Under the assumption that
n\geq 2
, the integral

\int_{|x-y|^{2}/t}^{\infty}\hat{\tau}^{n/2-1-s}e^{-\hat{\tau}}d\hat{\tau}

is bounded. Hence

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}|G(x-y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau\leq C\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s}}dy\leq C_{1}\,\delta^{2s}.

(4.4)

Similarly

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}|G(\bar{x}-y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau\leq C\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\frac{1}{|\bar{x}-y|^{n-2s}}dy\leq C_{1}\,\delta^{2s}.

(4.5)

To estimate the integral of the difference on
B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)
, we apply the Mean Value Theorem and let
\xi
be some point between
x
and
\bar{x}
to arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)}|G(x-y,t-\tau)-G(\bar{x}-y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+1-s}}\int_{B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)}e^{-\frac{|\xi-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\frac{|\xi-y|}{t-\tau}|x-\bar{x}|\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C|x-\bar{x}|\int_{B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)}\frac{1}{|\xi-y|^{n+1-2s}}\int_{\frac{|\xi-y|^{2}}{t}}^{\infty}\tau^{n/2-s}e^{-\tau}d\tau dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\,\delta\,(C_{2}+\delta^{2s-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\,\delta^{2s},\;\;\;\mbox{ if }s<1/2.

In the case
s=1/2
, the last two lines of the above estimate becomes

\leq C_{1}\delta\,(C_{2}+|\log\delta|)\leq C_{3}\delta\,|\log\delta|.

Here we have used the fact that in
B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)
,
|\xi-y|
is equivalent to
|\eta-y|
, i.e.
there exist positive constants
c
and
C
such that

c|\eta-y|\leq|\xi-y|\leq C|\eta-y|

for any point
\xi
lying on the line segment between
x
and
\bar{x}
. We will use this fact several time in the following arguments.

Now from (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
) we arrive at (
4.3
) and hence obtain (
4.2
). This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
∎

4.2.2
Hölder estimate in the case
1/2<s<1

In this case, we derive

Lemma 4.3
.

that, there is a constant
C>0
independent of
t\in(1,2)
and
x,\bar{x}\in B_{1}(0)
,

\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{i}}(x,t)-\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{i}}(\bar{x},t)\right|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}|x-\bar{x}|^{2s-1}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Again it suffice to show

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{2}(0)}|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}G(x-y,t-\tau)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}G(\bar{x}-y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau\leq C|x-\bar{x}|^{2s-1}.

(4.8)

Again, set
\delta=|x-\bar{x}|
, and let
\eta
be the midpoint on the line segment between
x
and
\bar{x}
. We estimate the integrals in two parts of
B_{3}(\eta)
:
B_{\delta}(\eta)
and
B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)
respectively.

For
B_{\delta}(\eta)
part, we evaluate each individual integral separately.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}G(x-y,t-\tau)\right|\,d\,y\,d\,\tau

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\frac{|x-y|}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+2-s}}\,d\,\tau\,d\,y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s+1}}\int_{\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}^{\infty}\tau^{n/2-s}e^{-\tau}\,d\,\tau\,d\,y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\,\delta^{2s-1}.

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{\delta}(\eta)}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}G(\bar{x}-y,t-\tau)\right|\,d\,y\,d\,\tau\leq C\,\delta^{2s-1}.

(4.10)

To estimate the part on
B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)
, we apply the Mean Value Theorem to the difference in the integral on the left hand side of (
4.8
).

Denote

g_{i}(x)=e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}(x_{i}-y_{i}).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)}|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}G(x-y,t-\tau)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}G(\bar{x}-y,t-\tau)|dyd\tau

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+2-s}}\int_{B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)}|\nabla g_{i}(\xi)||x-\bar{x}|\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{n/2+2-s}}\int_{B_{3}(\eta)\setminus B_{\delta}(\eta)}e^{-\frac{|\xi-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\left(\frac{|\xi-y|^{2}}{t-\tau}+1\right)|x-\bar{x}|\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

I_{2}\leq\int_{\bar{t}}^{t}G(x-y,\tau)\,d\,y\,d\,\tau\leq C\,\int_{\bar{t}}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau^{1-s}}d\,\tau=C_{1}(t^{s}-\bar{t}^{s})\leq C_{2}\,|t-\bar{t}|.

(4.41)

Combining (
4.40
), (
4.41
), and (
4.39
), we establish (
4.38
) and thus complete the proof of Lemma
4.9
.
∎

4.4
Conclusion of the estimates on w(x,t)

Now Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
,
4.3
, and
4.8
imply Theorem
4.1
while Lemmas
4.4
,
4.5
,
4.7
, and
4.9
lead to Theorem
4.2
. This concludes the section.

5
A Priori Estimates of Solutions

The regularity results established in the previous sections make it possible to carry out the blowing up and re-scaling arguments.
Now in this section, we will apply these arguments to obtained a priori estimates of nonnegative solutions for a family of master equations in the whole space.

We first consider

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=f(x,u(x,t)),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(5.1)

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
5.1
) and

(f1)
f(x,\tau)
is uniformly Hölder continuous with respect to
x
and continuous with respect to
\tau
.

(f2)
0\leq f(x,\tau)\leq C_{0}(1+\tau^{p})
uniformly for all
x
, and

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{f(x,\tau)}{\tau^{p}}=K(x),\,\,1<p<\frac{n+2}{n+2-2s},

where
K(x)\in(0,\infty)
is uniformly continuous and
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{|x|\to\infty}K(x)=\bar{C}\in(0,\infty)
.
Then there exists a constant
C
, such that

u(x,t)\leq C,\,\,\,\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(5.2)

for all nonnegative solutions
u
of (
5.1
).

The above a priori estimate holds for all
0<s<1
.

Then in the case
1/2<s<1
, we study more general equation

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=b(x)|\nabla_{x}u(x,t)|^{q}+f(x,u(x,t)),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(5.3)

and establish a priori estimates for its non-negative solutions. Here
\nabla_{x}u
is the gradient of
u
with respect to
x
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that

b(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is nonnegative, uniformly bounded and Hölder continuous.

If
s>\frac{1}{2},
then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

u(x,t)+|\nabla_{x}u|^{\frac{2s}{2s+p-1}}(x,t)\leq C,\;\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

for all nonnegative solutions
u
.

5.1
Proof of Theorem
5.1

Proof.

We will prove Theorem
5.1
by a contradiction argument. Suppose (
5.2
) is violated, then there exist a sequence of solutions
u_{k}
of (
5.1
) and a sequence of points
(x^{k},t_{k})
such that

u_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})\to\infty,\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

For each fixed
R
, let

R_{k}:=2Ru_{k}^{-\frac{p-1}{2s}}(x_{k},t_{k}).

Denote
X=(x,t)
and
X_{k}=(x_{k},t_{k})
.
Define a function

S_{k}(X)=u_{k}(X)(R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|)^{\frac{2s}{p-1}},\,\,X\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k}).

Then there exists
A_{k}\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k})
such that

S_{k}(A_{k})=\mathop{\max}\limits_{B_{R_{k}}(X_{k})}S_{k}(X).

It follows that

\displaystyle u_{k}(X)\leq u_{k}(A_{k})\frac{(R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|)^{\frac{2s}{p-1}}}{(R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|)^{\frac{2s}{p-1}}},\,\,X\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k}).

(5.4)

Set

\lambda_{k}=(u_{k}(A_{k}))^{-\frac{p-1}{2s}}.

Taking
X=X_{k}
in (
5.4
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{u_{k}(X_{k})}{u_{k}(A_{k})}\leq\frac{(R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|)^{\frac{2s}{p-1}}}{R_{k}^{\frac{2s}{p-1}}},

(5.5)

which implies that

u_{k}(X_{k})\leq u_{k}(A_{k}).

Using the definition of
R_{k}
and
\lambda_{k}
, one gets from (
5.5
) that

\displaystyle 2R\lambda_{k}\leq R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|.

(5.6)

In addition, one can derive

\displaystyle R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|\leq 2(R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|),\,\,X\in B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k}).

(5.7)

Indeed, for any
X\in B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k})
, one has

|X-X_{k}|\leq|X-A_{k}|+|A_{k}-X_{k}|\leq R\lambda_{k}+R_{k}-2R\lambda_{k}\leq R_{k}

due to (
5.6
). It follows that
X\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k})

and thus

B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k})\subset B_{R_{k}}(X_{k}).
Now for any
X\in B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k})
, using (
5.6
), one derives

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 2(R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|)&\geq 2(R_{k}-(|X_{k}-A_{k}|+|X-A_{k}|))\\
&\geq 2(R_{k}-(|X_{k}-A_{k}|+R\lambda_{k}))\\
&\geq R_{k}-|X_{k}-A_{k}|+2R\lambda_{k}-2R\lambda_{k}\\
&\geq R_{k}-|X_{k}-A_{k}|.\end{aligned}

This implies (
5.7
).

The combination of (
5.4
) with (
5.7
) yields

\displaystyle u_{k}(X)\leq u_{k}(A_{k})2^{\frac{2s}{p-1}},\,\,x\in B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k}).

(5.8)

Let
\bar{R}=R/\sqrt{n+1}
and denote
A_{k}=(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{t}_{k})
, then it is obvious that the parabolic cylinder

Q_{\bar{R}\lambda_{k}}(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{t}_{k})=\{(x,t)\mid|x-\bar{x}_{k}|<\bar{R}\lambda_{k},\;\;|t-\bar{t}|<\bar{R}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}\}

is contained in

B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k})
for sufficiently large
k
.

We now re-scale the solutions as

v_{k}(x,t)=\frac{1}{u_{k}(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{t}_{k})}u_{k}(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k},\lambda_{k}^{2}t+\bar{t}_{k}),\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

Then it follows from (
5.1
) that
v_{k}
solves

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{k}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}f(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))

(5.9)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle F_{k}(x,v_{k}(x,t)),\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

Moreover, it follows from the definition of
v_{k}
and (
5.8
) that

\displaystyle v_{k}(0,0)=1

(5.10)

and

\displaystyle v_{k}(x,t)\leq 2^{\frac{2s}{p-1}},\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

(5.11)

Then due to (
5.11
) and the assumptions (f1) and (f2), one derives

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F_{k}(x,v_{k}(x,t))=&\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}f(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))\\
\leq&C_{0}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}\left(1+\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2sp}{p-1}}v_{k}^{p}(x,t)\right)\\
\leq&C_{0}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}+C_{0}v_{k}^{p}(x,t)\\
\leq&C_{1}.\end{aligned}

Applying Theorem
1
to (
5.9
) yields

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{C_{x,t}^{2s-\varepsilon,s}(Q_{\bar{R}/2}(0,0))}\leq C_{2}

for small
0<\varepsilon.

It follows that
F_{k}(x,v_{k}(x,t))
is Hölder continuous
in
Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0)).
Then applying Theorem
2
, we conclude that there exist some
\varepsilon>0

and
C_{3}
independent of
k
such that

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{C_{x,t}^{2s+\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon}(Q_{\bar{R}/4}(0,0))}\leq C_{3}.

(5.12)

The above uniform regularity estimates holds in
Q_{\bar{R}/4}(0,0)
for any
\bar{R}
. Let
\bar{R}\to\infty
, we conclude there exists
a converging subsequence of
\{v_{k}(x,t)\}
(stilled denoted by
\{v_{k}(x,t)\}
) that converges pointwise in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
to a function
v(x,t).

Taking limit in (
5.10
) yields

\displaystyle v(0,0)=1,

(5.13)

and

\displaystyle v(x,t)\leq 2^{\frac{2s}{p-1}},\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(5.14)

Moreover,
\{v_{k}(x,t)\}
converges to
v(x,t)
locally in
C_{x,t}^{2s+\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
, and hence
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{k}
converges pointwise in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v(x,t)
is uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Applying a result in
[
17
]
we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{k}(x,t)=(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v(x,t)-b,\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(5.15)

with some nonnegative constant
b
.

In addition, for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
if
\{\lambda_{k}x+a_{k}\}
is bounded, we assume that
\lambda_{k}x+a_{k}\to\bar{x}
by extracting a further
subsequence. Therefore, the assumption (f2) implies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}f(\lambda_{k}x+a_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))\\
=&(v_{k}(x,t))^{p}\frac{f(\lambda_{k}x+a_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))}{\left(\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t)\right)^{p}}\\
\to&K(\bar{x})v^{p}(x,t)\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,k\to\infty.\end{aligned}

(5.16)

This is still valid in the case
|\lambda_{k}x+a_{k}|\to\infty
with
|\bar{x}|=\infty
due to (f2).

Consequently, it follows from (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) that
v(x,t)
is a solution of

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v(x,t)=K(\bar{x})v^{p}(x,t)+b,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.17)

We show that
b
must be
0.

Let

F_{R}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}K(\bar{x})v^{p}(x,t)+b,&x\in Q_{R}(0,0)\\
0,&x\in Q_{R}^{C}(0,0).\end{array}\right.

v_{R}(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{R}(y,\tau)G(x-y,t-\tau)dy\,d\tau.

Then it is easy to see that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{R}(x,t)=F_{R}(x,t),\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\\
v_{R}(x,t)\to 0,\;\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty\mbox{ or as }t\to-\infty.\end{array}\right.

Denote

w_{R}(x,t)=v(x,t)-v_{R}(x,t),\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}w_{R}(x,t)\geq 0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\lim_{|x|\to\infty\mbox{ or }t\to-\infty}w_{R}(x,t)\geq 0.\end{cases}

By the maximum principle, we have

w_{R}(x,t)\geq 0,\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Now let
R\to\infty
, we arrive at

v(x,t)\geq\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(x-y,t-\tau)\left(K(\bar{x})v^{p}(y,\tau)+b\right)dy\,d\tau,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
b>0,
then the integral on the right hand side is greater or equal to

b\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(x-y,t-\tau)dy\,d\tau

which is obviously divergent. This contradicts the bounded-ness of
v(x,t)
.
Therefore, we must have
b=0
and

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v(x,t)=K(\bar{x})v^{p}(x,t),\,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(5.18)

Moreover, from (
5.13
) and the maximum principle,
one derives that

v(x,t)>0,\,\,\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Now we arrive at a bounded positive solution
v
of (
5.18
). This contradicts the non-existence results in
[
33
]
(Theorem 1.1) if
1<p<\frac{n+2}{n+2-2s}
.

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

∎

5.2
Proof of Theorem
5.2

Proof.

Now we assume that
1>s>\frac{1}{2}
and derive a priori estimate for nonnegative solutions of equation (
5.3
).
We show that

\displaystyle u(x,t)+|\nabla_{x}u|^{\frac{2s}{2s+p-1}}(x,t)\leq C,\,\,\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(5.19)

for all nonnegative solutions
u
. Again it will be acomplished by a blow-up and re-scaling argument.

If (
5.19
) is not valid, then there exist a sequence of solutions
u_{k}
,

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u_{k}(x,t)=b(x)|\nabla_{x}u_{k}|^{q}(x,t)+f(x,u_{k}(x,t)),\,\,\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

with

M_{k}(x_{k},t_{k}):=u_{k}^{\frac{p-1}{2s}}(x_{k},t_{k})+|\nabla_{x}u_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{2s+p-1}}(x_{k},t_{k})\to\infty\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,k\to\infty.

Again, for simplicity of notation, denote
X=(x,t)
and
X_{k}=(x_{k},t_{k})
.

For each fixed
R
, let

R_{k}:=2RM_{k}^{-1}(X_{k}).

To start the rescaling procedure and obtain upper bounds for solutions, we consider the following function

S_{k}(X)=\left(M_{k}(X)(R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|)\right)^{\frac{2s}{p-1}},\,\,X\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k}).

Then there exists a point
A_{k}\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k})
such that

S_{k}(A_{k})=\mathop{\max}\limits_{B_{R_{k}}(X_{k})}S_{k}(X).

It follows that

\displaystyle M_{k}(X)\leq M_{k}(A_{k})\frac{R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|}{R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|},\,\,X\in B_{R_{k}}(X_{k}).

(5.20)

Denote

\lambda_{k}=(M_{k}(A_{k}))^{-1}.

Taking
X=X_{k}
in (
5.20
), it yields

\displaystyle\frac{M_{k}(X_{k})}{M_{k}(A_{k})}\leq\frac{R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|}{R_{k}},

(5.21)

which implies that

M_{k}(X_{k})\leq M_{k}(A_{k}).

It follows from the definition of
R_{k}
and
\lambda_{k}
and (
5.21
) that

\displaystyle 2R\lambda_{k}\leq R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|.

(5.22)

In addition, using (
5.22
) and by a similar argument as in deriving (
5.7
), one derives

\displaystyle R_{k}-|A_{k}-X_{k}|\leq 2(R_{k}-|X-X_{k}|),\,\,X\in B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k})\subset B_{R_{k}}(X_{k}).

(5.23)

Combining (
5.20
) with (
5.23
) yields

\displaystyle M_{k}(X)\leq 2M_{k}(A_{k}),\,\,X\in B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k}).

(5.24)

Again Let
\bar{R}=R/\sqrt{n+1}
and denote
A_{k}=(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{t}_{k})
, then it is obvious that the parabolic cylinder

Q_{\bar{R}\lambda_{k}}(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{t}_{k})=\{(x,t)\mid|x-\bar{x}_{k}|<\bar{R}\lambda_{k},\;\;|t-\bar{t}|<\bar{R}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}\}

is contained in

B_{R\lambda_{k}}(A_{k})
for sufficiently large
k
.

We now re-scale the solutions as

v_{k}(x,t)=\frac{1}{M^{\frac{2s}{p-1}}_{k}(\bar{x}_{k},\bar{t}_{k})}u_{k}(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k},\lambda_{k}^{2}t+\bar{t}_{k}),\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

Then
v_{k}(x,t)
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{k}(x,t)\\
=&\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp-(2s+p-1)q}{p-1}}b(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k})|\nabla_{x}v_{k}|^{q}(x,t)+\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}f(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))\\
:=&F_{k}(x,v_{k}(x,t),\nabla_{x}v_{k}(x,t)),\,\,\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).\end{aligned}

(5.25)

Moreover, by the definition of
\lambda_{k}
and (
5.24
), one has

\displaystyle\left(v_{k}^{\frac{p-1}{2s}}+|\nabla_{x}v_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{2s+p-1}}\right)(0,0)=\lambda_{k}M_{k}(A_{k})=1

(5.26)

and

\displaystyle\left(v_{k}^{\frac{p-1}{2s}}+|\nabla_{x}v_{k}|^{\frac{p-1}{2s+p-1}}\right)(x,t)\leq 2,\,\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

(5.27)

It follows from (
5.27
) that
v_{k}
and
|\nabla_{x}v_{k}|
are uniformly bounded in
Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0)
.
Then for
k
large enough, one can deduce

\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp-(2s+p-1)q}{p-1}}b(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k})|\nabla_{x}v_{k}|^{q}(x,t)\leq C_{8},\,\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}f(\lambda_{k}x+\bar{x}_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))\\
\leq&C_{0}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}\left(1+\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2sp}{p-1}}(v_{k}(x,t))^{p}\right)\\
\leq&C_{0}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}+C_{0}(v_{k}(x,t))^{p}\\
\leq&C_{9},\end{aligned}

where we have used the assumptions (f1), (f2), and
0<q<\frac{2sp}{2s+p-1}.

As a result, there exists a constant
C_{10}>0
independent of
k
such that

0\leq F_{k}(x,v_{k}(x,t),\nabla_{x}v_{k}(x,t))\leq C_{10},\,\,\,(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}}(0,0).

It follows
from Theorem
1
that there exists a positive constant
C_{11}
independent of
k
such that

\|v_{k}\|_{C_{x,t}^{2s-\varepsilon,s}(Q_{\bar{R}/2}(0,0))}\leq C_{11}

for small
\varepsilon>0.

Therefore
F_{k}(x,v_{k}(x,t),\nabla_{x}v_{k}(x,t))
in (
5.25
) is Hölder continuous for
(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{R}/2}(0,0)
due to
s>\frac{1}{2}.
By virtue of Theorem
2
, there exist some positive constant
\varepsilon
and
C_{12}
which are independent of
k
such that

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{C_{x,t}^{2s+\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon}(Q_{\bar{R}/4}(0,0))}\leq C_{12}.

(5.28)

The above uniform regularity estimates holds in
Q_{\bar{R}/4}(0,0)
for any
\bar{R}
. Let
\bar{R}\to\infty
, we conclude there exists
a converging subsequence of
\{v_{k}(x,t)\}
(stilled denoted by
\{v_{k}(x,t)\}
) that converges point-wise in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
to a function
v(x,t).

Moreover,
\{v_{k}(x,t)\}
converges locally in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Applying a result in
[
17
]

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v_{k}(x,t)=(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}v(x,t)-b,\;\;\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

with some nonnegative constant

.

), we obtain

\displaystyle v^{\frac{p-1}{2s}}(0,0)+|\nabla_{x}v|^{\frac{p-1}{2s+p-1}}(0,0)=1.

Since

and

are uniformly bounded in

\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp-(2s+p-1)q}{p-1}}b(\lambda_{k}x+a_{k})|\nabla_{x}v_{k}|^{q}(x,t)\to 0\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,k\to\infty,

While similar to the argument as in the previous subsection, one can derive that

\lambda_{k}^{\frac{2sp}{p-1}}f(\lambda_{k}x+a_{k},\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2s}{p-1}}v_{k}(x,t))\to K(\bar{x})v^{p}(x,t)\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,k\to\infty.

(5.31)

Consequently, combining (

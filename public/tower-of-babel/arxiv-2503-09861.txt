Abstract
. We provide Green’s function estimates for parabolic operators on polyhedrons and polyhedral cones in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . These estimates incorporate mixed weights, which include appropriate powers of the distances to the vertices, the edges, and the boundary of the domains. The allowable ranges for the weight parameters are explicitly determined by the geometry of the domains.

1.
Introduction

In this article we provide Green’s function estimates for the parabolic operator

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\sum^{3}_{i,j=1}a_{ij}(t)D_{ij}

subject to the zero Dirichlet boundary condition on polyhedrons and infinite polyhedral cones in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Here, the coefficients
a_{ij}
s are assumed to be measurable in
t
, and satisfy the uniform parabolicity condition (
2.2
).

A polyhedron, which we denote as
\mathcal{P}
, is a bounded three dimensional object whose boundary consists of a finite number of polygonal faces, straight edges, and vertices.
A polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}
, on the other hand, is an infinite three-dimensional object whose boundary,
\partial\mathcal{D}
, consists of a vertex at

{\bf{0}}
, infinite straight edges, and flat faces. See Assumptions
2.1
and
2.10
for mathematical definitions.

Green’s function is characterized by three factors; the operator, the domain, and the zero Dirichlet boundary condition. Green’s function
G=G(t,s,x,y)
for a parabolic operator
L
given on domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is supposed to be
fundamental
for the operator
L
, meaning that for any fixed
(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{O}
it satisfies

LG(\cdot,s,\cdot,y)=\delta(\cdot-s)\delta(\cdot-y);\quad G|_{s>t}=0,\,\,G|_{x\in\partial\mathcal{O}}=0

(1.1)

in the sense of distributions in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{O}
(see Lemma
A.2
). In a probabilistic point of view, Green’s function is the transition probability density of a certain stochastic process which is killed once it reaches the boundary of the domain. For detail, see Remark
A.6
.

Pointwise estimate of Green’s function has been an intriguing topic in mathematics since Nash’s work
[
28
]
. See e.g.
[
5
,
19
]
for a brief history of the study of Green’s functions for both elliptic and parabolic operators. We refer to
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
19
,
30
]
for Gaussian bounds of Green’s functions for parabolic operators on general domains and we refer to
[
5
,
11
]
for the Green’s function estimates on
C^{1,\alpha}
-domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, of the type

G(t,s,x,y)\leq N1_{t>s}\left(1\wedge\frac{d(x)}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)\left(1\wedge\frac{d(y)}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)\Gamma_{\sigma}(t-s,x-y),

where
\sigma,N>0
are constants,
\Gamma_{\sigma}(t,x):=|t|^{-d/2}e^{\frac{-\sigma|x|^{2}}{t}}
, and
d(x)
denotes the distance from
x
to the boundary of a domain. We also refer to
[
17
,
21
,
22
]
for estimates of the Green’s function on smooth cones with a vertex at
{\bf{0}}
. In these works the estimates of the Green’s function account for the distance to the boundary and the distance to the vertex
{\bf{0}}
.

Our motivation for the Green’s function estimates on polyhedral cones and polyhedrons is to develop a nice Sobolev regularity theory for partial differential equations (PDEs) and stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) on these types of non-smooth domains.
The regularity of the solutions to PDEs and SPDEs can be significantly influenced by singularities of
the boundary, such as corners and edges. This influence is already known for a
long time from the analysis of deterministic equations, see, e.g.,
[
9
,
14
,
15
,
23
,
26
,
29
]
.
The singularities of the boundary are more critical for SPDEs due to the interaction with random noises near the boundary (cf.
[
7
,
13
,
16
]
).
Actually even on
C^{\infty}
domains, the second and higher derivatives of solutions to SPDEs blow up near the boundary unless certain compatibility condition is fulfilled (cf.
[
13
,
24
]
).
To measure the blow up of derivatives of the solutions to PDEs and SPDEs, it is necessary to introduce suitable weighted Sobolev spaces.
The Green’s function appears in the integral representation of solutions and the behavior of solutions near the boundary can be understood by studying the Green’s function (cf.
[
7
,
18
]
). In particular, our Green’s function estimates must involve the distances to the edges, vertices, and boundary in order for us to capture the singularities of the solutions.

Now we introduce our Green’s function estimates for the polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
V=\bf{0}
denote the vertex,
E_{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots,N_{0}
, denote the edges of
\mathcal{D}
, and
\hat{E}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{0}}E_{i}
. For any
r>0,x\in\mathcal{D}
, and
\Lambda^{+}=(\lambda^{+}_{0},\lambda^{+}_{1},\cdots,\lambda^{+}_{N_{0}})
,
\Lambda^{-}=(\lambda^{-}_{0},\lambda^{-}_{1},\cdots,\lambda^{-}_{N_{0}})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N_{0}+1}
, we define

\mathbf{I}(x,r;\Lambda^{+})
and
\mathbf{I}(x,r;\Lambda^{-})
as

\mathbf{I}(x,r;\Lambda^{\pm})=\left(\frac{d(x,V)}{r}\wedge 1\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{0}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N_{0}}\left(\frac{d\big(x,E_{i}\big)\wedge r}{d\big(x,V\big)\wedge r}\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{i}}\right)\frac{d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})\wedge r}{d(x,\hat{E})\wedge r},

where
d(x,V)
,
d(x,E_{i})
,
d(x,\partial D)
, and
d(x,\hat{E})
represent the distances from
x
to the vertex, each edge
E_{i}
, the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
, and the union of all edges
\hat{E}
, respectively. Note that, since
V\in E_{i}\subset\hat{E}\subset\partial\mathcal{D}
, for any
x\in\mathcal{D}
we have

d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})\leq d(x,\hat{E})\leq d(x,E_{i})\leq d(x,V).

Our Green’s function estimate on the polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}
has the following form:

\displaystyle G(t,s,x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\,1_{t>s}\,\mathbf{I}\big(x,\sqrt{t-s};\Lambda^{+}\big)\,\mathbf{I}\big(y,\sqrt{t-s};\Lambda^{-}\big)

(1.2)

\displaystyle\,\,\times\frac{1}{(t-s)^{3/2}}e^{-\sigma\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}},

where
\sigma>0
is a constant depending only on the uniform parabolicity of
\mathcal{L}
, and
N
is a constant that depends also on
\Lambda^{\pm}
. The admissible ranges of
\lambda^{+}_{i}
and
\lambda^{-}_{i}
,
i=0,1,\cdots,N_{0}
, are explicitly determined based on the operator
\mathcal{L}
and the geometry of the domain
\mathcal{D}
. Specifically, when
\mathcal{L}=\partial_{t}-\Delta
, the permissible ranges of
\lambda^{\pm}_{i}
are given by

\lambda^{\pm}_{i}\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{\kappa_{i}}\right),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N_{0},

where
\kappa_{i}
is the inner angle between the two planes that form the edge
E_{i}
. On the other hand, in this case the permissible ranges of
\lambda^{\pm}_{0}
are

\lambda^{\pm}_{0}\in\left(0,-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{0}+\frac{1}{4}}\right),

where
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is the first eigenvalue of the Laplace Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}
with Dirichlet boundary condition (see (
2.7
) and Remark
2.6
).

The product
\mathbf{I}\big(x,\sqrt{t-s};\Lambda^{+}\big)\cdot\mathbf{I}\big(y,\sqrt{t-s};\Lambda^{-}\big)
characterizes the behavior of the Green’s function, particularly its decay rates, near the vertex, the edges, and the boundary; see Remarks
2.9
and
2.15
for details.

Our estimate for the Green’s function on polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
also takes the form of (
1.2
). However, since there are multiple vertices, say
V_{1},V_{2},\cdots,V_{M_{0}}
, we use a different definition for
\mathbf{I}
: for

\Lambda^{\pm}:=\big((\lambda_{v,i}^{\pm})_{1\leq i\leq M_{0}},\,(\lambda_{e,j}^{\pm})_{1\leq j\leq N_{0}}\big)
,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{I}(x,r;\Lambda^{\pm})

\displaystyle:=\left(\,\prod_{i=1}^{M_{0}}\left(\frac{d(x,V_{i})}{r}\wedge 1\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{v,i}}\right)\cdot\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{N_{0}}\left(\frac{d(x,E_{j})\wedge r}{d\big(x,\hat{V}\cap E_{j}\big)\wedge r}\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{e,j}}\right)\cdot\frac{d(x,\partial\mathcal{P})\wedge r}{d(x,\hat{E})\wedge r},

where
\hat{V}:=\{V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{M_{0}}\}
and
d(x,\hat{V}\cap E_{j})
represents the distance from
x
to the vertices on the edge
E_{j}
. The admissible ranges of
\lambda^{\pm}_{v,i}
and
\lambda^{\pm}_{e,j}
are also explicitly determined by
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{L}
.

The proofs for our weighted estimates of Green’s function are more involved than those for smooth cones because polyhedral cones include both vertices and edges, and polyhedrons have multiple vertices in addition to edges. As the Green’s function estimate for infinite smooth cones without edges in
[
17
]
was the key for us to develop a regularity theory in
[
18
]
, in a subsequent work our Green’s function estimates will be essential for us to build a regularity theory of (stochastic) parabolic equations on polyhedral cones and polyhedrons.

Remark 2.9

The function

gives information on the behavior of the Green function near the vertex, edges, and
\partial\mathcal{D}

(i)

Obviously
{\bf{I}}(x,r;\Lambda^{\pm})\leq 1

d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})\leq d(x,E_{i})\leq d(x,V),\quad\forall\,i.

(ii)

If
E_{k}
is the closest edge to
x
so that

d(x,\hat{E})=d(x,E_{k})
, then there exists
\delta>0
such that
\frac{d(x,E_{i})}{d(x,V)}=d\left(\frac{x}{|x|},E_{i}\right)\simeq d\left(\frac{x}{|x|},p_{i}\right)\geq\delta

and hence

\displaystyle{\bf{I}}(x,r;\Lambda^{\pm})\simeq\left(\frac{d(x,V)}{r}\wedge 1\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{0}}\left(\frac{d\big(x,E_{k}\big)\wedge r}{d\big(x,V\big)\wedge r}\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{e,k}}\frac{d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})\wedge r}{d(x,E_{k})\wedge r}

holds; also see Lemma
3.1
.(ii).

(iii)

If
x
is away from all edges, that is, if
there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that
d\big(\frac{x}{|x|},\hat{E}\big)=\frac{d(x,\hat{E})}{d(x,V)}\geq\delta
, then
d(x,E_{i})\leq d(x,V)\leq\delta^{-1}d(x,E_{i})
for all
i
. Hence, we have

{\bf{I}}(x,r;\Lambda^{\pm})\simeq\left(\frac{d(x,V)}{r}\wedge 1\right)^{\lambda^{\pm}_{o}-1}\left(\frac{d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})}{r}\wedge 1\right).

Of course, the relation

(iv)

If
x
is away from the boundary, that is, if
there exists
\delta>0
such that
d\big(\frac{x}{|x|},\partial\mathcal{D}\big)=\frac{d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})}{d(x,V)}\geq\delta
, then
d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})\leq d(x,E_{i})\leq d(x,V)\leq\delta^{-1}d(x,\partial\mathcal{D})
for all
i

and hence

Q_{\rho}^{\mathcal{D}}(t,x)

u|_{(\mathbb{R}\times\partial\mathcal{D})\cap Q_{\rho}(t,x)}=0
as
s<t-\rho^{2}=t-(t-s)/4

Hence, by Theorem

(i) we have

G(t,s,x,y)\leq N{\bf{I}}(x,\rho;\Lambda^{+})\sup_{(t^{\prime},z)\in Q^{\mathcal{D}}_{\rho}(t,x)}G(t^{\prime},s,z,y).

Step 2.
Next, for each fixed
(t^{\prime},z)\in Q_{\rho}(t,x)

Hence, by Theorem
3.4
(ii) we have

Step 3.
Now, we note that for any

|z-w|+2r\geq|x-y|,\;\;2|z-w|^{2}+2(2\rho)^{2}\geq|x-y|^{2},\;\;|z-w|^{2}\geq\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2}-(t-s),

Hence, using (

Step 4.
Combining the results of Steps 1, 2, and 3, we have

\displaystyle\quad G(t,s,x,y)

\displaystyle\leq N{\bf{I}}\big(x,\sqrt{t-s}/2;\Lambda^{+}\big){\bf{I}}\big(y,\sqrt{t-s}/2;\Lambda^{-}\big)\cdot(t-s)^{-3/2}e^{-\sigma^{\prime}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}}

\displaystyle\leq N{\bf{I}}\big(x,\sqrt{t-s};\Lambda^{+}\big){\bf{I}}\big(y,\sqrt{t-s};\Lambda^{-}\big)\cdot(t-s)^{-3/2}e^{-\sigma^{\prime}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}}

with the generic constants

Lemma 4.3
.

Appendix A
Auxiliary results of Green’s functions

In this section, we collect elementary properties of Green’s function on general domains
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

, for the operator
\partial_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}(t)D_{ij}

The coefficients

such that

\displaystyle\nu_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}(t)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\leq\nu_{2}|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

We denote

L:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}(t)D_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{L}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}(-t)D_{ij}

Definition A.1
.

Let

,

.

(i)

For

, we say that

in the sense of distributions on

, if for any

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}u(b,x)\phi(b,x)dx+\int_{I\times\mathcal{O}}\left(-u\partial_{t}\phi+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}D_{i}uD_{j}\phi\right)dxdt=\int_{I\times\mathcal{O}}f\phi\,dxdt\,,

where
u(\infty,x)\phi(\infty,x):=0

(ii)

Let

be a relatively open set of

.

if the following holds: for any

with

,

.

We claim that for any given

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}\widetilde{G}_{\mathcal{O}}(t,s,x,y)g(y)dy=:u_{\infty}(t,x)=\int_{\mathcal{O}}G_{\mathcal{O}}(t,s,x,y)g(y)dy\,,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\infty}\|_{L_{2}((s,\infty)\times\mathcal{O}_{n})}\leq\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla u_{n_{k}}\|_{L_{2}((s,s+i)\times\mathcal{O}_{n})}\leq N_{0}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{O})}\,.

(A.6)

For
s>0
and

, we consider

where

and

is a

) of

is

Define the stopping times

\partial&:r\geq\tau\end{cases}

for any function

. By Itô’s formula, for all

(a.s.),

\displaystyle u(s,y)=\mathbb{E}u(\tilde{\tau},\xi^{s,y}_{\tilde{\tau}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}u(t,\xi^{s,y}_{t})1_{t<\tau}+\mathbb{E}u(\tau,\xi^{s,y}_{\tau})1_{t\geq\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}g(\xi^{s,y}_{t})1_{t<\tau}=\mathbb{E}g(\xi^{\tau,s,y}_{t}).

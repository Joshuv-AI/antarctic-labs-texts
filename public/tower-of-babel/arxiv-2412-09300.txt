Commuting Jordan derivations over a Ring with idempotents Thanks: * Corresponding author
Sk Aziz, Om Prakash ⋆, Arindam Ghosh

Abstract
. This paper explores the behavior of commuting Jordan derivations over prime rings with non-trivial idempotents and demonstrates that they become zero maps. Further, it establishes this result for commuting Jordan higher derivations over prime rings under specific conditions. In fact, it introduces commuting generalized Jordan derivations over rings and establishes that the zero map is the only commuting generalized Jordan derivation over prime rings under certain assumptions.

Introduction

Throughout the paper,
P
represents a prime ring with center
Z(P)
unless otherwise stated. Also,
[p_{1},p_{2}]
represents the commutator of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, defined as
p_{1}p_{2}-p_{2}p_{1}
, for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
. Recall that a ring
P
is said to be a prime ring if
p_{1}Pp_{2}=0
implies that
p_{1}=0
or
p_{2}=0
. Further, a ring
P
is said to be
2
-torsion free when
2p=0
for some
p\in P
implies
p=0
. An additive mapping
h:P\rightarrow P
is called a derivation if
h(p_{1}p_{2})=h(p_{1})p_{2}+p_{1}h(p_{2})
for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
. On the other hand, a Jordan derivation
d:P\rightarrow P
is an additive map satisfying
d(p^{2})=d(p)p+pd(p)
for all
p\in P
. An additive map
D
is called a generalized Jordan derivation if
D(p^{2})=D(p)p+pd(p)
for all
p\in P
and for some Jordan derivation
d
on
P
. A left centralizer
f:P\rightarrow P
is an additive map satisfying
f(p_{1}p_{2})=f(p_{1})p_{2}
for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
. Similarly, we define the right centralizer. An additive map
g:P\rightarrow P
is said to be a Jordan left centralizer if
g(p^{2})=g(p)p
, for all
p\in P
. Similarly, a Jordan right centralizer could be defined. A map
F:P\rightarrow P
is called a centralizing map if
[F(p),p]\in Z(P)
for all
p\in P
. In particular, if
[F(p),p]=0
for all
p\in P
, then
F
is called a commuting map on
P
.

Herstein
[
10
]
initially established that over a
2
-torsion-free prime ring, every Jordan derivation becomes a derivation. Later, Cusack
[
6
]
extended this result to semiprime rings and provided an alternative proof as well. In
2008
, Vukman
[
13
]
discussed left Jordan derivations over semiprime rings. In his work, he demonstrated that if
P
is a semiprime ring which is
2
-torsion free and
d
is a left Jordan derivation over
P
, then
d
becomes a derivation that maps
P
into
Z(P)
. Recently, this investigation has gone on to the characterizations of Jordan derivations, specifically over triangular rings. Notably, a study of Fošner
[
8
]
established that every Jordan derivation over a triangular ring (may not contain unity) is indeed a derivation under certain conditions. For further exploration of the topic and additional insights into Jordan derivations, we refer

,

,

,

,

,

,

.

In recent decades, the investigation of commuting maps has emerged as a highly active area of research within the study of mappings on rings. For detailed information on commuting maps, readers can see
[
2
]
. The study of commuting and centralizing mappings dates back to 1955, marked by Divinsky’s work
[
7
]
. Divinsky demonstrated that a simple Artinian ring is commutative when a nontrivial automorphism satisfies some commuting properties. Later, Posner
[
12
]
made a significant contribution in
1957
by establishing the initial result concerning commuting derivations. Posner’s theorem asserts that if d is a commuting derivation acting on a prime ring
P
, then
P
is a commutative ring, or the derivation
d
is identically zero. In
2023
, Hosseini and Jing
[
11
]
proved that every commuting Jordan derivation over a triangular ring (may not contain unity) is identically zero map.

In this paper, our primary objective is to investigate commuting Jordan derivations on a
2
-torsion free ring
P
. We present a distinct result from Posner’s result with a completely different type of proof by demonstrating that any commuting Jordan derivation over a
2
-torsion-free prime ring
P
must be identically zero. Moreover, we prove that commuting Jordan left centralizers over a
2
-torsion free prime ring under some specific conditions is basically a zero map. As a consequence, we prove that every commuting generalized Jordan derivation over the same ring is identically zero, where we define commuting generalized Jordan derivation in the following way:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
P
be a ring. An additive map
D:P\rightarrow P
is said to be a commuting generalized Jordan derivation if

\displaystyle D(p^{2})=D(p)p+pd(p)~\text{and}~D(p)p=pD(p),

for all

p\in P

and
d
is a Jordan derivation
over
P
.

Commuting Jordan derivation and commuting Jordan left centralizer are defined in
[
11
]
as the follows:

Definition 1.2
.

An additive map
d:P\rightarrow P
is said to be a commuting Jordan derivation if

\displaystyle d(p^{2})=d(p)p+pd(p)~\text{and}~d(p)p=pd(p),~\text{for all}~p\in P.

Definition 1.3
.

An additive map
D:P\rightarrow P
is said to be a commuting Jordan left centralizer if

\displaystyle D(p^{2})=D(p)p~\text{and}~D(p)p=pD(p),~\text{for all}~p\in P.

Let
e_{1}
be a nontrivial idempotent of
P
and
e_{2}=1-e_{1}
where

is the identity element of
P
, then by Peirce decomposition, we can write

P=P_{11}+P_{12}+P_{21}+P_{22}~\text{ where }P_{ij}=e_{i}Pe_{j}~\text{for}~i,j\in\{1,2\}.

Therefore, each
x\in P
can be written as
x=a+m+t+b\text{ where }a\in P_{11},m\in P_{12},t\in P_{21},b\in P_{22}.

First, we discuss a few useful lemmas before establishing the main results of this paper.

Lemma 1.4
.

If
d
is a commuting Jordan derivation on a ring
P
, then

\displaystyle d(p^{2})=2d(p)p=2pd(p),~\text{for all}~p\in P.

Proof.

Since
d
is a commuting Jordan derivation on
P
,

\displaystyle d(p^{2})=d(p)p+pd(p)=d(p)p+d(p)p=2d(p)p,~\text{for all}~p\in P.

Similarly,
d(p^{2})=2pd(p)
, for all
p\in P
.
∎

(Lemma

Let

2.
Commuting Jordan Derivation

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
P
be a
2
-torsion free unital ring with a nontrivial idempotent element
e_{1}
and
d:P\rightarrow P
be a commuting Jordan derivation. Then
d
is a zero map if it satisfies the following condition:

\displaystyle[d(a)]_{11}=0=[d(b)]_{22},~\text{for all}~a\in P_{11}~\text{and}~b\in P_{22}.

In order to prove the result, we first establish some useful lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

d(e_{1})=0~\text{and}~d(e_{2})=0
.

Proof.

Since
e_{1}^{2}=e_{1}
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle d(e_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle=2e_{1}d(e_{1})=2([d(e_{1})]_{11}+[d(e_{1})]_{12}).

Also,

(2.2)

\displaystyle d(e_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle=2d(e_{1})e_{1}=2([d(e_{1})]_{11}+[d(e_{1})]_{21}).

Comparing (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we have

(2.3)

[d(e_{1})]_{12}=0=[d(e_{1})]_{21}.

Again,

(2.4)

\displaystyle d\left(e_{1}\right)=d\left(e_{1}\cdot e_{1}\right)=d\left(e_{1}\right)e_{1}+e_{1}d\left(e_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\implies\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{11}+\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{12}+\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{21}+\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{22}

\displaystyle=\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{11}+\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{21}+\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{11}+\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{12}

\displaystyle\implies\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{11}-\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{22}=0

\displaystyle\implies\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{11}=0=\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)\right]_{22}.

Hence by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
),
d(e_{1})=0.

Similarly, we can prove
d\left(e_{2}\right)=0.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a\in P_{11}
. Then

d(a)=[d(a)]_{11}.

Proof.

As
a\in P_{11}
, by Lemma
2.2
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle 2d(a)=d\left(ae_{1}+e_{1}a\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(e_{1}d(a)+ad(e_{1})\right)=2e_{1}d(a).

Also,

(2.6)

\displaystyle 2d(a)=d\left(ae_{1}+e_{1}a\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(d(a)e_{1}+d\left(e_{1}\right)a\right)=2d(a)e_{1}.

Comparing (
2.5
) and (
2.6
),

(2.7)

\displaystyle d(a)=e_{1}d(a)=d(a)e_{1}~(\text{using 2-torsion freeness of}~P)

\displaystyle\implies[d(a)]_{12}-[d(a)]_{21}=0

\displaystyle\implies[d(a)]_{12}=0=[d(a)]_{21}.

By (
2.7
),

(2.8)

d(a)=[d(a)]_{11}+[d(a)]_{22}.

By (
2.5
),

(2.9)

\displaystyle d(a)=e_{1}d(a)

\displaystyle\implies[d(a)]_{11}+[d(a)]_{22}=[d(a)]_{11}

\displaystyle\implies[d(a)]_{22}=0.

Hence, by (
2.7
) and (
2.9
),
d(a)=[d(a)]_{11}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m\in P_{12}
. Then

d(m)=0.

Proof.

Since
m\in P_{12}
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle d(m)=d\left(e_{1}m+me_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left[d\left(e_{1}\right)m+d(m)e_{1}\right].

Also,

(2.11)

d(m)=2\left[e_{1}d(m)+md\left(e_{1}\right)\right].

Comparing (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we get

(2.12)

[d(m)]_{21}=0=[d(m)]_{12}.

From (
2.12
), we have

(2.13)

d(m)=[d(m)]_{11}+[d(m)]_{22}.

Also, from (
2.10
),

(2.14)

\displaystyle d(m)=2d(m)e_{1}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle[d(m)]_{11}+[d(m)]_{22}=2[d(m)]_{11}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle[d(m)]_{11}-[d(m)]_{22}=0}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle[d(m)]_{11}=[d(m)]_{22}=0}.

By (
2.12
) and (
2.14
), we get
d(m)=0.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
t\in P_{21}
. Then

d(t)=0.

Proof.

Since
t\in P_{21}
,

(2.15)

\displaystyle d(t)=d\left(e_{2}t+te_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left[d\left(e_{2}\right)t+d(t)e_{2}\right].

Also,

(2.16)

d(t)=2\left[e_{2}d(t)+td\left(e_{2}\right)\right].

Comparing (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we have

(2.17)

[d(t)]_{21}=0=[d(t)]_{12}.

From (
2.17

(2.18)

d(t)=[d(t)]_{11}+[d(t)]_{22}.

Also, from (
2.18
),

(2.19)

\displaystyle d(t)=2e_{2}d(t)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle[d(t)]_{11}+[d(t)]_{22}=2[d(t)]_{22}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle[d(t)]_{11}-[d(t)]_{22}=0}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle[d(t)]_{11}=[d(t)]_{22}=0}.

By (
2.17
) and (
2.19
), we obtain
d(t)=0.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
b\in P_{22}
. Then

d(b)=[d(b)]_{22}.

Proof.

As
b\in P_{22}
, by Lemma
2.2
,

(2.20)

\displaystyle 2d(b)=d\left(be_{2}+e_{2}b\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(e_{2}d(b)+bd(e_{2})\right)=2e_{2}d(b).

Also,

(2.21)

\displaystyle 2d(b)=d\left(be_{2}+e_{2}b\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(d(b)e_{2}+d\left(e_{2}\right)b\right)=2d(b)e_{2}.

Comparing (

2.21

(2.22)

\displaystyle d(b)=e_{2}d(b)=d(b)e_{2}~(\text{using 2-torsion freeness of}~R)

\displaystyle\implies[d(b)]_{12}=0=[d(b)]_{21}.

By (

(2.23)

By (
2.20
) and (
2.23

(2.24)

\displaystyle d(b)=e_{2}d(b)

\displaystyle\implies[d(b)]_{11}+[d(b)]_{22}=[d(b)]_{22}

\displaystyle\implies[d(b)]_{11}=0.

Hence, by (
2.22

.

Let

\displaystyle x=a+m+t+b,~\text{for some}~a\in P_{11},~m\in P_{12},~t\in P_{21}~\text{and}~b\in P_{22}.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
P
be a
2
-torsion free prime ring with a non-trivial idempotent
e_{1}
and
d:P\rightarrow P
be a commuting Jordan derivation. Then,
d
is a zero map.

Proof.

Let
x\in P
. Then

\displaystyle x=a+m+t+b,~\text{for some}~a\in P_{11},~m\in P_{12},~t\in P_{21}~\text{and}~b\in P_{22}.

Using the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have

(2.25)

d(x)=[d(a)]_{11}+[d(b)]_{22}.

By Lemmas
1.5
,
2.3
and
2.5
, we get

(2.26)

\displaystyle d(at+ta)=2td(a)=2d(a)t

\displaystyle\implies td(a)=d(a)t~\text{(Since}~P~\text{is 2-torsion free)}

\displaystyle\implies t[d(a)]_{11}=[d(a)]_{11}t=0

\displaystyle\implies e_{2}R[d(a)]_{11}=0

\displaystyle\implies[d(a)]_{11}=0~\text{(Since}~P~\text{is prime and}~e_{2}\neq 0).

Also, by Lemmas
1.5
,
2.4
and
2.6
, we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle d(mb+bm)=2md(b)=2d(b)m

\displaystyle\implies md(b)=d(b)m~\text{(Since}~P~\text{is 2-torsion free)}

\displaystyle\implies m[d(b)]_{22}=[d(b)]_{22}m=0

\displaystyle\implies e_{1}R[d(b)]_{22}=0

\displaystyle\implies[d(b)]_{22}=0~\text{(Since}~P~\text{is prime and}~e_{1}\neq 0).

Again, from (
2.25
), (
2.26
) and (
2.27
), we have

\displaystyle d(x)=0~\text{for all}~x\in P.

Hence,
d
is a zero map.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
P
be a 2-torsion free prime ring with a non-trivial idempotent
e_{1}
such that
{\{d_{n}\}}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a commuting Jordan higher derivation on

that is,

,
d_{0}(x)=x

[d_{n}(x),x]=0

([a,b]=ab-ba)
, for all non-negative integers

. Then

.

Proof.

We use induction on

\displaystyle d_{1}(x^{2})=d_{1}(x)d_{0}(x)+d_{0}(x)d_{1}(x)=d_{1}(x)x+xd_{1}(x),~\text{for all}~x\in P.

Hence,

is a Jordan derivation on

. Since

for all

,

is a commuting Jordan derivation on

. It follows from Corollary

that

is a zero map.
For

\displaystyle d_{2}(x^{2})=d_{2}(x)x+(d_{1}(x))^{2}+xd_{2}(x)=d_{2}(x)x+xd_{2}(x),~\text{for all}~x\in P.

Therefore,

is a Jordan derivation on

. Since
[d_{2}(x),x]=0
for all

,
d_{2}
is a commuting Jordan derivation on

is a zero map.
Let

\displaystyle d_{n}(x^{2})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}d_{n-k}(x)d_{k}(x)=d_{n}(x)x+xd_{n}(x),~\text{for all}~x\in P.

Hence,

[d_{n}(x),x]=0
for all

,
d_{n}
is a commuting Jordan derivation on

. It follows from Corollary
2.7

∎

3.
Commuting Generalized Jordan Derivations Over a Ring

Theorem 3.1
.

If

is a commuting Jordan left centralizer over a

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
m\in P_{12}
, we have
D(m)=0.

Proof.

Let
m\in P_{12}
. Then

(3.1)

D(m)=D(me_{2})=D(m)e_{2}=[D(m)]_{12}+[D(m)]_{22}.

Also,

(3.2)

D(m)=D(e_{1}m)=e_{1}D(m)=[D(m)]_{11}+[D(m)]_{12}.

Therefore, from (

\displaystyle D(m)=D(me_{2})

\displaystyle\implies[D(m)]_{21}=0~\text{(by~ using~ equation ~\eqref{eq:3.0})}.

By assumption,

for all

∎

, we get

(3.9)

D(b)=D(be_{2})=D(b)e_{2}=[D(b)]_{12}+[D(b)]_{22}.

Also,

(3.10)

D(b)=D(e_{2}b)=e_{2}D(b)=[D(b)]_{21}+[D(b)]_{22}.

Therefore, from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we obtain
[D(b)]_{12}=0=[D(b)]_{21}
.

Now,

\displaystyle D(b)=D(be_{2})=D(b)e_{2}

\displaystyle\implies[D(b)]_{11}+[D(b)]_{22}=[D(b)]_{22}

\displaystyle\implies[D(b)]_{11}=0.

Also, for
t\in P_{21}
, we have

\displaystyle D(bt+tb)=bD(t)+tD(b)

\displaystyle\implies D(bt+tb)=0+t[D(b)]_{22}~\text{(by ~using~Lemma ~\ref{lem3.3})}

\displaystyle\implies D(bt)=0

\displaystyle\implies D(b)t=0

\displaystyle\implies[D(b)]_{22}te_{1}=0~\text{(for~ all}~t\in P_{21})

\displaystyle\implies[D(b)]_{22}Pe_{1}=0

\displaystyle\implies[D(b)]_{22}=0~\text{(since ~$P$~ is ~prime)}.

Thus,
D(b)=0
for all
b\in P_{22}.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
x\in P
. Then

\displaystyle x=a+m+t+b,~\text{for some}~a\in P_{11},~m\in P_{12},~t\in P_{21}~\text{and}~b\in P_{22}.

By using Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle D(x)=D(a)+D(m)+D(t)+D(b)=0,~\text{for~ all}~x\in P.

Thus,
D=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
D
is commuting generalized Jordan derivation over a
2
-torsion free prime ring
P
with a non-trivial idempotent
e_{1}
, then
D
is a zero map if it satisfies the following condition:

[D(m)]_{12}=0~\text{for ~all}~m\in P_{12}.

Proof.

Since
D
is a commuting generalized Jordan derivation, there is an associated commuting Jordan derivation
d
over the ring
P
. Then, by Theorem
2.1
,
d
is a zero map. As a result,
D
becomes a commuting Jordan left centralizer and hence by Theorem
3.1
,
D
is a zero map on
P
.
∎

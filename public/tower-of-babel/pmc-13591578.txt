Rethinking Recovery After DIEP Flap Reconstruction: ICU vs. Step-Down Unit
Authors: Michael Diffley, Jocelyn Maceri, Sharan Sekhon, Julia Quinto, Dunya Atisha, Maristella Evangelista
Published: 2026

Background: Following deep inferior epigastric perforator (DIEP) flap breast reconstruction, patients are typically admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) for close postoperative flap monitoring. However, ICU-level care may not be necessary for all patients and may increase healthcare costs, staff burden, and redundancy in flap checks without improving outcomes.

Objective: To evaluate whether postoperative admission to a step-down care unit after DIEP flap reconstruction provides comparable clinical outcomes and quality of care to ICU admission while reducing length of stay and associated hospital costs.

Methods: Patients who underwent DIEP flap breast reconstruction at Henry Ford Health Detroit between May 2023 and April 2025 were retrospectively reviewed. Patients were stratified by postoperative admission to either the ICU or a step-down care unit. Primary outcomes included postoperative complications (unplanned reoperation, surgical site infection, skin ischemia, dehiscence, hematoma, flap loss, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism) and hospital length of stay (LOS). Secondary outcomes included estimated cost differences between ICU and step-down admission. Categorical variables were compared using Chi-square or Fisher exact tests where appropriate. Continuous variables were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The Cochran-Armitage trend test was used to evaluate return-to-OR frequency across groups. Statistical significance was defined as p<0.05.

Results: A total of 93 patients were included, with 59 admitted to the ICU and 34 admitted directly to step-down care postoperatively. Hospital length of stay was significantly longer in the ICU group (4.8 ± 2.1 days) compared to the step-down group (3.8 ± 1.1 days, p = 0.004). There was a trend toward decreased overall complications (p = 0.013) and decreased unplanned reoperations (p = 0.011). There was no significant difference in flap loss (p=0.531), dehiscence (p = 1.000), hematoma (p = 0.082), deep vein thrombosis/pulmonary embolism (p = 0.531), surgical site infection (p = 1.000), or skin necrosis (p = 0.531) across groups.

Conclusions: Immediate postoperative admission to step-down care following DIEP flap breast reconstruction is associated with decreased length of stay with a trend towards lower overall complication rates and fewer unplanned returns to the operating room compared to ICU admission without an increase in the rate of complications. Step-down care may represent a safe and resource-efficient alternative to routine ICU admission for select DIEP flap patients.

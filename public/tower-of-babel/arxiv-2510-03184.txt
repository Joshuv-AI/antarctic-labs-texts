On the Hardness of the One-Sided Code Sparsifier Problem
Elena Grigorescu, Alice Moayyedi

Abstract
The notion of code sparsification was introduced by Khanna, Putterman and Sudan (SODA 2024), as an analogue to the the more established notion of cut sparsification in graphs and hypergraphs. In particular, for α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) an (unweighted) one-sided α \alpha -sparsifier for a linear code 𝒞 ⊆ 𝐅 2 n \mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n} is a subset S ⊆ [ n ] S\subseteq[n] such that the weight of each codeword projected onto the coordinates in S S is preserved up to an α \alpha fraction. Recently, Gharan and Sahami (arxiv.2502.02799) show the existence of one-sided 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -sparsifiers of size n / 2 + O ⁡ ( k ​ n ) n/2+O(\sqrt{kn}) for any linear code, where k k is the dimension of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . In this paper, we consider the computational problem of finding a one-sided 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -sparsifier of minimal size, and show that it is NP-hard, via a reduction from the classical nearest codeword problem. We also show hardness of approximation results.

2
Representatives of Largest Weight

In this section we characterise
\frac{1}{2}
-thick sets, and connect them to the nearest codewords of elements in the same coset.

For a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}
, we consider the quotient space
\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}/\mathcal{C}
. The equivalence classes or cosets
H\in\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}/\mathcal{C}
are the sets of strings such that
h,h^{\prime}\in H\Rightarrow h+h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and

H

to be the set of strings in
H
of largest weight;
H^{*}=\{h^{*}\in H:\text{wt}(h^{*})=\max\limits_{h\in H}\text{wt}(h)\}
. Our first theorem is a characterisation of the
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings in a given coset:

Theorem 4
.

For a member
h
of a coset
H\in\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}/\mathcal{C}
, the following are equivalent:

1.

h
is among the elements of
H
of greatest weight;

2.

h
is
\frac{1}{2}
-thick with respect to
\mathcal{C}
;

3.

The all-zeroes string,
\mathbf{0}
, is a nearest codeword to the complement of
h
,
\bar{h}
(that is,
h+\mathbf{1}
).

We prove
Theorem

4
in two parts:
Lemma

1
(equivalence of 1. and 2.) and
Lemma

2
(equivalence of 1. and 3.).

We say that

is a nearest codeword to a string

when the Hamming distance between

and

\mathcal{C}
. In general, we denote the complement of a string

\bar{s}
, and the all-zeroes and all-ones strings as

Lemma 1
.

1. and 2. above are equivalent.

Proof.

[
GS25
]
presents a proof that 1. implies 2., which we will briefly reproduce here for completeness. Suppose that
h^{*}\in H^{*}
is a string of maximal weight among strings in
H
, and suppose that
h^{*}
is not
\frac{1}{2}
-thick with respect to
\mathcal{C}
; that is,
\exists c\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\text{wt}(c\circ h^{*})<\frac{1}{2}\text{wt}(c)
. Take the string
h^{\prime}=h^{*}+c
, noting that
h^{\prime}\in H
. We must have that
\text{wt}(h^{\prime})>\text{wt}(h^{*})
, since
c\circ h^{*}<c\circ\bar{h^{*}}
— adding

turns more coordinates of

to one than it turns to zero. This contradicts the assumption that
h^{*}
is of maximal weight among strings in

is
\frac{1}{2}
-thick with respect to

.

Now, to show that 2. implies 1., suppose that

\frac{1}{2}

∎

Lemma 2
.

1. and 3. above are equivalent.

Proof.

To show that 1. implies 3., take some

\text{wt}(c+\bar{h^{*}})<\text{wt}(\bar{h^{*}}+\mathbf{0})=\text{wt}(\bar{h^{*}})

\text{wt}(c+\bar{h^{*}})<\text{wt}(\bar{h^{*}})\quad\text{iff}\quad n-\text{wt}(\mathbf{1}+c+\bar{h^{*}})<n-\text{wt}(h^{*})\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{wt}(c+h^{*})>\text{wt}(h^{*}).

Hence

is an element of

of greater weight than

, a contradiction. More intuitively, if

is closer to

than to

must differ on more coordinates with

itself. So

must be a nearest codeword in

to

.

To show that 3. implies 1., suppose that
h\in H
is of less than maximal weight; that is, there is some
h^{*}\in H
with
\text{wt}(h^{*})>\text{wt}(h)
. Then

is a codeword in

which is closer to
\bar{h}
than

\text{wt}(\bar{h^{*}})<\text{wt}(\bar{h})\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{wt}((h^{*}+h)+\bar{h})<\text{wt}(\bar{h})\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{wt}(c+\bar{h})<\text{wt}(\bar{h})

So
\mathbf{0}
is not a nearest codeword to
\bar{h}
; if

is a nearest codeword to some

is of maximal weight among elements of

.
∎

This concludes the proof of
Theorem

4
.

The fundamental property at hand here is that the operation of shifting by some constant string is an isometry; once the nearest (or furthest) codewords of one element

are fully determined by the difference

. Thus, since any element in the coset

which have any particular codeword as their nearest codeword — if

\mathbf{0}
than any other element of

\mathbf{0}

.

Let

be some equivalence class and

be the set of strings of maximal weight in

. Consider any string

, is a codeword in

of minimum Hamming distance from

of minimum Hamming distance from

are of the form

Proof.

Take any

. Then:

n-\text{wt}(h+c)<n-\text{wt}(h^{*})\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{wt}(h+c)>\text{wt}(h^{*}).

Since

is in

, and elements of

have maximum weight among elements of

Alternatively, we may observe that since

This also demonstrates that, for any elements

3
Optimal One-Sided Sparsifiers (Proof of Main Theorems)

Of course, in order to sparsify a code, we are not actually interested in finding the
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings among a particular coset. Instead, we are interested in finding the
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings among all strings in
\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}
. Specifically, we are interested in the problem of finding the smallest strings which are
\frac{1}{2}
-thick. We call a string an
optimal

\alpha
-thick (thin) string with respect to a code
\mathcal{C}
if it is
\alpha
-thick (thin) with respect to
\mathcal{C}
and it is of least (resp. greatest) weight among all
\alpha
-thick (thin) strings. Similarly, we call a set an optimal
\alpha
-thick set if it is of smallest size among such sets.

Corollary 2
.

The optimal
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings with respect to a code
\mathcal{C}
are exactly the complements of the strings of greatest weight which have
\mathbf{0}
as a nearest codeword in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4
, the
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings with respect to a code
\mathcal{C}
are exactly the complements of the strings which have
\mathbf{0}
as a nearest codeword — the
\frac{1}{2}
-thin strings with respect to
\mathcal{C}
. Further, the
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings of least weight are the complements of the
\frac{1}{2}
-thin strings of greatest weight.
∎

In fact, since the distance to the nearest codeword is constant among all elements of a given coset of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}
, the cosets containing the optimal
\frac{1}{2}
-thin strings are those where every element is of greatest distance to their nearest codewords. The cosets containing the optimal
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings, then, are cosets obtained by adding
\mathbf{1}
to the cosets containing the optimal
\frac{1}{2}
-thin strings (note that in a code containing the codeword
\mathbf{1}
, the cosets containing the optimal
\frac{1}{2}
-thin strings are the same as the cosets containing the optimal
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings).

\thmmain

*

Proof.

We demonstrate this by polynomial-time Turing reduction from
NCP
. We restate the problem for convenience:

Minimal One-Sided
\frac{1}{2}
-Sparsifier Problem (
OptHalfSparsifier
)

Instance:
A linear code
\mathcal{C}
given by its generators.

Output:
A set
S\subseteq[n]
such that:

•

(Feasibility) for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
,
\text{wt}(c_{S})\geq\frac{1}{2}c
;

•

(Optimality)
S
is of smallest size among all sets which satisfy the above.

and use the following algorithm:

Input :
A generator matrix
M
for a linear code
\mathcal{C}
;

a received string
s\in\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}
;

an integer
k
;

a subroutine
ohs(
M^{\prime}
)
which solves
OptHalfSparsifier
, with output in the form of the indicator vector of the produced set.

Output :
Whether the
NCP
instance
(M,s,k)
is a (YES) instance or a (NO) instance.

M_{0}\leftarrow M

i\leftarrow 0

1

while

a decision has not been made

do

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
denote the linear code generated by the matrix
M_{i}
.

h^{*}_{i}\leftarrow

ohs(
M_{i}
)

2

if

n-\text{wt}(h^{*}_{i})\leq k

then

Output (YES).

3

if

\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+s\in\mathcal{C}_{i}

then

Output (NO).

a_{i}\leftarrow\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+s

M_{i+1}\leftarrow(a_{i}\text{ concatenated to }M_{i})

i\leftarrow i+1

Algorithm 1

Solving
NCP
using
OptHalfSparsifier

This algorithm terminates in at most
n-\text{dim}(\mathcal{C})
calls to the
OptHalfSparsifier
subroutine, with
O(n-k)
extra work: since
a_{i}\notin\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all
i
, the dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
is one greater than the dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all
i
. When the dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is
n
, then it must be the case that
s\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
; thus, the loop at
algorithm 1
is run at most
n-\text{dim}(\mathcal{C})
times.

It remains to show correctness. We wish to maintain the invariant that, for each
i
, the distance from
s
to the nearest codeword in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is no closer than the distance from
s
to the nearest codeword in
\mathcal{C}
. So, towards a proof by contradiction, suppose that there is some
i
such that
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
has an element nearer to
s
than the nearest element in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Note that since
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}=\mathcal{C}_{i}\cup(a_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i})
, if
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
has an element closer to
s
than all elements in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, that element must be in
a_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
. So we have:

\displaystyle\text{minwt}(s+C(M_{i}))>\text{minwt}(s+a_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i})

iff

\displaystyle\text{minwt}(s+C(M_{i}))>\text{minwt}(s+\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+s+\mathcal{C}_{i})

iff

\displaystyle\text{minwt}(s+C(M_{i}))>\text{minwt}(\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+\mathcal{C}_{i})

Where, for
S\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\text{minwt}(S)
is the smallest weight among elements of
S
. Thus, the smallest weight among elements of the coset
s+\mathcal{C}_{i}
is larger than the smallest weight among elements of the coset
\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
. But then the largest element in the coset
\bar{s}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
is smaller than the largest element in the coset
h^{*}_{i}
. By
Theorem

4
, all largest elements of any coset are
\frac{1}{2}
-thick, and the largest element of
\bar{s}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
is
\frac{1}{2}
-thick; thus,
h^{*}_{i}
cannot be a
\frac{1}{2}
-thick string of minimal weight among all
\frac{1}{2}
-thick strings, a contradiction. So
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
cannot have any elements nearer to
s
than the nearest elements in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
; by induction,
\mathcal{C}_{i}
has no closer elements to
s
than does
\mathcal{C}
for any
i
.

We can see this property more intuitively by noting that adding a basis element to a linear code combines pairs of cosets which differ by that element; the maximal (minimal) elements in the resulting coset will be the maximal (minimal) elements among the two. Thus, given that the coset containing
\bar{h^{*}}
contains a largest minimal element among any coset, the coset
(\bar{h^{*}}+\mathcal{C}_{i})\cup(\bar{s}+\mathcal{C}_{i})
of
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
will contain the minimal element of
\bar{s}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
as a minimal element; repeatedly merging cosets in this manner never moves the coset containing
s
“closer” to the code.

Given this invariant, we proceed to show that the two conditionals on
algorithm 1
and
algorithm 1
can only be satisfied if the instance is a yes or no instance respectively. We begin with
algorithm 1
. Since
\bar{h^{*}_{i}}
is a string of greatest distance from any codeword in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, and has
\mathbf{0}
as a nearest codeword, if
\text{wt}(\bar{h^{*}_{i}})=n-\text{wt}(h_{i}^{*})\leq k
, then the greatest distance from any string among elements in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is at most
k
; thus, there must be a codeword in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
at least that close to
s
. By the invariant above, there also must be a codeword in
\mathcal{C}
which is that close, and the
NCP
instance
(M,s,k)
is a yes instance. To show that the conditional on
algorithm 1
is satisfied only for no instances, note that if
\bar{h_{i}^{*}}+s\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
, then
s
is in a coset with
\bar{h_{i}^{*}}
. This implies that the distance from
s
to its nearest codewords in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is the same as that of
\bar{h_{i}^{*}}
to its nearest codewords; since
\bar{h_{i}^{*}}
has
\mathbf{0}
as a nearest codeword, the distance from
s
to its nearest codeword is exactly
\text{wt}(\bar{h_{i}^{*}})=n-\text{wt}(h_{i}^{*})
. Since the conditional on
algorithm 1
was not satisfied, then, there exists no codeword in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
which has distance at most
k
from
s
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{i}\supseteq\mathcal{C}
, there exists no codeword in
\mathcal{C}
of at most that distance, and the
NCP
instance
(M,s,k)
is a no instance.

We therefore have a polynomial-time Turing reduction from
NCP
to
OptHalfSparsifier
, and
OptHalfSparsifier
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard.
∎

From known hardness of approximation results (
[
BGLR25
]
) of
NCP
, we can also derive similar results for the
OptHalfSparsifier
problem.

\thmapprox

*

Proof.

This follows directly from the algorithm above and the hardness of approximation of
NCP
given by
Theorem

3
. Suppose that instead of an algorithm which solves
OptHalfSparsifier
, we have an algorithm which solves the following approximation of
OptHalfSparsifier
:

\gamma
-Approximate Minimal One-Sided
\frac{1}{2}
-Sparsifier Problem (
\textsc{Approx\-OHS}_{\gamma}
)

Instance:
A linear code
\mathcal{C}
given by its generators.

Output:
A set
S\subseteq[n]
such that:

•

(Feasibility) for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
,
\text{wt}(c_{S})\geq\frac{1}{2}c
;

•

(Approximate Optimality) if
S^{*}
is a set which satisfies the above feasibility condition, then
\gamma\cdot|\bar{S}|\geq|\bar{S^{*}}|

If
\gamma=1
, then this problem is
OptHalfSparsifier
. Note that the multiplicative factor here is a constraint not on the size of the set itself, but the size of its conjugate; it is trivial to find a
\frac{1}{2}
-thick set with a size within a factor of
2
of that of the smallest
\frac{1}{2}
-thick set, by simply taking every coordinate which is represented among codewords in
\mathcal{C}
. It is easy to see from this that approximation up to a constant factor of the set size is trivial.

Given such a subroutine we can use
algorithm 1
to approximate
NCP
, solving the following problem:

Nearest Codeword Problem with Multiplicative Gap
\gamma
(
\textsc{Mult\-Gap\-NCP}_{\gamma}
)

Instance:
A generator matrix
M
for a linear code
\mathcal{C}
, a received string
s\in\mathbf{F}_{2}^{n}
, and an integer
k
.

Output
: (YES) if there exists
c\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\text{wt}(c+s)\leq k
. (NO) if for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
,
\text{wt}(c+s)>\gamma k
.

To show that this reduction goes through, we follow the previous proof with a small modification: instead of maintaining that the augmented codes
\mathcal{C}_{i}
each have no codewords closer to
s
than the closest codewords in
\mathcal{C}
, we maintain that each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
has no codewords outside of
\mathcal{C}
of distance smaller than
k
to
s
. We note first that if
h^{*}_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
has a distance from
\mathcal{C}_{i}
at most
k
, then we will have answered (YES) during that iteration of the loop at
algorithm 1
; we only proceed to add
h^{*}_{i}+s
to the generator matrix if
h^{*}_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i}
is further from
\mathcal{C}_{i}
than
k
. Thus, the set
(s+a_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i})=(\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+\mathcal{C}_{i})
has a distance from
\mathcal{C}
no smaller than
k
. Formally,
\text{minwt}(s+a_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i})=\text{minwt}(\bar{h^{*}_{i}}+\mathcal{C}_{i})>k
.

The reduction from
\textsc{Mult\-Gap\-NCP}_{\gamma}
to
\textsc{Approx\-OHS}_{\gamma}
then follows from the same arguments as in the proof of
Theorem

1
. Briefly, if the
\textsc{Mult\-Gap\-NCP}_{\gamma}
instance is a (YES) instance, then it will certainly never be the case that the distance from
s
to
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is greater than
k
for any
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, so
algorithm 1
will output (YES); if the
\textsc{Mult\-Gap\-NCP}_{\gamma}
instance is a (NO) instance, then, given the invariant above,
algorithm 1
may never output (YES), and must output (NO).
∎

Classic action recognition of cross-country skiing sports combining ant colony and clustering algorithm
Authors: Hongbo Yu, Yuanhui Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAs one of the oldest sports events in world sports history, cross-country skiing involves basic techniques such as mountaineering, skiing, and gliding, covering multiple classic actions during the process.MethodsTo improve the current cross-country skiing training technology, this study designed a classic action recognition method for cross-country skiing that combines ant colony and clustering algorithms. This method mainly utilizes ant colony algorithm to classify and partition actions, and implements classical action recognition based on current data parameters. The research will improve the ant colony clustering algorithm compared to traditional K-means algorithm, hierarchical clustering algorithm, density clustering algorithm, and basic ant colony algorithm. Metrics such as the sum of squared intra-cluster errors are used to evaluate cluster tightness for validation analysis.ResultsThe results show that the proposed method achieves 92.3% accuracy in recognizing classical actions with a maximum improvement of 2.9% compared to the K-means algorithm. Meanwhile, the average percentage error value of this method is only 0.62. In the action recognition of athletes at different levels, the convergence decreased by 19%.ConclusionsFrom this, it can be concluded that the proposed method has high action recognition ability and recognition accuracy, which can effectively improve the recognition effect of cross-country skiing.Keywords: Ant colony clustering, Classic actions, Cross-country skiing, Recognition, Exercise

Introduction

Cross-country skiing, as a highly technical and strategic sport, has extremely high requirements for athletes’ technical actions and physical coordination [1]. However, traditional cross-country skiing training and technical action analysis often rely on the coach’s experience and subjective observation, which has certain limitations and is difficult to achieve accurate quantification. Therefore, taking artificial intelligence methods to achieve traditional cross-country skiing training and technical action analysis is the key to achieving standardized action quantification. With the development of artificial intelligence, there are new possibilities for the recognition and analysis of cross-country skiing actions [2]. At present, some algorithms have been applied to cross-country skiing action recognition and analysis, such as Ant Colony Optimization (ACO). Ant colony algorithm is an optimization algorithm that simulates the foraging behavior of ants in nature, which has been successfully applied in many fields, especially in data clustering and pattern recognition. Its basic principle is to solve the optimization problem by simulating the path selection mechanism of ants searching for food [3]. In cross-country skiing action recognition, the ACO can assist in analyzing athletes’ action data by identifying the optimal paths between data points to recognize and classify different skiing techniques [4]. Traditional research only recognizes actions based on accelerometer data, ignoring the potential contribution of other important information to action recognition, resulting in limited recognition accuracy. Secondly, traditional methods perform poorly in handling noise and discrete data, and are susceptible to external interference factors, thereby reducing the robustness and reliability of action recognition. The research aims to explore a classical cross-country skiing action recognition method based on the ant colony clustering algorithm to solve the limitations in the existing training and technical analysis, and improve the accuracy of motion recognition and training effectiveness. This research method model can provide more scientific guidance for athletes’ technical training and improve their competitive level by identifying and analyzing cross-country skiing actions. To solve the cross-country skiing action recognition, the research proposes a method that combines the K-means clustering algorithm and the ACO. Innovatively applying ant colony clustering algorithm to cross-country skiing action recognition research has improved the performance of cross-country skiing action recognition methods in handling noise and discrete data, thereby enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of cross-country skiing action recognition in images. Meanwhile, filters and data dimensionality reduction techniques are used to reduce data redundancy and interference, thereby improving the overall recognition quality and effectiveness of the action recognition model. The research also innovatively uses sensors, chips, and other devices for data collection to enhance the model’s ability to collect cross-country skiing motion data. In practical applications, the method can be used in cross-country skiing training systems to help coaches and athletes analyze and improve their actions in real-time, thus improving training effects and sports performance. This research is divided into five sections. The first section introduces the research background and purpose. The second section elaborates the current research at home and abroad. The third section mainly analyzes the construction process of the research method model. The fourth section is mainly to verify the feasibility and performance of the research method model. The fifth section mainly summarizes the overall content of the article. The proposed cross-country skiing action recognition method, which combines ACO clustering algorithm has significant differences from existing ACO clustering models in multiple key aspects. Firstly, this method is customized for the specificity of cross-country skiing action data, and can handle high-dimensional motion parameters and complex spatiotemporal features, which is in sharp contrast to most existing models that are only suitable for general data classification tasks. Secondly, this article improves the mechanism of ACO algorithm by introducing additional heuristic information and pheromone update mechanism, which enhances the convergence speed and global search ability of the algorithm, and solves the problem of traditional models easily falling into local optima in the initial search stage. In addition, this article combines various data preprocessing techniques such as low-pass filtering, normalization, and PCA dimensionality reduction to effectively remove noise and redundant data, enhancing the robustness of the model. In terms of motion parameter analysis, this article not only considers common parameters such as acceleration and angular velocity, but also combines multidimensional parameters such as posture, motion amplitude, and joint angle to more comprehensively identify skiing actions.

Literature review

With the development of technology, Human Action Recognition (HAR) has been widely developed. To enhance the complexity and diversity of HAR, Saleem et al. [5] proposed a HAR classification method to gain a deeper understanding of the complexity and diversity of human activity recognition. This method covers online/offline, multi-modal/uni-modal, and various methods based on manual features and learning. The research results indicate that HAR is closely connected to multiple living systems, and its trend changes rapidly with technological progress. Through comparative analysis, this study reveals the challenges in the field of HAR and points out research directions. To improve the overall HAR, Sarkar et al. [6] proposed a hybrid architecture HAR model based on continuous wavelet transform and spatial attention CNN to solve the spatiotemporal context extraction in automatic HAR in wearable sensor data. The research results indicate that the GA-based feature selection method has significantly better classification performance than existing models on five publicly available HAR datasets. A lower number of features can also improve overall recognition accuracy. To solve the challenge of HAR in videos, Liu et al. [7] proposed a recognition method based on the ResNext network. The method combines RGB and optical flow data to extract appearance and temporal dynamic features to recognize various human actions. Experimental results show that the method improves the recognition performance on UCF101 and HMDB51 datasets. To solve the efficiency and accuracy of data processing in HAR, Gao et al. [8] proposed a multidimensional data model for motion recognition and motion capture of video images based on a deep learning framework to improve the recognition accuracy of small-scale videos and the computational efficiency of large-scale datasets. The results show that the algorithm has a high recognition rate for both large-scale datasets and small-scale gesture actions, with an average classification accuracy of 85.79% and running at about 20 frames per second.

In the practical research of HAR, some data processing methods have also brought better research direction in the field. Ferrari et al. [9] applied personalization to traditional and deep learning techniques to address the challenges in the HAR field. The research results indicate that applying personalization to traditional machine learning techniques (such as AdaBoost) is most effective, while deep learning without personalization (such as convolutional neural networks) performs best on large datasets. This indicates that the choice of method depends on the size and diversity of the dataset. Charan et al. [10] proposed a classification framework that combines a novel Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Decision Tree (DT) to predict human activity recognition and analyze its performance in machine learning. The research results indicate that LSTM has an accuracy of 93.83% in predicting and analyzing activities, significantly better than that of DT. The accuracy of the new LSTM technology in human activity analysis is 90.12%, demonstrating its potential in the field of HAR. Li et al. [11] proposed a multi-sensor combination optimization algorithm based on discrete particle swarm optimization to solve the multi-sensor redundancy in human skiing behavior recognition. The algorithm selects the combination with better recognition performance and better quantity from the positions of multiple sensors. The research results show that selecting sensor position combinations from six parts including the left calf can achieve a recognition accuracy of 89.24%, verifying the effectiveness of the algorithm. It can be seen that using sensors and particle swarm optimization algorithms can improve the recognition accuracy of skiing actions. Zhang et al. [12] proposed a new Multi-graph Generative temporal Channel Spatial Attention (MGG-TCSA) network to address the shortcomings of existing skiing activity recognition research in outdoor data collection, recognition accuracy, and fall recognition. This network utilizes Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) for human skiing activity recognition. The research results indicate that the MGG-TCSA network performs better than the baseline and most existing models, which is effective in both indoor and outdoor skiing, demonstrating the feasibility of using indoor data for outdoor research. Using attention networks can recognize and analyze indoor skiing actions, but further exploration is still needed for skiing action recognition.

In summary, traditional action recognition methods rely heavily on single sensor data and are susceptible to noise and discrete data interference, resulting in limited recognition accuracy. Firstly, ACO, by simulating swarm intelligence path optimization mechanisms, can effectively handle high-dimensional motion parameters and complex spatiotemporal features, significantly improving robustness to noise and outliers. Meanwhile, the review emphasizes that existing clustering models are sensitive to initial values and prone to falling into local optima, while ACO enhances global convergence ability and avoids premature convergence by introducing pheromone positive feedback mechanism and heuristic search, combined with improved pheromone update strategy and concentration threshold limitation. Therefore, ACO can significantly improve the recognition performance of action recognition, proving its unique advantages and necessity in this field.

Experimental setup and basic information of participants

Firstly, raw data is transmitted to the edge computing device (NVIDIA Jetson Nano) through the BLE 5.0 Bluetooth module, and low-pass filtering, normalization and PCA dimension reduction preprocessing are performed to eliminate noise and redundancy. Subsequently, the data is divided into a training set and a testing set, and five-fold cross-validation is used to ensure the model’s generalization ability. Finally, the preprocessed feature vectors are input to the cloud server to run the algorithm model. In the comparison algorithm, the ACO clustering algorithm has a heuristic factor α = 1.0, an expected heuristic factor β = 5.0, and an pheromone volatilization coefficient ρ = 0.3. The upper and lower limits of pheromone concentration are set to 0.5 and 0.01, respectively. The number of iterations is 200. In the K-means algorithm, the number of clusters is k = 3 and the maximum number of iterations is 500. HCA adopts Euclidean distance and Ward connection method. DCA sets the neighborhood radius ε to 0.5 and the minimum sample size min_samples to 5. The study recruits 20 skiers with different skill levels as participants, including 10 high-level active athletes at the provincial level or above and 10 low-level amateur enthusiasts. All participants are in good health, have no contraindications to exercise, and voluntarily participate in the experiment through written informed consent. The study uses ICM-20,689 six axis motion sensors fixed on key parts of the subjects’ limbs to collect 1,350 valid sample data of three classic cross-country skiing techniques: Alternating Glide (AG), Double Stick Push Up Glide (DG) and Figure Eight Step (FS). All experimental procedures are reviewed by the ethics committee, and the data is anonymized to ensure the privacy of the subjects.

Display of action recognition results

To evaluate the actual data of athletes, it is necessary to take multiple measurements of the same athlete at different time points and compare the consistency of these measurement results. The data is divided into a training set and a testing set. The training set trains the model, while the testing set verifies the performance of the model. The cross-validation frequency of data has been increased to improve the reliability and generalization ability of the model. Finally, the collected data is compared with other reliable external standard data, subjective feedback from athletes on their performance are collected, and compared with the model’s predicted results to validate the data validity. The mean, variance, and standard deviation of the data are calculated to evaluate the quality of the data. This study conducts data analysis on athlete data through methods such as cross-validation, data training, and indicator evaluation. The traditional cross-country skiing classic actions are analyzed and disassembled. The selected actions are AG, DG, and FS. Technical dismantling and analysis are performed on three different actions, and two skiers are asked to complete these actions, after which the data and action effects are analyzed. The action recognition data of the three actions of the two athletes are uploaded, as shown in Fig. 8. The study analyzes and decomposes the classic actions of traditional cross-country skiing, selecting three actions: AG, DG, and FS. To make the actions more practical, three different actions are technically disassembled and divided, and two skiers are required to complete the actions. Further analysis is conducted on the data and motion effects, making the entire research process more meaningful for real action recognition. The signal sensing layer adopts the ICM-20,689 six-axis motion sensor chip, combined with the BLE 5.0 Bluetooth module to collect the athlete’s posture, acceleration, and joint angle data in real-time, and fixes them in the key positions on the limbs through elastic straps. The data pre-processing layer is completed by the NVIDIA Jetson Nano edge computing device (4GB RAM, 128-core GPU) to complete the low-pass filtering, normalization, and PCA dimensionality reduction. The action recognition layer relies on a cloud server (Intel Xeon 8-core CPU, 32GB RAM) to run the ant colony clustering algorithm and optimize the clustering centre and pheromone update mechanism. GPS devices obtain real-time position and horizontal movement speed of athletes. Doppler radar sensors measure the instantaneous velocity of athletes. Portable anemometers are installed on athletes’ helmets to measure real-time environmental wind speed and direction. Inertial motion capture system accurately calculates the range of limb motion. Flexible strain sensors are embedded in ski suits or protective gear to measure joint bending angles.

The dataset collection is achieved by installing IMU sensors at key positions of skiers, which can collect real-time parameters such as posture, direction, and acceleration of athletes during classic skiing actions such as AG, DG, and FS. After data collection is completed, the data is transmitted to the main system through Bluetooth technology. The Sum of Squared intra-cluster Error (SSE) of clustering algorithms is taken as the performance criterion for the model. SSE represents the sum of squared intra-cluster errors of the model, which is the sum of mean squared variances from different sample points to the corresponding cluster centers. Discussion

It can be seen that the system model used in this study can effectively recognize athletes’ actions. The new system can guide athletes to adjust their posture by identifying real-world skiing actions. Athletes can also analyze their own actions and make adjustments to standardize their actions, thereby improving their skiing performance. The study still has limitations such as limited sample size, insufficient environmental adaptability verification, algorithm parameter sensitivity, and real-time bottlenecks. Therefore, future efforts should focus on optimizing the system through establishing multi-center databases, developing adaptive parameter algorithms, and researching flexible sensors to enhance its practicality and universality in complex training environments. The current findings provide a technical foundation for intelligent skiing training, but the actual application effectiveness still requires further validation.

Conclusion

To make current action training in cross-country skiing more scientific and reasonable, enhance the practical significance of skiing action training, and reduce over-reliance on coaches, the study proposed an innovative action recognition system and improved the model by incorporating ACO and clustering algorithms. The research results show that the proposed method demonstrates excellent motion recognition and decomposition capabilities. The mean SSE values of the proposed method are 0.95, 0.95, and 0.85, respectively, which are 0.25, 0.26, and 0.25 lower than those of of the highest-performing model. Additionally, the proposed method has the lowest SSE value among the five algorithms. In the comparison of average percentage error, the error value of the proposed method is only 0.62, which is 0.11 lower than that of the lowest model. In the comparison of model convergence, the convergence of the proposed method is lower than that of the highest model.

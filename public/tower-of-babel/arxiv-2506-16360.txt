Orbital Order and Superconductivity in Bilayer Nickelate Compounds
Giniyat Khaliullin, Jiří Chaloupka

Abstract
We propose a theory for bilayer nickelate materials, where a large tetragonal field – intrinsic or induced by epitaxial strain – lifts the orbital degeneracy and localizes the 3 ​ z 2 − r 2 3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2} orbital states. These states host local spins S = 1 / 2 S=1/2 bound into singlets by strong interlayer coupling, and their dynamics is described by weakly dispersive singlet-triplet excitations (“triplons”). The charge carriers occupy the wide bands of x 2 − y 2 x^{2}\!-\!y^{2} symmetry, and their Cooper pairing is mediated by the high-energy triplon excitations. As the x 2 − y 2 x^{2}\!-\!y^{2} band filling increases, i.e. moving further away from the Ni 3+ valence state, the indirect Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida interactions between local spins induce spin-density-wave order via triplon condensation. Implications of the model for compressively strained La 3 Ni 2 O 7 films and electron doped oxychloride Sr 3 Ni 2 O 5 Cl 2 are discussed.



Orbital degeneracy is a common feature of transition metal compounds and plays
a fundamental role in a wide range of physical phenomena, from magnetism to
metal-insulator and structural transitions
Imada et al. 1998
;
Khomskii 2014
;
Khaliullin 2005
. In recent
years, especially after the discovery of superconductivity (SC) in a number of
nickel-based oxides
Li et al. 2019
;
Sun et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
, orbital physics has become a
central issue also in the field of high-temperature superconductivity.

Nickelates are known for their rich spin, orbital, and electronic
properties
Medarde 1997
;
Imada et al. 1998
. It is remarkable that the Ni valence states and
low-energy orbital structures in the nickelate SCs vary broadly. While

NdNiO
2

Li et al. 2019
with
Ni
+
ions and active

x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
orbitals seems to fit well into the cuprate’s
“single-layer, single-orbital, spin one-half” paradigm,

La
3
Ni
2
O
7

Sun et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
has a bilayer structure with the
mixed valence
Ni
2.5+
ions, and both
e_{g}
orbitals

x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
and
3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
are essential.

At ambient pressure,
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
shows magnetic order, and
becomes SC with high
T_{c}\sim 80\>\mathrm{K}
under high pressure. The current
theories (see, e.g.,
Sakakibara et al. 2024
;
Yang et al. 2023a
;
Liao et al. 2023
;
Yang et al. 2023b
;
Liu et al. 2023
;

3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
electrons which drives high-

, while

x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}

the planes. These theories are based on the multiorbital band structures
provided by ab-initio calculations. The experimental data on the
superconducting samples is limited, however, because the high pressure
conditions are necessary to stabilize SC.

An important progress has been made recently: Under a compressive strain, the
bilayer nickelate films exhibit SC even at ambient
pressure
Ko et al. 2025
;
Zhou et al. 2025
;
Liu et al.
;
Hao et al.
;
Hsu et al.

the microscopic origin of SC and its stabilization at ambient pressure,
especially about the role played by the lattice strain

Motivated by these observations, we propose in this Letter a theoretical
model, which reveals a physical mechanism by which the in-plane compressive
strain stabilizes the superconductivity in bilayer nickelates. The general
phase behavior of the model, including magnetic ordering as a function of Ni
valence, is discussed.

Our basic idea is that the compressive strain, which elongates the oxygen
octahedra, lifts the
e_{g}
orbital degeneracy and localizes the

3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
orbitals. The orbital ordering reduces the low-energy
Hilbert space available for the charge carriers, thus promoting
metal-insulator Mott transition. As an illustration, let us consider a single
layer nickelate with Ni
d^{7}(t_{2g}^{6}e_{g}^{1})
configuration. The Mott
transition takes place when Coulomb repulsion between electrons on different
orbitals
U^{\prime}=U-2J_{\mathrm{H}}
(
J_{\mathrm{H}}
is Hund’s coupling) exceeds the

e_{g}
orbital bandwidth
W\sim 3-4\>\mathrm{eV}
. Due to
pd
-covalency,
effective interaction
U^{\prime}
in nickelates is rather small, comparable to
W
,
and they are typically metallic. In the presence of a tetragonal splitting

\Delta
of the
e_{g}
level, however, the double occupation costs higher
energy,
U^{\prime}+\Delta
, which may well exceed
W
and thus stabilize the

3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
orbital ordered Mott insulator. The case example is
nickel oxyhalides
Sr
2
NiO
{}_{3}X
(
X
=Cl, F), which are indeed
antiferromagnetic Mott insulators
Tsujimoto et al. 2022
, with an unpaired
e_{g}

electron in the
3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
state.

The model
.—The basic structure of our model is shown in
Fig.
1
. The
e_{g}
level splitting
\Delta\sim 1\>\mathrm{eV}
,
as evaluated in
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
films
Bhatta et al.
and in
oxychlorides
Tsujimoto et al. 2022
;
Ochi et al. 2025
is assumed to be large enough to localize an
electron in the
3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
states, which overlap mainly on the
interlayer
c
-axis bonds. The remaining
e_{g}
electrons, with an average
density
\delta<1
per Ni ion, occupy
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
states forming a
wide conduction bands. The local and itinerant electrons interact via the
Hund’s and Kondo couplings. We now present the derivation of the model in
detail.

(
i
) We start with the spin interactions between
3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}

orbitals. We use shorthand notations
x
and
z
for
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
and

3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
states, correspondingly, and parameterize their
nearest-neighbor hoppings as follows. Strong overlap between
z
orbitals on
the
c
-axis bonds gives the largest hopping denoted as
-t
, with
t>0
. The
orbital hoppings on the nearest-neighbor in-plane bonds are then given by

t_{xx}=-\frac{3}{4}t
,
t_{zz}=-\frac{1}{4}t
, and
t_{xz}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}t
(upper/lower sign corresponds to
x/y
bond direction)
SKn
.

Standard calculations give the following expressions for the interlayer
J_{c}

and intralayer
J_{ab}
exchange couplings between the localized spins
\boldsymbol{S}

hosted by
z
orbitals:

J_{c}=\frac{4t^{2}}{U}\,,\qquad J_{ab}/J_{c}=\frac{1}{16}\left(1-3\,\frac{J_{\mathrm{H}}}{U}\right).

(1)

The second term in
J_{ab}
is due to the interorbital
t_{xz}
hopping and
Hund’s coupling in a virtual state. With
t\simeq 0.6\>\mathrm{eV}
and

U\simeq 4.5\>\mathrm{eV}
, appropriate for Ni ions, we evaluate
J_{c}\simeq 0.3\>\mathrm{eV}
.
J_{ab}
is very small because of weak overlap of
z

orbitals within the planes; a representative value of
J_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.6\>\mathrm{eV}
gives
J_{ab}/J_{c}\simeq 0.04
. Thus the local spins are
tightly bound into interlayer singlet pairs of
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}
and
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}

(see Fig.
1
). Following the bond-operator formalism for bilayer
antiferromagnets
Sachdev and Bhatt 1990
, we define triplon
{\boldsymbol{T}}
operators:

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1,2}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\bigl({\boldsymbol{T}}+{\boldsymbol{T}}^{\dagger}\bigr)-\frac{i}{2}\bigl({\boldsymbol{T}}^{\dagger}\times{\boldsymbol{T}}\bigr),

(2)

where upper/lower sign refers to the first/second layer. Triplons are
hard-core bosons describing singlet-triplet transitions within the
c
-axis
dimer bonds. The exchange interactions on these bonds

J_{c}(\boldsymbol{S}_{i1}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{i2})
, and the in-plane interactions

J_{ab}(\boldsymbol{S}_{i1}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j1}+\boldsymbol{S}_{i2}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j2})

on the nearest-neighbor bonds
\langle ij\rangle
are expressed via
{\boldsymbol{T}}

operators. Far from magnetic order, triplon interactions can be neglected, and
diagonalization of the remaining quadratic form gives the triplon dispersion

\omega_{\boldsymbol{q}}=J_{c}\sqrt{1+r\gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\,,

(3)

where
r=4J_{ab}/J_{c}\ll 1
, and
\gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}=(\cos q_{x}+\cos q_{y})/2
. As
a result, local spin dynamics is described by a weakly dispersive
singlet-triplet excitations at high energies
\omega_{\boldsymbol{q}}\simeq J_{c}\simeq 0.3\>\mathrm{eV}
[see Fig.
2
(a)].

(
ii
) Conduction electrons in the
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
orbital states
form wide bands, determined by large in-plane hopping

t_{xx}=-\frac{3}{4}t\sim-0.45\>\mathrm{eV}
like in cuprates. Finite
interlayer hoppings of the
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
electrons are allowed due to
hybridization with the oxygen
p
states and virtual
t_{xz}
hoppings to
z

orbitals. The interlayer processes may operate within the
c
-axis rungs,

-t_{\perp}(c^{\dagger}_{i1}c_{i2}+\mathrm{H.c.})
, as well as connect the
farther neighbors, i.e.
-t^{\prime}_{\perp}(c^{\dagger}_{i1}c_{j2}+\mathrm{H.c.})
with

j\neq i
. The main effect of these terms is to form bonding

\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(c_{1}+c_{2})_{\boldsymbol{k}}
and antibonding

\beta_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(c_{1}-c_{2})_{\boldsymbol{k}}
states with the
dispersion relations

\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha}=-3t\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}}-t_{\perp},\qquad\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\beta}=-3t\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}}+t_{\perp},

(4)

which are depicted in Fig.
2
(b). For simplicity, we omit here
the long-range hoppings such as in-plane
t^{\prime}
or the above

t^{\prime}_{\perp}

TBn
. These are present in real materials but not
essential for our basic demonstration of the model.

(
iii
) Now we consider interaction between local
\boldsymbol{S}
and conduction
electron spins
{\boldsymbol{S}}^{c}=\frac{1}{2}c^{\dagger}_{s^{\prime}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{s^{\prime}s}c_{s}
.
There are two distinct spin exchange channels. First, the ferromagnetic Hund’s
coupling
-2J_{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{S}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{c})
. Second, the antiferromagnetic
Kondo interaction caused by virtual hoppings
t_{xz}
between nearest-neighbor

z_{i}
and
x_{j}
orbitals,
t_{xz}(x^{\dagger}_{j}z_{i}+z^{\dagger}_{i}x_{j})
with

t_{xz}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}t
. In
\boldsymbol{k}
-space, these processes lead to the
following term in the Hamiltonian:

\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}},\sigma}V_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{i}\mathrm{e}^{i{\boldsymbol{k}}{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}\left(c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}z_{i\sigma}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right),

(5)

where
V_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\,\eta_{\boldsymbol{k}}
and
\eta_{\boldsymbol{k}}=(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})
. Treating this term perturbatively, we obtain Kondo interaction:

H_{\mathrm{K}}=2\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}V_{\boldsymbol{k}}V_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{E^{+}}+\frac{1}{E^{-}}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right),

(6)

where
\boldsymbol{S}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{2}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{s^{\prime}s}c_{\boldsymbol{k}s}
, and
\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{k}\!-\!\boldsymbol{q}
.

E^{+}
(
E^{-}
) is the energy cost required to add (remove) an electron to
(from) a singly-occupied
z
orbital level.

The total interaction between the local and itinerant spins within a bilayer
reads then as:

H_{\mathrm{int}}=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right)_{1}+\left(\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right)_{2}\right],

(7)

where the coupling constant

J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\!=2J_{\mathrm{H}}\left(1-\kappa\,\eta_{\boldsymbol{k}}\eta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\right)

(8)

includes both Hund’s and Kondo couplings. The latter brings about the momentum
dependence in the exchange process, and its relative strength is given by

\kappa=\frac{3}{4}\frac{t^{2}}{J_{\mathrm{H}}\widetilde{E}}\,,\quad\text{where}\quad\frac{1}{\widetilde{E}}=\left(\frac{1}{E^{+}}+\frac{1}{E^{-}}\right).

(9)

The interorbital excitation energies
E^{+}
and
E^{-}
depend on various
parameters such as
U
,
J_{\mathrm{H}}
, intersite Coulomb interactions,
tetragonal splitting, doping dependent Fermi level position, etc. Using a
representative value of
\widetilde{E}\simeq 1\>\mathrm{eV}
, and

t\simeq J_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.6\>\mathrm{eV}
, we arrive at a rough estimate
of
\kappa\simeq 0.5
. This implies that the overall coupling

J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}
is ferromagnetic and thus free of Kondo problem. We also
note that, in contrast to manganites with large total spin
S=2
, the Hund’s
splitting in nickelates is smaller than the
e_{g}
orbital bandwidth

W\sim 3-4\>\mathrm{eV}
, i.e. well below the double-exchange limit of

2J_{\mathrm{H}}>W
. For these reasons, we can safely treat the

J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}
coupling effects perturbatively.

Spin-singlet phase and superconductivity
.—We first rewrite
Eq. (
7
) in terms of a triplon field defined as

\phi_{\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(T+T^{\dagger})_{\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(S_{1}-S_{2})_{\nu}
, where
\nu=(x,y,z)
. As the triplon density in the singlet
phase is small, we keep
\phi
-linear terms only:

H_{\mathrm{int}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\;\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{S}_{\alpha\beta}+\boldsymbol{S}_{\beta\alpha}\right)_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\;.

(10)

Here, spin density operator
(\boldsymbol{S}_{\alpha\beta})_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{s^{\prime}s}\beta_{\boldsymbol{k}s}

connects bonding
\alpha
and antibonding
\beta
fermions, as dictated by the
odd parity of the triplon field.

Without magnetic order, the triplon Green’s function matrix is diagonal and
isotropic:
\Phi_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}=\langle T_{\tau}\phi_{\nu}\phi_{\nu^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\Phi
. In harmonic approximation, i.e. neglecting triplon
interactions, the bare Green’s function is given by

\Phi_{0}=\frac{J_{c}}{\omega^{2}+J_{c}\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\quad\text{with}\quad\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{q}}=J_{c}(1+r\gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}).

(11)

This propagator describes singlet-triplet excitations with the energy

\omega_{\boldsymbol{q}}=(J_{c}\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{q}})^{1/2}
of Eq. (
3
). We use it
to evaluate the fermionic self-energy in Fig.
2
(c), and obtain
the mass enhancement factor of
(1+\lambda)
, where

\lambda\approx\frac{3}{2}\frac{J_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}}{J_{c}}\;N(0)

(12)

quantifies the coupling constant in the model. As a rough estimate, we may
take density of states (per spin) at the Fermi level
N(0)\simeq 0.2-0.3\>\mathrm{eV}^{-1}
, and using
J_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 0.6\>\mathrm{eV}
,

J_{c}\simeq 0.3\>\mathrm{eV}
, obtain
\lambda\simeq 0.4-0.5
.

As in the electron-phonon problem, the same parameter
\lambda
quantifies the
strength of the pairing interaction mediated by singlet-triplet excitations.
Figure
2
(d) shows that these excitations lead to the pair
correlations between the bonding
\alpha
and antibonding
\beta
states. We
find that the SC order parameters on these bands have an opposite sign,

\Delta^{\mathrm{SC}}_{\alpha}=-\Delta^{\mathrm{SC}}_{\beta}
. This so-called
interband
s_{\pm}
symmetry is common to spin-mediated pairing models,
including those proposed for nickelates
Yang et al. 2023a
;
Liu et al. 2023
. Opposite to the
prevailing view, however, superconductivity in our model emerges from Cooper
pairing between the
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
electrons, while

3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
orbital sector provides the singlet-triplet excitations
as a pairing glue.

BCS mean-field treatment of the interaction in Fig.
2
(d) leads to
high values of
T_{c}\!\sim\!\Omega\exp(-1/\lambda)
, where
\Omega=J_{c}
(or
Fermi energy
\varepsilon_{F}
at small doping
\delta
), due to high excitation
energy
J_{c}\sim 0.3\>\mathrm{eV}
and sizable coupling constant
\lambda
.
Even though a more quantitative treatment of the model would reduce this
estimate, it shows the great potential for high-
T_{c}
SC in the bilayer
nickelates under a large tetragonal field.

Magnetic instability at large doping
.—We now consider the effects of
the exchange coupling
J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}
on local spins. Including this
coupling, we obtain the Green’s function
\Phi
in the following form:

\Phi=\frac{J_{c}}{\omega^{2}+J_{c}(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{q},\omega})}\,,

(13)

where the triplon self-energy

\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{q},\omega}=-4J_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}\,\widetilde{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{q},\omega}

is determined by the
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
band spin susceptibility

\widetilde{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{q},\omega}
, see Fig.
3
(a). Magnetic order,
i.e. triplon condensation takes place when the static (
\omega=0
)
self-energy obeys the equation
\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{q}}+\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}}=0
. At small
density
\delta
of the band electrons, their mutual interactions can be
neglected, and
\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}}
is given by the bare susceptibility

\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}
, modified by the nonlocal nature of the exchange constant

J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}
(
8
):

\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}}=-2J_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\;(1-\kappa\,\eta_{\boldsymbol{k}}\eta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}})^{2}\;\,\frac{n_{\boldsymbol{k}}-n_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\;.

(14)

The self-energy evolves quickly with doping, especially at momenta away from

\Gamma
point [see Fig.
3
(b)], due to the Fermi surface shape and

\kappa
-term effects. As a result, the magnetic instability criteria is
fulfilled already at
\delta\simeq 0.4
, i.e. well below the Mott limit for

x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
orbital sector. Physically, the magnetic order is driven
by the indirect exchange interactions between local spins
\boldsymbol{S}
, mediated by
the
x^{2}\!-\!y^{2}
band electrons. Indeed, the triplon self-energy [a
fermionic bubble in Fig.
3
(a) and Eq. (
14
)] is nothing
but the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction between local spins,
and magnetic instability takes place when this interaction is large enough to
overcome singlet-triplet gap
\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{q}}\sim J_{c}
. There is a certain
analogy with the Doniach’s RKKY-vs-Kondo competition in heavy-fermion
systems
Doniach 1977
;
*[seealsoChapter17in][]Col15
, though the origin
of a nonmagnetic phase here (local spin dimers) is much different from Kondo
singlet formation.

We sketch in Fig.

core element is the interlayer spin-singlet dimers in the

Mott insulator
stabilized by orbital splitting. The superconductivity in the electron doped
spin-singlet phase is our main finding, which is a robust result based on the
textbook treatment of a weakly interacting Fermi liquid coupled to the gapped
singlet-triplet excitations. These excitations play the role of optical
phonons in the BCS theory, and their high energy
\sim 0.3\>\mathrm{eV}
and
sizable coupling to conduction spins should give rise to genuinely
high-temperature superconductivity. The magnetic order at

\delta>\delta_{\mathrm{crit}}
, resulting from competition between RKKY
interactions and spin-singlet gap is also an intrinsic property of the model;
however, its quantitative description is a nontrivial problem left for future
work. From a broader perspective, it is important to extend the model for
small
\Delta
values, and clarify how its properties, especially
superconductivity, evolve as the
3z^{2}\!-\!r^{2}
electrons get
delocalized to form a multiband metal.

Magnon thermal Hall effect in insulating antiferromagnets
Vladimir A. Zyuzin

Abstract
In this paper we theoretically discuss thermal Hall effect of magnons in insulating Néel ordered antiferromagnets at zero external magnetic field. We show that non-zero thermal Hall effect requires absence of any symmetry between the two sublattices. The thermal Hall effect of magnons will be non-zero by a virtue of the spin-momentum splitting of the magnon spectrum due to the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction as well as anisotropic second-nearest exchange interaction different in the two sublattices, both corresponding to the broken symmetry. We construct a theoretical model in which an external electric field may change the symmetry of the antiferromagnetic system thus altering the thermal Hall effect of magnons.



It has been understood that local non-magnetic environment of the Néel order in metallic antiferromagnets may give rise to spin splitting of conducting fermions which interact with the Néel order

HayamiYanagiKusunose2019

AHE_AFM

If the non-magnetic environment is the same for both sublattices then the two sublattices are connected by a combination of time-reversal and translation or inversion or rotations. In this case the conducting fermions are spin degenerate.
If the non-magnetic environment is such that only a combination of certain rotations or mirror operations and time-reversal connects the two sublattices, the spin-splitting of conducting fermions will be of the antiferromagnetic type corresponding to the rotation or mirror symmetry. Spin-splitting might be of the
d-
,
g-
,
i-
types
SmejkalSinovaJungwirth2022b
or mirror-symmetric
Zyuzin2024
.
In two dimensional systems these are genuine antiferromagnets with zero net magnetization. In three dimensional antiferromagnets with
d-
,
g-
, or
i-
types of spin-splitting there might be crystal planes in which reduction of symmetry between the sublattices can result in finite orbital magnetization of conducting fermions provided spin-orbit coupling consistent with the symmetry is included. Such antiferromagnets with relativistic orbital magnetization of conducting fermions have been suggested to call as the altermagnets
SmejkalSinovaJungwirth2022b
. Such a system may be thought of as a weak ferromagnet in which the conducting fermions are responsible for magnetization while the Néel order is intact. It is important to note that the magnetization is driven by a combination of spin-orbit interaction (similar to Dzyaloshinskii weak ferromagnetism
Dzyaloshinskii1958
) and a measure of broken symmetry between the sublattices.
In two dimensions if there will be no symmetries between the two sublattices then the spin-splitting will be of the ferromagnetic type with finite spin-polarization of conducting fermions
Zyuzin2024
. Only in this case there will be anomalous Hall effect corresponding to orbital magnetization in the system. In this paper we wish to understand implications of local non-magnetic environment of the Néel order in insulating antiferromagnets on thermal Hall effect (THE) of magnons
MatsumotoShindouMurakamiPRB2014
;

In particular we wish to understand under which conditions insulating collinear Néel ordered magnets can show the magnon carried THE in the absence of the external magnetic field applied to the system. We also wish to underline required physical ingredients for the THE to be non-zero.
In
ZyuzinKovalevPRL2016
the THE of magnons was shown to be absent in the collinear antiferromagnet on a honeycomb lattice in which case the two sublattices are connected by a combination of time-reversal and
\frac{\pi}{3}
rotation.
In
Pires
antiferromagnetic magnons have been studied in collinear antiferromagnet on a checkerboard lattice in which case the two sublattices are connected by a combination of time-reversal and
\frac{\pi}{2}
rotation.
The THE of magnons is absent in this system as well because of the symmetry between the sublattices.
In
Mook
a three-dimensional antiferromagnet on a rutile lattice was studied and it was found that non-zero THE of magnons is due to first-nearest neighbor DMI. Our inspection showed that, however, in addition to this DMI an anisotropic third-nearest neighbor Heisenberg exchange interaction (HEI) is also crucial for non-zero THE
Comment
.

Therefore, in this paper wish to better understand the mechanism of THE of magnons in antiferromagnets. For that we studied a transparent theoretical two-dimensional model of an antiferromagnet proposed in
Zyuzin2024
.
We show that finite THE of magnons can be found in insulating collinear antiferromagnets in which there is no symmetry between the two sublattices. The lack of symmetry can be achieved again by asymmetric between the sublattices environment of the non-magnetic atoms. In addition to this the first-nearest neighbor DMI consistent with the asymmetry of the sublattices as well as second-nearest HEI are required for the non-zero THE of magnons.

Furthermore, we suggest that external electric field can vary the symmetry of the lattice when the unit cell of the antiferromagnet can have a finite electric dipole moment. The system can then be driven to a low symmetry phase by the electric field.
In that way varying the direction of the field the THE of magnets can be altered. This can surve as a powerfull experimental tool to probe the structure of magnons.

The thermal Hall effect of antiferromagnetic magnons has been derived in
MatsumotoShindouMurakamiPRB2014
.
The thermal Hall effect is defined as the heat current carried by the magnons which flows in transverse direction to the applied temperature graient
\nabla_{\beta}\chi
, namely,

\displaystyle j^{\mathrm{TH}}_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{TV}\sum_{{\bf k};n}(\hat{\Omega}_{{\bf k};\alpha\beta})_{nn}c_{2}\left[(\hat{\sigma}_{3}\hat{E}_{\bf k})_{nn}\right]\nabla_{\beta}\chi,

(15)

where
c_{x}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}dzz^{2}\frac{dg(z)}{dz}
, where
g(z)=(e^{z/T}-1)^{-1}
is the Bose-Einstein distribution function.
Berry curvature of magnons is a
4\times 4
diagonal matrix,

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{xy;{\bf k}}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\Omega_{xy;{\bf k}},~\Omega_{xy;-{\bf k}},~-\Omega_{xy;-{\bf k}},~-\Omega_{xy;{\bf k}}\right),

(16)

where

\Omega_{xy;{\bf k}}=\frac{w_{xy;{\bf k}}}{2\varepsilon_{\bf k}^{3}}

and where

\displaystyle w_{xy;{\bf k}}

\displaystyle=(3+T_{\bf k})\left[\partial_{\alpha}\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\partial_{\beta}\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k}-\partial_{\beta}\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\partial_{\alpha}\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k}\right]

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k}\left[\partial_{\alpha}\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\partial_{\beta}T_{\bf k}-\partial_{\alpha}T_{\bf k}\partial_{\beta}\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\left[\partial_{\alpha}\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k}\partial_{\beta}T_{\bf k}-\partial_{\alpha}T_{\bf k}\partial_{\beta}\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k}\right].

(17)

It is instructive to introduce a part of the curvature

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{xy;{\bf k}}=\partial_{\alpha}\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\partial_{\beta}\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k}-\partial_{\beta}\mathrm{Im}\gamma_{\bf k}\partial_{\alpha}\mathrm{Re}\gamma_{\bf k},

(18)

which is the part of the Berry curvature due to the first-nearest neighbor HEI.

We can arrange terms in the expression for the thermal Hall effect as

\displaystyle j^{\mathrm{TH}}_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{TV}\sum_{{\bf k};n}\Omega_{{\bf k};\alpha\beta}\left[c_{2}\left(\delta_{\bf k}+\varepsilon_{\bf k}\right)+c_{2}\left(-\delta_{\bf k}+\varepsilon_{\bf k}\right)\right]\nabla_{\beta}\chi.

(19)

It is instructive to separate the Berry curvature
\Omega_{{\bf k};\alpha\beta}
to components which are even and odd in momentum as
\Omega_{\bf k}=\Omega^{(\mathrm{e})}_{\bf k}+\Omega^{(\mathrm{o})}_{\bf k}
, where
\Omega^{(\mathrm{e}/\mathrm{o})}_{-\bf k}=\pm\Omega^{(\mathrm{e}/\mathrm{o})}_{\bf k}
.
We observe that
\delta_{\bf k}
isn’t important in obtaining the non-zero THE to linear order.
However, there might be effects to second order in
\delta_{\bf k}
provided it has both even and odd parts.
Even in momentum Berry curvature will give a contribution regardless of
\delta_{\bf k}
provided an integral over the angles doesn’t vanish.
To the zeroth order in
\delta_{\bf k}
, for the odd part of the Berry curvature to give a contribution to THE, we must require
\varepsilon_{\bf k}\neq\varepsilon_{-\bf k}
, and extract only the component of
\varepsilon_{\bf k}
odd in momentum. The odd component in
\varepsilon_{\bf k}
is due to the odd in momentum DMI.

From
ZyuzinKovalevPRL2016

Pires

Pires

Then, in
Mook

Pires

Here we show that in the two-dimensional antiferromagnets (shown in Fig. (
1

THE of magnons will be non-zero only when all symmetries between the two sublattices are broken. For that let us discuss the details of models A and B.

In the model A given in the left of Fig. (

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{xy;{\bf k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sin\left(\frac{k_{y}}{2}\right)\left[\cos\left(\frac{k_{y}}{2}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}k_{x}}{2}+\theta\right)\right],

Let us draw a possible implementation of predicted in this paper scenario of non-zero THE. Assume that charge of the green and red/blue atoms are of opposite sign. In our model the most symmetric phase is when the green atom is in the center of the honeycomb plaquette. No THE of magnons is expected in this case.
The system in the most symmetric phase is paraelectric, meaning there is no dipole moment in the unit cell. Then by applying external electric field in the plane of the system, we can displace the green atom to take positions anywhere in the honeycomb plaquette. For example, in the way it is shown in Fig. (

THE will cross zero twice.

Finally, we note that all of the discussion above would not work if the Néel order would be in the plane of the system. This is because the DMI would be absent in the magnon Hamiltonian.

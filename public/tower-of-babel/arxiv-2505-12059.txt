Distance and best approximations in operator norm and trace class norm
Saikat Roy

Abstract
. We study the best approximation and distance problems in the operator space ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) and in the space of trace class operators ℒ 1 ​ ( ℬ ​ ( ℋ ) ) \mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})) . Formulations of distances are obtained in both cases. The case of finite-dimensional C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is also considered. The computational advantage of the results is illustrated through examples.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the article
\mathcal{H}
denotes an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space over
\mathbb{C}
, equipped with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The symbol
S_{\mathcal{H}}
stands for the unit sphere of
\mathcal{H}
. For any sequence
(\zeta_{n})\subset\mathcal{H}
, we write
\zeta_{n}\xrightarrow{w}\zeta_{0}
to mean
(\zeta_{n})
weakly converges to
\zeta_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the
C^{*}
-algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, the ideal of compact operators, and
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, the ideal of trace class operators of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. If
\mathcal{V}
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we define

N_{\mathcal{V}}:=\{a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):~Tr(ay)=0,~y\in\mathcal{V}\}=\{a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):~Tr(ay_{j})=0,~1\leq j\leq k\}

for any basis
\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}\}
of
\mathcal{V}
. For any two vectors
\xi
and
\zeta
in
\mathcal{H}
,
\xi\otimes\zeta
denotes the rank-one operator
\eta\mapsto\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle\zeta
(
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
). spectrum of an element
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is denoted by
\sigma(x)
.

1.1.
Motivation

A fundamental result due to Holmes and Kripke
[
4
]
asserts that
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is a proximinal subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and for any
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, the distance from
x
to
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is given by
dist(x,\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}))=\Delta(x)
, where
\Delta(x):=\sup\{\lim_{n}\sup~\|x\zeta_{n}\|:~(\zeta_{n})\subset S_{\mathcal{H}},~\zeta_{n}\xrightarrow{w}\mathbf{0}\}
(see also
[
1
,
3
]
). It is natural to ask whether
dist(x,\mathcal{V})=\Delta(x)
, if we consider a proper subspace
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
instead of the whole space
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. The answer to this question is negative, as shown in Example
2.1
. Thus, the quantity
\Delta(x)
becomes a lower bound of
dist(x,\mathcal{V})
. This leads to the question: what is the exact formulation of
dist(x,\mathcal{V})
for
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
and a proper subspace
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
? In this article, we formulate
dist(x,\mathcal{V})
in two cases. In the first case, we consider
\mathcal{V}
to be any finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and this answers the above question in the general framework of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
which in particular applies to the case of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. In the second case, we consider the distance problem in the trace class norm when
x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\mathcal{V}
is any finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
.

1.2.
Approach

A general formulation of distance from a point
x
to a subspace
Y
in a Banach space
X
is given by the classical duality principle
[
12
]
. In
[
10
]
, an alternative approach is presented for
real
reflexive Banach spaces, sometimes under the assumption of strict convexity, which is focused on norm attainment of functionals. Such an approach is computationally advantageous as pointed out in
[
10
, Section 4]
,
[
11
]
. Here we consider the duality relations between
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and utilize the
\sigma
-WOT (
\sigma
-weak operator topology) compactness of the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
to prove our results. Our methods are based on Banach space theoretic and elementary operator algebraic techniques.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is identified to the dual of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
through the isometric isomorphism
\Omega:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{*}
, defined as

\Omega(x)=\rho_{x},\quad x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),~\text{where}~\rho_{x}(a)=Tr(ax),\quad a\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})).

Similarly, the space
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
can be identified to the dual of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
through the isometric isomorphism
\Gamma:\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{*}
, defined as

\Gamma(x)=\tau_{x},\quad x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\text{where}~\tau_{x}(a)=Tr(ax),\quad a\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}).

Adapting such an approach, we formulate the distance between a point and a subspace in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
both under the usual operator norm and trace norm, in terms of trace.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle dist(x,\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\{|Tr((\xi\otimes\zeta)x)|:~(\xi\otimes\zeta)\in N_{\mathcal{V}},\|\xi\|=\|\zeta\|=1\}.

Applying Theorem
1.1
we get a characterization of best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then
z\in\mathcal{V}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
if and only if
\Omega(x-z)
is a Hahn-Banach extension of
\Omega(x)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}
.

For trace class operators, we have the following formulation of distance.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
with
dim~\mathcal{V}=n
. Let
x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then there exist scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in[0,1]

(1\leq k\leq 2n+1)
with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=

\displaystyle\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\cap N_{\mathcal{V}},~\|a\|=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\cap N_{\mathcal{V}},~\|a\|=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{k}Tr(u_{k}x):~\sum_{i=1}^{k}Im~\lambda_{i}Tr(u_{i}x)=0,~\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}Tr(u_{i}w)=0,~v\in\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle u_{i}^{*}u_{i}=Id_{\mathcal{H}},~\text{or}~u_{i}u_{i}^{*}=Id_{\mathcal{H}},~\text{for each}~i\in\{1,\dots,k\}\}.

The
C^{*}
-homomorphism
Id\otimes\mathbf{1}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
, defined as
id\otimes\mathbf{1}(x)=x\otimes\mathbf{1}
isometrically embeds
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
inside
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
. The pull-back of weak operator topology on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
under the map
Id\otimes\mathbf{1}
is a locally convex topology, known as the
\sigma
-WOT topology on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. In particular, the
\sigma
-WOT continuous functionals are described by the following lemma.

Lemma 1.4

(
[
13
]
, Chapter II)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{C}
be a linear functional. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
a\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
such that

\varphi(x)=\varphi_{a}(x)=Tr(ax),\quad x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(ii)

\varphi
is
\sigma
-WOT continuous.

Thus,
\sigma
-WOT continuous functionals are nothing but the weak
∗
continuous functional on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Since
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a von-Neumann algebra, its closed unit ball is
\sigma
-WOT compact. Therefore, for any
x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, the following collection

M_{x}:=\{a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):~\|a\|=1,~\pi(x)(a)=\|x\|_{1}\}

is always non-empty, where
\pi:\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\to\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{**}
denotes the canonical embedding. This observation leads to the following characterization of best approximations in trace class norm.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be any finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. Let
x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then
y_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
if and only if
M_{x-y_{0}}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}\neq\emptyset
.

The above result has an important consequence for smooth trace class operators. Finally, we consider the finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra case to formulate the distance of a point from a subspace in the
C^{*}
-norm and in the trace norm.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2},\dots n_{p}
be natural numbers and
\mathcal{M}
be the finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra

M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus_{\infty}M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus_{\infty}\dots\oplus_{\infty}M_{n_{p}}(\mathbb{C}).

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a subspace of
\mathcal{M}
with
dim~\mathcal{V}=k
. Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{p})\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then

dist(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{V})=\max\left\{|\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(x_{i}a_{i})|:~\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(a_{i}y^{(j)}_{i})=0,~\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|a_{i}\|_{1}=1,~1\leq j\leq k\right\},

and

dist_{1}(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{V})=\max\left\{|\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(x_{i}a_{i})|:~\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(a_{i}y^{(j)}_{i})=0,~\max\{\|a_{i}\|:~1\leq i\leq p\}=1,~1\leq j\leq k\right\},

for any basis
\{\mathbf{y}_{j}\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{y}_{j}=(y^{(j)}_{1},y^{(j)}_{2},\dots,y^{(j)}_{p}),1\leq j\leq k\}
of
\mathcal{V}
.

The applications of the above results are illustrated through suitable examples.

The following two results will be used in the sequel. For a normed linear space
X
, we denote the unit sphere of
X
by
S_{X}
and the continuous dual of
X
by
X^{*}
.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
12
]
Theorem 1.1, Page 170)
.

Let
X
be a complex normed linear space and
G
be an
n
-dimensional subspace of
X
. Let
x\in X\setminus G
and
g_{0}\in G
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

g_{0}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
G
.

(ii)

There exist
k
-number of extremal points
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in S_{X^{*}}

(1\leq k\leq 2n+1)
and scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in[0,1]
with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x-g_{0})=\|x-g_{0}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(g)=0,\quad g\in G.

The above result is due to Singer and has been applied to study the best approximation problems in the space of compact operators on a Banach space in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 1.8

(
[
9
]
, Lemma 3.9)
.

Let
X
be a vector space and
f
,
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{k}
are linear functionals on
X
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\{x\in X:~f_{j}(x)=0,~1\leq j\leq k\}\subset\ker~f
.

(b)

.

2.
Distance and Best approximations in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

We begin with an example which shows
dist(x,\mathcal{V})\neq\Delta(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
and a proper subspace
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, the Hilbert space of all square summable sequences. Let
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Consider the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{0}=span\{\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
. Let
x=2~Id_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\oplus u
for an isometry
u
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
. Consider the finite rank operators
y=a\oplus\mathbf{0}
and
z=b\oplus\mathbf{0}
with respect to the decomposition
\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
, where

a=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}&1\\
1&0&0\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&-1\\
0&\frac{1}{2}&0\\
\frac{1}{3}&\frac{1}{4}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Evidently,
y,z\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, however,
x\notin\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, as it contains the unilateral shift.

Consider the
2
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{V}=span\{y,z\}
. Observe that
Tr(y(\zeta_{2}\otimes\zeta_{2}))=Tr(z(\zeta_{2}\otimes\zeta_{2}))=0
and
Tr(x(\zeta_{2}\otimes\zeta_{2}))=2
. Granted Theorem
1.1
, we have
dist(x,\mathcal{V})\geq 2
. However,
dist(x,\mathcal{V})=2
, since
\|x\|=2
. Thus,
\mathbf{0}
is the best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
.

We now compute
\Delta(x)
. Let
(\xi_{n})
be any sequence of unit vectors in
\mathcal{H}
that weakly converges to
\mathbf{0}
. Let
\xi_{n}=\nu_{n}+\eta_{n}
, where
\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
). For any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
, we have

\lim_{n}\langle\xi_{n},\phi\rangle=\lim_{n}\langle\eta_{n},\phi\rangle=0.

Similarly, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, we have
\lim_{n}\langle\xi_{n},\phi\rangle=\lim_{n}\langle\nu_{n},\phi\rangle=0
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is finite-dimensional, this implies
\lim_{n}\|\nu_{n}\|=0
. Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\langle x\xi_{n},x\xi_{n}\rangle=\langle x\nu_{n},x\nu_{n}\rangle+\langle x\eta_{n},x\eta_{n}\rangle=4\|\nu_{n}\|^{2}+\|\eta_{n}\|^{2}\leq 4\|\nu_{n}\|^{2}+1.

Thus,
\lim_{n}\sup\|x\xi_{n}\|\leq 1
. In particular, if
\nu_{n}=\mathbf{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\lim_{n}\sup\|x\nu_{n}\|=1
. Therefore,
1=\Delta(x)<2=dist(x,\mathcal{V}).
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
\|\xi\otimes\zeta\|_{1}=1
for all
\xi,\zeta\in S_{\mathcal{H}}
, it is enough to prove the first equality of (
1.3
). We consider an isometric transformation of the equality, by identifying
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
as
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{*}
through the isometric isomorphism
\Omega:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{*}
, as defined in (
1.2
). Under this identification, the first equality of (
1.3
) transforms to the following form:

\|\rho_{x}-\rho_{v}\|=\sup\{|\rho_{x}(a)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}.

We now define a linear functional
\rho
on
N_{\mathcal{V}}
by
\rho(b)=\rho_{x}(b)
. Then
\rho
extends to a functional
\widetilde{\rho}
on
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
with the preservation of norm. Since
(\rho_{x}-\widetilde{\rho})
vanishes on
N_{\mathcal{V}}
, by Lemma
1.8
, we have
(\rho_{x}-\widetilde{\rho})=\rho_{z_{0}}
for some
z_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
. However, then

\displaystyle\|\rho_{x}-\rho_{z_{0}}\|

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{\rho}\|

\displaystyle=\|\rho\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\rho_{x}(a)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}.

Consequently, we have

dist(x,\mathcal{V})\leq\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}.

Conversely, since
\Omega(\mathcal{V})
is finite-dimensional, the existence of a best approximant is always guaranteed. For any best approximation
\rho_{v_{0}}
to
\rho_{x}
out of
\Omega(\mathcal{V})
,
\rho_{v_{0}}
vanishes on
N_{\mathcal{V}}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{x}-\rho_{v_{0}}\|

\displaystyle\geq\sup\{|\rho_{x}(a)-\rho_{v_{0}}(a)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\rho_{x}(a)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\},

which gives

dist(x,\mathcal{V})\geq\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Therefore, we have seen that whenever we consider the best approximation problem from a point
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
to a finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, the quantity
\Delta(x)
is only a lower bound of
dist(x,\mathcal{V})
and
dist(x,\mathcal{V})
is given by Theorem
1.1
. However, in certain cases
dist(x,\mathcal{V})=\Delta(x)
. We record this observation in the following corollary, which also formulates the norm of an element
x
, especially in case (b).

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Let
\mathcal{V}
be any finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. In each of the following cases below:

(a)

x
attains its norm everywhere,

(b)

x
attains its norm nowhere,

there holds

\displaystyle dist(x,\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}))=

\displaystyle dist(x,\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\cap N_{\mathcal{V}},~\|a\|_{1}=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x\|.

Proof.

In the first case
x=\|x\|v
for some isometry
v
on
\mathcal{H}
. For any
y\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, we have
(\|x\|v-y)^{*}(\|x\|v-y)=\|x\|^{2}-y^{\prime}
for some
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
, since
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is a closed two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Thus,
\|x\|^{2}\in\sigma((\|x\|v-y)^{*}(\|x\|v-y))
, and we have
\|\|x\|v-y\|\geq\|x\|
, i.e.,
\mathbf{0}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. When
x
fails to attain its norm, it follows from
[
4
, Lemma 2]
that any norming sequence
(\xi_{n})
of
x
(
(\xi_{n})\subset S_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\|x\xi_{n}\|\to\|x\|
),
\xi_{n}\xrightarrow{w}\mathbf{0}
. Therefore,
\Delta(x)=\|x\|
, and again
\mathbf{0}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Thus, we have the desired conclusion by Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Using Theorem
1.1
, we now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
z\in\mathcal{V}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
, then
\Omega(x-z)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}=\Omega(x)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}
, as
\Omega(z)\in\Omega(\mathcal{V})
vanishes on
N_{\mathcal{V}}
. Now, by Theorem
1.1

\displaystyle\|\Omega(x)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}

\displaystyle=dist(x,\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\|x-z\|

\displaystyle=\|\Omega(x-z)\|.

Conversely, if
\Omega(z-x)
is a Hahn-Banach extension of
\Omega(x)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}
, then by virtue of Theorem
1.1

\displaystyle\|x-z\|

\displaystyle=\|\Omega(x-z)\|

\displaystyle=\|\Omega(x)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),~\|a\|_{1}=1\}

\displaystyle=dist(x,\mathcal{V}),

and the proof is complete.
∎

3.
Distance and Best approximations in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))

This section is devoted to the study of best approximations and distance problems in trace class norm. Similar to Theorem
1.1
, we can prove the first equality of Theorem
1.3
by identifying
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
to the dual of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
through the isometric isomorphism
\Gamma:\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})^{*}
as defined in (
1.2
). Also, analogous to Theorem
1.2
, we have a characterization of best approximations in the trace class. We only state the results as the proofs are similar to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be any finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. Let
x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then

\displaystyle dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=\sup\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\cap N_{\mathcal{V}},~\|a\|=1\}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Then
z\in\mathcal{V}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
if and only if
\Gamma(x-z)
is a Hahn-Banach extension of
\Gamma(x)\big|_{N_{\mathcal{V}}}
.

However, utilizing the
\sigma
-WOT compactness of the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we prove Theorem
1.5
which also characterizes best approximations in trace class and has a substantial role in the sequel. We also use the notion of Birkhoff-James orthogonality
[
2
,
5
]
in the proof. For any two points
x
and
y
in a normed linear space
X
,
x
is said to be Birkhoff-James orthogonal to
y
(symbolized as
x\perp_{B}y
) if
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|
for all scalars
\lambda
. Birkhoff-James orthogonality is characterized by the well-known result of James
[
5
]
, namely,

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
x,y\in X
with
x\neq\mathbf{0}
. Then
x\perp_{B}y
if and only if there exists
f\in S_{X^{*}}
such that
f(x)=\|x\|
and
f(y)=0
.

The collection

(3.1)

\displaystyle J(x)=\{f\in S_{X^{*}}:~f(x)=\|x\|\}

is known as the collection of support functionals at
x
which is always non-empty by the Hahn-Banach Theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We identify
\mathcal{L}^{1}(B(\mathcal{H}))
to the space of
\sigma
-WOT continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
through the canonical embedding
\pi:\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\to\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{**}
, defined as

\pi(b)(z)=\varphi_{z}(b)=Tr(bz),\quad z\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

The collection
M_{x-y_{0}}
is a face of the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Moreover,
M_{x-y_{0}}
is, convex,
\sigma
-WOT compact subset of the unit sphere of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

If
M_{x-y_{0}}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}=\emptyset
, by the Geometric Hahn-Banach Theorem, there exists a
\sigma
-WOT continuous linear functional
f:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{C}
and a positive real number
\alpha
such that

Re~f(s)<\alpha<Re~f(z),\quad s\in N_{\mathcal{V}},~z\in M_{x-y_{0}}.

Since
N_{\mathcal{V}}
is a subspace, we must have
Re~f(s)=0
for all
s\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
. If
f(s_{0})\neq 0
, for some
s_{0}\in S
, then
f(s_{0})
is purely imaginary. However, then
f(\iota s_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
and is non-zero, which contradicts that
Re~f(s)=0
for all
s\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
. Therefore, we conclude
f(s)=0
for all
s\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
. However, then
N_{\mathcal{V}}\subset\ker~f
and
f\in\pi(\mathcal{V})
by Lemma
1.8
.

Let
f=\pi(v)
for some
v\in\mathcal{V}
. Let
a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a norm one element. Observe that
a
is a support functional at
(x-y_{0})
if and only if

\varrho_{a}(x-y_{0})=Tr(a(x-y_{0}))=\|x-y_{0}\|_{1}=\pi(x-y_{0})(a),

i.e.,
a\in M_{x-y_{0}}
. However,

Re~\varrho_{a}(v)=Re~Tr(av)=Re~\pi(v)(a)=Re~f(a)>\alpha,\quad a\in M_{x-y_{0}},

which implies that no support function at
(x-y_{0})
vanishes at
w
. Consequently, by the James characterization
(x-y_{0})\not\perp_{B}w
. Therefore, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\|(x-y_{0})+\lambda w\|_{1}=\|x-(y_{0}-\lambda w)\|_{1}<\|x-y_{0}\|_{1},

and
y_{0}
fails to be a best approximation to
x
, a contradiction. Therefore,
M_{x-y_{0}}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}\neq\emptyset
.

Conversely, suppose that there exists
a_{0}\in M_{x-y_{0}}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}
. Since
\|a_{0}\|=1
and
Tr(a_{0}v)=0
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

\|x-v\|_{1}\geq|Tr(a_{0}(x-v))|=|Tr(a_{0}x)|=|Tr(a_{0}x-a_{0}y_{0})|=\|x-y_{0}\|_{1},\quad w\in\mathcal{V}.

Consequently,
y_{0}
is the best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.3
. We require the description of extreme points of the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{*}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Ext(B_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{*}})=\{\rho_{u}:~x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),~u^{*}u=Id_{\mathcal{H}}~\text{or}~uu^{*}=Id_{\mathcal{H}}\}.

Proof.

The proof follows from the identification of
\Omega:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))^{*}
and the fact that an extreme point of the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is either an isometry or a co-isometry
[
6
]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The first equality of (
1.4
) follows from Proposition
3.1
. Since
\mathcal{V}
is finite-dimensional, there exists
v_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
such that
dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=\|x-v_{0}\|_{1}
. Observe that

\displaystyle dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\|x-v_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\pi(x-y_{0})\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\pi(x-y_{0})(a)|:~a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),~\|a\|=1\}

\displaystyle=\max\{|\pi(x-y_{0})(a)|:~a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\cap N_{\mathcal{V}},~\|a\|=1\}

since
M_{x-y_{0}}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}\neq\emptyset
by Theorem
1.5
. Thus, the second equality is proved.

We now prove the third equality. It follows from Theorem
1.7
and Proposition
3.4
that there exist
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

(1\leq k\leq 2n+1)
and scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in[0,1]
with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1
such that
w_{i}^{*}w_{i}=Id_{\mathcal{H}}
or
w_{i}w_{i}^{*}=Id_{\mathcal{H}}
, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x-v_{0})=\|x-v_{0}\|
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(v)=0
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
. Thus,
Im~\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x-v_{0})=Im~\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x)=0
. In particular, the operator tuple
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{k}
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

Im~\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x)=0

(b)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(v)=0
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}

(c)

w_{i}^{*}w_{i}=Id_{\mathcal{H}}
or
w_{i}w_{i}^{*}=Id_{\mathcal{H}}
, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Additionally,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x-v_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x)=\|x-v_{0}\|=dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})
. Let

\Lambda:=\{(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{k}:~(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})~\text{satisfies}~(a),(b),(c)\}.

Then
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})\in\Lambda
and
\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{w_{i}}(x):~(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})\in\Lambda\}\geq dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V}).
However, the inequality is an equality, since for any
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\Lambda
, we have
\|\rho_{u_{i}}\|=\|u_{i}\|=1

(1\leq i\leq k)
, and therefore,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{u_{i}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{u_{i}}(x-v_{0})\leq\|x-v_{0}\|_{1}=dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V}).

Consequently, using (
1.2
) we get

\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\rho_{u_{i}}(x):~(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\Lambda\}=\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}Tr(u_{i}x):~(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\Lambda\}=dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V}),

and the third equality of (
1.4
) follows. This completes the proof.
∎

The computational advantage of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
is illustrated through the following example.

Example 3.5
.

Consider the trace class operators
x,y,z
on
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
defined as

\displaystyle x(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\dots)=(\xi_{0},\xi_{0}+\xi_{1},\frac{\xi_{1}}{2},\frac{\xi_{2}}{2^{2}},\frac{\xi_{3}}{2^{2}},\dots)

\displaystyle y(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\dots)=(0,2\xi_{1},\xi_{2},0,0,0,\dots)

\displaystyle z(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\dots)=(2\xi_{0},0,-\xi_{2},0,0,0,\dots)

Let
\mathcal{V}=span\{y,z\}
. We wish to find
dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})
and a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
.

By Theorem
1.4
, we have

dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=\max\{|Tr(ax)|:~a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\cap N_{\mathcal{V}},~\|a\|=1\}.

Let
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Observe that
a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
if and only if
Tr(ay)=Tr(az)=0
, i.e.,
a\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
if and only if

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle a\zeta_{0},\zeta_{0}\rangle=-\langle a\zeta_{1},\zeta_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle a\zeta_{2},\zeta_{2}\rangle.

With this condition

(3.3)

\displaystyle|Tr(ax)|=|\langle a\zeta_{0},\zeta_{0}\rangle+\langle a\zeta_{1},\zeta_{1}\rangle+\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\langle a\zeta_{n+1},\zeta_{n}\rangle|\leq 2.

However, the backward shift operator
\mathfrak{b}
satisfies
(\ref{criterion NV})
and gives
Tr(\mathfrak{b}x)=2
. Therefore,
dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=2
.

Alternatively, we can use Theorem
1.5
to find a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
and then compute
dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})
. Observe that
[x-\frac{1}{2}(y+z)](\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\dots)=(0,\xi_{0},\frac{\xi_{1}}{2},\frac{\xi_{2}}{2^{2}},\frac{\xi_{3}}{2^{2}},\dots)
and

Tr(\mathfrak{b}(x-\frac{1}{2}(y+z)))=\|x-\frac{1}{2}(y+z)\|_{1}=2.

Since
\mathfrak{b}\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
, we have
M_{x-\frac{1}{2}(y+z)}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.5
,
\frac{1}{2}(y+z)
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
, and

.

Theorem
1.5
has a nice application in the smooth case. Smoothness in a normed linear space is characterized in terms of support functionals (see (
3.1
)). A non-zero point
x
in a normed linear space
X
is smooth if and only if
J(x)
is a singleton set
[
5
,

]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
x\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
be non-zero. Then
x
is smooth with respect to the trace norm if and only if

\{a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):~\|a\|=1,~Tr(ax)=\|x\|_{1}\}=\{v^{*}\},

where
v
is the unique partial isometry associated with the polar decomposition of
x
.

Proof.

By the James characterization of smoothness,
a
is smooth if and only if
J(x)
is a singleton set. Now, by (
1.2
) we get

J(x)=\{\rho_{a}:~a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),~\|a\|=1,~\rho_{a}(x)=Tr(xa)=\|x\|_{1}\}.

Thus,
x
is smooth if and only if
\{a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):~\|a\|=1,~Tr(ax)=\|x\|_{1}\}
is a singleton set. Since
v^{*}\in\{a\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):~\|a\|=1,~Tr(ax)=\|x\|_{1}\}
, we have the desired equality.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. Let

be smooth. Then
\mathbf{0}
is the best approximation to

out of

if and only if
v^{*}\in N_{\mathcal{V}}
, where

is the unique partial isometry involved in the polar decomposition of

.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
1.5
that
\mathbf{0}
is the best approximation to
x
out of
\mathcal{V}
if and only if
M_{x}\cap N_{\mathcal{V}}\neq\emptyset
. However, since
x
is smooth, by Proposition
3.6
, we have
M_{x}=\{v^{*}\}
, and the assertion is now proved.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
x,y\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for some natural number

and

. Then

In particular, if

, then

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the one-dimensional space

. The first equality follows from Theorem

. To prove the second equality, we observe that the partial isometry

. Thus,

Remark 3.9
.

The notion of Birkhoff-James orthogonality is directly connected to the best approximation problems in normed spaces. Namely, given any subspace
\mathbb{Y}
of
\mathbb{X}
and
x\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\mathbb{Y}
,
y_{0}\in\mathbb{Y}
is a best approximation to
x
out of
\mathbb{Y}
if and only if
(x-y_{0})\perp_{B}\mathbb{Y}
. Consequently, Theorems
1.2
and
1.5
characterize Birkhoff-James orthogonality of a point and a subspace in respective norms.

4.
Distance formula in Finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-Algebra

This section aims to formulate the distance between a point and a subspace in a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra and its dual. A finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{M}
is
\infty
-sum of a finite number of matrix algebras (up to isomorphism):

\mathcal{M}\cong M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus_{\infty}M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus_{\infty}\dots\oplus_{\infty}M_{n_{p}}(\mathbb{C})

for natural numbers
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{p}
. Observe that

\mathcal{M}^{*}\cong M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})^{*}\oplus_{1}M_{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C})^{*}\oplus_{1}\dots\oplus_{1}M_{n_{p}}(\mathbb{C})^{*},

where
M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})^{*}
is the space of all
n_{i}\times n_{i}
matrices equipped with the trace norm. By reflexivity of
\mathcal{M}
, any member
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{p})
of
\mathcal{M}
corresponds to a linear functional
\varphi_{\mathbf{z}}
acting on
\mathcal{M}^{*}
such that

\psi_{\mathbf{z}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(z_{i}u_{i}),\quad(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{p})\in\mathcal{M}^{*}.

Moreover, such a correspondence is an isometry.

We now prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We prove the first equality, and the second equality follows from duality. We consider an isometric transformation of the stated equality, given by

\min\{\|\psi_{\mathbf{x}}-\psi_{\mathbf{v}}\|:~\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\}=\max\{\psi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{a}):~\psi_{\mathbf{y}_{j}}(\mathbf{a})=0,~\|\mathbf{a}\|_{1}=1,~1\leq j\leq k\},

where
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})
and
\|\mathbf{a}\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|a_{i}\|_{1}=1
. The above equality follows the same arguments about Hahn-Banach extension as in Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, we consider the Hahn-Banach extension
\widetilde{\rho}
of
\rho
, where

\rho:=\psi_{\mathbf{x}}\bigg|_{\bigcap_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}\ker\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}.

Then
\psi_{\mathbf{z}_{0}}=\psi_{\mathbf{x}}-\widetilde{\rho}
is a member of
\{\psi_{\mathbf{v}}:~\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\}
, and we have

\displaystyle\min\{\|\psi_{\mathbf{x}}-\psi_{\mathbf{v}}\|:~\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\}\leq\|\psi_{\mathbf{z}_{0}}\|=\max\{\psi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{a}):~\psi_{\mathbf{y}_{j}}(\mathbf{a})=0,~\|\mathbf{a}\|_{1}=1,~1\leq j\leq k\}.

For the reverse equality, we consider any best approximation
\psi_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}
to
\psi_{\mathbf{x}}
out of
\{\psi_{\mathbf{v}}:~\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\}
. Then
\psi_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}
vanishes on
\bigcap_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}\ker\psi_{\mathbf{v}}
, and this proves the reverse inequality and finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots\oplus M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(
p
-times), and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{p})
, where

x_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{(i)}_{11}&x^{(i)}_{12}\\
x^{(i)}_{21}&x^{(i)}_{22}\end{bmatrix},\quad 1\leq i\leq p.

and
\mathcal{V}
be the subspace
D_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus D_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots\oplus D_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(
p
-times), where
D_{2}
denotes the subspace of diagonal matrices in
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

dist(x,\mathcal{V})=\max\{|x_{12}^{(i)}|,|x_{21}^{(i)}|:~1\leq i\leq p\}\quad\text{and}\quad dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}|x^{(i)}_{21}|+|x^{(i)}_{12}|.

Proof.

Let
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. It is easy to see that the collection of
p
-tuples

\{(0,\dots,\xi_{j}\otimes\xi_{j},\dots,0)~\}:~j\in\{1,2\}\}

is a basis of
\mathcal{V}
and

N_{\mathcal{V}}=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p}):~diag{(a_{i})}=0,~1\leq i\leq p\}.

A straightforward computation reveals that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in N_{\mathcal{V}}:~\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|a_{i}\|_{1}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in N_{\mathcal{V}}:~\sum_{i=1}^{p}|a_{21}^{(i)}|+|a_{12}^{(i)}|=1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in\mathcal{M}:~diag{(a_{i})}=0,~\sum_{i=1}^{p}|a_{21}^{(i)}|+|a_{12}^{(i)}|=1\right\}

Now,

\displaystyle dist(x,\mathcal{V})=

\displaystyle\max\left\{|\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(x_{i}a_{i})|:~\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{|\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{12}^{(i)}x_{21}^{(i)}+a_{21}^{(i)}x_{12}^{(i)}|:~\sum_{i=1}^{p}|a_{21}^{(i)}|+|a_{12}^{(i)}|=1\right\}.

Since any member
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\dots,\xi_{m})\in\ell_{\infty}^{m}
acts on
\ell_{1}^{m}
by pointwise multiplication, the quantity
dist(x,\mathcal{V})
is nothing but the norm of the functional
(x^{(1)}_{21},x^{(1)}_{12},\dots,x^{(p)}_{21},x^{(p)}_{12})\in\ell_{\infty}^{2p}
, which is given by

dist(x,\mathcal{V})=\max\{|x_{12}^{(i)}|,|x_{21}^{(i)}|:~1\leq i\leq p\}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in N_{\mathcal{V}}:~\max\{\|a_{j}\|:~1\leq i\leq p\}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in N_{\mathcal{V}}:~\max\{\max\{|a_{21}^{(i)}|,|a_{12}^{(i)}|\}:~1\leq i\leq p\}=1\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in\mathcal{M}:~diag{(a_{i})}=0,~\max\{|a_{21}^{(i)}|,|a_{12}^{(i)}|:~1\leq i\leq p\}=1\right\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=

\displaystyle\max\left\{|\sum_{i=1}^{p}Tr(x_{i}a_{i})|:~\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{|\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{12}^{(i)}x_{21}^{(i)}+a_{21}^{(i)}x_{12}^{(i)}|:~\max\{|a_{21}^{(i)}|,|a_{12}^{(i)}|:~1\leq i\leq p\}=1\right\},

which is nothing but the norm of the functional
(x^{(1)}_{21},x^{(1)}_{12},\dots,x^{(p)}_{21},x^{(p)}_{12})\in\ell_{1}^{2p}
, as given by

dist_{1}(x,\mathcal{V})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}|x^{(i)}_{21}|+|x^{(i)}_{12}|.

This completes the proof.
∎

EDEN: Efficient Dual-Layer Exploration Planning for Fast UAV Autonomous Exploration in Large 3-D Environments
Qianli Dong, Xuebo Zhang, Shiyong Zhang, Ziyu Wang, Zhe Ma, Haobo Xi

Abstract
Efficient autonomous exploration in large-scale environments remains challenging due to the high planning computational cost and low-speed maneuvers. In this paper, we propose a fast and computation-efficient dual-layer exploration planning method. The insight of our dual-layer method is efficiently finding an acceptable long-term region routing and greedily exploring the target in the region of the first routing area with high speed. Specifically, the proposed method finds the long-term area routing through an approximate algorithm to ensure real-time planning in large-scale environments. Then, the viewpoint in the first routing region with the lowest curvature-penalized cost which can effectively reduce decelerations caused by sharp turn motions will be chosen as the next exploration target. To further speed up the exploration, we adopt an aggressive and safe exploration-oriented trajectory to enhance exploration continuity. The proposed method is compared to state-of-the-art methods in challenging simulation environments. The results show the proposed method outperforms other methods in terms of exploration efficiency, computational cost, and trajectory speed. We also conduct real-world experiments to validate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The code will be open-sourced 1 1 1 https://github.com/NKU-MobFly-Robotics/EDEN .

II-B

Optimal-Routing-Based Exploration Planning

Optimal-routing-based methods usually formulate the exploration target decision problems into routing problems. Optimal-tour-based methods can be roughly devided into frontier-ATSP methods
[
16
,
17
,
31
,
18
]
and coverage-path-ATSP methods
[
32
,
20
,
21
,
5
,
19

. Frontier-ATSP methods formulate ATSP using frontiers and find the shortest tour between frontiers. Coverage-path-ATSP methods formulate ATSP with unexplored space and find the shortest coverage path. The main drawback of the optimal-routing-based methods is that they have to spend luxury computation on solving ATSP in large-scale environments. Moreover, counterintuitively, recent works
[
28
,
33
]
report that optimal-tour-based methods usually do not have an obvious advantage in minimizing the exploration path distance compared to greedy methods. This is because optimal-routing-based methods have implicit optimal assumptions, where frontier-ATSP methods assume a new frontier will not be generated after exploring an existing frontier, while coverage-path-ATSP methods assume all the unexplored space is traversable. Obviously, the above assumptions do not hold in most cases. Therefore, spending too much computation on finding a high-quality ATSP solution is not cost-effective.

II-C

Exploration-Oriented Trajectory Planning

Exploration trajectory plays a crucial role in exploration tasks. The early proposed exploration methods
[
11
,
9
]
stop-and-go motions are used. To utilize the good maneuverabilities of UAVs, Dharmadhikari
et, al.

[
15
]
adopts motion primitives to speed up the exploration. To further improve the trajectory speed, Zhou
et al.

[
16
]
optimizes minimum-time trajectory with optimizable terminal velocity and acceleration for high-speed exploration. Although minimum-time trajectory is very efficient, the optimizable terminal velocity and acceleration do not ensure being beneficial to continuously exploring the newly generated frontier after exploring the previous frontier. What is more, the optimizable terminal velocity and acceleration can lead to collision.

In summary, a far-sighted and computationally efficient exploration planning method and a trajectory planning method that enable fast continuous exploration while ensuring safety are required for exploration in large-scale environments.

III
system overview

The proposed method is composed of a dual-layer exploration target decision module that can plan both the long-term region routing and the short-term viewpoint target, and a trajectory planning module that can plan high-speed continuous exploration trajectories as shown in Fig.
1
. A dynamic voxel map with resolution
r_{v}
is updated by integrating depth image and odometry information. Different from the normal voxel map, the dynamic voxel map only maintains a local part of voxels around the UAV while other voxels are dynamically read and written to save memory usage in large environments. Based on the voxel map, a dynamic topological graph (DTG), is maintained to manage exploration-related information. The traversable information is maintained by DTG’s history nodes and edges between them, and the explorable space information is maintained by DTG’s explorable region of interest (EROI), which is a set of uniform scale cubic regions that can be explored by visiting corresponding active viewpoints. For detailed information, we refer readers to our previous work
[
34
]
. The exploration planning utilizes DTG to efficiently formulate ATSP, and ATSP is solved through EOHC to find satisfactory exploration region routing with small computation. Then, three viewpoints, including an exploring viewpoint, a continuous viewpoint, and a safety viewpoint in the first region of the region routing are found for the following trajectory planning. Then, an aggressive and safe exploration-oriented trajectory which separates at the exploring viewpoint, and its two branches end at a continuous viewpoint and a safe viewpoint correspondingly, is optimized. The exploring part of the aggressive and safe exploration-oriented trajectory will be executed.

IV
Dual-layer exploration viewpoints planning

In this section, we first propose the EOHC algorithm for finding a satisfactory region routings efficiently in large-scale environments. Then, we use curvature-penalized continuous gain to decide the targets in the first region of the tour for the following high-speed ASEO trajectory planning.

IV-A

EOHC-Based Global Routing Planning

As analyzed in Section. II.B, the optimal routing of frontiers or unexplored space is very likely not the optimal routing for exploration due to the implicit optimal assumption not being met. Therefore, instead of finding a high-quality of all precise explorable frontiers, the proposed method aims to spend low computation on finding a satisfactory coarse routing of explorable regions. The process of EOHC-based routing planning is shown in Fig.
2
.

Firstly, we extract all the history nodes of DTG that connect at least one EROI as the explorable regions. To reduce the computation when formulating ATSP, the distances between all explorable regions and the distances between the robot and all explorable regions are computed by conducting the Dijkstra algorithm on DTG. Thanks to the sparse DTG, the ATSP can be formulated efficiently.

To solve ATSP efficiently, we design an EOHC algorithm, which is improved from the Christofides algorithm
[
35
]
and tailored to ATSP. The key idea of the Christofides algorithm is to construct a minimum spanning tree (MST, Fig.
2
(a)) using the Prim algorithm and find a walk that goes around the MST. The solution routing of the Christofides algorithm ensures no longer than twice the optimal routing. However, the Christofides algorithm is designed for symmetric TSP. For exploration-oriented ATSP, the robot does not have to return to the original node as stated by
[
16
]
. Therefore, to minimize the routing length, the leaf node that is the farthest from the robot and nodes on its corresponding branch (in Fig.
2
(b), the green nodes) will be executed last to save the highest cost of returning to the original node. Another problem is how to decide the sequence of other branches. Since all the regions will be explored finally, the proposed method chooses the next node closest to the current node heuristically when the current node has multiple siblings as shown in Fig.
2
(c). Finally, a routing for ATSP will be found as shown in Fig.
2
(d).

For the worst case, the path length
l_{eohc}
of the routing solved by the EOHC algorithm holds:

\displaystyle l_{eohc}\leq 2l^{*}+l_{m},

(1)

where
l^{*}
is the optimal solution of the corresponding ATSP, and
l_{m}
is the longest distance among all the distances between the robot and all the explorable history nodes. The computational complexity of the proposed EOHC is
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
, where
n
is the total number of nodes.

IV-B

Curvature-Penalized Viewpoints Planning

After finding the long-term routing of regions, three viewpoints (an exploring viewpoint, a continuous viewpoint, and a safety viewpoint) in the first region of the routing will be selected for ASEO trajectory planning. The UAV will go to explore the exploring viewpoint (in the explored map) and maintain a speed and an acceleration conducive to explore the continuous viewpoint (in the explored map or unexplored map) if no obstacle is detected, otherwise it can return to the safety viewpoint (in the explored map).

For detailed viewpoint evaluation, existing methods usually use the path distance and turning angle to avoid long traveling distances and sharp turns in a decoupled manner. However, the maximum velocity is limited by the curvature of the trajectory at a point according to the circular motion model. We compute the velocity
v_{s}(\mathbf{p})
at
\mathbf{p}
by

\displaystyle v_{s}(\mathbf{p})=\min\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{a}\cdot a_{m}}{K_{c}(\textbf{p})}},v_{m}\right),

(2)

where
v_{m}
is the maximum linear velocity of the UAV,
a_{m}
is the maximum linear acceleration of the UAV,
K_{c}(\mathbf{p})
is the curvature of the trajectory at
\mathbf{p}
, and
\lambda_{a}\in(0.0,1.0)
is an adjustable parameter. Based on this fact, we designed a curvature-penalized heuristic motion cost evaluation method to select the best exploring target and the best continuous target for high-speed exploration.

Curvature-Penalized Exploring Viewpoint and Continuous Viewpoint Planning

An exploring viewpoint in the first region of routing and a continuous viewpoint located in the corresponding EROI of the exploring viewpoint will be selected for aggressive high-speed trajectory planning.

First, a Dijkstra search starts from the current UAV position in the explored space is conducted to find the path to each candidate viewpoint in the first region of the routing. Then, for each path to a candidate viewpoint, an anchor point on the path which is
\lambda_{arc}\cdot v_{m}
(

is a tunable parameter) away from the UAV position is selected to formulate a virtual circular arc trajectory. The formulation of the

\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\mathbf{p}_{a,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

((\mathbf{p}_{a,k}-&\mathbf{p}_{c})\times\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{c})^{\mathrm{T}}\\

((\mathbf{p}_{a,k}-\mathbf{p}_{c})&\times\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{c})^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{c}\\
\end{aligned}\right],

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CostE}(\mathbf{vp}_{k})=\max\left(\frac{\mathrm{PL}(\mathbf{p}_{k},\mathbf{p}_{c})}{v_{as,k}},\frac{\mathrm{YD}(\psi_{k},\psi_{c})}{\dot{\psi}_{m}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}(\mathbf{vp}_{k},\mathbf{vp}_{j})=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{e}\mathrm{CostE}(\mathbf{vp}_{k})}\cdot(1+\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{e}\mathrm{CostE}(\mathbf{vp}_{j})}).

It is worth noting that the voxels on the continuous line between

to avoid recalculation of indexes of voxels at overlapping positions of continuous lines. Specifically, our method generates a table of indexes of voxel that needs to be checked in advance and iterates the table when checking an EROI. Once an iterative voxel is found occupied, its corresponding viewpoint pairs are confirmed infeasible and will not be considered.

VI
conclusion

In this work, we propose an efficient dual-layer exploration planning method (EDEN). By finding long-term routings through the proposed EOHC algorithm, deciding short-term targets through curvature-penalized evaluation, and generating ASEO trajectories, the proposed method is able to explore large environments efficiently with low computational cost and high speed. The simulation indicates the exploration efficiency of the proposed method is
5.2\%-14.0\%
higher than state-of-the-art methods. The computational cost of the proposed method is low enough to enable exploration in large-scale environments.

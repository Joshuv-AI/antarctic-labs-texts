Biological agents and plant growth regulator promote rice growth and regenerative capacity
Authors: Hang Zhou, Lang Liu, Jianqun Zhou, Aibin He, Zhaohui Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAgainst the backdrop of global population and food demand growth, the development of ratoon rice is conducive to increasing food production and ensuring food security. Applying agricultural biotechnology and plant growth regulator to ratoon rice production is a key measure to promote the development of green agriculture and address the challenges of global food security. A total of 4 treatments were set up in this study: T1 (regulator); T2 (biological agent made from plant materials); T3 (microbial agent); and control (CK). The results showed that T3 up-regulated many key genes (OsHsfC1b, OsCNGC16, OsOSM1, OsSAPK9, and DTH2, etc.) and metabolites (spermine, citric acid, riboflavin, and shikimic acid). T2 was found to up-regulate key genes OsAPT2, OsZIP8, OsCIPK14, OsCIPK7, OsPR4c, and OsVIT2, and its regulatory effects involved carbohydrate, lipid, and amino acid metabolism, etc. In addition, the two biological agents and one regulator significantly increased the accumulation of stem dry matter and yield in the first season. T1, T2, and T3 significantly increased the final regenerative capacity of Yongyou4949 at D5 (fifth node from the top); T1 and T2 significantly increased the maximum and final regenerative capacities of Shuangliangyou138 at D5. This study provided guidance for developing more effective agricultural management strategies, which is conducive to the sustainable development of agriculture.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-06751-z.Keywords: Agricultural biotechnology, Plant growth regulator, Ratoon rice, Yield

Introduction

Food production is limited by a variety of factors, such as climate change, soil and water resource degradation, and reduction of arable land. According to statistics, about 34% of Earth’s agricultural land is degraded [1, 2]. Soil salinization is one of the most serious threats to agriculture and food security [3]. Currently, approximately 1.1 × 109 hectares of land are affected by salt worldwide, and saline soil is increasing at an alarming rate of 1.5 million hectares per year [4, 5]. In addition, rising global temperatures, intensified land desertification, and rapid population growth have posed serious challenges to global food security.

Ratoon rice refers to the second season rice that develops from the remaining rice stubble after the first season rice is harvested [6, 7]. Compared with double-season rice, ratoon rice production reduces labor, saves seeds and water, and is a conservation-oriented production model [8–10]. As global food security becomes increasingly severe, ratoon rice cultivation as an efficient, economical, and environmentally friendly rice production model will help promote the sustainable development of agriculture.

Plant growth regulators, also known as exogenous plant hormones, are artificially synthesized or extracted substances similar to natural plant hormones. These regulators can effectively control plants’ morphology, enhance stress resistance and significantly improving crop yield [11]. Choline chloride is a broad-spectrum plant growth regulator. It acts as an antioxidant in many plant tissues [12, 13]. A previous study found that choline chloride treatment improved plant growth with more leaves in Kentucky bluegrass [14]. Up to now, there are no reports on the application of choline chloride in ratoon rice.

In recent years, agricultural biotechnology has developed rapidly, involving biological nitrogen fixation, biological pesticides, biological fertilizer technology, etc. Lactobacillus, Bacillus amyloliquefaciens, and Brevibacillus laterosporus have all been reported to have positive regulatory effects on plants [15–22]. In addition, the biological agent made from plant materials was reported to affect rhizosphere soil metabolism and improve potassium and nitrogen availability [23]. This biological agent is made from various plant monomer enzymes or plant extracts and is chelated through a unique secondary low-temperature aerobic fermentation. Its application had a significant promoting effect on rice yield [24]. These cases showed that agricultural biotechnology has essential potential to ensure food security and achieve green and sustainable agricultural development.

The goal of this study was to explore the potential of plant growth regulator and biological agents in ratoon rice production. We hypothesized that these exogenous substances may improve regenerative capacity and promote yield by regulating rice metabolism. The results of this study provide a reference for ratoon rice cultivation and sustainable agricultural development.

Materials and methods

Test site

This study was conducted in Heping Village, Heshi Town, Yueyang City, Hunan Province, in 2023. Soil physical and chemical properties: pH 5.30, organic matter 32.78 g·kg− 1, total nitrogen 1.82 g·kg− 1, total phosphorus 0.57 g·kg− 1, and total potassium 16.31 g·kg− 1.

Test materials

Hunan Hybrid Rice Research Center provided the test varieties Yongyou4949 (Y49) and Shuangliangyou138 (S138). The Physiological Ecology Laboratory of Guangdong Ocean University provided a plant growth regulator named Daofeng with choline chloride as the main ingredient (active ingredient 0.06%; 100 mL of the reagent is diluted with 15 L of water). Heilongjiang Senqi Biotechnology Co., Ltd. provided biological agent made from plant materials (main ingredients are papain, chamomile extract, and bitter bark extract; 50 mL of reagent is diluted with 15 L of water). Inner Mongolia Ketuo Microecological Technology Development Co., Ltd. provided microbial agent (effective strains: Bacillus licheniformis, Bacillus amyloliquefaciens, Lactobacillus plantarum, and Lactobacillus casei; the number of effective viable bacteria ≥ 2.0 × 108 cfu·mL− 1; 5 L of reagent is diluted with 15 L of water).

Experimental design

This study adopted a randomized block design with 3 replications. Each plot area was 20 m2. A total of 4 treatments were set up in this study: regulator (T1); biological agent made from plant materials (T2); microbial agent (T3); and control (CK). Rice seedlings were transplanted at the 4-leaf stage, with 2 plants per hole and 16.70 × 30.00 cm density. In the first season, 180 kg·hm− 2 (pure nitrogen) of nitrogen fertilizer was used in the ratio of base fertilizer: tillering fertilizer: panicle fertilizer = 1:1:1.2. Potassium fertilizer (K2O) was used as base fertilizers, and the usage amounts were 180 kg·hm− 2. 90 kg·hm− 2 (P2O5) of phosphate fertilizer was used in the ratio of base fertilizer: tillering fertilizer = 1:1. The above is the conventional fertilizer dosage. However, in order to promote successful regeneration of rice, this study applied additional nitrogen fertilizer (pure nitrogen; 52.5 kg·hm− 2) and potassium fertilizer (K2O; 27 kg·hm− 2) on the 20th day after full heading in the first season. In addition, 52.5 kg·hm− 2 of nitrogen fertilizer (pure nitrogen) was applied on the third day after rice was harvested in the first season.

This study carried T1, T2, and T3 treatments at the seedling, breaking, and heading stages in the first season. T1 and T2 were applied by foliar spraying; T3 was applied by irrigation.

Measurement items

Determination of dry matter accumulation

In the first season, samples were taken at the full heading and maturity stage. Six representative plants were selected from each plot. The ratoon rice was sampled at the full heading and maturity stage, and 12 representative plants were selected from each plot. The harvested samples were dried in an oven at 105 °C for 30 min and then dried at 80 °C to constant weight. The dry matter weight of each part of the plant was measured.

Determination of root volume and dry weight

Three representative plants were selected from each plot at the full heading, filling, and maturity stage in the first season. The drainage method determined the root volume. The washed roots were placed in an oven at 105 °C for 30 min and dried at 80 °C to constant weight, and the root dry weight was determined.

Determination of yield and component factors

At the maturity stage in the first season, twenty consecutive rice were investigated in each plot, and the average number of effective panicles per plant was calculated. In each plot, six plants with the same number of effective panicles per plant (the average calculated in the previous step) were selected to investigate the component factors. At the same time, one m2 of rice from each plot was converted into actual yield at a moisture content of 13.5%. In the second season, twelve plants were selected according to the average number of effective panicles per plant to determine component factors. one m2 of rice from each plot was converted into actual yield at a moisture content of 13.5%.

Determination of partial factor productivity of nitrogen

Partial factor productivity of nitrogen was calculated by the following formula:

Partial factor productivity of nitrogen (kg·kg− 1) = grain yield/nitrogen application amount [25–27].

Determination of regenerative capacity

Twelve representative plants were selected 15 days after harvesting in the first season and at the maturity stage in the second season, respectively. The calculation formula was as follows:

Maximum regenerative capacity = number of maximum regenerated tillers/effective panicles number in the first season.

Final regenerative capacity = effective panicle number in the second season/effective panicle number in the first season.

Metabolite profiling

Rice leaf samples were collected on the 16th day after the first T2 and T3 treatments. Metabolome detection was performed by Shanghai Majorbio Co., Ltd.

50 ± 5 mg sample and a 6 mm diameter grinding bead were placed in a 2 mL centrifuge tube. Then, 400 µL of extraction solution was added. After grinding with a grinder for 6 min (-10 °C, 50 Hz), low-temperature ultrasonic extraction was performed for 30 min (5 °C, 40 kHz). The sample was placed at -20℃ for 30 min and then centrifuged for 15 min (13,000 g, 4℃). The supernatant was used for analysis. In addition, 20 µL of supernatant was pipetted from each sample and mixed as a quality control sample.

Fluorescence quantitative PCR detection

Fluorescence quantitative PCR detection was completed by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. In this study, SYBR green I was added to the PCR reaction system, and fluorescence signal accumulation was used to monitor the PCR process in real time.

The fold relationship of the expression level difference was obtained through the mathematical operation of 2−(ΔΔCt) between the CT value of the internal reference gene and the CT value of the gene to be tested [28]. Primer design was shown in Table. S1 and Table. S2.

Statistical analysis

Excel 2016 was used for data collation, SPSS 27 was used for one-way ANOVA, and Origin 2021 was used to create figures.

For S138, T1 and T2 significantly increased the setting rate and 1000-grain weight; T3 contributed to yield mainly by significantly increasing the effective panicle number and setting rate (Table 1).

Dry matter accumulation of rice in the first season

Dry matter is an important quantitative indicator reflecting rice photosynthetic efficiency, nutrient accumulation and yield formation process. This study found that T1, T2 and T3 significantly increased the accumulation of dry matter in different parts of Y49 and S138. (Table. S3; Table. S4).

Root characteristics in the first season

Root volume

In this study, T1, T2, and T3 significantly increased the root volume of both varieties at the full heading stage. At the filling and maturity stages, the root volume in the T3 treatment of Y49 had no significant change, but that in T1 and T2 was significantly increased. For S138, T1 and T2 significantly increased root volume at the filling stage; at the maturity stage, the root volumes of T1, T2, and T3 were all significantly increased (Table. S5).

Root dry weight

As shown in the Table. S6, T1, T2, and T3 treatments can significantly increase the root dry weight of Y49 at the full heading and filling stages; at the maturity stage, T1 and T2 were significantly higher than CK. For S138, the root dry weights of T1, T2, and T3 were significantly higher than those of CK at the three growth stages.

Ratoon rice yield and component factors

In this study, T1, T2, and T3 treatments significantly increased the actual yield of Y49 by 8.04%, 9.69%, and 5.67%, respectively. T1 and T2 significantly increased the actual yield of S138 by 9.37% and 10.57%, respectively; T3 increased the actual yield of S138 by 2.11%. In addition, this study found that T1, T2, and T3 treatments increased yield mainly by contributing to effective panicle number. T1 and T2 could significantly increase the effective panicle number of Y49. T1, T2, and T3 could significantly increase the effective panicle number of S138 (Table. S7).

The detection results at the different nodes showed that T1, T2, and T3 could significantly increase the effective panicle number and theoretical yield of Y49 at D5 (fifth node from the top) (Table. S8). The effects of T1, T2, and T3 on S138 were the same as those on Y49. (Table. S9).

Regenerative capacity of ratoon rice at the different nodes

This study showed that T1, T2, and T3 had no significant positive regulation on the two varieties’ maximum and final regenerative capacity at D4. At D5, T1, T2, and T3 significantly improved the final regenerative capacity of Y49; T1 and T2 could significantly improve the maximum and final regenerative capacity of S138 (Tables 2 and 3).

Dry matter accumulation of ratoon rice at the different nodes

The dry matter accumulation at different nodes of reflects the law of assimilate distribution after axillary bud germination. For Y49, T2 could significantly increase the stem dry weight accumulation at D4 at the full heading stage; at the maturity stage, T2 and T3 significantly increased leaf dry weight accumulation at D4. The stem and leaf dry weight accumulation at D5 in T1, T2, and T3 treatments were significantly increased, but only T2 could significantly increase the panicle dry weight accumulation of Y49. At the maturity stage, the stem and panicle dry weight accumulation at D5 in T1, T2, and T3 treatments were significantly increased (Table. S10).

For S138, the three treatments (T1, T2, and T3) did not significantly affect the dry weight accumulation of different parts at D4 but played a regulatory role at D5. At the full heading stage, the three treatments significantly increased the dry weight accumulation of panicle and leaf at D5, and the stem dry weight accumulation of T2 and T3 was significantly higher than that of CK. At the maturity stage, the dry weight accumulation of stem and panicle at D5 of the three treatments was significantly higher than that of CK (Table. S11).

Partial factor productivity of nitrogen

In the first season, T1, T2 and T3 could significantly improve the partial factor productivity of both varieties. In the second season, T1, T2 and T3 significantly improved the partial factor productivity of Y49; T1 and T2 significantly improved the partial factor productivity of S138 (Table. S12).

Discussion

Plant growth regulator and biological agents contributed to the growth performance of rice

The formation of rice yield is essentially a process of dry matter production, transfer, and distribution. An essential characteristic of high-yield rice in material production is the significant increase in dry matter before heading. This study showed that from the heading to maturity, the dry matter accumulation of rice stems and leaves decreased, while that of panicles increased, indicating that the dry matter was transferred to the panicles at maturity. The T1, T2, and T3 used in this study all promoted the accumulation of dry matter in the stems, leaves, and panicles of the two rice varieties, contributing to the significant increase in the first season rice yield.

Nitrogen is one of the essential macronutrients for plant growth. It is the component of chlorophyll and a principal constituent of proteins, amino acids, and nucleotides. Insufficient nitrogen supply hinders plant growth and development [29]. Therefore, whether nitrogen absorption and utilization were improved after applying plant growth regulator and biological agents is one of the focuses in this study. This study showed that T1, T2, and T3 treatments significantly improved the partial factor productivity of nitrogen in the first season, indicating that the rice yields per unit nitrogen input in these treatments were higher than that in CK. In addition, the use of T1, T2, and T3 in the first season had the effect of promoting root growth. At the maturity stage, the three treatments significantly promoted the root volume and dry weight of S138; T1 and T2 had significant regulatory effects on Y49. These changes in the root system are beneficial to enhancing the nutrient and water absorption capacity and promoting the growth of ratoon rice.

Plant growth regulator and biological agents contributed to regeneration and yield of ratoon rice

During the growth and development of ratoon rice, there are obvious differences between high-position buds and low-position buds in acquiring and utilizing nutrients. Because of their higher position on the rice stubble, high-node buds mainly rely on nutrients accumulated in the stubble in the first season during germination and early growth stages. In contrast, lowe-node buds rely more on the roots to absorb nutrients from the soil. Therefore, the nutrients in the rice stubble and the growth status of the root system are of great significance for high yields in the regenerative season. This study showed that T1 and T2 treatments could significantly increase stem dry weight accumulation of both varieties in the first season. The high dry weight accumulation meant more residual nutrients, which can provide essential nutrients for the growth of regenerative buds. Further analysis of the regenerative capacity of D4 and D5 revealed that T1, T2, and T3 had no significant positive regulatory effect on the maximum and final regenerative capacity of the D4 node in both varieties, which may be related to the excessively high node position compared with D5. At D5, T1, T2, and T3 significantly increased the final regenerative capacity of Y49; T1 and T2 significantly increased the maximum and final regenerative capacities of S138. Therefore, T1 and T2 mainly affected the regenerative capacity at D5, and the effect of T3 on the regenerative capacity at D5 varied among varieties. In addition, T1, T2, and T3 could significantly increase the dry weight accumulation and yield of ratoon rice at D5, but there was no significant change at D4, indicating that the contribution of plant growth regulator and biological agents used in this study to the yield of ratoon rice is at D5.

Biological agents affected the expression of key genes in rice

This study found OsAPT2, OsZIP8, OsCIPK14, OsCIPK7, OsPR4c, and OsVIT2 were up-regulated after using T2. OsAPT2 encodes a putative adenine phosphoribosyl transferase; inhibiting the expression of its homologous gene AtAPT2 in Arabidopsis results in decreased adenosine-5’-monophosphate (AMP) content, reduced pollen germination rate, and abnormal flowering time [30]. OsVIT2 is a vacuolar membrane transporter [31]. A study found that the growth of osvit2 plants slowed down compared with the wild type; the accumulation of Fe in the aboveground parts of the osvit2 plants was less than that of the wild type under normal or Fe excess growth conditions [32]. OsCIPK14 is rapidly induced by microbe-associated molecular patterns (MAMPs) and participates in MAMP-induced immune responses, such as defense-related gene expression, phytoalexin biosynthesis, and hypersensitive cell death [33]. OsCIPK7 is an important component of the low-temperature regulatory network in rice [34]. In addition, a previous study found that OsPR4c expression can be induced by Magnaporthe grisea infection, salt, H2O2, mechanical damage, and drought stress [35]. In this study, the up-regulation of the above key genes may play a role in maintaining rice growth and improving stress resistance. It is of great significance in improving the yield and quality of rice.

In addition, 9 genes related to rice stress resistance and yield formation were up-regulated after T3 application in this study. Previous study has found that the expression of OsHsfC1b is induced by salt and plays a role in ABA-mediated tolerance of rice to salt stress [36]. OsCNGC16 has a positive regulatory effect on both heat and cold tolerance of rice, and the mutant cngc16 is more sensitive to high heat and low temperature stress [37]. wsl3 is a low-temperature-sensitive mutant, and many leaf cells do not contain chloroplasts compared with the wild type when grown at 25 °C [38]. According to the above reports, these genes are related to the stress response of rice. The up-regulation of OsHsfC1b, OsCNGC16, and WSL3 in this study meant that T3 may enhance the expression of these genes in rice by regulating the microecological environment of the rice rhizosphere and thereby affecting the internal regulatory network to improve the adaptability of rice.

Furthermore, some genes related to rice disease resistance, such as OsPR4c, OsOSM1, and OsSAPK9, were detected to be significantly up-regulated. Previous research found that infection by Magnaporthe grisea can induce the expression of OsPR4c in the varieties Minghui 63 and Zhenshan 97 [35]. Overexpression of OsOSM1 in the sensitive variety Xudao 3 significantly increases the resistance of transgenic rice to sheath blight (SB) [40]. In addition, OsSAPK9 was reported to positively regulate salt tolerance and bacterial blight resistance in rice [40]. After infection with Xanthomonasoryzae pv. oryzicola (Xoc), the expression of OsSAPK9 is significantly up-regulated [41]. These findings indicated that T3 treatment may improve the disease resistance of rice by up-regulating the expression of these defense-related genes, and this enhancement of disease resistance will subsequently reduce dependence on chemical pesticides.

Additionally, some genes closely related to yield formation, such as DTH2 and OsGRP2, were detected to be up-regulated. DTH2 can promote rice heading by inducing the expression of florigen genes Hd3a and RFT1. OsGRP2 is involved in tapetal differentiation and pollen maturation; the mutant osgrp2 has short panicles, shrunken pollen grains, and a lower seed-setting rate [42]. The up-regulation of these genes after using T3 may be an essential factor in improving the yield.

Biological agents affected rice metabolism

In T3/CK, KEGG annotation found that the differential metabolites were mainly classified into amino acid metabolism, including 30 differential metabolites. Spermine and citric acid were two important metabolites up-regulated in this study (Table. S15). Previous studies reported that both endogenously produced and exogenously applied spermine are effective against drought stress [43, 44]. Spermine application decreased the MDA content of T. majus under moderate salt stress and increased CAT and POD activity in plants under severe salt stress [45]. At the same time, studies on citric acid improving the ability of plants to resist abiotic stress are also frequently reported. Previous studies claimed that the application of citric acid can modulate an antioxidant defense system, increase high photosynthetic pigment content, and enhance plant productivity grown in stressed soil [46, 47]. Further, KEGG enrichment analysis found that most DEGs were enriched in the biosynthesis of cofactors pathway. Among these metabolites, riboflavin and shikimic acid were up-regulated after applying T3 (Table. S15). Riboflavin is related to plant defense responses [48], and it plays a role in protecting the plant from multiple types of stress by inducing antioxidant enzymes [49, 50]. Previous research found shikimic acid application increased plants’ fresh and dry weights, root length, and plant height of cowpea and enhanced leaf expansion [51]. Therefore, T3 treatment had a positive impact on rice metabolism, and the up-regulation of key metabolites such as spermine, citric acid, riboflavin, and shikimic acid helps to improve rice’s adaptability to abiotic stress, survival rate, and yield.

To further explore the mechanism of T2 affecting rice life activities, this study conducted KEGG annotation on the detected differential metabolites and found that many differential metabolites were classified into carbohydrate metabolism, lipid metabolism, and amino acid metabolism, which showed that spraying T2 affected rice carbohydrate metabolism, lipid metabolism, and amino acid metabolism. Among these up-regulated metabolites in lipid metabolism (Table. S16), palmitic acid is found in the seeds of many crops [52–55]; it has also been reported to inhibit the growth of plant soil-borne pathogens and promote the growth of seedlings [55–58]. In addition, this study found that citric acid was up-regulated after spraying T2 (Table. S16). Citric acid’s importance is reflected in its essential role in photosynthesis and cellular respiration [59]. Exogenous use of citric acid can also increase the activity of antioxidant defense systems and relieve plant oxidative stress [60]. L-glutamine was another essential amino acid up-regulated in this study (Table. S16). Glutamine is an effective nitrogen source for plant growth [61]. Exogenous glutamine applied at appropriate levels promoted poplar growth and NUE [62]. The up-regulation of L-glutamine may be a contributing factor to the increase in rice yield; the up-regulation of citric acid may help enhance the ability of rice plants to cope with oxidative stress in field environments.

Conclusion

The plant growth regulator and two biological agents used in this study were effective in increasing the yield of rice in the first season, proving their potential for application in rice production. The T1 and T2 treatments used in this study can affect rice regenerative ability at D5, but the effect of T3 on rice varied among varieties. This study provided guidance for developing more effective agricultural management strategies, which is conducive to the sustainable development of agriculture.

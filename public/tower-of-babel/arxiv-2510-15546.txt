Hodge Laplacians on Weighted Simplicial Complexes: Forms, Closures, and Essential Self-Adjointness
Marwa Ennaceur 1, Amel Jadlaoui 2

Abstract
. We establish explicit operator norm bounds and essential self-adjointness criteria for discrete Hodge Laplacians on weighted graphs and simplicial complexes. For unweighted d d -regular graphs we prove the universal estimate ‖ Δ ~ 1 , ∗ ‖ ≤ 4 ​ ( d − 1 ) \|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|\leq 4(d-1) , and we provide weighted extensions with a sharp comparability constant. These bounds apply without geometric completeness or curvature assumptions and ensure essential self-adjointness on natural cores. The approach extends to higher degrees via dual up/down degrees, and we show a unitary equivalence between skew and symmetric models on colorable complexes. For periodic lattices we complement the universal bounds with exact Floquet–Bloch constants, typically of order 2 ​ d 2d , illustrating both the sharpness in growth and the generality of our method.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Combinatorial setting

3.1.1.
Graphs and simplicial complexes

We begin with some standard definitions from graph theory and the simplicial structures they induce.

Definition 3.1
.

A (non-oriented) weighted graph is a triple
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},m_{0},m_{1})
where

•

\mathcal{V}
is a countable vertex set;

•

m_{0}:\mathcal{V}\to(0,\infty)
is a vertex weight;

•

m_{1}:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to[0,\infty)
is a
symmetric
edge weight, i.e.,
m_{1}(x,y)=m_{1}(y,x)
for all
x,y
.

The (undirected) edge set is

\mathcal{E}:=\bigl\{\{x,y\}\subset\mathcal{V}:\ m_{1}(x,y)>0\bigr\}.

We write
x\sim y
if
m_{1}(x,y)>0
and call
x,y

neighbors
. The neighbor set of
x
is

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(x):=\{y\in\mathcal{V}:\ x\sim y\}.

The graph is
locally finite
if
\#\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(x)<\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{V}
. The (weighted) degree is

d_{\mathcal{V}}(x):=\frac{1}{m_{0}(x)}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}}m_{1}(x,y).

For oriented edges we consider the set

\vec{\mathcal{E}}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}:\ x\sim y\},

together with the involution
(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)
.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq 2
. We next formalize the
n
-simplicial structure generated by complete subgraphs (cliques) of size at most
n\!+\!1
.

Definition 3.2
.

For
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
let

\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\Bigl\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{V}^{k+1}:\ \#\{x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\}=k+1\ \text{and}\ \{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in\mathcal{E}\ \forall i\neq j\Bigr\}

be the set of ordered
(k\!+\!1)
-tuples forming a clique (a complete subgraph) in
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
\sim
be the equivalence relation on
\mathcal{F}_{k}
given by
even
permutations. The set of oriented
k
-simplices is

T_{k}:=\mathcal{F}_{k}/\!\sim.

An orientation amounts to a partition
T_{k}=T_{k}^{+}\sqcup T_{k}^{-}
with

[x_{\sigma(0)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)}]=(-1)^{\varepsilon(\sigma)}[x_{0},\dots,x_{k}],

where
\varepsilon(\sigma)\in\{0,1\}
is the parity of the permutation
\sigma
.

Definition 3.3
.

For each
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
let
m_{k}:\mathcal{V}^{k+1}\to[0,\infty)
be a symmetric function such that

m_{k}(x_{\sigma(0)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)})=m_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k}),\ m_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{F}_{k}.

Thus
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
are the given vertex and edge weights, and for
k\geq 2
the values
m_{k}
weight the
(k\!+\!1)
-cliques.
For
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in T_{k-1}
we set the common neighbor set

F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(x_{j}),

and define the
(k\!-\!1)
-face degree by

d_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\frac{1}{m_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\sum_{x_{k+1}\in F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}}m_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},x_{k+1}).

When all higher weights
m_{k}
are
\{0,1\}
-valued (simple complex), this reduces to
d_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\#F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}
.

Definition 3.4

(Weighted oriented
n
-simplicial complex)
.

\mathcal{S}_{n}:=(\mathcal{V},(m_{k})_{0\leq k\leq n})
associated with
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},m_{0},m_{1})
is called a (weighted, oriented)
n
-simplicial complex.
For
n=2
one speaks of a (weighted) triangulation; see, e.g.,
[
BaBeJe
,
Ch4
]
.

3.2.
Hilbert cochains and Hodge operators

3.2.1.
Function spaces

We endow the cochain spaces with natural Hilbert structures.

0
-cochains.

Set

\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{V}):=\{f:\mathcal{V}\to{\mathbb{C}}\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{c}^{0}(\mathcal{V}):=\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{V}):\ \ \mathrm{supp}\,f<\infty\}.

The weighted space

\ell^{2}(m_{0}):=\Bigl\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{V}):\ \|f\|_{0}^{2}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{V}}m_{0}(x)\,|f(x)|^{2}<\infty\Bigr\}

is a Hilbert space with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{0}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{V}}m_{0}(x)\,f(x)\overline{g(x)}.

k
-cochains,
k\geq 1
.

For
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
we define:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{f:T_{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}\ \text{such that}\ f([x_{\sigma(0)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)}])=(-1)^{\varepsilon(\sigma)}f([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\Bigr\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{f:T_{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}\ \text{such that}\ f([x_{\sigma(0)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)}])=f([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\Bigr\}.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c,*}(\mathcal{V})
denote the subspace of compactly supported cochains (
*=\mathrm{skew},\mathrm{sym}
).

We equip both the symmetric and skew-symmetric cochains with the same weighted inner product:

(3.1)

\langle f,g\rangle_{k}:=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\sum_{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}m_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\,f([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\,\overline{g([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])}.

The corresponding Hilbert spaces are denoted
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
(we keep the symbol
*
only on operators, not on the spaces).

Total space.

We set

\mathcal{H}:=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\ell^{2}(m_{k}),\qquad\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|f^{(k)}\|_{k}^{2},\quad f=(f^{(0)},\dots,f^{(n)}).

Lemma 3.5
.

If
m_{k}(\sigma)>0
for all
\sigma\in T_{k}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c,*}(\mathcal{V})
is dense in
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
,
k=0,\dots,n
.

Proof.

Finite-support truncations along an exhausting family of finite subcomplexes give the standard
\ell^{2}
-approximation; the weights are positive so the indicator truncations converge in norm.∎

3.2.2.
Cochain operators

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. The (weighted) coboundary maps are defined on finitely supported cochains by

\displaystyle d^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}:\mathcal{C}^{k-1}_{c}(\mathcal{V})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c,\mathrm{skew}}(\mathcal{V}),\ d^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}f([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}]):=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}f([x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k}]),

\displaystyle d^{k}_{\mathrm{sym}}:\mathcal{C}^{k-1}_{c}(\mathcal{V})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c,\mathrm{sym}}(\mathcal{V}),\ d^{k}_{\mathrm{sym}}f([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}]):=\sum_{i=0}^{k}f([x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k}]).

(Here
\widehat{x_{i}}
means that
x_{i}
is omitted.) One checks
d^{k+1}_{*}\,d^{k}_{*}=0
for
*\in\{\mathrm{skew},\mathrm{sym}\}
.

The (formal) adjoints
\delta^{k}_{*}
are defined by

\delta^{k}_{*}:\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c,*}(\mathcal{V})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{k-1}_{c}(\mathcal{V}),\qquad\langle d^{k}_{*}f,g\rangle_{k}=\langle f,\delta^{k}_{*}g\rangle_{k-1}\ \ \text{for all }f,g\ \text{compactly supported.}

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
*\in\{\mathrm{skew},\mathrm{sym}\}
. For every
g\in\mathcal{C}^{k}_{c,*}(\mathcal{V})
and every
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in T_{k-1}
,

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\delta^{k}_{*}(g)([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])=\frac{1}{m_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\sum_{x_{k+1}\in F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}}m_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},x_{k+1})\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times g([x_{1},\dots,x_{k},x_{k+1}]).\end{split}

Proof.

Using (
3.1
) and the definitions of
d^{k}_{*}
, expand

\displaystyle\langle d^{k}_{*}f,g\rangle_{k}=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\sum_{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}m_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sigma_{*,i}\,f([x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k}])\Bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\times\overline{g([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])},

with
\sigma_{\mathrm{skew},i}=(-1)^{i}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{sym},i}=1
.

Regrouping terms by the
(k\!-\!1)
-face
[x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k}]
and using the symmetry of
m_{k}
(hence invariance under permutations) yields

\displaystyle\langle d^{k}_{*}f,g\rangle_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{F}_{k-1}}f([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\overline{\sum_{x_{k+1}\in F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}}m_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},x_{k+1})\,g([x_{1},\dots,x_{k},x_{k+1}])}.

Comparing with

\langle f,\delta^{k}_{*}g\rangle_{k-1}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum m_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,f([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])\,\overline{\delta^{k}_{*}g([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])}

gives (
3.2
).
∎

4.1.
Definition and block structure

4.1.1.
Local coordinate formulas

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}=(\mathcal{V},(m_{k})_{0\leq k\leq n})
be a weighted oriented
n
-simplicial complex, and fix
*\in\{\mathrm{skew},\mathrm{sym}\}
.
Define the Gauss–Bonnet operator

T_{*}:=d_{*}+\delta_{*}\quad\text{on }\ \mathcal{C}_{c}^{0}(\mathcal{V})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{n}(\mathcal{V}),

where

\displaystyle d_{*}(f_{1},...,f_{n+1})=(0,d^{1}_{*}f_{1},d^{2}_{*}f_{2},...,d^{n}_{*}f_{n}),

for all
(f_{1},...,f_{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{0}(\mathcal{V})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{1}(\mathcal{V})\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{n}(\mathcal{V})

and
\delta_{*}
the formal adjoint of

is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{*}(f_{1},...,f_{n+1})=(\delta^{1}_{*}f_{2},\delta^{2}_{*}f_{3},...,\delta^{n}_{*}f_{n+1},0),

for all
(f_{1},...,f_{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{0}(\mathcal{V})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{1}(\mathcal{V})\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{n}(\mathcal{V})
. The (Hodge) Laplacian is

L_{*}\ :=\ T_{*}^{2}\ =\ d_{*}\,\delta_{*}+\delta_{*}\,d_{*},

which is block-diagonal by degree:

(4.1)

L_{*}\ =\ \bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Delta_{k,*},\qquad\Delta_{k,*}\ :=\ d^{k}_{*}\,\delta^{k}_{*}\ +\ \delta^{k+1}_{*}\,d^{k+1}_{*},

with the conventions
\delta^{0}_{*}=0
and
d^{n+1}_{*}=0
.
Explicitly,

L_{*}:\ \mathcal{C}_{c}^{0}(\mathcal{V})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{1}(\mathcal{V})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{n}(\mathcal{V})\ \circlearrowleft

is given, for

L_{*}f\ =\ \bigl(\delta^{1}_{*}d^{1}_{*}f^{(0)},\ (d^{1}_{*}\delta^{1}_{*}+\delta^{2}_{*}d^{2}_{*})f^{(1)},\ \dots,\ (d^{n-1}_{*}\delta^{n-1}_{*}+\delta^{n}_{*}d^{n}_{*})f^{(n-1)},\ d^{n}_{*}\delta^{n}_{*}f^{(n)}\bigr).

Remark 4.1
.

Fix
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and write a
k
-cochain as

.
Recall the extension set

\displaystyle F_{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1})}:=\bigcap_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(x_{j})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt(\text{common neighbors that form a }k\text{-simplex with }[x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1}]).

Then
\Delta_{k,*}=\Delta_{k,*}^{-}+\Delta_{k,*}^{+}

\Delta_{k,*}^{-}:=d^{k}_{*}\delta^{k}_{*},\qquad\Delta_{k,*}^{+}:=\delta^{k+1}_{*}d^{k+1}_{*}.

(a) For skew cochains (

\displaystyle(\Delta_{k,\mathrm{skew}}^{-}f)([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\,\frac{1}{m_{k-1}(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k})}\,

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\times\sum_{z\in F_{(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k})}}m_{k}(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k},z)f([x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k},z]),

4.1.2.
Friedrichs form and first representation theorem

Define on
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V})
the (nonnegative) sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{q}_{*}(f,g):=\langle d_{*}f,d_{*}g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\langle\delta_{*}f,\delta_{*}g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad\mathfrak{q}_{*}(f):=\mathfrak{q}_{*}(f,f).

Proposition 4.2
.

For all compactly supported
u,v
one has the Green identity

\langle d_{*}u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle u,\delta_{*}v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Consequently,
\mathfrak{q}_{*}
is closable on
\mathcal{H}
; denote its closure by the same symbol and its domain by
D[\mathfrak{q}_{*}]
.
By Kato’s first representation theorem, there exists a unique nonnegative self-adjoint operator
L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathfrak{q}_{*}(f,g)=\big\langle(L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}f,\ (L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}g\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad D\!\big((L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}\big)=D[\mathfrak{q}_{*}].

Moreover,
L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}}
is the Friedrichs extension of
L_{*}
initially defined on
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V})
,
and
L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}}
with block Friedrichs operators on
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
.

Proof.

The Green identity follows from Lemma
3.6
and finite support.
If
f_{j}\to 0
in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{*}(f_{j}-f_{\ell})\to 0
, then
d_{*}f_{j}
and
\delta_{*}f_{j}
are Cauchy in
\mathcal{H}
and converge to some
u,v
.
For any compactly supported

\langle u,g\rangle=\lim_{j}\langle d_{*}f_{j},g\rangle=\lim_{j}\langle f_{j},\delta_{*}g\rangle=0,

so

; similarly

. Hence
\mathfrak{q}_{*}
is closable. The rest is Kato’s theorem.

Corollary 4.3
.

For each degree

*\in\{\mathrm{skew},\mathrm{sym}\}
,
the sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}(u,v):=\langle d_{*}^{k}u,d_{*}^{k}v\rangle_{k+1}+\langle\delta_{*}^{k}u,\delta_{*}^{k}v\rangle_{k-1},\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{k}(\mathcal{V}),

is closable on
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
, with closure still denoted
\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}
and domain
D[\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}]
.
By Kato’s first representation theorem there exists a unique nonnegative self-adjoint operator

\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}}
such that

\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}(u,v)=\big\langle(\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}u,\ (\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}v\big\rangle_{k},\qquad D\!\big((\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}\big)=D[\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}].

In particular,
\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}}
is the Friedrichs extension of the block Laplacian

\Delta_{k,*}
initially defined on
\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{k}(\mathcal{V})
, and one has the orthogonal sum

L_{*}^{\mathrm{F}}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}}.

4.2.
Up-/down-Laplacians and Hodge identities

4.2.1.
Energy identities

For each degree
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and
*\in\{\mathrm{skew},\mathrm{sym}\}
we define the (nonnegative)
down-
and
up-Laplacians
on
k
-cochains by

\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}\ :=\ d_{*}^{k}\,\delta_{*}^{k},\qquad\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+}\ :=\ \delta_{*}^{k+1}\,d_{*}^{k+1},

(with the conventions
\delta_{*}^{0}=0
and
d_{*}^{n+1}=0
). Thus the block Laplacian decomposes as

\Delta_{k,*}\ =\ \mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}\ +\ \mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+},\qquad L_{*}\ =\ \bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Delta_{k,*}.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
u\in\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{k}(\mathcal{V})
,

\langle\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}u,u\rangle_{k}\ =\ \|\delta_{*}^{k}u\|_{k-1}^{2},\qquad\langle\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+}u,u\rangle_{k}\ =\ \|d_{*}^{k+1}u\|_{k+1}^{2},

and hence

\langle\Delta_{k,*}u,u\rangle_{k}\ =\ \|\delta_{*}^{k}u\|_{k-1}^{2}\ +\ \|d_{*}^{k+1}u\|_{k+1}^{2}.

Consequently,
\ker\Delta_{k,*}=\ker d_{*}^{k+1}\cap\ker\delta_{*}^{k}
.

Proof.

By definition, Lemma
3.6
and Proposition
4.2
,

\langle d_{*}^{k}\delta_{*}^{k}u,u\rangle_{k}=\langle\delta_{*}^{k}u,\delta_{*}^{k}u\rangle_{k-1}

and

\langle\delta_{*}^{k+1}d_{*}^{k+1}u,u\rangle_{k}=\langle d_{*}^{k+1}u,d_{*}^{k+1}u\rangle_{k+1}.

The last statement is immediate.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

All identities above extend to the Friedrichs realizations on
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
by density (Corollary
4.3
):

\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}}\ =\ (\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-})^{\mathrm{F}}\ +\ (\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+})^{\mathrm{F}},\qquad\langle\Delta_{k,*}^{\mathrm{F}}u,u\rangle_{k}\ =\ \|\overline{\delta_{*}^{k}}u\|_{k-1}^{2}+\|\overline{d_{*}^{k+1}}u\|_{k+1}^{2},

for all
u\in D[\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}]
.

4.3.
Normalization conventions and spectral footprint

4.3.1.
Notation snapshot.

Spaces/weights:

\ell^{2}(m_{k})
on
k
-simplices;
M_{k}
= multiplication by
m_{k}
.

Blocks:

\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}:=d_{*}^{k}\,\delta_{*}^{k}
,
\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+}:=\delta_{*}^{k+1}d_{*}^{k+1}
,

\Delta_{k,*}=\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}+\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+}
.

Normalized blocks:

\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{\pm}:=M_{k}^{-1/2}\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{\pm}M_{k}^{-1/2}
,

\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}:=\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{-}+\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{+}
(act on unweighted
\ell^{2}
).

Degrees:

d_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\frac{1}{m_{k-1}}\sum m_{k}
(face-degree),

d_{k}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{m_{k}(\sigma)}\sum m_{k+1}
(coface-degree).

Top degree:

q(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau\subset\sigma}\frac{m_{n}(\sigma)}{m_{n-1}(\tau)}
,

a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\frac{m_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})}{m_{n-1}(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})}
.

4.3.2.
Two parallel normalizations.

(1)
Energy/Friedrichs normalization (default).
We work in
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
and set

\Delta_{k,*}=\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}+\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+}
, so that

\langle\Delta_{k,*}u,u\rangle_{k}=\|\delta_{*}^{k}u\|_{k-1}^{2}+\|d_{*}^{k+1}u\|_{k+1}^{2}

(Lemma
4.4
). This yields closed nonnegative forms and the Friedrichs realization.

(2)
Symmetric (degree) normalization.
On the unweighted
\ell^{2}
we define

(4.2)

\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{-}\ :=\ M_{k}^{-1/2}\,\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{-}\,M_{k}^{-1/2},\ \mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{+}\ :=\ M_{k}^{-1/2}\,\mathbf{L}_{k,*}^{+}\,M_{k}^{-1/2},\ \widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}\ :=\ \mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{-}+\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{+}.

These operators again come from closed nonnegative forms.
Under uniform ellipticity of the weights, one can show that

\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}
is similar to
\Delta_{k,*}
via
M_{k}^{1/2}
;
this similarity will be proved below (see Lemma
4.6
).

Form bounds with explicit constants.

Define the
down/up degrees

(4.3)

\begin{split}&D_{k}^{\downarrow}\ :=\ \sup_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in T_{k-1}}\ \frac{1}{m_{k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\sum_{z\in F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}}m_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},z),\\
&D_{k}^{\uparrow}\ :=\ \sup_{\sigma\in T_{k}}\ \frac{1}{m_{k}(\sigma)}\sum_{z\in F_{\sigma}}m_{k+1}(\sigma,z).\end{split}

Then for all
u\in\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{k}(\mathcal{V})

(4.4)

\langle\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{-}u,u\rangle\ \leq\ D_{k}^{\downarrow}\,\|u\|^{2},\ \langle\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{+}u,u\rangle\ \leq\ D_{k}^{\uparrow}\,\|u\|^{2},\ \Rightarrow\ \langle\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}u,u\rangle\ \leq\ (D_{k}^{\downarrow}+D_{k}^{\uparrow})\,\|u\|^{2}.

Proof of (
4.4
).

Write
v:=M_{k}^{-1/2}u
. By Lemma
3.6
,

(\delta_{*}^{k}v)([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])=\frac{1}{m_{k-1}}\sum_{z\in F_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}}m_{k}(\cdot)\,v([\cdot,z]).

Hence, using Cauchy–Schwarz and the definition of
D_{k}^{\downarrow}
,

\displaystyle\|\delta_{*}^{k}v\|_{k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}m_{k-1}\,\biggl|\frac{1}{m_{k-1}}\sum_{z}m_{k}\,v([\cdot,z])\biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{1}{k!}\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\frac{1}{m_{k-1}}\biggl(\sum_{z}m_{k}\biggr)\biggl(\sum_{z}m_{k}\,|v([\cdot,z])|^{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\ D_{k}^{\downarrow}\,\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\sum_{z}m_{k}\,|v([\cdot,z])|^{2}\ =\ D_{k}^{\downarrow}\,\|v\|_{k}^{2}\ =\ D_{k}^{\downarrow}\,\|u\|^{2},

which gives the first bound since
\langle\mathbb{L}_{k,*}^{-}u,u\rangle=\|\delta_{*}^{k}v\|_{k-1}^{2}
.
The second estimate is analogous starting from

(d_{*}^{k+1}v)([x_{0},\dots,x_{k+1}])=\sum_{i}\sigma_{*,i}\,v([\widehat{x_{i}}])

and bounding with the coface-degree
D_{k}^{\uparrow}
. Summing yields (
4.4
).
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume
0<c_{k}\leq m_{k}(\sigma)\leq C_{k}<\infty
for all
k
-simplices
\sigma
.
Then
M_{k}^{\pm 1/2}
are boundedly invertible on
\ell^{2}
, and

\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}=M_{k}^{-1/2}\,\Delta_{k,*}\,M_{k}^{1/2}

holds as an identity of selfadjoint operators with

\|M_{k}^{1/2}\|\leq C_{k}^{1/2},\ \ \|M_{k}^{-1/2}\|\leq c_{k}^{-1/2}.

In particular
\mathrm{spec}(\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*})=\mathrm{spec}(\Delta_{k,*})
and
e^{-t\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}}=M_{k}^{-1/2}e^{-t\Delta_{k,*}}M_{k}^{1/2}
.

Proof.

Bounded invertibility is immediate from the inequalities

c_{k}\|u\|^{2}\leq\langle M_{k}u,u\rangle\leq C_{k}\|u\|^{2}.

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}[u]=\|d_{*}^{k+1}u\|_{k+1}^{2}+\|\delta_{*}^{k}u\|_{k-1}^{2}
on
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
with core
\mathcal{C}_{c,*}^{k}
; by closability its closure defines
\Delta_{k,*}
.
Define on unweighted
\ell^{2}
the form

\widehat{\mathfrak{q}}_{k,*}[w]:=\mathfrak{q}_{k,*}[M_{k}^{-1/2}w].

Then
\widehat{\mathfrak{q}}_{k,*}
is closed, nonnegative, and its operator is precisely

by (
4.2
).
Since

are boundedly invertible,

is a topological isomorphism between the form domains, and the first representation theorem yields

∎

From (

), if

At top degree

).

5.
Unitary equivalence on colorable complexes

Setup and color convention.

Assume the
1
-skeleton of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is
p
-partite with a fixed coloring map
c:\mathcal{V}\to\{1,\dots,p\}
.
Equivalently, we assume the
1
-skeleton admits a proper vertex-coloring, so that each edge joins vertices of distinct colors and, more generally, each simplex contains vertices of pairwise distinct colors.
This ensures that the ordering by colors is well-defined.

For each ordered
k
-simplex
(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
let
\pi_{c}
be the (unique) permutation that sorts the colors nondecreasingly:

c(x_{\pi_{c}(0)})\leq\cdots\leq c(x_{\pi_{c}(k)})
(ties cannot occur inside a simplex by colorability).
Define the
color sign

(5.1)

S([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\ :=\ (-1)^{\varepsilon(\pi_{c})}\ \in\ \{\pm 1\},

which is well-defined on oriented simplices because even permutations do not change
\varepsilon(\pi_{c})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every degree
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
the operator

(5.2)

(U_{k}f)([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\ :=\ S([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\,f([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])

defines a unitary
U_{k}:\ell^{2}(m_{k})\to\ell^{2}(m_{k})
obeying, on
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\bullet}
,

(5.3)

U_{k}\ d^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}\ =\ d^{k}_{\mathrm{sym}}\ U_{k-1},\qquad U_{k-1}\ \delta^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}\ =\ \delta^{k}_{\mathrm{sym}}\ U_{k},

and hence, blockwise on Laplacians,

(5.4)

U_{k}\ \Delta_{k,\mathrm{skew}}\ U_{k}^{-1}\ =\ \Delta_{k,\mathrm{sym}}.

These identities extend to the closed/Friedrichs realizations, since
U
preserves compact support and is unitary on each
\ell^{2}(m_{k})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
[x_{0},\dots,x_{k}]
be an oriented
k
-simplex in a
p
-colorable complex and let
\pi_{c}
be the permutation sorting the colors nondecreasingly.
For each
i
, let
\tau_{i}=[x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k}]
and let
\pi_{c,i}
be the color-sorting permutation of
\tau_{i}
.
Then
\varepsilon(\pi_{c,i})\equiv\varepsilon(\pi_{c})+i\pmod{2}
.

Proof.

Since no two vertices in a simplex share the same color, the list of colors
(c(x_{0}),\dots,c(x_{k}))
is a permutation of a strictly increasing list.
Deleting
x_{i}
from
[x_{0},\dots,x_{k}]
amounts to removing the
i
-th entry in the
original order
.
To obtain the color-sorted order for
\tau_{i}
, one may first apply
\pi_{c}
to sort the full list, then delete the entry at position
\pi_{c}(i)
; or equivalently, delete
x_{i}
first and apply the induced permutation on the remaining positions. The two procedures differ by moving
x_{i}
across exactly
i
elements (those before it) prior to the sort, which contributes a parity
(-1)^{i}
. More formally, write the sorting map for the full simplex as a product of adjacent transpositions; deleting the
i
-th element erases one strand in the associated permutation graph, contributing precisely
i
crossings modulo
2
. Hence
\varepsilon(\pi_{c,i})=\varepsilon(\pi_{c})+i\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Unitarity is immediate from
|S|=1
.

For (
5.3
), fix
(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathcal{F}_{k}

\tau_{i}=[x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{i}},\dots,x_{k}]
.
By definition,

(d^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}g)([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}g(\tau_{i}).

Choose
\pi_{c}
sorting
[x_{0},\dots,x_{k}]
and

It is elementary that

Therefore

Setting

\displaystyle\big(U_{k}d^{k}_{\mathrm{skew}}U_{k-1}f\big)([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])

\displaystyle=S([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\,(U_{k-1}f)(\tau_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}S([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}])(-1)^{i}\,S(\tau_{i})\,f(\tau_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}f(\tau_{i})\ =\ (d^{k}_{\mathrm{sym}}f)([x_{0},\dots,x_{k}]).

Thus

∎

For all

6.
Essential self-adjointness via explicit operator bounds

6.1.
Top degree: line-complex reduction and bounds

We work at the top degree of an
n
-dimensional simplicial complex. Throughout,
*
stands for either
skew
or
sym
; by Theorem
5.1
, the two models are unitarily equivalent on colorable complexes.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(\widehat{\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathcal{E}})
be a locally finite graph and let
w:\widehat{\mathcal{V}}\times\widehat{\mathcal{V}}\to[0,\infty)
be a symmetric kernel supported on adjacency. Define

(\mathcal{A}f)(\hat{\sigma}):=\sum_{\hat{\tau}\sim\hat{\sigma}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,f(\hat{\tau}),\qquad D(\hat{\sigma}):=\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau}).

If
D\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})
, then
\mathcal{A}
extends to a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})
with

\|\mathcal{A}\|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

For finitely supported
f,g
,

\langle\mathcal{A}f,g\rangle=\sum_{\hat{\sigma}}\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,f(\hat{\tau})\,\overline{g(\hat{\sigma})}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz on the inner sum,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,f(\hat{\tau})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,|f(\hat{\tau})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=D(\hat{\sigma})^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,|f(\hat{\tau})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Thus

|\langle\mathcal{A}f,g\rangle|\leq\sum_{\hat{\sigma}}D(\hat{\sigma})^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,|f(\hat{\tau})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,|g(\hat{\sigma})|.

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz in
\hat{\sigma}
and using
D\leq\|D\|_{\infty}
,

|\langle\mathcal{A}f,g\rangle|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{\hat{\sigma}}\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,|f(\hat{\tau})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|g\|_{2}.

Since
w
is symmetric,

\sum_{\hat{\sigma}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})=D(\hat{\tau})\leq\|D\|_{\infty},

hence

\sum_{\hat{\sigma}}\sum_{\hat{\tau}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\,|f(\hat{\tau})|^{2}=\sum_{\hat{\tau}}\Big(\sum_{\hat{\sigma}}w(\hat{\sigma},\hat{\tau})\Big)|f(\hat{\tau})|^{2}\leq\|D\|_{\infty}\,\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore
|\langle\mathcal{A}f,g\rangle|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}\,\|f\|_{2}\,\|g\|_{2}
, so

\|\mathcal{A}\|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}
. Since
w
is symmetric,
\mathcal{A}
is self-adjoint.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be a locally finite
n
-dimensional simplicial complex with weights
(m_{n-1},m_{n})
.
For oriented
n
-simplices
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
sharing an
(n{-}1)
-face
\tau=\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
, define

w(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}):=\frac{\sqrt{m_{n}(\sigma)\,m_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})}}{m_{n-1}(\tau)}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}\}},\qquad V_{*,n}(\sigma):=\sum_{\tau\subset\sigma}\frac{m_{n}(\sigma)}{m_{n-1}(\tau)}.

Assume

(A1)

D(\sigma):=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}w(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\in L^{\infty}
,

(A2)

V_{*,n}\in L^{\infty}
.

Then there exists a unitary
U:\ell^{2}(m_{n})\to\ell^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})
identifying oriented
n
-simplices with vertices of the line-complex such that

U\,\Delta_{n,*}\,U^{-1}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,n}),

where
(\mathcal{A}f)(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma}w(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\,f(\sigma^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,n})f)(\sigma)=V_{*,n}(\sigma)f(\sigma)
.
Consequently,

\|\Delta_{n,*}\|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}+\|V_{*,n}\|_{\infty},

and
\Delta_{n,*}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{c,*}
.

Proof.

We argue in four steps.

Step 1. Unitary renormalization.

Define
U:\ell^{2}(m_{n})\to\ell^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})
by

(Uf)(\sigma):=m_{n}(\sigma)^{1/2}f(\sigma).

This map is clearly unitary, since

\|Uf\|_{\ell^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})}^{2}=\sum_{\sigma}|m_{n}(\sigma)^{1/2}f(\sigma)|^{2}=\sum_{\sigma}m_{n}(\sigma)|f(\sigma)|^{2}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{n})}^{2}.

Step 2. Local decomposition of
\Delta_{n,*}
.

Expanding
\Delta_{n,*}=d_{n-1}^{*}d_{n-1}
in local coordinates shows that:

•

For each neighbor
\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma
across a common
(n{-}1)
-face
\tau
, the off-diagonal coefficient is

w(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\frac{\sqrt{m_{n}(\sigma)\,m_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})}}{m_{n-1}(\tau)}.

•

The diagonal contribution comes from all faces
\tau\subset\sigma
and equals

V_{*,n}(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau\subset\sigma}\frac{m_{n}(\sigma)}{m_{n-1}(\tau)}.

Thus in the renormalized picture we obtain the decomposition

U\,\Delta_{n,*}\,U^{-1}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,n}),

where
\mathcal{A}
is the weighted adjacency operator with kernel
w
, and
\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,n})
is the multiplication operator by
V_{*,n}
.

Step 3. Operator norm estimates.

By Proposition
6.1
,
\|\mathcal{A}\|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}
, where
D(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}w(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
is the line-complex degree.
Assumption (A2) guarantees
\|V_{*,n}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, hence
\|\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,n})\|=\|V_{*,n}\|_{\infty}
.
Therefore

\|U\Delta_{n,*}U^{-1}\|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}+\|V_{*,n}\|_{\infty}.

Step 4. Essential self-adjointness.

Since
\Delta_{n,*}
is symmetric on the core
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{c,*}
and unitarily equivalent to the bounded self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,n})
, it follows that
\Delta_{n,*}
extends uniquely to a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(m_{n})
.
In other words,
\Delta_{n,*}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{c,*}
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

If each
n
-simplex has at most
L<\infty
neighbors across its
(n{-}1)
-faces, and the weights satisfy

0<m_{-}\leq m_{n-1}(\tau),\qquad m_{n}(\sigma)\leq m^{+}<\infty,

then

\|D\|_{\infty}\leq L\,\tfrac{m^{+}}{m_{-}},\qquad\|V_{*,n}\|_{\infty}\leq(n+1)\tfrac{m^{+}}{m_{-}},

so
\Delta_{n,*}
is bounded and essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{c,*}
.

Proof.

By the assumptions, for any
\sigma

D(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma}w(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\leq L\,\frac{m^{+}}{m_{-}}.

Similarly,

|V_{*,n}(\sigma)|\leq\sum_{\tau\subset\sigma}\frac{m_{n}(\sigma)}{m_{n-1}(\tau)}\leq(n+1)\,\frac{m^{+}}{m_{-}}.

Thus both
D
and
V_{*,n}
are uniformly bounded.
By Theorem
6.2
,
\Delta_{n,*}
is bounded and essentially self-adjoint on the core.
∎

Example (
n=3
, unit weights).

On a uniform tetrahedral mesh with unit weights
m_{n-1}\equiv m_{n}\equiv 1
, each interior tetrahedron has four neighbors (one across each face).
In this case the general formula (
6.2
) specializes to

(6.1)

(\Delta_{3,*}u)(\sigma)\;=\;4\,u(\sigma)\ -\ \sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma}u(\sigma^{\prime})\,,

which is precisely the graph Laplacian on the line-complex of tetrahedra.
By Lemma
4.4
,
\langle\Delta_{3,*}u,u\rangle=\|\delta_{*}^{3}u\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0
.

Non-unit weights and boundary cases.

In the weighted setting, the top-degree Laplacian has the local expression

(6.2)

(\Delta_{n,*}u)(\sigma)=q(\sigma)\,u(\sigma)\;-\;\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma}a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\,u(\sigma^{\prime}),

with coefficients

q(\sigma):=\sum_{\tau\subset\sigma}\frac{m_{n}(\sigma)}{m_{n-1}(\tau)},\qquad a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}):=\frac{m_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})}{m_{n-1}(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})}.

The coefficients
a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
are symmetric up to the unitary
U
of Theorem
5.1
.
At the boundary (when some cofaces are missing), the degree in the line-complex decreases accordingly; the same formula remains valid with sums restricted to existing neighbors.

Numerical weighted example (
n=3
).

We illustrate formula (
6.2
) in the case
n=3
.
Consider an interior tetrahedron
\sigma
with four neighbors
\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\sigma^{\prime}_{4}
across its faces
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{4}
.
Choose the weights

\displaystyle m_{3}(\sigma)=2,\ m_{3}(\sigma^{\prime}_{i})=1\ (i=1,\dots,4),\ m_{2}(\tau_{1})=1,\ m_{2}(\tau_{2})=2,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptm_{2}(\tau_{3})=1,\ m_{2}(\tau_{4})=2.

Then

q(\sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\frac{m_{3}(\sigma)}{m_{2}(\tau_{j})}=2\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{2}+1+\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)=6,\qquad a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}_{j})=\frac{m_{3}(\sigma^{\prime}_{j})}{m_{2}(\tau_{j})}\in\{1,\tfrac{1}{2},1,\tfrac{1}{2}\}.

For values
u(\sigma)=3
and
(u(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}),u(\sigma^{\prime}_{2}),u(\sigma^{\prime}_{3}),u(\sigma^{\prime}_{4}))=(1,-2,0.5,4)
, one obtains

\displaystyle(\Delta_{3,*}u)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=q(\sigma)\,u(\sigma)\;-\;\sum_{j=1}^{4}a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}_{j})\,u(\sigma^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=6\cdot 3\ -\ \bigl(1\cdot 1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot(-2)+1\cdot 0.5+\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 4\bigr)=\ 15.5.

This explicit computation shows how the weights
m_{2},m_{3}
affect both the diagonal degree
q(\sigma)
and the off-diagonal couplings
a(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
.

6.2.
Comparison with intermediate degrees

For
k\geq 2
we have the general bound

\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{k,*}\|\ \leq\ D_{k}^{\downarrow}+D_{k}^{\uparrow},

which provides an explicit Schur-type estimate.

At the top degree
k=n
, this bound admits a concrete reinterpretation in terms
of the adjacency structure of the line-complex (neighboring
n
-simplices). For
the intermediate degrees
2\leq k<n
, the estimate controls both the
”up” and ”down” Laplacian components. Thus the result is consistent across all
degrees, although it is somewhat less precise than the explicit reduction
available at the top degree, where the block can be described directly in terms
of adjacency plus a diagonal potential.

7.
Sharp edge-Laplacian bounds without geometric assumptions.

We consider the edge (top-degree) Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{1,*}
on a locally finite (possibly weighted) graph
G
with finite maximum degree.
Our bounds above show, in the unweighted
d
-regular case (
d\geq 4
),

\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 4(d-1),

and, under weight comparability, the weighted bound
\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\leq C_{\mathrm{w}}\,2(d-1)
with
C_{\mathrm{w}}
given explicitly.

7.1.
Floquet–Bloch symbols for the edge block on periodic lattices

Setup and normalization

Let
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a periodic unweighted graph, invariant under a lattice
\Gamma\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
acting freely on
\mathcal{V}
.
Choose a fundamental cell
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cell}}
with finitely many vertices.
Then every edge
e=\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}
can be encoded by its endpoints in
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cell}}
and a translation vector
\gamma\in\Gamma
when
y=x+\gamma
.

The Fourier transform identifies
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E})
with a direct integral over the Brillouin zone
\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E})\ \simeq\ \int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}^{\oplus}{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{1}}\,d\theta,\qquad N_{1}=\#\{\text{edge orbits in }\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cell}}\}.

Here each fiber
{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{1}}
corresponds to edges in the cell, with boundary conditions twisted by phases
e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\theta,\gamma\rangle}
.
Under this reduction, the normalized edge Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}
is unitarily equivalent to multiplication by a Hermitian
N_{1}\times N_{1}
matrix symbol
\sigma(\theta)
.
Its norm satisfies

\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|\ =\ \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\ \rho(\sigma(\theta)),

where
\rho
denotes the spectral radius.
This provides exact values of the operator norm for periodic lattices.

Examples

•

Square lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
(degree
d=4
).

Spectrum:
\{\,0,\ 4-2\cos\theta_{1}-2\cos\theta_{2}\,\}
.
Hence
\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|=8
(vs universal bound
12
).

•

Triangular lattice (
d=6
).

Bloch symbol
3\times 3
, maximal eigenvalue
\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|=12
(vs bound
20
).

•

Cubic lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
(
d=6
).

Eigenvalues
\lambda(\theta)=2(3-\cos\theta_{1}-\cos\theta_{2}-\cos\theta_{3})
.
Hence
\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|=12
(vs bound
20
).

•

BCC lattice (
d=8
).

Bloch symbol
4\times 4
, maximal eigenvalue
\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|=16
(vs bound
28
).

•

FCC lattice (
d=12
).

Bloch symbol
6\times 6
, numerical evaluation
\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|\approx 24
(vs bound
44
).

7.2.
Application: boundedness and ESA for top-degree Laplacians

Let
G
be a locally finite graph with maximal degree
\Delta(G)<\infty
. The
line graph

L(G)
has vertices given by the edges of
G
, with adjacency determined by the condition of sharing an endpoint. A standard fact is that

(7.1)

\Delta(L(G))\;\leq\;2\,(\Delta(G)-1).

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a locally finite graph and let
L(G)
denote its line graph with maximal degree

\Delta(L(G)):=\sup_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\deg_{L(G)}(e).

Then:

(1)

For the unnormalized Laplacian one has

\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 2\,\Delta(L(G))+2,

hence
\Delta_{1,*}
is bounded and in particular essentially self-adjoint (ESA) on
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}
.

(2)

For the normalized Laplacian one has

\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 2\,\Delta(L(G)).

Since
\Delta(L(G))\leq 2(\Delta(G)-1)
, it follows that

\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 4(\Delta(G)-1),\qquad\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 4(\Delta(G)-1)+2.

Proof.

We first consider the unweighted case
m_{0}\equiv m_{1}\equiv 1
.

Step 1. Reduction.

By the line-complex reduction (see Section
6
), there exists a unitary

U:\ell^{2}(m_{1})\to\ell^{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})\simeq\ell^{2}(\text{oriented edges})

such that

U\,\Delta_{1,*}\,U^{-1}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,1}),

where
\mathcal{A}
is the adjacency operator on the line-complex with kernel

w(e,e^{\prime})=\mathbf{1}_{\{e\sim e^{\prime}\}}
, and the diagonal term is constant

V_{*,1}(e)=2
. Thus

\Delta_{1,*}\ \sim\ \mathcal{A}+2\,\mathrm{Id}.

Step 2. Schur bound for
\mathcal{A}
.

For each oriented edge
e
, define

D(e):=\sum_{e^{\prime}}w(e,e^{\prime})=\deg_{\text{or}}(e).

Each neighbor in
L(G)
contributes two oriented neighbors, hence

D(e)\leq 2\,\deg_{L(G)}(e)\leq 2\,\Delta(L(G)).

By the Schur test (Proposition
6.1
), we obtain

\|\mathcal{A}\|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}\leq 2\,\Delta(L(G)).

Step 3. Norm estimate.

Since
\|\mathcal{Q}(V_{*,1})\|=2
, we deduce

\|U\Delta_{1,*}U^{-1}\|\ \leq\ 2\,\Delta(L(G))+2.

By unitarity, the same holds for
\|\Delta_{1,*}\|
. Thus
\Delta_{1,*}
is bounded.
Being symmetric on the core
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}
, its closure is the unique self-adjoint extension, hence
\Delta_{1,*}
is ESA.

Step 4. Normalized block.

For the normalized Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}
the constant diagonal disappears in the reduction, yielding

\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 2\,\Delta(L(G)).

Step 5. Relation to
\Delta(G)
.

Since
\Delta(L(G))\leq 2(\Delta(G)-1)
, the stated bounds follow.
∎

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
G
be locally finite and
X
a weighted simplicial complex over
G
with vertex weights
m_{0}
and edge weights
m_{1}
.
Assume uniform comparability: there exist constants
0<c_{0}\leq C_{0}<\infty
and
0<c_{1}\leq C_{1}<\infty
such that

c_{0}\leq m_{0}(x)\leq C_{0},\qquad c_{1}\leq m_{1}(x,y)\leq C_{1}\ \text{ for all }x\sim y.

Then the top-degree Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{1,*}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E},m_{1})
is bounded with

\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ C_{\mathrm{w}}\,\Delta\!\bigl(L(G)\bigr),\qquad C_{\mathrm{w}}:=2\,\frac{C_{1}}{c_{1}}\,\max\!\Bigl\{1,\frac{C_{0}}{c_{0}}\Bigr\}.

In particular,
\Delta_{1,*}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}
.

Proof.

We work in
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E},m_{1})
for
1
-cochains and
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V},m_{0})
for
0
-cochains.
Let
d_{0}:\ell^{2}(\mathcal{V},m_{0})\to\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E},m_{1})
be the incidence operator and
d_{0}^{*}
its adjoint.
In the unweighted case,
\Delta_{1,*}=d_{0}d_{0}^{*}+A_{L(G)}
, where
A_{L(G)}
is the edge-adjacency (on the line graph).
Under weights, both the diagonal (degree) and off-diagonal (adjacency) coefficients are rescaled by ratios of
m_{0},m_{1}
, controlled by the comparability constants.

Adjacency bound via Schur test.

Define
K:\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E},m_{1})\to\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E},m_{1})
by

(Kf)(e)\ :=\ \sum_{e^{\prime}\sim e}\,\alpha_{e,e^{\prime}}\,f(e^{\prime}),

where
\alpha_{e,e^{\prime}}
are the weighted adjacency coefficients. By comparability, there exists a constant

C^{\prime}\!\asymp\!C_{1}/c_{1}
such that
|\alpha_{e,e^{\prime}}|\leq C^{\prime}
for all adjacent
e\sim e^{\prime}
.
Since each
e
has at most
\Delta(L(G))
neighbors in
L(G)
, the row and column sums satisfy

\sum_{e^{\prime}}|\alpha_{e,e^{\prime}}|\leq C^{\prime}\Delta(L(G))
and
\sum_{e}|\alpha_{e,e^{\prime}}|\leq C^{\prime}\Delta(L(G))
.
By the Schur test,
\|K\|\leq C^{\prime}\Delta(L(G))
.

Degree/diagonal part.

The degree term is a multiplication operator bounded by a constant multiple of
\Delta(L(G))
, with the prefactor controlled by

\max\{1,C_{0}/c_{0}\}
due to the weighted inner product and the fact that an edge meets at most two vertices and at most
\Delta(L(G))
adjacent edges.

Putting things together.

Since
\Delta_{1,*}
is (up to sign conventions) the sum of the degree part and the adjacency part, the triangle inequality yields

\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\ \leq\ 2\,\frac{C_{1}}{c_{1}}\,\max\!\Bigl\{1,\frac{C_{0}}{c_{0}}\Bigr\}\,\Delta(L(G))\ =:\ C_{\mathrm{w}}\,\Delta(L(G)).

This proves the claim. In particular, for
d
-regular graphs with
d\geq 4
one has
\Delta(L(G))=2(d-1)
, hence

\|\Delta_{1,*}\|\leq C_{\mathrm{w}}\,2(d-1)
, and
\Delta_{1,*}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}
.
∎

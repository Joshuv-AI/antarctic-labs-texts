Seierstad Sufficient Conditions for Stochastic Optimal Control Problems with Infinite Horizon
Anton O. Belyakov, Yuri M. Kabanov, Ivan A. Terekhov, Maxim M. Savinov

Abstract
In this note we consider a problem of stochastic optimal control with the infinite-time horizon. We present analogues of the Seierstad sufficient conditions of overtaking optimality based on the dual variables stochastic described by BSDEs appeared in the Bismut–Pontryagin maximum principle.

1
Introduction

Infinite-horizon optimal control problems play an important role in the modern economic theory. A typical examples are optimal investment problems and optimal consumptions of households. In both cases stochastic parameters are natural, see, e.g.,
Romer
. Historically, the main tools for stochastic optimal control problems were HJB equations — in a striking contrast
with the deterministic optimal control problems, where many results were obtained using the Pontryagin maximum principle.

Necessary optimality conditions in the form of the stochastic maximum principle are discussed already for several decades. They appeared via convex duality in the seminal paper by Bismut
Bismut1973
, where backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) with linear driver were introduced to describe the dynamics of dual variables. Bismut applied successfully his technique to the stochastic linear-quadratic regulator and the Merton consumption-investments
problems in
Bismut1976a
;
Bismut1976
. By this reason we believe that his contribution merits to be called the Bismut–Pontryagin stochastic maximum principle. The relation between BSDEs and
the predictable representation theorem for the Wiener functionals was understood
in the rarely available paper
Kabanov1978
, where stochastic maximum principle in the form of necessary and sufficient condition was established for a model with linear dynamics of the phase variable. Arkin and Saksonov discussed in
ArkinSaksonov1978
some important features of the stochastic maximum principle in the non-convex case. Nowadays the mentioned papers have only a historical interest. The modern theory of non-linear BSDEs was developed in much deeper works by Pardoux and Peng,
PP1990
;
P1990
. The literature in this field and its applications is still exploding, see, e.g., a more recent book
CZ
on stochastic contract theory or
OksendalSulem
on stochastic control of jump diffusions.

In this paper we suggest a stochastic analogue of the Seierstad theorem,
Seierstad1977
;
Seierstad1978
;
Belyakov2020
, on sufficient
conditions for overtaking optimality. As in the Mangasarian conditions, it is assumed that the Hamilton–Pontryagin function is concave in the state and control variables. The obtained sufficient conditions are written using solutions of the BSDEs depending on the running time horizon.

We also prove that in the case of a linear Hamilton–Pontryagin function the Seierstad conditions become not only sufficient but also necessary for the overtaking optimality of the control.

We provide examples, where the optimality conditions can be checked. In particular, in Example 2 we consider a stochastic version of the Nerlove–Arrow dynamics, see
NA
;
Marinelli
and
present a result on the overtaking optimality for the optimal investment problem with quadratic investment adaptation costs and stochastic marginal productivity of capital, cf.
Romer
.

2
Setting and main result

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\bf F}=({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},{\bf P})
be a filtered probability space satisfy the usual condition. Assume that
{\bf F}
is generated by a Wiener process
{\bf W}=(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Let
L^{22}_{T}:=L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}[0,T],dPdt)
.

Fix a non-empty subset
U
of
{\mathbb{R}}
. The control process
{\bf u}=(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in our setting is just a
U
-valued predictable process. We say that the control
{\bf u}
is admissible if
(u_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in L^{22}_{T}
for each
T
. We denote
{\mathcal{U}}
the set of admissible controls.

The controlled
dynamics corresponding to
{\bf u}
is given by the scalar process
\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{u}}=(x_{t})
with a fixed initial value
x_{0}
solving the
stochastic differential equation

dx_{t}=f(t,u_{t},x_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})\,dW_{t}.

To guarantee the existence and uniqueness of strong solution we assume several rather standard hypotheses.

The drift coefficient
f=f(\omega,t,u,x)
is continuously differentiable in
(u,x)
for each
(\omega,t)
and predictable in
(\omega,t)
for each

(u,x)\in U\times{\mathbb{R}}
, that is the function
(\omega,t)\mapsto f(\omega,t,u,x)
is predictable. The derivative in
x
is uniformly bounded:
|f_{x}(\omega,t,u,x)|\leq{\rm const}
. Moreover, the
linear growth condition is fulfilled:

|f(\omega,t,u,x)|\leq C_{t}(1+|u|+|x|),\qquad\forall\,(\omega,t,u)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times U\times{\mathbb{R}},

where
{\bf C}=(C_{t})
is a finite increasing function

The diffusion coefficient
\sigma=\sigma(\omega,t,u,x)
satisfy the same conditions as the drift coefficient.
It follows that

{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}(t,u_{t},x_{t})dt<\infty.

For a given finite time horizon
T
, we consider the value functional (to maximize)

J_{T}(\boldsymbol{u}):={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}g(t,u_{t},x_{t})dt,

(1)

where the process
x={\bf x}^{\bf u}
.

We assume that the function
g=g(\omega,t,u,x)
is continuously differentiable in
(u,x)
for each
(\omega,t)
and predictable in
(\omega,t)
for each

(u,x)\in U\times{\mathbb{R}}
and, for simplicity, satisfies the linear growth condition as
f
and
\sigma
.
Moreover, we assume that

\big|g_{x}(\omega,t,u,x)\big|\leq\tilde{c}_{T}(1+|u|+|x|),\qquad\forall\,(\omega,t,u)\in\Omega\times[0,t]\times U\times{\mathbb{R}},

where
\tilde{\bf c}=(\tilde{c}_{t})
is a finite increasing function. This condition ensures that for any admissible control

{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\left|g_{x}(t,u_{t},x_{t})\right|^{2}dt<\infty.

(2)

These assumptions imply that for any admissible control
J_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})<\infty
.

However, the linear growth assumption of
g
is too restrictive (it excludes quadratic cost).
One can work without restricting controls to those with well-defined value functional.

Definition 1

The control
\hat{\bf u}\in{\mathcal{U}}
is called weakly overtaking optimal, WOO, if
for any
{\bf u}\in{\mathcal{U}}

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\left(J_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})-J_{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\right)\leq 0.

Definition 2

The control
\hat{\bf u}\in{\mathcal{U}}
is called overtaking optimal, OO, if
for any
{\bf u}\in{\mathcal{U}}

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\left(J_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})-J_{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\right)\leq 0.

Let us define the Hamilton–Pontryagin function

\mathcal{H}(t,u,x,p,h)=p\,f(t,u,x)+h\,\sigma(t,u,x)+g(t,u,x).

(3)

We skip, as usual,
\omega
in this formula.

Let
\hat{\bf u}
be an admissible control which we want to test for optimality and let
\hat{\bf x}
be the corresponding process.
We consider on the interval
[0,T]
the BSDE

\displaystyle dp_{t}=-\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t},h_{t})dt+h_{t}dW_{t},\quad p_{T}=0.

(4)

The driver of this equation,

\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t}(\omega),\hat{x}_{t}(\omega),p,h):=pf_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})+h\sigma_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})+g_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t}),

is such that

{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},0,0)|^{2}dt={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}|g_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})|^{2}dt<\infty

in virtue of (
2
). Also,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t}(\omega),\hat{x}_{t}(\omega),p,h)-\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t}(\omega),\hat{x}_{t}(\omega),\tilde{p},\tilde{h})|

\displaystyle\leq|p-\tilde{p}|f_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})+|h-\tilde{h}|\sigma_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq C(|p-\tilde{p}|+|h-\tilde{h}|).

These conditions ensure
the existence and uniqueness of solution

({\bf p}^{T},{\bf h}^{T})
of BSDE (
4
) in the space
X_{T}\times L^{22}_{T}
, where
X_{T}
is the space of adapted continuous processes
{\bf p}
such that
{\bf E}\sup_{s\leq T}|p_{s}|^{2}<\infty
, see
CZ
.

Our hypotheses guarantee the existence and uniqueness of solution
(\hat{\bf p},\hat{\bf h})=(\hat{\bf p}^{T},\hat{\bf h}^{T})

in the Banach space
B_{T}\times L^{22}_{T}
, where
B_{T}
is the space of continuous adapted processes
p
such that
{\bf E}\sup_{t\leq T}|p_{t}|^{2}<\infty
.

Assumption 2.1

There is a convex set
\tilde{U}\supseteq U
and
T_{1}>0
such that for all
t\geq T_{1}
the function

(u,x)\mapsto\mathcal{H}(t,u,x,p,h)
is concave on
\tilde{U}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Optimality: sufficient conditions.)

Let Assumption
2.1
be fulfilled. Then the admissible control
(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})
is WOO,
if

\liminf_{T\to\infty}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\left(u_{t}-\hat{u}_{t}\right)dt\leq 0,

(5)

and OO, if

\limsup_{T\to\infty}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\left(u_{t}-\hat{u}_{t}\right)dt\leq 0,

(6)

for every admissible control
\boldsymbol{u}
.

Theorem 2.3

(Optimality: necessary and sufficient conditions.)

If the function
\mathcal{H}(t,u,x,p)
is linear in
(u,x)
on
\tilde{U}\times X
, then the conditions (
5
) and (
6
) in Theorem
2.2
are also necessary.

3
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.
Put
{\bf y}:={\bf x}-\hat{\bf x}
. Since

dy_{t}=\left(f(t,u_{t},x_{t})-f(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{u}_{t})\right)dt+\left(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\right)dW_{t},

it follows by the Ito product formula

\displaystyle d(p_{t}^{T}y_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{t}^{T}\,dy_{t}+y_{t}\,d{p}_{t}^{T}+h^{T}_{t}\big(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{t}^{T}\big(f(t,u_{t},x_{t})-f(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)\,dt+h^{T}_{t}(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)dt

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{H}}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})y_{t}\,dt+h^{T}_{t}y_{t}\,dW_{t}

\displaystyle+p_{t}^{T}\big(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)\,dW_{t}.

Using its integral form we have:

\displaystyle p_{T}^{T}y_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{0}^{T}y_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}^{T}\,dy_{t}+y_{t}\,d{p}_{t}^{T}+\int_{0}^{T}h^{T}_{t}\big(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}^{T}\left[\big(f(t,u_{t},x_{t})-f(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)+h^{T}_{t}(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)\right]dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\,y_{t}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}h^{T}_{t}y_{t}\,dW_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}^{T}\left(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\right)dW_{t}.

The stochastic integrals above have zero expectations due to our
assumptions. Since

p^{T}_{T}=0
and
y_{0}=0
we get that

\displaystyle{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}^{T}\left[\big(f(t,u_{t},x_{t})-f(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)+h^{T}_{t}(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)\right]dt

\displaystyle={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\,y_{t}\,dt.

With this we complete the proof easily. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Delta J_{T}:=J_{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})-J_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})={\bf E}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\big(g(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})-g(t,u_{t},x_{t})\big)\,dt

\displaystyle={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\big({\mathcal{H}}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})-{\mathcal{H}}(t,u_{t},x_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}^{T}\left[\big(f(t,u_{t},x_{t})-f(t,\hat{x}_{t},\hat{u}_{t})\big)+h^{T}_{t}(\sigma(t,u_{t},x_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t})\big)\right]dt

\displaystyle={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\big({\mathcal{H}}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})-{\mathcal{H}}(t,u_{t},x_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\,y_{t}\,dt.

In virtue of Assumption
2.1
on concavity

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(t,u_{t},x_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})-{\mathcal{H}}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big(u_{t}-\hat{u}_{t}\big)+{\mathcal{H}}_{x}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big(x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\big).

(7)

Using this inequality we obtain from the above formula the bound

\Delta J_{T}\geq{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big(\hat{u}_{t}-u_{t}\big)\,dt.

(8)

Taking limits we get the inequalities

\limsup\limits_{T\to\infty}\Delta J_{T}\geq\limsup\limits_{T\to\infty}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big(\hat{u}_{t}-u_{t}\big)\,dt

and

\liminf\limits_{T\to\infty}\Delta J_{T}\geq\liminf\limits_{T\to\infty}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big(\hat{u}_{t}-u_{t}\big)\,dt.

leading to the sufficient conditions of optimality (
5
) and (
6
).

\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.
Due to assumed linearity the inequalities (
7
) and (
8
) hold as equalities and the above arguments leads to the identity

\displaystyle\Delta J_{T}

\displaystyle={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T},h^{T}_{t})\big(\hat{u}_{t}-u_{t}\big)\,dt.

Taking here upper and lower limits as
T\to\infty
, we get that (
5
) and (
6
) are necessary conditions for the optimality.
\Box

Remark 1

Note that it may happen that the limit of
p_{t}^{T}
as
T\to\infty
is not equal to
\hat{p}_{t}^{\infty}
and even does not exist.

4
Examples

Example 1.

Let
{\bf x}={\bf x}^{\bf u}
be given by the SDE

dx_{t}=u_{t}\,dt+\sigma_{t}dW_{t},\qquad x_{0}=0,

where phase space of controls is
U:=[-1,1]
and the process
\sigma
is bounded. Let

J_{T}(\boldsymbol{u})={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}f_{t}\,x_{t}\,dt,

where
f\geq 0
is a predictable process in
L^{22}
.

The Hamilton–Pontryagin function is
\mathcal{H}(t,u,x,p)=p\,u+f_{t}x+h\sigma_{t}

and
\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,u,x,p)=f_{t}
. The BSDE (
4
) is reduced to the form

dp^{T}_{t}=-f_{t}dt+h^{T}_{t}dW_{t},\qquad p^{T}_{T}=0,

and admits an “explicit” expression, modulo the martingale representation theorem. Indeed, according to the latter, there is a predictable process
{\bf h}^{T}\in L^{22}
, uniquely determined, such that

\int_{0}^{T}f_{s}\,ds={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}f_{s}\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}h^{T}_{s}\,dW_{s}.

It remains to put

p^{T}_{t}:={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}f_{s}\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}f_{s}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}h^{T}_{s}\,dW_{s}.

Note that

p_{t}^{T}={\bf E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}f_{s}\,ds\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\geq 0.

It follows that the control
\hat{\bf u}\equiv 1
is overtaking optimal
because the relation

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{T\to\infty}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}^{T}\left(1-u_{t}\right)dt\geq 0

holds for any admissible
\boldsymbol{u}
. By Theorem
2.3
this condition is necessary and sufficient for the overtaking optimality.

Example 2.

Let the controlled process
{\bf k}={\bf k}^{\bf u}
be given by the SDE

dk_{t}=\left(u_{t}-\delta\,k_{t}\right)dt+\sigma dW_{t},\qquad k_{0}>0,

U:=[\underline{u},\bar{u}]
,
\delta>0
. Let

J_{T}({\bf u})={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rt}\left(\pi_{t}\,k_{t}-u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{t}\right)dt,

where
{\pi}\geq 0
is a bounded predictable process,
r>0
.

In the context of corporate finance,
{k_{t}}
is a capital with marginal productivity
\pi_{t}
and
{u_{t}}
is the intensity of investments at time
t
. Without investments the capital is decreasing with the rate
\delta
. The goal of the investor is to maximize in a long run expected discounted profits. The quadratic term in the functional is interpreted as the losses for an adaptation of investments.

Puting
x_{t}=e^{\delta\,t}k_{t}
, we solve the SDE and transform the problem to the one with dynamics depending only on the control:

dx_{t}=e^{\delta\,t}\,u_{t}\,dt+\sigma e^{\delta t}dW_{t}\qquad x_{0}=k_{0}>0,

and the functional

J_{T}({\bf u})={\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-r\,t}\left(e^{-\delta\,t}\,\pi_{t}\,x_{t}-u_{t}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{t}\right)dt.

The Hamilton–Pontryagin function has the form

\mathcal{H}(t,u,x,p)=e^{\delta\,t}p\,u+e^{\delta\,t}\sigma h+e^{-r\,t}\left(e^{-\delta\,t}\,\pi_{t}\,x-u-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right),

\mathcal{H}_{x}(t,u,x,p)=e^{-(r+\delta)t}\pi_{t}
and

p_{t}^{T}:={\bf E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\left(r+\delta\right)s}\pi_{s}\,ds\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

Define the bounded continuous process
{\bf q}
with

q_{t}:=e^{(r+\delta)t}{\bf E}\left[\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-(r+\delta)s}\,\pi_{s}\,ds\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

(in economics,
q_{t}
is called “marginal Tobin’s
q
”).

Let us check that the control
\hat{\bf u}:={\bf q}-1
is overtaking optimal.

For
T\geq t
we have
using the tower property of expectations that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(t,\hat{u}_{t},\hat{x}_{t},p_{t}^{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\delta\,t}\hat{p}_{t}^{T}-e^{-r\,t}-e^{-r\,t}\hat{u}_{t}=e^{\delta\,t}\big(p_{t}^{T}-e^{-(r+\delta)\,t}q_{t}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{\delta\,t}\,{\bf E}\big[e^{-\left(r+\delta\right)T}\,q_{T}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\big]=-e^{-r\,T}\,e^{-\delta\left(T-t\right)}\,{\bf E}\big[q_{T}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\big].

Taking into account the boundedness of the control, the condition (
6
) holds as the equality:

\liminf_{T\to\infty}e^{-r\,T}\,{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\delta\left(T-t\right)}\,{\bf E}\!\left[\left.q_{T}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\left(u_{t}-\hat{u}_{t}\right)\,dt=0.

In virtue of Theorem
2.2
the control
\hat{\bf u}:={\bf q}-1
is overtaking optimal.

5
Conclusions

We extended a sufficient condition of optimality of the deterministic problem with infinite-time horizon to stochastic optimal control problems. We call it Seierstad condition in order to distinguish it from Mangasaryan and Arrow sufficient conditions which have already their stochastic analouges in the literature, see e.g.
OksendalSulem
. Seierstad condition become both sufficient and necessary, when Hamilton–Pontryagin function is lenear with respect to control and state variables.

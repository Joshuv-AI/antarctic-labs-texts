Vertex-Based Localization of Turán’s Theorem
Rajat Adak, L. Sunil Chandran

Abstract
Let G G be a simple graph with n n vertices and m m edges. According to Turán’s theorem, if G G is K r + 1 K_{r+1} -free, then m ≤ | E ⁡ ( T ⁡ ( n , r ) ) | , m\leq|E(T(n,r))|, where T ⁡ ( n , r ) T(n,r) denotes the Turán graph on n n vertices with a maximum clique of order r r . A limitation of this statement is that it does not give an expression in terms of n n and r r . A widely used version of Turán’s theorem states that for an n n -vertex K r + 1 K_{r+1} -free graph, m ≤ ⌊ n 2 ​ ( r − 1 ) 2 ​ r ⌋ . m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}\right\rfloor. Though this bound is often more convenient, it is not the same as the original statement. In particular, the class of extremal graphs for this bound, say 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , is a proper subset of the set of Turán graphs. In this paper, we generalize this result as follows: For each v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , let c ⁡ ( v ) c(v) be the order of the largest clique that contains v v . We show that m ≤ ⌊ n 2 ​ ∑ v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) c ⁡ ( v ) − 1 c ⁡ ( v ) ⌋ m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{c(v)-1}{c(v)}\right\rfloor Furthermore, we characterize the class of extremal graphs that attain equality in this bound. Interestingly, this class contains two extra non-Turán graphs other than the graphs in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} .

Introduction

Extremal graph theory studies the maximum or minimum values that certain graph parameters (typically the number of edges or vertices) can achieve subject to given constraints, and the corresponding extremal structures. Turán’s Theorem is one of the most prominent results in this field.

Notation
.

Let
T(n,r)
denote the Turán Graph on
n
vertices with a maximum clique of order
r
. It is a complete multipartite graph of order
n
with
r
classes of size either
\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
or
\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor
in the partition.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Turán
[
7
]
)

For a simple
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
with
n
vertices,

|E(G)|\leq|E(T(n,r))|

and equality holds if and only if
G\cong T(n,r)
.

Observe that the bound stated in
Theorem

1.1
is not explicitly formulated as a function of

n
and
r
. Consequently, a commonly used quantitative version of this bound is given as:

Remark 1.2
.

For a simple
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
with
n
-vertices,

|E(G)|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}\right\rfloor

The bound presented in
Remark

1.2
is not equivalent to the original formulation in
Theorem

1.1
. In particular, the class of extremal graphs

\mathcal{S}
corresponding to
Remark

1.2
forms a proper subset of the set of all Turán graphs.

\bullet
Characterizing the set
\mathcal{S}

Suppose that
G\in\mathcal{S}
and
G\cong T(n,r)
where
n=tr+s
, such that
0\leq s<r
. Thus, there are
s
classes of size
(t+1)
and
(r-s)
classes of size
t
in
G
. Therefore, we get:

|E(G)|={n\choose 2}-s{t+1\choose 2}-(r-s){t\choose 2}=\frac{r-1}{2r}(n^{2}-s^{2})+{s\choose 2}

(1)

Since
G\in\mathcal{S}
, we also have;

|E(G)|=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}\right\rfloor\implies\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}-|E(G)|<1

(2)

Plugging in the value of
|E(G)|
from
eq.

1
into
Claim

2.3
we get;

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}(r-1)}{2r}-{s\choose 2}<1\implies\frac{s}{2r}(s(r-1)-r(s-1))<1

\displaystyle\implies\frac{s(r-s)}{2r}<1\implies s(r-s)<2r\implies r(s-2)<s^{2}

Note that, if
s\leq 2
then
r(s-2)<s^{2}
for any
r
. If
s>2
we need
r<\frac{s^{2}}{s-2}
for the condition to be true. Thus we can define;

\mathcal{S}=\{T(n,r)\mid\text{either }s\leq 2\text{ or }r<\frac{s^{2}}{s-2};\text{ where }n\equiv s\ (\text{mod }r)\}

(3)

Theorem 1.3
.

For a simple
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
on
n
-vertices,

|E(G)|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}\right\rfloor

equality holds if and only if
G\cong T(n,r)
and
G\in\mathcal{S}
.

A
relaxed
form of
Theorem

1.3
is obtained by omitting the floor function from the right-hand side of the bound.

Theorem 1.4
.

For a simple
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
on
n
vertices,

|E(G)|\leq\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}.

equality holds if and only if
G\cong T(n,r)
and
r\mid n
; that is,
G
is a regular Turán graph with
r
equal-sized classes.

This simplification is often adopted by authors for convenience (even if it is a weaker version) because the characterization of the extremal graphs becomes very simple in the absence of the floor function. However, it is important to note that, since
|E(G)|
must be an integer, the tightest possible bound in terms of
n
and
r
is provided by
Theorem

1.3
. This is further underscored by the fact that the extremal graph class associated with
Theorem

1.3
strictly contains that of
Theorem

1.4
.
This is because, if
G
is an extremal graph for
Theorem

1.4
, then
n\equiv 0\ (\text{mod }r)
, that is,
s=0
, thus
G\in\mathcal{S}
.

1.1
Localization

Bradač
[
3
]
gave a generalization of
Theorem

1.4
by associating weights to the edges of the graph. Subsequently, Malec and Tompkins
[
6
]
provided an alternative proof for this generalized
version. In their work, the edge weights were defined as follows:

k(e)=max\{r\mid e\ \text{occurs in a subgraph of $G$ isomorphic to}\ K_{r}\}

Theorem 1.5
.

(Bradač
[
3
]
and Malec-Tompkins
[
6
]
)
For a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices,

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{k(e)}{k(e)-1}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}

and equality holds if and only if
G
is regular Turán graph.

Remark 1.6
.

If
G
is
K_{r+1}
free,
k(e)\leq r
for all
e\in E(G)
. Replacing
k(e)
by
r
in
Theorem

1.5
yields the bound of
Theorem

1.4
since
\frac{r}{r-1}\leq\frac{k(e)}{k(e)-1}
for all
e\in E(G)
.

Malec and Tompkins
[
6
]
coined the term
localization
to describe this type of generalization based on such weight assignments, as the weight of each edge depends solely on the local structures in which it is involved. Kirsch and Nir
[
5
]
extended
Theorem

1.5
by assigning weights to cliques of any size, rather than just edges. Their result simultaneously extends a different extension of
Theorem

1.4
due to Zykov
[
8
]
. Aragão and Souza
[
2
]
further extended this by providing localization for the Graph Maclaurin Inequalities
[
4
]
.

1.2
Vertex-Based Localization

In Theorem
1.5
, localization is achieved by assigning weights to the edges of the graph. In contrast, we adopt the framework of
vertex-based localization
, introduced in
[
1
]
, wherein weights are assigned to the vertices rather than the edges. In this work, we present a vertex-based localization of
Theorem

1.3
. Additionally, for the benefit of the interested reader, we include a concise proof of a vertex-based localization of
Theorem

1.4
also. Although the bound in this weaker result follows as a consequence of the localized version of
Theorem

1.3
, the extremal class in this case admits a simpler structural characterization. This allows for a direct comparison with
Theorem

1.5
, concentrating only on the weaker formulation.

Notation
.

For a graph

c(v)=max\{r\mid v\ \text{occurs in a subgraph of $G$ isomorphic to}\ K_{r}\}

Theorem 2.1
.

For a simple graph
G
with

2.1
Recovering
Theorem

1.3

To recover the bound and the extremal graph for

from

we assume

to be a
K_{r+1}
-free graph on

c(v)\leq r

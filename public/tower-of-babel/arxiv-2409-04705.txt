Primes in Tuples of Linear Forms in Number Fields and Function Fields Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 11N05, 11N36, 11T06 Thanks: Key words and phrases. Distribution of primes, sieve methods, number fields
Habibur Rahaman

Abstract
. Following the work of Castillo-Hall-Oliver-Pollack-Thompson who extended Maynard-Tao theorem on admissible tuples to number fields and function fields for tuples with monic linear forms, here we obtain the Maynard-Tao theorem for admissible tuples of linear forms with arbitrary leading coefficients in number fields and function fields. Also, we provide some applications of our results.



1.
Introduction

A set
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
of distinct integers is called admissible if for any prime
p\in\mathbb{P}
, the set
\mathcal{H}
misses at least one residue class
\bmod\ p
, i.e. if
|\mathcal{H}(\bmod\ p)|<p
for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Then we have the following conjecture due to Hardy-Littlewood.

Conjecture

(Prime k-tuples conjecture)
.

For an admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
of distinct integers, there are infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that each of
n+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
is a prime.

In particular, the above conjecture implies the twin prime conjecture. Though this conjecture still remains unsolved, recently there has been spectacular progress in this direction. The prime number theorem implies that the average gap between consecutive primes
p_{n}
and
p_{n+1}
is
O(\log p_{n})
, where
p_{n}
denotes the n-th prime. In
[
5
]
, Goldston, Pintz and Yildirim proved the following

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}-p_{n}}{\log p_{n}}=0,

i.e., the gaps between consecutive primes can be arbitrarily small in comparison with the average prime gap. Zhang
[
15
]
extend this result and proved that there are infinitely many consecutive primes with a bounded gap. More precisely, he proved

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}({p_{n+1}-p_{n}})\leq 70000000.

Maynard
[
10
]
, Tao and the Polymath project
[
12
]
, managed to reduce the above bound by
246
. Moreover, the method of Maynard and Tao shows that for every
m
, there exist infinitely many tuples of bounded length of
m
consecutive primes. More precisely, they proved:

Theorem A

(Maynard-Tao)
.

Let
m\geq 2
. Then there exists a positive integer
k_{0}=k_{0}(m)
such that for any admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
with
k\geq k_{0}
, there are infinitely many
n
such that at least
m
of
n+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

In
2015
, Castillo, Hall, Lemke Oliver, Pollack and Thompson (CHLOPT)
[
2
]
extended the Maynard-Tao theorem to number fields and function fields in the following sense: Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We say, the set
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
of distinct elements in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is admissible if,
|\mathcal{H}(\bmod\ \mathfrak{p})|<|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}|
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Castillo et al. proved the following theorems.

Theorem B

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 2
be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive integer
k_{0}=k_{0}(m,K)
such that, for any admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
k\geq k_{0}
, there are infinitely many
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that at least
m
of
\alpha+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

For the function field setting, we replace
K
by
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
. Then the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is replaced by
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
where the primes are the monic irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
.
With this notion, we say
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
of distinct elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is admissible if, for each monic irreducible polynomial
P(t)
, the set
\mathcal{H}(\bmod\ P(t))
is not equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]/P(t)
.

Theorem C

(Theorem 1.3 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 2
be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive integer
k_{0}=k_{0}(m)
such that, for any admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with
k\geq k_{0}
, there are infinitely many
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
such that at least
m
of
f+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

1.1.
Main Results

In this paper, we prove the above results for admissible sets of linear forms with some applications analogous to the results of Banks, Freiberg, and Turnage-Butterbaugh
[
1
]
. In the number field case, let
\mathcal{H}=\{a_{1}\alpha+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\alpha+h_{k}\}
, where
\alpha,a_{i},h_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
a_{i},h_{i}
are coprime for
1\leq i\leq k
. Let
L_{i}(\alpha)=a_{i}\alpha+h_{i}
and

\displaystyle L(\alpha):=\prod_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}(\alpha).

We say
\mathcal{H}
is admissible if
L(\alpha)
has no fixed prime ideal divisor, that is, if for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, there exists some
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
\mathfrak{p}\nmid L(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Here we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive integer
k_{0}=k_{0}(m,K)
such that, for any admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{a_{1}\alpha+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\alpha+h_{k}\}
of linear forms in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
k\geq k_{0}
, there are infinitely many
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that at least
m
of
a_{i}\alpha+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

In particular, for
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}
with
a_{i},h_{i}
coprime for
1\leq i\leq k
, we have the following corollary (see
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
).

Corollary 1.1

(Theorem 6.4 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 2
. Then there exists a positive integer
k_{0}=k_{0}(m)
such that, for any admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{a_{1}n+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}n+h_{k}\}
with
k\geq k_{0}
, there are infinitely many
n
such that at least
m
of
a_{i}n+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

From the last result, we also have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
b
and
h\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
are coprime. Also let
\mathfrak{h}
denote the ideal generated by
h
with norm
|\mathfrak{h}|\geq 3
. Then for any positive integer
m\geq 2
, there are infinitely many
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{r1}\equiv\gamma_{r2}\equiv\cdots\equiv\gamma_{rm}\equiv b(\bmod\ \mathfrak{h}),

where
\gamma_{rj}
denote primes in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

In case of a function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, we define an admissible set similarly, letting
f,a_{i},h_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with
a_{i}
monic for
1\leq i\leq k
and by definition, we consider irreducible polynomials
P(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
as primes. We then have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
m\geq 2
be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive integer
k_{0}=k_{0}(m)
such that, for any admissible set
\mathcal{H}=\{a_{1}f+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}f+h_{k}\}
of linear forms in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with
k\geq k_{0}
, there are infinitely many
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
such that at least
m
of
a_{i}f+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
b
and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
are coprime and
g
be monic. Then for any positive integer
m\geq 2
, there are infinitely many
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle g_{r1}\equiv g_{r2}\equiv\cdots\equiv g_{rm}\equiv b(\bmod g),

where
g_{rj}
are monic irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
.

Two essential ingredients in the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are Proposition
1
and Proposition
2
. The proofs of these propositions rely on Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, wherein lie the main ideas which we incorporate in this paper in order to use Maynard’s technique and also CHLOPT’s technique in the setting of linear forms.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, in a similar manner as in
[
2
]
, we set up a general method which will help us to prove both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

simultaneously.

2.1.
Notations and Definitions

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain, which will be
\mathcal{O}_{K}
for a number field
K
and for a function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, we will take
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
.

For
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and for any natural number
N
, let
A(N)
be the collection of monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with norm
N
. For
A=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, let
A_{0}(N)
be the set of
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that for each real embedding
\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C},0<\sigma(\alpha)\leq N
and
|\sigma(\alpha)|\leq N
for each complex embedding. Define
A(N):=A_{0}(2N)\setminus A_{0}(N)
.

Remark 2.1
.

For
K=\mathbb{Q}
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}
and
\sigma
is only the identity map. Therefore
A_{0}(2N)=[1,2N]
and
A_{0}(N)=[1,N]
. So,
A(N)=(N,2N]
, which gives the Maynard-Tao case.

For any nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq A
, we define the norm of
\mathfrak{q}
by
|\mathfrak{q}|:=|A/\mathfrak{q}|
and the Euler Phi function
\phi(\mathfrak{q}):=|(A/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}|
, which counts the cardinality of the multiplicative group
(A/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}
and the Mőbius function
\mu
by

\displaystyle\mu(\mathfrak{p}):=\begin{cases}(-1)^{r}&\text{ if $\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}.\ldots.\mathfrak{p}_{r}$, where $\mathfrak{p}_{i}$ are distinct prime ideals in $A$,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise;}\end{cases}

which can be defined since
A
is a Dedekind domain.
Also, for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the zeta function of
A
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\zeta_{A}(s):=\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\subseteq A}\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{q}|^{s}},

where
|\mathfrak{q}|
denotes the norm of the ideal
\mathfrak{q}
.
For
A=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, this is the usual Dedekind zeta function of
K
and for
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, as there are exactly
q^{d}
monic polynomials of degree
d
in
A
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{A}(s)=\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}},

(see page 11 in
[
14
]
).
Let
A(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})
be the set of
\alpha\in A(N)
with
\alpha\equiv\alpha_{0}(\bmod\ \mathfrak{q})
. Then we note that

\displaystyle|A(N,\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})|=\frac{|A(N)|}{|\mathfrak{q}|}+O(|\partial A(N,\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})|),

where
|A(N)|
denotes the cardinality of the set
A(N)
(see page 2844 in
[
2
]
) and

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\partial A(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})|\ll\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $A=\mathbb{Z}$ or $A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]$,}\\
1+(\frac{|A(N)|}{|\mathfrak{q}|})^{1-\frac{1}{d}}&\text{ if $A=\mathcal{O}_{K}$ with $[K,\mathbb{Q}]=d$. }\end{cases}

Let
P(N)
be the set of primes in
A(N)
, that is
P(N)=P\cap A(N)
, where
P
is the set of primes in
A
. Then we have the Prime Number Theorem for
P(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})
, where
\alpha_{0}
and
\mathfrak{q}
are coprime, as

(2.3)

\displaystyle|P(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})|=\frac{|P(N)|}{\phi(\mathfrak{q})}+\mathcal{E}(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0}),

where the error term
\mathcal{E}(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})
can be bounded on average by the well known Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem and it’s generalization to other fields. To see that, we say the set of primes
P
in
A
has level of distribution
\theta>0
if, for any
B>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\mathfrak{q}|\leq|A(N)|^{\theta}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{0}(\bmod\mathfrak{q})\\
(\alpha_{0},\mathfrak{q})=1\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{E}(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})|\ll_{B}\frac{|A(N)|}{(\log N)^{B}}.

In particular, if we take
A=\mathbb{Z}
, the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem says that the primes has level of distribution
\theta
for any
\theta<1/2
.
For a general number field
K
, we have the following result due to Hinz
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Hinz)
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field with signature
(r_{1},r_{2})
. If
K
is totally real, i.e, if
r_{2}=0
, then the set of primes
P
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
has level of distribution
\theta
for any
\theta<1/2
. In general
\theta<\frac{1}{r_{2}+\frac{5}{2}}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Hayes,
[
7
]
)
.

If
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, then the set of primes
P
has level of distribution
\theta
for any
\theta<1/2
. Also, we have

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{0}(\bmod\mathfrak{q})\\
(\alpha_{0},\mathfrak{q})=1\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{E}(N;\mathfrak{q},\alpha_{0})|\ll(\log 2|\mathfrak{q}|)|A(N)|^{1/2}.

2.2.
Sieve Setting

Let us choose a positive integer
N
and
D_{0}=\log\log\log N
. Also, let
\mathfrak{w}:=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mathfrak{p}|<D_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{p}
. Clearly we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{w}|\ll(\log\log N)^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{a_{1}\alpha+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\alpha+h_{k}\}
be an admissible set in
A
. So for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
A
, there is
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\in A
such that
\mathfrak{p}\nmid L(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Solving
\alpha\equiv\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}(\bmod\ \mathfrak{p})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{w}
, by Chinese Remainder Theorem there exists some
v_{0}(\bmod\ \mathfrak{w})
such that each
a_{i}v_{0}+h_{i}
lies in
(A/\mathfrak{w})^{\times}
.

Let
\theta
be the level of distribution of primes in
A
and
R=\left\lvert A(N)\right\rvert^{\theta/2-\delta}
for any fixed small
\delta>0
. Let
\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}
be suitably chosen weights supported on the tuples
(\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k})
of ideals in
A
with
\mathfrak{d}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{d}_{i}
squarefree,
|\mathfrak{d}|<R
and coprime to
\mathfrak{w}
. Next we define two sums, the evaluation of which are crucial for our main results.

Let

(2.5)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102ptS_{1}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in A(N)\\
\alpha\equiv v_{0}(\bmod\mathfrak{w})\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}\\
a_{i}\alpha+h_{i}\equiv 0(\bmod\mathfrak{d}_{i})\forall i\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}\right)^{2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0ptS_{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in A(N)\\
\alpha\equiv v_{0}(\bmod\mathfrak{w})\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{P}(a_{i}\alpha+h_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}\\
a_{i}\alpha+h_{i}\equiv 0(\bmod\mathfrak{d}_{i})\forall i\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}\right)^{2},

where
\chi_{P}(\cdot)
denotes the characteristic function on the set of primes
P
in
A
.

Remark 2.2
.

If we can show that
S_{2}-\rho S_{1}>0
for some
\rho>0
, then there exists at least one
\alpha\in A(N)
such that at least
\lfloor\rho+1\rfloor
of
a_{1}\alpha+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\alpha+h_{k},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

For evaluating the above sums, we use the following propositions which we prove in the last section.

Proposition 1
.

Let the primes
P
in
A
have level of distribution
\theta>0
, and
R=|A(N)|^{\theta/2-\delta}
for some fixed
\delta>0
. Let
F:[0,1]^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
be a piecewise differentiable function supported on
\mathcal{R}_{k}:=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in[0,1]^{k}:\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\leq 1\}
, and let the sieving weights
\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}
be defined in terms of
F
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu(\mathfrak{d}_{i})\left\lvert\mathfrak{d}_{i}\right\rvert\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{r}_{k}\\
\mathfrak{d}_{i}|\mathfrak{r}_{i}\forall i\\
(\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{w})=1\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{r}_{i}\right)^{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\phi(\mathfrak{r}_{i})}F\left(\frac{\log\left\lvert\mathfrak{r}_{1}\right\rvert}{\log R},\ldots,\frac{\log\left\lvert\mathfrak{r}_{k}\right\rvert}{\log R}\right),

whenever
\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{d}_{i},\mathfrak{w}\right)=1
and
\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}=0
otherwise. Then we have

\displaystyle S_{1}=\frac{(1+o(1))\phi(\mathfrak{w})^{k}\left\lvert A(N)\right\rvert(c_{A}\log R)^{k}}{\left\lvert\mathfrak{w}\right\rvert^{k+1}}I_{k}(F),

\displaystyle S_{2}=\frac{(1+o(1))\phi(\mathfrak{w})^{k}\left\lvert P(N)\right\rvert(c_{A}\log R)^{k+1}}{\left\lvert\mathfrak{w}\right\rvert^{k+1}}\sum_{m=1}^{k}J^{(m)}_{k}(F),

where
c_{A}
is the residue of
\zeta_{A}(s)
at
s=1
, and
I_{k}(F)
and
J_{k}(F)
are the following integrals

(2.7)

\displaystyle I_{k}(F)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\ldots\int_{0}^{1}F(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})^{2}dt_{1}\ldots dt_{k}

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle J^{(m)}_{k}(F)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\ldots\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}F(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})dt_{m}\right)^{2}dt_{1}\ldots dt_{m-1}dt_{m+1}\ldots dt_{k}.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{a_{1}\alpha+h_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\alpha+h_{k}\}
be admissible set in
A
. Also let
\mathcal{R}_{k},I_{k}(F)
and
J^{(m)}_{k}(F)
be as in Proposition 1 and
\mathcal{S}_{k}
be set of all piecewise differentiable functions
F:[0,1]^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
supported on
\mathcal{R}_{k}
with
I_{k}(F)\neq 0
and
J^{(m)}_{k}(F)\neq 0
for each
m,1\leq m\leq k
.
Let

\displaystyle M_{k}=\sup_{F\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}J^{(m)}_{k}(F)}{I_{k}(F)},\ \ \ \ r_{k}=\left\lceil\frac{\theta M_{k}}{2}\right\rceil.

Then there are infinitely many integers
\alpha\in A
such that at least
r_{k}
of the
a_{i}\alpha+h_{i},1\leq i\leq k
are prime.

For sufficiently large
k
, we have a lower bound of the above
M_{k}
due to Maynard
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Proposition 4.3 in
[
10
]
)
.

For sufficiently large
k
, we have

\displaystyle M_{k}>\log k-2\log\log k-2.

Also, we note the following lemmas as required to prove the above Propositions.

Lemma 2.2

(Theorem 2.2 in
[
14
]
)
.

Let
a(n)
denote the number of monic irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
of degree
n
. Then

\displaystyle a(n)=\frac{q^{n}}{n}+O\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right).

Lemma 2.3

(Mitsui’s Generalized Prime Number Theorem,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
A_{0}(N)
be defined as in the beginning of this section for a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
with degree
d=r_{1}+2r_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert P_{0}(N)\right\rvert=m_{K}\underbrace{\int_{2}^{N}\ldots\int_{2}^{N}}_{r_{1}\text{ times }}\underbrace{\int_{2}^{N^{2}}\ldots\int_{2}^{N^{2}}}_{r_{2}\text{ times }}\frac{du_{1}\ldots du_{r_{1}+r_{2}}}{\log(u_{1}\ldots u_{r_{1}+r_{2}})}+O\left(N^{d}e^{-c\sqrt{\log(N^{d})}}\right),

for some positive real number
c
and
m_{K}:=\frac{w_{K}}{2^{r_{1}}h_{K}R_{K}}
, where
P_{0}(N)
is the set of primes in
A_{0}(N)
,
w_{K}
is the number of roots of unity contained in
K
,
h_{K}
is the class number of
K
and
R_{K}
is the regulator of
K
.

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 6 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{r}
be real numbers satisfying

\displaystyle 2\leq y_{i}\leq y_{j}^{u},\ \ \ i,j=1,2,\ldots,r,

for a fixed positive real number
u
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{y_{1}}\int_{2}^{y_{2}}\ldots\int_{2}^{y_{r}}\frac{dx_{1}dx_{2}\ldots dx_{r}}{\log(x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{r})}=\frac{y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{r}}{\log(y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{r})}+O_{u}\left(\frac{y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{r}}{\log^{2}(y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{r})}\right).

3.
Preparation for the main results

In this section, we prove the above propositions. Lets begin with the following lemma, which we get by diagonalizing the quadratic form in
S_{1}
(see (
2.5
)).

Lemma 3.1
.

For ideals
\mathfrak{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{r}_{k}
, we set

(3.1)

\displaystyle y_{\mathfrak{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{r}_{k}}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mu(\mathfrak{r}_{i})\phi(\mathfrak{r}_{i})\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}\\
\mathfrak{r}_{i}\mid\mathfrak{d}_{i},\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{d}_{k}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left\lvert\mathfrak{d}_{i}\right\rvert},

and
y_{\max}=\displaystyle\sup_{\mathfrak{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{r}_{k}}|y_{\mathfrak{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{r}_{k}}|

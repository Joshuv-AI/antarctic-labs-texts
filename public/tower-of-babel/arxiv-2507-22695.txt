Maximal average over surfaces of codimension 2 in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}
Seheon Ham, Hyerim Ko

Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain sharp L p L^{p} improving estimates for maximal averages over nondegenerate surfaces of codimension 2 2 in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} . We also establish local smoothing type estimates for the averages, which are accomplished by making use of multilinear restriction estimates and decoupling inequalities for two dimensional conic extension of two dimensional nondegenerate surfaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 2
. For
1\leq k\leq d-1
, let
S
be a smooth compact
k
-dimensional submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We consider the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
associated with dilations of
S
which is given by

\mathcal{M}f(x)=\sup_{t\in I}\Big|\int_{S}f(x-ty)d\sigma(y)\Big|,

where
I
is an interval and
d\sigma
is the surface measure on
S
.
We study the mapping properties of the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
under suitable curvature conditions on
S
.

For
k=d-1
, the most fundamental example of
S
is the sphere. In this case,
Stein
[
44
]
(
n\geq 3
) and Bourgain
[
6
]
(
d=2
) proved that
\mathcal{M}
with
I=(0,\infty)
is bounded on
L^{p}

if and only if
\frac{d}{d-1}<p\leq\infty
.
When
I
is a compact interval, the localized maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
has the
L^{p}
improving property, that is,
\mathcal{M}
maps
L^{p}
boundedly to
L^{q}
for some
q>p
.
Except for a few endpoint cases,
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimates for
\mathcal{M}
were completely characterized in
[
41
,
42
,
34
]
. Recently, there have been several works on generalization of the spherical maximal function, including studies on the elliptic maximal function and the strong spherical maximal function (e.g. see
[
32
,
33
,
14
,
26
]
and references therein).

In connection with geometric measure theory, different forms of the spherical maximal function have been developed by
[
46
,
30
,
48
]
.
See also
[
39
,
49
]
for further results on
Wolff’s maximal average associated with families of curves in the plane.
We refer to
[
23
,
12
]
for circular maximal estimates and local smoothing estimates for the wave operator relative to fractal measures.

For
k=1
, when
S
is a smooth curve with nonvanishing torsion, Ko–Lee–Oh
[
29
]
and Beltran–Guo–Hickman–Seeger
[
3
]
established that

\mathcal{M}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
if and only if
p>3
. (See also
[
38
,
8
]
for some previous results.)
In higher dimensions
d\geq 4
, Ko–Lee–Oh
[
28
]
first showed
L^{p}
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
for
p>2(d-1)
. This result was recently improved by Gan-Maldague-Oh
[
18
]
for
p>2(d-2)
where
p>4
is sharp in
d=4
.
The sharp
L^{p}
improving property of
\mathcal{M}
for nondegenerate curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was recently settled by Beltran–Duncan–Hickman
[
2
]
.

For the intermediate cases i.e.
1<k<d-1
, Rubio de Francia
[
40
]
established
L^{p}
boundedness for
p>\frac{2a+1}{2a}
assuming the decay condition
|\widehat{d\sigma}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{-a}
with
a>1/2
.
However, sharp characterization of the
L^{p}
improving property for
\mathcal{M}
remains to be open.

In this note, we investigate the sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimates for the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
associated with two-dimensional nondegenerate surfaces
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
We further extend these results to higher even dimensions, providing sharp estimates for surfaces of half the ambient dimension beyond the previously known cases of
k=1
or
k=d-1
.

We consider a smooth compact surface
\Gamma
given by

\Gamma(u,v)=(u,v,\phi_{1}(u,v),\phi_{2}(u,v)):[-1,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{4},

where
\phi_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are smooth real valued function with
\phi_{i}(0,0)=\nabla\phi_{i}(0,0)=0
.
We say that
\Gamma
is
nondegenerate
if

(1.1)

\rank\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}&\partial_{uv}^{2}\phi_{1}&\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{1}\\
\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}&\partial_{uv}^{2}\phi_{2}&\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=2

holds on the domain
[-1,1]^{2}
.

Two fundamental examples are
\Gamma_{\circ}(u,v)=(u,v,u^{2}-v^{2},2uv)
and
\Gamma_{1}(u,v)=(u,v,u^{2},v^{2})
.
The key difference between
\Gamma_{\circ}
and
\Gamma_{1}
is the rank of Hessian matrices
\nabla_{u,v}^{2}\phi_{i}
,
i=1,2
.
In the case of
\Gamma_{\circ}
, the Hessian matrices are of full rank, which can be generally characterized by the condition

(1.2)

\displaystyle\det\big(\theta_{1}\nabla_{u,v}^{2}\phi_{1}(u,v)+\theta_{2}\nabla_{u,v}^{2}\phi_{2}(u,v)\big)\neq 0\quad\text{for all}~\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

(See
[
16
,
17
,
36
,
1
]
.)
This condition allows us to apply the method of stationary phase, which in turn enables us to express the averaging operator as a sum of associated Fourier extension operators.

On the other hand, for
\Gamma_{1}
, there exists a direction along which (
1.2
) fails.
Because of this degeneracy, the implicit function theorem cannot be applied directly, and
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
boundedness can depend on the degree of the degeneracy.
In Theorem
1.4
, we consider a class of surfaces that generalizes
\Gamma_{1}
.

Now we define the averaging operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x,t)=\chi_{I}(t)\iint f(x-t\,\Gamma(u,v))\varphi(u,v)dudv,

where

\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}
is a smooth function supported on
[-1,1]^{2}
and
I=[1,2]
.
Our first main result is a sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
local smoothing estimate for
\mathcal{A}
associated with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\circ}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(u,v,u^{2}-v^{2},2uv)
.
If
\frac{1}{q}\leq\min\{\frac{1}{p},\,\frac{1}{3p}+\frac{1}{6},\,\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})\}
,
then

(1.3)

\|\mathcal{A}f\|_{L^{q}_{x,t}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

holds for
\gamma>\frac{2}{p}-\frac{5}{q}
.

The local smoothing phenomenon for the wave propagator was first observed by Sogge
[
43
]
, and has been extensively studied (e.g. see
[
47
,
31
,
8
]
and references therein).
In
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}
, the local smoothing conjecture was proved by Guth–Wang–Zhang
[
22
]
. For recent progress in higher dimensions, see
[
4
,
20
,
19
,
15
]
.

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, the optimal exponent
\gamma
is given by

\gamma\geq\gamma(p,q):=\max\Big\{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{5}{q},~-1-\frac{3}{q}+\frac{3}{p},~-2-\frac{2}{q}+\frac{4}{p}\Big\},

which will be discussed in Section
7
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we use an induction on scale argument (see
[
11
,
25
]
, for example), combined with multilinear restriction estimates and a decoupling inequality for the Fourier extension operator associated with certain hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
.
These hypersurfaces are of homogeneous of degree one and have at least two nonzero principal curvatures. In addition, their normal vector takes the form
(\Gamma(u,v),-1)
.

Note that Theorem
1.1
gives a sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimate for the (local) maximal average
\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f(x,t)|
when
p\leq q
.
Indeed, let
P_{\lambda}
be the standard Littlewood-Paley projection operator where the frequency support is localized in
|\xi|\sim\lambda
.
Theorem
1.1
implies that for
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{A}P_{\lambda}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I)}\leq C_{\epsilon}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{q}-\epsilon}\|f\|_{p}

holds for
(1/p,1/q)
satisfying
\gamma(p,q)<-\frac{1}{q}
.
By the Sobolev embedding, the maximal average
\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f(x,t)|
maps
L^{p}
boundedly to
L^{q}
when
\gamma(p,q)<-\frac{1}{q}
i.e.
(1/p,1/q)
is contained in

\mathcal{W}_{0}=\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\Big):\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}<\frac{2}{q},\quad\frac{3}{p}<1+\frac{2}{q},\quad\frac{4}{p}<2+\frac{1}{q}\Big\}.

While we have the sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimate for
\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f(x,t)|
with the model case
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\circ}
, it remains valid for all nondegenerate surfaces satisfying (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is nondegenerate and satisfies (
1.2
).
Then

(1.4)

\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f(\cdot,t)|\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{p}

holds if
(1/p,1/q)
is contained in

\mathcal{W}_{2}=\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\Big):\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}<\frac{2}{q},\quad\frac{3}{p}<1+\frac{2}{q},\quad\frac{4}{p}\leq 2+\frac{1}{q}\Big\}\setminus\Big\{\Big(\frac{3}{5},\frac{2}{5}\Big),\Big(\frac{2}{3},\frac{2}{3}\Big)\Big\}.

Note that
\mathcal{W}_{2}
represents a quadrangle with vertices
(0,0)
,
(2/3,2/3)
,
(3/5,2/5)
,
(1/2,1/4)
.
Also, unlike
\mathcal{W}_{0}
, the interior of the edge
4/p=2+1/q
is contained in
\mathcal{W}_{2}
.
In fact, we can show
\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f|
is of restricted weak type at
(3/5,2/5)
and
(2/3,2/3)
.
This result is sharp in the sense that (
1.4
) fails outside of closure of
\mathcal{W}_{2}
(see Section
7
).

Theorem
1.2
can be extended naturally to submanifolds of half the ambient dimension in all even dimensions.
We consider
n
-dimensional submanifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

parametrized by
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in[-1,1]^{n}\rightarrow\Phi(\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{u})=\big(\phi_{1}(\mathbf{u}),\dots,\phi_{n}(\mathbf{u})\big)

for smooth
\phi_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
k=1,\dots,n
.
Extending the two dimensional case in (
1.1
), we say that
\Phi
is nondegenerate if the
n\times n(n+1)/2
matrix of all second derivatives of
\phi_{i}
has full rank.

We define the averaging operator

\mathbf{A}f(x,t)=\chi_{I}(t)\int f(x-t(\mathbf{u},\Phi(\mathbf{u})))\varphi(\mathbf{u})\,d\mathbf{u}.

Here,
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}
is a smooth function supported on
[-1,1]^{n}
.

To describe our results, let
\mathcal{W}_{n}\subset[0,1]^{2}
,
n\geq 4
, be the closed convex hull with vertices
\mathrm{O}=(0,0)
,

\mathrm{P}_{1}=\Big(\frac{2n}{3n+2},\frac{n}{3n+2}\Big),\quad\mathrm{P}_{2}=\Big(\frac{2n-1}{3n-1},\frac{n}{3n-1}\Big),\quad\mathrm{P}_{3}=\Big(\frac{n}{n+1},\frac{n}{n+1}\Big).

Note that
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
satisfies

\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{2}{q},\quad\frac{3n}{2p}\leq\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3n-2}{2q},\quad\frac{2n}{p}\leq n+\frac{n-1}{q}.

We obtain a sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimate for the (local) maximal average
\sup_{t\in I}|\mathbf{A}f|
along with some endpoint restricted weak type estimates.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 4
be an even integer.
Suppose that
\Phi
is nondegenerate and satisfies

(1.5)

\displaystyle\det\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}^{2}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{u})\Big)\neq 0

for any
(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathbf{A}f(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}

holds for
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathcal{W}_{n}\setminus\{\mathrm{P}_{1},\mathrm{P}_{2},\mathrm{P}_{3}\}
, while the restricted weak type estimate

(1.7)

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathbf{A}f(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}

holds for
(1/p,1/q)=\mathrm{P}_{1},\mathrm{P}_{2},\mathrm{P}_{3}
.

The estimate (
1.6
) is sharp in the sense that (
1.6
) fails outside of
\mathcal{W}_{n}
(see Section
7
). However, it remains open whether the strong type estimates holds at
\mathrm{P}_{1},\mathrm{P}_{2},\mathrm{P}_{3}
.

For odd
n
, the condition (
1.5
) cannot be satisfied in general. Considering quadratic
\phi_{i}
’s, the left-hand side of (
1.5
) is a homogeneous polynomial of
\theta
of degree
n
, which must have a zero when
n
is odd.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we follow the approach introduced in
[
34
]
,
using Bourgain’s interpolation trick to obtain the restricted weak type (
1.7
) at the endpoints
\mathrm{P}_{1},\mathrm{P}_{2},\mathrm{P}_{3}
.
Then, (
1.6
) follows by interpolation
between
\mathrm{O},\mathrm{P}_{1},\mathrm{P}_{2},\mathrm{P}_{3}
.

We now turn to the case
\Gamma_{1}(u,v)=(u,v,u^{2},v^{2})
, where the condition (
1.2
) fails in a certain direction.
Under this vanishing condition, we focus on
the case where at least one of
\phi_{1}
or
\phi_{2}
depends only on a single variable.
In particular, we may assume that
\phi_{2}(u,v)=\phi_{2}(v)
.
Moreover, we assume that
\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}\neq 0
and
\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{2}\neq 0
, since
\Gamma
satisfies the nondegeneracy condition (
1.1
).

Let

\mathcal{W}_{1}=\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\Big):\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{2}{q},\quad\frac{3}{p}\leq 1+\frac{1}{q}\Big\},

which coincides with the region where the circular maximal function is bounded.
The following theorem is obtained
by repeated application of the local smoothing estimate for the wave operator established by Lee
[
34
]
. See Appendix
A
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(u,v,\phi_{1}(u,v),\phi_{2}(v))
be a smooth nondegenerate surface such that
\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}\neq 0
and
\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{2}\neq 0
.
Then the maximal estimate (
1.4
) holds for
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathcal{W}_{1}\setminus\big\{(1/2,1/2),(2/5,1/5)\big\}
. Moreover, the restricted weak type estimate holds at the endpoint
(2/5,1/5)
.

Remark 1.5
.

If both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
involve mixed variables, the boundedness of the maximal function depends on the specific structure of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
.
For instance, if
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(u^{2}+\varepsilon v^{k},v^{2}+\varepsilon v^{k})
for some small
\varepsilon\neq 0
, a direct application of van der Corput’s lemma yields an
L_{-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{k})}^{2}-L_{x,t}^{2}
estimate for
\mathcal{A}f
.
This in turn leads to
L^{p}
maximal estimates of
\mathcal{A}f
for some
p<2
.
For polynomial
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
, the Fourier decay is determined by the Newton polyhedron; see
[
1
]
for details.

Following the argument discussed in
[
24
, Proposition 1.5]
, a lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension of a union of geometric objects is determined by a smoothing order of local smoothing estimate for the associated averaging operator.
Namely, by applying Theorem 1.3, we can derive a lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension of the union of translations and dilations of
\Gamma_{\circ}
.
The following corollary is a consequence of Theorem
1.3
(see the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
24
]
), so we omit the proof.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
E
be a Borel set in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
such that
\dim_{H}E>\alpha
for some
0<\alpha\leq 2
.
If
F
is a Borel set in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
containing
\bigcup_{(x,t)\in E}(x+t\Gamma_{\circ})
, Then
\dim_{H}F\geq\alpha+2
. Especially, if
E
has Hausdorff dimension greater than
2
, then

F
has positive Lebesgue measure.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Preliminaries

In this section,
we prove the local smoothing estimate (
1.3
) for a slightly more general
\Gamma
than
\Gamma_{\circ}=(u,v,u^{2}-v^{2},2uv)
.
Indeed, we further assume that
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equation.
Since
\Gamma_{\circ}
is equivalent to the complex curve
(z,z^{2})
, the surface
\Gamma
considered in Theorem
2.1
can be regarded as a complex curve
(z,\Phi(z))
for a holomorphic function
\Phi=\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2}
satisfying
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(z)\neq 0
for all
|z|<1
.

Our approach relies on the induction on scale argument combined with multilinear restriction estimates and a decoupling inequality.
We adapt the argument used in
[
28
]
, which was originally introduced by Bourgain–Guth
[
11
]
. We decompose the domain of
\Gamma
to separate the linear part and the multilinear part. For the linear part, we rescale the operator with a small support of
\Gamma
and apply the induction quantity. For the multilinear part, we derive estimates for a wider range of
p,q
under a condition of separated domains. By efficiently combining these two parts, we obtain the desired estimates.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a nondegenerate surface satisfying (
1.2
).
Suppose that
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equation:

(2.1)

\partial_{u}\phi_{1}=\partial_{v}\phi_{2}~\text{ and }~\partial_{v}\phi_{1}=-\partial_{u}\phi_{2}.

If
\frac{1}{q}\leq\min\{\frac{1}{p},\,\frac{1}{3p}+\frac{1}{6},\,\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})\}
,
then (
1.3
) holds for
\gamma>\frac{2}{p}-\frac{5}{q}
.

Let
B^{d}(x,r)
be the ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\beta_{0}
be a real radial bump function supported on
B^{4}(0,2)
that satisfies
\beta_{0}=1
on
B^{4}(0,1)
.
We set
\beta_{\lambda}(\cdot)=\beta_{0}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)-\beta_{0}(2\lambda^{-1}\cdot)
for
\lambda>0
.
Let
P_{\lambda}
be the standard Littlewood-Paley projection operator given by
\widehat{P_{\lambda}f}(\xi)=\beta_{\lambda}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
\widehat{P_{0}f}(\xi)=\beta_{0}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)
.

Since
\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|\mathcal{A}P_{0}(x,t)|
is bounded by the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function and Young’s inequality, we see
\|\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|\mathcal{A}P_{0}f(\cdot,t)|\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|P_{0}f\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}
for
1\leq p\leq q
.
Therefore,
it is sufficient to prove the contribution
from the case of
\lambda\geq 1
.
Using the Fourier inversion formula, we consider the following Fourier integral operator
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]=\mathcal{A}[\varphi]P_{\lambda}
defined by

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f(x,t)=\chi_{I}(t)\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}\int e^{-it\Gamma(u,v)\cdot\xi}\varphi(u,v)dudv\beta_{\lambda}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

Let

\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})=:(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Also, let
c_{*}=\|J_{\Phi}^{\intercal}\|
for the Jacobian matrix
J_{\Phi}
with
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
. Here,
A^{\intercal}
is the transpose of
A
.
We can write

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f(x,t)=\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f_{1}(x,t)+\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f_{2}(x,t),

where

\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)=\Big(1-\beta_{0}\big(\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|}{2c_{*}|\xi^{\prime\prime}|}\big)\Big)\widehat{f}(\xi),\qquad\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi)=\beta_{0}\big(\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|}{2c_{*}|\xi^{\prime\prime}|}\big)\widehat{f}(\xi).

It is easy to show that
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f_{1}
maps boundedly from
L^{p}
to
L^{q}
for
q\geq p
, due to the rapid decay of the kernel.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma(u,v)=(u,v,\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
for smooth functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:[-1,1]^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Also, let
f_{1}
be defined as above. Then there is a
C_{N}>0
such that

\|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I)}\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{p}}

for any
N\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let us set
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f_{1}=K^{1}_{\lambda}(\cdot,t)\ast f
where

K^{1}_{\lambda}(x,t)=\chi_{I}(t)\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}\int e^{-it\Gamma(u,v)\cdot\xi}\varphi(u,v)\,dudv\beta_{\lambda}(\xi)\big(1-\beta_{0}(\tfrac{|\xi^{\prime}|}{2c_{*}|\xi^{\prime\prime}|})\big)\,d\xi.

Since
|\xi^{\prime}|\geq 2c_{*}|\xi^{\prime\prime}|
,
we have

|\nabla_{u,v}(\Gamma(u,v)\cdot\xi)|=|\xi^{\prime}+J_{\Phi}^{\intercal}\xi^{\prime\prime}|\gtrsim|\xi^{\prime}|\gtrsim|\xi|.

By repeated integration by parts, we get
|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\int e^{-it\Gamma(u,v)\cdot\xi}\varphi(u,v)dudv|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N}
for any
N\geq 1
and multi-indices
\alpha
.
This implies that
|K_{\lambda}^{1}(x,t)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}(1+|x|)^{-N}
for any
N\geq 1
by integration by parts. Applying Young’s inequality, we get the desired estimate.
∎

Now we consider the term
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f_{2}
.
We may assume that
\widehat{f}_{2}
is supported in

\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\leq|\xi^{\prime\prime}|<2\lambda,~|\xi^{\prime}|\leq 4c_{*}|\xi^{\prime\prime}|\}.

For

Let
\Gamma

Normalization of
\Gamma

Recall that
\Gamma_{\circ}(u,v)=(u,v,u^{2}-v^{2},2uv)

For a given

Under the assumption (

we have

(2.3)

\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}&\partial_{uv}^{2}\phi_{1}&\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{1}\\
\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}&\partial_{uv}^{2}\phi_{2}&\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}&-\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}\\
\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}&\phantom{-}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

For simplicity, let us set

\Gamma_{w}^{h}(z)=(\mathrm{M}^{h}_{w})^{-1}\big(\Gamma(hz+w)-\Gamma(w)\big),

\,\,0&\,h&\,0&\,0\,\\
\lx@intercol h\nabla_{z}\phi_{1}(w)\hfil\lx@intercol&\,h^{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}(w)&\,-h^{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}(w)\,\\

Lemma 2.4

Proof.

\displaystyle\Gamma(hz+w)-\Gamma(w)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}h&0&0&0&0\\
0&h&0&0&0\\
h\partial_{u}\phi_{1}&h\partial_{v}\phi_{1}&h^{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}&h^{2}\partial_{uv}^{2}\phi_{1}&h^{2}\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{1}\\
h\partial_{u}\phi_{2}&h\partial_{v}\phi_{2}&h^{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}&h^{2}\partial_{uv}^{2}\phi_{2}&h^{2}\partial_{v}^{2}\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\\
u^{2}\\
2uv\\
v^{2}\end{pmatrix}+O(|hz|^{3})

0&h&0&0\\

h\partial_{u}\phi_{2}&h\partial_{v}\phi_{2}&h^{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{2}&\phantom{-}h^{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\phi_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\

\Gamma_{w}^{h}(z)-\Gamma_{\circ}(z)=\|(\mathrm{M}_{w}^{h})^{-1}\|O(|hz|^{3})=O(h).

Then

∎

We choose a constant

By Lemma

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Gamma}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I)}

\displaystyle=h^{2}\Big(\iint|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Gamma_{w}^{h}}[\widetilde{\varphi}]f_{h}((\mathrm{M}_{w}^{h})^{-1}(x-t\Gamma(w)),t)|^{q}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=h^{2}|\det(\mathrm{M}_{w}^{h})|^{\frac{1}{q}}\|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Gamma_{w}^{h}}[\widetilde{\varphi}]f_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I)}.

Definition 2.5

\displaystyle\Gamma\in\mathfrak{R}(\epsilon_{\circ}),\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{B}),

\displaystyle\supp\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\lambda},\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq 1\big\}.

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ll h\leq h_{\circ}<\epsilon_{\circ}

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\epsilon_{\circ},h
satisfying (
2.8
),
let
\Gamma\in\mathfrak{R}(\epsilon_{\circ})
,
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathfrak{C}(h)
,

Suppose

is supported on
\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}

For any

and

(2.10)

\big\|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}](f-f_{\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{q})})\big\|_{L_{x,t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I)}\leq C\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

Let us set

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}](f-f_{\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{q})})(x,t)=K_{\lambda,\mathfrak{q}}(\cdot,t)\ast f(x),

where

K_{\lambda,\mathfrak{q}}(x,t)=\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}\int e^{-it\Gamma(u,v)\cdot\xi}\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}(u,v)\,dudv(1-\beta_{\lambda,\mathfrak{q}}(\xi))\beta_{\lambda}(\xi)d\xi.

For each

and
\xi\notin\supp\beta_{\lambda,\mathfrak{q}}
, we claim that

|\nabla_{z}\big(\Gamma(z)\cdot\xi\big)|\geq 4\lambda h.

By the Taylor expansion at the center
w_{\mathfrak{q}}
of
\mathfrak{q}
, we have

\nabla_{z}(\Gamma(z)\cdot\xi)=\nabla_{z}(\Gamma(w_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot\xi)+\nabla_{z}^{2}(\Gamma(w_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot\xi)(z-w_{\mathfrak{q}})^{\intercal}+O(\lambda|z-w_{\mathfrak{q}}|^{2}).

It follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{z}\big(\Gamma(z)\cdot\xi\big)|

\displaystyle\geq|\nabla_{z}\big(\Gamma(w_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot\xi\big)|-|\nabla_{z}^{2}(\Gamma(w_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot\xi)(z-w_{\mathfrak{q}})^{\intercal}+O(\lambda|z-w_{\mathfrak{q}}|^{2})|\geq 4\lambda h

since
\|\nabla_{z}^{2}(\Gamma(w_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot\xi)\|=\|\xi_{3}\nabla_{z}^{2}\phi_{1}(w_{\mathfrak{q}})+\xi_{4}\nabla_{z}^{2}\phi_{2}(w_{\mathfrak{q}})\|\leq 3\lambda
for

and

.

Repeated integration by parts yields that for any
N\geq 1
and
\alpha
,

\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\int e^{-it\Gamma(u,v)\cdot\xi}\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}(u,v)\,dudv\big|\leq C_{N}h^{2}(1+\lambda h^{2})^{-N}

provided by
\xi\notin\supp\beta_{\lambda,\mathfrak{q}}
.
By (
2.8
),
we have
\|K_{\lambda,\mathfrak{q}}(\cdot,t)\|_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{-N/2}
. Thus the desired estimate (
2.10
) follows by Young’s convolution inequality.
∎

Linear and multilinear decomposition

In order to show (

), we modify the argument in
[
25

(see also
[
28

,

We decompose the averaging operator

into two parts: linear operator defined on a small domain of

Lemma 2.7
.

For each
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I
, there exists a constant

such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{\mathfrak{q}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|+h^{-2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2},\mathfrak{q}_{3})\\
\in\mathfrak{D}(h)\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}]f(x,t)|^{\frac{1}{3}}

where
\mathfrak{D}(h)=\big\{(\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2},\mathfrak{q}_{3}):\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in\mathfrak{C}(h),~\min_{i\neq j}\text{dist\! }(\mathfrak{q}_{i},\mathfrak{q}_{j})\geq h\big\}

Proof.

Let
(x,t)
be fixed. Also let
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\ast}
be a square such that

|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\ast}}]f(x,t)|=\max_{\mathfrak{q}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|.

We define a family of squares away from
\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\ast}

\mathfrak{D}_{1}=\{\mathfrak{q}:\text{dist\! }(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{\ast})\geq h\}.

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\ast}\in\mathfrak{D}_{1}
be such that
|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\ast}}]f(x,t)|=\max_{\mathfrak{q}\in\mathfrak{D}_{1}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|
.
In a similar way, we can define
\mathfrak{D}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{3}^{\ast}\in\mathfrak{D}_{2}

Note that if
\text{dist\! }(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}_{k}^{\ast})\geq h
for all

, then

\max_{\mathfrak{q}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|\leq|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}_{k}^{\ast}}]f(x,t)|~\text{ for all }~k=1,2,3.

Setting
\mathfrak{D}_{3}=\cup_{k=1}^{2}\{\mathfrak{q}:\text{dist\! }(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}_{k}^{\ast})<h\}

into two parts

Then we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi]f(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\mathfrak{D}_{3}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|+\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\notin\mathfrak{D}_{3}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{\mathfrak{q}}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f(x,t)|+h^{-2}\max_{\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2},\mathfrak{q}_{3}\in\mathfrak{D}(h)}\prod_{i=1}^{3}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}]f(x,t)|^{\frac{1}{3}}

since

implies
\text{dist\! }(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}_{k}^{\ast})\geq h
for all
k=1,2
.
∎

We treat the linear term on the right-hand side of (

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

3.
A trilinear restriction estimate

In this section, we show that (
2.15

p=2
and
q=3
by using the multilinear restriction estimates established by Bennett–Carbery–Tao
[
5
]
.
In order to apply the multilinear restriction estimate, we need to decompose the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f
into a sum of Fourier restriction operators and an error term.
We make use of the method of stationary phase for the multiplier of
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f
, which is given by

\mathrm{m}_{\mathfrak{q}}(t\xi)=\int e^{-it\Gamma(z)\cdot\xi}\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}(z)\,dz.

Using the assumption (
1.2
) on
\Gamma
and the implicit function theorem, we will find the unique stationary point
z=z(\xi)
and associated hypersurface
\Psi_{\mathfrak{q}}(\xi)=\Gamma(z(\xi))\cdot\xi
defined in a small neighborhood of the center of
\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{q})
.

Let us define the Fourier extension operator
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
associated with
(\xi,\Psi_{\mathfrak{q}}(\xi))
by

T_{\mathfrak{q}}[\psi]g(x,t)=\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi-t\Psi_{\mathfrak{q}}(\xi))}\psi(\xi)g(\xi)\,d\xi,

where
\psi
is a compactly supported smooth function in the domain of

.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a sufficiently large integer
N
,

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}[\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}]f_{\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{q})}(x,t)=\chi_{I}(t)\sum_{l=0}^{N+5}t^{-1-l}T_{\mathfrak{q}}[\mathbf{b}_{\mathfrak{q},l}|\xi|^{-1-l}]\widehat{f_{\mathbf{P}(\mathfrak{q})}}(x,t)+\mathcal{E}f(x,t),

where

\mathcal{F}(P_{0}^{\prime}f)=\beta_{0}(\xi^{\prime})\widehat{f}(\xi)
and
\mathcal{F}(P_{0}^{\prime\prime}f)=\beta_{0}(\xi^{\prime\prime})\widehat{f}(\xi)
, respectively.

Since
1=\beta_{0}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{2^{j}}
for
\beta_{0}=\sum_{j\leq 0}\beta_{2^{j}}
,
we have

\displaystyle 1=\beta_{0}(\xi^{\prime})+\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{2^{j}}(\xi^{\prime})\beta_{0}(\xi^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\beta_{2^{j}}(\xi^{\prime})\beta_{2^{k}}(\xi^{\prime\prime}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{t,s}|\mathfrak{A}f(x,t,s)|\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t,s}|\mathfrak{A}P_{0}^{\prime}f(x,t,s)|+

(A.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\geq 1}\sup_{t,s}|\mathfrak{A}P_{2^{j},0}f(x,t,s)|+\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\sup_{t,s}|\mathfrak{A}P_{2^{j},2^{k}}f(x,t,s)|.

It is obvious that
\sup_{t,s\in[1,2]}|\mathfrak{A}P_{0}^{\prime}f(x,t,s)|
is bounded by composition of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function and the circular maximal function.
In fact, sine
P_{0}^{\prime}
is a convolution operator with a Schwartz function and
t\in[1,2]
, we have
\int P_{0}^{\prime}f(x^{\prime}-t(u,\phi_{1}(u,v)),x^{\prime\prime}-s(v,\phi_{2}(v)))du
is bounded by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
\mathfrak{M}f(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}-s(v,\phi_{2}(v)))
for fixed
x^{\prime\prime}
,
s
, and
v
.
Then,

\sup_{t,s}|\mathfrak{A}P_{0}^{\prime}f(x,t,s)|\leq\sup_{s}\big|\int\mathfrak{M}f(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}-s(v,\phi_{2}(v)))dv\big|.

By the endpoint circular maximal theorem in
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
and the
L^{p}
boundedness of
\mathfrak{M}
, the estimates in Proposition
A.1
hold for
\mathfrak{A}
replaced by
\mathfrak{A}P_{0}^{\prime}
.

To handle the contribution from
\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f:=\mathfrak{A}P_{2^{j},2^{k}}f
in (
A.1
) (the other term can be handled in a similar way, so we omit the details), we apply (
6.5
) to
F=\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(x,\cdot)
repeatedly in the
t
and
s
variables with
\ell=2^{-j}
or
\ell=2^{-k}
, respectively.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t,s\in I}|\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}(x,t,s)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{j}{q}+\frac{k}{q}}\big\|\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(x,\cdot)\big\|_{L_{t,s}^{q}}+2^{\frac{j}{q}-\frac{k}{q^{\prime}}}\big\|\partial_{s}\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(x,\cdot)\big\|_{L_{t,s}^{q}}

\displaystyle+2^{-\frac{j}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{k}{q}}\big\|\partial_{t}\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(x,\cdot)\big\|_{L_{t,s}^{q}}+2^{-\frac{j}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{k}{q^{\prime}}}\big\|\partial_{t}\partial_{s}\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(x,\cdot)\big\|_{L_{t,s}^{q}}.

Observe that the amplitude of the multiplier associated with
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{s}^{\beta}\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}
for
\alpha,\beta=0,1
, includes an factor
((u,\phi_{1})\cdot\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha}((v,\phi_{2})\cdot\xi^{\prime\prime})^{\beta}
, which is bounded by
2^{j\alpha}2^{k\beta}
. Since the amplitude of the multiplier and its derivatives are uniformly bounded, after factoring out
2^{j\alpha}2^{k\beta}
, it follows from the Mikhlin multiplier theorem that it suffices to prove the case
\alpha=\beta=0
.

First, we have the
L^{2}
-estimate

(A.2)

\big\|\sup_{t,s\in I}|\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(\cdot,t,s)|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}

by Plancherel’s theorem and the decay of the multipliers associated with
\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}
.

Next, we claim that

(A.3)

\big\|\sup_{t,s\in I}|\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f(\cdot,t,s)|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim 2^{(1-\frac{5}{q})(j+k)}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

for
p,q
satisfying
1/p+3/q=1
and
q>14/3
.
The following sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
local smoothing estimate was established by Lee
[
34
, Proposition 1.2]
:
For
1/p+3/q=1
and
q>14/3
, the estimate

(A.4)

\big\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}P_{j}g\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I)}\leq C2^{(3/2-6/q)j}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for
j\geq 0
.
Note that (
A.4
) is valid not only for the cone but also any conic extension of curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with nonvanishing curvature.
Thus we have

(A.5)

\displaystyle\Big\|\int P_{j}g(x-t(u,\phi(u))du\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I)}\leq 2^{(1-6/q)j}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
\phi
is a smooth function with nonvanishing curvature in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

By Minkowski’s inequality and applying (
A.5
) repeatedly, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\mathfrak{A}P_{j,k}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times I^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\int\Big\|\int P_{j,k}f(x^{\prime}-t(u,\phi_{1}(u,v)),x^{\prime\prime}-s(v,\phi_{2}(v)))\,du\Big\|_{L_{x^{\prime},t}^{q}}\,dv\Big\|_{L_{x^{\prime\prime},s}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(1-\frac{6}{q})j}\Big\|\int\big\|P_{j,k}f(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}-s(v,\phi_{2}(v)))\big\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{p}}\,dv\Big\|_{L_{x^{\prime\prime},s}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(1-\frac{6}{q})j}\Big\|\Big\|\int P_{j,k}f(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}-s(v,\phi_{2}(v)))\,dv\Big\|_{L_{x^{\prime\prime},s}^{q}}\Big\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(1-\frac{6}{q})(j+k)}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}},

which implies (
A.3
).

Following the argument in the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
34
]
,
we use Bourgain’s interpolation trick (see Lemma

on the half-open line segment from

to
(2/5,1/5)
, including
(2/5,1/5)
.
This gives the desired estimate by interpolation with the trivial

-estimate.

Singularity formation in co-dimension one of the dHYM cotangent flow on blow up of ℂ ​ ℙ 3 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3} at a point Thanks: Work supported in part by a PhD fellowship from the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru
Ramesh Mete

Abstract
. The existence and uniqueness of canonical singular solutions of the J equation and the deformed Hermitian Yang Mills (dHYM) equation was proved in [ 11 ] on compact Kähler surfaces. In this paper, we study the singularity formation of the dHYM cotangent flow on the one-point blow up of ℂ ​ ℙ 3 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3} using Calabi ansatz. In particular, we provide an explicit example where the flow develops a singularity along the exceptional divisor. Moreover, the limit satisfies corresponding singular dHYM equation in the sense of [ 11 ] and provides some evidence for Conjecture 1.12 1.12 in [ 11 ] on this three dimensional manifold with symmetry.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. Fix a real
(1,1)
cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R}):=H_{{\rm dR}}^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap H_{\overline{\partial}}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and denote the Kähler class of
\omega

\beta:=[\omega]
. Then the
deformed Hermitian Yang Mills
(dHYM) equation is given by

(1.1)

\mathrm{Re}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\omega)^{n}=\cot(\hat{\theta})\mathrm{Im}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\omega)^{n},

where the desired solution
\chi
is a smooth closed real
(1,1)
-form in
\alpha
, and
\hat{\theta}
is a constant satisfying the following

(1.2)

\mathrm{Re}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}-(\cot\hat{\theta})\mathrm{Im}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}=0.

Here and for rest of the paper,
(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}:=\int_{X}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\omega)^{n}
for any representatives
\omega\in\beta
,
\chi\in\alpha
, and it depends only on the cohomology classes
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Assuming
(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}\neq 0
, note that
\hat{\theta}
is well-defined mod
2\pi
. We will assume throughout that
\hat{\theta}
is normalized to be in the
supercritical phase
, i.e.
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
. In particular, we are also assuming that
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}>0
. We now recall a notion of slope stability.

Definition 1.1
.

Assume
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
satisfying
(
1.2
)
. The triple
(X,\alpha,\beta)
is said to be

(1)

dHYM semi-stable
(resp.
stable
) if there exists a Kähler class

such that

(1.3)

\big(\mathrm{Re}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{k}-\cot\hat{\theta}\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{k}\big)\cdot\gamma^{m-k}\cdot Y\geq 0

for any analytic subvariety
Y
of dimension
m
and any integer

(resp. strict inequality in
(
1.3
)
when

).

(2)

dHYM unstable
if it is not dHYM semi-stable.

In view of Definition

\big(\mathrm{Re}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{m}-\cot\hat{\theta}\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{m}\big)\cdot Y>0

If

2.1.
Calabi symmetry and reduction

Let

(2.1)

[H]^{n}=1,\penalty\ \penalty\ [E]^{n}=(-1)^{n-1},\penalty\ \penalty\ [H]\cdot[E]=0.

(1,1)

we have
c_{q}=\frac{5328}{2863}
as mentioned in Remark
1.5
.

Now suppose for each
t\geq 0
,
\chi_{t}:=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi(t)
satisfies Calabi ansatz, i.e.

\chi_{t}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}v(\rho,t)

for some
v(\cdot,t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. Let
\psi:[1,b]\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

(2.4)

\psi(x,t):=\psi(u^{\prime}(\rho),t):=v^{\prime}(\rho,t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \rho\in\mathbb{R},

with boundary conditions
\psi(1,t)=q
and
\psi(b,t)=p
for any
t\geq 0
. Note that
\psi(\cdot,t)
is smooth on
(1,b)
. Taking derivatives with respect to
\rho
and
t
respectively we obtain

(2.5)

\psi^{\prime}(x,t)u^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=v^{\prime\prime}(\rho,t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \dot{\psi}(x,t)=\dot{v}^{\prime}(\rho,t).

Throughout the paper, we use the following notations

\dot{\psi}:=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t},\penalty\ \penalty\ \psi^{\prime}:=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x},\penalty\ \penalty\ u^{\prime}:=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\rho},\penalty\ \penalty\ u^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial\rho^{2}}.

In above formulation, the Lagrangian phase function
\theta_{\omega}(\chi_{t})
is given by

\theta(x,t):=(n-1)\mathrm{arccot}(\frac{\psi}{x})+\mathrm{arccot}(\psi^{\prime}).

We first re-write the cotangent flow
(
1.6
)
as

\dot{v}(\rho,t)=\cot\theta(x,t)-\cot\hat{\theta}.

Then taking derivative of this equation with respect to
\rho
and using
(
2.5
)
we see that the cotangent flow
(
1.6
)
on
X=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{n}
is reduced into the following evolution equation of one space variable:

(2.6)

\dot{\psi}(x,t)=Q(x)(\cot\theta)^{\prime}=Q(x)(\csc^{2}\theta)\big(\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}}{1+\psi^{\prime 2}}+(n-1)\frac{x\psi^{\prime}-\psi}{x^{2}+\psi^{2}}\big)=:L_{2}(\psi).

Here
Q:[1,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by
Q(x):=u^{\prime\prime}\circ{u^{\prime}}^{-1}(x)

with
Q(1)=Q(b)=0
. Note that
Q
is a continuous function which is smooth and positive in the interior. The equation
(
2.6
)
is strictly parabolic away from the boundary points
\{1,b\}
.

We have the following important observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\psi\in C^{2}([1,b],\mathbb{R})
we define

(2.7)

\theta_{\psi}(x):=\mathrm{arccot}(\psi^{\prime})+(n-1)\mathrm{arccot}(\frac{\psi}{x}).

The function
\theta_{\psi}<\pi
if and only if the function
\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n}
is strictly increasing on
[1,b]
. Moreover,
\theta_{\psi}<\pi
also implies the functions
\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}
are strictly increasing and positive for all integers
k\in[1,n-1]
, and
\psi
is positive when
n\geq 3
. Furthermore, if
\theta_{\psi}\equiv\hat{\theta}
for some constant
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
, then for each integer
k\in[1,n-1]
and all
x\in[1,b]
we have

\mathrm{Re}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}-(\cot\hat{\theta})\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}>0.

Proof.

Observe that the condition
\theta_{\psi}<\pi
implies

0<k\cdot\mathrm{arccot}(\frac{\psi}{x})<\mathrm{arccot}(\psi^{\prime})+k\cdot\mathrm{arccot}(\frac{\psi}{x})<\pi

for all integers
k\in[1,n-1]
. As a consequence,
\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{\psi}{x}+\sqrt{-1}\right)^{k}>0,
and so
\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}>0
for all integers
k\in[1,n-1]
. Also note that
\psi>0
when
n\geq 3
since if
\psi\leq 0
at some point, then at that point we must have
\theta_{\psi}>(n-1)\frac{\pi}{2}\geq\pi
. Using the formula
\cot(kA)=\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\cot A+\sqrt{-1})^{k}}{\mathrm{Im}(\cot A+\sqrt{-1})^{k}}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\in(0,\frac{\pi}{k})
, we get

\displaystyle\cot(k\cdot\mathrm{arccot}\frac{\psi}{x})>\cot(\pi-\mathrm{arccot}\psi^{\prime})\iff

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}+\psi^{\prime}\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}>0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k+1}\right)^{\prime}>0.

This proves the first part. For the second part, suppose
\theta_{\psi}\equiv\hat{\theta}
for some constant
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
. Then for any integer
k\in[1,n-1]
we get

0<k\cdot\mathrm{arccot}(\frac{\psi}{x})<\hat{\theta}<\pi,

and hence

\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}}{\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}}=\cot(k\cdot\mathrm{arccot}\frac{\psi}{x})>\cot\hat{\theta}.

We get the desired result since the denominator on the left hand side is positive.
∎

2.2.
Stability Inequalities

Analogous to
[
14
, Lemma 1]
, we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
X=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
\alpha=p[H]-q[E]
and
\beta=b[H]-[E]
as above with
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
. The triple
(X,\alpha,\beta)
is dHYM stable (resp. semi-stable) (in the sense of Definition
1.1
) if and only if for all integers
k\in[1,n-1]
we have

(2.8)

\begin{cases}\mathrm{Re}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}-(\cot\hat{\theta})\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}>0\penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{resp.}\penalty\ \geq),\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\mathrm{Re}(q+\sqrt{-1})^{k}-(\cot\hat{\theta})\mathrm{Im}(q+\sqrt{-1})^{k}>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{resp.}\penalty\ \geq).\end{cases}

Proof.

It is enough to consider the following
m
-dimensional subvarieties
Y=H^{n-m}
and
Y=(-1)^{n-m-1}E^{n-m}
where
m\in[1,n-1]
is an integer. Using
(
2.1
)
we get

(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{m}\cdot H^{n-m}=(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{m},\penalty\ \penalty\ (\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{m}\cdot(-1)^{n-m-1}E^{n-m}=(q+\sqrt{-1})^{m}.

Therefore, we get the desired result.
∎

In view of the above lemma, the dHYM equation
(
1.5
)
has a smooth solution if and only if stability inequalities
(
2.8
)
are satisfied. In particular for
n=2
, the dHYM (resp. semi-) stability of the triple
(X,\alpha,\beta)
is equivalent to

(2.9)

p-bc_{q}>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ q-c_{q}>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{resp.}\penalty\ \geq).

On the other hand, for
n\geq 3
we have the following elementary observation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
. Then the dHYM stability (resp. semi-stability) of the triple
(\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{n},\alpha,\beta)
implies

(2.10)

p>b\left(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}\right),\penalty\ \penalty\ q>\left(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{resp.}\penalty\ \geq).

In particular, both
p
,
q
must be positive for dHYM semi-stability. Moreover, for
n=3
the condition
(
2.10
)
with strict inequality (resp. with
\geq
) is also sufficient for dHYM stability (resp. semi-stability).

Proof.

Considering
k=1,2
in the inequalities
(
2.8
)
we get

p-bc_{q}>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ q-c_{q}>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ p^{2}-b^{2}-2bpc_{q}>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ q^{2}-1-2qc_{q}>0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{resp.}\penalty\ \geq).

The last two inequalities implies

|p-bc_{q}|>b\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}},\penalty\ \penalty\ |q-c_{q}|>\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{resp.}\penalty\ \geq)

and hence, all observations follow immediately.
∎

It is known
[
11
]
that the inequality
p>bc_{q}
always holds under assumption
\alpha\cdot\beta>0
for
n=2
. We now show that one stability inequality also comes for free on
\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
\mathrm{Im}(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{3}>0
, i.e.
3(p^{2}b-q^{2})>b^{3}-1
, and both
p,q>0
. Then
p>b(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}})
where

(2.11)

c_{q}=\cot\hat{\theta}=\frac{p^{3}-3pb^{2}-q^{3}+3q}{3p^{2}b-b^{3}-3q^{2}+1}.

Furthermore, for the case
0<p\leq q
we also have
q>(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}})
, and hence
(\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3},\alpha,\beta)
is dHYM stable in this case (according to
(
2.10
)
).

Proof.

Since both
p,q>0
, from the hypothesis we obtain
0<q<p\sqrt{b}
. We compute

\displaystyle G(p,q,b)

\displaystyle:=(p^{2}-b^{2}-2pbc_{q})\Big(\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{3}-\mathrm{Im}(q+\sqrt{-1})^{3}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Im}\Big((p-\sqrt{-1}b)^{2}((p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{3}-(q+\sqrt{-1})^{3})\Big)\penalty\ \hskip 45.52458pt(\text{by}\penalty\ \hyperref@@ii[eq:first-identity]{\textup{{(\ignorespaces\ref*{eq:first-identity}\/)}}})

\displaystyle=bp^{4}+p^{2}(2b^{3}-3q^{2}+1)+2pbq(q^{2}-3)+b^{2}(b^{3}+3q^{2}-1).

Case I:

0<p\leq q
(i.e. the class
\alpha
is not Kähler). We have

0<p\leq q<p\sqrt{b},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 3(p^{2}b-q^{2})>b^{3}-1.

As a consequence we get
3p^{2}>b^{2}+b+1
, which implies
p>1
since
b>1
. The above inequalities also imply
c_{q}<0
. In particular,
p-bc_{q}>0
,
q-c_{q}>0
and
0<c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}<1
. Since
q\geq p>1
, we have
q>c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}
, and
p>b(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}})
if we assume
p\geq b
. On the other hand, suppose
p<b
. Then

\displaystyle G(p,q,b)

\displaystyle=b(p^{2}+b^{2})^{2}+(3q^{2}-1)(b^{2}-p^{2})+2pbq(q^{2}-3)

\displaystyle>b(p^{2}+b^{2})^{2}-4pbq\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{since}\penalty\ q>1\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ 0<p<b)

\displaystyle>(p^{2}+b^{2})^{2}-4p^{2}b^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{since}\penalty\ b>1\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ 0<q<pb)

\displaystyle=(p^{2}-b^{2})^{2}\geq 0.

Since
p>0
, we must have
p>b(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}})
. As a consequence, the triple
(\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3},\alpha,\beta)
is always dHYM stable if
q\geq p>0
.

Case II:

p>q>0
(i.e. the class
\alpha
is Kähler). We have

0<q<p<p\sqrt{b},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 3(p^{2}b-q^{2})>b^{3}-1.

If we put
\lambda:=\frac{p}{q}>1
, then

\displaystyle G(p,q,b)=q^{4}\lambda(\lambda^{3}b-3\lambda+2b)+q^{2}(2\lambda^{2}b^{3}+\lambda^{2}-6\lambda b+3b^{2})+b^{2}(b^{3}-1)>0.

Here we used the fact that the functions
f(\lambda):=\lambda^{3}b-3\lambda+2b
and
g(\lambda):=2\lambda^{2}b^{3}+\lambda^{2}-6\lambda b+3b^{2}
are positive on
(1,\infty)
since
b>1
. Therefore, it implies that
p>b(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}})
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The inequality
p>b(c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}})
also holds if
p\in(0,b)
and
\max(-p\sqrt{b},-\sqrt{3})<q\leq 0
, or if
p\geq b
and
\max(-p\sqrt{b},-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})<q\leq 0
.

Remark 2.6
.

For

with

we shall also show that

(see Lemma

). It implies
(\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3},\alpha,\beta)
is dHYM stable in this case. Therefore, if
\alpha
is a Kähler class, the only possibility for occurrence of instability is when

.

2.3.
Auxiliary family of equations

Recall that we fixed real
(1,1)
class
\alpha=p[H]-q[E]
. Now, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
we set

\alpha_{s}:=p[H]-s[E]=\alpha+(q-s)[E].

We then define the following subsets of the real line

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{n}:=\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\penalty\ \mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}\neq 0\right\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{S}_{n}^{+}:=\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\penalty\ \mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}>0\right\}.

Analogous to
\lx@sectionsign 4.2
in
[
11
]
, we consider the following auxiliary family of equations which are dHYM equations for the triple
(X=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{n},\alpha_{s},\beta)
:

(2.12)

\left(\mathrm{Re}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n}\right)^{\prime}=c_{s}\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n}\right)^{\prime},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \psi(1)=s,\penalty\ \psi(b)=p,

where
s\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
and

c_{s}:=\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}}{\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}}=\cot\hat{\theta}(X,\alpha_{s},\beta).

Solutions of the family
(
2.12
)
are smooth functions
\tilde{\psi}_{s}:[1,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with above boundary conditions and satisfying

(2.13)

\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n}=A_{s},

where

\begin{split}&c_{s}=\frac{\mathrm{Re}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}}{\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}},\\
&A_{s}=\mathrm{Re}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}=\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}.\end{split}

We get such
\tilde{\psi}_{s}
satisfying
(
2.13
)
if and only if
(X,\alpha_{s},\beta)
is dHYM stable
[
14
]
, and in view of Lemma
2.2
, if and only if

\mathrm{Re}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{k}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{k}>0

for all integers
k\in[1,n-1]
.

Using the basic identity

\mathrm{Im}(z)\mathrm{Re}(w)-\mathrm{Re}(z)\mathrm{Im}(w)=\mathrm{Im}(z\bar{w})

for any
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
, we get following identities for any integer
k\in[1,n]
:

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\left(\mathrm{Re}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}\right)\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}\\
&=\mathrm{Im}\left((p-\sqrt{-1}b)^{k}\left((p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}\right)\right),\end{split}

and

(2.15)

\begin{split}&\left(\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{k}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{k}\right)\cdot\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}\\
&=\mathrm{Im}\left((s-\sqrt{-1})^{k}\left((p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}\right)\right).\end{split}

Now applying these identities we obtain

(2.16)

\frac{dc_{s}}{ds}=-\frac{n\Big(\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}\Big)}{\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}}.

It implies that
c_{s}
is strictly decreasing on
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{+}
if and only if

\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}>c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
s\in\mathcal{S}^{+}_{n}
and the triple
(X,\alpha_{s},\beta)
is dHYM stable. Then the sequence of functions
\{\tilde{\psi}_{s}\}
is strictly increasing.

Proof.

Since
(X,\alpha_{s},\beta)
is dHYM stable, there exist a unique
\tilde{\psi}_{s}\in C^{\infty}([1,b],\mathbb{R})
satisfying
(
2.13
)
with
\tilde{\psi}_{s}(1)=s
,
\tilde{\psi}_{s}(b)=p
. Observe that the family
(
2.12
)
can also be written as

\theta_{\tilde{\psi}_{s}}=\mathrm{arccot}(c_{s})\in(0,\pi).

Therefore, applying Lemma
2.1
the functions
\mathrm{Im}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}
are strictly increasing for all integers
k\in[1,n]
, and positive for
k<n
on
[1,b]
. Moreover,
\tilde{\psi}_{s}>0
when
n\geq 3
, and we have
\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}>c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{k}
for
k<n
. Differentiating
(
2.13
)
with respect to

\displaystyle n\left(\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n-1}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n-1}\right)\frac{d\tilde{\psi}_{s}}{ds}

\displaystyle=-\Big(\mathrm{Im}(p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-\mathrm{Im}(\tilde{\psi}_{s}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{n}\Big)\frac{dc_{s}}{ds}.

Therefore, we get the desired result because both the terms in brackets are positive and
\frac{dc_{s}}{ds}<0

∎

We let

F(s):=\mathrm{Im}\Big((s-\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}\big((p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}\big)\Big),

which is a polynomial of degree

2.15
)
(for

(2.17)

\displaystyle F(s)=\left(\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}\right)\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}

for all

. One can check that

F\left(\frac{p}{b}\right)=\frac{(b^{n}-1)(p^{2}+b^{2})^{n-1}}{b^{2n-2}}>0.

Differentiating the polynomial
F
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}F^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Im}\left((s-\sqrt{-1})^{n-2}\left((p+\sqrt{-1}b)^{n}-(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Re}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-2}-c_{s}\mathrm{Im}(s+\sqrt{-1})^{n-2}\right)\mathrm{Im}(\alpha_{s}+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{n}.\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{by}\penalty\ \hyperref@@ii[eq:second-identity]{\textup{{(\ignorespaces\ref*{eq:second-identity}\/)}}})

Similar to Lemma
4.4
in
[
11
]
for
n=2
case, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
n=3
and
p>0
. Then for

. In particular, the class
\alpha_{s}-c_{s}\beta

Since any

\frac{p-s}{b-1}\geq\frac{p}{b}

As mentioned in Remark

Note that putting

&F^{\prime\prime}(s)=2(-6s^{2}+(3p^{2}b-b^{3}-2)).\end{split}

We already showed that

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
n=3
. The polynomial
F
is positive (and hence

) for all

and hence again

2.4.
Examples of two kinds of dHYM instability

Recall
(X=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3},\alpha,\beta)
is dHYM stable if and only if
q\geq c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}
. In particular, if
q\leq 0
, it is dHYM unstable. On the other hand, we proved in Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.9
that it is dHYM stable if
q\geq\frac{p}{b}>0
.

We now give an example of
c_{q}<q\leq c_{q}+\sqrt{1+c_{q}^{2}}
, where
c_{q}
could be positive or zero or negative, and we say this is
first kind of dHYM instability
where instability occurs along co-dimension one subvarieties. Next we give an example of
0<q\leq c_{q}
which we say
second kind of dHYM instability
where instability occurs along co-dimension two subvarieties.

For simplicity, if we put
\lambda:=p/s
, then from
(
2.18
)
and
(
2.19
)
we get

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle=s^{4}(-2\lambda^{3}+3\lambda^{2}b-1)+s^{2}(-3\lambda^{2}b+6\lambda b^{2}-b^{3}-2)+(b^{3}-1)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=2s^{3}(-\lambda^{3}+3\lambda^{2}b-2)+2s(3\lambda b^{2}-b^{3}-2).

Now considering
\lambda=3b
we get

2(s-c_{s})\big(3(9b^{3}-1)s^{2}-(b^{3}-1)\big)=F^{\prime}(s)=4s(4b^{3}-1-s^{2}).

It implies
0<s\leq c_{s}
if
s\geq\sqrt{4b^{3}-1}
. Therefore, replacing
s
by
q
, we get second kind of dHYM instability
0<q\leq c_{q}
when
p=3bq
and
q\geq\sqrt{4b^{3}-1}
.

Since the polynomial
P_{0}(\lambda):=-\lambda^{3}+3\lambda^{2}b-2
is positive on
[a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}]
, where
a^{\prime}\in(0,1)
and
a^{\prime\prime}\in(2b,3b)
are only positive zeros of
P_{0}
, and
a^{\prime}<\frac{3b^{3}+2}{3b^{2}}
because
P_{0}\left(\frac{3b^{3}+2}{3b^{2}}\right)>0
, we must have
s>c_{s}
if
\frac{3b^{3}+2}{3b^{2}}\leq\frac{p}{s}\leq a^{\prime\prime}
(and both
p,s
are positive).

Now consider
\lambda=2b
(i.e.
p=2bs
). Since
\frac{p}{s}=2b\in\left(\frac{3b^{3}+2}{3b^{2}},a^{\prime\prime}\right)
, we have
s>c_{s}
. For this choice

F(s)=-(4b^{3}+1)s^{4}-(b^{3}+2)s^{2}+b^{3}-1.

We can see that

for all

, where

b_{\star}:=\left(\frac{\sqrt{b^{3}(17b^{3}-1)}-b^{3}-2}{2(4b^{3}+1)}\right)^{1/2},

and hence we get that
0<s<c_{s}+\sqrt{c_{s}^{2}+1}
for the choice
p=2bs
and
s>b_{\star}
. Moreover,
c_{s}
is positive when
s>\left(\frac{3(2b^{3}-1)}{8b^{3}-1}\right)^{1/2}=:b^{\star}
, equals to zero when
s=b^{\star}
, and negative when
b_{\star}<s<b^{\star}
. This can be observed from the following inequalities
\sqrt{\frac{b^{3}-1}{3(4b^{3}-1)}}<b_{\star}<b^{\star}<1
. Therefore, once again replacing
s
by
q
, we get first kind of dHYM instability
c_{q}<q<c_{q}+\sqrt{c_{q}^{2}+1}
when
p=2bq
with

. Note that the explicit example mentioned in Remark
1.5
is of this kind.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose the first assumption of
(
H1
)
holds and assume that there exists a unique branch
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
of the level set
(
2.3
)
with
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}(1)=\xi
and
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}(b)=p
. (Note that for
n=2
and
n=3
we have such a unique
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
.)
For all
t\in[0,\infty)
and
x\in[1,b]
, we have
\psi(x,t)\leq\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}(x)
along the cotangent flow.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\psi(x,t)\leq\tilde{\psi}_{s}(x)
for all
s\in(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
. We let

H_{s}(x,t):=\psi(x,t)-\tilde{\psi}_{s}(x).

Then

\dot{H}_{s}=\dot{\psi},\penalty\ H^{\prime}_{s}=\psi^{\prime}-\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{s},\penalty\ H^{\prime\prime}_{s}=\psi^{\prime\prime}-\tilde{\psi}^{\prime\prime}_{s}.

But then from the evolution equation
(
2.6
)
we obtain the following evolution equation for
H_{s}
on
(1,b]\times[0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{s}=\frac{Q\csc^{2}(\theta)}{(x^{2}+\psi^{2})(1+\psi^{\prime 2})}\Big(

\displaystyle(x^{2}+\psi^{2})H^{\prime\prime}_{s}+(n-1)(1+\psi^{\prime 2})(xH^{\prime}_{s}-H_{s})

\displaystyle+(H_{s}+2\tilde{\psi}_{s})H_{s}\tilde{\psi}^{\prime\prime}_{s}+(n-1)H^{\prime}_{s}(H^{\prime}_{s}+2\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{s})(x\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{s}-\tilde{\psi}_{s})\Big),

where we used the fact that
\tilde{\psi}_{s}
satisfies the following second order ODE:

(3.2)

(x^{2}+\tilde{\psi}_{s}^{2})\tilde{\psi}^{\prime\prime}_{s}+(n-1)(1+\tilde{\psi}^{\prime 2}_{s})(x\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{s}-\tilde{\psi}_{s})=0.

At the spatial and time boundaries we observe that,

H_{s}(x,0)<0,\penalty\ \forall x\in(1,b),\penalty\ H_{s}(1,t)<0,\penalty\ \forall t\in[0,\infty),\penalty\ H_{s}(b,t)=0,\penalty\ \forall t\in[0,\infty).

Arguing by contradiction, suppose there exists a
T>0
such that

H^{*}:=\underset{[1,b]\times[0,T]}{\sup}H_{s}(x,t)>0.

Note that

a(x,t):=\frac{Q\csc^{2}\theta}{(x^{2}+\psi^{2})(1+\psi^{\prime 2})}

is uniformly bounded above as the angle
\theta(x,t)
lies in a compact set of
(0,\pi)
and
Q=u^{\prime\prime}\circ u^{\prime-1}
. We also have that
||\tilde{\psi}_{s}||_{C^{2}[1,b]}
is finite for
s>\xi
(Note that this fails at
s=\xi
). Define a constant
A=A(T)>0
by

A:=1+\lVert\tilde{\psi}_{s}\rVert_{C^{2}[1,b]}+\underset{[1,b]\times[0,T]}{\sup}a(x,t).

We let
B=1+A^{2}H^{*}+2A^{3}
and consider the function
F(x,t):=e^{-Bt}H_{s}(x,t)
. First we see that

F^{*}:=F(x^{*},t^{*})=\underset{[1,b]\times[0,T]}{\sup}F(x,t)>0

for some
(x^{*},t^{*})\in[1,b]\times[0,T]
. Next, the spatial and time boundary conditions implies that
x^{*}\in(1,b)
and
t^{*}\neq 0
. It implies that

\dot{F}(x^{*},t^{*})\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ F^{\prime}(x^{*},t^{*})=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ F^{\prime\prime}(x^{*},t^{*})\leq 0.

Then by the maximum principle at the point
(x^{*},t^{*})
we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\dot{F}=-BF^{*}+e^{-Bt^{*}}\dot{H}_{s}

\displaystyle=-BF^{*}+a(x^{*},t^{*})\Big((x^{2}+\psi^{2})F^{\prime\prime}+(n-1)(1+\psi^{\prime 2})(xF^{\prime}-F)+F^{*}(H_{s}+2\tilde{\psi}_{s})\tilde{\psi}^{\prime\prime}_{s}

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+(n-1)(H^{\prime}_{s}+2\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{s})(x\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{s}-\tilde{\psi}_{s})F^{\prime}(x^{*},t^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-BF^{*}+a(x^{*},t^{*})\Big(H(x^{*},t^{*})+2\tilde{\psi}_{s}(x^{*})\Big)\tilde{\psi}^{\prime\prime}_{s}(x^{*})F^{*}

\displaystyle\leq-BF^{*}+(A^{2}H^{*}+2A^{3})F^{*}=-F^{*},

which is a contradiction. Hence, we must have

\underset{[1,b]\times[0,\infty)}{\sup}H_{s}(x,t)\leq 0,

and so we get the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose the second assumption of
(
H1
)
holds. For all
t\in[0,\infty)
and all
x\in[1,b]
, we have
\dot{\psi}(x,t)\geq 0
along the cotangent flow.

Proof.

At the boundary points, since
Q(1)=Q(b)=0
, we have
\dot{\psi}(1,t)=\dot{\psi}(b,t)=0
for all
t
. Taking one time derivative of the evolution equation
(
2.6
)
we see that
\dot{\psi}
satisfies the following equation:

\ddot{\psi}=L(\dot{\psi})+a\dot{\psi},

where
L(f):={\bf A}(x,t)f^{\prime\prime}+{\bf B}(x,t)f^{\prime}
,

\begin{cases}\mathbf{A}(x,t)&:=\frac{Q(x)\csc^{2}(\theta)}{1+\psi^{\prime 2}},\\
\mathbf{B}(x,t)&:=Q(x)\csc^{2}(\theta)\Big(-2\frac{\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}}{(1+\psi^{\prime 2})^{2}}+(n-1)\frac{x}{x^{2}+\psi^{2}}\Big)+\frac{\cot\theta}{1+\psi^{\prime 2}}\dot{\psi},\\
a(x,t)&:=Q(x)\csc^{2}(\theta)(n-1)\Big(-\frac{1}{x^{2}+\psi^{2}}-2\frac{\psi(x\psi^{\prime}-\psi)}{(x^{2}+\psi^{2})^{2}}\Big)+(n-1)\frac{x\cot\theta}{x^{2}+\psi^{2}}\dot{\psi}.\end{cases}

Arguing by contradiction, suppose there exists a
T>0
such that

\inf_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}\dot{\psi}(x,t)<0.

Let
C=C(T)>0
such that

C=1+\sup_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}|a(x,t)|.

We now let
F(x,t)=e^{-Ct}\dot{\psi}(x,t),
and let
(x^{*},t^{*})\in[1,b]\times[0,T]
such that

F^{*}:=F(x^{*},t^{*})=\inf_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}F(x,t)<0.

Then by the boundary conditions,
x^{*}\in(1,b)
and
t^{*}\neq 0
. It implies that

\dot{F}(x^{*},t^{*})\leq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ F^{\prime}(x^{*},t^{*})=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ F^{\prime\prime}(x^{*},t^{*})\geq 0.

By the maximum principle at the point
(x^{*},t^{*})
we get

\displaystyle 0\geq\dot{F}(x^{*},t^{*})=-CF^{*}+L(F)(x^{*},t^{*})+a(x^{*},t^{*})F^{*}\geq(a(x^{*},t^{*})-C)F^{*}\geq 0

because
a(x^{*},t^{*})-C\leq-1
and
F^{*}<0
, and so we have a contradiction. Therefore,

\inf_{[1,b]\times[0,\infty)}\dot{\psi}(x,t)\geq 0,

and so we get the desired result.
∎

3.2.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=(\mu^{(\xi)}\tau+\lambda^{(\xi)})^{2}\left((27c_{\xi}^{2}+18-\mu^{(\xi)})\tau-\lambda^{(\xi)}\right)+27\tau(c_{\xi}\tau-A_{\xi})^{2}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -27\tau(\mu^{(\xi)}\tau+\lambda^{(\xi)})\left((2c_{\xi}^{2}+3)\tau-2c_{\xi}A_{\xi}\right).

We just have seen that two roots of the polynomial
\mathcal{R}_{0}
are
\tau=1
and
\tau=b^{3}
. By straightforward factorization, the third real root of the polynomial
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is given by

(3.5)

\tau=-b^{3}-1-\frac{54c_{\xi}(\mu^{(\xi)}-1)(\lambda^{(\xi)}c_{\xi}+A_{\xi})-3\lambda^{(\xi)}(\mu^{(\xi)}-3)(\mu^{(\xi)}-9)}{27c_{\xi}^{2}(\mu^{(\xi)}-1)^{2}-\mu^{(\xi)}(\mu^{(\xi)}-9)^{2}}.

Now using
(
3.4
)
, for the special values
q=b=3
and
p=2bq=18
, this third root is negative. It implies that the functions
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
and
\psi^{(\xi)}
do not intersect on
(1,b)
. Since
(\psi^{(\xi)})^{\prime}(1)=\frac{2\xi^{2}-\lambda^{(\xi)}}{2\xi}>0
and
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}^{\prime}(x)\to+\infty
as
x\to 1^{+}
, we must have
\psi^{(\xi)}<\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
on
(1,b)
for the above choice of
b,p,q
. We point out that this special choice gives us the first kind of dHYM instability (see
\lx@sectionsign

2.4
). The discussions above can be summarized as follows.

Lemma 3.3
.

For the special values
q=b=3
and
p=2bq=18
we have
\psi^{(\xi)}\leq\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
on
[1,b]
. In particular, the initial function
\psi_{0}
given by
(
3.3
)
satisfies
\psi_{0}\leq\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
on the interval
[1,b]
.

3.3.
Proof of the convergence

We are now in a position to prove the Theorem
1.2
. In fact, we prove the convergence result of the cotangent flow on
X=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3}
by generalizing the arguments of Proposition
4.11
in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{3}
with fixed Kähler class
\beta=b[H]-[E]
and a real
(1,1)
-class
\alpha=p[H]-q[E]
. Suppose
0<q\leq c_{q}+\sqrt{c_{q}^{2}+1}
(i.e.
(X,\alpha,\beta)
is not dHYM stable). Let
\psi(x,t)
solve the cotangent flow
(
2.6
)
with
\psi(x,0)=\psi_{0}(x)
satisfies the assumption
(
H1
)
. Then
\psi(x,t)
converges uniformly, and in
C^{1}_{loc}
on
(1,b]
, to the function
\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
.

Proof.

By the comparison and monotonicity from
\lx@sectionsign

3.1
, the point-wise limit
\psi_{\infty}(x):=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\psi(x,t)
exists, and it is a lower semi-continuous function on
[1,b]
. Moreover,
\psi_{\infty}\leq\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
on
[1,b]
with
\psi_{\infty}(b)=p
,
\psi_{\infty}(1)=q
. Also,
x\psi_{\infty}(x)
is increasing on
[1,b]
(by the Lemma
2.1
and equation
(
3.6
)
below). We let

s_{0}:=\lim_{x\rightarrow 1^{+}}\psi_{\infty}(x).

Since
\theta_{\omega}(\chi_{0})<\pi
(by the assumption
(
H1
)
) and this condition is preserved along the flow (cf.
[
13
, Lemma 3.2]
), we get

(3.6)

0<\inf_{x\in[1,b]}\theta(x,0)\leq\theta(x,t)\leq\sup_{x\in[1,b]}\theta(x,0)<\pi

for all
(x,t)\in[1,b]\times[0,\infty)
. It implies

(3.7)

\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}=3(\psi^{2}+x^{2})({\psi^{\prime}}^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sin(\theta(x,t))>0.

Moreover, setting
c(x,t):=\cot\theta(x,t)
, we have

\left(\mathrm{Re}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}=c(x,t)\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}.

We now prove the proposition through several claims.

Claim 1
.

There exists a sequence
t_{j}\rightarrow\infty
and
c_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
c(x,t_{j})\rightarrow c_{\infty}
for each
x\in(1,b)
with the convergence being uniform on any compact subset
K\subset(1,b)
.

Proof.

By the flow equation
(
2.6
)
and
\dot{\psi}\geq 0
, we have
c^{\prime}(x,t)\geq 0
, and so
c(x,t)
is increasing in
x
. Moreover,
c(x,t)
is a bounded function of
(x,t)
since
\theta(x,t)
is uniformly bounded on
(0,\pi)
. Let us now fix a
0<\delta\ll 1
and time
T>0
. Then

\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{1+\delta}^{b-\delta}\Big|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\Big|=\int_{1+\delta}^{b-\delta}\Big(\psi_{\infty}(x)-\psi(x,T)\Big)\,dx\leq C

for some uniform constant
C
independent of
T
. Now, since
Q(x)
is uniformly lower bounded away from zero on
[1+\delta,b-\delta]
we see that there exists a constant
C_{\delta}
independent of
T
such that

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{\infty}\Big(c(b-\delta,t)-c(1+\delta,t)\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{1+\delta}^{b-\delta}c^{\prime}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{1+\delta}^{b-\delta}\frac{1}{Q}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}dxdt

\displaystyle<C_{\delta}.

So there exists a sequence
t_{j}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Big(c(b-\delta,t_{j})-c(1+\delta,t_{j})\Big)=0.

By passing to a subsequence we may assume that
c(1+\delta,t_{j})\rightarrow c_{\infty}
. By monotonicity we then have that
c(x,t_{j})\rightarrow c_{\infty}
for all
x\in[1+\delta,b-\delta]
. Then a simple diagonalisation argument implies that
c_{\infty}
is independent of
\delta
and the claim is proved.
∎

Claim 2
.

For all
x\in(1,b)
we have

\mathrm{Re}(\psi_{\infty}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}-c_{\infty}\mathrm{Im}(\psi_{\infty}+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}=A_{\infty}

for some constant
A_{\infty}=\mathrm{Re}(s_{0}+\sqrt{-1})^{3}-c_{\infty}\mathrm{Im}(s_{0}+\sqrt{-1})^{3}
where
s_{0}=\psi_{\infty}(1+)
.

Proof.

For the sequence
\{t_{j}\}
in the previous claim, we let

\displaystyle h_{j}(x):=\mathrm{Re}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}-c_{\infty}\mathrm{Im}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3},

and we denote it’s limit by

h(x):=\mathrm{Re}(\psi_{\infty}(x)+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}-c_{\infty}\mathrm{Im}(\psi_{\infty}(x)+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}.

Once again let
0<\delta\ll 1
and take any
x\in[1+\delta,b-\delta]
. Then

\displaystyle\lvert h_{j}(x)-h_{j}(1+\delta)\rvert=\left\lvert\int_{1+\delta}^{x}h^{\prime}_{j}(x)dx\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{1+\delta}^{x}\left(\mathrm{Re}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}dx-c_{\infty}\int_{1+\delta}^{x}\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}dx\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1+\delta}^{x}\left\lvert(c(x,t_{j})-c_{\infty})\right\rvert\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in[1,b]}\left\lvert(c(x,t_{j})-c_{\infty})\right\rvert\int_{1+\delta}^{x}\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}\right)^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[1,b]}\left\lvert(c(x,t_{j})-c_{\infty})\right\rvert\left(\mathrm{Im}(\psi(x,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}x)^{3}-\mathrm{Im}(\psi(1+\delta,t_{j})+\sqrt{-1}(1+\delta))^{3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{x\in[1,b]}\left\lvert(c(x,t_{j})-c_{\infty})\right\rvert,

for some uniform constant
C>0
using Eq.
(
3.7
)
(or by the Lemma
2.1
). Letting
j\rightarrow\infty
, it implies that
h(x)=h(1+\delta)
for all
\delta>0
small and for all
x\in[1+\delta,b-\delta]
. Therefore,
h(x)=A_{\infty}
for all
x\in(1,b)
.
∎

Claim 3
.

Finally we prove that
\psi_{\infty}\equiv\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
.

Proof.

Firstly, note that since
\psi_{\infty}(1+)=s_{0}
and
\psi_{\infty}(b)=p
, we see that
\psi_{\infty}\equiv\tilde{\psi}_{s_{0}}.
Next, since
x\psi_{\infty}
is increasing, we have
s_{0}\geq\xi
. Next, by Lemma
3.1
we have that
\psi_{\infty}\leq\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
. On the other hand, by Lemma
2.7
, if
s_{0}>\xi
, then
\psi_{\infty}=\tilde{\psi}_{s_{0}}>\tilde{\psi}_{\xi}
on
[1,b)
, a contradiction. Hence
s_{0}=\xi
and this completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

∎

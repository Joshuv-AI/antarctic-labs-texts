Cost-effectiveness analysis of toripalimab and bevacizumab versus sorafenib for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma in China
Authors: Zhiwei Zheng, Tian Liu, Yuling Zhang, Huide Zhu, Ling Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study was to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab with bevacizumab compared to sorafenib for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) from the Chinese healthcare system perspective. To assess the cost and health outcomes of first-line advanced HCC therapy, we used a partitioned survival model.The model clinical dates were base on the Phase III HEPATORCH trials.The costs considered in this analysis such as drug costs, subsequent therapy cost, and the management of adverse events cost.Quality-adjusted life years (QALY) were used to measure health outcomes and the main economic assessment parameter was the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER).The willing to pay (WTP) level was 41,876.05 USD per QALY.The robustness and reliability of the model were evaluated using sensitivity analysis. The total cost of toripalimab plus bevacizumab treatment were 45,516.45 USD, while the sorafenib incurred costs of 24,579.02 USD. In compared to sorafenib, the toripalimab plus bevacizumab group achieved an additional 1.35 QALYs and an additional cost of 120,937.43 USD. The ICER was 15,509.21 USD per QALY for the toripalimab plus bevacizumab.One-way sensitivity analysis showed that our results were not reversed when the input paramete fluctuated ± 25%.Toripalimab plus bevacizumab had a 99% chance of being cost-effective at the WTP level. The therapy of toripalimab and bevacizumab was found to be a cost-effective treatment option for patients with advanced HCC in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-50815-5.Keywords: Advanced hepatocellular carcinoma, Cost-effectiveness, Toripalimab, Bevacizumab, SorafenibSubject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

The significant burden of disease and mortality associated with liver cancer made it a major and urgent worldwide health concern1. In 2020, there were 865,269 new cases recorded globally.It is the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with approximately 757,948 deaths annually2. Furthermore, liver cancer was the second most prevalent cause of cancer-related death and the fourth most common cancer in China, accounting for an anticipated 316,500 deaths and 367,700 new cases in 20223. The two most prevalent kinds of liver cancer are intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma(HCC)4.While intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma makes up around 10% to 15% of primary liver cancer cases, HCC makes up roughly 75% to 85% of cases5. HCC has a dismal prognosis, owing to the fact that it is commonly detected in later stages6. Current treatment for advanced HCC involving transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) and systemic therapies7,8. However, the 5-year survival rate of advanced HCC was just 10% to 20%9. Therefore, the exploration of novel therapeutic strategies and drugs has emerged as a prominent focus of advanced HCC.

The development of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) and programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitors, has profoundly revolutionized the area of advanced HCC care in the last five years.For instance, bevacizumab and atezolizumab treatment option have shown improvements in overall survival (OS) for advanced HCC10. Furthermore, durvalumab plus tremelimumab also demonstrated superior OS with advanced HCC11. Clinical trials have revealed promising objective response rates (ORRs) ranging from 26.7% to 43% with the use of a combination of ICIs, underscoring the escalating significance of immunotherapy as a pivotal modality in cancer treatment12. According to the Phase 3 HEPATORCH study, toripalimab and bevacizumab resulted in longer PFS than sorafenib. Furthermore, compared to sorafenib, the combination of toripalimab plus bevacizumab shown a substantial improvement in OS, with a median OS of 20.0 months against 14.5 months, respectively13.

This result suggests that toripalimab plus bevacizumab could be a promising therapy choice for advanced HCC.However, while evaluating a treatment plan, it is important to consider both its efficacy and the accompanying financial investment.Li et al. conducted a study to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab in combination with bevacizumab compared to sorafenib monotherapy as a first-line treatment for advanced HCC. Their findings suggested that toripalimab plus bevacizumab is a potentially cost-effective option for treating advanced HCC in China14. However, the study was limited to the data available up to the year 2024.In our current study, we aim to build upon the work of Li et al. by utilizing additional simulation extrapolation models and conducting a cost-benefit analysis with updated data for the year 2026. By incorporating more recent information and advanced modeling techniques, we seek to provide a comprehensive and up-to-date assessment of the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab in the treatment of advanced HCC from the Chinese healthcare system perspective.

Methods

Model structure

We created a partitioned survival model(PSM) to assess the outcomes and cost of therapies for advanced HCC.The model includes three separate and mutually incompatible health states—progression-free survival, disease progression, and death.Given the low five-year survival rate associated with advanced HCC, the model’s time horizon is set to 10 years.The regimen for the toripalimab plus bevacizumab and the sorafenib group was given 21-day cycles until disease progression. The selection of a 10-year horizon for the model is underpinned by several key considerations.Firstly, from a clinical perspective, the 5-year survival rate for patients with advanced HCC typically ranges from 10% to 20%. By extending the horizon to 10 years, we aim to capture the full survival cycle for over 95% of patients, thereby ensuring that the model adequately accounts for long-term outcomes. Drawing upon data from sources such as the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database and the China liver cancer registry, the 10-year horizon guards against underestimating the potential long-term survival benefits that may accrue from therapeutic interventions.Secondly, the choice of a 10-year timeframe also aligns with the feasibility of model extrapolation. By employing a Log-logistic model to fit the survival curve, we can draw upon real-world evidence, such as the ORIENT-32 study’s 3-year follow-up data which reported a median overall survival of 22.1 months. This approach allows us to verify the rationality of our extrapolations and guard against the risk of overextrapolation bias, thereby enhancing the robustness of the model’s projections.Lastly, by concluding the model after a 10-year period, we ensure that the vast majority (92%) of patients in the sorafenib group have reached the state of death, thus providing a comprehensive overview of the long-term outcomes associated with the intervention. The 2025 average exchange rate of 7.14 Ren Min Bin(RMB) to 1 United States dollar(USD)was used to convert all expenses from RMB to USD15. According to the recommendations of China’s pharmacoeconomic evaluation guidelines, the maximum willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) established in China is set at three times the country’s gross domestic product (GDP)16. Based on China’s projected GDP for 2025, this WTP threshold is set at $41,876.05 per QALY15.

Clinical parameters

The clinical parameters were obtained from the Phase 3 HEPATORCH trial.The model study populations were consistent with those enrolled in the clinical study. The baseline age of the model population was set to a uniform distribution of 50 to 66 years, which fully matches the interquartile range (IQR) of patient age in the Phase III HEPATORCH trial (median age 58.0 years, IQR 50.0–66.0 years).

Eligible patients were between the ages of 18 and 75 years and had a confirmed diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma. These patients were at Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage B or C, with disease that was not amenable to curative or locoregional therapy or had progressed following such therapy13. They had not previously received systemic treatment for advanced disease, had Child-Pugh Class A liver function without a history of hepatic encephalopathy13.

A total of 326 patients were randomly assigned to two treatment groups: 162 patients were allocated to receive toripalimab plus bevacizumab, while 164 patients were assigned to the sorafenib group13. The median age of patients in the toripalimab plus bevacizumab group was 58.0 years (interquartile range 50.0–66.0), while in sorafenib group it was 56.0 years (interquartile range 49.0–61.0). There were 282 (86%) male and 44 (14%)female included in the study13.

The regimen for the toripalimab plus bevacizumab included 240 mg of toripalimab and 15 mg/kg of bevacizumab once every three weeks13. The sorafenib group was given an oral dosage of 400 mg of sorafenib twice daily in 21-day cycles until disease progression13.

The percentage of subsequent therapy was 57% in the toripalimab plus bevacizumab group, in contrast to 69% in the sorafenib group13. More specifically, 53% in the toripalimab plus bevacizumab group (86 out of 162 patients) and 66% in the sorafenib group (108 out of 164 patients) underwent subsequent systematic antitumor treatments, including targeted therapy and immunotherapy13 .It was presumed that the subsequent antitumor therapy would entail the administration of either lenvatinib in the toripalimab plus bevacizumab group, or sintilimab plus bevacizumab in the sorafenib group.

The model identified treatment-related grade 3–4 serious adverse events (SAEs) based on their incidence exceeding 4% as observed in the phase 3 HEPATORCH trial.We assumed that SAE-related disutility is transient and that patients return to their pre-SAE baseline utility level after SAE resolution.We tested the impact of SAE parameter uncertainty in one-way sensitivity analysis (SAE incidence rate varied ± 25%) and probabilistic sensitivity analysis (SAE probability assigned a beta distribution).

The median duration of treatment was 29.9 weeks (interquartile range [IQR] 12.0–60.4) for toripalimab, 26.6 weeks (IQR 12.0–60.4) for bevacizumab, and 15.1 weeks (IQR 6.1–33.8) for sorafenib13.

Partitioned survival extrapolation parameters

GetData Graph Digitizer (version 2.25) was used to extract the partitioned survival extrapolation parameters from the Phase 3 HEPATORCH study17. Log-logistic, weibull, exponential, gamma, gompertz, and log-normal distributions were among the parametric survival distribution models to which the individual data points were rebuilt and fitted. A combination of visual examination and the identification of lower Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) values were used to choose the best fitting distribution18 (Supplementary Table S1).

The Phase 3 HEPATORCH study used in our analysis showed a delayed separation of overall survival (OS) curves between the intervention and control arms after 8 months of follow-up, indicating potential non-proportional hazards(Formal visual assessment of the PH assumption was performed via log-cumulative hazard plots supplementary Fig. 3. Non-parallel curves in the plots indicated a violation of the PH assumption. The log-cumulative hazard plots for OS showed that the curves of the two treatment groups crossed at the 8-month time point, which directly verified the time-dependent change of the hazard ratio between groups). Fitting separate models per arm allowed us to more accurately capture the distinct survival patterns of each treatment, especially the long-term survival tail effect of the toripalimab plus bevacizumab, which would be underestimated in a combined model assuming a constant treatment hazard ratio across the full time horizon.This approach is aligned with NICE Decision Support Unit (DSU) guidelines for oncology economic evaluation, which recommend separate model fitting per arm when non-proportional hazards are present to reduce extrapolation bias19. We tested 6 commonly used parametric distributions(log-logistic, weibull, exponential, gamma, gompertz, and log-normal distributions) for both PFS and OS in each arm. The Log-logistic was chosen as the best-fitting model based on the lowest AIC and BICvalues, as well as visual fit to the observed Kaplan-Meier data. All goodness-of-fit statistics are reported in Supplementary Table S1.

The low AIC and BIC values of the log-logistic distribution led to its selection as the best model. Additionally, internal verification also revealed that the OS and PFS curves closely resembled those observed in the Phase 3 HEPATORCH study (supplementary figures S1). During the follow-up period of the trial, survival rates were directly extracted from the survival curves using the model. The distribution of individuals in each health state was derived from these survival curves. Upon completion of the follow-up period, the model was run again, this time utilizing the best-fitting log-logistic distribution to estimate survival times and ascertain the survival function, denoted as S(t)(visual assessment at supplementary figures S2). The log-logistic distribution’s survival function at time t was S(t) = 1/(1 + λtγ)20. Table 1 shown the λ and γ parameters values.

SAE-related costs included costs of acute hospitalization, emergency care, diagnostic tests for SAE etiology, targeted intervention (e.g., antibiotics for infection, transfusion for severe hematological toxicity), We excluded indirect costs (e.g., productivity loss) as per the study perspective (healthcare system perspective).We assumed that SAE-related disutility is transient and that patients return to their pre-SAE baseline utility level after SAE resolution, except for SAEs leading to permanent disability.

The utility values utilized in this analysis were obtained from existing sources because no pertinent health utility values were provided for Phase 3 HEPATORCH study clinical studies. Table 2 shows a summary of the input parameters.

Sensitivity analysis

The influence of input parameters was assessed using probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) and one-way sensitivity analysis.In the one-way sensitivity analysis, the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) was evaluated by changing each model input parameter by ± 25%. Tornado plots were used to visualize the outcomes of this investigation.

Monte Carlo simulation methods with 1000 iterations were used in the PSA to account for input parameter uncertainty and variability. The parameters were assigned probabilistic distributions.The results were displayed using scatter plots.

The analysis adhered to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 (CHEERS 2022) guidelines31, and a completed checklist can be found in Supplementary Table S2.

Results

The base case results

The total costs of the toripalimab plus bevacizumab treatment group amounted to 45,516.45 USD, whereas the sorafenib treatment group incurred costs of 24,579.02 USD. The toripalimab plus bevacizumab group had an incremental gain of 1.35 QALYs compared to the sorafenib group.It was determined that the toripalimab plus bevacizumab therapy was cost-effective since the ICER was $15,509.21 USD/QALY, which is less than the WTP of 41,876.05 USD/QALY.Table 3 displayed the findings.

Discussion

Toripalimab with bevacizumab improved QALYs by 1.35 in our research when compared to sorafenib. The ICER was 15,509.21 USD/QALY, which is lower than the WTP of 41,876.05 USD/QALY. This suggests that the extra expenses for toripalimab with bevacizumab are reasonable considering the greater QALY advantages.Sensitivity studies revealed that even when all input parameters were altered within a range, the ICER values remained below the WTP threshold. This shows that changing these variables does not reverse the ICER results.

The HEPATORCH trial demonstrated the efficacy of toripalimab in combination with bevacizumab has exhibited superior outcomes in terms of PFS and OS compared tosorafenib therapy for advanced HCC. However, beyond the clinical efficacy and safety of a treatment regimen, the consideration of cost-effectiveness and affordability is essential when assessing the implementation of innovative therapies32. Thus, it is crucial to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of incorporating toripalimab with bevacizumab therapy for advanced HCC.

Our decision to utilize PSM in our cost-effectiveness analysis is in accordance with established best practices in oncology research. Two key considerations guided our selection of PSM methodology: Firstly, the use of PSM allows us to effectively leverage the evidence generated from the Phase 3 HEPATORCH study. This trial provides comprehensive individual-level data on PFS and OS, enabling us to directly incorporate parametric survival curves for these outcomes. By utilizing these data, we can allocate patients to distinct health states (progression-free, disease progression, death) without the need for additional assumptions regarding transition probabilities, thereby reducing potential uncertainties associated with traditional state-transition Markov models33.Secondly, PSM is endorsed as the preferred modeling approach for conducting cost-effectiveness analyses in oncology by prominent health technology assessment (HTA) bodies such as the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) and the Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health (CADTH)34,35. Particularly when utilizing head-to-head randomized controlled trial data on survival outcomes, PSM methodology is recommended for its ability to directly incorporate trial evidence and minimize reliance on extrapolations or assumptions.

QALY is a comprehensive metric that combines survival time and quality of life, calculated as the product of survival time and a quality of life score. Previous studies have reported the QALY values for traditional first-line targeted therapy sorafenib to be in the range of approximately 0.45–0.63, while the combination regimen of PD-1 inhibitors with antivascular agents has shown QALY values in the range of about 1.29–1.56.In our study, we observed longer QALY values in both treatment groups, which may be attributed to several factors. Firstly, the increased maturity of survival data, including longer follow-up periods, allowed for more accurate estimation of survival outcomes. By extrapolating 10-year survival data from clinical trials, our model was able to generate more comprehensive and reliable survival curves, leading to longer simulated survival times and ultimately higher QALY values.Additionally, the potential benefits of follow-up treatments may have contributed to the extended QALY values observed in our study. Existing literature suggests that the quality of life deteriorates rapidly in patients following the failure of sorafenib without effective second-line therapy. However, our study hypothesized that subsequent effective treatment regimens could further prolong survival and maintain or even improve quality of life, resulting in overall increased QALY values.

In health economics, the correlation between GDP per capita and the extra cost per QALY is crucial36. A suitable cost-effectiveness criterion for healthcare treatments must be established by making sure that the incremental cost per QALY stays proportional to the GDP per capita37. In accordance with the guidance provided by the World Health Organization, it is imperative to calibrate the threshold for cost-effectiveness by ensuring that the incremental cost per QALY aligns with the GDP per capita of a given country. This alignment is crucial for both sustainability and optimization of healthcare resource allocation. Specifically, it is recommended that the incremental cost per QALY falls within a range of 1 to 3 times the GDP per capita38. In this analysis, we established the WTP criterion at three times GDP at 41,876.05 per QALY.When compared to sorafenib, the ICER for toripalimab plus bevacizumab was 15,509.21 USD per QALY, which was below the WTP. This suggests that using toripalimab with bevacizumab for advanced HCC therapy may be considered cost-effective.Furthermore, it is important to note that our results continue to support the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab for advanced HCC treatment in China, despite the fact that some researchers and experts have suggested lowering the WTP threshold to 1.5–2.0 times the GDP due to the differences in GDP among various regions of China in the current economic landscape39,40.

The high prices of medications have led to limitations in healthcare access and have restricted the availability of life-saving interventions for those who require them the most41. In response to the escalating financial strain imposed by the rising costs of pharmaceuticals on patients and the healthcare system, China has proactively introduced a centralized procurement system for essential medications at the national level. This strategic approach entails consolidating demand and capitalizing on the collective purchasing power to engage in negotiations with pharmaceutical manufacturers and suppliers, with the objective of securing lower prices42. This measure not only serves to curtail overall drug expenditure but also establishes a more equitable and transparent pricing framework.By centralizing procurement, the government can optimize the drug supply chain, facilitate market entry for generic drugs, and enhance the efficiency of drug distribution. Through the implementation of a transparent bidding process and the standardization of procurement procedures, the system fosters fair competition among suppliers, thereby driving prices down while upholding quality standards.To reduce the cost of high-value pharmaceuticals and enhance drug accessibility, the National Health Administration established a trial program based on volume procurement in 201943.By using this calculated tactic, the Chinese government may negotiate more advantageous medicine prices with pharmaceutical corporations on a large scale by using its significant purchasing power. The Chinese government has effectively achieved noteworthy cost reductions for essential medications, as illustrated by its successful negotiation that resulted in a price decrease of over 50% for gefitinib. Following this initiative, the availability of gefitinib has also significantly increased to 60.33% after the year 202044.

Several studies have examined the cost-effectiveness of other PD-1 inhibitors in the treatment of advanced HCC. When evaluating the cost-effectiveness of nivolumab against sorafenib for the first therapy of patients with advanced HCC, the results showed that nivolumab was not a cost-effectivetreatment45. Furthermore, Xiang et al. used a survival model to discover that treatment with camrelizumab with rivoceranib was related with greater costs and QALYs than treatment with sorafenib. The ICER was $34,897 USD per QALY gained.The findings imply that using camrelizumab with rivoceranib for advanced HCC was a cost-effective choice in China46. Li et al. conducted a comprehensive study to assess the cost-effectiveness of the combination therapy of toripalimab with bevacizumab in comparison to sorafenib monotherapy as a first-line treatment for advanced HCC14. Their analysis indicated that the utilization of toripalimab plus bevacizumab may represent a favorable cost-effective strategy for managing advanced HCC in the context of the healthcare system in China.However, it is important to note that there have been recent updates and advancements in reference functions available for our research. This is due to the fact that the previously cited articles were published earlier and utilized exchange rates and willingness-to-pay (WTP) values from 2024. In contrast, our study incorporates the latest data from 2026 for the toripalimab prices, among other variables, thereby enhancing the precision and relevance of the cost-effectiveness analysis. Furthermore, we introduced a novel hypothesis regarding subsequent treatment therapy, diverging from the existing literature. This enabled us to explore the cost-effectiveness analysis of toripalimab from various viewpoints.Moreover, our methodology involved a unique calculation of transition probabilities (Log-logistic vs. Log-normal), allowing for a comprehensive examination of the cost-effectiveness analysis of toripalimab from diverse perspectives. Our research delved into the cost-effectiveness analysis of toripalimab over time, and the results were generally congruent with the existing literature.

It is important to underscore that while cost-effectiveness analysis, such as the findings presented in this study, provides valuable insights, it should not be the sole determining factor for the availability of toripalimab in combination with bevacizumab. It is imperative to consider other aspects such as cost-benefit analysis when making clinical decisions. Furthermore, it is essential to exercise caution in clinical practice and carefully consider patient-specific factors. For instance, it is crucial to note that toripalimab plus bevacizumab may not be suitable for individuals with combined esophageal and gastric varices, as an alternative treatment option such as sorafenib may be more appropriate in such cases.

The rationale behind assigning Beta distributions to probabilities, such as serious adverse event (SAE) incidence rates, in pharmacoeconomic models lies in the fact that Beta distributions naturally span the range of 0 to 1, which aligns with probability values. Additionally, Beta distributions are the standard choice for representing probability parameters in such models.Similarly, when considering health state utility values and SAE disutilities, the rationale for employing Beta distributions stems from the bounded nature of EQ-5D-measured utility values between 0 (indicating death) and 1 (representing full health). This makes Beta distributions a suitable choice for accurately capturing the distribution of these parameters.On the other hand, for costs such as drug costs, administration costs, and medical resource use costs, the rationale for utilizing Gamma distributions rests on their right-skewed nature and bound at 0, which accurately mirrors the non-negative and right-skewed characteristics often observed in healthcare cost data. By using Gamma distributions, the model can effectively capture the distribution of costs while preventing the occurrence of negative cost values, which may be a limitation when employing normal distributions.

There are many restrictions on this study.First, the 10-year follow-up period used in the analysis comes from model simulations based on current data from the HEPATORCH clinical trial, which may bias the results.Secondly, while we based our hypothesis regarding subsequent anticancer therapy on clinical trials, it is important to note that in clinical practice, the availability of additional options for subsequent anticancer therapy could influence the cost calculations. However, we carried out sensitivity tests to assess the influence of these parameters and found that did not weaken the validity of our conclusions.Lastly, this study ignored grade 1 and 2 occurrences in favor of concentrating only on grade 3 or 4 major adverse events(AE) in the model.Fortunately, one-way sensitivity analysis showed that these parameters related to grade 3 or grade 4 serious adverse events had no discernible impact on the study’s overall findings.AE management plays a crucial role in medical decision-making, particularly in the context of cost-effectiveness analysis. The ability to effectively manage AEs is essential for achieving a balance between cost control and enhancing health outcomes. The fundamental principle is rooted in the evidence-based management of AE, which aims to optimize the allocation of medical resources by mitigating the high cost-low benefit scenario associated with serious AEs.The impact of AE management on cost-effectiveness revolves around striking a delicate balance between resource allocation and risk mitigation. Through the reduction of serious AEs, enhancement of treatment adherence, extension of patient survival, and improvement of quality of life, unnecessary costs can be significantly curtailed while health benefits are maximized. This leads to an improved cost-effectiveness profile, characterized by lower ICER and higher QALY.

Conclusion

Toripalimab with bevacizumab was a cost-effective therapy for advanced HCC in China compared to sorafenib. These findings are useful for directing therapy decisions for patients with advanced HCC.

Abstract
. A CR-dynamical system is a pair ( X , G ) {\left({X,G}\right)} , where X X is a compact metric space and G G is a closed relation on X X . We introduce four ( i , j ) {\left({i,j}\right)} -shadowing properties, obtained by independently varying the quantifiers in the definitions of pseudo-orbit and shadowing. These properties coincide with the classical shadowing property for single-valued systems, while ( 2 , 2 ) {\left({2,2}\right)} -shadowing agrees with the established notion of shadowing for set-valued dynamical systems. We investigate how the classical characterisations of shadowing for the identity map, periodic maps and isometries extend to closed relations. In particular, we obtain rigidity results for ( 2 , 1 ) {\left({2,1}\right)} -shadowing and positive results for ( 1 , 2 ) {\left({1,2}\right)} -shadowing on totally disconnected spaces. Finally, whenever the infinite Mahavier product is non-empty, we prove that a closed relation has the ( 2 , 2 ) {\left({2,2}\right)} -shadowing property if, and only if, its associated Mahavier dynamical system has the shadowing property. This removes the surjectivity hypothesis from a recent corresponding result for set-valued dynamical systems.

1.
Introduction

An important concept in classical dynamics is the shadowing property, introduced independently by Anosov
[
4
]
and Bowen
[
9
]
, with its inception coming from computers. As such, topological dynamical systems with the shadowing property are suited for computer simulations, yielding fruitful applications
[
16
,
17
,
26
,
27
]
. In classical dynamics, we wish to calculate trajectories of points within our space, but computers make computational errors. This derives the notion of pseudo-orbits which have an error term. The shadowing property then aims to ask the question: given a pseudo-orbit, can we find an actual trajectory with close distance to the pseudo-orbit? Shadowing interacts nicely with other important dynamical properties. In a topological dynamical system with the shadowing property, many dynamical properties are equivalent
[
5
,
18
,
20
]
, allowing for richer dynamics. Standard references for shadowing include Palmer
[
22
]
and Pilyugin
[
24
]
.

Beyond topological dynamics, the shadowing property is widely studied in hyperbolic dynamics, with Anosov’s Shadowing Theorem
[
10
, Corollary
5.3.2
]
(also referred to as the Shadowing Lemma) being an important result. Due to the importance of such a property, it is no surprise shadowing has been generalised to set-valued dynamical systems
[
19
,
29
,
25
,
15
,
28
,
30
,
23
,
31
]
.

Banič, Erceg, Rogina, and Kennedy’s paper “Minimal dynamical systems with closed relations”
[
8
]
formally introduces CR-dynamical systems
{\left({X,G}\right)}
, where
X
is a compact metric space and
G
is a closed relation on
X
. Recently, this has led to the further study of CR-dynamical systems
[
1
,
6
,
12
]
, generalising transitivity and transitive points from topological dynamical systems to CR-dynamical systems. Studying the dynamics of closed relations and set-valued functions has proved useful, including in applied areas such as economics. The Christiano-Harrison model in macroeconomics
[
11
]
is one such example.

In this paper, we introduce shadowing for CR-dynamical systems. The closed-relation and set-valued viewpoints are closely related. Indeed, by
[
13
, Theorem
1.2
]
, a set-valued function
F\colon X\to 2^{X}
is upper semi-continuous if, and only if, its graph is closed in
X\times X
. Consequently, when
{\operatorname{dom}{\left({G}\right)}}=X
, the relation
G
is precisely the graph of a compact-valued upper
semi-continuous function, and hence gives a set-valued dynamical system. Allowing
{\operatorname{dom}{\left({G}\right)}}\neq X
amounts to permitting partial dynamics, in which points may have no successor at all. We use closed relations not to distinguish full-domain CR-dynamical systems from set-valued dynamical systems, but to accommodate partial dynamics, inverse relations and relation composition naturally.

We introduce four
{\left({i,j}\right)}
-shadowing properties by independently varying the quantifiers in the definitions of pseudo-orbit and shadowing. The
{\left({2,2}\right)}
-shadowing property is precisely the shadowing property already studied for set-valued dynamical systems. All four notions coincide with the classical shadowing property when
G
is the graph of a single-valued function.

It is known that the identity map on a compact metric space has the shadowing property if, and only if, the space is totally disconnected
[
20
, Theorem 3]
. The same characterisation holds for periodic maps
[
20
, Theorem 5]
and, more generally, for equicontinuous surjections
[
21
, Theorem 4]
; in particular, it holds for isometries.

The main purpose of this paper is to understand how the classical characterisations of shadowing for the identity map, periodic maps, and isometries change when a self-map is replaced by a closed relation. The quantifier choices are useful for different reasons: the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property is rigid enough to detect branching, the
{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing property gives natural positive generalisations, and the
{\left({2,2}\right)}
-shadowing property is the established notion from set-valued dynamics. Our first main theorem generalises the identity-map characterisation.

Theorem A
.

Let
{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system with
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G
.

(1)

If
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property, then
G=\Delta_{X}
and
X
is totally disconnected.

(2)

If
X
is totally disconnected, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing property.

Both statements are sharp with respect to the positions of the quantifiers.

The periodic-map characterisation suggests replacing the hypothesis
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G
with the existence of a positive integer
n
such that
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G^{n}
. The rigidity of the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property as in
{\left({1}\right)}
of Theorem
A
survives. For the other direction in the classical periodic-map characterisation, uniform continuity is employed in the proof. With
G^{0}=\Delta_{X}
, we define set-valued functions
F_{k}\colon X\to 2^{X}
for each
k\in{\left\{{0,\ldots,n}\right\}}
by

F_{k}{\left({x}\right)}={\left\{{{y\in X}\,\left|\vphantom{{y\in X}{y\in G^{k}{\left({x}\right)}\text{ and }x\in G^{n-k}{\left({y}\right)}}}\right.{y\in G^{k}{\left({x}\right)}\text{ and }x\in G^{n-k}{\left({y}\right)}}}\right\}}

for each
x\in X
. It is straightforward to check that
F_{k}
is upper semi-continuous for each
k
. We call
F_{k}
uniformly upper semi-continuous (see
[
2
]
) if, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for all
x,y\in X
,
d{\left({x,y}\right)}\leq\delta
implies

F_{k}{\left({x}\right)}\subseteq\bigcup_{z\in F_{k}{\left({y}\right)}}B{\left({z,\epsilon}\right)}.

We now give our second main theorem generalising the periodic-map characterisation.

Theorem B
.

Let
{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system, such that there exists positive integer
n
with
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G^{n}
, and let
F_{k}
be as above.

(1)

If
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property, then
G={\Gamma{\left({f}\right)}}
for a periodic homeomorphism
f\colon X\to X
with
f^{n}=\emph{id}_{X}
, and
X
is totally disconnected.

(2)

If
X
is totally disconnected and
F_{k}
is uniformly upper semi-continuous for each
0\leq k\leq n
, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing property.

For the graph
{\Gamma{\left({f}\right)}}
of a periodic map
f
, we have
F_{k}{\left({x}\right)}={\left\{{f^{k}{\left({x}\right)}}\right\}}
. Thus, uniform upper
semi-continuity of each
F_{k}
follows from uniform continuity of the iterates of
f
. Theorem
B
recovers both directions of the classical periodic-map theorem. We also give an example in which
{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing holds although one of the set-valued functions
F_{k}
is not uniformly upper semi-continuous. We now give our third main theorem generalising the isometry characterisation, which has a connection to Mahavier dynamical systems (see
[
7
]
).

Theorem C
.

Let
{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system such that there exists an isometry
f\colon X\to X
with
{\Gamma{\left({f}\right)}}\subseteq G
. Then:

(1)

{\left({X,f}\right)}
has the shadowing property if, and only if,
X
is totally disconnected if, and only if, the shift map on
X_{{\Gamma{\left({f}\right)}}}^{+}
has the shadowing property;

(2)

if
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property, then
G={\Gamma{\left({f}\right)}}
and
X
is totally disconnected;

(3)

if
X
is totally disconnected, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing property;

(4)

X_{G}^{+}
is totally disconnected if, and only if,
X
is totally disconnected.

Very recently, Zhao and Wang
[
31
, Corollary
3.4
]
proved, for a surjective upper semi-continuous set-valued map

, that
F
has shadowing if, and only if, the shift on the generalized inverse limit of its inverse multifunction has shadowing. Our final main theorem removes the surjectivity hypothesis.

Theorem D
.

Let
{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system such that
X_{G}^{+}\neq\varnothing
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,2}\right)}
-shadowing property.

(2)

The Mahavier dynamical system
{\left({X_{G}^{+},\sigma_{G}^{+}}\right)}
has the shadowing property.

Proposition 4.2

{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system such that
G=\Delta_{X}\cup{\left({X\times{\left\{{x}\right\}}}\right)}
for some
x\in X
. Then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,1}\right)}
-shadowing property.

Proof.

Suppose
\epsilon>0
. Let
\delta\in{\left({0,\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\right)}
. If
{\left\langle{{x_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{x_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
is a
{\left({\delta,1}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit, then
d{\left({x,x_{n+1}}\right)}\leq\delta
and
d{\left({x_{n},x_{n+1}}\right)}\leq\delta
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

d{\left({x,x_{n}}\right)}\leq d{\left({x,x_{n+1}}\right)}+d{\left({x_{n+1},x_{n}}\right)}\leq 2\delta<\epsilon

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, implying
x

{\left({\epsilon,1}\right)}
-shadows
{\left\langle{{x_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{x_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
. Thus,
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,1}\right)}
-shadowing property.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
{\left({X,G}\right)}
is a CR-dynamical system with
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G
and
j\in{\left\{{1,2}\right\}}
. Then,

(1)

if
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,2}\right)}
-shadowing property, then

need not be totally disconnected;

(2)

if
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,j}\right)}
-shadowing property, then

need not be totally disconnected;

(3)

if
X
is totally disconnected, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
need not have the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property;

(4)

if
X
is totally disconnected, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
need not have the
{\left({1,1}\right)}
-shadowing property;

(5)

if
X
is totally disconnected, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
need not have the
{\left({2,2}\right)}
-shadowing property.

Proof.

For
{\left({1}\right)}
, take
X=[0,1]
and
G=X\times X
.

For
{\left({2}\right)}
, take
X=[0,1]
and
G=\Delta_{X}\cup{\left({X\times{\left\{{0}\right\}}}\right)}
, then apply Proposition
4.2
.

For
{\left({3}\right)}
, take
X={\left\{{0,1}\right\}}
with discrete topology and
G=X\times X
.

For
{\left({4}\right)}
, take
X
to be the Cantor set and
G=\Delta_{X}\cup{\left({X\times{\left\{{0}\right\}}}\right)}\cup{\left({{\left\{{0}\right\}}\times X}\right)}
. Choose
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}
, and let
\delta>0
be arbitrary. There exists

such that
\frac{4}{3^{n}}\leq\delta
. Observe
{\left\langle{{\frac{2}{3^{k}}}\,\left|\vphantom{{\frac{2}{3^{k}}}{k\geq n}}\right.{k\geq n}}\right\rangle}
is a
{\left({\delta,1}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit. To derive a contradiction, suppose there exists

which
{\left({\epsilon,1}\right)}
-shadows
{\left\langle{{\frac{2}{3^{k}}}\,\left|\vphantom{{\frac{2}{3^{k}}}{k\geq n}}\right.{k\geq n}}\right\rangle}

. But this means
d{\left({\frac{2}{3^{n+2}},0}\right)}<\epsilon
and

. Applying the triangle inequality yields

, a contradiction. Thus,
{\left({X,G}\right)}
does not have the
{\left({1,1}\right)}
-shadowing property.

For
{\left({5}\right)}
, take
X
to be the Cantor set, and
G=\Delta_{X}\cup{\left\{{{{\left({a_{n},a_{n+1}}\right)}}\,\left|\vphantom{{{\left({a_{n},a_{n+1}}\right)}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\}}\cup{\left\{{{{\left({b_{n},b_{n+1}}\right)}}\,\left|\vphantom{{{\left({b_{n},b_{n+1}}\right)}}{n\in\mathbb{Z}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{Z}}}\right\}}

{\left\langle{{a_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{a_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
and
{\left\langle{{b_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{b_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{Z}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{Z}}}\right\rangle}
are sequences in
X
with the properties,

•

for each
{\left({n,m}\right)}\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
a_{n}<a_{n+1}
and
b_{m}<b_{m+1}
;

•

a_{0}=0
,
\lim_{n\to+\infty}a_{n}=\frac{2}{9}=\lim_{n\to-\infty}b_{n}
and
\lim_{n\to+\infty}b_{n}=\frac{1}{3}
.

Indeed,
{\left({X,G}\right)}
is a CR-dynamical system. Now, let
\epsilon=\frac{1}{27}
, and
\delta>0
be arbitrary. There exists
{\left({n,m}\right)}\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that
{\left\langle{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},b_{m},b_{m+1},\ldots}\right\rangle}
is a
{\left({\delta,2}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit. However, it is not
{\left({\epsilon,2}\right)}
-shadowed by any point in
X
: to derive a contradiction, suppose
y

{\left({\epsilon,2}\right)}
-shadows
{\left\langle{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},b_{m},b_{m+1},\ldots}\right\rangle}

. Since

, there exists
\ell\geq m

. There exists
y_{\ell}\in G^{k}{\left({y}\right)}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
d{\left({b_{\ell},y_{\ell}}\right)}<\frac{1}{27}
, by the assumption
y

{\left({\epsilon,2}\right)}
-shadows our sequence. But then

\frac{1}{3}=d{\left({\frac{1}{3},0}\right)}\leq d{\left({\frac{1}{3},b_{\ell}}\right)}+d{\left({b_{\ell},y_{\ell}}\right)}+d{\left({y_{\ell},0}\right)}<\frac{1}{27}+\frac{1}{27}+\frac{2}{9}=\frac{8}{27},

a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
{\left({X,G}\right)}
is a CR-dynamical system with
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G
. If
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property, then
G=\Delta_{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property. To derive a contradiction, suppose
G\neq\Delta_{X}
. Then, there exists
x\in X
such that there are
y\neq z\in G{\left({x}\right)}
. There exists
\epsilon>0
such that
d{\left({y,z}\right)}\geq\epsilon
. Let
\eta\in{\left({0,\frac{\epsilon}{4}}\right)}
. There exists
\delta>0
satisfying the
{\left({2,1}\right)}
-shadowing property for
\eta
. Observe
{\left\langle{x,y,y,\ldots}\right\rangle}
and
{\left\langle{x,z,z,\ldots}\right\rangle}
are both
{\left({\delta,2}\right)}
-pseudo-orbits. There exists
u,v\in X
which
{\left({\eta,1}\right)}
-shadow
{\left\langle{x,y,y,\ldots}\right\rangle}
and
{\left\langle{x,z,z\ldots}\right\rangle}
, respectively. Then,

d{\left({y,z}\right)}\leq d{\left({y,u}\right)}+d{\left({u,x}\right)}+d{\left({x,v}\right)}+d{\left({v,z}\right)}<4\eta<\epsilon,

a contradiction. Thus, it must be the case

, as desired.
∎

Indeed, together Propositions

,

, and

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
{\left({X,f}\right)}
be a topological dynamical system. Then
{\left({X,f}\right)}
has the shadowing property if, and only if,
{\left({X,f^{k}}\right)}
has the shadowing property for all

.

Note Lemma

has been generalised in set-valued dynamical systems
[

]
.

If

{\left({X,G^{k}}\right)}

. We now generalise Lemma

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system such that
D_{G}\neq\varnothing
, and
j\in{\left\{{1,2}\right\}}
. Then,
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,j}\right)}
-shadowing property if, and only if,
{\left({X,G^{k}}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,j}\right)}
-shadowing property for all positive integers
k
.

Proof.

Necessity is obvious, so we only provide proof of the forward direction; to this end, suppose
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,j}\right)}
-shadowing property, and that
k
is a positive integer. Let
\epsilon>0
. Then, there exists
\delta>0
satisfying the
{\left({2,j}\right)}
-shadowing property. Suppose
{\left\langle{{x_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{x_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
is a
{\left({\delta,2}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit in
{\left({X,G^{k}}\right)}
. That is to say, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
y\in G^{k}{\left({x_{n}}\right)}
such that
d{\left({y,x_{n+1}}\right)}\leq\delta
. Construct a sequence
{\left\langle{{y_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{y_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
in
{\operatorname{dom}{\left({G}\right)}}
such that we take
y_{0}=x_{0}
,
y_{1}\in G{\left({y_{0}}\right)}
,
y_{2}\in G{\left({y_{1}}\right)}
,
\ldots
,
y_{k-1}\in G{\left({y_{k-2}}\right)}
,
y_{k}=x_{1}
, and so on (observe
y_{nk}=x_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
). Choose this in such a way so that, for each positive integer
n
,
y_{nk-1}\in G^{k-1}{\left({x_{n-1}}\right)}
such that there is
y\in G{\left({y_{nk-1}}\right)}
with
d{\left({y,x_{n}}\right)}\leq\delta
(which we can do because
{\left\langle{{x_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{x_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
is a
{\left({\delta,2}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit of
{\left({X,G^{k}}\right)}
).

Now, we claim that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
y\in G{\left({y_{n}}\right)}
such that
d{\left({y,y_{n+1}}\right)}\leq\delta
. That is to say, we claim that
{\left\langle{{y_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{y_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
is a
{\left({\delta,2}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit in
{\left({X,G}\right)}
. Notice that if
n+1
is not divisible by
k
, that
y_{n+1}\in G{\left({y_{n}}\right)}
. If on the other hand
n+1
is divisible by
k
, say
n+1=mk
, then
y_{n+1}=x_{m}
and
y_{n}\in G^{k-1}{\left({x_{m-1}}\right)}
. By our construction, there exists
y\in G{\left({y_{n}}\right)}\subseteq G^{k}{\left({x_{m-1}}\right)}
with
d{\left({y,y_{n+1}}\right)}=d{\left({y,x_{m}}\right)}\leq\delta
. Hence, the claim follows.

Thus, there exists
z\in D_{G}=D_{G^{k}}
which
{\left({\epsilon,j}\right)}
-shadows
{\left\langle{{y_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{y_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
in
{\left({X,G}\right)}
. Furthermore, it is clear
z

{\left({\epsilon,j}\right)}
-shadows
{\left\langle{{x_{n}}\,\left|\vphantom{{x_{n}}{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
in
{\left({X,G^{k}}\right)}
, and thus
{\left({X,G^{k}}\right)}
has the
{\left({2,j}\right)}
-shadowing property.
∎

Proposition 4.8

{\left({X,G}\right)}
be a CR-dynamical system such that

{\Gamma{\left({f}\right)}}=G

.

. If

{\left({2}\right)}

.

Proposition 4.10
.

Suppose
{\left({X,G}\right)}
is a CR-dynamical system with
\Delta_{X}\subseteq G^{n}
for some positive integer
n
. If
X
is totally disconnected and
F_{k}
is uniformly upper semi-continuous for each
k\in{\left\{{0,\ldots,n}\right\}}
, then
{\left({X,G}\right)}
has the
{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing property.

Proof.

We abstract the proof strategies in
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
20
, Theorem 5]
. Suppose
X
is totally
disconnected and that
F_{k}
is uniformly upper semi-continuous for
each
k\in{\left\{{0,\ldots,n}\right\}}
.

We show that
{\left({X,{\Gamma{\left({F_{1}}\right)}}}\right)}
has the

{\left({1,2}\right)}
-shadowing property. Let
\epsilon>0
. Since
X
is a
compact totally disconnected metric space, there exists a finite
pairwise disjoint clopen cover
\mathscr{U}
of
X
such that
every member of
\mathscr{U}
has diameter strictly less than

\epsilon
. Choose
\eta>0
such that

d{\left({x,y}\right)}<\eta\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \text{$x$ and $y$ belong to the same member of $\mathscr{U}$.}

Such an
\eta
exists because
\mathscr{U}
is finite and its
members are pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
X
.

Since
F_{k}
is uniformly upper semi-continuous for each

k\in{\left\{{0,\ldots,n}\right\}}
, there exists
\delta\in{\left({0,\eta}\right)}
such that

d{\left({x,y}\right)}\leq\delta\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ F_{k}{\left({x}\right)}\subseteq\bigcup_{z\in F_{k}{\left({y}\right)}}B{\left({z,\eta}\right)}

for each
x,y\in X
and every
k\in{\left\{{0,\ldots,n}\right\}}
. Since

F_{0}=F_{n}=\text{id}_{X}
, extend the notation by setting
F_{k}:=F_{k\bmod n}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a subset
A\subseteq X
, write

F_{k}(A)=\bigcup_{a\in A}F_{k}{\left({a}\right)}.

Suppose
{\left\langle{{x_{i}}\,\left|\vphantom{{x_{i}}{i\in\mathbb{N}}}\right.{i\in\mathbb{N}}}\right\rangle}
is a

{\left({\delta,1}\right)}
-pseudo-orbit of
{\left({X,{\Gamma{\left({F_{1}}\right)}}}\right)}
. Then

d{\left({a,x_{i+1}}\right)}\leq\delta

for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and every
a\in F_{1}{\left({x_{i}}\right)}
.
Consequently, uniform upper semi-continuity gives

F_{k}{\left({F_{1}{\left({x_{i}}\right)}}\right)}\subseteq\bigcup_{z\in F_{k}{\left({x_{i+1}}\right)}}B{\left({z,\eta}\right)}

for every
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We next show that

F_{k+1}{\left({x}\right)}\subseteq F_{k}{\left({F_{1}{\left({x}\right)}}\right)}

for every
x\in X
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We need only consider
k\in{\left\{{0,\ldots,n-1}\right\}}
. Let

y\in F_{k+1}{\left({x}\right)}
. There exists

{\left\langle{x=w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}=y,\ldots,w_{n}=x}\right\rangle}\in{\bigstar^{n}_{i=0}{G}}.

Notice
z=w_{1}\in F_{1}{\left({x}\right)}
and
y\in F_{k}{\left({z}\right)}
. Consequently,

y\in F_{k}{\left({z}\right)}\subseteq F_{k}{\left({F_{1}{\left({x}\right)}}\right)},

as required. It follows that

Proposition 5.3

{\left({X,G}\right)}

{\left\{{{{\left({x,y,a,b}\right)}\in X^{4}}\,\left|\vphantom{{{\left({x,y,a,b}\right)}\in X^{4}}{{\left({a,b}\right)}\in G,\ d{\left({x,a}\right)}\leq\delta,\ d{\left({y,b}\right)}\leq\delta}}\right.{{\left({a,b}\right)}\in G,\ d{\left({x,a}\right)}\leq\delta,\ d{\left({y,b}\right)}\leq\delta}}\right\}}

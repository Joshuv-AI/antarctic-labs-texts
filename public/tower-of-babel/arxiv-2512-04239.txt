Abstract
Periodic operation often emerges as the economically optimal mode in industrial processes, particularly under varying economic or environmental conditions. This paper proposes a robust model predictive control (MPC) framework for uncertain systems modeled as polytopic linear differential inclusions (LDIs), where the dynamics evolve as convex combinations of finitely many affine control systems with additive disturbances. The robust control problem is reformulated as a convex optimization program by optimizing over configuration-constrained polytopic tubes and tracks a periodic trajectory that is optimal for a given economic criterion. Artificial variables embedded in the formulation ensure recursive feasibility and robust constraint satisfaction when the economic criterion is updated online, while guaranteeing convergence to the corresponding optimal periodic tube when the criterion remains constant. To improve computational efficiency, we introduce a quadratic over-approximation of the periodic cost under a Lipschitz continuity assumption, yielding a Quadratic Program (QP) formulation that preserves the above theoretical guarantees. The effectiveness and scalability of the approach are demonstrated on a benchmark example and a ball-plate system with eight states.



1  Introduction

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is widely adopted for constrained multivariable systems thanks to its ability to optimize performance online while enforcing state and input constraints
[
26
]
. In many processes, periodic operation often emerges as the economically optimal mode
[
19
]
, with the notable exception of linear time-invariant (LTI) systems with convex stage costs, where the optimal operation reduces to a unique steady state
[
14
]
. Such periodicity typically arises from exogenous factors—e.g., cyclic demands, time-varying prices—or from the nonlinear nature of underlying dynamics
[
5
]
.

Economic MPC (E‑MPC)
[
10
]
formalizes this by embedding an economic stage cost that optimizes transient behavior and asymptotic performance. Under strict dissipativity, E‑MPC guarantees convergence to an economically optimal equilibrium in the time‑invariant case
[
2
]
, and extensions to periodic operation exist for nonlinear systems
[
38
]
. Furthermore, including an artificial periodic trajectory as an online decision variable preserves recursive feasibility when the optimal periodic orbit changes, and—under suitable constraints on this trajectory and explicit terminal ingredients—ensures closed‑loop performance and stability
[
17
]
.

An alternative is to track a periodic solution that is optimal for a given economic criterion
[
34
,
24
]
. Two-layer architectures
[
34
]
compute this solution via an upper-level dynamic real-time optimizer (DRTO) and track it with a lower-level MPC
[
16
]
; such schemes achieve local equivalence with E-MPC
[
37
]
, but their performance may degrade under abrupt parameter changes or model mismatch, potentially compromising stability. Single-layer formulations integrate these tasks into one optimization problem
[
35
,
20
]
, ensuring recursive feasibility and convergence to the best admissible periodic operation
[
23
]
. However, these formulations introduce new challenges: online updates of the economic criterion increase problem dimensionality, and the associated cost is often non-quadratic, which precludes a Quadratic Program (QP) structure for linear systems. To address this, first-order Taylor approximations of the economic objective
[
6
]
have been proposed, recovering a QP structure at the expense of linear convergence to the economic optimum—a strategy proven practical in industrial applications
[
7
]
.

Uncertainty introduces additional challenges in periodic MPC design
[
38
]
. Disturbances, unmodeled dynamics, and parameter variations prevent the system from following a single periodic trajectory; instead, one can only guarantee that the state remains within a bounded region. Consequently, the control objective shifts from tracking a trajectory to tracking a
periodic sequence of sets
that is robustly forward invariant under all admissible uncertainties. Tube-based MPC (TMPC) addresses this by optimizing over Robust Forward Invariant Tubes (RFITs) that contain all possible trajectories under disturbances. Among polyhedral tube parameterizations—see
[
15
]
for a recent survey—rigid
[
27
,
28
]
, homothetic
[
29
,
18
]
, and elastic
[
30
,
11
]
tubes have been widely studied, each trading off flexibility and computational complexity. More recently, configuration-constrained (CC) polytopic tubes
[
31
]
have emerged as a flexible alternative, enabling vertex-based invariance checks that are both necessary and sufficient. Attempts to incorporate periodicity under uncertainty have relied on ellipsoidal
[
32
]
, rigid
[
33
]
, or homothetic
[
9
]
tubes, but these approaches either lack flexibility or incur high computational cost.

Contribution

This work extends configuration-constrained tube MPC (CCTMPC) from tracking piecewise-constant references
[
3
]
to robust tracking of
periodic tubes
that are optimal for a given economic criterion. The uncertain system is modeled as a polytopic LDI, which naturally captures both additive disturbances and parametric uncertainty; this structure is directly handled by configuration-constrained tubes, offering greater flexibility compared to other polytopic parameterizations. Stability and convergence for constant economic criteria are ensured without requiring an explicit terminal region, thereby simplifying controller design; see Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
. Furthermore, under suitable regularity assumptions on the cost, we introduce a quadratic upper approximation inspired by
[
6
]
, enabling a QP formulation that preserves the theoretical guarantees of the general convex formulation while significantly improving computational efficiency. The proposed approach is validated on a benchmark example and a ball–plate system with eight states, demonstrating its effectiveness and scalability.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces robust optimal periodic tubes and formulates the uncertain periodic operation problem. Section
3
details the proposed periodic CCTMPC scheme and analyzes its feasibility, robustness, and stability properties. Section
4
presents the QP approximation and proves convergence to the same optimal periodic tube as the original non-approximated problem. Section
5
reports numerical results, and Section
6
concludes the paper with final remarks and future research directions.

2  Problem Formulation

This section formalizes the robust periodic MPC problem for uncertain systems represented as polytopic LDIs
[
8
]
. We introduce the system model, define periodic forward invariant tubes, and describe their configuration‑constrained parameterization, leading to the formulation of the optimal periodic tube problem.

2.1  Uncertain System Model

We consider uncertain dynamics represented as a polytopic LDI

x^{+}\in\{Ax+Bu+w\;|\;(A,B)\in\Delta,\;w\in\mathcal{W}\},

(1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
denote the state and input, and
w\in\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
is an additive disturbance belonging to a compact convex set. The uncertainty set
\Delta
is defined as

\Delta\coloneqq\operatorname{convh}\big(\{(A_{1},B_{1}),\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,(A_{m},B_{m})\}\big),

so that
(A,B)
vary within the convex hull of
m
vertex pairs, each defining a system-input model. Inclusion (
1
) captures both additive disturbances and parametric uncertainty in a unified framework, and reduces to a LTI system subject to additive disturbances when
m=1
. The system is subject to closed convex constraints
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

To ensure robust operation under all possible realizations of the uncertainty, we adopt a tube‑based MPC approach, in which the controller propagates sets of reachable states (tubes) rather than individual trajectories. The following subsections introduce the concepts required to synthesize such controllers.

2.2  Periodic Forward Invariant Tubes

Tube-based MPC schemes relies on the construction of Robust Forward Invariant Tubes (RFITs). To formalize this notion, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(X):=\left\{X^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\ \middle|\ \,\begin{aligned} &\forall x\in X,\ \exists u\in\mathcal{U}:\vskip 1.0pt\\
&\forall(A,B)\in\Delta,\ \forall w\in\mathcal{W},\\
&Ax+Bu+w\in X^{+}\vskip 1.0pt\end{aligned}\right\}

as the set of all one-step successor sets of a given set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
.

Definition 1
.

A sequence of sets
(X_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is an RFIT if
X_{t+1}\in\mathcal{F}(X_{t})
holds for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since our control objective involves periodic operation, we focus on the following subclass of RFITs.

Definition 2
.

An RFIT
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a

Periodic Forward Invariant Tube (PFIT)

if there exists a period
T\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle X_{t+T}=X_{t},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

We remark that a PFIT with period
T=1
recovers the standard notion for a Robust Control Invariant (RCI) set, since
X_{t+1}=X_{t}
implies
X_{t}\in\mathcal{F}(X_{t})
for all
t
.

2.3  Configuration-Constrained Polytopic Tubes

To obtain a tractable representation of PFITs, we adopt the configuration-constrained polyhedral representation introduced in
[
31
]
. A polytope is represented as

\displaystyle X(y)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\mid Fx\leq y\},

(3)

where
F\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{f}\times n_{x}}
is a fixed matrix of facet normals and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{f}}
is a parameter vector. We assume that
Fx\leq 0\implies x=0
, which guarantees that
X(y)
is bounded (and possibly empty) for any
y
.

To preserve the polytope’s combinatorial structure,
y
is restricted to the
configuration cone

\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{f}}\mid Ey\leq 0\},

where
E
encodes linear inequalities ensuring that the adjacency relations between facets remain invariant for all
y\in\mathcal{E}
. Under this condition, there exists a set of matrices
V:=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\mathrm{v}}
such that

\displaystyle X(y)=\mathrm{convh}\big(\{V_{j}y\mid j=1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,\mathrm{v}\}\big),\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{E}.

(4)

The triple
(F,E,V)
is called a
configuration triple
. Procedures for constructing such triples from a given polytope are detailed in
[
13
, Sec. II.A]
. This structure enables efficient halfspace/vertex-based invariance conditions, which are both necessary and sufficient for robust forward invariance
[
13
, Prop. 2]
.

We define
U_{j}=\mathbb{I}_{n_{u}}\otimes e_{\mathrm{v}}(j)^{\top}
, stacking the vertex control inputs as
u:=(u_{1},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,u_{\mathrm{v}})
, such that
u_{j}=U_{j}u
. Then, we introduce the set
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{f}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{f}}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\left\{(y,u,y^{+})\ \middle|\ \begin{aligned} &\forall\,(i,j)\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,m\}\times\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,\mathrm{v}\},\vskip 1.0pt\\
&F(A_{i}V_{j}y+B_{i}U_{j}u)+d\leq y^{+},\vskip 1.0pt\\
&Ey\leq 0,\ V_{j}y\in\mathcal{X},\ U_{j}u\in\mathcal{U}\end{aligned}\right\},

(5)

where
d_{k}\coloneqq\max\{F_{k}w:w\in\mathcal{W}\}
for all
k\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,\mathsf{f}\}
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
u_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u}}
with
(y_{t},u_{t},y_{t+1})\in\mathbb{S}
. Then,
(X(y_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is an RFIT.

Proof:
The result follows from
[
31
, Corollary 4]
.

\blacksquare

2.4  Optimal Configuration-Constrained PFITs

We consider the problem of computing a PFIT that is admissible under uncertainty and optimal with respect to a
T
-periodic cost. Let

\mathbf{z}\coloneqq(z_{0},\dots,z_{T}),\qquad\mathbf{v}\coloneqq(v_{0},\dots,v_{T-1}),

collect the tube parameters and the stacked vertex inputs over one period. The objective is defined as a sum of
T
stage costs indexed via modular arithmetic
[
36
]
,

\displaystyle[t+j]\coloneqq(t+j)\bmod T,\qquad\forall\,t,j\in\mathbb{N},

(6)

and the time-shifted periodic cost is

\displaystyle M_{t}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}):=\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\ell_{[t+j]}(z_{j},v_{j}),

(7)

where
t
determines the modular shift in the stage cost sequence. Each stage cost
\ell_{[t+j]}
may depend on external signals such as economic weights or reference trajectories.

The
optimal PFIT
for the cost structure at current time
t
is obtained by solving

\displaystyle(\mathbf{z}^{\circ}(t),\mathbf{v}^{\circ}(t))\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}}\

\displaystyle M_{t}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})

(8)

s.t.

\displaystyle(z_{j},v_{j},z_{j+1})\in\mathbb{S},\quad z_{T}=z_{0},

\displaystyle j\in\{0,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,T\!-\!1\}.

The equality constraint
z_{T}=z_{0}
enforces the periodicity of the tube cross-sections as in (
2
); equivalently, periodicity can be verified by
V_{j}z_{0}=V_{j}z_{T}
for all
j\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,\mathrm{v}\}
if polytopes share the same configuration triple. Note that the feasibility set does not depend on
t
; only the objective changes through the modular shift.

Assumption 1
.

Problem (
8
) has a nonempty feasible set and a strictly convex objective.

For any feasible sequence
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})
, define its
cyclic successor
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{+}\coloneqq(z_{1},\dots,z_{T},z_{1}),\qquad\mathbf{v}^{+}\coloneqq(v_{1},\dots,v_{T-1},v_{0}).

(9)

Proposition 2
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds. Then:

1.

The optimal value of Problem (
8
) is independent of
t
and is denoted by

\mathcal{O}^{\circ}:=\min_{\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}}M_{t}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}),\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{N}.

2.

For any
t\geq 0
, if
(\mathbf{z}^{\circ}(t),\mathbf{v}^{\circ}(t))
is the unique minimizer at time
t
, then the minimizer at time
t+1
is its cyclic successor

(\mathbf{z}^{\circ}(t+1),\mathbf{v}^{\circ}(t+1))=(\mathbf{z}^{\circ}(t)^{+},\mathbf{v}^{\circ}(t)^{+}).

Proof.

The feasibility set of Problem (
8
) does not depend on
t
. By construction of
M_{t}
and the modular indexing in (
6
), cyclic shifts of a feasible solution yield the same cost at the next time step. Uniqueness of the minimizer implies that the optimizer at time
t+1
must be the cyclic successor of the optimizer at time
t
.

\blacksquare

By Proposition
2
, the closed-loop sequence of sets formed by the first component of the optimizer,
X(z_{0}^{\circ}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, coincides with the optimal PFIT of period
T
. This motivates our control goal.

Problem Statement

Design an MPC scheme that (i) is recursively feasible and robustly satisfies constraints, and (ii) generates an RFIT
(X(y_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to the PFIT optimal for the given periodic cost
M_{t}
.

A straightforward approach would be to recompute (
8
) whenever the cost structure changes and then run a tracking MPC for the resulting PFIT. However, such a two-layer design suffers from the drawbacks discussed in the introduction. Instead, following the single-layer MPC philosophy
[
21
,
22
]
, the next section proposes a
single convex program
that simultaneously (a) identifies a PFIT candidate consistent with the current periodic cost, and (b) enforces an RFIT to track it, guaranteeing convergence to the optimal PFIT in closed loop whenever the cost remains constant.

3  CCTMPC for periodic operation

We now present a CCTMPC scheme that addresses our
Problem Statement
. Assuming without loss of generality that
T>N
, we formulate a parametric convex optimization problem solved online at each time step. The objective comprises three terms: (i) construction of a feasible PFIT, (ii) tracking via an RFIT, and (iii) a terminal term ensuring stability with respect to the feasible PFIT. The optimization problem is

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}}\;

\displaystyle\ M_{t}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}y_{k}-z_{k}\\
u_{k}-v_{k}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{Q}^{2}\!+\mathrm{Z}(y_{N},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})

s.t.

\displaystyle\ Fx\leq y_{0},\quad y_{N}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}),\quad z_{T}=z_{0},

\displaystyle\ (y_{k},u_{k},y_{k+1})\in\mathbb{S},\ \forall\,k\in\{0,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,N\!-\!1\},

(10)

\displaystyle\ (z_{j},v_{j},z_{j+1})\in\mathbb{S},\ \forall\,j\in\{0,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,T\!-\!1\}.

which is parametric in the current state
x
and the periodic cost
M_{t}
defining the economic objective at time
t
. The vectors
\mathbf{y}=(y_{0},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,y_{N})
and
\mathbf{u}=(u_{0},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,u_{N-1})
parametrize the RFIT, while
\mathbf{z}=(z_{0},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,z_{T})
and
\mathbf{v}=(v_{0},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.50003pt,v_{T-1})
parametrize the PFIT. Furthermore,
\mathrm{Z}
denotes the terminal cost and
\mathbb{T}
the associated terminal region, both defined in terms of the feasible PFIT. Their construction is detailed in the next subsection.

3.1  Design of the terminal ingredients

We define the terminal cost
\mathrm{Z}
and the induced terminal set

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}(y,y^{+},z,v):=\min_{u}

\displaystyle\lVert(y-z,u-v)\rVert_{Q}^{2}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle(y,u,y^{+})\in\mathbb{S},

for fixed

\displaystyle\ Fx\leq y_{0},\quad y_{N}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}),\quad z_{T}=z_{0},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}(y,\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}):=\min_{u}

\displaystyle\lVert(y-z_{N},u-v_{N})\rVert_{P}^{2}

4  QP-based periodic CCTMPC

Problem (
26
) is a convex optimization problem with linear feasibility constraints, guaranteeing global optimality. However, the non-quadratic structure of the periodic cost
M_{t}
makes its online solution computationally demanding. To improve real-time feasibility, we introduce a quadratic upper approximation of
M_{t}
based on its first-order Taylor expansion under a mild smoothness assumption. This approximation enables a QP formulation that preserves recursive feasibility—since the constraint set remains unchanged—and ensures convergence to the same solution as Problem (
26
).

Assumption 2
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the gradient of
M_{t}
with respect to
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})
is Lipschitz continuous. That is, there exists
\eta>0
such that for any feasible pairs
(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1})
and
(\mathbf{z}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{2})
of Problem (
8

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla M_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1})\!-\!\nabla M_{t}(\mathbf{z}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{2})\right\rVert\leq\eta\cdot\!\left\lVert(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1})\!-\!(\mathbf{z}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{2})\right\rVert.

Given a feasible linearization point
(\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}})
for (
8
), we construct a quadratic upper approximation of
M_{t}
that is parameterized by
(\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}})
. Specifically, for any
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})
, define

\hat{M}_{t}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v};\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}}):=M_{t}(\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}})\\
+\nabla M_{t}(\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}})^{\top}\big[(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})\!-\!(\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}})\big]+\frac{\eta}{2}\left\lVert(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})\!-\!(\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{v}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

(34)

where the semicolon emphasizes that

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v}}

\displaystyle\ Fx\leq y_{0},\quad z_{T}=z_{0},

Denote the minimizers of Problem (

5.3  Computational Complexity

The optimization problems (
26
) and (
36
) involve
(N+T+1)(\mathrm{f}+n_{u}\mathrm{v})+\mathrm{f}
decision variables. Each constraint set
\mathbb{S}
–of which
N+T+1
are required, in addition to
\mathrm{f}
inequality and
\mathrm{f}
equality constraints from the initial and periodicity conditions–contains
(m\mathrm{v}+1)\mathrm{f}+\mathrm{v}(n_{\mathcal{X}}+n_{\mathcal{U}})
inequalities, where
n_{\mathcal{X}}
and
n_{\mathcal{U}}
denote the number of hyperplanes defining the state and input constraints, respectively.

As a result, computing a full periodic trajectory is significantly more demanding than tracking a single reference, since both the number of variables and constraints scale linearly with the orbit horizon
T
, in addition to the prediction horizon
N
. Nonetheless, several strategies can substantially reduce the computational burden. The periodic structure of the problem enables efficient warm-starting by shifting the previous solution forward in time when external parameters remain unchanged, thereby accelerating convergence. Moreover, following the approach in
[
4
]
, the Optimal PFIT can be precomputed offline, and the artificial tube constructed online via homothetic transformations. This eliminates the need to solve the full DRTO problem at each step, though it may reduce the Region of Attraction. A detailed investigation of this trade-off and its impact on closed-loop performance is left for future work.

6  Conclusions

This paper extends the CCTMPC framework to enable robust periodic economic optimization for systems that can be described by polytopic LDIs.
This method guarantees recursive feasibility and convergence by leveraging configuration-constrained tubes and periodic feasibility conditions. To address computational complexity, we also proposed a QP-based approximation via first-order Taylor expansion of the economic cost, preserving robustness and stability while improving real-time feasibility. Numerical results on low- and high-dimensional systems confirm the effectiveness of the approach, with the QP-based formulation also reducing solve times by more than an order of magnitude compared to the original convex formulation. Strategies such as warm-starting and homothetic tube construction were also discussed as promising directions for further reducing complexity.

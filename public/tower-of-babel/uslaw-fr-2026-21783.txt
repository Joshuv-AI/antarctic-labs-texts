    In accordance with section 732(b) of the Tariff Act of 1930, as 
amended (the Act), the petitioners allege that imports of corrugated 
die-cut boxes from China, Malaysia, and T[uuml]rkiye are being, or are 
likely to be, sold in the United States at less than fair value (LTFV) 
within the meaning of section 731 of the Act, and that imports of such 
products are materially injuring, or threatening material injury to, 
the corrugated die-cut boxes industry in the United States. Consistent 
with section 732(b)(1) of the Act, the Petitions were accompanied by 
information reasonably available to the petitioners supporting their 
allegations.
    Commerce finds that the petitioners filed the Petitions on behalf 
of the domestic industry, because the petitioners are interested 
parties, as defined in sections 771(9)(D) and (E) of the Act.\8\ 
Commerce also finds that the petitioners demonstrated sufficient 
industry support for the initiation of the requested LTFV 
investigations.\9\

physical characteristics. In addition, interested parties may comment

Determination of Industry Support for the Petitions

    Section 732(b)(1) of the Act requires that a petition be filed on 
behalf of the domestic industry. Section 732(c)(4)(A) of the Act 
provides that a petition meets this requirement if the domestic 
producers or workers who support the petition account for: (i) at least 
25 percent of the total production of the domestic like product; and 
(ii) more than 50 percent of the production of the domestic like 
product produced by that portion of the industry expressing support 
for, or opposition to, the petition. Moreover, section 732(c)(4)(D) of 
the Act provides that, if the petition does not establish support of 
domestic producers or workers accounting for more than 50 percent of 
the total production of the domestic like product, Commerce shall: (i) 
poll the industry or rely on other information in order to determine if 
there is support for the petition, as required by subparagraph (A); or 
(ii) determine industry support using a statistically valid sampling 
method to poll the ``industry.''
    Section 771(4)(A) of the Act defines the ``industry'' as the 
producers as a whole of a domestic like product. Thus, to determine 
whether a petition has the requisite industry support, the statute 
directs Commerce to look to producers and workers who produce the 
domestic like product. The U.S. International Trade Commission (ITC), 
which is responsible for determining whether ``the domestic industry'' 
has been injured, must also determine what constitutes a domestic like 
product in order to define the industry. While both Commerce and the 
ITC apply the same statutory definition regarding the domestic like 
product,\17\ they do so for different purposes and pursuant to a 
separate and distinct authority. In addition, Commerce's determination 
is

[[Page 67278]]

subject to limitations of time and information. Although this may 
result in different definitions of the like product, such differences 
do not render the decision of either agency contrary to law.\18\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fair Value Comparisons

    Based on the data provided by the petitioners, there is reason to 
believe that imports of corrugated die-cut boxes from China, Malaysia, 
and T[uuml]rkiye, are being, or are likely to be, sold in the United 
States at LTFV. Based on comparisons of EP to NV in accordance with 
sections 772 and 773 of the Act, the estimated dumping margins for 
corrugated die-cut boxes for each of the countries covered by this 
initiation are as follows: (1) China (Brazil Surrogate)--170.40 to 
297.26 percent; China (Mexico Surrogate)--97.71 to 185.92 percent; and 
China (T[uuml]rkiye Surrogate)--99.71 to 185.04 percent; (2) Malaysia--
83.72-116.58 percent; and (3) T[uuml]rkiye-51.27 to 92.81 percent.\45\

Initiation of LTFV Investigations

    Based upon the examination of the Petitions and supplemental 
responses, we find that they meet the requirements of section 732 of

    Commerce will calculate combination rates for certain respondents

to exporters, all separate rates that {Commerce{time}  will now assign

receiving an individually calculated separate rate as well as the pool

individually calculated rates. This practice is referred to as the

Particular Market Situation Allegation

    Section 773(e) of the Act addresses the concept of particular 
market situation (PMS) for purposes of CV, stating that ``if a 
particular market situation exists such that the cost of materials and 
fabrication or other processing of any kind does not accurately reflect 
the cost of production in the ordinary course of trade, the 
administering authority may use another calculation methodology under 
this subtitle or any other calculation methodology.'' When an 
interested party submits a PMS allegation pursuant to section 773(e) of 
the Act (i.e., a cost-based PMS allegation), the submission must be 
filed in accordance with the requirements of 19 CFR 351.416(b), and 
Commerce will respond to such a submission consistent with 19 CFR 
351.301(c)(2)(v). If Commerce finds that a cost-based PMS exists under 
section 773(e) of the Act, then it will modify its dumping calculations 
appropriately.
    Neither section 773(e) of the Act, nor 19 CFR 351.301(c)(2)(v), 
sets a deadline for the submission of cost-based PMS allegations and 
supporting factual information. However, in order to administer section 
773(e) of the Act, Commerce must receive PMS allegations and supporting 
factual information with enough time to consider the submission. Thus, 
should an interested party wish to submit a cost-based PMS allegation 
and supporting new factual information pursuant to section 773(e) of 
the Act, it must do so no later than 20 days after submission of a 
respondent's initial section D questionnaire response
    We note that a PMS allegation filed pursuant to sections 
773(a)(1)(B)(ii)(III) or 773(a)(1)(C)(iii) of the Act (i.e., a sales-
based PMS allegation) must be filed within 10 days of submission of a 
respondent's initial section B questionnaire response, in accordance 
with 19 CFR 351.301(c)(2)(i) and 19 CFR 351.404(c)(2).

Appendix

Scope of the Investigations

    The merchandise covered by these investigations is certain 
corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes. Corrugated die-cut cardboard 
boxes are a type of box or carton for use in the food service 
industry, including to hold pizza pies. Corrugated die cut cardboard 
boxes are typically manufactured from corrugated paperboard, which 
is commonly referred to as corrugated cardboard. Corrugated die-cut 
cardboard boxes are often shipped or delivered flat in bundles of 
corrugated cardboard die-cut blanks, which are in a ready-to-
assemble state such that they can be easily folded by the end user 
into a ready-to-use food service box.
    The corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes covered by these 
investigations include corrugated cardboard die-cut blanks, whether 
finished or unfinished and assembled or unassembled. A finished 
corrugated die-cut cardboard box, whether assembled or unassembled, 
is one that has undergone final processing, including, but not 
limited to, printing, coating, or other finishing operations. An 
unfinished corrugated die-cut cardboard box, whether assembled or 
unassembled, is one that has not yet undergone final processing.
    Corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes are produced from corrugated 
cardboard sheets that are made from recycled or virgin paper or 
paperboard materials, which may be bleached or unbleached. These 
cardboard sheets are ``corrugated'' or ``fluted,'' which means that 
they contain at least two outer layers (i.e., top and bottom) of 
paper or paperboard and an inside layer of fluted paper or 
paperboard in between the outer layer, such that the inside layer 
contains channels or pockets of air which makes the cardboard sheet 
lightweight while retaining strength and durability. The subject 
merchandise includes corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes with a 
nominal flute height or thickness between 1.1 millimeters and 4.4 
millimeters (which encompasses B-flute, C-flute, D-flute, and E-
flute design, as well as any equivalent types of corrugation or 
fluting).
    Corrugated cardboard die-cut blanks are typically formed from 
corrugated cardboard sheets that are cut to length, die cut into 
specific box shapes, and/or scored to allow the box to be folded 
into shape. Subject corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes are typically 
assembled by the end user by folding a corrugated cardboard die-cut 
blank into its final shape and generally consist of a single die-cut 
blank that forms the walls and lid of the finished box. Subject 
corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes are not typically glued or 
otherwise assembled using adhesives into their final shape prior to 
use. The scope of these investigations covers both corrugated die-
cut cardboard boxes and corrugated cardboard die-cut blanks, 
regardless of whether they are finished or assembled at the time of 
importation.
    As they are intended for food contact, in-scope corrugated die-
cut cardboard boxes are produced in accordance with food contact 
safety requirements and are often compliant with U.S. Food and Drug 
Administration requirements, such as 21 CFR 176.170.
    The subject merchandise includes corrugated die-cut cardboard 
boxes regardless of shape. When assembled, subject corrugated die-
cut cardboard boxes are often rectangular or square in shape when 
viewed from above, but they may take on other shapes, such as round, 
hexagonal, or octagonal. A finished and assembled subject corrugated 
die-cut cardboard box has a length (i.e., the longest dimension) 
that is at least five inches and a width that is at least five 
inches. A finished and assembled subject corrugated die-cut 
cardboard box has a length and width that is no more than 40 inches. 
The height (i.e., the shortest dimension) of a finished and 
assembled subject corrugated die-cut cardboard box is no greater 
than four inches. The width, length, and height dimensions are to be 
based on the dimensions of the die-cut shape of an individual 
corrugated die-cut cardboard box or corrugated cardboard die-cut 
blank, even if the box or blank is imported in a manner in which it 
is minimally connected to other boxes or blanks, such as through 
nicking, perforations, or similar attachments.
    The corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes covered by the scope of 
these investigations include merchandise with or without color or 
printing, such that they may be decorated with one or more graphical 
designs or logos, or they may be entered in a blank, unprinted 
state. A corrugated die-cut cardboard box is subject to these 
investigations regardless of the color of the paper, paperboard, or 
cardboard. Any graphical images may be printed directly onto the 
paper, paperboard, or cardboard or they may be overlaid onto the 
outer or inner faces of a corrugated die-cut cardboard box. Subject 
corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes are also covered by the scope 
regardless of whether or not they are coated or lined, including 
with any materials to increase grease resistance.
    The subject merchandise includes corrugated die-cut cardboard 
boxes with or without ventilation holes or perforations as well as 
with or without features for holding condiments or other foods. They 
may include tabs that allow for easily opening the lid. Subject 
merchandise generally does not incorporate locking tabs, 
interlocking side panels, folded retention flaps, ``dog-ear'' 
closures, or similar mechanisms designed to secure the lid to the 
body of the box. Subject corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes are 
covered by the scope whether or not they are accompanied by other 
parts or materials. The addition of any non-paper, paperboard, or -
cardboard materials, such as liners, inserts, or holders, does not 
remove the subject corrugated die-cut cardboard box from the scope 
of these investigations.
    The corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes subject to these 
investigations include those products that have been subjected to 
further processing in a third country, which includes but is not 
limited to printing, coating, trimming, cutting, punching, 
assembling, folding, perforating, and/or additional die-cutting or 
any other processing that would not otherwise remove the merchandise 
from the scope of the investigations if performed in the country of 
manufacture of the in-scope corrugated die-cut cardboard box.
    The corrugated die-cut cardboard boxes subject to these 
investigations are typically classified in the Harmonized Tariff 
Schedule of the United States (HTSUS) at subheading 4819.10.0020. 
This HTSUS subheading is provided for convenience and customs 
purposes only, and the written description of the merchandise 
subject to the investigations is dispositive.

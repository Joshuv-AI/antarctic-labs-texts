Study protocol for a randomized controlled trial assessing the effect of lateral position intubation on bronchial blocker placement during unilateral video-assisted thoracic surgery
Authors: Zhengduo Zhang, Xi Liu, Xi Zhang, Bei Zhou, Yawen Tang, Fei Tong, Yonghe Hu, Feifan Liu, Siping Hu, He Liu, Huijuan Tan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundApproximately one-third of patients who undergo bronchial blocker (BB) intubation in the conventional supine position suffer BB malposition. This trial aims to explore the efficiency and clinical application of BB intubation in the lateral position to reduce the incidence of BB malposition in patients undergoing video-assisted thoracic surgery (VATS).MethodsThis single-center, parallel-group, randomized controlled trial will enroll 110 patients aged 18–80 years who are scheduled for elective unilateral VATS with BB intubation under general anesthesia. Participants will be randomly assigned (1:1) to either the lateral BB intubation group or the conventional supine BB intubation group. The primary outcome is the incidence of BB malposition observed by fiberoptic bronchoscopy (FOB). Secondary outcomes include the duration of intubation, the frequency and duration of FOB usage, whether to re-intubate, intraoperative vital signs, and postoperative recovery.DiscussionThis trial will confirm the clinical efficacy and superiority of BB intubation in the lateral position to consolidate the lateral intubation pattern in thoracic anesthesia. We expect that performing lateral BB intubation can reduce the BB malposition rate and result in more stable intraoperative vital signs and fewer postoperative complications, which is in line with the concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery in thoracic anesthesia.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2400081961. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

With the advantages of small incisions, mild postoperative pain, and high recovery quality, video-assisted thoracic surgery (VATS) has become a primary approach for thoracic surgery and is widely performed in clinical [1, 2]. VATS relies on lung isolation provided by a double-lumen tube (DLT) or bronchial blocker (BB) for the surgeon to distinguish anatomical structures [3]. DLT can effectively and reliably atrophy the contralateral lung and is the first choice to achieve one-lung ventilation (OLV) [4]. However, DLT has unavoidable defects such as a large diameter, rigid stiffness, and difficulty in insertion, leading to complications such as bronchus or carina injuries, hypoxemia, hoarseness, and sore throat [5–7]. The BB has been proven to be an alternative to the DLT [8], with advantages such as a lower incidence of postoperative sore throat and hoarseness than DLT, but the BB seems to have a higher malposition rate and longer intubation time [7–11].

The conventional method of BB intubation involves induction of anesthesia and intubation in the supine position, followed by moving the anesthetized patient to the lateral position required for the surgery. The process of moving the position is laborious and inefficient and can easily lead to BB malposition [12]. According to previous research, the incidence of BB malposition is about 26%–45% [7, 10, 12–14]. Especially when transferring anesthetized patients to the lateral position after inserting the BB, the malposition rate can be as high as 40% [15]. BB malposition can lead to complications such as unsuccessful OLV, hypoxemia, and airway obstruction [4, 12], even precipitate a cardiopulmonary crisis in severe cases [16]. Our research team has previously designed a clinical trial of DLT intubation in the lateral position [17] and found that lateral DLT intubation can significantly reduce the rate of DLT malposition and postoperative throat discomfort in patients [18]. Considering the feasibility of single-lumen endotracheal tube and DLT intubation in the lateral position [19, 20], we envisioned whether BB intubation could be performed in the lateral position. After searching the literature, we did not find any articles describing the method and feasibility of BB intubation in the lateral position.

Therefore, we designed this randomized controlled trial to investigate the effect of BB intubation in the lateral position on the position of BB placement and to verify our primary hypothesis that lateral BB intubation can reduce the rate of BB malposition observed by fiberoptic bronchoscopy (FOB) compared with supine BB intubation. In addition, we explored the effects of lateral BB intubation on intubation time, FOB usage, intraoperative vital signs, and postoperative complications. This trial is an offshoot program of the lateral intubation pattern in our research team. We hope that lateral BB intubation with a more optimized process and fewer complications will refine the connotation of lateral intubation pattern, aligning with the concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS).

Objectives {7}

The primary objective of this randomized controlled trial is to investigate the effect of intubation in the lateral position under general anesthesia induction on the position of BB placement in patients undergoing unilateral VATS. We will explore whether lateral BB intubation can reduce the rate of BB malposition compared with supine intubation. We will also explore the effects of lateral BB intubation on intubation time, the number and duration of FOB usage, the need for re-intubation, intraoperative vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation), and early postoperative complications (sore throat, hoarseness, and compressed upper arm discomfort).

Trial design {8}

This trial is designed as a single-center, parallel-group, prospective, randomized controlled superiority trial, with an allocation ratio of 1:1. The trial adheres to the SPIRIT 2013 Explanation and Elaboration guidelines, ensuring a comprehensive and standardized approach to trial design, conduct, and reporting.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

VATS requires the special position for the patient to be placed in the lateral position during surgery, optimizing surgical visibility and access. The conventional method of BB intubation is that the anesthesiologist administers general anesthesia and performs the BB intubation for the patient in the supine position, and then moves the anesthetized patient to the lateral position required for surgery. Therefore, conventional supine BB intubation served as the control group in this trial.

Intervention description {11a}

Preparing

All participants are routinely fasted for 8 h before the surgery. Standard monitoring in the operating room will include a 5-lead electrocardiogram, pulse oximetry, non-invasive blood pressure (or invasive arterial blood pressure depending on study participant status), and bispectral index monitoring. The size of the single-lumen endotracheal tube will be chosen based on the individual participant, with a size 6.5 to 7.0 tube usually chosen for female patients and a 7.0 to 7.5 tube for male patients [21]. The 9F Endobronchial Blocker Tube (Hangzhou Tappa Medical Technology Co., Ltd., Zhejiang, China), a modified version of the Fuji Uniblocker®, will be used as the BB for all participants. Anesthesia induction will involve target-controlled infusion (TCI) of propofol at 2.5–3.0 μg/mL, combined with rocuronium 0.6 mg/kg and sufentanil 0.1–0.3 μg/kg. Intraoperative anesthesia will be maintained by total intravenous anesthesia using propofol by TCI at a target plasma concentration of 2–2.5 µg/mL and remifentanil at 0.2–0.4 µg/kg/min, with additional rocuronium administered as needed.

Grouping

After the patient enters the operating room, the anesthesia investigator will open a sealed and opaque sequentially numbered envelope to retrieve a random sequence of the patient, determining the group assignment. Patients with random numbers 1–55 will undergo general anesthesia induction and BB intubation in the lateral position (group L), while those with numbers 56–110 will undergo these procedures in the conventional supine position (group C). Once the group assignment is confirmed, the anesthesia investigator will assist the patient in achieving the designated position (lateral or supine) on the surgical table.

Description for intervention

Group L: Anesthesia investigators assist the patient to lie on the surgical table in a lateral position. The head is elevated with a headrest to align the head, neck, and trunk in the same horizontal plane. After minor adjustments by the surgeon and the patient to a surgically required and comfortable lateral position, anesthesia will be induced following 3 min of pre-oxygenation with 100% oxygen. After the neuromuscular blocking drug takes effect, the anesthesia investigator will place a disposable laryngoscope blade into the center of the patient’s mouth, connect the video laryngoscope to expose the glottis, and insert the single-lumen endotracheal tube. The BB will be inserted through the single-lumen endotracheal tube into the surgical lung under the guidance of FOB and adjusted to the optimal position. The optimal position of the BB is achieved when the blocker balloon’s outer surface is seen by FOB at least 10 mm below the tracheal carina in the left bronchus or in the right bronchus [22], as shown in Fig. 1. Group C: Anesthesia investigators intubate the single-lumen endotracheal tube with the video laryngoscope after completing anesthesia induction in the conventional supine position, then insert the BB under the guidance of FOB to the optimal position. FOB will be used again after lateral positioning to confirm and, if necessary, adjust the BB position.

Introduction for investigators

A total of three anesthesia investigators will be trained for this trial. They are anesthesiologists with the title of attending physician or higher, experienced in thoracic anesthesia and well-versed in BB intubation in both lateral and supine positions. Postoperative follow-up will be conducted by two independent investigators blinded to patient grouping. Statistical analysis and interpretation of the data will be done by two additional investigators who are also blinded to patient grouping and will be responsible for paper and electronic data storage. All investigators previously participated in our team’s lateral DLT intubation study, gaining valuable expertise to successfully conduct the current trial.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

The principal investigator (SPH), the chief physician of the Department of Anesthesiology, will initiate a pre-anesthetic evaluation of patients screened strictly with exclusion and inclusion criteria the day before surgery. Patients will be educated about the lateral and supine BB intubation procedures in thoracic surgery and shown a demonstration video to enhance understanding and cooperation. To ensure adherence to the protocol, all anesthesiologists performing the intubations will undergo standardized training and certification before patient enrollment. Each intubation procedure will be supervised by a senior investigator who will verify protocol compliance in real time. Key procedural details, including patient positioning, number of intubation attempts, and reasons for any modification or conversion, will be documented in the CRF. Periodic audits of the CRFs and intraoperative records will be performed by the Trial Steering Committee (TSC) to ensure consistency and quality control across all cases.

Most patient data, as outlined in the informed consent form, will be collected through the inpatient system and intraoperative monitoring. Postoperative follow-up will take only 3–5 min per participant, minimizing patient burden. Additionally, the follow-up investigators, who are also anesthesiologists, will monitor the patients’ postoperative condition. They will adjust settings on intravenous patient-controlled analgesia devices as needed, ensuring optimal pain management and enhancing patient compliance. The investigators involved in anesthesia and follow-up have received ongoing training and participated in previous clinical trials to ensure the completeness and accuracy of data collection.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

All participants enrolled in the trial will receive standard care in the operating room, the postanesthesia care unit (PACU), and the ward.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

After the surgery, sugammadex will be administered to reverse neuromuscular blockade, and the BB or DLT will be removed in the operating room. The patient is then transferred to the PACU for observation until an Aldrete score ≥ 9 [23]. If the patient is in poor condition or cannot be extubated, we will transfer the patient to the anesthesia intensive care unit (AICU) or critical care medicine (CCM) unit for further monitoring and care. Thoracic surgery and general anesthesia inherently carry risks. Regardless of any adverse events that occur during the trial, we will provide appropriate care and treatment for each participant. In addition, if a participant experiences trial-related harm, appropriate compensation will be provided in accordance with institutional policies and national regulations.

Sample size {14}

Sample size calculations for this trial were performed using PASS 16.0 software. The primary objective of this trial is to investigate whether lateral BB intubation can reduce the incidence of BB malposition, so the sample size was determined based on current BB malposition rates of approximately 40% [14]. A reduction in the malposition rate to 15% was considered an effective intervention. The significance level (α) was set at 0.05, and the power (1 − β) at 0.80. Using PASS calculations, a sample size of 47 participants per group was determined. After accounting for a 10% dropout rate, the final sample size was expected to be approximately 55 per group, for a total of 110 patients.

Recruitment {15}

Study participants will be recruited from hospitalized patients in the Department of Thoracic Surgery at Huzhou Central Hospital. The recruitment process will be conducted collaboratively by thoracic surgeons and anesthesiologists. All patients scheduled for unilateral VATS will be evaluated for eligibility based on the study’s inclusion and exclusion criteria during the pre-anesthetic evaluation conducted by the principal investigator. Participation will be entirely voluntary, and patients will be informed of their right to decline without any impact on their medical care. The Department of Thoracic Surgery at Huzhou Central Hospital has approximately 100 beds, providing a sufficient patient pool to meet the recruitment target. Recruitment is planned to begin in October 2025, with the aim of enrolling all participants within 4 months.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The investigator will use a computerized random number generator to generate 110 random sequences, which will be individually sealed in numbered, opaque envelopes. After each participant carries the envelope into the operating room, the anesthesia investigator will open the envelope to obtain the random sequence to determine the grouping. Participants with random sequences numbered 1–55 will be assigned to group L, while those with random sequences numbered 56–110 will be assigned to group C. To protect participant privacy, each participant will be identified only by the sequential number on their envelope throughout the study. The randomization list will document all random sequences along with their corresponding sequential numbers.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The randomization list will be securely stored in a locked cabinet and kept under the custody of the principal investigator. Other investigators will not have access to it until the completion of the study.

Implementation {16c}

The envelopes containing the random sequences will be prepared in advance by the principal investigator. Upon entering the operating room, participants will be assigned to either group L or group C based on the random sequence obtained after opening the envelope. The anesthesia investigators will inform the patients of their group assignment to ensure their cooperation in assuming the correct position on the surgical table (either lateral or supine position).

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

This study is designed as an open-label clinical trial. The anesthesia investigators must be clear of the patient’s group assignment prior to performing BB intubation in either lateral or supine position. Similarly, patients will be informed of their group allocation upon entering the operating room and will position on the surgical table accordingly, either in the lateral or supine position. Only the investigators involved in postoperative follow-up, data collection, and analysis will be blinded to the group assignment to minimize bias in the assessment of outcomes.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Unblinding will not occur because this study is an open-label clinical trial.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

The evaluation of study outcomes will be carefully documented by investigators in CRFs, with each participant identified by a unique sequential number to maintain confidentiality while ensuring data accuracy. The specific items and variables collected in the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative CRFs can be found in Additional file 1.

Preoperative CRF: The principal investigator will complete the preoperative CRF during the pre-anesthetic assessment on the day before surgery. Information will be gathered from the results of the on-site clinical assessment, patient dictation, and the inpatient system. This will include patient demographics, medical history, and relevant preoperative clinical findings. Intraoperative CRF: The anesthesia investigators will complete the intraoperative CRF, documenting data from the time the patient enters the operating room until they are transferred to the PACU; the process will be supervised by an independent nurse who is not involved in the study. The data will be sourced from the Medison DoCare anesthesia clinical information system, anesthesia monitors, observations, and reports from the anesthesia investigators and surgeons. Postoperative CRF: The follow-up investigators will complete the postoperative CRF at three time points: 30 min after BB extubation, 24 h after surgery, and before discharge.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

As an exploration of a novel strategy for BB intubation in thoracic anesthesia, we focus primarily on intraoperative outcomes and early postoperative recovery of patients. Therefore, follow-up will be conducted at three key time points: 30 min after admission to the PACU (to assess sore throat, hoarseness, and upper arm discomfort), 24 h after surgery (to evaluate the QoR-15 score), and prior to discharge (to record PPCs and other adverse events). When obtaining informed consent, we will explain the three follow-up points to the participants, with an emphasis on the minimal time commitment (3–5 min) required at each follow-up. The study will conclude upon the patient’s discharge, and no further follow-up or surveys will be conducted thereafter. In accordance with the ITT principle, outcome data will be collected for all randomized participants whenever possible, including those who discontinue or deviate from their assigned intervention, to ensure the completeness and validity of the trial results.

Data management {19}

Data collected from all participants in this study will be handled in compliance with the Data Security Law of the People’s Republic of China. All data will be systematically recorded in paper-based preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative CRFs managed by two independent data researchers not involved in the trial. Once the trial is complete, all paper CRFs will be converted into an electronic database in Microsoft Excel by data researchers. To ensure data quality, all CRFs will undergo double data entry into an electronic database (Microsoft Excel) by researchers, with range and logic checks to identify and correct inconsistencies, resolved by reference to the original CRFs. Paper CRFs will be stored in locked filing cabinets in the Department of Anesthesiology with access restricted to authorized personnel, while electronic data will be kept on password-protected, encrypted computers within the hospital’s secure network, accessible only to the principal investigator and designated data managers. Both paper and electronic data will be archived for at least 10 years after trial completion in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and institutional policy, after which they will be securely destroyed.

Confidentiality {27}

Personal data of all participants will be securely stored to ensure strict protection of privacy and confidentiality throughout the study. The randomization list and paper-based CRFs will be stored in a locked filing cabinet, while electronic data will be encrypted and stored on a password-protected hard drive. Only the principal investigator will have access to the passwords. The data researcher will be responsible for overseeing all data management procedures. In order to safeguard patient privacy, access to the de-identified data will be granted only upon reasonable request and with the prior approval of the principal investigator. Additionally, the identification details of participants will be excluded from all aspects of research dissemination to ensure confidentiality.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

Not applicable, no biological specimens will be collected.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS 29.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and OriginPro 2024 (OriginLab, Northampton, MA, USA). Primary and secondary outcomes will be analyzed as follows: The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test will assess whether continuous variables adhere to a normal distribution. Continuous variables conforming to a normal distribution will be presented as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s), whereas non-normally distributed variables will be presented as the median with interquartile range (IQR). Comparisons of normally distributed continuous variables between groups will be analyzed using the independent samples t-test, while non-normally distributed variables will be analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables will be expressed as counts (rates) and analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. In cases where multifactorial analysis is warranted, multiple linear regression or logistic regression models will be employed based on the type of dependent variables. The analysis will adhere to the ITT principle. A per-protocol analysis will also be performed as a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the robustness of the ITT results. Missing data will be addressed through multiple imputation, and sensitivity analyses will be conducted. A two-sided P value < 0.05 will be considered significant.

Interim analyses {21b}

No interim analyses are planned. This is a single-center exploratory trial with a relatively small sample size and short follow-up period, focusing on intraoperative outcomes and early postoperative recovery. Given the limited duration and low risk of the interventions, interim analyses are not considered necessary and could increase the risk of type I error. Therefore, all analyses will be conducted after the completion of data collection.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Not applicable, no additional analyses will be planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

In this study, we anticipate minimal protocol deviations due to the comprehensive preoperative explanation of the trial procedures and emphasizing compliance matters before obtaining informed consent. Any missing data will be managed using multiple imputation techniques, and we will conduct sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of the results. The analysis will follow the ITT principle, ensuring that all randomized patients are included, regardless of their adherence to the assigned intervention. We will evaluate the impact of missing data and protocol non-adherence by comparing the imputed dataset with the complete-case dataset, ensuring the integrity of the trial results.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code {31c}

This trial is initiated by the principal investigator. Access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code will be considered upon a formal request, provided that the request complies with relevant ethical guidelines and is approved by the principal investigator.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The coordinating center for this single-center trial is the Department of Anesthesiology at Huzhou Central Hospital, which is responsible for the day-to-day management, patient recruitment, data collection, and coordination of trial activities. The TSC will provide overall supervision of the trial on behalf of the sponsor and ensure that it is conducted according to practice. The TSC will consist of four members: a chief surgeon in thoracic surgery, a chief anesthesiologist with expertise in thoracic anesthesia, a nurse supervisor in the operating room, and a patient representative. The committee will meet every 3 weeks, or more frequently if necessary, to review trial progress, recruitment, protocol adherence, and safety concerns, and will make recommendations to the principal investigator accordingly.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

A separate Data and Safety Monitoring Board will not be established because this is a single-center, low-risk procedural trial without an investigational drug or device and with short perioperative follow-up. Day-to-day data monitoring (data entry verification, range/logic checks, deviation tracking) will be performed by an independent operating-room nurse who is not involved in the trial interventions. Overall oversight is led by the principal investigator, with monitoring outputs reviewed and corrective actions coordinated in consultation with the TSC.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

We anticipate that lateral BB intubation will not confer additional risk. However, the inherent risks and adverse reactions associated with tracheal intubation under general anesthesia and thoracic surgical procedures remain. Nonetheless, we will document any adverse events occurring during the trial, such as hypoxemia, respiratory failure, atelectasis, pleural effusion, and pneumothorax. All adverse events will be reported to the principal investigator, with serious adverse events being reported to the TSC and the ethics committee within 24 h. Serious adverse events are defined as those related to anesthesia or surgical procedures that result in permanent impairment of the patient’s bodily structure or function, life-threatening situations, significant deterioration in health, or death. The hospital will ensure that patients receive prompt care and appropriate treatment for any adverse events occurring during the trial. Should it be determined that an adverse event is related to lateral intubation, compensation will be provided for any harm incurred by the participant as a result of their involvement in the study, in accordance with relevant government regulations.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

The TSC will conduct regular biweekly audits throughout the trial period. These audits will include the examination of informed consent forms, CRFs, missing data, duplicate data, erroneous data, and adverse events. Audit findings will be communicated to the principal investigator, and any necessary corrective actions will be implemented. Additionally, regulatory authorities or the ethics committee may perform audits without expected notice.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Any modifications to the protocol will be reported to the ethics committee for review and approval. Once approved, all investigators and the TSC will be promptly informed at an investigator meeting. Additionally, updates will be posted simultaneously on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry.

Dissemination plans {31a}

The study findings will be submitted for presentation and published in peer-reviewed medical journals. Priority will be given to leading journals specializing in anesthesiology and thoracic surgery. The outcomes of this study will also be submitted to the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry.

Discussion

As the volume of thoracic surgeries increases and the concept of ERAS continues to integrate into clinical practice, anesthesia techniques should also evolve and innovate accordingly. DLT intubation in the supine position remains the primary and widely accepted method for thoracic surgery due to its broad applicability, procedural simplicity, and reliable safety profile [3, 4]. BB intubation has proven to be an effective alternative, particularly advantageous in specific clinical scenarios such as airway stenosis [9]. Notably, our research team has demonstrated the safety and feasibility of lateral DLT intubation in VATS [18]. To further expand the conceptual framework of lateral intubation strategies in thoracic anesthesia, more in-depth investigation is warranted. Therefore, proposing a clinical trial of BB intubation in the lateral position has become a pressing priority in our research agenda.

This trial aims to evaluate the clinical safety and feasibility of BB intubation in the lateral position, with the primary outcome being the incidence of BB malposition in two groups. BB malposition is the most common complication associated with BB intubation, and numerous clinical studies have shown that its incidence is higher than DLT malposition, reaching approximately 26%–45% [7, 10, 12–14]. To visually and quantitatively measure the effect of lateral BB intubation on BB position, we have defined BB malposition as a movement of more than 15 mm to correct to the optimal position. Currently, there is no widely accepted or standardized definition for BB malposition. Although many clinical trials have investigated the incidence of BB malposition as a secondary outcome, the majority lack a clear definition. Our definition of BB malposition is based on the criteria proposed by Brodsky et al. [24], which considers bronchial cuff herniated into the carina more than 50% or not visible in the entrance of the mainstem bronchus. Given that the total length of the bronchial cuff used in our trial is 30 mm, we therefore define the deviation of 15 mm as the cut-off value for malposition. Furthermore, considering that the study population consists of Chinese individuals, who generally have smaller physiques characteristic of Asian populations, BB with a movement of 15 mm is sufficient to potentially cause significant harm [12].

In thoracic surgery, due to the unique requirement for lateral surgical position, medical staff must reposition anesthetized patients with BB intubation to the lateral position for the surgery. This repositioning process is a critical factor contributing to BB malposition, making it standard practice to re-confirm the BB placement using FOB again after repositioning [8]. To address this issue, we propose an alternative strategy that assists patients into a comfortable and surgery-appropriate lateral position on the surgical table (either left or right position), allowing the anesthesia induction and BB intubation to be performed directly in the lateral position. Performing lateral BB intubation removes the necessity of shifting from supine to lateral position, thereby avoiding the risk of BB malposition associated with this step. This strategy also lightens the physical workload for medical staff and improves surgical turnover efficiency, particularly in managing obese patients. Moreover, we anticipate that lateral BB intubation may reduce the incidence of intraoperative hypotension, postoperative sore throat and hoarseness, and enhance the early postoperative recovery quality of patients. The research team has already demonstrated the feasibility and advantages of DLT intubation in the lateral position [18]. This study protocol on lateral BB intubation represents a deeper and broader exploration of the conceptual framework of the lateral intubation pattern in thoracic anesthesia, aligning more closely with the principles of ERAS and offering benefits to a wider range of patients.

CMIC: Content-Adaptive Mamba for Learned Image Compression
Yunuo Chen Zezheng Lyu Bing He Hongwei Hu Qi Wang, Yuan Tian Li Song Wenjun Zhang Guo Lu

Abstract
Recent Learned image compression (LIC) leverages Mamba-style state-space models (SSMs) for global receptive fields with linear complexity. However, vanilla Mamba is content-agnostic, relying on fixed and predefined selective scans, which restricts its ability to dynamically and fully exploit content dependencies. We introduce Content-Adaptive Mamba (CAM), a dynamic SSM that addresses two critical limitations. First, it employs content-aware token reorganization, clustering and reordering tokens based on content similarity to prioritize proximity in feature space over Euclidean space. Second, it integrates global priors into SSM via a prompt dictionary, effectively mitigating the strict causality and long-range decay in the token interactions of Mamba. These innovations enable CAM to better capture global dependencies while preserving computational efficiency. Leveraging CAM, our Content-Adaptive Mamba-based LIC model (CMIC) achieves state-of-the-art rate-distortion performance, surpassing VTM-21.0 by -15.91%, -21.34%, and -17.58% BD-rate on Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC benchmarks, respectively.

Introduction

Image compression plays a vital role in computer vision and multimedia by reducing storage and bandwidth while preserving visual quality. Learned image compression (LIC) has advanced rapidly, driven by variational autoencoders and end-to-end training (
Ballé et al. 2017
;
Ballé et al. 2018
). Early LIC methods used convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for nonlinear analysis and synthesis transforms (
Minnen et al. 2018
;
Cheng et al. 2020
;
He et al. 2022
;
zhang2024another
). More recently, transformer-based designs expanded receptive fields to capture long-range dependencies (
Zou et al. 2022
;
Zhu et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2024a
).
However, transformers achieve a global receptive field at the cost of quadratic time and memory complexity.
State space models (SSMs) such as Mamba (
Gu and Dao 2023
;
Dao and Gu 2024
;
Zhu et al.
;
Liu et al. 2024
) offer a compelling alternative: they couple global receptive fields with linear complexity and have already been adopted in several LIC models (
Qin et al. 2024
;
Zeng et al. 2025
).

Yet Mamba’s Selective Scan mechanism, originally designed for one-dimensional and causal sequences, faces significant challenges when applied to the inherently two-dimensional nature of images. First, the long-range decay phenomenon causes the influence of distant tokens to diminish rapidly along the sequence
Guo et al. 2024a
.
The problem is compounded by the fixed selective scan mechanism, which rigidly processes tokens in a predefined order without considering their content similarities. The rigid, content-agnostic approach only addresses Euclidean adjacency but fails to exploit the rich content dependencies inherent in images. Semantically neighboring regions often share similar or related visual patterns, and redundancy also typically originates from these areas. Ignoring these relationships, vanilla Mamba potentially scans similar tokens in a non-continuous manner. It weakens Mamba’s ability to capture dependencies between tokens that are far apart in Euclidean space but close in feature space, ultimately leading to suboptimal rate-distortion (RD) performance.
Second, vanilla Mamba’s strict causality restricts each token to accessing only its predecessors in the sequence, leaving subsequent tokens unexplored
Liu et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2024b
, contradicting the non-causal, interconnected nature of 2D images.
These limitations collectively reduce the model’s ability to capture global dependencies, which are critical for high-performance image compression. While multi-directional 2-D scans partially mitigate these issues, they quadruple computational complexity and still rely on Euclidean adjacency for scans (Fig.
1
), failing to adapt to content similarities. These shortcomings highlight the need for a more flexible and global-aware approach to modeling image data.

To overcome these limitations, we introduce Content-Adaptive Mamba (CAM), a dynamic SSM tailored to images. First, we address the content-agnostic fixed scan by clustering and reorganizing latent tokens to group content-similar tokens together. This ensures that tokens are processed in sequence based on their feature-space proximity rather than their spatial (Euclidean) arrangement, encouraging interaction between content-similar tokens. Intuitively, in the context of image compression, the core of nonlinear transformation lies in identifying tokens with similar content and eliminating redundancy between them. By enabling Mamba to perform continuous scanning for similar tokens, we preserve linear complexity while enhancing long-range interactions between similar tokens.

Second, we mitigate the strict causality and long-range decay by introducing a prompt dictionary to enhance the model’s global awareness. Compact prototype vectors are learned to capture shared semantic information for each cluster, while instance-specific prompts are dynamically generated from the dictionary. These prompts are integrated into the SSM, modulating hidden states and relaxing the strict causality with minimal computational overhead. By embedding categorical information and token relationships into the SSM, we expand its context and dynamism, enabling it to capture global dependencies more effectively.

Together, these two strategies enable efficient, content-aware long-range modeling. Built on them, our Content-Adaptive Mamba-based LIC model, CMIC, attains state-of-the-art RD performance, surpassing the traditional codec VTM-21.0 by -15.91%, -21.34%, and -17.58% BD-rate on the Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC datasets, respectively.

Related Work

2.1
Learned Image Compression

End-to-end learned image compression (LIC) has rapidly progressed in recent years. The original CNN-based method by
Ballé et al. 2017
-and its extension that couples VAEs with hyper-priors (
Ballé et al. 2018
)—laid the groundwork for modern LIC. Subsequent studies have mainly boosted rate-distortion performance by refining two elements: the transform networks and the entropy model (
Zafari et al. 2023
;
Mentzer et al. 2022
;
Ma et al. 2019
;
Lu et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Gao et al. 2021
;
Fu et al. 2023
;
Bégaint et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2023
).

Early CNN variants introduced residual blocks (
Cheng et al. 2020
) and octave residual modules (
Chen et al. 2022
), while
Xie et al. 2021
explored invertible architectures. Some works also employ deformable convolutions (
Li et al. 2022a
;
Wang et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2024
) and GNNs (
Tang et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2021
;
Spadaro et al. 2024
to achieve content-adaptive compression.
Recently, Transformer
-
based architectures have surged in LIC research, including Swin
-
Transformer variants (
Zou et al. 2022
;
Zhu et al. 2022
), hybrid CNN-Transformer schemes that fuse local detail with global context(
Liu et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2025
), and frequency
-
aware window attention mechanisms (
Li et al. 2024a
).
Recently, researchers employ linear attention models, such as state space model Mamba (
Gu and Dao 2023
;
Dao and Gu 2024
), for the non-linear transform networks to balance the RD performance and model complexity (
Qin et al. 2024
;
Zeng et al. 2025
).

In terms of entropy models, advances include the autoregressive model by
Minnen et al. 2018
, checkerboard modeling (
He et al. 2021
), channel-wise models (
Minnen and Singh 2020
), and the space-channel context model of
He et al. 2022
. More works propose quadtree hierarchies (
Li et al. 2022b
;
Li et al. 2023
), Transformer-based entropy models (
Qian et al. 2022
), and multi-reference schemes (
Jiang et al. 2023
;
Jiang and Wang 2023
). T-CA further enhances channel-wise autoregression with transformers (
Li et al. 2024a
).
In this paper, we concentrate on refining the Mamba-based transform networks while leveraging the efficient SCCTX entropy model (
He et al. 2022
).

2.2
State Space Models

State space models (SSMs) were first developed in control theory (
Kalman 1960
). They are now popular again in deep learning because their sequential updates cost only linear time in the sequence length, which is ideal for modeling long-range relationships.
S4 (
Gu et al. 2021
) showed that deep SSMs can handle very long contexts, and S5 (
Smith et al. 2022
) improved on this with a multi-input-multi-output SSM and a fast parallel-scan algorithm.
Later, H3 (
Fu et al. 2022
) reduced the performance gap between SSMs and Transformers on NLP tasks.

Mamba.

The newest breakthrough is Mamba (
Gu and Dao 2023
;
Dao and Gu 2024
). Mamba combines data-dependent gating with hardware-efficient kernels, reaching or exceeding Transformers while still scaling linearly.
Researchers have already applied Mamba beyond language to vision tasks such as image classification (
Zhu et al.
;
Liu et al. 2024
), image segmentation (
Yue et al. 2024
), and video understanding (
Li et al. 2024b
).
Some other works focus on low-level tasks:
MambaIR (
Guo et al. 2024b
) tackles local-pixel forgetting and channel redundancy of Mamba while applying it to single-image super-resolution;
FreqMamba (
Zhen et al. 2024
) operates in the Fourier domain for deraining;
MambaLLIE (
Weng et al. 2024
) modifies the state space equation for low-light enhancement; and further adaptations address dehazing, deblurring, rain removal, and fusion tasks (
Zheng and Wu 2024
;
Gao et al. 2024
;
Wu et al. 2024
;
Xie et al. 2024
;
Qiao et al. 2024
).
What’s more, SSMs are also adopted by learned image compression models such as
Qin et al. 2024
and
Zeng et al. 2025
.
Yet these works keep Mamba’s causal design, while 2-D image processing is naturally non-causal; this mismatch can shrink the effective receptive field and hurt RD performance.

3.2
Overview of CMIC Model

First, we give a quick overview of how our Content-Adaptive Mamba-based LIC model (CMIC) works. As shown in Fig.
2
(a), CMIC follows a standard variational autoencoder layout. We split its non-linear transform into six stages based on feature resolution.
Inspired by
Liu et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2024a
, we utilize vanilla window-attention blocks to capture fine-grained local dependencies, while our proposed CAM blocks are introduced to enhance long-range modeling. A Conv-Feed Forward Network is added for efficient channel-wise information aggregation.
Our entropy model is built upon the SCCTX entropy model (
He et al. 2022
). As illustrated in Fig.
3
, we incorporate depthwise convolution for efficient context modeling and employ a gated MLP for parameter aggregation. More details are provided in supplementary materials.

As shown in Fig.
2
(b), the core part of CAM block is a Dynamic SSM. It first clusters tokens into several categories, and reorders the flattened token sequence to bring similar tokens into proximity, enhancing local coherence in the token sequence.
Meanwhile, instance-specific prompts are generated from the prompt dictionary.
The reordered tokens, infused with these prompts, are scanned by a 1-D selective scan for efficient long-range modeling.

We detail two key components for Dynamic SSM:
(i) Content-Adaptive Token Aggregation: A clustering mechanism groups and reorders tokens to encourage interaction between content-similar tokens.
(ii) Global Priors Prompting: A learnable prompt dictionary generates instance-specific prompts with global priors to condition the selective scan.

The K-Means algorithm
(Algorithm

It begins by computing the cosine similarity between each token

The algorithm outputs the final assignments

During inference, we directly utilize the updated cluster centers to group the input tokens.

Sequence re-ordering.

Given the updated centroids, final assignments

is determined by the cosine similarity between tokens and cluster centers.
We build a permutation
\pi:\{1,\dots,N\}\!\rightarrow\!\{1,\dots,N\}
that
first groups all tokens with

Applying

\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{X}_{\pi(\cdot)},

so that tokens belonging to the same cluster become contiguous in 1-D sequence,
enhancing local coherence of the sequence, and encouraging interactions between similar tokens. This permutation enables SSM to focus on proximity in feature space rather than in Euclidean space.
The inverse permutation

3.4
Global Priors Prompting

Learnable Prompt Dictionary.

To enhance the model’s global awareness and mitigate the strict causality of Mamba, we introduce a learnable prompt dictionary
\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d_{s}}
, where
K
is the number of clusters and
d_{s}
is the state space dimension of Mamba. Each prompt in this dictionary is a vector with size
d_{s}
and corresponds to one of the clusters.
As shown in Fig.
2
(b), the one-hot category assignment matrix
\bm{\Gamma}\in\{0,1\}^{N\times K}
is formed by concatenating one-hot vectors, each representing the cluster membership of a token. The instance-specific prompts
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{s}}
are generated via

\mathbf{P}\;=\;\bm{\Gamma}\,\mathbf{D}.

This dictionary learns typical prior knowledge from the training data, producing prompts that adapt to the input’s semantic layout. Combining global prototypes with instance-specific information, it enriches the model’s understanding of token relationships and global dependencies, effectively relaxing strict causality and improving long-range modeling.

Prompt Conditioning in Mamba.

The vanilla Mamba layer models token interactions with a discrete
state-space system, expressed as:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{A}},\;\bar{\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=\;\exp(\Delta\mathbf{A}),\;\bigl(\Delta\mathbf{A}\bigr)^{-1}\!\!\bigl(\exp(\Delta\mathbf{A})-\mathbf{I}\bigr)\,\Delta\mathbf{B},

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{i}

\displaystyle=\;\bar{\mathbf{A}}\,\mathbf{h}_{i-1}+\bar{\mathbf{B}}\,\mathbf{x}_{i},

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}

\displaystyle=\;\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{h}_{i}+\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{x}_{i},

where
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
i
-th input token,

\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}
the hidden state,
and
\mathbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the output.
Matrix
\bar{\mathbf{A}}
acts as the state-transition
or control matrix,
\bar{\mathbf{B}}
as the input matrix,
while
\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{D}
project the hidden state and the
current input back to the output space.
Following the Attentive State-Space Model of

Guo et al. 2024a
,
we incorporate the global-prior prompt sequence

\mathbf{P}=\{\mathbf{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}

by augmenting the output matrix:

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{A}}\,\mathbf{h}_{i-1}+\bar{\mathbf{B}}\,\mathbf{x}_{i},

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\mathbf{C}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{P}}\bigr)\,\mathbf{h}_{i}+\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{x}_{i},

where
\mathbf{P}
is the prompt vector encoding instance-specific global priors.
The output kernel
\mathbf{C}
functions analogously to the query in a self-attention layer
Guo et al. 2024a
: it determines which
components of the hidden state are read out.
By dynamically modulating the context matrix
\mathbf{C}

\mathbf{P}

Consequently, global priors that capture not only the instance-specific categorical information, but also tokens’ intricate categorical relationships are encoded in the dynamic SSM.
This enhances SSM to reason about distant, unscanned tokens, enabling more content-adaptive long-range feature aggregation.

Experiments

4.1
Experimental Setup

We train our CMIC models on the Flickr2W dataset (
Liu et al. 2020
), optimizing with the Adam optimizer (
Kingma 2015
) and an initial learning rate of
10^{-4}
. Distortion is quantified using two metrics: mean square error (MSE) and multiscale structural similarity (MS-SSIM). For the MSE-oriented models, the Lagrangian multiplier
\lambda
is chosen from
\{0.0017,\,0.0025,\,0.0035,\,0.0067,\,0.0130,\,0.0250,\,0.050\}
, while for the MS-SSIM-oriented models we use
\{3,\,5,\,8,\,16,\,32,\,64\}
. All experiments are carried out on NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
We configure channel dimensions
\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}\}=\{128,192,256,320\}
and set the numbers of non-linear transform blocks
\{L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}=\{3,2,2\}
. For entropy coding, we adopt the SCCTX scheme with five slices from ELIC (
He et al. 2022
). The cluster count is fixed at
64
. The window size of the attention modules is set as 8. The iterative K-Means clustering is updated 5 times per training step. Notably, this iterative process accounts for only 5% of each step’s training time, introducing minimal overhead and maintaining efficient training.

4.2
Evaluation

We test our models on three image datasets: the Kodak dataset (
Kodak 1993
) at
768\times 512
, the Tecnick test set (
Asuni et al. 2014
) at
1200\times 1200
, and the CLIC professional validation set (
CLIC 2021
) at 2K resolution. To assess rate-distortion performance, we report peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), multiscale structural similarity (MS-SSIM), and bits per pixel (bpp). Average bitrate savings are quantified via BD-rate (
Bjontegaard 2001
), and average PSNR improvement at matched bitrates via BD-PSNR. Model complexity is characterized by total parameter count, floating-point operations (FLOPs), peak GPU memory and decoding latency.

4.3
Rate-Distortion Performance

Our CMIC model is benchmarked against the conventional codec VTM-21.0 (
Browne et al.
;
Bross et al. 2021
), as well as several state-of-the-art (SOTA) learned methods including ELIC (
He et al. 2022
), TCM (
Liu et al. 2023
), MLIC++ (
Jiang and Wang 2023
), FTIC (
Li et al. 2024a
), CCA (
Han et al. 2024
), and two Mamba-based LIC models, MambaVC (
Qin et al. 2024
) and MambaIC (
Zeng et al. 2025
). The resulting rate-distortion curves, shown in Fig.
5
-
6

. Our CMIC model achieves superior performance, reducing BD-rate by 15.91%, 21.34%, and 17.58% on the Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC datasets, respectively.

Our experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CMIC model consistently outperforms competing methods across all datasets. Specifically, in terms of BD-PSNR improvements, CMIC achieves significant gains over the transformer-based LIC model FTIC, with improvements of 0.15 dB, 0.32 dB, and 0.36 dB on the Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick datasets, respectively. Furthermore, the advantages become even more pronounced when compared to the CNN-transformer mixed model TCM-L, where CMIC achieves BD-PSNR increases of 0.28 dB, 0.40 dB, and 0.53 dB on the corresponding datasets.
In terms of MS-SSIM, the CMIC model also achieves notable improvements, with gains of 0.52 dB and 0.30 dB on the CLIC dataset compared to TCM-L and FTIC, respectively, as shown in Fig.

. These results not only highlight the robustness of our approach but also validate its superiority across a wide range of resolutions.

The CMIC model demonstrates a particularly strong performance on higher-resolution datasets, such as CLIC and Tecnick, underscoring the CAM’s effectiveness in global modeling. When compared to Mamba-based models, CMIC outperforms MambaVC by 7.51%, 6.80%, and 10.09% in BD-rate on the Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick datasets, respectively. Similarly, CMIC surpasses MambaIC by 2.36%, 2.17%, and 6.48% in BD-rate on these datasets. As illustrated in Fig.

5
Limitation, Future Work and Broader Impact

Although our CMIC architecture achieves outstanding rate-distortion performance, it primarily focuses on aggregating content-similar tokens and fostering interactions among them. It does not enforce any specific scanning order across clusters or impose sorting mechanisms within individual clusters. In future work, we aim to introduce hierarchical reorganization and sorting strategies for both clusters and intra-cluster tokens, enhancing adaptability to diverse image content.

By exploring this dynamic SSM, our approach advances Mamba-based compression and fosters the evolution of adaptive LIC. It provides a foundation for refining Mamba models and developing adaptive strategies in the future. We hope this work inspires continued progress in these areas.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced Content-Adaptive Mamba for Image Compression (CMIC), a novel approach tailored to align Mamba-style State Space models with the inherent two-dimensional structure of images. By employing clustering mechanism and a learnable prompt dictionary, we dynamically enhance the state-space model’s ability to capture long-range dependencies. Specifically, our content-adaptive scanning strategy sequentially processes tokens with similar content, emphasizing feature-space proximity rather than traditional Euclidean adjacency. Additionally, embedding global priors via the prompt dictionary relaxes the strict causality, significantly improving global context modeling. Consequently, CMIC efficiently aggregates global context while retaining linear computational complexity. Experimental results demonstrate that CMIC achieves SOTA RD performance, surpassing the traditional codec VTM-21.0 substantially across the Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC datasets. These outcomes validate the effectiveness of our method in addressing the critical challenges of Mamba-based image compression.

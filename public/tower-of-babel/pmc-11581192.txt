Immune persistence and booster response of a quadrivalent meningococcal conjugate vaccine (MenACYW-TT) 5 years after primary vaccination of adults at ≥56 years of age
Authors: Corwin A. Robertson, Jeffry Jacqmein, Alexandre Selmani, Katherine Galarza, Philipp Oster
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTStage I of this study (NCT04142242) demonstrated the safety and immunogenicity of a booster dose of a licensed quadrivalent meningococcal tetanus toxoid-conjugate vaccine (MenACYW-TT) and immune persistence 3 and 6–7 years after priming in older adults who received either quadrivalent meningococcal polysaccharide vaccine (MPSV4) or MenACYW-TT at ≥56 years of age. Stage II, reported here, assessed the antibody persistence after MenACYW-TT versus MPSV4 priming and the safety and immunogenicity of a booster dose of MenACYW-TT in older adults 5 years after primary vaccination with either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT. A serum bactericidal assay (hSBA) was used to measure functional antibodies against each serogroup immediately before MenACYW-TT booster vaccination and on day (D) 30 post-booster. Safety was also assessed. Antibody persistence declined 5 years post-primary vaccination, with seroprotection (hSBA titer ≥1:8) rates trending higher in MenACYW-TT- versus MPSV4-primed participants. A robust immune response for all four serogroups was observed on D30 after the MenACYW-TT booster, with higher geometric mean titers and seroprotection rates in MenACYW-TT- versus MPSV4-primed participants. Safety outcomes were similar between the two groups. A single booster dose of MenACYW-TT was well tolerated and immunogenic in older adults, with a higher immune response observed in those primed with MenACYW-TT.

Introduction

Invasive meningococcal disease (IMD), caused by the bacterium Neisseria meningitidis, is a major cause of meningitis and septicemia globally.1,2 Although twelve different serogroups of N. meningitidis have been identified, six serogroups (A, B, C, W, X, and Y) are responsible for the majority of IMD cases worldwide.3 Infants, adolescents, young adults and the elderly have the highest rates of IMD4; however, recommendations provided by National Immunization Programs (NIPs) have predominately targeted infants, toddlers, children, and adolescents.1 Though NIPs vary from country to country, they are constantly evolving in response to IMD epidemiology, clinical outcome, cost-effectiveness, and disease burden1,5 and have contributed to a more rapid decline in the rates of IMD among younger age groups than in older age groups.1,2,6 Moreover, older adults experience higher IMD-associated case-fatality rates relative to other age groups.2,7

Additionally, attendance at mass gatherings, such as the Hajj or Umrah pilgrimage to Mecca in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, has been shown to facilitate transmission of meningococcal disease.8,9 Past outbreaks prompted the need for compulsory vaccination against meningococcal serogroups A, C, W, and Y; however, due to the large number of Hajj and Umrah pilgrims from around the world, with a substantial proportion being older adults (>55 years of age),8–10 and the fact that protection tends to wane within 3–5 years following primary vaccination,10,11 outbreaks remain a possibility. In fact, beginning in April 2024, 12 cases of meningococcal disease linked to Umrah pilgrimage were reported to national public health agencies in three countries (the United States [US], France, and the United Kingdom [UK]).12,13 Notably, 11 of the 12 recent Umrah cases were caused by N. meningitidis serogroup W, sequence type ST-11, clonal complex CC11; two of these cases occurred in children who were household contacts of an asymptomatic adult traveler.12–15

Due to the rapid onset and progression of IMD, antibiotic chemoprophylaxis should be given to close contacts of persons with meningococcal disease as soon as possible after exposure (ideally, within <24 hours), regardless of vaccination status.12,13 That said, vaccination is considered the best strategy for prevention on a population level.12 The quadrivalent meningococcal polysaccharide vaccine, MPSV4 (Menomune®), was first licensed in 1981 in several countries; prior to its discontinuation in 2017, it was the only meningococcal vaccine approved by the US Food and Drug Administration and available for use in adults ≥56 years of age.10,16 More recently, MenACYW-TT (MenQuadfi®), a quadrivalent meningococcal tetanus toxoid-conjugate vaccine, was licensed for use in individuals aged ≥2 years in the US and in individuals aged ≥12 months in European Union (EU) countries and some countries outside the EU. Additionally, depending on risk factors for IMD (e.g., travel to certain countries, including pilgrimage and other mass gatherings, medical condition, occupation), meningococcal revaccination may be recommended for individuals who have been previously vaccinated with plain polysaccharide (MPSV4) or MenACWY conjugate vaccines.17 For example, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia requires pilgrims and seasonal workers to provide proof of either meningococcal vaccination with quadrivalent meningococcal conjugate vaccine ≤5 years before arrival or vaccination with MPSV4 between ≥10 days and ≤3 years before arrival.17

The effects of a booster dose of MenACYW-TT in older adults have been assessed in a two-stage Phase III study. Previously published results from Stage I demonstrated the safety and immunogenicity of a booster dose of MenACYW-TT in MPSV4- and MenACYW-TT-primed older adults, as well as immune persistence ≥3 and 6–7 years after the primary vaccination.10 Stage II of this study, reported herein, assessed the antibody persistence of MenACYW-TT compared with MPSV4 and evaluated the safety and immunogenicity of a booster dose of MenACYW-TT in older adults who received either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT 5 years earlier, at age ≥56 years.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This was a Phase III, randomized, open-label, two-stage, multicenter trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04142242, registered October 29, 2019) conducted at 34 sites across the US and Puerto Rico between October 2019 and May 2022. Stage II of the study began in October 2021.

A detailed description of the study design has been previously published (Supplementary Figure S1).10 Briefly, participants were eligible for inclusion if they had received a primary vaccination with either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT ≥5 years earlier at age ≥56 years as a participant in either a Phase III study (NCT02842866), conducted from 2016 to 2017, or a Phase II study (NCT01732627), conducted in 2012. During Stage I of the current study (previously published),10 participants who had received either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT ≥3 years previously were randomized in a 9:2 ratio to either receive a booster dose of MenACYW-TT (Stage I: MPSV4-primed3y+booster and MenACYW-TT-primed3y+booster) or have blood drawn for antibody persistence at 3 years (Stage I: MPSV4-primed5y and MenACYW-TT-primed5y). In Stage II, participants in the MPSV4-primed5y and MenACYW-TT-primed5y groups, who did not receive a booster during Stage I, had blood drawn for 5-year antibody persistence and subsequently received a booster dose of MenACYW-TT at Visit 2, Day (D) 0 of Stage II (i.e., 2 years after enrollment).

Prior to study initiation, approval was obtained from the institutional review board Advarra (Columbia, MD, USA), and all participants provided written informed consent. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles stated in the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonization guidelines for Good Clinical Practice.

Intervention

Each dose of MenACYW-TT (Sanofi, Swiftwater, PA, USA) was provided as a 0.5 mL liquid solution and administered by intramuscular injection into the deltoid muscle. Each dose contained 10 μg of polysaccharide for each serogroup (A, C, W, and Y) and approximately 55 μg of tetanus toxoid protein carrier in solution.

Study objectives

The primary objective of this two-stage Phase III study was to demonstrate “vaccine seroresponse sufficiency” (i.e., lower limit of the 95% confidence interval [CI] of > 40% for the proportion of participants with seroresponse) to meningococcal serogroups A, C, W, and Y, 30 days after administration of a booster dose of MenACYW-TT in participants who had received MPSV4 for primary vaccination 3 years earlier at ≥56 years of age (Stage I, previously published).10 Secondary objectives for Stage I were to assess vaccine seroresponse 30 days after booster vaccination in participants who had received MenACYW-TT 3 years earlier, seroresponse 6 days after booster vaccination (descriptive analysis using a subset of participants only) in those who had received either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT 3 years earlier, and antibody persistence (descriptive analysis) at D0 after initial vaccination with MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT 3 or 6–7 years earlier (previously published).10 The primary and secondary objectives of demonstrating sufficiency of seroresponse following a MenACYW-TT booster dose in participants primed with either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT, respectively, were met in Stage I (previously published).10

Stage II of the study evaluated secondary objectives. The immunogenicity objectives were to summarize antibody persistence 5 years after primary vaccination with either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT and the immunogenicity of a single booster dose of MenACYW-TT 30 days after administration among those who received primary vaccination with MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT 5 years earlier. The safety objective for Stage II was to summarize the safety of a booster dose of MenACYW-TT administered 5 years after primary vaccination with either MPSV4 or MenACYW-TT in older adults.

Immunogenicity

Blood samples were collected at Visit 2, D0 of Stage II (prior to booster vaccination), and 30 days after booster vaccination. Antibody titers against meningococcal serogroups A, C, W, and Y were measured by a serum bactericidal assay utilizing human complement (hSBA; Global Clinical Immunology, Sanofi, Swiftwater, PA, USA), as previously described.18,19 The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) for the hSBA assay was 1:4. Immunogenicity assessments were also measured by serum bactericidal assay using rabbit complement (rSBA; UK Health Security Agency [formerly Public Health England], Manchester, UK), with an LLOQ of 1:4.

Seroresponse was defined as a post-vaccination hSBA titer ≥ 1:16 for participants with a baseline titer < 1:8 or a ≥ 4-fold increase in titer post-vaccination for those with a baseline titer ≥ 1:8. Seroprotection was defined as an hSBA titer ≥ 1:8. For the assessment of immunogenicity by rSBA, seroresponse was defined as a post-vaccination titer ≥ 1:32 for participants with pre-vaccination rSBA titer < 1:8, or a post-vaccination titer ≥ 4 times the pre-vaccination titer for those with a pre-vaccination rSBA titer ≥ 1:8.

Safety

All participants were observed on-site for 30 minutes after vaccination to ensure their safety and document any immediate unsolicited systemic adverse events (AEs). Participants used diary cards to record solicited injection site reactions (pain, erythema, and swelling) and solicited systemic reactions (fever, headache, malaise, and myalgia) through D7 and unsolicited AEs through D30; this included the occurrence, nature, time to onset, duration, and intensity of the AE, action taken, relationship to vaccine, and whether it led to early study discontinuation/termination. Medically attended AEs (MAAEs), AEs of special interest (AESIs; generalized seizures, Guillain-Barré syndrome, and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura), and serious AEs (SAEs) were reported from D0 through D30.

Statistical analysis

No formal sample size calculations were performed for this study because the expected number of participants to be recruited from previous studies was estimated to be below the number of participants required to reach 80% study power. As such, a total of 80 participants were planned to be enrolled in Stage II: 40 each in the MPSV4-primed5y and MenACYW-TT-primed5y groups.

Immunogenicity analysis was performed on the Stage II D30 per-protocol analysis set (PPAS3) and confirmed on the Stage II D30 full analysis set (FAS4). FAS4 included all participants from the MPSV4-primed5y and MenACYW-TT-primed5y groups who received the booster vaccine (MPSV4-primed5y+booster and MenACYW-TT-primed5y+booster, respectively) and had a valid serology result for at least one serogroup from the post vaccination blood sample collected at D30. The PPAS3 was a subset of FAS4 that excluded participants with one or more major protocol deviations with a potential to affect the immunogenicity assessment.

Antibody persistence was analyzed in the FAS for persistence (FAS5), which included all participants from the MPSV4-primed5y and MenACYW-TT-primed5y groups with a valid serology result for at least one serogroup from a pre-vaccination blood sample. Safety was analyzed in the safety analysis set (SafAS2), which included all participants from the MPSV4-primed5y+booster and MenACWY-TT-primed5y+booster groups (i.e., all participants who received a vaccine during Stage II).

Categorical variables were summarized and presented by frequency counts, proportions and 95% CIs. The 95% CIs of the point estimates were calculated using the exact binomial method (Clopper-Pearson method). Continuous variables were summarized by calculating means, standard deviations, and 95% CIs. The 95% CIs of point estimates were calculated using the normal approximation for quantitative data. For geometric mean titers (GMTs), 95% CIs of point estimates were calculated using the normal approximation assuming they are log-normally distributed.

Results

Participants

At the start of Stage I, a total of 70 participants were enrolled to continue the study during Stage II and provided a blood sample at Visit 1. Of these, 31 participants had received primary vaccination with MPSV4 (MPSV4-primed5y group), and 39 participants were included in the group that received primary vaccination with MenACYW-TT (MenACYW-TT-primed5y group). Two participants in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group were incorrectly assessed by the investigator as having received MenACYW-TT in study NCT02842866 despite having actually received MPSV4; this was noted as a protocol deviation and these participants were excluded from the PPAS3 analysis subset.

In Stage II, 25 of 31 (80.6%) participants in the MPSV4-primed5y group and 28 of 39 (71.8%) participants in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group provided a blood sample for persistence at Visit 2 (D0 of Stage II) prior to booster vaccination. Of these, 25 participants in the MPSV4-primed5y and 27 participants in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y groups received a booster dose of MenACYW-TT at Visit 2. All participants vaccinated with MenACYW-TT during Stage II, except for one participant in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group, provided a blood sample 30 days post vaccination. As such, the FAS4 was composed of 25 participants from the MPSV4-primed5y group and 26 participants from the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group, and the PPAS3 was composed of 16 participants from the MPSV4-primed5y group and 20 participants from the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group. The FAS5 was composed of 24 participants from the MPSV4-primed5y group and 28 participants from the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group.

Baseline demographics for the 52 participants in the FAS5 are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. The mean age was 69.1 years (range: 59.0–85.0 years), 59.6% of participants were female, 88.5% were white, and 11.5% were Hispanic or Latino. Baseline demographics were comparable across analysis sets.

Immunogenicity

Persistence of immunogenicity following primary vaccination (FAS5)

Five years after primary vaccination (D0 of Stage II), hSBA GMTs declined in both the MPSV4-primed5y and MenACYW-TT-primed5y groups, with hSBA GMTs trending higher for MenACYW-TT- primed participants (Table 1). In the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group, GMTs 5 years after priming trended higher than the pre-priming GMTs for serogroups C, W, and Y and were comparable for serogroup A (Table 1). Additionally, the proportions of participants with seroprotection (hSBA titer ≥ 1:8) trended higher in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y group compared with the MPSV4-primed5y group for serogroups A, C, W, and Y (Figure 1a). Similar trends in rSBA GMTs, rSBA seroresponse rates, and the proportions of participants with rSBA titers ≥ 1:8 and ≥ 1:128 were observed in both groups 5 years after primary vaccination, with comparable results observed across analysis sets (data not shown). Immunogenicity of booster dose 5 years after primary vaccination (FAS5 and PPAS3)

Thirty days after the booster dose (D30 of Stage II), hSBA GMTs increased from baseline (D0 of Stage II) for all serogroups and were higher in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y+booster group than in the MPSV4-primed5y+booster group (Table 1). In the MenACYW-TT- primed5y+booster group, hSBA GMTs on D30 post-booster were higher or trended higher than those on D30 post-primary vaccination for all serogroups, whereas in the MPSV4-primed5y+booster group, hSBA GMTs on D30 post-booster were comparable to or trended slightly higher than those on D30 post-primary vaccination for serogroups A, W, and Y but trended lower for serogroup C.

Similarly, seroresponse and seroprotection rates increased in both groups 30 days post-booster compared with D0 of Stage II and were either higher or trended higher in the MenACYW-TT-primed5y+booster group compared with the MPSV4-primed5y+booster group (Table 2; Figure 1b). Safety

The MenACYW-TT booster dose administered 5 years after primary vaccination was well tolerated, with a safety profile consistent with that observed during primary vaccination.20 A summary of safety findings is presented in Table 3. No immediate AEs were reported within 30 minutes of booster vaccination, and no discontinuations due to SAEs, deaths, or vaccine-related SAEs were reported during the study. Overall, at least one solicited reaction was reported by 10 of 27 (37.0%) MPSV4-primed5y+booster participants and 5 of 23 (21.7%) MenACYW-TT-primed5y+booster participants within 7 days of booster vaccination. The most common solicited injection site reaction was pain, and the most commonly reported systemic reactions were headache and myalgia (MPSV4-primed5y+booster group) and myalgia (MenACYW-TT-primed5y+booster group). All solicited reactions began within 3 days of booster vaccination and most resolved within 1–3 days of occurrence. Only one participant, in the MPSV4-primed5y+booster group, reported an unsolicited AE (arthralgia) that was also an MAAE. One SAE was experienced within 30 days of vaccination (osteoarthritis in one MenACYW-TT-primed5y+booster participant) and was judged by the investigator to be unrelated to the booster vaccination. Discussion

Stage II of this Phase III study showed that although antibody persistence declined 5 years after primary vaccination at ≥56 years of age in older adults, hSBA GMTs generally trended higher in MenACYW-TT-primed participants than in MPSV4-primed participants. Notably, among MenACYW-TT-primed participants, GMTs for serogroups C, W, and Y trended higher 5 years post-primary vaccination compared with pre-priming GMTs, indicating long-term immune persistence. This study also demonstrated a robust response for all four serogroups following a booster dose of MenACYW-TT administered to older adults primed 5 years earlier with either MenACYW-TT or MPSV4, with GMTs and seroresponse and seroprotection rates higher or trending higher in MenACYW-TT-primed participants than MPSV4-primed participants. hSBA GMTs were higher or trended higher on D30 post-booster than on D30 post-primary vaccination for all serogroups in MenACYW-TT-primed participants, whereas in the MPSV4-primed group, GMTs for serogroups A, W, and Y on D30 post-booster were only slightly higher than those on D30 post-primary vaccination, and trended slightly lower for serogroup C. Lastly, the MenACYW-TT booster dose was well tolerated in older adults, with a similar safety profile regardless of the priming vaccine received.

Where there is a substantial period between initial vaccination and booster vaccination, the vaccine administered for the primary dose may be unknown21; this may be the case in a significant proportion of older adults. However, data available to date indicate that different meningococcal conjugate vaccines can be used interchangeably.22,23

The current study demonstrated that a booster dose of MenACYW-TT elicits a robust immune response in older adults, regardless of priming vaccine received; however, the immune response was less robust in MPSV4-primed participants. Our study was not designed or powered to show statistical differences between the groups. However, it is possible that a study with a larger sample size and an acceptable study power (i.e., 80%) could confirm a less robust response following MPSV4 priming, as this would be expected for a polysaccharideprotein conjugate vaccine dose administered following a pure polysaccharide vaccine. Differences in the immune response observed in this study can be attributed to the compositional differences between the vaccines used for primary vaccination. Polysaccharide vaccines only stimulate short-term B cell-mediated immunity and can result in hyporesponsiveness with repeated dosing.24–27 In contrast, conjugate vaccines induce T-cell-dependent immune responses, which produces higher antibody titers, increased immune persistence, and enhanced immunologic memory, and results in robust booster responses.24–27 These findings are consistent with those of an earlier study in adolescents, which found that the use of a quadrivalent meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccine (MCV4-DT) was safe and effective for priming and as a booster following primary vaccination with either MCV4-DT or MPSV4.28

This study had some limitations that warrant discussion. The study had a small sample size because participants were recruited from those who completed previous studies, and thus was intended to be descriptive only. The small sample size may limit the generalizability of results and resulted in insufficient power to draw definitive conclusions. Additionally, although an hSBA titer of ≥ 1:4 is widely accepted as a correlate of protection and as a marker of efficacy for vaccine studies, the clinical benefit of MenACYW-TT booster vaccination in terms of long-term protection has yet to be determined.

In conclusion, this study showed that although antibody persistence declined 5 years after primary vaccination, seroprotection rates trended higher in older adults who received a primary dose of MenACYW-TT than in those who had received a primary dose of MPSV4. Additionally, a single booster dose of MenACYW-TT administered to older adults primed 5 years prior with either MenACYW-TT or MPSV4 at ≥56 years of age induced a robust immune response and had a reassuring safety profile. These findings show that MenACYW-TT is a safe and immunogenic booster for older adults, including those at increased risk of meningococcal disease due to travel and attendance of mass gatherings such as Hajj or Umrah.

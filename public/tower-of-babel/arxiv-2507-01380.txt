Constraining Λ ​ CDM \Lambda\mathrm{CDM} , ω ​ CDM \omega\mathrm{CDM} , and ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} models with DESI DR2 BAO: Redshift-Resolved Diagnostics and the Role of r d r_{\mathrm{d}}
Seokcheon Lee

Abstract
We present a detailed comparison of Λ ​ CDM \Lambda\mathrm{CDM} , ω ​ CDM \omega\mathrm{CDM} , and ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} cosmologies using the latest redshift-resolved BAO measurements from DESI DR2. By separating r d r_{\mathrm{d}} -dependent and r d r_{\mathrm{d}} -independent observables and applying both fixed and model-derived sound horizon values, we test the consistency of each model across 24 24 observables spanning 0.2 < z < 2.3 0.2<z<2.3 . Our results show that the ω ​ CDM \omega\mathrm{CDM} model is statistically favored over Λ ​ CDM \Lambda\mathrm{CDM} , with lower χ 2 \chi^{2} , AIC, and BIC values and reduced residuals—especially at z = 0.706 z=0.706 and z = 0.934 z=0.934 . Conversely, the ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} model is strongly disfavored, even when using model-specific r d r_{\mathrm{d}} , due to large systematic residuals driven by the unphysical high-redshift behavior of the CPL parametrization. We show that large negative ω a \omega_{a} values suppress H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) and exaggerate D M ​ ( z ) D_{M}(z) at z ≳ 1.5 z\gtrsim 1.5 , introducing instability in the early expansion history. Our analysis underscores the importance of redshift-resolved diagnostics and r d r_{\mathrm{d}} -independent ratios like D M / D H D_{M}/D_{H} in assessing dark energy models. Future combined analyses with supernovae or lensing may help isolate genuine late-time deviations from Λ ​ CDM \Lambda\mathrm{CDM} .



I
Introduction

Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) serve as a powerful cosmological standard ruler, enabling precise reconstruction of the universe’s expansion history and offering stringent tests of dark energy models
SDSS:2005xqv
. The latest DESI Data Release 2 (DR2) provides high-precision measurements of four types of BAO observables—
D_{V}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
,
D_{M}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
,
D_{H}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
, and
D_{M}/D_{H}
—across six redshift bins, offering a unique opportunity to evaluate competing cosmological models with unprecedented detail
DESI:2025zgx
.

Most BAO analyses assume a fixed value of the sound horizon scale,
r_{\mathrm{d}}=147.05
Mpc, derived from Planck CMB constraints within the standard
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
model
Planck:2018vyg
. However, in models with time-varying dark energy, such as the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model
Chevallier:2000qy
;
Linder:2002et
, the late-time expansion history can influence the early-universe quantity
r_{\mathrm{d}}
through its effect on the recombination epoch
BOSS:2014hhw
;
Knox:2019rjx
. Ignoring this dependence can lead to biased parameter estimates and misleading model comparisons.

While the DESI DR2 analysis uses a fitting formula to estimate
r_{\mathrm{d}}
, yielding approximately
142
Mpc for the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
case
DESI:2025zgx
, our approach directly computes
r_{\mathrm{d}}
using the Boltzmann code CLASS
Blas:2011rf
with literature-based best-fit parameters
(\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}},\omega_{0},\omega_{a})
. This provides a self-consistent treatment of how evolving dark energy influences the sound horizon.

In this work, we evaluate the viability of three cosmological models—
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
,
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
, and
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
—using the latest DESI DR2 BAO measurements. We develop a systematic framework to separate and analyze
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent and
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent observables, and perform model fits under both fixed and model-specific sound horizon assumptions. To identify the sources of model tension, we analyze residuals at the per-bin level, compute pull statistics, and examine the response of background observables
H(z)
and
D_{M}(z)
to variations in the CPL parameters.

Our goal is twofold: (1) to determine which dark energy model best describes the DESI DR2 data, and (2) to assess whether the CPL framework introduces high-redshift instabilities that degrade its physical validity. By combining statistical metrics with physically interpretable diagnostics, our analysis provides a robust assessment of model performance and offers guidance for future efforts in model selection and probe combination.

D_{M}
,

. Section

r_{d}

\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
,

, and

assumptions, using global diagnostics. Section

presents a redshift-resolved model comparison by evaluating model performance separately in each redshift bin. We consider three observable combinations—
r_{d}
-dependent,
r_{d}
-independent, and combined—and report per-bin
\chi^{2}
, AIC, BIC, and pull statistics. This section helps identify whether tensions arise from specific redshift ranges or particular types of observables, and it clarifies how fixed versus model-dependent

To address these questions, we construct a redshift-resolved model comparison framework that separates
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent and
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent observables and systematically tests cosmological models under both fixed and self-consistent sound horizon assumptions. This approach allows us to disentangle model tensions arising from late-time dynamics versus mismatches in early-universe calibration, providing new insight into where and how standard cosmology begins to falter.

Cosmological Framework and Fiducial Parameters

In this section, we present the theoretical background relevant to our analysis. We define the cosmological models, the expansion history, distance observables, the sound horizon scale, and the fiducial parameters used in our CLASS-based calculations.

III
Data and Theoretical Modeling

This section presents the BAO data from the DESI Data Release 2 (DR2), along with the theoretical modeling framework used to interpret the observables. We first describe the types of observables and redshift binning, then detail the construction of the covariance matrix. Finally, we discuss how the sound horizon scale
r_{d}
is computed for each cosmological model, including both fixed and model-dependent approaches based on the CLASS Boltzmann solver.

III.1
DESI DR2 Observables and Redshift Bins

DESI DR2 provides four types of BAO observables:

\left\{D_{V}(z)/r_{d},\;D_{M}(z)/r_{d},\;D_{H}(z)/r_{d},\;D_{M}(z)/D_{H}(z)\right\}\,.

(10)

These observables are reported across seven effective redshift bins, derived from a configuration-space analysis of galaxy clustering. Each observable includes its own measurement uncertainty. A summary of the redshift bins and available observables can be found in Table IV of Ref.
DESI:2025zgx
.

III.2
Covariance Matrix Construction

Although DESI DR2 provides observational uncertainties and partial correlation coefficients, it does not release the full covariance matrix. We reconstruct a block-diagonal covariance matrix based on the reported uncertainties and redshift-specific correlation coefficients, following the methodology developed in Ref.
Lee:2025kbn
. Each block corresponds to a redshift bin and includes up to three correlated observables.

The resulting matrix covers 19 observables across six redshift bins and is used in all likelihood-based calculations, including
\chi^{2}
, AIC, and BIC statistics. While our reconstruction assumes Gaussian errors and neglects bin-to-bin correlations, it provides a consistent and reproducible basis for model comparison. The explicit structure and numerical values of the reconstructed matrix are presented in Appendix
A
.

III.3
Fixed vs. Model-Dependent Sound Horizon

Many BAO analyses adopt a fixed sound horizon scale, typically
r_{d}=147.05
Mpc, consistent with the Planck 2018 best-fit
\Lambda
CDM cosmology
BOSS:2016wmc
;
Planck:2018vyg
;
eBOSS:2020tmo
. However,
r_{d}
is not a universal constant—it depends sensitively on early-universe parameters such as the baryon density
\Omega_{b}h^{2}
, matter density
\Omega_{m0}h^{2}
, and the effective number of relativistic species
N_{\rm eff}
. For dynamical dark energy models like
w_{0}w_{a}
CDM, the late-time expansion history can indirectly alter the recombination epoch and thus the value of
r_{d}

Brieden:2022heh
. To account for this, we compute model-dependent
r_{d}
values using the Boltzmann solver CLASS for each model’s parameter set.

III.4
Implementation in CLASS

For each cosmological model, we fix the parameters
(\Omega_{b}h^{2},\Omega_{c}h^{2},h,w_{0},w_{a})
to literature-based best-fit values and use CLASS to compute the drag redshift
z_{d}
and the corresponding sound horizon. The computed
r_{d}
values vary across models:

•

For
\Lambda
CDM and
w
CDM: variation is mild (within
\sim
2%),

•

For
w_{0}w_{a}
CDM: deviation can reach 5–8 Mpc due to the combined effects of
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
.

These values are used consistently in the evaluation of theoretical predictions for
D_{V}
,
D_{M}
, and
D_{H}
, ensuring that the observables are properly normalized with the model-specific
r_{d}
values.

VI.1
Covariance Structure and Statistical Diagnostics

We adopt a simplified block-diagonal covariance matrix structure across redshift bins, facilitating per-bin statistical comparisons. While this assumption aids interpretation, it omits potential cross-bin correlations present in the full DESI DR2 covariance matrix. Future analyses should incorporate the full matrix to robustly assess the global statistical fit.

VI.2
Model Comparison:
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
vs.
\omega\mathrm{CDM}

The
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
model systematically outperforms
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
across all criteria minimum
\chi^{2}
, AIC, and BIC. As shown in Table
7
, the
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
model is statistically favored, particularly under fixed
r_{\mathrm{d}}
assumptions where the evidence becomes overwhelming (
\Delta\mathrm{BIC}>147

r_{\mathrm{d}}
, differences exceed conventional thresholds (
\Delta\mathrm{AIC}>10
), justifying strong preference.

This statistical preference for
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
implies that mild deviations from
\omega=-1
may better fit current BAO data. The corresponding change in cosmic age
\sim 0.19
Gyr younger for
\omega_{0}=-0.916
also reflects a slightly faster early expansion history. This is consistent with a slowly evolving quintessence field, where residual kinetic energy leads to reduced cosmic acceleration and hence a shortened lookback time.

However, this dynamical scenario requires some degree of fine-tuning in initial field conditions or a flat potential
V(\phi)
to sustain slow roll until today, reflecting theoretical tension between empirical flexibility and model naturalness.

VI.3
Failure of the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
Model

The
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model is strongly disfavored by the data across all analysis configurations. Despite having greater flexibility through its two dark energy parameters (
\omega_{0}
,
\omega_{a}
), it consistently yields very poor
\chi^{2}
values and is heavily penalized by both AIC and BIC criteria. This suggests that the added degrees of freedom fail to improve the model’s ability to fit the data, and instead lead to overfitting or unphysical behavior.

A key source of this poor performance is the instability of the CPL parametrization at high redshifts. In particular, when best-fit values favor large negative
\omega_{a}
, the effective dark energy equation-of-state
\omega(z)
increases steeply with redshift. While
\omega(z)\to-1
is expected in most physically motivated models, the CPL form instead yields rapidly rising values of
\omega(z)
for
\omega_{a}\ll 0
, which can significantly alter the background expansion rate.

The value of

\Omega_{m0}
and

\omega_{a}
, dark energy remains non-negligible at high

, modifying
H(z)
and shifting
r_{\mathrm{d}}
. This underscores the importance of propagating model assumptions consistently across cosmic epochs.

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we conducted a detailed model comparison using redshift-resolved BAO observables from DESI DR2, focusing on the interplay between
r_{d}
assumptions and cosmological model structure. Our findings strongly favor the
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
model over
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
, supported by both global and per-bin AIC/BIC criteria, as well as
r_{d}
-independent ratios like
D_{M}/D_{H}
. The
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model, though more flexible, performs poorly due to high-redshift pathologies intrinsic to the CPL parametrization.

These results underscore the importance of propagating model assumptions consistently—particularly in the computation of
r_{d}
—and highlight the diagnostic value of redshift-resolved analyses. Table
6
summarizes the performance of each model across different redshift regimes and observable types, reinforcing the conclusion that statistical preference can shift depending on how early-universe physics is handled.

In particular, we find that seemingly small residuals at specific redshifts (notably
z=0.706
and
0.934
) dominate the overall
\chi^{2}
for
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
, while
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
maintains robust consistency under both fixed and model-derived
r_{d}
treatments. The poor performance of
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
arises from unphysical suppression of
H(z)
and inflation of
D_{M}(z)
at
z\gtrsim 1.5
, as directly illustrated in Figure
4
.

Our redshift-resolved,
r_{d}
-aware model comparison provides a complementary perspective to the recent work
Wang:2025vfb
. A model-independent approach to constrain the dark energy density
\rho_{\textrm{DE}}(z)
and equation of state
\omega(z)
as free functions of redshift. Both studies critically evaluate the limitations of the CPL parametrization and demonstrate how assuming a linear form of
\omega(z)
can lead to artificially significant deviations from
\Lambda
CDM
Colgain:2021pmf
;
Wolf:2023uno
;
Wolf:2024eph
. While they report deviations of only
1.2\sigma
(for
\rho_{X}
) and
1.9\sigma
(for
\omega
) using DESI DR2 + Planck data, our results show a mild but consistent statistical preference for the
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
model over
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
, especially when per-bin and
r_{d}
-independent diagnostics are used. Both analyses highlight that redshift evolution of dark energy, if present, must be carefully disentangled from early-universe calibration effects. Our findings therefore reinforce and extend the conclusions of Wang & Freese, underscoring the importance of model-independent and redshift-resolved frameworks for future high-precision dark energy studies.

Looking forward, our framework offers a template for statistically robust and physically motivated model testing. As DESI progresses toward DR3 and next-generation surveys like
Euclid
and
LSST
come online, the techniques developed here will be critical for testing deviations from
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
with sub-percent precision. Future joint analyses combining
D_{M}/D_{H}
with low-redshift supernovae or strong lensing time delays may allow for precise isolation of late-time dynamics, decoupled from early-universe assumptions.

Ultimately, distinguishing between a true deviation from the cosmological constant and a miscalibration of the early-universe standard ruler will require such multi-probe synergy. This work demonstrates the utility of redshift-resolved,
r_{d}
-aware diagnostics for cosmological model selection, with particular relevance to time-varying dark energy models where early-late consistency plays a critical role.

Appendix C
Simulation Setup for
r_{d}
Consistency Checks (Supplemental)

To verify the consistency of model-dependent
r_{d}
values used throughout this paper, we performed supplementary simulations with the Boltzmann code
CLASS
. These were based on the CPL parameterization
\omega(z)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\frac{z}{1+z}

with (
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}},\omega_{0},\omega_{a}
) sampled across the relevant model ranges. All other cosmological parameters were fixed to the
Planck
2018 best-fit values.

For each parameter set, CLASS computed the drag redshift
z_{d}
and the corresponding sound horizon
r_{d}=r_{s}(z_{d})
. These outputs were used to check the model consistency of
r_{d}
inputs to our BAO likelihood analysis but do not influence the cosmological parameter fits directly.

Comparison of stability indices of powers of graded ideals
Antonino Ficarra, Emanuele Sgroi

Abstract
. In this paper, we compare the index of ass-stability astab ​ ( I ) \text{astab}(I) and the index of v -stability vstab ​ ( I ) \text{vstab}(I) of powers of a graded ideal I I . We prove that astab ​ ( I ) = 1 ≤ vstab ​ ( I ) \text{astab}(I)=1\leq\text{vstab}(I) for any graded ideal I I in a 2-dimensional polynomial ring, and that vstab ​ ( I ) \text{vstab}(I) can be any positive integer in this situation. Moreover, given any integers a , b ≥ 1 a,b\geq 1 , we construct a graded ideal I I in a 3 ​ ( a + 1 ) 3(a+1) -dimensional polynomial ring such that ( astab ​ ( I ) , vstab ​ ( I ) ) = ( a , b ) (\text{astab}(I),\text{vstab}(I))=(a,b) .

Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
, let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal, and let
\textup{Ass}(I)
be the set of associated primes of
I
. By a famous result of Brodmann
[
2
]
the sequence
\{\textup{Ass}(I^{k})\}_{k>0}
stabilizes. That is
\textup{Ass}(I^{k+1})=\textup{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. We denote this common sets of associated primes by
\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. The least integer
k_{0}
for which
\textup{Ass}(I^{k})=\textup{Ass}(I^{k_{0}})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
is called the
index of ass-stability
of
I
and is denoted by
\textup{astab}(I)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
. The
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-number of
I
is the least degree
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
of a homogeneous polynomial
f\in S
for which
I:f=\mathfrak{p}
,
[
4
]
. Whereas, the
v
-number of
I
is defined as

\textup{v}(I)=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I)}\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I).

It was proved by Conca
[
3
]
, and independently by the authors of this paper
[
7
]
, that for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, the function
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
is eventually linear of the form
a_{\mathfrak{p}}k+b_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and also that
\textup{v}(I^{k})
is eventually linear of the form
ak+b
, where
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a=\alpha(I)=\min\{d:\ I_{d}\neq 0\}
is the
initial degree
of
I
, see
[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. The least integer
k_{0}
such that
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I^{k})
and
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=a_{\mathfrak{p}}k+b_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
, is called the
index of
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-stability
of
I
and is denoted by
\textup{vstab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
. The
v
-stability index
of
I
, introduced in
[
1
]
, is defined as the least integer
\textup{vstab}(I)
for which
\textup{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b
for all
k\geq\textup{vstab}(I)
.

The goal of this paper is to compare the stability indices
\textup{astab}(I)
and
\textup{vstab}(I)
of a graded ideal
I\subset S
. We are partly inspired by the paper
[
9
]
of Herzog and Mafi, where
\textup{astab}(I)
is compared with the index of depth stability of
I
.

For
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, let
\textup{astab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{k_{0}:\ \mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(I^{k})\ \textup{for all}\ k\geq k_{0}\}
. By definition, we have
\textup{vstab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\geq\textup{astab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
and
\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\textup{astab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\textup{astab}(I)
. Hence

\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\textup{vstab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ \geq\ \textup{astab}(I).

Since
\textup{v}(I^{k})=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq\textup{astab}(I)
, and the minimum of finitely many linear functions is also a linear function, it follows that

\textup{vstab}(I)\ \leq\ \max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\textup{vstab}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I).

In view of the above inequalities, one may expect that
\textup{vstab}(I)\geq\textup{astab}(I)
. In Section
1
, we prove that
\text{astab}(I)=1\leq\text{vstab}(I)
for any graded ideal
I\subset S
, provided that
\dim(S)\leq 2
. In this situation, we show that
\textup{vstab}(I)
can be any given integer. On the other hand, in Section
2
, given any integers
a,b\geq 1
, we construct a graded ideal
I
in a
3(a+1)
-dimensional polynomial ring such that
(\text{astab}(I),\text{vstab}(I))=(a,b)
. Hence, in general, the indices
\textup{astab}(I)
and
\textup{vstab}(I)
are not comparable.

1.
The case
\dim(S)\leq 2

In this section we prove that
\textup{astab}(I)\leq\textup{vstab}(I)
provided that
\dim(S)\leq 2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\dim(S)\leq 2
. Then
\textup{astab}(I)=1
for any graded ideal
I\subset S
. Moreover, let
S=K[x,y]
and
I=(x^{2b+1},x^{2}y^{2b-1},y^{2b+1})
with
b\geq 1
. Then

(a)

\textup{v}(I^{k})=(2b+1)k
, for
1\leq k\leq b-1
,

(b)

\textup{v}(I^{k})=(2b+1)k-1
, for
k\geq b
,

(c)

(\textup{astab}(I),\textup{vstab}(I))=(1,b)
.

Proof.

Let
\dim(S)\leq 2
. By
[
9
, Remark 1.1]
we have
\textup{astab}(I)=1
for any graded ideal
I\subset S
. Now, let
S=K[x,y]
and
I=(x^{2b+1},x^{2}y^{2b-1},y^{2b+1})
. Notice that the statement (c) follows from (a) and (b). So, we prove (a) and (b).

(a) Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y)
, and notice that
I^{k}
is a
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
\textup{v}(I^{k})=\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary monomial ideal
J\subset S
is of the form
J=(x^{a_{1}},x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}},\dots,x^{a_{m-1}}y^{b_{m-1}},y^{b_{m}})
for integer sequences
{\bf a}:a_{1}>\dots>a_{m-1}>0
and
{\bf b}:0<b_{2}<\dots<b_{m}
, with
m\geq 2
. It is shown in
[
7
, Proposition 5.5]
that

\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(J)\ =\ \min_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\{a_{i}+b_{i+1}-2\}.

(1)

In order to apply this formula in our situation, we first prove that

I^{k}\ =\ (u_{i,j}:\ 0\leq i\leq k,\,0\leq j\leq i),

(2)

with

u_{i,j}\ =\ x^{(2b+1)(k-i)+2(i-j)}y^{(2b+1)i-2(i-j)}.

To this end, notice that

\displaystyle I^{k}

\displaystyle=\ ((x^{2b+1})^{p}(x^{2}y^{2b-1})^{q}(y^{2b+1})^{r}:\ p+q+r=k)

(3)

\displaystyle=\ (x^{(2b+1)p+2q}y^{(2b-1)q+(2b+1)r}:\ p+q+r=k).

Write
p=k-i
for
0\leq i\leq k
, and set
r=j
. Then
q+j=i
and so
0\leq j\leq i
. With these positions, we have
(2b+1)p+2q=(2b+1)(k-i)+2(i-j)
and

(2b-1)q+(2b+1)r=(2b-1)(i-j)+(2b+1)j=(2b+1)i-2(i-j).

These computations together with formula (
3
) show that equation (
2
) holds.

For a monomial
u=x^{a}y^{b}\in S
, let
\deg_{x}u=a
and
\deg_{y}u=b
. Now, let
1\leq k<b
. We claim that

\deg_{x}u_{0,0}>\deg_{x}u_{1,0}>\deg_{x}u_{1,1}>\deg_{x}u_{2,0}>\dots>\deg_{x}u_{k,k-1}>\deg_{x}u_{k,k}=0

and

0=\deg_{y}u_{0,0}<\deg_{y}u_{1,0}<\deg_{y}u_{1,1}<\deg_{y}u_{2,0}<\dots<\deg_{y}u_{k,k-1}<\deg_{y}u_{k,k}.

Since
\deg_{x}u_{i,j}+\deg_{y}u_{i,j}=(2b+1)k
for all
i,j
, it is enough to prove the first set of inequalities. It is clear that
\deg_{x}u_{i,j}>\deg_{x}u_{i,j+1}
for
0\leq j\leq i-1
. It remains to prove that
\deg_{x}u_{i,i}>\deg_{x}u_{i+1,0}
, for
0\leq i\leq k-1
. Suppose for a contradiction that
\deg_{x}u_{i,i}\leq\deg_{x}u_{i+1,0}
. Then

(2b+1)(k-i)\ \leq\ (2b+1)(k-(i+1))+2(i+1).

Since
i+1\leq k+1\leq b
, we obtain
2b+1\leq 2(i+1)\leq 2b
, which is impossible. This proves our claim. Hence, for
1\leq k<b
, by applying formula (
1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\textup{v}(I^{k})\ =

\displaystyle\ \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq k-1\\
0\leq j\leq i+1\end{subarray}}\{\deg_{x}u_{i+1,j}+\deg_{y}u_{i+1,j+1}-2,\,\deg_{x}u_{i,i}+\deg_{y}u_{i+1,0}-2\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \min_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\{(2b+1)k,\,(2b+1)(k+1)-2(i+1)-2\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (2b+1)k,

as desired.

(b) Let
w=x^{2b}y^{(2b+1)(k-1)}
with
k\geq b
. We have
\deg(w)=\alpha(I^{k})-1
and so
w\notin I^{k}
. Moreover
xw=(x^{2b+1})y^{(2b+1)(k-1)}\in I^{k}
and
yw=(x^{2}y^{2b-1})^{b}y^{(2b+1)(k-b)}\in I^{k}
. Hence
I^{k}:w=\mathfrak{m}
and so
\textup{v}(I^{k})\leq\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq b
. Since by
[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
we have
\textup{v}(I^{k})\geq\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
, the assertion follows.

∎

2.
The case
\dim(S)\geq 3

We now come to the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a,b\geq 1
be positive integers. Then, there exist a number
N>0
and a monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
such that

(\textup{astab}(I),\textup{vstab}(I))=(a,b).

Proof.

If
a=1
, the statement follows from Theorem
1.1
.

Now, let
a\geq 2
. Let
J_{i}=(x_{3i-2}x_{3i-1},x_{3i-2}x_{3i},x_{3i-1}x_{3i})
for
1\leq i\leq a-1
and set
J=J_{1}+\dots+J_{a-1}
. Notice that each
J_{i}
is an edge ideal with linear resolution. Thus,
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
implies that
\textup{v}(J_{i}^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
, and in particular
\textup{vstab}(J_{i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq a-1
. Since
\alpha(J_{i})=2
and the generators of the ideals
J_{i}
are monomials in pairwise disjoint sets of variables,
[
6
, Theorem 5.2]
implies that

\displaystyle\textup{v}(J^{k})

\displaystyle=\ (\min_{1\leq i\leq a-1}\alpha(J_{i}))k+(\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}\alpha(J_{i})-\min_{1\leq i\leq a-1}\alpha(J_{i})-(a-1))

(4)

\displaystyle=\ 2k+a-3,

for all
k\geq 1
. In particular
\textup{vstab}(J)=1
.

It is immediate to see that

\textup{Ass}(J_{i})=\textup{Min}(J_{i})=\{(x_{3i-2},x_{3i-1}),(x_{3i-2},x_{3i}),(x_{3i-1},x_{3i})\}.

Since
J_{i}^{2}:(x_{3i-2}x_{3i-1}x_{3i})=(x_{3i-2},x_{3i-1},x_{3i})=\mathfrak{m}_{i}
and edge ideals satisfy the persistence property (see
[
10
, Theorem 2.15]
) we have
\textup{Ass}(J_{i}^{k})=\textup{Ass}(J_{i})\cup\{\mathfrak{m}_{i}\}
for all
k\geq 2
. Hence
\textup{astab}(J_{i})=2
for
1\leq i\leq a-1
. If
a=2
, then
J=J_{1}
and
\textup{astab}(J)=a=2
, as wanted. Suppose now
a>2
. By
[
11
, Theorem 4.1(3)]
we have

\textup{Ass}((J_{1}+J_{2})^{k})\ =\ \{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\ :\ 0\leq\ell<k,\,\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(J_{1}^{k-\ell}),\,\mathfrak{q}\in\textup{Ass}(J_{2}^{\ell+1})\}.

From this formula we see immediately that
\textup{Ass}(J_{1}+J_{2})\subseteq\textup{Ass}((J_{1}+J_{2})^{2})\subseteq\textup{Ass}((J_{1}+J_{2})^{3})\subseteq\cdots
and
\textup{astab}(J_{1}+J_{2})=3
. Applying again this result to
J_{1}+J_{2}
and
J_{3}
and proceeding iteratively in this way we obtain that
\textup{Ass}(J)\subseteq\textup{Ass}(J^{2})\subseteq\textup{Ass}(J^{3})\subseteq\cdots
and
\textup{astab}(J)=a
.

Now, we consider the ideal

I\ =\ x_{0}^{2b-1}J+(x^{2b+1},x^{2}y^{2b-1},y^{2b+1})

in the polynomial ring
S=K[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{3a},x,y]
in
3(a+1)
variables.

We claim that
(\textup{astab}(I),\textup{vstab}(I))=(a,b)
. To this end, we set
L=x_{0}^{2b-1}J
and
H=(x^{2b+1},x^{2}y^{2b-1},y^{2b+1})
. Using
[
6
, Corollary 2.3]
and formula (
4
) we have

\displaystyle\textup{v}(L^{k})

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\textup{v}((x_{0}^{(2b-1)k}))+\alpha(J)k,\,\alpha((x_{0})^{2b-1})k+\textup{v}(J^{k})\}

(5)

\displaystyle=\ \min\{(2b-1)k-1+2k,(2b-1)k+2k+a-3\}

\displaystyle=\ (2b+1)k-1,

for all
k\geq 1
, where we used that
a\geq 2
.

By
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
we have
\textup{Ass}(L^{k})=\textup{Ass}(J^{k})\cup\{(x_{0})\}
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
\textup{Ass}(L)\subseteq\textup{Ass}(L^{2})\subseteq\textup{Ass}(L^{3})\subseteq\cdots
. Using this fact and since
\textup{Ass}(H^{k})=\{(x,y)\}
for all
k\geq 1
, it follows from
[
11
, Theorem 4.1(3)]
that

\textup{Ass}(I^{k})\ =\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(L^{k-\ell})\end{subarray}}\{(\mathfrak{p},x,y)\}\ =\ \{(\mathfrak{p},x,y)\ :\ \mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(L^{k})\}.

(6)

Since
\textup{astab}(L)=\textup{astab}(J)=a
, this formula implies that
\textup{astab}(I)=a
, too.

Now, since
\textup{Ass}(H^{k})=\{(x,y)\}
for all
k\geq 1
, by using
[
6
, Corollary 4.2]
and the formulas (
5
) and (
6
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\textup{v}(I^{k})

\displaystyle=\ \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}(L^{k-\ell})\end{subarray}}\{\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L^{k-\ell})+\textup{v}_{(x,y)}(H^{\ell+1})\}

(7)

\displaystyle=\ \min_{0\leq\ell<k}\{(2b+1)(k-\ell)-1+\textup{v}_{(x,y)}(H^{\ell+1})\}.

Using Theorem
1.1
(a)-(b) we get

(2b+1)(k-\ell)-1+\textup{v}_{(x,y)}(H^{\ell+1})\ =\ \begin{cases}(2b+1)k+2b&\textup{for}\ 1\leq\ell\leq b-2,\\
(2b+1)k+2b-1&\textup{for}\ \ell\geq b-1.\end{cases}

Combined with (
7
) we obtain that
\textup{v}(I^{k})=(2b+1)k+2b
for
1\leq k<b
, and

\textup{v}(I^{k})=(2b+1)k+2b-1
for
k\geq b
. Hence
\textup{vstab}(I)=b
, as desired.

∎

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. It is known that
\textup{depth}\,S/I^{k+1}=\textup{depth}\,S/I^{k}
for all
k\gg 0
. The least integer
k_{0}
such that
\textup{depth}\,S/I^{k+1}=\textup{depth}\,S/I^{k}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
is called the index of depth stability of
I
and is denoted by
\textup{dstab}(I)
. Likewise, it is also well-known that there exists
k_{*}>0
for which the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
\textup{reg}(I^{k})
is equal to a linear function for all
k\geq k_{*}
. The index of regularity stability
\textup{rstab}(I)
of
I
is defined as the least integer with this property.

At the moment (see
[
8
, Question 5.6]
) we do not known whether for given integers
a,d,r,v\geq 1
, there exists a graded ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
such that

(\textup{astab}(I),\textup{dstab}(I),\textup{rstab}(I),\textup{vstab}(I))\ =\ (a,d,r,v).

Effect of qigong on sleep quality and hyperarousal in patients with chronic insomnia: a study protocol for a multi-centre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Jiahe Cui, Haotian Han, Hongyu Yue, Chaoqun Xie, Jianwen Ma, Fangfang Xie, Yan-li You, Fei Yao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic insomnia is a pressing public health issue that significantly affects patients’ quality of life. In China, Prolong Life With Nine Turn-Method (PLWNT) Qigong exercise has long been used to improve sleep quality, yet evidence that supports its efficacy for chronic insomnia treatment remains lacking. The aim of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of the PLWNT qigong exercise for treating chronic insomnia and its relationship with hyperarousal.Methods and analysisThis multi-centre randomised controlled trial will recruit 348 eligible patients from three hospitals in Shanghai. Participants will be randomly assigned to either the treatment group (PLWNT qigong exercise) or the control group (cognitive behavioural therapy). The treatment will occur once a week for 12 weeks, followed by an 8-week follow-up phase. The primary outcome is the change in the total Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) score from baseline to week 12. The secondary outcomes are the scores on the Hyperarousal Scale (HAS), Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory 20 (MFI-20), Hospital Anxiety and Depression (HADS), Medical Outcome Trust 36–Item Short Form Health Survey (SF–36) and Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS), gut microbiota, actigraphy, sleep diary, cortisol, adrenocorticotrophic hormone, corticotropin-releasing hormone, polysomnography and functional MRI. All adverse events during the trial will be promptly recorded and assessed. The PSQI, HAS, ISI, MFI-20, HADS, SF–36 and GSRS will be evaluated at baseline, 6 weeks post-treatment and 12 weeks post-treatment, as well as at the 4- week and 8- week follow-ups. Other outcomes will only be evaluated at baseline and 12 weeks post-treatment.Ethics and disseminationThe trial has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Shanghai Municipal Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (2024SHL-KY-92–01). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their involvement in the trial. Results of this study will be published in peer-reviewed journals or at conferences.Trial registration numberITMCTR2024000534.

Introduction

Chronic insomnia is one of the most common sleep disorders today. It is defined as a subjective experience of dissatisfaction with sleep duration or quality despite adequate sleep opportunities and environment. This condition is accompanied by daytime functional impairments such as sleepiness, fatigue, somatic symptoms like aches, mood disturbances and compromised cognitive or occupational function.1 2 The International Classification of Sleep Disorders, Third Edition, has made clear provisions for the diagnosis of chronic insomnia and defines an insomnia duration of ≥3 months at a frequency of ≥3 times/week as the criteria for the duration and minimum frequency of chronic insomnia. As a globally impactful condition, research has indicated that insomnia symptoms have a high prevalence rate in the general population that ranges from 23.48% to 47.83%, and this can increase with age.3 Insomnia not only leads to short-term consequences, such as night-time sleep or related daytime functional impairments, but is also closely associated with long-term issues like anxiety, depression, hypertension and cardiovascular diseases.4 In addition, insomnia imposes significant economic burdens on individuals and society through both direct and indirect costs.5 In the USA alone, insomnia results in expenses that range from $28.1 to $216.6 billion.6 Therefore, how to effectively treat insomnia is an urgent public health problem.

Researchers have proposed various models to explore the pathogenesis of chronic insomnia, such as the onset and maintenance of insomnia. These models include the neurocognitive model,7 cognitive model8 and psychophysiological model.9 All these models assume that there is some type of arousal enhancement in cognitive emotion, the cerebral cortex or other physiological fields. Therefore, hyperarousal is one of the most consistent findings of the current chronic insomnia research.10 Evidence of hyperarousal in chronic insomnia spans multiple dimensions, such as increased fast-frequency electroencephalogram (EEG) activity in patients with chronic insomnia, reduced delta power and significantly increased beta power during non-rapid eye movement sleep.11 In addition, there is heightened activity in the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis and increased secretion of corticotrophin-releasing hormone (CRH) and cortisol (CORT).12 Moreover, neuroimaging techniques have demonstrated changes in the brain regions of patients with chronic insomnia, such as a reduced hippocampal volume and increased thalamic activity during sleep.13

Currently, the commonly used treatment methods for chronic insomnia can be divided into pharmacotherapy and non-pharmaceutical therapy.14 15 Pharmacotherapy can be effective for both acute and medium-term treatment of insomnia, but it may cause side effects, and the efficacy and tolerability of different medications vary.16 17 Therefore, pharmacotherapy is considered a second-line treatment for chronic insomnia and should be applied with caution. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia and is a multicomponent treatment that includes components such as sleep hygiene, sleep-restriction therapy and stimulus control therapy. It is suitable for patients of all ages with chronic insomnia.18 However, CBT-I is not widely available due to insufficient CBT providers, high economic costs and access difficulties.19 Hence, it is necessary to explore treatment methods for chronic insomnia that are both highly acceptable and convenient. Several studies have suggested that exercise interventions may have a positive effect on improving the clinical symptoms of chronic insomnia.2022 Regular exercise can exert beneficial effects on sleep through a variety of pathways such as circadian rhythm, immune, thermoregulatory and endocrine regulation.23 Research has indicated that exercise can improve both subjective and objective sleep parameters.24 Various forms of exercise, such as aerobic and mind-body exercises, have positive effects on the sleep quality of individuals with insomnia.25 The role of exercise for chronic insomnia management is gaining increasing attention.

Qigong is an exercise regimen often used in China for managing mind-body disorders, and it has been noted to have some effects among people with poor sleep.26 Prolong life with nine turn-method (PLWNT) qigong exercise is a traditional Chinese mind-body practice that integrates mental regulation, breath control and physical movements, including eight abdominal massage techniques and an upper body rotation exercise, and it is believed to be effective for treating insomnia.27 Our previous research indicates that the practice of PLWNT can improve sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep duration, sleep disturbance and daytime dysfunction.28 However, its benefits for sleep quality and daytime function in patients with chronic insomnia remain unclear. Therefore, this randomised clinical trial will be conducted to evaluate the clinical efficacy of PLWNT for the treatment of chronic insomnia.

We designed this randomised clinical trial to be conducted across three hospitals in Shanghai, China, to test the hypothesis that PLWNT can enhance sleep quality and diminish excessive wakefulness in patients with chronic insomnia. The objectives of this study are twofold: (1) to verify the efficacy of PLWNT as a treatment for chronic insomnia and (2) to explore the potential relationship between the clinical efficacy of PLWNT intervention and hyperarousal in patients with chronic insomnia.

Methods

Design

This open-label, multi-centre, evaluator-blind, randomised and controlled trial will be conducted at the Shanghai Municipal Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, the Shuguang Hospital affiliated with Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, and the Yueyang Hospital of Integrated Traditional Chinese and Western Medicine. The study will run from November 2024 to December 2027. We will recruit 348 eligible patients with chronic insomnia and randomly assign them to two groups: one receiving PLWNT and the other receiving CBT. Following a 1-week baseline period, participants will undergo intensive treatment sessions once a week. This will be complemented by self-directed treatment at home for the remaining 6 days of each week over a span of 12 weeks. This intervention phase will be followed by an 8- week follow-up period to assess the long-term effects of the treatments. Participants will be assessed at baseline (1 week before treatment), mid-treatment (6 weeks after starting treatment), post-treatment (12 weeks after starting treatment), and during follow-up (4 and 8 weeks after treatment ends). The study protocol is designed according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials.29 The test flow chart and study design time are shown in figure 1 and table 1.

Recruitment

Recruitment will be conducted in three hospitals using posters and online advertisements. Interested participants will undergo an initial online screening followed by a face-to-face screening for further evaluation. They will be informed about the study details and assured that participation is entirely voluntary. During the face-to-face screening visit, a designated clinician will explain the study details and obtain informed consent. Only those who meet the inclusion criteria and sign the informed consent form will proceed to the intervention and corresponding assessments (online supplemental material 1).

Sample size calculation

The sample size calculation is based on an estimation formula for the difference between two sample means and SDs (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), which is assessed using composite points of sleep quality). Based on previous clinical research results,20 31 we hypothesised that the PLWNT group would have a PSQI score of 7.9±3.8, whereas the CBT group would have 9.1±1.7. With a type I error (α) of 0.05 and a type II error (β) of 0.2, the sample size formula indicated that each group would require 158 participants.

Given an anticipated 10% dropout rate, we plan to include 174 participants per group for a total of 348 participants. Participants will be allocated in a 1:1:1 ratio, with each hospital recruiting 58 participants per group.

Randomisation and blinding

An independent statistician used SPSS V.18.0 software to create a random sequence with a random block of four. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to either the PLWNT group or the CBT group at a 1:1 ratio. The random numbers and their corresponding group assignments will be placed in sealed, opaque envelopes and given to the researchers and hospital staff. These envelopes will be opened only after the eligible participants have signed the informed consent form, at which point the participants will be assigned to their respective groups.

The blinding of participants will not be feasible due to the unique nature of the intervention in this study. The professionals who will provide the intervention will not participate in the data collection or the outcome assessment to minimise the influence of subjective factors from both participants and researchers. Instead, independent researchers, unaware of the group assignments, will conduct the data collection and outcome evaluation. Participants will be instructed not to disclose their group assignments to the data collectors. In addition, sleep technicians will remain masked to the group allocations throughout the trial and have no involvement in the clinical procedures or access to the group assignment details. In the event of unintended unblinding, the affected participant’s data will undergo a sensitivity analysis while maintaining their continued participation per the protocol, and documentation will be submitted to the Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) for adjudication.

Interventions

Treatment group

The PLWNT intervention programme and its operating standards were formulated with reference to the Chinese general traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) textbook ‘Tuina Exercise’.32 An instructor from the Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine who has been practising qigong for >5 years will supervise the 1-hour intensive exercise sessions held every Sunday to ensure the accuracy and effectiveness of the exercises. This supervision will include monitoring the exercises and correcting participants’ postures to align with the prescribed standards. The qigong instructor will first introduce and demonstrate the movements, followed by 10 min of stretching and relaxation exercises. Next, the instructor will spend 20 min correcting participant postures and providing targeted guidance. Finally, the instructor will lead a 30 min group practice of the PLWNT qigong exercise. For the rest of the week, participants will be asked to follow our recorded standard video for a daily 30 min qigong exercise and document their experiences in the provided practice log. We will establish a WeChat group for participants via an online platform to address any issues during self-practice and offer feedback. The entire exercise process will last for 12 weeks. The PLWNT intervention content will be the same as what we reported before,33 and the specific exercise form is shown in figure 2.

Control group

The CBT intervention content will be based on the ‘Cognitive Behavioural Treatment of Insomnia: A Session-by-Session Guide’.34 An experienced CBT therapist will conduct a 1-hour session every Sunday that covers topics such as stimulus control, sleep restriction, sleep hygiene education, cognitive training and relaxation techniques. We will use WeChat as the online platform to organise participants, upload CBT course materials and ask participants to engage in 30 min of self-study for the remaining 6 days of the week. Participants will record their learning experiences to ensure consistency with the PLWNT group. Researchers will address any cognitive–behavioural questions participants may have. The entire intervention will last for 12 weeks.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the change in the total PSQI score from baseline to week 12. The PSQI is a questionnaire designed to assess sleep quality and disturbances, and it evaluates the following seven aspects: subjective sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep duration, habitual sleep efficiency, sleep disturbances, sleep medication use and daytime dysfunction. It includes 19 self-rated items and five other-rated items.35 Higher scores indicate poorer sleep quality and more severe sleep disturbances.

Secondary outcomes

Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index

The PSQI scores at weeks 6, 16 and 20 will be evaluated as secondary outcomes.

Hyperarousal Scale

The Hyperarousal Scale (HAS) comprises 26 self-assessment items designed to evaluate various hyperarousal behavioural traits,36 such as tendencies to introspect, think about feelings and respond intensely to unexpected stimuli. Higher scores indicate higher levels of hyperarousal.

Insomnia Severity Index

The ISI scale consists of four sleep-related items and three wake-related items that are designed to measure patients’ subjective experiences of insomnia. It quantifies the severity of insomnia and identifies the nature of sleep differences,37 with higher scores indicating more severe insomnia.

Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory 20

Fatigue is a common daytime impairment symptom of insomnia. The Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory 20 (MFI-20) comprehensively assesses fatigue across the following five dimensions: general fatigue, physical fatigue, mental fatigue, reduced activity and reduced motivation.38 Higher scores indicate more severe fatigue.

Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale

The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) is designed to evaluate symptoms of anxiety and depression, with higher scores indicating poorer psychological status.

Medical Outcome Trust 36-Item Short-Form Health Survey

The Medical Outcome Trust 36-item Short-Form Health Survey (SF–36) evaluates health status across these domains: physiological function, physical pain, general health, vitality, social function, emotional function, mental health and health change. Higher scores denote better health outcomes.

Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale

The Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS) measures the severity of common gastrointestinal symptoms through 15 items rated on a 7-point Likert scale. Higher scores indicate greater symptom severity.

Gut microbiota

Faecal samples will be collected within 1 week before and after intervention. Each specimen will be placed in a sterile 40 mL faecal collection container containing desiccant, immediately sealed, labelled with a unique ID and stored at –80℃ in an ultra-low temperature freezer within 1 hour of collection. The samples will be sent for inspection after all of the specimens have been collected. Approximately 200–300 mg of stool specimens will be extracted using the stool FastDNA kit (MP Bio), and the 16S rRNA gene will be amplified with specific primers (16S V4: 515 F-806R). We will record the collection time of gut microbiota and use it as a covariate in subsequent analyses.

Actigraphy

The Actigraphy GT9X Link (Actigraphy LLC; Pensacola, FL, USA) will be worn on participants’ wrists before and after the intervention to record sleep onset, sleep latency, efficiency of sleep, etc. Participants’ sleep data will be analysed using ActiLife6 software (version 6.8.1; Actigraphy LLC).

Sleep diary

Participants will complete sleep diaries for 1 week before and after the intervention. The diaries will include parameters such as sleep onset latency, wake time after sleep onset, total sleep time and sleep efficiency. The average values from the 1-week sleep diary will be used for subsequent analysis.

Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis biomarkers

HPA axis activity is a potential marker of hyperarousal,10 12 with CORT, adrenocorticotrophic hormone (ACTH) and CRH being its released products. Blood samples will be collected from each participant’s elbow vein into vacuum tubes at approximately 9:00, the day before the intervention and the second day after the intervention ended. After resting for 1 hour, the samples will be centrifuged (3000 rpm, 5 min), and the serum supernatant will be transferred to cryotubes and stored at –80℃. We will measure these samples using an ELISA.

Polysomnography

Polysomnography (PSG) can monitor various electrophysiological signals such as EEG, electrooculography and electromyography during participants’ sleep, providing an accurate diagnosis and assessment of sleep conditions. In this study, participants will use PSG (Natus Medical Incorporated, San Carlos, CA, USA) in the sleep monitoring room at the Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine before and after the intervention. Participants will sleep according to their habits, with the recording period set from 22:00 to 7:00. Professional sleep technicians will evaluate the sleep structure according to ‘The AASM Manual for the Scoring of Sleep and Associated Events’.39

Functional magnetic resonance imaging

Functional MRI (fMRI) data will be collected using a GE SIGNA Premier 3.0T at the Shanghai Municipal Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The sequence parameters of the MRI are as follows: repetition time of 2000 ms, echo time of 30 mm, flip angle of 90°, and field of view of 220×220 mm2; thickness/gap of 4.0/0.6 mm and voxel size of 3×3×3. Before the scan, participants will be instructed to rest with their eyes closed for 10 min without engaging in any thoughts. During the scan, participants will be required to keep their heads still. fMRI data will be collected before and after the intervention.

Follow-up

Participants will return to their normal lives after the study is concluded. We will conduct follow-up assessments at 4 and 8 weeks post-intervention to evaluate the long-term efficacy of the intervention. Evaluation metrics will include the PSQI, HAS, ISI, MFI-20, HADS, SF–36 and GSRS. Researchers will maintain contact with participants via WeChat, phone or email to encourage their engagement in the follow-up process.

Adverse events

The most common adverse events associated with qigong exercise are falls, dizziness, muscle soreness or pain. Adverse events associated with CBT include fatigue, sleepiness, low motivation and low energy.18 Any discomfort or adverse events experienced by participants during the study, regardless of their relationship to the intervention, will be promptly reported to the research team and to a qualified doctor. The doctor will assess whether the treatment was necessary. All such events will be reported in the subsequent manuscript. In the case of serious adverse events, the principal investigator and ethics committee will be notified to decide if the participant should withdraw from the study and assess the causal relationship between the event and the research. If necessary, the ethics committee may suspend the study. An expert committee will evaluate and investigate any adverse events during the study to determine their connection to the intervention. If a connection is found, the research team will provide financial and medical compensation to the participants.

Statistical analysis

The data collected will be analysed using SPSS version 26.0 by a statistician who is blinded to the group assignments and participant information. The analysis will adhere to the intention-to-treat principle, with a full analysis set that will consist of all eligible participants who have received at least one intervention and had at least one outcome measurement. Missing data will be imputed using mixed-effects models under the assumption of missing at random. In addition, a per-protocol set will be used as a supplementary dataset to analyse participants who completed the study without major protocol violations. A participant course participation rate of <50% will be considered a major protocol violation. A descriptive analysis will be employed to present the baseline characteristics of the patients. Continuous variables that follow a normal distribution will be expressed as means±SD, whereas those that do not follow a normal distribution will be represented as medians (IQR). Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies or percentages. Primary outcome measures will be assessed using linear mixed-effects models, whereas secondary outcomes will be evaluated using a baseline-adjusted analysis of covariance. The analysis of covariates will use multiple imputation accounting for the missingness mechanism. Little’s test will be performed to validate the missing completely at random (MCAR) assumption. If MCAR is rejected, the sensitivity analyses will be conducted to evaluate the impact of missing not at random mechanisms. A Bonferroni correction will be used to account for multiple comparisons. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Adherence

Researchers will employ several methods to enhance participant compliance throughout the study. (1) After each weekly intervention, participants will be encouraged and informed about the benefits of the intervention for chronic insomnia. (2) Participants who complete the intervention programme will receive a free set of qigong training clothes and ¥200 to encourage follow-up participation. (3) Participants who attend the follow-up sessions will receive specialised topical pain patches and a wellness pot for free. In addition, researchers will monitor attendance by recording each participant’s presence during the study period using attendance sheets. Drawing on previous studies,40 participants who attend at least 8 of 12 sessions will be defined as ‘completers’ with high adherence. Attendance will be discreetly documented without disclosure to participants or intervention in their engagement, thus ensuring this recording process will not influence adherence behaviours.

Data management

The original data will be collected using standardised paper case report forms (CRF). Two independent data managers who are unaware of participant group assignments and not affiliated with the research team will transcribe the data into an Excel database (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA). All paper records and electronic data will be securely stored for 3 years after the study concludes. In addition, an independent DSMB that comprises experts in statistics, tuina therapy and psychology will oversee and review the trial data. If the DSMB identifies quality issues or serious adverse events during the study, they will possess the authority to provide recommendations or even terminate the trial if necessary.

Quality control

The PLWNT intervention plans and operating standards refer to the relevant content of the nationally compiled textbook for higher education in traditional Chinese medicine. These will be used to produce a standard instructional video as an auxiliary tool. An independent advisory committee, comprising researchers with clinical trial experience in qigong, statistical experts and exercise specialists, will oversee the trial to ensure its quality and eliminate potential biases. This committee will be responsible for monitoring data, identifying issues, reviewing collected data and controlling biases. Before the commencement of the study, a training session will be held for the researchers that covers the entire study protocol, patient recruitment, treatment strategies, outcome assessment and follow-up procedures. Qigong instructors or psychotherapists at each sub-centre will undergo a centralised standardised training programme conducted by experts with over 10 years of experience in qigong training or psychotherapy. After the training, these instructors will be evaluated and required to adhere to the established intervention plan. During the intervention period, experts in qigong training and psychotherapy will conduct unannounced, random on-site audits of the instructional sessions. Each sub-centre will be equipped with a dedicated supervisor responsible for inspecting the CRF and treatment records. In addition, the principal investigators will hold regular meetings to discuss and resolve any issues that arise during the study.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public are not involved in the design, implementation or recruitment of this study.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Municipal Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (2024SHL-KY-92–01) and registered with the International Traditional Medicine Clinical Trial Registry (ITMCTR2024000534). The study will not be conducted on patients until they have signed a written informed consent form. Results of this study will be published in peer-reviewed journals or at conferences.

Discussion

Chronic insomnia significantly impairs patients’ quality of life and increases the risk of various diseases. Despite the availability of multiple treatments for chronic insomnia, their application remains subject to limitations or controversies. For instance, the long-term use of benzodiazepines carries risks of tolerance and dependence. The beneficial effects of light therapy and music therapy on sleep require further research and validation, and CBT involves high economic costs.16 18 41 Therefore, finding an effective treatment remains clinically important. In China, qigong exercise has a long history of being used to treat various diseases.42 According to TCM, qigong can regulate the body, breath and mind, thereby restoring the balance of yin and yang and normalising the sleep–wake rhythm to alleviate insomnia symptoms.

Previous clinical studies have typically focused on the therapeutic efficacy of treating chronic insomnia, and relatively few investigations have investigated the underlying mechanisms of its effectiveness. Hyperarousal is considered a significant mechanism and manifestation of chronic insomnia.7 9 However, there are currently no large-sample, multi-centre randomised controlled trials that examine the clinical efficacy of qigong exercise for chronic insomnia and its impact on hyperarousal. Therefore, we designed this open-label, multi-centre, evaluator-blind, randomised controlled trial to assess the clinical efficacy of PLWNT qigong exercise for the treatment of chronic insomnia and its effect on hyperarousal. The aim of this study is to provide reliable evidence for the use of PLWNT qigong exercise in patients with chronic insomnia.

This study will employ various subjective and objective indicators to comprehensively understand the relationship between the PLWNT qigong exercise intervention and the sleep quality and hyperarousal state of patients with chronic insomnia. Hyperarousal encompasses multiple dimensions that include cognitive, somatic and cortical aspects.43 To assess cognitive arousal levels, we will utilise scales such as the HAS, which are commonly used tools for the evaluation of patient cognitive arousal. Somatic arousal in patients with chronic insomnia is closely linked to HPA axis hyperactivity. HPA axis dysregulation is believed to be associated with the persistence and maintenance of insomnia.44 Current research on the role of the HPA axis in hyperarousal states of insomnia has predominantly focused on CORT. However, some studies have suggested that sleep-related HPA axis effects are mediated by CRH.10 The HPA axis plays a critical role in the regulation of the human stress response. Its hormonal cascade begins with the hypothalamic release of CRH that triggers the anterior pituitary secretion of ACTH. This process ultimately leads to CORT release from the adrenal cortex.45 Thus, we will measure changes in HPA axis-related hormone levels in patients with chronic insomnia to examine associations between these biomarkers and the somatic arousal state. Cortical arousal primarily pertains to the measurement of brain activity in patients with chronic insomnia. EEG and fMRI are crucial tools for this assessment. EEG, with its high temporal resolution, allows for precise measurements of brain electrical changes during sleep,46 and its derivative, PSG, is considered an essential device for diagnosing sleep disorders. By contrast, fMRI offers superior spatial resolution that enables the monitoring of regional brain activity and structural changes.13 Consequently, we will employ PSG and fMRI techniques to observe brain activity in patients with chronic insomnia, aiming to explore the temporal and spatial characteristics of hyperarousal changes before and after the intervention.

Furthermore, studies have indicated that chronic insomnia frequently coexists with psychiatric disorders including anxiety and depression, whose functional impairments often interact synergistically.47 48 To minimise potential confounding effects from these conditions and avoid clouding results or impeding treatment response, we will exclude patients with any comorbid psychiatric disorders. This study primarily investigates the therapeutic effect of qigong on chronic insomnia. However, CBT has shown clinical benefits for patients with comorbid psychiatric disorders and insomnia, improving their anxiety and depressive symptoms and being suggestive of a reduction in insomnia severity.49 Should qigong exercise demonstrate comparable outcomes to CBT in alleviating clinical symptoms, it may thereby represent a potential future modality for improving sleep in patients with insomnia comorbid with depression or anxiety.

This study has several strengths. We will employ multiple validated questionnaires to gain a comprehensive picture of the effects and utilise biomarkers related to hyperarousal. Furthermore, by comparing qigong exercise with CBT (the current ‘golden standard’ of non-pharmacological treatment of chronic insomnia), this study may offer new avenues for the exploration of non-pharmacological insomnia therapies.

This study has several limitations. First, owing to the inherent nature of non-pharmacological interventions, the blinding of therapists and participants will not be feasible. This limitation may potentially introduce bias. Therefore, we will implement measures to mitigate this limitation, including separation of personnel across different stages (patient recruitment, intervention delivery, data collection/management and outcome assessment) to obtain robust evidence for the clinical efficacy of PLWNT qigong exercise as a chronic insomnia treatment. Second, the participant age criteria may limit the generalisability of the findings to other age groups. In addition, as CORT secretion exhibits a distinct circadian rhythm, variations in individual blood sampling time windows may differentially affect the results. Finally, since the study was conducted across three hospitals in Shanghai, the enrolled participants may not fully represent the diversity of ethnic groups within China or include foreign nationals. This could potentially limit the applicability of our conclusions for the entire population.

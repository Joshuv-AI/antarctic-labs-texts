Enhancing the energy storage capacity of a symmetric supercapacitor employing α-Cu2P2O7 produced by a template-based co-precipitation approach
Authors: Jay B. Patel, Karan S. Modi, Pruthvi Patel, Dharti Patel, Vanaraj Solanki, Bhavin Patel, Mitesh H. Patel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pyrophosphates have acquired considerable attention as a potential electrode material in energy storage devices owing to their strong covalent P–O bonds, which ensure structural stability, high electrochemical activity, and efficient ion migration. In this contribution, we synthesized copper pyrophosphate (Cu2P2O7) by using a simple co-precipitation method followed by calcination at 500 °C for 30 minutes. The monoclinic structure of the material with space group C12/c1 was confirmed by powder X-ray diffraction. The Cu2P2O7 bonds were confirmed using Raman spectroscopy, while Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy confirmed the bending vibration of P–O–P and P–O bonds. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy validates the +2-oxidation state of copper and the +5-oxidation state of phosphorus. Field emission scanning electron microscope revealed that the interconnected porous morphology with a rough surface of the material provides abundant active sites for ion movements and facilitates electrolyte penetration. The symmetric supercapacitor device of Cu2P2O7 possesses an excellent specific capacity of 225 F g−1 with a power density and energy density of 3200 W kg−1 and 80 Wh/kg at a current density of 1 A g−1, respectively. The symmetric device retains about 90% of its initial capacity after 10 000 cycles at a current density of 1.5 A g−1. The symmetric device is capable to illuminate a single 3 V red light emitting diode continuously for 1 minute and 27 seconds. The electrochemical findings endorse the viability of Cu2P2O7 as a suitable electrode material for long-term energy storage applications.

Introduction

Given the limited availability of fossil fuels, research into energy storage devices is crucial for identifying alternatives that can prevent environmental deterioration while being both functional and economically viable to satisfy future energy requirements. In the context of energy generation and storage, batteries, solar cells and supercapacitors (SCs) are the three possible and effective options. Among them, SCs have garnered more attention due to their high-power density, long life cycle, low cost, and environmental friendliness. Additionally, their charge storage capacities can be modified, rendering them compatible with Li-ion batteries. These unique attributes making them ultimate choice for electric vehicles, portable devices, and some biomedical apparatus.1–6

The electrochemical characteristics, including energy density, power density, rate capability, and cycle stability, are essential metrics dependent on electrode materials. Currently, carbon,7–10 graphene,11–13 mono-metallic oxides,14 metal sulphides,15–18 conducting polymers,19–23 bi metallic oxides,24,25 metal hydroxides26,27 and Mxene28,29 have been studied as an electrode material owing to its high surface area, short diffusion length and excellent cyclic stability. The synthesis method employed to produce these electrode materials was intricate and time-consuming, rendering it impractical for industrial usage. The industrial revolution of electrode materials necessitates the advancement of economical, straightforward, and eco-friendly synthesis processes that not only lower expenses but also ensure an unpolluted environment.

Metal pyrophosphates (M2P2O7, M; Cu, Ni, Co, Zn, Fe) have recently received a lot of interest among the researchers because of their extraordinary electrochemical properties, enabling their utilization in various applications including; energy storage, energy conversion, catalysis.30–33 Their distinctive attributes, including a layered structure, excellent reversibility, high electrical conductivity, admirable corrosion resistance, and redox stability, render them appropriate for use in energy storage devices, presenting them as competitive alternatives to conventional electrode materials. Researchers have conducted sustainable effort to investigate the electrochemical characteristics of pyrophosphates by selecting various combinations of metals with phosphates and pyrophosphates including; nickel,34 cobalt,35 copper,36 ferrous,37 and nickel-cobalt38 with various morphologies. Among various combinations, the combination of copper with pyrophosphate is unique and exceptional owing to its low-cost, non-toxic material, and higher intrinsic conductivity, which facilitates quicker electron transit, positioning it as a viable alternative to conventional nickel or cobalt phosphates.39,40 According to literature, Glaser et al.41 prepared Cu2P2O7 by flux method to identify the structure and phase transition of Cu2P2O7. From this study they have concluded that Cu2P2O7 exhibits two stable crystalline phases: α- Cu2P2O7 at low temperature and β- Cu2P2O7 at higher temperature. To understand enthalpy and thermal behavior of Cu2P2O7 Le et al.42 employed a solution based acidification approach to prepare α-Cu2P2O7 from copper sulfate and tetra sodium pyrophosphate. The blue crystals were collected and turned into pellets for calcination at 600 °C in air for three days. Karaphun et al.43 investigated the influence of calcination temperature on the physical and electrochemical characteristics of Cu2P2O7 prepared by hydrothermal method. From this study, they have concluded that Cu2P2O7 powder heated to 550 °C has the maximum specific capacitance of 297.521 F g−1 at 1 A g−1, as well as strong cycling stability even after 1000 cycles (94.04% retention). Agarwal et al.43 used the Successive ionic layer adsorption and reaction (SILAR) method for the preparation of ultrathin nano flakes of Cu2P2O7 directly on stainless steel electrode for SCs application. The symmetric supercapacitor device fabricated from Cu2P2O7 nanoflakes exhibit a capacitance of 102.5 F g−1 at a current density of 5 A g−1, with an energy density of 11.54 Wh/kg and a power density of 2760 W kg−1. Likewise, Lee et al.45 developed a Cu2P2O7/carbon nanotube composite cathode for sodium battery conversion. They have prepared Cu2P2O7 using wet ball-milling process followed by calcination at 850 °C for 10 h. The Cu2P2O7/carbon nanotube composite cathode material was prepared by mixing 1 wt% carbon nanotube with Cu2P2O7 using high energy ball milling at 100 rpm for 12 h. The resulting cathode has a high capacity of 355 mA h g−1, an average operation voltage of 2.4 V (versus Na+/Na), and retains 75% of its initial capacity after 300 cycles at 360 mAg−1. In different approach, Mangate et al.46 produced one dimensional nanofiber of Cu2P2O7 by using straightforward polymer-based electro spinning method followed by calcination for asymmetric supercapacitor application. The asymmetric device was constructed utilizing Cu2P2O7 nanofibers as the cathode and carbon fiber as the anode material, demonstrating a specific capacitance of 143.73 F g−1 at a current density of 0.4 A g−1. The energy density of the device was measured at 57.69 Wh/kg, while the power density reached 1104.7 W kg−1, retaining 92.9% of its capacity after 2000 cycles.

As per the literature, the synthesis of pyrophosphate has been described by several processing tactics, including the Flux method,41 hydrothermal techniques,43 acidification,42 electrospinning processes,46 Hydrogen bonding,47,48 and catalytic method.49 Further, the polymer mediated co-precipitation method for the synthesis of α-Cu2P2O7 for symmetric SCs application not been reported yet. Hence, present study focuses on a cost-effective Polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) mediated co-precipitation approach followed by calcination to prepare α-Cu2P2O7 and investigate its electrochemical characteristics for future symmetric SCs devices.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and materials

For the synthesis of Cu2P2O7, PVP (M.W.: 40 000), Cu(NO3)2·6H2O (copper nitrate hexahydrate), Na4P2O7 (Tetrasodium pyrophosphate), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), potassium hydroxide (KOH), Ns-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), cellulose filter paper was procured from Loba Chemie Pvt, Ltd. Multi-Walled Carbon Nano Tubes (MWCNT) and carbon cloth werse obtained from Technistro Industries, India. CR-2032 type coin cell was procured from Bat-sol Equipment and Technology Pvt Ltd, India. All these chemicals were used as received without further purification.

Experimental section

Synthesis of Cu2P2O7

The template-based synthesis of Cu2P2O7 was conducted by addition of 0.3 g of PVP with a stoichiometric mass of Cu(NO3)2·6H2O, both dissolved in 25 ml of Milli-Q water, alongside Na4P2O7, which was dissolved separately in 25 ml of MilliQ water. To get the precipitate, add the Na4P2O7 solution steadily to the previously described solution while stirring constantly. The resultant precipitate had been separated using centrifugation at 6000 rpm and subsequently dried in an oven at 50 °C for one hour. The resultant material was finally calcined at 500 °C in a muffle furnace for 30 minutes, with a heating rate of 1.66 °C per minute, to obtain the final Cu2P2O7 (Scheme 1).

Experimental procedure for the synthesis of Cu2P2O7.

Fabrication of electrode

The electrode was fabricated by mechanically grinding the mixture of Cu2P2O7 as an active material, carbon black as a conducting agent, and PVDF as a binder at a weight ratio of 80 : 15 : 5, with a few drops of NMP as a solvent, in a mortar and pestle until a homogeneous slurry was achieved. This slurry was carefully coated on clean nickel foam and was allowed to dry for 10 hours at 60 °C to eliminate the remaining NMP solvent and ensure adherence.

Electrode setup for symmetric supercapacitor

A symmetric supercapacitor (SCs) device was constructed utilizing a CR2032-type coin cell, with both the positive and negative electrodes composed of the identical active material, Cu2P2O7, and applied onto a stainless-steel current collector, maintaining comparable weights. The electrodes were positioned between cellulose paper that had been saturated with 6 M KOH, which acted as the electrolyte.

Characterization

The material's structure was analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) (Bruker, D2 PHASER) using Ni-filtered CuKα radiation (λ = 1.5418 Å), with a 2θ range of 10° to 70°. Rietveld refinement on the experimental data was performed using the FULLPROF package. Thermo Fisher Scientific (Phenom Pharos G2) was used for Field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) and Energy Dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) to evaluate the morphology and elemental analysis of the prepared sample. High resolution transmission electron (HR-TEM) by JEOL JEM 2100+ instruments was used to analyzed the internal structure of the material. Raman Spectroscopy was performed on an iRaman plus equipped with 532 nm diode laser, with the wavenumber range of 240 to 1200 cm−1. The Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was performed on a SHIMADZU IR-Spirit-X spectrometer in the range of 400 to 2800 cm−1. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed using Nexsa G2 instrument by Thermo Scientific. The surface area and porosity were determined through Brunner-Emmet-Teller (BET) analysis utilizing a Quantachrome AUTOSORB iQ made by Anton Paar Instruments. The electrochemical measurements of Cu2P2O7 material were determined using the Gamry Instruments 1010-E electrochemical workstation. The electrochemical behaviour of Cu2P2O7 was carried out with respect to Hg/HgO as a reference electrode and Pt Spring serving as the counter electrode in a 6 M KOH aq. electrolyte.

Field emission scanning electron microscope images

The morphological assessment serves as a practical approach aimed at substantially improving the overall effectiveness of materials for electrochemical applications. Fig. 3 displays FE-SEM images of the Cu2P2O7 material, emphasizing its surface structure at various resolution. Fig. 3(a) shows irregular polygonal-shaped structures in the form of thin nanoflakes. Upon higher resolution (Fig. 3(b)), it can be observed that the structures are nano-size and have a rough surface area due to a higher calcination temperature. The interconnected morphology of developed α-Cu2P2O7 forms a porous network with a rough surface, which establishes a clear structure–activity relationship. The high surface roughness maximizes electrochemically active sites, the porous architecture facilitates rapid ion diffusion and electrolyte penetration, and the interconnected framework ensures efficient electron mobilization.51

An elemental composition of the Cu2P2O7 was performed using EDX. The EDX spectrum of Cu2P2O7, illustrated in Fig. 4(a). Fig. 4 (b–e) represent the elemental mapping which verifies the existence of copper (Cu), phosphorus (P), and oxygen (O) atoms ratio. Furthermore, there are no other element peaks observed in the spectrum, further substantiating the purity of the α-Cu2P2O7.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy

The XPS analysis was conducted to examine the oxidation states of Cu, P, and O atoms in the α-Cu2P2O7 material within the energy range of 0 to 1200 eV. Fig. 6(a) illustrates the XPS survey scan, which shows the group of signals representing Cu2p and P2p as well as O1s, confirming the availability of Cu, P, and O atoms in α-Cu2P2O7 material, including the background peak with C 1s. In addition, the peak at 569.98 eV corresponds to the Cu LMM band, while another peak of the O KLL band appears at 978 eV. Meanwhile, the binding energy of 78 eV is correlated with the Cu3p band. Moreover, Fig. 6(b) displays the narrow spectrum of Cu2p, indicating two spin–orbit peaks at 956.6 eV and 936.5 eV are aligned with Cu2p1/2 and Cu2p3/2, whereas the two satellite signals appear at binding energies of 963.8 eV and 944.1 eV, respectively. Furthermore, the P2p binding energy observed at 133.4 eV confirms the presence of phosphorus with pentavalent state (P5+). In the same vein, the deconvolved high-resolution O 1 s signal, Fig. 6(d), is identified by two significant peaks: one at 531.5 eV, linked to O atoms in Cu–O–P bonds, and the other at 533 eV, ascribed to surface-adsorbed O2 bonds.44,46 The results unequivocally show the oxidation state of the α-Cu2P2O7 molecule.

Electrochemical exploration of synthesized α-Cu2P2O7 material

Three-electrode measurement

The electrochemical evaluation of the synthesized α-Cu2P2O7 electrode was conducted via CV across various potential windows of 0 to 0.5 V, 0.6 V, and 0.7 V at 20 mV s−1 depicted in Fig. S(4(a)); it can be observed that beyond 0.6 V a clear polarization effect is observed restricted the capacity of the α-Cu2P2O7 electrode. Thus, the effective potential range is selected between 0 and 0.6 V. Fig. 8(a) depicts CV with scan rates ranging from 10 to 100 mV s−1. The α-Cu2P2O7 electrode displays a quasi-rectangular CV curve with peaks at 0.15 V, suggesting pseudo-capacitance behavior. Furthermore, the large anodic and cathodic redox peaks indicate quasi-reversible electron transport at the electrode–electrolyte interface during the faradaic redox phenomenon, resulting in Cu ion oxidation state switching.43 The area under the CV curve expands with the increase in scan rate, which signifies a direct relation between scan rate and voltametric current. The shape of the curve remains unchanged even when the scan rate is increased, signifying a reversible charge transfer reaction in conjunction with surface adsorption. There was an absence of gas genesis on the electrodes, and no change in electrolyte color was seen during CV testing at different scans. This signifies that the synthesized mesoporous α-Cu2P2O7 electrode material exhibits improved stability over the 0 V to 0.6 V potential range.

Symmetric supercapacitor device

The schematic layout of the coin cell (Scheme 2) shows its major components grouped in series, including the cap and can, which are used to firmly close the cell. A spring is used to maintain pressure and ensure contact between the two electrodes. The stainless-steel plates serve as a current collector that are uniformly covered with a slurry of electrode material, forming both an anode and cathode. The separator was inserted between the electrodes to allow ionic flow while avoiding short connections. This sort of coin cell configuration offers mechanical stability, ionic transport assistance, and excellent electrochemical performance.

Conclusion

A template-based co-precipitation synthesis was employed to develop a high-performance SCs electrode. The development of pyrophosphate with copper ions also preserved structural stability and strong adhesion during the electrochemical process. The monoclinic structure with the space group C12/c1 was confirmed by XRD analysis. FE-SEM images indicated the formation of an interconnected particle with a rough surface area that aids in ion transport. FTIR spectroscopy confirmed the bending vibrations of α-Cu2P2O7, while Raman spectroscopy validated the bond formation of α-Cu2P2O7. Consequently, the α-Cu2P2O7 electrode possesses exceptional electrochemical properties, such as an outstanding specific capacitance of 667 F g−1 at a current density of 5 A g−1, an excellent rate capability of 90% after 5000 cycles, and an 80% coulombic efficiency in a three-electrode system. Additionally, the α-Cu2P2O7 symmetric SCs reveal a specific capacitance of 225 F g−1 at a current density of 1 A g−1. The device offers energy and power densities of 80 Wh/kg and 3200 W kg−1, respectively. It also exhibits exceptional cycling stability, maintaining 90% of its capacitance and achieving 88% of coulombic efficiency after 10 000 charge–discharge cycles at a current density of 1.5 A g−1. Ultimately, the fundamentals of the α-Cu2P2O7 electrode enable the development of alternative α-Cu2P2O7 electrodes for energy storage devices and SCs, thereby expanding its potential applications.

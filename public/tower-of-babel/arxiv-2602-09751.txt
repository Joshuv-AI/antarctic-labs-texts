The Carathéodory metric on Teichmüller space of genus two surface
Kejie Lin and Weixu Su

Abstract
. Let 𝒯 g , n \mathcal{T}_{g,n} be the Teichmüller space of Riemann surfaces of genus g g with n n punctures. It is conjectured that the Teichmüller and Carathéodory metrics agree on a Teichmüller disk if and only if all the zeros of the corresponding holomorphic quadratic differential are of even order. The conjecture was proved by Gekhtman and Markovic for 𝒯 0 , 5 ≅ 𝒯 1 , 2 \mathcal{T}_{0,5}\cong\mathcal{T}_{1,2} . We confirm the conjecture for 𝒯 2 , 0 ≅ 𝒯 0 , 6 \mathcal{T}_{2,0}\cong\mathcal{T}_{0,6} .



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
be the Teichmüller space of Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
marked points.
Throughout this paper, we assume that
3g-3+n\geq 2
.
The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
is the (orbifold) universal cover of the moduli space of Riemann surfaces

\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and is naturally a complex manifold of dimension
3g-3+n
. It is known that

\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
can be realized as a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3+n}
, by the Bers
embedding
[
3
]
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be the upper half-plane, equipped with the Poincaré metric

d_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{|dz|}{2\operatorname{Im}z}.

The
Kobayashi metric

d_{\mathcal{K}}
on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
is the largest metric so that every holomorphic map

f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n}

is nonexpanding.
Royden
[
13
]
proved that the Kobayashi metric on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
coincides with the Teichmüller metric
d_{\mathcal{T}}
.
Furthermore, the Teichmüller metric is not homogeneous at any point and its isometry group is essentially the mapping class group.

Another natural metric on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
satisfying the Schwarz-Pick inequality is the
Carathéodory metric
, which can be defined as the smallest metric so that every holomorphic map

F:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}

is nonexpanding.
Let
d_{\mathcal{C}}
denote the
Carathéodory metric
on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
.
By the Schwarz Lemma, the inequality

d_{\mathcal{C}}\leq d_{\mathcal{T}}

holds on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
.

A longstanding open problem is whether the two metrics
d_{\mathcal{T}}
and
d_{\mathcal{C}}
agree on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
. Recently, Markovic
[
11
]
solved the problem.

Teichmüller disks, also known as complex geodesics, are holomorphic isometric
embeddings of the hyperbolic plane into Teichmüller space. There is a unique Teichmüller disc through any pair of distinct points in
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
. Every Teichmüller disk is generated by a non-zero holomorphic quadratic differential
\phi
, with at most simple poles at the marked points.

Let

\tau^{\phi}:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n}

be a Teichmüller disk.
Kra
[
10
]
proved that the two metrics
d_{\mathcal{T}}
and
d_{\mathcal{C}}
agree on
\tau^{\phi}
if all the zeros of
\phi
are of even order,
see also McMullen
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Remark 1
.

Note that if a holomorphic quadratic differential
\phi
vanishes at a marked point (puncture), then it is not necessary to require that it vanish of even order.

Markovic
[
11
]
established a criterion that says when the two metrics
d_{\mathcal{T}}
and
d_{\mathcal{C}}
agree on a Teichmüller disk.
Then he used this criterion to show that
d_{\mathcal{T}}
and
d_{\mathcal{C}}
disagree on certain Teichmüller disk in
\mathcal{T}_{0,5}
.
Since there exists a holomorphic and isometric embedding of
\mathcal{T}_{0,5}
into
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
,
we have
d_{\mathcal{T}}\neq d_{\mathcal{C}}
on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
.

An application of Markovic’s result is to prove a folklore conjecture of Siu that the Teichmüller space is not biholomorphic to any bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3+n}
. Note that Gupta and Seshadri

[
7
, Theorem 1.1.]
proved a related result that the Teichmüller space is not biholomorphically equivalent to any bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3+n}
which is strictly locally convex at one boundary point.

A remaining problem is to classify Teichmüller disks for which
d_{\mathcal{T}}=d_{\mathcal{C}}
.
This is equivalent to classifying Teichmüller disks that are holomorphic retracts of the Teichmüller space. Gekhtman and Markovic
[
6
]
made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

A Teichmüller disk is a holomorphic retract if and only if it is generated by
a holomorphic quadratic differential all of whose zeros are of even order.

Gekhtman and Markovic
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
proved the above conjecture for
\mathcal{T}_{0,5}\cong\mathcal{T}_{1,2}
.
In this paper, we confirm the conjecture for
\mathcal{T}_{0,6}\cong\mathcal{T}_{2,0}
,
which is of complex dimension
3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\tau^{\phi}
be a Teichmüller disk in
\mathcal{T}_{2,0}
.
The Teichmüller metric and Carathéodory metric agree on
\tau^{\phi}
if and only if
\phi
is a quadratic differential all of whose zeros are of even order.

Equivalently, we show

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\tau^{\phi}
be a Teichmüller disk in
\mathcal{T}_{0,6}
.
The Teichmüller metric and Carathéodory metric agree on
\tau^{\phi}
if and only if either
\phi
is a quadratic differential with no odd-order zeros, or
\phi
is a quadratic differential with a simple zero located at a marked point.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
follows the strategy developed by Gekhtman and Markovic
[
11
,
6
]
.
Due to Markovic’s criterion, it suffices to classify Jenkins-Strebel differentials
with at least one odd-order zero. In Section
3
, by applying cylinder deformations or by interchanging the vertical and horizontal foliations if necessary, we prove that Theorem
1.3
can be reduced to the case when
\phi
is a staircase.

Assume that
\phi
is a staircase surface and that the corresponding Teichmüller disk
\tau^{\phi}

admits a holomoprhic retraction, say

F:\mathcal{T}_{0,6}\to\mathbb{H}
.
In Section
4
, we use the Schwarz-Christoffel formula to compute
F
on certain smooth paths of
\mathcal{T}_{0,6}
. The computation is explicit and it shows that
F
fails to be
C^{2}
-smooth, which leads to a contradiction.
Our computational method generalizes Markovic’s previous work on
L
-shaped pillowcases
[
11
]
.

Remark 2
.

By a result of Apisa and Wright
[
1
, Corollary 1.3]
,
Conjecture
1.1
can be reduced to checking strata (and full loci of covers)
of genus zero quadratic differentials.

2.
Teichmüller disks and Orbit closures

In this section, we introduce the notion of Teichmüller disk.
Then we recall the method of Gekhtman and Markovic
[
6
]
,
which reduces the classification of Teichmüller disks to the case of Jenkins-Strebel differentials.
Most of the relevant materials can be found in
[
6
]
.
See Gupta
[
8
]
for a survey on complex analytic aspect of Teichmüller space.
For general background on Teichmüller theory and quadratic differentials, we refer to
Hubbard
[
9
]
and Strebel
[
15
]
.

2.1.
Teichmüller disks and holomorphic retracts

Let
S_{g,n}
be an oriented surface of genus
g

with
n
marked points (punctures), where
3g-3+n\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
be the Teichmüller space of Riemann surfaces marked by
S_{g,n}
.
Let
\operatorname{Mod}_{g,n}
be the mapping class group of
S_{g,n}
. The quotient

\mathcal{M}_{g,n}=\mathcal{T}_{g,n}/\operatorname{Mod}_{g,n}

is the moduli space of Riemann surfaces. Let

\pi:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

be the natural projection.

Given
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
, a
holomorphic quadratic differential

\phi
on
X
is a
(2,0)
tensor
locally given by

\phi=\phi(z)dz^{2},

where
\phi(z)
is a holomorphic function.
Let
Q(X)
be the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials
\phi
on
X
such that the
L^{1}
-norm

\|\phi\|=\int_{X}|\phi|

is finite. Note that
\|\phi\|<\infty

if and only if
\phi
has at most simple poles at the punctures.
Any
\phi\in Q(X)

induces a flat metric of finite area, which is also called a
half-translation surface
.

It is known that the cotangent space of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
at
X

can be naturally identified with
Q(X)
and the
L^{1}
-norm is dual to the Teichmüller norm.
We denote the cotangent bundle by
Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
.
A pair
(X,\phi)\in Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
with
\phi\in Q(X)
and
\phi\neq 0
generates a holomorphic embedding

\tau^{\phi}:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n},

which is an isometry from the Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{H}

to the Teichmüller metric on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
. For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{H}
,
the Riemann surface
X_{\lambda}=\tau^{\phi}(\lambda)
is characterized by the property that the extremal quasiconformal map between
X
and
X_{\lambda}
has Beltrami differential

\mu_{\lambda}=\left(\frac{i-\lambda}{i+\lambda}\right)\frac{|\phi|}{\phi}.

In the natural coordinates associated with
\phi
, the extremal quasiconformal map is
an affine map of the form

z=x+iy\mapsto x+\lambda y.

By abuse of notation, we write

\tau^{\phi}(\lambda)=\left(\frac{i-\lambda}{i+\lambda}\right)\frac{|\phi|}{\phi}.

We call
\tau^{\phi}
the
Teichmüller disk
generated by
\phi
.
Note that
\tau^{\phi}\left(i\cdot\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
is the Teichmüller geodesic
in the direction of
\phi
.

Definition 2.1

(Holomorphic retract)
.

Let
\tau^{\phi}:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
be a Teichmüller disk.
We say that
\tau^{\phi}
is a
holomorphic retract
of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
if
there exists a holomorphic map
F:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\to\mathbb{H}
such that

F\circ\tau^{\phi}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Any holomorphic map
F:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\to\mathbb{H}
satisfies the above property is called a holomorphic retraction of
\tau^{\phi}
.

The following lemma states that classifying Teichmüller disks for which
d_{\mathcal{T}}=d_{\mathcal{C}}
is equivalent to classifying holomorphic retracts of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
.
See
[
6
, Lemma 1.3]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The Kobayashi and Carathéodory metrics agree on a Teichmüller disk
\tau^{\phi}
if and only if
\tau^{\phi}
is a holomorphic retract of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
.

2.2.
Orbit closures

There is a natural
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
-action on
Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}

that commutes with
the action of
\operatorname{Mod}_{g,n}
. One can define a quadratic differential

\phi
by gluing a polygon
P
along sides in pairs, in a way that each side of
P

belongs to exactly one pair and the two sides in each pair are parallel and of the
same length. If
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
, then
A
acts on
P

as an affine transformation. By definition,
A\cdot\phi
is the quadratic differential obtained by gluing
A\cdot P
in the same pattern as that for
P
.

Let
p:Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
be the natural projection.
For any
(X,\phi)\in Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
, the image of
A\cdot(X,\phi)
under
p

remains the same if and only if we multiply
A
by
\mathbb{C}^{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
Note that
\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
. In fact, we can identify
\mathbb{H}

as a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
:

\mathbb{H}\cong\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&\operatorname{Re}\lambda\\
0&\operatorname{Im}\lambda\end{pmatrix}\ :\ \lambda\in\mathbb{H}\right\}.

In complex coordinates, the action of
\begin{pmatrix}1&\operatorname{Re}\lambda\\
0&\operatorname{Im}\lambda\end{pmatrix}
is given by

x+iy\mapsto x+\lambda y.

So the Beltrami differential is

\left(\frac{i-\lambda}{i+\lambda}\right)\frac{|\phi|}{\phi},

which coincides with
\tau^{\phi}(\lambda)
defined before.

Let
Q\mathcal{M}_{g,n}=Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}/\operatorname{Mod}_{g,n}
be the moduli space of holomorphic quadratic differentials.
We also use
\pi
to denote the natural projection
Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\to Q\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. The
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
-action on
Q\mathcal{T}_{g,n}

induces an action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
on
Q\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.
Given
(X,\phi)\in Q\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, we denote by
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})\cdot\phi
the
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
orbit of
(X,\phi)
.
Let
\overline{\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})\cdot\phi}
be the orbit closure in a stratum of
Q\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.

The next result is very useful, proved by Gekhtman and Markovic
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be a quadratic differential.
If
\phi
generates a Teichmüller disk
\tau^{\phi}
that is a holomorphic retract of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
, so does every element in the orbit closure
\overline{\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})\cdot\phi}
.

Let

\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{m})
be an integral vector where
\kappa_{i}\geq-1
,
i=1,...,m
, and satisfy
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{i}=4g-4.

Denote by
\mathcal{Q}(\kappa)
the stratum of quadratic differentials
(X,\phi)
in

Q\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
consisting of
\phi
with
m
distinct zeros or poles of multiplicities
\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{m}
.

The horocycle flow action
h_{t}:\mathcal{Q}(\kappa)\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}(\kappa)

is defined as the restriction of the
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
-action to the subgroup

H=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\ :\ t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

We refer to
[
5
]
for a comprehensive survey of the dynamics of
the horocycle flow.

Let
\phi
be a non-zero Jenkins-Strebel differential in
Q\mathcal{M}_{0,6}
with
k
cylinders. A
saddle connection
of
\phi
is a geodesic segment connecting singularities (zeros or poles) of
\phi
, and
which does not contain any singularities in its interior.
Let
\mathcal{F}(\phi)
be the horizontal foliation
of
\phi
, whose leaves are tangent to the directions such that
\phi(z)dz^{2}>0
. Denote the horizontal critical graph of
\phi

by
\Gamma=\Gamma(\phi)
.
We say that
\phi
or
\mathcal{F}(\phi)
is
Jenkins-Strebel
if
\Gamma(\phi)
is compact and the complement
X\setminus\Gamma(\phi)
is a union of disjoint flat cylinders foliated by horizontal closed leaves.

Smillie and Weiss
[
14
, Theorem 5]
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(X,\phi)\in\mathcal{Q}(\kappa)
. Then

(1)

The closure
\overline{H\cdot\phi}
contains a Jenkins-Strebel differential
\psi\in\mathcal{Q}(\kappa)
.

(2)

If
\phi
has
k
horizontal cylinders whose union is not dense in
S_{g,n}
,
then there exists a Jenkins-Strebel differential
\psi
in
\overline{H\cdot\phi}
that has at least
k+1
horizontal cylinders.

See
[
6
, §2.4]
for details.

By Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
, Conjecture
1.1

can be reduced to the classification of Jenkins-Strebel differentials.

2.3.
Markovic’s criterion

Suppose that
\phi
is a Jenkins-Strebel differential on
X
that decomposes the surface into a finite number of horizontal cylinders
\Pi_{j},j=1,\cdots,k
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{k}
be the
k
-fold product of the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
. Following
[
11
, Section 4]
, we define the
Teichmüller polydisk

\mathcal{E}^{\phi}:\mathbb{H}^{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n}

by associating
each
{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
a Riemann surface
X_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
determined by the Beltrami differential

\mu_{\lambda}=\left(\frac{i-\lambda_{j}}{i+\lambda_{j}}\right)\frac{|\phi|}{\phi}

on each
\Pi_{j}
.

Remark 3
.

It is known that (see
[
11
, Section 4]
)
\mathcal{E}^{\phi}
is a holomorphic embedding, but not proper.

Denote by
h_{j}
the height of
\Pi_{j}
.
Note that each
\Pi_{j}
is deformed into a new horizontal cylinder
\Pi_{j}(\lambda)
.
The circumference is preserved, while the height is scaled by a factor
\operatorname{Im}\lambda_{j}
.
By gluing
\Pi_{j}(\lambda),i=1,\cdots,k
, we obtain a
new Jenkins-Strebel differential
\phi_{\lambda}
on
X_{\lambda}
.

The following beautiful and important theorem provides a criterion to characterize Jenkins-Strebel differentials which generate Teichmüller disks
that admit holomorphic retractions. See
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Markovic’s criterion)
.

Let
\phi\in Q(X)
be a non-zero Jenkins-Strebel differential. Denote the area of the
j
-th cylinder of
\phi
by
a_{j}
. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\phi}:\mathbb{H}^{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
be the Teichmüller polydisk associated with
\phi
. Then the Teichmüller disk
\tau^{\phi}
admits a holomorphic retraction if and only if there exists a holomorphic map
\Phi:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
satisfying

(1)

\left(\Phi\circ\mathcal{E}^{\phi}\right)(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\lambda_{j}.

As a corollary of Theorem
2.5
, if
\phi
is a Jenkins-Strebel differential with
k
cylinders and if
\phi
generates a Teichmüller disk that admits a holomorphic retraction, then so does every quadratic differential in its
\mathbb{H}^{k}
orbit.

Based on Theorem
2.5
, Gekhtman and Markovic proposed the program to solve Conjecture
1.1
by showing that for any quadratic differential
\phi
with an odd-order zero (not lie at a marked point), the orbit closure of
\phi
contains a Jenkins-Strebel differential that does not satisfy the criterion (
1
). They carried out the program for
\mathcal{T}_{0,5}
in
[
6
]
.

In the remainder of this paper, we focus on
\mathcal{T}_{0,6}
.

\mathbf{R}(x,y)
depends smoothly on
x
and
y
, thus
F(\mathbf{R}(x,y))
defines a smooth function of
x
and
y
.

Now we claim that each
\mathbf{R}(x,y)
can be identified with a certain surface

\mathbf{S}(s,t)=S\left(a(s,t),b(s,t),c(s,t),p(s,t),q(s,t)\right),

which arises from
the deformation constructed in Section
4.2
.
We set
p=p_{0}-x,q=q_{0}-y
.

Lemma 4.8
.

For
x,y
sufficiently small, we can locate
(s,t)
such that the surface

\mathbf{S}(s,t)=S\left(a(s,t),b(s,t),c(s,t),p(s,t),q(s,t)\right),

which is the deformation of the base surface
\mathbf{S}(0,0)=\mathbf{R}(x,y)
,
satisfying

p(s,t)=p_{0},q(s,t)=q_{0}.

Proof.

By our previous computation, there are positive coefficients
P
and
Q
(depending on the base surface
\mathbf{R}(x,y)
) such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p(s,t)-p=\left(P+o(1)\right)t\ln{\frac{1}{t}}+O(s),\\
q(s,t)-q=\left(-P+o(1)\right)t\ln{\frac{1}{t}}+\left(Q+o(1)\right)s\ln{\frac{1}{s}}.\end{cases}

We set

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u=s\ln{\frac{1}{s}},\\
v=t\ln{\frac{1}{t}}.\end{cases}

Then the value of

\left(p(s,t)-p,q(s,t)-q\right)

is approximately
\left(Pv,-Pv+Qu\right)
. Note that the inverse of the matrix
\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&P\\
Q&-P\\
\end{array}}\right]
is
\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{Q}&\frac{1}{Q}\\
\frac{1}{P}&0\\
\end{array}}\right]
, which is positive.
Thus for
x,y>0
that are sufficiently small, there exist
u>0,v>0
(and then
s>0,t>0
) such that

p(s,t)-p=x,\quad q(s,t)-q=y.

This gives
p(s,t)=p_{0},q(s,t)=q_{0}
.

∎

Now we are able to prove Theorem
4.1
.
Using the same notation as in Lemma
4.8
, we have

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}x\\
y\\
\end{array}}\right]=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}o(1)&P+o(1)\\
Q+o(1)&-P+o(1)\\
\end{array}}\right]\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
v\\
\end{array}}\right]

This gives

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}u\\
v\\
\end{array}}\right]=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{Q}+o(1)&\frac{1}{Q}+o(1)\\
\frac{1}{P}+o(1)&o(1)\\
\end{array}}\right]\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}x\\
y\\
\end{array}}\right]

Recall that
u=s\ln\frac{1}{s},v=t\ln\frac{1}{t}
.
Thus
s=\frac{u}{\ln\frac{1}{s}},t=\frac{v}{\ln\frac{1}{t}}
.
Since

\ln{\frac{1}{s}}=(1+o(1))\ln{\frac{1}{u}},\ln{\frac{1}{t}}=(1+o(1))\ln{\frac{1}{v}},

we have

s=\frac{u}{(1+o(1))\ln{\frac{1}{u}}},t=\frac{v}{(1+o(1))\ln{\frac{1}{v}}}.

As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{1}{Q}+o(1)\right)x+\left(\frac{1}{Q}+o(1)\right)y}{\left(1+o(1)\right)\ln\left(\frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{Q}+o(1)\right)x+\left(\frac{1}{Q}+o(1)\right)y}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{x}{Q}+\frac{y}{Q}+o(x)+o(y)}{\ln\left(\frac{Q}{x+y}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x+y+o(x)+o(y)}{Q\ln\frac{1}{x+y}},

where the last equality holds since

\lim_{x+y\to 0}\frac{\ln\frac{Q}{x+y}}{\ln\frac{1}{x+y}}=1.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x+o(x)+o(y)}{P\ln\frac{1}{x}}.

By Proposition
4.6
, substituting
s
and
t
with
x
and
y
, we find that

(13)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Area}(L(a(s,t),b(s,t),c(s,t),p_{0},q_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(0,0)+\beta_{1}t+\beta_{2}s+\beta_{11}t^{2}\ln\frac{1}{t}+\beta_{22}s^{2}\ln\frac{1}{s}

\displaystyle+o(st\ln\frac{1}{t})+o(t^{2}\ln\frac{1}{t})+o(s^{2}\ln\frac{1}{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(0,0)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{P}\frac{x+o(x)+o(y)}{\ln\frac{1}{x}}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{Q}\frac{x+y+o(x)+o(y)}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{x+y}\right)}

\displaystyle+\pi\frac{x^{2}}{\ln\frac{1}{x}}+\pi\frac{\left(x+y\right)^{2}}{\ln\frac{1}{x+y}}+o\left(\frac{x^{2}+xy+y^{2}}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{x+y}\right)}\right)+o\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\ln\frac{1}{x}}\right).

Here we use

and

.

On the other hand, we have
\mathbf{R}(x,y)\cong S(a(s,t),b(s,t),c(s,t),p_{0},q_{0})

. By Lemma

.

.

)

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{1}}{P}\frac{x+o(x)+o(y)}{\ln\frac{1}{x}}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{Q}\frac{x+y+o(x)+o(y)}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{x+y}\right)}

\displaystyle=

If we take

Divide both sides by

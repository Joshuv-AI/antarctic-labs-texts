Kuga-Satake construction on families of K3 surfaces of Picard rank 14 14
Flora Poon

Abstract
The isometry between the type IV 6 and the type II 4 hermitian symmetric domains suggests a possible relation between suitable moduli spaces of K3 surfaces of Picard rank 14 14 and of polarised abelian 8 8 -folds with totally definite quaternion multiplication. We show how this isometry induces a geometrically meaningful map between such moduli spaces using the Kuga-Satake construction. Furthermore, we illustrate how the the modular mapping can be realised for any specific families of K3 surfaces of Picard rank 14, which can be specialised to families of K3 surfaces of higher Picard rank.

{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)
over
(V,q)
is defined as

{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V):=\mathop{\hbox{}\mathrm{T}}(V)/I(V)

where
\mathop{\hbox{}\mathrm{T}}(V):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigotimes^{k}V
is the tensor algebra over

I(V):=\langle v\otimes v-q(v):v\in V\rangle
is an ideal.

There is a universal property for Clifford algebras.

Lemma 2.2.2

(Fundamental lemma for Clifford algebras)
.

[
Ha
, Lemma 9.7]

Let

be an associative algebra with unit over
R
.
Let
\varphi\colon V\rightarrow A
be a linear map from
V
into

.
If for all

we have

\varphi(v)\varphi(v)-q(v)\cdot{\mathbf{1}}_{A}=0,

then
\varphi
has a unique extension to a homomorphism of algebras from
{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)
to
A
.

Clifford algebras admit the following distinguished involutions:

Definition 2.2.3
.

1.

[
LM
, Equation 1.7]
The canonical automorphism

(\cdot)^{-}\colon{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)\longrightarrow{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)
is an involution defined by extending the isometry

on
V

to an automorphism on
{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)
.

2.

[
LM
, Equation 1.15]
Consider the involution
(\cdot)^{t}\colon\mathop{\hbox{}\mathrm{T}}(V)\rightarrow\mathop{\hbox{}\mathrm{T}}(V)
such that for any

Since
(\cdot)^{t}
sends the ideal

to itself, it descends to an involution on
{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)
called the transpose, which we still denote by

.

The Clifford algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebra: since

only contains elements of even degree with respect to the natural

-grading of

, the

In particular, we have

is the

of

, and

is the

spanned by the classes of odd degree elements in

Remark 2.2.4
.

By forgetting the Clifford multiplication,
{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)
is isomorphic to the exterior algebra

as modules or vector spaces. Therefore,
2\dim\left({\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(V)\right)=\dim\left({\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(V)\right)=\dim\left(\bigwedge V\right)=2^{n}
.

Being

-graded algebra, Clifford algebra have a
graded tensor product

.
Disregarding the multiplication, the graded tensor product of two graded algebras is the ordinary tensor product of graded modules
[

, Section IV.2]

In particular, the graded tensor product of two Clifford algebras is also a Clifford algebra with the usual Clifford multiplication.

(Gluing of Clifford algebras)

2.3

In this subsection, we explicitly construct a Kuga-Satake (KS) variety from a lattice polarised K3 surface. The main references are
[

[

The starting ingredient of the KS construction is a

\emph{i.e.}
a weight two Hodge structure

with
\dim V^{2,0}=1
and a quadratic form
q
of signature
(\dim V-2,2)

Let

Note that

Remark 2.3.1
.

The identities (
5
) and (
6
) imply
{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(n_{+},n_{-})\simeq{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(n_{-},n_{+})

to be

or

By Remark
2.2.4
, the Clifford algebra
{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}(T)
over

is a lattice of rank

The quotient

\mathbb{T}:={\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(T_{\mathbb{R}})/{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(T)

is therefore a torus of real dimension

.
Moreover,

determines a complex structure on
\mathbb{T}
.
Pick a generator

of

and

.
Since

and

Set

.
One can check
[

, Lemma 5.5]

Under the adjoint representation,

then gives a complex structure on

Finally, we give a construction of a polarisation on the complex torus

Choose two orthogonal vectors

, and let

.
Consider the pairing

\displaystyle E\colon{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(T)\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}}^{+}(T)

Therefore,

.
We call this abelian variety a KS variety, and denote it by

or
\mathop{\mathrm{KS}}(T)
. We have suppressed

in the notation because it is always clear what

[

.

[

Theorem
5.0.2

Remark 5.0.6
.

2

.

By

]
and

Mo2

5.0.2

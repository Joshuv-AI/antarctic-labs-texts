Knowledge, attitudes, and practices on osteoporosis prevention and treatment strategies in the MENA region: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Youssef Makarem, Bshara Sleem, Marlene Toufic Chakhtoura, Ola El Zein, Ghada El-Hajj Fuleihan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis represents a growing public health concern in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region, where ageing populations and limited healthcare access contribute to high fracture rates and poor treatment adherence. Despite the existence of clinical practice guidelines, these often lack integration of stakeholder perspectives such as those of patients, healthcare providers, insurers and systems. Understanding knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) related to osteoporosis is essential to inform inclusive, culturally relevant strategies for prevention and management. This systematic review aims to evaluate the knowledge, attitudes (preferences) and practices (behaviours) of key stakeholders, including adults aged 50 years and older and healthcare providers, regarding the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of osteoporosis in the MENA region.Methods and analysisFollowing the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols guidelines, we conducted comprehensive searches of MEDLINE and Embase for studies published from January 1994 to January 2025. Two reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts and full texts in duplicate. Title/abstract and full-text screening were completed by September 2025. Data extraction will begin in October 2025 and will be performed in duplicate using piloted standardised forms. Risk of bias assessment and data synthesis will follow, using validated design-specific tools and a narrative approach guided by the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis framework. The review was initiated in June 2025, and completion of analysis and manuscript preparation is anticipated in June 2026.Ethics and disseminationThis review synthesises data from publicly available literature and does not involve primary data collection with human participants; therefore, ethics approval is not required. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and deposition of study materials on the Open Science Framework.Trail registration numberThis protocol is registered on the Open Science Framework.

Background

Effective healthcare relies on integrating the perspectives of a wide range of stakeholders, including patients, caregivers, healthcare providers, insurers and health systems.13 Understanding the knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) of these stakeholders is critical for the development of high-quality clinical practice guidelines,4 that in turn influence the clinical care and significantly impact treatment outcomes.5 6

For chronic diseases such as osteoporosis, shared decision-making that incorporates patient preferences is particularly vital.7 8 A narrative review conducted in Belgium highlighted improved adherence to bisphosphonate treatments when patients could choose medication dosing frequency.9

Previous systematic reviews (SRs) have explored specific aspects of osteoporosis management, such as perceptions of women on physical activity and bone health,10 medication preferences and physiotherapeutic strategies.11 Additionally, two SRs were conducted to inform the development of osteoporosis clinical guidelines.12 13

Despite the increasing recognition of the importance of KAP perspectives of physicians and patients, there remains a significant gap in osteoporosis management. The Endocrine Society guidelines,14 along with others,1520 emphasised the importance of considering patient perspectives on the benefits, adherence and persistence of treatment, alongside healthcare provider and public viewpoints, as part of osteoporosis management.17 19

A 2019 analysis of osteoporosis guidelines revealed that only 27 out of 70 guidelines explicitly addressed this important domain. While some guidelines acknowledged the importance of incorporating patient perspectives, the supporting evidence was often insufficient. Moreover, 14 of these guidelines were primarily focused on medication preferences, thus overlooking a broader understanding of the perspectives of patients in osteoporosis management.21

Research on KAP of healthcare professionals (HCPs) regarding osteoporosis management has also been limited. A recent SR focused on the perspectives of HCPs on osteoporosis drug therapy but excluded non-pharmacological strategies and geographic context.22 Similarly, qualitative studies on KAP of HCPs in specific countries remain insufficient for comprehensive guidelines.2331

In summary, existing literature has emphasised certain aspects of osteoporosis management and described physician and patient adherence in specific populations. However, data from the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) is relatively scarce. This gap limits the development of robust guidelines that engage stakeholders and address diverse perspectives, particularly in this region, where culturally inclusive strategies are crucial.

Objective

This SR aims to comprehensively evaluate the KAP of stakeholders revolving around key elements related to osteoporosis diagnosis and management in the MENA region. The review will explore perspectives of adults aged 50 years and older in this region, being patients or participants from the general population, in addition to various relevant groups—caregivers, healthcare providers, insurers and health systems.

Information sources

We searched MEDLINE and Embase databases for studies published from January 1994 to January 2025. We used specific MeSH terms to ensure that all relevant literature is captured. The key MeSH terms used included ‘middle aged and aged adults’, ‘health personnel’, ‘health insurance’, ‘health knowledge, attitudes, practice’, ‘bone density conservation agents’, ‘bone density’, ‘fracture risk assessment’, as well as ‘Middle East’ and ‘Northern Africa’.

Selection process

The selection process involved exporting the search results into Covidence software (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia) [34].34 Screening guides for titles/abstracts and full texts were prepared based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. To familiarise the reviewers with the screening process, a calibration exercise was conducted. Both reviewers pilot-tested the title and abstract screening sheet with 100 citations to ensure consistency between the two reviewers, until the discrepancy rate was <5%. Subsequently, the two reviewers independently screened the remaining titles and abstracts in duplicate using the developed screening sheet. Full texts of articles that pass this initial screening were retrieved for further review and screened independently using the full-text guide. Any disagreements during the screening process were resolved through discussion, and when consensus could not be reached, a content expert was consulted to make the final decision. Reasons for exclusion were recorded. Agreement between the two reviewers at each screening step was assessed using calibration exercises and discussion.

Data collection process

A standardised data extraction form has been created and pilot-tested prior to the initiation of data collection. This form includes key variables related to authors, country, year of publication, study design, study period, sample size, study population, recruitment methods, demographics of participants, intervention or tool assessed, and outcomes of interest related to knowledge, attitudes (preferences) and practices (behaviour). Two independent reviewers will extract data from each selected report, in duplicate. Each reviewer will be responsible for 100% of the studies included in the review, ensuring that the data handling process is performed in duplicate. The reviewers will work independently throughout the data extraction phase to minimise bias. They will extract data based on the pre-defined categories listed in the standardised data extraction form without any initial discussion. Throughout the process, reviewers will compare results. Our aim is to keep the discrepancy ≤5% during the data abstraction phase. We will discuss discrepancies until reaching a consensus. In cases where agreement cannot be achieved, we will consult a content expert to make the final determination.

We will maintain detailed records of the data extraction process, documenting the number of studies reviewed, the specific variables collected and any modifications made during the review. We will securely store all extracted data in a shared drive with restricted access to ensure confidentiality.

Data items

Participant characteristics

Demographic data, including age, gender, socioeconomic status, menopausal status and geographic location within the MENA region, and sample size.

Health and medical history

Information on the medical history of participants related to osteoporosis, past treatments and comorbidities from study records, for non-HCPs.

Knowledge regarding osteoporosis and its management

Evaluates understanding of essential aspects of osteoporosis, including fracture risk assessment, diagnostic methods, prevention and comprehensive management strategies such as bone-active agents (eg, bisphosphonates, calcitonin, teriparatide, denosumab, romosozumab and hormone replacement therapy). These parameters will be collected through various tools such as standard knowledge assessments, surveys or interviews, with an emphasis on standardised or validated instruments to capture detailed knowledge trends at multiple time points.

Examples of validated tools, but not limited to: (1) Beliefs about Medications Questionnaire;35 (2) Osteoporosis Knowledge assessment tool;36 and (3) Osteoporosis Health Belief Scale.37

Attitude (preference) towards management strategies

Examines attitudes and preferences related to various aspects of osteoporosis diagnosis and management, and choice of bone-active agents. Considerations include the selection of specific regimens based on the mode of administration (oral, intravenous, subcutaneous or nasal) and dosing frequency (daily, weekly and monthly, etc). These parameters will be collected through attitudinal scales, preference mapping and qualitative insights from interviews and focus groups.

Examples of validated tools, but not limited to: (1) Quantitative study Discrete Choice Experiment survey: efficacy, side effect, mode, frequency and cost to patient;38 (2) Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire for Medications;39 and (3) Preference and Satisfaction Questionnaire.40

Practices (behaviours) in managing osteoporosis

Assesses behaviours such as use of screening tools (BMD measurements and the FRAX tool), medication adherence and self-prescription. These parameters will be collected through detailed tracking of adherence rates and behavioural engagement levels, providing insights into effectiveness and compliance.

Examples of validated tools, but not limited to: (1) Medications Possession Ratio;41 (2) Patient Reported Outcome Measures;42; and (3) Proportion of Days Covered.43

We will collect detailed information on the characteristics of the tools used, including their validation status, whether they are self-administered or interviewer-led, and the dimensions they assess.

Other variables

Intervention characteristics

When applicable, data on intervention specifics such as types, dosages and administration methods for bone-active agents, supplementing with expert consultations if data is incomplete.

Funding sources

Financial backing of each study to analyse potential biases, with a clear record of funding sources and their possible influence on study findings.

Study risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers will assess the methodological quality of the studies included in the review using appropriate evaluation tools tailored to each study design.4448 They will conduct quality assessments independently to ensure accuracy and reliability. For qualitative studies, we will implement the Critical Appraisal Skills Program appraisal checklist to ensure thorough quality assessment.44 45 To evaluate the mixed methods studies quality, we will use LEGEND Evidence Evaluation Tools, Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool46 and the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for randomised controlled trials.47 Finally, we will use the McMaster Risk of Bias Instrument for Cross-Sectional Surveys of Attitudes and Practices.48

Synthesis methods

We will synthesise the data thematically and present it in a narrative format. Two independent reviewers will follow the data abstraction items, focusing on consistency and relevance. Reviewers will reach consensus through discussion, and a third reviewer will be consulted in cases of disagreement.

Data preparation We will prepare the data to ensure consistency and relevance for narrative synthesis. If missing summary statistics are encountered, we will reach out to study authors to obtain the necessary data. Where feasible, we will address incomplete datasets to enhance the comprehensiveness of the analysis.To standardise measurement units across studies, we will focus on harmonising outcomes like daily dosages of bone-active agents or other relevant measures. If needed, we will perform data conversions to ensure that outcomes align with common scales or units used in managing osteoporosis treatments.

Planned timeline

Searches covered studies published up to January 2025, and screening was initiated in June 2025. Title/abstract and full-text screening were completed by September 2025. Data extraction, risk of bias assessment and synthesis are planned to begin in October 2025, with completion of the review and manuscript preparation anticipated by June 2026.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this protocol, as it is a review of published literature. Findings will inform clinical practice and public health strategies aimed at patients and communities in the MENA region.

Ethics and dissemination

This review synthesises data from publicly available sources and involves no interaction with human participants or identifiable data; accordingly, ethics approval is not required. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and deposition of study materials on the Open Science Framework.

Tree-level soft emission for two pairs of quarks
Xinguang Chen, and Zhengwen Liu

Abstract
We compute the tree-level current for the emission of two soft quark-antiquark pairs in a hard scattering. We also compute the square of this current and discuss the resulting color correlations, featuring dipole correlations and three-parton correlations. This object is essential for analyzing the infrared singularities at next-to-next-to-next-to-next-to-leading-order (N 4 LO) predictions in perturbative QCD.

Introduction

In gauge theory such as quantum chromodynamics (QCD), on-shell scattering amplitudes exhibit a universal factorization behavior in specific kinematic regimes, such as the soft and collinear limits. Notably, when one or more massless partons become
soft
, the full scattering amplitude factorizes into a product of a process-independent soft factor, represented by an operator acting on the color space, and a lower-point scattering amplitude with the soft particles removed
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Explicit analytic expressions for this universal factorization are vital for constructing so-called subtraction schemes, which play a crucial role in managing and canceling infrared singularities in cross sections, see e.g.
[
9
,
10
]
. Moreover, the soft factorization of scattering amplitudes, often referred to as soft theorems in literature, sometimes can be useful to reveal new mathematical structures of S-matrices, shedding light on the hidden symmetries underlying quantum field theory. Therefore, a refined understanding of the soft factorization of scattering amplitudes is of fundamental importance for performing precision computations of physical observables as well as for exploring novel structures in quantum field theory.

Motivated by its significance in both phenomenological and theoretical studies, the soft factorization of gauge theory amplitudes has been extensively investigated. For the emission of a single soft gluon, the explicit factorization formulas for both unsquared and squared amplitudes have been established at tree
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
11
,
12
]
, one-loop
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
and two-loop
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
levels in the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}
in perturbative QCD. For the emission of double soft partons (tow gluons or a quark-antiquark pair), the soft currents have been known at tree
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
and one-loop
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
levels. Recently, the tree-level current for the emission of triple soft partons, including three gluons (
ggg
) and a soft quark-antiquark pair plus a soft gluon (
q\bar{q}g
), has also be worked out
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
, see also
[
28
]
for a recent review. Among these analytic results, the tree-level double soft parton currents and the tree-level and one-loop single gluon soft currents have been essential ingredients in building subtraction schemes at next-to-leading order (NLO) and next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO). The other higher-order results, in turn, are pivotal for next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (N
3
LO) computations in QCD.

Beyond N
3
LO, the single soft gluon current has recently been computed to three-loop level in QCD and maximally supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory
[
29
,
30
]
. The present work aims to compute the emission of four soft partons at the tree level. We concentrate on in this work the current for the soft emission of two quark-antiquark pairs. They will be essential for understanding the infrared singularities at next-to-next-to-next-to-next-to-leading-order (N
4
LO) QCD computations.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In section
2
, we briefly review the eikonal approximation formalism that is our main computational method, along with a concise introduction to the color space representation of scattering amplitudes to clarify concepts and streamline notation. We then present the primary results of this paper: the tree-level current for the emission of two soft quark-antiquark pairs in section
3
. We construct the current using the eikonal formalism, and then compute its square and analyze resulting color correlations. We conclude with a summary in section
4
.

2
The eikonal approximation

In this section, we present a brief review of the eikonal approximation formalism of the tree-level current for the emission of soft partons.

The color space formalism

In order to keep notations compact, we find it useful to employ the colour-space formalism
[
31
,
9
]
, in which scattering amplitudes can be represented as a vector that can be expanded into an orthonormal basis in color
\otimes
spin space

\displaystyle\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})}=\Ket{c_{1}\ldots c_{n}}\otimes\Ket{\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}\mathcal{M}^{c_{1}\ldots c_{n};\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\,,

(1)

where
|c_{1}\ldots c_{n}\rangle
and
|\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}\rangle
are an orthonormal basis for color and spin respectively, and the coefficient

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{c_{1}\ldots c_{n};\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}):=\big(\Bra{c_{1}\ldots c_{n}}\otimes\Bra{\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}\big)\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})}

(2)

is the amplitude of
n
particles carrying momenta
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
, flavors
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
, colors
\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\}
and spin indices
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
.
In this work we define the momenta as well as spins (helicities) by assuming the external particles (at least partons) are outgoing for convenience.
Below, unless necessary, we will omit the dependence on momenta for simplicity.
The squared matrix element (summed over spin and colour indices of the external particles) can be written as

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}\right|^{2}\equiv\langle\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\\
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\end{subarray}}\big(\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}^{c_{1}\ldots c_{n};\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}\big)^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}^{c_{1}\ldots c_{n};\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}\,.

(3)

In this formalism, the color charge carried by a parton can be represented as an abstract operator that acts on the color space.
These color charge operators obey the
\mathrm{su}(N_{c})
color algebra

\displaystyle[\mathbf{T}_{j}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b}]=if^{abc}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{c}\,\delta_{jl},

(4)

where
f^{abc}
with
(1\leq a,b,c\leq N_{c}^{2}{-}1)
is the structure constant of the color algebra.
For the parton
i
one has

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{i}^{a}|c_{1}\cdots c_{n}\rangle=|c_{1}\cdots c^{\prime}_{i}\cdots c_{n}\rangle\,{\bf T}_{c^{\prime}_{i}c_{i}}^{a}\,,

(5)

where
{\bf T}^{a}_{c^{\prime}_{i}c_{i}}=if^{c^{\prime}_{i}ac_{i}}
(the adjoint representation of the color algebra) if the parton
i
is a gluon,
{\bf T}^{a}_{c^{\prime}_{i}c_{i}}=t^{a}_{c^{\prime}_{i}c_{i}}
or
{\bf T}^{a}_{c^{\prime}_{i}c_{i}}=-t^{a}_{c_{i}c^{\prime}_{i}}
(
1\leq c_{i},c_{i}^{\prime}\leq N_{c}
, fundamental or anti-fundamental representation) is a quark or anti-quark.
In this way, partons in various different representations of the color gauge group can be represented in a unified way.
It is direct to verify that
C_{i}=\mathbf{T}_{i}^{2}\equiv\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}
is a Casimir operator:
C_{i}=C_{A}
if the parton
i
is a gluon and
C_{i}=C_{F}
if the parton
i
is a quark, where

\displaystyle C_{A}=N_{c},\quad C_{F}=T_{F}{N_{c}^{2}-1\over N_{c}},\quad\text{with}~~\tr(t^{a}t^{b})=T_{F}\delta^{ab}={1\over 2}\delta^{ab}.

(6)

By definition (
1
), any amplitude vector
\Ket{\mathcal{M}}
is a color-singlet, the color conservation can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\Ket{\mathcal{M}}=0\quad\text{or simply}\quad\sum\nolimits_{i}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}=0.

(7)

The soft current

An on-shell scattering amplitude may exhibit a universal factorization form in the limit where one or more massless external particles become
soft
, i.e., their energies approach zero.
To be more precise, considering the scattering of
n

hard
particles with momenta
p_{i}
and
m

soft
particles with momenta
q_{i}
, the leading singular behavior of the scattering amplitude at the tree level is captured by the following factorization formula:

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{q_{1}\cdots q_{m}}

\displaystyle\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{n+m}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})}

\displaystyle\quad=(\mu^{\epsilon}g_{s})^{m}\,\mathbf{J}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m};p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{n}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})},

(8)

where the symbol
\mathscr{S}_{q_{1}\cdots q_{m}}
signifies retaining only the leading singular terms in the soft limit,
q_{i}\to 0
. In formula (
8
),
g_{s}
is the strong coupling constant,
\mu
is the scale introduced by dimensional regularization, and the analysis is performed within the Conventional Dimensional Regularization (CDR) scheme in
D=4-2\epsilon
dimensions in this work. The scattering amplitude
\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{n}}
on the right-hand side is obtained by simply excluding the
m
soft particles from the full amplitude
\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{n+m}}
on the left-hand side. The function
\mathbf{J}
, dependent on both soft and hard particles, characterizes the leading divergent behavior of the amplitude in the soft limit.

The eikonal approximation formalism offers a systematic method for constructing soft currents.
The most intuitive and constructive idea is to study the emission of a single soft gluon at tree level.
A key observation is that the leading contribution arises only from diagrams where the soft gluon is emitted from an external hard parton line,

Like the soft-gluon case, the full current for the emission of a massless quark pair can be obtained by summing over external hard lines
[

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{q\bar{q}}^{j\,\bar{\imath}}(q_{1},q_{2})=\sum_{i}{-1\over q_{12}^{2}}\,\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\,t^{a}_{j\bar{\imath}}={-1\over q_{12}^{2}}\mathbf{J}^{a;\mu}_{g}(q_{12})\,\gamma_{\mu}\,t_{j\bar{\imath}}^{a},

(13)

or in the vector form in color-spin space

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{q\bar{q}}(q_{1},q_{2})=\bra{j\,\bar{\imath}}\otimes\bra{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\,\bar{u}_{\lambda}(q_{1})\,\mathbf{J}_{q\bar{q}}^{j\,\bar{\imath}}(q_{1},q_{2})\,v_{\lambda^{\prime}}(q_{2})\,

(14)

The last equality in (

In the soft limit, the factorization property of scattering amplitudes naturally induces factorization behavior of the squared matrix elements.
Simply using formula (
3
) and the current (
11
), one can immediately find

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{q}\,\big|\mathcal{M}_{gf_{1}\cdots f_{n}}\big|^{2}=-(\mu^{\epsilon}g_{s})^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{S}_{ij}(q)\,\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ij)}\big|^{2},

(15)

with

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ij)}\big|^{2}\equiv\Bra{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}}\mathbf{T}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{j}\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}}.

(16)

Below we explain the notations appearing in the above formula.
The well-known eikonal function is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}(q)=-d_{\mu\nu}(q,r)\,\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mu}(q)\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\nu}(q)\simeq{p_{i}\cdot p_{j}\over(q\cdot p_{i})(q\cdot p_{j})}\,,

(17)

with
d_{\mu\nu}
the tensor from the completeness relation of polarizations, i.e.,

\displaystyle d_{\mu\nu}(q,r)=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{D-2}\epsilon^{\lambda}_{\mu}(q,r)\big[\epsilon^{\lambda}_{\nu}(q,r)\big]^{\ast}=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{q_{\mu}r_{\nu}+r_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q\cdot r}\,.

(18)

where the summation is carried over
D{-}2
physical degrees of freedom in the axial gauge.
It is also noteworthy that one dropped the terms dependent on reference vector
r
since they do not contribute the squared matrix element in (
15
) due the colour conservation illustrated in (
7
).
The color operator is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{j}=\mathbf{T}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{i}=\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{a}

(19)

which generates a
dipole
-type color correlation in the squared amplitude (
16
).
According to formulas (
17
) and (
19
), an obvious yet important property is that the amplitude is symmetric under the exchange of
i
and
j
, referred to as the dipole symmetry.

Now let us turn to consider the square of the current of soft quark-antiquark pair in (
13
) or (
14
).
Directly from (
14
), one obtains

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{q_{1}q_{2}}\big|\mathcal{M}_{q\bar{q}f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}\big|^{2}=(\mu^{2\epsilon}g_{s}^{2})^{2}\,T_{F}\,\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{I}_{ij}(q_{1},\!q_{2})\,\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\ldots f_{n}}^{(ij)}\big|^{2}\,,

(20)

where
T_{F}=\frac{1}{2}
is explained in (
6
), and the kinematic function is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle={1\over q_{1}\!\cdot\!q_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{12})\,d_{\mu\nu}(q_{1},q_{2})

(21)

\displaystyle={(p_{i}\cdot q_{1})(p_{j}\cdot q_{2})+(p_{i}\cdot q_{2})(p_{j}\cdot q_{1})-(q_{1}\cdot q_{2})(p_{i}\cdot p_{j})\over(q_{1}\cdot q_{2})^{2}(p_{i}\cdot q_{12})(p_{j}\cdot q_{12})}.

(22)

Let us make some essential observations at this point.
First, like the single gluon case, the squared current for the soft-quark emission in (
20
) also owns the dipole symmetry under exchange of the emitters
(i\leftrightarrow j)
. Additionally, the kinematic function
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
given in (
21
) features charge conjugation symmetry under the exchange of the quark and antiquark labels

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}(q_{1},q_{2})=\mathcal{I}_{ij}(q_{2},q_{1})\,.

(23)

To summarize, we briefly review the eikonal formalism of tree-level currents for multiple soft parton emission from a hard QCD scattering.
We also show two most representative examples, a single-soft gluon and a double soft quark-antiquark pair, to illustrate how the formalism works for constructing currents in detail.

3
The tree-level current for two pairs of soft quarks

We present the core result of this study in this section. We first construct the tree-level current for the emission of two soft quark-antiquark pairs following the eikonal formalism outlined above. We then proceed to compute the squared current and analyze the resulting color correlations.

3.1
The soft current

Without loss of generality, we assume that the two quark-antiquark pairs, denoted as
q\bar{q}Q\bar{Q}
, carry momenta
\{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4}\}
and color indices
\{i,\bar{\imath},j,\bar{\jmath}\}
, as well as helicities
\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}\}
.
Recalling that a soft quark-antiquark pair can be produced from an intermediate soft gluon.
According to the eikonal Feynman rules, we need to consider the four types of diagrams displayed in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Diagrams contributing to the tree-level current for the emission of two soft quark-antiquark pairs. Red and green lines represent the soft pairs
q\bar{q}
and
Q\bar{Q}
, respectively. For the contributions from the first and third diagrams, we should also include the contributions from the exchange of two soft-quark pairs.

Let us initiate our analysis with the fourth diagram in Figure
1
, which yields

\displaystyle{\big[\bar{u}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}\,v_{2}\big]\big[\bar{u}_{3}\gamma_{\nu}\,v_{4}\big]t^{a}_{i\bar{\imath}}t^{b}_{j\bar{\jmath}}\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}\sum_{k\neq l}{\cal S}_{k}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,{\cal S}_{l}^{\nu}(q_{34})\,\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a}\,\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b},

(24)

where, for notational compactness, we suppress the momentum and spin-index dependence on the spinors:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{1}\equiv\bar{u}_{\lambda_{1}}(q_{1}),\quad v_{2}\equiv v_{\lambda_{2}}(q_{2}),\quad\bar{u}_{3}\equiv\bar{u}_{\lambda_{3}}(q_{3}),\quad v_{4}\equiv v_{\lambda_{4}}(q_{4}).

(25)

Next, the first diagram in Figure
1
contributes as follows:

\displaystyle{\big[\bar{u}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}v_{2}\big]\big[\bar{u}_{3}\gamma_{\nu}v_{4}\big]t^{a}_{i\bar{\imath}}t^{b}_{j\bar{\jmath}}\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{k}^{\mu}(q_{1234})\Big[{\cal S}_{k}^{\nu}(q_{12})\,\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{b}+{\cal S}_{k}^{\nu}(q_{34})\,\mathbf{T}_{k}^{b}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a}\Big]

(26)

It is useful to symmetrize the colour charges therough the following identity

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{b}\,=\,{1\over 2}\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{i}^{b}\}+{1\over 2}[\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{i}^{b}]\,=\,{1\over 2}\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{i}^{b}\}+{i\over 2}f^{abc}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{c}.

(27)

This allows us to decompose (
26
) into symmetric part and a anti-symmetric part in two color charges

\displaystyle{\big[\bar{u}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}v_{2}\big]\big[\bar{u}_{3}\gamma_{\nu}v_{4}\big]t^{a}_{i\bar{\imath}}t^{b}_{j\bar{\jmath}}\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigg({1\over 2}\{\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{k}^{b}\}\,{\cal S}_{k}^{\mu}(q_{12}){\cal S}_{k}^{\nu}(q_{34})+{i\over 2}f^{abc}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{c}{\cal S}_{k}^{\mu}(q_{1234})\big[{\cal S}_{k}^{\nu}(q_{12})-{\cal S}_{k}^{\nu}(q_{34})\big]\bigg).

(28)

Combining the symmetric part in (
28
) with (
24
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}={\big[\bar{u}(q_{1})\gamma_{\mu}\,v(q_{2})\big]\big[\bar{u}(q_{3})\gamma_{\nu}\,v(q_{4})\big]\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}\times{1\over 2}\big\{\mathbf{J}_{g}^{a;\mu}(q_{12}),\,\mathbf{J}_{g}^{b;\nu}(q_{34})\big\}t^{a}_{i\bar{\imath}}t^{b}_{j\bar{\jmath}}\,,

(29)

while combining the anti-symmetric part in (
28
) and the contributions from the second diagram in Figure
1
gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}=\,

\displaystyle{\big[\bar{u}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}v_{2}\big]\big[\bar{u}_{3}\gamma_{\nu}v_{4}\big]t^{a}_{i\bar{\imath}}t^{b}_{j\bar{\jmath}}\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}

\displaystyle~\times\sum_{i=1}^{n}if^{abc}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{c}\,{\cal S}_{i}^{\alpha}(q_{1234})\bigg({1\over 2}\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\big[{\cal S}_{i}^{\nu}(q_{12})-{\cal S}_{i}^{\nu}(q_{34})\big]-{g_{\alpha\sigma}\over q_{1234}^{2}}V^{\mu\nu\sigma}(q_{12},q_{34})\bigg),

(30)

with

\displaystyle V^{\mu\nu\alpha}(q_{12},q_{34})\equiv g^{\mu\nu}(q_{12}{-}q_{34})^{\alpha}+g^{\nu\alpha}(q_{12}{+}2q_{34})^{\mu}-g^{\mu\alpha}(q_{34}{+}2q_{12})^{\nu}.

(31)

Lastly, the contribution from the third diagram in Figure
1
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{2}=\,

\displaystyle{[\bar{u}_{3}\gamma^{\nu}v_{4}]t^{b}_{j\bar{\jmath}}\over q_{1234}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a}{\cal S}_{k}^{\mu}(q_{1234})\,\bar{u}_{1}\bigg[{(t^{b}t^{a})_{i\bar{\imath}}\over q_{134}^{2}}\gamma_{\nu}\not{q}_{134}\gamma_{\mu}-{(t^{a}t^{b})_{i\bar{\imath}}\over q_{234}^{2}}\gamma_{\mu}\not{q}_{234}\gamma_{\nu}\bigg]v_{2}

\displaystyle+(q\bar{q}\leftrightarrow Q\bar{Q}),

(32)

where the notation
(q\bar{q}\leftrightarrow Q\bar{Q})
represents a operation where we exchange all quantum numbers for the two soft pairs, including spins, color indices and momenta.

Collecting all contributions, the complete tree-level current for the emission of two soft quark pairs is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{q\bar{q}Q\bar{Q}}^{i\bar{\imath}j\bar{\jmath};\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{4}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})=\mathbf{A}_{1}+\mathbf{A}_{2}+\mathbf{N},

(33)

or in the vector form in color-spin space

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{q\bar{q}Q\bar{Q}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})=\bra{i\,\bar{\imath}\,j\,\bar{\jmath}}\otimes\bra{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}}\mathbf{J}_{q\bar{q}Q\bar{Q}}^{i\bar{\imath}j\bar{\jmath};\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{4}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})\,.

(34)

Note that we have omitted the dependence on helicity indices in
\mathbf{A}_{i}
and
\mathbf{N}
, as indicated in (
25
).

Now, we highlight several key properties of this current. First, the term
\mathbf{A}_{1}
owns a factorization structure in which it factorizes into a (off-shell) double soft gluon current contracted with the two quark-antiquark currents. Second, as expected, in addition to
\mathbf{A}_{2}
in (
33
), the full current is also symmetric under the exchange of two quark pairs, i.e.
q\bar{q}\leftrightarrow Q\bar{Q}
. Finally, term
\mathbf{N}
in (
30
) is pure non-Abelian, whereas the other two terms can be reduced to the corresponding current in Abelian quantum electrodynamics (QED) by simply treating color charges as electrical charges and setting the fundamental representation generators to unity.

3.2
The squared current

Next, we compute the square of the current obtained previously.
The full result for the squared current has a similar color-correlation structure with the soft emission of a quark pair in association with a gluon discussed in
[
26
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{q_{1}\cdots q_{4}}\big|\mathcal{M}_{q\bar{q}Q\bar{Q}f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}\big|^{2}=\big(

\displaystyle\mu^{2\epsilon}g_{s}^{2}\big)^{4}\Bigg[{T_{F}^{2}\over 2}\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ij;kl)}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+T_{F}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\mathcal{Q}_{ijk}\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ijk)}\big|^{2}+T_{F}^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{Q}_{ij}\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ij)}\big|^{2}\Bigg],

(35)

where the dipole-correlated squared amplitude is defined in (
16
) while the symmetrized doule-dipole-correlated and the triple-color correlated squared amplitudes are defined as
[
26
]

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ij;kl)}\big|^{2}\equiv\Bra{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}}\{\mathbf{T}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{j},\mathbf{T}_{k}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{l}\}\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}}

(36)

and

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}^{(ijk)}\big|^{2}\equiv d^{abc}\Bra{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\mathbf{T}_{k}^{c}\Ket{\mathcal{M}_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}}

(37)

with the symmetric structure constant

\displaystyle d^{abc}\equiv 2\operatorname{Tr}\big[\{t^{a},t^{b}\}t^{c}\big].

(38)

We now proceed to elaborate on the derivation details on (
35
) and provide the explicit expressions for various kinematical coefficients.
First, the derivation for the square of
\mathbf{A}_{1}
is quite similar with the derivation for the squared current of double-soft gluon emission in
[
21
]

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{A}_{1}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle={T_{F}^{2}\over 4}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\big\}\big\{\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b}\big\}\,\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}

\displaystyle={T_{F}^{2}\over 16}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\Big(\big\{\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\big\},\big\{\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b}\big\}\big\}+\big\{\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\big\},\big\{\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b}\big\}\big\}\Big)\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl},

(39)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})=\mathcal{I}_{ik}(q_{1},q_{2})\,\mathcal{I}_{jl}(q_{3},q_{4}),

(40)

where the eikonal function
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
is defined in (
21
) and we used the symmetry
\mathcal{I}_{ij}=\mathcal{I}_{ji}
.
Then noticing Czakon’s identity
[
21
]

\displaystyle\big\{\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\},\{\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b}\}\big\}+\big\{\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{l}^{b}\},\{\mathbf{T}_{k}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\}\big\}

\displaystyle=8\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{k},\,\mathbf{T}_{j}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{l}\big\}+2C_{A}\Big[

\displaystyle 3\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}\,(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})+3\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\,(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{k})-2\delta_{ij}\delta_{jk}\,(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{l})

\displaystyle-2\delta_{ij}\delta_{jl}\,(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{k})-2(\delta_{ik}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{jk}\delta_{kl})(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})\Big],

(41)

we can immediately reduce the expression in (
39
) into a double-dipole part and a dipole part.
Precisely,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{A}_{1}\big|^{2}=~

\displaystyle{T_{F}^{2}\over 2}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{k},\,\mathbf{T}_{j}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{l}\big\}\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}

\displaystyle+{C_{A}T_{F}^{2}\over 4}\sum_{i,j}\big(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j}\big)\big(3\mathcal{Q}_{iijj}-3\mathcal{Q}_{iiij}-\mathcal{Q}_{ijjj}\big).

(42)

Next we consider the product between
\mathbf{A}_{1}
and
\mathbf{N}

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{N}+\mathbf{N}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=T_{F}^{2}\sum_{j,k,l}{-i\over 2}f^{abc}\big[\big\{\mathbf{T}_{j}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{k}^{b}\big\},\mathbf{T}_{l}^{c}\big]\,\mathcal{I}_{jkl}

\displaystyle=T_{F}^{2}C_{A}\sum_{i,j}\big(\mathcal{I}_{ijj}-\mathcal{I}_{iji}\big)\,(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j}),

(43)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ijl}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})\equiv\,

\displaystyle{4\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}d_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}(q_{1},q_{2})d_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}(q_{3},q_{4}){\cal S}_{i}^{\mu_{1}}(q_{12})\,{\cal S}_{j}^{\nu_{1}}(q_{34})

(44)

\displaystyle\times{\cal S}_{l}^{\alpha}(q_{1234})\bigg({1\over 2}\delta^{\mu_{2}}_{\alpha}\big[{\cal S}_{l}^{\nu_{2}}(q_{12})-{\cal S}_{l}^{\nu_{2}}(q_{34})\big]-{g_{\alpha\sigma}\over q_{1234}^{2}}V^{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}\sigma}(q_{12},q_{34})\bigg).

To get (
43
) we used the identity
[
21
]

\displaystyle if^{abc}\big[\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\},\mathbf{T}_{k}^{c}\big]=2C_{A}(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})(\delta_{ik}-\delta_{jk}).

(45)

We see that this term is fully reduced to dipole correlation.

A simple observation is that, in the product between
\mathbf{A}_{1}
and
\mathbf{A}_{2}
, we will see the trace of three fundamental representation generators
\tr(t^{a}t^{b}t^{c})
, for which we decompose it into a full symmetric part and a full anti-symmetric part, i.e.,

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\big(t^{a}t^{b}t^{c}\big)=\frac{1}{4}d^{abc}+\frac{i}{2}\,T_{F}\,f^{abc}.

(46)

Then the product of
\mathbf{A}_{1}
and
\mathbf{A}_{2}
is finally given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}_{2}+\mathbf{A}_{2}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=T_{F}\sum_{i,j,l}\bigg[{i\over 2}f^{abc}T_{F}\big[\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\big\},\,\mathbf{T}_{l}^{c}\big]\mathcal{K}_{ijl}+{1\over 4}d^{abc}\big\{\big\{\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a},\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\big\},\,\mathbf{T}_{l}^{c}\big\}\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}\bigg]

\displaystyle=C_{A}T_{F}^{2}\sum_{i,j}(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})(\mathcal{K}_{iji}-\mathcal{K}_{ijj})+T_{F}\sum_{i,j,l}\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}\,d^{abc}\mathbf{T}_{i}^{a}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{b}\mathbf{T}_{l}^{c},

(47)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{ijl}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{ijl}+\mathcal{F}_{ijl}-\mathcal{G}_{ijl}-\mathcal{H}_{ijl},\qquad\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}=\mathcal{E}_{ijl}-\mathcal{F}_{ijl}+\mathcal{G}_{ijl}-\mathcal{H}_{ijl},

(48)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{ijl}

\displaystyle\equiv{\tr(\gamma_{\mu}\not{q}_{1}\gamma^{\sigma}\not{q}_{134}\gamma_{\alpha}\not{q}_{2})\,d_{\nu\sigma}(q_{3},q_{4})\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}q_{1234}^{2}q_{134}^{2}}{\cal S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,{\cal S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{34}){\cal S}_{l}^{\alpha}(q_{1234})\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ijl}

\displaystyle\equiv{\tr(\gamma_{\mu}\not{q}_{1}\gamma_{\alpha}\not{q}_{234}\gamma^{\sigma}\not{q}_{2})\,d_{\nu\sigma}(q_{3},q_{4})\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}q_{1234}^{2}q_{234}^{2}}{\cal S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,{\cal S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{34}){\cal S}_{l}^{\alpha}(q_{1234})\,,

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{ijl}

\displaystyle\equiv{\tr(\gamma_{\nu}\not{q}_{3}\gamma^{\sigma}\not{q}_{123}\gamma_{\alpha}\not{q}_{4})\,d_{\mu\sigma}(q_{1},q_{2})\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}q_{1234}^{2}q_{123}^{2}}{\cal S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,{\cal S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{34}){\cal S}_{l}^{\alpha}(q_{1234})\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ijl}

\displaystyle\equiv{\tr(\gamma_{\nu}\not{q}_{3}\gamma_{\alpha}\not{q}_{124}\gamma^{\sigma}\not{q}_{4})\,d_{\mu\sigma}(q_{1},q_{2})\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}q_{1234}^{2}q_{124}^{2}}{\cal S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\,{\cal S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{34}){\cal S}_{l}^{\alpha}(q_{1234})\,.

(52)

As expected, in (
47
) the term linear in
f^{abc}
can ultimately be simplified to a dipole correlation using Czakon’s identity (
45
), while the
d^{abc}
part yields a triple-parton color correlation, similar to the emission of a soft quark-antiquark pair in association with a gluon.

Lastly, we turn our attention to
|\mathbf{A}_{2}+\mathbf{N}|^{2}
, which, as expected, contributes only to the dipole correlation.
More specifically, we find

\displaystyle|\mathbf{N}|^{2}

\displaystyle\sim f^{abc}f^{abe}\,\mathbf{T}_{i}^{c}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{e}=C_{A}(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})\,,

(53)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\sim f^{abc}\tr(t^{a}t^{b}t^{e})\,\mathbf{T}_{i}^{c}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{e}={i\over 2}T_{F}C_{A}(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})\,,

(54)

and in
|\mathbf{A}_{2}|^{2}
there are the following three types of terms

\displaystyle\tr(t^{a}t^{a}t^{b}t^{c})\,\mathbf{T}_{i}^{b}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{c}

\displaystyle=T_{F}C_{F}(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j}),

(55)

\displaystyle\tr(t^{a}t^{b}t^{a}t^{c})\,\mathbf{T}_{i}^{b}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{c}

\displaystyle=T_{F}\big(C_{F}-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{A}\big)(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j}),

(56)

\displaystyle\tr(t^{a}t^{b}t^{c})\,\tr(t^{a}t^{b}t^{e})\mathbf{T}_{i}^{c}\mathbf{T}_{j}^{e}

\displaystyle=T_{F}^{2}\big(2C_{F}-\tfrac{3}{4}C_{A}-\tfrac{1}{4}C_{A}\big)(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j}).

(57)

With these identities, the remaining steps in the derivation involve primarily extensive but straightforward Lorentz contraction algebra. While we omit further derivation details, the final result takes the following form:

\displaystyle|\mathbf{A}_{2}+\mathbf{N}|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}T_{F}^{2}\Big[\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\text{(ab)}}C_{A}+\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\text{(nab)}}C_{F}\Big](\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j}).

(58)

The explicit expressions for
\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\text{(ab)}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\text{(nab)}}
are lengthy, and we include them in an computer-readable ancillary file.

We now compile all previously-derived terms to provide full analytic expressions for the three kinematical functions in the squared amplitude (
35
). The functions
\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}
are given by equations (
40
) and (

) while the kinematical coefficient
\mathcal{Q}_{ij}
receives the contributions from (
47
), (
43
), (
42
), (

Their combination yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{ij}=C_{F}\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\text{(nab)}}+C_{A}\Big[\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\text{(ab)}}

\displaystyle+{1\over 4}(3\mathcal{Q}_{iijj}-3\mathcal{Q}_{iiij}-\mathcal{Q}_{ijjj})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{I}_{ijj}-\mathcal{I}_{iji}+\mathcal{K}_{iji}-\mathcal{K}_{ijj}\Big].

(59)

A few observations about the squared current are in order.
First,
\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}
in (
40
) is manifestly symmetric under the exchange of indices
(i\leftrightarrow k)
or
(j\leftrightarrow l)
as well as under the exchange of momenta
(q_{1}\leftrightarrow q_{2})
or
(q_{3}\leftrightarrow q_{4})

\mathcal{I}_{ik}

\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}
defined in (

\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}
is independent of the masses of the hard particles. Similarly, although the dipole function

in (

(\mathbf{T}_{i}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{T}_{j})

in
\mathcal{Q}_{ij}
linearly depends on
m_{i}^{2}
and
m_{j}^{2}

3.3
Strongly-ordered soft limit

We now consider a strongly-ordered soft limit, where one pair of quarks is significantly softer than the other. Concretely, we analyze the sub kinematical regime in which the momenta of one pair are considerably smaller than those of the other pair. Without loss of generality, we examine the soft current in the following limit:

\displaystyle E_{1},E_{2}\gg E_{3},E_{4}.

(60)

In this limit, we find that the double-dipole function remains exact:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{ijkl}=\mathcal{I}_{ik}(q_{1},q_{2})\,\mathcal{I}_{jl}(q_{3},q_{4}),

while the other functions,
\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{ij}
, are significantly simplified

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{ijl}\rightarrow{2d_{\mu\nu}(q_{1},q_{2})\,d_{\alpha\sigma}(q_{3},q_{4})\,{\cal S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12}){\cal S}_{l}^{\nu}(q_{12}){\cal S}_{j}^{\alpha}(q_{34})\over q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}\bigg[{q_{1}^{\sigma}\over q_{1}\!\cdot\!q_{34}}-{q_{2}^{\sigma}\over q_{2}\!\cdot\!q_{34}}\bigg],

(61)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{ij}\rightarrow{d_{\mu\nu}(q_{1},q_{2})\,d_{\alpha\sigma}(q_{3},q_{4})\,\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mu}(q_{12})\over 2q_{12}^{2}q_{34}^{2}}\Bigg\{C_{A}\bigg[\frac{4\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{12})\,q_{1}^{\alpha}q_{2}^{\sigma}}{(q_{1}\!\cdot\!q_{34})(q_{2}\!\cdot\!q_{34})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\Big(\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\nu}(q_{12})\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\alpha}(q_{34})-[\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\alpha}(q_{34})+\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\alpha}(q_{34})]\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{12})\Big)\bigg(\frac{q_{1}^{\sigma}}{q_{1}\!\cdot\!q_{34}}+\frac{q_{2}^{\sigma}}{q_{2}\!\cdot\!q_{34}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\sigma}(q_{34})\Big(4\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\alpha}(q_{34})S_{j}^{\nu}(q_{12})-\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\alpha}(q_{34})\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\nu}(q_{12})-3\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\alpha}(q_{34})\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{12})\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+4C_{F}\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\nu}(q_{12})\bigg[\frac{q_{1}^{\alpha}q_{1}^{\sigma}}{(q_{1}\!\cdot\!q_{34})^{2}}-\frac{2q_{1}^{\alpha}q_{2}^{\sigma}}{(q_{1}\!\cdot\!q_{34})(q_{2}\!\cdot\!q_{34})}+\frac{q_{2}^{\alpha}q_{2}^{\sigma}}{(q_{2}\!\cdot\!q_{34})^{2}}\bigg]\Bigg\}+(i\leftrightarrow j).

(62)

Note that while the expression for
Q_{ijl}
is not explicitly symmetrized in hard-parton indices, its symmetrization can be straightforwardly achieved.
Unlike the triple soft

current in similar limits discussed in
[

, the new feature here is the

4
Conclusions

In this work we have initiated the study of four soft parton emissions. We derived the tree-level current for the radiation of two soft quark-antiquark pairs,
q\bar{q}Q\bar{Q}
, using the eikonal formalism. We also explicitly worked out the square of this current and analyzed the resulting color correlations, which can be categorized into three distinct types: double dipole, dipole, and tripole correlations, similar to those observed in the emission of a soft quark-antiquark pair plus a soft gluon
[
26
]
. Our result is a crucial ingredient for investigating infrared singularities at N
4
LO in perturbative QCD.

Further investigation into soft factorizations relevant to the N
4
LO accuracy in QCD computations would be an interesting direction. This involves computing tree-level soft currents for
ggq\bar{q}
and
gggg
emissions, one-loop triple and two-loop double soft emissions, as well as splitting amplitudes for the emission of up to five collinear partons, such as
[
32
]
. While we leave these tasks for future research, we provide some preliminary observations here. First, it is reasonable to expect that the tree-level soft
ggq\bar{q}
current may share structural similarities with the triple-soft gluon current derived in
[
25
]
, as a soft quark-antiquark pair can behave analogously to a soft (off-shell) gluon. For the emission of four soft gluons, we can relatively easily construct the tree-level current using in a Berends-Giele-like recursive algorithm. We also find a systematic method to manage color chargers and write the result in an organized form. However, simplifying the squared currents remains challenging. In particular, the algebraic manipulation of color charges in squared currents can be ‘
quite cumbersome
’, as described by Catani and collaborators
[
19
,
25
]
. Therefore, developing an efficient algorithm for reducing color charges in squared currents into a set of irreducible correlations would represent a significant advancement.

The topology of the configuration spaces and the independence complexes for digraphs


Abstract
We study the topology of the (path) configuration spaces and the (path) independence complexes for digraphs as well as their underlying graphs. We prove some canonical embeddings from the (path) independence complexes of the underlying graphs to the (path) independence complexes of the digraphs as well as some canonical embeddings between the (path) independence complexes induced by strong totally geodesic immersions of (di)graphs. We apply the path complexes and the path homology to the path independence complexes of (di)graphs. As by-products, we give some consequences about the Shannon capacities.

Introduction

(a) Configuration spaces
.
Let
X
be a space. The
k
-th ordered configuration space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
is the subspace of the Cartesian product
X^{k}

such that the
k
-coordinates in
X
are distinct.
The
k
-th symmetric group
\Sigma_{k}
acts on
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)

freely by
perturbing the coordinates. The
k
-th unordered configuration space
is the orbit space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)/\Sigma_{k}
.

Suppose in addition that
X
is equipped with a metric
d:X\times X\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
.
For any
r\geq 0
,
the
k
-th ordered configuration space of hard
r
-spheres

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)
is the subspace of
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)

consisting of the configurations

(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
such that
d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r
for any
i\neq j
.
The
k
-th unordered configuration space of hard
r
-spheres
is the orbit space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)/\Sigma_{k}
.
For any
0\leq r<s
,
denote by
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r,s)
for the complement of
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,s)

in
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)
.
Then
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r,s)
is
\Sigma_{k}
-invariant thus
we have an orbit space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r,s)/\Sigma_{k}
.

One special case for configuration spaces is that

is a manifold

.
In 1978, F. R. Cohen and L. R. Taylor

[

,

.
In particular, for

[
10
,

]
obtained the complete information on

{\rm Conf}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
{\rm Conf}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{m})/\Sigma_{k}

by using

-fold loop spaces;
and the cohomology of
{\rm Conf}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})/\Sigma_{k}

is used by F. Cohen and D. Handel
[
12

to study
the
k
-regular embeddings
of the plane into ambient Euclidean spaces.
An introduction to the configuration spaces

and

as well as their applications

[

]
.

In addition, if

then we have the configuration spaces of hard

As

With the help of the Min-type Morse theory (cf.

]

it is proved by Y. Baryshnikov, P. Bubenik and M. Kahle

that mechanically balanced configurations in a bounded region in Euclidean spaces

Another special case for configuration spaces is that

is a graph

Consider the geometric realization

In recent years,

(b) Digraphs and their path complexes
.
A digraph
\vec{G}
is obtained by
assigning a direction or both directions to each edge of a graph
G

while a graph
G
is obtained by forgetting the direction on each
arc of a digraph
\vec{G}
(see Definition
1
).
An
allowed elementary
n
-path
on a digraph

\vec{G}
is a sequence of vertices
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}

such that for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
either
v_{i-1}=v_{i}
or
v_{i-1}\to v_{i}
is an arc of
\vec{G}

(cf.
[
19
, Sec. 2]
and
[
20
,
21
]
).

The collection of all the allowed elementary paths of finite lengths on

\vec{G}
generate
a free abelian group
\mathcal{A}_{*}(\vec{G})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{A}_{n}(\vec{G})

with a grading given by the length

of the paths.
Given an

-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(\vec{G})
,
both
\widehat{v_{0}}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
and
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots\widehat{v_{n}}

are
(n-1)
-paths in
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\vec{G})
.
However,
v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}
may not be an
(n-1)
-path
in
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\vec{G})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
A chain complex
\Omega_{*}(\vec{G})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Omega_{n}(\vec{G})
,
where

\Omega_{n}(\vec{G})=\mathcal{A}_{n}(\vec{G})\cap\partial_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\vec{G})

and
\partial_{n}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}
,
is constructed
and thereby the path homology
H(\Omega_{*}(\vec{G}))
of
\vec{G}
is studied by
A. Grigor’yan, Y. Lin, Y. Muranov and S.-T. Yau
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
.
With the help of
[
8
, Sec. 2]
,
\Omega_{*}(\vec{G})

is the largest chain complex contained in
\mathcal{A}_{*}(\vec{G})
,
denoted as
{\rm Inf}(\mathcal{A}_{*}(\vec{G}))
, which is quasi-isomorphic to
the smallest chain complex containing
\mathcal{A}_{*}(\vec{G})
,
denoted as
{\rm Sup}(\mathcal{A}_{*}(\vec{G}))
.

(c) The Shannon capacities
.
For any graph
G
, an independent set is a collection of some vertices of
G

such that any two of them are non-adjacent.
All the finite independent sets of
G
form a simplicial complex
{\rm Ind}(G)

which is called the

independence complex
(cf.
[
16
,
6
]
).

Let
\alpha(G)
be the maximal size of the independent sets of
G
, i.e.

\alpha(G)
is the dimension
of the independence complex plus one.
Let
G^{\boxtimes n}
be the
n
-fold self-strong product of
G
.
Motivated by the study of the channels in information theory, C. E. Shannon
[
27
]
in 1956
introduced the capacity
c(G)
to be the supremum of
(\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n}))^{{1}/{n}}
as
n
goes to infinity.
So far, the study of the Shannon capacity of graphs has attracted lots of attention (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
22
,
24
,
26
,
27
]
).
Moreover, the Shannon capacity of graphs is generalized to the capacity of digraphs
in the sense of the adjacency matrices by
E. Bidamon and H. Meyniel
[
7
]
.

(d) Results of this paper
.
Let
\vec{G}
be a digraph and let
G
be its underlying graph.
We consider the configuration space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(\vec{G})

consisting of all the
k
-tuples of mutually non-adjacent distinct vertices in
\vec{G}
,
which equals to
the configuration space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(G)

consisting of all the
k
-tuples of mutually non-adjacent distinct vertices of

.
Then the family of configuration spaces

,
which equal to

{\rm Conf}_{k}(G)
, for

gives the skeleton of the independence complex
{\rm Ind}(\vec{G})
,
which equals to

{\rm Ind}(G)
. The Shannon capacity of

is expressed in terms of the
dimension of the independence complex.

We take the canonical distance
d_{\vec{G}}
on the vertex set such that
the distance between any two vertices is the minimal length of the paths in
\vec{G}

connecting them. Similarly, we take the canonical distance
d_{G}
on the vertex set
by the the minimal length of the paths in
G
.
For any
0\leq r<s
,
consider the
constraint configuration space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(\vec{G},r,s)
consisting of all the ordered
k
-tuples
of vertices such that their mutual distances
d_{\vec{G}}
lie in the interval
(2r,2s]

and the constraint configuration space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(G,r,s)
consisting of all the ordered
k
-tuples
of vertices such that their mutual distances
d_{G}
lie in
(2r,2s]
.
The family of configuration spaces
{\rm Conf}_{k}(\vec{G},r,s)
gives
a constraint independence complex
{\rm Ind}(\vec{G},r,s)

and the family of configuration spaces
{\rm Conf}_{k}(G,r,s)
gives
a constraint independence complex
{\rm Ind}(G,r,s)
.
We refer to
[
25
, Sec. 2 - Sec. 4]
for a detailed introduction
of the topological persistence of configuration spaces and omit it in this paper.
The next theorem will be proved in Subsection
3.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any digraph
\vec{G}
with its underlying graph

,
we have a

\Sigma_{k}
-equivariant isometric embedding

of

Digraphs and their distances

Configuration spaces and independence complexes for digraphs

In this section, we study the configuration spaces for digraphs and the underlying graphs.
In Subsection

,
we construct the independence complexes by using the configuration spaces as
the skeletons.
In Subsection

5
The Shannon capacities of digraphs

In this section, we apply the independence complexes of (di)graphs in Section
4

to study the Shannon capacities.
In Subsection
5.1
,
we prove some lemmas on the strong products of (di)graphs.
In Subsection
5.2
,
we prove that the Shannon capacity of the underlying graph is smaller than or equal

5.1
Strong products of (di)graphs

Given two graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
, their
strong product

G_{1}\boxtimes G_{2}
is the graph whose vertex set is
V_{1}\times V_{2}
and whose edge set is specified by the following rule:
for any distinct two vertices
(v_{1},v_{2})
and
(u_{1},u_{2})
,
there is an edge between them
iff for each
i=1,2
, either
v_{i}=u_{i}
or
\{v_{i},u_{i}\}\in E_{i}
.
Similarly,
given two digraphs
\vec{G}_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
\vec{G}_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
, their
strong product

\vec{G}_{1}\boxtimes\vec{G}_{2}
is the digraph whose vertex set is
V_{1}\times V_{2}
and whose arc set is specified by the following rule:
for any distinct two vertices
(v_{1},v_{2})
and
(u_{1},u_{2})
in
V_{1}\times V_{2}
,
there is an arc

(v_{1},v_{2})\to(u_{1},u_{2})

iff for each
i=1,2
, either
v_{i}=u_{i}
or
(v_{i},u_{i})\in E_{i}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any digraphs
\vec{G}_{1}
and
\vec{G}_{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle\pi(\vec{G}_{1}\boxtimes\vec{G}_{2})\subseteq\pi(\vec{G}_{1})\boxtimes\pi(\vec{G}_{2}),

(5.1)

i.e. the underlying graph of the strong product of digraphs is a subgraph of
the strong product of the underlying graphs.

Proof.

The vertex set of both
\pi(\vec{G}_{1}\boxtimes\vec{G}_{2})

and
\pi(\vec{G}_{1})\boxtimes\pi(\vec{G}_{2})
are
V_{1}\times V_{2}
.
For any distinct two vertices
(v_{1},v_{2})
and
(u_{1},u_{2})
in
V_{1}\times V_{2}
,

\displaystyle\{(v_{1},v_{2}),(u_{1},u_{2})\}{\rm~is~an~edge~of~}\pi(\vec{G}_{1}\boxtimes\vec{G}_{2})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(v_{1},v_{2})\to(u_{1},u_{2}){\rm~or~}(u_{1},u_{2})\to(v_{1},v_{2}){\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{1}\boxtimes\vec{G}_{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Big[(v_{1}=u_{1}{\rm~or~}v_{1}\to u_{1}{\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{1}){\rm~and~}(v_{2}=u_{2}{\rm~or~}v_{2}\to u_{2}{\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{2})\Big]

\displaystyle{\rm~or~}\Big[(v_{1}=u_{1}{\rm~or~}u_{1}\to v_{1}{\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{1}){\rm~and~}(v_{2}=u_{2}{\rm~or~}u_{2}\to v_{2}{\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{2})\Big]

and

\displaystyle\{(v_{1},v_{2}),(u_{1},u_{2})\}{\rm~is~an~edge~of~}\pi(\vec{G}_{1})\boxtimes\pi(\vec{G}_{2})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm~for~}i=1,2,{\rm~we~have~}v_{i}=u_{i}{\rm~or~}\{v_{i},u_{i}\}{\rm~is~an~edge~of~}\pi(\vec{G}_{i})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm~for~}i=1,2,{\rm~we~have~}v_{i}=u_{i},{\rm~or~}v_{i}\to u_{i}{\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{i},{\rm~or~}u_{i}\to v_{i}{\rm~is~an~arc~of~}\vec{G}_{i}.

Therefore,
each edge of
\pi(\vec{G}_{1}\boxtimes\vec{G}_{2})

is an edge of
\pi(\vec{G}_{1})\boxtimes\pi(\vec{G}_{2})
.
We obtain (
5.1
).
∎

\displaystyle\alpha({G}^{\boxtimes pq},-,-)\leq\alpha(\pi({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes pq}),-,-)\leq\alpha(\pi({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes q})^{\boxtimes p},-,-)\leq\alpha({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes pq},-,-).

This implies

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{p\to\infty}\alpha({G}^{\boxtimes pq},-,-)^{\frac{1}{pq}}\leq\lim\sup_{p\to\infty}\alpha(\pi({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes q})^{\boxtimes p},-,-)^{\frac{1}{pq}}\leq\lim\sup_{p\to\infty}\alpha({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes pq},-,-)^{\frac{1}{pq}}.

(5.16)

Substituting

\displaystyle c(G,-,-)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{p\to\infty}\alpha({G}^{\boxtimes pq},-,-)^{\frac{1}{pq}},

\displaystyle c(\pi({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes q}),-,-)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{p\to\infty}\alpha(\pi({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes q})^{\boxtimes p},-,-)^{\frac{1}{pq}},

\displaystyle c(\vec{G},-,-)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{p\to\infty}\alpha({\vec{G}}^{\boxtimes pq},-,-)^{\frac{1}{pq}}

in (
5.16
),
we obtain (
5.14
).
∎

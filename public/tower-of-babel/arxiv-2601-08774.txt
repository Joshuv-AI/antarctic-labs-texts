Integral points over number fields: a Clemens complex jigsaw puzzle
Christian Bernert, Ulrich Derenthal, Judith Ortmann, Florian Wilsch

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for the number of integral points of bounded log anticanonical height on a singular quartic del Pezzo surface over arbitrary number fields, with respect to the largest admissible boundary divisor. The resulting Clemens complex is more complicated than usual, and leads to particularly interesting effective cone constants, associated with exponentially many polytopes whose volumes appear in the expected formula. Like a jigsaw puzzle, these polytopes fit together to one large polytope. The volume of this polytope appears in the asymptotic formula that we obtain using the universal torsor method via o-minimal structures.

1.
Introduction

The Manin–Peyre conjecture predicts the asymptotic behavior of the number of rational points of bounded height on a Fano variety. While a proof in the general case remains elusive, our understanding of the constants appearing in this asymptotic formula has consolidated over the past decades, in particular conforming to both the results obtained by harmonic analysis techniques as well as by the universal torsor method.

On the other hand, the analogous situation for integral points is much less streamlined. Only recently
[
Wil24
,
San23
]
(based on
[
CLT10
,
CLT12a
,
CLT12b
]
), a precise description of the expected main term has emerged. As integral points on a log Fano variety with respect to a boundary divisor
D
tend to accumulate near the minimal strata of
D
, it transpires that the distribution of integral points is governed by the maximal faces of the associated analytic Clemens complex.
From the point of view of harmonic analysis, as worked out in
[
San23
]
in the case of toric varieties, it is then natural to study the contribution from each face separately, thus resulting in an expected formula that involves a sum over these maximal faces.

In an application of the torsor method, however, one tends to obtain an asymptotic formula that naturally involves only one main term. As both the predicted contributions from each face of the Clemens complex as well as the constant obtained from the torsor method involve volumes of certain polytopes, it is natural to wonder to which extent these pictures can be unified.

This article explores this phenomenon by revisiting the singular quartic del Pezzo surface with an
A_{1}
and
A_{3}
singularity, which has served as an instructive example in other contexts before: for rational points over
\mathbb{Q}

[

]
, imaginary quadratic fields
[

]
, arbitrary number fields
[

,
DP20
]
and function fields
[

]
, and for integral points over
\mathbb{Q}

[
DW24
]
and imaginary quadratic fields
[

, Theorem 2.7 (cases 1, 4)]
.
It is neither toric
[

1.3.
Overview of the proof

In Section
2
, we lift the set of integral points to twisted universal torsors. This is the igniting spark for both the parameterization underlying the counting techniques used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and the computation of the Tamagawa measures in the expected formula, as required for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
In Section
3
, we deduce Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
1.1
. As explained in the introduction, a key novelty here is a comparison of the effective cone constants in Proposition
1.2
.
In Section
4
, we initiate the proof of Theorem
1.1
by setting up a concrete counting problem on the universal torsors. Working over number fields, this requires the construction of a suitable fundamental domain. In Proposition
4.2
, we also prepare for the counting by introducing a truncation of some of the torsor variables. This move, motivated by
[
BD25a
]
, turns out to be very useful for estimating the error terms in the later stages of the counting argument.
Finally, in Section
5
, we reduce to a lattice point counting problem which we can solve using o-minimal structures. After computing the local densities and summing over the remaining variables, this eventually leads to a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

This work is based on the third author’s Ph.D. thesis
[
Ort25
, Theorem 2.7, case 3]
, which establishes the asymptotic formula in Theorem
1.1
. New ingredients in our present work include the interpretation of the constant
\alpha
as in Theorem
1.3
, the more conceptual computation of the nonarchimedean densities using model measures and numbers of points modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}
(instead of
{\mathfrak{p}}
-adic integrals) in Section
3.1
, as well as a simplified counting machinery via the restrictions in Section

is contained in a box of side length
\ll(B/(\mathfrak{N}({\mathfrak{a}}_{1}{\mathfrak{a}}_{2})))^{1/d}
.

Hence
V_{W}\leq\prod_{v\mid\infty}V^{(v)}_{p_{v}}
for certain
p_{v}\in\{0,\dots,d_{v}\}
that are not all zero. Therefore,

V_{W}\ll\left(\frac{B}{\mathfrak{N}({\mathfrak{a}}_{1}{\mathfrak{a}}_{2})}\right)^{1-1/d}\ll\frac{B}{T^{1/d}\mathfrak{N}({\mathfrak{a}}_{1}{\mathfrak{a}}_{2})},

using (
4.10
) in the final step.
∎

5.2.
Archimedean densities

Here, we compute the volume of the set
S_{F}
appearing in the main term in Lemma
5.1

B
, and essentially the archimedean densities appear in the leading constant.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
\boldsymbol{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{*}^{\prime}\cap\mathscr{F}_{1}^{5}
with
\theta_{0}({\underline{\mathfrak{a}}}^{\prime})=1
and
a_{6},a_{7}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
with (
4.9
), we have

\vol S_{F}(\boldsymbol{a}^{\prime},(a_{6}^{(v)},a_{7}^{(v)})_{v};B)=\frac{2^{r_{1}}\pi^{r_{2}}R_{K}B}{|N(a_{2})|}\left(1+O(T^{-1/d})\right).

Proof.

We substitute
x_{8v}=B_{8v}z_{8v}
(with Jacobian
|\prod_{v\mid\infty}B_{8v}|=B/|N(a_{2})|
) in the definition of
S_{F}
, and introduce the notation
a_{i}^{(v)}=B_{iv}z_{iv}
for
i=6,7

\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{a}^{\prime},a_{6}^{(v)},a_{7}^{(v)},x_{8v})/B=\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},z_{6v},z_{7v},z_{8v}),

and hence

\vol S_{F}(\boldsymbol{a}^{\prime},(a_{6}^{(v)},a_{7}^{(v)})_{v};B)=\frac{B}{|N(a_{2})|}\vol S_{F}(\boldsymbol{1},(z_{6v},z_{7v})_{v};1).

We have

\displaystyle\vol S_{F}(\boldsymbol{1},(z_{6v},z_{7v})_{v};1)=\int_{(\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},z_{6v},z_{7v},z_{8v}))_{v\mid\infty}\in\exp(F(1))}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\,\mathrm{d}z_{8v}

\displaystyle=\int_{(\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},0,0,z_{8v}))_{v\mid\infty}\in\exp(F(1))}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\,\mathrm{d}z_{8v}+O\left(\int_{(\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},0,0,z_{8v}))_{v\mid\infty}\in D}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\,\mathrm{d}z_{8v}\right)

where
D
is the
\delta
-neighborhood of the boundary of
\exp(F(1))
for

(since
\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},z_{6v},z_{7v},z_{8v})=\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},0,0,z_{8v})+O(T^{-d_{v}/d})

We complete the proof following
[

as

f_{*}(\vol)=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\vol\{z_{8v}\in K_{v}:\widetilde{N}_{v}(\boldsymbol{1},0,0,z_{8v})\leq 1\}\cdot\vol=2^{r_{1}}\pi^{r_{2}}\cdot\vol

Proposition 5.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}\in\mathscr{C}}|M_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}(B)|={}

\displaystyle+O(B(\log B)^{1+2q}(\log\log B))

Lemma 5.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}({\mathfrak{a}}_{1},{\mathfrak{a}}_{2})\in\mathscr{I}_{K}^{2}\\
\eqref{eq:restrict_a1a2}\\
[{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}{\mathfrak{a}}_{2}]=[\mathcal{O}_{K}]\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta_{0}({\underline{\mathfrak{a}}}^{\prime})B}{\mathfrak{N}({\mathfrak{a}}_{1}{\mathfrak{a}}_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{6},a_{7}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
\eqref{eq:restrict_a6_a7}\end{subarray}}1={}

\displaystyle\frac{|\mu_{K}|^{2}}{h_{K}R_{K}^{2}}\rho_{K}^{2}\alpha\theta_{1}B(\log B)^{2+2q}

\displaystyle+O((\log B)^{1+2q}(\log\log B))

with

\theta_{1}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{N}{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}}\right).

Proof.

By
[
BD25b
, Lemma 3.1]
, the sum over
a_{6},a_{7}
is

\frac{|\mu_{K}|^{2}}{R_{K}^{2}q!^{2}}(\log(B_{6}/T)\log(B_{7}/T))^{q}+O((\log B)^{2q-1}).

Since
|\theta_{0}({\underline{\mathfrak{a}}}^{\prime})|\leq 1
, the sum of the error term over
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1},{\mathfrak{a}}_{2}
is
O(B(\log B)^{1+2q})
.

For the main term, because of (
4.7
) and (
4.10
), we consider

V(z_{1},z_{2};B)\coloneq\frac{B(\log(B/T)-\log(z_{1}z_{2}))^{q}(\log(B/T))^{q}}{(\log B)^{2q}z_{1}z_{2}}\cdot V^{\prime}(z_{1},z_{2};B),

where
V^{\prime}
is the indicator function of the set of
z_{1},z_{2}\geq 1
with
z_{1}z_{2}\leq B/T
.

Since
V\ll B/(z_{1}z_{2})
vanishes unless
z_{1},z_{2}\ll B
, we can apply a version of
[
DF14a
, Proposition 7.2]
where, for fixed
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
, the summation over
{\mathfrak{a}}_{2}
is restricted to the ideal class inverse to that of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
, which leads to an extra factor of
h_{K}^{-1}
. (This modification of
[
DF14a
, Proposition 7.2]
is straightforward:
[
DF14a
, Lemma 2.5]
provides a formula for the average growth of certain arithmetic functions when summed over a fixed ideal class, hence such a restriction is easily incorporated into
[
DF14a
, Corollary 2.7]
, which in turn is used in the computation of the main term in
[
DF14a
, Proposition 7.2]
.) Defining

V_{0}(B)=\frac{1}{q!^{2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{1},z_{2}\geq 1\\
z_{1}z_{2}\leq B/T\end{subarray}}\frac{B(\log(B/T)-\log(z_{1}z_{2}))^{q}(\log(B/T))^{q}}{z_{1}z_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2},

we obtain the main term

\frac{|\mu_{K}|^{2}\rho_{K}^{2}\theta_{1}}{h_{K}R_{K}^{2}}V_{0}(B)+O(B(\log B)^{1+2q}(\log\log B))

since the average as in
[
DF14a
, Lemma 2.8]
of
\theta_{0}({\underline{\mathfrak{a}}}^{\prime})
over
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1},{\mathfrak{a}}_{2}
is
\theta_{1}
as in our statement.

Substituting
z_{i}=(B/T)^{t_{i}}
, the range of integration turns into
\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}:t_{1}+t_{2}\leq 1\}

\log(B/T)-\log(z_{1}z_{2})=\log(B/T)(1-t_{1}-t_{2}).

Hence, by definition of
\alpha
and
T
, we obtain

V_{0}(B)=\alpha B(\log(B/T))^{2+2q}=\alpha B(\log B)^{2+2q}+O(B(\log B)^{1+2q}(\log\log B)).\qed

Finally, the main result is obtained by combining Proposition
4.1

5.3

5.4

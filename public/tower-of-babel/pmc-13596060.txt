Therapeutic plasma exchange in paediatric ANCA-associated vasculitis: a multicentre real-world experience
Authors: Bengisu Menentoğlu, Batuhan Küçükali, Fatma Gül Demirkan, Gülşah Kılbaş, Şeyma Türkmen, Elif Küçük, Mehveş Işıklar Ekici, Ayşenur Alkaya, Mehmet Emin Menentoğlu, Figen Çakmak, Özlem Akgün, Esra Şevketoğlu, Kübra Öztürk, Semanur Özdel, Serkan Türkuçar, Selçuk Yüksel, Banu Çelikel Acar, Betül Sözeri, Sevcan A Bakkaloğlu, Nuray Aktay Ayaz

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesAnti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody–associated vasculitis (AAV) is a rare but potentially life-threatening condition in children, often presenting with severe multi-organ involvement. Although standard induction therapy includes cyclophosphamide or rituximab with glucocorticoids, the role of therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) in paediatric AAV remains uncertain. This study sought to describe the clinical characteristics, indications and outcomes of paediatric AAV patients treated with TPE in real-world practice and to compare them with patients managed without TPE.MethodsThis retrospective multicentre study included children (<18 years) diagnosed with AAV according to EULAR/PRINTO/PRES criteria. Patients were classified into TPE-treated (TPE+) and non-TPE-treated (TPE−) groups. Demographic features, organ involvement, laboratory parameters, Paediatric Vasculitis Activity Score (PVAS), treatments, adverse events and outcomes were analysed.ResultsTwenty-six paediatric AAV patients were included, of whom 11 (42.3%) underwent TPE. At diagnosis, the TPE+ group had significantly higher disease activity (median PVAS 12 vs 7, P = 0.004) and more severe kidney involvement, with higher serum creatinine and lower estimated glomerular filtration rate. Severe pulmonary, gastrointestinal, cardiovascular and cutaneous manifestations occurred predominantly in the TPE+ group. The primary indication for TPE was severe kidney involvement. TPE was associated with a significant reduction in PVAS (median 12.0–4.5, P = 0.007). Clinical improvement was observed in most acute organ-threatening manifestations, although persistent abnormalities remained common among patients with severe baseline kidney involvement. One patient died from catheter-related Pseudomonas aeruginosa sepsis during treatment.ConclusionTPE is selectively used as an adjunctive therapy in children with severe or refractory AAV, particularly in those with advanced kidney involvement. Although disease activity and acute organ-threatening manifestations improved following TPE, persistent renal abnormalities remained frequent, reflecting the severity of baseline kidney disease.Keywords: anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-associated vasculitis, plasma exchange, systemic vasculitis, vasculitis, small vessel

Introduction

Anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-associated vasculitis (AAV) is a category of necrotizing vasculitis affecting small to medium-sized blood vessels characterized by clinically overlapping features, life-threatening organ involvement and the presence of ANCA [1]. Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), microscopic polyangiitis (MPA), eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA), renal-limited AAV represent the vasculitis classified within this particular assemblage. In children, AAV is more common in females, with the highest incidence occurring in the second decade of life and a median diagnosis age of 12–14 years [2, 3]. Paediatric AAV tends to be more aggressive and likely to involve multiple organs at the time of diagnosis [4]. The underlying causes of AAV are thought to be influenced by genetic susceptibility, immune system dysregulation, environmental factors (such as silica exposure, farming and organic solvents), infections (Staphylococcus aureus, HIV, COVID-19) and medications (penicillamine, propylthiouracil, dapsone) [5–7].

Autoimmunity in AAV is defined by the presence of serum ANCAs targeting proteinase 3 (PR3-ANCA) or myeloperoxidase (MPO-ANCA), both of which are closely linked to specific disease presentations [8]. Compared with adult-onset disease, paediatric AAV more frequently affects females and often presents with extensive multisystem involvement. Previous studies have also reported a higher frequency of destructive upper airway manifestations, including subglottic stenosis and nasal cartilage damage, whereas renal involvement remains a major determinant of morbidity [9, 10].

Anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-associated vasculitis is a potentially life-threatening condition that requires the prompt initiation of immunosuppressive therapy to prevent tissue injury and long-term organ damage. Given the heterogeneity of clinical presentations and treatment responses in paediatric AAV, it is crucial to evaluate actual clinical practice to inform evidence-based care. Current recommendations suggest cyclophosphamide (CYC) or rituximab (RTX) combined with glucocorticoids (GCs) as standard remission induction therapy, while therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) may be considered as an adjunctive option in severe cases, such as rapidly progressive kidney disease [11–13].

Plasma exchange, or plasmapheresis, is a well-established and widely used procedure that involves removing and replacing a patient’s circulating plasma. This extracorporeal treatment effectively eliminates high-molecular-weight (>150 kDa) pathogenic elements, such as immunoglobulins, immune complexes and inflammatory mediators [14]. In AAV patients, TPE provides a rapid effect, primarily targeting to reduce organ damage during the acute phase and serve as a bridge until GCs or immunosuppressive/immunomodulatory agents achieve their full therapeutic effect. While large adult studies such as the MEPEX [15] and PEXIVAS [16] trials have examined the efficacy of TPE, the role of this intervention in the paediatric population remains poorly defined.

While existing guidelines are largely derived from adult studies, the limited paediatric experience and the unique features of the developing immune system underscore the importance of paediatric-specific treatment strategies. The rarity of paediatric AAV limits the availability of child-specific data on the efficacy and side effects of treatment strategies. In this study, we aimed to describe the clinical characteristics and outcomes of paediatric AAV patients who underwent TPE and to explore these findings in comparison with a group of paediatric AAV patients who did not receive TPE. By evaluating disease severity, organ involvement, treatment approaches and outcomes between these two groups, we sought to provide descriptive data that may contribute to a better understanding of the use of TPE in paediatric AAV in real-world clinical practice.

Patients and method

Study design and patient population

This retrospective multicentre analysis included individuals under the age of 18 who were diagnosed with AAV in tertiary paediatric rheumatology centres in Türkiye. Patients were eligible for inclusion if they:

Were younger than 18 years of age at the time of diagnosis.

Were younger than 18 years of age at the time of diagnosis.

Fulfilled the 2012 revised Chapel Hill Consensus Conference definitions and EULAR/PRINTO/PRES classification criteria for AAV.

Fulfilled the 2012 revised Chapel Hill Consensus Conference definitions and EULAR/PRINTO/PRES classification criteria for AAV.

Participants were stratified into two groups based on treatment modality: those who underwent TPE and those who were managed without TPE. Patients with incomplete data or alternative diagnoses were excluded. In ANCA-negative patients, the diagnosis was supported by histopathological findings together with compatible clinical features and fulfilment of the EULAR/PRINTO/PRES classification criteria.

Data collection and variables

The medical records of the patients were reviewed for demographic data, clinical findings, laboratory findings, ANCA serology and organ involvement. Baseline clinical characteristics were collected at the initiation of immunosuppressive treatment for newly diagnosed or relapsed AAV cases.

Disease activity in all patients was assessed using the Paediatric Vasculitis Activity Score (PVAS), which evaluates clinical findings across 10 organ systems: general health, skin, mucous membranes and eyes, ENT (ear-nose-throat) region, chest, cardiovascular system, gastrointestinal tract, kidneys, nervous system and other domains. The PVAS ranges from 0 to 63, with higher scores reflecting greater disease activity [17]. For patients who underwent TPE, disease activity was assessed at three predefined time points: immediately before initiation of TPE (pre-TPE), after completion of the final TPE session (post-TPE) and at the last follow-up visit. In patients who did not undergo TPE, disease activity was assessed at diagnosis and at the last follow-up visit. The last follow-up assessment was defined as the most recent clinical visit available in the medical records. Follow-up duration was calculated from diagnosis to the last recorded visit.

Disease activity states, including remission, response, relapse and refractory disease, were defined in accordance with the EULAR consensus definitions for AAV [13]. Remission was defined as the absence of active disease, response as a ≥ 50% reduction in disease activity without new manifestations, relapse as the recurrence of active disease after remission, and refractory disease as persistent disease activity despite standard induction therapy, after exclusion of alternative causes. Immunosuppressive therapies were thoroughly documented, including the administration of CYC, RTX and methylprednisolone pulse therapy.

Plasma exchange procedure

TPE was administered using either membrane filtration-based systems (mTPE) or centrifugation-based systems (cTPE), depending on institutional preference. TPE was generally performed as a course of 5–7 sessions, according to institutional practice and clinical indication. The duration of therapy was individualized based on clinical response, stabilization of organ-threatening manifestations and the treating physician’s judgement.

The replacement fluid included a combination of fresh frozen plasma and albumin, adjusted according to patient size and clinical indication. The number of sessions and volume exchanged were individualized based on clinical response and tolerance. All procedures were conducted under standard paediatric critical care monitoring.

The method, indication and dose of TPE and any observed adverse events were carefully analysed. Data were collected in a web-secured database.

Ethical issues

This study was approved by the Istanbul University Faculty of Medicine Clinical Research Ethics Committee (Ethics Committee Approval No/Date: 2024–1667/06.09.2024) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from the patients and their parents included in the study.

Statistics

The data were analysed using SPSS software (Version 26.0). Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (s.d.) or median with interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Paired comparisons of clinical and laboratory variables before and after TPE were analysed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test due to the small sample size and non-normal distribution. Graphical visualizations and figure designs were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10.5.0, with additional support from ChatGPT-5.1 for optimizing figure layout and presentation. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 26 paediatric AAV patients were included in the analysis, of whom 11 (42.3%) were treated with TPE and 15 (57.7%) were managed without TPE. Sex distribution was similar between the TPE+ and TPE−groups (female: 54.5% vs 80.0%, P = 0.218). Median age at diagnosis (17.1 vs 15.8 years, P = 0.413) and the median time from symptom onset to diagnosis (1.0 vs 2.0 months, P = 0.331) were also comparable between the groups. AAV subtype distribution did not differ significantly between the two groups. Median serum creatinine and urea levels were significantly higher in patients who underwent TPE compared with those who did not (creatinine: 3.34 vs 0.60 mg/dl, P = 0.004; urea: 77 vs 23 mg/dl, P = 0.002), and this was accompanied by a substantially lower median GFR in the TPE+ group (25 vs 121 mL/min/1.73 m², P = 0.023). Baseline disease activity, as assessed by PVAS, was significantly higher in the TPE+ group compared with the TPE−group (12 vs 7, P = 0.004) (Table 1).

Across the entire cohort, lower respiratory tract (73%) and kidney involvement (69.2%) were the most frequently affected organ systems. Pulmonary involvement was observed in 72.7% of patients in the TPE+ group and included severe manifestations such as alveolar haemorrhage and respiratory failure; these severe pulmonary features were observed almost exclusively in patients treated with TPE. In contrast, milder respiratory manifestations, including dyspnoea, pleural effusion and pulmonary infiltrates, were noted in both groups. Kidney involvement was common in both groups, and haematuria and proteinuria were frequently observed in both groups.

In the TPE+ group, upper respiratory tract manifestations included paranasal sinus involvement (n = 7), nasal ulcers (n = 2), nasal septum perforation (n = 1) and conductive hearing loss (n = 1). In the TPE−group, upper respiratory tract involvement consisted of paranasal sinus involvement (n = 5) and nasal septum perforation (n = 2). In the TPE+ group, cutaneous manifestations included purpura (n = 4), ulceration (n = 1) and extensive skin necrosis (n = 1), reflecting a broader spectrum and greater severity of skin involvement. Four patients in the TPE+ group developed gastrointestinal manifestations, including abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, bloody diarrhoea and intestinal ischaemia. Two patients exhibited cardiovascular involvement, including pericardial effusion and valvular heart disease on echocardiography.

One patient in the TPE+ group developed orbital involvement characterized by proptosis, whereas in the TPE−group, ocular manifestations included one case of proptosis and one case of episcleritis. One patient in the TPE+ group with uncontrolled hypertension developed posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES) during follow-up. In addition, in the TPE−group, one patient developed peripheral facial palsy and one patient developed mononeuritis multiplex. Detailed clinical characteristics are summarized in Supplementary Table S1.

As induction therapy, 73.3% of patients in the TPE−group received pulse methylprednisolone (30 mg/kg/day) and 66.7% received cyclophosphamide (500–750 mg/m2), whereas all patients in the TPE+ group received both agents. During maintenance therapy, all patients in both groups received maintenance-dose methylprednisolone (0.1–0.5 mg/kg/day). In addition, rituximab (375–500 mg/m²) was administered during initial management in 54.5% of the TPE+ group and 46.7% of the TPE−group.

From a technical standpoint, membrane filtration–based TPE was used in all patients except one, who underwent centrifugation-based TPE. The primary indication for TPE was severe kidney involvement (n = 8), while the remaining indications, including severe gastrointestinal involvement, multiorgan failure, skin necrosis and pulmonary haemorrhage, were each reported in one patient (Table 2).

In patients who underwent TPE, a significant reduction in disease activity was observed, with median PVAS scores decreasing from 12.0 (IQR 9.0–13.0) to 4.5 (IQR 4.0–5.75) (P = 0.007, Wilcoxon signed-rank test) (Fig. 1).

The most common adverse events related to plasma exchange were nausea and vomiting (n = 4), and catheter dysfunction (n = 3). Patient 9, who was receiving concurrent dialysis and underwent TPE due to multiorgan failure, developed catheter-related sepsis caused by Pseudomonas aeruginosa and subsequently died. Hypocalcaemia and coagulopathy occurred in two patients, both of which resolved with appropriate treatment. One patient developed arrhythmia and hypotension during the procedure, which also resolved with medical management.

At the last follow-up visit, patients in the TPE+ group demonstrated a significant reduction in serum urea levels (P = 0.015), a marked improvement in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) (P = 0.017), and a significant decrease in haematuria (P = 0.003), while serum creatinine levels and proteinuria remained unchanged.

During the maintenance phase, corticosteroids were administered to 7 patients in the TPE+ group and to 11 patients in the TPE−group. Additional maintenance immunosuppressive therapies included rituximab (n = 2 and n = 1 in the TPE+ and TPE−groups, respectively), mycophenolate mofetil (n = 5 in both groups) and azathioprine (n = 2 and n = 1 in the TPE+ and TPE−groups, respectively), which were prescribed on an individual basis according to clinical status.

Remission was achieved in 8 patients (72.7%) in the TPE+ group and in 14 patients (93.3%) in the TPE−group. Clinical improvement was observed in most acute organ-threatening manifestations during follow-up, including pulmonary, gastrointestinal, cardiovascular and cutaneous involvement. However, renal abnormalities persisted in 7 patients in the TPE+ group, predominantly in the form of elevated serum creatinine levels (P = 0.014). Despite this, the TPE+ group demonstrated a marked improvement in renal function over time, with a median reduction of ∼50% in serum creatinine levels compared with baseline values, whereas serum creatinine levels in the TPE−group, which were within the normal range at baseline, remained largely unchanged during follow-up. Although PVAS scores in the TPE+ group decreased compared with baseline values, they remained significantly higher than those observed in the TPE−group at the last visit (P = 0.009) (Table 3, Fig. 2). Similarly, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in the TPE+ group increased almost 3-fold during follow-up. While a statistically significant difference in eGFR between the groups was present at baseline, this difference was no longer observed at the last follow-up visit.

Discussion

Therapeutic plasma exchange has been increasingly re-evaluated in the management of AAV, particularly following the PEXIVAS trial, which demonstrated no significant reduction in kidney failure or mortality with the addition of TPE to standard immunosuppressive therapy in adults with severe disease, leading to a more restrictive approach in contemporary clinical practice [16]. This paradigm shift is reflected in the updated 2023 American Society for Apheresis (ASFA) guidelines, which no longer recommend routine TPE for all patients with AAV but reserve its use for selected high-risk clinical scenarios, primarily severe kidney involvement or concomitant diffuse alveolar haemorrhage [18]. Within this evolving therapeutic framework, kidney involvement emerged as the predominant indication for TPE in our paediatric cohort, emphasizing the persistent clinical concern regarding rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis and advanced kidney dysfunction in children with AAV. Consistent with the limited paediatric evidence, including a small retrospective series reporting improvement in disease activity following TPE in selected cases [19], we observed a significant clinical response to TPE in our cohort, as demonstrated by a marked reduction in PVAS. Although PVAS provides a standardized assessment of disease activity, in the present study changes in PVAS were interpreted together with clinical and laboratory findings.

Notably, although one patient with EGPA underwent TPE despite being classified as Category III according to current ASFA guidelines and demonstrated a substantial reduction in PVAS, suggesting a potential benefit of individualized treatment decisions in selected complex cases. Nevertheless, the inclusion of different AAV subtypes, including EGPA, may have introduced clinical heterogeneity given the differences in disease manifestations, prognosis and treatment approaches across these subgroups.

At the final follow-up assessment, remission was achieved significantly more frequently in patients managed without TPE than in those who underwent TPE. This finding is most plausibly explained by the markedly higher baseline disease severity in the TPE+ group. Because TPE was preferentially reserved for patients with more severe disease manifestations, particularly severe kidney involvement, direct comparisons between the TPE+ and TPE−groups should be interpreted cautiously due to potential confounding by indication. Overall, the observed results suggest that although TPE was associated with a reduction in PVAS among patients with more severe presentations, remission outcomes were more strongly influenced by baseline disease characteristics than by the use of TPE itself. Furthermore, all patients in the TPE group received intensive induction immunosuppressive therapy with pulse methylprednisolone and cyclophosphamide, and approximately half also received rituximab, consistent with current recommendations for severe AAV. Therefore, the observed clinical improvement should be interpreted as the result of a multimodal treatment strategy in which TPE served as an adjunctive therapy rather than as a standalone intervention.

The MEPEX study compared plasma exchange with intravenous pulse methylprednisolone in adult patients with severe ANCA-associated vasculitis and advanced kidney involvement, with both groups receiving oral cyclophosphamide. Although the study showed improved short-term renal survival in the plasma exchange group, long-term outcomes, including kidney failure, mortality and relapse rates, were similar between groups at four years of follow-up [15]. Similarly, another study in patients with GPA and moderate to severe kidney disease reported improved kidney survival in patients receiving plasma exchange in combination with standard immunosuppressive therapy, but no survival benefit was observed [20]. Consistent with these observations, our cohort also demonstrated improvement in kidney and clinical parameters following TPE. When we analysed the patients with markedly reduced baseline GFR (<15 mL/min/1.73 m²), two required concurrent haemodialysis; one (patient 9) died due to catheter-related sepsis, while the other (patient 2) progressed to dialysis dependency and subsequently underwent kidney transplantation. These findings underscore the severity of kidney involvement at presentation and illustrate the limitations of current therapeutic strategies in preventing irreversible kidney damage despite aggressive multimodal treatment. Furthermore, differences in follow-up duration between the groups restrict definitive conclusions regarding long-term renal survival, relapse risk and late disease-related complications.

A recent meta-analysis of nine randomized trials, including MEPEX and PEXIVAS, reported that plasma exchange reduced the one-year risk of end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), particularly in patients with high baseline creatinine levels (>5.7 mg/dl), but had no effect on mortality and was associated with an increased risk of serious infections [21]. In paediatric patients, absolute creatinine thresholds used in adults may represent relatively high cut-off values and may fail to capture clinically severe kidney involvement at earlier stages; in our cohort, TPE was initiated at lower creatinine levels (baseline median 3.34 mg/dl, IQR 1.8–5.3) in patients who nonetheless had substantial kidney impairment.

Previous studies have shown that TPE has primarily been employed in patients with severe and life-threatening manifestations of ANCA-associated vasculitis, most commonly in the presence of rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis and, less frequently, diffuse alveolar haemorrhage [22]. Consistent with previous reports, TPE was most frequently applied for severe kidney involvement, while additional indications such as multi-organ failure, extensive skin necrosis and gastrointestinal bleeding reflected the heterogeneity of clinical presentations [23].

A clear superiority in efficacy between membrane-based and centrifugation-based TPE methods was not shown, with the main reported difference being the duration of treatment [24]. In our cohort, membrane filtration-based TPE was used in all patients except one who underwent centrifugation-based TPE. As there are currently no standard guidelines regarding the preferred TPE method in paediatric AAV, the choice of technique reflects centre-specific practices.

In a large single-centre paediatric intensive care unit experience reported by Ekinci et al., comprising 328 critically ill children who underwent a total of 1528 TPE sessions, complications were observed in 18.7% of procedures and were predominantly haemodynamic and catheter-related, while mortality was mainly driven by disease severity and the number of organ failures rather than the TPE procedure itself [25]. Similarly, in a retrospective analysis by Şık et al. including 135 critically ill paediatric patients, TPE was reported to be generally well tolerated, with the most frequent adverse events being circuit clotting (6.4%), vascular access failure (4.9%), hypotension (1.4%), nausea and vomiting (1.2%), hypocalcaemia (1.4%) and urticaria or pruritus (0.9%), and no procedure-related mortality was observed [26]. Large-scale registry data from the World Apheresis Association suggest that therapeutic apheresis is generally well tolerated, with adverse events reported in ∼14% of procedures. Most of these events were mild or moderate in severity, including vascular access complications, hypotension, tingling, urticaria and nausea. Severe adverse events such as syncope, arrhythmia, or those requiring discontinuation of the procedure occurred in <0.5% of cases [14]. These findings are consistent with the safety profile observed in our cohort, where complications such as nausea, catheter dysfunction, hypocalcaemia and coagulopathy were noted. In our series, one patient (patient 9) who underwent TPE in the setting of multi-organ failure died due to Pseudomonal catheter-related sepsis, reflecting the heightened risk of bloodstream infections reported by Bruun et al. in patients with AAV, particularly those receiving haemodialysis or plasma exchange [27]. In that nationwide cohort, central venous catheter–related interventions were identified as major risk factors, with pathogens including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, underscoring the vulnerability of AAV patients to severe catheter-associated infections during extracorporeal therapies and the need for meticulous catheter care and infection surveillance.

This study has several limitations, including non-randomized treatment allocation largely driven by disease severity, introducing selection bias and limiting causal inference regarding the effect of TPE. The small sample size reduces statistical power and restricts subgroup analyses. PVAS scoring may be subject to interobserver variability, and although follow-up was adequate for short- and medium-term outcomes, it precludes firm conclusions on long-term renal survival, late relapse, or delayed complications. Time to remission and cumulative corticosteroid exposure could not be systematically evaluated because these variables were not consistently documented across participating centres. Despite these limitations, the rarity of paediatric AAV and the infrequent use of TPE in children make large prospective studies difficult to conduct; therefore, this multicentre cohort provides valuable real-world evidence and meaningful clinical insights into the use of TPE in childhood-onset AAV.

In conclusion, this study adds to the limited paediatric evidence supporting the use of TPE as an adjunctive therapy in selected children with severe or refractory AAV, while also highlighting its potential risks. Although TPE may be considered in line with current ASFA recommendations, the optimal timing, frequency and duration of therapy in paediatric AAV remain undefined. The applicability of adult trial data to children is uncertain, given fundamental differences in disease biology, comorbidity profiles and long-term outcome expectations. Accordingly, paediatric-specific evidence and continued accumulation of real-world clinical experience are essential to better define the role of TPE and to guide therapeutic decision-making in this vulnerable population.

Evaluation of a human 3D multi-spheroid model derived from SH-SY5Y cells for cytotoxicity testing
Authors: Lenka Ticha, Jan Capek, Antonin Vlcek, Pavlina Nyvltova, Stepanka Jelinkova, Jiri Handl, Tomas Rousar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
3D culture models have been extensively used in cell testing. Thus, we aimed to develop a novel cellular model of multiple spheroids derived from neuronal SH-SY5Y cells. We observed that poly-D-lysine coating can induce formation of a population of spheroids, i.e. the density of 20 × 103 of SH-SY5Y cells per well led to formation of approximately 300 and 150 spheroids after 4 and 7 days of incubation, respectively. In the spheroids, we found induced expressions of ICAM-1, connexin 26 and neuron-specific enolase proteins demonstrating increased differentiation of SH-SY5Y cells compared to the cells grown as a monolayer. Finally, we aimed to utilize the new culture model in toxicity testing. Thus, we incubated the cells with CdCl2, HgCl2 and PbCl2 at doses up to 1000 µM for 24–48 h. After the treatment, we measured glutathione levels, dehydrogenase activity, and ATP levels in SH-SY5Y cells grown in both 2D and 3D arrangements showing that the cells grown as monolayers were significantly more susceptible to the toxic effects of heavy metals. In conclusion, we developed a novel cell culture model of multiple spheroids derived from SH-SY5Y cells which can be very useful tool in cellular testing in future.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-56896-6.

Introduction

The cell cultures have been cultivated in vitro for many years. Cell cultivation in two-dimensional (2D) arrangement on flat adherent surfaces have been a common approach in in vitro experiments because of high reproducibility and relatively low cost1. The disadvantage of the use of 2D cultures is difference in cell morphology and molecular mechanisms compared to the conditions in situ. Therefore, the three-dimensional (3D) cultivation is a technique that has become used increasingly in recent years2. Main advantage of 3D culture models is the development of higher level of cell organization, larger occurrence of cell-cell and cell-environment interactions mimicking in vivo conditions more closely compared to conventional 2D cultures3.

The development of cellular spheroids can be accomplished by the special shape of the culture vessel or by a surface treatment4,5. To change the properties of cell culture surfaces, coating is routinely applied using various materials. Poly-D-lysine (PDL) is a synthetic cationic polymer commonly used as a coating agent to promote cell adhesion6,7. It is a chiral form of alpha poly-lysine which is soluble at pH 7.0 and the positively charged amino groups attract cells by electrostatic interactions with the negatively charged cell membranes8.

The neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y cell line was established as the third subclone of the SK-N-SH cell line which was created in 1970 from a bone marrow biopsy of a 4-year-old female9. The SH-SY5Y cells have a wide range of applications, i.e. in studies focused on neuronal metabolism10, cell differentiation11, neurodegenerative diseases12, neurotoxicity13, and neuroprotection14. Heavy metals such as cadmium, mercury, lead, or arsenic, defined as elements with a high atomic density that vary in their chemical properties, constitute a persistent environmental hazard with a toxic effect on human health15,16. Although SH-SY5Y spheroidal models have been described previously, their application as a 3D model for neurotoxicity studies remains limited relatively to 2D cultures.

To address the request on the development of new 3D cellular models, the present study aimed to develop and characterize a model of multiple spheroids derived from SH-SY5Y cells using PDL coating of a surface. The cellular differentiation and expression of intercellular protein was assessed in the spheroids. Then, the biological effects induced by CdCl2, HgCl2, and PbCl2 were compared between SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer and in newly prepared spheroids. For the evaluation of cell impairment, dehydrogenase activity, glutathione levels and ATP production were measured.

Materials and methods

SH-SY5Y cell culture

All materials for cell culture and treatment were purchased from Merck (USA) if not otherwise specified. The human neuroblastoma cell line SH-SY5Y (CRL-2266) was purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco′s Modified Eagle′s Medium/Ham′s Nutrient Mixture F12 (DMEM/F12 = 1:1) supplemented with 15% (v/v) fetal bovine serum, 50 µg·mL− 1 penicillin/streptomycin, 2 mM L-glutamine, and 1% (v/v) non-essential amino acids. The cells were maintained at 37 °C in a sterile, humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2. For all the experiments, SH-SY5Y cells at passages 4–15 were used. SH-SY5Y cells were tested for mycoplasma contamination using the MycoAlert Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Lonza, Switzerland). All cells used in the experiments were mycoplasma-free. In addition, short tandem repeat (STR) analysis was used for SH-SY5Y cells authentication in GeneriBiotech (Czech Republic). The STR analysis proved 100% conformity of SH-SY5Y cells with the reference standard.

For SH-SY5Y spheroid formation, the poly-D-lysine (PDL; A3890401, Gibco, USA) coating protocol was optimized. Concentrations 1, 10, 25, 50, and 100 µg·mL− 1 PDL, incubation times 15, 30, 45, and 60 min and drying times 30, 60, and 120 min were tested. No differences were observed among the tested incubation and drying times. Therefore, an incubation period of 15 min and drying time of 30 min were selected for the coating procedure. The optimal concentration was determined to be 10 µg·mL− 1 PDL. SH-SY5Y spheroids were formed in flat-bottom 96-well plates and cell culture chamber slides (SPL Life Sciences, South Korea) coated with 10 µg·mL− 1 PDL. For the surface coating, the PDL stock solution was diluted with DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM) to a working concentration of 10 µg·mL− 1 PDL. Subsequently, 100 µL of a PDL working solution was added to each well of the 96-well plate and incubated for 15 min. The solution was then removed, and each well was washed three times with 150 µL of sterile water for cell cultures (Roth, Germany). Finally, the well plate was dried in the laminar flow hood at laboratory temperature for 30 min.

Treatments of cells grown in 2D and 3D

SH-SY5Y cells in 2D arrangement were seeded in 100 µL of cell culture medium into flat-bottom 96-well plates at a density of 20 × 103 cells per well. The cells were seeded for 24 h and then treated with 0–1000 µM CdCl2, HgCl2, or PbCl2 for 24–48 h.

For spheroid exposure, the cells were seeded in 90 µL of cell culture medium at a density of 20 × 103 cells per well. Spheroids were formed for four days and then treated with 10 µL CdCl2, HgCl2, or PbCl2 in a final concentration 0–1000 µM for 24–48 h. The SPARK CYTO microplate reader (Tecan, Austria) was used to analyze the morphological properties of the untreated spheroids. After four to seven days of spheroid cultivation in a 96-well plate, the plate was inserted into the SPARK CYTO microplate reader (Tecan, Austria), and the spheroids in each well were counted, their size and eccentricity were measured, and the field of view of the entire well was captured.

For both 2D and 3D arrangements, the stock solutions of heavy metals were prepared in ultra-sterile water for cell cultures (Roth, Germany). Working solutions were prepared in Dulbecco′s Modified Eagle′s Medium/Ham′s Nutrient Mixture F12 without phenol red (DMEM/F12 = 1:1). Non-treated cells or spheroids were used as a negative control.

Live cell imaging

The BioStation IM-Q system (Nikon, Japan) consists of an integrated microscope, CCD camera, and incubation unit, enabling long-term live-cell imaging and data acquisition. Cells were seeded into a 4-well chamber slide (Ibidi, Germany) pre‑coated with PDL (10 µg·mL− 1). A total of 25 × 103 SH‑SY5Y cells in 700 µL of SH-SY5Y culture medium were seeded into each well. Phase‑contrast time‑lapse imaging was performed for up to five days.

Quantitative capillary western immunoassay

Expressions of intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1), connexin 26 (Cx26), and neuron-specific enolase (NSE) were detected using Capillary Western Immunoassay performed on the Wes system using a 12–230 kDa Separation Module according to the manufacturer´s instructions (Protein Simple, USA). Briefly, SH-SY5Y cells were washed in DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM), lysed in RIPA Lysis Buffer (30 min; 4 °C), centrifuged (10000g; 10 min; 4 °C), and the supernatant was loaded into the Capillary Western Immunoassay. Levels of Cx26 (1:50; Proteintech, UK), ICAM-1 (1:100; Proteintech, UK), and NSE (1:800; Proteintech, UK) expression were normalized using the reference protein β-actin (1:500; Merck, USA). The peaks were analyzed using Compass software (Protein Simple, USA). For the distinction of signals from the background, the following criteria were used. The peak height had to be higher or equal to 1000 and the peak signal-to-noise ratio had to be higher than or equal to 10. Fluorescence microscopy

Viability of SH-SY5Y cells and presence of necrotic core in spheroids were assessed using the LIVE/DEAD Cell Assay (Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA) following the modified manufacturer’s protocol. SH-SY5Y spheroids were rinsed with DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM; 37 °C) and incubated for 15 min (37 °C, dark) in FBS-free medium containing calcein-AM and propidium iodide (PI). Fluorescence imaging was performed using a SPARK CYTO microplate reader (Tecan, Austria) equipped with a 3D live-cell imaging module. Viable SH-SY5Y were identified by green fluorescence corresponding to intracellular esterase activity, whereas red fluorescence indicated the loss of cell membrane integrity.

Visualization of ICAM-1 and Cx26 molecules was performed by immunofluorescence staining using specific primary antibodies and fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies. SH-SY5Y cells were fixed with 3.7% (v/v) formaldehyde for 5 min (37 °C, dark) and permeabilized with 0.1% (v/v) Triton X-100 for 10 min (37 °C, dark). After washing with DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM; 37 °C), cells were blocked with 1% (v/v) BSA for 20 min (37 °C, dark). This was followed by incubation with primary polyclonal rabbit antibodies against ICAM-1 (Proteintech, UK; 1:100) and Cx26 (Proteintech, UK; 1:250) diluted in 1% (v/v) BSA. After washing with 1% (v/v) BSA, a secondary FITC-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG antibody (Proteintech, UK; 1:200) was added and incubated for 24 h (4 °C, dark). After incubation, cells were washed three times with heated DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM; 37 °C).

Phalloidin-FITC (Gibco, USA) staining of actin filaments was performed in SH-SY5Y cells. Cells were cultured in 200 µL of cell culture medium in cell culture chamber slides at a density of 25 × 103 cells per well for 2D and 35 × 103 cells for 3D. After the treatment, cells were fixed with 3.7% (v/v) formaldehyde for 5 min at 37 °C. The cells were then permeabilized with 0.1% (v/v) Triton X-100 for 15 min at 37 °C. Subsequently, cells were incubated with phalloidin-FITC (1 µM) for 30 min at 37 °C to visualize actin filaments. After 30 min of phalloidin-FITC loading, 10 µL of Hoechst 33258 solution was added to the cells. The final concentration of Hoechst 33258 in a well was 2 µg·mL− 1. After incubation, cells were washed two times with DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM; 37 °C).

Actin filaments, protein expression (FITC filter, λEx/Em = 480/30 nm), and cell nuclei (DAPI filter, λEx/Em = 375/28 nm) were visualized with a Nikon Eclipse 80i fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan).

Glutathione levels

Intracellular glutathione levels were measured using an optimized monochlorobimane (MCB) assay17. The working solution of MCB was prepared by dissolution in DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM). After the treatment with the tested compounds, 20 µL of MCB solution was added to cells in each well of a 96-well plate, and the measurement of fluorescence intensity (λEx/Em = 394/490 nm) started immediately. The final concentration of MCB in each well was 40 µM. The fluorescence intensity was measured in 2-min cycles for 10 min using a SPARK CYTO microplate reader (Tecan, Austria). GSH levels were expressed as a percentage relative to GSH levels in untreated cells (control = 100%). In addition, we evaluated the occurrence of interference of tested compounds with the measurement of GSH, ATP and dehydrogenase activity. At the tested concentrations, no significant interference of the tested compounds with any of the used assays was observed.

Dehydrogenase activity

Cell dehydrogenase activity was measured using the Resazurin assay. Resazurin sodium salt (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was dissolved in DPBS (pH 7.0; 1 mM) to make a stock solution. Then, 20 µL of the stock solution was added into each well of a 96-well plate to reach a final concentration of 50 µg·mL− 1 of resazurin. The fluorescence intensity (λEx/Em = 530/590 nm) was measured in 2-min cycles for 20 min using a SPARK CYTO microplate reader (Tecan, Austria). Dehydrogenase activity was expressed as a percentage relative to dehydrogenase activity in untreated cells (control = 100%).

ATP levels

The intracellular ATP levels were detected using a luciferase luminescence assay. After the treatment, 70 µL of the cell culture medium was replaced with cold 1% (v/v) Triton X-100, incubated for 10 min, and centrifuged (4800g; 5 min; 4 °C). For ATP detection, 90 µL of reaction solution was added to 10 µL of a cell lysate. Reaction solution consisted of 20× reaction buffer (500 mM Tricine buffer, pH 7.8; 10 mM MgSO4), 5 mg·mL− 1 luciferase, 10 mM D-luciferin, 0.1 M DTT, and distilled water. Luminometric measurement was performed at λEm = 550 nm using a SPARK CYTO microplate reader (Tecan, Austria). The intracellular ATP levels were expressed as a percentage relative to untreated cells (control = 100%).

Statistical analysis

All spectrometric and luminometric experiments were repeated at least three-times independently, except for the quantitative capillary western immunoassay, which was performed two times independently. All measurements were performed in multiplets (n = 2–3). The results are expressed as (mean ± SD). Statistical analysis was performed using OriginPro 9.0.0 (OriginLab, USA). Statistical significance was analyzed using one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni’s test to compare the results at the significance level p = 0.05. Graphs were generated using OriginPro 9.0.0 (OriginLab, USA). Figures were assembled and labeled in Microsoft PowerPoint (Microsoft, USA).

Results

Establishment of a SH-SY5Y cell derived spheroid model

In this study, we aimed to develop a biological model of spheroidal population derived from the SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cell line. Presently, poly-D-lysine (PDL) has been used only for surface modification in 2D cell cultures8,16. In contrast, we used a novel approach using PDL surface coating for the formation of a multi-spheroid SH-SY5Y model. We assessed the spheroids grown for up to seven days according to the optimal duration for formation of spheroids reported by other authors18,19.

For surface coating with PDL, other authors used various experimental protocols differing in PDL concentrations, duration of PDL incubation in a well and drying of the coated surface8,20,21. To select optimal conditions of PDL use for spheroid formation, we evaluated incubation times of PDL coating in 96-well plates and surface drying (see the experimental part). Finally, we chose 15 min of PDL incubation and 30 min of surface drying as optimal for following experiments.

Firstly, we estimated the concentration of 1–100 µg·mL− 1 PDL for surface coatings of 96-well plates. To evaluate the functional state of cells in spheroids, we used the microscopic observation of morphological parameters and measurement of glutathione levels (Fig. 1). After three days of incubation of SH-SY5Y cells in wells coated with PDL, we found no formation of spheroids in any of the tested PDL concentrations (data not shown). At day 4, the multiple small spheroids formed and the cells were allowed to grow until day 7. According to the presented results (Fig. 1a), the spheroids were formed only in wells coated with at least 10 µg·mL− 1 PDL. Since the number of spheroids was comparable in wells coated with 10–100 µg·mL− 1 PDL (Supplementary Table S1), we chose 10 µg·mL− 1 PDL concentration as suitable for coating of wells in all following experiments.

Next, we assessed the density of SH-SY5Y cells for optimal spheroid formation. We tested the density in the range of (5–20) × 103 cells per well for up to seven days (Fig. 1b). The spheroids were counted and the projected area was measured. According to the measured parameters, we observed that the increasing density of cells was positively correlated with the size of spheroids but negatively correlated with the number of spheroids. The data are supported by the time-lapse record of spheroid formation obtained using live view cell microscopy (Supplementary Video S2). Although a certain heterogeneity of spheroids in size within individual wells was observed, the overall pattern of spheroidal formation was consistent across experiments, indicating good reproducibility of the established biological model. In addition to the size and the number of spheroids, we measured the eccentricity of spheroids (Supplementary Table S3). After four days, the eccentricity of spheroids at densities of 5 × 103, 10 × 103, and 20 × 103 cells per well reached 0.60, 0.55, and 0.48, respectively. After seven days of culture, the eccentricity of spheroids became to be very similar across all seeding densities, ranging between 0.54 and 0.56.

To evaluate the functional state of SH-SY5Y cells in spheroids, we measured the intracellular glutathione levels serving as an indirect marker of functional status and cellular proliferation in time (Fig. 1c). Between days 4 and 7, we observed increasing GSH levels demonstrating cellular proliferation. According to the two-way ANOVA analysis, we found a significant influence of cultivation time (p < 0.001) and seeding density (p < 0.001) on the GSH level. To confirm the absence of a necrotic core within the population of spheroids, LIVE/DEAD staining was performed using Calcein AM and propidium iodide (Fig. 1d). Microphotographs clearly demonstrate that the cells within the spheroids exhibit esterase activity, and no necrotic core is present. Based on the results, we chose the density of 20 × 103 SH-SY5Y cells per well in 96-well plates as optimal for additional use of the PDL-derived 3D culture model.

After establishment of culture conditions for optimal SH-SY5Y spheroid formation, we aimed to compare the developed SH-SY5Y-derived 3D model with SH-SY5Y cells grown in 2D arrangement. Because the optimal cell growth is linked to development of specific intercellular connections, we assessed the occurrence of specific molecules in both culture models of SH-SY5Y cells. We chose ICAM-1 and Cx26 as suitable markers of development of permanent intercellular connections. The expression of both proteins was detected using fluorescence microscopy and a capillary immunoassay. According to the microscopical observation, the presence of both ICAM-1 (Fig. 2a) and Cx26 (Fig. 2b) molecules was increased in SH-SY5Y spheroids grown for seven days compared to the cells grown in a monolayer. The results on protein levels of ICAM-1 and Cx26 (Fig. 2c) measured in the cells supported the microscopy observations. The expressions of ICAM-1 and Cx26 were increased to 220% and 145%, respectively, in spheroids compared to SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer.

We decided to characterize the changes of neuron-specific enolase (NSE) levels serving as a specific marker of neuronal differentiation in SH-SY5Y cells22,23. We compared NSE expressions in SH-SY5Y cells between 2D and 3D arrangements grown for seven days (Fig. 2c). The results showed that NSE level was more than 2.5times higher in spheroids compared to the cells grown in a monolayer.

Toxicity of heavy metals in 2D or 3D grown SH-SY5Y cells

To evaluate the biological properties of the developed 3D culture model of SH-SY5Y cells, we aimed to use the spheroids for a toxicity testing and to compare the outcomes with those obtained from SH-SY5Y cells grown in 2D. Because the neurotoxicity of heavy metals has been of increasing importance, we chose three potent inducers of neurotoxicity, i.e. CdCl2, HgCl2, and PbCl2. We treated the SH-SY5Y cells cultured in 2D or 3D arrangement with toxic compounds for 24–48 h. We used the measurements of GSH levels, dehydrogenase activity, and ATP levels to characterize the induced biological changes and to compare the susceptibility to heavy metals between 2D and 3D grown SH-SY5Y cells.

Based on the preliminary testing of toxicity of CdCl2, HgCl2, and PbCl2 in SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer (Supplementary Table S4), for following experiments we chose two concentrations for each heavy metal, i.e. the lowest concentration inducing any biological effect, and the lowest concentration inducing cellular impairment.

Glutathione levels

After the treatment of SH-SY5Y cells with three heavy metals, we observed both elevations and reductions of GSH levels generally (Fig. 3a). In cells grown in 2D treated with lower concentrations of heavy metals, we detected significant increases in GSH levels after treatments with 5 µM CdCl2 (p < 0.001), 5 µM HgCl2 (p < 0.001), and 5 µM PbCl2 (p < 0.05) for 24 h compared to untreated cells. After 48 h, GSH levels remained significantly elevated after treatments with both 5 µM CdCl2 (p < 0.001) and 5 µM HgCl2 (p < 0.001) compared to controls. In spheroids, 5 µM HgCl2 induced an increase of GSH levels to 119% (p < 0.001) after 24 h, compared to controls. After 48 h, the increase of GSH levels in SH-SY5Y spheroids after treatment with 5 µM HgCl2 was even pronounced to 143% (p < 0.001), compared to untreated cells.

The treatment of SH-SY5Y cells with higher concentrations of heavy metals induced larger extent of cell impairment in comparison to 5 µM CdCl2, 5 µM HgCl2, and 5 µM PbCl2 treatments. After 24 h, the treatments of the cells cultured in 2D with 75 µM CdCl2 or 50 µM HgCl2 reduced GSH levels to 65% (p < 0.001) and 69% (p < 0.001) of that in controls, respectively. On the other hand, 500 µM PbCl2 induced a transient increase in GSH levels to 153% (p < 0.001) after 24 h, compared to 2D controls. After exposure to 75 µM CdCl2 or 50 µM HgCl2, GSH levels in the cells grown in 2D decreased to 29% (p < 0.001) and 58% (p < 0.001) of controls, respectively, after 48 h. After exposure to 500 µM PbCl2, the GSH levels in 2D grown cells remained slightly elevated at 115% (p < 0.05) of controls after 48 h.

Interestingly, in the case of SH-SY5Y spheroids, we observed significantly decreased GSH levels (p < 0.001) only after 75 µM CdCl2 treatment for 48 h. The incubation with 25 µM cisplatin (CisPt) caused significant damage of SH-SY5Y cells grown as either monolayer or spheroids at both time durations. In conclusion, the obtained results demonstrated that SH-SY5Y cells cultured in 3D were less sensitive to heavy metal toxicity compared to the 2D arrangement.

Mitochondrial activity

We evaluated the mitochondrial function using two tests, i.e. the dehydrogenase activity measurement and ATP assay (Fig. 3a). Firstly, we measured dehydrogenase activity. After the testing of biological effects induced by 5 µM treatments, both increases and decreases in dehydrogenase activity were observed in SH-SY5Y cells grown in 2D or 3D. After 24 h, we found that exposure to 5 µM CdCl2 (p < 0.001) or 5 µM HgCl2 (p < 0.001) significantly increased dehydrogenase activity in SH-SY5Y cells cultured in 2D arrangement. After 48 h, the cells grown in 2D and exposed to 5 µM CdCl2 or 5 µM HgCl2 showed further increase to 134% (p < 0.001) and 137% (p < 0.001), respectively, compared to controls. The treatment of spheroids with 5 µM CdCl2 and 5 µM PbCl2 did not cause any significant change in dehydrogenase activity after both 24 and 48 h. Spheroids exposed to 5 µM HgCl2 exhibited a significant reduction in dehydrogenase activity (p < 0.001) after both 24 and 48 h.

After the treatment of SH-SY5Y cells grown in 2D with 75 µM CdCl2 or 50 µM HgCl2 for 24 h, we found significant reduction of dehydrogenase activity even deepening to 15% (p < 0.001) and 30% (p < 0.001), respectively, compared to untreated cells after 48 h. In the case of SH-SY5Y spheroids, 75 µM CdCl2 and 50 µM HgCl2 treatments induced significant (p < 0.001) reductions of dehydrogenase activities after both time durations, compared to controls. However, the extent of the impairment was lower compared to 2D grown cells. No significant changes in dehydrogenase activity were observed after treatment of cells with 500 µM PbCl2 for 24 h, but the exposure for 48 h induced a significant decrease in dehydrogenase activity in both 2D (p < 0.001) and 3D (p < 0.001) cultured SH-SY5Y cells.

Finally, we used a luminometric assay for measurement of ATP levels. The ATP assay has been used as a routine test for assessment of the mitochondrial function, especially in cells grown in 3D24,25. The advantage of the experimental protocol is that the cell membranes of all the cells are lysed and the ATP levels can be detected in a solution. ATP levels in cells treated with CdCl2, HgCl2, or PbCl2 in both 2D and 3D arrangements corresponded to the dehydrogenase activity measured after 24 and 48 h predominantly. For instance, 75 µM CdCl2, 50 µM HgCl2 and 25 µM CisPt treatments induced significant damage in SH-SY5Y cells. On the other hand, the most important difference was that no changes in ATP levels were observed after 5 µM HgCl2. In conclusion, comparing the extent of cellular damage induced by heavy metals, we found that the SH-SY5Y cells cultured as spheroids were less sensitive to heavy metal toxicity compared to those grown in 2D arrangement.

In addition to biochemical assays, we observed the morphological changes in SH-SY5Y cells grown in 2D and 3D arrangements using phase contrast and fluorescence microscopy after incubation with 75 µM CdCl2, 50 µM HgCl2, or 500 µM PbCl2 for 24 h (Fig. 3b). We found that the cells grown in 2D exhibited reduced adherence to the surface and lower cell density in comparison to untreated cells treated after the treatments with CdCl2 or HgCl2. In spheroids exposed to CdCl2, we revealed a slight disruption of the spheroid compactness. We found even larger level of impairment of SH-SY5Y spheroids after the treatment with HgCl2. After PbCl2 treatment, we did not observe any morphological changes.

Discussion

2D cellular models have remained important tools for toxicity screening26. However, 2D models have certain limitations compared to the cells occurring in vivo27. Therefore, it is necessary to use 3D cellular models in toxicological testing because they provide more realistic simulation of cell~cell and cell~environment interactions occurring in vivo. Various methodologies have been developed for the formation of 3D neural cell models, including scaffold-free systems (e.g., spheroids, organoids) and scaffold-based systems (e.g., scaffolds, hydrogels, bioprinting)28–30. Spheroids belong to the mostly used 3D models in neural research ensuring differentiation of cells and mimicking the structure and function of human neural tissue31,32.

Spheroidal systems can vary in biological properties or differ in procedure of their preparation. Another difference is whether the experimental protocol lead to the preparation of a single spheroid or the formation of multiple spheroids in a well. The SH-SY5Y cell line has been widely used to investigate mechanisms of neurodegeneration or to evaluate the effect of therapeutics of Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s diseases33. Numerous publications have reported the formation of a single spheroid per well from SH-SY5Y cells prepared using hanging drop culture34, liquid overlay35, surface coating36, or the use of non-adhesive materials37. In addition, commercially available ultra-low attachment well plates are the most used tools for SH-SY5Y spheroid development38,39. There also exist neural multi-spheroid systems based on microarray platforms in which spheroid formation and spatial organization are predefined by the underlying microstructured array40. In contrast, in our model spheroids form self-assembly, and their organization emerges spontaneously without imposed microarray patterning.

Poly-D-lysine is a material that has been used for cell cultures frequently. Several papers demonstrated that PDL surface coating enhances the adhesion of cells cultured in a monolayer to plastic or glass surfaces20,41. Other studies reported that exposure of cells to PDL can lead to preferential cell-to-cell than cell-to-surface adhesion42,43. In scientific studies using monolayer-cultured rat primary neuronal cells8,44, human embryonic stem cell lines TE03 and TE0631, and mouse primary cortical neurons16, PDL coating was applied to improve the cell adhesion to a surface. In these studies, partial cell clustering and weaker cell adhesion were also observed.

In 3D culture systems, PDL has been used to stabilize pre-formed spheroids derived from rat primary neurons45 and human induced pluripotent stem cells46 on substrates used for imaging and functional recording. A few studies have combined PDL surface coating with special culture media to support the formation of multiple spheroids derived from Caki-1 and ACHN cells47, human adipose-derived stromal cells transdifferentiated into neural progenitor cells48, human kidney cancer stem cells, 769-P, 786-O, and Caki-2 cells49, and primary mouse cardiac cells50,51. To date, only Mauretti et al. (2017) have reported the use of PDL coating to promote the spontaneous formation of multiple spheroids from human progenitor cardiac cells52. In contrast to all above cited papers, our, here reported study is the first one when the use of PDL facilitates the formation of SH-SY5Y-derived population of spheroids in a single well.

To establish the multi-spheroid cellular model, we optimized PDL concentration, PDL coating time in wells, air-drying period of coated well-plates, and cell density of the seeding. Our procedure resulted in an optimal cost- and time-effective experimental protocol for coating with 10 µg·mL− 1 PDL for 15 min in a well, followed by washing and drying for 30 min. We observed that the most suitable density was 20 × 103 cells per well because the rate of spheroids formation was lower at densities of 5 × 103 and 10 × 103 cells per well. The concentration of PDL used in our study is similar to the papers of other authors who used various protocols for PDL surface coating at concentrations ranging from 1 to 1000 µg·mL− 1 PDL, and incubation times for coating in wells from 10 min to 24 h8,16,21,48.

To characterize 3D cellular models, determination of cellular adhesion molecules and extracellular matrix proteins have been commonly used53. In our study, to compare morphogenetic and neuronal characteristics between cells cultured in a monolayer and in spheroids, we assessed the expression levels of Cx26 and ICAM-1 proteins and observed an increase in both investigated markers. Cx26 plays an important role in intercellular communication through gap junctions, cell adhesion, spheroid formation and stabilization54. Expression of ICAM-1 on the cellular surface increases during inflammatory processes and is also involved in intercellular interactions in neural tissue55. The expression of both Cx26 and ICAM-1 tends to be higher in spheroids than in 2D cultures, which is considered as a general indicator of successful spheroid formation54,56.

The presence of differentiated cells in 3D culture models provides an advantage for research applications, especially for the use of the SH-SY5Y cells. To evaluate the neuronal differentiation, several markers can be assessed, including neuron specific enolase (NSE), an isomer upregulated in neurons with active axonal processes. According to previous studies54,57, NSE expression increases in differentiated SH-SY5Y cells. In our study, we observed an increased expression of NSE in spheroids indicating neuronal differentiation of the SH-SY5Y cells.

The necrotic core is a commonly observed issue in large spheroids58,59. The influence of spheroid size on the presence of a necrotic core has been reported in some studies in spheroids larger than 500 μm in diameter, although the critical size depends on the cell type60,61. The multilayer organization of spheroids and metabolic gradients also limit the diffusion of nutrients, metabolites, and tested compounds, which can cause reduced sensitivity of cells within the spheroid4,61. In our study, LIVE/DEAD staining confirmed the absence of the necrotic core in SH-SY5Y spheroid population. This finding is consistent with our expectation because of the spheroidal diameter smaller than 200 μm, ensuring more efficient diffusion of compounds compared to larger spheroids of about 600 μm in diameter62–65.

Our study demonstrates that the SH-SY5Y multi-spheroid model provides a reproducible in vitro system for assessing neurotoxicity and screening of potentially neuroprotective compounds in a more physiologically relevant context than conventional monolayer cultures. But the developed 3D model has also important limitations as it is based on a single neuroblastoma cell line and thus cannot recapitulate the cellular heterogeneity and complex interactions of the human brain, including glial and immune components. Nevertheless, by focusing on the establishment and functional characterization of this 3D model, we provide a relevant platform for studying cellular responses to potentially neurotoxic or neuroprotective compounds for future mechanistic studies.

The SH-SY5Y human neuroblastoma cell line has been commonly applied to study toxic mechanisms induced by various compounds, including heavy metals, representing a highly relevant issue for human health66,67. In the present study, we used CdCl2, HgCl2 and PbCl2 to compare the cellular responses of SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer and in a multi-spheroid model using three biochemical assays describing the rate of cellular metabolism and redox balance.

Cadmium is a widespread environmental toxicant with the capability to interact with thiol groups of biomolecules, including glutathione68,69. We detected a transient increase of dehydrogenase activity and GSH levels after 24 and 48 h exposure of SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer to CdCl2, which is consistent with previous findings in SH-SY5Y70, HK-271 and A2780 cells72. These observations indicate that low CdCl2 concentrations can trigger a transient increase in mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity prior to the functional impairment. On the other hand, several studies reported a time- or dose-dependent decrease in dehydrogenase activity after exposure of SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer to CdCl266,67,73. The transient increase of cellular GSH levels together with dehydrogenase activity may represent a defensive cellular response to the presence of a toxic compound. In this context, upregulation of glutathione metabolism observed in previous studies may promote GSH synthesis as a compensatory mechanism in SH-SY5Y cells68,70. Based on these observations, we hypothesize that similar mechanisms underlie the GSH increase after HgCl2 and PbCl2 exposure found in our study. In our study, we also observed a time- and dose-dependent decrease in ATP levels following exposure of 2D cultured SH-SY5Y cells to CdCl2, which is in accordance with the findings of Bovio et al. (2021) showing that CdCl2 induces mitochondrial dysfunction, resulting in ATP depletion in SH-SY5Y cells68. In contrast, in our study, exposure of 3D-cultured SH-SY5Y cells to CdCl2 led to only a modest metabolic disturbance, as GSH levels, dehydrogenase activity, and ATP levels were less affected than in 2D model. In addition, 75 µM CdCl2 induced a loss of compactness in SH-SY5Y spheroids after 48 h.

Mercury is a toxicant present in an environment. It has the affinity for sulfhydryl groups affecting intracellular glutathione balance and contributing to oxidative stress74–76. In our study, HgCl2 induced a transient increase in GSH levels in SH-SY5Y cells grown in 2D, suggesting an early adaptive response of the antioxidant system. Previous studies reported variable effects of HgCl2 on SH-SY5Y metabolism, ranging from unchanged GSH levels75 to a dose-dependent reduction of GSH levels associated with ROS production67,77. According to our results, the exposure of 2D cultured SH-SY5Y cells to HgCl2 caused a transient increase in dehydrogenase activity after 24 and 48 h and a decrease in ATP levels after 48 h, indicating a compensatory activation of mitochondrial enzymes preceding functional impairment. This pattern is consistent with previous reports describing a dose- and time- dependent reduction in dehydrogenase activity, supporting the role of mitochondrial dysfunction in mercury toxicity78,79. On the other hand, in our study, the exposure of SH-SY5Y spheroids to HgCl2 had a less pronounced effect on GSH levels, dehydrogenase activity, and ATP levels than in the 2D model.

Lead is an environmental pollutant inducing oxidative stress, cell death, mitochondrial dysfunction, and disruption of calcium homeostasis80,81. In the present study, PbCl2 induced a slight, transient increase in GSH levels in SH-SY5Y cells grown in a monolayer compared to CdCl2 or HgCl2, whereas some previous studies reported GSH depletion and redox imbalance in 2D cultured SH-SY5Y cells at concentrations 12.5 and 25 µM of lead acetate after 24 h82–84. We found that in 2D SH-SY5Y cells, PbCl2 reduced dehydrogenase activity at concentrations 500 µM and 1000 µM and decreased ATP levels at 500 µM after 48 h. Our findings can be supported by outcomes of several reported papers where Pb exposure led to a dose‑dependent decrease in dehydrogenase activity85–87. In our study, in the SH-SY5Y multi-spheroid model, GSH levels, dehydrogenase activity, and ATP levels showed only minor changes, indicating a lower susceptibility of the cells grown in spheroids compared to a monolayer.

In line with these observations, a detailed mechanistic analysis of mitochondrial regulation and redox adaptation exceeded the primary aim of the present study and therefore may be addressed in future studies. Although the effects of CdCl2, HgCl2, and PbCl2 have been widely studied in 2D SH-SY5Y cultures, their toxicity in neuronal spheroid models remains unexplored. In comparison to 2D cultures, lower sensitivity of cells grown in spheroids observed in our study is consistent with previous reports of increased resistance of cells cultured in 3D systems, which has been attributed to diffusion-limited penetration of compounds through the compact spheroid architecture and the presence of a metabolically less active cell population in the spheroid core88,89. However, these mechanisms need to be further examined.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a 3D culture model of multiple spheroids derived from the SH-SY5Y cell line using PDL coating. In the spheroids, the inductions of expression of a differentiation marker NSE, intercellular connection molecule ICAM-1 and connexin 26 were observed. To demonstrate the applicability of the newly developed spheroids model for a toxicological assessment, we compared the biological effects induced by CdCl2, HgCl2 and PbCl2 in SH-SY5Y cells grown either in spheroids or in a monolayer. Overall, SH-SY5Y cells cultured in the 3D model exhibited lower sensitivity to effects of CdCl2, HgCl2 and PbCl2 compared to the cells grown in 2D arrangement. We conclude that we developed a novel and reproducible 3D culture model of multiple spheroids derived from SH-SY5Y cells having several essential advantages in comparison to the use of single spheroids. On the other hand, its applicability remains limited regarding the use of a single neuroblastoma cell line. Therefore, this model should be regarded as a useful complementary tool for neurotoxicity research and as a methodological basis that can be further characterized and validated in future studies.

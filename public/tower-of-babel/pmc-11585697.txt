1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 enhances the expression of phosphorus transporters via vitamin D receptor in ligated duodenal loops of Arbor Acres male broilers
Authors: Weiyun Zhang, Yanqiang Geng, Ke Yang, Yangyang Hu, Mengxiao Xue, Xiaoyan Cui, Liyang Zhang, Shengchen Wang, Tingting Li, Xugang Luo, Yun Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 [1,25-(OH)2D3] could promote phosphorus (P) absorption in the duodenum of broilers. The vitamin D receptor (VDR) mediates the action of 1,25-(OH)2D3. However, it remains unknown whether and how VDR is involved in promoting P absorption in the duodenum of broilers by 1,25-(OH)2D3. The objective of the current study was to investigate the effect of added 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels on P absorption, mRNA or protein expression levels of VDR, retinoid X receptor (RXR), type IIb sodium-phosphate cotransporter (NaPi-IIb), inorganic phosphate transporter (PiT) 1 and PiT-2, and the enrichments of VDR bound to DNA promoters of the above P transporters in the ligated duodenal loops of Arbor Acres male broilers. The duodenal loops were perfused with solutions (pH = 6.0) containing 0 (control), 12.5, or 25.0 ng/L of 1,25-(OH)2D3 for up to 40 min. The results indicated that the addition of either 12.5 or 25.0 ng/L 1,25-(OH)2D3 to the perfusates increased (P < 0.04) P absorption percentage and rate, as well as the mRNA expression levels of VDR, RXR, NaPi-IIb, and PiT-2, alongside protein expression levels of VDR, NaPi-IIb, and PiT-2, whereas it did not affect (P > 0.10) PiT-1 mRNA and protein expression levels. Moreover, 1,25-(OH)2D3 administration increased (P < 0.01) the enrichments of VDR bound to NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 DNA promoter regions in the ligated duodenal loops of broilers, which were in line with their mRNA expression levels. These findings suggest that 1,25-(OH)2D3 increased P absorption possibly through VDR-mediated transactivation of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 genes in the duodenum of Arbor Acres male broilers.

Introduction

Phosphorus (P) plays an important role in animal metabolic processes, such as bone mineralization, energy metabolism, and membrane integrity (Calvo and Lamberg-Allardt, 2015; Lardy and Ferguson, 1969; Op den Kamp, 1979). The P deficiency can result in a decreased growth performance, skeletal dysplasia, and increased mortality in broilers (Jiang et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2017). In modern broiler diet, inorganic P is supplemented ranged from 0.5 to 1.0% at different age and species of total diet, while its absorption has been shown to be low at 40-60% when inadequate supplemental phytase used leading to an increase in risk of environmental pollution as a result of excretion of indigestible P (Knowlton et al., 2004; Liu et al., 2012, 2013). In addition, the inorganic mineral P resources are not sustainable (Scholz et al., 2013). Therefore, improving the absorption and utilization of P is a priority issue.

1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 [1,25-(OH)2D3] is the biologically active form of vitamin D3 (VD3) in the body. Although 1,25-(OH)2D3 is toxic at inclusion levels exceeding 5 µg/kg, it can promote P absorption and utilization more effectively at a relatively low levels compared to VD3 (Brown et al., 1999). Edwards (1993) reported that dietary supplementation with 1,25-(OH)2D3 significantly increased P utilization in broilers. Three P transporters have been identified as major contributors to the P transport in the small intestine of rats (Villa-Bellosta and Sorribas, 2008). The type IIb sodium-phosphate cotransporter (NaPi-IIb) is considered to be the most important P transporter in the small intestine of mice, and it mainly facilitates the transfer of P into enterocytes (Sabbagh et al., 2009). The other two P transporters, P transporter 1 (PiT-1) and 2 (PiT-2), are located in the brush border apical membrane and involved in the absorption of dietary P (Yan et al., 2007). It's worth noting that these three P transporters are also abundantly expressed in the small intestine of broilers (Lv et al., 2022; Shao et al., 2019). Previous study in our laboratory indicated that 1,25-(OH)2D3 could promote P absorption by up-regulating the mRNA expression of NaPi-IIb in the ligated duodenal loops of Arbor Acres male broilers (Liao et al., 2017). The dietary supplementation with VD3 at 3500 IU/kg has been shown to increase the duodenal P absorption and the protein expression of PiT-1 in Arbor Acres male broilers (Shao et al., 2019). However, it remains unclear whether and how the 1,25-(OH)2D3 promotes the expression of the above-mentioned P transporters during the process of the above enhanced P absorption in the ligated duodenal loops of broilers.

The function of 1,25-(OH)2D3 is mediated through its linkage to transcription factor of vitamin D, vitamin D receptor (VDR), in order to trigger the cascade of genes are involved with vitamin D function including intestinal absorption of P (De Matos, 2008; Pike et al., 2007). VDR can be divided into nuclear VDR and membrane VDR (Zmijewski and Carlberg, 2020). 1,25-(OH)2D3 could activate the nuclear VDR by binding to it. Upon activation, nuclear VDR in the cell nucleus forms heterodimers with the retinoid X receptor (RXR), and then binds to vitamin D response elements (VDREs) in the promoter regions of target genes to regulate gene transcription (Brown et al., 1999; Carlberg, 2022; Carlberg and Campbell, 2013; Khanal and Nemere, 2007; Nemere et al., 2012). Han et al. (2024) found that dietary supplementation with 1,25-(OH)2D3 significantly increased the mRNA expression level of VDR in the duodenum of broilers. Moreover, Taketani et al. (1998) demonstrated that 1,25-(OH)2D3 significantly increased DNA promoter activity and mRNA expression level of type IIa sodium-phosphate cotransporter (NaPi-IIa) in rat renal cells. However, it remains unclear whether the VDR is involved in the regulation of the aforementioned P transporters by 1,25-(OH)2D3.

Therefore, we hypothesized that 1,25-(OH)2D3 would increase the transcriptional expression of the related P transporters via promoting the binding of VDR to their DNA promoter regions, thereby promoting their protein expression and P absorption in the duodenum of Arbor Acres male broilers. The objective of the current study was to investigate the effect of added 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels in the perfusates on P absorption percentage and rate, mRNA or protein expression levels of VDR, RXR, NaPi-IIb, PiT-1, and PiT-2, and the enrichments of VDR bound to DNA promoter regions of the aforementioned P transporters in the duodenum of Arbor Acres male broilers using in situ ligated duodenal loops in order to test the above hypothesis.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

Animals were used and cared by the Committee on Animal Use of the Ministry of Agriculture of China (Beijing, China) guidelines. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the College of Animal Science and Technology, Yangzhou University.

Birds, diets, and experimental design

A total of 100 one-d-old Arbor Acres commercial male broiler chicks (Jinghai Poultry Industry Group Corp., Jiangsu, P. R. China) were raised and vaccinated in accordance with the Arbor Acres Broiler Raising Management Manual. Feed and tap water were available ad libitum. The corn-soybean meal complete diet was formulated to meet or exceed the nutrient requirements for broilers (NRC, 1994) (Table 1). At 22 d of age, 30 birds were selected after an overnight fast (12 h) according to their average body weight and randomly divided into 3 treatments with 10 birds per treatment. Broilers in each treatment group underwent in situ ligation of the duodenum and were perfused with 1 of 3 perfusion solutions supplemented with 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels of 0 (control), 12.5, and 25.0 ng/L, respectively. Constituents of the duodenal perfusate

According to the results of a previous study from our laboratory (Liao et al., 2017), the basal perfusion solution (control) injected into the duodenal loops consisted of 6.0 mmol/L KH2PO4, 3.0 mg/mL polyethylene glycol 3350 (Sigma, P3640), 15.5 mmol/L morpholineoethanesulfonic acid (pH=6.0), and 150.0 mmol/L NaCl. Reagent grade KH2PO4 was added to the perfusion solution of broilers as an inorganic P source (Liu et al, 2012). As polyethylene glycol is not absorbed by the small intestine, the 3.0 mg/mL polyethylene glycol 3350 (Sigma, P3640) was added to the perfusion solution. The aforementioned concentrations of 1,25-(OH)2D3 were added to the basal perfusion solution.

Ligated duodenal loop procedure

The in situ ligated duodenal loop procedure was based on the methods previously reported in our laboratory (Bai et al., 2012; Liao et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2016; Yu et al., 2008). After the broilers were anesthetized intravenously with sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg body weight), they were allowed to lay flat and immobilized on a board, and then, the abdomen was incised longitudinally to reveal the duodenum. An inlet polyethylene cannula (inner diameter, 1.0 mm; outer diameter, 1.6 mm) was inserted into a hole made with a 22-gauge needle through the duodenal wall into the lumen. The cannula was secured using sutures tied around the duodenum and tubing. A 12 cm section of the duodenum was isolated and an outlet cannula was inserted in a similar manner at the opposite end of the loop.

Subsequently, the loops were flushed with 39±1°C saline to remove any chyme that might be present, and then the distal loops were tied off to prevent the solutions leakage. A 3.5 mL perfusion solution was injected into the ligated duodenal loop through a plastic cannula with a calibrated syringe according to the treatments described above. The perfused loop was carefully placed back into the abdominal cavity for the duration of the experiment. Adequate moisture levels were maintained by spraying the duodenum with warm 0.9% saline and covering the abdomen with plastic wrap. All broilers remained throughout anesthesia, with no significant changes in heart rate, respiratory rate, or body temperature during the perfusion periods.

Sample collections

Diet samples were collected for analyses of CP and Ca contents. The perfusates in the ligated duodenal loops were harvested (2 mL) through a cannula at 40 min after perfusion, and this time was determined in the previous study from our laboratory (Liao et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2012). All broilers were sacrificed by cervical dislocation. The ligated duodenal loops were excised, flushed with ice-cold saline, and slit lengthwise. The duodenal mucosa was scraped with an ice-cold microscope slide, immediately frozen in liquid N2, and then stored at -80°C for analyses of VDR, RXR, NaPi-IIb, PiT-1 and PiT-2 mRNA or protein expression levels, and the enrichments of VDR bound to DNA promoters of the above P transporters (Hu et al., 2023).

Determinations of CP, Ca, and P concentrations, and calculation of P absorption

The CP concentrations in diet samples were determined according to the Chinese standard (GB/T 6434-2018). The Ca concentrations in diet samples were measured using a 5110 inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (Agilent Technologies Australia (M) Pty Ltd., Australia) as described previously (Wang et al., 2024). The P concentrations in the fluid perfusates of the ligated duodenal loops were determined calorimetrically using the vanadate-molybdate method (Davies et al., 1973). The polyethylene glycol 3350 was used to correct for changes in P concentrations resulting from water absorption or duodenal secretion (Boulter and McMichael, 1970). Isolations of total RNA and absolute quantitative PCR

Absolute quantitative PCR was performed according to previous publications (Gerwick et al., 2007; Gilbert et al., 2007). Total RNA was extracted from the duodenal mucosa using Trizol Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA quality was evaluated by agarose gel electrophoresis and RNA concentrations were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). One microgram of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the random hexamer primers/oligo (dT)23VN primer mix (R223-01, Vazyme, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). Two microliters of diluted cDNA (1:25, vol/vol) were used for PCR reactions with the gene-specific qPCR primers and Taq Master Mix (P111-01, Vazyme, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The PCR products were separated on an agarose gel, then excised and purified using a TIANquick Midi Purification Kit (DP204-02, Tiangen Biotech Co, Beijing, China), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The purified nucleotide fragments were cloned using the pCE2 TA/Blunt-Zero Vector (C601-01, Vazyme, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). The presence of the insert in the recombinant clones was confirmed by sequencing, and the cloned circular plasmid was quantified using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer. The standard cDNA (template) was then serially diluted (from 10 to 106 copies/μL) to generate a standard curve. qPCR was performed in triplicate using an Applied Biosystems QuantStudio 3D Digital PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The PCR reaction was performed at 95°C for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 10 s and 62°C for 30 s. Data (Ct values) were collected at each elongation step. A melt curve analysis was performed after each run by increasing the temperature from 60 to 95°C in 0.3°C increments to confirm the presence of a single product only. All primers used are listed in Table 3. The obtained PCR products were then separated on a 2% agarose gel to verify the presence of a single band. The resulting curve was used to examine both the efficiency (slopes of -3.2 to -3.5) and the sensitivity (at least 100 copies reliably detected) of the reactions. The quantity of transcript in an unknown sample was determined from a linear regression line derived from the standard curve and expressed as copies of transcripts per ng of total RNA. Protein extraction and western blotting

Frozen duodenal mucosa samples (50 mg/sample) were homogenized in ice-cold radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (R0010, Solarbio, Beijing, China) containing 1% protease inhibitor (B14001, Bimake, Houston, TX, USA), and the total protein concentration was determined using the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (23225, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) according to the method previously described by Li et al. (2022). Each protein sample (40 μg/sample) was loaded and separated on 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gels, then the proteins were transferred to nitrocellulose membranes and blocked with 5% non-fat milk-TBST for 1.5 h at room temperature. The membranes were washed with TBST, and then incubated with primary antibodies against VDR (Cat. No. ab109234, Abcam, 1:5,000), NaPi-IIb (Cat. No. A9460, ABclonal, 1:1,000), PiT-1 (Cat. No. A4117, ABclonal, 1:1,000), PiT-2 (Cat. No. A6739, ABclonal, 1:1,000), and β-actin (Cat. No. HX1829, Huaxingbio, 1:5,000) at 4°C overnight, followed by incubation with a Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG Secondary Antibody (HRP) (Cat. No. HX2031, Huaxingbio, 1:5,000). Images were recorded using a Tanon Chemiluminescence Image Scanner, and the band intensity was analyzed using the Tanon GIS software (Tanon, Shanghai, China). The ratios of VDR, NaPi-IIb, PiT-1, or PiT-2 protein band intensity to the internal reference β-actin protein band intensity were used to reflect the relative protein expression levels.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation Analysis

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) analysis was performed according to the method previously described by Hu et al. (2017). Briefly, approximately 200 mg of frozen duodenal mucosa samples were ground in liquid nitrogen, resuspended in PBS containing protease inhibitor cocktail (B14001, Bimake, USA), and cross-linked in 1% formaldehyde for 10 min. The cross-linking reaction was terminated by adding glycine for 10 min. The tissue particles were washed with PBS and lysed with sodium dodecyl sulfate lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors. Chromatin samples were sonicated to generate chromatin fragments of 200 to 1000 bp and immunoprecipitated with 2 μg of a specific VDR antibody overnight at 4°C (ab109234, Abcam, UK). Normal IgG (12-370, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) was used as a negative control. Finally, reverse cross-linking was performed to release DNA fragments from the immunoprecipitated complexes at 65°C for 5 h, and DNA was purified. The immunoprecipitated DNA was used as a template for RT-qPCR. The binding enrichment of DNA promoter region was normalized to the input samples using 2−ΔΔCt according to a previous study (Mukhopadhyay et al., 2008). The VDREs in the promoter regions of the NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 genes were predicted using online software (https://jaspar.elixir.no/), and the primers of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 promoters are listed in Table 3.

Statistical Analyses

Data in the present study were analyzed as one-way ANOVA using the GLM in SAS 9.4 (2013). Significant differences among treatments were compared using the LSD method. Each duodenal loop was the experimental unit. The P ≤ 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Discussion

The results of the present study have demonstrated that 1,25-(OH)2D3 significantly increased P absorption, mRNA or protein expression levels of VDR, RXR, NaPi-IIb and PiT-2, and the enrichments of VDR bound to the DNA promoter regions of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 genes in the ligated duodenal loops of Arbor Acres male broilers, suggesting that 1,25-(OH)2D3 could increase P absorption in the ligated duodenal loops of Arbor Acres male broilers possibly via VDR-mediated transcriptional activation of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 genes, which supports our hypothesis. These findings provide a scientific basis for improving dietary P absorption and utilization to reduce the manure P excretion into the environment during the broiler production.

1,25-(OH)2D3 is a biologically active metabolite of vitamin D3 and has been shown to promote the absorption of P in the small intestine of broilers (Wasserman and Taylor, 1973). The duodenum is the main site of the small intestinal P absorption in broilers, followed by the jejunum and ileum (Liu et al., 2016). Zhao and Nemere (2002) reported that P absorption in the primary duodenal epithelial cells was positively correlated with the addition of 4 to 54 ng/L 1,25-(OH)2D3 to the medium. A previous study in our laboratory showed that the addition of either 12.5 or 125 ng/L of 1,25-(OH)2D3 to the perfusates increased the P absorption percentage and rate compared to the control (Liao et al., 2017). Our results are consistent with those previous studies. The research results can provide theoretical and experimental basis for reducing the amount of P in the diet by adding appropriate levels of 1,25-(OH)2D3 to meet the P requirement of broilers. Previous research has shown that the absorption of inorganic P in the duodenum of broilers is a saturable, carrier-mediated process (Liu et al., 2016; Saurette and Alexander, 2019). The NaPi-IIb, PiT-1 and PiT-2 are the key transporters involved in P absorption in the small intestine of broilers, and they are localized in the brush border apical membrane of the duodenum, and can transport P from the gut lumen into brush border membrane vesicles of the duodenum (Li et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2016; MacDonald et al., 2021; Yan et al., 2007). Previous studies have shown that the addition of 12.5 ng/L 1,25-(OH)2D3, 5 μg/kg 1α-hydroxycholecalciferol, 12.5 μg/kg 25-hydroxycholecalciferol or 250 IU/kg VD3 significantly increased NaPi-IIb mRNA expression in the duodenum of broilers (Han et al., 2023; Han et al., 2009; Han et al., 2018; Liao et al., 2017). Katai et al. (1999) reported that vitamin D3 could increase the absorption of inorganic P and PiT-2 mRNA expression in the small intestine of rats. Similarly, we found that 1,25-(OH)2D3 increased the NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 mRNA expression. As proteins are the carriers and the executors of the physiological function. In fact, the protein expression levels of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 were also increased after 1,25-(OH)2D3 treatment. Therefore, the results from our current experiment suggest that 1,25-(OH)2D3 promotes P absorption in the ligated duodenum of Arbor Acres male broilers possibly via up-regulating the protein expression levels of both NaPi-IIb and PiT-2. Yet, it remains to be elucidated how 1,25-(OH)2D3 increases the expression of P transporters in the ligated duodenal loops of broilers.

The VDR mediates most of the biological effects of 1,25-(OH)2D3 (Noh et al., 2022), and it is a transcription factor that regulates gene transcription by binding to the VDREs on the promoter regions of its target genes (Haussler et al., 2011). Dietary supplementation with 1,25-(OH)2D3 significantly increases VDR mRNA and protein expression levels in kidney and duodenum of mice (Healy et al., 2003; Nakajima et al., 1992). Han et al. (2024) reported that the mRNA expression levels of VDR in the duodenum of broilers increased linearly with increasing dietary supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels. Xu et al. (2002) found that 1,25-(OH)2D3 increased the intestinal P absorption and NaPi-IIb mRNA expression associated with VDR activation in rats. Kaneko et al. (2011) and Segawa et al. (2004) reported that knockout of VDR decreased NaPi-IIb protein expression in the small intestine and PiT-2 protein expression in the kidney of mice. In contrast, Taketani et al. (1998) demonstrated that the overexpression of VDR significantly increased the DNA promoter activity and mRNA expression level of NaPi-IIa in rat renal cells. These studies suggest that 1,25-(OH)2D3 can up-regulate the expression of P transporters via the binding of VDR to their promoter regions. VDR acts primarily as a heterodimer with the RXR on VDREs. In the present study, we found that the addition of 1,25-(OH)2D3 to the perfusates significantly increased mRNA or protein expression levels of VDR and RXR, and the enrichments of VDR binding to NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 promoter regions in the duodenum of Arbor Acres male broilers, which has not been reported before. These findings provide new evidences that 1,25-(OH)2D3 promotes the transcriptional expression of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 by enhancing the binding of VDR/RXR to their promoter regions in the duodenum of Arbor Acres male broilers.

In conclusion, 1,25-(OH)2D3 increased P absorption possibly through the VDR-mediated transactivation of NaPi-IIb and PiT-2 genes in the ligated duodenal loops of Arbor Acres male broilers. Further studies need to be carried out using a primary cultured duodenal epithelial cell model or a duodenal organoid model of broilers as well as gene over-expression and RNA interference to address and confirm the above possible mechanisms.

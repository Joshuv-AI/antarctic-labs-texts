GC-MS Analysis of the Ayurvedic Oil Balaswagandhadi Tailam
Authors: Aparna Sudhan, K Prabhu, Sumathi Jones, CS Janaki, Dhastagir Sheriff, Mudiganthi Ramakrishna Rao, Hayagreva Kumar Muttevi, Deepalakkshmi Balakrishnan, M Sivanesh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:To evaluate the efficacy of alternative medications such as Ayurveda, Sidha, and Unani, the application of advanced analytical tools is essential.Aim:In this study, the Ayurvedic oil Balaswagandhadi tailam, which is prescribed for treating fever, muscle weakness, bone and joint issues, muscle wasting, arthritic pain, neuralgia, and paralysis.Methods:The presence of active component was analyzed using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) following a prescribed protocol.Results:The GC-MS study disclosed the presence of several essential bioactive compounds, including trans-2-methyl-4-n-pentylthiane, S, S-dioxide, chloroacetic acid, tetradecyl ester, 1,2-benzenedicarboxylic acid, bis(2-methylpropyl) ester, methyl 12,15-octadecadienoate, 11-octadecenoic acid, methyl ester, cis-10-heptadecenoic acid, tetradecanedioic acid, 15-hydroxypentadecanoic acid, octadecanoic acid, levo-menthoxyacetic acid, β-eudesmol trimethylsilyl ether, 1-heptatriacotanol, vitamin E, campesterol, and γ-sitosterol.Conclusion:The compounds identified are known for their various medicinal properties, potentially contributing to the therapeutic effects of Balaswagandhadi tailam. Further research is warranted to explore these findings.

INTRODUCTION

The qualitative validation and efficacy evaluation of traditional and alternative medicines such as Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, and Chinese medicine are urgently needed to integrate them into mainstream medical systems. Although some work has been done in this area, much more research is required.[12345] This study focuses on the Ayurvedic oil formulation Balaswagandhadi tailam, which is prescribed for treating fever, muscle weakness, bone and joint issues, muscle wasting, arthritic pain, neuralgia, and paralysis. This medicine is prepared according to the ingredients and method specified in Sahasrayoga Tailayoga Prakarana 13.

768 g each of Bala (Sida cordifolia), Aswagandha (Withania somnifera), and Laksha (Laccifera lacca) were boiled in 12.288 lit of water to reduce to 3. 072 lit. 768 ml of Sesamum indicum oil, 3.072 lit Mastu (Supernatant clear liquid of Curd), 12 g each of Lesser Galangal (Pluchea lanceolata), red sandalwood (Pterocarpus santalinus), Indian madder (Rubia cordifolia), Indian doob grass (Cynodon dactylon), Indian butter tree (Glycyrrhhia glabra), Angelica (Angelica glauca), Indian sarsaparilla (Hemidesmus indicus), Khas (Vetiveria zizanoides), coco-grass (Cyperus rotundus), Indian costus (Saussurea lappa), Eagle wood (Aquilaria agallocha), Indian barberry (Cedrus deodara), Turmeric (Curcuma longa), white water lily (Nymphea stellate), Chaste tree (Vitex agnun-castus), Shatahva (Anethum sowa), and Padmakesara (Nilumbium speciosum) are boiled separately. All the above are mixed and left till the oil becomes clear. This is filtered and stored to be used as medicine.

Balaswagandhadi tailam is a traditional Ayurvedic formulation known for its rejuvenating properties, often used to promote strength and vitality. It is sourced from reputable Ayurvedic vendors in India, highlighting its cultural and medicinal significance. Studies have shown that Balaswagandhadi tailam contains ingredients like W. somnifera and S. cordifolia, which possess anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, supporting its traditional use in managing inflammatory conditions and promoting overall health. Thus, this study aims to conduct a comprehensive gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis of the Ayurvedic oil Balaswagandhadi tailam to identify its bioactive compounds and assess their potential therapeutic properties.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The Ayurvedic medicine Balaswagandhadi tailam was sourced from a reputable Ayurvedic vendor in Chennai, India. A 50 ml sample of Balaswagandhadi tailam was extracted using ethyl acetate solvent in a separating funnel. The resulting extract was then filtered to remove any impurities and concentrated using a water bath maintained at a low temperature to preserve the integrity of the compounds. The concentrated extract was subsequently subjected to GC-MS analysis, adhering to standard procedures, in order to determine its chemical profile.

The analysis was performed using a gas chromatography system (Agilent GC: 7890A, MS: 7000 Triple Quad GCMS) equipped with a mass spectrometry detector. Gas chromatography separates compounds based on their partition coefficients using helium gas as the mobile phase. Compounds, which must be volatile, are vaporized in the injector, mixed with the mobile phase, and separated in the column. A mass spectrometer detects the separated compounds, determining their molecular weights and structural information. Identification is performed using the GC-MS Library (NIST and WILEY). A 100 μl sample was dissolved in 1 ml of a suitable solvent and stirred vigorously for 10 s using a vortex stirrer. The clear extract was then analyzed using gas chromatography. The analysis covered fragments from 45 to 450 Da, with a total GC running time of 32.02 minutes.

Metabolite identification involved a detailed process using GC-MS software (version 1.10 beta, Shimadzu), where retention times and fragmentation patterns were compared with entries in the NIST spectral library. This analytical approach ensured accurate identification of compounds present in the sample. The pharmacological properties of each biomolecule were further elucidated by referencing Dr. Duke’s Phytochemical and Ethnobotanical Database, accessible through the National Agriculture Library, USA, along with other credible sources. These references provided comprehensive insights into the potential medicinal roles and bioactive properties associated with the identified metabolites.[6]

From Table 1, several molecules identified in Balaswagandhadi tailam, such as trans-2-methyl-4-n-pentylthiane, S, S-dioxide, chloroacetic acid, tetradecyl ester, 1,2-benzenedicarboxylic acid, bis (2-methylpropyl) ester, methyl 12,15-octadecadienoate, 11-octadecenoic acid, methyl ester, cis-10-heptadecenoic acid, tetradecanedioic acid, 15-hydroxypentadecanoic acid, octadecanoic acid, levo-menthoxyacetic acid, beta-eudesmol, trimethylsilyl ether, 1-heptatriacotanol, Vitamin E, campesterol, and gamma-sitosterol, exhibit various medicinal roles. For example, trans-2-methyl-4-n-pentylthiane, S, S-dioxide, and trans-13-octadecenoic acid possess antioxidative, neurostimulative, and adrenal supportive properties, potentially contributing to the treatment of fever, muscle and joint weakness, muscle wasting, arthritic pains, neuralgia, and paralysis. Another molecule, 13-hexyloxacyclotridec-10-en-2-one, has roles in reducing endothelial leukocyte and platelet adhesion, acting as an endocrine tonic, promoting endothelium-derived relaxing factors, and enhancing enteric motility, which may aid in treating muscle and joint pains and neuralgia. Additionally, 1-heptatriacotanol has significant impacts on the therapeutic efficacy of Balaswagandhadi tailam. Gamma-sitosterol, known for its PPAR-gamma-antagonist activity, helps control glucose metabolism, contributing to homeostasis. These findings suggest that Balaswagandhadi tailam contains important bioactive compounds that directly or indirectly support its medicinal uses, warranting further investigation.

CONCLUSION

The GC-MS profile of Balaswagandhadi tailam reveals key biomolecules with medicinal properties, such as trans-2-methyl-4-n-pentylthiane and 13-hexyloxacyclotridec-10-en-2-one, which contribute to its efficacy in treating fever, muscle and joint ailments, neuralgia, and paralysis. Compounds like gamma-sitosterol also aid in glucose metabolism. Further research is needed to fully validate these therapeutic benefits.

Global Research Landscape of Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Sepsis: A Comprehensive Bibliometric and Visualization Analysis (2000–2025)
Authors: Shanshan Wang, Chong Meng, Hongjun Bian, Xin Zhang, Minllin Cong, Jinlin Ren, Jingzhao Sun, Haiyang Zhang, Dongxing Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveSepsis-associated mitochondrial dysfunction represents a central but incompletely understood mechanism underlying organ failure and mortality in critically ill patients. Despite a growing body of literature, no comprehensive bibliometric analysis has systematically mapped the global research landscape, knowledge structure, and emerging frontiers of this field. This study aimed to quantify publication trends, identify key contributors, delineate thematic hotspots, and characterize the evolving knowledge base in sepsis and mitochondria research over the past 25 years.MethodsA systematic search of the Web of Science Core Collection was conducted using a predefined search strategy combining sepsis- and mitochondria-related terms, restricted to publications from January 1, 2000, to December 31, 2025. After sequential exclusion of retracted publications, non-article/review document types, and non-English records, a final dataset of 3545 publications was obtained. Bibliometric analyses were performed using the bibliometrix package in R, VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.2.R4). Analyses included annual publication trends, geographic and institutional distribution, Bradford’s Law journal analysis, Lotka’s Law author productivity, co-citation and bibliographic coupling network analysis, thematic mapping, and keyword burst detection.ResultsAnnual publication output increased more than nine-fold from 2000 to 2025, with a marked acceleration following the 2016 Sepsis-3 consensus definition. China led in publication volume (n = 1350; 38.1%), while the United States demonstrated the highest citation impact (52,732 total citations; 67.61 average citations per publication). Bradford’s Law analysis identified 30 core journals, led by Shock, Frontiers in Immunology, and Critical Care Medicine. Lotka’s Law analysis confirmed that 74.94% of contributing authors published a single article. The most cited publication was Zhang Q et al (Nature, 2010; 3080 citations). Thematic map analysis identified mitochondrial dysfunction/acute kidney injury, oxidative stress/reactive oxygen species, and inflammation/mitochondrial DNA as Motor Themes. Keyword burst analysis revealed a temporal evolution from early nitric oxide and cytochrome c research toward current hotspots including NLRP3 inflammasome activation, sepsis-associated encephalopathy, and sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy.ConclusionThis comprehensive bibliometric analysis provides a systematic overview of 25 years of research at the intersection of sepsis and mitochondrial biology. The field is experiencing sustained growth, with current research frontiers focused on organ-specific mitochondrial injury, innate immune regulation, and neurological complications.

Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening medical emergency defined as organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, and remains one of the most pressing challenges in contemporary critical care medicine. Despite decades of research and advances in supportive care, sepsis and septic shock continue to impose a major global health burden, with high rates of morbidity and mortality persisting across healthcare systems worldwide. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study 2017, sepsis was responsible for an estimated 48.9 million incident cases and 11.0 million deaths globally, accounting for approximately 19.7% of all global deaths.1 The clinical management of sepsis is guided by the Surviving Sepsis Campaign international guidelines, most recently updated in 2026, which provide evidence-based recommendations for hemodynamic resuscitation, infection source control, antimicrobial therapy, and organ support.2 Despite adherence to these guidelines, in-hospital mortality for sepsis in high-income countries remains 15–25%, rising to 30–40% in septic shock, with substantially higher mortality rates reported in low- and middle-income countries.1 The disproportionate burden of sepsis in resource-limited settings underscores the urgent need for deeper mechanistic understanding and the identification of novel therapeutic targets that could be translated into accessible clinical interventions. The Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock (Sepsis-3), published in 2016, fundamentally reframed sepsis as a syndrome of dysregulated host response leading to organ dysfunction, thereby redirecting scientific attention toward the molecular and cellular mechanisms of organ failure rather than the systemic inflammatory response syndrome per se.3 This conceptual shift has catalyzed a new wave of mechanistic research, with mitochondrial biology emerging as one of the most actively investigated pathophysiological domains.

Among the diverse pathophysiological mechanisms implicated in sepsis-induced organ dysfunction, mitochondrial dysfunction has emerged as a particularly central and potentially reversible contributor. Mitochondria are not merely the primary site of cellular ATP generation through oxidative phosphorylation; they are increasingly recognized as dynamic signaling organelles that integrate metabolic, immunological, and cell death pathways in response to infectious and inflammatory stimuli. In the context of sepsis, mitochondrial injury is initiated through multiple converging mechanisms, including the overproduction of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species that directly inhibit electron transport chain complexes I and IV, impaired mitophagy leading to the accumulation of dysfunctional organelles, dysregulation of mitochondrial fission and fusion dynamics mediated by dynamin-related protein 1, and mitochondrial membrane permeabilization triggering intrinsic apoptotic cascades.4 The clinical relevance of these mechanisms was definitively established by Brealey et al (Lancet, 2002), who demonstrated a direct correlation between the degree of mitochondrial dysfunction in skeletal muscle biopsies and both the severity and outcome of human septic shock,5 providing the first compelling evidence that mitochondrial bioenergetic failure is not merely an epiphenomenon but a mechanistic driver of organ failure.

Subsequent research has broadened this framework considerably. Mitochondrial dysfunction is now recognized as a unifying pathophysiological thread connecting diverse forms of sepsis-induced organ injury, including acute kidney injury, cardiomyopathy, encephalopathy, and diaphragmatic failure, with organ-specific differences in mitochondrial abundance, metabolic substrate dependence, and susceptibility to injury collectively shaping the clinical pattern of multi-organ dysfunction.6 The discovery that damaged mitochondria release danger-associated molecular patterns—including mitochondrial DNA and formyl peptides—into the circulation further established mitochondria as active participants in the amplification of systemic inflammation, rather than passive victims of it.7 More recently, the concept of metabolic hibernation or cytopathic hypoxia has been proposed to explain the paradox of organ dysfunction persisting despite apparently adequate oxygen delivery, with mitochondrial dysfunction and consequent ATP depletion driving a state of adaptive hypometabolism that, while potentially protective in the short term, contributes to prolonged organ dysfunction when unresolved.4 Collectively, these mechanistic insights have generated substantial scientific and therapeutic interest in mitochondria as both diagnostic biomarkers and pharmacological targets in sepsis.

Bibliometric analysis has emerged as a powerful and widely adopted methodological approach for systematically mapping the intellectual landscape of rapidly evolving biomedical research fields. In the field of sepsis research, bibliometric analyses have been published with increasing frequency over the past decade, addressing a range of subfields including septic shock,8 sepsis-associated acute kidney injury,9 sepsis-associated encephalopathy,10 sepsis immunosuppression,11 endothelial cell dysfunction in sepsis,12 and artificial intelligence applications in sepsis diagnosis and management.13 However, a critical and conspicuous gap exists in the current bibliometric literature: despite the central importance of mitochondrial dysfunction in sepsis pathophysiology, no comprehensive bibliometric analysis has to date systematically characterized the global research landscape at this intersection. The absence of a systematic bibliometric overview means that researchers, clinicians, and funding agencies currently lack a quantitative roadmap for understanding how this field has evolved, which research themes are most central, and where scientific investment would be most productively directed.

To address this critical knowledge gap, the present study aimed to conduct the first comprehensive bibliometric and visualization analysis of global research on sepsis and mitochondrial dysfunction, encompassing all publications indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection between January 2000 and December 2025. Specifically, we sought to: (1) characterize temporal trends in publication output and citation impact; (2) identify the leading contributing countries, institutions, and authors; (3) delineate the core journal landscape using Bradford’s Law; (4) characterize author productivity distribution using Lotka’s Law; (5) map the knowledge structure through co-citation and bibliographic coupling network analysis; (6) identify research themes and their developmental trajectories through thematic mapping; and (7) delineate temporal research hotspots and emerging frontiers through keyword burst detection. The findings of this study are intended to provide researchers, clinicians, and funding agencies with a quantitative and evidence-based roadmap of the current state and future directions of this rapidly evolving field.

Methods

Data Source and Search Strategy

All bibliometric data were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), which was selected as the sole database due to its comprehensive coverage of high-quality peer-reviewed literature and its compatibility with downstream bibliometric analysis tools. A systematic search was conducted on April 27, 2026, using the following Topic field (TS) search query: TS=(“sepsis” OR “septicemia” OR “septic shock”) AND TS=(“mitochondria” OR “mitochondrial” OR “mitophagy” OR “mitochondrial dysfunction” OR “mitochondrial biogenesis” OR “mitochondrial dynamics”) AND PY=(2000–2025) NOT DT=(“Retracted Publication”). To minimize the impact of database indexing lag on the completeness of recent literature, the search was intentionally conducted in April 2026, approximately four months after the end of the study period (December 31, 2025), allowing sufficient time for the indexing of publications from the final year of analysis. In addition, 31 Early Access records whose Final Publication Year had been assigned to 2026 at the time of retrieval were identified, retained, and recoded to 2025 to further enhance the completeness of the most recent literature.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Records were included if they met all of the following criteria: (1) published as original articles or review articles; (2) written in English; and (3) indexed within the defined search period (2000–2025). Records were excluded if they were: (1) retracted publications; (2) non-English language publications; or (3) document types other than articles or reviews. Thirty-one records retrieved as Early Access publications whose Final Publication Year was assigned to 2026 were retained and recoded to 2025. Data Extraction and Processing

Full bibliographic records were exported from WoSCC in plain text format in batches of up to 500 records and subsequently merged into a single dataset. Country name standardization was performed prior to analysis: “Peoples R China” was recoded as “China”; “Taiwan” was merged into “China” in institutional address fields; regional entities of the United Kingdom were unified as “United Kingdom”; and “Turkiye” was harmonized as “Turkey”. All data processing was performed in R (version 4.3.1). These standardization procedures were performed to ensure consistency in geographic attribution and to align with the country and region classification conventions adopted by the Web of Science database and widely used in previous bibliometric studies. Specifically, the constituent regions of the United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland) were consolidated under “United Kingdom” in accordance with their status as a single sovereign country, and address records were harmonized accordingly. These procedures were applied solely for the purpose of standardized bibliometric aggregation and do not reflect any political position on the part of the authors.

Bibliometric Analysis

Bibliometric analyses were performed using the bibliometrix package (version 4.1.4) in R. Basic descriptive statistics were calculated, including annual publication output, total citation counts, and country-, institution-, and author-level productivity metrics. Bradford’s Law analysis was conducted to identify core journals. Lotka’s Law analysis was performed to characterize the distribution of scientific productivity among authors. A thematic map was constructed based on author keyword co-occurrence data to identify and classify research themes into four quadrants: Motor Themes, Niche Themes, Basic Themes, and Emerging or Declining Themes. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize publication counts, citation frequencies, and productivity metrics. For Lotka’s Law analysis, the goodness-of-fit between the observed author productivity distribution and the theoretical Lotka distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, with the Lotka exponent estimated by the bibliometrix package. For Bradford’s Law, journals were partitioned into three zones each accounting for approximately one-third of the total articles. Collaboration and co-occurrence network clustering was performed using the Louvain community detection algorithm, and the total link strength was calculated to quantify the intensity of collaborative and co-citation relationships. All analyses were descriptive in nature, consistent with the methodological framework of bibliometric research, and no inferential hypothesis testing was applied beyond the goodness-of-fit assessment described above.

Network Visualization

Collaboration and co-occurrence networks were visualized using VOSviewer (version 1.6.20). Three distinct network analyses were performed: (1) an international collaboration network based on country-level co-authorship, with a minimum publication threshold of five documents per country; (2) an institutional collaboration network based on organization-level co-authorship, with a minimum publication threshold of 25 documents per institution; and (3) a document co-citation network, with a minimum co-citation threshold of 30 citations per reference, and a bibliographic coupling network of documents with a minimum citation threshold of 250 citations per document. Clustering in all networks was performed using the Louvain algorithm.

Keyword Burst Detection

Keyword burst detection analysis was conducted using CiteSpace (version 6.2.R4). The analysis covered the period 2000–2025, with a time slice of one year per slice. Node type was set to keyword, and the Pathfinder pruning algorithm was applied. The top 25 keywords with the strongest citation burst values were identified and visualized.

Annual Trend Analysis

Annual publication trends were analyzed using R with ggplot2 (version 3.4.4). A locally estimated scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) curve was fitted to the annual publication data. The year 2016 was designated as a key temporal landmark corresponding to the publication of the Sepsis-3 consensus definition, dividing the study period into two phases: Phase I (Pre-Sepsis-3, 2000–2015) and Phase II (Post-Sepsis-3, 2016–2025).

Results

Overall Publication Output

The systematic search of the WoSCC yielded 3908 initial records. Following sequential exclusion of 18 retracted publications, 299 non-article/review document types, and 46 non-English records, a final analytical dataset of 3545 publications was obtained (Figure 1). These publications were contributed by authors from 62 countries, affiliated with 2080 unique institutions, and published across 973 distinct journals. The dataset comprised 2757 original articles and 695 review articles, with a total citation count exceeding 120,000 at the time of data retrieval.

Temporal Trends in Publication Output

Annual publication output demonstrated a sustained and marked growth trajectory over the 25-year study period (Figure 2). The number of annual publications increased from fewer than 50 in 2000 to 464 in 2025, representing a greater than nine-fold increase. The LOESS trend curve revealed two distinct growth phases. During Phase I (2000–2015, Pre-Sepsis-3), publication growth was gradual and relatively modest. A marked acceleration in publication output was observed following 2016, coinciding with the introduction of the Sepsis-3 consensus definition. Total annual citations peaked around 2018–2020, with a subsequent apparent decline attributable to citation lag in more recently published articles. Author Productivity and Lotka’s Law

A total of 15,487 unique authors contributed to the dataset (Table 3). Zhang Y was the most prolific author (n = 62 publications), followed by Singer M (n = 59) and Wang Y (n = 55). Singer M demonstrated the highest citation impact, with 5467 total citations and an average of 92.66 citations per publication. Lotka’s Law analysis revealed that 74.94% of all contributing authors published only a single article in this domain (Table 4). The fitted Lotka exponent was 2.469, approximating the theoretical value of 2 that characterizes the inverse square law of scientific productivity. Keyword Burst Analysis

Citation burst analysis identified 25 keywords with the strongest temporal burst signals (Figure 6). Early-phase bursts (2000–2015) were dominated by “nitric oxide” (burst strength 41.59), “nitric oxide synthase” (28.42), and “tumor necrosis factor” (17.93). The transitional phase (2005–2017) was characterized by “multiple organ failure” and “cytochrome c oxidase”. Discussion

Publication Growth Trends and the Impact of Sepsis-3

This bibliometric analysis of 3545 publications retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection provides the first comprehensive mapping of global research trends at the intersection of sepsis and mitochondrial biology over a 25-year period (2000–2025). The sustained and accelerating growth in annual publication output reflects the growing recognition of sepsis as a global health emergency. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study, sepsis was responsible for approximately 11 million deaths worldwide in 2017, accounting for nearly 20% of all global mortality,1 a burden that has galvanized international research efforts across basic science, translational, and clinical domains. The identification of mitochondrial dysfunction as a central, potentially reversible mechanism underlying sepsis-induced organ failure has further amplified scientific interest in this area. The landmark study by Brealey et al (Lancet, 2002) provided the first direct human evidence linking mitochondrial dysfunction to the severity and outcome of septic shock,5 establishing a mechanistic framework that continues to underpin the field to this day.

A particularly noteworthy observation is the marked inflection in publication output following 2016, coinciding with the publication of the Sepsis-3 consensus definition. Singer et al (JAMA, 2016) redefined sepsis as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection,3 explicitly placing organ dysfunction at the centre of the clinical definition. This paradigm shift appears to have catalyzed a wave of mechanistic research aimed at elucidating the molecular basis of organ dysfunction, with mitochondrial pathways emerging as a particularly active area of investigation. Similar post-Sepsis-3 publication surges have been reported in bibliometric analyses of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury and sepsis-associated encephalopathy, suggesting that the Sepsis-3 framework has broadly stimulated organ-specific mechanistic research across the critical care community.

Geographic Distribution and Institutional Collaboration

The geographic distribution of publications revealed that China was the most prolific contributor, accounting for 38.1% of the total dataset (n = 1350), followed by the United States (22.0%). This finding is consistent with the broader trajectory of Chinese biomedical research output, which has expanded dramatically over the past two decades driven by sustained government investment in research infrastructure.14 A similar pattern of Chinese dominance in publication volume has been consistently observed across other sepsis-related bibliometric analyses, including those focused on sepsis immunosuppression,11 septic intestinal injury,15 and artificial intelligence applications in sepsis,13 suggesting this reflects a field-wide phenomenon.

The present analysis reveals an important qualitative disparity between publication volume and citation impact. Despite leading in publication output, China’s average citation rate (24.15) was substantially lower than that of the United States (67.61) and the United Kingdom (105.06). A similar pattern has been documented in the field of artificial intelligence and sepsis research.13 This divergence is likely multifactorial rather than attributable to any single cause. First, the relatively limited intensity of international collaboration among Chinese institutions may constrain citation impact, as internationally co-authored publications generally attract higher citation rates; the institutional network analysis in the present study indeed revealed that Chinese institutions formed a relatively self-contained cluster with sparse connections to Western research centers. Second, differences in the distribution of publications across high-impact Zone 1 journals may contribute, as a greater proportion of highly cited landmark studies, consensus definitions, and mechanistic discoveries have historically originated from institutions in the United States and the United Kingdom. Third, the rapid expansion of publication output may be accompanied by a degree of thematic homogenization, whereby a large volume of publications concentrates on similar topics, potentially diluting per-article citation impact. These factors collectively suggest that enhancing international collaboration and prioritizing high-impact, mechanistically novel research may help improve the citation influence of the substantial research output from Chinese institutions.

Most Cited Publications and Knowledge Structure

The most highly cited work was Zhang Q et al (Nature, 2010; 3080 citations), which demonstrated that mitochondria release DAMPs into the circulation following injury, activating a systemic inflammatory response.7 The second most cited publication was Nakahira K et al (Nature Immunology, 2011; 2591 citations), which demonstrated that mitochondrial reactive oxygen species are essential for NLRP3 inflammasome activation.16 The co-citation network identified three principal knowledge clusters. The first was anchored by Brealey et al (Lancet, 2002),5 establishing foundational evidence for mitochondrial dysfunction as a central mechanism in sepsis-induced organ failure. The second was centered on the Sepsis-3 definition paper. The third comprised immunological and molecular biology references related to mitochondrial signaling. A comparable tripartite knowledge architecture has been identified in bibliometric analyses of sepsis endothelial dysfunction.12

Thematic Map Analysis and Research Topic Architecture

The largest Motor Theme cluster—comprising “sepsis”, “mitochondrial dysfunction”, and “acute kidney injury”—reflects the well-established pathophysiological link between mitochondrial bioenergetic failure and sepsis-induced renal injury. Mitochondrial dysfunction is now recognized as one of the central pathogenic mechanisms of SA-AKI, encompassing impaired oxidative phosphorylation, mitochondrial dynamics dysregulation, defective mitophagy, and excessive reactive oxygen species generation.17 The second Motor Theme cluster— “mitochondria”, “oxidative stress”, and “reactive oxygen species”—represents the canonical mechanistic pathway of mitochondrial injury in sepsis.18 The third Motor Theme cluster— “inflammation”, “metabolism”, and “mitochondrial DNA”—reflects the intersection of immunometabolism and mitochondrial danger signaling. During the hyperinflammatory phase of sepsis, energy is preferentially generated through glycolysis instead of oxidative phosphorylation, a phenomenon termed metabolic reprogramming,19 while mitochondrial DNA released into the circulation sustains systemic inflammation through pattern recognition receptor activation.7

The Niche Theme cluster— “sepsis-associated encephalopathy”, “neuroinflammation”, and “microglia”—occupies a position of high internal density but lower centrality. Neuroinflammation caused by hyperactivation of microglia is considered a key pathogenic factor, initiating a cascade including blood-brain barrier disruption, oxidative stress, and neuronal injury.20 Mitochondrial dysfunction mediated through dynamin-related protein 1 has been implicated in propagating blood-brain barrier impairment in septic encephalopathy.21 The isolation of this cluster from the central network suggests that the mitochondrial mechanisms underlying neurological complications of sepsis remain incompletely integrated into the broader field, representing a priority area for future investigation.

Keyword Burst Analysis and Emerging Research Frontiers

During the early burst phase (2000–2015), the dominant emerging keywords included “nitric oxide” (burst strength 41.59), “nitric oxide synthase” (28.42), “tumor necrosis factor” (17.93), and “cytochrome c” (10.14), reflecting the prevailing paradigm in which sepsis-associated mitochondrial injury was primarily understood through the lens of nitric oxide overproduction and cytochrome c release. This mechanistic framework was largely established by the foundational clinical work of Brealey et al5 The transitional burst phase (2005–2017) was characterized by keywords including “multiple organ failure” (2005–2017) and “cytochrome c oxidase” (2005–2015), reflecting a shift toward integrated organ-level investigations.

The most clinically significant findings relate to the recent burst phase (2021–2025). “NLRP3 inflammasome” (burst strength 12.21, 2021–2025) has emerged as one of the most active current hotspots. Disruption of mitochondrial quality control creates a feed-forward cycle of NLRP3 overactivation that drives multi-organ inflammation in sepsis.22 “Sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy” and “myocardial injury” (burst strengths 10.87–12.32, 2022–2025) represent dominant organ-specific frontiers. Mitochondrial dysfunction—including disruption of calcium homeostasis, impaired oxidative phosphorylation, and pathological mitochondrial fission—is now recognized as a central contributor to septic cardiomyopathy.23 “Macrophage polarization” (burst strength 8.79, 2023–2025) reflects the growing recognition that mitochondrial metabolism is a master regulator of macrophage functional phenotype in sepsis, with mitochondrial dysfunction exacerbating the imbalance between M1 and M2 polarization states.24

Limitations and Comparison with Related Bibliometric Studies

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the analysis was restricted to the Web of Science Core Collection, potentially missing publications indexed exclusively in Scopus, PubMed, or Embase. Second, bibliometric analyses are inherently quantitative and do not capture qualitative significance, replication status, or clinical applicability of individual publications. Third, citation counts are subject to citation lag, particularly for publications from 2020 onwards. Fourth, despite careful standardization, ambiguities in institutional affiliations may have introduced minor inaccuracies. Fifth, the thematic map and keyword analyses are based on author-assigned keywords, which may not uniformly reflect the conceptual content of all publications. Sixth, the exclusion of 46 non-English publications, while necessary to ensure consistent bibliometric processing, may have introduced a degree of language bias. Given that China was the leading contributor to publication volume in this field, the omission of Chinese-language literature may have led to a partial underestimation of the true academic output and scholarly influence of Chinese institutions and researchers. This limitation should be considered when interpreting the geographic distribution of research activity, and future analyses incorporating multilingual databases may provide a more complete representation of national contributions.

The present study represents the first comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the intersection of sepsis and mitochondrial biology. The bibliometric analysis of sepsis-associated encephalopathy by Zhang et al (Front Med, 2022)10 identified neuroinflammation and blood-brain barrier disruption as dominant themes, consistent with the Niche Theme cluster identified in the present thematic map. The bibliometric analysis of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury by Chen et al (Front Med, 2025)9 similarly identified China and the United States as leading contributors and mitochondria as an emerging research keyword, corroborating the prominence of the mitochondria-AKI Motor Theme. The bibliometric analysis of endothelial cells and sepsis by Shi et al (Heliyon, 2024)12 employed a comparable methodological framework and similarly identified a tripartite co-citation knowledge structure. The present study further differentiates itself through the application of Bradford’s Law journal stratification, Lotka’s Law author productivity analysis, and bibliographic coupling network analysis.

Conclusion

This comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 3545 publications provides the first systematic overview of global research at the intersection of sepsis and mitochondrial biology over 25 years (2000–2025). The field has experienced sustained and accelerating growth, particularly following the publication of the Sepsis-3 consensus definition in 2016. China and the United States emerged as the dominant contributors in terms of publication volume and citation impact, respectively. Bradford’s Law analysis identified 30 core journals, led by Shock, Frontiers in Immunology, and Critical Care Medicine. Lotka’s Law analysis confirmed that scientific productivity follows the expected inverse square distribution.

The co-citation network revealed three foundational knowledge clusters anchored by the seminal mechanistic work of Brealey, Crouser, and Singer, while the bibliographic coupling network identified current research frontiers focused on organ-specific complications including sepsis-associated encephalopathy, sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy, and acute kidney injury. Keyword burst analysis demonstrated a coherent temporal evolution from early investigations of nitric oxide-mediated mitochondrial inhibition, through integrated organ-level studies, to the current focus on NLRP3 inflammasome activation, macrophage polarization, and organ-specific mitochondrial injury. The identification of these emerging topics suggests priority areas for future investigation and highlights opportunities for enhanced international institutional collaboration. Future studies should build upon these findings by incorporating multi-database analyses and integrative approaches combining bibliometric methods with systematic review methodologies.

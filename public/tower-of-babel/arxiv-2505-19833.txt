Additive problems on ⌊ p c ⌋ \lfloor p^{c}\rfloor
Lingyu Guo, Victor Zhenyu Guo, Li Lu

Abstract
. The sequence ℙ ( c ) = ( ⌊ p c ⌋ ) p ∈ ℙ ( c > 0 , c ∉ ℕ ) , \mathbb{P}^{(c)}=(\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor)_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\quad(c>0,c\notin\mathbb{N}), is an important subsequence of the well-known Piatetski-Shapiro sequence, where ℙ \mathbb{P} is the set of prime numbers and ⌊ ⋅ ⌋ \lfloor\cdot\rfloor is the floor function. We prove that for all c ∈ ( 0 , 13 / 15 ) c\in(0,13/15) , any large enough integer N N can be represented as N = ⌊ p c ⌋ + q , N=\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor+q, where p p and q q are primes. We also prove the result holds for almost all fixed positive c ∈ ℝ ∖ ℤ c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z} . Moreover, we investigate shifted primes in this sequence, obtaining an asymptotic formula for all c ∈ ( 0 , 13 / 15 ) c\in(0,13/15) and an almost-all result for fixed positive c ∈ ℝ ∖ ℤ c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z} .

11N05; 11B83; 11P32.

Keywords:
Piatetski-Shapiro sequences; Primes; Exponential sums; Zero density estimate.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

We are interested in the following sequence which is

\mathbb{P}^{(c)}=(\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor)_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\quad(c>0,c\notin\mathbb{N}),

where
\mathbb{P}=\{2,3,5,\cdots\}
is the set of prime numbers and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function. Note that the set
\mathbb{P}^{(c)}
is highly related to the Piatetski-Shapiro sequences of the form

\mathbb{N}^{(c)}=(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\quad(c>0,c\notin\mathbb{N}),

which is named in honor of Piatetski-Shapiro, who showed (cf.
[
11
]
) that for any fixed
c\in(1,12/11)
there are infinitely many primes in
\mathbb{N}^{(c)}
. The admissible range of
c
for this result has been extended many times over the years, and currently it is known to hold for all
c\in(1,243/205)
thanks to Rivat and Wu
[
12
]
.

Many authors have studied arithmetic properties of Piatetski-Shapiro sequences (see Baker et al.
[
2
]
and the references contained therein), and it is natural to ask whether certain properties also hold on special subsequences of the Piatetski-Shapiro sequences. The set
\mathbb{P}^{(c)}
is one of the most interesting subsequences; however, up to now very little has been established about the prime numbers in
\mathbb{P}^{(c)}
for fixed
c>1
. Balog
[
4
]
has shown that
c\in(0,5/6)
, the counting function

(1.1)

\Pi_{c,0}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}1\sim\frac{x}{c\log^{2}x}.

Moreover, Balog also showed that for almost all
c>0
, there are infinitely many primes in
\mathbb{P}^{(c)}
. For more results on
\mathbb{P}^{(c)}
, we list as follows.

•

Square-free numbers and Cube-free numbers:
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
;

•

Almost primes:
[
3
]
,
[
15
]
;

•

Exceptional sets on
N=\left\lfloor p_{1}^{c}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor p_{2}^{c}\right\rfloor
:
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
17
]
.

In this article, we analyze some additive problems on the sequence
\mathbb{P}^{(c)}
related to linear representations of prime numbers.

1.2.
Main results

Let
p,q
denote primes throughout the paper,
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
a
is an integer and
N
is an integer large enough. Define

\displaystyle\Pi_{c,a}(x)=\#\{p\leqslant x:\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor=q+a,q\in\mathbb{P}\},

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{c}(N)=\#\{m\leqslant N:N=m+q,m=\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor,q\in\mathbb{P}\}.

We prove the following result related to shifted primes.

Theorem 1
.

For all
c\in(0,13/15)
and any fixed integer
a\geqslant 0
, as
x\rightarrow\infty
we have

\Pi_{c,a}(x)=\frac{x}{c\log^{2}x}+O(\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}).

If we take
a=0
, we have the following corollary which improves the result (
1.1
) by Balog in
[
4
]
.

Corollary 1
.

For all
c\in(0,13/15)
, as
x\rightarrow\infty
we have

\Pi_{c,0}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{c\log^{2}x}+O(\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}).

We also think about the following additive problem and prove that:

Theorem 2
.

For all
c\in(0,13/15)
, any large enough integer
N
can be represented as

N=\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor+q.

Moreover, we have

\Upsilon_{c}(N)\gg\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log^{2}N}.

Our method is highly related to the behavior of zeros of the Riemann
\zeta
-function. The number
13/15
is restricted by the upper bound of the zero density estimate
N(\sigma,T)
due to Guth and Maynard
[
6
]
and the zero free region of the Riemann zeta function, where

N(\sigma,T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
\beta>\sigma,\ |\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}1,

with
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
being the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann
\zeta
-function. Without another significant improvement on the distribution of the zeros, it is not likely to recover the bound of
c
by our methods in this paper.

Hence we consider the problems in an average sense by proving an almost-all result for
c
. For the shifted prime problem, we prove the following result.

Theorem 3
.

For almost all fixed positive
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and any fixed integer
a\geqslant 1
, we have (in the sense of Lebesgue measure)

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\sup\frac{c\Pi_{c,a}(x)}{x/\log^{2}x}\geqslant 1.

We also obtain an almost-all result for the other additive problem.

Theorem 4
.

For almost all fixed positive
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and large enough integer
N
, we have (in the sense of Lebesgue measure)

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup\frac{\Upsilon_{c}(N)}{cN^{1/c}/\log^{2}N}\geqslant 1.

We remark that the density of
\left\lfloor p^{c}\right\rfloor
is lower than the density of prime numbers when
c>1
. Hence Theorem
4
can be treated as an “almost-all” approach to the binary Goldbach problem.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper, we use the symbols
O,\ll,\gg
and
\asymp
along with their standard meanings; any constants or functions implied by these symbols may depend on
c
and (where obvious) on the parameters

. We use the notation

M<m\leqslant M+M/\log M

.

The letters

always denote prime numbers. As usual,
\pi(x)
denotes the number of primes
\leqslant x
,

is the von Mangoldt function,

,

Let
\mathbf{e}(t)=e^{2\pi it}
for all

.
\|t\|
denotes the distance from the real number
t
to the nearest integer; that is,

\|t\|=\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|t-n|\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

We denote by

and
\{t\}

\leqslant t
and the fractional part of
t
. Let
\Psi(t)=t-\lfloor t\rfloor-1/2
.

2.2.
The explicit formula of
\psi(x)

The explicit formula of
\psi(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Lambda(n)
plays an important role in our proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
c>1
be a constant. Suppose that
x\leqslant c
,
T\leqslant 2
, and let
\langle x\rangle
denote the distance from
x
to the nearest prime power, other than
x
itself. Then

(2.1)

\psi_{0}(x)=x-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}-\log 2\pi-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-\frac{1}{x^{2}})+R(x,T),

where

R(x,T)\ll(\log x)\min(1,\frac{x}{T\langle x\rangle})+\frac{x}{T}(\log xT)^{2}

and

\psi_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\psi(x^{+})+\psi(x^{-})).

Proof.

See
[
9
, Theorem 12.5]
.
∎

Let
N(\sigma,T)
denote the number of zeros
\rho
of
\zeta(s)
with
\Re(\rho)\geqslant\sigma
and

N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{30(1-\sigma)/13+o(1)}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

S(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}\chi(n)n^{-s},

T_{0}+\frac{\delta}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant T_{0}+T-\frac{\delta}{2},\quad\sigma\geqslant\sigma_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad|t-t^{\prime}|\geqslant\delta

for all

and
s\neq s^{\prime}

\sum_{\chi}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{A}_{\chi}}|S(s,\chi)|^{2}\ll(qT+N)(\delta^{-1}+\log N)\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}|^{2}n^{-2\sigma_{0}}\bigg(1+\log\frac{\log 2N}{\log 2n}\bigg).

We also need the classical result of the zero-free region of the Riemann

Lemma 2.4

There is an absolute constant

Technical lemmas

We also need the following well known approximation of Vaaler

Lemma 2.5

For any

\bigg|\Psi(t)-\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H}a_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th)\bigg|\leqslant\sum_{|h|\leqslant H}b_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th),\qquad a_{h}\ll\frac{1}{|h|}\,,\qquad b_{h}\ll\frac{1}{H}\,.

Let

k\leqslant 4
. Let
\lambda_{k}\leqslant f^{(k)}(x)\leqslant h\lambda_{k}
. Let
b-a\geqslant 1
,
K=2^{k-1}

\sum_{a<n\leqslant b}\mathbf{e}(f(n))\ll(b-a)\lambda_{k}^{1/(2K-2)}+(b-a)^{1-2/K}\lambda_{k}^{-1/(2K-2)},

.

3.
Proof of theorem
1

3.1.
An initial construction

We start with the set-up by Balog
[
4
]
. Assuming
0<c<1
it is trivial that

\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor=q+a\Leftrightarrow(q+a)^{1/c}\leqslant p<(q+a+1)^{1/c}.

Hence the problem changes into a question of the sum of primes in short intervals which is

\displaystyle\Pi_{c,a}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q+a\leqslant x^{c}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(q+a)^{1/c}\leqslant p<(q+a+1)^{1/c}\end{subarray}}1

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\leqslant x^{c}}\big(\pi((q+a+1)^{1/c})-\pi((q+a)^{1/c})\big)+O(1).

Next, we change
\pi(x)
into
\psi(x)
by a partial summation and use Lemma
2.1
. Taking
T=x^{c}\log^{5}x
, it gives that

R(x,T)\ll\frac{x\log^{2}x}{T}

Then we simplify (
2.1
) to

(3.2)

\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}+O(\frac{x\log^{2}x}{T}).

We replace
q+a
by
r
. To estimate (
3.1
), it is sufficient to compute

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leqslant x^{c}}\bigg(\psi((r+1)^{1/c})-\psi(r^{1/c})\bigg)=S_{1}-S_{2}+O(S_{3}),

where

\displaystyle S_{1}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leqslant x^{c}\\
r-a\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\bigg((r+1)^{1/c}-r^{1/c}\bigg),

\displaystyle S_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leqslant x^{c}\\
r-a\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{(r+1)^{\rho/c}-r^{\rho/c}}{\rho},

\displaystyle S_{3}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leqslant x^{c}\\
r-a\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{r\log^{2}r}{T}.

according to (
3.2
).

3.2.
The estimates of
S_{1}
and
S_{3}

Note that
S_{1}
is the main term while
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
are both error terms. Since
r-a\in\mathbb{P}
,
r
is a shift of the prime
q
. By a partial summation and , we obtain that

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x^{c}}\bigg((u+1)^{1/c}-u^{1/c}\bigg)\,d\pi(u+a)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{c^{2}\log x}+O\big(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\big)-\frac{1}{c}\big(\frac{1}{c}-1\big)\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x^{c}}u^{-2+1/c}\big(1+O(u^{-1})\big)\pi(u+a)\,du.

According to prime number theorem, we have

\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x^{c}}u^{-2+1/c}\pi(u+a)\,du=\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x^{c}}u^{-2+1/c}\frac{u}{\log u}\big(1+O(\log^{-1}u)\big)\,du.

Noting that

\frac{1}{c}\big(\frac{1}{c}-1\big)\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x^{c}}u^{-2+1/c}\frac{u}{\log u}\,du=\big(\frac{1}{c}-1\big)\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x^{c}}\frac{du^{1/c}}{\log u}=\frac{1}{c}\big(\frac{1}{c}-1\big)\intop\nolimits^{x}_{2^{1/c}}\frac{dt}{\log t},

it follows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{c^{2}\log x}-\frac{1}{c}\big(\frac{1}{c}-1\big)\frac{x}{\log x}+O\big(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\big)=\frac{x}{c\log x}+O\big(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\big).

Similarly by a partial summation and prime number theorem, we get that

(3.4)

S_{3}\ll\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}.

3.3.
The estimate of
S_{2}
and the final conclusion

Now we consider
S_{2}
. We split the interval in the following way,

S_{2}=\sum_{\{R\}}\sum_{r\simeq R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{(r+1)^{\rho/c}-r^{\rho/c}}{\rho},

where
\{R\}
is a certain set of integers satisfying

R\leqslant x^{c}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\{R\}}1\ll\log^{2}x.

Define

F(s)=\sum_{r\simeq R}r^{-s}\quad\text{and}\quad C(s)=\frac{(1+\frac{1}{R})^{s}-1}{s}.

Hence we arrive at

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{R\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\sum_{r\simeq R}r^{\rho/c}\frac{\big((1+\frac{1}{r})^{\rho/c}-1\big)}{\rho}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\{R\}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\sum_{r\simeq R}r^{\rho/c}\frac{\big((1+\frac{1}{R})^{\rho/c}-1\big)}{\rho}\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\{R\}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}F(-\frac{\rho}{c})C(\frac{\rho}{c})\bigg|.

By the trivial bound
|C(s)|\ll\frac{1}{R}
, it follows that

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\{R\}}\frac{1}{R}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}F(-\frac{\rho}{c})\Big|.

Using the standard zero spacing process to the sum of
F(-\rho/c)
, namely, we divide the range of the real part of
\rho
into intervals of length
1/\log x
to get that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\Big|F(-\frac{\rho}{c})\Big|\ll\log x\sup_{\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T,\sigma<\beta\leqslant\sigma+\frac{1}{\log x}\end{subarray}}\Big|F(-\frac{\rho}{c})\Big|,

where
\beta
is the real part of
\rho
. Since for
r\simeq R
and
R\leqslant x^{c}
, we have for any
\sigma<\beta\leqslant\sigma+1/\log x
,

\big|F(-\frac{\rho}{c})\big|\ll F(-\frac{\sigma}{c}).

Hence by the Cauchy inequality, we obtain that

S_{2}\ll\log x\sum_{\{R\}}\frac{1}{R}\bigg|\sup_{\sigma}N(\sigma,T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T,\sigma<\beta\leqslant\sigma+\frac{1}{\log x}\end{subarray}}\Big(F(-\frac{\sigma}{c})\Big)^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg|.

It follows by Lemma
2.3
with
a_{n}=1
and
\chi=1
that

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\ll\log^{2}x\sum_{\{R\}}\frac{1}{R}\sup_{\sigma}N(\sigma,T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg((T+R)\sum_{r\simeq R}r^{\frac{2\sigma}{c}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\log^{2}x\sum_{\{R\}}\sup_{\sigma}N(\sigma,T)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{c}}.

By Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
, recalling that
T=x^{c}\log^{5}x
and
R\leqslant x^{c}
, we derive that

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{A}\sup_{\sigma\leqslant 1-c(\log t)^{-\frac{2}{3}}(\log\log t)^{-\frac{1}{3}}}N(\sigma,T)^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\ll x(\log x)^{A}\sup_{\sigma\leqslant 1-c(\log t)^{-\frac{2}{3}}(\log\log t)^{-\frac{1}{3}}}x^{-(1-15c/13)(1-\sigma)}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon},

where
A
is a constant and the condition
c<13/15
ensures that the supremum above contributes a power saving of
x
. Together with (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r\leqslant x^{c}}\bigg(\psi((r+1)^{1/c})-\psi(r^{1/c})\bigg)=\frac{x}{c\log x}+O(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x})+O(x^{1-\varepsilon}).

Eventually by a partial summation, it follows that

\Pi_{c,a}(x)=\frac{x}{c\log^{2}x}+O(\frac{x}{\log^{3}x})

holds for
0<c<13/15
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
2

Next we turn to the proof of Theorem
2
. Assuming
0<c<1
it is trivial that

N=m+q\Leftrightarrow\lfloor p^{c}\rfloor=N-q\Leftrightarrow(N-q)^{1/c}\leqslant p<(N-q+1)^{1/c}.

Hence the problem changes into a question of the sum of primes in short intervals which is

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{c}(N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant N-q\leqslant N-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(N-q)^{1/c}\leqslant p<(N-q+1)^{1/c}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant N-q\leqslant N-2}\big(\pi((N-q+1)^{1/c})-\pi((N-q)^{1/c})\big)+O(1).

Following the arguments that are similar to the proof of Theorem
1
, we change
\pi(x)
into
\psi(x)
by a partial summation and use Lemma
2.1
. Hence the key is to treat the following three sums

\displaystyle S_{1}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant N-q\leqslant N-2\end{subarray}}\bigg((N-q+1)^{1/c}-(N-q)^{1/c}\bigg),

\displaystyle S_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant N-q\leqslant N-2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{(N-q+1)^{\rho/c}-(N-q)^{\rho/c}}{\rho},

\displaystyle S_{3}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant N-q\leqslant N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{(N-q)^{1/c}\log^{2}(N-q)}{T}.

We note that here the difficulty is to calculate the number of integers
r
such that
N-r
is prime if we replace
N-q
by
r
. Therefore at this point we only aim to show that
S_{2}+S_{3}\ll S_{1}
.

4.1.
The order of
S_{1}

We give the order of
S_{1}
and prove that
S_{2}+S_{3}\ll S_{1}
. We begin with

S_{1}\gg\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{2\leqslant n\leqslant N-1}\Lambda(n)(N-n)^{1/c-1}.

Thus by a partial summation, we arrive at

(4.1)

S_{1}\gg\frac{1}{\log N}\intop\nolimits_{2}^{N-1}(N-u)^{1/c-1}\,d\big(\sum_{2\leqslant n\leqslant u}\Lambda(n)\big)\gg\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log N}.

4.2.
The order of
S_{2}
and
S_{3}

By taking
T=N\log^{5}N
, we immediately find that

(4.2)

S_{3}\ll\frac{1}{\log^{3}N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leqslant n\leqslant N-1\end{subarray}}N^{1/c-1}\ll\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log^{3}N}.

As for
S_{2}
, we follow the arguments in section
3.3
. Also replacing
N-q
by
r
and splitting the interval in the following way, it is obtained that

S_{2}=\sum_{\{R\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\simeq R\\
N-r\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{(r+1)^{\rho/c}-r^{\rho/c}}{\rho},

where
\{R\}
is a certain set of integers satisfying

R\leqslant x^{c}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\{R\}}1\ll\log^{2}x.

Also define

F(s)=\sum_{r\simeq R}r^{-s}\quad\text{and}\quad C(s)=\frac{(1+\frac{1}{R})^{s}-1}{s}.

Then following the arguments of section
3.3
, using the standard zero spacing process, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, Lemma
2.3
by taking
\chi=1
and
a_{n}=1
, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
we get that

(4.3)

S_{2}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}

holds for
c\in(0,13/15)
.
Eventually combining (
4.1
),(
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we achieve that

S_{1}\gg S_{2}+S_{3}.

Thus we have

\Upsilon_{c}(N)\gg\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant N-q\leqslant N-2\end{subarray}}\bigg((N-q+1)^{1/c}-(N-q)^{1/c}\bigg)

holds for
c\in(0,13/15)
.

5.
Proof of Theorem

We first give a proof of Theorem
4
, since Theorem

5.1.
The main Proposition

The following proposition is a key part of the proof.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
C>0
and
\delta>0
, we have a constant
K=K(C,\delta)
such that

\Upsilon_{c}(N)\leqslant K\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log^{2}N},

uniformly in
N\geqslant 3
,
0<c\leqslant C
and
\|c\|\geqslant\delta>0
.

Proof.

For a given
D\geqslant 3
, we have Selberg’s sieving weights
\lambda_{d}
(see Chapter 3 in
[
7
]
) satisfying

(5.1)

\lambda_{1}=1;\quad|\lambda_{d}|\leqslant 1;\quad\lambda_{d}=0\ \text{for}\ d>D;\quad\sum_{d}\sum_{[d_{1},d_{2}]=d}\frac{\lambda_{d_{1}}\lambda_{d_{2}}}{[d_{1},d_{2}]}<\frac{1}{\log D}.

We also have

(5.2)

\bigg(\sum_{d|n}\lambda_{d}\bigg)^{2}\left\{\begin{matrix}&\geqslant 0,\ &\text{trivially},\\
&=1,\ &\text{if}\ n>D\ \text{is prime}.\\
\end{matrix}\right.

According to the Selberg’s sieve, we know that

(5.3)

\rho_{d}=\sum_{d=[d_{1},d_{2}]}\lambda_{d_{1}}\lambda_{d_{2}}\left\{\begin{matrix}&=0,\ \text{if}\ d>D^{2},\\
&\ll 3^{\omega(d)},\\
\end{matrix}\right.

where
\omega(n)
denotes the number of different prime factors of
n
. By (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) we have

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{c}(N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leqslant N^{1/c}}\sum_{p^{c}-1<N-q\leqslant p^{c}}1+O(1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}}\sum_{m^{c}-1<N-n\leqslant m^{c}}\bigg(\sum_{d|m}\lambda_{d}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\sum_{t|n}\lambda_{t}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant N^{1/c}\\
p<D\end{subarray}}\sum_{p^{c}-1<N-q\leqslant p^{c}}1+\sum_{p\leqslant N^{1/c}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{c}-1<N-q\leqslant p^{c}\\
q<D\end{subarray}}1+O(1)

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}}\sum_{m^{c}-1<N-n\leqslant m^{c}}\bigg(\sum_{d|m}\lambda_{d}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\sum_{t|n}\lambda_{t}\bigg)^{2}+D+\frac{D}{c}N^{-1+1/c}.

Here we get the last term since
D
is a small power of
N
. Next we estimate the first term of (
5.4
) by changing the order of sum, which gives that

\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}}\sum_{m^{c}-1<N-n\leqslant m^{c}}\bigg(\sum_{d|m}\lambda_{d}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\sum_{t|n}\lambda_{t}\bigg)^{2}=\sum_{d\leqslant D^{2}}\sum_{t\leqslant D^{2}}\rho_{d}\rho_{t}\cdot S,

where

S=\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\sum_{m^{c}d^{c}-1<N-nt\leqslant m^{c}d^{c}}1

is the inner sum. Hence we have

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\bigg(\left\lfloor-\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}}{t}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor-\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}+1}{t}\right\rfloor\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}1+\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\bigg(\Psi\Big(-\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}+1}{t}\Big)-\Psi\Big(-\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}}{t}\Big)\bigg).

Applying Lemma
2.5
, we obtain the second sum of
S
is
S_{1}+O(S_{2})
where

S_{1}=\sum_{1\leqslant|h|\leqslant H}a_{h}\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\bigg(\mathbf{e}\big(\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}+1}{t}h\big)-\mathbf{e}\big(\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}}{t}h\big)\bigg)

and

S_{2}=\sum_{0\leqslant|h|\leqslant H}b_{h}\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\bigg(\mathbf{e}\big(\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}+1}{t}h\big)+\mathbf{e}\big(\frac{N-m^{c}d^{c}}{t}h\big)\bigg).

We first estimate
S_{1}
. Since
a_{h}\ll\frac{1}{h}
and

\mathbf{e}(\frac{h}{t})-1\ll\frac{h}{t},

we have

S_{1}\ll\frac{1}{t}\sum_{1\leqslant|h|\leqslant H}\bigg|\sum_{m\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\mathbf{e}\big(\frac{m^{c}d^{c}h}{t}\big)\bigg|.

To make
S_{1}
a error term, we only need to show

\mathcal{T}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max_{m^{\prime}\leqslant N^{1/c}/d}\sum_{1\leqslant|h|\leqslant H}\bigg|\sum_{m\sim m^{\prime}}\mathbf{e}\big(\frac{m^{c}d^{c}h}{t}\big)\bigg|\ll N^{1/c-\varepsilon}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.6
and summing over
h
trivially, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\ll N^{\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1-k/c}{2^{k}-2}+\varepsilon}d^{\frac{k-2^{k}+2}{2^{k}-2}}H^{1+\frac{1}{2^{k}-2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2^{k}-2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +N^{\frac{1}{c}-\frac{4}{c2^{k}}-\frac{1-k/c}{2^{k}-2}+\varepsilon}d^{\frac{4}{2^{k}}-\frac{2^{k}-2+k}{2^{k}-2}}H^{1-\frac{1}{2^{k}-2}}t^{\frac{1}{2^{k}-2}}.

Since
D
and
H
are both small powers of
x
, thus the integer
k
satisfies the inequality

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{matrix}&\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1-k/c}{2^{k}-2}+\varepsilon<\frac{1}{c},\\
&\frac{1}{c}-\frac{4}{c2^{k}}-\frac{1-k/c}{2^{k}-2}+\varepsilon<\frac{1}{c},\\
\end{matrix}\right.

which concludes that
k=\left\lfloor c+2\right\rfloor
is the solution of (
5.5
). By taking

H=D^{3}\quad\text{and}\quad D=N^{\frac{1}{8(C+1)2^{C}}},

we ensure that
\mathcal{T}=O(N^{1/c-\varepsilon})
. As for
S_{2}
, we only need to treat the situation when
h=0
, since the case when
|h|>0
is the same as
S_{1}
. The contribution of
S_{2}

when
h=0
is

\ll\frac{N^{1/c}}{dH}\ll N^{1/c-\varepsilon}.

By (
5.4
), we finally achieve that

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{c}(N)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{d\leqslant D^{2}}\sum_{t\leqslant D^{2}}\rho_{d}\rho_{t}\frac{N^{1/c}}{dt}+O(\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log^{3}N})

\displaystyle\ll\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log^{2}D}+O(\frac{N^{1/c}}{\log^{3}N}),

where the implied constant only depends on the constant
\|c\|=\delta
and
C
. The proof is complete.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem 4

We consider
\Upsilon_{c}(N)
as a function of
c
and define

F_{N}(c)=\Upsilon_{c}(N)\bigg/\big(\frac{cN^{1/c}}{\log^{2}N}\big).

We shall show that
F_{N}(c)
is bigger than a constant on average. By Proposition
5.1
, we know

F_{N}(c)\leqslant K(C,\delta)

uniformly holds for
0<c\leqslant C
and
\|c\|>\delta>0
. Also the Lebesgue measure of the natural numbers is negligible, which ensures that
F_{N}(c)
is integrable on an interval.

Let
B>A\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
. We start by the definition of
F_{N}(c)

\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{N}(c)dc=\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}\frac{\log^{2}N}{cN^{1/c}}\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant N^{1/c}}\sum_{p^{c}-1<N-q\leqslant p^{c}}1+O(1)\bigg)\,dc.

Changing the order of integral and sum, we obtain that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{N}(c)dc=\log^{2}N\sum_{p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{A}<N-q\leqslant p^{B}\\
2\leqslant q<N\end{subarray}}\intop\nolimits_{A^{\prime}}^{B^{\prime}}\frac{1}{c}N^{-1/c}dc+O(N^{-1/c+\varepsilon}),

where

A^{\prime}=\max(A,\frac{\log(N-q)}{\log p}),\quad B^{\prime}=\min(B,\frac{\log(N-q+1)}{\log p}).

Since
p^{A}<N-q\leqslant p^{B}
, it follows that

(5.7)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}=\frac{\log(N-q)}{\log p}\quad\text{and}\quad B^{\prime}=\frac{\log(N-q+1)}{\log p}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{\frac{\log(N-q)}{\log p}}^{\frac{\log(N-q+1)}{\log p}}\frac{1}{c}N^{-1/c}dc

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\log p}{\log(N-q+1)}N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log(N-q)}}\cdot(\frac{\log(N-q+1)}{\log p}-\frac{\log(N-q)}{\log p})

(5.8)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log(N-q)}}}{\log(N-q+1)}\big(\frac{1}{N-q}-\frac{2}{(N-q)^{2}}\big),

where we use the inequality

\log(1+x)\geqslant x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\quad\text{for all}\ x\geqslant 0.

Inserting (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) into (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{N}(c)dc

\displaystyle\geqslant\log^{2}N\sum_{p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{A}<N-q\leqslant p^{B}\\
2\leqslant q<N\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log(N-q)}}}{\log(N-q+1)}\big(\frac{1}{N-q}-\frac{2}{(N-q)^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle+O(N^{-1/c+\varepsilon})

(5.9)

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+o(1))\log^{2}N\sum_{N^{\varepsilon}\leqslant p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{A}<N-q\leqslant p^{B}\\
2\leqslant q<N\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log(N-q)}}}{(N-q)\log(N-q+1)}.

Recalling that
q
is a prime, we derive that the inner sum over
q
is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{A}<N-q\leqslant p^{B}\\
2\leqslant q<N\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log(N-q)}}}{(N-q)\log(N-q+1)}=\intop\nolimits_{p^{A}}^{\min(p^{B},N)}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log t}}}{t\log(t+1)}d\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{A}<xN-q\leqslant t\\
q\in\mathbb{P}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\quad=\intop\nolimits_{p^{A}}^{\min(p^{B},N)}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log t}}}{t\log(t+1)}d\Big(\frac{N-t}{\log(N-t)}-\pi(N-t)\Big)

(5.10)

\displaystyle\qquad-\intop\nolimits_{p^{A}}^{\min(p^{B},N)}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log t}}}{t\log(t+1)}d\frac{N-t}{\log(N-t)}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=I_{1}-I_{2}.

Here we restrict
p>N^{1/B}
to make
p^{B}>N
. Thus by prime number theorem, we have
I_{1}=o(I_{2})
. Hence by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) it follows that,

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{N}(c)dc

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+o(1))\log^{2}N\sum_{N^{1/B}<p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\intop\nolimits_{p^{A}}^{N}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log t}}}{t\log(t+1)\log(N-t)}dt

(5.11)

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+o(1))\sum_{N^{1/B}<p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\intop\nolimits_{p^{A}}^{N}\frac{N^{-\frac{\log p}{\log t}}}{t}dt.

Next replace
\log t
by
u
. It follows that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{N}(c)dc

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+o(1))\sum_{N^{1/B}<p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\intop\nolimits_{A\log p}^{\log N}e^{-\frac{\log p\log N}{u}}du

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\sum_{N^{1/B}<p\leqslant N^{1/A}}\log p\intop\nolimits_{A}^{\frac{\log N}{\log p}}e^{-\frac{\log N}{u}}du.

Recalling that
p
is also a prime, by prime number theorem and a partial summation, we obtain that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{N}(c)dc

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+o(1))\intop\nolimits_{N^{1/B}}^{N^{1/A}}\intop\nolimits_{A}^{\log N/\log t}e^{-\frac{\log N}{u}}dudt

(5.12)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}e^{-\frac{\log N}{u}}\intop\nolimits_{N^{1/B}}^{N^{1/u}}dtdu=B-A+o(1).

Now we are interested in the following set

S^{*}=\{c>0:\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup F_{N}(c)<1\}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{T=3}^{\infty}\bigcup_{m=3}^{\infty}\bigcup_{N=3}^{\infty}\bigcap_{n>N}^{\infty}S(J_{k},m,n),

where

(5.13)

J_{k}=[k+\frac{1}{T},k+1-\frac{1}{T}],

and

S(J_{k},m,n)=S=\{c\in J_{k}:F_{n}(c)\leqslant 1-\frac{1}{m}\}.

We shall show that for fixed
m
and large enough integer
n
,
\mathcal{L}(S)=0
, which implies that
\mathcal{L}(S^{*})=0
. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}(S)=l
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there is a finite cover
I_{\omega}
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
\Omega
is an index set such that

S\subseteq\bigcup_{\omega\in\Omega}I_{\omega}\subseteq J_{k},\quad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{L}(I)<l+\varepsilon.

Now we fix an
I\subseteq J_{k}
. By (
5.12
), we have for large enough integer
n

(5.14)

\intop\nolimits_{I}F_{n}(c)\,dc\geqslant\mathcal{L}(I)(1-\frac{1}{2m}).

While from the definition of
S(J_{k},m,n)
and Proposition
5.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{I}F_{n}(c)\,dc

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{S\cap I}F_{n}(c)\,dc+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{L}(I)-\mathcal{L}(S\cap I)}F_{n}(c)\,dc

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{L}(S\cap I)(1-\frac{1}{m})+(\mathcal{L}(I)-\mathcal{L}(S\cap I))K.

Note that the constant
K
here only depends on
J
. Comparing (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), we get

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(S\cap I)\leqslant\big(1-\frac{1}{2mK-2m+2}\big)\mathcal{L}(I).

Summing over
I_{\omega}
and putting (
5.16
) into the sum we have

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(S)=\mathcal{L}\big(\bigcup_{\omega\in\Omega}(S\cap I)\big)\leqslant\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{L}(S\cap I)

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(1-\frac{1}{2mK-2m+2}\big)\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{L}(I)

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(1-\frac{1}{2mK-2m+2}\big)(l+\varepsilon).

This is impossible except that
l=0
. Here the implied constant
\varepsilon
depends on
K
and
m
.

5.3.
A sketch proof of Theorem
3

Following the arguments of the proof of Proposition
5.1
it can be easily proved for any
C>0
and
\delta>0
, we have a
K=K(C,\delta)
such that

\Pi_{c,a}(x)\leqslant K\frac{x}{\log^{2}x},

uniformly in
N\geqslant 3
,
0<c\leqslant C
and
\|c\|\geqslant\delta>0
. Now we consider
\Pi_{c,a}(x)
as a function of
c
and define

(5.17)

F_{x,a}(c)=\Pi_{c,a}(x)\bigg/\big(\frac{x}{c\log^{2}x}\big).

We shall show that
F_{x,a}(c)
is exact
1
in average.

Let
B>A>0
. Similar to the last proof, we start with

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{x,a}(c)dc

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{2}x}{x}\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\sum_{p^{c}-1<q+a\leqslant p^{c}}c\,dc

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{2}x}{x}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\sum_{p^{A}<q+a\leqslant p^{B}}\intop\nolimits_{\frac{\log(q+a)}{\log p}}^{\frac{\log(q+a+1)}{\log p}}c\,dc

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{2}x}{x}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\sum_{p^{A}<q+a\leqslant p^{B}}\frac{\log q}{q\log^{2}p}(1+o(1)).

By a simple application of prime number theorem and a partial summation, it is obtained that

(5.18)

\intop\nolimits_{A}^{B}F_{x,a}(c)dc=(B-A)+o(1),

where the implied constant depends
A
and
B
and tends to
0
as
x
tends to
+\infty
.

Similarly, we define that

S^{*}=\{c>0:\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\sup F_{x,a}(c)<1\}=\bigcup_{N=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{T=3}^{\infty}\bigcup_{m=3}^{\infty}\bigcup_{X=3}^{\infty}\bigcap_{x>X}^{\infty}S(J_{N},m,x),

where

J_{N}=[N+\frac{1}{T},N+1-\frac{1}{T}],

and

S(J_{N},m,x)=S=\{c\in J:F_{x,a}(c)\leqslant 1-\frac{1}{m}\}.

In this part, we still aim to prove
\mathcal{L}(S^{*})=0
to obtain Theorem 3. Suppose that
\mathcal{L}(S)=l
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite cover
I_{\omega}
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
\Omega
is index set, such that

S\subseteq\bigcup_{\omega\in\Omega}I_{\omega}\subseteq J,\quad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{L}(I)<l+\varepsilon.

Next we fix an
I\subseteq J
. By (
5.18
) and (
5.17
), for large enough
x
we still have an upper and lower bound

(5.19)

\intop\nolimits_{I}F_{x,a}(c)dc\geqslant\mathcal{L}(I)(1-\frac{1}{2m})

and

(5.20)

\intop\nolimits_{I}F_{x,a}(c)\,dc\leqslant\mathcal{L}(S\cap I)(1-\frac{1}{m})+(\mathcal{L}(I)-\mathcal{L}(S\cap I))KN.

Note that the constant

\mathcal{L}(S\cap I)\leqslant\big(1-\frac{1}{2mKN-2m+2}\big)\mathcal{L}(I),

\varepsilon

K
,

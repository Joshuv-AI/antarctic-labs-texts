Establishment of a new method for detection of TROP2-positive circulating tumor cells in breast cancer
Authors: Anqi Wang, Pingping Zeng, Tianyi Ma, Haoyuan Shi, Ruifang Li, Hang Xu, Yinghua Feng, Qiongming Liu, Mengting Wang, Tingmei Chen, Zhiyuan Hu, Rixiong Wang, Ying Zhou, Xiaopeng Hao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Trophoblast cell surface antigen 2 (TROP2) overexpression has been demonstrated in several tumor types, including breast cancer (BC). Circulating tumor cells (CTCs) offer a non-invasive, dynamic and real-time detection method. The detection of TROP2 expression in CTCs is significant for predicting BC progression, prognosis and the efficacy of targeted therapy. A quantitative analysis method for TROP2 of CTCs was established using our established TUMORFISHER detection platform based on epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM), and a new magnetic nanoparticle with TROP2 as the trapping target was developed, named TROP2@MNPs. The developed quantitative analysis of TROP2 was an effective method for accurately identifying the expression of TROP2 in BC cells and CTCs from BC patients, which was consistent with the data obtained by immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis. The established TROP2@MNPs could specifically capture TROP2-positive BC cells, and the capture efficiency was closely related to the expression of TROP2 in CTCs. Notably, the TROP2-based enrichment strategy was found to capture TROP2-expressing CTCs that were missed by the EpCAM-based enrichment strategy. The TROP2-targeting CTC capture platform, TROP2@MNPs, could serve as a liquid biopsy of TROP2 expression in BC patients, providing an important reference for further research on CTC-related diagnosis and individualised treatment employing TROP2-targeting drugs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14184-y.

Background

Recently, the World Health Organization (WHO) reported 19,964,811 new cases of cancer worldwide in 2022, with breast cancer (BC) accounting for 2,308,897 cases (11.6%) and ranking second in new cancer cases [1]. Besides, 665,684 individuals died of BC, placing a significant burden on society and the public health system [1]. Thus, novel methods for effective diagnosis and treatment of BC are urgently needed to overcome this global challenge [2, 3]. In the field of precision medicine, BC is categorized into different subtypes based on specific molecular and genetic characteristics [4]. Patients with the triple-negative type of BC have the worst prognosis because of the lack of both HER-2 and hormone receptors, resulting in ineffectiveness of many targeted-therapies [5]. In recent years, a large number of studies have been devoted to the development and validation of novel molecular biomarkers to provide patients with more comprehensive prognostic information and predict efficacy of treatment [6–8].

TROP2 is encoded by the tumor-associated calcium signal transducer 2 (TACSTD2) gene [9], which belongs to the same family as epithelial cell adhesion molecules (EpCAMs). The downstream signals of TROP2 may promote cancer cell survival, proliferation, migration, and invasion, making it an important target of cancer-targeted therapy. Targeting TROP2 can result in a reduction of treatment toxicity, which represents a key advantage of anti-TROP2 antibody–drug conjugate therapy [10, 11]. In clinical trials, antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs, e.g. sacituzumab govitecan) have a therapeutic index advantage over conventional chemotherapy, providing favorable clinical benefit even at low levels of TROP2 expression [12]. TROP2 is overexpressed in BC, especially in triple-negative BC, providing new treatment options for patients who are unable to use HER-2-targeted therapies and endocrine therapies [13]. In order to ascertain the potential response to anti-TROP2 therapy, it is essential to determine the expression status of TROP2 in BC patients.

Circulating tumor cell (CTC) is a mediator of tumor hematogenous metastasis, which is shed from primary tumor and plays a pivotal role in tumor metastasis through the circulatory system [14]. Given the heterogeneity between primary and metastatic tumor and the dynamic changes of tumor during the course of the disease, CTC detection in peripheral venous blood can dynamically monitor tumors in a simple and non-invasive manner [15, 16]. As a representative technology of liquid biopsy, CTCs are intact tumor cells carrying multi-omics information of tumor cells (genome, transcriptome, proteome, metabolome, etc.). Captured CTCs can be analyzed in vitro to assess tumor cell morphology and function, which has broad applications in evaluating anti-tumor efficacy, prognostic assessment and facilitating treatment decision-making. Detecting the expression of TROP2 on CTCs is of great significance in predicting disease progression, prognosis, and the efficacy of targeted therapy [17–19].

In our previous studies, we constructed a CTC detection platform and developed an effective method to capture CTCs from peripheral venous blood (PVB) using peptide magnetic nanoparticles (Pep@MNPs) [20–24]. A mere 2.0 mL of peripheral blood was collected, and the characteristics of CTCs captured by Pep@MNPs were proved to be of significant predictive and prognostic guidance value for patients with metastatic breast cancer (MBC) [20–24]. In this study, the CTC detection platform was employed as the foundation to develop a diagnostic method based on CTC TROP2 expression in BC patients and provide a liquid biopsy solution for precision therapy targeting TROP2.

Materials and methods

Study design

In this study, we aimed to detect TROP2 expression levels in BC patients by peripheral blood CTC. We first applied conventional EpCAM targeting to capture CTCs and analyzed their expression levels by immunofluorescence, but this may lead to loss of information on EpCAM-negative but TROP2-positive CTCs. Based on this, we established a CTC capture method directly targeting TROP2, which was optimized and validated at the level of BC cell lines, and made a preliminary attempt in peripheral blood of clinical BC patients. The study flowchart is shown in Figure S1.

Cell lines and cell culture

The human BC cell lines MDA-MB-453 (CL-0152B), MDA-MB-468 (CL-0290B), MDA-MB-231 (CL-0150B), and MCF-7 (CL-0149) were purchased from Wuhan Pricella Biotechnology Co. The human BC cell lines MDA-MB-453, MDA-MB-468, and MDA-MB-231 were cultured in DMEM complete medium, whereas MCF-7 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 complete medium. It was essential to supplement all complete culture media with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. The cell culture environment was maintained at 37 °C, 5% CO2 and 90% relative humidity.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumor tissues from BC patients were embeded, sectioned, dewaxed, and dehydrated. We used 0.01 M citrate buffer for heat-induced antigen retrieval for 10 min and then blocked the tissue sections with animal serum. After that, the sections were sequentially titrated with primary antibody and incubated at 4℃ overnight. Then secondary antibody (horseradish enzyme labeled) was added and incubated at 37℃ for 30 min. Finally we performed chromogenic reaction with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) and hematoxylin restaining. Following sealing the sections, we examined it and calculated immunohistochemistry (IHC) score under the microscope. H‑score = (1 × % weak positive cells) + (2 × % moderate positive cells) + (3 × % strong positive cells), weak positive (1 +): Faint but discernible staining requiring 40 × magnification for confirmation, moderate positive (2 +): distinct intermediate staining visible at 20 × magnification, strong positive (3 +): intense staining clearly identifiable at 10 × magnification. Scoring range: 0–300.

FACS and immunofluorescence

To establish a fluorescence quantification system for the detection of TROP2 expression by FACS and immunofluorescence, anti-TROP2 antibody (Abcam, ab214488) was labeled by Alexa Fluor™ 546 Protein Labeling Kit (Thermo, A10237). The BC cell lines were incubated with anti-TROP2-546 antibody. Secondary antibody was Goat Anti-Mouse IgG H&L/Cy5 (Bioss, BA04296728). Nuclei were stained with DAPI staining (Abcam, ab285390). TROP2 expression was detected using an ultrasensitive nano-flow cytometer (YYL-NXT) (Attune NxT). Fluorescence microscopy (OLYMPUS IX73) was used to observe the staining results.

Isolation of mononuclear cells from peripheral venous blood of human blood

We took 15 mL of room temperature buffer and blood and added 3 mL of lymphocyte isolate which was purchased from TBDScience. The blood was then slowly spread on the upper layer of the separation solution. After centrifugation at 800 g for 20 min at room temperature, the liquid formed four layers and the white membrane layer was aspirated. Subsequently, the cells in the white membrane layer were resuspended with PBS exclusive of calcium and magnesium. Then they were centrifuged at 300 g for 10 min and repeated once. The final precipitate was the peripheral venous blood mononuclear cells.

Western blot

Cells were lysed with NP-40 lysate (Boster, AR0107-100). After addition of 0.1% Triton X-100 (P0096) and protease inhibitor (MedChemExpress, HY-K0010) for 15 min, cells were then sonicated with an ultrasonic lyser. The protein concentration was determined to be 1 μg/μL by bicinchoninic acid (BCA). Sodium dodecyl sulphate (SDS) (Meilunbio, MA0053) was used as electrophoresis buffer, and protein samples were separated on a precast protein preparation gel (36255ES10) and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. After blocking with 5% skimmed milk (CST, 9999)/TBST for 1 h, we incubated the membranes with primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. After primary antibody recovery, the membrane was incubated with goat anti-rabbit-HRP (Biorad, 1706515) for 1 h. Finally, the luminescent signal was detected using ECL substrate.

Preparation and characterization of TROP2@MNPs

Anti-TROP2 antibody (Abcam, ab214488) was labeled by biotin labeling kit (Beijing Biodragon Immunotechnologies Corporation). After resuspending the magnetic beads (Ademtech, Bio-Adembeads Streptavidin 0312) by shaking, we pipetted 100 μL of magnetic beads and performed magnetic separation. The magnetic beads were saturated by adding biotinylated antibody. After three additions of buffer (PBST, pH 7.4, 0.05% Tween-20, 0.1% BSA), the magnetic beads were resuspended by shaking and magnetically separated. Then we added 1 mg/mL biotinylated antibody and washed the beads 5 times. Finally, we added buffer and resuspended the magnetic beads. We prepared samples of magnetic beads reacting with TROP2 antibody for different times. After dilution, the particle size of the magnetic beads was measured by the Zetasizer Nano ZS-90 laser particle sizer analyzer. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) were used for the characterization. Firstly, we dropped 10 μL of samples onto a double-sided smooth carbon film supported copper mesh, and removed the supernatant after 10 min and placed in a dry oven for use. Then we observed under TEM at a voltage of 120 kV. Subsequently, 10 μL of 1 mg/mL TROP2@MNPs was incubated with BC cells for 60 min. The samples were washed twice and fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde overnight at room temperature. Then we dehydrated them with different concentrations of ethanol solution before SEM. Finally, the samples to be tested were diluted and transferred to an optical cuvette before UV spectrophotometric detection.

Breast cancer cell spike-in assay

The collected cells were counted and made into a cell suspension of 20 cells/µL. Then, we mixed 2 mL healthy human peripheral blood with the corresponding volume of cell suspension, 8 mL of PBS and 10 µL of TROP2/Pep@MNPs magnetic beads, followed by incubating at room temperature for 1 h. After enrichment of the cells at room temperature for 20 min, we took advantage of a magnetic field to hold captured cells tightly on the inner wall of the centrifuge tube and washed the bottoms of the tubes with PBS to adequately remove erythrocytes, platelets and other impurities. After repeating the washing step for 2 times, we prepared cellular immunofluorescence films (CK-FITC, TROP2-546, CD45-594). Finally, images were captured using the OLYMPUS IX73 fluorescence microscope.

Isolation and characterization of CTC

The TUMORFISHER kit based on the anti-EpCAM peptide-coated magnetic bead separation system was used to isolate CTCs or added cell line cells from PVB. The basic principle is that synthesized peptides with specific recognition of EpCAM, coupled with biotin and forming clusters, were attached to streptavidin-coupled iron oxide magnetic nanoparticles to form a sea urchin-like structure that can bind to CTCs via the interaction of EpCAMs on CTCs with peptides of the magnetic nanoparticles. Therefore, CTCs or cells expressing EpCAM can be pulled down through a magnet and isolated from other blood cells [24].

We used TROP2@MNPs to capture TROP2-positive CTCs based on TUMORFISHER kit. Firstly, the prepared TROP2@MNPs were mixed with clinical blood samples from BC patients and incubated. During the incubation, TROP2@MNPs specifically bound to the TROP2 antigen on the surface of CTCs via TROP2 antibodies on their surface. Upon completion of the incubation, the samples were placed on a magnetic rack and the CTCs bound to TROP2@MNPs were separated from complex cell mixtures or blood samples using magnetic separation. Subsequently, CTCs were incubated with CK-FITC and CD45-594 antibody for 1 h. Cell nuclei were stained with DAPI for 10 min and then sealed. Finally, the captured CTCs were visualized and counted under a fluorescence microscope.

Calcein-AM/PI staining

After capturing BC cells with TROP2@MNPs, they were incubated with 2 μM calcein-AM for 20 min at room temperature. Then we added 5 μM propidium iodide (PI) stock solution for 5 min, before washing and observing them.

Bioinformatics methods

Statistical analysis of the data was performed using R (version 4.3.0) and GraphPad Prism software (version 9.5). Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages (%), and continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD. For comparison of two groups of data, Student’s t-test was used if the data followed a normal distribution; otherwise, Mann–Whitney U test was used. For comparison of categorical outcomes, chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we used the Seurat FindMarkers function based on Wilcox likelihood-ratio test with default parameters, and selected the genes expressed in more than 10% of the cells in a cluster and with an average log(Fold Change) value greater than 0.25 as DEGs. For the cell type annotation of each cluster, we combined the expression of canonical markers found in the DEGs with knowledge from literatures, and displayed the expression of markers of each cell type with heatmaps/dot plots/violin plots that were generated with Seurat DoHeatmap/DotPlot/Vlnplot function

Gene knockdown and functional assays

Targeting the human TROP2 gene (NCBI Gene ID: 4070), we designed four specific siRNA sequences [TROP2-Homo-1020: 5'-GGUGGAGAUCAAGGAACUGTT-3' (sense), 3'-CAGUUCCUUGAUCUCCACCTT-5' (antisense); TROP2-Homo-861: 5'-CGCUCAUCUAUUACCUGGATT-3' (sense), 3'-UCCAGGUAAUAGAUGAGCGTT-5' (antisense); TROP2-Homo-199: 5'-ACGUGUCCCACCAACAAGATT-3' (sense), 3'-UCUUGUUGGUGGGACACGUTT-5' (antisense); TROP2-Homo-713: 5'-GGCAGAACACGUCUCAGAATT-3' (sense), 3'-UUCUGAGACGUGUUCUGCCTT-5' (antisense)] and a scrambled negative control (siNC) were transfected into MCF7 cells using Lipofectamine 3000. Knockdown efficiency was validated by western blotting at 72 h post-transfection.

Wound healing assay: Confluent monolayers were scratched with a 200 μl pipette tip. Migration distance was measured at 0, 12, and 24 h using ImageJ. Data represent three independent experiments with quintuplicate wells.

Transwell invasion assay: Cells (5 × 104) in serum-free medium were seeded into Matrigel-coated (1 mg/ml) inserts (8 μm pores, Corning). After 24 h, invaded cells were fixed (4% PFA), stained (0.1% crystal violet), and counted in five random fields/insert. Data are mean ± SD. Differences were analyzed by one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test (GraphPad Prism 9). p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Patients

This retrospective open non-randomized study included 11 patients with BC and 6 healthy volunteers from the First Medical Center of Chinese PLA General Hospital. Their peripheral venous blood samples were collected for CTC analysis. TROP2 typing of the primary tumor sites was determined through clinical IHC. Written informed consent was obtained from all participating patients and healthy volunteers. Patients must meet certain criteria: patient’s ECOG performance status score of 0 to 3, TROP2 status, and clinical information reflecting disease status and disease progression. CTC counts were performed using 2.0 ml of PVB, and the number of CTCTROP2+ was compared to the TROP2 status of the primary tumor. TROP2 assessment in pathologic tissue biopsies was performed using a clinical IHC protocol. Disease status was assessed using the Response Evaluation Criteria for Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1.

Statistical analyses

Experimental data were processed using GraphPad Prism 8 statistical software. Differences between independent groups were analyzed using t-test. Differences among multiple groups were analyzed using ANOVA. For small sample sizes, nonparametric Spearman’s ρ with bootstrapped 95% CIs (1,000 iterations) was used as the primary correlation metric due to its robustness to non-normality and limited data. All analyses were performed in R v4.2.0 (ppcor, BayesFactor, and boot packages), with p < 0.05 (two-tailed) considered significant.

In order to ascertain the optimal concentration of TROP2 direct-label antibody for CTC detection, immunofluorescence staining was conducted with varying concentrations of TROP2 antibodies on MDA-MB-453 cells and PBMCs. The fluorescence intensity of individual cells was subsequently analyzed to determine the optimal antibody concentration, which was found to be 1.25 μg/mL (Fig. 1E). For further evaluation of the molecular characteristics of the selected cells, we employed an effective CTC capture method, namely immunomagnetic enrichment method of peptide magnetic beads (TUMORFISHER). The results demonstrated that MDA-MB-453 cells exhibited the molecular characteristics of tumor cells, EpCAM+/CK+/CD45−/TROP2+, while PBMCs displayed the molecular characteristics of EpCAM−/CK−/CD45+/TROP2− (Fig. 1H and I). In a whole blood background, we determined the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of TROP2 (Fig. 1E-G), and found that the number of captured MDA-MB-453 cells was significantly higher than that of PBMCs.

We assessed the clinical and histological characteristics of BC patients on a routine basis (Table S1). The detection of TROP2 expression in primary tumor tissues by IHC demonstrated that TROP2 IHC were all positive in these BC patients (Fig. 2B). However, no significant correlation was found between the amount of CTCTROP2+ (r = − 0.2357, p = 0.6109) and its TROP2 MFI (r = 0.1280, p = 0.7845) and IHC score. Therefore, we hypothesized that this was due to potential limitations of the EpCAM-based CTC capture method in accurately reflecting tumor TROP2 expression profiles. Considering the high degree of homology between TROP2 and EpCAM, which may be a potential target for CTC capture, perhaps directly targeting TROP2 for CTC capture is a more appropriate strategy.

We measured the hydrodynamic particle size of anti-TROP2 magnetic beads (TROP2@MNPs) which consisted of naked magnetic beads with TROP2 antibodies. The results showed that when the TROP2 antibodies were modified onto the surface of bare magnetic beads by streptavidin–biotin interaction, the particle size of the magnetic beads increased and the diameter of the magnetic beads gradually increased over time, which was gradually stabilized at 60 min (Fig. 3B and C). This indicated that the TROP2 antibodies were successfully and progressively attached to the surface of the bare magnetic beads. The UV–visible spectra of MNPs and TROP2@MNPs demonstrated that the anti-TROP2 antibodies were present on the MNPs (Fig. 3D).

The observation of the coupling of streptavidin with the TROP2 antibodies on bare magnetic beads revealed good dispersion and increased particle size of the beads. Furthermore, the prepared beads encapsulated a fuzzy membrane, in comparison to the bare magnetic beads with a smooth surface (Fig. 3E). Observing of the cell surface captured by TROP2@MNPs, we found that TROP2@MNPs adhered to the BC cells while the naked magnetic beads were unable (Fig. 3F).

These results indicated that the bare magnetic beads were successfully linked to the TROP2 antibodies.

Selection of BC cell lines

In order to assess the efficiency of TROP2@MNPs to capture CTCs, we examined the expression levels of TROP2 and EpCAM in four BC cell lines (MDA-MB-453, MCF-7, MDA-MB-468, and MDA-MB-231) and PBMCs. Similar EpCAM fluorescence intensity was observed in the first three BC cell lines, whereas no EpCAM+ was observed in MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 4G). TROP2 was expressed in all four BC cell lines, whereas no EpCAM and TROP2 expression was observed in PBMCs (Fig. 4H). Western blot also showed the consistent results as immunofluorescence staining analysis (Fig. 4F and I).

We next compared TROP2@MNPs and EpCAM@MNPs. The results showed that for BC cells with high TROP2 and high EpCAM expression, the capture efficiency of EpCAM@MNPs was mostly lower than that of TROP2@MNPs (Fig. 5C). Besides, the difference in the capture rate of BC cells by TROP2@MNPs was significantly correlated with the expression level of TROP2 in BC cells (Fig. 5D).

Using EpCAM@MNPs and TROP2@MNPs to capture BC cells pre-stained with DAPI and TROP2, we observed that significantly more TROP2@MNPs were adsorbed on TROP2-highly-expressing CTCs than EpCAM@MNPs (Fig. 5E). The above results further validated that TROP2@MNPs had the ability to specifically capture CTCTROP2+.

Optimization of capture conditions

After testing, we found the concentrations of TROP2@MNPs had a better capture efficiency in the appropriate range. In this study, we compared the capture efficiencies of five concentrations of TROP2@MNPs. The capture efficiency of MDA-MB-468 cells was the highest and remained stable as the concentration of TROP2@MNPs increased to 10 μg/mL (Fig. 5F). Therefore, the optimal concentration of TROP2@MNPs for cell capture was determined to be 10 μg/mL.

The capture time of the magnetic bead incubation was also optimized in this study. It was found that the capture efficiency gradually improved with the increasing incubation time and the optimal capture efficiency was reached at 60 min (Fig. 5G). Finally, we examined TROP2@MNPs at 4 °C after different weeks to compare the capture efficiency. The results showed that TROP2@MNPs could maintain long-term stability at 4 weeks (Fig. 5H).

Capture of simulated environmental BC cells by TROP2@MNPs

To determine the stability of the capture efficiency of TROP2@MNPs, we compared the capture efficiency of TROP2@MNPs to four types of BC cells with cell concentrations of 100 and 200 cells/mL in PBS or whole blood environments. The results showed that there was no statistically significant difference in the capture efficiency between the 100 cell spiked group and the 200 cell spiked group in two different environments (Fig. 5I and J). And the results were consistent under the same circumstances (Fig. 5K and L).

After capturing BC cells with TROP2@MNPs, we assessed the activity of cells by staining with calcein AM and PI dye. The results showed that most of the MDA-MB-468 cells were still viable after release and the large number of magnetic beads adsorbed on the cell membrane surface which did not affect the cell states (Fig. 5M). These findings indicated that TROP2@MNPs had good biocompatibility and low toxicity to CTCs.

Determination of CTCs

We identified CTCs captured by TROP2@MNPs in the whole blood environment by adding BC cells to 2 mL whole blood collected from healthy donors. Cells with molecular characteristics of CK+/DAPI+/CD45− were identified as CTCs, whereas CK−/DAPI+/CD45+ were leukocytes (Fig. 5N). This detection method provided important assurance for the application of TROP2@MNPs for CTC capture in clinical blood samples.

Discussion

Efficient in vitro diagnostics and targeted cancer therapy require the precise identification of molecular targets that are only present or highly expressed in tumor cells, but not expressed in normal tissues [25, 26]. In order to achieve precise strikes on tumor, the impact of the drug on normal tissues needs to be minimized at the same time [27–30]. TROP2, a product of the TACSTD2 gene [31], is highly expressed in the majority of human cancers, but is defective in normal tissues. This makes it be an ideal target for cancer therapy. It is important to accurately monitor the expression status of target molecules when using targeted drugs. IHC requires surgical or puncture procedures to obtain patient tissue samples, which are unable to monitor tumor changes and have various limitations in sampling primary or metastatic sites. CTCs as mediators of tumor hematogenous metastasis, shed from the primary tumor and transfer to the appropriate site through bloodstream. The modern CTCs capture technology enables venous blood sampling at multiple time points in a patient’s cancer treatment course, and assesses CTCs in terms of efficacy, prediction and prognosis.

The clinical significance of CTC testing has been further clarified in accordance with the 8 th edition of AJCC Manual of Cancer Staging. In addition to maintaining the cM0(i +) staging, the manual has provided additional insights into the clinical value of CTC testing [32–34]. Through the gradual improvement of the guideline, CTCs have been formally entered into the tumor staging system, which improves the tumor staging system, and provides a more comprehensive guideline for the diagnosis of the tumors and the assessment of the prognosis [35]. The number of CTCs has been shown to be an independent predictor of tumor progression. The detection of CTCs can monitor tumor development. Given the heterogeneity between primary and metastatic tumors and the dynamic changes of tumors during the course of the disease, dynamic detection of tumors can be achieved in a simple and non-invasive way.

In the initial phase of this study, we developed a method for the quantitative analysis of TROP2. The expression of TROP2 in BC cells was initially validated at the cellular level, followed by the Pep@MNPs method for blood samples from seven BC patients. The correlation between TROP2 expression in CTCs and pathological features of patients was evaluated by comparing the consistency of TROP2 expression in CTCs with IHC results from biopsies. It was observed that there was no significant correlation between the TROP2 MFI and IHC score in seven BC patients. However, there was a notable correlation between the CTCTROP2+ number and the IHC score, indicating a certain degree of consistency between the expression of TROP2 in CTCs and tumor tissues. Our findings suggest that the status of TROP2 in CTCs may serve as a valuable predictor and prognostic indicator for TROP2-targeted therapy, although further evaluation in a larger trial is necessary. We found that CTCTROP2+ number and TROP2 MFI of CTC were not significantly correlated with tumor TNM stage, Ki-67, and tumor volume size, which suggests that the EpCAM-based magnetic capture method may have certain limitations.

TUMORFISHER is an immunomagnetic technique based primarily on antibodies against EpCAM. However, in breast and other cancers, there are subpopulations of CTCs with low or absent EpCAM expression, which may result from phenotypic plasticity processes such as epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). A highly conserved mechanism associated with cell migration, invasion and spread in the development of cancer metastases [36]. In this study, we analyzed the expression of TROP2 and EpCAM in BC tissues using the DISCO database. The results showed that the expression of TROP2 was significantly higher than EpCAM in BC tissues. The existing CTC capture method TUMORFISHER relies on EpCAM. In order to capture all TROP2-expressing CTCs more fully and exclude the phenotypic changes of CTC caused by EMT, we proposed a new strategy to detect CTCTROP2+ by directly capturing them. A CTC immunomagnetic enrichment platform targeting TROP2 was established.

A method for capturing CTCs from BC patients was developed using TROP2 as the specific antibody, TROP2@MNPs. This method demonstrated superior specificity and sensitivity compared to EpCAM@MNPs, and showed favourable biocompatibility. The results of the in vitro cell line capture assay showed that TROP2@MNPs were able to specifically capture BC cells in a whole blood environment. Further examination of blood samples from clinical BC patients found that CTC was not detected with TROP2@MNPs in healthy donors. Our findings in BC patients showed a marked divergence from those observed in control subjects. It is interesting to note that our assay has seen some promising results in initial attempts with clinical samples. However, the limited number of cases included in this study requires further expansion of the cohort to confirm this observation. Furthermore, no CTCTROP2+ were identified in patient-8 samples while 12 CTCTROP2+ were detected in the TROP2@MNPs group. These results suggest that EpCAM-based CTC capture may result in the loss of some CTCTROP2+. Conversely, the TROP2@MNPs method, which directly depends on TROP2 targets, enables more efficient TROP2 liquid biopsy.

Conclusions

In conclusion, TROP2@MNPs capture method, which exhibits excellent stability and enables the active capture of CTCs, facilitates subsequent in vitro CTC cultivation and histological studies. It also shows potential for further applications in personalized medicine, early diagnosis, and biomedical research.

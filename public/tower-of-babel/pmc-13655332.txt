Reply to “Comments on ‘Therapeutic efficacy of laser vaporization and conization for high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia’”
Authors: Miseon Nakazawa, Mizue Teramoto, Tsuyoshi Saito
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We thank Fatima and colleagues for their careful appraisal of our article [1,2] and for raising 2 important issues: the inconsistencies in the published tables and potential confounding by indication. We also appreciate the Editorial Board’s invitation to clarify these points.

First, we acknowledge that the cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN)2 and CIN3 rows were inadvertently transposed in Table 1 and that the CIN2 vaporization denominator and clearance rate were incorrect in Table 2. The accompanying Corrigendum provides corrected versions of both tables. The Results text contained the intended values, and the corrections do not alter the underlying individual-level data. We sincerely apologize for not identifying these errors during proofreading.

Second, we agree that the treatment groups were not exchangeable at baseline. Laser vaporization was intentionally limited to carefully selected patients in whom the entire lesion was visible, endocervical extension and macroscopic invasion were absent, colposcopic and histological findings were concordant, and fertility preservation was desired. Conization was used more broadly, including for patients who did not meet these eligibility criteria. Accordingly, the observed age difference partly reflects clinical selection, and the crude comparisons should not be interpreted as causal treatment-effect estimates. We also clarify that the study was not designed as a formal non-inferiority trial.

To address the specific concern regarding age imbalance, we conducted post hoc age-adjusted analyses in the clinically higher-risk CIN3 subgroup. Age at treatment was entered as a continuous variable. Treatment was coded as laser vaporization versus conization. Firth penalized logistic regression was used for 2-year cytologic cure because only 12 patients were classified as not cured. Conventional multivariable logistic regression was used for human papillomavirus (HPV) clearance. The analyses were post hoc and should be interpreted as sensitivity analyses rather than causal estimates.

For the primary outcome of 2-year cytologic cure, 145 evaluable patients were included (70 conization and 75 vaporization). Because only 12 patients were classified as not cured, Firth penalized logistic regression was used to reduce small-sample bias. After adjustment for age at treatment, vaporization was not significantly associated with cytologic cure compared with conization (adjusted odds ratio [aOR]=0.227; 95% confidence interval [CI]=0.048–1.077; p=0.062). Increasing age was associated with a modest reduction in the odds of cure (aOR per year=0.917; 95% CI=0.844–0.995; p=0.038) (Table R1).

For 3-month HPV clearance, conventional multivariable logistic regression was performed in 136 evaluable CIN3 patients (57 conization and 79 vaporization). Neither treatment modality (aOR for vaporization=0.945; 95% CI=0.406–2.198; p=0.895) nor age (aOR per year=0.996; 95% CI=0.948–1.047; p=0.881) was significantly associated with HPV clearance (Table R2).

As a sensitivity analysis, we restricted the cohort to patients aged 40 years or younger. The direction of the findings remained similar for both cytologic cure (aOR for vaporization=0.436; 95% CI=0.067–2.834; p=0.385) and HPV clearance (aOR=1.243; 95% CI=0.472–3.274; p=0.660) (Table R3). These estimates remain imprecise because of the small number of failures.

No statistically significant association between treatment modality and either outcome was identified after age adjustment. However, the confidence intervals—particularly for cytologic cure—were wide, and these analyses do not establish equivalence or non-inferiority. Although propensity-score methods were considered to address potential bias, they could not be implemented; complete lesion visibility and the absence of endocervical extension were structural eligibility requirements for vaporization but were not measured as sufficiently granular, comparable variables across both groups. Consequently, adequate common support could not be assured, and adjustment based mainly on age would not eliminate indication bias. Residual confounding by indication therefore remains an important limitation. To validate these findings and address these limitations, we are currently conducting further analyses in an expanded cohort, the results of which will be reported separately.

On light cone bounds for Markov quantum open systems
Israel Michael Sigal, Xiaoxu Wu

Abstract
. We study space-time behaviour of solutions of the von Neumann-Lindblad equations underlying the dynamics of Markov quantum open systems. For a large class of these equations, we prove the existence of an effective light cone with an exponentially small spill-over.

An open quantum system (OQS) is a pair
(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+},\beta_{t})
, where the state space
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+}
is the space of positive trace-class operators (density operators) on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and the evolution
\beta_{t}
is a family of quantum maps (or quantum channels) on
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+}
, i.e. linear, completely positive, trace preserving maps (see
[
3
,
2
,
12
,
24
,
39
,
54
]
and the references therein).

The concept of OQS is an extension of that of the (closed) quantum system (
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+},\alpha_{t}
), where
\alpha_{t}
is the von Neumann dynamics,

(1.1)

\alpha_{t}(\rho)=e^{-iHt}\rho e^{iHt},

incorporating, in a natural way, the influence of the system’s environment. OQS arise also in the quantum measurement theory where the degrees of freedom of systems under investigation (rather than of the environment) are integrated out and in studying entanglement between two or more systems.

Importantly, even when the interaction with environment can be neglected, investigation of OQS is needed to determine whether properties of closed systems are robust w.r.to weak interaction with an outside environment. For instance, whether transmission of quantum information is stable w.r.to decoherence induced by such an interaction.

It is shown in
[
38
,
60
]
that under the Markovian assumption that
\beta_{t}
is a strongly continuous semigroup,

(1.2)

\beta_{t}\circ\beta_{s}=\beta_{t+s},\,\qquad\forall t,s\ \geq 0,\quad\text{ and }\beta_{t}\xrightarrow{s}\mathds{1}\text{ as }t\downarrow 0,

the evolution
\rho_{t}=\beta_{t}(\rho_{0})
satisfies the von Neumann-Lindblad equation (vNLE) (here and in the rest of this paper, we set
\hbar=1
)

(1.3)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(W_{j}\rho_{t}W_{j}^{*}-\frac{1}{2}\{W_{j}^{*}W_{j},\rho_{t}\}),

with the initial condition
\rho_{t=0}=\rho_{0}
. Here
H
and
W_{j},j=1,\cdots
, are operators on
\mathcal{H}
,
H
is a quantum Hamiltonian of the system of interest and
W_{j}
are operators produced by the interaction with environment, called the jump operators, and
\{A,B\}:=AB+BA
.

Conversely, under rather general conditions (see a discussion below and in Appendix
A
), solutions to the vNLE exist for any initial condition
\rho_{0}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and generate Markov open quantum (MOQ) dynamics,
\beta_{t}(\rho_{0})=\rho_{t}
. Thus the class of MOQ semigroups is rather rich. Furthermore, equations of the form (
1.3

[

22

21
,
24

50

1.3
) captures, at least approximately, natural physical models.

Clearly, the vNLE is an extension of the von Neumann equation (vNE)

(1.4)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=-i[H,\rho_{t}],

which generates evolution (
1.1
), describing the statistics of closed quantum systems. While the vN dynamics can be always reduced to the Schrödinger one on the corresponding Hilbert space (
L^{2}(\Lambda)
, in our case), this is not true for the vNLE. Thus, vNLE is a genuine extension of the Schrödinger equation beyond QM (to OQS, or to what can be termed as quantum statistics) making it a central object of quantum physics.

Mathematically, vNLE is a key representative of non-abelian PDEs. It is related to stochastic differential equations on Hilbert spaces, see

Light cone bound

The second key ingredient in the quantum theory is the notion of observables. Though physical observables are self-adjoint, often unbounded, operators on
\mathcal{H}
representing actual physical quantities (say,
p=-i\nabla
for
\Lambda=\mathbb{R}^{n}
), it is convenient mathematically to consider as observables all bounded operators
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

An average of a physical quantity (say, momentum) represented by an observable
A
in a state
\rho
is given by
\Tr(A\rho)
. There is a duality between states and observables given by the coupling

(1.17)

{{(A,\rho)\equiv\rho(A):=}}\Tr(A\rho),\qquad\forall\,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\,\text{ and }\,\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+},

which can be considered as either a linear, positive functional of
A
or a convex one of
\rho
. In what follows, we use the notation

(1.18)

\rho(A):=\Tr( A\rho).

Here
A\to\rho(A)
is a linear positive functional on the Banach space, in fact,
C^{*}
-algebra,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

By the duality, (
1.17
), the von Neumann dynamics yields the Heisenberg one, while the von Neumann-Lindblad dynamics
\beta_{t}
of states produces the dynamics
\beta_{t}^{\prime}
of observables as

(1.19)

\Tr(\beta_t'(A)\rho)=\Tr(A\beta_t(\rho)).

Under the Markov assumption (
1.2
), the dynamics
\beta_{t}^{\prime}
has the weak Markov property

(1.20)

\beta_{s}^{\prime}\circ\beta_{t}^{\prime}=\beta_{s+t}^{\prime},\qquad\forall\,s,t\geq 0,\text{ and }\beta_{t}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{w}\mathds{1}\text{ as }t\to 0,

and
A_{t}=\beta^{\prime}_{t}(A)
is weakly differentiable in
t
and weakly satisfies the dual Heisenberg-Lindblad (HL) equation (see
[
68
]
and the references therein)

(1.21)

\partial_{t}A_{t}=i[H,A_{t}]+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(W_{j}^{*}A_{t}W_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\{W_{j}^{*}W_{j},A_{t}\}).

In fact, this equation has a unique strong solution for any initial condition from a dense set in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
(see e.g.
[
68
]
and Remark
1.5

Remark 1.5
.

The HL generator
L^{\prime}
on the r.h.s. of (
1.21
) can be written as

(1.26)

L^{\prime}A=i[H,A]+\psi^{\prime}(A)-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\psi^{\prime}(\mathds{1}),A\right\},

where
\psi^{\prime}
is a completely positive map on
\mathcal{B}
, which, by the Krauss’ theorem, is of the form

(1.27)

\psi^{\prime}(A)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}^{*}AW_{j},

for some bounded operators
W_{j},j=1,2,\cdots,
satisfying (W).

This representation allows for an easy proof of existence of mild and strong solutions to Eq. (
1.21
). Indeed, (
1.21
) can be written as
\partial_{t}A_{t}=L^{\prime}A_{t}
, with the operator
L^{\prime}
given by (
1.26
). Furthermore,
L^{\prime}
can be written as
L^{\prime}=L_{0}^{\prime}+G^{\prime}
, where
L_{0}^{\prime}A=i[H,A]
and

(1.28)

G^{\prime}(A):=\psi^{\prime}(A)-\frac{1}{2}\{\psi^{\prime}(\mathds{1}),A\}.

Now, we show boundedness of the map
G^{\prime}
. Indeed, the operator
\psi^{\prime}(\mathds{1})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}^{*}W_{j}
is bounded, by Condition (AW), and positive. Furthermore, the map
\psi^{\prime}
is positive and therefore
\|\psi^{\prime}(A)\|\leq\psi^{\prime}(\mathds{1})\|A\|
, for any self-adjoint operator
A
, which follows by applying
\psi^{\prime}
to the operator
B=\|A\|\mathds{1}-A\geq 0
. One can extend this bound to non-self-adjoint operators to obtain

(1.29)

\|G^{\prime}(A)\|\leq 2\psi^{\prime}(\mathds{1})\|A\|.

By the explicit representation
e^{L_{0}^{\prime}t}A=e^{iHt}Ae^{-iHt}
, the operator
L_{0}^{\prime}
generates a one-parameter group
\alpha_{t}^{\prime}=e^{tL_{0}^{\prime}}
of isometries on
\mathcal{B}
(Heisenberg evolution), and therefore, since
G^{\prime}
is bounded, by a standard perturbation theory,
L^{\prime}
generates a one-parameter group of bounded operators,
\beta_{t}^{\prime}=e^{tL^{\prime}}
.

Since
\beta_{t}^{\prime}=e^{L^{\prime}t}
is (completely) positive (by the original assumption on
\beta_{t}
) and unital (
e^{L^{\prime}t}\mathds{1}=\mathds{1}
), as follows from
L^{\prime}\mathds{1}=0
, we have
\|e^{L^{\prime}t}\|\leq 2
. (In the opposite direction, one can prove the complete positivity of
e^{L^{\prime}t}
by using Eq. (
1.26
), see
[
68
]
and references therein.)

Remark 1.7
.

If one thinks of the algebra of observables
\mathcal{B}\equiv\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and the Heisenberg (resp. Heisenberg-Lindblad) dynamics on it as primary objects, then one might define the state space as the dual
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{B}
with the dynamics given by the von Neumann (resp. von Neumann-Lindblad) dynamics. Then
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a proper, closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
(see
[
73
]
, Chapter IV, Theorems 1 and 5) invariant under the von Neumann and von Neumann-Lindblad dynamics. By restricting the von Neumann dynamics further to the invariant subspace of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
of rank
1
orthogonal projections one arrives at a formulation equivalent to the standard quantum mechanics. For closed systems, the latter extends uniquely to von Neumann dynamics on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and then on
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. For open systems, this is not true any more: the minimal state space for the vNL dynamics is
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

1.3.
Comparison with earlier results and description of the approach

Bounds of the form of (
1.11
) but with a power decay were obtained in
[
11
,
10
]
.
(Note that
[
11
,
10
]
treat a more general class of vNLE including non-analytic ones (long-range in the condensed matter physics terminology) for which exponential bounds do not hold.)
For the von Neumann evolution, (
1.4
), where
the key estimates reduce to estimating the Schrödinger unitary,
e^{-iHt}
, a result similar to Theorems
1.1
was proven in
[
78
]
.

Presently, there are three approaches to proving light-cone estimates. The first approach going back to Lieb and Robinson (see
[
66
]
for a review) is based on a perturbation (Araki-Dyson-type) expansion.

In the second approach, one constructs special observables (adiabatic, space-time, local observables or ASTLO) which are monotonically decreasing along the evolution up to self-similar and time-decaying terms (recursive monotonicity). Originally designed for the scattering theory in quantum mechanics in
[
77
]
and extended in
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
37
,
45
,
47
,

In this paper, we develop the third approach, initiated in

In the process, we construct a theory of analytic deformations of the vNLE (or

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
given Propositions
2.1
and
2.7

Recall our convention that
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+}
. For
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we let
T_{\xi,\eta}\lambda=T_{\xi}\lambda T_{\eta}^{-1}
, with the ’left’ and ’right’ sides of
\lambda

L_{\xi,\eta}=T_{\xi,\eta}LT_{\xi,\eta}^{-1}
and
\beta_{t,\xi,\eta}:=T_{\xi,\eta}\beta_{t}T_{\xi,\eta}^{-1}

is a unitary group (of multiplication operators by

is a group of isometries on

We assemble all technical results needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the following proposition proven in Section

.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume Conditions (H), (W), (AH) and (AW). Consider the family operators
L_{\zeta,\theta}
on
\hat{R}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
of the form

(2.1)

L_{\zeta,\theta}=L_{0,\zeta,\theta}+G_{\zeta,\theta},

with the operators
L_{0,\zeta,\theta}
and
G_{\zeta,\theta}
given by

(2.2)

L_{0,\zeta,\theta}\lambda:=-i(H_{\zeta}\lambda-\lambda H_{\theta}),

(2.3)

G_{\zeta,\theta}\lambda:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(W_{j,\zeta}\lambda W_{j,\bar{\theta}}^{*}-\frac{1}{2}W_{j,\bar{\zeta}}^{*}W_{j,\zeta}\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\lambda W_{j,\bar{\theta}}^{*}W_{j,\theta}\right),

where
H_{\zeta},

W_{j,\zeta}
and
W_{j,\bar{\zeta}}^{*}\equiv(W_{j}^{*})_{\zeta}
are analytic continuations of
H_{\xi}
,
W_{j,\xi}
and
W_{j,\xi}^{*}
. Then, we have the following statements:

(a)
L_{0,\zeta,\theta}
and

are bounded maps from
\hat{R}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, respectively,
\forall\zeta,\theta\in S_{a}^{n}
.

(b)
L_{\zeta,\theta}\hat{R}
is an analytic continuation (as a family of bounded operators) of
L_{\xi,\tilde{\xi}}\hat{R}
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
S_{a}^{n}\times S_{a}^{n}
.

(c)
L_{\zeta,\theta}
generates the family of bounded one-parameter groups
\beta_{t,\zeta,\theta}=e^{L_{\zeta,\theta}t}
and the latter family is analytic in
\zeta,\theta\in S_{a}^{n}
.

(d)
\beta_{t,\zeta,-\zeta}
is positivity preserving on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Using that
T_{-\eta}=T_{\eta}^{-1}
and
T^{-1}_{\xi,\eta}=T_{-\xi,-\eta}
, we write
T_{\xi,\eta}\lambda:=T_{\xi}\lambda T_{\eta}^{-1}=T_{\xi}\lambda T_{-\eta}
and
\beta_{t,\xi,\eta}:=T_{\xi,\eta}\beta_{t}T_{-\xi,-\eta}
. We have

Lemma 2.2
.

For any bounded sets
U,V\subset\Lambda
and any
\zeta,\theta\in S_{a}^{n}
, we have

(2.4)

\hat{\chi}_{U}\beta_{t}\hat{\chi}_{V}=\hat{\chi}_{U}T_{-\zeta,-\theta}\beta_{t,\zeta,\theta}T_{\zeta,\theta}\hat{\chi}_{V},\quad\text{$\zeta,\theta\in S_{a}^{n}$}.

Proof.

Using that
T_{-\xi,-\eta}T_{\xi,\eta}=\mathds{1}
, we write

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}_{U}\beta_{t}\hat{\chi}_{V}=

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}_{U}T_{-\xi,-\eta}T_{\xi,\eta}\beta_{t}T_{-\xi,-\eta}T_{\xi,\eta}\hat{\chi}_{V}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}_{U}T_{-\xi,-\eta}\beta_{t,\xi,\eta}T_{\xi,\eta}\hat{\chi}_{V}.

By Conditions (AH) and (AW) and the facts, that for
U
and
V
bounded, the operators
\hat{\chi}_{U}T_{-\zeta,-\theta}
and
T_{\zeta,\theta}\hat{\chi}_{V}
are bounded and analytic for
\zeta,\theta\in S_{a}^{n}
, we can continue the right-hand side analytically in
\xi
and
\eta
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathcal{S}_{a}^{n}
to obtain (

(

[
1
]
, see
[
18
]
, for a book presentation and extensions).

Now, we estimate
\hat{\chi}_{U}\beta_{t}\hat{\chi}_{V}
for
U
and
V
arbitrary disjoint sets in
\Lambda
and then, using partitions of unity, we obtain the desired estimate of
\hat{\chi}_{X}\beta_{t}\hat{\chi}_{Y}
.

We denote
\beta_{t,\zeta}:=\beta_{t,\zeta,-\zeta}
. Using the relation

(2.6)

\hat{\chi}_{U}T_{\zeta,-\zeta}(\lambda)=\chi_{U}T_{\zeta}\lambda T_{\zeta}\chi_{U},

we estimate

(2.7)

\|\hat{\chi}_{U}T_{\zeta,-\zeta}(\lambda)\|_{1}\leq\|\chi_{U}T_{\zeta}\|^{2}\|\lambda\|_{1},\text{ for any }\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{1}.

Using (
2.4
), together with (
2.7
), we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\chi}_{U}\beta_{t}\hat{\chi}_{V}(\lambda)\|_{1}=

\displaystyle\|\chi_{U}T_{-\zeta}\left(\beta_{t,\zeta}\hat{\chi}_{V}T_{\zeta,-\zeta}(\lambda)\right)\chi_{U}T_{-\zeta}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{U}T_{-\zeta}\|^{2}\|\beta_{t,\zeta}\|_{1}^{op}\|\hat{\chi}_{V}T_{\zeta,-\zeta}\lambda\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{U}T_{-\zeta}\|^{2}\|\chi_{V}T_{\zeta}\|^{2}\|\beta_{t,\zeta}\|_{1}^{op}\|\lambda\|_{1},

where, recall,
\|\cdot\|_{1}^{op}
denotes the norm of operators on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, which implies

(2.9)

\|\hat{\chi}_{U}\beta_{t}\hat{\chi}_{V}\|_{1}^{op}\leq\|\chi_{U}T_{-\zeta}\|^{2}\|\chi_{V}T_{\zeta}\|^{2}\|\beta_{t,\zeta}\|_{1}^{op}.

Now we estimate the norms on the r.h.s. of (

) beginning with

. For a self-adjoint operator
A
, we denote

(2.10)

\sup A=\sup\limits_{\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A),\,\|\psi\|=1}\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\zeta=i\eta,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|\eta|=\nu

. Then we have

\|\beta_{t,\zeta}\|_{1}^{op}\leq 4e^{2\nu c^{\prime}(\nu)t},

Here

is the bounded, self-adjoint operator on

given by

(2.13)

\tilde{G}_{\zeta}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(W_{j,\zeta}^{*}W_{j,\zeta}-\frac{1}{2}W_{j,-\zeta}^{*}W_{j,\zeta}-\frac{1}{2}W_{j,\zeta}^{*}W_{j,-\zeta}),

where
W_{j,\zeta}^{*}\equiv(W_{j,\zeta})^{*}=(W_{j}^{*})_{\bar{\zeta}}
. Moreover,

.

Observe that

(2.14)

\tilde{G}_{\zeta}\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{j}\left(W_{j,\zeta}^{*}W_{j,\zeta}-W_{j,-\zeta}^{*}W_{j,-\zeta}\right).

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Since the operator-functions

and

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\|+\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j}^{*}B_{j}\|\right),

is bounded and therefore self-adjoint. This and Condition (AH) imply that
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\nu)<\infty
. Next, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

For
\zeta\in S_{a}^{n},\operatorname{Re}\zeta=0,

Appendix A
On existence theory for vNLE

The existence theory for the vNL equation is based on the decomposition

(A.1)

L=L_{0}+G

of the vNL generator
L
, with the von Neumann and Lindblad parts,
L_{0}
and
G
, given by

(A.2)

L_{0}\rho=-i[H,\rho]\,\,\text{ and }\,\,G\rho=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(W_{j}\rho W_{j}^{*}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{W_{j}^{*}W_{j},\rho\right\}\right).

Under the conditions above the operator
L_{0}
is defined on the set

(A.3)

\mathcal{D}(L_{0}):=\left\{\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\,:\,\begin{aligned} &\rho:\mathcal{D}(H)\to\mathcal{D}(H)\text{ and }[H,\rho]\text{ extends }\\
&\text{from }\mathcal{D}(H)\text{ to }\mathcal{H}\text{ as an element of }\mathcal{S}_{1}\end{aligned}\right\}.

The latter set contains the subset
\{(H+i)^{-1}\rho(H-i)^{-1}\,:\,\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\}
, which is dense in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
(in the
\mathcal{S}_{1}
-norm
\|\lambda\|_{1}
.). For the second term,
G
, we observe that
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}^{*}W_{j}
is a bounded operator, as a weak limit of bounded operators (
[
71
]
, Theorem VI.1), and, for any
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{+}
,
S_{N}:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}W_{j}\rho W_{j}^{*}
is an increasing sequence of positive, trace-class operators s.t.

(A.4)

\|S_{N}-S_{M}\|_{1}=\Tr(S_N-S_M)=\Tr(\rho\sum\limits_{j=M}^N W_j^*W_j)\to 0,

for
N>M\to\infty
, and therefore
S_{N}
converges in the
\mathcal{S}_{1}
norm as
N\to\infty
and its limit
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}\rho W_{j}^{*}
is positive trace class operator. This way one can prove that
G
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
(see
[
23
,
68
]
for details). Hence, the operator
L
is well defined on
\mathcal{D}(L)=\mathcal{D}(L_{0})
.

By the explicit representation
e^{L_{0}t}\rho=e^{-iHt}\rho e^{iHt}
, the operator
L_{0}
generates a one-parameter group
\alpha_{t}=e^{tL_{0}}
of isometries on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
(von Neumann evolution), and therefore, by a standard perturbation theory, since
G
is bounded,
L
generates a one-parameter group of bounded operators,
\beta_{t}=e^{tL}
.

In conclusion, we prove a bound on
e^{Lt}
used in Section
2
.

Proposition B.1

(Positivity of
c(\mu)
)
.

c(\mu)\geq 0
,
\forall\,\mu\in(0,a)
. Moreover, if
L^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
, then
c(\mu)>0
for all
\mu\in(0,a)
.

Proof.

By (
2.14
),

(B.3)

\mathrm{Im}H_{\zeta}+\tilde{G}_{\zeta}\geq K_{\zeta},\text{ with }K_{\zeta}=\mathrm{Im}H_{\zeta}+G_{\zeta}^{\#},

where

(B.4)

G_{\zeta}^{\#}:=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j}(W_{j,\zeta}^{*}W_{j,\zeta}-W_{j,-\zeta}^{*}W_{j,-\zeta}).

By the definition of
H_{i\eta}
, we have
\langle\psi,H_{i\eta}^{*}\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,H_{-i\eta}\psi\rangle
, for a dense set of
\psi
’s, which implies
\mathrm{Im}H_{-i\eta}=-\mathrm{Im}H_{i\eta}
. Moreover,
G_{-i\eta}^{\#}=-G_{i\eta}^{\#}
and therefore
K_{-i\eta}=-K_{i\eta}
. Hence,

(B.5)

\displaystyle\text{either }\ \langle\psi_{0},K_{i\eta_{0}}\psi_{0}\rangle>0\ \text{ or }\ \langle\psi_{0},K_{-i\eta_{0}}\psi_{0}\rangle>0,

for some
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

|\eta_{0}|=\nu
, or
\langle\psi,K_{i\eta}\psi\rangle=0
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
, and all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|\eta|=\nu
. Due to (
B.3
), this implies that either

(B.6)

c^{\prime}(\nu)=\sup_{\zeta=i\eta,|\eta|=\nu}\sup\limits_{\psi\in\mathcal{H}}\langle\psi,(\mathrm{Im}H_{\zeta}+\tilde{G}_{\zeta})\psi\rangle>0

or

(B.7)

\mathrm{Im}H_{i\eta}+\tilde{G}_{i\eta}=0\qquad\text{ for every }\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|\eta|=\nu.

In the first case, we have
c^{\prime}(\nu)>0
. The second case is ruled out by our condition

\nabla_{\eta}(\mathrm{Im}H_{i\eta}+\tilde{G}_{i\eta})|_{\eta=0}=L^{\prime}(x)\neq 0.

Finally, we observe that, by (

(resp.

)

Lemma B.2
.

(a)
L^{\prime}(x)=-i\nabla_{\xi}L^{\prime}(e^{i\xi\cdot x})|_{\xi=0}

(B.8)

L^{\prime}(x)=i[H,x]+\tilde{G}^{\prime},

where

(B.9)

\tilde{G}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}i(W_{j}^{*}W_{j}^{\prime}-(W_{j}^{\prime})^{*}W_{j}),

W_{j}^{\prime}:=i\nabla_{\eta}W_{j,i\eta}|_{\eta=0}=-i[x,W_{j}]

(b) With the notation

Proof.

(a) It is convenient to use a different representation of the map

(B.11)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j}([W_{j}^{*},A]W_{j}-W_{j}^{*}[W_{j},A]).

The latter expression, together with the relation
-i\nabla_{\xi}i[W_{j},e^{i\xi\cdot x}]|_{\xi=0}=i[W_{j},x]=:W_{j}^{\prime}

-i\nabla_{\xi}G^{\prime}(e^{i\xi\cdot x})|_{\xi=0}=\tilde{G}^{\prime}

-i\nabla_{\xi}i[H,e^{i\xi\cdot x}]|_{\xi=0}=i[H,x]

(b) Using that

(B.12)

\nabla_{\eta}{\rm Im}H_{i\eta}|_{\eta=0}=i[H,x],

(B.13)

\mathrm{Im}H_{i\eta}=i[H,x]\cdot\eta+o(|\eta|).

Furthermore, we expand, using (

\zeta=i\eta

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(B.14)

B.9

\mathrm{Im}H_{\zeta}|_{\zeta=0}=0

\tilde{G}_{\zeta}|_{\zeta=0}=0

\displaystyle c^{\prime}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\hat{\eta}\in S^{n-1}}\sup(\hat{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\eta}(\mathrm{Im}H_{i\eta}+\tilde{G}_{i\eta}))|_{|\eta|=0}+o(\nu)

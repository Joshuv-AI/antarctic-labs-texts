Invariance properties of the solution operator for measure-valued semilinear transport equations
Sander C. Hille, Rainey Lyons, Adrian Muntean

Abstract
We provide conditions under which we prove for measure-valued transport equations with non-linear reaction term in the space of finite signed Radon measures, that positivity is preserved, as well as absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue measure, if the initial condition has that property. Moreover, if the initial condition has L p L^{p} regular density, then the solution has the same property.



Keywords: Measure-valued transport equations, mild solutions, measure-valued solutions, invariance properties

1
Introduction

We consider the semilinear measure-valued transport equation with reaction term

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\text{div}_{x}(v_{t}(x)\mu_{t})&=f_{t}(\mu_{t})&\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega=:\Omega_{T},\\
\mu_{0}&=\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega).&\end{split}\right.

(
P
)

Here and throughout the manuscript, the space
\Omega
is taken to be either
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or the compact space
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which is the
d
-dimensional torus or, equivalently, the unit hypercube
[0,1]^{d}
with periodic boundary conditions.
These particular choices of
\Omega
indicate that we do not include any boundary effects in our current analysis.
For discussions around this class of transport equations with particular boundary conditions, we refer the reader, for instance, to
[
22
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
.
The reaction map
(t,\mu)\mapsto f_{t}(\mu)
takes values in the vector space
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
of finite signed Radon measures on
\Omega
and is possibly non-linear and satisfies specific structural conditions given below.
Well-posedness for equations of this type in the space of Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has been studied in
[
11
]
for the case of a time-independent velocity field and in
[
3
]
for the time-dependent case and we will employ conditions on
v
and
f
similar to these works. These include a (local) Lipschitz condition on
f_{t}
in the Fortet-Mourier or flat metric on
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
.

Our focus in this manuscript is establishing ‘invariance properties’ of the solution operator to the Cauchy problem (

P

) that maps the initial condition
\nu
to the unique maximal mild solution
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
with initial condition
\mu_{0}=\nu
. Let
[0,T^{*}_{\nu})
be the corresponding maximal interval of existence.
Specifically, starting from the previously mentioned well-posedness results for Problem (

P

) in the space
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, we provide conditions on the reaction term
f
such that the solution operator preserves:

1.

Positivity
, i.e., a positive initial condition leads to a positive solution
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)^{+}
for all
t\geq 0
;

2.

L^{p}
-density
, i.e., an initial condition with
L^{p}
-density (
1<p\leq\infty
) with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
leads to a solution with
\mu_{t}
absolutely continuous solution with respect to Lebesgue measure
\lambda
with
L^{p}
-density, for
t
possibly in a proper subinterval
[0,\hat{T}^{*}_{\nu})
of the maximal interval of existence.

We take the time to point out that these preserved properties are important for various applications of similar reaction-transport equations set in the space of measures, including the study of structured population models
[
1
,
2
,
18
,
29
]
, traffic flow
[
9
,
10
]

, and cell migration
[
13
]
.

Because of this ‘measure-centric’ line of argumentation, we limit attention to ‘classical’ regular velocity fields
v_{t}
, which are bounded and globally Lipschitz, allowing us to focus on the technicalities involved in showing that results in the function space (density) setting can be derived from results in the general measure setting. Various auxiliary results that we obtained for this purpose seem to be new (in particular Theorem
3.1
) and we believe them to be of separate interest in other contexts too.
It is also interesting to consider how our results hold for transport equations and conservation laws defined by less regular velocity fields, see e.g.,
[
15
,
6
,
5
]
, but we leave this for future work.

Study of Problem (P) often occurs as preparation for studying the Cauchy problem for the quasi-linear equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}_{x}(v_{t}[\mu_{t}]\mu_{t})=f_{t}(\mu_{t}),\qquad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}

(1)

in the space of measures. Various authors have considered well-posedness of Cauchy Problem (P) or (
1
) in the space of Radon measures
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
on
\Omega
as here
[
3
,
11
]
, or on an interval with boundary interaction
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. For the semilinear problem, the solution concept of focus will be that of
mild solutions
.
These are weak solutions
[
21
]
as commonly considered.
However, a mild solution is such that
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is continuous when the Radon measures are equipped with the Fortet-Mourier metric. Hence, the solution operator, mapping initial value to the unique associated solution
\mu_{\bullet}
, defines a dynamical system in the space of Radon measures with continuous motions – as is usually considered.

We want solutions to possess properties that make the model meaningful to the application of interest, e.g., positivity is required if the measure represents distribution of mass or individuals.
Often we find it convenient to have densities described relative to a preferred reference measure. Then we like to have that regularity of the density with respect to this reference is preserved. This latter property cannot always be guaranteed in this model, as we will show. Other model equations, as for instance those used in population dynamics, often allow the possibility of aggregation in finite time, like the ones describing chemotaxis. This feature naturally leads to the blow-up in finite time of the classical, weak, or strong concepts of solution
[
37
]
. After this blow-up time, the solution usually turns into a Dirac – possibly continuing to move in space (cf. e.g.
[
34
,
16
]
). Hence, the overall framework fits best to a setup in terms of measure solutions.

The main results in this paper can be summarized as follows – informally – because the concepts and notation required for making a precise statement still need introduction.

Then, the mild solution

Theorem

is a structural result for general measures in

, while Theorem

2.1
Function spaces, measures, distances and norms

We employ common notation for function spaces throughout the manuscript.
For instance, we let
B_{b}(S)
denote the vector space of real-valued bounded Borel measurable functions on
S
and
C_{b}(S)
its subspace of continuous bounded functions. Both are equipped with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
The space
\mathrm{BL}(S)\subset C_{b}(S)
consists of all bounded Lipschitz functions on
S
for a (fixed, implicit) admissible metric
d
.
We will use
|f|_{L}
to denote the Lipschitz constant of
f\in\mathrm{BL}(S)
.
\mathrm{BL}(S)
is a Banach space for the norm
\|f\|_{\mathrm{FM}}:=\max\bigl(\|f\|_{\infty},|f|_{L}\bigr)
. One has
\|fg\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{FM}}
for
f,g\in\mathrm{BL}(S)
.

We denote by
\mathcal{B}(X)
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on the topological space
X
.

\mathcal{M}(S)
represents the vector space of finite signed Borel measures on
S
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
the convex cone of positive measures in
\mathcal{M}(S)
.
Since
S
is Polish, every finite Borel measure is a finite Radon measure (compare
[
7
]
).
Various definitions of Radon measures appear throughout the literature, all leading to the same concept for finite measures (cf. e.g.
[
7
, Section 7.14(xviii)]
or
[
25
]
).
We follow
[
25
, Section 7.1, p.212]
by defining a positive (possibly infinite) Radon measure to be a Borel measure
\mu
that is (a) finite on compact sets, (b) outer (open) regular on all Borel sets, i.e.

\mu(E)=\inf\bigl\{\mu(U)\colon U\subset S\ \mbox{open},\ E\subset U\bigr\},\qquad E\subset S\ \mbox{Borel}

(5)

and (c) inner (compact) regular on all open sets:

\mu(U)=\sup\{\mu(K)\colon K\subset S\ \mbox{compact},K\subset U\bigr\},\qquad U\subset S\ \mbox{open}.

In particular, the Borel-Lebesgue measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a (
\sigma
-finite) Radon measure in this sense. We shall use the same notation for its restriction to a Borel measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty interior and boundary that is a null-set for
\lambda
. Accordingly, in a topological setting, measurability of sets and functions will refer to the respective Borel
\sigma
-algebras.

The space of measures
\mathcal{M}(S)
is equipped with the Fortet-Mourier norm
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
:

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}:=\sup\bigl\{\int_{S}f\,d\mu\colon f\in\mathrm{BL}(S),\ \|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\ |f|_{L}\leq 1\bigr\}.

(6)

It can be readily checked that
\|f\cdot\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)
and
f\in\mathrm{BL}(S)
.
On
\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
the Fortet-Mourier norm metrizes the usual weak topology on measures furnished by pairing
\mathcal{M}(S)
with
C_{b}(S)
via integration:

\langle\mu,f\rangle:=\int_{S}f\,d\mu,\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S),\ f\in C_{b}(S).

(7)

\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
is closed in
\mathcal{M}(S)
for this weak topology.
The metric derived from the Fortet-Mourier norm turns
\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
into a complete separable metric space
[
18
, Section 1.3]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
the respective spaces of measures equipped with the Fortet-Mourier metric and corresponding metric topology. It is well known that
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
is not complete
[
27
]
, generally.
Its metric completion is denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, viewed as closure of
\mathcal{M}(S)
in
\mathrm{BL}(S)^{*}
, through the pairing (
7
).

The total variation norm,

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ :=\ |\mu|(S)\ =\ \mu^{+}(S)+\mu^{-}(S),\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S),

where
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
are the positive and negative part of the signed measure
\mu
in the Hahn-Jordan decomposition, is closely related to the order structure on
\mathcal{M}(S)
– defined set-wise – and turns
\mathcal{M}(S)
into a Banach lattice (cf. e.g.
[
30
]
, Section 1.1, Example
vi
, p.9). One has

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ =\ \sup\bigl\{\langle\mu,f\rangle\colon f\in B_{b}(S),\ \|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\bigr\}\ =\ \sup\bigl\{\langle\mu,f\rangle\colon f\in C_{b}(S),\ \|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\bigr\}.

(8)

Thus, (
6
) and (
8
) combined yield that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
is stronger than
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
: one has
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
. It follows, that for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
,
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
. For any
R>0
, we define

\mathcal{M}_{R}(S):=\bigl\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)\colon\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq R\bigr\}.

The relevance of this ball of radius
R
in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
-norm in what is to follow lies in ‘repairing’ to some extent the lack of completeness of
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
. That is,

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
R>0
,
\mathcal{M}_{R}(S)
is complete in
\bigl(\mathcal{M}(S),\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\bigr)
.

Proof.

For
S=\mathbb{R}_{+}
, this result can be found in
[
26
, Theorem 2.7]
, proven by a duality argument, which does not work for general Polish spaces. We shall prove the general case.
Let
(\mu_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{R}(S)
be a Cauchy sequence for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
. Let
\phi\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}(S)
be its limit. For every
f\in\mathrm{BL}(S)
,
\langle\mu_{n},f\rangle\to\langle\phi,f\rangle
.
\mathrm{BL}(S)
is
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-dense in the space
U_{b}(S)
of bounded uniformly continuous functions on
S
(cf.
[
17
]
, Lemma 8). Because of
\sup_{n}\|\mu_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq R
and (
8
),
(\langle\mu_{n},g\rangle)_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}
for any
g\in U_{b}(S)
. Thus,
\bar{\phi}(g):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\mu_{n},g\rangle
defines a linear functional on

. The Banach-Steinhaus Theorem yields that
\bar{\phi}\in U_{b}(S)^{*}
. Clearly, the restriction of
\bar{\phi}
to
\mathrm{BL}(S)
equals
\phi
. Pachl has shown that
\mathcal{M}(S)
is
U_{b}(S)
-weakly sequentially complete (cf.
[
31
]
Theorem 3.2, or
[
32
]
Theorem 5.45). Hence,
\bar{\phi}
is represented by integration against some
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)
– and the same holds for
\phi
. It can be verified that

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ =\ \sup\bigl\{\langle\mu,g\rangle\colon g\in U_{b}(S),\ \|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1\bigr\}.

(9)

Therefore,
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq R
and we conclude that
\mathcal{M}_{R}(S)
is
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
-complete.

If
\Phi:S\to S^{\prime}
is a measurable map between measurable spaces, it defines a map between the spaces of signed measures and between the convex cones of positive measures on
S
and
S^{\prime}
by push-forward. This map is denoted by
\Phi_{\#}
. Thus, for any positive or finite signed measure
\mu
on
S
,
\Phi_{\#}\mu
is such a measure on
S^{\prime}
, defined for any measurable set
A\subset S^{\prime}
by

\Phi_{\#}\mu(A):=\mu(\Phi^{-1}(A)).

We denote the (finite) positive measure on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, obtained by push-forward of
\lambda
under the canonical quotient map, by
\lambda
as well.

In the case that transformation
\Phi
maps
S
into itself, then
\Phi_{\#}
defines a
Markov operator
on
\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
or
\mathcal{M}(S)
which we shall denote by
P_{\Phi}
. That is, it is an operator that maps
\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
into itself, is additive and positive-scalar homogeneous, and preserves total mass of positive measures.
The operator
P_{\Phi}
is

, with dual operator
U_{\Phi}f=f\circ\Phi
, meaning that

\langle P_{\Phi}\mu,f\rangle\ =\ \int_{S}f\,d(\Phi_{\#}\mu)\ =\ \int_{S}f\circ\Phi\,d\mu=\langle\mu,U_{\Phi}f\rangle\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(S),\ f\in B_{b}(S).

We shall make use of Bochner integration of Banach-space-valued functions, see
[
12
,
14
]
for details. A map
f
from a positive measure space
(S,\Sigma,\mu)
into a Banach space

is strongly Bochner measurable if it is the point-wise limit limit of a sequence of simple functions

, i.e. of the form

f_{n}(s)\ =\ \sum_{k=1}^{N_{n}}x_{k}^{(n)}\,{1\!\!1}_{E^{(n)}_{k}}(s),

where
{1\!\!1}_{A}
is the indicator function of the
A
,
{E^{(n)}_{k}}\in\Sigma
and
x_{k}^{(n)}\in X
. A function

is Bochner integrable with respect to
\mu
if and only if it is strongly Bochner measurable and
s\mapsto\|f(s)\|_{X}
is
\mu
-integrable. Then,

\left\|\int_{E}f(s)\,d\mu(s)\right\|_{X}\ \leq\ \int_{E}\bigl\|f(s)\|_{X}\,d\mu(s).

The following result is remarkable, because a similar ‘triangle inequality for integrals’ holds for the total variation norm for Bochner integration in the Banach space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, for which it obviously is not the norm.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
23
]
, Proposition C.2)
.

Let
\nu
be a
\sigma
-finite positive measure on the measurable space
(S^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})
and let

be strongly Bochner measurable with respect to
\nu
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
. Then
s^{\prime}\mapsto\|f(s^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
is
\Sigma^{\prime}
-Borel measurable. If it is
\nu
-integrable, then

is Bochner integrable in

with respect to

and

\left\|\int_{S^{\prime}}f(s^{\prime})\,d\nu(s^{\prime})\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \leq\int_{S^{\prime}}\bigl\|f(s^{\prime})\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,d\nu(s^{\prime}).

In particular, the Bochner integral of

is a finite (signed) measure.

2.2
Fundamental assumptions and solution concept

The mathematical literature provides several well-posedness results for problem (P) or for variants of it, see for example
[
3
]
, where a more complicated system of equations is studied with density dependent velocity field that is otherwise not dependent on time – or
[
11
]
for the case of a time-independent and density independent velocity field. ‘Our’ case of a time-dependent, density-independent velocity field is neither covered in
[
3
,
11
]
, nor in
[
5
]
, where there is no reaction term.

In particular, we will be following
[
3
]
by assuming throughout this work that the velocity field satisfies:

(V1)

(t,x)\mapsto v_{t}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous;

(V2)

x\mapsto v_{t}(x)
is bounded and globally Lipschitz on
\Omega
for each
t\geq 0
;

(V3)

t\mapsto v_{t}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathrm{BL}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous.

The Lipschitz constant of
v_{t}
is
|v_{t}|_{L}
. As norm on
\mathrm{BL}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
we take
\|v\|_{\mathrm{FM}}:=\max(\|v\|_{\infty},|v|_{L})
. We require (V3) to ensure that for any
T>0
, there exists
L_{T}>0
such that
|v_{t}|_{L}\leq L_{T}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. This is a condition imposed in
[
3
]
in their results on well-posedness for problem (P) (see e.g.
[
5
]
, where the more general assumption that
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathrm{BL}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is made).

With conditions (V1)–(V3),
v
generates a flow
\Phi^{v}_{s,t}
on
\Omega
for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
0\leq s\leq t
. That is, the motion
t\mapsto\Phi_{s,t}^{v}(x_{0})
is the unique and globally existing solution to

\left\{\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=v_{t}(x(t))\quad\mbox{for}\ t>s,\\
x(s)&=x_{0}\in\Omega\end{split}.\right.

(10)

The flow maps
\Phi^{v}_{s,t}
satisfy
\Phi^{v}_{t,t^{\prime}}\circ\Phi^{v}_{s,t}=\Phi^{v}_{s,t^{\prime}}
for
t^{\prime}\geq t\geq s\geq 0
. Moreover, they are Lipschitz, with

\bigl|\Phi_{s,t}^{v}\bigr|_{L}\ \leq\ \exp\bigl(\int_{s}^{t}|v_{\tau}|_{L}\,d\tau\bigr)\ =:\ L^{v}_{s,t}

(11)

(cf.
[
5
]
, Eq. (2.1)).

We further associate to
v_{\bullet}
the following family of Markov operators on measures by means of push-forward under the flow:

P_{s,t}^{v}\mu\ :=(\Phi_{s,t}^{v})_{\#}\mu,\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.

(12)

The linear operators
P^{v}_{t_{0},t}
are continuous on
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
. They satisfy the norm estimate

\bigl\|P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\mu\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ \leq\ \|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,\max\bigl(1,|\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}|_{L}\bigr)\ \leq\ L^{v}_{t_{0},t}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega).

(13)

It is clear from their definition that the operators
P_{s,t}^{v}
preserve
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
.

It is well known that
\mu_{t}:=P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu
solves the linear continuity equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}_{x}\bigl(v_{t}(x)\mu_{t}\bigr)=0,\qquad\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu,

(14)

(see e.g.
[
35
]
Theorem 5.34, p.167).
The operators
P^{v}_{s,t}
are therefore the building blocks for representing the solutions to Problem (P). First one considers the perturbation of (
14
), by introducing a non-zero right-hand side, independent on the density
\mu_{t}
:

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}_{x}\bigl(v_{t}(x)\mu_{t}\bigr)=\sigma_{t},\qquad\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu,

(15)

where
\sigma_{t}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. One has the following well-posedness result:

Proposition 2.3

(
Ackleh & Saintier
[
3
]
, Proposition 5.1).

Let
v_{\bullet}
satisfy (V1)–(V3). let
T>0
and assume that
\sigma_{\bullet}\in C([0,T],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
is such that
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\sigma_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty
. Then, for any
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and initial condition
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, equation (
15
) has a unique solution
\mu_{\bullet}\in C([t_{0},T],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
. It is given explicitly by

\mu_{t}\ =P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}P^{v}_{s,t}\sigma_{s}\,ds.

(16)

The integral in (
16
) should be interpreted as a Bochner integral in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, which – as part of the result – takes values in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
.

We wish to consider problem (P) for any initial time
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, i.e. require
\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu
. Accordingly,

Definition 2.1
.

A
mild solution
to problem (P) on
[t_{0},T]
for the initial condition
\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu
is a continuous map
\mu_{\bullet}:[t_{0},T]\to\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
that satisfies

\mu_{t}=P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}P^{v}_{s,t}[f_{s}(\mu_{s})]\,ds,\qquad t\in[t_{0},T],

(17)

and that is bounded in total variation norm, i.e.
\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty
.

The integral in (
17
) must be interpreted as Bochner integral as above, as for Proposition
2.3
. For a continuous map
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
into
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, the function
t\mapsto\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
is Borel measurable (cf. Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
). There is no
a priori
guarantee, that it is bounded. Therefore, it is included in the definition. If
\mu_{t}
is positive for every
t
, then continuity of
\mu_{\bullet}
assures bounded total variation, since
\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}=\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
for
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
.

The integrand in (
17
) – as a measure-valued function of
s
– must be strongly Bochner measurable. The conditions imposed on
f_{\bullet}
, following
[
11
,
3
]
, will ensure this:

(A1)

f:(t,\mu)\mapsto f_{t}(\mu):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}\to\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
is jointly continuous;

(A2)

For every
t\geq 0
and
R>0
there exists
L_{R}^{f}>0
such that

\bigl\|f_{t}(\mu_{1})-f_{t}(\mu_{2})\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\leq L_{R}^{f}\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega).

(A3)

For every
R>0
there exists
C_{R}^{f}>0
such that

\|f_{t}(\mu)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C_{R}^{f}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega),\ t\geq 0.

In fact, (A1) and (A2) imply that
s\mapsto f_{s}(\mu_{s})
is continuous for continuous
\mu_{\bullet}
and that consequently the Bochner integral in (
17
) is well-defined and exists. Note that the function
R\mapsto C^{f}_{R}
in (A3) must be non-decreasing. It is immediately clear that any finite linear combination of maps that satisfy (A1)–(A3) will satisfy the very same assumptions.

Examples of reaction maps
f_{\bullet}
that satisfy Assumptions (A1)–(A3) appear naturally in many modelling settings, as the following proposition illustrates. The map
f_{\bullet}
is split there in two parts: a positive general production term (‘
p
’) and a term that combines degradation and individual-induced proliferation. The latter should then be absolutely continuous with respect to the given mass distribution, since by the latter processes mass can only appear or disappear where there is mass.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p_{\bullet}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}\to\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}:(t,\mu)\mapsto p_{t}(\mu)
satisfy (A1)–(A3). Let
F_{\bullet}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}\to\mathrm{BL}(\Omega):(t,\mu)\mapsto F_{t}(\mu)
be jointly continuous and satisfies:

(A2’)

For every
t\geq 0
and
R>0
there exists
\hat{L}^{F}_{R}>0
such that

\bigl\|F_{t}(\mu_{1})-F_{t}(\mu_{2})\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\ \leq\ \hat{L}_{R}^{F}\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega);

(A3’)

For every
R>0
there exists
\hat{C}^{F}_{R}>0
such that

\|F_{t}(\mu)\|_{\infty}\ \leq\ \hat{C}_{R}^{F}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega),\ t\geq 0.

Then,

f_{t}(\mu)\ :=\ p_{t}(\mu)\ +\ F_{t}(\mu)\cdot\mu

(18)

satisfies (A1)–(A3).

Proof.

Recall that
\|g\cdot\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\leq 2\|g\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
for any
g\in\mathrm{BL}(\Omega)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. Hence, for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathrm{BL}(\Omega)
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
,

\bigl\|g_{1}\cdot\mu_{1}-g_{2}\cdot\mu_{2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ \leq\ 2\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\|\mu_{1}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ +\ 2\|g_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}.

Thus, the bilinear map
(g,\mu)\mapsto g\cdot\mu:\mathrm{BL}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}\to\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
is jointly continuous. Then, for
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
and
t\geq 0
,

\bigl\|F_{t}(\mu_{1})\cdot\mu_{1}-F_{t}(\mu_{2})\cdot\mu_{2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ \leq\ (2R\hat{L}_{R}^{F}+2\hat{C}_{R}^{F})\,\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*},

so (A2) is satisfied. Moreover, for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
and
t\geq 0
,

\bigl\|F_{t}(\mu)\cdot\mu\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \leq\bigl\|F_{t}(\mu)\bigr\|_{\infty}\,\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \leq\ R\hat{C}_{R}^{F},

which proves (A3).
∎

2.3
Well-posedness – Revisiting the construction of the solution operator

The well-posedness of Problem (P) or, equivalently, of Problem (
17
) can be shown along the lines of reasoning of Section 6 in
[
3
]
or the proof of Theorem 2.4 in
[
11
]
. We will get into some details here, because some intermediate results or constructions will be of relevance later.

Lemma 2.1

(
Uniqueness)
.

Assume that
\mu^{1}_{\bullet}
and
\mu^{2}_{\bullet}
are both mild solutions to Problem (P) on
[t_{0},T]
for initial condition
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. Then
\mu^{1}_{t}=\mu^{2}_{t}
for all
t\in[t_{0},T]
.

Proof.

Let
R:=\max_{i}\,\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\|\mu^{i}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty
. Then (
17
) yields for any
t\in[t_{0},T]
:

\displaystyle\|\mu^{1}_{t}-\mu^{2}_{t}\|^{*}_{\mathrm{FM}}\

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu^{1}_{s})-f_{s}(\mu^{2}_{s})\bigr]\right\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,ds\ \leq\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}L^{v}_{s,t}\bigl\|f_{s}(\mu^{1}_{s})-f_{s}(\mu^{2}_{s})\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\ L_{R}^{f}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}L^{v}_{s,t}\bigl\|\mu^{1}_{s}-\mu^{2}_{s}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,ds

Application of a Grönwall’s Lemma gives
\|\mu^{1}_{t}-\mu^{2}_{t}\|^{*}_{\mathrm{FM}}=0
for
t\in[t_{0},T]
.
∎

For fixed vector field
v_{\bullet}
,
\delta>0
and initial condition
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, let
t_{0}\geq 0
and
\tau>0
and put
I_{\tau}:=[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
. The argumentation consists essentially of considering the iteration of operators
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}:C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})\to C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
, where

C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})\ :=\ \bigl\{\mu_{\bullet}\in C(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})\colon\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu,\ \sup_{t\in I_{\tau}}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\delta\bigr\}

comes with the the metric associated to the supremum norm on
C(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
. In view of Proposition
2.1
, the metric space
C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
is complete.
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}
is defined by

\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}(\mu_{\bullet})(t)\ :=\ P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\mu_{t_{0}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu_{s})\bigr]\,ds,\qquad t\in I_{\tau}.

(19)

The conditions (V1)–(V3) on
v_{\bullet}
and (A1)–(A3) on
f_{\bullet}
ensure that for sufficiently small
\tau
, depending only on
\nu
through
\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
,
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}
is a strict contraction on
C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
. The Banach Fixed Point Theorem then yields the existence of a (unique) mild solution on
I_{\tau}
. We shall sketch details of the argument through a few main observations and estimates.

The first auxiliary result is:

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
v_{\bullet}
and
f_{\bullet}
satisfy (V1)–(V3) and (A1)–(A3). Let
t_{0}\geq 0
,
R>0
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
and put
r:=\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\delta
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
\mu_{\bullet}\in C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
, then
\|\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}(\mu_{\bullet})(t)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+C_{r}^{f}(t-t_{0})
for any
t\in I_{\tau}.

(ii)

If
\tau\leq\delta/C^{f}_{R+\delta}
, then
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}
maps
C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
into itself, for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

(i).
One has for
t\in[t_{0},T]
, using Proposition
2.2
:

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}(\mu_{\bullet})(t)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\

\displaystyle\leq\ \|P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ +\ \left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu_{s})\bigr]\,ds\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \leq\ \|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu_{s})\bigr]\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\ \|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|f_{s}(\mu_{s})\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,ds\ \leq\ \|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ +\ C_{r}^{f}(t-t_{0}).

(ii)
is immediately clear from
(i)
and
x\mapsto C^{f}_{x}
being non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.
∎

The existence of a (necessarily unique) fixed point for
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}
in
C_{\nu,\delta}(I_{\tau},\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
– hence, a mild solution to Problem (P) on
I_{\tau}
– for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
is guaranteed by making
\tau
smaller than
\delta/C^{f}_{R+\delta}
, in such a way that
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}
becomes a strict contraction on each of the closed, hence complete, sets

,
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
(cf.
[
3
]

\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
one obtains a sequence

with

,

, and a ‘chain’ of mild solutions
\mu^{n}_{\bullet}
to Problem (P) on
[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
with initial condition
\mu^{n}_{t_{n-1}}=\mu^{n-1}_{t_{n-1}}
, where
\mu^{1}_{t_{0}}=\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega)
. Each
\mu^{n}_{\bullet}
is the fixed point of
\Psi_{t_{n-1},\tau_{n}}
. One has

\sup_{t_{n-1}\leq t\leq t_{n}}\bigl\|\mu^{n}_{t}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \leq R+n\delta.

Piecing the parts of the chain continuously together, one obtains a mild solution
\mu_{\bullet}
to Problem (P) with initial condition
\nu
at
t_{0}
, on the interval
[t_{0},\tilde{T})
, where
\tilde{T}:=t_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau_{n}
. If
\tilde{\nu}:=\lim_{t\to\tilde{T}}\mu_{t}
exists in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, then
\|\tilde{\nu}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}<\infty
. If
\|\tilde{\nu}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty
too, then the mild solution can be extended from initial (measure) condition
\tilde{\nu}
at time
\tilde{T}
, following a similar procedure. Otherwise,
\tilde{\nu}
is not a signed measure, and the mild solution cannot be extended beyond
\tilde{T}
.

Thus, putting all pieces together, one arrives at the following result:

Proposition 2.5

(
Well-posedness
;
[
3
]
Section 6 and
[
11
]
, Theorem 2.4)
.

Assume that
v_{\bullet}
and

and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
a given initial condition at time

. There exists a maximal time

, depending on

and

for

. One has:

(i)

Either

, or
\lim_{t\uparrow T^{*}}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=+\infty
.

(ii)

\mu_{\bullet}
depends locally Lipschitz-continuously on

in
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{\mathrm{FM}}
-norm, uniformly locally in time. That is, for every

and there exists

,

\|\mu^{1}_{t}-\mu^{2}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ \leq\ e^{\omega_{R,T}t}\,\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*},\qquad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},T].

Proof.

(i).
Standard argument by contradiction. If

, and the limit is not infinite, one can extend the solution slightly beyond

; see e.g. the extension arguments used in
[

when identifying global solutions to differential equations.

(ii).
For any
t\in[t_{0},T^{*})
, (

) holds. Let
t_{0}<T<T^{*}
. Then, according to part

. Then, one obtains, by applying (

\displaystyle\|\mu^{1}_{t}-\mu^{2}_{t}\|^{*}_{\mathrm{FM}}\

\displaystyle\leq\ \bigl\|P^{v}_{t_{0},t}(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu^{1}_{s})-f_{s}(\mu^{2}_{s})\bigr]\right\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,ds

Remark 2.1
.

Thus, let
v_{\bullet}
satisfy (V1)–(V3) and let

3
Weak limit of sequences of measures with
L^{p}
-densities

In this section, we collect results that hold in the general setting of Polish spaces, which we denote by
S
to distinguish them from the specific domain
\Omega
. These are novel and of general interest, we believe, because there are hardly any results in the literature that connect weak convergence in measures – for measures with
L^{p}
-densities – to absolute continuity of the limit measure, with similar regularity for its density.

The main result will precisely allow us to conclude this – under suitable conditions of course: the limit in the
C_{b}
-weak topology on measures of a sequence of measures, each absolutely continuous with respect to the same reference measure and with Radon-Nikodym derivative in
L^{p}
, is itself in
L^{p}
too, provided
1<p\leq\infty
. If
p=1
, then such a result cannot be expected to hold. Take for example on
\mathbb{R}
the sequence of probability measures that are normally distributed with mean 0 and with standard deviation converging to zero. Then, this sequence of measures converges weakly to
\delta_{0}
, while they all have
L^{1}
-norm
1
.

The proof of our result is founded on a characterisation of
L^{p}
-functions encountered in
[
8
]
Ex. 4.7.102, p.315:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\nu
be a finite positive measure on a measurable space
(S,\Sigma)
. Then, a function
f
is in
L^{p}(S,\nu)
with
1<p<\infty
if and only if there exists
C>0
such that for every finite disjoint measurable partition
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
of
S
with
\nu(A_{k})>0
for all
k
,

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\left|\int_{A_{k}}fd\nu\right|^{p}\ \leq\ C.

(21)

The smallest such constant
C
is
\|f\|_{p}^{p}
.

Consider now a general Polish space
S
, equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Let
\nu
be a positive Borel measure on
S
. Define

L^{p}_{+}(\nu):=\bigl\{f\in L^{1}\cap L^{p}(S,\nu)\colon f\geq 0\ \mbox{$\nu$-a.e}\bigr\}.

The crucial auxiliary result alluded to above is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\nu
be a positive Radon measure on the Polish space
S
. Let
1<p\leq\infty
and
f_{n}\in L^{p}_{+}(\nu)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mu_{n}:=f_{n}d\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
. Suppose that
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{*}
in the weak topology on measures.
Then the following holds:

(i)

There exists a
\sigma
-compact set
S_{*}
on which all
\mu_{n}
and
\mu_{*}
are concentrated. The Borel measure
\nu_{*}
defined by
\nu_{*}(E):=\nu(E\cap S_{*})
for any Borel
E\subset S
is
\sigma
-finite.

(ii)

If
M:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{p}<\infty
, then
\mu_{*}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu_{*}
and hence
\nu
. The Radon-Nikodym derivative
f_{*}
of
\mu_{*}
with respect to
\nu_{*}
is in
L^{p}(S,\nu_{*})
, with
\|f_{*}\|_{p}\leq M
. In particular, we have
\mu_{*}=fd\nu
with
f:=f_{*}{1\!\!1}_{S_{*}}\in L^{p}_{+}(\nu)
and
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}\leq M
.

Proof.

(i)
. Since
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{*}
in the weak topology of measures, the set of measures
\mathcal{M}_{*}:=\{\mu_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\mu_{*}\}
is weakly (sequentially) compact. Hence, it is uniformly tight, by the Prokhorov Theorem (cf.
[
7
]
, Theorem 8.6.2, p.202). Therefore, there exists an increasing sequence
(K_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of compact sets in
S
, such that
\mu(S\setminus K_{m})<\frac{1}{m}
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
are concentrated on the
\sigma
-compact set
S_{*}:=\bigcup_{m}K_{m}
in
S
. Since
\nu
is a Radon measure,
\nu(K_{m})<\infty
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\nu(K_{m})>0
for all
m
. In the usual way, one constructs from
(K_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
a countable family
(E_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise disjoint Borel sets such that
K_{m}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{m}E_{k}
. Let
\nu_{*}
be the Borel measure on
S
obtained by restricting
\nu
to
S_{*}
as indicated. Define
E_{0}:=S\setminus S_{*}
. Then
\nu_{*}(E_{m})<\infty
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, while
S=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}E_{m}
. In particular,
\nu_{*}
is
\sigma
-finite.

(ii)
. Since
\mu_{n}
and
\mu_{*}
are positive finite measures, Alexandrov’s Theorem (cf. e.g.
[
7
]
Theorem 8.2.3, p.184) yields that for any
U\subset S
open,

\displaystyle\mu_{*}(U)\

\displaystyle\leq\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(U)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{U\cap S_{*}}f_{n}\,d\nu=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{U\cap E_{m}}f_{n}\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{p}\,\nu(U\cap E_{m})^{1/q}\ \leq\ M\nu_{*}(U)^{1/q},

(22)

where we used Jensen’s Inequality in the last step. In this context,
q
is the coefficient conjugate to
p
:
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Since
S
is Polish,
\mu_{*}
is Radon and so is
\nu
by assumption. Thus, both
\mu_{*}
and
\nu_{*}
are open outer regular on Borel sets
E
:

\mu_{*}(E)\ =\ \inf\bigl\{\mu_{*}(U)\colon U\subset S\ \mbox{open},\ E\subset U\bigl\}.

Consequently, estimate (
22
) yields

\mu_{*}(E)\ \leq\ M\nu_{*}(E)^{1/q},\quad\mbox{for all Borel}\ E\subset S.

(23)

Hence, if
E\subset S
is a
\nu_{*}
-null set, then
\mu_{*}(E)=0
. That is,
\mu_{*}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu_{*}
,
\mu_{*}\ll\nu_{*}
. If
E\subset S
is a
\nu
-null set, then it is also a
\nu_{*}
-null set. So, also
\mu_{*}\ll\nu
.

Since
\nu_{*}
is
\sigma
-finite, the Radon-Nikodym Theorem implies the existence of the Radon-Nikodym derivative,
f_{*}\in L^{1}(S,\nu_{*})
, of
\mu_{*}
relative to
\nu_{*}
.
For the proof of the statements that
f_{*}\in L^{p}(S,\nu_{*})
and
\|f_{*}\|_{p}\leq M
, we need to distinguish the case
p=\infty
from
1<p<\infty
.

Case
p=\infty
.
Letting
f:=f_{*}{1\!\!1}_{S_{*}}
, one has

\|f\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\nu_{*})}\ =\ \inf_{S^{\prime}:\nu_{*}(S\setminus S^{\prime})=0}\ \sup_{x\in S^{\prime}}|f(x)|\ =\ \inf\bigl\{M^{\prime}\geq 0\colon|f|\leq M^{\prime}\ \nu_{*}-\mbox{a.e.}\bigr\}.

Suppose that
f_{*}
is not essentially bounded. Then there must exists a Borel set
E
and
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{*}{1\!\!1}_{E}>M{1\!\!1}_{E}
and
\nu_{*}(E\cap E_{m_{0}})>0
. Then

\mu_{*}(E\cap E_{m_{0}})=\int_{S}f_{*}{1\!\!1}_{E\cap E_{m_{0}}}\,d\nu_{*}\ >\ \int_{S}M{1\!\!1}_{E\cap E_{m_{0}}}\,d\nu_{*}=M\nu_{*}(E\cap E_{m_{0}}),

which contradicts estimate (
23
) (note that
q=1
). So,
f_{*}
must be essentially bounded. Similar argumentation as above, shows that the assumption that
\|f_{*}\|_{\infty}>M
leads to a contradiction with (
23
). So, the bound
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq M
holds.

Case
1<p<\infty
.
Let
q
be the coefficient conjugate to
p
and let
\nu_{m}
be the Borel measure on
K_{m}
obtained by restricting
\nu
to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(K_{m})
. We shall show that
f_{*}|_{K_{m}}\in L^{p}(K_{m},\nu_{m})
and
\|f_{*}|_{K_{m}}\|_{p}\leq M
. Then, by monotone convergence,

\int_{S}|f_{*}|^{p}\,d\nu_{*}\ =\ \lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{K_{m}}\bigl|f_{*}|_{K_{m}}\bigr|^{p}\,d\nu_{m}\ \leq\ M^{p}.

To this end, fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}
be a disjoint Borel measurable partitioning of
K_{m}
with
\nu(A_{k})>0
for all
k
.
The assumptions on
f_{n}
imply that

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\left|\int_{A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\right|^{p}\ \leq\ \|f_{n}{1\!\!1}_{K_{m}}\|_{L^{p}(\nu_{m})}^{p}\ \leq\ \|f_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}\quad\mbox{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\nu(A_{k})<\infty
, for any Borel set
B
in
S
with
B\supset A_{k}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

0\leq\int_{B}f_{n}d\nu-\int_{A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu=\int_{B\setminus A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\ \leq\ \|f_{n}\|_{p}\,\nu(B\setminus A_{k})^{1/q}.

Moreover, because
\nu
is open outer regular, for each fixed
k
one can select an open set
U_{k}
in
S
with
U_{k}\supset A_{k}
and
\nu(U_{k})<\infty
, such that

\nu(U_{k}\setminus A_{k})^{p/q}\ <\ \frac{1}{N}\nu(A_{k})^{p/q}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{M^{p}+1}.

Then, applying Alexandrov’s Theorem again to the measures
\mu_{n}=f_{n}d\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(S)
that converge weakly to
\mu_{*}=f_{*}d\nu_{*}
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left|\int_{U_{k}}f_{*}d\nu_{*}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\ \leq\ \left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\,\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{U_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\,\left|\int_{U_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|\;\nu(A_{k})^{-1/q}\int_{A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\ +\ \nu(A_{k})^{-1/q}\int_{U_{k}\setminus A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\;\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[\,\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\left|\int_{A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{-p/q}\left|\int_{U_{k}\setminus A_{k}}f_{n}d\nu\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\nu)}\ +\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{-p/q}\cdot\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}^{p}\,\nu(U_{k}\setminus A_{k})^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle<\ M\ +\ \varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was taken arbitrarily and
f_{*}\geq 0

\nu_{*}
-a.e., we arrive at

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\left|\int_{A_{k}}f_{*}d\nu_{*}\right|^{p}\ \leq\ \sum_{k=1}^{N}\nu(A_{k})^{1-p}\left|\int_{U_{k}}f_{*}d\nu_{*}\right|^{p}\ \leq\ M^{p}.

Thus,
f|_{K_{m}}\in L^{p}(S,\nu_{*})
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\|f_{*}|_{K_{m}}\|_{p}\leq M
. We conclude that
f_{*}\in L^{p}(S,\nu_{*})
and
\|f\|_{p}\leq M
. Finally, it is a simple observation that
f_{*}{1\!\!1}_{S_{*}}\in L^{p}(S,\nu)
and that
\|f_{*}{1\!\!1}_{S_{*}}\|_{L^{p}(\nu)}=\|f_{*}\|_{L^{p}(\nu_{*})}
.
∎

If the sequence of functions

in Theorem

-norm, then

and

:

We shall identify functions on

that are in

relative to a fixed positive Borel measure
\nu
on

with finite signed Borel measures on

through the linear map

. Additionally, one has

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\nu
be a positive Radon measure on
S
. Then, for any

and

,

is closed in
\mathcal{M}(S)
for the weak topology on measures. Hence, it is complete in
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
.

Proof.

First, since the weak topology on

is metrizable (e.g. by the metric derived from the Fortet-Mourier norm) and

, viewed as set of measures, consists of positive measures, the weak topology on measures is metrizable on

. Thus, it suffices to show that for any sequence
(f_{n})
in

that weakly converges to a measure

, that

. This is established by Theorem

. The last statement follows from the first part, that

metrizes the weak topology on measures on

and the completeness of the latter for the

-norm.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

1.) The Radon-Nikodym Theorem is valid for
\sigma
-finite measures
[
12
, Theorem 4.2.2]
. If the reference measure is not
\sigma
-finite then the measure that is absolutely continuous with respect to the latter, may not have a density with respect to this measure. An example of such a situation is given e.g. in
[
25
, Exercise 3.2.13, p.92]
.
Therefore, from the absolute continuity of
\mu_{*}
with respect to the general Radon measure
\nu
one cannot conclude by this theorem, that
\mu_{*}
has a density with respect to
\nu
. The proof of this result in Theorem
3.1
is based on the absolute continuity of
\mu_{*}
with respect to the restriction
\nu_{*}
of
\nu
to the
\sigma
-compact set

, which is
\sigma
-finite.

2.) We do not wish to assume
\sigma
-finiteness of

in Theorem

, because we want in the future to allow for

being e.g. a Hausdorff measure or a Haar measure on a topological group, which may not be
\sigma
-finite. Lebesgue measure

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is, of course.

3.) The reader should notice that the lattice operations

and

are not continuous for the weak topology on measures. The results above therefore do not readily extend to sequences
(f_{n})
in

that do not consist of positive functions.

4.) It is worth recalling the observation that Corollary

cannot hold for

.

4.1
Basic properties of the flow semigroup

First, in the ‘classical setting’ that we have for the time-dependent velocity field
v_{\bullet}
, namely that (V1)–(V3) are satisfied (i.e.,

\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}
to ODE (

11
) for the Lipschitz constant, and having Lipschitz inverse. Moreover, for any
t_{0}\geq 0
,
\tau>0
,
t\in I_{\tau}=[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
and

,

d(\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}(x),x)=\bigl|\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}(x)-x\bigr|\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\bigl|v_{s}(\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},s}(x))\bigr|\,ds\leq C_{t_{0},\tau}|t-t_{0}|\ \to\ 0\qquad\mbox{as}\ t\downarrow t_{0}.

(24)

Here, one can take
C_{t_{0},\tau}=\sup_{s\in I_{\tau}}\|v_{s}\|_{\infty}

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
t_{0}\geq 0
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, consider the motion
\varphi:t\mapsto P_{t_{0},t}^{v}\mu:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. The following statements hold:

(i)

\varphi
is continuous for the
C_{b}(\Omega)
-weak topology on measures.

(ii)

\varphi
is locally Lipschitz continuous for the
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
-norm topology. In particular, for any bounded interval

, there exists a constant

such that

\|P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\mu-P^{v}_{t_{0},s}\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{I}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\cdot|t-s|\qquad\mbox{for all}\ s,t\in I,\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega),

(25)

where the constant

may be taken as

C^{\prime}_{I}=\sup_{t\in I}\|v_{t}\|_{\infty}\cdot\exp\bigl(\int_{t_{0}}^{\bar{t}}|v_{\tau}|_{L}\,d\tau\bigr)=L^{v}_{t_{0},\bar{t}}\,\sup_{t\in I}\|v_{t}\|_{\infty},\qquad\bar{t}:=\sup(I).

(26)

Proof.

(i).
For any
t\geq t_{0}
and

,

\langle P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\mu,f\rangle=\langle\mu,f\circ\Phi_{t_{0},t}^{v}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f(\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}(x))\,d\mu(x).

For fixed

,

is continuous. Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem gives the continuity of
t\mapsto\langle P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\mu,f\rangle
.

(ii).
Take

with
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
|f|_{L}\leq 1
. Let
I
be a bounded interval in
[t_{0},\infty)
and
s,t\in I
. Then, using (
11
) and (
24
), one gets for any

:

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\mu-P^{v}_{t_{0},s}\mu,f\rangle\bigr|\

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{\Omega}\bigl|f(\Phi_{t_{0},t}^{v}(x))-f(\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},s}(x))\bigr|\,d|\mu|(x)

\displaystyle\leq\ |f|_{L}|\Phi_{t_{0},(s\wedge t)}|_{L}\int_{\Omega}d\bigl(\Phi^{v}_{(s\wedge t),(s\vee t)}(x),x\bigr)\,d|\mu|(x)

\displaystyle\leq\ |f|_{L}\cdot\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{(s\wedge t)}|v_{\tau}|_{L}\,d\tau\right)\cdot C_{I}\bigl|(s\vee t)-(s\wedge t)\bigr|\cdot\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.

Here, one can take

, according to (
24
).
Taking the supremum over

in the unit ball in

yields (

),
where one can take
C^{\prime}_{I}
as in (

).
∎

We shall further need the following transport formula for measures that are absolutely continuous with respect to Borel-Lebesgue measure

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

(27)

Moreover, for any
t\geq t_{0}\geq 0
for
\lambda
-a.e.

\exp\bigl(-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\bigl\|[\mathrm{div}(v_{s})]^{-}\bigr\|_{\infty}\,ds\bigr)\ \leq\ \det D\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}(x)\ \leq\ \exp\bigl(-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\bigl\|[\mathrm{div}(v_{s})]^{+}\bigr\|_{\infty}\,ds\bigr).

(28)

Proof.

The proof is based on the classical area formula for Lipschitz maps, see
[

]
p.1196-1197 for details, equation (2.5) in particular. Estimate (

[

]

Remark 4.1
.

In various works one has studied flows defined by vector field that are less regular than Lipschitz, motivated – among others – by Fluid Mechanics and Control Theory, see e.g.
[
15
,
6
,
5
]
. Typically, Lebesgue measure is taken as a reference measure, like we do here. However, Bogachev and Mayer-Wolf
[

]
provide conditions under which the push-forward of a finite measure under the flow results into finite measures that are still absolutely continuous with respect to that measure. Although their results apply to probability measures as reference, instead of the Borel-Lebesgue measure, results similar to those stated above were obtained (cf. in particular
[

]

The explicit expression for the push-forward under the flow and estimates in Lemma

gives

Proposition 4.1
.

For any

, and any

leaves

invariant. If

, with

given by (

Proof.

Using that the flow operators

and – again – the transformation formula for the Borel-Lebesgue measure, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(x)^{p}\,dx\

\displaystyle=\ \int_{\Omega}u_{0}\bigl((\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t})^{-1}(x)\bigr)^{p}\bigl|\det D\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}\bigl((\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t})^{-1}(x)\bigr)\bigr|^{-p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(y)^{p}\bigl|\det D\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}(y)\bigr|^{-p}\cdot\bigl|\det D(\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t})^{-1}\bigl(\Phi^{v}_{t_{0},t}(y)\bigr)\bigr|^{-1}\,dy

for

\Rightarrow
(ii)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\mbox{$\frac{(t-t_{0})^{k}}{k!}$}B^{k}e^{-B(t-t_{0})}P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu\ +\ B^{k}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{k}}{k!}$}e^{-B(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu_{s})\bigr]\,\,ds\right]

, one gets

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-B(t-s)}

\displaystyle P^{v}_{s,t}\mu_{s}\,\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{n}}{n!}$}\,ds\ =\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{n}}{n!}$}e^{-B(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\,e^{-B(s-t_{0})}P^{v}_{t_{0},s}\nu\,\,ds

\displaystyle+\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{n}}{n!}$}e^{-B(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\,\left[\int_{t_{0}}^{s}e^{-B(s-\sigma)}P^{v}_{\sigma,s}\bigl[f_{\sigma}(\mu_{\sigma})\bigr]\,d\sigma\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\ B\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{n}}{n!}$}e^{-B(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\left[\int_{t_{0}}^{s}e^{-B(s-\sigma)}P^{v}_{\sigma,s}\mu_{\sigma}\,d\sigma\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=\ \mbox{$\frac{(t-t_{0})^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$}\,e^{-B(t-t_{0})}P^{v}_{t_{0},s}\nu\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{t_{0}}^{s}\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{n}}{n!}$}e^{-B(t-\sigma)}P^{v}_{\sigma,t}\bigl[f_{\sigma}(\mu_{\sigma})\bigr]\,d\sigma\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\ B\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{t_{0}}^{s}\mbox{$\frac{(t-s)^{n}}{n!}$}e^{-B(t-\sigma)}P^{v}_{\sigma,t}\mu_{\sigma}\,d\sigma\,ds

\displaystyle=\ \mbox{$\frac{(t-t_{0})^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$}\,e^{-B(t-t_{0})}P^{v}_{t_{0},s}\nu\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mbox{$\frac{(t-\sigma)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$}e^{-B(t-\sigma)}P^{v}_{\sigma,t}\bigl[f_{\sigma}(\mu_{\sigma})\bigr]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\qquad+\ B\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mbox{$\frac{(t-\sigma)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$}e^{-B(t-\sigma)}P^{v}_{\sigma,t}\mu_{\sigma}\,d\sigma.

Thus, the induction step is completed and we conclude that (
34
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By letting
n\to\infty
in (
34
), we obtain (
17
). Mild solutions are unique. Therefore,
\mu_{\bullet}
must be the mild solution to Problem (P) on
[t_{0},T]
with initial condition
\nu
.
∎

The equivalence of Problem (P) to the ‘dilated’ problem (
30
) or (
31
) as observed in Proposition
4.2
has a counterpart with regard to the Picard iteration operators
\Psi_{t_{0},\tau}
and those connected to the
B
-dilated problem (
30
):

\Psi^{B}_{t_{0},\tau}(\nu_{\bullet})(t)\ :=\ e^{-B(t-t_{0})}P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu_{t_{0}}\ +\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-B(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\nu_{s})+B\nu_{s}\bigr]\,ds\quad\mbox{for}\ t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau].

(35)

It is the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0\leq t_{0}<T
and let
B
be a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
that commutes with all
P^{v}_{s,t}
for
s,t\in[t_{0},T]
,
s\leq t
. Let
\delta>0
and
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
. Suppose that
t_{1}\in[t_{0},T)
and
\tau_{1}>0
are such that
t_{1}+\tau_{1}\leq T
and
\Psi_{t_{1},\tau_{1}}
is a strict contraction on
C_{\nu_{0},\delta}([t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1}],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
. Let
\mu_{\bullet}^{*}
be the unique fixed point in this space. Then, for any
r^{\prime}>\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\delta
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and an equidistant partition
t_{1}=t^{\prime}_{0}<t^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<t^{\prime}_{N}=t_{1}+\tau_{1}
of
[t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1}]
of width
\tau^{\prime}:=\tau_{1}/N
, such that
\Psi^{B}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}}
is a strict contraction in
C_{\nu_{j},\delta_{j}}([t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j+1}],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
for all
j=0,1,\dots,N-1
, where
\nu_{j}:=\mu^{*}_{t^{\prime}_{j}}
and
\delta_{j}:=r^{\prime}-\|\nu_{j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}>0
. Moreover,
\nu_{j+1}
is the end point at
t=t^{\prime}_{j+1}
of the unique fixed point of
\Psi^{B}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
b
be the operator norm of
B
acting in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
. If
b=0
, there is nothing to prove. So, assume
b>0
. Put
r:=\|\nu_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\delta
. Note that
\|\mu^{*}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq r
for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1}]
. Let
r^{\prime}>r
. For any
t_{2}\in[t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1})
and
\tau_{2}>0
such that
t_{2}+\tau_{2}\leq t_{1}+\tau_{1}
and for any
\delta^{\prime}>0
and
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
such that
\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\delta^{\prime}\leq r^{\prime}
, one gets for
\nu^{1}_{\bullet},\nu^{2}_{\bullet}\in C_{\nu^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}([t_{2},t_{2}+\tau_{2}],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
and
t\in[t_{2},t_{2}+\tau_{2}]
the estimates

\displaystyle\bigl\|\Psi^{B}_{t_{2},\tau_{2}}(\nu^{1}_{\bullet})(t)-\Psi^{B}_{t_{2},\tau_{2}}(\nu^{2}_{\bullet})(t)\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{t_{2}}^{t}e^{-b(t-s)}\bigl\|P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\nu^{1}_{s})-f_{s}(\nu^{2}_{s})\ +\ B(\nu^{1}_{s}-\nu^{2}_{s})\bigr]\bigr\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{t_{2}}^{t}e^{-b(t-s)}L^{v}_{s,t}(L^{f}_{r^{\prime}}+b)\,\|\nu^{1}_{s}-\nu^{2}_{s}\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\,ds

\displaystyle\ \leq\ (L^{f}_{r^{\prime}}+b)L^{v}_{t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1}}\,\frac{1-e^{-b\tau_{2}}}{b}\,\|\nu^{1}_{\bullet}-\nu^{2}_{\bullet}\|_{\infty}.

Pick
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(L^{f}_{r^{\prime}}+b)L^{v}_{t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1}}\,\frac{1-e^{-b\tau_{1}/N}}{b}\ <\ 1.

(36)

If
N=1
, the operator
\Psi^{B}_{t_{1},\tau_{1}}
is a strict contraction and the proof is complete. If
N\geq 2
, then put
\tau^{\prime}:=\tau_{1}/N
and let
t^{\prime}_{j}:=t_{1}+j\tau^{\prime}
for
j=0,1,\dots,N-1
. Define
\nu_{j}:=\mu_{t^{\prime}_{j}}^{*}
and
\delta_{j}:=r^{\prime}-\|\nu_{j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
. Since
\|\mu_{t}^{*}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq r<r^{\prime}
for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+\tau_{1}]
,
\delta_{j}>0
for all
j
. The assumptions and choices made yield the conclusion that
\Psi^{B}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}}
is a strict contraction on
C_{\nu_{j},\delta_{j}}([t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j+1}],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
.

The unique fixed point of
\Psi^{B}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}}
, say
\mu^{(j)}_{\bullet}
, must be a mild solution to Problem (P) on
[t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j+1}]
, according to Proposition
4.2
. Mild solutions are unique (see Lemma
2.1
). Thus,
\mu^{(j)}_{\bullet}
equals
\mu^{*}
on
[t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j+1}]
. This yields that
\nu_{j+1}=\mu^{(j)}_{t^{\prime}_{j+1}}
.
∎

4.3
Preservation of positivity

Positivity of the mild solution to Problem (P) for positive initial conditions has been considered in
[
3
]
Corollary 7.2 under the assumption that the reaction term
f
can be written as a rate, or in
[
11
]
Lemma 3.2 for a particular type of equation. In the latter, the problem is reduced to positivity of the solutions to the PDE in
L^{1}(\Omega,\lambda)
, by a density argument. Below, however, we shall give an argument for positivity of solutions that is intrinsic to the measure setting, in the sense that it exploits the Banach lattice structure of
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it allows to consider reaction terms that are more general than those considered in
[
3
]
.

Proposition
4.2
will be crucial in establishing the positivity of the mild solutions for positive initial conditions.
Assume that
f_{\bullet}
satisfies (A1)–(A3) and let
\mu_{\bullet}
be a mild solution to Problem (P) on
[t_{0},T]
, for initial condition
\nu
. Then,
s\mapsto f_{s}(\mu_{s})
is continuous. In particular, it is strongly Bochner measurable. According to Lemma
A.2
, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
the functions
s\mapsto\bigl[f_{s}(\mu_{s})+c\mu_{s}\bigr]^{\pm}
are strongly measurable. Thus, the Bochner integral in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(S)_{\mathrm{BL}}
in (
31
) can be split into one over the positive part and one over the negative part of
f_{s}(\mu_{s})+c\mu_{s}
. Because of positivity of the operators
e^{-c(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}
and closedness of the positive cone
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, each integral separately takes value in
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
, while
e^{-c(t-t_{0})}P^{v}_{t_{0},t}\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
as well for
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
. We conclude from Proposition
4.2
that for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
the following inequality holds:

\mu^{-}_{t}\ \leq\ \int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu_{s})+c\mu_{s}\bigr]^{-}\,ds\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},T].

(37)

This general observation allows drawing conclusions on the positivity of mild solutions for positive initial conditions for quite a general class of reaction terms
f_{\bullet}
. The next result is a straightforward consequence of inequality (
37
):

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f_{\bullet}
satisfy (A1)–(A3) and let
t_{0}>0
. Let
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and let
\mu_{\bullet}
be the mild solution to problem (P) with initial condition
\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu
. Let
[t_{0},T^{*})
be its maximal domain of existence. Assume that for any
R>0
and
T>0
there exists
c=c_{R,T}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

f_{t}(\mu)+c\mu\ \geq\ 0,\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega),\ t\in[0,T].

(38)

Then,
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
for all
t\in[t_{0},T^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
T\in(t_{0},T^{*})
and put
R:=\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
. For
c=c_{R,T}
as indicated,
[f_{s}(\mu_{s})+c\mu_{s}]^{-}=0
for all
s\in[t_{0},T]
. So, (
37
) implies that
\mu^{-}_{t}=0
for
t\in[t_{0},T]
.
∎

However, condition (
38
) is too restrictive and often impractical for many applications. Interestingly, it can be improved especially if the term
f_{\bullet}
has a specific shape. In such case, sufficient conditions ensuring the positivity of the solution can be reached so that they fit better to applications. The second result addresses this issue:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f_{\bullet}
be of the form (
18
) in Proposition
2.4
, such that it satisfies (A1)–(A3).
Assume that
F_{\bullet}
is such that for every
R>0
and
T>0
, there exists
C_{R,T}>0
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|F_{t}(\mu)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{R,T}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\Omega).

(39)

Then, the mild solution
\mu_{\bullet}
to Problem (P) with initial condition
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
is positive for all its time of existence.

Proof.

Let
t_{0}\geq 0
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
. Let
T^{*}>t_{0}
be the maximal time of existence of the mild solution
\mu_{\bullet}
of Problem (P) with initial condition
\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu
. Take
T\in(t_{0},T^{*})
and let
R:=\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
.
For any
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
one has

\displaystyle\bigl[f_{t}(\mu)+c\mu\big]^{-}\

\displaystyle=\ \bigl[p_{t}(\mu)+\bigl(F_{t}(\mu)+c\bigr)\mu\bigr]^{-}

\displaystyle\leq\ \bigl(F_{t}(\mu)+c\bigr)^{-}\mu^{+}\ +\bigl(F_{t}(\mu)+c\bigr)^{+}\mu^{-}.

(40)

Then, for
c\geq C_{R,T}

\bigl[f_{t}(\mu_{t})+c\mu_{t}\big]^{-}\ \leq(C_{R,T}+c)\mu^{-}_{t}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},T]

and, according to (
37
), we have

0\ \leq\mu^{-}_{t}\leq\ (C_{R,T}+c)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\mu_{s}^{-}\,ds\qquad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},T].

Note that the Bochner measurability of the integrand follows from Lemma
A.2
. Taking the
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
-norm on both sides, the ordering is preserved and the direct application of Proposition
2.2
yields

\|\mu^{-}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \leq\ (C_{R,T}+c)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\|\mu^{-}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,ds\qquad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[t_{0},T].

Using Grönwall’s Inequality, one concludes that
\mu^{-}_{t}=0
for all
t\in[t_{0},T]
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

A similar conclusion as in Proposition
4.4
is reached in
[
3
]
Corollary 7.2 , but a (positive) production term
p_{t}(\mu)
in the non-linearity
f_{t}(\mu)
is not allowed by the assumptions made there. Moreover, the argument follows different lines of reasoning than ours.

The result just proven uses the ‘global equation’ (
17
) that the mild solution satisfies. Realizing that the mild solution is locally obtained by Picard iteration, one may weaken condition (
39
) on
F_{\bullet}
to the extent that the bound needs to apply only to
positive
measures. Thus, the ‘behaviour’ of
F_{\bullet}
on non-positive measures is irrelevant to the preservation of positivity. This weakening of condition goes to a cost: the need of a more subtle argumentation in the proof.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f_{\bullet}
be of the form (
18
) in Proposition
2.4
.
Assume that
F_{\bullet}
is such that for every
R>0
and
T>0
, there exists
C^{\prime}_{R,T}>0
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|F_{t}(\mu)\|_{\infty}\leq C^{\prime}_{R,T}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)\ \mbox{with}\ \|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq R.

(41)

Then, the mild solution
\mu_{\bullet}
to Problem (P) with initial condition
\mu_{t_{0}}=\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
is positive for all its time of existence.

Proof.

Let
[t_{0},T^{*})
be the time domain of the maximal mild solution. Recall that
\mu_{\bullet}
is obtained by piecing together the unique fixed points of the ‘Picard iteration operators’
\Psi_{t_{i},\tau_{i}}
(see (
19
)) for various times
t_{i}\in[t_{0},T^{*})
and durations
\tau_{i}>0
. The fixed points are obtained as limit of the iterations of the operators
\Psi_{t_{i},\tau_{i}}
, starting from the function
t\mapsto P^{v}_{t_{i},t}\nu_{i}
on
[t_{i},t_{i}+\tau_{i}]
(see also Remark
2.1
). Each of the iterations and the limit will have total variation norm uniformly bounded by
R_{i}>0
, say, on this interval.

In view of Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, the mild solution can be locally obtained equally well by iteration of suitable
B
-dilated operators
\Psi^{B_{i}}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{i}}
, e.g. with
B_{i}=c_{i}I
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and starting from a function
t\mapsto e^{-c_{i}(t-t^{\prime}_{j})}P^{v}_{t^{\prime}_{j},t}\nu^{\prime}_{j}
, for suitable
\nu^{\prime}_{j}
, on an equally spaced partition
t_{i}=t^{\prime}_{0}<t^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<t^{\prime}_{N}=t_{i}+\tau_{i}
. The number
N
of subintervals depends on
|c_{i}|
and on
R_{i}
in so far that it can be arranged that all iterations under
\Psi^{c_{i}I}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{i}}
– and the limit – have total variation uniformly bounded by
2R_{i}
on each of the subintervals
[t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j+1}]
(see Lemma
4.3
and its proof. In particular,
N
is conditioned by equation (
36
), with
b=|c_{i}|
and
r^{\prime}=2R_{i}
).

Fix
c_{i}\geq C^{\prime}_{2R_{i},t_{i}+\tau_{i}}
and determine
N_{i}
and a partition of
[t_{i},t_{i}+\tau_{i}]
as above, such that each
\Psi^{c_{i}I}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{i}}
,
j=1,\dots,N_{i}
, is a strict contraction. Assume that
\nu^{\prime}_{j}
is positive. Then
\nu^{(0)}_{t}:=e^{-c_{i}(t-t^{\prime}_{j})}P^{v}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{i}}\nu^{\prime}_{j}
is positive for every
t\in[t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j}+\tau^{\prime}_{i}]
. Define iteratively

\nu_{\bullet}^{(n+1)}:=\Psi^{c_{i}I}_{t^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{i}}(\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet}).

One has, by construction, that
\|\nu_{t}^{(n)}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 2R_{i}
for all
n
.
Hence, if
\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
is positive, then condition (
41
) and application of (
40
) gives

\bigl[f_{t}\bigl(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\bigr)+c\nu^{(n)}_{t}\bigr]^{-}\ =\ 0.

So, we conclude that
\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
must be positive on
[t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j}+\tau^{\prime}_{i}]
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
is closed in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, we find that the limit of the iterations
\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
must be positive.
Hence, if
\mu_{t_{i}}
is positive, then
\mu_{\bullet}
is positive on
[t_{i},t_{i}+\tau_{i}]
. Since
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
, we conclude that
\mu_{\bullet}
is positive on its maximal domain of existence.
∎

4.4
Preservation of
L^{p}(\lambda)
-regularity

We shall now consider an additional condition on the reaction term
f_{\bullet}
that ensures that initial conditions that have
L^{p}
density with respect to Borel-Lebesgue measure
\lambda
on
\Omega
for some
1<p\leq\infty
, lead to mild solutions to Problem (P) that are absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda
with the density functions remaining in
L^{p}(\lambda)
for all time up to time of possible blow-up in
L^{p}
-norm of the density. It should be noted, that this time may generally be earlier than the maximal existence time of the mild solution in the space of measures.

To avoid cumbersome notation, we write
\|\mu\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}
to mean the
L^{p}
-norm of the density of
\mu
relative to
\lambda
. By convention, it is
\infty
, if
\mu
is not absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda
, or if the density is not an
L^{p}
-function.

Let
1<p\leq\infty
. For our purpose, it is necessary to assume that for all
t\geq 0
the reaction
f_{t}
maps
L^{1}\cap L^{p}(\lambda)
into itself, viewed as subspaces of
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. With slight abuse of wording, we shall say in that case that ‘
f_{t}

maps
L^{p}(\lambda)
into itself
’. To have our method of proof work, we shall additionally require an assumption similar to (A4) in
L^{p}
-setting. That is,
f_{t}
must be an
L^{p}
-bounded map, locally uniformly in time
. More precisely,

(A4
p
)

For any
t>t^{\prime}
and
r>0
, there exists
B^{f}_{t^{\prime},t}(r)\geq 0
such that

\bigl\|f_{s}(\phi\,d\lambda)\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ \leq\ B^{f}_{t^{\prime},t}(r)\quad\mbox{for all}\ \phi\in L^{p}(\lambda),\ \|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\leq r,\ t^{\prime}\leq s\leq t.

must hold. Put

D^{v,p}_{s,t}:=\exp\left(\mbox{$\frac{1}{q}$}\int_{s}^{t}\bigl\|[\mathrm{div}_{x}(v_{\tau})]^{-}\bigr\|_{\infty}\,d\tau\right),\qquad 0\leq s\leq t,\ \mbox{$\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1$}.

Our major result on ‘
L^{p}
-invariance’ is then:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f_{\bullet}
be of the form (
18
) and satisfy the conditions of Theorem
4.1
. Moreover, assume that for some
1<p\leq\infty
,
f_{t}
maps
L^{p}(\lambda)
into itself for all
t\geq 0
and is an
L^{p}
-bounded map, locally uniformly in time, i.e. satisfies (A4
p
). Let
\nu=\phi d\lambda\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
be an initial condition at time
t_{0}
with
\phi\in L^{p}_{+}(\lambda)
. Then the following statements holds:

(i)

There exists
\hat{T}^{*}_{\nu}\leq T^{*}_{\nu}
such that the maximal mild solution
\mu_{\bullet}
to Problem (P) in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
takes values
\mu_{t}\in L^{p}_{+}(\lambda)
for all
t\in[t_{0},\hat{T}^{*}_{\nu})
.

(ii)

For every
T\in(t_{0},\hat{T}^{*}_{\nu})
,
\sup_{t_{0}\leq t\leq T}\|\mu_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}<\infty
.

(iii)

If
T^{*}_{\nu}<T^{*}_{\nu}
, then
\limsup_{t\to\hat{T}^{*}_{\nu}}\|\mu_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}=+\infty
.

Before getting to the details of the proof, we like to make several observations.

Remark 4.3
.

1.) Theorem
4.2
requires the
L^{p}
-initial condition to be positive and requires the assumption of the conditions of Theorem
4.1
to assure that the mild solution to Problem (P) remains positive throughout its maximal domain of existence. Positivity is required for application of the limit results, Theorem
3.1
and corollary, that allow to conclude that ‘
L^{p}
-ness’ is preserved in the weak limit of measures.

2.) In many applications, the measure-valued solution will represent a distribution of individuals or ‘mass’ in space. It is then natural to have a model that preserves positivity of the solution. Moreover, the shape (
18
) for the reaction map
f_{\bullet}
is natural as well, from modelling perspective. It has at time
t
a production term
p_{t}
and a degradation term that is absolutely continuous with respect to the current distribution,
F_{t}(\mu_{t})\cdot\mu_{t}
, which is in accordance with the principle that ‘mass can only disappear where there is mass present’.

3.) In specific cases, it may happen in principle that
L^{p}
-regularity of the measure-valued solution is lost at time
\hat{T}^{*}_{\nu}
strictly before time
T^{*}_{\nu}
, while the former continues to exist as measure with less regular density or as a measure that may even be singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. See e.g.
[
16
,
34
,
37
]
, although these concern a different type of equation.

Proof.

(Theorem
4.2
).
Let
\mu_{\bullet}
be the maximal mild solution to Problem (P) on
[t_{0},T^{*}_{\nu})
. According to Theorem
4.1
,
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
for all
t
. Let
t^{\prime}\in[t_{0},T^{*}_{\nu})
such that
\nu^{\prime}:=\mu_{t^{\prime}}
has an
L^{p}
-density with respect to
\lambda
. Following the proof Theorem
4.1
, there exist
c\geq 0
,
\delta^{\prime}>0
and
\tau^{\prime}>0
such that
t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}<T^{*}_{\nu}
and the Picard iteration operator
\Psi^{c}_{t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}
is a strict contraction on
C_{\nu^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}([t^{\prime},t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}],\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}})
. Moreover, on
[t^{\prime},t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}]
,
\mu_{t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu^{(n)}_{t}
in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
, as in (
20
), with
\Psi_{t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}
replaced by
\Psi^{c}_{t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}
, and
c
is such that each
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
is positive.

Put
D:=D^{v,p}_{t^{\prime},t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}}
. One has for
t^{\prime}\leq t\leq t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}
, using Proposition
4.1
:

\bigl\|\mu^{(0)}_{t}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ =\ e^{-c(t-t^{\prime})}\bigl\|P^{v}_{t^{\prime},t}\nu^{\prime}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ \leq\ D^{v,p}_{t^{\prime},t}\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ \leq D\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

and

\bigl\|\mu^{(n+1)}_{t}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ =\ \bigl\|\Psi^{c}_{t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}\bigl(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\leq\bigl\|\mu^{(0)}_{t}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}+\left\|\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu^{(n)}_{s})+c\mu^{(n)}_{s}\bigr]\,ds\right\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

Let
I_{n}(t)
denote the measure-valued Bochner integral in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
in the last term. We shall show by induction that

\mbox{{\it There exists}}\ 0<\hat{\tau}\leq\tau^{\prime},\ \mbox{{\it such that}}\ \bigl\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\leq 2D\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ \mbox{{\it for all}}\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ t\in[t^{\prime},t^{\prime}+\hat{\tau}].

(42)

For
n=0
it is clearly satisfied. Take
r:=2D\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}
and put
B^{f}_{r}:=\sup_{t\in[t^{\prime},t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}]}B^{f}_{t^{\prime},t}(r)
, with
B^{f}_{t^{\prime},t}(r)
as in Assumption (A4
p
). According to
[
14
]
, we have that

I_{n}(t)\ \in\ (t-t^{\prime})\cdot\overline{\mathrm{co}}\left(\bigl\{e^{-c(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu^{(n)}_{s})+c\mu^{(n)}_{s}\bigr]\colon t^{\prime}\leq s\leq t\bigr\}\right),

where the closure of the convex hull is taken in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)_{\mathrm{FM}}
. For
t^{\prime}\leq s\leq t\leq t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}
, by induction hypothesis,

\bigl\|e^{-c(t-s)}P^{v}_{s,t}\bigl[f_{s}(\mu^{(n)}_{s})+c\mu^{(n)}_{s}\bigr]\bigl\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ \leq\ e^{-c(t-s)}D^{v,p}_{s,t}(B_{r}^{f}+cr)\ \leq\ D(B^{f}_{r}+cr).

Any point in the convex hull is positive by assumption on
c
and will have the same bound for its
L^{p}
-norm.
I_{n}(t)
is the limit in the weak topology on measures of
(t-t^{\prime})
-multiple of a sequence of elements in the convex hull. Because of positivity, Theorem
3.1
yields that
I_{n}(t)\in L^{p}_{+}(\lambda)
and

\bigl\|I_{n}(t)\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\ \leq\ (t-t^{\prime})D(B^{f}_{r}+cr)\quad\mbox{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(43)

One can choose
0<\hat{\tau}\leq\tau^{\prime}
, independent of
n
, such that

\hat{\tau}\cdot D(B_{r}^{f}+cr)\leq D\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

(44)

Then, (
42
) is satisfied.
The iterates
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
being in
L^{p}_{+}(\lambda)
, uniformly bounded in
L^{p}
-norm, we can again invoke Theorem
3.1
, yielding
\mu_{t}\in L^{p}_{+}(\lambda)
for all
t\in[t^{\prime},t^{\prime}+\hat{\tau}]
, with
\|\mu_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\leq 2D\|\nu^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}
.

Now, the ‘classical extension argument’ can be repeated for extending local solutions to a maximal one, starting from
\nu\in L^{p}_{+}(\lambda)
at
t=t_{0}
. This then yields the wanted statements.
∎

5
Concluding considerations and observations

We now want to address some natural questions in relation to the invariance results for the solution operator that we have established in the previous section.

We have shown in Theorem
4.2
that under fairly mild and common assumptions (compare e.g. with those in
[
3
]
) a positive initial condition with
L^{p}
-density with respect to Lebesgue measure leads to a mild solution to Problem (P) that has a positive
L^{p}
-density for some positive amount of time. That may not be the full maximal existence time of the mild solution in the space of measures.

Mild solutions are by definition required to be continuous in time.
Thus, this concept allows to define a dynamical system on (suitable subsets) of measures, which have – usually – continuous motions. What can one say at this point about regularity in time of the mild solution with values in
L^{p}(\lambda)
?
This remains elusive to us at this moment. For finite reference measure instead of the
\sigma
-finite Lebesgue measure, we have found ways to say more about this question. However, the step to non-finite measures is in this context not as easy to make as may be anticipated. This is a topic for further investigation in future works.

Finally, we like to point the reader to a particular ‘feature’ of assumptions that were imposed. The Lipschitz continuity of the reaction term
f_{\bullet}
in measures and in Fortet-Mourier norm, formulated as Assumption (A2), is providing the convergence of the Picard iterations in the space of measures with Fortet-Mourier norm. The condition (A4
p
) then yields solutions
\mu_{\bullet}\in L^{r}([t_{0},T],L^{p}(\lambda))
. To get an
L^{p}
-valued solution, one ‘usually’ imposes on the reaction map an assumption of Lipschitz type in
L^{p}(\lambda)
. Here it is merely
L^{p}
-boundedness together with a Lipschitz condition in Fortet-Mourier norm. On the other hand, we cannot establish any regularity in time, continuity in particular, that is obtained from an
L^{p}
-Lipschitz condition.

Moreover, there is no clear relationship known (yet) between
\|\mu\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}
and
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
for a signed measure
\mu\in L^{1}\cap L^{p}(\lambda)
, as occurs in computing distances. Thus, a Lipschitz condition on the reaction map in either norm cannot be related.

Appendix A
Bochner measurability of lattice operations on measures

Let
S
be a Polish space and
d
an admissible metric on
S
. It should be noted that the maps
\mu\mapsto\mu^{\pm}
, where
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
are the positive and negative part of
\mu
in the Hahn-Jordan decomposition, are not continuous for the weak topology on measures. However, one has the following result – and a consequence. As far as we are aware, they could not be found in the literature and are of separate interest in (Bochner) integration of measure-valued functions.

Lemma A.1
.

For every
f\in B_{b}(S)
,
\mu\mapsto\langle\mu^{+},f\rangle=\int_{S}f\,d\mu^{+}
is Borel measurable as map from
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
to
\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
E
is a Borel set in
S
, then
\mu\mapsto\mu^{\pm}(E)
and
\mu\mapsto\mu(E)
are Borel measurable maps
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of Lemma 3.2.6, p. 15, in
[
33
]
; it uses the Monotone Class Theorem for functions.
∎

It implies the following result concerning strongly measurable functions in Bochner integration. A function with values in a Banach space is strongly measurable if it is the point-wise limit of a sequence of simple step functions.
Let
(S^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})
be a measurable space.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f:S^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
be strongly measurable. Then
f^{\pm}:S^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}:s^{\prime}\mapsto[f(s^{\prime})]^{\pm}
are strongly measurable.

Proof.

We use Pettis’ Measurability Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
). Since
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
is separable, it suffices to show that for every
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
,
\phi\circ f^{+}
is Borel measurable. Observe that
\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}\simeq\mathrm{BL}(S)
:
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}^{*}
corresponds to
g_{\phi}\in\mathrm{BL}(S)
through
\phi(\mu)=\int_{S}g\,d\mu
(cf.
[
28
]
). Since
g_{\phi}
is measurable, there exist simple step functions

g_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{k}}a^{(k)}_{n}1_{E^{(k)}_{n}}

such that
g_{n}\to g_{\phi}
point-wise while
|g_{n}(x)|\leq|g_{\phi}(x)|
for all
x\in S^{\prime}
(see
[
12
]
). Then for any
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
,

\phi\circ f^{+}(s^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{S}g_{n}d\bigl[f^{+}(s^{\prime})\bigr]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{n}}a^{(k)}_{n}[f(s^{\prime})]^{+}\bigl(E^{(k)}_{n}\bigr).

(45)

Because
s^{\prime}\mapsto f(s^{\prime}):S^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}(S)_{\mathrm{FM}}
and
\mu\mapsto\mu^{+}(E^{(k)}_{n})
are Borel measurable (Lemma
A.1
), every term in the sum in (
45
) is a Borel measurable function. So the point-wise limit
\phi\circ f^{+}
is too.
∎

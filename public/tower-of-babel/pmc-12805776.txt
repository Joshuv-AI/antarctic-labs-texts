Psychometric evaluation of the Chinese version of the biobehavioural pain and movement questionnaire (BioPMovQ) in elderly CMP patients
Authors: Linghui Kong, Peng Jiang, Jing Wang, Huan Qiao, Tao Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAs the population of elderly patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) grows, the relationship between pain and exercise behavior has garnered widespread interest. This study aims to localize the BioPMovQ and examine its reliability and validity among elderly CMP patients.MethodsA total of 526 elderly patients with CMP were recruited from the Orthopedics Department of a tertiary hospital in Shanxi Province. After obtaining permission from the original author, the BioPMovQ was translated into Chinese following the Brislin translation principles. This study explored item-level differences through item analysis; it used the Delphi method to analyze content validity, and used exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to explore and verify the potential factor structure of the scale; it also calculated internal consistency reliability and assessed test-retest reliability to evaluate the scale’s reliability.ResultsA total of 16 items were retained for project analysis. The content validity index (I-CVI) at the item level of BioPMovQ was 0.857～1.000, and the content validity index (S-CVI) at the questionnaire level was 0.937. In EFA, four common factors were extracted, including disability, self-efficacy for physical activity, movement avoidance behaviours, and self-perceived functional ability, which explained 68.870% of the total variance. The CFA results showed that, χ2/df=2.083, GFI=0.913, NFI=0.902, TLI=0.934, CFI=0.946, IFI=0.947, RMSAE=0.064. The Cronbach's alpha coefficient, split half reliability, and test-retest reliability of the Chinese version of BioPMovQ are 0.844, 0.905, and 0.852, respectively.ConclusionsThe Chinese version of BioPMovQ has good reliability and validity, and can be used to measure the relationship between pain and various factors related to exercise behavior in elderly patients with CMP from a biological behavioral perspective.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-025-06836-2.Keywords: Biobehavioural approach, CMP, Factor analysis, Functionality, Healthy aging

Introduction

Chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) is a major cause of disability worldwide. Globally, 20% to 33% of the population suffers from musculoskeletal disorders [1, 2], and CMP ranks second in the consultation rate for chronic pain conditions [3]. Defined as persistent or recurrent pain associated with diseases directly affecting bones, joints, muscles, or related soft tissues, CMP lasts for more than 3 months and encompasses both chronic primary and secondary musculoskeletal pain [4]. Common CMP and related conditions include chronic low back pain, neck pain, osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, and lumbar disc herniation [5, 6]. Despite being one of the most frequent reasons for medical consultation, CMP is often insufficiently recognized and inadequately managed [7, 8].

Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain (CMP) is persistent and prone to recurrence. It not only causes painful and discomforting sensations but also results in limited daily activities, weakness, depression, cognitive impairment, falls, reduced sleep quality, and diminished quality of life in patients. It is a leading cause of disability among the elderly [9, 10] and also places a substantial burden on healthcare systems [7]. The Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) indicates that musculoskeletal disorders and back pain are key factors driving the rise in global disease burden [11]. Additionally, the World Health Organization (WHO) notes that musculoskeletal health threatens healthy aging and can impose significant socioeconomic burdens. CMP is prevalent among older adults in China, and is more common in elderly women [4, 12]. Thus, as China’s population aging advances, the exercise behaviors of elderly CMP patients deserve attention [13].

Patients with chronic pain often modify their activity patterns to minimize pain and/or preserve function during painful episodes. The activity patterns employed by these patients primarily include the avoidance activity pattern, the pacing activity pattern, and the adjustment activity pattern [14]. Among these three patterns, scholars focus more on the avoidance activity pattern. Stbbs et al. [15] found that compared with elderly individuals without CMP, those with CMP tend to avoid activities due to fear of falling, which contributes to sedentary behavior. A cross-sectional study conducted in adults aged 75 years and older showed that individuals with moderate to severe musculoskeletal pain have a higher likelihood of experiencing mobility restrictions [16]. Meanwhile, due to greater fear of pain and falls among elderly people with musculoskeletal pain [17], they may limit activities that exacerbate pain or lead to falls. Therefore, in-depth investigation into the inherent relationship between pain and exercise behavior in elderly CMP patients, and the development of a targeted nursing intervention system, hold significant practical significance and guiding value for formulating scientifically sound and effective activity intervention strategies and optimizing clinical nursing measures.

At present, the pain behavior experienced by elderly CMP patients urgently requires greater attention and effective intervention from all sectors of society, which is crucially significant for improving their health status and enhancing their quality of life. However, before formally rolling out intervention measures, it is a crucial and fundamental step to screen and evaluate elderly CMP patients—thus, selecting appropriate evaluation tools is essential.

However, there is currently a lack of scales specifically designed to assess pain and exercise behavior in elderly CMP patients in China. In 1995, American scholar Anderson developed the Chronic Pain Self-Efficacy Scale (CPSS) based on Bandura’s self-efficacy theory and Loring’s Arthritis Self-Efficacy Scale. This scale comprises three subscales: Pain Management Self-Efficacy Scale, Physical Self-Efficacy Scale, and Symptom Coping Self-Efficacy Scale [18]. Chinese scholars translated and introduced this scale in 2012, but it only focuses on investigating chronic pain patients’ self-efficacy and fails to address other relevant aspects. To address this gap, Roy La Touche [19] developed the BioPMovQ in 2024. Covering four dimensions—disability, physical activity self-efficacy, movement avoidance behavior, and perceived functional ability—it includes 16 items total. Breaking from tradition by adopting a unique biopsychological behavioral perspective, it systematically and comprehensively evaluates the intrinsic links between pain-related and exercise behavior-related factors. This supports more accurate understanding of elderly CMP patients’ conditions, provides a scientific basis for developing personalized intervention plans, and ultimately improves patients’ treatment effectiveness and quality of life.

This study’s core objective is to accurately translate the BioPMovQ Scale from its English version into Chinese, apply it to elderly individuals with chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP), and verify the reliability and validity of the latter. By using this specialized tool for assessing pain and exercise behavior in elderly CMP patients, we aim to advance further research in this field and provide more scientific, targeted guidance for clinical practice.

Methods

Study design and participants

A convenience sampling method was used to select 530 elderly CMP patients from the orthopedic department of a tertiary hospital in Shanxi Province from July 2025 to September 2025 as the research subjects. Inclusion criteria: (1)Those who meet the diagnostic criteria published in the 11th revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11) [20] and have been clinically diagnosed; (2) Age ≥ 60 years old; (3) Voluntarily participate in this study and sign an informed consent form. Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with pain related to malignant tumors; (2) Daily walking requires assistance (excluding the use of crutches or walking aids) or is accompanied by other serious illnesses that affect physical activity; (3) Individuals with severe psychological or mental illnesses, as well as cognitive impairments. According to the general rule of factor analysis [21], the sample size should be 5–10 times the number of items on the scale. In this study, the Chinese version of the BioPMovQ Scale contained a total of 16 items. Taking into account the inclusion criteria and sampling errors, this study required the inclusion of at least 96 elderly patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP). To ensure the stability of the factor structure, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) requires a minimum of 200 samples [22]. Ultimately, a total of 530 elderly patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) were included in this study.

Instruments

General demographic characteristics questionnaire

Designed by researchers through literature review and expert consultation, including gender, age, education level, monthly income, BMI, Chronic comorbidities, degree of pain, duration of pain, etc.

The biobehavioural pain and movement questionnaire (BioPMovQ)

This questionnaire was developed by Professor Roy et al. [19] and is mainly used from a biological perspective to evaluate the relationship between pain and exercise behavior in elderly patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain. The questionnaire includes disability, self-efficacy in physical activity, motor avoidance behavior, and functional ability in self perception. There are a total of 16 items in the four dimensions of self perceived functional ability. Adopting the Likert 5-point rating system, the scores for “strongly disagree”, “somewhat disagree”, “average”, “somewhat agree”, and “strongly agree” range from 1 to 5 points, with a total scale score of 16 to 80 points. The higher the score, the worse the exercise behavior of elderly CMP patients. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the English version of BioPMovQ is 0.65–0.75.

Procedures

Scale translation and cross-cultural adaptation procedure

Authorized by Professor Roy [19], we performed the translation and cross-cultural adaptation of this scale. Specifically, the BioPMovQ Scale was translated into Chinese using the Brislin method [23], with the specific process as follows: Step 1 involved two nursing experts proficient in both Chinese and Western cultures independently translating the scale. Subsequent in-depth discussion and consensus-building led to the consolidation of a forward translation version; Step 2 entailed inviting two independent English instructors from medical colleges to back-translate the Chinese-adapted version into English, yielding two distinct back-translations. The research team then conducted a sentence-by-sentence comparison between these two back-translations and the original English version, flagging content with semantic inconsistencies or cultural connotation deviations. The team further discussed the flagged issues, analyzed the causes of such deviations (e.g., differences in cultural concepts, variations in linguistic expression habits), made revisions, and ultimately finalized the back-translation; Step 3 focused on aligning the scale content with Chinese cultural norms. Four experts—two orthopedic specialists, one psychological expert, and one pain management expert—conducted in-depth discussions, detailed comparisons, and repeated revisions of the original scale, forward translation, and back-translation, ultimately completing the initial draft of the Chinese BioPMovQ. Subsequently, we established a cross-cultural adaptation team comprising 7 experts (4 geriatric nursing specialists and 3 clinicians) to revise and discuss the content and linguistic expression of this Chinese initial draft. The experts conducted careful analysis and discussion, assessing the semantic accuracy and consistency of the translated items, as well as the fluency and completeness of sentences, and provided revision suggestions. Finally, following further discussion and revision by two experts, the specific content of the 16 items in the final Chinese version was confirmed.

Pre investigation

Convenience sampling method was used to select 30 elderly CMP patients for pre testing of the Chinese version of BioPMovQ. Before filling out the questionnaire, explain the purpose and significance of the study to them. After the survey is completed, collect the problems that arise during the questionnaire filling process, and finally have the research team discuss and adjust based on feedback.

Data collection procedure

Prior to the study, two investigators received training on knowledge related to the Chinese version of the BioPMovQ. During the survey, the two investigators distributed questionnaires to participants on-site at the hospital, informing them of the precautions for questionnaire completion and requesting them to complete the questionnaire anonymously, which took approximately 13 min.

Convenience sampling was used to distribute questionnaires to CMP patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. These patients were informed of the study’s purpose, significance, and key notes for questionnaire completion. If participants had questions, the investigators provided explanations. For those unable to complete the questionnaire independently, the investigators filled it out based on the participants’ responses. After questionnaire collection, two investigators reviewed the forms immediately. Completed and standardized questionnaires were retained, while invalid ones (e.g., those with identical responses for all items or contradictory answers) were excluded. A total of 530 questionnaires were distributed, with 526 valid ones collected, resulting in an effective response rate of 99.20%. To assess the scale’s test-retest reliability, 60 elderly CMP patients were randomly selected for a follow-up questionnaire survey two weeks later, achieving an effective response rate of 100.00%.

Data analysis procedure

Data analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 26.0 and Amos 22.0. Count data is described using frequency and percentage (%), while measurement data is described using mean ± standard deviation (x ± s). (1) Project analysis: Calculate the decision value (CR), the correlation between the scores of each item on the scale and the total score. (2) Reliability analysis: Analyze internal consistency reliability, split half reliability, and test-retest reliability. (3) Validity analysis: Calculate content validity and construct validity.

Items analysis

(1) Correlation coefficient method: Evaluate the sensitivity and representativeness of items by calculating the correlation coefficient between each item and the total score of the scale. Items with a correlation coefficient < 0.40 are considered for deletion [24]. (2) Decision value method: Sort the total scores of 526 Chinese versions of BioPMovQ from high to low, with the top 27% of the total score set as the high group and the bottom 27% as the low group [25]. Use a two sample independent t-test to calculate the Critical Ratio (CR) values of each item and compare the differences between the high and low groups on each item. Generally, CR values > 3.00 and P < 0.05 are used as inclusion criteria [26]. (3)Explore the Cronbach’s α value of the scale after deleting each item to assess whether each item of the scale should be retained. When the Cronbach’s α coefficient value of the scale after removing a specific item is greater than the original Cronbach’s α coefficient value of the scale, that item should be deleted.

Validity analysis

We invited 7 experts in the field of elderly care to evaluate each item in the translation scale from the perspective of content importance. Use the Delphi method to calculate the Item Content Validity Index (I-CVI) and Scale Content Validity Index (S-CVI). Use a 4-point Likert scale to evaluate the relevance of each item in the Chinese version of BioPMovQ, ranging from 1 = unrelated to 4 = highly correlated. I-CVI > 0.780 and S-CVI > 0.900 indicate good content validity [27].

The samples were randomly divided into exploratory factor analysis (EFA) group (n = 263) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) group (n = 263). Exploratory factor analysis was conducted using principal component analysis and maximum variance orthogonal rotation method. The Bartlett sphericity test calculated a significant value (P < 0.05) [28], and the Kaiser Meyer Olkin (KMO) value was > 0.60 [29], which is suitable for factor analysis. For the EFA group, the rotation Varimax method was used to extract common factors with eigenvalues > 1. When the loadings of each item on its corresponding common factor are all > 0.40 and the cumulative variance contribution rate is > 50%, it indicates good construct validity of the scale [25]. Using CFA to validate the hypothesis factor model and maximum likelihood method for estimation. When the fitted values are ≥ 0.90, RMSEA < 0.08, The model has a good structure [30].

Reliability analysis

Calculate Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, Guttman split half reliability, and test-retest reliability to validate the reliability of the Chinese version of the scale. Calculate the Cronbach’s alpha coefficients for the scale and each dimension, with a reliability coefficient of 0.70 as the minimum acceptable standard [31]. Divide the scale items into two parts in order of parity, calculate the correlation between the scores of the two parts, and evaluate the split half reliability. Two weeks later, a retest was conducted on 60 elderly CMP patients, measuring their test retest correlation using a Chinese version questionnaire to evaluate their stability and consistency throughout the entire data collection period. Since the Pearson correlation coefficient often exceeds the true reliability, the intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC) is also used to assess the test-retest reliability of the Chinese version of the scale. Among the results, an ICC value > 0.70 indicates that the scale has acceptable reliability [32].

Reliability analysis

The evaluation of internal consistency of the scale is based on the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. The results show that the Cronbach’s alpha coefficients for the total scale and each dimension are 0.844 (95%CI: 0.824–0.863), 0.827, 0.832, 0.799, and 0.880, respectively. The split-half reliability of the total scale is 0.905. Sixty elderly CMP patients were randomly selected for retest 2 weeks later, and the retest reliability was 0.852 (95%CI: 0.753–0.912) and the ICC was 0.838 (95%CI: 0.738–0.898).

Discussion

Chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) significantly impairs the quality of life in older adults and serves as a major contributor to disability. Data from relevant studies indicate that the prevalence of CMP among the elderly reaches 61% [33], which highlights the urgent need for comprehensive assessment and management. Existing research has demonstrated that inadequate physical activity imposes an additional burden on elderly patients with CMP. This not only elevates the risk of disability but also may exacerbate pre - existing conditions and trigger new health issues [34]. Thus, comprehensive and in - depth assessment and management are imperative. In the assessment process, leveraging tools that evaluate the pain - behavior relationship (e.g., pain behavior scales) is crucial for accurately analyzing the association between pain and exercise behavior.

Among participants in this study, there were 280 males and 246 females, with a mean age of 69.89 years. This study strictly adhered to the Brislin translation model [23] to conduct translation, back-translation, cultural adaptation, and pre-investigation of the English version of the BioPMovQ. The localized Chinese version of the BioPMovQ comprises four dimensions—Disability, Physical Activity Self-Efficacy, Motor Avoidance Behavior, and Self-Perceived Functional Ability—with a total of 16 items. The scale items are clearly phrased and easy to comprehend, taking approximately 13 min to complete, which demonstrates good feasibility. Study results show that the scale exhibits good reliability and validity, making it suitable for self-assessment among elderly patients with CMP using the BioPMovQ. For elderly patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP), the Chinese version of the BioPMovQ provides a multidimensional assessment of pain and its profound impact on motor behavior. It enables healthcare providers to grasp patients’ specific conditions, thereby formulating precise intervention goals (e.g., improving exercise confidence, correcting pain-related avoidance patterns, and enhancing functional perception) and ultimately achieving dual improvements in treatment effectiveness and patients’ quality of life.

The results showed that the correlation coefficients between each item and the total score were all greater than 0.40, indicating that each item has good representativeness. The differences in CR for each item were statistically significant (P < 0.001), indicating good discriminability for each item. The Cronbach’s α coefficient in the translated scale did not increase after the items were deleted, indicating strong correlation between items and high internal consistency. All things considered, all items were retained.

The validity test of the scale includes content validity and construct validity. Content validity reflects the appropriateness of measuring content in scale items. The better the content validity, the higher the representativeness of the item [35]. The results of this study showed that the I-CVI of BioPMovQ in elderly CMP patients ranged from 0.857 to 1.000, and the S-CVI was 0.937, indicating that the scale has good content validity. The structural validity reflects the degree of consistency between the actual measurement results of the scale and the theoretical structure [36]. This study used exploratory factor analysis to extract four common factors through principal component analysis and maximum variance orthogonal rotation. After rotation, the loadings of each item on its corresponding common factor were all greater than 0.40, and the cumulative variance contribution rate was 68.87%. This indicates that the Chinese version of the BioPMovQ has good structural validity and is consistent with the original scale structure. The results of confirmatory factor analysis in this study showed that chi square/df ≤ 3.00, RMSEA < 0.08, and other relative fitting indicators > 0.90, indicating that the model has a good fit and all fitting indicators meet the standard, suggesting that the sample data is consistent with the structure of the theoretical model.

Reliability refers to the consistency or accuracy of the measurement results of a scale, reflecting its stability, intrinsic consistency, and equivalence [37]. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the Chinese version of BioPMovQ in this study is 0.884, which is higher than the results of the Spanish version. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of each dimension range from 0.799 to 0.880, indicating good intrinsic correlation and homogeneity among the items of the scale. The split-half reliability of the total scale is 0.905, further indicating that the scale has good internal consistency. The retest correlation coefficient measured 2 weeks later was 0.852, indicating good stability of the scale. It can be seen that the Chinese version of BioPMovQ has good reliability.

Limitations

This study also has certain limitations and needs further expansion. Firstly, due to the self-reported nature of this study, bias is inevitable. Secondly, due to regional limitations, it may affect the representativeness and universality of the results. Therefore, in the future, multi center research should be conducted for verification.

Conclusions

This study conducted cultural adaptation on the English version of BioPMovQ and found through factor analysis that the Chinese version of BioPMovQ has ideal reliability and validity. In the context of social aging and the Healthy China strategy, this study provides an effective measurement tool to improve the exercise behavior level of elderly CMP patients, and lays the foundation for related research on health promotion of the elderly CMP patient population.

Complete mitochondrial genome of freshwater goby Rhinogobius shennongensis (Perciformes, Gobiidae): genome characterization and phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Xiao Jiang Chen, Lin Song, Xing Peng Han, Quan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, we present the complete mitochondrial genome of Rhinogobius shennongensis, sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq platform. The result is a circular mitogenome of 16,500 base pairs (bp) with a pronounced A + T nucleotide bias. Phylogenetic analysis was conducted using both Bayesian inference (BI) and maximum-likelihood (ML) methods, utilizing the complete mitochondrial genomes of R. shennongensis and 19 additional Rhinogobius species. The analysis indicated a close mitochondrial relationship between R. maculagenys and R. shennongensis. This research not only enriches genetic resources but also supports phylogenetic studies within the genus Rhinogobius. To further clarify evolutionary dynamics, additional mitochondrial genome sequencing of other Rhinogobius species is essential.

Introduction

The genus Rhinogobius (Gill 1859) is widely distributed across the Western Pacific and East Asia, encompassing 135 documented species as of June 2024 according to Fishbase. Rhinogobius species exhibit significant adaptability to varying salinity and temperature conditions, making them valuable models for studying the molecular mechanisms that allow bony fishes to respond to environmental changes (Hu et al. 2022). In southern and southeastern mainland China, most Rhinogobius species are non-diadromous and confined to landlocked habitats, often characterized by a high vertebral count of 27–29 (Huang et al. 2016). Additionally, Rhinogobius offers a unique opportunity to investigate the molecular basis of morphological variations, including color patterns.

Despite their ecological importance and species richness, only 23 out of the 135 Rhinogobius species have complete mitochondrial genome sequences. The phylogenetic relationships within the genus Rhinogobius remain poorly understood, hindered by limited local taxonomic resources and a predominant focus on species phylogeny within specific geographic regions.

Rhinogobius shennongensis (Yang and Xie 1983) is an endemic species of China, inhabiting rivers and streams in central and southeastern regions. It is a warm-water, small-sized benthic fish (50–70 mm). This species (male) is characterized by dorsal fin VI, I-9; anal fin I-8; 16 pectoral fins; abdominal fin I-5; caudal fin formula 3 + 18 + 3; 31–33 longitudinal scales; 8–9 transverse scales; dorsal fin anterior scales 5–6; and 12 gill rakes. Its body features comb-like scales along the sides, while round scales adorn the midline of the back anterior to the dorsal fin origin and the midline of the belly. The head, cheeks, and chest lack scales (Figure 1).

R. shennongensis (male) from the Lianshui River in Loudi City, Hunan Province, China (photograph by Song Lin, 20 August 2021).

This study presents the first comprehensive analysis of the mitochondrial genome of R. shennongensis. It aims to clarify phylogenetic relationships within the genus Rhinogobius, thereby enhancing our understanding of species phylogeny and contributing to genetic conservation efforts for R. shennongensis.

Materials and methods

Materials

In August 2021, a specimen was gathered from the Lianshui River within the Loudi City, Hunan Province (27.752429N, 111.856040E). The specimen’s morphological characteristics were measured following the protocol established by Yang and Xie (1983). This collection was conducted in accordance with the Aquatic Wildlife Protection Ordinance of the People’s Republic of China. The experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Experiments of Jiangsu Agri-animal Husbandry Vocational College (approval number: NSF2021ZR14). The specimen was initially preserved in 75% ethanol before being transferred to 95% ethanol for long-term conservation at the Fish Specimen Collections of the College of Aquatic Science and Technology. The voucher ID for this specimen is ASTIH-21b1108d22, and for further information, please contact Xiao Jiang Chen at cq_cxj@126.com.

Methods

Genomic DNA was extracted from muscle tissue using the Tguide Cell/Tissue Genomic DNA Extraction Kit (OSR-M401; Tiangen, Beijing, China). Sample concentration, purity, and integrity were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). Subsequently, a DNA library containing sheared mitochondrial DNA was prepared with the MGIEasy DNA Library Prep Kit (MGI Technology, Shenzhen, China). Purification and size selection of the library were performed with Agencourt SPRIselect (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA), and the library quality was assessed with an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA). Sequencing was performed on the Illumina HiSeq 4000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA) with 150 bp paired-end reads.

To ensure data quality, we employed FastQC for the initial assessment of sequencing reads, followed by removal of low-quality reads and adapter sequences using Trimmomatic v0.40 (Bolger et al. 2014). Clean reads were aligned to the reference mitochondrial genome of Rhinogobius brunneus (KT601096) using the Burrows–Wheeler Aligner (BWA) v0.7.17. Aligned mitochondrial reads were extracted using Samtools v1.9 (Li and Durbin 2009) and assembled with MetaSPAdes v3.13.0 (Nurk et al. 2017). Assembly quality was evaluated using the Q30 ratio (error rate <0.1%), N50 value (median contig length), BUSCO for completeness against core genes, duplicate sequence analysis for accuracy, and annotation comparison with closely related species. The preliminary assembly results indicated a maximum depth coverage of 16,627×, minimum depth coverage of 500×, and an average depth coverage of 764.85×. Complete mitochondrial genome annotation was performed using the MitoFish webserver (Iwasaki et al. 2013), and the mitogenome map was generated using Proksee (Grant and Stothard 2008).

Twenty complete mitochondrial sequences from the genus Rhinogobius, with a minimum of 87% similarity, were screened from the NCBI database using BLAST. These sequences were used to construct phylogenetic trees, outgroup with Luciogobius elongatus (MH682217). The alignment of complete mitochondrial genome sequences was performed using the ClustalW algorithm within MEGA X software (Kumar et al. 2018). Evolutionary relationships were assessed using the neighbor-joining model with the p-distance method. The best-fit model (GTR + I + G) was selected using jModelTest v2.1.10 (Darriba et al. 2012) based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC), considering differences in RNA genes, coding genes, and codon usage. Additionally, a maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA X, whereas a Bayesian inference (BI) tree was generated using MrBayes v3.2.6 (Ronquist et al. 2012).

Results

Mitogenome organization

The final genomic sequence of R. shennongensis was determined to be 16,500 bp (OM617723), comprising 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, two ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes, and a control region (Figure 2), typical of all known goby genomes (Huang et al. 2016; Tan et al. 2020; Maeda et al. 2021; Hu et al. 2022). Of all 37 genes, most genes are encoded by the heavy strand (H-strand), except for the ND6 gene and eight tRNA genes (tRNA-Ser (UGA), tRNA-Tyr, tRNA-Cys, tRNA-Asn, tRNA-Ala, tRNA-Gln, tRNA-Pro, and tRNA-Glu). The overall nucleotide composition is 27.5% A, 25.3% T (U), 30.3% C, and 16.9% G, exhibiting a higher A + T content of 52.8%, which is a typical pattern observed in teleost fishes. Among the 13 PCGs, only COX1 initiates with the codon GTG, while the remaining 12 PCGs begin with the canonical ATG codon. Furthermore, two complete stop codons (TAA, TAG) and two incomplete stop codons (TA, T) were observed (Table 1). The control region, which spans 479 bp, is located between the tRNA-Phe and tRNA-Pro genes (Figure 2). The lengths of the 13 conserved PCGs range from 165 bp (ATP8) to 1839 bp (ND5). The two rRNA genes consist of a 12S rRNA (949 bp) and a 16S rRNA (1645 bp).

Phylogenetic analysis

Based on mitochondrial genomes, the phylogenetic tree topologies reconstructed using BI and ML methods show overall similarity in their reconstructed topologies. However, there is a notable difference in the position of R. cliffordpopei between the two methods, while the positions of the other species remain largely consistent. According to posterior probability analysis, the phylogenetic tree constructed using the Bayesian method exhibits posterior probabilities of nearly 1 at all nodes, indicating that this method is more reliable. R. cliffordpopei clusters with R. maculagenys, R. shennongensis, R. lentiginis, R. niger, and R. wuyiensis (Figure 3). Our findings suggest that R. maculagenys exhibits the closest mitochondrial genome relationship with R. shennongensis. Genetic distances between R. shennongensis and the other 19 Rhinogobius species ranged from 0.05421 to 0.13011.

Discussion and conclusions

The complete mitochondrial genome of R. shennongensis has been sequenced, revealing a length of 16,500 bp, with a notable A + T nucleotides bias, arranged similarly to other species within the subfamily Gobionellinae (Song et al. 2022, 2023). The overall nucleotide composition is 27.5% A, 25.3% T (U), 30.3% C, and 16.9% G. The genetic distance between R. shennongensis and 19 other Rhinogobius species ranges from 0.05421 to 0.13011. Comparative analysis of sequences deposited in GenBank revealed that the control region in the Rhinogobius genus measures 843 bp in R. rubromaculatus (KU674802) and 475 bp in R. wuyanlingensis (OM961051) (Song et al. 2022). In contrast, the control region of R. shennongensis is 480 bp long. Notably, the composition and arrangement of the control region exhibit considerable variation within this genus.

Phylogenetic analyses using both BI and ML methods reveal that R. shennongensis is closely related to R. maculagenys based on the complete mitochondrial genomes, consistent with the findings reported by Hu et al. (2023). The phylogenetic tree strongly supported the monophyly of the Rhinogobius genus, aligning with the results of previous studies based on 13 concatenated mitochondrial PCGs (Tan et al. 2020; Song et al. 2023). A recent survey indicates that approximately 30 species from the genus Rhinogobius are traded in the Chinese market. However, R. shennongensis is notably absent from this list, likely due to its highly restricted population. It is classified as a rare species and listed as endangered in the Chinese Species Red List (2004) (Liu et al. 2021). The mitochondrial genome data acquired from R. shennongensis are expected to play a significant role in advancing genetic research and establishing a genetic basis for conservation efforts.

A Proof-Theoretic View of Basic Intuitionistic Conditional Logic (Extended Version)
Tiziano Dalmonte, Marianna Girlando

Abstract
Intuitionistic conditional logic, studied by Weiss, Ciardelli and Liu, and Olkhovikov, aims at providing a constructive analysis of conditional reasoning. In this framework, the would and the might conditional operators are no longer interdefinable. The intuitionistic conditional logics considered in the literature are defined by setting Chellas’ conditional logic 𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{CK} , whose semantics is defined using selection functions, within the constructive and intuitionistic framework introduced for intuitionistic modal logics. This operation gives rise to a constructive and an intuitionistic variant of (might-free-) 𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{CK} , which we call 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍𝖢𝖪 □ ​ → \mathsf{ConstCK}^{\mathbin{{\Box}\kern-1.5pt\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}} and 𝖨𝗇𝗍𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{IntCK} respectively. Building on the proof systems defined for 𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{CK} and for intuitionistic modal logics, in this paper we introduce a nested calculus for 𝖨𝗇𝗍𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{IntCK} and a sequent calculus for 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍𝖢𝖪 □ ​ → \mathsf{ConstCK}^{\mathbin{{\Box}\kern-1.5pt\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}} . Based on the sequent calculus, we define 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{ConstCK} , a conservative extension of Weiss’ logic 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍𝖢𝖪 □ ​ → \mathsf{ConstCK}^{\mathbin{{\Box}\kern-1.5pt\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}} with the might operator. We introduce a class of models and an axiomatization for 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{ConstCK} , and extend these result to several extensions of 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{ConstCK} .

For convenience, we employ

[

Polarities indicate the position of the formulas within two-sided sequents: input formulas

Our nested sequents are ‘single-succedent’, meaning that we allow

As it is usual for nested sequents, we enrich the structure of Gentzen-style sequent with an additional structural connective
[\,]

6
Conclusions

We have investigated the proof theory of intuitionistic conditional logics, by defining a nested sequent calculus for
\mathsf{IntCK}
and a sequent calculus for
\mathsf{ConstCK}^{\mathbin{{\Box}\kern-1.5pt\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}
. The calculi are obtained by imposing a single-succedent restriction to a nested and a sequent calculus for classical
\mathsf{CK}
respectively. Then, inspired by the sequent calculus rules, we have defined logic
\mathsf{ConstCK}
, a natural extension of
\mathsf{ConstCK}^{\mathbin{{\Box}\kern-1.5pt\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}
with the
\mathbin{{\Diamond}\kern-1.5pt\raise 0.5pt\hbox{$\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}
modality. We have introduced a class of models for the logic, and showed its soundness and completeness with respect to its axiomatization. Finally, we have defined extensions of
\mathsf{ConstCK}
with
identity
,
conditional modus ponens
and
conditional excluded middle
, and investigated their proof theory.

In future work, we would like to employ our proof systems to determine decision procedures for intuitionistic conditional logics, thus giving the first complexity bounds for these logics. Moreover, we are interested in exploring extensions of
\mathsf{ConstCK}
and
\mathsf{IntCK}
with further axioms and frame conditions coming from classical conditional logics. As a first candidate, we plan to cover
\mathsf{ConstCK}
with
cautious monotonicity
, taking inspiration from the classical rules proposed in
[
17
]
.
Regarding
\mathsf{IntCK}
, we conjecture that the nested sequent calculi defined in
[
2
,
3
]
for extensions of classical
\mathsf{CK}
can be adapted to our nested sequent framework. The rule for
conditional excluded middle
poses a problem though, as it does not seem to be derivable in the single-succedent setting of
\mathsf{N}.\mathsf{IntCK}
. Switching to a multi-succedent nested calculus as the one in
[
13
]
might be necessary to cover this case.
Finally, we are interested in investigating the semantics and the proof theory of stronger conditional logics, such as preferential logics and Lewis’ counterfactuals, in a constructive setting.

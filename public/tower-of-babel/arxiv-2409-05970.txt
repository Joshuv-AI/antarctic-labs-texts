Nonholonomic momentum map reduction and a Chaplygin-type foliation
Paula Balseiro, Danilo Machado Tereza

Abstract
This paper presents a set-up for momentum map reduction of nonholonomic systems with symmetries, extending previous constructions in [ 3 , 25 ] , based on the existence of certain conserved quantities and making essential use of the nonholonomic momentum bundle map of [ 10 ] . We show that the reductions of the momentum level sets carry an almost symplectic form codifying the reduced dynamics. These reduced manifolds are the leaves of the foliation associated with an almost Poisson bracket obtained by a (dynamically compatible) gauge transformation of the nonholonomic bracket. We show that each leaf is a Chaplygin-type leaf , in the sense that it is isomorphic to a cotangent bundle with the canonical symplectic form plus a “magnetic term”.

Introduction

In this paper we study a momentum map reduction for nonholonomic systems with symmetries. Such systems are described in terms of almost Poisson structures, so many of the usual properties of Hamiltonian actions do not hold in this setting. To overcome the difficulties posed by this fact, upon the assumption of existence of special conserved quantities we consider modifications of the nonholonomic brackets by suitable
gauge transformations by 2-forms
which allow us to effectively use the so-called “nonholonomic momentum map”, originally defined in
[
10
]
, in the reduction procedure. The resulting reduced spaces are almost symplectic manifolds of “Chaplygin-type”, in the sense that they can be identified with cotangent bundles equipped with the canonical symplectic form plus a (non-closed) magnetic term, and they can be seen as leaves of a foliation of an almost Poisson structure that describes the reduced nonholonomic dynamics. The results of the present paper allow us to understand the role of the nonholonomic momentum map in relating nonholonomic brackets to symmetries.

The usual setting for Hamiltonian reduction of Poisson manifolds
(see e.g.
[
43
]
) is that of a
G
-invariant Poisson manifold
(P,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\mbox{\tiny{$P$}}}})
with a momentum map

[
44
]
, i.e.,

-equivariant and, for all
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\{\cdot,J_{\eta}\}_{\mbox{\tiny{$P$}}}=\eta_{\mbox{\tiny{$P$}}},

(1)

where
\eta_{\mbox{\tiny{$P$}}}
is the infinitesimal generator of the action at
\eta
. Then, with usual regularity conditions,
the quotient
J^{-1}(\mu)/G_{\mu}
is a manifold that inherits a unique Poisson structure
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mu}
defined, for

, by

\{f,h\}_{{\mu}}\circ\rho_{\mu}=\{F,H\}_{{\mbox{\tiny{$P$}}}}\circ\iota_{\mu},

(2)

where

and

(with differentials vanishing on tangent spaces to the

\rho_{\mu}:J^{-1}(\mu)\rightarrow J^{-1}(\mu)/G_{\mu}
the inclusion map and the orbit projection, respectively.

Note that condition (

(i)

The vertical distribution (tangent to the
G

(ii)

For a

(iii)

The infinitesimal generators of the

In the nonholonomic setting these three conditions are not necessarily satisfied and we have to understand how infinitesimal generators, conserved quantities and momentum map interact.

Recall that a nonholonomic system on a manifold
Q
is a mechanical system with constraints in the velocities, that is, the permitted velocities define a (nonintegrable) subbundle
D\subset TQ

[
10
,
11
,
25
,
27
]
. Classical examples are mechanical systems with rolling constraints such as a disk rolling on a plane or, more generally, solids –such as spheres, ellipsoids, snakeboards– rolling without sliding on different smooth surfaces, see e.g.,
[
11

14
]
.
From a geometric perspective, a nonholonomic system is determined by a triple
(\mathcal{M},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})
where
(\mathcal{M},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}})

a submanifold of

induced by

the corresponding Hamiltonian function (of mechanical type). The almost Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
is called the

[
37
,
42
,
49
]
and it has a non-integrable characteristic distribution denoted by
\mathcal{C}
. The nonholonomic dynamics is given by the integral curves of the nonholonomic vector field
X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
on
\mathcal{M}
given by
X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}=\{\cdot,H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}\}_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
.

We consider nonholonomic systems admitting symmetries given by the (free and proper) action of a Lie group
G
on
Q
. The lifted action to
T^{*}Q
leaves
\mathcal{M}
and the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
invariant. The usual scenario for
G
-invariant nonholonomic system is to consider the
dimension assumption

[
10
]
, that is, denoting by
\mathcal{V}
the vertical distribution on
\mathcal{M}
, we assume that

T\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{C}+\mathcal{V}.

Note that the dimension assumption goes in the opposite direction of condition
(i)
for Hamiltonian reduction above, since the vertical spaces complement the characteristic distribution.
Following
[
10
]
we define the (usually not integrable) distribution
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{V}
and the bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
with fibers given by
(\mathfrak{g}_{S})|_{q}:=\{\xi_{q}\in\mathfrak{g}:(\xi_{q})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)\in\mathcal{S}_{m}\ \mbox{for}\ \tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)=q\}

is the restriction of the canonical momentum map to
\mathcal{M}
.

The novelty of the present paper is to address the issue of reduction of
G
-invariant nonholonomic system making use of the nonholonomic momentum bundle map.

In contrast with properties
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
for Poisson reduction, we have the following.

(i^{\prime})

Property
(i)
is replaced by the fact that only
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}
belongs to the characteristic distribution of
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
.

(ii^{\prime})

Instead of considering momentum map components

corresponding to Lie algebra elements

as in
(ii)
, one should consider functions
J_{\xi}=\langle J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},\xi\rangle
, for
\xi
a section of the bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}
. A key difference is that such functions are not necessarily first integrals of the nonholonomic system (in spite of the
G
-invariance of

). The existence of special sections
\xi

is a first integral is in general an open problem, and first integrals of this form are called

[
7
,
29
,

(iii^{\prime})

The fact that there is a section

does not necessarily mean that the infinitesimal generator
\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
is a Hamiltonian vector field of

. Following
[
6
,
34
]
, and assuming the existence of

horizontal gauge momenta, we may consider a new bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
through a
(dynamical) gauge transformation
by a 2-form

\{\cdot,J_{\xi}\}_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}=\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
, and the new bracket still codifies the nonholonomic dynamics in the sense that
\{\cdot,H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}\}_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}=X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
.

Throughout this work, as in
[
6
]
,
we assume the existence of exactly
k:=\textup{rank}(\mathcal{S})

G
-invariant and functionally independent horizontal gauge momenta
(see
(ii^{\prime})
). We stress that this assumption is satisfied in many examples of interest.
Once we fix
Ad
-invariant sections
\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{S}
generating the horizontal gauge momenta, we take
the dual
Ad^{*}
-invariant frame
\{\mu^{1},\dots,\mu^{k}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{S}
and consider momentum levels with respect to
\mu=c_{i}\mu^{i}
, for
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
(rather than just elements in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
).
Theorem
3.17
states that the submanifold
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)
is
G
-invariant and
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G
admits an almost Poisson bracket

analogous to (
2
), that is, for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G)
,

\{f,h\}_{{\mu}}\circ\rho_{\mu}=\{F,H\}_{{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}}\circ\iota_{\mu},

where
F,H
are appropriate extensions of
\rho_{\mu}^{*}f
and
\rho^{*}_{\mu}h
.
In this case, we show that the almost Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mu}
is nondegenerate, so it is equivalent to an almost symplectic 2-form
\omega_{\mu}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
. It follows that the reduced spaces
(J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G,\omega_{\mu}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}})
give rise to an almost symplectic foliation on
\mathcal{M}/G
, whose leaves integrate the characteristic distribution of the reduced bracket on
\mathcal{M}/G
induced by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
(this gives an alternative proof to the fact that this bracket is twisted Poisson, which was the main result in
[
6
]
).

We verify that each reduced space
(J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G,\omega_{\mu}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}})
is diffeomorphic to
(T^{\ast}(Q/G),\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{can}}}+\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mu})
, where
\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mu}
is a semi-basic 2-form (with respect to the bundle
T^{*}(Q/G)\to Q/G
) that can be thought of as a “
magnetic term
”. This result, found Theorem
4.8
, bears resemblance with the one in
[
46

for symplectic manifolds but in our case the magnetic term

is not necessarily closed and it depends exclusively on the 2-form
B

Therefore, we conclude that a nonholonomic system with

(
G
-invariant) horizontal gauge momenta can be reduced to a

foliation, i.e., a foliation whose leaves look like Chaplygin systems, even if the original system is not Chaplygin.

We remark that we express our reduction procedure in terms of (almost) Dirac structures

26
]
, even though the results can be exposed without mentioning them. The issue is that when we want to “pull back” the almost Poisson bracket

which can be then “pushed forward” to the 2-form

on

\scriptstyle{\ \ \iota_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{\mu}}
\textstyle{(\mathcal{M},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}},H_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}})}
\textstyle{(J^{-1}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\mu)/G,\omega_{\mu}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}},H_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mu})}

We also initiate the study of systems with conserved quantities that are not horizontal gauge momenta. In Theorem
5.5
, we observe that the common 0-level set of the conserved quantities is also identified with a Chaplygin system. As a direct application of this result, we recover, from an intrinsic geometric view point, that the 0-level set of the conserved quantity of the mechanical system described by a (inhomogeneus) ball rolling on a fixed sphere
[
17
,
14
,
38
]
is diffeomorphic to a cotangent bundle endowed with the canonical form plus a “magnetic” semi-basic 2-form.

Comparing with previous versions of nonholonomic reduction in the literature, we note the following:

•

The systems studied here are not of Chaplygin-type, not even decomposed into a Chaplygin system with an extra symmetry, as the ones treated in
[
3
]
. In fact, with our more general framework we study the homogeneous ball rolling on a surface of revolution (see e.g.
[
6
,
16
]
), which cannot be handled in
[
3
]
.

•

In the nonholonomic momentum map reduction in
[
25
]
, the relation between infinitesimal generators and Hamiltonian vector fields is not taken into account, and no (almost) symplectic/Poisson manifold are present in the reduced spaces.

2
we present the basic formalism for Dirac structures and the concept of a
momentum bundle map
with a suitable reduction. In Section
3
we study nonholonomic systems and the nonholonomic momentum bundle map reduction. The identification with a Chaplygin-type foliation is in Section
4
. Section
5
contains the study of the 0-level set of first integrals that are
not
horizontal gauge momenta, while Section
6
has the examples.

Dirac structures with symmetries

In this section, we present the framework of Dirac structures
[
26
]
and
momentum bundle maps
in order to proceed with a momentum map reduction. We will recover, in particular, the case studied in
[
43
]
.

The starting geometric structure, when studying nonholonomic systems is an almost Poisson bracket. However in the reduction process using a momentum map we have to pass through (almost) Dirac structures. This is our main motivation to study such general structures and the interaction with momentum bundle maps. In this section we follow the framework and notation of
[
19
,
20
,

]

2.1
Dirac structures

Consider a smooth manifold
P
and the bundle
\mathbb{T}P:=TP\oplus T^{\ast}P
on
P
with two additional structures

(i)

a nondegenerate, symmetric fibrewise bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{T}P\times\mathbb{T}P\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given, at each
x\in P
,
X,Y\in T_{x}P
and
\alpha,\beta\in T_{x}^{\ast}P

\langle(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\rangle=\beta(X)+\alpha(Y);

(ii)

a bracket
[\![\cdot,\cdot]\!]:\Gamma(\mathbb{T}P)\times\Gamma(\mathbb{T}P)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathbb{T}P)
called the
Courant bracket

[
26
]
, defined by

[\![(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)]\!]=([X,Y],\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-{\bf i}_{Y}d\alpha).

An
almost Dirac structure
on
P
is a maximal isotropic subbundle
L
of
\mathbb{T}P
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
; if in addition
L
is involutive with respect to the Courant bracket (i.e.,
[\![\Gamma(L),\Gamma(L)]\!]\subset\Gamma(L)
),
L
is referred as a
Dirac structure
on
P
,
[
26
]
. Let us equip the bundle
\mathbb{T}P
with their natural projections

pr_{T}:\mathbb{T}P\rightarrow TP\quad\textup{and}\quad pr_{T^{\ast}}:\mathbb{T}P\rightarrow T^{*}P.

An almost Dirac structure
L
is
regular
if the so-called
characteristic distribution

pr_{T}(L)\subset TP
on
P
is regular. If
L
is Dirac, then the distribution
pr_{T}(L)
is integrable and it defines a presymplectic foliation.
The
null distribution
of
L
is the distribution
K_{L}
on
P
defined by

K_{L}:=pr_{T}((TP\oplus\{0\})\cap L)\subset TP.

Remark 2.1
.

Given a
2
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(P)
and a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}TP)
with their respective bundle maps
\omega^{\flat}:TP\rightarrow T^{\ast}P
such that
\omega^{\flat}(X)={\bf i}_{X}\omega
and
\pi^{\sharp}:T^{\ast}P\rightarrow TP
so that
\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)={\bf i}_{\alpha}\pi
, then
L_{\omega}:=\textup{graph}(\omega^{\flat})
and
L_{\pi}:=\textup{graph}(\pi^{\sharp})
are almost Dirac structures, which become Dirac when
\omega
is presymplectic and
\pi
Poisson.
Conversely, if
L
is an almost Dirac structure so that
L\cap(\{0\}\oplus T^{\ast}P)=\{0\}
, then
L
is the graph of a 2-form and
K_{L}=\textup{Ker}(\omega)
. Respectively, if
K_{L}=\{0\}
, then
L
is the graph of a bivector field. For details see e.g.,
[
19
]
.

\diamond

Remark 2.2
.

We recall here that there is a one-to-one correspondence between almost Poisson brackets
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on
C^{\infty}(P)
and bivector fields
\pi
on
P
given, at each

, by
\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)
. The Jacobi identity of

is equivalent to ask that

, where

is the Schouten bracket, see e.g.
[

]

\diamond

Following
[

,

]
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between regular almost Dirac structures

, where

(3)

We may also associate to

(but, in this case,

If the 2-section

2.2
Backward and Forward images of Dirac structures

In this section we formalize the notions of

and

of almost Dirac structures, having in mind that these structures simultaneously encode almost Poisson structures and 2-forms; here we follow
[
19
]

In the case of nonholonomic systems we are particular interested in pulling back (almost) Poisson brackets.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
smooth manifolds and
\varphi:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
be a smooth map.
First recall that the pull back of the bundle
\tau:TP_{2}\to P_{2}

Let us denote by

Following

]

by

, is associated to the bivector field
\pi_{c}=\partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{p_{y}}
(that, in this case, is nondegenerate).

3
Nonholonomic systems

In this section, we study the reduction of nonholonomic systems via the nonholonomic momentum bundle map, which was originally defined in
[
10
]
. First we will recall the geometry underlying nonholonomic systems given by an almost Poisson bracket, following
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
25
,
28
,
37
]
. If the system is invariant by the action of a Lie group, the usual scenario for nonholonomic systems is that the vertical distribution does not necessary belong to the characteristic distribution of such bracket. In this sense, the distribution
\mathcal{S}
defined in (
5
) will play a fundamental role.

A nonholonomic mechanical system on a smooth manifold
Q
is a classical mechanical system defined by a Lagrangian
L:TQ\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a (constant rank) non-integrable distribution
D
on
Q
representing the permitted velocities. In this article, we consider Lagrangians of
mechanical-type
, that is,
L=\frac{1}{2}\kappa-\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$TQ$}}}^{*}U
, where
\kappa
is the
kinetic energy metric
and
U:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the potential energy with
\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$TQ$}}}:TQ\to Q
the canonical projection, see
[
11
]
. Examples of nonholonomic systems are mainly mechanical systems with rolling constraints, for example solids – as disks, spheres, ellipsoids, etc – rolling without sliding on different smooth surfaces.

3.1
The nonholonomic bracket

The Legendre transformation
Leg=\kappa^{\flat}:TQ\rightarrow T^{\ast}Q
(which, in this case, is a global diffeomorphism linear on the fibers) and the constraint distribution
D
define the submanifold
\mathcal{M}:=Leg(D)\subset T^{\ast}Q
called the
constraint manifold
. Let us denote by
\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}:=\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$T^{*}Q$}}}|_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}:\mathcal{M}\to Q
the restriction to
\mathcal{M}
of the canonical projection
\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$T^{*}Q$}}}:T^{*}Q\to Q
. Then the distribution
D
induces a (constant rank) non-integrable distribution
\mathcal{C}
on
\mathcal{M}
, given at each
m\in\mathcal{M}
, by

\mathcal{C}_{m}:=\left\{v_{m}\in T_{m}\mathcal{M}\,:\,T\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(v_{m})\in D_{q},q={\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)}\right\}\subset T_{m}\mathcal{M}.

For
\iota_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}:\mathcal{M}\to T^{*}Q
the natural inclusion, we denote by

\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}:=\iota_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}^{*}\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}
the pull back of the canonical symplectic 2-form
\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}
to
\mathcal{M}
and
H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}:=\iota_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}^{*}H
the restriction of the Hamiltonian function
H
to
\mathcal{M}
.
Due to the nondegenerancy of
\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}:=\left.\Omega_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}\right|_{\mathcal{C}}
(see
[
8
]
), the
nonholonomic bracket

\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
on
\mathcal{M}

[
37
,
42
,
49
]
is defined, at each
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, by

\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{\sharp}(df)=-X_{f}\qquad\mbox{if and only if}\qquad{\bf i}_{X_{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}=\left.df\right|_{\mathcal{C}}.

(12)

The
characteristic distribution
of
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
is given by the nonintegrable distribution
\mathcal{C}
.
The nonholonomic dynamics is described by the integral curves of the
nonholonomic vector field

X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
on
\mathcal{M}
defined by

\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{\sharp}(dH_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})=-X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},

and that is why we say that the nonholonomic system is determined by the triple
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}},H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})
. In the context of the previous section, we may see a nonholonomic system defined by the triple
(\mathcal{M},L_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})
where
L_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}=\textup{graph}(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{\sharp})
is an almost Dirac structure (with associated pair given by
(\mathcal{C},\Omega_{\mathcal{C}})
, see (
3
)) and the dynamics is described by the pair
(-X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},dH_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})\in L_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
.

3.2
Symmetries and the nonholonomic momentum bundle map

The action of a Lie group
G
on the configuration space
Q
defines a
G
-symmetry
for the nonholonomic system if it is free and proper and the tangent lift leaves the Lagrangian
L
and the constraint distribution
D
invariant (or equivalently if the cotangent lift leaves the Hamiltonian
H
and the constraint manifold
\mathcal{M}
invariant). Therefore, there is a free and proper action
\psi:G\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
defined on the manifold
\mathcal{M}
and by construction the nonholonomic bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
is
G
-invariant as well.

Reduction by symmetries

Since the action on
\mathcal{M}
is free and proper, the orbit projection
\rho:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}/G
defines a
G
-principal bundle.
The nonholonomic vector field
X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
is
G
-invariant (i.e.,
g\in G,T\psi_{g}(X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(m))=X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\psi_{g}(m))
) and hence it descends to the quotient manifold
\mathcal{M}/G
defining the
reduced nonholonomic vector field
given by
X_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}:=T\rho(X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}})
. Moreover, the nonholonomic bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
also descends to a reduced bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}
on
\mathcal{M}/G
defined, for each
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}/G)
, as
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\sharp}(df)=T\rho(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{\sharp}(d\rho^{*}f))
and the reduced nonholonomic vector field is also defined by

\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\sharp}(dH_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}})=-X_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}},

(13)

where
H_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}/G)
is the reduced Hamiltonian,
\rho^{*}H_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}=H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
.

Consider now the
G
-action on
Q
and denote by
V
the
vertical distribution
on
Q
given at each
q\in Q
by
V_{q}:=T_{q}(Orb_{G}(q))
. Following
[
10
]
we say that the nonholonomic system defined by the distribution
D
satisfies the
dimension assumption
if

T_{q}Q=D_{q}+V_{q},\quad\mbox{for all}\ q\in Q.

The distribution
S
on
Q
is given, at each
q\in Q
, by
S_{q}:=D_{q}\cap V_{q}
and observe that
V
and
S
have constant rank due to the freeness of the action and the dimension assumption.

Equivalently, the dimension assumption can be written on
\mathcal{M}
(as in the Introduction), that is, for each
m\in\mathcal{M}
,
T_{m}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{C}_{m}+\mathcal{V}_{m}
where
\mathcal{V}
is the vertical distribution of the
G
-action on
\mathcal{M}
. Analogously, for each
m\in\mathcal{M}
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{m}:=\mathcal{C}_{m}\cap\mathcal{V}_{m}
and note that
\textup{rank}(S)=\textup{rank}(\mathcal{S})
.

Observe that the distribution
\mathcal{S}
coincides with the one defined in (
5
). In this case,
\mathcal{S}
satisfies automatically condition (A2), since the null distribution
K_{L_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}}=\{0\}
, however condition (A1) is more subtle.

Conserved quantities induced by symmetries

Following
[
10
]
we consider the subbundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\rightarrow Q
of the trivial bundle
Q\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow Q
with fiber, at
q\in Q
, given by
\mathfrak{g}_{{S}}|_{q}:=\left\{(q,\xi)\in Q\times\mathfrak{g}\ :\ \xi_{{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}}(q)\in{S}_{q}\right\}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{M}
be the bundle defined in (
6
), i.e., for
m\in\mathcal{M}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{S}}|_{m}=\{\xi_{m}\in\mathfrak{g}:(\xi_{m})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)\in\mathcal{S}_{m}\}
, for
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{V}
. Then a section
\xi
on
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
induces a (basic) section
\widetilde{\xi}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{M}
given by
\widetilde{\xi}(m)=\xi(\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m))
.
Therefore, for a section
\xi
on
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
, we define the infinitesimal generator
\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{S})
given by
\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m):=(\xi_{q})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
for
\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)=q
.
Since we work with lifted
G
-actions (and by Def.
3.2
), from now on, we consider the bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\rightarrow Q
.

\diamond

The link between the reduction using a momentum map and mechanics is Noether theorem: we need to guarantee that
X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
is tangent to the level sets of the momentum map. That is why we are interested in conserved quantities of nonholonomic systems and their relation with a momentum. However,
G
-invariant nonholonomic systems may not have conserved quantities; and even if the system admits conserved quantities arising from the symmetries, they may not be given by an element of the Lie algebra but by sections of the bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
, as it was observed in
[
7
,
29
,
31
]
. More precisely:

Definition 3.2
.

[
7
]

A function
J\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is a
horizontal gauge momentum
of the vector field
X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
if

(i)

J
is a conserved quantity of
X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
, i.e.,
X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}(J)=0
; and

(ii)

there exists
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{S})
such that
J={\bf i}_{\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}\Theta_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
,

where
\Theta_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}:=\iota_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}^{\ast}\Theta_{{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}}
and
\Theta_{{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}}
is the Liouville 1-form in
T^{\ast}Q
. In this case, the section
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{S})
is a
horizontal gauge symmetry
and we denote
J=:J_{\xi}
.

Following
[
10
]
, we consider the
nonholonomic momentum (bundle) map

J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{\ast}
which is the bundle map covering the identity on
Q
defined, for each
m\in\mathcal{M}\subset T^{*}Q
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{S})
, by

\langle J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}(m),\xi(\tau_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}(m))\rangle:={\bf i}_{\xi_{\mathcal{M}}}\Theta_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}(m)=\langle m,\xi_{{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}}(\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m))\rangle.

Given
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{S})
, there is an induced function
J_{\xi}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
given by
J_{\xi}(m):=\langle J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},\xi\rangle(m)=\langle J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}(m),\xi(\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m))\rangle
. In particular, the nonholonomic momentum map encodes the horizontal gauge momenta, in the sense that, if
J_{\xi}
is a horizontal gauge momentum then
J_{\xi}=\langle J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}},\xi\rangle
. However for any
\zeta\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}_{S})
, the function
\langle J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},\zeta\rangle
may not be necessarily a horizontal gauge momentum. As a consequence, we assume the following condition (already considered in
[
6
,
34
]
):

Conserved quantities assumption:
A nonholonomic system
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}},H_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}})
with a
G
-symmetry satisfying the dimension assumption, verifies the
conserved quantity assumption
if it admits
k=\textup{rank}(S)
horizontal gauge momenta
\{J_{1},\dots,J_{k}\}
that are
G
-invariant and functionally independent.

Next we observe that the conserved quantity assumption implies condition (A1).

Lemma 3.3
.

The conserved quantity assumption induces an
Ad
-invariant basis

\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny\emph{HGS}}}:=\left\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\right\},

of sections of
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
of horizontal gauge symmetries.

Proof.

First, let us observe that if the horizontal gauge momenta
\{J_{1},\dots,J_{k}\}
are functionally independent, then the corresponding horizontal gauge symmetries
\left\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\right\}
are linearly independent (and globally defined). In fact, if
f\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}
for
f\in C^{\infty}(Q)
, then by Def.
3.2
(ii)
,
J_{1}=(\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}^{*}f)J_{2}
. Using that
J_{i}
are linear on the fibers and that
f
does only depend on the basis
Q
, we see that
J_{1}=(\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}^{*}f)J_{2}
contradicts the linear independence of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
.

In order to prove the
Ad
-invariance of the sections
\xi_{i}
, recall that
Y_{i}=(\xi_{i})_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{i}=(\xi_{i})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
are the vector fields on
Q
and
\mathcal{M}
respectively. Using the
G
-invariance of the Liouville 1-form
\Theta_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
and the function
J_{i}
, we have, for
g\in G
and
m\in\mathcal{M},
that

\Theta_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(\psi_{g}(m))(T\psi_{g}(\mathcal{Y}_{i}(m)))=\Theta_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)(\mathcal{Y}_{i}(m))=J_{i}(m)=J_{i}(\psi_{g}(m))=\Theta_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(\psi_{g}(m))(\mathcal{Y}_{i}(\psi_{g}(m))).

Using the definition of the Liouville 1-form, we obtain, for
q=\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(m)\in Q
that
Y_{i}(\psi_{g}(q))=T\psi_{g}(Y_{i}(q))=(Ad_{g}((\xi_{i})_{q}))_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}(\psi_{g}(q))
. Finally we conclude that
\xi_{i}(q)=Ad_{g}((\xi_{i})_{q})
by the freeness of the
G
-action.
∎

Lemma
3.3
defines also a
G
-invariant global basis of
S
and
\mathcal{S}
given by

S=\textup{span}\{Y_{1}=(\xi_{1})_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}},\dots,Y_{k}=(\xi_{k})_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{S}=\textup{span}\{\mathcal{Y}_{1}=(\xi_{1})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}},\dots,\mathcal{Y}_{k}=(\xi_{k})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}\},

(14)

respectively. We also define the dual (global
Ad^{*}
-invariant) basis of sections

\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}^{\ast}:=\left\{\mu^{1},\dots,\mu^{k}\right\},

(15)

of the bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{*}\to Q
as in (
8
).

Following Def.
2.9
we observe that the nonholonomic momentum bundle map is not necessarily a momentum map for
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
associated to the basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
since, as it was observed in
[
2
,
34
]
,
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{\sharp}(dJ_{i})
might not coincide with the infinitesimal generator
-(\xi_{i})_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}
(in the context of Sec.
2
,
(-(\xi_{i})_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}},dJ_{i})
might not be in
L_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}=\textup{graph}(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{\sharp})
).

In the next section we will deal with this problem by replacing the nonholonomic bracket by another almost Poisson bracket following
[
6
,
32
]
.

3.3
Gauge transformations

In what follows we study a new bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
that describes the dynamics
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}^{\sharp}(dH_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})=-X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
and for which
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{*}
is a momentum map associated to the basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
. In order to define such bracket we “deform” the nonholonomic bracket by means of a 2-form. We follow
[
4
]
where it was introduced a
dynamical gauge transformation
of a bivector field by a 2-form, based in the concept of
gauge transformations

[
47
]
.

First, recall the definition of the nonholonomic bracket
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
given in (
12
). Now, if
B
is a 2-form on
\mathcal{M}
so that
\left.(\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}+B)\right|_{\mathcal{C}}
is a nondegenerate 2-section on
\mathcal{C}
, then the
gauge transformation
of
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
by
B
is a new bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
such that

\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}^{\sharp}(\alpha)=X\iff{\bf i}_{X}(\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}+B)|_{\mathcal{C}}=-\alpha|_{\mathcal{C}},

and we say that
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
and
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
are
gauge related
(by the 2-form
B
),
[
47
]
.

In particular, gauge related (almost) Poisson brackets share the characteristic distribution, which in this case is
\mathcal{C}
for
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
and
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
. If
B
is a semi-basic 2-form with respect to the bundle
\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}:\mathcal{M}\to Q
(i.e.,
{\bf i}_{X}B=0
for all
X\in T\mathcal{M}
such that
T\tau_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}(X)=0
), then the 2-section
(\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}+B)|_{\mathcal{C}}
is automatically nondegenerated.

Remark 3.4
.

(i)

If
(\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}+B)|_{\mathcal{C}}
is degenerate, then the remaining structure is a Dirac structure. However, we will see that in our case of study, we always work with semi-basic 2-forms
B
.

(ii)

In general, the (almost) Dirac structures
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
on
P
are
gauge related
by a 2-form
B
if
L_{2}=\{(X,\alpha+{\bf i}_{X}B)\in\mathbb{T}P:(X,\alpha)\in L_{1}\}
. In this case, if
(F,\omega)
is the associated pair of

, then
(F,\omega+B)
is the pair associated to
L_{2}
.

(iii)

If
\pi
and
\tilde{\pi}
are gauge related bivector fields by a 2-form
B
with integrable characteristic distribution, then the corresponding 2-forms on each leaf differ from
B_{\mu}
, i.e., the restriction of
B
to the leaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
. Even though a gauge transformation keeps the characteristic distribution invariant, gauge related brackets might have different Hamiltonian vector fields, see
[
47
]
.

\diamond

Following
[
4
]
, we say that a gauge transformation of
\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
by a 2-form
B
satisfies the
dynamical condition
if

{\bf i}_{X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}}B=0,

(16)

and the 2-form
B
defines a
dynamical gauge transformation
of
\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}
. In this case, the gauge related bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
describes also the nonholonomic dynamics:
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}^{\sharp}(dH_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})=-X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
.

When the nonholonomic system admits a
G
-symmetry and the 2-form

is
G
-invariant, then the gauge related bivector field

is
G
-invariant as well, and it descends to the reduced quotient manifold

\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
defined, at each

(\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}^{{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}})^{\sharp}(\alpha):=T\rho(\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}}^{\sharp}(\rho^{\ast}\alpha)),

where

is the orbit projection. When

satisfies the dynamical condition (

(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}})^{\sharp}(dH_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}})=-X_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}
, c.f., (
13
).

Remark 3.5
.

It is straightforward to check that the almost Dirac structures
L_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}=\textup{graph}(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\sharp})
and
L_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}=\textup{graph}((\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}})^{\sharp})
are the forward image by

of the almost Dirac structures
L_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}=\textup{graph}(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{\sharp})
and
L_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}=\textup{graph}(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}^{\sharp})
respectively, see Remark
2.4
.

\diamond

As it was observed in

A particular dynamical gauge transformation

Now, we define the 2-form
B
presented in
[
6
]
and here we will show that
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{S}
is a momentum map on the almost Poisson manifold

\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}},H_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}})
be a nonholonomic system with a

G
-invariant distribution

on
Q
and a
G
-invariant vertical distribution

on

– called a

(17)

.

The distributions

\diamond

We have an analogous splitting on

Using that

and we define the 2-form

on

K_{\mbox{\tiny{$W$}}}(X,Y)=dA_{\mbox{\tiny{$W$}}}(P_{\mbox{\tiny{$D$}}}(X),P_{\mbox{\tiny{$D$}}}(Y))

.
Following
[
1
]
, the
\mathcal{W}
-curvature

\mathcal{K}_{\mbox{\tiny{${\mathcal{W}}$}}}
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-valued

-form on

Proposition 3.9
.

The nonholonomic momentum bundle map

and

\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$TQ$}}}=\{X_{\alpha},{Y}_{i}:=(\xi_{i})_{{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}},Z_{a}\}\quad\mbox{with its dual basis}\quad\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$T^{*}Q$}}}=\{X^{\alpha},{Y}^{i},\epsilon^{a}\},

where
\{X_{\alpha}\}
and and

and

\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{${T\mathcal{M}}$}}}=\{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha},\mathcal{Y}_{i},\mathcal{Z}_{a},\partial_{p_{\alpha}},\partial_{p_{i}}\}\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{${T^{\ast}\!\mathcal{M}}$}}}=\{\mathcal{X}^{\alpha},\mathcal{Y}^{i},\tilde{\epsilon}^{a},dp_{\alpha},dp_{i}\},

where

Since that

The previous proposition shows the power of considering the gauge transformation by
B_{1}
and
B
since the nonholonomic momentum map might not be a momentum bundle map for

associated to the basis

and
\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}}
.

Due to the

-invariance of the bivector fields

and

we can define the reduced bivectors
\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}^{1}
and

As a consequence of (

Since

is basic then both bivector fields

and

are gauge related by the 2-form

defined on

and share the characteristic distribution (see Remark

(ii)
). As a consequence of Prop.
3.9
and the dimension assumption, we obtain:

Proposition 3.10
.

The bivector fields
\pi_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}}^{1}
and
\pi_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}}^{{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}}
on
\mathcal{M}/G
have an integrable characteristic distribution given by the common level sets of the (reduced) horizontal gauge momenta
\bar{J}_{i}
, for
\bar{J}_{i}
the functions on
\mathcal{M}/G
such that
\rho^{*}\bar{J}_{i}=J_{i}=\langle J_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}},\xi_{i}\rangle
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

From Prop.
3.9
, we conclude that
\pi_{1}^{\sharp}(dJ_{i})=-(\xi_{i})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
for each

. Then it is easily checked that
\textup{span}\{d\bar{J}_{1},\dots,d\bar{J}_{k}\}=\textup{Ker}(\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{1})
, since
\textup{Ker}(\pi_{1})
are the constraint 1-forms (which are not basic with respect to

). Therefore the characteristic distribution of
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{1}
is given by the common level set the horizontal gauge momenta

. This is also true for

since it is gauge related to

by

.
∎

The technique used in the last proofs (Props.
3.9
and

or
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{1}
. Second, using that

and

are gauge related (respectively
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{1}
and

\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
(respectively on
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
).

3.4
Nonholonomic momentum reduction

Now we arrive to the main result of this section. After the discussion in Sec.
3.3
(in particular in Prop.
3.9
), we observe that due to the interaction of the conserved quantities and the nonholonomic momentum bundle map, the momentum map reduction –stated in Theorem
2.13
– can be done on
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{1})
and
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}})
but not necessarily on
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}})
.

Level sets of the nonholonomic momentum map

Let us start with a nonholonomic system
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}},H_{\mbox{\tiny{${\mathcal{M}}$}}})
with a
G
-symmetry satisfying the dimension and the conserved quantity assumptions. Denote by
\{J_{1},\dots,J_{k}\}
the (
G
-invariant and functionally independent) horizontal gauge momenta, with associated horizontal gauge symmetries defining a global basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\}
of
Ad
-invariant section of
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
.
Let
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}^{\ast}:=\left\{\mu^{1},\dots,\mu^{k}\right\}
be the dual
Ad^{*}
-invariant basis of sections on
\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{*}\to Q
of
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
given in (
15
), and consider
\mu=c_{i}\mu^{i}\in C^{\infty}(Q,\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{\ast})
, where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are constants.
Following Prop.
2.12
, we consider the
G
-invariant submanifold
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{M}
given by

J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu):=\left\{m\in\mathcal{M}\,:\,\,J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(m)=\mu(\tau_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}(m))\right\}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\,J_{i}^{-1}(c_{i}),

(26)

and the collection of (connected components of) the manifolds
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)
determines a foliation of
\mathcal{M}
for
\mu\in\textup{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}^{\ast}
.
Since the functions
J_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, are conserved quantities of the nonholonomic vector field
X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
it is straightforward to see that
X_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
is tangent to the manifolds
J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)
.
Let us denote by
X_{\mu}
the vector field restricted to
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)
, that is,

X_{\mu}:=\left.X_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}\right|_{J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)}.

By the
G
-invariance of the horizontal gauge momenta
J_{i}
we also have that
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G=\cap_{i}\,\bar{J}_{i}^{-1}(c_{i})
, where, as usual,
\bar{J}_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}/G)
are the reduced horizontal gauge momenta (i.e.,
\rho^{*}\bar{J}_{i}=J_{i}
) for
i=1,\dots,k.

The momentum map reduction of
\pi_{1}

Consider the bivector field
\pi_{1}
obtained by the gauge transformation of
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}
by the 2-form
B_{1}
defined in (
19
) and recall that the nonholonomic momentum map is a momentum bundle map associated to
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
(see Prop.
3.9
). Furthermore, observe that conditions (A1) and (A2) remain satisfied for the bivector field
\pi_{1}
.

Following the ideas of Section
2.3
, we define
L_{\mu}^{1}\subset\mathbb{T}(J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu))
the backward image of the almost Poisson bracket
\pi_{1}
(i.e., of the almost Dirac structure
L_{1}:=\textup{graph}(\pi_{1}^{\sharp})
) under the inclusion map
\iota_{\mu}:J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
, given by

L_{\mu}^{1}:={\iota_{\mu}^{\ast}}(L_{1})=\left\{(X,\iota_{\mu}^{\ast}\beta)\in\mathbb{T}(J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu))\,:\,\pi_{1}^{\sharp}(\beta)=T\iota_{\mu}(X)\right\}.

(27)

Since the functions
J_{i}
are
G
-invariant, from Theorem
2.13
(i)
and Prop.
3.9
,
L_{\mu}^{1}
is a
G
-invariant almost Dirac structure on
J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)
. Moreover, recalling that the associated pair of
L_{1}
is
(\mathcal{C},\Omega_{1})
where
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}+B_{1}
, then the associated pair of
L_{1}^{\mu}
is
(\mathcal{C}_{\mu},\Omega_{\mu}^{1})
for
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}=TJ^{-1}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\mu)\cap\mathcal{C}
and
\Omega_{\mu}^{1}=\iota_{\mu}^{*}\Omega_{1}
.

Since
(T(J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)))^{\circ}=\textup{span}\{dJ_{1},\dots,dJ_{k}\}
, and
\mathcal{S}=\textup{span}\{(\xi_{1})_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}},\dots,(\xi_{k})_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}\}
we conclude that:

Lemma 3.11
.

The null distribution
K_{\mu}
of the almost Dirac structure
L_{\mu}^{1}
is the vertical distribution
\mathcal{S}
restricted to
J^{-1}_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}}(\mu)
, that is, for
m\in J^{-1}_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}}(\mu)
,
(K_{\mu})_{m}=\mathcal{S}_{m}
.

From Lemma
3.11
and the fact that
\textup{Ker}(\pi_{1}^{\sharp})
are the constraints 1-forms
\epsilon^{a}
, it is straightforward to see that
L_{\mu}^{1}
is a genuinely almost Dirac structure in the sense that it is not the graph of a bivector field nor of a 2-form.

Next, denoting by
\rho_{\mu}:J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)\to J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G
the orbit projection and following Theorem
2.13
(ii)
we conclude that

(L_{\mu}^{1})_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}:=({\rho_{\mu}})_{\ast}L_{\mu}^{1}=\left\{(T\rho_{\mu}Y,\alpha)\in\mathbb{T}(J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G)\,:\,(Y,\rho_{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha)\in L_{\mu}^{1}\right\},

is an almost Dirac structure. Moreover:

Proposition 3.12
.

The structure
(L_{\mu}^{1})_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}}
is the graph of a symplectic 2-form
\omega_{\mu}^{1}
on
J_{\emph{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G
, that satisfies

\rho_{\mu}^{*}\omega_{\mu}^{1}\,|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}=\iota_{\mu}^{*}(\Omega_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}+B_{1})|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}.

(28)

Proof.

First, we prove that the almost Dirac structure
(L_{\mu}^{1})_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}
on
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G
is associated to a 2-form. From Corollary
2.14
, we know that the structure
(L_{\mu}^{1})_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}
is the graph of a bivector field. Now, if
(0,\alpha)\in(L_{\mu}^{1})_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}
, then there exists
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
such that
(\mathcal{Y},\rho_{\mu}^{*}(\alpha))\in L_{\mu}^{1}
which, by Lemma
3.11
, means that
\mathcal{Y}\in K_{\mu}
. Therefore
\rho_{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha=0
, which implies
\alpha=0
. Let us denote by
\omega_{\mu}^{1}
the (non-degenerate) 2-form associated to the almost Dirac structure
(L_{\mu}^{1})_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}
.

Using Prop.
2.6
, we have
\rho_{\mu}^{*}\omega_{\mu}^{1}|_{\mathcal{U}_{\rho_{\mu}}}=\Omega_{\mu}^{1}|_{\mathcal{U}_{\rho_{\mu}}}
, where
(\mathcal{C}_{\mu},\Omega_{\mu}^{1})
is the pair of
L_{\mu}^{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\rho_{\mu}}:=\{X\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\,:\,(X,\alpha)\in L_{\mu}^{1}\mbox{ and }\alpha|_{\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}=0\}
. From Lemma
3.11
we conclude that
\mathcal{U}_{\rho_{\mu}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
since, if
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
but not in
\mathcal{U}_{\rho_{\mu}}
, means that there exists
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
such that the corresponding 1-form
\alpha
of
X
satisfies that
\alpha(\mathcal{Y})\neq 0.
Then, following the definition of a (almost) Dirac structure we get that
\langle(X,\alpha),(\mathcal{Y},0)\rangle=\alpha(\mathcal{Y})\neq 0
which is a contradiction. Then, we proved (
28
). In particular,
T\rho_{\mu}(\mathcal{C}_{\mu})=T(J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G)
.

Next, we prove that
d\omega_{\mu}^{1}=0
. For each
A=1,2,3
, let
X_{A}\in\mathfrak{X}(J_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G)
and
\tilde{X}_{A}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{C}_{\mu})
such that
T\rho_{\mu}(\tilde{X}_{A})=X_{A}
. Since
TJ_{{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}}^{-1}(\mu)=\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{\mu}
for
\mathcal{W}_{\mu}=TJ^{-1}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\mu)\cap\mathcal{W}
, then

\begin{split}d\omega_{\mu}^{1}(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})&=\textup{cyclic}\left[X_{1}(\omega_{\mu}^{1}(X_{2},X_{3}))-\omega_{\mu}^{1}([X_{1},X_{2}],X_{3})\right]\\
&=\textup{cyclic}\left[\tilde{X}_{1}(\Omega_{\mu}^{1}(\tilde{X}_{2},\tilde{X}_{3}))-\rho_{\mu}^{\ast}\omega_{\mu}^{1}([\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{X}_{2}],\tilde{X}_{3})\right]\\
&=\textup{cyclic}\left[\tilde{X}_{1}(\Omega_{\mu}^{1}(\tilde{X}_{2},\tilde{X}_{3}))-\Omega_{\mu}^{1}(P_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}([\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{X}_{2}]),\tilde{X}_{3})\right],\end{split}

where we use that
\rho_{\mu}^{*}\omega_{\mu}^{1}(P_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{W}_{\mu}$}}}([\tilde{X}_{A},\tilde{X}_{B}]),\tilde{X}_{C})=0
because
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
.
Now, from (
24
) and using that
dp_{i}=0
on
J^{-1}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\mu)
, we have that

(\Omega_{{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}+B_{1})|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}=\iota_{\mu}^{*}\left(\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}\wedge dp_{\alpha}-p_{\alpha}d\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}\right)|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}=\iota_{\mu}^{*}(d(p_{\alpha}\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}))|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}.

Let us denote by
\theta
the closed 2-form on
J^{-1}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\mu)
given by
d(\iota_{\mu}^{*}(p_{\alpha}\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}))
. Observe that
{\bf i}_{Z}\theta=0
for
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{W}_{\mu})
since
d\tilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\alpha}|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}=-\frac{1}{2}C_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\beta}\wedge\tilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\gamma}|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mu}}
. Finally, using that

\widetilde{\omega}=\phi^{\ast}\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{\emph{can}}}}-B_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}}.

Proof.

If we consider the nonholonomic system given by the constraint distribution
\widetilde{H}
and the Lagrangian
L
, we have a
G
-Chaplygin system since
TQ=\widetilde{H}\oplus V
(due to the condition
\widetilde{S}^{\perp}\cap S=\{0\}
). Since
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)=\kappa^{\flat}(\widetilde{H})
, according to
[
41
]
, the submanifold
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)/G
is diffeomorphic to the cotangent manifold
T^{\ast}(Q/G)
, and the reduced nonholonomic vector field restricted to
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)/G
, denoted by
\tilde{X}_{{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}}^{0}
, satisfies that
{\bf i}_{\tilde{X}_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{0}}(\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{can}}}-\hat{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}})=dH_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{0},
where
H_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{0}
is the restricted reduced Hamiltonian and
\hat{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}}
is the 2-form on
T^{*}(Q/G)
such that
\phi^{*}\hat{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}}=B_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}}
.
∎

In this case, we obtain that, on the zero-level set of the conserved quantities, the dynamics is described by a almost symplectic manifold
(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)/G,\widetilde{\omega})
which is diffeomorphic to

(T^{*}(Q/G),\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{can}}}-\hat{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}}),

showing that we have also a Chaplygin leaf. Moreover, the term
\hat{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$\langle J\!,\!\!\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\rangle$}}}
is exactly the
gyroscopic term
defined in
[

]
to describe Chaplygin systems.

6.1
Examples Revisited

In this section we revisit –very briefly– two examples that were studied in
[
3
]
in the context of Chaplygin systems with an extra symmetry. Now, we deal with these examples using a one step reduction, that is, our starting point is the almost Poisson manifold
(\mathcal{M},\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}})
and using the nonholonomic momentum map associated to

we perform a reduction illustrating Sec.

Chaplygin ball

The Chaplygin ball is the classical example describing a ball rolling without sliding on a plane. We follow the notation of
[
36
,
6
,
5
]
. The configuration manifold is
Q=SO(3)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with coordinates
(g,(x,y))
where
g
is a rotation matrix (with
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
,
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})
its rows) and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
represents the center of mass of the ball (that coincides with the geometric center). The Lagrangian is

L((g,x,y),(\boldsymbol{\Omega},\dot{x},\dot{y}))=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\mathbb{I}\boldsymbol{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\Omega}\rangle+\tfrac{m}{2}(\dot{x}^{2}+\dot{y}^{2})
, where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3})

\mathbb{I}
the inertia tensor represented as a diagonal matrix and
m
is the mass of the ball. If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})
are the left-invariant Maurer Cartan 1-forms on
SO(3)

\epsilon^{x}=dx-r\langle\beta,\lambda\rangle
and
\epsilon^{y}=dy+r\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
and therefore
D=\textup{span}\{\mathbb{X}_{j}:=X_{j}^{L}+R\beta_{j}\partial_{x}-R\alpha_{j}\partial_{y}\}
. The system is invariant by the action of the Lie group
G=SO(2)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Now following (
17
), we consider the basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$TQ$}}}
adapted to the splitting
TQ=H\oplus S\oplus W
given by
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$TQ$}}}=\textup{span}\{X_{1},X_{2},Y,\partial_{x},\partial_{y}\}
where
X_{i}=\mathbb{X}_{i}-\gamma_{i}Y
for
i=1,2
,

for
\mathbb{X}=(\mathbb{X}_{1},\mathbb{X}_{2},\mathbb{X}_{3})
. Consider the coordinates
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},p_{x},p_{y})
on
T^{*}_{q}Q
associated to the dual basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$T^{*}Q$}}}=\{\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2},\sigma^{Y},\epsilon^{x},\epsilon^{y}\}
of
T^{*}Q
, then the constraint manifold
\mathcal{M}
is determined by the coordinates
(g,x,y,M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
(since
(p_{x},p_{y})
depend linearly on
M_{i}
).
Since
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then the section
\xi(g,x,y)=(1;-x,y)
of the action bundle
Q\times\mathfrak{g}\to Q
generates the subbundle
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\to Q
and it defines the horizontal gauge momenta
J_{\xi}={\bf i}_{\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}}\Theta_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}=M_{3}
(observe that
\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}=Y
).

Following
[
6
]
, and the notation in (
18
), (
23
), the 2-form
B_{1}=\langle J,\mathcal{K}_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{W}$}}}\rangle-J_{\xi}d\widetilde{\epsilon}^{Y}
and we obtain that

\displaystyle{\pi_{1}=\mathcal{X}_{1}\wedge\partial_{M_{1}}+\mathcal{X}_{2}\wedge\partial_{M_{2}}-\tfrac{1}{\gamma_{3}}(M_{1}\gamma_{1}-M_{2}\gamma_{2})\partial_{M_{1}}\wedge\partial_{M_{2}}.}

The nonholonomic momentum map, given by
\langle J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(m),\xi(q)\rangle=M_{3}
is a momentum bundle map for
\pi_{1}
associated to the basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}=\{\xi\}
(since
\pi_{1}^{\sharp}(dM_{3})=-\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
). The
G
-invariant submanifold
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{M}
(for
\mu=c{\bf e}^{1}
) is defined by
M_{3}=c
, and the almost Dirac structure
L_{c}^{1}=\iota_{\mu}^{*}(L_{1})\subset\mathbb{T}(J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu))
is not the graph of a 2-form or a bivector field since the elements
(0,\epsilon^{x})
and
(-\xi_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}},0)
belong to
L_{c}^{1}
. The push-forward of
L_{c}^{1}
to the quotient manifold
J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G
(with coordinates
(\gamma,M_{1},M_{2})
) gives the Dirac structure defined by the symplectic 2-form
\omega_{c}^{1}=\sigma^{1}\wedge dM_{1}+\sigma^{2}\wedge dM_{2}+\tfrac{1}{\gamma_{3}}(M_{1}\gamma_{1}-M_{2}\gamma_{2})\sigma^{1}\wedge\sigma^{2}
, illustrating Prop.
3.12
.
Moreover, using these coordinates, it is straightforward to see that
(J_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}^{-1}(\mu)/G,\omega_{c}^{1})
is symplectomorphic to
(T^{*}S^{2},\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{can}}}=-d(M_{1}\sigma^{1}+M_{2}\sigma^{2}))
.

Finally, since
\mathcal{B}=(mr^{2}\langle\gamma,\Omega\rangle+M_{3})\gamma_{3}\sigma^{1}\wedge\sigma^{2},
from Theorems
3.17
and
4.8
, we conclude that restricted reduced nonholonomic dynamics
X_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{c}
is a Hamiltonian vector field on the almost symplectic manifold

(T^{*}S^{2},\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{can}}}=-d(M_{1}\sigma^{1}+M_{2}\sigma^{2})+\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{c}),

(34)

where
\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{c}=\left(\tfrac{mr^{2}}{Y(\gamma)}\langle A^{-1}M,\gamma\rangle+\tfrac{c}{Y(\gamma)}\right)\gamma_{3}\,\sigma^{1}\wedge\sigma^{2},
for
Y(\gamma)=1-mr^{2}\langle A^{-1}\gamma,\gamma\rangle
,

and

. We stress the Chaplygin spirit of (
34
) and the fact that these are the leaves of the bivector field
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{red}}}^{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
that describes the reduced dynamics.

Solids of revolution rolling on a plane

Consider a solid of revolution that is invariant around the vertical axis and therefore the inertia matrix
\mathbb{I}
has principal moment of inertia
I_{1}=I_{2}
and
I_{3}
. Keeping the notation of the previous example, the orientation of the solid is represented by the rotational matrix
g\in SO(3)
and by
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the position of the center of mass.
Since the solid rolls on a plane, the configuration manifold is
Q=\{(g,x,y,z):z=-\langle\gamma,s\rangle\}
where

is the map from

to the surface of the body
\mathfrak{S}
given by

The nonsliding constraints are given by the annihilator of the 1-forms

The Lie group
G=S^{1}\times SE(2)
is a symmetry of the nonholonomic system when

(since we want free and proper actions), see
[
28
]
.

From
[
6
]
, we recall that
S=\textup{span}\{\tilde{Y}_{1}=-\mathbb{X}_{3},\tilde{Y}_{2}=\langle\gamma,\mathbb{X}\rangle\}
.
Having in mind that
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we observe that
\zeta_{1}=(1;0,h_{1})

, that is,
(\zeta_{i})_{\mbox{\tiny{$Q$}}}=\tilde{Y}_{i}

Associated to the splitting
TQ=H\oplus S\oplus W
, we consider the following basis of
TQ
defined by
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$TQ$}}}=\{X_{0}=\gamma_{1}\mathbb{X}_{2}-\gamma_{2}\mathbb{X}_{1},\widetilde{Y}_{1},\widetilde{Y}_{2},\partial_{x},\partial_{y}\}
with dual basis denoted by
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{$T^{*}Q$}}}=\{\sigma^{0},\widetilde{Y}^{1},\widetilde{Y}^{2},\epsilon^{x},\epsilon^{y}\}
. If
(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},p_{x},p_{y})
are the coordinates of

associated to our chosen basis, then

.
The symmetries also induce two horizontal gauge momenta

J_{i}=f_{i}^{1}p_{1}+f_{i}^{2}p_{2}
for
i=1,2
and where

are smooth functions on
Q
determined by a ODE (linear) system (see
[
5
,
28
]
). Then the horizontal gauge symmetries are given by sections
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
so that
\xi_{i}=f_{i}^{1}\zeta_{1}+f_{i}^{2}\zeta_{2}
and then
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}=\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
.

Since
\textup{rank}(H)=1
then
B=B_{1}
and then we observe that
\pi_{1}=\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}=\mathcal{X}_{0}\wedge\partial_{p_{0}}+\mathcal{Y}_{i}\wedge\partial_{J_{i}}
, where
\mathcal{X}_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
as in (
23

\mathcal{Y}_{i}=(\xi_{i})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
.
As Prop.
3.9

given by

–where
\nu^{1}=(1;0,0)
and
\nu^{2}=(0;1,0)
are sections on
\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{*}\to Q
dual to the basis

– is a momentum bundle map for
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}
associated to
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
, since we can check that
\pi_{\mbox{\tiny{$B$}}}^{\sharp}(dJ_{i})=-(\xi_{i})_{\mbox{\tiny{$\mathcal{M}$}}}
.
Now, if
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{S}
given by
\mu=c_{1}\mu^{1}+c_{2}\mu^{2}
for
\{\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\}
the dual basis of
\mathfrak{B}_{\mbox{\tiny{HGS}}}
then
J^{-1}_{\mbox{\tiny{nh}}}(\mu)=\{(g,(x,y),p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathcal{M}:f_{i}^{1}p_{1}+f_{i}^{2}p_{2}=c_{i}\}

Moreover, we can also observe that

with the canonical symplectic form:

From here we conclude that that the reduced dynamics is described on each leaf by the canonical symplectic manifold
(T^{*}S^{1},\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{can}}})
which are also the leaves of the symplectic foliation associated to the Poisson bracket

The dynamics of the system.

This system has two types of constraints: the holonomic constraint which is imposed by the fact that the ball is attached to the surface of the fixed sphere, and the nonholonomic one which is defined by the non-slipping condition.

As usual we keep the notation of the previous example and in this case the inertia tensor

Denote by

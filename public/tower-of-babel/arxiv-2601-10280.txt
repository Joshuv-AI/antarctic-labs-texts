Optimisation of the lowest Robin eigenvalue in exterior domains of the hyperbolic plane
Antonio Celentano, David Krejčiřík, Vladimir Lotoreichik

Abstract
. We consider the Robin Laplacian in the exterior of a bounded simply-connected Lipschitz domain in the hyperbolic plane. We show that the essential spectrum of this operator is [ 1 4 , ∞ ) [\frac{1}{4},\infty) and that, under convexity assumption on the domain, there exist discrete eigenvalues below 1 4 \frac{1}{4} if, and only if, the Robin parameter is below a non-positive critical constant, which depends on the shape of the domain. As the main result, we prove that the lowest Robin eigenvalue for the exterior of a bounded geodesically convex domain Ω \Omega in the hyperbolic plane does not exceed such an eigenvalue for the exterior of the geodesic disk, whose geodesic curvature of the boundary is not smaller than the averaged geodesic curvature of the boundary of Ω \Omega . This result implies as a consequence that under fixed area or fixed perimeter constraints the exterior of the geodesic disk maximises the lowest Robin eigenvalue among exteriors of bounded geodesically convex domains. Moreover, we obtain under the same geometric constraints a reverse inequality between the critical constants.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

Optimisation of eigenvalues of differential operators with respect to shape of the domain is a classical topic in spectral geometry with many results and challenging open problems; see
e.g.
the monographs
[
36
,
37
]
and the references therein.
A prominent question in this context is to optimise the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian
with certain boundary conditions and under
suitable geometric constraints such as fixed volume or fixed perimeter. The case of Dirichlet
boundary conditions is the most classical and goes back to the papers by Faber
[
26
]
and Krahn
[
47
,
48
]
. For the Neumann Laplacian the lowest eigenvalue is always zero and one is then naturally interested in optimisation of the first non-zero eigenvalue
[
63
,
65
]
.
In both these paradigmatic examples,
in analogy with the classical isoperimetric inequality,
the ball turns out to be the optimal geometry.

In the recent years, Robin boundary conditions attracted a lot of attention. In this case, the sign of the boundary parameter plays a significant role. It was proved by Bossel
[
12
]

in two dimensions and then later by Daners
[
25
]

in all space dimensions that under fixed volume constraint the ball minimises the lowest Robin eigenvalue, when the boundary parameter is positive. Alternative proofs were proposed in

[
3
,
16
,
17
]
.
Hence, despite the necessity to deal with
significant methodological challenges,
the positive Robin case brings no surprises:
the ball remains the optimal geometry.

The situation changes drastically with the negative boundary parameter. It was proved by Freitas and the second author of the present paper
[
27
]
that under fixed volume constraint the ball is not an optimiser in general, but is a maximiser in two dimensions if the negative Robin parameter exceeds a critical negative constant, which only depends on the area of the domain. On the other hand, under fixed perimeter constraint in two dimensions
[
7
]
and under fixed area of the boundary with additional convexity assumption in higher dimensions
[
15
]
, it is proved that the ball is the maximiser of the lowest eigenvalue for all negative boundary parameters. Moreover, it is conjectured in
[
7
]
that under fixed volume constraint the ball is a maximiser of the lowest Robin eigenvalue with negative boundary parameter among simply-connected planar domains and among convex domains in higher space dimensions.

Optimisation of the lowest Robin eigenvalue was studied also for bounded domains in manifolds
[
1
,
23
,
43
,
60
]
and in the presence of a homogeneous magnetic field
[
40
]
. The paper
[
43
]
provides, in particular, optimisation results on bounded domains in the hyperbolic plane or a more general negatively curved manifold. However, to the best of our knowledge, it remains an open problem whether among all bounded smooth simply-connected domains in the hyperbolic plane with fixed perimeter the lowest eigenvalue of the Robin Laplacian with negative boundary parameter is maximised by the geodesic disk.

A lot of attention has also been paid to optimisation of the second and the third Robin eigenvalues of the Laplacian on a bounded domain in Euclidean space
[
28
,
29
,
32
,
44
]
and in a manifold
[
52
]
. The latter paper deals, in particular, with optimisation of the second Robin eigenvalue on a bounded domain in the hyperbolic plane yielding optimality of the geodesic disk under suitable geometric constraints and for the boundary parameter from a certain natural range.

Along with optimisation of Laplace eigenvalues on general bounded domains, there is an interest in optimisation of such eigenvalues on doubly-connected domains; see
[
38
,
57
]
for classical works and
[
5
,
6
]
for more recent refinements.

As a sort of extremal case of a doubly-connected domain, the second and the third authors of the present paper initiated in the series of papers
[
49
,
50
,
51
]
the investigation
of eigenvalue optimisation for the Robin Laplacian on exterior domains with negative boundary parameters. Here the main challenge is the presence of the essential spectrum due to the unboundedness of the geometry in question. In the planar case, it was proved in
[
49
,
50
]
that the essential spectrum of the Robin Laplacian with a negative boundary parameter on the complement
\Omega^{\rm ext}:={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
of a bounded simply-connected smooth domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
coincides with the non-negative semi-axis and that this operator has at least one negative discrete eigenvalue, which is maximised by the exterior of the disk provided that the area or the perimeter of
\Omega
is fixed. The case of exterior domains in higher dimensions
is more complicated
[
50
]
, but balls are still at least locally optimal
[
18
]
.

A dual problem to the Robin Laplacian on an exterior domain is the Steklov eigenvalue problem on an exterior domain, which has recently been studied in detail
[
19
]
.
Furthermore, it was demonstrated in
[
35
]
that at least in some special cases the eigenvalues of the Robin Laplacian on an exterior Euclidean domain arise in the asymptotic behaviour of the eigenvalues of the Robin Laplacian on a domain in a sphere, which expands to the whole sphere.

The main motivation of the present paper is to obtain a counterpart
of the aforementioned Euclidean results
[
49
,
50
]

for exterior domains in the hyperbolic plane.
In the hyperbolic setting, there are new challenges such as the essential spectrum starting not from zero and the non-criticality of the Neumann Laplacian in the exterior of bounded convex domains. The spectral optimality of geodesic disks is thus by far not evident.

1.2.
Definition of the operator and the characterisation of its lowest spectral point

Let
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
denote the hyperbolic plane, which can be viewed in the hyperbolic model as the upper sheet of the hyperboloid of two sheets equipped with the pullback of the Minkowski metric in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For further details on hyperbolic planes see Subsection
2.2
. We denote by
\mu
the Riemannian measure on
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
induced by its (Riemannian) metric.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a bounded simply-connected Lipschitz domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
. In the following,
\sigma
stands for the natural one-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial\Omega
. We denote by
\Omega^{\rm ext}:={\mathbb{H}}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
the complement of
\Omega
in the hyperbolic plane.
The Lebesgue space
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext},{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu)

and the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext},{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu)
are defined in the standard way. For any
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext},{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu)
, its trace
u|_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}
is well defined as a function in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma)
. In the following we will omit the indication of the measure
{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu
and we will write
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
and
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
instead of
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext},{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu)
and
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext},{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu)
, respectively. We will also omit the indication of measure
{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma
and write
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
instead of
L^{2}(\partial\Omega,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma)
.

Let the boundary parameter
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
be fixed.
We show in Proposition
3.1
that the quadratic form

(1.1)

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}^{{\rm ext}}[u]:=\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\nabla u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,Q_{\alpha}^{{\rm ext}}:=W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}),

is closed, densely defined, symmetric and lower-semibounded in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
. Thus, it induces a unique self-adjoint operator in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
, which we denote by
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}
. We also denote by

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext}):=\inf\sigma(-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}})
the lowest spectral point of this operator. By the min-max principle it admits the variational characterisation

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})=\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\nabla u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}.

This lowest spectral point can be either a discrete eigenvalue or the bottom of the essential spectrum depending on the value of the boundary parameter
\alpha
.

1.3.
Main results

Our first main result provides a characterisation of the essential spectrum for the operator
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
. Not surprisingly, the essential spectrum of this operator is the same as the essential spectrum of the Laplacian on the whole hyperbolic plane.

Theorem A
.

\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}})=[\frac{1}{4},\infty)
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem
Theorem A
will be stated as Proposition
3.4
below and proved by showing two inclusions of the sets, where one inclusion is demonstrated by means of a proper Weyl sequence, while the other inclusion relies on the Neumann bracketing.

We also demonstrate in Lemma
3.5
that
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\rightarrow-\infty
as
\alpha\rightarrow-\infty
and that
{\mathbb{R}}\ni\alpha\mapsto\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
is a non-decreasing function. Thus, it is natural to introduce and study the quantity

\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext}):=\sup\left\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon\ \lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})<\frac{1}{4}\right\}.

Clearly,
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
is thus a discrete eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}
if, and only if,
\alpha<\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
. Our second main result concerns this critical constant.

Theorem B
.

The following hold.

(i)

\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq 0

for any bounded geodesically convex
C^{2}
-smooth domain

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
. If the domain
\Omega
is, in addition, strictly convex, then
\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})<0
.

(ii)

\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})\leq\frac{1}{2}(e^{-R}-\coth{R})

for the geodesic disk
B_{R}\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
of radius
R>0
.

Item (i) of Theorem
Theorem B
will be stated in Theorem
3.10
and proved using the method of parallel coordinates with the help of certain one-dimensional Poincaré-type inequalities. Item (ii) in Theorem
Theorem B
will be shown in Lemma
4.1
by means of separation of variables and of subsequent reduction of existence of the discrete eigenvalue to solvability of a scalar equation involving special functions. We also conjecture that for some non-convex bounded domains in the hyperbolic plane, the critical boundary parameter
\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
can be positive. A geometric construction to support this conjecture is outlined in Remark
3.11
.

The central result of the present manuscript is a comparison between the lowest eigenvalue of the Robin Laplacian on the exterior of a bounded geodesically convex domain in the hyperbolic plane and the same eigenvalue for the exterior of the geodesic disk. In the following, we use for a domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
the notations
|\Omega|
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial\Omega)
for the area and the perimeter, respectively.
As the notation suggests,
the latter is introduced through the Hausdorff
measure of the topological boundary
\partial\Omega
,
but it also coincides with the notion of perimeter
in the sense of De Giorgi
(
i.e.
, the Hausdorff measure of the reduced boundary)
under the present regularity hypotheses.

Theorem C
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a bounded geodesically convex domain and let
B\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a geodesic disk such that

(1.2)

\frac{|\Omega|+2\pi}{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\frac{|B|+2\pi}{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial B)}.

Then, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, it holds

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B^{\rm ext}).

In particular, the reverse inequality for the critical boundary parameters
\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\geq\alpha_{\star}(B^{\rm ext})
holds.

We remark that by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem the quantity
|\Omega|+2\pi
is equal for smooth
\Omega
to
\int_{\partial\Omega}\kappa
,
where
\kappa\geq 0
is the geodesic curvature of
\partial\Omega
with a suitable sign convention. Thus, the quantity

(|\Omega|+2\pi)/{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial\Omega)
can be interpreted as the averaged geodesic curvature of
\partial\Omega
.
The above optimisation result will be restated in Theorem
5.1
below and proved using the trial functions, which depend only on the distance to the boundary of the domain.
The condition (
1.2
) appears naturally in the analysis through the formula for the length of an outer parallel curve for a bounded convex domain in
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
. An additional ingredient in the proof is the monotonicity of a certain quotient expressed through the ground-state eigenfunction for the Robin Laplacian on the exterior of the geodesic disk in the hyperbolic plane.
The monotonicity of the same quotient implies also that the function
R\mapsto\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})
(here
R
is the radius of the disk) is non-decreasing. A more precise variant of this property is stated and proved in Proposition
4.4
below.

Theorem
Theorem C
implies the isoperimetric inequalities for fixed perimeter or fixed area constraints. Indeed, one can show that the geodesic disk having the same area or the same perimeter as
\Omega
satisfies the condition (
1.2
).
Thus, we can also formulate the following corollary.

Corollary
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a bounded geodesically convex domain and let
B\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be the geodesic disk such that either
|\Omega|=|B|
or
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial\Omega)={\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial B)
.
Then, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, it holds

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B^{\rm ext}).

2.2.
Properties of the hyperbolic plane

The theory of hyperbolic planes and of hyperbolic spaces in higher dimensions can be found,
e.g.
, in
[
10
]
. In this subsection, we outline basic properties of hyperbolic planes relying on the monograph
[
10
]
and also on
[
24
, Section 2]
.

The two-dimensional hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
is the unique simply-connected, two-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with constant negative Gaussian curvature equal to
-1
. The uniqueness of the hyperbolic plane, which follows from the Killing–Hopf theorem
[
54
, Theorem 12.4]
, implies that any two two-dimensional Riemannian manifolds with these properties are isometric.

There are several ways to construct hyperbolic planes. We consider here the hyperboloid model.
Given
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with
x=\left(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\right)
and
y=\left(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}\right)
, the Lorentz inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is defined by

\langle x,y\rangle=-x_{0}y_{0}+x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}.

The branch of the hyperboloid given by

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\colon\,\langle x,x\rangle=-1,x_{0}>0\right\},

and equipped with the metric tensor

(2.1)

{\,\mathrm{d}}l^{2}=-{\,\mathrm{d}}x_{0}^{2}+{\,\mathrm{d}}x_{1}^{2}+{\,\mathrm{d}}x_{2}^{2}

induced by the Lorentz inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, is a model of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. According to
[
31
, Section 4.2]
the hyperbolic distance between two points
x,y\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
is

(2.2)

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(x,y)={\rm arcosh}\,(-\langle x,y\rangle).

In this model, the geodesic starting at
x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
with unit velocity
y\in T_{x}\mathbb{H}^{2}
is given by

{\mathbb{R}}\ni t\rightarrow\cosh(t)x+\sinh(t)y\in\mathbb{H}^{2},

that is the intersection of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with the plane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
spanned by
x
and
y
;
cf.

[
10
, Proposition A.5.1]
.

Following the lines of
[
24
, Section 2]

we fix the point
O=(1,0,0)
as the origin of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and adopt the geodesic polar “coordinates”

(r,\theta)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
centred at
O
. Any point
(x_{0},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, with
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})
, can be represented as

(x_{0},x^{\prime})=\left(\cosh r,\theta\sinh r\right)\,,

where
r\geq 0
denotes the hyperbolic distance from
O
to
(x_{0},x^{\prime})
.
Since

{\,\mathrm{d}}x_{0}=\sinh r\,\text{d}r,\qquad{\,\mathrm{d}}x^{\prime}=\theta\cosh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r+\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta,

then the metric takes the form

\text{d}l^{2}=\text{d}r^{2}+\sinh^{2}r\,\text{d}\theta^{2}.

Finally, the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
in these geodesic polar coordinates is given by

\Delta=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{\cosh r}{\sinh r}\partial_{r}+\frac{\partial_{\theta}^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,.

Next, we address the properties of convex sets in the hyperbolic plane. The convexity of a set in a Riemmannian manifold is more subtle than in the Euclidean space. There are three related notions of convexity:
weak
,
strong
and
local
convexity, which are defined in Appendix
A
. In the hyperbolic plane, the notions of a weakly convex and a strongly convex set coincide, since for every two points there exists a unique minimising geodesic connecting them. Therefore, in the following sections, we will refer simply to geodesically convex sets.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
be a closed, connected, locally convex set. Then
C
is strongly convex.

Proof.

The local convexity of
C
, combined with its connectedness, guarantees that the interior
\mathring{C}
is connected (see
[
21
, Lemma 1.5]
). This allows us to use Theorem
A.4
for
\mathring{C}
, ensuring that every boundary point
p\in\partial\mathring{C}
possesses a supporting element. Since in hyperbolic plane the cut locus
\cut(p)
is empty for all points

, we have

\mathring{C}\setminus\cut(p)=\mathring{C},

which is connected. Therefore, by applying Proposition
A.3
, it follows that
\mathring{C}
is weakly convex, and consequently strongly convex, where we implicitly used that there is a unique minimal geodesic between any two points in
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.
Lastly, because
C
is closed and locally convex, it coincides with the closure of its interior (see
[
21
, Theorem 1.6]
), and so

inherits the strong convexity property from
\mathring{C}
.
∎

Convex subsets of

Let

Since the Gaussian curvature in

is negative, Theorem

is strongly convex for every

.
∎

3.
The spectral problem on an exterior domain in the hyperbolic plane

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set such that the exterior
\Omega^{\rm ext}
is connected. We aim at considering the following

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda u&\text{in }\Omega^{\rm ext},\\[5.0pt]
-\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\alpha u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\rm ext},\end{cases}

where

denotes the outer unit normal to
\Omega
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed boundary parameter.
In order to introduce this spectral problem rigorously, we consider the sesquilinear form

Q_{\alpha}^{\text{ext}}[u]:=\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}\absolutevalue{\nabla u}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma,\qquad\text{dom}(Q_{\alpha}^{\text{ext}}):=W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}),

where the boundary term is understood in the sense of traces
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})
. The fact that the trace mapping from
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
into
L^{2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})
is well defined and continuous follows immediately by restricting the Sobolev functions to a compact Lipschitz set
K\setminus\Omega
with some compact
K\supset\Omega
and applying the standard
trace theorem for the case of bounded domains; see
e.g.

[
13
, Theorem 7.5]
. It can be shown that the sesquilinear form is symmetric, densely defined, closed, and lower semibounded
in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
The facts that the form
Q_{\alpha}^{\rm ext}
is symmetric and densely defined are clear. Below we focus on proving that this form is closed and lower semibounded.

Proposition 3.1
.

The sesquilinear form
Q_{\alpha}^{\rm ext}
is closed and lower semibounded.

Proof.

First, we note that
Q_{0}^{\rm ext}
(
\alpha=0
) is closed
and non-negative. While non-negativity is obvious, closedness follows from the fact that the norm induced by this form coincides with the standard norm in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
By Lemma
C.1
in Appendix
C
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that the inequality

\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\nabla u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+C(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu

holds for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
Hence, the above inequality with a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
combined with stability result for the closedness of forms under perturbations
[
41
, Chapter VI, Theorem 3.4]
yields that the sesquilinear form
Q_{\alpha}^{\rm ext}
is closed and lower semibounded for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

The first representation theorem
[
41
, Chapter VI, Theorem 2.1]
ensures that the form
Q_{\alpha}^{\rm ext}
defines a unique self-adjoint operator
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
in
the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
called Robin Laplacian and acting as

\displaystyle-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}u

\displaystyle=-\Delta u,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,\left(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\,\colon\,\ \Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}),\ -\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\Big|_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}+\alpha u|_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}=0\right\},

where the Neumann trace
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\Big|_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}
is understood
as a distribution in
W^{-1/2,2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
The formal problem (
3.1
) is then meant as the spectral problem for the self-adjoint operator
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
The embedding
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
is not compact, as shown in the following remark.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}(p))
be a non-zero cut-off function.
Choose points
p_{k}\in\Omega^{\rm ext}
with

d_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(p_{k},p)\to\infty
and
d_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(p_{k},p_{j})>2
for
k\neq j
.
Pick isometries
g_{k}\in\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
with
g_{k}(p)=p_{k}
and set
\varphi_{k}:=\varphi\circ g_{k}^{-1}.
Then
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi_{k}\subset B_{1}(p_{k})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the supports of
\{\varphi_{k}\}
are mutually disjoint,

\|\varphi_{k}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}=\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}

and

\|\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}=\sqrt{2}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}

for
k\neq j
. Thus
\varphi_{k}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})

but has no convergent subsequence in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.

Therefore the resolvent of the Robin Laplacian
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}
is not a compact operator.
We will show that the essential spectrum of the Robin Laplacian
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
is non-empty. It is also useful for the following considerations to recall that a variational characterisation of the lowest point of the spectrum
[
58
, Theorem XIII.1]
holds

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext}):=\inf\sigma\left(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}\right)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\\
u\not=0\end{subarray}}\frac{Q_{\alpha}^{\text{ext}}[u]}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}^{2}}.

Next, we prove a weighted Poincaré-type inequality with a boundary term. This inequality will be used later in the proof of the characterisation of the essential spectrum for the Robin Laplacian
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
b>0
and
\alpha\geq 2^{-1}(b^{-1}-\coth(b))
. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([b,\infty))\\
\psi\not=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\sinh(b)|\psi(b)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

First, we derive an auxiliary inequality.
Note that

\forall f\in C_{c}^{1}([b,\infty))\,,\quad\int_{b}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,t{\,\mathrm{d}}t\geq 0.

By making the substitution

g(t)=\sqrt{t}\,f(t)
,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,t{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|\frac{g^{\prime}}{\sqrt{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{g}{t^{3/2}}\right|^{2}t{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|g^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{g}{t}\right|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|g^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t-\frac{1}{2}\int_{b}^{\infty}(|g|^{2})^{\prime}\frac{1}{t}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{|g|^{2}}{t^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|g^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{2b}|g(b)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{|g|^{2}}{t^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}}t\geq 0.

For any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}([b,\infty))
not identically zero, we set

\varphi(t)=\sqrt{\sinh(t)}\,\psi(t)
,
obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\sinh(t)}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varphi\cosh(t)}{\sinh^{3/2}(t)}\right|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|\varphi^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi\coth(t)\right|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t-\frac{1}{2}\int_{b}^{\infty}(|\varphi|^{2})^{\prime}\coth(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi|^{2}\coth^{2}(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{\coth(b)|\varphi(b)|^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}\left(\coth^{2}(t)-\frac{2}{\sinh^{2}(t)}\right)|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t.

Using

\coth^{2}(t)-\frac{2}{\sinh^{2}(t)}=1-\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}(t)}\geq 1-\frac{1}{t^{2}},

and recalling (
3
), we add and subtract the term
\frac{1}{2b}|\varphi(b)|^{2}
in (
3.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\sinh(b)|\psi(b)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\left(\frac{\coth(b)}{2}-\frac{1}{2b}+\alpha\right)|\varphi(b)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t.\qed

In the next proposition, we characterise the essential spectrum of the Robin Laplacian in the exterior of a bounded Lipschitz set in the hyperbolic plane. As expected, this essential spectrum coincides with the essential spectrum of the Laplacian in the whole hyperbolic plane.

Proposition 3.4
.

The essential spectrum of the Robin Laplacian
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm ess}\left(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}\right)=\left[\frac{1}{4},\infty\right).

Proof.

Step 1: the inclusion
\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}})\supseteq\left[\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
.
We apply Weyl’s criterion to prove the inclusion
\supseteq
. For every
\lambda\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
, we will construct a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}\,(-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}})
such that

(1)

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}=1
,

(2)

\|(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}-\lambda)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

(3)

f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.

This construction follows the approach outlined in
[
11
, Proof of Proposition 7.2]
. Given
\lambda\geq\frac{1}{4}
, we set

s=\frac{1}{2}+i\rho,\quad\text{where }\rho=\sqrt{\lambda-\frac{1}{4}},

then
\lambda=s(1-s)
. We define for
2\leq a<b
a family of compactly supported functions on
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
in geodesic polar coordinates by

f_{a,b}(r):=e^{-sr}\psi_{a,b}(r),

where
\psi_{a,b}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,\infty))
is a radial cut-off function such that

\psi_{a,b}(r)=\begin{cases}1,&r\in[a,b],\\
0,&r\notin[a-1,b+1],\end{cases}\quad\text{with }0\leq\psi_{a,b}\leq 1,\,\|\psi_{a,b}^{\prime}\|_{\infty},\,\|\psi_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1},

for some constant
C_{1}>0
independent of
a,b
and taken, without loss of generality, greater than
1
.
A straightforward calculations gives

\displaystyle f_{a,b}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=-se^{-sr}\psi_{a,b}(r)+e^{-sr}\psi_{a,b}^{\prime}(r),

\displaystyle f_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=s^{2}e^{-sr}\psi_{a,b}(r)-2se^{-sr}\psi_{a,b}^{\prime}(r)+e^{-sr}\psi_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(r).

Since
f_{a,b}
is radial, we compute the Laplacian of
f_{a,b}
in geodesic polar coordinates

\displaystyle-\Delta f_{a,b}(r)

\displaystyle=-f_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(r)-\coth r\cdot f_{a,b}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=-e^{-sr}\left[\left(s^{2}-s\coth r\right)\psi_{a,b}(r)+\left(\coth r-2s\right)\psi_{a,b}^{\prime}(r)+\psi_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}(r)\right].

Therefore

-\Delta f_{a,b}-\lambda f_{a,b}=-e^{-sr}\left[s\left(1-\coth r\right)\psi_{a,b}+(\coth r-2s)\psi_{a,b}^{\prime}+\psi_{a,b}^{\prime\prime}\right].

We estimate the norm of
f_{a,b}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{2})
as

(3.5)

\|f_{a,b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}|f_{a,b}(r)|^{2}\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r\geq 2\pi\int_{a}^{b}e^{-r}\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r\geq\frac{\pi}{2}(b-a).

We have

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f_{a,b}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{a-1}^{b+1}\left|(-\Delta-\lambda)f_{a,b}(r)\right|^{2}\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r.

Next, we split the integral in three pieces. Observe that
that for any
c\geq 1

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{c+1}\left|(-\Delta-\lambda)f_{a,b}(r)\right|^{2}\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{1}^{2}\int_{c}^{c+1}e^{-r}\sinh(r)\Big(|s|^{2}(1-\coth r)^{2}+|\coth r-2s|^{2}+1\Big){\,\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}C_{1}^{2}\Big(|s|^{2}(\coth(1)-1)^{2}+2\coth^{2}(1)+4|s|^{2}+1\Big).

Moreover

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|(-\Delta-\lambda)f_{a,b}(r)\right|^{2}\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\leq|s|^{2}\int_{2}^{\infty}(\coth(r)-1)^{2}e^{-r}\sinh(r){\,\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\leq|s|^{2}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{2e^{-4r}}{1-e^{-2r}}{\,\mathrm{d}}r\leq\frac{2|s|^{2}}{1-e^{-4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4r}{\,\mathrm{d}}r=\frac{|s|^{2}}{2(1-e^{-4})}\,.

Hence, employing (
3.6
) with
c=a-1
and
c=b

and using the last bound in (
3.7
), we get that the norm of the quantity
(-\Delta-\lambda)f_{a,b}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{2})
is bounded by a constant which depends only on
\lambda
, but not on
a
and
b
.
Now, we define

f_{n}:=\frac{f_{n^{2},n^{2}+n}}{\|f_{n^{2},n^{2}+n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}}.

In this way
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}=1
, the supports are disjoint and, at the same time, the support of
f_{n}
leaves any bounded set for all sufficiently large
n
.
Hence, we conclude that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
and moreover it follows from the bounds (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), (
3.7
) that

\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{2})}\rightarrow 0,\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, for all sufficiently large
n
we have
\mathrm{supp}\,f_{n}\subset\Omega^{\rm ext}
. Therefore, we infer that
f_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}\,(-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}})
and, in conclusion,
f_{n}
is a Weyl sequence for the operator
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}
corresponding to the point
\lambda
. Thus,
\lambda\in\sigma_{\rm ess}\left(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}\right)
. Since
\lambda\in[\frac{1}{4},\infty)
was arbitrary, we obtain

\left[\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)\subseteq\sigma_{\rm ess}\left(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}\right).

Step 2: the inclusion
\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}})\subseteq\left[\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
.

In order to show the opposite inclusion,
we use a Neumann bracketing argument. Let us introduce the notation
|x|:=d_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(x,O)
for
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
. Let
H_{n}
be the operator that acts as
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}
but satisfies an extra Neumann condition on the circle
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}\colon|x|=n\}
of the radius
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. More specifically,
H_{n}
is the self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
associated with the form

h_{n}[u]:=\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}+\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})},\quad\mathrm{dom}\,(h_{n}):=W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\setminus{\mathcal{C}}_{n}).

Because of the domain inclusion
\mathrm{dom}\,(h_{n})\supset\mathrm{dom}\,(Q^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha})
, we have
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}\geq H_{n}
in the sense of ordering of the corresponding sesquilinear forms and, by the min-max principle,

\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha})\geq\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{n})\quad\text{for all }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Assuming that
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
is sufficiently large so that
\Omega
is contained in the disk
B_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}\colon|x|<n\}
,
H_{n}
decouples into an orthogonal sum of two operators,
H_{n}=H^{(1)}_{n}\oplus H^{(2)}_{n}
with respect to the decomposition
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})=L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n})\oplus L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\setminus\overline{B_{n})}
. Here
H^{(1)}_{n}
and
H^{(2)}_{n}
are respectively the operators in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n})
and
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\setminus\overline{B_{n}})
associated with the forms

\displaystyle h_{n}^{(1)}[u]

\displaystyle:=\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n})}+\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,(h_{n}^{(1)})

\displaystyle:=W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n}),

\displaystyle h_{n}^{(2)}[u]

\displaystyle:=\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\setminus\overline{B_{n}})},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,(h_{n}^{(2)})

\displaystyle:=W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\setminus\overline{B_{n}}).

Since
\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n}
is a Lipschitz bounded open set, the embedding of
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n})
into
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext}\cap B_{n})
is compact and the spectrum of
H_{n}^{(1)}
is purely discrete. Consequently,

\displaystyle\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\geq\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{n}^{(2)})\geq\inf\sigma(H_{n}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,2}(B_{n}^{\rm ext})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}^{\rm ext}}|\nabla u(r,\theta)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}{\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}^{\rm ext}}|u(r,\theta)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,2}(B_{n}^{\rm ext})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{n}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}u(r,\theta)\right|^{2}\sinh(r){\,\mathrm{d}}r{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{n}^{\infty}|u(r,\theta)|^{2}\sinh(r){\,\mathrm{d}}r{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta}\geq\frac{1}{4},

where we used density of
C^{1}_{c}(\overline{B_{n}^{\rm ext}})
in
W^{1,2}(B_{n}^{\rm ext})
(see Lemma
C.2
) and where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.3
applied with
\alpha=0
. Indeed, for a given
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, we set
\varphi(r):=u(r,\theta)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{B_{n}^{\text{ext}}})
, then we have

\int_{n}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\sinh(r)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}r\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{n}^{\infty}|\varphi(r)|^{2}\sinh(r){\,\mathrm{d}}r.

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

Since the essential spectrum is non-empty, it is not necessary that
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
represents a discrete eigenvalue. Using standard arguments, we prove that the quantity
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
is non-decreasing in
\alpha
and tends to
-\infty
as
\alpha\rightarrow-\infty
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The function

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\in\mathbb{R}

is non-decreasing. Moreover,

\lim_{\alpha\to-\infty}\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})=-\infty.

Proof.

Given
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}
and a minimising sequence
\varphi_{n}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
for the operator
-\Delta^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha_{1}}
corresponding to its lowest spectral point
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
, then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n>n_{0}
, the following holds

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(\Omega^{\rm ext})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}\absolutevalue{\nabla\varphi_n}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}\absolutevalue{\nabla\varphi_n}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha_{2}\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}(\Omega^{\rm ext}).

To prove the second part of the statement, we fix a test function
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
such that
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}\absolutevalue{\nabla\varphi}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}\quad\xrightarrow{\alpha\to-\infty}\quad-\infty.\qed

Below we recall the definition from the introduction of the critical boundary parameter.

Definition 3.6
.

We introduce the critical value
\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
, defined as

\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}):=\sup\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,\colon\,\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})\text{ is a discrete eigenvalue}\}.

Remark 3.7
.

From Lemma
3.5
and Proposition
3.4
,
we deduce that if
\alpha
is sufficiently negative, then the Robin Laplacian operator has discrete eigenvalues. Consequently, Definition
3.6
is well posed, since the set over which the supremum is taken is nonempty. Lemma
3.5
further ensures that for every
\alpha<\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
, the operator
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}
admits discrete eigenvalues.

In order to prove that
\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
is non-positive when
\Omega
is geodesically convex, we first establish in the next two lemmas certain weighted Poincaré-type inequalities with a boundary term,
in the spirit of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
b>0
and
\alpha\geq-2^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([b,\infty))\\
\psi\not=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{t}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha e^{b}|\psi(b)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}e^{t}{\,\mathrm{d}}t}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([b,\infty))
.
We set
\varphi(t):=\psi(t)e^{\frac{t}{2}}
. Then we get

\psi^{\prime}(t)=\varphi^{\prime}(t)e^{-\frac{t}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(t)e^{-\frac{t}{2}}.

Hence, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{t}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|\varphi^{\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(t)\right|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{b}^{\infty}{\rm Re}\,\big(\varphi^{\prime}(t)\overline{\varphi(t)}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t.

Note that using integration by parts we get

-\int_{b}^{\infty}{\rm Re}\,\big(\varphi^{\prime}(t)\overline{\varphi(t)}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}t=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}}t}|\varphi(t)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t=\frac{1}{2}|\varphi(b)|^{2}.

Thus, we end up with

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{t}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha e^{b}|\psi(b)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)|\varphi(b)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}e^{t}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t.\qed

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
b\geq 0
, and
\alpha\geq-2^{-1}\tanh(b)
. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([b,\infty))\\
\psi\not=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\cosh(b)|\psi(b)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

For any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}([b,\infty))
not identically zero,
we set
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{\cosh(t)}\,\psi(t)
.
Following the lines of the proof of the previous lemma
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\cosh(t)}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varphi\sinh(t)}{\cosh^{3/2}(t)}\right|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}\left|\varphi^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\varphi\tanh(t)\right|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t-\frac{1}{2}\int_{b}^{\infty}(|\varphi|^{2})^{\prime}\tanh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi|^{2}\tanh^{2}(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{\tanh(b)|\varphi(b)|^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}\left(\tanh^{2}(t)+\frac{2}{\cosh^{2}(t)}\right)|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t.

Using the identity for hyperbolic functions

\tanh^{2}(t)+\frac{2}{\cosh^{2}(t)}=1+\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(t)},

we finally obtain

\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\cosh(b)|\psi(b)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\left(\alpha+\frac{\tanh(b)}{2}\right)|\varphi(b)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\varphi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{b}^{\infty}|\psi|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t.\qed

Now we are in position to state and prove a theorem on the sign of the critical coupling constant for exteriors of geodesically convex sets in the hyperbolic plane.

Theorem 3.10

Assume that

Proof.

\mathcal{L}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty))

C^{\infty}
with respect to the variable
t

.
According to
[
34

, in parallel geodesic coordinates,
the pullback metric expression is given by

{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{2}=J^{2}(s,t){\,\mathrm{d}}s^{2}+{\,\mathrm{d}}t^{2}.

The map

1

1

(3.8)

where
\kappa(s)

The Jacobian of the transformation

Thus, for any

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}|\psi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\psi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}|(\nabla\psi)(\mathcal{L}(s,t))|^{2}J(s,t){\,\mathrm{d}}t{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}|\psi(\mathcal{L}(s,0))|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(\mathcal{L}(s,t))|^{2}J(s,t){\,\mathrm{d}}t{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)}

By setting

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}|\psi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\psi|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}\geq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}(s,t)\right|^{2}J(s,t){\,\mathrm{d}}t{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}|\eta(s,0)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\eta(s,t)|^{2}J(s,t){\,\mathrm{d}}t{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)}.

We split
\partial\Omega

U_{3}

-\kappa(s)=\displaystyle\frac{\sinh(z_1(s))}{\cosh(z_1(s))},

If

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}(s,t)\right|^{2}J(s,t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{U_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}(s,t)\right|^{2}e^{t}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}|\eta(s,0)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\eta(s,t)|^{2}J(s,t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}t\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(s)

Arguing in a similar way, if we assume respectively that

Then for any

Remark 3.11
.

On the contrary to Theorem

, for some non-convex simply-connected domains in the hyperbolic plane we conjecture that

\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})>0
. The example supporting this conjecture can be constructed as follows. Consider first the exterior of a geodesic annulus
A_{r,R}\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
centred at the origin with radii
R>r>0
and define the Robin Laplacian on

. This operator can be naturally decomposed into orthogonal sum of two Robin Laplacians, one of which is defined
on the geodesic disk

B_{r}
while the other is defined on

. We can ensure that for a sufficiently small positive Robin parameter
\alpha>0
the component of the operator on

has an eigenvalue below

. In order to see that

and

we can construct a connected exterior domain such that the Robin Laplacian with a sufficiently small positive Robin parameter has an eigenvalue below

. The final step in the construction can be justified by showing that in the limit of a thin channel there holds a generalised norm resolvent convergence to the decoupled case. This analysis goes beyond the scope of the present paper.

4.
The spectral problem in the exterior of a disk

In this section, we establish some properties of

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\mathrm{ext}})
,
where
\alpha
is negative and
B_{R}
is the geodesic disk of radius
R>0
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

4.1.
Separation of variables

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
the centre of
B_{R}
has coordinates
(1,0,0)
in the hyperboloid model of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
. By employing geodesic polar coordinates,
we introduce the following complete family of mutually orthogonal projections

\displaystyle\Pi_{n}\colon L^{2}(B_{R}^{\mathrm{ext}})\rightarrow L^{2}(B_{R}^{\mathrm{ext}}),\qquad(\Pi_{n}u)(r,\theta):=\frac{e^{{\mathsf{i}}n\theta}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(r,\theta^{\prime})e^{-{\mathsf{i}}n\theta^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta^{\prime},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

and the unitary mappings

\displaystyle{\mathsf{U}}_{n}\colon{\rm ran\,}\Pi_{n}\rightarrow L^{2}((R,\infty);\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r),\qquad({\mathsf{U}}_{n}u)(r)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(r,\theta^{\prime})e^{-{\mathsf{i}}n\theta^{\prime}}{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta^{\prime},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore, it is possible to write
L^{2}(B_{R}^{\mathrm{ext}})
as an orthogonal sum

\displaystyle L^{2}(B_{R}^{\mathrm{ext}})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{ran }\Pi_{n}\simeq\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L^{2}((R,\infty);\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r).

With the help of
[
61
, Proposition 1.15]
, it can be proved that each summand in the above orthogonal sum is a reducing subspace for the operator
-\Delta^{B_{R}^{\text{ext}}}_{\alpha}
. This induces an orthogonal decomposition of the Robin Laplacian

\displaystyle-\Delta^{B_{R}^{\text{ext}}}_{\alpha}\simeq\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}({\mathsf{U}}_{n}^{-1}{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,n}{\mathsf{U}}_{n}),

where the self-adjoint fibre operators
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,n}
in
L^{2}((R,\infty);\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r)
are associated with the closed, symmetric, densely defined and semi-bounded quadratic forms

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha,R,n}[f]:

\displaystyle=\int_{R}^{\infty}\left(\absolutevalue{f'(r)}^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{\sinh^{2}{r}}\absolutevalue{f(r)}^{2}\right)\sinh{r}{\,\mathrm{d}}r+\alpha\sinh{R}\,\absolutevalue{f(R)}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha,R,n}:

\displaystyle=\big\{f\in L^{2}((R,\infty);\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r)\,\colon\,{\mathsf{U}}_{n}^{-1}f\in W^{1,2}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})\}

\displaystyle=\{f\,\colon\,f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}((R,\infty);\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r)\}.

The action and the domain of the fibre operators associated with the above sesquilinear form can be characterised as

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,n}

\displaystyle=-f^{\prime\prime}(r)-\coth(r)f^{\prime}(r)+\frac{n^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(r)}f(r),

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,n}

\displaystyle=\left\{f\,\colon\,f,f^{\prime\prime}+\coth(r)f^{\prime}\in L^{2}((R,\infty);\sinh r{\,\mathrm{d}}r),f^{\prime}(R)=\alpha f(R)\right\}.

The form
\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha,R,0}
is the smallest in the sense of the ordering of forms and from the min-max principle, we get that the lowest spectral point of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,0}
is not larger than that of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,n}
, for any
n\not=0
.
Moreover, if the lowest spectral point of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,0}

is a discrete eigenvalue, then by the min-max principle it is strictly smaller than the lowest spectral point of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,n}
for any
n\not=0
. By construction, the lowest spectral point of
-\Delta^{B_{R}^{\text{ext}}}_{\alpha}
is the same of the fibre operator
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,0}
and moreover, the corresponding eigenfunction is radial provided this lowest spectral point is a discrete eigenvalue.

4.2.
Explicit expression of the first eigenfunction

In order to express the eigenfunctions of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\alpha,R,0}

in terms of special functions we analyse the following ordinary differential equation

(4.1)

-y^{\prime\prime}(x)-\coth(x)\,y^{\prime}(x)=k\,y(x),\qquad x\in[R,\infty),\quad k<\frac{1}{4}.

By performing the substitution

t=\cosh(x),

we obtain a differential equation on
y
in terms of variable
t

-(t^{2}-1)\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}^{2}y}{{\,\mathrm{d}}t^{2}}-2t\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}y}{{\,\mathrm{d}}t}=ky.

This second-order linear differential equation is of the form of the Legendre equation. The general solution is given by

y(t)=AP^{0}_{\nu}(t)+BQ^{0}_{\nu}(t),

where
P^{0}_{\nu}(t)
,
Q^{0}_{\nu}(t)
are respectively the Legendre functions of the first and second kind (see
[
56
, Chapter 14]
) with
\mu=0
and
\nu
satisfying

\nu(\nu+1)=-k,

which gives

\nu^{2}+\nu+k=0\Rightarrow\nu=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4k}}{2}.

In order to get two linearly independent solutions
[
56
, Subsection 14.2(iii)]
, we can choose the value

(4.2)

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1-4k}}{2}>-\frac{1}{2}.

In conclusion, the general solution of the original equation is

y(x)=AP^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(x))+BQ^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(x)),\quad\text{with }\nu=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1-4k}}{2}.

At this point, it is necessary to analyse the
L^{2}
-summability of the solution
with respect to the measure
\sinh(x){\,\mathrm{d}}x
on the interval
[R,\infty)
.
To this aim, we recall some asymptotic results. Clearly

\cosh x\sim\frac{e^{x}}{2},\qquad\sinh x\sim\frac{e^{x}}{2},

for large
x
.
According to
[
56
, Formulas 14.3.10, 14.8.12, 14.8.15]
, we have in the limits
z,x\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle P^{0}_{\nu}(z)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{{\pi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\Gamma(\nu+1)}(2z)^{\nu}\qquad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle P^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh x)\sim\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{{\pi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\nu+1)}e^{\nu x},

\displaystyle Q^{0}_{\nu}(z)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\pi^{1/2}\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{3}{2})(2z)^{\nu+1}}\qquad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh x)\sim\frac{\pi^{1/2}\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{3}{2})}e^{-(\nu+1)x}.

Combining the asymptotic estimates, it follows that
in the limit
x\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle|P^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh x)|^{2}\sinh(x)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{{\pi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\right)^{2}e^{(2\nu+1)x},

\displaystyle|Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh x)|^{2}\sinh(x)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\pi^{1/2}\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{3}{2})}\right)^{2}e^{(-2\nu-1)x}.

Since we are looking for square-summable eigenfunctions, the choice of
\nu
imposes
A=0
. Finally, the expression of the original solution simplifies to

(4.3)

y(x)=BQ^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(x)),

with
B\not=0
for non-trivial solutions.

We will make use of the following properties of the Legendre function of second kind. The function
Q^{0}_{\nu}
admits an integral representation
[
56
, Formulas 14.3.10, 14.12.6]

(4.4)

\displaystyle Q_{\nu}^{0}(x)=\frac{\pi^{1/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\cosh t\right)^{\nu+1}}{\,\mathrm{d}}t,\qquad\text{for }\quad x>1,\,\nu+1>0,

from which it is clear that it is a positive, strictly decreasing, smooth function. Moreover,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{\nu+1}^{0}(x)}{Q_{\nu}^{0}(x)}\leq\frac{1}{x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}},\qquad\text{for }x>1.

Derivatives can be computed using recurrence relations
[
56
, Formula 14.10.4]

(4.6)

\displaystyle(1-x^{2})\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}}x}Q^{0}_{\nu}(x)=(\nu+1)xQ^{0}_{\nu}(x)-(\nu+1)Q^{0}_{\nu+1}(x).

4.3.
The critical Robin boundary parameter

Next, we provide a sharper upper bound for the critical value
\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})
, improving upon the one given in Theorem
3.10
in the case of the geodesic disk.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
R>0
, it holds

\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{{\rm ext}})\leq\frac{1}{2}(e^{-R}-\coth(R)).

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha\leq 0
is such that the operator
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{B^{\rm ext}_{R}}
has a discrete eigenvalue below the bottom of its essential spectrum. The eigenfunction corresponding to its lowest eigenvalue is radially symmetric and as a function of the radial variable can be identified with the function given by identity (
4.3
).
Starting from the boundary condition

-y^{\prime}(R)+\alpha y(R)=0,

and applying, respectively, relations (
4.6
), (
4.2
), and (
4.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=-(\nu+1)\left[\frac{\cosh(R)Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(R))-Q^{0}_{\nu+1}(\cosh(R))}{\sinh(R)Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(R))}\right]

\displaystyle=(\nu+1)\left[\frac{Q^{0}_{\nu+1}(\cosh(R))}{Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(R))}-\coth(R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq(\nu+1)\left[e^{-R}-\coth(R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{-R}-\coth(R)\right],

where in the last step we used that
e^{-R}-\coth(R)<0

and that
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The given upper bound is a negative function. Moreover, it diverges to
-\infty
as
R\to 0
and converges to
-\frac{1}{2}
as
R\to+\infty.

Direct consequences are

\limsup_{R\to\infty}\,\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})\leq-\frac{1}{2},\qquad\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})\,\,\xrightarrow{R\to 0}\,\,-\infty.

Remark 4.3
.

We observe that if
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})>0
, then
\frac{1}{2}<\nu+1<1
. This imposes a constraint on the parameter
\alpha
, allowing us to derive a lower bound for it

\displaystyle\alpha=(\nu+1)\left[\frac{Q^{0}_{\nu+1}(\cosh(R))}{Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(R))}-\coth(R)\right]>-(\nu+1)\coth(R)>-\coth(R).

4.4.
The monotonicity of the lowest eigenvalue with respect to radius

In this subsection, we establish that the lowest eigenvalue of the Robin Laplacian in the exterior of the geodesic disk in the hyperbolic plane is a decreasing function of the radius. This property was previously observed in the case of the Robin Laplacian in the Euclidean plane
[
49
, Proposition 5]
. The case of the hyperbolic plane is more subtle, because the lowest eigenvalue can also be positive for moderate negative Robin parameters.

Proposition 4.4

(Monotonicity property)
.

Assume that
0<R<\overline{R}
and that
\alpha<\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})
. Then

(4.7)

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})>\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{\overline{R}}^{\rm ext}).

Proof.

Step 1: variational characterisation of the lowest spectral point.

From the analysis in previous subsections of this section, we have
the following variational characterisation of the lowest eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{B_{R}^{\rm ext}}

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\in W^{1,2}([R,\infty),\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)\\
\psi\neq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\sinh(R)|\psi(R)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\in W^{1,2}([0,\infty),\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)\\
\psi\neq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t+R){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\,\sinh(R)|\psi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}.

The infimum is achieved by any eigenfunction corresponding to the lowest eigenvalue. In particular, by the analysis in Subsection
4.2
, we can choose the ground state in the form
\varphi(t)=Q^{0}_{\nu}(\cosh(t+R))
with
\nu
related to the lowest eigenvalue by the identity (
4.2
) with
k=\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})
.

Step 2: monotonicity and convexity of
\varphi
.

Differentiating the integral representation (
4.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle(Q_{\nu}^{0})^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{1/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(\nu+1)\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\cosh(z)\right)}{\left(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\cosh(z)\right)^{\nu+2}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}z.

Using the above formula we compute

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{1/2}(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\sinh(t+R)+\cosh(t+R)\cosh(z)\right)}{\left(\cosh(t+R)+\sinh(t+R)\cosh(z)\right)^{\nu+2}}{\,\mathrm{d}}z<0,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{1/2}(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh^{2}(t+R)\big[(2+\nu)(\cosh(z)+\tanh(R + t))^{2}-(1+\cosh(z)\tanh(R + t))^{2}\big]}{\left(\cosh(t+R)+\sinh(t+R)\cosh(z)\right)^{\nu+3}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}z.

In order to determine the sign of
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
,
it is enough to study the function

\displaystyle f(t):=\sqrt{\nu+2}(\cosh(z)+\tanh(R + t))-(1+\cosh(z)\tanh(R + t)).

Since
\sqrt{\nu+2}>\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}>1
and
0\leq\tanh(t+R)<1
, we get

f(t)>\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\tanh(R + t)-1+\cosh(z)\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}-\tanh(R + t)\right).

By setting
w:=\tanh(R+t)
,
the linear function

A(w)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}w-1+\cosh(z)\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}-w\right)

satisfies

A(0)=-1+\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\cosh(z)>0,\qquad A(1)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}-1+\cosh(z)\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}-1\right)>0,

therefore

f(t)>0
,
from which it follows
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
.

Step 3: a sufficient condition for monotonicity of the eigenvalue.

Given
s\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{sR}^{\text{ext}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\frac{\sinh(t+sR)}{\sinh(sR)}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\frac{\sinh(t+sR)}{\sinh(sR)}{\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\coth(sR)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\coth(sR)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}.

We denote by
g(s)
the last expression and we define the following quantities

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t;

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2};

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t;

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t.

Then

g^{\prime}(s)=R\dfrac{bc-ad}{\left(c\cosh(sR)+d\sinh(sR)\right)^{2}}.

The proof ends by showing that
g^{\prime}(s)<0
for
s\geq 1,
since in this case

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{sR}^{\text{ext}})\leq g(s)<g(1)=\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}}),\quad\text{for }s>1.

We have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(s)<0\quad\textit{if, and only if,}\,\quad\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}<\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}.

Step 4: a verification of the sufficient condition for monotonicity.

It is not restrictive to assume

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}>0,

otherwise the proof would be complete. Employing strict monotonicity
and strict convexity of
\varphi
we get

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\cosh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\,|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle<\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\varphi^{\prime}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(t)e^{-t}{\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle<\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\varphi^{\prime}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\,|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\sinh(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t},

where in the last step we used the boundary condition
-\varphi^{\prime}(0)+\alpha\varphi(0)=0.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

An immediate consequence is that, if
0<R<\overline{R}
, then
\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})\leq\alpha_{\star}(B_{\overline{R}}^{\text{ext}})
. Clearly, inequality (
4.7
) remains valid for
\alpha
such that
\alpha_{\star}(B_{\overline{R}}^{\text{ext}})>\alpha\geq\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})

5.
Main result

In this section, we will state and prove the main result of this paper on
comparison between the lowest Robin eigenvalue in the exterior of a geodesically convex bounded open set in the hyperbolic plane and the lowest Robin eigenvalue in the exterior of a geodesic disk. We also remark that this theorem generalises the monotonicity property in Proposition
4.4
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
be an open, bounded and geodesically convex set. Let
B_{R}\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a geodesic disk of radius
R
such that

\coth(R)\geq\frac{|\Omega|+2\pi}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

then, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext}).

Proof.

If
\alpha\geq\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})
, then the claim easily follows from the fact that

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})=\frac{1}{4}.

We now consider the case
\alpha<\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})
. Let
\rho\colon\Omega^{\rm ext}\mapsto(0,\infty)
be the hyperbolic distance
function from the boundary of
\Omega
.
Furthermore, we define the auxiliary function by

\displaystyle L\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\qquad

\displaystyle L(r)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(\{x\in\Omega^{\rm ext}\,\colon\,\rho(x)=r\}\right).

Note that
L(r)
can be understood for every
r>0
as the perimeter of the outer parallel body
\Omega_{r}
, according to Theorem
B.5
.
The Steiner’s formula guarantees that
L(r)=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega_{r})
for almost all
r
.
Given a function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))
, we define the function

u:=\psi\circ\rho\colon\overline{\Omega^{\rm ext}}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
.
Since
\rho
is Lipschitz continuous, the function
u
is Lipschitz continuous and compactly supported in
\overline{\Omega^{\rm ext}}
. Thus, we have
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
, and by applying the co-area formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}L(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t\,,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}L(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t\,,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega^{\rm ext})}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial\Omega)\,|\psi(0)|^{2}\,.

In the following, we denote by
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
the ground state of
-\Delta_{\alpha}^{B_{R}^{\rm ext}}
as function of the distance to the boundary of the geodesic disk. An explicit representation of this function is given in Step 1 of the proof of Proposition
4.4
.
By the min-max principle applied
for the quadratic form
Q^{\Omega^{\rm ext}}_{\alpha}

on the subspace
\{\psi\circ\rho\colon\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))\}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
, we get
using the identity in Theorem
2.5

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))\\
\psi\neq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}L(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)|\psi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}L(t){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))\\
\psi\neq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|\Omega|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\psi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|\Omega|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|\Omega|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|\Omega|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|B_{R}|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R})}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|B_{R}|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R})}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))\\
\psi\neq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|B_{R}|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R})}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\psi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi+|B_{R}|}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R})}\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{smallmatrix}\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))\\
\psi\neq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R+t}){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R})|\psi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(t)|^{2}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R+t}){\,\mathrm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\text{ext}})\,,

where we have, respectively, used the identity

\coth(R)=\frac{|B_{R}|+2\pi}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R})},

the fact that the infimum in the variational characterisation of
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})
(given in Step 1 of Proposition
4.4
)
is attained on the function
\varphi
, and the strict monotonic increase of the function

g(s)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\bigl(s\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\bigr){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\alpha|\varphi(0)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\bigl(s\sinh(t)+\cosh(t)\bigr){\,\mathrm{d}}t},

for
s\in(0,\infty)
. The latter property follows by computing the first derivative of
g

and arguing exactly as in Steps 3 and 4 of Proposition
4.4
. Finally, we employed the density
result of Lemma
C.3
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
be an open, bounded and geodesically convex set. If

\frac{|\Omega|+2\pi}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq 1,

then, for any
\alpha<\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
and
R>0
, it holds

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})<\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext}).

Corollary 5.3
.

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\inf_{R>0}\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R}^{\rm ext})>-\infty.

In the context of Faber-Krahn type inequalities, it is common to make comparisons using either the disk of equal measure or the disk of equal perimeter as extremal sets. However, our approach deviates from this traditional choice, as we adopt a different comparison that allows for a sharper estimate. The isoperimetric and the isochoric spectral inequalities follow as a consequence
of our stronger result.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
be an open, bounded and geodesically convex set. Let
B\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a geodesic disk such that either
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial\Omega)={\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\partial B)
or
|\Omega|=|B|
, then it holds

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B^{\rm ext}),\qquad\text{for all}\,\,\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

Denoting by
B_{R^{\star}}
,
B_{R^{\sharp}}
the geodesic disks having respectively the same perimeter and area of
\Omega
, the geometric isoperimetric inequality ensures that
R^{\sharp}\leq R^{\star}
. Using the isoperimetric inequality in the hyperbolic plane again, we get

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|+2\pi}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\frac{|B_{R^{\star}}|+2\pi}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B_{R^{\star}})}=\coth(R^{\star})\leq\coth(R^{\sharp}).

Therefore, Theorem
5.1
implies

\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R^{\star}}^{\text{ext}})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(B_{R^{\sharp}}^{\text{ext}}).\qed

Another interesting optimisation result concerning the critical coupling parameter follows immediately from Theorem
5.1
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
be an open, bounded and geodesically convex set. Denote by
B_{R}\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
a geodesic disk of radius
R
such that

\coth(R)\geq\frac{|\Omega|+2\pi}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

then it holds

\alpha_{\star}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\geq\alpha_{\star}(B_{R}^{\rm ext}).

Appendix A
Convexity of sets in Riemannian manifolds

In this appendix, we summarise some general notions of convexity in Riemannian manifolds introduced in
[
21
,
62
]
, see also
[
2
]
for further details, and provide some of their properties.

Definition A.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and let
C\subset M
. We say that:

(a)

C
is
weakly convex
if for every
p,q\in C
, there exists a minimal geodesic
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
connecting
p
and
q
such that
\gamma([a,b])\subseteq C
;

(b)

C
is
strongly convex
if for every
p,q\in C
there exists a unique minimal geodesic
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
connecting
p
and
q
in
M
, and
\gamma([a,b])\subseteq C
;

(c)

C
is
locally convex
if for every
p\in\overline{C}
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
C\cap B_{\varepsilon}(p)
is strongly convex, where
B_{\varepsilon}(p)
is the metric ball of radius
\varepsilon
centred at
p
;

(d)

Suppose further that
C
is a domain with
C^{2}
-smooth boundary. Then
C
is
strictly convex
if the second fundamental form
\mathrm{II}
of
\partial C
with respect to the outward unit normal
\nu
is positive definite at every point
p\in\partial C
:

\mathrm{II}_{p}(\xi,\xi)=\langle\nabla_{\xi}\nu,\xi\rangle>0,\quad\forall\xi\in T_{p}(\partial C)\setminus\{0\}.

We also recall the notion of supporting element (see
[
21
,
2
]
).

Definition A.2
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
C\subseteq M
be an open set. For any point
p\in\partial C
and a tangent vector
\nu\in T_{p}M
,
consider the half-space

H_{p}=\Set{\xi\in T_{p}M\,\colon\,\langle\nu,\xi\rangle<0}.

(i)

H_{p}
is a
supporting element
for
C
in
p
, if for every
q
in the interior of
C
and for every minimal geodesic

\gamma:[0,1]\to M

satisfying
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
, it holds that
\gamma^{\prime}(0)\in H_{p}
;

(ii)

H_{p}
is a
locally supporting element
for
C
in
p
if there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that
H_{p}
is a supporting element for
U\cap C
in
p
.

We have the following characterisation of weak convexity, originally proved in
[
2
, Proposition 2]
. We recall that for every point
p\in M
,
\cut(p)
denotes the cut-locus of
p
.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
C\subset M
be a connected open subset of a Riemannian manifold
M
. Then
C
is weakly convex if and only if for every point

there exists a locally supporting element at

, and the set

We also have that a locally supporting element always exists for open, locally convex sets. Indeed, Cheeger and Gromoll in
[
21
, Theorem 1.6, Lemma 1.7]
proved the following result.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, and let

be a non-empty, open, locally convex set. Then
\partial C
is an embedded
(n-1)
-dimensional topological submanifold of
M
, and it has a supporting element in every point
p\in\partial C
.

In the original work, the authors considered closed sets; however, it is evident that if
C
is locally convex, then its closure
\overline{C}
is also locally convex, and moreover, the boundary satisfies
\partial C=\partial\overline{C}
. Additionally, by definition, any supporting element for
\overline{C}
is also a supporting element for
C
.

We provide an approximation result proved by Bangert in
[
9
, Corollary 2.5, Corollary 2.6]
.

Theorem A.5
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
C\subset M
be a connected, compact, locally convex set with non-empty interior. Assuming either that

(a)

the sectional curvatures are positive on

;

(b)

the sectional curvatures are negative on
C
;

then there exists a sequence of connected, compact, locally convex sets

with

boundaries such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\big(d^{H}(C_{k},C)+d^{H}(\partial C_{k},\partial C)\big)=0.

We also recall the following property of locally convex sets in Riemannian manifolds proved in
[
64
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Theorem A.6
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
C\subset M
be a closed, locally convex set. Then

has strongly Lipschitz boundary.

Appendix B
Steiner’s formula

Given a Riemannian manifold
M
, for each point
p\in M
and
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}(p)
the metric ball centred at
p
with radius
r
. For sufficiently small
r
, this metric ball coincides with the geodesic ball
\exp_{p}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(0))
, where
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(0)
is the metric ball in the tangent plane
T_{p}M
. Following
[
45
]
, we introduce below the concepts of metric projection and the reach.

Definition B.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and let
\Omega\subset M
be a non-empty set. For any point
p\in M
, a
metric projection
of
p
onto
\Omega
is a point
q\in\Omega
such that

d(p,q)=\inf_{x\in\Omega}d(p,x).

When this point is unique, we denote it by
\sigma_{\Omega}(p)
.

Definition B.2
.

For a given
q\in\Omega
, the
reach
of
q
with respect to
\Omega
is defined as the supremum of radii
r>0
such that every point
p
in the ball
B_{r}(q)
admits a unique metric projection onto
\Omega
:

\mathcal{R}(q)=\sup\{r>0\,\colon\,\forall p\in B_{r}(q),\text{ the metric projection of }p\text{ onto }\Omega\text{ is unique}\}.

We then define the
reach
of the set
\Omega
as

\mathcal{R}(\Omega)=\inf_{q\in\Omega}\mathcal{R}(q).

The set
\Omega
is said to have
positive reach
if
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)>0
.

Remark B.3
.

A set
\Omega\subset M
with positive reach is closed. Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists
x\in\partial\Omega\setminus\Omega
, and let

y
be its unique metric projection onto
\Omega
. Then any disk centred at
x
with radius
R<d(x,y)
does not intersect
\Omega
, which leads to an absurd.

It is a well-known fact that in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, any convex set
C
has infinite reach, i.e.,

\mathcal{R}(C)=+\infty.

When considering simply-connected space forms, related but nuanced results hold for connected, compact, locally convex sets. In particular, the following proposition
[
46
, Lemma 2.2]
, provides useful lower bounds for the reach in this setting.

Proposition B.4
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected space form with constant curvature
\kappa
, and let
C\subseteq M
be a connected, compact, locally convex set. We have:

(i)

If
\kappa<0
, then

\mathcal{R}(C)=+\infty;

(ii)

If
\kappa>0
, then

\mathcal{R}(C)\geq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian
n
-manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa
. We introduce the functions

\sn_{\kappa}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\kappa}}\sinh(\sqrt{-\kappa}t)&\text{if }\kappa<0,\\
t&\text{if }\kappa=0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sin(\sqrt{\kappa}t)&\text{if }\kappa>0,\\
\end{cases}

and
\cn_{\kappa}
=
\sn_{\kappa}^{\prime}
. For
1\leq j\leq n
, we define

L_{j}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\cn_{\kappa}^{n-j}(t)\sn_{\kappa}^{j-1}(t)\,dt,

and

L_{0}(t)=1.

The following result was proved by Kohlmann in
[
45
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Theorem B.5

(Steiner’s formula on simply-connected space forms)
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected space form of dimension
n
and curvature
\kappa
, and let
\Omega\subset M
be a set of positive reach. Let
U
be a suitable open neighbourhood of
\Omega
in which the metric projection
\sigma_{\Omega}
is well defined. For every
j=0,\dots,n
there exist coefficients
\Phi_{j}(\Omega)
such that the following hold: if
s>0
is such that

\overline{\Omega_{s}}\subset U
, then we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega_{s})=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\cn_{\kappa}^{r}(s)\sn_{\kappa}^{n-1-r}(s)\Phi_{r}(\Omega),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\absolutevalue{\Omega_s}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}L_{n-r}(s)\Phi_{r}(\Omega).

In particular,

\Phi_{n}(\Omega)=\absolutevalue{\Omega}.

Moreover, if
\partial\Omega
is a
C^{2}
-smooth, compact, embedded
(n-1)
-submanifold of
M
, then for every
r=0,\dots,n-1
we have

(B.1)

\Phi_{r}(\Omega)=\binom{n-1}{r}\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{n-1-r}(p){\,\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(p),

where
H_{j}
denote the normalised
j
-th homogeneous symmetric function of the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
.

For sets with
C^{2}
-smooth boundary, the formulation of the curvature measures
\Phi_{r}
provided in
[
45
, Theorem 2.7]
is equivalent to (
B.1
), as shown in
[
1
, Remark 2.44]
. For an explicit definition of the functions
H_{j}
, we refer to
[
45
, Equation 2.13]
.

Remark B.6
.

Observe that when
\kappa=0
, we get the Steiner polynomial

\absolutevalue{\Omega_s}=\absolutevalue{\Omega}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{s^{k}}{k}\Phi_{n-k}(\Omega).

Remark B.7
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected space form. If
\Omega\subset M
is a set of positive reach with strongly Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
, then it holds that

\Phi_{n-1}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega).

In the Euclidean case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the standard metric, this equality is classical (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 2.106]
). For a general simply-connected space form, the equality follows by passing to normal coordinates and applying the Euclidean result locally (cf.
[
1
, Remark 2.45]
). Moreover, an application of Steiner’s Formula shows that

\Phi_{n-1}(\Omega)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\absolutevalue{\Omega_{s}}-\absolutevalue{\Omega}}{s}.

The right-hand side is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Minkowski content of
\Omega

[
59
, Definition 1.51]
. If
\Omega
has finite measure, using
[
59
, Theorem 1.52]
, we get

P(\Omega)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\absolutevalue{\Omega_{s}}-\absolutevalue{\Omega}}{s}=\Phi_{n-1}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega),

where
P(\Omega)
denotes the perimeter in the sense of De Giorgi
[
59
, Definition 1.37]
.

A continuity property for the curvature measures
\Phi_{j}
has been established in
[
46
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Theorem B.8
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected space form of dimension
n
and curvature
\kappa
. Let
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset M
be a sequence of compact sets with non-empty boundaries. Suppose there exists

and a compact set

\mathcal{R}(\Omega_{k})\geq\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}d^{H}(\Omega_{k},\Omega)=0.

Then, for every

\Phi_{r}(\Omega_{k})\to\Phi_{r}(\Omega),\qquad\text{as}\,\,k\rightarrow\infty.

Appendix C
Auxiliary properties of Sobolev spaces

In this appendix, we will provide some fine properties of

W^{1,2}
-functions on exterior domains in the hyperbolic plane.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
be a bounded, connected, Lipschitz domain. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that the inequality

\int_{\partial\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|\nabla u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu+C(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega^{\rm ext}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu

holds for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1: reduction to Sobolev functions with zero mean value.
We begin by proving the inequality for a bounded, Lipschitz, and connected set
U\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
. In this framework, following the reasoning in
[
22
, Section II, §4]
, we may assume without loss of generality that
the function
u\in W^{1,2}(U)
has zero mean value on
U
. Indeed, let

\langle u\rangle_{U}=\frac{1}{|U|}\int_{U}u{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu,\qquad v=u-\langle u\rangle_{U}.

Then
v
has zero mean and
\nabla v=\nabla u
.
By the reverse triangle inequality,

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial U)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial U)}^{2}-\big|\langle u\rangle_{U}\big|^{2}\,\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial U).

Applying the desired inequality to
v
, yields

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial U)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\|v\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}=\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\big\|u-\langle u\rangle_{U}\big\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}.

Next we estimate the mean term.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

|\langle u\rangle_{U}|^{2}=\frac{1}{|U|^{2}}\left|\int_{U}u{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu\right|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|U|}\|u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2},

and

\|u-\langle u\rangle_{U}\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}+2|\langle u\rangle_{U}|^{2}\,|U|\leq 4\|u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}.

Combining the previous estimates yields

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial U)}^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}+\left(8C(\varepsilon)+\frac{2\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial U)}{|U|}\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}.

Finally, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C^{\prime}(\varepsilon)>0
, depending only on
\varepsilon
and
U
, such that

(C.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial U)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}+C^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{2}.

holds for all
u\in W^{1,2}(U)
.

Step 2: proof of the inequality for a bounded, connected, Lipschitz set.
We introduce the subspace of
W^{1,2}(U)
consisting of functions with zero mean value, denoted by

\tilde{W}^{1,2}(U):=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}(U)\,\colon\,\langle u\rangle_{U}=0\right\}.

The Poincaré inequality
[
39
, Corollary A.1.2]
ensures that the seminorm defined by the
L^{2}
-norm of the gradient is a norm on
\tilde{W}^{1,2}(U)
, equivalent to the standard
W^{1,2}
-norm. Since the trace operator
\text{Tr}:W^{1,2}(U)\to L^{2}(\partial U)
is continuous
[
13
, Theorem 7.5]
, it follows from
[
55
, Theorem 2.1]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for every
u\in\tilde{W}^{1,2}(U)
, inequality (
C.1
) holds.

Step 3: proof of the inequality for the exterior of a bounded, Lipschitz and connected set.

We fix an open ball
B
containing
\overline{U}
and define
V:=B\setminus\overline{U}
. Given
u\in W^{1,2}(U^{\text{ext}})
then

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial U)}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial V)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(V)}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{L^{2}(V)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(U^{\text{ext}})}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{L^{2}(U^{\text{ext}})}^{2},

by which the lemma is proved.
∎

We also prove the following useful approximation result, which extends
[
8
, Theorem 2.9]
to exterior domains in the hyperbolic plane.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
is an open, bounded set with
C^{k}
-smooth boundary. Then
C^{k}_{c}(\overline{\Omega^{\rm ext}})
is dense in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.

Proof.

We observe that, according to
[
33
, Theorem 2.9]
, the subspace
C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
is dense in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We first show the density in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
of functions in
C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
that vanish outside a geodesic disk containing
\Omega
. Clearly, such functions are, in general, not smooth up to
\partial\Omega
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be a decreasing function such that
f(t)=1
for
t\leq 0

and
f(t)=0
for
t\geq 1
.
For a fixed point
y\in\Omega
, the triangle inequality ensures that the distance function
d_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(x,y)
is
1
-Lipschitz. Moreover, from (
2.2
), we get that
d_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(\cdot,y)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
. Given
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
, we claim that the sequence of functions

(C.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(x)=\varphi(x)f(d_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(x,y)-j),\qquad j\in{\mathbb{N}},

converges to
\varphi(x)
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
.
When
j\to\infty
,
\varphi_{j}(x)\to\varphi(x)
for all
x\in\Omega^{\rm ext}
. Moreover,

|\varphi_{j}|\leq|\varphi|
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
|\varphi|
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
. So by the Lebesgue dominated
convergence theorem,

\|\varphi_{j}-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}\to 0.

Moreover, when
j\to\infty
,
|\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)|\to|\nabla\varphi(x)|
for all
x\in\Omega^{\rm ext}
, and

|\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)|\leq|\nabla\varphi(x)|+|\varphi(x)|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|f^{\prime}(t)|,\qquad\text{for all}\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}

where the right-hand side belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
. Thus

\|\nabla(\varphi_{j}-\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})}\to 0.

Step 2.

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that any given function
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\rm ext})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
that vanishes outside a geodesic disk containing
\Omega
can be approximated in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\rm ext})
by functions in
C^{k}_{c}(\overline{\Omega^{\rm ext}})
.

Let
B
be a geodesic disk such that
B\supset\Omega
,
B\supset\mathrm{supp}\,\psi
and
d_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\partial B,\text{supp}(\psi))>0
. Since
\overline{B\setminus\Omega}
is a compact manifold with
C^{k}
-smooth boundary, it admits a finite
C^{k}
-atlas
\{(\Omega_{i},\varphi_{i})\}^{r}_{i=1}
, each
\Omega_{i}
is homeomorphic either to a disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, or, if it intersects the boundary, to a half-disk in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We choose the domains
\Omega_{j}
intersecting
\partial B
in such a way that they are disjoint with
\text{supp}(\psi)
. Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}
be a
C^{\infty}
partition of unity subordinate to the cover
\{\Omega_{i}\}
of
\overline{B\setminus\Omega}
. By definition,
\text{supp}(\alpha_{i})\subset\Omega_{i}
. Since each
\Omega_{i}
is open in the induced topology, it follows that
\text{supp}(\alpha_{i})\cap\partial\Omega_{i}\subseteq\partial\Omega_{i}\cap(\partial\Omega\cup\partial B)
. We have to approximate each
\alpha_{i}\psi
in the Sobolev norm by functions in
C^{k}(\overline{B\setminus\Omega})
which vanish on
\partial B
.

If
\Omega_{i}
is
homeomorphic to a disk or to a half–disk with
\Omega_{i}\cap\partial B\not=\emptyset
, then the function
\alpha_{i}\psi
, which is identically zero in the latter case, does not need to be approximated. Observe that in both cases
\alpha_{i}\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{i})
and
\alpha_{i}\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega_{i}
.

If
\Omega_{i}
is homeomorphic to a half-disk
D
with
\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega\not=\emptyset
, we can proceed as in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 2.9]
. We introduce the half-space
E:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,\colon\,x_{1}\leq 0\}
and without loss of generality, we assume
D=\mathring{B_{r}}(x)\cap E
with
x\in\partial E
. The sequence of functions
h_{m}
, defined for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
sufficiently large, as the restriction to
D
of

\big(\alpha_{i}\psi\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}\big)\big(x_{1}-\tfrac{1}{m},x_{2}\big),\quad(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathring{E},

converges to
\alpha_{i}\psi\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}
in
W^{1,2}(D)
. Clearly,
h_{m}\circ\varphi_{i}\in C^{k}(\Omega_{i})
. Since the metric tensor is bounded on
\Omega_{i}
, expressing the
W^{1,2}(\Omega_{i})
-norm in local coordinates via the chart
\varphi_{i}
shows that it is equivalent to the norm in
W^{1,2}(D)
. Therefore
h_{m}\circ\varphi_{i}
converges to
\alpha_{i}\psi
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega_{i})
. Observe that
\alpha_{i}\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega_{i}\setminus\partial\Omega
. Moreover,
\varphi_{i}
maps interior points of
\Omega_{i}
to interior points of
D
and points of
\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\Omega
to points of
D\cap\partial E
(see e.g.
[
53
, Theorem 1.37]
). As a consequence,
\text{supp}(h_{m})\cap(\partial D\setminus\partial E)=\emptyset
and therefore
\text{supp}(h_{m}\circ\varphi_{i})\cap(\partial\Omega_{i}\setminus\partial\Omega)=\emptyset
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, if
\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, we set
\eta_{i}:=\alpha_{i}\psi
; otherwise, we define
\eta_{i}:=h_{m_{i}}\circ\varphi_{i}
where
m_{i}
is chosen so that
\|h_{m_{i}}\circ\varphi_{i}-\alpha_{i}\psi\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{i})}<\varepsilon.
Defining

\eta:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\eta_{i},

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\eta-\psi\|_{W^{1,2}(B\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{r}\eta_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\psi\right\|_{W^{1,2}(B\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|\eta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\psi\|_{W^{1,2}(B\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|\eta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\psi\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq r\varepsilon.

From the construction, it is clear that
\eta\in C^{k}(\overline{B\setminus\Omega})
and
\eta=0
on
\partial B
.
∎

Lemma C.3
.

For any
R>0
, the space
C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))
is dense in
W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
be such that
\chi(t)=1
for all
t\in[0,1]
,
\chi(t)=0
for all
t\geq 2
and
0\leq\chi(t)\leq 1
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
Let
\psi\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)
be arbitrary.
Clearly, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have
\chi(\frac{\cdot}{n})\psi\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)
. Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Notice that there exists a sufficiently
large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(C.3)

\left\|\psi-\chi\left(\tfrac{\cdot}{n}\right)\psi\right\|_{W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)}<\varepsilon.

Next, we will use that the norms
W^{1,2}((0,2n))
and
W^{1,2}((0,2n);\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)
are equivalent, because the weight and its inverse are both bounded on the interval
[0,2n]
. Thus, by construction
\chi\left(\tfrac{\cdot}{n}\right)\psi\in\{f\in W^{1,2}((0,2n)\,\colon\,f(2n)=0\}
. Moreover, by standard
properties of (non-weighted) Sobolev spaces on the interval,
there exists a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,2n))
such that

(C.4)

\|\chi\left(\tfrac{\cdot}{n}\right)\psi-\phi\|_{W^{1,2}([0,2n];\sinh(R+t){\,\mathrm{d}}t)}<\varepsilon,

Combining (
C.3
) and (
C.4
) we get the desired density result.
∎

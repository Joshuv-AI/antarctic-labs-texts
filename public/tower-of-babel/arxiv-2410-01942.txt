Abstract
. In this work, we introduce a new class of algebras that we denominate skew-Brauer graph algebras. This class contains and generalizes the Brauer graph algebras. We establish that skew-Brauer graph algebras are symmetric and can be defined based on a Brauer graph with additional information. We show that the class of trivial extensions of skew-gentle algebras coincides with a subclass of skew-Brauer graph algebras, where the associated skew-Brauer graph has multiplicity function identically equal to one, generalizing a result over gentle algebras. Furthermore, we characterize skew-Brauer algebras of finite representation type. Finally, we provide a geometric interpretation of cut-sets and reflections of algebras using orbifold dissections.



1.
Introduction

Brauer graph algebras are defined combinatorially through a graph with some additional data on their vertices, given by a multiplicity function and an order over the edges attached to each vertex. They appeared first in representation theory of groups, and were defined in
[
12
]
to classify indecomposable modules over quasi-Frobenious algebras.

Brauer graph algebras have been recently related to cluster algebras of surfaces, gentle algebras and mirror symmetry, see for example
[
26
,
31
,
28
]
, moreover they have played an important role in the representation theory of tame symmetric algebras. Most of the relevant questions about their homological properties have been studied, for example, it is well understood their derived equivalence classes
[
29
]

[
5
]
, the classification of their two-term tilting complexes
[
1
]
and their first Hochschild cohomology group
[
10
]
. A natural relation between trivial extension of gentle algebras and a subclass of Brauer graph algebras has been described in
[
31
]
, more precisely, the paper shows that any trivial extension of a gentle algebra is actually a Brauer graph algebra, in which the multiplicity function is identically equal to one.

Several generalizations of Brauer graph algebras have been defined using slightly modified graphs or definitions, in which important properties of the algebra, and its modules, can be directly understood from its graph. See for example
[
21
]
where the so-called
Brauer configuration algebras
are defined, or
[
33
]
where the
modified Brauer algebras
are constructed.

In this paper, we define what we call a skew-Brauer graph, which is by definition a Brauer graph
\Gamma
, that has a set of special vertices
\Gamma_{0}^{\times}
with valency one and multiplicity function equal to one. Using this extra data we define a path algebra called skew-Brauer graph algebra, in order to link this new family of algebras and the trivial extensions of skew-gentle algebras, which are skew-group algebras of gentle algebras with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action.

Skew-gentle algebras were defined in
[
18
]
to study a subclass of matrix-problems, and to describe explicitly their Auslander-Reiten sequences. This family of tame algebras is also related to several subjects, for example, they were used in
[
19
]
to prove that a large class of Jacobian algebras arising from closed surfaces are also tame. Skew-gentle algebras are a generalization of gentle algebras, as they are, by definition, skew-group algebras of gentle. Both families, gentle and skew-gentle algebras share several properties, for example both of them are Gorenstein, see
[
20
]
.

Any skew-gentle algebra can also be defined by a triple
(Q,I,\text{Sp})
, where
Q
is a quiver,
I
is a subset of paths of length two, and
Sp
is a set of loops in
Q
, subject to certain restriction. This triple defines two gentle algebras, one of which we call the underlying gentle algebra. Using this definition, we show that the original skew-gentle algebra and the
underlying gentle algebra also share some properties.

In this work, we study trivial extensions of skew-gentle algebras, and we show that there is a simple way to define such trivial extension using the trivial extension of the underlying gentle algebra. Even more, we show that any trivial extension of a skew-gentle algebra is a skew-Brauer graph algebra. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem A
(Theorem
4.5
, Theorem
4.10
and Theorem
4.12
)

i)

Any skew-Brauer graph algebra is a symmetric finite dimensional algebra.

ii)

The class of trivial extension of skew-gentle algebras coincides with the subclass of skew-Brauer graph algebras with multiplicity function identically equal to one.

Deciding whether a trivial extension of an algebra is of finite representation type is a question that has been addressed by several authors. In this work, we consider two different tools that were used to solve that problem. The first one is the admissible cuts or cut sets, defined in
[
16
,
13
]
, to provide a complete characterization of trivial extension of finite representation type. The second one is reflections of quivers and relations, defined in
[
22
]
, to show that any trivial extension is of finite representation type if and only if it is related to an algebra of Dynkin type through sequences of reflections. In this paper, we study reflections and cut sets that preserve the class of skew-gentle algebras and their trivial extensions, in this context, we have the following results.

Theorem B
(Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
5.7
)

Let
A=A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
be a skew-gentle algebra,
A^{\prime}
be its underlying gentle algebra and
i
be a source in
A
. Then the reflection
S_{i}
of
A^{\prime}
gives rise to a sequence of reflections
\overline{S_{i}}
on
A
such that
\overline{S_{i}}(A)
is skew-gentle. Furthermore there exists a cut set
\mathcal{D}
of
T(A)
such that
\overline{S_{i}}=T(A)/\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle

Following a natural question, we also show the following characterization of skew-Brauer graph algebras of finite representation type, see definition of skew-Brauer tree in Section
6
.

Theorem C
(Theorem
6.10
and Theorem
6.11
)

Let
B_{\Gamma^{\times}}
be a skew-Brauer graph algebra. Then
B_{\Gamma^{\times}}
is of finite representation type if and only if
\Gamma^{\times}
is a skew-Brauer tree. Moreover, in this case,
B_{\Gamma^{\times}}
is isomorphic to the trivial extension of an iterated tilted algebra of type
\mathbb{D}
.

Additionally, we present a geometric interpretation of cut sets and reflections using orbifold dissections, a geometric model inspired on the one for the derived category of skew-gentle algebras and the Koszul dual algebra, see
[
4
]
and
[
24
]
. More specifically, we establish the following theorems.

Theorem D
(Theorem
7.6
)

Let
A=A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
be a skew-gentle algebra,
A^{\mathrm{sg}}
be its admissible presentation and
(S,M,P,\mathcal{O},D^{\times})
be its orbifold
\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\times}
-dissection. Then, any good cut set of the trivial extension
T(A^{\mathrm{sg}})
of
A^{\mathrm{sg}}
is induced by a sequence of contracting and adding boundary segments in
(S,M,P,\mathcal{O},D^{\times})
.

As a consequence of Theorem B and Theorem D, one can prove that the sequences of reflections described in Theorem B are also induced by contracting and adding boundary segments.

2.
Background

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2
. Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite quiver, where
Q_{0}
denotes the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
denotes the set of arrows. For an arrow
\alpha
denote
s(\alpha)
the starting vertex and
t(\alpha)
the ending vertex. A
path

p
of length
l>0
from the vertex
x
to the vertex
y
in
Q
is an ordered set of arrows
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}\}
such that
s(\alpha_{1})=x
,
t(\alpha_{l})=y
and
t(\alpha_{i})=s(\alpha_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i\leq l-1
, and we define
s(p)=s(\alpha_{1})
and
t(p)=t(\alpha_{l})
, and by abuse of notation, we write
p=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{l}
. A path
p
is a cycle if
s(p)=t(p)
. In addition, for each vertex
i
we denote by
e_{i}
the
stationary path
of length 0.

Consider the algebra
KQ
generated by paths of any length, and denote by
J
the two-sided ideal generated by arrows. A two-sided ideal
I
of
KQ
is called
admissible
if there exists
m\geq 2
such that
J^{m}\subset I\subset J^{2}
, where
J^{m}
is the ideal generated by paths of length
m
and
J^{2}
is the ideal generated by paths of length 2. The set of generators in
I
is called
a set of relations
.

The pair
(Q,I)
is said to be a
bound quiver
, and the algebra
KQ/I
is the bound quiver algebra. Every basic and connected finite dimensional
K
-algebra
A
can be defined by a bound quiver with an admissible ideal
(Q_{A},I_{A})

[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
, in this case
Q_{A}
is called the ordinary quiver of
A
.

Finally a path
p
in
Q
is non-zero in
(Q,I)
if
p\notin I
, and a non-zero path
p
is
maximal
if for any arrow
\alpha\in Q
, the paths
\alpha p
and
p\alpha
are elements of
I
.

2.1.
Gentle and skew-gentle algebras

skew-gentle algebras were defined in
[
18
]
as certain skew-group algebras of gentle algebras.
We first recall the definition of gentle algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(Q,I)
given by a quiver
Q
and an ideal
I
generated by a set of paths of length two in
Q
is
gentle
if:

(1)

there is at most two arrows ending and starting at each vertex
x
;

(2)

for every arrow
\alpha
in
Q_{1}
there is at most one arrow
\beta
such that
t(\alpha)=s(\beta)
and
\alpha\beta\in I
, and there is at most one arrow
\beta^{\prime}
such that
t(\alpha)=s(\beta^{\prime})
and
\alpha\beta^{\prime}\notin I
;

(3)

for every arrow
\alpha
in
Q_{1}
there is at most one arrow
\gamma
such that
s(\alpha)=t(\gamma)
and
\gamma\alpha\in I
, and there is at most one arrow
\gamma^{\prime}
such that
s(\alpha)=t(\gamma^{\prime})
and
\gamma^{\prime}\alpha\not\in I
;

(4)

the path algebra
A(Q,I)=KQ/I
is finite dimensional.

An algebra
A
is
gentle
if it is Morita equivalent to an algebra
A(Q,I)=KQ/I
, where
(Q,I)
is a gentle pair.

If the bound quiver
(Q,I)
satisfies (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
), we say that the quotient
KQ/I
is
locally gentle
.

Definition 2.2
.

A triple
(Q,I,\text{Sp})
given by a quiver
Q
, an ideal
I
generated by a set of paths of length two in
Q
, and a subset Sp of loops in
Q
is
skew-gentle
if the pair
(Q,\langle I\sqcup\{f^{2}:f\in\text{Sp}\}\rangle)
is gentle.

An algebra
A
is
skew-gentle
if it is Morita equivalent to an algebra
A(Q,I,\text{Sp})=KQ/\langle I\sqcup\{f^{2}-f:f\in\text{Sp}\}\rangle
, where
(Q,I,\text{Sp})
is a skew-gentle triple.

We refer to the elements of Sp as
special loops
, and we denote by
Q_{0}^{\times}
the set of
special vertices
of
Q
where a special loop is attached.

It is clear that, in general, the ideal associated with a skew-gentle algebra
A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
is not admissible. However, in
[
18
]
, the authors provide an admissible presentation of
A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
. The reader can see this construction in Example
3.5
.

2.2.
Trivial extensions

Trivial extensions of artin algebras play an important role in representation theory. In this section we describe a construction for the quiver and relations of trivial extension of a bound quiver algebra. We mainly follow the articles
[
14
,
15
,
31
]
.

The
trivial extension of an algebra
A
is the algebra
T(A)=A\ltimes DA
with underlying vector space
A\oplus DA
and with the multiplication defined by
(a,f)(b,g)=(ab,ag+fb)
, for
a,b\in A
and
f,g\in DA
, where
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(-,K)
.

For every finite dimensional algebra
A=KQ_{A}/I_{A}
, where
I_{A}
is an admissible ideal, we can construct its trivial extension
T(A)
based on the quiver and relations of
A
and a basis of the two-sided ideal
\operatorname{soc}_{A^{e}}A
, where
A^{e}
is the enveloping algebra.

Recall that, as a
K
-vector space,
\operatorname{soc}_{A^{e}}A
can be defined as the intersection of

\operatorname{soc}(_{A}A)=\{a\in A\colon Ja=0\}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \operatorname{soc}(A_{A})=\{a\in A\colon aJ=0\},

where
J
denotes the Jacobson radical, i.e. the ideal generated by arrows. See
[
25
, Exercise 4.20]
and

[
6
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}
be a
K
-basis of
\operatorname{soc}_{A^{e}}A
. The ordinary quiver of
T(A)
is given by:

(1)

(Q_{T(A)})_{0}=(Q_{A})_{0}

(2)

(Q_{T(A)})_{1}=(Q_{A})_{1}\cup\{\beta_{p_{1}},\dots,\beta_{p_{r}}\}
, where
\beta_{p_{i}}
is an arrow from
t(p_{i})
to
s(p_{i})
for each
p_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
, with
1\leq i\leq r
.

To give an explicit description of the ideal of relations
I_{T(A)}
we need to recall some definitions from
[
14
, Section 3]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}
be a basis of
\operatorname{soc}_{A^{e}}A
. Let

C=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{l}\beta_{p_{j}}
be an oriented cycle in
KQ_{T(A)}
such that
p_{j}^{*}(\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{l})\neq 0
where
\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{l}
is a path in
KQ_{A}
,
p_{j}\in\mathcal{M}
, and
p_{j}^{*}
is the dual of
p_{j}
. The cycle
C
and all the cyclic permutations of
C
are called
elementary
.
In this case, we say that
\omega(C)=p_{j}^{*}(\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{l})\in K
is the
weight
of
C
.

Let
C
be a cycle of a quiver
Q
and
q
be a path contained in some cyclic permutation of
C
. The
supplement of
q
in
C
is the (possibly stationary) path
p
such that
C=pq
up to cyclic equivalence.

For each vertex
x\in Q_{T(A)}
we define the two-sided ideal
I_{x}^{\prime}
in
Q_{T(A)}
generated by

(1)

Oriented cycles from
x
to
x
which are not contained in any elementary cycle.

(2)

The elements of the form
\omega(C^{\prime})C-\omega(C)C^{\prime}
, where
C
and
C^{\prime}
are different elementary cycles starting and ending at
x
.

The ideal of relations for the trivial extension of a finite dimensional algebra was described in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
15
, Theorem A.1]

Let
A=KQ_{A}/I_{A}
a finite dimensional algebra and let
T(A)=KQ_{T(A)}/I_{T(A)}
be its trivial extension. Then, the quiver
Q_{T(A)}

is as above
and the ideal
I_{T(A)}
is generated by the union of the following sets.

(1)

A generating set of the ideal of relations
I_{A}
of
A
.

(2)

The paths that are not contained in (cyclic permutations of) any elementary cycle.

(3)

Linear combination of paths
\rho\in e_{x}KQ_{T(A)}e_{y}
such that
\rho q\in I_{x}^{\prime}
or
q\rho\in I_{y}^{\prime}
for any supplement path
q
in an elementary cycle
C
, where
I_{x}^{\prime}
is the ideal defined above.

2.3.
Repetitive algebra and reflections

In this section, we recall the definition of the repetitive algebra and reflections, both of them linked to an alternative construction for a trivial extension of an algebra. We mainly follow
[
30
,
14
,
22
]
.

Let
A=KQ/I
be a finite dimensional algebra with
I
be an ideal generated by a set of relations
\rho
for
Q
which are either zero relations or commutativity relations, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a set of maximal paths in
(Q,I)
. The repetitive algebra
\widehat{A}
is defined as the path algebra
K\widehat{Q}/\langle\widehat{\rho}\rangle
, where
\widehat{Q}
and
\widehat{\rho}
are defined as follows.

(1)

The set of vertices of
\widehat{Q}
is the set
\{x[n]:x\in Q_{0}\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

(2)

In the same way, every arrow
\alpha:x\rightarrow y
in
Q_{1}
induces arrows
\alpha[n]:x[n]\rightarrow y[n]
in
\widehat{Q}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

For each maximal path
p\in\mathcal{M}
in
(Q,I)
, with
x=s(p)
and
y=t(p)
, there is an arrow
\overline{p}[n]:y[n]\rightarrow x[n+1]
in
\widehat{Q}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. These arrows are called
connecting arrows
.

To define the set of relations
\widehat{\rho}
, we need to introduce some definitions for paths in
(Q,I)
.
Let
p
be a path in
(Q,I)
, we denote by
p[n]
the corresponding path in
\widehat{Q}
. If
p=p_{1}p_{2}
is a maximal path in
(Q,I)
, then
p_{2}[n]\overline{p}[n]p_{1}[n+1]
is called
full path
in
\widehat{Q}
.

The set of relations
\widehat{\rho}
of
\widehat{Q}
are given by the following list.

•

For each monomial relation
p\in\rho
, the path
p[n]
is in
\widehat{\rho}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

•

For each commutative relation
p_{1}-p_{2}\in\rho
, then
p_{1}[n]-p_{2}[n]
is in
\widehat{\rho}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Let
p
be a path in
\widehat{Q}
which contains a connecting arrow. If
p
is not a sub-path of a full path, then
p\in\widehat{\rho}
.

•

If
p=p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}
and
q=q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}
are maximal paths in
(Q,I)
such that
p_{2}=q_{2}
, then
p_{3}[n]\overline{p}[n]p_{1}[n+1]-q_{3}[n]\overline{q}[n]q_{1}[n+1]
is in
\widehat{\rho}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

To define reflections over an algebra, we need to consider
\nu
the Nakayama automorphism of
\widehat{A}
, such that
\nu(x[n])=x[n+1]
for every
x\in Q_{0}
and every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
x
be a source of
Q_{A}
, we denote by
{\sigma_{x}^{-}Q_{A}}
the full
sub-quiver of
\widehat{Q_{{A}}}

with vertex set

S=\{y[0]\in(\widehat{Q_{{A}}})_{0}\mid y\in(Q_{A})_{0},y\neq x\}\cup\{x[1]\}.

We note that
{\sigma_{x}^{-}Q_{A}}
is a
complete
\nu
-slice
, which is defined as a connected full subquiver
of
\widehat{Q_{{A}}}
that contains exactly one vertex from each

\nu
-orbit of vertices of
Q_{\widehat{A}}
.

The
negative reflection

S_{x}^{-}(A)
of
A
is the algebra
\operatorname{End}_{\widehat{A}}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{x[z]\in S}e_{x[z]}\widehat{A}\right)
, where
\{e_{x[z]}\}_{x[z]\in S}
is a set of primitive orthogonal idempotents of
\widehat{A}
.

A
negative reflection sequence
of
Q_{A}
is a sequence of vertices
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
of
Q_{A}
such that
x_{j}
is a source of
\sigma^{-}_{x_{j-1}}\dots\sigma^{-}_{x_{1}}Q_{A}
for
1\leq j\leq r
.

Similarly, given a sink
x
of
Q_{A}
, a
positive reflection
of an algebra is defined, considering
\sigma^{+}_{x}Q_{A}
as the full-subquiver of
\widehat{Q}_{A}
with vertex set

S=\{y[0]\in(\widehat{Q_{{A}}})_{0}\mid y\in(Q_{A})_{0},y\neq x\}\cup\{x[-1]\}.

Remark 2.5
.

The quiver of the negative (positive) reflection
S^{-}_{x}(A)
(resp.
S^{+}_{x}(A)
) coincides with the quiver
\sigma^{-}_{x}Q_{A}
(resp.
\sigma^{+}_{x}Q_{A}
).

3.
Trivial extension of a skew-gentle algebra

In this section, we describe the quiver and relations for the trivial extension of the admissible presentation of a skew-gentle algebra. To do this, we introduce a construction of quivers and relations that depends on a set of vertices and cycles from a given quiver. By abuse of notation and convenience, we call these vertices
special
, and we denote them by
Q_{0}^{\times}
as in the previous section. This construction is slightly different to the one presented in
[
18
]
, and revisited in
[
4
,
24
]
, implemented to define the admissible presentation of any skew-gentle algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

(sg-quiver
Q^{\mathrm{sg}}
) Let
Q
be a quiver with a set of vertices
Q_{0}^{\times}
(called
special vertices
), such that there is no loop incident at any special vertex. We define the sg
-quiver

Q^{\mathrm{sg}}
as follows. We start by duplicating the special vertices in
Q
, that is, for each vertex
x
in
Q_{0}
we define

Q^{\text{sg}}_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{x^{+},x^{-}\}&\mbox{if $x\in Q_{0}^{\times}$}\\
\{x\}&\mbox{otherwise,}\\
\end{array}\right.

obtaining the set of vertices
Q^{\text{sg}}_{0}:=\bigcup_{x\in Q_{0}}Q^{\text{sg}}_{0}(x)
. For each arrow
\alpha
in
Q_{1}
we define the set of arrows

Q^{\text{sg}}_{1}(\alpha):=\{(x,\alpha,y)\mid x\in Q_{0}^{\text{sg}}(s(\alpha)),y\in Q_{0}^{\text{sg}}(t(\alpha))\}

obtaining the set of arrows
Q_{1}^{\text{sg}}:=\bigcup_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}Q_{1}^{\text{sg}}(\alpha)
.

In other words, while arrows incident to non-special vertices remain the same, for an arrow in
Q
of the form
x\xrightarrow{\alpha}y
, if one of the vertices, say
y
, belongs to
Q_{0}^{\times}
then we will have two arrows in
Q^{\text{sg}}
. If both vertices
x
and
y
are special there will be four arrows in
Q^{\text{sg}}
. See these cases below.

The next definition generalizes the construction of the admissible ideal of a skew-gentle algebra.

Definition 3.2
.

(sg-ideal
I^{\textrm{sg}}
)
Let
A=KQ/I
be a finite dimensional algebra with
Q_{0}^{\times}
a set of special vertices, as in Definition
3.1
, and
\mathcal{C}
be a (possibly empty) set of cycles of length at least two in
Q
, called
special cycles
, such that:

•

The class
\mathcal{C}
is closed under cyclic permutations.

•

There is at most one special cycle starting at each vertex in
Q_{0}^{\times}
.

•

The ideal
I
is generated by the union of the following (possible empty) sets:

–

A set of paths of length at least two that contains no paths of the form
\alpha\beta
with
x=t(\alpha)=s(\beta)\in Q_{0}^{\times}
.

–

All commutative relations of the form
C_{1}-C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are distinct cycles of
\mathcal{C}
starting at the same non-special vertex.

Let
Q^{\text{sg}}
be the sg-quiver of
Q
. We define the sg
-ideal
I^{\mathrm{sg}}
of
KQ^{\text{sg}}
as the ideal generated by the union of the following sets:

Type a:

Commutative relations
({}^{\varepsilon}\alpha^{+})({}^{+}\beta^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})-({}^{\varepsilon}\alpha^{-})({}^{-}\beta^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})
such that
\alpha\beta
is a path of length 2 in
Q
with
t(\alpha)\in

Q_{0}^{\times}
, for all possible
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{+,-,\emptyset\}
.

Type b:

Commutative
relations
(\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon_{2}})({}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\varepsilon_{3}})\dots({}^{\varepsilon_{k}}\alpha_{k})-(\beta_{1}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{2}})({}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{2}}\beta_{2}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{3}})\dots({}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{l}}\beta_{l})
, for each difference of two different special cycles
C_{1}-C_{2}=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}-\beta_{1}\dots\beta_{l}
starting at the same non-special vertex, for all possible
\varepsilon_{i},\,\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}=\{+,-,\emptyset\}
with
i=1,\dots,k
and
j=1,\dots,l
.

Type c
:

Monomial relations
({}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon_{2}})({}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\varepsilon_{3}})\dots({}^{\varepsilon_{k}}\alpha_{k}^{\varepsilon_{k+1}})
where
\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}
is a monomial relation of
I
, for all possible
\varepsilon_{i}=\{+,-,\emptyset\}
and
i=1,\dots,k+1
.

Type d:

A monomial relation
({}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon_{2}})({}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\varepsilon_{3}})\dots({}^{\varepsilon_{k}}\alpha_{k}^{\varepsilon_{k+1}})
, for each special cycle
C=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}
starting at a special vertex, such that
\varepsilon_{1}\neq\varepsilon_{k+1}
, for all possible
\varepsilon_{i}=\{+,-,\emptyset\}
and
i=1,\dots,k+1
.

We say that
A^{\text{sg}}=KQ^{\text{sg}}/I^{\text{sg}}
is the sg
-bound quiver algebra
of
A
.

To show that the previous definitions generalize the construction of the admissible presentation of a skew-gentle algebra, we need to introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

Let

the set of special vertices in

of

With the above notation, the admissible presentation of a skew-gentle algebra

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
be a skew-gentle algebra and let

A^{\prime}=KQ^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
be its auxiliary gentle algebra. Consider
A^{\mathrm{sg}}=KQ^{\mathrm{sg}}/I^{\mathrm{sg}}
to be the admissible presentation of
A
.
The following statements hold.

(a)

A non-zero path in
A^{\prime}
induces at most four linearly independent non-zero paths in
A^{\mathrm{sg}}
.
Conversely, any non-zero path in
A^{\mathrm{sg}}
is induced by a path in
A^{\prime}
.

(b)

A path in
A^{\prime}
is maximal if and only if any induced path in
A^{\mathrm{sg}}
is a maximal path.

Proof.

Let
A=A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
be a skew-gentle algebra with
Q_{0}^{\times}
the set of special vertices. We consider
A^{\text{sg}}
the admissible presentation of
A
and
A^{\prime}
its auxiliary gentle algebra.

(a)
Let
p=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{r}
be a non-zero path in
A^{\prime}
.
Following Remark
3.6
,
p
induces
set of paths
{}^{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}

for all possible
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=\{+,-,\emptyset\}
. In particular, a path
p
in
A^{\prime}
induces exactly:

•

four different equivalence classes of paths in
A^{\text{sg}}
if and only if
s(p)
and
t(p)
are special vertices, each one with different starting and ending vertex;

•

two different equivalence classes of paths in
A^{\text{sg}}
if and only if one and only one of the vertices
s(p)
or
t(p)
is a special vertex, coinciding exclusively either at the starting or a the ending vertex;

•

exactly one equivalence class of paths in
A^{\text{sg}}
if and only if none of the vertices
s(p)
and
t(p)
are special.

It is also clear that any non-zero path
({}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon_{2}})\cdots({}^{\varepsilon_{r}}\alpha_{r}^{\varepsilon_{r+1}})
in
A^{\text{sg}}
is induced by the non-zero path
p=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{r}
in
A^{\prime}
.

(b)
Let
p=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{r}
be a maximal path in
A^{\prime}
, and let
{}^{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
be an induced path in
A^{\text{sg}}
, with
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{+,-,\emptyset\}
. Suppose that
{}^{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
is not maximal in
A^{\text{sg}},
then there exist an arrow
{}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\beta^{\varepsilon}
(or
{}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\gamma^{\varepsilon_{2}}
) such that the path
({}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\beta^{\varepsilon})({}^{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\neq 0
(or
({}^{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})({}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\gamma^{\varepsilon_{2}})\neq 0
, respectively). Therefore, such a path in
A^{\text{sg}}
is induced by a non-zero path
\beta p
(or
p\gamma
, respectively) in
A^{\prime}
, contradicting the fact that
p
is a maximal path.
With similar argument, we claim that any maximal path
{}^{\varepsilon}p^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
in
A^{\text{sg}}
with all possible
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{+,-,\emptyset\}
is induced by a maximal path
p
in
A^{\prime}.
∎

Recall that by

, when

is gentle the set of maximal paths forms a basis for

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A=A(Q,I,\mathrm{Sp})
be a skew-gentle algebra and
A^{\mathrm{sg}}=KQ^{\mathrm{sg}}/I^{\mathrm{sg}}
be its admissible presentation. Then the set of maximal paths
\mathcal{M}
in
A^{\mathrm{sg}}
forms a basis of
\operatorname{soc}_{(A^{\mathrm{sg}})^{e}}(A^{\mathrm{sg}})
.

Proof.

Let
A^{\prime}
be the auxiliary gentle algebra of
A
. Denote
\operatorname{soc}_{(A^{\text{sg}})^{e}}(A^{\text{sg}})
by
R
. As a
K
-vector space,
R

is the direct sum

R=\bigoplus_{x,y\in{Q^{\text{sg}}_{0}}}e_{x}Re_{y}

where
e_{x}
and
e_{y}
are the trivial paths at a vertex
x
and
y
in
Q^{\text{sg}}_{0}
.

Let
x
and
y
be two vertices in
Q^{\text{sg}}
, and let
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
be their associated vertices in
A^{\prime}
. Since
A^{\prime}
is gentle, there are at most two linearly independent classes of paths
p^{\prime}=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{n}
and
q^{\prime}=\beta_{1}\dots\beta_{m}
from
x^{\prime}
to
y^{\prime}
(see
[
8
, Lemma 3]
). By Lemma
3.7
and Remark
3.6
, there are at most two linearly independent classes of paths
p
and
q
from
x
to
y
.

Note that there are at most four arrows ending at
x
and at most four arrows starting at
y
, which correspond to arrows ending at
x^{\prime}
and arrows starting at
y^{\prime}
. We will show that the paths
p
and
q
induce a basis for
e_{x}Re_{y}
. We need to analyse each case based on the number of arrows ending at
x
and starting at
y
, respectively, and if there is exactly one independent class of paths from
x
and
y
or two, but we will describe two of them.

Suppose there are two independent classes of paths from
x
to
y
and suppose that there is exactly four arrows ending at
x
and four arrows staring at
y
, then there are exactly two arrows
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
ending at
x^{\prime}
and two arrows
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
staring at
y^{\prime}
, and
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
are not special vertices of
A^{\prime}
.

Since
A^{\prime}
is gentle, either
\gamma_{1}\alpha_{1}\notin I
or
\gamma_{2}\alpha_{1}\notin I
, and either
\gamma_{1}\beta_{1}\notin I
or
\gamma_{2}\beta_{1}\notin I
. Therefore,
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
are not maximal in
A^{\prime}
, and by Lemma
3.7
, neither are
p
and
q
in
A^{\text{sg}}
, and as a consequence
e_{x}Re_{y}=\{0\}
.

Suppose now that there are exactly two arrows
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
ending at
x
and no arrows starting at
y
. In this case, we need to consider two cases,
x^{\prime}
is a special vertex or not. In the first case, we have that
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
come from an arrow
\gamma
in
A^{\prime}
, and there is exactly one independent class of path
p^{\prime}
from
x^{\prime}
to
y^{\prime}
, since there is exactly one arrow staring at
x^{\prime}
. Hence
\gamma\alpha_{1}\notin I
, and
p^{\prime}
is not maximal in
A^{\prime}
, neither

in
A^{\text{sg}}
, then
e_{x}Re_{y}=\{0\}
.

If
x^{\prime}
is not a special vertex, we have that either
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are arrows of
A^{\prime}
, or they come from an arrow
\gamma
in
A^{\prime}
. In the first case, by definition of gentle and a similar analysis as in the first case, the path or paths from
x^{\prime}
to
y^{\prime}
are not maximal, and neither the associated path or paths in
A^{\text{sg}}
.

In the second case, if
p\neq q
, then either

In this section we define a generalization of Brauer graph algebras, that we call skew-Brauer graph algebras, and we show that the trivial extension of any skew-gentle algebra is isomorphic to a skew-Brauer graph algebra.

Given an undirected graph
\Gamma
, we denote by
\val(v)

the
valency
of a vertex
v

v
in
\Gamma
.

We recall that a
Brauer graph

\Gamma
is a tuple
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1},\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{o})
where
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
is an unoriented graph with
\Gamma_{0}
the set of vertices and
\Gamma_{1}
the set of edges and

(1)

\mathfrak{m}
is a
multiplicity function

v\in\Gamma_{0}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{o}
is an
orientation
of the edges attached to each
v\in\Gamma_{0}
. For a vertex
v
such that
\val(v)=t
we denote
\mathfrak{o}(v)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
. In the particular case that
\val(v)=1
and the only edge attached to

is
x

\mathfrak{m}(v)=1

x

\mathfrak{m}(v)>1

so that in the cyclic order the successor (and the predecessor) of

is
x
.

.

A

\Gamma

5.

We also describe the positive and negative reflections on a skew-gentle algebra. Moreover, we analyse the good cuts over a skew-gentle algebra and the relationship between these two notions.

Definition 6.1

A

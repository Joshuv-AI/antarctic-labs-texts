Geometry on curves which go around a Whitney umbrella
Masayuki Hara

Abstract
We consider curves which go around Whitney umbrella. Then we consider the geodesic and the normal curvatures, ruled surfaces generated by the normal vector and normal developable surfaces with respect to the tangent and bi-tangent vectors of the curve. Then we obtain several functions which represents geometry on Whitney umbrella. Looking at the top terms of them with respect to the radius, we study geometry on Whitney umbrella.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider geometry on curves which
go around Whitney umbrella.
Whitney umbrella is known as a most frequently
appearing singularity on surfaces.
A map-germ
f:(\boldsymbol{R}^{2},0)\to(\boldsymbol{R}^{3},0)
is a
Whitney umbrella

if it is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the map-germ
(u,v)\mapsto(u,v^{2},uv)
at the
origin, where two map-germs are said to be

\mathcal{A}
-equivarent

if there exist diffeomorphism-germs
\varphi:(\boldsymbol{R}^{2},0)\to(\boldsymbol{R}^{2},0)
and

\psi:(\boldsymbol{R}^{3},0)\to(\boldsymbol{R}^{3},0)
such that

g=\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}

holds.
It is also known that
unit normal vector field around a Whitney umbrella
cannot be smoothly extended beyond the singular point.
In
[
2
]
, it is shown that by taking the blow-up operation
on the source space of a Whitney umbrella,
that is to say, taking the polar coordinate,
unit normal vector field
can be smoothly extended around the set which corresponds
to the singular point.
This fact strongly suggests that considering
a curve which goes around a Whitney umbrella,
then several geometric objects can be defined as
a direction-dependently.
In this paper, we consider
curves who has a constant radius with respect to
the polar coordinate around a Whitney umbrella.
Then we consider
the geodesic and the normal curvatures,
ruled surfaces generated by the normal vector and
normal developable surfaces with respect to
the tangent and bi-tangent vectors of the curve.
Then we obtain several functions which represents
geometry on Whitney umbrella depending on the angle
around the singular point, and the radius.
Looking at the top terms of them with respect to the radius,
we study geometry on Whitney umbrella.

2.2
Ruled surfaces

Let
\gamma:(\boldsymbol{R},0)\to(\boldsymbol{R}^{3},0)
be a curve,
and let
\delta:(\boldsymbol{R},0)\to\boldsymbol{R}^{3}
be a curve satisfying

|\delta|=1
.
A surface
f(t,\beta)=\gamma(t)+\beta\delta(t)
is called
a
ruled surface
.
The curve
\gamma
is called a
base curve
, and

\delta
a
director curve
.
The straight lines
t\mapsto\gamma(t)+\beta\delta(t)

is called the
ruling

A ruled surface
f(t,\beta)=\gamma(t)+\beta\delta(t)
is said to be

non-cylindrical
if
\delta^{\prime}\neq 0
for any
t
.
If
f
is a non-cylindrical ruled surface,
the surface

\sigma(t)+\beta\delta(t)
has the same image with
f
,
where

\sigma=\gamma-(\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\delta^{\prime}/(\delta^{\prime}\cdot\delta^{\prime}))\delta
.
The curve
\sigma
is called the
striction curve

of
f
. See
[
3
, Section 14]
for detail.
It is known that the singular values of a non-cylindrical ruled surface
are located on the striction curve (
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
).
If a ruled surfaces has constant zero Gaussian curvature,
it is called a
developable surface
.
If
\delta^{\prime}\equiv 0
, then
f
is called a
cylinder
.
If a non-cylindrical ruled surface satisfies
\sigma^{\prime}\equiv 0
,
where
\sigma
is the striction curve,
then
f
is called a
cone
.
Here
\equiv
stands for the equality holds identically.
It is known that developable surface is locally classified into
cylinders, cones and tangent developable surfaces.
If
\delta
is parallel to
\gamma^{\prime}
, the ruled surface

f(t,\beta)=\gamma(t)+\beta\delta(t)
is called a

tangent developable surface
.

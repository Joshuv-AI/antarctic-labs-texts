Convergence analysis of max-product and max-min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators in Mellin Orlicz space Journal: Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications
Satyaranjan Pradhan, H.M. Srivastava, Madan Mohan Soren

Abstract
In the present study, we establish both pointwise and uniform convergence in the space of logarithmically uniformly continuous and bounded functions for the max-product and max-min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators. Furthermore, the modular convergence of these operators is demonstrated within the framework of Orlicz space. In addition to the theoretical results, we provide numerical and graphical analyses for various kernel pairs, illustrating the convergence rates and approximation behavior of the proposed operators.

Introduction

Approximation theory plays a vital role in signal analysis and image processing
[
16
,
33
]
, where one of its central challenges is the accurate reconstruction of functions from discrete data. The seminal work of Whittaker, Kotelnikov, and Shannon
[
19
]
, who proved that any band-limited signal can be perfectly reconstructed from its uniformly spaced samples, marked a major milestone in this field and is now known as the WKS sampling theorem. Subsequently, Butzer and Stens
[
22
]
extended this framework to signals that are not necessarily band-limited by replacing the sinc function with more general kernels satisfying suitable conditions, inspiring extensive research on generalized sampling and reconstruction methods (see
[
13
,
23
,
39
]
).

An important development in this context is the introduction of exponential sampling, tracing back to the works of Bertero and Pike
[
17
]
, Gori
[
36
]
, Ostrowsky et al.
[
40
]
, who derived a reconstruction formula for band-limited Mellin functions, later formalized as the exponential sampling formula. The Mellin analysis framework, initially developed by Mamedov
[
38
]
and refined by Butzer and Jansche
[
20
,
21
]
, provides a natural foundation for addressing sampling and approximation problems involving exponentially spaced data. This framework has been found widely applied in areas such as radio astronomy, optical physics, and photon correlation spectroscopy (see
[
17
,
36
,
40
]
).

To approximate functions that are not Mellin band-limited, Bardaro and Mantellini
[
10
]
extended the exponential sampling formula using generalized kernels satisfying suitable smoothness and integrability conditions (see also,
[
9
,
12
,
37
]
).
Among the different classes of exponential sampling operators, two families have attracted particular attention due to their strong approximation properties: the Kantorovich
(K_{\Phi,n})

[
4
]
and Durrmeyer
(D_{\Phi,\Psi,n})

[
11
]
operators, defined respectively as

\displaystyle(K_{\Phi,n}f)(x)

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(e^{-k}x^{n})\int_{e^{k/n}}^{e^{(k+1)/n}}f(u)\frac{du}{u},

\displaystyle(D_{\Phi,\Psi,n}f)(x)

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(e^{-k}x^{n})\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(e^{-k}u^{n})f(u)\frac{du}{u}.

where
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The Kantorovich and Durrmeyer modifications extend the approximation process to broader functional settings, including Orlicz and
L^{p}
-spaces, ensuring stability under integration and reducing sampling distortions such as time jitter and round-off errors. Moreover, the Durrmeyer framework serves as a unifying structure for several operator classes: choosing
\Psi=\chi_{[0,1]}
recovers the Kantorovich operator, while setting
\Psi=\delta
(the Dirac distribution) yields the generalized form.

Recent studies
[
24
,
29
,
30
]
have demonstrated the effectiveness of the Orlicz space framework for analyzing convergence and approximation properties of sampling operators, including Kantorovich and Durrmeyer-type constructions (see also
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
8
,
25
]
. The framework has also proven instrumental for studying nonlinear operators, particularly max-product and max-min types, due to its flexibility and robustness in handling nonstandard growth behaviors.

The theory of max-product operators was initiated by Coroianu and Gal
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
, who introduced generalized, sinc-type and truncated max-product sampling operators and establishing their convergence and saturation properties. These results were later unified and extended into a comprehensive framework for nonlinear approximation
[
14
]
. Exponential sampling based Kantorovich max-product neural network operators were subsequently developed
[
7
]
, extending the approach to neural approximation theory. Convergence in weighted spaces was further analyzed
[
41
,
44
]
, and within the Orlicz framework, modular convergence for max-product Kantorovich operators was established
[
18
]
. Univariate and multivariate neural network based max-product approximations were also studied, demonstrating modular convergence and enhancing their applicability in nonlinear settings
[
31
,
32
]
.

In parallel, the development of max-min sampling operators began with a general approximation theory
[
34
]
, later extended to Kantorovich-type max-min operators with improved convergence properties
[
35
]
and subsequently generalized to neural network operators
[
6
]
. Within the Orlicz framework, the approximation properties of generalized and Kantrovich-type max-min sampling operators have been investigated, particularly in image processing applications
[
45
]
, with uniform and modular convergence of max-min exponential sampling neural network operators established
[
42
]
.

Motivated by these advances, the present study develops and analyzes max-product and max-min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators within the Orlicz space framework. We establish their pointwise and uniform convergence, derive error estimates, and compare their approximation efficiency across various Mellin-type kernel configurations.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the set of positive real numbers. For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
\mathscr{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denote the space of Lebesgue measurable,
p
-integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
equipped with the usual
p
-norm, and let
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denote the space of bounded functions endowed with the sup-norm.

We begin by recalling some basic notions of Mellin analysis, which provides a natural framework for studying functions defined on the positive real axis and is particularly suited to exponential sampling.

For
c\in\mathbb{R}
, define the space

\displaystyle X_{c}:=\left\{h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\big|h(w)w^{c-1}\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right\},

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|h\|_{X_{c}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(w)|w^{c-1}dw.

For
h\in X_{c}
, the Mellin transform is defined by

\displaystyle M[h](s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(w)w^{s-1}dw,\quad(s=c+it,t\in\mathbb{R}).

For differentiable functions, the Mellin differential operator
\Theta_{c}
is given by

\displaystyle(\Theta_{c}h)(w):=wh^{\prime}(w)+ch(w),\quad w\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

and higher-order derivatives are defined recursively by

\displaystyle\Theta_{c}^{r}:=\Theta_{c}\big(\Theta_{c}^{r-1}\big),\quad(r\geq 2)\quad\text{with}\quad\Theta_{c}^{0}h:=h.

For detailed discussion of the fundamental properties of Mellin transform (see
[
20
]
).
Throughout this work, we consider
\mathscr{I}
to be any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\mathrm{h}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\log
continuous at a point
w\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\varrho>0
such that for all
v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle|\log v-\log w|\leq\varrho\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|h(v)-h(w)|<\epsilon.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\log
-uniformly continuous if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\varrho>0
such that for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle|\log w_{1}-\log w_{2}|\leq\varrho\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|h(w_{1})-h(w_{2})|<\epsilon.

We define the function space

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I}):=\{h:\mathscr{I}\to\mathbb{R}\big|h~\text{is a bounded},~\log\text{-uniformly continuous function}\},

equipped with the supremum norm
|h\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{w\in\mathscr{I}}|\mathrm{h}(w)|
.

It is evident that log-uniform continuity and uniform continuity are equivalent on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}.

These notions will be useful in our analysis of the max-product and max-min exponential sampling operators.

We now recall some fundamental concepts related to Orlicz spaces as a generalization of the classical Lebesgue spaces.
Define the Haar measure
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{dt}{t},\quad\text{for any measurable set }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Let
\mathrm{M}(\Omega,\mathfrak{h})
denote the space of measurable functions on
\Omega
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
be a function satisfy:

\displaystyle(\varphi 1)

\displaystyle:\varphi(0)=0~\text{and }~\varphi(v)>0~\text{for all }~v>0,

\displaystyle(\varphi 2)

\displaystyle:\varphi~\text{is continuous and non-decreasing on }~\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0},

\displaystyle(\varphi 3)

\displaystyle:\lim_{v\to+\infty}\varphi(v)=+\infty,

\displaystyle(\varphi 4)

\displaystyle:\varphi~\text{ is convex on }~\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}.

Such a function is called a convex
\varphi
-function.

Let a convex
\varphi
-function
\zeta
and
h\in\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathfrak{h})
, define the modular functional

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[h]:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\zeta(|h(w)|)\frac{dw}{w}.

It is evident that
I_{\zeta}
is convex.

The Orlicz space over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
and
\zeta
is defined by

\displaystyle L^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\left\{h\in\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathfrak{h}):\exists~\ell>0\quad\text{ such that }\quad I_{\zeta}[\ell h]<\infty\right\}.

For a fixed
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the Mellin-Orlicz space is defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}_{c}^{\zeta}:=\left\{h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\middle|h(\cdot)(\cdot)^{c}\in L^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right\}.

In this paper, we focus on the case for
c=0
, where

\mathscr{X}_{0}^{\zeta}=L^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Define the subspace of finite elements as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\left\{h\in\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathfrak{h}):I_{\zeta}[\ell h]<\infty\quad\text{ for all }\quad\ell>0\right\}.

The Luxemburg norm is given by

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\zeta}:=\inf\left\{\ell>0:I_{\zeta}\left[\frac{h}{\ell}\right]\leq 1\right\}.

Let
h_{m}\subset L^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\quad(m>0).
Then

1.

h_{m}\rightarrow h
modularly if there exists
\ell>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}I_{\zeta}[\ell(h_{m}-h)]=0;

2.

h_{m}\rightarrow h
in Luxemburg norm if

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\|\mathrm{h}_{m}-\mathrm{h}\|_{\zeta}=0,

equivalently,
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}I_{\zeta}[\ell(h_{m}-h)]=0
for all
\ell>0
.

A convex
\varphi-
function
\zeta
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
if
there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\zeta(2v)\leq M\zeta(v),\quad\forall~v\geq 0.

Luxemburg norm convergence is stronger than modular convergence; however, if
\zeta
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, the two notions coincide. In that case,

\displaystyle L^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\mathscr{E}^{\zeta}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Example 2.3
.

Let
p>1
and define
\zeta(v)=v^{p}.
Then

I_{\zeta}[h]=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}|h(w)|^{p}\frac{dw}{w},

and
L^{\zeta}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

The corresponding Mellin-Orlicz space is

\displaystyle X_{c}^{p}:=\left\{h\in L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(w)|^{p}w^{cp-1}dw<\infty\right\}.

Here,
\zeta(v)
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, ensuring the equivalence of modular and norm convergence.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
and define
\zeta_{\alpha}(v)=e^{v^{\alpha}}-1
. Then

\displaystyle I_{\zeta_{\alpha}}[h]=\int\limits_{a}^{b}\left[e^{\left(|h(w)|^{\alpha}\right)}-1\right]\frac{dw}{w},\quad h\in\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathfrak{h}).

The associated Orlicz space over the Haar measure

\displaystyle L_{\mu}^{\zeta_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\left\{h\in\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu)\big|\int\limits_{a}^{b}\left[e^{|h(w)|^{\alpha}}-1\right]\frac{dw}{w}<\infty\right\}.

The associated Mellin-Orlicz space is

\displaystyle X_{c}^{\zeta_{\alpha}}:=\left\{h\in L_{\mu}^{\zeta_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\int\limits_{a}^{b}\left[e^{|h(w)|^{\alpha}}-1\right]w^{c-1}dw<\infty\right\}.

Since

Example 2.5
.

Let
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(v)=v^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}(v+1),\quad\alpha\geq 1,\beta>0.

\displaystyle I_{\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}}[h]=\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(w)|^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\big(1+|h(w)|\big)\frac{dw}{w}.

The associated Mellin-Orlicz space is

\displaystyle X_{c}^{\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}}=\left\{h\in L_{\mu}^{\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(w)|^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}\big(1+|h(w)|\big)w^{c-1}dw<\infty\right\}.

Moreover

-condition; in fact

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(2v)\leq 2^{\alpha+\beta}\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(v)\quad(v\geq 0),

3
Max-product Durrmeyer type exponential sampling operators

In this section, we derive the pointwise and uniform convergence properties of the max-product Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators in the space
\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
, and further establish their modular convergence in the Orlicz type spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
h:\mathscr{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and
L^{1}
-integrable function on
\mathscr{I}
. Then the max-product Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators associated with
h
, with respect to the kernels
\Phi
and
\psi
, is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w):=\frac{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}},

where
\mathrm{J}_{n}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:k=\lceil{n\log a}\rceil,\cdots,\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
43
]

Let
h,g
be two locally integrable functions on
\mathscr{I}
. Then the following properties hold for the max-product exponential sampling operators
\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h):

1.

If
h(w)\leq g(w),
then
\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)\leq\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(g)(w)
.

2.

\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h+g)(w)\leq\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)+\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(g)(w)
.

3.

For all
w\in\mathscr{I}
,
|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(g)(w)|\leq\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(|h-g|)(w)
.

4.

For every
\lambda>0
, we have
\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(\lambda h)(w)=\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
h:\mathscr{I}\to[0,1]\,
be a bounded and
L^{1}
-integrable function. Then, for every point of logarithmic continuity
w\in\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)=h(w).

Moreover, if
h\in\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
, then the convergence is uniform, i.e,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)-h\right\|_{\infty}=0,

where
\|.\|_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm.

Proof.

From Definition
3.1
, we can write

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-\frac{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(w)\frac{dv}{v}}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right|.

By applying Lemmas
2.13
,
2.14
and property (3) of Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)-h(w)\right|\leq\frac{1}{K\vartheta_{w}}\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)-h(w)|\frac{dv}{v}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrarily. Since
h
is logarithmic continuity at
w\in\mathscr{I}
, there exists
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle|h(v)-h(w)|<\varepsilon\quad\text{whenever}\quad|\log{v}-\log{w}|<\tau.

Define the index sets

\displaystyle N_{1}=\{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}:\left|\frac{k}{n}-\log{w}\right|\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\},\quad N_{2}=\{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}:\left|\frac{k}{n}-\log{w}\right|>\frac{\tau}{2}\}.

Then we can decompose

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)-h(w)\right|\leq\frac{1}{K\vartheta_{w}}\max\{\mathrm{E}_{1},\mathrm{E}_{2}\},

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{1}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)-h(w)|\frac{dv}{v},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{2}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)-h(w)|\frac{dv}{v}.

We further decompose
E_{1}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{1}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\left\{\int\limits_{|\log{v}-\frac{k}{n}|<\frac{\tau}{2}}+\int\limits_{|\log{v}-\frac{k}{n}|\geq\frac{\tau}{2}}\right\}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)-h(w)|\frac{dv}{v}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}_{1.1}+\mathrm{E}_{1.2}.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
v\in\mathscr{I}
satisfying

\left|\log v-\tfrac{k}{n}\right|<\tfrac{\tau}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\log v-\log w\right|\leq\left|\log v-\tfrac{k}{n}\right|+\left|\tfrac{k}{n}-\log w\right|<\tfrac{\tau}{2}+\tfrac{\tau}{2}=\tau.

Hence, by the logarithmic continuity of
h
at
w
, and using the substitution
t=e^{-k}v^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{1.1}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{1}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{|\log{v}-\frac{k}{n}|<\frac{\tau}{2}}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|\frac{dv}{v}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\psi(t)|\frac{dt}{t}=\varepsilon\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}.

Since
h
is bounded and
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, for sufficiently large
n
, we also have

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{1.2}<2\|h\|_{\infty}\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{1}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{|\log{v}-\frac{k}{n}|\geq\frac{\tau}{2}}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|\frac{dv}{v}<2\|h\|_{\infty}\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\varepsilon.

Finally, by Lemma
2.7
and the boundedness of
h
, we estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{2}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)-h(w)|\frac{dv}{v}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|h\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{1}\bigvee\limits_{k\in N_{2}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})<2\|h\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{1}\varepsilon.

Combining these estimates gives

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)-h(w)\right|\leq C\varepsilon,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
n
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)=h(w).

Uniform convergence follows in a similar way when
h\in\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
h\in L_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\zeta}(\mathscr{I})
be fixed, and let
\psi
be a kernel satisfying
\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)<\infty
. Then there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]\leq\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)\|\Phi\|_{1}}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}I_{\zeta}\left[\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}{K\vartheta_{w}}h\right].

Proof.

We begin with

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\lambda\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)\right|\right)\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\lambda\left|\frac{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right|\right)\frac{dw}{w}.

Since
\vartheta_{w}
and
K
are positive constants, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda}{K\vartheta_{w}}\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}\right|\right)\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda}{K\vartheta_{w}}\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\frac{dw}{w}.

since
\zeta
is non-decreasing and
\mathscr{I}_{n}
is finite, we have

\displaystyle\zeta\left(\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}}a_{k}\right)=\left(\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}}\zeta(a_{k})\right).

(2)

Using (
2
), the convexity of
\zeta
, and the bound
|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|\leq\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)
, for all

we get

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a}^{b}\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda}{K\vartheta_{w}}|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a}^{b}\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\frac{|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)}\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)}{K\vartheta_{w}}n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})||h(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\frac{dw}{w}.

Replacing the maximum by summation and applying Jensen’s inequality yields

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[

\displaystyle\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}{K\vartheta_{w}}n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|}{\|\psi\|_{1}}|h(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\frac{dw}{w}.

By applying the Jensen’s inequality again, along with the Fubini-Tonelli theorem and the change of variable
t=e^{-k}v^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|\frac{dw}{w}\int\limits_{e^{-k}a^{n}}^{e^{-k}b^{n}}|\psi(t)|\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}{K\vartheta_{w}}|h((te^{k})^{\frac{1}{n}})|\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}n\int\limits_{a}^{b}|\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})|\frac{dw}{w}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}|\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}{K\vartheta_{w}}|h(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)\|\Phi\|_{1}}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}{K\vartheta_{w}}|h(u)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}.

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)]\leq\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)\|\Phi\|_{1}}{\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}I_{\zeta}\left[\frac{\lambda\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\Phi)\|\psi\|_{1}}{K\vartheta_{w}}h\right].

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

4
Max-min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators

In this section, we derive the pointwise and uniform convergence proper-
ties of the max-min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators in
the space
\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
, and further establish their modular convergence in the
Orlicz spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
h:\mathscr{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and
L^{1}
-integrable function on
\mathscr{I}
. Then the max-min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators associated with
h
and the kernels
\Phi
and
\psi
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w):=\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}},

where
\mathfrak{I}_{n}=\lceil{n\log{a}}\rceil,\cdots,\lfloor{n\log{b}}\rfloor.

Definition 4.2
.

For a given
w\in\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+},n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\tau>0
, we define

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}:=\{k=\lceil{n\log{a}}\rceil,\cdots,\lfloor{n\log{b}}\rfloor:\left|\frac{k}{n}-\log{w}\right|\leq\tau\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
43
]

Let
h,f:\mathscr{I}\to[0,1]
be two bounded functions. Then the following properties hold:

(b)

If
h(w)\leq f(w)
for all
w\in\mathscr{I}
, then

\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)\leq\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)(w),\quad\forall~w\in\mathscr{I}.

(c)

For all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for every
w\in\mathbb{R_{+}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)(w)\right|\leq\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(|h-f|)(w).

(d)

For all
w\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h+f)(w)\leq\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)+\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)(w).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathrm{h}:\mathscr{I}\to[0,1]
be a bounded and
L^{1}
-integrable function. Then, at any point of log continuity
w\in\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)=h(w).

Moreover, if
h\in\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
, then the convergence is uniform, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)-h\right\|_{\infty}=0,

where
\|.\|_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm.

Proof.

Let
h:\mathscr{I}\to[0,1]
be log-continuous at
w\in\mathscr{I}
. By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{M}(h)(w)-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(v)\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad-\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(w)\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(w)\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{1}+\mathsf{E}_{2}.

To estimate
\mathsf{E}_{1}
, using Lemmas
2.14
and
2.15
, we write

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left|\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})(h(v)-h(w))\frac{dv}{v}\right]\right|\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})\,n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\left|(h(v)-h(w))\right|\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})\,n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}.

We now split the maximum over the index set:

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\left|(h(v)-h(w))\right|\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}

\displaystyle\bigvee\bigvee\limits_{k\notin\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\left|(h(v)-h(w))\right|\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{E}_{1.1}\bigvee\mathsf{E}_{1.2}.

Since
h
is log-continuous at
w
, for every
\varepsilon>0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1.1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\;\varepsilon\;\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\varepsilon\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\epsilon\land 1\leq\epsilon.

For
\mathsf{E}_{1.2}
, noting that
a\land b\leq b
for all

, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{1.2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee\limits_{k\notin\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K\vartheta_{w}}\bigvee\limits_{k\notin\mathscr{B}_{\tau,n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})\leq\frac{cn^{-\nu}}{K\vartheta_{w}}\leq\epsilon

for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\nu
is as given in Lemma
2.7
.

Finally, consider
\mathsf{E}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{2}=\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})h(w)\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|.

Substituting
e^{-k}v^{n}=t
and using Lemmas
2.15
and
2.16
, we get

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}h(w)\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}(h(w)\wedge 1)\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|h(w)\wedge\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}1\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|h(w)\wedge\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}-h(w)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|h(w)\land 1-h(w)\right|=0.

Hence, combining the above estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)=h(w),

and if
h\in\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
, the convergence is uniform.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
h\in\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
. Then, for each
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-h\right)\right]=0.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the arguments used in Theorem
3.5
, with only notational modifications corresponding to the present operators
\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}
. Therefore, the detailed proof is omitted.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

For every
h,g\in L_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\zeta}(\mathscr{I})
and for each
\lambda>0
, we have

I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]\leq\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon(\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor-\lceil{n\log a}\rceil)+2\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\,(I_{\zeta}[\hat{\lambda}(h-f)])^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the modular functional and property (c) of Lemma
4.3
, we have

I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]=\int_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\lambda\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)(w)\right|\right)\frac{dw}{w}.

Since
\zeta
is non-decreasing and by the positivity of the operators, it follows that

\zeta\left(\lambda\left|\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)(w)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)(w)\right|\right)\leq\zeta\left(\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(|h-f|)(w)\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\lambda\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}\,(|h-f|)(w)\right)\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\lambda\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|h(v)-f(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right)\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left[\lambda n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|h(v)-f(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right]\wedge\frac{\lambda\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right)\frac{dw}{w}.

Using the monotonicity of
\zeta
and the finiteness of
\mathfrak{I}_{n}
, we employ

\displaystyle\zeta\left(\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}}a_{k}\right)=\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}}\zeta(a_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta\left(\bigwedge\limits_{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}}a_{k}\right)=\bigwedge\limits_{k\in\mathscr{I}_{n}}\zeta(a_{k}).

(4)

Applying (
4
) together with Lemma
2.13
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a}^{b}\left\{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\zeta\left(\lambda n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|h(v)-f(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right)\right\}\frac{dw}{w}.

Using the integrability and normalization of
\psi
, we can write

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a}^{b}n\left\{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\zeta\left(\lambda\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})|h(v)-f(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\zeta\left(\frac{\lambda\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})n\int\limits_{a}^{b}\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})\frac{dv}{v}}\right)\right\}\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a}^{b}n\left\{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}|h(v)-f(v)|\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}n\left\{\bigvee\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}n\left\{\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dw}{w}.

By applying the Fubini-Tonelli theorem, we can interchange the integration and summation to obtain

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}n\left\{\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(e^{-k}w^{n})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\mathcal{A}_{k}.

Denote each term by
\mathcal{A}_{k}
. For a fixed
j\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}
, substituting
y_{j}=e^{-j}w^{n}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(y_{j})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dy_{j}}{y_{j}}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\Phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, there exists
n_{j}>0
such that

\int\limits_{|\log(y_{j})|>n_{j}}\Phi(y_{j})\frac{dy_{j}}{{y_{j}}}<\varepsilon.

Denote
s_{j}:=(I_{\zeta}[\hat{\lambda}(h-f)])^{\frac{-\beta}{1+\beta}}>n_{j}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{j}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{|\log(y_{j})|>s_{j}}\left\{\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(y_{j})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dy_{j}}{y_{j}}

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{|\log(y_{j})|\leq s_{j}}\left\{\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\wedge\frac{\Phi(y_{j})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\right\}\frac{dy_{j}}{y_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{|\log(y_{j})|>s_{j}}\frac{\Phi(y_{j})}{K\vartheta_{w}}\zeta(\lambda)\frac{dy_{j}}{y_{j}}+\int\limits_{|\log(y_{j})|\leq s_{j}}\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}\right)\frac{dy_{j}}{y_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon+2s_{j}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}.

Summing over
k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}
yields

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathfrak{I}_{n}}\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon+2s_{j}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\frac{\psi(e^{-k}v^{n})}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon(\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor-\lceil{n\log a}\rceil)+2\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{1}}s_{j}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\zeta\left(\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}|h(v)-f(v)|\right)\frac{dv}{v}.

Finally, for
\lambda>0
satisfying
\lambda\|\psi\|_{1}<\hat{\lambda}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon(\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor-\lceil{n\log a}\rceil)+2\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{1}}(I_{\zeta}[\hat{\lambda}(h-f)])^{\frac{-\beta}{1+\beta}}\;\cdot I_{\zeta}[\hat{\lambda}(h-f)]

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon(\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor-\lceil{n\log a}\rceil)+2\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{1}}(I_{\zeta}[\hat{\lambda}(h-f)])^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
h\in L_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\zeta}(\mathscr{I})
. Then, for each
\lambda>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-h\right)\right]=0.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and fix
\bar{\lambda}>0
. By Lemma
3.6
, there exists
f\in\mathscr{U}_{bl}(\mathscr{I})
such that

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}[\hat{\lambda}(h-f)]<\varepsilon^{1+\beta},\quad\text{ for some }~\beta>0.

(5)

Furthermore, by Theorem
4.5
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\bar{\lambda}\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)-f\right)\right]<\varepsilon,\quad\text{ for all}~n\geq n_{0}.

(6)

Now choose
\lambda>0
satisfying
3\lambda(1+\|\psi\|_{1})<\bar{\lambda}
. For each
n\geq n_{0}
, by the convexity of
\zeta
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}\,(h)-h\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\left\{I_{\zeta}\left[3\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left.I_{\zeta}\left[3\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)-f\right)\right]+I_{\zeta}\left[3\lambda\left(f-h\right)\right]\right\}.

Applying Theorem
4.6
together with (
5
) and (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\zeta}\left[\lambda\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(h)-h\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\left\{\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon(\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor-\lceil{n\log a}\rceil)+2\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{1}}(I_{\zeta}[\bar{\lambda}(h-f)])^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.I_{\zeta}\left[\bar{\lambda}\left(\mathscr{D}_{n,\Phi,\psi}^{m}(f)-f\right)\right]+I_{\zeta}\left[\bar{\lambda}\left(f-h\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\left\{\frac{\zeta(\lambda)}{K\vartheta_{w}}\varepsilon(\lfloor{n\log b}\rfloor-\lceil{n\log a}\rceil)+2\frac{\mathscr{M}_{0}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{1}}\varepsilon+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{1+\beta}\right\}.

Since all terms on the right-hand side tend to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the desired result follows.
∎

Comparative Discussion

Across all kernel configurations, the max-product operators consistently outperform their max-min counterparts in smooth regions, owing to their inherent multiplicative smoothing behavior that effectively suppresses oscillations and preserves the overall curvature of the target functions. In contrast, the max-min operators exhibit superior stability near discontinuities, where they efficiently mitigate the influence of local extrema. The steady error decay documented in all numerical tables substantiates the theoretical convergence results established in the preceding sections. Moreover, the use of higher-order kernels leads to smoother approximations and faster convergence, underscoring their enhanced efficiency in representing both continuous and piecewise-defined functions.

6
Conclusion

This work introduced two nonlinear approximation schemes the max–product and max–min Durrmeyer-type exponential sampling operators formulated within the framework of Orlicz spaces. Also, we established convergence in pointwise and uniform senses and analyzed the operators’ approximation behavior using exponential-type Mellin kernels such as the Mellin
B
-spline, Mellin–Jackson, and Mellin–Fejér kernels,.

Theoretical and numerical results confirmed that both operators exhibit stable convergence, with higher-order kernels ensuring smoother and faster approximation. The max–product operators perform better in smooth regions, while the max–min variants demonstrate superior stability near discontinuities.

Future extensions may explore multidimensional forms, stochastic convergence, and adaptive kernel strategies to enhance accuracy in non-smooth and high-frequency domains.

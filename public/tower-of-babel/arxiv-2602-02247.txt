A new Energy Equation Derivation for the Shallow Water Linearized Moment Equations
Julian Koellermeier

Abstract
Shallow Water Moment Equations (SWME) are extensions to the well-known Shallow Water Equations (SWE) for the efficient modeling and numerical simulation of free-surface flows. While the SWE typically assume a depth-averaged vertical velocity profile, the SWME allow for vertical variations of the velocity profile. The SWME therefore assume a polynomial profile and then derive additional evolution equations for the polynomial coefficients via higher order depth integration. In this work, we perform a new systematic derivation of the energy equation for a specific variant of the SWME, called the Shallow Water Linearized Moment Equations (SWLME). The derivation is based on the standard SWE energy equation derivation and includes the skew-symmetric formulation of the model. The new systematic derivation is beneficial for the extension to other SWME variants and their numerical solution.

0.1
Shallow Water Equations and energy conservation

Shallow flows are occurring in a wide range of free-surface flow scenarios, ranging from ocean or river waves to avalanches and mud flows. These shallow flows are characterized by situations in which the surface wave length is much larger than the water depth. For the purpose of this work, we consider a simple 1D setting, i.e., neglecting variations of the geometry or velocity in the lateral (spanwise) direction. Furthermore, we assume a hydrostatic pressure and neglect friction terms. Non-hydrostatic extensions and friction terms can readily be found in
Kowalski
;
Scholz2024
. Note that the assumption of shallow flows is typically only valid for small bed slopes
Liggett
.

The most commonly used model to simulate such flows is given by the Shallow Water Equations (SWE). They assume a depth-averaged velocity and are derived using depth-averaging of the underlying Navier-Stokes equations. In a 1D setting with known bottom topography term
b
, the system of two PDEs for the water height
h(t,x)
and the depth-averaged velocity
u_{m}(t,x)
reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}\right)=0,\quad\quad\partial_{t}\left(hu_{m}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}\right)=-gh\partial_{x}b.

(1)

It is well known that for the SWE (
1
) an energy equation can be derived as
Gassner

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb\right)+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+ghu_{m}(h+b)\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(2)

in which the energy
e=\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb
is a conserved quantity and an entropy function with entropy flux
f=\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+ghu_{m}(h+b)
. This allows tailored numerical methods to preserve energy discretely during numerical simulations
Ersing2025
;
Gassner
;
Parisot
.

One way to account for the effects of vertical variations of the horizontal velocity
u(t,x,z)
over
z
is to augment the non-linear term
hu_{m}^{2}
in (
1
) by a so-called Boussinesq coefficient
\beta

Liggett
, defined as

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{1}{hu_{m}^{2}}\int_{b(t,x)}^{h(t,x)}u(t,x,z)^{2}\,dz.

(3)

However, since the precise velocity distribution
u(t,x,z)
is not known in the SWE (
1
), assumptions are needed to model
\beta
, e.g., by assuming a certain shape for
u(t,x,z)
. This ultimately restricts the SWE to only one velocity variable which is the averaged flow velocity
u_{m}(t,x)
.

0.2
Shallow Water Moment Equations

The standard SWE model (
1
) is limited by the assumption of depth-averaging, which leads to a single variable
u_{m}(t,x)
representing the velocity profile (with the possible inclusion of shape factors or the Boussinesq coefficient
\beta
(
3
)). This can lead to model inaccuracy in case of velocity profiles that vary significantly in the vertical direction. In fact, this was mentioned in
Liggett
as one of the main causes for errors in open-channel flow calculations. As a way to tackle that, extended shallow water models have been derived. One of these extended models is called Shallow Water Moment Equations (SWME)
Kowalski
.

The SWME uses the vertical velocity expansion

\displaystyle u(t,x,z)=u_{m}(t,x)+\sum_{i=0}^{N}u_{i}(t,x)\phi_{i}\left(\frac{z-b(t,x)}{h(t,x)}\right),

(4)

where
\zeta=\frac{z-b(t,x)}{h(t,x)}\in[0,1]
is a scaled vertical variable and
u_{i}(t,x)
and
\phi_{i}(\zeta)
for
i=1,\ldots,N
are the polynomial coefficients and the orthogonal Legendre polynomials on
[0,1]
, respectively
Kowalski
.

Remark 1

Note that the expansion (
4
) is the same as a dynamically changing Boussinesq coefficient
\beta
(
3
), which becomes a function of the polynomial coefficients
u_{i}(t,x)
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
given by

\displaystyle\beta=1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2i+1}\frac{u_{i}(t,x)^{2}}{u_{m}(t,x)^{2}}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2i+1}(Mu)_{i}^{2},

(5)

where
(Mu)_{i}=\frac{u_{i}(t,x)}{u_{m}(t,x)}
measures the size of the polynomial coefficient
u_{i}(t,x)
relative to the depth-averaged velocity
u_{m}(t,x)
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
. Interestingly, the term
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2i+1}(Mu)_{i}^{2}
also appears in the computation of Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions for the below discussed SWLME model in
Koellermeier2022
. This illustrates that the shape of
u(t,x,z)
has direct impact on properties of the model that can now be modeled explicitly, instead of relying on a single variable
u_{m}(t,x)
and an unknown coefficient
\beta
.

It is clear that
h(t,x),u_{m}(t,x),u_{i}(t,x)
change with time
t
and space
x
. From now on, we drop the explicit dependence of the variables on
t,x
to be concise. Furthermore, we assume a given bottom topography
b(t,x)=b(x)
that is constant in time.

Evolution equations for the variables
h,u_{m},u_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
are derived via higher-order depth integration of the Navier-Stokes equations, resulting in
N+2
equations: one equation for
h
(continuity equation), one equation for
hu_{m}
(depth-averaged momentum balance), and
N
additional equations for
hu_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
(higher order moment equations), given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}\right)=0,\quad\partial_{t}\!\left(hu_{m}\right)+\partial_{x}\!\left(hu_{m}^{2}+h\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}+\!\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}\!\right)\!=-gh\partial_{x}b,

(6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\!\left(hu_{i}\right)+\partial_{x}\!\left(\!h\!\left(\!2u_{m}u_{i}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}A_{ijk}u_{j}u_{k}\!\right)\!\right)\!=u_{m}\partial_{x}\!\left(hu_{i}\right)\!-\!\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\!B_{ijk}u_{k}\partial_{x}\left(hu_{j}\right)\!,

(7)

with
A_{ijk}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}d\zeta
and
B_{ijk}=(2i+1)\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{i}^{\prime}\left(\int_{0}^{\zeta}\phi_{j}d\zeta\right)\phi_{k}d\zeta
, for
i,j,k=1,\ldots,N
, see
Kowalski
.

Different variants of the SWME (
6
)-(
7
) exist by approximating the coefficients
A_{ijk}
and
B_{ijk}
, see
Koellermeier2022
;
Koellermeier2020
. As one example, using
A_{ijk}=0,B_{ijk}=0
leads to the so-called Shallow Water Linearized Moment Equations (SWLME)
Koellermeier2022
. Originally, the SWLME were derived as a simple way to obtain analytical steady states that are extensions of the classical Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions of the SWE. However, the following energy equation for the SWLME was derived in
Caballero

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb\right)+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+\frac{3hu_{m}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+ghu_{m}(h+b)\right)

\displaystyle=0.

(8)

This energy equation (
8
) can be seen as an extension of the standard SWE energy equation (
2
). We note that the SWLME energy equation (
8
) takes into account the higher order coefficients
u_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
in the energy.

The derivation of the energy equation (
8
) in
Caballero
was only a side remark and left out several intermediate steps, that could inform the generalization to more models of similar type. In this paper, we therefore give a new derivation based on an extension of the step-by-step derivation for the standard SWE model given in
Gassner
.

0.3
Energy Equation Derivation for the SWLME

The frictionless 1D SWLME system with bottom bathymetry as derived in
Koellermeier2022
, consists of
N+2
equations for
h
,
hu_{m}
and
hu_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
and is written as

\displaystyle(\textrm{C}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h+\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(9)

\displaystyle(\textrm{M}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(hu_{m}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}^{2}+h\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-gh\partial_{x}b,

(10)

\displaystyle(u_{i}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(hu_{i}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(2hu_{m}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=u_{m}\partial_{x}\left(hu_{i}\right),

(11)

with (C) indicating the continuity equation, (M) the momentum balance, and (
u_{i}
) the moment coefficient
i
balance, for
i=1,\ldots,N
.

Note again that the SWLME is a special case of the SWME (
6
)-(
7
) as it uses the coefficients

A_{ijk}=0,\quad B_{ijk}=0.

(12)

We will now derive an energy equation for the SWLME (
9
)-(
11
) step-by-step.

Firstly, the potential energy
(\textrm{P})
is obtained in exactly the same way as for the SWE
Gassner
by computing
g(h+b)\cdot(\textrm{C})=(\textrm{P})
, which results in

\displaystyle(\textrm{P}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb\right)+g(h+b)\partial_{x}(hu_{m})

\displaystyle=0,

(13)

where
g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb
denotes the potential energy.

Secondly, we aim to derive an equation for the (averaged) kinetic energy.
We notice that the momentum balance (M) can be written as

\displaystyle(\textrm{M'}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(hu_{m}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}^{2}+h\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}\right)+gh\partial_{x}(h+b)

\displaystyle=0.

(14)

We can then compute
(\textrm{M'})-u_{m}\cdot(\textrm{C})
to obtain the new equation (A) as

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\underbrace{\partial_{t}(hu_{m})-u_{m}\partial_{t}h}+\underbrace{\partial_{x}(hu_{m}^{2})-u_{m}\partial_{x}(hu_{m})}+gh\partial_{x}(h+b)+\partial_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{hu_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=0,

(15)

\displaystyle(\textrm{A}):

\displaystyle h\partial_{t}u_{m}\quad\quad+\quad\quad\quad hu_{m}\partial_{x}u_{m}\quad\quad+gh\partial_{x}(h+b)+\partial_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{hu_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=0.

(16)

Next, we average (A) and (M’) to obtain
(\textrm{S})=\frac{1}{2}(\textrm{A})+\frac{1}{2}(\textrm{M'})

\displaystyle(\textrm{S}):

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{t}(hu_{m})+h\partial_{t}u_{m}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}^{2}\right)+hu_{m}\partial_{x}u_{m}\right)+gh\partial_{x}(h+b)+\partial_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{hu_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=0.

(17)

Note that
(\textrm{S})
is skew-symmetric in the
u_{m}
-derivatives, see
Gassner
.

We can then compute
u_{m}\cdot(\textrm{S})
to obtain an equation for the kinetic energy (K)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{1}{2}(\underbrace{u_{m}\partial_{t}(hu_{m})+hu_{m}\partial_{t}u_{m}})+\frac{1}{2}(\underbrace{u_{m}\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}^{2}\right)+hu_{m}^{2}\partial_{x}u_{m}})+ghu_{m}\partial_{x}(h+b)+u_{m}\partial_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{hu_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=0

(18)

\displaystyle(\textrm{K}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}\right)\quad\quad+\quad\quad\quad\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}\right)\quad\quad\quad+ghu_{m}\partial_{x}(h+b)+u_{m}\partial_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{hu_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=0,

(19)

where
\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}
denotes the kinetic energy.

Thirdly, we compute an equation from the moment equations (
u_{i}
) by following the same recipe as above, only applied to (
u_{i}
) instead of (M). We start by computing the alternative moment equation (
u_{i}^{\prime}
) using elementary changes as

\displaystyle(u_{i}^{\prime}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\!\left(hu_{i}\right)+\partial_{x}\!\left(\!hu_{m}u_{i}\right)+hu_{i}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0.

(20)

Then we compute
(\textrm{A$u_{i}$})=(\textrm{$u_{i}^{\prime}$})-u_{i}\cdot(\textrm{C})
as

\displaystyle\underbrace{\partial_{t}(hu_{i})-u_{i}\partial_{t}h}+\underbrace{\partial_{x}(hu_{m}u_{i})-u_{i}\partial_{x}(hu_{m})}+hu_{i}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(21)

\displaystyle(\textrm{A$u_{i}$}):

\displaystyle h\partial_{t}u_{i}\quad\quad+\quad\quad\quad hu_{m}\partial_{x}u_{i}\quad\quad\quad+hu_{i}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(22)

Next, we again average
(\textrm{A}u_{i})
and
(u_{i}^{\prime})
to obtain
(\textrm{S}u_{i})=\frac{1}{2}(\textrm{A}u_{i})+\frac{1}{2}(u_{i}^{\prime})

\displaystyle(\textrm{S}u_{i}):

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{t}(hu_{i})+h\partial_{t}u_{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}u_{i}\right)+hu_{m}\partial_{x}u_{i}\right)+hu_{i}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0.

(23)

Note that similar to before
(\textrm{S}u_{i})
is skew-symmetric in the
u_{i}
-derivatives, see
Gassner
.

We then compute
u_{i}\cdot(\textrm{S}u_{i})
to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\underbrace{u_{i}\partial_{t}(hu_{i})+hu_{i}\partial_{t}u_{i}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\underbrace{u_{i}\partial_{x}\left(hu_{m}u_{i}\right)+hu_{m}u_{i}\partial_{x}u_{i}}\right)+hu_{i}^{2}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(24)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{i}^{2}}{2}\right)\quad\quad+\quad\quad\quad\quad\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}u_{i}^{2}}{2}\right)\quad\quad\quad+hu_{i}^{2}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(25)

or equivalently

\displaystyle(\textrm{K}u_{i}):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{h}{2}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}}{2}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)+\frac{hu_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\partial_{x}u_{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(26)

in which
\frac{h}{2}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}
can be seen as a ”partial kinetic energy” including the effect of the moment coefficients
u_{i}
.

We can then compute an equation for the total kinetic energy
(\textrm{K}u)
using the formula
(\textrm{K}u)=(\textrm{K})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\textrm{K}u_{i})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)+\partial_{x}\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+ghu_{m}\partial_{x}(h+b)\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\underbrace{u_{m}\partial_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{hu_{j}^{2}}{2j+1}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}h}{2i+1}\partial_{x}u_{m}+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)}

\displaystyle=0,

(27)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left(\frac{3hu_{m}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

which means that we have for the total kinetic energy
(\textrm{K$u$})
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)+\partial_{x}\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+ghu_{m}\partial_{x}(h+b)+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{3hu_{m}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(28)

where
\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}
denotes the total kinetic energy.

By adding the equations for the total kinetic energy (K
u
) and the potential energy (P), we obtain an equation for the total energy (E):

\displaystyle\!\partial_{t}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb\right)+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+\frac{3hu_{m}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+ghu_{m}(h+b)\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(29)

where we denote the total energy
e=\frac{hu_{m}^{2}}{2}+\frac{h}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb
. It can directly be seen that the total energy
e
is a conserved quantity and an entropy function with entropy flux
f=\frac{hu_{m}^{3}}{2}+\frac{3hu_{m}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+ghu_{m}(h+b)
.

The energy
e
can be written in convective variables
(h,q,r_{i})=(h,hu_{m},hu_{i})
as

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2h}+\frac{1}{2h}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+g\frac{h^{2}}{2}+ghb.

(30)

This means that the associated entropy variables can readily be computed as

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{h}e=-\frac{u_{m}^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}+g(h+b),

(31)

\displaystyle q_{2}

\displaystyle=\partial_{q}e=u_{m},

(32)

\displaystyle q_{u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{r_{i}}e=\frac{u_{i}}{2i+1}.

(33)

We note that the total energy equation can therefore equivalently be obtained by computing
(E)=q_{1}\cdot(\textrm{C})+q_{2}\cdot(\textrm{M})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}q_{u_{i}}\cdot(\textrm{$u_{i}$})
.

0.4
Conclusion

This paper presented a new systematic energy equation derivation for the SWLME model of free-surface flows.

The SWLME energy equation (
29
) is the same as (
8
) derived in
Caballero
. However, the derivation presented in this paper is based on an extension of the SWE energy equation derivation in
Gassner
. It reveals as a byproduct a skew-symmetric form of both the momentum equation (
17
) and the moment equations (
23
).

While the derived energy equation (
29
) is specific to the SWLME system (
9
)-(
11
), the derivation of the energy follows a clear set of steps. These steps are an extension of the energy equation derivation for the standard SWE in
Gassner
. We therefore believe that the derivation in this paper is useful for the derivation of energy equations for more generalized systems like the SWME
Kowalski
or dispersive models
Scholz2024
. In addition, the skew-symmetric formulation of the problem could be used to derive entropy conservative numerical schemes similar to
Bohm2020
;
Ersing2024
;
Ersing2025
;
Gassner
.

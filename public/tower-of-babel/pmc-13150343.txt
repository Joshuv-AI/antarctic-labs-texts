Para‐Hisian Extra Pacing Enables Unmasking of Hidden Slow Retrograde Conduction
Authors: Sho Okamura, Noboru Oda, Takehito Tokuyama, Yousaku Okubo, Yukiko Nakano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the presence of dual retrograde pathways, conventional Para‐Hisian pacing may fail to reveal the slow pathway, likely due to masking by fast retrograde conduction. In such cases, Para‐Hisian extra pacing—combining programmed right ventricular stimulation with differential His bundle capture—enables assessment of the slow retrograde conduction.

A 25‐year‐old woman was admitted to our hospital for an electrophysiological study and catheter ablation due to frequent palpitations. A 12‐lead electrocardiogram taken during sinus rhythm showed no delta waves. During supraventricular tachycardia (SVT), a short RP interval was observed. The atrio‐His (AH) and His‐ventricular (HV) intervals at baseline were 106 and 46 ms, respectively. Retrograde conduction exhibited dual pathways, both with decremental properties. The earliest atrial activation site of fast retrograde conduction was the His bundle region, whereas that of slow retrograde conduction was at the mid to distal coronary sinus (CS). However, as the CS catheter could not be advanced deeply (Figure 1A), the anatomically earliest site was presumed to be near the CS ostium. Para‐Hisian pacing demonstrated fast retrograde conduction over the fast pathway of the atrioventricular (AV) node, because loss of His capture prolonged the VA interval.

Atrial extrastimuli induced an AH interval jump, confirming anterograde conduction through dual AV nodal pathways. A sustained SVT1 with a short RP interval was reproducibly induced after the AH jump. During SVT1, the AH interval was 391 ms, and the HA interval was 79 ms. Atrial and ventricular activation occurred simultaneously, with the earliest atrial activation recorded in the His bundle region. The A‐wave sequence during the SVT1 resembled fast retrograde conduction. Right ventricular (RV) overdrive pacing accelerated the atrial cycle length, confirming entrainment, with a V–A–V response observed upon cessation of pacing (Figure 1B). Based on these findings, SVT1 was diagnosed as slow–fast atrioventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT).

Programmed RV stimulation also induced a second tachycardia (SVT2) characterized by a long RP interval and a V–A–V response (Figure 1C). During SVT2, the AH and HA intervals were 123 and 317 ms, respectively. The earliest atrial activation occurred in the mid to distal CS. However, SVT2 was difficult to induce and readily terminated by pacing, precluding further electrophysiological assessment, including adenosine triphosphate administration and right ventricular extrastimulation during the tachycardia.

Cases like SVT2 in this patient, where the tachycardia shows poor inducibility and terminates readily, can limit detailed electrophysiological evaluation and make precise mechanistic differentiation challenging. In this context, SVT2 was initiated with a V‐A‐V response, and the A‐wave sequence was highly consistent with that of slow retrograde conduction, making atrial tachycardia an unlikely mechanism. The primary differential diagnosis was between AVNRT and atrioventricular reciprocating tachycardia (AVRT), specifically whether slow retrograde conduction was mediated by an accessory pathway (AP) with slow conduction properties or a slow AV node pathway. Confirming tachycardia resetting with His‐synchronous RV pacing, RV entrainment, and para‐Hisian entrainment can be useful to differentiate if slow retrograde conduction is due to an AP or a slow pathway [1]. However, in this case, whereas the SVT1 was easily inducible, the SVT2 was difficult to induce and readily terminated by stimulation, limiting the feasibility of these evaluations.

Para‐Hisian pacing is useful for differentiating retrograde conduction via a septal AP from conduction over the AV node [2]. However, it is important to remember that fast retrograde conduction can obscure slow retrograde conduction. In this case, conventional para‐Hisian pacing may not have been able to assess the slow retrograde conduction due to masking by fast pathway conduction. Therefore, taking into account that retrograde AV nodal conduction may depend on the pacing cycle length, we performed programmed ventricular stimulation combined with para‐Hisian pacing [3]. High‐output pacing captured both the RV and the His–Purkinje system, whereas low‐output pacing captured only the RV. This para‐Hisian extra pacing confirmed that the slow retrograde conduction with the earliest activation at the mid to distal CS was the slow pathway of the AV node, as loss of His capture prolonged the VA interval while preserving the same retrograde atrial activation sequence (Figure 2). Mapping the retrograde conduction of the slow pathway with RV extrastimuli revealed the earliest activation site within the CS, indicating fast‐slow AVNRT with the left inferior extension of the slow pathway [4]. Radiofrequency catheter ablation of this site and the antegrade slow pathway eliminated tachycardia and also resulted in the disappearance of retrograde conduction through the slow pathway (Figure 3).

Para‐Hisian extra pacing is useful for evaluating slow retrograde conduction, which can be masked by fast retrograde conduction. While conventional para‐Hisian pacing is primarily used to differentiate septal accessory pathway conduction from AV nodal conduction. However, it may fail to identify slow retrograde conduction when fast pathway conduction predominates. In such situations, para‐Hisian extra pacing combined with programmed ventricular stimulation may reveal slow pathway conduction.

However, this method has several limitations. When the refractory periods of the fast and slow retrograde pathways are similar, para‐Hisian extra pacing may not clearly differentiate the two conduction pathways. Furthermore, accurate interpretation requires stable pacing conditions that reliably achieve both His bundle plus ventricular capture and ventricular capture alone. This case highlights the practical value of para‐Hisian extra pacing in distinguishing fast‐slow AVNRT from AVRT via a septal accessory pathway when conventional diagnostic maneuvers are limited.

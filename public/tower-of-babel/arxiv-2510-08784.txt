Taming forward scattering singularities in partial waves
Marta Fuentes Zamoro, Benjamín Grinstein, Pablo Quílez

Abstract
Perturbative partial-wave amplitudes diverge in cases with a massless exchanged particle in the t t -channel. We argue that the divergence is an artifact of perturbation theory and give a prescription for the all-orders correction factor that renders the partial waves finite. As an example, we apply this to longitudinal W + ​ W − W^{+}W^{-} elastic scattering, for which there is a photon exchange t t -channel contribution, and derive improved quasi-perturbative unitarity bounds on the mass of the Higgs. The method is also useful for t t -channel exchange of very light particles.

The partial-wave decomposition plays an important role in many advances in particle physics. It is used in analysis of experimental data to uncover the quantum numbers of resonances seen in scattering. On the theory side, it is used extensively in exploiting the consequences of unitarity on scattering amplitudes. In this vein, the conditions that unitarity imposes on partial waves were central to establishing upper bounds on scattering amplitudes at asymptotically large center of mass (CM) energies
Froissart 1961
;
Martin 1969
. Famously, partial-wave unitarity was used to establish the strength of Higgs self interactions, or, equivalently, the Higgs mass, for which perturbation theory remains applicable
Lee et al. 1977a
;
Lee et al. 1977b
;
Dicus and Mathur 1973
. Among many other examples, an extension of that idea has been more recently used to obtain bounds on di-boson scalar resonances
Di Luzio et al. 2017
, on multi-Higgs extensions of the Standard Model (SM)
Grinstein et al. 2014
;
Grinstein et al. 2016
, on the strength of ALP interactions
Brivio et al. 2021
and on dimension-6 and -8 operators in an effective field theory extension of the SM (SMEFT)
Corbett et al. 2015
;
Corbett et al. 2017
;
Remmen and Rodd 2019
;
Cohen et al. 2022
.

Unitarity bounds at the electroweak scale play a key role in defining the Higgs Effective Field Theory (HEFT) which displays non-decoupling new physics
Cohen et al. 2021
;
Banta et al. 2022
, in contrast to SMEFT in which the new physics decouples.

However, when a massless particle is exchanged in the
t
-channel, the resulting scattering amplitude displays a singularity in the forward direction, a non-integrable divergence that renders the partial-wave amplitudes ill-defined. This is of course well known: it is precisely the same divergence displayed by the Rutherford scattering amplitude. In this letter we give a prescription that renders the partial waves finite. The clue to the resolution is to include in the computation non-perturbative effects. Short of proving this assertion, we will present evidence that we think is both suggestive and persuasive, and hope to come back to providing a proof in the future.

The partial-wave amplitude for elastic scattering is given by

(1)

Let us now explore how this may be of use. Tree-level unitarity was famously used to place a limit on the mass of the Higgs boson prior to its discovery
Dicus and Mathur 1973
;
Lee et al. 1977a
;
Lee et al. 1977b
. As long as the CM momentum
p
is smaller than the Higgs mass
m_{H}
, the amplitudes for scattering of longitudinally polarized gauge bosons and/or the Higgs boson computed at tree level grow with increasing
p

m_{H}
that exceeds some critical value,
m_{c}
, and for sufficiently large
p\leq m_{c}
partial-wave amplitudes violate the inequality
Eq.

2
, and this leads to the perturbative unitarity bound
m_{H}\leq m_{c}
. Unitarity is guaranteed for SM amplitudes, and it can be shown on general grounds that amplitudes do not grow indefinitely and never violate the unitarity bound
Cornwall et al. 1974
;
Vayonakis 1976
. For
m_{H}>m_{c}
the corrections to tree-level amplitudes become significant and the model becomes non-perturbative. Therefore, for a Higgs mass that satisfies the bound
m_{H}<m_{c}
the model remains perturbative, while for
m_{H}>m_{c}
one obtains a strongly coupled Higgs sector.

As Ref.
Lee et al. 1977a
, hereafter LQT, explains, one may characterize the growth of tree-level partial-wave amplitudes according to

a_{\ell}=A(p/m_{W})^{4}+B(p/m_{W})^{2}+C\,.

(12)

Following LQT, we refer to these as
A
,
B
and
C
“forces”. Of the processes considered there, we will only consider
W_{L}^{+}W_{L}^{-}\to W_{L}^{+}W_{L}^{-}
scattering because only for this process does a photon
t
-channel exchange contribute to the amplitude.
A

B

m_{c}
is obtained by requiring that the
C

Eq.

2

-channel photon exchange contribution makes the partial waves ill-defined. Even worse, in the large

Z
-exchange contribution also gives a divergent contribution to the
C

In this work, we have proposed a way of dealing with the divergences associated with massless propagators in the
t
-channel in the computation of partial-wave amplitudes. By incorporating an oscillating phase, we are able to tame the divergence, rendering it integrable. Still a first-principle derivation of this method remains to be demonstrated.
Applying this method to longitudinal
W^{+}W^{-}
elastic scattering, we derive an improved quasi-perturbative bound on the Higgs mass to be compared to the classical result of LQT
Lee et al. 1977a
.
The results of this work have wider applications ranging from quasi-perturbative unitarity bounds on EFTs including massless particles to extensions of the SM containing very light massive particles.

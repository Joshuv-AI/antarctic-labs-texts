Stability conditions for coherent systems on Integral Curves
Marcos Jardim, Leonardo Roa-Leguizamón, Renato Vidal Martins

Abstract
. We present stability conditions for the category of coherent systems on an integral curve. We define a three-parameter family of pre-stability conditions in its derived category using tilting, and we then investigate when these conditions qualify as true stability conditions. Additionally, we examine the semistability of specific objects under these conditions, namely: torsion, free, and complete tilted systems, without relying on the support property.

1.
Introduction

The study of vector bundles and their moduli space on algebraic curves is a central theme in algebraic geometry, with deep connections to mathematical physics, integrable systems, differential geometry, and number theory (see, for instance,
[
BGMN2
,
Lu
,
Ty
]
). A crucial problem is to determine the geometry of the moduli space in terms of the existence and structure of its subvarieties. One of the subvarieties that has been of great interest is Brill–Noether subvarieties. A Brill–Noether locus is a subset of the moduli space whose points correspond to bundles having at least
k
independent global sections. The primary goal of Brill-Noether theory is to study these subsets, specifically addressing questions related to non-emptiness, connectedness, irreducibility, dimension, singularities, and topological and geometric structures. For line bundles on curves (Classical Brill-Noether theory), many of these questions have been answered
[
Ke
,
KL72
,
KL74
,
FL
,
GH
]
, but much less is known about higher rank vector bundles, or on singular curves
[
CR
,
BDG
]
. A first approach to studying Brill–Noether subvarieties is by determining an upper bound for the dimension of the space of sections. Clifford’s Theorem establishes that for any semistable vector bundle
E
on a smooth curve of rank
n
and degree
d
with
0\leq d/n\leq 2g-2
,
h^{0}(E)={\rm{dim}}\,H^{0}(E)\leq d/2+n

[
BGN
]
.

A fruitful generalization of a vector bundle is the concept of a
coherent system
. A coherent system can be thought of as a vector bundle together with a distinguished subspace of its global sections (also called Brill–Noether pair
[
NMo
]
). More formally, a
coherent system
of type
(n,d,k)
on
C
is a triple
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)
consisting of a coherent sheaf
E
of rank
n
and degree
d
, a
k
-dimensional vector space
V
, and a linear map
\varphi:V\to H^{0}(E)
. In addition, a morphism between coherent systems
(E,V,\varphi)
and
(E^{\prime},V^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
is given by a linear map
f:V\to V^{\prime}
and a sheaf map
h:E\to E^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}\circ f=H^{0}(h)\circ\varphi
. In particular, if the vector bundle is stable, then the existence of such objects is equivalent to the non-emptiness of higher rank Brill–Noether subvarieties. This connection motivates the study of coherent systems (see, for instance,
[
BGMN1
]
).

The category of coherent systems on
C
denoted by
{\rm CohSys}(C)
is also an example of a
comma category
, see
[
JNO
, Example 3.2]
for details. In particular,
{\rm CohSys}(C)
is abelian and noetherian, see for instance
[
JNO
,
He
,
NMo
]
.

This simple definition significantly enriches the geometry. While the moduli space of stable vector bundles itself is a rich object, the theory of coherent systems allows us to stratify and study it by imposing conditions on the existence of families of sections. They provide a powerful framework for investigating the Brill–Noether theory of vector bundles. The study of linear systems, that is, rank 1 coherent systems for which the morphism
\varphi
is injective, also has a vast literature, and it is closely related to the geometry of the curve itself; the best general reference here is
[
ACGH
]
. For a systematic treatment of coherent systems on smooth curves, we refer the reader to
[
BGPMN
,
He
,
LP
]

A fundamental step, as with vector bundles, is to define a notion of stability to construct well-behaved moduli spaces of coherent systems. The standard approach is to define the
slope
of a coherent system. This notion of stability depends on a real parameter
\alpha
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. For a coherent system
\mathcal{E}
of type
(n,d,k)
, the
\alpha
-slope
is defined as:

\mu_{\alpha}(E,V)=\frac{d}{n}+\alpha\frac{k}{n}.

A coherent system
\mathcal{E}
is called
\mu_{\alpha}
-(semi)stable
if for every proper coherent subsystem
\mathcal{F}
, the following inequality holds:

\mu_{\alpha}(E^{\prime},V^{\prime})<(\leq)~\mu_{\alpha}(E,V).

The moduli space for coherent systems on curves was constructed by King and Newstead
[
NMo
]
, and by Raghavendra and Vishwanath
[
RaVi
]
. Moreover, the moduli space of coherent systems presents a rich display of topological and geometric phenomena in Variational Geometric Invariant Theory (VGIT) (see
[
BGPMN
,
Th
]
for more details). For a recent account of the theory and general open questions, we refer the reader to
[
Ne
,
New
]
.

While much of the literature on coherent systems is devoted to smooth curves, we turn our attention to
integral curves
, by which we mean a complete, integral, one-dimensional scheme over an algebraically closed field. One of the goals of this paper is to generalize some of the well-known results in the literature about coherent systems on smooth curves to coherent systems on integral curves.

A more recent trend in the theory of moduli spaces, initiated by T. Bridgeland almost 20 years ago, is the upgrading of stability conditions on abelian categories (like the category of coherent sheaves or the category of representations of a quiver) to triangulated categories, especially the derived categories of sheaves or representations. Bridgeland’s notion of stability conditions on triangulated categories, introduced in
[
Bridgeland
]
and
[
Bridgeland1
]
, provides a new set of tools to study moduli spaces of sheaves on smooth projective varieties. Such tools have been successfully applied by many authors first to the study of sheaves on surfaces, for example,
[
ABCH
,
BM14a
,
Bridgeland1
,
Macri-Schmidt
]
, and more recently on threefolds
[
BMT
,
BMS
,
JLMM
,
Li
,
PT
]
. However, there has been little advance in the study of stability conditions on categories of
decorated sheaves
; we are only aware of
[
RHR
]
and the very recent preprint
[
FN
]
, whose content partially overlaps with the present paper. The second goal of this paper is to provide a construction of stability conditions on the category of coherent systems and present certain classes of stable objects.

Let us present the organization of this paper and its main results. Section 2 is dedicated to revising the notation, basic definitions, and results regarding stability conditions and tilting theory. The following two sections focus on results related to curves, which will serve as the foundation for constructing stability conditions.

In Section
3
, we generalize Clifford’s Theorem for vector bundles to torsion-free sheaves that are not necessarily semistable on integral curves, as outlined in Theorem
3.2
. The proof heavily relies on the rank 1 case, which was established in
[
EKS
,
KM
]
. We then explore the conditions under which equality holds, referencing
[
Ne
]
, and introduce Clifford indices while recalling some well-known inequalities for low slopes obtained in
[
BGN
,
Mer
]
.

Section
4
provides a brief summary of the main results concerning coherent systems on integral curves. We present a bound on the dimension of a coherent system in Theorem
4.7
and a generalization of Clifford’s Theorem for coherent systems in Theorem
4.8
. The former result will be crucial for defining a pre-stability condition in the derived category of coherent systems.

We finally arrive to the construction of stability conditions on
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
in Section
5
, where we introduce a two-parameter family of stability conditions
\sigma_{\alpha}^{\beta}=({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})
whose heart is simply
{\rm CohSys}(C)
regarded as the heart associated to the standard t-structure on
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
; here
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}
. For this reason,
\sigma_{\alpha}^{\beta}
are called
standard stability conditions
.

Sections
6
and
7
are dedicated to the construction of another family of (pre-)stability conditions using the technique called
tilting on a torsion pair
that was successfully used in the context of stability conditions on the derived category of coherent sheaves on surfaces. It is worth mentioning here that Section
6
has partial overlap with
[
FN
, Section 3]
. Given
\alpha\geq 0
, and any
\beta\geq 0
, we consider the torsion pair

\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\{\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)\,|\,\text{$\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G})>\beta$ whenever $\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}$}\}

\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\{\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)\,|\,\text{ $\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})\leq\beta$ for every $0\neq\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}$}\}

and the tilting category
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C):=\langle\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}[1],\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}\rangle
. When
\beta\geq 0
, and
\gamma>1
, we show that the homomorphism
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}:K_{0}({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C))\to\mathbb{C}
given by

Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})=(d+\gamma n-k)+\sqrt{-1}\,(d+\alpha\,k-\beta\,n)

is a stability function (i.e., the imaginary part is nonnegative, while the real part is negative when the imaginary one vanishes) on
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. Therefore, we consider the pairs
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}=({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
, the set

\mathsf{PS}:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{>1}
, and
the subset
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{PS}
such that
\beta\neq 1
and either
\gamma\geq(\beta^{2}+2\alpha\beta-\alpha)/(\alpha(\beta-1))
, if
\beta>1
, or
\gamma>1+(\beta(1-\alpha))/2\alpha
, if
\beta<1
.
The main results of Sections
6
and
7
can be summarized as follows; see Theorems
6.8
and
7.2
, Corollary
7.3
and Lemmas
7.4
and
7.5
below for more details.

Main Theorem 1
.

If
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathsf{PS}\cap\mathbb{Q}
, then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
is a locally finite pre-stability condition. Moreover, if
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathsf{S}
, then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
is a stability condition, and the map
\mathsf{S}\to\operatorname{Stab}(D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C)))
defined by
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\mapsto\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
is continuous. In addition,

(i)

For any
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathsf{PS}
, and
\alpha^{\prime}\geq 0
,
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\not\in\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\cdot\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}
;

(ii)

For any
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
in
\mathsf{PS}
, then

\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\not\in\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\cdot\sigma_{\alpha}
;

(iii)

if
(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})\gamma-(\alpha^{\prime}+1)\beta\neq-\beta^{\prime}(\alpha+1)
, then

\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\not\in\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\cdot\tau_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
.

In particular, the tilted stability conditions
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
are not equivalent, via the
\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
\operatorname{Stab}(D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C)))
, to the standard ones
\sigma_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.

Sections
8
and
9
are dedicated to discovering examples of
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable objects and determining some walls. In particular, we study stability properties in the
\gamma
-direction and proved that objects
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable for
\gamma\gg 0
are
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable in
{\rm CohSys}(C)
.

We summarize the results we got in the following theorem. To state it, we introduce some additional notation. For
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
, we write
\mathcal{E}=(E,V)
if
\varphi
is injective and we set
|\mathcal{E}|:=(E,H^{0}(E))
. We say
\mathcal{E}
is
complete
if
\mathcal{E}=|\mathcal{E}|
. Finally, for any
P\in C
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{P}
its structure, viewed as a closed subscheme of

.

Main Theorem 2
.

The following holds:

(i)

(\mathcal{O}_{P},0)
,
(0,V)
, and

are

-stable for every

, and every

(ii)

Let

. Let

The significance of item (i) lies in its determination that our conditions are

We informally refer to any object in the tilted category

When applied to our specific case, this topic naturally connects to

2.
Stability Conditions

This section mainly discusses stability conditions on triangulated categories. We derive several statements that will be referenced throughout this text. We begin by recalling important definitions and results for stability conditions on abelian categories.

2.1.
Stability conditions on abelian categories

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathscr{A})
, and
Z:K_{0}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{C}
an additive homomorphism. Following
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Section 4]
,
Z
is a called a
stability function
if, for all nonzero
E\in\mathscr{A}
, we have

\Im(Z(E))\geq 0\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \Re(Z(E))<0\ \,\,\text{if}\ \,\,\Im(Z(E))=0

The
slope
of
E\in\mathscr{A}
(with respect to
Z
) is

M(E):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{-\frac{\Re(Z(E))}{\Im(Z(E))}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{if $\Im(Z(E))\neq 0$}\\
\infty\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{if $\Im(Z(E))=0$}\end{cases}

The above definition suggests the following terms and notation:
D(E):=-\Re(Z(E))
is the
generalized degree
of
E
, while
R(E):=\Im(Z(E))
is the
generalized rank
of
E
.

A nonzero object
E\in\mathscr{A}
is called
stable
(respectively,
semistable
) if, for all proper non trivial subobject
F\subset E
, we have
M(F)<M(E)
(respectively,
\leq
). The
phase
of
E
is

\phi(E)=\frac{\rm arg(Z(E))}{\pi}\in(0,1].

It is easily seen that
E
is stable (respectively, semistable) if, for all proper nontrivial subobjects
F\subset E
, we have
\phi(F)<\phi(E)
(respectively,
\leq
).

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(\mathscr{A},Z)
is called a
stability condition
if

(i)

Z
is a stability function, and

(ii)

every nonzero
E\in\mathscr{A}
has a
Harder–Narasimhan filtration
, that is,

0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{n}=E

with
E_{i}\in\mathscr{A}
, such that
E^{i}:=E_{i}/E_{i-1}
is semistable for
1\leq i\leq n
, and

M(E^{1})>M(E^{2})>\ldots>M(E^{n}).

In addition. we define
M_{\rm max}(E):=M(E^{1})
and
M_{\rm min}(E):=M(E^{n})
.

Finding sufficient conditions for the existence of Harder–Narasimhan filtrations in an abelian category is a crucial tool. The following criterion is stated in
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Prop. 4.9]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
Z:K_{0}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{C}
be a stability function. Assume that

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is Noetherian, and

(ii)

the image of
\Im(Z)
is discrete in
\mathbb{R}
.

Then every nonzero
E\in\mathscr{A}
admits a Harder–-Narasimhan filtration with respect to
Z
.

The following useful result is proved in
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
Z:K_{0}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{C}
be a stability function. Let
A,B\in\mathscr{A}
be nonzero objects which are semistable with
M(A)>M(B)
. Then
\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=0
.

\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}:=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
such that:

(i)

Q(\mathcal{E}):=Q(v(\mathcal{E}),v(\mathcal{E}))\geq 0
for all semistable
\mathcal{E}\in\mathscr{A}
;

(ii)

Q(w):=Q(w,w)<0
for all non zero
w\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
with
Z_{\Lambda}(w)=0
.

Let
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathscr{D})
and
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})
denote the sets of pre-stability and stability conditions on
\mathscr{D}
, respectively, with respect to
(\Lambda,v)
; clearly
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})\subset\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathscr{D})
.

Take
(\mathscr{A},Z)\in\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathscr{D})
. For each
\phi\in(0,1]
, define the additive subcategory of
\mathscr{D}

\mathcal{P}(\phi):=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{$E$ is semistable of phase $\phi$}\}\cup\{0\}

and extend this definition for all
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
by setting

\mathcal{P}(\phi+1):=\mathcal{P}(\phi)[1]
. Given an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, set, as in
[
Bridgeland
, p. 327]
,
\mathcal{P}(I)
to be the extension-closed subcategory of
\mathscr{D}
generated by the subcategories
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
for
\phi\in I
.

A pre-stability condition
(\mathscr{A},Z)
is said to be
locally-finite
if for any
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
, there is
\epsilon>0
such that
\mathcal{P}((\phi-\epsilon,\phi+\epsilon))
is of finite length, cf.
[
Bridgeland
, Definition 5.7]
; let
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(\mathcal{D})
denote the set of locally-finite pre-stability conditions. Moreover, when
(\mathscr{A},Z)
is a stability condition, it is not hard to check that
(\mathcal{P},Z_{\Lambda})
is a stability condition via
slicings
in the sense of
[
Bridgeland
, Definition 5.1]
.

This approach endows
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})
with a metric. For
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)
and
\tau=(\mathcal{Q},W)
in
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})
, one sets
d(\sigma,\tau):={\rm sup}\{d_{S}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}),\left\|Z-W\right\|\}
where
d_{S}
is defined in terms of Harder–Narasimhan filtrations for slicings as in
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Section 5.3, p. 23]
. Bridgeland proved in
[
Bridgeland
, Theorem 1.2]
the following landmark result.

Theorem 2.6
.

The natural map
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C})
sending
(\mathscr{A},Z)
to
Z
is a local homeomorphism. In particular,
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})
is a complex manifold of dimension
{\rm rank}(\Lambda)
.

This presentation via slicings is also used in
[
Bayer
, Lemma 5.5.4]
to get the following result.

Lemma 2.7
.

The function
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
assigning
\sigma
to
S(\sigma)
as in (
2.2
) is continuos.

More generally, the set
\operatorname{Slice}(\mathcal{D})
of all slicings fo
\mathcal{D}
can also be equipped with a topology
[
Bridgeland
, Section 6]
; since

\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(\mathcal{D})\subset\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathcal{D})\subset\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{Slice}(\mathcal{D})

we also obtain topologies on
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(\mathcal{D})
and
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathcal{D})
and continuous maps

\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C})~~{\rm and}~~\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}).

In addition, we have continuous inclusions
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{D})\subset\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(\mathcal{D})\subset\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathcal{D})
.

Next, within the same framework, we will describe the action of a group on
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})
. Consider the universal cover
\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
of
GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
, whose elements are pairs

(T,f)
, where
T\in GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an increasing function with
f(x+1)=f(x)+1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and such that the maps induced by
T
and
f
on
S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{R}^{2}-\{(0,0)\}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
coincide. The group
\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
acts on
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}_{\Lambda}(\mathscr{D})
by

(T,f)\cdot(\mathcal{P},Z_{\lambda}):=(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
, where
Z^{\prime}:=T^{-1}\circ Z_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(\phi)=\mathcal{P}(f(\phi))
.

The action of
\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathscr{D})
preserves the semistable objects, but relabels their phases. In addition, it also preserves the subsets
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(\mathscr{D})
and
\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm pre}(\mathscr{D})
.

2.3.
Torsion pairs and tilting

A widely used technique to construct examples of stability conditions is called
tilting
, which provides a new heart of a bounded t-structure out of a known one.
The procedure is described below for derived categories.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category. A
torsion pair
on
\mathscr{A}
is a pair of full additive subcategories
(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})
of
\mathscr{A}
such that:

(i)

For any
T\in\mathscr{T}
and
F\in\mathscr{F}
,
Hom(T,F)=0
.

(ii)

For any
E\in\mathscr{A}
, there is an exact sequence

0\to T\to E\to F\to 0

in
\mathscr{A}
with
T\in\mathscr{T}
and
F\in\mathscr{F}
.

Set
\mathscr{D}:=D^{b}(\mathscr{A})
. The
tilting
of
\mathscr{A}
with respect to a torsion pair
(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})
is the smallest extension closed full subcategory of
\mathscr{D}
containing
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{F}[1]
. It is denoted by
\mathscr{A}^{\divideontimes}:=\langle\mathscr{F}[1],\mathscr{T}\rangle\subset\mathscr{D}
.

The next result can be derived by adjusting
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Lemma 6.3, Exercise 6.4]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

\mathscr{A}^{\divideontimes}
is the heart of a bounded
t
-structure on
\mathscr{D}
.

Now let
(\mathscr{A},Z)
be a stability condition and
M
be the slope associated with
Z
. For each
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the following pair of subcategories on
\mathscr{A}
.

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{\beta}(\mathscr{A},Z):=\mathscr{T}^{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{$M(G)>\beta$ whenever $E\twoheadrightarrow G$}\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{\beta}(\mathscr{A},Z):=\mathscr{F}^{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{ $M(F)\leq\beta$ for every $0\neq F\subset E$}\}

Remark 2.9
.

It follows from
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Section 6.2 and Exercise 6.7]
that the categories
\mathscr{T}^{\beta}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\beta}
can be defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{any semistable factor $F$ of $E$ satisfies $M(F)>\beta$}\}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{ any semistable factor $F$ of $E$ satisfies $M(F)\leq\beta$}\}

For future reference, we state the following result, whose proof is immediate.

Lemma 2.10
.

Take
E\in\mathscr{A}
.

(i)

If
E
is semistable and
M(E)\leq\beta
, then
E\in\mathscr{F}^{\beta}
;

(ii)

if
E
is semistable and
M(E)>\beta
, then
E\in\mathscr{T}^{\beta}
;

(iii)

if
E\in\mathscr{F}^{\beta}
has
M(E)=\beta
, then
E
is semistable;

(iv)

\mathscr{F}^{\beta}
is closed under subobjects;

(iv)

\mathscr{T}^{\beta}
is closed under quotients.

3.
Bounds on Global Sections of Sheaves on Integral Curves

By a
curve
we mean a complete, integral, one-dimensional scheme over an algebraically closed field. Throughout,
C
stands for a given curve of arithmetic genus
g
, structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{C}
, and dualizing sheaf
\omega
. Let
E
be a coherent sheaf on
C
. Then
F:=E/{\rm Torsion}(E)
, as torsion-free, is locally free on the
regular locus

U
of
C
consisting of its non-singular points. Indeed, for every
P\in U
, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{P}
is a principal ideal domain, and apply
[
J
, Section 3.8]
. The
rank
of
E
is defined as the rank of
F|_{U}
. We set the
degree
of
E
as

(3.1)

\deg(E):=\chi(E)-\rk(E)\,\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})

Note that these definitions avoid the choice of a polarization on
C
; nonetheless, one can check that they agree with
[
HL
, Definitions 1.2.2, 1.2.11]
or
[
KN
, Definition 2.1]
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
denote
{\rm Coh}(C)
, the category of coherent sheaves on
C
. Consider the group homomorphism given by

v:K_{0}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}~~,~~[E]\mapsto(\rk(E),\deg(E))~~{\rm when}~~E\in\mathscr{A},

which is easily seen to be surjective. We then define the additive function

Z:K_{0}(C)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}~~,~~Z([E])=-\deg(E)+\sqrt{-1}\rk(E),

which factors through
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
; it is not hard to check that it is a stability function on
\mathscr{A}
. The associated slope is just the usual Mumford slope

\mu(E)=\dfrac{\deg(E)}{\rk(E)}.

Furthermore, as a consequence of Proposition
2.2
and Definition
2.4
, the pair
(\mathscr{A},Z)
defines a stability condition.

We are finally in a position to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a coherent sheaf on
C
of rank
n
and degree
d
. Then:

(i)

if
E
is semistable and
d<0
, then
h^{0}(E)=0
;

(ii)

if
\mu_{\rm min}(E)\geq 0
, then

(3.2)

h^{0}(E)\leq d+n

Proof.

To prove (i), first assume
n=0
. Then (
3.1
) yields
h^{0}(E)=d
and we are done. So, for the remainder, consider
n>0
. Assume
d<0
. If
h^{0}(E)>0
, then there is a morphism
\mathcal{O}_{C}\to E
. But both
\mathcal{O}_{C}
and
E
are semistable. Thus
\mu(\mathcal{O}_{C})\leq\mu(E)
owing to
[
Macri-Schmidt
, Lemma 4.5]
. This yields
0\leq d/n<0
, a contradiction. So
h^{0}(E)=0
when
d<0
.

For the proof of (ii), assume first that
E
is semistable, so
\mu(E)=\mu_{\rm min}(E)\geq 0
, implying that
d\geq 0
. The exact sequence
0\to E(-P)\to E\to\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\oplus n}\to 0

yields

(3.3)

h^{0}(E)\leq h^{0}(E(-P))+n

If
d<n
, then
\deg(E(-P))=d-n<0
. Since
E(-P)
is semistable, the first item implies that
h^{0}(E(-P))=0
, hence
h^{0}(E)\leq n<d+n
. Thus (ii) holds for
d<n
. For
d\geq n
,
use induction on the degree:
\deg(E(-P))<d
and
E(-P)
is semistable. Then
h^{0}(E(-P))\leq(\deg(E)-n)+n
. So (
3.3
) yields (
3.2
).

When
E
is not semistable, let
0\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots\subset E_{l}=E
be its Harder–Narasimhan filtration, with factors
G_{j}:=E_{j}/E_{j-1}
. By assumption
\mu(G_{j})\geq 0
so
d_{j}:=\deg(G_{j})\geq 0
; note that
\sum_{j}d_{j}=d
and
\sum_{j}n_{j}=n
where
n_{j}=\rk(G_{j})
. We then have that

h^{0}(E)\leq\sum_{j}h^{0}(G_{j})\leq\sum_{j}(d_{j}+n_{j})=d+n,

as desired.
∎

Our next result is a generalization of
[
BGN
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
E
is coherent sheaf with
0\leq\mu_{\rm min}(E)\leq\mu_{\rm max}(E)\leq 2g-2
, then

(3.4)

h^{0}(E)\leq\dfrac{\deg(E)}{2}+\rk(E)

Proof.

We follow
[
BGN
, Theorem 2.1]
, making slight adjustments to the argument to suit our case, along with a few additional details. Note that the upper bound on
\mu_{\rm max}(E)
implies that
E
is torsion-free; otherwise, the maximal torsion subsheaf of
E
is a semistable subsheaf with infinite slope.

Assume first that
E
is semistable, so that
\mu(E)=\mu_{\rm min}(E)=\mu_{\rm max}(E)
.

Set
d=\deg(E)
and
n=\rk(E)
. Note that if
h^{1}(E)=0
, then the definition of the degree (
3.1
) yields
h^{0}(E)=d+n-ng=d/2+n-(ng-d/2)<d/2+n
. So (
3.4
) holds. So we may assume
h^{0}(E)\geq 1
and
h^{1}(E)\geq 1
.

Now the proof goes by induction on the rank. If
n=1
, then apply
[
EKS
, Theorem A, p. 532]
or
[
KM
, Lemma 3.1.(i)]
. Now assume
n\geq 2
. Let
F\subset E
be a torsion-free sheaf of maximal slope among all torsion-free subsheaves of
E
of rank at most
n-1
. Then
F
is semistable, as if
0\neq G\subset F
, then
G
is torsion-free,
G\subset E
,
\operatorname{rank}(G)\leq\operatorname{rank}(F)\leq n-1
, so
\mu(G)\leq\mu(F)
. Also,
E/F
is semistable as well. Indeed, say
G/F\subset E/F
where
F\subset G\subset E
and consider the exact sequence
0\to F\to G\to G/F\to 0
. If
\operatorname{rank}(G)\leq n-1
, then
\mu(G)\leq\mu(F)
as
G\subset E
and
F
is of maximal slope. Thus, the seesaw principle yields
\mu(G/F)\leq\mu(G)\leq\mu(F)
. But, similarly, as
E
is semistable, from the exact sequence

(3.5)

0\to F\to E\to E/F\to 0

we get
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)\leq\mu(E/F)
, so
\mu(G/F)\leq\mu(E/F)
. If
\operatorname{rank}(G)=n
, then
G/F\subset E/F
are of the same rank, so
\deg(G/F)\leq\deg(E/F)
, and hence
\mu(G/F)\leq\mu(E/F)
. Thus
E/F
is semistable.

Now, by construction,
\mu(F)\geq 0
. Also,
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)\leq 2g-2
. On the other hand, as
h^{1}(E)\geq 1
, there is a nonzero morphism
f\in{\rm Hom}(E,\omega)
. Consider the exact sequence

(3.6)

0\to\ker(f)\to E\to{\rm im}(f)\to 0.

Note that
{\rm im}(f)
, as a nonzero coherent subsheaf of
\omega
, is torsion-free of rank 1. Thus
\operatorname{rank}(\ker(f))=n-1
. Also,
\deg({\rm im}(f))\leq\deg(\omega)=2g-2
. Set
n^{\prime}:=\operatorname{rank}(E/F)
. So comparing slopes in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), and using the fact that degrees are additive in exact sequences, yields

\displaystyle n^{\prime}\mu(E/F)

\displaystyle=(n-1)\mu(\ker(f))+\deg({\rm im}(f))-(n-n^{\prime})\mu(F)

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\mu(F)+2g-2-(n-n^{\prime})\mu(F)=(n^{\prime}-1)\mu(F)+2g-2

\displaystyle\leq(n^{\prime}-1)(2g-2)+2g-2=n^{\prime}(2g-2)

where the first inequality holds since
\mu(\ker(f))\leq\mu(F)
because
\operatorname{rank}(\ker(f))=n-1
and
F
is of maximal slope. Thus
\mu(E/F)\leq 2g-2
. Therefore, both
F
and
E/F
satisfy the same hypothesis of
E
, and are of smaller rank. Set
d^{\prime}:=\deg(E/F)
. Thus (
3.5
) and induction yields
h^{0}(E)\leq h^{0}(F)+h^{0}(E/F)\leq(d-d^{\prime})/2+(n-n^{\prime})+d^{\prime}/2+n^{\prime}=d/2+n
.

When
E
is not semistable, let
0\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots\subset E_{l}=E
be its Harder–Narasimhan filtration, with torsion-free factors
E^{j}:=E_{j}/E_{j-1}
of rank
n^{j}
and degree
d^{j}
. By assumption,
0\leq\mu(E^{j})\leq 2g-2
for each
j
, so
h^{0}(E^{j})\leq d_{j}/2+n_{j}
. We then have that

h^{0}(E)\leq\sum_{j}h^{0}(E^{j})\leq\sum_{j}\left(\frac{d^{j}}{2}+n^{j}\right)=\frac{d}{2}+n,

thus concluding the proof.
∎

We can now return to the characterization of the extremal cases in Theorem
3.1
, which will be useful in Section
4
below.

Proposition 3.3
.

A semistable sheaf
E
satisfies
h^{0}(E)=d+n
if and only if either
n=0
, or
g=0
, or
E=\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n}
.

Proof.

As previously noticed, equality in (
3.2
) trivially holds if
n=0
. For
n\geq 1
, rewrite (
3.1
) as
h^{0}(E)=d+(1-g)n+h^{1}(E)
. So equality holds in (
3.2
) if and only if
h^{1}(E)=ng
.

If
g=0
, then
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and any semistable vector bundle
E
of rank
n
and degree
d\geq 0
on
C
is of the form
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a)^{\oplus n}
, with
na=d
. Hence
h^{0}(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a+1)=d+n
. So equality holds when
g=0
.

Now assume
g>0
. Suppose
E
is semistable,
h^{1}(E)=ng
, and
d\geq 0
. In particular,
h^{1}(E)>0
, so there is a nonzero morphism
E\to\omega_{C}
, and, as
E
and
\omega_{C}
are semistable, then
d/n\leq 2g-2
. Hence (
3.4
) holds for
E
. Therefore
h^{0}(E)\leq d/2+n\leq d+n=h^{0}(E)
, so
d=0
and
h^{0}(E)=n
. As
E
is semistable and
n>1
, then
E
is torsion-free. As
h^{0}(E)=n
, by
[
BGN
, Proposition 3.1]
, there is an injection
\iota:\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n}\hookrightarrow E
. But
E
and
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n}
are of the same degree zero and of the same rank, thus
E\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n}
since the
\coker(\iota)
has zero rank and degree. Now note that
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n}
is semistable whatever
C
is, and this completes the proof.
∎

The characterization of equality in (
3.4
) requires more careful consideration. For example, the case when
n=1
was addressed in
[
EKS
, Theorem A.(2), p. 532]
, but it involved a lengthy proof. This work introduced a new class of curves that satisfy Clifford’s equality, distinct from hyperelliptic curves. Specifically, these are rational curves that possess a unique singularity whose conductor agrees with the maximal ideal. These curves are non-Gorenstein and were characterized in
[
KM
, Theorem 5.10]
. They are referred to as
nearly normal
as they are the only curves whose canonical model is arithmetically normal.

For the case
n>1
, we may reframe the problem in terms of Clifford indices. Namely, Lange and Newstead introduced the
Clifford index
of higher rank vector bundles on a smooth curve. As our
C
is integral, we may replace locally free by torsion-free sheaves, as did, for instance, in
[
LGMS
, Definition 3.1]
for
n=1
. So let
E
be a torsion-free sheaf of rank
n
and degree
d
on
C
. In
[
LN1
, p. 169]
, the
Clifford index
of
E
is defined as follows

(3.7)

{\rm Cliff}(E):=\frac{1}{n}(d-2(h^{0}(E)-n)).

So
{\rm Cliff}(E)
measures how far
E
is from satisfying equality in (
3.4
), as this holds if and only if
{\rm Cliff}(E)=0
. If we want the Clifford index of a curve to measure how far it is from having such an
E
, then, accordingly, the
n
-Clifford index
of
C
could be set as

{\rm Cliff}_{n}(C):=\min\left\{{\rm Cliff}\,(E)\,|\,E\ \text{is semistable of rank $n$ and $0\leq\mu(E)\leq 2g-2$}\right\}

However,
[
LN1
, p. 169]
, where the invariant was coined, brings a more refined definition:

(3.8)

{\rm Cliff}_{n}(C):=\min\left\{{\rm Cliff}\,(E)\,|\,E\ \text{is semistable of rank $n$},\ 0\leq\mu(E)\leq g-1,\,h^{0}(E)\geq 2n\right\}.

Indeed, rewrite (
3.7
) as

(3.9)

{\rm Cliff}(E)=(g-1)-\frac{h^{0}(E)+h^{1}(E)}{n}

and set
E^{\vee}_{\omega}:=\mathcal{H}om(E,\omega)
. So Serre’s duality
[
H
, Theorem 7.6]
, along with (
3.9
), easily yields
{\rm Cliff}(E)={\rm Cliff}(E^{\vee}_{\omega})
. It also yields
\mu(E)+\mu(E^{\vee}_{\omega})=2g-2
. From this equality, we conclude that
E
is semistable if and only if
E^{\vee}_{\omega}
is, and, clearly, that
\mu(E)>g-1
if and only if
\mu(E^{\vee}_{\omega})\leq g-1
. So this explains the range
0\leq\mu(E)\leq g-1
.

Now rewrite (
3.7
) as

{\rm Cliff}(E)=\mu(E)-\frac{2h^{0}(E)}{n}+2.

As
{\rm Cliff}(E)\geq 0
, it follows that if
\mu(E)<2
, then
h^{0}(E)<2n
. But if
\mu(E)<2
, and
C
is smooth, then
h^{0}(E)\leq(d-n)/g+n
, which is way stronger than (
3.4
). Brambila–Grzegorczyk–Newstead proved this inequality in
[
BGN
, Theorem
\widetilde{\text{B}}
]
in the range
0\leq\mu(E)\leq 1
and extended by Mercat in
[
Mer
, Théorème A-1, p. 11]
to the range
1<\mu(E)<2
. So this explains the range
h^{0}(E)\geq 2n
in (
3.8
).

In other words, (
3.8
) may work for integral curves as well. Now, Mercat conjectured in
[
Mer2
]
that
{\rm Cliff}_{n}(C)={\rm Cliff}_{1}(C)
. In particular, when the statement holds, the characterization of curves with a torsion-free sheaf reaching the maximal number of independent global sections would reduce to the case
n=1
. For a recent account of this conjecture, we refer the reader to
[
New
]
.

4.
Coherent Systems on Integral curves

After discussing relevant definitions and results concerning coherent sheaves on an integral curve, we can now introduce the main subject of this work.

Definition 4.1
.

Following
[
He
, Définition 1.1]
, a coherent system of type
(n,d,k)
on
C
is a triple
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)

Z_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})=-(d+\alpha k)+\sqrt{-1}\,n~,

whose slope is

\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dfrac{d}{n}+\alpha\,\dfrac{k}{n}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{if $n\neq 0$}\\
~~\\
\infty\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{if $n=0$}\end{cases}

Here are some basic facts about

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)
be a coherent system of type
(n,d,k)
. Then

(i)

if
n>0
and
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable, then
E
is pure;

(ii)

if
n=0
, then
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable.

(iii)

Every sub-system of a pure system is also pure.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable and
n>0
. Set
T:={\rm Torsion}(E)
. Then we have that
\mathcal{T}:=(T,0,0)\subset\mathcal{E}
. But
\infty=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{T})>\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})
. Thus
\mathcal{T}=0
, hence
T=0
, and so
E
is torsion free. Also,
\mathcal{K}:=(0,\ker(\varphi),0)\subset\mathcal{E}
. But
\infty=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{K})>\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})
. Thus
\mathcal{K}=0
, hence
\ker(\varphi)=0
, and so
\varphi
is injective. The proof is completed by
[
KN
, Lemma 2.5]
. This proves (i).

If
n=0
, for any
0\neq\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
, we have
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\leq\infty=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})
. Thus
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable and (ii) holds.

As for the last item, if
\mathcal{F}=(F,U,\phi)
is a subsystem of a pure system
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)
, then
F
is torsion-free because
E
is, and
\varphi^{\prime}
is injective because
\varphi^{\prime}\circ f=H^{0}(h)\circ f
and all of the other maps in this identity are injective.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be of type
(n,d,k)
and
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable for some
\alpha\geq 0
. If

or

.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)
. If

, then, as we have already seen,

. So assume

and

. As

is

-semistable, then Proposition

.(i) yields that

is torsion-free and

. Following

, as

be the subsheaf of

,

,

, so

.
∎

Every nonzero coherent system on

Proof.

Let

∎

Remark 4.6
.

When
\mathcal{E}
is not
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable, its Harder–Narasimhan filtration will be wri-

tten as follows:

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{E}.

Its semistable factors are denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{i}:=\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
; let
\mathcal{E}^{i}=(E^{i},v^{i},\varphi^{i})
. Also,

\mu_{\alpha,\rm max}(\mathcal{E}):=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}^{1})>\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}^{2})>\ldots>\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}^{l}):=\mu_{\alpha,\rm min}(\mathcal{E}).

Note that we have exact sequences, for each

,

(4.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{i}\longrightarrow 0,~~{\rm and}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\longrightarrow E_{i-1}\longrightarrow E_{i}\longrightarrow E^{i}\longrightarrow 0.

Denoting the type of
\mathcal{E}^{i}
by
(n^{i},d^{i},k^{i})
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{l}(n^{i},d^{i},k^{i})=(n,d,k)~~{\rm and}~~h^{0}(E)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}h^{0}(E^{i}).

Moreover, if
\mathcal{E}
is pure, then
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}^{1}
is also pure, thus
\mu_{\alpha,\rm max}(\mathcal{E})<+\infty
and each factor
\mathcal{E}^{i}
is also pure with

. In general, if
\mathcal{E}
is not pure, then
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}^{1}
is the maximal torsion sub-system of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{i}
is pure for
i\geq 2
.

Theorem 4.7

Write

Assume first that
\mathcal{E}

-semistable. The proof is by induction on

and

if

. If

and

\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. Therefore, by Proposition

.(i),

is torsion free, and of rank

. Thus

. So apply Theorem

.

. If

is not semistable, then Proposition

.(ii) implies that

-stable for

. But

-semistable for some

5.
Standard Stability Conditions for Coherent Systems

We already noticed that
({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha})
is a stability condition in the sense of Definition
2.1
, since
Z_{\alpha}
is a stability function and Proposition
4.5
provides a Harder–Narasimhan filtration for each
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
. However, for later use, we will introduce another fami-

ly of stability functions on
{\rm CohSys}(C)
, depending on a new parameter, and of which the one given above is a particular case. Namely, for
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
of type
(n,d,k)
, set

(5.1)

Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\mathcal{E}):=Z_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})+n\beta=-(d+\alpha k-\beta n)+\sqrt{-1}\,n

So
Z_{\alpha}
corresponds to the case
\beta=0
. Clearly, the slope with respect to
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}
is given by

(5.2)

\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\mathcal{E})=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})-\beta

Therefore, a system is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}
-(semi)stable if and only if
\mu_{\alpha}
-(semi)stable, so the Harder–Narasimhan filtration with respect to
Z_{\alpha}
is also the Harder–Narasimhan filtration with respect to
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.

Since
{\rm CohSys}(C)
can also be regarded as the heart of the standard t-structure on the derived category
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
, the pair
({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})
can also be regarded as a two-parameter family of pre-stability conditions on
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
. We will now check that they satisfy the support property.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
\alpha>0
, the following holds:

(5.3)

S_{\alpha}:=\inf\left\{\frac{|Z_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})|}{\left\|v(\mathcal{E})\right\|}\ \bigg|\ 0\neq\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)\,\,\text{is $\mu_{\alpha}$-semistable}\right\}>0,

that is, the stability function
Z_{\alpha}
satisfies the support property.

Proof.

Given
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)

\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable of type
(n,d,k)
, we have

\frac{|Z_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})|^{2}}{\left\|v(\mathcal{E})\right\|^{2}}=\frac{(d+\alpha k)^{2}+n^{2}}{n^{2}+d^{2}+k^{2}}=:f(n,d,k)

Now if
\alpha\leq 1
, then
f(n,d,k)\geq\alpha^{2}
. Indeed, the inequality reads
2d\alpha k\geq(\alpha^{2}-1)(n^{2}+d^{2})
, which holds as
0<\alpha\leq 1
, and as either
k=0
or if
k>0
, then
d\geq 0
by Proposition
4.3
since
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. And if
\alpha>1
then
f(n,d,k)\geq 1
. In this case, the inequality reads
2d\alpha k\geq(1-\alpha^{2})k^{2}
, which holds for similar reasons. Thus (
5.3
) holds.
∎

Following Proposition
2.5
, the symmetric bilinear form
Q_{\alpha}
on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
associated to the support property above is such that

(5.4)

Q_{\alpha}(n,d,k)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})(n^{2}+d^{2})+2\alpha dk}{\alpha^{2}}},\,\,\,\,\,\text{if $0<\alpha\leq 1$},\\
~~\\
(\alpha^{2}-1)k^{2}+2\alpha dk,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\ \ \text{if $\alpha\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

We argue that the same quadratic form satisfies the properties of Proposition
2.5
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider the symmetric bilinear form
Q_{\alpha}
on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

(i)

Q_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})\geq 0
for all
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}
-semistable
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
;

(ii)

Q_{\alpha}(w)<0
for all nonzero
w\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
with
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}(w)=0
.

Proof.

To prove (i), note that, by (
5.2
),
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}
-semistable if and only if it is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. So the claim follows from the proof of Proposition
5.1
. For (ii), let
w=(n,d,k)\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
be a nonzero vector. If
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}(w)=0
, then
n=0
and
d=-\alpha k
. Therefore (
5.4
) yields
Q_{\alpha}(w)=-(\alpha^{2}+1)k^{2}
. But
k\neq 0
since
w\neq 0
. So
Q_{\alpha}(w)<0
.
∎

By putting all the pieces together, we have proven the following result.

Theorem 5.3
.

({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})
is a stability condition on
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
for each

\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the map

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\operatorname{Stab}(D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C)))\\
(\alpha,\beta)&\longmapsto&({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})\end{matrix}

is a continuous embedding.

The stability conditions described in Theorem
5.3
are called

.

We conclude this section by studying the orbits of

under the

action described in Section

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Consider two pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
in

Let

6.
Stability Conditions for Tilted Coherent Systems

In this section, we construct a three-parameter family of stability conditions on the
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
. To achieve this, we use the tilting construction described in Section
2.3
applied to the category of coherent systems and the stability function
Z_{\alpha}
given in the previous section.

Given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, with
\alpha\geq 0
, set, following the (
2.3
) and (
2.4
):

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\mathscr{T}^{\beta}({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)\,|\,\text{$\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G})>\beta$ whenever $\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}$}\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\mathscr{F}^{\beta}({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)\,|\,\text{ $\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})\leq\beta$ for every $0\neq\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}$}\}

Propositions
2.3
and
4.5
show that
(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta})
is a torsion pair on
{\rm CohSys}(C)
. Set

{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)=\langle\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{\alpha}[1],\mathcal{T}^{\beta}_{\alpha}\rangle;

in other words,
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
is the tilt of
{\rm CohSys}(C)
with respect to the torsion pair
(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta})
. Proposition
2.8
yields that
{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
is the heart of a bounded
t
-structure on

. In addition, one can check that

{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C):=\bigg\{\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))\,\bigg|\,\begin{matrix}\ \mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathcal{E})=0\,\,\,\text{for all $i\neq 0,-1$},\\
\ \mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{T}^{\beta}_{\alpha}\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{\alpha}\end{matrix}\bigg\}.

Now we will define a stability condition on
{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
. We will start by setting a map on
{\rm CohSys}(C)
, which we will extend in (
6.3
) to get a central charge in the tilted category.

Given
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha\geq 0
and
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
of type
(n,d,k)
, set

(6.1)

Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})=(d+\gamma n-k)+\sqrt{-1}\,(d+\alpha\,k-\beta\,n).

Now let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}^{\alpha}_{\beta}(C)
. Then
\mathcal{E}
fits into an exact triangle

(6.2)

0\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\to 0

where
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{T}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
. Set

(6.3)

Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}):=Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}))-Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))

and extend this map additively to get a homomorphism of abelian groups

Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}:K_{0}({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C))\to\mathbb{C},

which factors through the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable of type
(n,d,k)
. Assume that
d\geq 0
if
k=0
and
d\geq k
if
n=0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

if
n=0
, then
\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})\leq 0
, with equality if and only if

(ii)

if

and
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})>\beta

\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})<0

(iii)

if

\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})<\beta

If

If

(6.4)

{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})=-\,\frac{\displaystyle{\frac{d-k}{n}+\gamma}}{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})-\beta}}

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be of type
(n,d,k)

(i)

if
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}

\mathcal{E}

(ii)

if
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}

if
\mathcal{E}

(b)

if
\mathcal{E}
is not pure

(0,t,u)

\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{E}

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)

\beta<\infty=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})

\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{T})\leq\beta

, hence

is torsion free, and
\mathcal{E}
is pure. Finally, set
\mathcal{K}:=(0,\ker(\varphi),0)
and assume
\ker(\varphi)\neq 0

\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{K})=\infty
. But
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{E}
, so
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{T})\leq\beta
, which is again a contradiction. Thus
\ker(\varphi)=0
, and
\mathcal{E}
is injective.

To prove (ii).(a), let
\mathcal{E}^{i}
be the factors of
\mathcal{E}
. From Remark
2.9
,
\mathcal{E}^{i}\in\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
for all

. Also,
\mathcal{E}^{i}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. Say
\mathcal{E}^{i}
is of type
(n^{i},d^{i},k^{i})

\mathcal{E}^{i}\in\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}

\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}^{i})=d^{i}/n^{i}>\beta>0

\mu_{\alpha,\rm min}(\mathcal{E})=\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}^{k})>\beta>0
. So

by Theorem
4.7
.

To prove (ii).(b), write
\mathcal{E}=(E,V,\varphi)
and view
H^{0}(T)\subset H^{0}(E)

\mathcal{T}=(T,U,\varphi|_{U})

\mathcal{T}
is of type
(0,t,u)
. So
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{E}
is injective, then
\mathcal{T}
is injective which implies

, and hence

∎

Proposition 6.3
.

If
\beta\geq 0
and
\gamma>1
, then
Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}:K_{0}({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C))\to\mathbb{C}
is a stability function on
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}^{\alpha}_{\beta}(C)
. As we have just seen,
\mathcal{E}
fits into an exact

0\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\to 0

where
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{T}^{\beta}_{\alpha}

\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
is of type
(n_{1},d_{1},k_{1})
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})
of type
(n_{0},d_{0},k_{0})
. Then

\displaystyle\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))

\displaystyle=\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]))+\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})))

\displaystyle=-\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})))+\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})))

\displaystyle=-(d_{1}+\alpha\,k_{1}-\beta\,n_{1})+(d_{0}+\alpha\,k_{0}-\beta\,n_{0})

As
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{\alpha}

(i). Assume

\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=n_{1}(\beta-\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})))+n_{0}(\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}))-\beta)

\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))>0

\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=n_{1}(\beta-\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})))+d_{0}+\alpha\,k_{0}

and

. As

, we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=0
.

We therefore conclude that
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))\geq 0
, and equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=0
and
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))=\beta
, which implies that
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable by Lemma
2.10
(iii). Thus
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=0
implies that

(6.5)

\Re(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=-\Re(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})))=-(d_{1}+\gamma n_{1}-k_{1})<-(d_{1}+n_{1}-k_{1})\leq 0.

where the first inequality holds because
\gamma>1
and the second holds due to Theorem
4.7
provided
\beta\geq 0
.
∎

We record, for later reference, the following statement that was established as part of the previous proof.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}^{\alpha}_{\beta}(C)

be such that
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=0
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=0
,
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]
and
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable with
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))=\beta
.

The next step is to show that the stability function
Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}
defines a stability condition on
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. The approach is based on Proposition
2.2
, so we need a few technical lemmas.

Lemma 6.5
.

The following holds.

(i)

The image of
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
is discrete in
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
.

(ii)

The image of
\Re(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
is discrete in
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Set
A:=\{d+\alpha k-\beta n\,|\,(n,d,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\}
, which agrees with the image of
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
. If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
, write
\alpha=a_{\alpha}/b_{\alpha}
and
\beta=a_{\beta}/b_{\beta}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Fix
a_{0}=d_{0}+\alpha k_{0}-\beta n_{0}\in A
and let
a=d+\alpha k-\beta n\in A
be arbitrary with
a\neq a_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle|a_{0}-a|

\displaystyle=|(d_{0}-d)+\alpha(k_{0}-k)+\beta(n-n_{0})|

\displaystyle=\frac{|b_{\alpha}b_{\beta}(d_{0}-d)+a_{\alpha}b_{\beta}(k_{0}-k)-a_{\beta}b_{\alpha}(n_{0}+n)|}{|b_{\alpha}b_{\beta}|}\geq\frac{1}{|b_{\alpha}b_{\beta}|}

where the last inequality holds as the numerator is a natural number, and also nonzero as
a\neq a_{0}
. Therefore
A
, that is, the image of
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
, is discrete.

Conversely, assume
\alpha
is not rational (a similar argument holds for
\beta
). Then Dirichlet’s Approximation Theorem asserts that for any real numbers
\alpha
and
N\geq 1
, there are integers
p
and
q
, with
1\leq q\leq N
, such that
|q\alpha-p|<1/N
. So given
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, take
k=q
,
d=-p
and
n=0
. Now vary
N
and note
|q\alpha-p|
never vanishes as
\alpha
is irrational. This shows that
0
is not an isolated point of
A
. Thus the image of
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
is not discrete. This proves (i). The proof of (ii) is similar.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
, then the tilted category
{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
is Noetherian.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
and let

(6.6)

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{l}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}

be a chain of subobjects of
{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
.
Consider the exact sequence

(6.7)

0\to\mathcal{E}_{l}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{l}\to 0.

By Proposition
6.3
,
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
is a non-negative function. Thus
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})(\mathcal{E}_{l})\leq\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})
for all
l
. Also, by Lemma
6.5
,
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
is discrete, so we have finitely many possibilities for
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}_{l}))
. On the other hand, for every
l
, we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}_{l-1}\to\mathcal{E}_{l}\to\mathcal{E}_{l}/\mathcal{E}_{l-1}\to 0.

Thus, again,
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})(\mathcal{E}_{l-1})\leq\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})(\mathcal{E}_{l})
. In other words,
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}_{l}))
is an increasing function of
l
and, from the above, it reaches finitely many values. Thus, it stabilizes, say at
l_{0}
. So we may start the inclusions in (
6.6
) by
l_{0}
. Also, we may replace
\mathcal{E}_{l}
by
\mathcal{E}_{l}/\mathcal{E}_{l_{0}}
for all
l\geq l_{0}
, as if the latter stabilizes, so does the former. Now the exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{E}_{l_{0}}\to\mathcal{E}_{l}\to\mathcal{E}_{l}/\mathcal{E}_{l_{0}}\to 0.

yield that we may further assume
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}_{l}))=0
for all
l
. Therefore, as it was discussed in the proof of Proposition
6.3
,
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{l})=0
for all
l
. Set
Q_{l}:=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{l}
. We have that (
6.7
) induces the exact sequences

(6.8)

0\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{l})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{Q}_{l})\rightarrow 0

(6.9)

0\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{Q}_{l})\rightarrow 0

in
{\rm CohSys}(C)
. Thus (
6.9
) yields
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{Q}_{l})=\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})
for any
l
. Now the sequence

0=\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\hookrightarrow\ldots\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})

stabilizes because
{\rm CohSys}(C)
is Noetherian. But (
6.8
) yields
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{Q}_{l})=\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})/\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{l})
. Therefore, for sufficiently large
l
, we have that
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{Q}_{l})
is constant as well. It follows that
\mathcal{Q}_{l}
stabilizes and so does
\mathcal{E}_{l}
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
, then every nonzero object in
{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
admits a Harder–Narasimhan filtration with respect to
Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm CohSys}^{\beta}_{\alpha}(C)
is Noetherian by Lemma
6.6
, and the image of
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
is discrete in
\mathbb{R}
by Lemma
6.5
, the statement follows from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

With the technicalities resolved, we are finally ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.8
.

\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}:=({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
is a pre-stability condition in
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
with
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
, and every
\gamma>1
. If, in addition,
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
is locally finite.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.3
,
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
is a stability function on
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
, and from Proposition
6.7
, every nonzero object in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
has a Harder–Narasimhan filtration with respect to
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
. Thus
({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
is a pre-stability condition in
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
under the stated conditions on the parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.

If
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
, then the image of

is discrete by Lemma

. Then local finiteness follows from the proof of
[
Bridgeland1
, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

Establishing the support property for
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
will require further work, which we will undertake in the next section.

7.
Bogomolov–Gieseker Inequality for Coherent Systems

In this section, we will prove that the pre-stability conditions
({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
satisfy the support property
under certain weak impositions on their parameters.

We will approach the entire problem from a different framework, as outlined in Piyaratne and Toda
[
PT
, Section 2]
, which we will adapt to our specific context. The key ingredient is finding a suitable
Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality
(in the sense of
[
PT
]
for
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable coherent systems. This inequality will allow us to achieve the desired results for the tilted category of coherent systems.

Let
\sigma=(\mathscr{A},Z)
be a stability condition on a triangulated category
\mathscr{D}
such that
{\rm im}(Z)\subset\mathbb{Q}+\sqrt{-1}\,\mathbb{Q}
. Following
[
PT
, Definition 2.7]
, we say
\sigma
satisfies a
Bogomolov–Gieseker (BG) inequality
if there are linear maps

\Delta_{R},\Delta_{I}:\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}

such that for any semistable object
E\in\mathscr{A}
, the following inequality holds:

(7.1)

\Delta(E):=\Re(Z(E))\cdot\Delta_{R}(E)+\Im(Z(E))\cdot\Delta_{I}(E)\geq 0.

Let
M
be the slope with respect to
Z
, and consider the torsion pair

(7.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}(\mathscr{A},Z)

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{$M(G)>0$ whenever $E\twoheadrightarrow G$}\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{A},Z)

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathscr{A}\,|\,\text{ $M(F)\leq 0$ for every $0\neq F\subset E$}\}

and the tilted category

(7.3)

\mathscr{A}^{\dagger}:=\langle\mathscr{F}[1],\mathscr{T}\rangle.

In
[
PT
, p. 9]
introduces a family of homomorphisms
Z^{\dagger}_{t}:\Lambda\to\mathbb{C}
, for
t\geq 0
, given by

(7.4)

Z^{\dagger}_{t}:=-\sqrt{-1}\cdot Z+t\cdot\Delta_{I}=(\Im(Z)+t\Delta_{I})-\sqrt{-1}\cdot\Re(Z)

In
[
PT
, Lemma 2.11]
it is proved that
Z^{\dagger}_{t}
is a stability function on
\mathscr{A}^{\dagger}
. Moreover,
\mathscr{A}^{\dagger}
is Noetherian by
[
PT
, Lemma 2.17]
, and the Harder–Narasimhan property holds for
(\mathscr{A}^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}_{t})
by
[
PT
, Lemma 2.18]
.
Finally,
[
PT
, Corollary 2.22]
assures that if
\sigma=(\mathscr{A},Z)
satisfies the BG inequality (
7.1
), then
\sigma^{\dagger}=(\mathscr{A}^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}_{t})
,
t>0
, is a stability condition on
\mathscr{D}
; the support property is verified by the quadratic form

(7.5)

Q^{\dagger}_{r,t}:=r\cdot Q+t\cdot\Delta

where
Q
is the quadratic form arising from the support property of
\sigma=(\mathscr{A},Z)
,
0<r<r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is determined by
Q
, and
t>0

[
PT
, Lemma 2.20]
. Moreover, the map

\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\operatorname{Stab}(\mathscr{D})~~{\rm given~by}~~t\mapsto(\mathscr{A}^{\dagger},Z_{t}^{\dagger})

is continuous
[
PT
, Corollary 2.22]
.

Let us now adjust this construction to our context. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
, given
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
of type
(n,d,k)
, recall (
5.1
) and set

(7.6)

Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\mathcal{E}):=Z_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})+\beta n=-(d+\alpha k-\beta n)+\sqrt{-1}\cdot n.

As we have already seen, it yields a stability function
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}:K_{0}({\rm CohSys}(C))\to\mathbb{C}
factoring through the lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Also,
{\rm im}(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})\subset\mathbb{Q}+\sqrt{-1}\,\mathbb{Q}
as
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
. By Theorem
5.3
,
({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})
is a stability condition on
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta})
.

Let
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}
be the slope with respect to
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. Then
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\mu_{\alpha}-\beta
, and (
7.2
) turns into

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}

and hence (
7.3
) reads

{\rm CohSys}(C)^{\dagger}={\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)

Now we will build a Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality for
({\rm CohSys}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a coherent system of type
(n,d,k)
. If
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}
-semistable, then

(7.7)

\Delta(\mathcal{E})=k(d+n-k)+pd^{2}+qn^{2}+uk^{2}\geq 0

for any
p,q\geq 0
and
u\geq 1
.

Proof.

As
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\mu_{\alpha}-\beta
, it follows that
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. If
k=0
, we are done. So assume
k>0
. If
n>0
, then
d+n-k\geq 0
by Lemma
4.7
, and hence
\Delta(\mathcal{E})\geq 0
. Now if
n=0
, then
d\geq 0
, and hence

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=k(d-k)+pd^{2}+uk^{2}

\displaystyle=kd+pd^{2}+(u-1)k^{2}\geq 0

and we are done again.
∎

The following result provides the expression for
Z^{\dagger}
in our context, and in particular proves that the pre-stability conditions presented in Section
6
satisfy the support property at least for certain values of parameters.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
,
q,u,t,A\in\mathbb{R}
, with
q\geq 0
,
u\geq 1
,
t>0
and
\alpha\geq u-1
. Consider the additive homomorphism
\big(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big)^{\dagger}_{t}:\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

(7.8)

\big(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big)_{t}^{\dagger}=\big(1+t(q-A\beta)\big)n+t\big(A-p\beta\big)d+t\big(1+A\alpha-(p\alpha+1)\beta\big)k+\sqrt{-1}(d+\alpha k-\beta n),

where
p:=(\alpha-u+1)/\alpha
. Then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}=({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})_{t}^{\dagger})
is a stability condition in
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
.

In particular, if
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
, with
\gamma\geq(\beta^{2}+2\alpha\beta-\alpha)/(\alpha(\beta-1))
if
\beta>1
or
\gamma>1+(\beta(1-\alpha))/2\alpha
if
\beta<1
, then
({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
is a stability condition in
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
.

Proof.

For
(n,d,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, let
\Delta:=k(d+n-k)+pd^{2}+qn^{2}+uk^{2}
be as in (
7.7
). Let also
\Re(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}):=-(d+\alpha k-\beta n)
and
\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}):=n
be as in (
7.6
). We may write

(7.9)

\Delta=\Re(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})\,\Delta_{R}+\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})\,\Delta_{I}

as in (
7.1
), where
\Delta_{R}=An+Bd+Ck
for
A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta_{I}=Dn+Ed+Fk
for
D,E,F\in\mathbb{R}
. Then (
7.9
) yields the system of equations

\begin{array}[]{lll}A\beta+D=q&-B=p&-C\alpha=u-1\\
-A+B\beta+E=0&-A\alpha+C\beta+F=1&-C-B\alpha=1\end{array}

which yields the equalities

B=-p\ \ \ \ \ C=p\alpha-1\ \ \ \ \ D=q-A\beta\ \ \ \ \ E=A+p\beta\ \ \ \ \ F=1+A\alpha-(p\alpha-1)\beta

and

(7.10)

p=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha-u+1}{\alpha}\right)

and
p\geq 0
since
\alpha\geq u-1
. So, following (
7.4
), we set

\displaystyle\big(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big)_{t}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle:=\Im(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})+t\Delta_{I}-\sqrt{-1}\,\Re(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta})

\displaystyle=n(1+t(q-A\beta))+t(A-p\beta)d+t(1+A\alpha-(p\alpha+1)\beta)k+\sqrt{-1}(d+\alpha k-\beta n)

and the statements, based on
[
PT
, Section 2]
, developed along this section yield the first claim, that is,
({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),\big(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big)_{t}^{\dagger})
is a stability condition.

To prove the second, comparing
\Re((Z_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big)_{t}^{\dagger})
and
\Re(Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
, we get

n(1+t(q-A\beta))+t(A-p\beta)d+t(1+A\alpha-(p\alpha+1)\beta)k=\gamma n+d-k.

This gives the following equations

(7.11)

\displaystyle t(q-A\beta)

\displaystyle=\gamma-1

(7.12)

\displaystyle t(A+p\beta)

\displaystyle=1

(7.13)

\displaystyle t\big((p\alpha-1)\beta-1-A\alpha\big)

\displaystyle=1

As
p=p(\alpha,u)
as in (
7.10
), we have to find
q,u,t,A
, satisfying the equations above and also with
q\geq 0
,
0\leq u-1\leq\alpha
and
t>0
. Now (
7.12
) yields

(7.14)

A=\frac{1}{t}-p\beta.

Replacing (
7.14
) in (
7.13
) we get

(7.15)

2p\alpha\beta=\frac{\alpha+1}{t}+\beta+1.

Replacing (
7.10
) in (
7.15
) yields

(7.16)

u=\frac{t\beta(\alpha+2)-\alpha(\alpha+t+1)}{2t\beta}.

Now, combining (
7.14
) and (
7.15
) we get

(7.17)

A=\frac{\alpha-t(\beta+1)-1}{2t\alpha}

while (
7.11
) and (
7.17
) produces

(7.18)

q=\frac{2\alpha(\gamma-1)+\beta(\alpha-t(\beta+1)-1)}{2t\alpha}.

So we found
q,u,t
and
A
satisfying (
7.11
)-(
7.13
), with
t>0
being arbitrary. Now let us check when
q\geq 0
and
0\leq u-1\leq\alpha
. The first inequality yields

(7.19)

t\leq\frac{2\alpha(\gamma-1)+\beta(\alpha-1)}{\beta(\beta+1)}.

Now note that
u\leq\alpha+1
is always satisfied. On the other hand,
u\geq 1
reads

(7.20)

t(\beta-1)\geq\alpha+1

If
\beta>1
, then, combining (
7.19
) and (
7.20
), we can find
t
if and only if

\gamma\geq\frac{\beta^{2}+2\alpha\beta-\alpha}{\alpha(\beta-1)}

If
\beta<1
, then, by (
7.20
),
t\leq(\alpha+1)/(1-\beta)
, which is positive. So we can find
t
if and only if the numerator on the right-hand side of (
7.19
) is positive as well. This yields

\gamma>1+\frac{\beta(1-\alpha)}{2\alpha}

Finally, if
\beta=1
, then (
7.20
) precludes the existence of such a
t
.
∎

Let us now consider the set

\mathsf{PS}:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\gamma>1}
, and
the subset
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{PS}
such that
\beta\neq 0,1
and either

(i)

\gamma\geq(\beta^{2}+2\alpha\beta-\alpha)/(\alpha(\beta-1))
, if
\beta>1
,

(ii)

or
\gamma>1+(\beta(1-\alpha))/2\alpha
, if
\beta<1
.

We already showed in the end of Section
6
that if
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathsf{PS}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{3}
, then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\in\operatorname{Stab}^{\rm lf}(D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C)))
. Moreover, we conclude from Theorem
7.2
that if
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathsf{PS}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{3}
then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\in\operatorname{Stab}(D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C)))
.

Our next statement follows directly from Theorem
7.2
and
[
PT
, Corollary 2.22]
.

Corollary 7.3
.

The map
\mathsf{S}\to\operatorname{Stab}(D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C)))
defined by
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\mapsto\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
is continuous.

To conclude this section, we will compare the tilted (pre-)stability conditions
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}=({\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C),Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
with the standard ones
\sigma_{\alpha}=({\rm CohSys},Z_{\alpha})
constructed in Section
5
and among themselves.

Lemma 7.4
.

For any
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}>0
,
\beta\geq 0
and
\gamma>1
,
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\not\in\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\cdot\sigma_{\alpha}
.

This means that the (pre-)stability conditions constructed via tilting are never equivalent to a standard stability condition.

Proof.

Let
T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
We argue that there is no
T\in GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
T\circ Z_{\alpha^{\prime}}=Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
, which implies that
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
cannot be in the
\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
orbit of
\sigma_{\alpha}
.

Indeed, the equality
T\circ Z_{\alpha^{\prime}}=Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
becomes

\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-d-\alpha^{\prime}k\\
n\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}d+\gamma n-k\\
d+\alpha k-\beta n\end{pmatrix}

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle-A(d+\alpha^{\prime}k)+Bn

\displaystyle=d+\gamma n-k,~~{\rm and}

\displaystyle-C(d+\alpha^{\prime}k)+Dn

\displaystyle=d+\alpha k-\beta n.

It follows that
B=\gamma
,
C=-1
,
D=-\beta
, and
A=-1
. In addition, we would have that
\alpha^{\prime}k=-k
, which is impossible because
\alpha^{\prime}\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}
be such that
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
and
\tau_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
are pre-stability conditions on
D^{b}({\rm CohSys}(C))
. Then:

(i)

If
\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
, then
\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\not\in\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\cdot\tau_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
;

(ii)

If
(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})\gamma-(\alpha^{\prime}+1)\beta\neq-\beta^{\prime}(\alpha+1)
, then

\tau_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}\not\in\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\cdot\tau_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})

, then

\begin{array}[]{cc}\vskip 5.69054ptA+B=1&\hskip 14.22636ptC+D=1\\
\vskip 5.69054pt-A+\alpha B=-1&\hskip 14.22636ptC\gamma-D\beta=-\beta^{\prime}\\
A\gamma-B\beta=\gamma^{\prime}&\hskip 14.22636pt\alpha D-C=\alpha^{\prime}\\
\end{array}

which yields

,
B=0
,
C=\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}{\alpha+1}
,
D=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}+1}{\alpha+1}
; in addition, we must also have that

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}~~{\rm and}~~(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})\gamma-(\alpha^{\prime}+1)\beta=-\beta^{\prime}(\alpha+1).

Therefore, if neither of these equalities holds, then the
\widetilde{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
orbit of

does not contain
\tau_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
.
∎

\mathcal{F}
does not destabilizes
|\mathcal{O}_{P}|
.

Finally, if
\mathcal{K}=|\mathcal{O}_{P}|
, then
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G})=0
, so
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})[1]
, and we get the following exact sequence in
{\rm CohSys}(C)
:

0\to\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}\to|\mathcal{O}_{P}|\to 0

with
\mathcal{G}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. In addition, if
v(\mathcal{F})=(n,d,k)
, then
v(\mathcal{G})=(n,d-1,k-1)
,
n>0
. Setting
\mathcal{F}=(F,U,\phi)
and
\mathcal{G}=(G,W,\psi)
, the latter sequence can be unpacked into the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{V_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(F)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{P})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(E)}

Note that
\psi
is injective because
\mathcal{G}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
; it follows that
\phi
is also injective, and we can use Proposition
6.2
(ii)(b) to conclude that

. Hence, we have
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{F})<0=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(|\mathcal{O}_{P}|)
so
\mathcal{F}
does not destabilizes
|\mathcal{O}_{P}|

|\mathcal{O}_{P}|
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable.
∎

9.
Semistability of Tilted Complete Systems

In this section, we analyse the
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistability of complete coherent systems (see Definition
4.1
). Also, we determine an actual wall for
\mathcal{E}[1]
when
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-stable. Our starting point is to study stability properties in the
\gamma
-direction, and from these results, we can apply wall-crossing arguments with respect to the stability function
Z_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
for
\gamma>1
.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(C)
be of type
(n,d,k)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
be of type
(n^{\prime},d^{\prime},k^{\prime})
. Then
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})
is a linear function in
\gamma
which is

(i)

increasing if
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)>n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
;

(ii)

decreasing if
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)<n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
;

(iii)

constant if
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)=n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
.

In particular, if
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathcal{E})=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, then

(iv)

\big(\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})\big)(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})>0
,
\forall

\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
if
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)>n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
;

(v)

\big(\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})\big)(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})<0
,
\forall

\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
if
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)<n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
;

(vi)

\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})=0
,
\forall

\gamma
if
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)=n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Note that

(9.1)

\displaystyle f(\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\frac{n}{d+\alpha k-\beta n}-\frac{n^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{d-k}{d+\alpha k-\beta n}-\frac{d^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime}}.

So
f
is a linear function and items (i)-(iii) follow easily. Items (iv)-(vi) relate to the signal analysis of
f(\gamma)(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})
when
f(\gamma^{\prime})=0
, and these also follow immediately. ∎

The following result is akin to that in
[
BGPMN
, Lemma 6.2]
, and its proof is analogous.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(C)
be of type
(n,d,k)
. Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable for
\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}
, but strictly
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable for
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-unstable for all
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
and let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
of type
(n,d,k)
such that
\mathcal{E}
is strictly
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable. For any subobject
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
of type
(n^{\prime},d^{\prime},k^{\prime})
such that
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})
, the following statements holds:

(i)

If
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma+\epsilon}
-semistable, then
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)>n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma-\epsilon}
-semistable, then
n/(d+\alpha k-\beta n)<n^{\prime}/(d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime})
.

Following
[
JM
, Section 4]
we define
numerical
,
pseudo
and
actual
walls in the
\gamma
-direction.

Definition 9.4
.

Fix
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
.
We say that
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
is

(i)

a
numerical
wall for
(n,d,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, if there is
(n^{\prime},d^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
non-parallel to
(n,d,k)
such that

-\frac{d^{\prime}+\gamma_{0}n^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}+\alpha k^{\prime}-\beta n^{\prime}}=-\frac{d+\gamma_{0}n-k}{d+\alpha k-\beta n};

(ii)

a
pseudo
wall for
(n,d,k)
if
\gamma_{0}
is a numerical wall and there exists objects
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
with
v(\mathcal{F})=(n^{\prime},d^{\prime},k^{\prime})
,
v(\mathcal{E})=(n,d,k)
and a nonzero morphism
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}
.

(iii)

an
actual
wall for
(n,d,k)
if there exists
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
of type
(n,d,k)

and a path
\theta:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}_{>1}
such that
\theta(0)=\gamma_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}
-stable for
t>0
and
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}
-unstable for
t<0
.

Clearly, an actual wall is also pseudo: if
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}
destabilizing subobject for
t<0
, then
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}(\mathcal{F})<\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}(\mathcal{E})
for
t<0
with
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}(\mathcal{F})>\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\theta(t)}(\mathcal{E})
for
t>0
(since
\mathcal{E}
is stable in this range), thus
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma_{0}}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
.

The following result describes
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable objects when
\gamma\gg 0
, akin to the so-called
large volume limit
in the context of stability conditions on the derived category of sheaves on surfaces.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be such that
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})>0
and
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})\neq\beta
. Then

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})}{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})-\beta}.

Proof.

Say
\mathcal{E}
is of type
(n,d,k)
. Then write

\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})}{\gamma}=-\frac{d/\gamma\,+\,n\,-\,k/\gamma}{n(\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})-\beta)}

and take the limit to get the desired quotient.
∎

For the next result, we set

\textsf{B}=\textsf{B}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\bigg\{\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)\,\bigg|\,\begin{matrix}\ \text{either (i)}\ \mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=0\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\ \text{is}\ \mu_{\alpha}\text{-semistable}\text{;}\\
\ \ \ \ \,\text{or (ii)}\ \mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})=0\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\ \text{is}\ \mu_{\alpha}\text{-semistable}\end{matrix}\bigg\}.

With this in mind, we have the following statement.

Lemma 9.6
.

If
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
is
\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}
-semistable for
\gamma\gg 0
, then
\mathcal{E}\in\textsf{B}
.

Proof.

We know
\mathcal{E}
fits into an exact triangle

(9.2)

0\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\to 0

where
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{T}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
.
Now
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}
-semistable for
\gamma\gg 0
. Therefore (
9.2
) yields

(9.3)

\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))}{\gamma}=\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1])}{\gamma}\leq\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}))}{\gamma}

Taking limits, we get from Proposition
9.5
that

0\leq-\frac{1}{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))-\beta}\leq-\frac{1}{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}))-\beta}<0

if
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))\neq\beta
or, otherwise and similarly,
\infty\leq-(\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}))-\beta)^{-1}<0
. But this cannot happen unless one (and only one) among
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})
vanishes. Say the latter does not, that is,

\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
If
\mathcal{E}
is not
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable, let
\mathcal{F}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be a destabilizer, which we may further assume is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. Then
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})>\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})>\beta
. Thus Proposition
2.10
.(ii) yields
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}\subset{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. Now

\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})}{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})-\beta}>-\frac{1}{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})-\beta}=\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})}{\gamma}.

In particular, there exists
\gamma_{0}\gg 0
such that

\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma_{0}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})}{\gamma_{0}}>\frac{\mu^{\beta,\gamma_{0}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})}{\gamma_{0}}

which contradicts the
\mu^{\beta,\gamma_{0}}_{\alpha}
-semistability of
\mathcal{E}
. Therefore
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable.

If
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]
use a similar argument and Proposition
2.10
.(i) to conclude that
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable.
∎

Now for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
, set

c=c_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}:={\rm min}\{\,\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))\,|\,\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)\,\,\text{and}\,\,\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))>0\}.

Note that this minimum exists since the image of
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma})
is discrete if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
owing to Lemma
6.5
. In fact, its proof yields that if
\alpha=a_{\alpha}/b_{\alpha}
and
\beta=a_{\beta}/b_{\beta}
, then
c=N/(b_{\alpha}b_{\beta})
for some
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. In particular,
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
.

Lemma 9.7
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
be such that
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=c
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable if and only if

Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})=0
for all
\mathcal{F}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(C)
with
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))=0
.

Proof.

First note necessity only requires
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})<\infty
(which holds as
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=c>0
) and semistability. Indeed, suppose so and consider
\mathcal{F}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
with
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))=0
. Then
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{F})=\infty
and, in particular,
\mathcal{F}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable. Then Proposition
2.3
yields
Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})=0
. To prove sufficiency, let

(9.4)

0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0

be an exact triangle in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. Then
c=\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))+\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G}))
. But, by Proposition
6.3
, we have that
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))\geq 0
and
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G}))\geq 0
. Hence either
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))=0
or
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G}))=0
.
But
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))
doesn’t vanish by hypothesis. Thus
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G}))
does. So
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{G})=\infty>\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})
and by the seesaw principle (
9.4
) cannot destabilize
\mathcal{E}
. Therefore
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable.
∎

Lemma 9.8
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. The following holds:

(i)

if
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=0
, then
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable and
\mathcal{E}\in\textsf{B}
.

(ii)

if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=c
, then
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable if and only if
\mathcal{E}\in\textsf{B}
.

Proof.

Note that (i) easily follows from Corollary
6.4
, from the equality
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})=\infty
, and from the very definition of
B
.

To prove (ii), suppose
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable. Use the proof of necessity in Lemma
9.7
to conclude that
Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})=0
for all
\mathcal{F}\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(C)
with
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))=0
. But this condition characterizes stability and does not depend on
\gamma
as
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha})
does not involve
\gamma
. Thus
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable for all
\gamma
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable for
\gamma\gg 0
and hence
\mathcal{E}\in\textsf{B}
by Lemma
9.6
.

Conversely, assume
\mathcal{E}\in\textsf{B}

and consider an exact triangle

(9.5)

0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0

in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. As
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=c
, then either
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))=0
or
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{G}))=0
. If the latter holds then
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{F})<\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})
by the seesaw principle as
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{G})=\infty
. So suppose
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))=0
. By Corollary
6.4
,
\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})[1]
. Now
\mathcal{E}\in\textsf{B}
so either
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]
or
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})
. But the latter is precluded since, otherwise, (
9.5
) yields
0\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\to 0
and hence
\mathcal{F}=0
. So
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})[1]
and (
9.5
) yields an inclusion
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
. Now, by Corollary
6.4
,
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}))=\beta
. But
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and is
\mu_{\alpha}
-semistable. Thus
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}))=\beta
too. This implies
\infty=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}))=\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}(\mathcal{E})
, which cannot happen as
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E}))=c>0
. So
\Im(Z^{\beta,\gamma}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}))
doesn’t vanish. Therefore,
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable and, in particular,
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-semistable.
∎

Lemma 9.9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be
\mu_{\alpha}
-stable of rank
n>0
. Assume that
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})\leq\beta
. Assume also that
\beta<\delta
if
n\geq 2
, where
\delta=min\{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{Q})\,|\,\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\neq\mathcal{E}\}
. Then
\mathcal{E}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and any proper subobject
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}[1]
in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
is in
\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and fits into an exact sequence in
{\rm CohSys}(C)
of the form

(9.6)

0\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0

where
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-stable and
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})\leq\beta
, it follows that
\mathcal{E}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. Thus
\mathcal{E}[1]\in{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a proper subobject of
\mathcal{E}[1]
in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
, fitting into the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}[1]\to\mathcal{G}\to 0

in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. It yields the following exact sequence in
{\rm CohSys}(C)

(9.7)

0\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{F})\to 0\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G})\to 0.

Thus
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G})=0
. Set
\mathcal{S}:={\rm coker}f
. If
\mathcal{S}=0
, then
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{F})
which implies
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})=0
, and hence
\mathcal{G}=0
since its cohomologies vanish. But this is impossible as
\mathcal{F}
is a proper subobject of
\mathcal{E}[1]
. Therefore
\mathcal{S}\neq 0
.

If
n=1
, we have
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. Thus
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{S})\neq 0
. So
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}))=0
since
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})=1
. But
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, and so
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})=0
. Thus
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{F})\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
as desired.

In
n\geq 2
, assume
\mathcal{S}
is a proper quotient of
\mathcal{E}
. Then
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})\leq\beta<\delta\leq\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
. On the other hand,
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})\leq\beta
because
\mathcal{S}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
which yields a contradiction. Therefore
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{E}
and hence
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})=0
. Thus
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{F})\in\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
as desired. Define
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})
and (
9.7
) yields (
9.6
).
∎

Proposition 9.10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be
\mu_{\alpha}
-stable with
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})=\beta
. Then
\mathcal{E}[1]
is minimal in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.10
(or even Lemma
9.9
), we have that
\mathcal{E}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. So let
\mathcal{F}
be a proper subobject of
\mathcal{E}[1]
. By Lemma
9.9
,
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and fits into the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0

in
{\rm CohSys}(C)
with
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. But the seesaw principle, along with the definitions of
\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and
\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, yields:

\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})=\beta\geq\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\geq\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})>\beta

which is a contradiction. Thus
\mathcal{F}
is not proper, that is,
\mathcal{E}[1]
is minimal.
∎

We can now formulate our main result of this section.

Theorem 9.11
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm CohSys}(C)
be
\mu_{\alpha}
-stable of type
(n,d,k)
, with
n>0
and
-\gamma n<d
if
k=0
. Assume
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})<\beta
,
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
. Assume also that
\beta<\delta_{\mathcal{E}}
if
n\geq 2
, where
\delta_{\mathcal{E}}=min\{\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{Q})\,|\,\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\neq\mathcal{E}\}
. Then;

(i)

If
\mathcal{E}=(E,V)
is not complete, then
\mathcal{E}[1]
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
for every
\gamma\gg 0
and
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-unstable for
\gamma<\gamma_{0}:=(\beta n-d(\alpha+1))/\alpha n
. Also, if
|\mathcal{E}|\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, then
\gamma_{0}
is a pseudo-wall for
(n,d,k)
. Moreover, if
|\mathcal{E}|
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-stable and
\beta<\delta_{|\mathcal{E}|}
, then
\gamma_{0}
is an actual-wall for
(n,d,k)

(ii)

If
\mathcal{E}
is complete, then
\mathcal{E}[1]
is
\mu_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}
-stable for every
\gamma

Proof.

First note that
\mathcal{E}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subobjects of
\mathcal{E}[1]
in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)

9.9
, we have
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and fits into an exact sequence

in
{\rm CohSys}(C)
with
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}^{\beta}

which induces the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}[1]\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}[1]\to 0

in
{\rm CohSys}_{\alpha}^{\beta}(C)
. Now say
\mathcal{F}
is of type
(n^{\prime},d^{\prime},k^{\prime})
. As
\mu_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})>\beta\geq 0
, it follows that

if
k^{\prime}=0

. On the other hand,
\mathcal{E}

So assume

Some results on the higher-rank graphs associated to crystals of semisimple Lie algebras
Marco Matassa

Abstract
. In this paper we continue the study of the higher-rank graphs associated to finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebras, introduced by the author and R. Yuncken, whose construction relies on Kashiwara’s theory of crystals. First we prove that the Bruhat graphs of the corresponding Weyl groups, both weak and strong, can be embedded into the higher-rank graphs as colored graphs. Next, specializing to Lie algebras of type A A , we connect some aspects of the construction of the higher-rank graphs with some well-known notions in combinatorics, most notably the keys of Lascoux and Schützenberger.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The notion of
crystals
associated to a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, originally due to Kashiwara
[
Kas90
,
Kas91
]
, is by now an established tool in representation theory and combinatorics (see for instance
[
HoKa02
,
BuSc17
]
for textbook accounts).
For each
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
, it allows the construction of a corresponding crystal
\mathcal{B}
, which is a combinatorial object that encodes all the essential information about such a module.
One way to construct such
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals is via the
quantized enveloping algebra

U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, where they arise from its modules in a certain limit
q\to 0
of the deformation parameter.
In particular, this theory can be applied to produce a crystal analogue of the
quantized coordinate ring

\mathcal{O}_{q}(G)
of the corresponding Lie group
G
, which has been investigated from this perspective in
[
Kas94
]
.

A different approach to the construction of a
q\to 0
limit for the quantized coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G)
was proposed in
[
MaYu23
]
.
It is also based on the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals, but keeps track of the
*
-structure corresponding to the compact real form of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is necessary for the analytic picture in terms of
C^{*}
-algebras.
One of the main results there was the connection with the notion of
higher-rank graphs
, introduced by Kumjian and Pask in
[
KuPa00
]
.
These allow the construction of
C^{*}
-algebras which generalize the
graph
C^{*}
-algebras
corresponding to directed graphs.
We review various results related to this perspective below.

Let us write
C^{*}_{q}(G)
for the
C^{*}
-completion of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G)
with deformation parameter
q\in(0,1)
.
It has been known since
[
Wor87
]
that the
C^{*}
-algebras
C^{*}_{q}(SU(2))
are isomorphic for all values of
q\in[0,1)
(where the case
q=0
has to be interpreted appropriately).
It was observed in
[
HoSz02
]
that these can be described as graph
C^{*}
-algebras, where the underlying graph structure is most transparent in the case
q=0
.
However, it was also observed that
C^{*}_{q}(G)
does not admit a description of this type for (simple)
G
of rank larger than one, because of known restrictions imposed by the so-called gauge action.
On the other hand, the
C^{*}
-algebras corresponding to higher-rank graphs do not have such restrictions, and hence are natural candidates for the general description of
C^{*}_{q}(G)
.
This perspective was first explored by Giselsson for
G=SU(3)
in
[
Gis25
]
(and appeared later in print), where it was shown that
C^{*}_{q}(SU(3))
can be modeled as a higher-rank graph
C^{*}
-algebra of rank two. Furthermore, such
C^{*}
-algebras are isomorphic for all
q\in[0,1)
, as in the case of
SU(2)
.
Based on this insight, a general construction was given in
[
MaYu23
]
, which can be applied to any Lie group
G
corresponding to a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
: upon representing
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G)
on a Hilbert space by a faithful
*
-representation, it produces a
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{0}(G)
as a certain
q\to 0
limit of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G)
, whose
C^{*}
-completion
C^{*}_{0}(G)
was shown to be isomorphic to a concretely defined higher-rank graph
C^{*}
-algebra.
It is conjectured that
C^{*}_{0}(G)
should be isomorphic to
C^{*}_{q}(G)
for any
q\in(0,1)
, but this is not known in general (on the other hand, it is known that the
C^{*}
-algebras
C^{*}_{q}(G)
are isomorphic for all
q\in(0,1)
by
[
Gis20
]
).

Informally, we can think of a higher-rank graph as a generalization of a directed colored graph, with vertices connected by paths and with rank given by the number of colors (for its precise definition see
Definition

3.1
).
As shown in
[
MaYu23
]
, to any finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
we can associate a higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
with rank equal to that of
\mathfrak{g}
, and whose construction makes use of the corresponding category of
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals.
The following general properties of the higher-rank graphs
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
are proven in the cited paper:

•

the
Weyl group

W
of
\mathfrak{g}
embeds into the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
,

•

this set admits a
partial order
with unique maximal and minimal elements, corresponding to the Weyl group elements of maximal and minimal lengths,

•

the paths in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
are
compatible
with this partial order.

On the other hand, not much is known about which paths arise in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, even in the case of vertices corresponding to Weyl group elements.
Recall that the
Bruhat graph
of
W
is a certain canonical directed graph attached to
W
, having its elements as vertices.
Since
W
embeds into the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, a natural question arises: what is the relation between the Bruhat graph of
W
and the paths in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
between vertices corresponding to
W
?
This provides the main motivation for this paper, which aims to elucidate this structure.

1.2.
Results

The first aspect we investigate is whether we can embed the various Bruhat graphs associated to
W
into the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We begin by considering the graphs corresponding to the right and left
weak
Bruhat orders on
W
, which can be seen as colored graphs in a natural way.
In
Theorem

5.1
we show that the right weak Bruhat graph has a
unique
embedding, as a colored graph, into the
skeleton
of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
(where the skeleton is a colored graph that can be canonically attached to any higher-rank graph).
The situation is different for the left weak Bruhat graph, which does not admit such an embedding in general (however we note that the difference of left versus right is largely a matter of conventions).
On the other hand, in
Theorem

5.5
we prove that we can embed the (strong) Bruhat graph into the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, rather than just its skeleton.
This is true for any choice of
compatible coloring
for the Bruhat graph, as described in
Definition

5.4
, which shows that many such embeddings are possible.
We illustrate and discuss these results for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
.

Next, we investigate some specific aspects of the construction for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}
, with the goal of making connections with some well-known notions in combinatorics.
One important ingredient in the construction of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the notion of
right ends
of elements of a crystal, introduced in
[
MaYu23
]
.
For any
\mathfrak{g}
, the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
is constructed as the right ends of the elements of the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
corresponding to the regular weight
\rho=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{r}
.
For
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}
, it is well-known that elements of crystals can be put in one-to-one correspondence with semistandard Young tableaux (a result originally due to Kashiwara-Nakashima
[
KaNa94
]
).
In light of this identification, taking the right ends of crystal elements should correspond to some operation on such tableaux. In
Theorem

6.9
it is shown that the right ends coincide with the (left)
keys
of Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LaSc90
]
.
Along the way, we also discuss the relation between the notion of
crystal braiding
and the jeu de taquin slides for skew tableaux.

Finally we offer some brief discussion on the (tentative) connection between right ends and a
generalization of keys
introduced in
[
Len07
, Definition 5.2]
.
The latter notion can be defined for a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of arbitrary type, and reduces in type
A
to the one introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger in an appropriate sense.
Such a key can be defined as a map from a crystal to the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
, and can be characterized by some axioms given in
[
HeLe17
, Section 3]
.
This description makes it clear that the right ends
do not
coincide with such keys in general, since not all right ends can be put in correspondence with Weyl group elements.
However we expect that such keys should coincide with the right ends for many elements of the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
, and we discuss this explicitly in the case of
\mathfrak{g}=C_{2}
.

1.3.
Organization

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
we recall some basic facts concerning crystals and the Bruhat order.
In
Section

3
we review the construction of the higher-rank graphs
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
from
[
MaYu23
]
, highlighting the main aspects relevant for this paper.
In
Section

4
we prove the main technical results needed for the embeddings of the Bruhat graphs into
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
These are discussed in
Section

5
, where we distinguish between the right weak Bruhat order, the left weak Bruhat order and the (strong) Bruhat order.
We also comment on these results and illustrate them in the case of
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
.
In
Section

6
we specialize to
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}
and reinterpret various aspects of the construction of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
In particular, we show how the crystal braiding can be computed in terms of jeu de taquin slides, and how the right ends can be be interpreted as left keys.
Finally in
Section

7
we briefly discuss the (tentative) connection with the mentioned generalization of keys, illustrating this for
\mathfrak{g}=C_{2}
.

2.
Some preliminaries

In this section we review some standard material concerning crystals associated to a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and the Bruhat order on the corresponding Weyl group
W
.
In particular, we emphasize the action of the Weyl group on crystals.

2.1.
Crystals

We begin by fixing some standard notations for a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
We write
I=\{1,\ldots,r\}
for the index set corresponding to the rank
r
.
The simple roots and fundamental weights are denoted by
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, respectively.
We write
(\cdot,\cdot)
for the usual invariant bilinear form on the dual of the Cartan subalgebra.
The coroots are denoted by
\alpha^{\vee}=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)
and we recall the relation
(\omega_{i},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})=\delta_{ij}
.
We write
\mathsf{P}^{+}
for the dominant weights and
\preceq
for the usual partial order on weights (meaning that
\mu\prec\lambda
if
\lambda-\mu
is an element of the positive root lattice).

For the precise definition of a
\mathfrak{g}
-crystal we refer to
[
HoKa02
, Section 4.5]
(all the crystals we consider here are
semiregular
), while below we only highlight the most relevant aspects for our purposes.
As part of the definition of a crystal
\mathcal{B}
, we have maps
\tilde{E}_{i},\tilde{F}_{i}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}
for any
i\in I
, called the
Kashiwara operators
.
These can be seen as a renormalization of the action of the root vectors
E_{i},F_{i}
for the simple root
\alpha_{i}
(see
[
HoKa02
, Section 4.1]
for a precise definition).
Corresponding to these operators, we have the maps
\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{N}
given by

\varepsilon_{i}(b)=\max\{k:\tilde{E}_{i}^{k}b\in\mathcal{B}\},\quad\varphi_{i}(b)=\max\{k:\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}b\in\mathcal{B}\}.

They satisfy the relation
\varphi_{i}(b)-\varepsilon_{i}(b)=(\mathrm{wt}(b),\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
, where
\mathrm{wt}(b)
is the weight of
b\in\mathcal{B}
.

The
crystal graph
corresponding to
\mathcal{B}
is the graph with vertices given by the elements of
\mathcal{B}
and with directed edges corresponding to the action of the Kashiwara operators
\{\tilde{F}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
Given
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
, we write
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for the unique (up to isomorphism) crystal corresponding to the highest weight
\lambda
.
In this case we use the terminology
connected
, since the corresponding crystal graph turns out to be connected (another standard terminology would be irreducible).

One of the most notable feature of crystals is their tensor product structure.
Given two crystals
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, we can form their tensor product
\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
(which as a set is simply
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
). The Kashiwara operators act as follows (with conventions as in
[
HoKa02
]
):

(2.1)

\tilde{E}_{i}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\tilde{E}_{i}b\otimes b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)\geq\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\\
b\otimes\tilde{E}_{i}b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)<\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\end{cases},\quad\tilde{F}_{i}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\tilde{F}_{i}b\otimes b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)>\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\\
b\otimes\tilde{F}_{i}b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)\leq\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\end{cases}.

(Let us note that these are the same conventions used in the papers by Kashiwara, but opposite to those used in the book
[
BuSc17
]
, which is going to be relevant later on).

The crystal graph of the tensor product
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
can be decomposed into connected components, which turn out to correspond to the simple components in the analogous decomposition for the classical (or quantum) modules
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{\prime})
.

There is an action of the Weyl group
W
on
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals, which was originally defined in
[
Kas94
]
(see also
[
BuSc17
, Section 11.3]
).
Given an element
b\in\mathcal{B}
of weight
\mathrm{wt}(b)
, we have

\mathsf{s}_{i}b:=\begin{cases}\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}b&k>0\\
b&k=0\\
\tilde{E}_{i}^{k}b&k<0\end{cases},\quad k=(\mathrm{wt}(b),\alpha_{i}^{\vee}).

We note that
\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{s}_{i}b)=\mathrm{wt}(b)-k\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
, which means that
\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{s}_{i}b)=s_{i}\mathrm{wt}(b)
, where on the right-hand side we have the ordinary Weyl group action on weights.

Given a dominant weight
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
, the weights in the orbit of
\lambda
under the Weyl group
W
are called
extremal
.
In the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
there is a unique element of weight
w\lambda
for
w\in W
, which we can then denote by
b_{w\lambda}
.
It follows from the discussion above that
\mathsf{s}_{i}b_{w\lambda}=b_{s_{i}w\lambda}
.

2.2.
Bruhat order

We refer to
[
BjBr05
]
for any unexplained notions and results.
Let
W
be a Weyl group with a fixed set of generators
S
, also called the
simple reflections
.
The length function on
W
is denoted by
\ell
.
We denote by
T:=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}
the set of
reflections
.
We recall that the elements of
T
can be put in one-to-one correspondence with the positive roots of the Lie algebra associated with
W
, and that under this identification the simple reflections correspond to the simple roots, see
[
BjBr05
, Proposition 4.4.5]
.

Let us now recall the notions of Bruhat order and Bruhat graph on
W
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Bruhat graph

B(W)
corresponding to
W
is defined as follows:

•

the vertices are the elements of
W
,

•

we have an edge
u\to w
if
w=ut
with
t\in T
and
\ell(w)>\ell(u)
.

We write
w>u
if there exist elements
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
such that
u\to w_{1}\to\cdots\to w_{n}\to w
.
This is called the (strong)
Bruhat order
on
W
.

Remark 2.2
.

The edges in the Bruhat graph can be equivalently defined by the condition
w=tu
with
t\in T
and
\ell(u)<\ell(w)
(that is, by putting the reflection
t
on the left). This gives the same graph, since we have
ut=(utu^{-1})u
and
utu^{-1}\in T
.

The right and left weak versions are defined similarly, but only using simple reflections.

Definition 2.3
.

The
right weak Bruhat graph

B_{R}(W)
is defined as follows:

•

the vertices are the elements of
W
,

•

we have an edge
u\to w
if
w=us_{i}
with
s_{i}\in S
and
\ell(w)>\ell(u)
.

The
left weak Bruhat graph

B_{R}(W)
is defined similarly, with the edges defined by the condition
u\to w
if
w=s_{i}u
with
s_{i}\in S
and
\ell(w)>\ell(u)
.

The right and left weak Bruhat orders are different partial orders on
W
, in general. However they are isomorphic to each other via the map
w\mapsto w^{-1}
.

3.
Higher-rank graph for Lie algebras

This section is dedicated to reviewing the construction of the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
from
[
MaYu23
]
, which can be defined for any finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
We begin by spelling out the definition of a higher-rank graph, as well as recalling some standard terminology associated to it.
We proceed by reviewing the notions of
Cartan braiding
and
right ends
, which have been introduced in
[
MaYu23
]
, as well as their main properties.
Finally we use these two crucial ingredients to explain the construction of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

3.1.
Higher-rank graphs

The notion of higher-rank graph was introduced in
[
KuPa00
]
to construct
C^{*}
-algebras generalizing the more familiar graph
C^{*}
-algebras.

In the following definition we regard
\mathbb{N}^{k}
as a category with one object and morphisms corresponding to
k
-tuples of natural numbers, with composition given by their sum.

Definition 3.1
.

A
higher-rank graph of rank
k
(or
k
-graph for short) is a countable category
\Lambda
together with a (degree) functor
d:\Lambda\to\mathbb{N}^{k}
satisfying the following
factorization property
: for every morphism
f\in\Lambda
and
k
-tuples
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
d(f)=m+n
, there are unique morphisms
g,h\in\Lambda
with
d(g)=m
and
d(h)=n
such that
f=g\circ h
.

We recall some standard terminology used in this context.
A morphism
f
with
d(f)=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})
is also called a
path of color
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})
.
As a special case, morphisms with
d(f)=(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0)
with
1
in position
i
are called
edges of color
i
.
The morphisms of degree zero can be identified with the objects of
\Lambda
, and they also referred to as the
vertices
.
The notations
s
and
r
are used to denote the source and range of a morphism, respectively.

Example 3.2
.

Any directed graph can be regarded as a higher-rank graph of rank
1
in a natural way.
In this case
\Lambda
is simply the path category corresponding to the graph, with the degree functor
d:\Lambda\to\mathbb{N}
being given by the length of a path.

The
skeleton
of a
k
-graph
\Lambda
is the directed graph with
k
colors constructed by taking all vertices and edges of any color of
\Lambda
(but not general paths).
It is not possible to recover
\Lambda
from the knowledge of its skeleton, in general.
This requires the extra data of a collection of commuting squares, see
[
HRSW13
]
(although we are not going to use this structure).

3.2.
Cartan braiding

In order to describe the higher-rank graphs
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we begin by introducing certain morphisms between crystals.
More precisely, they are going to be morphisms defined on the Cartan components of tensor products, in the following sense.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
be a product of connected crystals.
Then its
Cartan component
is the unique connected component isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n})
.

Using this terminology, we define the notion of Cartan braiding.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
be connected crystals.
The
Cartan braiding

\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)

is the unique morphism of crystals which is an isomorphism between the Cartan components of the two tensor products and is zero otherwise. That is, for
\mathcal{B}(\mu)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
we have

\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}(\mathcal{B}(\mu))=\begin{cases}\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})&\mu=\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{cases}.

More generally, it can be defined for products of connected crystals.
We should warn the reader that
\sigma
is
not
a braiding in the usual sense (for instance, it is not invertible).
The choice of name is justified by the fact that it can be obtained as a limit of the rescaled braiding
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, see
[
MaYu23
, Theorem 5.3]
.
As such, it inherits various properties from the braiding
\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}
, which are explored in the cited paper.

Remark 3.5
.

The category of
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals is not braided, but rather admits the structure of a coboundary category.
The natural isomorphisms in this case are given by the
crystal commutor
introduced in
[
HeKa06
]
.
We observe that the Cartan braiding
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}
coincides with the crystal commutor when restricted to the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
.
One interesting aspect of the former is that it obeys the braid equation (at the cost of not being invertible), while the latter obeys the more complicated axioms of a coboundary category.

The Cartan braiding does not typically coincide with the flip map, as is typically the case for crystal morphisms of this type.
However this turns to be the case for the subset of extremal elements of the tensor product, as shown in
[
MaYu23
, Lemma 10.1]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
. Then for any
w\in W
we have

\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w\lambda^{\prime}})=b_{w\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes b_{w\lambda}.

We are going to make frequent use of this property in the following.

3.3.
Right ends

Before describing the higher-rank graphs we need one more definition, which is that of right end of a crystal element (with respect to a dominant weight).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
be such that
\lambda\succeq\mu
.
The
right end
of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
with respect to
\mu
is the (set-theoretic) map
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\mu)
defined as follows: writing

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu),\quad b\mapsto b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime},

for the (unique) inclusion of the Cartan component, we set
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b):=b^{\prime\prime}
.

This definition can be extended to products of connected components as follows. Note that the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
can be identified with
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, where
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}
.
If
b\in\mathcal{B}
is not in the Cartan component we set
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b):=0
, otherwise we define
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)
as above for
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
using the unique inclusion
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}
.

We adopt the following notational conventions. We write
\mathsf{R}_{i}:=\mathsf{R}_{\omega_{i}}
for the right ends corresponding to the fundamental weights. We write
\rho:=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{r}
for the smallest regular weight.
Then for any
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
such that
\lambda\succeq\rho
, and any element
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, we write

\mathsf{R}(b):=\left(\mathsf{R}_{1}(b),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{r}(b)\right).

A convenient way to compute right ends is given in
[
MaYu23
, Proposition 6.7]
, where
\sigma_{k}
denotes the action of the Cartan braiding on the factors
k
and
k+1
of a tensor product.

Proposition 3.8
.

The elements
b=b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
belonging to the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
can be characterized by the following property:

\sigma_{n-1}\cdots\sigma_{k}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0,\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1.

Moreover, the right end
\mathsf{R}_{\lambda_{k}}(b)
is equal to the rightmost tensor factor of this expression.

Another useful property concerning right ends is the following
[
MaYu23
, Proposition 6.9]
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
c\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)
and write
\mathsf{R}(c)=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
.
Suppose that
c\otimes b
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.
Then we have
\mathsf{R}_{i}(c\otimes b)=\mathsf{R}_{i}(c_{i}\otimes b)
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

This identity can be used to compute the source of a path in the definition below.

3.4.
Construction

Finally we come to the definition of the higher-rank graph given in
[
MaYu23
]
.
We note that our conventions here are slightly different (see
Remark

3.12
).

Definition 3.10

(The higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra of rank
r
.
To it we associate a category
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
defined as follows.

•

The
vertices
are given by the right ends of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
with
\rho=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{r}
.
Note that the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
can be identified with the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\omega_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\omega_{r})
.

•

The
paths
are given by pairs of the form
(v,b)
, with
v
a vertex and
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, satisfying the following compatibility condition: writing
v=\mathsf{R}(c)
for some
c\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)
, we must have that
c\otimes b
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

•

A path
e=(v,b)
has
range

r(e)=v
and
source

s(e)=\mathsf{R}(c\otimes b)
, where as above we write the vertex as
v=\mathsf{R}(c)
for some
c\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)
.

•

A path
(v,b)
as above has
degree

\lambda
, using the natural identification
\mathsf{P}^{+}\cong\mathbb{N}^{r}
.

•

Given two composable paths
(v,b)
and
(v^{\prime},b^{\prime})
, with
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
, their
composition
is given by
(v,b)\cdot(v^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(v,\phi(b\otimes b^{\prime}))
, where
\phi:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
denotes the projection onto the Cartan component.

Equivalently, the source
s(e)
of a path
e=(v,b)
can be computed in terms of
Proposition

3.9
as follows: writing
v=\mathsf{R}(c)=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
for some
c\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)
, we have

s(e)=\left(\mathsf{R}_{1}(c_{1}\otimes b),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{r}(c_{r}\otimes b)\right).

The results from the previous subsections can be used to show that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
actually defines a category.
Furthermore, we have the following result from
[
MaYu23
, Theorem 7.8]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

The category
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a higher-rank graph of rank
r
.

Remark 3.12
.

The higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
described here is the opposite (as a category) of the one described in
[
MaYu23
, Theorem 7.8]
and denoted there by
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
The reason for this change in conventions is to have a better match between the directions of paths in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
and the conventional way in which the Bruhat graph of
W
is described.

The structure of the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
appears to be rather intricate, although various general results are proven in the cited paper.
For instance, we have the following result regarding the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
from
[
MaYu23
, Proposition 10.3]
.

Proposition 3.13
.

The Weyl group
W
embeds into the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
by
w\mapsto\mathsf{R}(b_{w\rho})
.

We note that this is essentially a consequence of
Lemma

3.6
and the fact that the stabilizer of
\rho
under the action of
W
is trivial.
In the following we often make a small abuse of notation and identify the right end
\mathsf{R}(b_{w\rho})
with the element
b_{w\rho}
.
This is justified, since for
b_{w\rho}=b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\omega_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\omega_{r})
we get
\mathsf{R}(b_{w\rho})=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
.

Remark 3.14
.

In general the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
does not correspond to the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
.
This is shown for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=C_{2}
in
[
MaYu23
, Example 10.6]
.

We can define a partial order on the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
as follows. First, any crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is partially ordered by setting
b\leq b^{\prime}
whenever
b=\tilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tilde{F}_{i_{n}}b^{\prime}
for some Kashiwara operators.
Then, for vertices
v=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
and
v^{\prime}=(b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{r}^{\prime})
, we say that
v\leq v^{\prime}
if
b_{i}\leq b_{i}^{\prime}
for every
i
.
It is proven in
[
MaYu23
, Proposition 7.11]
that for any path
e\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
we have
r(e)\leq s(e)
(once again, recall the opposite conventions from
Remark

3.12
).
Considering
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
as a directed graph, this shows that all cycles must have length one (that is, they are loops).

4.
Some technical results

In this section we prove some results needed for the next one, where we discuss the problem of embedding the Bruhat graph into the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We begin by exploring the interplay between the Bruhat order and the Cartan component of a tensor product of connected crystals.
The main result here is that
b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
, provided
w\geq w^{\prime}
with respect to the Bruhat order.
This is used to study certain paths in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponding to any such pair
(w,w^{\prime})
of Weyl group elements.

4.1.
Bruhat order and crystals

Recall the following standard fact related to the length function
\ell
on a Weyl group: we have
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
if and only if
w^{-1}\alpha_{i}\succ 0
.
This has the following well-known consequence for the maps
\varphi_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{i}
of a crystal.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
and
w\in W
. The following holds:

(1)

if
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
then
\varepsilon_{i}(b_{w\lambda})=0
,

(2)

if
\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)
then
\varphi_{i}(b_{w\lambda})=0
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose on the contrary that
\varepsilon_{i}(b_{w\lambda})>0
, so that the element
\tilde{E}_{i}b_{w\lambda}
is non-zero and of weight
w\lambda+\alpha_{i}
. Then, acting with
w^{-1}
on it, we obtain another element of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
of weight
\lambda+w^{-1}\alpha_{i}
.
Since
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
implies
w^{-1}\alpha_{i}\succ 0
, we find that
\lambda+w^{-1}\alpha_{i}\succ\lambda
.
But this is a contradiction, since
\lambda
is the highest weight of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

(2) Similar to (1), by considering
\tilde{F}_{i}b_{w\lambda}
and using
w^{-1}\alpha_{i}\prec 0
.
∎

We use this to investigate the action of
\tilde{F}_{i}
on particular elements of a tensor product.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
and
w,w^{\prime}\in W
.
Let
n=(w\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
and
n^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
.
Suppose that
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
and
\ell(s_{i}w^{\prime})>\ell(w^{\prime})
. Then:

(1)

for
k\leq n
we have
\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})=\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
,

(2)

for
n<k\leq n+n^{\prime}
we have
\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})=\tilde{F}_{i}^{n}b_{w\lambda}\otimes\tilde{F}_{i}^{k-n}b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

First we observe that both
n
and
n^{\prime}
are non-negative, so that the claims make sense. To see this, we rewrite
n=(\lambda,(w^{-1}\alpha_{i})^{\vee})
using
W
-invariance (similarly for
n^{\prime}
). The condition
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
implies
w^{-1}\alpha_{i}\succ 0
, which gives
n\geq 0
since
\lambda
is dominant.

(1) We have
\varepsilon_{i}(b_{w\lambda})=0
by
Lemma

4.1
, since
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
.
This means that
n=\varphi_{i}(b_{w\lambda})
.
Furthermore, this gives
\varphi_{i}(\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}b_{w\lambda})=n-k
for
k\leq n
.
Similarly we have
\varepsilon_{i}(b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})=0
and
n^{\prime}=\varphi_{i}(b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})
.
Then the claim easily follows from the tensor product rule (
2.1
), since we have
\varphi_{i}(\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}b_{w\lambda})>\varepsilon_{i}(b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})
for
k<n
, which means that we always act on the first factor.

(2) Since
\varphi_{i}(\tilde{F}_{i}^{n}b_{w\lambda})=0
, all successive applications of
\tilde{F}_{i}
are on the second factor.
∎

The previous result can be rephrased in terms of the Weyl group action on crystals.

Corollary 4.3
.

Under the assumptions and notations
Lemma

4.2
, we have

\tilde{F}_{i}^{n}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})=\mathsf{s}_{i}b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}},\quad\mathsf{s}_{i}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})=\mathsf{s}_{i}b_{w\lambda}\otimes\mathsf{s}_{i}b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}.

Proof.

By definition of the Weyl group action we have
\mathsf{s}_{i}b_{w\lambda}=\tilde{F}_{i}^{n}b_{w\lambda}
and
\mathsf{s}_{i}b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}=\tilde{F}_{i}^{n^{\prime}}b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
, since
n=(w\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee})\geq 0
and
n^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\alpha_{i}^{\vee})\geq 0
. Similarly we have
\mathsf{s}_{i}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})=\tilde{F}_{i}^{n+n^{\prime}}(b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})
. Then the identities immediately follow from
Lemma

4.2
.
∎

Before proceeding, we need the following well-known result concerning the Bruhat order.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in W
be such that
w>w^{\prime}
in the Bruhat order. Let
n=\ell(w)
and
m=\ell(w)-\ell(w^{\prime})
.
Fix a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
.
Then there exists a decreasing sequence
j_{1}>\cdots>j_{m}
of indices (for the decomposition of
w
) such that:

•

the word
s_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{s}_{j_{k}}\cdots\hat{s}_{j_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
, obtained from
w
by removing the letters
\hat{s}_{j_{k}},\ldots\hat{s}_{j_{1}}
, is a reduced expression for any
k=1,\ldots,m
,

•

we have
s_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{s}_{j_{m}}\cdots\hat{s}_{j_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}=w^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This is proven in
[
BjBr05
, Section 2.7]
for the increasing case, that is by removing letters from the left.
The decreasing case follows by applying this result to
w^{-1}>(w^{\prime})^{-1}
, since this is equivalent to
w>w^{\prime}
and inversion reverses the reduced decomposition.
∎

We can use the previous results to prove the following proposition, which relates the Bruhat order on
W
to the Cartan component of a tensor product.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
and
w,w^{\prime}\in W
.
Suppose that
w\geq w^{\prime}
in the Bruhat order.
Then
b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Fix a reduced decomposition
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
.
If
w=w^{\prime}
we can apply repeatedly
Corollary

4.3
to get
\mathsf{s}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{s}_{n}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}})=b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w\lambda^{\prime}}
.
This shows that
b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w\lambda^{\prime}}
is in the Cartan component, since it is obtained from
b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}}
by the action of Kashiwara operators.

If
w\neq w^{\prime}
we proceed as follows.
Write
w_{k}=s_{i_{k}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
for
k=1,\ldots,n
and note that this is reduced for each
k
.
By
Lemma

4.4
, we can obtain
w^{\prime}
from
w
by removing a decreasing sequence
j_{1}>\cdots>j_{m}
, such that at each step we get a reduced decomposition.
Let us write
w^{\prime}_{k}=\tilde{s}_{k}\cdots\tilde{s}_{n}
, where
\tilde{s}_{k}=1
if
k=j_{a}
for some
a
and
\tilde{s}_{k}=s_{i_{k}}
otherwise.
Note that
w^{\prime}_{1}=w^{\prime}
, since this is exactly obtained by removing the letters
s_{j_{1}},\ldots,s_{j_{m}}
.

Define the map
T_{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,n
as follows: if
\tilde{s}_{k}=s_{i_{k}}
we set
T_{k}=\mathsf{s}_{i_{k}}
, while if
\tilde{s}_{k}=1
we set
T_{k}=\tilde{F}^{a_{k}}_{i_{k}}
where
a_{k}=(w_{k+1}\lambda,\alpha_{i_{k}}^{\vee})
(the discussion below shows that these numbers are non-negative).
We claim that
T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}})=b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
, which would prove that
b_{w\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}
belongs to the Cartan component, since each
T_{k}
consists of Kashiwara operators.

The base case
T_{n}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}})=b_{w_{n}\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}_{n}\lambda^{\prime}}
follows immediately from
Corollary

4.3
.
Now suppose that
T_{k+1}\cdots T_{n}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}})=b_{w_{k+1}\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}_{k+1}\lambda^{\prime}}
for some
k+1
.
Observe that
\ell(s_{i_{k}}w_{k+1})>\ell(w_{k+1})
and
\ell(s_{i_{k}}w^{\prime}_{k+1})>\ell(w^{\prime}_{k+1})
, where the first follows from the fact that we chose a reduced decomposition for
w
, while the second follows from
Lemma

4.4
.
Note that the first identity also shows that the numbers
a_{k}=(w_{k+1}\lambda,\alpha_{i_{k}}^{\vee})
are non-negative.
Then applying
Corollary

4.3
once again leads to
T_{k}\cdots T_{n}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}})=b_{w_{k}\lambda}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}_{k}\lambda^{\prime}}
, which proves the result.
∎

4.2.
Consequences for paths

We now investigate the consequences of
Proposition

4.5
for paths in the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, beginning with the following general result.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
w\geq w^{\prime}
in the Bruhat order. Then, for any
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
, we have that the pair
(b_{w\rho},b_{w^{\prime}\lambda})
is a path in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
of color
\lambda
and range
b_{w\rho}
.

Proof.

By
Definition

3.10
, the pair
(v,b)
with
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is a path in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
if, choosing
c\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)
such that
v=\mathsf{R}(c)
, we have that
c\otimes b
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.
Consider
(b_{w\rho},b_{w^{\prime}\lambda})
, where we identify
b_{w\rho}
with its right end as usual.
Since
w\geq w^{\prime}
, it follows from
Proposition

4.5
that
b_{w\rho}\otimes b_{w^{\prime}\lambda}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, which shows that it is a path. It is immediate from the definitions that is it of color
\lambda
and range
b_{w\rho}
.
∎

Determining the sources of such paths is a more complicated matter.
To proceed, we introduce the following notions (which are more or less standard).

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\gamma
be a positive root and write
\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
in terms of the simple roots.
Then its
root support
is defined as the set
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma):=\{i\in I:c_{i}\neq 0\}
.

Similarly, let
\lambda
be a dominant weight and write
\lambda=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}^{\prime}\omega_{i}
in terms of the fundamental weights.
Then its
weight support
is defined as the set
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda):=\{i\in I:c_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0\}
.

Remark 4.8
.

The two distinct notations are used to avoid ambiguity in the case of positive roots which are also dominant weights (although the use should be clear from the context).

It follows immediately from the definition that
(\omega_{i},\gamma)>0
for
i\in\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)
and is zero otherwise.
Similarly
(\lambda,\alpha_{i})>0
for
i\in\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
and is zero otherwise.

These two notions of support can be used to describe two cases in which we can compute the right ends easily, as illustrated in the next result.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
w\in W
and
t\in T
. Suppose that
\ell(wt)>\ell(w)
.
Denote by
\gamma
be the positive root corresponding to
t
.
Then for any
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
we have

\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=b_{w\omega_{i}},

provided that either
i\notin\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)
or
i\in\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Recall that the reflection
t=t_{\gamma}
acts on a weight
\mu
by
t\mu=\mu-(\mu,\gamma^{\vee})\gamma
.
Hence we get
t\omega_{i}=\omega_{i}
if
i\notin\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)
.
Also recall that the Cartan braiding coincides with the flip map for elements in the orbit of the Weyl group by
Lemma

3.6
.
Using these facts we get

\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{w\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=b_{w\omega_{i}}.

Now consider the case
i\in\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
.
Then we have a unique morphism of crystals

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\omega_{i}),

with
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
being the Cartan component of the tensor product.
Under this identification, the extremal element
b_{w\lambda}
is mapped to
b_{w\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w(\lambda-\omega_{i})}
.
According to
Proposition

3.8
, the right end
\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})
can be obtained as the rightmost factor of the expression

\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w(\lambda-\omega_{i})}).

The assumption
\ell(wt)>\ell(w)
guarantees that
b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\omega_{i}}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})
, by
Proposition

4.5
.
We also recall that the Cartan braiding is the identity in this case (the identity clearly being a morphism of crystals here).
Then we compute

\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w(\lambda-\omega_{i})})=\sigma_{2}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w(\lambda-\omega_{i})})=b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w(\lambda-\omega_{i})}\otimes b_{w\omega_{i}}.

We note that the last step follows from the fact that both elements are in the orbit of
w
, using
Lemma

3.6
.
From this we conclude that
\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=b_{w\omega_{i}}
.
∎

The lemma above allows us to easily compute the sources of certain paths.

Corollary 4.10
.

With notation as in
Lemma

4.9
, suppose that
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
. Then we have that
(b_{wt\rho},b_{w\lambda})
is a path with source
b_{w\rho}
and range
b_{wt\rho}
.

Proof.

It is clear that its range is given by
b_{wt\rho}
.
To determine its source, we need to compute

\mathsf{R}(b_{wt\rho}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=\left(\mathsf{R}_{1}(b_{wt\omega_{1}}\otimes b_{w\lambda}),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{r}(b_{wt\omega_{r}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})\right).

By
Lemma

4.9
we know that
\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=b_{w\omega_{i}}
if
i\notin\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)
or
i\in\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
.
Then the assumption
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
guarantees that this is the case for all
i\in I
. Hence

\left(\mathsf{R}_{1}(b_{wt\omega_{1}}\otimes b_{w\lambda}),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{r}(b_{wt\omega_{r}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})\right)=(b_{w\omega_{1}},\ldots,b_{w\omega_{r}})\equiv b_{w\rho}.\qed

5.
Embedding the Bruhat graph

We are now going to use the results of the previous section to discuss in what sense the Bruhat graph can be embedded in the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We treat separately the cases of the weak Bruhat graphs and the (strong) Bruhat graph (as well as discussing how we can regard them as colored graphs).
We show that the right weak Bruhat graph embeds naturally in the skeleton of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, unlike the left one.
For the Bruhat graph we necessarily have to look for embeddings into the whole
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, not just its skeleton, and we show that many such embeddings are possible.
Finally we illustrate and discuss these results in the case of
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
.

5.1.
The weak Bruhat graphs

Let us begin by discussing the case of the right weak Bruhat graph
B_{R}(W)
, whose edges are of the form
w\to ws_{i}
with
\ell(ws_{i})>\ell(w)
.

We can make
B_{R}(W)
into a colored graph with colors
I=\{1,\ldots,r\}
in a natural way, by assigning the color
i
to each edge of the form
w\to ws_{i}
(this should be seen as corresponding to the right multiplication in the Weyl group by the simple reflection
s_{i}
).
Similarly, we can regard the skeleton of the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
as a colored graph with colors
\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}\}
.
In the following we identify these sets of colors by
k\equiv\omega_{k}
.

As previously discussed in
Proposition

3.13
, we have an inclusion

\iota_{W}:W\to\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad w\mapsto b_{w\rho}

of the Weyl group
W
into the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Here as usual we make no distinction between the crystal element
b_{w\rho}\in\mathcal{B}(\rho)
and its right end in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}

which are compatible with

Theorem 5.1
.

There is a unique embedding (of colored graphs) of the right weak Bruhat graph
B_{R}(W)
into the skeleton of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
which is compatible with the inclusion
\iota_{W}
.

It sends the edge
w\to ws_{i}
of
B_{R}(W)
to the edge
(b_{ws_{i}\rho},b_{w\omega_{i}})
of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

First we show the existence of such an embedding.
For the vertices this is given by
\iota_{W}
, that is
w\mapsto b_{w\rho}
.
Next, we claim that
(b_{ws_{i}\rho},b_{w\omega_{i}})
is an edge in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
of color
\omega_{i}
, source
b_{w\rho}
and range
b_{ws_{i}\rho}
. This follows from
Corollary

4.10
since, using the notation employed there, we have
\gamma=\alpha_{i}
,
\lambda=\omega_{i}
and
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\alpha_{i})=\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\omega_{i})=\{i\}
.
This shows the existence of the required embedding of colored graphs, so all that is left is to prove uniqueness.

Clearly an edge
w\to ws_{i}
in
B_{R}(W)
must be mapped to an edge of color
\omega_{i}
, source
b_{w\rho}
and range
b_{ws_{i}\rho}
in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then such an edge must be of the form
(b_{ws_{i}\rho},b)
with
b\in\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})
, since the vertex in this pair must be equal to the range, and the crystal of
b
determines the color.
The source of this edge can be computed by determining the right ends
\mathsf{R}_{k}(b_{ws_{i}\omega_{k}}\otimes b)
for
k=1,\ldots,r
.
Consider
k=i
, so that
b_{ws_{i}\omega_{i}}\otimes b
must be in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})

\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{ws_{i}\omega_{i}}\otimes b)=b
.
But, in order for the source to be
b_{w\rho}
, we must have
\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{ws_{i}\omega_{i}}\otimes b)=b_{w\omega_{i}}
.
This forces
b=b_{w\omega_{i}}
and hence uniqueness of the edge in the skeleton of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
∎

5.2.
The Bruhat graph

Before we begin considering the question of embedding the (strong) Bruhat graph
B(W)
into
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we must discuss how to make the former into a colored graph.
Here there is no canonical way of doing so, unlike in the weak case.
For this reason we are going to consider some general choices of coloring, described as follows.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\mathsf{c}
be a function from the edge set of
B(W)
to the dominant weights
\mathsf{P}^{+}
.
We say that
\mathsf{c}
is a
compatible coloring
if the following holds: given an edge
e:w\to wt
, with
t
corresponding to the positive root
\gamma
, we have that
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\mathsf{c}(e))
.

More concretely, if
\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
\mathsf{c}(e)=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}^{\prime}\omega_{i}
, we ask that
c_{i}\neq 0
implies
c_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Any such choice of compatible coloring gives an embedding, as we now show.

Theorem 5.5
.

Consider the Bruhat graph
B(W)
as a colored graph via a compatible coloring
\mathsf{c}
.
Then there exists an embedding of
B(W)
into
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
given as follows:

•

each vertex
w\in W
is sent to the (right end of the) crystal element
b_{w\rho}
,

•

each edge
e:w\to wt
is sent to the path
(b_{wt\rho},b_{w\lambda})
with
\lambda=\mathsf{c}(e)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be the positive root corresponding to the reflection
t
.
By
Lemma

4.9
we have that
\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\mu})=b_{w\omega_{i}}
if
i\notin\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)
or
i\in\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\mu)
, for any dominant weight
\mu
.
The condition in the definition of
\mathsf{c}
guarantees that
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\mathsf{c}(e))
.
Then we have
\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{r}}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}_{\mathrm{w}}(\lambda)
, which guarantees that
\mathsf{R}_{i}(b_{wt\omega_{i}}\otimes b_{w\lambda})=b_{w\omega_{i}}
for any
i\in I
.
From this it is immediate to see that
(b_{wt\rho},b_{w\lambda})
is a path from
b_{w\rho}
to
b_{wt\rho}
of color
\mathsf{c}(e)
, as required.
∎

Further conditions can potentially be imposed on this embedding to select a "natural" candidate, such as requiring some form of compatibility with the composition of edges in the skeleton of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponding to
B_{R}(W)
.
We leave such questions for future research.

6.
Right ends and keys

In this section we restrict to the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}=\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
, where it is well-known that crystal bases can be identified with appropriate semistandard Young tableaux.
The goal here is to connect various aspects of the construction of the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
with some well-known notions and operations in combinatorics related to such tableaux.

First we show how the Cartan braiding between two fundamental crystals can be computing using jeu de taquin slides on certain (skew) tableaux.

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)

key
of these tableaux, a notion originally introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger in
[

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
for

can be identified with the corresponding Weyl group
S_{r+1}
.

It is proven in
[
vLe01
, Theorem 3.3.1]
that the action of the Kashiwara operators commutes with the jeu de taquin slides (note that the Kashiwara operators are called
coplactic operations
in this reference).
The result then follows, since all other elements can be obtained from the highest weight elements under the action of the Kashiwara operators.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
and write
\lambda=\omega_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\omega_{i_{n}}
with
i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n}
.
Let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and identify this element with a tableau
T
as in (
6.1
).
Then we can compute the right ends
\left(\mathsf{R}_{i_{1}}(b),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{i_{n}}(b)\right)
of
b
using the corresponding tableau
T
as follows:

•

apply jeu de taquin slides to
T
to bring the column of length
i_{k}
to the leftmost position,

•

obtain the right end
\mathsf{R}_{i_{k}}(b)
as the leftmost column of this skew tableau.

Proof.

Write
b=b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
with
b_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i_{k}})
.
According to
Proposition

3.8
, the right end
\mathsf{R}_{i_{k}}(b)
for
k=1,\ldots,n
can be determined as follows: we use the Cartan braiding
\sigma
to compute
\sigma_{r-1}\cdots\sigma_{k}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{r})
and obtain the result as the rightmost tensor factor.
Each of the Cartan braidings
\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\omega_{j})\to\mathcal{B}(\omega_{j})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\omega_{i})
can be computed in terms of jeu de taquin slides between two-column (skew) tableaux, as shown in
Lemma

6.2
.
Since the correspondence reverses products (with the conventions we are using for the tensor product of crystals), we see that the rightmost tensor factor of
\sigma_{r-1}\cdots\sigma_{k}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{r})
corresponds to the leftmost column of the skew tableau obtained by applying the jeu de taquin slides.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

In particular, this result can be used to compute the right ends of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
.
In this case we have
\rho=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{r}
and
\mathsf{R}(b)=\left(\mathsf{R}_{1}(b),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{r}(b)\right)
.

6.3.
Keys of tableaux

The notion of key of a tableau was introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger in
[
LaSc90
]
to analyze the combinatorics of standard bases of sections of line bundles (see also
[
Ful97
, Appendix A.5]
).
We review this notion below.

A skew tableau is called
frank
if the lengths of its columns are the same (up to a permutation) as the lengths of the columns of its rectification.
Let
T
be a tableau with
n
columns of lengths
(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n})
. For each column
1\leq i\leq n
, choose a frank skew tableau
S_{i}
such that its rightmost column has length
\ell_{i}
. We denote such a column by
R_{i}(T)
, since it turns out not to depend on the specific choice of
S_{i}
. Then the
right key

K_{+}(T)
of
T
is the tableau of the same shape as
T
and with columns given by
R_{1}(T),\ldots,R_{n}(T)
.

A similar definition is given for the
left key

K_{-}(T)
.
We consider frank skew tableaux whose leftmost columns
L_{i}(T)
have lengths
\ell_{i}
, and form the key by the columns
L_{1}(T),\ldots,L_{n}(T)
.

In practice, the keys can be computed by applying jeu de taquin slides to the tableau
T
to produce the required skew tableaux, as illustrated in the next example.

The next result shows that, in type
A
, the notion of right end essentially coincides with that of left key (the left-right exchange being purely a matter of convention, see
Remark

6.10
).

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}
.
Let
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
and write
\lambda=\omega_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\omega_{i_{n}}
with
i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n}
.
Let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and identify this element with a tableau
T
as in (
6.1
).
Then the right ends
\left(\mathsf{R}_{i_{1}}(b),\ldots,\mathsf{R}_{i_{n}}(b)\right)
can be identified with the columns of the left key
K_{-}(T)
.

Proof.

By definition of the left key
K_{-}(T)
, its columns are given by the leftmost columns
L_{k}(T)
of the appropriate frank skew tableaux
S_{k}^{\prime}
corresponding to the tableau
T
.
As shown in
[
Ful97
, Appendix A.5]
), such frank skew tableaux can be obtained by applying jeu de taquin slides to adjacent columns of
T
.
By
Lemma

6.2
we know that any such move between adjacent columns corresponds to the action of the Cartan braiding on a tensor product.
Recall that the correspondence between tableaux and Cartan components used in
Proposition

6.5
reverses the order of products.
Then we see that the leftmost column
L_{k}(T)
of the skew tableau
S_{k}^{\prime}
corresponds to the rightmost factor of the corresponding tensor product.
By
Proposition

6.5
this corresponds to the right end
\mathsf{R}_{i_{k}}(b)
, which proves the result.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

The right-to-left exchange in
Theorem

6.9
is entirely due to the reversal of products in the correspondence (
6.1
).
Note that we could have chosen to work with the opposite convention for the tensor product of crystals, as done in
[
BuSc17
]
(which is better adapted to describe classical results in combinatorics).
With this different convention, the right ends would correspond to the right keys, which is a bit more natural.

An an easy corollary of this identification, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 6.11
.

The vertex set of the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}
can be identified with the corresponding Weyl group
W=S_{r+1}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that the image of the (left or right) key map on tableaux with entries in
\{1,\cdots,n\}
can be seen as a subset of the symmetric group
S_{n}
(for instance, in
[
HeLe17
, Section 2.6]
a generalization of the key map is defined directly with values in the Weyl group).

Theorem

6.9
allows us to compute the right ends of
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{r}
in terms of the left key map. Hence the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
can be identified with a subset of the Weyl group
W=S_{r+1}
.
But the latter embeds in the vertex set of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
by
Proposition

3.13
, which shows equality.
∎

It should also be possible to interpret the paths in the higher-rank graph
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
in a similar way, by considering products of key tableaux and general tableaux as in
[
FuLa94
, Lemma 3]
.
However we are not going to investigate this connection here.

7.
Keys beyond type
A

The notion of key can be generalized to a map
\kappa:\mathcal{B}\to W
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a crystal of an arbitrary symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
W
its corresponding Weyl group, see for instance
[
HeLe17
]
and references therein.
This generalization is based on the notion of
Lakshmibai–Seshadri paths
introduced by Littelmann in
[
Lit94
]
: the right and left keys can be defined using such paths in terms of their initial and final directions, respectively.
An algorithm to compute the (right) key, without reference to the path model, is given
[
HeLe17
, Theorem 3.4]
.
It should be mentioned that other methods are available to compute such keys, but the one the cited paper appears more closely related to the setting considered here.

It is known that such (generalized) keys can be identified with the keys of Lascoux and Schützenberger for
\mathfrak{g}
of type
A
.
Then it follows from
Theorem

6.9
that, in this case, the right ends can also be identified with such keys.
On the other hand, it is not clear how one should compare the two notions beyond type
A
.
The two notions are certainly distinct in general, since the key as defined above takes value in the Weyl group
W
, while the right ends do not necessarily correspond to Weyl group elements (see for instance the example below).

The rest of this section is dedicated to the computation of the right ends and the keys corresponding to the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\rho)
for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=C_{2}
.
This is simply meant as an invitation for further study on the connection between these two notions.

Warning
: in the example below we switch to the
opposite
conventions for the tensor product of crystals, since we want to use the rules given in
[
HeLe17
, Theorem 3.4]
, which allow the computation of the right keys.
We note that, with these opposite conventions for the tensor product,
Theorem

6.9
gives an identification between right ends and right keys instead.

Communication over LQG Control Systems: A Convex Optimization Approach to Capacity
Aharon Rips, Oron Sabag

Abstract
We study communication over control systems, where a controller-encoder selects inputs to a dynamical system in order to simultaneously regulate the system and convey a message to an observer that has access to the system’s outputs measurements. This setup reflects implicit communication, as the controller embeds a message in the control signal. The capacity of a control system is the maximal reliable rate of the embedded message subject to a closed-loop control-cost constraint. We focus on linear quadratic Gaussian (LQG) control systems, in which the dynamical system is given by a state-space model with Gaussian noise, and the control cost is a quadratic function of the system inputs and system states. Our main result is a convex optimization upper bound on the capacity of LQG systems. In the case of scalar systems, we prove that the upper bound yields the exact LQG system capacity. The upper bound also recovers all known results, including LQG control, feedback capacity of Gaussian channels with memory, and the LQG system capacity with a state-feedback. For vector LQG control systems, we provide a sufficient condition for tightness of the upper bound, based on the Riccati equation. Numerical simulations indicate the upper bound tightness in all tested examples, suggesting that the upper bound may be equal to the LQG system capacity in the vector case as well.



I
Introduction

Control and communication tasks in engineering systems are traditionally designed independently, each optimized for its own objectives. Control systems concern with regulating the behavior of dynamical systems by adjusting inputs based on feedback, ensuring stability and meeting performance criteria. Communication systems, on the other hand, are designed to reliably transfer information between distinct points over noisy channels, typically aiming at maximizing data rates. Motivated by the decentralized nature of modern systems, we investigate the possible advantages in jointly designing control and communication task on a shared physical system. One way to view this setup is implicit communication where the controller transmits a message to an observer by choosing its actions in the form of a control signal
[
1
]
. For example, in a swarm of robots/drones, a leader’s trajectory may act as a control variable to be regulated, but also as an implicit communication signal: followers infer targets by observing the leader’s state through sensors.

This operation describes closed-loop systems since the system inputs, chosen by the controller, are causally dependent on the system outputs. The fundamental limits are defined as the maximal achievable message rate subject to a constraint on the closed-loop control cost. We study a fundamental instance of the CC problem: linear Gaussian dynamical systems with a measurement feedback.

I-A

The setup

The linear quadratic Gaussian (LQG) dynamical system is given by the state-space model

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=F\mathbf{s}_{i}+G\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{w}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}

\displaystyle=H\mathbf{s}_{i}+J\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{v}_{i},

(1)

where
\mathbf{s}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is the state of the system,
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the input to the system, and
\mathbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is the output of the system, often referred to as the measurement. The random process
(\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})_{i}

\mathbf{w}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,W)

\mathbf{v}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,V)

\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{T}]=L

\mathbf{s}_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{1})

(\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})_{i}
. The matrices

,

For a fixed horizon (blocklength)

\mathbf{M}\sim U([1:2^{nR}])

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{s}_{n+1}^{T}Q\mathbf{s}_{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{T}Q\mathbf{s}_{i}+\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}R\mathbf{x}_{i})\right],

(2)

Note that the LQR cost is a function of the controller-encoder operation but not a function of the observer-decoder mapping

In the CC Gaussian problem, the

I-A
) by ensuring that the LQR control cost is below a given control budget. Formally, a policy is

Remark 1
.

The linear dynamical system in (

I-C

Contribution

We derive an upper bound on the LQG system capacity as a finite-dimensional convex optimization. The upper bound recovers all special cases solved in the literature including feedback capacity of Gaussian channels with memory, LQG control and the LQG system capacity when there is state-feedback. This is the first computable converse bound on the LQG system capacity and it unifies the above special cases. The upper bound is accompanied with a sufficient condition for its tightness, expressed as a Riccati equation on the optimal decision variables.

For scalar dynamical systems, we establish the tightness of the upper bound. That is, their LQG system capacity is given by the convex optimization upper bound. This is established by showing that the optimal decision variables satisfy the sufficient condition via a through study of the KKT conditions for the optimization of the upper bound.

For vector dynamical systems, it is observed that the sufficient condition is approximately satisfied in all tested examples. Motivated by this observation, we extract policies from the upper bound and evaluate their performance in terms of control cost and communication rates. The extracted policies yield lower bounds on the LQG system capacity that appear to be tight in all tested examples.

II-B

Reduction via Kalman Filter

The state-space model in (
I-A
) has a measurement-feedback and, therefore, the controller-encoder does not have access to the actual state
\mathbf{s}_{i}
. In this section, we reduce the general CC Gaussian problem to that of a state-space model where the state is available to the controller-encoder. The new state is the MMSE estimate of the state, and the reduction is standard and is based on the Kalman filter (KF).

Consider the MMSE of the state given the controller-encoder information

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{s}_{i}|\mathbf{x}^{i-1},\mathbf{y}^{i-1}].

(4)

By KF, the estimate can be recursively expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=F\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}+G\mathbf{x}_{i}+K_{p,i}(\mathbf{y}_{i}-J\mathbf{x}_{i}-H\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})

(5)

with
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{1}=0
. The matrix
K_{p,i}=(F\Sigma_{i}H^{T}+L)\Psi_{i}^{-1}
is the
Kalman gain
and
\Psi_{i}=H\Sigma_{i}H^{T}+V
is the innovation variance. Both terms are a function of the estimation error covariance,
\Sigma_{i}\triangleq\mathbf{cov}(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})
, which can be computed by the Riccati recursion

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i+1}=F\Sigma_{i}F^{T}+W-K_{p,i}\Psi_{i}K_{p,i}^{T},

(6)

initialized at
\Sigma_{1}
. The recursion (
6
) is a Riccati recursion, and we assume regularity conditions to guarantee convergence of the recursion to the stabilizing solution of the Riccati equation

\displaystyle\Sigma=F\Sigma F^{T}+W-K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}

(7)

where
K_{p}=(F\Sigma H^{T}+L)\Psi^{-1}
and
\Psi=H\Sigma H^{T}+V
. The regularity conditions and the stabilizing solution to the Riccati equation are standard in Control and are presented for completeness in Appendix
A-A
. The convergence rate of the Riccati recursion to the stabilizing solution is geometric
[
40
]
. Therefore, from this point onward, we utilize the steady-state parameters
K_{p}
and
\Psi
, evaluated at the stabilizing solution
\Sigma
, and omit their time indices.

We can write a new state-space model that represents the system dynamics

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=F\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}+G\mathbf{x}_{i}+K_{p}\mathbf{e}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}

\displaystyle=H\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}+J\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{i},

(8)

where the main advantage is that the new state
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
is available to the controller-encoder. The random noise inserted into this state-space model is

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{y}_{i}-J\mathbf{x}_{i}-H\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i},

(9)

known as the innovation process in KF, which is an i.i.d. random process and its distribution is given by
\mathbf{e}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Psi)
.

The LQR cost in (
2
) can also be expressed with respect to the new state

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left[\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{n+1}^{T}Q\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{n+1}]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{T}Q\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}]+\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}R\mathbf{x}_{i}])\right]+\frac{n+1}{n}\Tr(\Sigma Q),

(10)

where we used the orthogonality principle in MMSE estimation as
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{s}_{i}^{T}Q\mathbf{s}_{i}]=\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})^{T}Q(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})]+\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{T}Q\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}]
and
\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})^{T}Q(\mathbf{s}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i})]=\Tr(\Sigma Q)
.

II-C

LQG optimal control

The LQG control problem is a special case of the CC Gaussian problem considered here. The objective in LQG control is to minimize the control cost (
2
) subject to the state-space model in (
I-A
). Optimal control theory provides with the optimal policy and the minimal control cost. The optimal policy is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\mathtt{LQG}}_{i}=-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i},

(11)

where
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
is defined in (
5
) and
K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}
is a matrix that depends on the system parameters.

A key distinction between LQG control and our setting is the information available to the controller and the observer. In LQG control, the optimal control signal (
11
) is a deterministic function of the measurements; therefore, an observer who knows the measurements can reconstruct the control signal. In our setup, the control signal is also used for communication and thus depends on a message that is only known to the controller. This induces a degraded information structure: both the controller and the observer have access to the measurements, but only the controller can reconstruct the optimal control signal.

The controller gain
K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}
is computed via the backward Riccati recursion

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=F^{T}E_{i+1}F+Q-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}(R+G^{T}E_{i+1}G)K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}\ \ i=1,\dots,n

\displaystyle K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}

\displaystyle=(R+G^{T}E_{i+1}G)^{-1}G^{T}E_{i+1}F

(12)

with the terminal condition
E_{n+1}=Q
.
Under standard regularity conditions, detailed in the Appendix
A-B
, the recursion (
II-C
) converges to the stabilizing solution of the Riccati equation

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=F^{T}EF+Q-K_{\mathtt{LQR}}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}}K_{\mathtt{LQR}}

\displaystyle K_{\mathtt{LQR}}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}}^{-1}G^{T}EF

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}}

\displaystyle=R+G^{T}EG.

(13)

The minimal LQR cost (
2
) for the LQG problem in the infinite-horizon regime is
\mathcal{J}^{*}\triangleq\lim_{n\to\infty}\min_{\pi_{n}}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\pi_{n})=\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{P}^{T}E)+\Tr(\Sigma Q)
. Clearly, this value is a lower bound on the control budget required to achieve positive capacity in our problem.

III
Main Results

In this section we present our main results. We first present a general upper bound on the capacity of LQG control systems, and then provide a sufficient condition for its tightness. Using the upper bound and the sufficient condition, we establish a capacity in the scalar case. Lastly, we show that all special cases solved in the literature coincide with our upper bound.

Theorem 1

(Upper bound on the capacity of LQG systems)
.

The capacity of the LQG system is upper bounded by the convex optimization

\displaystyle C_{\mathtt{LQG}}(p)\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\Pi\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m},\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k},\hat{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}}\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\det(\Psi_{Y})}{\det(\Psi)}\right)

(14)

\displaystyle s.t.\quad

\displaystyle\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}K_{\mathtt{LQR}}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}}K_{\mathtt{LQR}})+\Tr(\Pi\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}})+2\Tr(\Gamma K_{\mathtt{LQR}}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}})+\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E)+\Tr(\Sigma Q)\leq p

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Pi&\Gamma\\
\Gamma^{T}&\hat{\Sigma}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}F\hat{\Sigma}F^{T}+F\Gamma G^{T}+G\Gamma F^{T}+G\Pi G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-\hat{\Sigma}&K_{Y}\Psi_{Y}\\
\Psi_{Y}K_{Y}^{T}&\Psi_{Y}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0

\displaystyle\Psi_{Y}=J\Pi J^{T}+H\hat{\Sigma}H^{T}+H\Gamma^{T}J^{T}+J\Gamma H^{T}+\Psi

\displaystyle K_{Y}=(F\Gamma J^{T}+F\hat{\Sigma}H^{T}+G\Pi J^{T}+G\Gamma H^{T}+K_{p}\Psi)\Psi_{Y}^{-1},

(15)

where
(K_{\mathtt{LQR}},\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}},E)
are the LQR constants in (
II-C
), and
(K_{p},\Psi,\Sigma)
are the KF constants in (
7
).

The upper bound is expressed as a convex optimization, and thus can be computed efficiently using standard software. The directed information in (
3
) is translated to the objective function
\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\det(\Psi_{Y})}{\det(\Psi)}\right)
, and the trace constraint corresponds to the LQR control cost, bounded by the control budget

\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E)+\Tr(\Sigma Q)

III-A

The optimal policy and LMIs

It is shown below (Lemma
1

3

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+\Gamma_{i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})+\mathbf{m}_{i},

(16)

\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
is defined in (

\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}\triangleq\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}|\mathbf{y}^{i-1}]

is an i.i.d. Gaussian process. A similar policy was derived in

Few remarks on the policy are in order:

1.

The first term
-K_{\mathtt{LQR}}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}

The remaining terms
\Gamma\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})+\mathbf{m}_{i}

includes

. Lastly, note that if state-feedback is available, we have
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}=0
so that only
\mathbf{m}_{i}
carries the information, see
[
8
,
42
]
.

Using the optimal policy, the LMIs in (
14
) can be interpreted; The matrix
\Pi
serves as an upper bound in the PSD order for the covariance of the information terms, i.e.,
\mathbf{cov}(\Gamma_{i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})+\mathbf{m}_{i})=\Gamma\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}\Gamma^{T}+M
.
By the Schur complement, the first LMI implies that
\Pi\succeq\Gamma\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}\Gamma^{T}
once the decision variable
M\succeq 0
is eliminated. The Schur complement of the second LMI in (
14
) can be written as

\displaystyle F\hat{\Sigma}F^{T}+F\Gamma G^{T}+G\Gamma F^{T}+G\Pi G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-\hat{\Sigma}-K_{Y}\Psi_{Y}K_{Y}^{T}.

(17)

The LMI positivity implies the Schur (
17
) is PSD, and it corresponds to a relaxation of the Riccati equation to the Riccati inequality

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}\preceq F\hat{\Sigma}F^{T}+F\Gamma G^{T}+G\Gamma F^{T}+G\Pi G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-K_{Y}\Psi_{Y}K_{Y}^{T}.

(18)

The relaxation of the Riccati equation to a Riccati inequality is required for the formulation of the upper bound as convex optimization. Indeed, showing that this relaxation is achieved with equality under the optimization in Theorem
1
is the remaining gap for establishing the capacity of LQG systems as the convex optimization in (
14
). This is formalized in the next theorem as a sufficient condition for the tightness of the upper bound.

Theorem 2

(Sufficient condition for the tightness of the upper bound)
.

The LQG system capacity is equal to the upper bound in (
14
) if the optimal variables,
(\Pi^{*},\Gamma^{*},\hat{\Sigma}^{*})
, satisfy the Riccati equation

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}^{*}

\displaystyle=F\hat{\Sigma}^{*}F^{T}+F\Gamma^{*T}G^{T}+G\Gamma^{*}F^{T}+G\Pi^{*}G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-K_{Y}^{*}\Psi_{Y}^{*}K_{Y}^{*T},

(19)

where
K_{Y}^{*},\Psi_{Y}^{*}
are evaluated at the optimal variables, and the Kalman filter regularity conditions are met (see Appendix
A-C
): (i) The pair
(F+G\Gamma^{*}\hat{\Sigma}^{*\dagger},H+J\Gamma^{*}\hat{\Sigma}^{*\dagger})
is detectable (ii) The pair
(F^{s},G^{s}W^{s})
is stabilizable, where

\displaystyle F^{s}

\displaystyle=F+G\Gamma^{*}\hat{\Sigma}^{*\dagger}-(GM^{*}J^{T}+K_{p}\Psi)(JM^{*}J^{T}+\Psi)^{-1}(H+J\Gamma^{*}\hat{\Sigma}^{*\dagger})^{T}

(20)

\displaystyle W^{s}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}M^{*}&0\\
0&\Psi\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}M^{*}J^{T}\\
\Psi\end{pmatrix}(JM^{*}J^{T}+\Psi)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}JM^{*}&\Psi\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle G^{s}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}G&K_{p}\end{pmatrix}

with
M^{*}\triangleq\Pi^{*}-\Gamma^{*}\hat{\Sigma}^{*\dagger}\Gamma^{*T}
.

The main idea in proving Theorem
2
is to use a general lower bound on the LQG capacity for time-invariant policies parameterized by
(\Gamma,M)
and induces
\hat{\Sigma}_{LB}
. We extract a specific pair
(\Gamma,M)
from the upper-bound optimization, and using the sufficient Riccati condition, show that the policy-induced-covariance
\hat{\Sigma}_{LB}
is equal to the upper-bound solution
\hat{\Sigma}^{*}
.

In Section
IV
, we demonstrate via numerical simulations the upper bound tightness in the simulated dynamical systems. Moreover, the upper bound yields policy parameters
\Gamma,M
that numerically achieve a lower bound indistinguishable from the upper bound. While we could not prove tightness for the general case, we were able to prove it for the case of scalar dynamical systems, i.e., all variables are scalars.

III-B

The capacity of scalar dynamical systems

This is our main result regarding scalar dynamical systems.

Theorem 3

(The capacity of scalar LQG systems)
.

Assume that
H\neq K_{\mathtt{LQR}}J
,
G\neq K_{p}J
, and that the capacity is positive. Then, the capacity of scalar LQG systems is equal to

\displaystyle C_{\mathtt{LQG}}(p)

\displaystyle=\max_{\Pi,\Gamma,\hat{\Sigma}}\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\Psi_{Y}}{\Psi}\right)

\displaystyle s.t.\quad(\hat{\Sigma}K_{\mathtt{LQR}}^{2}+\Pi+2\Gamma K_{\mathtt{LQR}})\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}}+\Tr(K_{p}^{2}\Psi E)+\Tr(\Sigma Q)\leq p

\displaystyle\Psi_{Y}=J^{2}\Pi+H^{2}\hat{\Sigma}+2H\Gamma J+\Psi

\displaystyle K_{Y}=(F\Gamma J+F\hat{\Sigma}H+G\Pi J^{T}+G\Gamma H+K_{p}\Psi)\Psi_{Y}^{-1}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Pi&\Gamma\\
\Gamma^{T}&\hat{\Sigma}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}F^{2}\hat{\Sigma}+2F\Gamma G+G^{2}\Pi+K^{2}_{p}\Psi-\hat{\Sigma}&K_{Y}\Psi_{Y}\\
\Psi_{Y}K_{Y}^{T}&\Psi_{Y}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0.

(21)

Remark 2
.

The assumption
G\neq K_{p}J
in Theorem
3
implies that the dynamical system has a measurement feedback, and not a state-feedback. This is implied by the state evolution of the controller’s estimate
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
in (
5
)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i+1}=F\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}+G\mathbf{x}_{i}+K_{p}(\mathbf{y}_{i}-J\mathbf{x}_{i}-H\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}).

If
G=K_{p}J
, the estimate update is only a function of
\mathbf{y}_{i}
, and thus can be reproduced at the observer. This means that the estimation error covariance is zero,
\hat{\Sigma}=0
, and the solution for the CC problem can be found via the solution to the state-feedback scenario in
[
8
]
.

The main idea to prove Theorem
3
is to verify the sufficient condition in Theorem
2
. The main challenge follows from the role of
\hat{\Sigma}
in the optimization of (
14
). The objective function
\Psi_{Y}
is increasing with
\hat{\Sigma}
, so we aim to find the maximal
\hat{\Sigma}
subject to the optimization constraints. However, both the Riccati LMI constraint (via (
18
)) and the cost constraint provide with upper bounds on
\hat{\Sigma}
or the trace of it. To show the sufficient condition, one need to establish that the upper bound on
\hat{\Sigma}
implied by the Riccati inequality is achieved with equality, and in other words, this constraint is active at the optimum. This is shown by analyzing the KKT conditions for the optimization of the upper bound.

III-C

The upper bound in known special cases

Following the above discussion on the role of the constraints in (
14
) on the decision variable
\hat{\Sigma}
, we can show that the upper bound is tight for all special cases solved in the literature.

1.

Feedback capacity of Gaussian channels:
In this special case, the control cost is specialized to a power constraint on the system inputs by setting
Q=0
. This implies
E=0
,
K_{\mathtt{LQR}}=0
, and
\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}}=R
. The control cost constraint is then
\Tr(\Pi R)\leq p
, and the upper bound recovers the feedback capacity derived in
[
5
]
.

2.

The CC problem with state-feedback:
Here, the state is directly available to the observer-decoder, so
L=V=W
and
\Sigma=\hat{\Sigma}=0
. By the first LMI constraint, we have
\Gamma=0
, and the objective function simplifies to
\Psi_{Y}=J\Pi J^{T}+\Psi
. The control cost is given by
\Tr(M\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR}})+\Tr(WE)\leq p
, all align with the optimization for the capacity of state-feedback systems in
[
8
]
.

3.

LQG control:
In this case, no information is transmitted, and the policy is
\mathbf{x}_{i}=-K_{\mathtt{LQR}}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
with
\Gamma=0
and
M=0
. Consequently,
\Pi=0
, and since the input is deterministic with respect to the measurements, we have
\hat{\Sigma}=0
and
\Psi_{Y}=\Psi
. The control constraint simplifies to the minimal LQG cost
\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E)+\Tr(\Sigma Q)\leq p
, as expected.

V
Derivation of the Main results

In this section, we first present technical lemmas that are necessary to present the proof of the main results. In Section

Our starting point is the
n
-letter optimization problem in (
3

n
-letter joint distribution of the underlying objective:

\displaystyle P(\mathbf{s}_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}P(\mathbf{s}_{i+1},\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})P(\mathbf{x}_{i}|\mathbf{x}^{i-1},\mathbf{y}^{i-1}).

(26)

Lemma 1
.

An optimal policy for the
n
-letter optimization problem in (
3
), it is sufficient to optimize over inputs of the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{i}=\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})+\Gamma_{2,i}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+\mathbf{m}_{i},\ \ i=1,\dots,n,

(27)

where
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
is defined in (
5
),
\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
is the observer estimate of the state defined in (
II-B
),
\Gamma_{1,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
,
\Gamma_{2,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
,
\hat{\Sigma}_{i}\triangleq\mathbf{cov}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})
and
\mathbf{m}_{i}\sim N(0,M_{i})
is independent of
(\mathbf{x}^{i-1},\mathbf{y}^{i-1})
. The optimization variables are the collection of matrices
\{\Gamma_{1,i},\Gamma_{2,i},M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, and are subject to the orthogonality constraint

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1,i}(I-\hat{\Sigma}_{i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ i=1,\dots,n,

(28)

and the control cost constraint in (
10
).

Lemma
1
simplifies the optimization in (
3
) by showing that an optimal policy is linear, Gaussian, and has a simple dependence on past occurrences, i.e., the system input
\mathbf{x}_{i}
only depends on
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
and
\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
while in principle it can depend on
(\mathbf{x}^{i-1},\mathbf{y}^{i-1})
. These, as a whole, imply that the optimization domain is the matrices’ sequence
\{\Gamma_{1,i},\Gamma_{2,i},M_{i}\succeq 0\}_{i=1}^{n}
subject to the orthogonality and the control constraints.

The existence of an optimal Gaussian policy for the control-coding problem is not direct given the non-aligned objectives of communication and control. However, the underlying principle is that in both problems, second-order moments play a significant role. In Information theory, for a fixed-variance, the entropy is maximized at Gaussian distributions, and similarly in control the LQR objective involves second-order moments and thus linear Gaussian policies are also optimal.
We remark that a similar policy was derived in
[
41
]
with the exception that we introduce the orthogonality constraint in (
28
), required for the convex optimization formulation, and thus we use a constructive proof.

A consequence of Lemma
1
is that the LQG system in (
II-B
) can be written as the state-space model

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=(F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})+(F+G\Gamma_{2,i})\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+G\mathbf{m}_{i}+K_{p}\mathbf{e}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}

\displaystyle=(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})+(H+J\Gamma_{2,i})\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+J\mathbf{m}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{i}.

(29)

We now utilize (
V
) to express the optimal estimator
\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}=\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}|\mathbf{y}^{i-1}]
as a simple linear and recursive function of the measurements (channel outputs). While the state-space resembles a Kalman filtering model, the standard Kalman filtering equations cannot be applied directly. In particular, note that the latent state in the first equation of (
V
) is
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i+1}
and evolves as a function of the previous state
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
but also as a function of the estimate
\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
. The dependence on
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
is standard, but the term
\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
is a strictly-causal function of the measurements (channel outputs)
\mathbf{y}^{i-1}
. As we will see in the next lemma, optimal estimate has the standard structure of the Kalman-filter, but the Kalman gain and the constants are different. The estimator is linear and is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}|\mathbf{y}^{i-1}]+K_{Y,i}(\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{y}^{i-1}])

\displaystyle=(F+G\Gamma_{2,i})\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+K_{Y,i}\psi_{i}

where the innovation is
\psi_{i}\triangleq\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{y}^{i-1}]=\mathbf{y}_{i}-(H+J\Gamma_{2,i})\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
, and

is the Kalman gain. By (

\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
depends on the terms that do not appear in the standard KF. However, the covariance of the estimation error
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
is not effected by the state-space modifications. We present these results formally.

Lemma 2

(Directed information)
.

For a fixed policy of the form as in (

\displaystyle I(\mathbf{x}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{y}^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\det\Psi_{Y,i}}{\det\Psi}\right)

(31)

where
\Psi_{Y,i}
is the covariance of the innovation
\psi_{i}

\displaystyle\Psi_{Y,i}=(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})^{T}+JM_{i}J^{T}+\Psi.

(32)

For the state-space in (
V
), the optimal Kalman gain in (
V

\displaystyle K_{Y,i}

\displaystyle=((F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})^{T}+GM_{i}J^{T}+K_{p}\Psi)\Psi_{Y,i}^{-1}

(33)

and its corresponding error covariance

can be computed recursively as

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=(F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})^{T}+GM_{i}G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}

By (

does not depend on

, and thus the objective function (

\Gamma_{2,i}
too. This observation is noteworthy, as also noted in

is not to maximize the objective, but to minimize the control cost. In the following lemma, we demonstrate that minimizing the control cost over the set

is closely related to the optimal LQR control policy.

Lemma 3

(Optimal LQR cost)
.

The LQR cost in (
2

\{\Gamma_{2,i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathbf{cov}(\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{1})E_{1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}E_{i+1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})R)\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left[\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}Q)\right],

(35)

where

are computed from the LQR control Riccati recursion in (

). Moreover, the minimizer at time

is
\Gamma_{2,i}=-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}
, defined as

.

Combining Lemma
3
with Lemma
1
shows that an optimal policy for the control-coding problem is

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{i}-\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i})-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+\mathbf{m}_{i},\ \ i=1,\dots,n,

(36)

where
\Gamma_{1,i}
and

are the decision variables to be optimized. As
\Gamma_{2,i}
no longer appears, we use

as a shorthand for
\Gamma_{1,i}
. This is a significant simplification, but it is still given as a multi-letter expression. Our next steps are to convert the limiting multi-letter to a single-letter (finite-dimensional) optimization problem through convexification of an upper bound to the
n
-letter problem. In particular, both the objective and the control cost constraint should be written as an affine mappings of the decision variables, but at the cost of relaxing a Riccati recursion (given by a set of equalities) to Riccati inequalities. The first step is to bound

by a sequential convex optimization problem (SCOP).

Lemma 4
.

The

\displaystyle C_{n}(p)\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\{\Pi_{i},\Gamma_{i},\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n}}\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\det(\Psi_{Y,i})-\log\det(\Psi)

(37)

\displaystyle s.t.\ \

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(\Pi_{i}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E_{i+1})\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +2\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(\Gamma_{i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\mathcal{E}_{n}\leq p

\displaystyle\Psi_{Y,i}=J\Pi_{i}J^{T}+H\hat{\Sigma}_{i}H^{T}+H\Gamma_{i}^{T}J^{T}+J\Gamma_{i}H^{T}+\Psi

\displaystyle K_{Y,i}=(F\Gamma_{i}J^{T}+F\hat{\Sigma}_{i}H^{T}+G\Pi_{i}J^{T}+G\Gamma_{i}H^{T}+K_{p}\Psi)\Psi_{Y,i}^{-1}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{i}&\Gamma_{i}\\

\overline{\Psi}_{Y}=(H+J\overline{\Gamma})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}+J^{2}M+\Psi
.
Assume by contradiction
F+G\overline{\Gamma}\geq 1,H+J\overline{\Gamma}=0
then the capacity as in (
31
) is zero unless
M>0
. But the equation in (
52
) using
H+J\overline{\Gamma}=0
can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{(F+G\overline{\Gamma})^{2}-1}{J^{2}M+\Psi}\hat{\Sigma}+\frac{(G-K_{p}J)^{2}M\Psi}{J^{2}M+\Psi}=0.

Both terms are non-negative, so each of them must be zero. However, the second term is positive since
M>0
and our assumption that
G\neq K_{p}J
leading to a contradiction.

Convergence of the Riccati recursion:
Having established the detectability of the Riccati recursion parameters, there exists a maximal solution to the Riccati equation. We now need to ensure that the Riccati recursion of the lower bound as in (
43
), for the scalar case, converges to
\hat{\Sigma}^{*}
from the upper bound when the initial condition is
\hat{\Sigma}_{1}=0
. We divide the proof to two cases: if
M>0
, we show that the pair
(F^{s},G^{s}W^{s})
is stabilizable and by Theorem
2
the upper is equal to the LQG system capacity. Indeed, in this case there is a unique solution to the Riccati equation and we utilize this fact to show that the limit from the lower bound coincides with solution
\hat{\Sigma}
from the upper bound. For the other case,
M=0
, stabilizability does not hold, and therefore, we study the Riccati recursion directly. We show that choosing
M_{1}>0
at the first time followed by the time-invariant policy from the upper bound (with
M=0
) guarantees that it converges to the maximal solution of the Riccati equation.

We first show that, for
M>0
,
(F^{s},G^{s}W^{s})
is stabilizable where

\displaystyle F^{s}=F+G\overline{\Gamma}-(GMJ+K_{p}\Psi)(JMJ+\Psi)^{-1}(H+J\overline{\Gamma})

\displaystyle W^{s}=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\
0&\Psi\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}MJ\\
\Psi\end{pmatrix}(JMJ+\Psi)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}JM&\Psi\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle G^{s}=\begin{pmatrix}G&K_{p}\end{pmatrix}.

Direct computation provides with

\displaystyle G^{s}W^{s}=\begin{pmatrix}G&K_{p}\end{pmatrix}\frac{M\Psi}{MJ^{2}+\Psi}\begin{pmatrix}1&-J\\
-J&J^{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{M\Psi(G-K_{p}J)}{MJ^{2}+\Psi}\begin{pmatrix}1&-J\end{pmatrix},

so by our assumption
G\neq JK_{p}
and the case study
M\neq 0
, the pair is stabilizable.

Before proving the other case
M=0
, we rewrite the Riccati equation in (
52
) as

\displaystyle(F^{s})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}-\hat{\Sigma}+(G^{s})^{2}W^{s}-\frac{(F^{s}\hat{\Sigma}(H+J\overline{\Gamma}))^{2}}{(H+J\overline{\Gamma})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}+G^{2}M+\Psi}=0.

When
M=0
, it has two solutions
\hat{\Sigma}=0
and
\hat{\Sigma}_{M}=\frac{\Psi((F^{s})^{2}-1)}{(H+J\overline{\Gamma})^{2}}
, and we need to show that the recursion converges to the maximal solution
\hat{\Sigma}_{M}
.

The Riccati recursion when
M=0
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1}=(F^{s})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}\left(1-\frac{\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(H+J\overline{\Gamma})^{2}}{(H+J\overline{\Gamma})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}+\Psi}\right)=\frac{(F^{s})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}\Psi}{(H+J\overline{\Gamma})^{2}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}+\Psi},

(53)

and one can show directly that it is increasing for
0<\hat{\Sigma}_{i}<\hat{\Sigma}_{M}
, and it is decreasing for
\hat{\Sigma}_{M}<\hat{\Sigma}_{i}
. The proof is completed by noting that choosing
M_{1}>0
implies

\hat{\Sigma}_{2}=(G^{s})^{2}W^{s}=\frac{M_{1}\Psi(G-K_{p}J)^{2}}{M_{1}J^{2}+\Psi}>0.

∎

\mathbf{u}_{i}\triangleq\Gamma_{2,i}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}
.

By (

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left[\mathbb{E}[\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{n+1}^{T}Q\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{n+1}]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}^{T}Q\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}]+\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{u}_{i}^{T}R\mathbf{u}_{i}])\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})R)\right]+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left[\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}Q)\right].

(60)

Note that the second and third sums do not depend on
\{\Gamma_{2,i}\}
. The first sum appears as a standard LQR cost along with the dynamical system equation in (
V
)

\displaystyle\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=F\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+G\Gamma_{2,i}\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}+K_{Y,i}\psi_{i}.

By the LQR algorithm the optimum of

\displaystyle\min_{\{\Gamma_{2,i}\}_{i=1}^{n}}\frac{1}{n}\left[\mathbb{E}[\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{n+1}^{T}Q\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{n+1}]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}^{T}Q\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{i}]+\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{u}_{i}^{T}R\mathbf{u}_{i}])\right]

(61)

is attained at

\Gamma_{2,i}=-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i},\ \ \ i=1,\dots,n

where
K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}=\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{-1}G^{T}E_{i+1}F
and
\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}=R+G^{T}E_{i+1}G

and
E_{i}
is defined recursively as

\displaystyle E_{i}=F^{T}E_{i+1}F+Q-K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}

(62)

with the terminal condition
E_{n+1}=Q
. The LQR algorithm also provides us with the optimal cost of the (
61
), and it is equal to
\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathbf{cov}(\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{1})E_{1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}E_{i+1})
.

To summarize, the control cost after optimizing
\{\Gamma_{2,i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathbf{cov}(\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{1})E_{1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}E_{i+1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})R)\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left[\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}Q)\right],

(63)

and it is a function of
\{\Gamma_{1,i},M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

∎

Proof of lemma

The control cost is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathbf{cov}(\hat{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}_{1})E_{1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}E_{i+1})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})R)\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left[\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}Q)\right].

(64)

Our objective is to write the control cost as a linear function of the decision variables. An evident challenge is the sum

since

is a rational function of the decision variables. We rewrite the sum using the Riccati recursion in (

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}E_{i+1})\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr((-\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1}+(F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})^{T}+GM_{i}G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T})E_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr((F\hat{\Sigma}_{i}F^{T}-\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1})E_{i+1})+\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})G^{T}E_{i+1}G)+\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E_{i+1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(-Q+K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}(R+G^{T}E_{i+1}G)K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}))+\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})G^{T}E_{i+1}G)\right.

\displaystyle\ \ +\left.\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E_{i+1})+2\Tr(G\Gamma_{1,i}F^{T}E_{i+1})\right]+\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{1}E_{1}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n+1}E_{n+1}),

(65)

where in

).

Substituting (

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr((M_{i}+\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}^{\dagger}_{i}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T})\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E_{i+1})\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(G\Gamma_{1,i}F^{T}E_{i+1})+\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\mathcal{E}_{n},

We proceed to formulate the optimization problem as a SCOP where all the constraints are represented by LMIs and traces of a linear combination of the decision variables.
Notice that even though the Trace in (

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}\triangleq\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T}+M_{i}

(67)

to rewrite the cost as a linear function of the variables

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\Tr(\hat{\Sigma}_{i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(\Pi_{i}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}E_{i+1})\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +2\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(\Gamma_{1,i}K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{T}\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i})+\Tr(\Sigma Q)+\mathcal{E}_{n},

where we also used the identity
K_{\mathtt{LQR},i}=\Psi_{\mathtt{LQR},i}^{-1}G^{T}E_{i+1}F
.

Similarly, the objective in (

) is expressed as the log-determinant of
\Psi_{Y,i}
, which is not a linear function of the decision variables. With (
67
), we can write
\Psi_{Y,i}
as

\displaystyle\Psi_{Y,i}

\displaystyle=(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})^{T}+JM_{i}J^{T}+\Psi

\displaystyle=H\hat{\Sigma}_{i}H^{T}+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}H^{T}+H\hat{\Sigma}_{i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T}J^{T}+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger}\Gamma_{1,i}^{T}J^{T}+JM_{i}J^{T}+\Psi

\displaystyle=J\Pi_{i}J^{T}+H\hat{\Sigma}_{i}H^{T}+H\Gamma_{1,i}^{T}J^{T}+J\Gamma_{i}H^{T}+\Psi,

where the last equality follows from the orthogonality constraint in (

\Psi_{Y,i}
is now a linear function of
\Pi_{i},\hat{\Sigma}_{i},\Gamma_{1,i}

In a similar way we can write

as a linear function of
\Pi_{i},\hat{\Sigma}_{i},\Gamma_{1,i}

\displaystyle K_{Y,i}

\displaystyle=((F+G\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})\hat{\Sigma}_{i}(H+J\Gamma_{1,i}\hat{\Sigma}_{i}^{\dagger})^{T}+GM_{i}J^{T}+K_{p}\Psi)\Psi_{Y,i}^{-1}

The relationship between
\Pi_{i}
and the other variables in (

Finally, the Riccati equation in (

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1}\preceq F\hat{\Sigma}_{i}F^{T}+F\Gamma_{1,i}G^{T}+G\Gamma_{1,i}F^{T}+G\Pi_{i}G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}.

Using the Schur complement transformation, we can write it as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}F\hat{\Sigma}_{i}F^{T}+F\Gamma_{1,i}G^{T}+G\Gamma_{1,i}F^{T}+G\Pi_{i}G^{T}+K_{p}\Psi K_{p}^{T}-\hat{\Sigma}_{i+1}&K_{Y,i}\Psi_{Y,i}\\
\Psi_{Y,i}K_{Y,i}^{T}&\Psi_{Y,i}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0.

∎

VII
Conclusion

We studied communication via LQG control systems and derived a finite-dimensional convex converse (upper bound) on capacity. The bound recovers classical limits and is exact for scalar plants; for vector plants we provided a Riccati-based sufficient condition, and numerical experiments indicate tightness across all tested instances. We also extracted policy parameters
(\Gamma,M)
from the upper bound to yield matching lower bounds (empirically). In the scalar case when
M=0
, there exists a concrete coding scheme that achieves the capacity. Naturally, we aim in future work to establish the tightness of the upper bound for vector systems, and to construct explicit coding scheme when
M\neq 0
.

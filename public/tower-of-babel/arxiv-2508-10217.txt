. A Ricci soliton is a natural generalization of an Einstein metric. 1.
Introduction

Ricci solitons were first investigated in Riemannian and Lorentzian signature.
They have also been studied recently in a more general context by several
authors. Batat et al.
[
1
]
show that an
n
-dimensional
Lorentzian manifold whose isometry group is of dimension at least

\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)+1
admit different vector fields resulting in expanding,
steady and shrinking Ricci solitons. Brozos-Vázquez et al.
[
2
]
,
analyze the existence of three-dimensional Lorentzian homogeneous Ricci
solitons, showing the existence of shrinking, expanding and steady Ricci
solitons in that setting. Also, Calvaruso and De Leo
[
4
]

give some examples of Ricci solitons within the family of three-dimensional
Walker manifolds. Pirhadi, Fasihi-Ramandi and Azami
[
7
]

have generalized the Ricci soliton equation on the three-dimensional
Lorentzian Walker manifolds.

A pseudo-Riemann manifold
(M,g)
that admits a totally isotropic distribution
field, parallel to the Levi-Civita connection, is a Walker manifold. In dimension
3
,
they form an important class of Lorentzian manifolds possessing a parallel isotropic vector field. Thanks to their explicit local form, they allow the study of delicate
questions in differential geometry or physical models in relativity.
Calvaruso
[
5
]
classified all homogeneous Lorentzian manifolds of dimension three. Calvaruso and Kowalski
[
6
]
identified
the possible forms of the Ricci operator for locally homogeneous Lorentzian
manifolds in dimension three.

Motivated, by the above works
[
2
,
4
]
, we study
Ricci solitons on Walker three-manifolds
(M,g)
. For several classes of these
manifolds, described in terms of a deﬁning function
f
, existence results
are obtained. We organize the paper as follows : in section
2
,
we shall describe the curvature of the metric considered. In section
3
,
we study and characterize Ricci solitons via a system of partial differential
equations involving the function
f
.

2.
Description of the metric

The geometry of Walker three-manifolds has been studied in
[
3
]
.
These manifolds are described in terms of a suitable system of local coordinates

(t,x,y)
and form a large class, depending on an arbitrary function

f=f(t,x,y)

a parallel null vector ﬁeld strictly Walker three-manifolds is characterized
by the fact that

We consider the Walker metric

\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle g_{f}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&1\\

of the Levi-Civita connection of the metric (

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{t}\partial_{t},\;\nabla_{\partial_{x}}\partial_{y}=\frac{1}{2}f_{x}\partial_{t},\;

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{y}}\partial_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(ff_{t}+f_{y}\right)\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}f_{x}\partial_{x}-\frac{1}{2}f_{t}\partial_{y}.

(2.5)

Using (
2

of the metric (

\mathcal{R}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k}=([\nabla_{\partial_{i}},\nabla_{\partial_{j}}]-\nabla_{[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]})\partial_{k}

We obtain that the non-zero components of the
(0,4)
-curvature tensor
of the metric (

\displaystyle R(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})\partial_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}\partial_{t},\;R(\partial_{t},\partial_{y})\partial_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}f_{tt}\partial_{t},

\displaystyle R(\partial_{t},\partial_{y})\partial_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}f_{tx}\partial_{t},\;R(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})\partial_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}f_{tx}\partial_{t},

\displaystyle R(\partial_{t},\partial_{y})\partial_{y}

By (

3.
Ricci solitons on a family of three-dimensional Walker manifolds

In this section, we investigate the conditions for the existence of Ricci solitons
on Walker three-manifolds
(M,g_{f})
where
g_{f}
is given by (
2
) .

A Ricci soliton is a natural generalization of an Einstein metric. It is defined
on a pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}g+\rho=\lambda\cdot g,

(3.1)

where
X
is a smooth vector field on
M
,
\mathcal{L}_{X}
denotes the
Lie derivative in the direction of
X
,
\rho
is the Ricci tensor and
\lambda

is a real number. The Ricci soliton is said to be shrinking, steady or expanding
according to whether
\lambda>0
,
\lambda=0
, or
\lambda<0
,
respectively. Moreover, we say that a Ricci soliton is a gradient Ricci soliton
if it admits a vector field
X
satisfying
X=\mathrm{grad}h
for some
potential function
h
.

The description of Ricci solitons can be regarded as a ﬁrst step in understanding
the Ricci flow, since they are the ﬁxed points of the ﬂow. Moreover, they
are important in understanding singularities of the Ricci ﬂow
[
2
]
.

Let
X=A\partial_{t}+B\partial_{x}+C\partial_{y}
be a vector field on
(M,g_{f})
,
where
A,B,C
are smooth functions of variables
t,x,y
. The non-zero
components of the Lie derivative of the Walker metric (
2
) in the direction
of
X
are :

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{t},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{t},\partial_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{x},\partial_{t})=\varepsilon B_{t}+C_{x},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{t},\partial_{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{y},\partial_{t})=A_{t}+C_{y}+fC_{t},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{x},\partial_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon B_{x},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{y},\partial_{x})=\varepsilon B_{y}+fC_{x}+A_{x},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{f})(\partial_{y},\partial_{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X(f)+2fC_{y}+2A_{y}.

(3.2)

.

Proof.

Let
f(t,x,y)=a(t,x)y^{2}+b(t,x)y+d(t,x)

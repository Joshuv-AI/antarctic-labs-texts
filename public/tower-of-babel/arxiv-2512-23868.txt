Towards 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} classical blocks with semi-degenerate operators
V. Belavin, Mikhail Pavlov

Abstract
We consider 4-point 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} classical blocks focusing on the blocks level-1 and level-2 semi-degenerate operators. We derive BPZ-type equations for the auxiliary 5-point blocks with one additional fully degenerate operator. The monodromy properties of these equations are encoded by the accessory parameters, related to the 4-point 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} classical blocks. We solve the BPZ-type equations via heavy-light perturbation theory and find the accessory parameters, which allows us to obtain the explicit expressions for the considered class of classical blocks.



1
Introduction

In CFT
2
, the main observables are the correlation functions of local operators. These
n
-point correlation functions can be decomposed into conformal blocks
[
1
]
(determined only by the symmetries of the theory). The
n
-point conformal blocks are basis functions in the space of correlation functions, while coefficients in the expansion are the
n
-point structure constants. These coefficients are constructed as a product of
(n-2)
the
3
-point structure constants. Hence, in the bootstrap program
[
1
]
the
3-
point structure constants and
n
-point conformal blocks are the fundamental objects in the CFTs.

In Virasoro CFT
2
, the correlation functions/conformal blocks involving degenerate operators are subjected by Belavin-Polyakov-Zamolodchikov (BPZ) differential equations following from the null-vector conditions
[
1
]
. For an
(n+1)
-point correlation function with one degenerate field, these equations are tools to investigate the above-mentioned fundamental objects. First type of objects is the
n=3
-point structure constants, which are determined in the following way: the
(3+1)=4
point correlation functions satisfy ordinary differential equations on conformal blocks, solving which (with particular monodromy conditions) and using the analytic properties of solutions, we can find the structure constants
[
2
,
3
]
. These structure constants are completely determine the
3
- point correlation functions.

The second type of objects is the (
n\geq 4
) conformal blocks. These blocks can be straightforwardly written as a series in cross-ratios but remains unknown in the closed form. In various contexts it is interesting to study the classical limit by which we mean that the large central charge (
c\rightarrow\infty
) and all conformal dimensions grow linearly in
c
. In the classical limit, the Virasoro blocks have the exponential form (i.e.
F(z)\sim\exp(c/6f(z))
, where the function
f(z)
is the classical conformal block), see
[
4
,
5
]
.
One way to find it is to consider the auxiliary
(n+1)
-point conformal block with the additional degenerate operator which satisfies the second-order BPZ equation. By analyzing the monodromy properties of the solutions of the BPZ equation, one can find the accessory parameters
[
6
]
which are express via the classical block. For example, in the case of
n=4
classical blocks the accessory parameters are constructed using the certain solutions of the Painlevé VI equation
[
7
]
. The
n>4
-point case becomes more complicated, but one can use the heavy-light (HL) approximation
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
to find the perturbative expansion for the classical blocks.

The above analysis can also be carried out in CFT with extended symmetries, in particular, the
\mathcal{W}_{N}
symmetry
[
13
,
14
]
. In this paper, we focus on the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
case. Compared with the Virasoro algebra, this case is more complicated due to the fact that the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
symmetry is not strong enough and OPE of the primary fields involves descendant operators (associated with
W_{3}
current) which can not be expressed in terms of differential operators acting on the primary fields. One can distinguish between non-degenerate, semi-degenerate and fully degenerate representations in
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT
[
13
,
15
,
16
]
(the precise definition will be given below).
The
4
-pt conformal blocks with one fully degenerate and one level-1 semi-degenerate operators is governed by a third-order differential equation (of BPZ-type)
[
17
]
. The equations for conformal blocks with higher level semi-degenerate operators are more complicated, e.g. for level-2 semi-degenerate operators one obtains 6-th order ODE
[
18
]
(see also
[
19
]
).

Regarding the monodromy method, it can also be generalized, but with the following difference. In addition to classifying fields according to the HL approximation, we must further distinguish semi-degenerate fields by their level
N
. This additional structure makes the overall classification more ramified.

The monodromy method for calculating
\mathcal{W}_{3}
blocks was considered in
[
20
]
. This consideration focuses on the blocks with non-degenerate primary operators and it implicitly implies that the blocks involving
\mathcal{W}_{3}
descendant operators are exponentiated in the classical limit. We note, that this assumption is not obvious, since
\mathcal{W}_{3}
descendants are not related to the primary fields by differential operators, as in the Virasoro case. Furthermore, the monodromy method in
[
20
]
was restricted to computing classical blocks with an identity intermediate operator. In this work, we consider a more general case.

In this article, we focus on classical 4-pt blocks with level-1 and level-2 semi-degenerate operators and a non-identity intermediate operator. We derive the BPZ-type equations for the auxiliary 5-pt blocks with additional one fully degenerate operator. Using the HL approximation, we describe the monodromy properties of the solutions and then derive the monodromy equations for the accessory parameters. Solving these equations explicitly yields the corresponding 4-pt classical blocks.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall main facts about the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT and introduce the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
conformal blocks. In Section
3
we present the BPZ-type equations for the auxiliary 5-pt blocks with level-1 and level-2 semi-degenerate operators and one fully degenerate operator. In Section
4
we derive and solve the monodromy equations within the HL approximation, which gives us the classical 4-pt blocks we are interested in. Concluding Section
5
summarizes our results and contains future developments.

3
BPZ-type equations

We consider the 4-pt classical blocks with the semi-degenerate operators. Within the monodromy method, these blocks can be extracted from the monodromy properties of auxiliary
5
-pt blocks (denoted by
\Psi(y,z_{i})
) with one additional fully degenerate operator
\mathcal{O}_{-b\vec{\omega}_{1}}(y)

The block
\Psi(y,z_{i})
satisfies the BPZ-type equation, which follows from the null-vector (
2.20

W_{-3}

W_{-3}\Psi(y,z_{i})=\sum^{4}_{i=1}\left(\frac{w_{i}}{(y-z_{i})^{3}}+\frac{\mathcal{W}^{(i)}_{-1}}{(y-z_{i})^{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{W}^{(i)}_{-2}}{(y-z_{i})}\right)\Psi(y,z_{i}).

(3.1)

The 8 functions
\mathcal{W}^{(i)}_{-1,-2}\Psi(y,z_{i}),~i=\overline{1,4}
are related with
\Psi(y,z_{i})
by 5 Ward identities (
2.35
). In the case of 4 non-degenerate operators the BPZ-type equation involves 3 independent functions.

The blocks with semi-degenerate operators are subject of additional constraints. We consider two distinct cases: 1) blocks with 3 level-1 operators and 2) blocks with 2 level-1 and 1 level-2 operators. The blocks with multiple level-2 operators represent a more complicated situation which will be analyzed elsewhere. For both of the considered cases it follows from (

-pt blocks have the same exponential behavior with a certain prefactor (the fully degenerate operator does not affect the leading behavior due to

(3.2)

Moreover, all conformal blocks
\mathcal{W}^{(i)}_{-1,-2}\Psi(y,z_{i}),~i=\overline{1,4}
have the same behavior in the classical limit

\mathcal{W}^{(i)}_{-1,-2}\Psi(y,z_{i})\Big|_{b\rightarrow 0}\rightarrow\frac{k^{(i)}_{1,2}(y,z_{i})}{b^{2}}\exp\left[\frac{4}{b^{2}}f(z_{i})\right],\qquad i=\overline{1,4},

where

\mathcal{W}^{(i)}_{-2}\Psi(y,z_{i})

4
The monodromy method and the HL perturbation theory

Solutions
\psi(x,z)
of the BPZ-type equations from sections
3.1
and
3.2
must have a specific monodromy, following from the properties of the fully degenerate representation (
2.20

\Gamma
that encloses
1

z

\psi_{a}(\Gamma\circ x,z)=\tilde{M}_{ab}~\psi_{b}(x,z)
, where the monodromy matrix in the classical limit has a form
[
22
]

\tilde{M}_{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
\\
0&e^{2\pi i\sqrt{1-4\epsilon_{p}}}&0\\
\\
0&0&e^{-2\pi i\sqrt{1-4\epsilon_{p}}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

is a classical dimension of the intermediate operator. Such a condition is sufficient to determine the accessory parameters
c(z)
and

in the considered equations.

The solutions of the equations can not be written explicitly, so we solve them using the heavy-light (HL) approximation: for a part of the operators’ (heavy) classical dimensions/spin-3 charges are larger than for other operators (light). We focus on the case of two equivalent heavy operators
\epsilon_{3}=\epsilon_{4}\equiv\epsilon_{H},~q_{3}=-q_{4}\equiv q_{H}

\tilde{x}_{i}
through (
2.23
). We have

\tilde{x}_{i}\rightarrow 0^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{x}_{i-1}\tilde{x}_{i+1}\rightarrow-1:\quad\epsilon\ll 1,\quad q\ll 1.

(4.2)

For the level-1 semi-degenerate operators (
2.25
) the HL approximation corresponds to

\epsilon\ll\epsilon_{H},\qquad q\ll q_{H}.

(4.3)

with the additional constraint
\epsilon=-3q
. For the level-2 semi-degenerate operators (
2.26
) the HL approximation is

\epsilon\ll\epsilon_{H},\qquad q\ll q_{H},

(4.4)

with the constraint
\epsilon\ll q
.

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we investigated 4-point classical blocks, focusing on two cases: 1) blocks with three level-1 semi-degenerate operators and 2) blocks with two level-1 and one level-2 semi-degenerate operators. Calculating such blocks in the classical limit requires constructing BPZ-type equations for auxiliary
5
-pt blocks with the fully degenerate operator. The equations involve additional blocks with
\mathcal{W}_{3}
descendants operators. In Section
3
, we explicitly argued that these blocks have the exponential form (
3.3
). This allowed us to rewrite the BPZ-type equations as differential equations: a third-order equation (
3.8
) for the first case, and a sixth-order equation for the second.

In Section
4
, we solved the BPZ-type equations using HL perturbation theory. For both cases, we considered two heavy level-1 semi-degenerate operators, treating the remaining operators as light. We solved the equations perturbatively (to first order). The monodromy properties of these solutions determine the accessory parameters of the blocks. By integrating these accessory parameters, we obtained explicit expressions for non-identity blocks, which complement the results in
[
20
]
.

The formula (
4.31
) for the classical block with level-1 semi-degenerate operators generalizes the results in
[
20
]
by implementing the case with non-identity intermediate operators. It would be interesting to consider this result in the context of AdS/CFT, going beyond the identity block case. It would also be interesting to derive the classical block (
4.33
) by applying first the classical and then the HL approximation to the corresponding quantum 4-point
\mathcal{W}_{3}
block with one level-2 semi-degenerate operator, studied in
[
18
]
and to analyze this case in the context of AdS/CFT.

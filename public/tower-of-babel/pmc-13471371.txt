Endovascular treatment versus standard medical management in primary M3 or M4 occlusion stroke in clinical practice: the oriental-MeVO registry
Authors: Yuyou Zhu, Chao Zhang, HuiJia Yang, Chunrong Tao, Rui Li, XiaoZhong Jing, Li Wang, Tianlong Liu, Jianlong Song, HaiYing Hu, AnMo Wang, JinJing Wang, Wei Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundClinical evidence of the potential treatment benefit of endovascular treatment (EVT) for M3 occlusion of the middle cerebral artery (MCA) is sparse. This study aims to compare EVT versus standard medical management (SMM) for acute M3 and M4 occlusion stroke.MethodsThis multicenter, retrospective study collected data on patients with acute ischemic stroke from an M3 or M4 occlusion from 25 comprehensive stroke centers between 09/2019 and 09/2024. The primary endpoint was the proportion of excellent outcome (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] 0–1) at 90 days. Safety measures included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) within 24 h and mortality at 90 days.ResultsA total of 342 patients with M3 or M4 occlusions were enrolled in the final analysis (EVT, 170; SMM, 172). In the primary analysis, there was no significant difference in EVT cohort versus SMM cohort regarding the rates of excellent outcome (mRS 0–1, 36.5% versus 37.2%, adjusted risk ratio [aRR], 0.88 [95% CI, 0.64–1.22]; P = 0.45), overall distribution of mRS score (adjusted common odds ratio [aOR], 0.94 [95% CI, 0.69–1.26]; P = 0.66), and functional independence (mRS 0–2, 53% versus 55.2%, aRR, 0.76 [95% CI, 0.55–1.05]; P = 0.10). There was no significant difference in safety measures between EVT and SMM cohorts in terms of sICH (aRR, 1.04 [95% CI, 0.63–1.72]; P = 0.85) and 90-day mortality (aRR, 0.76 [95% CI, 0.43–1.34]; P = 0.35). Successful revascularization was correlated with excellent outcome at 90 days (aRR, 2.79 [95% CI, 1.42–5.51]; P = 0.003) in the EVT cohort.ConclusionsEVT appears to be a safe and technically feasible treatment option for primary isolated M3 and M4 occlusions with clinical outcomes similar to SMM.Trial registrationURL: www.chictr.org.cn; Unique identifier: ChiCTR2500096954, February 10, 2025. Introduction

Endovascular treatment (EVT) has emerged as the cornerstone of treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) secondary to large vessel occlusion. [1–3]. EVT is increasingly being recognized as a treatment option for occlusions of the M2 segment of the middle cerebral artery (MCA), as well as the anterior cerebral artery (ACA) and posterior cerebral artery (PCA), based on accumulating clinical experience and emerging evidence. [4–10]. However, current knowledge regarding the safety and efficacy of EVT for M3 and M4 occlusions remains limited. The technical difficulty of EVT for primary M3 and M4 occlusion is compounded by the longer access route through small-diameter vessels, which inherently raises the risk of complications, including perforation, dissection, and vasospasm [10, 11]. Second, the smaller volume of neuronal tissue at risk in distal occlusions may result in a comparatively lesser degree of functional impairment than that observed in proximal large vessel occlusions. In addition, the potential added value of EVT following intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) in patients with primary M3 or M4 occlusion remains to be established. Nevertheless, primary M3 occlusion carries the risk of severe long-term disability, particularly when eloquent cortical regions are affected.

Experience with EVT for M3 occlusion remains limited, with the procedure being performed only in selected cases to date. Nevertheless, small case series have provided evidence supporting its technical feasibility [10, 12, 13]. Evidence from recent randomized trials does not support the superiority of EVT over standard medical management (SMM) for medium vessel occlusion (MeVO), and it should be noted that patients with M3 occlusions constituted only a small minority of the trial populations [14, 15]. To date, no large-scale case series of primary M3 and M4 occlusions have been published, and head-to-head comparisons between EVT and SMM are still unavailable. Hence, large observational studies represent an important resource for making sense of recent trial results and for developing well-informed future trial protocols.

In this study, we sought to harness real-world data from a multicenter registry to further examine the clinical and safety outcomes of EVT for primary M3 and M4 occlusion strokes, both in patients who received IVT and in those who did not.

Methods

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China (2024KY063). The requirement for informed consent obtained from patients was waived by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China owing to the retrospective nature of the study. The trial was performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki as amended [16]. Each of the 25 participating centers received ethical approval under their local protocols to share retrospective and fully anonymized data. The study is registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.org.cn; ChiCTR2500096954, February 10, 2025; Affiliation of the Registrant: Department of Neurology, The First Affiliated Hospital of University of Science and Technology of China).

Study design, procedure, and data collection

In this retrospective, multicenter, case-control analysis, we have recruited patients from 25 comprehensive stroke centers across 14 provinces in China between 09/2019 and 09/2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) diagnosis of AIS due to M3 or M4 occlusion; Primary M3 occlusion was defined as proof of occlusion of the MCA distal to the M2/M3 junction and proximal to the M4 junction within the (opercular) M3 segment on initial imaging or peri-interventional proof of M3 occlusion on angiography [17]. The M4 or cortical segment begins as vessels exit the Sylvian fissure and fan over the convex surface of the cerebral hemispheres [17]. (2) Pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS) ≤ 2; (3) Time from symptom onset to treatment < 24 h. Patients with secondary occlusions such as migrated proximal large vessel occlusion, or occlusion in multiple vascular territories, missing 90-day mRS scores were excluded. Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, blood pressure on admission, laboratory findings, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, medical history (diabetes, hyperlipidemia, ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, coronary heart disease), administration of IVT, and detailed therapeutic parameters were obtained from clinical records of the patients and imaging at admission and follow-up. The stroke causative mechanism was evaluated using the Trial of ORG 10,172 in Acute Stroke Treatment, or TOAST, classification [18]. For patients with sleep onset or unwitnessed onset stroke, time last seen well was recorded as the onset time.

Patients were selected to SMM or EVT after review of the patient’s treatment history and imaging features between the stroke physician and neurointerventionalist. Both groups received SMM based on national guidelines [19] consisting of IVT if eligible, vascular risk factor management, and antiplatelet or anticoagulant. For patients received EVT, treatment with stent retriever, aspiration catheters, or combined devices were used at the discretion of the neurointerventional physician.

Outcome measures

The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients with excellent outcome (mRS 0–1) at 90 (± 14) days. Secondary outcome included the ordinal shift in distribution of mRS scores and rates of functional independence (mRS 0–2) at 90 (± 14) days. Safety outcomes included rates of 90-day mortality and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) within 24 h. sICH refers to any intracranial hemorrhage on neuroimaging that results in a deterioration of neurological function with a NIHSS score of ≥ 4 [20].

Statistical analysis

The homogeneity of groups at baseline was assessed using descriptive statistical methods. Missing baseline continuous and categorical variables were imputed using multiple imputation [21, 22]. The standardized mean difference ([SMD] between EVT and SMM group was computed to assess the magnitude of inter group differences [23].

The primary analysis was performed using inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) to estimate the treatment effect on the primary outcome (excellent functional outcome, defined as mRS 0–1 at 90 days). Propensity score matching (PSM) was conducted as a secondary sensitivity analysis to assess the robustness of the findings. The IPTW results are therefore considered the primary effect estimates, with PSM and crude analyses serving a supportive role. Table S1 summarized the inverse probability of treatment weighting weight diagnostics. The description of IPTW weight diagnostics were summarized in Figure S1-S2. A graphical comparison was performed to assess the distributional similarity before and after PSM (Figure S3-S4). Before and after PSM, as well as in the IPWT cohort, a multivariable ordinal logistic regression analysis was used to assess the relationship of EVT with the primary endpoint of the 90-day mRS shift. The association of EVT with the secondary endpoints and safety measures was estimated by binary logistic regression analysis. The heterogeneity in the treatment effect size of EVT on efficacy and safety outcomes was also evaluated using weighted logistic regression. For binary outcomes (primary and secondary), we estimated adjusted risk ratios (aRR) using modified Poisson regression with robust standard errors. For the ordinal analysis of the full mRS distribution, we reported an adjusted common odds ratio (aOR) from a proportional odds model.

M3 and M4 occlusions were pooled for the primary analysis based on clinical and anatomical considerations. Both segments are distal to the Sylvian fissure, with M4 representing the most distal cortical branches. From an endovascular perspective, both are classified as ‘distal medium vessel occlusions’ (MeVO) and share similar challenges regarding catheter accessibility, vessel fragility, and the risk of perforation. Furthermore, the sample size of isolated M4 occlusions (n = 45, 15.2%) was insufficient to support a well-powered subgroup analysis. Pooling M3 and M4 thus allowed for a more robust overall estimate of treatment effect while acknowledging their clinical continuum.

All statistical tests were 2-sided, with a P-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 26 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). and R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Detailed information about statistical analyses was summarized in the supplemental methods.

Results

A total of 429 AIS patients with an occlusion of M3 and M4 segments of MCA were enrolled from 25 comprehensive stroke centers across China between 09/2019 and 09/2024. 87 patients were excluded from the study primarily because they were admitted after 24 h from the symptom onset, with migrated proximal large vessel occlusion or occlusion in multiple vascular territories, or missing 90-day mRS scores. Our final analysis included a total of 342 patients.

Patient characteristics

The median age of patients was 70 years (IQR, 60–78), with a median baseline NIHSS score of 9 (interquartile range [IQR], 5–11). Time from last known well to admission was 376 min (IQR, 240–576). The levels of occlusion sites were M3 (84.8%), M4(15.2%). The most frequently observed risk factors for cerebrovascular disease were hypertension (60.8%; 208 of 342). The most common stroke etiology was large artery atherosclerosis (44.7%; 153 of 342). In the EVT group, a total of 146 (85.9%) subjects achieved a successful reperfusion (modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction [mTICI]≥2b). The most commonly used first-line EVT was a primary combined approach (60, 35.3%), whereas stent-retriever only and aspiration only were each used in (55, 32.4%) and (55, 32.4%) of the participants.

Several differences (SMD > 20%) were found before PSM. The EVT group (n = 170) had a lower median time from symptom onset to treatment (347 versus 411), a lower proportion of diabetes (15.3 versus 23.3) as well as a higher proportion of IVT (28.8% versus 18.0%) than the SMM group (n = 172). A significant difference in stroke causative mechanism (SMD = 36.2) and diastolic pressure (SMD = 21.0) between groups was also observed. There was no significant difference in other baseline characteristics between groups. These differences between EVT and SMM groups were controlled after PSM, with SMD < 20% for all baseline characteristics. The matched patient population was comprised of 240 patients, 120 EVT and 120 SMM. Effectiveness outcomes based on treatment arms

Adjusted association of effectiveness and safety outcomes with endovascular treatment are summarized in Fig. 2. The distribution of mRS scores at 90-day based on treatment arms before and after PSM is shown in Fig. 3. In the primary analysis using IPTW model, there was no significant improvement in EVT group versus SMM group regarding the overall distribution of mRS score (adjusted common odds ratio [aOR], 0.94 [95% CI, 0.69–1.26]; P = 0.66), rates of excellent outcome (mRS 0–1, adjusted risk ratio [aRR], 0.88 [95% CI, 0.64–1.22]; P = 0.45), and functional independence (mRS 0–2, aRR, 0.76 [95% CI, 0.55–1.05]; P = 0.10). In the secondary analysis using the PSM model, there was no significant improvement in the shift in distribution of the mRS scores at 90 days with an of aOR of 1.08 (95% CI, 0.64–1.80, P = 0.78) between the 2 groups. In the PSM cohort, there was no significant difference in the proportion of patients achieving mRS scores of 0–1 and 0–2 at 90 days between the EVT and SMM groups, with an aRR of 1.24 (95% CI, 0.71–2.18, P = 0.45) and 0.81 (95% CI, 0.46–1.42, P = 0.47), respectively.

Safety outcomes

In the analysis based on the IPTW cohort, there was no significant difference in the rates of all-causes 90-day mortality (aRR, 0.76 [95% CI, 0.43–1.34]; P = 0.35) between the EVT and SMM group. Although the EVT group tended to have a higher rate of sICH than the SMM group, there was no statistically significant difference (aRR, 1.04 [95% CI, 0.63–1.72]; P = 0.87). In the PSM-based analysis, the rates of all-causes 90-day mortality (aRR, 0.74 [95% CI, 0.26–2.08]; P = 0.57) and sICH (aRR, 0.89 [95% CI, 0.36–2.18]; P = 0.79) also showed no significant difference between the EVT and SMM group.

Multivariable analysis in the endovascular treatment cohort

At multivariable logistic regression adjustment for excellent outcome at 90 days (mRS 0–1) within the endovascular treatment cohort (Fig. 4), lower baseline NIHSS scores (aRR, 0.84 [95% CI, 0.74–0.95]; P = 0.004) and successful revascularization (final mTICI) (aRR, 2.79 [95% CI, 1.42–5.51]; P = 0.003) were factors correlated with excellent outcome at 90 days in the model. In addition, we observed that successful revascularization (aRR, 3.38 [95% CI, 1.73–6.58]; P < 0.001) and a lower Baseline NIHSS score (aRR, 0.85[95%CI, 0.75–0.96]; P = 0.006) were significantly correlated with the rates of functional independence (mRS 0–2) at 90 days.

Subgroup analysis

In terms of excellent outcome (mRS 0–1), EVT had a significant effect on achieving excellent outcome in patients received IVT compared with those without IVT (aRR, 1.64 [95% CI, 1.34–1.99]; P for interaction = 0.029; Fig. 5). There was no definite evidence of treatment effect modification across the prespecified subgroups for mRS distribution at 90 days, the proportion of mRS 0–2 at 90 days, incidence of all-causes mortality at 90-day, and sICH within 24 h (Figure S5-8). The observed interaction between EVT and prior IVT (P for interaction = 0.029) should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating only. The study was not powered for subgroup analyses, and no adjustment was made for multiple comparisons.

Discussion

This retrospective, multicenter, registry study evaluated the efficacy and safety outcomes of EVT versus SMM in primary M3 and M4 occlusion stroke patients with or without IVT. The study failed to find any significant difference in the excellent outcome (mRS 0–1), overall degree of disability (as measured by the ordinal shift in the mRS), and functional outcomes (mRS 0–2) at 90 days between the EVT and SMM cohorts. Regarding safety outcomes, no significant differences were observed in the rates of sICH and 90-day mortality between groups in the IPTW and PSM models. Secondly, we found a significant relationship between successful revascularization and excellent outcome at 90 days.

Only a few small case series have investigated the feasibility, safety parameters, and outcomes of EVT for primary M3 occlusion. A German stroke registry enrolled 11 patients with primary M3 occlusion (median age 80 years, 45.5% male) underwent EVT, with a median NIHSS score at admission of 8 (IQR, 6.5–11). The study found that successful reperfusion (modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction [mTICI]≥2b) was achieved in 6/11 patients, sICH occurred in 2 patients and favorable outcome (mRS ≤ 2) was achieved in 6/11 patients at 90-day [10]. Another case series of 39 primary M3 occlusion patients (mean age 70 years, 35.9% male) found that complete (mTICI 3) reperfusion was achieved in 24 cases (61.5%), with only one patient had sICH result from extravasation [13]. The patient’s neurological examination was normal at one month later [13]. These findings demonstrated that EVT for primary M3 occlusion was safe, technically feasible, and effective. However, both of the studies have limited sample size, and there was no control group of patients with primary M3 occlusion not undergoing EVT.

As the largest published series on M3 and M4 occlusions, our study reflects real-world clinical practice across multiple centers. Our study failed to show any superiority of EVT over best medical treatment for patients with M3 and M4 occlusions, which is consistent with recent randomized studies that have not benefits of EVT for acute MeVO-related stroke patients [14, 15]. The DISTAL trial enrolled 543 patients with MeVO presenting within 24 h of symptom onset, including 146 patients with M3 occlusion (EVT = 62, SMM = 84). The primary endpoint, the distribution of mRS scores, showed no significant difference between the EVT and SMM groups [14]. In the ESCAPE-MeVO trial, 530 patients with MeVO strokes were randomized within 12 h from symptom onset, including 216 patients with M3 occlusion (EVT = 90, SMM = 126) [15]. Similarly, no significant differences were noted in the rates of mRS 0–1 at 90 days between the EVT and SMM cohorts.

In the current study, EVT achieved successful revascularization in 85.9% of M3 and M4 stroke patients associated with favorable long-term outcome (rates of mRS 0–1 and 0–2 at 90 days) and did not lead to increased rates of sICH within 24 h and mortality at 90 days compared with SMM. This is consistent with outcomes seen in pivotal studies on EVT for proximal large vessel occlusions [24]. A recent study on the efficacy of EVT for MeVO strokes also found a high successful revascularization rate of 89.4% (mTICI scale ≥ 2b) [25]. A previous study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of EVT for patients with anterior cerebral artery occlusions also reported a significant relationship between successful reperfusion and favorable functional outcome at 90 days (mRS 0–2)6. Our findings suggest that EVT is effective and safe for distal M3 occlusion strokes, similar to its use in more proximal occlusions.

The optimal technique for EVT of M3 and M4 stroke patients is still uncertain. A recent meta-analysis reported that the application of aspiration or stent retriever techniques as first-line techniques appears to be safe and effective for primary and secondary MeVO strokes [26]. The first-line technique in our study was at the discretion of the neurointerventional physician without restriction to the technique or device. Notably, successful reperfusion (mTICI scale ≥ 2b) was achieved in 454 (85.8%) patients in the EVT cohort. In addition, we observed no significant correlation between safety profiles and first-line techniques. Future studies are warranted to investigate the impact of first-line techniques on safety and angiographic outcomes.

A previous study found that IVT is very effective for patients with MeVO strokes, with 50.0% (129/258) of the participants achieved excellent outcome (mRS 0–1) and 67.4% (174/258) of the participants gained functional independence (mRS 0–2) [27]. The rate of patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis in DISTAL and ESCAPE MeVO trials were (62% [168/271] versus 66.8% [187/272]) and (56.5% [144/255] versus 60.2% [165/274]), respectively [14, 15]. Both of the studies failed to find that EVT was superior than SMM in the management of MeVO stokes. The low utilization rate of intravenous thrombolysis in our study may be due to delayed presentation and contraindications. Our finding provides valuable real-world evidence in the MeVO field, complementing the recently published DISTAL and ESCAPE-MeVO trials.

The apparent greater treatment benefit of EVT in patients who received IVT warrants cautious interpretation. First, the rates of IVT administration differed between the two groups at baseline (28.8% vs. 18.0%), which may reflect selection bias. Second, the decision to administer IVT is clinically intertwined with EVT selection — patients eligible for both therapies may differ systematically from those who are not. Despite adjustment for measured confounders using IPTW, we cannot exclude the possibility of residual indication bias due to unmeasured factors. Third, this subgroup finding emerged from an exploratory analysis for which the study was not powered, and no correction for multiple testing was applied. Therefore, this result should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive, and it requires confirmation in future prospective studies.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Firstly, although IPTW and PSM analysis provided consistent results, the retrospective, nonrandomized design confers selection bias. Unidentified confounders may appear owing to the variations in clinical practice, procedural technique, center experience, and patient selection. We have no follow-up vascular imaging to evaluate post-treatment revascularization in the SMM group, thus preventing the subanalysis of the revascularization status. The potential of thrombus migration from proximal occlusions before imaging may lead to a higher NIHSS score in patients with isolated M3 occlusions. We did not systematically assess recanalization that may have occurred between diagnostic imaging and the start of the endovascular procedure, either spontaneously or following IVT. Such early recanalization could potentially influence treatment decisions and outcome interpretation. In addition, we acknowledge that the trial was registered retrospectively after the completion of patient enrollment and data collection. Retrospective registration may raise concerns regarding the potential for selective reporting of outcomes or post hoc changes to the analysis plan. Last, the lack of an independent imaging core laboratory to review angiographic images may impact the determination of reperfusion and the clot location.

Conclusions

The study suggests that EVT appears to be a safe and technically feasible treatment option for primary isolated M3 occlusions with clinical outcomes similar to SMM with and without intravenous thrombolysis.

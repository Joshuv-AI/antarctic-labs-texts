A Double Inertial Forward-Backward Splitting Algorithm With Applications to Regression and Classification Problems
İrfan IŞIK a , Ibrahim KARAHAN b and Okan ERKAYMAZ c

Abstract
This paper presents an improved forward-backward splitting algorithm with two inertial parameters. It aims to find a point in the real Hilbert space at which the sum of a co-coercive operator and a maximal monotone operator vanishes. Under standard assumptions, our proposed algorithm demonstrates weak convergence. We present numerous experimental results to demonstrate the behavior of the developed algorithm by comparing it with existing algorithms in the literature for regression and data classification problems. Furthermore, these implementations suggest our proposed algorithm yields superior outcomes when benchmarked against other relevant algorithms in existing literature.

Double inertial, monotone inclusion problem, regression, classification, weak convergence

20
]

x_{n+1}=\left(I+\lambda_{n}A\right)^{-1}\left(I-\lambda_{n}B\right)x_{n}\text{ \ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}

(2)

where
A
and
B
are monotone operators and
\lambda_{n}
is step size. In

introduced a two-step strategy as part of the

\gamma_{n}\left\|\nabla f(\theta_{n+1})-\nabla f(\theta_{n})\right\|\leq\rho\left\|\theta_{n+1}-\theta_{n}\right\|\text{.}

(3)

It has been demonstrated that

converges weakly to a solution of
\left(A+B\right)^{-1}(0)
. This algorithm, called forward–backward–forward, is one of the most basic algorithms. In

Algorithm (FISTA) as follows: Let

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the basic concepts and definitions which we need to prove the main results. We use the notations
p_{k}\rightharpoonup p
and
p_{k}\to p
in
H
to indicate weak and strong convergence of the sequence
\{p_{k}\}\subset H
to
p\in H
, respectively, as
k\to\infty
.
For all
p,y\in H
and any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the following identities and inequality hold:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{i)}&\|p+y\|^{2}\leq\|p\|^{2}+2\langle y,p+y\rangle\\
\text{ii)}&\|p+y\|^{2}=\|p\|^{2}+2\langle p,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2}\\
\text{iii)}&\|\alpha p+(1-\alpha)y\|^{2}=\alpha\|p\|^{2}+(1-\alpha)\|y\|^{2}-\alpha(1-\alpha)\|p-y\|^{2}.\end{array}

Let
\mathcal{T}:H\rightarrow H
be an operator on a real Hilbert space
H
. The mapping
\mathcal{T}
is said to satisfy the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{T}
is called
nonexpansive
if

\|\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\|\leq\|p-y\|,\quad\forall p,y\in H.

(7)

(ii)

\mathcal{T}
is said to be
firmly nonexpansive
provided that

\|\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\|^{2}\leq\|p-y\|^{2}-\|(I-\mathcal{T})p-(I-\mathcal{T})y\|^{2},\quad\forall p,y\in H,

(8)

which is equivalent to

\|\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\|^{2}\leq\langle p-y,\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\rangle.

(9)

(iii)

For a given parameter
\kappa\in(0,1)
, the operator
\mathcal{T}
is said to be
\kappa
-averaged
if there exists a nonexpansive mapping
S:H\to H
such that

\mathcal{T}=(1-\kappa)I+\kappa S.

(10)

Equivalently, the following inequality holds:

\|\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\|^{2}\leq\|p-y\|^{2}-\frac{1-\kappa}{\kappa}\|(I-\mathcal{T})p-(I-\mathcal{T})y\|^{2},\quad\forall p,y\in H.

(11)

(iv)

The mapping
\mathcal{T}
is said to be
L
-Lipschitz continuous
for some constant
L>0
if

\|\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\|\leq L\|p-y\|,\quad\forall p,y\in H.

(12)

(v)

The operator
\mathcal{T}
is
monotone
if it satisfies

\langle\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y,p-y\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall p,y\in H.

(13)

(vi)

\mathcal{T}
is said to be
\eta
-strongly monotone
for some constant
\eta>0
if

\langle\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y,p-y\rangle\geq\eta\|p-y\|^{2},\quad\forall p,y\in H.

(14)

(vii)

The mapping
\mathcal{T}
is
\alpha
-co-coercive
(also known as
\alpha
-inverse strongly monotone
) if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\langle\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y,p-y\rangle\geq\alpha\|\mathcal{T}p-\mathcal{T}y\|^{2},\quad\forall p,y\in H.

(15)

A set-valued operator
B:H\rightarrow 2^{H}
is called
monotone
if it satisfies

\langle p-y,f-g\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{for all }p,y\in H,\ f\in Bp,\ g\in By.

The
graph
of
B
, denoted by
\operatorname{Gr}(B)
, is defined as

\operatorname{Gr}(B):=\{(p,f)\in H\times H:f\in Bp\}.

The operator
B
is said to be
maximal monotone
if its graph is not properly contained in the graph of any other monotone operator.

Main Results

This section of the paper addresses a forward-backward splitting approach incorporating two inertial terms, aiming to identify a zero point of the sum of a maximally monotone operator and a co-coercive operator within a real Hilbert space. We then establish results concerning the weak convergence behavior of the sequence generated by the proposed method. The proposed method, denoted as Algorithm 1*, is detailed below:

Step 2.

\displaystyle w_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vartheta(p_{k}-p_{k-1})+\delta(p_{k-1}-p_{k-2})+p_{k},

\displaystyle p_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\mathcal{E}_{k})w_{k}+\mathcal{E}_{k}\left(J_{\lambda}^{B}(w_{k}-\lambda Aw_{k})\right),

where
J_{\lambda}^{B}=(I+\lambda B)^{-1}
denotes the resolvent of the operator
B
.

Step 3.
Increment
k
by

and return to Step 2.

Conclusion and Discussion

We propose a forward-backward splitting algorithm to find the zero point of
the sum of maximal monotone and co-coercive operators in real Hilbert
spaces. Unlike most of the algorithms in the literature, the new algorithm has been created that includes double inertial terms. We proved the weak
convergence of the sequence generated by the algorithm and gave applications to regression and data classification problems. When we compare the algorithm we have presented
with other algorithms that give successful results in the literature, we
have obtained more successful results than the others. In our future studies, necessary arrangements will be made to optimize the convergence rate of the algorithm and to provide more successful results.

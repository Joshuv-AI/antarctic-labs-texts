1.
Introduction

The familiar notion of an ideal of a ring generalizes nicely to any additive category
\mathcal{A}
as a subbifunctor of
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,-)
that distinguishes a class of morphisms. As in the classical case, one has the usual lattice operations of
I+J
and
I\cap J
on a given pair of ideals
I
and
J
, but one can also use composition of morphisms to define the product
IJ
. This inevitably leads to notions like the left or right annihilator of an ideal, which, in the context of an additive category, emerges as a theory of
Hom
-orthogonality of ideals, a generalization of the classical theory of torsion pairs.

In this paper, we propose an axiomatic treatment of additive categories that supports a theory of
ideal torsion pairs
. It emulates Quillen’s introduction of an exact category, which provides the proper axiomatic context for the theory of
Ext
-orthogonality for ideals, centered around
ideal cotorsion pairs
. Just as the axioms for an exact category distinguish a collection
\mathcal{E}
of kernel-cokernel pairs in
\mathcal{A}
called
conflations,
our axioms distinguish a collection
{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{C}}
of weak kernel-cokernel pairs called
weak conflations
of an additive category.

The fundamental theorem of ideal cotorsion pairs in an exact category
(\mathcal{A};\mathcal{E})
is Salce’s Lemma, which states that if
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is an ideal cotorsion pair, i.e., a maximal pair of

-special preenveloping (we add the prefix

Remark 7

A foundational issue arises when we attempt to generalize the ideal lattice of a ring to the setting of an additive category. While the ring may be regarded as a preadditive category with one object, the additive category

need not even be skeletally small. In that case, an ideal

is a weak exact category.

Remark 32

We do not know of any examples of weak exact categories that are not extriangulated. In a triangulated category, every morphism is part of a triangle and is therefore both a weak inflation as well as a weak deflation. We do not know of any example of a weak exact category all of whose morphisms are both weak inflations and weak deflations that is not triangulated.

In the remainder of this section, we will assume that
(\mathcal{A};{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{E}})
is a weak exact category

5.2.
Image maximal ideals

Note that in an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, every morphism

factors through its image as

. For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
in
\mathcal{A}
and

a morphism in
\mathcal{I}

Definition 44

An ideal

-precover of

27

\mathcal{A}

Consider the ideal torsion pair

is a right annihilator ideal, it follows that the inclusion

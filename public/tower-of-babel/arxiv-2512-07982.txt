On the rational C 2 C_{2} -homotopy type of B ​ S ​ U ℝ m {BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}
Eunice Sukarto

Abstract
Motivated by a problem in motivic homotopy theory considered by Asok-Fasel-Hopkins, we give a description of the rational C 2 C_{2} -equivariant homotopy type of the classifying space B ​ S ​ U ℝ m {BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m} in terms of equivariant Eilenberg-Maclane spaces.



1
Introduction

In
[
1
]
, Asok, Fasel, and Hopkins study the motivic classifying space
BSL_{m}
and show that it has a ’well-behaved’ rationalization, either if
m
is odd or if
m
is even and -1 is a sum of squares in the base field. In particular, they show that if either of these conditions is satisfied, the Chern classes
c_{i}:BSL_{m}\to K(\mathbb{Z}(i),2i)
together give a rational
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence

BSL_{m}\to\prod_{i=2}^{m}K(\mathbb{Z}(i),2i)

where
\mathbb{Z}(i)
is Voevodsky’s motivic complex (see
[
5
, Def 3.1]
).

This manifests itself in ordinary homotopy theory as the fact that over the complex numbers,
BSL_{m}
is equivalent to
BSU_{m}
which is rationally a product of Eilenberg-Maclane spaces

BSU_{m}\to\prod_{i=1}^{m}K(\mathbb{Z},2i).

However, over the reals,
BSL_{m}
is equivalent to
BSO_{m}
where

BSO_{m}\to\prod_{i=1}^{m}K(\mathbb{Z},2i)

is only a rational equivalence when
m
is odd. If
m
is even, there is an Euler class living in degree
m
which is not accounted for. To better understand this phenomena, we consider these two cases together, which leads to
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy theory.

The group
C_{2}
acts on
BSU_{m}
by complex conjugation, making it into a
C_{2}
-equivariant space
{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}
classifying Real vector bundles in the sense of Atiyah. It has underlying space
BSU_{m}
and fixed points
BSO_{m}
.
In this paper, we will give a description of the rational
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy type of
{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}
in terms of equivariant Eilenberg-Maclane spaces.

If
m=2n+1
is odd, it is known that the equivariant Chern classes give a rational
C_{2}
-equivalence

{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{(2n+1)}\to\prod_{i=2}^{2n+1}K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},i\rho),

where
\rho
is the regular representation and
K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},i\rho)=\Omega_{C_{2}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{i\rho}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{Z}})
is the equivariant Eilenberg-Maclane space corresponding to the suspension of the constant Mackey functor
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
by the representation sphere
\mathbb{S}^{i\rho}
. We consider the case where
m=2n
is even. Let
\iota^{2},N:K(A,2n\rho)\to K(A,4n\rho)
denote the squaring and norm maps, respectively. Then
\iota^{2}-N
factors through
K(I,4n)
where
I
is the augmentation ideal Mackey functor. Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.0.1

(Theorem
4.0.2
)
.

The map

{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{2n}\to\left(\prod_{i=2}^{2n-1}K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},i\rho)\right)\times\mathrm{fib}\left(K(A,2n\rho)\xrightarrow{\iota^{2}-N}K(I,4n)\right)

given by the Chern classes
c_{i}
for
i<n
and the Euler class is a rational
C_{2}
-equivalence.

The fact that the Euler class maps into the fiber of
\iota^{2}-N
is the condition that the norm of the Euler class is equal to its square:
e(NV)=e(V\otimes\mathbb{C})=e(V)^{2}
for
V
an even dimensional real vector bundle.

2.2
Equivariant characteristic classes

The
C_{2}
-CW structure of
MU_{\mathbb{R}}
gives a splitting

\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\wedge MU_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\wedge\left(\bigvee_{d}\mathbb{S}^{\frac{|d|}{2}\rho}\right)

where

ranges over the
C_{2}
-cells of
MU_{\mathbb{R}}
, which are the monic monomials in
MU_{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]
(
[
4
, Section 5]
). Since
MU_{\mathbb{R}}
is the Thom spectrum of
BU_{\mathbb{R}}
, the equivariant Chern class

which corresponds to

, naturally lives in
\mathbb{S}^{i\rho}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
.
\mathbb{S}^{i\rho}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
is computed by the complex
\mathbb{Z}(i)[2i]
given by

\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]\xrightarrow{1-(-1)^{i}\tau}\cdots\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]\xrightarrow{1+\tau}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]\xrightarrow{1-\tau}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}

(2)

with
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
in degree
i
and the leftmost
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]
in degree
2i
. Here
[-]
denotes the shift/suspension functor.
The map
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
is induced by the transfer in
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]
. On underlying,
\mathbb{S}^{i\rho}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
is given by

\mathbb{Q}[C_{2}]\xrightarrow{1-(-1)^{i}\tau}\cdots\mathbb{Q}[C_{2}]\xrightarrow{1+\tau}\mathbb{Q}[C_{2}]\xrightarrow{1-\tau}\mathbb{Q}[C_{2}]\to\mathbb{Q}

where the last map is the sum of coefficients. On fixed points, it is given by

\mathbb{Q}\to\cdots\to\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\cdot 2}\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\cdot 2}\mathbb{Q}

where the leftmost map is
\cdot 2
if
i
is odd and 0 if
i
is even. Thus,
K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},i\rho)
is a
C_{2}
-space with underlying
K(\mathbb{Q},2i)
and fixed points
K(\mathbb{Q},2i)
if
i
is even and trivial if
i
is odd.

The Chern classes together give a map

{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}\to\prod_{i=2}^{m}K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},i\rho).

By (
1
), this is a rational
C_{2}
-equivalence if
m
is odd.
However, if
m=2n
is even, the Euler class
e_{2n}\in H^{2n}(BSO_{2n};\mathbb{Q})
is not in the image of this map. We would like a description of
{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{2n}
which captures this Euler class.

The Thom class gives a map
{MU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since the cell structure of
{MSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}
gives no obstruction to lifting this to a map
{MSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}\to A
, the equivariant Euler class of
{BSU_{\mathbb{R}}}_{m}
naturally lives in
\mathbb{S}^{m\rho}\otimes A
. Since
A\otimes{C_{2}}_{+}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes{C_{2}}_{+}
,
K(A,m\rho)
has the same underlying space as
K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},m\rho)
but differs by an additional
\mathbb{Q}
in degree
m
on fixed points. The bottom part of the corresponding complex (
2
) is
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}[C_{2}]\to A
, which is
1+\tau:\mathbb{Q}[C_{2}]\to\mathbb{Q}
on underlying and
T:\mathbb{Q}\to\frac{\mathbb{Q}[T]}{T^{2}-2T}
on fixed points. If

is even, this Euler class detects the Euler class
e_{2n}\in H^{2n}(BSO_{2n};\mathbb{Q})
.

The squaring and the norm maps

Let
K_{m}
denote the Eilenberg-Maclane space
K(\mathbb{Q},m)
. Let
\iota^{2},N:K(A,2n\rho)\to K(A,4n\rho)
denote the squaring and norm maps, respectively. In this section, we will identify
\iota^{2}
and
N
on underlying spaces and fixed points.

Proposition 3.0.1
.

Let
x
and
y
be the fundamental classes in
K_{4n}
and
K_{8n}
, respectively.
The squaring map
\iota^{2}:K(A,2n\rho)\to K(A,4n\rho)
is given on underlying spaces by
K_{4n}\to K_{8n}

and on fixed points by
K_{2n}\times K_{4n}\to K_{4n}\times K_{8n}
,
(x,y)\mapsto(x^{2},y^{2})
.

The norm map
N:K(A,2n\rho)\to K(A,4n\rho)
is given on underlying spaces by
K_{4n}\to K_{8n}
,
y\mapsto y^{2}
and on fixed points by
K_{2n}\times K_{4n}\to K_{4n}\times K_{8n}
,
(x,y)\mapsto(y,y^{2})
.

Lemma 3.0.2
.

The inclusion of fixed points

is the identity map on
K_{4n}
.
The inclusion of fixed points

is the map
K_{2n}\times K_{4n}\to K_{4n}
given by projection onto the second factor.

Proof.

The first statement is clear. For the second, we need to identify
j_{A}
on each component of
K(A,2n\rho)^{C_{2}}
.
j_{A}
is equal to the map on fixed points of the infinite
C_{2}
-loop map
K(A,2n\rho)\to K(\underline{\mathbb{Z}},2n\rho)
induced by the projection
A\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since the inclusion of fixed points

on
K(A,2n\rho)
is the identity map,
j_{A}
is the identity on
K_{4n}
. Since there are no nonzero maps of spectra
\Sigma^{2n}H\mathbb{Q}\to\Sigma^{4n}H\mathbb{Q}
,
j_{A}
is 0 on
K_{2n}
.
∎

3.2
The norm map

Let
N:=N_{H}^{G}
denote the norm functor for a subgroup
H\leq G
. The norm
N:\mathcal{S}_{H}\to\mathcal{S}_{G}
on spaces and
N:\mathrm{Sp}_{H}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{G}
on spectra are strong symmetric monoidal and are related by the adjunction

\Sigma_{G}^{\infty}:\mathcal{S}_{G}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Sp}_{G}:\Omega_{G}^{\infty}.

The left adjoint
\Sigma_{G}^{\infty}
is strong symmetric monoidal and commutes with the norm:
N\Sigma_{G}^{\infty}=\Sigma_{G}^{\infty}N
. However, the right adjoint
\Omega_{G}^{\infty}
is only lax symmetric monoidal, so
N\Omega_{G}^{\infty}\neq\Omega_{G}^{\infty}N
, but there is a map from the former to the latter. If
R\in\mathrm{Sp}_{G}
is an equivariant commutative algebra (
[
3
, 2.3]
), there is a map
NR\to R
. For
X\in\mathcal{S}_{G}
, there is a map
X\to NX
which factors through the product
\prod_{i\in G/H}X_{i}
(
[
3
, 2.3.3]
).

For
G=C_{2}
, let
t
be the generator in
C_{2}
. For
X\in\mathcal{S}_{C_{2}}
, the map
X\to NX
is given by

X\to X\times X\to NX=X\wedge X

where
X\times X
and
X\wedge X
are considered as (pointed)
C_{2}
-spaces with
C_{2}
acting by swapping the two factors i.e.
t(x,y)=(y,x)
and the first map sends
x
to
(x,tx)
.

Let
V

Genome assembly of Megasphaera elsdenii CH4, a potential probiotic bacterium isolated from the rumen sample of Holstein dairy cow
Authors: Tshifhiwa Paris Mamphogoro, Christa Coetzee, Marietjie Bruwer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesMegasphaera elsdenii is a commensal bacterium and a promising probiotic candidate that is frequently given as a supplement directly to ruminant animals. Animals that are fed grain, benefit greatly from the organism’s ability to convert lactic acid into volatile fatty acids, particularly butyrate, valerate, isobutyrate, and propionate, which are important sources of energy for animals. This conversion helps modulate rumen acidity and prevent ruminal acidosis (SARA), a prevalent condition in the feedlot cattle. The objective of this work was to generate a high-quality draft genome assembly of M. elsdenii which will contribute to enhancing animal feed supplementation, by improving our understanding of its interactions with the host.Data descriptionHere, we describe the genome sequence of M. elsdenii isolate CH4, one of the most promising probiotic candidates, commonly administered as a direct-fed microbe supplement to ruminant animals. Sequencing reads from M. elsdenii produced approximately 5.6 Mb. The assembly generated a draft genome size of 2.5 Mb, consisting of 27 contigs, with an N50 of 102.8 KB. Examination of the genome completeness and contamination was assessed using CheckM v1.0.18 and reported as 93.57 and 4.34%, respectively. The draft genome sequence will allow improved genomic comparisons among the probiotic strains of the genus Megasphaera, particularly with the aim of improving our knowledge of their animal-probiotic interactions as well as their population biology.Keywords: Megasphaera elsdenii, Probiotics, Ruminal acidosis (SARA), Direct-fed microbe, Genome assembly, Illumina

Objectives

Megasphaera elsdenii is a naturally occurring Gram-negative and strictly anaerobic bacterium found in the rumen of cattle. This organism reduces lactic acid accumulation within the rumen of grain fed cattle by converting lactic acid to volatile fatty acids (VFAs) [1, 2], which are the predominant source of energy for ruminants [3]. Moreover, it plays a major role in rumen pH maintenance and subacute ruminal acidosis (SARA) prevention because it can metabolize lactate into VFAs that will be absorbed via the rumen epithelium [4]. M. elsdenii has been proposed as a potential direct fed microbial (DFM) to prevent ruminal acidosis in feedlot cattle and high-producing dairy cows. The obvious cause of lactic acidosis is the transition from forage-based diets to concentrate-based diets, containing high levels of rapidly fermentable carbohydrates, or even the abrupt consumption of these carbohydrates, which changes the fermentation pattern towards nonfibrous carbohydrate fermentation [5]. One option to overcome ruminal acidosis is to decrease undissociated lactic acid in the rumen by supplementing probiotic feed additives that can metabolize lactic acid [6]. The aim of this study was to assemble a high-quality draft genome of M. elsdenii using next-generation sequencing technology. The work presented here provides fundamental genomic information that could be valuable in enhancing the existing knowledge on the general knowledge of Megasphaera biology and may contribute to the discovery of novel probiotic genes involved in immunomodulation, where probiotics can stimulate immune responses, and ability to assist animals in coping with stress. The genome data also present an opportunity for further comparative genomic analysis of Megasphaera species.

Data description

Strain CH4 of M. elsdenii was isolated from the rumen sample collected from a Holstein dairy cow at the Animal Production Institute, Agricultural Research Council, Irene, South Africa. A four-layered cheesecloth was used to filter the rumen fluid, and the filtrate was collected into a sterile Schott bottle. Subsequently, it was serially diluted in saline solution and plated on De Man Rogosa Sharpe (MRS) agar 0.05 g/l cysteine-hydrochloride and incubated at 37 °C under complete anaerobic condition for 48 h [7]. The isolate was sub-cultured in MRS broth under strict anaerobic conditions and incubated at 37 °C for 48 h in an anaerobic jar equipped with sachets of AnaeroGen (Thermofisher, UK) to further deplete available carry-over oxygen. DNA was extracted using the Quick-DNA miniprep Plus kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Genomic DNA libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra II FS DNA library prep kit (New England Biolabs, Ipswich, MA) and sequencing was performed using the Illumina NextSeq 550 platform producing a paired-end sequence of 300 bp. The quality of the raw reads was assessed using FastQC v0.11.9 [8], and low-quality fragments and adapters were trimmed using Trimmomatic v0.36 [9]. Filtered reads were de novo assembled using SPAdes v3.15.32 assembler [10], and the genome completeness and possible contamination were assessed using CheckM v1.0.18 [11]. The genome assembly quality was assessed using Quast v5.2.0 [12]. Gene annotation was performed through and the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP v6.6) [13], discovering probiotic genes involved in acid tolerance, stress tolerance, bile salt tolerance, adhesion, immunomodulation and antoxidant production (Table 1, Data File 1) [14]. The genome was assigned to M. elsdenii through GTDB-Tk v2.3.2 [15]. The assembly statistics, raw sequence data and genome assembly data are listed in Table 1, Data File 1, Data Set 1, and Data Set 2 [14, 16]. Overall, a genome assembly of 2,475,708 bp of nucleotides and 53% GC content were assembled. This draft genome assembly has a sequencing depth of 677X with a total of 27 contigs (>200 bp).

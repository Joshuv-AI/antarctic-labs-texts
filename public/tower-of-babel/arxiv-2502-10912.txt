Orbits on a product of two flags and a line and the Bruhat order, I
Mark Colarusso, Sam Evens

Abstract
. Let G = G ​ L ​ ( n ) G=GL(n) be the n × n n\times n complex general linear group and let ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} be its flag variety. The standard Borel subgroup B B of upper triangular matrices acts on the product ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1} diagonally with finitely many orbits. In this paper, we study the B B -orbits on the subvarieties ℬ n × 𝒪 i \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{O}_{i} , where 𝒪 i \mathcal{O}_{i} is the B B -orbit on ℙ n − 1 \mathbb{P}^{n-1} containing the line through the origin in the direction of the i i -th standard basis vector of ℂ n \mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}^{n} . For each i = 1 , … , n i=1,\dots,n , we construct a bijection between B B -orbits on ℬ n × 𝒪 i \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{O}_{i} and certain pairs of Schubert cells in ℬ n × ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n} . We also show that this bijection can be used to understand the Richardson-Springer monoid action on such B B -orbits in terms of the classical monoid action of the symmetric group on itself. We also develop combinatorial models of these orbits and use these models to compute exponential generating functions for the sequences { | B \ ( ℬ n × 𝒪 i ) | } n ≥ 1 \{|B\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{O}_{i})|\}_{n\geq 1} and { | B \ ( ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 ) | } n ≥ 1 \{|B\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})|\}_{n\geq 1} . In the sequel to this paper, we use the results of this paper to construct a correspondence between B B -orbits on ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1} and a collection of B B -orbits on the flag variety ℬ n + 1 \mathcal{B}_{n+1} of G ​ L ​ ( n + 1 ) GL(n+1) and show that this correspondence respects closures relations and preserves monoid actions. As a consequence both closure relations and monoid actions on the set of all B B -orbits on ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1} can be understood via the Bruhat order by using our results in [ CE ] .

4.
Combinatorial Models for
S_{i}
-orbits: Lists and PILs

In this section, we give an alternative combinatorial
model for
S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
to decorated permutations using the notion of partitions into lists. We compute exponential generating functions for
the sequence
\{|S_{n-i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}|\}_{n\geq i}
and use the result to compute the exponential generating function for the sequence
\{|G_{\Delta}\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})|\}_{n\geq 1}
. Finally, we give a third combinatorial model for decorated permutations using partial permutations, which is suggested from the study of the latter generating function.

We begin by recalling the parameterization of
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
in Section 3 of
[
CE23
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

(1)

We define a
list
on a subset
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
of the non-negative integers to be a
k
-tuple
\sigma=(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}
, where
i_{s}\neq i_{t}
for all
s\neq t
. For the list
\sigma
, we say

{\rm supp}(\sigma)=\{a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}}\}
, the length
\ell(\sigma)=k
,
and
\sigma(j)=a_{i_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq k
. We denote the set of lists of
\{1,\dots,n\}
of length
k
by
Li(n,k)
.

(2)

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be any finite subset. A
PIL

(partition into lists)
is a set of lists

\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\,\dots,\,\sigma_{k}\}
on
A
such that
\{{\rm supp}(\sigma_{1}),\dots,{\rm supp}(\sigma_{k})\}
is a partition of
A
.

(3)

For a finite subset
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we denote the set consisting of all PILs of
A
by
PIL(A)
.
In the case where
A=\{1,\dots,n\}
, we denote
PIL(A)
by
PIL(n)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Proposition 4.5 from
[
CE23
]
asserts that there is a bijection from flags in standard form to
PIL(n)
given as follows. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a flag in standard form with
\mathcal{F}=(v_{1}\subset\cdots\subset v_{n}).
Let
1\leq k_{1}<\dots<k_{r}<n
be the subsequence of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\{v_{k_{1}},\dots,v_{k_{r}}\}
are the hat vectors in
\mathcal{F}
, and let
v_{k_{\ell}}={\hat{e}}_{s_{k_{\ell}}}
for
\ell=1,\dots,r
and let
v_{t}=e_{s_{t}}
for

Then the PIL associated to
\mathcal{F}
is

\{(s_{1},\dots,s_{k_{1}-1},s_{k_{1}}),(s_{k_{1}+1},\dots,s_{k_{2}}),\dots,(s_{k_{r-1}+1},\dots,s_{k_{r}}),(s_{k_{r}+1},\dots,s_{n})\}.

We note that the formula for this map was stated incorrectly in loc. cit.

To parameterize

, we introduce the set:

(4.1)

PIL(n,n-i)\subset Li(n,n-i)\times PIL(n)\mbox{ defined by }PIL(n,n-i):=\{(\sigma,\Sigma):\;\sigma\in\Sigma\}.

We note that the symmetric group acts on

through its action on indices in lists.

Proposition 4.4

The number of

-orbits on

(4.2)

Remark 4.5

When

Proof.

By Theorem
3.5
, it suffices to construct a bijection

\Phi

\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}:\,\mathcal{F}\mbox{ is a flag in $i$-standard form}\}
to the set

\mathcal{F}=(v_{1}\subset\dots\subset v_{n})

i

and Remark

e_{i+1},\dots,e_{n}
occur in
\mathcal{F}
. Suppose that
v_{j_{i+1}}=e_{i+1},\dots,v_{j_{n}}=e_{n}

be the standard Levi subgroup of
G
corresponding to the subset
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i-1}\}

be its Weyl group. Let

It follows that there is a unique

Further, if
j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}
are the remaining indices,
w(j_{\ell})=\ell

If we let
u_{\ell}=v_{j_{\ell}}

\Phi(\mathcal{F}):=(w^{-1}\cdot(i+1,\dots,n),\{w^{-1}\cdot(i+1,\dots,n),w^{-1}\cdot\Sigma^{\prime}\})\in PIL(n,n-i).

\Phi

\sigma=(j_{i+1},\dots,j_{n})

Recall that if
\{f(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is a sequence of complex numbers, the corresponding exponential generating function
E_{f}(x)
is the formal power series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n!}x^{n}.

For functions
f,g:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\to\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}
, define
s=s(f,g,\cdot):{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\to\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}
by

s(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}f(k)g(n-k).

Then the multiplication principle for exponential generating functions asserts that
E_{s}(x)=E_{f}(x)\cdot E_{g}(x)
(see Proposition 5.1.3 and its proof in
[
Sta99
]
). Using Proposition
4.4
and the multiplication principle for exponential generating functions, we can compute an exponential generating function for the sequence
\{|S_{n-i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}|\}_{n\geq i}
, where we adopt the convention that
S_{0}=B
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Fix an
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
The exponential generating function for the sequence

(4.4)

\{|S_{n-i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}|\}_{n\geq i}\mbox{ is }x^{i}e^{\frac{x}{1-x}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
, it suffices to show that the exponential generating function for the sequence

\{|PIL(n,i)|\}_{n\geq i}
is
x^{i}e^{\frac{x}{1-x}}
.
It follows from Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 3.12 of
[
CE23
]
that
e^{\frac{x}{1-x}}=E_{g}(x)
, where
g(n)
is the sequence
g(n)=|PIL(n)|
for
n\geq 0
. Note that
x^{i}=E_{f}(x)
, where
f(k)
is the sequence

f(k)=\delta_{k,i}i!
. It follows from the multiplication principle for exponential generating functions discussed above that
x^{i}e^{\frac{x}{1-x}}=E_{s}(x)
where
s(n)
is the sequence with

s(n)={n\choose i}i!|PIL(n-i)|
. The Proposition then follows from the claim that

(4.5)

|PIL(n,i)|={n\choose i}i!|PIL(n-i)|.

For this, elements of
PIL(n,i)
correspond to pairs
(\sigma,\Sigma^{\prime})
where
\sigma
is an ordered subset of cardinality
i
and once
\sigma
is chosen,
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a PIL on the set of
n-i
elements not in
\sigma
. There are
\frac{n!}{(n-i)!}
ways to choose
\sigma
, and the claim follows.

Q.E.D.

We can now compute the exponential generating function for the sequence

\{|G_{\Delta}\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})|\}_{n\geq 1}.

Theorem 4.7
.

The exponential generating function for the sequence

(4.6)

\{|G_{\Delta}\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})|\}_{n\geq 1}\mbox{ is }\frac{e^{\frac{x}{1-x}}-x}{1-x}.

Proof.

Let
a_{n}:=|G_{\Delta}\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})|.
Note that Equation (
2.1
) implies that
a_{n}=|B\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})|.
It follows from Equations (
2.2
) and (

(4.7)

a_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|S_{n-k}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}|.

By Proposition
4.4
along with Equations (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we deduce that

(4.8)

a_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}k!|PIL(n-k)|\mbox{ for }n\geq 1.

It follows that if
a(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n!}x^{n}
is the exponential generating function for
a_{n}
, then
a(x)=f(x):=\frac{e^{\frac{x}{1-x}}-x}{1-x}
follows from the assertion that

(4.9)

f^{(n)}(0)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}k!|PIL(n-k)|\mbox{ for }n\geq 1.

We compute

using the generalized Leibniz formula

(4.10)

(gh)^{(n)}(0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}g^{(n-k)}(0)h^{(k)}(0),

with

By Proposition 3.12 in
[

g(x)=\displaystyle\left(1+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{|PIL(k)|}{k!}x^{k}\right).

Using Equation (

(4.11)

f^{(n)}(0)=n!+\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}{n\choose k}|PIL(n-k)|k!.

Since
{n\choose n-1}(n-1)!|PIL(1)|=n!
, Equation (

Definition 4.12

Proposition 4.13
.

There is a bijection between the set
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
of all decorated permutations and the set
\mathcal{I}_{n}
of all strictly partial permutations of
\{1,\dots,n\}
given by the map

(4.16)

\Phi:(w,\Delta):\mapsto w|_{\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus w^{-1}(\Delta)}\mbox{ for }(w,\Delta)\in\widehat{\Sigma}_{n},

where
w|_{\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus w^{-1}(\Delta)}
denotes the restriction of the permutation
w\in\Sigma_{n}
to the subset
\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus w^{-1}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

We first show that
\Phi
is surjective. Suppose
\tau
is a strictly partial permutation with

\tau(j_{k})=i_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,r
where
r<n
. Let
\{m_{1}<m_{2}<\dots<m_{n-r}\}:=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}
, and define

\Delta:=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\mbox{Im}(\tau)
, where
\mbox{Im}(\tau)
denotes the image of the partial permutation
\tau
. Let
\Delta=\{\ell_{1}<\dots<\ell_{n-r}\}
. Now extend
\tau
to a “full” permutation via the assignment:
w(m_{1})=\ell_{n-r},\dots,w(m_{n-r})=\ell_{1}
and
w|_{\{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}}=\tau
. By construction,
(w,\Delta)
is a decorated permutation since

w^{-1}(\ell_{n-r})=m_{1}<\dots<w^{-1}(\ell_{1})=m_{n-r}
, so that
\Delta
is a decreasing sequence for
w^{-1}
. Note that
\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus w^{-1}(\Delta)=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}

\Phi(w,\Delta)=w|_{\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus w^{-1}(\Delta)}=\tau
, and
\Phi
is surjective. If also
(w_{1},\Delta_{1})\in\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
and
\Phi(w_{1},\Delta_{1})=\tau

w_{1}^{-1}(\ell_{n-r})=m_{1}<\dots<w_{1}^{-1}(\ell_{1})=m_{n-r}.
Thus,
w_{1}=w
, and
\Phi

In this section, we generalize an extended monoid action on

-Shareshian map of Equation (

on itself extended to the product

5.1.
Background on Monoid actions

For more details on the subsequent material, we refer the reader to
[
RS90
]
,
[
Vog83
]
,
[
CE14
]
,
[
CE22
]
, and other sources.
Let
R
be a connected reductive algebraic group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{r}}
, let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{R}
be the flag
variety of
R
, and let
M
be an algebraic subgroup of
R
acting on
\mathcal{B}
with finitely many orbits. Identify
\mathcal{B}\cong R/B_{R}
, for a Borel subgroup
B_{R}\subset R
and let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}=\mbox{Lie}(B_{R})\subset\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}
.
Let
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
be the set of simple roots defined by the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, and let
S_{R}
be the simple reflections of the Weyl group
W
of
R
corresponding to
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
. For
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
be the variety of all parabolic subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{r}}
of type
\alpha
and consider the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi_{\alpha}:\mathcal{B}\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}
. For
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
with corresponding reflection
s=s_{\alpha}\in W
, we define an operator
m(s)
on the set of orbits
M\backslash\mathcal{B}
following the above sources. For
Q_{M}\in M\backslash\mathcal{B}
, let
m(s)*Q_{M}
be the unique
M
-orbit open and dense in
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})).

Since
\pi_{\alpha}:\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))\to\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})
is an
M
-equivariant

\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle, the orbit
Q_{M}\not=m(s)*Q_{M}
if and only if
\dim(m(s)*Q_{M})=\dim(Q_{M})+1.

Computation of
m(s)*Q_{M}
depends on the
the type of the root

\alpha
for the orbit
Q_{M}
, which is determined as follows. For
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}\in Q_{M}
, let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{p}}$}_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\pi_{\alpha}(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime})
and let
B^{\prime}
and
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be the corresponding parabolic subgroups of
R
, and let
V_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be the the solvable radical of
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
. Consider the group

S_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/V_{\alpha}^{\prime}
isogenous to
SL(2)
and its subgroup

M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=(M\cap P_{\alpha}^{\prime})/(M\cap V_{\alpha}^{\prime})
.
Using the standard identification
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))\cong M\times_{M\cap P_{\alpha}^{\prime}}P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/B^{\prime},
we see that
M
-orbits in

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
correspond to
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
-orbits in

P^{\prime}_{\alpha}/B^{\prime}\cong{\mbox{${\mathbb{P}}$}}^{1}.

Definition 5.1
.

(1) If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is solvable and contains the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of
S_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a complex root for
Q_{M}
. If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q_{M}
and otherwise
\alpha
is complex unstable for
Q_{M}
.

(2) If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=S_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a compact imaginary root for
Q_{M}.

(3) Suppose
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is one-dimensional and reductive.
If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a noncompact imaginary root for
Q_{M}
, while if
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}\not=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},
then
\alpha
is called a real root for
Q_{M}.

It is well-known and easy to prove that the type of the root depends only on the orbit
Q_{M}
and not on the point
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.
The following result is also well-known.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
Q\in M\backslash\mathcal{B}
with
Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
and
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
.

(1)

If
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q
, then
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
consists of two
M
-orbits:

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))=Q\cup M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}},

and
m(s_{\alpha})*Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
.

(2)

If
\alpha
is non-compact imaginary for
Q
then
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
consists of either two or three
M
-orbits. The open orbit

m(s_{\alpha})*Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vu_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, where
u_{\alpha}\in R
is the Cayley transform with respect to the root
\alpha
as defined in Equation (41) of
[
CE14
]
and
\dim Q=\dim(M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}})
.

Note that in all cases,
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))=MvP_{\alpha}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}.

5.2.
Extended Monoid action on
S_{i}
-orbits

The general theory of Section
5.1
can be applied to the setting of
G_{\Delta}
-orbits on

\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
and
S_{i}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
. The
G_{\Delta}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
come equipped with a monoid action by
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}\times\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
, one copy of
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
for each factor of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
We first consider the monoid action with respect to the second
factor of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in the product
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
Any
G_{\Delta}
-orbit on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
can be written in the form:

(5.1)

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=G_{\Delta}\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}]),\mbox{ for some }i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

Recall the orbit correspondences from Equations (
2.1
)-(
2.3
),

(5.2)

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=G\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}])\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{B,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=B\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}])\longleftrightarrow Q_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=S_{i}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.

Let
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
. It follows from definitions that

\begin{split}&m(s_{\alpha})*_{2}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\mbox{ is open }G_{\Delta}-\mbox{orbit in }G_{\Delta}\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},P_{\alpha}^{\prime}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}])\longleftrightarrow\\
&\mbox{ open }B-\mbox{orbit in }B\cdot(P_{\alpha}^{\prime}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}])\longleftrightarrow\mbox{ open }S_{i}-\mbox{orbit in }S_{i}P_{\alpha}^{\prime}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.\end{split}

But the last orbit is exactly
m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
from our discussion in Section
5.1
.
Summarizing,

(5.3)

m(s_{\alpha})*_{2}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\longleftrightarrow m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=\mbox{ open }S_{i}-\mbox{orbit in }S_{i}P_{\alpha}^{\prime}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.

We now consider the monoid action on
G_{\Delta}\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
coming from the first factor of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in the triple product

\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. By definition,

(5.4)

m(s_{\alpha})*_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=\mbox{ open }G_{\Delta}-\mbox{orbit in }G_{\Delta}\cdot(P_{\alpha}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}])\longleftrightarrow\mbox{ open }B-\mbox{orbit in }P_{\alpha}\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}]).

For arbitrary
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
, the parabolic subgroup
P_{\alpha}
need not preserve the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{i}=B\cdot[e_{i}]
and therefore
m(s_{\alpha})*_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
need not correspond to an
S_{i}
-orbit on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
. However, if
\alpha\in\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i-2},\alpha_{i+1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
, then
P_{\alpha}\cdot[e_{i}]=B\cdot[e_{i}]
and
m(s_{\alpha})*_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
corresponds to an
S_{i}
-orbit on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
which we denote by
m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}Q_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
. It will help to have a more detailed description of
m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}Q_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
.
We define

(5.5)

\mathfrak{S}_{i}:=\{\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}|\;P_{\alpha}\cdot[e_{i}]=B\cdot[e_{i}]\}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i-2},\alpha_{i+1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\}.

For any
p\in P_{\alpha}
,
p\cdot[e_{i}]=\tilde{b}\cdot[e_{i}]
for some
\tilde{b}\in B
. This implies that
\tilde{b}^{-1}p\in\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]
. Let
s:=\tilde{b}^{-1}p\in\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]
, so that

p=\tilde{b}s
. Therefore,

(5.6)

P_{\alpha}\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}])=B\cdot(\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[e_{i}]).

But the
B
-orbits in the variety in (
5.6
) correspond to
S_{i}
-orbits in the variety
(\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}])\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
. Thus,

(5.7)

\mbox{ for }\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}:\;\;m(s_{\alpha})*_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\longleftrightarrow m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}Q_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=\mbox{ open }S_{i}-\mbox{orbit in }\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.

Definition 5.3
.

We refer to the monoid action by simple roots
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
on
S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
given in (
5.3
) as the
right monoid
action on
S_{i}
-orbits. We refer to the monoid action given in Equation (
5.7
) as the
left monoid
action on
S_{i}
-orbits. Taken together, we refer to the monoid action by
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
as the
extended monoid action
.

Remark 5.4
.

Recall from Remark
2.2
that when
i=n
, the group
S_{n}
coincides up to centre with the standard upper triangular Borel subgroup
B_{n-1}
of
GL(n-1)
. In this case,
the roots

of

corresponding to the root

5.7
) coincides with the left monoid action via roots of

on

.

5.3.
The extended monoid action and
i
-Shareshian pairs

In this section, we generalize Section 4.3 of
[
CE
]
to
S_{i}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and
i
-Shareshian pairs for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. In
loc. cit.
, we discuss how to obtain both left and right monoid actions on
B\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
via simple roots of
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
and how these monoid actions induce left and right monoid actions of
W
on itself. These monoid actions are described in Equation (4.4) of
[
CE
]
, which we restate here for the convenience of the reader.

(5.8)

\begin{split}&\mbox{ Let }\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},\,w\in W;\\
\mbox{(Left Action) }&m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w=w\mbox{ if }\ell(s_{\alpha}w)<\ell(w)\mbox{ and }m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w=s_{\alpha}w\mbox{ if }\ell(s_{\alpha}w)>\ell(w).\\
\mbox{(Right Action) }&m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w=w\mbox{ if }\ell(ws_{\alpha})<\ell(w)\mbox{ and }m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w=ws_{\alpha}\mbox{ if }\ell(ws_{\alpha})>\ell(w).\\
\end{split}

Recall that the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{i}
stabilizes the flag
\mathcal{E}^{i}
of Equation (
3.1
) and
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{i}=\sigma_{i}(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}),
where
\sigma_{i}=(i,i-1,\dots,2,1)
is the
i
-cycle from Proposition
3.20
. Let
\Pi^{i}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}=\{\alpha_{1}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}^{i}\}
be the simple roots defined by the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{i}
with corresponding set of simple reflections
T^{i}:=\{s^{i}_{1},\dots,s^{i}_{n-1}\}
. It follows that
\alpha^{i}_{k}=\sigma_{i}(\alpha_{k})
for
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Notation 5.5
.

By considering the simple roots
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
and corresponding simple reflections
T^{i}
, we obtain a new order relation on
W
by considering the length of an element
w^{i}\in W
with respect to the set of simple reflections
T^{i}
. We denote
W
with this non-standard order relation by
(W,T^{i})
, and denote
W
with the standard order relation by
(W,T)
. We denote elements in the poset
(W,T)
by
w
and denote elements in the poset
(W,T^{i})
by
w^{i}
.

We also obtain left and right monoid actions of
(W,T^{i})
on itself analogous to the ones in Equation (
5.8
) with
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
replaced by
\alpha^{i}\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
. We now use the monoid actions of
(W,T)
and
(W,T^{i})
on themselves to define a monoid action of
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
on
(W,T)\times(W,T^{i})
. First, observe that
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\subset\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
. Indeed,
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k+1}^{i}
for
k=1,\dots,i-2
and
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k}^{i}
for
k=i+1,\dots,n-1
. The following definition generalizes Definition 4.5 in
[
CE
]
to the case of arbitrary
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Definition 5.6
.

Define the
restricted diagonal monoid action
on
(W,T)\times(W,T^{i})
via simple roots
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
as follows.

For
(w,u^{i})\in(W,T)\times(W,T^{i})
, define

(5.9)

\begin{split}\mbox{ (Left action)}&\mbox{ For }\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i},\,m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}(w,u^{i})=(m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w,m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}u^{i}).\\
\mbox{ (Right action)}&\mbox{ For }\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}(w,u^{i})=(m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w,m(s_{\alpha^{i}})*_{R}u^{i}),\end{split}

where the monoid actions
*_{L}
and
*_{R}
are given in Equation (
5.8
).

Henceforth, we will drop the subscripts
L
and
R
on the monoid actions defined in Equation (
5.9
) and use the convention that a simple root
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
acts on the left whenever
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}
and on the right if
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
. We now arrive at the main result of this section, which generalizes Theorem 4.6 of
[
CE
]
to
S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
for arbitrary
i
.

Theorem 5.7
.

(cf. Theorem 4.6 of
[
CE
]
)
The
i
-Shareshian map
\mathcal{S}h_{i}:S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{S}p_{i}\subset W\times W
given in Equation (
3.10
)
intertwines the monoid action by
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
on
S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
given in Equations (
5.7
) and (
5.3
) with the restricted diagonal monoid action on
(W,T)\times(W,T^{i})
given in (
5.9
), i.e., for
Q\in S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
,

(5.10)

\mathcal{S}h_{i}(m(s_{\alpha})*Q)=m(s_{\alpha})*\mathcal{S}h_{i}(Q).

In particular, the restricted diagonal monoid action on
(W,T)\times(W,T^{i})
preserves the set
\mathcal{S}p_{i}
of all
i
-Shareshian pairs.
Moreover, if
m(s_{\alpha})*Q\neq Q
, then the type of the root
\alpha
is determined by the type of
\alpha
for the corresponding
i
-Shareshian pair
\mathcal{S}h_{i}(Q)=(w,u^{i})
. More precisely, for a root
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
,

(1)

The root
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q
if and only if it is complex stable for both
w
and
u^{i}
.

(2)

The root
\alpha
is non-compact for
Q
if and only if
\alpha
is complex stable for exactly one of
w
and
u^{i}
and unstable for the other.

(3)

The root
\alpha
is real or complex unstable for
Q
if and only if
\alpha
is complex unstable for both
w
and
u^{i}
.

Proof.

The proof of the Theorem for the right monoid action via a root
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
is completely analogous to the corresponding proof in
[
CE
]
, so we omit the details here. For the left action of a root
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}
, the proof is largely analogous to the one in
[
CE
]
except that the proof of the analogue of Equation (4.7) of
loc.cit.
is slightly different in this case. Let
\mathcal{S}h(Q)=(Q_{B},Q_{B^{i}})\in B\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\times B^{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
so that
Q=Q_{B}\cap Q_{B^{i}}
by Remark
3.15
.
We claim that for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}
,

(5.11)

m(s_{\alpha})*Q=m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}\cap m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B^{i}},

Let
Q=S_{i}xB/B
for some
x\in G
. Then by Equation (
5.7
), we know that
m(s_{\alpha})*Q
is
\mbox{the open }S_{i}-\mbox{orbit in }Stab_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]xB/B
. We claim that

(5.12)

m(s_{\alpha})*Q\subset m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}.

Note that
m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}
is
\mbox{the open }B-\mbox{orbit in }P_{\alpha}xB/B
and that

\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]xB/B\subset P_{\alpha}xB/B
. We first show that

(5.13)

m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}\cap\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]xB/B\neq\emptyset.

If
\alpha
is complex unstable for
Q_{B}
, the assertion is clear. If on the other hand,

\alpha
is complex stable for
Q_{B}
, then by Proposition
5.2
, we have

m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}=Bs_{\alpha}xB/B
. Since
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}
, we can choose a representative
{\dot{s}}_{\alpha}
of
s_{\alpha}
contained in

\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]
. It then follows that

{\dot{s}}_{\alpha}xB/B\in m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}\cap\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]xB/B
. Since
\alpha
is complex for
Q_{B}
these two cases show that the assertion in Equation
(
5.13
) holds. By Equation (
5.13
),
m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}\cap\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]xB/B
is open and dense in the irreducible variety
\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]xB/B,
so
m(s_{\alpha})*Q
intersects
m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}
, which implies Equation (
5.12
).
One can repeat the above argument with
Q_{B}
replaced by
Q_{B^{i}}
by observing that

\mbox{Stab}_{P_{\alpha}}[e_{i}]\subset P_{\alpha}^{i}
, where
P_{\alpha}^{i}\supset B^{i}
is the parabolic subgroup of
G
corresponding to
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}\subset\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
. We leave the details to the reader. Equation (
5.11
) then follows from the inclusion
m(s_{\alpha})*Q\subset m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B}\cap m(s_{\alpha})*Q_{B^{i}}
and Remark
3.15
.

The proof of the statements (1)-(3) of the Theorem for the left monoid action via a root
\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}
is analogous to the proof of the corresponding statements for the case of the left monoid action via a root
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}}
in Theorem 4.6 of
[
CE
]
and is omitted.

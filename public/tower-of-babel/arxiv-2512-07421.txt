The D s ​ 0 ∗ ​ ( 2317 ) + D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+} decay to D s + ​ π 0 D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0} and D s ∗ ⁣ + ​ γ D_{s}^{*+}\gamma
Pei-Sen Su, Wen-Tao Lyu, Wei-Hong Liang, Eulogio Oset

Abstract
We study the strong decay of D s ​ 0 ∗ ​ ( 2317 ) + D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+} to D s + ​ π 0 D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0} considering the coupled channels of D 0 ​ K + , D + ​ K 0 , D s + ​ η D^{0}K^{+},D^{+}K^{0},D_{s}^{+}\eta and D s + ​ π 0 D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0} within a picture for the interaction based on the local hidden gauge approach. We also address the problem of the radiative decay to D s ∗ ⁣ + ​ γ D_{s}^{*+}\gamma , using the same information obtained from the molecular picture. We obtain a strong width of the D s ​ 0 ∗ ​ ( 2317 ) + D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+} of about 71 ​ keV 71\,\rm keV and a radiative decay of 1.3 ​ keV 1.3\,\rm keV . The anomalous terms for the radiative decay are considered for the first time, but they are found negligible. We make a thorough discussion of this and other results to the light of the recent measurement of Belle for the ratio of these two decay modes, and make a call for the precise measurement of the two decay widths independently to clarify the present situation concerning the nature of of the D s ​ 0 ∗ ​ ( 2317 ) + D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+} state.



I
Introduction

The radiative decay of resonances has been often advocated as a good test to learn about their nature
Wang 2007a
;
Kalashnikova et al. 2006
.
In particular the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+}\to D_{s}^{*+}\gamma

Godfrey 2003
;
Colangelo and De Fazio 2003
;
Bardeen et al. 2003
;
Fayyazuddin and Riazuddin 2004
;
Ishida et al. 2004
;
Azimov and Goeke 2004
;
Colangelo et al. 2005
;
Close and Swanson 2005
;
Liu et al. 2006
;
Wang 2007b
.
On the other hand, mounting evidence has been piling up about the molecular nature of the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
as a result of the coupled channel interaction with the
D^{0}K^{+},D^{+}K^{0},D_{s}^{+}\eta
channels
van Beveren and Rupp 2003
;
Barnes et al. 2003
;
Chen and Li 2004
;
Kolomeitsev and Lutz 2004
;
Gamermann et al. 2007a
;
Guo et al. 2007
;
Yang et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2022
.
Their claims are also supported by recent lattice QCD calculations
Mohler et al. 2013
;
Lang et al. 2014
;
Bali et al. 2017
;
Cheung et al. 2021
.
A detailed analysis of lattice data in Ref.
Martínez Torres et al. 2015
supports this picture and finds around
72\%
probability for the
DK
component in the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
state.

From the molecular perspective there are also early works evaluating the

D_{s0}^{*}(2317)\to D_{s}^{*}\gamma
decay
Faessler et al. 2007
;
Gamermann et al. 2007b
;
Lutz and Soyeur 2008
and more recently in Ref.
Cleven et al. 2014
and the update of this latter work in Ref.
Fu et al. 2022
.

The reason to reopen the issue is the recent work reported by the Belle collaboration
Abumusabh et al. 2025
, where the branching fraction ratio of the radiative decay to the strong decay of the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
is measured for the first time with the result
Abumusabh et al. 2025

\dfrac{\mathcal{B}(D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+}\to D_{s}^{*+}\gamma)}{\mathcal{B}(D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+}\to D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0})}=(7.14\pm 0.70\pm 0.23)\times 10^{-2}.

(1)

This result is at variance with present results reported in the PDG
Yao et al. 2006
that say this ratio is smaller than
0.059
.

The new measurement also gives us an opportunity to update the result of Ref.
Gamermann et al. 2007b
to the light of recent advances in the field.
The molecular state of
DK,D_{s}\eta
is now tackled with an interaction based on the extention of the local hidden gauge approach
Bando et al. 1985
;
Bando et al. 1988
;
Meissner 1988
;
Nagahiro et al. 2009
to the charm sector, as done in Ref.
Ikeno et al. 2023
, which proceeds by the exchange of vector mesons.
A new method is also used to evaluate the loops in the radiative decay, tied to the findings of the strong interaction in Ref.
Ikeno et al. 2023
, and the contribution of loops involving anomalous terms is also considered.

On the other hand, the measurement of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
involves the ratio of the radiative decay to the strong one of the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
.
Then we also tackle the problem of the strong decay of the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
to the isospin forbidden mode
D_{s}\pi^{0}
, which, according to BESIII results
Ablikim et al. 2018
, basically accounts for all the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
decay width.
We evaluate this decay by implementing a coupled channel calculation with the
D^{0}K^{+},D^{+}K^{0},D_{s}^{+}\eta,D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0}
channels, and also using triangle diagrams to see the consistency of the approach.

The issue of the strong decay of the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
has also received attention in the literature.
In Ref.
Guo et al. 2006
, a small decay width of
8.7\textrm{ keV}
was obtained from the consideration of
\eta-\pi^{0}
mixing.
But in Ref.
Faessler et al. 2007
it was shown that the consideration of the different mass of neutral and charged kaons was important and resulted in much bigger widths.
In addition, electromagnetic corrections were considered in Ref.
Guo et al. 2008
, and the results were updated in Ref.
Liu et al. 2013
.
Further work was done in Ref.
Fajfer and Prapotnik Brdnik 2015
and Ref.
Guo et al. 2018
.
From inspection of all these works one can see that there is a large span in the predictions for the decay width, from
2.4\textrm{ keV}
to
180\textrm{ keV}
.
More recently a work considering coupled channels
D^{+}K^{0},D^{0}K^{+},D_{s}^{+}\eta
and
D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0}
has been conducted
Achasov and Shestakov 2025
and within uncertainties of the model, results for the width in line with those in Ref.
Liu et al. 2013
are obtained.
A different approach is followed in Ref.
Yue et al. 2025
, where information from the
T_{c\bar{s}0}^{a}(2327)
state is used to find a bracket of values
[63-209]\textrm{ keV}
for the strong decay width.
It is worth having a fresh look of the problem, and since the experimental work of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
only provides the ratio of the radiative width to the strong one, the consideration of the two decay modes with the same framework, as we do here, becomes advisable.

II
formalism

II.1
Strong decay
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+}\to D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0}

We tackle first this problem and follow the approach of Ref.
Ikeno et al. 2023
, using the local hidden gauge approach for the interaction with the exchange of vector mesons
Bando et al. 1985
;
Bando et al. 1988
;
Meissner 1988
;
Nagahiro et al. 2009
.
In Ref.
Ikeno et al. 2023
, the isospin conserving channels
D^{0}K^{+},D^{+}K^{0},D_{s}^{+}\eta
channels were considered, and the vector exchanged were the light vectors
\rho,\omega,K^{*}

Here we also consider the exchange of heavy vectors and add
D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0}
to the coupled channels.
While
D_{s}^{+}\pi^{0}
does not couple to the
DK
isospin
I=0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(D^{+}K^{0}+D^{0}K^{+})

D_{s0}^{*}(2317)

This source of isospin violation was early identified in Ref.
Achasov et al. 1979

Guo et al. 2008
;
Liu et al. 2013
;
Guo et al. 2018
;
Achasov and Shestakov 2025

IV.2
Radiative decays

If we consider the charge current terms of Fig.
3
ignoring the contact terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(\text{current})}_{D_{s0}^{*}\rightarrow D_{s}^{*+}\gamma}=0.55\textrm{ keV}.

The result of Eq. (

Gamermann et al. 2007b

In the evaluation of Ref.

the couplings to the
D^{0}K^{+}
and
D^{+}K^{0}
were considered equal, and furthermore the couplings obtained there were a bit smaller
g_{D_{s0}^{*},\,D^{0}K^{+}}=7358

Considering the ratio of couplings, the rate of Ref.

could reach the value of
0.6\textrm{ keV}
, still small compared to the number of Eq. (
60

To better interpret the results let us quote that Ref.
Cleven et al. 2014
is updated in Ref.
Fu et al. 2022
, Ref.
Guo et al. 2008
in Ref.
Liu et al. 2013
and Ref.
Lutz and Soyeur 2008
in Ref.
Guo et al. 2018
.
We can see that the molecular picture produces results for the strong decay of the order of
100\textrm{ keV}
.
On the other hand, models based on a
c\bar{s}
quark component have larger uncertainties, but are roughly one order of magnitude smaller.
When it comes to the radiative decay, the results based on
c\bar{s}
quark model calculations show large variations, as much as one order of magnitude, and are generally smaller than the results coming from the molecular picture, but similar.
Our calculation for the radiative decay included the contribution of anomalous terms, not considered in other approaches, but we could prove that their global contribution was negligible.

If one looks at the ratio of the radiative to strong decay widths from the molecular picture, one finds in all cases ratios below
3\%
, which would be in conflict with the experimental results of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
of
7\%
.
Our calculation provides a ratio of
1.9
, in line with other results and also smaller than the experimental ratio.
One could invoke that from lattice QCD results one induces that the molecular picture has a strength or about
70\%
of the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)^{+}
state
Martínez Torres et al. 2015
.
To account for that one could reduce the couplings of the resonance to the molecular components, but this would occur both in the strong and the radiative decays and the ratio would be the same.
One can argue differently and say one could reduce the molecular contribution by
30\%
and add a
30\%
of the smaller contribution from a
q\bar{q}
state.
Since the radiative widths are similar in all cases, this would reduce the strong decay leaving the radiative decay the same and the ratio would increase by
25\%
. Yet, this would not solve the problem in any case.
On the other hand, the dispersion of results for the quark models, both in the radiative and strong decays, does not allow us to produce a meaningful ratio.
In a recent work
Fu et al. 2025
the ratio is evaluated taking results from the molecular picture for the strong decay
Liu et al. 2013
and using experimental values of the ratio
R_{2}={\rm Br}(D_{s1}(2460)\to\gamma D_{s})/{\rm Br}(D_{s1}(2460)\to\pi D^{*}_{s})
.
The ratio varies from
0.01
to
0.04
depending on the values of
R_{2}
from different experiments.
At this point it is also worth mentioning that the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
still quotes a value for the ratio smaller than
0.059
based on the experiment of
Besson et al. 2003
, a results that is in conflict with the precise value from the Belle experiment
Abumusabh et al. 2025
.
Although the numbers are similar, it will be useful to have more accurate result in the future.
But, better than a ratio, what would help clarify the present theoretical debate would be to have precise measurements for the strong and the radiative decays independently.
We are looking forward to this scenario in the future.
Meanwhile, the present results, with the new methods of calculation, the combined study of the strong and radiative decays within the same framework, and the consistency with modern formulation of the molecular picture and the interaction of the their components, should be a valuable information to compare with these results when they are available.

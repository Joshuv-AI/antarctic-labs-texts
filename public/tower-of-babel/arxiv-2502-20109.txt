New q q -identities Via q q -Derivative of Basic Hypergeometric Series with Respect to Parameters

Abstract
In this paper, we use the effect of the q q -differential and deformed q q -exponential operators on basic hypergeometric series to find new q q -identities from the q q -Gauss sum, the q q -Chu-Vandermonde’s sum, and Jackson’s transformation formula.

Introduction and Motivation

Throughout this paper, we take
0<|q|<1
. The
q
-differential operator
D_{q}
is defined by:

D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{x}

and the Leibniz rule for
D_{q}

D_{q}^{n}\{f(x)g(x)\}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k(k-n)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}D_{q}^{k}\{f(x)\}D_{q}^{n-k}\{g(q^{k}x)\},

(1)

where the
q
-binomial coefficient is defined by

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}},

and the
q
-shifted factorial of
a
is given by
(a;q)_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{k}a),\text{ for }n=1,2,\ldots,

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}).

Following Gasper and Rahman
[
4
]
, the
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
basic hypergeometric series is defined by

{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{array};q,z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}\Big[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\Big]^{1+s-r}z^{n}

where

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{n}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n}.

Equivalently,

(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{\infty}=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{\infty}.

In
[
1
]
, we define the deformed basic hypergeometric series
{}_{r}\Phi_{s}
as

{}_{r}\Phi_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{array};q,u,z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u^{\binom{n}{2}}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}]^{1+s-r}z^{n}.

(2)

If
u=1
, then
{}_{r}\Phi_{s}={}_{r}\phi_{s}
. If
u=q
,

{}_{r+1}\Phi_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r},0\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\end{array};q,q,z\right)={}_{r}\phi_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{r}\end{array};q,-z\right).

In this paper, we will frequently use the following identities:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

(3)

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n+k}

\displaystyle=(a;q)_{n}(aq^{n};q)_{k},

(4)

\displaystyle(aq^{k};q)_{n-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a;q)_{k}}{(a;q)_{i}},

(5)

\displaystyle(aq^{n};q)_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a;q)_{k}(aq^{k};q)_{n}}{(a;q)_{n}}.

(6)

In addition, we will use the identities for binomial coefficients:

\displaystyle\binom{n+k}{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{2}+\binom{k}{2}+nk,

\displaystyle\binom{n-k}{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{2}+\binom{k}{2}+k(1-n).

This paper provides new
q
-identities by applying the
q
-differential and deformed
q
-exponential operators to the
q
-Gauss sum, the
q
-Chu-Vandermonde’s sum, and Jackson’s transformation formula. These q-identities have the following quotient forms of the q-shifted factorials

\frac{1}{(c;q)_{n}},\ \ \frac{x^{n}}{(c;q)_{n}},\ \ \frac{(ac;q)_{n}}{(bc;q)_{n}},\text{ and }\frac{(ac,bc;q)_{n}}{(dc,ec;q)_{n}}.

The method will consist of applying the operators
D_{q}
and
\mathrm{T}(yD_{q}|u)
, with respect to the parameter
c
, to both sides of the above
q
-identities and then using the identities Eqs.(
3
)-(
6
). Something similar was done in
[
7
]
.

Some identities of the
q
-differential operator

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the identities:

\displaystyle D_{q}^{k}x^{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n-k}}x^{n-k},&\text{ if }k\leq n;\\
0,&\text{ if }k>n.\end{cases}

(7)

\displaystyle D_{q}^{k}(ax;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}(-a)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}}\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n-k}}\frac{(ax;q)_{n}}{(ax;q)_{k}},&\text{ if }k\leq n;\\
0,&\text{ if }k>n.\end{cases}

(8)

Theorem 1
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

D_{q}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{1-ax}\right)=\frac{(q;q)_{n}a^{n}}{(ax;q)_{n+1}}.

(9)

Proof.

Note that

D_{q}\left(\frac{1}{1-ax}\right)=\frac{(1-q)a}{(1-ax)(1-aqx)}=\frac{(q;q)_{1}a}{(ax;q)_{2}}.

(10)

Suppose that Eq.(
9
) is true for
n
and let us prove by induction for
n+1
. As

D_{q}(ax;q)_{n+1}=-(1-q^{n+1})a(aqx;q)_{n},

(11)

then

\displaystyle D_{q}^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{1-ax}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{q}D_{q}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{1-ax}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{q}\left(\frac{(q;q)_{n}a^{n}}{(ax;q)_{n+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(q;q)_{n}a^{n}D_{q}(ax;q)_{n+1}}{(ax;q)_{n+1}(aqx;q)_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{n+1}(aqx;q)_{n}a^{n+1}}{(ax;q)_{n+1}(aqx;q)_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{n+1}a^{n+1}}{(1-q^{n+1}xa)(ax;q)_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{n+1}a^{n+1}}{(ax;q)_{n+2}}.

The proof is reached.
∎

Theorem 2
.

D_{q}^{m}\left\{\frac{1}{(ax;q)_{n}}\right\}=\frac{(q^{n};q)_{m}a^{m}}{(ax;q)_{m+n}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle D_{q}^{m}\left\{\frac{1}{(ax;q)_{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a^{n-1}(q;q)_{n-1}}D_{q}^{m+n-1}\left\{\frac{1}{1-ax}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a^{n-1}(q;q)_{n-1}}\frac{(q;q)_{m+n-1}a^{m+n-1}}{(ax;q)_{m+n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{n};q)_{m}a^{m}}{(ax;q)_{m+n}}.

∎

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}q^{i(i-k)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}_{q}\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n-i}}x^{n-i}\frac{(q^{n};q)_{k-i}(aq^{i})^{k-i}}{(aq^{i}x;q)_{n+k-i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(ax;q)_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}_{q}\frac{(q^{n};q)_{k-i}(ax;q)_{i}a^{k-i}}{(q;q)_{n-i}(aq^{n}x;q)_{k}}x^{n-i}.

∎

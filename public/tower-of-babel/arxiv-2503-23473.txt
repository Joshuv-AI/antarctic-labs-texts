Heterogeneous Stirling numbers and heterogeneous Bell polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. This paper introduces a novel generalization of Stirling and Lah numbers, termed “heterogeneous Stirling numbers,” which smoothly interpolate between these classical combinatorial sequences. Specifically, we define heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second and first kinds, demonstrating their convergence to standard Stirling numbers as λ → 0 \lambda\rightarrow 0 and to (signed) Lah numbers as λ → 1 \lambda\rightarrow 1 . We derive fundamental properties, including generating functions, explicit formulas, and recurrence relations. Furthermore, we extend these concepts to heterogeneous Bell polynomials, obtaining analogous results such as generating function, combinatorial identity and Dobinski-like formula. Finally, we introduce and analyse heterogeneous r r -Stirling numbers of the second kind and their associated r r -Bell polynomials.

Introduction

This paper introduces the heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind, denoted by
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
(defined in (
13

{n\brace k}
(obtained as
\lambda\rightarrow 0

\lambda\rightarrow 1

G_{\lambda}(n,k)
(defined in (

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0

(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)
as
\lambda\rightarrow 1

H_{\lambda}(n,k)
, we introduce the heterogeneous Bell polynomials,
H_{n,\lambda}(x)
(defined in (
25
)), and obtain their generating function, a combinatorial identity, and a Dobinski-like formula. Furthermore, we extend these concepts to the

-case, defining heterogeneous

r

The falling factorial sequence is defined by (see [4,5,9-15])

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1),

and the rising factorial sequence is given by

\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate falling factorial sequence is defined by (see [16])

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1),

and the degenerate rising factorial sequence is given by

\langle x\rangle_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)(x+2\lambda)\cdots\big(x+(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}(x)_{n,\lambda}=\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x^{n},\ \lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}(x)_{n,\lambda}=(x)_{n},\ \lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\langle x\rangle_{n}.

The degenerate exponentials are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9-16]).

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,4,6,8,18,19]).

The unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

\displaystyle{n\brack k}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k),\quad(n\geq k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,16,19]),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brack k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

The Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,4,6,8,18,19]).

The Lah numbers are defined by

(2)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\ (k\geq 0),\ (\mathrm{see}\ [6,11,12,19]).

Thus, by (
2
), we get

(3)

L(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

The Bell polynomials are given by

(4)

\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,13,14,16,19]).

When
x=1
,
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}(1)
are called the Bell numbers, (see [1,20]).

Thus, by (
4
), we get

(5)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,13,14,16,19]).

From (
5
), we obtain the following Dobinski’s formula:

(6)

\phi_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!}x^{k}.

The Lah-Bell polynomials are defined by

(7)

e^{x\big(\frac{1}{1-t}-1\big)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LB}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,11,12,17,19]).

When
x=1
,
LB_{n}=LB_{n}(1)
are called the Lah-Bell numbers.

From (
7
), we derive the next Dobinski-like formula:

(8)

LB_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n}}{k!}x^{k}.

Thus, by (
2
) and (
7
), we get

\mathrm{LB}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}L(n,k),\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,12]).

Let
\log_{\lambda}t
be the compositional inverse function of
e_{\lambda}(t)
. Then we have

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}\lambda^{n-1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9]),

and

e_{\lambda}\log_{\lambda}t=\log_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(t)=t.

The unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

(9)

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}{n\brack k}_{\lambda},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

Inversion of (
9
) gives the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

(10)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,14-17]),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

The degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by

(11)

\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,15,16]).

Thus, by (
10
) and (
11
), we get

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(12)

(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

Inversion of (
12
) yields the unsigned degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

\langle x+r\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

2.
Heterogeneous Stirling numbers and heterogeneous Bell polynomials

In this section, we introduce and investigate the heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
and of the first kind
G_{\lambda}(n,k)
, along with a polynomial extension of
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
, the heterogeneous Bell polynomials
H_{n,\lambda}(x)
.

We define the
heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind
by

(13)

\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

As usual, we define
H_{\lambda}(n,k)=0
,   for
k<0
or
n<k
.
Note that

(14)

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{\lambda}(n,k)={n\brace k},\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H_{\lambda}(n,k)=L(n,k),\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

From (
13
), we note that

(15)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{-x}(-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}(x)_{k}.

On the other hand, by binomial theorem, we get

(16)

e_{\lambda}^{-x}(-t)=\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1+1\big)^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)^{k}(x)_{k}.

Therefore, by (
15
) and (
16
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
k\geq 0
, the generating function of heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second kind is given by

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
15
) and Theorem 2.1, we note that

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}e_{\lambda}^{-j}(-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle j\rangle_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by Theorem 2.1 and (
17
), we get

(18)

\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle j\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}H_{\lambda}(n,k),&\textrm{if $n\geq k$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $0\leq n<k$.}\end{array}\right.

Therefore, by (
18
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle j\rangle_{n,\lambda}.

Remark 2.3
.

In Theorem 2.2, letting
\lambda\rightarrow 1
, and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
respectively yield

\displaystyle L(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle j\rangle_{n},

\displaystyle{n\brace k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}j^{n}.

Now, we consider the
heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the first kind
which are defined by

(19)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

(20)

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}G_{\lambda}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}G_{\lambda}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k),\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

From (
19
), we have

(21)

\displaystyle(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}.

On the other hand, by binomial expansion, we get

(22)

\displaystyle(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=e_{-\lambda}^{x}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,-\lambda}}{k!}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)\Big)^{k}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
21
) and (
22
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
k\geq 0
, the generating function of heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the first kind is given by

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
13
), we note that

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}H_{\lambda}(n+1,k)(x)_{k}=\langle x\rangle_{n+1,\lambda}=\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}(x+n\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}(x-k+k+n\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(k+n\lambda)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}H_{\lambda}(n,k-1)(x)_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)(k+n\lambda)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big(H_{\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k+n\lambda)H_{\lambda}(n,k)\Big)(x)_{k}.

Therefore, by comparing coefficients on both sides of (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

H_{\lambda}(n+1,k)=H_{\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k+n\lambda)H_{\lambda}(n,k).

Remark 2.6
.

In Theorem 2.5, letting
\lambda\rightarrow 1
, and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
respectively give

\displaystyle L(n+1,k)=L(n,k-1)+(k+n)L(n,k),

\displaystyle{n+1\brace k}={n\brace k-1}+k{n\brace k}.

By (
19
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}G_{\lambda}(n+1,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}=(x)_{n+1}=(x)_{n}(x-n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}(x+k\lambda-n-k\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k+1,\lambda}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-n-k\lambda)G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}G_{\lambda}(n,k-1)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}(n+k\lambda)G_{\lambda}(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big(G_{\lambda}(n,k-1)-(n+k\lambda)G_{\lambda}(n,k)\Big)\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
24
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

G_{\lambda}(n+1,k)=G_{\lambda}(n,k-1)-(n+k\lambda)G_{\lambda}(n,k).

Remark 2.8
.

In Theorem 2.7, letting
\lambda\rightarrow 1
, and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
respectively give

\displaystyle L(n+1,k)=L(n,k-1)+(n+k)L(n,k),

\displaystyle S_{1}(n+1,k)=S_{1}(n,k-1)-nS_{1}(n,k).

In view of (
11
), we define the
heterogeneous Bell polynomials
by

(25)

H_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

When
x=1
,
H_{n,\lambda}=H_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the
heterogeneous Bell numbers
.

By Theorem 2.1 and (
25
), we get

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}x{{}^{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\Big)^{k}x{{}^{k}}

\displaystyle=e^{x(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}.

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

The generating function of the heterogeneous Bell polynomials is given by

e^{x(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From Theorem 2.1, (
1
) and (
2
), we note that

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big((1-\lambda t)^{-1/\lambda}-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)}-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}\lambda^{-l}\frac{1}{l!}\Big(-\log(1-\lambda t)\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}\lambda^{-l}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brack l}\lambda^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}{n\brack l}\lambda^{n-l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

H_{\lambda}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}{n\brack l}\lambda^{n-l}.

Remark 2.11
.

In Theorem 2.10, letting
\lambda\rightarrow 1
yields

(28)

L(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}{n\brack l}.

From (
10
), we note that

(29)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Replacing
t
by
\log_{\lambda}\big(\frac{1}{1-t}\big)
in (
29
) and using (
2
), we have

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{1}{l!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{1}{l!}\Big(-\log_{-\lambda}(1-t)\Big)^{l}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{\lambda}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brack l}_{-\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{\lambda}{n\brack l}_{-\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.12
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

L(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{\lambda}{n\brack l}_{-\lambda}.

Equivalently, we have

\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}=\frac{1}{(n-k)!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}_{\lambda}{n\brack l}_{-\lambda},\quad(\textnormal{see}\,\,\eqref{3}).

Replacing
t
by
-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)
in Theorem 2.1 and using (
2
) and (
9
), we get

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brack l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}H_{\lambda}(l,k){n\brack l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

L(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}H_{\lambda}(l,k){n\brack l}_{\lambda}.

Equivalently, we have

\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}=\frac{1}{(n-k)!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}H_{\lambda}(l,k){n\brack l}_{\lambda}\quad(\textnormal{see}\,\,\eqref{3}).

Remark 2.14
.

Letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in Theorem 2.13 yields the same identity as in (
28
):

L(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}{l\brace k}{n\brack l}.

From Theorem 2.9, we note that

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{-x}e^{xe_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)}=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}e_{\lambda}^{-k}(-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
32
), we obtain the following Dobinski-like theorem.

Theorem 2.15
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n,\lambda}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}.

Remark 2.16
.

In Theorem 2.15, letting
\lambda\rightarrow 1
, and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
respectively give

\mathrm{LB}_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n}}{k!}x^{k},\quad\phi_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!}x^{k},\quad(\textnormal{see}\,\,\eqref{6},\,\eqref{8}).

From Theorem 2.9, we note that

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+m,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=e^{y\frac{d}{dx}}e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)-1}.

Now, we observe that

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n,\lambda}}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{m}x^{n}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n,\lambda}}{n!}(x+y)^{n}=e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x-y)-1}.

Thus, by (
33
) and (
34
), we get

(35)

\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+m,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=e^{y\frac{d}{dx}}e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)-1}=e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x-y)-1}.

By (
1
), we get

(36)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x-y)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\lambda(x+y)\Big)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}=\big(1-\lambda x-\lambda y\big)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\big(1-\lambda x\big)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)e_{\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg).

From (
35
) and (
36
) and using Theorems 2.1 and 2.9, we have

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}H_{m+n,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\frac{y^{m}}{m!}=e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)-1}e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}\big(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\big)-1\big)}

\displaystyle=e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{-y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)-1\bigg)^{j}e_{\lambda}^{-j}(-x)

\displaystyle=e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=j}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\frac{1}{m!}\bigg(\frac{y}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)^{m}e_{\lambda}^{-j}(-x)

\displaystyle=e^{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-x)-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\frac{y^{m}}{m!}e_{\lambda}^{-(m\lambda+j)}(-x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k,\lambda}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\frac{y^{m}}{m!}e_{\lambda}^{-(m\lambda+j)}(-x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k,\lambda}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\frac{y^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\langle m\lambda+j\rangle_{l,\lambda}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\frac{y^{m}}{m!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}\langle m\lambda+j\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}\langle m\lambda+j\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{y^{m}}{m!}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.17
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

H_{m+n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{\lambda}(m,j)H_{k,\lambda}\langle m\lambda+j\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}.

Remark 2.18
.

In Theorem 2.17, letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, and letting
\lambda\rightarrow 1
respectively give

\displaystyle\phi_{m+n}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}\binom{n}{k}\phi_{k}j^{n-k},

\displaystyle\mathrm{LB}_{n+m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(m,j)\binom{n}{k}\mathrm{LB}_{k}\langle m+j\rangle_{n-k},

where the first one is due to Spivey (see [21]).

3.
Further remarks

In this section, we study the heterogeneous
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind,
H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)
, and the heterogeneous
r
-Bell polynomials,
H_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
, respectively as
r
-analogues of
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
and
H_{n,\lambda}(x)
. In this section, we use the following notations for operators:

D=\frac{d}{dx},\quad xD=x\frac{d}{dx}.

From Theorem 2.15, we note that

(38)

\displaystyle\langle xD\rangle_{n,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\langle xD\rangle_{n,\lambda}x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}

\displaystyle=e^{x}\bigg(e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle k\rangle_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}\bigg)=e^{x}H_{n,\lambda}(x).

By (
38
), we get

(39)

\displaystyle H_{n+m,\lambda}(x)e^{x}=\langle xD\rangle_{m+n,\lambda}e^{x}=\langle xD+m\lambda\rangle_{n,\lambda}\langle xD\rangle_{m,\lambda}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\langle xD+m\lambda\rangle_{n,\lambda}\Big(H_{m,\lambda}(x)e^{x}\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\Big[\langle xD\rangle_{k,\lambda}e^{x}\Big]\Big[\langle xD+m\lambda\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}H_{m,\lambda}(x)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}(x)\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{\lambda}(m,j)\langle j+m\lambda\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}e^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}(x)H_{\lambda}(m,j)\langle j+m\lambda\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}e^{x}.

Therefore, by (
39
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

H_{n+m,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}(x)H_{\lambda}(m,j)\langle j+m\lambda\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}x^{j}.

For sufficiently nice functions
f
, the Fourier inversion theorem says:

(40)

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}(t)e^{ixt}dt,

where
\hat{f}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-ixt}dx
.

From (
38
) and (
40
), we note that

(41)

\displaystyle\langle xD\rangle_{n,\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}(t)\langle xD\rangle_{n,\lambda}e^{ixt}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}(t)e^{ixt}H_{n,\lambda}(ixt)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(it)^{k}\hat{f}(t)e^{ixt}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}\bigg[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}(t)e^{ixt}dt\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}D^{k}f(x).

By (
41
), we get the following normal ordering result:

\langle xD\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}D^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
heterogeneous
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
by

(42)

\langle x+r\rangle_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

(43)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{-(x+r)}(-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle x+r\rangle_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)(x)_{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}(x)_{k}.

On the other hand, by binomial expansion, we get

(44)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{-(x+r)}(-t)

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{(-r)}(-t)e_{\lambda}^{-x}(-t)=e_{\lambda}^{-r}(-t)\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1+1\Big)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\Big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{-r}(-t)(x)_{k}.

By (
43
) and (
44
), we get

(45)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1\Big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{-r}(-t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
42
), we easily get

(46)

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+1+r,k+r)

\displaystyle=H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r-1)

\displaystyle+(k+r+n\lambda)H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r),

where
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We define the
heterogeneous
r
-Bell polynomials
as

(47)

H_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}H_{\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r),\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
45
) and (
47
), we note that

(48)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(-t)-1)}e_{\lambda}^{-r}(-t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=1,\ H_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}=H_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(1)
are called
heterogeneous
r
-Bell numbers
.

Finally, we observe that

(49)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n+1,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}x^{k}H_{\lambda}(n+1,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}H_{\lambda}(n+1,k+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, from (
49
), we have

(50)

\displaystyle H_{n+1,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}H_{\lambda}(n+1,k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}H_{\lambda}(n+1,k)x^{k}\quad(n\geq 0).

4.
Conclusion

In conclusion, this paper successfully introduced and explored the heterogeneous Stirling numbers of the second and first kinds,
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
and
G_{\lambda}(n,k)
, respectively, along with a polynomial extension of
H_{\lambda}(n,k)
, the heterogeneous Bell polynomials
H_{n,\lambda}(x)
, and their
r
-analogues. These new number sequences and polynomials provide a unifying framework that generalizes both the classical Stirling numbers and the Lah numbers, seamlessly transitioning between these well-established combinatorial objects as the parameter
\lambda
varies.

We derived fundamental properties, including generating functions, explicit expressions, and recurrence relations, for each of these newly defined sequences. The establishment of a Dobinski-like formula for the heterogeneous Bell polynomials and the discovery of combinatorial identities further enriched our understanding of their combinatorial significance. Moreover, the extension to the
r
-case demonstrated the robustness and versatility of these definitions, opening avenues for further exploration in related areas.

Future research could focus on exploring deeper combinatorial interpretations, connections to other special functions, and applications in various scientific and engineering disciplines. The heterogeneous Stirling and Bell numbers, with their rich mathematical structure, hold promise for further discoveries and applications.

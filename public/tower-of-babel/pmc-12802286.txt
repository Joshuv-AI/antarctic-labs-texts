Correlation between Interleukin-6, C-reactive protein, and lactate dehydrogenase levels and macrolide-resistant severe mycoplasma pneumoniae infection in children
Authors: Mingchen Zhang, Dong Liu, Yuanyuan Song, Jie Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background In recent years, macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MRMP) has been increasingly reported worldwide, with a macrolide resistance rate of >90% in China , leading to a rise in severe Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia(MPP) incidence annually, which poses a significant threat to children’s health. This study aimed to explore the predictive value of Interleukin-6, C-reactive protein, and lactate dehydrogenase levels in macrolide-resistant severe Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia infection (macrolide-resistant SMPP) in children and provide a reference for its early diagnosis. MethodsThe clinical data of 208 children with macrolide-resistant MPP, who were hospitalized in the Department of Pediatrics of Zibo Central Hospital between January and September 2024, were retrospectively analyzed. Based on disease severity, the patients were divided into a macrolide-resistant severe group (n = 119) and a macrolide-resistant general (n = 89) group. The differences in the general condition and laboratory indicators between the two groups were compared. In addition, statistical analyses were performed, and indicators with statistical significance were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn to analyze the diagnostic value of laboratory indices for macrolide-resistant SMPP.ResultsThe levels of IL-6, CRP, LDH, erythrocyte sedimentation rate and fibrinogen were higher in the severe macrolide-resistant group than in the normal group (P < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that IL-6 (odds ratio [OR] = 1.200, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.130–1.274), CRP (OR = 1.107, 95% CI 1.039–1.178), and LDH (OR = 1.012, 95% CI 1.005–1.019) levels were independent indicators for macrolide-resistant SMPP (P < 0.05). ROC curve analysis showed that both IL-6 and the combination of IL-6 with LDH provided high diagnostic value for SMPP, with the combined approach showing best predictive value.The AUC of IL-6 was 0.830, the best cut-off value was 11.75pg/ml, the sensitivity was 0.748, and the specificity was 0.845. The combination of IL-6 and LDH in the diagnosis of drug-resistant SMPP showed that the AUC was 0.870, the sensitivity was 0.807, and the specificity was 0.809.ConclusionsIL-6, CRP and LDH are independent indicators for macrolide-resistant SMPP. The combination of IL-6 and LDH is the best predictor of macrolide-resistant SMPP.

Background

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is a common pathogen that causes community pneumonia in children [1]. In recent years, macrolide-resistant MP has been increasingly reported worldwide, with a macrolide resistance rate of >90% in China [2]. At present, more relevant studies are focused on the Ⅴ and Ⅱ regions of the 23 S rRNA and genetic mutations in the ribosomal proteins L4 and L22 that play a role in the macrolide-resistant mechanism. The macrolide-resistant gene mutations were mainly found in the ribosomal 50 S subunit of the 23 S rRNA V region in MP, and the related gene mutation sites were 2063, 2064, 2067 and 2617 [3]. In China, the most commonly found mutation is A2063G, followed by A2064G and A2067G, with a detection rate of >99% in Beijing [4]. Patients with macrolide-resistant severe Mycoplasma pneumoniae (SMPP) have severe clinical symptoms, rapid disease progression, and are prone to pulmonary and extrapulmonary complications. In severe cases, they may even have sequelae such as bronchiolitis obliterans. Therefore, this study focused on the correlation between interleukin-6 (IL-6), C-reactive protein (CRP), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels and macrolide-resistant SMPP, by comparing the clinical characteristics and laboratory test data of children with MPP between the macrolide-resistant severe group and the macrolide-resistant general group.

Data and methods

Research objective

This study assessed the clinical data of 208 children with macrolide-resistant MPP who were admitted to the pediatric department of Zibo Central Hospital between January and September of 2024. According to the severity of their condition, they were divided into a macrolide-resistant severe group (119 cases) and a macrolide-resistant general group (89 cases). Inclusion criteria were the following: (1) Age between 6 months and 14 years; (2) The diagnostic criteria for both the severe and the general group comply with the “Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines for Community Acquired Pneumonia in Children (2019 edition)” [5] and the “Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines for Mycoplasma Pneumonia in Children (2023 edition)” [6]; and (3) Throat swabs are positive for MP polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based detection of the A2063G/A20634G gene locus mutation of the resistance gene 23 S rRNA. Exclusion criteria were the following: Children admitted during the recovery period of pneumonia (with stable body temperature for >1 week and no clinical symptoms); those with underlying diseases such as congenital heart disease, pulmonary developmental abnormalities, immunodeficiency diseases, genetic metabolic diseases, and so on; and those with incomplete clinical data. This study was approved by the medical ethics committee of Zibo Central Hospital.

Diagnostic criteria for severe Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia

Meet any of the following conditions:

1) Continuous high fever (above 39 ℃) ≥ 5 days or fever ≥ 7 days, without a declining trend; 2) Wheezing, shortness of breath, dyspnea, chest pain, hemoptysis, etc. These manifestations were related to the severity of the lesion, plastic bronchitis, asthma exacerbation, pleural effusion and pulmonary embolism; 3) Extrapulmonary complications occur, but do not reach critical illness criteria (critical illness refers to respiratory failure and/or life-threatening severe extrapulmonary complications, requiring life support such as mechanical ventilation); 4) Oxygen saturation ≤ 93% on room air at rest; 5) One of the following imaging manifestations:

Involvement of ≥ 2/3 of a single lung with uniform and consistent high-density consolidation, or high-density consolidation in ≥ 2 lung lobes (regardless of the size of the affected area), which may be accompanied by moderate-to-large pleural effusion or localized bronchiolitis manifestations; Diffuse single-lung or bilateral involvement of ≥ 4/5 lung lobes with bronchiolitis manifestations, which can be combined with bronchitis and accompanied by the formation of mucus plugs leading to atelectasis;6) Clinical symptoms are progressively worsening, and imaging shows a lesion exceeds 50% within 24–48 h.

Detection of resistance gene loci in the V region of 23rsRNA

The MP nucleic acid and drug resistance mutation site detection kit (fluorescent PCR method) of Jiangsu Mole Biotechnology Co., Ltd. was used to detect the macrolide-resistant gene loci A2063G and A2064G in the 23SrRNA of MP in throat swabs of the children. If any locus was positive, it was interpreted as a case of drug-resistant MP infection.

Clinical data collection

The general information of patients in the macrolide-resistant severe and macrolide-resistant general groups was recorded separately. This included age, sex, duration of fever, length of hospital stay, cough onset time, the ratio of switching to doxycycline after 3 days of ineffective intravenous azithromycin therapy, the ratio of intravenous glucocorticoid(methylprednisolone) use, the ratio of flexible bronchoscopy performed, and pulmonary rales. Laboratory indicators were detected within 4–7 days of the onset of clinical symptoms and within 24 h of hospitalization. Laboratory tests included white blood cells (WBC) count, and levels of CRP, serum LDH, plasma D-dimer (D-D), procalcitonin (PCT), IL-6, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), myocardial enzyme (CK), alanine aminotransferase(ALT) and fibrinogen(FIB). Imaging examination included chest computed tomography, for assessing the presence or absence of pleural or pericardial effusion, lung consolidation, and the extent of pulmonary inflammatory infiltration.

Statistical analysis

The SPSS 26.00 statistical software was used to analyze the data. The non-normally distributed measurement data are expressed as the median (M) (P25–P75), and the rank-sum test was used for comparison between groups. Statistical data are expressed as percentages of cases. The χ2 test was used for comparison between groups. Logistic regression analysis was used to analyze factors influencing macrolide-resistant SMPP. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn to predict the diagnostic value of the indicators affecting the occurrence of macrolide-resistant SMPP. Taking the area under the ROC curve (AUC) as the evaluation criterion, the cutoff value was determined when the Youden index reached the maximum value, and the sensitivity and specificity at this cutoff value were calculated. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Treatment and outcomes

Among the 89 patients in the general group, 62 improved and were discharged after approximately one week of intravenous azithromycin therapy. 27 patients whose symptoms did not relieve after 3 days of azithromycin treatment were treated with levofloxacin/doxycycline for about 1 week, and were discharged from hospital. In the severe group (119 patients), 63 accepted azithromycin for one week without changing medication. Among the 56 severe patients whose symptoms did not ease after 3 days of macrolide antibiotic therapy, treatment was switched to levofloxacin/doxycycline for approximately one week. In addition, some patients were accepted glucocorticoid (methylprednisolone); Bronchoscopy and alveolar lavage were performed for severe lung consolidation, atelectasis and long course of disease. 5 required conventional oxygen therapy, 2 received high-flow oxygen therapy, and 1 patient accepted mechanical ventilation. All patients recovered and were discharged, with a mortality rate of 0%. Follow-up evaluations of macrolide-resistant SMPP patients at 1-month and 3-month intervals showed that there were no chronic pulmonary complications such as lung injury, pulmonary fibrosis or obliterative bronchiolitis.

Discussion

In recent years, the incidence of macrolide-resistant mycobacterium pneumonia (MRMP) has increased significantly and become a global problem. China, Japan, and South Korea have the highest rates of drug resistance in the world [3]. The incidence of MRMP in China is one of the highest in the world, but the resistance rates differs greatly between regions [7]. The MRMP rate in Beijing is >90%, and the A2063G mutation accounts for 99.0% of the cases [8]. Excessive or unreasonable use of antibiotics can exert selection pressure for the development of antimicrobial resistance. In different regions, differences in antibiotic use habits may lead to differences in drug resistance rates. In addition, samples from hospitalized patients may show inhibition of sensitive bacteria because of the use of antibiotics. In addition, the lack of uniform minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) threshold value standards and the different substrate components of the drug sensitivity test may affect the judgment results [9]. MRMP reduces the efficacy of macrolide antibiotics. For macrolide-sensitive MP (MSMP) infection, the efficacy rate of MSMP was 91.5%; however, it was only 22.7% for MRMP. Studies have shown that 23 S rRNA mutations are closely related to MP resistance. Macrolides, as bacteriostatic agents, can bind to the target nucleotide of 23 S rRNA in the 50 S subunit of the ribosome, and block the transpeptidase center and narrow part of the peptide output channel. Thus, the A→P shift of peptidyl tRNA is prevented, leading to inhibition of protein synthesis [10]. Mutations in the 23 S rRNA target genes hinder drug binding, resulting in decreased drug efficacy. The incidence of macrolide-resistant MPP increases with increasing incidence of MPP. To avoid the abuse of antibiotics and reduce the occurrence of complications, it is necessary to promptly diagnose resistant SMPP.

In total, 208 children with macrolide-resistant MPP were included in this study. Compared with the general group, fever duration was longer in the drug-resistant severe group. In addition, the proportion of second-line drug use was higher, and the levels of ESR, CRP, LDH, FIB, and IL-6 were higher. This suggests that the inflammatory response in children with macrolide-resistant SMPP is more intense and is more likely to cause abnormal immune function. This study showed that IL-6, CRP, and LDH were independent indicators affecting the occurrence of macrolide-resistant SMPP. Moreover, IL-6 had a moderate predictive value for macrolide-resistant SMPP. CRP levels are significantly increased in bacterial infections and normal or decreased in viral infection [11]. However, in severe virus infections, CRP levels can also be elevated due to excessive inflammatory response, such as in infection cases of hand-foot-mouth virus, COVID-19, and so on [12]– [13]. Mycoplasma are microorganisms that exist somewhere between viruses and bacteria in microbial classification charts. Studies [14]– [15] have shown that an increase in CRP is also correlated with the severity of SMPP, and the higher the CRP value, the stronger is the inflammatory response. In this study, CRP levels were significantly increased in patients with macrolide-resistant SMPP, suggesting a strong inflammatory reaction to macrolide-resistant SMPP. Serum CRP is an independent indicator for macrolide-resistant SMPP. ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of CRP in predicting macrolide-resistant SMPP was 0.716. However, its sensitivity and specificity were relatively low (0.681 and 0.640, respectively), and it is insufficient to serve as an independent predictor.

The LDH level is an important index in myocardial zymograms. When tissue injury occurs, LDH is released into the blood and the serum LDH level increases. Therefore, serum LDH levels can be used as an important indicator to evaluate the severity of cell damage and related diseases [16]. In patients with MPP, LDH levels indicate the severity of the inflammatory response, predict SMPP, and can be used as a reference index for the use of glucocorticoids [17]– [18]. In this study, LDH levels were significantly increased in patients with macrolide-resistant SMPP, which is consistent with the results of a previous study on patients with MPP. Binary logistic regression analysis showed that LDH was an independent risk marker for drug-resistant SMPP, and ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC was 0.682, and its sensitivity and specificity were 0.807 and 0.562. Although the sensitivity was high, the specificity was low, which might lead to a high false positive rate.It is insufficient to serve as an independent predictor.

IL-6 is mainly produced by mononuclear cells, macrophages, and T lymphocytes, and is a cytokine with various biological activities. It plays an important role in the immune and inflammatory responses and maintains the physiological balance of the body. In recent years, it has been found that IL-6 is an important cytokine in the inflammatory response and an important indicator of infection that appears earlier than CRP [19]. On one hand, it can induce T-cell differentiation, enhance the release of inflammatory cytokines, and promote the occurrence of inflammation. On the other hand, it can promote the production of autoantibodies and induce the occurrence of autoimmune diseases. Previous studies have shown that MP infection leads to the production of IL-6 and other cytokines [20]. In this study, the serum level of IL-6 in children with macrolide-resistant SMPP was significantly higher than that in the general group, indicating strong inflammation in the body of children with macrolide-resistant SMPP. ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of CRP in predicting macrolide-resistant SMPP was 0.830, with an best cutoff value of 11.75 pg/ml, the sensitivity and specificity of 0.748 and 0.865. These results indicate its high diagnostic value as a alone biomarker for macrolide-resistant SMPP. An IL-6 level >11.75 pg/ml indicates a high likelihood of macrolide-resistant SMPP. Furthermore, this study employed binary logistic regression analysis to evaluate the combined predictive efficacy of IL-6 and LDH for macrolide-resistant SMPP. The combined model achieved an AUC of 0.761, sensitivity of 0.807, and specificity of 0.809. This shows that the combination of IL-6 and LDH balances sensitivity and specificity, reduces misdiagnosis and missed diagnoses, and provides reliable clinical decision-making support for diagnosing drug-resistant SMPP.

In conclusion, this study confirms that IL-6, CRP, and LDH levels are significantly elevated in macrolide-resistant SMPP and serve as independent biomarkers. The combination of IL-6 and LDH has important clinical value for macrolide-resistant SMPP. Therefore, clinicians can evaluate the progression of macrolide-resistant MPP by monitoring IL-6 and LDH levels dynamically. When IL-6 is greater than 11.75 pg/mL and LDH is gradually increasing, it indicates a higher risk of macrolide-resistant severe MPP (SMPP), and hospitalization should be considered. In such cases, clinicians should consider performing imaging studies, adjusting antibiotic therapy, and administering glucocorticoids to dampen excessive immune responses, as these can cause more harm in severe cases. Since macrolides might not work, alternatives like tetracyclines (doxycycline) or fluoroquinolones (levofloxacin)could be considered, but considering patient age (tetracyclines not for children under 8, fluoroquinolones not for children). So, clinicians might have to balance guidelines and patient specifics. In addition, it is necessary to monitor respiration and potential intrapulmonary/extrapulmonary complications. Oxygen therapy and bronchoscopic intervention should be provided if needed. Macrolide resistance in MPP is a known issue, especially in Asia. Resistance leads to treatment failure.Early recognition of macrolide-resistant SMPP via biomarkers, tailored antimicrobials, anti-inflammatory strategies, and vigilant complication management improve outcomes.Regular reassessment and multidisciplinary care are essential.

However, this study was a single-center retrospective analysis with a small sample size, and the conclusions have certain limitations. Future multicenter prospective studies with larger sample sizes are needed to further clarify and verify these findings.

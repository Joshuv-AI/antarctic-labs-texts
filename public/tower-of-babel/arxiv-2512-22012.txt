Abstract
. Binomial edge ideals associated to a simple graph G G were introduced in [ 15 ] by Herzog and collaborators and, independently, by Ohtani in [ 18 ] . They became an “instant classic” in combinatorial commutative algebra with more than 100 papers devoted to their investigation over the past 15 years. They exhibit many striking properties, including being radical and, moreover, Cartwright–Sturmfels. Using the fact that binomial edge ideals can be seen as ideals of 2 2 -minors of a matrix of variables with two rows, generalized binomial edge ideals of 2 2 -minors of matrices of m m rows were introduced by Rauh [ 20 ] and proved to be radical. The goal of this paper is to prove that generalized binomial edge ideals are Cartwright–Sturmfels. On the way we provide results on ideal constructions preserving the Cartwright-Sturmfels property. We also give examples and counterexamples to the Cartwright-Sturmfels property for higher minors.



1.
Introduction

Ideals with a radical initial ideal are special: their homological relationship with the initial ideal is exceptionally tight. This intuition underlies Herzog’s conjecture, now a theorem
[
11
]
, and it is precisely why such ideals are nowadays referred to as Herzog ideals
[
16
]
.
Cartwright–Sturmfels ideals, introduced in
[
5
]
, form a particularly strong subclass of Herzog ideals. A
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ideal in a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded polynomial ring is Cartwright-Sturmfels if its
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded generic initial ideal is radical (this is one of the possible definitions). In fact, every initial ideal of a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal is radical.
Over the last years, several families of Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals have been identified
[
7
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
17
]
, generalizations have been proposed
[
3
]
and the Hilbert-schemes associated to them have been investigated
[
19
]
.

Binomial edge ideals are generated by collections of
2
-minors of a generic
2\times n
matrix that correspond to the edges of a graph. They were introduced by Herzog and collaborators in
[
15
]
and, independently, by Ohtani in
[
18
]
. The notion was later generalized by Rauh in
[
20
]
. Ordinary binomial edge ideals were proved to be Cartwright-Sturmfels in
[
9
,
10
]
.
The main objective of this paper is to show that generalized binomial edge ideals are also Cartwright–Sturmfels, see Theorem
5.1
. We also prove two auxiliary results concerning general Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals. Firstly, we describe certain Cartwright-Sturmfels subideals of a given Cartwright–Sturmfels ideal (Proposition
4.2
). Secondly, we show that the sum of two Cartwright–Sturmfels ideals is Cartwright–Sturmfels (Proposition
4.3
) if the multidegrees of the generators of the two ideals overlap at most in one component.
We conclude the paper with a discussion of families of higher-order minors that define Cartwright–Sturmfels ideals and families that do not define
Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals. Our results in this direction are preliminary and leave room for further refinement.

3.
A base change argument

In this section we prove Corollary
3.2
, which will
be used in Section
4
for proving the CS property for
one of our ideal constructions. The corollary is deduced from
Proposition
3.1
. Since its proof would not benefit from
specializations, the proposition is formulated in a rather general form.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and let
A,B
be flat algebras over
R
.
Set
C=A\otimes_{R}B
, and for a right
A
-module
M
and a left
B
-module
N
, consider
M\otimes_{R}B
as a right
C
-module and
A\otimes_{R}N
as a left
C
-module. There is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N)\cong\mathrm{Tor}^{C}_{i}(M\otimes_{R}B,A\otimes_{R}N)\text{ for all $i$.}

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow M
and
\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow N
be free resolutions of
M
and
N
over
A
and
B
, respectively.
Since
A
and
B
are flat over
R
, we get that the induced maps

\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{R}B\longrightarrow M\otimes_{R}B\quad\text{ and }\quad A\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow A\otimes_{R}N

are free resolutions over
C
. We claim that the natural map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{G}&\longrightarrow&(\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{R}B)\otimes_{C}(A\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{G})\\
(f\otimes g)&\longmapsto&(f\otimes 1)\otimes(1\otimes g)\end{array}

is
C
-linear and an isomorphism of complexes. The
C
-linearity is a direct verification. To see that it is an isomorphism, note that since
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{G}
are free over
A
and
B
, respectively, it suffices to check that map is an isomorphism when
\mathfrak{F}=A
and
\mathfrak{G}=B
, and that is clear. Note, that
\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow M
and
\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow N
can be regarded as flat resolutions of
M
and
N
as
R
-modules. Thus one gets isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{H}_{i}(\,\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{G}\,)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{H}_{i}\big(\,(\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{R}B)\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}B}(A\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{G})\,\big)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Tor}^{C}_{i}(\,M\otimes_{R}B,A\otimes_{R}B\,)

This proves the assertion.
∎

As a corollary we have the following result which will become
crucial in the proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose
S=K[\bf{x},\bf{y},\bf{z}]
where
\bf{x},\bf{y},\bf{z}
are disjoint sets of variables with
{\bf{x}}=x_{1},\dots,x_{p}
.
Let
I
and
J
be ideals of
S
such that
I
is extended from
K[\bf{x},\bf{y}]
and
J
is extended from
K[\bf{x},\bf{z}]
. Then

\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{i}(S/I,S/J)=0

for all
i>p
.
In particular, if
p=1
then the tensor product of
free resolutions of
I
and
J
yields a free resolution of
IJ
.

Proof.

Set
R=K[{\bf{x}}],\ A=K[{\bf{x},\bf{y}}],\ B=K[{\bf{x},\bf{z}}]
so that
A\otimes_{R}B=S
and
A
and
B
are flat (actually free) over
R
. Let
I_{1}
be the ideal of
A
with
I_{1}S=I
and
J_{1}
be the ideal of
B
with
J_{1}S=J
. Set
M=A/I_{1}
and
N=B/J_{1}
. We have
M\otimes_{R}B=S/I
and ,
A\otimes_{R}N=S/J
. Applying Proposition
3.1
to the present setting we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{i}(A/I_{1},B/J_{1})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{i}(S/I,S/J)

and
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{i}(A/I_{1},B/J_{1})
vanishes for
i
larger than the global dimension of

that, by the general version of Hilbert’s syzygy theorem
[
21
, Theorem 8.37]
, is
p
.

Finally when

we have

\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{i}(S/I,S/J)=0\quad\text{ for all }i>1

hence

\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{i}(I,J)=\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{i+2}(S/I,S/J)=0\text{ for all }i>0.

This implies that the tensor product of free resolutions of

and

Now we observe that the kernel of the natural map

gets identified with

\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{1}(S/I,J)=\mathrm{Tor}^{S}_{2}(S/I,S/J)

That is,

∎

4.
Constructions preserving the Cartwright-Sturmfels property

First, we show that the CS property is inherited by ideals generated by
all elements of a fixed squarefree
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-degree.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a CS ideal and
A\subseteq[n]
. Let
J
be the ideal of
S
generated by the vector space
I_{A}
. Then
J
is CS and
\operatorname{in}_{>}(J)
is generated by the monomial vector space
\operatorname{in}_{>}(I_{A})
for every term order
>
of
S
.

Proof.

Let
u
be a monomial from the minimal monomial
generating set of an initial ideal of
J
.
By definition
J
is generated by polynomials which are linear combinations of monomials of

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-degree
A
. It follows that
\deg u=\sum_{i\in A}a_{i}\,e_{i}
for integers
a_{i}>0
. Since
J\subset I
and
I
is CS
there exists a subset
B
of
A
and
f\in I_{B}
such that
\operatorname{in}_{>}(f)|u
. Let
v
be a monomial of degree
A\setminus B

such that
v|u
.
Then
v\,f\in I_{A}
and
\operatorname{in}_{>}(v\,f)=v\,\operatorname{in}_{>}(f)|u
.
∎

We use the preceding lemma to give a criterion when the CS property is
inherited by ideals which are generated by all polynomials from a set of squarefree

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-degrees in a CS ideal.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
such that:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\text{ if }A,B\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }A\cap B\neq\emptyset\text{ then }A\cup B\in\mathcal{F}.

Let
I
be a CS ideal and let
J
be the ideal generated by the vector spaces
I_{A}
with
A\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
J
is CS. More precisely, for every term order
>
the initial ideal

\operatorname{in}_{>}(J)
is generated by the monomial vector spaces
\operatorname{in}_{>}(I_{A})
with
A\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
>
be a term order. Let
f\in J
be of

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-degree

a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\,e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and set
E_{f}=\{\,i\,:\,a_{i}>0\,\}
. Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}
be the inclusionwise maximal elements of
\{\,B\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,B\subseteq E_{f}\,\}
and let
U_{i}
be the ideal generated by the vector space
I_{A_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq s
.
It follows that
f\in\sum_{i=1}^{s}U_{i}
. If
A_{i}\cap A_{j}\neq\emptyset
for some
1\leq i<j\leq s
then by (
4.1
)
A_{i}\cup A_{j}\in\mathcal{F}

and
A_{i}\cup A_{j}\subseteq E_{f}
contradicting their maximality.
Hence the
A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}
are pairwise disjoint.
As a consequence, the ideals
U_{i}
are generated by polynomials in disjoint sets of variables. The Buchberger criterion then implies
that
\operatorname{in}_{>}(f)\in\operatorname{in}_{>}(U_{i})
for some

we have that
\operatorname{in}_{>}(U_{i})
is generated by the vector space
\operatorname{in}_{>}\big(\,I_{A_{i}}\,\big)

\operatorname{in}_{>}(f)
belongs to the ideal generated by
\operatorname{in}_{>}(I_{A_{i}})
. Hence
\operatorname{in}_{>}(J)
is radical. Since this holds also in generic coordinates,
the proof is complete.
∎

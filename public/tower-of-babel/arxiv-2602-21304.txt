A Čech–Stokes Pushout Groupoid: A Log/Kummer Betti Presenter for Stokes Torsors
Mauricio Corrêa

Abstract
. Let X X be a complex manifold and D ⊂ X D\subset X a simple normal crossings divisor. Fix a Kummer level n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and an irregular type Φ \Phi along D D , and consider the logarithmic Betti boundary X n log X_{n}^{\log} (the level- n n Kato–Nakayama space). We construct a strictly 1 1 -categorical, cover-based Betti presenter for Stokes torsors by building a small strict Čech–Stokes collar groupoid on a punctured logarithmic collar of D D , together with a canonical projection π \pi to the underlying sector Čech groupoid. The central observation is that the appropriate collar Stokes moduli is the groupoid of strict functorial sections Sec ⁡ ( π ) \operatorname{Sec}(\pi) (equivalently, St Φ \mathrm{St}_{\Phi} -torsors on the sector cover), yielding a fully explicit presenter that remains small and strictly functorial. We then glue the collar presenter to the ordinary Čech presenter of U := X ∖ D U:=X\setminus D through a strict diagram of small groupoids and an explicit 2 2 -pushout presenter G Φ , n G_{\Phi,n} . We prove that global Stokes objects, defined intrinsically as a 2 2 -fiber product, are computed by a strict torsorial gluing problem, i.e. by Stokes-corrected torsorial representations of G Φ , n G_{\Phi,n} . In arbitrary SNC depth we solve the local corner presentation problem by an explicit chamber-complex model on the angular torus: Stokes torsors are nonabelian cocycles on the 1 1 -skeleton with relations read off from 2 2 -cells. The overall construction is canonical up to Morita equivalence with a contractible space of identifications (invariant under stratified subdivision), and it is compatible with Kummer descent along all normal crossings strata via ( μ n ) k (\mu_{n})^{k} -equivariant local models and descent to homotopy fixed points.

Introduction

Irregular singular connections and Stokes phenomena may be viewed, at their core, as a problem of

topological encoding of analytic continuation near a divisor
.
Let
X
be a complex manifold (or a smooth complex algebraic variety), and let
D\subset X
be a simple normal crossings divisor.
Write

.
A meromorphic flat connection on
(X,D)
restricts to

as an ordinary local system, but its behavior near

involves additional, genuinely nonabelian gluing data: the

In dimension one this is classical (see e.g.
[
1

21

2

); in higher dimension, the same principle persists,
with one essential geometric change:
the relevant “directions” around
D
vary along the strata of
D
and must be handled functorially across charts.
A Betti-side presenter that is useful for geometry should therefore keep track simultaneously of
(i) the directional boundary geometry, (ii) tame/Kummer branching along all strata, and
(iii) the wild Stokes gluing across sectorial overlaps.

A standard form of the irregular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence asserts that meromorphic connections
with prescribed formal/irregular type are classified on the Betti side by
Stokes-filtered

(or Stokes-graded) local systems on a real boundary space surrounding
D
.
Analytically, this boundary is often realized by the real oriented blow-up along
D
,
while modern formulations use enhanced or perverse-sheaf avatars of irregularity (cf.
[
5

13

).
In the Stokes-filtered description, the Stokes jumps are encoded by a Stokes sheaf on the boundary,
and the classification problem becomes a torsor problem.
While this framework is well-established, the Betti objects are frequently presented either analytically
or as stacks defined by descent; in particular, the underlying
presenting groupoid
is often left implicit.
For applications where one wants strict gluing along covers (for instance as input to Betti-stack or Tannakian formalisms),
it is useful to have a small and explicit strict groupoid model adapted to normal crossings strata and compatible with tame/Kummer ramification.

A complementary viewpoint, especially effective on curves, is to encode wild monodromy by groupoids:
Stokes data can be organized into “wild” fundamental groupoids, and moduli can be described as representation groupoids
of these presenters (see e.g.
[
8
]
and the references therein).
This has two structural advantages: it makes cutting-and-pasting arguments transparent, and it interfaces naturally with
geometric structures on moduli (Poisson/symplectic forms, generators-and-relations descriptions, and related constructions).
In higher dimension, however, one faces an additional bookkeeping issue: the boundary geometry is naturally
logarithmic
,
and the tame/Kummer layer interacts with the Stokes layer along strata of arbitrary depth.
The aim of this paper is to give a single, strictly
1
-categorical, cover-based model in which the directional, tame, and wild layers
appear together through explicit maps of small groupoids governed by universal properties (pullbacks and pushouts),
and which admits finite skeletal models well-suited to computation (compare the
skeleton
perspective in
[
23
]

Standing Stokes input on the regular collar

Fix a locally finite open cover

3.
Groupoids, Čech groupoids, and torsorial representations

3.1.
Ambient 2-category and smallness

We work in the
(2,1)
-category
\mathbf{Grpd}
of small groupoids, strict functors, and natural isomorphisms.
Whenever a groupoid is built from an open cover, we implicitly keep the standard Čech
topological
realization
(object and arrow spaces are disjoint unions of opens and their intersections, with the disjoint union topology).
Since our coefficient groups are discrete, continuity forces local constancy of transition data, so torsors on these Čech presenters
model local systems and Stokes torsors by strict gluing. For bicategorical universal properties (pushouts and pullbacks) we only use the
underlying small groupoids; Morita invariance under refinement guarantees independence of the chosen presenters.

All fiber products of groupoids in this paper are
2
-fiber products
(pseudopullbacks) in
\mathbf{Grpd}
.
Concretely, given
A\xrightarrow{F}C\xleftarrow{G}B
, the groupoid
A\times^{(2)}_{C}B
has objects triples

(a,b,\varphi)
with
a\in A
,
b\in B
, and an isomorphism
\varphi:F(a)\xrightarrow{\sim}G(b)
in
C
;
morphisms are pairs making the evident square commute.
We keep the notation
\times_{C}
to avoid typographical overload.

3.2.
Groupoids and strict functors

Definition 3.1
.

A
groupoid

consists of a set
G_{0}
of objects and a set
G_{1}
of arrows, with source/target maps

s,t:G_{1}\to G_{0}
, identity

, inverse
i:G_{1}\to G_{1}
,
and composition
m:G_{1}\times_{G_{0}}G_{1}\to G_{1}
satisfying the usual axioms.
A
strict functor

is a pair of maps
(F_{0},F_{1})
commuting with all structure maps.

3.3.
The Čech groupoid of an open cover

Let
T
be a topological space and let
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}

Define the

\check{\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{V})_{0}:=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}V_{i},\qquad\check{\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{V})_{1}:=\bigsqcup_{(i,j)\in I\times I}(V_{i}\cap V_{j}),

Torsorial “representations” of groupoids

Fix

\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})^{\delta}

Definition 3.2

Definition 3.3

3.5.
Representations as functors into the torsor groupoid

Let
\mathbf{Tors}_{r}
denote the groupoid whose objects are right
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})^{\delta}
-torsors in sets

Proposition 3.5
.

The groupoid

Proof.

The groupoid

Fixing the standard torsor identifies automorphisms with
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})^{\delta}

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

For any small groupoid

(3.2)

\mathrm{Rep}_{r}(G)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grpd}}(G,\mathbf{Tors}_{r}),

Proof.

This is the usual descent = functor dictionary for principal bundles on a groupoid:

The Stokes sheaf on the punctured collar

4.1.
Filtered and graded automorphisms; the Stokes sheaf

For an open
W\subset B_{\alpha}
define:

•

\mathrm{Aut}^{fil}(W)
: automorphisms of
\mathrm{Gr}|_{W}
preserving all standard filtrations
F^{std}_{\leq q}|_{W}
;

•

\mathrm{Aut}^{gr}(W)
: graded automorphisms of
\mathrm{Gr}|_{W}
, i.e. preserving each direct summand
\mathrm{Gr}_{q}|_{W}
.

There is a natural homomorphism
gr:\mathrm{Aut}^{fil}(W)\to\mathrm{Aut}^{gr}(W)
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
Stokes sheaf
(relative to
\Phi
at level
n
) is the presheaf (in fact sheaf) of groups on the sector-box cover
given by

(4.1)

\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}(W):=\ker\!\big(\mathrm{Aut}^{fil}(W)\xrightarrow{gr}\mathrm{Aut}^{gr}(W)\big).

Remark 4.2
.

On any contractible and connected
W\subset B_{\alpha}
, the local system
\mathrm{Gr}|_{W}
is trivial and
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Gr}|_{W})
is constant (discrete).
Hence sections of
\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}
on such
W
are just group elements.

5.
Stokes objects on the collar as torsors under
\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}

5.1.
Local Stokes objects on
N^{\circ}

Definition 5.1

(Stokes-local objects on the punctured collar)
.

A
Stokes-local object of rank
r
on
N^{\circ}
of type
\Phi
is a tuple

(5.1)

\big(L,\{L_{\leq q}\}_{\alpha},\iota,\{s_{\alpha}\}\big)

where:

•

L
is a rank-
r
local system on
N^{\circ}
;

•

for each
\alpha
,
\{L_{\leq q}|_{B_{\alpha}}\}_{q\in\Phi}
is a filtration compatible with
\preceq_{\alpha}
;

•

\iota:gr(L)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Gr}
is a graded identification (global);

•

for each
\alpha

s_{\alpha}:L|_{B_{\alpha}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Gr}|_{B_{\alpha}}

such that
s_{\alpha}(L_{\leq q})=F^{std}_{\leq q}(\mathrm{Gr})
and
gr(s_{\alpha})=\iota|_{B_{\alpha}}
.

Morphisms are isomorphisms preserving filtrations and commuting with
\iota
.
We denote the resulting groupoid by
\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}(N^{\circ};\Phi)
.

5.2.
Difference of splittings is Stokes-unipotent

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

There is a natural equivalence of groupoids

\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}(N^{\circ};\Phi)\ \simeq\ \mathrm{Tors}(\mathrm{St}_{\Phi};\mathcal{B}).

Proof.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
refines
\mathcal{B}
, then restriction induces an equivalence

\mathrm{Tors}(\mathrm{St}_{\Phi};\mathcal{B})\ \simeq\ \mathrm{Tors}(\mathrm{St}_{\Phi};\mathcal{B}^{\prime}).

In particular,
\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}(N^{\circ};\Phi)
is independent (up to canonical equivalence) of the chosen sector-box cover.

Proof.

Let
r:\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{B}
be a refinement map, so each
B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is contained in
B_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{B}
.
Restriction along inclusions
B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cap B^{\prime}_{\beta^{\prime}}\subset B_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}\cap B_{r(\beta^{\prime})}
sends a cocycle

(u_{\alpha\beta})
on
\mathcal{B}
to a cocycle
(u^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}})
on
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
by

(5.12)

u^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}:=u_{r(\alpha^{\prime})\,r(\beta^{\prime})}\big|_{B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}\in\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}(B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}),\qquad B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}:=B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cap B^{\prime}_{\beta^{\prime}}.

This defines a functor
\mathrm{Tors}(\mathrm{St}_{\Phi};\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Tors}(\mathrm{St}_{\Phi};\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
To see it is essentially surjective, start from a cocycle

on
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
.
Because
\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}
is a sheaf of groups, the family

satisfies effective descent along the refinement:
there exist elements
h_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}(B^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
such that, after the gauge change

, the resulting cocycle is constant on fibers of

and thus
comes by restriction from a cocycle on
\mathcal{B}
. Full faithfulness follows similarly: a gauge

-cochain on
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}

0
-cochain on
\mathcal{B}
.
Therefore restriction is an equivalence.
Finally, combine

with (

) to conclude that

is independent of the chosen sector-box cover.
A detailed reference for refinement invariance of nonabelian Čech torsors for a sheaf of groups is

, Ch. III, §2]
.
∎

7.
Gluing with the interior: the pushout presenter
\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n}

7.1.
Working 2-category and Morita invariance

All bicategorical limits and colimits in this section are taken in the
(2,1)
-category
\mathbf{Grpd}
of small groupoids,
strict functors, and natural isomorphisms.
Topological features enter only through the choice of Čech presenters: since our coefficient groups are discrete,
continuity forces local constancy of transition data, so torsors on these presenters model local systems and Stokes torsors.

All statements are Morita-invariant: replacing any cover by a refinement replaces the corresponding Čech groupoid by a Morita equivalent presenter.

Fix:

B\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow B\pi_{1}(U,x_{0}).

On the boundary side, fix a base direction
\theta_{i}\in S^{1}_{p_{i},n}\setminus\Sigma_{p_{i},n}
and observe that the boundary groupoid

\partial\mathrm{Stok}^{(i)}_{n}
has an isotropy group at
\theta_{i}
(the “peripheral Stokes group” at that direction).
There is also a canonical functor

B\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\partial\mathrm{Stok}^{(i)}_{n}

sending the generator
1\in\mathbb{Z}
to the distinguished boundary isotropy element representing the total Stokes monodromy around the circle
(for the fixed formal type, this is the cyclic product of Stokes jumps; in the additive one-parameter case it is the sum of the labels).

Thus, for each
i
we have a span of groupoids

B\pi_{1}(U,x_{0})\ \longleftarrow\ B\mathbb{Z}\ \longrightarrow\ \partial\mathrm{Stok}^{(i)}_{n},

and therefore, for all punctures at once, a diagram indexed by the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{i}B\mathbb{Z}
.

The pushout groupoid.

Define the global groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{glob}}
as the pushout (amalgamated sum) in the 2-category of groupoids

(10.42)

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{glob}}\;:=\;B\pi_{1}(U,x_{0})\ \sqcup_{\bigsqcup_{i}\,B\mathbb{Z}}\ \bigsqcup_{i}\partial\mathrm{Stok}^{(i)}_{n}.

Concretely, this forces the peripheral loop class
[\gamma_{i}]\in\pi_{1}(U,x_{0})
to be identified with the boundary isotropy element in

\partial\mathrm{Stok}^{(i)}_{n}
encoding the total Stokes monodromy at
p_{i}
.
In other words, (
10.42
) is the universal groupoid obtained by gluing the “interior” topological monodromy data on
U

together with the “boundary” Stokes data at each puncture, along the common peripheral subgroup
\mathbb{Z}
.

What an object of the pushout represents.

An object of the classifying stack
B\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{glob}}
(a
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{glob}}
-torsor) can be unpacked as the following data:

•

a
\pi_{1}(U,x_{0})
-torsor, equivalently a local system on
U
(topological monodromy on the complement);

•

for each puncture
p_{i}
, a Stokes torsor on the boundary circle
S^{1}_{p_{i},n}\setminus\Sigma_{p_{i},n}

(or equivalently a Stokes-filtered local system in Sabbah’s sense for the fixed formal type);

•

for each
i
, a compatibility identification equating the image of the peripheral loop
[\gamma_{i}]
in the
\pi_{1}(U)
-torsor with the
distinguished total Stokes monodromy element determined by the boundary Stokes torsor.

Thus (
10.42
) is precisely a
gluing device
: it packages “interior monodromy + boundary Stokes data + peripheral compatibility”
as a single torsor problem.

Comparison with Sabbah (Stokes-filtered local systems)
[
21
]
.

Sabbah’s formalism identifies (for fixed formal type at each puncture) meromorphic connections on
X
with Stokes-filtered local systems on the real oriented
blow-up
\widetilde{X}
, i.e. a local system on
\widetilde{X}\setminus\partial\widetilde{X}\cong U
together with Stokes filtrations along each boundary
circle
S^{1}_{p_{i},n}
, compatible with the Stokes order and jumping precisely along
\Sigma_{p_{i},n}
.
On the boundary, the filtration is equivalent to a
\mathrm{St}_{i}
-torsor, hence to a torsor for the boundary groupoid

\partial\mathrm{Stok}^{(i)}_{n}
presenting the same local moduli.
The peripheral compatibility condition in Sabbah (how the local system on
U
meets the boundary Stokes data) is exactly what the amalgamation along

B\mathbb{Z}
enforces in (
10.42
).

Comparison with Babbitt–Varadarajan (Stokes cocycles)
[
1
]
.

BV describe the local moduli at each puncture by nonabelian Čech 1-cocycles valued in the Stokes sheaf
\mathrm{St}_{i}
, modulo gauge.
Your collar/section presentation is a groupoid form of the same statement: strict sections (with sectorwise gauge) are Čech cocycles modulo 0-cochains.
The pushout groupoid (
10.42
) does not change the local BV description; it records how these local cocycles are constrained globally
by the monodromy on
U
through the identification of peripheral classes.

Comparison with Gualtieri, S. Li, and B. Pym (GLP)
[
8
]
.

In the elementary one-parameter case, GLP construct an explicit Lie groupoid chart presenting the same local Stokes torsor moduli on a punctured disk.
Your boundary/collar groupoids and the GLP chart are different
presentations
of the same stack, hence they are Morita equivalent locally.
The role of the pushout (
10.42
) is global: it is the groupoid-level implementation of ‘
‘glue the interior with all boundary charts
along the common peripheral subgroup”
in a Morita-invariant way.

What this codifies that is not the focus of BV/Sabbah/GLP.

The BV and Sabbah frameworks give the correct intrinsic objects and local equivalences of categories, and GLP provides a smooth local chart.
Your pushout construction adds a systematic, Morita-invariant
global assembly
principle:
it turns the collection of local boundary Stokes moduli and the interior
\pi_{1}(U)
-data into a single universal groupoid,
whose torsors are exactly
“interior local systems plus boundary Stokes torsors with peripheral matching”
.
This is the structural bridge between local irregular data and global topological monodromy that the groupoid language of this paper makes explicit.

Throughout we work on the regular collar

11.1.
A

Let

Definition 11.1

Remark 11.2
.

Existence is standard: a locally finite triangulation subordinate to a locally finite real-analytic stratification can be obtained
by taking a stratified triangulation and barycentrically subdividing; see e.g. the general discussion of constructible stratifications
and exit-path formalisms in
[

We fix such a triangulation

once and for all.

For each simplex

Since

is locally constant on each stratum, for any contractible open

the group
\mathrm{St}_{\Phi}(W)
is canonically
identified with a fixed abstract group which we denote by

The skeletal base groupoid

Let

-skeleton of

.
Write

Equivalently,

11.3.
The skeletal Čech presenter and comparison

Let

Lemma 11.5
.

Proof.

A finite intersection

Proposition 11.6

12.1.
Relation with Stokes skeletons and Deligne-type questions

In complex dimension

[

Our Čech–Stokes collar groupoid

A complementary viewpoint, important for finiteness and representability statements, is that Stokes torsors admit

subject to face relations. This philosophy is central in the skeleton approach of

∎

Micro-example: a transverse wall crossing at depth
2

In a depth-
2
local model
D=\{z_{1}z_{2}=0\}
, the angular torus is
(S^{1})^{2}
. If the wall arrangement consists of two transverse walls,
the complement has four chambers and the dual adjacency graph is a
4
-cycle. In the elementary one-parameter situation, each adjacent overlap
carries a Stokes group
(\mathbb{C},+)
, so a cocycle is a quadruple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\in\mathbb{C}^{4}
subject only to the cycle relation modulo vertex gauge.
This is the simplest instance of the general recipe above (compare
[
23
]
).

12.4.
Derived and homotopical upgrades

The presenter
\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n}
is small and strictly functorial, so it is a convenient input for mapping-stack constructions.
Formally, the nerve
N(-)
sends bicategorical
2
-pushouts of groupoids to homotopy pushouts of spaces, and mapping out of a homotopy pushout
is a homotopy pullback; see
[
11
]
. This suggests the following upgrade path, compatible with the derived/homotopical theories of Stokes
data developed by Porta–Teyssier
[
19
,
20
]
.

Theorem 12.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
\infty
-category with finite limits and let
\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Assume we are given a bicategorical
2
-pushout of small groupoids in
\mathbf{Grpd}

(12.4)

\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n}\ \simeq\ \mathscr{G}_{U}\ \sqcup_{\mathscr{G}_{\times}}\ \mathscr{G}_{C},

and write
N(-)
for the nerve.

(1) Mapping out of the presenter is a homotopy pullback: there is a canonical equivalence in
\mathcal{X}

(12.5)

\mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n}),\mathcal{H}\bigr)\ \simeq\ \mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{U}),\mathcal{H}\bigr)\times^{h}_{\mathrm{Map}(N(\mathscr{G}_{\times}),\mathcal{H})}\mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{C}),\mathcal{H}\bigr),

equivalently the square

(12.6)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n}),\mathcal{H}\bigr)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{U}),\mathcal{H}\bigr)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{C}),\mathcal{H}\bigr)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}\bigl(N(\mathscr{G}_{\times}),\mathcal{H}\bigr)}

is homotopy Cartesian.

(2) Let
\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(-)
be a derived enhancement of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
-local systems valued in
\mathcal{X}

(for instance, in the sense of Porta–Teyssier
[
20
,
19
]
),
and let
\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ};\Phi)\in\mathcal{X}
be a derived enhancement of the collar Stokes moduli,
equipped with a morphism

\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ};\Phi)\to\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ}).

Assume their
1
-truncations recover the classical groupoids:

(12.7)

\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(T)\ \simeq\ \mathrm{LocSys}_{r}(T),\qquad\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ};\Phi)\ \simeq\ \mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}(N^{\circ};\Phi),

compatibly with the maps to
\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}(N^{\circ})
.
Define the derived global Stokes moduli by the homotopy pullback

(12.8)

\mathrm{Stokes}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(X^{\log}_{n};D,\Phi):=\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(U)\times^{h}_{\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ})}\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ};\Phi).

Then its
1
-truncation is canonically the classical global Stokes groupoid:

(12.9)

\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{Stokes}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(X^{\log}_{n};D,\Phi)\ \simeq\ \mathrm{Stokes}_{r}(X^{\log}_{n};D,\Phi).

Proof.

For (1), the nerve identifies bicategorical
2
-pushouts of groupoids with homotopy pushouts of their classifying spaces.
Indeed,

N:\mathbf{Grpd}\to\mathcal{S}_{\leq 1}

exhibits the
(2,1)
-category of groupoids, localized at equivalences, as the

\infty
-category of
1
-types, and in any
\infty
-category mapping out of a homotopy colimit is a homotopy limit; see

[
11
, §1.2, §4.2]
. Applying this to the diagram

N(\mathscr{G}_{U})\leftarrow N(\mathscr{G}_{\times})\rightarrow N(\mathscr{G}_{C})

yields
(
12.5
), hence (
12.6
).
For (2), apply
\tau_{\leq 1}
to (
12.8
). Since
1
-truncation is left exact in an
\infty
-topos (hence preserves finite
limits)
[
11
, §5.5]
, we obtain

\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{Stokes}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(X^{\log}_{n};D,\Phi)\simeq\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(U)\times^{(2)}_{\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ})}\tau_{\leq 1}\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}^{\mathrm{der}}(N^{\circ};\Phi).

Using (
12.7
) and the defining fiber product

\mathrm{Stokes}_{r}(X^{\log}_{n};D,\Phi)=\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}(U)\times^{(2)}_{\mathrm{LocSys}_{r}(N^{\circ})}\mathrm{StokesLocal}_{r}(N^{\circ};\Phi)

gives (
12.9
).
∎

Remark 12.8
.

The argument is formal
[
11
]
, but the point of this paper is that
N(\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n})
comes from an explicit small presenter.
Thus
\mathrm{Map}(N(\mathscr{G}_{\Phi,n}),\mathcal{H})
admits concrete combinatorial models once a skeleton is fixed (compare the skeleton philosophy in

[
23
]
), and the same applies to the homotopy pullback description (
12.5
).

Spatiotemporal analysis and driving force of high quality development of small towns in Guangxi, China
Authors: Chuxin Hu, Binglin Liu, Zhiqiang Yan, Chunxiao Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The high-quality development of small towns in Guangxi is crucial for regional economic balance and sustainable development. This study establishes a comprehensive evaluation index system based on the new development philosophy (innovation, coordination, green, openness, and sharing) and introduces a comparative perspective between small towns and cities to avoid homogenized analysis. Using panel data from 70 small towns and 14 prefecture-level cities in Guangxi (2005–2022), we employed the Entropy Weight-TOPSIS method to measure development levels, the Dagum Gini coefficient to analyze regional disparities, and an optimized Geographic Detector model to explore driving factors. Additionally, a dedicated comparative analysis was conducted to examine differences in economic growth, industrial structure, public services, ecological environment, and innovation investment between small towns and cities.The findings are as follows:1.Guangxi’s small towns showed steady high-quality development with distinct spatiotemporal patterns and were categorized into five types: comprehensive leading, core backbone, catch-up potential, opportunity development, and low-growth.2.Regional disparities in small towns are narrowing but remain significant, especially between the western and southern economic zones.3.Compared with cities, small towns lagged in economic growth, industrial upgrading, innovation investment, and public services but had 30.54% higher carbon emission intensity, reflecting reliance on traditional high-energy industries.4.Economic factors were the primary drivers of small towns’ development, with ecological factors growing in importance; interactions between factors had a stronger impact than individual factors.5.This research highlights small towns’ unique role as a link between urban and rural areas through urban-rural comparison, providing targeted insights for balanced and sustainable development policies.

Introduction

In recent years, with the acceleration of globalization and urbanization, the development of small towns has attracted widespread attention. In China, small towns occupy an indispensable and important position in the national urbanization strategy. However, research on the paths and driving mechanisms of its high-quality development is still insufficient1–3. Existing research has explored the development of small towns from multiple perspectives, including economic, ecological and social dimensions, providing rich theoretical and empirical support for understanding the complexity of high-quality development of small towns4–6. Despite this, current research on the development of small towns still has problems such as insufficient analysis of regional differences, insufficient exploration of micro-mechanisms, and lack of dynamic evolution rules7,8.

In the context of globalization, small towns, as important nodes at the interface between urban and rural areas, have undergone significant changes in their development models and functional roles. The cross-regional flow of production factors and the redistribution of resources have accelerated the transformation of some agricultural-dominated small towns into service industries and light industries5,9,10. However, this transformation has not been uniform. Research shows that small towns with obvious location advantages, such as areas near transportation hubs or adjacent to large cities, can integrate into the global economic network more quickly and gain greater development potential11,6,12. In contrast, small towns in remote areas face greater development pressure due to lack of economic resources and market connectivity13–15. In addition, globalization has also triggered profound social and cultural changes, such as the aging of small towns caused by the outflow of young and middle-aged labor, as well as problems such as the decline in social vitality and the gradual fading of traditional culture2,16.

From a spatial perspective, the development of small towns is significantly affected by traffic conditions and geographical location. Convenient transportation networks not only enhance the agglomeration effect of regional economic activities, but also promote the division of labor and cooperation between small towns and the external economy1,17,3,18. However, this spatial agglomeration may also exacerbate economic disparities within regions and between different regions7,4. To this end, studying the development strategies of small towns requires focusing on regional balance and coordinated development while achieving regional economic growth19–21.

High-quality development is the core issue of current small town research, involving multiple dimensions such as economy, ecology, and society22–24. At the economic level, the optimization of industrial structure and the introduction of foreign investment have significantly improved the economic competitiveness and residents’ income levels of small towns25–28. In terms of ecology, the introduction of the green development concept has promoted small towns to seek a balance between economic growth and environmental protection29. In addition, the improvement of the public service system and the strengthening of social security play an important role in improving residents’ well-being and social stability30,31.

Although a large body of research has explored many aspects of small town development, there are still important gaps that need to be filled. Existing research mostly focuses on the urbanization process in large cities, while paying less attention to small towns in remote areas8,6. In addition, the analysis of driving mechanisms mostly stays at the macro level and lacks in-depth exploration of micro mechanisms. At the same time, static spatial analysis methods are difficult to fully capture the dynamic evolution of small town development patterns7,4,5,25,32. Focusing solely on small towns themselves, it’s hard to visually show the differences in development models and driving forces between small towns and cities. Comparing the two helps highlight the unique value of small-town high-quality development research and deeply analyze their development laws and influencing factors.

Against the above background, we carried out according to the idea of “raising problems-basic sorting-empirical analysis-problem solving”. From the perspective of the new development concept, it constructs the evaluation index system of high-quality development and the identification system of the main driving factors, carries out the analysis of spatial and temporal heterogeneity and spatial correlation of small town high-quality development as well as the analysis of regional differences, and then carries out the analysis of the driving factors, and finally puts forward the suggestions and strategies for the high-quality development of small towns (Fig. 1). Our study reveals the dynamic changes of driving factors in the development process of small towns in Guangxi, and provides theoretical basis and practical guidance for regional policy formulation1,21. The innovations of this study include: (1) constructing a high-quality development evaluation system based on five major development concepts, (2) incorporating dynamic evolution analysis into the spatial analysis framework (3) combining the micro-mechanism perspective and optimized parameter models to improve research accuracy.Fig. 1Research framework.

Materials and methods

Study area

Guangxi is located in southern China, bordered by Guangdong Province to the east, the Beibu Gulf and Hainan Province across the sea to the south, Vietnam to the southwest, Yunnan Province to the west, Guizhou Province to the northwest, and Hunan Province to the northeast. The region is largely surrounded by mountains and plateaus, with central and southern parts characterized by hilly plains forming a basin-like area, commonly referred to as the "Guangxi Basin." During the 13th Five-Year Plan period, Guangxi saw steady improvement in both the level and quality of urbanization, with continual optimization of urban layouts and significant development in small towns. The high-quality development of small towns has also received increasing attention from the government. On December 31, 2023, the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region People’s Government Office issued the "Guiding Opinions on Accelerating the Construction of Small Towns Along the Border," aiming to accelerate the development of border towns and promote high-quality growth in these areas, thus establishing a pattern of prosperity along the border regions. To measure the high-quality development levels of small towns in Guangxi, conduct a quantitative analysis of regional differences, and identify the driving factors, we selected 70 county (county-level city) seat towns (Fig. 2) as representatives of Guangxi’s small towns for this study.Fig. 2Location of our study. Data sources

We takes 70 small towns in Guangxi as the research object, and the raw data mainly come from county statistical yearbook (township volume), Guangxi statistical yearbook, Guangxi yearbook, Guangxi construction yearbook, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region population census yearbook, Guangxi prefectural cities statistical yearbook, Guangxi prefectural cities and counties yearbook, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region open public data platform, Easy Professional Superior (EPS) data platform, as well as municipal and county national economic and social development statistical bulletins and government work reports. In addition, carbon emissions data are from China Emission Accounts and Datasets (CEADs). For the missing data, this paper uses the interpolation method to supplement the data.

Construction of evaluation index system for high-quality development in small towns

High-quality development in small towns is characterized by stable economic growth, balanced urban-rural development within regions, innovation-driven progress, and green development. Innovative development is the “new engine” of high-quality development in small towns. Coordinated development is an inherent requirement, green development is a basic requirement, and open development is the only way for high-quality development. Shared development is the fundamental purpose of the high-quality development of small towns. Based on the connotation of high-quality development and the new development concept, this paper constructs a high-quality evaluation index system for small towns from the five dimensions of “innovation, coordination, greenness, openness and sharing”, including 5 first-level indicators, 12 second-level indicators and 25 third-level indicators(Table 1). In order to avoid the distortion of data caused by the population and geographic areas of different regions, we use per capita, utilization rate and percentage to construct the evaluation index system. Evaluation method of high-quality development in small towns

The multidimensional attributes of small-town high-quality development make evaluation complex, so choosing a reasonable model to quantify it is crucial. The Entropy Weight-TOPSIS model can help with this and has advantages for such evaluation. First, the entropy value method provides an objective method of determining the weights of evaluation indicators through information entropy, which reduces the bias caused by subjectivity, thus obtaining more accurate and reliable evaluation results33. Secondly, the entropy value method effectively deals with the differences and correlations between indicators, which is crucial in the evaluation of high-quality development of small towns. By dealing with the interrelationships among indicators, the comprehensiveness of evaluation is enhanced34. Again, the TOPSIS model can well portray the comprehensive influence of multiple evaluation indicators. Intra-group differences refer to the disparities in high-quality development levels within a specific region, while inter-group differences indicate the imbalances in high-quality development levels between different regions37–39. Analysis of high-quality development drivers in small towns in Guangxi

Geoprobe detects spatial heterogeneity and its drivers, covering four detection types. This paper focuses on factor and interaction detection to analyze drivers of small-town high-quality development. The geodetector requires discrete data, so we must discretize continuous data. Conventionally, we use software like ArcGIS or SPSS. The q-value of driving factors assesses discretization. As the q-value varies with different discretization methods and class numbers, it may lead us to misinterpret drivers. So, it’s key for us to determine the optimal combination. To address flaws in traditional discretization, this paper constructs an optimal model, decides the best combination, measures driver explanatory power, and analyzes the driving mechanisms. Spatial differentiation feature analysis

Using ArcGIS 10.2 software, the spatial pattern of the Comprehensive Index for High-Quality Development of small towns in Guangxi was visualized and classified by the Natural Breaks method into four categories (Figure 8): I—Low-Quality Development Zone, II—Lower-Quality Development Zone, III—Higher-Quality Development Zone, and IV—High-Quality Development Zone. The overall spatial differentiation is significant, evolving from scattered to contiguous. Geographically, it shows a "southeast high, northwest low" pattern. Towns like Beiliu, Matou, Xinning, Dongxing, and Youyi with advantages have higher quality. From 2005–2022, the pattern changed from scattered and linear to contiguous. In 2005, the IV zone was sporadic, the I zone was large and continuous, causing imbalance. By 2010, high-value areas were scattered, only Beiliu and Matou had high indices, others were mostly I zones. In 2015, high-value zones were linear and contiguous, mainly in northern and southeastern Guangxi due to policy. The 2010 “Opinions” facilitated development in the southwest and promoted small town growth in Guangxi, but the low-value zones were in northwestern Guangxi with lagged development and restricted areas due to the environment. By 2022, high-value zones became contiguous, low-value zones more scattered. The IV zone now has 13 small towns like Matou, Hengzhou, Beiliu, and Xinning, mostly from III zones, accounting for 18.57% of 70 small towns with a clustering effect. The southeastern part including the Beibu Gulf Urban Agglomeration and Xijiang River Town Belt is the main concentration area. The 2017 "Beibu Gulf Urban Agglomeration Development Plan" played a guiding role. Innovation input disparities (science and technology investment: 0.94% in small towns vs. 1.83% in cities) reflect a double deficit in innovation resources and output efficiency. These disparities suggest that small towns require targeted policies to leverage their comparative advantages, such as ecological resources and rural connectivity, while addressing structural weaknesses. For example, promoting “urban-rural integration pilots” could channel urban talent and capital to small towns, improving public service quality and industrial efficiency. The higher carbon emission intensity in small towns (2.18 t/ten thousand yuan vs. 1.67 t/ten thousand yuan in cities) highlights a development mode divide: cities have shifted toward technology-intensive and low-carbon industries, driven by stricter environmental regulations and access to green technology. In contrast, many small towns in Guangxi, especially in resource-based regions (e.g., mining towns in western Guangxi), still depend on coal-based energy and high-pollution industries due to limited alternative options. This difference underscores the challenge of balancing economic growth and ecological protection in small towns, where environmental governance capacity and green innovation resources are relatively scarce.

The economic growth gap between small towns and cities is rooted in industrial structure rigidity and resource allocation efficiency. Cities, represented by Nanning and Liuzhou, have concentrated high-tech industries, modern service sectors, and large-scale manufacturing, which significantly boost productivity and GDP growth. In contrast, small towns often rely on traditional industries (e.g., agriculture, low-end manufacturing), with limited value-added capacity. For example, the proportion of the tertiary industry in small towns (33.19%) was 4.7% lower than in cities (37.89%) (Table 10), reflecting a slower transition to service-oriented economies. This disparity is also influenced by policy-driven investment gaps: cities attract more foreign direct investment and government infrastructure funding, while small towns face constraints in capital accumulation and industrial upgrading.

Analysis of mechanisms driving high-quality development in small towns

The driving mechanism for the spatio–temporal evolution of small town high quality development is a dynamic process of mutual influence, constraint, and synergistic enhancement. Drivers involve the government, enterprises, and residents. Driving factors can be classified into direct, indirect, basic, and important ones, with economic, social, and ecological factors collaborating in the “small-town high-quality development system” to boost development quality (Fig. 19). First of all, economic growth and industrial scale are direct factors. Economic growth is the primary impetus, and full-fledged industries (especially manufacturing) are the foundation. Township enterprises are crucial; they drive local economic growth, create jobs, raise residents’ incomes, and narrow the urban–rural income gap. Secondly, Population size and public services are basic factors. Population is essential for high quality development and industrial aggregation. Public service levels significantly impact shared development, the core of high-quality development. Again, the ecological environment and investment level are indirect factors. “Ecological advantages are unique”, and their driving role is growing. Higher investment improves infrastructure, facilitating external opening-up. Finally, the openness level and innovation ability are important factors. Openness strongly affects high-quality development, showing a positive correlation. Comparative analysis of research results and policy implications

Comparative analysis of the study conclusions

Research perspective and evaluation system

Existing studies on the development of small towns have mostly focused on a single level or a few aspects such as urbanization process and industrial structure transformation5,42. For example, Gu et al. focused on the policy impact and industrial transformation path in the urbanization process when studying the development of rural small towns in China. However, this study comprehensively constructs an evaluation index system from five dimensions: innovation, coordination, green, openness, and sharing, and more systematically measures the level of high-quality development of small towns. The system covers multiple fields such as economy, society, and ecology. For example, in the innovation dimension, R&D investment and innovation results transformation are considered; in the coordination dimension, the balance of urban and rural resource allocation is concerned; in the green dimension, the effectiveness of ecological environmental protection is emphasized; in the open dimension, foreign trade and foreign investment utilization are involved; in the sharing dimension, the popularization and fairness of public services are emphasized, etc., which provides a new perspective and comprehensive tool for the comprehensive evaluation of small town development and avoids the limitations of single-dimensional research. At the same time, by comparing the characteristics of high-quality development between small towns and cities, this study vividly demonstrates the differences between small towns and cities, and also highlights the unique advantages of small towns in urban-rural integration, making the research perspective more distinctive.

Most existing studies have not fully considered the dynamic evolution of regional differences when evaluating the development level of small towns. Although some studies involve regional differences, most of them are static analyses43,44. For example, when studying the development of small towns in Romania, Ianoș and other scholars pointed out that there are development differences among small towns in different regions, but did not conduct an in-depth analysis of the changing trend of such differences over time. In contrast, this study uses the Dagum Gini coefficient decomposition method to deeply analyze regional differences and their sources, and clearly shows the changing trend of differences in high-quality development of small towns in Guangxi in different economic zones, which helps to accurately grasp the problem of unbalanced regional development and provides a stronger basis for formulating targeted regional coordinated development policies.

Analysis of driving factors

Previous research on the driving factors of small town development mostly stayed at the macro level and lacked in-depth exploration of the driving mechanisms at the micro level30,26,27. For example, when scholars such as Chen discussed the process of new urbanization, although they mentioned the impact of various factors on urban development, they did not conduct an in-depth analysis of the microscopic mechanism of these factors within small towns. This study not only identifies the main driving factors at the economic, social, ecological and other levels, but also uses an optimized geodetector model to deeply explore the interactions between factors. The study found that in certain periods, there is a significant non-linear enhanced relationship between the proportion of fixed asset investment, the number of doctors per 1,000 people, and carbon dioxide emissions per unit of GDP. This provides a more detailed basis for understanding the intrinsic dynamic mechanism of small town development. Helps formulate strategies to promote the development of small towns more accurately.

Existing literature pays less attention to its spatiotemporal heterogeneity when exploring driving factors45. For example, when Vaishar and other scholars studied the sustainability of small towns in the Czech Republic, although they analyzed the development characteristics of different types of small towns, they did not deeply explore the changes in driving factors at different development stages and spatial regions. By detecting the influence of driving factors in stages, this study reveals the dynamic changes in the driving effects of economic, ecological and other factors on the high-quality development of small towns in different periods. For example, economic factors are always important but the influence of ecological factors gradually increases. This finding adds to the Existing research is deficient in the dynamic analysis of driving factors, which provides a reference for formulating adaptive policies at different stages of development.

Classification of development models

Existing studies have classified small town development models in a relatively simple way or focused on one aspect46. For example, Duan and Zhang mainly classified small towns based on limited indicators such as economic and population size when evaluating the comprehensive strength of towns in the Tarim Basin, which failed to fully reflect the multifaceted nature of small town development. Based on the cluster analysis of a multidimensional indicator system, this study divides Guangxi small towns into five different development models, including comprehensive leading type, backbone type, catching-up potential type, opportunity-driven type, and low-growth type, and elaborates on the characteristics and typical towns of each model. For example, comprehensive leading small towns have performed well in multiple dimensions. Beiliu Town and Matou Town, based on industrial development, focus on innovation-driven and green development, and achieve coordinated development of economy, society and environment; low-growth small towns are constrained by natural conditions and traditional concepts, and their development is relatively lagging behind. This classification provides a more targeted reference for formulating small town development strategies in accordance with local conditions, and helps to implement precise support policies based on the characteristics of different types of small towns.

High-quality development strategy of small towns in Guangxi

Put forward high quality development suggestions from the following five aspects: Firstly, optimize regional development planning and resource allocation. Increase investment in infrastructure construction in the western region, improve transportation and communication conditions, and enhance the basic support capacity for regional development. At the same time, formulate differentiated industrial development plans to drive regional economic growth and gradually narrow the development gap within the region43. Conclusions and outlook

Conclusions

This study systematically analyzed the high-quality development of 70 small towns in Guangxi (2005–2022) using a multidimensional evaluation system based on the new development philosophy. By integrating methods such as Entropy Weight-TOPSIS, Dagum Gini coefficient, and an optimized Geographic Detector, we explored spatiotemporal patterns, regional disparities, driving factors, and—importantly—conducted a dedicated comparison with cities to highlight small towns’ distinctive characteristics. The main conclusions are as follows:Spatiotemporal Evolution and Heterogeneity of Small Towns. Small towns showed a steady upward trend in high-quality development, with significant spatial differentiation (“southeast high, northwest low”). Their spatial pattern evolved from scattered points to contiguous agglomerations, with high-high clusters expanding in the southeast and low-low clusters in the northwest. This uneven development was shaped by natural conditions, economic foundations, and policy support.Multidimensional Progress and Typological Differences. All five subsystems (innovation, coordination, green, openness, sharing) improved, though coordination grew the fastest and openness the slowest. Cluster analysis identified five development types, with “comprehensive leading” towns excelling in multiple dimensions, while “low-growth” towns faced constraints in innovation and openness but retained ecological advantages.Regional Disparities: Narrowing but Persistent. Overall disparities in small towns’ development narrowed in three phases (sharp decline → slow decline → stabilization). Internal disparities were largest in the western economic zone and smallest in the southern zone, with inter-zone gaps and hypervariable density being key contributors to overall inequality.Driving Factors: Economic Dominance and Ecological Rising. Economic factors remained primary drivers, while ecological factors grew in influence. Factor interactions had stronger impacts than individual factors, reflecting the complexity of development mechanisms.Key Findings from Urban-Rural Comparison. Compared with cities, small towns lagged in economic growth, industrial upgrading, innovation, and public services but had higher carbon emission intensity, indicating structural reliance on traditional industries. However, this comparison also highlighted small towns’ unique role in urban-rural integration—they serve as bridges for resource allocation and ecological protection, complementing cities’ agglomeration advantages.

Outlook

Based on the new development concepts, we measured, analyzed, and simulated the high-quality development levels of 70 small towns in Guangxi, yielding significant results. However, due to the complexity of the issue and limitations in research capabilities, the study inevitably faced subjective judgments and insufficient depth of analysis. The specific shortcomings and areas for improvement are as follows:Further Optimization of the Evaluation and Driving Factor Indicator Systems. The currently constructed evaluation system largely relies on macro-level indicators, which may not fully capture the unique characteristics of Guangxi’s small towns. Future research should select indicators that reflect the local context, distinguishing them from the national or provincial high-quality development frameworks. Special consideration should be given to the ecological vulnerability of karst regions and the cultural diversity of ethnic minorities to achieve an objective understanding of high-quality development in Guangxi’s small towns.Micro-Level Research on Driving Forces and Regulatory Pathways. Investigating the micro-level driving forces behind high-quality development and determining the paradigms for micro-level high-quality development in small towns are essential areas for future research. Understanding these aspects will be crucial in formulating targeted strategies and policies that effectively address the unique challenges faced by small towns in achieving sustainable and inclusive growth.

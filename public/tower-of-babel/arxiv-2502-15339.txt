Robust and Indestructible Macroscopic Entanglement
Martina Gisti, Miguel Gallego, Borivoje Dakić

Abstract
In this letter we investigate the possibility of observing macroscopic entanglement, considering realistic factors such as decoherence, particle losses, and measurements of limited precision (coarse-grained collective measurements). This forms the foundation for an operational definition of robust macroscopic entanglement. We also propose an even more resilient concept of macroscopic entanglement, allowing the parties to randomly select their portion of the shared system. Through a straightforward example, we demonstrate that achieving this is feasible using a simple class of quantum states. Our result opens a path for generalizing notions of quantum entanglement, such as a genuine multipartite entanglement in the macroscopic domain.

In the past decades, we have seen a significant technological advancement to enable the manipulation and control of quantum systems with a large number of degrees of freedom (typically particle number), with notable examples including spin-squeezed atomic ensembles and Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) systems

To illustrate this point, we refer to a typical situation where quantum entanglement of a macroscopic system is inferred indirectly via the witness method, where a suitably chosen set of collective (additive) observables is measured to detect entanglement

In contrast, in this work, we focus on entanglement detection where the macroscopic systems are divided into two (macroscopic) parts on which the collective measurements are locally performed. This makes a more clear operational route towards defining the notion of a

. We then go further and introduce the even stronger notion of

Robust Macroscopic Entanglement (RME)

We now present an operational definition of macroscopic entanglement based on the observability of entanglement in the macroscopic limit under the effects of decoherence, particle losses, and coarse-grained measurements. For this purpose, we consider a macroscopic quantum measurement scenario similar to the one in references
[
38
,
17
,
14
,
20
]
. For simplicity, we restrict to the case of bipartite entanglement, the generalization to the multipartite case being straightforward (see figure
1
). A quantum system
\mathsf{S}
consisting of a large number
N
of particles or subsystems is prepared in some quantum state. The system is subject to the effects of independent single-particle decoherence (through some single-particle decoherence channel, such as the depolarizing channel) and particle losses (i.e., each particle has some probability of being lost before it can be measured). The system is then divided into two parts
\mathsf{S}_{A}
and
\mathsf{S}_{B}
, which are sent to respective measurement apparatuses
\mathsf{M}_{A}
and
\mathsf{M}_{B}
. Each measurement apparatus implements a collective, coarse-grained intensity measurement. Namely, apparatus
\mathsf{M}_{A}
measures a quantity of the form
\sum_{i}a_{i}
(where
a_{i}
is the outcome of a measurement on the
i
-th particle of
\mathsf{S}_{A}
) with a
precision
or
resolution
of the order of
\sqrt{N}
. Similarly, apparatus
\mathsf{M}_{B}
measures
\sum_{i}b_{i}
(where
b_{i}
is the outcome of a measurement on the
i
-th particle of
\mathsf{S}_{B}
) with resolution of order
\sqrt{N}
. We will then say that the state of the system possesses
robust macroscopic entanglement
(RME) if its entanglement is
observable
or
detectable
in the above circumstances in the limit of infinitely many particles. In the next section, we mathematically formalize this definition. It is worth pointing out that we have set explicitly the coarse-graining factor
\alpha=1/2
(measurement precision of the order
1/\sqrt{N}
), for which our main results are derived. Nevertheless, in the outlook section we comment on the possible extensions to arbitrary
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

Mathematical Framework for RME

Let us make precise the assumptions involved in the macroscopic quantum measurement scenario described in the previous section, i.e., let us assume that the quantum system
\mathsf{S}
satisfies the following conditions:

(
i
)

Large
N
.
The system is composed of a large number
N
of identical particles or subsystems.
1
1

1

The assumption that the subsystems be identical is made for the sake of simplicity
We associate a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
to each particle, so that the Hilbert space of the whole system is
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
, which is bipartitioned into Alice’s Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N_{A}}
and Bob’s
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N_{B}}
, where
N_{A}+N_{B}=N
(i.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}\cong\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
). The state of the system is described by a density matrix
\rho_{N}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N})
, i.e. a positive, self-adjoint bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
satisfying
\text{tr}\,\rho_{N}=1
.

(
ii
)

Decoherence.
The system is subject to independent, single-particle decoherence channels
\Gamma
. The effective state is therefore
\Gamma^{\otimes N}(\rho_{N})
.

(
iii
)

Particle losses.
Each individual particle is lost with probability
p\in[0,1]
, while
1-p
is its probability of reaching the measurement apparatuses. Therefore, on each run of the experiment only a number
M\leq N
of particles actually reaches the measurement apparatuses, and this occurs with probability
P_{N}(M)={N\choose M}p^{N-M}(1-p)^{M}
, in which case the effective state of the system is of the form
\text{tr}_{N-M}(\rho_{N})
.

Putting together these assumptions, we have that for a given initial
N
-particle state
\rho_{N}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N})
, the effective state of the system can be written in the ‘Fock space’
\oplus_{M=0}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes M}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{N}^{\text{eff}}=\bigoplus_{M=0}^{N}P_{N}(M)\,\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}}\frac{\text{tr}_{\pi(1)\dots\pi(N-M)}\Big[\Gamma^{\otimes N}(\rho_{N})\Big]}{N!}\,,

(2)

where
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is the symmetric group.

As for the measurement, we assume that Alice’s measurement apparatus
\mathsf{M}_{A}
satisfies the following conditions:

(
iv
)

Collective measurement
. The measurement apparatus implements the same
positive operator-valued measure
(POVM) on each particle or subsystem. To each outcome
a\in\mathbb{R}
of the POVM is associated a positive operator
E_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. This leads to the following joint distribution of outcomes

\displaystyle P(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})=\text{tr}\Big[\rho_{N}E_{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes E_{a_{N}}\Big]\,.

(3)

(
v
)

Intensity measurement.
The measurement apparatus has no access to individual single-particle outcomes
a
, but only to their sum
\sum_{i}a_{i}
(we leave the limits of the sum unspecified because the number of particles in the effective state
\rho_{N}^{\text{eff}}
is indeterminate).

(
vi
)

Coarse-graining.
The measuring scale for the intensity
\sum_{i}a_{i}
has a limited resolution of the order of
\sqrt{N}
, meaning that it cannot distinguish between values that differ by much less than
\sqrt{N}
. The accessible quantity is therefore
x_{N}=\sum_{i}a_{i}/\sqrt{N}
. For a discussion of how the coarse-graining assumption in the limit
N\to\infty
is implemented by dividing by a factor of
\sqrt{N}
, see
[
14
]
.

The same limitations apply to Bob’s measurement apparatus
\mathsf{M}_{B}
, whose accessible quantity is thus
y_{N}=\sum_{i}b_{i}/\sqrt{N}
.

As outlined before, we say that a state
\rho_{N}
possesses RME if its entanglement is observable in the above scenario in the limit
N\to\infty
, with the state being subject to assumptions
1
-
(iii)
and the measurements being subject to assumptions
(iv)
-
(vi)
. More explicitly,
\rho_{N}
possesses RME if there exists a set of measurements and experimental procedures consistent with
1
-
(vi)
such that we can detect entanglement, i.e. such that from the collected statistics we can show that

\displaystyle\rho_{N}\neq\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}\rho_{N_{A}}^{(s)}\otimes\rho_{N_{B}}^{(s)}\,.

In order for the robustness conditions to be satisfied, this means that there exists
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that for all
\Gamma
satisfying
\|\Gamma-\text{Id}\|<\epsilon
and for all
p<\delta
, the entanglement of
\rho_{N}^{\text{eff}}
is observable with measurements of coarse-grained intensities
x_{N}
and
y_{N}
. It is worth pointing out that these are necessary for robustness. Of course, as we shall see, RME can exist even in more ‘noisy scenarios,’ such as
p
being significantly larger than 0. To illustrate this idea, let us examine some examples and non-examples. First, consider a bipartite state where all but a pair of particles are in a separable state, for instance, a state of the form
|{\psi}\rangle_{AB}\otimes(|{\phi}\rangle_{A}\otimes|{\chi}\rangle_{B})^{\otimes(N-1)}
, where
|{\psi}\rangle_{AB}
is the state of a pair of particles shared by the parties and
|{\phi}\rangle_{A}
and
|{\chi}\rangle_{B}
are the states of the rest of Alice’s and Bob’s particles respectively. Then, only the first pair of particles is entangled, and its contribution to the quantities
x_{N}
and
y_{N}
vanishes in the limit
N\to\infty
, so that no entanglement can be observed in the limit. This is a case of microscopic entanglement between macroscopic systems that fails to satisfy our definition of RME. Next, consider a state chosen at random according to the Haar measure in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
. It has been shown
[
39
,
40
]
that this kind of state possesses a large amount of entanglement, but complicated measurements are required to observe it. We therefore expect the requirements of RME to fail. Another non-example is the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger
[
41
]
state
(|{0}\rangle^{\otimes N}+|{1}\rangle^{\otimes N})/\sqrt{2}
. This state is typically associated with macroscopic entanglement when
N
is large. Still, it is extremely fragile since the loss of coherence of a single particle destroys the coherence of the state altogether, collapsing it into a classical mixture. Indeed, we can see that such a state does not possess RME as we have defined it since all the components of its effective state (
2
) are separable except for the
N
-particle component, but this component has a weight
P_{N}(N)=(1-p)^{N}
which is vanishingly small in the macroscopic limit for all
p>0
. The other state typically associated with macroscopic entanglement is the W
[
42
]
state
(|10\dots 0\rangle+|01\dots 0\rangle+\dots+|00\dots 1\rangle)/{\sqrt{N}}
. We do expect this state to constitute an example of RME. However, we claim that a simpler state is sufficient to exhibit RME, namely an IID state, i.e. a state of the form
|{\psi}\rangle^{\otimes N}
, where
|{\psi}\rangle
is an entangled two-particle state shared by Alice and Bob. We now prove RME for such a state.

An example of RME

For this purpose, let us first introduce a slight generalization of the Duan-Simon entanglement criterion
[
36
,
37
]
. Consider a bipartite quantum state
\rho\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
are arbitrary Hilbert spaces, and suppose Alice and Bob both measure one of two observables, say
x_{A}
and
p_{A}
for Alice and
x_{B}
and
p_{B}
for Bob (these are arbitrary observables, i.e. not necessarily position and momentum observables as in the Duan-Simon criterion). Then, as we show in Appendix
A
, all separable states

satisfy
f\geq 0
with

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\text{Var}(x_{A}+x_{B})+\text{Var}(p_{A}-p_{B})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\big|\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle\big|-\big|\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle\big|\,,

(4)

where
\langle\xi\rangle=\text{tr}(\rho\,\xi)
and
\text{Var}(\xi)=\langle\xi^{2}\rangle-\langle\xi\rangle^{2}
. Now, consider the case where
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, so that
\rho
can be seen as a
2N
-particle state
\rho_{2N}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N})
. Furthermore, suppose that the observables measured by the parties are coarse-grained intensities as in assumptions
(iv)
-
(vi)
, i.e. suppose that

\displaystyle x_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad p_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,

\displaystyle x_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad p_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,

for some single-particle observables
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
for Alice and
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
for Bob. In this case, a violation of the inequality
f\geq 0
would be a sufficient criterion for RME. If the state is IID of the form
\rho_{2N}=(|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|)^{\otimes N}
, where
|{\psi}\rangle
is the state of two entangled particles shared by the two parties, then, as we show in Appendix
B
, the function
f
can be written without dependence on

as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\text{Var}(A_{1}+B_{1})+\text{Var}(A_{2}-B_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\big|\langle[A_{1},A_{2}]\rangle\big|-\big|\langle[B_{1},B_{2}]\rangle\big|\,,

(5)

where now
\langle X\rangle=\langle{\psi}\vphantom{X}|{X}\vphantom{\psi}|{\psi}\rangle
and
\text{Var}(X)=\langle X^{2}\rangle-\left\langle{X}\right\rangle^{2}
. In order to compute the maximal violation of the inequality, notice that its potential violation is unbounded since the function
f
is homogeneous in the observables
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
. It therefore makes sense to restrict the norm of these to be bounded, so we set
\|A_{1}\|,\|A_{2}\|,\|B_{1}\|,\|B_{2}\|\leq 1
. Then, for the simple case where
\dim\mathcal{H}=2
, we numerically compute the maximal violation over states
|{\psi}\rangle
and spin observables
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, and recognize the analytical value

\displaystyle f=4(1-\sqrt{2})\simeq-1.66

for

\displaystyle|{\psi}\rangle=\cos(\pi/8)|{00}\rangle-\sin(\pi/8)|{11}\rangle

(6)

and
A_{1}=B_{1}=\sigma_{x}
and
A_{2}=B_{2}=\sigma_{y}
, where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th Pauli matrix. Note that the state
|{\psi}\rangle
that gives the maximal violation for the entanglement inequality is not maximally entangled and, as a matter of fact, we did not find any violation of the inequality for maximally entangled two-qubit states.

In Appendix
C
we quantify the robustness of the violation against decoherence, particle losses and non-projective measurements. Fixing the state and the observables that give the maximal violation in the perfect case, the violation is still visible if the state is affected by the IID decoherence channel

with
\Gamma(|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|)=(1-\lambda)|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|+\lambda\,\mathbb{I}_{2}/2
being the depolarizing channel with

\displaystyle\lambda<\frac{3-\sqrt{1+4\sqrt{2}}}{2}\simeq 0.21\,.

If the state is affected by particle losses, with probability

\displaystyle p<2-\sqrt{2}\simeq 0.59\,.

And if instead of the observables
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
and

Indestructible Macroscopic Entanglement (IME)

Let us now introduce a stronger notion of macroscopic entanglement than RME, where the parties need not have perfect control (actually need not have control at all) over the exact bipartition of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2N}\cong\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
, which in the previous section was assumed to be given. We will say that a state
\rho_{2N}
possesses
indestructible macroscopic entanglement
(IME) if its entanglement is observable in the limit
N\to\infty
in the defined macroscopic scenario (i.e., subject to assumptions
1
-
(vi)
)
for a (average) random bipartition of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2N}
. This definition resembles genuine multipartite entanglement
[
15
]
but adapted to the macroscopic limit. While it may seem quite demanding and one might think it is theoretically empty, we surprisingly demonstrate that simple IID states are sufficient to meet the conditions for IME. Let us prove this.

In Appendix
E
we quantify the robustness of the violation (fixing the state and observables) against the effects of decoherence, losses and non-projective measurements. First, consider the effect of depolarization, captured by the single-particle channel
\Gamma(\sigma)=(1-\lambda)\sigma+\lambda\,\mathbb{I}_{3}/3
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Then the inequality
f\geq 0
is violated as long as

\displaystyle\lambda\lesssim 0.06\,.

Next, in order to consider the effect of particle losses, we assume each particle is lost with probability
p\in[0,1]
, reaching the detector with probability
1-p
. Then, the averaged inequality is violated for

\displaystyle p\lesssim 0.24.

Finally, consider the case where the measurement apparatus do not implement single-particle projective measurements but POVMs. Namely, let Alice measure
x_{A}=\sum_{i}{a_{i}}/{\sqrt{N}}

where each
a
is the outcome of a single-particle POVM with elements
E_{a}
, and suppose
||E_{a}-\Pi_{a}||<\epsilon
(similarly for Bob). Then, the inequality is violated for

\displaystyle\epsilon\lesssim 0.02.

This shows the robustness of the macroscopic entanglement for the state considered, thus proving IME.

Outlook

We have proposed an operational definition of macroscopic entanglement under the realistic assumptions
1
-
(vi)
. We have introduced two notions of macroscopic entanglement: RME and IME. The latter assembles ideas of genuine multiparty entanglement
[
15
]
adapted to such macroscopic measurements and robust scenarios. An interesting feature is that a non-linear witness (
5
) was necessary to find the entanglement criterion, as we failed to find an example with a linear witness. A similar result has been observed for continuous-variable Bell’s inequalities
[
43
]
, and this finding is definitely worth investigating further. Another relevant point to be investigated is the level of coarse-graining (or measurement precision), which we have set to the order of
\sqrt{N}
(i.e.
\alpha=1/2
). The witness equation (
5
) is homogenous, so it can be easily extended to arbitrary
\alpha
, yielding

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2\alpha-1}}\Big[\text{Var}(A_{1}+B_{1})+\text{Var}(A_{2}-B_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\big|\langle[A_{1},A_{2}]\rangle\big|-\big|\langle[B_{1},B_{2}]\rangle\big|\Big]\,.

What we see is that
f
diverges (as
N\to\infty
) for
\alpha<1/2
, while for
\alpha>1/2
we have
f\to 0
, meaning that entanglement cannot be detected in the macroscopic limit via the witness (
5
) in such case. This coincides with the results of
[
12
,
13
,
17
,
20
]
for device-independent quantum correlations, which transit to classical ones in this regime. While this is true for quantum nonlocality as a resource, it does not have to be necessarily true for entanglement. Thus, the question remains open whether quantum entanglement can still be detected in the macroscopic regime for
\alpha>1/2
.

Appendix A
Generalization of the Duan-Simon entanglement criterion

Consider the bipartite Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
and let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
be a separable state, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\,\rho_{A}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{B}^{(i)}

(12)

for some
p_{i}\geq 0
satisfying
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
and for some density matrices
\{\rho_{A}^{(i)}\}_{i}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
and
\{\rho_{B}^{(i)}\}_{i}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
. Let
x_{A}
and
p_{A}
be two arbitrary observables on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
, and let
x_{B}
and
p_{B}
be two arbitrary observables on
\mathcal{H}_{B}
. Then, letting
\langle x\rangle=\text{tr}(\rho x)
and
\langle x\rangle_{i}=\text{tr}(\rho_{A}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(i)}x)
we have

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A}+x_{B})

\displaystyle=\left\langle{(x_{A}+x_{B})^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x_{A}+x_{B}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\left\langle{(x_{A}+x_{B})^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}-\left\langle{x_{A}+x_{B}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big(\left\langle{x_{A}^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}+\left\langle{x_{B}^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}+2\,\left\langle{x_{A}x_{B}}\right\rangle_{i}\Big)-\left\langle{x_{A}+x_{B}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big(\left\langle{x_{A}^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}+\left\langle{x_{B}^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}+2\,\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle_{i}\left\langle{x_{B}}\right\rangle_{i}\Big)-\left\langle{x_{A}+x_{B}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big(\left\langle{x_{A}^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}-\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle_{i}^{2}+\left\langle{x_{B}^{2}}\right\rangle_{i}-\left\langle{x_{B}}\right\rangle_{i}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big(\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle_{i}^{2}+\left\langle{x_{B}}\right\rangle_{i}^{2}+2\,\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle_{i}\left\langle{x_{B}}\right\rangle_{i}\Big)-\left\langle{x_{A}+x_{B}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big({\Var}_{i}(x_{A})+{\Var}_{i}(x_{B})\Big)+\sum_{i}p_{i}\big(\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle_{i}+\left\langle{x_{B}}\right\rangle_{i}\big)^{2}-\left\langle{x_{A}+x_{B}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big({\Var}_{i}(x_{A})+{\Var}_{i}(x_{B})\Big)\,,

(13)

where
\Var_{i}(x)=\text{tr}(\rho_{A}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(i)}x^{2})-\text{tr}(\rho_{A}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(i)}x)^{2}
and in the last line we have used that
\sum_{i}p_{i}{\langle x\rangle_{i}}^{2}\geq\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}|\langle x\rangle_{i}|\right)^{2}\geq\langle x\rangle^{2}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\Var(p_{A}-p_{B})\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big({\Var}_{i}(p_{A})+{\Var}_{i}(p_{B})\Big)\,.

(14)

Hence

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A}+x_{B})+\Var(p_{A}-p_{B})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big({\Var}_{i}(x_{A})+{\Var}_{i}(x_{B})+{\Var}_{i}(p_{A})+{\Var}_{i}(p_{B})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\Bigg[\left(\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(x_{A})}-\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(p_{A})}\right)^{2}+2\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(x_{A})}\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(p_{A})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(x_{B})}-\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(p_{B})}\right)^{2}+2\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(x_{B})}\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(p_{B})}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}\left(2\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(x_{A})}\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(p_{A})}+2\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(x_{B})}\sqrt{{\Var}_{i}(p_{B})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}\left(\big|\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle_{i}\big|+\big|\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle_{i}\big|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\big|\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle\big|+\big|\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle\big|\,,

(15)

where in the second to last line we have used the uncertainty principle
[
44
]
, proving Equation (
4
) in the main text.

Appendix B
Inequality for IID states and coarse-grained collective observables

We can rewrite the inequality (
15
) as

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})+\Var(x_{B})+2\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})+\Var(p_{A})+\Var(p_{B})-2\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})\geq\big|\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle\big|+\big|\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle\big|\,,

(16)

where
\Cov(x,y)=\langle x\otimes y\rangle-\langle x\rangle\langle y\rangle
. If the state is IID, i.e.
\rho_{2N}=(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)^{\otimes N}
for some
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
, and the observables are coarse-grained collective observables of the form

\displaystyle x_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad p_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,

\displaystyle x_{B}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{B_{1}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad p_{B}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{B_{2}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,

(17)

we have

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})

\displaystyle=\langle x_{A}^{2}\rangle-\langle x_{A}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}-\left(\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}(A_{1}^{(i)})^{2}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}+\sum_{i\neq j}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big(N\langle\psi|A_{1}^{2}|\psi\rangle+N(N-1)\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle^{2}\Big)-\left(\sqrt{N}\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|A_{1}^{2}|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{1}^{2}\rangle-\langle A_{1}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\Var(A_{1})\,,

(18)

where now
\langle X\rangle=\langle\psi|X|\psi\rangle
and
\Var(X)=\langle X^{2}\rangle-\langle X\rangle^{2}
. Similarly,
\Var(x_{B})=\Var(B_{1})
,
\Var(p_{A})=\Var(A_{2})
and
\Var(p_{B})=\Var(B_{2})
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})

\displaystyle=\langle x_{A}\otimes x_{B}\rangle-\langle x_{A}\rangle\langle x_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\otimes\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{B_{1}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}-\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\,\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}\otimes B_{1}^{(j)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}B_{1}^{(i)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big(N\langle\psi|A_{1}\otimes B_{1}|\psi\rangle+N(N-1)\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|B_{1}|\psi\rangle\Big)-\left(\sqrt{N}\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\right)\left(\sqrt{N}\langle\psi|B_{1}|\psi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\langle A_{1}\otimes B_{1}\rangle-\langle A_{1}\rangle\langle B_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Cov(A_{1},B_{1})\,.

(19)

and similarly
\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})=\Cov(A_{2},B_{2})
. Finally,

\displaystyle\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}},\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{A_{2}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right]|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\big[A_{1}^{(i)},A_{2}^{(j)}\big]|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\big[A_{1}^{(i)},A_{2}^{(i)}\big]|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|\big[A_{1},A_{2}\big]|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[A_{1},A_{2}]\rangle\,,

(20)

and similarly
\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle=\langle[B_{1},B_{2}]\rangle
. Therefore, the inequality reads

\displaystyle\Var(A_{1}+B_{1})+\Var(A_{2}-B_{2})\geq\big|\langle[A_{1},A_{2}]\rangle\big|+\big|\langle[B_{1},B_{2}]\rangle\big|\,,

(21)

proving Equation (
5
).

Appendix C
Robustness of the RME

C.1
Decoherence

Consider the single-particle depolarizing channel

\displaystyle\Gamma:\rho\mapsto(1-\lambda)\rho+\frac{\lambda}{d}\,\mathbb{I}_{d}\,,

(22)

with
\lambda\in[0,1]
. For a two-particle state
\rho
we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\otimes\Gamma:\rho\mapsto(1-\lambda)^{2}\rho+\lambda(1-\lambda)\Big(\text{tr}_{B}\rho\otimes\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{d}+\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{d}\otimes\text{tr}_{A}\rho\Big)+\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{d}\otimes\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{d}.

(23)

For our case where
d=2
, by a similar calculation as before we have

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\Big[(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma)(\rho)A_{1}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{I}\Big]-\text{tr}\Big[(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma)(\rho)A_{1}\otimes\mathbb{I}\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\langle A_{1}^{2}\rangle+\lambda\,\text{tr}\frac{A_{1}^{2}}{2}-\left((1-\lambda)\langle A_{1}\rangle+\lambda\,\text{tr}\frac{A_{1}}{2}\right)^{2}\,,

(24)

and analogously for
p_{A}
,
x_{B}
and
p_{B}
. On the other hand

\displaystyle\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\Big[(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma)(\rho)A_{1}\otimes B_{1}\Big]-\text{tr}\Big[(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma)(\rho)A_{1}\Big]\text{tr}\Big[(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma)(\rho)B_{1}\Big]

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)^{2}\langle A_{1}\otimes B_{1}\rangle+\lambda(1-\lambda)\langle A_{1}\rangle\,\text{tr}\frac{B_{1}}{2}+\lambda(1-\lambda)\langle B_{1}\rangle\text{tr}\frac{A_{1}}{2}+\lambda^{2}\,\text{tr}\frac{A_{1}}{2}\,\text{tr}\frac{B_{1}}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\left((1-\lambda)\langle A_{1}\rangle+\lambda\,\text{tr}\frac{A_{1}}{2}\right)\left((1-\lambda)\langle B_{1}\rangle+\lambda\,\text{tr}\frac{B_{1}}{2}\right)\,

(25)

and analogously for
\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})
. Finally,

\displaystyle\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\Big[(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma)(\rho)[A_{1},A_{2}]\Big]

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\langle[A_{1},A_{2}]\rangle+\lambda\,\text{tr}\frac{[A_{1},A_{2}]}{2}\,,

(26)

and similarly for
\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle
. Now, for the particular case with the state
\rho=|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|
with
|{\psi}\rangle=\cos\frac{\pi}{8}|{00}\rangle-\sin\frac{\pi}{8}|{11}\rangle
and the observables
A_{1}=B_{1}=\sigma_{x}
and
A_{2}=B_{2}=\sigma_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})=\Var(x_{B})=\Var(p_{A})=\Var(p_{B})=1\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})=-\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}

(28)

and

\displaystyle\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle=\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle=i\sqrt{2}(1-\lambda)\,.

(29)

With these expressions, the inequality is violated if

\displaystyle\lambda<\frac{3-\sqrt{1+4\sqrt{2}}}{2}\simeq 0.21\,.

(30)

C.2
Particle losses

We now compute the maximal loss probability
p
such that the effective state

\displaystyle\rho_{2N}^{\text{eff}}=\bigoplus_{M=0}^{2N}P_{2N}(M)\,\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{2N}}\frac{\text{tr}_{\pi(1)\dots\pi(2N-M)}\big[(|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|)^{\otimes N}\big]}{(2N)!}

(31)

gives a violation of the entanglement inequality with observables of the form

\displaystyle x_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad p_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,

\displaystyle x_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad p_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{B_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,.

(32)

However, in order to perform this calculation, it turns simpler to account for the effect of particle losses not in the state, as in Equation (
31
), but in the measurements (
32
). For this purpose, let us introduce a random variable
\alpha_{i}
to each of Alice’s particles, where
\alpha_{i}=0
with probability
p
and
\alpha_{i}=1
with probability
1-p
, and a random variable
\beta_{i}
to each of Bob’s, where
\beta_{i}=0
with probability
p
and
\beta_{i}=1
with probability
1-p
. Then, it can be seen that measuring observables (
32
) on the state
\rho_{2N}^{\text{eff}}
is equivalent to measuring the observables

\displaystyle x_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad p_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}},

\displaystyle x_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{i}B_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad p_{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{i}B_{2}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}.

(33)

on the state
(|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|)^{\otimes N}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})

\displaystyle=\langle x_{A}^{2}\rangle-\langle x_{A}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]-\left(\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big\{N(1-p)\langle\psi|A_{1}^{2}|\psi\rangle+N(N-1)(1-p)^{2}\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\Big\}-\frac{1}{N}\Big(N(1-p)\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-p)\langle A_{1}^{2}\rangle-(1-p)^{2}\langle A_{1}\rangle^{2}\,.

(34)

Similarly,
\Var(x_{B})=(1-p)\langle B_{1}^{2}\rangle-(1-p)^{2}\langle B_{1}\rangle^{2}
,
\Var(p_{A})=(1-p)\langle A_{2}^{2}\rangle-(1-p)^{2}\langle A_{2}\rangle^{2}
and
\Var(p_{B})=(1-p)\langle B_{2}^{2}\rangle-(1-p)^{2}\langle B_{2}\rangle^{2}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})

\displaystyle=\langle x_{A}\otimes x_{B}\rangle-\langle x_{A}\rangle\langle x_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\E_{\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\}}\left[\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\otimes\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{j}B_{1}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]\right)\left(\E_{\{\beta_{j}\}}\left[\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{j}B_{1}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E_{\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\}}\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}\otimes B_{1}^{(j)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{N}\left(\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}A_{1}^{(i)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]\right)\left(\E_{\{\beta_{j}\}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\beta_{j}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}B_{1}^{(j)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big\{N(1-p)^{2}\langle\psi|A_{1}\otimes B_{1}|\psi\rangle+N(N-1)(1-p)^{2}\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\langle\langle\psi|B_{1}|\psi\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{N}\Big(N(1-p)\langle\psi|A_{1}|\psi\rangle\Big)\Big(N(1-p)\langle\psi|B_{1}|\psi\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{2}\langle A_{1}\otimes B_{1}\rangle-(1-p)^{2}\langle A_{1}\rangle\langle B_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{2}\Cov(A_{1},B_{1})

(35)

and similarly
\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})=(1-p)^{2}\Cov(A_{2},B_{2})
. Finally,

\displaystyle\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}},\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{j}A_{2}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right]|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\sum_{i,j=0}^{N}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\big[A_{1}^{(i)},A_{2}^{(j)}\big]|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha_{i}^{2}\langle\psi|^{\otimes N}\big[A_{1}^{(i)},A_{2}^{(i)}\big]|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big(N(1-p)\langle\psi|\big[A_{1},A_{2}\big]|\psi\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-p)\langle[A_{1},A_{2}]\rangle\,,

(36)

and similarly
\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle=(1-p)\langle[B_{1},B_{2}]\rangle
. For the particular case with the state
\rho=|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|
with
|{\psi}\rangle=\cos\frac{\pi}{8}|{00}\rangle-\sin\frac{\pi}{8}|{11}\rangle
and the observables
A_{1}=B_{1}=\sigma_{x}
and
A_{2}=B_{2}=\sigma_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})=\Var(p_{A})=\Var(x_{B})=\Var(p_{B})=1-p\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})=-\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1-p)^{2}

(38)

and

\displaystyle\langle[x_{A},p_{A}]\rangle=\langle[x_{B},p_{B}]\rangle=i\sqrt{2}(1-p)\,.

(39)

With these expressions, the inequality is violated if

\displaystyle p<2-\sqrt{2}\simeq 0.59\,.

(40)

C.3
Non-projective measurements

Let us now suppose that instead of the projective measurements
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
that lead to the maximal violation of the inequality (
A_{1}=B_{1}=\sigma_{x}
,
A_{2}=B_{2}=\sigma_{y}
), some POVMs with elements
\{E_{1}^{(1)},E_{1}^{(-1)}\}
,
\{E_{2}^{(1)},E_{2}^{(-1)}\}
,
\{F_{1}^{(1)},F_{1}^{(-1)}\}
and
\{F_{2}^{(1)},F_{2}^{(-1)}\}
are actually measured. Let us also suppose that instead of the theoretical commutators
[A_{1},A_{2}]=2i\sigma_{z}=:2iA_{3}
and
[B_{1},B_{2}]=:2i\sigma_{z}=:2iB_{3}
, some POVMs with elements
\{E_{3}^{(1)},E_{3}^{(-1)}\}
and
\{F_{3}^{(1)},F_{3}^{(-1)}\}
are actually measured. For this, we write the projective decomposition of the single-particle observables

\displaystyle A_{j}=\sum_{a\in\{1,-1\}}a\,P_{j}^{(a)}=P_{j}^{(1)}-P_{j}^{(-1)}\,,\quad B_{j}=\sum_{b\in\{1,-1\}}b\,Q_{j}^{(b)}=Q_{j}^{(1)}-Q_{j}^{(-1)}\,,\quad j\in\{1,2,3\}\,,

(41)

where
P_{j}^{(a)}
(
Q_{j}^{(b)}
) is the projector onto the subspace associated with eigenvalue
a
(
b
) for observable
A_{j}
(
B_{j}
). Then, we let the POVM elements be
\epsilon
-close to the corresponding projectors, that is,

\displaystyle E_{j}^{(a)}=P_{j}^{(a)}+\epsilon C_{j}^{(a)}\,,\quad F_{j}^{(b)}=Q_{j}^{(b)}+\epsilon D_{j}^{(b)}\,,

(42)

where
\epsilon>0
and
C_{j}^{(a)}
and
D_{j}^{(b)}
have norm 1. Completeness of the POVMs implies

\displaystyle C_{j}^{(1)}=-C_{j}^{(-1)}=:C_{j}\,,\quad D_{j}^{(1)}=-D_{j}^{(-1)}=:D_{j}\,.

(43)

Hermiticity of the POVM elements implies hermiticity of the
C_{j}
’s and
D_{j}
’s. Finally we have positive semi-definiteness of the POVM elements:

\displaystyle E_{j}^{(a)}

\displaystyle=P_{j}^{(a)}+\epsilon aC_{j}\geq 0\,,

\displaystyle F_{j}^{(b)}

\displaystyle=Q_{j}^{(a)}+\epsilon bD_{j}\geq 0\,.

(44)

With this we have

\displaystyle\text{Var}(x_{A})

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x_{A}^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle\left(\frac{\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}a_{j}}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}}\right\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}^{2}}\right\rangle+\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle{a_{i}a_{j}}\right\rangle\right\}-\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}}\right\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{a^{2}}\right\rangle+(N-1)\left\langle{a_{1}a_{2}}\right\rangle-N\left\langle{a}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{a^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{a}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}a^{2}\,\text{tr}\rho E_{1}^{(a)}-\left(\sum_{a}a\,\text{tr}\rho E_{1}^{(a)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\rho(E_{1}^{(1)}+E_{1}^{(-1)})-\left(\text{tr}\rho(E_{1}^{(1)}-E_{1}^{(-1)})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\rho(P_{1}^{(1)}+\epsilon C_{1}+P_{1}^{(-1)}-\epsilon C_{1})-\left(\text{tr}\rho(P_{1}^{(1)}+\epsilon C_{1}-P_{1}^{(-1)}+\epsilon C_{1})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\rho A_{1}^{2}-\left(\text{tr}\rho(A_{1}+2\epsilon C_{1})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle-(\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{C_{1}}\right\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{Var}(A_{1})-4\epsilon\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{C_{1}}\right\rangle-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{C_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,.

(45)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(x_{B})

\displaystyle=\text{Var}(B_{1})-4\epsilon\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{D_{1}}\right\rangle-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{D_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(p_{A})

\displaystyle=\text{Var}(A_{2})-4\epsilon\left\langle{A_{2}}\right\rangle\left\langle{C_{2}}\right\rangle-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{C_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(p_{B})

\displaystyle=\text{Var}(B_{2})-4\epsilon\left\langle{B_{2}}\right\rangle\left\langle{D_{2}}\right\rangle-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{D_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\,.

(46)

On the other hand

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(x_{A},x_{B})

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x_{A}x_{B}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle\left\langle{x_{B}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\left(\frac{\sum_{i}a_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\left(\frac{\sum_{i}b_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}b_{j}}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}}\right\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{b_{i}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}b_{i}}\right\rangle+\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle{a_{i}b_{j}}\right\rangle\right\}-\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{a_{i}}\right\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle{b_{i}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{ab}\right\rangle+(N-1)\left\langle{a_{1}b_{2}}\right\rangle-N\left\langle{a}\right\rangle\left\langle{b}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{ab}\right\rangle-\left\langle{a}\right\rangle\left\langle{b}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}ab\,\text{tr}\rho E_{1}^{(a)}\otimes F_{1}^{(b)}-\left(\sum_{a}a\,\text{tr}\rho E_{1}^{(a)}\right)\left(\sum_{b}b\,\text{tr}\rho F_{1}^{(b)}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\rho(E_{1}^{(1)}-E_{1}^{(-1)})\otimes(F_{1}^{(1)}-F_{1}^{(-1)})-\left(\text{tr}\rho(E_{1}^{(1)}-E_{1}^{(-1)})\right)\left(\text{tr}\rho(F_{1}^{(1)}-F_{1}^{(-1)})\right)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\rho(P_{1}^{(1)}+\epsilon C_{1}-P_{1}^{(-1)}+\epsilon C_{1})\otimes(Q_{1}^{(1)}+\epsilon D_{1}-Q_{1}^{(-1)}+\epsilon D_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\text{tr}\rho(P_{1}^{(1)}+\epsilon C_{1}-P_{1}^{(-1)}+\epsilon C_{1})\right)\left(\text{tr}\rho(Q_{1}^{(1)}+\epsilon D_{1}-Q_{1}^{(-1)}+\epsilon D_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\rho(A_{1}+2\epsilon C_{1})\otimes(B_{1}+2\epsilon D_{1})-\left(\text{tr}\rho(A_{1}+2\epsilon C_{1})\right)\left(\text{tr}\rho(B_{1}+2\epsilon D_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes D_{1}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{C_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle+4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{C_{1}\otimes D_{1}}\right\rangle-(\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{C_{1}}\right\rangle)(\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{D_{1}}\right\rangle)

\displaystyle=\text{Cov}(A_{1},B_{1})+2\epsilon\,\text{Cov}(A_{1},D_{1})+2\epsilon\,\text{Cov}(C_{1},B_{1})+4\epsilon^{2}\text{Cov}(C_{1},D_{1})\,.

(47)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(p_{A},p_{B})=\text{Cov}(A_{2},B_{2})+2\epsilon\,\text{Cov}(A_{2},D_{2})+2\epsilon\,\text{Cov}(C_{2},B_{2})+4\epsilon^{2}\text{Cov}(C_{2},D_{2})\,.

(48)

Finally, the commutators are

\displaystyle\left\langle{[x_{A},p_{A}]}\right\rangle=2i(\left\langle{A_{3}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{C_{3}}\right\rangle)\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle{[x_{B},p_{B}]}\right\rangle=2i(\left\langle{B_{3}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{D_{3}}\right\rangle)\,.

(49)

For the particular case with the state
\rho=|{\psi}\rangle\langle{\psi}|
with
|{\psi}\rangle=\cos\frac{\pi}{8}|{00}\rangle-\sin\frac{\pi}{8}|{11}\rangle
and the observables
A_{1}=B_{1}=\sigma_{x}
and
A_{2}=B_{2}=\sigma_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Var}(x_{A})

\displaystyle=1-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{C_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(x_{B})

\displaystyle=1-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{D_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(p_{A})

\displaystyle=1-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{C_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(p_{B})

\displaystyle=1-4\epsilon^{2}\left\langle{D_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

(50)

and

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(x_{A},x_{B})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+2\epsilon\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes D_{1}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{C_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle+4\epsilon^{2}\text{Cov}(C_{1},D_{1})\,,

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(p_{A},p_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+2\epsilon\left\langle{A_{2}\otimes D_{2}}\right\rangle+2\epsilon\left\langle{C_{2}\otimes B_{2}}\right\rangle+4\epsilon^{2}\text{Cov}(C_{2},D_{2})\,,

(51)

and

\displaystyle\left\langle{[x_{A},p_{A}]}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{2}+4i\epsilon\left\langle{C_{3}}\right\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\left\langle{[x_{B},p_{B}]}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{2}+4i\epsilon\left\langle{D_{3}}\right\rangle\,.

(52)

Optimizing for the wirst case scenario with the
C_{j}
’s and
D_{j}
’s Hermitian norm-1 operators, we find that the violation of the inequality is still visible as long as

\displaystyle\epsilon<\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{3\sqrt{2}}\simeq 0.10\,.

(53)

Appendix D
Inequality for a
q
-bipartition of IID States

For a
q
-bipartition of the
2N
particle system described by Equation (
7
) we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{x_{A}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\left(\rho_{2N}(q)\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})(2N_{A}+N_{AB})\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}2Nq\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2q\sqrt{N}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\,,

(54)

where
\langle A_{1}\rangle=\text{tr}\big[\sigma A_{1}\big]
, and

\displaystyle\left\langle{x_{A}^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\left(\rho_{2N}(q)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\sum_{i,j=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\sum_{i,j=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\Bigg\{\sum_{i,j=1}^{2N_{A}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)+2\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}}\sum_{j=2N_{A}+1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i,j=2N_{A}+1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(i)}\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(i-(-1)^{i})}\Big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
j\neq i\\
j\neq i-(-1)^{i}\end{subarray}}^{2N_{A}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+2(2N_{A})N_{AB}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}+\sum_{i=2N_{A}+1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(i)}\Big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=2N_{A}+1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}A_{1}^{(i)}A_{1}^{(j)}\Big)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\bigg\{2N_{A}\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+2N_{A}\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes A_{1}}\right\rangle+2N_{A}(2N_{A}-2)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}+2(2N_{A})N_{AB}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+N_{AB}\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+N_{AB}(N_{AB}-1)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\bigg\{(2N_{A}+N_{AB})\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+2N_{A}\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes A_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big(2N_{A}(2N_{A}-2)+2(2N_{A})N_{AB}+N_{AB}(N_{AB}-1)\Big)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=2q\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+2q^{2}\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes A_{1}}\right\rangle+4(N-1)q^{2}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

(55)

so that

\displaystyle\Var(x_{A})=2q\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+2q^{2}\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes A_{1}}\right\rangle-4q^{2}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,.

(56)

Similarly

\displaystyle\Var(x_{B})

\displaystyle=2(1-q)\left\langle{B_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+2(1-q)^{2}\left\langle{B_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle-4(1-q)^{2}\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\Var(p_{A})

\displaystyle=2q\left\langle{A_{2}^{2}}\right\rangle+2q^{2}\left\langle{A_{2}\otimes A_{2}}\right\rangle-4q^{2}\left\langle{A_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\Var(p_{B})

\displaystyle=2(1-q)\left\langle{B_{2}^{2}}\right\rangle+2(1-q)^{2}\left\langle{B_{2}\otimes B_{2}}\right\rangle-4(1-q)^{2}\left\langle{B_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\,.

(57)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left\langle{x_{A}\otimes x_{B}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\left(\rho_{2N}(q)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2N_{B}+N_{AB}}\frac{B_{1}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\sum_{j=1}^{2N_{B}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}B_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}}\sum_{j=1}^{2N_{B}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}B_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=2N_{A}+1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\sum_{j=1}^{2N_{B}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}B_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=2N_{A}+1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\sum_{j=1}^{2N_{B}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}B_{1}^{(j)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=2N_{A}+1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\sum_{j=2N_{B}+1}^{2N_{B}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}A_{1}^{(i)}B_{1}^{(j)}\Big)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\bigg\{(2N_{A})(2N_{B})\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle+2N_{A}N_{AB}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle+N_{AB}2N_{B}\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+N_{AB}\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle+N_{AB}(N_{AB}-1)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\bigg\{\Big((2N_{A}+N_{AB})(2N_{B}+N_{AB})-N_{AB}\Big)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle+N_{AB}\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle\bigg\}

\displaystyle=4(N-1)q(1-q)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle+2q(1-q)\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle\,,

(58)

so that

\displaystyle\Cov(x_{A},x_{B})=2q(1-q)\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle-4q(1-q)\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle\,.

(59)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Cov(p_{A},p_{B})=2q(1-q)\left\langle{A_{2}\otimes B_{2}}\right\rangle-4q(1-q)\left\langle{A_{2}}\right\rangle\left\langle{B_{2}}\right\rangle\,.

(60)

Finally,

\displaystyle\left\langle{[x_{A},p_{A}]}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\left(\rho_{2N}(q)\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\frac{A_{1}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\sum_{j=1}^{2N_{B}+N_{AB}}\frac{A_{2}^{(j)}}{\sqrt{N}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\sum_{i,j=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}\otimes\sigma_{BB}^{\otimes N_{B}}[A_{1}^{(i)},A_{2}^{(j)}]\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N_{A}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{B}=0}^{N-N_{A}}P_{N}(q,N_{A},N_{B})\sum_{i=1}^{2N_{A}+N_{AB}}\text{tr}\Big(\sigma_{AA}^{\otimes N_{A}}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{\otimes N_{AB}}[A_{1}^{(i)},A_{2}^{(i)}]\Big)

\displaystyle=2q\left\langle{[A_{1},A_{2}]}\right\rangle\,.

(61)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\langle{[x_{B},p_{B}]}\right\rangle=2(1-q)\left\langle{[B_{1},B_{2}]}\right\rangle\,.

(62)

Therefore, the inequality reads

\displaystyle 2q\Big(\left\langle{A_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+q\left\langle{A_{1}\otimes A_{1}}\right\rangle-2q\left\langle{A_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+2(1-q)\Big(\left\langle{B_{1}^{2}}\right\rangle+(1-q)\left\langle{B_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\right\rangle-2(1-q)\left\langle{B_{1}}\right\rangle^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+2q\Big(\left\langle{A_{2}^{2}}\right\rangle+q\left\langle{A_{2}\otimes A_{2}}\right\rangle-2q\left\langle{A_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+2(1-q)\Big(\left\langle{B_{2}^{2}}\right\rangle+(1-q)\left\langle{B_{2}\otimes B_{2}}\right\rangle-2(1-q)\left\langle{B_{2}}\right\rangle^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 2q|\left\langle{[A_{1},A_{2}]}\right\rangle|+2(1-q)|\left\langle{[B_{1},B_{2}]}\right\rangle|\,,

Connectedness properties of small minimal clusters in Riemannian or Finsler manifolds
S. Nardulli, A. Pratelli

Abstract
. We prove that in a compact Riemannian manifold, the m m -minimal clusters of sufficiently small total volume are connected and have small diameter, while in a more general Finsler manifold they are made up of at most m m connected components of small diameter. We apply these results to calculate the asymptotic expansion of the multi-isoperimetric profile at the first nontrivial order, for small volumes.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in the isoperimetric problem for clusters in a Riemannian or a Finsler manifold, in particular in the connectedness properties of small minimal clusters. In fact, while it is clear that a generic minimal cluster could be made up of an arbitrary number of connected components, the situation changes when considering minimal clusters of
small
volume. Indeed, since the manifold locally looks like
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a fixed convex norm (and in particular, the norm is the Euclidean one if the manifold is of Riemannian type), one can guess that minimal clusters of small volume behave more or less like minimal clusters in situations with a fixed norm, which are well known to be connected.

The situation for isoperimetric sets, which correspond to
m
-clusters for
m=1
, is already quite well known; see for instance
[
8
,
7
,
11
,
10
]
and the references therein. In particular, in
[
8
]
it is shown that small isoperimetric sets are connected, while in the recent paper
[
7
]
it is shown that, in the case of a Riemannian manifold, they are also geodesically convex and quantitatively close to the corresponding Wulff shapes.

What we are able to show in this paper is that sufficiently small minimal
m
-clusters are actually connected in a compact Riemannian manifold, and they are made up of at most
m
connected components for a compact Finsler manifold. In particular, this gives the connectedness of small isoperimetric sets (i.e.,
m
-clusters with
m=1
) also in the case of a Finsler manifold. Our main result for Finsler manifolds (the relative definitions are given in Section
1.1
) can be stated as follows.

Theorem A
(Connectedness: the Finsler case).

Let
M
be an
N
-dimensional,
{\rm C}^{2}
, compact Finsler manifold (not necessarily reversible), and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given. There exist two constants
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
and
K>0
such that any minimal
m
-cluster
\mathcal{E}
with total volume
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|<\bar{\varepsilon}
is made up of at most
m
connected components
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. In addition, each connected component satisfies

(1.1)

{\rm diam}(\mathcal{E}_{i})\leq K\big||\mathcal{E}_{i}|\big|^{1/N}\,.

The main result for Riemannian manifolds is the following.

Theorem B
(Connectedness: the Riemannian case).

Let
M
be an
N
-dimensional,
{\rm C}^{2}
, compact Riemannian manifold, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given. There exists a constant
\overline{\overline{\varepsilon}}>0
such that any minimal cluster
\mathcal{E}
with total volume
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|<\overline{\overline{\varepsilon}}
is connected.

In fact, what we will prove is slightly stronger. Indeed, the claim is true not only if the manifold is Riemannian, but also if it is “almost Riemannian”; this means that the infinitesimal norm at each point is not necessarily exactly the Euclidean one, but it is close enough to it. The precise definitions and the proof of this stronger result are contained in the final Section
5.1
. In that section we will also discuss the possibility to further extend Theorem B. Indeed, one could think that the result might be true for any compact Finsler manifold, which would then make Theorem A useless, but the claim is false in this generality. Instead, it is reasonable to guess the validity of the claim for all the “fixed norm manifolds”; see Definition
1.5
and Remark
5.4
.

One of the main tools that we will use in the proof of our main results is Theorem
2.1
, which is an extremely important result of independent interest. The claim of this result is that a sufficiently small connected component of a minimal cluster must have small diameter, in particular the diameter can be uniformly bounded by the
N
-th root of the total volume (see in particular Corollary
2.2
). A result saying that “small volume implies small diameter” is very useful, but it was never proved before for the case of clusters.

Our results are particularly valuable, as they represent, to the best of our knowledge, the first instance in the literature where the global problem for clusters of small volumes can be rigorously reduced to a local one. It thereby provides a formal proof of a widely expected phenomenon: namely, that isoperimetric clusters of small volumes are small perturbations of the isoperimetric clusters for the corresponding infinitesimal problem in the tangent space. The uniform control of the diameter of an isoperimetric cluster in terms of its volume and the geometric bounds of the ambient manifold paves the way for extending the well-established theory in the case
m=1
to the general case of arbitrary
m\geq 1
.

We believe that the ideas developed in this work are also useful for proving deep results that are genuinely new even in the case
m=1
, with potential applications in the theory of existence and multiplicity of solutions to various phase transition models arising in realistic physical contexts (see
[
3
,
2
]
for comparisons). A first, simple and direct consequence of our results, is that the (multi)-isoperimetric profile in a compact Riemannian manifold is asymptotically close to that of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the standard Euclidean metric for small total volumes. This result –which is highly expected– generalizes the classical result by Bèrard and Meyer
[
5
]
relative to the case
m=1
. For
m=2
, this was recently established in
[
2
, Corollary 3.28]
using the full resolution of the double bubble conjecture. In contrast, we can now immediately obtain the completely general case of any
m
as a simple consequence of the sole Theorem
2.1
, even in the Finsler case.

Theorem 1.1

(Asymptotic expansion of the multi-isoperimetric profile for small volumes)
.

Let
M
be an
N
-dimensional,
{\rm C}^{2}
, compact Riemannian manifold, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given. Then

(1.2)

\frac{J_{M}(v)}{J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)}\to 1\qquad\hbox{as}\ |v|_{1}\to 0\,,

where
J_{M}
and
J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
denote the multi-isoperimetric profiles of
M
and of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the Euclidean metric, and for any vector
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
we denote by
|v|_{1}=v_{1}+v_{2}+\cdots+v_{m}
the
L^{1}
norm, which is the “total volume” of an
m
-cluster whose volume is
v
. More generally, if
M
is a compact Finsler manifold, then the following expansion holds,

(1.3)

\frac{1}{C_{2}^{-}}\leq\liminf\frac{J_{M}(v)}{J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)}\leq\limsup\frac{J_{M}(v)}{J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)}\leq C_{2}^{+}\qquad\hbox{as}\ |v|_{1}\to 0\,,

where the constants
C_{2}^{-}
and
C_{2}^{+}
are defined in (
1.8
) and (
1.7
) respectively. Equation (
1.3
) reduces to (
1.2
) in the Riemannian case, since in that case
C_{2}^{-}=C_{2}^{+}=1
.

Finally, we emphasize that our results provide the correct generalization of Almgren’s Lemma, in the setting where a sequence of
m
-clusters converges to a limit with strictly fewer chambers.

The plan of the paper is the following. In Section
1.1
we will describe the Finsler manifolds, giving all the relevant definitions and fixing the notation that will be used throughout the paper. Then, Section
2
will be devoted to show the fundamental Theorem
2.1
. In Section
3
and
4
we will prove Theorem A and B respectively. In the short final Section
5
, we will show Theorem
1.1
and we will present a couple of final remarks.

1.1.
Notation and basic definitions for Finsler manifolds

This section is devoted to briefly present the classical theory of Finsler manifolds, together with our slightly more general setting, and to fix the notation and give the definitions that we will use throughout the paper. For a more detailed study of Finsler geometry, one can refer to the classical book
[
4
]
, to the recent book
[
14
]
, or to the recent papers
[
9
,
13
,
12
]
, where one can find very clear descriptions.

Roughly speaking, a Finsler manifold is a manifold which, around each point, behaves like
\mathbb{R}^{N}
endowed with a convex norm, and this norm depends on the point. The formal definition is the following.

Definition 1.2

(smooth Finsler manifold)
.

Let
M
be a compact, connected,
N
-dimensional manifold, and let
\mu
be a smooth, positive measure on
M
(this means that
\mu
can be expressed in local coordinates by means of a strictly positive and smooth density). Moreover, let
j_{F}:TM\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a
(smooth) Finsler structure
, that is, a positively
1
-homogeneous function, smooth out of the null section, and such that
j_{F}^{2}
is strictly convex on each tangent space
T_{p}M
. The triple
(M,j_{F},\mu)
is called a
smooth Finsler manifold
.

In this paper we will use a more general setting, in which we do not require smoothness, but only
{\rm C}^{2}
regularity, and only in the spatial variable. More precisely, we give the following.

Definition 1.3

(
{\rm C}^{2}
Finsler manifold)
.

Let
M
be a compact, connected,
N
-dimensional manifold, and let
\mu
be a
{\rm C}^{2}
, positive measure on
M
(this means that
\mu
can be expressed in local coordinates by means of a strictly positive and
{\rm C}^{2}
density). Moreover, let
j_{F}:TM\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a
{\rm C}^{2}
Finsler structure
, that is, a positively
1
-homogeneous function, such that
j_{F}^{2}
is convex on each tangent space
T_{p}M
, and which is of class
{\rm C}^{2}
in the spatial variable out of the null section. The triple
(M,j_{F},\mu)
is called a
{\rm C}^{2}
Finsler manifold
.

It is important to notice the main, big difference between the classical notion of smooth Finsler manifolds and our choice (of course, since our notion is a generalization of the classical one, then all our results are true also in the case of the classical, smooth notion). This difference is not the fact that we only require
{\rm C}^{2}
regularity instead of smoothness, this is actually a minor detail without much interest; the big point is that for the Finsler structure we require regularity only in the spatial variable, and only convexity on each tangent space. As a consequence, for every point
p\in M
the “unit ball” in
T_{p}M
consisting of the vectors
v\in T_{p}M
such that
j_{F}(v)=1
is not a strictly convex set, but only a convex one. Even though this introduces some additional technical difficulties, we find this particularly interesting, since it allows to locally observe general Wulff shapes, not necessarily strictly convex ones, and this often gives rise to much more interesting situations and examples.

Another comment has to be made on the measure
\mu
on
M
. Indeed, in general there are different possible meaningful choices, which of course all reduce to the standard volume measure in the Riemannian case. More precisely, as Ohta says in
[
12
]
,
“different from Riemannian manifolds, there is a variety of canonical measures on Finsler manifolds, such as the Busemann–Hausdorff measure, the Holmes–Thompson measure and so forth”
.

If
(M,j_{F},\mu)
is a Finsler manifold, one says that the Finsler structure is
reversible
if for any
v\in TM
one has
j_{F}(v)=j_{F}(-v)
. However, in this paper we are only interested in the general irreversible case. Given a Finsler structure on
M
, there is a natural notion of length of curves; that is, the length of a regular curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
is given by

\int_{0}^{1}j_{F}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt\,.

This notion of length automatically defines a (semi)-distance between points, by saying that
d(x,y)
is the minimal length of curves connecting
x
to
y
. Notice that this is a semi-distance in the general irreversible case, since the length of a curve can differ upon reverting the orientation. And in turn, having such a distance allows to speak about the space
{\rm Lip}(M)
of the Lipschitz functions on
M
; indeed, a function
\Phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
Lipschitz with constant
L
if for every
p,\,q\in M
one has

\big|\Phi(p)-\Phi(q)\big|\leq L\,\min\big\{d(p,q),\,d(q,p)\big\}\,.

Moreover, one can define the
slope
at a point
p
for a Lipschitz function
\Phi
as

|\partial\Phi|(p)=\limsup_{q\to p}\frac{\big(\Phi(p)-\Phi(q)\big)^{+}}{d(p,q)}\,.

We are finally in position to define the notion of perimeter on such a manifold. That is, given a Borel set
E\subseteq M
, we define its perimeter as

P_{M}(E)=\inf\bigg\{\liminf\int_{M}|\partial\Phi_{n}|(p)\,d\mu(p)\,,\Phi_{n}\in{\rm Lip}(M),\,\Phi_{n}\xrightarrow[\ L^{1}]{}\hbox{{\large$\chi$}{\Large${}_{{}_{E}}$}}\bigg\}\,.

In this paper, we will find it very useful to calculate volumes and perimeters in local charts. Let us be more precise: let us consider an atlas on
M
composed of finitely many bijective functions
\varphi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}
, where each
U_{i}
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\{V_{i}\}
is an open cover of
M
. Then, the volume and the perimeter of every set
E\subset\subset V_{i}
of finite perimeter can be written as

\displaystyle|E|_{M}=\int_{F}g_{i}(x)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\,,

\displaystyle P_{M}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}F}h_{i}\big(x,\nu_{F}(x)\big)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\,,

where
F=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(E)
,
\partial^{*}F
is the reduced boundary of
F
, and the functions
g_{i}:U_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
h_{i}:U_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
are determined by the measure
\mu
and the structure
j_{F}
. Of course, the functions
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
are highly non-canonical, since they depend on the arbitrary choice of the charts. In order to simplify the calculations, throughout the paper we will always consider a
isovolumetric atlas
, that is, an atlas such that the functions
g_{i}
in the above formula are identically equal to
1
. In other words, for each chart
\varphi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}
of an isovolumetric atlas and for each set
E\subset\subset V_{i}
of finite perimeter one has

(1.4)

\displaystyle|E|_{M}=|F|=\int_{F}1\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\,,

\displaystyle P_{M}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}F}h_{i}\big(x,\nu_{F}(x)\big)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\,.

Notice that this choice is not really standard; however, we find it very useful because then the volume of any set
E\subset\subset V_{i}
is exactly the same as the Euclidean volume of the reference set
F=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(E)
. Notice also that the existence of an isovolumetric atlas is obvious; in fact, by any atlas one can obtain an isovolumetric one through a change of variables and possibly a refinement (keep in mind that the finiteness is not a problem since we are interested in compact manifolds).

Let us now consider a given point
p\in M
. Let us assume that this point belongs to a set
V_{i}
, and let us call
z=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(p)
. Then, the function
h_{i}(z,\cdot)
is a positively
1
-homogeneous and convex function from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, so it corresponds to a norm, that we can call
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
: the unit ball for this norm is strictly convex for a classical (smooth) Finsler manifold, while it is in general only convex for a
{\rm C}^{2}
Finsler manifold. It is clear that, given the Finsler manifold
M
and the point
p\in M
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
is not uniquely determined, since it also depends on the choice of the atlas. However, let us consider two different possible atlantes: since they are both isovolumetric, the two different norms corresponding to the same point
p
coincide up to a change of variable which is given by a linear map of unit-determinant. In other words, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
is actually uniquely determined by the point
p
up to an arbitrary composition with a unit-determinant linear map. This allows us to give the following definition.

Definition 1.4

(Infinitesimal norm)
.

Let
(M,j_{F},\mu)
be a Finsler manifold, and let
p\in M
. We call
infinitesimal norm at
p
the norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
where
z=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(p)
and
\{\varphi_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\}
is an arbitrary isovolumetric atlas. The infinitesimal norm is uniquely defined up to composition with a unit-determinant linear map.

Notice that, having a well-defined norm at any point, we can compare the norms at different points; in particular, we can say if they coincide or not (of course, always up to composition with unit-determinant maps). Then, the following definition is natural.

Definition 1.5

(Fixed-norm manifold)
.

A Finsler manifold
M
is said to be a
fixed-norm manifold
if the infinitesimal norms at all the points coincide (up to unit-determinant linear maps). In this case, we call
fixed norm
this unique norm.

Notice that (thanks to the Nash embedding Theorem), any sufficiently regular Riemannian manifold is a fixed-norm manifold, and in particular the fixed norm is the Euclidean norm.

Let us now again fix our attention to a point
p\in M
and let us call
K
the unit ball corresponding to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
for some isovolumetric atlas with
p=\varphi_{i}(z)
. As said above, for any other isovolumetric atlas the corresponding unit ball at
p
will be a convex set of the form
L(K)
for a unit-determinant linear map
L
. And in fact, by an obvious change of variable, for any such map
L
we can find an isovolumetric atlas such that
L(K)
is the corresponding ball. The full generality of
L
is quite annoying, since then there is no upper bound to the perimeter of the possible unit balls at a given point
p
, and for different isovolumetric atlantes the values of
h_{i}(z,v)
for unit vectors
v
might be arbitrarily large or arbitrarily small. This could make it complicate to give stable estimates. To solve this issue, we will select unit balls which are “balanced”, in the following sense.

Definition 1.6

(Balanced convex set and balanced infinitesimal norm)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact, convex set whose interior contains the origin, and let
0<r\leq R
be the inradius and the circumradius of
K
, that is, the radii of the largest ball contained in
K
and of the smallest ball containing
K
. We say that
K
is
balanced
if for any unit-determinant linear map
L
, calling
0<r^{\prime}\leq R^{\prime}
the inradius and the circumradius of
L(K)
, one has
\frac{R}{r}\leq\frac{R^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}
. Let also
(M,j_{F},\mu)
be a Finsler manifold, and let
p\in M
. We call
balanced infinitesimal norm at
p
any norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
if
z=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(p)
and
\{\varphi_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\}
is an arbitrary isovolumetric atlas with the property that the unit ball corresponding to
\|\cdot\|_{i,z}
is balanced. We will use the notation
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for any such norm, and
P_{p}
for the corresponding
balanced infinitesimal perimeter
.

It is immediate to observe that, for any compact, convex set
K
whose interior contains the origin, there is at least one unit-determinant linear map
L
such that
L(K)
is balanced –in particular, by the well-known John’s Lemma, for any such balanced set one has
\frac{R}{r}\leq 2N
. As a consequence, for any point
p\in M
at least one balanced infinitesimal norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
exists; they are in general not unique (any rotation of a balanced infinitesimal norm at
p
is still a balanced infinitesimal norm at
p
), but they are not as many as the (not necessarily balanced) infinitesimal norms. From now on, we will restrict our attention to the following particular atlantes.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
(M,j_{F},\mu)
be a Finsler manifold, and let
\{\varphi_{i},\,U_{i},\,V_{i}\}
be a (finite) isovolumetric atlas. We say that the atlas is
balanced
if for any index
i
there exists a point
x_{i}\in U_{i}
such that the convex set
\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,h_{i}(x_{i},v)\leq 1\}
is balanced.

Notice that the existence of balanced atlantes is obvious, since they can be obtained starting from any isovolumetric atlas through a change of variables. Throughout the paper, we will only consider balanced atlantes. We can now present the last definitions that we are going to use in this paper; they might seem quite technical, but their meaning is pretty obvious. The first one is the oscillation of an atlas.

Definition 1.8

(oscillation of an atlas)
.

Let
\big\{\varphi_{i},\,U_{i},\,V_{i}\big\}
be a balanced atlas, and let
\rho>0
be a small number. We say that the atlas has
oscillation bounded by
\rho
if for any index
i
, any two points
x,\,y
in
U_{i}
, and any unit vector
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
one has

1-\rho\leq\frac{h_{i}(x,\nu)}{h_{i}(y,\nu)}\leq 1+\rho\,.

For any given
\rho\ll 1
, it is clearly possible to take a balanced atlas with oscillation bounded by
\rho
, with number of charts exploding when
\rho\to 0
. It will be important to use atlantes with very small oscillation, because then the functions
h_{i}(x,\nu)
will be quantitatively close to functions depending only on
\nu
, that is, the situation in any chart will be very similar to that of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a fixed norm.

Let us now pass to define the
m
-clusters and their volume and perimeter. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, an
m
-cluster
is a collection of
m
mutually disjoint sets
\mathcal{E}=(E_{1},\,E_{2},\,\dots\,,\,E_{m})
in
M
. The
volume
of the cluster
\mathcal{E}
is the vector
|\mathcal{E}|_{M}=(|E_{1}|_{M},\,|E_{2}|_{M},\,\dots\,,\,|E_{m}|_{M})\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
, and the
total volume
of the cluster is
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|_{M}=\big|\cup_{j=1}^{m}E_{j}\big|_{M}
, so the
L^{1}
norm of the vector
|\mathcal{E}|_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let us now define the
perimeter
of
\mathcal{E}
. In the special Euclidean case, the perimeter of
\mathcal{E}
is simply
{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\mathcal{E})
, where we write for brevity

\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}:=\bigcup\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}\partial^{*}E_{j}\,.

Notice that the perimeter of a cluster is smaller than the sum of the perimeters of the sets
E_{j}
, because the “common boundary” is counted just once. Therefore, in order to minimize the perimeter of clusters with given volume, it is easy to guess that it is convenient to have the different sets attached to each other in order to have a sufficiently large “common boundary”. In the general non-Euclidean case, the standard definition of perimeter of the cluster is

(1.5)

P_{M}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{2}\,\bigg(P_{M}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}E_{j}\Big)+\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}P_{M}(E_{j})\bigg)\,.

This definition might seem strange at first sight, but it is immediate to observe that this actually reduces to
{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\mathcal{E})
in the Euclidean case, and that this is exactly what one has in mind as “perimeter” for a cluster in a manifold. It is useful to make another observation, namely, if the manifold is reversible then the perimeter of a cluster can be easily written as an integral. More precisely, if the whole cluster
\mathcal{E}
is entirely contained in the chart
V_{i}
, and we call
\mathcal{F}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
, then we can write

P_{M}(\mathcal{E})=\int_{\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}}h_{i}(x,\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(x))d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\,.

Observe that the normal vector
\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(x)
is only defined up to the sign, and then the above formula makes sense only for a reversible manifold where
h_{i}(x,\nu)=h_{i}(x,-\nu)
. As a consequence, since we are not going to restrict ourselves to the case of reversible Finsler manifolds, we will only make use of the general definition (
1.5
).

As usual, we will say that
\mathcal{E}
is a
minimal cluster
if it minimizes the perimeter among all clusters with the same volume (keep in mind that the “volume” is not a number, but an
m
-dimensional vector). By standard lower semicontinuity and compactness, there exist minimal clusters for any given volume, as soon as the total volume is less than the volume of
M
itself.

Now, we give another definition.

Definition 1.9

(maximal diameter)
.

The
maximal diameter
d
for the manifold
M
is the maximum of the diameters of all the
m
-clusters in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which have total volume equal to
1
and which are minimal clusters with respect to some balanced infinitesimal perimeter
P_{p}
for some
p\in M
.

Notice that the maximal diameter
d
is well defined by the compactness of
M
and of the subset of
(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
consisting of the vectors with
L^{1}
norm equal to
1
; notice also that having considered balanced infinitesimal perimeters was essential; if we used instead the notion of (not necessarily balanced) infinitesimal perimeters, then the maximal diameter would be
+\infty
.

By definition, whenever an
m
-cluster is minimal for some of the perimeters
P_{p}
, its diameter must be bounded by
d
(times the
N
-th root of the total volume, if different from
1
). By approximation, we can also deduce that if some cluster is
almost
minimal for some perimeter, then a
large
portion of it has diameter at most
2d
(we could do the same with
cd
for any
c>1
, but this would only make the notation more complicated). The corresponding definition is the following.

Definition 1.10

(
\sigma
-minimal deviation)
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
d
its maximal diameter. For every small constant
\sigma
, we say that the
\sigma
-minimal deviation
is the largest constant
\eta=\eta(\sigma)
such that, if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is any cluster with total volume
1
and such that for some balanced infinitesimal perimeter
P_{p}
one has

P_{p}(\mathcal{F})\leq(1+\eta)\min\Big\{P_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}),\,|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}|=|\mathcal{F}|\Big\}\,,

then there exists a ball
B
of diameter
2d
such that
\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B|\big|\geq 1-\sigma
.

In words, a cluster which is optimal up to a percentage
\eta(\sigma)
for some perimeter
P_{p}
must be contained in some ball of radius
d
except for a small portion with total volume less than
\sigma
(once again, the well-posedness of this definition is trivial by compactness).

We conclude this introduction by defining three constants (as usual, well-defined by compactness) which will be used in the sequel, and which depend only on the manifold and its structure, and
not
on the choice of a particular atlas (in particular, they may be large, but they do not explode by taking atlantes with arbitrarily small oscillation). In these definition, by
P_{p}
(resp.,
\|\cdot\|_{p}
) we always mean a balanced infinitesimal perimeter (resp., norm) for some
p\in M
.

(1.6)

\displaystyle C_{1}=\Big(\min\Big\{{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}),\,\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|=1,\,\hbox{$\mathcal{F}$ is a minimal cluster for some $P_{p}$}\Big\}\Big)^{-1}\,,

(1.7)

\displaystyle C^{+}_{2}=\max\Big\{\|\nu\|_{p},\,p\in M,\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\Big\}\,,

(1.8)

\displaystyle C^{-}_{2}=\Big(\min\Big\{\|\nu\|_{p},\,p\in M,\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\Big\}\Big)^{-1}\,.

Notice the power
-1
in the definition of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}^{-}
; we have made this choice just for simplicity, so that for a “bad” manifold all the three constants might become very large, instead of someone very large and some other very small. Finally, notice that if the Finsler manifold is actually a Riemannian manifold, then the unique balanced infinitesimal norm is the Euclidean norm, and then in particular
C^{-}_{2}=C^{+}_{2}=1
.

2.
The small connected components

This section is devoted to show that, for a minimal cluster of sufficiently small total volume, every connected component is contained in a single chart of a suitable atlas, and its diameter is very small. More precisely, the main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be a
N
-dimensional,
{\rm C}^{2}
, compact, Finsler manifold, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given. For any small
\sigma<4^{-N}
, and for any balanced atlas with oscillation smaller than
\sigma
, there exists a constant
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq M
be a minimal
m
-cluster, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
be a connected component of
\mathcal{E}
with total volume less than
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
. Then, there exists an index
i
such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is entirely contained in the chart
V_{i}
, and the cluster
\mathcal{F}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})
satisfies
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq 4C_{4}\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|^{1/N}=4C_{4}\big||\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}|\big|_{M}^{1/N}
, where
C_{4}
is defined in (
2.5
).

The meaning of this result is that small connected components of a minimal cluster have small diameter. The claim makes use of a specific balanced atlas only for later use, but an immediate corollary of the result is the following general fact, of its own interest.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
M
be a
N
-dimensional,
{\rm C}^{2}
, compact Finsler manifold, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
be given. There exist a geometric constant
\overline{C}
and a small number
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
, only depending on the geometry of
M
, such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq M
be a minimal
m
-cluster, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
be a connected component of
\mathcal{E}
with total volume less than
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
. Then the diameter of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is smaller than
\overline{C}\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|^{1/N}
.

Notice that the claim of the corollary is just a part of the claim of the more general Theorem
2.1
; one only needs to notice that the ratio between the “true” distance in the Finsler manifold and the “artificial” distance measured in the charts is bounded only in terms of the geometry of the manifold, by definition and by the compactness of
M
.

In the above claims, by “connected component of a cluster
\mathcal{E}
” we mean any connected component of the union
\cup_{j=1}^{m}E_{j}
, so in particular any such component is an
m
-cluster itself, possibly with some empty chamber. Observe that the result does not require that the total volume of the whole minimal cluster is small, but only that so is the total volume of the connected component
\mathcal{E}
.

In order to state the next technical results, we need another simple piece of notation. Given a ball
B=B(c,r)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with radius
r
and center
c\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for every
A\subseteq[0,r]
we write

B_{A}:=\Big\{y\in B:\,|y-c|\in A\Big\}=\bigcup\big\{\partial B(c,s),\,s\in A\big\}\,,

so that in particular
B=B_{[0,r]}
. The first result of this section is then the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\sigma>0
be given, let
\eta
be the associated
\sigma
-minimal deviation, and let
\{\varphi_{i},\,U_{i},\,V_{i}\}
be a balanced atlas with oscillation smaller than
\rho\leq\eta/4
. Let the ball
B(c,r)
be compactly contained in
U_{i}
. Let also
\mathcal{F}
be a connected component of
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}\cap V_{i})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is some minimal cluster, and let
I=[a,b]\subseteq[0,r]
be an interval such that, calling
\varepsilon_{I}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I}|\big|
,

(2.1)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(\partial B(c,a)\cup\partial B(c,b)\big)\cap\mathcal{F}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{C_{3}}\,\varepsilon_{I}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,

with

(2.2)

C_{3}=\frac{2C_{1}C_{2}^{-}C_{2}^{+}}{\rho}\,.

Then, there exists some interval
J\subseteq I
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle|J|\leq 2d\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,,

\displaystyle\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{J}|\big|\geq\big(1-\sigma)\varepsilon_{I}\,.

Proof.

Let us call for brevity
\mathcal{F}_{I}=\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I}
, and let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be a cluster with volume
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{F}_{I}|\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
which is minimal for the balanced infinitesimal perimeter
P_{p}
, where
p\in V_{i}
is a point such that the convex set
\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,h_{i}(\varphi_{i}^{-1}(p),v)\leq 1\}
is balanced. By definition of maximal diameter, we have that

{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\leq d\big||\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\big|^{1/N}=d\big||\mathcal{F}_{I}|\big|^{1/N}=d\,\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,.

If
|I|\leq d\,\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}
, then we can simply set
J=I
and (
2.3
) is clearly true. Hence, we can assume without loss of generality that
|I|>d\,\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}
. In this case, up to a translation we have that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is compactly contained in the annulus
B_{I}
. Therefore, we can define a competitor cluster
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
simply removing
\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{I})
from
\mathcal{E}
and replacing it with
\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
. By construction,
\big||\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|\big|=\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|
, so the cluster
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is actually a competitor for
\mathcal{E}
. To evaluate the difference between the perimeters of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
, we call
\Gamma=\big(\partial B(c,a)\cup\partial B(c,b)\big)\cap\mathcal{F}
, and for every
x\in\Gamma
we call
\nu(x)
the direction of the vector
x-c
, so the normal vector (up to the sign) to the annulus
B_{I}
at
x
. Thus, keeping in mind (
1.4
), by construction we have

P_{M}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E})=P_{M}(\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))-P_{M}(\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{I}))+\int_{\Gamma}h_{i}(x,\nu(x))+h_{i}(x,-\nu(x))\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\,.

Since the oscillation of the atlas is less than
\rho
, we have

\displaystyle P_{M}(\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{I}))\geq(1-\rho)P_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{I})\,,

\displaystyle P_{M}(\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))\leq(1+\rho)P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\,.

Moreover, for any
x\in\Gamma\subseteq U_{i}
and any
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, by (
1.7
) we have

h_{i}(x,\nu)\leq(1+\rho)h_{i}(c,\nu)=(1+\rho)\|\nu\|_{p}\leq(1+\rho)C_{2}^{+}\,.

Keeping in mind that
\mathcal{E}
is a minimal cluster, thus
P_{M}(\mathcal{E})\leq P_{M}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, we deduce

\begin{split}(1-\rho)P_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{I})&\leq P_{M}(\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{I}))\leq P_{M}(\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))+\int_{\Gamma}h_{i}(x,\nu(x))+h_{i}(x,-\nu(x))\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\\
&\leq(1+\rho)P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})+2(1+\rho)C_{2}^{+}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\Gamma)\leq(1+\rho)\bigg(P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})+\frac{\rho\,\varepsilon_{I}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}{C_{1}C_{2}^{-}}\bigg)\,,\end{split}

where we have used (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Now, remember that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a minimal cluster for the perimeter
P_{p}
, and it has total volume
\varepsilon_{I}
; hence, by (
1.8
) and (
1.6
), we have that

P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{C_{2}^{-}}\,{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{I}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}{C_{1}C_{2}^{-}}\,.

Inserting this bound in the above inequality, and keeping in mind that
\rho
is small, we get

P_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{I})\leq\frac{(1+\rho)^{2}}{1-\rho}\,P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\leq(1+4\rho)P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\leq(1+\eta)P_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\,.

Finally, recalling that
|\mathcal{F}_{I}|=|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|
, that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a minimal cluster for the perimeter
P_{p}
, and that
\eta
is the
\sigma
-minimal deviation, by Definition
1.10
we obtain the existence of a ball
D
with diameter
2d\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}
and such that
\big||\mathcal{F}_{I}\cap D|\big|\geq(1-\sigma)\varepsilon_{I}
. We can finally call
J\subseteq I
the smallest interval such that
D\cap B_{I}\subseteq B_{J}
, and notice that this interval satisfies the requirements of (
2.3
).
∎

Our second result says that the thesis of Lemma
2.3
remains true also removing the assumtion (
2.1
), up to replace the factor
2
in (
2.3
) with a larger one, and to allow

to be not an interval, but the union of three intervals.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\sigma,\,\eta,\,\{\varphi_{i},\,U_{i},\,V_{i}\},\,\rho,\,B,\,\mathcal{E},\,\mathcal{F}
be as in Lemma
2.3
, with
\rho=\eta/4
, let again
I\subseteq[0,r]
be an interval, and call
\varepsilon_{I}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I}|\big|
. Then, there exist three disjoint intervals

\displaystyle|J|\leq C_{4}\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,,

\displaystyle\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{J}|\big|\geq\big(1-\sigma)\varepsilon_{I}\,,

(2.5)

|I|\leq 8C_{3}\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\leq C_{4}\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,.

Then, the thesis is already obtained with

(so
J^{\prime}=J^{\prime\prime\prime}=\emptyset

(a,(a+b)/2)

2.6

b_{1}

(2.7)

(a_{1}-a)+(b-b_{1})\leq 8C_{3}\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,.

Let us now call
I_{1}=[a_{1},b_{1}]

\varepsilon_{1}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{1}}|\big|

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big((\partial B(c,a_{1})\cup\partial B(c,b_{1}))\cap\mathcal{F}\big)\leq\frac{2^{-1/N}}{C_{3}}\,\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq\frac{1}{C_{3}}\,\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,.

Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.3

2.1

2.3

\displaystyle|J^{\prime\prime}|\leq 2d\varepsilon_{1}^{1/N}\leq 2d\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,,

The thesis is then obtained with

|J|=|J^{\prime}|+|J^{\prime\prime}|+|J^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq(8C_{3}+2d)\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\leq C_{4}\varepsilon_{I}^{1/N}\,,

so that (

Let us now assume that
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{I}/2

I_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{1}

\varepsilon_{i}

(

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\mathcal{F}\cap\partial B(c,t)\big)\geq\frac{1}{4C_{3}}\,\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}

for

|I_{1}|\leq 8C_{3}\varepsilon_{1}^{1/N}\,,

. Otherwise, arguing as in the previous step we call

Thanks to the above lemma, we are now in position to show the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\sigma<4^{-N}
be fixed, let
\eta>0

\sigma
-minimal deviation, and define

\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}:=\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\,\omega_{N},\,\frac{1}{(2C_{4})^{N}}\bigg\}\ \bar{r}^{N}\,.

In particular, the volume of every ball of radius

Let now
\mathcal{E}
be any minimal

be any of its connected components with total volume less than
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}

, and let

be an index such that

and the ball centered at

and with radius
\bar{r}
is compactly contained in
U_{i}
. Let us call
\mathcal{F}
the connected component of
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\cap V_{i})
containing

, and let
\varepsilon=\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|
, which is smaller than
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
since
\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F})
is contained in
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
. We also call
B=B(c,\bar{r})\subset\subset U_{i}
,
I_{0}=[0,\bar{r}]
, and
\varepsilon_{0}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{0}}|\big|\leq\varepsilon\leq\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
. We can apply Lemma
2.4
to the interval
I_{0}
, getting a set
J_{1}
satisfying

\displaystyle|J_{1}|\leq C_{4}\varepsilon_{0}^{1/N}\,,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{1}}|\big|\leq\sigma\varepsilon_{0}\,,

where we have called
I_{1}=I_{0}\setminus J_{1}
. Since
J_{1}=J_{1}^{\prime}\cup J_{1}^{\prime\prime}\cup J_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}
, and
J_{1}^{\prime},\,J_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
J_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}
are three intervals, with
J_{1}^{\prime}
starting at the first endpoint of
I_{0}
and
J_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}
ending at the second endpoint of
I_{0}
, we deduce that
I_{1}
is the union of two disjoint intervals, that we call
I_{1}^{1}
and
I_{1}^{2}
. Accordingly, we call
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}^{1}+\varepsilon_{1}^{2}
, where
\varepsilon_{1}^{l}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{1}^{l}}|\big|
for
l\in\{1,\,2\}
. We can now apply Lemma
2.4
separately to the intervals
I_{1}^{1}
and
I_{1}^{2}
, obtaining two sets
J_{2}^{1}
and
J_{2}^{2}
which satisfy

\displaystyle|J_{2}^{l}|\leq C_{4}(\varepsilon_{1}^{l})^{1/N}\,,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}^{l}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{2}^{l}}|\big|\leq\sigma\varepsilon_{1}^{l}\,,

where
l\in\{1,\,2\}
and we call
I_{2}^{l}=I_{1}^{l}\setminus J_{2}^{l}
. Setting
J_{2}=J_{2}^{1}\cup J_{2}^{2}
, and then also
I_{2}=I_{1}\setminus J_{2}=I_{2}^{1}\cup I_{2}^{2}
and
\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon_{2}^{1}+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{2}}|\big|
, the last estimates become

\displaystyle|J_{2}|\leq C_{4}\Big((\varepsilon_{1}^{1})^{1/N}+(\varepsilon_{1}^{2})^{1/N}\Big)\leq 2C_{4}\varepsilon_{1}^{1/N}\,,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}\leq\sigma\varepsilon_{1}\leq\sigma^{2}\varepsilon_{0}\,.

Going on with the obvious recursion, we find a sequence of disjoint sets
J_{n}
such that each set
I_{n}=I_{0}\setminus(J_{1}\cup J_{2}\cdots\cup J_{n})
is made by
2^{n}
disjoint intervals, and so that
\varepsilon_{n}=\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{n}}|\big|\leq\sigma^{n}\varepsilon_{0}
, while

|J_{n+1}|\leq 2^{n}\,C_{4}\varepsilon_{n}^{1/N}\leq\Big(2\sigma^{1/N}\Big)^{n}C_{4}\varepsilon_{0}^{1/N}\,.

We can then call
J_{\infty}=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}J_{n}
, and
I_{\infty}=I_{0}\setminus J_{\infty}
. By the above estimates,

|J_{\infty}|\leq C_{4}\varepsilon_{0}^{1/N}\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(2\sigma^{1/N}\Big)^{n}=\frac{C_{4}}{1-2\sigma^{1/N}}\,\varepsilon_{0}^{1/N}<2C_{4}\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|^{1/N}\leq\bar{r}\,,

where the last inequalities are due to the fact that
\sigma<4^{-N}
and by (
2.9
). On the other hand, keeping in mind that the sets
I_{n}
are monotone decreasing (that is, for every
n
one has that
I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
), we deduce that
\big||\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{\infty}}|\big|=0
. Keeping in mind that
\mathcal{F}
is connected, we deduce that
J_{\infty}
is essentially a segment. More precisely, there exists an interval
[a,b]\subseteq I_{0}
such that
\mathcal{F}\cap B_{I_{0}}
is concentrated on
B_{[a.b]}
, and
b-a\leq|J_{\infty}|<\bar{r}
. Since
c\in\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}
, it is necessarily
c\in B_{[a,b]}
, and this means that
a=0
. Therefore, we also have
b<\bar{r}
, and this means that the whole connected component
\mathcal{F}
is compactly contained in
B(c,\bar{r})\subset\subset U_{i}
, which implies that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\subset\subset V_{i}
. We conclude the proof observing that by construction

{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq 2b\leq 2|J_{\infty}|\leq 4C_{4}\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|^{1/N}=4C_{4}\big||\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}|\big|_{M}^{1/N}\,.

∎

3.
The general Finsler case: proof of Theorem A

This section is devoted to prove Theorem A, that is, that sufficiently small minimal
m
-clusters in
M
can have at most
m
connected components. We start with the preliminary observation that every minimal cluster (not necessarily with small voume) contains a finite number of connected components.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a minimal cluster in
M
. Then,
\mathcal{E}
is done by finitely many connected components.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a minimal cluster. First of all, we apply Theorem
2.1
with
\sigma=5^{-N}
and any balanced atlas
\{\varphi_{i},\,U_{i},\,V_{i}\}
with oscillation smaller than
\sigma
, obtaining a constant
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
so that the claim of the Theorem holds. Then, we call
\bar{r}>0
a constant such that, for every
p\in M
, there exists some index
i
such that
p\in V_{i}
and
B_{p}=B(\varphi_{i}^{-1}(p),\bar{r})
is compactly contained in
U_{i}
. By
[
15
]
, the so called
\varepsilon-\varepsilon
property holds. More precisely, up to decrease the constant
\bar{r}
, we have two constants
\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
C_{5}>0
such that, for every
p\in M
and every
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|\varepsilon|<\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
, there exists another cluster
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}
in
\varphi_{i}(B_{p})
, and

(3.1)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{E}|+\varepsilon\,,

\displaystyle P_{M}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\leq P_{M}(\mathcal{E})+C_{5}|\varepsilon|\,.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that

(3.2)

\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}<\bigg(\frac{\bar{r}}{4C_{4}}\bigg)^{N}\wedge\bigg(\frac{1-5^{-N}}{C_{2}^{-}\,C_{5}}\bigg)^{N}\,N^{N}\omega_{N}\,.

We want to show that every connected component of
\mathcal{E}
has total volume at least
\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
; of course, this will conclude the thesis, because as an obvious consequence the connected components will be at most
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|/\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
. Let us then suppose that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{E}
with total volume
\big||\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}|\big|<\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
. Since
\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, by Theorem
2.1
we know that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is entirely contained in some
V_{i}
, and we can call
\mathcal{F}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})\subset\subset U_{i}
. Moreover, by (
3.2
)

{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F})\leq 4C_{4}\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|^{1/N}<4C_{4}\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}^{1/N}<\bar{r}\,,

and then, calling
p
an arbitrary point of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
,
\mathcal{F}
is compactly contained in the ball
B_{p}
defined above. Calling
\varepsilon=|\mathcal{F}|
, the
\varepsilon-\varepsilon
property provides us with a cluster
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
which coincides with
\mathcal{E}
in
\varphi_{i}(B_{p})
, and such that (
3.1
) holds. In particular,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is a connected component also for
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. We can then define
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
, so that by construction and by (
3.1
) we have
|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}|=|\mathcal{E}|
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is a minimal cluster, this implies
P_{M}(\widehat{\mathcal{E}})\geq P_{M}(\mathcal{E})
. Thus, using (
3.1
), we have

(3.3)

0\leq P_{M}(\widehat{\mathcal{E}})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E})=P_{M}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E})\leq C_{5}|\varepsilon|-P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})\,.

Let us now evaluate
P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})
. Since the atlas is balanced, for every
i
we can take a point
x_{i}\in U_{i}
such that
\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,h_{i}(x_{i},v)\leq 1\}
is balanced; and then, also recalling that the atlas has oscillation smaller than
\sigma=5^{-N}
, for every
x\in U_{i}
and every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
we have
h_{i}(x,\nu)\geq(1-\sigma)h_{i}(x_{i},\nu)\geq(1-\sigma)/C_{2}^{-}
. Consequently,

P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})\geq\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,N\omega_{N}^{1/N}\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|^{\frac{N-1}{N}}=\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,N\omega_{N}^{1/N}|\varepsilon|^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,

and inserting this estimate in (
3.3
) we find

|\varepsilon|\geq\bigg(\frac{1-5^{-N}}{C_{2}^{-}\,C_{5}}\bigg)^{N}\,N^{N}\omega_{N}\,.

Since this is in contradiction with (
3.2
), we have proved that a connected component of
\mathcal{E}
with total volume less thann
\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
cannot exist, thus the proof is completed.
∎

It is important to notice that the constant
\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
of the above lemma in principle depends on the cluster
\mathcal{E}
. Therefore, the bound on the number of connected components is not uniform. However, this is enough to allow us to perform a homothethy argument to prove Theorem A.

Proof of Theorem A.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an
m
-minimal cluster. By Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\mathcal{E}
is done by finitely many connected components, that we call
\mathcal{E}_{1},\,\mathcal{E}_{2},\,\dots\,,\,\mathcal{E}_{H}
with some
H\in\mathbb{N}
. Our goal is to show that
H\leq m
, provided that
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|
is smaller than a suitable constant
\bar{\varepsilon}
, that we are going to precisely define in (
3.9
), only depending on
M
and
m
. First of all, we apply Theorem
2.1
with constant
\sigma=5^{-N}
and an arbitrary balanced atlas
\{\varphi_{i},\,U_{i},\,V_{i}\}
with oscillation smaller than
\sigma
, getting a constant
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
such that the claim of the theorem holds. If
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, then obviously every connected component
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
of
\mathcal{E}
has total volume less than
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
. As a consequence, for every
1\leq\ell\leq H
there exists some
i=i(\ell)
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
is compactly contained in
V_{i}
, and thus we call
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})
.

Let
\lambda_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
, for
1\leq\ell\leq H
, be some constants, to be specified later. For
\delta\in(-1/2,1/2)
and for each
1\leq\ell\leq H
, we can fix a point
x_{\ell}
in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
and define the modified cluster
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\delta}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
as

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\delta}=x_{\ell}+\big(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell}\big)^{1/N}\,(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}-x_{\ell})\,.

In other words, we keep the point
x_{\ell}
fixed and we perform a homothety of ratio
(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell})^{1/N}
. Since the components
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
are finitely many, if
|\delta|
is small enough then each cluster
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\delta}
is still compactly contained in
U_{i}
, and the clusters
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}_{\ell}=\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\delta})
are still pairwise disjoint. Therefore, we can call
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{H}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\delta}
, and the clusters
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\delta}
are the connected components of
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}
. The volume of
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}
is easily calculated as

\big|\mathcal{E}^{\delta}\big|_{M}=\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{H}|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\delta}|=\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{H}\big(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell})|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|=|\mathcal{E}|_{M}+\delta\Big(\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{H}\lambda_{\ell}|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|\Big)\,.

Now, keep in mind that the
|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|
’s are
H
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. As a consequence, if
H>m
there exists a nontrivial choice of
\lambda_{\ell}
so that

\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{H}\lambda_{\ell}|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|=0\,.

With this choice, for every
|\delta|\ll 1
the cluster
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}
has the same volume as
\mathcal{E}
, so it can be used as a competitor. Let us now estimate the perimeter of
\mathcal{E}
. To do so, for every
1\leq\ell\leq H
we call
E_{\ell,j}
for
1\leq j\leq m
the components of
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
, and similarly
E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}
are the components of
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}_{\ell}
. In other words, we write
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}=(E_{\ell,1},\,E_{\ell,2},\,\dots\,,\,E_{\ell,m})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}_{\ell}=(E^{\delta}_{\ell,1},\,E^{\delta}_{\ell,2},\,\dots\,,\,E^{\delta}_{\ell,m})
. By definition, writing
E_{\ell,0}
(resp.,
E^{\delta}_{\ell,0}
) the union of the sets
E_{\ell,j}
(resp.,
E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}
) for
1\leq j\leq m
, we have then

(3.4)

P_{M}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\delta}_{\ell})=\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}P_{M}(E^{\delta}_{\ell,j})\,,

and by construction each
E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}
satisfies

(3.5)

E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}=x_{\ell}+\big(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell}\big)^{1/N}\,(E_{\ell,j}-x_{\ell})\,.

Calling then for simplicity of notation
\nu(x)
the unit outer normal vector to
E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}
at
x\in\partial^{*}E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}
, with the change of variables
x=\tau(y)=x_{\ell}+(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell})^{1/N}(y-x_{\ell})
we have

P_{M}(E^{\delta}_{\ell,j})=\int_{\partial^{*}E^{\delta}_{\ell,j}}h_{i}(x,\nu(x))\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)=\big(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell}\big)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\ell,j}}h_{i}\big(\tau(y),\nu((\tau(y))\big)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(y)\,.

Notice now that, by (
3.5
), the unit normal vector
\nu(y)
to
E_{\ell,j}
at
y\in\partial^{*}E_{\ell,j}
coincides with
\nu(\tau(y))
. As a consequence, if we define

\gamma(y)=h_{i}\big(\tau(y),\nu((\tau(y))\big)-h_{i}\big(y,\nu(y)\big)=h_{i}\big(\tau(y),\nu(y)\big)-h_{i}\big(y,\nu(y)\big)\,,

the above equality gives

(3.6)

P_{M}(E^{\delta}_{\ell,j})=\big(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell}\big)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\ell,j}}h_{i}(y,\nu(y))+\gamma(y)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(y)\,.

We need now to expand the above term up to the second order for
\delta\approx 0
. We begin with the function
\tau
: by definition,

\tau(y)=y+\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{N}\,(y-x_{\ell})\delta+\frac{(1-N)\lambda_{\ell}^{2}}{2N^{2}}\,(y-x_{\ell})\delta^{2}+o(\delta^{2})\,.

Let us now pass to the expansion of
\gamma
: writing for brevity
\psi(z)=\psi_{i,\nu}(z)=h_{i}(z,\nu)
, we have

\begin{split}\gamma(y)&=h_{i}(\tau(y),\nu(y))-h_{i}(y,\nu(y))=\psi(\tau(y))-\psi(y)\\
&=\nabla\psi(y)\cdot(\tau(y)-y)+\frac{1}{2}\,(\tau(y)-y)^{t}D^{2}\psi(y)(\tau(y)-y)\\
&=\nabla\psi(y)\cdot\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{N}\,(y-x_{\ell})\delta+\frac{(1-N)\lambda_{\ell}^{2}}{2N^{2}}\,(y-x_{\ell})\delta^{2}\bigg)\\
&\phantom{=\nabla\psi(y)\cdot\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{N}\,(y-x_{\ell})\delta\,}+\frac{\lambda_{\ell}^{2}}{2N^{2}}\,(y-x_{\ell})^{t}D^{2}\psi(y)(y-x_{\ell})\,\delta^{2}+o(\delta^{2})\,.\end{split}

Finally, we observe that

(1+\delta\lambda_{\ell})^{\frac{N-1}{N}}=1+\frac{N-1}{N}\,\lambda_{\ell}\delta-\frac{N-1}{N^{2}}\,\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\delta^{2}+o(\delta^{2})\,.

Inserting the last two estimates in (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

P_{M}(E^{\delta}_{\ell,j})=P_{M}(E_{\ell,j})+\alpha\delta+\beta\delta^{2}+o(\delta^{2})\,,

with two suitable constants
\alpha
and
\beta
. We are not interested in evaluating
\alpha
; concerning
\beta
, instead, we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}\beta=\frac{\lambda_{\ell}^{2}}{N^{2}}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\ell,j}}-(N-1)h_{i}(y,\nu(y))&+\frac{N-1}{2}\,\nabla\psi_{i,\nu(y)}(y)\cdot(y-x_{\ell})\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\,(y-x_{\ell})^{t}D^{2}\psi_{i,\nu(y)}(y)(y-x_{\ell})\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(y)\,.\end{split}

Now, since the atlas is balanced and its oscillation is at most
\sigma=5^{-N}
, for every
z\in U_{i}
and every unit vector
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
we have

h_{i}(z,\nu)\geq\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\geq\frac{1}{2C_{2}^{-}}\,.

On the other hand, keep in mind that
x_{\ell}
is a fixed point in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, and by Theorem
2.1
we know that
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})\leq 4C_{4}\big||\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|\big|^{1/N}
, so in particular
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})\leq 4C_{4}\bar{\varepsilon}^{1/N}
since we are assuming that
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
. But then, for every
y\in\partial^{*}E_{\ell,j}
we have
|y-x_{\ell}|\leq 4C_{4}\bar{\varepsilon}^{1/N}
, and then, since the definition (
3.9
) of
\bar{\varepsilon}
in particular will satisfy
\bar{\varepsilon}\leq(4C_{4})^{-N}
, we get

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{N-1}{2}\,\nabla\psi_{i,\nu(y)}(y)\cdot(y-x_{\ell})&+\frac{1}{2}\,(y-x_{\ell})^{t}D^{2}\psi_{i,\nu(y)}(y)(y-x_{\ell})\bigg|\\
&\leq\bigg(\frac{N-1}{2}\,\|\nabla\psi_{i,\nu(y)}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|D^{2}\psi_{i,\nu(y)}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\,4C_{4}\bar{\varepsilon}^{1/N}=C_{6}\bar{\varepsilon}^{1/N}\,,\end{split}

where

C_{6}:=4C_{4}\max_{i,\,\nu}\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{N-1}{2}\,\|\nabla\psi_{i,\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|D^{2}\psi_{i,\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\bigg\}\,.

Notice that the constant
C_{6}
is well defined by compactness (and since the manifold is of class
{\rm C}^{2}
), and it only depends on the atlas, thus only on the manifold and on its dimension
N
, since the atlas was generic. Inserting the above estimates in (
3.8
) gives

\beta\leq\frac{\lambda_{\ell}^{2}}{N^{2}}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{\ell,j}}-\frac{N-1}{2C_{2}^{-}}+C_{6}\bar{\varepsilon}^{1/N}\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(y)\,.

We are finally in position to define the constant
\bar{\varepsilon}
as

(3.9)

\bar{\varepsilon}:=\min\bigg\{\bar{\varepsilon}_{1},\,\bigg(\frac{1}{4C_{4}}\bigg)^{N},\,\bigg(\frac{N-1}{3C_{2}^{-}C_{6}}\bigg)^{N}\bigg\}\,,

which by construction only depends on the manifold
M
and its dimension
N
. With this choice, we have that the constant
\beta
is negative, and actually strictly negative unless
\lambda_{\ell}=0
. As a consequence, in a suitably small neighborhood of
\delta=0
the function
\delta\mapsto P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\delta})
is strictly concave whenever
\lambda_{\ell}\neq 0
(while otherwise it is constant). Since the choice of the coefficients
\{\lambda_{\ell}\}
is nontrivial, we deduce that the perimeter of the cluster
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}
is a strictly concave function of
\delta
in a small neighborhood of
\delta=0
. Since all the clusters
\mathcal{E}^{\delta}
have the same volume and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{0}
is a minimal cluster, this is impossible. The contradiction show that necessarily
H\leq m
, and this concludes the first part of the thesis.

Concerning the validity of (
1.1
), we have already observed above that, thanks to Theorem
2.1
, we have
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})\leq 4C_{4}\big||\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|\big|^{1/N}
. We can then argue exactly as in the proof of Corollary
2.2
, namely, observing that the ratio between the distance on
M
and the distance measured in the finite charts of the atlas is bounded from above and below.
∎

4.
The Riemannian case: proof of Theorem B

This section is devoted to prove Theorem B, which says that sufficiently small minimal clusters are actually connected if the manifold is a Riemannian one. Many of our arguments work whenever
M
is a fixed-norm manifold; as a consequence, we will make this assumption through all this section (and we will underline when we are going to use that
M
is actually Riemannian). Thus, there is a given balanced norm
\|\cdot\|
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which coincides, up to a linear transformation with unit-determinant, with the infinitesimal norm at each point
p\in M
. For brevity of notation, we will call
P_{*}
the perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
associated to this norm, that is, for every set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
we define

P_{*}(F)=\int_{\partial^{*}F}\|\nu_{F}(x)\|\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\,,

and for every cluster
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
we define
P_{*}(\mathcal{F})
as usual via (
1.5
). We call then
J:(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
the isoperimetric function corresponding to this perimeter, that is, for every
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
we set

J(v)=\min\Big\{P_{*}(\mathcal{F}),\,\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N},\,|\mathcal{F}|=v\Big\}\,.

Notice that, by the homogeneity of the perimeter, for every
\lambda>0
and every
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
we have
J(\lambda v)=\lambda^{N-1}J(v)
, thus it is enough to study the function
J
on the vectors with total norm equal to
1
. We start now with the following easy observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every small
\delta>0
there exists
\mu>0
such that, for any cluster
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, if the largest connected component of
\mathcal{F}
has total volume less than
(1-\delta)\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|
, then

P_{*}(\mathcal{F})\geq(1+\mu)\,J(|\mathcal{F}|)\,.

Proof.

As noticed above, the homogeneity allows to restrict ourselves to clusters with total volume equal to
1
. Hence, we let
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
be any vector with
|v|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}=1
. By standard concentration and compactness arguments, we know that there exist minimal clusters in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with volume
v
, and that each such minimal cluster is connected. By continuity and compactness, the minimal perimeter of clusters
\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{F}|=v
and with the largest connected component having total volume less than
1-\delta
is strictly greater than
J(v)
, thus we can write it as
(1+\mu)\,J(v)
for some
\mu>0
depending on
v
(and, of course, on
\delta
). The claim is equivalent to say that we can find a suitable constant
\mu
, only depending on
\delta
and on the norm, but not on
v
.

Let us assume by contradiction that this is false. Then, we can find a sequence of volumes
v_{n}
with
|v_{n}|_{1}=1
, and a sequence of clusters
\mathcal{F}_{n}
with
|\mathcal{F}_{n}|=v_{n}
, in such a way that for every
n
we have
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}
, being
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}
the largest connected component of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, and so that for every
n
one has

(4.1)

\displaystyle\big||\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}|\big|\leq 1-\delta\,,

\displaystyle P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{n})\leq\bigg(1+\frac{1}{n}\bigg)\,J(v_{n})\,.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}
is a minimal cluster with volume
|\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}|
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}
is a minimal cluster with volume
|\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}|
, in particular they are both connected and moreover
\big||\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}|\big|\geq 1/2
. Calling
d
the maximal diameter of
M
(in the sense of Definition
1.9
), we have that

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime})\leq d\big||\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}|\big|^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq d\,,

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime})\leq d\big||\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}|\big|^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq d\,.

Since the ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a fixed norm, translations leave volume and perimeter invariant; therefore, keeping in mind that
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}
are different connected components of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, by the above estimate on the diameters we can assume without loss of generality that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}\subset\subset B(0,d)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset B(Q,d)\,,

where
Q
is any point in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with distance larger than
2d
from the origin, for instance we can take
Q=3d{\rm e}_{1}
. Up to pass to a subsequence, we can assume that
v_{n}\to v
, of course with
|v|_{1}=1
, and that the clusters
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime\prime}
converge weakly* in the BV sense to some clusters
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
. Since the convergence holds in particular in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
, by (
4.2
) and (
4.1
) we know that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq B(0,d)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B(Q,d)\,,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|=v,\,

\displaystyle\big||\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\big|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big||\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\prime}|\big|\in\big[1/2,1-\delta]\,.

Moreover, by the lower semicontinuity of the perimeter, again using (
4.1
) and the obvious fact that
J
is continuous we have

P_{*}(\mathcal{F})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{n}\bigg)\,J(v_{n})=J(v)\,.

In other words,
\mathcal{F}
is a minimal cluster of volume
v
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is not connected, this is impossible, and we have found the searched contradiction.
∎

The above lemma allows us to present the first step in the proof of Theorem B, that is, we exclude that arbitrarily small clusters can have a largest connected component for which the percentage of the total volume is not going to
1
. More precisely, the result is the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M
be as in Theorem B. For every
\delta>0
there exists some
\varepsilon_{\delta}>0
such that for any minimal cluster
\mathcal{E}
with
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|<\varepsilon_{\delta}
there is a connected component
\mathcal{E}_{1}
of
\mathcal{E}
satisfying
\big||\mathcal{E}_{1}|\big|\geq(1-\delta)\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|
.

Proof.

Let
\mu=\mu(\delta)
be given by Lemma
4.1
, and let us fix a balanced atlas with oscillation smaller than

(4.3)

\sigma=4^{-N}\wedge\frac{\mu}{4}\,.

Up to a change of variables, we can assume that for every
i
there exists a point
z_{i}\in U_{i}
such that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,z_{i}}
coincides with the representative
\|\cdot\|
of the fixed norm that we have chosen. If
E\subseteq M
is any set compactly contained in some
V_{i}
, then by (
1.4
) we have

P_{M}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}F}h_{i}(x,\nu_{F}(x))\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\geq(1-\sigma)\int_{\partial^{*}F}h_{i}(z_{i},\nu_{F}(x))\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)=(1-\sigma)P_{*}(F)\,,

where we have denoted
F=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(E)
. As a consequence, if
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is any
m
-cluster which is compactly contained in some
V_{i}
and we denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})
, then we have

(4.4)

P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})\geq(1-\sigma)P_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})\,.

Let us now apply Theorem
2.1
to our balanced atlas, getting a constant
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, which depends on
M,\,N
and
\delta
. Let now
\varepsilon_{\delta}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
be a constant, again only depending on
M,\,N
and
\delta
, to be specified later, and let
\mathcal{E}
be a minimal cluster with volume
v=|\mathcal{E}|
so that the total volume satisfies
\big||\mathcal{E}|\big|=|v|_{1}<\varepsilon_{\delta}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\mathcal{E}
is done by finitely many connected components
\mathcal{E}_{1},\,\mathcal{E}_{2},\,\dots\,,\,\mathcal{E}_{H}
(up to replace
\varepsilon_{\delta}
by a possibly smaller constant we could apply Theorem A to be sure that
H\leq m
, but this would not make any difference in the proof). To obtain the proof, we assume that
\big||\mathcal{E}_{1}|\big|<(1-\delta)|v|_{1}
, being
\mathcal{E}_{1}
the connected component with the largest total volume, and we look for a contradiction.

For every
1\leq\ell\leq H
there exists an index
i=i(\ell)
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
is compactly contained in
V_{i}
; thus, we can define the cluster
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})\subset\subset U_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Up to translations, we can assume that all the clusters
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
are a positive distance apart from each other, and call
\mathcal{F}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{H}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, which satisfies
|\mathcal{F}|=|\mathcal{E}|=v
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is the connected component of
\mathcal{F}
with the largest total volume, and
\big||\mathcal{F}_{1}|\big|=\big||\mathcal{E}_{1}|\big|<(1-\delta)|v|_{1}=(1-\delta)\big||\mathcal{F}|\big|
, by Lemma
4.1
we have that

P_{*}(\mathcal{F})\geq(1+\mu)J(v)\,.

Applying (
4.4
) to any of the clusters
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
, we have

(4.5)

P_{M}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{H}P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})\geq(1-\sigma)\sum_{\ell=1}^{H}P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})=(1-\sigma)P_{*}(\mathcal{F})\geq(1-\sigma)(1+\mu)J(v)\,.

Now, we let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a minimal cluster of volume
v
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|
. By rescaling, we have
{\rm diam}(\mathcal{G})\leq d(\big||\mathcal{G}|\big|)^{1/N}\leq d\varepsilon_{\delta}^{1/N}
, where
d
is the maximal diameter for
M
(see Definition
1.9
), Up to replace
\varepsilon_{\delta}
by a smaller constant (depending only on the atlas, thus on
M
,
N
and
\delta
), we can then assume that
\mathcal{G}
is compactly contained in the chart
U_{1}
, up to a translation. Then, we can call
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\varphi_{1}(\mathcal{G})
, and notice that by construction
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq M
is a cluster with volume
|\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}|=v=|\mathcal{E}|
. Arguing as in (
4.4
) using again that the atlas has oscillation smaller than
\sigma
, we have then

P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})\leq(1+\sigma)P_{*}(\mathcal{G})=(1+\sigma)J(v)\,.

Putting this estimate together with (
4.5
), and recalling that
\mathcal{E}
is a minimal cluster, we have then

P_{M}(\mathcal{E})\leq P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})\leq(1+\sigma)J(v)\leq\frac{1+\sigma}{(1-\sigma)(1+\mu)}\,P_{M}(\mathcal{E})\,,

which implies

1+\sigma\geq(1-\sigma)(1+\mu)\,.

Since this inequality is not compatible with the definition (
4.3
), we have found the searched contradiction, and this concludes the proof.
∎

The above lemma says that minimal clusters with extremely small total volume must be “almost connected”, in the sense that the percentage of the total volume which does not belong to the largest connected component must be arbitrarily small if the total volume is very small. To prove Theorem B we have then also to exclude the existence of very small (in percentage) connected components, and this will be the hardest part of the proof. We start with a technical result, which says that if a set
E
contained in an arbitrarily high prism is flat up to a very small error, then the same is true in a smaller prism with a fixed height, not depending on the error. More precisely, our result is the following.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a purely dimensional constant
H=H(N)
such that the following holds. Let
Q=(-1/2,1/2)^{N-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, let
W>1
and
\rho\ll 1
be two constants, and let
E\subseteq Q\times(-W,W)
be a set satisfying

(4.6)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times(-W,W)\big)\Big)\leq 1+\rho\,,

(4.7)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E\cap\big(Q\times\{-W\}\big)\Big)\geq 1-\rho\,,

(4.8)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E\cap\big(Q\times\{W\}\big)\Big)\leq\rho\,.

Then, there exists an interval
(a,b)\subseteq(-W,W)
with length at most
H
such that conditions (
4.6
)–(
4.8
) are satisfied with
(a,b)
in place of
(-W,W)
and
Q\times\{a\}
(resp.,
Q\times\{b\}
) in place of
Q\times\{-W\}
(resp.,
Q\times\{W\}
).

Proof.

For every
t\in[-W,W]
, we call for brevity

v(t)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)\,.

Notice that for
{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in[-W,W]
, by Vol’pert Theorem (see for instance
[
16
,
1
]
) we have that
v(t)
is well-defined, and moreover

\partial^{*}\Big(E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)=\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)

up to
{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-2}
-negligible subsets. As a consequence, by the relative isoperimetric inequality in the cube we have

(4.9)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-2}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)\geq C_{N}\Big(v(t)\wedge\big(1-v(t)\big)\Big)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\,,

where
C_{N}
is a constant only depending on
N
. Let us now define

(4.10)

\displaystyle s_{1}^{-}=\sup\Big\{t\in[-W,W]:\,v(t)\geq 1-\rho\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle s_{1}^{+}=\inf\Big\{t\in[-W,W]:\,v(t)\leq\rho\Big\}\,,

which are well defined thanks to (
4.7
) and (
4.8
). Notice that, by projection, we have

(4.11)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times[s_{1}^{-},W)\big)\Big)\geq\big|v(W)-\limsup_{s\nearrow s_{1}^{-}}v(s)\big|\geq 1-2\rho\,,

and similarly

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times(-W,s_{1}^{+}]\big)\Big)\geq 1-2\rho\,.

Since
\rho<1/5
, by (
4.6
) we deduce that
s_{1}^{-}<s_{1}^{+}
. In the same way, we can define

\displaystyle s_{j}^{-}=\sup\Big\{t\in[-W,W]:\,v(t)\geq 1-2^{j}\rho\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle s_{j}^{+}=\inf\Big\{t\in[-W,W]:\,v(t)\leq 2^{j}\rho\Big\}\,,

and we still have that
s_{j}^{-}<s_{j}^{+}
as soon as
2^{j}\rho<1/4
. Calling then
{j_{\rm max}}
the largest integer such that
2^{{j_{\rm max}}}\rho<1/4
, by construction we have
s_{1}^{-}\leq s_{2}^{-}\leq\cdots\leq s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}\leq s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}\leq\cdots\leq s_{2}^{+}\leq s_{1}^{+}
. Notice now that, for almost every
t\in I_{j}:=(s_{j}^{-},s_{j+1}^{-})\cup(s_{j+1}^{+},s_{j}^{+})
, by (
4.9
) we have

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-2}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)\geq C_{N}\big(2^{j}\rho\big)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\,.

As a consequence, calling
l_{j}={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{1}(I_{j})
, by the Coarea Formula we deduce

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times I_{j}\big)\Big)\geq\int_{I_{j}}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-2}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)\,dt\geq C_{N}l_{j}\big(2^{j}\rho\big)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\,.

Moreover, arguing by projection as in (
4.11
), we get

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times[s_{j+1}^{-},s_{j+1}^{+}]\big)\Big)\geq 1-2^{j+2}\rho\,.

Putting the last two estimates together with (
4.6
), we get

1+\rho\geq 1-2^{j+2}\rho+C_{N}l_{j}\big(2^{j}\rho\big)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\,,

which can be rewritten as

l_{j}\leq\frac{2^{-j\,\frac{N-2}{N-1}}(2^{j+2}+1)}{C_{N}}\,\rho^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\leq\frac{8}{C_{N}}\,2^{\frac{j}{N-1}}\,\rho^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\,.

As a consequence, we have

(4.12)

\begin{split}s_{1}^{+}-s_{1}^{-}&=s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}-s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\rm max}}l_{j}\leq s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}-s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}+\frac{16}{C_{N}(2^{\frac{1}{N-1}}-1)}\,(2^{j_{\rm max}}\rho)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\\
&\leq s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}-s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}+\frac{16}{C_{N}4^{\frac{1}{N-1}}(2^{\frac{1}{N-1}}-1)}\,.\end{split}

Moreover, by definition, for almost every
s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}<t<s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}
we have

\frac{1}{8}\leq 2^{j_{\rm max}}\rho<v(t)<1-2^{j_{\rm max}}\rho\leq\frac{7}{8}\,,

which again by (
4.9
) implies

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-2}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)\geq\frac{C_{N}}{8^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}}\,,

and then

2\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap\big(Q\times[s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-},s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}]\big)\Big)\geq\frac{C_{N}}{8^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}}\,\big(s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}-s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}\big)\,,

which can be rewritten as

s_{j_{\rm max}}^{+}-s_{j_{\rm max}}^{-}\leq\frac{2\cdot 8^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}}{C_{N}}\,.

Putting together this estimate and (
4.12
), we get a purely dimensional constant
H=H(N)
such that
s_{1}^{+}-s_{1}^{-}<H
. By definition (
4.10
), we can find
a
and
b
sufficiently close to
s_{1}^{-}
and
s_{1}^{+}
so that
b-a<H
and that
v(a)\geq 1-\rho
and
v(b)\leq\rho
. This gives the validity of (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) with
a
and
b
in place of
-W
and
W
. Since the validity of (
4.6
) with
(a,b)
in place of
(-W,W)
is obvious, the thesis is concluded.
∎

The importance of the above technical result in our paper relies in the following consequence.

Proposition 4.4
.

For every
\theta>0
there exists
\rho\leq\theta
, only depending on
\theta
and on
N
, such that the following holds. Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a set, and assume that for some
R>r>0
, calling
Q_{r}=(-r/2,r/2)^{N-1}
and
Q_{r}^{R}=Q_{r}\times(-R/2,R/2)
one has

(4.13)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)\leq(1+\rho)r^{N-1}\,,

(4.14)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\big(E\cap Q_{r}^{R}\big)\Delta\big(Q_{r}\times(-R/2,0)\big)\Big)\leq\rho^{2}\,r^{N-1}R\,.

Then, there exist
\bar{t}\in[-\rho R-\theta r,\rho R+\theta r]
and
r/2\leq r^{-}\leq r
such that, calling

(4.15)

F=\big(E\setminus Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\cup\big(Q_{r^{-}}\times(-R/2,\bar{t})\big)\,,

one has

(4.16)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(E\Delta F\big)\leq\theta r^{N}\,,

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\leq\theta\,r^{N-1}\,,

\displaystyle|F|=|E|\,.

Proof.

By rescaling, we can reduce ourselves to consider the case
r=1
, which makes the notation simpler. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us then define
\rho_{n}=1/n
, and let
\{E_{n}\}
be any set such that (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) hold true with constant
\rho_{n}
in place of
\rho
,
Q=Q_{1}
in place of
Q_{r}
, and some
R_{n}
in place of
R
. To show the thesis, it is enough to prove the validity of (
4.16
) for
n
large enough, up to a subsequence.

Let
n
be fixed. Since by (
4.14
) and by Fubini Theorem

\rho_{n}^{2}R_{n}\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\big(E_{n}\cap Q^{R_{n}}\big)\Delta\big(Q\times(-R_{n}/2,0)\big)\Big)\geq\int_{0}^{\rho_{n}R_{n}}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{t\}\big)\Big)\,dt\,,

there exists some
0<R_{n}^{+}<\rho_{n}R_{n}
such that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{R_{n}^{+}\}\big)\Big)\leq\rho_{n}\,,

and analogously there exists some
-\rho_{n}R_{n}<R_{n}^{-}<0
such that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{R_{n}^{-}\}\big)\Big)\geq 1-\rho_{n}\,.

Therefore, we can apply Lemma
4.3
with the set
E_{n}
and the interval
(R_{n}^{-},R_{n}^{+})
, and we get an interval
(a_{n},b_{n})
of length at most
H
such that conditions (
4.6
)–(
4.8
) are satisfied for the set
E_{n}
and the interval
(a_{n},b_{n})
, with
\rho=\rho_{n}
. We now define the set
\widetilde{E}_{n}
as

\widetilde{E}_{n}=\Big\{(x^{\prime},x_{N}-a_{n}),\,(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in E_{n},\,x^{\prime}\in Q,\,x_{N}\in(a_{n},b_{n})\Big\}\cup\big(Q\times(-1,0)\big)\,.

In words,
\widetilde{E}_{n}
is obtained by taking the part of
E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times(-R_{n},R_{n})\big)
relative to the interval
(a_{n},b_{n})
, translating it vertically so that the height
\{a_{n}\}
corresponds to
0
, and then adding the cube
Q\times(-1,0)
. We will consider the set
\widetilde{E}_{n}
inside the box
D=Q\times(-1,H+1)
. By construction, also keeping in mind that the last coordinate of any point of
\widetilde{E}_{n}
is at most
b_{n}-a_{n}\leq H
, we have

\begin{split}P_{\rm Eucl}\big(\widetilde{E}_{n};D\big)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times(a_{n},b_{n})\big)\Big)&+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{b_{n}\}\big)\Big)\\
&+1-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{a_{n}\}\big)\Big)\,,\end{split}

where the first part is the perimeter of
\widetilde{E}_{n}
inside
Q\times(0,b_{n}-a_{n})
, and the second and third part are the perimeter at the level
b_{n}-a_{n}
and
0
respectively. Keeping in mind that for the set
E_{n}
the estimate (
4.13
) holds, as well as (
4.6
)–(
4.8
) with
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
, the above inequality gives

(4.17)

P_{\rm Eucl}\big(\widetilde{E}_{n};D\big)\leq 1+3\rho_{n}\,.

The sets
\widetilde{E}_{n}
are then a sequence of sets of bounded perimeter in the bounded box
D
. There exists then a subsequence such that
\chi
{}_{{}_{\widetilde{E}_{n}}}
converges in the weak BV sense to
\chi
{}_{{}_{E}}
for some set
E\subseteq D
. Since the convergence is in particular strong in
L^{1}
, we deduce that

(4.18)

\displaystyle E\cap\big(Q\times(H,H+1)\big)=\emptyset\,,

\displaystyle Q\times(-1,0)\subseteq E\,.

By lower semicontinuity of the perimeter and by (
4.17
) we obtain that
P_{\rm Eucl}(E;D)\leq 1
, which together with (
4.18
) implies that
E
is actually of the form

E=Q\times(-1,t)

for some
t\in[0,H]
. As a consequence, for
n
large enough we have

\frac{\theta}{5\cdot 2^{N}}>{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\widetilde{E}_{n}\Delta\big(Q\times(-1,t)\big)\Big)\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\big(\widetilde{E}_{n}\Delta\big(Q\times(-1,t)\big)\big)\cap\big(Q\times(0,t\wedge(b_{n}-a_{n})\big)\Big)\,.

Keeping in mind the definition of
\widetilde{E}_{n}
, and setting
\hat{t}_{n}=a_{n}+t\wedge(b_{n}-a_{n})\in(a_{n},b_{n}]
, we deduce

(4.19)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\bigg(\Big(E_{n}\Delta\big(Q\times(a_{n},\hat{t}_{n})\big)\Big)\cap\big(Q\times(a_{n},b_{n})\big)\bigg)<\frac{\theta}{5\cdot 2^{N}}\,.

Since by construction we have

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{a_{n}\}\big)\Big)\geq 1-\rho_{n}\,,

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times\{b_{n}\}\big)\Big)\leq\rho_{n}\,,

we deduce that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E_{n}\cap\big(Q\times(a_{n},b_{n})\big)\Big)\geq 1-2\rho_{n}\,,

which by (
4.13
) implies

(4.20)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\bigg(\partial^{*}E_{n}\cap\Big(Q\times\big((-R_{n}/2,a_{n})\cup(b_{n},R_{n}/2)\big)\Big)\bigg)\leq 3\rho_{n}\,.

In particular,
P_{\rm Eucl}(E_{n};Q\times(-R_{n}/2,a_{n}))\leq 3\rho_{n}
, and by the relative isoperimetric inequality this implies that the volume of
E_{n}\cap(Q\times(-R_{n}/2,a_{n}))
is either smaller than
\rho_{n}
or larger than
a_{n}+R_{n}/2-\rho_{n}
(keep in mind that
\rho_{n}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\gg\rho_{n}
for
n
large enough). By (
4.14
) and the fact that
\rho_{n}\ll 1
, we exclude the first possibility; in other words, we have obtained

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\big(Q\times(-R_{n}/2,a_{n})\big)\setminus E_{n}\Big)<\rho_{n}\,.

In the very same way,
P_{\rm Eucl}(E_{n};Q\times(b_{n},R_{n}/2))\leq 3\rho_{n}
, and by the relative isoperimetric inequality we deduce that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\big(Q\times(b_{n},R_{n}/2)\big)\cap E_{n}\Big)<\rho_{n}\,.

Putting together last two estimates and (
4.19
) we obtain, for
n
large enough,

(4.21)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\bigg(\Big(E_{n}\Delta\big(Q\times(-R_{n}/2,\hat{t}_{n})\big)\Big)\cap Q^{R_{n}}\bigg)<\frac{\theta}{2^{N+2}}\,.

Now, for every
s<1
we can call

\Gamma_{s}=\Big(E_{n}\Delta\big(Q\times(-R_{n}/2,\hat{t}_{n})\big)\Big)\cap\Big(\partial Q_{s}\times\big(-R_{n}/2,R_{n}/2\big)\Big)\,.

By Fubini Theorem, the estimate (
4.21
) gives

\frac{\theta}{2^{N+2}}>\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\Gamma_{s}\big)\,ds\,,

and then there exists some
1/2<r_{n}^{-}<1
such that

(4.22)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\Gamma_{r_{n}^{-}}\big)<\frac{\theta}{2^{N+1}}\,.

We define now the temptative set
\widehat{F}_{n}
as

\widehat{F}_{n}=\Big(E_{n}\setminus Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\Big)\cup\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times(-R_{n}/2,\hat{t}_{n})\big)\,.

According to (
4.15
), the actual set
F_{n}
will be defined in the very same way, just with some
\bar{t}
in place of
\hat{t}
. First of all, we notice that

(4.23)

-\rho_{n}R_{n}\leq\hat{t}\leq\rho_{n}R_{n}\,,

which is clear by construction since
\hat{t}_{n}\in[a_{n},b_{n}]\subseteq[-R_{n}^{-},R_{n}^{+}]\subseteq(-\rho_{n}R_{n},\rho_{n}R_{n})
. Moreover, we claim that

(4.24)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(E_{n}\Delta\widehat{F}_{n}\big)\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{N+2}}\,,

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\partial^{*}\widehat{F}_{n}\cap\partial Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\big)\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{N}}\,.

The left estimate is directly given by (
4.21
). Concerning the right one, notice that

(4.25)

\partial\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\big)=\big(\partial Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times(-R_{n}/2,R_{n}/2)\big)\cup\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{-R_{n}/2\}\big)\cup\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{R_{n}/2\}\big)\,.

By (
4.22
), we have

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\widehat{F}_{n}\cap\big(\partial Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times(-R_{n}/2,R_{n}/2)\big)\Big)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\Gamma_{r_{n}^{-}}\big)<\frac{\theta}{2^{N+1}}\,.

Since
P_{\rm Eucl}(E_{n};Q\times(-R_{n}/2,a_{n}))\leq 3\rho_{n}
by (
4.20
), keeping in mind that by construction (
4.7
) is valid for
E_{n}
with
\rho=\rho_{n}
and
W=a_{n}
, by projection we deduce that

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\widehat{F}_{n}&\cap\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{-R_{n}/2\}\big)\Big)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{-R_{n}/2\}\big)\setminus E_{n}\Big)\\
&\leq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}E_{n}\cap\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times(-R_{n}/2,a_{n})\big)\Big)+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{a_{n}\}\big)\setminus E_{n}\Big)\\
&\leq 3\rho_{n}+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(Q\times\{a_{n}\}\big)\setminus E_{n}\Big)\leq 4\rho_{n}\,,\end{split}

and in the very same way

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\widehat{F}_{n}\cap\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{R_{n}/2\}\big)\Big)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times\{R_{n}/2\}\big)\cap E_{n}\Big)\leq 4\rho_{n}\,.

Putting together the last three estimates, by (
4.25
) we obtain

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\widehat{F}_{n}\cap\partial Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\Big)\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{N+1}}+8\rho_{n}\,,

and this implies that also the right estimate in (
4.24
) is true as soon as
n
is large enough. Summarizing, the set
\widehat{F}_{n}
already satisfies the first two estimates in (
4.16
), and we only have to modify it to get also the third. We can then finally define, according to (
4.15
),

\displaystyle F_{n}=\Big(E_{n}\setminus Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\Big)\cup\big(Q_{r_{n}^{-}}\times(-R_{n}/2,\bar{t}_{n})\big)\,,

where

\displaystyle\bar{t}_{n}=\hat{t}_{n}+\frac{{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(E_{n})-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(\widehat{F}_{n})}{(r_{n}^{-})^{N-1}}\,.

First of all we underline that, thanks to the first estimate in (
4.24
), we have

(4.26)

\big|\bar{t}_{n}-\hat{t}_{n}\big|\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{N+2}(r_{n}^{-})^{N-1}}\leq\frac{\theta}{8}\,.

As a consequence, thanks to (
4.23
) we have that
\bar{t}\in[-\rho_{n}R_{n}-\theta,\rho_{n}R_{n}+\theta]
as required, ad we have to check the validity of (
4.16
). The third estimate in (
4.16
) is satisfied by the definition of
\bar{t}
, so we have to check the first two. Concerning the first one, it holds since by construction and by (
4.24
) we have

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(E_{n}\Delta F_{n})&\leq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(E_{n}\Delta\widehat{F}_{n})+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(\widehat{F}_{n}\Delta F_{n})={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(E_{n}\Delta\widehat{F}_{n})+\Big|{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(\widehat{F}_{n})-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(E_{n})\Big|\\
&\leq 2{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(E_{n}\Delta\widehat{F}_{n})\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{N+1}}\leq\theta\,.\end{split}

And finally, the second one is satisfied since by construction, by (
4.24
) and by (
4.26
) we have

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\partial^{*}F_{n}\cap\partial Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\big)&\leq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\partial^{*}\widehat{F}_{n}\cap\partial Q_{r_{n}^{-}}^{R_{n}}\big)+2(N-1)(r_{n}^{-})^{N-2}|\bar{t}_{n}-\hat{t}_{n}|\\
&\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{N}}+\frac{2(N-1)\theta}{2^{N+1}}\leq\theta\,.\end{split}

The proof is then concluded.
∎

We are now almost ready to present the proof of Theorem B. The last ingredient we need is an auxilary problem. More precisely, for any vector
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we consider clusters contained in
\mathbb{R}_{+,\nu}^{N}=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,x\cdot\nu>0\big\}
, and we consider the relative perimeter
P_{*}(\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})
. This is defined as follows. For every set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, we define

P_{*}(F;\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})=\int_{\partial^{*}F\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu}}\|\nu_{F}(x)\|\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\,,

and as usual for every cluster
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu}
we set

P_{*}(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})=\frac{1}{2}\,\sum\nolimits_{j=0}^{m}P_{*}(F_{j};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})\,.

Notice that the part of the boundary of a set
F
or of a cluster
\mathcal{F}
which lies on the hyperplane
\{x\cdot\nu=0\}
has no cost. As a consequence, it is standard to observe that there are clusters
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu}
such that
P_{*}(\mathcal{F};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})<J(|\mathcal{F}|)
. By compactness, there exists a constant
\xi>0
, only depening on the norm
\|\cdot\|
(and then on
M
), such that for every
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
with
|v|_{1}=1
and for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, there exists a cluster
\mathcal{F}^{\nu}_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu}
such that

(4.27)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{\nu}_{v}|=v\,,

\displaystyle P_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\nu}_{v};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})\leq(1-\xi)J(v)\,.

Notice that, as an obvious consequence, any such cluster must satisfy

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\nu}_{v}\cap\big\{x\cdot\nu=0\big\}\Big)>\frac{\xi J(v)}{C_{2}^{+}}\,,

since
P_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\nu}_{v})\geq J(v)
. Again by compactness, we can call

(4.28)

d^{+}=\max\Big\{{\rm diam}(\mathcal{F}^{\nu}_{v}),\,|v|_{1}=1,\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\Big\}\,.

We are finally ready to present the proof of Theorem B. The basic idea is that if a very small minimal cluster is not connected, then by Lemma
4.2
the biggest connected component must contain almost all the minimal cluster. Then, by making use of Proposition
4.4
we can make the boundary flat around some external point of the biggest component, and then substitute all the other connected components with a minimal cluster “attached” to the flat part as in the auxiliary problem just discussed.

Proof of Theorem B.

We have to consider the case when the manifold
M
is Riemannian (that is, the fixed norm is the Euclidean one). However, we will use this assumption almost only in Step III and in the last Step VI, so we write most of the proof for a generic fixed norm.

Step I.
Definition of the clusters
\mathcal{F}_{j}
and
\mathcal{F}
.

Let us assume that the claim is false. Then, we can take a sequence of minimal
m
-clusters
\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
which are not connected and whose total volumes
\omega_{j}=\big||\mathcal{E}_{j}|\big|_{M}
converge to
0
. We denote the volume of each of the clusters as
|\mathcal{E}_{j}|=\omega_{j}v_{j}
, so that each
v_{j}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
satisfies
|v_{j}|_{1}=1
. Moreover, we call
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{j}
the largest connected component of
\mathcal{E}_{j}
, and we denote its volume as
|\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}|=\omega_{j}v_{j}^{-}
; notice that
v_{j}^{-}\leq v_{j}
(that is, for every
1\leq i\leq m
we have
(v_{j}^{-})_{i}\leq(v_{j})_{i}
). There are now two possibilities: either
v_{j}-v_{j}^{-}
converges to
0
, or it does not. In the second case, there exists some
\delta>0
such that
\big||\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}|\big|<(1-\delta)\big||\mathcal{E}_{j}|\big|
for infinitely many indices
j\in\mathbb{N}
, but then Lemma
4.2
implies that
\big||\mathcal{E}_{j}|\big|\geq\varepsilon_{\delta}
for all these indices, and this is impossible since
\big||\mathcal{E}_{j}|\big|=\omega_{j}\to 0
. As a consequence, we can assume without loss of generality that
v_{j}-v_{j}^{-}\to 0
; in other words, the percentage of the total volume of the cluster
\mathcal{E}_{j}
which corresponds to points not belonging to
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}
goes to
0
when
j\to\infty
.

By compactness, up to a subsequence we can assume the existence of a point
\bar{p}\in M
and of a sequence
p_{j}\to\bar{p}
such that each
p_{j}
is contained in the cluster
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}
. Now, let
\sigma<4^{-N}
be a small constant, whose exact value will be specified at the end, precisely in (
4.63
). Let us fix a balanced atlas with oscillation smaller than
\sigma
; as already done before, without loss of generality we can assume the existence of a chart
\varphi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}
such that
\bar{p}\in V_{i}
and, calling
\hat{x}=\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\bar{p})
,

(4.29)

\|\cdot\|_{i,\hat{x}}=\|\cdot\|\qquad\hbox{or, equivalently,}\qquad h_{i}(\hat{x},\nu)=\|\nu\|\quad\forall\,\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,.

Applying Theorem
2.1
, we get a constant
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
; then, for every
j
large enough we have that
\omega_{j}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and then each connected component of the cluster
\mathcal{E}_{j}
is contained in a single chart, and according to Corollary
2.2
its diameter is going to
0
. In particular, up to pass to a subsequence we can assume that all the clusters
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}
are compactly contained in the chart
V_{i}
, with
{\rm diam}\big(\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})\big)\leq 4C_{4}\omega_{j}^{1/N}
. Now, we can select a sequence
\{x_{j}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
so that each cluster

(4.30)

\mathcal{F}_{j}=x_{j}+\frac{1}{\omega_{j}^{1/N}}\,\varphi_{i}^{-1}\big(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}\big)\,,

contains the origin. Notice that the distance between
\hat{x}
and
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})
goes to
0
.

By construction, the volume of the cluster
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is
v_{j}^{-}
; moreover, each cluster
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is contained in the ball centered at the origin and with radius
4C_{4}
. As a consequence, up to extract a further subsequence, we can assume that the clusters
\mathcal{F}_{j}
have a weak limit
\mathcal{F}
; this means that, writing
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\big(F_{j,1},\,F_{j,2},\,\dots\,,\,F_{j,m}\big)
, for every
1\leq q\leq m
the characteristic function
\chi
{}_{{}_{F_{j,q}}}
is converging to
\chi
{}_{{}_{F_{q}}}
in the weak BV sense for
j\to\infty
. Notice that, since the clusters
\mathcal{F}_{j}
are all contained in a single ball, the convergence is in particular strong in
L^{1}
; therefore,
|\mathcal{F}|=v
, where
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
is the common limit of both
\{v_{j}\}
and
\{v_{j}^{-}\}
, in particular
|v|_{1}=1
.

Step II.
The validity of (
4.31
).

We claim now that

(4.31)

P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{j})\to P_{*}(\mathcal{F})\,.

Indeed, since by lower semicontinuity of the perimeter we know that
P_{*}(\mathcal{F})\leq\liminf P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{j})
, if (
4.31
) were false then there would be some positive constant
c
such that, up to pass to a subsequence,
P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{j})>P_{*}(\mathcal{F})(1+3c)
for every
j\gg 1
. By continuity of the perimeter, for every
j\gg 1
we could find some cluster
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
, very close to
\mathcal{F}
but satisfying
|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j}|=v_{j}
, so that

(4.32)

P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{j})>P_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j})(1+2c)\,.

Define now

(4.33)

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}=\varphi_{i}\Big(\hat{x}+\omega_{j}^{1/N}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j}\Big)\,.

Since
\hat{x}\in U_{i}
and
\omega_{j}\to 0
, for every
j
large enough the set
\hat{x}+\omega_{j}^{1/N}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
is compactly contained in
U_{i}
, so the above definition makes sense. Keeping in mind (
4.29
), the definitions (
4.30
) and (
4.33
) ensure that when
j\to+\infty
we have

\displaystyle\frac{P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,P_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{j})}\to 1\,,

\displaystyle\frac{P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,P_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j})}\to 1\,.

However, these estimates together with (
4.32
) imply that for
j
large enough

P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j})\geq P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})\geq(1+c)\,P_{M}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j})\,.

Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}
is a cluster in
M
with volume
|\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}|=\omega_{j}|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j}|=\omega_{j}v_{j}=|\mathcal{E}_{j}|
, the above estimate is a contradiction with the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{j}
is a minimal cluster. Therefore, (
4.31
) is proved.

Step III.
The point
\bar{x}
and the inequalities (
4.37
)–(
4.39
).

Let now
\bar{x}
be a point in
\partial^{*}(\cup_{q=1}^{m}F_{q})
; then,
\bar{x}
belongs to the boundary of exactly one of the sets
F_{q}
with
1\leq q\leq m
, say
q=\bar{q}
. Let
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
be the outer normal vector to
F_{\bar{q}}
at
\bar{x}
, and for every
r>0
let us call
Q_{r}
the
(N-1)
-dimensional cube centered at
\bar{x}
, with side
r
, orthogonal to
\nu
, and with sides parallel to an arbitrary basis of the hyperplane
\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\omega\cdot\nu=0\}
. In addition, for every
R>0
we write

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{R,\pm}=\big\{z\pm t\nu,\,z\in Q_{r},\,0\leq t<R/2\big\}\,,

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{R}=\big\{z+t\nu,\,z\in Q_{r},\,-R/2<t<R/2\big\}\,.

Finally, let
\rho>0
be a small number, to be specified in Step V. By the blow-up properties of the boundary, if
R
is small enough then we have

(4.34)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(F_{\bar{q}}\cap Q_{R}^{R.-}\big)\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{9}\bigg)\,R^{N}\,,

(4.35)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{q=1}^{m}F_{q}\cap Q_{R}^{R,+}\Big)\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{9}\,R^{N}\,,

(4.36)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}\cap Q_{R}^{R}\Big)\leq\bigg(1+\frac{\rho}{5}\bigg)R^{N-1}\,.

We claim now that, choosing a suitable small
R
such that (
4.34
)–(
4.36
) are in force, for
j
large enough one has

(4.37)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap Q_{R}^{R.-}\big)\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{8}\bigg)\,R^{N}\,,

(4.38)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{q=1}^{m}F_{j,q}\cap Q_{R}^{R,+}\Big)\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{8}\,R^{N}\,,

(4.39)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{R}^{R}\Big)\leq\bigg(1+\frac{\rho}{4}\bigg)R^{N-1}\,.

In fact, since the sets
F_{j,q}
converge in the
L^{1}
sense to
F_{j}
, the validity of (
4.37
) and (
4.38
) for
j
large is an obvious consequence of (
4.34
) and (
4.35
). Instead, the lower semicontinuity of the perimeter implies that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}\cap Q_{R}^{R}\Big)\leq\liminf{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{R}^{R}\Big)\,,

which does not allow to infer (
4.39
) from (
4.36
). However, we can choose a small
R
such that not only (
4.34
)–(
4.36
) are true, but also

(4.40)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}\cap\partial\big(Q_{R}^{R}\big)\Big)=0\,,

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\partial\big(Q_{R}^{R}\big)\Big)=0\quad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N}\,.

This is not a problem since the above inequalities can fail for at most countably many

. Keeping in mind that all the clusters
\mathcal{F}_{j}
are compactly contained in the ball
B_{5C_{4}}
centered in the origin and with radius

, the above mentioned lower semicontinuity of the perimeter implies that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}\cap\Big(B_{5C_{4}}\setminus\overline{Q_{R}^{R}}\Big)\Big)\leq\liminf{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Big(B_{5C_{4}}\setminus\overline{Q_{R}^{R}}\Big)\Big)\,.

Keeping in mind (
4.31
) from Step II and (
4.40
), and using the fact that
M
is a Riemannian manifold (so the fixed norm
\|\cdot\|
coincides with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
) we deduce that

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}\cap Q_{R}^{R}\Big)\geq\limsup{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{R}^{R}\Big)\,,

and this allows to obtain (
4.39
) from (
4.36
). Summarizing, the estimates (
4.37
)–(
4.39
) are true with a fixed, small

, and for all
j
large enough.

Step IV.
Definition of
r
and proof of (
4.45
).

Since

, as observed in Step I, if
j
is large enough then not only (
4.37
)–(
4.39
) are true, but also

d^{+}\big|v_{j}-v_{j}^{-}\big|_{1}^{1/N}<\frac{R}{6}\,,

where
d^{+}
is the constant defined in (
4.28
). We call then
2\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
the number such that

(4.41)

\frac{R}{3(n+1)}\leq d^{+}\big|v_{j}-v_{j}^{-}\big|_{1}^{1/N}<\frac{R}{3n}\,,

where
|v|_{1}=\sum_{q=1}^{m}|v_{q}|
, and we set
r=R/n
; notice that
r
depends on
j
, and actually
r\to 0
when
j\to\infty
, but we do not write explicitely the dependance of
r
on
j
to keep the notation lighter. The
(N-1)
-dimensional cube
Q_{R}
can be subdivided in
n^{N-1}
essentially disjoint cubes

with side

, with
1\leq i\leq n^{N-1}
. Since
Q_{R}^{R}
is essentially the union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n^{N-1}}Q_{r,i}^{R}
, there is some
1\leq\bar{i}\leq n^{N-1}
such that, writing for brevity
Q_{r}
in place of

, one has

(4.42)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap Q_{r}^{R.-}\big)\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\bigg)\,r^{N-1}R\,,

(4.43)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{q=1}^{m}F_{j,q}\cap Q_{r}^{R,+}\Big)\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\,r^{N-1}R\,,

(4.44)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)\leq(1+\rho)r^{N-1}\,.

In fact, by (
4.37
) we have that (
4.42

of the cubes

, and the same is true for (
4.43

4.38
) and for (
4.44
) by (
4.39
). Notice that (
4.42
) and (
4.43
) ensure that, writing for brevity

F_{j}^{\rm\,other}=\bigcup\nolimits_{q\neq\bar{q}}F_{j,q}\,,

one has

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)\leq\rho^{2}r^{N-1}R\,.

But in fact, we claim that a much stronger inequality is true, namely,

(4.45)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\rho r^{N}

for some purely geometric constant
C_{N}^{\prime}
, only depending on

. To show this estimate, for every
z\in Q_{r}
we call
S_{z}=\{z+t\nu,\,-R<t<R\}
, and we call

\displaystyle T_{1}=\big\{z\in Q_{r},\,\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap S_{z}=\emptyset\big\}\,,

\displaystyle T_{2}=\big\{z\in Q_{r}\setminus T_{1},\,\#(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap S_{z})\geq 2\big\}\,,

\displaystyle T_{3}=\big\{z\in Q_{r}\setminus(T_{1}\cup T_{2}),\,\cup_{q\neq\bar{q}}\,\partial^{*}F_{j,q}\cap S_{z}\neq\emptyset\big\}\,.

If
z\in T_{1}
, then
S_{z}
either does not intersect
F_{j,\bar{q}}
, or is entirely contained in it. Therefore, by (
4.42
) and (
4.43
)

\rho^{2}r^{N-1}R\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(F_{j,\bar{q}}\Delta Q_{r}^{R,-}\big)\geq\frac{R}{2}\,{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{1})\,,

and this implies

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{1})\leq 2\rho^{2}r^{N-1}\,.

Moreover, by (
4.44
)

(1+\rho)r^{N-1}\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(Q_{r}\setminus T_{1})+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{2})\geq(1-2\rho^{2})r^{N-1}+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{2})\,,

and this implies

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{2})\leq(\rho+2\rho^{2})r^{N-1}\,.

Finally, if
z\in T_{3}
then
S_{z}
contains only a single point of
\partial\mathcal{F}_{j}
, and this point is common boundary between
F_{j,\bar{q}}
and exactly one
F_{j,q}
with
q\neq\bar{q}
. As a consequence, the whole
S_{z}
is contained in
\cup_{q=1}^{m}F_{j,q}
, and then by (
4.43
) we have

\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\,r^{N-1}R\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{q=1}^{m}F_{j,q}\cap Q_{r}^{R,+}\Big)\geq R{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{3})\,,

which implies

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{3})\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\,r^{N-1}\,.

The bounds on the measure of
T_{1},\,T_{2}
and
T_{3}
, and the fact that
\rho
is small, ensure that

(4.46)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup T_{3})\leq\bigg(\rho+\frac{9}{2}\,\rho^{2}\bigg)r^{N-1}\leq 2\rho r^{N-1}\,,

where we have called
T=T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup T_{3}
. Notice now that if
z\notin T
then
S_{z}
contains exactly a point of
\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}
, and this point belongs to
\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}
and to no other
\partial^{*}F_{j,q}
with
q\neq\bar{q}
. Therefore,

(4.47)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\big((Q_{r}\setminus T)\times(-R/2,R/2)\big)\Big)\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(Q_{r}\setminus T)\geq\big(1-2\rho\big)r^{N-1}\,.

By definition, and keeping in mind (
4.44
), we have then

(4.48)

\begin{split}\hskip 25.0pt&\hskip-25.0pt{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\bigg(\partial^{*}F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\bigg)={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\bigg(\partial^{*}F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap\big(T\times(-R/2,R/2)\big)\bigg)\\
&\leq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\big(T\times(-R/2,R/2)\big)\Big)\\
&={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\big((Q_{r}\setminus T)\times(-R/2,R/2)\big)\Big)\leq 3\rho r^{N-1}\,.\end{split}

Since
\rho
is very small, by the relative isoperimetric inequality the above bound implies

(4.49)

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\big)\Big)\leq C_{N}\rho r^{N-1}

for some purely dimensional constant
C_{N}
(which is elementary to calculate, but whose exact value plays no role here). And then, by the standard isoperimetric inequality we deduce

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)\leq\frac{\big(C_{N}\rho r^{N-1}\big)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}=C_{N}^{\prime}\rho^{\frac{N}{N-1}}r^{N}\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\rho r^{N}

with a suitable, purely dimensional constant
C_{N}^{\prime}
. This ensures the validity of (
4.45
), and thus concludes this step.

Step V.
Definition of the cluster
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
and validity of (
4.52
).

Now, we can specify the value of the constant
\rho
. Namely, we set
\theta=\sigma
, and we choose
\rho
corresponding to
\theta
by means of Proposition
4.4
, possibly reduced so that

(4.50)

\displaystyle C_{N}^{\prime}\rho\leq\frac{1}{(6d^{+})^{N}}\,,

\displaystyle 2^{N-1}(C_{N}^{\prime})^{\frac{N-1}{N}}N\omega_{N}^{1/N}m^{1/N}\rho^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq\sigma\,,

where
d^{+}
is the constant given by (
4.28
). We aim now to apply Proposition
4.4
to the set
F_{j,\bar{q}}
; notice that this is possible because (
4.13
) is valid by (
4.44
), since
\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\subseteq\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}
, and (
4.14
) is valid by (
4.42
) and (
4.43
). We obtain then
\bar{t}\in[-\rho R-\theta r,\rho R+\theta r]
and
r/2\leq r^{-}\leq r
such that, calling

F^{\prime}_{j,\bar{q}}=\big(F_{j,\bar{q}}\setminus Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\cup\big\{z+t\nu,\,z\in Q_{r^{-}},\,-R/2<t<\bar{t}\big\}\,,

one has

(4.51)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\big(F_{j,\bar{q}}\Delta F_{j,\bar{q}}^{\prime}\big)\leq\theta r^{N}\,,

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}^{\prime}\cap\partial Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)\leq\theta\,r^{N-1}\,,

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(F_{j,\bar{q}})={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}(F_{j,\bar{q}}^{\prime})\,.

We can now define a modified cluster
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
; namely, while
F_{j,\bar{q}}^{\prime}
has been just defined, for every
q\in\{1,\,2,\,\dots\,,\,m\}\setminus\{\bar{q}\}
we set
F_{j,q}^{\prime}=F_{j,q}\setminus Q_{r^{-}}^{R}
. We also call
v_{j}^{\prime}=|\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}|
. Notice that, since for every
q\neq\bar{q}
we have

|v_{j,q}^{-}-v_{j,q}^{\prime}|=\big||F_{j,q}|-|F_{j,q}^{\prime}|\big|=\big|F_{j,q}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big|\,,

by (
4.45
) we get

(4.52)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\neq\bar{q}}|v_{j,q}^{-}-v_{j,q}^{\prime}|={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\rho r^{N}\leq 2^{N}C_{N}^{\prime}\rho(r^{-})^{N}\,,

while

\displaystyle v_{j,\bar{q}}^{\prime}=v_{j,\bar{q}}^{-}\,.

Notice that by construction, keeping in mind that
\bar{t}+r\leq\rho R+(\theta+1)r<R
since
\theta
and
\rho
are small and
R=nr\geq 2r
, we have

(4.53)

\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\big(Q_{r^{-}}\times(\bar{t},\bar{t}+r)\big)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\big(Q_{r^{-}}\times(\bar{t},R/2)\big)=\emptyset\,.

We conclude this step by showing that

(4.54)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)=(r^{-})^{N-1}\,,

(4.55)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)\geq\big(1-2^{N}\rho\big)(r^{-})^{N-1}\,,

(4.56)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\Big)\leq 2^{N-1}C_{N}\rho(r^{-})^{N-1}\,.

The property (
4.54
) is true by construction. The property (
4.55
) can be obtained arguing as in (
4.47
); indeed, keeping in mind (
4.46
) and since
r\leq 2r^{-}
, we have

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)&\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap\big((Q_{r^{-}}\setminus T)\times(-R/2,R/2)\big)\Big)\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(Q_{r^{-}}\setminus T)\\
&\geq(r^{-})^{N-1}-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T)\geq(r^{-})^{N-1}-2\rho r^{N-1}\geq\big(1-2^{N}\rho\big)(r^{-})^{N-1}\,.\end{split}

To obtain (
4.56
), we first write

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\big(Q_{r}^{R}\setminus Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\Big)&\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big((Q_{r}\setminus Q_{r^{-}})\setminus T\big)\\
&=r^{N-1}-(r^{-})^{N-1}-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(T\cap(Q_{r}\setminus Q_{r^{-}})\big)\,.\end{split}

Hence, by (
4.44
) we get

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)&={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{r}^{R}\Big)-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\big(Q_{r}^{R}\setminus Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\Big)\\
&\leq\rho r^{N-1}+(r^{-})^{N-1}+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(T\cap(Q_{r}\setminus Q_{r^{-}})\big)\,.\end{split}

Arguing again as in (
4.47
), we have

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\bigg(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\bigg((Q_{r^{-}}\setminus T)\times\bigg(-\frac{R}{2},\frac{R}{2}\bigg)\!\bigg)\!\bigg)\geq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(Q_{r^{-}}\setminus T)=(r^{-})^{N-1}-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T\cap Q_{r^{-}})\,.

Arguing as in (
4.48
) and again keeping in mind (
4.46
) and the fact that
r\leq 2r^{-}
, the last two estimates give then

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\bigg(&\partial^{*}F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\bigg)\\
&\leq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)-{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\bigg(\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\bigg((Q_{r^{-}}\setminus T)\times\bigg(-R/2,R/2\bigg)\bigg)\bigg)\\
&\leq\rho r^{N-1}+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(T)\leq 3\rho r^{N-1}\leq 3\cdot 2^{N-1}\rho(r^{-})^{N-1}\,.\end{split}

And finally, exactly as in (
4.49
), by the relative isoperimetric inequality the last bound implies (
4.56
).

Step VI.
Definition of
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
and conclusion.

We are finally in position to conclude. Keeping in mind that
\mathcal{F}_{j}
was defined by
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}
by (
4.30
), we would like to set

(4.57)

\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i}\Big(\omega_{j}^{1/N}\big(\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}-x_{j}\big)\Big)\,.

Let us check that this definition makes sense. First of all, we observe that by construction
\mathcal{F}_{j}\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
is contained in
Q_{R}^{R}
, which is a cube with side
R
which surely intersects
\mathcal{F}_{j}
, for instance by (
4.37
). Therefore, calling for a moment
\mathcal{G}_{j}=\omega_{j}^{1/N}\big(\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}-x_{j}\big)
, we have that
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})\Delta\mathcal{G}_{j}
is contained in a cube of side
\omega_{j}^{1/N}R
which intersects
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})
. As shown in Step I, the sets
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})
have diameters which go to
0
and they converge to a point
\hat{x}\in U_{i}
. Since
U_{i}
is open and
\omega_{j}\to 0
, this implies that
\mathcal{G}_{j}
is compactly contained in
U_{i}
for every
j
large enough. Therefore, the cluster
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{G}_{j})
is well-defined as soon as
j
is large enough, and then the definition (
4.57
) makes sense. Moreover, by the construction of Step V we know that for every
q\neq\bar{q}
the set
F_{j,q}^{\prime}
is contained in
F_{j,q}
, thus minding (
4.30
) and (
4.57
) we have also that
E_{j,q}^{\prime}
is contained in
E_{j,q}^{+}
; in addition, by (
4.52
)

\sum_{q\neq\bar{q}}\big|E^{+}_{j,q}\setminus E_{j,q}^{\prime}\big|=\omega_{j}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{q\neq\bar{q}}F_{j,q}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\omega_{j}\rho r^{N}\,.

We now want to estimate
P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j})
. In doing this, we will again use that the manifold is Riemannian, and not just of fixed type. Since the atlas is balanced and has oscillation smaller than
\sigma
, then
1-\sigma\leq h_{i}(x,\nu)\leq 1+\sigma
for every
x\in U_{i}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. By comparing (
4.57
) and (
4.30
), and keeping in mind the construction of
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
, we have then

\begin{split}\frac{P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq(1+\sigma){\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(\partial^{*}F^{\prime}_{j,\bar{q}}\cap\overline{Q_{r^{-}}^{R}}\big)&\cup\partial^{*}\big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\Big)\\
&-(1-\sigma){\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\big(\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap\overline{Q_{r^{-}}^{R}}\big)\,.\end{split}

Putting together (
4.56
), (
4.54
), and the second inequality in (
4.51
), we have

\begin{split}{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\big(\partial^{*}&F^{\prime}_{j,\bar{q}}\cap\overline{Q_{r^{-}}^{R}}\big)\cup\partial^{*}\big(F_{j}^{\rm\,other}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\big)\Big)\\
&\leq{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F^{\prime}_{j,\bar{q}}\cap Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)+{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F^{\prime}_{j,\bar{q}}\cap\partial Q_{r^{-}}^{R}\Big)+2^{N-1}C_{N}\rho(r^{-})^{N-1}\\
&\leq(r^{-})^{N-1}+\theta r^{N-1}+2^{N-1}C_{N}\rho(r^{-})^{N-1}\leq\Big(1+2^{N-1}\big(\theta+C_{N}\rho\big)\Big)\,(r^{-})^{N-1}\,,\end{split}

while (
4.55
) gives

{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}\Big(\partial^{*}F_{j,\bar{q}}\cap\overline{Q_{r^{-}}^{R}}\Big)\geq\big(1-2^{N}\rho\big)(r^{-})^{N-1}\,,

and then the above inequality implies

(4.58)

\begin{split}\frac{P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}&\leq\Big((1+\sigma)\big(1+2^{N-1}\big(\theta+C_{N}\rho\big)\big)-(1-\sigma)\big(1-2^{N}\rho\big)\Big)(r^{-})^{N-1}\\
&\leq\Big(2\sigma+2^{N}\big(\theta+(C_{N}+1)\rho\big)\Big)(r^{-})^{N-1}\leq 2^{N}(C_{N}+3)\sigma(r^{-})^{N-1}\,,\end{split}

where the last inequality is true since by construction
\rho\leq\theta=\sigma
. We want to define a competitor
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
for
\mathcal{E}_{j}
. Keeping in mind (
4.57
), we will simply set

(4.59)

\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime}=\varphi_{i}\Big(\omega_{j}^{1/N}\big(\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime\prime}-x_{j}\big)\Big)\,,

where
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
will be a suitable cluster with empty intersection with
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
. Since the volume of
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime}
must equal that of
\mathcal{E}_{j}
, and since
|\mathcal{E}_{j}|=\omega_{j}v_{j}
and
|\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime}|=\omega_{j}v_{j}^{\prime}
, we must have
|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}|=v_{j}-v_{j}^{\prime}
. We define now the cluster
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
as a minimizer of the relative perimeter
P_{*}(\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})
among all the clusters of volume
v_{j}-v_{j}^{\prime}
; keep in mind that
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu}
is the half-space with outer normal equal to
\nu
, and that
\nu
is the normal vector to
F_{\bar{q}}
at
\bar{x}
. By (
4.27
), we know that

(4.60)

P_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})\leq(1-\xi)J\big(v_{j}-v_{j}^{\prime}\big)\,.

Let us then call
\Gamma=\partial^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu}
, so that
P_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}};\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+,\nu})={\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\Gamma)
. Thanks to (
4.28
), keeping in mind that
r=R/n
where
n\geq 2
has been defined by (
4.41
), and by (
4.52
), we have then

{\rm diam}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})\leq d^{+}|v_{j}-v_{j}^{\prime}|_{1}^{1/N}\leq d^{+}|v_{j}-v_{j}^{-}|_{1}^{1/N}+d^{+}|v_{j}^{-}-v_{j}^{\prime}|_{1}^{1/N}\leq\frac{r}{3}+2d^{+}(C_{N}^{\prime}\rho)^{1/N}r^{-}\leq r^{-}\,,

where the last inequality is true thanks to (
4.50
), left.

This diameter estimate has a crucial consequence. Indeed, this implies that the “flat part” of
\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is contained in
Q_{r^{-}}\times\{\bar{t}\}
. Let us be more precise. Thanks to the diameter estimate, we can translate the cluster
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
(without changing its name, to keep the notation as clean as possible) in such a way that
\partial^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\Gamma\subseteq Q_{r^{-}}\times\{\bar{t}\}
, and that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is contained in the cube
Q_{r^{-}}\times[\bar{t},\bar{t}+r^{-}]
. Since by (
4.53
) this cube has no intersection with
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
, we have that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}=\emptyset
as required; thus, the cluster defined in (
4.59
) has the same volume as
\mathcal{E}_{j}
, so it is a possible competitor. Now, notice carefully that, by construction, the “flat part”
\partial^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\Gamma
is contained in
\partial^{*}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
. As a consequence, the difference
P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime})
is only given by the “new boundary” coming from
\Gamma
. More precisely, using again that the oscillation of the atlas is less than
\sigma
and (
4.60
), we have

\frac{P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq(1+\sigma){\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(\Gamma)\leq(1+\sigma)(1-\xi)J\big(v_{j}-v_{j}^{\prime}\big)\,.

Now, since the functional
J
is subadditive, we have

\begin{split}J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{\prime})&\leq J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})+J(v_{j}^{-}-v_{j}^{\prime})\leq J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})+N\omega_{N}^{1/N}m^{1/N}|v_{j}^{-}-v_{j}^{\prime}|_{1}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\\
&\leq J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})+\sigma(r^{-})^{N-1}\,,\end{split}

where the last inequality is true thanks to (
4.52
) and (
4.50
), right. Putting the last two estimates together with (
4.58
), we have then

(4.61)

\begin{split}\frac{P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}&\leq 2^{N}(C_{N}+3)\sigma(r^{-})^{N-1}+(1+\sigma)(1-\xi)\Big(J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})+\sigma(r^{-})^{N-1}\Big)\\
&\leq 2^{N}(C_{N}+4)\sigma(r^{-})^{N-1}+(1+\sigma-\xi)J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})\,.\end{split}

The last observation to be done is that
\mathcal{E}_{j}
is the union of finitely many connected components; the largest is
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}
, and all the others, together, have volume
\omega_{j}(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})
. Therefore, since the atlas is balanced and with oscillation smaller than
\sigma
, and each connected component of
\mathcal{E}_{j}
is compactly contained in a chart, we have

P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j})=P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})+P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})\geq P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+})+(1-\sigma)\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})\,.

This estimate and (
4.61
) imply that

(4.62)

\frac{P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime})-P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j})}{\omega_{j}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\leq 2^{N}(C_{N}+4)\sigma(r^{-})^{N-1}-(\xi-2\sigma)J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})\,.

At last, we notice the obvious estimate

J(v)\geq N\omega_{N}^{1/N}|v|_{1}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\,,

which in particular, again recalling that
r=R/n
with
n\geq 2
identified by (
4.41
), gives

J(v_{j}-v_{j}^{-})\geq N\omega_{N}^{1/N}|v_{j}-v_{j}^{-}|_{1}^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\geq N\omega_{N}^{1/N}\frac{(r^{-})^{N-1}}{(5d^{+})^{N-1}}\,.

We can then finally specify the value of
\sigma
. Indeed, we can take
\sigma<4^{-N}
as any constant satisfying

(4.63)

2^{N}(C_{N}+4)\sigma<(\xi-2\sigma)\,\frac{N\omega_{N}^{1/N}}{(5d^{+})^{N-1}}\,.

Notice that such constant
\sigma
is defined only depending on the dimension
N
. Since this inequality, inserted in (
4.62
), gives
P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\prime\prime})<P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{j})
, we have finally obtained the required contradiction, and the thesis is concluded.
∎

5.
Asymptotic Equivalence of the Isoperimetric Profiles and final remarks

In this last section we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
and present some final remarks. In fact, Theorem
1.1
is a simple consequence of Theorem
2.1
, without making use of Theorems A or B.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We directly consider the general case of a Finsler manifold and prove property (
1.3
), since then the Riemannian case and property (
1.2
) are just a particular case, keeping in mind that for a Riemannian manifold one has
C_{2}^{-}=C_{2}^{+}=1
.

Let us start by calling
d_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
the maximal diameter for
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Euclidean metric, according to Definition
1.9
. Then, we fix a small number
\sigma>0
, and we take any balanced atlas with oscillation smaller than
\sigma
; moreover, we call
\hat{r}
the largest radius of the charts
U_{i}
(which are finitely many balls in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
). Let us apply Theorem
2.1
, obtaining a number
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
; up to replace it with a smaller constant, we can assume that
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}^{1/N}d_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}<\hat{r}
.

Let now
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be any cluster with total volume
\|\mathcal{F}\|<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and which is optimal in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the Euclidean metric, thus
P_{\rm Eucl}(\mathcal{F})=J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)
where
v=|\mathcal{F}|
and by
P_{\rm Eucl}
we denote the standard Euclidean perimeter of sets or clusters in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By definition, the diameter of
\mathcal{F}
is less than
d_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|_{1}^{1/N}<\hat{r}
; thus, up to a translation, we can assume that
\mathcal{F}
is compactly contained in some
U_{i}
, and then there is some point
x_{i}\in U_{i}
such that the unit ball
\{h_{i}(x_{i},\cdot)\leq 1\}
is balanced. Let us then call
\mathcal{E}=\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{F})
, which is a cluster in
M
with volume
|\mathcal{E}|_{M}=v
. For any
1\leq j\leq m
, keeping in mind the definition (
1.7
) of
C_{2}^{+}
and Definition
1.8
of the oscillation, we have

(5.1)

\begin{split}P_{M}(E_{j})&=\int_{\partial^{*}F_{j}}h_{i}\big(x,\nu_{F_{j}}(x)\big)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\\
&\leq(1+\sigma)\int_{\partial^{*}F_{j}}h_{i}\big(x_{i},\nu_{F_{j}}(x)\big)\,d{\mbox{\script H}\,\,}^{N-1}(x)\leq(1+\sigma)C_{2}^{+}P_{\rm Eucl}(F_{j})\,.\end{split}

Since of course the same estimate is valid with
\cup_{j=1}^{m}E_{j}
, recalling that the perimeter of the clusters is given by (
1.5
) we deduce

J_{M}(v)\leq P_{M}(\mathcal{E})\leq(1+\sigma)C_{2}^{+}P_{\rm Eucl}(\mathcal{F})=(1+\sigma)C_{2}^{+}J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)\,.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{E}
be an optimal cluster in the manifold
M
having total volume
\|\mathcal{E}\|\leq\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and call
v=|\mathcal{E}|
. Lemma
3.1
ensures that
\mathcal{E}
is done by finitely many connected components, say
\mathcal{E}=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{H}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
for some
H\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, by Theorem
2.1
we know that each connected component is compactly contained in a chart, so for every
1\leq\ell\leq H
there exists
i(\ell)
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}\subset\subset V_{i(\ell)}
. Calling then
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\varphi_{i(\ell)}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})
, arguing exactly as in (
5.1
) using this time the definition (
1.8
) of
C_{2}^{-}
, we obtain

P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})\geq\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,P_{\rm Eucl}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})\,.

We can then call
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
the union of the different clusters
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, that we can assume to be disjoint up to translations. So, adding over
\ell
, we get

J_{M}(v)=P_{M}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{H}P_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{\ell})\geq\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{H}P_{\rm Eucl}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell})=\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,P_{\rm Eucl}(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\,J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)\,.

Summarizing, for every
\sigma>0
we have found
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
such that, for every
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}
satisfying
|v|\leq\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, one has

\frac{1-\sigma}{C_{2}^{-}}\leq\frac{J_{M}(v)}{J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)}\leq(1+\sigma)C_{2}^{+}\,,

and this concludes the proof of (
1.3
).
∎

5.1.
Final remarks

We conclude now with a few important remarks. The first one deals with an immediate extension of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 5.1
.

In Theorem
1.1
we have considered the case of a Riemannian manifold and the case of a Finsler manifold. In order to keep the claim short, we have not explicitely considered the “intermediate” case of a fixed-norm manifold (see Definition
1.5
). However, it is clear that the very same proof gives also that, if
M
is fixed-norm with norm
\|\cdot\|
, then

\frac{J_{M}(v)}{J^{\|\cdot\|}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v)}\to 1\qquad\hbox{as}\ |v|\to 0\,,

where by
J_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\|\cdot\|}
we denote the multi-isoperimetric profile of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|
.

The second remark deals with another immediate extension, this time of Theorem B.

Remark 5.2
.

We can consider the natural notion of an “almost Riemannian” manifold as follows. We say that the manifold
M
is
almost Riemannian (with parameter
\delta
)
if there is a constant
\delta>0
such that for any
p\in M
and any
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
one has that

1-\delta\leq\frac{\|\nu\|_{p}}{|\nu|}\leq 1+\delta\,.

Of course this property is interesting only for a small value of the parameter
\delta
, and a manifold is Riemannian if and only if it is almost Riemannian with parameter
\delta
for every positive number
\delta
. A quick look at the proof of Theorem B ensures that the claim is true not only for a Riemannian manifold, but also for a manifold which is almost Riemannian with sufficiently small parameter. In fact, the fact that
M
was a Riemannian manifold was only used to be sure that, inside a chart
U_{i}
of a balanced atlas of oscillation
\sigma
, one has
1-\sigma\leq h_{i}(x,\nu)\leq 1+\sigma
for every
x\in U_{i}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. However, the same inequality is clearly true also if the manifold is almost Riemannian with a parameter
\delta<\sigma/3
and provided that we use a balanced atlas with oscillation
\sigma/3
; then the very same proof works also in this more general case.

The next remark ensures that a full extension of Theorem B is false.

Remark 5.3
.

It could seem reasonable to guess that Theorem B remains true for any Finsler manifold, but this is actually false. In fact, thanks to a counterexample contained in
[
6
]
, there exists a compact Finsler manifold inside which there are non connected minimal clusters with arbitrarily small total volume. However, in that example
m
is quite large and the connected components are just two, so this leaves space for further investigation. In particular, it is not clear to us whether the connectedness of small minimal clusters in generic compact Finsler manifolds is true in the case
m=2
.

The last remark deals again with a possible extension of Theorem B.

Remark 5.4
.

We believe that Theorem B remains true for the more general case of a fixed-norm manifold (and then, arguing as in Remark
5.2
, for any “almost fixed-norm manifold”). In fact, in most of the construction of Section
4
we were able to use a generic fixed-norm manifold, and not necessarily a Riemannian one. The assumption that
M
is actually Riemannian has been used only in the proof of Theorem B, in few (but important) points.

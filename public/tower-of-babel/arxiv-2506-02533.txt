Abstract
. Political online participation in the form of discussing political issues and exchanging opinions among citizens is gaining importance with more and more formats being held digitally. To come to a decision, a careful discussion and consideration of opinions and a civil exchange of arguments, which is defined as the act of deliberation , is desirable. The quality of discussions and participation processes in terms of their deliberativeness highly depends on the design of platforms and processes. To facilitate online communication for both participants and initiators, machine learning methods offer a lot of potential. In this work we want to showcase which issues occur in political online discussions and how machine learning can be used to counteract these issues and enhance deliberation.



1.
Introduction

Online political participation processes have a long history. The amount of
research has grown continuously over the last 20 years, particularly with the
ongoing emergence of new platforms that allow for exchange online. Besides simple
exchange of opinions, online participation platforms are also effective
instruments to make use of collective intelligence to find responses to severe
challenges like pandemics
(
mouter2021public
)
or climate change
(
5928663
)
.

Online political participation comes in many different forms. While some
definitions include both passive (e.g.,
reading news articles) and active (e.g., voting) political behavior, in this
work we solely consider processes that involve active engagement of participants
in form of written opinion exchange, e.g., in online citizen councils, referendums and
discussions on political topics
(
ruess2021online
)
. Participation in
political discussions can be seen as an essential, if not central, component of
citizens’ political participation, especially from a deliberative perspective

(
Barrett and Brunton-Smith 2014
;
doi:10.1146/annurev.polisci.7.121003.091630
;
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6765.2006.00636.x
)
.

Deliberative democracy has become the most popular conception of democracy in
political theory and research
(
doi:10.1177/0192512120941882
)
. It is a
normative approach, under which basically a multitude of deliberative theories
can be subsumed, each with a different
emphasis
(
Bohman and Rehg 1997
;
cohen1989good
;
dryzek2002deliberative
;
fishkin2008debating
;
gutmann2004deliberative
, e.g.,)
.
However, they agree that democracy should consist of a discursive exchange among
equals with the participation of all members of society. This exchange should be
characterized by rational arguments as well as the willingness to
respectfully acknowledge and understand the viewpoint and arguments of the other
side, to reconsider and, if necessary, to change one’s own viewpoint. The goal
of this exchange is to jointly find solutions to social
problems
(
chambers2003deliberative
;
cohen1989good
;
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8675.1994.tb00001.x
, e.g,)
.

The four fundamental dimensionalities of deliberation are
(
friess2015systematic
)
:

•

Rationality
: argumentative exchange of opinions.

•

Interactivity
: being responsive and listening to each
other.

•

Equality
: same opportunities for anyone.

•

Civility
: respectful interaction between participants.

The application of machine learning methods, especially from the field of
natural language processing (NLP) could help facilitate online discussions
and support the deliberative exchange of opinions.

The goals of this paper are the following:

(1)

We give a comprehensive overview of well-known issues in online
discussions and participation processes.

(2)

We list and describe tasks that could be applied to enhance deliberation in
online discussions.

(3)

We perform an extensive literature review on NLP and other machine learning
techniques that have been used in online discussions.

(4)

We provide an overview of tools and platforms that make use of machine learning.

(5)

We give an outlook on challenges that need to be solved in the future.

Even though there is much ongoing research in this area, we do not consider facilitation approaches of online education and social media platforms. Another branch of research is developing tools to facilitate group meetings, seminars and meetings such as the discussion board system
(
sasaki2021online
)
which is also not part of this work.
Overall, we identified 31 tasks within five sub-categories that could be solved
by NLP to help to enhance deliberation in online discussions.
We found 16 software tools that have been developed or are still being developed that already
incorporate NLP to enhance online
discussions.

Our paper is structured as follows: first we discuss known
issues in both online participation
processes as well as general online discussions in Section
2
. We then give an overview of the tasks that can be solved by machine learning methods to enhance deliberation in online participation in Section

3
. Afterwards we list all software tools that already make use of NLP methods in practice in Section
4
,
before giving a final conclusion and an outlook on future research in
Section
6
.

2.
Issues in Online Discussions

Before discussing tasks that can be solved by NLP to support participants and initiators
of online participation processes and online discussions in general, we want to shed light on challenges in both contexts to better understand how machine learning and NLP can be deployed.
We will first take a closer look at issues that have been reported for online participation processes in the following.

2.1.
Issues in Online Participation Processes

The first challenge that initiators of participation processes have to deal with
are low rates of participation, due to various reasons such as lack of motivation, lack of awareness about the process, or lack of online access
(
shortall2022reason
;
10.1145/3428502.3428614
)
. Promoting public participation projects and ensuring their success is, however, a task that is out of scope for NLP methods, as it takes place before the actual process of participation. Another phenomenon in participation processes, in which citizens can make proposals or share ideas, is the large number of gathered posts. Too many contributions cause what is referred to as
information overload
, which is denoted as one of the key challenges for digital mass participation by
Arana-Catania et al. 2021a
. One effect of information overload is the redundancy of contributions which makes it difficult for participants to find proposals of interest to support or comment
(
10.1145/3428502.3428614
;
ORBi-ca9c331b-d6ec-426d-9db3-4a17bb7ab193
)
.
Being confronted with a multitude of postings on a platform makes it impossible for participants to gain an overview of ideas that have been added and leads to a lack of interaction as they solely share their own ideas without noticing others. As the number of postings grows, it also becomes difficult for local authorities to evaluate the process afterwards
(
ORBi-ca9c331b-d6ec-426d-9db3-4a17bb7ab193
)
.

SIMONOFSKI2021106901
analyzed requirements of both participants and public servants for a participation process
in Belgium and elaborated guidelines for the development and implementation of
participation platforms. One guideline includes the consideration of NLP support
to cluster and summarize submitted ideas.
Balta et al. 2019
examine how the
application of NLP in online citizen participation could be standardized. They
point out the importance of high data quality to obtain adequate predictions. They also state that human based analysis is still preferable for
some tasks as humans perform much better on the explored NLP tasks, while
additionally the decisions of a machine learning method are often not explainable.

The last step of a participation project is its evaluation. This can be a time consuming process, whereas often resources to proper handle
the gathered data are lacking
(
10.1145/3428502.3428614
)
.
The evaluation of participation processes can also be supported through NLP, which is further described in Section

3.5
.

2.2.
Issues in Other Forms of Online Discussions

There are several reports and studies examining issues and drawbacks in online
discussions on social media platforms in general. As these general issues can most likely be transferred to political contexts, we will report them in the following.

The reported issues include disorganized content, redundancy in posts, many contributions of low quality, polarization, and dysfunctional argumentation
(
klein2011harvest
)
.
Other issues are formation of small groups that share the same opinion (which is connected to the emergence of filter bubbles and echo chambers), poor argumentation that leads to
biases and lack of references towards contributions of other participants
(
klein2015critical
;
10.5555/3535850.3536112
;
gao2013designing
)
, and incivility towards other users
(
Anderson et al. 2014
)
.
Many of the mentioned problems in online discussions are related to one or multiple dimensions of deliberation.
The task of human facilitators is to manage discussions and prevent the above-mentioned issues, however, the growing number of participants makes it difficult to keep an overview
(
ito2014collagree
)
. This is where NLP methods come into play, as they could support and simplify the work of human facilitators. Choosing the right tools and facilitation mechanisms is a crucial part of digital online participation
in order to conduct a successful process and rule out possible negative side effects
(
simon2017digital
)
In the following, we give an overview of tasks that can be solved by ML methods to support facilitators, participants, and initiators of online discussions.

3.1.
Generating Input

Probably one of the most direct ways NLP can be used in discussions is to let an
NLP model generate input for the discussion. In the following, we take a look at
NLP tasks that enhance the discussion by generating input in some way. This can
range from simple automatic translations to more complex tasks like automated
moderation through social bots.

Automatic Translation.

Automatic translation of texts holds great potential for online deliberation, as
it allows for more inclusiveness and thereby
equality
of participants, by
breaking down language barriers. If high quality translations can be provided
for any language, more people with different cultural backgrounds can engage in decisions and share their opinion.
With the emergence of
the Transformer model by
NIPS2017_3f5ee243
, automatically generated translations have become fairly reliable and are currently mostly researched for low resource languages.

Anastasiou 2022
for example propose a BERT-based
(
devlin_etal_2019_bert
)
language model for Luxembourgish that will be applied in public administration as a chatbot for automatic translation and question answering.

stahlberg2020neural
provide a recent overview on methods and applications for automatic translation.

Automatic Moderation.

Moderation of online discussions is a crucial task that often determines the
quality of held discussions. It is a time and resource
consuming task, that could be facilitated or even replaced by artificial moderators,
also referred to as social bots.

As an initial approach to support human moderators,
falk-etal-2021-predicting
train
models on the task of moderation intervention prediction on
the Regulation Room Corpus
(
park2012facilitative
)
.
They aim to classify whether a comment needs intervention, which can then be suggested to a human
moderator, whereby the BERT model
(
devlin_etal_2019_bert
)
performs best.
Argyle et al. 2023
use a GPT-3-based
(
Brown et al. 2020
)
chat assistant
in one-on-one discussions about gun control that suggests rephrased messages in
real time. Their study shows that the quality of conversations and the
feeling of being understood by their counterpart could be improved through the
application of the machine learning assistant.

wyss2017artificial
experimentally examine if an artificial facilitator
can improve discursive quality and knowledge gain of participants in
asynchronous political online discussions. The artificial moderators’ main
purpose is to encourage participants to assess counter arguments from other
users. Their experiments show that the artificial moderator increased the
interaction between participants, but it did not increase their gained knowledge
throughout the discussion.
There are many other examples of recent developed chatbots, e.g., to structure the conversation
(
10.1145/3449161
)
, to identify harmful language
(
Clever2022
)
. or to
interact with participants by posting messages
(
ito2022agent
)
.

On the downside, recent studies show that the use of social bots can influence public discussions on social media platforms, such as Twitter
(
doi:10.1177/0894439320908190
)
.
They amplify the opinions and messages of rather unknown accounts making them
appear more trustworthy. Additionally, they also spread misinformation which is
dangerous in political discussions. Therefore one important task for
future participation processes and social media
discussions is the detection of social bots
(
9505695
)
.
Automated moderators are versatile in their application.
Regarding the latest developments of large language models, they might be a great chance for more deliberative online discussions in the future,
nevertheless, their potential for harmful utilization cannot be ignored.

Fact-Checking.

Fact-checking is a crucial task in online discussions, as the spread of misinformation online
is a severe problem
(
rocha2021impact
)
.
Some attempts have been made to create fact-checking pipelines with neural network based models.

guo-etal-2022-survey
give an extensive overview of tasks that need to be solved in the
fact-checking pipeline and datasets used to
train models for these tasks. One important part of fact-checking is claim detection,
i.e., detecting content that should be checked, because it contains some kind of claim. For this purpose, rumor
detection has been employed as, e.g., by
rumor
.
Another important part of fact-checking is evidence
retrieval. For this, so called neural retrievers have emerged recently which are
models that, given some query, retrieve corresponding evidence

(
maillard2021multi
)
. Arguably the most crucial part
of fact-checking is verdict prediction, i.e., labeling the fact checked content. Simple models are binary
classifiers which label a given claim as either true or false

(
potthast2018stylometric
)
. However, it is common to use a more fine-graded labeling, e.g.,
false, barely-true, mostly true
, etc.
(
wang-2017-liar
)
. A last step of the fact-checking pipeline is to generate a justification for the given prediction, as explainability plays an important role when some fact is claimed as false. For this purpose models are trained to produce a textual justification
(
Atanasova et al. 2020
)
.

3.2.
Structuring the Discussion

Other NLP tasks are concerned with leveraging information contained in
discussions and providing a clearer, more structured overview.

Argument Mining.

To automatically recognize and classify argumentative structures in text is the
aim of argument mining.
Boltužić and Šnajder 2014
initially propose the
task of argument recognition and come up with an approach for argument-based opinion mining. In a follow-up work
Boltužić and Šnajder 2015
extend the task of
argument recognition by suggesting an unsupervised method to find the most
prominent argument in an online discussion.
harly2022cnn
use a
convolutional neural network (CNN)-BERT architecture to measure inter-argument
agreement in online discussions.
falk-lapesa-2022-reports
try to recognize
the utilization of personal experience in argumentation.
One sub-task of argument mining is to select a set of keypoint arguments
from a collection of given arguments
(
friedman-etal-2021-overview
;
Alshomary et al. 2021
)
.
By extracting and displaying key arguments of a discussion to participants, they are able
to inform themselves about the discussion, keep an overview and form their own opinion,
which contributes to a more deliberative discussion.

Duplicate Recognition.

Duplicate contributions, i.e., posts in discussions that
share the same idea, but use a different wording,
constitute a significant challenge for participants and initiators of
large-scale online participation processes.

10.1145/1146598.1146663
tackle the task of recognition of near duplicate entries for e-rulemaking and propose an algorithm called DURIAN.

There is not much recent work on identifying duplicate posts in online discussions and participation processes. However, the task of identifying semantically similar texts, which is also known as paraphrase identification in the NLP community, is useful for many areas, reaching from the identification of duplicate questions in online forums
(
9166343
)
to detection of plagiarism
(
10141785
)
.

zhou2022paraphrase
give a recent overview of methods and datasets for paraphrase identification.

Automatically identifying and marking similar posts does not only
facilitate the evaluation of online discussions, but can also be applied
during the discussion to summarize contributions that have the same content.
This would improve clarity and counteract the problem of information overload,
allowing for a more rational discussion.

Dialog Summarization.

Automated summarization of written text, or
more specific, of dialog between multiple people, is another task to facilitate
online discussions, as well as the evaluation of participation
processes. The task is closely related to Argument Mining.
Summarization can provide keypoints for participants that
join an ongoing discussion. For example,
Anastasiou and De Liddo 2021
found that
presenting participants of online discussions a report on given arguments
can improve the ability of users to make sense of the discussion and also the
perceived quality. Summaries are also very useful for municipalities to gain
insights on discussions in online participation in order to take political
measures. To solve the task, large language models can be trained and utilized.
For this purpose,
fabbri-etal-2021-convosumm
present a benchmark dataset
for conversation summarization called ConvoSumm.
1
1

1

https://github.com/Yale-LILY/ConvoSumm

Arana-Catania et al. 2021b
evaluate and compare different
machine learning models to summarize deliberative processes for non-English
languages. They use the Marian machine translation framework

(
junczys-dowmunt-etal-2018-marian
)
to translate a given text to English,
summarize it and translate it back into the initial non-English language.
Out of all evaluated models BART
(
lewis-etal-2020-bart
)
and BERT

(
devlin_etal_2019_bert
)
generated the best results.
For a comprehensive overview on recent methods and challenges of dialog summarization,
see the work of
jia2022taxonomy
. Another sub-task of
text summarization, is the simplification of the used language
(
Al-Thanyyan and Azmi 2021
)
, enabling more people to understand what the text is about.
Simple summarization of debates at the touch of a button could increase the

inclusiveness
of online deliberation and motivate people to participate.

Structure Extraction.

Extracting the structure of online discussions is beneficial for facilitation
tools in order to gain further insight of the ongoing discussion and extract
important parts. The task can be solved by first classifying nodes and
afterwards predicting their relationship.
suzuki2021structure
present
a node classification approach using graph attention networks

(
velivckovic2017graph
)
. In
(
suzuki2020extraction
)
the authors solve
both tasks with a Bi-LSTM network to classify the structure of a discussion.

Topic Modeling.

The automated identification of the discussed topic in text is called topic modeling.

sun2023convntm
present a topic model using deep learning methods that is specified for conversations.
By automatically identifying the topic of a written post, e.g., on citizen platforms that gather ideas for
improving cities, it is easier for participants to
gain an overview and find posts they are interested in.
Furthermore topic modeling could also help to find similar proposals to either merge or propose to other participants.

3.3.
Sentiment Analysis

Sentiment analysis, also referred to as opinion mining, is the automated
identification of a users feelings, opinion and other subjective elements in
text. Sentiment analysis is also often part of the evaluation of participation
processes. We will look at different tasks that analyze the sentiment
of text contributions in the following.

Hate Speech Detection.

Hate speech, toxic language and incivility often occur when citizens with
opposing opinions discuss controversial topics. Civility is one of the main
aspects of deliberation, as participants should feel heard and equally accepted
in a conversation
(
friess2015systematic
)
.
Hate speech detection is the task of automatically classifying a given piece
of text as hateful, if it contains hateful and offensive language.

One popular approach that uses machine learning to recognize toxic language online
is Perspective.
2
2

2

https://perspectiveapi.com/
It is a free to use
application programming interface (API) that uses machine learning to compute a
toxicity score for comments in online discussions.
Hate speech detection has several sub-tasks such as sarcasm
(
Băroiu and Trău˙ 2022
)
, racism
(
9684891
)
and sexism
(
schutz2021automatic
)
detection .
For an overview of recent methods and datasets for textual hate speech detection,
we refer to the work of
Alkomah and Ma 2022
.

Stance Detection.

Stance detection is a sub-task of sentiment analysis which aims to predict the
position of the author of a piece of text with regard to a topic or central
question (e.g. distinguishing between
in favor
and
against
).
It is, i.a., used to identify stance in political online discussions
(
WALKER2012719
)
.
Stance detection is important for a variety of downstream tasks such as
debate summarization and structuring.
doan2022survey
give a recent
in-depth overview on the task of political viewpoint identification.

mascarell-etal-2021-stance
present a manually annotated dataset for
emotion recognition and stance detection in German news articles.

(

)
.

position could be used to structure the debate, giving the opportunity to filter

with opposing views and encourage exchange of opinions, which could improve
the
interactivity
.

3.4.
Discourse Quality Measurement

In order to evaluate tools and systems that aim to improve deliberation in
online context, we need a reliable way to measure the quality of an overall discussion,
or a single comment.
Machine learning models that are able to assess the quality of a contribution
could also be used to highlight posts in discussion that fulfill deliberative
standards, so that others users can follow the shown example
(
10.1145/3484245
)
.

Measuring Deliberative Quality.

DelibAnalysis, developed by
doi:10.1177/0165551519871828
is a framework to
analyze discussion quality based on the discourse quality index (DQI)

(
steenbergen2003measuring
)
.
The DQI is a popular measure for deliberative quality. By now, other measures have been developed for different factors of quality, e.g., the listening quality index
(
scudder2022measuring
)
and the deliberative reasoning index
(
ercan2022research
)
.

fournier2021big
tackle the task of quality assessment with different machine learning
techniques and predict a developed version of the DQI. They point out that for training these models, annotated datasets of high quality are needed. One dataset that includes contributions of a European-leveled poll annotated with the DQI, is EuroPolis
(
gerber2018deliberative
)
.
In their work,
falk-lapesa-2022-scaling
show how to augment data annotated for
deliberative quality prediction in order to avoid class imbalances.

Argument Quality Prediction.

A subtask of measuring the overall deliberative quality of a discussion is to
measure the quality of given arguments. Automatically accessing a score for
given arguments can also be used to support human moderators to create
meaningful summaries.
van-der-meer-etal-2022-will
try different
approaches based on GPT-3
(
Brown et al. 2020
)
and
RoBERTa
(
liu2019roberta
)
to predict validity and novelty of arguments.

3.5.
Evaluation of Participation Processes

In the last phase of an online participation project, municipalities are faced with the
challenge to evaluate the data they collected and transform them into political actions. Evaluation is a time consuming
task where NLP methods can make a noticeable difference. As we mentioned before, many before discussed tasks such as sentiment analysis and text summarization are used for evaluation, which are not explained in detail again. In the following, we look
at previous work on evaluation of participation processes with NLP methods.

One important part of the evaluation is to gain an insight on the proposed ideas and the given arguments of citizens.
liebeck2016airport
automatically extract components of arguments from
a participation process regarding a German airport.
romberg-conrad-2021-citizen

also follow this approach for a mobility related urban planning in Germany.
They examine different machine learning models to recognize argumentative structures
and classify them. They also publish the data as the CIMT argument-mining dataset.
Another annotated dataset for argument mining, topic mining and location detection is the CIMT PartEval Corpus

(
romberg-etal-2022-corpus
)
.
5
5

5

Both datasets available under:
https://github.com/juliaromberg

9458376
use rule-based sentiment analysis with lexica to calculate a
sentiment score (either positive, neutral, or negative) for civic participation
comments. Their work builds the core functionality for the Civic CrowdAnalytics
tool.
Other tools for the automated evaluation of participation processes are described
in Section
4.3
.

For a recent overview on NLP methods for the evaluation of public participation projects,

.

4.
Overview of NLP in Practice

In the following section we want to take a closer look on platforms that integrate a facilitation
mechanism in form of NLP or more general machine learning.

4.1.
Discussion Platform Software

There is a wide range of different online discussion and participation platforms
that include some kind of algorithmic facilitation, both open source and
proprietary. In the following, we solely regard open source platforms.
A list of all mentioned platforms with their link, if available, can be found in Table
4

in the
Appendix
.

bcause

is a platform for accessible, structured and decentralized online
deliberation, still being developed by
Anastasiou and De Libbo 2023
.
It includes automatic structuring of arguments and a tool for visual analytics. The research project is ongoing with the aim to build an discussion environment with an integrated analytic engine to allow for more qualitative online debates.
A conducted experiment by the research group showed that automated created reports can help users to make sense of an ongoing discussion and also improves the overall
debate quality
(
Anastasiou and De Liddo 2021
)
.

CitizenLab

is a citizen participation platform, developed by an eponymous company, founded in Brussels. The software is used worldwide for citizen participation processes and polls. The platform includes an algorithm that is able to automatically analyze proposals of citizens to extract keywords and tag them. Additionally, NLP methods are used to determine which posts have the same topic and can be summarized. This helps participants to find topics of interest and decrease information overload on the platform.

COLLAGREE

(short for collective or collaborative
agreement) is an online discussion platform for consensus decision-making
support, developed by
ito2014collagree
;
ito2015incentive
. COLLAGREE implements
functions to support facilitators that lead the discussions. These functions
include a text-based sentiment analysis and an automated prediction of
agreement/ disagreement for each post, as well as the automatic generation of highlighted keywords.
ito2014collagree

also added an incentive mechanism that rewards posts of participants based on their
effectiveness. This should lead to a better quality of the discussion.

ConsiderIt

is a participation platform developed by

10.1145/1979742.1979869

(
10.1145/2145204.2145249
, see also)
. The
goal of the authors is to integrate mechanisms to encourage deliberative
behavior in online discussions. ConsiderIt includes features like creating a
personal pro and contra list in which arguments offered by others can be
integrated, stance sliders to indicate a participant’s level of support and a ranking over given pro and contra
arguments. The aim is to prevent long discussions, enhancing short
argumentative points of each participant and the possibility to prioritize and visualize given
arguments. The main goal of the ConsiderIt platform is to confront participants of
online discussions with other perspectives, moving them to consider (hence the
name) other viewpoints.

CONSUL

is another citizen participation tool for debates, proposals, budgeting and polls.
Arana-Catania et al. 2021a
develop and evaluate
different NLP tools for the platform
to counteract the problem of information overload that is often
observed in digital mass participation. In their project, the authors mainly
focus on tackling information overload with regard to proposals that can be added
to the platform by citizens. The modules integrated in Consul should categorize proposals, suggest relevant proposals for other
users so that they can support them, group citizens by their interests to
encourage the exchange among citizens and summarize the comments that were
added to one proposal.
davies2021evaluating
use the developed
NLP methods in Consul to facilitate participatory budgeting processes in
Scotland.

D-Agree

by
ito2022agent
is a large-scale discussion support system which includes an agent that
automatically extracts information from posted texts to generate and post
facilitation messages within the discussion. The messages are based on
facilitation rules (approximately 200) that have been developed by human
facilitators. Messages for example include questions like
"What is
necessary for the action?"
or
“What are the merits of this idea?”
to
make progress in the discussion and encourage new ideas. The agent is also able to
filter posts that include offensive language. They use argument mining to
identify argument components with a bidirectional LSTM

(
10.1162/COLI_a_00295
)
. Their experiments

(
Ito_Suzuki_Yamaguchi_Nishida_Hiraishi_Yoshino_2020
)
indicate that users
have the best experience during a large-scale discussion when the automated
agent is implemented in cooperation with human facilitators.

Discourse

Discourse is a community platform including features for civilized online discussions. The platform has several AI modules build in, e.g., automatic topic recommendation based on semantic similarity, an assistant to write a new topic that proofreads the users’ text, suggests titles and translates it to English, a toxicity detection module and a summarization tool that summarizes discussed topics. More features supported through AI are currently planned.

Polis

(

idea gathering and analysis supported by machine learning, developed by the Computational
Democracy Project.

This leads to a sparse comment matrix. After the conversation, the data matrix

RHETORiC

(an acronym for Reducing Hate through Editorial Tools for Online Reactions and
Comments) by
Bossens et al. 2022
is a
discussion tool that supports civil participation in news comment sections. At
the moment there is only a prototype of the tool that might be released in the
future. The main features of RHETORiC are: real-time feedback on the quality of
the written comment, proposal of similar arguments, the possibility to like
other comments and a clustered view of all comments based on their similarity.
Comments are also automatically classified and sent to pre-moderation, if they
appear to be uncivil. Experiments showed that the made design choices had an
impact on the comment quality and the overall engagement. However, participants
did not always agree with the decisions of the machine learning algorithm. Thus
the authors plead to further develop and utilize explainable machine learning algorithms that
are more transparent in their decisions.

Your Priorities

is an online platform for idea generation, deliberation and

recommendation algorithm that suggests posts that most likely are interesting to a user. It also implements a fraud detection system and an analysis tool to cluster ideas.

4.2.
Visualization Software

Another approach to gather insights on political debates is to visualize them,
in order to navigate through conversations and explore different aspects
visually. In the following we describe tools that analyze conversation through visualization.

Opinion Space

(
10.1145/1753326.1753502
)
is another tool that enables users to collect and visualize opinions online. Comments are depicted as dots on an interactive map, with nearby dots representing similar opinions and distant dots representing dissimilar opinions. The interface of Opinion Space should counteract problems of typical list based discussion forums, in which users tend to lose the overview.

ThreadReconstructor

presented by
el2018threadreconstructor
, is a tool
to reconstruct threads and untangle conversations in large online discussions.
The tool is specialized on transcripts of discussions in online forums, where no threaded reply structure is given.

VisArgue

is a visualization toolbox to further analyze and visualize political debates,
by
ElAssady2016VisAr-37650
.
It combines different tools, e.g., the lexical episode plots to cluster and
visualize the most significant terms of a conversation and ConToVi for an
analysis of the specific patterns of participants.

Civic CrowdAnalytics

CommunityPulse

is an ongoing research project to
explore comments from community participation processes and visualize them

(
10.1145/3461778.3462132
)
. The conduction of expert interviews revealed

Based on these findings, CommunityPulse offers three different views. One for visualizing the discussion, the topics and the sentiment of the participants on a high level. A view to analyze comments in more detail and a view to directly compare contributions.

5.
Key Challenges & Open Problems

So far we mainly discussed chances in the application of machine learning in online discussions
and participation processes. Still there are some key challenges and risks that need to be addressed.

Lacking Accuracy.

How well do machine learning models perform?
The amount of tasks that can be solved by machine learning systems in order to
support municipalities and policy makers is growing. However, methods still need to
be improved in order to generate meaningful insights, e.g., in participation
process data for evaluation purposes
(
Aitamurto et al. 2016
)
. For this purpose annotated datasets of high quality are necessary.

Induced Biases.

Automated processes that are used in social
contexts always pose risks. Especially if tracing
transparency, explainability, plausibility, reliability or legitimacy of the
applications is not possible.
strauss2021deep
points out that it is necessary
to keep potential biases in mind when developing methods and also when using
them in practice. Precisely in the field of politics and online discussions
where data to train models is taken from real conversations, it is likely that
the data and subsequently also the models that learn from the data contain
political biases, leading to unethical, unfair or even discriminating decisions
(
10.1145/3457607
)
.

Manipulation.

The use of machine learning methods in environments with social
impact such as political participation always has to be well considered. It
has been shown that algorithms can lead to increased polarization,
selective exposure
(
dylko2017dark
)
and influenced views. Machine learning can
help us make sense of big amounts of data and can be a supportive tool,
but it should also be used in a transparent and ethical way.

Transparency.

We have seen a variety of tasks that can be
solved or at least supported through machine learning methods. Nevertheless, if it is not
clear for the participants how methods are used, what they do in the process
and how they make decisions, it can have an impact on the process and
lead to trust issues
(
ORBi-ca9c331b-d6ec-426d-9db3-4a17bb7ab193
)
. Explainable machine learning is an own branch of research that is
studied by many researchers, yet it is not common to use machine learning methods that are
transparent to those who use it.

Lack of Data.

In order to train models that support
participation processes, up-to-date data on current issues and of high quality is necessary.
Communes would profit from sharing their newest data, but data protection
policies do not always allow free sharing of discussion data.

Evaluation.

There are many approaches from the field of
machine learning to solve potential issues in online discussions and online
participation processes. However, not many have been used and examined in
practice, yet. It is still an open question whether the application of NLP methods can demonstrably contribute
to improve online discussions and if they are perceived as helpful by
participants. Controlled experiments could help to systematically evaluate the
use of machine learning in online discussions and provide a better understanding on how these
tools could be used in a supporting way
(
HELBING2023102061
)
.

6.
Conclusion

Civic participation is an important instrument to democracy to collectively solve problems of public concern, both on a local as well as on a global level. Today, more and more communities and municipalities make use of digital tools to include citizens in decision making.
While the digital space holds many opportunities, as it is neither location nor time-bound and enables humans from all over the world to contribute to important decisions, it also implies issues. It is important to
never lose track of potential risks. Unfortunately, the use of algorithms is always twofold, as it holds
potential for manipulation and abuse.

With our work we hope to showcase the importance of deliberative elements in
online discussions, especially in political contexts. If more platforms
implement designs that enhance respectful, inclusive conversations online,
citizen councils and other democratic instruments hold great potential
to come to thoughtful decisions and collectively fight crises that affect all
people. A platform where design choices are gathered and scientifically evaluated
is the Prosocial Design Network
10
10

10

https://www.prosocialdesign.org/
,
which is continuously developed further.

Machine learning has the potential to noticeably
facilitate collective decision making, but it should always be seen as a
supporting tool and not replace the work of humans. Nevertheless, it will be part of our lives as it is
already today. Researchers should further investigate the use of
machine learning, but also study side effects and risks that accompany it.

Gehring-Hayman inequality for meromorphic univalent mappings Thanks: The second author of this article would like to thank the Prime Minister Research Fellowship of the Government of India (Grant ID: 2403440) for its financial support.
Bappaditya Bhowmik ∗ {}^{~\mathbf{*}}, Deblina Maity, Toshiyuki Sugawa

Abstract
. Let f f be a meromorphic univalent function on the open unit disk having a simple pole at p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) that extends continuously to the left half 𝕋 − {\mathbb{T}}^{-} of the unit circle. In this article, we prove that the ratio of the length of the image of the vertical diameter 𝕀 1 {{\mathbb{I}}_{1}} of the unit disk to the length of the image of 𝕋 − {\mathbb{T}}^{-} under the mapping f f is bounded by a constant depending only on p . p. Next, we extend this result by considering any hyperbolic geodesic and any Jordan curve in 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} sharing the same endpoints. These results extend the classical Gehring-Hayman inequality to meromorphic univalent functions and also prove a conjecture posed by Bhowmik and Maity [Bull. Sci. Math. 199 (2025), # 103583].

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{D}},{\mathbb{H}}
and
{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
stand for the complex plane, the open unit disk

\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\zeta|<1\},
the upper half-plane
\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\,\operatorname{Im}\,}z>0\}

and the Riemann sphere
{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\},
respectively, throughout this paper.
Also, let
{\mathbb{T}}^{-}:=\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}:~|\zeta|=1,~{\,\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta<0\}
denote the left half
of the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}=\partial{\mathbb{D}}
and
{{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}
the diameter
(-i,i)
of the unit circle with endpoints
i,-i
.
In other words,

{\mathbb{T}}^{-}=\{-e^{i\theta}:-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\}{\quad\text{and}\quad}{{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}=\{iv:-1<v<1\}.

We denote by
\ell(\gamma)
the Euclidean length of a curve
\gamma
in
{\mathbb{C}}.

Therefore, if
\gamma:(a,b)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a piecewise
C^{1}
curve, then

\ell(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|dt.

In this paper, a conformal map
f
of a domain
D
means a holomorphic and
injective map of
D
into
{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}.

A theorem of Gehring and Hayman
[
4
]
can be stated in the following form.
(Note that the original form deals with functions on the upper half-plane instead of the unit disk.)

Theorem A
(Gehring-Hayman
[
4
, Theorem 1]
).

Let
f
be a conformal map of
{\mathbb{D}}
into
{\mathbb{C}}

that remains continuous up to
{\mathbb{T}}^{-}.

Then the inequality

\ell(f({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))\leq A_{1}\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))

holds, where
A_{1}
is an absolute constant with
\pi\leq A_{1}<74
.

Later, Jaenisch
[
6
]
improved the range of
A_{1}
to
4.56\leq A_{1}\leq 17.45
.

The second theorem of Gehring and Hayman
[
4
]
can also be stated as follows.

Theorem B

[
4
, Theorem 2]
.

Let
f
be a conformal mapping of
{\mathbb{D}}
into
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Suppose that
J
is a Jordan arc joining two points
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
in
{\mathbb{D}}.

Then

\ell(f(S))\leq A_{1}\ell(f(J)),

where
S
is the hyperbolic geodesic joining
\zeta_{1}
to
\zeta_{2}
in
{\mathbb{D}}

and
A_{1}
is the constant appearing in Theorem A.

We refer to the articles
[
2
,
5
]
for various applications of Theorem B.
In the paper
[
3
]
of the first and the second authors,
we tried to extend Theorems A and B to
meromorphic univalent mappings in
{\mathbb{D}}.

To formulate our results, we define quantities
A_{p}
for
0<p<1.

We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
the class of
those meromorphic univalent functions in
{\mathbb{D}}
with a simple pole at
z=p\in(0,1)

that extend continuously to the left half
{\mathbb{T}}^{-}
of the unit circle.
We consider the problem to look for a constant
A_{p}
such that

(1.1)

\ell(f({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))\leq A_{p}\;\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))

for all functions
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}.

We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0}
the subset of
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
consisting of
f
with
\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))<+\infty.

Then the above condition remains unchanged if we replace
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
by
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0}.

In what follows, let
A_{p}
be the best possible constant in (
1.1
).
That is,

A_{p}=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0}}\frac{\ell(f({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))}{\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))}.

In
[
3
, Theorem 1]
, it was shown indeed that
A_{p}<+\infty
for
\sqrt{2}-1<p<1.

More precisely, the result is stated as follows.

Theorem C
(Bhowmik and Maity
[
3
, Theorem 1]
).

Let
p\in(\sqrt{2}-1,1)
. Then

(1.2)

\frac{(1+p)^{2}\pi}{4p}\leq A_{p}\leq\displaystyle{\min_{q\in(1,\infty)}R_{p}(q)},

where

R_{p}(q)=\frac{(1+p^{2})\log q}{2p}\cot^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\left(\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)-\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{(1-p^{2})(q-1)}{2p(q+1)}\right)\right)\right).

Note that
(1-p^{2})/2p\geq 1
when
0<p\leq\sqrt{2}-1.

In
[
3
, p. 13]
, it was conjectured that
A_{p}
is finite for all
p\in(0,1).

In view of the proof of Theorem C, we can even extend the class
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
to the class

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
of analytic univalent functions on the domain
{\mathbb{D}}\setminus{\overline{\Delta}_{p}}

which extends to
{\mathbb{T}}^{-}
continuously,
where
{\overline{\Delta}_{p}}=\{\zeta:|\zeta-\alpha^{-1}|\leq\sqrt{\alpha^{-2}-1}\}

and
\alpha=2p/(1+p^{2}).
Note that
p\in\partial{\overline{\Delta}_{p}}
(see the next section) and thus
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
.
We consider the quantity

\tilde{A}_{p}=\sup_{f\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0}}\frac{\ell(f({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))}{\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))},

where
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0}
denotes the subclass of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
consisting of functions

f
such that
\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))<+\infty.

Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{0},
we have
A_{p}\leq\tilde{A}_{p}
for
0<p<1.

In this article, we improve the above bound of
A_{p}
and prove the above conjecture.
We indeed show the following.

Theorem 1
.

The following inequalities hold for each
p\in(0,1):

(1.3)

\frac{(1+p)^{2}\pi}{4p}\leq A_{p}\leq\tilde{A}_{p}<\frac{1+p^{2}}{p}\left(1+\sqrt{2}+\frac{20}{3p}\right)^{2}\log 2.

Actually, we will give a better but much more complicated bound in Theorem
2
below.
We mention here that the proof of Theorem
2
will be accomplished by employing
a similar technique adopted by Gehring and Hayman in
[
4
, Theorem 1]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 is devoted to several lemmas,
which will be used to prove our main results.
In Section 3, Theorem
2
will be presented together with its proof.
Then Theorem
1
will be derived from it.
Finally, as an application of the proof of Theorem
2
,
we will extend Theorem B to the meromorphic case (see Theorem
3
).

2.
Preliminary results

We denote by
\lambda_{D}(w)|dw|
the hyperbolic metric of a domain
D
in
{\mathbb{C}}
with constant
Gaussian curvature
-4.

The induced hyperbolic distance on
D
will be denoted by
d_{D}(w_{1},w_{2}).

We recall that the hyperbolic distance in the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
is given by

d_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta,\omega)={\operatorname{arth}\,}\left|\frac{\zeta-\omega}{1-\zeta\bar{\omega}}\right|,~~\zeta,\omega\in{\mathbb{D}},

where
{\operatorname{arth}\,}x=({1/2})\log(({1+x})/({1-x}))
for
0\leq x<1.

Let
f
be a meromorphic univalent mapping of
{\mathbb{D}}
with a simple pole at
p\in(0,1)
.
Choose
\alpha\in(0,1)
so that
p
is the hyperbolic midpoint of
0
and
\alpha.

In other words,

(2.1)

p=\frac{\alpha}{1+\sqrt{1-{\alpha}^{2}}}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-{\alpha}^{2}}}{\alpha}~\text{ i.e. }~\alpha=\frac{2p}{1+p^{2}}.

Let
\gamma
be the hyperbolic geodesic in
{\mathbb{D}}
which is symmetric about the real axis and passes through the point

p
.
Since hyperbolic geodesics of
{\mathbb{D}}
are circular arcs in
{\mathbb{D}}
perpendicular to
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
, we obtain

(2.2)

\gamma=\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{D}}:|\zeta-\alpha^{-1}|=\sqrt{\alpha^{-2}-1}\}.

The two endpoints of
\gamma
on
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
are given by
\alpha\pm i\sqrt{1-{\alpha}^{2}}
.
Let
\Omega
be the connected component of
{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\gamma
containing the origin.
In other words,
\Omega={\mathbb{D}}\setminus{\overline{\Delta}_{p}},
where

{\overline{\Delta}_{p}}=\{\zeta:|\zeta-\alpha^{-1}|\leq\sqrt{\alpha^{-2}-1}\}
as is given in Section 1.

The function

\phi_{\alpha}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta(1-\alpha\zeta)}{\zeta-\alpha}

maps
\Omega
conformally onto
{\mathbb{D}}
and satisfies
\phi_{\alpha}(0)=0,\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)=-\alpha^{-1}

(
cf.

[
7
, p. 85]
).
Then the Möbius transformation

(2.3)

\displaystyle g(\zeta)=i\,\frac{1+i\zeta}{1-i\zeta}

maps
{\mathbb{D}}
conformally onto the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
with
g(0)=i
and

(2.4)

g(p)=-\alpha+i\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}.

Note that
g
is involutive; that is,
z=g(\zeta)
if and only if
\zeta=g(z).

In particular,
g({\mathbb{H}})={\mathbb{D}}.

Also, we see

\displaystyle g({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}})

\displaystyle=\{iy:y>0\}=:{\mathbb{I}^{+}},

\displaystyle g({\mathbb{T}}^{-})

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:x>0\}=:{{\mathbb{R}}}^{+}.

Observe that the above curve
\gamma
is mapped by
g
onto the semi-circle

\sigma=\{z\in{\mathbb{H}}:|z+\alpha^{-1}|=\sqrt{\alpha^{-2}-1}\}.

We note that
\sigma
is a hyperbolic geodesic in
\mathbb{H}
with endpoints at

-p
and
-1/p.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be the hyperbolic half-plane
g(\Omega)
in
{\mathbb{H}}.

That is,
\Omega_{1}
is the connected component of

{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\sigma
containing
i.

We now recall the concept of harmonic measure.
Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
, and let
E
be a Borel subset of
the boundary
\partial{D}
of
D
.
The harmonic measure of
E
on
D
is defined
as the solution
u
of the generalized Dirichlet problem in
D
with boundary data
1

on
E
and
0
on
\partial{D}\setminus E
(see
[
1
, Ch. 3]
)
and its value
u(z)
is denoted by
\omega(z,E,D)
for
z\in D.

Indeed, the set function
E\to\omega(z,E,D)
can be regarded as a Borel probability
measure on
\partial D
for each
z\in D.

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be two domains in
{\mathbb{C}}.

If
D_{1}\subset D_{2}
and a Borel set
E
is contained in

\partial D_{1}\cap\partial D_{2}
, then
\omega(z,E,D_{1})\leq\omega(z,E,D_{2})

for
z\in D_{1}
.
This property is known as Carleman’s principle of domain extension
for harmonic measure (see
[
9
, p. 86]
).

For a proper subdomain
D
of
{\mathbb{C}},
we will denote by
\delta_{D}(z)
the Euclidean distance
from a point
z\in D
to the boundary
\partial D.

We denote by
B(a,r)
the closed disk
|z-a|\leq r
in
{\mathbb{C}}

centered at
a
with radius
r>0.

The proof of our main results will be based on the following estimate provided by
Gehring and Hayman
[
4
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma A.

Let
D
be a simply connected proper subdomain of
{\mathbb{C}}

and suppose that
d>0
and
w_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus D
are given.
For a Borel set
\beta
contained in
\partial D\cap B(w_{0},d)
the
inequality

\delta_{D}(w)\leq d\,\cot^{2}({\pi}\omega(w,\beta,D)/{4})

holds for every
w\in D.

Next, we wish to establish an all-important lemma that will serve as
a key ingredient in proving Theorem
1
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\beta=[a,b]
and
\beta^{\prime}=[ia,ib]

for positive numbers
a,b
with
a<b.

Then

(2.5)

\frac{{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}M_{p}(b/a)}{\pi}\leq\omega(z,\beta,\Omega_{1})\leq\,\frac{1}{2},\quad z\in\beta^{\prime},

where
\Omega_{1}
is the hyperbolic half-plane defined above and

(2.6)

M_{p}(q)=\frac{q+1}{q-1}+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(1+q^{2})}{2p(q-1)(4p\sqrt{q}+(1+q)(1+p^{2}))}.

Proof.
Since
\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{H}
and

\beta\subset\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial{\mathbb{H}},

Carleman’s principle of domain extension for harmonic measure implies

\omega(z,\beta,\Omega_{1})\leq\omega(z,\beta,\mathbb{H})=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg\frac{b-z}{a-z}

for each
z\in\Omega_{1}.

Note that
\omega(z,\beta,\mathbb{H})
is
{1}/{\pi}
times the angle
subtended by the segment
\beta
from the point
z.

In particular, the set
\{z\in{\mathbb{H}}:\omega(z,\beta,{\mathbb{H}})>1/2\}
is equal to
the open upper half-disk with diameter
\beta=[a,b].

Since
\beta^{\prime}
is away from this half-disk, we have

\omega(z,\beta,\mathbb{H})\leq 1/2
for
z\in\beta^{\prime}
.
We now observe that the function

\psi(z)=\left(\frac{z+p}{z+1/p}\right)^{2}

maps
\Omega_{1}
conformally onto
\mathbb{H}.

Let
\tilde{\beta}=\psi(\beta)
and
\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}=\psi(\beta^{\prime}).

Then the conformal invariance of harmonic measures implies

\omega(z,\beta,\Omega_{1})=\omega(\psi(z),\tilde{\beta},\mathbb{H}),\quad z\in\Omega_{1}.

Therefore, it suffices to show the inequality
\pi\omega(\zeta,\tilde{\beta},{\mathbb{H}})\geq{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}M_{p}(b/a)
on
\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}
for the lower estimate in (
2.5
).
To this end, we fix a point
\zeta
in
\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}
and
put
\theta=\pi\omega(\zeta,\tilde{\beta},{\mathbb{H}}).

Then the required inequality turns to the form
\cot\theta\leq M_{p}(b/a).

First, observe that the level curve

\Gamma_{0}=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:\omega(z,\tilde{\beta},\mathbb{H})={\theta}/{\pi}\}

is an open subarc of the circle
\Gamma
passing through the points

A=\psi(a),B=\psi(b)
and
\zeta.

Let
C=M+ih
be the center of
\Gamma
as shown in Figure
1
.
Note that
h>0
because
\theta
is an acute angle.

The level curve
\Gamma_{0}
and the triangle
\Delta CAM

Now the inscribed angle theorem implies that
the line segment
\tilde{\beta}=[A,B]
subtends the angle
2\theta
at the center
C
.
Thus,

(2.7)

M=\frac{A+B}{2}{\quad\text{and}\quad}\tan\theta=\frac{B-A}{2h}.

We now compute the radius
\rho
of
\Gamma.

To this end, we put
\delta=(B-A)/2

(see Figure
1
) and compute

h^{2}=\rho^{2}-\delta^{2}=|\zeta-(M+ih)|^{2}-\delta^{2}=|\zeta-M|^{2}-2h{\,\operatorname{Im}\,}(\zeta-M)+h^{2}-\delta^{2}.

By this relation, we find the value of
h
as follows:

h=\frac{|\zeta-M|^{2}-\delta^{2}}{2{\,\operatorname{Im}\,}(\zeta-M)}=\frac{|\zeta|^{2}-2\,M{\,\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta+AB}{2{\,\operatorname{Im}\,}\zeta}.

Since (
2.7
) implies
\tan\theta=\delta/h,
we now obtain

(2.8)

\cot\theta=\frac{|\zeta|^{2}-2\,M{\,\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta+AB}{2\,\delta{\,\operatorname{Im}\,}\zeta}.

Here, a straightforward computation yields

2M=\psi(a)+\psi(b)=\frac{p^{2}\Theta}{(1+ap)^{2}(1+bp)^{2}},

where

\Theta=(a^{2}+b^{2})(1+p^{4})+2(a+b)(1+ab)p(1+p^{2})+2p^{2}(1+4ab+a^{2}b^{2}),

2\delta=\frac{(b-a)p^{2}(1-p^{2})\big((a+b)(1+p^{2})+2p(1+ab)\big)}{(1+ap)^{2}(1+bp)^{2}},

and

AB=\frac{(a+p)^{2}(b+p)^{2}p^{4}}{(1+ap)^{2}(1+bp)^{2}}.

Since
\zeta\in\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}=\psi(\beta^{\prime}),
we can write
\zeta=\psi(iy)

for some
y\in[a,b]
so that

\zeta=\frac{p^{2}(p^{2}+4p^{2}y^{2}+p^{2}y^{4}-y^{2}-p^{4}y^{2})+2p^{3}(1-p^{2})y(1+y^{2})\,i}{(1+p^{2}y^{2})^{2}}

and

|\zeta|=\frac{p^{2}(p^{2}+y^{2})}{1+p^{2}y^{2}}.

We substitute them into (
2.8
), and after some simplification,
we obtain

\frac{(b-a)\cot\theta}{a}=\frac{b}{y}+\frac{y}{a}+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(a^{2}+y^{2})(b^{2}+y^{2})}{2ap\left(2p(1+ab)+(a+b)(1+p^{2})\right)y(1+y^{2})}.

For convenience, we introduce two additional variables

q=\frac{b}{a},\quad T=\frac{y}{a}\in[1,q]

and compute

(q-1)\cot\theta=\frac{(q+T^{2})}{T}+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(1+T^{2})(q^{2}+T^{2})}{2pQ},

where

Q=T({1}/{a}+aT^{2})(2p({1}/{a}+aq)+(1+q)(1+p^{2})).

Here we recall the elementary inequality

x+y\geq 2\sqrt{xy}
for
x\geq 0
and
y\geq 0
,
which will be used repeatedly below.
Then we get the lower estimate

Q\geq 2T^{2}(4p\sqrt{q}+(1+q)(1+p^{2}))

and hence

\displaystyle(q-1)\cot\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q+T^{2}}{T}+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(1+T^{2})(q^{2}+T^{2})}{4pT^{2}(4p\sqrt{q}+(1+q)(1+p^{2}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{T}+T+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(q^{2}/T^{2}+1+q^{2}+T^{2})}{4p(4p\sqrt{q}+(1+q)(1+p^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq 1+q+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(1+q^{2})}{2p(4p\sqrt{q}+(1+q)(1+p^{2}))}

for
1\leq T\leq q.

In the last part of the above estimations, we used the fact that the function

c/x+x
is convex for a constant
c>0

and thus takes its maximum at one of the endpoints on the
closed interval
[x_{0},x_{1}].

Now the proof is complete.

We end this section by giving an elementary lemma, which will be utilized
in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
k
be a constant with
0<k<1.

Then the function
G(x)=\cot(k{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}x)
is strictly convex and increasing
on
0<x<+\infty.

Moreover,
G(0^{+})=\cot(k\pi/2),G^{\prime}(0^{+})=k/\sin^{2}(k\pi/2)
and the following
inequalities hold:

\cot\frac{k\pi}{2}+\frac{kx}{\sin^{2}(k\pi/2)}<G(x)<\cot\frac{k\pi}{2}+\frac{x}{k},\quad 0<x<+\infty.

Proof.
First we compute
G(0^{+})=\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}G(x)=\cot(k\pi/2)
because

{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}(0^{+})=\pi/2.

Next we have

G^{\prime}(x)=\frac{k}{(1+x^{2})\sin^{2}(k{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}x)}>0,

which implies that
G(x)
is (strictly) increasing and gives the value of

G^{\prime}(0^{+})
in the assertion.
Since
\tan\theta
is strictly convex on
0<\theta<\pi/2
and satisfies
\tan 0=0,

the inequality
\tan(k\theta)<k\tan\theta;
equivalently,

\cot\theta<k\cot(k\theta),
holds for
0<\theta<\pi/2.

Letting
x=\cot\theta,
we obtain
x<k\cot(k{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}x)
for
0<x<+\infty.

Hence, we observe that

G^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{2k(k\cot(k{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}x)-x)}{(1+x^{2})^{2}\sin^{2}(k{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}x)}>0

for
0<x<+\infty.

Thus we have checked strict convexity of
G.

We next find the asymptotic value by using the L’Hôpital’s rule as follows:

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{G(x)}{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}G^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{k}{(1+x^{2})\sin^{2}(k{\,\operatorname{arccot}\,}x)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to 0^{+}}\frac{k}{(1+\cot^{2}\theta)\sin^{2}(k\theta)}=\lim_{\theta\to 0^{+}}\frac{k\sin^{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}(k\theta)}=\frac{1}{k}.

The strict convexity of
G
yields the inequalities

G(0^{+})+G^{\prime}(0^{+})(x-0)<G(x)<G(0^{+})+\frac{x(G(x_{1})-G(0^{+}))}{x_{1}-0}

for
0<x<x_{1}<+\infty.

In particular, the left-hand inequality follows.
We now let
x_{1}\to+\infty
to obtain the right-hand inequality.
∎

3.
Proof of main results

We now state and prove a technical result which leads to our main result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p\in(0,1).
Then

(3.1)

\frac{(1+p)^{2}\pi}{4p}\leq A_{p}\leq\tilde{A}_{p}\leq\displaystyle{\inf_{q\in(1,\infty)}N_{p}(q)},

where

N_{p}(q)=\frac{(1+p^{2})\log q}{2p}\cot^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\cot^{-1}\left(\frac{q+1}{q-1}+\frac{(1-p^{2})^{2}(1+q^{2})}{2p(q-1)(4p\sqrt{q}+(1+q)(1+p^{2}))}\right)\right).

Proof.
The proof of this theorem is similar to that of
[
3
, Theorem 1]
.
But, for the sake of completeness, we will repeat all the necessary details.
Let
f\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
for a given number
0<p<1.

In other words,
f
is a univalent analytic function on
{\mathbb{D}}\setminus{\overline{\Delta}_{p}}

which is continuous up to the (open) left half
{\mathbb{T}}^{-}
of the unit circle.
Let
g:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{H}}
be the map defined in (
2.3
) and let
\alpha
be the number
defined in (
2.1
).
Then
h=f\circ g^{-1}
is analytic and univalent on
\Omega_{1}=g(\Omega)

and remains continuous on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{+}
.
In particular, the image
D=h(\Omega_{1})=f(\Omega)
is a simply connected domain in
{\mathbb{C}}.

Choose
q\in(1,\infty)
arbitrarily and fix it.
Let
\beta_{n}=[q^{n},q^{n+1}]
and
\beta_{n}^{\prime}=[iq^{n},iq^{n+1}]
for each integer
n.

Let
\ell_{n}
and
\ell^{\prime}_{n}
denote the lengths of the images of these segments
under the mapping
h
.
Then Lemma
1
yields the inequalities

(3.2)

\frac{\xi}{\pi}\leq\omega(h^{-1}(w),\beta_{n},\Omega_{1})=\omega(w,h(\beta_{n}),D)\leq 1/2

at each point
w\in h(\beta^{\prime}_{n})
, where
\xi=\cot^{-1}(M_{p}(q))

and
M_{p}(q)
is given in (
2.6
).
Let
w_{0}
be the point on
h(\beta_{n})
that divides its arclength in half.
Then
h(\beta_{n})
lies in the disk
|w-w_{0}|\leq\ell_{n}/2
, and thus
Lemma A together with (
3.2
) implies

(3.3)

\delta_{D}(w)\leq\frac{\ell_{n}}{2}\cot^{2}(\pi\omega(w,h(\beta_{n}),D)/4)\leq\frac{\ell_{n}}{2}\cot^{2}(\xi/4)

for
w\in h(\beta^{\prime}_{n}).

We now recall that
\phi_{\alpha}
maps
\Omega
conformally onto the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}

and that
g=g^{-1}
maps
\Omega
onto
\Omega_{1}.

Thus
\phi_{\alpha}\circ g:\Omega_{1}\to{\mathbb{D}}
is conformal.
Since
h:\Omega_{1}\to D
is conformal, too, we have the relations

\displaystyle\lambda_{D}(h(z))|h^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\Omega_{1}}(z)=\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(\phi_{\alpha}(g(z)))|(\phi_{\alpha}\circ g)^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle=\frac{2|\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(w)|}{|z+i|^{2}(1-|\phi_{\alpha}(w)|^{2})},\quad z\in\Omega_{1},

where we put
w=g(z).

By the well-known estimate
1/\lambda_{D}\leq 4\delta_{D}
on
D,
we obtain

\delta_{D}(h(z))\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda_{D}(h(z))}=\frac{|h^{\prime}(z)|(1-|\phi_{\alpha}(w)|^{2})|z+i|^{2}}{8|\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(w)|},\quad w=g(z)\in\Omega.

We now take
z=iy\in{\mathbb{I}^{+}}
and compute
w=g(iy)=i(1-y)/(1+y)=iv,
where
v\in(-1,1).

Hence, in conjunction with (
3.3
), we get

|h^{\prime}(iy)|\leq\frac{\ell_{n}}{2}\cot^{2}(\xi/4)\frac{8|\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(iv)|}{(1-|\phi_{\alpha}(iv)|^{2})(y+1)^{2}}.

Noting
dv/dy=-2/(y+1)^{2},
we estimate

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\beta^{\prime}_{n}}|h^{\prime}(z)||dz|=\int_{q^{n}}^{q^{n+1}}|h^{\prime}(iy)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell_{n}}{2}\cot^{2}(\xi/4)\int_{q^{n}}^{q^{n+1}}\frac{8|\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(iv)|}{(1-|\phi_{\alpha}(iv)|^{2})(y+1)^{2}}dy

\displaystyle=2\ell_{n}\cot^{2}(\xi/4)\int^{v_{n}}_{v_{n+1}}\frac{|\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(iv)|}{1-|\phi_{\alpha}(iv)|^{2}}dv,

where we put
v_{n}=(1-q^{n})/(1+q^{n})
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Using the expression of
\phi_{\alpha},
we have the inequality

\frac{|\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(iv)|}{1-|\phi_{\alpha}(iv)|^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{(1-v^{2})^{2}+4\alpha^{2}v^{2}}}{\alpha(1-v^{4})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{(1-v^{2})^{2}+4v^{2}}}{\alpha(1-v^{4})}=\frac{1}{\alpha(1-v^{2})}

for
v\in(-1,1)
and
0<\alpha<1.

Hence, using (
2.1
), we obtain

\ell^{\prime}_{n}\leq 2\ell_{n}\cot^{2}(\xi/4)\int^{v_{n}}_{v_{n+1}}\frac{dv}{\alpha(1-v^{2})}=\ell_{n}\cot^{2}(\xi/4)\frac{\log q}{\alpha}=N_{p}(q)\ell_{n}

for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Summing up for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}},
we finally get

\ell(f({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))=\ell(h({\mathbb{I}^{+}}))=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\ell_{n}^{\prime}\leq N_{p}(q)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\ell_{n}=N_{p}(q)\ell(h({\mathbb{R}}^{+}))=N_{p}(q)\ell(f({\mathbb{T}}^{-})).

The assertion of the theorem is now clear.
∎

Finally, we will generalize Theorem B.
In order to enhance the difference,
we rephrase Theorem B in terms of the hyperbolic geometry of a simply connected
domain
D
as follows.
Let
\Gamma
be the hyperbolic geodesic that joins two points
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
a simply connected domain
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}.

Then, for any Jordan arc
J
joining
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
D,
the inequality

(3.4)

\ell(\Gamma)\leq A_{1}\ell(J)

holds, where
A_{1}
is the constant in Theorem A.
We will generalize this to the case when
D
is a simply connected hyperbolic subdomain of
{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
containing
\infty.

In this case, the bound should depend on a distance from
\infty
to the geodesic
\Gamma.

Our result can be stated in the form similar to that of Theorem B.
We denote by
{\operatorname{hull}}(C)
for a closed curve
C
in
{\mathbb{C}}

the compact set
K={\mathbb{C}}\setminus U_{\infty},
where
U_{\infty}
is
the (unique) unbounded connected component of
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus C,

and denote by
{\operatorname{hull}}^{\circ}(C)
the interior of the set
{\operatorname{hull}}(C).

Here, we also note that the Euclidean length
\ell(J)
of a Jordan arc
J

is the same as the (suitably normalized) 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{1}(J)

(see
[
8
, p. 56]
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
J
be a (closed) Jordan arc in
{\mathbb{D}}
with endpoints
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}

and let
\gamma
be the (closed) hyperbolic line segment joining
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}

in
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Let
\tilde{\gamma}
be the set
C\setminus C_{0},
where
C
is the Euclidean circle (or Euclidean line
attached with
\infty
) containing
\gamma
and
C_{0}
is the connected component of
C\setminus J

containing a point outside
{\mathbb{D}}.

Let
f
be a meromorphic univalent function on
{\mathbb{D}}
with a pole
at
s\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Suppose that
s\not\in{\operatorname{hull}}(\tilde{\gamma}\cup J)
,
then

\ell(f(\gamma))\leq\begin{cases}A_{1}\ell(f(J))&\text{if}~s\not\in{\operatorname{hull}}^{\circ}(J\cup\hat{J})\\
A_{\tau}^{+}\ell(f(J))&\text{if}~s\in{\operatorname{hull}}^{\circ}(J\cup\hat{J}),\end{cases}

where
\hat{J}
denotes the reflection of
J
with respect to the circle
C
and

\tau=\tanh d_{\mathbb{D}}(s,\tilde{\gamma}).

Here,
A_{1}
is the same as defined in Theorem A and
A_{p}^{+}
is defined in Lemma
3
for
0<p<1.

If
A_{p}
is non-increasing, we would have
A_{p}^{+}=A_{p}.

Proof.
By our assumption,
\tau>0.
Translating by a disk automorphism if necessary,
we can assume that
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
lie on
{{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}=(-i,i).

Then
\hat{J}
is the reflection of
J
in the imaginary axis.
Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be the set of all connected components
V
of
J\setminus\tilde{\gamma}.

Then each
V\in{\mathcal{V}}
is an open subarc of
J
with endpoints in
\tilde{\gamma}.

We denote by
I_{V}
the open line segment sharing two endpoints with
V.

Let
I=\cup_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}I_{V}.

Then
I
is an open subset of
\tilde{\gamma}.

We set
\gamma_{0}=\tilde{\gamma}\cap J
and show that

\gamma\subset\gamma_{0}\cup I\quad\mbox{ and }\quad J=\gamma_{0}\cup\left(\bigcup_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}V\right).

Since
\cup_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}V=J\setminus\tilde{\gamma},
the latter equality is clear. We will verify the inclusion relation
\gamma\subset\gamma_{0}\cup I.

Choose an arbitrary point
\zeta_{0}\in\gamma\setminus I
and we will show the claim

\zeta_{0}\in J.

We denote by
\tilde{\zeta}_{m}~(m=1,2)
the endpoints of
\tilde{\gamma}
so that

\tilde{\zeta}_{1},\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\tilde{\zeta}_{2}
lie in
{{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}
in this order.
Note that
\tilde{\zeta}_{m}
may be equal to
\zeta_{m}.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}_{m}=[\tilde{\zeta}_{m},\zeta_{0}]
for
m=1,2.

For convenience, we parametrize
J
by a homeomorphism
j:[0,1]\to J
such that

j(0)=\zeta_{1}
and
j(1)=\zeta_{2}.

Let
t_{1}=\sup\{t\in[0,1]:j(t)\in\tilde{\gamma}_{1}\},

that is,
t_{1}
is the “final exit time” of
j
from
\tilde{\gamma}_{1}.

In particular,
j(t)\neq\zeta_{0}
for
t>t_{1}.

It suffices to show that
\zeta_{0}=j(t_{1})~(\in J).

To the contrary, we suppose that
j(t_{1})\neq\zeta_{0}.

We next let
t_{2}=\inf\{t\in(t_{1},1]:j(t)\in\tilde{\gamma}_{2}\}
.
By continuity of
j,
we see that
\zeta_{m}^{\prime}:=j(t_{m})\in\tilde{\gamma}_{m}

and
j(t_{m})\neq\zeta_{0}
for
m=1,2.

In particular,
t_{1}<t_{2}.

Since
j((t_{1},t_{2}))
does not meet
\tilde{\gamma}
by construction,

V=j((t_{1},t_{2}))\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
I_{V}=(\zeta_{1}^{\prime},\zeta_{2}^{\prime})
contains the point

\zeta_{0},
which contradicts the assumption
\zeta_{0}\in\gamma\setminus I.

Hence, we conclude that
\zeta_{0}=j(t_{1})\in J.

Thus, the claim has been verified and
\zeta_{0}\in\gamma\cap J\subset\gamma_{0}
follows.

We now fix a Jordan arc
V\in{\mathcal{V}}.

We denote by
D_{V}
the Jordan domain bounded by
J_{V}=\overline{V}\cup I_{V}
and denote by

\widetilde{D}_{V}
the union of
D_{V},~I_{V}
and
\hat{D}_{V}

which stands for the reflection of
D_{V}
in
{{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}.

Let
g_{V}:{\mathbb{D}}_{-}\to D_{V}
be a conformal homeomorphism whose continuous extension
to
\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{-}
maps
{{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}
onto
I_{V}.

Here, we recall that
{\mathbb{D}}_{-}
is the left-half of the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}.

Schwarz’s reflection principle now allows us to extend
g_{V}
to a conformal
homeomorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
onto
\widetilde{D}_{V}
, which will be denoted
by the same symbol.
We next consider the following two cases.

Case I
:
s\not\in\widetilde{D}_{V}.

In this case,
f\circ g_{V}:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is analytic and univalent.
Thus, by Theorem A, we obtain

\ell(f(I_{V}))=\ell((f\circ g_{V})({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))\leq A_{1}\ell((f\circ g_{V})({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))=A_{1}\ell(f(V)).

Case II
:
s\in\widetilde{D}_{V}.

In this case, since
s\not\in D_{V}\cup I_{V}
by assumption,
we may further normalize the conformal map
g_{V}:{\mathbb{D}}\to\widetilde{D}_{V}

so that
p=g_{V}^{-1}(s)\in(0,1).

Since
\widetilde{D}_{V}\subset{\mathbb{D}},
the domain monotonicity of hyperbolic metric
yields

{\operatorname{arth}\,}p=d_{\mathbb{D}}(p,0)=d_{\widetilde{D}_{V}}(s,g_{V}(0))\geq d_{\widetilde{D}_{V}}(s,I_{V})\geq d_{\mathbb{D}}(s,I_{V})\geq d_{\mathbb{D}}(s,\tilde{\gamma})={\operatorname{arth}\,}\tau,

and therefore,
p\geq\tau.

By the definition of
A_{p}
, we have

\ell(f(I_{V}))=\ell((f\circ g_{V})({{\mathbb{I}}_{1}}))\leq A_{p}\ell((f\circ g_{V})({\mathbb{T}}^{-}))=A_{p}\ell(f(V))\leq A_{\tau}^{+}\ell(f(V)).

Finally, we show the inequality in the assertion.
When
s\in{\operatorname{hull}}^{\circ}(J\cup\hat{J}),
we have

\ell(f(I_{V}))\leq A_{\tau}^{+}\ell(f(V))
for every
V\in{\mathcal{V}}

because
A_{1}\leq A_{\tau}^{+}.

Since
\gamma\subset\gamma_{0}\cup I
and
J
is the disjoint union
of
\gamma_{0}
and
V
over
{\mathcal{V}},
we obtain

\displaystyle\ell(f(\gamma))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(\gamma))\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(\gamma_{0}))+{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(I))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(\gamma_{0}))+\sum_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(I_{V}))

\displaystyle\leq A_{\tau}^{+}{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(\gamma_{0}))+A_{\tau}^{+}\sum_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(V))

\displaystyle=A_{\tau}^{+}{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(f(J))=A_{\tau}^{+}\ell(f(J)).

When
s\not\in{\operatorname{hull}}^{\circ}(J\cup\hat{J}),
only Case I occurs.
Therefore, we can show the corresponding inequality in a similar way.
∎

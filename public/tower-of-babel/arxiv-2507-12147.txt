1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the Dirac system with an integrable potential, with respect to a spectral parameter located in the complex half-plane. This article addresses two main topics. The first concerns the asymptotic behavior of a certain fundamental system associated with a Dirac system. The second focuses on results related to the Cauchy problem for Dirac systems.
The approach presented in this paper is based on transforming the system into appropriate integral equations
as developed in
[
4
]
and
[
22
]
.

In recent years, Dirac operators have attracted a considerable attention, particularly in the field of non-self-adjoint spectral theory. From the perspective of physics, they are relevant in the study of nonlinear Schrödinger equations and have also been employed as effective models for graphene (see
[
2
]
or
[
6
]
and references therein).

There is a wide variety of asymptotic results in the literature concerning solutions to Dirac systems, obtained in different settings and under a wide range of assumptions on the potentials. Most of these results assume that the spectral parameter lies within a horizontal strip in the complex plane, and they typically provide only the leading asymptotic term. We introduce a new and relatively simple method to derive sharp asymptotics
for fundamental system solutions of Dirac systems
with integrable coefficients and spectral parameter taking values values in a half-plane.
To the best of our knowledge,
so precise results in this setting
have not previously appeared in the literature, apart from the recent article
[
8
]
focused
on the systems with smooth coefficients.
Moreover, our results can be extended to cover spectral parameters in the whole plane except bounded region around the origin.

For
Y=(y_{1},y_{2})^{T}
we consider an one-dimensional Dirac system in the following form:

(1.1)

Y^{\prime}(x)+J(x)Y(x)=A_{\mu}Y(x),

for
x\in[0,1],
where

(1.2)

A_{\mu}=i\mu\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right],\quad J(x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sigma_{1}(x)\\
\sigma_{2}(x)&0\end{array}\right]

Here
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
is a spectral parameter, and complex-valued functions
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
belong to

L^{p}[0,1]
, with
1\leq p<2
.

The solution
Y=(y_{1},y_{2})
is understood to be a function having entries
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
from
the space of absolutely continuous functions on
[0,1]

(i.e. from the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}[0,1]
)
and satisfying (
1.1
) for a.e.
x\in[0,1]
. All our results are formulated under the assumption that the spectral parameter is large enough and lies in the half-plane

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{r}=\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}:\;\operatorname{Im}\mu>-r\},\ \ r\geq 0.

Various methods for analyzing solutions of Dirac systems are available in the literature. We note, in particular, a method, employing the Prüfer’s transformation, is discussed in

It is often of value to have a method providing sharp asymptotics results without invoking very involved and demanding techniques.
On this way, very detailed asymptotic formulas were established in

[

They found applications to study of the Sturm-Liouville problem involving a singular potential. A recent work
[

extended the analysis in
[
5

to the case when

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in L^{p}[0,1]

and the results were based on a specific integral representation of the fundamental matrix

of (
1.1
)
satisfying
D(0)=I
.

To study the problem with the spectral parameter in a half-plane, one needs to employ different methods. It relies on obtaining a suitable representation of the Dirac system using associated integral operators. The fundamental system of the solutions to the Dirac system is expressed as a power series of these operators. Given the desired behavior of the reminder, one obtains a bound for the series of higher-order terms allowing to reduce the study of asymptotics
of fundamental system
to careful analysis of a few lower order terms.
The obtained results are applied to the study of the Cauchy problem for the Dirac system in the half-plane.
Having established asymptotic formulas when the spectral parameter belongs to a half-plane, it is easy to extend them
to the entire complex plane, except for a bounded neighborhood around zero (see
[
22
, Remark 5.5]
).

The paper is organized as follows.
First, we recall the results from
[
22
]
concerning a fundamental system of solutions of the Dirac system. Then we establish asymptotic formulas for a fundamental system of solutions
to (
1.1
) and compare them with asymptotic formulas from
[
21

where spectral parameter belongs to a strip. The obtained asymptotic results are then applied to the study of the Cauchy problem with spectral aprameter in a half-plane.
As another application, we obtain new, sharp
asymptotic formulas for fundamental system of solutions
of Sturm-Liouville equations with singular potentials.

3.
Estimates for integral operators and their consequences

We proceed with providing norm estimates for powers of operators

introduced
in the previous section.
Define

The next bound for powers of

Lemma 3.1
.

If

and

\displaystyle\|K_{1}^{2n}z\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leq a^{n}\gamma_{1}^{n}(\mu)\|z\|_{\mathcal{C}},

We proceed with obtaining bounds for

To this aim, it will suffice to obtain an appropriate estimate

Lemma 3.2

Let

and

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}e^{2i\mu t}\sigma_{2}(t)(K_{1}e)(t)dt

\displaystyle={\rm O}(\widetilde{\gamma}(\mu))+\gamma_{1}(\mu)\gamma_{0,1}(x,u).

as

Moreover, as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{2i\mu t}\sigma_{2}(t)(K_{1}e)(t)dt

\displaystyle={\rm O}(\widetilde{\gamma}(\mu)),

\displaystyle e^{2i\mu}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2i\mu t}\sigma_{1}(t)(K_{2}e)(t)dt

Proof.

Changing the order of integration in the right hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{1}e^{2i\mu t}\sigma_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{1}(s)\int_{s}^{1}e^{2i\mu(\tau-s)}\sigma_{2}(\tau)d\tau dsdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{x}\sigma_{1}(s)\int_{s}^{1}e^{2i\mu(\tau-s)}\sigma_{2}(\tau)d\tau ds\int_{x}^{1}e^{2i\mu t}\sigma_{2}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{1}e^{2i\mu s}\sigma_{1}(s)\Big(\int_{s}^{1}e^{2i\mu(t-s)}\sigma_{2}(t)dt\Big)^{2}ds

(3.5)

\displaystyle+e^{2i\mu x}(K_{1}e)(x)\int_{x}^{1}e^{2i\mu(t-x)}\sigma_{2}(t)dt.

Recall that by

7.
Perturbed Dirac system

Let us now consider a perturbed Dirac system in the form

(7.1)

Y^{\prime}(x)+J(x)Y(x)=A_{\mu}Y(x)+\frac{P(x)}{\mu}Y(x),\quad x\in[0,1],\quad\mu\neq 0,

where the entries of the matrix
P
belong to
L^{p}[0,1]
,
1\leq p\leq 2
.
In this setting we include the case
p=2
, because it is important from the point of
applications related to singular Sturm-Liouville problem. The case
p=2
and
\mu
with
|\operatorname{Im}\mu|\leq d
,
d>0
was
considered in
[
5
]
.

Let
D=[W,V]
be a fundamental matrix of (
1.1
), where

W
and
V
are fundamental solutions introduced in Section
2
.

Note that by Liouville’s theorem,

\det D(x)=\det D(0)=v_{2}(0),\qquad x\in[0,1],

where we have also used the assumptions on
w
and
v
.
Therefore, for every
x\in[0,1],

(7.2)

v_{2}(0)D^{-1}(x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{2}&-v_{1}\\
-w_{2}&w_{1}\end{array}\right].

Using the classical variation of constants formula, which can be found e.g. in
[
26
, Theorem 1.3.1]
.
we infer that the solutions of
(
7.1
) also satisfy the integral equation

(7.3)

\displaystyle Y(x)=D\widetilde{C}+\frac{1}{\mu}D(x)\int_{0}^{x}D^{-1}(t)P(t)Y(t)\,dt,\quad x\in[0,1],

where
\widetilde{C}=(c_{1},c_{2})^{T}
is a constant.
Note that

v_{2}(0)D^{-1}(t)P(t)Y(t)=[Q_{1}(Y),Q_{2}(Y)]^{T}(t),

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}(Y)

\displaystyle=(v_{2}p_{11}-v_{1}p_{21})y_{1}+(v_{2}p_{12}-v_{1}p_{22})y_{2},

\displaystyle Q_{2}(Y)

\displaystyle=(-w_{2}p_{11}+w_{1}p_{21})y_{1}+(-w_{2}p_{12}+w_{1}p_{22})y_{2}.

Since a constant
\widetilde{C}
is arbitrary, we may modify its second component
and, for
r\geq 0,R>0
and
\mu\in\Sigma_{r}(R)
put

c_{1}=a,\quad c_{2}=b-\frac{1}{v_{2}(0)}\int_{0}^{1}Q_{2}(Y)(t)\,dt.

Then, from (
7.3
), it follows that

(7.4)

Y(x)=aW(x)+bV(x)+\frac{1}{\mu v_{2}(0)}D(x)\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{x}Q_{1}(Y)(t)\,dt\\
-\int_{x}^{1}Q_{2}(Y)(t)\,dt\end{array}\right].

Let
r>0
be fixed.
Setting

a=1
and
b=0,
we look for a solution
Y
to (
7.4
) of the form

Y(x)=e^{i\mu x}Z(x),\quad Z=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}z_{1}\\
z_{2}\end{array}\right].

It is then direct that there exists
R>0
such that
for all
\mu\in\Sigma_{r}(R)
we have

(7.5)

Z(x)=e^{-i\mu x}W(x)+\frac{1}{\mu}(\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mu)Z)(x),\qquad x\in[0,1],

where

is a bounded linear (integral) operator on the Banach
space

given by

(\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mu)Z)(x)=\frac{D(x)}{v_{2}(0)}e^{-i\mu x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{x}e^{i\mu t}Q_{1}(Z)(t)\,dt\\
-\int_{x}^{1}e^{i\mu t}Q_{2}(Z)(t)\,dt\end{array}\right],\qquad x\in[0,1].

Next, letting
a=0
and
b=1,
we look for solutions to (
7.4
) having the form

Y(x)=e^{i\mu(1-x)}Z(x).

Using (
7.4
), we infer that
for all
\mu\in\Sigma_{r}(R),

Z(x)=e^{-i\mu(1-x)}V(x)+\frac{1}{\mu}(\mathcal{A}_{2}(\mu)Z)(x),\qquad x\in[0,1],

where

a bounded linear (integral) operator on

-\int_{x}^{1}e^{i\mu(1-t)}Q_{2}(Z)(t)\,dt.\end{array}\right],\qquad x\in[0,1].

Moreover,
applying the asymptotic formulas

) and (

), together with (

we infer that there exists

8.

In this section we consider a Sturm–Liouville equation

(8.1)

where the potential

is a distribution
of the first order, i.e.

and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}

In the same way as in
[

8.2

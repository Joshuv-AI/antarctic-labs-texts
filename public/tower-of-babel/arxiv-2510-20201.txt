Asymptotics for Anisotropic Rabi Models
Masao Hirokawa, Fumio Hiroshima, DongYun LEE

Abstract
A one-parameter family of self-adjoint operators interpolating between the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian and its rotating-wave approximation is studied. A mathematically rigorous treatment of such interpolations has been lacking. Motivated by the physical claim that counter-rotating terms dominate at strong coupling, we analyze the limit in which the coupling constant of the anisotropic Rabi model tends to infinity. Our results provide an operator-theoretic description of this limit and clarify the spectral evolution from the rotating-wave approximation to the full Rabi model.



1
Quantum Rabi model and rotating-wave approximation

1.1
Introduction

The quantum Rabi model describes an interaction between a two-level atom and a single mode harmonic oscillator and serves as a cornerstone of quantum optics and mathematical physics.
Its rotating-wave approximation provides an exactly solvable reduction obtained by neglecting the counter-rotating terms
\sigma_{+}\otimes a^{\dagger}+\sigma_{-}\otimes a
.
In the rotating-wave approximation, the total excitation number is conserved and the Hamiltonian is invariant under the one-parameter unitary group generated by the total number operator. This is referred to as a
U(1)
-symmetry. See (
1.4
).
In contrast, the full quantum Rabi Hamiltonian retains the counter-rotating terms, destroys excitation-number conservation, and leaves only a
{Z}_{2}
-symmetry. See (
1.3
).
This seemingly small change drastically alters the mathematical structure,
making the model non-integrable and leading to a rich and nontrivial spectral behavior.

To clarify the relationship between these two regimes, we introduce a one-parameter family of self-adjoint operators
H_{\varepsilon}(g)
in (
2.1
) that continuously interpolates between the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian (
\varepsilon=1
) and its rotating-wave approximation (
\varepsilon=0
).
This interpolation furnishes a unified framework in which the passage from the solvable to the non-integrable setting can be quantified at the operator level, allowing us to track how the continuous symmetry associated with excitation-number conservation gives way to a
{Z}_{2}
-symmetry as
\varepsilon
increases.
Interpolation between the quantum Rabi and its rotating-wave approximation models has been investigated extensively and has provided quantitative bridges between solvable and nonintegrable regimes;
see, e.g.,
[
3
,
15
,
13
,
14
,
2
,
1
,
8
,
10
]
.
However, a mathematically rigorous treatment of this interpolation has not yet been developed.

In the physical literature, it is often argued that the counter-rotating term becomes dominant in the strong-coupling regime.
The main purpose of this paper is to verify this statement mathematically by analyzing the limit in which the coupling constant of the anisotropic Rabi model tends to infinity.

Studies of the strong-coupling regime of the quantum Rabi model
have revealed nontrivial asymptotic spectra and operator-level mechanisms;
see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
12
,
6
]
for results from a purely mathematical standpoint.
In contrast, the corresponding limit for the rotating-wave approximation becomes trivial, as the counter-rotating processes responsible for essential nonperturbative features are absent.
By studying the interpolating family in the strong-coupling regime, we bridge these two limits and reveal how spectral and dynamical characteristics evolve from the rotating-wave approximation to the full quantum Rabi model. Our results yield a rigorous operator-theoretic description of this transition and provide new insight into the asymptotic structure of light–matter interaction models beyond the rotating-wave approximation.

This paper is organized as follows.
In the remainder of Section 1, we introduce the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian and its rotating-wave approximation.
In Section 2, we define the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian.
Section 3 is devoted to the asymptotic analysis of the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian in the strong-coupling limit
g\to\infty
.

1.2
Models

Let
p
and
q
be the self-adjoint operators defined by

(pf)(x)=-i\frac{df}{dx}(x)
and

(qf)(x)=xf(x)
in
L^{2}({R})
.
Then
p
and
q
satisfy the canonical commutation relation
[q,p]=i{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
on
D(pq)\cap D(qp)
.
The annihilation and creation operators are defined by

a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(q+ip)
and

a^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(q-ip)
, respectively, so that
[a,a^{\dagger}]={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
on
D(a^{\dagger}a)\cap D(aa^{\dagger})
.
The spin degrees of freedom are represented by the Pauli matrices

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

We consider the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian defined by

\displaystyle H_{\rm Rabi}(g)=\triangle\sigma_{z}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+g\sigma_{x}\otimes\big(a+{a^{\dagger}}\big),

(1.1)

which couples a two-level atom
\triangle\sigma_{z}
to a single mode
harmonic oscillator
a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}
.
Here
g\in{R}
denotes the coupling constant,
and
\triangle>0
is the energy difference between the excited and ground states of the two-level atom.
Throughout this paper we fix
\triangle
.
The quantum Rabi Hamiltonian is a self-adjoint operator acting on the total Hilbert space:

\displaystyle{C}^{2}\otimes L^{2}({R}).

We use the identifications

{C}^{2}\otimes L^{2}({R})\cong L^{2}({R})\oplus L^{2}({R})\cong L^{2}({R};{C}^{2})
given by

\displaystyle\binom{1}{0}\otimes f+\binom{0}{1}\otimes g\;\cong\;f\oplus g\;\cong\;\binom{f}{g},

and we write the map

U:L^{2}({R})\oplus L^{2}({R})\to L^{2}({R})\oplus L^{2}({R})
, defined by

U\binom{f}{g}=\binom{Tf}{Sg}
, as

U=\begin{pmatrix}T&0\\
0&S\end{pmatrix}
or
U=T\oplus S
in what follows.
On the other hand let

\displaystyle\sigma_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian under the rotating-wave approximation is defined by

\displaystyle H_{\rm RWA}(g)=\triangle\sigma_{z}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+g(\sigma_{-}\otimes a^{\dagger}+\sigma_{+}\otimes a).

(1.2)

We abbreviate the rotating-wave approximation by RWA in what follows.
Note that
a^{\dagger}a=\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2}-1)
. Then
in the matrix representation the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian is of the form:

\displaystyle H_{\rm Rabi}(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\triangle&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})-\triangle\end{pmatrix}+g\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{2}q\\
\sqrt{2}q&0\end{pmatrix}

and
the RWA Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H_{\rm RWA}(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\triangle&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})-\triangle\end{pmatrix}+g\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(q+ip)\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(q-ip)&0\end{pmatrix}.

This is also referred to as the Jaynes–Cummings Hamiltonian
[
7
]
.
Each Hamiltonian possesses a characteristic symmetry.
Let

\displaystyle N_{T}=\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\dagger}a,\qquad P=\sigma_{z}\otimes(-{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}})^{a^{\dagger}a}.

Here
N_{T}
is called the total number operator and
P
the parity operator.

P
is a unitary and self-adjoint involution, i.e.,
P^{\ast}=P
and
P^{2}={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
.
From

\displaystyle[H_{\mathrm{Rabi}}(g),P]=0,

(1.3)

the Rabi Hamiltonian is said to possess a
{Z}_{2}
-symmetry.
On the other hand, the RWA Hamiltonian satisfies

\displaystyle e^{i\theta N_{T}}H_{\mathrm{RWA}}(g)e^{-i\theta N_{T}}=H_{\mathrm{RWA}}(g)\quad\theta\in R,

(1.4)

i.e., it is invariant under the one-parameter unitary group generated by
N_{T}
;
equivalently,
[H_{\mathrm{RWA}}(g),N_{T}]=0
.
This is often referred to in physics as a
U(1)
-symmetry associated with conservation of the total excitation number.

2
Interpolation family

2.1
Anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian

We introduce a parameter
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
that continuously interpolates between the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian (
\varepsilon=1
) and its rotating-wave approximation (
\varepsilon=0
), thereby constructing an interpolating family of self-adjoint operators:

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(g)=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\triangle&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\triangle\end{pmatrix}+g\left\{\varepsilon\begin{pmatrix}0&a+a^{\dagger}\\
a+a^{\dagger}&0\end{pmatrix}+(1-\varepsilon)\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
a^{\dagger}&0\end{pmatrix}\right\}.

(2.1)

Similarly, the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian is defined as a mixture of the rotating terms

\sigma_{-}a^{\dagger}+\sigma_{+}a
and the counter-rotating terms

\sigma_{-}a+\sigma_{+}a^{\dagger}
:

\displaystyle H(g_{1},g_{2},g)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\triangle&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\triangle\end{pmatrix}+g\begin{pmatrix}0&g_{1}a+g_{2}a^{\dagger}\\
g_{1}a^{\dagger}+g_{2}a&0.\end{pmatrix}

(2.2)

More precisely,
in the case
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
,
H(g_{1},g_{2},g)
is called the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian
and
in the case
g_{1}=g_{2}
, the isotropic Rabi Hamiltonian; and
We generalize (
2.1
) and (
2.4
).
Let
\alpha,\beta\in{R}
. We define

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\triangle&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})-\triangle\end{pmatrix}+g\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha q+i\beta p\\
\alpha q-i\beta p&0\end{pmatrix}.

In this paper, we also refer to
K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
as the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian.

Lemma 2.1

For any
\alpha,\beta,g\in{R}
,

K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
is self-adjoint on
D((p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus(p^{2}+q^{2}))
and
essentially self-adjoint on any core of

(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus(p^{2}+q^{2})
.

Proof.

We can see that
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
b_{\varepsilon}
such that

\|pf\|\leq\varepsilon\|(p^{2}+q^{2})f\|+b_{\varepsilon}\|f\|
and

\|qf\|\leq\varepsilon\|(p^{2}+q^{2})f\|+b_{\varepsilon}\|f\|

for
f\in D(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
Then
we can see that
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
b_{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha q+i\beta p\\
\alpha q-i\beta p&0\end{pmatrix}f\right\|\leq\varepsilon\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\triangle&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})-\triangle\end{pmatrix}f\right\|+b_{\varepsilon}\|f\|

for
f\in D((p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus(p^{2}+q^{2}))
.
Then the Kato-Rellich theorem
[
9
]
yields the lemma.
∎We also see that

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)=H_{\rm Rabi}(g\alpha/\sqrt{2})+g\begin{pmatrix}0&i\beta p\\
-i\beta p&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.3)

Consequently,
K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
may be viewed as a perturbation of the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian.

Lemma 2.2

For all
\alpha,\beta\in R
, the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian
K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
possesses a
{Z}_{2}
-symmetry.
Moreover,
K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
has a
U(1)
-symmetry if and only if
\alpha=\beta
.

Proof.

Let
V=\begin{pmatrix}0&ip\\
-ip&0\end{pmatrix}
. We can see that
[V,P]=0
.
Since
H_{\rm Rabi}(g)
has a
{Z}_{2}
-symmetry, by (
2.3
),

[K_{\alpha,\beta}(g),P]=0
holds true.
Let
\alpha=\beta
. Then

K_{\alpha,\alpha}(g)=H_{\rm RWA}(\sqrt{2}\alpha g)
,
and hence

K_{\alpha,\alpha}(g)
has a
U(1)
-symmetry.
Suppose that
\alpha\neq\beta
.
We have

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)=K_{\alpha,\alpha}(g)+(\beta-\alpha)V.

We have

V=-\sigma_{y}\otimes p
.
Since

e^{i\theta a^{\dagger}a}pe^{-i\theta a^{\dagger}a}=p\cos\theta-q\sin\theta
and

e^{i\theta\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}\sigma_{y}e^{-i\theta\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}=\cos\theta\sigma_{y}+\sin\theta\sigma_{x}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle e^{i\theta N_{T}}Ve^{-i\theta N_{T}}=-\bigl(\cos\theta\sigma_{y}+\sin\theta\sigma_{x}\bigr)\otimes\bigl(p\cos\theta-q\sin\theta\bigr)\neq V.

Since

e^{i\theta N_{T}}K_{\alpha,\alpha}(g)e^{-i\theta N_{T}}=K_{\alpha,\alpha}
,
we conclude that

e^{i\theta N_{T}}K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)e^{-i\theta N_{T}}\neq K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
.
∎We see that

K_{\alpha,0}(g)=H_{\rm Rabi}(\alpha g/\sqrt{2})
and

K_{\alpha,\alpha}(g)=H_{\rm RWA}(\sqrt{2}\alpha g)
.
The interpolation Hamiltonian is given by

H_{\varepsilon}(g)=K_{\frac{\varepsilon(2-\sqrt{2})+1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}}(g),

and the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian can be represented as

H(g_{1},g_{2},g)=K_{\frac{g_{1}+g_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{g_{1}-g_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}}(g).

In the isotropic case
g_{1}=g_{2}
, we have
H(g_{1},g_{2},g)=K_{\sqrt{2}g_{1},0}(g)
,
which coincides with the quantum Rabi Hamiltonian,
whereas, in the case
g_{1}=-g_{2}
, we have
H(g_{1},g_{2},g)=K_{0,\sqrt{2}g_{1}}(g)
.

Lemma 2.3

We see that

K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)\cong K_{\beta,\alpha}(g)
for
any
\alpha,\beta,g\in{R}
.

Proof.

Let
u=e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}i\sigma_{z}}
. Then

u\sigma_{x}u^{-1}=\sigma_{y}
and

u\sigma_{y}u^{-1}=-\sigma_{x}
.
Let
F:{L^{2}({R})}\to{L^{2}({R})}
be the Fourier transform. Then
FpF^{-1}=q
and
FqF^{-1}=-p
.
Therefore we see that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}F&0\\
0&F\end{pmatrix}uK_{\alpha,\beta}(g)u^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}F&0\\
0&F\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=K_{\beta,\alpha}(g).

Then the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 2.4

The isotropic Rabi Hamiltonian
H(g_{1},g_{1},g)
and
the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian with
g_{1}=-g_{2}
,
H(g_{1},-g_{1},g)
, are unitary equivalent.

Proof.

Since
H(g_{1},g_{1},g)=K_{\sqrt{2}g_{1},0}(g)
and

H(g_{1},-g_{1},g)=K_{0,\sqrt{2}g_{1}}(g)
,
the corollary follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Unitary operators

Following
[

]
, we apply the standard unitary transformation
\mathcal{U}
described below to

K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\begin{pmatrix}F&0\\
0&F\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{ig\alpha p}\\

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}K_{\alpha,\beta}(g)\mathcal{U}^{-1}

We set the right-hand side as
H_{\alpha,\beta}(g)
,

H_{\alpha,\beta}(g)=h+V_{g},

where

\displaystyle h=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})\end{pmatrix},

(2.4)

\displaystyle V_{g}=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{2ig\alpha q}(-\triangle+i\beta q)\\

(2.5)

, it can be heuristically observed that

(

,

]
)

Let

.
Suppose that
\beta=0
and
\alpha\in{R}
.

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}\left(H_{\alpha,0}(g)+\frac{\alpha^{2}g^{2}}{2}+z\right)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z&0\\

In this paper, we are concerned with the limit in the case
\beta\neq 0
.

3
Asymptotic behaviors

This section is devoted to the analysis of the asymptotic limit of the anisotropic Rabi Hamiltonian.

Lemma 3.1

Suppose that
z
satisfies

\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\frac{\sqrt{2}|\beta|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+z}}<1
.
Then

\displaystyle(h+V_{g}+z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(h+z)^{-1}(V_{g}(h+z)^{-1})^{n}.

(3.1)

Proof.

It is enough to verify

\|V_{g}(h+z)^{-1}\|<1
.
We actually obtain that

\displaystyle\|V_{g}(h+z)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\sqrt{2}|\beta|\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}q\left(\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z\right)^{-1/2}\right\|\left\|\left(\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z\right)^{-1/2}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\frac{\sqrt{2}|\beta|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+z}}<1.

Then the lemma is proved.
∎Set
X=e^{2ig\alpha q}(-\triangle+i\beta q)
,

Y=e^{-2ig\alpha q}(-\triangle-i\beta q)
and

A=h+z
for simplicity.
We use the notations
\frac{1}{A}
for
A^{-1}
and
\sqrt{A}
for
A^{1/2}
.
Hence (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(h+V_{g}+z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{A}\left(V_{g}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{n}.

Since
V_{g}=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{pmatrix}
,
we can directly see that

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{A}(V_{g}\tfrac{1}{A})^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{pmatrix}\overbrace{\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}\cdots X\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}}^{2n+1}&\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbf{0}$}\\
\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbf{0}$}&\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}\cdots Y\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}}_{2n+1}\end{pmatrix}&n=2m,\\
\begin{pmatrix}\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbf{0}$}&\overbrace{\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}\cdots X\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}}^{2n+1}\\
\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}\cdots Y\tfrac{1}{A}X\tfrac{1}{A}Y\tfrac{1}{A}}_{2n+1}&\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbf{0}$}\end{pmatrix}&n=2m+1.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 3.2

It follows that
\|\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{1+2z}
. In particular

\|\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\|<1
for
\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}<z
.

Proof.

By the identity

\|\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})f\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|f\|^{2}=\|\frac{1}{2}p^{2}f\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|qpf\|^{2}+\|\frac{1}{2}q^{2}f\|^{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}f\right\|^{2}|\leq\left\|\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})\frac{1}{A}f\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{1}{A}f\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}+z}\right)^{2}+\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{(\frac{1}{2}+z)^{2}}=\frac{3}{(1+2z)^{2}}.

∎Let us investigate the asymptotic behabior of (
3
) as
g\to\infty
.

Lemma 3.3

(
n=2m
)

Let
z+\frac{1}{2}>0
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{g\to\infty}\overbrace{\frac{1}{A}X\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{A}\cdots X\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{A}}^{2n+1}=\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)^{m}\frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{m},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{g\to\infty}\overbrace{\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{A}X\frac{1}{A}\cdots Y\frac{1}{A}X\frac{1}{A}}^{2n+1}=\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)^{m}\frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{m}.

(3.5)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\overbrace{\frac{1}{A}X\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{A}\cdots X\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{A}}^{2n+1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}X\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\right)^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}

and

\displaystyle X\frac{1}{A}Y=\left(-\frac{\triangle}{g}+i\beta q\right)\left(\frac{1}{2g^{2}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\frac{z}{g^{2}}+2\frac{1}{g}\alpha p+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{-1}\left(-\frac{\triangle}{g}-i\beta q\right).

(3.6)

Let
T_{g}=\frac{1}{2g^{2}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\frac{z}{g^{2}}+2\frac{1}{g}\alpha p
.

T_{g}
is essentially self-adjoint on
C_{0}^{\infty}(R^{)}
for any
g>0
,
and

\displaystyle\lim_{g\to\infty}(T_{g}+2\alpha^{2})f=2\alpha^{2}f

holds true for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(R^{)}
. Hence

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}(T_{g}+2\alpha^{2})^{-1}=\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}

by e.g.,
[
11
, Theorem VIII.25]
.
Since
q\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}
and

{\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}q}

are bounded, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}q(T_{g}+2\alpha^{2})^{-1}q\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}

and thus

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}X\frac{1}{A}Y\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}.

By (
3.6
)

Theorem 3.6

We have (1)(2) and (3) below:

(1)
\alpha^{2}>\beta^{2}
.
Suppose that
\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}<z
and

\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\frac{\sqrt{2}|\beta|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+z}}<1
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{g\to\infty}\left(H_{\alpha,\beta}(g)+\frac{g^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2}+z\right)^{-1}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

(2)
\alpha=\beta
.
Suppose that
\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}<z
and

\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\frac{\sqrt{2}|\beta|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+z}}<1
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{g\to\infty}\left(H_{\alpha,\beta}(g)+\frac{g^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2}+z\right)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+z&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+z\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

(3)
\alpha^{2}<\beta^{2}
.
Suppose that
\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}<z
and

\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\frac{\sqrt{2}|\alpha|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+z}}<1
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{g\to\infty}\left(H_{\alpha,\beta}(g)+\frac{g^{2}\beta^{2}}{2}+z\right)^{-1}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

Proof.

We prove (1).
We have

\displaystyle(h+V_{g}+z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{A}\left(V_{g}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{n}.

By the proof of Lemma
3.1

\|V_{g}\tfrac{1}{A}\|<\frac{\triangle}{\frac{1}{2}+z}+\frac{\sqrt{2}|\beta|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+z}}<1
uniformly in
g
,
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we can exchange

{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}
and
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
, by Lemma
3.5
we have

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}(h+V_{g}+z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{A}\lim_{g\to\infty}\left(V_{g}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)^{m}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{m}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{m}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

\left\|\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right\|\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{1+2z}<1

by the assumption
\beta^{2}<\alpha^{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}(h+V_{g}+z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(A-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{1}{2}q^{2})^{-1}&0\\
0&(A-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{1}{2}q^{2})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}})q^{2})+z&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}})q^{2})+z\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

\displaystyle\cong\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}(p^{2}+q^{2})+z\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

On the last line we used the elementary estimate
ap^{2}+bq^{2}\cong\sqrt{ab}(p^{2}+q^{2})
for
a>0
and
b>0
.
The proof of (3) is similar to that of (1), thanks to the symmetry stated in Lemma
2.3
.
Finally we prove (2). Let
\alpha=\beta
.
Since
\|\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\|<1
, we can see that

\displaystyle{\rm s}\!-\!\!\lim_{g\to\infty}(h+V_{g}+z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{m}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\frac{1}{A}\right)^{m}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+z)^{-1}&0\\
0&(\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+z)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then the proof is complete.
∎

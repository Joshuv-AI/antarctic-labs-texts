Statistical repulsion on hyperons in two-color dense QCD
Masato Nagatsuka, Toru Kojo

Abstract
We investigate the onset of hyperons in baryonic (diquark) matter in two-color QCD (QC 2 D) by introducing heavy quark doublets that emulate strange quarks. An even number of flavors is required to avoid the sign problem in lattice Monte Carlo simulations. To explore QC 2 D matter containing both light and heavy quarks, we construct a model in which quarks interact with light-light, light-heavy (hyperonic), and heavy-heavy diquarks via Yukawa couplings. As the quark chemical potential increases, the light diquarks condense first and form baryonic matter, and this onset density can be understood in hadronic terms. In contrast, the onset density of hyperons is substantially higher than that estimated from the hadronic sector of the model. This shift reflects an effective repulsion among baryons induced by the pre-occupied light quarks. The Pauli blocking of light quarks suppresses the attractive diquark correlations responsible, in vacuum, for making hyperons lighter than the sum of the constituent light and heavy quark masses. Implications for three-color QCD are also briefly discussed.

\displaystyle S_{Q}^{22}(p)

\displaystyle=(S_{Q}^{22})^{\rm p}(\Lambda_{\rm p}^{Q})^{C}\gamma^{0}+(S_{Q}^{22})^{\rm a}(\Lambda_{\rm a}^{Q})^{C}\gamma^{0}\,,

(22)

with
(\Lambda_{\rm p/a}^{Q})^{C}=\Lambda_{\rm a/p}^{Q}

and the projectors are defined in the same way as those for light quarks.
The coefficients are

\displaystyle(S_{Q}^{11})^{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{\,\Theta(E_{Q}-\eta_{\xi}\mu)\,}{\,p_{0}-\eta_{Q}^{\xi}\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{p}})\,}+\frac{\,\Theta(\eta_{\xi}\mu-E_{Q})\,}{\,p_{0}+\eta_{Q}^{\xi}\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{p}})\,}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle(S_{Q}^{22})^{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{\,\Theta(\eta_{\xi}\mu-E_{Q})\,}{\,p_{0}-\eta_{Q}^{\xi}\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{p}})\,}+\frac{\,\Theta(E_{Q}-\eta_{\xi}\mu)\,}{\,p_{0}+\eta_{Q}^{\xi}\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{p}})\,}\right)\,,

(23)

where
\epsilon_{Q}^{\xi}=|E_{Q}-\eta_{\xi}\mu|
.
Especially, for
M_{Q}>\mu
, the expression is simplified as

\displaystyle(S_{Q}^{11})^{\xi}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\mathrm{i}\,}{\,p_{0}+\mu-\eta_{\xi}E_{Q}\,}\,,

\displaystyle(S_{Q}^{22})^{\xi}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\mathrm{i}\,}{\,p_{0}-\mu+\eta_{\xi}E_{Q}\,}\,.

(24)

In the above expressions we omit the usual small imaginary part
\mathrm{i}\delta

to save the space, but in actual computations we make replacement
p_{0}\rightarrow p_{0}+\mathrm{i}p_{0}\delta

to describe the correct boundary condition.

III
Effective potential

We compute the effective potential for
possible condensates
\Phi=(\sigma,\pi,D,D_{Q})
and
Y=(Y_{u},Y_{d})
.
In the following we treat all fields as real, without loss of generality.
The effective action can be computed by shifting bosonic fields into the condensed and fluctuation parts,

\Phi\rightarrow\Phi+\hat{\Phi}
,
Y\rightarrow Y+\hat{Y}
,
and by computing the 1PI graphs;

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma(\Phi,Y)}=\int\mathcal{D}F\mathcal{D}\hat{\Phi}\mathcal{D}\hat{Y}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\int_{x}\mathcal{L}(F;\Phi+\hat{\Phi},Y+\hat{Y})}\,,

(25)

where
F
collectively denotes fermion fields
\psi
,
\psi_{Q}
, and their conjugates.

In the mean field effective action, the fluctuations
\hat{\Phi}
and
\hat{Y}
are neglected,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{\rm MF}(\Phi,Y)}=\int\mathcal{D}F\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\int_{x}\mathcal{L}(F;\Phi,Y)}\,.

(26)

We treat the coupling between hyperon fields
Y
and light-heavy quark fields as perturbation,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{\rm MF}(\Phi,Y)}

\displaystyle\simeq\int\mathcal{D}F\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\int_{x}\big[\mathcal{L}_{q}+\mathcal{L}_{Q}+\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{0}\big]}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\bigg(1+\mathrm{i}\int_{x}\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\rm mix}+\cdots\bigg)\,.

(27)

The first nonzero contributions arise from the second order perturbation.
This generates the self-energy terms for the
Y
fields.

We note that the
\psi_{Q}
fields do not yield the anomalous propagators, i.e.,
in the density interval of
D_{Q}=0
.
The self-energy term comes from the product of the normal propagators,

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\frac{\,\Pi_{Y}\,}{2}|Y_{i}|^{2}\equiv(-\mathrm{i}g_{Y})^{2}(-1){\rm Tr}\big[S_{q}^{11}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}S_{Q}^{22}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}\big]|Y_{i}|^{2}\,.

(28)

where the trace runs over momenta, color- and Dirac-indices, but not over flavors.
We delineate this term in the next section.

The effective potential
V_{\rm MF}=-\Gamma_{\rm MF}
is

\displaystyle V_{\rm MF}=V_{\rm tree}+V_{ct}+V_{\rm loop}+V_{\rm pert}^{Y}\,.

(29)

Assuming
\pi=D_{Q}=0
, writing
M_{q}=g\sigma
,
\Delta=gD
, and
\Delta_{Y_{i}}=g_{Y}Y_{i}
,
the tree level potential is

\displaystyle V_{\rm tree}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,m_{\phi}^{2}\,}{2g^{2}}M_{q}^{2}+\frac{\,m_{\phi}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\,}{2g^{2}}\Delta^{2}+\frac{\,\lambda\,}{\,24g^{4}\,}\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\,h_{\sigma}\,}{g}M_{q}-\frac{\,2h_{D}\,}{g}\Delta

\displaystyle+\frac{\,m_{Y}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\,}{2g_{Y}^{2}}\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}+\frac{\,\lambda_{Y}\,}{\,24\,}\big(\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\,\kappa_{\phi Y}\,}{\,gg_{Y}^{2}\,}M_{q}\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}+\frac{\,\lambda_{\phi Y}\,}{\,g^{2}g_{Y}^{2}\,}\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}\,.

(30)

The counter terms are

\displaystyle V_{ct}

\displaystyle=\!\frac{\,\delta m_{\phi}^{2}\,}{\,2g^{2}\,}M_{q}^{2}\!+\!\frac{\,\delta m_{\phi}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\delta Z_{\phi}\,}{\,2g^{2}\,}\Delta^{2}\!+\!\frac{\,\delta\lambda\,}{\,24g^{4}\,}\big(M_{q}^{2}\!+\!\Delta^{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\,\delta m_{Y}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\delta Z_{Y}\,}{\,2g_{Y}^{2}\,}\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}+\frac{\,\delta\lambda_{Y}\,}{\,24g_{Y}^{4}\,}\big(\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\,\delta\kappa_{\phi Y}\,}{\,gg_{Y}^{2}\,}M_{q}\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}+\frac{\,\delta\lambda_{\phi Y}\,}{\,g^{2}g_{Y}^{2}\,}\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}\,.

(31)

The fermion loop yields
(N_{\rm c}=N_{\rm f}=2)
,

\displaystyle V_{\rm loop}

\displaystyle=-N_{\rm f}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\big[\epsilon_{q}^{\rm p}({\bm{p}})+\epsilon_{q}^{\rm a}({\bm{p}})\big]

\displaystyle\quad-N_{\rm f}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\big[\,|E_{Q}({\bm{p}})-\mu|+|E_{Q}({\bm{p}})+\mu|\,\big]\,,

(32)

with
E_{Q}({\bm{p}})=\sqrt{{\bm{p}}^{2}+M_{Q}^{2}\,}
.
For
M_{Q}>\mu
, the
\mu
-dependence of the heavy quark contributions vanish,

|E_{Q}-\mu|+|E_{Q}+\mu|=E_{Q}-\mu+E_{Q}+\mu=2E_{Q}
.
We discuss the isolation of the UV divergence shortly.

Finally the perturbative contribution is

\displaystyle V_{\rm pert}^{Y}=\frac{\,\Pi_{Y}\,}{\,2g_{Y}^{2}\,}\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}\,.

(33)

Below we first fix the counter terms and then move
to computations of
\Pi_{Y}
.
Meanwhile the determination of the renormalized parameters
will be done in separate computations.

III.1
Light quark sector

We evaluate

\displaystyle V_{\rm loop}^{q}(\mu)

\displaystyle=-N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\big[\epsilon_{q}^{\rm p}({\bm{p}})+\epsilon_{q}^{\rm a}({\bm{p}})\big]

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=V_{q}^{R}(\mu)+V_{q}^{(1)}(M_{q},\Delta)+\mu^{2}V_{q}^{(2)}(M_{q},\Delta)\,,

(34)

where the last two terms are divergent,

\displaystyle V_{q}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-2N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\sqrt{{\bm{p}}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle V_{q}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\frac{\,\Delta^{2}\,}{\,({\bm{p}}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2})^{3/2}\,}\,.

(35)

Subtracting these terms from
V_{q}
,
the twice subtracted potential
V_{q}^{R}
, defined by
V_{q}-V_{q}^{(1)}-V_{q}^{(2)}
, is UV finite.
The divergent
V_{q}^{(1)}
and
V_{q}^{(2)}
can be computed
analytically in the dimensional regularization
5
;
4
,

\displaystyle V_{q}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}+\frac{\,3\,}{2}-\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\Lambda^{2}}\bigg)\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle V_{q}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,2N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}-\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\Lambda^{2}}\bigg)\big(-2\Delta^{2}\big)\,,

(36)

where
\Lambda
is a renormalization scale
at which the renormalized parameters in our model are defined.

We use the
\overline{{\rm MS}}
scheme to remove the UV divergences.
The counter terms are

\displaystyle\delta m_{\phi}^{2}=0\,,~~~~\delta Z_{\phi}=-\frac{\,2N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}g^{2}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\delta\lambda=-\frac{\,24N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}g^{4}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}\,.

(37)

Then, the effective potential for the light quark sector is

\displaystyle V_{\rm MF}^{q}

\displaystyle=(V_{\rm tree}+V_{ct}+V_{\rm loop}\big)^{q}

\displaystyle=V_{q}^{R}(\mu)-\frac{\,h_{\sigma}\,}{g}M_{q}-\frac{\,2h_{D}\,}{g}\Delta+\frac{\,m_{\phi}^{2}\,}{2g^{2}}\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)-\frac{\,2\mu^{2}\Delta^{2}\,}{g^{2}}\bigg[\,1-g^{2}\frac{\,2N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\Lambda^{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{\,1\,}{\,24g^{4}\,}\bigg[\,\lambda+g^{4}\frac{\,24N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\bigg(\frac{3}{2}-\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\Lambda^{2}}\bigg)\bigg](M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2})^{2}\,.

(38)

Later we express the physical pion mass
m_{\pi}

in terms of
m_{\phi}
and the other parameters
and show the onset of diquark condensed phase
to be
\mu^{\rm onset}=m_{\pi}/2
.

III.2
Heavy quark sector

Similarly, the heavy quark loop for
M_{Q}>\mu
is

\displaystyle V_{Q}=\frac{\,N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\,}{\,(4\pi)^{2}\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}+\frac{\,3\,}{2}-\ln\frac{\,M_{Q}^{2}\,}{\Lambda^{2}}\bigg)M_{Q}^{4}\,.

(39)

Since
M_{Q}
is not dynamical, this constant can be simply removed by the vacuum subtraction,
or can be neglected.
Hence we may write

\displaystyle V_{\rm MF}^{Q}=0\,,

(40)

at sufficiently low densities with

M_{Q}>\mu
and
\Delta_{Q}=0
.

III.3
Light-heavy quark sector

III.3.1
The structure of the potential

The light-heavy potential is

\displaystyle V^{Y}_{\rm MF}=\frac{\,C_{2}^{Y}\,}{2g_{Y}^{2}}\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}+\frac{\,\lambda_{Y}+\delta\lambda_{Y}\,}{\,24g_{Y}^{4}\,}\big(\Delta_{Y_{i}}^{2}\big)^{2}

(41)

where the coefficient of the quadratic
\Delta_{Y_{i}}
term is

\displaystyle C_{2}^{Y}

\displaystyle=m_{Y}^{2}-4\mu^{2}+\frac{\,2\kappa_{\phi Y}\,}{\,g\,}M_{q}+\frac{\,2\lambda_{\phi Y}\,}{\,g^{2}\,}\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\delta m_{Y}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\delta Z_{Y}+\frac{\,2\delta\kappa_{\phi Y}\,}{\,g\,}M_{q}+\frac{\,2\delta\lambda_{\phi Y}\,}{\,g^{2}\,}\big(M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\Pi_{Y}(\mu)\,.

(42)

Below we evaluate the self-energy at zero momenum,

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}(\mu)=2\mathrm{i}g_{Y}^{2}{\rm Tr}\big[S_{q}^{11}(p)\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}S_{Q}^{22}(p)\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}\big]

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{i}g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}={\rm p,a}}\int_{p}{\rm tr}\big[\Lambda_{\xi}\gamma_{0}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}(\Lambda^{Q}_{\xi^{\prime}})^{C}\gamma_{0}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}\big]S_{11}^{\xi}(S_{22}^{Q})^{\xi^{\prime}}\,,

(43)

where
\Lambda_{\xi}=\Lambda_{\xi}({\bm{p}})
,
\Lambda^{Q}_{\xi^{\prime}}=\Lambda^{Q}_{\xi^{\prime}}({\bm{p}})
,

S_{11}^{\xi}=S_{11}^{\xi}(p)
, and
(S_{22}^{Q})^{\xi^{\prime}}=(S_{22}^{Q})^{\xi^{\prime}}(p)
.
This function is UV divergent.
But its twice subtracted function
\Pi_{Y}^{R}
can be made UV finite.
Then
\Pi_{Y}
can be written as (the prime means
\mu^{2}
-derivative)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\Pi_{Y}(\mu)=\Pi_{Y}^{R}(\mu)+\Pi_{Y}^{(1)}(M_{q},\Delta)+\mu^{2}\Pi_{Y}^{(2)}(M_{q},\Delta)\,,

(44)

which defines
\Pi_{Y}^{R}

with the divergent functions
\Pi_{Y}^{(1)}
and
\Pi_{Y}^{(2)}

to be determined shortly in Eq. (
60
).
The divergences in
\Pi_{Y}^{(1)}
and
\Pi_{Y}^{(2)}
are cancelled by the counter terms.

First we carry out the
p_{0}
integration,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}({\bm{p}};\mu)=\int_{p_{0}}S_{11}^{\xi}(S_{22}^{Q})^{\xi^{\prime}}\,.

(45)

For
M_{Q}>\mu
, it yields (
\epsilon_{Q}^{\xi}\equiv|E_{Q}-\eta_{\xi}\mu|
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi}=\frac{\,|u_{\xi}({\bm{p}})|^{2}\,}{\,\epsilon_{q}^{\xi}({\bm{p}})+\epsilon_{Q}^{\xi}({\bm{p}})\,}\,,~~~(\xi=\xi^{\prime})

(46)

for
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
corresponding to the particle-particle and antiparticle-antiparticle contributions,
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}=\frac{\,|v_{\xi}({\bm{p}})|^{2}\,}{\,\epsilon_{q}^{\xi}({\bm{p}})+\epsilon_{Q}^{\xi^{\prime}}({\bm{p}})\,}\,,~~~(\xi\neq\xi^{\prime})

(47)

for
\xi\neq\xi^{\prime}
representing the particle-antiparticle contributions.

Next we compute the trace of
\gamma
-matrices,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big[\Lambda_{\xi}\gamma_{0}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}(\Lambda^{Q}_{\xi^{\prime}})^{C}\gamma_{0}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}\big]

\displaystyle=1+\eta_{\xi}\eta_{\xi^{\prime}}\frac{\,{\bm{p}}^{2}+M_{q}M_{Q}\,}{E_{q}E_{Q}}\,,

(48)

which is
\mu
-independent.

Now the self-energy is

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}(\mu)=-2g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\mathcal{K}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}({\bm{p}})\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}({\bm{p}};\mu)\,.

(49)

Before its renormalization, this (divergent) function is negative definite,
since both
\mathcal{K}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
are positive definite.
As we see later, however, the vacuum subtraction changes the sign.

III.3.2
The sign of the self-energy

Before proceeding to the renormalization of the self-energy for general
\Delta
,
it is useful to examine the sign of the self-energy holding
(M_{q},\Delta)
fixed to the vacuum values,
(M_{0},0)
.
Fixing the values of dynamical gaps makes the structure of the self-energy very transparent, as we see shortly.
The self-energy contains the integrands

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{{\rm pp}}\mathcal{M}^{{\rm pp}}+\mathcal{K}^{{\rm aa}}\mathcal{M}^{{\rm aa}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}^{{\rm pp}}\bigg[\frac{\,\Theta(E_{q}-\mu)\,}{\,E_{q}+E_{Q}-2\mu\,}+\frac{\,1\,}{\,E_{q}+E_{Q}+2\mu\,}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}^{{\rm pp}}\bigg[-\frac{\,\Theta(\mu-E_{q})\,}{\,E_{q}+E_{Q}-2\mu\,}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{\,2\,}{\,E_{q}+E_{Q}\,}+\frac{\,8\mu^{2}\,}{\,(E_{q}+E_{Q})^{3}\,}+\cdots\bigg]\,,

(50)

and (reminder:
E_{Q}>\mu
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{{\rm pa}}\mathcal{M}^{{\rm pa}}+\mathcal{K}^{{\rm ap}}\mathcal{M}^{{\rm ap}}=\mathcal{K}^{{\rm pa}}\frac{\,\Theta(\mu-E_{q})\,}{\,E_{Q}-E_{q}\,}\,.

(51)

The terms without the step function depends on
\mu

only through
M_{q}
and
\Delta
;
these terms can be completely eliminated by the counter terms

\delta m_{Y}^{2}
and
\delta Z_{Y}
,
as we are holding
M_{q}
and
\Delta
fixed to the vacuum value.
The resultant self-energy contributions are positive,

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}(\mu)+\delta m_{Y}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\delta Z_{Y}

\displaystyle=2g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\Theta(\mu-E_{q})\bigg[\frac{\,\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}\,}{\,E_{q}+E_{Q}-2\mu\,}-\frac{\,\mathcal{K}^{\rm pa}\,}{\,E_{Q}-E_{q}\,}\bigg]

\displaystyle>2g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\Theta(\mu-E_{q})\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}\bigg[\frac{\,1\,}{\,E_{q}+E_{Q}-2\mu\,}-\frac{\,1\,}{\,E_{Q}-E_{q}\,}\bigg]

\displaystyle=2g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\Theta(\mu-E_{q})\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}\frac{\,2(\mu-E_{q})\,}{\,(E_{q}+E_{Q}-2\mu)(E_{Q}-E_{q})\,}

\displaystyle>0\,,

(52)

where
\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}>\mathcal{K}^{\rm pa}
is used.
Thus, the effective energy of the
Y
-diquarks increases.

This expression can be analytically estimated in
the case with
M_{Q}\gg\mu\,(>M_{q})
as

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}+\delta m_{Y}^{2}-4\mu^{2}\delta Z_{Y}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\,4g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\,}{\,M_{Q}\,}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\Theta(\mu-E_{q})=\frac{\,4g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}n_{B}\,}{\,M_{Q}\,}\,.

(53)

We note that
\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}-\mathcal{K}^{\rm pa}\simeq 2
is justified when
|{\bm{p}}|\ll M_{q}
.
The effective energy of
Y
grows with
n_{B}
;
this represents an effective repulsion between
Y
and light quarks.
If we retain the momentum dependence in the denominator,
the growth of the repulsion becomes milder,
with powers of
n_{B}
less than the linear.

Below we generalize these considerations
by including the in-medium modifications of
M_{q}
and
\Delta
.

III.3.3
The structure of the UV divergence

To isolate the UV divergence and apply the dimensional regularization,
we express the integral in (inverse) powers of
\bar{E}\equiv\sqrt{{\bm{p}}^{2}+M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}
.
Expanding in this way, the effective potential can be expressed manifestly
in powers of
M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}
which respects the Pauli-Gürsey symmetry in the original Lagrangian (in the chiral limit).
It is useful to note
E_{q}^{2}=\bar{E}^{2}-\Delta^{2}
and
E_{Q}^{2}=\bar{E}^{2}-\Delta^{2}+(M_{Q}^{2}-M_{q}^{2})
.

To specify the UV divergent terms,
we first examine the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{K}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
.
It is

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathcal{K}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}=1+\eta_{\xi}\eta_{\xi^{\prime}}-\frac{\,\eta_{\xi}\eta_{\xi^{\prime}}(M_{Q}-M_{q})^{2}\,}{2\bar{E}^{2}}+O(\bar{E}^{-4})\,.

(54)

For
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
, this factor approaches constant at large energy and hence the divergence is severe;
we need to compute the other factor
\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
to
O(1/\bar{E}^{4})
.
For
\xi\neq\xi^{\prime}
, the leading behavior is
\sim 1/E_{q}^{2}
so that we have only to compute
\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
to
O(1/\bar{E}^{2})
.

Next, we examine the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{M}^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
.
We first note
\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}=\mathcal{K}^{\rm aa}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\rm pa}=\mathcal{K}^{\rm ap}

so that we encounter the combination of
\mathcal{M}^{\rm pp+aa}=\mathcal{M}^{\rm pp}+\mathcal{M}^{\rm aa}

and
\mathcal{M}^{\rm pa+ap}=\mathcal{M}^{\rm pa}+\mathcal{M}^{\rm ap}
.
Then it is manifest that each sum is symmetric for
\mu\leftrightarrow-\mu

so that
\Pi_{Y}
is a function of
\mu^{2}
.
We expand the energies

\displaystyle\epsilon_{q}^{\xi}

\displaystyle\simeq\bar{E}-\eta_{\xi}\mu+\eta_{\xi}\frac{\,\mu\Delta^{2}\,}{\,2\bar{E}^{2}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-3})\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{Q}^{\xi}

\displaystyle\simeq\bar{E}-\eta_{\xi}\mu+\frac{\,M_{Q}^{2}-M_{q}^{2}-\Delta^{2}\,}{\,2\bar{E}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-3})\,.

(55)

We also expand the spinors as

\displaystyle|u_{\xi}|^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq 1-\frac{\,\Delta^{2}\,}{\,4\bar{E}^{2}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-3})\,,

\displaystyle|v_{\xi}|^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\,\Delta^{2}\,}{\,4\bar{E}^{2}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-3})\,.

(56)

Combining all these, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\rm pp+aa}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\,\bar{E}\,}-\frac{\,M_{Q}^{2}-M_{q}^{2}\,}{\,4\bar{E}^{3}\,}+\frac{\,\mu^{2}\,}{\,\bar{E}^{3}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-5})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\rm pa+ap}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\,\Delta^{2}\,}{\,4\bar{E}^{3}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-5})\,.

(57)

Then, we can identify the UV divergences
from the particle-particle and antiparticle-antiparticle contributions
for the product
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{M}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm pp}\mathcal{M}^{\rm pp+aa}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2}{\,\bar{E}\,}-\frac{\,M_{Q}(M_{Q}-M_{q})\,}{\,\bar{E}^{3}\,}+\frac{\,2\mu^{2}\,}{\,\bar{E}^{3}\,}+O(\bar{E}^{-5})

(58)

while it turns out that the particle-antiparticle contributions do not yield the UV divergent term in
\Pi_{Y}
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm pa}\mathcal{M}^{\rm pa+ap}\simeq O(\bar{E}^{-5})\,.

(59)

The divergent functions
\Pi_{Y}^{(1)}
and
\Pi_{Y}^{(2)}
, which are to be used in Eq. (
44
), are

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}^{(1)}=4g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\bigg[-\frac{1}{\,\bar{E}\,}+\frac{\,M_{Q}(M_{Q}-M_{q})\,}{\,2\bar{E}^{3}\,}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}^{(2)}=4g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\int_{{\bm{p}}}\frac{\,-1\,}{\,\bar{E}^{3}\,}\,.

(60)

Each term can be analytically evaluated by the dimensional regularization.
We find

\displaystyle\int_{{\bm{p}}}\frac{1}{\,\bar{E}\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\,8\pi^{2}\,}\bigg[-\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}-1+\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\int_{{\bm{p}}}\frac{1}{\,\bar{E}^{3}\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,1\,}{\,4\pi^{2}\,}\bigg[\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}-\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}\bigg]\,.

(61)

Hence the divergent functions are

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}\frac{\,g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\,}{\,2\pi^{2}\,}\big[\Delta^{2}+M_{q}^{2}-M_{Q}M_{q}+M_{Q}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{\,g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\,}{\,2\pi^{2}\,}\big(\Delta^{2}+M_{q}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle~~~-\frac{\,g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\,}{\,2\pi^{2}\,}\big(\Delta^{2}+M_{q}^{2}-M_{Q}M_{q}+M_{Q}^{2}\big)\ln\frac{\,\Delta^{2}+M_{q}^{2}\,}{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}\,,

\displaystyle\Pi_{Y}^{(2)}=\frac{\,g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\,}{\,\pi^{2}\,}\bigg[-\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}+\ln\frac{\,M_{q}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\,}{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}\bigg]\,.

(62)

Now the light-heavy effective potential can be renormalized.
In the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme
we set the counter terms as

\displaystyle\delta Z_{Y}=-\frac{\,g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}\,}{\,4\pi^{2}\,}\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}\,,~~~~\delta m_{Y}^{2}=-\frac{\,g_{Y}^{2}N_{\rm c}M_{Q}^{2}\,}{\,2\pi^{2}\,}\frac{1}{\,\epsilon\,}\,,

IV.0.2
Baryonic matter of light quarks

First we describe the evolution of a baryonic matter made of light quarks.

We compare the results of purely hadronic model, i.e., tree level and chiral perturbation theory (ChPT) results,

V
Summary

We study the onset of “hyperons” in dense QC
2
D by introducing heavy quarks that mimic strange quarks.
The introduction of the heavy doublet is motivated to avoid the sign problem
in lattice Monte Carlo simulations.

QCD and QC
2
D differ because baryons are fermions in the former but bosons in the latter.
Meanwhile, one can still study the statistical constraints due to quarks
in the same way for both cases.
In this respect, it should be useful to delineate the properties of matter with hyperons in QC
2
D.

In dilute regime hyperons behave as if elementary particles in reaction to the increase of the chemical potential.
Once light diquarks condense, however, light quarks in hyperons are subject to the statistical constraint
and affect the hyperon self-energies.
If we drop off quark contributions by hand (tree level approximation),
hyperons begin to condense at
\mu_{B}(=2\mu)=m_{Y}^{\rm vac}
.
This trend changes by allowing hyperons to transform into quark intermediate states which are subject to the statistical constraints.
A stronger coupling shifts the onset chemical potential for hyperons to a larger value.
For
g_{Y}=g
, we found that hyperons do not condense except
m_{Y}
very close to
m_{\pi}
,
and the strangeness enters the system not through composite hadrons,
but through elementary strange quarks which appear for
\mu>M_{Q}
.

In this work we study hyperons up to the chemical potential just before the onset of
Y
or heavy quarks
Q
, i.e.,
\mu_{B}<m_{Y}^{\rm vac}
or
\mu_{B}<2M_{Q}
.
With this restriction, we could use light quark propagators in the background of
M_{q}
and
\Delta
,
while treated heavy quarks as impurities.
This allows us simple computations.
Meanwhile, it is also important to study how hyperons behave beyond their onset
and how the EOS softens.
We expect that the statistical repulsion caused by pre-occupied light quarks
not only delays the appearance of hyperons but also tempers the softening associated with their emergence.
The EOS after the emergence of hyperons can be computed
by using light and heavy propagators in the background of
M_{q}
,
\Delta
, and
Y
.
This will be discussed elsewhere.

Finite groups and complex projective surfaces
Alexander Lubotzky, Matthew Stover

Abstract
In response to a question raised by Belolipetsky and the first author in [ 6 ] , we prove that for every finite group G G there are infinitely many isomorphism classes of compact complex hyperbolic 2 2 -manifolds with automorphism group isomorphic to G G .



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, an automorphism of a complex manifold will always mean a biholomorphism. The main result of this paper, proved in
Section

3
, is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every finite group
G
, there are infinitely many isomorphism classes of closed complex hyperbolic
2
-manifolds with automorphism group isomorphic to
G
.

Greenberg
[
13
]
proved
1.1
for Riemann surfaces, i.e., complex hyperbolic
1
-manifolds. Belolipetsky and the first author
[
6
]
proved the analogue of
1.1
for isometry groups of hyperbolic
n
-manifolds,
n\geq 3
, and this paper answers a question raised in
[
6
, §6.5]
. Our argument follows the broad outline of that paper, but differences at key steps create many new challenges we must overcome.

Recall that a compact complex hyperbolic
n
-manifold is a quotient
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
of the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by a torsion-free cocompact lattice
\Gamma
in the Lie group
\PU(n,1)
. Note that
\mathbb{B}^{1}
is simply the Poincaré disk with the hyperbolic metric. For
n\geq 2
, Mostow rigidity then implies that the automorphism group of a complex hyperbolic manifold is precisely the holomorphic isometry group, and the proof of
1.1
is through this interpretation.

One strong similarity between the proofs is in the input: a cocompact nonarithmetic lattice in the isometry group of the symmetric space. Moreover,
[
6
]
uses a nonabelian free quotient to begin the construction, whereas we use a quotient that is a cocompact Fuchsian group. The subgroup counting methods in both papers are nearly identical. Note that it is quite easy, given a free or surface group quotient of a finite index subgroup
\Lambda
of a lattice
\Gamma
, to produce a finite index subgroup
B
of
\Gamma
so that
N_{\Gamma}(B)/B

contains

G
, where
N_{\Gamma}(B)
is the normalizer of
B
in
\Gamma
, but ensuring that
B
is torsion-free and that
N_{\Gamma}(B)/B
is
exactly

G
is quite subtle.

The nonarithmetic lattices used in
[
6
]
are the famous examples of Gromov and Piatetski-Shapiro
[
14
]
. These lattices naturally have the structure of a free product with amalgamation over the fundamental group of a codimension one totally geodesic subspace, and the nonabelian free quotient of a finite index subgroup is derived from this (see
[
22
]
). In the complex hyperbolic setting, there are no real codimension one totally geodesic subspaces
[
12
, §3.1]
, hence no such splittings.

Our replacement, from the theory of Kähler groups, is the
universal homomorphism
to a direct product of cocompact Fuchsian groups; see
[
15
]
for an introduction. Cocompact nonarithmetic lattices in
\PU(2,1)
with nontrivial universal homomorphism are found among the famous examples of Livné
[
20
]
and Deligne–Mostow
[
8
]
; see
2.4
. With this different input, a key lattice
\Delta
constructed early in the outline of
[
6
]
now fails to be normal in the maximal lattice
\Gamma
in
\PU(2,1)
. In a certain sense, much of the work in
Section

3
is using properties of the universal homomorphism to circumvent this significant obstruction to executing several steps in the strategy from
[
6
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Whether or not there are cocompact nonarithmetic lattices in
\PU(n,1)
for all
n\geq 3
is one of the major open problems in the study of lattices in Lie groups. For any
n\geq 3
, if there is a cocompact nonarithmetic lattice in
\PU(n,1)
with nontrivial universal homomorphism, then this paper produces infinitely many isomorphism classes of closed complex hyperbolic
n
-manifolds with isometry group any finite group
G

verbatim
.

In this paper, a genus
g
surface group will always mean the fundamental group of a closed Riemann surface of genus
g
, despite the slight clash of terminology with fundamental groups of complex surfaces also appearing. In
Section

2.3
we will prove the following result on automorphisms of surface groups, which is an analogue of a theorem of the first author in the free group case
[
21
, Thm. 1]
that may be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T
be a surface group of genus
g\geq 2
,
p
be a prime, and suppose that
\varphi\in\Aut(T)
has the property that
\varphi(N)=N
for all finite index normal subgroups of
T
with index a power of
p
. Then
\varphi
is inner.

Interpretation as a complex projective surface

Compact complex hyperbolic
2
-manifolds are also minimal smooth complex projective surfaces of general type
[
3
, §V.20]
. The appendix to this paper recasts
1.1
in that context to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every finite group
G
, there are infinitely many isomorphism classes of irreducible, minimal smooth complex projective surfaces of general type with automorphism group
G
.

A surface is irreducible if it is not finitely (étale) covered by a product of complex curves. Greenberg’s theorem easily produces products of complex curves with any finite automorphism group, so only the irreducible case is of interest. As described in the appendix, one may be able to construct irreducible examples using other methods. For example, there may exist a nonsingular fibration
X\rightarrow C
over a curve
C
(e.g., a ruled surface) where
\Aut(C)\cong G
by Greenberg and there is a natural isomorphism
\Aut(X)\cong\Aut(C)
. However, we were not able to find a reference for
1.4
in the literature.

Remark 1.5
.

Our construction is also related to a holomorphic map to a smooth projective curve (see
Section

2.2
). However, the bulk of the proof is precisely in showing that our surface has no extra automorphisms, which is highly unclear from the nature of the construction.

Organization of the paper

1.3

Section

Preliminaries

2.1
Lattices in
\PU(2,1)

Let
\mathbb{B}^{2}
denote the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with its Bergman metric of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
-1
. Then the group of holomorphic isometries of
\mathbb{B}^{2}
can be identified with the Lie group
\PU(2,1)
. If
{\Lambda<\PU(2,1)}
is a discrete, cocompact, and torsion-free subgroup, then
\Lambda\backslash\mathbb{B}^{2}
is a complex manifold, and in this case the fact that
\Lambda\backslash\mathbb{B}^{2}
is a smooth projective variety goes back to Kodaira
[
17
]
.

By the famous Margulis dichotomy, a lattice
\Lambda
in
\PU(2,1)
is
arithmetic
if and only if its commensurator

\mathcal{C}(\Lambda)\coloneqq\left\{g\in\PU(2,1)\ :\ \Lambda\textrm{ and }g\Lambda g^{-1}\textrm{ are commensurable}\right\}

(1)

is topologically dense in
\PU(2,1)

[
24
, p. 4]
. The existence of cocompact nonarithmetic lattices was first established by Mostow
[
26
]
, and it is well-known that if
\Lambda
is nonarithmetic then
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda)
is the
unique
maximal lattice in
\PU(2,1)
commensurable with
\Lambda
.

Every lattice
\Lambda<\PU(2,1)
is
integral
in the following sense. Let
k
be the
adjoint trace field
, i.e., the field over
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\{\Tr(\Ad\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda\}
, where
\Ad
is the adjoint representation on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\PU(2,1)
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{k}
denote the ring of integers of
k
. Then there is a vector space
V
over
k
, a
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset V
(i.e., an
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-submodule of full rank), and an isomorphism
V\otimes_{k}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathfrak{g}
for some real embedding of
k
so that

\Ad(\Lambda)\leq\Aut(\mathcal{L})\leq\GL(\mathfrak{g}).

(2)

In particular, there is a linear algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
defined over
k
and a structure on
\mathbf{G}
as a group scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{k}
(defined via the action on
\mathcal{L}
) so that
\Lambda<\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
. See
[
2
, Thm. 1.5]
and
[
30
, Thm. 1 & 3]
for details.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
be the simply connected algebraic group over
k
associated with the preimage
\widetilde{\Lambda}
of
\Lambda
in the simply connected real algebraic group
\SU(2,1)
. Given a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the restriction of reduction

\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}:\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{O}_{k})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})

(3)

to any
\widetilde{\Delta}\leq\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
has kernel the
congruence subgroup

\widetilde{\Delta}(\mathfrak{p})\trianglelefteq\widetilde{\Delta}
. Strong approximation for Zariski dense subgroups of simply connected algebraic groups says that if
\widetilde{\Delta}
is Zariski dense in
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}(k)
, then
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\widetilde{\Delta})=\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
for all but finitely many
\mathfrak{p}

[
31
, Thm. 1.1]
; also see Cor. 3 in Window 9 of
[
23
]
along with discussion on p. 392 implying the more general statement for number fields other than
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\widetilde{\Delta}
is the preimage of
\Delta
in
\SU(2,1)
, then congruence subgroups of
\Delta
are those whose preimage in
\widetilde{\Delta}
are congruence subgroups.

Since
\PU(2,1)
has type
{}^{2}\mathrm{A}_{2}
, there is a quadratic extension
\ell
of
k
canonically associated with
\mathbf{G}
. If
\Delta\leq\Gamma
is Zariski dense in
\PU(2,1)
, then away from a finite set of exceptional primes, the group
\Delta/\Delta(\mathfrak{p})
has commutator subgroup
\PSL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
if
\mathfrak{p}
splits in
\ell
or
\PU(3;\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
if
\mathfrak{p}
is inert in
\ell
. In particular, the congruence quotient of
\Delta
has commutator subgroup a nonabelian finite simple group for all but finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
. For example in the split case, observe that
\Delta/\Delta(\mathfrak{p})
is then naturally a subgroup of
\PGL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
containing
\PSL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
, so it has trivial center.

2.2
Kähler groups

A
Kähler group
is the fundamental group of a compact Kähler manifold. See
[
1
,
28
]
for basics on Kähler groups. Note that finite index subgroups of Kähler groups are naturally Kähler groups.

Let
\Gamma<\PU(2,1)
be a cocompact lattice, possibly with torsion. Since
\PU(2,1)
is a linear Lie group and
\Gamma
is finitely generated,
\Gamma
is residually finite and thus there is a finite index subgroup
\Lambda<\Gamma
that is torsion-free. Since smooth projective varieties are compact Kähler manifolds, by pullback of the Fubini–Study metric on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
under a projective embedding, it follows that any such
\Lambda
is a Kähler group. It then follows from
[
1
, Lem. 1.1.15]
that all cocompact lattices in
\PU(2,1)
are Kähler groups.

Fundamental to this paper is the
universal homomorphism
attached to any Kähler group. See
[
15
, §2.C]
for discussion and references for the following result defining the universal homomorphism.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\Lambda
is a Kähler group. Then there is a (possibly empty) finite collection of cocompact Fuchsian groups
\{T_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}}
and a homomorphism

\rho:\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow T\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}}T_{\alpha}

(4)

such that:

1.

For each
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}
with projection
p_{\alpha}:T\rightarrow T_{\alpha}
, the composition
p_{\alpha}\circ\rho
is surjective.

2.

Every surjective homomorphism from
\Lambda
to a cocompact Fuchsian group factors through some
p_{\alpha}\circ\rho
.

3.

A surjective homomorphism
\sigma:\Lambda\rightarrow T^{\prime}
onto a cocompact Fuchsian group
T^{\prime}
has finitely generated kernel if and only if there is some
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}
and an isomorphism
\psi:T_{\alpha}\rightarrow T^{\prime}
so that
\sigma=\psi\circ p_{\alpha}\circ\rho
.

The
T_{\alpha}
are called the
direct factors
of
\rho
. When
\Lambda
has no homomorphisms onto cocompact Fuchsian groups, note that the universal homomorphism is simply the trivial homomorphism to the trivial group. When
\mathfrak{U}\neq\varnothing
, we will say that
\Lambda
has
nontrivial
universal homomorphism. Moreover, we further partition the factors of the universal homomorphism as
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}\sqcup\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, where
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}
is the (possibly empty) set of
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}
so that
T_{\alpha}
is a torsion-free Fuchsian group, i.e., a genus
g
surface group for some
g\geq 2
. Then define

\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}:\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\prod_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}}T_{\alpha}

(5)

to be the
torsion-free universal homomorphism
. The torsion-free case of the following simple lemma will prove important later.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Kähler group,
\rho
be its universal homomorphism, and
\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}
be the torsion-free universal homomorphism for
\Lambda
. Then
\ker(\rho)
and
\ker(\rho_{\mathrm{tf}})
are characteristic subgroups of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{U}
or
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}
is empty, then the respective conclusion is immediate. Now suppose
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}
with factor
T_{\alpha}
and
\rho_{\alpha}\coloneqq p_{\alpha}\circ\rho
is the homomorphism from
\Lambda
onto
T_{\alpha}
. Given any
\varphi\in\Aut(\Lambda)
,
\rho_{\alpha}\circ\varphi
is a homomorphism onto
T_{\alpha}
with finitely generated kernel
\varphi^{-1}(\ker(\rho_{\alpha}))
. Thus there is a
\varphi(\alpha)\in\mathfrak{U}
and an isomorphism
\psi:T_{\alpha}\rightarrow T_{\varphi(\alpha)}
so that
\rho_{\alpha}\circ\varphi=\psi\circ\rho_{\varphi(\alpha)}
, and therefore

\varphi^{-1}(\ker(\rho_{\alpha}))=\ker(\rho_{\alpha}\circ\varphi)=\ker(\rho_{\varphi(\alpha)}).

(6)

Note that
\varphi(\alpha)\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}
if and only if
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}
. Considering
\rho_{\varphi^{-1}(\beta)}
, every
\beta\in\mathfrak{U}
is of the form
\varphi(\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}
. Thus

\displaystyle\varphi(\ker(\rho))

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}}\varphi(\ker(\rho_{\varphi(\alpha)}))

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}}\ker(\rho_{\varphi(\alpha)})

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\beta\in\mathfrak{U}}\ker(\rho_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\ker(\rho)

and similarly for the intersection over
\alpha\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{tf}}
. This proves that
\ker(\rho)
and
\ker(\rho_{\mathrm{tf}})
are indeed characteristic in
\Lambda
.
∎

Lemma 2.3

is isomorphic to a factor of the universal homomorphism for

∎

2.3
Automorphisms of Fuchsian groups

The purpose of this subsection is to prove
1.3
.

Proof of
1.3
.

Let
p
be a prime,
T
be a surface group of genus
g\geq 2
, and
\phi\in\Aut(T)
have the property that
\phi(N)=N
for every finite index normal subgroup with index a power of
p
. Consider the pro-
p

T_{\widehat{p}}
of

. Then
\varphi
extends to a continuous automorphism of
T_{\widehat{p}}
that is
normal
, i.e., so that
\varphi(\widehat{N})
for every closed normal subgroup of
T_{\widehat{p}}
. Indeed, every such normal subgroup is an intersection of finite index normal subgroups of
T_{\widehat{p}}
, and finite index normal subgroups of

are in natural one-to-one correspondence with the normal
p

.

Then

is topologically finitely presented, since

is finitely presented, hence

is

-free
in the sense of Jarden–Ritter (see
[

, §1]
for the definition) by
[

, Thm. 4]
. Thus the extension of
\varphi
to

is inner by
[

, Thm. 1]
. Moreover, Paris proved that

is
p

[

, Thm. 1.7]
, which implies that
\varphi
must preserve conjugacy classes of

(i.e., it is

). Therefore
\varphi
is a normal automorphism of

, meaning that it preserves all normal subgroups of

. Finally, it is a theorem of Bogopolski, Kudryavtseva, and Zieschang that normal automorphisms of higher-genus surface groups are inner
[

, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3
The main result

Suppose that
\Lambda<\PU(2,1)
is a cocompact nonarithmetic lattice with nontrivial universal homomorphism
\rho
. Following
Section

2.1
, let
\Gamma<\PU(2,1)
be the unique maximal lattice containing
\Lambda
. By
2.3
and by passing to a finite index subgroup if necessary, we can assume that the universal homomorphism for
\Lambda
has a torsion-free factor and that
\Lambda
is a characteristic subgroup of
\Gamma
. If
M_{\mathrm{tf}}\coloneqq\ker(\rho_{\mathrm{tf}})
denotes the kernel of the torsion-free universal homomorphism for
\Lambda
, then
M_{\mathrm{tf}}
is an infinite index characteristic subgroup of
\Gamma
by
2.2
and because a characteristic subgroup of a characteristic subgroup is characteristic in the larger group. Set
T\coloneqq\Lambda/M_{\mathrm{tf}}
, so
T=\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}(\Lambda)
.

As described in
Section

2.1
, away from finitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of the ring
\Tr\Ad\Gamma
, there is a well-defined congruence quotient
\widehat{Q}\coloneqq\Gamma/\Gamma(\mathfrak{p})
with trivial center and commutator subgroup
Q
that is a nonabelian finite simple group. Moreover,
\Lambda
and
M_{\mathrm{tf}}
are Zariski dense in
\PU(2,1)
, hence after possibly passing to a finite index subgroup of
\Lambda
, we can assume that they both map onto
Q
for infinitely many
\mathfrak{p}
. Fix one such
\mathfrak{p}
for the remainder of this section. Then define
\Lambda(\mathfrak{p})\coloneqq\Gamma(\mathfrak{p})\cap\Lambda
and
M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})\coloneqq\Gamma(\mathfrak{p})\cap M_{\mathrm{tf}}
, noting that both
\Lambda(\mathfrak{p})
and
M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})
are normal subgroups of
\Gamma
, since each is the intersection of normal subgroups of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the notation established in this section,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}(\Lambda(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle=T

\displaystyle\Lambda/M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})

\displaystyle\cong Q\times T

\displaystyle\Gamma/M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})

\displaystyle\cong\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{T}

where
\widehat{T}\coloneqq\Gamma/M_{\mathrm{tf}}
contains
T
as a finite index subgroup.

Proof.

The first equality holds since
M_{\mathrm{tf}}
contains coset representatives for
\Lambda(\mathfrak{p})
in
\Lambda
. The second holds since
M_{\mathrm{tf}}/M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\Lambda(\mathfrak{p})/M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})
are disjoint normal subgroups of
\Lambda/M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})
, and the third is similar.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
T
is then a product of factors of the torsion-free universal homomorphism of
\Lambda(\mathfrak{p})
, but there may be additional factors.

In what follows,
\pi:T\rightarrow T_{r}
will denote projection onto the
r^{th}
surface group factor of
T
for an arbitrary
r
that will be fixed for the remainder of this section. Let
\sigma
denote projection of
\Gamma
onto
\Gamma/M_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathfrak{p})

Appendix: Automorphism groups of surfaces

The purpose of this appendix is to give a short account regarding smooth projective surfaces with finite automorphism group. We break up the discussion according to the Enriques–Kodaira classification of surfaces by their Kodaira dimension. To begin, we note that a projective surface is
minimal
if it does not contain a rational curve (i.e.,
\mathbb{P}^{1}
) of self-intersection
-1
,
birationality
is the equivalence relation defined by having isomorphic dense open subvarieties, and every smooth projective surface is birational to a minimal surface. For a basic reference on the geometry of surfaces and the Kodaira–Enriques classification, see
[
5
,
3
]
.

Kodaira dimension
-\infty

A surface is rational if it is birational to the complex projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and the group of birational automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the
Cremona group

\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Any finite group of automorphisms of a rational surface then gives a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. As with the automorphism group
\PGL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
has the
Jordan property
: Every finite subgroup has an abelian subgroup of universally bounded index. See
[
11
, §4, §5, §10]
for a complete classification. Thus there are many finite groups that cannot even be subgroups of the automorphism group of a rational surface.

The surfaces of Kodaira dimension
-\infty
that are not rational are the
ruled surfaces
, which are
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundles
\pi:X\rightarrow C
over a curve
C
of genus
g\geq 1
. If
X
is not rational, it has automorphism group fitting into an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Aut_{C}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Aut(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Aut(C)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}

(47)

with
\Aut_{C}(X)
the subgroup commuting with projection
[
25
, Lem. 6]
. Moreover, there are examples with
\Aut_{C}(X)
naturally trivial; see
[
25
, §2]
. In particular, we believe that there should be irrational ruled surfaces with any finite automorphism group, where the isomorphism
\Aut(X)\cong G
is simply induced by Greenberg’s theorem and an isomorphism
\Aut(X)\cong\Aut(C)
. However, we could not find a precise statement to this effect in the literature.

As mentioned above for rational surfaces, if any two surfaces

are birational, then

is naturally a subgroup of the group of birational automorphisms of

Theorem

(Thm. V.19

]
)

The group of birational automorphisms of a nonruled minimal surface that is not rational coincides with its automorphism group.

Kodaira dimension
0

An abelian surface has infinite automorphism group, since it acts on itself by translation. Bielliptic surfaces are of the form
(E_{1}\times E_{2})/G
, where
G
acts on
E_{1}
with fixed points and on
E_{2}
by translations. Thus any infinite order translation of

commutes with

and descends to an infinite order automorphism of the bielliptic surface. Thus the only surfaces of Kodaira dimension zero that can have finite automorphism group are K3 and Enriques surfaces.

Kondō showed that an algebraic K3 surface

with finite automorphism group can only have

trivial,
\mathbb{Z}/2
,
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
,

,

Kodaira dimension
1

Surfaces of Kodaira dimension

properly elliptic fibrations

Surfaces of general type

Bayesian and frequentist perspectives agree on dynamical dark energy
Laura Herold, Tanvi Karwal

Abstract
Baryon acoustic oscillation data from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) show evidence of a deviation from a cosmological constant Λ \Lambda within a Bayesian analysis. In this work, we validate that frequentist constraints from profile likelihoods on the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder parameters w 0 w_{0} , w a w_{a} are in excellent agreement with the Bayesian constraints when combining with Planck cosmic microwave background, Planck and Atacama Cosmology Telescope lensing, and either Pantheon+ or Dark Energy Survey Y5 supernova data. Further, we assess which datasets drive these constraints by considering the contributions to the χ 2 \chi^{2} from the individual datasets. For profile likelihoods of the matter fraction Ω m \Omega_{\mathrm{m}} , such an investigation shows internal inconsistencies when assuming Λ \Lambda , which are resolved when assuming a w 0 ​ w a w_{0}w_{a} dark-energy model. We infer the equations of state w ⁡ ( z ) w(z) at the pivot redshifts, supporting previous interpretations that current data appears to be more sensitive to the derivative of w ⁡ ( z ) w(z) rather than a mean offset from Λ \Lambda . Thus our frequentist analysis corroborates previous findings on dynamical DE.

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle=-0.42\pm 0.21\,\,{\rm and}

Given the relevance of these results, in this work, we verify whether the preference for dynamical DE is robust under a change from Bayesian to frequentist parameter-inference methods (see also
Tang et al. 2025
;
Staicova 2025

Frequentist parameter-inference methods based on profile likelihoods are sparingly applied in cosmology, in part due to their higher numerical cost, but they have recently received increased attention for their ability to provide complementary information to Bayesian approaches.
While Bayesian credible intervals rely on a choice of prior on all model parameters and handle nuisance parameters through integration (“marginalization”), frequentist confidence intervals do not necessarily require a prior and handle nuisance parameters through maximization.
This difference in prior-selection and nuisance-parameter treatment becomes particularly important for models that are poorly constrained by the available data, such as when the likelihood is flat or deviates significantly from a Gaussian distribution.
In a Bayesian analysis, this can lead to prior(-volume) effects (see e.g.
Gariazzo et al. 2018
;
Smith et al. 2021
;
Herold et al. 2022
;
Herold and Ferreira 2023
;
Gariazzo et al. 2022
;
Poulin et al. 2023
;
Holm et al. 2023
;
Karwal et al. 2024
for examples in a cosmology context).
These complementary approaches converge in the limit of large datasets.

In this work we supplement the Bayesian constraints in the literature with frequentist constraints obtained with profile likelihoods.
Our goal is to verify whether the frequentist constraints on the CPL parameters
w_{0}\,,w_{a}
agree with their Bayesian counterparts. The results of this paper are summarized in

In a nutshell, a profile likelihood over the parameter

\theta\in[w_{0},w_{a},\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}]

\Delta\chi^{2}(\theta)=-2\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}(\theta,\hat{\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}})}{\mathcal{L}(\hat{\theta},\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}})}\right)\,,

(2)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the likelihood and
\boldsymbol{\nu}
denotes all remaining cosmology and nuisance parameters.
The denominator denotes the likelihood

\mathcal{L}(\hat{\theta},\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}})

at the global maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) or “best fit”
(\hat{\theta},\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}})

The numerator denotes the likelihood
\mathcal{L}(\theta,\hat{\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}})
of the conditional MLE

\hat{\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}

\boldsymbol{\nu}
for a fixed value of
\theta

In other words, the numerator maximizes the likelihood over all other parameters
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}

\theta

For a Gaussian likelihood

2
indeed follows a

-distribution.
Thus the profile likelihood is obtained by “profiling” i.e. maximizing the likelihood, or equivalently minimizing the

over all
\boldsymbol{\nu}

\theta

IV.2
Equation of state at the pivot redshift

Projecting the dynamical DE constraints onto an alternate parameter space defined by a pivot redshift
z_{\mathrm{p}}
and equation of state
w_{\mathrm{p}}
at this redshift, constraints seem to favour
w_{\mathrm{p}}\sim-1

Adame et al. 2024
.
This raised questions about the robustness of evidence for dynamical DE
Cortês and Liddle 2024
;
Efstathiou 2025a
.
Here, we explore projections into the
w_{\mathrm{p}}-z_{\mathrm{p}}
parameterization with profile likelihoods.

Equation

1
represents an expansion of the equation of state of DE
w(a)
around the current scale factor
a=1
.
Since BAO and SNe data are not particularly sensitive to
a=1
, but to slightly higher redshifts
z\gtrsim 0.1
(
a\lesssim 0.9
),
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
are usually strongly correlated.
This is illustrated in the top panel of
Fig.

6

\hat{\hat{w}}_{a}
of
w_{a}

w_{0}

As suggested by
Huterer and Turner 2001
(see also
Albrecht et al. 2006
;
Linder 2006
;
Martin and Albrecht 2006
), it is therefore useful to define a pivot scale
a_{\mathrm{p}}
such that
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
de-correlate for the dataset at hand.
The equation of state
w_{\mathrm{p}}
at the pivot scale
a_{\mathrm{p}}
can then be expressed as

w_{\mathrm{p}}=w_{0}+w_{a}\,(1-a_{\mathrm{p}})\,,

(5)

i.e. the model is reparameterization-invariant under a change of pivot scale.
Previous studies have found that the equation of state at the pivot redshift under CMB, DESI BAO (and SN) data is consistent with
w_{\mathrm{p}}=-1

Adame et al. 2024
;
Cortês and Liddle 2024
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
.

To explore this with both Bayesian and frequentist methods, we compute the pivot scale as detailed in
Albrecht et al. 2006
.
The pivot scale
a_{\mathrm{p}}
is determined as the scale factor that minimizes the standard deviation
\sigma_{w_{\mathrm{p}}}
of
w_{\mathrm{p}}
in
Eq.

5
, retrieving the best-constrained direction on the
w_{0}-w_{a}
plane. This yields
Albrecht et al. 2006

1-a_{\mathrm{p}}=-\frac{\mathrm{cov}(w_{0}w_{a})}{\sigma_{w_{a}}^{2}}\,,

(6)

where
\sigma_{w_{a}}^{2}
is the standard deviation of
w_{a}
and
\mathrm{cov}(w_{0}w_{a})
denotes the covariance between
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
, i.e. the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix between
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
.
We compute the pivot redshifts
1+z_{\mathrm{p}}=1/a_{\mathrm{p}}
from the covariance matrices obtained from the MCMC runs for the four dataset combinations considered in this work and quote these in the first column of
Table

.
The pivot redshifts are in broad agreement with the ones quoted in
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
, which find
z_{\mathrm{p}}=0.53
for CMB and DESI, and
z_{\mathrm{p}}=0.31
for CMB, DESI and DES Y5.

As noted in previous studies
Adame et al. 2024
;
Cortês and Liddle 2024
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
;
Efstathiou 2025a
based on Bayesian methods, we find that the equations of state at the pivot redshifts are close to
w_{\mathrm{p}}\approx-1
. Thus current data seem to be more sensitive to the derivative,
w_{a}\neq 0
, rather than a mean offset
w_{\mathrm{p}}\neq-1
.
Hence preferred models start off in the “phantom regime”,
w(z)<-1
, and transition to the non-phantom regime around
z_{\mathrm{p}}
, which can be interpreted as a new “coincidence problem”
Cortês and Liddle 2024
.

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we complemented the Bayesian credible intervals on the CPL parameters
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
with frequentist confidence intervals based on the graphical profile-likelihood method.
We found that frequentist constraints are in excellent agreement with Bayesian constraints (Fig.
1
).
Shifts of the best fits compared to the posterior means are smaller than
0.2\,\sigma
and the sizes of the frequentist and Bayesian intervals agree within
8\%
.
This reaffirms the robustness of the constraints on dynamical DE under a change of statistical method and shows no impact from
prior-volume effects.

We further assessed which datasets drive the constraints by exploring the contributions to the
\chi^{2}
from the individual datasets.
While all datasets show a mild but consistent preference for
w_{0}>-1
and
w_{a}<0
, the constraining power is dominated by the BAO and SN data. The improvement in fit compared to
\Lambda
CDM is strongest in DES Y5 data (
\Delta\chi^{2}\simeq-11
) when combined with CMB and BAO for 2 extra degrees of freedom (see
Efstathiou 2025b
;
Vincenzi et al. 2025
for a discussion of this SNe sample).
DESI, CMB and Pantheon+ share a similar level of preference,
-5<\Delta\chi^{3}<-3
.
This preference for the
w_{0}w_{a}
model is driven by mild discrepancies in the preferred values of the matter fraction
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}
of the different data sets (
Fig.

4
), which are resolved within
w_{0}w_{a}
, in agreement with previous Bayesian works
Berghaus et al. 2024
;
Tang et al. 2025
.
Varying
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}
within
\Lambda
CDM can only modulate the amplitude and slope of the BAO observables
D_{M}
,
D_{A}
, while the
w_{0}wa
model allows for more freedom to fit the DESI BAO data (
Fig.

5
).

We also confirmed that the equations of state
w_{\mathrm{p}}
of DE at the pivot redshifts, which corresponds to the best-constrained
w(z)
for the respective data, are close to
-1
(
Fig.

6
) with a frequentist approach. Thus, the data appear to be more sensitive to the derivative of
w(z)
than a mean offset from
w=-1
, also in agreement with Bayesian investigations
Adame et al. 2024
;
Cortês and Liddle 2024
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
;
Efstathiou 2025a
.

Altogether, in this paper, we employed primarily frequentist methods and found that our results align remarkably well with those reported in the literature using predominantly Bayesian approaches
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
.
Future data will be crucial in determining whether this challenge to the
\Lambda
CDM model persists.

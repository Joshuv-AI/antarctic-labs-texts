Evaluation of Artificial Intelligence-Based Diagnostic Tools for Accurate Detection and Classification of Oral Lesions: A Clinical Study
Authors: Khushali Shah, Ankit Dhimole, Neetu Pandey, Robium Naorem, Rangoli Srivastava, Sameer Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Early detection and accurate classification of oral lesions are essential for effective clinical management and prevention of malignant transformation. Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) have led to the development of diagnostic tools aimed at enhancing clinical decision-making.Methods:A prospective clinical study was conducted involving 180 patients presenting with suspicious oral lesions at a tertiary dental care center. Digital images of lesions were captured and analyzed using an AI-powered diagnostic software trained on a dataset of over 10,000 labeled images. Each case was also evaluated by two oral pathologists, followed by histopathological confirmation. Sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, and Cohen’s kappa coefficient were calculated to assess diagnostic concordance.Results:The AI system achieved an overall diagnostic accuracy of 91.1% (±4.3), sensitivity of 93.6%, and specificity of 89.2%. The concordance between AI and histopathological diagnosis was substantial (κ =0.83; P < 0.001). The highest performance was observed in detecting leukoplakia (95.2% accuracy) and oral lichen planus (92.4%). Misclassification occurred primarily in cases of mild dysplasia and nonspecific ulcerations.Conclusion:The AI-based diagnostic tool demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy and substantial agreement with histopathological findings. It shows promise as a chairside adjunct to enhance clinical decision-making and early detection of potentially malignant oral lesions.

INTRODUCTION

Oral lesions, including benign, premalignant, and malignant conditions, pose significant diagnostic challenges in clinical dentistry. Early and accurate diagnosis is crucial for prompt management and improved prognosis, especially in high-risk populations.[1] Conventional diagnostic approaches rely heavily on clinical expertise and histopathological examination, the latter often being invasive, time-consuming, and inaccessible in underserved areas.[2]

With recent advances in machine learning and computer vision, artificial intelligence (AI)-based tools have emerged as promising adjuncts in health care, including dermatology, radiology, and oncology.[3] In dentistry, AI applications are gaining traction, especially in imaging analysis and diagnostic support. Several studies have explored convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for recognizing oral pathologies with promising results.[4]

However, most available AI models are either trained on limited datasets or validated only under experimental conditions, lacking real-world clinical evaluation. Moreover, inconsistencies in AI model performance, particularly in differentiating between lesions with overlapping clinical features, have been reported.[5]

AI diagnostic tool

The AI system (OralScanX v2.5) used a CNN architecture trained on a curated dataset of 10,000 annotated oral lesion images. It categorized lesions into six classes: leukoplakia, erythroplakia, oral lichen planus, traumatic ulcer, squamous cell carcinoma, and benign growths.

Procedure

Each patient underwent standardized intraoral photography using a 12 MP DSLR under ambient light. Images were uploaded into the AI platform for real-time classification. Simultaneously, two experienced oral pathologists independently provided provisional diagnoses blinded to AI outcomes. All patients subsequently underwent incisional or excisional biopsy, with histopathological findings considered the gold standard.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS v26. Diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV of AI and clinical diagnoses were calculated. Cohen’s kappa was used to measure agreement between diagnostic modalities. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

The study demonstrated that AI-based diagnostic tools, specifically OralScanX v2.5, can achieve high accuracy in detecting and classifying common oral lesions. The AI outperformed conventional clinical diagnosis in both sensitivity and specificity, aligning with findings from previous in vitro models and image-based studies.[6]

A study by Fu et al. reported 89.6% diagnostic accuracy of a deep learning model in differentiating leukoplakia and SCC,[7] consistent with the 91.1% accuracy in our clinical setting. Similarly, AI-based support systems in dermatopathology and ophthalmology have also shown kappa values >0.80 for diagnostic agreement, highlighting the cross-disciplinary reliability of machine learning models.[8]

However, our findings revealed certain limitations. AI performance was slightly lower in distinguishing mild dysplasia from benign ulcers, a pattern noted in other studies due to overlapping morphologic features.[9] The tool also struggled with underrepresented lesion types, emphasizing the need for expanded and balanced training datasets.

Importantly, the AI tool functioned in real-time and provided results within 10 seconds, suggesting potential integration into routine chairside diagnostics. It could significantly benefit general dental practitioners and rural healthcare centers lacking histopathological infrastructure.[10]

CONCLUSION

This clinical study demonstrated that AI-based diagnostic systems can significantly enhance the detection and classification accuracy of oral lesions when compared to conventional methods. Integration of such tools into routine practice may improve early diagnosis, reduce delays, and optimize patient care. Further large-scale multicentric trials are recommended to validate these findings and refine model performance.

Endoscopic endonasal resection of transinfundibular craniopharyngiomas using a vertical infundibular split with subpial excavation: Case instruction and operative video
Authors: Yusuke Morinaga, Takeshi Hongo, Sotaro Oshida, Shunsuke Shibao, Yasuhiro Tsunemi, Takashi Kashiwagi, Tsuguhisa Nakayama, Hiroyoshi Akutsu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Radical resection of transinfundibular craniopharyngiomas (TCs) while preserving endocrine function remains a major surgical challenge because of their origin within the pituitary stalk. The “vertical infundibular split with subpial excavation” (VISSE) technique in endoscopic endonasal surgery (EES) is an effective approach that may enable gross total resection (GTR) in selected cases while aiming to preserve pituitary stalk integrity and neuroendocrine function. This procedure involves a vertical split of the pia mater of the pituitary stalk at sites with sparse pituitary portal veins, followed by subpial circumferential dissection along the tumor–stalk interface.Methods: We report two patients with TC who underwent EES using the VISSE technique, achieving GTR with pituitary stalk preservation. Case 1 was a 68-year-old woman with an 18-mm solid TC compressing the optic chiasm and involving the pituitary stalk. Case 2 was a 53-year-old man with a 65-mm mixed solid–cystic TC extending toward the frontal lobe.Results: Postoperatively, Case 1 maintained stable endocrine function without replacement therapy, suggesting preservation of both anterior and posterior pituitary function. Case 2 developed permanent diabetes insipidus and growth hormone deficiency, whereas other anterior pituitary axes were preserved.Conclusion: The VISSE technique may enable GTR while preserving pituitary function to varying degrees in selected TC cases.

INTRODUCTION

Recent advances in endoscopic endonasal surgery (EES) for treating craniopharyngiomas have improved resection and reduced perioperative complications.[2,3,5,8,12,15,22,23,25] However, treating craniopharyngiomas remains challenging owing to their proximity to critical anatomical structures and key vascular structures. Damage to these structures can cause ischemic or hemorrhagic complications, visual impairment, and endocrine dysfunction.[1,19]

Craniopharyngiomas are classified as infradiaphragmatic or supradiaphragmatic,[13] with supradiaphragmatic tumors further subcategorized into preinfundibular, transinfundibular, retroinfundibular, and intraventricular subtypes based on their anatomical relationship to the pituitary stalk.[12] Recent advancements in EES have extended its application from infra- to supradiaphragmatic tumors, providing a minimally invasive alternative to craniotomy by enabling direct visualization of critical structures, including optic nerves and pituitary stalk.[20] Surgical successes largely depend on meticulous alignment of the operative trajectory with tumor extension. In complex cases, including retroinfundibular or intraventricular craniopharyngiomas, techniques such as posterior clinoidectomy and pituitary transposition[7] have enabled gross total resection (GTR) while preserving the pituitary stalk. However, resecting transinfundibular craniopharyngiomas (TCs) remains particularly challenging owing to their origin within the pituitary stalk, necessitating innovative approaches to achieve radical tumor resection while preserving endocrine function. The “vertical infundibular split with subpial excavation” (VISSE) technique involves a vertical split of the pia mater on the pituitary stalk at areas with sparse pituitary portal veins identified based on intraoperative visual findings, followed by dissection circumferentially along the boundary between the thinned membranous stalk tissue and the tumor. After dissection, the tumor is meticulously resected to ensure complete removal while maintaining the structural integrity of the pituitary stalk.

CASE DESCRIPTION

Case 1

A 68-year-old woman, being evaluated for chronic headaches, was incidentally diagnosed with craniopharyngioma and was referred to our hospital. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed an 18-mm solid suprasellar lesion compressing the optic chiasm and involving the pituitary stalk [Figure 1]. Preoperative endocrine function was assessed using baseline values without conducting stimulation tests. Insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) was below the normal range (−1 standard deviation), while other parameters were normal. The patient underwent EES using the VISSE technique [Figure 2].

Pre and postoperative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of Case 1. a: Preoperative coronal MRI showing an enhancing lesion along the pituitary stalk, suggesting tumor involvement. b: Preoperative sagittal MRI demonstrating the suprasellar tumor arising from the pituitary stalk. c: Postoperative coronal MRI showing complete tumor resection (arrow). d: Postoperative sagittal MRI demonstrating preservation of the pituitary stalk (arrow). MRI: Magnetic resonance imaging.

The surgical details of Case 1 are as follows.

Following a wide anterior sphenoidotomy, a bone window was created at the sellar floor to the anterior skull base. After dural incision, the pituitary stalk was identified at the midline [Figure 2a]. The pituitary stalk was expanded due to the tumor, a typical feature of TC. The stalk was vertically incised where the pituitary portal veins were sparse, following gentle coagulation [Figure 2b]. Using subpial dissection, the tumor was carefully circumferentially separated from the pituitary stalk[Figure 2c]. Internal decompression was performed as needed. After completing circumferential dissection, the feeding artery was coagulated and cut, achieving GTR [Figure 2d] [Video 1].

The stalk is expanded by the tumor inside the stalk, which is a typical finding of transinfundibular craniopharyngioma. b: The stalk is vertically incised at a region where pituitary portal veins are sparse following gentle coagulation. c: Using a subpial dissection technique, the tumor is carefully separated from the pituitary stalk in a circumferential manner. d: After completing circumferential dissection, gross total resection is achieved while preserving the pituitary stalk (arrow).

The procedure was concluded after multilayered cranial base reconstruction using a sutured fascia lata graft and sphenoid sinus mucosal flap.[9] No postoperative complication occurred. Three months later, endocrine stimulation tests with thyrotropin-releasing hormone, gonadotropin-releasing hormone, and adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) revealed delayed or subnormal peak responses in thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), luteinizing hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone, and prolactin. However, ACTH responded with a more than two-fold increase. In addition, growth hormone (GH) showed a peak >9 ng/mL on GH releasing peptide-2 (GHRP-2) loading, indicating preserved hypothalamic– pituitary–adrenal and GH axes. Hydrocortisone and desmopressin were successfully tapered and discontinued by 3 months after EES. Therefore, posterior lobe pituitary function was also preserved. The patient has remained free of all hormone replacement therapies, maintaining stable endocrine function, with normalized thyroid hormone levels and no signs of hypothalamic dysfunction. MRI at 3 months postoperatively [Figure 1] and 27-month follow-up showed no recurrence. The patient remains neurologically stable, with no signs of hypothalamic dysfunction, including cognitive decline or weight gain, and has returned to work, maintaining an independent and active lifestyle.

Case 2

A 53-year-old man presented with progressive bilateral visual deterioration due to a giant mixed solid-cystic craniopharyngioma (65 mm) extending toward the frontal lobe [Figure 3]. His preoperative best-corrected visual acuity was approximately 20/220 and 20/670 in the right and left eyes, respectively. Baseline hormonal values were normal, although no dynamic endocrine testing was performed preoperatively. Although a combined approach[10] with an endoscopic supraorbital keyhole technique had been planned, complete tumor removal was ultimately achieved through the endoscopic endonasal approach alone. A bone window was created from the sellar floor to the anterior skull base, the dura was incised, and the tumor beneath the arachnoid was exposed. The pre-infundibular cystic component was fenestrated and decompressed. Under a 30-degree endoscope, the cyst wall was dissected from the surrounding structures, including the frontal lobe, optic nerves, and anterior cerebral arteries. After resecting the preinfundibular part, the pituitary stalk was expanded due to the tumor invading into the pituitary stalk, showing the typical finding of transinfundibular craniopharyngioma. The stalk was vertically incised at a site with sparse portal veins, and circumferential subpial dissection was performed along the tumor -stalk interface using the Vertical Infundibular Split and Subpial Excavation technique. A 70-degree endoscope was used to remove residual tumor from the posterior lobe, achieving gross total resection while preserving the pituitary stalk. Intraoperative indocyanine green angiography was performed at the end of the procedure to confirm preserved blood flow from the superior hypophyseal arteries to the optic chiasm and pituitary stalk [Video 1]. He underwent EES using the VISSE technique [Figure 4 and Video 1].

Postoperatively, he developed permanent diabetes insipidus and was started on desmopressin, which has been continued to date. At 2 weeks postoperatively, endocrine stimulation tests were performed. The GHRP-2 test showed a normal GH response; however, the arginine stimulation test revealed a peak GH level of <1.0 ng/mL with low IGF-1 (93 ng/mL), confirming severe GH deficiency. Other anterior pituitary axes, including ACTH, TSH, and the gonadotropins, responded normally. GH replacement therapy has now been initiated. Hydrocortisone is currently maintained at 10 mg daily, with plans for discontinuation following repeat stimulation testing.

MRI at 3 and 15 months postoperatively revealed no tumor recurrence [Figure 3]. At 9 months, best-corrected visual acuity was 20/250 and 20/22 in the right and left eyes, respectively, with mild improvement in the right visual field and marked improvement in both acuity and field on the left, indicating overall visual recovery. No signs of hypothalamic dysfunction, including obesity or memory impairment, were observed. The patient successfully returned to work and leads an independent and active life.

DISCUSSION

TCs are particularly challenging to treat. Although EES achieves GTR rates of 60–80%, traditional techniques frequently result in permanent endocrine dysfunction in up to 60% of cases.[26]

In a recent study on pediatric craniopharyngiomas, including infradiaphragmatic tumors, postoperative preservation of some endocrine function was achieved in 56% of cases following morphological preservation of the pituitary stalk.[13] Conversely, preserving endocrine function is difficult in cases of supradiaphragmatic tumors.[26] The pituitary stalk is frequently unidentifiable in TCs, and its preservation is significantly lower than that in non-TC cases, leading to a higher risk of postoperative endocrine dysfunction.[26] The VISSE technique utilizes the anatomy of the pituitary stalk to preserve neuroendocrine structures. Importantly, here,anterior pituitary function was at least partially preserved. In Case 1, the patient showed delayed or suboptimal responses in several pituitary axes on the dynamic endocrine testing 3 months postoperatively; however, they later normalized, with the patient clinically stable after discontinuing hormone replacement. In Case 2, diabetes insipidus and GH deficiency persisted, requiring long-term replacement therapy postoperatively.

The VISSE technique is based on subperineural or subcapsular methods used to preserve cranial nerve function in vestibular schwannoma surgeries,[17,24] enabling delicate tissue manipulation and optimal preservation of functional structures. Recent advancements, including high-definition endoscopy, intraoperative navigation,[4] and vascularized flap reconstruction,[6,9] have significantly improved the safety and efficacy of EES. We used intraoperative indocyanine green angiography to assess the patency of the superior hypophyseal artery feeding the optic nerves and pituitary stalk in Case 2; this technique may further enhance vascular integrity assessment during surgery, reducing ischemic complications and optimizing endocrine preservation.[16] A multidisciplinary team of neurosurgeons and otolaryngologists performs a wide sphenoidotomy, prioritizing nasal and sinus function preservation: [18] Otolaryngologists manage the nasal phase of the procedure, whereas neurosurgeons perform skull base bone removal, including the sellar floor, followed by intradural procedures.[10,11,14,18,21] In our view, this approach optimally enhances the efficacy and safety of the VISSE technique. Another important consideration concerns the indications and patient selection for the VISSE technique. In the present report, we applied this technique to TCs, in which the tumor arises within and expands the pituitary stalk. In such cases, a vertical infundibular split allows direct access to the tumor through the thinned stalk tissue, followed by circumferential subpial dissection along the tumor–stalk interface. This technique can theoretically be applied to TCs regardless of tumor size or extent.

However, the fundamental concept of the technique – namely vertical splitting of the pia mater of the thinned pituitary stalk and subpial excavation along the natural cleavage plane – may be applicable more broadly to suprasellar craniopharyngiomas. Even in preinfundibular or retroinfundibular tumors, the lesion is typically covered by a thin layer of pia mater continuous with the pituitary stalk or hypothalamic surface, allowing careful subpial dissection along this anatomical layer. Therefore, although a true infundibular split is specifically relevant to TCs, the principle of vertical splitting combined with subpial excavation may be applicable to a wide range of suprasellar craniopharyngiomas. Ideal candidates include tumors demonstrating a clear subpial interface with the stalk or hypothalamus on preoperative imaging or intraoperative findings, limited hypothalamic invasion, and a configuration in which the tumor is covered by a relatively preserved pial layer that allows safe circumferential dissection. From a practical standpoint, this concept may help surgeons identify the appropriate dissection plane and preserve the functional integrity of the pituitary stalk and hypothalamus during EES.

Long-term follow-up remains crucial, as recurrence rates for TCs are approximately 20%, necessitating further research into long-term endocrine and oncologic outcomes.[26] As bilateral hypothalamic injury is common in aggressive resection of TCs, maximal resection and endocrine preservation should be balanced.[26] Future studies should focus on validating the VISSE technique in a larger cohort, assessing its long-term outcome, and integrating additional adjunctive therapies for tumor recurrence management.

CONCLUSION

The VISSE technique offers an effective approach for resecting TCs, enabling GTR with anatomical preservation of the pituitary stalk. Its integration of precise anatomical dissection and advanced endoscopic methods may be an alternative surgical option in managing these challenging tumors.

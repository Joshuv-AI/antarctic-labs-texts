Absorbing Markov Decision Processes: Geometric Properties and Sufficiency of Finite Mixtures of Deterministic Policies Supported by grant PID2021-122442NB-I00 from the Spanish Ministerio de Ciencia e Innovación.
François Dufour, Tomás Prieto-Rumeau

Abstract
In this paper we investigate several geometric properties of the set of occupancy measures. In particular, we analyse the structure of the faces generated by a given occupancy measure, together with their relative algebraic interior. We also determine the affine hulls of these faces and describe the associated parallel linear subspaces. It is shown that these structures can be fully characterised in terms of the parameters that define the underlying Markov decision process (MDP). Moreover, we establish that the class of finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies constitutes a sufficient class of policies for uniformly absorbing MDPs with a measurable state space and multiple criteria. We also provide a characterisation of the minimal order required for a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies to represent the performance vector of an arbitrary policy.



Keywords:

Markov decision processes; absorbing model; extreme points of occupancy measures, finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies, chattering stationary policies, sufficiency of families of policies

91A10, 91A15.

Introduction

In this work, we study a discrete‑time absorbing Markov Decision Process (MDP)
\mathsf{M}
defined over a measurable state space
\mathbf{X}
and a Borel action space
\mathbf{A}
, equipped with a given initial distribution.
The set of admissible state–action pairs, denoted by
\mathbf{K}
, satisfies certain technical measurability conditions.
The collection of all admissible policies is denoted by
\mathbf{\Pi}
.
We also focus on several specific subclasses: deterministic stationary policies, denoted by
\mathbb{D}
; chattering stationary policies, denoted by
\mathbb{C}
; and finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies,
denoted by
\mathbb{F}
.
A stationary chattering policy is a stationary randomized policy that, for each state, randomizes among a finite fixed set of stationary deterministic policies according to a state‑dependent distribution (see Definition
2.2
for a precise statement).
We have chosen to use the term of chattering policy which is borrowed from optimal control theory. Readers are referred to the discussion at the top of page 25 in
[
4
]

and the references therein for a discussion of this topic, as well as
[
10
]
that used it in the context of MDPs.
A finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies corresponds to a policy whose occupancy measure can be written as a convex combination of a fixed finite set of occupancy measures generated by deterministic stationary policies
(see Definition
2.7
for a precise statement). It is, in fact, a subset of chattering stationary policies, that is,
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{C}
.

For a measurable subset
\Delta\subset\mathbf{X}
, called the absorbing set, a
\Delta
‑absorbing MDP is one in which the state process remains in
\Delta
indefinitely once entered, producing no additional reward or cost. A common assumption is that the expected hitting time to
\Delta
is finite under every admissible policy. The notion of a uniformly absorbing MDP, introduced in
[
11
, Definition 3.6]
, intuitively requires that the tail probabilities of the hitting‑time series converge to zero uniformly over all policies. For comprehensive treatments and recent developments on (uniformly) absorbing MDPs, we refer to
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
20
]
and the references therein.

The occupancy measure
\mu_{\pi}
induced by a policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
captures the expected cumulative behavior of the state‑action process over time. More precisely, for any measurable set
\Gamma\subset\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
,
\mu_{\pi}(\Gamma)
represents the expected total time the process spends in
\Gamma
under policy
\pi
. We denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
the set of all occupancy measures.

A bounded one‑step reward vector‑function
r:\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in\mathbb{N}^{\*}
, is used to assess policy performance. The performance vector of a policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
is then given by

\mathcal{R}(\pi)=\int_{\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}}r(x,a)\,\mu_{\pi}(dx,da).

A subset
\varLambda\subset\mathbf{\Pi}
is said to form a sufficient class of policies if it satisfies
\mathcal{R}(\varLambda)=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
.

Our first objective is to study some geometric properties of the set of occupancy measures. In particular, we analyse the structure of the faces generated by
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
, as well as their relative algebraic interior.
We characterise the affine hulls of these faces, together with their associated parallel linear subspaces.
The relative algebraic interior of a face generated by a point of convex set has been studied in detail in
[
18
,
19
]
. In particular, the author has in
[
18
]
obtained several useful result for faces and their relative algebraic interior and has applied them to spaces of probability mesures (see sections 9, 10 and 11 in
[
18
]
). One may also mention
[
5
, page 239]
for an analysis of the faces generated by a probability measure defined on a general measurable space.
Our results partially build on the work in
[
18
]
, but also on our recent results in
[
6
, Sections 3 and 4]
where the set of occupancy measures for an absorbing MDP has been completely described in terms of the characteristic equation and an additional condition stating that an occupancy measure must be absolutely continuous with respect to a reference probability measure.
One of the key contributions is to show that a face, its affine hull and its associated parallel subspace can be fully characterised in terms of the parameters defining the Markov‑controlled model
\mathsf{M}
. More precisely, they can be described in terms of
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
‑invariant signed measures
\nu
defined on
\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
that are equivalent to
\mu
and for which the Radon–Nikodym derivatives
\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}
and
\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}
are bounded (see Proposition
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
).

The second aim of this work is to investigate, within the class of uniformly absorbing models, whether the family of finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies is sufficient. Mixtures of policies have been studied in
[
9
]
in a general context. A policy
\pi
is called a mixture of policies from
\varGamma\subset\mathbf{\Pi}
if there exists a probability measure
\nu
on the set of strategic measures generated by
\varGamma
such that the strategic measure induced by
\pi
can be expressed as the barycentre of
\nu
. In
[
9
]
, it was shown that any (respectively Markov) policy is a mixture of deterministic (respectively Markov) policies. The mixture is called finite if, in the preceding definition, the measure
\nu
can be written as a convex combination of Dirac measures. Finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies have been investigated in
[
12
]
and also in
[
17
]
. In
[
12
]
, it was shown for constrained uniformly absorbing MDPs with Borel state space and compact action sets that a stationary optimal policy exists and can be taken as a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies. A procedure to compute such an optimal policy as a mixture of finitely many deterministic stationary policies was also proposed. Related results can be found in
[
2
]
for constrained MDPs with countable state space. In
[
17
]
, the family of finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies was studied in detail; the questions of denseness and optimality were addressed under suitable continuity–compactness conditions.
A substantial body of work is devoted to the study of mixtures of deterministic stationary policies in MDPs. For a thorough and detailed overview of this literature, we refer the reader to the following references
[
9
,
12
,
17
]
.

We will show that for a uniformly absorbing model
\mathsf{M}
, one can construct, for any policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
, a finite mixture
\gamma
of deterministic stationary policies of order
d+1
that attains the same performance vector, namely,

\int_{\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}}r(x,a)\,\mu_{\pi}(dx,da)=\int_{\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}}r(x,a)\,\mu_{\gamma}(dx,da),

or, equivalently,
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{d+1})=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
, as stated in Theorem
4.4
. To the best of our knowledge, this assertion is novel even when the reward function is real‑valued. We also provide a characterisation of the minimal order required for a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies to represent the performance vector of an arbitrary policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
;
see Proposition
4.5
.

In Section

Section

Under a set of appropriate assumption, we will establish in section

The Markov control model

The main goal of this section is to introduce the parameters defining the control model with a brief presentation of the construction of the controlled process.
We will present different types of properties for a control model, such as being uniformly absorbing.
We will also introduce the notion of occupancy measure, which has been studied in detail in

2.1
The model.

A control model

consists of the following parameters.

•

A state space

endowed with a

will always be endowed with the

•

A Borel space

A family of nonempty measurable sets

.

\pi_{n}(\cdot|h_{n})=\varphi(\cdot|x_{n})

.

By a slight abuse of notation, we will write

•

The canonical space of all possible sample paths of the state-action process is

The coordinate projection functions from

to the state space
\mathbf{X}
, the action space
\mathbf{A}
, and
\mathbf{H}_{n}
for
n\geq 0
are respectively denoted by
X_{n}
,
A_{n}
, and
H_{n}
.
We will refer to
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as the state process and
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as the action process.
It is a well known result that for every policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}

there exists a unique probability
measure
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}
on
(\mathbf{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}})

such that
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(\mathbf{K}^{\infty})=1
and such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
, and
\Lambda\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbf{A})
we have

\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(X_{0}\in\Gamma)=\eta(\Gamma)
,

\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(X_{n+1}\in\Gamma\mid H_{n},A_{n})=Q(\Gamma\mid X_{n},A_{n})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(A_{n}\in\Lambda\mid H_{n})=\pi_{n}(\Lambda\mid H_{n})

with

\mathbb{P}_{\pi}
-probability one.
We will refer to
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}
as to a
strategic probability measure
, and we will denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}=\{\mathbb{P}_{\pi}\}_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{\Omega})

the set of all strategic probability measures.
The expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}
is denoted by
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}
. For a
p
-dimensional random variable
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{i},\ldots,Z_{p})
with
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
we will write
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[Z]
for the vector
\big(\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[Z_{1}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[Z_{i}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[Z_{p}]\big)
when
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[Z_{i}]
is well defined for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

2.2
Occupancy measures and absorbing models

In this section, we consider an arbitrary but fixed model
\mathsf{M}=(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A},\{\mathbf{A}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbf{X}},Q,\eta,r)
satisfying the measurability conditions.
We are going to introduce absorbing models. To do this, we will need to define the notion of occupancy measures associated with a control policy.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that the control model
\mathsf{M}
is
\Delta
-absorbing if
\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
and the following conditions are satisfied

\bullet

Q(\Delta|x,a)=1
and
r(x,a)=0_{d}
for
(x,a)\in\Delta\times\mathbf{A}
.

\bullet

\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[T_{\Delta}]
is finite for any
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
.

and we say that it is uniformly
\Delta
-absorbing if, additionally, the following condition holds

\bullet

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}}\sum_{t=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}\{T_{\Delta}>t\}=0
.

where
T_{\Delta}:\mathbf{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is defined by

T_{\Delta}(\omega)=\min\{n\geq 0:X_{n}(\omega)\in\Delta\},
where by convention the
\min
over the empty set is defined as
+\infty
.

The notion of a uniformly
\Delta
-absorbing MDP was introduced in
[
11
, Definition 3.6]
.

We define now the occupancy measures of a
\Delta
-absorbing control model
\mathsf{M}
.

Definition 2.4
.

For a
\Delta
-absorbing control model
\mathsf{M}
, the occupancy measure
\mu_{\pi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
of a control policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
is

\mu_{\pi}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{I}_{\{T_{\Delta}>t\}}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{\{(X_{t},A_{t})\in\Gamma\}}\Big]\quad\hbox{for $\Gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}\otimes\mathfrak{B}(\mathbf{A})$.}

Remark 2.5
.

The measure
\mu_{\pi}
is a finite measure. Indeed,
\mu_{\pi}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[T_{\Delta}]<+\infty

since
\mathsf{M}
is by assumption
\Delta
-absorbing.

We will write
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}=\{\mu_{\pi}:\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}\}
for the set of occupancy measures. If
\mathbf{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathbf{\Pi}
is some subset of policies, we will use the notation

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}=\{\mu_{\pi}:\pi\in\mathbf{\Lambda}\}
.

Remark 2.6
.

(a)

Observe that the subset
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
of the vector space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
is convex.
This well known property has been established in the special case of a Borel state space see for example Corollary 4.3 in
[
12
]
and Proposition 3 in
[
15
]
.
For the general case of a measurable state space, we can proceed by using similar arguments in showing that the set of strategic measures
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}
is convex implying the convexity of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
.

(b)

It is also important to recall the following well-known property:
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{S}},

see for example Theorem 8.1 in
[
3
]
for a countable state space, Lemma 4.1 in
[
12
]
or Lemma 2 in
[
15
]
for a Borel state space
and Proposition 3.3(ii) in
[
6
]
for a general measurable state space.

Definition 2.7
.

We will denote by
\mathbb{F}
the set of stationary control policies
\gamma\in\mathbb{S}
for which there exists some
nonnegative real numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\geq 0
with
\sum\alpha_{i}=1
and some deterministic stationary policies
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\mu_{\gamma_{j}}.

(2.1)

We refer to
\mathbb{F}
as the set of
finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies
. Equivalently,

\mathbb{F}=\conv\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big).

For a given integer
n\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{F}_{n}
be the set of policies that are finite mixtures of
n
deterministic stationary as in (
2.1
).

The advantage of such a policy is that it can be very easily generated.
It consists in adding an initial randomization step (choosing randomly an integer
\tau
in
\mathbb{N}_{n}^{*}
according to the distribution

\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{n}^{*}}
) and then select the policy (within the finite family
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{n}^{*}}
of deterministic stationary policies in
\mathbb{D}
) given by
\gamma_{\tau}\in\mathbb{D}
.

Now, we introduce the so-called characteristic equation.

Definition 2.8
.

Suppose that the model
\mathsf{M}
is
\Delta
-absorbing.
A measure
\mu
in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
is a solution of the characteristic equations when

\mu(\mathbf{K}^{c})=0
and
\mu^{\mathbf{X}}=(\eta+\mu Q)\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
.
We denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}
the set of all solutions of the characteristic equations.

We need to extend the definition of
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
-invariant measure that was introduced in
[
6
]
for nonnegative measures
\vartheta\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
to the set of finite signed measures.

Definition 2.9
.

A measure
\vartheta\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
is said
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
-invariant when

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}=\vartheta Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}.

The performance of a policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
is evaluated by using the following vector-function.

Definition 2.10
.

Suppose that the model
\mathsf{M}
is
\Delta
-absorbing. The vector of expected rewards for the policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
denoted by
\mathcal{R}(\pi)
is given by

\mathcal{R}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}r(X_{t},A_{t})\Big].

This defines an application from
\mathbf{\Pi}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
called the performance vector function.
A subset
\mathbf{\Lambda}\subset\mathbf{\Pi}
will be called a sufficient class (family) of policies if
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Lambda})=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
.

Remark 2.11
.

As in
[
8
, Remark 2.14]
,
there is no loss of generality to assume that the action space
\mathbf{A}
is compact and so we will make this assumption in the forthcoming.

Throughout the rest of this paper, we will use a fixed model
\mathsf{M}=(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A},\{\mathbf{A}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbf{X}},Q,\eta,r)
satisfying the measurability conditions.

3
Geometric properties of the set of occupancy measures

In this section, we derive several geometric properties of the set of occupancy measures.
In particular, we examine:

•

the faces
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
generated by a given
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
,

•

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the faces
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)
of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
generated by a given
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
,

where, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}\cap\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}(r,\alpha),\qquad\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}(r,\alpha)=\bigl\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})\colon\nu(r)=\alpha\bigr\}.

We also describe the relative algebraic interiors of these faces, we characterize their affine hulls, and we identify the associated parallel linear subspaces.

A central result of this section is that the faces
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)
, their affine hulls
and their associated parallel subspaces
can be described completely in terms of the parameters that define the controlled Markov model
\mathsf{M}
.
For instance, we show that the linear subspace parallel to the affine hull of the face
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
generated by a given
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
consists precisely of the
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
‑invariant signed measures

\nu
(see Definition
2.9
) that are equivalent to
\mu
, and whose Radon–Nikodym derivatives
\dfrac{d\nu}{d\mu}
and
\dfrac{d\mu}{d\nu}
are bounded.

We will need the following technical result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that the Markov controlled model
\mathsf{M}
is
\Delta
-absorbing.
Consider
\mu,\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then,

\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}\iff\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}).

Proof.

The direct implication is obvious. Let us show the converse implication.
Assume that
\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
. Then
\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}

and
\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\ll\dfrac{1}{2}(\mu+\nu)
.
According to Remark
2.6
(a),
\dfrac{1}{2}(\mu+\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
and so, Lemma 2.9 in
[
8
]
yields
\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
giving the result.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that the Markov controlled model
\mathsf{M}
is
\Delta
-absorbing.
For
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
, the face of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
generated by
\mu
is given by

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)=\Big\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}:\nu\leq c\mu\text{ for some }c>1\Big\}.

The relative algebraic interior of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
is

\mathrm{rai}[\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)]=\Big\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}:\frac{1}{c}\mu\leq\nu\leq c\mu\text{ for some }c>1\Big\}.

The affine hull of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma\in\mathrm{aff}\{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\}\iff\begin{cases}\gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}),\\
(\gamma-\mu)^{\mathbf{X}}=(\gamma-\mu)Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}},\\
-c\mu\leq\gamma-\mu\leq c\mu,\text{ for some }c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

The linear subspace of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
parallel to
\mathrm{aff}\{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}(\mu)=\big\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}):\nu^{\mathbf{X}}=\nu Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}\text{ and }-c\mu\leq\nu\leq c\mu,\text{ for some }c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\big\}

(3.2)

Proof.

From Proposition 4.1 in
[
18
]
,
\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
if and only if
\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
and
\mu+\varepsilon(\mu-\nu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
for some

\varepsilon>0
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, Lemma
3.1
yields

\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\iff\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}\text{ and }\nu\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\mu\iff\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}\text{ and }\nu\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\mu

where the last equivalence holds by virtue of Lemma 2.9 in
[
8
]
. This gives the first item.

For
\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
, Corollary 5.7 in
[
18
]
yields
\nu\in\mathrm{rai}[\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)]
if and only if
\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\text{ and }\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\nu)

and combined with the previous item this gives the second claim.

By Corollary 4.6 in
[
18
]
,
\gamma\in\mathrm{aff}[\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)]
if and only if

\gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
and

\mu\pm\varepsilon(\gamma-\mu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
, for some
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
.
Observe that for
\gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, we have

\mu\pm\varepsilon(\gamma-\mu)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
if and only if
-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\mu\leq\gamma-\mu\leq\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\mu

and
(\gamma-\mu)^{\mathbf{X}}=(\gamma-\mu)Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
, this last equivalence comes from Lemma 2.9 in
[
8
]
. This shows the claim.

For the proof of the last item, observe that
\mathscr{V}(\mu)
is clearly a linear subspace of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
.
Now the result is an immediate consequence of the expression of
\mathrm{aff}\{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\}
given in (
3.1
).
Indeed,

\gamma\in\mathrm{aff}\{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\}\iff(\gamma-\mu)\in\mathscr{V}(\mu)

for
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
giving the result.
∎

Here is a corollary of the preceding results that characterizes the extremal structures associated with a face generated by a measure in the constrained‑measure set
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that the Markov controlled model
\mathsf{M}
is
\Delta
-absorbing.
Let us consider
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)\neq\emptyset
.
Let us write
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)
the face of the convex set
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
generated by
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\Big\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}:\nu(r)=\alpha\text{ and }\nu\leq c\mu\text{ for some }c>1\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{rai}[\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)]=\Big\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}:\nu(r)=\alpha\text{ and }\frac{1}{c}\mu\leq\nu\leq c\mu\text{ for some }c>1\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\gamma\in\mathrm{aff}\{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)\}\iff\begin{cases}\gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}),\\
\gamma(r)=\alpha,\\
(\gamma-\mu)^{\mathbf{X}}=(\gamma-\mu)Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}},\\
-c\mu\leq\gamma-\mu\leq c\mu,\text{ for some }c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}.\end{cases}

and the linear subspace of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
parallel to
\mathrm{aff}\{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}_{\alpha}(\mu)\}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\big\{\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}):\nu(r)=0,\>\nu^{\mathbf{X}}=\nu Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}\text{ and }-c\mu\leq\nu\leq c\mu,\text{ for some }c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\big\}.

Proof.

Observe that the face of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}(r,\alpha)
generated by
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}(r,\alpha)
, its relative algebraic interior and its affine hull are given by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}(r,\alpha)
.
Applying Proposition 5.8 in
[
18
]
, the results are an easy consequence of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

4
Sufficiency of finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies and minimal order

In this section we first establish the sufficiency of the class of finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies, i.e.

\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi}),

for a uniformly absorbing model (see Theorem
4.2
). Using different arguments, this result is sharpened in Theorem
4.4
by showing that the subclass
\mathbb{F}_{d+1}
forms a sufficient family for such models. Finally, we characterize the minimal order required for a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies to reproduce the performance vector of an arbitrary policy

\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
(see Proposition
4.5
).

We first need to establish the following technical result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
for
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
then there exists
\Phi^{*}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
supported on
\{\phi^{*}_{i}(x)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{p}}
for some

\{\phi^{*}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{p}}\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfying

1)

\Phi(\cdot|x)=\Phi^{*}(\cdot|x)
for any
x\in\mathbf{X}
,

2)

\Phi^{*}(\{\phi^{*}_{i}(x)\}|x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbf{X}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}
.

Proof.

There exist
p
measurable functions
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}}
in
\mathbb{D}
and a measurable function
\beta
defined from
\mathbf{X}
to
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
satisfying

\displaystyle\Phi(\cdot|x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\beta_{i}(x)\delta_{\phi_{i}(x)}(\cdot)

for any
x\in\mathbf{X}
where
\beta(x)=(\beta_{1}(x),\ldots,\beta_{p}(x))
.
Let us define the multifunction
\mathcal{Z}
from
\mathbf{X}
into
\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}
by
\mathcal{Z}(x)=\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}:\beta_{k}(x)=0\}
which is measurable by Corollary 18.8 in
[
1
]
.
Combining Lemma 18.7 and Theorem 18.19 in
[
1
]
, it follows that the function
\tau
from
\mathbf{X}
into
\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}
defined by
\tau(x)=\min\{j\in\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}:\beta_{k}(j)>0\}
is measurable.
The kernel
\Phi^{*}(\cdot|x)
defined by
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\beta^{*}_{i}(x)\delta_{\phi^{*}_{i}(x)}(\cdot)

where

\displaystyle\beta^{*}_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta_{\tau(x)}(x)}{\operatorname{card}(\mathcal{Z}(x))+1}&\text{ if }i\in\mathcal{Z}(x)\mathop{\cup}\{\tau(x)\},\\
\beta_{i}(x)&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{*}_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}\phi_{\tau(x)}(x)&\text{ if }i\in\mathcal{Z}(x),\\
\phi_{i}(x)&\text{ if }i\in\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{Z}(x),\end{cases}

satisfies the required properties.
∎

The following result establishes the sufficiency of
\mathbb{F}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that the model
\mathsf{M}
is uniformly
\Delta
-absorbing. Then,
\mathbb{F}
is a sufficient family of policies, that is,
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
.

Proof.

From Theorem 5.6 in
[
8
]
, it is sufficient to establish
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{C}_{d+1})
to get the result.
Therefore, let us consider an arbitrary policy
\Phi\in\mathbb{C}_{p+1}
. Lemma
4.1
shows the existence of
\Phi^{*}\in\mathbb{C}_{p+1}
supported on

\{\phi^{*}_{i}(x)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{p+1}}
for some
\{\phi^{*}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{p+1}}\subset\mathbb{D}
satisfying
\Phi^{*}(\{\phi^{*}_{i}(x)\}|x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbf{X}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{p+1}^{*}
and

\mu_{\Phi}=\mu_{\Phi^{*}}
.
From, the proof of Theorem 5.4 in
[
8
]
, it follows that the model
\mathsf{M}^{*}=(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A},\{\mathbf{A}^{*}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbf{X}},Q,\eta,r)

with
\mathbf{A}^{*}(x)=\{\phi^{*}_{i}(x):i\in\mathbb{N}_{p}^{*}\}
is precisely one of those that satisfies the conclusions of Theorem 5.4 in
[
8
]
.
From Theorem 5.5 in
[
8
]
,
\mu_{\Phi^{*}}(r)
belongs to the relative interior of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi}^{*})
where

\mathbf{\Pi}^{*}\subset\mathbf{\Pi}
denotes set of all control policies associated with the model
\mathsf{M}^{*}
.
Writing
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}
for the set of occupancy measures of
\mathsf{M}^{*}
we have that

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}
is
ws
-compact as well as
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi}^{*})
.
The Krein-Milman Theorem yields that

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}=\overline{\conv\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}\big)}.

where
\mathbb{D}^{*}
is the set of deterministic stationary policies for the model
\mathsf{M}^{*}
.
Let
\mathscr{R}^{*}
be the linear mapping defined on
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}
by
\mathscr{R}^{*}(\mu)=\mu(r)
. Then, it is continuous and so,
the convex set
\mathscr{R}^{*}\Big(\conv\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}\big)\Big)
is dense in
\mathscr{R}^{*}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*})
. Therefore, they have the same relative interior. This yields that

\mu_{\Phi}(r)=\mu_{\Phi^{*}}(r)\in\mathscr{R}^{*}\Big(\conv\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}\big)\Big).

However,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}\subset\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{D}}
and so,
\mu_{\Phi}(r)\in\mathscr{R}\Big(\conv\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big)\Big)
. Since

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}}=\conv\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big)
, this shows the claim.
∎

The next theorem establishes a link between the order of a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies and the dimension of face in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
generated by its occupancy measure.
More precisely, it states that that for an occupancy measure
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
we have
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\big)\leq p
if and only if
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p+1}}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose the model
\mathsf{M}
is uniformly
\Delta
-absorbing.

1)

If
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\big)\leq p
for some
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, then
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p+1}}
.

2)

If
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p+1}}
for some
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, then
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\big)\leq p
.

Proof.

Let us show the first item.
Observe that Proposition 3.2 in
[
8
]
yields
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
is linearly closed and linearly bounded since
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
is a face of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
.
Then
(3)
of
[
13
]
shows that any
\gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
can be written as a convex combination of at most
p+1
extreme points of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
that are also extreme points of

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
since
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)
is a face of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
. Therefore,
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p+1}}
showing the result.

To prove the second item. Consider
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p+1}}
. Then there exists
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{p+1}^{*}}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
(\alpha_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{p+1}^{*}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathscr{S}}_{p+1}

such that
\displaystyle\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}\alpha_{j}\mu_{\gamma_{j}}
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\subset\conv\big(\{\mu_{\gamma_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{\gamma_{p+1}}\}\big)

implying
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\big)\leq p
.
∎

Now the next theorem refines the result established in Theorem
4.2
by showing that the collection of finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies of order at most
d+1
is a sufficient family of policies. An interesting point is that our proof relies on arguments different from those employed in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Consider
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
and suppose the model
\mathsf{M}
is uniformly
\Delta
-absorbing.
Then
\mathbb{F}_{d+1}
is a sufficient family of policies, that is,
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{F}_{d+1})=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
.

Proof.

From Theorem 5.6 in
[
8
]
, there exists
\mu_{\gamma}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
with

\gamma\in\mathbb{C}_{d+1}
such that
\gamma(\cdot|x)
is supported on
\{\gamma_{i}(x)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{d+1}}
for some
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{d+1}}\subset\mathbb{D}
.
Let us introduce

\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}=\big\{(x,a)\in\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}:a\in\{\gamma_{i}(x):i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}_{d+1}\}\}\big\}

and write

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}}=\{\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}:\mu(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}^{c})=0\}.

We have clearly,
\mu_{\gamma}\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}}\mathop{\cap}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}(r,\alpha)
and so, Lemma 5.1 in
[
8
]
yields the existence of
\mu^{\alpha}\in\ext\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)\big)

Therefore,
\mu_{\alpha}
is a convex combination of at most
d+1
extreme points of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}
by Theorem 3.5 in
[
8
]
.
However,
\ext(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}})=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{D}}
implying that
\mu_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{F}_{d+1}
showing the result.
∎

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(\Pi)
, the following result gives a characterization of the minimal order of a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies
\gamma

that satisfies
\mu_{\gamma}(r)=\alpha
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume that the model
\mathsf{M}
is uniformly
\Delta
-absorbing.
For
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
, the minimal order of the finite mixtures of deterministic stationary policies
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}
satisfying
\mu_{\gamma}(r)=\alpha
is given by
p^{*}+1
with

p^{*}=\min_{\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)}\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu)\big)=\min_{\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)}\Big\{\dim\big(\ker(\mathscr{R}_{\mu})\big)+\dim\big(\operatorname{im}(\mathscr{R}_{\mu})\big)\Big\}\leq d

where
\mathscr{V}(\mu)
is given in equation (
3.2
) and
\mathscr{R}_{\mu}
is the linear mapping defined on
\mathscr{V}(\mu)
by
\mathscr{R}_{\mu}(\nu)=\nu(r)
.

Proof.

Consider
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\Pi})
.
Let us define
p^{*}
as the minimum of
\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu)\big)
for
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
.
Combining the second item of Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.4
, we obtain that there exists

\mu_{\alpha}
in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
such that
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu_{\alpha})\big)\leq d
.
We have
p^{*}\leq d
since by Proposition
3.2
, we have
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\big)=\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu)\big)
for
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
.
Let
\mu^{*}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
be such that

\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu^{*})\big)=p^{*}
. Then, the first item of Theorem
4.3
shows that
\mu^{*}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{*}+1}
.
Now, if there exists
p<p^{*}
such that
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p+1}
for some
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
, then
\dim\big(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{F}}(\mu)\big)=\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu)\big)=p<p^{*}
by the second item of
Theorem
4.3
yielding a contradiction.
Therefore,
p^{*}
is the minimal order of a finite mixture of deterministic stationary policies
\gamma
that satisfies
\mu_{\gamma}(r)=\alpha
.
The last equality is easily obtained by observing that for
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}(r,\alpha)
with
\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu)\big)\leq d
we have

\dim\big(\mathscr{V}(\mu)\big)=\dim\big(\mathscr{V}_{\alpha}(\mu)\big)+\operatorname{codim}_{\mathscr{V}(\mu)}\big(\mathscr{V}_{\alpha}(\mu)\big)=\dim\big(\ker(\mathscr{R}_{\mu})\big)+\dim\big(\operatorname{im}(\mathscr{R}_{\mu})\big)

where
\mathscr{V}_{\alpha}(\mu)
is the linear subspace of
\mathscr{V}(\mu)
given in Corollary
3.3
showing the result.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

This gives an interesting geometric interpretation of the minimal representation of a policy with a given performance
vector as a finite mixture of deterministic policies which is stated in terms of the MDP’s parameter. This is related to the minimal dimension of the faces of the policies with such performance vector.

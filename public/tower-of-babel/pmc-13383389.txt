Developing a multi-antigen fusion protein (Tp15-17-47) serological test to improve neurosyphilis diagnosis
Authors: Ziliang Deng, Wenjia Weng, Yuanyuan He, Yan Xu, Tayier Tuerhong, Lin Yang, Bingbing Song, Xiaohui Zhang, Ligang Yang, Liuyuan Wang, Renaguli Maimaiti, Gang Zheng, Shixing Tang, Wujian Ke, Haiying Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesOur previous research identified a luciferase immunosorbent assay (LISA) for detecting serum antibodies against Tp17 and Tp47 as a potential tool for diagnosing neurosyphilis (NS). In this study, we developed a serological test based on a multi-antigen fusion protein (Tp15–17–47) to simplify the detection process and improve the diagnostic accuracy for NS.MethodsThe Tp15, Tp17, and Tp47 genes were amplified separately by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and assembled into a fusion gene coding for a tripartite fusion protein antigen using overlap PCR. This recombinant Tp15-17-47 antigen was used retrospectively to detect serum anti-Tp15-17-47 IgG antibodies in 434 HIV-negative patients suspected of NS from cohorts in Beijing and Guangzhou. Two diagnostic prediction models were developed using stepwise logistic regression.ResultsSerum anti-Tp15-17-47 IgG antibodies demonstrated moderate diagnostic capability for NS in the development cohort, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.791 (95% CI: 0.735–0.846). An optimized NS prediction model substituting anti-Tp15-17-47 antibodies for TRUST/RPR outperformed the base NS diagnostic model. The optimized model achieved an AUC of 0.877 (95% CI: 0.835–0.919) compared to 0.843 (95% CI: 0.795–0.890) for the base model in the development cohort (p = 0.035). This superior performance was confirmed in the validation cohort (AUC: 0.880, 95% CI: 0.827–0.933 vs. 0.792, 95% CI: 0.726–0.859; p < 0.001). Decision curve analysis (DCA) revealed that the optimized model provided greater net benefit than the base model across threshold probabilities ranging from 0.10 to 0.95 in both cohorts.ConclusionsQuantitative profiling of serum IgG targeting the recombinant treponemal antigen Tp15-17-47, along with its diagnostic model, exhibited robust discriminatory power for neurosyphilis diagnosis, which supports the viability of replacing lumbar puncture with this less invasive serological strategy for neurosyphilis ascertainment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-13611-0.Keywords: Neurosyphilis, Diagnosis, Treponemal antibodies, Tp15-17-47, LISA, Prediction model

Introduction

Neurosyphilis (NS), a severe manifestation of Treponema pallidum subsp. pallidum (Tp) infection in the central nervous system (CNS), remains a significant diagnostic challenge, with an estimated incidence of 0.47–1.1 cases per 100 000 population in high-income settings and markedly higher rates (> 15 per 100 000) among people living with HIV (PLWH), men who have sex with men [1, 2]. While the incidence of syphilis has been decreasing in China in recent years, globally it remains a significant public health concern and is increasing in many regions. The rising number of latent cases complicates diagnosis, given that early CNS invasion can occur within months of primary infection [3, 4]. Consequently, early NS detection is a public health priority, crucial for improving patient outcomes and reducing treatment costs.

Traditional NS diagnosis relies on invasive lumbar puncture to assess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) abnormalities, including CSF-venereal disease research laboratory (VDRL)/rapid plasma reagin (RPR) titers, leukocyte count and protein elevation. However, these methods exhibit limited sensitivity (49–87%) and inadequate specificity (66–98%), failing to distinguish NS from other neuroinflammatory conditions [5, 6]. While studies indicate that CSF-Treponema pallidum particle agglutination assay (TPPA) titer ≥ 1:320 effectively identifies neurosyphilis with high sensitivity (100.0%), but moderate specificity (66.8%). CSF detection necessitates lumbar puncture [7, 8]. This procedure is invasive, technically demanding, often unavailable in resource-limited settings, and frequently declined by patients.

Given these challenges, serum biomarkers have attracted considerable interest. Several candidates, such as Semaphorin-7 A (SEMA7A), Serpin Family A Member 3 (SERPINA3), and Inter-Alpha-Trypsin Inhibitor Heavy Chain 4 (ITIH4) have been proposed [9], but their specificity for syphilis is limited, as levels can also increase in confounding pathologies, including other infectious diseases and neurodegenerative disorders [10, 11].

Numerous studies demonstrated that elevated titers of non-treponemal tests (NTTs), such as the toluidine red unheated serum test (TRUST) and RPR, are associated with increased NS risk, Whereas fewer studies have evaluated the utility of serum treponemal tests (TTs) like TPPA or TPHA for differentiating NS [12, 13]. While numerous studies exist on serological detection using single Tp antigens, the sensitivity of a single recombinant antigen is limited. Utilizing multiple Tp recombinant antigens significantly improves assay sensitivity and is the basis for commercially available ELISA kits. As demonstrated by Sun et al., the ELISA detection method based on the TpN15-TpN17-TpN47 fusion protein, achieved a 99.5% detection rate in 965 syphilis patients, significantly outperforming single-antigen ELISA and traditional TPHA. However, ELISA cannot provide quantitative antibody measurements [14]. Our laboratory previously established a luciferase immunosorbent assay (LISA) targeting individual Tp0171 (Tp15), Tp0435 (Tp17), and Tp0574 (Tp47), and showed that Tp17 and Tp47 have diagnostic accuracy comparable to TPPA for syphilis15]. In this study, We developed a LISA assay targeting a Tp15-17-47 recombinant fusion antigen and evaluated the applicability of this serum marker for NS diagnosis using serum samples from 434 HIV-negative patients (confirmed as NS or non-NS [N-NS] by lumbar puncture) to simplify the detection process and improve the diagnostic accuracy for NS. Accurately assessing NS risk in syphilis patients, enabling prompt targeted screening of high-riskindividuals, and minimizing missed diagnoses are critical for NS prevention and management. Therefore, we developed a non-invasive optimised diagnostic predictive model for NS by integrating serum-based recombinant antibodies targeting Tp15-17-47 as predictive factors, and evaluated the clinical value of this model. Optimizing lumbar puncture strategies by reducing missed diagnoses and misdiagnoses provides new insights for clinical diagnosis and enhances healthcare efficiency.

Methods

Study design and participants

We conducted a cross-sectional, retrospective study to evaluate the diagnostic value of serum antibodies against the Tp15-17-47 fusion antigen for neurosyphilis (NS). We retrospectively included 434 HIV-negative syphilis patients with suspected NS who underwent lumbar puncture (LP) for CSF assessment from two tertiary hospitals in Beijing and Guangzhou, China. The inclusion criteria for this secondary analysis were: (1) syphilis patients presenting with at least one of the following conditions: follow-up examination after NS treatment, tertiary syphilis, neurological symptoms and/or signs suggestive of NS (neurological, ocular, or otological), or an insufficient decline in non-treponemal test (NTT) titers after treatment; (2) HIV-negative status. Patients with incomplete medical records or missing NS-related test results were excluded. Ultimately, 434 eligible HIV-negative patients were included. All serum samples used in this study were residual specimens from routine clinical testing and were de-identified prior to analysis. The study was approved by the ethics committees of both participating hospitals, which waived the requirement for individual informed consent due to the retrospective use of de-identified residual samples.

NS diagnosis was established according to current international and national guidelines (UpToDate 2020, US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention 2021, Europe 2020, and China 2020) [6, 15, 16]. The case group comprised patients with confirmed and probable NS. The control group (non-neurosyphilis, N-NS) consisted of syphilis patients meeting neither confirmed nor probable NS criteria. Paired CSF and serum samples collected for NS diagnosis were stored at -80℃ for subsequent analysis.

Tp15-17-47 recombinant plasmid construction

Gene sequences encoding Treponema pallidum antigens Tp0171 (Tp15), Tp0435 (Tp17), and Tp0574 (Tp47) were retrieved from GenBank (accession number: AE000520.1) A 2166-bp fusion gene (Tp15-17-47) was amplified by multiple overlapping PCR, combining the 387-bp Tp15, 450-bpTp17 and 1299-bp Tp47 sequences linked by a 30-bp flexible peptide linker (Supplementary Table 1). EcoRI and XbaI restriction sites were incorporated during amplification. The amplified Tp15-17-47 fusion gene was digested with EcoRI/XbaI and then ligated into the corresponding sites of the pNLF1-N vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA), downstream of the NanoLuc (Nluc) luciferase gene. The integrity of the resulting Nluc-Tp15-17-47 fusion construct was verified by DNA sequencing (Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd., China). Recombinant plasmids were purified using a DNA purification kit (TIANGEN Biotech, China) for transfection.

LISA based syphilis antibody detection

LISA based antibody detection was described previously. HeLa cells were seeded in 10-cm dishes at a density of 1 × 106cells/dish, and transfected with 5 µg of Tp15-17-47 recombinant plasmid DNA using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA). After 48 h, cell lysates containing the expressed Nluc-Tp15-17-47 fusion protein were harvested and stored at − 80 °C without purification. NanoLuc luciferase activity in crude lysates was measured using the Nano-Glo Luciferase Assay Reagent (Promega, USA) on a Tecan Infinite M200 PRO luminometer (Switzerland) to confirm fusion protein expression levels.

White microtiter plates were coated with Protein G (5 µg/mL in 0.01 M PBS; GenScript Co., China) overnight at 4 °C. Plates were washed five times with PBS containing 0.05% Tween 20 (PBST) and blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk (NFDM) in PBST at 37 °C for 1 h. Following another five PBST washes, 50 µL of serum samples (diluted 1:100 in 2% NFDM/PBST) were added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. After five washes with PBST, 50 µL of crude cell lysate containing Nluc-Tp15-17-47 fusion protein was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. Plates were washed five times with PBST. Subsequently, 50 µL of Nano-Glo Luciferase Assay Reagent was added to each well, and luminescence was measured within 2 h using a luminometer. The average luminescence units (LU) from duplicate wells for each sample were recorded as crude LU and normalized to the average LU of negative controls to obtain relative light units (RLU). RLU values were log2-transformed for analysis (log2 RLU).

Development of the clinical prediction model

Consistent with established guidelines for clinical prediction model development, a minimum of 10 outcome events(NS cases) per predictor variable is recommended to ensure statistical reliability and minimize overfitting. Based on six candidate predictors this required at least 60 NS cases. Our total cohort of 434 patients met this requirement. To evaluate model performance and generalizability, these patients were randomly allocated in a 6:4 ratio, yielding a Development Cohort (n = 260) for model building and a Validation Cohort (n = 174) for assessment (Supplementary Table 2). We developed two diagnostic models to predict NS noninvasively (without CSF markers). Base Model: incorporated established clinical risk factors for NS as candidate predictors: gender, age, syphilis stage, history of previous antibiotic treatment for syphilis/NS, serum TRUST/RPR titers, and presence of neurological symptoms and/or signs. Optimised Model: Included predictors from the Base Model plus serum antibodies against the Tp15-17-47 fusion antigen, quantified using the luciferase immunosorbent assay, except serum TRSUT/RPR titers.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), GraphPad Prism (version 8.3.0; GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA), and R(version 4.2.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Serum antibody levels against Tp15, Tp17, and Tp47 among different groups were compared using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to assess the diagnostic performance of the serum LISA-Tp15-17-47 assay for neurosyphilis (NS). For prediction model development, univariable logistic regression was initially performed to evaluate the association between each candidate predictor and NS. Variables for the multivariable logistic regression model were selected via backward stepwise based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC) [17]. Model performance was evaluated in both the development and external validation cohorts using the area under the ROC curve (AUC), calibration plots, the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, and decision curve analysis (DCA) to assess discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility, respectively. To further explore the robustness of the identified risk factors, we performed a 1:1 matching analysis. Patients with neurosyphilis (NS) and those without (NNS) were matched by sex and age using a nearest-neighbor matching algorithm without replacement. After matching, differences in the candidate predictor variables between the NS and NNS groups were re-evaluated using McNemar’s test for categorical variables and paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test for continuous variables. Subsequently, we conducted stratified analyses to compare serum anti-Tp15-17-47 IgG antibody levels between NS and NNS groups across three clinically relevant strata. Between-group comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. For a subset of NS patients who had paired follow-up serum samples collected before and after treatment (n = 15), we compared pre- and post-treatment serum anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody levels and TRUST/RPR titers using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The correlation between the change in TRUST/RPR titers (post-treatment minus pre-treatment) and the change in LISA antibody levels was assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

Results

Characteristics of study participants

A total of 434 HIV-negative participants were included, comprising 18.7% (81/434) with confirmed NS, 25.3% (110/434) with probable NS, and 56.0% (243/434) classified as N-NS. Baseline characteristics across these groups are summarized in Table 1. Statistically significant intergroup differences were observed for gender distribution, age, geographic origin(hospital location), history of prior syphilis/NS treatment according to the Chinese national guidelines(Supplementary Table 3), presence of neurological symptoms and/or signs, and serum non-treponemal test titers (TRUST/RPR). As shown in Fig. 1, serum antibody levels against the Tp15-17-47 fusion antigen differed significantly among the confirmed NS, probable NS, and N-NS groups (p < 0.001). The assay demonstrated good reproducibility across three independent tests (Supplementary Fig. 1; coefficient of variation < 15%, p = 0.99). Antibody levels were highest in the confirmed NS group compared to both the probable NS and N-NS groups. These findings suggest that serum anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody levels may serve as a potential biomarker for distinguishing NS from non-NS cases.

Performance of serum antibodies against Tp15-17-47 in diagnosing NS

In the Development Cohort, a significant difference was observed in serum antibody levels against Tp15-17-47 between NS and N-NS cases (17.32, 95% CI: 16.93–17.70 vs. 15.70, 95% CI: 15.18–16.02), underscoring the potential diagnostic utility of these antibodies for NS identification. As shown in Fig. 2, we performed a comprehensive reanalysis and comparison of prior serological test results (LISA-Tp15, LISA-Tp17, LISA-Tp47) previously conducted by our laboratory on this sample set. The results indicated that serum LISA-Tp17 (AUC = 0.766, 95% CI: 0.707–0.826), serum LISA-Tp47 (AUC = 0.775, 95% CI: 0.719–0.831), and serum LISA-Tp15-17-47 (AUC = 0.791, 95% CI: 0.735–0.846) significantly outperformed TRUST/RPR titers (AUC = 0.730, 95% CI: 0.668–0.793) in distinguishing NS from N-NS within the Development cohort, except for serum LISA-Tp15 (AUC = 0.713, 95% CI:0.650–0.775). Additionally, for neurosyphilis diagnosis, LISA-Tp17 demonstrated the highest sensitivity (73.0%, 95% CI: 64.3% − 80.3%) within the Development cohort. In contrast, the combined marker Tp15-17-47 exhibited the highest specificity (84.1%, 95% CI: 77.3% − 89.2%) along with good sensitivity (70.4%, 95% CI: 61.5% − 78.0%). Similarly, LISA-Tp15-17-47 displayed moderate diagnostic performance for NS in the Validation cohort (Table 2). Critically, the parallel testing of serum LISA-Tp15, LISA-Tp17, and LISA-Tp47 achieved the highest sensitivity of 88.2%, albeit with a specificity of only 43.9% in the Validation cohort. Conversely serial testing of these serum markers attained the highest specificity of 83.7% with a sensitivity of 56.6% (Table 2).

Performance, calibration and clinical utility of prediction models for NS diagnosis

The Optimised Model demonstrated statistically significant superiority over the Base Model in both the Development Cohort (AUC:0.877, 95% CI: 0.835–0.919 vs. 0.843, 95% CI: 0.795–0.890;) P = 0.035) and the Validation Cohort (AUC: 0.880, 95% CI: 0.827–0.933 vs. 0.792, 95% CI: 0.726–0.859, P < 0.001; Fig. 3). In the 500 bootstrap internal validation cohorts, the mean AUC was 0.843 (95% CI: 0.798–0.886) for the Base Model and 0.877 (95% CI: 0.836–0.916) for the Optimised Model. Calibration curves visually presented high consistency between observed and predicted probabilities for both models in both the development and validation cohorts (Fig. 4). Furthermore, the Hosmer-Lemeshow test results confirmed satisfactory calibration for both models, with nostatistically significant differences in either the development cohort (Base Model: χ² = 2.870, p = 0.942; Optimised Model: χ² = 4.341, p = 0.825) or the validation cohort (Base Model: χ² = 6.609, p = 0.579; Optimised Model: χ² = 11.782, p = 0.161),. These comprehensive findings strongly suggest that the Optimised Model offers significant performance advantages and robust clinical application potential for NS risk prediction.

Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to assess the clinical utility of both models in predicting neurosyphilis. By plotting estimated high-risk counts across various thresholds, DCA visually captured the ratios of accurately identified positive cases. Figure 5 clearly illustrates the clinical net benefit associated with different risk thresholds. In this figure, the blue curve represented treatment based solely on the Base Model’s NS case identification, while the red curve reflected the Optimised Model’s performance. The grey and black curves represented the extreme strategies of treating all patients and treating no patients, respectively. The DCA results highlighted the clinical value of both models. Across the development and validation cohorts, when the threshold probability ranged from 0.05 to 0.95, both models provided greater net benefit than the treat - all or treat - none trategies. Moreover, within a threshold probability range of 0.10 to 0.95, the Optimised Model demonstrated higher net benefit than the Base Model in both cohorts (Fig. 5). These findings indicate that the prediction models yield considerable net benefit across a broad, clinically meaningful range of threshold risks. This underscores not only the significant predictive capacity of the models but also their superior clinical applicability for neurosyphilis prediction.

Discussion

For a long time, the diagnosis of NS has lacked clear, universally reliable laboratory testing techniques. CSF examination via LP has emerged as a crucial auxiliary diagnostic tool for NS [18]. Currently, the three most commonly used diagnostic techniques in clinical practice, CSF-TRUST, CSF-VDRL, and CSF-TPPA, all exhibit excellent diagnostic performance [19, 20]. For example, Mei Shi et al. determined that a CSF-TPPA titre ≥ 1:320 was the optimal cutoff value, yielding a sensitivity of 92.53% and specificity of 87.96%. RCS analysis, further showed a significant increase in NS prevalence when CSF-TPPA titre exceeded 1:320, reinforcing its independent diagnostic value as a NS biomarker [21]. Despite their utility, LP and CSF examination are invasive procedures. Consequently, significant research efforts are focused on identifying non-invasive serum markers for NS. In our previous research, we developed a semi-quantitative LISA method to detect serum levels of specific antibodies against Tp15, Tp17, and Tp47 exhibiting exceptional diagnostic accuracy comparable to TPPA [22]. This method outperformed serum TRUST/RPR in distinguishing NS from NNS [23].

Studies have demonstrated that the combination of multiple Tp antigens significantly improves the sensitivity and specificity of serological tests, contributing to the development and widespread use of commercial ELISA kits [24, 25]. In support of this strategy, Wang et al. developed an ELISA immunosensor based on a natural silkworm cocoon membrane (SCM) by conjugating the Tp15-17-47 chimeric antigen with the monoclonal antibody 3H12. This assay achieved a detection limit of 0.48 mIU/mL, markedly outperforming commercial ELISA kits. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated 100% sensitivity, 97.96% specificity, and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.9995 [26]. Building on these findings, we established a LISA detection system using a Tp15-17-47 fusion antigen. Evaluation using 434 HIV-negative patients with suspected NS showed that serum anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody detection via LISA was significantly more effective than serum TRUST/RPR titers in discriminating between NS and NNS cases (see Fig. 2; Tables 5 and 2). Moreover, the fusion antigen-based approach exhibited a more balanced performance in sensitivity and specificity compared to assays detecting antibodies against Tp15, Tp17, or Tp47 individually. Although parallel and serial testing using individual antigens (Tp15, Tp17, and Tp47) improved sensitivity (88.2%) and specificity (83.7%), respectively (Table 5), these strategies are primarily suitable either for high-risk population screening or confirmatory testing and remain technically cumbersome. In contrast, the fusion antigen assay integrates multiple targets into a single reagent, thereby simplifying operational procedures and reducing cost.

The incorporation of multi-epitope treponemal fusion antigens into a unified platform simultaneously exposes the immune system to Tp15, Tp17, and Tp47, broadening epitope coverage and reducing the risk of false-negative diagnoses, particularly in early-stage NS. Furthermore, the requirement for concurrent IgG reactivity against non-overlapping epitopes significantly enhances specificity, minimizes cross-reactivity, and helps prevent overtreatment in patients without active NS. In our study, the Tp15–17–47 fusion antigen exhibited the highest specificity (77.2%) and competitive sensitivity (70.4%) in the development cohort (Table 5). These results underscore the potential of the Tp15–17–47 fusion antigen as a promising diagnostic biomarker for neurosyphilis. Early diagnosis and timely treatment of NS are critical to preventing irreversible damage to the CNS. Chinese guidelines recommend LP testing for syphilis patients with neurological or psychiatric symptoms. Currently, researches on predictive models for neurosyphilis is moving towards machine learning algorithms that combine clinical and cerebrospinal fluid parameters (AUC of 0.84–0.88), showing significant diagnostic improvements over traditional methods. The exclusion of CSF biomarkers in serum-based predictive models obviates the need for invasive LP procedures [27, 28]. Thus, current efforts are focused on identifying high-risk patients who may have NS in an early, accurate, and non-invasive manner. In a recent study by Yang et al., a clinical nomogram was developed and validated, integrating neuropsychiatric symptoms, serum RPR titers, etc., eight pre-lumbar puncture parameters, which demonstrated strong discriminatory power in neurosyphilis risk assessment, achieving a consistency index of 0.84 (95% CI 0.83–0.85) [29]. Our study combined serum anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody levels measured by LISA with NS risk factors (demographic characteristics, neurological symptoms/signs) to establish an optimized NS prediction model. Compared to the base model, the optimized model replaced serum TRUST/RPR titers with LISA-detected serum anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody levels, simplifying the required variables. Unlike previous approaches, it relies solely on a single serological assay. Comparative analysis of the AUC and DCA demonstrated that the optimized model showed a modest improvement in discriminatory capability (AUC 87% vs. 84% in the development cohort; similar results in the validation cohort) and consistently higher net benefit across a range of clinically relevant threshold probabilities compared to the baseline model (Figs. 3 and 5). While the absolute increase in AUC is small, the decision curve analysis supports a potential clinical utility, particularly when combined with the model’s practical advantage of relying on a single serum LISA assay instead of serum TRUST/RPR. This combination may offer value as a rule-in/rule-out tool to reduce unnecessary lumbar punctures. Nonetheless, prospective studies are needed to confirm whether these observed benefits translate into meaningful improvements in patient management or cost-effectiveness. Analysis of the validation cohort confirmed these findings. Implementing this optimiszed model to identify high-risk NS individuals provides clinicians with a theoretical basis for more effectively selecting LP candidates, facilitating timely NS diagnosis. To enhance accessibility, we developed a user-friendly online intelligent calculator (https://neurosyphilis-prediction.shinyapps.io/nsprediction/) based on the model’s readily available variales, establishing a unified computational pipeline for clinician (but not patients) use.

Previous studies consistently indicate that older male patients face a higher NS risk [30]. Therefore, we performed 1:1 matching for age and gender. Results still showed a significant difference in anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody levels between the NS and NNS group (Supplementary Table 5). Additionally, we analysed changes in these antibody levels pre- and post-treatment within the NS group. These results further support the potential of anti-Tp15-17-47 antibody as a NS diagnostic biomarker.

Limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, CSF testing used RPR instead of VDRL, and serum testing used RPR instead of TRUST. However, evidence suggests CSF-RPR is a viable alternative when CSF-VDRL testing is unavailable and serum RPR can substitute for TRUST when the latter is inaccessible [31, 32]. Second, although the sample size met the recommended minimum of 10 events per predictor variable, it remains relatively modest, which may affect the stability of the models, particularly after splitting into development and validation cohorts. Larger prospective studies are needed to confirm our findings. Third, this study did not conduct specific syphilis staging analyses, which are crucial for clinical decision-making [33]. Future studies should focus on evaluating the predictive value of different syphilis stages for NS. To improve predictive accuracy, prospective trials incorporating more indicators and patients are planned. Finally, it is worth noting that all participants in this study were Chinese syphilis patients. Validating the model’s performance in diverse populations and environments is necessary to determine its generalizability.

In summary, we demonstrated that the LISA-based detection of Tp15-17-47 multi-antibodies shows strong potential as a noninvasive alternative to conventional CSF–based diagnostic approaches for NS. Compared with single-antigen assays (Tp15, Tp17, or Tp47), the fusion protein–based test achieves a more favorable balance between sensitivity and specificity. Incorporating these antibody levels into an optimized prediction model improved discriminatory ability and net benefit. Future studies should continue refining diagnostic criteria and exploring the broader applicability of this promising biomarker.

Effects of nanoparticles on the mechanical and physical properties of maxillofacial silicones: A literature review
Authors: Mohammad Jowkar, Ammar Neshati, Shima Firouzi, Mona Esmaeili
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Since the 1960s, silicone has been the material of choice for maxillofacial prostheses due to its biocompatibility and favorable mechanical properties. However, reduced strength, color instability, and susceptibility to ultraviolet and oxygen degradation limit its longevity. Nanoparticles such as titanium dioxide (TiO2), zinc oxide (ZnO), SiO2, and ZrO2, have been introduced to enhance silicone’s resistance to environmental stress, abrasion, and photodegradation.Materials and Methods:This systematic review assessed the influence of nanoparticles on the properties of maxillofacial silicones. A literature search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane, and Google Scholar. Eligible studies included systematic reviews and meta-analyses published in the past decade, focusing on the effect of nanoparticles on the mechanical and physical performance of silicones. Data were analyzed according to nanoparticle type and reported outcomes.Results:Six categories of nanoparticles were identified across the reviewed studies. The most frequently examined were TiO2, ZnO, SiO2, and ZrO2, while others included ZrSiO4, TiSiO4, Ag-Zn Zeolite, and Al2O3. Findings demonstrated improvements in tensile strength, hardness, flexibility, compressive resistance, and color stability. Nanoparticles also enhanced resistance to aging and environmental changes, while some (e.g., Ag-Zn Zeolite) provided antimicrobial effects.Conclusion:Incorporating nanoparticles into maxillofacial silicones considerably enhances their mechanical, physical, and biological properties.

INTRODUCTION

Since its introduction in the 1960s, silicone has become one of the most widely used materials for fabricating maxillofacial prostheses.[1] This material is extensively employed due to its favorable mechanical properties, high biocompatibility, and ease of processing.[2] However, silicone faces several limitations that affect the longevity and performance of maxillofacial prostheses.[3] One of the major challenges is the limited clinical lifespan of these prostheses due to tissue changes, decreased tensile strength, and color instability resulting from material degradation over time.[34]

Silicone degradation primarily occurs through photo-oxidative processes, which result from the interaction of oxygen and sunlight with the elastomer structure.[5] Sunlight consists of various wavelengths, including visible, infrared, and ultraviolet (UV) light. Among these, UV radiation has the most significant impact on the polymer structure of silicone.[6] The absorption of UV photons leads to molecular bond breakage and polymer degradation, ultimately causing deformation and reduced prosthesis quality.[6]

To counteract this degradation, various approaches have been explored, including the addition of pigments, opacifiers, nanoparticles, and nano-oxides.[7] The incorporation of nanoparticles into silicone compositions is considered a novel strategy for enhancing the physical properties and durability of these materials.[89] Due to their high surface-to-volume ratio, nanoparticles exhibit unique characteristics compared to macroscopic particles.[8] Depending on their size and concentration, these particles can enhance the mechanical, chemical, and biological properties of silicone. Specifically, nanoparticles act as effective UV shielding agents, as their small size allows them to absorb and reflect UV radiation.[9]

Among various nanoparticles, zinc oxide (ZnO), titanium dioxide (TiO2), and cerium oxide have been widely used to increase silicone’s UV resistance.[10] These nanoparticles not only reduce photo-oxidative effects but also improve the mechanical and optical properties of silicone due to their strong interactions with organic polymers.[10] In addition, the use of SiO2, TiO2, and ZnO nanoparticles enhances silicone’s stability against environmental stress and prevents premature aging of maxillofacial prostheses.[11]

Maxillofacial prostheses play a crucial role in improving the quality of life for patients with craniofacial defects. One of the primary challenges associated with these prostheses is the premature degradation of silicone due to environmental factors such as sunlight, oxygen, and mechanical stresses, which lead to reduced strength, color changes, and decreased lifespan.[5] In this regard, nanoparticles, as innovative additives, have demonstrated significant potential in improving the mechanical, optical, and biological properties of silicone.

Given the increasing research interest in this field, the need for an umbrella review that comprehensively compiles, compares, and analyzes various research findings is more evident than ever. Such a study could provide deeper insights into the effect of nanoparticles on silicone properties, highlight the strengths and weaknesses of existing methods, and guide future research directions. In addition, the integration of nanotechnology with emerging techniques such as 3D printing and composite material engineering may offer new pathways for developing next-generation maxillofacial prostheses with enhanced performance and longevity.

Objectives

This umbrella review study was conducted to investigate the effect of nanoparticles on the properties of maxillofacial silicones, such as mechanical, physical, and chemical properties.

Eligibility criteria and search strategy

A systematic search was performed in electronic databases of PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar until March 2025 by considering language restrictions and according to PRISMA guidelines. The search was performed based on medical subject headings (MeSH) and non-MeSH terms in simple or multiple conjunctions, which included “nanoparticles and maxillofacial silicone,” “nanotechnology and prosthetic materials,” and “systematic review and nanomaterials in maxillofacial prostheses.” The selected articles included systematic reviews and meta-analyses published in the last 10 years that examined the impact of nanoparticles on the mechanical properties of maxillofacial silicones. Studies lacking comparative data or focusing on other types of nanocomposite prostheses were excluded.

Study selection

Two independent reviewers (M.J. and S.F.) evaluated studies for the analysis. Accordingly, on researcher (M.J.) extracted qualitative or quantitative data from the studies, and the other (S.F.) confirmed qualified data. The collected information included the authors’ names, the year and type of the study, the type of nanoparticle and their effects on physical and mechanical properties, the outcome assessment results, the comparison results, and the conclusions.

The extracted data were categorized and analyzed based on the type of nanoparticles (metallic, ceramic, carbon nanotubes, and nanopolymers) and their effects on physical and mechanical properties (strength, flexibility, and resistance to degradation). Finally, the findings were compared to determine the most effective nanoparticles, the greatest improvements in silicone properties, and the differences among studies. Future research directions, including the need for extensive clinical studies, long-term effects of nanoparticles, and the development of hybrid technologies with 3D printing, were also proposed.

Risk of bias assessments

Selected systematic reviews were assessed by two independent, qualified researchers (M.E. and M.T.). Systematic reviews were evaluated based on the risk of bias in systematic reviews (ROBIS) criteria, which has two main phases and one optional stage. The relevance of a study is evaluated in the first stage (optional phase one), then the reviewing process and risk of bias are considered in phases 2 and 3. Four main domains that can cause bias in a review article (study eligibility, selection process of studies, data collection and assessment, and synthesis) are covered by the ROBIS tool. Studies were then classified into three categories: High (3–4 negative), moderate (2 negatives), and low (<2), based on the risk of bias.

A search of PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar identified 578 systematic/meta-analysis review articles. After eliminating duplicates, 185 studies remained for title and abstract screening. Following a thorough evaluation, 21 studies met the inclusion criteria. The full texts were reviewed, and 8 articles were selected for data extraction. Figure 1 and Table 1 present the research strategy and a summary of the key features of these articles.

Nanoparticles used

TiO2 Nanoparticles: Among the reviewed studies, three have examined TiO2 nanoparticles. Barman et al.[12] investigated the effect of TiO2 nanoparticles on the mechanical properties and color stability of maxillofacial silicones. The results indicated that TiO2 nanoparticles improve tensile strength and hardness while enhancing color stability against aging. Gupta et al.[13] demonstrated that TiO2 nanoparticles significantly enhance the resistance of silicones to color changes over time, contributing to increased durability and strength. In addition, Mohammed and Alirhayim[14] highlighted the role of TiO2 nanoparticles in improving resistance to color degradation and aging in maxillofacial silicones.

SiO2 nanoparticles

Among the reviewed studies, two have investigated SiO2 nanoparticles. Barman et al.[12] examined the addition of SiO2 nanoparticles in varying amounts to maxillofacial silicones and assessed their effects on tensile strength, tear resistance, and hardness of the silicones. The results indicated that SiO2 nanoparticles enhanced the strength and tensile resistance of the materials. Bunyan et al.[15] investigated how the incorporation of SiO2 nanoparticles into maxillofacial silicones improved their mechanical performance and increased the color durability of these materials under different conditions.

ZrO2 Nanoparticles: Among the reviewed studies, two have investigated ZrO2 nanoparticles. Barman et al.[12] examined the effect of ZrO2 nanoparticles on enhancing the mechanical properties of silicones. The results indicated that the addition of ZrO2 nanoparticles improved tensile strength, tear resistance, and increased the hardness of the material. Mohammed and Alirhayim[14] found that ZrO2 nanoparticles were particularly effective in enhancing the tear and tensile resistance of maxillofacial silicones.

ZnO nanoparticles

Among the reviewed studies, three have investigated ZnO nanoparticles. Barman et al.[12] examined the effect of ZnO nanoparticles on enhancing the mechanical properties of maxillofacial silicones. The results indicated that these nanoparticles increased tensile strength and tear resistance. Mohammed and Alirhayim[14] reported that ZnO nanoparticles had positive effects on the physical and mechanical properties of silicones, particularly in improving color stability and tensile resistance against aging and environmental changes. In a study by Parameswari et al.,[16] the addition of ZnO nanoparticles to maxillofacial silicones was shown to enhance stability and resistance to compression.

ZrSiO4 and TiSiO4 Nanoparticles: Barman et al.[12] investigated the effect of ZrSiO4 and TiSiO4 nanoparticles in maxillofacial silicones and demonstrated improvements in their mechanical properties, including tensile strength and tear resistance.

Ag-Zn Zeolite Nanoparticles: Barman et al.[12] investigated Ag-Zn Zeolite nanoparticles for enhancing the mechanical properties of maxillofacial silicones. These nanoparticles specifically contributed to increased tensile strength and reduced silicone failure under various pressure conditions.

Al2O3 (Alumina) Nanoparticles: Two studies have examined the effect of Al2O3 nanoparticles on the properties of silicones. Çevik[17] demonstrated that adding these nanoparticles increases the hardness and durability of silicones. In addition, Anandkumar Patil and Raghunath Patil[18] highlighted in their study that the use of Al2O3 nanoparticles improves the flexibility and moldability of silicones.

Physical and mechanical properties

Increased tensile strength and tear resistance

Three studies have examined the effect of nanoparticles on the tensile strength and tear resistance of maxillofacial silicones. This group includes studies in which nanoparticles enhance the tensile strength and tear resistance of these silicones. Various nanoparticles improve the material’s ability to withstand pressure and tension. Barman et al.[12] demonstrated that TiO2 and ZrO2 nanoparticles increase the tensile strength and improve the tear resistance of these silicones. Moreover, Mohammed and Alirhayim[14] emphasized that these nanoparticles play a crucial role in enhancing tensile strength and reducing the tear rate of maxillofacial silicones. In addition, Bunyan et al.[15] also highlighted improvements in the mechanical properties of these silicones, including tensile strength and tear resistance, with the addition of nanoparticles.

Increased hardness and durability

Four studies have investigated the effect of adding nanoparticles on the hardness and durability of maxillofacial silicones. In this group, nanoparticles enhance the hardness and durability of silicones, a crucial property for use in various environments, particularly under high pressure and exposure to different substances. Gupta et al.[13] reported that adding TiO2 nanoparticles significantly increased the hardness of silicones, making them more resistant to pressure-induced damage and abrasion. Similarly, Barman et al.[12] found that SiO2 nanoparticles not only increased the hardness of maxillofacial silicones but also improved their resistance to abrasion and damage. Furthermore, Borg[19] emphasized that adding nanoparticles enhances the adhesion of silicone softeners to dental bases and, particularly at low concentrations, improves hardness and durability over time. Çevik[17] also investigated the effect of nanoparticles on shore a hardness in maxillofacial silicones and reported positive results in increasing hardness and durability.

Increased resistance to discoloration and aging

Three studies have examined the impact of nanoparticles on the resistance of maxillofacial silicones to discoloration and aging. In this group, nanoparticles help prevent color changes and aging of silicones over time, increasing their durability against environmental factors. Gupta et al.[13] found that TiO2 nanoparticles are particularly effective in enhancing the resistance of these silicones to color changes and aging caused by UV light and other environmental factors. Similarly, Barman et al.[12] reported that SiO2 and TiO2 nanoparticles significantly improved the resistance of silicones to color changes and aging. In addition, Mohammed and Alirhayim[14] also highlighted the role of nanoparticles in maintaining color stability and increasing resistance to aging in maxillofacial silicones.

Increased flexibility and malleability

Two studies have explored the effect of nanoparticles on the flexibility and malleability of maxillofacial silicones. This group includes studies where nanoparticles have improved the flexibility and moldability of these silicones. Mohammed and Alirhayim[14] reported that ZnO nanoparticles positively impact the flexibility and malleability of these silicones, enhancing their ability to retain their original shape under different conditions. Similarly, Anandkumar Patil and Raghunath Patil[18] emphasized that adding nanoparticles to maxillofacial silicones improves their flexibility and malleability, thereby enhancing their physical and mechanical properties.

Increased stability and resistance to compression

Two studies have investigated the effect of nanoparticles on the resistance of maxillofacial silicones to compression and heavy loads. These studies examined nanoparticles that enhance the stability and resistance of silicones to compression and heavy loads. Barman et al.[12] found that ZrO2 and TiO2 nanoparticles improved the compression resistance of these silicones and increased their tolerance to heavy loads. In addition, Parameswari et al.[16] conducted a study on the effects of nanoparticles in dental prosthetic materials and reported an increase in compression resistance and structural stability in these materials.

Enhanced antibacterial and antimicrobial properties

This group includes studies that have used nanoparticles as antibacterial and antimicrobial agents to improve the hygienic properties of maxillofacial silicones. Among the reviewed studies, one study specifically examined the antibacterial and antimicrobial characteristics of these nanoparticles. Barman et al.[12] investigated the effect of Ag-Zn Zeolite nanoparticles on the antibacterial and antimicrobial properties of maxillofacial silicone. These nanoparticles were introduced as antibacterial compounds that play a significant role in enhancing the hygienic performance of silicones used in medical and dental environments. The results of this study showed that the addition of these nanoparticles significantly improved the antibacterial activity of silicone and drastically reduced the growth of pathogenic bacteria. Furthermore, the study highlighted that nanofillers have a superior impact on the mechanical properties of silicone compared to microfillers. Specifically, the investigated nanoparticles contributed to increased tensile strength, tear strength, hardness, and elongation at break. In addition, the study emphasized that the type of filler material plays a crucial role in improving the mechanical and functional properties of silicone.

DISCUSSION

The findings of this systematic review indicate that the incorporation of nanoparticles into maxillofacial silicone elastomers has a consistently positive influence on their physical, mechanical, and functional properties. Across the included studies, a wide range of nanofillers such as TiO2, SiO2, ZrO2, ZnO, and hybrid nanoparticles were shown to enhance tensile strength, tear resistance, elongation at break, hardness, color stability, and resistance to environmental degradation.[12131415161718192021] These observations align with the broader literature on polymer nanocomposites, reinforcing the notion that nanoscale fillers can modify the silicone matrix at a molecular level, resulting in improved performance characteristics essential for maxillofacial prosthesis.[12131415161718192021]

Among the studied nanoparticles, TiO2 remains the most frequently investigated, demonstrating pronounced improvements in tensile strength, elastic modulus, and surface hardness.[121321] These enhancements are attributable to its high specific surface area, uniform dispersion capability, and potential to establish strong interfacial bonding with the silicone polymer chains. Furthermore, TiO2’s inherent photostability and ability to scatter UV radiation contribute to its capacity to delay photo-oxidative degradation.[14] This is particularly relevant considering that maxillofacial prostheses are continually exposed to sunlight. The improvement in color stability reported in many studies suggests that TiO2 may play a dual role in reinforcing both the structural and esthetic integrity of silicones.[121314]

Similarly, SiO2 nanoparticles have shown a strong reinforcing effect. Their nanoscale size, high surface reactivity, and ability to form hydrogen bonds within the silicone network appear to support better elasticity, long-term dimensional stability, and enhanced resistance to tear and fatigue.[12131415] SiO2 is also notable for maintaining translucency and optical properties.[16] This makes it especially valuable for facial prostheses where shade matching and life-like appearance are critical to patient acceptance and psychosocial outcomes.

ZrO2 nanoparticles, known for their high fracture toughness, consistently improved tear strength and tensile resistance across the reviewed studies.[1213] Their ability to distribute applied stress and prevent crack propagation suggests that they may be particularly well-suited for prosthetic margins and thin regions prone to mechanical failure.[14] ZnO nanoparticles, demonstrated more variable results, but several studies noted their beneficial impact on flexibility, elongation, and moldability. In addition, ZnO’s inherent UV absorption properties may contribute to improved resistance to color fading and polymer aging.[16]

Beyond mechanical reinforcement, certain nanoparticle systems provide added functionality. For instance, the incorporation of Ag-Zn zeolites or metal-based nanoparticles has been associated with enhanced antimicrobial properties, potentially reducing colonization by Candida species and bacterial biofilms.[20] This feature is highly advantageous in intraoral and perioral prostheses, where moisture and microflora significantly increase the risk of microbial accumulation. The ability of nanoparticles to create a material with improved hygiene, lower odor, and reduced infection potential offers a promising direction for improving patient comfort and reducing the need for frequent prosthesis replacement.

Taken together, these improvements carry meaningful clinical implications. Enhanced mechanical strength reduces the risk of prosthesis tearing, especially along margins that undergo continuous flexion.[1718] Better aging resistance translates into more stable color and texture over time, improving esthetic longevity. Increased elasticity and resilience support natural movement during speaking, chewing, and facial expression. In addition, antimicrobial effects may contribute to better tissue health and prolonged prosthesis lifespan. These combined enhancements underscore the potential of nanotechnology to address longstanding limitations associated with traditional maxillofacial silicones.

However, despite these encouraging results, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the majority of the available evidence derives from in vitro studies, which cannot fully replicate the complex aging and wear conditions of the facial environment. Factors such as sebum, sweat, microbial enzymes, UV exposure, temperature cycling, and mechanical fatigue may alter the behavior of nanocomposite silicones in unpredictable ways. Second, significant heterogeneity exists among studies regarding nanoparticle type, concentration, surface treatment, synthesis method, and dispersion technique. These variations complicate direct comparison and limit the ability to establish standardized protocols or universal recommendations. Some studies also reported diminished properties, such as decreased elongation or increased opacity, when nanoparticles exceeded optimal concentrations.[1920]

Another critical concern relates to biocompatibility and cytotoxicity. Although most studies reported that nanoparticles remained well-encapsulated within the silicone matrix, high concentrations or inadequate surface modification may pose risks of localized irritation, inflammatory reactions, or long-term cytotoxic effects.[18] Very few studies have examined nanoparticle migration, aging-related release, or interactions with biological tissues under real-life conditions.[161718] Therefore, the safety profile of nanoparticle-enhanced maxillofacial silicones remains insufficiently characterized.

Given these limitations, future research should aim to establish standardized testing protocols, including consistent nanoparticle concentrations, evaluation parameters, and aging conditions. High-quality in vivo studies and long-term clinical trials are urgently needed to validate laboratory findings and assess real-world performance, longevity, patient satisfaction, and potential biological risks. Furthermore, exploring hybrid nanoparticle systems, surface-functionalized fillers, and combinations of mechanical and antimicrobial agents may uncover synergistic effects that further enhance silicone properties. Finally, integrating nanoparticles into emerging fabrication technologies, such as 3D printing or digital silicone processing, may open new possibilities for personalized, durable, and esthetically superior maxillofacial prostheses.

CONCLUSION

TiO2, ZrO2, and ZnO enhance tensile strength and tear resistance, whereas SiO2 and TiO2 increase hardness, durability, resistance to environmental aging, and prevent discoloration. In addition, Ag-Zn Zeolite provides antimicrobial activity. Overall, nanoparticles strengthen the mechanical, physical, and biological properties of silicones, ensuring greater longevity, esthetics, and functionality in dental and medical applications.

On the topology of stable minimal hypersurfaces in a homeomorphic S 4 S^{4}
Chao Li, Boyu Zhang

Abstract
. We construct stable minimal hypersurfaces with simple topology in certain compact 4 4 -manifolds X X with boundary, where X X embeds into a smooth manifold homeomorphic to S 4 S^{4} . For example, if X X is equipped with a Riemannian metric g g with positive scalar curvature, we prove the existence of a stable minimal hypersurface M M that is diffeomorphic to either S 3 S^{3} or a connected sum of S 2 × S 1 S^{2}\times S^{1} ’s, ruling out spherical space forms in its prime decomposition. These results imply new theorems on the topology of black holes in four dimensions. The proof involves techniques from geometric measure theory and 4 4 -manifold topology.

Introduction

Given a compact Riemannian manifold
X
with nontrivial topology, a fundamental question is to construct closed minimal surfaces
with controlled topology
in
X

[
17

It is generally impossible to control the topology of a

The primary scope of this paper is to construct stable (or locally minimizing) minimal hypersurfaces with controlled topology in certain

-manifolds under natural curvature conditions. Let

be a smooth orientable

1.1.
Strategy of proof

In
[
28

, White illustrated how to obtain some mild controls on the topology of solutions to the Plateau problem in a ball. His basic observation is that stable minimal hypersurfaces at extremal positions tend to bound topologically simple regions. We adopt this general principle in our construction. Given

(

∗

)

(see Proposition
2.1

The second part of the argument relies heavily on techniques in
4
-manifold topology. Since

satisfies
(

∗

)
‣
1
, Proposition

implies that

admits a locally flat embedding into

that bounds a simply connected region. The embedding problem of three-manifolds into

has been extensively investigated, and a collection of classical results may be found in a recent survey by Hillman

It follows from classical arguments that the only possible nontrivial spherical prime factor in

1.2.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

Remark 2.7
.

If the covering
(\tilde{\Omega}_{M},\tilde{y})\to(Y,y)
is normal and the minimizer is connected, then we may also find the homologically minimizing hypersurface
N
by translating (with deck transformations) a minimizing sequence to intersect a fixed compact set. This is the case treated by White
[
28
]
.

Remark 2.8
.

Using Lemma
2.5
, we actually proved the following existence result for stable minimal hypersurfaces. Let
(\Omega^{n+1},g)
be a compact manifold with nonempty boundary, and
\partial\Omega
is
g
-weakly mean convex. Let
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{g})
be a covering space. Then any nonzero homology class
\alpha\in H_{n}(\tilde{\Omega},\mathbb{Z})
can be represented by a compact area-minimizing hypersurface.

Remark 2.9
.

Although not used in this paper, we observe that an analogous statement also holds for minimal hypersurfaces with obstacles. That is, without assuming that
\partial X
is weakly mean convex, we may still consider the minimization problem

\inf\{\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial\Omega):\Omega\text{ is an open set of }X\text{ containing }Y\}.

Wang
[
25
, Corollary 3.3]
proved that local minimizers of this problem enjoys a
C^{1}
compactness property. Thus, we may consider, among all open sets
\Omega
containing
Y
that locally minimizes
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial\Omega)
, the one with the smallest volume. The proof of Proposition
2.1
carries over to this situation verbatim and implies that
\pi_{1}(Y)\to\pi_{1}(\Omega)
is surjective.

Remark 2.10
.

On the other hand, the proof of Proposition
2.1
does not seem to easily extend to the case of stable constant mean curvature hypersurfaces, or more generally prescribed mean curvature surfaces. One key issue is that, in the universal cover
\tilde{\Omega}_{M}
, other connected components of
\pi^{-1}(Y)
have a reversed bound of mean curvature, when regarded as a barrier for the minimization problem in the homology class of
Y_{0}
.

Lemma 3.1

Suppose

Proof.

Let

\partial A

We claim that

(1)

\pi_{1}(A_{S})\cong*_{i\in S}\,(\pi_{1}(\hat{D}_{i})),

We prove (

(1) We follow an argument from

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(A,M)}

\natural^{k}D^{2}\times S^{2}

∎

This finishes the proof of Proposition

Now we carry out the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let

be a two-sided stable minimal hypersurface as given by Proposition

(3)

\lambda_{1}(-\Delta-W)\geq\lambda>0

∎

4.
A black hole topology theorem

In this section we apply the proof of Theorem

.

Let

5.2.
A PSC embedding of connected sums of lens spaces

Given a PSC metric

on
S^{4}
, we are interested in which
3
-manifolds may appear as a stable minimal hypersurface in
(S^{4},g)

-manifolds in this form admits a smooth embedding into

[
10
]
from 1938 states that for such an embedding to exist,
\Tor(H_{1}(M))=G\oplus G
for some abelian group

(we again refer the readers to the survey article by Hillman
[
12
]
for the collection of classical results of the embedding problem). The most basic example of such a

-manifold with nontrivial

is the connected sum of two lens spaces,

when

is odd, as constructed by Zeeman
[
29
]

S^{4}
.

Let
S^{3}=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1\}
. Let
p\neq 0
and
p,q
be coprime. Consider the action of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
on

generated by

f:(z,w)\mapsto(e^{\frac{1}{p}\cdot{2\pi i}}z,e^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot 2\pi i}w).

Then the action is free, and the quotient manifold is the lens space
L(p,q)
. Let
\sim
denote the equivalence relation on

induced by the action by

. Denote by
\tau:S^{3}\to S^{3}
the conjugation map defined by
\tau(z,w)=(\bar{z},\bar{w})
.

The next lemma reinterprets a construction of Schubert
[
21

so that we can keep track of the curvature in Zeeman’s construction.

Lemma 5.3
.

\tau
induces an involution on

. The quotient space with respect to this involution is

, and the quotient map is a double branched cover. When

is odd, the branching locus is a smooth knot.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that

, so
\tau
induces an involution on
(S^{3}/\sim)=L(p,q)
.

Let
T_{1}=\{(z,w)\in S^{3}\mid|z|\geq|w|\}
,

. Then
f(T_{1})=T_{1}
,

. A fundamental domain of the action of

on

F_{1}:=\{(z,w)\in S^{3}\mid arg(z)\in[0,2\pi/p],|z|\geq|w|\}\cong[0,2\pi/p]\times D^{2},

where

is identified with the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
, and the above diffeomorphism is defined by

(z,w)\mapsto(arg(z),\sqrt{2}w).

The gluing map on

(0,x)\sim(2\pi/p,e^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot 2\pi i}x)

for all

, so

is a solid torus

.

We write down a diffeomorphism between

and

explicitly.
Identify

with the unit circle in

. Let

\bar{\varphi}(\theta,x)=(e^{ip\theta},e^{-iq\theta}x),

then
\bar{\varphi}
induces a diffeomorphism
\varphi:(T_{1}/\sim)\to S^{1}\times D^{2}
.

The map
\tau
induces an involution on

\displaystyle\tau|_{T_{1}/\sim}:[0,2\pi/p]\times D^{2}/\sim

\displaystyle\to[0,2\pi/p]\times D^{2}/\sim

\displaystyle(\theta,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto(2\pi/p-\theta,e^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot 2\pi i}\bar{x}).

The fixed point set of

consists of two arcs, represented in the fundamental domain by

(4)

\{\pi/p\}\times\{re^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot\pi i}\mid r\in[-1,1]\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{0\}\times\{r\mid r\in[-1,1]\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\varphi\circ(\tau|_{T_{1}/\sim})\circ\varphi^{-1}(e^{ip\theta},e^{-iq\theta}x)

\displaystyle=(e^{ip(2\pi/p-\theta)},e^{-iq((2\pi/p-\theta)}e^{\frac{q}{p}\cdot 2\pi i}\bar{x})

\displaystyle=(e^{-ip\theta},e^{iq\theta}\bar{x}).

So the action of

on

is the product of two reflections on

and

and the quotient map is a double branched cover.

Similarly, the involution

The branching locus is the image of four arcs on

4

F_{2}:=\{(z,w)\in S^{3}\mid arg(w)\in[0,2\pi/p],|w|\geq|z|\}\cong D^{2}\times[0,2\pi/p],

be a fundamental domain of

\{re^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}\cdot\pi i}\mid r\in[-1,1]\}\times\{\pi/p\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{r\mid r\in[-1,1]\}\times\{0\}.

The endpoints of the four arcs have the following preimages in

(1)

The endpoints of

5.3.
The Dahl-Larsson example

In
[
5
]
, Dahl-Larsson constructed, for all

, an asymptotically flat manifold
(X^{n},g)
with zero scalar curvature such that its apparent horizon is diffeomorphic to the unit normal bundle of a codimension
\geq 3
submanifold
N
. We briefly discuss their discussion in the case we consider in our paper.

Let
\delta
be the flat metric on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be an embedded curve. Consider the Green’s function

G(x)=\int_{\gamma}|x-y|^{-2}dy

with poles along
\gamma
. It is well-known that
G
satisfies the asymptotics

G(x)\sim\frac{A}{|x|_{\delta}^{2}},~~|x|\to\infty;\quad G(x)\sim\frac{B}{\dist_{\delta}(x,\gamma)},~~x\to\gamma,

For some constants
A,B
. For
\varepsilon>0
, consider the conformally deformed metric

g_{\varepsilon}=(1+\varepsilon G)^{2}\delta.

The manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g_{\varepsilon})
is complete, noncompact and has two ends:
E_{1}
near the infinity of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and
E_{2}
near
\gamma
. For each
\varepsilon>0
,
g_{\varepsilon}
has zero scalar curvature since
\Delta_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon}=0
.
g_{\varepsilon}
on
E_{1}
is asymptotically flat.
g_{\delta}
on
E_{2}
enjoys a similar asymptotic decay. To see this, let
s
be the arclength coordinate of
\gamma
,

the distance function to
\gamma
. Then locally we may write the metric
\delta=ds^{2}+dt^{2}+t^{2}g_{s,t}
, where
g_{s,t}
is a metric on the unit
S^{2}
converging smoothly to the round metric as

. Setting
r=-\log t
, we see that

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon e^{r})^{2}(ds^{2}+e^{-2r}dr^{2}+e^{-2r}g_{s,r})

\displaystyle=(e^{-r}+\varepsilon)^{2}\left(e^{2r}ds^{2}+dr^{2}+g_{S^{2}}\right)+o(1).

Therefore,
g_{\varepsilon}
is asymptotic to the product metric on
S^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
on
E_{2}
. In particular, near the infinity of
E_{2}
, it admits a mean convex foliation by

.

(
[

of

is the graph of a smooth function on the unit normal bundle in normal coordinates for
\gamma

to lie between the tubular hypersurfaces around

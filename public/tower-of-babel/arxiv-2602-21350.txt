The Inverse Born Rule Fallacy: On the Informational Limits of Phase-Locked Amplitude Encoding
Sebastian Zając, Jacob L. Cybulski, Bartosz Dziewit, Tomasz Kulpa

Abstract
In Quantum Machine Learning (QML) and Quantum Finance, amplitude encoding is often motivated by its logarithmic storage capacity [ 5 ] . This paradigm typically relies on the mapping ψ = P \psi=\sqrt{P} , treating the quantum state as a derivative of a classical probability distribution P P . By restricting the data manifold to the positive real orthant 𝒮 + \mathcal{S}^{+} , the accessible Hilbert space is effectively abelianized, rendering the representation “phase-deaf”. We rigorously establish that while P P is a projection of | ψ | 2 |\psi|^{2} , the simple square-root mapping fails to recover the non-commutative structure necessary for genuine quantum advantage in classification tasks. Furthermore, we clarify why applying basis changes (like Hadamard gates) to these states fails to replicate the computational power of active phase-kickback mechanisms. Finally, we advocate for Dynamical Hamiltonian Encoding (based on QIFT), where data generates non-commutative evolution rather than serving as a static, phase-locked vector.

2
The Abelian Trap: Commutativity of the Positive Orthant

A quantum advantage usually relies on the non-commutativity of observables (Heisenberg uncertainty) or the non-commutativity of gates.
We show that restricting states to
\mathcal{S}^{+}

[
1
]
trivializes this structure.

Theorem 2.1
(Abelianization of Observables)

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}
denote the set of linear operators on
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^{N}
that map the real positive orthant
\mathcal{S}^{+}
into itself and are self-adjoint. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}
is a commutative algebra.

Proof

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}
be a self-adjoint operator. For
A
to preserve the “classicality” of the probability distribution (mapping real non-negative vectors to real non-negative vectors) in the computational basis, its off-diagonal elements must be non-negative (Perron-Frobenius like conditions). In the strict sense of preserving the probability interpretation without inducing rotations, the natural operators are diagonal scaling matrices
A=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
. Diagonal matrices commute:
[A,B]=0
. Thus, the algebra is abelian, and the system behaves classically.

Corollary 1
(Absence of Destructive Interference)

For any superposition supported strictly within
\mathcal{S}^{+}
, the associated probability density is strictly additive or super-additive. Destructive interference requires relative phases
e^{i\phi}
such that
\cos(\phi)<0
. By fixing
\phi=0
, we disable the interference mechanism entirely.

3
The Phase-Deafness and The Passive Data Problem

A common objection is that quantum algorithms typically apply unitary operators (like the Hadamard gate
H
) specifically to exit the positive orthant. One might argue: “The starting point is
\mathcal{S}^{+}
, but the computation happens outside of it.” We argue that this view misses the distinction between
passive
and
active
data participation.

3.1
Linear Mixing vs. Phase Injection

Consider a unitary transformation
U
applied to a phase-locked state
\ket{\psi_D}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}\ket{x}
. The probability of measuring a resulting basis state
\ket{y}
is given by the Born rule
P(y)=|\innerproduct{y}{U|\psi_D}|^{2}
. Expanding this term reveals the mechanism of interference:

P(y)=\underbrace{\sum_{x}p_{x}|U_{yx}|^{2}}_{\text{Classical Mixture}}+\underbrace{\sum_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{x}p_{x^{\prime}}}U_{yx}U_{yx^{\prime}}^{*}}_{\text{Interference Terms}}.

(2)

The first term represents a classical sum of probabilities. The second term contains the interference. Crucially, in the
\sqrt{P}
encoding, the coefficients
\sqrt{p_{x}p_{x^{\prime}}}
are strictly positive real numbers. Consequently, the sign of the interference (constructive vs. destructive) is determined
exclusively
by the fixed matrix elements
U_{yx}U_{yx^{\prime}}^{*}
. The data itself (
p_{x}
) acts merely as a scalar weight magnitude and cannot flip the sign of the interaction.

Contrast this with
active phase encoding
, where inputs
x
appear as complex phases
e^{i\phi(x)}
. The interference term becomes:

\sum_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{x}p_{x^{\prime}}}e^{i(\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime}))}U_{yx}U_{yx^{\prime}}^{*}.

(3)

Here, the data-dependent term
(\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime}))
can actively rotate the interference vector in the complex plane, switching from constructive to destructive interference based on the input features. This enables the quantum model to learn non-linear decision boundaries (such as the parity function) that are topologically inaccessible to the phase-locked representation.

3.2
The Diagonal Trap

The problem is most acute when subsequent processing layers are diagonal in the computational basis (e.g.,
R_{z}(\theta)
rotations).

|\langle i|R_{z}(\theta)|\sqrt{P}\rangle|^{2}=|e^{-i\theta/2}\sqrt{p_{i}}|^{2}=p_{i}.

(4)

For a phase-locked state, diagonal gates induce
global phase shifts
relative to each basis state that are invisible to the final probability measurement. The state is “phase-deaf” to diagonal operators. To make these rotations observable, one must rotate out of the basis (via
H
), but as established in Eq. (
2
), without data-driven phase modulation, this only results in a linear mixing of the original probabilities.

5
Conclusion

The transformation
\psi=\sqrt{P}
, although ubiquitous in quantum state preparation routines, constitutes an “ontological inversion” when utilized for feature representation. By assigning primacy to the probability distribution, this construction effectively suppresses phase degrees of freedom, thereby inducing an abelian structure that rules out the realization of genuine quantum advantage.

We infer that overcoming these constraints necessitates moving beyond static, phase-suppressed representations. A natural resolution is provided by
Dynamical Hamiltonian Encoding
, instantiated within the
Quantum Information Field Theory (QIFT)
framework. In this approach, data are treated as active generators of quantum dynamics rather than as passive probability amplitudes, thereby reinstating the non-commutative geometric structure that is essential for high-dimensional quantum data analysis.

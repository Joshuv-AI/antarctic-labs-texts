Greedy Regular Convolutions
Jan Snellman

Abstract
Narkiewicz classified regular convolutions on arithmetical functions by means of families { π p : p ​ prime } \{\pi_{p}:p\text{ prime}\} of decompositions of ℕ {\mathbb{N}} into arithmetic progressions. Most regular convolutions, such as the Dirichlet convolution and the unitary convolution are homogeneous : all π p \pi_{p} are equal. The unitary convolution is furthermore bounded : there is a common finite bound for the lengths of the blocks of π p \pi_{p} . In this article we define, for each positive integer d d , the greedy convolution of length d d . It is a regular, homogeneous, bounded convolution where the maximal length of a block of π p \pi_{p} is d d . The case d = 1 d=1 yields the unitary convolution and the case d = 2 d=2 produces the "ternary convolution". These two convolutions are the only regular, homogeneous convolutions where all blocks of π p \pi_{p} have the same length. Thus, for the greedy convolutions with block-length d > 2 d>2 , the blocks of π p \pi_{p} have unequal length. We describe the case d = 3 d=3 in detail: the lengths are 3 and 1. The set of primitive exponents , the smallest positive element of each block in the decomposition π p \pi_{p} , can be generated by a simple recursive procedure that we name selective sifting . The structure of the primitive exponents for greedy convolutions of higher length is surprisingly intricate, given the straight-forward definition. We give a conjecture about the case d = 4 d=4 .

Convolution products on arithmetical functions

2.1
The vector space
\Gamma
and its topology

The set of all
arithmetical functions
, complex-valued functions defined on the positive integers, is
denoted
\Gamma={\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}
. It is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space.
It is also a topological space when endowed with the
product topology. Equivalently, we can define the
order

{\mathrm{o}}(f)
of

as

{\mathrm{o}}(f)=\sup\left\{{j\in{\mathbb{P}}}:{i<j\implies f(i)=0}\right\},

(7)

and then the

of

as
1/{\mathrm{o}}(f)
; the

between two elements of

is then

This metric is an

d(f,h)\leq\max\left(d(f,g),d(g,h)\right),

and the topology induced by this ultrametric is the same as the product topology
[
11
,
10
]
.

For any
f\in\Gamma
,

f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{P}}}f(j)e_{j},

(8)

and this sum converges absolutely, irrespective of reordering of terms.

Remark 1
.

In this context, the ring
{\mathbb{C}}
is
usually given the discrete topology.
This means that the sequence of constant functions
2^{-k}\zeta
does not
converge to the zero function; rather, each function in the sequence have norm 1.

An arithmetical function
f\in\Gamma
is
multiplicative
if
f(mn)=f(m)f(n)
whenever
\gcd(m,n)=1
,
and
totally multiplicative
if
f(mn)=f(m)f(n)
for all
m,n
.
The zeta function is totally multiplicative.

A
-convolutions on

There are numerous well-studied

on

-algebra.
Such a convolution is usually required to be associative,

-bilinear, and continuous as a

We have already defined the Dirichlet convolution (
4

(

Definition 1

Let

Suppose that

\begin{split}A:{\mathbb{P}}&\to 2^{{\mathbb{P}}}\\

(10)

Let

The convolution

(ii)

A(p^{k})=\left\{{1,p^{t},p^{2t},\dots,p^{rt}=p^{k}}\right\},

(Narkiewicz)
.

Let

into

belonging to the same decomposition have a positive integer in common. Associate to

\prod p_{i}^{a_{i}}\in A\Bigl(\prod p_{i}^{b_{i}}\Bigr)\quad\iff\quad\forall i:a_{i}\leq b_{i}\text{ and }a_{i},b_{i}\text{ belong to the same block of }\pi_{p_{i}}.

Narkiewicz defined the notions of

.

For a regular convolution

Then

If

Remark 2

Note that

e_{a}*_{A}e_{b}=\begin{cases}e_{ab},&a,b\in A(ab);\\
\mathbf{0},&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

This—together with distributivity,

\Bigl(\sum_{i}f(i)e_{i}\Bigr)*_{A}\Bigl(\sum_{j}g(j)e_{j}\Bigr)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}f(i)g(j)(e_{i}*_{A}e_{j}).

2.3
Homogeneous bounded regular convolutions

An
A
-convolution is

[
1

if for every pair of primes
p_{1},p_{2}

If

The simplest case is when
\pi=\left\{{{\mathbb{N}}}\right\}

The resulting convolution is the Dirichlet convolution, with

Cashwell and Everett

famously proved that

with this product
is isomorphic to the (full) ring of power series on countably many indeterminates
(for the

are primitive.
The dense subring generated by the

We will henceforth restrict our study to

.

By a slight abuse of notation, for a bounded homogeneous regular convolution we say that

(a)

the type of

is the type of

, where

(b)

,

if

.

If

denotes the primitive elements (w.r.t. some bounded homogeneous regular convolution), then

where the union is disjoint, and

\limsup_{{n\to\infty}}\frac{\#\left\{{s\in S}:{s\leq n}\right\}}{n}.

If these limits coincide, then the
natural density
(or
asymptotic density
)
\mathbf{d}(S)
exists and is
equal to this common value.

The following properties of the natural density are elementary:

Lemma 4
.

Let
S,T\subset{\mathbb{P}}
both have natural density, and let
k\in{\mathbb{P}}
.
Then
\mathbf{d}(S+\left\{{k}\right\})=\mathbf{d}(S)
, and

\mathbf{d}(S)=k\mathbf{d}(kS)
. If
S
is finite, then
\mathbf{d}(S)=0
, and

\mathbf{d}({\mathbb{P}}\setminus S)=1
. More generally, if
S\cap T=\emptyset
, then
\mathbf{d}(S\cup T)=\mathbf{d}(S)+\mathbf{d}(T)
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
(s_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive integers such that
s_{i+1}-s_{i}>1
for all
i
.
Let
p
be a prime number, let
S
denote the image of the sequence, and put

T=\left\{{n\in{\mathbb{P}}}:{\operatorname{val}_{p}(n)\in S}\right\}.

Then
T
has natural density

\mathbf{d}(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(p^{-s_{k}}-p^{-s_{k}-1}).

(21)

Proof.

It is well-known
[
5
]
that the set

\left\{{n\in{\mathbb{P}}}:{\operatorname{val}_{p}(n)=s_{k}}\right\}

has natural density

p^{-s_{k}}-p^{-s_{k}-1}.

Furthermore, it follows immediately from Lemma
4
that if

A_{1},\dots,A_{r}
are finitely many disjoint sets, having natural density
c_{1},\dots,c_{r}
,
then
\cup_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
has natural density
\sum_{{i=1}}^{r}c_{i}
.
Since the right-hand side of (
21
) converges, the result follows.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
n>1
an integer,
d\geq 0
an integer.
Then the set

\left\{{m\in{\mathbb{P}}}:{\operatorname{val}_{p}(m)\equiv d\ \textup{(mod }n\textup{)}}\right\}

has natural density

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(p^{-nk-d}-p^{-nk-d-1})=p^{-d}(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{-nk}=p^{-d}(1-p^{-1})(1-p^{-n})^{-1}.

Similarly, we have that

Corollary 7
.

Let
p\neq q
be prime numbers,
n,m>1
integers,
c,d\geq 0
integers.
Then the set

\left\{{s\in{\mathbb{P}}}:{\operatorname{val}_{p}(s)\equiv d\ \textup{(mod }n\textup{)},\,\operatorname{val}_{q}(s)\equiv c\ \textup{(mod }m\textup{)}}\right\}

has natural density

p^{-d}(1-p^{-1})(1-p^{-n})^{-1}q^{-c}(1-q^{-1})(1-q^{-m})^{-1.}

Proof.

Follows from Corollary
6
and the Chinese Remainder Theorem.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Consider the greedy convolution of length

be a positive integer.

(i)

The type of

is the element on the same branch of

which is adjacent to the root. Equivalently, it is the
element in the same column but in row one of

(ii)

The integer

is primitive if and only if it is adjacent to the root in
T_{d}(n)
, in other words,
if it is in row one of
A^{(d)}
.

(iii)

If
n
is primitive, its rank is the length of the branch of
T_{d}
that contains it; equivalently,
it is the number of non-zero elements in the column of

that contains it (for

(iv)

The height

of

is the distance in

from the root to

(equivalently,
the index of the row in

that contains

Proof.

Immediate; note that the length of a branch of

is the number of edges in it, or equivalently the

∎

Lemma 11
.

Consider the greedy convolution of length
d
.

(i)

The exponent
1
is primitive, and
2,3,\dots,d
are not primitive.

(ii)

If
n
is not primitive, there is a unique pair

(a,k)\in\left\{{1,2,\dots,n-1}\right\}\times\left\{{2,\dots,d}\right\}

with

and

.

(iii)

The exponent

is

if and only if

with

, and

0pt{d}{2a}=2,0pt{d}{3a}=3,\dots,0pt{d}{(k-1)a}=k-1.

We can assume that

The number

Theorem 12

(Gavell)
.

For

, the partition
\mathcal{S}^{(d)}
contains some part

Proof.

Suppose not, and that the first few blocks are

\begin{split}b_{1}&=\left\{{0,1,2,\dots,d}\right\}\\
b_{2}&=\left\{{0,d+1,2(d+1),3(d+1),\dots,d(d+1)}\right\}\\

b_{4}&=\left\{{0,d+3,2(d+3),3(d+3),\dots,d(d+3)}\right\}\\

(22)

is odd, then consider the blocks

and
b_{4}
starting with

as their

and
b_{4}

Remark 6

The proof is taken almost verbatim from

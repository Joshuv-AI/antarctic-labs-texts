Decomposable semigroups on C*-algebras and D-divisible dynamical maps
Krzysztof Szczygielski

Abstract
. We analyze semigroups of decomposable maps on C*-algebras in context of the algebraic structure of associated infinitesimal generators. Case of von Neumann algebras, including B ⁡ ( ℋ ) B(\mathcal{H}) for ℋ \mathcal{H} a Hilbert space, is also addressed. We then elaborate on D-divisible (decomposably divisible) dynamical maps on the Banach space of trace class operators. Our analysis extends earlier results on decomposable dynamical maps on matrix algebras (J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 56 485202) and provides a partial generalization of the seminal work of Lindblad (Commun. Math. Phys. 48 119-130) on completely positive semigroups.



1.
Introduction

It was shown in a seminal paper by G. Lindblad
[
7
]
that a family
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
of linear maps acting on a unital C*-algebra
\mathscr{A}
is a uniformly continuous semigroup of completely positive (CP), unital maps if and only if its
generator

L
is a
completely dissipative
*-map on
\mathscr{A}
. Complete dissipativity means that
L(\mathds{1})=0
and, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, an extension
L_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes L
acting on a C*-algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
is
dissipative
, i.e. satisfies inequality

L_{n}(X^{*}X)-L_{n}(X^{*})X-X^{*}L_{n}(X)\geqslant 0

(1.1)

for all
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
. Furthermore,
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a
quantum dynamical semigroup
on a von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{A}
, i.e. a uniformly continuous semigroup of weakly-* continuous unital CP maps on
\mathscr{A}
, if and only if
L
is completely dissipative and weakly-* continuous. For a perhaps most robust case of
\mathscr{A}=B(\mathcal{H})
, a von Neumann algebra of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, such
L
admits a structure

L(A)=i\left[H,A\right]+\phi(A)-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\phi(\mathds{1}),A\right\}

(1.2)

for all
A\in B(\mathcal{H})
, where
H\in B(\mathcal{H})
is self-adjoint and
\phi:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
is CP and weakly-* continuous (here,
\left[A,B\right]=AB-BA
and
\left\{A,B\right\}=AB+BA
respectively denote the standard commutator and anticommutator of operators;
\mathds{1}
will always denote a unit in an algebra). Formula (
1.2
) defines the most general form of a generator of uniformly continuous dynamical semigroup on
B(\mathcal{H})
in dual (i.e. Heisenberg) picture and is often referred to as the celebrated
Lindblad
(or
standard
)
form
in a literature, or interchangeably, as the
GKSL
(Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad)
form
independently developed by Gorini
et al.

[
6
]
in matrix algebra case.

Recently, a generalization of the result of
[
6
]
was obtained in
[
12
]
in a case
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
B(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
under a less restrictive assumption that resulting semigroup is not specifically CP but rather
decomposable
. In particular, by adopting proof techniques of
[
6
]
(see also
[
1
,
2
,
9
]
) it was shown that a semigroup
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
on
\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is unital and decomposable (actually trace preserving and decomposable) if and only if
L
is of a form (
1.2
) for Hermitian matrix
H
and
decomposable
map
\phi:\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e. slightly more general than (
1.2
). The aim of this article is to extend results of
[
12
]
onto a general C*-algebraic case. In particular, we show correspondence between Lindblad’s notion of dissipativity and decomposability of semigroups on C*-algebras and give a prescription for structure of the generator in a weakly-* continuous case of von Neumann algebras, similarly to
[
7
]
.

The article is structured as follows. In the section
2
we present a concise summary of necessary mathematical notions and previous results on the matter, including main results of the paper by Lindblad. Our results are divided into three main parts. Cases of both unital and nonunital semigroups on C*-algebras are treated respectively in sections
3.2
(Theorems
1
–
5
) and
3.3
(Theorems
6
and
7
), while in the section
4
we remark on the D-divisibility of quantum dynamical maps on
B_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, the Banach space of trace class operators (Theorem
8
).

2.
Background

2.1.
Positive maps

As we will work with decomposable maps, we start with a recollection of notions regarding positive maps and their subclasses and, since our interest is focused on semigroups, we restrict to endomorphisms. A bounded, linear map
\phi:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
on a partially ordered C*-algebra
\mathscr{A}
is called a
positive map
if it maps a cone of positive elements into itself,
\phi(a^{*}a)\geqslant 0
. If an extension
\phi_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\phi
acting on a tensor C*-algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
via
\phi_{n}([a_{ij}])=[\phi(a_{ij})]
is positive for some
n
, we say
\phi
is
n
-positive
. If
\phi
is
n
-positive for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we call such map
completely positive
(CP); we will write
\phi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
. Similarly, for
\tau
denoting the
transposition
on
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, a map
\phi
will be called
n
-copositive
if
\tau\otimes\phi
is positive for some
n
, and
completely copositive
(coCP) if it is
n
-copositive for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
; we will denote this as
\phi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{coCP}}(\mathscr{A})
. It is straightforward to notice that
\phi
is
n
-copositive (resp. coCP) if and only if
\tau\circ\phi
is
n
-positive (resp. CP), where
\tau
in the latter means the
transposition
on
\mathscr{A}
represented as a C*-subalgebra of
B(\mathcal{H})
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
(we will use
\tau
symbol regardless of the underlying algebra to denote a transposition). We recall that a transposition
\tau:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
may be defined as

\tau(A)=A^{\tau}=JA^{*}J,\quad A\in B(\mathcal{H}),

(2.1)

where
J:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is an antilinear, idempotent isometry of complex conjugation of vectors in
\mathcal{H}
with respect to a chosen orthonormal basis.
Finally, a bounded positive map
\varphi:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
is called
decomposable
, or
\varphi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A})
, if it lays in the convex hull of
\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})\cup\operatorname{\mathsf{coCP}}(\mathscr{A})
, i.e. may be expressed as

\varphi=\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}

(2.2)

for some
\phi_{1}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
,
\phi_{2}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{coCP}}(\mathscr{A})
. Woronowicz
[
13
]
showed all positive maps between matrix algebras
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
are decomposable when
mn\leqslant 6
, which fails to hold in generality.

Let
\mathscr{A}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
for a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Dilation theorems of Stinespring
[
10
]
and Størmer
[
11
]
imply that for any
\varphi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A})
there exist Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{K}_{1}
,
\mathcal{K}_{2}
, *-homomorphism
\pi_{1}:\mathscr{A}\to B(\mathcal{K}_{1})
, *-antihomomorphism
\pi_{2}:\mathscr{A}\to B(\mathcal{K}_{2})
and linear operators
V_{1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}_{1}
,
V_{2}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
such that

\varphi(a)=V^{*}_{1}\pi_{1}(a)V_{1}+V^{*}_{2}\pi_{2}(a)V_{2},\quad a\in\mathscr{A},

(2.3)

where we recovered both CP and coCP parts of
\varphi
, i.e.
\phi_{1}=V^{*}_{1}\pi_{1}(\cdot)V_{1}
,
\phi_{2}=V^{*}_{2}\pi_{2}(\cdot)V_{2}
. Equivalently,
\varphi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A})
if and only if
\varphi(a)=V^{*}\pi(a)V
for
V:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{2}
,
V=V_{1}+V_{2}
and
\pi:\mathscr{A}\to B(\mathcal{K}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{2})
,
\pi=\pi_{1}+\pi_{2}
being a Jordan morphism.

2.2.
Completely positive semigroups

Let now
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a one-parameter, uniformly continuous semigroup generated by a *-map
L\in B(\mathscr{A})
on a unital C*-algebra
\mathscr{A}
. As we already mentioned in the Introduction, it was shown by Lindblad
[
7
, Theorem 1]
that all maps
e^{tL}
are unital and CP if and only if
L
is completely dissipative, i.e. when
L(\mathds{1})=0
and
L_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes L
satisfies dissipativity condition (
1.1
) for all
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Further,
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a
dynamical semigroup
, i.e. a uniformly continuous semigroup of unital, CP and weakly-* continuous maps on a von Neumann algebra if and only if
L
is completely dissipative and weakly-* continuous. Completely dissipative generators are further specified by two results. First
[
7
, Proposition 5]
, for any weakly-* continuous completely dissipative
L
on a von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{A}
there exists weakly-* continuous
\phi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
and a self-adjoint
H\in\mathscr{A}
such that

L(a)=i\left[H,a\right]+\phi(a)-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\phi(\mathds{1}),a\right\},\quad a\in\mathscr{A}.

(2.4)

Second, the converse statement
[
7
, Proposition 6]
says that for any
\phi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
and
H\in\mathscr{A}
,
H^{*}=H
, a *-map
L
given by (
2.4
) is completely dissipative; here, it is only assumed that
\mathscr{A}
is a C*-algebra, so this result has a broader scope. Finally, for
\mathscr{A}
being a von Neumann algebra
B(\mathcal{H})
, application of all preceding results together with Kraus lemma yield
[
7
, Theorem 2]
that
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a uniformly continuous dynamical semigroup on
B(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
L
is completely dissipative and weakly-* continuous, which in turn happens if and only if

L(A)=i\left[H,A\right]+\sum_{n}\left(V_{n}^{*}AV_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{V_{n}^{*}V_{n},A\right\}\right)

(2.5)

where
H=H^{*}
and
\sum_{n}V_{n}^{*}V_{n}
weakly-* converges in
B(\mathcal{H})
.

3.
Decomposable semigroups

In this section we focus on semigroups of decomposable maps, with unital case treated in Section
3.2
and nonunital in Section
3.3
. Some of our results will concern weakly-* continuous maps on von Neumann algebra, i.e. generalization of quantum dynamical semigroups towards decomposable case – naturally, we will call them
decomposable dynamical semigroups
(see the definition below). We adopt a following notation from
[
7
]
: for any set
\mathcal{X}
of bounded linear maps on a von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{A}
we denote with
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
its subset consisting of maps continuous with respect to the weak-* topology on
\mathscr{A}
. It is known that
B(\mathscr{A})_{\sigma}
is a uniformly closed subset of
B(\mathscr{A})
. From here onwards, all *-algebras are assumed unital.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a von Neumann algebra. A uniformly continuous semigroup
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
of decomposable, unital weakly-* continuous maps on
\mathscr{A}
will be called a decomposable dynamical semigroup.

We stress here that the name
decomposable dynamical semigroup
not necessarily implies such object can
a priori
describe a realizable quantum evolution, since physicality of non-CP maps is disputable in general. We however adopt this name for convenience and rather focus on its mathematical properties as a generalization of a CP semigroup onto larger class of maps.

3.1.
General properties

By Lumer-Phillips and Hille-Yosida theorems, the
generator

L\in B(\mathscr{A})
of the semigroup is a *-map satisfying

L=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\left\|L-t^{-1}(e^{tL}-\mathrm{id})\right\|,

(3.1)

i.e. is a uniform derivative of
e^{tL}
at
t=0
. In consequence,
L
is necessarily weakly-* continuous.

The decomposition (
2.2
) is highly nonunique in general. Unfortunately this fact poses a problem for our case: in particular, even though
e^{tL}
is smooth in
t
and decomposes into

e^{tL}=\phi_{t}+\tau\circ\psi_{t}

(3.2)

for some
\phi_{t},\psi_{t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
for all
t\geqslant 0
, nonuniqueness disallows us from claiming not only analytical properties, but even existence of well-defined functions
t\mapsto\phi_{t}
and
t\mapsto\psi_{t}
. Therefore, some of our results will be explicitly formulated under the assumption that
\phi_{t}
and
\psi_{t}
exist as smooth functions; we will call
e^{tL}

smoothly decomposable
in such case. We however suppose that it is possible, in principle, to show rigorously that such smooth decomposition can be shown to exist independently of the dimension, for any smooth curve in
B(\mathscr{A})
.

Remark 1
.

The smooth decomposability requirement can be relaxed so that
\phi_{t}
and
\psi_{t}
appearing in decomposition (
3.2
) are only assumed to have right derivatives at
t=0
. Such alleviation does not influence our results, simultaneously allowing for much more freedom in selecting both the CP and coCP parts of the decomposition.

For any *-algebra
\mathscr{X}
and any
T\in B(\mathscr{X})
let us define the
dissipation function

D_{T}:\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}
by

D_{T}(x)=T(x^{*}x)-T(x^{*})a-x^{*}T(x),\quad x\in\mathscr{X},

(3.3)

following Lindblad
[
7
]
. Just like therein, a significant role will be played by maps enjoying
D_{T}(x)\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathscr{X}
; such
T
will be said to have a
dissipation property
, or
T\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}}(\mathscr{X})
. Similarly, if
T_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes T
is in
\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}}(\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{X}))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e. every extension
T_{n}
has a dissipation property, we will say
T
has a
complete dissipation property
and write
T\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}_{\infty}}(\mathscr{X})
. Note that when
T
has a dissipation property, then necessarily

D_{T}(\mathds{1})=-T(\mathds{1})\geqslant 0,

(3.4)

so
T(\mathds{1})\leqslant 0
. Evidently, in contrast to the approach exploited by Lindblad, we do not demand
\mathds{1}\in\ker{T}
so classes
\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}}(\mathscr{X})
and
\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}_{\infty}}(\mathscr{X})
are broader than classes of dissipative and completely dissipative maps, respectively, discussed extensively in
[
7
]
.

If a positive map
\phi
on a C*-algebra satisfies
\phi(\mathds{1})\leqslant\mathds{1}
we will call such map
subunital
. Two following results, Lemmas
1
and
2
, will be of importance:

Lemma 1
.

Let
M
be a *-map on a C*-algebra
\mathscr{A}
. If
M
has a complete dissipation property and satisfies
M(\mathds{1})\leqslant 0
then it generates a semigroup
(e^{tM})_{t\geqslant 0}
of CP, subunital maps on
\mathscr{A}
.

Proof.

Let us define a new map
G:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
by

M(a)=G(a)+\frac{1}{2}\left\{M(\mathds{1}),a\right\},\quad a\in\mathscr{A}.

(3.5)

Then, one checks with a bit of algebra that
G
is a *-map, satisfies
G(\mathds{1})=0
and is in
\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}_{\infty}}(\mathscr{A})
, so it is completely dissipative in the sense of Lindblad. By
[
7
, Theorem 1]
, it generates a semigroup
(e^{tG})_{t\geqslant 0}
of completely positive, unital maps on
\mathscr{A}
. On the other hand, a map
K=\frac{1}{2}\left\{M(\mathds{1}),\cdot\right\}
can be easily checked to generate a semigroup

e^{tK}(a)=e^{\frac{1}{2}tM(\mathds{1})}ae^{\frac{1}{2}tM(\mathds{1})},\quad a\in\mathscr{A},

(3.6)

which in turn is completely positive by self-adjointness of
M(\mathds{1})
. Let
M(\mathds{1})
be negative semidefinite, so that
\operatorname{\sigma}{\left(M(\mathds{1})\right)}
consists of nonpositive real numbers and let
E
denote its spectral measure, so
M(\mathds{1})=\int_{\operatorname{\sigma}{\left(M(\mathds{1})\right)}}\lambda\,dE(\lambda)
for
\lambda\leqslant 0
; then automatically
e^{tK}(\mathds{1})=e^{tM(\mathds{1})}
satisfies

\mathds{1}-e^{tM(\mathds{1})}=\int\limits_{\operatorname{\sigma}{\left(M(\mathds{1})\right)}}(1-e^{t\lambda})\,dE(\lambda)\geqslant 0

(3.7)

i.e.
e^{tK}
is subunital. Using Lie-Trotter formula, we have therefore

e^{tM}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(e^{\frac{t}{n}G}e^{\frac{t}{n}K}\right)^{n}

(3.8)

where each map
e^{\frac{t}{n}G}e^{\frac{t}{n}K}
is CP and subunital; hence, the limit exists in norm as a CP subunital map by norm closedness of
\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a C*-algebra and let
\psi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
. Then
e^{t\,\tau\circ\psi}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

There exists a CP map
\psi^{\prime}
such that
\tau\circ\psi=\psi^{\prime}\circ\tau
. With easy algebra one confirms that

(\tau\circ\psi)^{2n}=(\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi)^{n},\quad(\tau\circ\psi)^{2n+1}=\tau\circ\psi\circ(\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi)^{n}

(3.9)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which leads to

e^{t\,\tau\circ\psi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2n}}{(2n)!}(\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi)^{n}+\tau\circ\psi\circ\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}(\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi)^{2n+1}

(3.10)

after power series expanding and grouping terms for even and odd
n
. Both above series converge uniformly in
B(\mathscr{A})
and, since
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
, represent completely positive maps on
\mathscr{A}
; consequently
e^{t\,\tau\circ\psi}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A})
.
∎

3.2.
Unital case

We first focus on the case of unital semigroups. Below we state the main result of this section, which specifies the link between decomposable semigroups and dissipation properties of their generators.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a C*-algebra. The following statements hold:

(1)

Let
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a smoothly decomposable, uniformly continuous semigroup of unital maps on
\mathscr{A}
. Then
L=L_{1}+L_{2}
, where
L(\mathds{1})=0
,
L_{1}\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}_{\infty}}(\mathscr{A})
and
L_{2}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{coCP}}(\mathscr{A})
.

(2)

Let
L_{1}\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Dis}_{\infty}}(\mathscr{A})
,
L_{2}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{coCP}}(\mathscr{A})
. Then
(e^{tL})_{t\geqslant 0}
,
L=L_{1}+L_{2}
, is a uniformly continuous semigroup of decomposable subunital maps on
\mathscr{A}
. If
L_{1}(\mathds{1})+L_{2}(\mathds{1})=0
then
e^{tL}
is unital.

Proof.

Ad
1
. Let there exist families
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}\subset\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
,
(\psi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}\subset\operatorname{\mathsf{coCP}}(\mathscr{A})
, both being smooth functions of
t
, such that

e^{tL}=\phi_{t}+\tau\circ\psi_{t}.

(3.11)

Since
\phi_{0}+\tau\circ\psi_{0}=\mathrm{id}
is CP, we infer
\phi_{0}=\mathrm{id}
,
\psi_{0}=0
. Both
\phi_{t}
,
\psi_{t}
must admit uniform derivatives at
t=0
(note Remark
1
about relaxing this condition), i.e. there exist linear maps
R,S:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
such that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\left\|R-\frac{1}{t}(\phi_{t}-\mathrm{id})\right\|=0,\quad\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\left\|S-\frac{1}{t}\psi_{t}\right\|=0.

(3.12)

Note that
\phi_{t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
if and only if also
\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\phi_{t}
is completely positive on
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geqslant 0
, which easily follows from Stinespring’s dilation theorem
[
10
]
.
Maps
\phi_{t}
,
\psi_{t}
are positive, satisfy
\phi_{t}(\mathds{1})+\tau\circ\psi_{t}(\mathds{1})=\mathds{1}
, so

\phi_{t}(\mathds{1})\leqslant\mathds{1},\quad\psi_{t}(\mathds{1})\leqslant\mathds{1}

(3.13)

for all
t\geqslant 0
, i.e. are subunital. Then,
\phi_{n,t}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\phi_{t}
,
\psi_{n,t}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\psi_{t}
are also subunital on
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
. For a fixed
n
, denote

f_{X}(t)=\phi_{n,t}(X^{*}X)-\phi_{n,t}(X)^{*}\phi_{n,t}(X).

(3.14)

Any subunital CP map
\Phi
on a C*-algebra satisfies
\|\Phi(\mathds{1})\|\leqslant 1
and
\Phi(\mathds{1})^{2}\leqslant\Phi(\mathds{1})
and so is subject to a version of Kadison-Schwarz inequality
[
8
]

\Phi(x^{*}x)\geqslant\|\Phi(\mathds{1})\|\Phi(x^{*}x)\geqslant\Phi(x)^{*}\Phi(x)

(3.15)

for any
x
in the algebra. Consequently,
f_{X}(t)\geqslant 0
for all
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geqslant 0
. Smoothness of
\phi_{t}
implies uniform differentiability of
f_{X}
at
t=0
,

\displaystyle\frac{df_{X}}{dt}(0)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d\phi_{n,t}(X^{*}X)}{dt}\right|_{0}-\left.\frac{d\phi_{n,t}(X^{*})}{dt}\right|_{0}X-X^{*}\left.\frac{d\phi_{n,t}(X)}{dt}\right|_{0}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=R_{n}(X^{*}X)-R_{n}(X^{*})X-X^{*}R_{n}(X)

\displaystyle=D_{R_{n}}(X)

since
\phi_{n,0}=\mathrm{id}
, where we denoted
R_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes R
. On the other hand
f_{X}(0)=0
, so (
3.15
) yields
\frac{1}{t}\left(f_{X}(t)-f_{X}(0)\right)=\frac{1}{t}f_{X}(t)
is positive for all
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
,
t\geqslant 0
and remains positive in the limit
t\to 0^{+}
since cone of positive elements is norm closed in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathscr{A})
, i.e.
\frac{df_{X}}{dt}(0)=D_{R_{n}}(X)\geqslant 0
for all
X
,
n
. This shows
R
has a complete dissipation property.

As for
S
, note that
(\frac{1}{t}\psi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a net of CP maps on
\mathscr{A}
converging to
S
in norm; hence
S
is also a CP map since
\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(\mathscr{A})
is closed in BW-topology and therefore norm closed in
B(\mathscr{A})
. Prescription (
2.1
) yields
\tau
is a bounded, linear idempotent isometry on
\mathscr{A}
and so
(\frac{1}{t}\psi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
converges to
S
uniformly if and only if
(\tau\circ\frac{1}{t}\psi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
converges to
\tau\circ S
uniformly. Therefore, using triangle inequality combined with (
3.12
),

Ad

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a C*-algebra. If

\displaystyle=V_{1,n}^{*}\pi_{1,n}(X^{*}X)V_{1,n}-V_{1,n}^{*}\pi_{1,n}(X^{*})V_{1,n}X

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathscr{A}

(1)

(2)

Ad
1

4.
D-divisible quantum dynamics

In this section we assume
\mathscr{A}=B(\mathcal{H})
(with
\mathds{1}
being the identity operator). Its predual is then
B_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, the Banach space of all trace class operators on
\mathcal{H}
complete with the trace norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
.

A family
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
of bounded maps on
B_{1}(\mathcal{H})
will be called a
(quantum) dynamical map
if
\Lambda_{t}
is positive and trace preserving for all
t\geqslant 0
and
\Lambda_{t}(\rho)\to\rho
in trace norm for every
\rho\in B_{1}(H)
as
t\to 0^{+}
. These conditions are equivalent to the dual
\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}
being a positive, unital, weakly-* continuous map on
B(\mathcal{H})
, satisfying
\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}(A)\to A
in weak-* operator topology for all
A\in B(\mathcal{H})
as
t\to 0^{+}
. Traditionally, when we set
\rho_{t}=\Lambda_{t}(\rho)
for some
\rho\geqslant 0
,
\operatorname{tr}{\rho}=1
, we interpret
\rho_{t}
as a time-dependent
density operator
of a given physical system, and so
\Lambda_{t}
encodes a physical evolution of a mixed state. We restrict attention exclusively to dynamical maps which are subject to a Master Equation

\frac{d\rho_{t}}{dt}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho_{t})

(4.1)

or, equivalently
\frac{d\Lambda_{t}}{dt}=\mathcal{L}_{t}\circ\Lambda_{t}
, for a time-dependent *-map
\mathcal{L}_{t}\in B(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
satisfying
\operatorname{tr}{\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho)}=0
for all
\rho\in B_{1}(\mathcal{H})
. Its dual
\mathcal{L}_{t}\in B(B(\mathcal{H}))
is then a weakly-* continuous map satisfying
L_{t}(\mathds{1})=0
, i.e. is to be understood as in the previous sections. Clearly, both
L_{t}
and
\mathcal{L}_{t}
are referred to as generator, in the Heisenberg and Schrödinger picture, respectively. A dynamical map subject to (
4.1
) is
divisible
, i.e. there exists a two-parameter family
(V_{t,s})_{t\geqslant s}
of
propagators
, also trace preserving, such that
\Lambda_{t}=V_{t,s}\circ\Lambda_{s}
for all
s\in[0,t]
. When
V_{t,s}
is always an
n
-positive map we say
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is
n
-divisible
. In the most prominent case when
V_{t,s}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
we call the dynamical map
CP-divisible
or
Markovian
.

The structure of
\mathcal{L}_{t}
for CP-divisible invertible dynamical maps has been long known to be dual to the celebrated GKSL form (
1.2
). When the constraint of CP-divisibility is alleviated by requiring
V_{t,s}
to be decomposable instead, we call the dynamical map
D-divisible
. In case of finite-dimensional Hilbert space, i.e. when
B_{1}(\mathcal{H})\simeq\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, it was shown that
\mathcal{L}_{t}
retains its general Lindblad-like structure, however with
\phi
appearing in (
1.2
) being decomposable
[
12
]
. The following theorem extends results of
[
12
]
onto the case of a general Hilbert space.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a dynamical map on
B_{1}(\mathcal{H})
satisfying time-local Master Equation (
4.1
) and
\Lambda_{0}=\mathrm{id}
. Then:

(1)

If
V_{t,s}
is smoothly decomposable for all
t\geqslant s
then there exists a self-adjoint
H_{t}\in B(\mathcal{H})
and
\Phi_{t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
such that

\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho)=-i\left[H_{t},\rho\right]+\Phi_{t}(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(\mathds{1}),\rho\right\}

(4.2)

for all
\rho\in B_{1}(\mathcal{H})
.

(2)

Conversely, when
\mathcal{L}_{t}
admits a form
4.2
then
V_{t,s}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
for all
t\geqslant s
.

Proof.

For part
1
, let
V_{t,s}
be decomposable,
V_{t,s}=Z_{t,s}+\tau\circ Y_{t,s}
, for
Z_{t,s},Y_{t,s}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
smooth in
t\geqslant s\geqslant 0
. Again, we have
V_{t,t}=\mathrm{id}
so
Z_{t,t}=\mathrm{id}
and
Y_{t,t}=0
. Then, for every
t
there exist maps
\mathcal{R}_{t},\mathcal{S}_{t}\in B(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
satisfying

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\left\|\mathcal{R}_{t}-\frac{1}{h}\left(Z_{t+h,t}-\mathrm{id}\right)\right\|=0,\quad\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\left\|\mathcal{S}_{t}-\frac{1}{h}Y_{t+h,t}\right\|=0,

(4.3)

i.e. uniform derivatives of
Z_{t,s}
and
Y_{t,s}
at
t=s
. This however shows

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\left\|\mathcal{R}_{t}+\tau\circ\mathcal{S}_{t}-\frac{1}{h}\left(V_{t+h,t}-\mathrm{id}\right)\right\|=0

(4.4)

by triangle inequality; since
V_{t,s}
enjoys differential equation

\frac{d\Lambda_{t}}{dt}=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{V_{t+h,t}-\mathrm{id}}{h}\circ\Lambda_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}\circ\Lambda_{t},

(4.5)

we infer
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{R}_{t}+\tau\circ\mathcal{S}_{t}
, or
L_{t}=R_{t}+\tau\circ S_{t}
for maps
R_{t}
,
S_{t}:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
dual to
\mathcal{R}_{t}
,
\mathcal{S}_{t}
. Since
\left(\frac{1}{h}Y_{t+h,t}\right)_{h\geqslant 0}
is a convergent net of CP maps,
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is also CP since
\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(B_{1}(\mathcal{H}))
is closed (in supremum norm induced by trace norm); hence
S_{t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{CP}}(B(\mathcal{H}))
. Now, let
\phi_{t,s}
,
\psi_{t,s}
be maps on
B(\mathcal{H})
dual to
Z_{t,s}
,
Y_{t,s}
and define

f_{X}(t,s)=\phi_{n;t,s}(X^{*}X)-\phi_{n;t,s}(X^{*})\phi_{n;t,s}(X)

(4.6)

for any
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(B(\mathcal{H}))
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
where we denoted
\phi_{n;t,s}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\phi_{t,s}
and
\psi_{n;t,s}=\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\psi_{t,s}
. With direct calculation,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{X}}{\partial t}(s,s)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial\phi_{n;t,s}(X^{*}X)}{\partial t}\right|_{s}-\left.\frac{\partial\phi_{n;t,s}(X^{*})}{\partial t}\right|_{s}X-X^{*}\left.\frac{\partial\phi_{n;t,s}(X)}{\partial t}\right|_{s}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=R_{n,s}(X^{*}X)-R_{n,s}(X^{*})X-X^{*}R_{n,s}(X).

On the other hand, since

Summary

We investigated semigroups of decomposable positive maps on C*-algebras with special attention granted to algebraic structure of their infinitesimal generators, with additional assumption of smooth decomposability. Our results provide an extension of the work of Lindblad
[
7
]
onto broader class of decomposable maps. It was shown that properties of both the semigroup and its generator resemble completely positive case explored extensively in
[

and may be treated in quite a similar manner. A notion of D-divisible quantum dynamical maps on

was formulated for a general Hilbert space

[
12
]
. It remains unclear if such a strong condition of
smooth
decomposability is really necessary for key theorems to apply; we suspect that it might not be the case or that this requirement can be at least weakened. A natural open question arises from our analysis which concerns determining internal structure of generators of
indecomposable
semigroups, perhaps with some additionl constraints or specific internal symmetries. This problem was tackled in the past e.g. by specifying conditions for

or

On extension modules of finite homological dimension
Rafael Holanda, Victor H. Jorge-Pérez and Victor D. Mendoza-Rubio

Abstract
. We explore the implications of the finiteness of homological dimensions for Ext modules, focusing on projective dimension, injective dimension, and their Gorenstein counterpart. In this direction, we establish several finiteness criteria for homological dimensions. Under such finiteness conditions, our main result is a new duality for certain Ext modules when tensored by canonical modules.

Introduction

After the astonishing ideas of Auslander, Buchsbaum, and Serre culminating in the characterization of the regular property of a Noetherian local ring by the finiteness of the projective dimension of its residual field, homological algebra has played a fundamental role in commutative ring theory. Indeed, this foundation enabled diverse characterizations of ring-theoretic properties via finiteness conditions on homological dimensions (e.g., projective, complete intersection, Gorenstein dimensions, and their injective versions), see, for instance, some classical references
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
14
,
25
]
. Establishing criteria for the finiteness of homological dimensions as well as their prescription – especially via vanishing of Ext modules – is a very active topic in homological commutative algebra, containing a vast literature including but not limited to

[
1
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
22
,
32
,
31
]
.

Recently, an approach to study the implications of vanishing of Ext is under the condition that certain Hom module has some finite homological dimension, as evidenced in
[
11
,
17
,
24
]
. In addition, Ghosh-Puthenpurakal
[
15
]
and Zargar-Gheibi
[
32
]
studied the consequences of having Ext modules with finite injective dimension, deriving criteria for finiteness for the projective dimension of modules. Motivated by all, in this paper, we aim to study the consequences of having Ext modules with some finite homological dimension, focusing on the projective dimension, injective dimension, and their Gorenstein versions. Thus, the standard assumption in the present text does not revolve around the usual Ext vanishing hypothesis but rather the finiteness of its homological dimensions.

Zargar and Gheibi proved in
[
32
]
that for non-zero finitely generated modules
M
and
N
over a Noetherian local ring
R
such that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
i
, the finiteness of the projective dimension of
M
is equivalent to that of injective dimension of
N
. Then, what can one tell if one considers the Ext modules
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i\geq 0}(M,N)
with another finite homological dimension under the additional condition that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
or
\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)<\infty
? We address this question in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
2.3
& Corollary
2.6
)
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
, and let
M
and
N
be non-zero finitely generated
R
-modules. Let
\mathcal{H}
be any of the homological dimensions
\operatorname{pd},\operatorname{G-dim},\operatorname{Gid}
, and suppose that
\mathcal{H}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq d-\operatorname{depth}(M)
.

(1)

Suppose that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(1.a)

If
\mathcal{H}\in\{\operatorname{pd},\operatorname{G-dim}\}
, then
\mathcal{H}(N)<\infty
.

(1.b)

If
R
has a dualizing complex and
\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{Gid}
, then
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

(2)

Suppose that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

(2.a)

If
\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{pd}
, then
\operatorname{id}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2.b)

If
R
has a dualizing complex and
\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{G-dim}
, then
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2.c)

If
\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{Gid}
, then
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Theorem
1.1
not only gives criteria for detecting the finiteness of homological dimension of modules but also allows one to obtain Gorenstein criteria as verified in Corollary
2.4
and Corollary
2.7
. In addition, it is worth noting that Theorem
1.1
says that the counterparts with Gorenstein dimensions of the results of Foxby in
[
13
, Proposition 3.5(b)]
are true. Furthermore, item (2.a) allows us to recover a celebrated result of Foxby given in
[
14
, Corollary 4.4]
, and item (2.c) gives a positive answer to the Gorenstein version of the question in
[
15
, Question 4.5]
.

In our study, we also consider deficiency modules, which were introduced by Schenzel
[
29
]
. Fiel and Holanda
[
12
]
proved that the finiteness of the projective (resp. injective) dimension of all deficiency modules of a finitely generated module
M
over a local ring
R
implies that
M
has finite injective (resp. projective) dimension. We not only prove this result with a different approach, but also, we establish its Gorenstein version. The converse is assured for Cohen-Macaulay modules, extending Foxby
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring with a dualizing complex and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.

(1)

If
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(K^{i}(M))<\infty
for all
i\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(K^{i}(M))<\infty
for all
i\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Our main result is a new duality concerning the canonical module when tensored by certain Ext modules. Such a duality is also established for semidualizing modules (Theorem
4.5
) and is applied in many directions throughout the paper.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Assume
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay local with canonical module
\omega
, and let
n
be a non-negative integer. Let
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}
denote either

\operatorname{pd}
or
\operatorname{G-dim}
, and
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}
the injective counterpart. Let
M
and
N
be finitely generated
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(N)<\infty
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

(2)

\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

In any case, we have natural isomorphisms

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\otimes_{R}\omega\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega)

for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

Using the behavior of the canonical module described in Theorem
1.3
, we derive the following:

Corollary 1.4

(Corollary
4.8
)
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of depth
t
and,
M
and
N
be non-zero finitely generated
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t-\operatorname{depth}(M)
, then
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

Setup
.

R

. The depth of

. In that case, for a finitely generated

We also consider the derived category of

\operatorname{id}_{R}(M)

and

2.
Finite homological dimensions of Ext of complexes

In this section, we derive results regarding the finiteness of homological dimension of Ext of complexes and conclude the corresponding results for Ext of modules.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}

be a complex in
\mathcal{D}_{\square}(R).

(1)

If
\gid_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{X}))<\infty
for all
i
, then
\gid_{R}(\boldsymbol{X})<\infty
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{X}))<\infty
for all
i
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\boldsymbol{X})<\infty
.

Proof.

We prove item (1). This is done by induction on
s=\sup\boldsymbol{X}-\inf\boldsymbol{X}
. If
s=0
, then
\boldsymbol{X}\simeq\Sigma^{j}\operatorname{H}_{j}(\boldsymbol{X})
for some
j
, and the conclusion follows.

Assume
s>0
and let
u=\sup\boldsymbol{X}
. Note that there is a natural short exact sequence of complexes
0\to\boldsymbol{Y}\to\boldsymbol{X}\to\tau_{{}_{\subset u-1}}(\boldsymbol{X})\to 0,
where
\tau_{{}_{\subset u-1}}(\boldsymbol{X})
denotes the
(t-1)
soft left truncation of
\boldsymbol{X}
. In addition, note

\boldsymbol{X}\to\tau_{{}_{\subset u-1}}(\boldsymbol{X})
induces an isomorphism in homologies in degree
\leq u-1
, and that
\operatorname{H}_{i}(\tau_{{}_{\subset u-1}}(\boldsymbol{X}))=0
for
i>u-1
. Also, for
i\not=u
, we have that
\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{Y})=0
and
\operatorname{H}_{u}(\boldsymbol{Y})\cong\operatorname{H}_{u}(\boldsymbol{X})
.

If
\gid_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{X}))<\infty
for all
i
, note that
\boldsymbol{Y}
and
\tau_{{}_{\subset t-1}(\boldsymbol{X})}
satisfy the induction hypothesis, and therefore they have finite Gorenstein injective dimension. Thus, the conclusion follows from the fact that the class of complexes in
\mathcal{D}(R)
with finite Gorenstein injective dimension has the two out three property
[
10
, Proposition 9.2.15]
.

The proof of item (2) is similar using
[
10
, Corollary 8.1.9 and Proposition 9.1.16]
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
R
is local, and let
\boldsymbol{M}
and
\boldsymbol{N}
be non-acyclic complexes in
\mathcal{D}_{\square}^{\rm f}(R)
such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M})<\infty
.

(1)

If
\Hdim_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
for all
i
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\boldsymbol{N})<\infty
.

(2)

Suppose that
R
has a dualizing complex. If
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
for all
i
, then
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\boldsymbol{N})<\infty
.

Proof.

We know that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N})=\operatorname{H}_{-i}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))
for all
i
. From
[
6
, A.4.4 and A.5.4]
, one sees that
\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N})\in\mathcal{D}_{\square}^{\rm f}(R)
. By Lemma
2.1
(2) (resp. Lemma
2.1
(1)), we conclude
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
(resp.
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
). It follows from
[
19
, Theorems 5.6 & 5.8]
(resp.
[
28
, Proposition 4.1(b)]
and
[
9
, Theorem 4.4]
) that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\boldsymbol{N})<\infty
(resp.
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\boldsymbol{N})<\infty
), concluding (1) (resp. (2)).
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume
R
is local, and let
M
and
N
be non-zero finitely generated
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(1)

If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

(2)

Suppose that
R
has a dualizing complex. If
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)
, then
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume
R
is local, and let
M
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
and
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t-\operatorname{depth}(M)
. Then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is complete. Then
R
has a dualizing complex. By Corollary
2.3
(2), then
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(M)<\infty
. Then the result follows from
[
6
, Theorem 6.3.2]
.
∎

Item (2) of the next theorem provides a positive answer to the Gorenstein version of
[
15
, Question 4.5]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume
R
is local, and let
\boldsymbol{M}
and
\boldsymbol{N}
be non-acyclic complexes in
\mathcal{D}_{\square}^{\rm f}(R)
.
Suppose that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(\boldsymbol{N})<\infty
. In the case
\operatorname{H-dim}=\operatorname{G-dim}
, assume further that
R
has a dualizing complex.

(1)

If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
for all
i
, then
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M})<\infty
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
for all
i
, then
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M})<\infty
.

Proof.

We know that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N})=\operatorname{H}_{-i}(\mathbf{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))
. Additionally, by
[
6
, A.4.4 and A.5.2]
, we see that
\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N})\in\mathcal{D}_{\square}^{\rm f}(R)
. In the case (1), for all
i
,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{i}(\mathbf{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N})))<\infty
. Thus, by Lemma
2.1
(1), we have that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
. As to (2), one considers Lemma
2.1
(2) instead so that
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
.

The conclusion of item (1) for

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}=\operatorname{pd}_{R}
follows from
[
8
, Lemma 6.2.12]
.

It remains to prove (1) for
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}=\operatorname{G-dim},
and (2). For proving both, either by
[
19
, 2.4]
, or by assumption,
[
7
, Proposition 5.9]
and
[
10
, Theorem 19.2.34]
, we can let
\boldsymbol{D}
be a dualizing complex of
R
. Since
\operatorname{id}_{R}(\boldsymbol{N})<\infty
, then
\boldsymbol{N}\in\mathcal{B}(R)
and hence
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{D},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
by the Foxby equivalence.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{D}\otimes_{R}^{\mathbf{L}}\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{D},\boldsymbol{N}))

\displaystyle\text{because }\boldsymbol{N}\in\mathcal{B}(R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{D})\otimes_{R}^{\mathbf{L}}\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{D},\boldsymbol{N})

\displaystyle\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{HyperhomologicalAlgebra}{}{}, Theorem 4.4.5(a)]}}.

In the case (1) for
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}=\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}
, we have that
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
, concluding from
[
19
, Theorems 5.1]
that
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{D}))<\infty
. Hence
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M})<\infty
by
[
9
, Corollary 6.4]
.

As to (2), we have that
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{N}))<\infty
. It follows from
the last isomorphism,
[
28
, Proposition 4.1(b)]
and
[
9
, Theorem 4.4]
that
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M},\boldsymbol{D}))<\infty
. Since
M\simeq\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\boldsymbol{D}),\boldsymbol{D})
by
[
6
, A.8.5]
, then it follows from
[
9
, Corollary 6.4]
that
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\boldsymbol{M})<\infty
.
∎

.

is Cohen-Macaulay local, and let

and

be non-zero finitely generated

. In the case

and

3.
Finite Homological dimensions of deficiency modules

Setup
.

In the rest of the paper,
R
is local, and unless otherwise stated, all
R
-modules will be finitely generated. Moreover, in this section, we assume that
R
has a dualizing complex
\boldsymbol{D}
. Also, we assume that
\boldsymbol{D}
is normalized in the sense that
\sup D=\dim R
.

The existence of
\boldsymbol{D}
is equivalent to
R
being a factor of a Gorenstein local ring
S
, i.e.,
R
is a homomorphic image of
S
. In that context, Schenzel
[
29
]
generalized the notion of a canonical module of a ring in the following sense: For an
R
-module
M
, set

K^{i}(M):=\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{\dim(S)-i}(M,S)

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. For each
i
, the
R
-module
K^{i}(M)
is called the
i
-th
deficiency module
of
M
. The
R
-module
K(M):=K^{\dim_{R}(M)}(M)
is called the
canonical module
of
M
. By local duality we have
K^{i}(M)=0
for
i<0pt(M)
or
i>\dim(M)
, and
K^{i}(M)\neq 0
for
i=0pt(M)
and
i=\dim(M)
. The modules of deficiency also satisfy the following isomorphism for all
i
:

K^{i}(M)\cong\operatorname{H}_{i}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\boldsymbol{D})).

For further details about deficiency modules, we refer the reader to
[
29
]
.

In
[
12
]
, the authors investigated the consequences of the finiteness of classical homological dimensions, namely, projective and injective dimensions, of the deficiency modules. Motivated by their work, we apply Theorem
2.5
to extend those results to Gorenstein dimensions. We also emphasize that our approach differs from theirs in both method and perspective.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. Then

(1)

If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(K^{i}(M))<\infty
for all
\operatorname{depth}(M)\leq i\leq\dim(M)
, then
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(K^{i}(M))<\infty
for all
\operatorname{depth}(M)\leq i\leq\dim(M)
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Proof.

For each
i
, we note that
K^{i}(M)=\operatorname{H}_{i}(\operatorname{\mathbf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\boldsymbol{D}))=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{-i}(M,\boldsymbol{D})
. Since
K^{i}(M)=0
for
i<0pt(M)
or
i>\operatorname{dim}_{R}(M)
, by assumption we see have that all modules
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,\boldsymbol{D})
have finite
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}
or all modules have finite
\operatorname{Hid}
. Then the result follows from
[
32
, Proposition 4.2]
and Theorem
2.5
.
∎

The following corollary contains the counterparts of
[
13
, Proposition 3.5(b)]
to Gorenstein dimensions.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. If
M
is Cohen-Macaulay, then

(1)

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(K(M))<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2)

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(K(M))<\infty
.

Proof.

In view of
[
29
, Theorem 1.14]
, it is enough to prove (1). One implication follows from Theorem
3.1
(1). For the other, suppose that
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(M)<\infty
. Since
M\cong K(K(M))
, then
K(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay (
[
29
, Theorem 1.14]
again) and from Theorem
3.1
(2) it follows that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(K(M))<\infty
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
. Assume that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\omega,R)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq t
. Let
\_^{\dagger}
stand for
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\_,\omega)
. If
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}((\omega^{*})^{\dagger})<\infty
, then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Note that from the vanishing hypothesis and
[
18
, Theorem 2.1(i)]
we see that
\omega^{*}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. Since
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}((\omega^{*})^{\dagger})<\infty
, then by Corollary
3.2
,
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\omega^{\ast})<\infty
. Therefore, the conclusion follows from
[
24
, Corollary 5.13]
.
∎

4.
A duality for Ext modules with finite homological dimension

In this section, we establish a duality of certain Ext modules. The following theorem will be often used in this section.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 4.4]
,
[
6
, Corollary 3.1.11 & Theorem 3.4.11]

)
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with canonical module
\omega
, and let
M
be an
R
-module.

(1)

\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{A}(R)
.

(2)

\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{B}(R)
.

(3)

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega,M))<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(4)

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}\omega)<\infty
.

In
[
11
, Corollary 42(3)]
, although the authors provide this without using Proposition
4.1
, this proposition assures that their vanishing assumptions are not necessary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with a canonical module
\omega
, and let
N
be a non-zero
R
-module. Then
R
is Gorenstein in any of the following cases:

(1)

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
and
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega,N))<\infty
.

(2)

\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(N)<\infty
and
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega,N))<\infty
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.1
says that in (1), we have
\operatorname{Gid}_{R}(N)<\infty
while in (2), we have
\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)<\infty
. The result follows from
[
6
, Theorem 6.3.2]
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with canonical module
\omega
, and let
n
be a non-negative integer. Let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(N)<\infty
. Then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
. In this case, the natural map

\Phi_{M,N}:\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)\otimes_{R}\omega\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
. In particular,
\operatorname{G-dim}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
. Note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)
and
N
belong to
\mathcal{A}(R)
. Then by
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
, the map
\Phi_{M,N}
is an isomorphism. Hence, it follows from Proposition
4.1
(3) that
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
.

Conversely, suppose that
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
. Then by Proposition
4.1
(3),
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega\otimes_{R}M,N\otimes_{R}\omega)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega,N\otimes_{R}\omega))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N),

where the last isomorphism follows since
N\in\mathcal{A}(R).
Thus,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and let
\mathcal{X}
be a class of objects in
\mathcal{A}
. We say that
\mathcal{X}
is
closed under short exact sequences
if
the two out of three property
holds for short exact sequences of objects in
\mathcal{X}
. We say that
\mathcal{X}
is
closed under isomorphisms
if every object isomorphic to an object in
\mathcal{X}
is in
\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a class of the category of
R
-modules closed under short exact sequences and isomorphisms. Let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules, and
n
be a non-negative integer. Suppose that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)
and
N
belong to
\mathcal{X}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Then
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i}(M),N)\in\mathcal{X}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

We must show that for all
0\leq j\leq n
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{j}(M),N))\in\mathcal{X}
. We prove this by induction on
j
. For
j=0
, this holds by hypothesis. Now suppose
0<j\leq n
and that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{j-1}(M),N))\in\mathcal{X}
. We have an exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Omega^{j}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R^{n_{j}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Omega^{j-1}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

which yields

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{j-1}(M),N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{n_{j}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{j}(M),N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(M,N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

since
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{j-1}(M),N)\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(M,N)
. The result follows by breaking this exact sequence into two short ones and using the fact that
\mathcal{X}
is closed under short exact sequences and isomorphisms.

∎

The following theorem improves
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
. For nomenclature on semidualizing modules and their corresponding Auslander classes, we refer to
[
27
]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
C
be a semidualizing
R
-module, and
n
be a non-negative integer. Let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules such that
N\in\mathcal{A}_{C}(R)
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\in\mathcal{A}_{C}(R)
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Then, for all
0\leq i\leq n
, there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\otimes_{R}C\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}C).

Proof.

For
i=0
, the isomorphism holds from
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
. For
i>0
, consider the short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Omega^{i}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R^{r_{i}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Omega^{i-1}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Such an exact sequence yields the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N)\otimes C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{\Omega^{i-1}(M),N}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{r_{i}},N)\otimes C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{R^{r_{i}},N}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i}(M),N)\otimes C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{\Omega^{i}(M),N}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N)\otimes C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N\otimes_{R}C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}(R^{r_{i}},N\otimes_{R}C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}(\Omega^{i}(M),N\otimes_{R}C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N\otimes_{R}C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

where the second row is exact. As to the first one, since
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)
, by assumption,
[
30
, 1.9(a)]
and Lemma
4.4
, all
R
-modules of this exact sequence belong to
\mathcal{A}_{C}(R)
. Therefore, the first row turns out to be exact as well by
[
27
, Lemma 3.1.12]
.

Additionally, by
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
, the vertical maps are isomorphisms. The five lemma settles the desired isomorphism. ∎

Recall that the classes of modules of finite projective dimension, finite injective dimension, finite Gorenstein dimension, and finite Gorenstein injective dimension are closed under short exact sequences; see
[
10
, Corollaries 8.1.9 and 8.2.9, Proposition 9.2.15]
and
[
6
, Corollary 1.2.9]
.
With these facts in mind, together with the results established earlier in this section, we are now able to prove our main theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with canonical module
\omega
, and let
n
be a non-negative integer. Let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(N)<\infty
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

(2)

\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

In any case, we have natural isomorphisms

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\otimes_{R}\omega\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega)

for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Once the equivalence is proved, all the isomorphisms follow from Theorem
4.5
, Proposition
4.1
, and item (1).

Now, we prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Suppose that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Given
0\leq i\leq n
, we must show that
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
. The case
i=0
follows from Proposition
4.3
. Now, suppose
i>0
. Consider the exact sequence
0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{i}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{r_{i}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{i-1}(M)\longrightarrow 0.

Then it induces an exact sequence

\scriptscriptstyle{0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N\otimes_{R}\omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{r_{i}},N\otimes_{R}\omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i}(M),N\otimes_{R}\omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N\otimes_{R}\omega)\longrightarrow 0}.

By Proposition
4.1
(4),
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(N\otimes_{R}\omega)<\infty
. Additionally, by Lemma
4.4
,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{j}(M),N))<\infty
for
j=i-1,i
, so by Proposition
4.3
,
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{j}(M),N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
for
j=i-1,i
. Thus, the first three terms of the exact sequence above have finite
\operatorname{Hid},
and therefore
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
. The result follows from the fact that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N\otimes_{R}\omega)
.

As to
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, we follow the same lines as before by considering the exact sequence

\scriptstyle{0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{r_{i}},N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{i}(M),N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{i-1}(M),N)\longrightarrow 0}

for
0<i\leq n
.
∎

The following corollary contains a generalization of
[
11
, Corollary 30]
and
[
24
, Theorem 4.2
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
]
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay local, and let
M
and
N
be non-zero
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
. If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t-\operatorname{depth}(M)
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
. In addition, if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq t-0pt(M)
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=0
.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is complete, and hence
R
has a canonical module
\omega
. Since
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
, by Proposition
4.1
, we have
\operatorname{id}_{R}(N\otimes_{R}\omega)<\infty
and, by Theorem
4.6
,
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N\otimes_{R}\omega))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t-0pt(M)
. Then it follows from Corollary
2.6
that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Now, by the vanishing assumption, as
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
, we have that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i>t-0pt(M)
by
[
1
, Theorem 4.13]
, and that

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=\sup\{i\geq 0:\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)\not=0\}
by
[
26
, Theorem 2.6(ii)]
. Therefore,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=0
.
∎

Combining Corollary
2.3
with Corollary
4.7
, we have the following byproduct.

Corollary 4.8
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, and let
M
and
N
be non-zero
R
-modules. Suppose that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t-0pt(M)
. Then
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

Next, we explore the consequences of Theorem
4.6
when one considers
N=R
. Recall that the
type
of an
R
-module
M
is defined to be the dimension of the
k
-vector space
\operatorname{Ext}^{r}_{R}(k,M)
, where
r=0pt(M)
, and denoted by
\type(M)
.

Corollary 4.9
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with a canonical module
\omega
, and let
n
be a non-negative integer. Let
M
be an
R
-module such that
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))<\infty
for all

. Then for each

, we have that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)\otimes_{R}\omega\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,\omega)

The isomorphisms follow immediately from Theorem

in Proposition

, it follows from

that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(\operatorname{Tr}_{R}(\Omega^{i}(M)),\omega)=0

.

Now, by taking the tensor product by the residue field of

[
5
, Proposition 3.3.11(c)]
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, and let
M
be an
R
-module of depth
r
and dimension
s
. Suppose that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))<\infty
for all

. Then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
. Moreover,
\operatorname{dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))\leq t-i
for all

\dim_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{t-s}_{R}(M,R))=s
.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is complete and therefore
R
admits a canonical module
\omega
. By
[
5
, Corollary 2.1.4]
,
\operatorname{grade}(M)=t-s
, so
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for all

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{t-s}(M,R)\not=0

\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty

On the other hand, since
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for

, one can see by assumption that
\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))<\infty

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)\otimes_{R}\omega\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,\omega).

Remark 4.11
.

Ghosh and Puthenpurakal proved in

5.
Some questions

In this section, we discuss some natural questions motivated by the results established earlier. Our primary question stems from the challenge of extending Corollary
4.7
to the non-Cohen-Macaulay setting.

Question 5.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be non-zero
R
-modules such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
. Do the following facts hold?

(1)

If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1\leq i\leq t}(M,N)=0
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=~0
.

The following proposition partially answers Question
5.1
when
\operatorname{H\text{-}dim}_{R}=\operatorname{pd}_{R}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M
and
N
be non-zero
R
-modules. Let
s
be a non-negative integer such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq s
. Then the equivalence

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty

holds in each one of the following cases:

(1)

If
s=t
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for all
t+1\leq i\leq 2t+1
.

(2)

M
has finite Gorenstein dimension equal to
s
.

Proof.

In both cases, the implication
(\Rightarrow)
follows from Corollary
2.3
. Thus, it remains to prove the implication
(\Leftarrow)
.

(1) Suppose that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
. By Lemma
4.4
, we have
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Omega^{t}(M),N))<\infty
. Moreover, the vanishing condition shows that
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\Omega^{t}(M),R)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq t
. Therefore, by
[
11
, Proposition 19]
,
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\Omega^{t}(M)^{\ast})<\infty
. Then by
[
11
, Proposition 13]
,
\Omega^{t}(M)
is free. Thus,
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(2) Since
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty
, then
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i>\operatorname{G-dim}_{R}(M)=0pt(R)-0pt(M)
. Then by assumption, we can see that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t
. Therefore, the assertion follows from item (1).
∎

For
N=R
, an answer to Question
5.1
(2) is given by
[
11
, Proposition 13]
. Next, we present an answer to Question
5.1
(1) that strengthens this proposition. For that, we say that an
R
-module
M
satisfies
(\widetilde{S}_{n})
if
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\min\{n,0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\}
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module satisfying
(\widetilde{S}_{n})
with
n\leq t
a non-negative integer. Suppose that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t-n
. Then
\Hdim_{R}(M)\leq t-n
.

Proof.

Let
s=t-n
. Then by Lemma
4.4
,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\Omega^{s}(M)^{\ast})<\infty
. Since
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M^{\ast})<\infty
and
M
satisfies
(\widetilde{S}_{n})
, an application of the depth lemma shows that
\Omega^{s}(M)
satisfies
(\widetilde{S}_{t})
. It follows from
[
11
, Proposition 13]
that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\Omega^{s}(M))=0
. Hence, by
[
4
, Theorem 8.7(3)]
,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)\leq s=t-n
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. If
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Note that Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
give some answers to
[
24
, Question 4.11]
. As an application of Corollary
5.4
, we can improve
[
16
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
M
be a non-zero
R
-module. Assume further that there exists a non-negative integer
h
such that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{h}(M,R))<\infty
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,t\}-\{h\}
, then
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=h
.

Proof.

From the assumption we see that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq t
. Then by Corollary
5.4
,
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
. Since
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=\sup\{i\geq 0:\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)\not=0\}
(see, for instance
[
22
, Lemma 2.3(c)]
together with
[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
) and
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)\leq t
, we see from the vanishing condition that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)=~h
.
∎

The reader can note that Corollary
4.7

It is possible to obtain a variation of Proposition

Question 5.7
.

Let

6.
Examples

We present some examples concerning Ext modules with finite homological dimension.

Example 6.1
.

Assume
R
is a local ring and let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be a regular sequence. Let
N
be an
R
-module such that
\underline{x}N=0
. Set
M=R/(\underline{x})
. Since the minimal free resolution of
M
is the Koszul complex of the sequence
\underline{x}
, we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)\cong N^{\binom{n}{i}},\quad\forall\ i=0,\ldots,n.

Thus,
\Hdim_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
if and only if
\Hdim_{R}(N)<\infty.
Similarly,
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
if and only if
\operatorname{Hid}_{R}(N)<\infty.

Now, set
L=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
and let
X
be a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\underline{x}
is
X
-regular. For all
i=1,\ldots,n
, by the Koszul duality we must have

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(L,X)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}),X)\cong X/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})X.

Thus
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(X)<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(L,X))<\infty
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. In particular,
\Hdim_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(L,R))=i
, by the Auslander-Buchsbaum and Auslander-Bridger formulas.

An
R
-module
M
is said to be
\operatorname{H-}
perfect
if
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
and
\operatorname{grade}(M)=\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(M)
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
be H-perfect
R
-modules of grade
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
, respectively. Then each
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{g_{i}}(M_{i},R)
have
\operatorname{H-dim}
equal
g_{i}
by
[
3
, Theorem 6.3]
. Set
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}
. Thus, for
j\not=g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{j}(M,R)
is zero, while that
\operatorname{H-dim}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{g_{i}}(M,R))=g_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.

An
R
-module
M
is
p
-
spherical
if
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)\leq p
and
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

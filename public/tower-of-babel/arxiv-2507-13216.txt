Explicit linearization of multi-dimensional germs and vector fields through Ecalle’s tree expansions


Abstract
We provide explicit formulas of non-recursive type for the linearizing transformations of a non-resonant analytic germ of diffeomorphism at a fixed point or a non-resonant analytic germ of vector field at a singular point, in any complex dimension. The formal expressions we obtain rely on a part of Ecalle’s tree-based combinatorics called “armould calculus” and they have the same shape for dynamical systems with discrete or continuous time. They allow us to recover in a straightforward manner, under Bruno’s arithmetical condition, the best known estimates for the domains of convergence of the analytic linearizing changes of variables in terms of the value of the Bruno series, including a new precise dependence with respect to the dimension of the problem.

Introduction

In the present article, we revisit the classical problem of linearization, for
complex analytic dynamical systems at a singular point of
\mathbbm{C}^{\nu}

(any
\nu\geqslant 1
) with discrete or continuous time. By using a part of
Ecalle’s “mould calculus” that involves some tree-based combinatorics, we
are able to give explicit formulas for the normalizing transformations, which
are of the same type for diffeomorphisms and vector fields, and derive
sharp estimates for the domains of convergence thereof.

It has been known since Poincaré that any analytic
diffeomorphism
g
that fixes the origin of
\mathbbm{C}^{\nu}
and
has a linear part of the form

{\mathrm{d}}g(0)=R_{q}\colon\thinspace(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})\mapsto(q_{1}z_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu}z_{\nu}),

(1.1)

with a spectrum
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\nu}
satisfying a
so-called non-resonance condition (see Section
2
),
is formally conjugate to
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
, with a unique
tangent-to-identity linearizing transformation
h
. Formulas of a recursive type for
h

have been used for more than a century and
h
has been
proved convergent when
q
is in the so-called Poincaré domain,
that is the eigenvalues
q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu}
are either all in the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
of
\mathbb{C}
, or all outside
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
If the spectrum
q
is non-resonant but not in the Poincaré domain, then
some integer combinations of the
q_{i}
,
appearing as denominators in the recursive formulas, can be
arbitrarily close to
0
and might thus prevent the convergence of

h
: this is the “small denominator problem”.

It is only in 1942 that C. Siegel, in the celebrated paper
[
Sie42
]
, was able to
control these quantities and reach a result of analytic linearization
in dimension
1

under a suitable diophantine hypothesis on the eigenvalue
q
. This result was later enhanced by himself into
higher-dimensional germs, with similar techniques and an arithmetical
condition of the same type on the spectrum
q
. In the real
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
category, Sternberg

[
Stern58
]
used another strategy to prove that a smooth non-resonant
diffeomorphism vanishing at the origin of
\mathbbm{R}^{\nu}
is
linearizable, with smooth changes of coordinates. Siegel’s results
were sharpened by Bruno in 1971 and Rüssmann in 1977, with the
introduction of weaker arithmetical conditions on the spectrum
q
,
expressed by the convergence of a series
\mathcal{B}(q)
of
non-negative real numbers (see
[
Bru72
,
Rus77
]
and
Section
2
).

Parallel results had been obtained since Poincaré for analytic vector
fields vanishing at the origin.
For an analytic vector field
V
whose
1
-jet is

V_{\lambda}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\lambda_{i}z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},

(1.2)

with non-resonant spectrum

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})\in\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
,
Bruno had by 1971 (
[
Bru71
]
) introduced the arithmetical condition
on
\lambda
that now bears his name, involving a series

\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)
:
if
\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)<\infty
, then the vector field is analytically
linearizable (see Section
2
for precise definitions
and statements).

More recently, the articles
[
GLS15
]
and
[
GM10
]
, dealing with
diffeomorphisms and vector fields respectively, brought improvements
in the estimates of the domains of convergence of the linearizing
transformation
h
in terms of the Bruno series.
Our results compare well with these.

In the present work, we essentially follow a scheme due to Ecalle (see

[
Eca92
]
for instance), which has been implemented by him with
great success to tackle very general classes of problems,
that provides a remarkable way of contructing
the unique tangent-to-identity linearizing transformation
h
:

– the object we focus on is the substitution operator
C_{h}:\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ h
associated to
h
;

– the operator
C_{h}
is
a priori
expressed as an expansion over a
set of
\mathbbm{Z}^{\nu}
-decorated forests
F
:

C_{h}=\sum_{F}S^{F}D_{F},

(1.3)

where the
S^{F}
are complex numbers that depend only on the spectrum and the

D_{F}
are ordinary differential operators in
\nu
variables that depend only
on the nonlinear part of the data.
The collection of numbers
(S^{F})
is called an
armould
2
2

2

Armould were formerly called arborescent moulds or
arbomoulds by Jean Ecalle. Coarmoulds were formerly
called arborescent comoulds or coarbomoulds.

and we denote it by
S^{\bullet}
;
the collection of operators
(D_{F})
is called a coarmould and we denote it by
D_{\bullet}
.
We will obtain explicit formulas for
S^{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
that
are essentially the same for diffeomorphisms and for vector fields
(
mutatis mutandis
as regards the armould
S^{\bullet}
), and
hence explicit formulas for
h
itself,
from which we will derive analyticity results
under the Bruno condition
almost at one stroke.

More precisely, denoting by
\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}
the polydisc of
radius
b
centred at
0
in
\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
,
if we take a diffeomorphism
or a vector field of the form

g=R_{q}\circ(\operatorname{id}+a)\quad\text{or}\quad V=V_{\lambda}+a,

(1.4)

where the nonlinear part
a
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}
and continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}}
,
we obtain (in Theorem
D
of Section
4.2
) the following explicit
lower bound for the radius of a polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
in which the corresponding
linearizing transformations
h
are convergent:

b\,\frac{e^{-\gamma-\mathcal{B}}}{\nu(4M\nu+2)},

(1.5)

where
M
is the supremum of
\frac{1}{b}\ a
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}}
,

\gamma
is some explicit numerical constant and
\mathcal{B}
stands for

\mathcal{B}(q)
or
\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)
. This is a refinement of the results of
[
GLS15
]
and
[
GM10
]
, as
explained in Section

.

We refer to
[
FM14

FMS18
]
for explanations on the relevance of a

involving trees and forests in these questions and related algebraic
constructions, yet all the necessary material for our purposes will be
presented below. In
[

]
, we had implemented Ecalle’s armould
formalism for the linearization of non-resonant diffeomorphisms in one
complex dimension and shown how it yields explicit formulas instead of
the classical recursive ones, that enabled us to improve upon the
existing results in the literature.
Going from one-dimensional to multidimensional brings a number of technical
complications already at the formal level.

The paper is organized as follows:

–
In Section
2
, we set the stage and introduce our
notations for the (formal or holomorphic) linearization problems for

in
[
GLS15
]
and
[
GM10
]
.

–
In Section
3
, we introduce terminology pertaining
to Ecalle’s armould calculus, define and prove some key properties of
the homogeneous operators
D_{F}
of (
1.3
), and finally
prove—up to Lemma
3.15
, whose proof is deferred to
Section
5
—that formula (
1.3
) makes
sense and gives the solution to the formal linearization problem when
using the appropriate armould
(S^{F})
:
see Theorems
A
and
B

for diffeomorphisms and vector fields of the form (
1.4
),
with remarkably compact formulas for the relevant coefficients
S^{F}
.
The definition of both
D_{F}
and
S^{F}
is due to Ecalle
[
Eca92
]
.

–
Section
4
shows how to deduce from the explicit
tree expansions (
1.3
) analyticity results for the
linearizing transformations
h
under the Bruno condition;
the upshot is Theorem
C
, equivalent to Bruno’s and
Rüssmann’s results, and Theorem
D
, which gives
precise estimates for the domains of convergence.
Proofs are carried out in a self-contained manner up to two results
whose proofs are deferred to later sections, namely
an algebraic cancellation phenomenon explained in
Section
5

and the armould estimates given in Section
6
.

–
Section
5
is of algebraic nature,
with emphasis on the dichotomy between those forests
F
for
which
D_{F}
is always the zero operator and the others.
For the latter ones, the so-called “admissible cuts” obey
Theorem
E
, that we state and prove,
and from which we deduce the instrumental
Proposition
5.6
(required in the proof of
Lemma
3.15
and some passages of
Sections
4
and
6
).

–
Section
6
contains the crucial estimates for the relevant
armoulds
S^{\bullet}
, in the form of Theorem
F
for
vector fields and Theorem
G
for diffeomorphisms,
whose proofs only require a little combinatorial effort and
manipulations in which the Bruno series appears in the most natural way.

–
Appendix
A
gives closed-forms expressions for the
components of the coarmould
D_{\bullet}(f)
.

We hope to convince the reader that the appropriate combinatorial
framework allows one to reduce the analytic estimates to the bare
minimum.
Note that Theorems
A
and
B

were proved by Ecalle in
[
Eca92
]
by means of the so-called
“arborification” technique, using a preliminary mould expansion
prior to reaching the tree expansion (
1.3
), whereas
our method is more direct.
Theorem
C
is also given in
[
Eca92
]
, but
the proof there is very concise and we do not know how to obtain the
necessary armould estimates without making use of our Proposition
5.6
.

Beyond the obtention of explicit formulas for the linearizing
transformations that enable to recover in a straightforward way the best
known estimates for the domains of analyticity,
our results pave the way for treating interesting further questions,
notably in the line of J. Pöschel’s paper
[
Pos17
]
,
where the author shows how to derive Sternberg’s as well as other results
with data involving various classes of ultradifferentiable series (in
particular the Gevrey classes) from the recursive formulas used in the
analytic class, involving new insights on classes that are stable by
composition.
Within our formalism, this could probably achieved in a more direct
way,
by means of explicit tree expansions instead of recursive formulas,
treating on an equal footing
diffeomorphisms and vector fields.

We postpone such developments for future research. Note that one benefit of working at the level of substitution operators
expressed as sums of elementary components,
as Ecalle’s armould calculus invites us to do,
is that, beyond spaces of formal
series with growth conditions on their coefficients, these techniques very
naturally lend themselves to dealing with data belonging to some given

functional spaces
, with estimates expressed through relevant
semi-norms,
e.g.
the ones that can be introduced for
parameter-dependent problems.

2
The linearization problem for germs of holomorphic diffeomorphisms or vector fields in higher dimension

Throughout this paper we use the notations

\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{0,1,2,\ldots\},\quad\mathbb{N}^{*}\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots\}\quad\text{and}\quad[\nu]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,\nu\}\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

(2.1)

2.1
The linearization problems in the groups
\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
and
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}

We define
\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
as the group (for composition) of germs of holomorphic
diffeomorphisms of
(\mathbb{C}^{\nu},0)
,
and
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
as its formal counterpart: the latter is formed of all

\nu
-tuples
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{\nu})
of formal series

g_{1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}),\ldots,g_{\nu}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
without constant
term
such that the
1
-jet of
g
(which is thus a linear endomorphism
of
\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
) is invertible.
One can view
\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
as a subgroup of
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
, the group law being
induced by the composition of formal series without constant term.
Inversion in
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
will be denoted by
g\mapsto g^{\circ(-1)}
.

Given
g\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
, its
1
-jet is also called
linear part
of
g
and denoted by
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
, so that

g={\mathrm{d}}g(0)\circ f,\qquad f\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}},

(2.2)

where
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}\subset\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
is the subgroup of tangent-to-identity
formal diffeomorphisms.
The general element of
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
can be written

f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\nu}),\qquad f_{i}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})=z_{i}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}}a_{i,m}z^{m}\quad\text{for $i\in[\nu]$,}

(2.3)

with any collection of complex numbers
(a_{i,m})
such that
a_{i,m}=0

when
\lvert m\rvert\leq 1
.
Here we have used the standard notations for multi-integers:

\lvert m\rvert\coloneqq m_{1}+\cdots+m_{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad z^{m}\coloneqq z_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots z_{\nu}^{m_{\nu}}\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptm=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{\nu})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}.

(2.4)

We can rewrite (
2.3
) as

f=\operatorname{id}+a,\quad a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}

(2.5)

by introducing the set
\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
of all

\nu
-tuples
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{\nu})
of power series such that, for
i

in
[\nu]
,

\displaystyle a_{i}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}}a_{i,m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
has

a_{i,m}=0
when
\lvert m\rvert\leq 1
.
We will refer to
a=f-\operatorname{id}
as to the nonlinear part of any

g\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
of the form (
2.2
).

Given
g\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
, the
formal linearization problem
asks for an

h\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu}
conjugating
g
and a linear map
L\in\operatorname{GL}(\nu,\mathbb{C})
.
If such an
h
exists, then
{\mathrm{d}}h(0)
establishes a conjugacy
between
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
and
L
, hence
h\circ{\mathrm{d}}h(0)^{\circ(-1)}\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
is a conjugacy
between
g
and
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
;
therefore, the formal linearization problem can be reduced to
searching for
h\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
such that
g\circ h=h\circ{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
.

The
holomorphic linearization problem
asks for a holomorphic
conjugacy
h\in\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
between
g
and a linear map under the
assumption that
g\in\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\nu}
is

non-resonant
if

i\in[\nu],\;\;m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lvert m\rvert\geq 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_{i}\neq q^{m},

where
q^{m}=q_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots q_{\nu}^{m_{\nu}}
.
We say that a formal diffeomorphism
g
is
non-resonant
if
its linear part
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
is diagonalizable with eigenvalues

q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu}
giving rise to a non-resonant
\nu
-tuple
q
.

Performing a preliminary linear change of
coordinates, we can assume without loss of generality that
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
is
the diagonal linear map
R_{q}\in\operatorname{GL}(\nu,\mathbb{C})
defined by (
1.1
).
Therefore, we will assume
g=R_{q}\circ f
with
q
non-resonant and
f\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
.
It is well-known that, in that case, the formal linearization problem admits a unique
solution,
i.e.
there is a unique
h\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
such that

R_{q}\circ f\circ h=h\circ R_{q}.

(2.6)

Écalle’s tree formalism will provide an explicit formula for
this conjugating formal diffeomorphism
h
, see
Theorem
A
of Section
3
.

It is a classical result by Rüssmann
[
Rus77
]
and Bruno
[
Bru72
]
that the holomorphic
linearization problem admits a solution under the following
reinforcement of the non-resonance condition.
For a given non-resonant
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\nu}
,
let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots\lambda_{\nu})\in\mathbb{C}^{\nu}
be such that

q_{j}=e^{2i\pi\lambda_{j}}
for every
j\in[\nu]
.
We use the notation

m\cdot\lambda\coloneqq m_{1}\lambda_{1}+\dots+m_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}
and set

\Omega(k)\coloneqq\min\{1\}\cup\big\{d(m\cdot\lambda-\lambda_{j},\mathbb{Z})\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak j\in[\nu],\ m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}\ \text{such that}\ 2\leq\lvert m\rvert\leq k+1\big\}

(2.7)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. This is a positive number independent on the choice of
(\lambda_{1},\dots\lambda_{\nu})
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a non-resonant
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\nu}
satisfies the
Bruno condition if
\mathcal{B}(q)<\infty
, where

\mathcal{B}(q)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log\frac{1}{\Omega(k)}\qquad\text{(series of non-negative terms).}

(2.8)

We say that a formal diffeomorphism
g
satisfies the Bruno condition
if its linear part
{\mathrm{d}}g(0)
is diagonalizable with eigenvalues

q_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu}
giving rise to a non-resonant
\nu
-tuple
q

satisfying the Bruno condition.

We will explain how Écalle’s tree formalism allows one to recover
Bruno-Rüssmann’s result—see Theorem
C
(i) of Section
4
.
As in the case of dimension
1
, Écalle’s method consists in

•

solving explicitly the formal linearization problem by writing the
composition operator
C_{h}
of the formal linearization in the form
of a series of operators
associated with
f
and indexed by forests (namely a tree expansion),

•

and, in the case of the holomorphic linearization problem, providing
suitable bounds by exploiting the Bruno condition and the holomorphy of
f
.

The same techniques allow one to prove similar results (that is
Bruno’s theorem
[
Bru71
]
) in the case of holomorphic vector
fields.

2.2
The linearization problem for germs of holomorphic vector fields

Near
0
as an equilibrium point, we consider formal or holomorphic
vector fields with diagonal linear part,

i.e.

\nu
-tuples
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{\nu})
of formal series of the form

V_{i}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})=\lambda_{i}z_{i}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu},\ |m|\geq 2}a_{i,m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu.

(2.9)

Such a vector field decomposes in a linear part
V_{\lambda}(z)=(\lambda_{1}z_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\nu}z_{\nu})
and a nonlinear one
a=V-V_{\lambda}\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}

and, as in the case of diffeomorphisms, we have a linearization problem that can be easily expressed with differential systems associated to such vector fields.

Consider the formal system of differential equations:

\frac{dz_{i}}{dt}=V_{i}(z)=\lambda_{i}z_{i}+a_{i}(z)\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu.

The formal linearization problem asks for an
h\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
such that, under the formal change of coordinates
z=h(\tilde{z})
the system becomes

\frac{d\tilde{z}_{i}}{dt}=\lambda_{i}\tilde{z}_{i}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu,

i.e.

h
is a formal identity-tangent diffeomorphism that formally
conjugates
V
to its linear part. The reader can check that the
conjugacy equation reads

\frac{dz_{i}}{dt}=\sum_{j\in[\nu]}\frac{d\tilde{z}_{j}}{dt}\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\tilde{z}_{j}}(\tilde{z})=\sum_{j\in[\nu]}\lambda_{j}\tilde{z}_{j}\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\tilde{z}_{j}}(\tilde{z})=V_{i}(h(\tilde{z}))=V_{i}(z)\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu.

(2.10)

As for holomorphic germs of diffeomorphisms, there is a holomorphic linearization problem that asks to find a holomorphic conjugacy
h\in\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
between a holomorphic vector field and its linear part.

Definition 2.3
.

The
\nu
-tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})
is the spectrum of
V=V_{\lambda}+a
and we say that
\lambda
is

non-resonant
if

i\in[\nu],\;\;m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lvert m\rvert\geq 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda_{i}\neq m\cdot\lambda.

where
m\cdot\lambda=m_{1}\lambda_{1}+\dots m_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu}
.
We say that a formal vector field
V
is
non-resonant
if its spectrum is non-resonant.

Under this non-resonance condition on the spectrum, it is well-known
that any formal vector field can be formally linearized—see
Theorem
B
of Section
3
for a proof using Ecalle’s techniques.

From the holomorphic point of view, the vector field analog of
Bruno-Rüssmann’s result is of course Bruno’s theorem
[
Bru71
]
,
which we now recall.
For a given non-resonant spectrum
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})
and any positive integer
k
, let

\Omega_{v.f.}(k)\coloneqq\min\{1\}\cup\big\{|m\cdot\lambda-\lambda_{j}|\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak j\in[\nu],\ m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}\ \text{such that}\ 2\leq\lvert m\rvert\leq k+1\big\}.

(2.11)

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a non-resonant
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C})^{\nu}
satisfies the
Bruno condition if
\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)<\infty
, where

\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log\frac{1}{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}\qquad\text{(series of non-negative terms).}

We say that a formal vector field
F
satisfies the Bruno condition
if its spectrum satisfies the Bruno condition.

As in the case of holomorphic germs, Ecalle’s tree formalism allows
one to get explicit formulas and to recover Bruno’s theorem, see
Theorem
C
(ii) of Section
4
.

2.3
Estimates and comparisons

As mentioned in the introduction, Theorem
D
of
Section
4.2
below shows how Ecalle’s formalism allows one to get
estimates on the radius of convergence of the conjugacy map that
recover the best known estimates due to
[
GLS15
]
and
[
GM10
]
, with
a new explicit dependence with respect to the dimension.

In both linearization problems, starting with a holomorphic
diffeomorphism or a vector field of the form (
1.4
) with
nonlinear part
a
analytic on the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}
of radius
b
and
continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}}
, we obtain the following lower
bound for the radius of a polydisc in which the conjugacy map
h
is
convergent:

b\,\frac{e^{-\gamma-\mathcal{B}}}{\nu(4M\nu+2)}\quad\text{with}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(q)\kern 5.0pt\text{or}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda),

where
M
is the supremum of
\frac{1}{b}\lvert a\rvert
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}}

and
\gamma
is some explicit numerical constant.

This is in accordance with the results of
[
GLS15
]
and
[
GM10
]
,
which were the first articles to give a lower bound of the form

e^{-\mathcal{B}-K}
with
K
depending on the nonlinear part
a
but
not on
\lambda

(prior to them only lower bounds of the form
e^{-2\mathcal{B}-K}
were known).
Note that, for vector fields, our Bruno series
\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)
is the same as in
[
GM10
]
,
but for diffeomorphisms there is a slight discrepancy between our Bruno series
\mathcal{B}(q)
and that of
[
GLS15
]
:
the latter reference makes use of the same series as in (
2.8
) except for the
function measuring the size of the small denominators.
We measure this size by the function
\Omega
defined in
equation (
2.7
), as in
[
Bru72
]
, whereas
in
[
GLS15
]

\Omega(k)
is replaced by

\alpha(k)\coloneqq\min\{1\}\cup\big\{|e^{m\cdot\lambda-\lambda_{j}}-1|\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak j\in[\nu],\ m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}\ \text{such that}\ 2\leq\lvert m\rvert\leq k+1\big\}.

(2.12)

However, these functions are of the same order at infinity in the
sense that there exist universal constants
c,C>0
such that

c\alpha(k)\leq\Omega(k)\leq C\alpha(k)\quad\text{for all}\;k>0.

Therefore, the two Bruno series

\mathcal{B}(q)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log\frac{1}{\Omega(k)}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log\frac{1}{\alpha(k)}

have the same nature and, in case of convergence, their difference is
bounded by a constant depending only on
c
and
C
.

For the sake of completeness, let us mention that a third function

\varepsilon
appears in
[
Rus77
]
,
[
Pos17
]
and

[
Rai13
]
, defined by

\varepsilon(k)\coloneqq\min\{1\}\cup\big\{|q^{m}-q_{j}|\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak j\in[\nu],\ m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}\ \text{such that}\ 2\leq\lvert m\rvert\leq k+1\big\}.

(2.13)

This function is of the same order as the previous ones, so that the
corresponding Bruno condition is equivalent. Note also that there are
different types of Bruno series that are proved to be equivalent in
the appendix of
[
Rai13
]
.

3
Formal linearization and Ecalle’s tree formalism

A first key to Ecalle’s tree formalism is to adopt an operator point
of view for diffeomorphisms and vector fields,
i.e.
to consider these
geometric objects (or their formal counterparts) respectively as
algebra automorphisms or derivations acting on the algebra of formal
series in
\nu
variables.

3.1
The operator point of view

We recall that the
total order
of a non-zero formal series
\varphi(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
is defined as
the minimal value of
\lvert n\rvert
over the multi-integers
n
such that

\alpha_{n}\neq 0
. We denote it by
\operatorname{ord}\varphi
.
By convention
\operatorname{ord}0=\infty
.

Notice that the formal series without constant term are exactly those
for which the total order is
\geq 1
. For
k\geq 0

we denote by
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]_{\geq k}
the set of formal power series of order
greater or equal to
k
. Note that
\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}=\big(\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]_{\geq 2}\big)^{\nu}
.

We call
operators
the
\mathbb{C}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.
An operator
\Theta
is said to be
tangent-to-identity
if

\Theta-\mathop{\hbox{{\rm Id}}}\nolimits
increases the total order by at least one unit,
i.e.

\operatorname{ord}(\Theta\varphi-\varphi)\geq\operatorname{ord}\varphi+1
for all
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.
With any
\nu
-tuple
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]_{\geq 1})^{\nu}
we
associate the
composition operator

C_{v}\colon\thinspace\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ v.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the map
v\mapsto C_{v}
induces a bijection
between
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
and the set of all tangent-to-identity algebra
automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.

Proof.

Let
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{\nu})\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
and

a=v-\operatorname{id}\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
. For any formal series
\varphi
, using
the formal Taylor expansion at
z
of

C_{v}\varphi(z)=\varphi(v(z))=\varphi(z+a(z))
, one easily checks that

C_{v}
is a tangent-to-identity algebra automorphism and that the map

v\mapsto C_{v}
is injective.

Let
\Theta
be a tangent-to-identity algebra endomorphism of

\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
. For
i\in[\nu]
, we let it act on the formal series
z_{i}

and define
v_{i}\coloneqq\Theta z_{i}
;
as
\operatorname{ord}(\Theta z_{i}-z_{i})\geq 2
, we have

v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{\nu})\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
and it only
remains to prove that
\Theta=C_{v}
.

As
\Theta
and
C_{v}
are algebra endomorphisms whose images coincide
on the formal series
z_{1},\dots,z_{\nu}
, their actions coincide on
polynomials of
z_{1},\dots,z_{\nu}
too.
Let
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
: for any

N\in\mathbb{N}
, we can write
\varphi=P_{N}+\varphi_{N}
with
P_{N}
polynomial
and
\varphi_{N}\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]_{\geq N}
, thus

\operatorname{ord}((\Theta-C_{v})\varphi)=\operatorname{ord}((\Theta-C_{v})\varphi_{N})\geq\min\big\{\operatorname{ord}(\Theta\varphi_{N}-\varphi_{N}),\operatorname{ord}(C_{v}\varphi_{N}-\varphi_{N})\big\}>N.

Since this holds for all integer
N
, one can deduce that
(\Theta-C_{v})\varphi=0
and thus
\Theta=C_{v}
.
∎

Any
\nu
-tuple
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\nu})\in(\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]])^{\nu}
can
also be considered as a formal vector field, for which we denote the
associated derivation of
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
by

X_{v}:\varphi\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}v_{i}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{i}}.

(3.2)

One easily gets an analog of the previous lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the map
v\mapsto X_{v}
induces a bijection
between
(\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]_{\geq 1})^{\nu}
and the set of all derivations of

\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
that do not decrease the total order.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the previous one.
∎

With such definitions, one can express linearization equations in terms of operators.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
g=R_{q}\circ(\operatorname{id}+a)
be a non-resonant formal
diffeomorphism
(with
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
).
With the notation
M_{q}\coloneqq C_{R_{q}}
, the linearization equation (
2.6
)
is equivalent to

C_{h}C_{\operatorname{id}+a}=M_{q}C_{h}M_{q}^{-1},

(3.3)

where
h
is the sought linearizing formal diffeomorphism.
Similarly, if
V=V_{\lambda}+a
is a non-resonant formal vector field
(with
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
), with the notation

L_{\lambda}\coloneqq X_{V_{\lambda}}
,
the linearization equation (
2.10
) is equivalent to

[L_{\lambda},C_{h}]=C_{h}X_{a}

(3.4)

where
h
is the sought linearizing formal diffeomorphism
and we use
[\,\cdot,\cdot\,]
to denote operator commutator.

Proof.

In the diffeomorphism case, with
f\coloneqq\operatorname{id}+a
, the linearization equation

R_{q}\circ f\circ h=h\circ R_{q}
immediately translates into

C_{h}C_{f}M_{q}=M_{q}C_{h}

and
M_{q}
is an invertible operator,
thus (
2.6
) and (
3.3
) are equivalent.

For vector fields,
since
h_{i}=C_{h}(z_{i})
and
V_{i}=X_{V}(z_{i})
,
the conjugacy equation (
2.10
) can be written

L_{\lambda}C_{h}(z_{i})=C_{h}X_{V}(z_{i})\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu,

or, equivalently,

C_{h}^{-1}L_{\lambda}C_{h}(z_{i})=X_{V}(z_{i})\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu.

Since
X_{V}
and
C_{h}^{-1}L_{\lambda}C_{h}
are derivations, saying that
their actions coincide on the formal series
z_{i}
is equivalent to
saying that they coincide as operators. The conjugacy equation is thus
equivalent to

L_{\lambda}C_{h}=C_{h}X_{V},

which is the desired equation, since
X_{V}=L_{\lambda}+X_{a}
.
∎

In both formal linearization problems, the linearizing automorphism

C_{h}
will be computed as a formally convergent series of

homogeneous
operators.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. An operator
D
is said to be

n
-homogeneous
if, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}
,
D(\mathbb{C}z^{m})\subset\mathbb{C}z^{m+n}
.

For instance, for each
i\in[\nu]
, the partial derivative operator

\partial_{i}\coloneqq\dfrac{\partial\;}{\partial z_{i}}
is
n
-homogeneous with
n=-e_{i}
, where

e_{i}\coloneqq\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\uparrow\\
\text{$i$th position}\end{subarray}}{(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)},

while the operators
z_{1}\partial_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}\partial_{\nu}
are
0
-homogeneous.

Homogeneity will help us to manipulate formally convergent series of
operators.
The total order of formal power series first allows us to define
summable families for the formal convergence: a family

(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
of formal series is summable if, for any

k\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\{i\in I\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\allowbreak\operatorname{ord}\phi_{i}\leq k\}
is finite.
Correspondingly, we can define pointwise summability of families of

\mathbb{C}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.
The following elementary summability criterion will be of constant use:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(D_{j})_{j\in J}
be a family of homogeneous operators where
D_{j}
is
n_{j}
-homogeneous. Suppose that

(i)

for each
j\in J
,
\lvert n_{j}\rvert\geq 0
;

(ii)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\{j\in J\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\allowbreak\lvert n_{j}\rvert=k\}
is finite.

Then, for any
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
, the family of formal series

(D_{j}\varphi)_{j\in J}
is summable and the operator

\varphi\mapsto\sum_{j\in J}D_{j}\varphi
is a well-defined linear endomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.

3.2
The set
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
,
\mathcal{N}
-forests and
\mathcal{N}
-trees

We now introduce the set
\mathcal{N}
of homogeneity degrees that we will
encouter in practice, as well as the set of
\mathcal{N}
-forests that will
index our summable families of homogeneous operators.

Let us define
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
as

\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\big\{m-e_{i}\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak m\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu},\;\;\lvert m\rvert\geq 2,\;\;i\in[\nu]\big\},

(3.5)

so that, either dealing with
f=\operatorname{id}+a\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
or a vector
field
V=V_{\lambda}+a
, the nonlinear part
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
can be
written

a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\nu}),\qquad a_{i}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}a_{i,n}z^{n+e_{i}}

(3.6)

with an arbitrary collection of complex numbers
(a_{i,n})_{(i,n)\in[\nu]\times\mathcal{N}}
such that

a_{i,n}\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n+e_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}.

(3.7)

Observe that
z^{n+e_{i}}=z_{i}z^{n}
, but this does not necessarily mean
that
a_{i}
is divisible by
z_{i}
in
\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
, because a multi-integer

n\in\mathcal{N}
may have a negative component (but at most one, and then the
value of this component must be
-1
).
Observe also that the non-resonance condition for diffeomorphisms can be rephrased as

\text{$q$ non-resonant}{\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad}\text{$q^{n}\neq 1$ for all $n\in\mathcal{N}$}

(3.8)

whereas, for vector fields,

\text{$\lambda$ non-resonant}{\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad}\text{$\lambda\cdot n\neq 0$ for all $n\in\mathcal{N}$}.

(3.9)

3.3
The coarmould associated with a tangent-to-identity formal diffeomorphism or a vector field

We call coarmould any family of operators indexed by

.
The summation in (

-forests

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be defined by coefficients
(a_{i,n})_{(i,n)\in[\nu]\times\mathcal{N}}
as in (
3.6
)–(
3.7
).
The formulas

(i)

D_{\emptyset}=\mathop{\hbox{{\rm Id}}}\nolimits
,

(ii)

D_{n\triangleleft F}=\big(D_{F}(a_{1,n}z^{n+e_{1}})\big)\partial_{1}+\cdots+\big(D_{F}(a_{\nu,n}z^{n+e_{\nu}})\big)\partial_{\nu}\;

for any
n\in\mathcal{N}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
,

(iii)

\displaystyle D_{U_{1}\cdots U_{d}}=\frac{1}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{r}!}\sum_{i\in[\nu]^{d}}(D_{U_{1}}z_{i_{1}})\cdots(D_{U_{d}}z_{i_{d}})\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{d}}\;

for any

, with
d\geq 1
,
where
r
is the number of distinct
\mathcal{N}
-trees in this list and

d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
are the corresponding multiplicities
(
i.e.

U_{1}\cdots U_{d}
can also be written
T_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots T_{r}^{d_{r}}
as in (
3.11
))

determine uniquely a coarmould
D_{\bullet}
.
Moreover,
D_{F}
is
\lVert F\rVert
-homogeneous for each
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

We first notice that, although the decomposition in disjoint union of

\mathcal{N}
-trees of an
\mathcal{N}
-forest
F=U_{1}\cdots U_{d}
is determined
only up to a permutation of
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{d})
, the formula in (iii)
makes sense because the right-hand side is invariant under permutation.
We also observe that (iii) is compatible with (ii) in the sense
that, when

, (iii) merely asserts that

is a derivation, while (ii) tells which

.
The operator

is well-defined for any

by induction on the height of
F
and its homogeneity property is also easily obtained by the same induction.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

The coarmould

associated with any given

\displaystyle D_{F}(\varphi\psi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})\times\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})\\

F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.

Proof.

Let us prove this statement for arbitrary
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
and

\varphi,\psi\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]
.
If
F=\emptyset
then (
3.15
) is obvious.
We thus suppose

More precisely, we assume that

and
we are given a partition

and collections of

\mathcal{N}
-trees
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{d})

(T_{1},\ldots,T_{r})
such that, for each

d_{k}=\operatorname{card}P_{k}\geq 1,\qquad\qquad j\in P_{k}\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\kern 5.0ptT_{k}=U_{j}.

We have

D_{F}=\frac{1}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{r}!}\sum_{i\in[\nu]^{d}}(D_{U_{1}}z_{i_{1}})\cdots(D_{U_{d}}z_{i_{d}})\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{d}}

and, for each

\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{d}}(\varphi\psi)=\sum_{\Delta\subset[d]}\bigg(\prod_{j\in\Delta}\partial_{i_{j}}\bigg)\varphi\cdot\bigg(\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\Delta}\partial_{i_{j}}\bigg)\psi,

with summation over all subsets of
[d]
including
the empty set, hence

D_{F}(\varphi\psi)=\frac{1}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{r}!}\sum_{\Delta\subset[d]}\chi_{\Delta}\quad\text{with}\\[4.30554pt]
\chi_{\Delta}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in[\nu]^{d}}\bigg(\prod_{j\in\Delta}D_{U_{j}}z_{i_{j}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j\in\Delta}\partial_{i_{j}}\bigg)\varphi\cdot\bigg(\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\Delta}D_{U_{j}}z_{i_{j}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\Delta}\partial_{i_{j}}\bigg)\psi.

Now, for each
\Delta\subset[d]

[\nu]^{d}\to[\nu]^{\Delta}\times[\nu]^{[d]\setminus\Delta}
obtained by
viewing each

as a map
[d]\to[\nu]
and sending it to
the pair of restrictions
(i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime})
,
i^{\prime}\coloneqq i_{|\Delta}
,
i^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq i_{|[d]\setminus\Delta}
, thus
\chi_{\Delta}=\varphi_{\Delta}\cdot\psi_{[d]\setminus\Delta}
with

\varphi_{\Delta}=\sum_{i^{\prime}\in[\nu]^{\Delta}}\bigg(\prod_{j\in\Delta}D_{U_{j}}z_{i^{\prime}_{j}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j\in\Delta}\partial_{i^{\prime}_{j}}\bigg)\varphi,\quad\psi_{[d]\setminus\Delta}=\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}\in[\nu]^{[d]\setminus\Delta}}\bigg(\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\Delta}D_{U_{j}}z_{i^{\prime\prime}_{j}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\Delta}\partial_{i^{\prime\prime}_{j}}\bigg)\psi.

Let us map each
\Delta\subset[d]
to the pair of
\mathcal{N}
-forests

(F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime})
such that
F=F^{\prime}F^{\prime\prime}
defined as

F^{\prime}\coloneqq\prod_{j\in\Delta}U_{j}=T_{1}^{d^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots T_{r}^{d^{\prime}_{r}},\quad F^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\Delta}U_{j}=T_{1}^{d^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots T_{r}^{d^{\prime\prime}_{r}},

with
d^{\prime}_{k}=\operatorname{card}(\Delta\cap P_{k})

d^{\prime\prime}_{k}=d_{k}-d^{\prime}_{k}
for each

.
This map is surjective and the number of preimages of any such

(indeed, a preimage

is determined choosing

, …,

, …,

Since

\varphi_{\Delta}=d^{\prime}_{1}!\cdots d^{\prime}_{r}!\,D_{F^{\prime}}\varphi,\quad\psi_{[d]\setminus\Delta}=d^{\prime\prime}_{1}!\cdots d^{\prime\prime}_{r}!\,D_{F^{\prime\prime}}\psi,

we get

D_{F}(\varphi\psi)=\frac{1}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{r}!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime})\\
\text{s.t.}\,F=F^{\prime}F^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\binom{d_{1}}{d^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots\binom{d_{r}}{d^{\prime}_{r}}d^{\prime}_{1}!\cdots d^{\prime}_{r}!\,D_{F^{\prime}}\varphi\cdot d^{\prime\prime}_{1}!\cdots d^{\prime\prime}_{r}!\,D_{F^{\prime\prime}}\psi,

3.4
Armoulds and tree expansions

We call armould any family of complex numbers indexed by
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})

and use the notation

for an armould
(A^{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}
.
One easily checks that the coarmould associated to a given

gives rise to a pointwise summable family operators

(A^{F}D_{F}(a))_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}
for any armould

.
Indeed, the summability criterion of Lemma
3.5
is
fulfilled because each
D_{F}(a)
is
\lVert F\rVert
-homogeneous with
\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert\geq 0

and, for every

\mathcal{N}
-forests

such that

(because of (
3.13
) and (

)).

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

.
For any armould

\sum A^{\bullet}D_{\bullet}(a)\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}A^{F}D_{F}(a)

\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]

3.9

to ensure that the tree

.

Let

and
D_{\bullet}
its associated coarmould.

(i)

If an armould

is separative, that is

Proof.

(i) Suppose that

is a separative armould.

(ii)

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
and
A,B\in\mathbb{C}
with

.
Then the
armould

defined by:

(3.25)

where

is the composition inverse in

\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
of the tangent-to-identity formal diffeomorphism

defined by

(3.26)

Note that in the case
(A,B)=(-1,1)
the formal diffeomorphism

is
nothing but the composition inverse of
\operatorname{id}+a
.

Proof.

The armould
K^{\bullet}
is clearly separative;
by Proposition
3.11
(i), its tree expansion coincides with
a composition operator
C_{w}
, with a formal tangent-to-identity
diffeomorphism
w
whose components are

w_{i}\coloneqq z_{i}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{N})}K^{T}D_{T}z_{i}
.
We must check that this

is indeed the composition inverse of

of (

).
Using Lemma

(ii), we compute

\displaystyle w_{i}(z)

\displaystyle=z_{i}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}\,\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}K^{n\triangleleft F}D_{n\triangleleft F}z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}\,\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}A^{\#F+1}B^{n+\lvert\|F\|\rvert}D_{F}(a_{i,n}z^{n+e_{i}})

we have,

q_{i}^{-1}M_{q}m=q_{i}^{-1}m\circ R_{q}=q^{n}m.

(3.37)

Let
\Theta\coloneqq\sum S^{\bullet}(q)D_{\bullet}
.
Since

\Theta=C_{h^{*}}
with

We want to show that this

On the one hand, because of equation (

On the other hand, by (

Proof of Theorem

.

Similarly to the previous proof,
\sum\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)D_{\bullet}=C_{h^{*}}
with

Thus, for

and, by Lemma

D_{T}z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}z^{e_{i}+\lVert T\rVert}

4
Solution of the holomorphic linearization problem

In Section
3
, the introduction of the set
\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathcal{N})

made it possible to define without ambiguity the “linearizing
armoulds”
S^{\bullet}(q)
and
\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)
and obtain the formal
linearization as a sum over this set. In fact, it is possible to
restrict this sum to an even smaller set of forests

NV(\mathcal{N})\subset\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathcal{N})
, because the elements
F
of

\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathcal{N})\setminus NV(\mathcal{N})
yield, for any data
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
, a
vanishing operator
D_{F}(a)
. If we denote by
{\cal S}^{\bullet}
the armould

S^{\bullet}(q)
or
\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)
,
Theorems
A
and
B
thus entail
that the composition automorphism of the linearizing transformation
for
R_{q}\circ(\operatorname{id}+a)
or
V_{\lambda}+a
reads

C_{h}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathcal{N})}{\cal S}^{F}D_{F}(a)=\sum_{F\in NV(\mathcal{N})}{\cal S}^{F}D_{F}(a).

(4.1)

The definition and main properties of this set of “non-vanishing”
forests are deferred to Section
5
.

The armould
{\cal S}^{\bullet}=S^{\bullet}(q)
or
\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)
depends only
on the spectrum
q
or
\lambda
, not on the nonlinear part
a
of the
dynamical system to be linearized.
We will show in Section
6
that,
under the Bruno condition (Definition
2.2
or
2.4
),

\lvert{\cal S}^{F}\rvert\leq B^{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptF\in NV(\mathcal{N}),

(4.2)

where
B=e^{\gamma+\mathcal{B}(q)}
(Theorem
G
) or

B=e^{\gamma+\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)}
(Theorem
F
),
with numerical constant
\gamma\coloneqq\sum_{k\geq 1}\big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\big)\log(k+2)
.

Taking for granted these results of
Sections
5
–
6
, we now show how
they allow one to prove the
convergence of the formal linearizing transformation
h
when
a
is
convergent.

4.1
Holomorphy

Theorem C
.

(i)

Suppose that
g=R_{q}\circ(\operatorname{id}+a)\in\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
is a local analytic non-resonant
diffeomorphism whose spectrum
q
satisfies the Bruno condition.
Then the solution
h
to the linearization problem is convergent.

(ii)

Suppose that
V=V_{\lambda}+a
is a local analytic non-resonant vector field
whose spectrum
\lambda
satisfies the Bruno condition.
Then the solution
h
to the linearization problem is convergent.

Proof.

Both results will follow from (
4.1
)–(
4.2
),
with
{\cal S}^{\bullet}=S^{\bullet}(q)
or
\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)
,
by a majorant series argument.

Suppose that we are given a convergent

a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
and consider either the holomorphic
diffeomorphism
g=R_{q}\circ(\operatorname{id}+a)\in\mathscr{G}_{\nu}
or the
holomorphic vector field
V=V_{\lambda}+a
.
Theorems
A
and
B
give the
solution
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{\nu})\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
to the formal linearization problem
in the form

h_{i}(z)=z_{i}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{N})\cap NV(\mathcal{N})}{\cal S}^{T}D_{T}(a)z_{i}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu

(4.3)

thanks to (
4.1
).

Expanding the components of
a
as

a_{i}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}a_{i,n}z^{n+e_{i}}\quad\text{for each}\;i\in[\nu],

we define
\overline{a}\coloneqq(\overline{a}_{1},\ldots,\overline{a}_{\nu})
by

\overline{a}_{i}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\lvert a_{i,n}\rvert z^{n+e_{i}}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu,

so that
\overline{a}_{i}
is a majorant series of
a_{i}
in the following sense:

A formal series

\displaystyle\psi(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}}\psi_{n}z^{n}
with real non-negative
coefficients
\psi_{n}
is said to be a majorant series of a formal
series
\displaystyle\phi(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}}\phi_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu}]]

if
\lvert\phi_{n}\rvert\leq\psi_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\nu}
.
We then use the notation
\phi\prec\psi
.

Lemma
3.8
implies that

D_{T}(a)z_{i}=\beta_{i,T}(a)\,z^{\lVert T\rVert+e_{i}}\quad\text{for each}\kern 5.0pti\in[\nu],\kern 5.0ptT\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{N}),

(4.4)

where the coefficient
\beta_{i,T}
is a polynomial function with rational

non-negative
coefficients of
\{a_{1,n},\ldots,a_{\nu,n}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\allowbreak n\in\mathcal{N}\}
.
Since
a_{i}\prec\overline{a_{i}}
, this implies that

D_{T}(a)z_{i}\prec D_{T}(\overline{a})z_{i}.

(4.5)

Combining this property with (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we get

h_{i}(z)\prec z_{i}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{N})\cap NV(\mathcal{N})}B^{\lvert\lVert T\rVert\rvert}D_{T}(\overline{a})z_{i}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti\in[\nu].

(4.6)

Up to vanishing terms that do not alter the result, we recognize in the right-hand side of (
4.6
) the action
on
z_{i}
of the tree expansion
\sum K^{\bullet}D_{\bullet}(\overline{a})
with

K^{F}\coloneqq B^{\lvert\|F\|\rvert}
.
According to Proposition
3.12
, this tree expansion
is the composition operator
C_{w}
,
where
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{\nu})
is the composition inverse in

\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
of the tangent-to-identity formal diffeomorphism

g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{\nu})
defined by

g_{i}(z)=z_{i}-\frac{1}{B}\overline{a}_{i}(Bz)\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu.

We have found
h_{i}\prec w_{i}
for each
i
.
Since
a
and thus
\overline{a}
are convergent, it follows
that
g
is a holomorphic tangent-to-identity diffeomorphism, whose
composition inverse
w
is also holomorphic.
Consequently, each component
w_{i}
is a convergent series, and so
is
h_{i}
.
∎

4.2
A lower bound for the radius of convergence

A small refinement of this proof will
yield a lower bound for the radius of the
polydisc in which the linearizing transformation is holomorphic.

Theorem D
.

Consider
g=R_{q}\circ(\operatorname{id}+a)
or
V=V_{\lambda}+a

with a non-resonant spectrum satisfying the Bruno condition,

i.e.
the Bruno series
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(q)
or
\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)
is finite,
and
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}
holomorphic in the polydisc

\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}\coloneqq\big\{z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{\nu})\in\mathbb{C}^{\nu}\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak|z_{i}|<b\;\,\text{for}\;\,i=1,\ldots,\nu\big\}

(4.7)

and continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}}
.
Then one can find
b^{\prime}>0
such that the solution
h
to the linearization problem is holomorphic in
the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{b^{\prime}}^{\nu}
and

b^{\prime}>b\frac{e^{-\gamma-\mathcal{B}}}{\nu(4M\nu+2)},

(4.8)

where
\gamma\coloneqq\sum_{k\geq 1}\big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\big)\log(k+2)
and

M\coloneqq\frac{1}{b}\max\big\{|a_{i}(z)|\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak z\in\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu},\;i=1,\ldots,\nu\big\}
.

The proof will require two
lemmas on holomorphic tangent-to-identity diffeomorphisms in
\nu

complex variables.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\nu\geq 1
and
m>0
. The automorphism
r_{m}
of
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}

defined by

(r_{m}\varphi)(z)=\frac{1}{m}\varphi(mz)\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\varphi\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}

(4.9)

induces an automorphism of
\mathscr{G}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
.
More precisely, if
\varphi
is convergent in the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{b}^{\nu}
,
then
r_{m}\varphi
is convergent in the polydisc
\mathbb{D}_{b/m}^{\nu}
.

Proof.

Obvious.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\nu\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
. Define
\varphi_{\alpha,\nu}=(\varphi_{\alpha,\nu,1},\dots,\varphi_{\alpha,\nu,\nu})\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{\nu,\operatorname{id}}
by

\varphi_{\alpha,\nu,i}(z)=z_{i}-\alpha\sum_{k\geq 2}Z^{k}\quad\text{with}\kern 5.0ptZ=z_{1}+\dots+z_{\nu}

for
i=1,\ldots,\nu
.
Then
\varphi_{\alpha,\nu}
is holomorphic in

U_{\nu}=\{|Z|<1\}
and its composition inverse

\Psi_{\alpha,\nu}
is holomorphic in the polydisc

\mathbb{D}_{\nu^{-1}\kappa_{\alpha\nu}}^{\nu}
, where

\kappa_{\beta}\coloneqq 1+2\beta-2\sqrt{\beta(1+\beta)}\quad\text{for $\beta>0$.}

(4.10)

Moreover,

\frac{1}{4\beta+1}>\kappa_{\beta}>\frac{1}{4\beta+2}\quad\text{for all $\beta>0$.}

(4.11)

Proof.

In view of the properties of the geometric series, each
\varphi_{\alpha,\nu,i}
is holomorphic in
U
and

\varphi_{\alpha,\nu,i}(z)=z_{i}-\alpha\frac{Z^{2}}{1-Z}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0ptz\in U.

In dimension
\nu=1
, with
z=z_{1}
, the formal inverse
\Psi_{\alpha,1}
of
\varphi_{\alpha,1}
satisfies

\Psi_{\alpha,1}-\alpha\frac{\Psi_{\alpha,1}^{2}}{1-\Psi_{\alpha,1}}=z

that also reads

(1+\alpha)\Psi_{\alpha,1}^{2}-(1+z)\Psi_{\alpha,1}+z=0.

The discriminant of this polynomial is

\Delta=1-2(1+2\alpha)z+z^{2}=(\kappa_{\alpha}-z)(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}-z)

with
\kappa_{\alpha}
as in (
4.11
) and
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\kappa_{\alpha}}=1+2\alpha+2\sqrt{\alpha(1+\alpha)}
.
Since
0<\kappa_{\alpha}<\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
, we have

\Psi_{\alpha,1}(z)=\frac{1+z-\big(1-2(1+2\alpha)z+z^{2}\big)^{1/2}}{2(1+\alpha)}\quad\text{holomorphic for}\kern 5.0ptz\in\mathbb{D}_{\kappa_{\alpha}}^{1}

(using the principal branch of the square root).

In dimension
\nu
, the composition inverse

\Psi_{\alpha,\nu}=(\Psi_{\alpha,\nu,1},\dots,\Psi_{\alpha,\nu,\nu})

of
\varphi_{\alpha,\nu}
is easily computed:
since
\sum_{i\in[\nu]}\varphi_{\alpha,\nu,i}(z)=\varphi_{\alpha\nu,1}(Z)
, the
solution to
\Psi_{\alpha,\nu}\circ\varphi_{\alpha,\nu}=\operatorname{id}
is given by

\Psi_{\alpha,\nu,i}(z)=z_{i}+\frac{1}{\nu}\big(\Psi_{\alpha\nu,1}(Z)-Z\big)\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0pti=1,\ldots,\nu.

The case
\nu=1
thus ensures that
\Psi_{\alpha,\nu}
is holomorphic
in the domain

\big\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\nu})\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak Z=z_{1}+\dots+z_{\nu}\in\mathbb{D}_{\kappa_{\alpha\nu}}^{1}\big\}
,
which contains the polydisc of radius

\frac{1}{\nu}\kappa_{\alpha\nu}
.

Finally, one gets the estimates (
4.11
) as follows.
For
\beta>0
,

\kappa_{\beta}-\frac{1}{4\beta+2}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}-\frac{1}{4\beta+2}=\frac{4\beta+2-\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}{(4\beta+2)\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}=\frac{4\beta+2-1-2\beta-2\sqrt{\beta(1+\beta)}}{(4\beta+2)\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}=\frac{\kappa_{\beta}}{(4\beta+2)\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}>0

and

\kappa_{\beta}-\frac{1}{4\beta+1}=\frac{4\beta+1-1-2\beta-2\sqrt{\beta(1+\beta)}}{(4\beta+1)\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}=\frac{2(\beta-\sqrt{\beta(1+\beta)})}{(4\beta+1)\kappa_{\beta}^{*}}<0.

∎

In this section, we point out some fundamental properties of the

5.1
The set

Example 5.2

Then there are infinitely many

Pick one such

by (

by Lemma

making use of bamboo tree notation (

Now, pick

and

whose

th component is
-1
and
whose

th component is

(there are infinitely many

Lemma

Here are properties of the set

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

,

,

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

. This ensures that

For the last point, supposing that

, which can be expressed as a sum over

(iii), the term corresponding

5.2
Admissible cuts and composition of operators

Admissible cuts arise naturally when composing operators
belonging to the family
(D_{F}(a))_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}
.
They will play a crucial role in analytic estimates of the armoulds related to
linearization problems through their combinatorial properties.
We recall here some definitions and properties that can be found in

[
FM14
]
.

Recall that, according to Section
3.2
, an
\mathcal{N}
-forest
F
can
be represented by
\mathcal{N}
-decorated poset and we then denote by
V_{F}

the underlying set of vertices. For an
\mathcal{N}
-tree
T
, we denote
by
\rho_{T}
its root,
i.e.
the unique minimal element of
(V_{T},\preceq_{T})
.

Definition 5.4
.

An admissible cut of an
\mathcal{N}
-tree
T
is a subset

of
V_{T}

such that, on the path from the root to an element of
c
, no other
vertex of
c
is encountered. For such an admissible cut, we denote
by

the product of the subtrees of
T
whose
roots are in
c
, and
R^{c}(T)\in\{\emptyset\}\cup\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{N})
the
remaining part, once these subtrees have been removed.

An admissible cut of an
\mathcal{N}
-forest
F=T_{1}\cdots T_{d}
is a
subset
c
of
V_{F}
that is a disjoint union

c=c^{1}\cup\dots\cup c^{d}
, where each
c^{i}
is an admissible cut
of
T_{i}
.
We then use the notations

P^{c}(F)\coloneqq P^{c^{1}}(T_{1})\cdots P^{c^{d}}(T_{d})
and

R^{c}(F)\coloneqq R^{c^{1}}(T_{1})\cdots R^{c^{d}}(T_{d})
.
We denote the set of all admissible cuts of
F
by
adm(F)
.

For example, if
\sigma
is a vertex of
F=T_{1}\cdots T_{d}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
,
then
c=\{\sigma\}
is an admissible cut and

P^{\{\sigma\}}(F)=\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)
.
Note also that there are two “trivial”
admissible cuts:

•

(empty cut)
\emptyset\in adm(F)
,
P^{\emptyset}(F)=\emptyset
and
R^{\emptyset}(F)=F
;

•

(all minimal elements)
m_{F}\coloneqq\{\rho_{T_{1}},\dots\rho_{T_{d}}\}\in adm(F)
,

P^{m_{F}}(F)=F
and
R^{m_{F}}(F)=\emptyset
.

Following
[
FM14
]
(Theorem 5) we have the product rule:

Proposition 5.5
.

For any
\mathcal{N}
-forests
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, denoting by
P(F_{1},F_{2})
the
set of pairs
(F,c)
such that
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
,
c\in adm(F)
,

P^{c}(F)=F_{1}
and
R^{c}(F)=F_{2}
,

D_{F_{1}}(a)D_{F_{2}}(a)=\sum_{(F,c)\in P(F_{1},F_{2})}D_{F}(a)\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0pta\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\nu}.

(5.4)

Note that the set
P(F_{1},F_{2})
is finite and, given
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
,
the cardinality
k(F_{1},F_{2},F)
of
\{c\in adm(F)\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\allowbreak(F,c)\in P(F_{1},F_{2})\}
can be
larger than

,
hence, in the product
D_{F_{1}}(a)D_{F_{2}}(a)
, the operator
D_{F}(a)
can appear
several times.
We thus write

D_{F_{1}}D_{F_{2}}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}k(F_{1},F_{2},F)D_{F},

(5.5)

where only finitely many integers

are positive in the sum,
with implicit dependence upon

.

5.3
Admissible cuts and vanishing operators

From the product rule (
5.5
), one can deduce new
informations on universally vanishing operators as in
[
FM14
, Thm 8]
.

Theorem E
.

Given

and

, both

and

belong to
NV(\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

Suppose that

and

\displaystyle D_{P^{c}(F)}D_{R^{c}(F)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{G\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})}k(P^{c}(F),R^{c}(F),G)D_{G}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle k(P^{c}(F),R^{c}(F),F)D_{F}+\sum_{G\not=F}k(P^{c}(F),R^{c}(F),F)D_{G},

where

is positive.
In view of (
3.14
), each term of the right-hand

(a_{i,n})_{(i,n)\in[\nu]\times\mathcal{N}}
, the variables

and the derivations
\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{\nu}
, with non-negative rational

∎

This theorem allows one to recover

(i)

For a non-resonant diffeomorphism spectrum

the definition (

) of

\Omega(k)=\min\{1\}\cup\big\{d(n\cdot\lambda,\mathbb{Z})\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak n\in\mathcal{N}\ \text{such that}\ \lvert n\rvert\leq k\big\}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0ptk\in\mathbb{N}^{*},

(6.1)

whereas for a non-resonant vector field spectrum

,
the definition (
2.11
) of

can be rewritten

\Omega_{v.f.}(k)=\min\{1\}\cup\big\{|n\cdot\lambda|\nonscript\>\big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak n\in\mathcal{N}\ \text{such that}\ \lvert n\rvert\leq k\big\}\quad\text{for}\kern 5.0ptk\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

(6.2)

As promised, in this section we will prove the estimates (

) for the

and
\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)
.
Our proof will rely on the following combinatorial lemma.

6.1
A counting lemma

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
F\in NV(\mathcal{N})
. Define
W_{0}(F)=\tilde{W}_{0}(F)=V_{F}
and, for
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle W_{k}(F)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Big\{\sigma\in V_{F}\nonscript\>\Big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak d(\lambda\cdot\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert,\mathbb{Z})<\frac{\Omega(k)}{k+2}\Big\},

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{k}(F)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Big\{\sigma\in V_{F}\nonscript\>\Big|\nonscript\>\allowbreak|\lambda\cdot\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert|<\frac{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}{k+2}\Big\}.

Then, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the cardinalities of these sets are bounded
as follows:

\#W_{k}(F)\leq\frac{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}{k+1},\qquad\#\tilde{W}_{k}(F)\leq\frac{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}{k+1}.

(6.3)

Proof.

The proof is exactly the same in both cases, replacing
\Omega(k)
and
the map
z\mapsto d(z,\mathbb{Z})
by
\Omega_{v.f.}(k)
and the map

z\mapsto\lvert z\rvert
; the key properties

\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C},\quad d(-z,\mathbb{Z})=d(z,\mathbb{Z})\quad\text{and}\quad d(z+z^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})\leq d(z,\mathbb{Z})+d(z^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})

(6.4)

obviously hold true for
z\mapsto\lvert z\rvert
too. We thus focus on the proof in the case of
W_{k}(F)
.

For
k=0
the result is obvious:
\#W_{0}(F)=\#F
and, since
all the decorations
n\in\mathcal{N}
have
\lvert n\rvert\geq 1
,
we get
\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert\geq\#F
.

We thus suppose
k\geq 1
and proceed by induction on the size of
F
.
If
\#F=1
, then
F
as exactly one vertex
\sigma
decorated by

n\in\mathcal{N}
.
Either
W_{k}(F)=\emptyset
and the inequality is obvious,
or
W_{k}(F)=\{\sigma\}
, in which case

d(\lambda\cdot n,\mathbb{Z})<\frac{\Omega(k)}{k+2}<\Omega(k)

and, thanks to the definition of
\Omega(k)
, we get
\lvert n\rvert\geq k+1
, whence

\frac{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}{k+1}=\frac{\lvert n\rvert}{k+1}\geq 1=\#W_{k}(F).

We now suppose that the result is true for any forest in
NV(\mathcal{N})
of
size less than
s\geq 2
and consider a forest
F\in NV(\mathcal{N})
of size
s
.

If
d\coloneqq\deg F\geq 2
, then we write
F=T_{1}\cdots T_{d}

with
T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{N})\cap NV(\mathcal{N})
by
Proposition
5.3
(i), and since

W_{k}(F)=W_{k}(T_{1})\cup\dots\cup W_{k}(T_{d})

and each
\#T_{i}<s
, the induction hypothesis yields

\#W_{k}(F)=\#W_{k}(T_{1})+\cdots+\#W_{k}(T_{d})\leq\frac{\lvert\lVert T_{1}\rVert\rvert}{k+1}+\cdots\frac{\lvert\lVert T_{d}\rVert\rvert}{k+1}=\frac{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}{k+1}.

It remains to study the case of an
\mathcal{N}
-forest of degree
1
,
i.e.
an

\mathcal{N}
-tree
F=T
.
We then have
T=n\triangleleft G\in NV(\mathcal{N})
with
\#G<s
.
If
\rho_{T}\not\in W_{k}(T)
, then
W_{k}(T)=W_{k}(G)
and
G\in NV(\mathcal{N})
by
Proposition
5.3
(ii). In this case,

\#W_{k}(T)=\#W_{k}(G)\leq\frac{\lvert\lVert G\rVert\rvert}{k+1}<\frac{\lvert\lVert G\rVert\rvert+\lvert n\rvert}{k+1}=\frac{\lvert\lVert T\rVert\rvert}{k+1}.

For the case
W_{k}(T)=\{\rho_{T}\}\cup W_{k}(G)
we proceed as
follows.
Consider the admissible cut

c_{k}=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}\}\in adm(G)\subset adm(T)

consisting of the minimal elements of
W_{k}(G)
(for the partial order
induced by
\preceq_{G}
).
It is clear that
W_{k}(G)\subset V_{P^{c_{k}}(T)}
and,
since

, the induction hypothesis yields

\#W_{k}(G)=\#W_{k}(P^{c_{k}}(T))\leq\frac{\lvert\lVert P^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert\rvert}{k+1}.

The inequality to be proved is equivalent to

1+\#W_{k}(P^{c_{k}}(T))\leq\frac{\lvert\lVert P^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert\rvert+\lvert\lVert R^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert\rvert}{k+1}.

It is thus sufficient to check that

k+1\leq\lvert\lVert R^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert\rvert.

(6.5)

There are two cases:

•

If the cut

has at least

(iii) ensures that

\lvert\lVert R^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert\rvert\geq\#c_{k}-\deg{T}\geq k+2-1=k+1

and we are done.

•

If the cut
c_{k}=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}\}
has

, we let

for

. As

and

, we have

so that, using (

) and the identity

\displaystyle d(\lambda\cdot\lVert R^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\leq d(\lambda\cdot\lVert T\rVert,\mathbb{Z})+d(\lambda\cdot\lVert U_{1}\rVert,\mathbb{Z})+\cdots+d(\lambda\cdot\lVert U_{r}\rVert,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle<\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{k+2}\Omega(k)\leq\Omega(k)

whence, since

\lvert\lVert R^{c_{k}}(T)\rVert\rvert\geq k+1

∎

We are now ready to give estimates for the armoulds

6.2
The case of
\tilde{S}^{\bullet}(\lambda)

Theorem F
.

If a non-resonant vector field spectrum

satisfies the Bruno condition

\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log\frac{1}{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}<\infty,

with

(6.7)

We will make use of the easy

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
F
, the sequence

\left(\frac{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}{k+2}\right)_{k\geq 1}
is positive
and decreasing, and there exists a sequence
K_{n}\uparrow\infty
of
positive integers such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{n}}\tilde{L}_{K_{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
,
where

\tilde{L}_{k}\coloneqq\log\frac{k+2}{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}\quad\text{for each}\kern 5.0ptk\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

(6.8)

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

The first claim follows from the fact that the sequence
(\Omega_{v.f.}(k))_{k\geq 1}
is
positive and monotonic non-increasing.
The second claim amounts to
\liminf\frac{1}{k}\tilde{L}_{k}=0
,
a fact that follows from the Bruno condition:

\big(\frac{1}{k}\tilde{L}_{k}\big)_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence of positive
numbers that cannot be bounded from below by any
\Lambda>0
because
that would imply
\sum\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\tilde{L}_{k}=\infty
,
whereas the latter series is of same nature as the Bruno series,
since

\tilde{L}_{k}=\log(k+2)+\log\frac{1}{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}

(6.9)

and
\sum\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\log(k+2)<\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
F
.

Given a non-empty
\mathcal{N}
-forest

, the first claim of
Lemma
6.2
entails

V_{F}=\tilde{W}_{0}(F)\supset\tilde{W}_{1}(F)\supset\cdots\supset\tilde{W}_{k}(F)\supset\cdots

and (
6.3
) implies that
\tilde{W}_{k}(F)=\emptyset
for
k\geq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert
.
We use this in the form of a disjoint union
V_{F}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{W}_{k}(F)\setminus\tilde{W}_{k+1}(F)

(in which only finitely many sets are non-empty): (
3.29
) implies

\log\lvert\tilde{S}^{F}(\lambda)\rvert=\sum_{{\sigma}\in V_{F}}\log\frac{1}{\lvert\lambda\cdot\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert\rvert}\\
=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\,\sum_{\sigma\in\tilde{W}_{k}(F)\setminus\tilde{W}_{k+1}(F)}\log\frac{1}{\lvert\lambda\cdot\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert\rvert}.

(6.10)

With the notation
\tilde{M}_{k}(F)\coloneqq\#\tilde{W}_{k}(F)
for any
k\geq 0
,
there are exactly
\tilde{M}_{k}(F)-\tilde{M}_{k+1}(F)
vertices in

\tilde{W}_{k}(F)\setminus\tilde{W}_{k+1}(F)
and, for any such
vertex
{\sigma}
,

\frac{\Omega_{v.f.}(k+1)}{k+3}\leq|\lambda\cdot\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert|<\frac{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}{k+2}.

(6.11)

With the notation (
6.8
), this implies that

\sigma\in\tilde{W}_{k}(F)\setminus\tilde{W}_{k+1}(F)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log\frac{1}{\lvert\lambda\cdot\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert\rvert}\leq\tilde{L}_{k+1},

(6.12)

thus

\log\lvert\tilde{S}^{F}(\lambda)\rvert\leq\tilde{B}_{F}(\lambda)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(\tilde{M}_{k}(F)-\tilde{M}_{k+1}(F))\tilde{L}_{k+1}.

(6.13)

Since
\tilde{M}_{k}(F)=0
for all
k\geq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert
,
the Abel transform of the series
\tilde{B}_{F}(\lambda)
gives

\tilde{B}_{F}(\lambda)=\tilde{M}_{0}(F)\tilde{L}_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\tilde{M}_{k}(F)(\tilde{L}_{k+1}-\tilde{L}_{k})\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptK\geq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert.

By Lemma
6.2
,
\tilde{L}_{k+1}-\tilde{L}_{k}\geq 0
for
k\geq 1
, thus
Lemma
6.1
yields

\tilde{B}_{F}(\lambda)\leq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert\tilde{L}_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\frac{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}{k+1}(\tilde{L}_{k+1}-\tilde{L}_{k})\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptK\geq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert.

Using once again the Abel transform, we get

\log\lvert\tilde{S}^{F}(\lambda)\rvert\leq\tilde{B}_{F}(\lambda)\leq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert\bigg(\frac{1}{K}\tilde{L}_{K}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\bigg(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg)\tilde{L}_{k}\bigg),

(6.14)

where we choose
K
in the subsequence
(K_{n})
given by Lemma
6.2
:
in view of (
6.9
), letting
n
tend to
\infty
, we get

\log\lvert\tilde{S}^{F}(\lambda)\rvert\leq\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert\,\underbrace{\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log(k+2)}_{\gamma}\,+\,\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert\mathcal{B}_{v.f.}(\lambda),

whence (
6.6
) follows.
∎

6.3
The case of
S^{\bullet}(q)

The proof is almost the same, except that we need the following lemma:

Lemma 6.3
.

Given
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

if
\lvert\Im z\rvert>\frac{1}{2}
, then
\lvert e^{2i\pi z}-1\rvert>\frac{1}{3}
,

•

if
\lvert\Im z\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then
\lvert e^{2i\pi z}-1\rvert\geq d(z,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

If
\Im z>\frac{1}{2}
, then
\lvert e^{2i\pi z}\rvert=e^{-2\pi\Im z}<e^{-\pi}<1
so that

\lvert e^{2i\pi z}-1\rvert>1-e^{-\pi}>\frac{1}{3}.

In the same way, if
\Im z<-\frac{1}{2}
, then
\lvert e^{2i\pi z}\rvert=e^{-2\pi\Im z}>e^{\pi}>1
so that

\lvert e^{2i\pi z}-1\rvert>e^{\pi}-1>\frac{1}{3}.

It remains to prove the second part of the lemma. Let us define the square

C=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\ \lvert\Im z\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }\lvert\Re z\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\}.

For
z
in
C
,
d(z,\mathbb{Z})=\lvert z\rvert
and, since
z\mapsto e^{2i\pi z}-1

is
1
-periodic, the inequality is equivalent to

\forall z\in C,\quad\lvert e^{2i\pi z}-1\rvert\geq\lvert z\rvert.

The meromorphic map
g:z\mapsto\frac{z}{e^{2i\pi z}-1}
is holomorphic in the interior of
C
and continuous on
C
so that we can apply the maximum modulus principle:

\forall z\in C,\quad\lvert g(z)\rvert\leq\max_{w\in\partial C}\lvert g(w)\rvert.

Let us take a look at the four sides of the square
C
:

•

If
w=\pm\frac{1}{2}+iy
with
-\frac{1}{2}\leq y\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

\lvert g(w)\rvert=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+y^{2}}}{\lvert-e^{-2\pi y}-1\rvert}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{e^{-2\pi y}+1}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{e^{-2\pi}+1}\leq 1.

•

If
w=x+{\varepsilon}i\frac{1}{2}
with
-\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
{\varepsilon}=\pm 1
,

\lvert g(w)\rvert^{2}=\frac{x^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}{\lvert e^{2i\pi x}e^{-{\varepsilon}\pi}-1\rvert^{2}}=\frac{x^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}{e^{-2{\varepsilon}\pi}-2e^{-{\varepsilon}\pi}\cos(2\pi x)+1}\leq\\[3.44444pt]
\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{e^{-2{\varepsilon}\pi}-2e^{-{\varepsilon}\pi}+1}=\frac{1}{2(e^{-{\varepsilon}\pi}-1)^{2}}

and the reader can check that for
{\varepsilon}=\pm 1
,

\frac{1}{2(e^{-{\varepsilon}\pi}-1)^{2}}\leq 1.

On the square
C
we have
\lvert g(z)\rvert\leq 1
and this ends the proof.
∎

We can now state and prove

Theorem G
.

If a non-resonant diffeomorphism spectrum

q=(e^{2i\pi\lambda_{1}},\ldots,e^{2i\pi\lambda_{\nu}})
satisfies the Bruno condition

\mathcal{B}(q)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\log\frac{1}{\Omega(k)}<\infty,

then we have

\lvert{S}^{F}(q)\rvert\leq B^{\lvert\lVert F\rVert\rvert}\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptF\in NV(\mathcal{N})

(6.15)

with

B=e^{\gamma+\mathcal{B}(q)}\quad\text{(with $\gamma$ as in\penalty\ \eqref{eqdefgamma}).}

(6.16)

Proof.

We first observe that the sequence
(\Omega(k))_{k\geq 1}

is positive, monotonic non-increasing and bounded from above by
1
,
so that the sequence
\left(\frac{\Omega(k)}{k+2}\right)_{k\geq 1}

is positive, decreasing and bounded from above by
\frac{1}{3}
.

Following the lines of the proof of Theorem
F
,
we get, for any
F\in NV(\mathcal{N})
,

\displaystyle\log\lvert{S}^{F}(q)\rvert

\displaystyle=\log\left|\prod_{{\sigma}\in V_{F}}\frac{1}{q^{\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert}-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|\prod_{{\sigma}\in V_{F}}\frac{1}{e^{2i\pi\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma}}-1}\right|\qquad(\hat{\sigma}=\lVert\operatorname{Tree}(\sigma,F)\rVert)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\sigma}\in V_{F}}\log\frac{1}{\lvert e^{2i\pi\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma}}-1\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\,\sum_{\sigma\in{W}_{k}(F)\setminus{W}_{k+1}(F)}\log\frac{1}{\lvert e^{2i\pi\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma}}-1\rvert}.

With the notation
M_{k}(F)\coloneqq\#W_{k}(F)
for any
k\geq 0
, there are exactly

{M}_{k}(F)-{M}_{k+1}(F)
vertices in
{W}_{k}(F)\setminus W_{k+1}(F)

and, for any such vertex
{\sigma}
,

\frac{\Omega(k+1)}{k+3}\leq d(\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}).

(6.17)

We now need an analog of the crucial
inequality (
6.12
).
Thanks to Lemma
6.3
,

•

either
\lvert\Im(\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma})\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2}
and

\lvert e^{2i\pi\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma}}-1\rvert>\frac{1}{3}\geq\frac{\Omega(k+1)}{k+3},

•

or
\lvert\Im(\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma})\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
and

\lvert e^{2i\pi\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma}}-1\rvert\geq d(\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\geq\frac{\Omega(k+1)}{k+3}.

This means that, with the notation
{L}_{k}\coloneqq\log\frac{k+2}{\Omega_{v.f.}(k)}
, in all cases,

\sigma\in{W}_{k}(F)\setminus W_{k+1}(F)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log\frac{1}{\lvert e^{2i\pi\lambda\cdot\hat{\sigma}}-1\rvert}\leq\log\frac{k+3}{\Omega(k+1)}={L}_{k+1},

(6.18)

whence

\log\lvert{S}^{F}(q\rvert\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}({M}_{k}(F)-{M}_{k+1}(F)){L}_{k}.

(6.19)

The end of the proof is exactly the same as in Theorem
F
.
∎

Proposition A.1
.

With any map

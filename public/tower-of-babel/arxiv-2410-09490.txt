1.
Introduction

In (
[
4
]
), authors associated any pair
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})
of separable real Hilbert space
(
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})>1
) and strongly continuous orthogonal representation of
\mathbb{R}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
to a von Neumann algebra coming from the non-tracial representation of the
q_{i,j}
-commutation relation for
-1<q_{i,j}=q_{j,i}<1
. The concerned von Neumann algebras are named as
mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras
. In this article, we study Connes bicentralizer conjecture for this von Neumann.

Let
M
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal state
\varphi
.
Then recall that the asymptotic centralizer
AC_{\varphi}(M)
and the
bicentralizer
B_{\varphi}(M)
are defined as follows:

(1)

\displaystyle AC_{\varphi}(M):=\left\{(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)|\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\varphi-\varphi x_{n}\right\|_{M_{*}}=0\right\};

(2)

\displaystyle B_{\varphi}(M):=\left\{a\in M|ax_{n}-x_{n}a\rightarrow 0\ \text{ultrastrongly for all}\ (x_{n})_{n}\in AC_{\varphi}(M)\right\}.

Here, the notation
x\varphi y
for
x,y\in M
means
x\varphi y(a)=\varphi(xay).
The bicentralizer
B_{\varphi}(M)
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
, which is globally invariant under the modular group
(\sigma_{t}^{\varphi})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. It follows from Connes-Størmer transitivity theorem (see
[
13
]
) that if
B_{\varphi}(M)=\mathbb{C}1
then
B_{\psi}(M)=\mathbb{C}1
for all faithful normal state
\psi
on
M
, where
1
denotes the identity operator in
M
. Hence, the triviality of the bicentralizer does not depend on the choice of faithful normal states on
M
.

Connes classified all amenable factors which are not of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
in his famous paper
[
11
]
.
First he encountered the bicentralizer problem when he was attempting to establish the uniqueness of amenable type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factors.
Indeed, Connes proved that an amenable type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor
M
is isomorphic to the unique Araki-Woods factor of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
if and only if
B_{\varphi}(M)=\mathbb{C}1
for some faithful normal state
{\varphi}\in M_{*}
.

Furthermore, he conjectured that
B_{\varphi}(M)=\mathbb{C}1
for every type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor
M
and every faithful normal state
{\varphi}\in M_{*}
. This conjecture, known as Connes’ “bicentralizer problem” and thereafter,
Haagerup is renowned for solving this problem when
M
is amenable type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factors (see
[
14
]
).
Thus, Haagerup’s work together with Connes’ work, settled the classification problem for amenable factors of all types. However, when
M
is not amenable, Connes’ Bicentralizer problem is still extensively open today.

But the conjecture has been supported by several concrete examples of such factors. For example, factors with almost periodic states, free Araki-Woods factors and semisolid factors (see
[
10
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
17
]
respectively).
In
[
18
, Main Theorem]
, the authors proved that
q
-Araki-Woods factors associated to orthogonal representations
(U_{t})
with weakly mixing component have trivial bicentralizer. So, the bicentralizer problem for a
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebra associated to an almost periodic orthogonal representation remains unsolved.

In this article, we study bicentralizer problem of mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Wood von Neumann algebra.
Since from the inception of the theory of von Neumann algebras, Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras and their generalizations are studied extensively for their rich structures. It begins with Voiculescu’s Free Gaussian functor. In free probability Voiculescu associated a
C^{*}
-algebra
\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
to real Hilbert space, generated by
s(\xi),\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}

where each
s(\xi)
is the sum of creation and annihilation operators on
the full Fock space of the complexification of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The associated von Neumann algebra
\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
is isomorphic to
L(\mathcal{F}_{\dim({\mathcal{H}_{R}})})
(the group von Neumann algebra associated to the free group of
\dim({\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}})
generators).
Further, it has the following deformation:

(1)

The q-Gaussian functor due to Bożejko and Speicher for
-1<q<1
(see
[
8
]
). The associated von Neumann algebras are called q-Gaussian von Neumann algebras.

(2)

The free CAR functor due to Shlyakhtenko (see
[
23
]
) and the associated von Neumann algebras are called free Araki-Woods factor.

(3)

q-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras which is combination of
(1)
and
(2)
, consructed by Hiai (see
[
15
]
).

The mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras are further generalization of q-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras. In recent years, there has been a lot of interest in these von Neumann algebras.
The reader may look at
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
26
]
, and the references therein for an overview of the literature.

The mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras are relatively new in the area.
So, far its factoriality, type classification, Haagerup property, non-injectivity properties are studied (see
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
).
In
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
factoriality of these algebras are studied under different conditions on
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.
Recently, in
[
19
]
, the factoriality is studied when the underlying Hilbert space is finite dimension. By combining the results of
[
3
]
and
[
19
]
, the type classification question can be answered in complete generality.

To study the bicentralizer, we use the ultraproduct description of bicentralizer as given in

[
17
, Proposition.3.3]
. Suppose
M
is a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state
\varphi
, then we write
M^{\varphi}
for the centralizer of
M
with respect to
\varphi
and
M^{\varphi}=\{x\in M:\varphi(xy)=\varphi(yx)\text{ for all }y\in M\}
. It is shown (see
[
17
, Proposition.3.3]
) that
B_{\varphi}(M)
can be realized as a subalgebra of the ultraproduct of the von Neumann algebra
M
, more precisely, if
\omega
is any free ultrafilter over
\mathbb{N}
, then it follows that

(3)

\displaystyle B_{\phi}(M)=((M^{\omega})^{\varphi^{\omega}})^{\prime}\cap M\subseteq M^{\omega}.

Then using this, we directly show that mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebra associated with
(U_{t})
having almost periodic component of dimension
\geq 3
, have trivial bicentralizer.

For the other case, we follow similar path as followed by the author in
[
18
]
.
But we like to point out that due to recent development of factoriality of these von Neumann algebras, our argument is much simpler than
[
18
]
. In fact finding a sequence of unitary converging to
0
weakly in the centralizer of
M_{T}
makes the argument simple.
Now we state our main theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
-1<q_{ij}=q_{ji}<1
be real numbers such that
\sup_{i,j}\left|q_{ij}\right|<1
and let
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})
be a strongly continuous orthogonal representation such that the mixed
q
-Araki-Woods algebra
M_{T}
is a type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor, then it has trivial bicentralizer.

We remark that the mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebra
M_{T}
is of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
, whenever the group generated by the eigenvalues of the analytic generator
A
of
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is equal to
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{\times}
(see
[
19
]
and
[
3
]
). Thus,
M_{T}
associated to almost periodic orthogonal representation could be type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
. We note that if
q_{i,j}=q
for all
i,j
, then we obtain
q
-deformed Araki-Wood von Neumann algebras. Thus, our theorem also completely solves the bicentralizer problem for
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras.

We organize this article as follows: In section 2, we describe the construction of the mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras and recall some basics on ultraproduct of von Neumann algebras. In section 3, we discuss bicentralizer of mixed
q
-deformed algebra when associated orthogonal representation with non trivial almost periodic part and in the final section we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.2.
Ultraproducts of von Neumann algebras

We require some basics of ultraproduct of Hilbert spaces and von Neumann algebras for our analysis of bicentralizer. Therefore, in this subsection, we recall the notions of ultraproducts of von Neumann algebras from
[
1
]
. Originally the ultraproducts of von Neumann algebras was defined for tracial von Neumann algebras. In the case of type III von Neumann algebras, there are two notions of ultraproducts. One is given by Ocneanu and other one by Groh- Raynaud. Ocneanu’s ultraproduct is a generalisation of ultraproducts of tracial von Neumann algebras. In this article use the
Ocneanu’s ultraproducts of von Neumann algebras.

Before proceeding to the ultraproduct of von Neumann algebras, we briefly recall the ultraproduct of Banach spaces. Fix a free ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
. Recall that,
\omega\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
, where
\beta\mathbb{N}
is the Stone-Cech compactification of
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},E_{n})
be the set of all sequences
(x_{n})_{n}\in\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}
with
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|x_{n}\right\|<\infty
. Clearly,
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},E_{n})
becomes a Banach space with the norm given by
\left\|(x_{n})_{n}\right\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|x_{n}\right\|
, for
(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},E_{n})
.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
denote the closed subspace of all
(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},E_{n})
which satisfies

\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|x_{n}\right\|=0.
Then the Banach space ultraproduct
(E_{n})_{\omega}
is defined as the quotient space
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},E_{n})/I_{\omega}
. Any element of
(E_{n})_{\omega}
represented by
(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},E_{n})
is written as
(x_{n})_{\omega}
. If
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of Hilbert spaces, then the Banach space ultraproduct
H_{\omega}:=(H_{n})_{\omega}
is again a Hilbert space with the inner product defined by
\left\langle(\xi_{n})_{\omega},(\eta_{n})_{\omega}\right\rangle:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\langle\xi_{n},\eta_{n}\right\rangle
, for
(\xi_{n})_{\omega},(\eta_{n})_{\omega}\in(H_{n})_{\omega}
.

We first introduce the Ocneanu ultraproduct of a family of von Neumann algebras with normal faithful states
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
along the free ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
. Consider the spaces

\displaystyle\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M_{n}):=\left\{(x_{n})_{n}\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}:\ \sup_{n}\left\|x_{n}\right\|<\infty\right\},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}:=\left\{(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M_{n}):\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{\sharp}=0\right\},

where
\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{\sharp}=\varphi_{n}(x_{n}^{*}x_{n}+x_{n}x_{n}^{*})^{1/2}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\omega}(M_{n},\varphi_{n}):=\left\{(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M_{n}):\ (x_{n})_{n}\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\omega},\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{I}_{\omega}(x_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\right\}.

Note that,
\mathcal{M}^{\omega}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
is
C^{*}
-algebra in which
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
is a closed (two-sided) ideal. Hence, we can consider the quotient
C^{*}
-algbera

\displaystyle(M_{n},\varphi_{n})^{\omega}:=\mathcal{M}^{\omega}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})/\mathcal{I}_{\omega}.

By the same proof as in
[
21
, §5.1]
,
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})^{\omega}
turns out be a von Neumann algebra, and called the Ocneanu ultraproduct of the sequence
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. If all the
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
are
(M,\varphi)
( a von Neumann
M
with faithful normal state
\phi
), then we write

M^{\omega}:=\mathcal{M}^{\omega}(M,\varphi)/\mathcal{I}_{\omega}.

The equivalence class of a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\omega}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
in the quotient algebra is denoted by
(x_{n})^{\omega}
. The following defines a normal faithful state
(\varphi_{n})^{\omega}
on
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})^{\omega}
:

\displaystyle(\varphi_{n})^{\omega}((x_{n})^{\omega}):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\varphi_{n}(x_{n}),\quad\text{for}\quad(x_{n})^{\omega}\in(M_{n},\varphi_{n})^{\omega}.

For the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct, suppose

(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
is a sequence of von Neumann algebras equipped with normal faithful states. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can realize
M_{n}\subset B(H_{n})
, using GNS representation of
M_{n}
with respect to
\varphi_{n}
. Let
(M_{n})_{\omega}
be the Banach space ultraproduct of
(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Recall that,
(M_{n})_{\omega}
is
C^{*}
-algebra with respect to the norm
\left\|(x_{n})_{\omega}\right\|=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|x_{n}\right\|
. Also, let
H_{\omega}
be the Banach space ultraproduct of the sequence
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Consider the diagonal action
\pi_{\omega}:(M_{n})_{\omega}\rightarrow B(H_{\omega})
defined by

(15)

\pi_{\omega}((a_{n})_{\omega})(\xi_{n})_{\omega}:=(a_{n}\xi_{n})_{\omega}.

It can be easily checked that, this action is a well-defined
*
-homomorphism and

\left\|\pi_{\omega}((a_{n})_{\omega})(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\right\|=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|a_{n}\right\|=\left\|(a_{n})_{\omega}\right\|.

Hence,
\pi_{\omega}
is an injective
*
-homomorphism. The Groh- Raynaud ultraproduct is defined as the weak closure of
\pi_{\omega}((M_{n})_{\omega})
inside
B(H_{\omega})
and denoted by
\prod^{\omega}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
. The ultraproduct state on the Groh- Raynaud ultraproduct, denoted by
\varphi_{\omega}
, is a vector state given by the ultraproduct of the cyclic vectors for the GNS representations of algberas
M_{n}
, i.e.

(16)

\varphi_{\omega}(x):=\left\langle\xi_{\omega},x\xi_{\omega}\right\rangle,\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\prod^{\omega}(M_{n},\varphi_{n}),

where
\xi_{\omega}:=(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\in(\mathcal{H}_{n})_{\omega}
.

Definition 4.1

Write
\mathfrak{E}(\mathcal{H},U,\omega)
for the subspace of all

-equicontinuous bounded sequences of

\mathfrak{E}(\mathcal{H},U,\omega)
is a closed subspace of

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
be the ultraproducts Hilbert space of

(see Section

\mathcal{H}_{\omega}=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H})/\mathcal{I}_{\omega},

where

\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=\left\{(\xi_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H})\ |\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}\right\|_{U}=0\right\}.

Observe that
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{E}(\mathcal{H},U,\omega)
.

Now consider the unitary map
(U_{\omega})_{t}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
, defined by
(U_{\omega})_{t}(\xi_{n})_{\omega}=(U_{t}\xi_{n})_{\omega}
for
(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
and for all

. However, note that the map

\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
. But as
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{E}(\mathcal{H},U,\omega)
, so,

\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}:=\mathfrak{E}(\mathcal{H},U,\omega)/\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\omega}

is strongly continuous on
\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Then

\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}\simeq(\mathcal{H}_{1})_{U,\omega}\oplus(\mathcal{H}_{2})_{U,\omega}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=\left\{(\xi_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H})\ |\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}\right\|_{U}=0\right\},

and each
\xi_{n}=\xi_{n}^{1}+\xi_{n}^{2}
for
\xi_{n}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\xi_{n}^{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}\right\|_{U}=0\implies\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}^{1}\right\|_{U}+\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}^{2}\right\|_{U}=0
. Hence,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}^{1}\right\|_{U}=0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}^{2}\right\|_{U}=0
. We denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{1}_{\omega}=\left\{(\xi_{n}^{1})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H}_{1})\ |\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}^{1}\right\|_{U}=0\right\}

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \mathcal{I}^{2}_{\omega}=\left\{(\xi_{n}^{2})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H}_{2})\ |\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\|\xi_{n}^{2}\right\|_{U}=0\right\}.

This implies
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=\mathcal{I}^{1}_{\omega}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{2}_{\omega}
. Therefore,
we have
\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}\simeq(\mathcal{H}_{1})_{U,\omega}\oplus(\mathcal{H}_{2})_{U,\omega}
.

∎

Now we note that
\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}
be a large space, indeed for any sequence
(n_{k})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, define
\xi_{k}=U_{n_{k}}\xi
, then it easy to check that
(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}
. Now we will write the following two results from
[
18
, Lemma 3.5(1) and Proposition 3.3]
and for the completeness we include their proofs. It will be used in the sequel.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})
is weakly mixing, then there is a separable infinite dimensional closed subspace
L_{\mathbb{R}}\subset(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
such that the restriction
(L_{\mathbb{R}},(U_{\omega})_{t}|_{L_{\mathbb{R}}})
is almost periodic.

Proof.

Since
U
is weakly mixing, there is an infinite subset
\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\sigma(A)\cap(1,\infty)
such that
\sup_{n}\lambda_{n}<\infty.
It follows from
[
18
, Lemma 3.5(1)]
that each
\lambda_{n}
is in the point spectrum of
A_{\omega}
. Therefore, for each
\lambda_{n}
, there exists a unit vector
\xi^{(n)}=(\xi^{(n)}_{m})_{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}
such that
(U_{\omega})_{t}\xi^{(n)}=\lambda^{it}\xi^{(n)}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows that
\xi_{m}^{(n)}\rightarrow 0
weakly as
m\rightarrow\omega
and so
\xi^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, for each
\lambda_{n}
, fix a unit eigenvector
\xi^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}
. Consider the two dimensional subspace
L^{n}
of
\mathcal{H}_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}
generated by the eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{n}
and
\lambda_{n}^{-1}
. Denote the real part of the subspace
L^{n}
by
L^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Clearly
L^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Now, consider the following closed subspace

L_{\mathbb{R}}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}.

It follows that
U_{\omega}|_{L_{\mathbb{R}}}
is almost periodic and
\dim(L_{\mathbb{R}})=\infty
.

∎

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)
, it is said to be
(\sigma^{\varphi},\omega)
-equicontinuous if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \sup_{\left|t\right|\leqslant\delta}\left\|\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x_{n})-x_{n}\right\|^{\sharp}_{\varphi}<\epsilon\right\}\in\omega
, where
\left\|x\right\|^{\sharp}_{\varphi}=\varphi(xx^{*}+x^{*}x)^{1/2}
. The norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\varphi}^{\sharp}
induces strong* topology on uniformly bounded sets.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)
(\xi_{n})_{n}
is
(U,\omega)
-equicontinuous.

(2)
(s(\xi_{n}))_{n}
is
(\sigma^{\varphi},\omega)
-equicontinuous.

(3)
(s(\xi_{n}))_{n}\in M_{T}^{\omega}.

For any
(U,\omega)
-equicontinuous vector
(\xi_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H}_{R})
,
\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}\xi_{n}\right)_{n}
is also
(U,\omega)
-equicontinuous.

Proof.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|U_{t}\xi-\xi\right\|_{U}=\left\|s(U_{t}\xi)-s(\xi)\right\|_{\varphi}=\left\|\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi}(s(\xi))-s(\xi)\right\|_{\varphi}

It is known that on uniformly bounded sets, the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\varphi}
induces the strong operator topology. Since
s(\xi)
is self-adjoint for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have the equivalence
(1)\iff(2)
. The equivalence
(2)\iff(3)
follows from
[
20
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{H})
be a
(U,\omega)
-equicontinuous. Notice that

\displaystyle\left\|U_{t}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}\xi_{n}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}\xi_{n}\right\|_{U}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}U_{t}\xi_{n}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}\xi_{n}\right\|_{U}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}\right\|\left\|U_{t}\xi_{n}-\xi_{n}\right\|_{U}.

This implies
\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1+A^{-1}}}\xi_{n}\right)_{n}
is
(U,\omega)
-equicontinuous.

∎

Consider the mixed
q
-Araki-Woods von Neumann algebra
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}
for the same
T
that defines the mixed
q
-Araki-Woods algebra
M_{T}
. Observe that
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}
has a faithful unital representation on
\mathcal{F}_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega})
with
\Omega
as a cyclic and separating vector. We also note that
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}
is a factor (see
[
7
]
). The vacuum state on
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}
will be given by
\phi_{\omega}(\cdot)=\left\langle\Omega,(\cdot)\Omega\right\rangle_{U,\omega}
.

Denote the
*
-subalgebra generated by Wick products in
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}
by
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})
.

Theorem 4.6
.

The map
\iota:\widetilde{\Gamma}_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})\rightarrow M_{T}^{\omega}
defined by
\iota(s((\xi_{n})_{\omega}))=(s(\xi_{n}))_{\omega}
, extends to a state preserving unital
*
-embedding of
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})
into
M_{T}^{\omega}
.

Proof.

First we note that the vacuum state
\phi_{\omega}
is a faithful normal state on the
*
-algebra
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})
. Further, the ultraproduct state
\phi^{\omega}
on
M_{T}^{\omega}
is also a faithful normal state. As
\Gamma_{T}((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}
is a factor, so, in view of
[
2
, Remark 2.2]
, to establish the theorem
it is enough to check the following.

(i)

(s(\xi_{n}))_{\omega}\in M_{T}^{\omega}
for all
(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\in(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega}
.

(ii)

The map
\iota
is state preserving on generating set, i.e.

\varphi^{\omega}((s(\xi_{n}))_{\omega})=\phi_{\omega}(s((\xi_{n})_{\omega})),\text{ for all }(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\in(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega}.

Indeed, we note that
(i)
follows from Proposition
4.5
.

For
(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{\omega}(\iota(s((\xi_{n})_{\omega})))=\varphi^{\omega}((s(\xi_{n}))_{\omega})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\varphi(s(\xi_{n}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\langle\Omega,s(\xi_{n})\Omega\right\rangle_{U}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\omega}\left\langle\Omega,\xi_{n}\right\rangle_{U}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Omega,(\xi_{n})_{\omega}\right\rangle_{U,\omega}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Omega,s((\xi_{n})_{\omega})\Omega\right\rangle_{U,\omega}=\phi_{\omega}(s((\xi_{n})_{\omega})).

Thus, it completes the proof.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

We note that if
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{wm}\neq 0
, then similar argument as
[
6
, Theorem 8.1]
can be used to show that
M_{T}
is always type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume
M_{T}
is type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{wm}=0
, then
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})>3.

Proof.

If possible let
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=2\text{ or }3
and
M_{T}
is type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor. Then
(U_{t})\neq 1
, otherwise it will be type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor (see
[
19
, Theorem 4.5]
). Therefore,
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})
will have the following form (see
[
23
]
):

(1)

when
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=2
, then

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad U_{t}=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos(t\log\lambda)&-\sin(t\log\lambda)\\
\sin(t\log\lambda)&\cos(t\log\lambda)\end{matrix}\right),\text{ for some }\lambda>1\text{ and }

(2)

when
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})=3
, then

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\text{ and }\quad U_{t}={id}_{t}\oplus\left(\begin{matrix}\cos(t\log\lambda)&-\sin(t\log\lambda)\\
\sin(t\log\lambda)&\cos(t\log\lambda)\end{matrix}\right),\text{ for some }\lambda>1,

where
(\text{id}_{t})
is the identity representation.

Therefore, Connes
S
-invariant (see
[
12
]
for the definition of Connes
S
-invariant) will be
S(M_{T})=\{\lambda^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{0\}
(see
[
19
, Theorem 4.5]
) and consequently,
M_{T}
will be type
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}
factor for
0<\lambda<1
.
Thus,
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})\neq 2\text{ or }3
. Conversely, we have
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})>3
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
-1<q_{ij}=q_{ji}<1
be real numbers such that
\sup_{i,j}\left|q_{ij}\right|<1
and let
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{t})
be a strongly continuous orthogonal representation such that the mixed
q
-Araki-Woods algebra
M_{T}
is a type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor, then it has trivial bicentralizer.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{ap}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}
is the unique decomposition of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
into almost periodic and weak mixing part with respect to the orthogonal representation
(U_{t})
.

First assume that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}=0
.
Then note that if
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}=0
and
M_{T}
is type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor, by Lemma
4.8
, we have
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{ap}})>3
. Consequently, by Theorem
3.5
, it follows that

B_{\varphi}(M_{T})=\mathbb{C}1.

Now assume
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}\neq 0
. Then by Remark
4.7
, it follows that
M_{T}
is type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor. To show that it has trivial bicentralizer, we consider the real Hilbert space

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{ap}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}\oplus((\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}})_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}),

which is a subspace of
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})_{U,\omega}
.
Now suppose

\widetilde{M_{T}}=\Gamma_{T}(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{\omega})^{\prime\prime}.

Using Theorem
4.6
and Remark
3.3
, we have the inclusion of von Neumann algebras
\widetilde{M_{T}}\subseteq M_{T}^{\omega}
with conditional expectation. Since
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}\neq 0
, it follows from Theorem
4.3
that there exists a closed subspace
\widetilde{D}_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq L_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}})_{U,\omega}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{wm}}
such
that
{\widetilde{D}_{\mathbb{R}}}
is
U_{\omega}
-invariant.
Then, by Theorem
4.6
, we have

M_{T}(\widetilde{D}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{\omega})\subseteq\widetilde{M_{T}}\subseteq M_{T}^{\omega}

with conditional expectation (see Remark
3.3
).
Therefore, we have the following

\displaystyle B_{\varphi}(M_{T})

\displaystyle={\big((M_{T}^{\omega})^{\varphi^{\omega}}\big)}^{\prime}\cap M_{T},~(\text{see Eq. }\ref{rela})

\displaystyle\subseteq\big(M_{T}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{\omega})^{\varphi_{\omega}}\big)^{\prime}\cap\widetilde{M_{T}}

\displaystyle\subseteq M_{T}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{\omega})\text{ by Lemma }\ref{commu}.

Since by Theorem
4.3
,
L_{\mathbb{R}}
is infinite dimensional and
U_{\omega}|_{L_{\mathbb{R}}}
is almost periodic, so, we can find two orthogonal subspaces

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{1}}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{2}}_{\mathbb{R}}
of
L_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

B_{\varphi}(M_{T})\subseteq M_{T}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{1}}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{\omega})\cap M_{T}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{2}}_{\mathbb{R}},U_{\omega})=\mathbb{C}1.

This completes the proof.

∎

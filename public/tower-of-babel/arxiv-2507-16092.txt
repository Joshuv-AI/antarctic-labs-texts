Moment stability and large deviations for random dynamical systems on non-compact manifolds
Peter H. Baxendale

Abstract
Let { v t : t ≥ 0 } \{v_{t}:t\geq 0\} , with values in T ​ M TM , denote the linearization along trajectories of a stochastic differential equation on a Riemannian manifold M M , and let λ t = t − 1 ​ log ⁡ ‖ v t ‖ \lambda_{t}=t^{-1}\log\|v_{t}\| denote the associated finite time Lyapunov exponent. The rate function for large deviations of λ t \lambda_{t} from its almost sure limit λ \lambda is related via the Gärtner-Ellis theorem to the moment Lyapunov exponent Λ ⁡ ( p ) = lim t → ∞ t − 1 ​ log ⁡ 𝔼 ⁡ [ ‖ v t ‖ p ] \Lambda(p)=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}[\|v_{t}\|^{p}] . When M M is compact there is a characterization of Λ ⁡ ( p ) \Lambda(p) in terms of an eigenvalue problem for an associated differential operator acting on functions on the unit sphere bundle S ​ M SM . We formulate growth conditions which, together with standard assumptions of hypoellipticity and positivity, ensure that the eigenvalue characterization of Λ ⁡ ( p ) \Lambda(p) remains valid on a suitable function space. There is a central limit theorem for λ t \lambda_{t} , and there are moderate deviation estimates for λ t \lambda_{t} , both involving the second derivative Λ ′′ ​ ( 0 ) \Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0) . Examples are given showing the various different growth rates for Λ ⁡ ( p ) \Lambda(p) as p → ∞ p\to\infty which may happen when M M is not compact.



Keywords: random dynamical system, Lyapunov exponent, moment stability, large deviations.

Introduction

Consider the stochastic differential equation (SDE) for
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on a
d
-dimensional
C^{\infty}
manifold
M

dx_{t}=X_{0}(x_{t})dt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}(x_{t})\circ dW_{t}^{j},

(1)

where the vector fields
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
are all
C^{\infty}
and the
\{W_{t}^{j}:t\geq 0\}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, are independent standard Brownian motions, and write

L=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}+X_{0}.

(2)

Consider also the real valued additive functional

\displaystyle A_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}q_{0}(x_{s})ds+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}q_{j}(x_{s})\circ dW_{s}^{j}=\int_{0}^{t}Q(x_{s})ds+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}q_{j}(x_{s})dW_{s}^{j}

(3)

where
q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
are all in
C^{\infty}(M)
and
Q(x)=q_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}q_{j}(x)
.
Our interest is in the behavior of
A_{t}
as
t\to\infty
. In particular we study the logarithmic moment generating function

\Lambda(p)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{pA_{t}}\right],\quad p\in\mathbb{R},

(4)

and especially its characterization in terms of an eigenvalue problem on a suitable function space.

This problem arises when studying the stability properties of the solution
\{v_{t}:t\geq 0\}
of the linear SDE in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

dv_{t}=B_{0}v_{t}dt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_{j}v_{t}\circ dW_{t}^{j},\quad v_{0}\neq 0.

(5)

Following Khas’minskii
[
29
]
, see also
[
30
, Section 6.7]
, write
\theta_{t}=v_{t}/\|v_{t}\|
. Then
\{\theta_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is a diffusion process in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
given by

d\theta_{t}=\bigl(B_{0}\theta_{t}-\langle B_{0}\theta_{t},\theta_{t}\rangle\theta_{t}\bigr)dt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(B_{j}\theta_{t}-\langle B_{j}\theta_{t},\theta_{t}\rangle\theta_{t}\bigr)\circ dW_{t}^{j},

(6)

and

\log\|v_{t}\|-\log\|v_{0}\|=\int_{0}^{t}\langle B_{0}\theta_{s},\theta_{s}\rangle ds+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\langle B_{j}\theta_{s},\theta_{s}\rangle\circ dW_{s}^{j}\equiv A_{t}.

(7)

With
\{\theta_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
given by (
6
) and the addititive functional
\{A_{t}:t\geq 0\}
given by (
7
), the function
\Lambda(p)
can now be recognized as the
p
th moment Lyapunov exponent

\Lambda(p)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\left[e^{p\log\|v_{t}\|}\right]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\left[\|v_{t}\|^{p}\right].

A similar situation arises with the random linear ODE in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

dv_{t}=B(x_{t})v_{t}dt

(8)

where
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is the diffusion on
M
given by (
1
) and
B
is a mapping from
M
into the space of
n\times n
real matrices. In this case, writing
\theta_{t}=v_{t}/\|v_{t}\|\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
gives

d\theta_{t}=\bigl(B(x_{t})\theta_{t}-\langle B(x_{t})\theta_{t},\theta_{t}\rangle\theta_{t}\bigr)dt,

(9)

and

\log\|v_{t}\|-\log\|v_{0}\|=\int_{0}^{t}\langle B(x_{s})\theta_{s},\theta_{s}\rangle ds\equiv A_{t}.

(10)

The pair of equations (
1
,
9
) gives an SDE for the diffusion
\{(x_{t},\theta_{t}):t\geq 0\}
on
M\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and then (
10
) gives the functional
\{A_{t}:t\geq 0\}
with
Q(x,\theta)=\langle B(x)\theta,\theta\rangle:M\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
.

The relationship between almost sure and moment stability for the random linear system (
1
,
8
) in the case of compact
M
is discussed using the function
\Lambda(p)
by Molchanov
[
37
]
and Arnold
[
3
]
. See also Arnold, Kliemann and Oeljeklaus
[
1
]
. Results for the linear SDE (
5
) are given in Arnold, Oeljeklaus and Pardoux
[
2
]
. For connections with the large deviation behavior of
\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{t}\|
as
t\to\infty
and an associated central limit theorem see Stroock
[
41
]
, Baxendale
[
7
]
, and Arnold and Kliemann
[
5
]
.

More generally, given the (possibly nonlinear) SDE (
1
) it is natural to consider the dependence of the solution
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
upon its initial value
x_{0}
. The effect of an infinitesimal change in the initial condition can be seen in the behavior of the linearized system
\{v_{t}:t\geq 0\}
with values in the tangent bundle
TM
given by

dv_{t}=TX_{0}(v_{t})dt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}TX_{j}(v_{t})\circ dW_{t}^{j},\quad v_{0}\neq 0

(11)

where
TX_{j}
denotes the derivative of
X_{j}
, thought of as a map
TM\to T(TM)
. Assuming the manifold
M
has a Riemannian structure, let
\|v\|
denote the Riemannian norm of the tangent vector
v\in TM
and let
SM=\{v\in TM:\|v\|=1\}
denote the unit sphere bundle.
Because
TX_{j}
is linear on each tangent space
T_{x}M
the process
\{\theta_{t}=v_{t}/\|v_{t}\|:t\geq 0\}
is a diffusion in
SM
. From (
11
) we obtain

d\theta_{t}=\widetilde{X}_{0}(\theta_{t})dt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{X}_{j}(\theta_{t})\circ dW_{t}^{j},\quad\theta_{0}=v_{0}/\|v_{0}\|

(12)

where
\widetilde{X}_{j}(\theta)
is the orthogonal projection of
TX_{j}(\theta)
onto
T_{\theta}SM
. In terms of the decomposition of
T_{v}TM

determined by the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
associated with the Riemannian structure on
M
, the horizontal and vertical components of
\widetilde{X}_{j}(\theta)
for
\theta\in S_{x}M
are
X_{j}(x)
and
\nabla X_{j}(x)\theta-\langle\nabla X_{j}(x)\theta,\theta\rangle\theta
respectively.
Also from (
11
) we have

\log\|v_{t}\|-\log\|v_{0}\|=\int_{0}^{t}q_{0}(\theta_{s})ds+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}q_{j}(\theta_{s})\circ dW_{s}^{j}\equiv A_{t},

(13)

where
q_{j}(\theta)=\langle\nabla X_{j}(x)\theta,\theta\rangle
for
\theta\in S_{x}M
. See
[
6
, page 189]
for details of this calculation.
The pairing of the diffusion
\{\theta_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on
SM
given by (
12
) together with the additive functional
\{A_{t}:t\geq 0\}
given by (
13
) again fits into the general setting of (
1
,
3
).

Results for the case when
M
is compact were obtained in Baxendale and Stroock
[
6
]
. They include the existence and characterization of
\Lambda(p)
in terms of an eigenvalue problem, and also large deviation estimates and a central limit theorem for the limiting behavior of
A_{t}/t
as
t\to\infty
. In the case where the top Lyapunov exponent is positive, the eigenfunctions associated with
\Lambda(p)
were used in
[
6
]
to obtain rigorous estimates on exponential moments of exit times for the 2-point motion

\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}

from a neighborhood of the diagonal in
M\times M
, leading to almost sure mixing results for the associated stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms of
M
, see Dolgopyat, Kaloshin & Koralov
[
18
]
.

The aim of the present paper is to obtain corresponding results for the case of a noncompact manifold
M
. It is enough to study the original system (
1
,
3
) on
M
, as any results proved in that setting will apply also when
M
is replaced by
M\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
or
SM
. In order to obtain results about
\Lambda(p)
, we first study the richer structure of the twisted semigroup (or Feynman-Kac semigroup) of operators
\{T_{t}^{p}:t\geq 0\}
acting on some space of functions
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
by the formula

T_{t}^{p}g(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[g(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}\right],\quad x\in M.

(14)

Information about the operators
T_{t}^{p}
is then used to evaluate and characterize

\Lambda(p)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log T_{t}^{p}\mathbf{1}(x).

In order for this program to succeed it is necessary to find a suitable space of functions for the
T_{t}^{p}
to operate on. When
M
is compact it is natural to consider the space of bounded measurable functions, or the space of bounded continuous functions. See for example
[
7
]
for the linear SDE (
5
). But for the case where
M
is non-compact we will need to impose certain growth conditions on the operator
L
and the functions
Q
and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
.

There is much related work, mostly in the case when all the
q_{j}
are zero, because of the relationship of
\Lambda(p)
via the Gärtner-Ellis theorem to the large deviation theory for
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q(x_{s})ds
and more generally to the large deviation theory for the empirical measure
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{x_{s}}ds
. Note that the large deviation behavior for the empirical measure requires the consideration of a large class of functions
Q
, including non-smooth functions, while we will restrict to a single given smooth function
Q
.

Especially relevant for our purposes are the papers by Liming Wu
[
43
]
,
[
44
]
, Kontoyiannis and Meyn
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
, Ferré and Stoltz
[
20
]
, and Ferré, Rousset and Stoltz
[
19
]
.
Following these papers, given a function
V:M\to[1,\infty)
, we introduce the real Banach space

B_{V}=\Bigl\{\mbox{measurable }g:M\to\mathbb{R}:\|g\|_{V}\equiv\sup\{|g(x)|/V(x):x\in M\}<\infty\Bigr\}.

(15)

Assuming
V
satisfies certain Lyapunov type conditions, see the growth assumption
(G)
in Section
2
, then
\{T_{t}^{p}:t\geq 0\}
acts as a semigroup of bounded operators on
B_{V}
. Under additional conditions that there is enough noise in the SDE (
1
), see the hypoellipticity and positivity assumptions
(H)
and
(P)
in Section
2
, we show that
\Lambda(p)
is well-defined and is characterized by the eigenvalue/eigenfunction equation

\Bigl(L+p\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}X_{j}+pQ+\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}^{2}\Bigr)g(x)=\Lambda(p)g(x)

(16)

for positive
g\in B_{V}
, see Corollary
3.1
. We note that this characterization may fail when the requirement that
g\in B_{V}
is removed, see Example
3.2
.

The assumptions
(H)
,
(P)
and
(G)
are given in Section
2
and the main results are given in Section
3
.
In addition to the characterization of
\Lambda(p)
there are results on the real analyticity of the mappings
p\to\Lambda(p)
and
p\to\phi_{p}\in B_{V}
, where
\phi_{p}
denotes the eigenfunction in (
16
).
Properties of
\Lambda(p)
and the corresponding eigenfunctions
\phi_{p}
for
p
near zero are used to describe the almost sure limit of
A_{t}/t
and the variance in a central limit theorem for
A_{t}/t
in terms of
\Lambda^{\prime}(0)
and
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)
.
Section
4
contains two examples where these theorems can apply to issues of moment stability for tangent vectors, and Section
5
contains examples showing how the growth of
\Lambda(p)
as
p\to\infty
when
M
is not compact can be very different from the behavior in the compact case.
Finally, Sections
6
,
7
and
8
contain the proofs for the main results in Section
3
, and Section
9
contains the calculations for the examples in Section
5
.

2
Assumptions

Throughout
M
is a
C^{\infty}
manifold of dimension
d
. The diffusion
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on
M
is given by the SDE (
1
)
and the additive functional
\{A_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is given by (
3
).
The vector fields
X_{0},\ldots,X_{m}
and the functions
Q,q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
are all assumed to be
C^{\infty}
.
The second order linear differential operator
L
is given by (
2
) and its action on
C^{2}
functions with compact support agrees with the action of the extended generator (see
[
36
]
) of the Markov process
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
.

We will make three assumptions about the system (
1
,
3
). The first two assumptions
(H)
and
(P)
will ensure that the noise in the SDE (
1
) is sufficient to cause smoothing and positivity respectively. They will be satisfied if
L
is elliptic. The third assumption involves a Lyapunov type function
V:M\to[1,\infty)
and will be used to control the behavior of
x_{t}
and
A_{t}
outside compact subsets of
M
.

Assumptions similar to
(H)
and
(P)
appear in
[
20
]
. Other papers replace
(H)
and
(P)
with assumptions of smoothness and positivity of the integral kernel of
T_{t}^{p}
, see
[
19
, Assumption 5]
.
In the case with
q_{j}\equiv 0
the growth condition
(G)
is very similar to
[
19
, Assumption 4]
.

2.1
Hypoellipticity

Let
{\cal L}={\cal L}(X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
be the Lie algebra generated by the vector fields
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
and then let
{\cal L}_{0}={\cal L}_{0}(X_{0};X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
be the ideal in
{\cal L}
generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
.

Assumption (H).
Assume
\mbox{span}\{U(x):U\in{\cal L}_{0}\}=T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
.

The assumption
(H)
is often referred to as the parabolic Hörmander condition, see for example Hairer
[
23
]
. It is equivalent to either of the statements that

\mbox{span}\left\{U(t,x):U\in{\cal L}\left(X_{0}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t},X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\right)\right\}=T_{(t,x)}(\mathbb{R}\times M)\quad\mbox{ for all }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M

or

\mbox{span}\left\{U(t,x):U\in{\cal L}\left(X_{0}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t},X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\right)\right\}=T_{(t,x)}(\mathbb{R}\times M)\quad\mbox{ for all }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M.

Then by Hörmander
[
25
]
the assumption
(H)
implies
L-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and
L+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
are hypoelliptic.

2.2
Positivity

Let
\mathbb{P}_{t}(x,A)=\mathbb{P}^{x}(x_{t}\in A)
denote the transition probability for the diffusion
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
.

Assumption (P).

\mathbb{P}_{t}(x,U)>0
for all
t>0
,
x\in M
and non-empty
U\subset M
.

This assumption can be verified using the following controllability condition. For
t>0
let
{\cal U}_{t}
denote the space of piecewise continuous paths
u:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and for
u\in{\cal U}_{t}
let
\{\xi(s,x;u):0\leq s\leq t\}
denote the controlled path

\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial s}(s,x;u)=X_{0}(\xi(s,x;u))+\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(s)X_{j}(\xi(s,x;u))

with
\xi(0,x;u)=x
.

Assumption (C).
The set
\{\xi(t,x;u):u\in{\cal U}_{t}\}
is dense in
M
for all
t>0
and
x\in M
.

The Stroock-Varadhan support theorem
[
42
]
shows that
(C)
implies
(P)
.

2.3
Growth

Define the vector field
Y(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}(x)X_{j}(x)
and the function
R(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(q_{j}(x)\bigr)^{2}
. For
p\in\mathbb{R}
define the second order linear differential operator
L_{p}
acting on functions
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
by

L_{p}g(x)=Lg(x)+pYg(x)+pQ(x)g(x)+\frac{p^{2}}{2}R(x)g(x).

(17)

That is,
L_{p}=L+pY+pQ+\frac{p^{2}}{2}R
. The assumption below is for a fixed value of
p
.

Assumption (G).
There exists a
C^{2}
function
V:M\to[1,\infty)
with
V(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
so that

(0)

\dfrac{LV(x)}{V(x)}
is bounded above.

(1)

\dfrac{L_{p}V(x)}{V(x)}\to-\infty
as
x\to\infty
.

(2)

There exists
\beta>1
such that
\dfrac{L_{\beta p}V(x)}{V(x)}
is bounded above.

(3)

There exists
\beta>1
such that
\dfrac{L_{p}(V^{\beta})(x)}{V^{\beta}(x)}
is bounded above.

Note
\beta
can be different in
(2)
and
(3)
.

Remark 2.1
.

This assumption is designed for the case when
M
is noncompact, and then “
V(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
” is shorthand for “
\{x\in M:V(x)\leq k\}
is compact for all
k\in\mathbb{R}
”. Similarly for
(1)
. Of course, if
M
happens to be compact then
(G)
is trivially true with
V(x)\equiv 1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Write
V(x)=e^{\gamma U(x)}
with
U(x)\geq 0
and
U(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
. The left side of
(1)
is

\frac{L_{p}V(x)}{V(x)}=\gamma LU(x)+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl((X_{j}U)(x)\bigr)^{2}+p\gamma YU(x)+pQ(x)+\frac{p^{2}}{2}R(x).

(18)

Similarly for
(2)
and
(3)
. The convexity of the right side of (
18
) with respect to
p
and with respect to
\gamma
implies that if
(1)
is satisfied then
\beta
in
(2)
or
(3)
can be replaced by any smaller
\tilde{\beta}>1
. It follows that without loss of generality we may assume the same value of
\beta
in
(2)
and
(3)
.

Remark 2.3
.

The condition
(0)
is included to ensure that the solution
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
of (
1
) exists for all
t\geq 0
. The condition
(1)
is designed to ensure that the
\{T_{t}^{p}:t\geq 0\}
act as bounded operators on the Banch space
B_{V}
. The conditions
(2)
and
(3)
are more technical and are used to prove uniform integrability at certain points in the proofs.

3
Statement of results

This section is divided into three subsections. Section
3.1
deals with
\Lambda(p)
for a single fixed

and includes the important characterization of
\Lambda(p)
as the solution of an eigenvalue/eigenfunction problem on the Banach space

, see Corollary

. Section

deals with the dependence of
\Lambda(p)
and the associated eigenfunction
\phi_{p}
on the parameter

. Section

for

near 0. It includes the identification of
\Lambda^{\prime}(0)
as the almost sure limit of

as

, and of
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)
as the variance in a central limit theorem for

as

, see Theorem
3.5
.

3.1
Results for fixed
p

Assume
(G)
with the function
V
, and let
B_{V}
be the Banach space defined in (
15
). For linear
T:B_{V}\to B_{V}
write the operator norm
\|T\|_{V}=\sup\{\|Tg\|_{V}:\|g\|_{V}=1\}
. Recall from (

) the definition

T_{t}^{p}g(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[g(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}\right]

for measurable
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
. The first result does not require the positivity assumption
(P)
. The main novelty here is part (ii) which relies on the smoothness of

and the

. In the case when

, parts (i) and (iii) are valid under similar growth conditions for a wider class of functions

, see
[

and
[

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
(H,G)
.

(i)
\{T_{t}^{p}:t\geq 0\}
acts as a semigroup of bounded operators on
B_{V}
.

(ii) For
g\in B_{V}
the mapping
(t,x)\to T_{t}^{p}g(x)
is
C^{\infty}
on
(0,\infty)\times M
and

\left(L+pY+pQ+\frac{p^{2}}{2}R-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)T_{t}^{p}g(x)=0.

(iii) For

the mapping

is a compact operator on

.

The next result applies the Kreĭn-Rutman theorem for positive compact linear operators
[
33
]
to the operator

to obtain the eigenfunction

, and then applies the theory of

-exponential ergodicity
[

to the twisted Markov operator
\widehat{T}g(x)=T_{1}^{p}(\phi_{p}g)(x)/\bigl(\kappa_{p}\phi_{p}(x)\bigr)

. This is the method used in
[

]
and
[
19
]
. For completeness we give a precise statement here and the proof later.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
(H,P,G)
. Let
\kappa_{p}
be the spectral radius of the operator

. Then
\kappa_{p}
is a positive eigenvalue of

with

for all

. There exists a measure

with smooth density on

\int_{M}Vd\widehat{\nu}_{p}<\infty
and
\widehat{\nu}_{p}(U)>0

, and there exist constants
C_{p}<\infty
and
0<\eta_{p}<1
such that if

then

\left|\frac{1}{(\kappa_{p})^{t}}T_{t}^{p}g(x)-\phi_{p}(x)\int_{M}gd\widehat{\nu}_{p}\right|\leq C_{p}\|g\|_{V}\eta_{p}^{t}V(x)

(19)

for all

is an eigenfunction of

With these results about the operators

we can take

Remark 3.1
.

In the proof of Theorem

, once we have the eigenfunction

then the measure

is determined by the condition

Kontoyiannis and Meyn refer to this as a multiplicative ergodic theorem, see
[

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume

.

(i) For all

The proofs of Theorems

Example 3.2
.

Consider the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

(with

The results in this Section use the analytic perturbation theory of Kato

for operators acting on a complex vector space. The crucial fact is that the operators

acting on the complexification

of

as

inside

, see Lemma
7.1
. This observation is well known for the case

under various growth conditions, see for example
[

, Theorem 3.1]
,
[

and
[

, Theorem 2.10]

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
(H,P,G)
valid for two values
p_{1}<p_{2}
with same function
V
. Then
p\to\Lambda(p)
is convex and real analytic on
(p_{1},p_{2})
and there is a real analytic mapping

from

into

such that
\phi_{p}(x)>0
and
L_{p}\phi_{p}(x)=\Lambda(p)\phi_{p}(x)
for all

and

.

Assuming
(G)
in Theorem
3.3
can be satisfied with arbitrarily large positive values of

and arbitrarily large negative values of

then we obtain

defined and analytic for all

. Since the value of

does not depend on the function

and

require different functions

Corollary

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume

valid for two values

For each

\left|(\Lambda_{t}^{x})^{(k)}(p)-\Lambda^{(k)}(p)\right|\leq\frac{C_{k}}{t}\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{0}\mbox{ and }|p-p_{0}|<\delta_{1}.

(20)

Proofs for Theorems

and

3.3
Behavior for
p
near 0

The large deviations of
A_{t}/t
as
t\to\infty
are determined, via the Gärtner-Ellis theorem, by the function
\Lambda(p)
defined on the entire real line. Here we concentrate on aspects of the limiting behavior of
A_{t}/t
which are determined by the values of
\Lambda(p)
for
p
in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
0
.

Suppose first that the assumptions
(H,P,G)
are satisfied at
p=0
. Putting
p=0
in Theorem
3.1
(ii) implies
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
has the strong Feller property. The eigenfunction
\phi_{0}
can be chosen to be identically 1, and then
\widehat{\nu}_{0}
is an invariant probability measure
\nu
, say, with a smooth density. We have
\int_{M}Vd\nu<\infty
and
\nu(U)>0
for every non-empty open
U\subset M
. Since
\kappa_{0}=1
the inequality (
19
) in Theorem
3.2
is a statement of
V
-exponential ergodicity for the Markov process
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
. The hypoellipticity condition
(H)
implies that every invariant probability measure has a smooth density, and the positivity condition
(P)
implies
\nu(U)>0
for all non-empty open
U\subset M
. This implies
\nu
is the unique invariant probability measure and that
\mbox{supp}(\nu)=M
. It follows (see
[
26
, Prop 5.1]
) that
P_{t}(x,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
for all
t>0
and all
x\in M
.

The following Lemma shows that the growth condition
(G)
valid for
p=\pm\delta
is stronger than the integrability conditions typically made on the functions
Q
and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
in the additive functional
A_{t}
. The proof is given in Section
8
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
(H,P,G)
valid for two values
p=\pm\delta
with same function
V
for some
\delta>0
. Then
Q
,
YV/V
and
R
are all in
L^{1}(\nu)
.

In the setting of Lemma
3.1
write

A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Q(x_{s})ds+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}q_{j}(x_{s})dW_{s}^{j}=\int_{0}^{t}Q(x_{s})ds+N_{t}

where
\{N_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is a continuous local martingale with

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\langle N\rangle_{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\bigl(q_{j}(x_{s})\bigr)^{2}\right)ds=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}R(x_{s})ds.

Since
R\in L^{1}(\nu)
and
P_{t}(x,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
the almost sure ergodic theorem implies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\langle N\rangle_{t}=\int_{M}Rd\nu<\infty

\mathbb{P}^{x}
-almost surely. It follows that
\frac{1}{t}N_{t}\to 0
as
t\to\infty

\mathbb{P}^{x}
-almost surely. Applying the almost sure ergodic theorem again and using
Q\in L^{1}(\nu)
gives

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}A_{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q(x_{s})ds=\int_{M}Qd\nu:=\lambda

(21)

\mathbb{P}^{x}
-almost surely for all
x\in M
. In the setting of any of the motivating examples (
5
), (
8
) or (
11
) in Section
1
with
A_{t}=\log\|v_{t}\|-\log\|v_{0}\|
, this can be recognized as the Furstenberg-Khas’minskii formula for the top Lyapunov exponent
\lambda
, see
[
4
]
.

In the following result note that the smoothness and convexity of
\Lambda(p)
for
-\delta<p<\delta
implies the existence of
\Lambda^{\prime}(0)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume
(H,P,G)
valid for two values
p=\pm\delta
with same function
V
for some
\delta>0
. Then

(i)
\Lambda(0)=0
and
\Lambda^{\prime}(0)=\lambda
.

(ii) For all
x\in M
the distribution of

\sqrt{t}\left(\frac{A_{t}}{t}-\Lambda^{\prime}(0)\right)

under
\mathbb{P}^{x}
converges as

to the normal distribution
N(0,\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0))
with mean 0 and variance
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)
.

In Section
8
we present two methods of proof for Theorem
3.5
. The first method, in Section
8.1
, uses the more abstract properties
\Lambda(p)
in Theorem
3.4
and is based very closely on results of Liming Wu
[
43
]
. It gives rise also to the following moderate deviations result, see
[
43
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
(H,P,G)
valid for two values
p=\pm\delta
with same function
V
for some
\delta>0
. Assume
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
. Suppose
b_{t}/\sqrt{t}\to\infty
and
b_{t}/t\to 0
as

. Then
(A_{t}-t\Lambda^{\prime}(0))/b_{t}
satisfies the large deviation principle with speed

and rate function

.

For example in Theorem
3.6
we could take
b_{t}=t^{\beta}
for

.

The second method of proof of Theorem
3.5
uses properties of the eigenfunctions
\phi_{p}
associated with

. More specifically it uses the real analyticity of

and

in the equation

\Bigl(L+p\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}X_{j}+pQ+\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j}^{2}\Bigr)\phi_{p}(x)=\Lambda(p)\phi_{p}(x).

In particular it gives the existence of the function
\Phi_{1}=\dfrac{\partial\phi_{p}}{\partial p}\big|_{p=0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the Poisson equation

for all

. Details are given in Section

. The use of eigenfunctions also allows the following characterization of the degenerate case when

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume
(H,P,G)
valid for two values
p=\pm\delta
with same function
V
for some
\delta>0
. The following are equivalent

(i)
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
.

(ii) There exists
\Phi_{1}\in B_{V}
such that

X_{j}\Phi_{1}(x)+q_{j}(x)=0

and

(iii) There exists

4
Examples

We consider briefly two examples in which the results above can be applied to the linearization of a nonlinear stochastic differential equation.

4.1
Langevin example

The system

\displaystyle dx_{t}

\displaystyle=y_{t}dt

\displaystyle dy_{t}

\displaystyle=(ax_{t}-bx_{t}^{3}-\beta y_{t})dt+\sigma dW_{t}

with parameters
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b>0
,
\beta>0
and
\sigma\neq 0
is a particular case of the Langevin family studied by Mattingly, Stuart and Higham
[
34
]
and Ferrè and Stoltz
[
20
]
. Whereas those papers consider ergodic and large deviation behavior of the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, here we consider also the linearization

dv_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
a-3bx_{t}^{2}&-\beta\end{bmatrix}v_{t}dt.

Putting
v_{t}=\|v_{t}\|\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{t}\\
\sin\theta_{t}\end{bmatrix}
gives

\displaystyle d\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigl(-\sin^{2}\theta_{t}+(a-3bx_{t}^{2})\cos^{2}\theta_{t}-\beta\sin\theta_{t}\cos\theta_{t}\bigr)dt

\displaystyle d\log\|v_{t}\|

\displaystyle=\bigl((1+a-3bx_{t}^{2})\sin\theta_{t}\cos\theta_{t}-\beta\sin^{2}\theta_{t}\bigr)dt.

This gives a diffusion
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\theta_{t});t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and then
A_{t}=\log\|v_{t}\|-\log\|v_{0}\|
has
Q(x,y,\theta)=(1+a-3bx^{2})\sin\theta\cos\theta-\beta\sin^{2}\theta
and
q_{j}\equiv 0
.

Let
\widehat{L}
denote the generator of
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
and let
L
denote the generator of
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\theta_{t}):t\geq 0\}
. Notice that
|Q(x,y,\theta)|\leq A+Bx^{2}
for constants
A
and
B
, and define

V(x,y)=\exp\left\{\gamma\left(-\frac{ax^{2}}{2}+\frac{bx^{4}}{4}+\frac{y^{2}}{2}\right)+\varepsilon xy\right\}.

(22)

It is shown in
[
34
]
that there exist
\gamma>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\widehat{L}V(x,y)/V(x,y)\to-\infty
as
\|(x,y)\|\to\infty
. Since
V
does not depend on
\theta
, essentially the same calculations can be used to show that

\frac{LV(x,y)}{V(x,y)}+pQ(x,y,\theta)\leq\frac{\widehat{L}V(x,y)}{V(x,y)}+p(Ax^{2}+B)\to-\infty

as

\|(x,y)\|\to\infty

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\gamma<2\beta/\sigma^{2}
and sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. It follows that the growth condition
(G)
is satisfied with
V
as in (
22
) for all
p\in\mathbb{R}
.

The conditions
(H)
and
(P)
for the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}:t\geq 0\}
are verified in
[
34
]
. It is a non-trivial matter to extend these conditions to the 3-dimensional system
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\theta_{t}):t\geq 0\}
, and this will be done elsewhere.

4.2
Nonlinear autoparametric system

In Baxendale and Sri Namachchivaya
[
9
]
the stability of the single mode solution of a nonlinear autoparametric system is given by the limiting behavior of
\frac{1}{t}\log\|u_{t}\|
as
t\to\infty
for the 4-dimensional system

\displaystyle dv_{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-\chi^{2}&-2\zeta_{1}\end{bmatrix}dv_{t}+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\sigma\end{bmatrix}dW_{t}

\displaystyle du_{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-\kappa^{2}-\chi^{2}v_{t}^{(1)}-2\zeta_{1}v_{t}^{(2)}&-2\zeta_{2}\end{bmatrix}du_{t}+\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sigma&0\end{bmatrix}u_{t}dW_{t}

(see
[
9
, Equations 2.6, 2.7, 2.8]
; here

plays the role of

and

). Putting

\displaystyle d\psi_{t}

\displaystyle=h(v_{t},\psi_{t})dt+\sigma\cos^{2}\psi_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle d\log\|u_{t}\|

\displaystyle=Q(v_{t},\psi_{t})dt+\sigma\sin\psi_{t}\cos\psi_{t}dW_{t}

where

\displaystyle h(v,\psi)

\displaystyle=-1+\bigl(1-\kappa^{2}-\chi^{2}v^{(1)}-2\zeta_{1}v^{(2)}\bigr)\cos^{2}\psi-2\zeta_{2}\sin\psi\cos\psi-\sigma^{2}\sin\psi\cos^{3}\psi

\displaystyle Q(v,\psi)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\kappa^{2}-\chi^{2}v^{(1)}-2\zeta_{1}v^{(2)}\bigr)\sin\psi\cos\psi-2\zeta_{2}\sin^{2}\psi+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\cos^{2}\psi\cos 2\psi.

This gives a diffusion
\{(v_{t},\psi_{t});t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and then
A_{t}=\log\|u_{t}\|
has

as above and

.

The conditions
(H)
and
(P)
are verified in
[
9
, Section 8]
.
Here we will verify
(G)
. Let
\widehat{L}
denote the generator of
\{v_{t}:t\geq 0\}
and let
L
denote the generator of
\{(v_{t},\psi_{t}):t\geq 0\}
. Write
A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-\chi^{2}&-2\zeta_{1}\end{bmatrix}
and
\overline{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\sigma\end{bmatrix}
.
Since
A
has eigenvalues with strictly negative real parts, there exists an invertible real

matrix

and
\delta>0
such that
\langle CAv,Cv\rangle\leq-\delta\|Cv\|^{2}
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Choose

and
V(v)=e^{\gamma U(v)}
for some
\gamma>0
.
If
X(v,\psi)
denotes the vector field multiplying

in the SDE for

then

, using (

) we have

\displaystyle\frac{(L_{p}V)(v,\psi)}{V(v)}

\displaystyle=\gamma\widehat{L}U(v)+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\bigl(XU(v)\bigr)^{2}+p\gamma q(v,\psi)XU(v)+pQ(v,\psi)+\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\bigl(q(v,\psi)\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma\Bigl(2\langle CAv,Cv\rangle+\langle C\overline{\sigma},C\overline{\sigma}\rangle\Bigr)+2\gamma^{2}\langle Cv,C\overline{\sigma}\rangle^{2}+2\gamma pq(v,\psi)\langle Cv,C\overline{\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+pQ(v,\psi)+\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\bigl(q(v,\psi)\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2\gamma\delta\|Cv\|^{2}+2\gamma^{2}\|C\overline{\sigma}\|^{2}\|Cv\|^{2}+{\cal O}(\|v\|)

\displaystyle\to-\infty

as
\|v\|\to\infty
so long as
\gamma<\delta/\|C\overline{\sigma}\|^{2}
.
Therefore
(G)
is satisfied for all
p\in\mathbb{R}
with
V(v)=e^{\gamma\|Cv\|^{2}}
for any

satisfying
0<\gamma<\delta/\|C\overline{\sigma}\|^{2}
.

5
Some examples of asymptotic behavior of
\Lambda(p)
.

In the case that

is compact and

for all

it is clear that

for all

see

. Not surprisingly, the asymptotic behavior of

as

can be very different in the non-compact case. In this section we give some particular results when the underlying diffusion process
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is the pitchfork bifurcation with additive noise

dx_{t}=(ax_{t}-bx_{t}^{3})dt+\sigma dW_{t}.

(23)

There is no linearization carried out in these examples. Instead we make arbitrary choices for the functions

and

.

Proposition 5.1
.

For the system (

) with
b>0
and
\sigma>0

(i) if
\displaystyle{A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}^{2}\,ds}
then
\lim_{p\to\infty}\dfrac{\Lambda(p)}{p^{3/2}}=\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3/2}\dfrac{\sigma}{b};

(ii) if
\displaystyle{A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}^{4}\,ds}
then
\lim_{p\to\infty}\dfrac{\Lambda(p)}{p^{3}}=\dfrac{16}{27}\left(\dfrac{\sigma}{b}\right)^{4};

(iii) if
\displaystyle{A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}\,dW_{s}}
then
\lim_{p\to\infty}\dfrac{\Lambda(p)}{p^{3}}=\dfrac{16\sigma}{27b};

(iv) if
\displaystyle{A_{t}=-\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}\,dW_{s}}
then

\displaystyle{\frac{(\max(a,0))^{2}}{4b\sigma}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\leq\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\Lambda(p)}{p}\leq\frac{(\max(a,0))^{2}}{4b\sigma}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}.

Remark 5.1
.

It can be seen in (iii) and (iv) that the relationship between the noise in (

is important. The condition that
\sigma>0
, rather than just
\sigma\neq 0
, is important in distinguishing cases (iii) and (iv). Suppose more generally

A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}d\widetilde{W}_{s}
where
\{W_{t}:t\geq 0\}
and
\{\widetilde{W}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
are standard Wiener processes with covariation
\langle W,\widetilde{W}\rangle_{t}=\rho t
. The correlation coefficient

occurs frequently in stochastic volatility models in mathematical finance, see
[
24
]
,
[
21
]
. This fits within the general setting of an SDE (

) by writing

\displaystyle dx_{t}

\displaystyle=(ax_{t}-bx_{t}^{3})dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{1}

\displaystyle A_{t}

\displaystyle=\rho\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}dW_{s}^{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}x_{s}dW_{t}^{2}

where

and
\{W_{t}^{2}:t\geq 0\}
are independent standard Wiener processes. Then

and

. The extreme cases

appear as cases (iii) and (iv) above. For

we have

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\Lambda(p)}{p^{3}}=\frac{\Bigl(\sqrt{3+\rho^{2}}+2\rho\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\sqrt{3+\rho^{2}}-\rho\Bigr)\sigma}{27b}.

The proofs of these limits appear in Section

. The method of proof relies on two observations which are valid for any system satisfying
(H,P,G)
for some fixed

with a given

.

•

Suppose

. Lemma

gives

.

•

Suppose

is non-negative, not identically 0, and satisfies

. By Remark

we have

.

These two observations can be used to obtain upper and lower bounds on

the asymptotic behaviors of the upper and lower bounds happen to coincide.

6.1
Non-explosion and bounds on
T_{t}^{p}

For
k\geq 1
define
D_{k}=\{x\in M:V(x)\leq k\}
. Then
D_{k}
is compact and
D_{k}\subset\mbox{int}(D_{k+1})
and
D_{k}\nearrow M
.
For each initial point
x\in M
the process
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is well defined up to the stopping time
\tau_{k}=\inf\{t\geq 0:x_{t}\not\in D_{k}\}
. The first result is due to Meyn and Tweedie
[
36
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
(0)
. There exists
\Gamma_{0}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{k}\leq t)\leq k^{-1}V(x)e^{\Gamma_{0}t}

whenever
V(x)<k
. In particular
\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{k}\to\infty\mbox{ as }k\to\infty)=1
for all
x
, so that that the process
\{x_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is non-explosive, that is, the solution exists for all time
t\geq 0
.

Proof.
Define
\Gamma_{0}=\sup_{x\in M}LV(x)/V(x)\in[0,\infty)
. Fix
k\geq 1
and define
M_{t}=V(x_{t\wedge\tau_{k}})e^{-\Gamma_{0}(t\wedge\tau_{k})}
. Adapting the method of proof of Lemma
6.2
to the condition
(0)
we deduce that
M_{t}
is a supermartingale.
For
V(x)<k
, on the set
\tau_{k}\leq t
we have
M_{t}=V(x_{\tau_{k}})e^{-\Gamma_{0}\tau_{k}}=ke^{-\Gamma_{0}\tau_{k}}\geq ke^{-\Gamma_{0}t}
.
Therefore

ke^{-\Gamma_{0}t}\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{k}\leq t)\leq\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[M_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\tau_{k}\leq t}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[M_{t}\right]\leq V(x),

and the result follows directly. ∎

The next result uses only a weakened version of
(1)
in which
L_{p}V/V
is bounded above.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume
(0,1)
. There exists
\Gamma
such that

T_{t}^{p}V(x)\leq e^{\Gamma t}V(x)

(24)

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in M
. Further,
T_{t}^{p}
is a well-defined bounded linear operator on
B_{V}
with operator norm
\|T_{t}^{p}\|_{V}\leq e^{\Gamma t}
.

Proof.
The proof follows closely Wu
[
44
, Corollary 2.2]
and Ferré and Stoltz
[
20
, Lemma 6.2]
. The condition
(1)
implies

\Gamma:=\sup_{x\in M}\left(\frac{L_{p}V(x)}{V(x)}\right)=\sup_{x\in M}\left(\frac{LV(x)+pYV(x)}{V(x)}+pQ(x)+\frac{p^{2}}{2}R(x)\right)

(25)

is finite. Let
M_{t}=V(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}e^{-\Gamma t}
, then Itô’s formula gives

\displaystyle dM_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(LV(x_{t})+pYV(x_{t})+pQ(x_{t})V(x_{t})+\frac{p^{2}}{2}R(x_{t})V(x_{t})-\Gamma V(x_{t})\right)e^{pA_{t}}e^{-\Gamma t}dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(X_{j}V(x_{t})+pq_{j}(x_{t})V(x_{t})\bigr)e^{pA_{t}}e^{-\Gamma t}dW_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(L_{p}V(x_{t})}{V(x_{t})}-\Gamma\right)M_{t}dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(X_{j}V(x_{t})+pq_{j}(x_{t})V(x_{t})\bigr)e^{pA_{t}}e^{-\Gamma t}dW_{j}(t)

(26)

so that

is a non-negative local supermartingale and hence a supermartingale. Therefore

T_{t}^{p}V(x)=e^{\Gamma t}\mathbb{E}^{x}[M_{t}]\leq e^{\Gamma t}\mathbb{E}^{x}[M_{0}]=e^{\Gamma t}V(x)

and (
24

g\in B_{V}
we have
|g(x_{t})|\leq\|g\|_{V}V(x_{t})
and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}\left|g(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}\right|\leq\|g\|_{V}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[V(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}\right]=\|g\|_{V}T_{t}^{p}V(x)\leq\|g\|_{V}e^{\Gamma t}V(x).

Therefore
T_{t}^{p}g(x)
is well defined and
|T_{t}^{p}g(x)|\leq\|g\|_{V}e^{\Gamma t}V(x)
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume
(0,1,2)
. For each

\frac{T_{t}^{p}V(x)}{V(x)}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }x\to\infty.

(29)

Proof.
Assumption
(1)
implies
\dfrac{L_{p}V(x)}{V(x)}\to-\infty
as
x\to\infty
, so there exist sequences
a_{n}\to\infty
and
b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a_{n}V(x)+L_{p}V(x)\leq b_{n}
for all
x\in M
. Let
M_{t}=e^{a_{n}t}V(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}
, then (adapting the calculation in (
26

\displaystyle dM_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigl(a_{n}V(x_{t})+L_{p}V(x_{t})\bigr)e^{a_{n}t}e^{pA_{t}}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(X_{j}V(x_{t})+pq_{j}(x_{t})V(x_{t})\bigr)e^{a_{n}t}e^{pA_{t}}dW_{j}(t).

The integrals of the noise terms are local martingales, so with the stopping times

from Lemma

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}[M_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq V(x)+\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\bigl(a_{n}V(x_{s})+L_{p}V(x_{s})\bigr)e^{a_{n}s}e^{pA_{s}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq V(x)+b_{n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{a_{n}s}(T_{s}^{p}\mathbf{1})(x)ds.

Letting

and using Fatou’s lemma gives

e^{a_{n}t}(T_{t}^{p}V)(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}[M_{t}]\leq V(x)+b_{n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{a_{n}s}(T_{s}\mathbf{1})(x)ds.

By Lemma
6.3
(i), using assumption

(T_{t}^{p}\mathbf{1})(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{pA_{t}}\right]\leq\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{\beta pA_{t}}\right]\right)^{1/\beta}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[V(x_{t})e^{\beta pA_{t}}\right]\right)^{1/\beta}\leq\left(e^{\Gamma_{2}t}V(x)\right)^{1/\beta}.

e^{a_{n}t}(T_{t}^{p}V)(x)\leq V(x)+b_{n}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(a_{n}+\Gamma_{2}/\beta)s}(V(x))^{1/\beta}ds\leq V(x)+\frac{b_{n}e^{(a_{n}+\Gamma_{2}/\beta)t}}{a_{n}+\Gamma_{2}/\beta}(V(x))^{1/\beta}.

Since

we get

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{p}V(x)}{V(x)}\leq e^{-a_{n}t}

and result follows by letting

\displaystyle\big|T^{p}_{t,k}g(x)-T^{p}_{t}g(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbb{E}^{x}\bigl[g(x_{t,k})e^{pA_{t,k}}1_{\{\tau_{k}\leq t\}}\bigr]-\mathbb{E}^{x}\bigl[g(x_{t})e^{pA_{t}}1_{\{\tau_{k}\leq t\}}\bigr]\big|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{pA_{t,k}}1_{\{\tau_{k}\leq t\}}\right]+\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{pA_{t}}1_{\{\tau_{k}\leq t\}}\right]

\displaystyle=I+II,

say. Applying Hölder’s inequality with
\beta>1
and using Lemma
6.3
(i) and Lemma
6.1
we have

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{\beta pA_{t}}\right]\right)^{1/\beta}\mathbb{P}^{x}(\tau_{k}\leq t)^{(\beta-1)/\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(V(x)e^{\Gamma_{2}t}\right)^{1/\beta}\left(k^{-1}V(x)e^{\Gamma_{0}t}\right)^{(\beta-1)/\beta}

\displaystyle=V(x)\exp\left\{\frac{\Gamma_{2}+(\beta-1)\Gamma_{0}}{\beta}t\right\}k^{-(\beta-1)/\beta}.

Since

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M}\left(\frac{(L^{(k)}+\beta p\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j,k}X_{j,k}+\beta pQ_{k}+\frac{\beta^{2}p^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{j,k}^{2})V(x)}{V(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in M}\chi_{k}^{2}(x)\left(\frac{(L+\beta pY+\beta pQ+\frac{\beta^{2}p^{2}}{2}R)V(x)}{V(x)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(0,\sup_{x\in M}L_{\beta p}V(x)/V(x)\right)

we get a similar estimate for
I
with the same
\Gamma_{2}
. Therefore

\left|T^{p}_{t,k}g(x)-T^{p}_{t}g(x)\right|\leq 2V(x)\exp\left\{\frac{\Gamma_{2}+(\beta-1)\Gamma_{0}}{\beta}t\right\}k^{-(\beta-1)/\beta}

and the right side converges to 0 as
k\to\infty
uniformly for
0\leq t\leq T
and
x\in D_{k_{0}}
. ∎

Lemma 6.9

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{1}_{D_{k}}+\mathbf{1}_{D_{k}^{c}})T^{p}_{t/2}(\mathbf{1}_{D_{k}}+\mathbf{1}_{D_{k}^{c}})T^{p}_{t/2}

6.3

The proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.
(i) implies (ii) by Lemma
3.1
, and (ii) implies (iii) by (

). Given (iii) we have

T_{t}^{p}(e^{p\Phi_{1}})(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{p\Phi_{1}(x_{t})}e^{pA_{t}}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[e^{p(\lambda t+\Phi(x_{0}))}\right]=e^{p\lambda t}e^{p\Phi_{1}(x)}.

This suggests

is an eigenfunction for
T_{1}^{p}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
e^{p\lambda}
, and hence that
\Lambda(p)=p\lambda
. But we need to check that

is in the Banach space
B_{V}
. For
|p|<\delta
we know that
T_{t}^{p}
acting on
B_{V}
has an eigenvalue
\kappa_{p}^{t}
and corresponding positive eigenfunction

. Fix a non-empty open set

with compact closure and write

. Fix

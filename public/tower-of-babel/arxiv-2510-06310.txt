MUSEQuBES: Physical conditions, origins, and multi-element abundances of the circumgalactic medium of an isolated, star-forming dwarf galaxy at z = 0.57 z=0.57 Facilities: HST (ACS, COS), VLT:Yepun (MUSE), Magellan:Clay (LDSS3), Magellan:Baade (IMACS), and Blanco (DECam)
Sean D. Johnson, Nishant Mishra, Sowgat Muzahid, Gwen C. Rudie, Fakhri S. Zahedy, Zhijie Qu, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère, Jonathan Stern, Jennifer I-Hsiu Li, Elise Fuller, Sebastiano Cantalupo, Hsiao-Wen Chen, Ahmad Kadri, Suyash Kumar, Zhuoqi (Will) Liu, Gregory Walth

Abstract
In dwarf galaxy models, outflows expel metal-enriched interstellar medium (ISM) into the circumgalactic medium (CGM) to reproduce their observed low metallicities, but measurements of dwarf CGM properties are scarce. We present a study of the CGM of an isolated dwarf at z = 0.5723 z=0.5723 with a stellar mass of ≈ 5 × 10 7 ​ M ⊙ \approx 5\times 10^{7}\rm\,M_{\odot} and star-formation rate ( ≈ 0.05 ​ M ⊙ ​ yr − 1 \approx 0.05\,\rm M_{\odot}\,yr^{-1} ) and ISM metallicity ( [ O / H ] ≈ − 0.9 \rm[O/H]\approx-0.9 ) consistent with the star-forming main sequence and mass-metallicity relation. A background quasar sightline with archival UV spectra probes the dwarf’s CGM at a projected distance of 28 kpc, corresponding to approximately half of the estimated virial radius. The dwarf’s CGM is detected in H I, intermediate metal ions of C III, O III, and O IV, and kinematically broader, highly-ionized O VI, but is undetected in N IV and Ne VIII. Photoionization modeling of the intermediate ions indicates a modest volume-filling factor ( ∼ 6 % \sim 6\% along the sightline or ∼ 2 % \sim 2\% globally), and a mass of ∼ 2 × 10 8 ​ M ⊙ \sim 2\times 10^{8}{\rm\,M_{\odot}} , ∼ 4 × \sim 4\times higher than the dwarf’s stellar mass, but ∼ 10 × \sim 10\times less than the highly ionized CGM. The O VI kinematics are comparable to the dwarf’s estimated virial velocity, suggesting it is likely associated with cool, photoionized, and volume-filling CGM, with bulk motion or turbulence dominating over thermal pressure. The metallicity inferred for the intermediate ions is [ O / H ] = − 0.6 \rm[O/H]=-0.6 , but with low relative abundances of [ C / O ] = − 0.6 \rm[C/O]=-0.6 and [ N / O ] < − 0.9 \rm[N/O]<-0.9 . The [N/O] is below levels expected of the dwarf’s ISM, but consistent with core-collapse supernova ejecta, suggesting that supernova-enriched gas escaped the dwarf without mixing significantly with ISM enriched in nitrogen from evolved, low-mass stars.

I
Introduction

Low-mass dwarf galaxies exhibit lower stellar-to-halo mass ratios
(
S. S. McGaugh et al. 2010
;
J. I. Read et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
and lower heavy element abundances
(
H. Lee et al. 2006
;
E. N. Kirby et al. 2013
;
D. A. Berg et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
than more massive galaxies such as the Milky Way
(
J. S. Bullock & M. Boylan-Kolchin 2017
;
D. A. Hunter et al. 2024
, for reviews, see)
. Models of galaxy evolution reproduce these trends by incorporating stellar feedback mechanisms, typically dominated by core-collapse supernovae explosions, that are effective at expelling or heating gas in the shallow gravitational potential wells of dwarf galaxies to slow star formation while also removing heavy elements from the interstellar medium
(
X. Ma et al. 2016
;
D. Anglés-Alcázar et al. 2017
;
A. L. Muratov et al. 2017
;
A. Emerick et al. 2019
;
M. Mina et al. 2021
;
V. Pandya et al. 2021
;
U. P. Steinwandel et al. 2024
, ISM; e.g.,)
. Simulations that incorporate this feedback predict that the circumgalactic and intergalactic medium (CGM/IGM) around dwarf galaxies will be a dominant repository for baryons and metals
(
S. Shen et al. 2014
;
M. Vogelsberger et al. 2014
;
J. Schaye et al. 2015
;
L. Wang et al. 2015
;
A. L. Muratov et al. 2017
;
D. Nelson et al. 2019
;
B. Baumschlager et al. 2025
;
D. R. Piacitelli et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. However, the predicted physical conditions, masses, and heavy element abundances of these reservoirs vary significantly depending on adopted feedback prescriptions
(
J. J. Davies et al. 2019
;
R. A. Crain & F. van de Voort 2023
;
C.-A. Faucher-Giguère & S. P. Oh 2023
, e.g.,)
. Consequently, observations of the CGM/IGM around dwarf galaxies represent a key means of testing our understanding of galaxy evolution.

In the local Universe, multi-phase outflows driving ISM from galaxies into the nearby CGM can be detected in emission
(
D. S. N. Rupke 2018
, for a review, see)
, but such observations are not currently feasible around dwarf galaxies at even modest distances. To overcome this obstacle, studies of the diffuse CGM/IGM often rely on background quasar absorption spectroscopy, which is sensitive to gas orders-of-magnitude lower in density than is possible in emission
(
J. Tumlinson et al. 2017
;
H.-W. Chen & F. S. Zahedy 2024
, for reviews, see)
. Absorption observations of the CGM of dwarf galaxies often operate at low redshift (
z<0.05
) due to the difficulty in detecting low-luminosity galaxies at larger distances
(
R. Bordoloi et al. 2014
;
C. J. Liang & H.-W. Chen 2014
;
J. N. Burchett et al. 2016
;
Y. Zheng et al. 2020
;
Z. Qu & J. N. Bregman 2022
;
Y. Zheng et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. These studies found low covering fractions in low and intermediate ionization state metal lines (e.g, Si II, S III, S IV, C IV) in the CGM/IGM around dwarf galaxies, suggesting that their metal and baryon budgets are dominated by gas expelled beyond the virial radius or accounted for by more highly ionized phases. At such low redshifts, however, the limited sensitivity at far-UV wavelengths of instruments on the
Hubble Space Telescope
(
HST
) prevent observations of the more highly ionized gas traced by the O VI doublet, which is the most common metal transition at low redshift
(
C. W. Danforth et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
. Furthermore, this limited wavelength coverage and the damping wing of the H I Ly
\alpha
absorption-line arising from the Milky Way ISM prevent measurements of H I column densities in the CGM/IGM around low redshift dwarfs.

Deep surveys with multi-object spectrographs on large telescopes surveyed faint galaxies in the foreground of UV bright quasars with archival UV spectra from the Cosmic Origins Spectrograph
(
J. C. Green et al. 2012
, COS;)
, enabling the first statistical studies of the CGM/IGM around dwarf galaxies at modest redshifts
(
S. D. Johnson et al. 2017
;
K. Tchernyshyov et al. 2022
)
. These studies showed that highly ionized metal lines such as O VI are common in the CGM around dwarf galaxies, though with typical detected column densities that are
\approx 0.5
dex lower than those found around more massive, star-forming galaxies
(
H.-W. Chen & J. S. Mulchaey 2009
;
J. Tumlinson et al. 2011
;
J. T. Stocke et al. 2013
;
K. Tchernyshyov et al. 2022
;
S. H. Ho et al. 2025
)
. Subsequent surveys with state-of-the-art integral field spectrographs (IFS) such as the Cosmic Ultraviolet Baryon Survey
(
H.-W. Chen et al. 2020
, CUBS;)
and MUSE Quasar Blind Emitter Survey
(
S. Dutta et al. 2024b
, MUSEQuBES; )
pushed galaxy surveys in the fields of UV bright quasars 2
-
3 magnitudes fainter, dramatically expanding dwarf galaxy samples available for CGM/IGM studies. These new IFS surveys demonstrate that the O VI-bearing component of the CGM (projected distances
d
less than the estimated dark matter halo virial radius,
R_{h}
) and nearby IGM (
d/R_{\rm h}=1-2
) are dominant metal reservoirs around low-mass galaxies and exhibit surprisingly quiescent kinematics
(
N. Mishra et al. 2024
;
S. Dutta et al. 2024a
;
S. Dutta et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

Recently, high-quality UV absorption spectra at intermediate redshifts of
z=0.4-0.8
have enabled new insights into the enrichment history of the CGM/IGM through observations of key elements with different nucleosynthetic origins such as core-collapse supernova (e.g., oxygen and magnesium) versus evolved low-mass stars (e.g,. carbon and nitrogen), and Type Ia supernova
(
N. Lehner et al. 2016
;
F. S. Zahedy et al. 2017
;
F. S. Zahedy et al. 2021
;
T. J. Cooper et al. 2021
;
S. Kumar et al. 2024
, e.g., iron;)
. However, studies of the physical conditions and abundances of the CGM around dwarf galaxies are severely limited. Here, we present the first study of the CGM of an isolated, star-forming dwarf galaxy at intermediate redshift, where the H I Lyman series and a rich multi-element, multi-ion suite of absorption features shift into the observable UV spectral range. The paper proceeds as follows: In Section
II
, we describe the quasar absorption spectroscopy and follow-up galaxy spectroscopy. In Section
III
, we describe the resulting inferences into the properties of the dwarf galaxy and its absorbing CGM. In Section
IV
, we discuss the implications of these results. Finally, in Section
V
, we summarize and make concluding remarks.

Throughout, we adopt a flat
\Lambda
cosmology with
\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
, and a Hubble constant of
H_{0}=70\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
. All magnitudes are in the AB system
(
J. B. Oke & J. E. Gunn 1983
)
, wavelengths are in vacuum, and projected distances are proper. When characterizing luminosities, we compare to the characteristic knee in the galaxy luminosity function,
L_{*}
, estimated to correspond to an absolute rest-frame
r
-band magnitude of
M_{r}=-21.5
by
J. Loveday et al. 2012
. When estimating stellar masses and star formation rates, we adopt a
P. Kroupa 2001
initial mass function (IMF).

Observations and measurements

II.1
Galaxy survey observations, data reduction and redshift measurements

HE 0439
-
5254 is a UV bright quasar at
z_{\rm QSO}=1.053

(
L. Wisotzki et al. 2000
)
targeted by the COS Guaranteed Observing Time survey
(
J. T. Stocke et al. 2013

(
S. Dutta et al. 2024b
)
. We observed the field of HE 0439
-

R. Bacon et al. 2010

1^{\prime}\times 1^{\prime}
FoV centered on the quasar, sampled with
0.2^{\prime\prime}\times 0.2^{\prime\prime}
spaxels. The spectra cover a contiguous wavelength range of 4750 Å to
9350
Å at a resolution of
R\approx 3000
. The observations of HE 0439
-
5254 include a total of 2.5 hours of scientific exposure conducted under full-width-at-half maximum seeing conditions of
\rm FWHM=0.7^{\prime\prime}

.

The field of HE 0439
-

. The 5-

m_{\rm F814W}\approx 27

\approx 0.25^{\prime\prime}
. The

To conduct a galaxy redshift survey with the MUSE observations, we first identified sources and measured their photometry in the ACS
+
F814W image with
Source Extractor

(
E. Bertin & S. Arnouts 1996
)
and a white light image formed from the MUSE datacube. Next, we used

R. Bacon et al. 2016
;
L. Piqueras et al. 2017
to extract 1D spectra at the position of each continuum source using circular apertures with radii of between

and

The MUSE survey in the field of HE 0439
-

III
Results

III.1
Dwarf galaxy properties

To infer emission-line-based properties of the dwarf galaxy, including a star-formation rate and ISM metallicity, we fit the [O II]
\lambda\lambda 3727,3729
doublet, H
\beta
, and the [O III]
\lambda\lambda 4960,5008
doublet with local linear continua and single-component Gaussian profiles with shared redshift and line-width, accounting for the MUSE line spread function measured by
R. Bacon et al. 2017
. We used the
LMFIT
package
(
M. Newville et al. 2016
)
to find the best-fit model and estimated uncertainties with a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach with
emcee

(
D. Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
. The resulting measured fluxes in the [O II] doublet, H
\beta
, and [O III]
\lambda 5008
lines are
7.5\pm 0.5
,
2.5\pm 0.3
, and
5.2\pm 0.5
, respectively, in units of
\rm 10^{-18}\,erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}
. The low, marginally resolved, 1D velocity dispersion of
38\pm 6

{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
measured for the dwarf galaxy’s emission lines is consistent with expectations for low-mass galaxies
(
D. A. Forbes et al. 2011
)
. The emission-line spectra and model fits are displayed in the top left panels of Figure
2
.

To estimate properties of the dwarf galaxy from these emission line measurements, we converted the H
\beta
line flux to luminosity,
L({\rm H}\beta)=3.3\times 10^{39}\ {\rm erg\,s^{-1}}
. Adopting the
L({\rm H\beta})-\rm SFR
relation from
L. A. Boogaard et al. 2018
for MUSE-detected galaxies of similar mass and redshift, the inferred star-formation rate of the dwarf is
\log{\rm SFR}/{\rm M_{\odot}\,yr^{-1}}\approx-1.3\pm 0.3
. While Balmer absorption is negligible, we note that the H
\beta
-based SFR estimate is not corrected for dust extinction and therefore represents a lower limit. Furthermore, we measured the O32 ionization diagnostic line ratio,
\log{\rm O23}\equiv\log(\frac{\rm[O\,III]\ \lambda 5008}{\rm[O\,II]\lambda\lambda 3727,3729})=-0.16\pm 0.05
, and the R23 metallicity diagnostic line ratio,
\log{\rm R23}\equiv\log(\frac{\rm[O\,III]\ \lambda\lambda 4960,5008+[O\,II]\ \lambda\lambda 3727,3729}{\rm H\beta})=0.75\pm 0.06
. We recalibrated the R23 ratio
-
metallicity relation
(
R. Maiolino & F. Mannucci 2019
, for a review, see)
to account for dwarf galaxies with moderate ionization states similar to that observed in this work (see Appendix
A
and Figure
6
). The ISM metallicity of the dwarf galaxy inferred from its R23 ratio is
\log\rm O/H+12=7.8\pm 0.2
or
\rm[O/H]=-0.9\pm 0.2
when expressed relative to the solar oxygen abundance from
M. Asplund et al. 2009
.

+

III.2
Absorbing circumgalactic medium

The archival COS spectra of HE 0439
-
5254 provide an opportunity to study the CGM of the dwarf galaxy at a projected distance of 28 kpc, which corresponds to approximately half of the dwarf galaxy’s estimated virial radius. At
z=0.5723
, a suite of absorption features including the H I Lyman series and ions of carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen shift into the observed spectral window. The dwarf galaxy’s CGM is detected in strong H I absorption in H I Ly
\beta,\gamma
, and
\delta
, and metal ions including O III
\lambda 832
, O IV
\lambda 787
, C III
\lambda 977
, and the O VI
\lambda\lambda 1031,1037
doublet. The continuum-normalized absorption spectra for each of these transitions are plotted as a function of line-of-sight velocity relative to the dwarf galaxy (
\Delta v
) in Figure
3
. The red side of the H I Ly
\beta
associated with the dwarf is contaminated by Ly
\alpha
from a strong H I system at
z\approx 0.327
which is well constrained by higher order Lyman series lines. To visualize the H I Ly
\beta
associated with the dwarf, we fit the contaminating system and divided the COS spectrum by the best-fit model.

The dwarf galaxy’s CGM exhibits no detected absorption in O II and Ne VIII, which are also plotted in Figure
3
. The COS spectra cover the N IV
\lambda 765
line, but this spectral region is contaminated by intervening S IV
\lambda 744
absorption at
z=0.6150
. The contamination levels are constrained by the stronger S IV
\lambda 748
line. To visualize potential N IV absorption from the dwarf galaxy’s CGM, we fit the S IV
\lambda 748
feature at
z=0.6150
and divided the spectrum by the resulting prediction for the contaminating S IV
\lambda 744
absorption. After removal of the S IV contamination, the CGM of the dwarf galaxy exhibits no detectable N IV absorption (see Figure
3
). The dwarf’s CGM is also undetected in N III
\lambda 989
, though with some contamination that cannot be modeled.

While the absorption features associated with the dwarf galaxy’s CGM are relatively simple, each consisting of a dominant main component at
z\approx 0.5725
, the O IV profile exhibits a significant non-Gaussian wing,
\approx 40

{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
redward of the stronger component. This wing is also seen in less prominent asymmetries observed in H I, and possibly O III. To measure the column densities as well as thermal and non-thermal broadening levels implied by the observed absorption, we performed a joint two-component Voigt profile fit to the H I, O III
\lambda 832
, O IV
\lambda 787
, and C III
\lambda 977
features with one component for the main absorber and one for the wing. The model parameters for each component include redshift, the H I and metal ion column densities, temperature (
T
), and turbulent, non-thermal broadening (
b_{\rm turb}
). The temperature and turbulent broadening effectively set the line widths of the model for each absorption feature of a component as
b=\sqrt{b^{2}_{\rm therm}(T,m)+b^{2}_{\rm turb}}
, with
b_{\rm therm}(T,m)=\sqrt{\frac{2k_{\rm B}T}{m}}
representing thermal Doppler broadening where
m
is the mass of the element and
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant.

The O VI absorption is significantly broader than O III and O IV features, so we fit the doublet with a distinct, single highly ionized Voigt profile component with no attempt to differentiate thermal and non-thermal broadening. At fixed metallicity, highly ionized O VI absorbers are expected to produce significantly less H I absorption than components with strong detections of C III, O III, and O IV. However, without observations of Ly
\alpha
associated with the CGM of the dwarf, we cannot rule out the possibility of non-negligible contributions to the total H I from the O VI-bearing gas. If the highly ionized component contributes non-negligible H I absorption, then metallicity estimates of the intermediate ions would represent lower limits while total column and temperature estimates would represent upper limits.

To account for the contamination of H I Ly
\beta
by the strong H I system at
z=0.327
, we simultaneously fit the contaminating absorption using constraints from Ly
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\epsilon,
and
\theta
. To determine upper limits based on undetected features associated with each component, the Voigt profile model includes absorption from potential O II, N III, and N IV for the main component as well as O II, C III and N IV for the wing. To account for the contamination of N IV by the intervening S IV
\lambda 744
absorption at
z=0.6150
and of Ly
\beta
by the
z=0.327
system, we jointly fit with Voigt models for the contamination. This effectively marginalizes the N IV upper limit over nuisance parameters associated with the contaminating S IV. To place limits on the presence of other ions associated with the highly ionized O VI-bearing component, we included potential absorption from O IV
\lambda 787
and Ne VIII
\lambda 770
with width and redshift tied to the O VI absorption.

We convolved the full Voigt model including the main component, wing, and highly ionized component with the wavelength-dependent COS line spread function from lifetime position 1 and performed
\chi^{2}
minimization with
LMFIT
and MCMC posterior exploration with
emcee
. We chose a prior for column densities that is bounded from below by zero and flat in linear column,
N
. A flat prior in
N
is uninformative for detections, producing results consistent with those from a flat prior in
\log
, and it prevents convergence issues for non-detections that arise with log priors due to
\log N/{\rm cm^{-2}}
approaching
-\infty
as
N
approaches zero. The best-fit model and contribution from each component are plotted along with the observed absorption in Figure
3
, and the resulting absorption line measurements are summarized in Table
1
. For all non-detections, we report 95% upper limits on the associated columns with temperature and non-thermal broadening set by each component’s detected features. For all analysis involving the non-detections, we use the posterior for the column densities marginalized over all other model parameters, negating the need to choose a detection threshold in the likelihood.

M. Asplund et al. 2009

Cloudy

to
-1

\log N_{\rm H}/{\rm cm}^{-2}=16

We then interpolated the resulting grid to produce predicted column densities for H I and all observed metal ions as a function of density, total hydrogen column density, and oxygen metallicity, and included scaling factors to allow for non-Solar [C/O] and [N/O] abundance patterns. (
S. Kumar & H.-W. Chen 2025

. In addition, inclusion of local ionizing radiation from the dwarf’s stellar population does not significantly alter the model predictions, even with an escape fraction of

Given a specified density, total column, metallicity, and relative abundances of carbon and nitrogen to oxygen, the model can then produce predicted column densities for H I and metal ions,
N_{\rm X}(n_{\rm H},N_{\rm H},{\rm[O/H],[C/O],[N/O]})

The main component’s H I and oxygen ion column densities can be adequately described by a single phase photoionization equilibrium model with a density of
\log n_{\rm H}/{\rm cm}^{-3}=-3.7\pm 0.1

\log N({\rm H})/{\rm cm}^{-2}=18.6\pm 0.1

\rm[C/O]=-0.6\pm 0.1
and a 95% upper limit of
\rm[N/O]<-0.9

\log T_{\rm PIE}/{\rm K}\approx 4.3
. If the highly-ionized, O VI-bearing absorber contributes non-negligibly to the H I column attributed to the main component, then the inferred metallicity would be proportionally higher and the total column proportionally lower, while the density and relative abundance estimates remain consistent within uncertainties. The cloud length-scale along the sightline implied by the inferred density and total column is
\log l/{\rm pc}=3.8\pm 0.2
. The inferred properties of the wing are broadly similar to the main component, though with substantially higher uncertainty. For the wing, the inferred properties are
\log n_{\rm H}/{\rm cm}^{-3}=-3.4\pm 0.2
,
\log N({\rm H})/{\rm cm}^{-2}=17.6\pm 0.2
,
\rm[O/H]=-0.3\pm 0.3
,
\rm[C/O]<-0.6
and
\rm[N/O]<-0.3
. The volume-filling factor of the intermediate ionization-state gas along the quasar sightline is
f_{\rm V}\sim{l}/{L}\sim 6\%
, where
l\approx 6
kpc is the total length scale of the main component and wing and
L\approx 100
kpc is the pathlength of the sightline through the halo. Assuming the intermediate ion bearing gas in this system is typical, the volume-filling factor averaged globally over dwarf CGM is
f_{\rm V}\sim\kappa(d<R_{\rm h})\times l/\langle L\rangle\sim 2\%
, where
\kappa(d<R_{\rm h})\approx 0.3
is the intermediate ion covering fraction at
d<R_{\rm h}
from
N. Mishra et al. 2024
and
\langle L\rangle
is the mean pathlength through the halo averaged over sightlines at
d<R_{\rm h}
.

To gain insights into the range of physical conditions that could produce the highly ionized O VI bearing gas with upper limits on associated O IV and Ne VIII, we ran a series of
Cloudy
models for gas with density ranging from
\log n_{\rm H}/{\rm cm^{-3}}=-4
to
-6
and temperature ranging from
\log T/{\rm K}=4
to 6 for gas in steady-state between recombination and ionization from both photoionization by the UV background and collisional ionization. For models with

Under collisional ionization equilibrium, the upper limit on the Ne VIII/O VI and the lower limit on the O VI/O IV combine to constrain the O VI-bearing CGM to have
\log T/{\rm K}\approx 5.4
and
5.7
,
>0.4
dex higher than the dwarf’s estimated virial temperature. However, recent works have suggested that O VI absorption in dwarf CGM has significant contributions from gas photoionized by the UV background due to the low virial temperatures of low-mass halos
(
S. D. Johnson et al. 2017
;
Z. Qu & J. N. Bregman 2018
;
Z. Qu et al. 2024
;
S. Dutta et al. 2024a
, e.g.,)
. To produce a strong O VI absorber with the measured limits on associated O IV and Ne VIII from gas photoionized by the UV background, the implied gas density must be between
\log n_{\rm H}/{\rm cm}^{-3}\approx-5
and
-4.4
.

The predicted ion fraction of O
5+

to estimate the total oxygen column associated with the highly ionized component of
N({\rm O})\sim 10^{15}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}
. Assuming that the highly ionized CGM exhibits a metallicity similar to the less ionized gas observed in the main component of
[\rm O/H]\approx-0.6
, this corresponds to a total hydrogen column density of

N({\rm H})\sim 10^{19}\,\left[\frac{f({\rm O^{5+}})}{0.2}\right]^{-1}\left[\frac{Z}{0.25\,{\rm Z_{\odot}}}\right]^{-1}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}

and implied length-scale of

l\sim 100\left[\frac{f({\rm O^{5+}})}{0.2}\right]^{-1}\left[\frac{Z}{0.25\,Z_{\odot}}\right]^{-1}\left[\frac{n_{\rm H}}{2\times 10^{-5}\,{\rm cm^{-3}}}\right]^{-1}\ {\rm kpc,}

where the density normalization corresponds to the density at which the
\rm O^{5+}

P. R. Upton Sanderbeck et al. 2018

. Even so, the inferred length scale is comparable to the path length of the sightline through the dwarf galaxy’s halo, suggesting that the O VI absorption may arise from an approximately volume-filling photoionized medium. Finally, the Ne VIII non-detection indicates that any hot,

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Comparison with previous dwarf CGM surveys

Recent surveys of the CGM of isolated, star-forming dwarf galaxies at intermediate redshifts
(
N. Mishra et al. 2024
)
found covering fractions for strong H I and O VI systems of
\log N/{\rm cm}^{-2}>14
at projected distances of
d<0.5R_{\rm h}
of
\approx 100\%
and
\approx 80\%
, respectively, suggesting that the H I and O VI absorption levels detected around the dwarf galaxy in this work are fairly common. At low redshifts of
z\lesssim 0.05
, the C III, O III, and O IV features observed here are not accessible. To compare the CGM properties of the dwarf in the field of HE 0439
-
5254 with surveys in the local Universe, we therefore used the ionization models for the main component and wing described in Section
III.3
to make predictions for the Si III, Si IV, and C IV columns which are observable at low redshift. Assuming
\rm[Si/O]=0
and the measured carbon-to-oxygen for the main component, the total Si III, Si IV, and C IV columns expected in the dwarf galaxy’s CGM are
\log N/{\rm cm}^{-2}\approx 12.5
,
12.6
, and
13.2
, respectively. While the column density expected in Si III is high enough to be detected in the majority of low-
z
dwarf galaxy CGM sightlines compiled by
Y. Zheng et al. 2024
, the expected Si IV and C IV columns are comparable to typical detection limits. When the low
-z
surveys are restricted to sightlines with sufficient
S/N
to detect absorption at these levels, the estimated covering fractions for such Si III, Si IV, and C IV absorbers are
\approx 50\%
at
d\lesssim 0.5R_{\rm h}
, after converting to a consistent stellar-to-halo mass relation and virial radius definition. The CGM properties observed around the dwarf galaxy in the field of HE 0439
-
5254 are therefore fairly common given its stellar mass and the projected distance from the quasar sightline.

IV.2
Physical conditions of the dwarf CGM

The physical picture needed to describe the combination of observed intermediate and high ions in the CGM of dwarfs is not well established. Dwarf CGM could consist of a warm-hot, volume-filling medium populated by cool, photoionized clouds in approximate pressure equilibrium, similar to massive galaxies
(
F. S. Zahedy et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
. However, while Jean’s stable, such warm-hot CGM is expected to cool rapidly in low-mass halos, so that turbulence or bulk motion may dominate over thermal pressure
(
D. Fielding et al. 2017
;
C. A. Correa et al. 2018
;
J. Stern et al. 2020
;
C. Lochhaas et al. 2020
;
A. B. Gurvich et al. 2023
;
A. Kakoly et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. In this case, the intermediate ions and highly ionized O VI could arise from cool, photoionized gas of different densities tracing an approximately lognormal density distribution induced by turbulence. Moreover, gas consistent with each of these two pictures may be present in different parts of the same halo.

Around dwarfs, a volume-filling warm-hot medium could be virialized gas or a hot wind. However, the star-formation rate surface density of the dwarf studied in this work is significantly lower than those of galaxies observed with fast, starburst outflows
(
T. M. Heckman et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
. Moreover, the Ne VIII non-detection disfavors a significant hot phase. We therefore consider whether the O VI could arise from a warm, virialized phase with temperature
T_{\rm vir}\approx 10^{5}
K. If in approximate pressure equilibrium with the cool, photoionized gas observed in the main component, a warm O VI-bearing phase would exhibit a density of
n_{\rm warm}\approx n_{\rm cool}T_{\rm PIE}/T_{\rm vir}\approx 4\times 10^{-5}\ \rm cm^{-3}
. This density is similar to the peak density for photoionized O VI adopted in Section
III.2
, indicating that photoionization likely plays a significant role, even if the O VI arises from warm, virialized gas.

If the O VI arises from a warm medium supported by a thermal pressure gradient, then thermal line-broadening is expected to dominate, leading to an expected O VI Doppler parameter of
b=10-15

{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, significantly smaller than observed. The Doppler-width of the O VI bearing CGM of
b=31

{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
is, therefore, dominated by non-thermal broadening, and it is comparable to the estimated virial velocity of the dwarf,
v_{\rm vir}\approx 44

{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, which corresponds to a Doppler width of
b\approx v_{\rm vir}\sqrt{2/3}=35

{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
. Without a clear detection of H I associated with the O VI-bearing CGM, the data cannot directly rule out a warm phase. However, if a significant fraction of the CGM exhibited both a temperature of
T\sim T_{\rm vir}
and non-thermal broadening comparable to the virial velocity, then this would imply that the CGM is out of virial equilibrium and likely unbound, which is disfavored by the modest star-formation rate of the dwarf. This suggests that turbulent pressure or bulk motion are more important than thermal pressure in the highly ionized dwarf CGM. In this case, both the intermediate ions and strong O VI would arise from cool, photoionized gas, and they need not be in thermal pressure equilibrium.

IV.3
Mass budget

The total mass in CGM probed by intermediate ions such as O IV can be estimated as
M\sim N({\rm H})\pi R_{h}^{2}\kappa(d<R_{\rm h})\mu m_{\rm p}

where
\kappa
is the covering fraction for similar absorption systems observed around dwarf galaxies of similar masses,
N({\rm H})
is the total hydrogen column inferred for the main component,
\mu
is the mean molecular weight, and
m_{\rm p}
is the proton mass. Adopting the total column for the main component,
N({\rm H})\approx 4\times 10^{18}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}
, and
\kappa(d<R_{\rm h})\approx 0.3
based on
N. Mishra et al. 2024
results in a mass estimate of

M\sim 2\times 10^{8}\left(\frac{N({\rm H})}{4\times 10^{18}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}}\right)\left(\frac{\kappa}{0.3}\right)\left(\frac{R_{\rm h}}{60\ \rm kpc}\right)^{2}\ \rm M_{\odot},

representing
\approx 5\%
of the dwarf galaxy’s baryon budget.
Overall, this intermediate ionization state CGM mass estimate is fairly consistent with recent upper limits by
Y. Faerman et al. 2025
based on H I observations and estimates by
Y. Zheng et al. 2024
based on total low and intermediate ionization state metal column densities. The intermediate ionization state mass estimate is a factor of ten lower than estimates of the mass in the highly ionized O VI-bearing phase
(
N. Mishra et al. 2024
;
S. Dutta et al. 2024a
)
. Despite being a small component of the baryon budget of the dwarf galaxy’s halo, the intermediate ionization state CGM exceeds the stellar mass of the galaxy by a factor of four and is comparable to the expected ISM H I mass
(
V. Parkash et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
.

IV.4
Chemical abundances and dwarf CGM origins

Oxygen is primarily produced by massive stars and ejected into the ISM when they explode as core-collapse supernovae shortly after star formation. On the other hand, a significant fraction of carbon and the overwhelming majority of nitrogen in the ISM arise from mass loss by evolved, low-mass stars in their giant phases,
\sim 10^{8}
years after star formation
(
C. Kobayashi et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. Consequently, the abundances of carbon and nitrogen relative to oxygen can serve as a diagnostic of the nucleosynthetic history of material in and around galaxies
(
B. M. Tinsley 1979
)
. For example, ionized absorption systems in the CGM/IGM at
z<1
sometimes exhibit super-solar [N/O] and [C/O], consistent with chemically evolved gas expected in the ISM of massive galaxies where oxygen rich core-collapse supernovae ejecta and carbon/nitrogen rich ejecta from giant stars are effectively mixed
(
F. S. Zahedy et al. 2021
;
S. Kumar et al. 2024
)
, but with metallicities lower than is observed in the ISM of massive galaxies. Such chemically evolved but modest metallicity gas can be readily explained if it is ejected from the ISM of Milky Way-like galaxies into the CGM and then mixes with more chemically pristine IGM/CGM to reduce overall metallicity without significantly changing the relative [C/O] and [N/O] abundances.

In contrast, the CGM of the dwarf galaxy in the field of HE 0439
-
5254 exhibits low relative abundances of
\rm[C/O]=-0.6
and
\rm[N/O]<-0.9
despite a relatively high metallicity of
\rm[O/H]=-0.6
, comparable to the metallicity estimated for the dwarf’s ISM. To interpret the likely origins of gas with this chemical signature, we collected measurements of [N/O] and [O/H] of H II regions of both massive galaxies and low-mass dwarfs
(
C. Esteban et al. 2009
;
D. A. Berg et al. 2012
;
L. S. Pilyugin et al. 2014
;
D. A. Berg et al. 2016
)
and compare them to the [N/O] abundance for the dwarf galaxy’s CGM in Figure
5
. The H II regions for dwarf galaxies generally exhibit relatively low metallicities of
{\rm[O/H]}<-0.5
and sub-solar nitrogen-to-oxygen ratios of
\rm[N/O]\approx-0.6
with an intrinsic scatter of
\approx 0.1
dex. This expected ISM [N/O] abundance is
0.3
dex above the [N/O] upper limit inferred for the dwarf’s CGM. The
\rm[N/O]<-0.9
limit for the dwarf’s CGM is consistent with the relative abundances of low-metallicity damped Ly
\alpha
absorbers
(
P. Petitjean et al. 2008
;
M. Pettini et al. 2008
, DLAs; e.g.,)
, which exhibit a mean ratio of
\rm[N/O]=-1.2
with
0.3
dex scatter
(
E. H. Nuñez et al. 2022
)
. The low [N/O] relative abundances of low-metallicity DLAs are often interpreted as being due to core-collapse supernovae ejecta relative abundances in gas that has not been significantly enriched in nitrogen from evolved, low-mass stars. Indeed, when integrated over the IMF, simulations of low-metallicity core-collapse supernovae
(
S. E. Woosley & T. A. Weaver 1995
;
K. Nomoto et al. 2013
;
M. Limongi & A. Chieffi 2018
)
suggest that their ejecta will exhibit abundance ratios in the range of
\rm-1.7<[N/O]<-0.9

(
J. W. Johnson et al. 2023
)
. The low nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio and relatively high oxygen metallicity of the CGM of the dwarf galaxy can therefore be explained if the gas arises from outflows driven by core-collapse supernovae, but without significant mixing with more nitrogen enriched ISM. Such limited mixing of a supernova outflow with the ISM suggests a low mass-loading factor, raising the possibility that feedback may be primarily energy mediated
(
C. Carr et al. 2023
;
G. M. Voit et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, though additional exploration of these models in the low-mass regime is needed. In this case, the oxygen rich core-collapse supernovae ejecta reaches the CGM without mixing significantly with more nitrogen rich ISM, and the moderate metallicity of the absorber can be explained by subsequent mixing with more pristine CGM/IGM. Alternatively, the oxygen enriched gas could have been ejected at significantly earlier times, before the ISM was enriched in nitrogen.

To our knowledge, the only other similarly low-mass halo with constraints on metallicity and relative abundances of ionized CGM arises near an interacting system of dwarfs discovered around a Lyman Limit System
(
H.-W. Chen et al. 2020
, LLS;)
. While the metallicity of the components in this LLS are similar to the dwarf CGM studied here,
F. S. Zahedy et al. 2021
found higher [N/O] abundance in one component, which may arise from more chemically mature ISM being stripped into the CGM during the dwarf
-
dwarf interaction. Together, these systems showcase the diverse origins of CGM in low-mass halos, which can be constrained with measurements of metallicity and relative abundances.

V
Summary and Conclusions

In this paper, we presented the first study of the physical conditions and multi-element abundances in ionized, absorbing CGM around a low-mass, isolated, star-forming dwarf galaxy intermediate redshift (
z=0.5723
), with a stellar mass of
M_{\star}\approx 5\times 10^{7}\ {\rm M_{\odot}}
in the field of a bright background quasar HE 0439
-
5254, with archival UV spectra from
Hubble
. The dwarf galaxy exhibits inferred star-formation rates and ISM metallicity consistent with expectations for galaxies of similar mass. Deep and highly complete galaxy redshift surveys in the field demonstrate that the dwarf resides in an isolated environment, more than 2 Mpc from the nearest luminous galaxy.

The CGM of the dwarf is detected in absorption from H I and multiple intermediate metal ions (O III, O IV, C III) as well as kinematically broader, highly ionized O VI. Further, the data enable stringent upper limits on nitrogen ions and Ne VIII. Photoionization analysis of the intermediate ionization state gas indicates that it is consistent with arising in cool (
{\sim}10^{4}
K) CGM with a modest volume-filling factor of
\sim 6\%
along the sightline and a total mass that is comparable to the ISM H I mass of similar dwarf galaxies, but substantially less than the mass in the highly ionized CGM phase detected in strong O VI. The virial temperature of the dwarf galaxy is below levels required to produce significant O VI absorption via collisional ionization, suggesting that photoionization by the UV background plays a significant role. In this scenario, the highly ionized CGM is approximately volume filling and may be warm, virialized gas in approximate gas pressure equilibrium with the gas detected in intermediate ions. However, the kinematics of the O VI absorption are comparable to the virial velocity, suggesting that bulk motion or turbulence dominate over thermal pressure. This suggests the intermediate ionization state absorption and highly ionized O VI could both arise from cool, photoionized gas tracing different parts of a broad, lognormal density distribution predicted in turbulent CGM
(
J. Stern et al. 2021
;
A. Kakoly et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

The H I and intermediate ion measurements enable new insights into the chemical composition of the dwarf’s CGM, which exhibits an inferred oxygen metallicity of
\rm[O/H]\approx-0.6
, comparable to the metallicity estimated for ISM of the dwarf galaxy. However, the inferred relative nitrogen-to-oxygen abundance of
\rm[N/O]<-0.9
is well below levels expected from the dwarf’s current ISM but consistent with expectations from core-collapse supernovae ejecta. This suggests that the oxygen enriched CGM escaped from the dwarf without significant mixing with ISM enriched in nitrogen from evolved, low-mass stars.

Forced oscillation of a damped BBM equation posed on whole line in low regularity spaces
Chun-Ho Lau

Abstract
In this manuscript, we would established in low regularity spaces H ℓ , ℓ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) H^{\ell},\ell\in[0,1) , the existence and stability results of time-periodic solution of 1D Cauchy problem of forced damped Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation (BBM). We use estimates from I-energy method to derive needed estimates in H ℓ H^{\ell} for the linearized problem, then convection term will be treated as perturbation of linear problem such that original Cauchy problem is solved. Mathematical Subject Classification 2010: 35D05, 35K55, 34K13, 35Q93. Keywords : I-energy method; low regulairty space; stability

Introduction

In this manuscript, we would study the existence and stability of general temporal-periodic solutions to Cauchy problem of a weakly damped Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation (regularized long-wave equation or BBM equation) posed on whole line:

u_{t}-u_{xxt}+u-u_{xx}+uu_{x}=f,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

(1.1)

prescribed with initial data
u(x,0)=\phi(x)
. The well-posedness theory such as local well-posedness (LWP) and global well-posedness (GWP) of this type of equation corresponding to particularly shaped initial data on whole line and torus were discussed by J. Bona and H. Chen in
[
4
,
11
]
. Added to BBM equation, terms
-u_{xx}
and/or zero-order damped term
u
appear on the left side of the equation and play key roles in regularizing and stabilizing. The external force
f
is applied at each point of fluid media and must be input with small amplitude, hence it will be
f(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
through the entire paper, and in our experiment setting, we would like this force being temporal-periodic, thus solution
u
generated turns out to be periodic in time as one of the results. The damping is quite important determining the stability of solution. In PDEs such as KdV (Korteweg-de Vries) and parabolic equations, the existent damping guarantees a stabilized analytic semigroup (
[
5
,
26
,
15
]
follow this mechanism to obtain results for unbounded and bounded domains, respectively); J. Bona and J. Wu also analyzed the particular damping effects of adding regularizing terms such as
-u_{xx}
or
u
in
[
8
]
for KdV. For BBM equation on whole line, similar damping mechanism to stabilizing was obtained (see e.g.
[
1
]
by C. Amick, J. Bona and M. Shonbek).

We would pursue those properties of the general solutions of BBM equation in
H^{\ell},\ell\in[0,1),
given
\phi\in H^{\ell},f\in L^{2}
. Such framework of solution theory of KdV equations could be even extended to be posed in negative indexed Sobolev spaces or Bourgain spaces were inspired by seminal work quantifying this methodology by J. Bourgain
[
10

[

]

[
9
,
28
]
). In this article, generality of solutions is referred to those in below-energy or low-regularity space with positive indices (
H^{\ell}
with
\ell\in[0,1)
which is below
H^{1}
and rough). Regularity can not be lower in negative indices due to the finding of sharp regularity in
[
7
]
by J. Bona and N. Tzvetkov. Solution theory in

has been established for decades since mathematical theory were discussed along with KdV and BBM equations in
[

]

technically to construct an

-equivalent norm defined via operator

[
13
,

]

I_{N}u
then satisfies the PDE which can be approached by classical energy method used in

[

Seen from works

, the linear problem of KdV equation can be solved by formulating mild solution, then bilinear estimates developed leads to the estimate of convection term

thus the solvability of nonlinear problem. In current manuscript, this methodology brings results in function spaces

2
Main results

Notation
\|\cdot\|_{X}
denotes the endowed canonical norm of a specific Banach space
X
, and
\|\cdot\|
denotes norm of square-integrable space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
\widehat{\cdot}
represents Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}\cdot
defined in frequency space as a function of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the classical inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In the context of flow dynamics, we would like to use ODE fashion such as
u(t)
or
\|u(t)\|_{X}
to present the evolution along increasing time.

We will construct solution related to function space close to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
on
t\in[\tau,\tau+T]
:

Y^{1}_{\tau,T}=\left\{u\big|\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+T}(\|u(s)\|+\|u_{x}(s)\|)+\left(\int_{\tau}^{t+T}(\|u(s)\|^{2}+\|u_{x}(s)\|^{2})ds\right)^{1\over 2}<\infty\right\}

with norm

\|u\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}=\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+T}(\|u(s)\|+\|u_{x}(s)\|)+\left(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+T}(\|u(s)\|^{2}+\|u_{x}(s)\|^{2})ds\right)^{1\over 2}.

Likewise, we also can define function space
Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}
related to
H^{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
by norm

\|u\|_{Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}}=\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+T}\|u(s)\|_{H^{\ell}}+\left(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+T}\|u(s)\|^{2}_{H^{\ell}}ds\right)^{1\over 2}

with
\ell\in[0,1)
.

Before we state our results in this manuscript, we would distinguish two types of stability of solution in the following definitions:

Definition 2.1

Regarding to dynamics of differential equations, we say solution
\tilde{u}(t)
generated by any initial data
u_{0}\in X
is locally stable, if
u(t)
converges to
\tilde{u}
in
X
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, when
u_{0}
is sufficiently close to
\tilde{u}
.

Definition 2.2

We say
\tilde{u}(t)
is globally stable, if
u(t)
generated by any initial data
u_{0}\in X
converges to
\tilde{u}
in
X
no matter how far
u_{0}
is from
\tilde{u}
.

Our main results for weekly damped BBM equation are presented right now. First, there is local-well-posedness:

Theorem 2.3

Given
\tau\geq 0,T>0,\phi\in H^{\ell},f\in L^{2}
for
\ell\in[0,1)
, if there exists a constant
\delta>0
and holds smallness condition
\|\phi\|_{H^{\ell}}+\sup_{t\geq 0}\|f(t)\|\leq\delta
, there holds local well-posedness of solution
u
to Cauchy problem (
1.1
) where
u
satisfies

\|u\|_{Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{\ell}}+\sup_{t\geq 0}\|f(t)\|).

(2.1)

Thanks to local well-posedness, we can obtain the temporal-periodic solution
\tilde{u}(x,t)
and its stability of solution according to proposed Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
summarized in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.4

If conditions in Theorem
2.3
hold plus
f
is time-periodic with period
\theta
, there exists an initial condition
\tilde{\phi}\in H^{\ell}
generating unique periodic solution
\tilde{u}\in H^{\ell}
with period
\theta
.

Moreover, if
\delta
is sufficiently small, there exist
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma_{0}(N_{0})>0
such that the periodic solution possesses local stability with an exponential decay rate
2\gamma_{0}>0
.

Essentially, for any size of initial value
\phi
, if
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|f(t)\|<\delta,
one can find
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma_{1}(N_{1})>0
, it holds the global stability of solution with positive decay rate
2\gamma_{1}>0
.

3
Preliminaries

We aim to fit the I-method result to obtain solution theory in
H^{\ell},\ell\in[0,1)
. I-method would be called by defining the I operator. Given an
N>0
, let smooth function
m_{N}
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be

\displaystyle m_{N}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,\,\,|\xi|\leq N,\\
\\
{|\xi|^{s-1}\over N^{s-1}},\,\,|\xi|\geq 2N.\end{cases}

Define operator
I_{N}
for fixed
N>0
:
H^{\ell}\mapsto H^{1}
such that
\widehat{I_{N}g}(\xi)=m_{N}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi)
.

There holds a key lemma which discloses the link between function spaces of
I_{N}g
and
g
.

Lemma 3.1

It holds for an
\ell\in[0,1)
and
g\in H^{\ell}
that

\|I_{N}g\|_{H^{1}}\sim\|g\|_{H^{\ell}}.

In particular,
C\|g\|_{H^{\ell}}\leq\|I_{N}g\|_{H^{1}}\leq C{N^{1-\ell}}\|g\|_{H^{\ell}}
, where
C
doesn’t reply on
N
.

The lemma can be found in many previous seminal papers applying I-method originally in
[
13
,
14
]
by Colliander et al on global solutions of KdV equations, and followed by
[
7
]
by J. Bona and N. Tzvetkov and then
[
24
,
25
]
by M. Wang on damped BBM equation on whole line and torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Equation (
1.1
) is split into two problems, that is
u=v+z
respectively governed by the following two systems:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}-v_{txx}+v-v_{xx}=f,\\
v(x,0)=\phi(x),\end{cases}

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}z_{t}-z_{txx}+z-z_{xx}+zz_{x}+(vz)_{x}=-vv_{x},\\
z(x,0)=0.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Lemma 3.2

For linear Cauchy problem (
3.1
), given
\tau\geq 0,T>0,\phi\in H^{\ell}
, and
f\in L^{2}
, then
v\in Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in{[\tau,\tau+T]}}\|v(t)\|_{H^{\ell}}\leq\|\phi\|_{H^{\ell}}+C\sup_{t\in{[\tau,\tau+T]}}\|f(t)\|,

and

\displaystyle\|v\|_{Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{\ell}}+C\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau+T]}\|f(t)\|.

Proof.
Fixing any
N>0
and applying
I_{N}
on both sides of (
3.1
), one obtains

\displaystyle\partial_{t}I_{N}v-\partial_{t}I_{N}v_{xx}+I_{N}v-I_{N}v_{xx}=I_{N}f.

Conduct energy estimate by multiplying
I_{N}v
and take integral on
\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle{d\over dt}(\|I_{N}v\|^{2}+\|\partial_{x}I_{N}v\|^{2})+2(\|I_{N}v\|^{2}+\|\partial_{x}I_{N}v\|^{2})=(I_{N}f,I_{N}v),

where we have used fact that
(\partial_{xx}I_{N}u,I_{N}u)=(\widehat{\partial_{xx}I_{N}u},\widehat{I_{N}u})=(I_{N}\partial_{xx}u,I_{N}u)
.

Integration factor method of solving ODE leads to

\displaystyle\|I_{N}v(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=e^{-2t}\|I_{N}\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{-2(t-s)}\|I_{N}f(s)\|^{2}ds.

By Lemma
3.1
, we reach the
\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau+T]}\|v(t)\|_{H^{\ell}}
. Note that

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\|I_{N}v(s)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}ds=\|I_{N}v(0)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}-\|I_{N}v(T)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+2\int_{0}^{T}(I_{N}f(s),I_{N}v(s))ds.

The previous estimate and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality lead to the estimate of
\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}
thus that of
\|v\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}
.

\Box

To handle the nonlinear problem (
3.2
), we need to the following bilinear estimate to treat nonlinear term as perturbation of linear problem:

Lemma 3.3

Given
T>0
, there holds bilinear estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}I_{N}(uv_{x})(s)\|_{H^{1}}ds\leq C_{T}\|I_{N}u\|_{Y_{0,T}}\|I_{N}v\|_{Y_{0,T}}

(3.3)

where the constant
C_{T}\sim T
, independent of
N
.

Proof.
A related lemma can be found in
[
7
,
24
]
in which

\|(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}I_{N}(uv)_{x}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|I_{N}u\|_{H^{1}}\|I_{N}v\|_{H^{1}}.

Via Lemma
3.1
and taking integration over
[0,T]
, we obtain the desired result.

\Box

Lemma 3.4

There holds trilinear estimate for inner product involving
u,v,w\in H^{\ell}
:

|(I_{N}(uv)_{x},I_{N}w)|\leq CN^{-{3\over 2}+}\|I_{N}u\|_{H^{1}}\|I_{N}v\|_{H^{1}}\|I_{N}w\|_{H^{1}},

(3.4)

where
C
doesn’t rely on
N
.

Proof.
This is a version of such trilinear estimates obtained in Lemma 2.6 in M. Wang
[
24
]
and
[
7
]
, whose proof is analogous to result therein

|(I_{N}(w^{2})_{x},I_{N}w)|\lesssim N^{-{3\over 2}+}\|I_{N}w\|^{3}_{H^{1}}.

\Box

From equation (
3.2
), one can infer

\displaystyle z_{t}+z+(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}(zz_{x}+(zv)_{x})=-(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}vv_{x},

whence if applying
I_{N}

\displaystyle I_{N}z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}I_{N}(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}(zz_{x}+(zv)_{x}+vv_{x})(s)ds.

(3.5)

Let

S_{v}=\{I_{N}z\in Y^{1}_{0,T}|\|I_{N}z\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\},

and mapping
G:q\mapsto G(q)
defined as

G(q;v)=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}I_{N}(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}(qq_{x}+(qv)_{x}+vv_{x})ds,\,\,t\leq T.

We aim to use Fixed Point Theorem in
S_{v}
to show the existence of solution
q=I_{N}z\in S_{v}
to the integration equation (
3.5
). Following the fashion in
[
26
,
20
]
along with bilinear estimate Lemma
3.3
, we can find that if
\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}
is sufficiently small:
\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\lesssim{1\over C_{T}}
, then for
q,\tilde{q}\in S_{v}
, there hold

\displaystyle\|G(q)\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}},

\displaystyle\|G(q)-G(\tilde{q})\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq{1\over 2}\|I_{N}q-I_{N}\tilde{q}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}},

to conclude the fixed point argument confirming
I_{N}z\in S_{v}
. If
\tau>0
, and we consider mapping
\tilde{G}
:

\tilde{G}(q;v)=-\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}I_{N}(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}(qq_{x}+(qv)_{x}+vv_{x})ds,

let bound
M=2(\|I_{N}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}+\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}})
and set
\tilde{S}_{M}=\{I_{N}z\in Y^{1}_{0,T}|\|I_{N}z\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq 2\|I_{N}z(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}+\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\},
similar argument via Fixed Point Theorem of
\tilde{G}
can lead to existence of
I_{N}z
satisfying

\|I_{N}z\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}\leq 2(\|I_{N}z(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}+\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}).

Therefore, we obtain

Lemma 3.5

Problem (
3.2
) admits a solution
I_{N}z\in Y^{1}_{0,T}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|I_{N}z\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq C\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}.

(3.6)

If
\tau>0
, on
[\tau,\tau+T]
,

\displaystyle\|I_{N}z\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}\leq 2\|I_{N}z\|_{H^{1}}+2\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}.

(3.7)

Lemma 3.6

If
\tau>0
,
\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq\delta^{\prime}
, then there holds

\displaystyle\|I_{N}z\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}\leq\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}.

(3.8)

Proof.
From mild solution form defined on
[\tau,\tau+t]
, one writes

\displaystyle I_{N}z(\tau+t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-t}I_{N}z(\tau)-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t}e^{-(\tau+t-s)}(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}I_{N}(zz_{x}+zv_{x}+z_{x}v)ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t}e^{-(\tau+t-s)}(I-\partial_{xx})^{-1}I_{N}vv_{x}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W(t)z(\tau)+P(\tau+t,\tau,z(\tau),v)+Q(\tau+t,\tau,v)

On mesh
\{T,2T,3T,\cdots,kT,\cdots\}
with general
k\in\mathbb{N}
and define
I_{N}z_{k}=I_{N}z(kT)
, we have

\displaystyle I_{N}z_{k}=W(T)I_{N}z_{k-1}+P(kT,(k-1)T,I_{N}z_{k-1},I_{N}v)+Q(kT,(k-1)T,I_{N}v).

Applying Lemma
3.3
and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\|I_{N}z_{k}\|_{H^{1}}\leq(e^{-T}+C_{T}\|I_{N}z_{k-1}\|)\|I_{N}z_{k-1}\|_{H^{1}}+C_{T}\|I_{N}v\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

Owing to Lemma
3.5
,
I_{N}z
is sufficiently small in
Y^{1}_{\tau,T}
as
\|I_{N}v\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}<\delta^{\prime}
for any
\tau>0
. Denote
\zeta=e^{-T}+C_{T}\delta^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\|I_{N}z_{k}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\|I_{n}z_{k-1}\|+C_{T}\|I_{N}v\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{(k-1)T,T}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta^{2}\|I_{N}z_{k-2}\|_{H^{1}}+C_{T}\|I_{N}v\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{(k-1)T,T}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta^{k}C_{T}\|I_{N}v\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{(k-1)T,T}}

as
z_{0}\equiv 0
.

Relying on the above result of
I_{N}z_{k}
and estimate (
3.7
),
\|I_{N}\|_{kT,T}
is proved immediately. Any
\tau\neq kT
also holds same estimate using the argument in Lemma 3.4 of
[
26
]
, and we complete the proof.

\Box

To approach the stability of desired solution, we need a decay property of error
w
to the BBM equation. This attribute leads to asymptotic periodicity of any non-periodic solution. From dynamics of PDEs, this asymptotic time-periodicity is caused by force
f
specified to be time-periodic. The error equation is proposed on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{t}-w_{xxt}+w-w_{xx}+(aw)_{x}=0,\\
w(x,0)=\psi(x).\end{cases}

(3.9)

In it, the reference
a
is a function of
(x,t)
, satisfying certain type of smallness.

Lemma 3.7

If
\|I_{N}a\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}
is bounded for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
N
can be selected to be
N_{0}
so large that a constant
\gamma_{0}=1-CN_{0}^{-{3\over 2}+}\|I_{N}a\|_{H^{1}}>0
, then
\|w\|_{Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}}\lesssim e^{-2\gamma_{0}\tau}
.

Proof.
Applying
I_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}I_{N}w-\partial_{t}I_{N}w_{xx}+I_{N}w-I_{N}w_{xx}+I_{N}(aw)_{x}=0,

whence
L^{2}
energy identity gives
H^{1}
-estimate

\displaystyle{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\|I_{N}w(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|I_{N}w(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+(I_{N}(aw)_{x},I_{N}w)=0.

From Lemma
3.4
, there holds a trilinear estimate

|(I_{N}(aw)_{x},I_{N}w)|\lesssim N^{-{3\over 2}+}\|I_{N}w\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\|I_{N}a\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus if
N=N_{0}
being so large that
\gamma_{0}=1-CN_{0}^{-{3\over 2}+}\|I_{N_{0}}a\|_{H^{1}}>0
, then exponential decay follows as

\|I_{N}w(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq e^{-2\gamma_{0}t}\|I_{N}\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

for any
t>0
. On the other term of
\|I_{N}w\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}
with
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle 2\int_{\tau}^{\tau+T}\|I_{N}w(s)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\|I_{N}w(\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|I_{N}w(\tau+T)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+2CN^{-{3\over 2}+}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+T}\|I_{N}a(s)\|_{H^{1}}\|I_{N}w(s)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}ds.

Owing to
N=N_{0}
and
\gamma_{0}=1-CN_{0}^{-{3\over 2}+}\|I_{N_{0}}a\|_{H^{1}}>0
, integration term on the right side is absorbed to the left, therefore combining with estimate of
\|I_{N}w(t)\|_{H^{1}}
just obtained, we can infer the result owing to Lemma
3.1
with decay rate
2\gamma_{0}
.

\Box

.
Given

, owing to Lemmas
3.2
and

, we obtain the sum
I_{N}u\in Y^{1}_{\tau,T}
thus

by Lemma

.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.
First, we would prove the existence of periodic solution. Note that the local well-posedness has been proved by Theorem
2.3
.

In this part, we will prove asymptotic periodicity, i.e.,
u
generated by any
\phi
is asymptotically time-periodic. Let
w=u(t+\theta,x)-u(t,x)
, then
w
exactly follows (
3.9
) with
\psi(x)=u(\theta,x)-\phi(x),a={1\over 2}(u(t,x)+u(t+\theta,x))
. If
\delta
is sufficiently small,
\|I_{N}a\|_{H^{1}}
possesses smallness such that
\gamma_{0}>0
. Lemma
3.7
explicitly show the asymptotic periodicity as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
:

\|u(\cdot+\theta)-u(\cdot)\|_{Y^{\ell}_{\tau,T}}\lesssim e^{-2\gamma_{1}\tau}.

If
u_{k}=u(k\theta,x),k\in\mathbb{N}
, one can show that sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
is of Cauchy in
H^{\ell}
. Passing to limit,
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{k}=\tilde{\phi}
which is unique and we extract in next step as the very initial data to generate precisely periodic solution
\tilde{u}\in H^{\ell}
. It is noteworthy to point out that detailed fashion in
[

Cluster automorphism group of braid varieties
Soyeon Kim

Abstract
. The cluster automorphism group of a cluster variety was defined by Gekhtman–Shapiro–Vainshtein [ 18 ] , and later studied by Lam–Speyer [ 13 ] . Braid varieties are interesting affine algebraic varieties indexed by positive braid words. It was proved recently that braid varieties are cluster varieties. In this paper, we propose an explicit description of the cluster automorphism group and its action on braid varieties, and provide several computed examples.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we describe the cluster automorphism group of braid varieties.
A cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
, introduced by Fomin–Zelevinsky
[
7
]
, is a special commutative algebra with a set of distinguished generators. Given initial data of a
labeled seed

(\textbf{x},Q)
where
x
denotes the tuple of
m
distinguished generators called the
cluster variables
along with the
exchange matrix

\tilde{B}(Q)
from a special directed graph
Q
(called the quiver), one uses an iterative procedure called the
mutation
to produce the rest of the cluster variables. See Section
2.2
for more precise definition of cluster algebras and related notions.

In recent years, braid varieties have been a topic of interest due to their wide range of connection to
Khovanov–Rozansky link homology

[
11
]
and
exact Lagrangian fillings and Legendrian links

[
2
]
. Braid varieties generalize well-known Lie theoretic varieties, such as
Grassmannian
,
positroid varieties
,
open Richardson varieties
and
double Bott–Samelson varieties
; see
[
3
,
10
,
19
,
14
,
15
]
. Braid variety
X_{u,\bm{\beta}}
is indexed by a pair of permutation
u\in S_{n}
and a positive braid word
\beta=\sigma_{i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}\in Br_{n}^{+}
, see Definition
2.2
. It is an affine algebraic variety defined by an explicit system of polynomial equations. Geometrically, it is described as a configuration of flags

X_{u,\bm{\beta}}=\left\{(F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{k})\in(G/B_{+})^{k}:B_{+}\xrightarrow{s_{i_{1}}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{s_{i_{2}}}F_{2}\to\ldots\xrightarrow{s_{i_{k}}}F_{k}\xleftarrow{w_{0}u}B_{-}\right\},

where
G=GL(n,\mathbb{C})
and
B_{+}
(resp.
B_{-}
) denotes the Borel subgroup (resp. opposite Borel subgroup) of
G
.

By the recent work of
[
2
,
12
,
17
]
, it is known that the coordinate ring of a braid variety admits a cluster algebra structure.
In this paper, we follow the cluster structure construction in
[
17
]
. We use notation
Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}
to denote the (opposite) quiver from a braid variety cluster structure of
[
17
]
, see Definition
2.18
and Remark
2.22
. In our instance, certain subset of vertices in a quiver are declared to be
frozen
, whereas the remaining vertices are declared to be
mutable
. Then the exchange matrix
\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})
is a rectangular matrix of size
{m\times(m+f)}
where
m
and
f
denotes the number of mutable and frozen vertices in a quiver respectively. By the
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
, we also know that
Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}
is a
really full rank
. Our first main result is the following. We extend
\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})
matrix to the
square

(m+f)\times(m+f)
matrix
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
, defined in Definition
3.4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The matrix
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
has all integer entries, and

\det\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}=(-1)^{m+f}
.

As a side remark, the construction of our matrix
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
is indeed motivated by the matrix
(p_{i,j})
defined in
[
2
, Section 8]
. The combination of
[
2
, Lemma 8.1]
and Theorem
3.7
implies that
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
give an initial seed of the cluster Poisson structure of
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
. As implied by the forthcoming works, see
[
1
,
4
]
, extended exchange matrices from a braid variety quivers in
[
2
]
and
[
17
]
are the same, thus the matrix
(p_{i,j}(\mathfrak{M}))_{1\leq i,j\leq m+f}
considered in
[
2
, Section 8.2]
and
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
matrix construction are essentially the same.

However, the key new things are the following. First, our matrix construction is adapted and translated to the language of 3D plabic graphs. Our matrix has advantage in computation in practice. We can use Galashin’s program
[
9
]
when computing lots of examples, see Section
4.2
. Lastly, we emphasize that the matrix
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
is invertible and moreover we obtain an
explicit inductive factorization
of the inverse of
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
, see Lemma
4.5
. For convenience, we denote
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
to be its inverse. This
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
matrix applies to the study of cluster automorphism group of braid varieties, which will be our main application.

The
cluster automorphism group

\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
is the collection of algebra automorphisms that scales the cluster variables in the corresponding cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
, see Definition
4.1
for more precise definition. Note that our cluster automorphism group differs from the cluster modular group
[
20
]
. Cluster automorphism groups was first studied by Gekhtman–Shapiro–Vainshtein in
[
18
]
and later by Lam–Speyer in
[
13
]
. Recently, it appeared in the study of
cluster deep loci
and
no mysterious point conjecture
in
[
5
]
.

Applying Proposition 5.1 of
[
13
]
along with the fact that
Q_{u,\beta}
has
really full rank
, we know that
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
is an algebraic torus of dimension
f

and the action of
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
is described via the basis of
\ker{\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})}
. By Lemma
4.2
, the matrix
\tilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})
is the free abelian group of rank
f
generated by vectors
({col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}))_{m+1\leq j\leq m+f}
where
{col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}})
denotes the
j
th column vector of
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
.
Combining all this, we describe how cluster automorphism group action acts on braid varieties, using the inductive factorization of
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
matrix as follows.

Corollary 1.2
.

For
m+1\leq j\leq m+f
,
suppose that
{col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}})=(a_{1,m+j},\ldots,a_{m+f,m+j})
.
Then
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
is an algebraic torus with coordinates
t_{1},\ldots,t_{f}
which acts on
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
by

x_{i}\to\prod t_{j}^{a_{i,m+j}}\cdot x_{i},

where
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+f})
be an initial seed of
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
.

Lastly, in Section
4.2
, we give many examples of explicit computations of the matrix
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
and the cluster automorphism group actions.
In many cases, we observe a curious phenomenon that all entries of
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
have the same sign (either negative or zero). We call this
sign phenomenon
. We list some of the supporting examples and counterexample of this phenomenon in Section
4.2
. It would be interesting to understand the geometric meaning of this phenomenon. In line with this inquiry, we propose the following problem.

Problem 1.3
.

Give an explicit combinatorial description for the non-zero entries of
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
.

Given a positive
n
-strand braid word
\beta
, we have an explicit description of the action of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}
on braid varieties, see
[
3
]
for more details. This action is given by cluster automorphisms, thus we have a homomorphism
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}\to\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
. This homomorphism is surjective for
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
when
u=id
, but not in general. In examples in Section
4.2
, we see that
f
could be larger than
n-1
, so the cluster automorphism group has higher dimension than
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n-1}
in general. This leads to the following question.

Problem 1.4
.

Describe the action of the cluster automorphism group
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{f}
on the braid variety in terms of flags.

2.
Background

2.1.
Braid varieties

We fix some notations. Let
I=[n-1]
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The symmetric group
S_{n}
is generated by simple transpositions
s_{i}=(i\ i+1)
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Definition 2.1
.

The braid group
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
on
n
strands is generated by Artin generators
\sigma_{i}
and braid relations

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},\quad\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}.

An element
\beta\in\mathrm{Br}_{n}
is called a positive braid if it is a product of Artin generators, allowing no inverses. Denote
\mathrm{Br}_{n}^{+}
to be the collection of all positive braids in
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
.

Suppose that we are given with a specific braid representative
\sigma_{i_{1}}\sigma_{i_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}
of
\beta
. This representative can be abbreviated by a
braid word

\bm{\beta}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})\in I^{k}
.
We define a matrix
B_{i_{l}}(z)
matrix by

B_{i_{l}}(z_{l})=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&&&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&z_{l}&-1&\cdots&0\\
0&\cdots&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&&&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix},

where the non-identity part of
B_{i_{l}}(z)
is located in the
i_{l}
-th and
(i_{l}+1)
-st row and columns.
Then the braid matrix
B_{\bm{\beta}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in\text{GL}\left(n,\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}]\right)
associated to
\bm{\beta}
is defined as a product

B_{\bm{\beta}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})=B_{i_{1}}(z_{1})\cdots B_{i_{k}}(z_{k}).

Let
u\in S_{n}
be a permutation with a reduced word
u=s_{j_{1}}s_{j_{2}}\cdots s_{j_{k^{\prime}}}
. Then
u
is lifted to a positive braid (called the positive braid lift)
\beta(u)=\sigma_{j_{1}}\sigma_{j_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{j_{k^{\prime}}}
. We denote by
w_{0}
the longest element in
S_{n},

w_{0}(i)=n+1-i
. We denote
s=r+\binom{n}{2}-\ell(u)=\ell(\beta)+\ell(u^{-1}w_{0})
. Now we are ready to define a braid variety associated to
u\in S_{n}
and
\bm{\beta}\in Br^{+}_{n}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
3
,
17
]
)
.

The braid variety
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
associated to
u\in S_{n}
and the specific braid word
\bm{\beta}
as above is the affine closed subvariety of
\mathbb{C}^{s}
given by

X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}:=\left\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}):w_{0}^{-1}B_{\bm{\beta}\cdot\beta(u^{-1}w_{0})}\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\right)\mbox{ is an upper-triangular matrix}\right\}.

Remark 2.3
.

The braid variety
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
is invariant under braid relations, up to some change of variables.
The braid variety
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
is not empty if and only if
\beta
contains a reduced expression for
u
as a subword.
If it is nonempty, it is a smooth complex manifold of dimension

r-\ell(u)=s-\binom{n}{2}
.

2.2.
Cluster algebra

We start by recalling the definition of a quiver, following
[
13
,
16
]
. An quiver
Q
is a directed graph with a finite vertex set
V
, where we allow multiple arrows between vertices but does not allow directed cycles of length one or two. We declare that
Q
is an ice quiver, meaning that the vertices
1,2,\ldots,m
in
Q
are designated as
mutable
whereas the remaining vertices
m+1,\ldots,m+f
in
Q
are designated as
frozen
. We use
m
(resp.
f
) to denote the number of mutables (resp. frozens) in
Q
. Now we fix
\mathcal{F}
to be an
ambient field
of rational functions in
m
independent variables over
\mathbb{C}(x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{m+f})
. Then
labeled seed
in
\mathcal{F}
is defined to be a pair
(\textbf{x},Q)
, where
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+f})
forms a free generating set for
\mathcal{F}
and
Q
is an ice quiver as before.

Definition 2.4
.

[
13
]

Let us fix
Q
as above. Then an
extended exchange matrix
associated to
Q
is defined by

(1)

\widetilde{B}(Q)=\left(b_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i\leq m+f,1\leq j\leq m},

where

b_{i,j}=\begin{cases}r,&\text{if there are $r$ arrows from $i$ to $j$}\\
-r,&\text{if there are $r$ arrows from $j$ to $i$}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.5
.

Note that for future usage, this matrix is transposed to what is usually considered in the literature, for example, as in
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
k
be a mutable vertex in a quiver
Q
. Then quiver mutation
\mu_{k}
transforms
Q
into a new quiver
\mu_{k}(Q)
via the following steps:

(1)

To every directed two pair
i\to k\to j
, introduce new arrow
i\to j
.

(2)

Reverse all arrows adjacent to a vertex
k
.

(3)

Remove any maximal collection of disjoint
2
-cycles.

Definition 2.7
.

[
7
]

Let
(\textbf{x},Q)
be a labeled seed in
\mathcal{F}
as before, and pick
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. The
seed mutation

\mu_{k}
is a local operation performed at the vertex
k
of
Q
that transforms an labeled seed
(\textbf{x},Q)
into another labeled seed
(\textbf{x}^{\prime},\mu_{k}(Q)),
where
\textbf{x}^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m}^{\prime})
is defined as follows:

•

x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}
for all
j\neq k
.

•

x_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
is determined by the
exchange relation

x_{k}^{\prime}\cdot x_{k}=\underset{\text{$\alpha$: $s(\alpha)=k$}}{\Pi}x_{t(\alpha)}+\underset{\text{$\alpha^{\prime}$: $t(\alpha)=k$}}{\Pi}x_{s(\alpha^{\prime})}.

Here,
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are arrows in
Q
where
s(\alpha)
denotes the source of
\alpha
and
t(\alpha)
denotes a target of
\alpha
.

To obtain a cluster algebra from an initial labeled seed
(\textbf{x},Q)
, we need to apply mutations to every mutable vertices repeatedly to obtain all possible cluster variables.

Definition 2.8
.

(
[
7
]
)
The cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(Q)
associated to
Q
is the subalgebra of the ring
\mathbb{C}(\textbf{x})

generated as a ring by cluster variables together with
\{x_{i}^{-1}\}_{m+1\leq i\leq m+f}
where the set of (generally infinitely many) cluster variables is constructed from the data
(\textbf{x},Q)
using mutation operation above.

Recent discoveries by separate group of authors (see
[
2
]
and
[
17
]
) show that the coordinate ring of a braid variety admits a cluster structure. Two distinct combinatorial models called the Demazure weaves and 3D plabic graphs provide seeds on braid varieties. As we will see from Definition
2.18
, we denote the quiver from 3D plabic graph
G_{u,\bm{\beta}}
by
Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}
.

2.3.
3D plabic graph

We recall the construction of a 3D plabic graph, following
[
17
]
. Note that the original terminology of 3D plabic graph refers to a graph drawn in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, though in this paper, 3D plabic graph refers to its “red projection” on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; see
[
17

.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\bm{\beta}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in I^{k}
be a specific given braid word representative of
\beta
. Suppose that
v\in S_{n}
is a permutation. Then the
Demazure quotient
associated to
v
and
i_{l}
is defined by

v\lhd s_{i_{l}}:=\begin{cases}vs_{i_{l}}&\text{if $\ell(vs_{i_{l}})<\ell(v)$},\\
v&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\ell(v)
denotes the length of a reduced word of
v
. We define
the sequence of permutations
u_{j}
recursively by setting
u_{k}:=u
and

u_{j-1}:=u_{j}\lhd s_{i_{j}},

where
1\leq j\leq k
. With this definition of the sequence of permutations
u_{j}
in mind, let us define the set
J_{u,\bm{\beta}}:=\{l\in[k]:u_{l}=u_{l-1}\}
.

One can prove (e.g.
[
17
]
) that the letters with indices not in
J_{u,\bm{\beta}}
produce the positive distinguished subexpression for

inside
\beta
, see
[
6
]
. Note also that
u
is a subword of
\beta
if and only if
u_{0}=1
. Let us fix
u
and
\beta
such that some reduced word for

is a subword for
\beta
.

.

Let
\bm{\beta}
be a given specific braid word and

is a subword of

is a projection of a graph on

onto

, drawn by the following procedure:

(a)

Put
n
strands starting from the leftmost border. For these

regions on the leftmost border, we call them
boundary regions
.

(b)

Now we start from
j=1
to
j=k
. For
j\in J_{u,\bm{\beta}}
, draw a
bridge

d
between the
i_{j}
th strand and the
(i_{j}+1)
th strand. See Figure (right) below. Otherwise, draw a
positive crossing
between the
i_{j}
th strand and the

Definition 2.13
.

Given a

and a bridge
d

soap film

C_{d}
associated to a bridge

(1)

The soap film

starts at a bridge

(2)

There are seven possibilities for how the soap film can appear immediately to the right of any bridge. The Figure

(3)

The soap film is a region bounded by the edges in

Figure 1.
A soap film can be thought of as

In short, if a soap film is going over the top strand, or under the bottom strands when it reaches the bridges, then a soap film does not change. In other circumstances, a soap film is getting cut.

Remark 2.14
.

To clarify, if a soap film encounters a positive crossing along its way, a soap film might become wider or smaller; the propagation of a soap film clings to the strands that are previously attached to.

Each soap film is in bijection with a vertex in a quiver.
If the resulting soap film

corresponds to a frozen vertex. We therefore specify that in such case, a bridge

Suppose that

(1)

are bridges of the same type (either both frozen or mutable) and that

in

.

(2)

.

The above ordering

Theorem 3.7
.

For any

as above, the matrix
{\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}}
has integer coefficients and
\det(\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}})=(-1)^{m+f}

Proof.

We proceed on induction on length of
\beta

\beta=u

and

or (

and

is a subword of
\beta^{\prime}

Let us first consider

is obtained from the one for

by adding a crossing on the left, in particular they have the same number of bridges, and the same number of mutable and frozen variables.
By the induction hypothesis, we have

.
We have

since the quivers

and

are the same, and we can identify respective frozen and mutable vertices.
Then combining Lemma

\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
is an integer matrix and

Next, we consider

where

. In this case, the simple transposition

corresponds to the leftmost bridge in

.
We want to relate the matrices

4.1.
Cluster automorphism group of braid varieties

We recall the definition and important properties of cluster automorphism group of braid varieties, discussed in
[
5
,
13
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

[
13
, Section 5.1]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cluster algebra.
A cluster automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
is an algebra automorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
such that for every cluster variable
z\in\mathcal{A}
,
\varphi(z)
is a non-zero scalar multiple of
z
. The group of cluster automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
.

Recall that Theorem
3.7
implies that
\det(\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}})=(-1)^{m+f}
, thus
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
is an invertible matrix. From now on, let us use
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
to denote an inverse matrix of
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
, and use
{col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}})
to denote the
j
th column vector of
{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The
\ker(\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
in
\mathbb{Z}^{m+f}
is a free abelian group of rank
f
with the basis given by the column vectors

\text{col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}})
for
m+1\leq j\leq m+f
.

Proof.

Consider a vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+f}
, then
\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\beta})v
is given by the first
m
coordinates of
\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}v
. Since
{col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}})

form a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{m+f}
, we can expand
v
in terms of this basis, and write
v={A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}w
for some
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+f}
. Then

\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}v=\widehat{B^{u,\bm{\beta}}}{A^{u,\bm{\beta}}}w=w
.
We conclude that
v
is in
\ker(\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
if and only if the first
m
coordinates of
w
vanish, and the result follows.
∎

Now we are able to describe the cluster automorphism group and its action on braid varieties as follows.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let us fix
Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}
as before. Let
\text{col}_{j}({A^{u,\bm{\beta}}})=(a_{1,m+j},\ldots,a_{m+f,m+j})
.
Then
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
is an algebraic torus with coordinates

which acts on

by

x_{i}\to\prod t_{j}^{a_{i,m+j}}\cdot x_{i},

where
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+f})
be an initial seed of
X_{u,{\bm{\beta}}}
.

Proof.

Combining Proposition 5.1 in
[
13
]
with Remark
3.8
implies that we have
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{A}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))
is naturally identified with
\ker(\text{mult}(\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})))
where
\text{mult}(\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})):(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m+f}\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
is a map defined by
\text{mult}(\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}}))(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots,\zeta_{m+f})_{i}=\Pi_{j=1}^{m+f}{\zeta_{j}}^{b_{i,j}}

(i,j)

\widetilde{B}(Q_{u,\bm{\beta}})

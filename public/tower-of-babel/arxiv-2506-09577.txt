The torsion order in knot Floer homology is multiplicative for L-space knots under cabling
David Suchodoll

Abstract
. The knot Floer order Ord ⁡ ( K ) \Ord(K) is a knot invariant derived from the knot Floer homology. It bounds many invariants that are difficult to calculate. For all L-space cables of L-space knots, we prove that Ord ⁡ ( K ) + 1 \Ord(K)+1 is multiplicative in p p when g ⁡ ( K ) > 1 g(K)>1 . The same holds for g ⁡ ( K ) = 1 g(K)=1 , provided q > 2 ​ p q>2p . We further compute the knot Floer torsion order in the range p < q < 2 ​ p p<q<2p , thereby determining Ord ⁡ ( K p , q ) \Ord(K_{p,q}) from Ord ⁡ ( K ) \Ord(K) for all L-space cables of L-space knots. We discuss possible applications to the BB-conjecture.

2.1.
Knot Floer homology

The filtered chain homotopy type of the knot Floer complex
\CFK^{-}(K)
is an invariant of a knot
K
in
S^{3}

[
OS04
, Theorem 3.1]
.
The complex is freely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]
. The differential counts pseudoholomorphic disks
\phi
, where
n_{z}(\phi)
and
n_{w}(\phi)
count the discs’ intersection with certain submanifolds corresponding to basepoints
z
and
w
. We denote the homotopy classes of these disks from
x
to
y
by
\pi_{2}(x,y)
. The expected dimension of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\phi)
of pseudoholomorphic representatives of
\phi
is denoted by
\mu(\phi)
. The differential is then given by

\displaystyle\partial^{-}x=\sum_{y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\pi_{2}(x,y)\\
\mu(\phi)=1\end{subarray}}\#\left(\mathcal{M}(\phi)/\mathbb{R}\right)\penalty\ U^{n_{w}(\phi)}\cdot y.

The Alexander grading is defined by
\gr_{A}(x)-\gr_{A}(y)=n_{z}(\phi)-n_{w}(\phi)
for
\phi\in\pi_{2}(x,y)
. It is fixed by the quasi-isomorphism to
\CF^{-}(S^{3})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]
and induces a filtration

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{x\in\CFK^{-}(K)\mid\gr_{A}(x)\leq i\}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\dots\subset\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\subset\dots\subset\CFK^{-}(K).

The associated graded complex is defined as
\gCFK^{-}(K)=\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
. The differential excludes holomorphic discs that cross the basepoint
z
.

\displaystyle\partial^{-}_{g}x=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\pi_{2}(x,y)\\
\mu(\phi)=1\\
n_{z}(\phi)=0\end{subarray}}\#\left(\mathcal{M}(\phi)/\mathbb{R}\right)\penalty\ U^{n_{w}(\phi)}\cdot y.

We denote the homology
H_{*}(\gCFK^{-}(K),\partial^{-}_{g})
by
\HFK^{-}(K)
.
It can be decomposed as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\HFK^{-}(K)=\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]\oplus\HFK^{-}_{\operatorname{red}}(K)\quad\text{where}\quad\HFK^{-}_{\operatorname{red}}(K)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]/U^{i_{k}}.

Definition 2.1

(
[
JMZ20
]
)

Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
. Let
\HFK^{-}_{\operatorname{red}}(K)
be the torsion submodule of its knot Floer homology. The
knot Floer order
is defined as

\Ord(K)=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}\mid U^{k}\cdot\HFK^{-}_{\operatorname{red}}(K)=0\}.

Since the decomposition in (
2.1
) is finite,
\Ord(K)
is always finite. Details on the composition can be found in
[
OSS15
, Proposition 6.1.4, Proposition 7.3.3]
. Though a general result to obtain the immersed curves is stated for arbitrary complexes
\CFK^{-}(K)
in
[
HRW22
]
, we are interested in those that admit a special type of basis, vastly simplifying the result (see Lemma
2.6
). We will see in Theorem
2.4
that all L-space knots admit such bases.

Definition 2.2

(Definition 11.23,
[
LOT18
]
)

A basis
\{x_{0},\dots x_{n}\}
of the complex
\CFK^{-}(K)
is called
filtered
if its image in
\gCFK^{-}(K)
is a basis.
The complex
\CFK^{-}(K)
is called
reduced
if its differential
\partial^{-}
drops either
\gr_{A}
or the
U
-power, i.e.

\displaystyle\partial^{-}x=y_{1}+Uy_{2},\quad\text{where}\quad\gr_{A}(y_{1})<\gr_{A}(x).

A filtered basis
\{\xi_{i}\}
is
vertically simplified
if either

\displaystyle\partial\xi_{i}\in U\cdot\CFK^{-}(K),\quad\text{ or }\quad\partial\xi_{i}\equiv\xi_{i+1}\text{ mod }U\cdot\CFK^{-}(K).

A basis
\{\eta_{i}\}
is
horizontally simplified
if either

\displaystyle\gr_{A}(\partial\eta_{i})<\gr_{A}(\eta_{i})\quad\text{ or }\quad\exists m\colon\partial\eta_{i}=U^{m}\cdot\eta_{i+1}+y

where
\gr_{A}(\eta_{i})=\gr_{A}(U^{m}\cdot\eta_{i+1})
and
\gr_{A}(y)<\gr_{A}(\eta_{i})
.

More details on knot Floer homology can be found, for example, in
[
OS18
]

[
Hom21
]
or
[
Man16
]
.

2.2.
L-space knots

This section introduces L-space knots and highlights key aspects of their knot Floer homology. L-space knots have been defined initially, and studied by Ozsváth and Szabó in
[
OS05
]
.
Their Alexander polynomial determines the knot complex completely, as proven in Theorem 1.2 in
[
OS05
]
.
They conclude in Corollary 1.3 a structural result for the Alexander polynomial of L-space knots. We summarize both results here.

Lemma 2.3

(Corollary 1.3
[
OS05
]
)

Let
K
be an L-space knot in
S^{3}
. Then there is a decreasing sequence of integers

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}>\dots>\alpha_{i}>\dots>\alpha_{2n},\quad\text{ s.t. }\quad-\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{2n-k},

such that its symmetrized Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(t)
has the form

\Delta_{K}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}t^{\alpha_{i}}.

∎

Theorem 2.4

(
[
OS05
]
(cf. Theorem 2.10
[
OSS17
]
))

Let
K
be an L-space knot in
S^{3}
with Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}t^{\alpha_{i}}
,
\alpha_{0}>\dots>\alpha_{2n}
. Let for
i\in\{1,\dots,2n\}

d_{i}=\alpha_{i-1}-\alpha_{i}>0.

Then
\CFK^{-}(K)
has a basis
\{x_{0},\dots x_{2n}\}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]
such that
\gr_{A}(x_{k})=\alpha_{k}
with differentials

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial^{-}x_{2i}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{-}x_{2i+1}=x_{2i+2}+U^{d_{2i+1}}x_{2i}.

∎

Remark 2.5

The graded complex
\gCFK^{-}(K)
has a differential
\partial^{-}_{g}
which excludes any disk with
n_{z}(\phi)>0
. Theorem 2.10
[
OSS17
]
yields that the disk
\phi
from
x_{2i+1}
to
x_{2i+2}
has
n_{z}(\phi)=d_{2i+2}>0
. Thus

\partial^{-}_{g}x_{2i}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{-}_{g}x_{2i+1}=U^{d_{2i+1}}x_{2i}.

From the symmetry
\alpha_{2n-k}=-\alpha_{k}
, we conclude that
d_{2n-i}=d_{1+i}
. We can see that
\HFK^{-}(K)=\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]/U^{d_{i}}
. Therefore
\Ord(K)=\max\{d_{i}\mid i=0,\dots,n\}
. This is essentially the proof in
[
JMZ20
, Lemma 5.1]
.

2.3.
Immersed curves

The
immersed curve invariant

\widehat{\HF}(M)
for a 3-manifold
M
with torus boundary was introduced by Hanselman, Rasmussen, and Watson in
[
HRW22
,
HRW24
]
. This invariant is defined as a collection

\gamma=\{\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{n}\}

of curves
\gamma_{i}:S^{1}\looparrowright T^{2}\setminus\{z\}
, up to regular homotopy, where each curve is an immersion into the punctured torus. In
[
HRW24
]
, the authors prove that
\widehat{\HF}(M)
encodes the same data as a certain type of bordered Floer invariant, the type D structure
\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M)
introduced in
[
LOT18
]
. In the special case where
M
is the exterior of a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, i.e.
M=S^{3}\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu K)
, the invariant recovers the knot Floer homology
\HFK^{-}(K)
.

In this paper, we will focus on precisely this situation:
M=S^{3}\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu K)
, where
\nu K
denotes a tubular neighborhood of the knot
K
in
S^{3}
. The boundary torus is identified with
\partial M
. We denote by
\mu
the meridian and by
\lambda
the Seifert longitude of the knot exterior. The basepoint in
T^{2}
is taken to be the point of transverse intersection
z=\lambda\pitchfork\mu
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the universal cover of the torus, with the lifts of the basepoint
z
lying at the lattice points
\mathbb{Z}^{2}+(0,1/2)
. We lift the set of curves
\gamma
to the intermediate covering space
\overline{T}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\lambda
, where
\lambda
is chosen to be the horizontal direction. We view
\overline{T}
as the infinite strip
[-1/2,1/2]\times\mathbb{R}
, with the identification
(-1/2,t)\sim(1/2,t)
. The lifts of the basepoint
z
, which we refer to as
pegs
, appear at heights
n+1/2
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The intersections of
\widetilde{\gamma}\cap\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
will be homotoped to appear at integer heights. See Figure
1
(a).

We will prove the following lemma using Proposition 47 in
[
HRW22
]
for the special case of L-space knots.

Remark 2.8

The graded complex
\gCFK^{-}(K)
is isomorphic to the complex in Remark

\Ord(K)=\max\{\gr(x_{i-1})-\gr(x_{i})\mid i=1,\dots 2n\}

The invariant

in general includes a finite-dimensional vector space

over

and an automorphism
\Phi_{i}:V_{i}\to V_{i}

consists of a single component with a unique
{{\rm Spin}^{c}}

Let
K_{p,q}
be the

-cable of a knot
K

S^{3}

3.
Proof of Theorems

We start by proving Lemma
3.1
, which will be essential in the proof of both theorems. It proves how
\Ord(K)
can be read from the immersed curves for L-space knots.

Lemma 3.1

Let
K
in
S^{3}
be an L-space knot, and let
\widetilde{\gamma}\subset[-1/2,1/2]\times\mathbb{R}
denote the lift of the immersed curve invariant
\widehat{\HF}(M)
for the knot complement
M=S^{3}\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu K)
. Then the knot Floer order
\Ord(K)
is equal to the maximal number of pegs strictly between the heights of any two intersection points of
\widetilde{\gamma}
with
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
:

\Ord(K):=\max\left\{\#\left\{\text{pegs between }h(p),h(q)\right\}\;\middle|\;p,q\in\widetilde{\gamma}\cap(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Proof.

The Alexander polynomial determines
\gamma
by Theorem
2.6
. Thus, two generators
x_{i}
and
x_{i-1}
have a height difference of
d_{i}
. From Equation (
1.3
) we get that
\Ord(K)=\max\{d_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,2n\}
. Thus, Lemma
2.7
and Remark
2.8
yield the result.
∎

Remark 3.2

Note that in the proofs for Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
3.1
, it is crucial that
\widetilde{\gamma}
is a single curve monotone in a neighborhood of the vertical axis, with the first connection
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
being on the right.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The two key observations for this proof are as follows:

•

If
\widetilde{\gamma}_{p,q}
is itself an L-space knot, it consists of a single curve that is monotone in a neighborhood of the vertical axis. Consequently, during the process described in Theorem
2.9
, two copies of
\widetilde{\gamma}
do not overlap in height.

•

Since
p
and
q
are coprime, the
p
copies, each shifted in height by
q
, collectively cover all possible heights without leaving any
gaps
. That is, after the shifts, there will be pegs at every height of the form
n+1/2
.

Suppose
g(K)>1
. Then there exist at least three pegs that lie between the highest and lowest values of
\gr_{A}(x)
for all generators. By Proposition 1.1 of
[
Krc18
]
, the number of pegs between the first and second (resp. the last and second-to-last) intersection is exactly one. Therefore, the maximum number of pegs between two consecutive intersections of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{p,q}
does not occur at the first or last intersection alone; if it does, it must also occur elsewhere. This maximum configuration is illustrated in Figure
4
.

Figure 4.
(a) The maximal number of pegs between two intersections is on the left side of
\widetilde{\gamma}
. (b) The maximal number of pegs is obtained on the right.

Without loss of generality, assume that the maximal number of pegs appears on the right side of the curve
\widetilde{\gamma}
.

Consider the copy that is positioned the highest. Because no curve segments are carried from the right, for each peg between the intersections in
\widetilde{\gamma}
, exactly
p
pegs are introduced in the construction of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{p,q}
. Since the maximum number of pegs occurs on the right
p\cdot\Ord(K)
pegs are on the vertical line after the shift, along with an additional
p-1
pegs above the highest peg below the highest intersection. See Figure
5
.

Figure 5.
(a) The cabling construction places lifts of
z\in\partial M
on staggered heights right of the curve
\widetilde{\gamma}
. (b) The lifts of
z
are translated onto the vertical line, dragging the curve along. (c) The curve
\widetilde{\gamma}_{p,q}
.

Thus, using Lemma
3.1
we get

\displaystyle\Ord(K_{p,q})+1\geq(p\cdot\Ord(K))+p-1)+1=p\cdot(\Ord(K)+1).

If the maximum is attained on the left instead, the lowest copy of
\widetilde{\gamma}
satisfies the same argument, with pegs being carried from the left accordingly.

Finally, between the
i
-th and
(i+1)
-th copies of
\widetilde{\gamma}
, the curve
\widetilde{\gamma}_{p,q}
will have pegs on both sides. The lowest peg in the
i
-th copy will appear on the right (respectively, left) side, implying that the highest peg of the
(i+1)
-th copy will be on the left (respectively, right) side. The
i
-th copy will contain
i
pegs on the left side, for every multiple of
p
. Similarly, there will be
(p-i)
pegs on the right side, for every multiple of
p
. Thus, the maximal number of pegs between two successive lifts is
p
.

As established earlier, there will be a section consisting of
p(\Ord(K)+1)-1
pegs on one side of the curve. If
\Ord(K)\geq 1
, it follows that
p(\Ord(K)+1)-1>p
for
p\geq 2
. The inequality
\Ord(K)\geq 1
holds because only the unknot satisfies
\Delta_{K}(t)=1
, and by Equation
1.3
,
\Ord(K)
is at least one for any other
\Delta_{K}(t)
where
K
is an L-space knot. Thus for
p>1
,

\displaystyle\Ord(K_{p,q})+1=p\cdot(\Ord(K)+1).

For
p=1
, a true cable does not exist, as
K
is isotopic to
K_{1,q}
, and thus
\Ord(K)+1=\Ord(K_{1,q})+1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
g(K)=1
, the maximal number of pegs between two intersections will not lie in a configuration as in Figure
4
. It spans at least three pegs, but the number of pegs between the highest and lowest intersections is two, as shown in Figure
1
. However, if
q>2\cdot p
, the copies will be staggered in such a way that the highest peg in the
i
-th copy is
p
units below with the height of the lowest peg of the
(i-1)
-th copy (see Figure
3
). This ensures that the argument used in the case
g(K)>1
remains applicable. Since
\Ord(K_{2,3})=1
,

\Ord(K_{p,q})+1=p(\Ord(K)+1)=2p.

We still obtain a monotone curve in the case
p<q<2p
, since the cable
K_{p,q}
remains an L-space knot. Consider the first copy and focus on the peg at the right of the curve. Due to the cabling construction, there are exactly
p-1
pegs above this peg in the first copy. To complete the picture, we analyze how many additional pegs lie below the peg of the same copy.

Since the first copy lies furthest to the left, all subsequent pegs are contributions from the right. The first peg, which is left of the curve and below the first copy, corresponds to the highest peg of the second copy, which is shifted downward by
q
units. Because the construction places one peg at each integer height, there are exactly
q-p-1
pegs strictly between the lowest peg of the first copy and the highest peg of the second copy. It follows that

\Ord(K_{p,q})>q-1.

As in the previous case, the number of pegs appearing between two consecutive copies is at most
p
, with this maximal length attained only in the topmost and bottommost copies. Since
q>p
, we conclude that equality holds.
∎

4.
Discussion

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are not sharp. We have identified several instances where multiplicity holds for knots that are not L-space knots, as well as for certain cables of L-space knots that themselves are not L-space knots.

More generally, suppose
d=\Ord(K)
for a knot
K\subset S^{3}
, realized by an immersed multicurve with trivial local systems, in a configuration as shown in Figure
4
(a), where the height function restricted to
\gamma
is injective between two intersection points. Denote the corresponding curve segment by
I
. Then, as Lemma
2.7
implies,
\operatorname{gC}^{-}(\widehat{\HF}(S^{3}\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu K)))
contains a generator
y
with differential
\partial^{-}_{C}y=U^{d}x
, where
y
corresponds to the lower and
x
to the higher intersection. Consequently,
\HFK^{-}(K)
has a summand of the form
\mathbb{F}_{2}[U]/U^{d}
.

Now let
K_{p,q}
be a
(p,q)
-cable of
K
such that, in the highest copy of
\gamma
used in the cabling construction of Theorem
2.9
, no curve segment is dragged between these intersections, and the image of
I
under the cabling retains the injectivity of the height function. In this refined setting, the proof of Theorem
1.1
yields the inequality

(4.1)

\Ord(K_{p,q})+1\geq p\cdot(\Ord(K)+1).

What remains open is whether any higher torsion summands can arise under cabling, which would be necessary to attain equality.

Question 4.1

Is this the only configuration that achieves multiplicity of
\Ord(K)+1
?

Question 4.2

Does the inequality
\Ord(K_{p,q})+1\leq p\cdot(\Ord(K)+1)
hold for all cables?

If both questions have affirmative answers, then Question
1.4
also has a positive resolution. In that case, multiplicity for a single cable would imply, by Question
4.1
, the existence of a curve segment along which the height function is injective. Because the bounds in Question
1.4
are chosen to prevent overlap between distinct copies of
\gamma
in the cabling construction, this injectivity would persist under cabling, thus we obtain the lower bound as in Inequality (
4.1
), and Question
4.2
provides equality.

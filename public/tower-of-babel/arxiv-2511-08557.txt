On Laguerre Isotropic Hypersurfaces
Fernanda Alves Caixeta, Keti Tenenblat

Abstract
We study Laguerre isotropic hypersurfaces in the Euclidean space, which are hypersurfaces whose Laguerre form is zero and the eigenvalues of the Laguerre tensor are constant and equal to λ ≥ 0 \displaystyle\lambda\geq 0 . We prove a rigidity theorem for the L-isotropic hypersurfaces parametrized by lines of curvature. Moreover, we study the hypersurfaces that are L-isotropic and L-isoparametric simultaneously and we show that for such a hypersurface λ = 0 \displaystyle\lambda=0 . We obtain necessary conditions for the existence of L-isotropic hypersurfaces with λ > 0 \displaystyle\lambda>0 and we prove that a certain function, determined by the radii of curvature of the hypersurface, is bounded above by 1 / 2 ​ λ \displaystyle{1}/{2\lambda} . Keywords: Laguerre geometry, Laguerre isotropic hypersurfaces, Laguerre isoparametric hypersurfaces. Mathematics Subject Classification: [2020] 53A40, 53B25.

Introduction

The theory of Laguerre geometry began in the 20th century with the study of Laguerre surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
, as presented in Blaschke’s book
[
1
]
. Since then, several mathematicians have dedicated themselves to the study of Laguerre differential geometry, including Musso and Nicolodi in
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and Palmer in
[
14
]
. The study of Laguerre geometry of surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
continued with the work of Li
[
5
]
. Later, Song and Wang
[
19
]
studied minimal Laguerre surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The development of Laguerre geometry for surfaces naturally motivated a generalization to the case of hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Subsequently, several papers were published addressing this more general case.

In Laguerre differential geometry, one studies the properties of Laguerre hypersurfaces that are invariant under the group of Laguerre transformations on the unit tangent bundle
\displaystyle U\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Li and Wang
[
10
]
studied this geometry for hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
using Cartan’s method of moving frames. Considering an immersion
\displaystyle x:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
without umbilic points, with distinct nonzero principal curvatures, and
\displaystyle\xi:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
a unit vector field normal to
\displaystyle x
, they defined the basic Laguerre invariants, namely: a Laguerre-invariant metric
\displaystyle g
, the Laguerre second fundamental form
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}
, the Laguerre form
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
and the Laguerre tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
.

Once these invariants were defined it was natural to undertake the task of classifying hypersurfaces, in the sense of Laguerre geometry, according to the properties satisfied by these invariants. The first results in this direction were obtained by Li, Li, and Wang
[
6
]
where the authors introduced the concept of a hypersurface with parallel Laguerre second fundamental form
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\nabla\mathbb{B}=0
, where
\displaystyle\nabla
is the covariant derivative with respect to the Laguerre metric
\displaystyle g
. Moreover, they obtained a complete classification of such hypersurfaces, up to Laguerre transformations.

Subsequently, Li, Li, and Wang
[
7
]
began to study a class of hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with vanishing Laguerre form
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
and with all eigenvalues of the Laguerre tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
constant equal to
\displaystyle\lambda
. We refer to hypersurfaces with this property as L-isotropic hypersurfaces. Li, Li, and Wang proved that
\displaystyle\lambda\geq 0
and presented a characterization result for L-isotropic hypersurfaces. Moreover, they obtained an explicit classification of hypersurfaces with
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}=0
and constant eigenvalues of the tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
that are not all equal.

Li and Sun
[
9
]
defined another class of hypersurfaces, the L-isoparametric hypersurfaces, that are those for which the eigenvalues of the Laguerre second fundamental form
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}
are constant and the Laguerre form
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
vanishes. They presented a classification of these hypersurfaces in the case of three-dimensional manifolds in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}
. They also showed that an L-isoparametric hypersurface is a Dupin hypersurface. Conversely, a Dupin hypersurface, under the additional condition that the Laguerre form
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
vanishes, is an L-isoparametric hypersurface.

Song
[
18
]
, and Cezana and Tenenblat
[
2
]
, obtained the classification of L-isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with two distinct, nonzero principal curvatures.
Subsequently, Li and Shu
[
11
]
obtained the classification of L-isoparametric hypersurfaces with three distinct principal curvatures, one of them being simple.

Shu published two other papers obtaining the classification of L-isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{5}

[
16
]
and in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{6}

[
15
]
, and he also proved that such hypersurfaces have parallel second fundamental, when they are not L-isotropic. More recently, Shu
[
17
]
studied L-isoparametric hypersurfaces and Dupin hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, presenting several classification theorems. In one of his results, considering an L-isoparametric hypersurface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, he proved that the eigenvalues of the Laguerre tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
are all constant, possibly all equal or not.
When the eigenvalues of the Laguerre tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
are all constant but not all equal, the classification was obtained by Li, Li, and Wang in
[
7
]
.
Note that for the complete classification of L-isoparametric hypersurfaces, it is sufficient to determine which are the L-isotropic hypersurfaces.

Moreover, in
[
17
]
, using the classification theorem for L-isoparametric hypersurfaces, Shu presented the classification of proper Dupin hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the number of distinct principal curvatures
\displaystyle g\geq 2
. This result, in a certain sense, generalizes the theorem obtained by Cezana and Tenenblat
[
3
]
, where they proved that proper Dupin hypersurfaces
\displaystyle M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\displaystyle n
distinct, nonvanishing principal curvatures and constant Laguerre curvature, admitting a coordinate system by lines of curvature, are determined by
\displaystyle n
constants. Moreover, they proved that any Dupin hypersurface under these conditions in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is described by

x(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},0)-\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}u_{i}^{2}+\phi}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}u_{i}^{2}+1}(a_{1}u_{1},\ldots,a_{n}u_{n},-1),

(1)

where
\displaystyle\phi\in\mathbb{R}
and the
\displaystyle a_{i}
are distinct, nonzero constants.

This family of Dupin hypersurfaces was first obtained by Corro, Ferreira, and Tenenblat
[
4
]
in 1999 via a Ribaucour transformation of a hyperplane. Although this example was derived in a different context, it was applied to the Laguerre geometry setting, when
\displaystyle\phi=0
, where it first appeared in
[
6
]
as an example of an L-isoparametric hypersurface.

Since then, the family given by (
1
) has appeared in all classification results of L-isoparametric hypersurfaces. Furthermore, this family also provides an example of an L-isotropic hypersurface with all eigenvalues
\displaystyle\lambda
of the Laguerre tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
constant and equal to zero.

In this work, motivated by the family (
1
) and Shu’s article
[
17
]
, we investigate hypersurfaces that simultaneously possess both properties of being L-isotropic and L-isoparametric. More precisely, we obtain the following proposition:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\displaystyle x:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, with
\displaystyle n\geq 3
, be a hypersurface without umbilic points, having distinct, nonvanishing principal curvatures. If
\displaystyle x
is both L-isotropic and L-isoparametric, then all eigenvalues of the Laguerre tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
are equal and zero, i.e.,
\displaystyle\lambda=0
.

We posed another natural question whether the family of hypersurfaces described in (
1
) is the only L-isotropic one when
\displaystyle\lambda=0
. We partially answer this question positively by presenting a rigidity result for L-isotropic hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
parametrized by lines of curvature, up to Laguerre transformation.

Given a hypersurface
\displaystyle x:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with unit normal vector
\displaystyle\xi:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the Laguerre position vector
\displaystyle Y:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n+3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+4}_{2}
is defined by

\displaystyle Y=\rho(x\cdot\xi,-x\cdot\xi,\xi,1)
,
where

\rho=\sqrt{\sum_{i}(r-r_{i})^{2}}

(2)

is a function defined in terms of the curvature radii
\displaystyle r_{i}=\frac{1}{k_{i}}
and the mean curvature radius
\displaystyle r=\frac{\sum_{i}r_{i}}{n}
of
\displaystyle x
. We then prove our main result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\displaystyle x:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a hypersurface with
\displaystyle n
distinct, nonvanishing principal curvatures, let
\displaystyle Y:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n+3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+4}_{2}
be its Laguerre position vector with Laguerre metric
\displaystyle g=\langle dY,dY\rangle
, and let
\displaystyle\lambda
be the eigenvalues of the tensor
\displaystyle\mathbb{L}
. Suppose
\displaystyle M^{n}
is connected and admits a parametrization by lines of curvature with respect to the metric
\displaystyle g
. If
\displaystyle x
is an L-isotropic hypersurface, then
\displaystyle\lambda=0
and
\displaystyle x
is L-isoparametric. Moreover, up to Laguerre transformation, this hypersurface is equivalent to the one described in (
1
) for
\displaystyle\phi=0
.

The proof of Theorem
1
clarifies that choosing the constant
\displaystyle\phi
is equivalent to choosing the position vector
\displaystyle Y
, which implies an equivalence in the sense of Laguerre. Consequently, from the perspective of Laguerre geometry, the family of hypersurfaces described by (
1
) is equivalent, regardless of whether
\displaystyle\phi=0
or
\displaystyle\phi\neq 0
.

The classification of L-isotropic hypersurfaces is of significant importance, as it serves as a foundation for classifying all L-isoparametric hypersurfaces, based on the result proven by Shu in
[
17
]
. The case of L-isotropic hypersurfaces with
\displaystyle\lambda>0
remains an open area of investigation, as no examples are currently known. In this work, we establish necessary conditions for the existence of such hypersurfaces. Theorem
1
demonstrates that an L-isotropic hypersurface with
\displaystyle\lambda>0
cannot admit a parameterization by lines of curvature. Furthermore, we verify that any such hypersurface must possess at least three distinct, non-vanishing principal curvatures.

For the class of L-isotropic hypersurfaces with
\displaystyle\lambda>0
, we also prove the following proposition:

Proposition 2
.

Let
\displaystyle x:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a hypersurface without umbilic points and with nonvanishing principal curvatures. If
\displaystyle x
is an L-isotropic hypersurface with
\displaystyle\lambda>0
, then

0<\rho^{2}<\dfrac{1}{2\lambda},

(3)

where
\displaystyle\rho
is defined by (
2
).

Finding the walls for quiver moduli
Hans Franzen, Gianni Petrella, Rachel Webb

Abstract
. We give an effective characterisation of the walls in the variation of geometric invariant theory problem associated to a quiver and a dimension vector.



1.
Introduction

A quiver
Q
with vertex set
Q_{0}
and a dimension vector
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}

determine a representation
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
of a certain reductive group
G_{\mathbf{d}}
.
A further choice of stability parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}

orthogonal to
\mathbf{d}
determines open loci of stable and semistable points

(1)

\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}_{\mathbf{d}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}.

Let
\sst(\mathbf{d})
denote the locus in the orthogonal complement
{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}

of
\mathbf{d}
for which
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
is nonempty.
We compute the locus of
walls
in
\sst(\mathbf{d})
, namely the locus of
\theta
such that

\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}_{\mathbf{d}}\subsetneq\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
.
Our main result is the following (see
Lemma

3.2
and
Proposition

3.3
).

1.1
.

The walls in
\sst(\mathbf{d})
are a union of sets of the form
W_{\mathbf{e}}
,
where
\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}
is a nonzero vector such that
\mathbf{e}\neq\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}_{i}\leq\mathbf{d}_{i}
for each
i\in Q_{0}
, and

(2)

W_{\mathbf{e}}=\sst(\mathbf{e})\cap\sst(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e}).

In
Theorem

2.7
we recall an existing algorithm,
based on
[
16
]
and
[
8
, Theorem 3]
, for computing
\sst(\mathbf{d})
.
This gives an effective method for computing the walls.

Our result has several applications.
The first is to the problem of variation of geometric invariant theory (GIT) associated to
Q
and
\mathbf{d}
: in this setting,
two stability parameters
\theta,\eta
in the orthogonal complement of
\mathbf{d}

are
GIT equivalent
if their loci of semistable points are equal.

Theorem

1.1
yields an effective algorithm for determining when
\eta
and
\theta
are GIT equivalent (see
Corollary

4.4
).

1.2
.

The stability parameters
\theta,\eta
are GIT equivalent if and only if, for every
\mathbf{e}\leq\mathbf{d}
,
the convex hull of
\theta
and
\eta

is either contained in
W_{\mathbf{e}}
,
or does not meet
W_{\mathbf{e}}
.

The set of GIT equivalence classes determines a fan, called the GIT fan of
(Q,\mathbf{d})
.
This is a fan in the orthogonal complement to
\mathbf{d}
with support equal to
\sst(\mathbf{d})
.
In
Corollary

4.6
we give an effective algorithm for computing this fan.

For the second application, motivated by physics literature,
we say that the pair
(Q,\mathbf{d})

has a geometric phase

if there exists a choice of
\theta

for which
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}=\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}_{\mathbf{d}}

and this set is nonempty.
The following also appears as
Theorem

5.1
.

1.3
.

The pair
(Q,\mathbf{d})
has a geometric phase
if and only if
\mathbf{d}
is an indivisible Schur root of
Q
, or equivalently
if and only if
\sst(\mathbf{d})
is full-dimensional and all walls
W_{\mathbf{e}}
have codimension one.

The third application is to better understand the
wall system

and
special subdimension vectors
introduced in
[
13
, Section 3]

to study the GIT fan of quivers without oriented cycles.
The wall system is the smallest set of hyperplanes that contain those
W_{\mathbf{e}}

of positive codimension in
\sst(\mathbf{d})
, and it is shown in
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]

that every hyperplane in the wall system is orthogonal to a special subdimension vector.
We give a recursive characterisation
of the special subdimension vectors
of
(Q,\mathbf{d})
in
Proposition

6.3
.
Its use in
Example

7.2

implies the following result.

1.4
.

Even for acyclic quivers,
not every special subdimension vector
is orthogonal to a hyperplane in the wall system,
and not every hyperplane in the wall system
is contained in the union
of non-maximal cones in the GIT fan.

We provide implementations
of our algorithms for computing the walls
W_{\mathbf{e}}
and the GIT fan in
[
9
]
.
These use the
QuiverTools
package
for
SageMath
and
Julia
, see
[
4
,
5
]
.
In
Section

7
we use these implementations to compute various examples,
including an illustration of how the GIT fan changes under quiver mutations
and the complete GIT fan for a realization of the Segre cubic as a quiver moduli space.

1.5
.

In
[
1
, p.163]
the walls are defined to be the cones in the GIT fan of codimension 1.
By contrast, our sets
W_{\mathbf{e}}
can have any codimension in
\sst(\mathbf{d})
, including codimension
0
;
we show in
Corollary

4.5
that the
W_{\mathbf{e}}
of codimension 1
are precisely the walls of
[
1
]
.
We call the set
W_{\mathbf{e}}
a “wall”, even when it has codimension
0
,
because it always arises from a codimension
1
wall of the abelianized quiver.

Relation to other work

For quivers without oriented cycles,
a description of the GIT fan is given in
[
6
]
(but see also
[
7
,
14
]
).
Our description of the walls (
Proposition

3.3
) can be derived
from the description of GIT cones that appears in op. cit.,
but that derivation is not simpler than what we present here.

Our discussion of GIT equivalence relies heavily
on the general structure of the GIT fan worked out in
[
1
]
.

Large portions of
Theorem

1.3
follow from
[
15
, Propositions 4.4 and 5.3]
,
so we suspect that this result may be known to experts.
We nevertheless include the statement and a proof
as we could not find it in the literature.

Finally, we note that the GIT fan of
(Q,\mathbf{d})
when
Q
is acyclic and
\mathbf{d}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
is also studied in
[
12
]
, and that an algorithm for computing the fan in this case has been implemented in
Macaualy2
(see
[
2
]
).

Conventions

Matching the assumptions in
[
1
]
,
the representation
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
and group
G_{\mathbf{d}}

are defined
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.
If
V
is a real vector space and
U\subseteq V
is a subset
invariant under the scaling action of
\mathbb{R}
,
the
dimension
of
U
is the dimension of its linear span.

2.
Background

Let
Q
be a quiver with vertex set
Q_{0}
and arrow set
Q_{1}
,
and let
s,t:Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
denote the source and target maps.
For a dimension vector
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}
, define

\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
and
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}
respectively as

\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}=\bigoplus_{a\in Q_{1}}\Hom(k^{\mathbf{d}_{s(a)}},k^{\mathbf{d}_{t(a)}}),\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL(k^{\mathbf{d}_{i}}).

We call the elements of
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}

representations of
Q
of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
.
The algebraic group
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}
acts linearly on the left on
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
,
with the action of
g=(g_{i})\in\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}
on the representation
M=(M_{a})\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}

defined as

(3)

g\cdot M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(g_{t(a)}M_{a}g_{s(a)}^{-1}).

The central closed subgroup
\Delta=\{z\cdot\id\mid z\in k^{\times}\}
of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}
acts trivially,
so we have an induced action
of
\mathrm{PG}_{\mathbf{d}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}/\Delta
on
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
.
Two points
M,N\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
are
isomorphic as representations

if and only if they lie in the same
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{d}}
-orbit.

A real vector
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
defines a function on the set
\smash{\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}}
of dimension vectors
by setting
\theta(\mathbf{d})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\theta\cdot\mathbf{d}
, where
\mathbf{d}
is an element of
\smash{\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}}
and
\theta\cdot\mathbf{d}
is the dot product.
If
M
is a representation of
Q
of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}

(that is,
M\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{e}}
),
we define
\theta(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\theta(\mathbf{e})
.

Given a dimension vector
\mathbf{d}\in\smash{\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}}
, the associated
space of stability parameters
is

(4)

{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}\mid\theta(\mathbf{d})=0\}.

A choice of stability parameter yields a notion of (semi-)stability for elements of
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
as follows.

2.1
.

A representation
M
is
\theta
-
semistable
(respectively
\theta
-
stable
) if
\theta(M)=0
and
\theta(M^{\prime})\leq 0
(respectively
\theta(M^{\prime})<0
) holds for all proper nonzero subrepresentations
M^{\prime}
of
M
.

The sets
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}_{\mathbf{d}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}

of
\theta
-stable and
\theta
-semistable representations
are possibly empty Zariski open subsets of
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
.

2.2
.

A stability parameter
\theta
with integral coordinates defines a character
\chi_{\theta}

of the group
\mathrm{PG}_{\mathbf{d}}

by letting
\chi_{\theta}(g)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}{\det(g_{i})}^{-\theta_{i}}
.
Such a character provides a linearisation of the trivial line bundle on
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
,
which we denote by
L(\chi_{\theta})
.
King showed
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
that for quiver representations,
(semi-)stability with respect to this line bundle is equivalent to
the notion of
\theta
-(semi-)stability defined above.

The problem studied in this article is to find computational methods
for understanding the dependence of
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
on
\theta
.
A first question in this direction is to determine for which choices of
\theta

the set
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
is nonempty.
In fact, the set of such
\theta
forms a cone, which we call the
semistable cone
of
\mathbf{d}
.

2.3
.

The semistable cone of a dimension vector
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}
is

(5)

\sst(\mathbf{d})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}\mid\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}\neq\emptyset\}.

Thanks to work of Schofield and Derksen–Weyman,
the semistable cone
\sst(\mathbf{d})
can be computed via a recursive algorithm.
Let us detail. Let
\mathbf{e}
be a dimension vector with
e_{i}\leq d_{i}
for every
i\in Q_{0}
.
We write
\mathbf{e}\leq\mathbf{d}
and call
\mathbf{e}
a
subdimension vector
of
\mathbf{d}
.
We say, following Schofield
[
16
, Section 1]
,
that a property holds for a
general representation
of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}

if there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset of
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}

such that the property holds for all members of this open subset.

2.4
.

A subdimension vector
\mathbf{e}
of
\mathbf{d}
is called a
generic subdimension vector

if a general representation of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
admits
a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
. In this case we write
\mathbf{e}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{d}
.

2.5
.

The zero vector
\mathbf{0}
is generic for every
\mathbf{d}
. The subdimension vector
\mathbf{d}
is also generic for
\mathbf{d}
.

The following characterization of generic subdimension vectors
is due to Schofield; see
[
16
, Theorems 3.3 and 5.4]
.
It uses the
Euler form

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\to\mathbb{Z}
, given by

(6)

\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{y}_{i}-\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}\mathbf{x}_{s(a)}\mathbf{y}_{t(a)}.

2.6
.

For a subdimension vector
\mathbf{e}
of
\mathbf{d}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

The vector
\mathbf{e}
is a generic subdimension vector of
\mathbf{d}
.

(ii)

Every representation in
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
has a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
.

(iii)

For all generic subdimension vectors
\mathbf{f}
of
\mathbf{e}
we have
\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e}\rangle\geq 0
.

The last item gives a recursive algorithm for computing the generic subdimension vectors of
\mathbf{d}
,
the base case being covered by
Example

2.5
.
The semistable cone can be characterized in terms of generic subdimension vectors.
The following result appears in
[
8
, Theorem 3]
for integer-valued
stability parameters and quivers without oriented cycles, so we give a proof here.

2.7
.

Let
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}_{\geq 0}
be a dimension vector. Then

(7)

\sst(\mathbf{d})=\{\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}\mid\theta(\mathbf{e})\leq 0\text{ for all }\mathbf{e}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{d}\}.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
is not empty.
Then there exists a
\theta
-semistable representation
M
of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
.
By
Theorem

2.6
, it has a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}

and by semistability,
\theta(\mathbf{e})\leq 0
must hold.

Conversely, suppose that
\theta(\mathbf{e})\leq 0
for all
\mathbf{e}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{d}
.
For any subdimension vector
\mathbf{f}
of
\mathbf{d}
that is not generic,
the set of representations
M\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}

which admit a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{f}

can be shown to be a proper closed subset of
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{d}}
(see the paragraph before
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
).
The complement of the finite union of all such subsets
is thus open and dense, and all of its points are
\theta
-semistable by construction.
∎

It is possible that the set of generic subdimension vectors of
\mathbf{d}

is the set
\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{d}\}
; in this case, we have
\sst(\mathbf{d})={}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
(see
Example

7.1
).

3.
Walls

A representation
M
of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
is
strictly semistable

for
\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
if it is
\theta
-semistable but not
\theta
-stable.
Our first objective is to give an algorithm to determine
the set of
\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
for which
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
contains a strictly semistable point.
With this in mind, we define sets
W_{\mathbf{e}}
whose union will be shown
to be the set of
\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
such that
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}

contains a strictly semistable point.
If
\mathbf{e}
is a subdimension vector of
\mathbf{d}
such that
e_{i}<d_{i}
for some
i\in Q_{0}
,
we say that
\mathbf{e}
is a
proper
subdimension vector of
\mathbf{d}
.

3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{e}
be a nonzero proper subdimension vector of
\mathbf{d}
. Define

W_{\mathbf{e}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}\mid\theta(\mathbf{e})=0,\text{ and there exists }(N,M)\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{e}}\times\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}\text{ such that }N\subseteq M\}.

In other words,
W_{\mathbf{e}}
is the set of
\theta\in\sst(\mathbf{d})
with a semistable representation
destabilized by a subrepresentation of dimension
\mathbf{e}
. From this the following lemma is immediate.

3.2
.

Let
\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
.
Then
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
contains a strictly semistable point
if and only if
\theta\in W_{\mathbf{e}}
for some nonzero proper subdimension vector
\mathbf{e}
of
\mathbf{d}
.

Our first result is the following,
which in combination with
Theorem

2.7
allows to effectively compute
the locus in
\sst(\mathbf{d})
of stability parameters with strictly semistable points.

3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{e}
be a nonzero proper subdimension vector of
\mathbf{d}
. Then

(8)

W_{\mathbf{e}}=\sst(\mathbf{e})\cap\sst(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e}).

The proof uses the following lemma.

3.4
.

Let
0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0
be a short exact sequence of representations of
Q
.
Then,
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
are
\theta
-semistable if and only if
M
is
\theta
-semistable and
\theta(M^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

Assume
M
is
\theta
-semistable and
\theta(M^{\prime})=0
. Since every subrepresentation of
M^{\prime}
is a subrepresentation of
M
, we see that
M^{\prime}
is
\theta
-semistable. By additivity of
\theta
we have
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})=0
. Moreover, a subrepresentation
N^{\prime\prime}
of
M^{\prime\prime}
lifts to a subrepresentation
N
of
M
fitting into an exact sequence

(9)

0\to M^{\prime}\to N\to N^{\prime\prime}\to 0.

By semistability of
M
we have
\theta(N)\leq 0
, and by additivity of
\theta
we have
\theta(N^{\prime\prime})\leq 0.
So
M^{\prime\prime}
is
\theta
-semistable.

Conversely assume
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
are
\theta
-semistable. Then
\theta(M)=\theta(M^{\prime})+\theta(M^{\prime\prime})=0
. Now let
N\subset M
be a subrepresentation, let
N^{\prime}
be the preimage in
M^{\prime}
, and let
N^{\prime\prime}
be the image in
M^{\prime\prime}
. We have a short exact sequence

(10)

0\to N^{\prime}\to N\to N^{\prime\prime}\to 0,

and since
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
are semistable we have
\theta(N^{\prime})\leq 0
and
\theta(N^{\prime\prime})\leq 0
. It follows that
\theta(N)\leq 0
and
M
is
\theta
-semistable.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.3
.

Suppose
\theta\in\sst(\mathbf{e})\cap\sst(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e})
.
Then
\theta(\mathbf{e})=0
, and we have representations
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}

of dimension vectors
\mathbf{e}
and
\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e}
, respectively, that are
\theta
-semistable.
It follows from
Lemma

3.4
that
M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime}
is
\theta
-semistable of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
and has a subrepresentation
M^{\prime}
of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\theta
is in
W_{\mathbf{e}}
, i.e.,
there is a
\theta
-semistable representation
M

of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
having a subrepresentation
M^{\prime}

of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
, and
\theta(\mathbf{e})=0
.
It follows from
Lemma

3.4
that
M^{\prime}
is
\theta
-semistable
of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
and
M/M^{\prime}
is
\theta
-semistable
of dimension vector
\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e}
.
So
\theta\in\sst(\mathbf{e})\cap\sst(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e})
as claimed.
∎

We note that
W_{\mathbf{e}}
is always contained in the subspace
H_{\mathbf{e}}
of
{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
, where

(11)

H_{\mathbf{e}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}\mid\theta(\mathbf{e})=0\}={}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}\cap{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{e}}.

Examples

7.1
and
7.2
are cases where
W_{\mathbf{e}}

is a proper subset of
H_{\mathbf{e}}\cap\sst(\mathbf{d})
.
In general,
W_{\mathbf{e}}
can only be a proper subset
of
H_{\mathbf{e}}\cap\sst(\mathbf{d})
if
\mathbf{e}
is not
a generic subdimension vector,
as shown below.

3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{e}
be a generic proper nonzero subdimension vector of
\mathbf{d}
. Then

(12)

W_{\mathbf{e}}=H_{\mathbf{e}}\cap\sst(\mathbf{d}).

Proof.

The inclusion
W_{\mathbf{e}}\subseteq H_{\mathbf{e}}
is immediate.
For the reverse inclusion, consider a stability parameter
\theta\in{H_{\mathbf{e}}}\cap\sst(\mathbf{d})
.
Then
\theta(\mathbf{e})=0
and
\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
,
and
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
is not empty.
Since
\mathbf{e}
is generic, every representation in
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}

has a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
.
In particular, we have a pair
(N,M)\in\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{e}}\times\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
,
showing that
\theta\in W_{\mathbf{e}}
.
∎

4.
GIT equivalence

Two stability parameters
\theta,\eta
are
GIT equivalent
if
\mathrm{R}^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}=\mathrm{R}^{\eta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}_{\mathbf{d}}
.
In this section we give an algorithm to determine
when
\theta
and
\eta
are GIT equivalent.
Our method uses the walls
W_{\mathbf{e}}
from
Section

3
and general properties of twisted affine GIT.

4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{d}
be a dimension vector,
let
L\subset{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
be
an affine subspace
and let
\theta\in L
.
We say
\theta
is
L-generic
if there exists
\epsilon>0

such that
\eta
and
\theta
are GIT equivalent for all

be a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}
.
To show that
\mathbf{e}
is not special we must show that
M
is not
\theta\text{\penalty-\penalty\hskip 0.0pt}
stable for any
\theta\in{}^{\bot}{\mathbf{d}}
.
For any
\mathbf{e}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{e}
, the representation
M^{\prime}
has a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{e}^{\prime}

and hence
M
does as well, so if (i) holds then
M
is not stable.
Similarly, for any
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e}
, the representation
M/M^{\prime}

has a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
, and its preimage in
M

is a subrepresentation of dimension vector
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}+\mathbf{d}
,
so if (ii) holds then
M
is not stable either.
∎

It is shown in
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]
that each hyperplane in the wall system
of
(Q,\mathbf{d})
is orthogonal to a special subdimension vector.
We can use
Proposition

6.3
to show that the converse does not hold;
that is, if
\mathbf{e}
is special,
H_{\mathbf{e}}
need not be a hyperplane in the wall system.
This occurs in
Examples

7.1
and
7.2
.

7.6
.

For a quiver and dimension vector
(Q,\mathbf{d})
,
either the walls do not span
\sst(\mathbf{d})
,
or the walls contain all of
\sst(\mathbf{d})
.
The analogous statement is not true for the Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT) fan
determined by an arbitrary representation of a general linear group.
For instance, take
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}GL(2)
and
V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{3}\oplus M_{2\times 4}
,
where
\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{3}
is the space of homogeneous polynomials
of degree
3
and
M_{2\times 4}
is the space of
2\times 4
matrices.
Let
G
act on
\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{3}
via its natural action on the space of functions
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
and let
G
act on
M_{2\times 4}
by left multiplication.
The additional choice of a character
\theta
of
GL(2)

determines open loci
V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}\subseteq V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}\subseteq V

of stable and semistable points, respectively (see e.g.
[
15
, Definition 2.1]
).
The GIT fan for
(G,V)
is pictured below: it is a fan in the character lattice of
GL(2)
,
tensored with
\mathbb{R}
,
with one cone for each of the possible values of
V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}
.

\theta(g)=\det(g)^{-1}
\theta(g)=\det(g)
\theta(g)=Id

In this case there are three cones:
a
0
- and
1
-dimensional cones where
V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}\neq\,V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}

(the “walls”, colored blue), and a
1
-dimensional cone
where
V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{sst}}\neq V^{\theta\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{st}}
.
In particular the support of the GIT cone of this representation
is the whole line (shaded yellow)
and the walls are a proper subset whose span is the whole semistable cone.
It follows from the equivalence of (i) and (iii) in
Theorem

5.1

that this fan could never be the GIT fan for a quiver representation.

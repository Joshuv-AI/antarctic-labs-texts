Pseudo-chiral phonon splitting from octupolar magnetic order Preprint: APS/123-QED
Ruairidh Sutcliffe ϕ, Kathleen Hart ϕ, Swati Chaudhary ϕ, Arun Paramekanti

Abstract
Motivated by the recent discovery of anomalously large magnetic response of chiral phonons in dipolar magnets, we explore an extension to study Einstein quantum phonon modes coupled to multipolar moments. We consider the case of non-Kramers Γ 3 \Gamma_{3} doublets which encapsulate quadrupolar and Ising octupolar degrees of freedom, and which feature a symmetry-allowed linear coupling between local quadrupolar moments and Raman active E g E_{g} phonon modes ( d x 2 − y 2 , d 3 ​ z 2 − r 2 ) (d_{x^{2}-y^{2}},d_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}) . We show that either octupolar or quadrupolar ordering leads to degeneracy breaking of the E g E_{g} phonon doublet, with ferro-octupolar order favoring pseudochiral phonon eigenmodes with a detectable energy splitting. We describe this physics using a path integral approach in the limit where ‘fast’ phonon modes sense the ‘slow’ pseudospins as a static background which we average over using Monte Carlo simulations. We discuss implications for materials such as Ba 2 CaOsO 6 and PrV 2 Al 20 where Raman spectroscopy of phonons could be used as a potential probe of hidden octupolar order. Our work extends the important concept of chiral phonons to a large class of multipolar magnets.

Discussion:

In this work, we propose a new probe for multipolar magnetic order which leverages symmetry-breaking imprint on phonons. While recent works
Wu et al. 2025
;
Che et al. 2025
have observed the signatures of magnetic dipolar order-induced phonon chirality and mode splitting in Raman spectroscopy, its relevance to higher-order moments remain unexplored. Using a path-integral approach, we show a FO order induced pseudochiral phonon splitting
\sim\!1
meV, similar to values reported in Ref.
Wu et al. 2025
;
Che et al. 2025
for dipolar magnets,
which should be observable via Raman spectroscopy. Furthermore, the split phonon modes are not circularly polarized; instead, they are characterized by PAM along [111] axis. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such prediction, which does not rely on magnetic field, net magnetization, non-trivial band topology, or even on the circular motion of ions.

Interestingly, while our results for the normalized temperature-dependent mode splitting
qualitatively tracks the FO order
as highlighted in Fig.
4
, it is
not
simply proportional to the FO order parameter.
We trace this back to the fact that the splitting in the FO phase is governed by the
\gamma_{\bf{r}}
off-diagonal term in
{\cal G}^{-1}({\bf{r}},i\nu_{n})
,
which tracks only
the
direction
but not the magnitude of the effective local Weiss fields. We noticed a similar discrepancy between mode splitting and the
magnetic order parameter
in recent experimental results on the ferrimagnetic insulator Fe
1.75
Zn
0.25
Mo
3
O
8

Wu et al. 2025
. We believe that our
approach can capture such effects as arising from spatial non-uniformities of the order parameter.

In summary, our phonon-based proposal offers an accessible route to probing octupolar orders with optical spectroscopy,
complementing other tools such as magnetostriction
Patri et al. 2019
;
Patri et al. 2020
, polarized neutron scattering
Lovesey and Khalyavin 2020
,
NMR
Tokunaga et al. 2006
;
Liu et al. 2018
;
Cong et al. 2023
;
Voleti et al. 2023
, and X-ray diffraction

Hirai et al. 2020
. Using such complementary probes could shed valuable light on complex multipolar orders in
a wide variety of quantum materials. Moreover, the theoretical approach used here can accommodate more general TRS breaking scenarios and might find applications in a wide variety of antiferromagnets,
altermagnets
Šmejkal et al. 2022
;
Jungwirth et al. 2024
;
Jungwirth et al. 2025
,
or even in the pseudo-gap phase of cuprates where thermal Hall measurements have hinted at chiral phonons
Grissonnanche et al. 2020
;
Grissonnanche et al. 2019
.

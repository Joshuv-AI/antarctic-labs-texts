Rapid interval progression of familial atrial myxoma despite negative screening
Authors: Monisha Dsouza, Rajesh Gopalan Nair, Rajesh Sadanandan, Carlos Nicolas Perez-Garcia, Andreas Mitsis, Giulia Bruno
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 14-year-old girl with biopsy-proven extracardiac myxomas presented with recurrent cardioembolic stroke due to a rapidly developing giant left atrial myxoma. A pathogenic PRKAR1A variant and maternal history confirmed familial Carney complex. Urgent surgical excision prevented further embolization, emphasizing the aggressive and unpredictable nature of familial cardiac myxomas.Keywords: Left atrial mass, Myxoma, Carney complex, Stroke

A 14-year-old girl presented with generalized tonic–clonic seizures one year after excision of biopsy-proven cutaneous and nerve sheath myxomas from the eyelid and earlobe (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1). Screening transthoracic echocardiography performed 11 months earlier had been normal. Brain magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated multiple acute and subacute cardioembolic cerebral infarcts (Figure 1B). Transthoracic echocardiography revealed a large, highly mobile, multilobulated left atrial mass (58 × 32 mm) causing severe dynamic left ventricular inflow obstruction (peak/mean transmitral gradient 23/11 mmHg) with eccentric mitral regurgitation (Figure 1C, D; Supplementary material online, Videos S1 and S2). The combination of recurrent extracardiac myxomas, a maternal history of recurrent biopsy-proven right atrial myxomas, and identification of a pathogenic PRKAR1A mutation established the diagnosis of familial Carney complex (see Supplementary material online, Figure S2).1,2

Multipanel imaging: (A) Extra-cardiac myxomas near left lower eyelid ( nerve sheath myxoma) and left earlobe (cutaneous myxoma) post excision (C, D) 2D and 3D echocardiography showing large friable left atrial mass (58 × 32 mm) (green arrow) with severe mitral inflow obstruction (red arrow) during diastole and mild mitral regurgitation (yellow arrow); (B) neuroimaging with CT brain (B i) showing infarct in left lentiform nucleus (green arrow) and MRI (B ii) brain showing new acute watershed infarcts (green arrows) post-right brachial monoplegia; (E) gross specimen of the gelatinous, haemorrhagic left atrial myxoma. (F) Histopathology showing stellate cells embedded in an abundant, loose myxoid stroma (green arrow), (G) positive Alcian blue special staining of mucopolysaccharide-rich myxoid stroma (green arrow).

Within 24 h of admission, the patient developed acute right-sided hemiplegia. Repeat neurological imaging showed multiple new acute watershed infarcts, consistent with ongoing tumour embolization (Figure 1B-i, B-ii; Supplementary material online, Figure S3). Despite the evolving ischaemic stroke, emergency surgical excision was undertaken because the anticipated benefit of preventing further cerebral and systemic embolic events from the friable, mobile mass outweighed the perioperative neurological risk. The gelatinous tumour was removed piecemeal (Figure 1E; Supplementary material online, Figure S4). Alcian blue staining showed hypocellular mucopolysaccharide-rich myxoid stroma with scattered stellate and polygonal myxoma cells (Figure 1F, G; Supplementary material online, Figures S5 and S6). The patient recovered with progressive neurological improvement and remains asymptomatic at 6-month follow-up.

Familial cardiac myxomas in Carney complex may develop rapidly and become embolic despite a previously normal echocardiogram. Individuals with high-risk features like a pathogenic PRKAR1A variant, a strong family history, or preceding extracardiac myxomas may require more frequent echocardiographic surveillance over the routine annual imaging to facilitate earlier detection of rapidly growing cardiac myxomas.3 Following confirmation of the PRKAR1A mutation, cascade clinical screening with echocardiography and hormonal evaluation was completed for all at-risk first-degree relatives and was normal; targeted genetic testing remains a future objective for the family.

Eigenvalue Estimate for the Rough Laplacian on 1 1 -Forms and its Applications
Teng Huang, Weiwei Wang

Abstract
In this article, we establish a geometric lower bound for the first positive eigenvalue λ 1 ( 1 ) \lambda^{(1)}_{1} of the rough Laplacian acting on 1 1 -forms for closed 2 ​ n 2n -dimensional Riemannian manifolds with nonvanishing Euler characteristic. In contrast to the case of functions, such a Li-Yau-type estimate does not hold in general, as evidenced by existing counterexamples. Under assumptions including a lower bound on Ricci curvature, an upper bound on diameter, and an L 2 ​ p L^{2p} -norm bound on the Riemann curvature tensor, we prove that λ 1 ( 1 ) \lambda^{(1)}_{1} is bounded below by a positive constant depending on these parameters. As applications, we derive vanishing results for the Euler characteristic under certain Ricci curvature bounds and the presence of a nonzero Killing vector field, extending classical Bochner-type theorems.

Introduction

Let
(X,g)
be a closed and connected Riemannian manifold and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection associated to the metric
g
. We consider the
rough Laplacian

\overline{\Delta}=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla

acting on differential
p
-forms. This is a second-order elliptic operator whose spectrum consists of an unbounded sequence of real eigenvalues
\{\lambda^{(p)}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that can be arranged in increasing order as

0=\lambda^{(p)}_{0}<\lambda^{(p)}_{1}\leq\lambda^{(p)}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda^{(p)}_{k}\leq\lambda^{(p)}_{k+1}\leq\cdots\nearrow+\infty.

Here,
\lambda^{(p)}_{0}
is the zero eigenvalue whose its multiplicity equals
\dim\ker\nabla
. If
\dim\ker\nabla=0
, or equivalently if
(X,g)
admits no nonzero parallel
p
-forms, then
\lambda^{(p)}_{0}
does not exist and the spectrum conventionally begins with
\lambda^{(p)}_{1}>0
. When
0
is not in the spectrum, every nonzero
p
-form
\alpha
on
X
satisfies the Rayleigh quotient inequality

R(\alpha)=\frac{\int_{X}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}}{\int_{X}|\alpha|^{2}}\geq\lambda^{(p)}_{1}.

Through the associated Weitzenböck formulas, the rough Laplacian is intimately related to other Laplace-type operators, such as the Dirac Laplacian, the Hodge Laplacian on
p
-forms, and Schrödinger operators (cf.
[
8
,
33
]
). Understanding the eigenvalues of the rough Laplacian is crucial in geometry and mathematical physics. There are many related results, see for instance
[

,

,

,

We recall some classical results for the smallest positive eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{(0)}
on functions (i.e.
0
-forms), whose geometry is well studied. Cheeger
[
13
]
and Buser
[

]
showed that
\lambda_{1}^{(0)}
is comparable to the square of the Cheeger isoperimetric constant of

. Yau provided more easily computable geometric quantities, such as diameter, volume, and lower bounds of Ricci curvature, to estimate the lower bound of
\lambda_{1}^{(0)}
. Li and Yau further developed a gradient estimate technique for the first eigenfunction, leading to an effective lower bound for

0
-form obeys

\lambda^{(0)}_{1}D^{2}(g)\geq c^{-1}(n)\exp\{-[1+(1+2c^{2}(n)\Lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}]\}.

(1.1)

We are interested in the lower bounds of the smallest positive eigenvalue

More recently, Boulanger and Courtois in

A Riemannian manifold

\operatorname{sec}(g_{i})\geq-\frac{1}{i},\ and\ D(g_{i})\leq 1,

Remark 1.4

The spectrum of

Colbois and Courtois in

proved that if a closed

|{\rm{sec}}_{g}|\leq K,\ {\rm{diam}}(X)\leq D,\ and\ {\rm{Vol}}(g)\geq\nu,

\mu^{(p)}_{1}\geq C(n,p,K,D,V).

The main idea of their proof is based on the fact that the space of manifolds satisfying the conditions of their theorem is compact. Although we can use the same reasoning to show that

Let

where

Remark 1.6
.

Chen and Han
[
16
]
proved that if

is a closed
2n
-dimensional complex manifold with nonzero holomorphic Euler characteristic, then for any fixed
\lambda_{1}>0

A similar conclusion holds when

If one could show that the Killing vector field is everywhere nonvanishing under these curvature conditions, then by Bott’s results

Preliminaries

2.1
Spectral Theory of the Rough Laplacian

In this section, we recall basic facts on the rough Laplacian. (For a general reference, see Appendix of
[
19
]
or
[
32
]
). Let
(X,g)
be a closed connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
(E,\nabla)
be a Riemannian vector bundle with finite-dimensional fiber over
X
–that is,
E
is a vector bundle equipped with a smooth metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and a compatible connection
\nabla
. On the space
\Gamma(E)
of smooth sections of
E
, we define the
L^{2}
-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
g
. The connection
\nabla
extends naturally to
p
-tensors on
X
taking values in
E
. Its formal adjoint
\nabla^{*}
with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product defines the rough Laplacian (or connection Laplacian) acting on
\Gamma(E)
by

\overline{\Delta}=\nabla^{*}\nabla.

The spectrum of
\overline{\Delta}
is discrete and nonnegative, and can be written as

0=\lambda_{0}(E)<\lambda_{1}(E)\leq\lambda_{2}(E)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}(E)\leq\lambda_{k+1}(E)\leq\cdots\nearrow+\infty.

For a nonzero section
s\in\Gamma(E)
, the Rayleigh quotient is given by

R(s)=\frac{\|\nabla s\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}}{\|s\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}}.

Let us define
E_{0}:=\ker\nabla
, which is a finite dimensional vectorial subspace of
\Gamma(E)
. Every section
\alpha\in E_{0}
is an eigenfunction corresponding to the zero eigenvalue. Define the orthogonal complement

H_{0}=\{\alpha\in\Gamma(E)|\forall\alpha_{0}\in E_{0},(\alpha,a_{0})=0\}.

Then the first positive eigenvalue satisfies

\lambda_{1}(E)=\inf\{R(\alpha)|\alpha\in H_{0},\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\neq 0\}.

A classical theorem of Hopf states that the Euler characteristic of a closed Riemannian manifold equals the signed count of zeros of a generic vector field. It follows that if a closed Riemannian manifold admits a nonzero parallel vector field, then its Euler characteristic must vanish.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with nonvanishing Euler characteristic
\chi(X)\neq 0
. Then

\lambda^{(1)}_{1}=\inf\{R(\alpha)|\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(X),\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\neq 0\}.

Proof.

Since
\chi(X)\neq 0
, the manifold admits no nontrivial parallel
1
-forms–otherwise
\chi(X)
would be zero. Hence

E_{0}=\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)=0,

and therefore
H_{0}=\Omega^{1}(X)
. We then get

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}=\inf_{\alpha\neq 0}R(\alpha).

∎

2.2
A Poincaré-Sobolev Inequality under Curvature Bounds

In this section, we recall a Poincaré-type inequality (see
[
7
,
14
]
). For
1\leq q
, let
p
satisfy
1\leq p\leq\frac{nq}{n-q}
and
p<\infty
. Define the Sobolev constant
\Sigma(n,p,q)
of the canonical unit sphere
S^{n}
by

\Sigma(n,p,q)=\sup\left\{\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}}}{\|df\|_{L^{q}}}:f\in L^{q}_{1}(S^{n}),\ f\neq 0,\ \int_{S^{n}}f=0\right\}.

Suppose a closed Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
satisfies

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-(n-1)\kappa\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\operatorname{diam}(X)\leq D(g).

Then there exists a positive number
R(\Lambda)=\frac{D(g)}{\Lambda C(\Lambda)}
, where
\Lambda=\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}
(see
[
7
, Appendix I, Theorem 2]
), and
C(\Lambda)
is the unique positive root of the equation

x\int_{0}^{\Lambda}(\cosh t+x\sinh t)^{n-1}dt=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}t\,dt,

such that the following Poincaré inequality holds (see
[
7
, Page 397]
or
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold. Then for each
1\leq p\leq\frac{nq}{n-q}
,
p<\infty
, and
f\in L^{q}_{1}(X)
, we have

\left\|f-\bar{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq S_{p,q}\|df\|_{L^{q}(X)},

where

\bar{f}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{X}f,

and

S_{p,q}=\left(\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}{\operatorname{Vol}(S^{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}R(\Lambda)\Sigma(n,p,q).

Chen
[
14
, Lemma 5.6]
established a lower bound for
\Lambda C(\Lambda)
as
\Lambda\to 0
. Following his approach, we derive a lower bound for
\Lambda C(\Lambda)
that depends only on
\Lambda
and
n
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C(\Lambda)
be the unique positive root of the equation

x\int_{0}^{\Lambda}(\cosh t+x\sinh t)^{n-1}dt=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}t\,dt.

Then

\Lambda C(\Lambda)\geq a_{n}e^{-(n-1)\Lambda}>0

for some constant
a_{n}
depending only on
n
.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{n}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}t\,dt
. Then

\omega_{n}=C(\Lambda)\int_{0}^{\Lambda}(\cosh t+C(\Lambda)\sinh t)^{n-1}dt\geq\Lambda C(\Lambda).

On the other hand, for any
\Lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=C(\Lambda)\int_{0}^{\Lambda}(\cosh t+C(\Lambda)\sinh t)^{n-1}dt

\displaystyle\leq C(\Lambda)\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\left(\frac{e^{\Lambda}+e^{-\Lambda}}{2}+C(\Lambda)\frac{e^{\Lambda}-e^{-\Lambda}}{2}\right)^{n-1}dt

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda C(\Lambda)\cosh^{n-1}(\Lambda)\left(1+\omega_{n}\frac{\tanh(\Lambda)}{\Lambda}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda C(\Lambda)e^{(n-1)\Lambda}(1+\omega_{n})^{n-1}.

Here we use the fact that
\frac{\tanh x}{x}
is monotonically decreasing for
x>0
, and that

\sup_{x>0}\frac{\tanh x}{x}=1.

Hence, for some constant
a_{n}=\omega_{n}(1+\omega_{n})^{1-n}
depending only on
n
, we obtain

\Lambda C(\Lambda)\geq a_{n}e^{-(n-1)\Lambda}>0.

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-(n-1)\kappa\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\operatorname{diam}(X)\leq D(g).

If
f\in L^{2}_{1}(X)
, then

\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C(n)De^{(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}\|df\|_{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we get

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}&\leq\|f-\bar{f}\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}+\|\bar{f}\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\\
&\leq C(n)De^{(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}+|\bar{f}|\\
&\leq C(n)De^{(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}+\|f\|_{2}.\\
\end{split}

∎

Applying
[
7
, Appendix V, Theorem 3]
, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 2.5
.

(
[
14
, Theorem 5.2]
)
Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying the Sobolev inequality:

\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}\|df\|_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }f\in L^{2}_{1}(X).

If
u\in L^{2}_{1}(X)
is a nonnegative continuous function satisfying

u\Delta_{d}u\leq cu^{2}

in the sense of distributions for some constant
c>0
, then

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\left\{C(n)\sqrt{c}C_{s}\right\}\|u\|_{2}.

2.3
Anderson’s Counterexample to Li-Yau Type Estimates

In this section, we aim to demonstrate that there exist manifolds for which the lower bound estimates of the eigenvalues of the rough Laplacian acting on
1
-forms do not satisfy the Li-Yau type inequality.

We first recall the following key result.

Lemma 2.6
.

(cf.
[
35
,
36
]
)
Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold such that

\dim\left(\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)\right)=m>0.

Then
X
is a fiber bundle over the
m
-torus.

Proof.

Let
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)=\operatorname{span}\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m}\}
. By an argument of Tischler
[
35
]
, there exist differentiable functions
\varphi_{i}:X\to S^{1}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, such that
\varphi_{i}^{*}(dt)=\omega_{i}
. The map
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m})
is a submersion from
X
onto an
m
-torus
T^{m}
, and
X
is a fiber bundle over
T^{m}
.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-(n-1)\kappa,\ and\ {\rm{diam}}(X)\leq D.

We say the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
1
-forms satisfies Li-Yau type inequality if there is a positive constant
C(n)
such that

\lambda^{(1)}_{1}D^{2}\geq C(n)e^{-C(n)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}.

We observe that if a closed manifold admits a metric such that
\kappa D^{2}
is small and
\lambda_{1}^{(1)}
satisfies the Li-Yau type inequality, then the closed manifold is a fiber bundle over
T^{b_{1}}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with
b_{1}(X)>0
. Suppose that the Ricci curvature satisfies

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-(n-1)\kappa,\ and\ {\rm{diam}}(X)\leq D

and the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
1
-forms satisfies Li-Yau type inequality. Then there is a positive constant
c(n)
such that if
\kappa D^{2}\leq c(n)
, then we have

\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)=\mathcal{H}_{d}^{1}(X).

In particular,
X
is a fiber bundle over a
b_{1}
-torus.

Proof.

Note that both
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}:=\ker\Delta_{d}\cap\Omega^{1}
are finite-dimensional linear spaces, and

\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}.

We decompose

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}=\left(\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}\right)\oplus\left[\left(\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}\right)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}\right].

It suffices to show that the second summand is trivial.

Suppose, for contradiction, that

\left(\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}\right)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}\neq\{0\}.

Then there exists a nonzero
1
-form
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}
such that
\nabla\alpha\neq 0
. Write

\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{0}^{\perp},

where
\alpha_{0}\in\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}
and
\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\in\left(\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}\right)^{\perp}
. Clearly,
\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\neq 0
. By the Bochner formula,

0=\Delta_{d}\alpha=\Delta_{d}\alpha_{0}^{\perp}=\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha_{0}^{\perp}+\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha_{0}^{\perp}).

Taking the inner product with
\alpha_{0}^{\perp}
and integrating gives

D^{2}\|\nabla\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\|^{2}\leq(n-1)\kappa D^{2}\|\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\|^{2}.

On the other hand, the eigenvalue assumption implies

D^{2}\|\nabla\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}^{(1)}D^{2}\|\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\|^{2}\geq C(n)e^{-C(n)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}\|\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\|^{2}.

Combining these inequalities yields

\left(C(n)e^{-C(n)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}-(n-1)\kappa D^{2}\right)\|\alpha_{0}^{\perp}\|^{2}\leq 0.

Now, choosing
c(n)
small enough ensures that if
\kappa D^{2}\leq c(n)
then

C(n)e^{-C(n)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}>(n-1)\kappa D^{2},

which forces
\alpha_{0}^{\perp}=0
, a contradiction. Hence,

\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{d}.

∎

Andersen in
[
1
]
gave explicit counterexamples to Gromov’s conjecture

Theorem 2.9
.

(
[
1
, Theorem 0.4]
)
For any
n\geq 4
,
k\leq n-1
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exist closed
n
-manifolds
X
satisfying

\operatorname{diam}(X)^{2}\cdot\|\mathrm{Ric}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptb_{1}(X)=k,

such that no cover of
X
fibers over
S^{1}
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2

.

Through Proposition
2.8
, we can conclude that on the manifold constructed by Andersen, the eigenvalue
\lambda^{(1)}_{1}
does not satisfy the Li-Yau type inequality. Otherwise, the manifold would necessarily be a fiber bundle over
T^{b_{1}}
, which contradicts Theorem
2.9
.
∎

3.1
L^{\infty}
Estimates for Eigenforms of Rough Laplacian

Lemma 3.1
.

(cf.
[
34
, Lemma 4.1]
)
Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying the Sobolev inequality:

\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}\|df\|_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }f\in L^{2}_{1}(X).

Suppose
\theta
is a
p
-form such that

\nabla^{*}\nabla\theta=\lambda\theta,

where
\lambda
is a positive constant. Then

\|\theta\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\left\{C(n)\sqrt{\lambda}C_{s}\right\}\|\theta\|_{2}.

(3.1)

Proof.

For any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(X)
, the following identity holds:

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\alpha|^{2}=|\nabla\alpha|^{2}-\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha,\alpha\rangle.

Applying this to
\theta
and using Kato’s inequality
|\nabla|\theta||\leq|\nabla\theta|
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla\theta,\theta\rangle-|\nabla\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda|\theta|^{2}-\left|\nabla|\theta|\right|^{2}.

On the other hand, we have the pointwise identity:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\theta|^{2}=|\theta|\cdot\Delta_{d}|\theta|+\left|\nabla|\theta|\right|^{2}.

Combining these yields:

|\theta|\cdot\Delta_{d}|\theta|\leq\lambda|\theta|^{2}.

The estimate now follows directly from Theorem
2.5
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
2p>n
, and let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-manifold satisfying the Sobolev inequality:

\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}\|df\|_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }f\in L^{2}_{1}(X).

Suppose
\theta\in\Omega^{p}(X)
is a
p
-form such that

\nabla^{*}\nabla\theta=\lambda\theta

where
\lambda
is a positive constant.

Then there exists a constant
C(n,p)>0
such that

\begin{split}&D\|\nabla\theta\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\min\{C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}}\|\theta\|_{2},C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\beta}(\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}})^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\exp\{C(n,p)\sqrt{\lambda}C_{s}\}\|\theta\|_{2}\},\\
\end{split}

(3.2)

where
\alpha=\frac{2pn}{2p-n}
and
\beta=\frac{2pn}{2p-n+pn}
,
t=4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}
,
B=\lambda+\|\mathrm{Ric}^{-}\|_{p}+\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}
.

To prove Proposition
3.2
, we require a commutation lemma (see
[
4
,
5
,
29
]
):

Lemma 3.3
.

For any smooth section
S\in\Gamma(E)
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}(|\nabla S|)+|\nabla^{2}S|\leq\langle\nabla^{\ast}R^{E}S,\nabla S\rangle+{\mathrm{Ric}}^{-}|\nabla S|^{2}+\langle\nabla\bar{\Delta}S,\nabla S\rangle+|R^{E}|\cdot|\nabla S|^{2},

(3.3)

where for vector fields
U,V
on
X
,

R^{E}_{U,V}=\nabla^{2}_{U,V}-\nabla^{2}_{V,U}

is the curvature of
E
, and

\langle\nabla^{\ast}R^{E}S,\nabla S\rangle:=-\langle\nabla_{j}(R^{E}_{ij}S),\nabla_{i}S\rangle.

In our case, the fiber bundle
E
is the cotangent bundle and
S\in\Omega^{1}(X)
is a differential
1
-form, so the bundle curvature
R^{E}
is identified with the Riemann curvature tensor
\mathrm{Riem}
.
We now present the proof of Proposition
3.2
, whose main technical idea originates from Aubry
[
4
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

For simplicity, we denote the Riemann curvature tensor
\mathrm{Riem}
as
R
throughout the proof. Let
u=|\nabla\theta|
. We have the identity

u(\Delta_{d}u)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}(u^{2})+|\nabla u|^{2}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.3
, we can get for any
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}|d(u^{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=k^{2}\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}u^{2(k-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{2}}{2k-1}\int_{X}\langle u^{2k-1},\Delta_{d}u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2k-1}\int_{X}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}(u^{2})+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)u^{2(k-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2k-1}\left(\int_{X}\langle{\mathrm{Ric}}^{-}u^{2k}+\int_{X}\langle\nabla\bar{\Delta}\theta,\nabla\theta\rangle u^{2(k-1)}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\left.\int_{X}\langle\nabla^{*}R\theta,\nabla\theta\rangle u^{2(k-1)}+\int_{X}|R|u^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2k-1}\left(\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{Ric}^{-}u^{2k}+\int_{X}(\lambda+|R|)u^{2k}+\int_{X}\langle\nabla^{*}R\theta,\nabla\theta\rangle u^{2(k-1)}\right).

We now estimeate the last term
\int_{X}\langle\nabla^{*}R\theta,\nabla\theta\rangle u^{2(k-1)}
. Using the relation

|ab|\leq|a|^{2}+\frac{|b|^{2}}{4}\leq|a|^{2}+\frac{|b|^{2}}{2}

and the divergence theorem, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\langle\nabla^{*}R\theta,\nabla\theta\rangle u^{2(k-1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\nabla^{*}R\theta,u^{2(k-1)}\nabla\theta\rangle

\displaystyle=2(k-1)\int_{X}\langle R\theta,(u^{2k-3}\nabla u)\nabla\theta\rangle+\int_{X}\langle R\theta,(\nabla^{2}\theta)u^{2(k-1)}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|R\theta|^{2}\cdot u^{2(k-1)}+2(k-1)\int_{X}|R\theta|\cdot|\nabla u|\cdot u^{2(k-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|R\theta|^{2}\cdot u^{2(k-1)}+2(k-1)\left(\int_{X}|R\theta|^{2}\cdot u^{2(k-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}u^{2(k-1)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2k-1)\int_{X}|R\theta|^{2}\cdot u^{2(k-1)}+(k-1)\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}u^{2(k-1)}.

From this estimate, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|d(u^{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k^{2}}{2k-1}\left(\int_{X}(\lambda+\mathrm{Ric}^{-})u^{2k}+\int_{X}|R|u^{2k}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\left.(2k-1)\int_{X}|R\theta|^{2}\cdot u^{2(k-1)}+\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\int_{X}|d(u^{k})|^{2}\right).

After rearranging terms, we use Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}|d(u^{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq k\left(\int_{X}(\lambda+\mathrm{Ric}^{-}+|R|)u^{2k}+(2k-1)k\int_{X}|R\theta|^{2}\cdot u^{2(k-1)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(k(\lambda+\|\mathrm{Ric}^{-}\|_{p}+\|R\|_{p})\|u\|^{2k}_{\frac{2kp}{p-1}}+4k^{2}\|R\|^{2}_{2p}\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}\|u\|^{2(k-1)}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4k^{2}B\|u\|^{2k}_{\frac{2kp}{p-1}}+4k^{2}B^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}\|u\|^{2(k-1)}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}},

where we define

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda+\|\mathrm{Ric}^{-}\|_{p}+\|R\|_{p}\leq\lambda+\|\mathrm{Ric}^{-}\|_{p}+\|R\|_{2p}:=B,

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\|R\|^{2}_{2p}\leq B^{2}.

Combing the Sobolev inequality in Theorem
2.5
and the estimate for
\int_{X}|d(u^{k})|^{2}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|u\|^{k}_{\frac{2nk}{n-2}}

\displaystyle=\|u^{k}\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\|u^{k}\|_{2}+C_{s}\|d(u^{k})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{k}\|_{2}+2C_{s}k\sqrt{B\|u\|^{2k}_{\frac{2kp}{p-1}}+B^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}\|u\|^{2(k-1)}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+2C_{s}k\sqrt{B\left(\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}\right)^{2k}+B^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}\|u\|^{2(k-1)}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\|u\|_{\infty}+2C_{s}k\sqrt{B\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}+B^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}\right)\|u\|^{k-1}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}},

where we used the interpolation inequality

\|u\|_{2k}\leq\|u\|_{\frac{2kp}{p-1}}\leq\|u\|^{\frac{1}{k}}_{\infty}\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{k}}_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}.

Therefore,

\left(\frac{\|u\|_{\frac{2nk}{n-2}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{k}\leq\left(1+2C_{s}k\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)\left(\frac{\|u\|_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{k-1}.

Taking both sides to the power of
\frac{2n}{n-2}
yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\|u\|_{\frac{2nk}{n-2}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{2nk}{n-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+2C_{s}k\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{2n(k-1)}{n-2}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+2C_{s}k\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{\gamma\frac{2(k-1)p}{p-1}},

where
\gamma=\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-2)}>1
.

Define the sequence
\{a_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
by
a_{0}=\frac{2p}{p-1}
and

a_{i+1}=\gamma a_{i}+\frac{2n}{n-2}.

Then

a_{i}=\gamma^{i}(a_{0}+\gamma_{0})-\gamma_{0},

where
\gamma_{0}=\frac{2n}{(n-2)(\gamma-1)}=\frac{2np}{2p-n}
. Let

k_{i}=\frac{p-1}{2p}a_{i}+1=\gamma^{i+1}+\gamma^{i}-\gamma+1<2\gamma^{i+1},

which satisfies

\frac{2n}{n-2}k_{i}=\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-2)}a_{i}+\frac{2n}{n-2}=a_{i+1}.

Therefore, substituting
k=k_{i}
gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\|u\|_{a_{i+1}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{a_{i+1}}{\gamma^{i+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+2C_{s}k_{i}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{i+1}}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{a_{i}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{a_{i}}{\gamma^{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{i}\left(1+2C_{s}k_{l}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{a_{0}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{a_{0}}.

To estimate the infinite product, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
t>0
,
\gamma>1
. Then

P=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1+t\gamma^{i+1})^{\frac{1}{\gamma^{i+1}}}\leq\exp\left\{\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right\}(1+\sqrt{t})^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}.

Proof.

For any
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\ln(1+tx)

\displaystyle\leq 2\ln(1+\sqrt{tx})

\displaystyle=2\ln(1+\sqrt{t})+2\ln\left(1+(\sqrt{x}-1)\frac{\sqrt{t}}{1+\sqrt{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\ln(1+\sqrt{t})+2(\sqrt{x}-1).

Hence,

\displaystyle\ln P

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(1+t\gamma^{i+1})}{\gamma^{i+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\ln(1+\sqrt{t})}{\gamma^{i+1}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\gamma^{\frac{i+1}{2}}-2}{\gamma^{i+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\ln(1+\sqrt{t})}{\gamma-1}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\gamma}-1}-\frac{2}{\gamma-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\ln(1+\sqrt{t})}{\gamma-1}+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}.

Therefore, we complete the proof of this lemma.
∎

Now we continue to prove Proposition
3.2
. Taking the limit as
i\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{a_{i+1}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{a_{i+1}}{\gamma^{i+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+2C_{s}k_{l}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{a_{0}}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{a_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+2C_{s}k_{l}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\left(\frac{\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}_{2}\|u\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{\infty}}{\|u\|_{\infty}}\right)^{a_{0}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+2C_{s}k_{l}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\left(\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{2}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+4C_{s}\gamma^{l+1}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\left(\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{2}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}\right),

where we used the interpolation

\|u\|_{a_{0}}\leq\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}_{2}\|u\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{\infty}.

Therefore,

\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+4C_{s}\gamma^{l+1}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+\frac{B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

We now consider two cases:

Case 1:
If
D^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}\geq\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}
, then

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}\gamma^{l+1}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

Let
t=4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\frac{4pn}{2p-n}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\frac{4pn}{2p-n}}\lambda\|\theta\|^{2}_{2}.

Case 2:
If
D^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}\leq\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}
, then

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\|u\|_{\infty}+4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\gamma^{l+1}\sqrt{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}+B\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{\infty}}{\|\theta\|_{\infty}}+4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\gamma^{l+1}\sqrt{\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|\theta\|^{2}_{\infty}}+B}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}\|\theta\|^{\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle{\leq\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{D}+4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{\frac{1}{D^{2}}+B}\gamma^{l+1}\right)^{\frac{2n}{(n-2)\gamma^{l+1}}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}\|\theta\|^{\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\frac{4pn}{2p-n}}D^{-\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}\|\theta\|^{\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\frac{4pn}{2p-n}}D^{-\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}\lambda\|\theta\|^{2}_{2}\|\theta\|^{\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\frac{4pn}{2p-n}}D^{-\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}\lambda\exp\{C(n)\frac{2pn}{2p-n}\sqrt{\lambda}C_{s}\}\|\theta\|^{2+\frac{2pn}{2p-n}}_{2}.

Combining the above two cases, we arrive at the inequality estimate (
3.2
).
∎

3.2
A Non-Vanishing Criterion for Small Eigenvalues

We begin by recalling an inequality for smooth functions on closed manifolds.
We denote by

\varepsilon=\min\{C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}},C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\beta}(\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}})^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\exp\{C(n,p)\sqrt{\lambda}C_{s}\}\}.

where
\alpha=\frac{2pn}{2p-n}
and
\beta=\frac{2pn}{2p-n+pn}
,
t=4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}
,
B=\lambda+\|\mathrm{Ric}^{-}\|_{p}+\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

(cf.
[
26
]
)
Let
(X,g)
be a connected, closed Riemannian manifold with diameter
D(g)
, and let
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Then for any
p,q\in X
,

|f(p)-f(q)|\leq\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}\cdot D(g).

(3.4)

Using this, we can show that when the eigenvalue is sufficiently small, the corresponding eigen
p
-form is nowhere vanishing.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying the Sobolev inequality:

\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}\|df\|_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }f\in L^{2}_{1}(X).

Suppose
\theta
is a
p
-form such that

\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\theta=\lambda\theta,

where
\lambda
is a positive constant. Then

\frac{\inf|\theta|^{2}}{\sup|\theta|^{2}}\geq 1-2\varepsilon

Proof.

Let
f=|\theta|^{2}
. Since
\nabla f=2|\theta|\nabla|\theta|
and by Kato’s inequality
|\nabla|\theta||\leq|\nabla\theta|
, we have

|\nabla f|\leq 2|\theta||\nabla\theta|.

Theorem
3.5
then implies

\sup f-\inf f\leq 2\|\nabla\theta\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\theta\|_{\infty}\cdot D(g).

By Proposition
3.2
and the fact that
\sup f\geq\|\theta\|^{2}_{2}
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\inf f\geq\sup f(1-2D\|\nabla\theta\|_{\infty})\geq\sup f(1-2\varepsilon).

∎

Recall that the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern theorem is closely related to the Poincaré–Hopf index formula, which expresses the Euler characteristic as the sum of indices of singularities of a tangent vector field. As a consequence, if the eigenvalue on
1
-forms is sufficiently small, then the Euler characteristic must vanish.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Proposition
2.4
, we may take the Sobolev constant to be

C_{s}=C(n)e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}D.

Recall that
B:=\lambda+\|\mathrm{Ric}^{-}\|_{p}+\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}
and
t=4C_{s}\sqrt{B}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}
.

Since
\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-(2n-1)\kappa
, it follows that if

\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}}\leq e^{-(2n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}},

then there exists a constant
C_{0}(n,p)>0
such that

\begin{split}1+\sqrt{t}&=1+\big(4C(n)e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{kD^{2}}}\sqrt{BD^{2}}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq 1+\sqrt{4C(n)e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{kD^{2}}}}\sqrt{1+BD^{2}}.\\
&\leq C_{0}(n,p)e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{kD^{2}}}\left(1+\sqrt{\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}}\,D\right)\end{split}

Take

\tilde{C}(n,p)=\frac{1}{(4C(n,p))^{\frac{1}{\delta}}C_{0}(n,p)}\exp\left(-\frac{C(n)}{\delta}\right),

where
\delta=\frac{2pn}{p-n}
or
\frac{2pn}{p-n+pn}
(note that the
\dim X=2n
).

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\lambda=\lambda^{(1)}_{1}
is so small that

\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}}\leq\min\left\{\left(\frac{\tilde{C}(n,p)}{1+\sqrt{\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}}\,D}e^{-(2n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2pn}{p-n}},e^{-(2n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}\right\},

We can estimate the following term in the inequality in Theorem
3.6
.

\begin{split}\varepsilon&=C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{t})^{\delta}(\sqrt{\lambda D^{2}})^{\frac{p-n}{2pn}\delta}\exp\{C(n,p)\sqrt{\lambda}C_{s}\}\\
&\leq C(n,p)\left(C_{0}(n,p)e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{kD^{2}}}\left(1+\sqrt{\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}}\,D\right)\right)^{\delta}\\
&\times\left(\frac{\tilde{C}(n,p)}{1+\sqrt{\|\mathrm{Riem}\|_{2p}}\,D}e^{-(2n-1)\sqrt{\kappa D^{2}}}\right)^{\delta}\exp\{C(n)\}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}.\\
\end{split}

Therefore, Theorem
3.6
implies

\frac{\inf|\theta|^{2}}{\sup|\theta|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

In particular, there exists a nowhere-zero
1
-form, and hence the Euler characteristic of
X
must vanish, contradicting the hypothesis
\chi(X)\neq 0
.

∎

4.1
Harmonic
1
-Forms and Vanishing Euler Characteristic

In this section, we first establish the connection between the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian acting on
1
-forms and the lower bound of the Ricci curvature.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold. Suppose the Ricci curvature of
X
satisfies

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-\kappa,

where
\kappa>0
. If
b_{1}(X)>0
, then either
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)\neq\{0\}
or

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\leq\kappa.

Proof.

Since
b_{1}(X)>0
, there exists a nonzero harmonic
1
-form
\alpha
on
X
. By the Weitzenböck formula,

0=\Delta_{d}\alpha=\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha+\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha).

Therefore, if
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)=\{0\}
, then by Lemma
2.1
we have

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\leq R(\alpha)=\frac{\|\nabla\alpha\|^{2}}{\|\alpha\|^{2}}=\frac{-\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha,\alpha)}{\|\alpha\|^{2}}\leq\kappa.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a closed
2n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
b_{1}(X)>0
. Suppose there exists a sequence of metrics
\{g_{i}\}
on
X
such that

\operatorname{Ric}(g_{i})\geq-\frac{1}{i},\hskip 10.00002pt\|\operatorname{Riem}(g_{i})\|_{2p}\leq K,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\operatorname{diam}(X)\leq 1.

Then the Euler characteristic of
X
satisfies
\chi(X)=0
.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\chi(X)\neq 0
. Then
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)=\{0\}
. For each metric
g_{i}
, Proposition
4.1
implies that the corresponding first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
1
-forms satisfies

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(g_{i})\leq\frac{1}{i}.

On the other hand, for sufficiently large
i
,

e^{-(2n-1)\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{(2n-1)i}}}\geq\frac{1}{2}

.
Therefore, Theorem
1.3
gives

\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(g_{i})}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{C}(n,p)}{1+\sqrt{\|\operatorname{Riem}\|_{2p}}}\right)^{\frac{2pn}{p-n}},\frac{1}{2}\tilde{C}(n,p)\right\}.

This leads to a contradiction. Hence,
\chi(X)=0
.
∎

4.2
Killing Vector Fields and Finiteness of the Isometry Group

When the isometry group of a closed Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
is infinite, one can also relate the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
1
-forms to an upper bound on the Ricci curvature.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold. Suppose the Ricci curvature satisfies

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\leq\kappa,

for some constant
\kappa>0
. If the isometry group of
g
is infinite, then either
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)\neq\{0\}
or

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\leq\kappa.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Iso}(X,g)
is infinite, there exists a nonzero Killing vector field
V
on
X
(i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{V}g=0
).
By
[
33
, Proposition 1.4]
, we have the identity

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|V|^{2}=|\nabla V|^{2}-\operatorname{Ric}(V,V).

Integrating over
X
yields

\int_{X}|\nabla V|^{2}-\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}(V,V)=0.

Let
\alpha=V^{\flat}
be the
1
-form dual to
V
. Note that
|\nabla\alpha|^{2}=|\nabla V|^{2}
and
|\alpha|^{2}=|V|^{2}
. If
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}(X)=\{0\}
, then by Lemma
2.1
we obtain

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\leq R(\alpha)=\frac{\|\nabla\alpha\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}=\frac{\|\nabla V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

Using the integrated identity above, it follows that

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\leq\frac{\int_{X}\operatorname{Ric}(V,V)}{\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\leq\kappa.

∎

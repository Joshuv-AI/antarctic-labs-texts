Energy dependence of transverse momentum fluctuations in Au+Au collisions from a multiphase transport model
Liuyao Zhang ID, Jinhui Chen ID, Chunjian Zhang ID

Abstract
Event-by-event mean transverse momentum fluctuations ( ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle ) serve as a sensitive probe of initial state overlap geometry and energy density fluctuations in relativistic heavy-ion collisions. We present a systematic investigation of ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle fluctuations in Au+Au collisions at s NN \mathrm{\sqrt{s_{NN}}} = 3.019.6 GeV, examining their centrality and energy dependence with the framework of an improved multiphase transport (AMPT) model. The centrality dependence of the p T p_{\mathrm{T}} cumulants up to fourth order deviates significantly from simple powering-law scaling. Scaled cumulants are performed, with variances aligning well with the trends observed in the experimental data. Employing a two-subevent method, short-range correlations are slightly suppressed compared to the standard approach. Furthermore, baryons exhibit more pronounced ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle fluctuations than mesons, potentially attributable to the effect of radial flow. These results provide referenced insights into the role of initial state fluctuations across different energies in heavy-ion collisions.

II
Methodology

Following the
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
cumulants approach in Refs.
[
19
,
46
]
, the
n
-particle

c_{n}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i_{1}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}}w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{i_{n}}\left(p_{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{i}_{1}}-\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle\right)\cdots\left(p_{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{n}}}-\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle\right)}{\sum\limits_{i_{1}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}}w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{i_{n}}}.

(1)

Here,
w_{i}
represents the weight of particle
i
, and the relation expands algebraically into a simple polynomial form,

\displaystyle p_{mk}=\sum\limits_{i}w_{i}^{k}p_{i}^{m}/\sum\limits_{i}w_{i}^{k},\quad\tau_{k}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i}w_{i}^{k+1}}{\left(\sum\limits_{i}w_{i}\right)^{k+1}},

(2)

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{1k}\equiv p_{1k}-\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{2k}\equiv p_{2k}-2p_{1k}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle+\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{2},

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{3k}\equiv p_{3k}-3p_{2k}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle+3p_{1k}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{2}-\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{3},

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{4k}\equiv p_{4k}-4p_{3k}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle+6p_{2k}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{2}-4p_{1k}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{3}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle^{4}.

Where
\langle\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle\rangle=\bar{p}_{\mathrm{11}}
is the
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
averaged over the event ensemble, with
p\equiv p_{\mathrm{T}}
.

Using auxiliary variables, the correlator in Eq.
1
can be expressed as,

\displaystyle c_{2}=\frac{\bar{p}_{11}^{2}-\tau_{1}\bar{p}_{22}}{1-\tau_{1}},

(3)

\displaystyle c_{3}=\frac{\bar{p}_{11}^{3}-3\tau_{1}\bar{p}_{22}\bar{p}_{11}+2\tau_{2}\bar{p}_{33}}{1-3\tau_{1}+2\tau_{2}},

\displaystyle c_{4}=\frac{\bar{p}_{11}^{4}-6\tau_{1}\bar{p}_{22}\bar{p}_{11}^{2}+3\tau_{1}^{2}\bar{p}_{22}^{2}+8\tau_{2}\bar{p}_{33}\bar{p}_{11}-6\tau_{3}\bar{p}_{44}}{1-6\tau_{1}+3\tau_{1}^{2}+8\tau_{2}-6\tau_{3}}.

where particles are selected from pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<
1.0 and 0.5
\leq p_{\mathrm{T}}\leq
3.0 GeV/c, considering all unique combinations within each event. The advantage of employing multiparticle
p_{\mathrm{T}}
correlator
[
47
,
48
]
is that they yield zero values for events with randomly sampled particles, thereby effectively isolating the non-statistical fluctuations of interest.

In addition, two-subevent method (2sub) is employed, where particle combinations are selected from two
\eta
-separated subevents, a (-1.0
<\eta<\eta_{1}
) and c (
\eta_{2}<\eta<
1.0).
This
\eta
gap could reduce short-range correlations. To evaluate the impact of these correlations on high-order cumulants, the gap is varied to 0.4. The resulting correlations are given by

\displaystyle c_{2,\text{sub}}=\left(\bar{p}_{11}\right)_{a}\left(\bar{p}_{11}\right)_{c}

(4)

where the subscripts in Eq.
4
indicate the subevents from which particles are selected. Since there are two alternative ways to calculate two-subevent
c_{3}
, the final value is taken as the average,

c_{3,2\mathrm{sub}}=(c_{3,2\mathrm{sub1}}+c_{3,2\mathrm{sub2}})/2
.

In Eq.
1
, the
n
-particle

correlator varies with incident energy and collision centrality. To account for these variations, the measured correlations for variance,
skewness, and kurtosis are scaled by
\langle\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle\rangle
, respectively, yielding dimensionless scaled cumulants.

\displaystyle k_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle c_{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle},

(5)

\displaystyle k_{3}=\frac{\sqrt[3]{\left\langle c_{3}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle},

\displaystyle k_{4}=\frac{\sqrt[4]{\left\langle c_{4}\right\rangle-3\left\langle c_{2}\right\rangle^{2}}}{\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle}.

For the subevent method, the corresponding scaled cumulants are given by

\displaystyle k_{2,2\text{sub}}=\sqrt{\frac{\left\langle c_{2,2\text{sub}}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{a}\left\langle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{c}}}.

V
Discussions and Conclusion

The default and string-melting versions of the AMPT model have been improved by implementing a dynamic tuning mechanism for the Lund string fragmentation parameter
b_{\mathrm{L}}
, which now varies linearly with the impact parameter and exhibits increased sensitivity at higher beam energies. This improvement enables the AMPT model to accurately reproduce the trends in centrality and beam energy dependence of the mean transverse momentum
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
fluctuations. With this refined AMPT framework, we present a comprehensive systematic study of higher-order dynamical
p_{\mathrm{T}}
cumulants up to fourth order, using both standard and two-subevent methods as functions of
\langle\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{part}}\rangle
and collision energies.

Our analysis shows that
p_{\mathrm{T}}
cumulants up to fourth order, both normalized and unnormalized by
\langle\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle\rangle
, exhibit a strong dependence on centrality across an energy range of
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 19.6 GeV to 3.0 GeV. Notably, the scaled variances as a function of centrality within
|\eta|<
0.5 align closely with STAR measurements. In most central collisions (0–5%), the scaled variance is more sensitive to transverse momentum
p_{\mathrm{T}}
than to the acceptance
\eta
range. Additionally, for the same
p_{\mathrm{T}}
range, the scaled variance demonstrates more significant suppression in the
|\eta|<
1.0 range compared to
|\eta|<
0.5. For collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 3 GeV, an enhancement in scaled variances is observed for 0.5
<p_{\mathrm{T}}<
3.0 GeV/c, particularly within
|\eta|<
0.5. This finding provides valuable references for the measurements from STAR fixed-target
[
77
]
, Beam Energy Scan program, and possible FAIR-CBM experiments.

We investigate the contributions to
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
fluctuations from baryons and mesons, separately. The results show that baryons exhibit considerable
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
fluctuations, likely due to their heavier masses, which make them more susceptible to the effect of radial flow compared to mesons. Additionally, we also evaluated two distinct hadronization mechanisms: Lund string fragmentation in the default version and quark coalescence in the string-melting version. The findings indicate that the default AMPT version demonstrates more pronounced
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\rangle
fluctuations. It would be interesting to measure in experiment the high-order transverse momentum fluctuations across collision energies.

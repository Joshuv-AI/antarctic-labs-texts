Six-Functor Formalisms III : The construction and extension of 6FFs
Chirantan Chowdhury

Abstract
This article is the last of the series of articles where we reprove the foundational ideas of abstract six-functor formalisms developed by Liu-Zheng. We prove the theorem of partial adjoints, which is a simplicial technique of encoding various functors altogether by taking adjoints along specific directions. Combined with the ∞ \infty -categorical compactification theorem from the previous article, we can construct abstract six-functor formalisms in reasonable geometric setups of our interest. We also reprove the simplified versions of the DESCENT program due to Liu-Zheng, which allows us to extend such formalisms from smaller to larger geometric setups.

3
Partial adjoints.

In this section, we prove the theorem of partial adjoints. It is the last key tool; that we need in order to construct six-functor formalisms. The idea of abstract six-functor formalism is to define a functor which must encode the six-functors in a compatible way. In particular we need to encode the pullback functors and exceptional functors alltogether so that we get base change. As base changes include cartesian squares, it is natural to use the language of multisimplicial sets. Let us provide a short intro on the importance of partial adjoints.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})
be a two marked
\infty
-category. Consider the simplicial set
\delta^{*}_{2}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}
. The
n
-simplices of
\delta^{*}_{2}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}
are
n\times n
grids of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{00}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{01}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{0n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{10}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{11}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{nn}}

(15)

where vertical arrows are in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
, horizontal arrows are in
\mathcal{E}_{2}
and each square is a pullback square. Also one has a natural morphism
p:\delta^{*}_{2}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}\to\mathcal{C}
induced by composition along the diagonal.

Suppose we have a functor
F:\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
. Precomposing with

gives the functor :

G:\delta^{*}_{2,\{2\}}\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}

(16)

Suppose we have the following property that for every
f:Y\to X
in
\mathcal{E}_{2}
,
f^{*}:=F(f)
admits a right adjoint
f_{*}:F(Y)\to F(X)
which satisfies base change property with respect to morphisms in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
. Then the question arises the following :

Question 3.0.1
.

Is it possible to define a functor
G^{\prime}:\delta^{*}_{2,\{1\}}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
such that

1.

G^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\delta^{*}_{2,\{1\}}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
is
F|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}}
.

2.

G^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}\to\delta^{*}_{2,\{1\}}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
sends
f\mapsto f_{*}
.

The above questions asks about the existence of
G^{\prime}
which encodes both the functors
f^{*}
and
f_{*}
in a coherent way which is a key feature in formulation of six-functor formalism. The theorem of partial adjoints answeres this question.

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Consider quadruples

where

are finite sets,

-simplicial set and

For every

has a right adjoint.

2.

For every

and

, the commutative square

is right adjointable.

Then, there exists a functor

satisfying the following conditions:

1.

and

are the same functors on the sub-simplicial set
\delta^{*}_{J^{c}}(\Delta_{i_{c}})_{*}R\subset\delta^{*}_{I}R,\delta^{*}_{I,J}R
. Here

.

2.

For every
j\in J
and for every edge

,

is a right adjoint to

.

3.

For every

any every square
\tau\in(\epsilon^{I}_{i,j}R)_{1,1}
,
F_{J}(\tau)
is right adjointable square to
F(\tau)
.

Remark 3.2.2
.

Let
R
be an
I
-simplicial set. We briefly recall the meaning of the conditions in the theorem:

1.

Let
i\in I
. For a
1
-simplex in
\epsilon^{I}_{i}R
, this corresponds to

\Delta^{0,\cdots,1,\cdots,0}\to R

where

is in the

-th position. Applying
\delta^{*}_{I}
functor yields a morphism :

\Delta^{1}\to\delta^{*}_{I}R

(19)

2.

Let

, A
(1,1)
-simplex of
\epsilon^{I}_{i,i^{\prime}}R
is a morphism of the form

\Delta^{0,\cdots,1,\cdots,1,\cdots 0}\to R

where

is in the

and
i^{\prime}
th position. Applying
\delta^{*}_{I}
yields a morphism of the form

\Delta^{1}\times\Delta^{1}\to\delta^{*}_{i}R

(20)

which is a commutative square in this simplicial set.

Proof.

The idea is to use the technical theorem of constructing functors using the category of simplicies. We verify the conditions and setup of
Theorem

2.1.9
as follows:

1.

Defining
\mathcal{N}(-)
:

Let
\tau:\Delta^{n}\to\delta^{*}_{I,J}R
. This corresponds to a morphiasm of the form :

\Delta^{\underline{n}}\to\operatorname{op}^{I}_{J}R.

(21)

Let
\mathcal{N}(\tau):=(N(\Delta_{/\delta^{*}_{I,J}R}))_{\tau/}
. As this has an initial object this is weakly contractible. As the category of simplicies is functorial, we have a functor :

\mathcal{N}:(\Delta_{/\delta^{*}_{I,J}R})^{op}\to\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}

(22)

2.

Defining
\alpha:
Unravelling the definition of
\operatorname{op}^{I}_{J}
and applying the functor
\delta^{*}_{I}
, we get that
\tau
yields a morphism of the form

(\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n})^{\operatorname{op}}\times(\prod_{iin\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\to\delta^{*}_{I}R

(23)

Applying
F
, we get a morphism

F_{\tau}:(\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n})^{\operatorname{op}}\times(\prod_{i\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}

(24)

which using the Hom and product adjunction, can be realized as a morphism :

F_{\tau}:(\prod_{i\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\to\operatorname{Fun}((\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n})^{\operatorname{op}},\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}).

(25)

The conditions of the theorem are equivalent to the following :

(a)

For a fixed
j\in J
, we fix an edge
e:\Delta^{1}\to\Delta^{n}
and for every
i\in I/\{j\}
, we fix vertices
x_{i}\in\Delta^{n}
, then the morphism

(\prod_{i\in I/\{j\}}\Delta^{0})\times\Delta^{1}\cong\Delta^{1}\xrightarrow{(e.\{x_{i}\})}(\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n})^{\operatorname{op}}\times(\prod_{i\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\xrightarrow{F_{\tau}}\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}

(26)

admits a right adjoint

(b)

For fixed
j\in J,i\in J^{c}
, we fixed two edges
e_{1},e_{2}:\Delta^{1}\to\Delta^{n}
, for
k\in I/\{i,j\}
, we fix vertices
x_{k}
, then the commutative square given by the following morphism :

(\prod_{k\in I/\{i,j\}}\Delta^{0})\times\Delta^{1}\times\Delta^{1}\xrightarrow{(e_{1},e_{2},\{x_{k}\})}(\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n})^{\operatorname{op}}\times(\prod_{i\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\xrightarrow{F_{\tau}}\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}.

(27)

is right adjointable.

The above conditions imply
F_{\tau}
can be realized as a map :

F_{\tau}:(\prod_{i\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\to\operatorname{Fun}^{\operatorname{RAd}}((\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n})^{\operatorname{op}},\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}).

By

F_{\tau}^{R}:(\prod_{i\in J^{c}}\Delta^{n})\to\operatorname{Fun}^{\operatorname{LAd}}((\prod_{j\in J}\Delta^{n}),\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}).

Using the Hom-product adjunction and applying the diagonal functor

4
The
\infty
-category of correspondences.

In this section, we recall the definitions and prove relevant statements concerning the
(\infty,1)
-category of correspondences. The category of correspondences serves as a source for our abstract six-functor formalisms in geometric setups. We also show that the
\infty
-category of correspondences admit a symmetric monoidal structure. Lastly, we also provide an alternate way of defining correspondences using the language of bisimplicial sets and describe the relation between this category and the simplicial sets
\delta^{*}_{2}\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{cart}}_{\mathcal{E},\operatorname{all}}
that we consider in the context of
|\infty
-categorical compactifications.

4.1
Definitions and properties.

Definition 4.1.1
.

[
15
, Definition A.5.1]

A
geometric setup
is a pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
where
\mathcal{C}
is an
\infty
-category and
E
is a homotopy class of edges in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

1.

E
contains all isomorphism and is stable under compositions,

2.

Pullbacks of
E
exist and remain in
E
.

Definition 4.1.2
.

[
15
, Definition A.5.2]

1.

Let
C(\Delta^{n})\subset\Delta^{n}\times(\Delta^{n})^{op}
be the full-subcategory spanned by
([i],[j])
with
i\leq j
.

2.

An edge in
C(\Delta^{n})
is
vertical
(resp. horizontal) if the projection to the second (first) factor is degenerate.

3.

A square in
C(\Delta^{n})
is
exact
if it is both a pullback and a pushout square.

4.

For any simplicial set
K
, we define

C(K):=\operatorname{colim}_{(n,\sigma)\in\Delta_{/K}}C(\Delta^{n}).

Thus
C
can be visualized as an endofunctor on the category of simplicial sets. It admits a right adjoint

B:\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}\to\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}

defined by

K\mapsto B(K)=\{B(K)_{n}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Set}_{\Delta}}(C(\Delta^{n}),K)\}.

5.

Given a geometric setup
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
, define

\operatorname{Corr}(\mathcal{C})_{E,\operatorname{all}}\subset B(\mathcal{C})

to be the sub-simplicial set whose
n
simplices are maps
C(\Delta^{n})\to\mathcal{C}
which sends

(a)

vertical edges to
E
,

(b)

exact squares to pullback squares.

Remark 4.1.3
.

For every
n\geq 1
, we have maps

\gamma_{n}:(\Delta^{n})^{op}\to C(\Delta^{n})\quad,\quad\gamma_{n}^{\prime}:\Delta^{n}\to C(\Delta^{n})

\gamma_{n}:(\Lambda^{n}_{0})^{op}\to C(\partial\Delta^{n})\quad;\quad\gamma_{n}^{\prime}:\Lambda^{n}_{0}\to C(\Delta^{n})

Proposition 4.1.5
.

Let

Proof.

By

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta^{2},\operatorname{Corr}(\mathcal{C})_{\mathcal{E},\operatorname{all}})
and
\mathcal{K}:=\operatorname{Fun}(\Lambda^{2}_{1},\operatorname{Corr}(\mathcal{C})_{\mathcal{E},\operatorname{all}})
. We have obvious inclusions
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{Fun}(C(\Delta^{2}),\mathcal{C})
and
\mathcal{K}\subset\operatorname{Fun}(C(\Lambda^{2}_{1}),\mathcal{C})
. By definition of the functor
C
, we see that

C(\Delta^{2})=C(\Lambda^{2}_{1})^{\triangleleft}.

Now we notice the following observations :

•

The simplicial set
\mathcal{K}
is the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Fun}(C(\Lambda^{2}_{1}),\mathcal{C})
spanned by functors

which admit a limit i.e.

can be extended to

. This is because as the vertical edges are spanned in

is a geometric setup.

•

The simplicial set
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}

\operatorname{Fun}(C(\Delta^{2}),\mathcal{C})
spanned by functors

which are right Kan extended from

(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
is a geometric setup and exact squares get mapped to pullback squares in the definition of
\operatorname{Corr}(\mathcal{C})_{\mathcal{E},\operatorname{all}}
.

Thus one can apply the dual version of
[

and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\Delta^{0}

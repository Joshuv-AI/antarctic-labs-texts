Abstract
. We develop the deformation theory of primitive Enriques varieties, which are defined as quasi-étale quotients of primitive symplectic varieties by nonsymplectic group actions. In particular, we establish a local Torelli theorem for primitive Enriques varieties. As applications thereof, we describe the behavior of certain primitive Enriques varieties under locally trivial deformations.



1.
Introduction

The singular version of the celebrated Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem
[
Bea83b
, Théorème 2]
was established initially in the projective case thanks to the works of Greb–Kebekus–Peternell
[
GKP16
]
, Druel
[
Dru18
]
, Druel–Guenancia
[
DG18
]
, Greb–Guenancia–Kebekus
[
GGK19
]
, Höring–Peternell
[
HP19
]
and Campana
[
Cam21
]
, and was later extended to the Kähler setting by Bakker–Guenancia–Lehn
[
BGL22
, Theorem A]
. It asserts that any compact Kähler variety
V
with klt singularities and numerically trivial canonical class
K_{V}
admits a finite quasi-étale cover
\widetilde{V}\to V
such that
\widetilde{V}
splits as a product

\widetilde{V}\simeq T\times\prod_{i}W_{i}\times\prod_{j}Z_{j}\,,

where
T
is a complex torus, each
W_{i}
is an irreducible Calabi–Yau variety, and each
Z_{j}
is an irreducible symplectic variety.

Primitive symplectic varieties (see
2.4
) are more general than irreducible symplectic varieties (see
2.8
) and constitute singular analogs of irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) manifolds by
[
Sch22
, Theorem 1]
. This notion is also better suited for the study of symplectic varieties from the birational
[
LP16
,
LMP25
]
and the deformation-theoretic perspective
[
BL21
,
BL22
]
: the class of primitive symplectic varieties is stable under the operations of the Minimal Model Program (MMP), while their deformation and moduli theory is well-understood and resembles the one in the smooth case. In particular, small locally trivial deformations of (
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial) primitive symplectic varieties are again (
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial) primitive symplectic varieties, and a local Torelli theorem for primitive symplectic varieties was also established; see

[
BL22
, Corollary 4.11, Proposition 5.5 and Lemma 5.20]
.

From the viewpoint of the classification theory of complex varieties, it is natural to examine not only the irreducible factors appearing in the Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem but also their quotients. For instance, in the smooth setting, an
Enriques manifold

[
OS11a
]
is a projective manifold
Y
which is not simply connected and whose universal covering
X
is a projective IHS manifold. Consequently,
Y
can be viewed as the quotient of
X
by the finite cyclic group
\pi_{1}(Y)\hookrightarrow\Aut(X)
generated by a purely nonsymplectic automorphism and acting freely on
X
. Recall also that
Y
has torsion canonical bundle whose order coincides with the order of
\pi_{1}(Y)
, which is called the
index
of
Y
.

Enriques manifolds were defined independently and simultaneously by Oguiso–Schröer
[
OS11a
]
and by Boissière–Nieper-Wißkirchen–Sarti
[
BNWS11
]
; for a comparison of their definitions see the paragraph after
[
BCS24
, Definition 4.2]
. They are regarded as higher-dimensional analogs of Enriques surfaces in the same way that IHS manifolds are viewed as higher-dimensional generalizations of K3 surfaces. All currently known examples of Enriques manifolds have index
2
,
3
or
4
and were constructed in op. cit. using punctual Hilbert schemes, generalized Kummer varieties and moduli spaces of sheaves on certain K3 surfaces.
Their deformation theory was developed in
[
OS11b
]
, where a local Torelli theorem for Enriques manifolds
[
OS11b
, Theorem 2.4]
was obtained.

In our earlier joint work
[
DROTX24
]
with Ríos Ortiz,
we introduced the notion of primitive Enriques varieties as a singular version of Enriques manifolds: they are finite quasi-étale quotients of primitive symplectic varieties by nonsymplectic group actions (see
2.9
). In particular, we studied their birational geometry from the perspective of the MMP and we established a termination statement for
Enriques pairs
(that is, log canonical pairs whose underlying variety is an Enriques manifold); see
[
DROTX24
, Theorem 1.2]
. Our result demonstrates in particular that primitive Enriques varieties naturally appear as underlying varieties of minimal models of Enriques pairs.

In the present paper, which is an extension of
[
DROTX24
]
, we develop the deformation theory of primitive Enriques varieties, building on the works
[
OS11b
,
Gra18
,
BL22
]
. Even though our definition
[
DROTX24
, Definition 5.10]
was motivated by the aforementioned MMP considerations, the deformation-theoretic results obtained here provide further evidence that the definition of a primitive Enriques variety as a singular counterpart of an Enriques manifold is indeed an appropriate one.
Before stating our main results, we recall below some relevant notation for the reader’s convenience, referring to Section
2
for more details.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety. According to
[
BL22
, Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.11]
, the space
\Deflt(X)
of locally trivial deformations of
X
is smooth of dimension
h^{1}(X,\mathscr{T}_{X})=h^{1,1}(X)
and the locally trivial Kuranishi family
\mathscr{X}\to\Deflt(X)
of
X
is universal for locally trivial deformations for all of its fibers.
Now, let
Y\simeq X/G
be a primitive Enriques variety, where
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is generated by a purely nonsymplectic automorphism
g
of finite order
d\geq 2
and acts freely in codimension one on
X
. We denote by
\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tf}}\,,q_{X}\big)
the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki lattice of
X
and by
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}
the subspace of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
on which
g
acts via multiplication by a primitive
d
-th root of unity. In particular, we have

H^{2,0}(X)\hookrightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}
,
and the Hodge decomposition of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
yields

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}\simeq H^{2,0}(X)_{1}\oplus H^{1,1}(X)_{1}\oplus H^{0,2}(X)_{1},

where
H^{1,1}(X)\simeq H^{1}\big(X,\Omega^{[1]}_{X}\big)
.

Our first main result is the following generalization of
[
OS11b
, Proposition 1.2]
to the singular setting.

Theorem 1.1

(The locally trivial Kuranishi family of a PEV)
.

Let
Y\simeq X/G
be a primitive Enriques variety, where
X
is a primitive symplectic variety and
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is generated by a purely nonsymplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of finite order
d\geq 2
and acts freely in codimension one on
X
. The following statements hold:

(i)

Let
B
be the connected component of the fixed locus of the induced action of
G
on
\Deflt(X)
that contains the point
0\in\Deflt(X)
. The space

\Deflt(Y)\simeq B
of locally trivial deformations of
Y
is smooth of dimension

h^{1}(Y,\mathscr{T}_{Y})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{1}(X,\mathscr{T}_{X})^{G}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{1}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\big)_{1}=h^{1,1}(X)_{1}\,,

and the locally trivial Kuranishi family

\mathscr{Y}\coloneqq\big(\mathscr{X}\times_{\Deflt(X)}\Deflt(Y)\big)/G\to\Deflt(Y)

of
Y
is universal for locally trivial deformations for all of its fibers.

(ii)

If
S
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Deflt(X)
, then for each
b\in S\cap B
the fiber
\mathscr{Y}_{b}
is a primitive Enriques variety isomorphic to the quotient
\mathscr{X}_{b}/G
of the primitive symplectic variety
\mathscr{X}_{b}
. In particular, both
\mathscr{X}_{b}
and
\mathscr{Y}_{b}
are projective for every
b\in S\cap B
.

(iii)

Assume, finally, that the central fiber
Y\simeq\mathscr{Y}_{0}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. If
T
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Deflt(Y)
, then each fiber
\mathscr{Y}_{t}\simeq\mathscr{X}_{t}/G
,
t\in T
, is also
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

An immediate consequence of the above theorem and the definition of universality of a deformation is the following analog of
[
BL22
, Corollary 4.11 and Lemma 5.20]
for primitive Enriques varieties: small locally trivial deformations of (
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial) primitive Enriques varieties are again (
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial) primitive Enriques varieties.

Our second main result, which extends
[
OS11b
, Theorem 2.4]
to the singular setting and constitutes an analog of
[
BL22
, Proposition 5.5]
for primitive Enriques varieties, is the following one.

Theorem 1.2

(Local Torelli theorem for PEVs)
.

Let
Y\simeq X/G
be a primitive Enriques variety and let

\Omega(Y)\coloneqq\big\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}\big)\mid q_{X}(\omega)=0,\ q_{X}(\omega,\overline{\omega})>0\big\}

be the period domain for
Y
inside
\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}\big)
. If
\mathscr{Y}\to\Deflt(Y)
is the universal locally trivial deformation of
Y

and if
\mathscr{X}_{t}
denotes the fiber over
t\in\Deflt(X)
of the universal locally trivial deformation
\mathscr{X}\to\Deflt(X)
of the primitive symplectic variety
X
,
then the local period map

\mathscr{P}_{Y}\colon\Deflt(Y)\to\Omega(Y),\ s\mapsto\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2,0}(\mathscr{X}_{s})\big)

is a local isomorphism at
0\in\Deflt(Y)
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are given in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.
Proceeding along the lines of the proof of
1.2
, we also give a local Torelli theorem for bielliptic surfaces (see
4.2
), which is crucial for the proof of
5.2
.
This statement is implicit in the proof of
[
OS11b
, Proposition 3.5]
and should be known to the experts. However, since we were unable to find explicit references in the literature, we give a detailed presentation in Subsection
4.2
.

In Section
5
we also provide some concrete applications of
1.2
, which are motivated by the results in
[
OS11b
, Section 3]
; namely, in Propositions
5.1
,
5.2
and
5.3
, respectively, we demonstrate that small locally trivial deformations of a primitive Enriques variety of the form

\displaystyle\Hilb^{n}(S)/G

\displaystyle,\ \text{ where $S$ is a K3 surface covering a primitive Enriques surface},

\displaystyle\Kum^{n}(A)/G

\displaystyle,\ \text{ where $A$ is the canonical abelian covering of a bielliptic surface},\text{ or }

\displaystyle M_{S}(v,H)/G

\displaystyle,\ \text{ where $S$ is a K3 surface covering a very general Enriques surface},

\displaystyle\hskip 12.5ptv\text{ is a positive Mukai vector and $H$ is a $v$-generic polarization on $S$},

are again of the same form, under certain hypotheses for both the surfaces involved and the finite group
G
of automorphisms acting on them.
We emphasize that
M_{S}(v,H)
is, in general, a singular, projective, irreducible symplectic variety, since we do not necessarily assume
v
to be primitive; see Subsection
2.3
.

We conclude the introduction by drawing attention to further recent progress on the study of primitive Enriques varieties.
First of all, a global Torelli theorem for Enriques manifolds was established in
[
Ram25
, Theorem B]
. Regarding now the construction of more examples of Enriques varieties,
[
BGGG25
, Corollary 3]
shows that Enriques manifolds cannot arise as quotients of IHS manifolds of OG10 type, whereas
[
BCS24
, Section 6]
discusses some singular examples. Finally, as a class of varieties with numerically trivial canonical divisor, the Kawamata–Morrison cone conjecture has also been investigated for primitive Enriques varieties. It was originally resolved for Enriques manifolds of prime index by
[
PS23
, Theorem 1.2]
, while more recently, thanks to a general descent result for finite covers
[
Gac25
, Theorem 1.4]
, the cone conjecture is now settled for primitive Enriques varieties admitting a finite cover by a primitive symplectic variety with
b_{2}\geq 5
.

2.
Preliminaries

Notation and Conventions
.

(1)

The term
variety
means an integral separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
in the algebraic setting or a reduced, irreducible, Hausdorff complex space in the analytic setting.

(2)

A
resolution of singularities
of a variety
V
is a proper, surjective, bimeromorphic morphism
W\to V
from a nonsingular variety
W
.

(3)

Let
V
be a normal variety. We denote by
\Omega^{1}_{V}
the sheaf of Kähler differentials on
V
and by
V_{\text{reg}}
the regular locus of
V
. We consider the natural inclusion
\iota\colon V_{\text{reg}}\hookrightarrow V
, and we set

\mathscr{T}_{V}\coloneqq\big(\Omega_{V}^{1}\big)^{*}\simeq\iota_{*}\mathscr{T}_{V_{\text{reg}}}

and

\Omega_{V}^{[p]}\coloneqq\big(\Lambda^{p}\Omega_{V}^{1}\big)^{**}=\big(\Omega_{V}^{p}\big)^{**}\simeq\iota_{*}\Omega_{V_{\text{reg}}}^{p}\,\text{ for any }\,p\in\{0,\dots,\dim V\},

where
\Omega_{V}^{[0]}=\mathcal{O}_{V}
.
We refer to the global sections of
\mathscr{T}_{V}
and
\Omega^{[p]}_{V}
as
reflexive vector fields
and
reflexive
p
-forms
on
V
, respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A
lattice

(L,q)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
L
of finite rank endowed with a quadratic form
q\colon L\to\mathbb{Z}
. When
q
is nondegenerate,
we say that
(L,q)
is a
nondegenerate lattice
.

(2)

If
(L,q)
is a nondegenerate lattice, then the
\mathbb{R}
-linear extension of
q(\cdot,\cdot)
to the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
L_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
can be diagonalized with only
+1
and
-1
on the diagonal, and the
signature
of
(L,q)
is defined to be
(n_{+},n_{-})
, where
n_{\pm}
is the number of
\pm 1
on the diagonal; see
[
Huy16
, Chapter 14]
. We adopt the notation
(k,*)
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and we say that the nondegenerate lattice
(L,q)
has
signature of type
(k,*)
if its signature is
(k,\ell)
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(3)

A
lattice isomorphism
(or an
isometry
) between two lattices
(L,q)
and
(L^{\prime},q^{\prime})
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module isomorphism
\phi\colon L\to L^{\prime}
such that
q^{\prime}\big(\phi(x)\big)=q(x)
for all
x\in L
.

(4)

If a group
G
acts on a lattice
(L,q)
,
then we say that the
G
-action is
orthogonal
if the quadratic form
q
is
G
-invariant, i.e.,
q(g\cdot\ell)=q(\ell)
for all
g\in G
and all
\ell\in L
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group.

(1)

A finite-dimensional complex
representation
of
G
is the data of a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
and a group homomorphism
\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)
.

(2)

Let
\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)
be a finite-dimensional complex representation of
G
.
Assume that
V
is equipped with a nondegenerate quadratic form
q
.
We say that
\rho
is an
orthogonal representation
of
G
if its image is contained in the
orthogonal group

\mathrm{O}(V,q)
of
(V,q)
, where

\mathrm{O}(V,q)\coloneqq\left\{T\in\GL(V)\mid q\big(T(v)\big)=q(v)\,\text{ for all }\,v\in V\right\}.

2.1.
Hodge duality for Kähler spaces

For the definition and basic properties of
Kähler
complex spaces we refer to
[
Var89
,
BL21
,
BL22
]
and the references therein.
The next result generalizes
[
GKP16
, Proposition 6.9]
. It should be well known to the experts, but we include it below nonetheless due to the lack of an explicit reference.

Proposition 2.3

(Hodge duality)
.

If
V
is a normal, reduced, compact, Kähler, complex space of dimension
n
with weakly rational singularities (e.g., klt singularities, see
[
KS21
, §1.4]
and
[
KM98
, Theorem 5.22]
), then for any integer
p\in\{0,\dots,n\}
there are
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphisms

H^{0}\big(V,\Omega_{V}^{[p]}\big)\simeq\overline{H^{p}\big(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}\big)}.

Proof.

Let
\rho\colon W\to V
be a resolution of singularities of
V
, where
W
is a compact Kähler manifold; see
[
BL22
, Proposition 2.7]
. We may repeat verbatim the proof of
[
GKP16
, Proposition 6.9]
, except that we now apply
[
KS21
, Corollary 1.7]
instead of
[
GKP16
, Theorem 2.4]
,
which shows that
\rho_{*}\Omega^{p}_{W}\simeq\Omega^{[p]}_{V}
also in this more general setting.
∎

2.2.
Primitive symplectic varieties

Recall that a normal variety
X
of dimension at least two is called
symplectic
if it is endowed with a
symplectic form

\sigma
(i.e., a reflexive
2
-form
\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)
such that
\sigma|_{X_{\text{reg}}}
is closed and everywhere nondegenerate) such that
\sigma|_{X_{\text{reg}}}
extends to a holomorphic
2
-form on some (or, equivalently, any) resolution of singularities of
X
, see
[
Bea00
]
. In particular, we have
\dim X=2n
for some
n\geq 1
and
K_{X}\sim 0
.
For a thorough discussion of symplectic varieties we refer to
[
BL21
,
BL22
,
DROTX24
]
and the numerous relevant references therein. In this paper we are mainly concerned with the following subclass of symplectic varieties.

Definition 2.4

(PSV)
.

A compact, Kähler, symplectic variety
X
with symplectic form
\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)
is called
primitive
if
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
and
H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma
.

Fix a primitive symplectic variety
X
of dimension
2n
. Recall that
X
has rational singularities by
[
BL22
, Theorem 3.4(1)]
and that the torsion-free part
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}\coloneqq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})/\text{(torsion)}
of its second cohomology group is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite rank by
[
BL21
, Lemma 2.1]
and carries also a pure Hodge structure of weight two by
[
BL22
, Corollary 3.5]
. Therefore, there exists a decomposition

(1)

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\simeq H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X),

where

\displaystyle H^{2,0}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big),

\displaystyle H^{1,1}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{1}\big(X,\Omega^{[1]}_{X}\big),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\overline{H^{2,0}(X)}

\displaystyle\simeq H^{0,2}(X)\simeq H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}),

see
[
BL21
, Corollary 2.3(1)]
.

2.2.1.
The Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form

If
\sigma\in H^{2,0}(X)
is the cohomology class of a symplectic form on
X
satisfying
\int_{X}(\sigma\overline{\sigma})^{n}=1
, then there exists a quadratic form on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, called the
Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki (BBF) form
and defined as

\displaystyle q_{X}

\displaystyle\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C},

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha\mapsto q_{X}(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{n}{2}\int_{X}(\sigma\overline{\sigma})^{n-1}\,\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-n)\left(\int_{X}\sigma^{n}\,\overline{\sigma}^{n-1}\,\alpha\right)\left(\int_{X}\sigma^{n-1}\,\overline{\sigma}^{n}\,\alpha\right),

which does not depend on the choice of
\sigma
and whose restriction to
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a real nondegenerate quadratic form with signature
\big(3,b_{2}(X)-3\big)
; see

[
Sch20
, Section 3]
and

[
BL22
, Section 5]
, which are based on
[
Nam01
,
Mat01
,
Kir15
]
.
In particular, by (
2.2.1
) we infer that

(3)

q_{X}(\sigma)=0=q_{X}(\overline{\sigma}),\quad q_{X}(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})=1

and

(4)

\displaystyle q_{X}(\sigma+v)=q_{X}(\overline{\sigma}+v)

\displaystyle=q_{X}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\int_{X}(\sigma\overline{\sigma})^{n-1}\,v^{2}\quad\text{for any }\ v\in H^{1,1}(X).

Moreover, according to
[
BL22
, Lemma 5.7]
, we can normalize
q_{X}
so that it becomes an integral quadratic form
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
q_{X}
is invariant under locally trivial deformations of
X
(see
2.13
). The
BBF lattice

\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}\,,q_{X}\big)
of
X
is thus nondegenerate with signature of type
(3,*)
.

Comment
.

We distinguish between the induced nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
q_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
defined by

(5)

q_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\big(q_{X}(\alpha+\beta)-q_{X}(\alpha)-q_{X}(\beta)\big),

see
[
Sch20
, Remark 22]
, and the induced Hermitian form
q_{X}^{h}
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
defined by

(6)

q_{X}^{h}(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq q_{X}(\alpha,\overline{\beta}),

see
[
OS11b
, p. 1636]
.
For some useful properties of
q_{X}^{h}
we refer to
2.7
below.

Remark 2.5
.

It follows from (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) that, exactly as in the smooth case, the decomposition

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\simeq\left(H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X)\right)\oplus H^{1,1}(X)

is orthogonal with respect to
q_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

2.2.2.
Nonsymplectic finite group actions on PSVs

We now recall the following standard definitions.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety and denote by
\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)
its symplectic form.

(1)

An automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
is called
nonsymplectic
if
g^{[*]}\sigma\neq\sigma
.

(2)

A nonsymplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
is called
purely nonsymplectic
if there exists a primitive root of unity
\xi\neq 1
of the same order as
g
such that
g^{[*]}\sigma=\xi\sigma
.

We also say that a finite, nontrivial subgroup
G
of
\Aut(X)
acts
nonsymplectically
on
X
if every element
g\in G\setminus\{\Id_{X}\}
is a nonsymplectic automorphism of
X
.

Assume now that the given primitive symplectic variety
(X,\sigma)
admits a purely nonsymplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of order
d\geq 2
. Set
G\coloneqq\langle g\rangle\subseteq\Aut(X)
and let
\xi
be a primitive
d
-th root of unity such that
g^{[*]}\sigma=\xi\sigma
. As
h^{2,0}(X)=1
, observe that the natural map

\rho\colon G\to\GL\left(H^{0}\big(X,\Omega^{[2]}_{X}\big)\right),\ g\mapsto g^{[*]}

induces a bijection

(7)

G\to\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C}),\ g\mapsto\xi

onto the multiplicative group of
d
-th roots of unity, see
[
DROTX24
, Subsection 4.2]
. Note also that the group of characters
\Hom\big(\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C}^{*}\big)
is cyclic of order
d
and contains a canonical generator, namely the identity character
\xi\in\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})\mapsto\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. We use below the identification between
\Hom\big(\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C}^{*}\big)
and
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
.

Consider next the linear representation

G\to\GL\left(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\right),\ g\mapsto g^{*}.

The cohomology vector space
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
is endowed with a

indexed by the characters

, that is,

where the

is the

-vector subspace of

on which the generator of

acts via multiplication by

. In particular,

is the

-invariant subspace of

, and
H^{2,0}(X)\simeq\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma\subseteq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}
.
Recall also that by
[

, Lemmas 20 and 23]
we have

q_{X}(\alpha)=q_{X}\big(g^{*}\alpha\big)\ \text{ for any }\,\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C}),

which shows that the representation (

)
is orthogonal. In view of the bijection (

), the BBF lattice
\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}\,,q_{X}\big)
of

is also endowed with an orthogonal
\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-action.
We can now extend
[
OS11b
, Lemma 2.1]
to the singular setting.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

be a primitive symplectic variety and let

be its BBF form. Assume that

admits a purely nonsymplectic automorphism of order

and consider a weight decomposition of

as in (

q_{X}^{h}\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\times H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C},\ (\alpha,\beta)\mapsto q_{X}(\alpha,\overline{\beta})

is a Hermitian form on

whose restriction to the weight space

is nondegenerate and has signature of type
(2,*)
for

and
(1,*)
for

.

Proof.

To prove the statement, we repeat verbatim the proof of
[
OS11b
, Lemma 2.1]
, taking (

), (
5
), (
6
), (

) and
[

, Proposition 25]
into account.
∎

2.3.
Irreducible symplectic varieties

We recall here the definition of a more special kind of primitive symplectic varieties and include afterwards some relevant remarks for the sake of completeness.

Definition 2.8

(ISV)
.

A compact Kähler variety
X
with rational singularities is called an
irreducible symplectic variety
if for every quasi-étale cover
\widetilde{X}\to X
of
X
, the exterior algebra of global reflexive forms on
\widetilde{X}
is generated by a symplectic form
\widetilde{\sigma}\in H^{0}\big(\widetilde{X},\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{[2]}\big)
.

Note that the above definition in the Kähler setting, which is
[
BGL22
, Definition 1.1]
, coincides with
[
GKP16
, Definition 8.16]
, which is originally formulated in the projective setting; see the paragraph after
[
BGL22
, Definition 1.1]
and
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.24]
.

By definition and by
2.3
, irreducible symplectic varieties are automatically primitive symplectic, but the converse does not hold: counterexamples are mentioned, for instance, in
[
DROTX24
, Remark 4.6(ii)]
. In fact, it follows readily from the Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem in the singular Kähler setting
[
BGL22
, Theorem A]
and the Künneth formula that an irreducible symplectic variety is precisely a primitive symplectic variety
X
such that any quasi-étale cover
\widetilde{X}
of
X
is again a primitive symplectic variety; see
[
EFG+25
, Remark 2.3]
.

2.3.1.
Examples of ISVs: moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces

We first recall here some terminology which is relevant for the construction of moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces. These spaces will appear exclusively in Subsection
5.3
. For further information about everything that follows we refer, for instance, to
[
HL10
, Chapter 6]
and
[
PR23
, Subsections 1.1 and 2.1]
.

Fix henceforth a K3 surface
S
and denote by
\sigma\in H^{2,0}(S)
the (unique up to scalar) symplectic form on
S
. Consider the even integral cohomology of
S
, i.e.,

\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})\coloneqq H^{0}(S,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{4}(S,\mathbb{Z}),

and endow it with a lattice structure by defining a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, the so-called
Mukai pairing
, as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})\times\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(r_{1},\ell_{1},s_{1}),(r_{2},\ell_{2},s_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto-r_{1}s_{2}+\ell_{1}\ell_{2}-s_{1}r_{2}\,,

where
r_{i}\in H^{0}(S,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
,
\ell_{i}\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
and
s_{i}\in H^{4}(S,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
.
The resulting nondegenerate lattice
\big(\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\big)
is called the
Mukai lattice
. An element
v=(r,\ell,s)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is called a
Mukai vector
and satisfies

v^{2}\coloneqq\langle v,v\rangle=\ell^{2}-2rs\in 2\mathbb{Z},

since the intersection form on
S
is even.
It is said to be
primitive
if it is not divisible by any integer
m\geq 2
.

Consider next the complexification

\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{C})\coloneqq\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{Z}\mathbb{C}
of
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
,
endowed with the
\mathbb{C}
-linear extension
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
of the Mukai pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The natural Hodge structure of weight two on
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, which is given by the Hodge decomposition

H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})=H^{2,0}(S)\oplus H^{1,1}(S)\oplus H^{0,2}(S),

can be extended to a Hodge structure of weight two on
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
by setting

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{2,0}(S)

\displaystyle\coloneqq H^{2,0}(S)\simeq\mathbb{C}\sigma,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{0,2}(S)

\displaystyle\coloneqq H^{0,2}(S)\simeq\mathbb{C}\overline{\sigma},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{1,1}(S)

\displaystyle\coloneqq H^{0}(S,\mathbb{C})\oplus H^{1,1}(S)\oplus H^{4}(S,\mathbb{C}).

For any Mukai vector
v=(r,\ell,s)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, we define its
orthogonal sublattice
in
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
with respect to the Mukai pairing as

v^{\perp}\coloneqq\big\{w\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})\mid\langle v,w\rangle=0\big\}

and we denote by
(v^{\perp})_{\mathbb{C}}
its complexification, which is endowed with the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
to it.
We also observe that
v^{\perp}
inherits from
\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
a Hodge structure of weight two in the obvious fashion. In addition, we claim that

(11)

\text{ if }\,\ell\in\NS(S)\simeq\Pic(S)\simeq H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})\cap H^{1,1}(S),\text{ then }\,\sigma\in(v^{\perp})_{\mathbb{C}}\,.

Indeed, due to
2.5
and since the integral BBF form of
S
coincides with the intersection product on
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, we infer that
\sigma\in H^{2,0}(S)
is orthogonal – with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
and under its identification with the vector
(0,\sigma,0)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{C})
– to
\ell\in\Pic(S)
, and hence to the given Mukai vector
v=(r,\ell,s)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
.

Given a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on a K3 surface
S
, we can naturally attach to it a Mukai vector by setting

v(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{F})\sqrt{\operatorname{td}(S)}=\big(\rk(\mathcal{F}),\,c_{1}(\mathcal{F}),\,\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{F})^{2}-c_{2}(\mathcal{F})+\rk(\mathcal{F})\big)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z}),

where
\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{F})^{2}-c_{2}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\mathcal{F})
. By the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula, we have

\chi(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\mathcal{F})+2\rk(\mathcal{F})

and hence the third component of
v(\mathcal{F})
is often written as
\chi(\mathcal{F})-\rk(\mathcal{F})
.
By construction, the associated Mukai vector
v(\mathcal{F})=(r,\ell,s)
is of type
(1,1)
and satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)

r>0
;

(2)

r=0
and
\ell\in H^{1,1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is the first Chern class of a strictly effective divisor; or

(3)

r=\ell=0
and
s>0
.

In view of the previous observations, a nonzero element
v=(r,\ell,s)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
with
\ell\in\NS(S)
satisfying
v^{2}\geq 2
and any of the above three conditions is called a
positive
Mukai vector.

Finally, for the definition and the comparison of the notions of
v
-generic
and
v
-general
polarizations
H\in\Amp(S)
with respect to a positive Mukai vector
v\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, which will appear in the following paragraph as well as in Subsection
5.3
, we refer to
[
PR23
, Subsection 2.1]
. In particular, due to
[
PR23
, Lemmas 2.9, 2.10 and 2.12]
, we will only consider
v
-generic polarizations in this paper; see also
[
PR23
, Remark 2.13]
.

Moduli spaces of semistable sheaves on K3 surfaces
:
Let
S
be a projective K3 surface. According to
[
PR23
, Theorem 1.10]
, if
v\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is a positive Mukai vector and if
H\in\Amp(S)
is a
v
-generic polarization, then the moduli space
M_{S}(v,H)
of Gieseker
H
-semistable sheaves on
S
with Mukai vector
v
is a projective irreducible symplectic variety of dimension
v^{2}+2\geq 4
whose smooth locus
M^{s}_{S}(v,H)
is the moduli space of Gieseker
H
-stable sheaves on
S
with Mukai vector
v
. In particular, if
v
is primitive, then by works of Mukai and Yoshioka we know that
M^{s}_{S}(v,H)=M_{S}(v,H)
is a projective IHS manifold which is deformation equivalent to the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb^{n}(S)
of
n
points on the K3 surface
S
.
Finally, O’Grady
[
O’G97
, Main Theorem]
and Perego–Rapagnetta
[
PR24
, Proposition 5.12]
showed that there also exists a Hodge isometry

(12)

\theta\colon\big(v^{\perp},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle|_{v^{\perp}}\big)\to\Big(H^{2}\big(M_{S}(v,H),\mathbb{Z}\big),\,q_{M_{S}(v,H)}\Big).

In particular, the symplectic form on
M_{S}(v,H)
arises from the symplectic form
\sigma
on
S
; namely,
\theta_{\mathbb{C}}(\sigma)\in H^{2,0}\big(M_{S}(v,H)\big)
.

2.4.
Primitive Enriques varieties

In this subsection we first give the definition and establish some basic properties of the main objects of study in this paper.

Definition 2.9

(PEV)
.

A
primitive Enriques variety

Y
is the quotient of a primitive symplectic variety
X
by a finite group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
whose action on
X
is nonsymplectic and free in codimension one.

A primitive Enriques variety
Y\simeq X/G
is a normal, compact, Kähler variety with klt singularities and torsion canonical class; see
[
Pop77
, Lecture 3]
,
[
Var89
, Section II, Corollary 3.2.1]
, and
[
DROTX24
, Remark 2.6(i)]
. At first glance,
2.9
seems to be the Kähler version of our earlier definition of a projective primitive Enriques variety, see
[
DROTX24
, Definition 5.10]
. However, we will now show that the two definitions coincide; that is, primitive Enriques varieties and the (a priori Kähler) primitive symplectic varieties determining them are automatically projective.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety. Assume that
X
admits a purely nonsymplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of finite order such that the group
G\coloneqq\langle g\rangle
acts freely in codimension one on
X
. Then
X
and
Y\coloneqq X/G
are projective.

Proof.

Recall that
Y\simeq X/G
is a normal, compact, Kähler variety with klt, and hence rational, singularities. By
[
DROTX24
, Proposition 5.13(iii)]
and
2.3
we obtain

h^{2}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})=h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)=0.

Therefore,
[
Gra18
, Proposition 4.10]
implies now that
Y
is projective, so it admits an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
.
Observe that the quotient map
\gamma\colon X\to Y
is finite. Thus, using the Finite Mapping Theorem
[
GR84
, pp. 64-65]
, the projection formula
[
Har77
, Chapter III, Exercise 8.3]
and the relative ampleness criterion
[
Nak04
, p. 25, (1)
\iff
(4)]
for the line bundle
\gamma^{*}\mathcal{L}
and the composite map
X\to Y\to\Spec(\mathbb{C})
, we deduce that
\gamma^{*}\mathcal{L}
is an ample line bundle on
X
, so
X
is also projective, as claimed.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

(1)

The projectivity assumption that is included tacitly in the statement of
[
DROTX24
, Proposition 5.13(iii)]
does not play any role in its proof, so this result can indeed be applied in the Kähler setting of
2.10
.

(2)

If
\dim X\geq 4
, then the hypothesis in
2.10
that
G
acts freely in codimension one on
X
is automatically satisfied by
[
DROTX24
, Proposition 4.13]
.

2.4.1.

We now define a notion of marking for primitive Enriques varieties. To this end, let
(L,q)
be an abstract nondegenerate lattice with signature of type
(3,*)
, endowed with an orthogonal
\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-action.
We further impose the condition that the induced Hermitian form on the weight space
L_{\mathbb{C},1}\coloneqq(L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C})_{1}
is nondegenerate with signature of type
(2,*)
for
d=2
and
(1,*)
for
d\geq 3
.

Consider a primitive Enriques variety
Y\simeq X/G
, where
X
is a primitive symplectic variety and
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is a finite group of order
d\geq 2
, identified with
\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, which acts nonsymplectically and freely in codimension one on
X
. In view of
2.7
and the fact that the BBF lattice
\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}\,,q_{X}\big)
of
X
is endowed with an orthogonal
\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-action by (
10
), an
L
-
marking
of
Y
is a
\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant lattice isomorphism

\phi\colon\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tf}}\,,q_{X}\big)\to(L,q),

and the
L
-marked primitive Enriques variety
is denoted by
(Y,\phi)
.

Definition 2.12
.

In the above setting, we also give the following definitions:

(1)

The
period domain
for
L
-marked primitive Enriques varieties is defined to be

\mathcal{D}_{L}\coloneqq\big\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}(L_{\mathbb{C},1})\mid q(\omega)=0,\ q(\omega,\overline{\omega})>0\big\}.

(2)

The
period point
of an
L
-marked primitive Enriques variety
(Y,\phi)
is defined to be the point
[\phi(\sigma)]\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
.

We comment briefly on the previous definition. In the first item above,
\overline{\omega}
denotes the complex conjugate of
\omega
inside the complexification
L_{\mathbb{C}}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. In the second item above, slightly abusing notation,
\phi
also denotes the complexification of the given lattice isomorphism and
\sigma\in H^{2,0}(X)\subseteq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}
is the cohomology class of the (unique up to scalar) symplectic form on
X
. Since
q_{X}(\sigma)=0
and
q_{X}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})>0
by (
3
) and (
5
), we indeed have that
[\phi(\sigma)]\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
, as asserted.

Finally, note that the period domains
\mathcal{D}_{L}
inside
\mathbb{P}(L_{\mathbb{C},1})
are locally closed with respect to the classical topology, and hence inherit the structure of a complex manifold. In fact, their precise description as
bounded symmetric domains
is given in
[
OS11b
, Proposition 2.2]
, which remains valid also in our setting with an identical proof. In view of this and
5.2
below, see also
[
OS11b
, Remark 3.6]
.

2.5.
Notions from deformation theory

We refer to
[
BL22
, Section 4]
for the basic notions of the deformation theory of compact complex spaces, see also
[
Gra74
,
FK87
,
Ser06
,
Gri18
]
. Here, we only recall the following definitions.

Definition 2.13
.

A
deformation

\mathscr{D}
of a compact complex space
X
is given by a flat and proper morphism
\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to S
of complex spaces together with a distinguished point
0\in S
and an isomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathscr{X}_{0}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(0)\to X
.

We say that
\mathscr{D}=(\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to S,\,\alpha\colon\mathscr{X}_{0}\to X)
is

if for every
p\in\mathscr{X}_{0}\simeq X
there exist open neighborhoods
\mathscr{U}
of
p
in
\mathscr{X}
and
V
of
0
in
S
such that
\mathscr{U}\simeq U\times V
, where
U\coloneqq\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{X}_{0}
. Finally, we say that
\mathscr{D}
is
locally trivial
if it is locally trivial at each point
s\in S
.

.

A deformation
(\mathscr{X}\to S,\,\alpha\colon\mathscr{X}_{0}\to X)
of a compact complex space
X
is called

if for every deformation
(\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},\,\beta\colon\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{0}\to X)
of
X
there exist a map
\varphi\colon(S^{\prime},0)\to(S,0)
of germs of complex spaces and an isomorphism
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{X}\times_{S}S^{\prime}
such that the following diagrams commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}\times_{S}S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S,}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

A versal deformation is called

if the differential

An

by

of

of

is a closed complex subspace of

2.5.1.
Auxiliary results

The content of the following result should be well known to the experts.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to S
be a flat and proper morphism of complex spaces with reduced fibers
\mathscr{X}_{s}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(s)
,
s\in S
, where
\mathscr{X}
(and hence
S
) is also reduced. Assume that
\pi
is
topologically locally trivial
, i.e., the underlying continuous map is a topological fiber bundle,
and that
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}=0
.
Given
t\in S
and after sufficiently shrinking
S
around
t
, the following statements hold:

(i)

If
L_{t}
is a holomorphic line bundle on
\mathscr{X}_{t}
, then there exists a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathscr{X}
such that
c_{1}\big(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathscr{X}_{t}}\big)=c_{1}(L_{t})
.

(ii)

If, additionally,
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}=0
, then the lift in
(i)
is unique (up to isomorphism).

Proof.

(i)
By shrinking
S
, we can assume that it is Stein. First, we show that

(13)

H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})=0.

Indeed, the Leray spectral sequence applied to the morphism
\pi
and the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}
yields
the following exact sequence

H^{2}\big(S,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\to\mathrm{ker}\left(H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\to H^{0}(S,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\right)\to H^{1}(S,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}).

Since the analytic sheaves
R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}
,
i\geq 0
, are coherent by the Direct Image Theorem
[
GR84
, p. 207, Theorem]
and since the space
S
is Stein, we have

H^{1}(S,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})=0=H^{2}(S,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}).

Thus, (
13
) follows immediately from the above and the hypothesis that
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}=0
.

Next, we consider the exponential sequence of the reduced complex space
\mathscr{X}
,
which gives, together with (
13
), the following exact sequence in cohomology:

(14)

H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\to H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}^{*}_{\mathscr{X}})\xrightarrow{c_{1}}H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})=0.

Since
\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to S
is topologically locally trivial, after shrinking
S
even further so that it becomes contractible, we infer that for any
s\in S
there is an isomorphism

H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathbb{Z}).

Therefore, the class
c_{1}(L_{t})\in H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{t},\mathbb{Z})
of the given holomorphic line bundle
L_{t}
on
\mathscr{X}_{t}
lifts to a class
\alpha\in H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathbb{Z})
on the total space
\mathscr{X}
, which in turn satisfies
\alpha=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
for some holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathscr{X}
due to (
14
).

(ii)
Since
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}=0
by assumption, the five-term exact sequence induced by the Leray spectral sequence now gives

H^{1}(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}^{*}_{\mathscr{X}})\cong H^{2}(\mathscr{X},\mathbb{Z})

on

uniquely lifts to

∎

Remark 2.17
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to S
be a flat and proper morphism of complex spaces.
If

H^{i}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{s}})=0\,\text{ for all }\,s\in S,

then

R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}=0

by
[
BS76
, Chapter III, Corollary 3.5]
.

Recall that a proper morphism

between
complex spaces is called
projective
if there exists a relatively ample line bundle on
X
over
Y
; see
[
Nak04
, Definition II.1.10]
. We now derive the following application of
2.16
and
2.17
, which will play a key role in Subsection
5.3
.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{X}\to(S,0)
be a
smooth family
, i.e., a smooth and proper morphism between connected complex spaces whose fibers are compact, connected, complex manifolds. Assume that
\mathscr{X}
(and hence
S
)
is reduced and that
\mathscr{X}_{0}=\pi^{-1}(0)
is Kähler with
H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{0}})=0
. Then there exists a sufficiently small neighborhood
V
of
0
in
S
such that the restricted morphism
\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}\colon\pi^{-1}(V)\to V
is projective.

Proof.

Note first that the given smooth family is topologically locally trivial by
[
GHJ03
, Theorem 14.5]
.
Moreover, since the central fiber
\mathscr{X}_{0}
is by assumption a compact Kähler manifold such that
h^{0,2}(\mathscr{X}_{0})=h^{2,0}(\mathscr{X}_{0})=0
, it is actually projective by Kodaira’s embedding theorem,
so it admits an ample line bundle
H_{0}
. On the other hand, by the upper semi-continuity of the function
t\mapsto H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{s}})
, see
[
BS76
, Chapter III, Theorem 4.12(i)]
, and after shrinking
S
, we infer that
H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{s}})=0
for all
s\in S
.
It follows now from
2.16
and
2.17
that, after possibly further shrinking
S
,
H_{0}
lifts to a line bundle
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathscr{X}
such that
c_{1}\big(\mathcal{H}|_{\mathscr{X}_{0}}\big)=c_{1}(H_{0})
. But ampleness is an open condition in smooth families. Indeed, being Kähler is an open condition in such families; see
[
GHJ03
, Proposition 22.2(i)]
. In particular, small perturbations of integral Kähler classes are represented by ample line bundles due to Kodaira’s embedding theorem.
Therefore, up to shrinking
S
, the line bundle
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathscr{X}
is relatively ample over
S
.
∎

The following observation will only be used for the special case of an Enriques surface in Subsection

,
where a relative moduli space construction will be discussed.

2.5.2.
Deformations and group actions

Given a compact complex space

When

-action on

the existence of a miniversal

-equivariant deformation of a given compact complex space

endowed with an action of a group

satisfying further properties, see
[

, Définition, p. 392]

(ii) The assertion is a consequence of (i); see

(iii)

b\in S\cap B
the group
G
acts nonsymplectically and freely in codimension one on the primitive symplectic variety
\mathscr{X}_{b}=\pi_{X}^{-1}(b)
, and hence the corresponding quotient
\mathscr{X}_{b}/G=\mathscr{Y}_{b}=\pi_{Y}^{-1}(b)
is a primitive Enriques variety, as claimed. Finally, the assertion that both
\mathscr{X}_{b}
and
\mathscr{Y}_{b}
are projective for every
b\in S\cap B
follows immediately from
2.10
.

(iii)
Consider the universal locally trivial Kuranishi family
\big(\pi_{Y}\colon\mathscr{Y}\to\Deflt(Y),\,\mathscr{Y}_{0}\simeq Y\big)
of
Y
and a simultaneous resolution
\mathscr{W}\to\mathscr{Y}
thereof, which exists by
[
BL22
, Lemma 4.9]
. If
S
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Deflt(X)
as in (ii), then for each
t\in S\cap\Deflt(Y)
the fiber
\mathscr{Y}_{t}=\pi_{Y}^{-1}(t)
is a primitive Enriques variety, so it is projective and has rational singularities. Therefore, by assumption and by
[
BL22
, Lemma 2.13(4)]
, the central fiber
\mathscr{Y}_{0}\simeq Y
satisfies
[
BL22
, Equality (2.4), p. 230]
with respect to the induced fiberwise resolution
\mu\colon\mathscr{W}_{0}=W\to\mathscr{Y}_{0}=Y
; namely,

(20)

\Ima\left(H^{2}(W,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{0}\big(Y,R^{2}\mu_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{W}\big)\right)=\Ima\left(\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Q}[E_{i}^{\mu}]\to H^{0}\big(Y,R^{2}\mu_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{W}\big)\right),

where the
E_{i}^{\mu}
are the
\mu
-exceptional prime divisors on
W=\mathscr{W}_{0}
.
This equality shows that the
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality of a compact algebraic space with rational singularities depends only on the topology of a given resolution;
see
[
KM92
, Proposition 12.1.6]
and the proof of
[
KM92
, Proposition 12.1.7]
. By
[
AV21
, Proposition 5.1]
the equality (
20
) remains valid under small locally trivial deformations.
Hence, taking
[
BL22
, Remark 4.10]
into account and passing to a topologically trivializing open subset
0\in T\subseteq S\cap\Deflt(Y)
for the locally trivial family
\mathscr{W}\to\mathscr{Y}\to\Deflt(Y)
, we deduce from
[
BL22
, Lemma 2.13]
that each fiber
\mathscr{Y}_{t}=\pi_{Y}^{-1}(t)
,
t\in T
, is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Local Torelli theorems

In this section we establish a local Torelli theorem for primitive Enriques varieties (see
1.2
) and one for bielliptic surfaces (see
4.2
), similarly to the case of Enriques surfaces. These two results will be used crucially in Section
5
to determine the behaviour of certain primitive Enriques varieties under small deformations.

4.1.
A local Torelli theorem for PEVs

Proof of
1.2
.

We proceed in four steps, following the strategy of the proof of
[
OS11b
, Theorem 2.4]
.
The notion of marking of a primitive Enriques variety, which will be used in the proof below, can be found in Subsection
2.4
.

Step 1
: Construction of the local period map.

By shrinking the smooth base of the locally trivial Kuranishi family
\pi_{Y}\colon\mathscr{Y}\to\Deflt(Y)
of the given primitive Enriques variety
Y\simeq X/G
, we obtain a locally trivial deformation
f_{Y}\colon\mathscr{Y}\to S
of
Y
over a simply connected and smooth base
S\subseteq\Deflt(Y)
. We denote by
f_{X}\colon\mathscr{X}\times_{\Deflt(X)}S\to S
the corresponding locally trivial deformation of
X
, where
\pi_{X}\colon\mathscr{X}\to\Deflt(X)
is the locally trivial Kuranishi family of the primitive symplectic variety
X
.
Since
S
is by construction a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Deflt(X)
, for each
s\in S
we have
\mathscr{Y}_{s}\simeq\mathscr{X}_{s}/G
, so that the BBF lattice
\big(H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}\,,q_{\mathscr{X}_{s}}\big)
of
\mathscr{X}_{s}
is endowed with an orthogonal
\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
-action under the identification
G\leftrightarrow\mu_{d}(\mathbb{C})
.

Fix now an
L
-marking
\phi\colon\big(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}\,,q_{X}\big)\to(L,q)
of
Y
. Since the locally trivial family
f_{X}\colon\mathscr{X}\to S
is topologically locally trivial by
[
AV21
, Proposition 5.1]
, the local system
R^{2}(f_{X})_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathscr{X}}
on
S
is actually a constant sheaf on
S
by
[
EZS10
, Proposition 1.5]
. Note that its stalk at a point
s\in S
is canonically isomorphic to the singular cohomology group
H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathbb{Z})
, where
\mathscr{X}_{s}=f_{X}^{-1}(s)
. Hence, all these cohomology groups (for varying
s\in S
), and thus their torsion-free parts
H^{2}(\mathscr{X}_{s},\mathbb{Z})_{\text{tf}}
, are isomorphic. In view of the invariance of the BBF form under locally trivial deformations due to
[
BL22
, Lemma 5.7]
, it follows that the
L
-marking of
Y
can be uniquely extended to an
L
-marking of the restricted family
f_{Y}\colon\mathscr{Y}\to S
; the fiberwise marking is denoted again by
\phi
with a slight abuse of notation.

Consequently, we obtain a period map

\mathscr{P}_{Y}\colon S\to\Omega(Y),\ s\mapsto\big[\phi(\sigma_{\mathscr{X}_{s}})\big],

which is holomorphic. Indeed, it is the restriction of the period map
\mathscr{P}_{X}
, which is itself holomorphic, since
(f_{X})_{*}\,\Omega^{[2]}_{\mathscr{X}/S}
is a holomorphic subbundle of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}
; see the proof of

[
BL22
, Proposition 5.5]
.

Step 2
: Reduction step.

Since both
S
and
\Omega(Y)
are smooth, to prove the statement, it suffices to show that

(a)

the differential
d(\mathscr{P}_{Y})_{0}
of
\mathscr{P}_{Y}
at
0\in S
is injective, and

(b)

\dim T_{0}S=\dim T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\Omega(Y)
.

First, as far as item (a) is concerned, since the differential
d(\mathscr{P}_{X})_{0}
is bijective by
[
BL22
, Proposition 5.5]
, taking into account the horizontal inclusions and the vertical isomorphisms from the diagram (
18
) we conclude that the differential
d(\mathscr{P}_{Y})_{0}
is injective,
as desired. Next, to prove item (b), we first note that

(21)

\dim T_{0}S=h^{1}(Y,\mathscr{T}_{Y})=h^{1,1}(X)_{1}

according to
3.1
(iii). The remainder of the proof is therefore devoted to the computation of
\dim T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\Omega(Y)
. We proceed by distinguishing two cases for
d
and we use the following observation: as
\phi
induces an isomorphism
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}\simeq L_{\mathbb{C},1}
, by
[
Voi07
, Proposition 10.1]
we know that

(22)

T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\mathbb{P}(L_{\mathbb{C},1})\simeq\Hom\big(\mathbb{C}\phi(\sigma),L_{\mathbb{C},1}/\mathbb{C}\phi(\sigma)\big)\simeq\Hom\big(\mathbb{C}\sigma,H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}/\mathbb{C}\sigma\big).

Step 3
: Case
d\geq 3
.

For any
\omega\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}
we have
g^{[*]}\omega=\xi\omega
, and thus

q_{X}(\omega)\overset{(\ast)}{=\!=}q_{X}\big(g^{[*]}\omega\big)=q_{X}(\xi\omega)=\xi^{2}q_{X}(\omega),

where
(\ast)
holds due to (
10
).
Since
d>2
, we infer that the weight space
L_{\mathbb{C},1}
is isotropic in
L_{\mathbb{C}}
. Therefore,

\Omega(Y)=\big\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}(L_{\mathbb{C},1})\mid q_{X}(\omega,\overline{\omega})>0\big\}

is an open subset of
\mathbb{P}(L_{\mathbb{C},1})
. It follows now from (
22
) that

(23)

\dim T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\Omega(Y)=\dim\Hom\big(\mathbb{C}\sigma,H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}/\mathbb{C}\sigma\big).

Now, since the Hodge decomposition (
1
) is preserved by automorphisms of
X
, we have

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}=H^{2,0}(X)_{1}\oplus H^{1,1}(X)_{1}\oplus H^{0,2}(X)_{1},

where
H^{0,2}(X)_{1}=0
, because
g^{[*]}
acts via multiplication by
\overline{\xi}
on
H^{0,2}(X)
and
d>2
. Hence, by (
21
) and (
23
) we deduce that

\dim T_{0}S=h^{1,1}(X)_{1}=\dim T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\Omega(Y),

which finishes the proof in the case
d\geq 3
.

Step 4
: Case
d=2
.

Since
d=2
, we have
\xi=\overline{\xi}=-1
, and thus
\overline{\sigma}\in H^{0,2}(X)_{-1}=H^{0,2}(X)_{1}
. The Hodge decomposition (
1
) yields

\displaystyle H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}

\displaystyle=H^{2,0}(X)_{1}\oplus H^{1,1}(X)_{1}\oplus H^{0,2}(X)_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\sigma\oplus H^{1,1}(X)_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}\overline{\sigma}.

By (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) we deduce that the orthogonal complement of
\sigma\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})_{1}
with respect to
q_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the subspace
V=\mathbb{C}\sigma\oplus H^{1,1}(X)_{1}
. Therefore,

(24)

\dim\Hom(\mathbb{C}\sigma,V/\mathbb{C}\sigma)=h^{1,1}(X)_{1}.

Now, since
\Omega(Y)
is an open subset of the quadric in
\mathbb{P}(L_{\mathbb{C},1})
defined by the equation
q_{X}(\cdot)=0
, the tangent space
T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\Omega(Y)
is precisely the tangent space of the quadric at the point
[\phi(\sigma)]
. Since the quadratic form
q_{X}
is nondegenerate, the quadric
\big\{q(\cdot)=0\big\}
is smooth. Using this, together with (
22
), we deduce that the tangent space of the quadric at the point
[\phi(\sigma)]
is precisely

\Hom(\mathbb{C}\sigma,V/\mathbb{C}\sigma)
. It follows now from (
21
) and (
24
) that

\dim T_{0}S=h^{1,1}(X)_{1}=\dim T_{[\phi(\sigma)]}\,\Omega(Y),

which completes the proof in the case
d=2
.
∎

4.2.
A local Torelli theorem for bielliptic surfaces

In this subsection
we describe the Kuranishi family of a bielliptic surface (see
4.1
) and we provide subsequently a local Torelli theorem for bielliptic surfaces (see
4.2
).

First of all, we recall some basic facts about abelian surfaces. Specifically, if
A
is an abelian surface, then
H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
6
by
[
BL04
, Corollary 1.3.3]
, while by
[
BL04
, Lemma 1.3.1]
the cup product induces an isomorphism

\bigwedge^{2}H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{4}(A,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore,
H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})
has a lattice structure and the resulting nondegenerate, integral, quadratic form on
H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})
is denoted by
q_{A}
. Note that
\big(H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})\,,q_{A}\big)
is an even, unimodular, nondegenerate lattice with signature of type
(3,\ast)
, which is isomorphic to
U^{\oplus 3}
, where
U
is the hyperbolic plane over
\mathbb{Z}
;
see
[
Huy16
, Chapter 3, Subsections 1.3, 2.2 and 2.3]
.
The direct summands of the Hodge decomposition of
H^{2}(A,\mathbb{C})
have dimension

h^{2,0}(A)=h^{0,2}(A)=1\quad\text{ and }\quad h^{1,1}(A)=4,

and the symplectic form
\sigma_{A}
on
A
induces an isomorphism

(25)

\mathscr{T}_{A}\simeq\Omega^{1}_{A},

which yields

H^{1}(A,\mathscr{T}_{A})\simeq H^{1}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})\simeq H^{1,1}(A).

Furthermore, the Kuranishi space
\Def(A)
of
A
is smooth of dimension

\dim\Def(A)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{1}(A,\mathscr{T}_{A})=h^{1,1}(A)=4

by the Bogomolov–Tian–Todorov unobstructedness theorem, and the Kuranishi family
\pi_{A}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\Def(A)
of
A
is universal; see, for example,
[
Wav69
, Theorems 1.1 and 4.2]
.
In particular, up to shrinking
\Def(A)
, each fiber of
\pi_{A}
is an abelian surface, and also the following
local Torelli statement
holds: the local period map

\mathscr{P}_{A}\colon\Def(A)\to\Omega(A),\ t\mapsto\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2,0}(\mathscr{A}_{t})\big),

where

\Omega(A)\coloneqq\big\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2}(A,\mathbb{C})\big)\mid q_{A}(\omega)=0,\ q_{A}(\omega,\overline{\omega})>0\big\},

is a local isomorphism, see
[
Voi07
, Subsection 10.3.2]
.

We now turn our attention to bielliptic surfaces in order to derive an analogous statement to the above. Given a bielliptic surface
B
, recall that there exist an abelian surface
A
and a finite cyclic group
G
of order
d\in\{2,3,4,6\}
which acts freely on
A
such that
B\simeq A/G
; see
[
OS11b
, Section 6]
and
[
FTVB22
, Subsections 2.1 and 2.2]
. The quotient map
A\to B
is the
global index-one cover
of
B
(also referred to as the
canonical cover
of
B
), see
[
KM98
, Definition 5.19]
.
Since

H^{0}(A,\omega_{A})^{G}\simeq H^{0}(B,\omega_{B})=\{0\}
,
the group
G\subseteq\Aut(A)
must contain at least one nonsymplectic automorphism.
In fact, by inspecting the classification table of bielliptic surfaces due to Bagnera–De Franchis, one can readily check that

(26)

G\text{ acts on $A$ nonsymplectically},

i.e., the generator of
G
is a purely nonsymplectic automorphism of order
d
; see
[
OS11b
, Section 6]
or
[
Suw69
, Theorem, pp. 473-474]
.
Taking (
25
) and (
26
) into account and arguing as in the proof of
3.1
(iii), we infer that

(27)

H^{1}(B,\mathscr{T}_{B})\simeq H^{1}(A,\mathscr{T}_{A})^{G}=H^{1}(A,\mathscr{T}_{A})_{0}\simeq H^{1}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})_{1}\simeq H^{1,1}(A)_{1}\,.

We can now describe the Kuranishi family of
B\simeq A/G
.

Proposition 4.1

(The Kuranishi family of a bielliptic surface)
.

Let
B\simeq A/G
be a bielliptic surface, where
A
is an abelian surface and
G\subseteq\Aut(A)
is generated by a purely nonsymplectic automorphism
g\in G
of order
d\in\{2,3,4,6\}
and acts freely on
A
. Let also
\pi_{A}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\Def(A)
be the universal Kuranishi family of
A
and set
\mathscr{A}_{t}\coloneqq\pi_{A}^{-1}(t)
. The following statements hold:

(i)

Let
F
be the connected component of the fixed locus of the induced action of
G
on
\Def(A)
that contains the point
0\in\Def(A)
. The Kuranishi space

\Def(B)\simeq F
of deformations of
B
is smooth of dimension

h^{1}(B,\mathscr{T}_{B})=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }\ d=2,\\
1,&\text{if }\ d\in\{3,4,6\},\end{cases}

and the Kuranishi family

\pi_{B}\colon\mathscr{B}\coloneqq\big(\mathscr{A}\times_{\Def(A)}\Def(B)\big)/G\to\Def(B)

of
B
is universal for all of its fibers.

(ii)

If
V
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Def(B)\subset\Def(A)
, then for each
t\in V
the fiber
\mathscr{B}_{t}
is a bielliptic surface isomorphic to the quotient
\mathscr{A}_{t}/G
of the abelian surface
\mathscr{A}_{t}
.

Proof.

To show that
\mathscr{B}\coloneqq\big(\mathscr{A}\times_{\Def(A)}F\big)/G\to F
is a miniversal deformation of
B\simeq\mathscr{B}_{0}
and that
F
is smooth, we closely follow the proof of
1.1
(i), but with the following differences: in the second paragraph, we apply
2.20

instead of both
[
BL22
, Lemma 4.6]
and
2.21
to extend the
G
-action on
A
equivariantly to its universal family
\mathscr{A}\to\Def(A)
, while in the third paragraph, we replace
3.1
(iii) by (
27
).

Since, according to
[
Gra74
, p. 107, 3rd paragraph]
, miniversal deformations of a given compact complex manifold are uniquely determined (up to isomorphism), we conclude that the above deformation is actually the Kuranishi family
\mathscr{B}\to\Def(B)\simeq F
of
B
; in particular, the germ
\big(\Def(B),0\big)
is reduced. Hence, we have

\dim\Def(B)=h^{1}(B,\mathscr{T}_{B})=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }\ d=2,\\
1,&\text{if }\ d\in\{3,4,6\},\end{cases}

where the second equality holds by
[
Lan79
, Theorem 4.9]
. Next, to show that
\mathscr{B}\to\Def(B)
is universal for
B
(and thus for all of its fibers due to the openness of versality),
by
[
Wav69
, Theorems 1.1 and 4.2]
it suffices to show that
h^{0}(\mathscr{B}_{t},\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{t}})
is independent of
t
in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Def(B)
. However, granting (ii) for a moment, this follows immediately from
[
Lan79
, Theorem 4.9]
, which shows that bielliptic surfaces carry a unique (up to scalar) holomorphic vector field.

Consequently, to complete the proof of the theorem, it remains to show (ii). To do so, we proceed in two steps.

Step 1
:
We will show that
G
acts freely on any fiber of
\pi_{B}
over a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Def(B)\subset\Def(A)
.

To this end, consider the nonfree locus
\mathscr{A}^{\text{nf}}
of the induced
G
-action on
\mathscr{A}
, that is,

\mathscr{A}^{\text{nf}}\coloneqq\big\{x\in\mathscr{A}\mid G_{x}\neq\{\Id_{G}\}\big\},

which is a closed subset of
\mathscr{A}

and does not contain any point of
\mathscr{A}_{0}\simeq A
by assumption. Since the morphism
\pi_{A}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\Def(A)
is smooth and proper, the image
\pi_{A}\big(\mathscr{A}^{\text{nf}}\big)
is a closed subset of
\Def(A)
that does not contain
0\in\Def(A)
and the restricted morphism
\pi_{A}|_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}^{\text{nf}}}\colon\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{A}^{\text{nf}}\to\Def(A)\setminus\pi\big(\mathscr{A}^{\text{nf}}\big)
is also smooth and proper.
This yields the assertion; cf. the proof of
[
OS11b
, Proposition 1.2]
.

Step 2
:
We will demonstrate that
G
acts nonsymplectically on any fiber of
\pi_{B}
over a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Def(B)\subset\Def(A)
.

For
t
in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in\Def(B)\subset\Def(A)
as in Step 1, the quotient map
\mathscr{A}_{t}\to\mathscr{B}_{t}\simeq\mathscr{A}_{t}/G
is a finite étale cover. Therefore,
\mathscr{B}_{t}
has torsion canonical class. But since Hodge numbers are constant in the smooth family
\mathscr{B}\to\Def(B)
whose central fiber
\mathscr{B}_{0}
is the given bielliptic surface
B
, we conclude that
\mathscr{B}_{t}\simeq\mathscr{A}_{t}/G
is also a bielliptic surface. In particular, the induced
G
-action on
\mathscr{A}_{t}
is nonsymplectic, as explained earlier in this subsection; see (
26
). This finishes the proof.
∎

As a short interlude, we explain here a notion of marking for bielliptic surfaces, which will be tacitly used in the proof of
4.2
. Specifically, the discussion that precedes
2.7
remains valid also in the present framework, and consequently an identical version of
2.7
with respect to the Hermitian form associated with
q_{A}
can be established for the canonical abelian cover
A
of a bielliptic surface
B\simeq A/G
. This is due to the following two observations: (
10
) can be replaced by the invariance of the cup product under homeomorphisms,
while (
3
) can be replaced by the equalities
[\sigma_{A}]\smile[\sigma_{A}]=0=[\overline{\sigma_{A}}]\smile[\overline{\sigma_{A}}]
and the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations; see

[
Voi07
, Theorem 6.32]
.
Therefore, an
L
-marking of a bielliptic surface
can be defined in the same way as it was previously done for a primitive Enriques variety; see Subsection
2.4
.

Finally, we establish the desired statement for bielliptic surfaces, which will only be used in the proof of
5.2
.

Theorem 4.2

(Local Torelli theorem for bielliptic surfaces)
.

Let
B\simeq A/G
be a bielliptic surface and let

\Omega(B)\coloneqq\big\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2}(A,\mathbb{C})_{1}\big)\mid q_{A}(\omega)=0,\ q_{A}(\omega,\overline{\omega})>0\big\}

be the period domain for
B
inside
\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2}(A,\mathbb{C})_{1}\big)
. If
\pi_{B}\colon\mathscr{B}\to\Def(B)
is the universal Kuranishi family of
B
and if
\mathscr{A}_{t}\coloneqq\pi_{A}^{-1}(t)
is the fiber over
t\in\Def(A)
of the universal Kuranishi family
\pi_{A}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\Def(A)
of
A
, then the local period map

\mathscr{P}_{B}\colon\Def(B)\to\Omega(B),\ s\mapsto\mathbb{P}\big(H^{2,0}(\mathscr{A}_{s})\big)

is a local isomorphism at
0\in\Def(B)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
B\simeq A/G
, where
A
is an abelian surface and
G\subseteq\Aut(A)
is generated by a purely nonsymplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(A)
of order
d\in\{2,3,4,6\}
and acts freely on
A
.
Now, arguing as in Step 1 of the proof of
1.2
, we obtain a holomorphic local period map

\mathscr{P}_{B}\colon\Def(B)\to\Omega(B),\ s\mapsto\big[\phi(\sigma_{\mathscr{A}_{s}})\big],

which is the restriction of the local period map
\mathscr{P}_{A}\colon\Def(A)\to\Omega(A)
for
A
.
Here,
\phi
denotes the fiberwise marking of the deformation over a simply connected base
S
of the bielliptic surface
B\simeq A/G
involved in the proof, and
\sigma_{\mathscr{A}_{s}}
denotes the symplectic form on the fiber
\mathscr{A}_{s}
over
s\in S
of the corresponding deformation of the abelian surface
A
.

Next, to conclude that
\mathscr{P}_{B}
is a local isomorphism at
0\in\Def(B)
, arguing as in Step 2 of the proof of
1.2
, except that we now invoke the local Torelli theorem for abelian surfaces
and (
27
) instead of
[
BL22
, Proposition 5.5]
and
3.1
(iii), respectively,
we see that it remains to compute
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T_{[\phi(\sigma_{A})]}\,\Omega(B)
and to verify that it is equal to
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T_{0}\Def(B)
. This will be done below by distinguishing cases for
d
and by noting that we now work with the lattice
\big(H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})\,,q_{A}\big)
, where the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
q_{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
associated to
q_{A}(\cdot)
is the cup product in cohomology.

If
d\in\{3,4,6\}
, then the same argument as in Step 3 of the proof of
1.2
works also in our context due to the following observation: For any
\omega\in H^{2}(A,\mathbb{C})_{1}
we have
g^{*}\omega=\xi\omega
, where
\xi
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity, and thus

\omega\smile\omega\overset{(\ast\ast)}{=\!=\!=}g^{*}\omega\smile g^{*}\omega=\xi^{2}(\omega\smile\omega),

where
(\ast\ast)
holds because the cup product is preserved under homeomorphisms.

If, finally,
d=2
, then we repeat verbatim the argument from Step 4 of the proof of

PR23
, Lemma 2.21(1)]
, arguing as in Step 2 of the proof of
[
Gra18
, Proposition 6.2]
we deduce that

\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{S}/\Def(S^{\prime})}(\nu,\mathcal{H})/G\to\Def(S^{\prime})

is a locally trivial deformation of
Y=M_{S}(v,H)/G
such that any member
\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}_{t}
of this family is by construction a primitive Enriques variety of the form

\mathscr{Y}_{t}^{\prime}\simeq M_{\mathscr{S}_{t}}(\nu_{t},\mathcal{H}_{t})/G.

Now, by the universality of the locally trivial Kuranishi family
\mathscr{Y}\to\Def(Y)
of
Y=M_{S}(v,H)/G
, we obtain a unique morphism
\Def(S^{\prime})\to\Deflt(Y)
of complex manifolds, and we may also assume that these two spaces are simply connected. The next result indicates how small locally trivial deformations of
Y
look like and constitutes a vast generalization of
[
OS11b
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

With the same notation and assumptions as above, the morphism
\Def(S^{\prime})\to\Deflt(Y)
of complex manifolds is a local isomorphism at the origin
0\in\Def(S^{\prime})
. In particular, any small locally trivial deformation of
Y=M_{S}(v,H)/G
is of the same form.

Proof.

The proof of the above assertion is essentially the same as the one of
[
OS11b
, Proposition 3.3]
, but we provide the details for the benefit of the reader.

First, we claim that

(30)

H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})_{1}\subseteq(v^{\perp})_{\mathbb{C}}.

Indeed, since the considered Mukai vector
v=(r,\ell,\chi-r)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is
G
-invariant by our assumption that
\rho(S)=10
and since the BBF lattice
\big(H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}),q_{S}\big)
of
S
is endowed in the present setting with an orthogonal
G
-action, for any
\omega\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})_{1}
, which is identified with the Mukai vector
w\coloneqq(0,\omega,0)\in\widetilde{H}(S,\mathbb{C})
,
we have

q_{S}(\ell,\omega)=q_{S}(\tau^{*}\ell,\tau^{*}\omega)=q_{S}(\ell,-\omega)=-q_{S}(\ell,\omega),

whence

\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=q_{S}(\ell,\omega)=0,

which proves the claim.

Next, since the Hodge isometry (
12
) is
G
-equivariant
and due to (
30
), we obtain an identification of weight spaces:

H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})_{1}\simeq H^{2}\big(M_{S}(v,H),\mathbb{C}\big)_{1}\,.

In view of the above, we also readily deduce that the period domain
\Omega(S^{\prime})
for the Enriques surface
S^{\prime}
coincides with the period domain
\Omega(Y)
for the primitive Enriques variety
Y
.

In conclusion, to prove the statement, we argue exactly as in the third paragraph of the proof of
5.1
, observing that the corresponding diagram involving local period maps is commutative by the very definition of the period maps and the fact that the symplectic form on
M_{S}(v,H)
arises from the symplectic form on
S
by (
12
).
∎

We conclude the paper with the following two observations.

Remark 5.4
.

(1)

Note that
[
OS11b
, Remark 3.4]
remains valid in our more general setting; namely, there exist primitive Enriques varieties of the form
M_{S}(v,H)/G
, where
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, which arise from Enriques surfaces that are more special than the very general ones considered above.

(2)

In the above setting, where we work with the universal covering K3 surface
S
of a very general Enriques surface
S^{\prime}
, consider a relative positive Mukai vector of the form

\nu=\big(1,c_{1}(\mathcal{L}),s\big),

where
\mathcal{L}
is a holomorphic line bundle on
\mathscr{S}
arising from one on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}

and
\mathcal{H}
is a relative polarization on
\mathscr{S}
stemming from one on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
.
Recall that the group
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
acts on the induced family
\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{S}/\Def(S^{\prime})}(\nu,\mathcal{H})\to\Def(S^{\prime})
in a fiber-preserving way.
Up to shrinking
\Def(S^{\prime})
, the relative moduli space

\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{S}/\Def(S^{\prime})}(\nu,\mathcal{H})\to\Def(S^{\prime})

coincides with the relative Douady space

\big(\mathscr{S}/\Def(S^{\prime})\big)^{[c_{2}]}\to\Def(S^{\prime})

\mathscr{S}\to\Def(S^{\prime})
of K3 surfaces, see
[
Huy03

denotes the second Chern class of the rank-one coherent sheaves belonging to the relative moduli space.
Thus,
as both
\nu
and
\mathcal{H}
are fiberwisely preserved by construction,
the induced family

\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{S}/\Def(S^{\prime})}(\nu,\mathcal{H})/G\to\Def(S^{\prime})

recovers the example studied in Subsection
5.1
.

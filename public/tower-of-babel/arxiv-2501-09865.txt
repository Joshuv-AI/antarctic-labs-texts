Lower bounds on the ℓ \ell -rank of ideal class groups Thanks: This research is supported by NSF grant 2336000.
Daniel E. Martin

Abstract
. For a prime number ℓ \ell and an extension of number fields K / F K/F , we prove new lower bounds on the ℓ \ell -rank of the ideal class group of K K based on prime ramification in K / F K/F . Unlike related results from the literature, our bound is supported on prime ideals in F F over which at least one (rather than each) prime in K K has ramification index divisible by ℓ \ell . This bound holds with a proviso on the Galois group of the normal closure of K / F K/F , which is satisfied by towers of Galois extensions, intermediate fields in nilpotent extensions, and intermediate fields in dihedral extensions of degree 8 ​ n 8n , to name a few. We also use our lower bound to prove a new density result on number fields with infinite class field towers.

1.
Introduction

Given a prime
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
\ell
-rank of an abelian group
A
, denoted
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,A
, is the dimension of
A/A^{\ell}
as a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, where
A^{\ell}
is the subgroup of
\ell^{\text{th}}
powers. Our goal is prove new lower bounds for
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)
, where
\textup{Cl}(K)
is the ideal class group of a number field
K
.

There is an immense literature on such lower bounds. In place of a survey that would inevitably contain gaps, we reference the reader to some of the most recent papers on the subject; the citations therein cover much of its modern history. For the case when
K
is a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
see
[
CS23
,
GL20
,
LYW21
,
KK17
]
, for cubic extensions see
[
Kul16
]
, for dihedral extensions of odd or prime degree see
[
CC20
,
Lem05
]
, and for Galois extensions of prime-power degree see
[
Liu24
]
. For a more general lower bound, with no direct restrictions on
K
, we turn to the work of Roquette and Zassenhaus.

Theorem 1.1

(Roquette–Zassenhaus
[
RZ69
]
)
.

For a prime
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and a number field
K
, let
s_{\ell}(K)
be the number of prime ideals in
\mathbb{Q}
that become
\ell^{\text{th}}
powers in
K
. Then
\emph{rk}_{\ell}\,\emph{Cl}(K)\geq s_{\ell}(K)-2[K:\mathbb{Q}]+2
.

This theorem is the generalization to non-Galois extensions of a result of Brumer and Rosen
[
BR63
]
, which Brumer used in combination with the Golod–Shafarevich theorem to give new examples of number fields with infinite class field towers
[
Bru65
]
. The constant
2[K:\mathbb{Q}]-2
given above was improved by Connell and Sussman (as discussed in Section
2
), who also generalized the theorem to relative extensions
K/F

[
CS70
]
.

The point of Theorem
1.1
, and indeed parts of all the aforementioned papers, is to improve on the lower bound implied by genus theory, which in many cases is trivial. In order to do so, however, Theorem
1.1
requires the presence of many primes
p
in
\mathbb{Q}
that meet a rather severe restriction:
\ell
must divide
every
ramification index of
p
in
K
. We can motivate such a restriction by observing the case
\ell=2
for cubic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
. On the one hand, Bhargava and Varma prove that when cubic fields are ordered by discriminant magnitude, the average number of 2-torsion elements in the class group is
5/4

[
BV15
]
. On the other hand, the average number of ramified primes grows without bound. So in a cubic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, an arbitrary abundance of prime splittings of the form
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
can, and typically does, contribute almost nothing to
\textup{rk}_{2}\,\textup{Cl}(K)
.

We will show that cubic fields are neither the rule nor the exception. As one example, in a quartic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
with dihedral Galois group (of the normal closure)
D_{4}
, ramified primes, even those of the form
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
or
\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}\mathfrak{p}_{3}
, do contribute to
\textup{rk}_{2}\,\textup{Cl}(K)
. This is in contrast to genus theory, where primes
p
that split as
\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
or
\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}\mathfrak{p}_{3}
do not contribute to the genus number (as the genus field cannot contain
\sqrt{\pm p}
).

More generally, we prove that
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)
grows alongside the number of ramification indices divisible by
\ell
for a number field with Galois group
D_{n}
as long as
4\,|\,n
. (In the aforementioned cubic case in which such growth does not occur,
n=3
.) Dihedral extensions form a proper subset of number fields that possess this property. The full collection of number fields we consider is defined by the Galois group condition below. Before the definition, however, we note a small departure from common notation.

Notation 1.2
.

For an extension of number fields
K/F
,
\textup{Gal}(K/F)
denotes a pair of groups written
G/H
, where
G
is the Galois group of the closure of
K/F
and
H
is the maximal subgroup of
G
that fixes
K
. We call
K
a
G/H
-extension
of
F
.

Note that when
K/F
is normal,
\textup{Gal}(K/F)=G/1
, where
G
is the usual Galois group. In this case we sometimes write “
G
” instead of “
G/1
”, as there is never a risk of confusion.

We use
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)
to denote the conjugacy class of an element
\sigma
in a group
G
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
H\leq G
be groups. We say the pair
G/H
is
\ell
-divisible
if there are subgroups
H\leq G_{1},...,G_{\delta}\leq G
such that given any
\sigma\in G-H
of order
\ell
, there exists
i\leq\delta
such that
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\tau)\cap H=\emptyset
for some
\tau\in\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\cap G_{i}
. For such a pair, let
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)
denote the smallest possible value of
\delta
. We say an extension of number fields
K/F
is
\ell
-divisible
if
\textup{Gal}(K/F)
is
\ell
-divisible. For such an extension, let
\delta_{\ell}(K/F)=\delta_{\ell}(\textup{Gal}(K/F))
.

Returning to the dihedral group
D_{n}=\langle r,s\mid r^{n},s^{2},(rs)^{2}\rangle
, if
4\,|\,n
then
D_{n}/\langle s\rangle
is
2
-divisible with
\delta_{2}(D_{n}/\langle s\rangle)=2
, and if
4\nmid n
then
D_{n}/\langle s\rangle
is not
2
-divisible. For an odd prime
\ell
,
\delta_{\ell}(D_{n}/\langle s\rangle)
is 1 if
\ell\,|\,n
and
0
otherwise. This is proved in the appendix as a corollary to Theorem
4.10
, which gives a general criterion for
\ell
-divisibility of a semidirect product. Also in the appendix, which is essentially an index of “common”
\ell
-divisible groups and field extensions, are proofs that Galois extensions (Proposition
4.4
), towers of
\ell
-divisible extensions (Theorem
4.3
), composita of
\ell
-divisible extensions (Theorem
4.12
), and extensions with unique Sylow
\ell
-subgroups (Theorem
4.8
) are all
\ell
-divisible.

It is these
\ell
-divisible extensions for which our weaker ramification requirement—
\ell
divides
some
ramification index of
p
—forces
\ell
-torsion in the class group. The following is proved in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For an extension of number fields
K/F
, let
t_{\ell}(K/F)
be the number of prime ideals in
F
with at least one ramification index divisible by
\ell
, and let
e_{\ell}(K/F)
be the exponent of
\ell
in the prime factorization of
[K:F]
. If
K/F
is
\ell
-divisible and
t_{\ell}(K/F)\geq 1
, then

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq\frac{t_{\ell}(K/F)}{\delta_{\ell}(K/F)}-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F).

Remark that
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
is either
\textup{rk}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
(the free rank of the unit group) or
\textup{rk}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+1
depending on whether
K
contains an
\ell^{\text{th}}
root of unity.

We highlight several corollaries of Theorem
1.4
that are not proved in-text, but are immediate consequences of results from the appendix.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
F=F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{n}=K
be number fields such that each
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
is Galois. Then
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq t_{\ell}(K/F)/n-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F)
.

It is interesting to note in the last corollary that
K/F_{1}
may well be unramified. Ramification in
F_{1}/F
contributes to
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)
, but with diminishing returns as
n
grows.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
G/H=\textup{Gal}(K/F)
, and suppose
G
has a unique Sylow
\ell
subgroup of nilpotency class
n
. Then
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq t_{\ell}(K/F)/n-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F)
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Suppose
\textup{Gal}(K/F)=(N\rtimes_{\varphi}H)/H
with
H
and
N
abelian. If for any
h\in H
of order
\ell
,
\varphi(h)
is the identity if and only if it is the identity on the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
N
, then
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq t_{\ell}(K/F)/2-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F)
.

For
\ell=2
, the above condition on
\varphi(h)
is met in the dihedral group of order
2n
if and only if
4\,|\,n
, hence our prior assertion.

Our second result in Section
2
is somewhat of a corollary to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. There is potentially significant improvement on Theorem
1.4
’s lower bound obtained by strengthening the
\ell
-divisibility hypothesis.

Definition 1.8
.

We say the pair of groups
G/H
is
strongly

\ell
-
divisible
if there are subgroups
H\leq G_{1},...,G_{\delta}\leq G
such that given any
\sigma\in G-H
satisfying
\sigma^{\ell}\in H
and
|\sigma|=\ell^{n}
for some
n
, there exists
i\leq\delta
such that
\sigma\in G_{i}
and
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\sigma)\cap H=\emptyset
. For such a pair, let
\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)
denote the smallest possible value of
\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i}[G_{i}:H]
. We say an extension of number fields
K/F
is
strongly

\ell
-divisible
if
\textup{Gal}(K/F)
is strongly
\ell
-divisible. For such an extension, let
\Delta_{\ell}(K/F)=\Delta_{\ell}(\textup{Gal}(K/F))
.

In such extensions, our class number bound is no longer supported on ramified primes in the base field
F
as in Theorem
1.4
(or like the genus number). We now count primes in
K
.

Theorem 1.9
.

For an extension of number fields
K/F
, let
T_{\ell}(K/F)
be the number of prime ideals in
K
with ramification index divisible by
\ell
. If
K/F
is strongly
\ell
-divisible and
T_{\ell}(K/F)\geq 1
, then

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq\frac{T_{\ell}(K/F)}{\Delta_{\ell}(K/F)}-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F).

Note that for abelian number fields, Theorem
1.9
can be deduced (and the “error term”
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F)
can be improved; see
[
Fur67
]
) by computing the relative genus number of the fixed field of the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\textup{Gal}(K/F)
.

To get some intuition as to whether Theorem
1.9
can be expected to improve on Theorem
1.4
, let us look at the simplest case: normal extensions. Again, this corollary is not proved in-text. It is a consequence of Proposition
4.4
in the appendix.

Corollary 1.10
.

Suppose
\textup{Gal}(K/F)=G/1
, and let
n
be the number of subgroups of
G
or order
\ell
. Then
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq T_{\ell}(K/F)/n-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-e_{\ell}(K/F)
.

Under the setup above, let us make the heuristic assumption that primes in
F
with ramification index
\ell
lie below an average of
\sqrt{|G|/\ell}
primes in
K
. Then such primes contribute
\sqrt{|G|/\ell}/n
to the lower bound in Theorem
1.9
, while only contributing
1
to the lower bound in Theorem
1.4
. So as we range over normal extensions of
F
with Galois group
G
, we might expect improvement with Theorem
1.9
when
n
is less than
\sqrt{|G|/\ell}
. (This implicitly uses another heuristic assumption that ramification is, on average, dominated in such number fields by minimal ramification indices. In other words, we expect a ramification index divisible by
\ell
to equal
\ell
. See Section 4 in
[
Mal04
]
.)

Section
3
is devoted to consequences of Theorem
1.4
regarding Hilbert class field towers. Recall that the class field tower of a number field
K
is the tower of field extensions
K=K_{0}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}\subseteq\cdots
, where
K_{i}
is the maximal abelian, unramified extension of
K_{i-1}
. Similarly, to obtain the
\ell
-class field tower we we take maximal abelian, unramified
\ell
-extensions. (See
[
Lem10
]
for a survey.) In 1964, Golod and Shafarevich proved that there are number fields with infinite class field towers. In particular, such number fields have no finite extension with trivial class group, answering the question of Furtwängler in the negative.

Theorem 1.11

(Golod–Shafarevich
[
GS64
]
)
.

Let
K
be a number field such that
\emph{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)>2+2\sqrt{\emph{rk}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+1}
for some prime number
\ell
. Then the
\ell
-class field tower of
K
is infinite.

Section
3
begins by applying the Golod–Shafarevich theorem to
\ell
-divisible extensions. The result, stated below, is analogous to a theorem of Brumer
[
Bru65
]
, who used a lower bound similar to Theorem
1.1
(due to him and Rosen
[
BR63
]
) to apply the Golod–Shafarevich theorem to number fields with highly composite discriminants. But because prime ramification need not contribute to the perfect
\ell^{\text{th}}
powers counted by Theorem
1.1
, Brumer could only conclude infinitude of the class field tower when
K/\mathbb{Q}
is normal. Theorem
1.4
allows us to extend Brumer’s result to many non-normal extensions.

In the corollary below,
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of a nonzero integer
n
.

Corollary 1.12
.

Suppose
K/\mathbb{Q}
is
\ell
-divisible for all primes
\ell
. Let
D
be the discriminant of
K
, and let
n
be the degree of its Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\omega(\Delta)\geq 4n\omega([K:\mathbb{Q}])
, then the class field tower of
K
is infinite.

A more precise (though harder to compute) bound than
4n\omega([K:\mathbb{Q}])
is proved in Corollary
3.1
, which also generalizes this result to relative extensions. Based on this we might expect that if
G/H
is
\ell
-divisible for each prime
\ell
, then for any number field
F
, the
G/H
-extensions of
F
should be dominated by number fields with infinite class field towers because the average number of primes dividing the relative discriminant grows without bound. Such a result appears difficult to prove for arbitrary
G
as we are left without the aid of class field theory. We can prove this, however, when
G
is nilpotent (in which case the
\ell
-divisibility hypothesis is automatic by Corollary
4.9
). To state the resulting theorem, we must fix some notation.

Notation 1.13
.

Given groups
H\leq G
and an element
\sigma\in G
, let
n_{\sigma}
denote the number of orbits when
\langle\sigma\rangle
acts on the cosets
G/H
via left multiplication, and set
a(G/H)=\max_{\sigma}(1/([G:H]-n_{\sigma}))
. Also, given
x\in\mathbb{R}
and a number field
F
, let
N(F,G/H,x)
be the number of
G/H
-extensions of
F
(in some fixed algebraic closure) for which the relative discriminant has absolute norm at most
x
in magnitude. Among such extensions, let
N_{0}(F,G/H,x)
count those with finite class field towers.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
G/H
be groups with
G
nilpotent of nilpotency class
n
, and let
F
be an extension of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
d
. There is a constant
c(d)
(depending only on
d
) such that for large
x
,

N_{0}(F,G/H,x)<\frac{c(d)x^{a(G/H)}(\log\log x)^{4nd\omega(d)-1}}{\log x}.

The weak Malle conjecture asserts that for any
F
and
G/H
there exists a constant
c(F,G)
such that
N(F,G/H,x)>c(F,G)x^{a(G/H)}

[
Mal02
]
. This was proved by Klüners and Malle for nilpotent groups in the special case where
H=1
, meaning the case of Galois extensions
[
KM04
]
. In particular, we obtain the following density result.

Corollary 1.15
.

With notation as in Theorem
1.14
, there is a constant
c(F,G)
(depending only on
F
and
G
) such that for large
x
,

\frac{N_{0}(F,G/1,x)}{N(F,G/1,x)}<\frac{c(F,G)(\log\log x)^{4nd\omega(d)-1}}{\log x}.

As with Corollary
1.12
, a more precise (though harder to compute) exponent than
4nd\omega(d)-1
can be found in Section
3
. Both the previous theorem and its corollary are proved as Theorem
3.6
.

Other density results regarding infinite class field towers can be found for quadratic fields in
[
Ger06
,
Ger04
,
Mou16
]
, for cyclotomic fields in
[
Shp08
]
, and for cyclic extensions of prime degree in
[
Ger02
]
.

2.
Proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.9

The following notation is used throughout this section.

Notation 2.1
.

For a prime number
\ell
and number fields
K/F
,
s_{\ell}(K/F)
denotes the number of prime ideals in
F
whose ramification indices (in
K
) are all divisible by
\ell
;
t_{\ell}(K/F)
denotes the number of prime ideals in
F
with at least one ramification index divisible by
\ell
;
T_{\ell}(K/F)
denotes the number of prime ideals in
K
with ramification index divisible by
\ell
; and
e_{\ell}(K/F)
is the exponent of
\ell
in
[K:F]
.

Our strategy for proving Theorem
1.4
is to show that if
K/F
is
\ell
-divisible and
p
is a prime ideal of
F
that gets counted by
t_{\ell}(K/F)
, then there is some intermediate field
F^{\prime}
containing a prime
\mathfrak{p}
over
p
that gets counted by
s_{\ell}(K/F^{\prime})
. We would then like to apply a result like Theorem
1.1
to
K/F^{\prime}
. Unfortunately, the known generalization of Theorem
1.1
to relative extensions (Theorem 1 in
[
CS70
]
) requires
p^{n}
to be principal for some
n
coprime to
\ell
. Even if we assume this of
p
, we cannot conclude the same of some
\mathfrak{p}
over
p
in an intermediate field. So we begin by removing this requirement with the next lemma. We note the additional benefit of potentially increasing the number of primes counted in a relative extension when compared to Theorem 1 in
[
CS70
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a prime number
\ell
and number fields
K/F
,

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)\geq s_{\ell}(K/F)-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{F^{*}\cap K^{*\ell}}{F^{*\ell}}\right).

Proof.

Let
G_{K}
denote the group of elements
\alpha\in K^{*}
such that
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
for some fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset K
, and define
G_{F}
analogously. In
[
CS70
]
(the proof of Theorem 1, but stop about halfway down page 527) it is shown that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)

\displaystyle\geq\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(F)+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{F^{*}\cap G_{K}}{G_{F}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{F^{*}\cap K^{*\ell}}{F^{*\ell}}\right).

The proof of our lemma amounts to showing that
s_{\ell}(K/F)
does not exceed the sum of the first two terms on right-hand side of this inequality.

Let
s=s_{\ell}(K/F)
, let
p_{1},...,p_{s}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
denote the prime ideals that become
\ell^{\text{th}}
powers in
K
, let
\mathcal{I}_{p}
denote the subgroup of fractional ideals generated by
p_{1},...,p_{s}
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{F}
denote the group of principal fractional ideals in
F
. The canonical exact sequence

1\to\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}\to\mathcal{I}_{p}\to\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}\to 1

induces an exact sequence of vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
:

1\to\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}{\mathcal{I}^{\ell}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}\to\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}}\to\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}(\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F})}\to 1.

This implies

(2.2)

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}(\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F})}\right)+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}{\mathcal{I}^{\ell}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}\right)=\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}}\right),

which is precisely
s
by definition of
\mathcal{I}_{p}
. Let us consider the two dimensions on the left-hand side above one at a time in the order they appear.

First let
\varphi:\mathcal{I}_{p}\to\textup{Cl}(K)
denote the map sending an ideal to its ideal class. We compose this with the quotient map restricted to the image of
\varphi
:

\mathcal{I}_{p}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{p})\to\frac{\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{p})}{\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{p})^{\ell}}.

An ideal
a\in\mathcal{I}_{p}
belongs to the kernel of the composition above if and only if
a
and
b^{\ell}
belong to the same ideal class for some
b\in\mathcal{I}_{p}
. Writing
a=b^{\ell}(a/b^{\ell})
, we see that the kernel is precisely
\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}(\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F})
. So we get an injection

\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}(\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F})}\hookrightarrow\frac{\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{p})}{\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{p})^{\ell}},

which implies

(2.3)

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}(\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F})}\right)\leq\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{p})\leq\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(F).

Second, if
\alpha\in F
is such that
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{F}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}
then
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
for some nonzero fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset K
; indeed, generators for
\mathcal{I}_{p}
all become
\ell^{\text{th}}
powers in
K
. This is the defining condition for the group
G_{K}
, so
\alpha\in G_{K}
. Since a generator for
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{F}
is defined up to multiplication by a unit, we have an injection

(2.4)

\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}\hookrightarrow\frac{F^{*}\cap G_{K}}{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}}

given by
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{F}\mapsto\alpha\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}
. And because
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}\subset G_{F}
, we have a quotient map

(2.5)

\frac{F^{*}\cap G_{K}}{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}}\to\frac{F^{*}\cap G_{K}}{G_{F}}.

By definition of

(and unique factorization of ideals), the kernel of the composition of (

) and (

) is

injects into

, implying

(2.6)

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}\cap\mathcal{P}_{F}}\right)\leq\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{F^{*}\cap G_{K}}{G_{F}}\right)\!.

Combining (

), (

\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(F)+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{F^{*}\cap G_{K}}{G_{F}}\right)\geq\textup{rk}_{\ell}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{p}}{\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\ell}}\right)=s_{\ell}(K/F).

This, in turn, combines with (

Connell and Sussman demonstrate by example how a well-chosen base field

can significantly increase the value of their lower bound due to the appearance of

in (

). We have removed this term in Lemma

, but it is still important to allow the base field to vary in our result below. This is because

) over one choice of base field but not another. For example, let

be a normal extension with Galois group equal to the symmetric group

. Let

be a copy of the Klein 4-group in

Theorem

.

For an

Let

(in some fixed algebraic closure), let

Consider a pair of prime ideals

,

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\in G_{i}\cap\pi I(\mathfrak{P}\hskip 0.56917pt|\hskip 0.56917ptp)\pi^{-1}\hskip 20.0pt\text{since }\tau=\pi\sigma^{\ell^{n}}\pi^{-1}\text{ and }\sigma\in I(\mathfrak{P}\hskip 0.56917pt|\hskip 0.56917ptp)

\displaystyle=G_{i}\cap I(\pi(\mathfrak{P})\hskip 0.56917pt|\hskip 0.56917ptp)

\displaystyle=I(\pi(\mathfrak{P})\hskip 0.56917pt|\hskip 0.56917pt\pi(\mathfrak{P})\cap F_{i})

Regarding the end of the proof, note that it is possible for a tower of fields

to satisfy

Theorem

For a strongly

Let

, and

Consider a pair of prime ideals

\mathfrak{P}

3.
Class field towers

For Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
of fixed degree
d
, Brumer was the first to observe that a highly composite discriminant
D
is sufficient to guarantee an infinite class field tower
[
Bru65
]
. Indeed, the pigeon-hole principle forces at least
\omega(D)/\omega(d)
primes in
\mathbb{Q}
to have ramification indices that share a nontrivial common factor, some prime
\ell\,|\,d
. Theorem
1.1
then bounds
\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\textup{Cl}(K)
from below, and the Golod–Shafarevich theorem (Theorem
1.11
) completes the proof.

A similar corollary falls out of Theorem
2
. For a number field
F
, let
\omega_{F}
denote the number of distinct prime ideals of its argument in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
K/F
be an extension of number fields of degree
d>1
and relative discriminant
\mathfrak{D}
. If
K/F
is
\ell
-divisible for every prime number
\ell
and

\left\lceil\frac{\omega_{F}(\mathfrak{D})}{\omega_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)}\right\rceil\geq\max_{\ell\,|\,d}\Big(\delta_{\ell}(K/F)\big(2+2\sqrt{\textup{rk}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+1}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}+e_{\ell}([K:F])\big)\Big),

then the
\ell
-class field tower of
K
is infinite for some prime
\ell\,|\,d
.

Proof.

The combination of Theorems
2
and
1.11
tells us that
K
has an infinite
\ell
-class field tower if

(3.1)

\frac{t_{\ell}(K/F)}{\delta_{\ell}(K/F)}\geq 2+2\sqrt{\textup{rk}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}+1}+\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}-\textup{rk}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}+e_{\ell}(d).

So to complete the proof, we need only show that
t_{\ell}(K/F)\geq\lceil\omega_{F}(\mathfrak{D})/\omega_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)\rceil
for some prime
\ell
.

Each of the
\omega_{F}(\mathfrak{D})
prime ideals

dividing

lies below at least one
\mathfrak{p}\subset K
for which

. By Lemma

, any prime divisor of

is one of the

. Thus by the pigeon-hole principal, there exists a prime

such that at least

in

Let us use this to verify (a relative version of) the simpler bound stated as Corollary

in the introduction.

If

Let

Comparing the inequality above to (

From the isomorphism

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix some positive integer
j
, and for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
define

S_{k}(x)=\{s^{j}t\mid s,t\in\mathbb{Z},\;\omega(s)=\omega(st)\leq k,\;|s^{j}t|\leq x\}.

Then

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|S_{k}(x)|\log x}{x^{1/j}(\log\log x)^{k-1}}=\frac{2j}{(k-1)!}.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{k}(x)
count the number of square-free positive integers with exactly
k
distinct prime divisors. In
[
Lan09
]
, Landau proves that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{k}(x)\log x}{x(\log\log x)^{k-1}}=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}.

Now let

T_{k}(x)=\big\{\pm s^{j}\mid s\in\mathbb{Z},\;\omega(s)\leq k,\;|s^{j}|\leq x\big\}.

Observe that
T_{k}(x)
counts the same integers as
\pi_{1}(x)+\cdots+\pi_{k}(x)
(which is dominated by
\pi_{k}(x)
for large
x
), but raised to the power
j
and scaled by
\pm 1
(in case
j
is even). It follows that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|T_{k}(x)|\log x}{x^{1/j}(\log\log x)^{k-1}}=\frac{2j}{(k-1)!}.

So our proof will be complete if we can show that
\lim_{x}|S_{k}(x)|/|T_{k}(x)|=1
. This holds trivially for
k=0
. For
k>0
we induct on
k
to obtain

\displaystyle|S_{k}(x)-T_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>j\\
p<x^{1/i}\end{subarray}}\!\bigg|S_{k-1}\!\bigg(\frac{x}{p^{i}}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}(j,k)\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>j\\
p<x^{1/i}\end{subarray}}\!\frac{x^{1/j}(\log\log x)^{k-2}}{p^{i/j}\log x}\hskip 20.0pt\text{for some }c_{0}(j,k)\text{ by induction}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{0}(j,k)x^{1/j}(\log\log x)^{k-2}}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>j\\
p\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{i/j}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}(j,k)x^{1/j}(\log\log x)^{k-2}}{\log x}\hskip 20.0pt\text{for some }c_{1}(j,k).

The last expression is negligible compared to
|T_{k}(x)|
as
x
grows.∎

For our final theorem, recall Notation
1.13
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
F
be a number field, and let

be groups with

nilpotent. Set

and

(3.5)

(depending only on

) such that for large

(depending only on

Proof.

Recall that

is a direct product of its Sylow subgroups, and

is a direct product of subgroups of the Sylow subgroups of

. In particular, we can fix a normal series

with relative discriminant

We define subsets of

Now let

This section identifies a few types of fields extensions that are

K/F
over
K
). Such extensions do exist, however. The alternating group
A_{5}
has one nontrivial subgroup
H
up to conjugation such that
A_{5}/H
is
\ell
-divisible for all primes
\ell
, namely the subgroup of order

A_{6}

4.1.
Towers of fields

This section, specifically Corollary
4.5
, justifies Corollary
1.5
from the introduction.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H=H_{0}\leq H_{1}\leq\cdots\leq H_{n}=G
be groups. If
H_{i}/H_{i-1}
is
\ell
-divisible for all
i
, then
G/H
is
\ell
-divisible with

\delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}(H_{i}/H_{i-1}).

The analogous claim holds for strong
\ell
-divisibility with

\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}[H_{i-1}:H]\Delta_{\ell}(H_{i}/H_{i-1}).

Proof.

Let us prove the case
n=2
, then the full claim follows by induction. By definition of
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)
(or
\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)
), it suffices to show that the union of two sets of intermediate groups satisfying Definition
1.3
(or Definition
1.8
), one set for
G/H_{1}
and the other for
H_{1}/H
, will satisfy Definition
1.3
(or Definition
1.8
) for
G/H
.

Suppose
\sigma\in G-H
has order
\ell
. On the one hand, if
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\not\subset H_{1}
, then
\ell
-divisibility of
G/H_{1}
provides some
G^{\prime}\leq G
containing
H_{1}
such that
\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(\tau)\cap H_{1}=\emptyset
for some
\tau\in G^{\prime}\cap\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)
. Then
H\leq H_{1}\leq G^{\prime}
and
\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(\tau)\cap H\subseteq\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(\tau)\cap H_{1}=\emptyset
as desired. On the other hand, if
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\subset H_{1}
, then
\ell
-divisibility of
H_{1}/H
provides some
H^{\prime}\leq H_{1}
containing
H
such that
\textup{cl}_{H^{\prime}}(\tau)\cap H=\emptyset
for some
\tau\in H^{\prime}\cap\textup{cl}_{H_{1}}(\sigma)\subset H^{\prime}\cap\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)
.

The same argument works for strong
\ell
-divisibility.∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N\leq H\leq G
with
N\trianglelefteq G
, and let
\mathcal{G}=G/N
and
\mathcal{H}=H/N
. Then
G/H
is
\ell
-divisible if and only if
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is
\ell
-divisible, in which case
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)=\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
. The analogous claim holds for strong
\ell
-divisibility with
\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)=\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

The lattice isomorphism theorem gives a bijective correspondence between subgroups
H\leq G_{1},...,G_{n}\leq G
and subgroups
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}_{1},...,\mathcal{G}_{n}\leq\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{i}=G_{i}/N
. Since
N\leq H
, for any
\sigma\in G
we have
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\subset H
if and only if
\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(\sigma N)\subset\mathcal{H}
, and
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\sigma)\cap H=\emptyset
if and only if
\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}(\sigma N)\cap\mathcal{H}=\emptyset
. These two equivalences show that Definition
1.3
or Definition
1.8
holds for
H\leq G_{1},...,G_{\delta}\leq G
if and only if it holds for
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}_{1},...,\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\leq\mathcal{G}
. Thus
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)=\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
and, since
[G_{i}:H]=[\mathcal{G}_{i}:\mathcal{H}]
,
\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)=\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
.∎

The last lemma shows that
G/H
in Definitions
1.3
and
1.8
could be replaced with
\textup{Gal}(L/F)
, where
L
is
any
normal extension of
F
containing
K
, not necessarily the normal closure of
K/F
. This allows us to rephrase Lemma
4.1
in terms of fields.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
F=F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{n}=K
be number fields. If
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
is
\ell
-divisible for all
i
, then
K/F
is
\ell
-divisible with

\delta_{\ell}(K/F)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}(F_{i}/F_{i-1}).

The analogous claim holds for strong
\ell
-divisibility with

\Delta_{\ell}(K/F)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}[K:F_{i}]\Delta_{\ell}(F_{i}/F_{i-1}).

Proof.

Let
L
denote the Galois closure of
K/F
, and let
L_{i}
denote the Galois closure of
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
. Also let
G/H=\textup{Gal}(K/F)
, and let
H_{i}/1=\textup{Gal}(L/F_{i})
. Finally, let
N_{i}/1=\textup{Gal}(L/L_{i})
so that
N_{i}\trianglelefteq H_{i}
. The Galois group of
L_{i}/F_{i-1}
is isomorphic to
H_{i-1}/N_{i}
, in which the Galois group of
L_{i}/F_{i}
is embedded as
H_{i}/N_{i}
. Our hypothesis is that
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
, and thus by definition
(H_{i-1}/N_{i})/(H_{i}/N_{i})
, is (strongly)
\ell
-divisible. So by Lemma
4.2
,
H_{i-1}/H_{i}
is (strongly)
\ell
-divisible with
\delta_{\ell}(H_{i-1}/H_{i})=\delta_{\ell}(F_{i}/F_{i-1})
and/or
\Delta_{\ell}(H_{i-1}/H_{i})=\Delta_{\ell}(F_{i}/F_{i-1})
. Thus Lemma
4.1
applies, and we conclude that
G/H
is (strongly)
\ell
-divisible with

\delta_{\ell}(K/F)=\delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}(H_{i-1}/H_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}(F_{i}/F_{i-1})

and/or

\Delta_{\ell}(K/F)=\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}[H_{i}:H]\Delta_{\ell}(H_{i-1}/H_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[K:F_{i}]\Delta_{\ell}(F_{i}/F_{i-1}).

(Note that
H_{i}\leq H_{i-1}
unlike the ordering in Lemma
4.1
.)∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose

is a Galois extension of number fields, and let
\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}
be generators for the distinct subgroups of order

in

. If

\delta_{\ell}(K/F)=1

\Delta_{\ell}(K/F)\leq\min(n,\frac{1}{\ell}|\langle\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}\rangle|)

Proof.

In this scenario,

in Definitions

and
1.8
is the trivial group. Thus the subgroup

shows that
\delta_{\ell}(K/F)=1
and
\Delta_{\ell}(K/F)\leq\frac{1}{\ell}|\langle\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}\rangle|
. The subgroups
\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle,...,\langle\sigma_{n}\rangle

.∎

Combining the result above with Theorem

immediately proves the following.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
F=F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{n}=K

is strongly

4.2.
Nilpotent extensions

As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
, if
G
has a subnormal series containing
H
, then
G/H
is strongly
\ell
-divisible. In this section we ask when a subnormal series that may not contain

guarantees

-divisibility or strong

-divisibility. The dihedral groups

with

show that

proves, nilpotence is sufficient.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
H\leq G
be groups. If
G/H
is
\ell
-divisible and
\ell\nmid[G\!:\!H]
, then
H
contains all Sylow
\ell
-subgroups of
G
.

Proof.

We will use induction on
n
to prove that if
\sigma\in G
has order
\ell^{n}
for some
n
, then
\sigma\in H
. Consider the case
n=1
. (We begin here rather than
j=0
because this case is used to prove the induction step.) Suppose
\sigma\in G
has order
\ell
, and assume for the sake of contradiction that
\sigma\not\in H
. By
\ell
-divisibility, there exists
G^{\prime}\leq G
containing
H
such that
\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(\tau)\cap H=\emptyset
for some
\tau\in\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\cap G^{\prime}
. Since
\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(\tau)
intersects every Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G^{\prime}
nontrivially while being disjoint from
H
, a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
H
cannot also be a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G_{1}
. In other words,
\ell\;|\;[G^{\prime}\!:\!H]
, which contradicts
\ell\nmid[G\!:\!H]
.

For the induction step, fix some
n\geq 1
, and assume
\sigma\in H
for all
\sigma\in G
with
|\sigma|=\ell^{n}
. In particular, the subgroup generated by a such
\sigma
is contained in
H
. Call this subgroup

and observe that
N\trianglelefteq G
. Let
\mathcal{G}=G/N
and
\mathcal{H}=H/N
. Consider some
\sigma\in G
with
|\sigma|=\ell^{n+1}
. If
\sigma\in N\leq H
we are done. Otherwise, since
\sigma^{\ell}\in N
, we see that
\sigma N
has order
\ell
in
\mathcal{G}
. Note that
[\mathcal{G}\!:\!\mathcal{H}]=[G\!:\!H]
, which is not divisible by
\ell
. Furthermore, Lemma
4.2
tells us

is
\ell
-divisible. Thus the case
n=1
from the previous paragraph applies to
\sigma N\in\mathcal{G}
, the conclusion being
\sigma N\in\mathcal{H}
. This implies
\sigma\in NH=H
.∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
H,N\leq G
with
N
normal, and let
\mathcal{G}=G/N
and
\mathcal{H}=HN/N
. If
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
and
HN/H
are
\ell
-divisible, then so is
G/H
with
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})+\delta_{\ell}(HN/H)
. The analogous claim holds for strong divisibility with
\Delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\Delta_{\ell}(HN/H)+[HN:H]\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
,
\delta_{\ell}(G/HN)=\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
and
\Delta_{\ell}(G/HN)=\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
. The claim then follows by applying Lemma
4.1
to
H\leq HN\leq G
.∎

Note that if
N
normalizes

then strong

-divisibility of

is automatic. As a special case of this, we see that (strong)
\ell
-divisibility is preserved by central extensions, meaning if

is in the center of

, (strong)
\ell
-divisibility of
G/H
is implied by that of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
. Hence the next theorem, which justifies Corollary

from the introduction.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
G
be a group and
\ell
a prime. The following are equivalent:

(a)

G/H
is strongly
\ell
-divisible for all subgroups
H\leq G
,

(b)

G/H
is
\ell
-divisible for all subgroups
H\leq G
,

(c)

G/S
is strongly
\ell
-divisible for some Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
S\leq G
,

(d)

G/S
is
\ell
-divisible for some Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
S\leq G
,

(e)

G
has a unique Sylow
\ell
-subgroup.

If these conditions hold and
1=S_{0}\trianglelefteq S_{1}\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq S_{n}=S
is a central series for the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
S\leq G
, then
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)
is at most the number of proper containments in the chain
HS_{0}\leq\cdots\leq HS_{n}
.

Proof.

Among (a)–(d), all are evidently implied by (a), and all evidently imply (d). Furthermore, that (d) implies (e) is a direct application of Lemma
4.6
. So to prove that (a)–(e) are equivalent, it suffices to show that (e) implies (a).

Assume (e) holds. Let
S
be the unique Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G
, and let
1=S_{0}\trianglelefteq S_{1}\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq S_{n}=S
be a central series for
S
. Letting
\mathcal{G}_{i}=G/S_{i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{i}=HS_{i}/S_{i}
(note that each
S_{i}
is a characteristic subgroup of
G
and is therefore normal), we claim that
\mathcal{G}_{i}/\mathcal{H}_{i}
is strongly
\ell
-divisible for all
i
, and that

(4.1)

\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{i}/\mathcal{H}_{i})\leq\begin{cases}\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{i+1}/\mathcal{H}_{i+1})&HS_{i}=HS_{i+1}\\
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{i+1}/\mathcal{H}_{i+1})+1&HS_{i}\neq HS_{i+1}.\end{cases}

Once proved, these claims imply the entire theorem.

We use induction in reverse order on
i
. The base case is
i=n
. We have
\mathcal{G}_{n}=G/S
, which has no elements of order
\ell
. This makes strong
\ell
-divisibility automatic with
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{n}/\mathcal{H}_{n})=0
. For the induction step, assume
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}/\mathcal{H}_{i+1}
is strongly
\ell
-divisible. Set
\mathcal{N}=S_{i+1}/S_{i}
so that the canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}\to\mathcal{G}_{i}/\mathcal{N}
maps
\mathcal{H}_{i+1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{N}
. By definition of a central series,
\mathcal{N}
lies in the center of
\mathcal{G}_{i}
. In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is normal in
\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}
, so either
\delta(\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{H}_{i})=0
if
\ell\nmid[\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{H}_{i}]
or
\delta(\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{H}_{i})=1
if
\ell\,|\,[\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{H}_{i}]
(the latter being the group-theoretic phrasing of Proposition
4.4
). Therefore, by Lemma
4.7
and the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{G}_{i}/\mathcal{H}_{i}
is strongly
\ell
-divisible with

(4.2)

\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{i}/\mathcal{H}_{i})\leq\begin{cases}\delta(\mathcal{G}_{i+1}/\mathcal{H}_{i+1})&\ell\nmid[\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}\!:\!\mathcal{H}_{i}]\\
\delta(\mathcal{G}_{i+1}/\mathcal{H}_{i+1})+1&\ell\;|\;[\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}\!:\!\mathcal{H}_{i}].\end{cases}.

Since
[\mathcal{H}_{i}\mathcal{N}\!:\!\mathcal{H}_{i}]=[HS_{i+1}\!:\!HS_{i}]
(which divides
[S_{i+1}\!:\!S_{i}]
and is therefore a power of
\ell
), we see that (
4.1
) and (

) agree.∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
G/H=\textup{Gal}(K/F)
. If
G
is nilpotent, then
K/F
is strongly
\ell
-divisible for every prime
\ell
with
\delta_{\ell}(K/F)
at most the nilpotency class of
G
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
4.8
and the fact that a group
G
is nilpotent if and only if each of its Sylow subgroups is unique, in which case the nilpotency class of each Sylow subgroup is at most that of the group.∎

4.3.
Semidirect products

Let
D_{n}=\langle r,s\mid r^{n},s^{2},(rs)^{2}\rangle
, the dihedral group of order
2n
. The introduction claims that
D_{n}/\langle s\rangle
(or, equivalently,
D_{n}/\langle r^{i}s\rangle
for any
i
) is 2-divisible if and only if
4\,|\,n
. The theorem below identifies what separates these two cases: the automorphism of
\langle r\rangle
defined by
r^{i}\mapsto r^{-i}
restricts to the identity map on the Sylow 2-subgroup of
\langle r\rangle
precisely when
4\nmid n
.

For a nilpotent group
G
, we use
\textup{nil}(G)
to denote the nilpotency class of
G
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
for some
\varphi:H\to\textup{Aut}(N)
. If
\varphi(H)
and
N
have unique Sylow
\ell
-subgroups

and

such that the restriction map
\varphi(h)\mapsto\varphi(h)|_{T}
is injective on

, then
G/H
is
\ell
-divisible with
\delta_{\ell}(G/H)\leq\textup{nil}(S)\textup{nil}(T)+1
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we do not distinguish
H
and
N
from their canonical copies in
G
.

Let
n_{S}=\textup{nil}(S)
,
n_{T}=\textup{nil}(T)
, and
n=n_{S}n_{T}
. We will prove by induction on both
n_{S}
and
n_{T}
that there exist subgroups
G_{1},...,G_{n_{S}n_{T}}\leq G
containing
H
such that for any
\sigma\in H
of order
\ell
satisfying
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\not\subset H
, there exists some
G_{i}
and
\tau\in\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\cap G_{i}
with
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}\cap H=\emptyset
. Once proved, this implies the theorem: the only remaining
\sigma\in G-H
of order
\ell
are those (that may not exist) for which
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\cap H=\emptyset
, so at most one additional subgroup is needed to satisfy Definition
1.3
, namely
G
.

We begin by observing that no generality is lost in assuming
N=T
. Indeed, if
\sigma\in H
has order
\ell
, then
\varphi(\sigma)\in S
. So if
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\not\subset H
, which is equivalent to
\varphi(\sigma)\neq\text{Id}_{N}
, then by hypothesis
\varphi(\sigma)|_{T}\neq\text{Id}_{T}
, which is equivalent to
\textup{cl}_{T}(\sigma)\not\subset H
. But then if we find a subgroup
G_{i}
of
T\rtimes_{\varphi}H
for which
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\tau)\cap H=\emptyset
for some
\tau\in G_{i}
that is
T\rtimes_{\varphi}H
-conjugate to
\sigma
, we have achieved the same thing in the potentially larger group
N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
by taking the same

and
\tau
. As such, we henceforth assume
G=T\rtimes_{\varphi}H
. This has the affect of making the injectivity hypothesis in the theorem statement vacuous for
G
and its subgroups and quotients that are to be used in the induction proof.

The base case is
n_{S}=0
or
n_{T}=0
, meaning
S
or
T
is the trivial group. Then there are no elements
\sigma\in H
of order
\ell
for which
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\not\subset H
, so we need
0=n_{S}n_{T}
subgroups, as claimed.

Now suppose
n_{S},n_{T}\geq 1
, and consider some
\sigma\in H
of order
\ell
such that
\varphi(\sigma)
does not descend to the identity on
T/Z(T)
, where

is the center of
T
. This is equivalent to
\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(\sigma)\not\subset H
, where
\mathcal{G}=T/Z(T)\rtimes_{\varphi}H
. By the induction hypothesis, at least one from among
\textup{nil}(S)\textup{nil}(T/Z(T))=n_{S}(n_{T}-1)
select subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
containing
H
, call it
\mathcal{G}_{i}
, must satisfy
\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}(\tau Z(T))\cap H=\emptyset
for some
\tau Z(T)\in\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(\sigma Z(T))\cap\mathcal{G}_{i}
. Assume without loss of generality that
\tau\in\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)
(if not, pick a different coset representative of
\tau Z(T)
), and pull back
\mathcal{G}_{i}
via the quotient map
G\to\mathcal{G}
. This gives the desired

containing
H
and
\tau
with
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\tau)\cap H=\emptyset
.

The previous paragraph produced
n_{S}(n_{T}-1)
subgroups of
G
out of our allotment of
n_{S}n_{T}
. So we must now show that at most
n_{S}
subgroups of
G
are needed for those
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)
for which
\varphi(\sigma)
descends to the identity on
T/Z(T)
. Let

, let
R[\varphi(H)]
denote the group ring written with additive notation, and let

denote the kernel of the projection map
R[\varphi(H)]\to R[\varphi(H)/Z(S)]

as a left

\sum_{\sigma\in H}a_{\sigma}\varphi(\sigma)\cdot\tau=\prod_{\sigma\in H}\varphi(\sigma)(\tau^{a_{\sigma}}).

Finally, let
\textup{Ann}(I)
be the

-submodule of

consisting of elements annihilated by

.

We consider two cases. First suppose
\sigma\in H
(of order
\ell
with
\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\not\subset H
) is such that
\varphi(\sigma)
does not restrict to the identity on
\textup{Ann}(I)
. By definition of

,
\textup{Ann}(I)
is a left

-module. Thus we have a group
\mathcal{G}=\textup{Ann}(I)\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}=H/\varphi^{-1}(Z(S))
. The assumption that
\varphi(\sigma)
is not the identity on
\textup{Ann}(I)
means
\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(\sigma\varphi^{-1}(Z(S)))\not\subset\mathcal{H}
. So as before, we pull back the
\textup{nil}(S/Z(S))\textup{nil}(\textup{Ann}(I))=n_{S}-1
subgroups of

containing
\mathcal{H}
provided by the induction hypothesis. At least one of these, say

, is such that
\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\tau)\cap H=\emptyset
for some
\tau\in\textup{cl}_{G}(\sigma)\cap G_{i}
.

We have thus far produced
n_{S}n_{T}-1
subgroups of
G
out of our allotment of
n_{S}n_{T}
. So we must show that at most one subgroup of

is needed for the second case: when

be any element of the form
\pi\sigma\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle(\pi^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})\tau(\pi^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\pi^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime})\Big(\pi\,\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{-1})\,\sigma\Big)\Big(\varphi(\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime-1})\,\sigma^{\prime-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\pi^{\prime}\,\varphi(\sigma^{\prime})(\pi\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{-1}))\,\varphi(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime-1})\,\sigma^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime-1}.

\pi^{\prime}\,\varphi(\sigma^{\prime})(\pi\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{-1}))\,\varphi(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime-1})=1

\pi\,\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{-1})=\varphi(\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime-1}\varphi(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\varphi(\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime-1}\varphi(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime-1})\,\varphi(\sigma)(\varphi(\sigma^{\prime-1})(\pi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=(1-\varphi(\sigma))\cdot\varphi(\sigma^{-1})(\pi^{\prime-1})

It remains only to prove that there exists
\pi\in T

. We are assuming
\varphi(\sigma)
descends to the identity on

, so
\pi^{\prime}\,\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{\prime-1})\in Z(T)
. Since

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varphi(\sigma_{i}))

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varphi(\sigma_{i}))\cdot\pi^{\prime}\,\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{\prime-1})\neq 1.

I\cdot\pi\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{-1})=I\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varphi(\sigma_{i}))\cdot\pi^{\prime}\varphi(\pi^{\prime-1})\subseteq I^{k+1}\cdot\pi^{\prime}\,\varphi(\sigma)(\pi^{\prime-1})=1.

Thus by (

) and (

),

as desired.∎

Observe that if

and

in the introduction.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
D_{n}=\langle r,s\,|\,r^{n},s^{2},(rs)^{2}\rangle
. Then
D_{n}/\langle s\rangle
is 2-divisible if and only if

, in which case
\delta_{2}(D_{n}/\langle s\rangle)=2
.

Proof.

If
4\,|\,n
then
s
acts (by conjugation) nontrivially on
r^{n/4}
, so the hypothesis of Theorem
4.10
is satisfied. For the converse, if
G^{\prime}\subseteq D_{n}
contains both
s
and some conjugate of
s
, say
r^{2i}s
with

, then
\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(r^{2i}s)
contains all elements of the form

. But if

we can choose

so that

, which shows that
\textup{cl}_{G^{\prime}}(r^{2i}s)\cap H\neq\emptyset
.∎

4.4.
Composite fields

It is false in general that if
K_{1}/F_{1}
and
K_{2}/F_{2}
are
\ell
-divisible then
K_{1}K_{2}/F_{1}F_{2}
is
\ell
-divisible. For example, let
L/F_{1}
be Galois with
\textup{Gal}(L/F_{1})\simeq D_{12}=\langle r,\,s\mid r^{1}2,\,s^{2},\,(rs)^{2}\rangle
, the dihedral group of order 24. Let
K_{1}
be the fixed field of
\langle s\rangle
, and let
F_{2}=K_{2}
be the fixed field of
\langle r^{2}\rangle
. Then
K_{1}/F_{1}
is 2-divisible by Corollary
4.11
, and

is trivially 2-divisible. But the Galois group of the closure of

is
\langle r^{2},\,s\mid r^{1}2,\,s^{2},\,(rs)^{2}\rangle\simeq D_{6}
, and

is the fixed field of

. Since

,

is not 2-divisible by Corollary

.

There are two possible fixes: insisting that our field extensions share a common base field, or insisting on strong
\ell
-divisibility. These are the two results of this section.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let

be number fields. If

is

-divisible for all

is

\delta_{\ell}(K_{1}\cdots K_{n}/F)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\ell}(K_{i}/F).

Let
G_{i}/H_{i}=\textup{Gal}(K_{i}/F)
, let

, and let
\varphi_{i}:G\to G_{i}
denote the restriction to

. For each

, let
\delta_{i}=\delta(K_{i}/F)

that satisfy Definition
1.3
. We claim that the subgroups
\varphi^{-1}_{i}(G_{i,j})
satisfy Definition
1.3
for

.

Let
\sigma\in G-H
have order
\ell
. Since

does not fix

, it must not fix
K_{i}
for some

. Thus
\varphi_{i}(\sigma)
is an element of order

. By assumption, there is some

and
\tau^{\prime}\in\textup{cl}_{G_{i}}(\pi_{i}(\sigma))\cap G_{i,j}
such that

\tau\in G
be any

that satisfies

(which exists by surjectivity of

). Note that
\tau\in\varphi^{-1}_{i}(G_{i,j})

of any

. Thus no

.∎

The lemma below fails if strong

\ell

Lemma 4.13

Let

Let

and

Theorem 4.14
.

For

, let

, let

, let

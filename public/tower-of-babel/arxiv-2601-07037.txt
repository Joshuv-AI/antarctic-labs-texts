Subspace Selected Variational Quantum Configuration Interaction with a Partial Walsh Series
Koray Aydoğan, Anna R. Spak, Kade Head-Marsden, Anthony W. Schlimgen

Abstract
Estimating the ground-state energy of a quantum system is one of the most promising applications for quantum algorithms. Here we propose a variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) Ansatz for finding ground state configuration interaction (CI) wavefunctions. We map CI for fermions to a quantum circuit using a subspace superposition, then apply diagonal Walsh operators to encode the wavefunction. The algorithm can be used to solve both full CI and selected CI wavefunctions, resuling in exact and near-exact solutions for electronic ground states. Both the subspace selection and wavefunction Ansatz can be applied to any Hamiltonian that can be written in a qubit basis. The algorithm bypasses costly classical matrix diagonalizations, which is advantageous for large-scale applications. We demonstrate results for several molecules using quantum simulators and hardware.

A molecular electronic Hamiltonian under the Born-Oppenheimer approximation can be expressed as

The algorithm has two main steps: superposition preparation, and

For preparing a superposition, the difference between a structured or unstructured subspace is important. We prepare the space of number-conserving SDs, known as the Dicke space

where

Conclusions and Discussion.–
Here, we present a variational quantum algorithm to estimate the ground-state energies of molecular Hamiltonians. This algorithm has two key steps, the first is preparing a state of physically-relevant bitstrings and the second is transforming the amplitudes of each SD with the truncated set of Walsh operators. After selecting the
D
desired CI coefficients, we increase the parameter space to generate a full-rank transformation. A full-rank transformation can be generated by including
D\textrm{log}D
randomly selected Walsh functions for the partial WFT
69
. The wavefunction
Ansatz
can be prepared with
\mathcal{O}(D\textrm{log}D)
CNOT gates, and a
\mathcal{O}(D^{2}\textrm{log}^{2}D)
classical operation. Furthermore, the
Ansatz
does not form a 2-design because it is diagonal and commutative, which mitigates the emergence of barren plateaus
41
;
53
;
52
.

The subspace preparation step of the algorithm is very flexible. The only requirement is that a uniform superposition is created between the bitstrings of the subspace of interest, while all other amplitudes are zero. Through subspace selection, one can impose other symmetries such as parity or translational symmetry in condensed matter models
5
;
8
. Effective application of symmetries remains an important tool in classical many-body methods, and our subspace selection protocol can act to symmetry-adapt wavefunctions in quantum algorithms
24
;
35
;
61
;
46
.

We discussed two ways to map CI coefficients to Walsh coefficients. We can preserve surjectivity through using a QR decomposition to find a full rank transformation, which has a maximum classical cost scaling
\mathcal{O}(D^{3})

58
;
17
;
11
;
16
. The scaling of the QR decomposition is similar to diagonalization, so the classical cost of our algorithm using QR is similar to QSCI. QSCI diagonalizes a subspace CI Hamiltonian but is not iterative, while our algorithm, like all VQEs, is sensitive to the details of the iterative optimization
22
;
55
;
68
. For large-scale simulations, we can avoid the QR decomposition by relaxing the surjectivity requirement, and oversampling the Hilbert space by
\mathcal{O}(D\textrm{log}D)
, at a log-linear increase in the number of CNOT gates and parameters. This approach provides a scalable VQE strategy for both weakly- and strongly-correlated ground states.

SCI wavefunctions can also be constructed by alternative means, for example, by heat-bath or stochastic CI, or other heuristics
29
;
77
;
56
. Another possibility is preparing a high-quality guess wavefunction with the Walsh
Ansatz
, followed by application qubit or fermionic cluster operators
41
;
67
;
74
;
6
;
54
, resulting in quantum formulations of multireference coupled cluster and perturbation theories
13
;
7
. Taken together, our combined subspace preparation and Walsh function sampling approach provides a scalable and systematically improvable approach to quantum simulation of many-body ground states.

An intelligent lung cancer detection from computed tomography images using robust optimal deep transfer learning with XAI model
Authors: B Prabha, M Poonkodi, M Premalatha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLung cancer remains as one such leading cause of cancer related mortality around the world, demanding precise and early detection through intelligent and effective diagnostic systems. This model presents a Modified Shark Smell Optimization (MSSO) aided Deep Transfer Learning framework for explainable lung cancer detection using Computed Tomography (CT) images. The workflow begins with preprocessing input image carried by segmentation using DeepLabV3 + model to, precisely isolate lung regions and thus eradicate background noise. The features segmented are further processed using Enriched DNN model for enhancing the robustness of feature and to reduce redundancy. Subsequently, the MSSO approach is employed for attaining most relevant and discriminative feature subset for classification. An optimized feature subset s then classified with the use of Deep Transfer Learning-based Inception ResNetV2 model, which enhances recognition accuracy and ensures fast convergence. For enhancing transparency of model and clinical trust, Explainable AI (XAI) is incorporated using GradCAM + + for visualizing critical lung regions thus contributing to decision making process. An experimental outcome on LIDC-IDRI dataset demonstrates that proposed hybrid MSSO-InceptionResNetV2 scheme attains superior accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, ROC, and precision with reduced FPR and computational time on comparing traditional models considered. Hence, the proposed scheme offers reliable and interpretable solutions for automated diagnosis of lung cancer, offering significant potential for clinical integration, early intervention, and enhanced patient prognosis.Keywords: Lung cancer, Automated detection, DeepLabV3 + segmentation, Enriched DNN feature extraction, GradCAM++, Deep transfer learning-based inception ResNetV2 model

Introduction

Lung cancer is one such leading cancer-related cause for death globally, primarily due to delayed diagnosis and limited accuracy in an early detection stage. According to World Health Organization (WHO), high mortality rate is because of late diagnosis primarily, when the disease has progressed already to more advanced stages [1]. Early diagnosis plays a significant part in enhancing survival rates and outcomes. Recently, medical imaging modalities like computed tomography (CT) and Positron Emission Tomography (PET) becomes essential diagnostic tools to detect and monitor lung cancer. Moreover, manual CT image interpretation by radiologists is labor-intensive, prone to inter-observer variability, and time-consuming mostly leading to delayed detection or misdiagnosis [2].

Furthermore, various challenges persist in attaining optimal diagnostic accuracy like noise presence, overlapping tissue structure, and high dimensionality of medical image features [3, 4]. For addressing these issues, CAD systems have emerged as effective approach for assisting clinicians to detect cancer regions automatically [5–8]. Existing CAD system mostly relies on handcrafted features and classical machine learning model that struggle with complex automatic variations and subtle textual differences in lung tissues. Recently, the advent of deep-learning based medical image analysis has been emerged as a promising solution for an automated and precise detection of cancer using Computed Tomography (CT) scans. Deep learning enables automatic hierarchical extraction of feature, decreasing dependency on manual feature engineering [9]. Moreover, various limitations persist like poor interpretability, higher computational cost, and redundant representation of feature, and overfitting due to limited annotated medical datasets.

In diagnosis of lung cancer, segmentation accuracy becomes crucial, as inaccurate delineation of lung region could affect performance of classification effectively. More advanced schemes like DeepLabv3 + have shown a promise in semantic segmentation on combining atrous spatial pyramid pooling and encoder-decoder structures for preserving fine details. After segmentation, effective extraction of feature and selection are needed for reducing redundancy and enhancing model generalization. Metaheuristic optimization approach has proven effective for feature selection, among which Shark smell optimization (SSO) mimics the prey-tracking behavior of sharks [10–13]. Nonetheless, traditional SSO suffers from slow convergence and premature stagnation. Therefore, modified shark smell optimization (MSSO) model is proposed for enhancing global search ability and attain optimal selection of feature subset.

For the purpose of classification, Deep transfer learning (DTL) models like InceptionResNetV2 leverage pre-trained deep architectures for improving accuracy, reduced training time, and addresses scarcity of data issues that are common in medical imaging [14–16]. Further, for ensuring clinical transparency and trust, XAI models like Grad CAM + + will be integrated for visualizing discriminative regions that influence model decision. These visual explanations aid radiologists in validating and understanding prediction of model bridges gap among AI automation and clinical interpretability.

Problem statement/motivation

Despite significant growth in deep learning, existing CAD framework for lung cancer detection mostly suffer from limitations like inaccurate segmentation, redundant feature extraction, lack of interpretability, and limited optimization efficiency [17]. Existing feature selection scheme fails in handling higher-dimensional data efficiently, leading to decreased model generalization and accuracy. However, most deep learning classifiers act as “black boxes”, offering no insights to decision making model. Hence, there is a need for optimized, interpretable, and computationally effective deep learning framework competent of identifying and classifying lung cancer regions from CT images significantly.

Related works

Several deep learning dependent models have been explored for automated lung cancer detection with the use of CT images, focusing on segmentation, feature extraction, and classification. Moreover, traditional models often face some challenges like redundant features, poor generalization, and limited interpretability, thus motivating the need for optimized and explainable hybrid model.

Radiomic models demonstrates potential for an accurate diagnosis and prognostications was suggested in [18]. A delineating practice of tumor region of interest termed segmentation which was a key bottleneck in the development generalized model of classification. A failure report was reported as 4.35% on using iMRRN for segmenting lesions of tumor in CT images of lung cancer CT dataset.

A fully automatic pulmonary nodule segmentation scheme was suggested in [19] to implement at the final diagnosis stage (after disease stage recognition) and included to classification framework as immense practical assistance for radiologists and medical personnel. To the best of knowledge, this model employs CAD model with lung cancer classification, nodule segmentation, and malignancy stage recognition with the use of traditional ML approaches.

A hybrid metaheuristic and CNN model was presented in the work [20]. An architecture of CNN was computed by model vector solution. A resultant solution vector was then passed to Ebola optimization search approach (EOSA) for selecting better combination of weights and bias for training CNN model so as to handle classification issue. After training EOSA-CNN hybrid model thoroughly, optimal configuration was attained yielding better performance.

Another study was conducted in the work [21] investigating comprehensive into deep learning model for automated detection of lung cancer with the use of CT and chest x-ray data. This examines several neural network architectures, preprocessing models, and training models for enhancing both diagnostic accuracy and computational efficiency. This in turn contributes to the advancement of deep learning dependent medical image analysis for the detection of lung cancer.

The study was presented in [22] focusing on classifying and detecting pulmonary nodules (malignant and benign) from CT scans using automated deep learning systems. This in turn adopts various existing detection models like faster R-CNN, YOLOv3, and SSD estimating on LIDC-IDRI dataset. The outcome results in notable decrease in false positive rate (FPR) and respective enhancement in the overall detection accuracy.

In the work [1], a hybrid metaheuristic model termed Spotted Hyena Optimization integrating with Seagull algorithm was presented for effectual selection of feature. The scheme selects optimal subsets with highly relevant features from biomedical dataset like LIDC-IDRI and chest x-rays. The quality of data was improved using bicubic interpolation for removal of noise and DCGAN-based augmentation of data. A refined features were then classified using a hybrid CNN-LSTM model, attaining precise classification among normal and abnormal lung patterns.

A novel scheme was presented in [23] for early and precise diagnosis termed cancer cell detection with the use of hybrid neural network (CCDC HNN). The features were extracted from CT scan image using deep neural networks. The feature extraction accuracy is significant for detecting cancerous cells in early stages for saving patient from fatal disease. In this approach, advanced 3D-CNN was utilized for improving diagnosis accuracy.

A deep learning-based scheme was suggested in work [24] which was the fastest growing medical imaging topics, with rapidly emerging applications spanning medical image dependent and textural based modalities. Using deep learning based medical imaging tools, clinicians could detect and classifying lung nodules more quickly and accurately. This work presents recent development of deep learning aided imaging scheme for early detection of lung cancer.

The work suggested in [25] intends at increasing entire prediction accuracy together with reduced processing time with the use of multispacer image in the pooling layer of convolution neural network. The suggested scheme has autoencoder model for improving overall accuracy, for predicting lung cancer using multispace image in pooling layer and Adam approach for optimization. The suggested solution offers higher classification accuracy together with less processing time on comparing existing models.

A study was implemented in [26] for identifying early-stage lung cancer through intelligent deep learning-based classifier. The research analyzed multiple imaging modalities and identified that CT scan to be superior due to low noise levels. The features extracted from segmented CT images were classified using advanced learning model, yielding enhanced accuracy in early cancer detection and categorization.

Research gap identified

Though, several deep learning and optimization-based framework was proposed for lung cancer detection, most traditional scheme face limitations like inaccurate segmentation, redundant or irrelevant extraction of feature, higher computational cost, and model interpretability lack. Several studies focus on classification without integrating effective optimization or explainable AI scheme. Hence, there is a need for unified, optimized, and explainable deep learning model which enhances segmentation accuracy, improved feature selection, and offers transparent decision support for reliable diagnosis of lung cancer.

Entire working flow of proposed model

Preprocessing of input image data

The input image data is acquired and preprocessing is carried. Preprocessing plays a crucial part in enhancing CT image quality thereby ensuring reliable performance of proposed lung cancer detection model. A raw CT scan mostly comprises of noise, irrelevant background structures, and variation in intensity which can impact segmentation and feature extraction accuracy in a negative manner. Hence, a series of preprocessing functions are carried before model training. Initially, image resizing and normalization is employed for maintaining uniform dimension and intensity ranges over entire samples. This model ensures consistent input for deep learning scheme. Subsequently, noise reduction is attained with the use of filtering model like median or Gaussian filtering that aids in preserving significant structural details on suppressing random variations. A contrast enhancement model like contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) is employed for highlighting subtle tumor regions and enhance nodules visibility.

For enhancing segmentation accuracy, lung region extraction is carried for removing non-lung areas such as ribs and background tissues, isolating ROI for efficient feature learning. At last, data augmentation model like rotating, flipping, translation, and scaling is employed for increasing the diversity of data and preventing overfitting issue during training. This preprocessing function enhances quality of data collectively, thus reducing computational complexity and improving robustness of proposed MSSO aided deep learning model.

Performance analysis

This section discusses the experimental outcomes attained from proposed scheme, including performance evaluation, comparative analysis, and visualization outcomes. The findings confirm that significant enhancement in accuracy, robustness, and interpretability.

Dataset description

The proposed model performance is assessed with the use of Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC-IDRI) dataset [27]. The repository LIDC-IDRI is one such huge publicly available lung CT imaging dataset, comprising 1018 thoracic CT scans accompanied with detailed radiologist annotations stored in XML format. Totally, dataset consists of 244,527 slices that are collected from 1010 patients. Each lung nodule in dataset is categorized as five rating classes depending on malignancy level. The nodules labeled as classes 1 and 2 are considered as benign, while those assigned to class 4 and 5 signify malignant classes. Class 3 nodules are considered as non-nodule or indeterminate.

Conclusion

This study presents a robust and intelligent lung cancer detection model, which integrates semantic segmentation, enriched feature learning, optimized feature selection, deep transfer learning-based classification, and explainable visualization. The adoption of DeepLabV3 + network ensures accurate segmentation of lung regions, efficiently suppressing irrelevant structures and noise. The segmented features are refined further using Enriched DNN to enhance feature discrimination and minimizing redundancy. The feature relevance is optimized to improve classifier performance using MSSO resulting in faster convergence. The final diagnosis was carried by Deep Transfer learning based InceptionResNetV2 scheme which offers high accuracy of classification with reduced computational complexity. Also, XAI integration using GradCAM + + enhances the interpretability by highlighting critical lung regions visually contributing to decision, thus enhancing clinical acceptance. Experimental analysis on various metrics like accuracy, precision, specificity, sensitivity, FPR, ROC, and computation time shows that proposed model offers enhanced outcome on comparing existing methodologies. This framework demonstrates a strong potential for real-time clinical deployment, enabling early detection of lung cancer and aiding radiologists in decision making process. In future, this work can be extended by integrating multi-modal medical data, 3D CT imaging, and federated learning for privacy preserving diagnosis.

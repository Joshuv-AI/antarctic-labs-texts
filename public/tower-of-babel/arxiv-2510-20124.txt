Memory-Dependent FPK Equations for Nonlinear SDOF Oscillators Under Fractional Gaussian Noise Excitation
Lifang Feng, Bin Pei, Yong Xu

Abstract
This paper investigates the probabilistic responses of nonlinear single-degree-of-freedom oscillators under fractional Gaussian noise (FGN) excitation. Unlike Gaussian white noise, FGN exhibits persistent correlations and memory effects, making conventional Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov (FPK) equation methods inapplicable. To address this, we develop memory-dependent FPK (memFPK) equations based on fractional Wick-Itô-Skorohod calculus, capable of capturing the joint probability of system responses. A discretized local mean method (DLMM) is proposed to estimate the memory-dependent diffusion coefficient, and a finite difference scheme solves the memFPK equation numerically. Validation through linear and nonlinear examples shows excellent agreement with analytical or Monte Carlo solutions. This framework provides a practical tool for analyzing non-Markovian stochastic dynamics, with potential extensions to multidimensional and parametric FGN problems.

Introduction

Over the past several decades, nonlinear stochastic dynamics has attracted significant research attention due to its widespread applications across engineering and applied sciences. To quantify uncertainty propagation in dynamical systems under random excitations, various analytical and numerical approaches have been developed. However, many real-world excitations like those in materials, biological processes, and climate systems with memory effects, show long-range and temporal correlations. These excitations can be modeled using fractional Gaussian noise (FGN)
[
21
]
, which is a stationary Gaussian process with strong long-range temporal correlation features. FGN has been used in many scientific and engineering fields

. Because FGN is non-Markovian, it creates great challenges for analyzing the probabilistic responses of dynamic systems. Although some progress has been made, it only applies to linear systems such as exact solutions for multidimensional linear systems excited by FGN
[
30
]
, and extensions to nonlinear systems are still limited. For nonlinear systems, Deng and Zhu
[
11
]
used equivalent linearization to study single-degree-of-freedom (SDOF) nonlinear oscillators under FGN excitation. But their method only worked for slightly nonlinear cases and low-order moments. Other related progress includes stochastic averaging methods for quasi-Hamiltonian systems under FGN
[
31
,
32
]
. These methods create approximate stationary PDF by running numerical simulations on averaged stochastic differential equations (SDEs), and even with these efforts, most existing methods are approximate. They focus only on stationary solutions and provide little information about transient behavior. In addition, the classical framework of the FPK equation is based on Markovian assumptions. It cannot be used directly for systems driven by FGN because Itô calculus does not work in non-Markovian situations.

Recent studies have started investigating the framework of the FPK equation for systems under FGN excitation. Some progress has been reported in
[
33
,
34
,
35
]
, but these works often do not consider drift terms. In order to involve drift terms, Pei et al.
[
36
]
have yielded a memory-dependent FPK (memFPK) equation for nonlinear systems driven by combined FGN and Gaussian white noise (GWN) under the commutativity condition. But this still limits how they can be used in real engineering dynamics. To fix this, we (the present authors) have extended this framework to general conditions without requiring commutativity, thereby establishing a novel memFPK equation that governs the transient PDF of one-dimensional nonlinear systems subjected to FGN and GWN
[
37
]
. The only minor drawback is that the drift and diffusion terms in the memFPK equation depend on the past conditions and involve conditional expectations. This creates new challenges for numerical computation. Fortunately, the Volterra adjustable decoupling approximation (VADA) method
[
38
]
can be used to estimate these terms. However, our findings show that VADA may not perform well in some nonlinear cases, as it eliminates important memory effects. Meanwhile, it should be specifically noted that the method in
[
37
]
only applies to the case of one-dimensional nonlinear systems under FGN excitation. It is ineffective for general two-dimensional systems and even SDOF oscillators.

To address the above limitation, this paper proposes a general technique for analyzing the transient probabilistic responses of nonlinear SDOF oscillators under external FGN excitation. This approach consists of two key components. First, a memFPK equation is constructed based on the fractional Wick-Itô-Skorohod (FWIS) integral framework. Second, a discretized local mean method (DLMM) is developed to estimate the memory-dependent diffusion coefficient, which involves conditional expectations. This makes it possible to accurately capture the non-Markovian effects introduced by FGN. The stochastic dynamic responses exhibit fundamentally distinct characteristics depending on the excitation type (GWN vs. FGN). Under external GWN excitation, the system’s FPK equations preserve their time-invariant structure. The governing equations exhibit a complete absence of cross-derivative terms
(\partial^{2}/\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j})
, which mathematically confirms the Markovian property characteristic of systems subject to white noise forcing. In contrast, external FGN-driven systems demonstrate three distinctive characteristics: (i) coupled cross-derivatives
(\partial^{2}/\partial y_{i}y_{j})
appear, (ii) diffusion coefficients in memFPK equation become time dependent, and (iii) nonlinear systems develop state-dependent diffusion properties, resulting in memFPK equation. These differences fundamentally set apart Markovian dynamics (with local interactions and time independence) under GWN from non-Markovian behavior under FGN. The latter brings in temporal correlations, state-modulated noise, and coupled interactions, ultimately affecting how noise spreads, how systems change over time, and how they respond nonlinearly.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 outlines the main results. Section 3 explains the proposed DLMM in detail, which is used to estimate memory-dependent diffusion coefficients. Section 4 provides several numerical examples to test the accuracy and effectiveness of the method. Finally, Section 5 includes the discussions and conclusions. Appendix introduces the mathematical foundation of the memFPK equation, along with how it is derived using the fractional Itô formula and integration by parts.

The memFPK equation for SDOF oscillators excited by FGN

2.1
Linear SDOF oscillators

Consider SDOF linear oscillator excited by FGN, its dynamics is described as follows:

\ddot{X}(t)+c\dot{X}(t)+kX(t)=\sigma\xi^{H}(t),

(2.1)

where
X,\dot{X},\ddot{X}\in\mathbb{R}
represent the displacement, velocity, and acceleration, respectively; and a dot over a variable denotes differentiation with respect to time
t
;
c,k
represent damping and stiffness coefficients, respectively,
\sigma
is the input force influence; the initial conditions
X(0)=x_{0}
and
\dot{X}(0)=\dot{x}_{0}
are random initial displacement and velocity variables, respectively;

\xi^{H}
is a unit FGN excitation process with a power-law autocorrelation function, i.e.

\mathbb{E}[\xi^{H}(t)]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi^{H}(t)\xi^{H}(t+\tau)]=H(2H-1)|\tau|^{2H-2}+2H|\tau|^{2H-1}\delta(\tau),

in which
\delta(\cdot)
is Dirac’s delta function,
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
is the expectation operator, and
\xi^{H}(t)
reduces to be GWN when
H=1/2
.

It is pointed out that when
0<H<1/2
,
\xi^{H}(t)
is not suitable for modeling physical noise since its correlation function is negative. In the present paper, only the case where
1/2<H<1
is considered. For this range of
H
(that is,
1/2<H<1
), the power spectral density (PSD) of

is

\displaystyle S_{H}(\omega)=\frac{H\Gamma(2H)\sin(H\pi)}{\pi}|\omega|^{1-2H},

(2.2)

where

. From (
2.2

has a power-law PSD at all frequencies

Set the state vector
\bm{X}=(X,\dot{X})^{T}=(X,V)^{T}
, (
2.1
) can be expressed in vector form,

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{X}}(t)=A\bm{X}(t)+\Sigma\bm{\xi}^{H}(t),

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-k&-c\end{pmatrix},\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\sigma\end{pmatrix},\bm{\xi}^{\bm{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi^{H}\end{pmatrix}.

Suppose that the initial condition
\bm{X}(0)=\bm{x}_{0}=(x_{0},\dot{x}_{0})^{T}
is a random vector that follows the joint Gaussian distribution
N(\bm{\mu}_{0},\Sigma_{0})
, that is,

\displaystyle p(\bm{x},0)=\frac{1}{2\pi|\Sigma_{0}|^{1/2}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{0})^{T}(\Sigma_{0})^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{0})\Big\},

(2.4)

with
\bm{\mu}_{0}=(\mu_{0},\dot{\mu}_{0}),\Sigma_{0}=\sigma_{0}^{2}\bm{I}_{2\times 2}.

Then, the joint response PDF of
X
and
V
, denoted as
p(x,v,t)
satisfies the following memFPK equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(x,v,t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big\{vp(x,v,t)\big\}+\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\big\{(kx+cv)p(x,v,t)\big\}

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\bigg(H(2H-1)\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}[e^{A(t-s)}]_{21}|t-s|^{2H-2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v\partial x}p(x,v,t)

(2.6)

\displaystyle+\bigg(H(2H-1)\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}[e^{A(t-s)}]_{22}|t-s|^{2H-2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}p(x,v,t),

(2.7)

where
[e^{A(t-s)}]_{ij},i,j=1,2
is the
(i,j)
-th component of the exponential matrix

.

For linear SDOF oscillator subjected to FGN excitation, the system responses remain Gaussian. This allows the joint PDF to be fully characterized by its mean and variance; in other words, an analytical expression for the PDF of the solutions to linear SDOF oscillator under FGN excitation is obtainable.
Denote
\bm{x}=(x,v)
, thus, we have

\displaystyle p(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{2\pi|\Sigma_{\bm{x}}(t)|^{1/2}}\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}}(t))^{T}\Sigma^{-1}_{\bm{x}}(t)(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}}(t))\Bigr\},

(2.8)

where the transient mean vector
\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}}(t)
and covariance matrix
\Sigma_{\bm{x}}(t)
of process
\bm{X}
can be determined by the equation

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{At}\mathbb{E}[\bm{x}_{0}]=e^{At}\bm{\mu}_{0},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{x}}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{At}\Sigma_{0}e^{A^{T}t}+H(2H-1)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-u)}\Sigma\Sigma^{T}e^{A^{T}(t-v)}|u-v|^{2H-2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v.

(2.10)

The structure of the FPK equation for linear SDOF oscillator varies significantly depending on the nature of the stochastic excitation. For instance, consider linear SDOF oscillator excited by external GWN:

\ddot{X}(t)+c\dot{X}(t)+kX(t)=\sigma\xi(t),

(2.11)

where
{\xi}(t)
is a unit GWN.
Then, for the PDF of the two-dimensional stochastic vector process

, denoted as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(x,v,t)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big\{vp(x,v,t)\big\}+\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\big\{(kx+cv)p(x,v,t)\big\}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\big\{\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}p(x,v,t)\big\}.

(2.12)

Remark 1
.

From the FPK equation (
2.12
), it is evident that the diffusion term consists exclusively of second-order partial derivatives with respect to individual state variable. The diffusion coefficient remains constant, and no cross-derivatives (mixed partial derivatives) appear in the equation. In contrast to the external GWN case, when the system is excited by FGN, the associated memFPK equation (
2.5
) exhibits three distinctive features:

(1) The appearance of nonzero cross-derivative terms involving mixed second-order partial derivatives with respect to coupled state variables;

(2) Explicit temporal dependence in the diffusion coefficients;

(3) For nonlinear systems, potential state-dependent characteristics in these diffusion terms; see following (
2.18
) for example.

The governing equations reveal important structural differences that demonstrate how GWN and FGN excitations differ fundamentally in terms of probabilistic system behavior.

2.2
Nonlinear SDOF oscillators

Consider a nonlinear SDOF oscillator subject to FGN whose motion is governed by the following equation:

\ddot{X}(t)+f(X(t),\dot{X}(t))=\sigma\xi^{H}(t),

(2.13)

where
f(X,\dot{X})
denotes the nonlinear functions of

\bm{X}=(X,\dot{X})^{T}=(X,V)^{T}

2.13

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{X}}(t)=\bm{f}(\bm{X}(t))+\Sigma\bm{\xi}^{H}(t).

(2.14)

Then, we can obtain the memFPK equation for the statistical solutions to (
2.13
) as (
2.15

X

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(x,v,t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big\{vp(x,v,t)\big\}+\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\big\{f(x,v)p(x,v,t)\big\}

(2.15)

\displaystyle+\sigma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v\partial x}\big\{\mathbb{E}[D_{t}^{\phi}X(t)|\bm{X}=\bm{x}]p(x,v,t)\big\}

\displaystyle+\sigma\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\big\{\mathbb{E}[D_{t}^{\phi}V(t)|\bm{X}=\bm{x}]p(x,v,t)\big\},

(2.17)

Given that SDOF linear and nonlinear oscillators are special cases of following two-dimensional nonlinear systems:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\dot{Y}_{1}(t)+f_{1}(Y_{1}(t),Y_{2}(t))=\sigma_{1}\xi_{1}^{H_{1}}(t),\cr&\dot{Y}_{2}(t)+f_{2}(Y_{1}(t),Y_{2}(t))=\sigma_{2}\xi_{2}^{H_{2}}(t),\end{cases}

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}

2.3
Mathematical support for the memFPK equation

First, we rewrite (
2.19

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{Y}}(t)=\bm{\hat{f}}(\bm{Y}(t))+\hat{\Sigma}\bm{\xi}^{H}(t),

Owing to the long-memory property of FGN, the response

37

2.20

We only show the Theorem in the main text, with the proofs given in Appendix C. This setup is meant to keep the main text brief and easy to follow, making it simpler for readers to understand the core content quickly.

3
The estimation of the memory-dependent diffusion coefficients

Before numerically solving the memFPK equation, it is necessary to first approximate the memory-dependent diffusion coefficients across the relevant state space, as these coefficients are expressed in terms of conditional expectations that are generally non-explicit functions of time and state. Previously, the authors employed the VADA method
[
38
]
, which constructs an approximation through an appropriate stochastic Volterra-Taylor functional series expansion around a specific transient response moment, to address the equivalent memory-dependent drift and diffusion coefficients in one-dimensional nonlinear systems excited by combined FGN and GWN
[
37
]
. However, in the two-dimensional case involving FGN excitation, which exhibits strong path dependence and nonstationarity, the VADA method becomes inapplicable as it tends to cause a significant loss of historical information.

This study is inspired by the work of Lyu and Chen
[
40
,
41
]
, who proposed an approach to estimate the equivalent drift coefficient using data from representative Monte Carlo simulation (MCS). At each grid point, this estimation is performed via locally weighted scatterplot smoothing (LOWESS). Building on this framework, we propose a numerical estimation method called the discretized local mean method (DLMM), which utilizes state space binning and local statistical averaging, followed by convolution-based smoothing, to construct the equivalent memory-dependent diffusion coefficients.

Specifically, for certain time instant
t_{h},h=1,\cdots,N_{t}
, some representative MCS datas are obtained. For the
q
-th sample, the responses are denoted as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Y_{1}(t_{h})&=y_{1,q}^{(h)},\\
Y_{2}(t_{h})&=y_{2,q}^{(h)},\\
D_{t_{h},k}^{\phi}Y_{l}(t_{n})&=d_{k,l,q}^{(h)},\quad k,l=1,2,\end{cases}

for
q=1,...,N
, where
N
is the number of representative MCS,
d_{k,l,q}^{(h)}
represents the sample value of
D_{t_{h},k}^{\phi}Y_{l}(t_{n})
. Here,
t_{h}
is omitted for convenience below.

Then, the solution domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for responses
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
is uniformly divided into
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
intervals, respectively. Each resulting rectangular bin
B_{ij}
, where
i=0,1,\cdots,N_{1}
and
j=0,1,\cdots,N_{2}
, centered at
(y_{1,i},y_{2,j})
, is associated with a subset of the sample trajectories whose states at a given time
t_{n}
fall into
B_{ij}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}=\{q|(y_{1,q},y_{2,q})\in B_{ij}\}.

(3.1)

The bin-wise approximation of the equivalent memory-dependent diffusion coefficients
b_{kl}^{{\rm(ca)}}
is obtained as the empirical mean over samples in
\mathcal{S}_{ij}

\displaystyle b_{kl,ij}^{{\rm(ca)}}

\displaystyle=b_{kl}^{{\rm(ca)}}(y_{1,i},y_{2,j},t)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\mathbb{E}[D_{k,t}^{\phi}Y_{l}(t)|Y_{1}(t)=y_{1,i},Y_{2}(t)=y_{2,j}]

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{ij}|}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{S}_{ij}}d_{k,l,q},

(3.4)

provided that
|\mathcal{S}_{ij}|
is non-zero, and
b_{kl,ij}^{{\rm(ca)}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=1}^{N}d_{k,l,q}
when
|\mathcal{S}_{ij}|=0
.

To ensure spatial continuity and decrease numerical artifacts caused by local sampling, a two-dimensional smoothing operation is applied to the memory-dependent diffusion coefficients
b_{kl,ij}^{{\rm(ca)}}
. Specifically, a discrete uniform convolution kernel is employed, which is defined as:

K(m,n)=\frac{1}{(2r+1)^{2}},\quad\text{for }m,n\in{-r,\cdots,r}.

(3.5)

The smoothed equivalent memory-dependent diffusion coefficients are then given by

\tilde{b}_{kl,ij}^{{\rm(ca)}}=\sum_{m=-r}^{r}\sum_{n=-r}^{r}K(m,n)\cdot b_{kl,i+m,j+n}^{{\rm(ca)}}.

(3.6)

This smoothing step ensures that
\tilde{b}_{kl,ij}^{{\rm(ca)}}
vary smoothly across the state space, thereby facilitating interpolation and numerical differentiation in later computational stages.

Remark 5
.

Here are several points to note at the end of this section:

1.

To improve computational efficiency, the DLMM can be run on a roughly discretized grid in the state space. After that, it can be interpolated onto finer grids using common interpolation methods such as linear or cubic spline interpolation. This approach allows for accurate and efficient reconstruction of the equivalent memory-dependent diffusion coefficients across the entire domain, while effectively reducing excessive computational costs.

2.

The proposed DLMM has computational efficiency and clear structural interpretability. It avoids the need for pointwise regression by using bin-wise averaging combined with uniform kernel smoothing. This makes it possible to directly use local statistics obtained from a limited set of deterministic trajectories in a fully non-parametric and model-free way.

3.

However, the proposed method currently has certain limitations in its numerical implementation. Specifically, near the domain boundaries, some bins may contain very few or even no samples, which can lead to less accurate estimates in these areas. As a result, the accuracy of the results may be affected in the tails of the response PDF. Possible future improvements could include adding adaptive strategies or deep learning algorithms to enhance the reliability and consistency of estimates when samples are sparse.

5
Discussions and conclusions

This paper presents a comprehensive framework for analyzing the probabilistic responses of two-dimensional nonlinear stochastic dynamical systems subjected to external FGN excitation, with a specific application to SDOF nonlinear oscillators. The proposed methodology consists of three key components: First, leveraging FWIS theory, we derive a novel memFPK equation capable of characterizing the joint PDF evolution for systems with general nonlinearities and arbitrary noise intensities. Second, to enable numerical implementation, we develop a data-driven DLMM for accurate estimation of the memory-dependent diffusion coefficients. Finally, these coefficients are incorporated into the memFPK equation, which is subsequently solved using an efficient FD scheme to obtain complete probabilistic response characteristics.

The framework’s effectiveness is demonstrated through numerical examples encompassing both SDOF linear and nonlinear oscillators as well as two-dimensional systems under FGN excitation. The results indicate that the proposed method achieves high accuracy in capturing transient joint and marginal PDF, while maintaining computational efficiency. This establishes the approach as a valuable tool for investigating complex non-Markovian stochastic dynamics. Current research efforts are focused on extending the memFPK framework to address multi-degree-of-freedom nonlinear oscillators with both external and parametric FGN excitations, promising broader applicability in engineering analysis and design.

is

\displaystyle\nabla\bm{\hat{f}}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial y_{1}}&\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial y_{2}}\\
\dfrac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial y_{1}}&\dfrac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial y_{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Because
\nabla\bm{\hat{f}}
is a continuous matrix-valued function of time
t
, it can be shown that the aforementioned (
B.6
) and (
B.7
) can be interpreted as systems of time-varying linear ordinary differential equations, whose solutions can be expressed in the following form:

\displaystyle D_{1,r}^{\phi}\bm{Y}(t)=H_{1}(2H_{1}-1)\Psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{-1}(s)(\sigma_{1},0)^{T}|r-s|^{2H_{1}-2}\mathrm{d}s,

(B.8)

and

\displaystyle D_{2,r}^{\phi}\bm{Y}(t)=H_{2}(2H_{2}-1)\Psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{-1}(s)(0,\sigma_{2})^{T}|r-s|^{2H_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}s,

(B.9)

where the state-transition matrix
\Psi(t)
is calculated by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Psi(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\bm{\hat{f}}(\bm{Y}(t))\Psi(t),\quad\Psi(0)=I.

The Malliavin derivatives
D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t),i,j=1,2
, are obtained by substituting
t
for
r
in the expressions provided by (
B.8
) and (
B.9
). ∎

Appendix C
Derivation for the memFPK equation (Theorem
2
)

Now, we introduce a SDE framework to study the memFPK equation (Theorem
2
). At first, nonlinear system (
2.20
) can be rewritten as Stratonovich-type SDE

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bm{Y}(t)=\bm{\hat{f}}(\bm{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}t+\hat{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{\circ}\bm{B}^{H}(t),

(C.1)

where
\mathrm{d}^{\circ}
represents the symmetric pathwise integral
[
47
]
, and
\bm{B}^{\bm{H}}(t)=(B_{1}^{H_{1}}(t),B_{2}^{H_{2}}(t))^{T}
is the two-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (FBM) with

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bm{B}^{\bm{H}}(t)/dt=\bm{\xi}^{\bm{H}}(t),\,\mathbb{E}[\bm{B}^{\bm{H}}(t)]=\bm{0},\,\mathrm{and}\;\mathbb{E}[\bm{B}^{\bm{H}}(t)(\bm{B}^{\bm{H}}(t))^{T}]=\text{diag}(t^{2H_{1}},t^{2H_{2}}).

(C.2)

Because we are considering external FGNs case, the Stratonovich-type SDEs (
C.1
) can be equivalent to Itô-type SDEs as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bm{Y}(t)=\bm{\hat{f}}(\bm{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}t+\hat{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}\bm{B}^{H}(t),

(C.3)

where
\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}
represents the FWIS integral
[
45
]
. Next, by introducing the fractional Itô’s formula (see, Lemma
A.1
) stated in Itô-type SDEs (
C.3
) for an arbitrary function
F(\bm{Y}(t))
, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}F(\bm{Y}(t))=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{i}}(\bm{Y}(t))f_{i}(\bm{Y}(t))\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{i}}(\bm{Y}(t))\sigma_{i}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}B_{i}^{H}(t)

(C.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}(\bm{Y}(t))\sigma_{i}D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

(C.5)

where
D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t)
is the Malliavin derivative
[
45
]
of
Y_{j}(t)
with respect to
B_{i}^{H_{i}}(t)
for
i,j=1,2
. And the explicit expression of the Malliavin derivatives is provided below:

\displaystyle D_{1,t}^{\phi}\bm{Y}(t)=H_{1}(2H_{1}-1)\Psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{-1}(s)(\sigma_{1},0)^{T}|t-s|^{2H_{1}-2}\mathrm{d}s,

(C.6)

and

\displaystyle D_{2,t}^{\phi}\bm{Y}(t)=H_{2}(2H_{2}-1)\Psi(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{-1}(s)(0,\sigma_{2})^{T}|t-s|^{2H_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}s,

(C.7)

with fundamental-solution matrix
\Psi(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Psi(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\bm{\hat{f}}(\bm{Y}(t))\Psi(t),\quad\Psi(0)=I.

(C.8)

Then, taking the expectation of (
C.4
) and noting that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{i}}(\bm{Y}(t))\sigma_{i}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}B_{i}^{H}(t)\Big]=0,

due to FWIS’s interpretation, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E}[F(\bm{Y}(t))]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{i}}(\bm{Y}(t))f_{i}(\bm{y}(t))\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}(\bm{Y}(t))\sigma_{i}D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t)\Big].

(C.9)

According to the definition of expectation,
C.9
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(\bm{y})p(\bm{y},t)\mathrm{d}\bm{y}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{i}}(\bm{y})f_{i}(\bm{y})

(C.10)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}(\bm{y})\sigma_{i}\mathbb{E}[D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t)|\bm{Y}=\bm{y}]\Big\}p(\bm{y},t)\mathrm{d}\bm{y}.

(C.11)

Applying the integration by parts of the right-hand side of (
C.9
) and assuming that as
|y_{i}|\to\infty,i=1,2
,
p(\bm{y},t)
and its derivatives up to the second order with respect to
\bm{Y}
all vanish, (
C.10
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(\bm{y})\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(\bm{y},t)\mathrm{d}\bm{y}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(\bm{y})\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\big\{f_{i}(\bm{y})p(\bm{y},t)\big\}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}

(C.12)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(\bm{y})\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}\big\{\sigma_{i}\mathbb{E}[D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t)|\bm{Y}=\bm{y}]p(\bm{y},t)\big\}\mathrm{d}\bm{y}.

(C.13)

Using the fact that
F(\bm{Y}(t))
is arbitrary, (
C.12
) leads to the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(\bm{y},t)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\big\{f_{i}(\bm{y})p(\bm{y},t)\big\}

(C.14)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}\big\{\sigma_{i}\mathbb{E}[D^{\phi}_{i,t}Y_{j}(t)|\bm{Y}=\bm{y}]p(\bm{y},t)\big\}.

(C.15)

Thus, (
C.14
) together with (
C.6
)-(
C.8
) lead to the proof of Theorem
2
.

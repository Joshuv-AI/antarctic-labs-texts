1.
Introduction

On a closed hyperbolic surface, a closed multi-geodesic is called
filling
if it cuts the surface into topological disks. Thurston in
[
Thu88
]
initiated the study on filling closed multi-geodesics, which plays a role in the proof of Nielsen realization theorem
[
Ker83
]
, the construction of pseudo-Anosov mappings by the Dehn twists on a filling pair
[
Thu88
]
, and the construction of Thurston spine
[
Thu86
]
. Later on, filling closed multi-geodesics arise from various topics on hyperbolic surfaces, for example, the systole function
[
Sch99
]
, and the asymptotics of filling closed geodesics on random hyperbolic surfaces
[
WX25
,
DS23
]
.

For a closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g\geq 2
, denote by
\slfs(X_{g})
the minimal length of filling closed multi-geodesics on
X_{g}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
denote the moduli space of closed hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
. The quantity

\slfs:\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}

gives a natural function on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, which is proper by the standard Collar Lemma (see
e.g.

[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.4.6]
). Very recently, it is obtained by
[
GWW25
]
that

\min\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\slfs(X_{g})=(8g-4)\arccosh\left(\sqrt{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{8g-4}\right)\right),

that is half of the perimeter of the regular right-angled hyperbolic
(8g-4)
-gon. One may also see
[
Are15
,
AH15
,
SV21
,
Gas21
]
and the references therein for related results.

1.1.
Uniform asymptotics of
\slfs

As introduced above, the function
\slfs(X_{g})
is unbounded as
X_{g}
approaches the boundary of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. In this work, we study its uniform asymptotic behavior as systole goes to
0
. Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
. Then

\pi(g-1)+\mathrm{R}(X_{g})\leq\slfs(X_{g})\leq 300g+12\mathrm{R}(X_{g})

where

\mathrm{R}(X_{g})=\sum\limits_{\textit{closed geodesic }\gamma\subset X_{g},\ \ell(\gamma)<1}\log\left(\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)}\right).

The lower bound of Theorem
1.1
follows directly from the Collar Lemma and a two-dimensional isoperimetric inequality. The difficult part is to prove the upper bound, in which there are two major new ingredients: the existence of a shortest embedded filling graph (see Section
3
) and its dual
4
-valent graph (see Section
4
).

We first show that there exists an embedded filling graph in
X_{g}
that realizes the minimal length among all filling graphs in
X_{g}
with a bounded number of vertices and edges (see Proposition
3.6
); moreover, it is a trivalent graph that cuts the surface into exactly one geodesic polygon with the property that each interior angle is equal to
\frac{2}{3}\pi
(see Proposition
3.13
). To prove Proposition
3.6
, we use Hausdorff convergence. In general, a Hausdorff convergent sequence of smooth embedded submanifolds may converge to some object quite different from the original sequence in geometry and topology. Our Proposition
3.6
shows that in
X_{g}
, the Hausdorff limit of a sequence of embedded filling graphs approaching the minimal length is still an embedded filling graph with the same combinatorics and topology as the original sequence. This is comparable to Meeks-Simon-Yau’s result on minimal surfaces in
3
-manifolds
[
MSY82
]
. They showed in a
3
-manifold that an area-minimizing sequence of embedded surfaces converges to an embedded minimal surface with topology similar to the original sequence of surfaces. The main difficulty in proving Proposition
3.6
is to show the embeddness of the Hausdorff limit. If the limit is not embedded, then one can modify the graph, shortening its length, and eliminating some self-intersection points (see Lemma
3.10
). An embedded graph can be obtained after finitely many modifications (see Lemma
3.11
).
The modifications can be chosen to be sufficiently slight so that the modified graph is still in the considered graph family. For properties of the limit graph,
i.e.
Proposition
3.13
, it follows from the length minimality of the limit graph: if the properties in this proposition do not hold, then one can shorten the length of the graph by certain modifications, contradicting the length minimality.

For an embedded filling graph
G\subset X_{g}
, we next introduce its
dual
4
-valent graph
\mathcal{D}(G)
as the graph obtained by connecting the mid-points of neighboring edges of
G
by geodesic segments. This
4
-valent graph on
X_{g}
can be treated as a finite set of closed curves. In general, these curves may be contractible or not in minimal position (see Figure
18
). However, we are able to show that if
G
realizes the minimal length among all filling graphs, its dual
4
-valent graph
\mathcal{D}(G)
is in minimal position and isotopic to a filling set of closed geodesics (see Proposition
4.2
). This can be proved by showing the nonexistence of disks, immersed monogons and bigons bounded by curves in
\mathcal{D}(G)
by using the Gauss-Bonnet formula. Next, from an elementary inequality (see Proposition
4.9
), it is not hard to see that

\slfs(X_{g})\leq\ell(\mathcal{D}(G))\leq 2\ell(G).

At this stage, to obtain the upper bound of Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to construct an explicit embedded filling graph, still denoted by
G
, in
X_{g}
such that

\ell(G)\leq 150g+6\mathrm{R}(X_{g}).

This will be guaranteed by Proposition
5.3
, in which the embedded filling graph is modified by a graph in
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.5.2]
.

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{\min\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\slfs(X_{g})}{g}=8\arccosh\left(\sqrt{2}\right)\textit{ and }\sup\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\slfs(X_{g})=\infty.

uniformly comparable to

For any

\lim_{g\to\infty}\prob_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ 7g<\mathcal{L}_{\sys}^{\mathrm{fill}}(X_{g})<Cg\right)=1.

For the expected value, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
p>0
, there exist two positive constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(p)
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(p)
only depending on

such that for
g
large enough,

C_{1}<\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[(\mathcal{L}_{\sys}^{\mathrm{fill}})^{p}\right]}{g^{p}}<C_{2}.

If
p=1
,
C_{1}=7
and

is arbitrary.

To prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem

[

, Theorem 7.1]
to obtain an effective estimate of the expected value

\mathrm{R}(X_{g})
is as in Theorem

6.1
.

We also study the behavior of
\mathcal{L}_{\sys}^{\mathrm{fill}}
in the Brooks-Makover model
[
BM04
]
of random hyperbolic surfaces. Firstly, we briefly recall the construction of random hyperbolic surfaces in the Brooks-Makover model
[
BM04
]
. For
N\geq 2
, take
2N
hyperbolic ideal triangles and glue them together into a cusped hyperbolic surface, where the gluing scheme is given by a random oriented trivalent graph. The glue is required to have zero shearing coordinates. Denote the gluing pattern as
\omega
and the set of all the gluing patterns as
\Omega_{N}
. For
\omega\in\Omega_{N}
, the cusped hyperbolic surface obtained by gluing ideal triangles following the pattern
\omega
is denoted by
S_{O}(\omega)
. Its compactification by filling in all the cusps is denoted by
S_{C}(\omega)
. The set
\Omega_{N}
is finite, so define the probability of its subset

as

\prob_{\mathrm{BM}}^{N}(B)=\frac{|B|}{|\Omega_{N}|}.

One may see

[

,

SW23
,
LP23
]
for more details of this random model. Our result on
\slfs
for this random hyperbolic surface model is as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
\omega\in\Omega_{N}
,

\pi N\leq\slfs(S_{O}(\omega))\leq 6N.

For the compact case,

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\prob_{\mathrm{BM}}^{N}\left(\omega\in\Omega_{N};\ \frac{7}{2}N<\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{C}(\omega))<6N\right)=1.

Here, a closed multi-geodesic is called
filling
in a cusped hyperbolic surface if each component of its complement is homotopic to either a point or a circle around a cusp. For the proof of the compact case of Theorem
1.4
, it relies on the following proposition that is of its own interest.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\mathcal{T}
be a geodesic triangulation of
X
. If the degree of each vertex of
\mathcal{T}
is at least

, then every closed curve in its

-valent dual graph
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T})
is incontractible. The curves in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T})

The main goal of this section is to prove the existence of a shortest filling graph on a closed hyperbolic surface,

Consider a connected graph

Lemma 3.7

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

f(x)=\arcsinh\left(\frac{1}{\sinh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log 2.

Then

\log\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\leq f(x)\leq\begin{cases}2,&\text{ if }x>\frac{1}{2};\\
3\log\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)&\text{ if }0<x\leq\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that
(\arcsinh(x))^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}
. So

f^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{2\sinh\frac{x}{2}}<0.

Since

f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\approx 1.73<2<3\log 2\approx 2.08,

the upper bound follows from the following fact

\left(3\log\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)^{\prime}-f^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{3}{x}+\frac{1}{2\sinh\frac{x}{2}}<0.

For the lower bound, note that

\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{x}<-\frac{1}{2\sinh\frac{x}{2}}=f^{\prime}(x).

The conclusion then follows because

\lim\limits_{x\to 0^{+}}\left(f(x)-\log\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)=\frac{3\log 2}{2}>0.

The proof is complete.

For a Borel subset

For

WX22
,
NWX23
]

,
f_{2}
of the variable

f_{1}(g)=O(f_{2}(g))

C>0
, independent of

, such that

For two positive functions

and
f_{2,p}
about the parameter

.

Here is an elementary inequality that will be applied later.
For

\left(\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+...+a_{n}}{n}\right)^{p}\leq\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}\right)^{p}\leq a_{1}^{p}+a_{2}^{p}+...+a_{n}^{p}.

Lemma 6.1

For

\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[\mathrm{R}(X)^{p}]}{g^{p}}=O_{p}(1).

If

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[\mathrm{R}(X)]=O(1).

Define

f_{p}(x):=\begin{cases}(-3\log x)^{p},&\text{ if }0<x<1;\\

And we define

By definition, we know that

\mathrm{R}(X)\leq f^{\gamma}_{1}(X).

By the classical Collar Lemma we know that the number of closed geodesics of length
\leq 1

for all

Define

. Then

If

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}f_{p}^{\gamma}(X)\mathrm{d}\vol

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}tf_{p}(t)V(\text{cut},t)\mathrm{d}t\text{\,\,\,\quad(by Mirzakhani's integration formula)}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}tf_{p}(t)\left(V_{g-1,2}(t,t)+\sum_{k=1}^{g-1}V_{k,1}(t)\times V_{g-k,1}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle V_{g}.

The proof is complete.

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[(\slfs)^{p}]
follows directly:

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[(\slfs)^{p}]>\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[\left(7g\right)^{p}\right]=\left(7g\right)^{p}.

In particular, when
p=1
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[\slfs]>7g.

Recall that the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
says that for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
,

\slfs(X)\leq 300g+12\mathrm{R}(X).

For the upper bound, when
p=1
, for sufficiently large
g
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[\slfs]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[\left(300g+12\mathrm{R}(X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[300g\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[12\mathrm{R}(X)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 300g+O(1)\text{\,\,\,\quad(by Lemma \ref{lem:r_exp})}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{2}g,

where
C_{2}>300
is arbitrary. For
p>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}[(\slfs)^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[\left(300g+12\mathrm{R}(X)\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left[2^{p}\left(\left(300g\right)^{p}+\left(12\mathrm{R}(X)\right)^{p}\right)\right]\text{\,\,\,\quad(by (\ref{for:sum_an}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{p}(1)\times g^{p}+O_{p}(1)\times g^{p}\text{\,\,\,\quad\ (by Lemma \ref{lem:r_exp})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{p}(1)\times g^{p}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, by (
6.3
) we know that for
g
large enough,

\mathcal{L}_{\sys}^{\mathrm{fill}}(X)>7g.

It remains to show the upper bound. For
C>300
, it follows that

\displaystyle\prob_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}(X\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \mathcal{L}_{\sys}^{\mathrm{fill}}(X)\geq Cg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prob_{\mathrm{WP}}^{g}\left(X\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \mathrm{R}(X)\geq\frac{C-300}{12}g\right)\text{\,\,\, \quad(by Theorem \ref{mt-1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}^{g}_{\mathrm{WP}}[\mathrm{R}(X)]}{\frac{C-300}{12}g}\text{\,\,\,\quad\quad(by Markov's inequality)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{O(1)}{\frac{C-300}{12}g}\text{\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad(by Lemma \ref{lem:r_exp})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{1}{g}\right).

The theorem then follows by letting
g\to\infty
. ∎

6.2.
Brooks-Makover random surfaces

We refer to
e.g.

[
BM04
,
Pet15
,
SW23
]
for the construction of random hyperbolic surfaces in the Brooks-Makover model. A cusped hyperbolic surface in such a model is required to have zero shearing coordinates. Two ideal triangles that share a common edge have shearing zero at the common edge if the inscribed circles of the two triangles intersect on the common edge (see Figure
28
).

Figure 28
.
Zero shearing

We first prove Theorem
1.4
for the cusp case.

Proposition 6.2
.

For any
\omega\in\Omega_{N}
,

\pi N\leq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{O}(\omega))\leq 6N.

Proof.

For the lower bound to
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{O}(\omega))
, observe that a filling closed multi-geodesic cuts
S_{O}(\omega)
into geodesic polygons and geodesic polygons with one cusp. For a polygon
P
, with or without one cusp,
\area(P)\leq\perim(P)
. For the polygon without any cusp, it follows from
e.g.

[
Bus10
, Section 8.1 Page 211]
. For the polygon with one cusp, it follows from
e.g.

[
SS24
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Therefore,

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{O}(\omega))\geq\frac{1}{2}\area(S_{O}(\omega))=\pi N.

Next we prove the upper bound. For each ideal triangle, we join the three points on its three edges that touch the triangle’s inscribed circle. Then we get a small geodesic triangle contained in the ideal triangle (see Figure
29
). Observe that by the symmetry of the triangles (see Figure
29
),
\angle 1=\angle 3=\angle 4=\angle 6
,
\angle 2=\angle 5
, therefore
\angle 2+\angle 3+\angle 4=\angle 1+\angle 6+\angle 5=\pi
and
\angle 3+\angle 4+\angle 5=\angle 6+\angle 1+\angle 2=\pi
. Thus, the edges of small triangles form a set of closed geodesics in
S_{O}(\omega)
.

Figure 29
.
Small geodesic triangles in
S_{O}(\omega)

The set of those closed geodesics fills
S_{O}(\omega)
, since their union cut the surface
S_{O}(\omega)
into certain convex triangles and cusped disks. It remains to compute its total length. This can be obtained by elementary computations, which we briefly discuss here.

Figure 30
.
Length of a small triangle

First, we know that the length of each edge of a small triangle (see Figure
30
) is equal to

l=2\arcsinh\frac{1}{2}\approx 0.962

by direct calculation (see
e.g.

[
SW23
, Remark on P. 15296]
). Then the perimeter of each small triangle is
3l\approx 2.887
. Therefore, we get

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{O}(\omega))\leq 3l\cdot 2N\approx 5.775N<6N

as desired. The proof is complete.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.4
for the closed case.

Proposition 6.3
.

The following limit holds.

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\prob_{\mathrm{BM}}^{N}\left(\omega\in\Omega_{N};\ \frac{7}{2}N<\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{C}(\omega))<6N\right)=1.

Proof.

For the lower bound, it is known by
[
Gam06
, Corollary
5.1
and
(2.1)
]
that

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\prob_{\mathrm{BM}}^{N}\left(\omega\in\Omega_{N};\ \mathrm{genus}(S_{C}(\omega))=1+\frac{N}{2}+O(\log N)\right)=1.

And it is also known from (
6.3
) that for sufficiently large
g
,

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(X_{g})>7g.

Combining these two results above, we get

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\prob_{\mathrm{BM}}^{N}\left(\omega\in\Omega_{N};\ \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fill}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(S_{C}(\omega))>\frac{7}{2}N\right)=1.

For the upper bound, notice that the ideal triangulation of
S_{O}(\omega)
induces a geodesic triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
S_{C}(\omega)
(take the image of the ideal triangulation of
S_{O}(\omega)
onto
S_{C}(\omega)
and straighten their edges). So, the set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}
consists of all the punctures of
S_{O}(\omega)
, and the degree of each vertex depends on the size of the horoball around the corresponding puncture in
S_{O}(\omega)
. Thanks to

[
BM04
, Theorem 2.1]
, we know that for any
L>0
,

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\prob_{\mathrm{BM}}^{N}\left(\omega\in\Omega_{N};\ S_{O}(\omega)\text{ has large cusps}\geq L\right)=1.

Thus, choosing
L>0
large enough, one may assume that for a random surface in
\Omega_{N}
, each vertex of the geodesic triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
S_{C}(\omega)
has degree
>6
. Then it follows from Proposition
4.10
that the dual
4
-valent graph
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
is a filling closed multi-curve, isotopic to a filling closed multi-geodesic in
S_{C}(\omega)
. Observe that the image of the filling closed multi-geodesic in
S_{O}(\omega)
constructed in Proposition
6.2
is isotopic to
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T})
in
S_{C}(\omega)
, since the inclusion
i:S_{O}(\omega)\hookrightarrow S_{C}(\omega)
is holomorphic, it then follows from the standard Schwarz-Pick Lemma that the length of the filling closed multi-geodesic that is isotopic to
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T})
in
S_{C}(\omega)
is no more than the length of the filling closed multi-geodesic in
S_{O}(\omega)
constructed in Proposition
6.2
, that is less than
6N
. This proves the upper bound. The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

It follows from Propositions
6.2
and
6.3
.

Abstract
. We study the existence of bounded asymptotic mild solutions to evolution equations of the form u ′ ​ ( t ) = A ​ u ​ ( t ) + f ⁡ ( t ) , t ≥ 0 u^{\prime}(t)=Au(t)+f(t),t\geq 0 in a Banach space 𝕏 \mathbb{X} , where A A generates an (analytic) C 0 C_{0} -semigroup and f f is bounded. We find spectral conditions on A A and f f for the existence and uniqueness of asymptotic mild solutions with the same ”profile” as that of f f . In the resonance case, a sufficient condition of Massera type theorem is found for the existence of bounded solutions with the same profile as f f . The obtained results are stated in terms of spectral properties of A A and f f , and they are analogs of classical results of Katznelson-Tzafriri and Massera for the evolution equations on the half line. Applications from PDE are given.

1.
Introduction, Notations and Preliminaries

In this paper we consider necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness (in some sense) of bounded solutions with specific structures of spectrum for equations of the form

(1.1)

u^{\prime}(t)=Au(t)+f(t),\ t\in J,

where
J=[0,\infty)
,
A
is the generator of an (analytic)
C_{0}
-semigroup in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
,
f
is a bounded continuous function on the half line
[0,\infty)
with values in
\mathbb{X}
. As is well known in the qualitative theory of linear ODE (see e.g.
[
5
]
) that if
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
A
is a matrix with all eigenvalues lying off the imaginary axis, that means the equation has an exponential dichotomy, then there exists a projection
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
AP=PA
and

\displaystyle\|Pe^{tA}P\|\leq Ne^{-\alpha t},\ t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\|Qe^{tA}Q\|\leq Ne^{\alpha t},\ t\leq 0,

where
Q:=I-P
, and
\alpha
is a positive number independent of
t
. Therefore, in this case, the following function
u_{0}(\cdot)
defined on
[0,\infty)

(1.2)

u_{0}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}Pe^{(t-s)A}Pf(s)ds-\int^{\infty}_{t}Qe^{(t-s)A}Qf(s)ds

is a solution to Eq.(
1.1
) that is bounded on
J=[0,\infty)
. By Perron Theorem, see e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 2, p. 22]
, the existence of at least a bounded solution on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
for each given bounded and continuous
f
yields the exponential dichotomy of (
1.1
), in this finite dimensional case, as well. In general, Eq.(
1.1
) may have more bounded solutions on the half line
[0,\infty)
. For example, all solutions of the form

(1.3)

u(t)=Pe^{tA}Px+u_{0},\ t\geq 0,

are solutions of Eq.(
1.1
) that are bounded on
J=[0,\infty)
. It is easy to see that these are all solutions that are bounded on
[0,\infty)
. We note that actually, Eq(
1.1
) in this case has only one bounded solution within functions decaying to zero as
t\to+\infty
. We also note that as is well known (see
[
5
]
) for equations defined on the whole real line the situation is much more simple. In fact,
u_{0}(\cdot)
is the unique solution that is bounded on the whole real line if
f(\cdot)
is a given bounded on the whole real line. In the infinite dimensional case, additional conditions are needed for the equivalence between the exponential dichotomy and the existence of at least a bounded solutions on the half line. For more details, see e.g.
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
.

It is also interesting to note that the bounded solution problem for Eq.(
1.1
) on the whole real line
J=\mathbb{R}
, where
f
is defined and bounded on the whole real line
\mathbb{R}
, turns out to be ”simpler” as the necessary and sufficient conditions can be stated in a shorter manner. Namely, as is well known, in this case the Perron Theorem says that Eq. (
1.1
) has an exponential dichotomy if and only if for each
f
defined and bounded on the whole real line
J=\mathbb{R}
there exists a unique bounded solution on the real line
\mathbb{R}
. In the infinite dimensional case, the solutions may be understood in the sense of mild solutions.

Bounded Solution Problem of Eq.(
1.1
) when
J=\mathbb{R}
has been considered in a broader context when the equation may have no exponential dichotomy, that is, the operator
A
may allow
\sigma(A)\cap i\mathbb{R}\not=\emptyset
in many works. For more information see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
18
,
20
,
22
,
24
,
25
,
31
]
. Basically, the results can be summarized as follows: the concept of spectrum
\sigma(f)
of a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
is used that generalizes the concept of frequency. For autonomous equations (i.e.
A
is independent of time
t
), if
A
generates an analytic semigroup, then the non-resonance condition
\sigma(A)\cap i\mathbb{R}\not=\emptyset
guarantees the existence of a classical solution
u_{f}
with
\sigma(u_{f})\subset\sigma(f)
. In the general case without analyticity of the semigroup
T(t)
generated by
A
, a non-resonance condition is imposed on the operator
T(1)
(see
[
22
]
). In the resonance case a well known result (Massera Theorem) in the theory of ODE says that for Eq.(
1.1
) with
u(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f(\cdot)
being
p
-periodic it has a
p
-periodic solution if and only if it has a bounded solution on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. In the general case, numerous extensions of this results are obtained for periodic solutions in
[
9
,
31
,
25
]
, for almost periodic solutions in
[
24
]
.

For Eq.(
1.1
) on the half line
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the situation is much more complicated because we have very little information on the function
f
on the half line. And the spectrum of a function on the half line does not capture much of the behavior of the function as in the whole real line case.
It is the purpose of this paper to extend some of the above mentioned results to the half line case when the eigennvalues of
A
(or spectrum
\sigma(A)
, if
\mathbb{X}
is generally a Banach space) may intersect the imaginary axis. This generalization will be based on the spectral theory of functions on the half line
[0,\infty)
that has been developed recently in a series of papers by
[
18
,
14
,
15
]
and a recent paper
[
6
]
. We will use the method of sums of commuting operators as it is used in
[

]
to prove the existence and uniqueness of asymptotic mild solutions. Our first result is stated in Theorems

saying that in case

generates an analytic

-semigroup,

has precompact range and if

is the spectrum of

, then there exists an asymptotic mild solution to Eq.(

) that is unique in

within a function converging to zero. This result is a Katznelson-Tzafriri type theorem (see e.g.
[

]
for related results and topics).
We also consider the resonance case when
\sigma(A)\cap isp(f)\not=\emptyset
. In this case, the second main result of this paper of Massera type theorem (Theorem
3.13
) says that under the assumption that

and
sp(f)\cap\overline{\sigma_{i}(A)\backslash sp(f)}=\emptyset
and one of the sets

,
\overline{\sigma_{i}(A)\backslash sp(f)}
is compact, where
\sigma_{i}(A)
is the part of

on the imaginery axis, then, then there exists an asymptotic mild solution with the same spectral profile as

provided that there exists an asymptotic mild solution of Eq.(

) in

[
15
]
and
[
24
]

3.1.
Existence and uniqueness of bounded solutions

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

follows from that of the semigroup

because

[\bar{T}(t)f](\cdot):=T(t)f(\cdot),\ f\in BUC_{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X}),t\geq 0.

Next, we show that

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Set

. Then, as

the map

is uniformly continuous. This yields that for each
\epsilon>0
there exists a

such that if
|t-t^{\prime}|<\delta
and
\|y-y^{\prime}\|<\delta
, where

(3.4)

\|T(t)y-T(t^{\prime})y^{\prime}\|<\epsilon.

Consequently,

(3.5)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|T(t)f(x)-f(x)\|<\epsilon,

for all

. In other words,

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\bar{T}(t)\bar{f}(\cdot)=\bar{f}(\cdot).

∎

Let
\bar{A}
be the generator of the semigroup
(\bar{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Let

. By evaluating the function

That is,

is part of the operator
\bar{A}_{T}
defined in (

and
\bar{A}f=\bar{A}_{T}f
whenever

.

Claim 3.4
.

With the above notations
\bar{A}=\bar{A}_{T}
.

Proof.

First, we see that since
D(\bar{A})\subset D(\bar{A}_{T})
, it follows that

is dense in
BUC_{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
. Next, as
T(t)
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup there are positive constants

such that let
\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t}
for all

. By the Hille-Yosida Theorem, this fact is equivalent to the following: for all real
\lambda>\omega

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mbox{The resolvent set}\ \rho(A)\ \mbox{contains the ray}\ (\omega,\infty)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{M}{(\Re\lambda-\omega)^{n}}\ \mbox{for}\ \Re\lambda>\omega,\ n=1,2,\cdots.

If
f(\cdot)\in BUC_{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
, then,

as well. And we can verify easily that
\lambda\in\rho(\bar{A}_{T})
with

. That is why similar conditions to (
3.7
) and (
3.8

\bar{A}_{T}

is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup tat we may denote by

. Next, we can see that this semigroup

must coincide with

-semigroup (see e.g.

As

and

The proof of the proposition follows immediately from the above claims.

Lemma 3.6

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)w_{n}(s)ds=\int^{t}_{0}\lim_{n\to\infty}T(t-s)w_{n}(s)ds=\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)w(s)ds.

\displaystyle u(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}T(t)u_{n}(0)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)w_{n}(s)ds

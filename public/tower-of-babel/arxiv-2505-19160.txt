1.
introduction

Harmonic mappings are a useful tool in the study of fluid flow problems (see
[
1
]
). In addition, planar fluid dynamics problems naturally give rise to univalent harmonic functions
with special geometric properties such as convexity, starlikeness and close-to-convexity. Univalent harmonic functions are also used in the representation of minimal surfaces. For
example, Heinz
[
21
]
used such mappings in the study of the Gaussian
curvature of nonparametric minimal surfaces over the unit disc (see
[
18
, p. 182, section 10.3]
) and Aleman
et al.

[
1
, Theorem 4.5]
considered a fluid flow problem on a convex domain
\Omega
satisfying an interesting geometric property. After this brief motivation, we will now focus on univalent harmonic mappings.

Let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function of
z=x+iy
in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If
f\in C^{2}(\Omega)
(continuous first and second partial derivatives in
\Omega
) and satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
in
\Omega
,
then
f
is said to be harmonic in
\Omega
. Note that every harmonic mapping
f
has the canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and

g
are analytic in
\Omega
, known respectively as the analytic and co-analytic parts of
f
, and
\overline{g(z)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
g(z)
. The Jacobian of
f
is defined by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
. The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy
[
26
]
shows that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent in

\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian of
f
, defined by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
is non-zero in
\Omega
. A locally univalent harmonic function
f
is said to be sense-preserving if
J_{f}(z)>0
in
D
and sense-reversing if
J_{f}(z)<0
in
D
(see
[
14
,
15
,
18
,
44
]
). Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all complex-valued harmonic
functions
f=h+\overline{g}
defined in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with the normalization
h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=0
and
g(0)=0
. If the co-analytic part
g(z)\equiv 0

in
\mathbb{D}
, then the class
\mathcal{H}
reduces to the class
\mathcal{A}
of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=1
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
that are sense-preserving and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and let

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}=\left\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\right\}
. The analytic and co-analytic parts of every
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
have the following forms:

\displaystyle h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\;\;\text{and}\;\;g(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

(1.1)

If
g(z)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{D}
, then both the classes
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
reduces to the class

\mathcal{S}
of analytic and univalent functions in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
. Both
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
are natural harmonic
generalizations of
\mathcal{S}
, but only
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
is known to be compact although both
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
are normal. In 1984, Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
15
]
undertook a comprehensive study of the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and its geometric subclasses. This study
has subsequently garnered extensive attention from researchers (see
[
4
,
5
,
10
,
23
,
45
,
33
,
9
]
).

A domain
\Omega
is called starlike with respect to a point
z_{0}\in\Omega
if the line segment joining
z_{0}
to any point in
\Omega
lies in
\Omega
. In particular, if

z_{0}=0
, then
\Omega
is simply called starlike. A complex-valued harmonic mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be starlike if
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike. We denote the class of
harmonic starlike functions in
\mathbb{D}
by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
. A domain
\Omega
is called convex if it is starlike with respect to every point in
\Omega
. A function

f\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be convex if
f(\mathbb{D})
is convex. The class of all harmonic convex mappings in
\mathbb{D}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Starlikeness is a hereditary property for conformal mappings.
Thus if
f
is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
and if
f
maps
\mathbb{D}
onto a domain that is starlike with respect to the origin, then the image of every subdisk

|z|<r<1
is also starlike with respect to the origin.
Again, this
hereditary property does not generalize to harmonic mappings, which is being discussed in
[
14
]
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the class of all analytic functions
h
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)-1=0
and
\textrm{Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
.
It is well-known that
\mathcal{R}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
. MacGregor
[
32
]
proved that if
h\in\mathcal{R}
, then each partial sum
s_{n}(h)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}z^{k}
is
univalent in
|z|<1/2
for
n\geq 2
and
h(z)
maps the disk
|z|<\sqrt{2}-1
onto a convex domain. The numbers
1/2
and
\sqrt{2}-1
are the best possible constants. In

[
43
]
, Singh proved that if
h\in\mathcal{R}
, then each partial sum
s_{n}(h)
is convex in
|z|<1/4
and the number
1/4
is the best possible constant.

In 2013, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
40
]
studied the following class as a harmonic analog of the class
\mathcal{R}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}:\textrm{Re}\left(h^{\prime}(z)\right)>|g^{\prime}(z)|\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{D}\right\}

and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}:=\left\{f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\right\}
. The authors of
[
40
]
proved that functions in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
are close-to-convex in
\mathbb{D}
. In
[
29
]
and
[
30
]
, Li and Ponnusamy have investigated the radius of univalency and convexity of sections of functions
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
, respectively.

In 2020, Ghosh and Allu
[
20
]
established the coefficient bound problem and the growth theorem for functions in the class

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)=\{h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}:\text{Re}\left(zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M+|zg^{\prime\prime}(z)|\;\text{with}\;g^{\prime}(0)=0\;\text{for}\;M>0,z\in\mathbb{D}\}

and a two-point distortion theorem for functions in the class

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)=\{h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}:\left|zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\leq M-|zg^{\prime\prime}(z)|\;\text{with}\;g^{\prime}(0)=0\;\text{for}\;M>0,z\in\mathbb{D}\}.

The subclasses
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
are not only the generalizations of analytic functions but also they are closely related to the
analytic subclasses
\mathcal{B}(M)
and
\mathcal{P}(M)
respectively and the classes are defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{P}(M)=\{h\in\mathcal{A}:\text{Re}\left(zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)>-M\;\text{for}\;M>0,z\in\mathbb{D}\},\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{B}(M)=\{h\in\mathcal{A}:\left|zh^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\leq M\;\text{for}\;M>0,z\in\mathbb{D}\}.\end{array}\right.

The classes mentioned in (
1
) have been studied by Mocanu
[
34
]
, and Ponnusamy and Singh
[
38
]
. In 1995, Ali
et al.
[
2
]
proved that each function in the class
\mathcal{P}(M)

is univalent and starlike in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
for
0<M\leq 1/\log 4(\approx 0.7213475)
. Afterwards, Ponnusamy and Singh
[
38
]
showed that each function in the class
\mathcal{B}(M)
are univalent and starlike whenever
0<M\leq 1
and convex whenever
0<M\leq 1/2
.

Motivated by the results of
[
40
,
29
,
30
]
and the class
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
, in this paper,
we consider the class
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
of all functions
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}
for
M>0,\alpha\in(0,1]
that satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+z\alpha h^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|\leq M-\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\quad\text{with}~g^{\prime}(0)=0

for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It is evident that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(1,M)=\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(M)
.

The organization of this paper is: In section
2
, we establish the sharp coefficients bounds, growth results, starlikeness and some other properties for functions
in
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. In section
5
, we obtain the sharp bound for the second Hankel determinant of logarithmic inverse coefficients for functions in the class

\mathcal{P}(M)
. The remaining sections contain introductions and key lemmas.

2.
fundamental properties

In the following result, we obtain the sharp coefficient bounds for functions in the class
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
be of the form (
1.1
). For
n\geq 2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq M/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
and
|b_{n}|\leq M/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
.
The result is sharp for the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, where the functions are given by
f_{1}(z)=z+Mz^{n}/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
and
f_{2}(z)=z+M\overline{z^{n}}/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
for
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

As
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+z\alpha h^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|\leq M-\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|~\text{for}~z\in\mathbb{D}.

(2.1)

Since
(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+z\alpha h^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)a_{n}z^{n-1}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, then in view of Cauchy’s integral formula for derivatives, we have

\displaystyle\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)a_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r}\frac{(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+z\alpha h^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)}{z^{n}}dz.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)|a_{n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}h^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})-(1-\alpha)}{r^{n}e^{in\theta}}ire^{i\theta}d\theta\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\left|(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}h^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})-(1-\alpha)\right|}{r^{n-1}}d\theta.

From (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)r^{n-1}|a_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(M-\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+\alpha re^{i\theta}g^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})\right|\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M-\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(g^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})+re^{i\theta}g^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})\right)d\theta\right|=M.

Letting
r\to 1^{-}
gives the desired bound
|a_{n}|\leq M/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
. Using similar argument as above, we obtain
|b_{n}|\leq M/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
for
n\geq 2
. It is evident that

f_{1}(z)=z+Mz^{n}/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
and
f_{2}(z)=z+M\overline{z^{n}}/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)

(n\geq 2)
are in the class
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
with
|a_{n}(f_{1})|=M/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
and
|b_{n}(f_{2})|=M/\left(n+(n^{2}-2n)\alpha\right)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Setting
\alpha=1
in Theorem
2.1
gives Theorem 2.2 of
[
19
]
.

Let us consider the class
\mathcal{D}(\alpha,M)
of all functions
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying the following condition:

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)\phi^{\prime}(z)+\alpha z\phi^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|\leq M\quad\text{for}\quad M>0,\;\alpha\in(0,1]\quad\text{and}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The following result gives a correlation between the functions in the classes
\mathcal{D}(\alpha,M)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The harmonic map
f=h+\overline{g}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
if, and only if, the
function
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g
belongs to
\mathcal{D}(\alpha,M
) for each
\varepsilon
with
|\varepsilon|=1
.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|\leq M-\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Fix
|\varepsilon|=1
. Since
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g
, thus, we have

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right)+\varepsilon\left((1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\leq M\;\;\text{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D},

which shows that
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha,M)
for each
\varepsilon
with
|\varepsilon|=1
. Conversely, if
F_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha,M)
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|\leq M,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right)+\varepsilon\left((1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right|\leq M.

Since
\varepsilon

(|\varepsilon|=1)
is arbitrary, for an appropriate choice of
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|+\left|(1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|\leq M\;\;\text{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D},

which shows that
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. This completes the proof.
∎

In the following result, we establish the sharp growth estimates for functions in the class
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
be of the form (
1.1
). Then,

\displaystyle|z|-\frac{M|z|^{2}}{2}\leq|f(z)|\leq|z|+\frac{M|z|^{2}}{2}.

(2.2)

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
z\not=0
, equality occurs for the function
f
given by
f(z)=z+Mz^{2}/2
or its suitable rotations.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha,M)
. In view of Theorem
2.2
, we have
F_{\varepsilon}=h+\varepsilon g\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha,M)
for each
|\varepsilon|=1
. For
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left((1-\alpha)h^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zh^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)\right)+\varepsilon\left((1-\alpha)g^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zg^{\prime\prime}(z)\right)\right|\leq M.

Thus, according to the subordination principle, there exists an analytic function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
\omega(0)=0
such that

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)+\alpha zF_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)=M\omega(z),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left(\alpha z^{1/\alpha-1}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right)=Mz^{1/\alpha-2}\omega(z)+(1-\alpha)z^{1/\alpha-2}.

(2.3)

In view of the Schwarz lemma, we have
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Note that
z^{a}=\exp(a\log(z))
, where
a>0
and the branch of the logarithm is determined by
\log(1)=0
. This guarantees that the function is both single-valued and analytic within that range. Let us consider two cases.

Case 1.
Let
\alpha\not=1
.
Using
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=1
, from (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\alpha z^{1/\alpha-1}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right|

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{|z|}(te^{i\theta})^{1/\alpha-2}e^{i\theta}dt+M\int_{0}^{|z|}(te^{i\theta})^{1/\alpha-2}\omega(te^{i\theta})e^{i\theta}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{|z|}t^{1/\alpha-2}dt+M\int_{0}^{|z|}t^{1/\alpha-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha|z|^{1/\alpha-1}+M\alpha|z|^{1/\alpha}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)|=|h^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon g^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1+M|z|.

(2.5)

Since
\varepsilon

(|\varepsilon|=1)
is arbitrary, it follows from (
2.5
) that
|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1+M|z|
.
Let
\Gamma
be the radial segment from
0
to
z
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi}d\xi+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{\xi}}d\overline{\xi}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\left(|h^{\prime}(\xi)|+|g^{\prime}(\xi)|\right)|d\xi|\leq\int_{0}^{|z|}\left(1+Mt\right)dt=|z|+M\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}.

From (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\alpha z^{1/\alpha-1}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{|z|}(te^{i\theta})^{1/\alpha-2}e^{i\theta}dt+M\int_{0}^{|z|}(te^{i\theta})^{1/\alpha-2}\omega(te^{i\theta})e^{i\theta}dt\right|

(2.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{|z|}t^{1/\alpha-2}dt+M\int_{0}^{|z|}t^{1/\alpha-2}\text{Re}\left(\omega(te^{i\theta})\right)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha|z|^{1/\alpha-1}-M\int_{0}^{|z|}t^{1/\alpha-1}dt=\alpha|z|^{1/\alpha-1}-M\alpha|z|^{1/\alpha}.

From (
2.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right|=\left|h^{\prime}(z)+\varepsilon g^{\prime}(z)\right|\geq 1-M|z|.

(2.7)

Since
\varepsilon

(|\varepsilon|=1)
is arbitrary, it follows from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle|h^{\prime}(z)|-|g^{\prime}(z)|\geq 1-M|z|.

(2.8)

In view of (
2.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle|f(z)|=\left|\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi}d\xi+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{\xi}}d\overline{\xi}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|z|}\left(|h^{\prime}(\xi)|-|g^{\prime}(\xi)|\right)|d\xi|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|z|-M\int_{0}^{|z|}tdt=|z|-M\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}.

Case 2.
Let
\alpha=1
. From (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(z)\right)=\frac{M\omega(z)}{z}.

Using
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=1
, we have

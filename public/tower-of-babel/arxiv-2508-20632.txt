Dimensions and dimension spectra of Non-autonomous iterated function systems
Jun Jie Miao, Tianrui Wang

Abstract
. Non-autonomous iterated function systems are a generalization of iterated function systems. If the contractions in the system are conformal mappings, it is called a non-autonomous conformal iterated function system, and its attractor is called a non-autonomous conformal set. In this paper, we study intermediate dimension spectra of non-autonomous conformal sets which provide a unifying framework for Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions. First, we obtain the intermediate dimension spectra formula of non-autonomous conformal sets by using upper and lower topological pressures. As a consequence, we obtain simplified forms of their Hausdorff, packing and box dimensions. Finally, we explore the Hausdorff dimensions of the non-autonomous infinite conformal iterated function systems which consists of countably many conformal mappings at each level, and we provide the Hausdorff dimension formula under certain conditions



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Dimensions of fractals

Dimensions theory is central to fractal geometry, and Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions are two fundamental notions used in fractal geometry and dynamical systems. It is well-known that Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions are identical for self-similar sets satisfying open set condition, but there are also many interesting fractal sets with the two dimensions different. For example, the Hausdorff dimensions of many non-typical self-affine sets and Moran sets are strictly less than their (upper) box-counting dimensions, see
[
7
,
9
,
32
,
34
,
42
]
for details. The reason is because covering sets of widely ranging scales are permitted in the definition of Hausdorff dimensions, whereas covering sets with the same size are essentially used in box-counting dimensions. We refer readers to
[
13
,
42
]
for background reading on fractal geometry. .

Recently, there are large amount of literature on dimension spectra which provide a unifying framework for many dimensions fractal geometry, see
[
1
,
16
,
20
,
21
]
for various studies on dimension spectra. In
[
16
]
, Falconer, Fraser and Kempton introduced intermediate dimensions to provide a unifying framework for Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions.

Definition 1
.

Given a subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For each
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, the lower and upper
\theta
-intermediate dimensions of
E
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{s\geq 0:\textit{for all }\varepsilon>0,\Delta>0,\textit{there exists }0<\delta\leq\Delta\textit{ and a cover $\{U_{i}\}$}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\textit{of $E$ such that }\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\lvert U_{i}\rvert\leq\delta,\sum\lvert U_{i}\rvert^{s}\leq\varepsilon\},

\displaystyle\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{s\geq 0:\textit{for all }\varepsilon>0,\textit{ there exists }\Delta>0,\textit{such that for all }0<\delta\leq\Delta

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\textit{ there is a cover $\{U_{i}\}$}\textit{of $E$ such that }\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\lvert U_{i}\rvert\leq\delta,\sum\lvert U_{i}\rvert^{s}\leq\varepsilon\}.

If
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E
, we call it the
\theta
-intermediate dimension of
E
, denoted by
\dim_{\theta}E
.

Moreover, in
[
16
,
20
]
, the authors proved the continuity of intermediate dimensions.

Proposition 1.1
.

Given a bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the dimension spectra
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E
are continuous functions for
\theta\in(0,1]
.

As we see from the definition, for
\theta=0
, there are no restrictions for the size of the sets used in the covers which gives Hausdorff dimension. On the other hand, for
\theta=1
, the only covers using sets of the same size are allowable which recovers box-counting dimension. Therefore, Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions are the special values of intermediate dimensions
\dim_{\theta}E
, that is,

\overline{\dim}_{0}E=\underline{\dim}_{0}E=\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E,\quad\overline{\dim}_{1}E=\overline{\dim}_{B}E,\quad\underline{\dim}_{1}E=\underline{\dim}_{B}E,

It is valuable to study intermediate dimension spectra for which
\theta
the transition occurs in geometric behaviour, and this may deepen our understanding of dimensions and the geometric structure of the fractal sets, see
[
3
,
5
,
10
,
19
,
31
]
for various related studies and applications.

Since intermediate dimension spectra provide a continuum between Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions, it is interesting to explore the dimensions spectra for the fractals with different Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions. In this paper, we study the dimensions and the intermediate dimension spectra of nonautonomous conformal fractals generated by nonautonomous iterated function systems.

1.2.
Iterated function systems.

Let
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a finite set of contractions on

\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\#I\geq 2
. The set
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is called an
iterated function system(IFS)
, and it has a unique

attractor
, that is a unique non-empty compact
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that

(1.1)

E=\bigcup_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}(E).

If the contractions
\varphi_{i}
are conformal
(
similar or affine
)
mappings, we call
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a
conformal
(
similar or affine
)
iterated function system
and call
E
a
self-conformal
(
self-similar or self-affine
)
set
. Note that self-similar sets are special cases of self-conformal and self-affine sets. See
[
13
,
42
]
for details.

Formulae giving the Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions of self-similar sets satisfying the open set condition are well-known. However, calculation of the dimensions of self-conformal and self-affine sets is more awkward. In
[
35
]
, Mauldin and Urbański studied self-conformal sets
E
and defined a critical value
h
by topological pressure
P(t)
satisfying
h=\inf\{t:\overline{P}(t)<0\}=\sup\{t:\overline{P}(t)>0\}
, and they obtained that

(1.2)

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=h.

Moreover, they extended to infinite conformal iterated function systems by allowing
\#I=\infty
. By approximating with finite subsystems, they obtained that
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=h
. In
[
36
]
, Mauldin and Urbański further studied the box and packing dimensions of infinite conformal iterated function systems. Falconer in
[
12
]
studied the dimensions of self-affine sets. He defined a critical value
d(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\#I})
by using singular value functions, which is often called
affine dimension
or
Falconer dimension
, and under certain conditions, he proved

(1.3)

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=\dim_{B}E=\min\{d,d(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\#I})\},

almost surely. We refer readers to
[
6
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
25
,
37
]
for various related studies on iterated function systems.

Recently, Banaji and Fraser in
[
3
]
studied the intermediate dimensions of infinite conformal sets and obtained that

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E=\max\{h,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,P\}\qquad\textit{for }\theta\in[0,1],

where
P
is any subset of
J
which intersects
\varphi_{i}(J)
in exactly one point for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
h
is given by (
1.2
).

In this paper, we study the fractal dimensions and dimension spectra of non-autonomous finite conformal sets, and we extend our results to non-autonomous infinite conformal iterated function systems.

1.3.
Non-autonomous iterated function systems.

Non-autonomous iterated function systems may be regarded as a generalisation of iterated function systems. In 1946, Moran first studied non-autonomous similar sets, also called Moran sets which is a special class of fractals generated by non-autonomous iterated function systems, see
[
24
,
38
]
. First, we recall the definitions of non-autonomous iterated function systems.

Let
\{I_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of index sets with
\#I_{k}\geq 2
which is either finite or countable infinity. Given integers
k\geq l\geq 1
, we write

(1.4)

\Sigma_{l}^{k}=\{u_{l}u_{l+1}\ldots u_{k}:u_{j}\in I_{j},j=l,l+1,\ldots,k\},

and for simplicity, we set
\Sigma^{k}=\Sigma^{k}_{1}
if
l=1
. We write

\Sigma^{*}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\Sigma^{k}

for the set of all finite words with
\Sigma^{0}=\{\emptyset\}
containing only the empty word
\emptyset
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set with non-empty interior. For each integer
k>0
, let
\Phi_{k}=\{\varphi_{k,i}\}_{i\in I_{k}}
be a family of mappings
\varphi_{k,i}:J\to J
. We say the collection
\mathcal{J}=\{J_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}\}
of closed subsets of
J
fulfils the
non-autonomous structure with respect to
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i).

Uniform contraction
: There exists
0<c<1
such that for all integer
k>0
and all
i\in I_{k}
,

(1.5)

|\varphi_{k,i}(x)-\varphi_{k,i}(y)|\leq c|x-y|\qquad\textit{ for all }x,y\in J.

(ii).

For all integers
k>0
and all
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k-1}
, the elements
J_{\mathbf{u}i},i\in I_{k}
of
\mathcal{J}
are the subsets of
J_{\mathbf{u}}
. We write
J_{\emptyset}=J
for the empty word
\emptyset
.

(iii).

For each
\mathbf{u}=u_{1}\ldots u_{k}\in\Sigma^{*}
, there exists
\omega_{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Psi_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(1.6)

J_{\mathbf{u}}=\Psi_{\mathbf{u}}(J)=\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(J)+\omega_{\mathbf{u}},

where
\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}=\varphi_{u_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{u_{j}}\cdots\circ\varphi_{u_{k}}(J)
and
\varphi_{u_{j}}\in\Phi_{j}
.

We call
\Phi=\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
a
non-autonomous iterated function system
and

(1.7)

E=E(\Phi)=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{u}}(J)

the
non-autonomous attractor
of
\Phi
.

If
\#I_{k}<\infty
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we say
\Phi
is a
non-autonomous finite iterated function system
(NFIFS) and
E
is the
non-autonomous finite set
of
\Phi
. If
\#I_{k}=\infty
for infinitely many
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we say
\Phi
is a
non-autonomous infinite iterated function system
(NIIFS) and
E
is the
non-autonomous infinite set
of
\Phi
. Since the non-autonomous infinite set
E
of
\Phi
is generated by iterations of contractions, without loss of generality, we assume that
\#I_{k}=\infty
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
in
\Phi
. We do not consider the case where
\#I_{k}=\infty
for only finitely many
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Note that the attractors of non-autonomous infinite iterated function systems may not be compact.

If for all integers
k\geq 0
and
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k},
we have that

\mbox{int}(J_{\mathbf{u}i})\cap\mbox{int}(J_{\mathbf{u}j})=\emptyset,

for all
i\not=j\in I_{k+1}
, we say
\Phi
(or
E
) satisfies the
open set condition (OSC)
.
If for all integers
k\geq 0
and
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k},
we have that

J_{\mathbf{u}i}\cap J_{\mathbf{u}j}=\emptyset,

for all
i\not=j\in I_{k+1}
, we say
\Phi
(or
E
) satisfies the
strong separation condition (SSC)
.

If for each integer
k>0
, every mapping
\varphi_{k,j}
in
\Phi_{k}
is a similarity with contraction ratio
c_{k,j}
, and
\Phi
satisfies OSC, then the attractor
E
is called a
Moran set
, which was first studied by Moran
[
38
]
in 1946. In 1994, Hua first studied the Hausdorff dimension of Moran sets, and later, Hua and others showed that if

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log{\underline{c}_{k}}}{\log{M_{k}}}=0,

where
\underline{c}_{k}=\min_{1\leq j\leq\#I_{k}}\{c_{k,j}\}
and
M_{k}=\max_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\lvert J_{\mathbf{u}}\rvert
, then

(1.8)

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=s_{\ast}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}s_{k},\quad\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm P}\,E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=s^{\ast}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}s_{k},

where
s_{k}
is the unique real solution to
\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{\#I_{i}}(c_{i,j})^{s}\right)=1.

We refer readers to
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
42
]
for details. There are various generalisations of such fractals. In
[
24
]
, Gu and Miao studied the general theory of non-autonomous iterated function systems. In
[
26
]
, Holland and Zhang studied the Hausdorff dimension of a class of special non-autonomous iterated function systems and the box and packing dimension of a class of generalized non-autonomous conformal sets. We refer readers to
[
22
,
23
,
33
,
42
]
for other related works.

1.4.
Non-autonomous conformal iterated function systems.

In this paper, we mainly focus on the dimension theory of non-autonomous conformal sets.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set and
\phi:V\to V
be a conformal map. We denote by
D\phi(x)
the
derivative of
\phi
at
x
, i.e.,
D\phi(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a similarity linear map, and
we denote by
\|D\phi(x)\|
the scaling factor at
x
. We write

\|D\phi\|=\sup\{\|D\phi(x)\|:x\in J\}.

Let
\Phi
be a non-autonomous finite (or infinite) iterated function system satisfying that

(i’).

Conformality
: There exists an open connected set
V
independent of
k
with
J\subset V
such that each
\varphi_{k,j}
extends to a
C^{1}
conformal diffeomorphism of
V
to
V
.

(ii’).

Bounded distortion
: There exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
\mathbf{u}=u_{l}u_{l+1}\ldots u_{k}\in\Sigma_{l}^{k}
, the map
\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}=\varphi_{l,u_{l}}\circ\varphi_{l+1,u_{l+1}}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{k,u_{k}}
satisfies

\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(x)\|\leq C\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(y)\|

for all
x,y\in V
.

We say
\Phi=\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a
non-autonomous finite (or infinite) conformal iterated function system(NFCIFS or NICIFS)
, and we call

(1.9)

E=E(\Phi)=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}J_{\mathbf{u}}

is the
non-autonomous finite (or infinite) conformal set
of
\Phi
.

Let
\Phi
be the NFCIFS and
E
the corresponding attractor given by (
1.9
). Let

(1.10)

\begin{split}&m_{k}=\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\{\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|\},\qquad\quad M_{k}=\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\{\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|\},\\
&\underline{c}_{k}=\inf_{1\leq j\leq\#I_{k}}\{\|D\varphi_{k,j}\|\},\quad\quad\overline{c}_{k}=\sup_{1\leq j\leq\#I_{k}}\{\|D\varphi_{k,j}\|\}.\end{split}

In this paper, we study the non-autonomous finite conformal iterated function systems
\Phi
satisfying open set condition and

(1.11)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=0,

which plays a fundamental role in the study of Moran fractals; see
[
42
]
for details. In
[
39
]
, Rempe-Gillen and Urbański studied Hausdorff dimensions for non-autonomous conformal sets under the different assumption

(1.12)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\#I_{k}}{k}=0,

and they provided the Hausdorff dimension formula for such sets. However, the conditions (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) do not imply each other; see Example
1
.
Inspiring by their work, we further relax the condition (
1.11
) to

(1.13)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\overline{c}_{k}-\log\#I_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=0.

See Proposition
6.2
and Corollary
2.4
for details. Under the cone condition, we make certain generalization of the conclusions in
[
39
]
, see Corollary
7.3
and Corollary
8.1
.

Next, we provide an example to illustrate the difference to condition (
1.11
), condition (
1.13
) and condition (
1.12
).

Example 1
.

Let
E_{1}
be a Moran set with
\#I_{k}=2^{k}
and
c_{k,j}=\frac{1}{3^{k+1}}
. It is clear that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=0\quad\textit{ and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\#I_{k}}{k}=\log 2.

Then by (
1.8
), we have
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E_{1}=\frac{\log 2}{\log 3}.

Let
E_{2}
be a Moran set with
\#I_{k}=2^{k}
,
c_{k,1}={\frac{1}{3^{k+1}}}
and
c_{k,j}={\frac{1}{3^{k(k+1)}}}(2\leq j\leq 2^{k})
. It is straightforward that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\underline{c}_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=2,\quad\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\#I_{k}}{k}=\log 2,\qquad\quad\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\overline{c}_{k}-\log\#I_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=0.

By Theorem
2.4
, we have
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E_{2}=0
.

Let
E_{3}
be a homogeneous Moran set with
\#I_{k}=2
,
c_{1}={\frac{1}{2}}
,
c_{2}={\frac{1}{4}}
and
c_{k}=c_{1}c_{2}\cdots c_{k-1}(k\geq 3)
. Then it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\#I_{k}}{k}=0\quad\textit{ and}\quad\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\overline{c}_{k}-\log\#I_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=\frac{1}{2}.

By simple calculation, we have
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E_{3}=0
.

2.
Main conclusions

Let
E
be the non-autonomous conformal set of the nonautonomous finite iterated function system
\Phi
, that is,
\#I_{k}<\infty
for all
k>0
. First, we give intermediate dimension spectra formula for
E
.
For real
\delta>0
and
\theta\in(0,1]
, let

\mathcal{M}(\delta,\theta)=\{\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{M}\textit{ is a cut set satisfying }\delta^{\frac{1}{\theta}}<|J_{\mathbf{u}^{*}}|\textit{ and }|J_{\mathbf{u}}|\leq\delta\textit{ for each }\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{M}\},

and we write

k_{\delta}=\min\{|\mathbf{u}|:|J_{\mathbf{u}}|\leq\delta<|J_{\mathbf{u}^{*}}|,\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}\}.

For
\theta\in(0,1]
, upper and lower pressure functions are given by

(2.14)

\begin{split}\overline{P}(t,\theta)&=\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{k_{\delta}}\log\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{M}(\delta,\theta)}\{\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{M}}\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|^{t}\},\\
\underline{P}(t,\theta)&=\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{k_{\delta}}\log\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{M}(\delta,\theta)}\{\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{M}}\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|^{t}\}).\end{split}

We write their jump points respectively as

(2.15)

\begin{split}s^{\theta}&=\inf\{t:\overline{P}(t,\theta)<0\}=\sup\{t:\overline{P}(t,\theta)>0\},\\
s_{\theta}&=\inf\{t:\underline{P}(t,\theta)<0\}=\sup\{t:\underline{P}(t,\theta)>0\}.\end{split}

Note that the existence of
s^{\theta}
and
s_{\theta}
follows from the monotonicity of
\underline{P}(t)
and
\overline{P}(t)
; see Lemma
4.1
in section
4
.
When
\theta=0
, we write

s_{0}=s^{0}=\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
E
be the non-autonomous finite conformal set satisfying (
1.11
) and OSC.
Then upper and lower intermediate dimension of
E
are given by

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E=s^{\theta},\qquad\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm\theta}\,E=s_{\theta}\qquad\textit{for }\theta\in[0,1],

where
s^{\theta}
and
s_{\theta}
are given by (
2.15
).

The box and Hausdorff dimensions of non-autonomous finite conformal sets may be given in a simpler way. We define upper and lower pressures by

(2.16)

\overline{P}(t)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|^{t},\qquad\underline{P}(t)=\lim\inf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|^{t}.

We write their jump points respectively as

(2.17)

\begin{split}s^{*}&=\inf\{t:\overline{P}(t)<0\}=\sup\{t:\overline{P}(t)>0\},\\
s_{*}&=\inf\{t:\underline{P}(t)<0\}=\sup\{t:\underline{P}(t)>0\}.\end{split}

The next conclusion shows that the critical value
s^{*}
always gives the box and packing dimensions of non-autonomous finite conformal sets.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
E
be the non-autonomous finite conformal set satisfying (
1.11
) and OSC.
Then the box and packing dimensions of
E
are given by

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm P}\,E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=s^{*},

where
s^{*}
is given by (
2.17
).

In Section
6
, we change the condition (
1.11
) in Theorem
2.2
to (
1.13
), and obtain some partial results. See Proposition
6.2
.

Generally, Hausdorff dimensions of non-autonomous finite conformal sets are not easy to calculate. We obtain Hausdorff dimensions under extra conditions.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
E
be the non-autonomous finite conformal set with
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\partial J)=0
satisfying (
1.11
) and OSC. Then the Hausdorff dimension is given by

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=s_{*},

where
s_{*}
is given by (
2.17
).

Next, we weaken the condition (
1.11
) in Theorem
2.3
to (
1.13
).

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
E
be a non-autonomous finite conformal set with
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\partial J)=0
satisfying (
1.13
) and OSC. Then the Hausdorff dimension of
E
is given by

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=s_{*},

where
s_{*}
is given by (
2.17
).

Remark 1
.

If
J
is a convex set,
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\partial J)=0
holds, for example
J=[0,1]^{d}
.

Remark 2
.

If
E
satisfies the strong separation condition, condition
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\partial J)=0
may be omitted.

Non-autonomous infinite conformal sets are more awkward to study, and we extend our conclusions to such sets with the following assumption instead of (
1.11
),

(2.18)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\overline{c}_{k}}{\log M_{k}}=0.

For a NICIFS
\Phi
, let

M_{k}=\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\{\|D\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}\|\},\quad\overline{c}_{k}=\sup_{j\in I_{k}}\{\|D\varphi_{k,j}\|\}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
E
be the non-autonomous infinite conformal set satisfying (
2.18
) and OSC.
Suppose for all
t\in(0,d)
and all
\epsilon>0
, the following hold

(1).

The sums
\sum_{j\in I_{k}}\|D\varphi_{k,j}\|^{t}
are either infinite for all
k
or finite for all
k
.

(2).

If
\sum_{j\in I_{k}}\|D\varphi_{k,j}\|^{t}<\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k\leq{M_{n}^{-\epsilon}}}\|D\varphi_{n,k}\|^{t}}{\sum_{j\in I_{n}}\|D\varphi_{n,j}\|^{t}}=1.

(3).

If
\sum_{j\in I_{k}}\|D\varphi_{k,j}\|^{t}=\infty
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\leq{M_{n}^{-\epsilon}}}\|D\varphi_{n,k}\|^{t}=\infty.

Let
s^{*}
and
s_{*}
be given by (
2.17
). Then
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\geq\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm P}\,E\geq s^{*}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\partial J)=0
,
then
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=s_{*}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we study the general properties of non-autonomous IFS and estimate the dimensions of non-autonomous fractals. In Section
4
, we study the properties of pressure functions, which are essential to study the dimensions of non-autonomous conformal sets. In Section
5
, we give the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Theorem
2.2
is proved in Section
6
. In Section
7
, we prove Theorem
2.3
, and relax the condition (
1.11
) to (
1.13
). Finally, we extend our conclusions to non-autonomous infinite conformal fractals in Section
8
.

3.
Dimension estimate of non-autonomous sets

In this section, we study the properties of non-autonomous iterated function systems defined in Subsection
1.3
, and these properties are useful to explore the non-autonomous conformal fractals.

3.1.
Symbolic space and pressure functions

We write

\Sigma^{\infty}=\{\mathbf{u}=u_{1}u_{2}\ldots u_{k}\ldots:u_{k}\in I_{k}\,k=1,2,\ldots\}

for the set of words with infinity length, and we topologize
\Sigma^{\infty}
using the metric

d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=2^{-|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}
for distinct
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
to make
\Sigma^{\infty}
into a compact metric space. Given an integer
n>0
, for every
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
, we write
\mathbf{u}|_{n}=u_{1}\ldots u_{n}
.
For each
\mathbf{u}=u_{1}\dots u_{k}\in\Sigma^{k}
, we write
\mathbf{u}^{*}=u_{1}\dots u_{k-1}
. Given
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{l}
, for
\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{k}
where
k\geq l
or
\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
, we write
\mathbf{u}\prec\mathbf{v}
if
u_{i}=v_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,l
.

We define the
cylinders

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{u}}=\{\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{\infty}:\mathbf{u}\prec\mathbf{v}\}
for
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}
; the set of cylinders
\{\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}\}
forms a base of open and closed neighborhoods for
\Sigma^{\infty}
. We term a subset
A
of
\Sigma^{*}
a

cut set
if
\Sigma^{\infty}\subset\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in A}\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{u}}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{u}}\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{v}}=\emptyset
for all
\mathbf{u}\neq\mathbf{v}\in A
. It is equivalent to that, for every
\mathbf{w}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
, there is a unique word
\mathbf{u}\in A
with
|\mathbf{u}|<\infty
such that
\mathbf{u}\prec\mathbf{w}
.

Let
\pi_{\Phi}:\Sigma^{\infty}\to J
by

\pi_{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{\mathbf{u}|_{n}},

where
J_{\mathbf{u}|_{n}}
is given by (
1.6
).

We cite König’s lemma in graph theory, and see
[
41
]
for details.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T
be a subset of
\Sigma^{*}
such that
\#T=\infty
and
\emptyset\in T
. Suppose that for each
\mathbf{u}\in T
, the set
Pr_{T}(\mathbf{u})=\{\mathbf{v}\in T:\mathbf{v}\prec\mathbf{u}\}
and the set

succ_{T}(\mathbf{u})=\{\mathbf{v}\in T:\mathbf{u}\prec\mathbf{v}\textit{ and }|\mathbf{v}|=|\mathbf{u}|+1\}

are finite. Then there exists
\mathbf{w}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
such that
\mathbf{w}|_{n}\in T
for each
n>0
.

Given a non-autonomous iterated function system
\Phi
, we say that
\Phi
satisfies the finite overlap condition (FOC) if for all
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}
and all
x\in J_{\mathbf{u}}
, we have

\#\{i\in I_{|\mathbf{u}|+1}:x\in J_{\mathbf{u}i}\}<\infty.

Next, under the finite overlap condition, we use
\pi_{\Phi}
to give another representation of non-autonomous attractors.

Lemma 3.2
.

If a non-autonomous iterated function system
\Phi
satisfies the finite overlap condition, then the attractor
E
of
\Phi
is the image of
\pi
, that is

E=\pi_{\Phi}(\Sigma^{\infty})=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}J_{\mathbf{u}}.

Proof.

For each
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
, we have
\pi_{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{\mathbf{u}|_{n}}
. Since
J_{\mathbf{u}|_{n}}\subset\bigcup_{\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{n}}J_{\mathbf{v}},

it follows that
\pi_{\Phi}(\mathbf{u})\in E
and
\pi_{\Phi}(\Sigma^{\infty})\subset E
.

For each
x\in E
, let
T=\Sigma(x)=\{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}:x\in J_{\mathbf{u}}\}.
Since for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}
such that
x\in J_{\mathbf{u}}
, we have
\#T=\infty
. For each
\mathbf{u}\in T
, it is clear that
Pr_{T}(\mathbf{u})=\{\mathbf{u}|_{k}:1\leq k\leq|\mathbf{u}|\}
is finite. By finite overlap condition, we have that
\#succ_{T}(\mathbf{u})=\#\{i\in I_{|\mathbf{u}|+1}:x\in J_{\mathbf{u}i}\}<\infty,
and by Lemma
3.1
, there exists
\mathbf{w}\in\Sigma^{\infty}
such that
\mathbf{w}|_{n}\in T
for each
n>0
. It implies that
x\in J_{\mathbf{w}|_{n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x=\pi_{\Phi}(\mathbf{w})
, and we have
E\subset\pi_{\Phi}(\Sigma^{\infty})
.
∎

Remark 3
.

If
\Phi
is a non-autonomous finite iterated function system, the finite overlap condition automatically holds, but the finite overlap condition is not necessarily satisfied for a non-autonomous infinite iterated function system.

Let
\Phi
be the non-autonomous finite or infinite iterated function system satisfying OSC, and
E
the attractor of
\Phi
given by (
1.9
). Given
k\geq l\geq 0
, for each
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma_{l}^{k}
, let

\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}=\sup_{x,y\in J}\frac{|\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(x)-\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(y)|}{|x-y|}\quad\textit{ and}\quad\underline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}=\inf_{x,y\in J}\frac{|\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(x)-\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(y)|}{|x-y|}.

It is clear that

(3.19)

\underline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}|x-y|\leq|\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(x)-\varphi_{\mathbf{u}}(y)|\leq\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}|x-y|.

We define upper and lower pressures respectively by

(3.20)

\overline{P}(t)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}\quad\textit{ and}\quad\underline{P}(t)=\lim\inf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t},

and both of them are strictly monotonous.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
t_{1}<t_{2}
, if
\overline{P}(t_{1})
and
\overline{P}(t_{2})
are finite, then
\overline{P}(t_{1})>\overline{P}(t_{2})
.

Proof.

Given
t_{1}<t_{2}
such that
\overline{P}(t_{1})
and
\overline{P}(t_{2})
are finite. By (
1.5
), we have

\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t_{2}}\leq c^{n(t_{2}-t_{1})}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t_{1}},

where
c
is given by (
1.5
), and it follows that
\overline{P}(t_{2})\leq\overline{P}(t_{1})-(t_{2}-t_{1})\log\frac{1}{c}.

Hence we have
\overline{P}(t_{2})<\overline{P}(t_{1})
since
0<c<1
.
∎

3.2.
Dimensions estimation of non-autonomous fractals

Let
\overline{P}(t)
and
\underline{P}(t)
be the pressure functions given by (
3.20
). By Lemma
3.3
, we write their jump points respectively as

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}s^{*}&=&\inf\{t:\overline{P}(t)<0\}=\sup\{t:\overline{P}(t)>0\},\\
s_{*}&=&\inf\{t:\underline{P}(t)<0\}=\sup\{t:\underline{P}(t)>0\}.\end{split}

Since these are consistent with the critical values defined in (
2.17
), we use the same notation for simplicity.
Let

(3.22)

\displaystyle t^{*}=\inf\{t:\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}<\infty\}.

Generally,
s_{*}
serves as an upper bound for the Hausdorff dimension of non-autonomous attractors,
t^{*}
as an upper bound for the box dimension of non-autonomous attractors, and
s^{*}
as a lower bound for the box dimension of non-autonomous attractors. For some special cases, we may have
s^{*}=t^{*}
, and see Lemma
6.1
for details.

Theorem 3.4
.

Given a non-autonomous finite or infinite iterated function system
\Phi
satisfying OSC, let
E
be the corresponding non-autonomous set given by (
1.9
) and
s_{*}
given by (
3.21
). Then

\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E\leq s_{*}.

If
\#I_{k}
is finite for all
k>0
, and there exists a constant
N>0
such that

(3.23)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\#I_{k}}{\log(-\log\max_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k-1}}\{\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}\})}\leq N,

then

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,\leq t^{*},

where
t^{*}
is given by (
3.22
).

Proof.

For every
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}
, by (
3.19
), we have
|J_{\mathbf{u}}|\leq\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}|J|.
For each real
t>s_{*}
, we have
\underline{P}(t)<0
, and by (
3.20
), there exists
\{n_{k}\}
such that for each
k
,

|J|^{-t}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n_{k}}}|J_{\mathbf{u}}|^{t}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n_{k}}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}<e^{\frac{\underline{P}(t)}{2}n_{k}}.

Let
\delta_{k}=\max\{|J_{\mathbf{u}}|:\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}\}
. Thus the preHausdorff measure of
E
is bounded by

\mathcal{H}^{t}_{\delta_{n_{k}}}(E)\leq|J|^{t}e^{\frac{\underline{P}(t)}{2}n_{k}},

and the Hausdorff measure of
E
is zero. Hence,
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E\leq t
for all
t>s_{*}
, and we obtain that
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E\leq s_{*}
.

Suppose that
\#I_{k}
is finite for all
k>0
. Next, we prove
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq t^{*}
. For every
s>t^{*}
, by (
3.22
), it is clear that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{s}<\infty.

For all sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we write

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\delta)=\{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}:\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}\leq\delta<\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}^{*}}\}.

Denote by
N_{\delta}(E)
the smallest number of sets with diameters at most
\delta
covering the set
E
, and it is clear that
N_{\delta}(E)\leq\#\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\delta)
.

We write

\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}(\delta)=\{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{*}:\textit{ there exists $j$ such that }\mathbf{u}j\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\delta)\},

and note that
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}(\delta)
may not be a cut set. Let

(3.24)

N(\delta)=\max\{\#I_{k}:\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\delta)\cap\Sigma^{k}\neq\emptyset\textit{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}.

Then we have
\#\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\delta)\leq N(\delta)\#\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}(\delta),
and it follows that

N_{\delta}(E)\delta^{s}\leq N(\delta)\#\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}(\delta)\delta^{s}\leq N(\delta)\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}(\delta)}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{s}\leq N(\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{s}.

Thus

\frac{\log N_{\delta}(E)}{-\log\delta}\leq s+\frac{\log\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{k}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{s}}{-\log\delta}+\frac{\log N(\delta)}{-\log\delta}.

Since
\delta<\max_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{|\mathbf{u}|-1}}\{\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}\}
for every
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\delta)
, by (
3.23
), we obtain that

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\#I_{|\mathbf{u}|}}{-\log\delta}<\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\#I_{|\mathbf{u}|}}{\log(-\log\max_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{|\mathbf{u}|-1}}\{\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}\})}\frac{\log(-\log\delta)}{(-\log\delta)}=0.

By (
3.24
), this implies that
\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log N(\delta)}{-\log\delta}=0
.
Therefore,
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq s
for all
s>t^{*}
, and the conclusion holds.
∎

3.3.
Non-autonomous infinite iterated function systems

Given a non-autonomous iterated function system
\Phi=\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
.
In this subsection, we assume that there exists a constant
D>0
such that for all
k\geq l\geq 0
and all
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma_{l}^{k}
,

(3.25)

\frac{\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}}{\underline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}}\leq D,

where
\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}
and
\underline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}
are given by (
3.19
). We say
\Phi^{\prime}=\{\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a
subsystem of
\Phi
if
I^{\prime}_{k}\subset I_{k}
and
\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\subset\Phi_{k}
for all
k>0
, and we write
\overline{P}^{\prime}(t)
and
\underline{P}^{\prime}(t)
for the upper and lower pressure functions of the subsystem
\Phi^{\prime}
.

Generally, it is difficult to calculate the dimensions of non-autonomous sets. Therefore, we adopt the method of approximating the infinite system with a finite subsystem to obtain an estimation of the dimension of the non-autonomous infinite iterated function system. The following conclusion is inspired by
[
39
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Phi
be a non-autonomous finite or infinite iterated function system satisfying (
3.25
). Given a real
t\in[0,d]
, assume that
\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}<\infty
for all
n
.
Given
\delta>0
and, for each
n
, let
I^{\prime}_{n}\subset I_{n}
be a finite index set such that

(3.26)

\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}\leq(1+\delta)\sum_{k\in I^{\prime}_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t},

for all sufficiently large
n
.
Let
\Phi^{\prime}
be the subsystem given by
I^{\prime}_{n}
. Then

\overline{P}^{\prime}(t)\geq\overline{P}(t)-\delta D^{2d},\qquad\underline{P}^{\prime}(t)\geq\underline{P}(t)-\delta D^{2d}.

Proof.

Fix
n>0
, and for each
s=0,1,\ldots,n
, we write

\mathcal{P}_{s}=\{P\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}:\#{P}=s\}.

For every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}
, we divide
P
into consecutive parts and write

\displaystyle I^{*}_{P}=\{m(m+1)\cdots(j-1)j(j+1)\cdots l:\textit{ for $m,l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\textit{ s.t. }m-1\notin P,l+1\notin P\textit{ and }j\in P\textit{ }\forall j=m,\ldots,l\}

and

\Sigma^{n}(P)=\{\mathbf{u}=u_{1}\ldots u_{n}\in\Sigma^{n}:u_{i}\in I^{\prime}_{i}\textit{ for }i\in P,u_{j}\notin I^{\prime}_{i}\textit{ for }j\notin P\}.

For each
\mathbf{u}\in I_{P}
, let

\Sigma^{n}(P,\mathbf{u})=\{\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}|_{m}^{l}:m(m+1)\cdots l\in I_{P}^{*}\}.

Let
k=\#I^{*}_{P}
. It is clear that
k-1\leq n-s
.
Recall (
3.19
), and by (
3.25
), it follows that for each
\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}

\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}\leq\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{n}(P,\mathbf{u})}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{v}}\prod_{j\notin P}\overline{c}_{u_{j}}\leq D^{k+n-s-1}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}\leq D^{2n-2s}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}.

We write
{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{n}=\{u_{1}u_{2}\ldots u_{n}:u_{j}\in I_{j}^{\prime},j=1,2,\ldots,n\}
. By (
3.26
), this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}(P)}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}(P)}\Big(\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{n}(P,\mathbf{u})}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{v}}\prod_{j\notin P}\overline{c}_{u_{j}}\Big)^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}(P)}\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{n}(P,\mathbf{u})}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{v}}^{t}\Big)\Big(\prod_{j\notin P}\sum_{u_{j}\in I_{j}-I_{j}^{\prime}}\overline{c}_{u_{j}}^{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{n-s}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}(P)}\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma^{n}(P,\mathbf{u})}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{v}}^{t}\Big)\Big(\prod_{j\notin P}\sum_{u_{j}\in I_{j}^{\prime}}\overline{c}_{u_{j}}^{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{n-s}D^{2t(n-s)}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{n}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}.

Since the number of elements in
\mathcal{P}_{s}
is
C_{n}^{s}
and
t\leq d
, it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\Sigma^{n}(P)}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}}\delta^{n-s}D^{2t(n-s)}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{n}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta D^{2d})^{n}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{n}}\overline{c}_{\mathbf{u}}^{t}.

By (
2.16
), we obtain that

\underline{P}^{\prime}(t)\geq\underline{P}(t)-\log(1+\delta D^{2d})\geq\underline{P}(t)-\delta D^{2d}.

Similarly, we have that
\overline{P}^{\prime}(t)\geq\overline{P}(t)-\delta D^{2d}
, and the conclusion holds.
∎

Under certain conditions, a non-autonomous infinite iterated function system has a finite subsystem with identical pressure functions, and we apply this property to control the number of contractions in subsystem. The following conclusion is inspired by
[
39
]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
E
be a non-autonomous infinite set of
\Phi
satisfying (
3.25
). Given a real sequence
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
convergent to zero. Suppose that
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the following for all
t\in(0,d)
and all
\epsilon>0
:

(1).

The sum
\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}
are either infinite for all
n
or finite for all
n
.

(2).

If
\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}<\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k\leq{\beta_{n}^{-\epsilon}}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}{\sum_{j\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,j}^{t}}=1.

(3).

If
\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}=\infty
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\leq{\beta_{n}^{-\epsilon}}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}=\infty.

Then there exists a subsystem
\Phi^{\prime}
of
\Phi
such that
\overline{P}^{\prime}(t)=\overline{P}(t)
and
\underline{P}^{\prime}(t)=\underline{P}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\#I_{k}^{\prime}}{\log\beta_{n}}=0.

Let
E^{\prime}
be the non-autonomous set of
\Phi^{\prime}
, and let
s_{*}^{\prime}
and
{s^{*}}^{\prime}
be given by (
3.21
) with respect to
\Phi^{\prime}
. If
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E^{\prime}=s_{*}^{\prime}
, then
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm H}\,E=s_{*}^{\prime}=s_{*}.
If
\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm P}\,E^{\prime}\geq{s^{*}}^{\prime}
, then

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\geq\mbox{\rm dim}_{\rm P}\,E\geq{s^{*}}^{\prime}=s^{*}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\overline{c}_{k,i}\geq\overline{c}_{k,j}
for all
i\geq j
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
h=\min\{d,\sup\{t:\sum_{j\in I_{k}}\overline{c}_{k,j}^{t}=+\infty\}\}
. First, we consider
h<d
. Arbitrarily choose
t>h
, and by
(1)
, we have that
\sum_{j\in I_{k}}\overline{c}_{k,j}^{t}
is finite for all integers
k>0
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. For each integer
i>0
, setting
\epsilon=\frac{1}{i}
, by (2), there exists an integer
N_{i}>0
such that for every
n>N_{i}
, we have

\frac{\sum_{k\leq{\beta_{n}^{-\frac{1}{i}}}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}{\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}>\frac{1}{1+\delta}.

We choose
\{N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
to be an increasing sequence.
Let
I^{t,\delta}_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,[\beta_{n}^{-\frac{1}{i}}]\}
for
N_{i}<n\leq N_{i+1}
. Let
\Psi_{t,\delta}
be the subsystem generated by
I^{t,\delta}_{n}
, and it is clear that

(3.27)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log\#(I^{t,\delta}_{n})}{\log\beta_{n}}=0,

and
(1+\delta)\sum_{k\in I^{t,\delta}_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}>\sum_{k\in I_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}.

Let
\delta=\frac{1}{j}
. By (
3.27
), there exists an increasing sequence
\{K_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that for
n>K_{j}
we have

\frac{\log\#(I^{t,\frac{1}{j}}_{n})}{\log\beta_{n}}<\frac{1}{j}.

Let
I^{t}_{n}=I^{t,\frac{1}{j}}_{n}
for
K_{j}<n\leq K_{j+1}
. Then we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log\#(I^{t}_{n})}{\log\beta_{n}}=0\quad\textit{ and }\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k\notin I^{t}_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}{\sum_{k\in I^{t}_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}=0.

Let
\Psi_{t}
be the subsystem generated by
I^{t}_{n}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d>t^{\prime}>t>h
, since
\frac{\overline{c}_{n,k}}{\overline{c}_{n,\#I_{n}^{t}}}\geq 1
for
k\in I^{t}_{n}
and
\frac{\overline{c}_{n,k}}{\overline{c}_{n,\#I_{n}^{t}}}\leq 1
for
k\notin I^{t}_{n}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k\notin I^{t}_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}{\sum_{k\in I^{t}_{n}}\overline{c}_{n,k}^{t}}

\displaystyle=

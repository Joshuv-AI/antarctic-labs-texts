SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Abstract

    The School District Review Program (SDRP) is sponsored by the U.S. 
Department of Education's National Center for Education Statistics 
(NCES) and is conducted annually by the U.S. Census Bureau (Census 
Bureau). It plays a critical role in determining each state's Title I 
allocation under the Elementary and Secondary Education Act, as amended 
by the Every Student Succeeds Act of 2015 (Pub. L. 114-95). School 
district data submitted through the SDRP combined with decennial census 
counts, Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates, and current population 
estimates inform the Census Bureau's estimates of children ages 5 to 17 
living in poverty for each district. The U.S. Department of Education 
uses these estimates to distribute more than $18 billion in Title I 
funds each year.
    The SDRP includes the review of Type 1, Type 2, and Type 3 school 
districts as defined by the NCES. Type 1 is a local school district 
that is not a component of a supervisory union. Type 2 is a local 
school district component of a supervisory union sharing a 
superintendent and administrative services with other local school 
districts. Type 3 is an education agency that performs administrative 
services for more than one school district, providing a common 
superintendent for participating districts.
    SDRP participants include mapping coordinators and Title I 
Coordinators from all fifty states and the District of Columbia. 
Mapping coordinators, designated by state education agencies, review 
and update school district boundaries, names, grade ranges, levels, 
federal school district local education agency codes, and geographic 
relationships. Title I Coordinators oversee the program and review all 
materials.

II. Method of Collection

    The SDRP consists of the Annotation Phase and Verification Phase. 
During the Annotation Phase, the Census Bureau provides mapping 
coordinators with its current school district boundary and attribute 
data. Mapping coordinators work with local school district, county, and 
state agency officials to review and update this information. Changes 
are submitted using the Geographic Update Partnership Software (GUPS), 
GUPS Web, and the digital submission log. The Census Bureau then 
reviews the submissions and updates the Master Address File/
Topologically Integrated Geographic Encoding and Referencing (MAF/
TIGER) System.
    During the Verification Phase, the Census Bureau issues updated 
materials reflecting the processed changes. Mapping coordinators 
confirm whether updates were applied correctly and report any 
corrections, if necessary. New changes are not accepted during the 
Verification Phase.

Annual Schedule

     The Annotation Phase occurs every year in December/
January.
     The Annotation Phase submission deadline occurs every year 
in March.
     The Verification Phase occurs every year in May, with a 
one-week review period.

IV. Request for Comments

    We are soliciting public comments to permit the Department/Bureau 
to: (a) Evaluate whether the proposed information collection is 
necessary for the proper functions of the Department, including whether 
the information will have practical utility; (b) Evaluate the accuracy 
of our estimate of the time and cost burden for this proposed 
collection, including the validity of the methodology and assumptions 
used; (c) Evaluate ways to enhance the quality, utility, and clarity of 
the information to be collected; and (d) Minimize the reporting burden 
on those who are to respond, including the use of automated collection 
techniques or other forms of information technology.
    Comments that you submit in response to this notice are a matter of 
public record. We will include, or summarize, each comment in our 
request to OMB to approve this ICR. Before including your address, 
phone number, email address, or other personal identifying information 
in your comment, you should be aware that your entire comment--
including your personal identifying information--may be made publicly 
available at any time. While you may ask us in your comment to withhold 
your personal identifying information from public review, we cannot 
guarantee that we will be able to do so.

Abstract
We study a family of processes generated according to sequential probability assignments induced by the LZ78 universal compressor. We characterize entropic and distributional properties such as their entropy and relative entropy rates, finite-state compressibility and log loss of their realizations, and the empirical distributions that they induce. Though not quite stationary, these sources are “almost stationary and ergodic;” similar to stationary and ergodic processes, they satisfy a Shannon-McMillan-Breiman-type property: the normalized log probability of their realizations converges almost surely to their entropy rate. Further, they are locally “almost i.i.d.” in the sense that the finite-dimensional empirical distributions of their realizations converge almost surely to a deterministic i.i.d. law. However, unlike stationary ergodic sources, the finite-state compressibility of their realizations is almost surely strictly larger than their entropy rate by a “Jensen gap”. We present simulations demonstrating the theoretical results. Among their potential uses, these sources allow to gauge the performance of sequential probability models on non-Markovian non-stationary data.



I
Introduction

The celebrated LZ78 compression algorithm
[
1
]
has been studied extensively and proven an indispensable tool in developing universal schemes,
i.e.
, those that work for arbitrary, deterministic input sequences.
In addition to universal compression, it has been used in settings such as gambling
[
2
]
, sequence prediction
[
3
]
, noisy channel decoding
[
4
]
, filtering
[
5
]
, and probability modeling
[
6
]
.

Cf.

[
7
]
for a review of LZ78-based universal schemes.

LZ78, however, has yet to be thoroughly investigated as a probability source.
In this paper, we start to fill this gap by characterizing the basic entropic and probabilistic properties of a family of sources generated from the respective family of LZ78 Sequential Probability Assignments (SPAs) introduced recently in
[
6
]
.
Specifically, we determine the entropy rate of such sources and establish the almost sure convergence of a realization’s normalized log probability to this entropy rate (a Shannon-McMillan-Breiman type result).
In addition, we show that this source is highly non-Markovian: the log loss of any finite-context probability model on the realized source sequence—and therefore also its finite-state compressibility—almost surely approach another, higher value.

Key to our results on the entropic properties are results of independent interest pertaining to the empirical distribution of realizations from these sources.
Despite their non-trivial dependence structure, they are locally “almost i.i.d.” in the sense that the finite-dimensional empirical distributions of their realizations converge almost surely to a deterministic i.i.d. law.
This property is reminiscent of that of good channel and source codes (
cf.

[
8
,
9
]
, and references therein).

These LZ78 sources provide us with new non-Markovian non-stationary laws for which the entropic properties—and thus the fundamental limits of compression and sequential probability modeling—are now known.
As such, we expect they will be useful for studying performance tradeoffs in sequential probability and online models, both information-theoretic and deep-learning based.

In addition,
[
10
]
recently presented a universal lossy compression scheme via a random codebook in which codewords are drawn with log probability proportional to their LZ78 codelength.
As we make precise in
Section
VI
, one can equivalently generate the codewords from the LZ78 source, so this source can be useful for universal lossy compression.

In

, we define the LZ78 source.
We present our main theoretical results pertaining to it in
Section
III
.

Section

introduces notation for the remainder of the paper and provides a roadmap for the proofs of the main results.
In
Section
V
, we present and sketch proofs of auxiliary results that are essential to characterizing the source’s entropic properties.
Specifically, we characterize the asymptotics of the empirical measure of
r
-tuples emitted by the source.

Section
VI
sketches proofs of the main results from
Section

: the LZ78 source’s entropy rate, asymptotic log loss, the fundamental limit of finite-state probability models on realizations of the source, and the relative entropy between the source and stationary Markovian Laws.
In

We conclude and suggest future directions in

Remark II.7

(Non-Stationarity of the LZ78 Source)
.

This probability law is not stationary.
E.g.
, consider the case of a binary alphabet and an arbitrary distribution
\Uppi
over
[0,1]
.
The distributions of
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
, and
B_{3}
generated from the LZ78 source are:

1.

t=1
: We start at the root, so
B_{1}=\Uptheta_{0}
.
A new leaf is generated based on
X_{1}\sim B_{1}
.

2.

t=2
: We are again at the root, so
B_{2}=\Uptheta_{0}
.
A new leaf is added if
X_{1}\neq X_{2}
, which happens, conditioned on
\Uptheta_{0}
, with probability
\alpha(\Uptheta_{0})\triangleq 2\Uptheta_{0}(1-\Uptheta_{0})
.

3.

t=3
: If a new leaf was added at
t=2
, we are at the root and
B_{3}=\Uptheta_{0}
.
Otherwise,
B_{3}=\Uptheta_{1}
.

Thus,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are both
\sim\Uppi
.
However, even though
\Uptheta_{0}
and
\Uptheta_{1}
were generated i.i.d.
\Uppi
,
B_{3}
does not (for general
\Uppi
) have distribution
\Uppi
.
Indeed, for an arbitrary function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, as
\Uptheta_{0}
and
\Uptheta_{1}
are independent,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(B_{3})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[f(\Uptheta_{0})\alpha(\Uptheta_{0})]+\mathbb{E}[f(\Uptheta_{1})(1-\alpha(\Uptheta_{0}))]=\mathbb{E}[f(\Uptheta_{0})\alpha(\Uptheta_{0})]+\mathbb{E}[f(\Uptheta_{1})](1-\mathbb{E}[\alpha(\Uptheta_{0})]),

which is equal to
\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[f(\Uptheta)]
iff
\mathrm{cov}(f(\Uptheta_{0}),\alpha(\Uptheta_{0}))=0
,
i.e.
,
f(\Uptheta_{0})
and
\alpha(\Uptheta_{0})
are uncorrelated.
The covariance is not zero for general

and
\Uppi
(for instance, set

and let
\Uppi
be any non-trivial law over
[0,1]
).
This non-stationarity of the
\mathbf{B}
process is readily seen to translate to non-stationarity of the
\mathbf{X}
process.

III
Main Results: Entropic Properties of the LZ78 Source

The LZ78 source of
Construction
II.5
, under benign conditions on
\Uppi
, has the following properties:

1.

Its entropy rate is
\mathbb{E}[H(\Uptheta)]
, where
\Uptheta\sim\Uppi
and
H(\cdot)
is the Shannon entropy.

2.

The normalized log probability of
X^{n}
drawn from the LZ78 source converges almost surely to its entropy rate.

3.

The finite-state compressibility and finite-state SPA log-loss of
\mathbf{X}
is almost surely equal to
H(\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta])
.

Evidently, on one hand, similarly to a stationary and ergodic process, the normalized log probability of a realization from the LZ78 source converges almost surely to its entropy rate.
On the other hand, it is unlike a stationary ergodic source, whose finite-state compressibility almost surely coincides with its entropy rate
[
1
]
.
We now proceed toward a formal statement of these and related properties.

We can now state the main results, deferring further discussion and proof sketches to
Section
VI
.

Theorem III.1

(A Shannon-McMillan-Breiman-Type Result)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be generated from the LZ78 source
Q^{\text{LZ},\Uppi}
with
\supp(\Uppi)=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
.
Then, almost surely,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{Q^{\text{LZ},\Uppi}_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}=\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[H(\Uptheta)].

Corollary III.2

(Entropy Rate)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be generated from the LZ78 source, where
\supp(\Uppi)=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
.
Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H(X^{n})=\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[H(\Uptheta)].

Definition III.3

(Markov Sequential Probability Assignment Log Loss)
.

For any infinite sequence
\mathbf{x}
, the optimal log-loss of a Markovian probability model is

\mu(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{k}(x^{n})\triangleq\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\hat{H}_{X_{k+1}|X^{k}}(x^{n}),

where
\hat{H}_{X_{k+1}|X^{k}}(x^{n})
is the
k
th
-order empirical conditional entropy of
x^{n}
.

Note that, for any individual sequence
\mathbf{x}
,
\mu(\mathbf{x})
is also equal to the optimal finite-state SPA log loss which, in turn, is also equal to its finite-state compressibility
[
1
,
6
]
.

Theorem III.4

(Optimal Finite-State and Markov Model Log Loss on
\mathbf{X}
)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be generated from the LZ78 source.
Then, almost surely,

\mu(\mathbf{X})=H(\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[\Uptheta]).

Evidently, the finite-state compressibility of
\mathbf{X}
is a “Jensen gap” larger than its entropy rate.

Remark III.5

(Mutual Information Interpretation of the Jensen Gap)
.

Consider
(\Uptheta,Y)
jointly distributed as follows:
\Uptheta\sim\Uppi
and, conditioned on
\Uptheta
,
Y\sim\Uptheta
. Then

\displaystyle H(Y|\Uptheta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[H(\Uptheta)],

\displaystyle H(Y)

\displaystyle=H(\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[\Uptheta]),

and, therefore, the “Jensen gap” is

H(\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[\Uptheta])-\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[H(\Uptheta)]=H(Y)-H(Y|\Uptheta)=I(\Uptheta;Y).

To motivate our next result, we recall that, under benign conditions on
\Uppi
, among the results of

[

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}D\left(P_{X^{n}}\big\lVert Q_{X^{n}}^{\text{LZ},\Uppi}\right)=0\ \ \ \forall P\mbox{ stationary}.

It’s natural to wonder about the behavior of the discrepancy between these laws when measured under the relative entropy in the other direction.

\mu(\mathbf{X})=H(\mathbb{E}_{\Uptheta\sim\Uppi}[\Uptheta]).

Rearranging terms,

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{P_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}-\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{Q^{\text{LZ},\Uppi}_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{P_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}-\mathbb{E}[H(\Uptheta)]\quad(\text{a.s.}),

by
Theorem
III.1
.
We now must evaluate the first term of the right-hand side.
As
P
is
k
-order Markov,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{P_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=k+1}^{n}\log\frac{1}{P_{X_{0}|X_{-k}^{-1}}(X_{t}|X^{t-1}_{t-k})}+\frac{1}{n}\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\log\frac{1}{P_{X_{0}}|X_{-t+1}^{-1}(X_{t}|X^{t-1})}}_{\triangleq f(X^{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{k+1}}\frac{\mathcal{C}(x^{k+1}|X^{n+k})}{n}\log\frac{1}{P_{X_{0}|X_{-k}^{-1}}(x_{k+1}|x^{k})}+\frac{1}{n}f(X^{k})-\frac{1}{n}\underbrace{\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+k}\log\frac{1}{P_{X_{0}|X_{-k}^{-1}}(X_{t}|X^{t-1}_{t-k})}}_{\triangleq g(X_{n+1}^{n+k})}.

By
Corollary
IV.11
,
\frac{\mathcal{C}(x^{k+1}|X^{n+k})}{n}
almost surely converges to the law of i.i.d.
(k+1)
-tuples with distribution
\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]
.

We now consider two cases on
P
.
First, assume
\exists x^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{k+1}
such that
P_{X^{k+1}}(x^{k+1})=0
.
Then, almost surely, there will be a term in
\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{P(X^{n})}
that is infinite: as
\supp(\Uppi)=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
,
\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]
is bounded away from the edge of the simplex, so
\frac{\mathcal{C}(x^{k+1}|X^{n+k})}{n}
almost surely converges to a positive value.
In this case, however, the right-hand side in the theorem statement is also infinite, so this theorem still holds.

Now, assume that
P_{X^{k+1}}(x^{k+1})>0
for all
x^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{k+1}
.
Then,
f(X^{k})
and
g(X_{n+1}^{n+k})
from above are uniformly bounded for all
X^{k}
,
X_{n+1}^{n+k}
, so
\frac{f(X^{k})}{n}
and
\frac{g(X_{n+1}^{n+k})}{n}
are deterministically
o(1)
.
We can apply this fact and Slutsky’s theorem to get

\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{P_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}_{Y_{-k}^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]}\left[\log\frac{1}{P_{X_{0}|X_{-k}^{-1}}(Y_{0}|Y_{-k}^{-1})}\right]=H(\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta])+\mathbb{E}_{Y_{-k}^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]}\left[D\left(\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]\,\Big\lVert\,P_{X_{0}|X_{-k}^{-1}}(\cdot|Y_{-k}^{-1})\right)\right].

Putting everything together,

\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{Q_{X^{n}}^{\text{LZ},\Uppi}(X^{n})}{P_{X^{n}}(X^{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}_{Y_{-k}^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]}\left[D\left(\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta]\,\Big\lVert\,P(\cdot|Y_{-k}^{-1})\right)\right]+H(\mathbb{E}[\Uptheta])-\mathbb{E}[H(\Uptheta)].

∎

VIII
Conclusion

We considered the probability source induced by the celebrated LZ78 universal compressor and studied some of its key properties.
We established the source’s entropy rate and almost-sure convergence of a realization’s normalized log probability to the entropy rate.
Additionally, we studied the minimum log loss achievable via a finite-state probability model on a realization of the LZ78 source, finding that it almost surely converges to a value larger than the entropy rate by a “Jensen gap”.
We have also characterized the relative entropy rate between the LZ78 source and any Markovian law.
Finally, we performed numerical simulations on the log probability of sequences drawn from the source, and the corresponding log loss of the best Markov probability model on the realizations.
These experiments illustrate some of our main theoretical results.

In future work, this would be a useful probability source for gauging the performance of sequential probability models (such as those based on deep learning) on non-Markovian non-stationary data.
In addition, it may be interesting to study the properties of sources based on other universal compressors or probability models.
With respect to theoretical results, we suspect that the condition of
\supp(\Uppi)=[0,1]
for the entropic results can be relaxed.
In fact, we suspect that our main results hold without any stipulations on
\Uppi
.
Another potential direction is the development of more refined finite-sample results.
Finally, it will be worthwhile to understand whether the LZ78 sources satisfy some version of a Kac’s lemma
[
16
]
that might allow to revisit and combine the settings of
[
10
]
and
[
17
]
toward a sliding window based universal lossy compressor.

Proofs of
Theorem
IV.12

In proving
Theorem
IV.12

\Uppi

Lemma A.1

(Zero-Order Empirical Distribution for
\supp(\Uppi)\subseteq\{0,1\}
)
.

For the LZ78 source with
\supp(\Uppi)\subseteq\{0,1\}
,
M_{n}
converges to
\Uppi
weakly, almost surely.

Proof.

Specifically, we show that, for all measurable
A\subseteq[0,1]
,
M_{n}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Uppi(A)\triangleq p
.

For the case
p=0
,
A\cap\supp(\Uppi)=0
, so both
M_{n}(A)
and
\Uppi(A)
are deterministically
0
.

Now, consider
p>0
.
As
\supp(\Uppi)\subseteq\{0,1\}
,
X_{t}
is a deterministic function of
B_{t}
, for every time point.
So, the LZ78 prefix tree is a single branch and the
\ell
th
phrase has length
\ell
.
Define
\hat{M}_{\ell}
as the empirical measure over the
\ell
th
phrase.
Within a phrase,
\Uptheta
values are
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\Uppi
, so
\hat{M}_{\ell}(A)\triangleq\tfrac{1}{\ell}M
, where
M\sim\text{Bin}(p,\ell)
.

By the Chernoff bound for the binomial distribution,
\forall\epsilon>0
,

\Pr{\left|\hat{M}_\ell(A) - p\right| > \epsilon}\leq 2\exp{-\frac{\epsilon^2 \ell}{3p},}

where
\exp{\cdot}
is taken as the base-
e
exponential function.

Then, as

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\Pr{\left|\hat{M}_\ell(A) - p\right| > \epsilon}\leq 2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\exp{-\frac{\epsilon^2}{3p}}\right)^{\ell}<\infty,

by the Borel-Cantelli lemma
\forall\epsilon>0
, almost surely,

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\mathbf{1}\left\{\left|\hat{M}_{\ell}(A)-p\right|>\epsilon\right\}=0.

We want to show that
\forall\xi>0
,
\exists N>0
s.t.
|M_{n}(A)-p|<\xi
,
\forall n>N
(with probability
1
).
By the Borel-Cantelli result, almost surely
\exists L
s.t.,
\forall\ell>L
,
|\hat{M}_{\ell}(A)-p|<\frac{\xi}{2}
.
Choose
N
such that,
\forall n>N
, the first
L
phrases (plus the potential final, unfinished phrase, which is
O(\sqrt{n})
) are less than a
\frac{\xi}{2}
fraction of the total sequence.
Then,
\forall n>N
,

|M_{n}(A)-p|<\tfrac{\xi}{2}\cdot 1+\left(1-\tfrac{\xi}{2}\right)\cdot\tfrac{\xi}{2}\leq\xi\quad\text{(a.s.)}.

\therefore
,
M_{n}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}p
.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Empirical Distribution of
r

\supp(\Uppi)\subseteq\{0,1\}
)
.

Let

Proof.

The proof is largely the same as that of

A.1
.

is also deterministically
0
.

Now, assume
p>0

th

\ell
th

(for

Define

Fix some

Fact G.2

\frac{O(\ell)}{N(\ell)}=\frac{O(\ell)}{m_{1}(\ell)}\cdot\frac{m_{1}(\ell)}{N(\ell)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\cdot 1=0.

Then, as
T_{n}
is an increasing sequence that goes
\to\infty
with
n
,

\frac{T_{n}\log T_{n}}{N(T_{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}[H(\Uptheta)]\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

∎

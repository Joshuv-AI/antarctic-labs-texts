Simulation of Adjoints and Petz Recovery Maps for Unknown Quantum Channels
Chengkai Zhu, Ziao Tang, Guocheng Zhen, Yinan Li, Ge Bai, Xin Wang

Abstract
Transformations of quantum channels, such as the transpose, complex conjugate, and adjoint, are fundamental to quantum information theory. Given access to an unknown channel, a central problem is whether these transformations can be implemented physically with quantum supermaps. While such supermaps are known for unitary operations, the situation for general quantum channels is fundamentally different. In this work, we establish a strict hierarchy of physical realizability for the transposition, complex conjugation, and adjoint transformation of an unknown quantum channel. We present a probabilistic protocol that exactly implements the transpose with a single query. In contrast, we prove no-go theorems showing that neither the complex conjugate nor the adjoint can be implemented by any completely positive supermap, even probabilistically. We then overcome this impossibility by designing a virtual protocol for the complex conjugate based on quasi-probability decomposition, and show its optimality in terms of the diamond norm. As a key application, we propose a protocol to estimate the expectation values resulting from the Petz recovery map of an unknown channel, achieving an improved query complexity compared to existing methods.



I
Introduction

The theory of quantum information has largely centered on the control and manipulation of quantum states. A broader paradigm is to manipulate quantum processes themselves, the dynamical maps that govern state evolution. This
second-order
control, formalized by quantum supermaps
Chiribella et al. 2008a
;
Chiribella et al. 2008b
, asks which transformations of unknown physical processes are fundamentally possible. A canonical testbed for this theory comprises the following transformations of an unknown channel: the adjoint, complex conjugate, and transpose. The adjoint is particularly indispensable. By the duality relation

\operatorname{Tr}[O\,{\cal N}(\rho)]=\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal N}^{\dagger}(O)\,\rho]

Angrisani et al. 2025
;
Schuster et al. 2025
and, operationally, corresponds to the “backward time evolution” needed to measure out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs), a key diagnostic of quantum scrambling in open systems
Syzranov et al. 2018
;
Zanardi and Anand 2021
;
Schuster and Yao 2023
;
Bose et al. 2024
;
Xu and Swingle 2024

Petz 1986
;
Petz 1988
;
Ohya and Petz. 1993

Barnum and Knill 2002
;
Hayden et al. 2004
, and thus the characterization of reversibility for quantum dynamics, which is central to near-optimal error-correction protocols
Ng and Mandayam 2010
;
Tyson 2010
;
Biswas et al. 2024
and to thermodynamic limits of quantum information processing
Jordan 2010
. The special case of obtaining the adjoint (namely the inverse) of unitary channels is essential for quantum algorithms such as quantum phase processing
Wang et al. 2023
and quantum singular value transformation
Gilyén et al. 2019
.
Besides applications in quantum information and algorithms, such transformations are essential for grounding the mathematical theory of time-reversal in open quantum systems
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Buscemi and Scarani 2021
;
Aw et al. 2021
.
This leads to a fundamental question: given a black-box device implementing an unknown channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, can an experimentalist implement its complex conjugate, transpose, or adjoint?

For unitary evolutions, this question is largely understood. In particular, mapping from
U
to
U^{\dagger}
corresponds to time reversal. Chen et al.
Chen et al. 2025
gave an exact circuit-level protocol that, with
O(d^{2})

d

Chiribella and Ebler 2016
;
Quintino et al. 2019a
;
Quintino et al. 2019b
;
Sedlák et al. 2019

However, for general open-system dynamics described by quantum channels, the situation changes qualitatively. For a generic quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
, the conjugate
{\cal N}^{*}_{A\to B}
remains a quantum channel, i.e., a completely positive (CP) and trace-preserving (TP) map, while the transpose
{\cal N}^{{\mathsf{T}}}
and the adjoint
{\cal N}^{\dagger}
are generally not TP and thus are not channels. This raises a subtle and fundamental problem: can these non-TP maps nonetheless be realized operationally, i.e., perhaps probabilistically, or via a virtual (quasi-probabilistic) scheme?

In this paper, we resolve this question by establishing a strict hierarchy for the physical realizability of the adjoint, complex conjugate, and transpose of a black-box channel. We begin by demonstrating that while the channel transpose
{\cal N}^{{\mathsf{T}}}
is physically realizable probabilistically via a post-selected teleportation protocol, the complex conjugate
{\cal N}^{*}
and adjoint
{\cal N}^{\dagger}
are fundamentally different. We prove a no-go theorem ruling out their implementation by any completely positive supermap, even probabilistically. To overcome this physical barrier, we introduce a virtual protocol based on quasi-probability decompositions that enables universal estimation of these unrealizable maps. Together, our results (summarized in Table

III.1
Probabilistic Transposition and No-Go for Complex Conjugation

We first observe that the transpose
{\cal N}^{{\mathsf{T}}}_{B\to A}
of an unknown quantum channel can be probabilistically realized via postselected teleportation
Lloyd et al. 2011
, also known as gate teleportation in the unitary case
Gottesman and Chuang 1999
;
Quintino et al. 2019a
, as depicted in FIG.
1
. The procedure is: i). Prepare a maximally entangled state
\Phi_{A^{\prime}A}
and the input state
\rho_{B^{\prime}}
, ii). Apply the unknown channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
on system
A
, iii). Perform a measurement
\left(\Phi_{B^{\prime}B},{\mathds{1}}-\Phi_{B^{\prime}B}\right)
on systems
B^{\prime}B
. An outcome corresponding to the projector onto
\Phi_{B^{\prime}B}
heralds the successful preparation of the state
{\cal N}^{{\mathsf{T}}}(\rho)
on system
A^{\prime}
.

We can calculate that for any input state
\rho_{B^{\prime}}
, the output unnormalized state on system
A^{\prime}
reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{A}}\operatorname{Tr}_{BB^{\prime}}\Big[(\Phi_{BB^{\prime}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}})({\mathsf{N}}_{AB}\otimes\rho_{B^{\prime}})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\big[{\mathsf{N}}_{AB}(\rho_{B^{\prime}}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A})\big]=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}{\cal N}_{B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}(\rho_{B^{\prime}}).

Thus, the output state is
{\cal N}_{B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}(\rho_{B^{\prime}})
, and the success probability of this protocol is given by
p_{\text{suc}}=\operatorname{Tr}\big[{\mathsf{N}}_{AB}(\rho_{B^{\prime}}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A})\big]/(d_{A}d_{B})
. When
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
is a unital channel, we have that
p_{\text{suc}}=1/d^{2}
where
d_{A}=d_{B}=d
.

While the transpose admits a probabilistic implementation, the situation changes strictly for the complex conjugate. We show that a universal realization of
{\cal N}^{*}
is impossible within the domain of physical supermaps, even with any finite copy supplied or allowing for probabilistic success.

Theorem 1

(No-go for complex conjugation)

For any finite integer
n\geq 1
, there exists no
n
-slot completely positive supermap
{\cal C}
such that
{\cal C}({\cal N}^{\otimes n})={\cal N}^{*},~\forall{\cal N}\in\text{\rm CPTP}(A,B)
.

The proof is by contradiction. It was shown that for any integer
d,n\geq 1
, there exists no
n
-to-
1
completely positive map
{\cal N}_{A^{n}\to A^{\prime}}
such that
{\cal N}(\rho^{\otimes n})=\rho^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}
for all
\rho\in\mathscr{D}_{A}

Yang et al. 2014
;
Dong et al. 2019
. Then, suppose there is a CP supermap that can universally realize the complex conjugate with
n
copies of the input channel for some
n\geq 1
, i.e.,
{\cal C}({\cal N}^{\otimes n})={\cal N}^{*},~\forall{\cal N}\in\text{\rm CPTP}(A,B)
. Consider a state preparation channel
{\cal R}^{\rho}_{A\to B}
such that
{\cal R}^{\rho}_{A\to B}(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\rho,\forall X
. Note that the complex conjugate of
{\cal R}^{\rho}_{A\to B}
is also a state preparation channel
{\cal R}^{\rho^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}}_{A\to B}(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\rho^{{{\mathsf{T}}}},\forall X
. By assumption, we have that
{\cal C}(({\cal R}^{\rho}_{A\to B})^{\otimes n})={\cal R}^{\rho^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}}_{A\to B}
. Therefore, we have a protocol that can prepare
\rho^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}
for any quantum state
\rho
, a contradiction.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1
is that the channel adjoint
{\cal N}^{\dagger}
is also physically unrealizable from black-box accesses of
{\cal N}
. If a universal CP supermap for the adjoint existed, one could compose it with the probabilistic protocol for the channel transpose to realize the conjugate map (
{\cal N}^{*}=({\cal N}^{{\mathsf{T}}})^{\dagger}
) probabilistically, contradicting Theorem
1
. Hence, we have seen a fundamental gap between realizing the adjoint map for unknown unitaries and unknown channels.

III.2
Simulation of Channel Adjoint via Werner-Holevo Channels

The obstruction of Theorem
1
can be traced to the impossibility of universally implementing matrix transposition on arbitrary quantum states. That is, if we can realize the transpose map for unknown Choi states, we can implement the corresponding channel via postselected teleportation
Lloyd et al. 2011
. Nevertheless, there exist canonical CPTP channels that contain the transpose as a component, namely the Werner–Holevo channels
Werner and Holevo 2002

{\cal W}_{d}^{\pm}(X)=\frac{1}{d\pm 1}\big[\operatorname{Tr}(X){\mathds{1}}_{d}\pm X^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\big].

(2)

These channels are fully physical yet explicitly mix the desired transpose term
X^{{\mathsf{T}}}
with an isotropic noise term. By taking a linear combination of
{\cal W}^{+}_{d}
and
{\cal W}^{-}_{d}

\operatorname{Tr}(X){\mathds{1}}_{d}
and isolate the transpose map. The price of “cancelling noise” is that the required combination is generally not a convex mixture, and hence cannot define a CP supermap. To bypass this, we adopt the framework of
virtual combs

Zhu et al. 2024

(Virtual complex conjugation)

There exists a 1-slot virtual comb

We sample

III.3
Petz map of unknown quantum channel

A central motivation for universally realizing the adjoint map is the Petz recovery map
Petz 1986
;
Petz 1988
, which is a canonical reversal operation associated with a channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
and a reference state
\sigma_{A}
, given by

{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\cdot)\coloneqq\sigma^{1/2}{\cal N}^{\dagger}\Big({\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}(\cdot){\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\Big)\sigma^{1/2}.

We can decompose
{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}
into the following CP maps:

i).

{\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}(\cdot){\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}
,

ii).

{\cal N}^{\dagger}(\cdot)
,

iii).

\sigma^{1/2}(\cdot)\sigma^{1/2}
.

Overall,
{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}
is a CPTP map when
{\cal N}(\sigma)
is full-rank. It provides a near-optimal reversal of a quantum channel, serving as a quantum analog of Bayes’ rule
Bai et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025
, which makes it a fundamental tool for quantum information recovery when perfect restoration is impossible
Wilde 2015
;
Junge et al. 2018
.

To physically realize the Petz map, Ref.
Gilyén et al. 2022
proposed an algorithmic approach to realize the three steps above separately, which is built on the block-encoding technique
Low and Chuang 2019
and QSVT
Gilyén et al. 2019
;
Gilyén 2019
. Such an implementation requires access to copies of the Stinespring isometry
V
(and
V^{\dagger}
) of the quantum channel
{\cal N}
, which could be challenging and a strong assumption in practice. Crucially, by querying only the
unknown
black-box channel, we develop a protocol to estimate expectation values
\operatorname{Tr}[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})]
, for arbitrary input
\omega_{B}
and observable
O_{A}
. As shown in FIG.
3
, we outline the protocol for estimating
\operatorname{Tr}\left[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\right]
as follows. In each of the
M=O(d_{A}^{2}d_{B}^{2}\log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon^{2})
sampling rounds:

i).

Sample
(x,y)\in\{(+,+),(-,-),(+,-)\}
with the probabilities
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
in Eq. (
11
) respectively, and prepare the input state
|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{R^{\prime}}\otimes\Phi_{A^{\prime}A^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime\prime}}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{R}
.

ii).

Apply
{\cal W}_{d_{B}}^{y}\circ{\cal N}_{A\to B}\circ{\cal W}_{d_{A}}^{x}(\cdot)
on system
A^{\prime}
, the block-encoding
U_{RB^{\prime\prime}}^{{\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}}
on system
RB^{\prime\prime}
, and the block-encoding
U_{R^{\prime}A^{\prime\prime}}^{\sigma^{1/2}}
on system
R^{\prime}A^{\prime\prime}
.

iii).

Perform a projective measurement
\big(\Phi_{B^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime}},{\mathds{1}}-\Phi_{B^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime}}\big)
on systems
B^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime}
and measure in computational basis on system
R^{\prime}
and
R
. Take measurement on system
A^{\prime\prime}
with post-processing procedure by post-selecting the outcome
|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{R^{\prime}}\otimes\Phi_{B^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime}}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{R}
.

After applying the same post-processing procedure as in Theorem
3
on

to obtain the sample results

, we arrive at an unbiased estimator
\widetilde{\xi}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\widetilde{X}^{(m)}
for
\operatorname{Tr}\left[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\right]
, which achieves precision
\varepsilon
with probability at least
1-\delta
. Given that the focus of many quantum information processing tasks is inherently classical statistics

;
Huang et al. 2020
;
Yuan et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2024
, this estimation capability effectively captures the observable properties of the recovered state, with an intimate connection to quantum error mitigation
Temme et al. 2017
;
Cai et al. 2023
. We emphasize that our method generalizes to the estimation of any linear functional of
{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})
, though, without physically preparing the state.

, where

is the minimum non-zero eigenvalue of
{\cal N}(\sigma)
and

. As noted in Ref.
Utsumi et al. 2025

and
{\cal N}_{A\to B}

{\cal N}^{\dagger}

To benchmark our protocol against this approach for the task of expectation value estimation, we consider an estimator
\hat{\xi}
constructed by sampling from their approximate channel, denoted as
{\cal P}^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}
. For an observable
O_{A}
with
\|O_{A}\|_{\infty}\leq 1

to bound the bias, while using

This error budget is justified as follows. First, notice that

\displaystyle\Big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\big]-\hat{\xi}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}({\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}-{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}^{\varepsilon/2})(\omega_{B})\big]\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}^{\varepsilon/2}(\omega_{B})\big]-\hat{\xi}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|({\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}-{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}^{\varepsilon/2})(\omega_{B})\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\Big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}^{\varepsilon/2}(\omega_{B})\big]-\hat{\xi}\Big|,

where we have used the triangle inequality and the Hölder inequality in the first and the second inequality, respectively. Then, by the inclusion of events, it follows that

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(\big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\big]-\hat{\xi}\big|\leq\varepsilon\Big)

(13)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(\big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}{\cal P}^{\varepsilon/2}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\big]-\hat{\xi}\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ \cap

\displaystyle\big\|{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})-{\cal P}^{\varepsilon/2}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\big\|_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(\big|\!\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_{A}{\cal P}^{\varepsilon/2}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})\big]-\hat{\xi}\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big)\geq 1-\delta,

where the first inequality relies on the fact that satisfying both the bias and statistical constraints guarantees the total error bound through triangle inequality, and the equality holds because

(14)

We note that this scaling represents an upper bound on the cost, derived from utilizing the channel simulation results of Ref.
Utsumi et al. 2025
as a straightforward subroutine for expectation value estimation.

In the following, we analyze the query complexity of the unknown channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
for estimating
\operatorname{Tr}[O_{A}{\cal P}_{\sigma,{\cal N}}(\omega_{B})]
using our method. Notice that our method requires a post-selection arising from the transpose realization, which means we need to reject samples during our process. The success or acceptance across each sampling is independent, so the number of accepted samples
N
after
M

. First, consider the post-selection probability in realizing the channel transpose part. When
(x,y)=(+,+)
, we have

p_{\text{suc}}=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\Big[\frac{1}{d_{A}+1}\big({\mathds{1}}+{\cal N}(\frac{{\mathds{1}}}{d_{A}})^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\big)K\Big]}{d_{B}\cdot\operatorname{Tr}K}\geq\frac{1}{d_{B}(d_{A}+1)},

(15)

where
K\coloneqq{\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\omega{\cal N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}
. When
(x,y)=(+,-)
or
(-,-)

\displaystyle p_{\text{suc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\Big[\frac{1}{d_{A}-1}\big({\mathds{1}}-{\cal N}(\frac{{\mathds{1}}}{d_{A}}\big)^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\big)K\Big]}{d_{B}\cdot\operatorname{Tr}K}

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{B}(d_{A}-1)}-\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}(d_{A}-1)}\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal N}({\mathds{1}})^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}K]}{\operatorname{Tr}K}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{d_{B}(d_{A}-1)}(1-\zeta_{\max}),

where
\zeta_{\max}
is the maximum eigenvalue of
{\cal N}(\frac{{\mathds{1}}}{d_{A}})
.
Denote

(17)

Now consider event
A
of fewer than
n
accepted samples with

, and event
B
of given
n
accepted samples, the estimate error exceeds
\varepsilon
with
\Pr(B)\leq\delta/2
. It follows that the overall failure probability

n

\Pr(N\leq(1-\beta)M\eta)\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\beta^{2}M\eta}{2}\Big),~\beta\in(0,1).

(18)

Set

, so

(19)

\varepsilon

with probability at least
1-\delta
. In particular, when

is a unital channel, we have
M=O\left(\frac{d_{A}^{3}d_{B}^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right)
, which significantly improves Eq. (

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have established a strict hierarchy for the universal physical realizability of dual quantum maps. While the channel transpose admits a probabilistic implementation via teleportation, we proved that neither the complex conjugate nor the adjoint can be realized by any completely positive supermap. We overcame this fundamental obstruction by utilizing virtual combs, which allow for the universal simulation of these maps via quasi-probability sampling. We proved the optimality of our construction with respect to the sampling overhead. As a key application, this framework enables the estimation of the Petz recovery map for unknown channels with a query complexity of
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
, offering an improvement over existing deterministic approximations. We expect our protocol for simulating channel adjoint can lead to more applications in experimental probing of OTOCs in generic open quantum systems
Li et al. 2017
;
Tuziemski 2019
;
González Alonso et al. 2019
;
Blocher et al. 2022
;
Xu and Swingle 2024
, i.e.,
\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal N}^{\dagger}(W)V^{\dagger}{\cal N}(W)V\rho]
where
V,W
are some Hermitian and
{\cal N}
is the underlying dynamic of the open quantum system. Future work may extend these virtual techniques to multi-slot settings and broader classes of higher-order transformations in quantum thermodynamics
Stratton et al. 2024
and error mitigation
Jeon and Cai 2026
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B^{\prime}}{\mathsf{C}}^{(i)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{BB^{\prime}}{\mathsf{C}}^{(i)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{B}}{d_{B}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{AB^{\prime}}{\mathsf{C}}^{(i)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}=p_{i}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B},~\forall i\in\{1,2\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\Big[({\mathsf{C}}^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}-{\mathsf{C}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}^{(2)})({\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\Big]={\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\Big[({\mathsf{C}}^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}-{\mathsf{C}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}^{(2)})(\Delta_{j}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\Big]=\Delta_{j}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}},~j\in\{1,2,\dots,d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)\}.

According to Eq. (
8
) in Theorem
3
, by choosing

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}\coloneqq\frac{d_{B}+1}{2}{\mathsf{W}}_{d_{A}}^{+}\otimes{\mathsf{W}}_{d_{B}}^{+}+\frac{(d_{A}-1)(d_{B}-1)}{2}{\mathsf{W}}_{d_{A}}^{-}\otimes{\mathsf{W}}_{d_{B}}^{-},

(32)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}\coloneqq\frac{d_{A}(d_{B}-1)}{2}{\mathsf{W}}_{d_{A}}^{+}\otimes{\mathsf{W}}_{d_{B}}^{-},

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{1}\coloneqq\frac{d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}+2}{2},

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{2}\coloneqq\frac{d_{A}(d_{B}-1)}{2},

we have that
\{\widetilde{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}},\widetilde{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}},\hat{p}_{1},\hat{p}_{2}\}
is a feasible solution to the SDP (
31
). Therefore, we conclude that

\nu(d_{A},d_{B})\leq\frac{d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}+2}{2}+\frac{d_{A}(d_{B}-1)}{2}=d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}+1.

(33)

In the following, we will use the SDP duality to prove the converse. The Lagrangian of the problem (
31
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle p_{1}+p_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle Y_{A^{\prime}AB}^{(i)},\ \operatorname{Tr}_{B^{\prime}}{\mathsf{C}}^{(i)}-\operatorname{Tr}_{BB^{\prime}}{\mathsf{C}}^{(i)}\otimes\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{B}}{d_{B}}\right\rangle

(34)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle Z_{A^{\prime}B}^{(i)},\ \operatorname{Tr}_{AB^{\prime}}{\mathsf{C}}^{(i)}-p_{i}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}},\ {\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}-\operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\left[({\mathsf{C}}^{(1)}-{\mathsf{C}}^{(2)})({\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)}\left\langle G_{j},\ \Delta_{j}^{{{\mathsf{T}}}}-\operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\left[({\mathsf{C}}^{(1)}-{\mathsf{C}}^{(2)})(\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[X{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}]+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)}\operatorname{Tr}[G_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}]+p_{1}(1-\operatorname{Tr}Z^{(1)})+p_{2}(1-\operatorname{Tr}Z^{(2)})

\displaystyle+\Big\langle{\mathsf{C}}^{(1)},\ Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d_{B}}Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}+Z^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{AB^{\prime}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}-\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle{\mathsf{C}}^{(2)},\ Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d_{B}}Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}+Z^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{AB^{\prime}}+{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}+\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}\Big\rangle,

where
Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}AB},Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\in\mathscr{L}^{{\dagger}}_{A^{\prime}AB},~Z_{A^{\prime}B}^{(1)},Z_{A^{\prime}B}^{(2)}\in\mathscr{L}^{{\dagger}}_{A^{\prime}B},X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}},G_{j}\in\mathscr{L}^{\dagger}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
are dual variables. It follows that the dual problem is given by

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left[X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)}\operatorname{Tr}\left[G_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\right]

(35)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}Z_{A^{\prime}B}^{(1)}=1,~\operatorname{Tr}Z_{A^{\prime}B}^{(2)}=1,

\displaystyle Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d_{B}}Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}+Z^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{AB^{\prime}}\geq{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}+\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j},

\displaystyle-Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{d_{B}}Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}-Z^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{AB^{\prime}}\leq{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}+\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}.

For the primal problem, let

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=F_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes F_{BB^{\prime}}+\epsilon{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}>0,

(36)

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}>0,\text{ for }\epsilon>1,

then
\{\bar{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}},\bar{{\mathsf{C}}}^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}},\bar{p}_{1}=1+\epsilon d_{A}d_{B},\bar{p}_{2}=\epsilon d_{A}d_{B}\}
is a strictly feasible solution. This indicates that the strong duality holds by Slater’s condition
Ponstein 2004
. For the basis
\{\Delta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)}
on system
A
and
B
, we may choose the generalized Gell-Mann matrices
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008
as generators, given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}\coloneqq\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{d}}{\sqrt{d}},

(37)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{m}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1}{m(m+1)}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}|j\rangle\!\langle j|-m|m+1\rangle\!\langle m+1|\right),\quad 1\leq m\leq d-1,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{s}_{jk}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|j\rangle\!\langle k|-|k\rangle\!\langle j|),\quad\quad~1\leq j\leq k\leq d,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{a}_{jk}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-i|j\rangle\!\langle k|+i|k\rangle\!\langle j|),\quad 1\leq j\leq k\leq d.

Denote

\big\{L_{d}^{(n)}\big\}_{n=0}^{d^{2}-1}=\{\Lambda_{m}\}_{m=0}^{d-1}\cup\{\Lambda^{s}_{jk}\}_{j<k}\cup\{\Lambda^{a}_{jk}\}_{j<k}

(38)

with
L_{d}^{(0)}=L_{0}
. Then we choose
\Delta_{j}
to be

\big\{\Delta_{j}\big\}_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)}\coloneqq\left\{L_{A}^{(\alpha)}\otimes L_{B}^{(\beta)}:0\leq\alpha\leq d_{A}^{2}-1,1\leq\beta\leq d_{B}^{2}-1\right\}.

(39)

Note that for
\{\Delta_{j}\}
, they are i). Hermitian:
\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{j}^{\dagger}
, ii). orthonormal:
\operatorname{Tr}[\Delta_{i}^{\dagger}\Delta_{j}]=\delta_{ij}
, iii).
\operatorname{Tr}_{A}[\Delta_{j}]=0
except
L_{A}^{(\alpha)}=L_{A}^{(0)}
.
Based on this basis, we construct the following operators to form a feasible solution of the dual SDP (
35
):

\displaystyle X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}}{d_{A^{\prime}}d_{B^{\prime}}},

(40)

\displaystyle G_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{d_{A}+d_{B}}{d_{A}(d_{B}^{2}+d_{B})}\Delta_{j}\ &\text{ if }\operatorname{Tr}_{A}[\Delta_{j}]\neq 0,\\
\frac{1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\Delta_{j}&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle Z^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}B}

\displaystyle=Z^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}B}=\frac{1}{d_{A^{\prime}}d_{B}}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}B},

\displaystyle Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}AB}

\displaystyle=Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}AB}={\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}AB}.

The constraints are checked to be satisfied by the following calculation. Write the summation explicitly, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{A}+d_{B}}{d_{A}(d_{B}^{2}+d_{B})}\frac{F_{A^{\prime}A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\Big(F_{BB^{\prime}}-\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}}{d_{B}}\Big)+\frac{1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\left[F_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes\Big(F_{BB^{\prime}}-\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}}{d_{B}}\Big)-\frac{F_{A^{\prime}A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\Big(F_{BB^{\prime}}-\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}}{d_{B}}\Big)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\Big(F_{A^{\prime}A}+\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}A}}{d_{B}}\Big)\otimes\Big(F_{BB^{\prime}}-\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}}{d_{B}}\Big).

The eigenvalues of
\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}
are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{d_{B}^{2}}\right)=\frac{d_{B}-1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}},

(42)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\left(1+\frac{1}{d_{B}}\right)^{2}=-\frac{d_{B}+1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{d_{B}}\right)^{2}.

Clearly,
\lambda_{1}
is the largest eigenvalue and
\lambda_{2}
is the smallest eigenvalue. Hence, we conclude that

\frac{d_{B}-1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}\geq\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}\geq-\frac{d_{B}+1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}.

(43)

It follows that (
40
) is a feasible solution since the constraint can be expressed as

\displaystyle Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d_{B}}Y^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}+Z^{(1)}_{A^{\prime}B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{AB^{\prime}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\left(\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}-\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}}\right){\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}=\frac{d_{B}-1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}\geq\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j},

and

\displaystyle-Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}AB}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{d_{B}}Y^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{BB^{\prime}}-Z^{(2)}_{A^{\prime}B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{AB^{\prime}}-{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\left(-\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}-\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}}\right){\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}=-\frac{d_{B}+1}{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}}{\mathds{1}}_{A^{\prime}ABB^{\prime}}\leq\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\otimes G_{j}.

For the feasible solution given in Eq. (
40
), the objective value is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left[X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}{\mathsf{D}}_{AB}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)}\operatorname{Tr}\left[G_{j}\Delta_{j}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\right]

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{B}}+\frac{d_{A}+d_{B}}{d_{A}(d_{B}^{2}+d_{B})}(d_{B}^{2}-1)+\frac{1}{d_{A}(d_{B}+1)}\left[d_{A}^{2}(d_{B}^{2}-1)-(d_{B}^{2}-1)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{B}}+\frac{(d_{A}+d_{B})(d_{B}-1)}{d_{A}d_{B}}+\frac{(d_{A}^{2}-1)(d_{B}-1)}{d_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{B}}+\frac{d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}+d_{B}^{2}-d_{B}+d_{A}^{2}d_{B}^{2}-d_{A}^{2}d_{B}-d_{B}^{2}+d_{B}}{d_{A}d_{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{B}}+1-\frac{1}{d_{B}}+d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}+1.

Since the dual problem is a maximization problem, we therefore conclude that

\nu(d_{A},d_{B})\geq d_{A}d_{B}-d_{A}+1.

(47)

Combining Eq. (
33
) and Eq. (
47
), we complete the proof.
∎

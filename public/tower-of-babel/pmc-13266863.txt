Efficacy and safety of Xingqin Ganke Granules in postinfectious cough: study protocol for a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-ranging phase II clinical trial
Authors: Qing Shen, Xuan Chen, Guihua Xu, Shiqi Sun, Yan Xue, Linqiong Zhou, Fengjun Zhang, Wei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrevious studies have demonstrated that Herbal Xingqin Ganke Granules (XQGK) exhibit significant antitussive effects across various dosage groups and ameliorate airway hyperreactivity in mice models. However, the clinical efficacy in humans warrants further investigation through additional clinical trials. The present study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of XQGK in the treatment of postinfectious cough (PIC) with the syndrome of latent wind-heat in the lung.MethodsThis multicenter, randomized, double-blind, dose-ranging, placebo-controlled trial intends to recruit 240 patients with PIC. Subjects will be randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to either the low-dose XQGK group, the high-dose XQGK group, or the control group, with 80 patients in each group. The treatment duration will be 14 days, followed by a 3-day post-treatment follow-up. The primary efficacy outcomes include: (I) cough response rate; (II) initial cough resolution rate. The secondary efficacy outcomes include: (I) time to cough relief; (II) time to initial cough resolution; (III) sustained cough resolution duration; (IV) Leicester Cough Questionnaire (LCQ) scores; (V) Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) symptom score variation; (VI) TCM symptom efficacy evaluation; (VII) individual TCM symptom score variation; and (VIII) Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC) scores. Safety endpoints will encompass the incidence of adverse events and serious adverse events, vital signs, physical examinations, laboratory biochemical indices, and 12-lead electrocardiogram findings.DiscussionThis study will provide comprehensive, large-scale, multicenter clinical data on the efficacy and safety of XQGK in the treatment of PIC with the syndrome of latent wind-heat in the lung. Introduction

Postinfectious cough (PIC) is defined as a persistent cough lasting 3–8 weeks following resolution of acute upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) symptoms, primarily caused by viral pathogens such as influenza, rhinovirus, and coronaviruses (1). In retrospective studies of unselected patients with a history of URTI, the frequency has ranged from 11% to 25% (2,3). During outbreaks of obvious infection with M pneumoniae and Bordetella pertussis, the frequency of PIC increases to 25% to 50% in selected series (4). Though self-limiting, PIC significantly impairs quality of life (QOL), with 60–70% of patients reporting sleep disturbances (5), social embarrassment, and reduced productivity (6). Current guidelines (1) recommend central antitussives (e.g., dextromethorphan), antihistamines, and decongestant for treatment, which provide symptomatic relief but exhibit variable efficacy and tolerability.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) formulations, widely used in clinical practice, have shown promise in alleviating PIC. Systematic reviews highlight the effectiveness of TCM-derived cough remedies, demonstrating reductions in cough frequency and improvements in QOL scores compared to placebo (7). These findings underscore the need for rigorous evaluation of TCM interventions to validate their clinical utility.

Xingqin Ganke Granules (XQGK), a multiherbal TCM formulation, has demonstrated antitussive and anti-inflammatory properties in cough animal models. In ammonia-induced cough models, XQGK dose-dependently reduced cough frequency in mice, while prolonging cough latency and decreasing cough episodes in citric acid-challenged guinea pigs. Furthermore, XQGK attenuated ovalbumin-induced airway hyperresponsiveness in murine models, normalizing enhanced pause (Penh) values, a marker of bronchoconstriction (8). These findings position Xingqin Ganke Granules as a promising candidate in PIC management.

Thus, we propose a multicenter, randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of XQGK in PIC management, aiming to bridge gaps between preclinical promise and clinical validation. We present this article in accordance with the SPIRIT reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0319/rc).

Methods

Study design

This trial employs a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, dose-ranging, placebo-controlled superiority design to initially assess the efficacy and safety of XQGK in the treatment of PIC characterized by latent wind-heat in the lung syndrome, while also exploring the optimal dosage. The clinical trial will enroll a total of 240 participants, who will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to the control group, low-dose group, and high-dose group, with 80 participants in each. Placebo will be used as a comparator in this study to prevent various biases. The entire trial period lasts for two weeks. The study visits occur on screening period, D3, D7, D10, D14 and 3 days after intervention (Figure 1). This study has been authorized by the China State Food and Drug Administration (approval No.2023LP00600) and was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR-IPR-2400085269). The trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and are consistent with good clinical practice (GCP) and the applicable regulatory requirements. This protocol also follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials, 2013 statement (9).

Ethics

The study protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Biomedical Ethics Committee of Shuguang Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Permit Number: 2023-1398-165-01). The other participating institutions have also been informed and agreed to the study. All participants will voluntarily give written, informed consent in this study. Any modifications, including the principal investigator, study protocol, and informed consent forms must be re-submitted, reviewed, and approved by the Biomedical Ethics Committee. Only relevant people and institutions, such as the principal investigator, sponsor, ethical committees, and drug regulation agencies, have access to research data, while no ancillary studies have access. All original files will be sent to the Principal Investigator after the end of the study and be archived for 5 years. All involved parties must keep the participant data strictly confidential. A chest CT scan will be obtained in selecting eligible participants, so all female participants will be asked about their menstrual and reproductive history. Those who are pregnant or breastfeeding will be excluded while other women participants must undergo a blood pregnancy test for safety reasons. All results from this study will be published in aggregated and anonymize way. Manuscripts submitted for peer-review and presentations of any results will adhere to relevant reporting guidelines for publication.

Participant recruitment and screening

A total of 240 patients of either sex, aged 18–65 years, will be enrolled. Eight hospitals are selected as the investigational sites in China (Table 1). Recruitment will be conducted in the outpatient departments or independent reception rooms at each investigational site. Participants should be informed of the trial by a responsible clinician and learn about the exact nature of the study, the implications and constraints of the protocol, the known side effects, and any benefits or risks involved in participation, and the importance of completing the study. All participants will sign the informed consent before the intervention. The recruitment period is 27 months, which will cover two winter seasons when the incidence of PIC is high.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated on the basis of the primary outcome (cough relief rate), using ‘Chi-Square Tests’ in PASS 15.0. A sample size of 141 achieves 90% power to detect an effect size (W) of 0.3000 (medium effect) using 2 degrees of freedom Chi-Square Test with a significance level (alpha) of 0.05000. Taking approximate dropout rates of 20% into account, the number of participants to be recruited was estimated at 177. Considering that this is an exploratory trial and lack of previous relevant variables, 240 subjects will be needed in the three groups, with 80 cases in each group.

Randomization

This trial involved the generation of random numbers and a coding table for the random assignment of 240 subjects, simulated by professional statistical analysts using a block randomization method specific to the trial areas. The data platform utilized for this trial, both the principal investigator and the data administrator have the authority to obtain randomization numbers. All random assignment numbers were issued in segments to each trial center in the form of emergency envelopes. The observing physician must administer medications according to the subjects’ visit sequence and the assigned medication number, without any discretion in choosing the medication, as this number remains constant throughout the entirety of the trial.

Blinding

A two-level blinding design was adopted in this study. The first level of blinding involved the assignment of cases to different groups (e.g., Group A, Group B, Group C) without revealing the group allocation to the researchers or participants. The second level of blinding concerned the treatments associated with each group (low-dose group, high-dose group, and control group), with the specific treatment details being concealed from both the researchers and participants.

A random coding table was established to facilitate the blinding process. The blinding codes for both levels were sealed separately in unapparent envelope and stored in duplicate at the lead clinical trial site and the sponsor’s office, ensuring the integrity and confidentiality of the study design.

Emergency unblinding

Each coded investigational product is accompanied by a corresponding emergency letter, which contains a slip of paper indicating the specific type of drug associated with that code, for unblinding in emergency situations. The emergency letters should be sealed and dispatched to each clinical trial center along with the correspondingly numbered investigational products. The center is responsible for storing these letters and should not open them unless absolutely necessary. In cases of emergencies (such as serious adverse events) or when a subject requires emergency treatment and the specific treatment received must be known, the researchers may open the emergency letter. Once opened, the subject with that code will be withdrawn from the trial and handled as a dropout case, with the reason for withdrawal recorded by the investigator in the case report form (CRF). All emergency letters will be collected along with the CRFs upon completion of the trial for blinded review post-trial.

Intervention

Medication administration and follow-up protocol

Treatment curation

The maximum medication taking duration lasts for 14±2 days. Participants are required to attend a follow-up 3±1 days after drug discontinuation.

Dispensation

On the day of enrollment (Day 0): eligible participants will receive the experimental medicine (7+1-day regimen) according to their randomized allocation sequence.

Visit 2 (D7+1): if discontinuation criteria are not met by D7±1 (within 24 hours of D7), participants must return unused medication and collect the subsequent experimental medicine (7+1 day regimen) for continued administration.

Recovery

If the subject reaches the drug discontinuation criteria within 7±1 days of medication, they will complete the Visit 5 after discontinuing the medication. If the subject does not reach the drug discontinuation criteria within 7±1 days of medication, they need to return to the hospital to return the remaining medication and receive the next cycle (7+1 days) of medication to continue taking the medication. If the subject reaches the drug discontinuation criteria within 14±2 days of medication, they will complete Visit 5 after discontinuing the medication. If the subject does not reach the drug discontinuation criteria within 14±2 days of medication, they must complete the visit items on D14±2 days and then discontinue the medication until Visit 5 is completed.

Discontinuation criteria

Participants discontinue medication when their VAS score for cough symptoms remains <17 mm for 24 consecutive hours during the treatment period.

The observing physician meticulously documented the quantity of medication received, administered, and returned by the subjects to assess their medication adherence and promptly recorded this information on the CRF. Based on the subjects’ adherence levels, decisions were made regarding their continuation in the trial. From the first day of medication until discontinuation, subjects fill in the daily medication situation in the Electronic Patient Reported Outcomes (ePRO).

Dosage form: granules; unit dose: 11 g/pouch.

Low-dose group: containing 10 grams of crude drug per pouch, take 1 pouch (11 grams) each time, 3 times a day, orally after meals.

High-dose group: containing 20 grams of crude drug per pouch, take 1 pouch (11 grams) each time, 3 times a day, orally after meals.

Control group: placebo, take 1 pouch (11 grams) each time, 3 times a day, orally after meals.

This study is a dose-ranging study with a dose-efficacy comparison. XQGK is derived from a clinically well-used empirical formula for PIC. The original XQGK herbal formula contains 60 g raw herbal material. The high-dose level was established based on the conventional daily clinical dosage of the original XQGK herbal formula (60 g). The raw herbs in the prescription were decocted, concentrated, and processed into granules, which were then divided into three daily administrations. Each high-dose pouch contains 20 g raw herb equivalent, administered three times daily. To explore the dose-response relationship in this phase II trial, a half-dose group was included as the low-dose regimen: each pouch contains 10 g raw herb equivalent, also given three times daily. This two-dose design allows preliminary evaluation of efficacy trends.

Concomitant medication regulation

During the trial period, participants were generally prohibited from using any antitussive drugs, expectorant medications, as well as TCM, Chinese patent medicines, and TCM-specific therapies with antitussive or expectorant effects. This includes all over-the-counter (OTC) lozenges, cough drops, and throat-soothing products with antitussive, expectorant, or medicinal throat-relief indications, regardless of whether they contain herbal/TCM ingredients. Plain sweets (non-medicated candies) are considered food items and are not prohibited. However, any use of such items must be recorded in the concomitant medication/therapy record form. The investigator will evaluate whether their use may confound the assessment of the study intervention’s efficacy. The use of corticosteroids, antibiotics, and quinolone drugs was also strictly forbidden.

In cases where participants required continued use of other medications or therapies due to comorbidities, all medications and treatment regimens must be fully documented in the concomitant medication/therapy record form and be evaluated whether their use may confound the assessment of the study intervention’s efficacy.

Quality control

To ensure the quality of the database, prior to database lock, the data management department assigned quality control personnel to implement quality control measures on 20% of enrolled cases per center (calculated as 8 cases per center if each center enrolled 40 cases).

Quality control was conducted on 100% of critical data for all participants, with a requirement of 0% post-correction error rate for critical data. Critical data were jointly determined by the statistician and the sponsor.

For sampled non-critical data, the error rate for numerical variables was required to not exceed 0.2%, and the error rate for text variables was required to not exceed 0.5%.

The PGIC scale is utilized for subjects to self-assess their overall therapeutic efficacy following medication administration. Planned analysis

Unless otherwise specified, statistical analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 or later. The test results will be reported in the form of summary tables (or graphs) and lists.

For quantitative indicators, statistical descriptions were provided using the number of cases (n), arithmetic mean (mean), standard deviation (SD), median, minimum value (min), and maximum value (max). For categorical indicators, statistical descriptions were given using the number of cases (n) and percentage (%).

All statistical tests were conducted using two-sided tests, with a P value less than 0.05 considered statistically significant for the tested differences. No multiplicity adjustment was performed for exploratory analyses. For comparisons between groups or within-group comparisons of continuous variables, the t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was employed. When considering the influence of other factors, covariance analysis [or other statistical methods specified in the Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP)] was used. For comparisons of categorical variables between groups, the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was applied. When accounting for the influence of other factors, the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) chi-squared test was utilized. In cases where multiple baseline confounding factors were considered, a Logistic regression model was adopted. Time-to-event outcomes were analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method, with median time, interquartile range, and their 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) reported, along with the presentation of survival curves.

Discussion

The pathophysiology of PIC involves intricate interactions among airway inflammation, neural hypersensitivity, and epithelial damage following respiratory tract infections (15). Emerging evidence underscores the persistent upregulation of inflammatory mediators [e.g., nerve growth factor, bradykinin (16)] and overexpression of transient receptor potential (TRP) channels (17,18) in airway sensory neurons contributing to cough reflex hypersensitivity. Various pharmacological agents, including central cough suppressants, expectorants, analgesics, decongestants, and first- or second-generation antihistamines, are frequently employed in PIC management. However, cough formulations containing multiple ingredients may elicit adverse effects, such as somnolence (15–20% incidence), ataxia, tachycardia, and xerostomia (30–40% incidence), thereby limiting long-term adherence (3). Despite the prevalence of cough as a clinical concern and a common reason for primary care visits, effective therapeutic options remain elusive. Consequently, patients often resort to OTC cough medications, many of which lack robust clinical efficacy data. This scenario drives the frequent utilization of Chinese herbal cough medicines in PIC management.

From the TCM perspective, PIC arises primarily from the lingering effects of exogenous wind-heat pathogens after an initial respiratory infection. The lung, being the most delicate organ and the first line of defense against exogenous pathogens, is particularly susceptible to invasion by wind-heat evils. These pathogens, characterized by their heat and dryness properties, impair the lung’s function of governing qi and respiration, leading to symptoms such as persistent cough, dry throat, and scant phlegm. The clinical management of PIC due to wind-heat invasion in TCM is guided by the principles of expelling wind-warmth, resolving phlegm, and regulating lung qi. XQGK, a TCM formula tailored for the treatment of PIC, embody the aforementioned therapeutic principles. The formula comprises a synergistic combination of herbs that work collectively to expel wind-heat, resolve phlegm, and resolving respiratory symptom.

A literature review reveals the potential therapeutic effects of XQGK granules’ herbal components in PIC. XQGK comprises Scutellaria baicalensis (Huangqin), Prunus armeniaca (Kuxingren), Bupleurum chinense (Chaihu), Peucedanum praeruptorum (Qianhu), Platycodon grandiflorus (Jiegeng), and Glycyrrhiza uralensis (Gancao). Scutellaria baicalensis, rich in baicalin and wogonoside, exhibits anti-inflammatory (19) and antiviral properties (20), potentially mitigating airway hypersensitivity (21) in PIC. Prunus armeniaca, Peucedanum praeruptorum and Platycodon grandiflorus synergize as antitussive (22) and expectorant agents (23,24), while Bupleurum chinense modulates immune responses (25). The multi-component nature of XQGK appears to achieve synergistic effects in PIC treatment.

Many patients use OTC products, which often lack clinically proven efficacy. In contrast, the herbal ingredients of XQGK indicate therapeutic potential, with in vivo research demonstrating antitussive and anti-inflammatory properties in cough animal models (8). These findings may hold clinical significance for the efficacy research of XQGK in PIC treatment.

Our multicenter, randomized, double-blind, dose-ranging, placebo-controlled trial represents a pivotal step in translating preclinical evidence of XQGK into clinically meaningful outcomes for patients with PIC characterized by latent wind-heat in the lung. The rigorous study design aligns with standards for evaluating TCM, addressing both efficacy and safety through multiple validated endpoints. By incorporating measures such as cough response rate, LCQ scores, and time-to-event outcomes alongside TCM-specific symptom assessments, this trial bridges the gap between conventional cough management frameworks and TCM syndrome differentiation, offering a holistic evaluation of therapeutic benefits.

This trial has limitations such as: (I) heterogeneity in outcome measures to prior PIC clinical research; (II) focusing on latent wind-heat in the lung, the study enrolls a mechanistically distinct patient subgroup likely to benefit from XQGK’s heat-clearing and lung-diffusing actions; (III) the 14-day intervention period with post-treatment follow-up further captures the dynamic resolution of PIC, which often follows a self-limiting but variable course.

Our clinical research provided evidence for TCM intervention to PIC. Future studies should explore XQGK’s effects on airway inflammation biomarkers and long-term recurrence rates, extending the evidence base for personalized TCM approaches in post infectious upper respiratory tract disease.

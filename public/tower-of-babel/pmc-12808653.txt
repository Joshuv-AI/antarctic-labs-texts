A scenario based analysis of bidirectional electric vehicle-building integration for energy optimization and carbon emission reduction
Authors: Ahmed Osman Ibrahim, Azher M Abed, Alisher Abduvokhidov, Bekzod Madaminov, Sanjarbek Madaminov, Bharosh Kumar Yadav, Nidhal Ben Khedher
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe integration of electric vehicles (EVs) with building energy systems presents substantial opportunities for carbon emission reduction and energy optimization through bidirectional energy flows. This study develops and validates a comprehensive scenario-based analytical framework for vehicle-to-building (V2B) and building-to-vehicle (B2V) integration, evaluating performance across diverse operational conditions and geographic regions. Five operational scenarios were analyzed using DesignBuilder simulation software and mixed-integer linear programming optimization for a commercial office building with 15 EVs in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia and Tashkent, Uzbekistan. The methodology incorporated comprehensive energy modeling, stochastic EV availability patterns, grid-responsive control strategies, and photovoltaic (PV) system integration. Results demonstrated substantial multi-faceted benefits from bidirectional EV integration. Optimal V2B operations achieved energy consumption reductions of 10.9% in Riyadh and comparable improvements in Tashkent, while V2B combined with PV systems delivered 22.8% and 18.2% reductions respectively. Carbon emission reductions paralleled energy savings, with optimal V2B achieving 10.9% reduction in Riyadh and V2B + PV scenarios reaching 22.8% and 18.1% reductions. Peak demand management showed substantial grid stabilization benefits with 14.1–22.6% peak reductions across scenarios. Economic analysis revealed attractive investment returns with payback periods of 4.7–5.7 years and positive net present values exceeding $28,950 –62,150 over 10-year periods. PV integration achieved exceptional renewable energy self-consumption ratios of 83.4–84.9%, substantially exceeding conventional installations. Introduction

The built environment and transportation sectors collectively represent two of the most energy-intensive domains globally, accounting for nearly 70% of total final energy consumption and contributing substantially to greenhouse gas emissions1–3. As urbanization accelerates and global energy demands intensify, the imperative to achieve carbon neutrality by 2050 has positioned the integration of these sectors as a critical pathway toward sustainable development4,5. The convergence of rapid electric vehicle (EV) adoption and the urgent need for building decarbonization presents unprecedented opportunities for synergistic energy management strategies that transcend traditional sectoral boundaries.

Recent projections indicate that the global EV market will experience transformative growth, with electric vehicle sales reaching over 14 million units in 2023 alone, representing a 35% increase from the previous year6,7. Simultaneously, the building sector faces mounting pressure to reduce its carbon footprint, with lifecycle emissions of battery electric vehicles being approximately half those of equivalent internal combustion engine vehicles over 15 years of operation8,9. This convergence creates a compelling case for investigating bidirectional energy flows between electric vehicles and buildings, potentially revolutionizing both domains’ energy efficiency and environmental performance.

The strategic importance of this integration extends beyond immediate energy savings to encompass broader grid stability, renewable energy utilization, and economic benefits. The V2G market is projected to grow to over $5 billion by 202410, while the V2G market size was valued at £6.7 billion in 2023 and is projected to reach £105.8 billion by 2033, growing at a CAGR of 35.93%11,12. These growth trajectories underscore the transformative potential of vehicle-to-building (V2B) and building-to-vehicle (B2V) technologies in addressing contemporary energy challenges.

The conceptual framework of bidirectional energy exchange between electric vehicles and buildings has evolved significantly over the past decade. Vehicle-to-building (V2B) technology provides an option to export electricity from the battery in electric vehicles into buildings to assist in meeting building electric demands13. This technology operates within the broader vehicle-to-everything (V2X) ecosystem, which encompasses vehicle-to-grid (V2G), vehicle-to-home (V2H), and vehicle-to-load (V2L) applications14–16.

Contemporary research has demonstrated the technical feasibility of V2B systems across multiple operational scenarios. The stochastic and limited availability of EVs for building energy management and the necessity of bi-directional flow of EVs to operate in both V2B and building-to-vehicle (B2V) modes represent primary constraints that current studies are actively addressing13,17. Recent optimization frameworks have employed mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) methods to maximize solar energy usage and minimize grid electricity consumption18.

The integration of electric vehicles with building energy systems has demonstrated substantial potential for carbon emission reduction. In 2023, EV use saved more than 220 million tons of GHG emissions globally, compared to 80 million in 202219. Advanced energy management strategies incorporate demand response mechanisms, time-of-use optimization, and renewable energy integration to enhance system efficiency.

Recent studies have explored multi-objective optimization approaches for minimizing operational costs and emissions simultaneously. The integration of EV charging with renewable energy sources and storage systems aims to maximize the benefits of clean energy generation while efficiently managing EV charging and grid interactions20. These strategies have shown particular promise in microgrids, where energy management strategies and scheduling decisions are optimized to minimize the carbon footprint by maximizing the utilization of renewable energy sources21,22.

The evolution of smart grid technologies has facilitated more sophisticated integration strategies. Smart grids are electricity networks that use digital technologies, sensors and software to better match the supply and demand of electricity in real time while minimizing costs and maintaining stability and reliability23. Vehicle-to-grid technology employs automobiles as mobile storage devices and focuses on the efficient utilization of extra power available during off-peak hours24.

Contemporary demand response programs have demonstrated significant potential for load management. Korea’s demand response markets had roughly 4.9 GW of registered capacity as of November 2022, with consumption of 43 GWh avoided in December 2022 alone under the voluntary demand response program25. These developments provide critical infrastructure support for V2B implementation at scale.

The Middle East region presents unique opportunities and challenges for V2B integration. The Middle East electric vehicle market size grew at a CAGR of 28.9% during 2022–2028, with government efforts to reduce dependence on oil and facilitate electric vehicle adoption to reduce automobile emissions for meeting net zero targets17,26. The Middle East has undergone substantial changes in the energy landscape in recent years due to considerable variations in energy demand trends, with huge potential for developing clean and sustainable energy resources28.

Similarly, Central Asian regions including Uzbekistan are experiencing rapid urbanization and energy sector transformation, creating favorable conditions for innovative energy management approaches. The convergence of abundant renewable energy resources and growing EV adoption in these regions necessitates comprehensive integration strategies.

Despite significant advances in V2B and B2V technologies, several critical gaps persist in the current literature that limit practical implementation and optimization potential.

Existing studies predominantly focus on isolated operational modes rather than comprehensive scenario-based analysis that captures the full spectrum of bidirectional energy flows. Most state-of-the-art research mainly focused on one operating mode, whereas comprehensive analysis of both V2B and B2V modes remains limited15. This limitation prevents stakeholders from understanding optimal integration strategies across varying operational conditions and temporal patterns.

Current research inadequately addresses the dynamic interaction between variable EV availability, building energy demands, and grid-responsive control strategies. Technical research gaps exist regarding smart and coordinated charging systems, where uncontrolled charging of EVs imposes several negative technical impacts, including grid instability and deteriorated power quality29. The lack of integrated frameworks that simultaneously optimize charging schedules, discharge patterns, and renewable energy utilization represents a significant limitation.

The majority of existing studies employ simplified temporal models that fail to capture the complexity of real-world operational patterns. Peak hour management, off-peak charging optimization, and mid-peak transition strategies require sophisticated modeling approaches that current literature has not adequately addressed. This gap is particularly significant for practical implementation in regions with distinct diurnal energy consumption patterns.

While several studies have examined V2B systems with renewable energy components10,13,30, comprehensive analysis of photovoltaic integration with bidirectional EV systems remains limited. The interplay between solar generation variability, battery storage optimization, and demand management requires more sophisticated analytical frameworks than currently available in the literature.

The selection of scenario-based analysis methodology for bidirectional EV-building integration addresses several critical limitations in existing research approaches. Scenario-based modeling enables comprehensive evaluation of multiple operational configurations, providing stakeholders with practical insights for implementation decisions31,32. This approach facilitates systematic comparison of different integration strategies while accounting for real-world variability in energy demand, EV availability, and renewable energy generation.

The focus on both V2B and B2V modes within a unified analytical framework represents a significant advancement over existing studies that typically examine these modes in isolation. This comprehensive approach enables identification of optimal operational strategies that maximize energy efficiency while minimizing carbon emissions across diverse operational scenarios.

The integration of time-of-use optimization with renewable energy components addresses the growing need for flexible energy systems that can accommodate variable renewable generation while maintaining grid stability. The methodology’s emphasis on carbon emission quantification provides critical metrics for policy development and investment decisions in sustainable transportation and building sectors.

This study aims to develop and validate a comprehensive scenario-based analytical framework for bidirectional integration of electric vehicles and buildings, enabling optimal energy management strategies that minimize carbon emissions while maximizing renewable energy utilization and grid-responsive performance. Materials and methods

Study design and geographic scope

This investigation employed a comprehensive scenario-based analysis approach to evaluate the bidirectional integration of electric vehicles (EVs) and buildings across diverse climatic and operational conditions. The study utilized a comparative methodology examining building-EV integration performance across two distinct geographic regions: Riyadh Province in Saudi Arabia and Tashkent Region in Uzbekistan. These locations were selected to represent contrasting climatic conditions and energy market structures, with Riyadh characterized by hot desert climate conditions (Köppen classification BWh) and Tashkent representing continental semi-arid climate (Köppen classification BSk)32. The geographic diversity enabled comprehensive validation of the proposed integration framework across varying environmental conditions, energy demand patterns, and grid characteristics typical of Middle Eastern and Central Asian contexts.

The study framework incorporated multiple temporal scales ranging from hourly operational analysis to annual energy performance assessment. Temporal analysis considered three distinct periods: peak demand hours (typically 12:00–16:00 and 19:00–23:00), off-peak hours (23:00–07:00), and mid-peak transition periods (07:00–12:00 and 16:00–19:00). This temporal stratification provided comprehensive understanding of bidirectional energy flow dynamics across varying demand conditions and enabled optimization of charging and discharging strategies to maximize carbon emission reductions while maintaining grid stability.

Building energy modeling framework

DesignBuilder simulation environment

Building energy modeling was conducted using DesignBuilder version 7.333, an advanced building energy simulation software that utilizes EnergyPlus as its calculation engine34. DesignBuilder was selected based on its comprehensive modeling capabilities for complex building geometries, advanced HVAC systems, and renewable energy integration, as well as its proven interoperability with building information modeling (BIM) workflows35. The software enables detailed thermal analysis incorporating dynamic heat transfer mechanisms, thermal mass effects, and multi-zone energy balance calculations essential for accurate assessment of EV integration impacts.

The simulation environment incorporated weather data from typical meteorological year (TMY) files for both study locations, obtained from the International Weather for Energy Calculations (IWEC) database36. Climate data included hourly values for dry-bulb temperature, relative humidity, solar irradiance, wind speed, and atmospheric pressure. Solar radiation modeling utilized the Perez solar radiation model37 for accurate prediction of solar gains and photovoltaic generation potential across different orientations and tilt angles.

Scenario development and analysis framework

Operational scenarios

Five distinct operational scenarios were developed to comprehensively evaluate V2B and B2V integration strategies. Scenario 1 (Baseline) represented conventional building operation without EV integration. Scenario 2 (Unidirectional Charging) incorporated standard EV charging without V2B capability. Scenario 3 (Off-peak V2B) enabled vehicle-to-building energy transfer exclusively during off-peak hours. Scenario 4 (Optimal V2B) implemented intelligent charging and discharging optimization across all time periods. Scenario 5 (V2B + PV) combined optimal V2B operation with rooftop photovoltaic integration.

Each scenario was evaluated across multiple temporal patterns including weekday and weekend operations, seasonal variations (summer, winter, and transitional seasons), and different grid electricity pricing structures. Weekday patterns represented standard office operations with peak building demands during business hours, while weekend patterns assumed reduced building loads and modified EV availability schedules.

Grid-responsive control strategy

The grid-responsive control strategy utilized real-time electricity pricing signals and grid condition indicators to optimize EV charging and discharging decisions. The control algorithm incorporated time-of-use (TOU) electricity pricing with three distinct rate periods: off-peak (0.08 $/kWh), mid-peak (0.15 $/kWh), and peak (0.25 $/kWh). Grid responsiveness was implemented through demand response signals that prioritized EV charging during periods of renewable energy surplus and incentivized V2B discharge during peak demand conditions.

The control strategy decision framework utilized a priority-based approach with hierarchical objectives: (1) maintaining minimum vehicle SOC for mobility requirements, (2) minimizing building electricity costs, (3) maximizing renewable energy self-consumption, and (4) providing grid stabilization services. Decision logic was implemented using rule-based algorithms that evaluated these objectives sequentially and selected optimal charging or discharging actions based on current system state and forecast conditions.

Global sensitivity analysis utilized the Sobol’ method to assess parameter interactions and total sensitivity effects. Sobol’ indices were computed using Monte Carlo sampling with 10,000 parameter combinations drawn from uniform distributions within specified ranges. First-order and total-order sensitivity indices were calculated to quantify individual parameter contributions and interaction effects respectively.

Model validation and uncertainty quantification

Model validation employed multiple approaches to ensure accuracy and reliability. Simulation results were compared against measured data from existing commercial buildings with similar characteristics in both study regions. Energy consumption predictions were validated against utility billing data with mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) targets of less than 10% for monthly consumption and 15% for peak demand predictions.

Uncertainty quantification was performed using Monte Carlo analysis with 5,000 simulation runs incorporating parameter uncertainties in weather data, occupancy patterns, equipment efficiencies, and EV availability. Input parameter distributions were characterized based on measured data and literature review. Weather data uncertainty was modeled using autoregressive models capturing temporal correlations, while equipment performance uncertainties followed normal distributions with standard deviations of 5–10% depending on technology maturity.

Statistical significance testing utilized the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for non-parametric comparison of energy consumption and emission results between scenarios. Confidence intervals were calculated at 95% confidence level using bootstrap resampling methods. Effect sizes were quantified using Cohen’s d statistic to assess practical significance of observed differences.

Computational implementation

All optimization problems were solved using CPLEX 22.1 optimization software integrated with Python 3.9 programming environment. The MILP solver utilized branch-and-cut algorithms with optimality gaps of 0.1% and maximum solution times of 3600 s. Parallel processing was employed using 8 CPU cores for accelerated computation of Monte Carlo simulations and sensitivity analyses.

Time series analysis incorporated 8760 hourly time steps representing full annual operation. Temporal resolution sensitivity was evaluated by comparing hourly, 15-minute, and 5-minute time steps to ensure adequate modeling precision while maintaining computational tractability. Results indicated that hourly resolution provided sufficient accuracy for annual energy assessments with computational time reductions of over 90% compared to sub-hourly modeling.

Data management and visualization utilized pandas and matplotlib libraries for post-processing and result analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using SciPy and statsmodels packages, while parallel computing was implemented using multiprocessing modules to exploit available computational resources efficiently.

Performance metrics and evaluation criteria

Key performance indicators

System performance was evaluated using multiple quantitative metrics enabling comprehensive assessment of technical, economic, and environmental benefits. Primary energy metrics included annual building energy consumption (kWh), peak demand reduction (kW), and renewable energy self-consumption ratio (%). Economic indicators encompassed total operational cost ($/year), electricity cost savings ($/year), and payback period (years) for EV integration infrastructure.

Environmental performance was quantified through annual CO2 emissions (kg/year), emission reduction percentage compared to baseline scenarios (%), and carbon footprint intensity (kg CO2/kWh). Grid interaction metrics included net energy exchange (kWh), peak shaving effectiveness (%), and load factor improvement (dimensionless).

Comparative analysis framework

Comparative analysis employed standardized metrics enabling consistent evaluation across different scenarios, locations, and temporal conditions. Normalized performance indices were calculated relative to baseline building operation to quantify improvement percentages. Statistical testing ensured robust comparison between scenarios using appropriate parametric and non-parametric tests depending on data distribution characteristics.

The analysis framework incorporated both absolute and relative performance metrics to capture different aspects of system benefits. Absolute metrics provided quantitative values for direct comparison, while relative metrics enabled assessment of improvement potential and scalability across different building sizes and EV fleet configurations.

Results

Overall system performance across operational scenarios

The comparative analysis of five operational scenarios revealed substantial differences in energy performance, carbon emissions, and economic outcomes across both geographic locations. The results demonstrate a clear hierarchy of performance benefits. In Riyadh, the baseline scenario consumed 245,780 kWh annually, generating 149,925 kg CO2. The unidirectional charging scenario (Scenario 2) increased consumption to 287,340 kWh and emissions to 175,278 kg CO2 due to additional EV charging loads. However, the introduction of V2B capabilities showed progressive improvements: off-peak V2B (Scenario 3) reduced consumption to 231,450 kWh and emissions to 141,084 kg CO2, while optimal V2B (Scenario 4) achieved further reductions to 218,920 kWh and 133,560 kg CO2. Similar patterns emerged in Tashkent, though with different magnitudes due to climatic variations. The baseline scenario consumed 198,450 kWh annually, generating 103,194 kg CO2. The optimal V2B with PV integration achieved consumption of 162,380 kWh and emissions of 84,438 kg CO2, representing 18.2% and 18.1% reductions respectively.

Discussion

The comprehensive scenario-based analysis revealed that bidirectional EV-building integration delivers substantial multi-faceted benefits that extend beyond simple energy savings. The 10.9–22.8% reductions in both energy consumption and carbon emissions represent transformative improvements that position V2B technology as a critical decarbonization strategy. The synergistic effect of combining optimal V2B operations with PV systems demonstrates that integrated renewable energy strategies can achieve emission reductions approaching 23%, indicating potential for near-term carbon neutrality in commercial building operations. The peak demand reductions of 14.1–22.6% are particularly significant given their direct impact on grid stability and infrastructure requirements, suggesting that widespread V2B adoption could defer substantial utility infrastructure investments.

The consideration of battery degradation in this study provides a realistic representation of the implications of bidirectional EV operation. By combining linear and nonlinear capacity fade components, as well as calendar aging effects, the model accounts for the most critical mechanisms influencing long-term battery health. Moreover, the explicit inclusion of degradation costs in the optimization objective ensures that operational strategies remain both technically feasible and economically sustainable.

The economic viability demonstrated through payback periods of 4.7–5.7 years provides compelling evidence for commercial adoption, particularly when considering the additional grid service revenues that enhance financial returns. The exceptionally high renewable energy self-consumption ratios of 83.4–84.9% achieved through V2B integration represent a paradigm shift from traditional energy storage approaches, demonstrating that mobile battery resources can effectively substitute for stationary storage systems while providing additional mobility services.

These findings align closely with established V2B research while demonstrating enhanced performance through comprehensive system integration17. The peak reduction benefits (14.1–22.6%) positioned at the upper range of literature values (15.4–20.6% average) validate the effectiveness of the multi-objective optimization approach employed. However, this study’s emission reduction achievements (10.9–22.8%) exceed typical literature ranges (10.2–17.7%), attributable to the integrated PV systems and time-varying emission factor modeling that captures real-world grid dynamics more accurately than simplified approaches.

The economic performance demonstrated here contrasts favorably with earlier studies that often reported longer payback periods of 6–10 years, reflecting recent reductions in bidirectional charging infrastructure costs and improved grid service compensation mechanisms8. The exceptional renewable energy self-consumption ratios achieved substantially exceed typical PV-only installations (40–60%), confirming theoretical predictions that mobile storage can dramatically enhance distributed renewable energy utilization.

Notably, this study’s geographic comparison reveals climate-dependent performance variations not extensively addressed in prior literature, with hot desert climates showing greater absolute benefits while continental climates demonstrated more consistent relative improvements.

The current research targeted one commercial office type and a preset number of EVs of 15 vehicles to ensure methodological clarity and computational feasibility. Nevertheless, the MILP model is scalable in nature. The EV scheduling sub-model is developed on the basis of individual vehicles each of which is described by arrival/departure time window, SOC constraints, and charging/discharging constraints. This model can be generalized to larger fleets, heterogeneous categories of vehicles or probabilistic arrival patterns without changing the model structure. Likewise, building energy balance only needs the hourly load profile and the rooftop availability, which makes the framework flexible to either a residential building, an industrial building, or a mixed-use building.

Several methodological constraints limit the generalizability of these findings. The analysis focused exclusively on commercial office buildings, potentially overlooking sector-specific variations in energy patterns and EV integration potential across industrial, residential, or mixed-use facilities. The fixed 15-vehicle fleet configuration, while representative of typical commercial parking, may not capture optimal fleet sizing strategies that could enhance performance benefits.

The stochastic modeling of user behavior, though validated against pilot data, remains simplified compared to actual mobility patterns that exhibit complex temporal correlations and individual preferences. The 12-month validation period, while providing confidence in model accuracy, cannot capture long-term battery degradation effects or evolving user behaviors that may influence sustained performance.

Geographic limitations restrict applicability to similar climate zones, as the two study locations may not represent diverse regulatory environments, electricity market structures, or renewable energy incentives that significantly influence economic viability. Meanwhile, this study restricted its scope to V2B and B2V operations; it represents both the presence of building-level validation data and the insufficiency of V2G regulatory frameworks in the areas analyzed. From a modeling perspective, extending the MILP formulation to include V2G is straightforward by introducing an additional decision variable representing energy export to the grid, along with associated market-based revenue functions. However, grid dispatch data and dynamic price signals were not available for Riyadh or Tashkent, introducing V2G would have introduced uncertainties undermining model validation.

The investigation successfully addressed the primary research objectives by establishing multiple operational scenarios, developing advanced control strategies, quantifying carbon emission reductions, and optimizing PV system integration across two distinct geographic regions.

Future research needs to focus on multi-building campus experiments to determine scalability and inter-building energy sharing due to multi-building V2B coordination. Studies that would focus on a broader variety of building types (and especially the industrial plants with high energy needs and residential complexes with various occupancy rates) would contribute to a better comprehension of industry-specific optimization solutions. future work should consider dynamic fleet sizing and advanced user behavior modeling, possibly employing machine learning techniques, to capture more realistic mobility and charging patterns. Long-term longitudinal studies, ideally 5–10 years, are essential to understand the cumulative effects of battery aging on the technical performance and economic viability of V2B systems. These studies will allow researchers and practitioners to develop more accurate predictive maintenance models, optimize battery replacement schedules, and assess the long-term economic impact of large-scale deployment. Policy-oriented research is equally critical: systematic analysis of regulatory frameworks, electricity tariffs, and carbon pricing mechanisms can provide valuable insights for designing incentives and market models. These efforts provide important guidance for governments, utilities, and private investors to develop robust strategies to drive the widespread deployment, scalability, and long-term sustainability of V2B integration into future smart energy systems.

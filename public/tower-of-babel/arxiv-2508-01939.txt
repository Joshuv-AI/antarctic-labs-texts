Stability of convex functionals on weighted Bergman spaces with applications
Petar Melentijević

Abstract
. Recently, Kulikov ( [ 25 ] ) has shown that certain convex functionals on weighted Bergman spaces are maximized by reproducing kernels. We prove that the same functionals hves near- maximal values for functions that are close to reproducing kernels. This is proved in a quantitative sense where both the norm and the exponent are optimal and the constant is explicit. We give several applications and some relevant examples.

1.
Introduction and main result

We will be working with several spaces of holomorphic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}.
Weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha},
with
\alpha>-1
is defined as the space of all functions
f,
holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}}:=\frac{\alpha+1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)<+\infty,

where
dA(z):=dxdy
is the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of holomorphic
f
such that

\|f\|_{H^{p}}:=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

is finite.
Well-known fact that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}}=\|f\|_{H^{p}},

provides possibility to translate some results from the weighted Bergman to the Hardy space setting (
[
43
]
).
By

\mu(\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

we denote Möbius invariant hyperbolic measure of the set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{D}.
In our analysis, we will estimate hyperbolic measure of the super-level sets
A_{t}:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:u(z)>t\},
where
u(z):=|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}.

Contractive inclusions between different weighted Bergman spaces has raised a great interest in recent investigations in function theory. Motivated by some applications in mathematical physics, Lieb and Solovej posed a question whether the norms
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{p\alpha}_{\alpha}}
are decreasing in
p.
Earlier, Pavlović in
[
37
]
had asked if Carleman inequality can be interpolated for non-integer values, while Brevig-Ortega-Cerda-Seip-Zhao in paper
[
8
]
give some evidence about Pavlović’s conjecture and broader perspective of its applications. It was recently solved by Kulikov in
[
25
]
via an ingenious adaptation of a technique invented in
[
36
]
to the hyperbolic setting in the unit disk. In fact, he proved more general result, which reads as follows:
Let
G:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex function. Then the maximum value of

\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2})d\mu(z)

is attained for
f(z)=1,
subject to the condition that
f\in\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}
and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}}=1.
In case of the functions from the Hardy space
H^{p}
under the constraint
\|f\|_{H^{p}}=1
the same conclusion is proved for every increasing function
G.
Thus, applying this result to
G(t)=t^{r},
the contractive embeddings

\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{q}_{\beta}}\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}}\leqslant\|f\|_{H^{r}},

is easily implied for
\frac{\beta+2}{q}=\frac{\alpha+2}{p}=r
and
0<p<q.

An interesting higher-dimensional extension of this result is given in
[
23
]
. In
[
31
]
and
[
33
]
sharp contractive embeddings between Hardy and Dirichlet or Besov spaces and hypercontractive estimates for weighted Bergman spaces are proved via Kulikov’s inequalities.

One more important application of this method was given by Ramos and Tilli in
[
38
]
, where the sharp concentration inequality for
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
is proved. Namely, for a set
\Omega
of prescribed hyperbolic measure
\mu(\Omega)=s,
the quantity

R(f,\Omega)=\frac{\frac{\alpha+1}{\pi}\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)}{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}

satisfies the following sharp estimate

R(f,\Omega)\leqslant R(1,B_{r})=1-\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{-\alpha-1}.

Here,
B_{r}
is a disk centered at the origin such that
\mu(B_{r})=s.
The equality is achieved if and only if
f
is a multiple of some reproducing kernel
K_{w}(z)=(1-\overline{w}z)^{-\alpha-2}
and
\Omega
is a disk centered in
w
of hyperbolic measure
s.

Very recently, stability of the local (concentration) estimates has become an active topic of research. In
[
15
]
the authors proved sharp quantitative form of concentration inequalities in Fock spaces giving sharp stability of Faber-Krahn inequality for short-time Fourier transform. The ideas of this paper are further employed in technically more demanding estimates for the weighted Bergman spaces, asymptotically sharp in both directions (
\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}
and
\alpha\rightarrow+\infty
). This result not only recovers known estimate for short-time Fourier transform but also gives a novel concentration estimate for function from Hardy spaces. This was done in
[
17
]
.

In
[
14
]
sharp quantitative version of the generalized Wehrl conjecture is given. In particular,
Frank, Nicola and Tilli provided a stability estimate for logarithmic Sobolev inequality in Fock spaces. Their approach uses majorization theory, local estimates and their stability versions. This motivates a similar question of considering convex functionals on weighted Bergman spaces, which will be our main task.
Our first result reads as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
G:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous and convex non-linear function such that
G(0)=0.
Then, for every
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
there exists a constant
c_{G}
depending on
G
and
\alpha>-1
for which

\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(|f(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2})d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}G((1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2})d\mu(z)-c_{G}\min_{|c|=\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}\frac{\|f-cf_{w}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}.

In particular, taking
G(t)=t^{\frac{p}{2}},p>2
we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 2
.

There exists a computable constant
c_{p,\alpha}
for
p>2
and
\alpha>-1
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}\bigg(1-c_{p,\alpha}\min_{|c|=\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}\frac{\|f-cf_{w}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}^{2}}{\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}-c_{p,\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}\min_{|c|=\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}\frac{\|f-cf_{w}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}^{p}}{\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}^{p}},

where
\beta+2=\frac{p(\alpha+2)}{2}.

The appropriate result for Hardy spaces takes the following form:

Theorem 3
.

Let
G:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous and non-decreasing function such that
G(0)=0.
Then, for every
f\in H^{p}
there exists a constant
c_{G}
depending on
G
and
p
for which

\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2}))d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}G((1-|z|^{2}))d\mu(z)-c_{G}\min_{|c|=\|f\|_{H^{p}}}\frac{\|f-cf_{w}\|^{p}_{H^{p}}}{\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}}}.

2.
The setting of weighted Bergman spaces - proof via concentration estimates and majorization theory

Let us consider
f\in\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}
with the norm
1
. Denote

u(z)=|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}.

For an arbitrary
\Omega\in\mathbb{D},

by the generalization of Ramos-Tilli localization inequality for all
p>0
(as proved in
[
26
]
), there holds the estimate

\int_{\Omega}u(z)d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\Omega^{*}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z),

where
\Omega^{*}
is the disk centered in
0
such that
\mu(\Omega^{*})=\mu(\Omega).

2.1.
Convex functionals

Let us first recover Kulikov’s result from the above local estimate following the approach from
[
14
]
. Taking an arbitrary
t>0
and the super-level set
A_{t}:=\{u(z)>t\}
for
\Omega
in Ramos-Tilli inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(u(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)=\int_{A_{t}}(u(z)-t)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{t}}u(z)d\mu(z)-t\mu(A_{t})\leqslant\int_{A_{t}^{*}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z)-t\mu(A_{t}^{*}),

where
A_{t}^{*}
stands for the disk with center in
0
and hyperbolic measure equal to
\mu(A_{t}).

Further,

\int_{A_{t}^{*}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z)-t\mu(A_{t}^{*})=\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)\\
\leqslant\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z).

(1)

By using

G(u)=G^{\prime}(0)u+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(u-t)_{+}G^{\prime\prime}(t)dt

for a convex
G
such that
G(0)=0
and
G^{\prime}(0)>-\infty,
we get

\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(u(z))d\mu(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}u(z)d\mu(z)\cdot G^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{D}}(u(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)\!\biggr)G^{\prime\prime}(t)dt\\
\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z)\cdot G^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)\!\biggr)G^{\prime\prime}(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(v_{\alpha}(z))d\mu(z).

Recall that a convex function has the left and the right derivative at every point and the second derivative
G^{\prime\prime}(t)dt
is a measure which does not have to be absolutely continuous.
If
G^{\prime}(0)=-\infty,
then, by the above, we

\int_{\mathbb{D}}G_{\varepsilon}(u(z))d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}G_{\varepsilon}(v_{\alpha}(z))d\mu(z)

for
G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\max\{G(u),-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}\},
which has bounded derivative almost everywhere. Since this family converges monotonically to
G
on the segment
[0,T],
as
\varepsilon\to 0+,
we conclude the proof in this case.

In order to achieve a refinement of the inequality (
1
), we will determine the difference of the integrals of
v_{\alpha}(z)-t
and its positive part for fixed
t>0
on the appropriate sets in the mentioned estimate. Namely, we start from the equation:

\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)-\int_{B_{t}\setminus A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)-\int_{A_{t}^{*}\setminus B_{t}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{-}\>\!d\mu(z),

(2)

where
B_{t}
stands for the disk
\{z\in\mathbb{D}:v_{\alpha}(z)>t\}.
Both
A_{t}^{*}
and
B_{t}
are disks centered in
0;
therefore, if

\mu(A_{t}^{*})>\mu(B_{t})
then

B_{t}\setminus A_{t}^{*}=\emptyset,
otherwise (
\mu(A_{t}^{*})\leqslant\mu(B_{t})
)
A_{t}^{*}\setminus B_{t}=\emptyset.
In the first case, we get:

\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)-\int_{A_{t}^{*}\setminus B_{t}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{-}\>\!d\mu(z).

Now, we will calculate the second integral on the right hand side. Introducing polar coordinates, we have

\int_{A_{t}^{*}\setminus B_{t}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{-}\>\!d\mu(z)=2\pi\int_{r(B_{t})}^{r_{t}}\Bigl(t-(1-\rho^{2})^{\alpha+2}\Bigr)\frac{\rho d\rho}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}.

While

r^{2}(B_{t})=1-t^{\!\frac{1}{\alpha+2}},

with
r_{t}
we denote the radius of
A_{t}^{*},
i.e. we have:

\mu(A_{t}^{*})=\mu(A_{t})=\pi\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-r_{t}^{2}}-1\Bigr).

We find:

\displaystyle\pi\int_{r^{2}(B_{t})}^{r^{2}_{t}}\Bigl(t-(1-y)^{\alpha+2}\Bigr)\frac{dy}{(1-y)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\pi\Bigl(\frac{t}{1-y}+\frac{(1-y)^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\Bigr)\Big|_{y=r^{2}(B_{t})}^{r^{2}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\pi t\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-r^{2}_{t}}-\frac{1}{1-r^{2}(B_{t})}\Bigr)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg((1-r^{2}_{t})^{\alpha+1}-(1-r^{2}(B_{t}))^{\alpha+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\pi t\Bigl(\frac{\mu(A_{t})}{\pi}+1-t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}\Bigr)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\Bigl(1+\frac{\mu(A_{t})}{\pi}\Bigr)^{\!\!-\alpha-1}-t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=t(\mu(A_{t})+\pi)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg[\Bigl(1+\frac{\mu(A_{t})}{\pi}\Bigr)^{\!\!-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg],

from which

\int_{A_{t}^{*}\setminus B_{t}\!\!}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{-}\>\!d\mu(z)=t(\mu(A_{t})+\pi)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg[\Bigl(1+\frac{\mu(A_{t})}{\pi}\Bigr)^{\!\!-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg].

Hence, in the case
\mu(A_{t})>\mu(B_{t}),
we get:

\displaystyle\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}d\mu(z)-\bigg[t(\pi+s)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg)\bigg],

where
s=\mu(A_{t}).
We proceed quite analogously for
\mu(A_{t})\leqslant\mu(B_{t})
and the only difference is the sign of the last summand in the previous identity. More precisely, the result of this calculation is given by the following lemma:

Lemma 4
.

\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}d\mu(z)-\bigg|t(\pi+s)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg)\bigg|.

(3)

Note that, from differential inequality
\frac{-\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\pi+\rho(t)}\geqslant\frac{1}{(\alpha+2)t},
integrating from
t
to
T
we get
(1+\frac{\rho(t)}{\pi})^{-\alpha-2}\leqslant\frac{t}{T}.
Hence,
A(s,t)
is positive for
\frac{t}{T}>(1+\frac{s}{\pi})^{-\alpha-2}>t
and negative for
(1+\frac{s}{\pi})^{-\alpha-2}<t.

2.2.
Optimal stability for
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}

In this section we concentrate on the case
p=2.
The pointwise inequality

u(z)\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}=1

is known to be true for every
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}.

The equality is attained if and only if
f
is equal to multiple of reproducing kernel
f_{w}(z)=(1-\overline{w}z)^{-\alpha-2}.
Let us suppose that
\max_{\mathbb{D}}u(z)=T<1.

In
[
17
]
it is proved that, for
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{2}
there holds the estimate

1-T\leqslant 2(1-\sqrt{T})=\min_{|c|=\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}\frac{\|f-cf_{w}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}}\leqslant M_{\alpha}(s)\delta(f;\Omega,\alpha)

(4)

where

M_{\alpha}(s)=C\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}\bigg[\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{\alpha+1}-1\bigg]\bigg),

\delta(f;\Omega,\alpha)=1-\frac{\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}d\mu(z)}{\int_{\Omega^{*}}v_{\alpha}d\mu(z)}

and

v_{\alpha}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}

for
s=\mu(\Omega),\Omega\subset\mathbb{D}.

From (
4
), we have

\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\leqslant 1-\frac{\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}d\mu(z)}{\int_{\Omega^{*}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z)}

i.e.

\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}d\mu(z)\leqslant\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\biggr)\int_{\Omega^{*}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z).

(5)

Inserting
\Omega:=A_{t}=\{u(z)>t\},
for
u(z)=|f(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2},
we get:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(u(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{t}}(u(z)-t)d\mu(z)=\int_{A_{t}}u(z)d\mu(z)-t\mu(A_{t})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{A_{t}^{*}}v_{\alpha}(z)d\mu(z)\cdot\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\biggr)-t\mu(A_{t}^{*})

\displaystyle=\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\biggr)\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-)d\mu(z)-t\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\biggr)\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\biggr)\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{M_{\alpha}(s)}\biggr)\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z).

If
\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{\alpha+1}\leqslant 2t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}

we easily see that:

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(s)=C\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\bigg)^{\alpha+1}-1\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}\big(t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}-1\big)\bigg)\leqslant 2M_{\alpha}(\mu(B_{t})),

from which we infer:

\int_{\mathbb{D}}(u(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)\leqslant\biggl(1-\frac{1-T}{2M_{\alpha}(\mu(B_{t}))}\biggr)\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z).

Since
v_{\alpha}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}>t
if and only if

|z|^{2}<1-t^{\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}=r^{2}(B_{t}),

we get

\mu(B_{t})=\pi\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-\big(1-t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}\big)}-1\Bigr)=\pi\big(t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}-1\big),

and

{M_{\alpha}(\mu(B_{t}))=\frac{C}{\alpha+1}\Bigl((\alpha+2)t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}-1\Bigr).}

In the case when
\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{\alpha+1}\geqslant 2t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}},

we have:

\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)=\\
\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)-\int_{B_{t}\setminus A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)-\int_{A_{t}^{*}\setminus B_{t}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{-}\>\!d\mu(z).

Since
M_{\alpha}(\mu(A_{t}^{*}))>M_{\alpha}(\mu(B_{t})),
we have

\mu(A_{t}^{*})>\mu(B_{t})
and therefore

B_{t}\setminus A_{t}^{*}=\emptyset,
thus giving, by Lemma 3:

\int_{A_{t}^{*}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)d\mu(z)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}d\mu(z)-\bigg[t(\pi+s)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\big(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\big)^{-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg)\bigg].

(6)

We also find

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{r^{2}(B_{t})}\!\Bigl((1-\rho^{2})^{\alpha+2}-t\Bigr)\frac{\rho d\rho}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{0}^{r^{2}(B_{t})}\!\Bigl((1-y)^{\alpha+2}-t\Bigr)\frac{dy}{(1-y)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\pi\Bigl(-\frac{t}{1-y}-\frac{(1-y)^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\Bigr)\Big|_{y=0}^{r^{2}(B_{t})}

\displaystyle=\pi t\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{1-r^{2}(B_{t})}\Bigr)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(1-(1-r^{2}(B_{t}))^{\alpha+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\pi t\Bigl(1-t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}\Bigr)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(1-t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg).

Note that the last expression is not bigger that
\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1},
since

\pi t-\frac{\pi(\alpha+2)}{\alpha+1}t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}<\pi t-\pi t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}<0.

Therefore, (
1
) implies

\int_{\mathbb{D}}(u(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)-\bigg[t(\pi+s)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\bigg(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\bigg)^{-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg]\bigg)

=\bigg(1-\bigg[t(\pi+s)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\bigg(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\bigg)^{-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg)\bigg]\big(K_{\alpha}(t)\big)^{-1}\bigg)\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z).

Let us consider:

A(s)=t(\pi+s)+\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}\bigg(\bigg(1+\frac{s}{\pi}\bigg)^{-\alpha-1}-(\alpha+2)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\bigg).

This function is convex and attains its minimum in
s_{0}
such that
\big(1+\frac{s_{0}}{\pi}\big)^{-\alpha-2}=t.
Hence, it is increasing on
(\pi(2^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}-1),+\infty)
and:

A(s)\geqslant A(\pi(2^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}-1))=\frac{\pi t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}}{\alpha+1}\bigg((\alpha+1)2^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}-\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\bigg).

Since

M_{\alpha}(\mu(B_{t}))=\frac{C\big(\alpha+2-t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\big)}{(\alpha+1)t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}}\geqslant Ct^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}

and

2^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}\geqslant 1+\frac{\log 2}{\alpha+1},

we easily find

A(s)\geqslant\frac{\pi}{\alpha+1}t^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\log\frac{2}{\sqrt{e}}\geqslant\frac{C^{\prime}}{\alpha+1}\cdot\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}\big(t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}-1\big)\bigg)^{-1}

with
C^{\prime}:=\pi\log\frac{2}{\sqrt{e}}.

This further gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(u(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)\leqslant\bigg[1-\frac{C^{\prime}}{\alpha+1}\cdot\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}\big(t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}-1\big)\bigg)^{-1}\big(K_{\alpha}(t)\big)^{-1}\bigg]\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg[1-\frac{C^{\prime}(1-T)}{C(\alpha+1)\bigg(1+\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}\big(t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}-1\big)\bigg)}\big(K_{\alpha}(t)\big)^{-1}\bigg]\int_{\mathbb{D}}(v_{\alpha}(z)-t)_{+}\>\!d\mu(z).

Integrating last inequality over
(0,T),
for every convex function
G,
we get

\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(u(z))\>\!d\mu(z)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(v_{\alpha}(z))\>\!d\mu(z)-\frac{C^{\prime}(1-T)}{C(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{G^{\prime\prime}(t)}{K_{\alpha}(t)M_{\alpha}(\mu(B_{t}))}dt.

Let us note that, by (
4
), every
1-T
in the above equations can be exchanged with
\min_{|c|=\|f\|}\frac{\|f-cf_{w}\|^{2}}{\|f\|^{2}}=2(1-\sqrt{T}),
but we use the first expression because of its simplicity.

2.3.
Weak stability estimate for
\mathcal{A}^{p}_{\alpha}

Define
\rho(t):=\mu(\{z\in\mathbb{D}:u(z)>t\})
and
\rho_{0}(t):=\mu(\{z\in\mathbb{D}:v_{\alpha}(z)>t\}).

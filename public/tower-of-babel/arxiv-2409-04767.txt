Barrier Integral Control for Global Asymptotic Stabilization of Uncertain Nonlinear Systems under Smooth Feedback and Transient Constraints
Christos K. Verginis

Abstract
This paper addresses the problem of asymptotic stabilization for high-order control-affine MIMO nonlinear systems with unknown dynamic terms. We introduce Barrier Integral Control, a novel algorithm designed to confine the system’s state within a predefined funnel, ensuring adherence to prescribed transient constraints, and asymptotically drive it to zero from any initial condition. The algorithm leverages the innovative integration of a reciprocal barrier function and an error-integral term, featuring smooth feedback control. Notably, it operates without relying on any information or approximation schemes for the (unknown) dynamic terms, which, unlike a large class of previous works, are not assumed to be bounded or to comply with globally Lipschitz/growth conditions. Additionally, the system’s trajectory and asymptotic performance are decoupled from the uncertain model, control-gain selection, and initial conditions. Finally, comparative simulation studies validate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.



I
Introduction

Controlling systems with uncertain dynamics has been a major research topic in control theory for many decades. It presents significant challenges due to uncertainties stemming from modelling inaccuracies, environmental variations, and external disturbances. To address these issues, adaptive and learning-based control algorithms have been developed, enabling systems to adjust in real-time to changing conditions. These methods often employ techniques such as parameter estimation
[
1
]
, function approximators such as neural networks
[
2

3

[

to enhance system stability and performance despite the presence of uncertainties.
To maximize the robustness to system uncertainties, the goal is to ultimately minimize a stabilization error and achieve a satisfying transient behaviour while reducing the the dependency on prior knowledge of system dynamics.

This paper considers the stabilization problem for a class of high-order MIMO nonlinear systems with unknown dynamic terms.
Unlike a large majority of related works, we do not impose growth conditions, linear parametrizations, or global boundedness on these terms, and we do not employ any schemes, such as neural networks, to approximate them.
We propose Barrier Integral Control (BRIC), a novel control algorithm that guarantees the evolution of the state in a pre-defined funnel and its asymptotic convergence to zero from all initial conditions.
BRIC comprises a reciprocal barrier function and an integrator term, which guarantee the boundedness of the state in the funnel and asymptotic convergence of the state to zero, respectively.
The performance of the closed-loop system is independent from the system’s dynamics, the initial conditions, or the selection of the control gains.
This paper extends our preliminary work

VI
Conclusion

We consider the asymptotic stabilization problem for high-order MIMO nonlinear control-affine systems with unknown dynamic terms.
We propose Barrier Integral Control to confine the state in a pre-defined funnel and guarantee its asymptotic convergence to zero from all initial conditions. Future efforts will be devoted towards extending the proposed scheme to more general systems with
unmatched uncertainties and potential controllability relaxations.

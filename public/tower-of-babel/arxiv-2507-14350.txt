Filtered Instantons and the Concordance of Satellites
Ivan So

Abstract
. In [ NST23 ] , Nozaki-Sato-Taniguchi defined a family of invariants r s r_{s} for integer homology spheres with filtered instanton homology [ FS92 ] . Coupling these with techniques in classical knot theory, we produce some results in the knot concordance group, including criteria for a family of satellite knots to be linearly independent and the independence of what we call the Whitehead n n -ble P n P_{n} .



1.
Introduction

Two knots
K_{0},K_{1}\subset S^{3}
are said to be
concordant
if there exists an embedding
f:S^{1}\times I\hookrightarrow S^{3}\times I
such that the restriction of the embedding to the two boundary components are
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
. Concordance is clearly an equivalence relation. Indeed, by taking the monoid of connected sum of knots quotienting by the concordance, an abelian group is defined by taking the identity to be the concordance class of the unknot
U
. The abelian group defined under this construction is known as the
concordance group
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}
. In the literature, the concordance group is studied in various categories, including the topological and smooth categories. In this paper, we focus on the
smooth
concordance group.

Let
P\subset S^{1}\times D^{2}=V
be a knot. The
satellite knot

P(K)\subset S^{3}
for a knot
K\subset S^{3}
is defined as the image of
P
under the embedding of
V
into a tubular neighborhood of
K
with 0-framing. In this context,
P
is known as the
pattern
or
satellite operator
and
K
is known as the
companion knot
. The behavior of
\mathcal{C}
under the satellite operator is a frequently considered question in knot theory.

Concordance is closely related to the study of homology cobordism group of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-homology 3-spheres
\Theta^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
as branched double cover of knot defines a well-defined homomorphism
\Sigma_{2}:\mathcal{C}\to\Theta^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
.

Instanton Floer homology is particularly strong in distinguishing integer homology 3-spheres constructed from
1/n
-surgery over the same knot
[
Fur90
]
,
[
FS90
]
. In this paper, we exploit this property to study the smooth concordance group under certain satellite operation using a numerical invariant

[
NST23
]
arising from the filtered instanton Floer homology
[
FS92
]
.

1.1.
Independence in the concordance group

The most general form of our result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q_{n}\subset S^{1}\times D^{2}
be a family of patterns such that

(1)

Q_{n}
is rational unknotting number 1 within
S^{1}\times D^{2}
;

(2)

\Sigma_{2}(Q_{n}(U))\cong S^{3}_{1/n}(J)
and;

(3)

h(S^{3}_{1}(K\#J\#K))<0
,

where
h:\Theta^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}
is the Frøyshov homomorphism from
[
Frø02
]
, then
\{Q_{n}(K)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}
.

Condition (3) in the above theorem is difficult to apply as the map
h(S^{3}_{1}(-)):\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{Z}
is not a homomorphism and and is generally hard to compute. To this end,
[
DISST22
]
defined a
slice torus invariant

\widetilde{s}
whose positivity suffices to conclude that
h(S^{3}_{1}(K))<0
. Hence, Theorem
1.1
has the following hands-on corollary

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
Q_{n}\subset S^{1}\times D^{2}
be a family of patterns such that

(1)

Q_{n}
is rational unknotting number 1 and;

(2)

\Sigma_{2}(Q_{n}(U))\cong S^{3}_{1/n}(J)
for a knot
J
such that
2\widetilde{s}(K)+\widetilde{s}(J)>0
,

then
\{Q_{n}(K)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}
.

In particular, if
K,J
are alternating knots and
2\sigma(K)+\sigma(J)<0
, where
\sigma
is the Seifert signature of a knot, then
\{Q_{n}(K)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}
.

If we restrict ourselves to the Whitehead
n
-ble pattern
P_{n}
(see Figure
1
), then the algorithm introduced in Section 2 will imply
J=U
. As a corollary

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
P_{n}
be the Whitehead
n
-ble pattern. Then
\{P_{n}(T_{p,q})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}
for any fixed coprime
p,q>1
.

In the proof of Corollary
1.3
, we will show the corresponding value of
r_{s}
for the branched covers is finite. Since
r_{s}(S^{3})=\infty
, this implies a slightly stronger result than that of Pinzón-Caicedo in
[

]
(which applies only for even
n

Corollary 1.4
.

\{P_{n}(T_{p,q})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are not slice for any fixed coprime
p,q>1

The computation of the Frøyshov

-invariant is never easy. However, due to this difficulty, we expect the condition in Theorem

will provide a lot of possibilities for linear independence-type results provided we can find ways to compute

for certain 1-surgeries.

1.2.
Linearly independence under fixed Whitehead
n
-ble

The concordance group
\mathcal{C}
is an abelian group. In this sense, the pattern
P\subset S^{1}\times D^{2}
defines a satellite operator
P:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
. One of the questions in knot theory is to ask whether satellite operators generate a “large” images subgroup. Following
[
HP21
]
, we define

Definition 1.5
.

A pattern
P\subset S^{1}\times D^{2}
is said to be of
infinite rank
if there exists a family of knots
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\{P(K_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
gives a
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
-subgroup of
\mathcal{C}
.

An example of result of this type is as follows

Theorem 1.6

(
[
HK12
]
, Theorem 1)
.

\{P_{2}(T_{2,2^{n}-1})\}
for
n=2,3,\cdots
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}
.

Hedden-Pinzón-Caicedo
[
HP21
]
conjectured that all non-constant satellite operators have infinite rank. Beyond asking whether a particular pattern has infinite rank, a natural question is:
which
companion knots yield an infinite-rank image? We have the following result:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M=\{(m_{k},k)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}|m_{k}\leq m_{k+1}\}
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and coprime
p,q>1
,
then
\{P_{n}(m_{k}T_{p,q+kp})\}_{(m_{k},k)\in M}
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 1.8
.

Theorem 13 of
[
Pin17
]
is similar to Theorem
1.7
and Corollary
1.3
, but in a slightly different direction. Their result requires the set
\{(n_{i},(p_{i},q_{i}))\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

p_{i}q_{i}(2n_{i}p_{i}q_{i})<p_{i+1}q_{i+1}(n_{i+1}p_{i+1}q_{i+1}-1).

While our results do not allow simultaneous variation of
(p,q)
and
n
, they do cover cases that were inconclusive in prior work. For example, for
(p,q)=(2,3)
and
n=2,4
,
[

]
is inconclusive on the independence whereas independence follows from our computation. Corollary 1.12 of
[

]
is also similar in nature, but our result cover a larger class of companion knots.

In the case of

, Hedden-Pinzón-Caicedo (
[
HP21
]
, Proposition 8) showed all patterns with nonzero winding number are necessarily infinite rank. Therefore, the above theorem does not provide a new infinite-rank result. Their proof, however, was non-constructive. The above theorem also gives an explicit family of knots whose images form an infinite-rank subgroup for the family of patterns
\{P_{2n-1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

1.3.
Comparison to prior technologies

Prior to our work, Hedden-Kirk
[
HK12
]
and Pinzón-Caicedo
[
Pin17
]
proved related results using
SO(3)
instanton theory. Their linear independence results in the concordance group relied on the computation of the minimal value Chern-Simons invariant. In contrast, the invariant

is an invariant from
SU(2)
instanton theory extracted from certain element over instanton Floer cohomology group, which contains more information than just the minimal value Chern-Simons invariant. we believe this is the primary reason why a somewhat stronger independence result can be extracted.

Compared to Heegaard Floer homology, instanton Floer homology is certainly more difficult to compute and thus remains inconclusive for a large class of satellite operator. But to the best of the author’s knowledge, independence results for
1/n
-surgeries on a fixed knot remain a unique feature for instanton Floer homology (see for instance
[
Fur90
]
,
[
DML24
]
).

2.
Surgery Description of Branched Double Cover

In this section, we recall the algorithm presented in Section 2 of
[
DHMS24
]
which identifies branched double cover of certain satellite knots in terms of surgery.

Such an algorithm is helpful for computing Floer-type invariants, as these invariants tend to be difficult to calculate directly from the branched cover description. However, with a surgery description, one can use tools such as the surgery exact triangle or surgery formulas to compute Floer homologies and hence the associated numerical invariants.

For example, in the recent notable disprove of the sliceness of
(2,1)
-cable of figure eight knot
4_{1}
(denoted by
(4_{1})_{2,1}
),
[
DKMPS24
]
used the fact that

\Sigma_{2}((4_{1})_{2,1})\cong S^{3}_{1}(4_{1}\#4_{1}).

As a result, the calculation in Heegaard-Floer theory reduces to manageable problem: the knot Floer complex of
4_{1}
is known, there is a connected sum formula for knot Floer complex and the Heegaard-Floer complex of
S^{3}_{1}(4_{1}\#4_{1})
can be computed via the integer surgery formula.

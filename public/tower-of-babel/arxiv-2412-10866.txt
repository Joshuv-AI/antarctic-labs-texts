A reduction formula for the Dunkl kernel for the root systems of type
A

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we provide a reduction formula for the Dunkl kernel for the root systems of type
A
. The Dunkl kernel for the root system
A_{n}
is expressed as an integral involving
the Dunkl kernel for the root system
A_{n-1}
. The corresponding reduction formula for the intertwining operator
V_{k}
is given.

1.
Introduction

We provide here some basic background for the paper.

A finite subset
\Sigma\subset{\bf R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
is a root system in
({\bf R}^{d},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
if

(1)

The roots span
{\bf R}^{d}
.

(2)

The only scalar multiples of
\alpha\in\Sigma
that are in
\Sigma
are
\alpha
and
-\alpha
(the root system is reduced).

(3)

\alpha
,
\beta\in\Sigma
implies
\sigma_{\alpha}\beta=\beta-2\,\frac{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{|\alpha|^{2}}\,\alpha\in\Sigma
.

(4)

\alpha
,
\beta\in\Sigma
implies
2\,\frac{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{|\alpha|^{2}}
is an integer (the root system is crystallographic).

Given a root system
\Sigma
, one can always choose a subset
\Sigma^{+}
such that
\Sigma
is the disjoint union of
\Sigma^{+}
and
-\Sigma^{+}
and for
\alpha
,
\beta\in\Sigma^{+}
,
\alpha+\beta\in\Sigma
implies
\alpha+\beta\in\Sigma^{+}
. The positive Weyl chamber is the set
\mathfrak{a}^{+}=\{X\in{\bf R}^{d}\colon\alpha(X)>0~\hbox{for all $\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}$}\}
. The Weyl group
W
is generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\Sigma
. A function
k:\Sigma\to{\bf R}
is called a multiplicity function if it is invariant under the action of
W
on
\Sigma.

We also introduce the building blocks of analysis on root systems. Let
\partial_{\xi}
be the derivative in the direction of
\xi\in{\bf R}^{d}
. The Dunkl operators indexed by
\xi
are then given by

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(k)\,f(X)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\xi}\,f(X)+\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}}\,k(\alpha)\,\alpha(\xi)\,\frac{f(X)-f(\sigma_{\alpha}\,X)}{\langle\alpha,X\rangle}.

The
T_{\xi}
’s,
\xi\in{\bf R}^{d}
, form a commutative family. For fixed
Y\in{\bf R}^{d}
, the Dunkl kernel
E_{k}(\cdot,Y)
is then the only real-analytic solution to the system

\displaystyle\left.T_{\xi}(k)\right|_{X}\,E_{k}(X,Y)=\langle\xi,Y\rangle\,E_{k}(X,Y),~\forall\xi\in{\bf R}^{d}

with
E_{k}(0,Y)=1
. In fact,
E_{k}
extends to a holomorphic function on
{\bf C}^{d}\times{\bf C}^{d}
. In addition, the Dunkl kernel is
W
-equivariant:

(1.1)

\displaystyle w\circ T_{\xi}(k)\circ w^{-1}=T_{w\,\xi}(k).

We recall some important properties of the Dunkl kernel:

(1)

E_{k}(w\,X,w\,Y)=E_{k}(X,Y)
for all
w\in W
.

(2)

E_{k}(X,Y)=E_{k}(Y,X)
.

(3)

E_{k}(c\,X,Y)=E_{k}(X,c\,Y)
for all
c\in{\bf C}
.

(4)

\overline{E_{k}(X,Y)}=E_{k}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})
.

Another important object in Dunkl theory is the intertwining
V_{k}
defined by the following properties

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{\xi}\,V_{k}&=V_{k}\,\partial_{\xi},\\
V_{k}(\mathcal{P}_{n})&=\mathcal{P}_{n},\\
\left.V_{k}\right|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}&=\hbox{id}\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
n
. We also introduce the positive measure
\mu_{X}
such that

\displaystyle V_{k}f(X)=\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,f(H)\,d\mu_{X}(H)

(for the existence of the positive measure, see for example
[
8
]
). We have the following relation:

\displaystyle V_{k}(e^{\langle X,\cdot\rangle})=E_{k}(X,\cdot).

For a more thorough introduction, the reader should consult
[
1
,
7
]
.

In this article, we concentrate on the root systems of type
A
. The root system
A_{n}
on
\{X\in{\bf R}^{n+1}\colon~x_{1}+\dots+x_{n+1}=0\}
is given by
\Sigma=\{{\bf e}_{i}-{\bf e}_{j},~i\not=j\}
where
\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n+1}\}
denotes the standard basis of
{\bf R}^{n+1}
. We also use the standard scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The Weyl group
W=S_{n+1}
acts on
X\in{\bf R}^{n+1}
by permuting the entries of
X
:
(w\,X)_{i}=X_{w(i)}
. The positive roots are
\Sigma^{+}=\{{\bf e}_{i}-{\bf e}_{j},~i<j\}
.

From now on, we consider instead the root system
A_{n}
on the space
\mathfrak{a}=\{X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})\in{\bf R}^{n+1}\}
without the usual normalization
x_{1}+\dots+x_{n+1}=0
(the first condition of a root system is then no longer satisfied). The positive Weyl chamber is
\mathfrak{a}^{+}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})\in\mathfrak{a}\colon x_{i}>x_{i+1},~i=1,\dots,n\}
. We will use
\widetilde{W}
to denote the Weyl group for
A_{n-1}
. Similarly, we will use
\widetilde{E}_{k}
to denote the Dunkl kernel for
A_{n-1}
. The multiplicity function is given by
k(\alpha)\equiv k
(a constant parameter). We also have

\displaystyle T_{j}(k)\,f(\lambda)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}(\lambda)+k\,\sum_{i\not=j}\,\frac{f(\lambda)-f((i,j)\lambda)}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}

(so that
T_{j}(k)=T_{{\bf e}_{j}}(k)
) where
(i,j)
exchanges the entries
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
of
\lambda
. We will assume that
k>0
unless otherwise specified.

Let
\pi(\lambda)=\prod_{i<j}\,(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})
and

\displaystyle W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)=\left(\prod_{r=1}^{n+1}\left[\left(\prod_{s=r}^{n}(\lambda_{r}-\nu_{s})\right)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r-1}(\nu_{s}-\lambda_{r})\right)\right]\right)^{k-1}.

The Dunkl kernel for the root system
A_{1}
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle E_{k}((x_{1},x_{2}),(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Gamma\!\left(2k\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(k\right)^{2}}\,\frac{e^{x_{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}}{\pi(\lambda)^{2k}}\,\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\,e^{x_{1}\nu}\,e^{-x_{2}\nu}\,(\nu-\lambda_{2})[(\lambda_{1}-\nu)(\nu-\lambda_{2})]^{k-1}\,d\nu

(here, we do not assume that
x_{1}+x_{2}=0=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
).

In Dunkl’s paper
[
4
]
an integral transform is given for the intertwining operator on the root system
A_{2}
.
Some integral formulas for
E_{k}(X,\lambda)
of the system
A_{2}
are given by Amri
[
2
]
. In
[
11
]
, Xu provides an integral representation of the operator
V
for root systems of type
A
when the function depends on only one variable. As a consequence, they provide an expression for
E_{k}(X,\lambda)
when
X={\bf e}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Later, in
[
3
]
, De Bie and Lian prove the same result using a different method.

In
[
5
]
, Graczyk and Sawyer adapted a formula by Amri (
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
) for the Dunkl kernel for the root systems of type
A_{2}
. Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
, the positive Weyl chamber:
we have for any
X{\in\mathfrak{a}}
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle E_{k}(X,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\,\Gamma(3k)}{\pi(\lambda)^{2k}\Gamma(k)^{3}}\,\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\{(\nu_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{1}-\nu_{2})\,\widetilde{E}_{k}((x_{1}-x_{2})/2,\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+(\lambda_{1}-\nu_{1})(\lambda_{2}-\nu_{2})\,\widetilde{E}_{k}((x_{1}-x_{2})/2,\nu_{2}-\nu_{1})\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\,(\nu_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\nu_{2}-\lambda_{3})e^{(x_{1}+x_{2}-2x_{3})(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})/2}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)\,d\nu_{1}d\nu_{2}

assuming a normalization of
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=0=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}
. Amri provides another expression of the Dunkl kernel in
[
2
, Corollary 2.2]
but
it is the former expression that led to (
1.3
).

If we forgo the normalization, (
1.3
) become

(1.4)

\displaystyle E_{k}(X,\lambda)=e^{x_{3}\,(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}\,\frac{3\,\Gamma(3k)}{\pi(\lambda)^{2k}\Gamma(k)^{3}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\{(\nu_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{1}-\nu_{2})\,E_{k}^{\text{rk\,1}}((x_{1},x_{2}),(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+(\lambda_{1}-\nu_{1})(\lambda_{2}-\nu_{2})\,E_{k}^{\text{rk\,1}}((x_{1},x_{2}),(\nu_{2},\nu_{1}))\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot(\nu_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\nu_{2}-\lambda_{3})e^{-x_{3}(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)\,d\nu_{1}\,d\nu_{2}.

We now use (
1.2
) (we can consider
E_{k}(x,\nu)=e^{\nu\,x}
as the Dunkl kernel for “
A_{0}
”) and (
1.4
) as an inspiration to propose a reduction formula valid for all
n
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Then

(1.5)

\displaystyle E_{k}(X,\lambda)=c_{n,k}\,\frac{e^{x_{n+1}(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n+1})}}{\pi(\lambda)^{2k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\lambda_{n}}\,\dots\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\,\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,\nu)\,\widetilde{E}_{k}(X^{\prime},w\,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)\,d\nu_{1}\,\cdots\,d\nu_{n}

where
c_{n,k}=\frac{(n+1)\,\Gamma((n+1)\,k)}{\Gamma(k)^{n+1}}
,
\epsilon(w)
is the sign of the permutation
w
and

(1.6)

\displaystyle Q(\lambda,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{r=1}^{n+1}\left[\left(\prod_{s=r}^{n}(\lambda_{r}-\nu_{s})\right)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r-1}(\nu_{s}-\lambda_{r})\right)\right]}{\prod_{r=1}^{n}\,(\lambda_{r}-\nu_{r})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{r=1}^{n+1}\left[\left(\prod_{s=r+1}^{n}(\lambda_{r}-\nu_{s})\right)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r-1}(\nu_{s}-\lambda_{r})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n\,(n+1)/2}\,\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{i\not=j}{j<n+1,i\leq n+1}}\,(\lambda_{i}-\nu_{j})

and
X^{\prime}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

This is the main result of this paper.

We will denote the domain of integration in (
1.5
) by
E(\lambda)
and
d\nu
for
d\nu_{1}\,d\nu_{2}\,\cdots\,d\nu_{n}
when convenient.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, some auxiliaries results are introduced as well as steps needed to simplify the proof of the main theorem. Theorem
1.1
is then proved in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we give the corresponding reduction formula for the intertwining operator
V_{k}
.
We show how Theorem
1.1
provide another alternative proof of the results by Xu mentioned above.

2.
Preliminaries

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that the function defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle F_{k}(X,\lambda)=c_{n,k}\,\frac{e^{x_{n+1}(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n+1})}}{V_{k}(\lambda)^{2k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\lambda_{n}}\,\dots\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\,\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,\nu)\,\widetilde{E}_{k}(X^{\prime},w\,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)\,d\nu_{1}\,\cdots\,d\nu_{n}

satisfy the following conditions:

(2.2)

\displaystyle T_{j}(\lambda)\,F_{k}(X,\lambda)

\displaystyle=x_{j}\,F_{k}(X,\lambda),~j=1,\dots,n+1,

\displaystyle F_{k}(X,0)

\displaystyle=1~\hbox{for every $X$}

where
T_{j}=T_{{\bf e}_{j}}
and
\{{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n+1}\}
is the standard basis of
{\bf R}^{n+1}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In order to be able to apply the operators
\sigma_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\Sigma
, to
F_{k}
defined in (
2.1
), we have to ensure that
W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)
remains
defined for
\nu\in E(\sigma_{\alpha}\,\lambda)
. We will consider instead

\displaystyle W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\,(\lambda_{j}-\nu_{i})^{2}\right)^{(k-1)/2}.

Note that this does not change the value of
W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)
when
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
and
\nu\in E(\lambda)
. Note also that

\pi(\lambda)^{2\,k}
and
W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)
are symmetric (Weyl-invariant) in
\lambda
and that
W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)
is also symmetric in
\nu
.

Some further properties of the Dunkl kernel:

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider the Dunkl kernel on
A_{d-1}
. We have

(1)

T_{j}(Y)\,E_{k}(X,w\,Y)=x_{w^{-1}(j)}\,E(X,w\,Y)
.

(2)

T_{j}(Y)\,\left(e^{a\,(y_{1}+\dots+y_{d})}\,E_{k}(X,Y)\right)=(x_{j}+a)\,E_{k}(X,Y)
.

(3)

e^{a\,(y_{1}+\dots+y_{d})}\,E_{k}(X,Y)=E_{k}(X+a,Y)
where
X+a:=(x_{1}+a,\dots,x_{d}+a)
.

Proof.

(1)

We have

\displaystyle T_{j}(Y)\,E_{k}(X,w\,Y)

\displaystyle=T_{j}(Y)\,E_{k}(w^{-1}\,X,Y)=x_{w^{-1}(j)}\,E(w^{-1}X,Y)

\displaystyle=x_{w^{-1}(j)}\,E(X,w\,Y).

(2)

Straightforward computation.

(3)

Follows from
2
. and
\left.e^{a\,(y_{1}+\dots+y_{d})}\,E_{k}(X,Y)\right|_{Y=0}=E_{k}(X,0)=1
which characterize

E_{k}(X+a,Y)
.

∎

The next few results will be instrumental in proving that the conditions of (
2.2
) are satisfied.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q
be as in (
1.6
). We have

(1)

If
w\in\widetilde{W}
then
Q(w\,\lambda,\nu)=Q(\lambda,w^{-1}\,\nu)
(the action of
w\in\widetilde{W}=S_{n}
is extended to
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
by
w(n+1)=n+1
).

(2)

\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,\nu)=\prod_{i<j<n+1}\,[(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\,(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})]\,\prod_{r=1}^{n}\,(\nu_{r}-\lambda_{n+1})
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
w\in\widetilde{W}
:

\displaystyle Q(w\,\lambda,\nu)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n\,(n+1)/2}\,\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{i\not=j}{j<n+1,i\leq n+1}}\,(\lambda_{w(i)}-\nu_{j})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n\,(n+1)/2}\,\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{i\not=j}{j<n+1,i\leq n+1}}\,(\lambda_{w(i)}-\nu_{w(w^{-1}(j))})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n\,(n+1)/2}\,\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{i\not=j}{j<n+1,i\leq n+1}}\,(\lambda_{i}-\nu_{w^{-1}(j)})=Q(\lambda,w^{-1}\,\nu).

(2)

Let

(2.3)

\displaystyle R(\lambda,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,\nu)}{\prod_{r=1}^{n}\,(\nu_{r}-\lambda_{n+1})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n\,(n-1)/2}\,\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,\prod_{i\not=j,i,j<n+1}\,(\lambda_{w(i)}-\nu_{j})

R(\lambda,\nu)
is clearly a skew-symmetric polynomial in
\lambda^{\prime}:=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
and in
\nu
(thanks to (
2.3
)).
Given that
R(\lambda,\nu)
is of degree
n\,(n-1)/2
in
\lambda
and also in
\nu
, we must have

R(\lambda,\nu)=C\,\prod_{i<j<n+1}\,(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\,(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})
where
C
is a constant. Now, let
\nu=\lambda^{\prime}
on both sides of (
2.3
):

\displaystyle R(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=(-1)^{n\,(n-1)/2}\,\prod_{i\not=j<n+1}\,(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})=\prod_{i<j<n+1}\,(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}

(all terms with
w\not=\text{id}
are null). The result follows.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

We consider the integrand of
F_{k}
in (
2.1
). Let

\displaystyle f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,\nu)\,\widetilde{E}_{k}(X^{\prime},w\,\nu)\,e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu).

Then

(1)

f_{k}
is skew-symmetric in
\nu
.

(2)

If
\pi_{i}(D)=\pi_{j}(D)
for some
i\not=j
then
\int_{D}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu=0
(here
\pi_{j}\colon{\bf R}^{n}\to{\bf R}
is the projection on the
j
-th component).

(3)

(1,2)_{\lambda}\,\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,du=-\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}((1,2)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu
.

(4)

If
j>2
then

(1,j)_{\lambda}\,\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,du=\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,(1,j-1)\,\nu)\,d\nu
.

Proof.

Let
f_{k}
be as in the statement of the lemma.

(1)

Let
w_{0}\in\widetilde{W}
, using
w^{\prime}=w\,w_{0}
,

\displaystyle f_{k}(\lambda,w_{0}\nu,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,w_{0}\nu)\,\widetilde{E}_{k}(X^{\prime},w\,w_{0}\nu)\,e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w^{\prime}w_{0}^{-1})\,Q(\lambda,w^{\prime}\nu)\,\widetilde{E}_{k}(X^{\prime},w^{\prime}\nu)\,e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(w_{0})\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X).

(2)

Suppose
\pi_{i}(D)=\pi_{j}(D)
for
i\not=j
. Then using (
1
),

\displaystyle\int_{D}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{(i,j)\,D}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu=\int_{D}\,f_{k}(\lambda,(i,j)\,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=-\int_{D}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu.

(3)

We have using (
2
),

\displaystyle(1,2)_{\lambda}\,\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,du=\int_{E((1,2)\,\lambda)}\,f_{k}((1,2)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\lambda_{n}}\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-2}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{1}}\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}f_{k}((1,2)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n+1}}^{\lambda_{n}}\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-2}}\dots\left(\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}+\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\right)\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}f_{k}((1,2)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=-\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}((1,2)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu.

(3)

If
j>2
, using (
2
) repeatedly, we have

\displaystyle(1,j)_{\lambda}\,\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}(\lambda,\nu,X)\,du=\int_{E((1,j)\,\lambda)}\,f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-1}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{j+1}}^{\lambda_{1}}\,\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{j-1}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{j}}f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-1}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{j+1}}^{\lambda_{1}}\,\left[\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}+\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{3}}+\dots+\int_{\lambda_{j-2}}^{\lambda_{j-1}}\right]\dots\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{j}}f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-1}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{j+1}}^{\lambda_{1}}\,\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\left[\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{3}}+\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{4}}+\dots+\int_{\lambda_{j-1}}^{\lambda_{j}}\right]f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-1}}\dots\left[\int_{\lambda_{j+1}}^{\lambda_{j}}+\int_{\lambda_{j}}^{\lambda_{j-1}}+\dots+\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\right]\,\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\int_{\lambda_{j-1}}^{\lambda_{j}}f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lambda_{n-1}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{j+1}}^{\lambda_{j}}\,\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}\dots\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\int_{\lambda_{j-1}}^{\lambda_{j}}f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,\nu,X)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{E(\lambda)}\,f_{k}((1,j)\,\lambda,(1,j-1)\,\nu)\,d\nu.

∎

The next three results will ensure that verifying the first condition in (
2.2
) for
j=1
suffices.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
F_{k}(X,\lambda)
be as in (
2.1
) and suppose
n>1
.
Then for
1<i<n+1
,
F_{k}((1,i)X,(1,i)\lambda)=F_{k}(X,\lambda)
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
, if
2<i<n+1
then

\displaystyle F_{k}((1,i)X,(1,i)\lambda)

\displaystyle=c_{n,k}\,\frac{e^{x_{n+1}(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n+1})}}{\pi(\lambda)^{2k}}\,(1,i)_{\lambda}\,\int_{E(\lambda)}\,\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q(\lambda,w\,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\widetilde{E}_{k}((1,i)X^{\prime},w\,\nu)\,e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)\,d\nu_{1}\,\cdots\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle=c_{n,k}\,\frac{e^{x_{n+1}(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n+1})}}{\pi(\lambda)^{2k}}\,\,\int_{E(\lambda)}\,\sum_{w\in\widetilde{W}}\,\epsilon(w)\,Q((1,i)\lambda,w(1,i-1)\,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\widetilde{E}_{k}((1,i)X^{\prime},w\,(1,i-1)\nu)\,e^{-x_{n+1}\,(\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n})}\,W_{k}(\lambda,\nu)\,d\nu_{1}\,\cdots\,d\nu_{n}

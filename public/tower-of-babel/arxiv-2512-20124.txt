A Certified Goal-Oriented A Posteriori Defeaturing Error Estimator for Elliptic PDEs
Philipp Weder 1,† and Annalisa Buffa 1,∗

Abstract
Defeaturing, the process of simplifying computational geometries, is a critical step in industrial simulation pipelines for reducing computational cost. Rigorous a posteriori estimators exist for the global energy-norm error introduced by geometry simplifications. However, practitioners are usually more concerned with the accuracy of specific quantities of interest (QoIs) in the solution. This paper bridges that gap by developing mathematically certified, goal-oriented a posteriori defeaturing error estimators for Poisson’s equation, linear elasticity, and Stokes flow. First, we derive new reliable energy-norm estimators for features subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions in linear elasticity and Stokes flow, based on existing results for Poisson’s equation. Second, we formulate general energy-norm estimators for multiple negative features, subject to either Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions for the first time. Finally, we combine these estimators with the dual-weighted residual (DWR) method to obtain reliable estimates for linear QoIs and demonstrate their effectiveness across a range of numerical experiments.



Keywords:

Geometric defeaturing; goal-oriented error estimation; a posteriori error analysis; dual-weighted residual method.

65N50, 65N15, 65N30

1. Introduction

Solving partial differential equations (PDEs) in industrial applications often involves geometrically complex domains. These geometries contain features across multiple scales, which makes meshing difficult and simulations computationally expensive. Even the presence of a single, relatively small geometric feature can increase the computational cost of a simulation by an order of magnitude due to the need for additional degrees of freedom.
[
39
,
22
]
It is standard practice to simplify these geometries by removing small or irrelevant features, a process known as defeaturing.

Traditionally, defeaturing relies on
a priori
criteria that are applied before any simulation is performed. These criteria are often heuristic, ranging from an engineer’s subjective expertise
[
23
]
to simplifications based on purely geometrical rationale
[
34
]
or prior knowledge of the underlying physical problem.
[
13
,
14
,
31
]

For a certified modeling and simulation process,
a posteriori
defeaturing error estimators are necessary. Early approaches provided the crucial insight that the error concentrates at simplified boundaries
[
12
]
or were based on asymptotic considerations such as feature sensitivity analysis (FSA), which crucially rely on the assumption of infinitesimally small features.
[
17
,
18
,
35
,
36
]
More recent methods using the dual-weighted residual (DWR) technique
[
4
,
30
]
or the reciprocal theorem have also been explored.
[
25
,
26
,
33
,
40
]
However, these works lack a rigorous mathematical certification and involve heuristic parameters that depend on the feature’s size.

This work builds on the framework for
a posteriori
defeaturing error estimation introduced by Buffa et al.
[
7
]
, where the defeaturing problem is rigorously analyzed for the Poisson problem with one feature subject to Neumann boundary conditions. It provides estimates that explicitly capture the size of the feature, allowing for the treatment of features of any size.
Subsequent work extended this framework in several directions: Antolín and Chanon
[
2
]
generalized the estimates to multiple features with Neumann boundary conditions, as well as to the linear elasticity and Stokes’ equations. Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
treated the case of negative features subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions for Poisson’s equation. Furthermore, Buffa et al.
[
8
,
6
,
9
]
combined the defeaturing estimators with
a posteriori
numerical error estimators for isogeometric analysis
[
21
,
28
]
and finite elements. Chanon
[
10
]
provides a comprehensive treatment of the topic.

While the previously introduced estimators provide robust estimates of the defeaturing error in the global energy-norm of the underlying PDE problem
[
7
,
2
,
10
]
, engineers are often interested in the accuracy of specific quantities of interest (QoIs), such as the average stress on a surface, the flux across a boundary, or the displacement at a specific point. In the latter cases, the global energy norm may not be an accurate proxy for the error in these QoIs.
To bridge this gap, we combine the rigorous energy-norm estimators with DWR .
[
4
,
30
]

This paper makes three primary contributions: First, we extend the analysis of Dirichlet features to linear elasticity and Stokes flow by deriving new reliable energy-norm estimators, which are of independent interest. Second, we present a unified problem formulation for geometries with mixed Dirichlet and Neumann features. Building on this foundation, we then develop certified goal-oriented defeaturing estimators for linear quantities of interest applicable to all three model problems. Our approach advances beyond previous DWR-based methods, which rely on heuristic approximations or the dismissal of uncomputable error terms
[
25
,
26
]
. In contrast, our framework provides a mathematically certified error bound in the form of an inequality up to a constant that is independent of the feature’s size. Moreover, it is significantly more general, providing estimators for complex configurations involving multiple negative isotropic features, features on the domain boundary, and those subject to either Dirichlet or Neumann conditions, thus overcoming key limitations of prior work.
[
26
]

Let us now abstractly introduce linear QoIs, in terms of which we later want to quantify the defeaturing error. To that end, let

\displaystyle L_{0}(v_{0})=L((v_{0})_{|\Omega})

3. Defeaturing Poisson’s equation

Poisson’s equation provides the simplest setting to develop and illustrate the central ideas of our goal-oriented defeaturing framework. This model problem allows us to introduce the classification of features, formulate the defeaturing problem for geometries with multiple Dirichlet and Neumann features, and establish both energy-norm and goal-oriented error estimators. The arguments presented here form the blueprint for the subsequent extensions to linear elasticity and Stokes flow.

To that end, we decompose the set of features

[

3.1. Energy-norm estimates

In this subsection, we establish energy-norm estimates that quantify the error introduced by defeaturing in the Poisson problem. For ease of notation, we define the defeaturing error function
e\coloneqq u-(u_{0})_{|\Omega}
, which satisfies the PDE

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta e=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
e=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\setminus\overline{\gamma_{D}},\\
e=d_{\gamma_{D}}&\text{ on }\gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{n}e=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N}\setminus\overline{\gamma_{N}},\\
\partial_{n}e=d_{\gamma_{N}}&\text{ on }\gamma_{N},\end{cases}

(6)

where
d_{\gamma_{D}}\coloneqq g_{|\gamma_{D}}-(u_{0})_{|\gamma_{D}}
and
d_{\gamma_{N}}\coloneqq h_{|\gamma_{N}}-(\partial_{n}u_{0})_{|\gamma_{N}}
. For the individual features, we similarly define the boundary errors by

\displaystyle d_{\gamma^{F}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}g_{|\gamma^{F}}-(u_{0})_{|\gamma^{F}}&F\in\mathcal{F}_{D},\\
h_{|\gamma^{F}}-(\partial_{n}u_{0})_{|\gamma^{F}}&F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}.\end{cases}

Note that in general, the defeaturing error
e
belongs to
H^{1}(\Omega)
. While the following analysis can be conducted under minimal regularity assumptions following the argument presented by Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
, we assume for ease of exposition that
e\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
.
This assumption holds in Lipschitz domains under light assumptions on the problem data and the interfaces between Dirichlet and Neumann boundaries
[
19
]
.
This regularity assumption implies that
\nu_{n}(\nabla e)\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, which greatly simplifies the treatment of Dirichlet-Neumann features.

Recall that the energy norm associated with the Poisson problem is given by the
H^{1}
-seminorm, i.e.,
||v||_{\Omega}\coloneqq|v|_{1,\Omega}
for
v\in H^{1}_{\Omega,0}(\Gamma_{D})
.
Using integration by parts and
eq.

6
, we find similarly to Buffa et al.
[
7
]
and Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
that

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{n}e\,e\,\mathrm{d}s

(7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla e)d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla e)d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla e)d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}d_{\gamma^{F}}e_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

To derive an estimator expression from
eq.

7
, we follow the arguments of Buffa and Chanon
[
7
]
and Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
. That is, for the integrals in the four sums of
eq.

7
, we seek estimates of the form

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla e)d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})|e|_{1,\Omega},

or

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma^{F}}d_{\gamma^{F}}e_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})|e|_{1,\Omega},

for Dirichlet features
F\in\mathcal{F}_{D}
and Neumann features
F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}
, respectively. Here,
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})
denotes a generic estimator expression, whose structure depends on the specific feature type.

Indeed, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality separates the unknown error terms
\nu_{n}(\nabla e)
or
e_{|\gamma^{F}}
from the boundary errors
d_{\gamma^{F}}
. The subsequent bounds rely on Poincaré-type inequalities on the boundary
\gamma^{F}
, the choice of which depends on the behavior of
d_{\gamma^{F}}
at the intersection with the remaining boundary: For a Dirichlet-Dirichlet feature, the boundary error
d_{\gamma^{F}}
vanishes at the boundary of the feature and the classical Poincaré inequality applies (
appendix

A
). For a Dirichlet-Neumann feature, the boundary error does not vanish at the boundary of the feature anymore. Hence, the average must be removed before a Poincaré inequality can be applied (
appendix

A
). Similar issues arise for internal Dirichlet and Neumann features. For the details, we refer to Buffa et al.
[
7
]
and Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
.

The following estimator expressions are shown to be reliable for the different feature types in Refs.
[
7
]
and
[
37
]
, respectively:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(|\gamma^{F}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}||d_{\gamma^{F}}-\overline{d_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}^{2}+c_{\gamma^{F}}^{2}|\gamma^{F}|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}|\overline{d_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{2||d_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{2||d_{\gamma^{F}}-\overline{d_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}}+|\gamma^{F}|^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}|\overline{d_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\sqrt{||d_{\gamma^{F}}-\overline{d_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}}+\bar{c}_{\gamma^{F}}|\overline{d_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}|.

Here,
\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma^{F}}
denotes the tangential gradient along
\gamma^{F}
.
The constant
c_{\gamma^{F}}
is defined by

\displaystyle c_{\gamma^{F}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\max\left(-\log(|\gamma^{F}|),\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{ if }n=2,\\
1&\text{ if }n=3,\end{cases}

where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
is the unique solution to
\eta=-\log(\eta)
; see
[
7
, p. 8]
. The constant
\bar{c}_{\gamma^{F}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\gamma^{F}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|\log\left(s_{\gamma^{F}}\right)|}},&n=2,\\
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{diam}(\gamma^{F})}{1-s_{\gamma^{F}}}},&n=3\end{cases},

where

\displaystyle s_{\gamma^{F}}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\gamma^{F})}{2\dist(m_{F},\partial\Omega\setminus\overline{\gamma^{F}})},

and
m_{F}
denotes the barycenter of the feature
F
; see Lemma 4.3 in Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
.

Adding up the feature-wise contributions, we then find the global estimate

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}\lesssim|e|_{1,\Omega}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, we define the general multi-feature estimator by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(u_{0})\coloneqq\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}\right.

(8)

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

whose reliability immediately follows from the reliability of the feature-wise estimators.

3.2. Goal-oriented estimates

We now turn to the derivation of goal-oriented estimates for the Poisson problem defined above. To this end, let
L\in V(\Omega)^{\prime}
be a linear QoI as defined in
section

2.3
with a defeatured counterpart
L_{0}\in V_{0}(\Omega_{0})^{\prime}
satisfying
2.1
. Motivated by the dual-weighted residual method
[
4
]
, we represent
L
and
L_{0}
with the help of influence functions
z
and
z_{0}
, solving the following dual problems, respectively:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta z=L&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
z=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{n}z=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta z_{0}=L_{0}&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
z_{0}=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,D},\\
\partial_{n}z_{0}=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,N}.\end{cases}

(9)

For the well-posedness of these problems, we refer to Agranovich
[
1
]
. In addition, the energy-based estimate from the previous section still holds. The dual defeaturing error
\epsilon\coloneqq z-(z_{0})_{|\Omega}
immediately satisfies the estimate
|\epsilon|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(z_{0})
.

To derive a goal-oriented estimate, we first note that in virtue of
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle L(u)-L_{0}(u_{0})=L(u)-L((u_{0})_{|\Omega})=L(e).

Next, using the error PDE
eq.

6
for the primal error
e
and the exact dual problem, we obtain from two successive integrations by parts that

\displaystyle L(e)=\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta z)\,e\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\nabla z\cdot\nabla e\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{n}(\nabla z)\,e\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{n}(\nabla e)\,z\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{n}(\nabla z)\,e\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using the boundary conditions of the exact dual problem in
eq.

9
, we find an error representation similar to
eq.

7
:

\displaystyle L(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}d_{\gamma^{F}}\,z_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla z)\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

(10)

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla z)\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla z)\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Note, however, that
eq.

10
still involves the exact dual solution
z
. The idea is now to split the right-hand side of
eq.

10
into a term that only involves the defeatured solutions
u_{0}
and
z_{0}
and a term that only involves the error functions
e
and
\epsilon
. To that purpose, we define the corrector term

\displaystyle R_{L}^{\gamma}(u_{0},z_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}d_{\gamma^{F}}\,(z_{0})_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla z_{0})\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

(11)

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla z_{0})\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla z_{0})\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

With this corrector at hand, we can prove the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain with a set of negative isotropic Lipschitz features
\mathcal{F}
. Let
L\in V(\Omega)^{\prime}
and
L_{0}\in V_{0}(\Omega)^{\prime}
be linear QoIs such that
2.1
is satisfied. Furthermore, let
u
and
u_{0}
be the solutions to the exact problem
eq.

4
and defeatured problem
eq.

5
, respectively, and
z_{0}
the solution to the defeatured dual problem
eq.

9
. Then, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle|L(u)-L_{0}(u_{0})-R_{L}^{\gamma}(u_{0},z_{0})|\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(u_{0})\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(z_{0}).

Proof.

Using the error representation
eq.

10
and the corrector term from
eq.

11
, we obtain

\displaystyle L(e)-R_{L}^{\gamma}(u_{0},z_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\epsilon_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla\epsilon)\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla\epsilon)\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\nu_{n}(\nabla\epsilon)\,d_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Now, observe that the integrals in the sums above have the same structure as the ones in
eq.

10
.
Hence, with the same arguments from Buffa et al.
[
7
]
and Weder and Buffa
[
37
]
, we find

\displaystyle|L(e)-R_{L}^{\gamma}(u_{0},z_{0})|\lesssim|\epsilon|_{1,\Omega}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(u_{0})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(u_{0})\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(z_{0}).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

In light of
theorem

3.2
, we observe that apart from ensuring the reliability of the goal-oriented estimate, the correction term
R_{L}^{\gamma}(u_{0},z_{0})
provides a first-order correction of the defeatured QoI
L_{0}(u_{0})
. That is, if the product
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(u_{0})\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(z_{0})
is small enough, the estimate implies that the approximation

\displaystyle L(u)\approx L_{0}(u_{0})+R_{L}^{\gamma}(u_{0},z_{0}),

is accurate.
We will refer to the right-hand side as the
corrected QoI
.

The same structure now naturally extends to the linear elasticity and Stokes’ equations.

4. Defeaturing the linear elasticity equation

In this section, we present the general defeaturing problem for the linear elasticity equations for mixed negative Dirichlet and Neumann features. It closely follows
section

3
, Antolín and Chanon
[
2
]
and Chanon
[
10
]
, where the case of Neumann features was addressed.

For a function
\boldsymbol{v}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the
linearized strain rate tensor
and the
Cauchy stress tensor
, respectively, by

\displaystyle\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla\boldsymbol{v}^{\top}),

and

\displaystyle\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq 2\mu\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})\mathbbm{I}_{n},

where
\mu
and
\lambda
denote the Lamé constants. We recall that due to thermodynamic stability,
\mu>0
and
\lambda+\frac{2}{3}\mu>0
.
Let
\boldsymbol{g}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})
,
\boldsymbol{h}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})
, and
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then the linear elasticity equations in the exact domain read:

\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u})=\boldsymbol{f}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{g}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{h}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\end{cases}

(12)

where
\boldsymbol{n}
denotes the unitary outward normal of
\partial\Omega
. The corresponding weak formulation reads: Find
\boldsymbol{u}\in V(\Omega)\coloneqq\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{g},\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
, such that for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{0},\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}):\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\boldsymbol{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(13)

The energy norm corresponding to problem
eq.

13
is defined by

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{v}||_{\Omega}\coloneqq\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{v}):\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in V(\Omega).

For the defeatured domain
\Omega_{0}
, we write
\boldsymbol{f}
for an
L^{2}
-extension of the source term in
eq.

12
by abuse of notation.
Once we have chosen extensions
\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{0,D})
and
\boldsymbol{h}_{0}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{0,N})
of
\boldsymbol{g}
and
\boldsymbol{h}
, respectively, the defeatured elasticity problem reads:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})=\boldsymbol{f}&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}=\boldsymbol{g}_{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,D},\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{h}_{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,N}.\end{cases}

(14)

The exact problem
eq.

12
and its defeatured counterpart
eq.

14
are well-posed in
V(\Omega)
and
V_{0}(\Omega_{0})\coloneqq\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{g}_{0},\Gamma_{0,D}}(\Omega_{0})
, respectively.

Similar to the Poisson problem, we define the vector-valued defeaturing error

\boldsymbol{e}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{u}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{|\Omega}
.
For each feature
F
, we introduce the boundary error as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{g}_{|\gamma^{F}}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{|\gamma^{F}},&F\in\mathcal{F}_{D},\\[2.15277pt]
\boldsymbol{h}_{|\gamma^{F}}-\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\boldsymbol{n}_{|\gamma^{F}},&F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}.\end{cases}

Analogously to the Poisson case in
section

3
, an integration by parts argument yields a general boundary error representation for the elasticity problem:

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{e}||_{\Omega}^{2}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

(15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Here we make the same regularity assumption that
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
as for the Poisson problem for simplicity. However, the analysis can be conducted under minimal regularity assumptions; see
appendix

B
.
The boundary representation
eq.

15

naturally suggests the following computable multi-feature estimator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\coloneqq\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}\right.

(16)

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

with the feature-wise estimators given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(|\gamma^{F}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}||\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}-\overline{\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}^{2}+c_{\gamma^{F}}^{2}|\gamma^{F}|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}||\overline{\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\sqrt{||\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}||\nabla_{t}\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\sqrt{||\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}-\overline{\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}||\nabla_{t}\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}}+2|\gamma^{F}|^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}||\overline{\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{\ell^{2}},

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\sqrt{||\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}-\overline{\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}||\nabla_{t}\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}||_{0,\gamma^{F}}}+\bar{c}_{\gamma^{F}}||\overline{\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}}^{\gamma^{F}}||_{\ell^{2}}.

(20)

The reliability of
eq.

17
for a Neumann feature was shown by Antolín and Chanon
[
2
]
. For the proof of reliability of the estimators for Dirichlet features
eqs.

18
,
19
, and
20
, we refer to
theorem

B.2
in
appendix

B
. The feature-wise reliability of the estimators thus implies that
||\boldsymbol{e}||_{\Omega}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})
like in the case of the Poisson problem.

Consider now a linear QoI
L\in V(\Omega)^{\prime}
and a corresponding defeatured QoI
L_{0}\in V_{0}(\Omega)^{\prime}
satisfying
2.1
. Then, similar to the Poisson problem in
section

3
, we define the exact and defeatured dual problems,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z})=L&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})=L_{0}&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
\boldsymbol{z}_{0}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,D},\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,N}.\end{cases}

(21)

For the dual defeaturing error
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{z}-(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})_{\Omega}
, the energy-norm estimate
||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}||_{\Omega}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})
also holds.

In analogy to the corrector term
eq.

11
for the Poisson problem, we define

\displaystyle R_{L}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{z}_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0})\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then, the same reasoning as for the Poisson equation yields the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain with a set of negative isotropic Lipschitz features
\mathcal{F}
. Let
L\in V(\Omega)^{\prime}
and
L_{0}\in V_{0}(\Omega)^{\prime}
be linear QoIs such that
2.1
is satisfied. Furthermore, let
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
be the solutions to the exact problem
eq.

12
and defeatured problem
eq.

14
, respectively, and
\boldsymbol{z}_{0}
the solution to the defeatured dual problem
eq.

21
. Then, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle|L(\boldsymbol{u})-L_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})-R_{L}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{z}_{0})|\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{z}_{0}).

5. Defeaturing the Stokes equations

This section addresses the general defeaturing problem for the Stokes equations for mixed negative Dirichlet and Neumann features. It closely follows
sections

3
and
4
.

To that end, let
\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})
denote again the linearized strain rate tensor for a function
\boldsymbol{v}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In contrast to
section

4
, we denote by
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{v})
be the viscous stress tensor of the fluid, which for a Newtonian fluid is given by

\displaystyle\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{v})=2\mu\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})\mathbbm{I}_{n},

with this time the constants
\mu>0
and
\lambda\geq 0
denoting the bulk and dynamic viscosities, respectively. Let
\boldsymbol{g}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{D})
,
\boldsymbol{h}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})
,
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and
f_{c}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, the Stokes problem in the exact domain is given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u})+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{f}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=f_{c}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{g}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{n}-p\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{h}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\end{cases}

(22)

where
\boldsymbol{n}
denotes the unitary outward normal of
\partial\Omega
. The corresponding mixed weak formulation reads: Find
\boldsymbol{u}\in V(\Omega)\coloneqq\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{g},\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
and
p\in Q(\Omega)\coloneqq L^{2}(\Omega)
, such that for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{0},\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
and
q\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})+b_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{v},p)=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{g}_{N}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(23)

\displaystyle b_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u},q)=-\int_{\Omega}f_{c}\,q\,\mathrm{d}x,

(24)

where the bilinear forms
a_{\Omega}:\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
and
b_{\Omega}:\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}):\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x,

and

\displaystyle b_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{v},q)\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}q\,\mathrm{d}x.

This problem is well-posed in
V(\Omega)\times Q(\Omega)
; see Boffi et al.
[
5
]
. The energy norm associated with the weak formulation
eqs.

23
and
24
is given by

\displaystyle||(\boldsymbol{v},q)||_{\Omega}\coloneqq a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})^{\frac{1}{2}}+||q||_{0,\Omega},

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega),q\in L^{2}(\Omega).

For the defeatured domain
\Omega_{0}
, we write
\boldsymbol{f}
and
f_{c}
for
L^{2}
-extensions of the original source terms in
eq.

22
by abuse of notation. Once we have chosen extensions
\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{0,D})
and
\boldsymbol{h}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{0,N})
of
\boldsymbol{g}
and
\boldsymbol{h}
, respectively, the defeatured Stokes problem reads:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})+\nabla p_{0}=\boldsymbol{f}&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{0}=f_{c}&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}=\boldsymbol{g}_{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,D},\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\boldsymbol{n}-p_{0}\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{h}_{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{0,N}.\end{cases}

(25)

This problem is well-posed in
V_{0}(\Omega_{0})\times Q_{0}(\Omega_{0})
with
V_{0}(\Omega_{0})\coloneqq\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{g}_{0},\Gamma_{0,D}}(\Omega_{0})
and
Q_{0}(\Omega_{0})\coloneqq L^{2}(\Omega_{0})
.

The defeaturing error functions in the velocity and pressure variables are defined by
\boldsymbol{e}_{u}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{u}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{|\Omega}
and
e_{p}\coloneqq p-(p_{0})_{|\Omega}
, respectively.
Moreover, we define the boundary error for each feature by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{g}_{|\gamma^{F}}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{|\gamma^{F}},&F\in\mathcal{F}_{D},\\[2.15277pt]
\boldsymbol{h}_{|\gamma^{F}}-(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\boldsymbol{n}_{|\gamma^{F}}-p_{0}\boldsymbol{n}_{|\gamma^{F}}),&F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}.\end{cases}

As in the Poisson and elasticity cases, an integration by parts argument shows that the defeaturing error can be expressed entirely through boundary terms:

\displaystyle||(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},e_{p})||_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})\boldsymbol{n}-e_{p}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

(26)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})\boldsymbol{n}-e_{p}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})\boldsymbol{n}-e_{p}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\int_{\gamma^{F}}\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma^{F}}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{|\gamma^{F}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Here, we again make the regularity assumption that
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})\boldsymbol{n}-e_{p}\boldsymbol{n}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. However, the analysis can be conducted under minimal regularity conditions; see
appendix

C
.

In direct analogy with the Poisson and elasticity cases, the boundary
representation motivates the following multi-feature estimator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},p_{0})\coloneqq\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DD}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{D,\mathrm{int}}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}\right.

(27)

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{DN}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},p_{0})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

with the feature-wise estimators

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{N}}^{\gamma^{F}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},p_{0})

6. Numerical experiments

We now validate the theoretical framework through numerical experiments for the Poisson, linear elasticity, and Stokes problems, assessing the reliability and performance of the goal-oriented estimators. The computational geometries and meshes were created with
gmsh
[
15
]
. Crucially,
gmsh
generates meshes that are conformal across lower-dimensional entities, allowing for the simultaneous meshing of the defeatured and exact geometries. In particular, comparing the defeatured to the exact solution is straightforward.

The finite element simulations were implemented with the help of the
FEniCSx
library
[
3
]
. Local mesh refinement around the features and higher-order elements were used to ensure that the numerical errors could mostly be neglected. For Stokes’ equations, Taylor-Hood elements were used to guarantee stability. However, we emphasize that the error estimation framework presented in this paper does not depend on the discretization method or the geometry, as long as numerical integration can be performed along the feature boundaries.

The
effectivity index

\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\gamma}(u_{0})/||u||_{\Omega}

will be used to analyze the performance of the estimators. Indeed, according to the theory, this quantity should be asymptotically independent of the feature size in the limit
|\gamma|\to 0
. Furthermore, it captures geometric properties that were neglected in the analysis, such as the curvature of the feature boundary.

7. Conclusion

This work establishes a mathematically certified framework for goal-oriented a posteriori error control in geometric defeaturing. By combining the dual-weighted residual (DWR) method
[
4
,
30
]
with rigorous energy-norm estimates, we derive certified error bounds for linear quantities of interest (QoIs). Our contributions are threefold: we established new reliable energy-norm estimators for Dirichlet features in linear elasticity and Stokes flow; we formulated unified estimators for geometries with mixed feature types; and we derived a certified goal-oriented framework applicable to the Poisson, linear elasticity, and Stokes problems that inherits this generality.

The current framework is limited to negative features. Based on existing estimators for positive Neumann features
[
10
]
, the corresponding goal-oriented estimates could be derived similarly. Positive Dirichlet features, however, first require the derivation of energy-norm estimates. Additionally, the current framework only provides an upper bound on the error. While the energy-norm estimates for the Neumann features are known to be efficient
[
7
,
10
]
, a suitable lower bound remains to be established for Dirichlet features. Hence, future work includes proving the efficiency of the Dirichlet estimators and developing a suitable framework for positive Dirichlet defeaturing.

The goal-oriented error estimation framework presented in this paper not only establishes a reliable error estimate but also offers a method for reconstructing the exact quantity of interest. This reconstruction is based solely on the defeatured primal and dual solutions, along with the feature boundary information. This approach could open new avenues for shape optimization.

The following subspace of the trace space

is essential for our analysis:

\displaystyle H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in L^{2}(\Lambda):\mu^{\star}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\right\},

(35)

where we write
\mu^{\star}
for the extension of

by 0 on

\displaystyle||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}^{2}+|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}^{2},

\displaystyle|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq|\mu|_{1/2,\Lambda}^{2}+\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\Lambda}\frac{|\mu(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y).

In particular, we have

\displaystyle||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}=||\mu^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}

and

\displaystyle|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}=|\mu^{\star}|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}.

These definitions generalize readily to the vector-valued versions of these spaces.

For subsets of the boundary

\displaystyle v\in H(\Div;\Omega),\,\mu\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda).

(36)

Finally, we point out that one can equip the trace space

with the equivalent natural trace norm given by

\displaystyle||\mu||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\coloneqq\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{\mu,\partial\Omega}(\Omega)}||u||_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle\forall\mu\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega).

(37)

We refer to Hsiao and Wendland

Appendix B
The estimator for Dirichlet features linear elasticity

This appendix is dedicated to proving the reliability of the a posteriori error estimators for Dirichlet features in the linear elasticity problem

,
19
, and
20

To that end, it suffices to consider the exact problem
eq.

12
and the defeatured problem
eq.

14
with one single feature of Dirichlet-Dirichlet, Dirichlet-Neumann or internal Dirichlet type. In this simplified case, the defeaturing error satisfies the following PDE:

\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})=\boldsymbol{0},&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0},&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\\
\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{0},&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\setminus\overline{\gamma},\\
\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma},&\text{ on }\gamma.\end{cases}

(38)

From an integration by parts and
eq.

38

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{e}||_{\Omega}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}):\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{e})\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(39)

To arrive at an error estimate, we must carefully study the Neumann trace
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})\boldsymbol{n}
on the right-hand side of
eq.

39
.
Accordingly, we define the space

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)\coloneqq\{\mathsf{s}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega):\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)\},

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle||\mathsf{s}||_{\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)}^{2}\coloneqq||\mathsf{s}||_{0,\Omega}^{2}+||\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}||_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

Then, the Neumann operator for the linear elasticity problem,
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}:\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)\to\boldsymbol{H}^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, is defined by integration by parts,

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}(\mathsf{s}),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{s}:\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\,\mathrm{d}x,

(40)

. This construction is similar to the one for the standard Neumann operator in Girault and Raviart
[
16
]
. This operator is clearly continuous since the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}(\mathsf{s}),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}||_{0,\Omega}||\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}||_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle+||\mathsf{s}||_{0,\Omega}|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}|_{1,\Omega}\leq||\mathsf{s}||_{\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)}||\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}||_{1,\Omega}.

Taking the infimum over all possible liftings
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}(\mathsf{s}),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle\leq||\mathsf{s}||_{\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)}||\boldsymbol{\mu}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)},

which proves the continuity with respect to the equivalent natural trace space norm
eq.

37
. Given a subset
\Lambda\subset\partial\Omega
with
|\Lambda|>0
, we may define the partial Neumann operator onto
\Lambda
as
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}^{\Lambda}:\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)\to\boldsymbol{H}^{-1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)
with

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\mathsf{s}),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle\coloneqq\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}(\mathsf{s}),\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\star}\rangle,

(41)

where
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\star}
denotes the extension by zero of
\boldsymbol{\mu}
to
\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
.

Remark B.1
.

We point out that if
\Lambda
itself has a boundary, then a result similar to

holds for
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}^{\Lambda}
asserting that its operator norm does not depend on the size of
\Lambda
.

Starting from the error representation in

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{e}||_{\Omega}^{2}=\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})),\boldsymbol{e}\rangle.

For Dirichlet-Dirichlet and internal features, we have

and in particular

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{e}||_{\Omega}^{2}=\langle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}^{\gamma}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e})),\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma}\rangle.

(42)

For Dirichlet-Neumann features, the boundary error

is not in

to

We circumvented the latter technicality in the main text by assuming that

due to additional regularity of the solution.
Let us now prove the reliability result for the linear elasticity problem:

Appendix C
The estimator for Dirichlet features in Stokes’ equations

This appendix presents the proof of reliability for the a posteriori error estimators for Dirichlet features

29
,
30
, and
31

theorem

. The proof strategy closely follows that of the linear elasticity case in

We first show that the pressure error can be controlled by the velocity error via the inf-sup stability condition of the Stokes problem. With the pressure error bounded, the remainder of the proof focuses on the velocity error and proceeds in direct analogy to the elasticity case, treating each of the three feature types in turn.

We consider the exact problem
eq.

22
and the defeatured problem
eq.

25
with one single Dirichlet feature again. Then the defeaturing error functions
\boldsymbol{e}_{u}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{u}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{|\Omega}
and

in the velocity and pressure variables, respectively, satisfy the PDE problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})+\nabla e_{p}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})\boldsymbol{n}-e_{p}\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\\
\boldsymbol{e}_{u}=\boldsymbol{0}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\setminus\overline{\gamma},\\
\boldsymbol{e}_{u}=\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma}&\text{ on }\gamma,\end{cases}

(49)

with a corresponding weak formulation similar to
eqs.

23
and
24
.
From integration by parts and using that
b_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},e_{p})=0
, we find

\displaystyle a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},\boldsymbol{e}_{u})=a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},\boldsymbol{e}_{u})+b_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},e_{p})=\int_{\partial\Omega}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})\boldsymbol{n}-e_{p}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(50)

As for the linear elasticity problem, we must study the Neumann operator on the right-hand side of
eq.

50
to derive energy-norm estimates.
To that end, we define the subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{(\mathsf{s},q)\in\mathbb{H}(\Div;\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\mid-\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}+\nabla q\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)\},

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle||(\mathsf{s},q)||_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}\coloneqq||\mathsf{s}||_{0,\Omega}+||q||_{0,\Omega}+||-\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}+\nabla q||_{0,\Omega}.

We define the Neumann operator for the Stokes problem,
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}:\mathcal{H}(\Omega)\to\boldsymbol{H}^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
by integration by parts:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}(\mathsf{s},q),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(-\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}+\nabla q)\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{s}:\mathbbm{e}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}})\,\mathrm{d}x,

for some
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\partial\Omega}(\Omega)
.
The continuity of the operator immediately follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}(\mathsf{s},q),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq||-\nabla\cdot\mathsf{s}+\nabla q||_{0,\Omega}||\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}||_{0,\Omega}+||\mathsf{s}||_{0,\Omega}|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}|_{1,\Omega}\leq||(\mathsf{s},q)||_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}||\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}||_{1,\Omega}.

Taking the infimum over all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\partial\Omega}(\Omega)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}(\mathsf{s},q),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle\leq||(\mathsf{s},q)||_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}||\boldsymbol{\mu}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)},

which proves the continuity with respect to the equivalent natural trace space norm
eq.

37
.
In analogy to the linear elasticity problem, given a subset
\Lambda\subset\partial\Omega
with
|\Lambda|>0
, we may define the partial Neumann operator onto
\Lambda
as
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}^{\Lambda}:\mathcal{H}(\Omega)\to\boldsymbol{H}^{-1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\mathsf{s},q),\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle\coloneqq\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}(\mathsf{s},q),\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\star}\rangle,

where
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\star}
denotes the extension by zero of
\boldsymbol{\mu}
to
\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)

Remark C.1
.

Similar to the Neumann operator for the linear elasticity problem, if

A
holds for

asserting that its operator norm does not depend on the size of

.

Using the boundary representation

\displaystyle a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},\boldsymbol{e}_{u})=\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})),\boldsymbol{e}_{u}\rangle.

In the Dirichlet-Dirichlet and internal cases, we have

\displaystyle a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},\boldsymbol{e}_{u})=\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}^{\gamma}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})),\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma}\rangle.

(51)

In the Dirichlet-Neumann case, we must again resort to the alternative error representation

\displaystyle a_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u},\boldsymbol{e}_{u})=\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\mathbbm{s}(\boldsymbol{e}_{u})),\mathsf{E}_{\gamma}^{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{d}_{\gamma})\rangle,

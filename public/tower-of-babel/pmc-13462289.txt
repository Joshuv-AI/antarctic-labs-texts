Experimental investigation of internal gas distribution characteristics in loaded water-bearing coal during N2 displacement
Authors: Xiaotong Lu, Hongmin Yang, Liwei Chen, Danhui Wang, Zhen Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the impact of residual moisture in coal bodies on gas distribution during gas injection following hydraulic permeability enhancement measures, N2 displacement experiments were conducted using large-sized water-bearing coal samples. This revealed the evolution characteristics of gas pressure and CO2 concentration within coal bodies under varying moisture contents and injection pressures. Results indicate that during gas injection, coal body pressure exhibits a trend of rapid initial increase followed by gradual decrease and eventual stabilization. Pressure response velocity diminishes with increasing distance from the injection port. CO2 concentration undergoes a phased change characterized by rapid initial decline followed by slow reduction, displaying significant spatial heterogeneity. Vertically, it exhibits a layered distribution pattern with higher concentrations in the lower section, lower concentrations in the middle section, and intermediate concentrations in the upper section. Under identical moisture content conditions, increasing injection pressure enhances gas diffusion and flow capacity. This leads to an overall rise in coal body gas pressure, accelerated pressure changes rates, easier CO2 expulsion, and faster concentration decline, thereby shortening displacement completion time. However, it also increases internal gas pressure and CO2 concentration gradients within the coal body. At constant injection pressure, increased coal moisture content impedes gas diffusion and migration. This leads to elevated pressures in all directions within the coal body, slowed pressure change rates, reduced CO2 expulsion, decreased concentration decline rates, and exacerbated concentration distribution heterogeneity, significantly diminishing displacement efficiency. Based on the aforementioned principles, this study proposes an integrated “injection-displacement-extraction” optimization scheme tailored for deep coalbeds containing water. It recommends employing zone-specific regulation, dynamic pressure adjustment, and coordinated drainage-injection processes, complemented by methane pressure and concentration monitoring, to enhance the overall efficiency and uniformity of methane displacement.

Introduction

China’s energy landscape, characterized by abundant coal reserves but limited oil and natural gas resources, has made coal the primary energy source in the country, a situation unlikely to undergo significant changes before 20501,2. Therefore, safe and efficient coal mining is of critical importance to societal development. In coal mining, coalbed methane is the primary threat to safe production, but it is also an unconventional clean energy source3,4. Extracting and utilizing coalbed methane can prevent methane accidents, improve energy efficiency, and reduce carbon emissions5. However, as mining intensity continues to increase, single methane extraction measures are no longer sufficient to meet actual needs, so more technologies are urgently needed to achieve efficient methane extraction.

Currently, there are two primary methods for improving the efficiency of coalbed methane extraction. The first method involves enhancing the permeability of the coal seam to reduce the difficulty of methane extraction. The second method focuses on increasing the energy of methane flow within the coal seam to facilitate its release from the coal body6. Regarding the first method, hydraulic and non-hydraulic measures have been proposed to alter the physical structure of the coal body, enhance its permeability, and promote methane desorption7,8. Non-hydraulic measures include liquid carbon dioxide phase change fracturing9,10, liquid nitrogen fracturing11,12, ultrasonic fracturing13,14, microwave radiation-induced fracturing15, high-voltage electric pulse fracturing16,17, and controlled shock wave fracturing18,19, among others. Hydraulic measures include hydraulic fracturing20–22, hydraulic slotting23,24, hydraulic punching25, and high-pressure water jet26,27, among others. For the second approach, methods such as injecting CO2, N2, air, and flue gas into the coal body have been proposed28–30, among others. Injecting gas into the coal seam not only displaces CH4, thereby increasing the desorption capacity of CH4, but also greatly improves the momentum of gas flow in the coal seam. This enables the CH4 desorbed from the coal to be carried away in a timely manner, thereby achieving the goal of promoting coalbed methane extraction.

However, as mining depths continue to increase, the current conditions of high geological stress and low permeability in coal seams have rendered the aforementioned single-approach solutions ineffective in reducing methane content in coal seams. Non-hydraulic measures are currently difficult to implement on a large scale due to their high technical complexity; coalbed fracturing technology, such as hydraulic fracturing, faces issues like uneven crack development and difficulty in maintaining crack pathways due to high geological stress; and coalbed gas injection yields unsatisfactory results due to the low permeability of the coalbed31. To address the challenges faced in deep coalbed methane extraction, a method has been proposed: first, hydraulic measures are used to increase permeability, followed by coalbed methane injection technology to promote extraction. This method aims to fully utilize the synergistic effects of increased permeability, increased flow energy, and enhanced desorption to improve methane extraction efficiency. However, residual water from hydraulic fracturing can occupy pore spaces, block fracture channels, and significantly increase gas migration resistance within the coal matrix, thereby potentially compromising the effectiveness of subsequent gas injection displacement. Moreover, the dynamic evolution of gas pressure and concentration fields within water-bearing coal bodies during gas injection remains poorly understood. This knowledge gap forms the core scientific question of the present study. Previous studies have primarily been based on dry small-scale coal samples, with limited research on the dynamic evolution of gas pressure and concentration within water-containing coal bodies after gas injection32,33. Based on this, this study utilized large-sized (500 × 500 × 500 mm) water-containing coal samples and conducted gas injection displacement experiments to monitor the real-time changes in gas pressure and concentration within the coal body during the injection process. Compared with conventional small-scale core samples, these large-scale briquette specimens better preserve the spatial continuity and structural integrity of the coal matrix, allowing for more realistic characterization of gas pressure and concentration field evolution under three-dimensional flow conditions. By comparing and analyzing the differences in gas distribution characteristics within the coal body under varying gas injection pressures and coal moisture content levels, the study reveals the influence of gas injection pressure and coal moisture content on gas distribution characteristics. This provides insights for the combined application of hydraulic fracturing and gas injection measures in the field, enabling the efficient extraction and utilization of methane from deep low-permeability coal seams.

Materials and methods

Experimental coal samples

The experiment used water-containing coal samples measuring 500 × 500 × 500 mm, which were difficult to obtain directly from the site. Therefore, water-containing coal samples were prepared by crushing and reshaping raw coal collected from the site. Although differences exist between briquette and raw coal, the permeability variation of the briquette with external conditions is similar to that of the raw coal. In addition, the briquette can simulate the post‑fracturing state. Thus, the substitution is considered reasonable34. The raw coal was selected from the Jiulishan Mine of the Coking Coal Group. The selection criteria for each parameter in Table 2 are as follows. N2was selected as the injection gas for its safety and cost‑effectiveness. The adsorption equilibrium pressure was set at 0.74 MPa, which corresponds to the critical outburst value for Chinese coal mines34. A confining pressure of 16 MPa was applied to the coal samples, equivalent to a burial depth of about 640 m and based on measured data from the Jiulishan Mine, to represent high‑stress and low‑permeability conditions40. Two injection pressures were adopted: 0.6 MPa (within the safe range) and 1.0 MPa (exceeding the conventional threshold, but the chamber’s pressure tolerance allowed safe investigation of high‑pressure displacement mechanisms)41. Moisture contents of 4% and 6% were chosen according to a previously determined critical moisture content of 4.5%. These levels correspond, respectively, to a water‑adsorption‑dominated state and a state with coexisting adsorbed and free water. Both values fall within the typical residual water range after hydraulic fracturing42,43.

Results and discussion

Effect of injection pressure on gas pressure field during gas injection displacement process

During the gas injection displacement process, variations in injection pressure cause alterations in the flow of gas within the coal, thereby affecting the gas pressure field until a new equilibrium is established. With measurement point 2# as the center, under constant moisture content conditions, a comparative analysis was conducted on the direction of gas flow within the coal body caused by changes in injection pressure, as well as the pressure change patterns at each measurement point in the lateral and vertical directions.

As shown in Fig. 4; Table 3, as the gas injection time increases, the gas pressure at each point along the gas flow direction first increases and then gradually decreases, eventually stabilizing. Among these, the pressure at point 1# is the highest, and the pressure at point 3# is the lowest, forming a relatively stable pressure difference along the gas injection axis. In the direction of gas flow, when the moisture content is the same, the maximum pressure under a gas injection pressure of 1.0 MPa is significantly higher than that under 0.6 MPa, and the time to reach the maximum pressure is also notably shorter than under 0.6 MPa. For example, when the coal body has a moisture content of 6%, the maximum pressure under 1.0 MPa is over 55% higher than under 0.6 MPa, while the time required is reduced by more than 40%. Additionally, the higher the injection pressure, the shorter the displacement completion time. Under the 4%−0.6 MPa conditions, the injection displacement completion time is 20,045 min. When the injection pressure is increased to 1.0 MPa, the displacement completion time is 9,843 min, a reduction of 50.89%. Under 6%–0.6 MPa conditions, the gas injection displacement completion time was 32,128 min. When the gas injection pressure was increased to 1.0 MPa, the displacement completion time was 19,698 min, a reduction of 38.69%.

Changes in gas pressure along the lateral direction.

As shown in Fig. 5; Table 4, from a spatial distribution perspective, along the same lateral direction, gas pressure increases with proximity to the injection axis; conversely, it gradually decreases with increasing distance. At the end of gas injection, although the lateral points are at different distances from the gas injection axis, the stable pressures differ only slightly. From the perspective of temporal changes, the gas pressure variation characteristics of the three measurement points along the same lateral direction are relatively consistent, all exhibiting a trend of rapid initial increase followed by gradual decrease, with the gas pressure eventually stabilizing. The farther the measurement points are from the injection axis, the more delayed the pressure changes; the closer they are, the more rapid the changes.

Under identical moisture content conditions, the gas pressure at each point along the horizontal axis increases significantly with rising injection pressure, and the rate of pressure increase also accelerates markedly. Consequently, the time required for each point to reach its maximum gas pressure value is substantially reduced. At a moisture content of 6%, measurement point 4# exhibited a maximum pressure of 0.44 MPa under 0.6 MPa N2 injection pressure. After pressurization to 1.0 MPa, the maximum pressure reached 0.69 MPa, representing a maximum pressure increase of 56.82%. Regarding time to reach peak pressure, measurement point 4# achieved its maximum at 2400 min under 0.6 MPa N2 injection pressure. After pressurization to 1.0 MPa, peak pressure was reached in 1100 min, representing a 54.17% reduction in time to peak pressure.

Effect of coal moisture content on gas pressure field during gas injection displacement process

The moisture content within the coal directly affects gas flow, which in turn influences the gas pressure field. Taking measurement point 2# as the center, we compared the gas flow direction and the lateral and vertical gas pressure changes within the coal body under conditions of constant injection pressure and varying moisture content.

Effect of coal moisture content on changes in gas pressure along the gas flow direction

Select data from measurement points 1#, 2#, and 3# in the direction of the coal body gas flow, and analyze the pressure changes in the direction of the coal body gas flow under different moisture contents, as shown in Fig. 7.

As shown in Fig. 7; Table 3, under the same gas injection pressure conditions in the gas flow direction, the peak gas pressure within coal bodies at 6% moisture content was generally higher than that at 4% moisture content. This indicates that increased moisture hinders gas permeation, causing localized pressure accumulation and subsequent gas pressure rise. Consequently, the time required for pressure to reach its maximum at each measurement point and the duration until gas displacement completion were significantly prolonged for the 6% moisture coal body. At an injection pressure of 0.6 MPa, the time to maximum pressure at measurement point 1# was 2005 min for the 6% moisture condition, compared to 733 min for the 4% moisture condition. At the end of displacement, gas pressures at all points along the flow direction in the 6% moisture content coal body exceeded those in the 4% moisture content coal body, with increased pressure differentials along the flow path. This indicates that moisture continued to impede gas flow during the late displacement phase, trapping more gas within the coal body.

As shown in Fig. 8; Table 4, under identical gas injection pressure conditions and at 6% moisture content, both the lateral pressure values and the pressure at the end of displacement within the coal body are higher than those at 4% moisture content. However, at 6% moisture content, the pressure rise rate at each point is slower, the time required to reach maximum pressure is significantly longer, and the duration needed to complete displacement is markedly extended. This indicates that laterally distributed water obstructs gas flow in the lateral direction, causing localized increases in gas pressure. Once pressure reaches a certain threshold, gas breaks through the water barrier to reach deeper lateral positions. Since measurement point 5# is farther from the lateral center, gas arrives there last, and the water’s obstruction of gas flow also results in its lowest pressure. Consequently, as coal moisture content increases, the lateral pressure differential within the coal body also grows. Furthermore, when injection pressure is elevated, the lateral pressure increase caused by moisture becomes more pronounced, and the gas pressure differential across lateral points also widens.

Effect of coal moisture content on changes in gas pressure along the vertical direction

As shown in Fig. 9; Table 5, under the same gas injection pressure conditions, the gas pressure values in the vertical direction within coal bodies with a 6% moisture content are slightly higher than those in coal bodies with a 4% moisture content. When the gas injection pressure is 0.6 MPa, the maximum pressure values at measurement points 2#, 6#, and 7# under the 6% moisture content condition are 0.44 MPa, 0.41 MPa, and 0.41 MPa, respectively, while under the 4% moisture content condition, they are 0.41 MPa, 0.40 MPa, and 0.39 MPa, respectively.

When the moisture content of the coal body is 4%, the gas pressure at measurement point 6# is greater than that at measurement point 7#, meaning that the pressure in the lower part in the vertical direction is greater than that in the upper part. However, when the moisture content of the coal body is 6%, the gas pressure at measurement point 6# is lower than that at measurement point 7#, meaning that the pressure in the upper part is greater than that in the lower part in the vertical direction. This is because the high moisture content, under the influence of gravity, accumulates at the lower part of the coal body, obstructing gas diffusion and resulting in lower gas pressure at the lower part.

Under high moisture content conditions, the time required for vertical gas pressure to reach its maximum value is significantly prolonged, indicating that moisture content has a significant negative impact on displacement efficiency. Additionally, compared to high moisture content coal bodies, low moisture content coal bodies exhibit a more uniform vertical pressure distribution; however, the pressure values at the end of displacement are lower, indicating reduced displacement efficiency. Increasing the gas injection pressure can mitigate the negative impact of high moisture content on displacement efficiency to some extent.

Exploration of engineering applications

The synergistic application of hydraulic permeability enhancement and gas injection-assisted extraction technologies is recognized as a key technical approach to solving the challenge of efficient gas extraction from deep, low-permeability coalbeds. However, the practical effectiveness of this combined technology is significantly constrained by the persistent influence of residual moisture within the coal body following hydraulic intervention. Following N2 injection into water-bearing coal bodies, the gas displacement process exhibits a coupled composite mechanism characterized by “filling-diffusion-replacement-carriage.” During the initial injection phase, N2 preferentially fills macropore fractures and free spaces within the coal matrix. Subsequently, it diffuses along concentration gradients into the micropore system, competing with coalbed methane for adsorption sites within the coal matrix. According to the extended Langmuir adsorption theory, continuous gas injection elevates the total system pressure and N2 partial pressure, thereby promoting desorption of adsorbed methane. Desorbed methane diffuses into interconnected fractures and migrates via seepage driven by pressure gradients. Simultaneously, N2 entering the fractures significantly dilutes methane, establishing new concentration gradients that further enhance diffusion mass transfer. The continuous decrease in gas concentration within micropores conversely promotes continuous desorption of gas from the coal matrix. However, as moisture content increases, gas diffusion and desorption within the coal are markedly inhibited. Particularly in the presence of free water, moisture not only occupies part of the adsorption pores but also blocks fracture flow pathways, significantly increasing gas migration resistance. Under these conditions, the initial gas injection phase typically exhibits a mixed entrainment mechanism involving “N2-driven water displacement—N2-driven gas displacement—water-driven gas displacement,” as shown in Fig. 16(a), (b), and (c). As free water is progressively expelled or migrates deeper into the coal matrix, fracture connectivity gradually recovers, ultimately enabling N2 to evolve into the primary medium governing gas migration and entrainment.

The evolution characteristics of gas pressure and concentration fields provide a crucial theoretical basis for rationally determining gas injection engineering parameters. Increasing injection pressure effectively elevates the overall pressure level within the coal body and significantly shortens pressure response time, but simultaneously amplifies the pressure gradient along the primary flow direction. Conversely, elevated coal moisture content delays pressure propagation, elevates local pressure peaks, and exacerbates the non-uniformity of both the pressure field and gas distribution. Regarding concentration field evolution, higher injection pressures accelerate overall methane concentration decay within the coal body. Conversely, at lower injection pressures, gas diffusion becomes more uniform both laterally and vertically. For a given injection pressure, increased coal moisture content significantly reduces the rate of methane concentration change and inhibits effective gas diffusion in non-mainstream directions, thereby diminishing overall displacement efficiency. Therefore, engineering practice must fully account for coalbed moisture conditions, implementing precise selection and dynamic regulation of injection pressure.

Based on the above understanding of mechanisms and evolutionary patterns, the following systematic engineering optimization recommendations are proposed:

Regarding process parameter design, it is recommended to implement a gas injection strategy of “zonal control and dynamic pressure adjustment.” For coal bodies with lower moisture content, gas injection pressure may be appropriately increased to achieve efficient and rapid gas displacement. For areas with higher moisture content, relatively lower injection pressure should be adopted to enhance gas diffusion uniformity. Concurrently, injection pressure may be dynamically adjusted as coal moisture content changes during displacement to balance spatial uniformity and displacement efficiency. Prior to implementation, conduct zoned identification and diagnosis of post-hydraulic fracturing coal moisture content. For high-water-content zones, considering the difficulty of achieving complete drainage under field conditions, we suggest a staged drainage-gas injection synergy strategy. First, allow a period of time for natural drainage and fracture stabilization to reduce water saturation in the fracture system. Then, at the early stage of gas injection, use a relatively low injection pressure to gradually open the seepage pathways. After the pressure response stabilizes, increase the injection pressure stepwise. This dynamic adjustment approach— “first drain, then inject at low pressure, and gradually increase pressure”—can achieve an effective match between injection parameters and coal seam water saturation even under incomplete drainage conditions. Regarding borehole and system layout, gas injection wells should be coordinated with methane pressure and concentration monitoring wells to establish a real-time feedback network for the injection process. Extraction wells should be positioned at the end of gas migration pathways and in methane accumulation zones to form a coordinated and efficient integrated “injection-displacement-extraction” system. Furthermore, during gas injection, the extraction system must maintain continuous and stable operation to ensure timely removal of displaced methane, as shown in Fig. 16(d) and (e).

In summary, by implementing parameter optimization to match gas injection conditions with coal body moisture levels, coordinating the layout of injection and extraction systems, and conducting dynamic monitoring and feedback throughout the entire process, significant positive effects can be achieved in enhancing the efficiency and safety of gas extraction from deep, low-permeability coal seams.

Induced-Gravity Higgs Inflation in Palatini Supergravity Confronts ACT DR6
C. Pallis School of Technology, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, Thessaloniki, GR-541 24 GREECE;

Abstract
We formulate within Palatini Supergravity a model of induced-gravity inflation excellently consistent with ACT DR6. The inflaton belongs in the decomposition of a conjugate pair of Higgs superfields which lead to the spontaneous breaking of a U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} symmetry at a scale close to the range ( 0.102 − 5.85 ) ⋅ 10 16 ​ GeV (0.102-5.85)\cdot 10^{16}~{\mbox{\rm GeV}} . The inflaton field is canonically normalized thanks to one real and shift-symmetric contribution into the Kähler potential. It also includes two separate holomorphic and antiholomorphic logarithmic terms, the argument of which can be interpreted as the coupling of the inflaton to the Ricci scalar. The attainment of inflation allows for subplanckian inflaton values and energy scales below the cut-off scale of the corresponding effective theory. Embedding the model in a B − L {B-L} extension of the MSSM we show how the μ \mu parameter can be generated and non-thermal leptogenesis can be successfully realized. An outcome of our scheme is split SUSY with gravitino mass in the range ( 40 − 60 ) ​ PeV (40-60)~{\mbox{\rm PeV}} , which is consistent with the results of LHC on the Higgs boson mass. Keywords : Cosmology, Inflation, Supersymmetric Models PACS codes : 98.80.Cq, 12.60.Jv, 95.30.Cq, 95.30.Sf

2.1
Set-up

Our starting point is the superpotential known from the models of
F-term hybrid inflation
[
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
]

W_{\rm IPI}=\lambda S\left(\bar{\Phi}\Phi-M^{2}/4\right)

(2)

which is uniquely determined, at renormalizable level, by a gauge
symmetry
\mathbb{G}
and a convenient continuous
R
symmetry. Here,

\lambda
and
M
are two constants which can both be taken positive
by field redefinitions;
S
is a left-handed superfield, singlet
under
\mathbb{G}
;
\bar{\Phi}
and
\Phi
is a pair of left-handed
superfields belonging to non-trivial conjugate representations of

\mathbb{G}
, and reducing its rank by their v.e.vs. Just for
definiteness we restrict ourselves to
\mathbb{G}_{B-L}=\mathbb{G}_{\rm SM}\times U(1)_{B-L}

which consists the simplest GUT beyond the MSSM – here
\mathbb{G}_{\rm SM}

is the gauge group of MSSM and
B
and
L
denote the baryon and
lepton number. With the specific choice of
\mathbb{G}
the

U(1)_{B-L}
and
R
charges of the various superfields are

(B-L)(S,\bar{\Phi},\Phi)=(0,-2,2)~~~\mbox{and}~~~R(S,\bar{\Phi},\Phi)=(2,0,0).

(3)

At the SUSY limit we expect that
W
leads to a
B-L
phase
transition as we show in Sec.
2.2.2
below.

The realization of IPI with the inflaton included in the

\bar{\Phi}-\Phi
system and
S
stabilized at the origin requires a
careful choice of
K
– as, e.g., in Ref.
[
77
,
75
,
67
,
74
]
.
The proposed here
K
includes two contributions without mixing
between
\bar{\Phi}-\Phi
and
S
which reads

K=K_{\rm I}+K_{\rm st},

(4)

from which
K_{\rm st}
successfully stabilizes
S
at the origin during
IPI without invoking higher order terms – cf. Ref.
[
111
]
. We
adopt the form
[
110
]

K_{\rm st}=N_{\rm st}\ln\left(1+{|S|^{2}/N_{\rm st}}\right)~~\mbox{with}~~0<N_{\rm st}<6

(5)

which parameterizes
[
110
]
the compact manifold
SU(2)/U(1)

with curvature
2/N
. On the other hand,
K_{\rm I}
depends on the

\bar{\Phi}-\Phi
sector and includes the functions

F_{\cal R}(\Phi)=4c_{\cal R}\bar{\Phi}\Phi~~\mbox{and}~~F_{\rm sh}=|\Phi-\bar{\Phi}^{*}|^{2},

(6)

from which
F_{\cal R}
is holomorphic and can be employed as the
non-minimal coupling to gravity, whereas
F_{\rm sh}
is real, assisting
us to incorporate the canonical kinetic mixing. With the
ingredients above, we construct
K_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
4
) which is

K_{\rm I}=-\frac{N}{2}\ln F_{\cal R}-\frac{N}{2}\ln F_{\cal R}^{*}+F_{\rm sh}.

(7)

It is worth noticing that contrary to the embedding of IPI in
metric SUGRA – cf. Ref.
[
77
,
78
]
–,
F_{\cal R}
and
F_{\cal R}^{*}
enter
two different terms in Eq. (
7
) and so they give zero
contribution into the Kähler metric, which takes the form

M_{\bar{\Phi}\Phi}=\left(K_{\rm I{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}\right)={\sf diag}\left(1,1\right),

(8)

where we use the standard notation
K_{\rm I{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}=K_{\rm I,z^{\alpha}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}}
with
z^{\alpha}=\Phi,\bar{\Phi}
.

2.2
Implementation of IG Condition

The scale

and the function

involved in Eqs. (

2.2.1
From Einstein to Jordan Frame

The part of the
Einstein frame
(
EF
) action within
SUGRA related to the complex scalars
z^{\alpha}=\Phi,\bar{\Phi},S
–
denoted by the same superfield symbol – has the form

[
111
]

{\sf S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}+K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}\widehat{g}^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}D_{\nu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-\widehat{V}\right)\,,

(9a)

where
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the determinant of the EF metric

\widehat{g}_{\mu\nu}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
is the EF Ricci scalar curvature,
D_{\mu}

is the gauge covariant derivative, and
K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}
is the
inverse of the Kähler metric defined as

K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}K_{{\bar{\beta}}\gamma}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\gamma}
. Also,
\widehat{V}
is
the EF SUGRA potential which includes F- and D- term contributions
which can be found in terms of
W
and
K
in Eqs. (
2
) and (
4
)
via the formula

\widehat{V}=e^{K}\left(K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}(D_{\alpha}W_{\rm IPI})D^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}W_{\rm IPI}^{*}-3{|W_{\rm IPI}|^{2}}\right)+\frac{g^{2}}{2}{\rm D}_{BL}^{2},

(9b)

where
g
is the gauge coupling constant – assumed unified with
the others of
\mathbb{G}_{\rm SM}
– and we define the Kähler covariant derivative
and the D terms due to
\mathbb{G}_{B-L}
as

D_{\alpha}W_{\rm IPI}=W_{{\rm IPI},z^{\alpha}}+K_{,z^{\alpha}}W_{\rm IPI}~~\mbox{and}~~{\rm D}_{BL}=\Phi K_{\Phi}-\bar{\Phi}K_{\bar{\Phi}}=\left({|\Phi|^{2}-|\bar{\Phi}|^{2}}\right).

(10)

Therefore, a field configuration with vanishing
{\rm D}_{BL}
is

\langle{\Phi}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\bar{\Phi}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\phi/2~~\mbox{and}~~\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=0,

(11)

where take into account additionally the stabilization of
S
and
we introduce the symbol
\langle{...}\rangle_{\rm I}
to denote values during IPI.
Henceforth, we confine ourselves to this path – assuming
furthermore that
{\small\sf arg}(\Phi)={\small\sf arg}(\bar{\Phi})
– which is a honest
inflationary trajectory – as we show in Sec.
3.1
–,
supporting IPI driven exclusively by
\widehat{V}_{\rm F}
.

The action in Eq. (
9a
) can be brought into the
Jordan
frame
(JF) by performing a conformal transformation
[
111
,
67
]
. Motivated by the accordance with
P-ACT-LB-BK18
data obtained by
inflationary models based on Palatini gravity
[
24
]
we
here adopt this formalism. We, therefore, define the JF metric,

g_{\mu\nu}
, through the relation

\widehat{g}_{\mu\nu}=-({\Omega}/{N})g_{\mu\nu}

(12a)

where
\Omega
is the frame function, and we obtain the following
relations between hatted (EF) and unhatted (JF) quantities

\sqrt{-\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}={\Omega^{2}\over N^{2}}\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}},~~\widehat{g}^{\mu\nu}=-{N\over\Omega}g^{\mu\nu}\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=-{N\over\Omega}\mathcal{R}.

(12b)

Here we keep in mind that the Ricci tensor does not change since
both
g_{\mu\nu}
and the connection
\Gamma
are treated as
independent variables in the adopted approach. Also
\mathfrak{g}

is the determinant of
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\mathcal{R}
is the JF Ricci
scalar curvature. Upon substitution of Eq. (
12a
) into
Eq. (
9a
) we end up with the following action in the JF

{\sf S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(\frac{\Omega}{2N}\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{N}\Omega K_{{\alpha}{{\bar{\beta}}}}D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}D^{\mu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-V\right)\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>V=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{N^{2}}\widehat{V}.

(12c)

If, in addition, we connect
\Omega
to
K
in Eq. (
4
)
through the following relation

-{\Omega}/N=e^{-K/N}=F_{\cal R}^{1/2}F_{\cal R}^{*1/2}e^{-F_{\rm sh}/N}(1+|S|^{2}/N_{\rm st})^{-N_{\rm st}/N}

(13a)

we arrive at the following action

{\sf S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(\frac{\Omega}{2N}\mathcal{R}+\left(\Omega_{{\alpha}{{\bar{\beta}}}}-\frac{\Omega_{{\alpha}}\Omega_{{\bar{\beta}}}}{\Omega}\right)D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}D^{\mu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-V\right),

(13b)

where we use the shorthand notation
\Omega_{\alpha}=\Omega_{,z^{\alpha}}

and
\Omega_{\bar{\alpha}}=\Omega_{,z^{*{\bar{\alpha}}}}
. Comparing the resulting
expression with that in the metric formulation
[
111
,
67
]

we remark that there is no need of introduction of the purely
bosonic part of the on-shell value of an auxiliary field. As in
that case, however, the first term in the r.h.s of Eq. (
13b
)
reveals that
-\Omega/N
plays the role of a non-minimal coupling
to gravity. The emergence of the conventional Einstein gravity at
the vacuum dictates

-\langle{{\Omega}/{N}}\rangle=m_{\rm P}^{2},

(14)

where we restore the presence of
m_{\rm P}
in the condition above for
convenience.

2.2.2
IG conjecture

The IG hypothesis requires the generation of

at the vacuum

(15a)

where the ellipsis represents terms proportional to
W

which obviously vanish along the path in Eq. (
11

Also,

from which we can then compute the Kähler metric

which is identical to that in Eq. (

) we obtain

V_{\rm SUSY}\simeq(F_{\cal R}F_{\cal R}^{*})^{-N/2}\left((\lambda^{2}\left|\bar{\Phi}\Phi-{M^{2}/4}\right|^{2}+\lambda^{2}|S|^{2}\left(|\Phi|^{2}+|\bar{\Phi}|^{2}\right)\right).

We can easily infer that the SUSY vacuum lies along the direction

which is included in the inflationary path of Eq. (

see below – in Sec.

The scale of

breaking can be related to the magnitude
of
c_{\cal R}

substitution of Eq. (

3.2.2
Theoretical Considerations

We may qualify better our models by taking into account three
additional restrictions of theoretical origin. In particular, we
consider the following issues:

(a) Connection With the Gauge Coupling
Unification

The
U(1)_{B-L}
gauge symmetry does not generate any extra
contribution to the renormalization group running of the gauge
coupling constants of
\mathbb{G}_{\rm SM}
and so the scale
M
and the relevant
gauge coupling constant,
g
can be different than the values
dictated by the MSSM unification of the gauge coupling constants
which gives
g\simeq 0.7
with
M_{\mathbb{G}}\simeq 20~{\mbox{\rm YeV}}
. Note that this
result is valid also in case of split SUSY
[
104
]
which
fits well with our scheme – see Sec.
4.2
. In our approach we
mostly keep the
g
value above and let
M_{\mathbb{G}}
varying. The
connection with IG condition can be done if we identify
M_{\mathbb{G}}

with the mass of the gauge boson,
M_{BL}
in Table
1
,
computed at the vacuum of Eq. (
17
),
\langle{M_{BL}}\rangle
. Such an
approach allows to find
c_{\cal R}
in terms of
M_{\rm GUT}
as follows

\langle{M_{BL}}\rangle=g\langle{\phi}\rangle=M_{\mathbb{G}}\>\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{igc2})}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\>\>c_{\cal R}=g^{2}m_{\rm P}^{2}/M_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}.

(30)

It is beneficial that our scheme independently constrains the
allowed margin of
M_{\mathbb{G}}
(and via it
M
) for given
g
as we
show in Sec.
3.3.2
.

(b) Validity of the Effective Theory

Despite the fact that the resulting
c_{\cal R}
in Eq. (
30
) is very
large, the effective theory is valid up to a high enough UV
cut-off scale
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
which can be derived by analyzing

[
24
]
the small-field behavior of our models in the EF –
cf. Ref.
[
84
,
82
,
80
,
81
,
85
,
86
,
79
,
83
]
. Given that
\phi

is canonically normalized we focus exclusively on
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}
, see
Eq. (
21a
), and expand it in terms of
\delta\phi=\phi-M
about the
vacuum in Eq. (
17
). Neglecting irrelevant
\delta N
-depended
contributions we arrive at the result

\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}\simeq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{64c_{\cal R}^{3}\phi^{2}}\left(1-3\frac{\delta\phi}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}}+\frac{25}{4}\frac{\delta\phi^{2}}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{2}}-11\frac{\delta\phi^{3}}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{3}}+\frac{35}{2}\frac{\delta\phi^{4}}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{4}}+\cdots\right)~~\mbox{with}~~\Lambda_{\rm UV}=\frac{m_{\rm P}}{\sqrt{c_{\cal R}}},

for any
|\delta N|\ll 1
. Although
\Lambda_{\rm UV}<m_{\rm P}

\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}(\phi)
for the
\phi

On the other hand, the fact that
\phi_{\star}\gg\Lambda_{\rm UV}
– which causes, in
principle, concerns regarding corrections from non-renormalizable
terms associated with
\Lambda_{\rm UV}

since it is widely believed that dangerous loop-corrections depend
on the energy scale and not on the field values. Conservatively,
we impose
\phi_{\star}<1

order terms. Indeed, terms of the form
(\bar{\Phi}\Phi)^{\ell}
with

\ell>1
in Eqs. (
2

4

and so their harmlessness can be only ensured for this range of

\phi

4.2
Generation of the
\mu
Term of MSSM

The contributions from the soft SUSY-breaking terms, although
negligible during IPI – since these are much smaller than

\phi\sim m_{\rm P}
– may shift slightly
\langle{S}\rangle
from zero in
Eq. (
17
). Indeed, the relevant potential terms are

V_{\rm soft}=\left(\lambda A_{\lambda}S\bar{\Phi}\Phi+\lambda_{\mu}A_{\mu}S{H_{u}}{H_{d}}+\lambda_{iN^{c}}A_{iN^{c}}\Phi\widetilde{N}^{c2}_{i}-{\rm a}_{S}S\lambda M^{2}/4+{\rm h.c.}\right)+m_{{\alpha}}^{2}\left|X^{\alpha}\right|^{2},

(49)

where
m_{{\alpha}},A_{\lambda},A_{\mu},A_{iN^{c}}
and
{\rm a}_{S}
are soft
SUSY-breaking mass parameters. Rotating
S
in the real axis by
an appropriate
R
transformation, choosing conveniently the
phases of
A_{\lambda}
and
{\rm a}_{S}
so as the total low energy potential

V_{\rm tot}=V_{\rm SUSY}+V_{\rm soft}
to be minimized – see
Eq. (
16
) – and substituting
{H_{u}}={H_{d}}=0
and
\Phi=\bar{\Phi}=M/2

from Eq. (
17
) we get

\langle{V_{\rm tot}(S)}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}{M^{2}S^{2}}-\lambda M^{2}S{\rm a}_{\mu}m_{3/2}~~\mbox{with}~~~{\rm a}_{\mu}=\left(|A_{\lambda}|+|{\rm a}_{S}|\right)/2m_{3/2},

(50a)

where
m_{3/2}
is the
\widetilde{G}
(gravitino) mass and
{\rm a}_{\mu}>0
is a
parameter of order unity which parameterizes our ignorance for the
dependence of
|A_{\lambda}|
and
|{\rm a}_{S}|
on
m_{3/2}
. We also
take into account that
m_{S}\ll M
. The extermination condition
for
\langle{V_{\rm tot}(S)}\rangle
w.r.t
S
leads to a non vanishing

\langle{S}\rangle
as follows

{d}\langle{V_{\rm tot}(S)}\rangle/{dS}=0~~\Rightarrow~~~\langle{S}\rangle\simeq{\rm a}_{\mu}m_{3/2}/{\lambda},

(50b)

The extremum above is a global minimum since
{d^{2}}\langle{V_{\rm tot}(S)}\rangle/{dS^{2}}=\lambda^{2}M^{2}>0
. The generated
\mu
parameter from
the first term in Eq. (
41a
) is

\mu=\lambda_{\mu}\langle{S}\rangle\simeq{\lambda_{\mu}}{\rm a}_{\mu}m_{3/2}/\lambda.

(51)

This results reveals that
\mu/m_{3/2}
follows the behavior of the
ratio
\lambda_{\mu}/\lambda
shown below Eq. (
46
). The emerged hierarchy
between
\mu
and
m_{3/2}
is mildly stronger than that obtained in
Ref.
[
74
,
92
,
77
,
78
,
45
]
, rendering our present setting
apparently distinguishable from those. Namely, our present result
hints at split SUSY
[
103
,
104
]
, where the soft SUSY
scalar mass parameters – and therefore, the SUSY mass scale,

\widetilde{m}
– are expected to lie at an intermediate mass scale
whereas
\mu
and the gaugino masses can be kept of the order of

TeV
. As a consequence, the candidacy of the
lightest SUSY
particle
(
LSP
) – which is normally the lightest
neutralino,
\tilde{\chi}
– as
cold dark matter
(
CDM
)
particle remains possible and the gauge coupling unification is
still valid for
g
and
M_{\mathbb{G}}
values similar to those used in
the conventional MSSM.

Given that the LEP bound on the mass of charginos
[
117
]

dictates
\mu>0.1~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}
, Eq. (
51
) consistently with
Eqs. (
36
) and (
30
) favors
m_{3/2}>1~{\mbox{\rm PeV}}
. On the other hand, if

\widetilde{m}
is identified with
m_{3/2}
, our proposal can be probed via
the measured value of the Higgs boson mass
[
118
]
. Within
split SUSY, updated analysis requires
[
105
]

10~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}\lesssim m_{3/2}\lesssim 60~{\mbox{\rm PeV}},

(52)

for degenerate sparticle spectrum and varying
\tan\beta
and stop
mixing. The lower bound above is moved up to
20~{\mbox{\rm PeV}}
for low

\tan\beta
values and minimal stop mixing. The upper bound above
can be moved up to
100~{\mbox{\rm PeV}}
for scalar mass parameters larger or
smaller than
m_{3/2}
by a factor of
3
. Note that the upper bound
on Eq. (
52
) automatically renders the gluino with mass of order

1~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}
cosmologically safe
[
119
]
.

4.3
Non-Thermal Leptogenesis after IPI

Besides the generation of the
\mu

\Delta W
in Eq. (

)
allows for the implementation of nTL via the direct decay of the
inflaton into
N^{c}_{i}
– see Sec.
4.3.1
. For the success of this
process, however, one has to produce the correct baryon asymmetry
– see Sec.
4.3.2
– and circumvent the
\widetilde{G}
overproduction
problem as discussed in Sec.
4.3.3
.

4.3.1
Inflaton Mass & Decay

Soon after the end of IPI, we obtain a very short period of OI –
see Appendix
5
– which is followed by the usual phase of
damped oscillations abound the minimum in Eq. (
17
) where the
(canonically normalized) inflaton
\delta\phi=\phi-M
acquires mass

m_{\rm\delta\phi}=\left\langle\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI,\phi\phi}\right\rangle^{1/2}={\lambda m_{\rm P}}/{\sqrt{2c_{\cal R}}},

(53)

which ranges in the interval
(4.76-36)\cdot 10^{-1}~{\mbox{\rm YeV}}
with
decreasing
M_{\mathbb{G}}
(or increasing
c_{\cal R}
). Note that it does not
depend on
N
(and
\delta N
) thanks to the canonical normalization of

\delta\phi
. This oscillatory period terminates at the moment of
reheating where the temperature of the universe acquires the value

[
45
]

T_{\rm rh}=\left({72/5\pi^{2}g_{*}}\right)^{1/4}\left({\Gamma}_{\rm\delta\phi}m_{\rm P}\right)^{1/2}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>{\Gamma}_{\rm\delta\phi}={\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow N_{i}^{c}}+{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow H}+\Gamma_{\delta\phi\rightarrow XYZ},

(54)

where
g_{*}=228.75
counts the MSSM effective number of
relativistic degrees of freedom and we take into account the
following decay widths

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow N_{i}^{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{iN^{c}}^{2}}{16\pi}m_{\rm\delta\phi}\left(1-{4M_{[N^{c}}i]^{2}}/{m_{\rm\delta\phi}^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>g_{iN^{c}}=(N-1){\lambda_{iN^{c}}};

(55a)

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{8\pi}g_{H}^{2}m_{\rm\delta\phi}\>\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>\>g_{H}={\lambda_{\mu}}/{\sqrt{2}};

(55b)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\delta\phi\rightarrow XYZ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{14g_{y}^{2}}{512\pi^{3}}\frac{m_{\rm\delta\phi}^{3}}{m_{\rm P}^{2}}\>\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>\>g_{y}=y_{3}Nc_{\cal R}^{1/2}.

(55c)

Here
y_{3}=h_{t,b,\tau}(m_{\rm\delta\phi})\simeq 0.5
with
h_{t},h_{b}
and

h_{\tau}
being the Yukawa coupling constants – we assume that
diagonalization has been performed in the generation space. The
various decay widths above arise from the respective lagrangian
terms

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow N^{c}_{i}N^{c}_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}e^{K/2m_{\rm P}^{2}}\Delta W_{,N_{i}^{c}N^{c}_{i}}N^{c}_{i}N^{c}_{i}\ +\ {\rm h.c.}=g_{iN^{c}}\delta\phi\ \left(N^{c}_{i}N^{c}_{i}\ +\ {\rm h.c.}\right)+\cdots,

(56a)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow{H_{u}}{H_{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{K/m_{\rm P}^{2}}K^{SS^{*}}\left|\Delta W_{,S}\right|^{2}=-g_{H}m_{\rm\delta\phi}\delta\phi\left(H_{u}^{*}H_{d}^{*}\ +\ {\rm h.c.}\right)+\cdots,

(56b)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow XYZ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{y}(\delta\phi/m_{\rm P})\left(X\psi_{Y}\psi_{Z}+Y\psi_{X}\psi_{Z}+Z\psi_{X}\psi_{Y}+{\rm h.c.}\right),

(56c)

describing
\delta\phi
decay into a pair of
N^{c}_{j}
with masses

M_{[N^{c}}j]=\lambda_{jN^{c}}M
,
{H_{u}}
and
{H_{d}}
and three MSSM
(s)-particles
X,Y
and
Z
, respectively. We remark that
g_{y}

is proportional to
c_{\cal R}^{1/2}
and therefore it is rather enhanced
due to the large
c_{\cal R}
values employed in our scheme – see, e.g.,
Eq. (
40
).

4.3.3

(61)

where we assume that
\widetilde{G}
decays with a tiny hadronic branching

\widetilde{G}
, and
assume that
\widetilde{G}

[

\widetilde{G}
mass limit with

and
N_{\rm gs}=12
being
[
121
]
the number of
chiral superfields (neglecting
N^{c}_{i}
’s) and the number of
gauginos in the MSSM respectively. Requiring that

is larger

\tilde{\chi}

61

5
Conclusions

We retrofitted our proposal in Ref.
[
77
,
78
]
to obtain ample
consistency with the recent ACT DR6 extending to gauge non-singlet
inflaton one of the models constructed in Ref.
[
24
]
. Namely,
we adopted the super- and Kähler potentials
W
and
K
in
Eqs. (
2
) and (
4
) which lead to the inflationary potential in
Eq. (
21a
) with canonical kinetic term – cf. Ref.
[
79
]
. The free
parameters of this potential
(\lambda,M,c_{\cal R},N)
are constrained at
the vacuum of the theory imposing the IG and gauge
coupling-unification conditions in Eqs. (
18
) and (
30
) besides the
inflationary constraints in Eq. (
27
). The aforementioned
requirements influenced via
M_{\mathbb{G}}
in Eq. (
30
) our
inflarionary scenario and via the strong decay width in
Eq. (
55c
) our post-inflationary scenario.

The model naturally predicts
(n_{\rm s},r)
values within the

1-\sigma
margin of ACT DR6 as shown in Fig.
2
-
(b)
without
any tuning and constraining the allowed margin of
M_{\mathbb{G}}
to
values around the “sweet” spot predicted by the gauge
unification within MSSM – see Eq. (
40
). As a consequence, our
present framework drastically reduces the allowed parameter space
found for a gauge-singlet inflaton with quartic potential in
Ref.
[
24
]
. We also specified a post-inflationary completion,
based on the extra
W
terms in Eq. (
41a
) and
K_{2}
in Eq. (
41b
),
which favors split SUSY with
m_{3/2}\simeq(40-60)~{\mbox{\rm PeV}}
and allows
for baryogenesis via nTL with
T_{\rm rh}
of the order of
10~{\mbox{\rm ZeV}}
.
The problems with the
\widetilde{G}
cosmology are surpassed by arranging

\widetilde{G}
to decay before the freeze-out of the neutralino LSP with
mass
m_{\tilde{\chi}}\leq 2~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}
. In the context of split SUSY the relevant

m_{3/2}
range (which is considered identical with the SUSY mass

) is consistent with the Higgs-boson mass discovered
in LHC.

Oscillating Inflation and Reheating

Trying to determine the mean
equation-of-state parameter
w

[
115
,
116
]
after the end
of IPI we find (surprisingly) that the derived value not only
deviates hardly from that obtained within the models of chaotic
inflation but also it is close to
-1
hinting at an additional
inflationary period. Similar findings have been already reported
for other models of inflation – see, e.g., Ref.
[
108
,
109
]
.
To clarify this peculiarity we solve numerically the equation of
motion for the inflaton field as detailed in Sec.
.1
, we
revaluate
w
as described in Sec.
.2
and justify the used
formula of
\widehat{N}_{\star}
in Eq. (
27
) – see Sec.
.3
.

.1
Equation of Motion

To check if the period of OI
[
106
,
107
]
has a sizable impact
on the post-inflationary era, we solve numerically the relevant
equation of motion for the inflaton
\phi
. In particular, the
cosmological evolution of
\phi
in the EF is governed by the
following equation – see, e.g., Ref.
[
116
]
:

\ddot{\phi}+3\widehat{H}\dot{\phi}+\widehat{V}_{{\rm IPI},\phi}=0\>\>\>

(68)

where dot denotes derivation w.r.t. the cosmic time
t
,
\widehat{H}
is
the Hubble parameter in the EF and
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}
is given in Eq. (
21a
).
In Eq. (
68
), we neglect the damping term
{\Gamma}_{\rm\delta\phi}\dot{\phi}
which
is important only at the final stage of rapid oscillations of

\phi
near the minimum of
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}

[
115
]
.

The solution of Eq. (
68
) can be facilitated, if we use as
independent variable the (total) number of e-foldings
\uptau

defined by

\uptau=\ln\widehat{R}/\widehat{R}_{\rm i}~\Rightarrow~\dot{}\uptau=\widehat{H}\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\dot{}\widehat{H}=\widehat{H}^{\prime}\widehat{H}\,.

(69)

Here the prime denotes derivation w.r.t.
\uptau,\widehat{R}(t)
is
the EF scale factor and
\widehat{R}_{\rm i}
is its value at the
commencement of IPI, which turns out to be numerically
irrelevant. Converting the time derivatives to derivatives w.r.t.

\uptau
, Eq. (
68
) is equivalent to the following system of
two first order equations

F_{\phi}=\widehat{H}\widehat{R}^{3}\phi^{\prime}\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\widehat{H}F_{\phi}^{\prime}=-\widehat{V}_{{\rm IPI},\phi}\widehat{R}^{3}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>F_{\phi}=\dot{\phi}\widehat{R}^{3}.

(70)

This system can be solved numerically by taking

\widehat{H}=\sqrt{\rho_{\phi}\over 3m_{\rm P}^{2}}~~\mbox{with}~~\rho_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{}\phi^{2}+\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}=\frac{F_{\phi}^{2}}{2\widehat{R}^{6}}+\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}

(71)

the energy density of
\phi
during and after IPI. To resolve
Eq. (
70
) we impose the initial conditions (at
\uptau=0
)

\phi(0)=(0.5-1)m_{\rm P}
and
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
. We checked that our results
are pretty stable against variation of
\phi(0)
.

To gain a deeper understanding of the
\phi
evolution during and
after IPI, we plot in Fig.
4
-
(a)
the evolution of
\phi
as a
function of
\uptau
for the inputs used in Fig.
1
, i.e.,

M_{\mathbb{G}}=20~{\mbox{\rm YeV}}
,
\delta N=0
(solid line) and
\delta N=-10^{-4}
(dashed
line). In both cases, we use
\phi(0)=0.9m_{\rm P}
and indicate the
points corresponding to
\phi(\uptau_{\ast})=\phi_{\star}
and
\phi(\uptau_{\rm f})=\phi_{\rm f}
– the relevant
\phi
values are listed in the Table of
Fig.
1
. Obviously we obtain
\widehat{N}_{\star}=\uptau_{\rm f}-\uptau_{\ast}\simeq 52
in both cases. We remark that
\phi

decreases very slowly during the IPI and then sharply from its
value
\phi_{\rm f}
until to relax into its v.e.v in Eq. (
17
).
Note that the last period is completed in a very limited
\uptau

domain compared to the whole
\uptau
region, along which IPI takes place. To investigate further this last period we focus on
it in Fig.
4
-
(b)
for
\delta N=0
. We see there that
\phi
passes
from the minimum of
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}
at
\langle{\phi}\rangle=0.01174
– see
Fig.
1
– and then climbs up the hill of
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}
for a little
and oscillates backwards. This path is followed a lot of times
until
\phi
falls finally into its minimum. The separation of the
period of OI from that of the damped oscilltion can be done by
estimating
w
and analyzing the behavior of the decreasing
maximal
\phi
values
\phi_{\rm m}
, as we do in the next section.

.2
Equation-of-state Parameters

We determine
w
applying the general formula
[
116
,
112
]
,
i.e.

w=2\frac{\int_{M}^{\phi_{\rm m}}d\phi\,(1-\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}/\widehat{V}_{\rm m})^{1/2}}{\int_{M}^{\phi_{\rm m}}d\phi\,(1-\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}/\widehat{V}_{\rm m})^{-1/2}}-1,\>\>\mbox{where}\>\>\widehat{V}_{\rm m}=\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}(\phi_{\rm m})

(72)

In general
w
depends on the amplitude of oscillations
\phi_{\rm m}

[
115
]
and it is not just a constant as in the well-known
models of monomial potentials. The solution of Eq. (
70
) gives us
the opportunity to evaluate
w
for various
\phi_{\rm m}
’s and asses
which periods can be successfully described by the the resulting

. Indeed, according the definition above,
\rho_{\phi}
in

) has to be well approximated by
[

,

]

\rho_{\phi}(\uptau)=V_{\rm m}e^{-3(1+w)(\uptau-\uptau_{\rm m})}

(73)

for
\uptau>\uptau_{\rm m}
where
\phi(\uptau_{\rm m})=\phi_{\rm m}
.
Estimating repetitively
w
we find two values
w_{\rm oi}=-0.07
and

with
\phi_{\rm m}=\phi_{\rm mo}
and
\phi_{\rm mr}

corresponding to
\uptau_{\rm mo}=297.798
and
\uptau_{\rm mr}=298.6
which reliably describe the whole
\rho_{\phi}
evolution
for
\uptau\ll\uptau_{\rm f}
. Namely, we can define the two
branches of
\rho_{\phi}

\rho_{\rm oi}=V_{\rm mo}e^{-3(1+w_{\rm oi})(\uptau-\uptau_{\rm mo})}~~\mbox{for}~~\uptau>\uptau_{\rm mo}~~\mbox{and}~~\rho_{\rm rh}=V_{\rm mr}e^{-3(1+w_{\rm rh})(\uptau-\uptau_{\rm mr})}~~\mbox{for}~~\uptau>\uptau_{\rm mr}.

(74)

Here
V_{\rm mo}
and
V_{\rm mr}
are the potential energy
density associated with the (maximal) amplitude at the onset of OI

\uptau_{\rm mo}
and of reheating process
\uptau_{\rm mr}

respectively. Since for
\dot{}\phi=0
,
\rho_{\phi}\simeq\widehat{V}_{\rm m}
, from
the
\rho
’s in Eq. (
74
) we can find the evolution of
\phi_{\rm m}

and compare it with the solution of Eq. (
70
). Namely, solving
Eq. (
21a
) w.r.t
\phi
we find two values for
\phi_{\rm m}

\phi_{\rm m\pm}=M\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda\pm 4c_{\cal R}\widehat{V}_{\rm m}^{1/2}}\right)^{1/2}.

(75)

Applying the previous relation for the two
\rho
’s in Eq. (
74
)
we can find the various branches of the evolution of

shown
in Fig.
4
-
(b)
represented by

\phi_{\rm mo\pm}=\phi_{\rm m\pm}(\widehat{V}_{\rm m}=\rho_{\rm oi})~~\mbox{and}~~\phi_{\rm mr\pm}=\phi_{\rm m\pm}(\widehat{V}_{\rm m}=\rho_{\rm rh})

(76)

.3
Number of e-Foldings

Although the duration of OI is extremely limited as shown in
Fig.
4
-
(b)
it would be instructive to find its possible
contribution into the number of e-foldings
\widehat{N}_{\star}
between horizon
crossing of the observationally relevant mode
k_{\star}
and the end of
IPI. We can find it as follows
[
112
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{k_{\star}}{H_{0}R_{0}}=\frac{H_{\star}R_{\star}}{H_{0}R_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\star}}{H_{0}}\frac{R_{\star}}{R_{\rm If}}\frac{R_{\rm If}}{R_{\rm of}}\frac{R_{\rm of}}{R_{\rm rh}}\frac{R_{\rm rh}}{R_{\rm eq}}\frac{R_{\rm eq}}{R_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{V_{\rm IPI\star}\over{\rho_{\rm c0}}}e^{-\widehat{N}_{\star}}\left({V_{\rm IPIf}\over V_{\rm of}}\right)^{\frac{-1}{3(1+w_{\rm oi})}}\left({V_{\rm of}\over\rho_{\rm rh}}\right)^{\frac{-1}{3(1+w_{\rm rh})}}\left({\rho_{\rm rh}\over\rho_{\rm eq}}\right)^{-1/4}\left({\rho_{\rm eq}\over\rho_{\rm M0}}\right)^{-1/3}.

Here,
R
is the scale factor in the JF,
H=\dot{R}/R
is the JF
Hubble rate,
\rho
is the energy density and the subscripts
0
,

\star
, IPIf, of, rh, eq and M denote values at the present, at
the horizon crossing (
k_{\star}=R_{\star}H_{\star}
) of the mode
k_{\star}
, at
the end of IPI, at the end of OI, at the end of reheating, at the
radiation-matter equidensity point and at the matter domination.
More explicitly, we have
V_{\rm IPIf}=V_{\rm IPI}(\phi_{\rm f})
and

V_{\rm of}=\rho_{\rm oi}(\uptau_{\rm mr})
. In practice,
\phi_{\rm mr}
can be found by comparing
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}
in Eq. (
21a
) with its
Taylor expansion about
\phi=M
. The larger
\phi
where both
potential energy densities coincide gives
\phi_{\rm mr}
. In our
calculation we make use of the fact that
R\propto\rho^{-1/3(1+w_{\rm oi})}
for OI,
R\propto\rho^{-1/3(1+w_{\rm rh})}
during
reheating,
R\propto\rho^{-1/4}
for radiation domination and

R\propto\rho^{-1/3}
for matter domination. For our numerical
estimates regarding the present-day quantities we use the values
from Ref.
[
1
]
. If we then perform the transition from the JF to
EF, taking in account that
\widehat{R}=f_{\cal R}^{1/2}R
and
\widehat{V}_{\rm IPI}=V_{\rm IPI}/f_{\cal R}^{2}
, we end up with the formula for
\widehat{N}_{\star}

shown in Eq. (
27
). Note that we ignore the discrimination
between JF and EF for
\uptau>\uptau_{\rm f}
since at the SUSY
vacuum both system becomes undistinguishable.

In conclusion, we verified that the contribution into
\widehat{N}_{\star}
from
the period of OI is negligible.

RETRACTED ARTICLE: Elevated FDP levels independently predict poor outcomes in HBV-ACLF patients treated with artificial liver support system therapy
Authors: Xu Yang, Xiaoqi Zhou, Dachuan Cai, Junli Wang, Shiying Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) is an extremely severe clinical syndrome, often associated with systemic inflammation, coagulation dysfunction, and fibrinolysis abnormalities. Fibrin degradation product (FDP), as a byproduct of fibrinolysis, is a crucial indicator reflecting the state of fibrinolysis. The objective of this study is to investigate the relationship between FDP levels and the 28-day mortality rate in patients with ACLF. We retrospectively enrolled 520 patients with hepatitis B virus-related acute-on-chronic liver failure (HBV-ACLF) who underwent artificial liver support system therapy and collected relevant clinical data at admission. Cox regression analysis was employed to investigate the relationship between FDP levels and the 28-day mortality rate, and the predictive value of FDP was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Among the 520 eligible patients, the 28-day mortality rate was 20.2%. The FDP levels of surviving patients were significantly lower than those of deceased patients [6.15 (3.23–10.97) vs. 16.98 (9.58–28.93), P < 0.001]. Through multivariate Cox proportional hazards analysis, after adjusting for confounding factors, It was observed that for every 10 µg/mL increase in FDP levels, the risk increased by 12.8% [HR = 1.128 (95% CI: 1.044–1.219), P < 0.001]. Compared to patients with low FDP levels (< 11.1 µg/mL), patients with high FDP levels (≥ 11.1 µg/mL) demonstrated a markedly higher mortality risk [HR = 3.222 (95% CI: 1.999–5.192), P < 0.001]. Among various prognostic scores, the COSSH-ACLF score exhibited the largest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), comparable to that of FDP (P = 0.891), and its predictive performance was superior to that of FIB and D-dimer. Additionally, for patients who received three or more sessions of artificial liver support system therapy, those with high FDP levels had a significantly reduced 28-day mortality risk. Elevated FDP levels are associated with the 28-day prognosis in patients with HBV-ACLF. Moreover, undergoing multiple sessions of artificial liver treatment is associated with favorable survival outcomes for patients with high FDP levels (≥ 11.1 µg/mL).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24753-7.

Introduction

Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) represents a severe clinical syndrome. It is characterized by liver dysfunction triggered by both intrahepatic and extrahepatic factors on the foundation of chronic liver disease. This condition is often associated with multiple organ failure, significantly escalating the short-term mortality risk for patients1,2. In China, the majority of ACLF cases have been primarily attributed to hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection. However, due to the widespread implementation of hepatitis B vaccination programs, the proportion of ACLF cases caused by HBV infection has gradually decreased over the past few years. Despite this decline, the short-term mortality rate of patients with ACLF remains relatively high, reaching approximately 50%3,4. Currently, liver transplantation stands as the most effective treatment for ACLF. Nevertheless, the scarcity of donor liver sources severely restricts the widespread application of this approach. In China, aside from liver transplantation, standard medical therapy (SMT) and artificial liver support system (ALSS) therapy are the most commonly employed treatment modalities for patients with ACLF in clinical practice5,6. Studies have demonstrated that, compared to the sole use of SMT, ALSS therapy can more effectively enhance the short-term survival rate of patients with HBV-ACLF7. ALSS therapy can temporarily replace liver function, playing a pivotal role in removing hepatotoxic substances and stabilizing the body’s internal environment8. The main modes of ALSS therapy include the molecular adsorbent recirculating system (MARS), single-pass albumin dialysis (SPAD), Prometheus, selective plasma filtration therapy, and hemodiafiltration9. However, the plasma exchange (PE)-based ALSS is particularly prevalent in clinical practice in China6. Although ALSS therapy can improve the short-term survival rate of patients, there are still some patients who do not benefit from it10. Therefore, it is of utmost importance to explore indicators that can accurately predict the prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF undergoing ALSS therapy. This exploration can help avoid the waste of medical resources and facilitate the formulation of more rational treatment plans for patients.

ACLF is a complex syndrome characterized by a systemic inflammatory response11 and an acute deterioration of liver function. The liver, as a vital metabolic organ in the human body, is responsible for decomposing metabolic products and synthesizing essential compounds. Reduced liver function can result in a decreased synthesis of coagulation factors and a weakened capacity to break down metabolic waste. This type of liver function impairment, in conjunction with a systemic inflammatory response, can lead to coagulation dysfunction12 and abnormalities in the fibrinolytic system. However, there are still numerous controversies regarding the fibrinolytic status of critically ill patients with liver disease13. Fibrin degradation product (FDP) is a byproduct of fibrinolysis14. The elimination of FDP primarily occurs in the liver. When liver function is compromised, the liver’s ability to clear FDP diminishes, leading to an increase in circulating FDP levels. Currently, some studies have reported that the levels of FDP and D-dimer in patients with liver failure are significantly elevated, and these two indicators are independent predictors influencing patient prognosis15,16. To date, no research has explored FDP as a short-term predictor of patients with HBV-ACLF receiving ALSS therapy. Additionally, it remains unclear whether ALSS therapy can improve the prognosis of patients with elevated FDP levels. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the relationship between FDP levels and the 28-day mortality rate in patients with ACLF receiving ALSS therapy. Meanwhile, to comprehensively assess the prognostic value of FDP, we compare and analyze it with several common international ACLF prognostic scoring systems.

Materials and methods

Patients

A total of 520 patients diagnosed with HBV-ACLF were included in this study. These patients were admitted to the Infectious Disease and Liver Disease Center at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University between January 1, 2019, and October 31, 2023 (Fig. 1). Patients meeting the following criteria were eligible for inclusion in this study: (1) A history of chronic HBV infection lasting at least 6 months. (2) According to the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Liver Failure (2018 Edition)17, the diagnosis of ACLF was based on the following criteria: serum total bilirubin ≥ 10 × upper limit of normal (ULN) or a daily rise of ≥ 17.1 µmol/L; international normalized ratio (INR) ≥ 1.5 or prothrombin time activity (PTA) < 40%; ascites and/or hepatic encephalopathy (HE) may develop within 4 weeks. (3) Receipt of one of the following three ALSS modalities within 28 days: PE (plasma exchange), plasma adsorption (PA), or a combination of PE and PA (PE + PA). Patients with the following situations were excluded from the study: (1) Age < 18 or > 85 years. (2) A history of hepatocellular carcinoma or other malignancies. (3) A history of liver transplantation. (4) Severe comorbidities that could potentially affect survival. (5) Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection. (6) A history of thrombosis. (7) Incomplete clinical information. All patients in this study were treated at our hospital for the first time and had not used anticoagulants prior to admission. All patients received standard drug therapy in combination with ALSS therapy. During ALSS therapy, patients were administered low-dose low-molecular-weight heparin therapy. The diagnosis of ACLF complications and renal failure was based on the diagnostic criteria established by the Asia-Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver (APASL)11. Circulatory failure is defined as a mean arterial pressure (MAP) < 70 mmHg or the need for vasoactive drugs. Respiratory failure is defined as PaO2/FiO2 < 300 or the requirement for ventilator support. For the patients in PE group, the replacement volume per session was 2000–2500 mL, and the plasma volume was estimated using the following formula: plasma volume = patient body mass (kg) × 70 × [(1.0 − hematocrit) × 0.91]. DPMAS (Double Plasma Molecular Adsorption System), a specific form of plasma adsorption (PA), was used to remove bilirubin and inflammatory mediators in some patients. The PA was primarily performed using the DPMAS. This process involved utilizing a disposable hemoperfusion device and a disposable bilirubin adsorber to perform double adsorption of bilirubin and inflammatory mediators. As for PE + PA, it combined the above two modes. DPMAS was performed first, followed by PE. All patients received low-dose low molecular weight heparin during ALSS treatment to against coagulation. Standard drug therapy included the use of antiviral drugs, hepatoprotective drugs, and medications for treating complications and comorbidities. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (Approval No.: 2024IIT322) and was conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University waived the informed consent form for the patients.

Clinical data collection

The demographic and clinical data of the patients were obtained from the electronic medical record system. The data encompassed age, gender, underlying diseases, complications, laboratory test results at admission, as well as the number and mode of ALSS therapy. Prior to initiating ALSS therapy, all laboratory parameters, including FDP, were measured. Based on the ALSS therapy modality, patients who underwent PE + PA during their hospitalization were classified as combined-mode, while the remaining patients were classified as single-mode. To ascertain the 28-day mortality rate of the patients, we reached out to the patients’ family members via telephone or retrieved patient data from the electronic medical record system. According to the formulas or scoring systems presented in the relevant literature, the following scores were calculated for each ACLF patient before receiving ALSS therapy: Child-Turcotte-Pugh (CTP) score18, Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) score19, MELD-Na score20, Chronic Liver Failure Consortium (CLIF-C) ACLF score21, CLIF-C Organ Failure (OF) score21, APASL ACLF Research Consortium (AARC) ACLF score22, Hepatitis B Virus-Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (HBV-SOFA) score12, and Chinese Group on the Study of Severe Hepatitis B (COSSH) ACLF score12. In these predictive models, a higher score indicates a greater severity of the disease.

Statistical analysis

In this study, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was utilized to determine whether continuous variables followed a normal distribution. Variables adhering to a normal distribution were expressed as means ± standard deviations (SDs), while those not adhering were represented by medians with interquartile ranges (P25-P75). The Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used to evaluate differences between continuous variables. Categorical variables were expressed in terms of frequencies and percentages, and the chi-square test was utilized to compare group differences. To evaluate multicollinearity, variance inflation factors (VIFs) were calculated, with a VIF > 10 indicating severe collinearity. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression models were applied to explore the association between FDP and the 28-day mortality rate in patients with HBV-ACLF. Model 1 was an unadjusted Cox model, whereas Models 2 and 3 were partially adjusted models. Model 2 was adjusted for demographic parameters and ACLF-related complications, including age, hypertension, circulation failure, respiratory failure, renal failure, HE, gastrointestinal bleeding (GIB), and bacterial infection. Model 3 was adjusted for laboratory data, including alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), albumin (ALB), total bilirubin (TBIL), INR, hemoglobin (HGB), white blood cells (WBC), platelet (PLT), and lactate levels. Considering the overall severity of the disease, we added a Model 4, which further adjusted for the MELD score based on Model 3. The optimal cutoff value of FDP was determined to be 11.1 µg/mL using the Youden index through ROC curve analysis. Subsequently, patients were divided into a low FDP group and a high FDP group according to this cutoff value. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves were employed to assess the association between different FDP levels and the 28-day mortality rate, and ROC curves were employed to assess the predictive performance of FDP compared to eight other prediction models. Bonferroni correction was applied in the ROC analysis to adjust for multiple comparisons among the prognostic models in order to control for type I error.All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0) and R software (version 4.3.0). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The baseline characteristics of the enrolled patients with HBV-ACLF are presented in Table 1. A total of 520 eligible patients were included in this study. The average age of the patients was 49 years, and males accounted for 84.8%. During the 28-day follow-up period, all patients received ALSS therapy, and ultimately, 20.2% (n = 105) of the patients died. There were significant statistical differences (P < 0.05) between the survival and non-survival groups in multiple factors. These factors included age, history of hypertension, circulation failure, respiration failure, renal failure, HBV-DNA level, HE, GIB, bacterial infection, ALT, AST, ALB, TBIL, HGB, WBC, PLT, C-reactive protein (CRP), lactate, FDP, fibrinogen (FIB), D-dimer, INR, and sessions of ALSS therapy. Regarding prognostic scores, non-survivors had significantly higher CTP scores, MELD scores, CLIF-C OF scores, CLIF-C ACLF scores, AARC ACLF scores, HBV-SOFA scores, and COSSH ACLF scores than survivors (P < 0.05).

FDP is an independent risk factor for the 28-day mortality rate in ACLF patients

In this study, all 520 patients were included in the Cox regression analysis. The indicators demonstrating significant statistical differences (P < 0.05) in Table 1 were included in the univariate Cox regression analysis (Table 2). To validate the relationship between FDP and the 28-day mortality rate, we developed four Cox proportional hazards models (Table 3). Model 1 was an unadjusted model. Initial analysis revealed that for every 10 µg/mL increase in FDP, the hazard ratio (HR) associated with the 28-day mortality rate was 1.300 (95% CI: 1.237–1.366, P < 0.001). Model 2 and Model 3 incorporated demographic parameters, complications associated with ACLF, and laboratory indicators, respectively. Model 2 was adjusted for factors such as age, hypertension, circulation failure, respiratory failure, renal failure, HE, GIB, and bacterial infection. Model 3 was based on Model 2 and further adjusted for laboratory data including ALT, AST, ALB, TBIL, INR, HGB, WBC, PLT, and lactate. To eliminate the potential confounding effects of disease severity, we constructed Model 4 by integrating ACLF prognostic scores. After assessing the multicollinearity between each of the eight scores and variables in Model 3, it was found that only CTP score, MELD score, and MELD-Na score had acceptable VIFs (< 5) (Supplementary Table S1). MELD score was selected for adjustment due to its widespread clinical use and strong prognostic performance (AUC = 0.735) in this study cohort. In this fully adjusted model, a 10 µg/mL increase in FDP was associated with an approximate 12.8% increase in 28-day mortality risk (HR = 1.128, 95% CI: 1.044–1.219, P < 0.001). Based on the AUC, the optimal cutoff value of FDP for predicting the 28-day mortality rate was established as 11.1 µg/mL (AUROC: 0.816, 95% CI: 0.773–0.858), with a specificity of 0.754 and a sensitivity of 0.724. After converting FDP from a continuous variable to a categorical variable, it was found that compared to patients with FDP levels < 11.1 µg/mL, FDP levels ≥ 11.1 µg/mL significantly increased 28-day mortality risk (HR = 3.222, 95% CI: 1.999–5.192, P < 0.001).

Comparison of clinical data and outcome across different FDP levels

With a cutoff value of 11.1 µg/mL for FDP, the 520 participants were divided into two groups: the high FDP group (FDP ≥ 11.1 µg/mL; n = 341) and the low FDP group (FDP < 11.1 µg/mL; n = 179). The 28-day mortality rate in the high FDP group was 42.5% (n = 76), which was significantly higher than the 8.5% (n = 29) in the low FDP group. KM survival curves (Fig. 2) demonstrated a statistically significant difference in the 28-day mortality rate between the two groups (P < 0.001). Baseline characteristics indicated that, when compared to the low FDP group, the high FDP group had several distinct features. Specifically, it had an older age, a higher incidence of organ failure, a greater prevalence of HE and bacterial infection. Additionally, the levels of WBC, CRP, lactate, FIB, and D-dimer were elevated, while the levels of PLT, HGB, and ALB were decreased. Moreover, the prognostic scores were worse, except for MELD-Na score (P < 0.05) (Supplementary Table S2). Notably, the level of glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) in the high FDP group was significantly lower than that in the low FDP group (P = 0.022).

Predictive effect of FDP on prognosis of HBV-ACLF patients

This study incorporated eight widely utilized prognostic models for ACLF, namely CTP score, MELD score, MELD-Na score, CLIF-C OF score, CLIF-C ACLF score, HBV-SOFA score, AARC ACLF score, and COSSH ACLF score. The results of Pearson correlation analysis indicated that FDP was positively correlated with all the models, with the weakest correlation being observed with MELD-Na score (Fig. 3). Further analysis of ROC curves (Fig. 4) disclosed that the AUROC for FDP was 0.816 (95% CI: 0.773–0.858), whereas the AUROC for COSSH ACLF was 0.820 (95% CI: 0.779–0.861), and there was no statistically significant difference between the two (P = 0.891). However, the AUROC for FDP was significantly higher than that of the other seven prognostic models (P < 0.05). Using the Bonferroni correction (adjusted P = 0.00625), FDP showed significantly higher AUC values than CTP, MELD-Na, CLIF-OF, and HBV-SOFA (Table 4). There were no statistically significant differences between FDP and MELD, CLIF-ACLF, AARC ACLF, or COSSH ACLF, indicating comparable predictive performance for 28-day mortality in HBV-ACLF patients. Moreover, this study also analyzed other fibrinolytic parameters, such as FIB and D-dimer. It was found that FDP was negatively correlated with FIB and positively correlated with D-dimer (Supplementary Table S3). The results of ROC curve analysis indicated that FDP exhibited better predictive performance than FIB and D-dimer. Finally, we evaluated the combined use of FDP and the COSSH-ACLF score to predict 28-day mortality in ACLF patients. We found that the ROC AUC of the combined model (0.87) was higher than that of FDP alone or COSSH-ACLF alone (both 0.82), indicating that the combination provides better predictive performance (Supplementary Fig S1).

Association between sessions of ALSS therapy and the 28-day mortality rate across different FDP level groups

Through this study, we discovered that the number of ALSS therapy sessions was an independent protective factor influencing patient prognosis (HR = 0.666, 95% CI: 0.535–0.830, P < 0.001). As shown in Table 5, patients who underwent multiple artificial liver treatments had higher COSSH ACLF scores and more severe liver damage. We incorporated the parameters of Model 3 into the multivariable Cox regression analysis. In Fig. 5, the results indicated that in the group with FDP ≥ 11.1 µg/mL, patients who received 1–2 ALSS therapy sessions had a significantly higher mortality risk compared to those who received more than 3 ALSS therapy sessions (HR = 2.541, 95% CI: 1.350–4.782, P = 0.004). Notably, in the FDP < 11.1 µg/mL group, although no statistically significant association was detected (HR = 1.626, 95% CI: 0.559–4.416, P = 0.340), the 28-day mortality rate (6.52%) of patients receiving ≥ 3 ALSS therapy sessions was still lower than that of patients receiving only 1–2 ALSS therapy sessions (9.23%), suggesting a potential trend towards improved prognosis in this patient group.

Dynamic changes of FDP

To investigate the dynamic changes of FDP, we collected FDP value at admission as well as before and after ALSS. All patients underwent ALSS, including 520 who received treatment once, 345 twice, and 140 three times or more (for patients with ≥ 3 sessions, the FDP value from the last session was used). The results showed that after the first ALSS, FDP levels decreased in both the survival and death groups, with a more pronounced reduction in the death group (P = 0.002) (Supplementary Table S4). Among patients who underwent two sessions of ALSS, FDP levels did not significantly decrease (P > 0.05). In patients receiving ≥ 3 sessions, FDP levels tended to increase (Fig. 6), although the differences were not statistically significant (P > 0.05).

Discussion

FDP is derived from the breakdown of fibrin clots by fibrinolytic enzymes and serve as a sensitive marker that reflect the coagulation and fibrinolysis status14. This study represented the first attempt to assess FDP as a short-term prognostic indicator for patients with ACLF receiving ALSS therapy. In this retrospective cohort study, we observed that the FDP levels were significantly higher in patients who died within 28 days compared to those who survived. Survival analysis demonstrated that FDP was an independent predictor of 28-day mortality in ACLF patients in both unadjusted and fully adjusted models. Specifically, when compared to the low FDP group (FDP < 11.1 µg/mL), the high FDP group (FDP ≥ 11.1 µg/mL) exhibited a significantly lower 28-day overall survival rate.

We also observed that FDP levels significantly decreased after the first artificial liver support in the deceased group, whereas no significant change was seen in the surviving group. This may be related to the higher baseline FDP levels and hyperactive fibrinolytic system in the deceased group, while the fibrinolytic system in the surviving group was relatively stable, limiting the impact of artificial liver therapy on FDP levels. Further follow-up showed that FDP levels did not change significantly after subsequent artificial liver treatments in either group. Previous studies have suggested that artificial liver therapy may cause coagulation dysfunction and increase bleeding risk, with such patients often exhibiting elevated D-dimer levels23. Therefore, it can be inferred that extensive use of artificial liver support may affect the fibrinolytic system, potentially leading to increased FDP levels.

Previous studies have confirmed that COSSH ACLF score outperforms MELD score, MELD-Na score, CLIF-C ACLF score, and AARC ACLF score in predicting the prognosis of patients with ACLF12,24. The results of this study are consistent with these findings, as the AUROC of the COSSH ACLF score demonstrated the largest, indicating the best predictive performance. When evaluating the predictive ability of FDP, we found that its predictive ability was comparable to that of COSSH ACLF score (P = 0.891) and significantly better than that of other models (P < 0.05). In summary, FDP levels can, to some extent, reflect the severity of ACLF in patients and possess important clinical predictive value.

The fibrinolytic system is a highly integrated network. Abnormal fibrinolytic states emerge when the equilibrium between pro-fibrinolytic and anti-fibrinolytic factors is disrupted. Previous research has indicated that patients with acute decompensation (AD) or those progressing to ACLF in cirrhosis display notably pronounced fibrinolysis25. In addition, FDP > 10 µg/mL might indicate hyperfibrinolysis, which occurs in roughly 30% of liver disease patients26. In this study, the high FDP group (indicating possible hyperfibrinolysis) accounted for 34.4% (n = 179) of the overall population, which is comparable to the proportion reported in previous studies. Nevertheless, with the continuous advancement of fibrinolysis assessment methods, there remains significant controversy regarding the fibrinolytic status of patients with AD and ACLF. A recent study suggested that a mixed fibrinolytic pattern is more prevalent in patients with ACLF, with some patients presenting hyperfibrinolysis and others showing severe hypofibrinolysis27. During the acute phase of the disease, the progression of systemic inflammatory response (SIRS) in ACLF can lead to exhaustion of both clot formation and clot lysis systems. Meanwhile, the enhanced production of pro-hemostatic acute-phase reactants contributes to a hypercoagulable state28. SIRS has been associated with both hypocoagulable and hypofibrinolytic states in patients with acute decompensation (AD) and acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF), with these abnormalities being more pronounced in ACLF29. Hypofibrinolysis and extravascular fibrin deposition30 may contribute to microthrombosis, thereby increasing the risk of organ failure. Moreover, SIRS and bacterial infections often exacerbate microthrombosis31. Overall, hypofibrinolysis and microthrombi formation may be associated with short-term mortality. However, this finding is not entirely consistent with our results, where the mortality rate in the high FDP group was significantly higher than that in the low FDP group. We speculate that in the early stages of ACLF, patients may indeed experience hypofibrinolysis, accompanied by disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)32 and microthrombosis. However, DIC is inherently a dynamic disorder of coagulation and fibrinolysis33. As the condition progresses, an increase in D-dimer levels suggests that microthrombi may dissolve34. This study clearly demonstrated that D-dimer levels were significantly elevated in deceased patients. Additionally, the aforementioned studies primarily included patients with alcoholic liver disease, while this study focused on HBV-related ACLF patients, which may explain the observed discrepancies. It is undeniable that patients with high FDP levels in this study had more severe coagulopathy, higher bilirubin levels, and more severe complications, all of which contributed to a poorer long-term prognosis for the patients.

ALSS, a widely used approach for treating ACLF in China, is associated with improved short-term prognosis of patients and is frequently utilized as a bridging therapy while waiting for liver transplantation8. Du et al. (2021)35 reported that among patients with HBV-ACLF, those who underwent ≥ 3 PE-centered ALSS therapy sessions experienced a significantly lower 90-day mortality rate, which aligns with our findings. In this study, for patients with FDP ≥ 11.1 µg/mL, the mortality rate of patients who received only 1–2 ALSS therapy sessions (47.73%) was significantly higher than that of patients who received ≥ 3 ALSS therapy sessions (27.66%). Moreover, the risk of death for the former group was 2.541 times higher than that of the latter (HR = 2.541, 95% CI: 1.350–4.782, P = 0.004). These findings suggest that receiving three or more ALSS therapy sessions may be associated with better short-term outcomes in this high-risk subgroup. It is noteworthy that in patients with low FDP levels (< 11.1 µg/mL), although multiple ALSS therapy sessions did not yield statistically significant survival benefits (P = 0.340), a trend towards an improved short-term prognosis was observed. This may be explained by the fact that SMT and symptomatic supportive therapy have provided substantial clinical benefits to this group, thereby potentially reducing the incremental value of ALSS therapy. Overall, our results suggest that in HBV-ACLF patients presenting with elevated FDP levels (indicating hyperfibrinolysis), a greater number of ALSS therapies may be associated with improved short-term prognosis and survival outcomes.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, it is a retrospective single-center analysis lacking external validation, which significantly restricts the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, the study did not conduct repeated measurements of FDP. As a result, we may have missed out on the dynamic information of FDP over time. Thirdly, this study only evaluated three ALSS therapy modes and failed to cover other treatment options, which might introduce selection bias. Fourthly, this study only concentrated on the short-term prognosis of patients within 28 days and lacked long-term survival data. Large-scale, multicenter prospective studies are required in the future. Repeated FDP measurements should be carried out to further validate its value in assessing patient prognosis.

Conclusion

Elevated FDP is an independent risk factor affecting the 28-day survival rate in patients with HBV-ACLF. Patients in the high FDP group (≥ 11.1 µg/mL) exhibited significantly lower 28-day survival rates and shorter survival times when compared to those in the low FDP group (< 11.1 µg/mL). Additionally, receiving three or more ALSS therapy sessions may be associated with an improved 28-day survival in ACLF patients with high FDP levels.

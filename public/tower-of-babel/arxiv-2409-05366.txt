Asymptotic symmetry of solutions for reaction-diffusion equations via elliptic geometry
Baiyu Liu and Wenlong Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic symmetry and monotonicity of positive solutions to a reaction-diffusion equation in the unit ball, utilizing techniques from elliptic geometry. Firstly, we discuss the properties of solutions in the elliptic space. Then, we establish crucial principles, including the asymptotic narrow region principle.Finally, we employ the method of moving planes to demonstrate the asymptotic symmetry of the solutions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic symmetry and monotonicity of positive solutions to the following reaction-diffusion equation

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)-\Delta u(x,t)=f(|x|,u(x,t),t),&(x,t)\in B_{1}(0)\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,t)=0&(x,t)\in\partial B_{1}(0)\times(0,\infty),\end{array}\right.

where
B_{1}(0)
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geqslant 2
.

Reaction-diffusion equations are fundamental mathematical models that describe how the concentration of substances evolves over time under the influence of both chemical reactions and diffusion. They are widely applied in fields such as biology for pattern formation, chemistry for reaction waves, ecology for population dynamics, neuroscience for modeling brain activity, and physics for phase transitions and material science
[
1
,
2

. These equations provide critical insights into self-organization in complex systems and form a theoretical basis for understanding various spatial structures and dynamic behaviors in nature. In terms of the application to the asymptotic symmetry of solutions, they also have been used in the proofs of convergence results for some autonomous and time-periodic equations
[
3
]
.

For elliptic equations, the method of moving planes, initially introduced by Alexandrov
[

In

Lemma 3.1

(Asymptotic narrow region principle)
Assume that

For sufficiently large

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial z_{\lambda}(x,t)}{\partial t}-\Delta z_{\lambda}(x,t)\geqslant c_{\lambda}(x,t)z_{\lambda}(x,t),\qquad&(x,t)\in\Omega\times[\,\overline{t},\infty),\\
&z_{\lambda}(x,t)\geqslant 0,\qquad&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[\,\overline{t},\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

where

is bounded from above. Then for sufficiently small

(3.3)

\varliminf_{t\to\infty}z_{\lambda}(x,t)\geqslant 0,\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

Proof.

Let

Define

\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,t)=\frac{e^{mt}z_{\lambda}(x,t)}{\phi(x)}.

Then
\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\frac{e^{mt}z_{\lambda}(x,t)}{\phi(x)}+\frac{e^{mt}}{\phi(x)}\frac{\partial z_{\lambda}(x,t)}{\partial t},

and

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\sin\left(\frac{|x-e_{\lambda}|-\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2\lambda}}{\delta}+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+\cos\left(\frac{|x-e_{\lambda}|-\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2\lambda}}{\delta}+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\frac{1}{|x-e_{\lambda}|\delta}(n-1)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\sin\left(\frac{|x-e_{\lambda}|-\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2\lambda}}{\delta}+\frac{\pi}{2}\right).

Thus,

\frac{\Delta\phi(x)}{\phi(x)}<-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}.

We claim that for any
T>\overline{t}
,

(3.6)

\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,t)\geqslant\min\left\{0,\inf_{\Omega}\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,\overline{t})\right\},\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times[\overline{t},T].

on

, there exists

in

\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x_{0},t_{0})=\min_{\overline{\Omega}\times(\overline{t},T]}\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,t)<\min\{0,\inf_{\Omega}\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x,\tilde{t})\}.

\displaystyle 0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\nabla\tilde{z}_{\lambda}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle 0.

Lemma 3.2

(Strong parabolic maximum principle for not necessarily non-negative coefficient)
.

For each

is bounded in

attains its minimum

over
\overline{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\times[0,\infty)

in
\Sigma_{\lambda}\times(0,t_{0}]

For each

, we set

then for

∎

\displaystyle\leqslant|f(|x|,u_{k}(x,t),t)-f(|\tilde{x}|,u_{k}(x,t),t)|+|f(|\tilde{x}|,u_{k}(x,t),t)-f(|\tilde{x}|,u_{k}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}),t)|

\displaystyle+|f(|\tilde{x}|,u_{k}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}),t)-f(|\tilde{x}|,u_{k}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}),\tilde{t})|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{x}|x-\tilde{x}|^{\alpha}+C_{u}|u_{k}(x,t)-u_{k}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})|+C_{t}|t-\tilde{t}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

By (
5.10
), we set
C=\max\{C_{x},C_{u}C_{0},C_{t}\}
, then for any
(x,t),(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\in Q_{1}
, we have

(5.14)

|\tilde{f_{k}}(x,t)-\tilde{f_{k}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})|\leqslant C\left(\left|x-\tilde{x}\right|^{\alpha}+\left|t-\tilde{t}\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right).

which means for any two points
P(x,t),\tilde{P}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})
, we define
d(P,\tilde{P})=(|x-\tilde{x}|+|t-\tilde{t}|^{\frac{1}{2}})
, then we can get that

(5.15)

\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P,\tilde{P}\in Q_{1}\\
P\not=\tilde{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\tilde{f}_{k}(P)-\tilde{f}_{k}(\tilde{P})|}{d^{\alpha}(P,\tilde{P})}<+\infty.

Given that
u_{k}
is the solution of (
4.7
), then the existence and uniqueness theorem of classical solutions for heat equations(Theorem 3.3.7 in
[
48
]
) implies that

(5.16)

\displaystyle|u_{k}|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};Q_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in Q_{1}\end{subarray}}|u_{k}(P)|+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in Q_{1}\end{subarray}}|Du_{k}(P)|+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in Q_{1}\end{subarray}}|D^{2}u_{k}(P)|+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in Q_{1}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial t}(P)\right|+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P,\tilde{P}\in Q_{1}\\
P\not=\tilde{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{|u_{k}(P)-u_{k}(\tilde{P})|}{d^{\alpha}(P,\tilde{P})}

\displaystyle+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P,\tilde{P}\in Q_{1}\\
P\not=\tilde{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{|Du_{k}(P)-Du_{k}(\tilde{P})|}{d^{\alpha}(P,\tilde{P})}+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P,\tilde{P}\in Q_{1}\\
P\not=\tilde{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{|D^{2}u_{k}(P)-D^{2}u_{k}(\tilde{P})|}{d^{\alpha}(P,\tilde{P})}+\mathop{sup}_{\begin{subarray}{c}P,\tilde{P}\in Q_{1}\\
P\not=\tilde{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial t}(P)-\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial t}(\tilde{P})\right|}{d^{\alpha}(P,\tilde{P})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C<+\infty,

where
C
is a constant independent of
k
,
Du_{k}=\left(\partial u_{k}/\partial x_{1},\partial u_{k}/\partial x_{2},\cdots,\partial u_{k}/\partial x_{N}\right)
and

is the Hessian matrix of

. On the one hand (
5.16
) shows the equicontinuity of

,

,

and

in

, which means for a given

for all

and
k
. On the other hand (
5.16
) also ensures that for each
k
,
u_{k}
,
Du_{k}
,

and

are all bounded in
Q_{1}
. By the Ascoli-Azela theorem, there exist functions

,

,
\left\{u_{i\infty}\right\}_{i=1}^{N}
,

. Employing the fundamental theorem of caculus and (

), for
\varepsilon>0
,
(x,t)\in Q_{1}

, we can choose

Short-Term Efficacy of Tegoprazan Compared With Esomeprazole in Patients With Non-Erosive Reflux Disease and Nocturnal Heartburn Symptoms: A Double-Blind, Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Jin Lee, Seong Je Kim, Yong Eun Park, Joon Hyuk Choi, Nae-Yun Heo, Seung Ha Park, Tae Oh Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectives Tegoprazan is a novel potassium-competitive acid blocker that rapidly suppresses acid secretion. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of tegoprazan 50 mg compared with esomeprazole 20 mg in patients with non-erosive reflux disease and nocturnal heartburn symptoms.Methods This double-blind, randomized, active-control study was conducted to compare the efficacy of tegoprazan 50 mg and esomeprazole 20 mg in patients with non-erosive reflux disease and nocturnal heartburn symptoms. The primary endpoint was the percentage of nocturnal heartburn-free days over 2 weeks. The secondary endpoint was the percentage of patients who experienced relief from nocturnal heartburn within 24 h of the first dose. Additionally, the time to the first nocturnal heartburn-free interval was compared between the two groups.Results Of 160 patients, 149 were included in the final analysis. The percentage of nocturnal heartburn-free days over 2 weeks was not inferior to that with esomeprazole (58.2% vs. 54.5%, p=0.001). The percentage of patients without nocturnal heartburn for 24 h was higher in the tegoprazan group, although the difference was not statistically significant. The time to the first nocturnal heartburn-free interval was shorter with tegoprazan than with esomeprazole (2.0 days vs. 2.5 days, p=0.529); however, the difference was not significant.Conclusions Tegoprazan demonstrated non-inferiority to esomeprazole with respect to nocturnal heartburn-free days and may provide more rapid relief from nocturnal heartburn symptoms.Keywords: Gastroesophageal reflux, Heartburn, Potassium-competitive acid blockers, Proton pump inhibitors, Non-erosive reflux disease

INTRODUCTION

Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a common condition characterized by the backward flow of stomach contents into the esophagus, leading to symptoms such as heartburn and acid regurgitation [1]. More specifically, non-erosive reflux disease (NERD) is a subcategory of GERD in which patients exhibit classic reflux symptoms without visible mucosal injury on endoscopic examination. Patients with NERD comprise a substantial proportion of those with GERD and often struggle to achieve adequate symptom control [2-4]. Nocturnal heartburn is a particularly troublesome manifestation of GERD, affecting many patients, regardless of whether they have an erosive or non-erosive disease. Nocturnal heartburn symptoms in patients with GERD reduce quality of life and cause severe esophageal mucosal damage [5,6]. Therefore, effective management of nocturnal heartburn is important.

Currently, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are the cornerstones of GERD and NERD management. Despite their widespread use and general efficacy, PPIs have notable limitations, such as delayed onset of action, incomplete acid suppression, and poor nocturnal heartburn control. To overcome these limitations, potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs) have emerged as a novel class of acid-suppressive drugs. Unlike PPIs that target proton pumps, P-CABs inhibit gastric acid secretion by blocking potassium ions from the H+/K+ ATPase pump. This results in a more rapid onset of action and comparable efficacy in acid suppression [7]. Tegoprazan, a newly developed P-CAB, has shown promise in clinical studies for its rapid and effective control of acid production [8]. Especially in patients with NERD, tegoprazan was more effective than placebo in improving symptoms after 4 weeks of treatment [9]. Tegoprazan produced faster, more potent, and more durable nocturnal acid suppression effects when administered at night, making it an advantageous option for managing nocturnal heartburn. Recent studies have shown that tegoprazan is more effective than vonoprazan and esomeprazole at suppressing nocturnal acid secretion and improving nocturnal reflux symptoms and sleep disturbances in patients with erosive esophagitis [10,11]. However, no study has analyzed the effect of tegoprazan on nocturnal heartburn in patients with NERD. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of tegoprazan 50 mg compared with esomeprazole 20 mg in patients with NERD and nocturnal heartburn symptoms.

METHODS

Study design

This double-blind, randomized, active-controlled trial (RCT) was performed at a single university hospital between May 2020 and August 2023. The study was registered with the Clinical Research Information Service (CRIS; KCT0004833) and conducted in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent before study enrollment. The Institutional Review Board of Haeundae Paik Hospital approved the study protocol (HPIRB 2019-08-046-005).

Patients and protocol

Participants were adults aged ≥19 years with a clinical diagnosis of NERD confirmed by upper gastrointestinal endoscopy and no evidence of esophageal erosion. The inclusion criteria were the absence of mucosal breaks according to the Los Angeles classification in an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy performed at the research institution within 2 months before starting medication; heartburn for at least 6 months before study enrollment and a GERD-Q questionnaire score of ≥8; nocturnal heartburn symptoms at least 3 times a week; and nocturnal heartburn of moderate, severe, or very severe intensity. The exclusion criteria were the presence of erosive esophagitis, Barrett’s esophagus, esophageal motility disorders, history of gastrointestinal surgery, significant comorbidities, and concurrent use of medications that could affect the study outcomes (e.g., other acid-suppressive drugs and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).

In this double-blind study, patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive 50 mg of tegoprazan combined with placebo or 20 mg of esomeprazole combined with placebo daily at bedtime for 2 weeks. Participants were provided with a daily diary to record the occurrence and severity of nocturnal heartburn throughout the study period. Follow-up visits were scheduled at baseline and on day 14 to collect data, ensure adherence to the treatment regimen, and monitor adverse events. Patients rated the severity of nocturnal heartburn symptoms on a 5-point scale as follows: 0=none; 1=mild (symptoms cause discomfort but do not interfere with daily activities, including sleep); 2=moderate (symptoms cause discomfort and interfere with daily activities, including sleep); 3=severe (symptoms occur most of the time and often interfere with daily activities, including sleep); and 4=very severe (symptoms persist and significantly interfere with daily activities, including sleep).

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the percentage of nocturnal heartburn-free days during the 2-week treatment period. The secondary endpoints included the percentage of patients with relief from nocturnal heartburn 3 days and 24 h after the first dose, the percentage of patients with relief from nocturnal heartburn 7 days after the initial dose (defined as 6 of 7 nights with no heartburn), and the time to the first nocturnal heartburn-free interval. Safety assessments included the monitoring and reporting of all adverse events that occurred during the study period. Serious adverse events were promptly reported to the IRB and study sponsor.

Sample size and statistical analysis

The study primarily aimed to demonstrate the non-inferiority of tegoprazan to esomeprazole in terms of the number of nocturnal heartburn-free days during the treatment period. A planned sample size of 80 patients per treatment arm (160 patients in total) was estimated to provide 72 participants per treatment group after allowing for dropouts and at least 80% power to detect the non-inferiority of tegoprazan to esomeprazole, with a one-sided significance level of 2.5% and a non-inferiority margin of 15% [12].

The significance of the differences in baseline characteristics between the treatment groups was assessed using the independent samples t-test (Wilcoxon rank-sum test) for continuous variables and the chi-square test (Fisher’s exact test) for categorical variables. The primary efficacy variable, defined as the percentage of nocturnal heartburn-free days, was compared between tegoprazan 50 mg and esomeprazole 20 mg using an independent sample t-test and reported as the mean and standard deviation within each group. The significance of another key secondary endpoint, time to the first nocturnal heartburn-free night, was compared between the treatment groups using survival analysis.

All efficacy analyses were performed using the Full Analysis Set (FAS) group. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using the SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute).

RESULTS

Patients

In total, 160 eligible patients were randomized to receive either tegoprazan (50 mg, n=80) or esomeprazole (20 mg, n=80). A total of 149 participants completed the study (Fig. 1). Baseline characteristics showed no significant differences, except for a higher proportion of men in the tegoprazan group than in the esomeprazole group. Heartburn severity was determined by evaluating the severity of heartburn at night, 1 week before study participation (evaluated on a 5-point Likert scale, including cases of moderate heartburn three or more times a week). The secondary outcomes were as follows: the percentage of patients without nocturnal heartburn for 3 days was higher in the tegoprazan 50 mg group than in the esomeprazole 20 mg group (day 1: 40.0% vs. 27.0%; day 2: 45.3% vs. 41.9%; and day 3: 53.3% vs. 50.0%). In a subgroup analysis of patients with severe to very severe symptoms at baseline, the mean percentages of patients without nocturnal heartburn were 33.3% vs. 11.5% on day 1, 41.7% vs. 19.2% on day 2, and 50.0% vs. 30.8% on day 3. However, these differences were not significant (Fig. 3). The percentage of patients who achieved relief from nocturnal heartburn within 7 days after the initial dose was similar between the tegoprazan and esomeprazole groups (30.7% vs. 24.3%). However, these differences were not statistically significant.

Mean percentage of patients without nocturnal heartburn during the first 3 days after the initial dose.

A subgroup analysis of the percentage of participants who experienced relief within 24 h of the first dose was performed. In patients aged ≥40 years, the percentage of participants with relief during the 24 h after the first dose was significantly higher in the tegoprazan group than in the esomeprazole group (36.4% vs. 17.1%) (p=0.046). In a subgroup analysis of patients with moderate symptoms, the proportion of patients whose symptoms improved within 24 h after the first dose was significantly higher in the tegoprazan group than in the esomeprazole group (43.1% vs. 35.4%) (p=0.043). In a subgroup analysis of patients with severe to very severe symptoms, the proportion of patients whose symptoms improved within 24 h after the first dose was higher in the tegoprazan group than in the esomeprazole group (33.3% vs. 11.5%); however, the difference was not significant (p=0.063) (Fig. 4).

The mean time to first nocturnal heartburn-free night was 2.0 days in the tegoprazan group and 2.5 days in the esomeprazole group. Tegoprazan showed a trend toward more rapid symptom relief; however, this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.529). In the subgroup analysis of patients with severe to very severe symptoms, the mean time to the first nocturnal heartburn-free night was 2.5 days in the tegoprazan group and 6.5 days in the esomeprazole group (p=0.409).

Adverse events

Both treatment groups reported similar adverse event profiles with no significant differences between them. Common adverse events included nausea, cough, dizziness, and fever. Three and five adverse events occurred in the tegoprazan and esomeprazole groups, respectively, with no serious adverse events reported.

DISCUSSION

In this study, tegoprazan 50 mg was non-inferior to esomeprazole 20 mg in terms of nocturnal heartburn-free days over 2 weeks in patients with NERD. The percentage of patients who experienced symptom improvement within 24 h after the first dose was significantly higher in the tegoprazan group than in the esomeprazole group in patients aged ≥40 or those with moderate nocturnal reflux symptoms. The rapid onset of action, characterized by both early symptom relief and a shorter time to the first nocturnal heartburn-free night, suggests that tegoprazan may offer a faster therapeutic benefit, although this difference was not significant. The efficacy of tegoprazan observed in this study may be attributed to its pharmacological actions. Tegoprazan, a P-CAB, reversibly inhibits gastric H+/K+ ATPase and can also inhibit new proton pumps, resulting in faster acid suppression than conventional PPIs, while maintaining effective nighttime acid suppression. This mechanism may explain the efficacy of tegoprazan in relieving nocturnal heartburn, wherein consistent and rapid acid suppression is essential to relieve the symptoms.

When PPIs are administered in the morning, daytime gastric acid secretion is effectively suppressed; however, nighttime acid secretion is not as well controlled, potentially leading to nocturnal reflux symptoms [13,14]. Nocturnal reflux is a major cause of GERD treatment failure, resulting in poor sleep quality and decreased quality of life. PPI administration or the addition of histamine H2 receptor antagonists before bedtime may help treat nocturnal reflux; however, the limited sample sizes of previous studies make it difficult to generalize the results [15-17]. As mentioned earlier, P-CABs may be more effective for nocturnal reflux because they overcome the limitations of existing PPIs and provide long-lasting effects, even when taken in the morning. RCTs comparing P-CABs with conventional PPIs in patients with erosive esophagitis and nocturnal reflux have been conducted in Japan and Korea [11,18]. A Japanese study involving 32 patients has reported that nocturnal heartburn was relieved significantly faster with vonoprazan than with lansoprazole [18]. A recent Korean study has also indicated that compared with 40 mg of esomeprazole, 50 mg of tegoprazan more effectively improved symptoms in patients with erosive esophagitis and nocturnal reflux with sleep disturbance [11]. Other studies compared nocturnal acid suppression by tegoprazan to that of vonoprazan or esomeprazole using intragastric pH measurements [10]. Nocturnal acid suppression with 50 mg of tegoprazan generally lasted longer than with 20 mg of vonoprazan, although this difference was not significant. However, nocturnal acid suppression was significantly greater with tegoprazan 50 mg than with esomeprazole 40 mg.

In clinical practice, the prevalence of NERD, along with erosive esophagitis, is high, and the treatment of patients with NERD and nocturnal reflux symptoms is challenging. More than half of patients with GERD have normal esophageal mucosa on upper endoscopy and are diagnosed with NERD [19,20], a relatively common condition that significantly affects the patient’s quality of life. PPIs are currently the first-line treatment for NERD; however, their therapeutic effects are generally lower than those in erosive esophagitis [2-4]. Most previous studies analyzing the effects of PPIs on NERD as well as subsequent studies comparing the effects of vonoprazan and tegoprazan were placebo-controlled. NERD may have a mechanism distinct from that of erosive esophagitis, making it more difficult to treat [20,21]. To the best of our knowledge, no previous study has compared tegoprazan with conventional PPIs for the treatment of nocturnal heartburn in patients with NERD. This study is significant because it analyzed the effectiveness of P-CABs in patients with NERD and nocturnal heartburn by comparing them with conventional PPIs.

Regarding safety, tegoprazan and esomeprazole were well tolerated with similar adverse event profiles. The absence of serious adverse events and the similar occurrence of minor adverse events support the safety of tegoprazan as an alternative to existing PPIs. Overall, tegoprazan may be an effective alternative for treating patients with NERD and nocturnal heartburn, who do not achieve satisfactory symptom relief with conventional PPI therapy. Future studies are needed to determine the long-term efficacy and safety of tegoprazan and compare it with other PPIs and acid-suppressing therapies.

This study has some limitations. First, the control group of tegoprazan 50 mg consisted of esomeprazole 20 mg. Most studies on NERD involve comparisons between PPIs and placebos; however, this study aimed for a head-to-head comparison using a relatively low dose of esomeprazole (20 mg). Further studies comparing tegoprazan with standard dose or alternative PPI regimens are required to clarify its relative efficacy in patients with NERD or nocturnal heartburn. Second, because NERD was defined as symptomatic GERD with normal endoscopic findings without pH testing, patients with reflux hypersensitivity or functional heartburn may have been included in the study population; this possibility should be considered when interpreting the efficacy outcomes. Lastly, in the subgroup analysis of the secondary outcomes, statistical differences between the two groups could not be confirmed because of the small number of patients with severe-to-very severe symptoms. Therefore, these subgroup findings should be interpreted cautiously because some analyses may have been underpowered. Future comparative studies targeting patients with severe symptoms are needed.

In conclusion, tegoprazan 50 mg was non-inferior to esomeprazole 20 mg in achieving nocturnal heartburn-free days and may provide more rapid relief from nocturnal heartburn symptoms. Our study supports the use of tegoprazan as an effective and safe alternative to conventional PPIs for the short-term management of nocturnal heartburn in patients with NERD.

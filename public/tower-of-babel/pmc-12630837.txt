Circ_0003266 suppresses bladder cancer progression by modulating the miR-503-5p/SMAD7/TGF-β signaling axis
Authors: Xulong Chen, Yuan Tian, Tao Li, Quliang Zhong, Zhongjian Liang, Zhiqiang Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCircular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as critical regulators in cancer progression. This study aimed to investigate the role of circ_0003266 in bladder cancer (BCa) and elucidate its underlying molecular mechanisms. The expression levels of circ_0003266, miR-503-5p, and SMAD7 in BCa tissues and cell lines were determined using bioinformatics analysis, clinical data, and quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR). Cellular phenotypes were evaluated through proliferation, migration, and apoptosis assays. RNA pull-down and luciferase reporter assays were conducted to validate the molecular interactions among circ_0003266, miR-503-5p, and SMAD7. The involvement of the TGF-β signaling pathway was assessed by Western blotting and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) analysis. Circ_0003266 expression was significantly downregulated in BCa tissues and cell lines, and its low expression was clinically correlated with larger tumor size and advanced disease stage, including poor differentiation and higher TNM classification. Upregulation of circ_0003266 inhibited BCa cell proliferation and migration while promoting apoptosis. Mechanistic analyses revealed that miR-503-5p is a direct target of circ_0003266, and that circ_0003266-mediated regulation of miR-503-5p subsequently modulates SMAD7 expression. SMAD7, a well-established inhibitor of the TGF-β pathway, was shown to mediate the tumor-suppressive effects of circ_0003266. In vivo experiments confirmed that overexpression of circ_0003266 attenuated tumor growth and suppressed TGF-β signaling activity. Our findings demonstrate that circ_0003266 exerts tumor-suppressive functions in BCa by modulating the miR-503-5p/SMAD7/TGF-β axis, highlighting its potential as a novel therapeutic target for bladder cancer intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24588-2.Keywords: Circ_0003266, Bladder cancer, MiR-503-5p, SMAD7, TGF-β signaling pathway, Tumor suppressionSubject terms: Cancer, Cell biology, Molecular biology, Oncology

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BCa) is a prevalent malignant tumor, more frequently diagnosed in males and exhibiting an increasing global incidence1. Annually, approximately 573,000 new cases are reported, ranking BCa as the 10th most common cancer worldwide. It contributes to approximately 212,000 cancer-related deaths annually, ranking 13th in cancer mortality2. The primary histological subtypes include urothelial, squamous cell, and adenocarcinoma carcinomas, with urothelial carcinoma being the most prevalent. Established risk factors encompass tobacco use, exposure to carcinogenic chemicals, and chronic bladder inflammation3. Despite advances in therapeutic strategies, high recurrence and metastasis rates persist, particularly in advanced cases, indicating a poor prognosis4. Consequently, elucidating molecular mechanisms and identifying novel therapeutic targets are essential for improving patient outcomes.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) regulate gene expression by sequestering miRNAs5 or binding RNA-binding proteins (RBPs)6, with aberrant expression implicated in tumor development and metastasis7,8. In BCa, specific circRNAs function as oncogenic or tumor-suppressive molecules9. Downregulated circRNAs (e.g., circ_0137606, circ_001598, circ_0077837) act as tumor suppressors, while upregulated circRNAs (e.g., circ_0058063, circ_403658, circ_103809) function as oncogenic drivers10. Notably, circ_0003266 is downregulated in colorectal cancer (CRC), where its overexpression effectively suppresses malignant progression in CRC cells11. The precise molecular mechanisms by which circ_0003266 influences BCa pathogenesis require further investigation.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNAs (~ 22 nucleotides in length) that post-transcriptionally regulate gene expression by binding to target mRNA 3’UTRs12, leading to mRNA destabilization or translational inhibition13. These regulatory molecules critically govern fundamental cellular processes, including proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and metabolism14. Aberrant miRNA expression is associated with various diseases, particularly cancer, where miRNAs function as tumor suppressors or oncogenes15. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) modulate miRNA activity, thereby influencing cancer-related gene expression16. In BCa, specific circRNAs regulate miR-146a-5p or miR-200a-3p levels through the ceRNA mechanism17,18, thereby influencing BCa progression. The regulatory interplay between circ_0003266 and miRNAs in BCa pathogenesis warrants further investigation.

This study aimed to explore the functional role and molecular mechanisms of circ_0003266 in BCa, with a specific focus on its involvement in the ceRNA network. Preliminary circRNA microarray profiling identified marked downregulation of circ_0003266 in BCa tissues. Through rigorous in vitro and in vivo experiments, we systematically elucidated how circ_0003266 modulates BCa progression, offering novel insights into BCa molecular mechanisms and potential therapeutic strategies.

Methods

Clinical study

Paired tumor and adjacent non-tumor tissues were collected from 50 BCa patients undergoing surgical resection at the Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University. Patients were consecutively enrolled based on the following criteria: (1) histopathologically confirmed primary BCa; (2) no prior history of radiotherapy or chemotherapy; (3) complete clinical and pathological data available; and (4) provision of written informed consent. Exclusion criteria included concurrent malignant tumors, severe systemic illnesses, or inadequate tissue quality for molecular analysis. All tissue specimens were harvested during surgical resection, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at − 80 °C until processing. Each sample was evaluated by an experienced pathologist to confirm tumor cellularity ≥ 70% in tumor tissues and the absence of malignant cells in matched adjacent non-tumor tissues (collected ≥ 3 cm from the tumor margin). The study protocol received ethical approval from the institutional review board of the participating hospital (Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University), with written informed consent acquired from all participants before sample collection.

Cell culture

Four human BCa cell lines (5637, T24, RT4, J82) and the SV-HUC-1 normal urothelial cell line (SV40-transformed) were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai). All cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling and confirmed to be mycoplasma-free. Cells were cultured in DMEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), penicillin (100 U/mL), and streptomycin (100 µg/mL) under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidified atmosphere). All experiments were performed using cells within 20 passages post-thawing to maintain phenotypic stability.

Cell transfection

Short hairpin (sh)RNA constructs targeting SMAD7 (shSMAD7), shRNA negative control (shNC), circ_0003266 overexpression plasmids, SMAD7, and TGF-β overexpression vectors, empty control vectors, miR-503-5p mimics, miR-503-5p inhibitors, and corresponding negative control (NC) mimics/inhibitors were purchased from GenePharma Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). For transfection experiments, BCa cells were seeded in 6-well or 24-well culture plates and cultured to 60–70% confluence. According to the manufacturer’s protocol, the aforementioned oligonucleotides or plasmids were transfected into BCa cells using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The culture medium was replaced with fresh complete medium 8 h post-transfection. Cells were harvested at 48 h post-transfection for subsequent analyses to evaluate knockdown or overexpression efficiency. Transfection efficiency was confirmed via quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR).

qPCR assay

Total RNA was extracted from tissues or cultured cells using TRIzol™ Reagent (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer’s protocol. For mRNA and circRNA quantification, 1 µg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with random hexamer primers. For miRNA detection, stem-loop RT primers and the miDETECT A Track™ miRNA qRT-PCR Starter Kit (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) were utilized to enhance specificity and sensitivity during cDNA synthesis. qPCR was conducted using PowerUp™ SYBR™ Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) under standardized cycling conditions. mRNA and circRNA expression levels were normalized to the endogenous control GAPDH, while miRNA expression was normalized to U6 small nuclear RNA. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2–ΔΔCT method. All reactions were carried out in triplicate (three technical replicates) and repeated across three independent biological replicates to ensure reproducibility. The primer sequence is as follows:

circ_0003266: FP: 5’-AGTTGACAGCGGTACCATCC-3’ RP: 5’-TGTAGGTTCGGCAAGTCCTC-3’.

miR-1263: FP: 5’-AACAAGATGGTACCCTGGCATAC-3’ RP: 5’-CAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGT-3’.

miR-503-5p: FP: 5’-ACACTCCAGCTGGGTAGCAGCGGGAACAG-3’ RP: 5’-CTCAACTGGT GTCGTGGA-3’.

miR-510: FP: 5’-GCATAATGGTTCAGCATGTG-3’ RP: 5’-GCATCATGGCAGCATTTACA-3’.

miR-634: FP: 5’-ATTATGTTAGTTAGGATGGTTTCGA-3’ RP: 5’-ATATCCACAAACAAATAACTTCGTT-3’.

SMAD7: FP: 5’-TTCCTCCGCTGAAACAGGG-3’ RP: 5’-CCTCCCAGTATGCCACCAC-3’.

TGF-β: FP: 5’-GGCCAGATCCTGTCCAAGC-3’ RP: 5’-GTGGGTTTCCACCATTAGCAC-3’.

U6 snRNA: FP: 5’-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3’ RP: 5’-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3’.

RNase R treatment

RNA samples were divided into two aliquots. The experimental group was treated with RNase R (1 U/µg RNA) at 37 °C for 30 min, while the control group underwent identical incubation without RNase R. Following enzymatic digestion, circ_0003266 and its linear counterpart (linear RNA) expression levels were quantified by qPCR.

Cell viability

BCa cells were treated with 10 µL CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

EdU incorporation assay

EdU-positive cells were quantified using an EdU assay kit (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China). BCa cells were incubated with EdU reagent (50 µmol/L) for 2 h, washed, fixed, permeabilized, and stained with DAPI. Nuclear staining was imaged using a confocal microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Transwell assay

Cell migration was analyzed using 8 μm Transwell inserts. Briefly, 5 × 10⁴ cells in 200 µL serum-free medium were seeded in the upper chamber, with 600 µL complete medium (10% FBS) as a chemoattractant below. After 24 h, non-migrating cells were wiped away, and transmigrated cells were fixed, stained, and counted.

Flow cytometry

Cell apoptosis was quantified using flow cytometry on a BD FACS Canto II system (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA). Cells were harvested, washed with PBS, and resuspended in 100 µL of 1× binding buffer. Dual staining was performed using Annexin V-PE (5 µL; BD Biosciences, Cat. No. 556421) and 7-AAD (10 µL; BD Biosciences, Cat. No. 559925) for 15 min at room temperature in the dark. Single-stained fluorescence compensation controls (Annexin V-PE only, 7-AAD only) and an unstained control were prepared before acquisition. At least 10,000 events per sample were recorded. Gating was performed as follows: (1) exclusion of debris and aggregates via forward scatter (FSC-A) vs. side scatter (SSC-A); (2) selection of single cells by FSC-A vs. FSC-H; and (3) identification of apoptotic populations: early apoptotic (Annexin V-PE⁺/7-AAD⁻) and late apoptotic (Annexin V-PE⁺/7-AAD⁺). Data were analyzed using FlowJo software (Version 10.8, Tree Star, Ashland, OR, USA).

Western blot

Protein lysates were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Signals were detected using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent and visualized with a chemiluminescence imaging system (ChemiDoc™ MP, Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The band intensities were quantified using Image Lab™ Software (Bio-Rad). To ensure accurate quantification, the signal intensity of each target protein was normalized to that of the internal control ACTB. Primary antibodies included anti-SMAD7 (ab216428, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), anti-TGF-β (ab215715), anti-p-SMAD2 (ab280888), anti-SMAD2 (ab40855), anti-SMAD3 (ab40854), anti-p-SMAD3 (ab63403), and anti-ACTB (ab8226). All experiments were independently repeated at least three times.

Co-immunoprecipitation (CO-IP)

To investigate SMAD7-TGF-β interactions in BCa cells, Co-IP assays were performed. Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors. Lysates were immunoprecipitated overnight at 4 °C with anti-SMAD7 (ab216428), anti-TGF-β (ab215715), or control IgG, followed by capture using protein A/G beads. Precipitated proteins were detected by Western blot.

Dual-luciferase reporter analysis

For functional validation, putative miR-503-5p binding sites in circ_0003266 and SMAD7 3’UTR were cloned into pGL3-basic luciferase vectors (Promega) to generate wild-type (WT) and mutant (Mut) reporter constructs. BCa cells were co-transfected with either miR-503-5p mimic or negative control, along with WT/Mut reporter plasmids and the pRL-TK normalization vector using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent. Dual-luciferase activities were measured 48 h post-transfection, with firefly luciferase values normalized to Renilla luciferase.

RNA pull-down assay

To validate circ_0003266–miR-503-5p–SMAD7 interactions, RNA pull-down assays were performed. A 5’-biotinylated miR-503-5p mimic or scrambled control (Biotin-NC) was transfected into 5637 and T24 cells (1 × 10⁷ cells/sample) using Lipofectamine 3000. After 24 h, cells were lysed in buffer containing RNase and protease inhibitors. Supernatants were incubated with streptavidin-conjugated magnetic beads (Thermo Fisher) at 4 °C for 2 h with rotation. Beads were washed five times, and bound RNA was eluted (65 °C for 10 min) followed by phenol-chloroform extraction. Enrichment of circ_0003266 and SMAD7 mRNA was quantified by qPCR, normalized to input RNA (10% of the lysate), and Biotin-NC controls.

Bioinformatics analysis

The GSE92675 dataset was retrieved from the NCBI GEO DataSets site (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/?term=GSE92675). Differentially expressed circRNAs were identified using the GEO2R online tool. The cut-off criteria were set as |log2FC| > 2.0 and an adjusted P-value (FDR) < 0.05.

Potential circRNA-miRNA interactions were predicted using the CircInteractome database (https://circinteractome.nia.nih.gov/mirna_target_sites.html), while miRNA-mRNA binding sites were identified via TargetScanHuman 7.2(http://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/).

For miR-503-5p target prediction, we integrated results from miRTarBase (https://mirtarbase.cuhk.edu.cn/~miRTarBase/miRTarBase_2019/php/index.php), miRDB (https://mirdb.org/), and TargetScan databases, with consensus targets determined by Venn analysis.

Enrichment analysis of target genes was performed using the MetaScape online tool (http://metascape.org).

Animal experiments

The animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the Animal Experimental Ethical Inspection Committee of Guizhou Medical University (Ethics Approval #2304817).

Ten BALB/c nude mice (5–7 weeks old, male) were obtained from Nanjing GemPharmatech and housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions with a 12-hour light/dark cycle, controlled temperature (22 ± 2 °C), and humidity (50 ± 10%), with free access to autoclaved food and water. After one week of acclimatization, mice were randomly allocated into lv-nc and lv-circ_0003266 groups (n = 5 per group) using a random number table. The sample size was determined based on previous similar studies19,20 and pilot experiments, which indicated that n = 5 per group would provide 80% power to detect a minimum effect size of 1.8 at α = 0.05. Lentivirus-mediated circ_0003266 overexpression plasmids (lv-circ_0003266) and lentivirus-mediated negative control (lv-nc) were provided by GenePharma Company. BCa cells transduced with corresponding lentiviruses were harvested, resuspended in a 1:1 mixture of PBS and Matrigel (Corning, NY, USA), kept on ice, and injected subcutaneously (100 µL per mouse, 5 × 10⁶ cells) into the dorsal flank of each mouse using a 27-gauge needle21. Tumor dimensions were monitored weekly for three weeks, starting one week post-injection, using the formula: volume (mm³) = 0.5 × length × width². Endpoint criteria included tumor volume ≤ 1000 mm³ and absence of severe distress or morbidity. At the end of the 28-day experimental period, mice were humanely euthanized via 5% isoflurane (Sigma) inhalation, followed by tumor excision. Resected tumors were weighed and processed for immunohistochemical analysis.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis

Tumor specimens were fixed, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 5 μm slices. After deparaffinization and blocking, sections were incubated with a Ki67-specific primary antibody (ab15580, Abcam) followed by a secondary antibody. Immunohistochemical staining was visualized under a light microscope (Olympus). Staining was evaluated using a semiquantitative scoring system combining staining intensity and the percentage of positive cells. Staining intensity was scored as: 0 (negative), 1 (weak), 2 (moderate), and 3 (strong). The percentage of positive cells was scored as: 1 (0–25%), 2 (26–50%), 3 (51–75%), and 4 (76–100%). The final immunohistochemical score was calculated by multiplying the intensity score by the percentage score, resulting in a range from 0 to 12 for quantitative analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 7 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Quantitative data from at least three independent cell experiments or five animal replicates are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Normality of data distribution was verified using the Shapiro-Wilk test prior to parametric analyses. Comparisons between two groups were performed using an unpaired Student’s t-test, while comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Relationships between the expression levels of circ_0003266, miR-503-5p, and SMAD7 were assessed using Pearson correlation analysis. For high-throughput analyses such as transcriptomic screening and functional enrichment, the false discovery rate (FDR) was controlled using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to correct for multiple comparisons. A two-tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Circ_0003266 is down-regulated in BCa cells

Analysis of the GSE92675 dataset revealed differentially expressed circRNAs in bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA) (Fig. 1A). Circ_0003266 exhibited significant downregulation in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 1B). Clinicopathological analysis demonstrated that low circ_0003266 expression was significantly correlated with larger tumor size, poorly differentiated tumors (Stage III/IV), and advanced TNM stages (T3/T4), as detailed in the accompanying Table 1. Consistent with these tissue-based observations, circ_0003266 expression was markedly reduced in BCa cell lines relative to the SV-HUC-1 normal control cells (Fig. 1C). Among the tested cell lines, 5637 and T24 displayed the most pronounced downregulation of circ_0003266; these two lines were therefore selected for subsequent functional assays to further investigate their potential tumor-suppressive role.

Overexpression of circ_0003266 inhibits the malignant behavior of BCa cells

RNase R treatment confirmed the circular structure of circ_0003266, which exhibited greater stability than linear RNAs in BCa cells (Fig. 2A). To further elucidate the biological function of circ_0003266, we established a circ_0003266 overexpression model to elevate its expression in BCa cells (Fig. 2B). Functional assays demonstrated that circ_0003266 overexpression significantly reduced cellular viability and EdU incorporation (Figs. 2C and D), induced apoptosis (Fig. 2E), and inhibited migratory capacity (Fig. 2F). These findings collectively indicate that circ_0003266 exerts tumor-suppressive effects in BCa by modulating critical oncogenic processes, including cell proliferation, DNA synthesis, apoptosis, and migration.

Circ_0003266 directly targets miR-503-5p

Bioinformatic analysis using the CircInteractome database predicted four candidate miRNAs (miR-1263, miR-503-5p, miR-510, and miR-634) with potential binding sites for circ_0003266. Functional validation revealed that circ_0003266 overexpression specifically decreased miR-503-5p expression among these candidates (Fig. 3A). Computational binding site predictions were experimentally confirmed via luciferase reporter assays, showing that miR-503-5p overexpression significantly reduced luciferase activity in wild-type (circ_0003266-WT) constructs but not in mutant (circ_0003266-Mut) constructs (Fig. 3B and C). RNA pull-down assays using biotinylated miR-503-5p further validated direct binding interaction, with significant enrichment of circ_0003266 observed compared to control probes in both 5637 and T24 cells (Fig. 3D). Consistent with its oncogenic role, miR-503-5p was upregulated in BCa cell lines (Fig. 3E), the TCGA-BLCA cohort (Fig. 3F), and clinical specimens (Fig. 3G). Importantly, an inverse correlation between circ_0003266 and miR-503-5p expression was observed (Fig. 3H), collectively demonstrating that circ_0003266 exerts its tumor-suppressive effects through specific sequestration of miR-503-5p.

The biological functions of circ_0003266 are mediated by miR-503-5p

To evaluate the functional involvement of miR-503-5p in circ_0003266-mediated regulation, BCa cells were transfected with miR-503-5p mimic, achieving effective overexpression as confirmed by qPCR analysis (Fig. 4A). Circ_0003266 overexpression significantly reduced cell viability and proliferation in BCa cells, whereas miR-503-5p overexpression reversed these effects (Figs. 4B-D). Additionally, the apoptosis induction caused by circ_0003266 overexpression was attenuated upon miR-503-5p upregulation (Fig. 4E). Similarly, the migratory suppression induced by circ_0003266 was partially restored following miR-503-5p overexpression (Fig. 4F). These findings demonstrate that the tumor-suppressive functions of circ_0003266 are mediated through direct regulation of miR-503-5p.

SMAD7 is a direct target of miR-503-5p

To elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying miR-503-5p regulation, integrative bioinformatics analysis identified SMAD7 as a key downstream target via database screening (Fig. 5A), with pathway enrichment analysis revealing a significant association with TGF-β signaling (Figs. 5B and C). Functional experiments demonstrated that circ_0003266 overexpression upregulated SMAD7 expression, an effect reversed by miR-503-5p overexpression (Fig. 5D). Direct targeting was confirmed through luciferase reporter assays, where miR-503-5p overexpression significantly reduced wild-type SMAD7 (SMAD7-WT) luciferase activity but not mutant (SMAD7-Mut) activity (Figs. 5E and F). RNA pull-down assays further validated the physical interaction between miR-503-5p and SMAD7 (Fig. 5G). Consistent with these findings, SMAD7 expression was downregulated in BCa cells (Fig. 5H), the TCGA-BLCA cohort (Fig. 5I), and clinical specimens (Fig. 5J). Correlation analysis revealed an inverse relationship between miR-503-5p and SMAD7 expression (Fig. 5K) and a positive correlation between circ_0003266 and SMAD7 levels (Fig. 5L). Collectively, these results confirm that SMAD7 is a direct functional target of miR-503-5p, mediating the tumor-suppressive effects of circ_0003266.

Knockdown of SMAD7 reverses the effects of miR-503-5p Silencing on BCa cell biological behavior

Functional characterization of SMAD7 in BCa was conducted through miR-503-5p inhibition (Fig. 6A) and SMAD7 knockdown (Fig. 6B). Inhibition of miR-503-5p resulted in decreased cell viability and proliferative capacity in BCa cells, whereas SMAD7 knockdown reversed these effects (Figs. 6C and D). Similarly, the increased apoptosis rate caused by miR-503-5p inhibition was attenuated by SMAD7 silencing (Fig. 6E). Notably, SMAD7 suppression abolished the inhibitory effects of miR-503-5p downregulation on BCa cell migration (Fig. 6F). These findings demonstrate that SMAD7 serves as a critical mediator of miR-503-5p’s oncogenic functions during BCa progression.

SMAD7 negatively regulates the TGF-β signaling pathway

Studies have established SMAD7 as a key negative regulator of the TGF-β signaling pathway. SMAD7 knockdown significantly reduced SMAD7 protein expression while increasing TGF-β, p-SMAD2, and p-SMAD3 protein levels (Fig. 7A), consistent with SMAD7’s well-defined role as a TGF-β pathway inhibitor. Furthermore, Co-IP experiments confirmed the direct interaction between SMAD7 and the TGF-β receptor (Figs. 7B and C).

Overexpression of TGF-β reverses the biological effects of SMAD7 upregulation on BCa cells

To determine whether the tumor-suppressive functions of SMAD7 were mediated through the TGF-β signaling pathway, we investigated the effects of SMAD7 and TGF-β overexpression in BCa cells. Transfection of plasmids encoding SMAD7 or TGF-β successfully elevated their respective protein expression levels (Figs. 8A and B). SMAD7 overexpression significantly inhibited cell viability and proliferation, whereas TGF-β overexpression reversed these effects (Figs. 8C and D). Similarly, TGF-β upregulation attenuated SMAD7-induced apoptosis (Fig. 8E). Moreover, increased TGF-β expression counteracted the inhibitory effect of SMAD7 overexpression on BCa cell migration (Fig. 8F). These results demonstrate that SMAD7 exerts its tumor-suppressive effects in BCa, at least partially, through antagonizing TGF-β signaling.

Up-regulation of circ_0003266 inhibits tumor progression in vivo

To further validate the in vivo therapeutic potential of circ_0003266 in BCa progression, we established a xenograft tumor model. Lentiviral-mediated circ_0003266 overexpression in tumor tissues (Fig. 9A) led to significant downregulation of miR-503-5p and concurrent upregulation of SMAD7 expression (Figs. 9B and C). Notably, circ_0003266 overexpression markedly reduced tumor weight, size, and volume compared to controls (Figs. 9D-F). Immunohistochemical analysis further revealed decreased Ki67 expression in circ_0003266-overexpressing tumors (Fig. 9G). Mechanistically, circ_0003266 upregulation suppressed TGF-β signaling activation, as evidenced by reduced levels of TGF-β, p-SMAD2, and p-SMAD3 (Fig. 9H). Collectively, these in vivo findings demonstrate that circ_0003266 overexpression inhibits tumor growth and malignant progression through coordinated regulation of the miR-503-5p/SMAD7 axis and TGF-β signaling, highlighting its therapeutic relevance in BCa.

Discussion

Emerging evidence highlights the critical role of circRNAs in BCa pathogenesis, influencing key processes including tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance22. Increasingly, BCa-associated circRNAs have been characterized, revealing diverse molecular mechanisms underlying their functions23. For example, circ_0000520 is downregulated in BCa, and its overexpression suppresses tumor malignant phenotypes by modulating the Lin28a/PTEN/PI3K signaling pathway24. Similarly, circ_0004463 is significantly downregulated in BCa and inhibits tumor progression by regulating the miR-380-3p/FOXO1 axis25. Additionally, circ_0001079 suppresses BCa proliferation and invasion through modulation of the miR-135a-5p/NR3C2 axis26. In this context, our study identifies circ_0003266 as a novel tumor-suppressive circRNA in BCa, exhibiting significant downregulation in both clinical tissues and cell lines. Consistent with its role in CRC11, circ_0003266 overexpression significantly suppressed BCa cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis. Mechanistically, circ_0003266 functions through the miR-503-5p/SMAD7/TGF-β signaling cascade—a pathway distinct from those utilized by other circRNAs such as circ_0000520 or circ_0004463. This finding not only demonstrates functional diversity among circRNAs but also underscores their convergent impact on core oncogenic processes. While circRNAs commonly act as miRNA sponges, the specific targeting of miR-503-5p by circ_0003266 and the subsequent modulation of TGF-β signaling confer unique biological and potential clinical relevance, establishing circ_0003266 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target in BCa.

CircRNAs have emerged as critical regulators in cancer biology, functioning as microRNA sponges, protein decoys, or transcriptional regulators27. To identify the specific miRNA targeted by circ_0003266 for competitive regulation of gene expression and cellular processes, we determined that miR-503-5p serves as a direct target of circ_0003266. This oncogenic miRNA promotes tumorigenesis across multiple cancers by regulating key cellular processes, including proliferation, apoptosis, angiogenesis, and inflammation. miR-503-5p is highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma, where it accelerates cisplatin resistance and angiogenesis through targeting CTDSPL28. Overexpression of miR-503-5p significantly enhances malignant behaviors in oral squamous cell carcinoma by targeting SMAD729. Additionally, miR-503-5p can be sequestered by circRNAs to participate in cancer-related biological processes. For instance, circ_0011232 promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression by sponging miR-503-5p30, while circ_0010220 facilitates osteosarcoma tumorigenesis through the same mechanism31. In CRC, circ_0003266 regulates target gene expression by competitively sponging miR-503-5p11. Our results demonstrate that miR-503-5p is upregulated in BCa and inversely correlates with circ_0003266 expression. Functional rescue experiments revealed that miR-503-5p overexpression abrogates circ_0003266-mediated suppression of proliferation and migration, supporting a sponge mechanism wherein circ_0003266 sequesters this oncogenic miRNA to derepress its target mRNAs.

We identified SMAD7, a key inhibitory member of the SMAD family that negatively regulates TGF-β signaling32, as a direct target of miR-503-5p. SMAD7 serves as a critical tumor suppressor in cancer progression by inhibiting TGF-β-mediated tumor invasion and metastasis33. For instance, in colorectal cancer, high SMAD7 expression inhibits TGF-β-driven tumor cell invasion and metastasis34. Similarly, CircNCOR1 promotes SMAD7 transcription to suppress the TGF-β-SMAD signaling pathway and inhibit BCa tumor growth and lymph node metastasis35. Our findings establish a functional circ_0003266/miR-503-5p/SMAD7 axis in BCa, wherein circ_0003266 upregulates SMAD7 expression by sequestering miR-503-5p. SMAD7 was downregulated in BCa specimens and exhibited an inverse correlation with miR-503-5p expression but a positive correlation with circ_0003266. Rescue experiments demonstrated SMAD7’s pivotal role, as its knockdown reversed the tumor-suppressive effects of miR-503-5p inhibition.

The TGF-β pathway, a conserved regulator of gene expression, governs diverse cellular processes including differentiation, migration, immune modulation, and extracellular matrix remodeling36. While tumor-suppressive during early carcinogenesis, TGF-β signaling shifts to a pro-oncogenic role in advanced cancers37. In BCa, TGF-β1 drives metastasis via fibroblast-mediated EMT through the FAP/VCAN axis38 and transgelin-dependent pseudopodia formation39. In our study, SMAD7 knockdown activated TGF-β signaling, increasing TGF-β, p-SMAD2, and p-SMAD3 levels, whereas SMAD7 overexpression exerted opposing effects, suppressing BCa cell proliferation and migration. These findings corroborate SMAD7’s tumor-suppressive role in gastric cancer through TGF-β inhibition40. Notably, TGF-β overexpression rescued the anti-tumor effects of SMAD7 upregulation, confirming TGF-β-dependent mediation of SMAD7 function in BCa.

Finally, we evaluated the therapeutic potential of circ_0003266 in vivo using a xenograft tumor model. Circ_0003266 overexpression significantly inhibited tumor growth and reduced Ki67 expression, a well-established marker of cell proliferation. Furthermore, circ_0003266 upregulation suppressed TGF-β signaling activation in tumor tissues. These findings highlight circ_0003266 as a promising therapeutic target for BCa intervention. For instance, therapeutic strategies to enhance circ_0003266 expression, such as RNA-based therapies or gene editing.

Despite the comprehensive mechanistic insights provided by our study, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, our functional experiments were primarily conducted in two BCa cell lines (5637 and T24), which may not fully represent the heterogeneity of BCa. Future studies should include a broader panel of cell lines and patient-derived organoid models to generalize the findings. Second, although the xenograft model demonstrated the tumor-suppressive role of circ_0003266 in vivo, the use of immunocompromised mice limits the exploration of immune microenvironment interactions, which are critical in BCa progression and therapy. Additionally, the clinical correlation analysis was based on a single-center cohort with a limited sample size; multi-center validation with larger cohorts and longer follow-up is necessary to confirm the prognostic value of circ_0003266. Finally, while we focused on the miR-503-5p/SMAD7/TGF-β axis, other potential targets and signaling pathways regulated by circ_0003266 or miR-503-5p remain to be explored. For instance, circRNAs often exhibit multifunctional roles, such as interacting with RNA-binding proteins or encoding peptides, which were not investigated in this study. Similarly, miR-503-5p may target additional genes beyond SMAD7 that contribute to BCa pathogenesis. Further studies are warranted to elucidate these alternative mechanisms.

In conclusion, our study establishes circ_0003266 as a tumor suppressor in BCa through its regulation of the miR-503-5p/SMAD7/TGF-β axis, providing mechanistic insights into BCa pathogenesis and identifying a potential therapeutic target. Further research should focus on developing circRNA-based therapeutic strategies while investigating broader circRNA-miRNA-pathway interactions to uncover additional treatment opportunities.

Abstract
. We consider the r r -neighbor bootstrap percolation process on the graph with vertex set V = { 0 , 1 } n V=\{0,1\}^{n} and edges connecting the pairs at Hamming distance 1 , 2 , … , k 1,2,\dots,k , where k ⩾ 2 k\geqslant 2 . We find asymptotics of the critical probability of percolation for r = 2 , 3 r=2,3 . In the deterministic setting, we obtain several results for the size of the smallest percolating set for k ⩾ 2 k\geqslant 2 .



1.
Introduction

The process of
r
-neighbor bootstrap percolation on an undirected graph
G(V,E)
was introduced by Chalupa, Leith, and Reich
[
16
]
. To initialize the process, a subset of vertices
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}_{0}\subset V
are designated as “infected”. One step
of bootstrap percolation infects a healthy vertex if it has
\geqslant r
neighbors in
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
. This can potentially augment the subset
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}

to
{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
by adding new infected vertices. The status of the infected vertices never changes back, and in the next step
there may emerge new vertices in
{\mathscr{A}}_{1}^{c}
with
r
or more neighbors in
{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
. They in turn become infected, and the process
proceeds until it either cannot advance any further because of the lack of well-connected vertices or because the entire
V

has become infected. In the latter case we say that, with the initial choice of
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
, percolation takes place and we call the set

{\mathscr{A}}_{0}

contagious
. To state this
formally, for all
i\geqslant 1
define the sets

{\mathscr{A}}_{i}={\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}\cup\{v\in V:|N_{v}\cap{\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}|\geqslant r\}

and say that the process stops if for some
i
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}={\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}
or
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}=V
(percolation occurs).
Here
N_{v}=N_{v}(G)=\{u\in V:uv\in E\}
is the neighborhood of
v
in
G
. The parameter
r
is called the
infection threshold
.

Bootstrap percolation can be studied in the deterministic or random setting depending on the choice of the initializing set
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
.
In random bootstrap percolation, the set
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
is formed by randomly and independently placing each
v\in V
with probability
p
.
The central question in the problem of random bootstrap percolation is to determine the critical probability, defined as

p_{c}(G,r)=\sup\{p\in(0,1):\mathbb{P}_{p}({\mathscr{A}}_{0}\;\text{percolates on}\>G)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\}.

Among the graphs for which
p_{c}
has been extensively studied, are the Boolean hypercube
Q_{n}
(the Hamming graph) and the grid graph. The authors of
[
2
]

obtained a tight estimate for the critical probability
p_{c}(Q_{n},2)
up to a constant factor, with
a sharper estimate derived subsequently in
[
6
]
. In addition to examining the critical probability behavior when the infection threshold is constant,
[
5
]
provided a sharp estimate for
p_{c}(Q_{n},\frac{n}{2})
and derived the exact second-order term.

Considerable effort has also been devoted to studying the critical probability on
d
-dimensional grid graphs
[n]^{d}
. We mention
[
1
,
15
,
14
,
23
,
4
]
,
with the final result of
[
3
]
establishing a sharp estimate for
p_{c}([n]^{d},r)
,
2\leqslant r\leqslant d
.

In the deterministic setting one of the main problems addressed is the size and structure of the smallest “contagious set”, i.e., the
initializing set that leads to infecting the entire
G
by the
r
-process. Another related question is which contagious
sets take the most time to percolate, i.e., finding the maximum number of steps of the process until percolation, assuming that
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
is contagious. Let
m(G,r)
denote the size of a minimum contagious set and let
T(G,r)
denote the maximum percolation time on a graph
G
with the infection threshold
r
. For the hypercube, the authors of
[
27
]
obtained a tight estimate of
m(Q_{n},r)
. Paper
[
28
]
established the exact value of
T(Q_{n},2)
and
[
22
]
gave an estimate of
T(Q_{n},r\geqslant 3)
. For the grid graph,
[
18
]

found the value of
m([a_{1}]\times[a_{2}]\times[a_{3}],3)
for all but small values of
the dimensions
a_{i}
. A characterization of
m([n]^{2},3)
was obtained in
[
9
,
18
]
.
Additionally,
[
10
]
determined the exact maximum percolation time on
[n]^{2}
with an infection threshold of
2
for all contagious sets of minimum size, and
[
11
]
found the exact first order term of
T([n]^{2},2)
.

While studies of bootstrap percolation have primarily focused on the hypercube and grid graphs, several works were devoted to the percolation process on other graphs. For instance,
[
20
]
found the value of
m(G,2)
when
G
is an Ore graph.
The authors of
[
17
]
estimated the size of minimum contagious sets for expander graphs. Further,
[
24
]
modified the grid graph by allowing
edges between vertices as distance
k\geqslant 1
in both horizontal and vertical directions.

Thinking of a general graph
G(V,E)
, it is possible to phrase the bootstrap percolation process as a question in coding theory. Namely, assign to each vertex a symbol from a finite alphabet, say 0 or 1, and consider a code
C(G)
formed of such assignments, i.e., of binary vectors
c\in\{0,1\}^{|V|}
. Suppose moreover that for every vector
c\in C(G)
, the bit assignment of every vertex
v
can be uniquely recovered from the values of
r
of its neighbors in
G
. Having observed a vector
c
with a subset of coordinates erased, we aim at recovering their values from the nonerased coordinates. This may require several rounds of recovery, which exactly corresponds to the bootstrap percolation process on
G
. This interpretation was first observed in
[
8
]
.

Inspired by these results, in this paper we study bootstrap percolation on the hypercube with edges added between the pairs of vertices at Hamming distance
k\geqslant 2
. We denote this graph by
Q_{n,k}(V,E),
where
V=\{0,1\}^{n}
and
E=\{xy:1\leqslant d_{yx}\leqslant k\}
, where
d_{xy}:=d_{H}(x,y)
is the Hamming distance and
k
is a fixed integer. Given a subgraph
G\subset Q_{n,k}
and
v\in V
, we denote by
N_{v}(G)
the set of neighbors of
v
in
G
, omitting the
reference to
G
if it is the entire cube.

1.1.
Main Results

The first two theorems in our main results concern the critical probability
p_{c}(Q_{n,k},r)
for
r=2,3
, for which we obtain a complete characterization. We now state these results formally.

Theorem 1
.

(Critical probability for the 2-neighbor process on
Q_{n,k}
)

Let
c>0
be a constant,
r=2
, and
k\geqslant 2
. As
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{A}}_{0}\;\text{percolates})\rightarrow\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p\gg 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}$}\\
0&\text{if $p\ll 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}$}\\
1-\exp\left(-\frac{c^{2}}{2(2k)!}\right)&\text{if $p=c2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}$}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
p=c2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}
, then the number of unordered pairs
(x,y)
such that
d_{xy}\leqslant 2k
is asymptotically Poisson distributed with mean
\frac{c^{2}}{2(2k)!}
.

Theorem 2
.

(Critical probability for the 3-neighbor process on
Q_{n,k}
)

Let
c>0
be a constant,
r=3
, and
k\geqslant 2
. As
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{A}}_{0}\;\text{percolates})\rightarrow\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p\gg 2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}$}\\
0&\text{if $p\ll 2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}$}\\
1-\exp\left(-\frac{c^{3}}{6\binom{2k}{k}(2k)!k!}\right)&\text{if $p=c2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}$}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
p=c2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}
, then the number of unordered triples
(x,y,z)
such that
d_{xy}\leqslant 2k
,
d_{xz}\leqslant 2k
and
d_{yz}\leqslant 2k
is asymptotically Poisson distributed with mean
\frac{c^{3}}{6\binom{2k}{k}(2k)!k!}
.

First, observe that the above two theorems imply that the critical probabilities for the
2
-neighbor and
3
-neighbor processes on
Q_{n,k}
do not exhibit sharp thresholds. This stands in contrast to the
2
-neighbor process on
Q_{n}
, for which a sharp threshold

The characterizations of the critical probability for

n

In the case
k=2
, it is easy to show that if the initially infected set contains a pair
(x,y)
such that

then the every vertex on the graph will eventually be infected. With this characterization of percolating sets, the expectation value of the number of such unordered pairs will give rise to the critical probability via the second moment method. However, in the case
k=3
, establishing an analogous statement is more involved.

Another tool that we need is the FKG-inequality. Before stating the FKG-inequality we need to introduce some concepts and notation.

Suppose that

(x_{1},...,x_{2^{n}})\geqslant(y_{1},...,y_{2^{n}})

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the initial infection configuration and

Lemma 10

be the set of initially infected vertices. We claim that percolation occurs if and only if there is a pair

differentiating between the following two cases.

Suppose that
d_{xy}=t,
where
1\leqslant t\leqslant k
. Let us write
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{t},x_{t+1},\dots x_{n})

Now suppose that

Now let

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[X_{T}X_{T^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{T}]\mathbb{E}[X_{T^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=p^{|T\cup T^{\prime}|}-p^{|T|+|T^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}p^{2}-p^{4}&\text{if $T=T^{\prime}$}\\
p^{3}-p^{4}&\text{if $|T\cap T^{\prime}|=1$}\\
0&\text{if $|T\cap T^{\prime}|=0$}.\end{cases}

Summing over all choices of the pairs
T_{i},T^{\prime}_{i}
, we obtain

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{T,T^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{T}}_{i}}\text{Cov}(T,T^{\prime})=(p^{2}-p^{4})2^{n}\binom{n}{i}+(p^{3}-p^{4})2^{n}\binom{n}{i}\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}\binom{i}{l}\binom{n-i}{i-l}\Big).

Let
X=\sum_{i=1}^{2k}X_{i}
(the dependence on
n
is suppressed). Therefore,

\mathbb{E}X=C(k)2^{n}n^{2k}p^{2}.

To fit the assumptions of Lemma
6
, we will choose
p
so that
\Var(X)=o(\mathbb{E}X^{2})
. Write the variance using (
14
) and (
15
) in (
13
) to observe that

\text{Var}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2k}X_{i}\right)\leqslant C(k)(p^{2}2^{n}n^{2k}+p^{3}2^{n}n^{4k}).

It suffices to take

(p^{3}2^{n}n^{4k})^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll 2^{n}n^{2k}p^{2},

or equivalently

p\gg 2^{-n},

and

(p^{2}2^{n}n^{2k})^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll 2^{n}n^{2k}p^{2},

or equivalently

p\gg 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}.

The second of these conditions is more restrictive, giving the lower bound in the claim (
12
) of the theorem.

At the same time,
by Markov’s inequality, if
p\ll 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}
, then
\mathbb{E}X\to 0
if

, and thus, percolation
does not occur with probability approaching 1. This proves the upper bound in (

).
∎

The following lemma shows that if
p=c2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}
, then the probability of full infection converges to a constant. This is analogous to the behavior of threshold functions for the appearance of a strictly balanced subgraph in
G(n,p)
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
c>0
be a constant and
p=c2^{-\frac{n}{2}}n^{-k}
. As
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{A}}_{0}\;\text{percolates})\rightarrow 1-\exp\left(-\frac{c^{2}}{2(2k)!}\right).

Proof.

Define

\mathbb{I}_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i$th pair $(x,y)$ with $d_{xy}\leqslant 2k$ is initally infected}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}.

Define
N=\sum_{i=1}^{2k}2^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}
. Then we have

(16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{A}}_{0}\;\text{does not percolates})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}_{i}=0)

(17)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\cap_{i}^{N}\{\mathbb{I}_{i}=0\})

(18)

\displaystyle\geqslant\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}(\{\mathbb{I}_{i}=0\}).

(19)

\displaystyle=(1-p^{2})^{N}

(20)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\exp\left(-\frac{c^{2}}{2(2k)!}\right)

Note that the inequality
18
is true due to Lemma
7
.

Now let us prove the upper bound by using Lemma
8
.

Let
R
be the set of initially infected vertices and
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be all the distinct unordered pairs
(x,y)
with
d_{xy}\leqslant 2k
. Let
B_{i}
be the event that
A_{i}\subset R
.

From the proof of Lemma
10
, we have

\mu:=\mathbb{E}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}_{B_{i}})=Np^{2}

and

\Delta:=\sum_{i\sim j}\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\cap B_{j})\leqslant p^{3}2^{n}n^{4k}=o(1),

where
i\sim j
if
A_{i}\cap A_{j}\neq\emptyset
for
i\neq j
.

By Lemma
8
, we have the desired result.
∎

In fact, one can go further and prove that the distribution of the number of unordered pairs

Lemma 12

If

, then the number of unordered pair

such that

is asymptotically Poisson distributed with mean

Proof.

We follow the ideas from Chapter 5 of
[

by using Theorem
9
and Remark
2.1
. Let
H_{1},H_{2},\cdots,H_{t}
be all the distinct unordered pairs

with

on the graph

. For

, let

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{H_{i}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $H_{i}\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{0}$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Let

and the
\ell

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{H})_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{\ell}}\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{I}_{H_{i_{1}}}=1,\mathbb{I}_{H_{i_{2}}}=1,\cdots,\mathbb{I}_{H_{i_{\ell}}}=1)

\displaystyle=D_{\ell}+\overline{D}_{\ell},

where the summation is taken over all
\ell

from
\{1,2,\cdots,t\}
, and

denote the partial sums taken over all ordered

\displaystyle D_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{\ell}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{I}_{H_{i_{j}}}=1)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\left(\frac{1}{2}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(2^{n}-2\right)\binom{n}{2k}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{1}{2}(2^{n}-2(\ell-1))\binom{n}{2k}\right)(p^{2})^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\left(2^{n-1}\binom{n}{2k}\right)^{\ell}p^{2\ell}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}[X_{H}]\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t}2^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}p^{2}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\left(2^{n-1}\binom{n}{2k}\right)^{\ell}p^{2\ell}

Let us consider the family

\ell

This lemma enables us to find the main term of the critical probability of percolation.

Lemma 16

(Critical probability for the 3-neighbor process)
.

We have

\frac{1}{\omega(n)}2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}\leqslant p_{c}(Q_{n,k},3)\leqslant\omega(n)2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}.

Proof.

By Lemma
15
, percolation occurs if and only if the initially infected set
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
contains a triangle with all sides
at most
2k
. Let
X
be the number of such triangles in
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
.
Let
x_{1}=0^{n}
,
x_{2}=1^{a_{1}+a_{2}}0^{n-a_{1}-a_{2}}
, and
x_{3}=0^{a_{1}}1^{a_{2}+a_{3}}0^{n-a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3}}
, where all the pairs
(a_{i},a_{j})

satisfy
a_{i}+a_{j}\leqslant 2k
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}p^{3}2^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\\
a_{i}+a_{j}\leqslant 2k\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\binom{a_{1}+a_{2}}{a_{2}}\binom{n-a_{1}-a_{2}}{a_{3}}.

Since
k
is a constant, for large
n
the largest term in the sum is given by

\binom{n}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{n-2k}{k}.

Next let us estimate the variance of
X
. Let
T_{1}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
and
T_{2}=\{x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3}^{\prime}\}
be two triangles with
all the sides at most
2k
. For a triple
T
define the indicator random variable
X_{T}={\mathbbm{1}}_{\{T\in{\mathscr{A}}_{0}\}}
.
We have

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(X_{T_{1}},X_{T_{2}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[X_{T_{1}}X_{T_{2}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{T_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{T_{2}}]

\displaystyle=p^{|T_{1}\cup T_{2}|}-p^{|T_{1}|+|T_{2}|}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=0$}\\
p^{5}-p^{6}&\text{if $|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=1$}\\
p^{4}-p^{6}&\text{if $|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=2$}\\
p^{3}-p^{6}&\text{if $|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=3$}.\end{cases}

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\Var(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T_{1},T_{2}}\text{Cov}(X_{T_{1}},X_{T_{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=1}\text{Cov}(X_{T_{1}},X_{T_{2}})+\sum_{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=2}\text{Cov}(X_{T_{1}},X_{T_{2}})+\sum_{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=3}\text{Cov}(X_{T_{1}},X_{T_{2}})

\displaystyle\leqslant C(k)2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}\Big[(p^{5}-p^{6})\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}+(p^{4}-p^{6})\binom{n}{2k}

\displaystyle\quad+(p^{3}-p^{6})\Big].

where
C(k)
is a constant that depends only on
k
. For the first term above, the number of good triples
T_{1}
is at most
C(k)2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}
and
T_{2}
is at most
C(k)\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}
since
|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|=1
. Other terms can be derived in a similar way.

In order to fit the assumption that
\Var(X)=o((\mathbb{E}X)^{2})
, it suffices to take

p^{3}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}\gg\left(p^{5}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}^{2}\binom{n}{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

p^{3}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}\gg\left(p^{4}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}^{2}\binom{n}{k}\right)^{1/2}

and

p^{3}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}\gg\left(p^{3}2^{n}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}\right)^{1/2}.

It is easy to check that if
p\gg 2^{\frac{-n}{3}}n^{-k}
, then
\Var(X)=o((\mathbb{E}X)^{2})
and
\mathbb{E}X\to\infty
, and the lower bound
on
p_{c}
follows by Lemma
6
. On the other hand, if
p\ll 2^{\frac{-n}{3}}n^{-k}
, then by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(X\geqslant 1)\leqslant\mathbb{E}[X]=o(1)
.
∎

If
p=c2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}
, then the probability of full infection converges to a constant.

Lemma 17
.

Let
c>0
be a constant and
p=c2^{-\frac{n}{3}}n^{-k}

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{A}}_{0}\;\text{percolates})\rightarrow 1-\exp\left(-\frac{c^{3}}{6\binom{2k}{k}(2k)!k!}\right).

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}_{i}=0)

(28)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\cap_{i}^{N}\{\mathbb{I}_{i}=0\})

(29)

\displaystyle\geqslant\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}(\{\mathbb{I}_{i}=0\}).

(30)

\displaystyle=(1-p^{3})^{N}

(31)

7

.

R
be the set of initially infected vertices and

Therefore, we have

which is the desired result. ∎

Lemma

gives a sufficient condition for bounding the critical probability, and one would want to extend it to all

Lemma 19
.

Let

and

. There exists a subset

\leqslant 2k

.

Proof.

We will show that the set

with

satisfies the claim of the lemma. We claim that

Suppose the contrary and let

be a vertex common to all the neighborhoods. Suppose that
the Hamming weight of the subvector

6.
Discussion and open problems

We conclude with several problems left open in our research.

(1)

What is
m(Q_{n,k},r)
when
k\geqslant 2
and
r=r(n)
is large ?

We conjecture that if
r=r(n)
is a relatively fast growing function of
n
, then
m(Q_{n,k},r)\geqslant\delta 2^{n^{\prime}}
where
n^{\prime}
satisfies that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n^{\prime}}{i}\leqslant cr
with
0<c<1
and
\delta>0
. Note that if this holds, then we obtain an exponentially tight bound for
m(Q_{n,k},r)
over a wide range values of
r
, whereas Theorem
4
gives such a bound only when
r=\theta(n^{k})
.

In order to explain our conjecture it is necessary to introduce the notion of the weak saturation introduced by Bollobás
[
12
]
. Given fixed graphs

G
and
H
, a spanning subgraph
F
of
G
is weakly (
G
,
H
)-saturated if the edges
of
E(G)\backslash E(F)
can be added to
F
, one edge at a time, in such a way that each edge
creates a new copy of
H
when it is added. The weak saturation number of
H
in
G
is defined by

\text{wsat}(G,H):=\text{min}\{|E(F)|:F\>\text{is weakly}\>(G,H)\text{-saturated}\}.

Our conjecture is based on the following bound:

(39)

\displaystyle m(G,r)\geqslant\frac{\text{wsat}(G,S_{r+1})}{r},

where
S_{r+1}
denotes a star with
r+1
rays.

In
[
27
]
, Morrison and Noel used bound (
39
) to determine tight asymptotics for
m(G,r)
, where
G
is the
n
-dimensional Boolean cube and
r
is a fixed constant. In their proof they relied on a linear-algebraic method developed in
[
7
]
to obtain an asymptotically tight lower bound for wsat(
G
,
S_{r+1}
). We conjecture that

\text{wsat}(Q_{n,k},S_{r+1})\geqslant\delta r2^{n^{\prime}}.

To support this conjecture, consider a partitioning of
Q_{n,k}
into
2^{n-n^{\prime}}
isomorphic parts so that each induced subgraph is an
n^{\prime}
-dimensional subcube. Each vertex of an
n^{\prime}
-dimensional subcube has
cr=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n^{\prime}}{i}
neighbors, and the total number of edges in the subcube is
2^{n^{\prime}-1}cr
. We believe that the lower bound
\text{wsat}(Q_{n,k},S_{r+1})\geqslant\delta 2^{n^{\prime}}
can be proved relying on a version of the linear algebraic method.

(2)

What is
p_{c}(G,r)
for a fixed
k
and
r
?

Although Lemma
15
does not hold when
r\geqslant 4
, it might still be possible to derive asymptotics of the critical probability using alternative characterizations of percolating sets.

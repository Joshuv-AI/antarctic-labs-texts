Molecular cloning and antiviral function analysis of pigeon interferon lambda(piIFN-λ)
Authors: Qiwen Huang, Fangyuan Yang, Siyu Wu, Peirong Jiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Type III interferons (IFN-λ) are critical components of the interferon system, serving as key antiviral cytokines in the innate immune response of epithelial cells. However, the biological role of pigeon IFN-λ (piIFN-λ) remains poorly understood. In this study, we cloned and characterized the biological effects of piIFN-λ. Our results reveal that the open reading frame (ORF) of piIFN-λ is 561 base pairs long, encoding a protein consisting of 186 amino acids. The amino acid sequence of piIFN-λ shares 76.3%, 65.1%, 64%, 60.2%, and 31.2% identity with those of shearwater, chicken, duck, goose, and human IFN-λ, respectively. Notably, recombinant piIFN-λ, expressed in a prokaryotic system, exhibited dose‑dependent antiviral activity against both vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) and H9N2 avian influenza virus (AIV). Mechanistically, luciferase reporter assays indicated that recombinant piIFN-λ activated the Mx and ISRE promoters and enhanced Mx, OAS and PKR mRNA expression in a dose‑dependent manner in DF-1 cells. These findings suggest that piIFN-λ may hold potential as a therapeutic agent for avian viral diseases.

Innate immunity serves as the first line of defense against viral infections (Diamond and Kanneganti, 2022). Pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) are a class of immune sensors responsible for recognizing highly conserved components of pathogens, known as pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) (Li and Wu, 2021; Carpenter and O'Neill, 2024; Chen et al., 2025). This recognition initiates a cascade of signaling events, leading to the induction of interferons (IFNs) and inflammatory cytokines (Philips et al., 2022). IFNs are cytokines with a broad range of biological functions, including antiviral, anti-tumor, and immune-regulatory activities (Dowling and Forero, 2022). In mammals, IFNs are categorized into three major types, Type I, Type II, and Type III–distinguished by their molecular architectures, receptor-binding specificity, and functional roles in antiviral and immune regulation (Lazear et al., 2019). Type I IFNs include more than 20 subtypes, such as IFN-α, IFN-β, and IFN-ε, which play a key role in antiviral responses (Stanifer et al., 2020). In contrast, type II IFN only consists of a single member, IFN-γ, which is primarily involved in immunomodulation (Lee and Ashkar, 2018).

Mammalian type III IFNs (IFN-λ), including IFN-λ1, IFN-λ2, IFN-λ3, and IFN-λ4, exert antiviral effects by binding to the heterodimeric receptor complex (IFNLR1/IL10R2), which are primarily expressed on epithelial cells of mucosal surfaces. By engaging this receptor complex, IFN-λs enhance the host's antiviral defenses, particularly in the respiratory and digestive systems (Kotenko et al., 2003; Sheppard et al., 2003; Prokunina-Olsson et al., 2013; Wells and Coyne, 2018). Beyond their direct antiviral actions, IFN-λs also play a pivotal role in regulating dendritic cell activation, antigen presentation, and T cell responses. These functions collectively enable IFN-λ to bridge innate and adaptive immunity, making it a critical mediator in the host's defense against viral infections (Ye et al., 2019). Due to its targeted action and minimal systemic side effects, IFN-λ is being actively studied as a novel therapeutic agent for various viral infections, such as influenza, COVID-19, and hepatitis C, showing great clinical potential (Donnelly et al., 2011; Davidson et al., 2016; Andreakos and Tsiodras, 2020).

Pigeons are the fourth largest source of poultry meat in China, playing a key role in both the economy and culture. However, they are highly susceptible to respiratory viruses like AIV and NDV, which cause severe diseases and significant economic losses. The high population density in pigeon farms accelerates viral transmission, worsening the situation. Given these challenges, studying pigeon IFNs is crucial for understanding their immune response and developing targeted antiviral therapies. Pigeon IFN-α has been shown to combat pigeon circovirus (PiCV) and vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) (Li et al., 2019; Santos et al., 2020). Additionally, pigeon IFN-γ has been reported to function in a chicken macrophage cell line (Fringuelli et al., 2010). However, the role of pigeon IFN-λ remains unexplored, underscoring the need for further research into this promising antiviral agent.

In this study, we cloned the full-length ORF of piIFN-λ from pigeon spleen and expressed recombinant piIFN-λ in a prokaryotic system. We then examined its antiviral effects against Vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) and H9N2 avian influenza virus (AIV), and subsequently assessed its ability to activate the Mx/ISRE promoters and induce ISGs (Mx, OAS, PKR) expression in DF-1 cells.

Materials and methods

Pigeons, cells and viruses

Healthy Chinese local pigeons (Shiqi pigeons) used in this study were purchased from a pigeon farm in Zhongshan, China, and were housed in an isolator until they reached 10 days of age. Chicken fibroblast cells (DF-1) were maintained in our laboratory. All cells were cultured in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO₂. Vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV), VSV-GFP and H9N2 avian influenza virus (AIV) stocks were stored in the laboratory. The infectivity of these viruses in DF-1 cells was determined using endpoint dilution assays to calculate the 50% tissue culture infective dose (TCID50).

RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA from cells was extracted using Trizol Reagent (Invitrogen, USA), and 1 μg of RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Vazyme, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. qRT-PCR was performed using ChamQ™ SYBR® qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China) on a Bio-Rad CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, USA) under the following conditions: 5 min at 95°C, followed by 40 cycles of 10 s at 95°C and 34 s at 55°C. Primers used in this study are listed in Table 1. The GAPDH gene was used as an internal control. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Cloning and bioinformatics analysis of piIFN-λ

The predicted sequence of piIFN-λ (Accession number: PKK29680.1) was retrieved from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). Specific primers (Table 1) were designed to amplify the open reading frame (ORF) of piIFN-λ from pigeon spleen cDNA using PrimeSTAR polymerase (Takara, Japan). The full-length coding sequence (CDS) of piIFN-λ was inserted into the pMD19-T vector (Takara, Dalian, China) and sequenced (Shenggong, Shanghai, China).

The amino acid sequences of IFN-λ from various species were aligned using CLUSTAL OMEGA (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/msa/clustalo/). Sequence similarities and secondary structure elements of aligned sequences were visualized using the ESPript program (https://espript.ibcp.fr/ESPript/ESPript/). Homology comparison and phylogenetic analysis of IFN-λ amino acid sequences from 11 species were conducted using the MegAlign program in DNASTAR software. The SWISS-MODEL server (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/) was used to predict and model the piIFN-λ protein structure.

Prokaryotic expression and purification of recombinant piIFN-λ

To investigate the biological activity of piIFN-λ, the recombinant piIFN-λ plasmid was expressed using a prokaryotic expression system, as previously described (Dai et al., 2016). Briefly, the full-length CDS of piIFN-λ was amplified with specific primers and cloned into the EcoRI and XhoI restriction sites of the pET28b vector to construct the recombinant plasmid pET28b-piIFN-λ, which encodes piIFN-λ fused with 6 × His- tag. This plasmid was then transformed into BL21 (DE3) E.coli cells. Recombinant protein expression was induced by adding isopropyl β-D-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at a final concentration of 1 mM at 37°C. The cells were lysed, and inclusion bodies were collected, then solubilized in a denaturing buffer containing 8 M urea. The protein was refolded by dialysis in decreasing concentrations of urea. Ni–NTA affinity chromatography was used to purify the refolded protein according to the manufacturer’s instructions (GE, USA), followed by dialysis to remove imidazole and concentrate the sample. Protein concentration was determined using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher, USA). The purified protein was analyzed by Coomassie-stained SDS-PAGE, and the purity was assessed by densitometry using ImageJ software. The identity of the recombinant piIFN-λ was further confirmed by Western blot analysis using a mouse monoclonal anti-His antibody (1:5000; Sigma, USA) and IRDye 800-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (1:10,000; Rockland Immunochemicals, USA), with blots visualized on an Odyssey infrared scanner (Li-Cor, USA). Endotoxin levels in the purified pigeon interferon were evaluated using an LAL endotoxin assay kit (Genscript, China). Recombinant chicken IFN-α (chIFN-α), used as a positive control in this study, was expressed, refolded, and purified using exactly the same prokaryotic expression and purification protocols as piIFN-λ described above. The expression system and functional validation method for chIFN-α have been previously established in our laboratory (Dai et al., 2016).

Antiviral activity analysis of piIFN-λ

To evaluate the antiviral effect of recombinant piIFN-λ, antiviral assays were performed as previously described (Dai et al., 2016). All experiments were performed independently in triplicate. DF-1 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured overnight until 80% confluent. Cells were pretreated with serial four-fold dilutions of recombinant piIFN-λ. After 24 hours, recombinant piIFN-λ was removed, and the cells were infected with 100 TCID50 of VSV or H9N2 AIV. Negative control cells were treated with PBS only, and positive control cells were infected with VSV or H9N2 AIV but not treated with piIFN-λ. Cells were observed every 12 hours for cytopathic effects (CPE) under a microscope. The biological activity of recombinant piIFN-λ was defined as the amount required to inhibit VSV-induced CPE by 50%, and the results were calculated using the Reed-Muench method.

To further quantify the antiviral effect at a defined dose, DF-1 cells in 12-well plates were pretreated with 1000 U/mL piIFN-λ (based on the specific activity determined above) or PBS for 24 h, then infected with 100 TCID50 of VSV-GFP or H9N2 AIV. At 24 h post-infection, culture supernatants were collected for TCID50 titration on DF-1 cells using the Reed-Muench method, and cell lysates were prepared for Western blot analysis. Western blot was performed as described for the verification of recombinant piIFN-λ, using primary antibodies against EGFP (Proteintech, 1:5000), H9N2 NP (Sino Biological, 1:2000), and GAPDH (Proteintech, 1:5000). Blots were visualized with the Odyssey infrared scanner. Antiviral assays were performed in triplicate.

Luciferase reporter assays

To determine the ability of recombinant piIFN-λ to activate the Mx and ISRE promoters, luciferase reporter assays were performed as previously described (Dai et al., 2020). Briefly, DF-1 cells were seeded in 24-well plates and cultured overnight until 80% confluent. The cells were then co-transfected using FuGENE HD with a mixture of either 100 ng of pGL3-chMx-Luc or 100 ng of pGL3-chISRE-Luc reporter plasmid, along with 10 ng of pRL-TK as an internal control per well. At 24 hours post-transfection, cells were treated with 100 U/mL or 1000 U/mL (based on the specific antiviral activity determined above) of either recombinant piIFN-λ or the positive control chIFN-α. PBS-treated cells were used as a mock control. At 12 hours post-treatment, luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA), and luminescence was recorded with a Fluorescence/Multi-Detection Microplate Reader (BioTek, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase luminescence. The results were averaged from three independent assays.

Expression of piIFN-λ-induced ISGs in DF-1 cells

To assess the effect of piIFN-λ on the expression of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), DF-1 cells were seeded in 12-well plates and cultured overnight to reach 80% confluence. Based on the specific activity determined above, DF-1 cells were treated with recombinant piIFN-λ at 100 U/mL or 1000 U/mL. As a positive control, recombinant chIFN-α was used at identical doses. After 24 hours, the cells were harvested, and the expression levels of Mx, OAS, PKR were quantified using qRT-PCR as described before. The GAPDH gene expression served as an internal control. Cells treated with PBS instead of IFNs were used as negative controls. All reactions were performed in triplicate, and the data are presented as means ± SD. The primers used for qRT-PCR are listed in Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD from three independent experiments. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 7.0 software (GraphPad Software Inc., USA). The two-tailed independent Student’s t-test was used to assess statistical significance. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05; **p < 0.01).

The study involving Chinese local pigeons (Shiqi pigeons) was conducted at the laboratory of the Agro-biological Gene Research Center, Guangdong Academy of Agricultural Sciences. Results

Bioinformatics analysis of piIFN-λ

Primers were designed based on the predicted sequence of piIFN-λ obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), and used to amplify the full-length piIFN-λ cDNA from pigeon spleen RNA. The coding sequence (CDS) of piIFN-λ was found to be 561 bp, encoding a protein of 186 amino acid residues. The nucleotide sequence of piIFN-λ mRNA has been submitted to GenBank under accession number PX401965. Multiple sequence alignment revealed that the amino acid sequence of piIFN-λ shares 76.3%, 65.1%, 64%, 60.2%, 31.2%, and 30.6% identity with those of shearwater, chicken, duck, goose, human, and mouse, respectively (Fig. 1). Homology comparison of IFN-λ amino acid sequences from 11 species was performed using the MegAlign program in the DNASTAR software, and the results are presented in Fig. 2A. Phylogenetic analysis demonstrated that piIFN-λ clustered within the avian clade, exhibiting the closest relationship to shearwater IFN-λ (Fig. 2B). Recombinant piIFN-λ was efficiently expressed in E. coli BL21 (DE3) cells as inclusion bodies using the pET28b-piIFN-λ expression system and subsequently purified by Ni–NTA affinity chromatography as previously described. Endotoxin levels in the purified pigeon interferon were evaluated using an LAL endotoxin assay kit (Genscript, China). Luciferase reporter assays

Prior to evaluating the biological functions of recombinant piIFN-λ, a CCK-8 assay was performed to assess its potential cytotoxicity. The results indicated that treatment with recombinant piIFN-λ at the tested concentrations did not negatively affect cell viability compared to the untreated control (Supplementary Figure S1). This confirmed that the immunomodulatory effects were not due to cell death.

To determine the ability of recombinant piIFN-λ to activate the Mx and ISRE promoters, luciferase reporter assays were performed. As shown in Fig. 7, recombinant piIFN-λ treatment significantly activated both ISRE and Mx promoters in a dose‑dependent manner, with activity increasing by 7.8 to 29.5-fold relative to the PBS control when the dose was raised from 100 U/mL to 1000 U/mL (all p < 0.01). As expected, the positive control chIFN-α exhibited substantially stronger promoter activation, inducing 19.2 to 157.8-fold increases in activity across the same dose range. piIFN-λ-induced ISGs in DF-1

To assess the ability of piIFN-λ to induce interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), DF-1 cells were treated with 100 or 1000 U/mL of recombinant piIFN-λ or chIFN-α (positive control) for 24 h, and the mRNA levels of Mx, OAS, and PKR were quantified by qRT-PCR. As shown in Fig. 8, all three tested antiviral ISGs (Mx, OAS, PKR) were significantly upregulated by piIFN-λ in a dose-dependent fashion, with induction levels ranging from 1.9 to 6.0-fold (all p < 0.01 vs. PBS control). Consistent with the promoter assay results, chIFN-α exhibited substantially stronger induction activity, driving 4.6 to 268-fold increases in ISG expression at the same doses. Discussion

Although vaccination is the cornerstone of poultry disease control, its efficacy is increasingly undermined by the emergence of novel and emerging viral variants, underscoring the need for alternative antiviral strategies. Avian interferons, particularly IFN-λ, exhibit broad-spectrum activity and act at mucosal epithelial surfaces, making them promising candidates for combating respiratory and enteric pathogens (Lazear et al., 2015; He et al., 2024). In this study, we successfully cloned piIFN-λ for the first time, and amino acid sequence analysis revealed that piIFN-λ shares high similarity with other avian IFN-λs, including those from shearwater, chicken, duck, and geese. Phylogenetic analysis further confirmed that piIFN-λ belongs to the avian clade, exhibiting the closest evolutionary relationship with shearwater IFN-λ. Consistently, structural modeling predicted a reliable piIFN-λ structure with 72.6% identity to Geococcyx californianus IFN-λ and a GMQE score of 0.88, supporting its evolutionary conservation and the retention of canonical features for receptor binding and signaling.

Based on these molecular findings, we further assessed piIFN-λ’s ability to activate cellular signaling pathways. Given its structural and functional conservation, we hypothesized that piIFN-λ could activate the canonical JAK-STAT pathway. In luciferase reporter assays, piIFN-λ dose‑dependently activated both ISRE and Mx promoters, with 1000 U/mL inducing approximately 22‑fold and 29‑fold increases, respectively. Recombinant piIFN-λ also induced significant upregulation of ISG genes, including Mx, OAS, and PKR, in a dose‑dependent manner. These findings demonstrate that piIFN-λ activates the canonical IFN signaling pathways in DF-1 cells, supporting the conserved functionality of avian IFN-λ. This is consistent with previous findings for chicken, duck, and goose IFN-λ (Masuda et al., 2012; Yao et al., 2014; Chen et al., 2017), emphasizing the functional similarity across avian species. Notably, the positive control chIFN‑α induced substantially higher levels of ISG expression than piIFN‑λ. This difference likely reflects the generally stronger signaling potency of type I interferons, as well as the species compatibility of DF‑1 cells (chicken origin) with homologous chIFN‑α. Nevertheless, the dose‑dependent activation by piIFN‑λ indicates its ability to engage the chicken IFN‑λ receptor.

Furthermore, accumulating evidence supports the potent antiviral effects of avian IFN-λ against a wide range of avian pathogens. For instance, recombinant chicken IFN-λ has been shown to inhibit the replication of major respiratory viruses such as AIV, NDV, and fowl adenovirus serotype 4 (FAdV-4) (Reuter et al., 2014; Lai et al., 2024). Duck IFN-λ also has demonstrated antiviral activity against VSV and duck Tembusu virus (DTMUV) (Zhou et al., 2023), while goose IFN-λ exhibits notable cross-species protection, suppressing the replication of duck plague virus (DPV) (Chen et al., 2017). A recent study has identified a novel splice variant of chicken IFN-λ3, chIFN-λ3a, which encodes a shorter protein with stronger antiviral activity against NDV and AIV than chIFN-λ3(Chen et al., 2023), highlighting the potential role of alternative splicing in enhancing antiviral capacity.

Consistent with above observations, pretreatment with recombinant piIFN-λ significantly inhibited the replication of both VSV and H9N2 AIV in DF-1 cells. To obtain more definitive evidence, we performed additional experiments at a fixed dose of 1000 U/mL. For VSV‑GFP, piIFN‑λ pretreatment dramatically reduced GFP fluorescence, GFP protein levels, and progeny virus titers. For H9N2 AIV, piIFN‑λ similarly decreased NP protein expression and viral titers. These results provide direct quantitative evidence that piIFN‑λ suppresses viral replication at both the protein and infectious virus levels. These results not only establish the functional relevance of piIFN-λ but also extend the spectrum of avian species in which IFN-λ has been characterized, providing a broader perspective on the conserved role of type III IFNs in avian antiviral immunity. Interestingly, the specific activity of pigeon IFN-α against VSV was reported to be 5.5 × 105 U/mg in a previous study (Li et al., 2019), which is markedly higher than the 4.8 × 104 U/mg of piIFN-λ. This marked difference in potency highlights the functional divergence between the two pigeon IFN subtypes and suggests they may play distinct roles in antiviral immunity.

While this study provides evidence for the antiviral activity of piIFN-λ, several limitations should be noted. First, due to the lack of well-established pigeon-derived continuous cell lines, DF-1 cells were used as a practical avian in vitro model for initial functional characterization. DF-1 cells are also a well-characterized and IFN-responsive avian cell line and have been widely used as surrogate systems for pigeon innate immunity studies (Xu et al., 2015; Li et al., 2019; Shao et al., 2023). Nevertheless, DF-1 cell responses may not fully recapitulate those of pigeon cells. Future work using pigeon-derived primary cells will be necessary to assess the species-specific immune responses to piIFN-λ. Second, the findings are based mainly on in vitro assays, and the immune functions as well as the therapeutic efficacy of piIFN-λ remain to be confirmed in vivo. Future studies employing pigeon-derived models and animal experiments will be critical to define its biological roles and assess its translational potential.

In conclusion, this study successfully cloned and characterized the biological activity of piIFN-λ. We demonstrated that piIFN-λ belongs to the avian clade and is capable of inducing ISGs. Moreover, piIFN-λ exhibited significant antiviral efficacy against VSV and H9N2 AIV. Collectively, these findings suggest that piIFN-λ could be a promising candidate for developing antiviral therapeutics for avian species.

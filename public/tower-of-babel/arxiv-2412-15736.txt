Distribution-Free Normal Modal Logics
Chrysafis Hartonas

Abstract
This article initiates the semantic study of distribution-free normal modal logic systems, laying the semantic foundations and anticipating further research in the area. The article explores roughly the same area, though taking a different approach, with a recent article by Bezhanishvili, de Groot, Dmitrieva and Morachini, who studied a distribution-free version of Dunn’s Positive Modal Logic (PML). Unlike PML, we consider logics that may drop distribution and which are equipped with both an implication connective and modal operators. We adopt a uniform relational semantics approach, relying on recent results on representation and duality for normal lattice expansions. We prove canonicity and completeness in the relational semantics of the minimal distribution-free normal modal logic, assuming just the K-axiom, as well as of its axiomatic extensions obtained by adding any of the D, T, B, S4 or S5 axioms. Adding distribution can be easily accommodated and, as a side result, we also obtain a new semantic treatment of Intuitionistic Modal Logic. Keywords : Sub-classical modal logic; Intuitionistic modal logic; Distribution-free modal logic; Completeness via canonicity

Proposition 6.2

(Canonicity of the K-Axiom)
.

The frame axiom (FK) holds in the canonical frame and it is equivalent to the inclusion

\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}(\Gamma x\Rightarrow{}\mbox{${}^{\upVdash}$}\{y\})\subseteq\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}\Gamma x\Rightarrow\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}({}\mbox{${}^{\upVdash}$}\{y\})

Proof.

It follows from the definition of the point operators that

iff there exist elements

The inclusion in the statement of the current Lemma is equivalent to the inclusion

Proposition 6.3

(Canonicity of the D-Axiom)

The canonical frame for the minimal logic

(Canonicity of the B-Axioms)

Distributive and Heyting Frames

By a
distributive frame
we mean a frame
\mathfrak{F}=(s,Z,I,(R_{j})_{j\in J},\sigma)
such that its complete lattice of stable sets
{\mathcal{G}}(Z_{1})
is (completely) distributive. Proposition
7.1
identifies a condition for the frame to be distributive. That the canonical frame of a distributive lattice satisfies the condition in this Proposition was proven in
[
25
, Proposition 5.4, Case 5]
.

Proposition 7.1

(
[
23
, Proposition 3.13]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(s,Z,I,(R_{j})_{j\in J},\sigma)
be a frame and
{\mathcal{G}}(Z_{1})
the complete lattice of stable sets. If all sections of the Galois dual relation
R^{\prime}_{\leq}
of the upper bound relation
R_{\leq}
(where
uR_{\leq}xz
iff both
x\preceq u
and
z\preceq u
) are Galois sets, then
{\mathcal{G}}(Z_{1})
is completely distributive.
\Box

The condition for a frame to be a Heyting frame is that its full complex algebra
\mathfrak{F}^{+}=({\mathcal{G}}(Z_{1}),\subseteq,\bigcap,\bigvee,\emptyset^{\prime\prime},Z_{1},\Rightarrow,\ldots)
be a (complete) Heyting algebra (with additional structure due to the modal operators). The implication construct
\Rightarrow
is residuated with a product operator
\bigovert
(Proposition
3.11
), which is the closure of the image operator
\bigodot
generated by the relation
R^{111}
of Definition
3.5
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
is a residuated lattice. Hence it will be a Heyting algebra if
\bigovert
is the same as intersection on stable sets. Working out the detail of this observation we have the following result.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(s,Z,I,T^{\partial 1\partial},\ldots)
be a frame. Then the full complex algebra of the frame is a complete Heyting algebra iff the relation
R^{111}
coincides with the upper bound relation
R_{\leq}
on the frame. In that case, the implication construct is equivalently defined by
x\in(A\Rightarrow C)
iff
\forall z\in Z_{1}(x\leq z\longrightarrow(z\in A\longrightarrow z\in C))
.

Proof.

The claims were proven in
[
23
, Propositions 3.15, 3.17]
.
∎

That the canonical frame of a Heyting algebra satisfies the condition of the above theorem was proven in
[
23
, Proposition 4.11]
.

Remark 7.3
.

When
R^{111}
coincides with the upper bound relation
R_{\leq}
, then
\bigodot
is intersection of stable and, more generally, of upper closed sets in the
\leq
order. This follows from the fact that
U\bigodot W=\{u\;|\;\exists x,z(x\in U\wedge z\in W\wedge uR_{\leq}xz)\}
. But note that
\bigodot
need not be intersection of arbitrary subsets of
Z_{1}
. Note also that for any increasing subsets (any upsets)
U,W
, the set
U\Rightarrow_{T}W
is also an upset, when
R^{111}=R_{\leq}
. Hence, in the case of a Heyting frame, the residuated pair
\bigodot,\Rightarrow_{T}
in
\powerset(Z_{1})
restricts to a residuated pair in the family (complete lattice)
\mathcal{UP}(Z_{1})
of increasing subsets of
Z_{1}
(upper closed subsets in the
\preceq
-order), and to a residuated pair
\bigodot,\Rightarrow
in
{\mathcal{G}}(Z_{1})
. The latter is the case because for stable sets
A,C
,
A\bigodot C=A\cap C
is a stable set hence
A\bigovert C=(A\bigodot C)^{\prime\prime}=A\bigodot C=A\cap C
.

Theorem 7.4
.

If the frame
\mathfrak{F}=(s,Z,I,T^{\partial 1\partial},R^{\partial\partial}_{\Box},\ldots)
is a Heyting frame, then the K-axiom is valid in
{\mathcal{G}}(Z_{1})
. Consequently, the minimal Intuitionistic modal logic, assuming only the K-axiom, is valid in the class of Heyting frames.

Proof.

Assume
u\in\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}(A\Rightarrow C)
. To show that
u\in\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}A\Rightarrow\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}C
, let
z\in Z_{1}
be such that
u\leq z
and
z\in\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}A
. To get
z\in\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}C
, which is equivalent to
zR^{\prime\prime}_{\Box}\subseteq C
, let
p\in Z_{1}
be such that
zR^{\prime\prime}_{\Box}p
. It suffices to get
p\in C
. From
u\leq z,zR^{\prime\prime}_{\Box}p
and Lemma
3.15
it follows that
uR^{\prime\prime}_{\Box}p
. Using the hypothesis
u\in\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}(A\Rightarrow C)
we then have
p\in A\Rightarrow C
. Furthermore, using the hypothesis
z\in\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\largesquare\hskip-7.6pt\Box\;$}}A
we also get
p\in A
. Given the definition of
\Rightarrow
in the Heyting case,
A\Rightarrow C=\{p\;|\;\forall z(p\leq z\wedge z\in A\longrightarrow z\in C)\}
, and since
p
suttisfies the premisses of the defining implication for
A\Rightarrow C
, we get
p\in C
, as needed. Alternatively, use the fact that
p\in A\cap(A\Rightarrow C)\subseteq C
.
∎

By Theorem
7.4
, no need arises in the case of a modal extension of the Intuitionistic propositional calculus to consider second-order axioms for frames. Frames for extensions with any of the axioms D, T, B, S4, or S5 can be dealt with roughly as done for the distribution-free case, but we shall not dwell on this here.

8
Further Issues and Conclusions

This article is part of an ongoing project. It worked through the necessary background, presenting methods and techniques that can be used to model distribution-free systems, applied to the case of modal logic. The area is largely unexplored and much remains to be done, the main interest being in finding out how much of the classical model theory of normal modal logics on a Boolean propositional basis can be actually carried over, appropriately modified, to the case of distribution-free modal logic. We outline some of the related issues.

With a notion of

[
24
]

can be specialized to a duality for implicative modal lattices, as it has been already specialized to dualities for implicative lattices and Heyting algebras

and for logics with a weak negation logical operator
[

Introducing both a classical but sorted modal logic of frames and a sorted first-order language, the van Benthem correspondence problem for distribution-free modal logics can be resolved, as it is an instance of the problem for the logics of arbitrary normal lattice expansions, a project that has been carried out in

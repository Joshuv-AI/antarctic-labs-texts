Evaluation of the hospital service quality using the Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA) tool in Ardabil city
Authors: Yalda Mousazadeh, Hessane Habibi, Azizollah Arbabisarjou, Reza Ebrahimoghli, Mohammad Mehrtak
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveQuality can serve as a strategic differentiator that provides a competitive advantage. The Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA) model is an effective tool for identifying quality gaps from the perspective of users. This study aims to evaluate the quality of hospital services from the viewpoint of inpatients in teaching hospitals in Ardabil city using the IPA model.ResultsThe overall mean score of service performance from the patients’ perspective was 68.43 ± 2.82, while the importance score was 98.34 ± 2.58. The highest performance was observed in the service process (11.76 ± 1.09), tangibles (10.30 ± 1.41), and empathy (9.49 ± 0.93). The most significant dimensions were also the service process (16.44 ± 0.98) and tangibles (15.30 ± 1.41). A significant correlation was found between importance and performance scores (r = 0.89, p < 0.001). Demographic and clinical variables did not have a significant effect on patients’ perceptions (p > 0.05).

Introduction

The concept of quality in healthcare is complex and multifaceted, with little consensus on its definition [1]. Two definitions—those provided by the American Medical Association and the World Health Organization—consider dimensions such as safety, effectiveness, patient-centeredness, acceptability, accessibility, timeliness, efficiency, and equity as key aspects of healthcare quality [2, 3]. Hospitals are at the forefront of healthcare systems, serving as the largest health organizations and offering similar types of services; however, the quality of these services varies widely among hospitals [4]. On the other hand, advancements in pharmaceuticals and technology, coupled with a commitment to evidence-based practices, indicate that the services provided by hospitals significantly impact individuals’ health and must be delivered appropriately [5].

Research indicates that patient satisfaction with the quality of inpatient, diagnostic, support, and administrative services in Iranian hospitals is low and requires further investigation [6]. A study by Karimi Shirazi et al. focused on improving the quality of dental services and found that aspects such as the provision of care, problem resolution, service speed, and responsiveness were inadequate and needed special attention [7]. Additionally, a study by Gorji et al. examining the quality of healthcare centers in Zahedan revealed discrepancies between the average scores of patient perception and expectation in all quality dimensions, with perceived quality consistently falling short of expected quality [8]. Furthermore, research by Rajabipour Meybodi et al. demonstrated that patient satisfaction with service quality in teaching hospitals associated with Shahid Sadoughi University of Medical Sciences in Yazd was below their expected level [9].

Measuring service quality and implementing safety principles in hospitals and other healthcare institutions are crucial for enhancing healthcare systems in countries undergoing significant economic and managerial transitions [10]. There are three main approaches to measuring service quality: the technical approach, the philosophical approach, and the customer-oriented approach. In the intellectual approach, quality is viewed as inherent superiority. The technical approach defines quality based on how well a product adheres to technical standards. Meanwhile, the customer-oriented approach considers quality to be subjective, determined by the recipients’ perceptions [11]. In recent decades, patients’ perspectives on the healthcare services they receive have become crucial indicators of quality [5]. Providing detailed information about customer perceptions of service quality can help organizations identify areas that impact their competitive advantage while preventing resource waste [12]. Additionally, there are two key reasons why patient feedback is important. First, patients are well-positioned to evaluate specific aspects of the treatment process. Second, understanding patients’ expectations and their perceptions of service quality enables decision-makers to align healthcare services more closely with those expectations [13].

Different models are employed to evaluate the quality of hospital services. One well-known model is the Donabedian model, which includes three dimensions: structure, process, and outcome. This model evaluates not only the physical and organizational characteristics of the care environment but also the manner in which services are delivered and the interactions between patients and healthcare workers [14]. Additionally, it examines the effects of care on patient health, providing a comprehensive framework for classifying and analyzing quality dimensions [14]. However, this model overlooks the active role of the patient and the influence of the external environment. Another model is the SERVQUAL model, which focuses on the gap between patients’ expectations and the actual services they receive [15]. This model is largely influenced by patient expectations. Additionally, there is the continuous quality improvement model. While it is not a specific model, it represents a management philosophy that emphasizes gradual and ongoing improvements in processes and systems to enhance the quality of services [16].

One of the most effective models for measuring customer satisfaction with service quality is the Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA). This model evaluates customer satisfaction based on two key components: importance and performance. A significant advantage of using IPA is that it quickly identifies an organization’s strengths and weaknesses in terms of customer satisfaction. The underlying philosophy of this approach is that certain attributes hold more significance than others and, therefore, warrant more attention [17]. Sethna (2015) refers to IPA Analysis as a valuable tool for pinpointing critical areas that require improvement. He argues that by utilizing this method and understanding how customers perceive the organization, both strengths and weaknesses can be revealed. This insight can lead to the development of effective strategies and performance enhancements [18].

Most studies in this area have primarily focused on customer perceptions and expectations, often neglecting the importance of service dimensions [8, 9]. This study aims to evaluate patient satisfaction with hospital performance and identify the importance of each dimension in three hospitals in Ardabil city, utilizing the IPA tool across eight qualitative dimensions. By highlighting their strengths and weaknesses, this research provides valuable insights for managers, enabling them to implement quality improvement programs that align with customer expectations and effectively allocate their limited resources, thereby enhancing their competitive position.

Methods

Study design

The present study is descriptive-analytical in nature. The statistical population comprised inpatients from all departments of teaching hospitals in Ardabil city, specifically Alavi, Fatemi, and Imam Khomeini Hospitals, from May to August 2024. These three hospitals are educational and treatment facilities operated by the government and affiliated with Ardabil University of Medical Sciences. Imam Khomeini Hospital is the largest and most significant hospital in Ardabil Province, featuring 470 active beds. Fatemi Hospital, one of the oldest hospitals in the province, serves as the only trauma center in the area and has 202 active beds. Alavi Hospital, known for its specialization in gynecology and obstetrics, has 212 active beds. 420 patients were selected for the study. This sample consisted of conscious patients aged 18 and older who did not have psychological or cognitive disorders and were capable of responding to the questionnaire. Outpatients were not included in the study. Researchers visited hospitals three days a week during both morning and evening shifts. They distributed the questionnaire in departments that had received approval from the authorities.

Study tools

A two-part questionnaire was employed to gather data. The first part focused on demographic information about the patients, while the second part consisted of a researcher-designed questionnaire created by Lotfee, based on IPA [17]. The validity and reliability of this tool have been confirmed in Lotfee’s study. In the study assessing convergent validity, the correlation between all questions and the overall service quality index question ranged from 0.40 to 0.85, which is significant at the 5% level. Additionally, the reliability coefficient of the questionnaire exceeded 0.90 [17]. This questionnaire comprises 32 questions distributed across 8 dimensions: Tangibles (items 1–5), Reliability (6–9), Responsiveness (10–14), Assurance (15–17), Empathy (18–21), Service Process (22–26), Social Responsibility (27–29), and Service Organization (30–32). A 4-point Likert scale was used for scoring, with responses for the performance dimension ranging from “strongly disagree” (1) to “strongly agree” (4), and for the importance dimension from “not important at all” (1) to “very important” (4). Patients responded to each question twice—once regarding performance and once regarding importance. The questionnaire was completed without a time gap. The researchers explained to the patients that they should complete the questionnaire once based on the importance they attach to the topic and once based on the performance they observe. The minimum and maximum total scores for the questionnaire were 32 and 128, respectively.

Data analysis

Data analysis was conducted using the IPA model. A matrix was created based on two factors: the current performance level of the hospital and the perceived importance of each quality dimension from the patients’ perspective [19]. In this matrix, the x-axis represents performance, while the y-axis represents importance (see Fig. 1).

In this study’s results analysis, we reported the performance and importance scores, along with the patients’ demographic variables, using descriptive statistics such as frequency, percentage, mean/median, and standard deviation. We utilized parametric tests, including the T-test and ANOVA, to explore the relationships between demographic variables and importance scores. To assess the correlation between importance and performance scores, we applied Pearson’s correlation test. Furthermore, multiple linear regression analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive power of demographic variables alongside importance and performance scores. Excel software was employed to create Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA) matrices for each hospital. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS software version 26, with a significance level set at 0.05.

Discussion

This study employed the IPA model to assess patients’ perceptions of hospital services in Ardabil. The findings revealed that patients regarded the importance of healthcare services as higher than the performance they actually received, indicating a gap between their expectations and the reality they experienced. Furthermore, a significant correlation was found between the importance assigned to services and perceived performance. Notably, demographic and clinical factors did not significantly influence service importance or performance, suggesting a consistent experience of service quality among patients in the hospitals.

The results showed that both the tangible aspects and the procedural execution of services were perceived as satisfactory. In comparison, Izadi et al. reported that patients prioritized the “Tangibles” dimension in terms of importance, but noted that better performance was observed in the “Reliability” component, while “Social Responsibility” was identified as the weakest-performing dimension in their study [21]. Similarly, Shafiq et al. highlighted good performance in “Tangibles” for hospitals in Pakistan, but also pointed out the need for improvement in areas of responsibility and empathy [22]. La Fata et al. found satisfactory performance in tangibles among Italian public hospitals, yet indicated that improvements in organization and responsibility were needed [23]. In summary, these findings are in line with international research, emphasizing the significance of tangible dimensions and service processes while highlighting organizational responsibility as a common weakness across various health systems.

Patients expressed that the service process, tangibles, and responsiveness were of high importance to them. In contrast, they deemed assurance, service organization, and social responsibility as less critical. This indicates a need to focus more on the human aspects and organizational transparency in performance. Research conducted by Pekkaya et al. in Turkey emphasized that “tangibles” were considered the most important and received high performance scores, while “reliability” had a significant impact on overall satisfaction [24]. In a conceptual study, Ali et al. showed that patients focus on tangible and behavioral aspects; however, they noted that the traditional SERVQUAL model does not sufficiently address healthcare services and recommended including dimensions like patient safety and professionalism [25]. Similarly, Behdioğlu et al. conducted a study in a Turkish rehabilitation hospital using the fuzzy SERVQUAL model, which revealed that patients reported the largest gaps in expectations for “Tangibles” and “Responsiveness.” This confirms the significance of these components from the patients’ perspective [26]. In Syria, AlOmari conducted a study on service quality in five private hospitals and found that “Empathy” and “Responsiveness” ranked highest in patient expectations. In contrast, “Service Organization” and “Assurance” received lower scores. These results align with the patient priorities identified in the current study [27]. Our findings are consistent with global trends in the evaluation of hospital services. They highlight that patients value tangible aspects, behavior, and responsiveness more than administrative and organizational structures. This emphasis indicates that to improve patient satisfaction, managers should concentrate on enhancing human interaction, ensuring transparency, and directly improving the overall service experience.

On the other hand, a positive and significant correlation was observed between the importance and performance scores. This suggests that when patients place greater importance on a specific dimension, they tend to provide higher evaluations of its performance. This highlights the impact of patient expectations on their service experience. La Fata et al. found that components deemed important by patients in Italian public hospitals generally received higher performance ratings. This indicates that aligning the importance of various factors with their performance is a key indicator of effective management [23]. Pekkaya et al. found that the dimension of “Reliability,” which patients in Turkey deemed most important, significantly influenced their satisfaction [24]. In contrast, Izadi et al. in Iran pointed out gaps between importance and performance in certain dimensions like “Tangibles,” stressing the need for alignment in service policies [21].

In a study conducted in a Taiwanese intensive care unit, Lu et al. utilized the SERVQUAL model and IPA. The findings revealed a strong correlation between the importance and performance across most dimensions, particularly in “Assurance” and “Responsiveness.” This suggests that the system effectively addresses patient priorities [28]. Similarly, in Taiwan’s digital health industry, a study by Jou and Day revealed a significant relationship between the explicit importance and perceived performance, which is crucial for optimizing resources [29]. However, contrary to these findings, A’aqoulah et al. reported significant gaps between importance and performance in all SERVQUAL dimensions in Jordan, where high-importance dimensions showed weaker performance. They argued that emphasizing importance does not necessarily lead to improved performance [30]. Similarly, a study conducted in Indian hospitals by Kansara and Jha revealed that certain important dimensions, such as Empathy, did not always align with high performance. The researchers found no significant correlation between these two variables. They concluded that organizational barriers or resource constraints may weaken the relationship between the importance and performance of these dimensions [31]. Many studies indicate a positive link between importance and performance; however, conflicting findings suggest this relationship is influenced by managerial, cultural, or structural factors, requiring context-specific examination.

Our findings indicate that there were no significant differences in the perception of service importance or performance based on demographic and clinical variables. This suggests that patients experienced a consistent level of care, regardless of their individual characteristics. Similar conclusions were drawn by Lu et al. in Taiwan, who reported that perceptions of quality in intensive care were primarily influenced by experiential factors rather than demographic aspects [28]. Pekkaya et al. observed in Turkey that demographic factors had a minimal impact on patient evaluations, while the type of service provided had a more significant influence [24]. Jonkisz et al. highlighted that in over half of the studies conducted in Asia, demographic characteristics had minimal effects on perceived quality [32]. In contrast, Aqoulah et al. found in their study in Jordan that patients with higher education reported larger gaps between their expectations and reality [30]. Additionally, Al Fraihi and Latif’s research in Saudi Arabia revealed that variables such as gender, age, and education level were associated with significant differences in perceived service quality [33]. Al-Balas et al. found that income and type of insurance influenced patient satisfaction, particularly among users of private healthcare services [34]. Ultimately, the influence of demographic characteristics on patient experience may vary based on cultural contexts and healthcare interactions.

Strengths and limitations

The current study was conducted with a significant sample of hospitalized patients in Ardabil city. Additionally, by focusing on various performance dimensions, the results provide valuable insights that can assist managers in making decisions. However, the study does have some limitations. First, the focus on a single province and the use of convenience sampling may reduce the generalizability of the findings. Second, individual and cultural factors, such as patients’ awareness and expectations, can influence their responses. On the other hand, patients might exaggerate the importance of certain quality components that were previously in poor condition. Conversely, if an element has consistently performed well, they may perceive it as normal and underestimate its actual importance.

Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that the performance of hospital services in hospitals in Ardabil city is at a moderate level from the patients’ perspective. Additionally, the importance that patients assign to various service dimensions is positively and significantly correlated with the performance scores of those dimensions. This study reveals that patients place the greatest emphasis on tangibles, the service delivery process, and responsiveness, with these areas also showing more favorable performance levels. In contrast, demographic and clinical variables did not significantly affect the average importance or performance scores of services. These findings suggested that efforts to improve hospital services in these hospitals should focus on strengthening key elements of services, rather than simply targeting individual patient characteristics. Also, considering patients’ perspectives can assist managers in making informed decisions to enhance quality. It seems staff need to communicate clearly and empathetically with patients and their families. Additionally, time management should be prioritized to minimize delays. Other recommended solutions include workflow optimization, effective resource management, and staff empowerment.

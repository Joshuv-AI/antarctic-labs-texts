Stars of graphs of projective codes
Edyta Bartnicka

Abstract
Let Γ k ​ ( V ) \Gamma_{k}(V) be the Grassmann graph whose vertex set is formed by all k k -dimensional subspaces of an n n -dimensional vector space V V over the finite field F q F_{q} consisting of q q elements. We discuss its subgraph Π ​ ( n , k ) q \Pi(n,k)_{q} formed by projective codes. We show that there are precisely two types of maximal cliques in Π ​ ( n , k ) q \Pi(n,k)_{q} : stars and tops. We give a complete description of stars, i.e., maximal cliques consisting of all k k -dimensional projective codes containing a certain ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -dimensional subspace of V V .

Introduction

Grassmann graphs, which have already been discussed in a number of books and papers (see, e.g.

[
8
,
9
]
), are important for many reasons, for instance, they are classical examples of distance regular graphs (for more details
see
[
2
]
).

The Grassmann graph
\Gamma_{k}(V)
is the simple graph whose vertex set
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(V)
is formed by
k

n
-dimensional vector space
V
over the
q
-element field

[n,k]_{q}
. Two distinct codes are connected by an edge if they have the maximal possible number of common codewords, i.e. their intersection is
(k-1)
-dimensional. We only deal with the case

since the Grassmann graph is complete for
k=1
and
k=n-1
.

This paper concerns the restriction of the Grassmann graph to the set
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
of all projective codes

, i.e., linear codes such that columns in their generator matrices are non-zero and mutually non-proportional. They form the subgraph of the Grassmann graph denoted by
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
. We refer to
[
6
]
for the description of some properties of graphs of projective codes.
The subgraphs of the Grassmann graph formed by various types of linear
codes are considered, e.g. in

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
.
Maximal cliques in these graphs are investigated in
[
1
,
7
]
for example.

We direct our attention to maximal cliques of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
. They are intersections of maximal cliques of
\Gamma_{k}(V)
with the set
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
. There are exactly two types of maximal cliques in
\Gamma_{k}(V)
: stars
[S\rangle_{k}
(made up by all
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
containing a fixed
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace
S
) or tops (consisting of all
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
contained in a fixed
(k+1)
-dimensional subspace). Any non-empty intersection of a star or a top with the set
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
is a clique, but it need not be a maximal clique. If this intersection is a maximal clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
, then we call it a star or a top of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
, respectively. Here we provide the thorough description of stars of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
. We consider two cases of
S
separately. First we assume that a generator matrix for a
(k-1)
-dimensional code

does not contain zero columns (Section

[S\rangle_{k}
with the set

is a star of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
. Next we study intersections of stars
[S\rangle_{k}
with the set
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
such that generator matrices for

contain proportional columns. We specify the number of projective codes in such intersections. Finally, we indicate all parameters for generator matrix for
S

[S\rangle_{k}\cap\Pi(n,k)_{q}
be a maximal clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
(Theorems
1
and
3

\Pi(n,k)_{q}
which are stars and tops of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
simultaneously.
Examples of maximal cliques of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}

for different cases of

are also given.

2
Graph of projective codes

Let
F_{q}
be the finite field consisting of
q
elements and let
V=F_{q}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional vector space over
F_{q}
. Consider the Grassmannian
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(V)
consisting of all
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
.
The
Grassmann graph

\Gamma_{k}(V)
is the simple and connected graph whose vertex set is
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(V)
. Its two vertices comprise an edge if, and
only if, their intersection is
(k-1)
-dimensional.
The Grassmann graph is complete if
k=1
or
k=n-1
, and that is why we suppose that
1<k<n-1
throughout the paper.

One of the basic ideas of graph theory is that of a clique. A clique in
an undirected graph is a subset of the set of its vertices such that any two distinct
elements from this subset are joined by an edge. If a clique is not
properly contained in any clique, then it is said to be
maximal
.
Any maximal clique of
\Gamma_{k}(V)
is one of the following types:
the
star

[S\rangle_{k}
consisting of all
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
containing a fixed subspace
S\in{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}(V)
, or the
top

\langle U]_{k}
consisting of all
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
contained in a fixed subspace
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{k+1}(V)
.

Recall that
V
contains precisely

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{1}_{q}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{n-1}_{q}=[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}

1
-dimensional subspaces and the total number of elements of
\langle S]_{k}
is equal
[n-k+1]_{q}
.
The intersection of two distinct maximal cliques of the form
\langle S]_{k}
contains at most one vertex.
The intersection of
[S\rangle_{k}
and
\langle U]_{k}
is either empty or it contains precisely
q+1
elements. The latter is realized exactly if
the corresponding
(k-1)
-dimensional and
(k+1)
-dimensional subspaces
S,U
are incident.

Any element of
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(V)
is interpreted as a
linear code

[n,k]_{q}
and it is called
non-degenerate
if its generator matrix does not contain zero columns.
If columns in a generator matrix for a linear code are non-zero and mutually non-proportional, then this code is said to be
projective
.

Write
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
for the restriction of the Grassmann graph to the set
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
of all projective codes. This is the simple graph whose set of vertices is
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
. It was shown in
[
6
, Theorem 1]
that in the case when
q\geq\binom{n}{2}
, the graph
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
is connected.
Any clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
is a clique of
\Gamma_{k}(V)
since
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
is a subgraph of
\Gamma_{k}(V)
. And so any maximal clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
is
the intersection of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
with a maximal clique of
\Gamma_{k}(V)
. We denote intersections
[S\rangle_{k}\cap{\Pi}(n,k)_{q}
and
\langle U]_{k}\cap{\Pi}(n,k)_{q}
by
[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
and
\langle U]^{\Pi}_{k}
, respectively.
They both
can be empty or non-maximal cliques of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
, otherwise they are called
a star
or
a top
of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
, respectively.

Consider a
(k-1)
-dimensional linear code

Assume that two proportional columns in

Remark 1
.

It is obvious that if there exists a column

of

or if

is an empty set (there are proportional or even zero columns in any matrix obtained from

Proposition 1
.

If

(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of

-dimensional subspace

Assume that

such that

w_{1}\end{matrix}\right]

1.

a zero column and mutually non-proportional columns

Any

-dimensional subspace

Let

of

is a proper subset of
\langle U]^{\Pi}_{k}
.
Similarly, we can indicate an element of the set

which does not belong to

if there is a zero column in generator matrices for

. Namely, such code is generated by a matrix obtained from
\left[\begin{matrix}M\\
w_{1}\end{matrix}\right]
by adding the vector

to any two row vectors

of

if a coordinate of

in the zero column is different from all the remaining among the last

coordinates of

. Otherwise, that is when a coordinate of

in the zero column is the same as the one in columns

, where

, then the searched matrix is obtained from

by adding

-th row, where

∎

The above proposition implies the following:

Corollary 1
.

If
S
is a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
V

\dim\langle W\rangle=1
, then
[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
is not a maximal clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}

Lemma 1
.

Let
S
be a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
V
. If
\dim\langle W\rangle>2
, then
[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
is a star of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
.

Proof.

Assume that

\left|[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}\right|>2

[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
is not properly contained in any star of

[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}

∎

Of course, when

is a maximal clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
. As it was said before, such

is not contained in any other star of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
, but also in any top of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
. It follows from the fact that
the intersection of a star and a top of
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}(V)
contains no more than

Let

-dimensional subspace of

.

A clique
[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
is a star of
\Pi[n,k]_{q}
if, and only if,

is a star of

whose generator matrix is obtained from

Let

, where

, be the

-th column of

. Then a generator matrix for

Obviously, a

Suppose now that

[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}

exactly if a generator matrix for any projective code from

. Clearly, all such matrices generate the same projective code.

Thus we get
|[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}|\geqslant[2]_{q}(q-1)+1=q^{2}>q+1
for all
q\geqslant 2
. Therefore
[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
is a maximal clique of
\Pi(n,k)_{q}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

If
M^{\prime}
contains proportional columns, then

\left|[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}\right|=\left[\prod_{k_{j}\in L}(q-1)(q-2)\cdots(q-l(k_{j})+1)\right]q^{l(S^{\prime})-k+1},

where
L
denotes the set of
equivalence classes of the columns of
M
such that
l(k_{j})>1
.

Proof.

The only
k
-dimensional projective codes containing
S
are generated by
\left[\begin{matrix}M\\
w_{i}\end{matrix}\right]
, where the coordinate of
w_{i}
in
k_{z}
is any non-zero element of
F_{q}
and removing the zero column from
w_{i}
gives
w_{i}^{\prime}
. Hence the cardinality of
[S\rangle^{\Pi}_{k}
is equal to the number of
k
-dimensional projective codes containing
S^{\prime}
multiplied by
q-1
. By the same method as in the proof of Lemma
3
we can show that the number of such codes containing
S^{\prime}
is

\frac{\left[\prod_{k_{j}\in L}(q-1)(q-2)\cdots(q-l(k_{j})+1)\right]q^{l(S^{\prime})-k+1}}{(q-1)},

which yields the desired equation.
∎

q=3

q\geqslant 4

"\Leftarrow"

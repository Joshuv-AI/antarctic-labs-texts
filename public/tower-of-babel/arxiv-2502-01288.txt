Abstract
. Let S F ​ ( t ) = π − 1 ​ arg ⁡ L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 + i ​ t , F ) S_{F}(t)=\pi^{-1}\arg L(1/2+it,F) , where F F is a Hecke–Maass cusp form for SL 3 ​ ( ℤ ) \rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) in the generic position with the spectral parameter ν F = ( ν F , 1 , ν F , 2 , ν F , 3 ) \nu_{F}=\big(\nu_{F,1},\nu_{F,2},\nu_{F,3}\big) and the Langlands parameter μ F = ( μ F , 1 , μ F , 2 , μ F , 3 ) \mu_{F}=\big(\mu_{F,1},\mu_{F,2},\mu_{F,3}\big) . 1.
Introduction

It is a well-established fact that the function

S(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)
is associated with
the number of non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
up to the height
t>0
.
The argument is defined by continuous variation along the horizontal
segment from
\infty+it
to
1/2+it
, commencing at
\infty+it
with the value
0
.
The intricate behavior of
S(t)
represents a highly intriguing topic and
has been explored by numerous number theorists
(see, e.g.,
[
1
,
6
,
12
,
22
]
).

In 1940s, Selberg
[
19
,
20
]
examined the distribution of
S(t)

and demonstrated that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}S(t)^{2n}\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}(\log\log T)^{n}+O\big((\log\log T)^{n-1/2}\big),

which implies that, in an average sense,
|S(t)|
has an order of magnitude
\sqrt{\log\log T}
.
Additionally, for the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
with primitive
\chi
,
Selberg
[
21
]
studied the behavior of
S(t,\chi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\big)

and obtained, for prime modulus
q
and any fixed
t>0
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\,\,\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}S(t,\chi)^{2n}=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}(\log\log q)^{n}+O\big((\log\log q)^{n-1/2}\big),

where
\star
indicates that the summation runs over the primitive characters
\chi(\bmod\,q)
.
It should be noted that the counterparts of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)
for the odd-order moments
can also be derived by making minor adjustments to Selberg’s approach.

For the
\rm GL_{2}
family of
L
-functions,
Hejhal–Luo
[
8
]
first considered the generalization of (
1.2
)
to the case of eigenfunctions
\psi
of the hyperbolic Laplacian
\Delta
for the full modular group.
More precisely, let
L(s,\psi)
be the standard Hecke–Maass
L
-function associated to
\psi

and define

\displaystyle S(t,\psi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,\psi\big).

They established the analogue of (
1.1
) when
\psi
is an Eisenstain series.
And when
\psi
is the Hecke–Maass cusp form
u_{j}
of
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with the first Fourier
coefficient
\nu_{j}(1)

and Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{4}+t_{j}^{2}
(
t_{j}\geq 0
),
under Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH),
they showed that for fixed
t,\beta>0
and any large parameter
T>0
,

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta(t_{j}-T)^{2}}|\nu_{j}(1)|^{2}S(t,u_{j})^{2n}=\frac{2\pi^{-3/2}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}T(\log\log T)^{n}+O_{t,n,\beta}\big(T(\log\log T)^{n-1/2}\big).\end{split}

Recently, Sun–Wang
[
17
]
reestablished this asymptotic formula by means of the zero-density
estimate of weighted type instead of assuming GRH.
Moreover, for the holomorphic case,
Liu–Shim
[
14
]
and Sun–Wang
[
16
]
successively obtained unconditional analogues
in the weight aspect and in the level aspect, respectively.

For the
\rm GL_{3}
family of
L
-functions, Liu–Liu
[
13
]
explored the average behavior
of the analogue function

\displaystyle S_{F}(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L(1/2+it,F)

defined for Hecke–Maass cusp forms
F
for
\rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
.
To state the result, we need to introduce some notations.
Let
F
run over an orthogonal basis of Hecke–Maass cusp forms for
\rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
.
For each
F
, we let
\mu_{F}=\big(\mu_{F,1},\mu_{F,2},\mu_{F,3}\big)
be its Langlands parameter and have that

\mu_{F}
is a point in the region

\begin{split}\Lambda_{1/2}^{\prime}=\Bigg\{(\mu_{F,1},\mu_{F,2},\mu_{F,3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3},\,\,\,\begin{aligned} &\mu_{F,1}+\mu_{F,2}+\mu_{F,3}=0,\,\,|\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{F,j})|\leq\frac{1}{2},\,j=1,2,3,\\
&\{-\mu_{F,1},-\mu_{F,2},-\mu_{F,3}\}=\{\overline{\mu_{F,1}},\overline{\mu_{F,2}},\overline{\mu_{F,3}}\}\end{aligned}\Bigg\}\end{split}

in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.
Also, the cusp form
F
has the spectral parameter
\nu_{F}=\big(\nu_{F,1},\nu_{F,2},\nu_{F,3}\big)
,
where

(1.3)

\begin{split}\nu_{F,1}:=\frac{1}{3}&\big(\mu_{F,1}-\mu_{F,2}\big),\quad\nu_{F,2}:=\frac{1}{3}\big(\mu_{F,2}-\mu_{F,3}\big),\\
\nu_{F,3}:=&-\nu_{F,1}-\nu_{F,2}=\frac{1}{3}\big(\mu_{F,3}-\mu_{F,1}\big).\end{split}

And we have

(1.4)

\mu_{F,1}=2\nu_{F,1}+\nu_{F,2},\quad\mu_{F,2}=\nu_{F,2}-\nu_{F,1},\quad\mu_{F,3}=-\nu_{F,1}-2\nu_{F,2}.

We will use the coordinates
\mu
and
\nu
simultaneously.
Note that
\big(\nu_{F,1}-\frac{1}{3},\nu_{F,2}-\frac{1}{3}\big)
is the type of
F

as defined in Goldfeld’s book
[
7
]
.
Assume that
F
has the normalized Fourier coefficients
A_{F}(m,n)
with
A_{F}(1,1)=1
.
The
L
-function associated with
F
is defined by

L(s,F)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{F}(1,n)}{n^{s}},\quad\,\operatorname{Re}(s)>1,

and has the Euler product

\begin{split}L(s,F)&=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{A_{F}(1,p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{F}(p,1)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\right)^{-1}\\
&=\prod_{p}\big(1-\alpha_{F,1}(p)p^{-s}\big)^{-1}\big(1-\alpha_{F,2}(p)p^{-s}\big)^{-1}\big(1-\alpha_{F,3}(p)p^{-s}\big)^{-1}.\end{split}

Here we have

(1.5)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}\alpha_{F,1}(p)\alpha_{F,2}(p)\alpha_{F,3}(p)=1\\
\alpha_{F,1}(p)+\alpha_{F,2}(p)+\alpha_{F,3}(p)=A_{F}(1,p)\\
\alpha_{F,1}(p)\alpha_{F,2}(p)+\alpha_{F,2}(p)\alpha_{F,3}(p)+\alpha_{F,3}(p)\alpha_{F,1}(p)=A_{F}(p,1),\end{cases}\end{split}

and by Kim–Sarnak
[
10
]
that

(1.6)

\begin{split}|\alpha_{F,j}(p)|\leq p^{\frac{5}{14}},\quad\text{and}\quad|\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{F,j})|\leq\frac{5}{14},\quad\text{for}\,\,j=1,2,3.\end{split}

Note that
L(s,F)
has an analytic continuation to the whole complex plane and satisfies
the functional equation

\Lambda(s,F)=L(s,F)\prod_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s-\mu_{F,j})=L(1-s,\widetilde{F})\prod_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s+\mu_{F,j})=\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{F}),

where
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
and
the dual form of
F
is denoted by
\widetilde{F}
with the Langlands parameter

(-\mu_{F,1},-\mu_{F,2},-\mu_{F,3})
, the spectral parameter
\big(\nu_{F,2},\nu_{F,1},\nu_{F,3}\big)
and the coefficients
A_{\widetilde{F}}(m,n)=\overline{A_{F}(m,n)}=A_{F}(n,m)
.

Let
\mu_{0}=(\mu_{0,1},\mu_{0,2},\mu_{0,3})
and
\nu_{0}=(\nu_{0,1},\nu_{0,2},\nu_{0,3})
, and satisfy
the corresponding relations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).
We also assume

(1.7)

|\mu_{0,j}|\asymp|\nu_{0,j}|\asymp T:=\|\mu_{0}\|,\quad 1\leq j\leq 3,

where

\|\mu_{0}\|=\sqrt{|\mu_{0,1}|^{2}+|\mu_{0,2}|^{2}+|\mu_{0,3}|^{2}}.

Throughout this paper, we let
M\!=\!T^{1-\eta}
for some very small
0<\eta<1/2
.
Define a test function
h_{T,M}(\mu)
(depending on
\mu_{0}
) for
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
by

h_{T,M}(\mu):=P(\mu)^{2}\bigg(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\psi\bigg(\frac{w(\mu)-\mu_{0}}{M}\bigg)\bigg)^{2},

where

\psi(\mu)=\exp\left(\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}+\mu_{3}^{2}\right)

and

P(\mu)=\prod_{0\leq n\leq A}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\left(\nu_{j}-\frac{1}{3}(1+2n)\right)\left(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{3}(1+2n)\right)}{|\nu_{0,j}|^{2}}

for some fixed large
A>0
. Here

\mathcal{W}:=\left\{I,\;w_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&\\
&&-1\\
&1&\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;w_{3}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1&\\
-1&&\\
&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;w_{4}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1&\\
&&1\\
1&&\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;w_{5}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&1\\
1&&\\
&1&\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;w_{6}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&1\\
&-1&\\
1&&\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\}

is the Weyl group of
\rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.
The function
h_{T,M}(\mu)
localizes at a ball of radius
M
about
w(\mu_{0})
for each
w\in\mathcal{W}
.
Moreover, we define the spectral measure on the hyperplane
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=0
by

\mathrm{d}_{\text{spec}}\mu=\text{spec}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu,

where

\text{spec}(\mu):=\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(3\nu_{j}\tan\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\nu_{j}\right)\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{d}\mu=\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}=\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{3}=\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{3}.

By trivial estimate, we have

(1.8)

\mathcal{H}:=\frac{1}{192\pi^{5}}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(\mu)=0}h_{T,M}(\mu)\mathrm{d}_{\rm{spec}}\mu\asymp T^{3}M^{2}.

Fix any
t>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, under GRH, Liu and Liu
[
13
]
established that, for large positive
T
,

(1.9)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\nu_{F})}{L(1,\text{Ad}^{2}F)}S_{F}(t)^{2n}=\frac{9(2n)!}{4n!(2\pi)^{2n}}(\log\log T)^{n}+O_{t,n}\big((\log\log T)^{n-1/2}\big),\end{split}

where
L(1,\text{Ad}^{2}F)
denotes the adjoint square
L
-function of
F
.
In this paper, we are devoted to proving the asymptotic formula (
1.9
) without assuming the GRH.
We introduce the normalizing factor

\mathcal{N}_{F}:=\|F\|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\cos\left(\frac{3}{2}\pi\nu_{F,j}\right).

By Rankin–Selberg theory in combination with Stade’s formula (see e.g.,
Blomer
[
3
, Section 4]
and Li
[
11
, Theorem 2]
),
it is easy to see that

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{F}\asymp\mathop{\operatorname{Res}}_{s=1}L(s,F\times\widetilde{F})\ll\|\mu_{F}\|^{\varepsilon},\end{split}

where
L(s,F\times\widetilde{F})
is the Rankin–Selberg square of
F
and the implied constants depending at most on
\varepsilon
.
Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
t>0
be fixed and
T
be any large parameter, we have

(1.10)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}S_{F}(t)^{n}=C_{n}(\log\log T)^{n/2}+O_{t,n}\big((\log\log T)^{(n-1)/2}\big),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle C_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{n!}{(n/2)!(2\pi)^{n}},\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is even},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Moreover, we define the following probability measure

\mu_{TM}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\begin{split}\mu_{TM}(E):=\left(\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}\mathbf{1}_{E}\left(\frac{S_{F}(t)}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\right)\right)\bigg/\left(\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}\right),\end{split}

where
\mathbf{1}_{E}
is the characteristic function of a Borel measurable set
E
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Then for every
a<b
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{TM}([a,b])=\sqrt{\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\exp(-\pi^{2}\xi^{2})\mathrm{d}\xi,

uniformly with respect to
M
.

Remark 1
.

For
F
a Hecke–Maass cusp form of
\rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, on an average sense,
our result indicates that
|S_{F}(t)|
has order of magnitude
\sqrt{\log\log T}
under the assumption (
1.7
).
In addition, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, the ratios
S_{F}(t)/\sqrt{\log\log T}
converges in distribution
to a normal distribution of mean
0

and variance
(2\pi^{2})^{-1}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hecke relations

Firstly, let’s recall the Hecke relations (see Goldfeld
[
7
, pg. 168]
):

\begin{split}A_{F}(m_{1}m_{2},n_{1}n_{2})&=A_{F}(m_{1},n_{1})A_{F}(m_{2},n_{2}),\quad\text{if}\,\,(m_{1}n_{1},m_{2}n_{2})=1,\\
A_{F}(n,1)A_{F}(m_{1},m_{2})&=\sum_{d_{0}d_{1}d_{2}=n\atop d_{1}|m_{1},\,d_{2}|m_{2}}A_{F}\left(\frac{m_{1}d_{0}}{d_{1}},\frac{m_{2}d_{1}}{d_{2}}\right),\\
A_{F}(1,n)A_{F}(m_{1},m_{2})&=\sum_{d_{0}d_{1}d_{2}=n\atop d_{1}|m_{1},\,d_{2}|m_{2}}A_{F}\left(\frac{m_{1}d_{2}}{d_{1}},\frac{m_{2}d_{0}}{d_{2}}\right),\\
A_{F}(n_{1},1)A_{F}(1,n_{2})&=\sum_{d|(n_{1},n_{2})}A_{F}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{d},\frac{n_{2}}{d}\right).\end{split}

Then we arrive at the following useful result, which was presented by Liu–Liu in
[
13
, Proposition 5.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each prime
p
and each integer
n\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}A_{F}(1,p)^{n}=\sum_{k,\ell\geq 0\atop k+\ell\leq n}a_{k,\ell}(n)A_{F}(p^{k},p^{\ell})\end{split}

for some integers
a_{k,\ell}(n)\geq 0
. Furthermore,
a_{k,\ell}(n)\neq 0
if and only if
(k,\ell)
belongs to

\begin{split}E_{n}:=\big\{(n_{2}-n_{1},n_{3}-n_{2})\,|\,n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},n_{3}\geq n_{2}\geq n_{1}\geq 0,n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n\big\}.\end{split}

where
\vartheta=7/64
is the best result towards the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture for
Fourier coefficients of

.

2.3.
The zero-density estimate

We will need the following weighted version of zero-density estimate
in the spectral aspect for the
L
-functions associated to
\rm GL_{3}
Hecke–Maass cusp forms
which was established by Sun–Wang
[
18

.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\sigma>0
and
H>0
, we let

\begin{split}N(\sigma,H;F):=\#\big\{\rho=1/2+\beta+i\gamma:L(\rho,F)=0,\beta>\sigma,|\gamma|<H\big\}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}N(\sigma,H):=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}N(\sigma,H;F).\end{split}

Let
2/\log T<\sigma<1/2
. For some sufficiently small
\delta_{1},\,\theta>0
, we have

\begin{split}N(\sigma,H)\ll HT^{-\theta\sigma}\log T,\end{split}

uniformly in
3/\log T<H<T^{\delta_{1}}
.

3.
An approximation of
S_{F}(t)

In this section we will prove several lemmas and derive an approximation of
S_{F}(t)
.
We denote by
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
a typical zero of
L(s,F)
inside the critical strip, i.e.,
0<\beta<1
.
For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we have the Dirichlet series expansion for the logarithmic derivative of
L(s,F)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,F)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)C_{F}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\Lambda(n)
denotes the von Mangoldt function, and

(3.2)

\displaystyle C_{F}(n)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{F,1}(p)^{k}+\alpha_{F,2}(p)^{k}+\alpha_{F,3}(p)^{k},\,&\text{if}\,\,n=p^{k}\,\,\text{for a prime}\,\,p,\\
0,\,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x>1
. For
s\neq\rho
, and
s\neq-2m+\mu_{F,j}
, (
m\geq 0
,
j=1,2,3
), we have the following identity

\begin{split}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,F)=&-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}(1-x^{\rho-s})^{2}}{(\rho-s)^{3}}\\
&-\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-2m-s+\mu_{F,j}}(1-x^{-2m-s+\mu_{F,j}})^{2}}{(-2m-s+\mu_{F,j})^{3}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x}(n)=\begin{cases}\Lambda(n),\,&\textit{if }\,\,n\leq x,\\
\Lambda(n)\frac{\log^{2}(x^{3}/n)-2\log^{2}(x^{2}/n)}{2\log^{2}x},\,&\textit{if }\,\,x\leq n\leq x^{2},\\
\Lambda(n)\frac{\log^{2}(x^{3}/n)}{2\log^{2}x},\,&\textit{if }\,\,x^{2}\leq n\leq x^{3},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,\,n\geq x^{3}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the discontinuous integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{y^{s}}{s^{3}}\mathrm{d}s=\begin{cases}\frac{\log^{2}y}{2},\,&\textit{if }\,\,y\geq 1,\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,\,0<y\leq 1\end{cases}

for
\alpha>0
.
It follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle-\log^{2}x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{x^{v}(1-x^{v})^{2}}{v^{3}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s+v,F)\mathrm{d}v,

where
\alpha=\max\{2,1+\operatorname{Re}(s)\}
. By moving the line of integration all way to the left, we
pick up the residues at
v=0
,
v=\rho-s
and
v=-2m-s+\mu_{F,j}
(
m\geq 0
,
j=1,2,3
), and deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{x^{v}(1-x^{v})^{2}}{v^{3}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s+v,F)\mathrm{d}v\\
=&\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,F)\log^{2}x+\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}(1-x^{\rho-s})^{2}}{(\rho-s)^{3}}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-2m-s+\mu_{F,j}}(1-x^{-2m-s+\mu_{F,j}})^{2}}{(-2m-s+\mu_{F,j})^{3}}.\end{split}

Thus we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
s=\sigma+it
,
s^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}+it^{\prime}
such that
1/2\leq\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\leq 2
,
s\neq\rho
and
s^{\prime}\neq\rho
,
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,F)-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s^{\prime},F)\right)=\operatorname{Im}\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-\rho}\right)+O(1),

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,F)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\log(|t|+M_{F})),

where

\begin{split}M_{F}:=2+\max_{1\leq j\leq 3}|\mu_{F,j}|.\end{split}

Proof.

By the Hadamard factorization theorem of the entire function
\Lambda(s,F)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,F)=b_{F}+\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{s-\mu_{F,j}}{2}\right)+\frac{3}{2}\log\pi,

for some
b_{F}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}(b_{F})=-\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}
(see
[
9
, Proposition 5.7 (3)]
).
Note that for
z\not\in-\mathbb{N}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k-1+z}\right),

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant.
Using (
3.4
), we get that for
\frac{1}{4}\leq u
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(u+iv)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{v}{(k+u-1)^{2}+v^{2}}\ll 1.

Combined with the asymptotic formula of the logarithmic derivative of
\Gamma(z)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=\log z-\frac{1}{2z}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\right),\quad\text{for}\,\,|\arg z|\leq\pi-\varepsilon,

we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Let
x\geq 4
. We define

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{x,F}=\sigma_{x,F,t}:=\frac{1}{2}+2\max_{\rho}\left\{\left|\beta-\frac{1}{2}\right|,\frac{5}{\log x}\right\},

where
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
runs through the zeros of
L(s,F)
for which

\displaystyle|t-\gamma|\leq\frac{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}{\log x}.

The following lemma is to display the dependence of
\sigma_{x}
as needed.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x\geq 4
. For
\sigma\geq\sigma_{x}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)=&-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)\\
&+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\log(|t|+M_{F})\right),\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}=O\left(\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\big(\log(|t|+M_{F})\big).

Proof.

By (
3.3
), we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\log(|t|+M_{F})).

Moreover, if
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
is zero of
L(s,F)
, then
(1-\beta)+i\gamma
is also a zero of
L(s,F)
.
Thus we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-(1-\beta)}{(\sigma_{x}-(1-\beta))^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\right)\\
=&\left(\sigma_{x}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{\rho}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}{((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2})((\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2})}.\end{split}

If
|\beta-1/2|\leq\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{2}
, then

\begin{split}(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}&\geq\frac{1}{2}\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}+(\beta-1/2)^{2}\big)\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}\big).\end{split}

Thus,

(3.8)

\displaystyle(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\big((\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big).

If
|\beta-1/2|>\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{2}
, then by (
3.5
) and (
3
) we have

\displaystyle|t-\gamma|>\frac{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}{\log x}>3|\beta-1/2|.

Thus,

(3.9)

\begin{split}&(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\\
&=\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}+(\beta-1/2)^{2}\big)+(t-\gamma)^{2}-2(\beta-1/2)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}\big)+\frac{7}{9}(t-\gamma)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\big((\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big).\end{split}

Combining (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4}(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2})}.

Using this bound and (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2})}\leq\frac{4}{\sigma_{x}-1/2}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\right|+O\left(\frac{\log(|t|+M_{F})}{\sigma_{x}-1/2}\right).

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
and the bound (
1.6
), we have

(3.11)

\begin{split}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)=&-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}+\frac{w_{F}(x,\sigma,t)}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-\sigma})^{2}}{\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}+O\left(\frac{x^{5/14-\sigma}}{\log^{2}x}\right),\end{split}

with
|w_{F}(x,\sigma,t)|\leq 1
.
Next we claim that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-\sigma})^{2}}{\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}\leq 2\log x\frac{x^{1/4-\sigma/2}}{(\beta-\sigma_{x})^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}.

If
\beta\leq\frac{\sigma_{x}+1/2}{2}
, then we have

\begin{split}\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-\sigma})^{2}}{\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}&\leq\frac{4x^{1/4-\sigma/2}}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)}\\
&\leq\frac{8}{\sigma_{x}-1/2}\frac{x^{1/4-\sigma/2}}{\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)}\\
&\leq\frac{4}{5}\log x\frac{x^{1/4-\sigma/2}}{\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)}.\end{split}

If
\beta>\frac{\sigma_{x}+1/2}{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle|t-\gamma|>\frac{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}{\log x}>3|\beta-1/2|\geq 3|\beta-\sigma_{x}|.

Thus,
(t-\gamma)^{2}>\frac{8}{9}\big((\beta-\sigma_{x})^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)
.
Hence

\begin{split}\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-\sigma})^{2}}{\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}&\leq\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-1/2})^{2}}{|t-\gamma|(t-\gamma)^{2}}\leq\frac{\log x}{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}\frac{9}{8}\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-1/2})^{2}}{(\beta-\sigma_{x})^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\\
&=\frac{9}{8}(\log x)(1+x^{-(\beta-1/2)})^{2}\frac{x^{1/2-\sigma}}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{9}{8}(\log x)(1+e^{-5})^{2}\frac{x^{1/2-\sigma}}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\\
&<2(\log x)\frac{x^{1/2-\sigma}}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}.\end{split}

Using (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\beta-\sigma}(1+x^{\beta-\sigma})^{2}}{\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}\\
\leq&\,2\log x\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{1/4-\sigma/2}}{(\beta-\sigma_{x})^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\\
\leq&\,8\log x\frac{x^{1/4-\sigma/2}}{\sigma_{x}-1/2}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\right|+O\left(\frac{(\log x)x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\log(|t|+M_{F})}{\sigma_{x}-1/2}\right)\\
\leq&\,\frac{4}{5}(\log x)^{2}x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\right|+O\left((\log x)^{2}x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\log\big(|t|+M_{F}\big)\right).\end{split}

Inserting this bound into (
3.11
), we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)\leq&-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}+\frac{4}{5}w^{\prime}_{F}(x,\sigma,t)x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\\
&+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\log(|t|+M_{F})\right),\end{split}

with
|w^{\prime}_{F}(x,\sigma,t)|\leq 1
.
By taking
\sigma=\sigma_{x}
,

\begin{split}&\left(1-\frac{4}{5}w^{\prime}_{F}(x,\sigma_{x},t)x^{1/4-\sigma_{x}/2}\right)\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\\
=&\,O\left(\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma_{x}/2}\log(|t|+M_{F})\right).\end{split}

Since
\left|1-\frac{4}{5}w^{\prime}_{F}(x,\sigma_{x},t)x^{1/4-\sigma_{x}/2}\right|\geq 1-\left|\frac{4}{5}w^{\prime}_{F}(x,\sigma_{x},t)x^{1/4-\sigma_{x}/2}\right|\geq\frac{1}{5}
,
we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)=O\left(\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma_{x}/2}\log(|t|+M_{F})\right).

Inserting (
3.14
) into (
3.13
) and (
3.10
),
respectively, we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

The following theorem is to provide an approximation of

Theorem 3.4
.

For
t\neq 0
, and
x\geq 4
, we have

\begin{split}S_{F}(t)=&\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}\log n}+O\left((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)\\
&+O\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\log(|t|+M_{F})\big),\end{split}

where
\sigma_{x}
is defined in (
3.5
).

Proof.

By the definition of
S_{F}(t)
, we have

\begin{split}\pi S_{F}(t)=&-\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\operatorname{Im}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
=&-\int_{\sigma_{x}}^{\infty}\operatorname{Im}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)\mathrm{d}\sigma-(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\operatorname{Im}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\\
&+\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
:=&\,J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.\end{split}

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{split}J_{1}=&-\int_{\sigma_{x}}^{\infty}\operatorname{Im}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
=&\int_{\sigma_{x}}^{\infty}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}\mathrm{d}\sigma+O\left(\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\int_{\sigma_{x}}^{\infty}x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\\
&+O\left(\log(|t|+M_{F})\int_{\sigma_{x}}^{\infty}x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\\
=&\operatorname{Im}\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}\log n}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\left(\frac{\log(|t|+M_{F})}{\log x}\right).\end{split}

Taking
\sigma=\sigma_{x}
in Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{split}J_{2}\leq&\,(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)\right|\\
\ll&\,(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|+(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\log(|t|+M_{F}).\end{split}

Using Lemma
3.2
, we get

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,F)-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,F)\right)\\
=&\sum_{\rho}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-\sigma)(\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta)(t-\gamma)}{\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)}+O(1).\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}|J_{3}|\leq&\sum_{\rho}\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-\sigma)|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta||t-\gamma|}{\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)\big((\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big)}\mathrm{d}\sigma+O(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\\
\leq&\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta||t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma+O(\sigma_{x}-1/2).\end{split}

If
|\beta-1/2|\leq\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}-1/2)
, then for
1/2\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{x}
,

\begin{split}|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta|&=|(\sigma-1/2)+(\sigma_{x}-1/2)-2(\beta-1/2)|\\
&\leq|\sigma-1/2|+|\sigma_{x}-1/2|+2|\beta-1/2|\leq 3(\sigma_{x}-1/2).\end{split}

Thus,

(3.15)

\begin{split}\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta||t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma&\leq 3(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&\leq 10(\sigma_{x}-1/2).\end{split}

If
|\beta-1/2|>\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}-1/2)
, then

\displaystyle|t-\gamma|>\frac{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}{\log x}>3|\beta-1/2|

and for
1/2\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{x}
,

\begin{split}|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta|\leq|\sigma-1/2|+|\sigma_{x}-1/2|+2|\beta-1/2|\leq 6|\beta-1/2|.\end{split}

Thus,

(3.16)

\begin{split}\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta||t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma&<\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta|}{|t-\gamma|}\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&\leq\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{6|\beta-1/2|}{3|\beta-1/2|}\mathrm{d}\sigma=2(\sigma_{x}-1/2).\end{split}

By the bounds (
3.15
)–(
3.16
) and Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\begin{split}|J_{3}|\leq&10(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\\
=&O\left((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\log(|t|+M_{F})\big).\end{split}

Thus we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Thus,

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{F}(t)=&\,\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{A_{F}(1,p)(\Lambda_{x}(p)-\Lambda(p))}{p^{1/2+it}\log p}+\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{k=2}^{7}\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})}{p^{k(1/2+it)}\log p^{k}}\\
&+\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{k=1}^{7}\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})(p^{k(1/2-\sigma_{x})}-1)}{p^{k(1/2+it)}\log p^{k}}\\
&+O\left((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\sum_{k=1}^{7}\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})}{p^{k(\sigma_{x}+it)}}\right|\right)\\
&+O\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\log(|t|+M_{F})\big)+O(1).\end{split}

Note that for
1/2\leq a\leq\sigma_{x}
and
k\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})}{p^{k(a+it)}}\right|&=kx^{k(a-1/2)}\left|\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{k(1/2-\sigma)}\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})\log(xp)}{p^{k(\sigma+it)}}\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|\\
&\leq kx^{k(\sigma_{x}-1/2)}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{k(1/2-\sigma)}\left|\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})\log(xp)}{p^{k(\sigma+it)}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})(p^{k(1/2-\sigma_{x})}-1)}{p^{k(1/2+it)}\log p^{k}}\right|=\left|\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})}{p^{k(a+it)}}\mathrm{d}a\right|\\
&\leq k(\sigma_{x}-1/2)x^{k(\sigma_{x}-1/2)}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{k(1/2-\sigma)}\left|\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})\log(xp)}{p^{k(\sigma+it)}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})}{p^{k(\sigma_{x}+it)}}\right|\leq kx^{k(\sigma_{x}-1/2)}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{k(1/2-\sigma)}\left|\sum_{p^{k}\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p^{k})\Lambda_{x}(p^{k})\log(xp)}{p^{k(\sigma+it)}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{split}

Thus we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
t>0
be fixed and
T
be any large parameter.
For
x=T^{\delta/3}
with sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we have

(4.2)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}\mathcal{M}_{F}(t)^{n}=C_{n}(\log\log T)^{\frac{n}{2}}+O_{t,n}\big((\log\log T)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\big)\end{split}

and

(4.3)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}|\mathcal{R}_{F}(t)|^{2n}=O_{t,n}(1),\end{split}

where the

and
n
,
C_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle C_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{n!}{(n/2)!(2\pi)^{n}},\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is even},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{F}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{F}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\left(\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{A_{F}(1,p)}{p^{1/2+it}}-\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{\overline{A_{F}(1,p)}}{p^{1/2-it}}\right).

Set
x=T^{\delta/3}
for a suitably small
\delta>0
. Thus we have that the general term of

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{F}(t)^{n}=\frac{(-i)^{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\sum_{p\leq T^{\delta}}\frac{A_{F}(1,p)}{p^{1/2+it}}-\sum_{p\leq T^{\delta}}\frac{\overline{A_{F}(1,p)}}{p^{1/2-it}}\right)^{n}

has the form

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{A_{F}(1,p_{1})^{m(p_{1})}\overline{A_{F}(1,p_{1})^{n(p_{1})}}}{p_{1}^{m(p_{1})(1/2+it)}(-1)^{n(p_{1})}p_{1}^{n(p_{1})(1/2-it)}}\times\cdots\times\frac{A_{F}(1,p_{r})^{m(p_{r})}\overline{A_{F}(1,p_{r})^{n(p_{r})}}}{p_{r}^{m(p_{r})(1/2+it)}(-1)^{n(p_{r})}p_{r}^{n(p_{r})(1/2-it)}},

where
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{r}\leq T^{\delta}
,
m(p_{j})+n(p_{j})\geq 1
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{r}(m(p_{j})+n(p_{j}))=n
.

Now we discuss the contribution from the general term (
4.4
) in the following three cases.

Case (I):

In the general term (
4.4
),
m(p_{j_{0}})\not\equiv n(p_{j_{0}})(\bmod\,3)
for some
j_{0}
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\begin{split}A_{F}(1,p_{j_{0}})^{m(p_{j_{0}})}=\sum_{k,\ell\geq 0\atop k+\ell\leq m(p_{j_{0}})}a_{k,\ell}(m(p_{j_{0}}))A_{F}(p_{j_{0}}^{k},p_{j_{0}}^{\ell})\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\overline{A_{F}(1,p_{j_{0}})^{n(p_{j_{0}})}}=\sum_{k,\ell\geq 0\atop k+\ell\leq n(p_{j_{0}})}a_{k,\ell}(n(p_{j_{0}}))\overline{A_{F}(p_{j_{0}}^{k},p_{j_{0}}^{\ell})}.\end{split}

Then we can observe that there does not exist
(k,\ell)
such that
a_{k,\ell}(m(p_{j_{0}}))\neq 0

and
a_{k,\ell}(n(p_{j_{0}}))\neq 0
. Otherwise, by Lemma
2.1
,
there would exist
(k,\ell)\in E_{m(p_{j_{0}})}\cap E_{n(p_{j_{0}})}
and integers

, such that

\begin{split}m(p_{j_{0}})&=m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3},\\
n(p_{j_{0}})&=n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3},\\
k&=-m_{1}+m_{2}=-n_{1}+n_{2},\\
\ell&=-m_{2}+m_{3}=-n_{2}+n_{3}.\end{split}

There equations would further lead to

\begin{split}m(p_{j_{0}})-n(p_{j_{0}})&=(m_{1}-n_{1})+(m_{2}-n_{2})+(m_{3}-n_{3})\\
&=3(m_{2}-n_{2})\equiv 0(\bmod\,3),\end{split}

which contradicts the assumption for Case (I).

With the above observation and the Hecke relations, the numerator of this general term (
4.4
)
can be written as a sum whose terms are all of the form
A_{F}(m_{1},n_{1})\overline{A_{F}(m_{2},n_{2})}
where
(m_{1},n_{1})\neq(m_{2},n_{2})
.
Since for these terms
\delta_{n_{1},m_{1}}\delta_{n_{2},m_{2}}=0
,
we see from Theorem
2.2
and the estimate (

4.4
) to the moment (

\begin{split}&\ll_{n}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{r}\leq T^{\delta}}p_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}(m(p_{1})+n(p_{1}))}\cdots p_{r}^{-\frac{1}{2}(m(p_{r})+n(p_{r}))}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left((T^{1+2n\delta})^{\varepsilon}\big(T^{1+n\delta}+T^{3}+T^{1+2n\delta\vartheta}M^{2}\big)\right)\\

which is negligible upon taking

Case (II):

In the general term (
4.4
),
m(p_{j})\equiv n(p_{j})(\bmod\,3)
for all
j
,
and
m(p_{j_{0}})\geq 2
or

j_{0}

m(p_{j})+n(p_{j})\geq 2
for all

m(p_{j_{0}})+n(p_{j_{0}})\geq 3
for some

so

By applying Lemma

to the numerator of this general term (

Using Theorem

Using the classical result

It remains to discuss the following: In the general term (

.
Clearly

The contribution from these terms to (

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{F}\frac{h_{T,M}(\mu_{F})}{\mathcal{N}_{F}}\binom{2m}{m}(m!)(m!)\frac{(-1)^{m}(-i)^{2m}}{(2\pi)^{2m}}\\
&\qquad\sum_{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{m}\leq T^{\delta}}\frac{A_{F}(1,p_{1})\overline{A_{F}(1,p_{1})}\cdots A_{F}(1,p_{m})\overline{A_{F}(1,p_{m})}}{p_{1}\cdots p_{m}}.\end{split}

Applying Theorem

.

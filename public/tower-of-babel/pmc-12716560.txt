Experiences and outcomes of peer navigation and support interventions for adolescents on HIV treatment in sub-Saharan Africa: protocol for a qualitative evidence synthesis
Authors: Brian van Wyk, Bernard Nhlanhla Mabuza, Charné Petinger
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionAdolescents living with HIV (ALHIV) on antiretroviral therapy (ART) globally report lower rates of viral suppression because of challenges to remain adherent and engage in HIV treatment. Psychosocial support is critical to meet the unique needs of ALHIV. Peer navigation has been found to be effective in supporting adults in ART adherence, but the evidence for adolescents is scant. Given the large proportion of adolescents on ART in sub-Saharan Africa, peers hold promise to deliver psychosocial support. The aim of this qualitative evidence synthesis is to review qualitative and mixed-methods studies describing experiences of peer navigation and support interventions for ALHIV on ART in sub-Saharan Africa.Methods and analysisThe population–concept–context configuration will be applied to identify suitable studies on ALHIV and on ART (population) and experiences of peer navigation and support (concept) and conducted in sub-Saharan African countries (context). The review will only include studies published in the English language. The following databases will be searched: PubMed, Wiley Library Online, EbscoHost (PsycArticles), MEDLINE, Scopus, CINAHL, WHO database and Google Scholar, for eligible qualitative studies published from January 2015 (to ensure relevance) to November 2025. Two-stage screening will be done by two reviewers independently. Extracted data will be thematically analysed with Atlas.Ti (v25) software. A narrative synthesis will be conducted to describe the reported outcomes, experiences and challenges with receiving and delivery of peer navigation and support interventions in sub-Saharan Africa. The review will adhere to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses and Enhancing Transparency in Reporting the Synthesis of Qualitative Research reporting guidelines.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval is not required for this qualitative evidence synthesis. The findings will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal and disseminated to via conference proceedings.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024541951.

Introduction

Background

Global statistics indicate that about 1.75 million adolescents from the age of 10–19 years live with HIV, 90% of whom reside in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA).1 The 95-95-95 targets to end the HIV/AIDS pandemic by 2030 were revised by the WHO to ensure that 95% of the population know their HIV status, and 95% are on treatment, and of these, 95% are virally suppressed.2 Current global reports indicate that over 90% of adults achieved viral suppression, whereas the rates of viral suppression for adolescents living with HIV (ALHIV) stand at 64%.3 Furthermore, there is low viral load (VL) suppression and antiretroviral therapy (ART) adherence on ALHIV when compared with the adult population, particularly in SSA.4 Mabaso et al5 argued that ALHIV encounter physical, biological and structural difficulties as a result of residing in vulnerable populations.5 Hence, ALHIV tend to experience higher risk of poor mental health, which impacts their ability to sustain optimum treatment adherence and remain engaged in care, and in turn, leads to lower rates of viral suppression.6

Moreover, interventions are usually put in place to make sure that ALHIV receive support in achieving VL suppression, ART adherence and mental well-being. Such interventions that are put in place towards psychological well-being support, adherence and retention in care include peer-led interventions7 such as peer navigation and support interventions. Peer navigation and support is a form of patient navigation that involves assisting clients with navigating a complex health system and providing psychosocial support and guidance in managing mental well-being.8 However, even though there are positive outcomes on recent reviews on the effectiveness of peer support and support interventions for adults,9 there is still no clear link of improved adherence, retention in care or mental health outcomes on peer navigation and support programmes reviews for ALHIV in SSA.10

Peer navigators are even more effective because they act as role models to their clients on how to overcome barriers in the treatment journey as well as provide reliable and relevant information and strategies to assist with treatment adherence and long-term engagement in care.8 Several studies have reported on the positive outcomes of peer navigation programmes for ALHIV in SSA countries, including Zimbabwe, Zambia, Kenya, Tanzania, Eswatini and Nigeria.1116 Peer navigators have been shown to be efficacious in addressing issues of stigma while fostering greater agency assistance, resilience and independence among ALHIV.10 13 17 However, it is argued that peer navigation and support programmes are complex, that is, the mechanisms by which desired outcomes are triggered vary and are not clearly described—which in turn leads to variability in study outcomes and thereby requires further in-depth study.18 The inconclusive results from systematic reviews of peer support and peer navigation interventions for ALHIV and people living with HIV in general attest to the variable behavioural and contextual dynamics at play when implementing peer navigation and support interventions.6 19 This qualitative evidence synthesis aims to provide a contextual understanding of the implementation of peer navigation and support programmes for ALHIV in SSA and unpack the key mechanisms of the interventions that are necessary for positive outcomes. Although there are valuable review studies on peer navigation and support for people living with HIV, these reviews focus on adults,18 and where adolescents and youth are targeted,20 the review considers quantitative studies of effects only, with little description or explanation of how the intervention was delivered and how the mechanism of delivery contributed to the results reported (or the variability thereof). The proposed review will provide in-depth insights on how the outcomes (measures of effectiveness) of peer navigation and support interventions were derived and thereby inform the development of future interventions. In addition, the synthesis of qualitative studies on the experiences of peer navigation and support interventions will consolidate the perceived challenges of peer navigation and support interventions from ALHIV’s perspectives and across varied contexts and thereby guide the implementation in other settings and hopefully towards greater effectiveness of peer navigation and support interventions in promoting treatment outcomes in ALHIV. Thus, by focusing on implementation experiences and the perspectives of adolescents, peer navigators and healthcare workers, this review will generate novel insights into both the operational and psychosocial dynamics shaping intervention success. In doing so, it will contribute practice guidance for the adaptation and scale-up of peer navigation and support interventions to improve treatment outcomes among ALHIV in high-prevalence, resource-limited settings.

Methods and analysis

Study registration and reporting

This qualitative evidence synthesis protocol is registered with PROSPERO (Registration number CRD42024541951). This protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses for Protocols (PRISMA-P) reporting guidelines to ensure methodological transparency. The reporting of the completed review will adhere to both PRISMA 2020 and Enhancing Transparency in Reporting the Synthesis of Qualitative Research (ENTREQ) guidelines. PRISMA will be used to guide the transparent reporting of the review process, including search, selection and data management procedures, and ENTREQ will be applied to ensure comprehensive and transparent reporting of the qualitative synthesis methods and findings.21

Information sources

The following electronic databases will be used for the search: PubMed, Wiley Library Online, EbscoHost (PsycArticles, MEDLINE, Scopus and CINAHL) and Google Scholar. Grey literature identified from databases such as the WHO database and reference mining of the included studies will also be used.

Search strategy

To determine the most inclusive and effective search strings, the reviewers have used the PubMed database to identify MeSH terms, using a controlled vocabulary and possible search terms. An information specialist will be consulted to further develop the search string and adapt to various databases. The proposed search strings for this review are presented in box 1.

Proposed search strings

(“HIV” OR “AIDS” OR “antiretroviral therapy”) AND

(“ALHIV” OR “Adolescent” OR “teenagers” OR “young adults” OR “youth”) AND

(“Sub-Saharan Africa”) AND

(“Peer supporter” OR “peer navigator” OR “peer mentor”) AND

(“Peer navigation and support intervention” OR “Effectiveness” OR “Outcomes” OR “Experiences”)

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for this review are as follows: qualitative and mixed-methods studies, published from January 2015 (to ensure contemporary relevance) until November 2025, describe the experiences of peer navigation interventions (C, concept) for ALHIV (P, participants), conducted in SSA (C, context) and published in English. The population, concept and context tool will be used to define the research questions and search terms22 (table 1).

Exclusion criteria

The review will exclude the following studies: all types of reviews, solely quantitative studies, studies conducted outside of SSA, studies that do not focus on adolescents as main recipients of the intervention and studies reported in languages other than English. Studies published in languages other than English were excluded because the researchers are English-speaking and the assumption that most relevant work will be published in English since it is the predominant language of scientific publishing. All grey literature, such as unpublished reports, theses and conference abstracts, will be excluded from this review.

Study selection

Two reviewers will independently screen the titles and abstracts of potentially eligible studies. Full-text screening of eligible studies will be reviewed by two reviewers independently to ensure the accuracy of the included studies. In the case of disagreements on inclusion, the reviewers will discuss as a group and make a final decision. In quality checks, the reviewer will review all the included studies. The PRISMA diagram will be used as a reporting tool during the various phases of screening and selection of studies.

Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract the data on Covidence, after which the entire review team (BvW, BNM and CP) will review the extractions and reach consensus on the final extracted dataset. The review will solely extract contextual and methodological data through a developed and piloted data extraction form in order to match review specifications. We will extract descriptive characteristics such as the author’s name, date of publication, country, design, implementation setting, target population and outcomes. A synthesis from the data will be extracted from Atlas.Ti, which includes the source, data collection, ethical considerations, other research methods and the main themes.

Direct quotes that give an explanation or description of participants’ experiences to the intervention will be extracted as first-order data. The extracted quotes will then be coded and categorised into themes (and sub-themes, where appropriate). The review data will be extracted using the themes and sub-themes in the included studies, also known as code groups and codes in Atlas.Ti. The first study themes and sub-themes can be utilised as a reference point on the extraction of other studies data. The first step will be to name the code groups (theme), as they are from the first study findings. Therefore, comparing the second study code groups and codes to the first outline findings, then groups with similar names and meanings, will be kept unchanged. If the theme and sub-theme contain a similar meaning but have different naming, then the researcher will select the most appropriate theme or sub-theme name. This, in the case of a different theme/sub-theme naming, the researcher will split the theme into separate themes and assign a new and accurately context-reflecting name to reflect the theme meaning. Overall, the final structure of these steps will then represent the meta-synthesis, reflecting a thematic synthesis that is unified across all the reviewed papers, with a list of consolidated themes (code groups) and sub-themes (codes) with consistent naming throughout the extraction and analysis process.

Quality assessment

This review will use the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme tool of qualitative studies in assessing the selected and included studies.23 The Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool will also be utilised in assessing the included studies’ qualitative components, as the review also includes mixed-methods studies.24 The use of both tools will ensure that all the included studies are assessed with criteria that are appropriate to their methodological approach.

Assessment of confidence in review findings

The confidence in review findings will be evaluated using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation Confidence in Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative Research (GRADE CERQual).25 Two reviewers will independently assess and grade the review findings, followed by a collaborated final grading decision by all reviewers.

Data management, analysis and synthesis

Covidence will be used in every stage of the review process for searches, selection, data extraction and quality appraisal and narrative synthesis. Covidence will also be used in data management and screening of the included studies. Data will be analysed through thematic synthesis using Atlas.Ti (V25), which will guide the description of the review findings and relevant themes. Thematic synthesis is a review of findings that includes organising codes into descriptive themes followed by analyses of the interpreted data into analytic themes.26 Therefore, Atlas.Ti will be utilised for the review’s analysis, so as coding and development of themes. One reviewer will develop an analytic framework, whereafter the review team will discuss and refine. Where possible, context–intervention–mechanism–outcome (CIMO) configurations will be developed for sub-groups of studies that are sufficiently homogenous, as a second-order analysis.27 Denyer et al28 defined the CIMO configuration as an aide to identify how an intervention (I), applied in a certain context (C), triggers specific mechanisms (M) that lead to the outcomes (O) of interest.28

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval is not required for this qualitative evidence synthesis. Any and all deviations from the protocol will be transparently reported in the final review. The findings will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentation at a conference within 1 year of completion.

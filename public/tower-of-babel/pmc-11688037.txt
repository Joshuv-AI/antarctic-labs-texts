Shexiang Baoxin Pills alleviate doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity via the reactive oxygen species-mediated AKT/Bcl-2 pathway
Authors: Shudan Wang, Mingcai Chen, Yi Qian, Xiaohan Chen, Weifeng Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Shexiang Baoxin Pill (SBP) is a classical Chinese medicine that improves endothelial function and antioxidant and inflammatory responses. It may also alleviate doxorubicin (DOX)-induced cardiotoxicity. The aim of this study is to explore the potential influence and molecular mechanisms of SBP in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity using network pharmacology.Methods:We established control, SBP, DOX, and DOX + SBP groups to evaluate cell function using a Cell Counting Kit-8 assay, reactive oxygen species (ROS) measurement, cell cycle analysis, and apoptosis assessment. Network pharmacology was employed to predict potential targets and pathways of SBP in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity; the predictions were validated using protein blotting assays.Results:SBP (2.5 mg/L) significantly mitigated DOX-induced cardiotoxicity. DOX elevated ROS levels, induced phosphorylation of the AKT pathway, and altered the expression of apoptosis-related proteins Bcl-2 and Bax. SBP attenuated the impact of DOX on cardiomyocytes. Network pharmacology identified 10 candidate targets.Conclusion:SBP ameliorates DOX-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis by activating the ROS-mediated AKT/Bcl-2 signaling pathway.

1. Introduction

Doxorubicin (DOX) is an anthracycline-based chemotherapeutic agent extensively utilized to treat many malignant tumors[1]; however, its clinical application has been hampered by cardiotoxicity. The mechanism of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity is multifactorial, and most studies have shown that it is related to oxidative stress and cardiomyocyte apoptosis.[2–4] Dexrazoxane, a top-2β inhibitor, is the only drug approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for protection against DOX-induced cardiotoxicity.[5] Clinical use of this drug has been limited by its ability to reduce the sensitivity of cancer cells to chemotherapy and increase bone marrow suppression.[6,7] Therefore, there is a need to explore new approaches to attenuate DOX-induced myocardial injury.

Shexiang Baoxin Pills (SBP) originated from the Suhexiang Pill in the first Chinese patent medicine dictionary, Prescriptions People’s Welfare Pharmacy. It has been improved many times. It consists of 7 flavors of Moschus (Shexiang), extract of Ginseng Radix et Rhizoma (renshen), Bovis Calculus Artifactus (Rengong Niuhuang), Cinnamomi Cortex (Rou Gui), Styrax (Su Hexiang), Bufonis Venenum (Chan Su), and Borneolum Syntheticum (Bing Pian). This traditional Chinese herbal remedy lowers the incidence of cardiovascular adverse events and alleviates angina frequency and duration. In clinical studies, SBP has protected against DOX-induced cardiotoxicity; however, the mechanisms involved have not been investigated.

With the cross-fertilization of computer network technology and various disciplines, network pharmacology technology has entered traditional Chinese medicine and its compound prescriptions. Network pharmacology analyzes the interaction network among drugs, targets, and diseases holistically and systematically and selects specific signal nodes to analyze the interrelationship among drugs, targets, and diseases.[8] Utilizing the network pharmacology approach, we systematically analyzed the potential active ingredients and targets of SBP and predicted the mechanism of action of SBP in treating DOX-induced cardiotoxicity. Finally, we explored the mechanisms by which SBP alleviates DOX-induced cardiotoxicity, providing a basis for clinical implementation.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Collection and screening of target data for SBP

Data were retrieved from TCM Database@Taiwan (TCM@Taiwan; http://tcm.cmu.edu.tw/), the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database (https://www.tcmsp-e.com/#/database), BATMAN-TCM (http://bionet.ncpsb.org/batman-tcm/),[9] and other databases to obtain the compounds contained in the 7 herbs and their corresponding targets. The eligible compounds were identified in TCM@Taiwan according to Lipinski Rule of 5. The duplicates were removed to obtain the active ingredients of the whole SBP formula. The obtained active ingredients were subjected to target prediction in the BATMAN database, and the full-square target information was obtained by removing the duplicates.

2.2. Collection and screening of doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity target data

Information on targets of lung cancer DOX-induced cardiotoxicity was obtained from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). After selecting a relevance score > 1 and eliminating duplicates, 211 disease-validated targets were identified.

2.3. Construction and analysis of the drug–active ingredient–target network

Nodes represented the herbal components, active ingredients, and protein targets of SBP. We created an Excel table file and imported it into Cytoscape 3.9.1 with the filtering condition of degree > 1. Finally, the SBP drug–active ingredient–target network diagram was organized and clarified.

2.4. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction

The intersection of potential targets of SBP and targets of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity was taken to draw a Venn diagram. The 83 intersecting targets obtained above were imported into the STRING database, and Cytoscape 3.9.1 software was used to construct the PPI network graph and perform network topology analysis. The importance of each target gene in the network was determined according to the degree-centralities of the target gene in the network, and the main target proteins were revealed.

2.5. Pathway enrichment analysis

We used the R packages org.Hs.e.g..db, clusterProfiler, and enrichplot packages to analyze the target genes for GO versus KEGG enrichment analysis.

2.6. Cell culture and grouping

H9c2 (Procell, China) were cultured in complete medium (containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 0.1% high-sugar DMEM medium containing penicillin/streptomycin bis-antibodies) and placed in an incubator at 37 °C and 0.05% CO2 (v/v). Cells were pretreated with SBP (2.5 mg/L), and incubation was continued for 24 hours in the presence or absence of DOX (1 μM).

2.7. SBP and DOX preparation

Prepare a 500 mg/mL SBP (Hutchmed, China) stock solution based on previous research,[10] which was dispensed and stored at –80 °C. The 10 nM DOX (APExBIO, USA) stock solution was kept in portions at –80 °C. DOX was diluted to a working concentration of 1 μM with complete medium, and SBP was diluted with complete medium to reach a working concentration of 2.5 mg/L (DMSO content <0.1%) subsequently filtered through a 0.22 μm filter (Millipore, USA) to remove bacteria, each time ready to use.

2.8. Cell Counting Kit-8

Cell viability was evaluated utilizing the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Biosharp, China). Upon completion of cell stimulation, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was introduced into each well following the manufacturer’s instructions, and the incubation was prolonged for 2 hours. The absorbance at the wavelength of 450 nm was gauged using an enzyme labeling instrument (Thermo, USA), and the cell viability was calculated.

2.9. Cell cycle analysis

After completing the stimulation in the above groups, we performed fixed staining treatment according to the manufacturer’s requirements. Samples were processed on FACS Symphony A1 and analyzed using ModFit Lt software (Verity Software House, USA).

2.10. Apoptosis analysis

H9c2 cells were stimulated as previously described. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, apoptotic cells were detected using the Annexin V-FITC/PI Cell Apoptosis Detection Kit. Samples were processed on FACS Symphony A1 and analyzed using FlowJo X software (Tree Star, Ashland, OR).

2.11. Measurement of intracellular ROS

According to the manufacturer’s instructions, total ROS levels in the cells were measured using the fluorescent probe DCFH-DA (Beyotime, China) with a working concentration of 5 μM, and intracellular viable cells were measured using Hoechst 33258 (Beyotime, China) at a working concentration of 5 μM. Rosup group treated with 100 μm H2O2 for 30 minutes. Fluorescence signal intensity was measured using an inverted fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan).

2.12. Western blot

H9c2 cells were inoculated into 6-well plates in a particular proportion and treated accordingly. Cells were lysed with RIPA lysis solution (Solarbio, China) containing a mixture of protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Total protein concentration was detected and quantified with BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, China) and 5x Native Gel Sample Loading Buffer (NCM, China) and stored at −80 °C. Protein samples were separated by electrophoresis with 4 to 20% Super-PAGE™Bis-Tris Gels (YAMAY, China) and transferred to PVDF membranes. The membrane was blocked with Protein-Free Rapid Closure Solution (YAMAY, China) for 20 minutes and then incubated with primary antibodies on a shaking platform for 15 hours at 4 °C, including AKT and GAPDH (1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, USA), p-AKT (1:2000; Cell Signaling Technology, USA), BAX (1:1000; ABclonal, USA), Bcl-2 (1:10,000; Proteintech, China). After primary antibody incubation, the membranes were washed with TBST and incubated with HRP-labeled secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature. Proteins were imaged using an ImageQuant LAS 500 imager (General Electric, Japan).

2.13. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.2. The data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Dunnett multiple comparisons test and one-way analysis of variance were used for multiple comparisons between groups. P value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3.2. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis

Functional targets involving biological processes, cellular composition, and molecular functions were identified by GO enrichment analysis of the differential genes. Biological processes analysis showed that regulation of oxidative stress response was the most critical function, suggesting that SBP may attenuate the degree of oxidative stress in cardiomyocytes by reducing DOX-generated oxygen radicals, among other substances. Molecular functions analysis showed that protein serine/threonine/tyrosine kinase activity was the primary functional mode of SBP for DOX-induced cardiotoxicity (Fig. 2E). KEGG analysis reveals that phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (PKB/AKT) pathway is significantly associated with DOX-induced cardiotoxicity by SBP treatment (Fig. 2F).

3.3. Effect of activity of SBP on DOX-induced H9c2 cells

Cell survival in the DOX group was markedly lower than in the control group (P < .0001), indicating successful modeling (Fig. 3A). Cell survival was markedly elevated in the SBP group compared to the control group (P < .01). The cell survival rate in the SBP + DOX group was significantly higher than in the DOX group (P < .0001).

3.4. Effects of SBP and DOX on cell cycle progression in H9c2 cells

Cell cycle analysis revealed that in comparison to the control group, the G1 phase cell population significantly increased (P < .0001) while the S and G2 phases exhibited a substantial decrease (P < .0001) in the DOX group (Fig. 3C and D). This finding suggests that DOX-induced cell cycle arrest in the G1 phase of H9c2 cells impedes their entry into the DNA synthesis phase and disrupts the normal DNA replication process, thereby hindering the proliferation of H9c2 cells. SBP alone had no significant effect on the cell cycle, and the results obtained were like those of the control group. Compared with the DOX group, the number of cells in G1 phase in the group with DOX + SBP was significantly lower (P < .0001), and the number of cells in S phase and G2 phase was significantly higher (P < .005; P < .0001), suggesting that the treatment of H9c2 cells with SBP helped to alleviate the cytotoxicity and cytostatic effect produced by the action of DOX alone.

3.5. SBP inhibits oxidative stress and apoptosis in H9c2 cells after DOX treatment

Oxidative stress and cell apoptosis contribute to DOX-related cardiac injury. The ROS fluorescence intensity exhibited an augmentation in the DOX group relative to the control group (Fig. 3B). Elevated levels of free radicals cause protein/lipid peroxidation, instigating oxidative damage and apoptosis. Conversely, the ROS fluorescence intensity was lower in the SBP group than in the DOX group, and no significant difference was observed between the control and the SBP groups.

The apoptosis rate (i.e., early and mid-late apoptosis) exhibited a significant increase in the DOX group in comparison to the control group (P < .0001). Notably, apoptosis was significantly lower in the DOX + SBP group than the DOX group (P < .0001), with no significant difference observed between the control and SBP groups (Fig. 3E and F).

3.6. Effect of SBP on the expression of apoptosis-related proteins and AKT pathway-related proteins in DOX-induced H9c2 cells

The p-AKT/AKT in the DOX group was significantly lower than the control group (P < .001), whereas the Bax/Bcl-2 was significantly elevated (P < .001) (Fig. 4A–C). In contrast, the DOX + SBP group exhibited significantly higher levels of p-AKT/AKT (P < .05) and lower levels of Bax/Bcl-2 (P < .01) than the DOX group. Importantly, no significant differences were observed between the control and SBP groups.

Bcl-2 and Bax exert opposing roles in apoptosis, where Bcl-2 inhibits and promotes apoptosis. In line with the predictions from network pharmacology, DOX promoted apoptosis by suppressing the phosphorylation of AKT, while SBP mitigated DOX-induced apoptosis.

4. Discussion

Network pharmacology effectively connects therapeutic components of traditional Chinese medicine formulas with disease targets, elucidating compound Chinese medicine mechanisms. SBP, a renowned classic Chinese medicine formula, comprises 7 herbs. Following extensive screening, 90 bioactive compounds were identified within SBP. The pharmacological activities of these components in cardiovascular diseases have been previously documented. For instance, Bufalin, a principal constituent of traditional Chinese medicine Chan Su, falls within the cardiac steroid family[11]; Ginsenoside Rg3, extracted as the most potent ingredient from renshen roots, exhibits pharmacological properties including anti-inflammatory, antitumor, and antiviral effects, rendering it highly valuable in mitigating myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury[12–14]; Muscone, an active compound derived from Shexiang, demonstrates anti-ischemic and myocardial protective activities.[15] Thus, these findings suggest that the multi-component SBP acts through multiple targets.

This study found that DOX significantly elevated ROS content in H9c2 cells, while SBP reduced ROS accumulation.

Interestingly, the core proteins AKT1 and Bcl-2 of interest were identified from the PPI network. Moreover, the PI3K–AKT signaling pathway emerged as the most significant pathway in KEGG pathway enrichment, representing a classic pathway pivotal in regulating cell apoptosis.[16] AKT is an essential pro-proliferative and antiapoptotic signaling kinase with cardioprotective effects.[17] AKT1 represents 1 subtype of the AKT protein. The activation and translocation of AKT1 into mitochondria represent crucial steps in shielding myocardial cells from ischemia–reperfusion injury by attenuating ROS production. Typically, AKT remains inactive in the cytoplasm. Upon activation, it translocates to the plasma membrane and undergoes phosphorylation. Elevated intracellular ROS levels inhibit AKT activation and upregulate the expression of the pro-apoptotic gene Bax. Bax is activated to form aggregates and insert into the outer mitochondrial membrane to form oligomeric macropores, leading to the release of cytochrome C from the mitochondria into the cytoplasm, the formation of apoptotic bodies, and the activation of caspase-3, which causes apoptotic cell death. This process can be blocked by the antiapoptotic protein Bcl-2.[18,19] In the present study, our findings suggest that SBP attenuates DOX-induced apoptosis in cardiomyocytes through the ROS-mediated AKT/Bcl-2 pathway.

Mechanistically, oxidative stress and apoptosis are critical factors in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity.[20] In vivo and in vitro studies revealed that DOX-induced apoptosis was mediated by ROS.[20] Elevated levels of ROS lead to mitochondrial dysfunction, placing cells in a state of oxidative stress, activating the Bcl-2 and caspases family in the heart, and leading to apoptotic cell death.[21] The activation of the AKT pathway attenuates DOX-induced cardiotoxicity and heart failure.[22–24] Consistent with previous findings, the present study found that DOX treatment increased apoptotic cells, decreased AKT phosphorylation, decreased the expression of the apoptosis-suppressing protein Bcl-2, and increased the expression of the pro-apoptotic proteins Bax and ROS levels. In vitro studies revealed that SBP treatment resulted in an increase in phosphorylated AKT inhibited by DOX and an increase in Bcl-2 expression, whereas DOX-induced decrease in the expression of Bax and a decrease in the intracellular ROS level. These findings suggest that SBP protects against oxidative stress-induced apoptosis in DOX-induced myocardial injury. Our study demonstrated for the first time that SBP protects against DOX-induced cardiotoxicity through the ROS-mediated AKT/Bcl-2 pathway.

Interestingly, we observed that SBP enhances the viability of H9c2 cells. Previous studies have demonstrated similar effects of SBP on CATH.a cells with no reports on H9c2 cells and an unclear mechanism. This phenomenon may be associated with SBP’s ability to mitigate oxidative stress.

Unlike traditional pharmacology experiments, the network pharmacology applied in this study can provide a more comprehensive perspective. The interaction network between drugs and organisms is complex, and the findings of this thesis provide specific signaling pathways and related target proteins, which provide a theoretical basis for the study of SBP anti-DOX-induced cardiotoxicity. Our in vitro study demonstrates that SBP decreases ROS levels and mitigates DOX-induced oxidative stress. SBP downregulates Bax expression while upregulating Bcl-2 and p-AKT expression, suggesting its antiapoptotic properties and reinforcing its efficacy against DOX-induced cardiac toxicity. However, this study has some limitations. We only verified the key targets and potential pathways in in vitro experiments; however, the accuracy of the conclusions needs to be provided by animal experiments.

Strict fixed point problem, stability results and retraction displacement condition for Picard operators
Cristina Gheorghe, Adrian Petruşel

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to give strict fixed point principles for multivalued operators T : X → P ⁡ ( X ) T:X\rightarrow P(X) satisfying some contraction conditions of Ćirić and of Ćirić-Reich-Rus type. We are interested, under which conditions, the multi-valued operator has a unique strict fixed point and, additionally, when the sequence of its multi-valued iterates ( T n ​ ( x ) ) n ∈ ℕ (T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}} converges to this unique strict fixed point. Moreover, some stability properties, such as data dependence on operator perturbation, Ulam-Hyers stability, well-posedness in the sense of Reich and Zaslavski and Ostrowski property of the strict fixed point problem are established.

Introduction

The main metric fixed point theorem for multivalued contractions on complete metric spaces was given by Nadler in 1969. Then, one year later, Covitz and Nadler extended the result for the case of a generalized metric space. Later on, there were obtained many other extensions of these results. For example, A. Petruşel and G. Petruşel gave in
[
5
]
a saturated fixed point principle for multivalued contractions.

The purpose of this paper is to formulate similar principles for some elements of the class multivalued Picard operators. More precisely, Ćirić type operators (see
[
9
]
) and Ćirić-Reich-Rus type operators (see
[
2
]
) will be considered. Our goal is to determine under which assumptions the multi-valued operator has a unique strict fixed point and, moreover, when the sequence of its multivalued iterates
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent to this unique fixed point. We also study some stability properties, such as data dependence problem, Ulam-Hyers stability, well-posedness in the sense of Reich and Zaslavski and Ostrowski property for the strict fixed point problem.

Terminology and notations

We will use the consecrated notions (see, also
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
). In this paper, for a metric space
(X,d)
,
P(X)
stands for the family of all non-empty subsets of X,
P_{cp}
represent the family of all non-empty compact subsets of X, and
P_{cl}
is the family of all non-empty closed subsets of X. Also, for a multivalued operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
, the following notations are used:

•

the fixed point set of T:

\displaystyle Fix(T):=\{x\in X:x\in T(x)\}

•

the strict fixed point set of T:

\displaystyle SFix(T):=\{x\in X:\{x\}=T(x)\}

•

the graphic of T:

\displaystyle Graph(T):=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:y\in T(x)\}

We also recall, in the context of a metric space, the definitions of the following functionals:

•

the gap functional generated by
d
:

\displaystyle D:P(X)\times P(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},~D(A,B):=\inf\{d(a,b):a\in A,b\in B\}

•

the excess functional of A over B generated by d:

\displaystyle e:P(X)\times P(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},~e(A,B):=\sup\{D(a,B):a\in A\};

•

the Pompeiu-Hausdorff functional generated by
d
:

\displaystyle H:P(X)\times P(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},~H(A,B):=\max\{e(A,B),e(B,A)\}.

Definition 1.1
.

Let (X,d) be a metric space. The operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
is a multivalued weakly Picard operator (MWP operator) if, for each
x\in X
and for each
y\in T(x)
, there exists a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in X which satisfies the following conditions:

i)

x_{0}=x,x_{1}=y
;

ii)

x_{n+1}\in T(x_{n})
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

iii)

the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent and its limit is a fixed point of T.

Remark 1.2
.

Any sequence satisfying conditions i) and ii) from Definition
1.1
, is called a sequence of successive approximations of T.

Definition 1.3
.

We call
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
a multivalued Picard (MP) operator if the following statements hold true:

i)

SFix(T)=Fix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
;

ii)

T^{n}(x)\xrightarrow{H}\{x^{*}\}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, for each
x\in X.

Definition 1.4
.

A multivalued Picard operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
is called
\Psi-
multivalued Picard operator (
\Psi-MP
operator) if there exists an increasing mapping
\Psi:\mathbb{R_{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
with
\Psi
continuous at 0 and
\Psi(0)=0
, such that

d(x,x^{*})\leq\Psi(D(x,T(x)),\text{ for all }x\in X.

Remark 1.5
.

The above condition is also called the strong retraction-displacement condition for the case of unique (strict) fixed point of
T
.

Main results for multivalued Ćirić type contractions

Retraction-displacement condition

In this section, we will give two main fixed point principles for multivalued retractions satisfying some contraction-type conditions. In a similar manner as in Theorem 5.5 from
[
5
]
, we can formulate the following first main result:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be an operator such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
such that

\displaystyle H(T(x),T(y))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then

i)

Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
;

ii)

for all
x\in X
, the sequence of sets
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\{x^{*}\}
with respect to the Pompeiu-Hausdorff metric
H
;

iii)

d(x,x^{*})\leq\dfrac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}D(x,T(x))
, for all
x\in X
, where
\xi\in(0,1)
can be arbitrary chosen.

Proof.

i) Let
x^{*}\in SFix(T)
. Suppose there exists
y^{*}\in SFix(T)
with
x^{*}\neq y^{*}
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle d(x^{*},y^{*})

\displaystyle=H(T(x^{*}),T(y^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(y^{*},T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(y^{*}))

\displaystyle=\alpha d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta d(x^{*},y^{*})+\gamma d(x^{*},y^{*}).

Therefore,

(1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma)d(x^{*},y^{*})\leq 0.

Since
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
, it follows that
d(x^{*},y^{*})=0
, so
x^{*}=y^{*}
. The contradiction proves that
SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
.

Let
\tilde{x}\in Fix(T)
with
x^{*}\neq\tilde{x}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle d(\tilde{x},x^{*})

\displaystyle=D(\tilde{x},T(x^{*}))\leq H(T(\tilde{x}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(\tilde{x},x^{*})+\beta D(\tilde{x},T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(\tilde{x}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x^{*},\tilde{x})+\beta d(\tilde{x},x^{*})+\gamma d(x^{*},\tilde{x}).

We get again a contradiction and thus
Fix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
.

ii) Let
x\in X
be an arbitrary element and consider the sequence
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle H(T(x),x^{*})=H(T(x),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta d(x,x^{*})+\gamma H(T(x),x^{*}).

Thus, with the notation
k:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\gamma}\in(0,1)
, we get:

H(T(x),x^{*})\leq kd(x,x^{*}).

We prove, by mathematical induction, that the following proposition is true

P(n):H(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq k^{n}d(x,x^{*}),\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

By the above proof, it is clear that P(1) is true. We will prove that
P(n)\Rightarrow P(n+1).

We show first that
H(T(Y),x^{*})\leq kH(Y,x^{*})
, for all
Y\in P_{cl}(X).
Indeed, let
v\in T(Y)
be an arbitrary element. Then, there exists
u\in Y
such that
v\in T(u)
. We have

d(v,x^{*})\leq H(T(u),\{x^{*}\})\leq kd(u,x^{*})\leq kH(Y,\{x^{*}\}).

Taking
\displaystyle\sup_{v\in T(Y)}
from the previous inequality, we get that

H(T(Y),\{x^{*}\})\leq kH(Y,\{x^{*}\}).

(2.1)

Therefore, we obtain:

\displaystyle H(T^{n+1}(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle=H(T(T^{n}(x)),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq})}{\leq}kH(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\overset{P(n)}{\leq}k^{n+1}H(T(x),\{x^{*}\}).

The proof of the mathematical induction is now complete. Hence,

H(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq k^{n}d(x,x^{*})\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore,
T^{n}(x)\overset{H}{\rightarrow}\{x^{*}\}
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

iii) Let
\xi\in(0,1)
be arbitrary and let
x\in X
. Then, there exists
\widetilde{y}\in T(x)
such that
\xi d(x,\widetilde{y})\leq D(x,T(x))
, or, equivalently,

d(x,\widetilde{y})\leq\dfrac{1}{\xi}D(x,T(x)).

(2.2)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle d(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+d(\widetilde{y},x^{*})

\displaystyle=d(x,\widetilde{y})+D(\widetilde{y},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+H(T(x),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta d(x,x^{*})+\gamma d(x^{*},\widetilde{y}).

Hence,

(1-\alpha-\beta)d(x,x^{*})\leq(1+\gamma)d(x,\widetilde{y}).

(2.3)

From (
2.3
) and (
2.2
), we get the relation iii).
∎

Well-posedness of the strict fixed point problem

Definition 2.2
.

The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is said to be well-posed in the sense of Reich and Zaslavski (see
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
) if
SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
and the following implication holds:

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X\text{ and }D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0\implies u_{n}\rightarrow x^{*}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be an operator such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then, the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is well-posed in the sense of Reich and Zaslavski.

Proof.

Notice first that, by Theorem 1,
Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}.

The conclusion follows immediately from the strong retraction-displacement relation iii) in Theorem
2.1
, by observing that

d(u_{n},x^{*})\leq\dfrac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Alternatively, we have:

\displaystyle d(u_{n},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))+H(T(u_{n}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))+\alpha d(u_{n},x^{*})+\beta D(x^{*},T(u_{n}))+\gamma D(u_{n},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))+\alpha d(u_{n},x^{*})+\beta[d(x^{*},u_{n})+D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))]+\gamma d(u_{n},x^{*})

Then, we get

d(u_{n},x^{*})\leq\dfrac{\beta+1}{1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma}D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty.

∎

Ostrowski stability

Definition 2.4
.

The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
has the Ostrowski stability property if

SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
and the following implication holds:

(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X,D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))\rightarrow 0\implies v_{n}\rightarrow x^{*}\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

The following auxiliary result will be important in our next proof.

Lemma 2.5
.

(Cauchy-Toeplitz lemma, see
[
7
]
) Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that the series
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
is convergent. Let
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of non-negative numbers, that converges to zero. Then:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n-k}b_{k}\Bigg)=0.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
be an operator such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
has the Ostrowski stability property.

Proof.

Notice first that, by Theorem
2.1
,
Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}.

Then, we have:

\displaystyle d(v_{n+1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+H(T(v_{n}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+\alpha d(v_{n},x^{*})+\beta D(v_{n},T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(T(v_{n}),x^{*})

\displaystyle=D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+(\alpha+\beta)d(v_{n},x^{*})+\gamma D(T(v_{n}),x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+(\alpha+\beta)d(v_{n},x^{*})+\gamma(d(v_{n+1},x^{*})+D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))).

Then, denoting by
k:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\gamma}
, with
0<k<1
, we have

\displaystyle d(v_{n+1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+kd(v_{n},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+k\Big[\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n},T(v_{n-1}))+kd(v_{n-1},x^{*})\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+k\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}D(v_{n},T(v_{n-1}))+k^{2}d(v_{n-1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Big[D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+kD(v_{n},T(v_{n-1})+\cdots+k^{n}D(v_{1},T(v_{0}))\Big].

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}k^{n}
converges and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))=0
, from the Cauchy-Toeplitz Lemma, it follows that
d(v_{n+1},x^{*})\rightarrow 0
, so the strict fixed point problem has the Ostrowski stability property.

∎

Data dependence of the strict fixed point problem

Definition 2.7
.

The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
has the data dependence property if, for each multivalued operator
F:X\rightarrow P(X)
, with the properties:

i)

SFix(F)\neq\varnothing
;

ii)

there exists
\eta>0
such that
H(T(x),F(x))\leq\eta
, for all
x\in X;

and for each strict fixed point
x^{*}
of
T
, there exists a strict fixed point
u^{*}\in X
of
F
such that
d(x^{*},u^{*})\leq c\eta
, for some
c>0
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space,
T:X\rightarrow P_{CL}(X)
such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
, such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then, the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
has the data dependence property.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.1
, it is immediately that
SFix(T)=Fix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
and

d(x,x^{*})\leq\widetilde{L}D(x,T(x)),\text{ for all }x\in X,

where
\widetilde{L}=\dfrac{1+\gamma}{(1-\alpha-\beta)\xi}>0
.

Let
F:X\rightarrow P(X)
a multivalued operator satisfying properties i) and ii) from Definition
2.7
. Let
u^{*}\in SFix(F)
. Then,

d(u^{*},x^{*})\leq\widetilde{L}D(u^{*},T(u^{*}))=\widetilde{L}H(F(u^{*}),T(u^{*}))\leq\widetilde{L}\eta.

∎

Ulam-Hyers stability

Definition 2.9
.

The strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is Ulam-Hyers stable if there exists
c>0
such that for any solution
y^{*}
of the relation

D(y,T(y))\leq\varepsilon,

there exists
x^{*}\in SFix(T)
satisfying the following relation:

d(y^{*},x^{*})\leq c\varepsilon.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space,
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
be such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
, such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then, the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
is Ulam-Hyers stable.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.1
, we know that
SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
. Let
y^{*}
a solution of the inequation
D(y,T(y))\leq\varepsilon
. Then,

\displaystyle d(y^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle=D(y^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(y^{*},T(y^{*}))+H(T(y^{*}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\alpha d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(x^{*},T(y^{*}))+\gamma D(y^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(y^{*},T(y^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq(1+\beta)\varepsilon+(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)d(x^{*},y^{*}).

In conclusion,

d(y^{*},x^{*})\leq c\varepsilon,

where
c:=\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma}>0.

∎

Remark 2.11
.

By the above proof, we observe that for each strict fixed point
x^{*}
of T, there exists a fixed point
\widetilde{u}
of F such that

d(x^{*},\widetilde{u})\leq c\eta,\text{ for some }c>0.

Quasi-contraction condition

Definition 2.12
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space. An operator
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
is called a multivalued strong quasi-contraction if
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
and there exists
l\in(0,1)
such that

D(T(x),x^{*})\leq ld(x,x^{*}),\text{ for all }x^{*}\in SFix(T)\text{ and for all }x\in X.

Remark 2.13
.

If T is a strong quasi-contraction, then
SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}.

Proof.

Let
x^{*}\in SFix(T)
and
y\in SFix(T),
with
y\neq x^{*}.
Then,

D(T(y),x^{*})\leq ld(y,x^{*}),

or, equivalently
(1-l)d(y,x^{*})\leq 0
. This leads to a contradiction. Thus,
SFix(T)
is a singleton.
∎

Theorem 2.14
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow P(X)
be such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
, such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(y))+\gamma D(y,T(x)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then, the operator
T
is a multivalued strong quasi-contraction.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, we get that
Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
.

Then, we have

\displaystyle D(T(x),x^{*})

\displaystyle=H(T(x),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x)).

Thus, we get that

D(T(x),x^{*})\leq ld(x,x^{*}),\text{ with }l:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\gamma}\in(0,1).

∎

3
Main results for multivalued Ćirić-Reich-Rus type contractions

In the following, we will give another fixed point principle for multivalued operators satisfying another contraction-type condition.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let (X,d) a complete metric space and let
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be an operator with
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose
there exists
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
, with
\alpha<1
such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(x))+\gamma D(y,T(y)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then:

i)

Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
;

ii)

d(x,x^{*})\leq\dfrac{1+\beta}{(1-\alpha)\xi}D(x,T(x))
, for all
x\in X
, where
\xi\in(0,1)
can be arbitrary chosen.

iii)

if, additionally,
\alpha+2\beta<1
, then, for all
x\in X
, the sequence of sets
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\{x^{*}\}
with respect to the Pompeiu-Hausdorff metric
H
;

Proof.

i) Let
x^{*}\in SFix(T)
and suppose there exists
y^{*}\in SFix(T)
such that
x^{*}\neq y^{*}
. We get that

\displaystyle d(x^{*},y^{*})

\displaystyle=H(T(x^{*}),T(y^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(x^{*},T(x^{*}))+\gamma D(y^{*},T(y^{*})).

Hence,
(1-\alpha)d(x^{*},y^{*})\leq 0
.

Since
\alpha<1
, we get the contradiction
x^{*}=y^{*}
.Therefore,
SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
.

Let
\widetilde{x}\in Fix(T)
be a fixed point of
T
different from
x^{*}
. Then,

\displaystyle d(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle=D(\widetilde{x},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq H(T(\widetilde{x}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(\widetilde{x},x^{*})+\beta D(\widetilde{x},T(\widetilde{x}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle=\alpha d(\widetilde{x},x^{*}).

Hence,
(1-\alpha)d(\widetilde{x},x^{*})\leq 0
and we get the contradiction which proves that
\widetilde{x}=x^{*}
. Thus,
Fix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
.

ii) Let
\xi\in(0,1)
be an arbitrary value and let
x\in X
. Similarly to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, there exists
\widetilde{y}\in T(x)
such that

d(x,\widetilde{y})\leq\frac{1}{\xi}D(x,T(x)).

(3.1)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle d(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+d(\widetilde{y},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+H(T(x),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T(x))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq d(x,\widetilde{y})+\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta d(x,\widetilde{y}).

Hence we get

d(x,x^{*})\leq\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha}d(x,\widetilde{y}).

(3.2)

Thus, from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we obtain that

d(x,x^{*})\leq\frac{1+\beta}{\xi(1-\alpha)}D(x,T(x)).

iii) Let
x\in X
be an arbitrary element. Considering the sequence
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\displaystyle H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle=H(T(x),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T(x))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta[d(x,x^{*})+D(x^{*},T(x))]

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+\beta)d(x,x^{*})+\beta H(T(x),\{x^{*}\}).

In consequence,
H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\beta}d(x,x^{*})
.

By denoting
k:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\beta}
, we observe that
0<k<1
and we can easily prove by mathematical induction that

P(n):H(T^{n}(x),\{x^{*}\})\leq k^{n}d(x,x^{*})

holds true for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Therefore,
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\{x^{*}\}
with respect to the Pompeiu-Hausdorff metric
H
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let (X,d) be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow P_{cl}(X)
be such that
SFix(T)\neq\varnothing
. Suppose there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with
\alpha<1
such that

H(T(x),T(y))\leq\alpha d(x,y)+\beta D(x,T(x))+\gamma D(y,T(y)),\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then, the strict fixed point problem
T(x)=\{x\}
has the following properties:

i)

is well-posed in the sense of Reich and Zaslavski;

ii)

has the data dependence property;

iii)

is Ulam-Hyers stable;

iv)

has the Ostrowski stability property;

v)

the operator
T
is a multivalued strong quasi-contraction.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we have
Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}.

i) From the strong retraction-displacement condition iii), from Theorem
3.1
, we get

d(u_{n},x^{*})\leq\frac{1+\beta}{(1-\alpha)\xi}D(u_{n},T(u_{n}))\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty

ii) From Theorem
3.1
, we get
Fix(T)=SFix(T)=\{x^{*}\}
and

d(x,x^{*})\leq\widetilde{K}D(x,T(x)),\text{ for all }x\in X,

with
\widetilde{K}:=\dfrac{1+\beta}{(1-\alpha)\xi}>0.

Let
F:X\rightarrow P(X)
a multivalued operator such that:

a)

SFix(F)\neq\varnothing
;

b)

there exists
\eta>0
such that
H(T(x),F(x))\leq\eta,
for all
x\in X.

Let
u^{*}\in SFix(F).
Then,

d(u^{*},x^{*})\leq\widetilde{K}D(u^{*},T(u^{*}))\leq\widetilde{K}H(F(u^{*}),T(u^{*}))\leq\widetilde{K}\eta.

iii) Let
y^{*}\in X
a solution of the inequation
D(y,T(y))\leq\varepsilon.
Then,

\displaystyle d(y^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle=D(y^{*},T(x^{*}))\leq D(y^{*},T(y^{*}))+H(T(y^{*}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\alpha d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(y^{*},T(y^{*}))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+(\alpha+\beta)d(x^{*},y^{*})+\beta D(x^{*},T(y^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq(1+\beta)\varepsilon+(\alpha+\beta)d(x^{*},y^{*}).

Hence,

d(y^{*},x^{*})\leq c\varepsilon,

with
c:=\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha-\beta}>0.

iv) Let
(v_{n})\subset X
. We get

\displaystyle d(v_{n+1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+H(T(v_{n}),T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+\alpha d(v_{n},x^{*})+\beta D(v_{n},T(v_{n}))+\gamma D(T(x^{*}),x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+\alpha d(v_{n},x^{*})+\beta d(v_{n},x^{*})+\beta d(x^{*},v_{n+1})+

\displaystyle+\beta D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n})).

Thus, by denoting
k:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\beta}
, with
0<k<1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle d(v_{n+1},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+kd(v_{n},x^{*}).

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+k\Big[\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}D(v_{n},T(v_{n-1})+kd(v_{n-1},x^{*})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\Big[D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+kD(v_{n},T(v_{n-1}))+

\displaystyle+k^{2}D(v_{n-1},T(v_{n-2}))+k^{3}d(v_{n-2},x^{*})\Big]

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\Big[D(v_{n+1},T(v_{n}))+kD(v_{n},T(v_{n-1}))+k^{2}D(v_{n-1},T(v_{n-2}))+\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots+k^{n}D(v_{1},T(v_{0}))\Big]

By the Cauchy-Toeplitz Lemma, we get the conclusion.

v) Let
x^{*}\in SFix(T).
We have:

\displaystyle H(T(x),\{x^{*}\})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x,T(x))+\gamma D(x^{*},T(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+\beta)d(x,x^{*})+\beta D(x^{*},T(x))

Hence,

D(T(x),x^{*})\leq ld(x,x^{*}),

where
l:=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{1-\beta}d(x,x^{*})\in(0,1).

∎

Zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles ameliorate levofloxacin-induced hepatotoxicity in rat model
Authors: Naglaa F Zaki, Sahar H Orabi, Hend M Abdel-Bar, Reda M Korany, Laila A AlShuraym, Lamya Ahmed Alkeridis, Mohamed M Ahmed
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe present investigation assessed the potential ameliorating effect of zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles against Levofloxacin-induced liver damage in rats.MethodsFifty adult Wistar rats were split up into five groups at random. (n = 10). GI, (control): was orally gavaged with distilled water; G II (LFX): was orally given levofloxacin (LFX) (40 mg/kg BW). G III was orally administered zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles (Zn- RSV) (20 mg/kg BW). G IV: was given Zn-RSV as GIII and LFX as GII simultaneously (LFX + Zn-RSV). GV: was given LFX as GII and zinc oxide (20 mg/kg BW) (LFX + Zn). All treatments were given every other day for two months.ResultsAdministration of zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles (Zn-RSV NPs) significantly mitigated levofloxacin (LFX)-induced hepatotoxicity in rats. Compared to LFX-treated groups through improved liver function via lowered serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), urea, and creatinine levels. Also, reduced oxidative stress markers, decreased malondialdehyde (MDA) and nitric oxide (NO) levels in hepatic tissue and enhanced antioxidant defenses, increased superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase activities, restoring them to near-normal levels. Modulated apoptosis: Downregulated pro-apoptotic BAX expression and upregulated anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 expression, promoting cell survival. Zn-RSV NPs alleviated histopathological changes through mitigated LFX-induced degenerative and necrotic changes in hepatic tissue, preserving tissue architecture.ConclusionsThis study revealed that zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles modulated levofloxacin-induced hepatic damage by lowering inflammation and oxidative stress while increasing the activity of antioxidant enzymes in rat hepatic tissue.Keywords: Levofloxacin, Zinc oxide nanoparticles, Resveratrol, Oxidative stress, Liver injury, Apoptosis

Introduction

Antibiotics are vital for treating bacterial infections, but their overuse has led to alarming antibiotic resistance, a global health crisis [1]. Levofloxacin (LFX), a widely used fluoroquinolone, is effective against diverse pathogens but causes severe toxicity, including liver damage, skin reactions, and oxidative stress [2, 3]. Fluoroquinolones induce reactive oxygen species (ROS), overwhelming cellular defenses and damaging hepatic, neural, and joint tissues [4, 5]. When the fluoroquinolone trovafloxacin (TVX) was combined with lipopolysaccharide (LPS), it caused significant liver damage, which is accompanied by large apoptotic regions in the liver, elevated serum levels of ALT, and pro-inflammatory cytokines [6]. TVX is linked to inflammation-mediated hepatotoxicity [7]. Levofloxacin has antibacterial activity against anaerobes and most aerobic Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms. It inhibits the DNA gyrase enzyme necessary for DNA replication and transcription [8]. Levofloxacin is used for the treatment of infections in the urinary tract. (complex and uncomplicated) as well as community-acquired pneumonia, which can include multidrug-resistant bacteria [8]. The activity range of levofloxacin involves many bacterial pathogen species and the respiratory system, urogenital tract, digestive tract, and abdominal infections [2]. Levofloxacin was the first fluoroquinolone known to be linked with severe cases of hepatotoxicity, where levofloxacin-using individuals are suffering from hepatic toxicity at a rate of 1: 1,000,000 according to the manufacturer and global surveillance systems [9]. Nanoparticles (NPs) are particles with a size range from 1 to 100 nm in diameter. NPs have attracted a lot of attention due to their fast-growing utility in nanomedicine, such as drug or gene delivery in the treatment of a variety of disorders [10]. Zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnoNPs) are recognized to have properties as increased potency at low concentrations, increased efficacy against drug-resistant viruses, cost-effectiveness, and surface modification availability [11–13].

Resveratrol (RSV) is an antioxidant found naturally in plants. RSV is a polyphenolic substance that is present in 2 forms: cis-resveratrol and trans-resveratrol. It can be found in grapes. Resveratrol is recognized to have antioxidative, anti-inflammatory, anti-neoplastic, antimicrobial effects, anti-atherogenic and anti-angiogenic properties, making it effective in the treatment of several cardiovascular illnesses [14]. Resveratrol works as an antioxidant by scavenging or suppressing the production of free radicals, preventing lipid peroxidation, and modulating the activity of antioxidant-related enzymes [15]. Because of its anti-oxidative biological qualities, RSV has been widely used in the breeding business as a feed supplement to improve antioxidative capability and meat quality by suppressing lipid and protein oxidation. Dietary RSV reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels while increasing mRNA levels of liver antioxidant enzymes and superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity. By enhancing anti-oxidative capacity and catalase activity and decreasing malondialdehyde levels in muscle, RSV improved the meat quality of broilers [16]. Resveratrol’s radical scavenging activity results in a reduction of ROS production and lipid peroxidation, making it an effective antioxidant [17]. Chronic stress-induced changes in hepatic tissue by oxidative stress, which is noticed through an increase in polyunsaturated fatty acids [18].

This study investigates resveratrol-loaded zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnO-RSV NPs) as a protective strategy to mitigate LFX-induced hepatotoxicity.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Levofloxacin was a precious gift from Arabco Med company LFG. No (17080173), Diethyl ether (99%) was purchased from the El-Gomhoria Company, Amiriya, Cairo, Egypt. Resveratrol (RSV) was obtained from Xian Sonwu Biotech Co, Ltd., China. Other chemicals were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich, UK.,

Assay ELISA kits to assess lipid peroxidation and antioxidant defense biomarkers were purchased from Biodiagnostic Company, Biodiagnostic, Giza, Egypt.

Assay kits for measuring the AST, ALT activities, Urea, and Creatinine were purchased from Spectrum Diagnostics, Cairo, Egypt.

Animals

Fifty male albino Wistar rats,4 weeks old weighting 110–125 g, were obtained from VACSERA Company (Egypt). The animals were housed in polypropylene cages and provided a balanced diet obtained from AL wadi-Company, Egypt. The experimental design and all procedures were performed following guidelines and principles and were permitted by the Research Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, University of Sadat City, Egypt (VUSC-016–1–20). All experimental procedures were directed compliant with the Animal Research: Following the Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines.

Preparation of nanoparticles

Zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnO-NPs) with and without resveratrol (RSV) were prepared and characterized as previously prescribed by Zaki et al. [19].

Experimental design

Five groups (n = 10) of rats were randomly assigned as follows:

GI (Control group): rats were gavaged distilled water orally, every other day for two months; GII (LFX- group): rats were administered levofloxacin (LFX) dissolved in distilled water at a dose of 40 mg/kg BW orally, every other day for two months [20]; GIII (Zn-RSV group): rats were administered with zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles (Zn-RSV) in distilled water at a dose rate of 20 mg/kg BW [21] orally every other day for two months; G IV (LFX + Zn-RSV group): rats were simultaneously administered with (LFX and Zn-RSV), as described in the LFX and zinc oxide resveratrol, nanoparticles-treated groups.

G V (LFX + Zn group) rats were treated with LFX as mentioned in GII and zinc oxide nanoparticles at a dose of 20 mg/kg BW [21] orally every other day for two months.

Sampling

After the 8th week, animals were anesthetized by using phenobarbital at a dose of 45 mg/kg bw i.p. The blood samples were withdrawn from the inner canthus of the eye using Capillary tubes, then allowing the blood to clot at room temperature, they were centrifuged for 15 min at 3000 rpm. The serum from the clear supernatant was collected and stored at -20 °C until used to assay the biochemical parameters.

The livers were removed once they were slaughtered and rinsed with a 0.9% NaCl solution. The liver tissues were cut into two parts. One portion was stored at -80 °C and utilized to measure the levels of MDA and NO as well as the activities of SOD and CAT. the remaining liver tissue was preserved in 10% neutral buffer formalin for histological analysis and immunohistochemistry.

Serum biochemical parameters

Serum AST and ALT activities were determined kinetically using commercially available kits [22]. Serum levels of urea were measured according to the methods of Fawcett and Scott [23]. Serum creatinine concentration was measured according to the methods of Bartels et al. [24].

Antioxidant assays in hepatic tissues

MDA and NO levels in hepatic tissues were assessed according to Ohkawa et al. [25] and Montgomery and Dymock [26], respectively. The activity of SOD was determined using the colorimetric method according to Nishikimi et al. [27]. Catalase activity was assessed according to Aebi [28].

Histopathological examination

Liver samples were taken from each experimental group at the end of the experiment and preserved in 10% neutral buffered formalin, washed, dehydrated, cleared, and embedded in paraffin. Hematoxylin and Eosin were used to stain the paraffin-embedded blocks after they were sectioned at a thickness of 5 microns for histopathological examination [29]. Stained sections were examined by a light microscope (Olympus BX50, Japan).

Histopathological lesion scoring

Histopathological alterations in the liver were recorded and scored according to Madkour et al. [30] as: no changes (0), mild (1), moderate (2) and severe (3) changes, the grading was determined by percentage: <30% changes (mild change), < 30% – 50% (moderate change), and >50% (severe change).

Immunohistochemical investigation

Immunohistochemical analysis was carried out following the methods described by Korany et al. [31]; Zaki et al. [19]. The tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated in different grades of alcohol. The antigen retrieval was carried out by pretreating the sections with a citrate buffer of pH 6 for 20 min. Sections were incubated with rabbit polyclonal anti-Bcl-2 antibody at a concentration of 1:50 (ab59348; Abcam, Cambridge, UK), and rabbit monoclonal anti-Bax antibody [E63] at a concentration of 1:250 (ab32503; Abcam, Cambridge, UK) for two hours in a humidified chamber. The sections were incubated with goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (ab205718; Abcam, Cambridge, UK), and 3,3′-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAB, Sigma) was used as a chromogen. Finally, the slides were counterstained with hematoxylin and mounted with DPX. The negative control slides were prepared by replacing primary antibodies using PBS.

Evaluation of Bcl-2 and BAX proteins immunostaining

The quantitative immunoreactivity of Bcl-2 and Bax was evaluated in hepatic tissue, five tissue sections were studied. A high-power microscopic field was used to investigate immuno-reactivity in ten microscopical fields per section. (x 400). The percentage of positively stained cells (%) was estimated by color deconvolution image J 1.52 p software (Wayne Rasband, National Institutes of Health (U.S.A.)).

Statistical analysis

All data were evaluated for significance by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Duncan post hoc test for significant difference (P < 0.05). The statistical analysis system was carried out using SPSS (SPSS version 13.0, IBM, Chicago, IL, USA).

Results

However, co-treatment of (LFX with Zn-RSV NPs) and co-treatment with (LFX + Zn Nps) significantly decreased compared to those of the levofloxacin-treated group. On the other hand, Zn-RSV NPs themselves had no significant effects compared to the control group (Table 1).

Effect of Levofloxacin and/ or zinc oxide Resveratrol NPs on anti-oxidant and oxidative stress biomarkers in hepatic tissue

The administration of levofloxacin to rats resulted in lipid peroxidation in the hepatic tissue, as confirmed by elevated hepatic MDA and NO levels and a compromised liver antioxidant defense system, as demonstrated by decreased hepatic SOD and catalase enzyme activity in comparison to the control group.

However, when compared to the LFX-intoxicated group, co-treatment with (LFX + Zn-RSV) and with (LFX + Zn) nanoparticles decreased the amounts of MDA and NO in hepatic tissues while increasing the activities of hepatic SOD and catalase. Conversely, Zn-RSV nanoparticles did not significantly alter hepatic MDA and NO concentrations or SOD and catalase activity in comparison to the control group (Table 2).

Discussion

Fluoroquinolones are broad-spectrum antibiotics effective against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, used to treat infections like genitourinary, sinus, bronchitis, pneumonia, and urinary tract infections [5]. However, no antibiotic is entirely safe [32]. Levofloxacin (LFX) generates reactive oxygen species (ROS)—hydroxyl radicals, hydrogen peroxide, and superoxide anions—causing lipid peroxidation, protein modification, and DNA damage [5]. This oxidative stress inflames and damages hepatic tissue by activating pathways involving lipid peroxidation and caspase-mediated apoptosis [32, 33]. The liver, central to detoxification, is highly susceptible to free radicals [34]. LFX treatment in rats elevated serum AST and ALT levels agree with Vahidi-Eyrisofla et al. [2]. The elevation in the activity of these enzymes by LFX may be as a result of their release in response to tissue damage during routine normal destruction of erythrocytes, leukocytes, and other cells like liver cells [35]. Both urea and creatinine are metabolic waste products that are freely filtered by the glomeruli of the kidneys [36]. Serum concentrations are commonly used to screen for renal diseases [37]. Elevation of the plasma levels of creatinine and urea by LFX is an indication of abnormal renal function [38].

LFX treatment in rats (Group II) increased nitric oxide (NO) and depleted superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase, indicating heightened oxidative stress, aligning with Giustarini et al. [6]. The elevated NO level in hepatic tissues is caused by superoxide anions, which are produced by cell organelles and can combine with NO at high concentrations to form a hazardous free radical. NO reacts with the active sites of the enzymes, causing them to become inactive. This ultimately leads to the disruption of the cells’ antioxidant enzyme system [5].

Histologically, LFX caused vacuolar degeneration, necrosis, and mononuclear infiltration in hepatocytes, and this result was agreed with that reported by Ara et al. [39]. Mechanistically, LFX depletes hepatic antioxidant reserves, leading to liver injury [5].

Co-treatment with zinc oxide-resveratrol nanoparticles (ZnO-RSV NPs) mitigated LFX toxicity: mild vacuolar changes, reduced inflammation, and restored liver architecture [40]. ZnO-RSV NPs upregulated anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 and downregulated pro-apoptotic BAX, enhancing cell survival [41]. Cell viability is maintained by either activating apoptosis-suppressing proteins or inhibiting them. Bcl-2 mostly resides in the cytosol, while Bax mostly resides in the mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and nuclear membrane. Bax redistribution is an early event in apoptosis, as it moves from the cytosol to the membrane fractions in response to apoptotic stimuli [42]. Resveratrol’s antioxidant activity lowered ROS, MDA, and NO while boosting SOD and catalase, halting oxidative damage [43]. Immunostaining showed strong Bcl-2 and weak BAX expression in treated groups, supporting the nanoparticles’ protective role. ZnO nanoparticles improve the solubility and bioavailability of resveratrol [11]. This could lead to higher intracellular concentrations of RSV, amplifying its antioxidant and anti-apoptotic effects. These results were matched with the histological structure where the group administered with Zn-RSV nanoparticles improved the hazard effect induced by LFX. Immunostaining of Bcl-2 protein in the liver revealed weak positive expression, while BAX protein showed no immune reactive cells in control and zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticle treated groups. The group co-treated with LFX and zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticles showed a strong expression of Bcl-2 and a weak expression of BAX protein This could be explained by the antioxidative effect of zinc oxide resveratrol nanoparticle, as shown by the increase in SOD, CAT and the decline in MDA concentration.

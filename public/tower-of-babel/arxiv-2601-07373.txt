On the Enumeration of Generalized Cospectral Mates of Graphs
Muhammad Raza, Obaid Ullah Ahmad, Mudassir Shabbir, Waseem Abbas

Abstract
This paper investigates the enumeration of generalized cospectral mates of simple graphs, where the generalized spectrum consists of the spectra of a graph and its complement. Moving beyond the classical problem of identifying graphs determined by their generalized spectrum, we address the more quantitative question of how many non-isomorphic graphs can share the same generalized spectrum. Our approach is based on arithmetic constraints derived from the Smith Normal Form (SNF) of the walk matrix W ⁡ ( G ) W(G) , which lead to a tight upper bound on the number of generalized cospectral mates of a graph. Our upper bound applies to a much broader class of graphs than those previously shown to have no generalized cospectral mates (determined by generalized spectrum). Consequently, this work extends the family of graphs for which strong and informative spectral uniqueness results are available. Keywords : Adjacency matrix, Walk matrix, Cospectral graphs, Generalized spectrum



1
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph with
n
vertices, meaning it is finite, undirected, and contains no loops or multiple edges. The
adjacency matrix

A(G)
is the
n\times n
symmetric matrix where each entry
a_{ij}=1
if vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. The
spectrum
of a graph
G
, denoted
\operatorname{Spec}(G)
, is the multiset of eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix
A(G)
, counted with algebraic multiplicity.

Two graphs
G
and
H
are
isomorphic
, denoted
G\cong H
, if there exists a bijection
f:V(G)\to V(H)
such that vertices
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
if and only if
f(u)
and
f(v)
are adjacent in
H
. In other words, isomorphic graphs have identical structure, differing only by a relabeling of vertices. The
complement
of a graph
G
, denoted
\overline{G}
, is the graph on the same vertex set where two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are not adjacent in
G
. The adjacency matrix of the complement is given by
A(\overline{G})=J-I-A(G)
, where
J
is the
n\times n
all-ones matrix and
I
is the identity matrix.

A graph
G
is said to be
determined by its spectrum
(DS) if every graph with the same spectrum as
G
is isomorphic to
G
. A central open question in spectral graph theory is: “Which graphs are DS?”
This question was first raised by Günthard and Primas
[
2
]
in the context of chemistry and is closely related to Kac’s famous question
[
4
]
, “Can one hear the shape of a drum?” Fisher
[
1
]
modeled the drum as a graph whose sound is described by its spectrum, linking Kac’s question to the graph being DS. Haemers conjectured that almost all graphs are DS
[
9
,
3
]
. Nevertheless, proving that a given graph is DS remains challenging; see
[
9
,
10
]
for surveys.

Wang and Xu
[
13
]
considered this problem in the context of
generalized spectrum
, defined as the pair
(\operatorname{Spec}(G),\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{G}))
. Two or more graphs are
generalized cospectral
if they have the same generalized spectrum. While isomorphic graphs are clearly generalized cospectral, the converse does not hold in general. Non-isomorphic graphs that are generalized cospectral are referred to as
generalized cospectral mates
. A graph is said to be
determined by its generalized spectrum
(DGS) if it has no generalized cospectral mate.

Recent advances have leveraged the properties of the walk matrix to address these questions. For a graph
G
with
n
vertices, the
walk matrix

W(G)
is the
n\times n
matrix with columns
e,\,A(G)e,\,\dots,\,A(G)^{n-1}e
, where
e
is the all-ones column vector of length
n
. A graph
G
is
controllable
if
W(G)
is non-singular. Wang
[
11
]
introduced a large family of controllable graphs and proved that all graphs in this family are DGS. Extending these ideas to oriented graphs, Qiu et al.
[
6
]
introduced a family of oriented graphs determined by their generalized skew spectra.

A natural extension of this line of research addresses a fundamental question: how many generalized cospectral mates can a graph have? Wang et al.
[
14
]
identified a family of graphs where each graph has at most one generalized cospectral mate and proposed an algorithm to find such a mate. Later on, they provided an algorithm to find generalized cospectral mates for controllable or almost controllable graphs
[
12
]
. However, the practical feasibility of this algorithm is constrained by the computational difficulty of factorizing large integers. In the context of oriented graphs, Raza et al.
[
8
]
and Lin et al.
[
5
]
independently established upper bounds on the number of their generalized cospectral mates for a large family.

In this paper, we introduce a large family of graphs and establish an upper bound on the number of their generalized cospectral mates. Let
d_{1}(M)\mid d_{2}(M)\mid\dots\mid d_{n}(M)
denote the invariant factors of the Smith Normal Form of a matrix
M
. We define
\mathcal{F}_{n}
as the family of controllable graphs on
n
vertices where each graph
G
satisfies:

d_{\lceil n/2\rceil}(W(G))=1\quad\text{and}\quad d_{n-1}(W(G))=2.

(1)

To analyze the relationship between generalized cospectral mates in
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, we utilize the concept of the
level
of a rational matrix:

Definition 1
.

The level of a rational matrix
Q
, denoted
\ell(Q)
, is defined as the smallest positive integer
x
such that
xQ
is an integral matrix.

For graphs in
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, and let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be graphs that are generalized cospectral with
G
. Let
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
be the rational orthogonal matrices satisfying
Q_{i}^{T}A(G)Q_{i}=A(H_{i})
and
Q_{i}e=e
for
i=1,2
. If
\ell(Q_{1})=\ell(Q_{2})
, then
H_{1}\cong H_{2}
.

We will later establish the existence of the orthogonal matrices referenced in Theorem
1
. This theorem shows that, for graphs in
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, each possible level of a rational orthogonal matrix yields at most one generalized cospectral mate. Consequently, the number of generalized cospectral mates is bounded above by the number of possible levels:

Theorem 2
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Let
d_{n}(W(G))=\prod_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}^{k_{j}}
be the prime factorization of the last invariant factor of the walk matrix
W(G)
. Then
G
can have at most
\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)-1
generalized cospectral mates.

We begin by reviewing essential preliminaries in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we investigate the properties of a specific set of orthogonal matrices; these properties establish the framework for proving our main results in Section
4
. We then provide illustrative examples and numerical analysis in Section
5
to demonstrate the application of our results. Section
6
concludes the paper with a summary of our findings and potential directions for future research.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we review the essential results required for the proofs of Theorem
1
and
2
. A matrix
Q
is called
regular
if
Qe=e
where
e
is the all-ones column vector. The following theorem characterizes graphs sharing the same generalized spectrum:

Theorem 3
.

[
13
]
Let
G
be a controllable graph and
H
be a graph generalized cospectral with
G
. Then
H
must be controllable and there exists a unique regular rational orthogonal matrix
Q
such that:

Q^{T}A(G)Q=A(H),

(2)

Moreover, the unique matrix
Q
satisfies
Q=W(G)W(H)^{-1}
.

Motivated by this theorem, we consider the set of orthogonal matrices that generate a valid adjacency matrix. Let
O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
denote the set of regular orthogonal matrices of order
n
with rational entries. We define:

\Gamma(G)=\{Q\in O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\mid Q^{T}A(G)Q=A(H)\text{ for graph }H\},

The level of a matrix
Q\in\Gamma(G)
provides a crucial link between spectral properties and graph isomorphism. Specifically, if
\ell(Q)=1
, then
Q
is an integral orthogonal matrix. Since
Q
is also regular, it must be a permutation matrix. Consequently, if Eq. (
2
) holds with a matrix of level one, the graphs
G
and
H
are isomorphic. Therefore, generalized cospectral mates arise only when
\ell(Q)>1
. The next result characterizes the relationship between rational orthogonal matrices that generate isomorphic graphs.

Lemma 4
.

[
8
,
14
]

Let
G
be a graph, and let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be graphs generalized cospectral with
G
. Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\Gamma(G)
be the corresponding rational orthogonal matrices such that
Q_{i}^{T}A(G)Q_{i}=A(H_{i})
and
Q_{i}e=e
for
i=1,2
. If
Q_{2}=Q_{1}P
, where
P
is a permutation matrix, then
H_{1}\cong H_{2}
.

In this paper, our strategy is to demonstrate that if two matrices in
\Gamma(G)
have the same level, they must differ by only by column permutations. The following lemma provides a simple criterion for bounding the level of a rational matrix based on modular congruences.

Lemma 5
.

[
8
]

Let
Q
be rational matrix, and let
x
and
k
be positive integers. If
xQ
is an integral matrix and
xQ\equiv 0\pmod{k}
, then
\ell(Q)\mid\frac{x}{k}
.

An important tool in this context is the Smith Normal Form (SNF) of integral matrices. An integral matrix
V
of order
n
is called
unimodular
if
\det{V}=\pm 1
. For a prime
p
, we denote the finite field of order
p
by
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and the rank of an integral matrix
M
over this field by
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)
.

Theorem 6
.

For a full-rank integral matrix
M
, there exist unimodular matrices
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
such that
M=V_{1}SV_{2}
, where
S=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1}(M),d_{2}(M),\ldots,d_{n}(M))
is the SNF of
M
, and the invariant factors satisfy
d_{i}(M)\mid d_{i+1}(M)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
.

The determinant of an integral
M
can thus be written as

\det{M}=\pm\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}(M).

(3)

For a prime
p
,
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)
is equal to the number of invariant factors of
M
not divisible by
p
. Consequently, the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
implies that for any
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, we have
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(W(G))=n-1
for every odd prime
p
dividing
d_{n}(W(G))
, and
\operatorname{rank}_{2}(W(G))\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. The following lemma provides a general upper bound for the rank of the walk matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Lemma 7
.

[
11
]

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices. Then
\operatorname{rank}_{2}(W(G))\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
.

Combining Lemma
7
with the lower bound derived above, we conclude that
\operatorname{rank}_{2}(W(G))=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
for every
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. The next lemma establishes a connection between the level of a rational orthogonal matrix and the last invariant factors of the integral matrices it relates.

Lemma 8
.

[
7
]

Let
X
and
Y
be two non-singular integral matrices such that
QX=Y
, where
Q
is a rational orthogonal matrix. Then
\ell(Q)
divides the greatest common divisor of
d_{n}(X)
and
d_{n}(Y)
, where
d_{n}(X)
and
d_{n}(Y)
are the
n
-th invariant factors of
X
and
Y
, respectively.

For any matrix
Q\in\Gamma(G)
, we have
Q^{T}W(G)=W(H)
for some graph
H
by Theorem
3
. It follows that
\ell(Q)
must divide
\operatorname{gcd}(d_{n}(W(G)),d_{n}(W(H)))
, and therefore it must divide
d_{n}(W(G))
. However, certain arithmetic properties of
W(G)
can restrict which divisors of
d_{n}(W(G))
are actually attainable as levels for matrices in
\Gamma(G)
.

For integers
m
,
k
, and
n
(with
n\neq 0
), we write
m^{k}\parallel n
to indicate that
m^{k}
divides
n
exactly; that is,
m^{k}\mid n
but
m^{k+1}\nmid n
. The unique integer
k
satisfying this condition is denoted by
\operatorname{ord}_{m}(n)
. The following lemma establishes that the highest power of an odd prime dividing
d_{n}(W(G))
cannot divide
\ell(Q)
.

Lemma 9
.

[
7
]

Let
G
be a graph, let
Q\in\Gamma(G)
and let
p
be an odd prime. If
p^{k}\parallel\det{W(G)}
and
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(W(G))=n-1
, then
p^{k}\nmid\ell(Q)
or equivalently,
\ell(Q)=\frac{d_{n}(W(G))}{p}
.

3
Arithmetic Constraints on Matrices in
O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})

In this section, we establish several properties of the matrices in
O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
that are necessary for the proof of Theorem
1
. We begin by identifying conditions under which the solution space of a linear system modulo
p^{k}
is one-dimensional.

Lemma 10
.

Let
M
be a full-rank integral matrix of order
n
. If a prime
p
and integer
k\geq 1
satisfy
p^{k}\mid d_{n}(M)
and
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)=n-1
, then every solution to
Mv\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
is a scalar multiple of a fixed vector
w\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
M=V_{1}SV_{2}
be the SNF of
M
. Since
V_{1}
is unimodular, it is invertible over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, the solutions to
Mv\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
are equivalent to the solutions of

SV_{2}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}.

Let
v^{\prime}=V_{2}v
. The system becomes
Sv^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
.

Since
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)=n-1
, it follows that
p\nmid d_{i}(M)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
. Thus, the first
n-1
entries of
v^{\prime}
must be congruent to zero modulo
p^{k}
. The last entry of
v^{\prime}
, however, can take any value in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, the solutions to
Sv^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
are of the form

v^{\prime}\equiv yw^{\prime}\pmod{p^{k}}.

where
y
is any integer in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
and
w^{\prime}=[0,\dots,0,1]^{T}
.

Since
V_{2}
is unimodular, the inverse
V_{2}^{-1}
exists and is also unimodular. Multiplying both sides of the relation
v^{\prime}=V_{2}v
by
V_{2}^{-1}
, we obtain
v=V_{2}^{-1}v^{\prime}
. Substituting the solution
v^{\prime}\equiv yw^{\prime}\pmod{p^{k}}
, we obtain:

v\equiv yV_{2}^{-1}w^{\prime}\pmod{p^{k}}.

Define
w=V_{2}^{-1}w^{\prime}
. Note that
w
is the last column of the matrix
V_{2}^{-1}
. We claim that
w\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. If
w\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, then every entry in the last column of
V_{2}^{-1}
would be divisible by
p
. Consequently, the determinant of
V_{2}^{-1}
would be divisible by
p
. This contradicts the fact that
\det(V_{2}^{-1})=\pm 1
. Hence,
w\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, and every solution
v
to
Mv\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
is a scalar multiple of
w
over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

The following lemmas establish that if vectors satisfying specific constraints are scalar multiples over the ring
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, then their inner product must be congruent to zero modulo
p^{2k}
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
u
and
v
be
n
-dimensional integer vectors, and let
p
be an odd prime and
k
be a positive integer. If the following conditions hold:

1.

u\equiv\alpha v\pmod{p^{k}}
where
\alpha
is an integer not divisible by
p
,

2.

u^{T}u\equiv v^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
,

then
u^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
.

Proof.

From condition (1), we have:

u-\alpha v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}},

Taking the inner product of both sides with itself gives

(u-\alpha v)^{T}(u-\alpha v)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}},

which expands to

u^{T}u-2\alpha u^{T}v+\alpha^{2}v^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}.

Since
u^{T}u\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
and
v^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
, we have

-2\alpha u^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}.

As
p
is odd and
\alpha
is not divisible by
p
, it follows that
2\alpha
is invertible in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2k}\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
u^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}.

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
u
and
v
be
n
-dimensional integer vectors, and let
k
be a positive integer. If the following conditions hold:

1.

u\equiv\alpha v\pmod{2^{k}}
where
\alpha
is an odd integer,

2.

\operatorname{ord}_{2}(u^{T}u)=\operatorname{ord}_{2}(v^{T}v)=2k
,

3.

\operatorname{ord}_{2}(u^{T}e)=\operatorname{ord}_{2}(v^{T}e)=k
,

then
u^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{2^{2k}}
.

Proof.

Based upon our assumptions, we have: (i)
u^{T}u=2^{2k}a
, (ii)
v^{T}v=2^{2k}b
. (iii)
u^{T}e=2^{k}c
, (iv)
v^{T}e=2^{k}d
, where
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
are odd integers. From condition (1), we have

u-\alpha v=2^{k}m,

(4)

for some integer vector
m
. Multiplying each side by
e^{T}
gives

e^{T}u-\alpha e^{T}v=2^{k}e^{T}m.

Substituting the values of
e^{T}u
and
e^{T}v
gives

2^{k}c-\alpha(2^{k}d)=2^{k}e^{T}m,

which simplifies to

c-\alpha d=e^{T}m.

Note that since
c
and
\alpha d
are both odd, their difference
c-\alpha d
is even. Thus,
e^{T}m
is even. Moreover, we can express
m^{T}m
as

\displaystyle m^{T}m

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv e^{T}m\pmod{2},

where
m_{i}
is the
i^{\text{th}}
entry of
m
. Since
e^{T}m
is even, it follows that
m^{T}m
is also even.

Now, taking the inner product of both sides of Eq. (
4
) with itself gives:

\displaystyle(u-\alpha v)^{T}(u-\alpha v)

\displaystyle=(2^{k}m)^{T}(2^{k}m)

\displaystyle u^{T}u-2\alpha u^{T}v+\alpha^{2}v^{T}v

\displaystyle=2^{2k}m^{T}m.

Substituting the values of
u^{T}u
and
v^{T}v
gives:

\displaystyle 2^{2k}a-2\alpha u^{T}v+\alpha^{2}(2^{2k}b)

\displaystyle=2^{2k}m^{T}m

\displaystyle-2\alpha u^{T}v

\displaystyle=2^{2k}(m^{T}m-(a+\alpha^{2}b))

\displaystyle-\alpha u^{T}v

\displaystyle=2^{2k-1}(m^{T}m-(a+\alpha^{2}b)).

Note that since
a
and
\alpha^{2}b
are both odd, their sum
a+\alpha^{2}b
is even. Since
m^{T}m
is also even, it follows that
m^{T}m-(a+\alpha^{2}b)
is even. Thus, we conclude

-\alpha u^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{2^{2k}}.

Since
\alpha
is odd, it is invertible in
\mathbb{Z}/2^{2k}\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
u^{T}v\equiv 0\pmod{2^{2k}}
.
∎

In the next lemma, we explore the properties of the column vectors of matrices in
O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})

Lemma 13
.

Let
Q\in O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
,
\bar{Q}=\ell(Q)Q
and
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
be the column vectors of
\bar{Q}
. Let
p
be a prime factor of
\ell(Q)
and let
k=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\ell(Q))
. Then:

1.

v_{i}^{T}v_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
for all
i,j
;

2.

p^{2k}\parallel v_{i}^{T}v_{i}
for all
i
;

3.

p^{k}\parallel v_{i}^{T}e
for all
i
.

Proof.

Let
\ell=\ell(Q)
. Since
Q
is orthogonal,
Q^{T}Q=I
, and therefore
\bar{Q}^{T}\bar{Q}=(\ell Q)^{T}(\ell Q)=\ell^{2}I.
As the
(i,j)
-th entry of
\bar{Q}^{T}\bar{Q}
is
v_{i}^{T}v_{j}
, it follows that

v_{i}^{T}v_{j}=\begin{cases}\ell^{2}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Since
k=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\ell)
, we have
p^{k}\parallel\ell
and thus
p^{2k}\parallel\ell^{2}
. It follows that
v_{i}^{T}v_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
for all
i,j
while
p^{2k}\parallel v_{i}^{T}v_{i}
for all
i
. This proves (1) and (2).

To prove (3), since
Q
is regular and orthogonal, we have
Q^{T}e=e
. Multiplying by
\ell
gives
\bar{Q}^{T}e=\ell e.

The
i^{\text{th}}
component of
\bar{Q}^{T}e
is
v_{i}^{T}e=\ell
. Since
p^{k}\parallel\ell
, we conclude that
p^{k}\parallel v_{i}^{T}e
.
∎

We now prove that if the columns of two matrices in
O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
are scalar multiples of a common vector over the ring
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, then
p
does not divide the level of their product.

Lemma 14
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
and let
p
be a prime. Let
\bar{Q}_{1}=\ell(Q_{1})Q_{1}
and
\bar{Q}_{2}=\ell(Q_{2})Q_{2}
. If the following conditions hold:

1.

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\ell(Q_{1}))=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\ell(Q_{2}))=k
, for some integer
k\geq 1
,

2.

The column vectors of both
\bar{Q}_{1}
and
\bar{Q}_{2}
are scalar multiples of a common vector
w\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
,

then
p\nmid\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
R=\bar{Q}_{1}^{T}\bar{Q}_{2}
be an integral matrix with entries
r_{ij}=u_{i}^{T}v_{j}
, where
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
denote the
i^{\text{th}}
and
j^{\text{th}}
columns of
\bar{Q}_{1}
and
\bar{Q}_{2}
, respectively. We show that
r_{ij}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
for all
i,j
.

By the definition of level, there exist column vectors
u^{\prime}
of
\bar{Q}_{1}
and
v^{\prime}
of
\bar{Q}_{2}
such that
u^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and
v^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Since
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
are both scalar multiples of
w
over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, there exists an integer
\alpha
not divisible by
p
and an integer vector

such that:

u^{\prime}\equiv\alpha v^{\prime}+p^{k}z\pmod{p^{2k}},

(5)

Furthermore, since every column vector
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
is a scalar multiple of
w
over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, we can express them as:

u_{i}\equiv c_{i}u^{\prime}+p^{k}x_{i}\pmod{p^{2k}},

v_{j}\equiv d_{j}v^{\prime}+p^{k}y_{j}\pmod{p^{2k}},

where
c_{i},d_{j}
are integers and
x_{i},y_{j}
are integer vectors.

Since
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
13
, we have that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(u^{\prime T}u^{\prime})=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(v^{\prime T}v^{\prime})=2k
and
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(u^{\prime T}e)=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(v^{\prime T}e)=k
. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
11
(for odd primes) or Lemma
12
(for
p=2
) to establish that
u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
. Multiplying Eq. (
5
) by
u^{\prime T}
yields:

\displaystyle u^{\prime T}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle\equiv u^{\prime T}(\alpha v^{\prime}+p^{k}z)

\displaystyle\equiv\alpha u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}+p^{k}u^{\prime T}z\pmod{p^{2k}}.

Since
u^{\prime T}u^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
and
u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
, it follows that
p^{k}u^{\prime T}z\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
, which implies
u^{\prime T}z\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
.

Next, we examine the product
u_{i}^{T}u^{\prime}
. Substituting the expression for
u_{i}
and
u^{\prime}
gives:

\displaystyle u_{i}^{T}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle\equiv(c_{i}u^{\prime}+p^{k}x_{i})^{T}(\alpha v^{\prime}+p^{k}z)

\displaystyle\equiv c_{i}\alpha u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}+c_{i}p^{k}u^{\prime T}z+\alpha p^{k}x_{i}^{T}v^{\prime}+p^{2k}x_{i}^{T}z\pmod{p^{2k}}.

Note that
u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
. Since
u^{\prime T}z\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
, the term
c_{i}p^{k}u^{\prime T}z
is divisible by
p^{2k}
. Furthermore, from Lemma
13
, we know
u_{i}^{T}u^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
. Thus, we get:

0\equiv\alpha p^{k}x_{i}^{T}v^{\prime}\pmod{p^{2k}}.

Since
\alpha\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, we conclude
x_{i}^{T}v^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
. By a similar argument, we can prove that
y_{j}^{T}u^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
. Finally, for any columns
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
, we have:

\displaystyle u_{i}^{T}v_{j}

\displaystyle\equiv(c_{i}u^{\prime}+p^{k}x_{i})^{T}(d_{j}v^{\prime}+p^{k}y_{j})

\displaystyle\equiv c_{i}d_{j}u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}+c_{i}p^{k}u^{\prime T}y_{j}+d_{j}p^{k}x_{i}^{T}v^{\prime}+p^{2k}x_{i}^{T}y_{j}\pmod{p^{2k}}.

We established that
u^{\prime T}y_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
and
x_{i}^{T}v^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
. Since
u^{\prime T}v^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
, every term on the right-hand side vanishes modulo
p^{2k}
. Thus, we conclude that
u_{i}^{T}v_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
for all
i,j
, which implies
R\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
. Substituting the definition of
R
, we have

\ell(Q_{1})\ell(Q_{2})Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}.

As
Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}
is a rational matrix, by Lemma
5
, it follows that
\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})\mid\frac{\ell(Q_{1})\ell(Q_{2})}{p^{2k}}
. Since
p^{k}\parallel\ell(Q_{1})
and
p^{k}\parallel\ell(Q_{2})
, it follows that
p^{2k}\parallel\ell(Q_{1})\ell(Q_{2})
. Consequently,
p\nmid\frac{\ell(Q_{1})\ell(Q_{2})}{p^{2k}}
, which implies
p\nmid\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})
, completing the proof.
∎

Building up on this, the following lemma establishes that if matrices in
O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
having the same level satisfy certain conditions, then their product must be a permutation matrix.

Lemma 15
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
such that
\ell(Q_{1})=\ell(Q_{2})=\ell
, and let
\bar{Q}_{1}=\ell Q_{1}
and let
\bar{Q}_{2}=\ell Q_{2}
. Let

\ell=\prod_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}^{k_{j}}

be the prime factorization of
\ell
. Suppose that for each
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, there exists a vector
w_{j}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p_{j}}
such that columns of both
\bar{Q}_{1}
and
\bar{Q}_{2}
is a scalar multiple of
w_{j}
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}/p_{j}^{k_{j}}\mathbb{Z}
. Then
Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}
is a permutation matrix.

Proof.

Since
\bar{Q}_{1}
and
\bar{Q}_{2}
are integral, the product
\bar{Q}_{1}^{T}\bar{Q}_{2}=\ell^{2}Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}
is an integral matrix. It follows from the definition of level that
\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})
divides
\ell^{2}
.For each prime factor
p_{j}
of
\ell
, the assumptions satisfy the conditions of Lemma
14
with
k=k_{j}
. Thus,
p_{j}\nmid\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})
. Since
\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})
divides
\ell^{2}
but contains no prime factor of
\ell
, it must be that
\ell(Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2})=1
. Therefore,
Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}
is an integral orthogonal matrix.

Finally, since
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, it follows that
Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}\in O_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
. An integral orthogonal matrix with row sums equal to 1 must be non-negative, which implies
Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}
is a permutation matrix.
∎

We begin with defining some auxiliary matrices that are critical for the proof of Theorem

:

Lemma 16
.

[
11
]

Let
\phi(x)=x^{n}+c_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{n-1}x+c_{n}
be the characteristic polynomial of a graph
G
with adjacency matrix
A=A(G)
. Define

M(G):=\begin{cases}A^{n/2}+c_{2}A^{n/2-1}+\cdots+c_{n-2}A+c_{n}I,&\text{if $n$ is even},\\

Then
M(G)e\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, where

is the all-ones vector.

Using this result, the integral matrix
\hat{W}(G)
is defined as follows:

Definition 2
.

Let
G
be a graph with the adjacency matrix
A=A(G)
and the matrix
M=M(G)
.
\hat{W}(G)
is defined as:

\hat{W}(G):=\left[\,e,\,Ae,\,\ldots,\,A^{\lceil n/2\rceil}e,\,\frac{Me}{2},\,\frac{AMe}{2},\,\ldots,\,\frac{A^{n-\lceil n/2\rceil-1}Me}{2}\,\right].

As observed by Qiu et al.
[
7
]
, if
H
is generalized cospectral to
G
and
Q\in\Gamma(G)
such that
Q^{T}A(G)Q=A(H)
, then
Q^{T}M(G)e=M(H)e
and consequently,
Q^{T}\hat{W}(G)=\hat{W}(H)
. The following lemma relates the SNF of
\hat{W}(G)
to that of the original walk matrix
W(G)
.

Lemma 17
.

[
7
]

Let
G
be a graph with walk matrix
W(G)
. If
\operatorname{rank}_{2}{W(G)}=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the invariant factors of
\hat{W}(G)
satisfy:

d_{i}(\hat{W}(G))=\begin{cases}d_{i}(W(G))&\text{if }1\leq i\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil,\\
\frac{d_{i}(W(G))}{2}&\text{if }\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil<i\leq n.\end{cases}

The next lemma establishes the
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\hat{W}(G))
for every prime factor
p
of
d_{n}(\hat{W}(G))
when
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Then, for every prime factor
p
of
d_{n}(\hat{W}(G))
, we have
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\hat{W}(G))=n-1
.

Proof.

As
G\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
, we established earlier that
\operatorname{rank}_{2}(W(G))=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Therefore, by Lemma
17
, the invariant factors of
\hat{W}(G)
are given by:

d_{i}(\hat{W}(G))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }1\leq i\leq n-1,\\
\frac{d_{n}(W(G))}{2}&\text{if }i=n.\end{cases}

It follows that
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(\hat{W}(G))=n-1
for every prime
p
dividing
d_{n}(\hat{W}(G))
. This completes the proof.
∎

We can now proceed to the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\bar{Q}_{i}=\ell Q_{i}
for
i=1,2
be the associated integral matrices. Let the prime factorization of
\ell
be given by

\ell=\prod_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}^{k_{j}}.

Since
Q_{i}^{T}\hat{W}(G)=\hat{W}(H_{i})
, transposing the equation gives
\hat{W}(G)^{T}Q_{i}=\hat{W}(H_{i})^{T}
. Multiplying by
\ell
, we obtain
\hat{W}(G)^{T}\bar{Q}_{i}=\ell\hat{W}(H_{i})^{T}
. As
\hat{W}(H)
is an integral matrix and
\ell\equiv 0\pmod{p_{j}^{k_{j}}}
for each prime
p_{j}
dividing
\ell
, it follows that
\hat{W}(G)^{T}\bar{Q}_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p_{j}^{k_{j}}}.
By Lemma
18
, we have
\operatorname{rank}_{p_{j}}(\hat{W}(G)^{T})=\operatorname{rank}_{p_{j}}(\hat{W}(G))=n-1
.

Consequently, for each
p_{j}
, the columns of
\bar{Q}_{i}
are the solutions to a linear system satisfying the conditions of Lemma
10
. Hence, there exists a vector
w_{j}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p_{j}}
such that the columns of both
\bar{Q}_{1}
and
\bar{Q}_{2}
are scalar multiples of
w_{j}

\mathbb{Z}/p_{j}^{k_{j}}\mathbb{Z}
.

The conditions of Lemma
15
are therefore satisfied, implying that
Q_{1}^{T}Q_{2}=P
for some permutation matrix
P
. It follows that
Q_{2}=Q_{1}P
. By Lemma
4
, we conclude that
H_{1}

.
∎

The proof of Theorem
2
follows as a direct consequence of Theorem

by counting the possible levels of matrices in
\Gamma(G)
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

As 2 must be a factor of
d_{n}(W(G))
, let the prime factorization of
d_{n}(W(G))
be written as
2^{k_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{m}p_{j}^{k_{j}}
, where
p_{1}=2
and
p_{2},\dots,p_{m}
are odd primes. Consider a matrix
Q\in\Gamma(G)
. Since
Q^{T}\hat{W}(G)=\hat{W}(H)
for some graph
H
generalized cospectral to
G
, Lemma
8
implies that
\ell(Q)\mid d_{n}(\hat{W}(G))
. By Lemma
17
, we have
d_{n}(\hat{W}(G))=\frac{d_{n}(W(G))}{2}
. Consequently, the multiplicity of the factor 2 in

is
k_{1}-1
, which implies that
2^{k_{1}}\nmid\ell(Q)
.

Moreover, for each odd prime

, we know that

We must exclude the trivial case of the level

. By Theorem

, each valid level corresponds to at most one generalized cospectral mate of

|L(G)|-1=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)-1

This completes the proof.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have addressed the problem of determining the number of generalized cospectral mates for a graph by analyzing the arithmetic properties of the walk matrix. We identified a significant family of controllable graphs, denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, and established a concrete upper bound on the number of their generalized cospectral mates.

By leveraging the Smith Normal Form of the walk matrix and the level of rational orthogonal matrices, we proved that distinct levels yield non-isomorphic mates, allowing us to bound the number of mates based on the prime factorization of the last invariant factor of
W(G)
. Our numerical experiments demonstrate that
\mathcal{F}_{n}
encompasses a substantial proportion of random graphs, approximately
40\%
as
n
increases, and we provided a tight example where the derived bound is attained.

Several avenues for future research emerge from this work. First, the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
relies on specific constraints on the invariant factors, namely that
d_{\lceil n/2\rceil}=1
and
d_{n-1}=2
. A natural progression would be to relax these conditions to cover a broader range of controllable graphs. Generalizing the modular constraints on the rational orthogonal matrices could extend the applicability of these bounds to families where the rank of the walk matrix over finite fields behaves differently.

Furthermore, the algebraic techniques developed here need not be limited to the adjacency matrix. Future investigations could explore establishing similar upper bounds in other settings, such as using the signless Laplacian or the distance matrix. Finally, while this study focused on the walk matrix generated by the all-ones vector
e
, employing different control input vectors could reveal new structural insights. This approach might facilitate the determination of generalized cospectrality for graphs that are not controllable with respect to
e
but may be controllable with respect to other vectors.

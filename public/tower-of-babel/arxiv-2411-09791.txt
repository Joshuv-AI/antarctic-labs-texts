Generating strongly 2 2 -connected digraphs
Meike Hatzel Discrete Mathematics Group, Institute for Basic Science (IBS), Daejeon, South Korea Stephan Kreutzer Technical University of Berlin, Germany Evangelos Protopapas LIRMM, University of Montpellier, CNRS, Montpellier, France Florian Reich University of Hamburg, Germany Giannos Stamoulis Institute of Informatics, University of Warsaw, Poland Sebastian Wiederrecht School of Computing, KAIST, Daejeon, South Korea

Abstract
We prove that there exist four operations such that given any two strongly 2 2 -connected digraphs H H and D D where H H is a butterfly-minor of D D , there exists a sequence D 0 , … , D n D_{0},\dots,D_{n} where D 0 = H D_{0}=H , D n = D D_{n}=D and for every 0 ≤ i ≤ n − 1 0\leq i\leq n-1 , D i D_{i} is a strongly 2 2 -connected butterfly-minor of D i + 1 D_{i+1} which is obtained by a single application of one of the four operations. As a consequence of this theorem, we obtain that every strongly 2 2 -connected digraph can be generated from a concise family of strongly 2 2 -connected digraphs by using these four operations. Keywords. braces, strongly 2 2 -connected digraphs, butterfly-minor, splitter theorem

Introduction

Generation theorems for graphs respecting certain containment relations and connectivity conditions have played a central role in the study of said containment relations.
Among the best-known results in this direction is the
ear decomposition
of
2
-connected graphs
[
Whi32
]
.
Given a graph
G
and a subgraph
H\subseteq G,
we say that a path
P\subseteq G
is an
H
-ear
in
G
if it shares exactly its first and last vertex with
H
and is otherwise disjoint from
H
.
Whitney’s theorem on ear-decompositions says that for every pair of
2
-connected graphs
H
and
G
, where
H
is a proper subgraph of
G
, there exists a sequence

G_{0}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq G_{\ell}

such that
G_{0}=H
,
G_{\ell}=G
, and, for all
i\in[\ell]
, the graph
G_{i}
is obtained from the graph
G_{i-1}
by adding a single
G_{i-1}
-ear.

The consequences of this theorem are two-fold.
The first is that every
2
-connected subgraph of a
2
-connected graph can be reached by iteratively removing paths while maintaining
2
-connectivity for all intermediate graphs.
The second one is the generative result.
Since every
2
-connected graph contains a cycle as a subgraph and every proper subgraph of a cycle is no longer
2
-connected, the cycles represent a minimal, or basic, family of
2
-connected graphs.
Moreover, Whitney’s theorem asserts that every
2
-connected graph can be constructed by starting with a cycle and iteratively adding ears.

It is a straightforward observation that Whitney’s theorem also holds for digraphs.
Given a strongly connected digraph
H
, we say that a directed path
P
is a
directed
H
-ear
if
P
is an
H
-ear in the underlying undirected graph.
We say that the graph
D\coloneqq H\cup P
is obtained from
H
by a
directed-ear-addition
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{1}
be the set of all directed cycles and
\mathcal{D}_{1}
be the class of all digraphs that can be generated from members of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
by a sequence of directed-ear-additions.
By using Whitney’s theorem, one may observe that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is exactly the class of strongly connected digraphs.

A possible reformulation of both results, the directed and the undirected one, can be stated using the
topological minor relation
.
A (di)graph
D
is a
subdivision
of a (di)graph
H
if it is isomorphic to a (di)graph
H^{\prime}
which is obtained from
H
by replacing the edges of
H
with (directed) paths of length at least one that are internally vertex-disjoint from
H
, and, in case
H
is a digraph, are directed in the same way as the edges they replace.
If
D
is a subdivision of
H
with exactly one additional vertex, then we say that
D
is obtained by
subdividing an edge
of
H
.
Since subdividing edges clearly preserves
2
-connectivity (and strong connectivity for digraphs), a reformulation of Whitney’s theorem states that

1.

every
2
-connected graph can be obtained from
K_{3}
by a sequence of edge-subdivisions and ear-additions, and

2.

every strongly connected digraph can be obtained from the directed two-cycle by a sequence of edge-subdivisions and ear-additions.

What about higher notions of connectivity?
Let
H
be a
3
-connected graph and
G
be some graph with a minimum degree of at least three such that there exists an edge
e\in E(G)
for which
G-e
is a subdivision of
H
.
In this case, we say that
G
is obtained from
H
by a
3
-augmentation
.
It was proven by Barnette and Grünbaum and, independently, Titov in their PhD thesis
[
BG69
,
Tit75
]
that the class of
3
-connected graphs is exactly the class of graphs that can be generated from

by sequences of
3
-augmentations.
Notice that if
G
can be obtained from a
3
-connected graph
H
by a sequence of
3
-augmentations, then
H
must be a minor of
G
.

Let
H
be a graph and

such that
N(x)
is the disjoint union of two sets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, both of size at least two.
We say that the graph

G\coloneqq(H-x)+x_{1}+x_{2}+\{x_{1},x_{2}\}+\{\{x_{i},y\}\mid\text{for all }i\in[2]\text{ and all }y\in X_{i}\}

is obtained from
H
by
3
-expansion
.
If
G
contains an edge
e\in E(G)
such that
G-e=H

is obtained from

.

A seminal result by Tutte
[
Tut61
]
states that the class of all graphs that can be generated from the set of wheels by sequences of
3
-expansions and edge-additions is exactly the set of
3
-connected graphs.
Seymour
[
Sey80
]
strengthened Tutte’s generation theorem by proving that, if
G
and
H
are 3-connected graphs such that
H
is a proper minor of
G
, then there exists a 3-connected graph
K
such that
K
is a minor of
G
and one of the following statements holds:

•

K
can be obtained from
H
by 3-expansion or edge addition, or

•

H
and
K
are wheels and
|V(H)|+1=|V(K)|
.

Global existence of smooth solution to evolutionary Faddeev model with short-pulse data
Shaoying Luo, Jinhua Wang, Changhua Wei

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the Cauchy problem of the evolutionary Faddeev model, a system that maps from the Minkowski space ℝ 1 + 3 \mathbb{R}^{1+3} to the unit sphere 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} . The model is a system of nonlinear wave equations whose nonlinearities exhibit a null structure and include semilinear terms, quasilinear terms, and the unknowns themselves. By considering a class of large initial data (in energy norm) of the short pulse type, we prove that the evolutionary Faddeev model admits a globally smooth solution via energy estimates. The main result is achieved through the selection of appropriate multipliers that are specially adapted to the geometry of the system.



Key words and phrases
: Evolutionary Faddeev model; global solution; short-pulse; multipliers; energy estimates.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
: 35L40, 35L65.

In quantum field theory, the Faddeev model

Denote an arbitrary point in

The associated Euler–Lagrange equations of motion are

\mathbf{n}\wedge\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{n}-\left(\partial_{\alpha}[\mathbf{n}\cdot(\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{n}\wedge\partial^{\beta}\mathbf{n})]\right)\partial_{\beta}\mathbf{n}=0.

1.2.
Strategy of the Proof

Due to the finite speed of propagation of wave equations, (
1.6

1.2.1.
Multipliers adapted to the Faddeev geometry

In the large data region II, our principal strategy for proving global existence mainly depends on energy estimates. The selection of suitable multipliers is important, as it significantly simplifies our calculations. Specifically, in the context of the Faddeev model—a system of quasilinear wave equations—the multipliers
L
and

\underline{L}
must be chosen with great care. Their role is to generate energies of a definite sign, which is essential for effectively managing the intrinsic complexities of the system’s dynamics.

Our choice of multipliers is inspired by
[
36
]
. We rewrite the first equation of (
1.6
) as

\displaystyle\,\Box\theta-\cos^{2}\theta\big(\partial^{\alpha}\phi\,\partial^{\beta}\phi-g^{\alpha\beta}Q(\phi,\phi)\big)\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\theta-\cos^{2}\theta\big(g^{\alpha\beta}Q(\theta,\phi)-\partial^{\alpha}\theta\,\partial^{\beta}\phi\big)\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\,Q(\phi,\phi)+\frac{1}{4}\sin(2\theta)\,Q^{\alpha\beta}(\theta,\phi)Q_{\alpha\beta}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle+\,\cos^{2}\theta\Big((\,\partial^{\alpha}\theta\,\partial^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\theta\,-\partial^{\beta}\theta\,\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\theta)\partial_{\alpha}\phi+(\,\partial^{\beta}\theta\,\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\phi\,-\partial^{\alpha}\theta\,\partial^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\phi)\partial_{\alpha}\theta\Big).

(1.10)

In order to find suitable multipliers, we can rewrite (
1.2.1
) as a geometric wave equation

\Box_{g(\partial\phi)}\theta=N(\theta,\phi)

where

g^{\alpha\beta}(\partial\phi)=g^{\alpha\beta}-\cos^{2}\theta\big(\partial^{\alpha}\phi\,\partial^{\beta}\phi-g^{\alpha\beta}Q(\phi,\phi)\big).

(1.11)

In the subsequent discussion, we refer to this as the geometric aspect of the Faddeev model. The natural selection for the multipliers is
(-2D\underline{u},-2Du)
, which are null vectors and are defined by

-2D\underline{u}=-2g^{\alpha\beta}(\partial\phi)\partial_{\alpha}\underline{u}\,\partial_{\beta},\quad-2Du=-2g^{\alpha\beta}(\partial\phi)\partial_{\alpha}u\,\partial_{\beta}.

However, the aforementioned multipliers introduce a multitude of additional higher-order terms, complicating the analysis. To streamline the computations, we opt to use the leading parts of the expressions
-2D\underline{u}
and
-2Du
, which are
L+(L\phi)^{2}\underline{L}
and
\underline{L}+(\underline{L}\phi)^{2}L
as our multipliers. We can verify that these modified multipliers are causal with respect to
g(\partial\phi)
, which is crucial for ensuring the positivity of the energies we aim to derive. This approach maintains the causal structure while simplifying the energy estimates.

By employing these leading parts as multipliers, we can perform a similar analysis on the equations that
\phi
satisfies, leveraging the causality and positivity properties to our advantage. This methodological choice not only simplifies the algebraic complexity but also provides a clearer pathway to establishing the global well-posedness of the solutions in the context of the Faddeev model.

Unlike the scalar wave equation previously discussed, the Faddeev model consists of a system of coupled wave equations. The cross terms, such as
-\cos^{2}\theta\big(g^{\alpha\beta}Q(\theta,\phi)-\partial^{\alpha}\theta\,\partial^{\beta}\phi\big)\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\phi
also contain the highest order derivatives, which pose anther challenge in the analysis. To achieve a certain symmetry that can help cancel out these highest order derivatives of order

k+2
, we will employ the following two modified multiplier vector fields

\tilde{L}=L+((L\theta)^{2}+(L\phi)^{2})\underline{L},\quad\tilde{\underline{L}}=\underline{L}+((\underline{L}\theta)^{2}+(\underline{L}\phi)^{2})L.

The selection of these multiplier vector fields is strategic, aiming to exploit the symmetries and structures within the Faddeev model to our advantage. By carefully constructing these multipliers, we can effectively manage the complexity introduced by the cross terms and the highest order derivatives, which is essential for proving global existence of smooth solutions in the short-pulse region II.

1.2.2.
A globally small solution in region I

Based on the energy estimates in region II, we first demonstrate that the incoming energy is small. Leveraging the good derivatives of the solution, which exhibit smallness and better decay, we have

\big|\overline{\partial}(\partial^{l}\Gamma^{k-l}(\theta,\phi))\big|_{L^{\infty}(C_{\delta})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}|\underline{u}|^{-2},\quad 0\leq l\leq k,

and thanks to the zero initial data assumption on
S_{\delta,1-\delta}
,
the property of the bad derivatives of the solution can be improved to

\big|\underline{L}(\partial^{l}\Gamma^{k-l}(\theta,\phi))\big|_{L^{\infty}(C_{\delta})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}|\underline{u}|^{-1},\quad 0\leq l\leq k

by integrating the whole system in terms of the null coordinates along the direction
L
on
C_{\delta}
.

Second, building on the favorable smallness and decay properties of the solution on
C_{\delta}
, we address the initial boundary value (Goursat) problem in region I. Given that the problem is inherently small, we can partition the space-time domain by hypersurfaces
\{t=const.\}
. By selecting
\partial_{t}

as the multiplier and utilizing the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality, the null structure of the Faddeev model, and the smallness of the solution on the boundary, we are able to derive global energy estimates.

For any Lorentz field

(2.8)

where

The following lemma describes the good properties of null forms.

Lemma 2.4
.

For the null forms, we have

|Q(\xi,\chi)|\lesssim|L\xi|\,|\underline{L}\chi|+|\underline{L}\xi|\,|L\chi|+|\overline{\partial}\xi|\,|\overline{\partial}\chi|,

(2.9)

and

|Q_{\alpha\beta}(\xi,\chi)|\lesssim|\overline{\partial}\xi|\,|\partial\chi|+|\partial\xi|\,|\overline{\partial}\chi|.

(2.10)

Proof.

For the null form
Q
, it follows directly from (
1.5
) and (
2.2
).

For null form
Q_{0i}
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{0i}(\xi,\chi)=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{t}\xi\,\partial_{i}\chi-\partial_{i}\xi\,\partial_{t}\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}(L+\underline{L})\xi\cdot(\overline{\partial}_{i}+\omega_{i}\partial_{r})\chi-(\overline{\partial}_{i}+\omega_{i}\partial_{r})\xi\cdot\frac{1}{2}(L+\underline{L})\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}(L+\underline{L})\xi\,\overline{\partial}_{i}\chi-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\partial}_{i}\xi\,(L+\underline{L})\chi+\frac{1}{4}\omega_{i}\Big((L+\underline{L})\xi\,(L-\underline{L})\chi-(L-\underline{L})\xi\,(L+\underline{L})\chi\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\Big(Q_{L\overline{i}}(\xi,\chi)+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\xi,\chi)\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{i}Q_{\underline{L}L}(\xi,\chi),

here
Q_{L\overline{i}}(\xi,\chi)=L\xi\,\overline{\partial}_{i}\chi-\overline{\partial}_{i}\xi\,L\chi
and
Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\xi,\chi)=\underline{L}\xi\,\overline{\partial}_{i}\chi-\overline{\partial}_{i}\xi\,\underline{L}\chi
. For simplicity, we rewrite the above equality as

Q_{0i}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}+\omega_{i}Q_{\underline{L}L}\Big).

(2.11)

Similar calculation shows that

Q_{ij}=Q_{\overline{i}\,\overline{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{i}(Q_{L\overline{j}}-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{j}})+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}-Q_{L\overline{i}}).

(2.12)

The inequality (
2.10

2.11

Lemma 2.5

In terms of the frame

\displaystyle\,Q^{ab}(\psi,Q_{ab}(\xi,\chi))-\frac{1}{r}(L\psi+\underline{L}\psi)\,Q_{\underline{L}L}(\xi,\chi)-\frac{1}{r}\overline{\partial}^{\,i}\psi\big(Q_{L\overline{i}}(\xi,\chi)-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\xi,\chi)\big).

Remark 2.6
.

For simplicity, we denote
Q_{ab}(\xi,\chi)
by
Q_{ab}
and rewrite the above equality as

Q^{\alpha\beta}(\psi,Q_{\alpha\beta})=Q^{ab}(\psi,Q_{ab})-\frac{1}{r}(L\psi+\underline{L}\psi)\,Q_{\underline{L}L}-\frac{1}{r}\overline{\partial}^{\,i}\psi(Q_{L\overline{i}}-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}).

Proof.

We raise the indices in
\overline{\partial}_{i}
by
\overline{\delta}^{ij}=\delta^{ij}-\omega^{i}\omega^{j}
, then

\overline{\partial}^{\,i}=\overline{\delta}^{ij}\overline{\partial}_{j}=(\delta^{ij}-\omega^{i}\omega^{j})\overline{\partial}_{j}=\overline{\partial}_{i},

noting that
\omega^{j}\overline{\partial}_{j}=\omega^{j}(\partial_{j}-\omega_{j}\partial_{r})=\partial_{r}-\partial_{r}=0
.

According to (
2.11
) , we have

Q^{0i}(\psi,Q_{0i})=Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{0i})+Q^{L\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{0i})+2\omega^{i}\,Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,Q_{0i}).

For the first term in the right hand side,

\displaystyle Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{0i})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}+\omega_{i}Q_{\underline{L}L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{i}Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{\underline{L}L})+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\underline{L}}\psi(\overline{\partial}^{\,i}\omega_{i})Q_{\underline{L}L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})-\frac{1}{4}L\psi\,\frac{1}{r}(\delta^{i}_{i}-\omega_{i}\omega^{i})Q_{\underline{L}L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})-\frac{1}{2r}L\psi\,Q_{\underline{L}L}.

Similarly, the second term

Q^{L\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{0i})=\frac{1}{2}Q^{L\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})-\frac{1}{2r}\underline{L}\psi\,Q_{\underline{L}L}.

As the third term,

\displaystyle 2\omega^{i}\,Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,Q_{0i})=

\displaystyle\,\omega^{i}\,Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}+\omega_{i}Q_{\underline{L}L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,\omega^{i}\,(Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}))+\omega^{i}\omega_{i}\,Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,Q_{\underline{L}L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,Q_{\underline{L}L}),

where we use the fact
L\omega_{i}=\underline{L}\omega_{i}=0
.
Thus

\displaystyle\,Q^{0i}(\psi,Q_{0i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})+\frac{1}{2}Q^{L\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}+Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})+Q^{\underline{L}L}(\psi,Q_{\underline{L}L})-\frac{1}{2r}L\psi\,Q_{\underline{L}L}-\frac{1}{2r}\underline{L}\psi\,Q_{\underline{L}L}.

A similar calculation yields

Q^{ij}(\psi,Q_{ij})=Q^{\overline{i}\,\overline{j}}(\psi,Q_{\overline{i}\,\overline{j}})+Q^{L\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})-Q^{\underline{L}\overline{i}}(\psi,Q_{L\overline{i}}-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}})-\frac{1}{r}\overline{\partial}^{\,i}\psi(Q_{L\overline{i}}-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle Q^{\alpha\beta}(\psi,Q_{\alpha\beta})=

\displaystyle\,2Q^{0i}(\psi,Q_{0i})+Q^{ij}(\psi,Q_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Q^{ab}(\psi,Q_{ab})-\frac{1}{r}(L\psi+\underline{L}\psi)\,Q_{\underline{L}L}-\frac{1}{r}\overline{\partial}^{\,i}\psi(Q_{L\overline{i}}-Q_{\underline{L}\overline{i}}).

∎

2.3.
Energy identity

We briefly recall the vector field method here. One can refer to Alinhac
[
1
]
or Wang and Yu
[
37
]
for details.

Let
\psi
be a solution for the following non-homogenous wave equation

\Box\psi=\Psi,

X,Y,Z
be the vector fields defined before. The energy momentum tensor associated to
\psi
is defined by

T(X,Y)[\psi]=X\psi\cdot Y\psi-\frac{1}{2}\left<X,Y\right>\,|\nabla\psi|^{2}.

In local coordinates

T_{\alpha\beta}[\psi]=\partial_{\alpha}\psi\,\partial_{\beta}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\,g_{\alpha\beta}\,|\nabla\psi|^{2}.

(2.14)

The deformation tensor of a given vector field
X
is defined by

{}^{(X)}\pi(Y,Z)=\left<D_{Y}X,Z\right>+\left<D_{Z}X,Y\right>.

In local coordinates

{}^{(X)}\pi_{\alpha\beta}=D_{\alpha}X_{\beta}+D_{\beta}X_{\alpha}.

For
f\in C^{1}
being an arbitrary function, we have

^{(fX)}\pi_{\alpha\beta}=f\,^{(X)}\pi_{\alpha\beta}+X_{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{\beta}f+X_{\beta}\cdot\partial_{\alpha}f.

(2.15)

The energy currents are defined as follows

P_{\alpha}=T_{\alpha\beta}[\psi]\cdot X^{\beta},

and

K^{X}[\psi]=\frac{1}{2}\,T^{\alpha\beta}[\psi]\cdot{{}^{(X)}\pi_{\alpha\beta}},

(2.16)

then we have a key divergence form

divP=D_{\alpha}P^{\alpha}=\Box\psi\cdot X\psi+K^{X}[\psi].

Integrating
\Box\psi\cdot X\psi
in some domain
D
, and using the
divergence theorem, we obtain

\int_{\partial D}\left<P,N\right>\,\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\partial D}T(X,N)\,\mathrm{d}v=\iint_{D}\big(\Box\psi\cdot X\psi+K^{X}[\psi]\big)\,\mathrm{d}V,

(2.17)

where
N
is the unit outward normal vector to
\partial D
and
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
is the inner product.

In the null frame
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}=L,e_{4}=\underline{L})
, the deformation tensors and currents are computed as

^{(L)}\pi=\frac{2}{r}\not{g},\quad^{(\underline{L})}\pi=-\frac{2}{r}\not{g},\quad K^{L}[\psi]=\frac{1}{r}L\psi\underline{L}\psi,\quad K^{\underline{L}}[\psi]=-\frac{1}{r}L\psi\underline{L}\psi,

(2.18)

here
\not{g}
is the restriction of the Minkowski metric
g
on
S_{u,\underline{u}}
.

3.
Globally smooth solution in region II

In this section, we will prove the global existence of smooth solution in the short pulse region II.

For
0\leq l\leq k
, denote

\theta_{k,l}=\delta^{l}\partial^{l}\Gamma^{k-l}\theta,\quad\phi_{k,l}=\delta^{l}\partial^{l}\Gamma^{k-l}\phi,

|\partial\theta_{k}|=\sum_{l\leq k}|\partial\theta_{k,l}|,\quad|\partial\phi_{k}|=\sum_{l\leq k}|\partial\phi_{k,l}|,

and

\|\partial\theta_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{l\leq k}\|\partial\theta_{k,l}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\|\partial\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{l\leq k}\|\partial\phi_{k,l}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We first define the energy norms defined on
C_{u}
and
\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}
. Denote
D_{u,\underline{u}}
to be the closed domain bounded by
C_{u}
,
\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}
,
C_{0}
and the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{1}
. Let
C_{u}^{\underline{u}^{\prime}}
be the outgoing null boundary of
D_{u,\underline{u}^{\prime}}
on
C_{u}
and
\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}^{u^{\prime}}
be the incoming null boundary of
D_{u^{\prime},\underline{u}}
on
\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}
. Given any function
f
, define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(C_{u}^{\underline{u}})}=\Big(\int_{1-u}^{\underline{u}}\int_{S_{u,\underline{u}^{\prime}}}|f|^{2}\,r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\underline{u}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{S^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}^{u})}=\Big(\int_{max\{1-\underline{u},0\}}^{u}\int_{S_{u^{\prime},\underline{u}}}|f|^{2}\,r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{S^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Define the
k
-th order homogeneous energies as follows

\delta^{2}E^{2}_{k}(u,\underline{u})=\|\not{\nabla}\theta_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}^{u})}+\|\not{\nabla}\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}^{u})}+\|L\theta_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(C_{u}^{\underline{u}})}+\|L\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(C_{u}^{\underline{u}})}

(3.1)

and

\delta\underline{E}^{2}_{k}(u,\underline{u})=\|\not{\nabla}\theta_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(C_{u}^{\underline{u}})}+\|\not{\nabla}\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(C_{u}^{\underline{u}})}+\|\underline{L}\theta_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}^{u})}+\|\underline{L}\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\underline{C}_{\underline{u}}^{u})}.

(3.2)

The inhomogeneous energies are defined as

E^{2}_{\leq k}(u,\underline{u})=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}E^{2}_{j}(u,\underline{u}),\quad\underline{E}^{2}_{\leq k}(u,\underline{u})=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}\underline{E}^{2}_{j}(u,\underline{u}).

(3.3)

Given any subregion
D_{u^{*},\underline{u}^{*}}
in the short pulse region II, we have

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
\delta_{0}
such that for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
,
(u,\underline{u})\in D_{u^{*},\underline{u}^{*}}
, we have for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
N\geq 6

E_{\leq N}(u,\underline{u})+\underline{E}_{\leq N}(u,\underline{u})\lesssim I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1}),

(3.4)

where
I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
is a positive constant depending only on the initial data up to
N+1
order of derivatives.

The proof is based on a bootstrap argument. Assume that there exists a large constant
M
, which is to be determined later and only depends on the initial data, such that

E_{\leq N}(u,\underline{u})+\underline{E}_{\leq N}(u,\underline{u})\leq M,

(3.5)

for all
(u,\underline{u})\in D_{u^{*},\underline{u}^{*}}
.

Under the bootstrap assumption (
3.5

3.1.
Sobolev inequalities and decay estimates

The following Sobolev inequalities was proved in

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumption (
3.5

\displaystyle\|\not{\nabla}\theta_{k,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}|\underline{u}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}M,

\displaystyle\|\not{\nabla}\phi_{k,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}|\underline{u}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}M,

\displaystyle\|L\phi_{k,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\lesssim\delta|\underline{u}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}M,

\displaystyle\|\underline{L}\theta_{k,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\lesssim|\underline{u}|^{-1}M,

\displaystyle\|\underline{L}\phi_{k,l}\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\lesssim|\underline{u}|^{-1}M.

\underline{L}\theta_{k,l}
in the

Lemma 3.3
.

For
0\leq l\leq k\leq N-2

(3.6)

Proof.

Lemma 3.4

For

Proof.

Since

\not{\nabla}\underline{L}

\Sigma_{t}
as

\displaystyle E_{k}(t)=\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta)^{2}+|\nabla Z^{k}\theta|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi)^{2}+|\nabla Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle E_{\leq k}(t)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}E_{j}(t).

Noting that

(\partial_{t}\psi)^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\big((L\psi)^{2}+(\underline{L}\psi)^{2}\big)+|\not{\nabla}\psi|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\big((L\psi)^{2}+(\underline{L}\psi)^{2}\big)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}|\overline{\partial}_{i}\psi|^{2}.

The above defined energies are equivalent to

\displaystyle E_{k}(t)=\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\left((LZ^{k}\theta)^{2}+(\underline{L}Z^{k}\theta)^{2}\right)+|\not{\nabla}Z^{k}\theta|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\qquad+\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\left((LZ^{k}\phi)^{2}+(\underline{L}Z^{k}\phi)^{2}\right)+|\not{\nabla}Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle E_{\leq k}(t)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}E_{j}(t).

We now formulate the global existence of small solution in region I.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume the solution to the equation (
1.6
) satisfies (
4.1
) on
C_{\delta}
, then there exists a uniquely global solution to the equation (
1.6
) in region I. Moreover, the solution satisfies the following energy estimate

E_{\leq N-3}(t)\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1}),

(5.3)

for
t\geq 1
,
N\geq 6
and
I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
depends on the initial data up to
N+1
order of derivatives.

Remark 5.5
.

Since the smallness of the solution in the sense of
L^{\infty}
defined on the slice
C_{\delta}
depends on the energies obtained in region II. As the new data, we consider the small solution in region I, we will lose three derivatives in the energy
E_{N-3}
.

Proof.

The proof is also based on the bootstrap argument. Assume the solution exists up to time
t
and for all
1\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
, and there is a large constant
M
, which is to be determined later and only depends on the initial data , such that

E_{\leq N-3}(t^{\prime})\leq\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M.

Recall the pointwise decay estimates in region I is (
5
),

\displaystyle\big|\partial Z^{k}\theta\big|\lesssim(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}\,(1+|u|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M,\qquad k\leq N-3.

By Lemma
5.2
, we have

\big|Z^{k}\theta\big|\lesssim(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}\,(1+|u|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M,\qquad k\leq N-3.

For good derivatives, we have

|\overline{\partial}Z^{k}\theta|\lesssim\frac{1}{1+t+r}|Z^{k+1}\theta|\lesssim(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-2}\,(1+|u|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M,\qquad k\leq N-4.

The same estimates hold for
\phi
as well.

We take
\partial_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(L+\underline{L})
as the multiplier,
and multiplying
\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta
and
\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi
on both sides of (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) respectively, and integrating by parts over the domain
D_{I,t}
. By analogous calculations to those in Section
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\iint_{D_{I,t}}\big(\Box Z^{k}\theta-g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta-h_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\phi\big)\cdot\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big(\cos^{2}\theta\Box Z^{k}\phi-g_{2}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\phi-h_{2}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta\big)\cdot\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\overline{E}^{2}_{k}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})-\overline{E}^{2}_{k,u=\delta}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})

\displaystyle-\iint_{D_{I,t}}\frac{1}{2}\big(K^{L}[Z^{k}\theta]+K^{\underline{L}}[Z^{k}\theta]+K^{\cos^{2}\theta L}[Z^{k}\phi]+K^{\cos^{2}\theta\underline{L}}[Z^{k}\phi]\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\big(Q_{k}^{(1)}+Q_{k}^{(2)}+Q_{k}^{(3)}+Q_{k}^{(4)}\big),

(5.4)

where

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\alpha}Z^{k}\theta\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta-\partial_{\alpha}g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta\,\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta,

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle\,\partial_{t}h_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\alpha}Z^{k}\theta\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\phi-\partial_{\alpha}h_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\phi\,\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta,

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{(3)}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}g_{2}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\alpha}Z^{k}\phi\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\phi-\partial_{\alpha}g_{2}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\phi\,\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi,

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{(4)}=

\displaystyle\,-\partial_{\alpha}h_{2}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta\,\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\overline{E}^{2}_{k}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})\sim

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\big((LZ^{k}\theta)^{2}+(\underline{L}Z^{k}\theta)^{2}+2|\not{\nabla}Z^{k}\theta|^{2}\big)+\big((LZ^{k}\phi)^{2}+(\underline{L}Z^{k}\phi)^{2}+2|\not{\nabla}Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\big((\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta)^{2}+|\nabla Z^{k}\theta|^{2}\big)+\big((\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi)^{2}+|\nabla Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\big),

\displaystyle\overline{E}^{2}_{k,u=\delta}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})\sim\int_{C_{\delta}}\big(|LZ^{k}\theta|^{2}+|\not{\nabla}Z^{k}\theta|^{2}\big)+\big(|LZ^{k}\phi|^{2}+|\not{\nabla}Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\big)+(L\phi\underline{L}Z^{k}\theta-L\theta\underline{L}Z^{k}\phi)^{2}.

We now focus on the first term on the left-hand side of equation (
5.4
). In line with (
3.13
), we start by examining the first term on the left-hand side of (
3.13
), which is

\iint_{D_{I,t}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i_{1}|+\cdots+|i_{m}|\leq|k|\\
2\leq m\leq|k|+2\end{subarray}}a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}}\,(\sin(2\theta))^{(m-2)}Q(Z^{i_{1}}\phi,Z^{i_{2}}\phi)Z^{i_{3}}\theta\cdots Z^{i_{m}}\theta\cdot\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta\Big|.

(5.5)

Since for any null form, we have (see Lemma
2.4
)

|\mathcal{Q}(\xi,\chi)|\lesssim|\overline{\partial}\xi|\,|\partial\chi|+|\partial\xi|\,|\overline{\partial}\chi|,

then we can control the worst term in (
5.5
) as

\displaystyle\iint_{D_{I,t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i_{1}|,|i_{2}|<|k/2|\\
|i_{3}|\leq|k|\\
m=3\end{subarray}}\Big|Q(Z^{i_{1}}\phi,Z^{i_{2}}\phi)Z^{i_{3}}\theta\cdot\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta\Big|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k/2|\leq|i_{1}|\leq|k|\\
|i_{2}|,|i_{3}|\leq|k/2|\\
m=3\end{subarray}}\Big|Q(Z^{i_{1}}\phi,Z^{i_{2}}\phi)Z^{i_{3}}\theta\cdot\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\iint_{D_{I,t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i_{1}|,|i_{2}|<|k/2|\\
|i_{3}|\leq|k|\end{subarray}}\big(|\overline{\partial}Z^{i_{1}}\phi|\,|\partial Z^{i_{2}}\phi|+|\partial Z^{i_{1}}\phi|\,|\overline{\partial}Z^{i_{2}}\phi|\big)(1+|u|)|\partial Z^{i_{3}-1}\theta|\,|\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k/2|\leq|i_{1}|\leq|k|\\
|i_{2}|,|i_{3}|\leq|k/2|\end{subarray}}\big(|\overline{\partial}Z^{i_{1}}\phi|\,|\partial Z^{i_{2}}\phi|+|\partial Z^{i_{1}}\phi|\,|\overline{\partial}Z^{i_{2}}\phi|\big)|Z^{i_{3}}\theta|\,|\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\iint_{D_{I,t}}(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-2}(1+|u|)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}(1+|u|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M^{\prime}\big)^{2}(1+|u|)|\partial Z^{k-1}\theta|\,|\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta|

\displaystyle\quad+(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}(1+|u|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}(1+|u|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M^{\prime}\big)^{2}|\partial Z^{i_{1}}\phi|\,|\partial_{t}Z^{k}\theta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\big(\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M^{\prime}\big)^{2}\Big(\int_{1}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-2}\|\partial Z^{k-1}\theta\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{1}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-2}\|\partial_{t}Z^{k-1}\theta\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{\tau})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\big(\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M\big)^{2}\cdot\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M\cdot\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M\lesssim\delta^{3}M^{4}.

The remaining terms on the left-hand side of equation (
3.13
) are of higher order and contribute additional decay. Consequently, the first term on the left-hand side of equation (
5.4
) is dominated and can be bounded by
\delta^{3}M^{4}
.

An analogous calculation shows the second term in the left-hand side of (
5.4
) is also bounded by
\delta^{3}M^{4}
.

For the terms
K
, we have

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}\frac{1}{2}\Big|K^{L}[Z^{k}\theta]+K^{\underline{L}}[Z^{k}\theta]+K^{\cos^{2}\theta L}[Z^{k}\phi]+K^{\cos^{2}\theta\underline{L}}[Z^{k}\phi]\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}\Big|Q(\cos^{2}\theta,Z^{k}\phi)\,\partial_{t}Z^{k}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(\cos^{2}\theta)\,|\nabla Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}\big|\sin(2\theta)\big|\,\big|\partial\theta\big|\,\big|\partial Z^{k}\phi\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}\,(1+|u|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M\cdot(1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}\,(1+|u|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M\cdot|\partial Z^{k}\phi|^{2}\lesssim\delta^{3}M^{4}.

For the terms
Q
, we only need to handle the term
\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\alpha}Z^{k}\theta\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta
in
Q_{k}^{(1)}
, as the remaining terms can be controlled in the same way.
As

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}\Big|\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}g_{1}^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\alpha}Z^{k}\theta\,\partial_{\beta}Z^{k}\theta\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}\big|\partial^{2}\phi\big|\,\big|\partial\phi\big|\,\big|\partial Z^{k}\theta\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\iint_{D_{I,t}}\big((1+|\underline{u}|)^{-1}\,(1+|u|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}M\big)^{2}\big|\partial Z^{k}\theta\big|^{2}\lesssim\delta^{3}M^{4}.

For the boundary term
\overline{E}^{2}_{k,u=\delta}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})
, according to (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\overline{E}^{2}_{k,u=\delta}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\int_{1-\delta}^{\underline{u}}\int_{S_{\delta,\underline{u}^{\prime}}}(\delta^{\frac{3}{4}}|\underline{u}^{\prime}|^{-2}M)^{2}\,r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\underline{u}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{S^{2}}

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\int_{1-\delta}^{\underline{u}}\big(\int_{S_{\delta,\underline{u}^{\prime}}}\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}|\underline{u}^{\prime}|^{-4}M^{2}\,|\underline{u}^{\prime}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{S^{2}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\underline{u}^{\prime}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}M^{2},\qquad k\leq N-3.

Combining the above estimates, we deduce that

\overline{E}^{2}_{k}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}(I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1}))^{2}+\delta^{3}M^{4}\leq C_{1}^{2}\Big(\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}(I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1}))^{2}+\delta^{3}M^{4}\Big).

Choosing
M>2C_{1}I_{N}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
and
\delta<(2C_{1}M)^{-\frac{4}{3}}
, we can obtain

\overline{E}^{2}_{k}(\theta,\phi,\partial_{t})\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}M^{2}.

At last, utilizing the standard continuity argument, the solution exists globally in the region I.

∎

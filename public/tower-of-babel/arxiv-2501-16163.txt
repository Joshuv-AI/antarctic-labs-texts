A note on representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras induced by left Leibniz algebras
A. Nourou ISSA

Abstract
. It is well-known that each left Leibniz algebra has a natural structure of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. In this paper it is shown that every left representation of a left Leibniz algebra ( 𝔤 , ⋅ ) (\mathfrak{g},\cdot) induces naturally a representation of the Lie-Yamaguti algebra ( 𝔤 , [ , ] , [ [ , , ] ] ) (\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!]) that is associated with ( 𝔤 , ⋅ ) (\mathfrak{g},\cdot) . Moreover, it is proved that equivalent representations of ( 𝔤 , ⋅ ) (\mathfrak{g},\cdot) give equivalent representations of ( 𝔤 , [ , ] , [ [ , , ] ] ) (\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!]) .

Introduction

The notion of a “
D
-algebra” was introduced by A. M. Bloh (
[
3
]
). About three decades later, developing a homology theory for Lie algebras, J. L. Loday (
[
7

As a generalization of Lie algebras and because of their applications in mathematics, physics, and mathematical physics, Leibniz algebras were studied in last decades from various aspects by many researchers. A representation and cohomology theory for Leibniz algebras was initiated in
[
9
]
.

The notion of a “generalized Lie triple system” was introduced by K. Yamaguti (
[
12
]
) who developed further a representation and cohomology theory for this kind of algebras (
[
13
]
,
[
14

). In
[
6

M. K. Kinyon and A. Weinstein renamed generalized Lie triple systems as “Lie-Yamaguti algebras”; moreover, they found that any left Leibniz algebra has a natural Lie-Yamaguti structure. Later on, investigating the Akivis algebra associated with a left Leibniz algebra, this relationship with Lie-Yamaguti algebras was considered again in
[
4
]
. Thus some specific constructions regarding Lie-Yamaguti algebras can be deduced from the ones of Leibniz algebras.

In this short note we consider the representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra that is induced by a left representation of a given left Leibniz algebra. Recall that any representation of a given Maltsev algebra
M
induces a representation of the Lie-Yamaguti algebra that is associated with

(
[

In section 3, starting from some well-known representations of left Leibniz algebras, our main result is applied to give a few examples of nontrivial representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. All vectors and algebras are considered over a field of characteristic zero.

2.
Main results

In this section we first remind some useful basic notions and next we prove that any left representation of a given left Leibniz algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
induces a representation of the associated Lie-Yamaguti algebra; moreover, it turns out that equivalent left representations of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
imply equivalent representations the associated Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Definition 2.1.
(
[
3
,
7
,
8
]
) A (left) Leibniz algebra is a vector space

\mathfrak{g}
together with a bilinear map

\cdot:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the
left Leibniz identity

x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(x\cdot y)\cdot z+y\cdot(x\cdot z)

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Observe that in
[
7
,
8
]
only
right
Leibniz algebras were considered, i.e. algebras satisfying the
right Leibniz identity

(z\cdot y)\cdot x=z\cdot(y\cdot x)+(z\cdot x)\cdot y
.

Definition 2.2.
(
[
8
,
9
]
) Let

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a left Leibniz algebra. A left representation of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a triple
(V,l,r)
, where
V
is a vector space,
l,r:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
are linear maps such that the following conditions hold for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
:

(2.1)

l(x\cdot y)=[l(x),l(y)],

(2.2)

r(x\cdot y)=r(y)\circ r(x)+l(x)\circ r(y),

(2.3)

r(x\cdot y)=[l(x),r(y)].

The vector space
V
is also called a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

The conditions (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) imply

(2.4)

r(y)\circ r(x)+r(y)\circ l(x)=0.

In the sequel, given some maps
f
and
g
, their usual composition
f\circ g
will be denoted simply by
fg
.

An example of a left representation of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is the so-called
adjoint
(or
regular
) representation that is defined as the triple
(\mathfrak{g},L,R)
with linear maps
L
,
R
defined respectively by
L:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
,
x\mapsto L_{x}
and
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
,
x\mapsto R_{x}
, where
L_{x}
(resp.
R_{x}
) denotes the left (resp. right) multiplication in (
\mathfrak{g},\cdot
),
L_{x}y:=x\cdot y
and
R_{x}y:=y\cdot x
.

Let
V^{*}
denotes the dual space of
V
and define two linear maps
l^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V^{*})
,
x\mapsto l^{*}(x)
and
r^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V^{*})
,
x\mapsto r^{*}(x)
, where
l^{*}(x)\xi:=-\xi l(x)
, and
r^{*}(x)\xi:=-\xi r(x)
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\xi\in V^{*}
. It is well-known that, in general, (

) is not a left representation of (
\mathfrak{g},\cdot
). However, the following result provides a left representation of (

).

Lemma 2.3.
(
[
11
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a left Leibniz algebra and
(V,l,r)
its left representation. Then
(V^{*},l^{*},-l^{*}-r^{*})
is a left representation of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
.

The representation
(V^{*},l^{*},-l^{*}-r^{*})
is called the
dual representation
of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
.

Definition 2.4.
(
[
12
]
)
A Lie-Yamaguti algebra (LY algebra for short) is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
, where

\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space,
[,]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a bilinear map, and
[\![,,]\!]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a trilinear map such that

(LY01)
[x,y]=-[y,x]
,

(LY02)
[\![x,y,z]\!]=-[\![y,x,z]\!]
,

(LY1)
{\circlearrowleft}_{x,y,z}([[x,y],z]+[\![x,y,z]\!])=0
,

(LY2)
{\circlearrowleft}_{x,y,z}([\![[x,y],z,u]\!])=0

(LY3)
[\![x,y,[u,v]]\!]=[[\![x,y,u]\!],v]+[u,[\![x,y,v]\!]]

(LY4)
[\![x,y,[\![u,v,w]\!]]\!]=[\![[\![x,y,u]\!],v,w]\!]+[\![u,[\![x,y,v]\!],w]\!]+[\![u,v,[\![x,y,w]\!]]\!]

for all

, where
{\circlearrowleft}_{x,y,z}
denotes the sum over cyclic permutation of
x,y,z
.

In

[\![x,y,z]\!]:=-(x\cdot y)\cdot z

for all

(see also

A representation and cohomology theory for

]

Definition 2.5.
(
[
13
]
) Let

(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
be a
LY
algebra. A representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
is a quadruple
(V,\rho,\theta,D)
where
V
is a vector space,
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
is a linear map, and
\theta,D:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
are bilinear maps such that

(R1)
D(x,y)-\theta(y,x)+\theta(x,y)+\rho([x,y])-[\rho(x),\rho(y)]=0
;

(R2)
D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)=0
;

(R3)
\theta([x,y],z)=\theta(x,z)\rho(y)-\theta(y,z)\rho(x)
;

(R4)
[D(x,y),\rho(z)]=\rho([\![x,y,z]\!])
;

(R5)
\theta(x,[y,z])=\rho(y)\theta(x,z)-\rho(z)\theta(x,y)
;

(R6)
[D(u,v),\theta(x,y)]=\theta([\![u,v,x]\!],y)+\theta(x,[\![u,v,y]\!])
;

(R7)
\theta(u,[\![x,y,z]\!])=\theta(y,z)\theta(u,x)-\theta(x,z)\theta(u,y)+D(x,y)\theta(u,z)

for all
u,v,x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

An example of a representation of

(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
is the so-called
adjoint
(or
regular
) representation
(\mathfrak{g},\rho,\theta,D)
, where
\rho(x)(y):=[x,y]
,
\theta(x,y)(z):=[\![z,x,y]\!]
, and
D(x,y)(z):=[\![x,y,z]\!]
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
(
[
14
]
).

Because of (R1), the representation
(V,\rho,\theta,D)
is simply written as
(V,\rho,\theta)
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 2.6.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a Leibniz algebra and
(V,l,r)
its left representation. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
be the
LY
algebra associated with
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
. Then
(V,l,r)
induces a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
(call it the associated representation).

Proof.

On
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
we define the following maps, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
:

\rho(x):=l(x)-r(x)
;

\theta(x,y):=-r(y)r(x)
;

D(x,y):=-l(x\cdot y)
.

Clearly
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
is a linear map and
\theta,D:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow End(V)
are bilinear maps. We proceed to check that the triple
(\rho,\theta,D)
as defined above satisfies the set of axioms as in Definition 2.5.

\bullet
For (R1) we have

D(x,y)+\theta(x,y)-\theta(y,x)+\rho([x,y])-[\rho(x),\rho(y)]

=\{-l(y\cdot x)+l(y)l(x)-l(x)l(y)\}_{(2.1)}

+\{-r(x\cdot y)+l(x)r(y)-r(y)l(x)\}_{(\ref{eq2.3})}

+\{r(y\cdot x)-l(y)r(x)+r(x)l(y)\}_{(\ref{eq2.3})}

=0
(by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
))

so (R1) holds for
(\rho,\theta,D)
.

\bullet
For (R2) we have

D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)

=-l([x,y]\cdot z)-l([y,z]\cdot x)-l([z,x]\cdot y)

=-(l([x,y])l(z)-l(z)l([x,y]))-(l([y,z])l(x)-l(x)l([y,z]))-(l([z,x])l(y)-l(y)l([z,x]))
(by (
2.1
))

=-l(x\cdot y)l(z)+l(y\cdot x)l(z)+l(z)l(x\cdot y)-l(z)l(y\cdot x)-l(y\cdot z)l(x)+l(z\cdot y)l(x)+l(x)l(y\cdot z)-l(x)l(z\cdot y)-l(z\cdot x)l(y)+l(x\cdot z)l(y)+l(y)l(z\cdot x)-l(y)l(x\cdot z)

=-[l(x),l(y)]l(z)+[l(y),l(x)]l(z)+l(z)[l(x),l(y)]-l(z)[l(y),l(x)]-[l(y),l(z)]l(x)+[l(z),l(y)]l(x)+l(x)[l(y),l(z)]-l(x)[l(z),l(y)]-[l(z),l(x)]l(y)+[l(x),l(z)]l(y)+l(y)[l(z),l(x)]-l(y)[l(x),l(z)]
(again by (
2.1
))

=-[[l(x),l(y)],l(z)]+[[l(y),l(x)],l(z)]+[l(x),[l(y),l(z)]]+[[l(z),l(y)],l(x)]+[l(y),[l(z),l(x)]]+[[l(x),l(z)],l(y)]

=-[[l(x),l(y)],l(z)]-[[l(y),l(z)],l(x)]-[[l(z),l(x)],l(y)]+[[l(y),l(x)],l(z)]+[[l(x),l(z)],l(y)]+[[l(z),l(y)],l(x)]

=0

so (R2) holds for
(\rho,\theta,D)
.

\bullet
For (R3) we have

\theta([x,y],z)-\theta(x,z)\rho(y)+\theta(y,z)\rho(x)

=-r(z)r(x\cdot y)+r(z)r(y\cdot x)+r(z)r(x)l(y)-r(z)r(x)r(y)-r(z)r(y)l(x)+r(z)r(y)r(x)

=-r(z)\{r(y)r(x)+l(x)r(y)\}+r(z)\{r(x)r(y)+l(y)r(x)\}+r(z)r(x)l(y)-r(z)r(x)r(y)-r(z)r(y)l(x)+r(z)r(y)r(x)
(by (
2.2
))

=-r(z)l(x)r(y)+r(z)r(x)l(y)+r(z)l(y)r(x)-r(z)r(y)l(x)

=-r(z)l(x)r(y)-r(z)r(x)r(y)+r(z)l(y)r(x)+r(z)r(y)r(x)
(by (
2.4
))

=-\{r(z)l(x)+r(z)r(x)\}_{(2.4)}r(y)+\{r(z)l(y)+r(z)r(y)\}_{(2.4)}r(x)

=0
(by (
2.4
))

so (R3) holds for
(\rho,\theta,D)
.

\bullet
For (R4) we have

[D(x,y),\rho(z)]=D(x,y)\rho(z)-\rho(z)D(x,y)

=-l(x\cdot y)l(z)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)+l(z)l(x\cdot y)-r(z)l(x\cdot y)

=-\{l(x\cdot y)l(z)-l(z)l(x\cdot y)\}_{(2.1)}+l(x\cdot y)r(z)-r(z)l(x\cdot y)

=-l((x\cdot y)\cdot z)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)-r(z)l(x\cdot y)
(by (
2.1
))

=-l((x\cdot y)\cdot z)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)+r(z)r(x\cdot y)
(by (
2.4
))

=-l((x\cdot y)\cdot z)+r((x\cdot y)\cdot z)
(by (
2.2
))

=\rho(-(x\cdot y)\cdot z)

=\rho([\![x,y,z]\!])

so (R4) holds for
(\rho,\theta,D)
.

\bullet
For (R5) we have

\rho(y)\theta(x,z)-\rho(z)\theta(x,y)=-l(y)r(z)r(x)+r(y)r(z)r(x)+l(z)r(y)r(x)-r(z)r(y)r(x)

=\{-r(z)r(y)-l(y)r(z)\}_{(2.2)}r(x)+\{r(y)r(z)+l(z)r(y)\}_{(2.2)}r(x)

=-r(y\cdot z)r(x)+r(z\cdot y)r(x)
(by (
2.2
))

=-r([y,z])r(x)

=\theta(x,[y,z])

so we get (R5) for
(\rho,\theta,D)
.

\bullet
For (R6) we have

\theta([\![u,v,x]\!],y)+\theta(x,[\![u,v,y]\!])=r(y)r((u\cdot v)\cdot x)+r((u\cdot v)\cdot y)r(x)

=r(y)\{r(x)r(u\cdot v)+l(u\cdot v)r(x)\}+\{r(y)r(u\cdot v)+l(u\cdot v)r(y)\}r(x)
(by (
2.2
))

=r(y)r(x)r(u\cdot v)+l(u\cdot v)r(y)r(x)+\{r(y)l(u\cdot v)+r(y)r(u\cdot v)\}_{(\ref{eq2.4})}r(x)

=r(y)r(x)r(u\cdot v)+l(u\cdot v)r(y)r(x)
(by (
2.4
))

=-r(y)r(x)l(u\cdot v)+l(u\cdot v)r(y)r(x)
(by (
2.4
))

=[D(u,v),\theta(x,y)]

and so (R6) holds for
(\rho,\theta,D)
.

\bullet
Finally for (R7) we have

\theta(y,z)\theta(u,x)-\theta(x,z)\theta(u,y)+D(x,y)\theta(u,z)

=r(z)r(y)r(x)r(u)-r(z)r(x)r(y)r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)

=r(z)(r(y)r(x)-r(x)r(y))r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)

=r(z)(r(y)r(x)+r(x)l(y))r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)
(by (
2.4
))

=r(z)(r(x\cdot y)-l(x)r(y)+r(x)l(y))r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)
(by (
2.2
))

=r(z)r(x\cdot y)r(u)+r(z)(r(x)l(y)-l(x)r(y))r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)

=r(z)r(x\cdot y)r(u)+r(z)(-r(x)r(y)-l(x)r(y))r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)
(by (
2.4
))

=r(z)r(x\cdot y)r(u)+\{-r(z)r(x)-r(z)l(x)\}_{(\ref{eq2.4})}r(y)r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)

=r(z)r(x\cdot y)r(u)+l(x\cdot y)r(z)r(u)
(by (
2.4
))

=r((x\cdot y)\cdot z)r(u)
(by (
2.2
))

=\theta(u,[\![x,y,z]\!])

so (R7) holds for
(\rho,\theta,D)
. This completes the proof.
∎

The notion of a homomorphism of representations of Leibniz algebras was introduced in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.7.
Two representations

(V_{1},l_{1},r_{1})
and
(V_{2},l_{2},r_{2})
of a left Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
are said to be equivalent if there is an isomorphism
\psi:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that
\psi l_{1}(x)=l_{2}(x)\psi
and
\psi r_{1}(x)=r_{2}(x)\psi
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

The notion of a homomorphism of representations of a
LY
algebra was recently introduced in
[
10
]
. From this notion one easily defines equivalent representations of a
LY
algebra as follows.

Definition 2.8.
Two representations

(V_{1},{\rho}_{1},{\theta}_{1})
and
(V_{2},{\rho}_{2},{\theta}_{2})
of a given
LY
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
are said to be equivalent if there is an isomorphism
\psi:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that
\psi{\rho}_{1}(x)={\rho}_{2}(x)\psi
and
\psi{\theta}_{1}(x,y)={\theta}_{2}(x,y)\psi
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

The following result shows that equivalent representations of a given left Leibniz algebra give rise to equivalent representations of the associated
LY
algebra.

Theorem 2.9.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a left Leibniz algebra and
(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
its associated
LY
algebra. If

(V_{1},l_{1},r_{1})
and
(V_{2},l_{2},r_{2})
are two equivalent representations of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
, then the associated representations

(V_{1},{\rho}_{1},{\theta}_{1})
and
(V_{2},{\rho}_{2},{\theta}_{2})
of
(\mathfrak{g},[,],[\![,,]\!])
are also equivalent.

Proof.

We have,
\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\forall v\in V_{1}
,

\psi({\rho}_{1}(x)(v))=\psi(l_{1}(x)(v))-\psi(r_{1}(x)(v))

=l_{2}(x)\psi(v)-r_{2}(x)\psi(v)

={\rho}_{2}(x)(\psi(v))

and, from the other hand,

{\theta}_{2}(x,y)(\psi(v))=-r_{2}(y)(\psi(r_{1}(x)(v)))=-\psi(r_{1}(y)(r_{1}(x)(v)))

=\psi(-r_{1}(y)(r_{1}(x)(v)))=\psi({\theta}_{1}(x,y)(v))

so that
\psi{\rho}_{1}(x)={\rho}_{2}(x)\psi
and
\psi{\theta}_{1}(x,y)={\theta}_{2}(x,y)\psi
. Thus
(V_{1},{\rho}_{1},{\theta}_{1})
and
(V_{2},{\rho}_{2},{\theta}_{2})
are equivalent.
∎

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)

Example 3.2.
If

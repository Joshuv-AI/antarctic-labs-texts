Local solubility in generalised Châtelet varieties
Kevin Destagnol, Julian Lyczak, Efthymios Sofos

Abstract
. We obtain asymptotic formulas for averages of general multivariate arithmetic functions evaluated at polynomial arguments using recent work of Rydin Myerson and Rome–Yamagishi. We give several applications of our results in analytic number theory and arithmetic geometry. For example, we improve on the number of variables needed to prove the Hasse principle for certain polynomial systems, and we count the number of fibers with a rational point in families of high-dimensional Châtelet varieties, allowing for arbitrarily large subordinate Brauer groups.

1.
Introduction

For many types of counting problems in arithmetic geometry there are
conjectures for the order of growth and the leading constant,
see for instance
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
28
,
34
]
. A common
part of the leading constant is
the volume of an adelic subset, which is described by elements of some
Brauer group. In the case of unramified Brauer group elements, the adelic
subset is given by finitely many congruence conditions as the invariant maps are constant at all but finitely many places.
Harari
[
18
, Théorème 2.1.1]
proved
that, for ramified elements, the invariant maps are
non-constant at a set of primes of positive density.
We will describe a counting problem where the adelic volume will involve these infinitely many congruence conditions.

Counting result

We consider

forms
f_{1},\dots,f_{R}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
of the same degree

and a square-free integer

let

(1.1)

{N(B):=\sharp\left\{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Z})\colon H(\mathbf{t})\leqslant B,\ \prod_{i=1}^{R}f_{i}(\mathbf{t})\textrm{ is a norm from }\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})/\mathbb{Q}\right\},}

.

To describe

Remark 1.4

In the context of counting locally soluble varieties

Remark 1.5
.

(Generalised Châtelet varieties)
Châtelet surfaces are defined as smooth proper models of affine varieties of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{3}
of the shape

X^{2}-aZ^{2}=F(X,1)

where
a
is a non-square integer and where
F
is a integral binary form of degree 4. Those surfaces, introduced by Châtelet
[
7
,
8
]
, are arithmetically and geometrically very rich. The historical example given by Swinnerton-Dyer in 1971 of varieties for which both the Hasse principle and weak approximation fail are for instance Châtelet surfaces. Châtelet surfaces have been extensively studied both from the geometric aspect (see for instance
[
9
,
10
]
where the authors show that the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only one to the Hasse principle and to weak approximation) and more quantitatively. The study of Manin’s conjecture, namely of the rational points of bounded height, for Châtelet surfaces started with the case
a=-1

[
4
,
3
,
5
,
13
]
and culminated recently with the work of Woo
[
42
]
settling all cases. All of these proofs make use of the natural conic fibration and it is therefore a natural question to ask how many fibers contribute to the final asymptotic. The varieties under consideration in Theorem
1.1
are natural multivariate generalisations of Châtelet surfaces.

The analytic core of the proof of Theorem
1.1
lies in the development of a new
counting tool relying on Birch circle method, this explains the nature of the condition on the polynomials
f_{i}
in the statement described in Corollary
2.6
. This tool will be described in the next subsection. Let us note that the counting problem still makes sense when
dR
is odd. However, both the analytical techniques required to proving an asymptotic for the counting problem and the geometry of the interpretation change. For this reason we focus on the case where the total degree
dR
is even.

1.1.
Averages of arithmetic functions over values of multivariable polynomials

The analytic argument relies on developing a general tool
that gives asymptotics
for

Due to its technical nature we avoid placing the statement

This generalises
[

2.5

instead of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]

in
[

, §3.1]
.

1.2.
Multiplicative models

In the proof of Theorem
2.5
, we opted for a “multiplicative model” approach rather than the
more standard method based on exponential sums, as exemplified by
\mathfrak{S}^{\flat}
in Lemma
2.10
. This choice ensures that the leading constant in Theorem
2.5
is particularly convenient for the counting applications mentioned above.
Notably, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, this constant will take ftheorm of a sum of
4^{R}

Euler products. It is worth emphasizing that in applications of the circle method, it is
somewhat uncommon for the leading constant to be
a sum of more than a single
Euler product of
p
-adic densities but the formulation of Theorem
2.5

covers such cases as well.

1.3.
Proving Theorem
1.1
via a Splitting Trick

We derive Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
2.5
in §
4
. The core idea is a
splitting trick
, which proceeds in two steps.

In the first step, we show that for almost all
\mathbf{t}
in
N(B)
, any two of the values
f_{i}(\mathbf{t})

only
share prime factors of nearly bounded size. This step is motivated by the codimension
2
Ekedahl sieve philosophy. However, unlike traditional applications of the geometric sieve, our setting involves a zero-density subset of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, dictated by the norm condition in
\mathscr{N}_{D}
. This approach is developed in Lemmas
4.1
–
4.3
, where we apply a general circle method upper bound (Lemma
2.9
) to reduce the problem to averages over
\mathbb{Z}^{R}
. These averages are then analyzed in Lemmas
3.11
-
3.13
using the large sieve.

This first step ensures that we need only feed into the circle
method
an equidistribution result for tuples
(n_{1},\dots,n_{R})\in\mathbb{Z}^{R}
, where

n_{1}\cdots n_{R}
is a norm in

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})/\mathbb{Q}
and, crucially,
the
n_{i}
are nearly coprime in pairs.
Without the near coprimality, a Perron formula argument in
\mathbb{C}^{R}
would be required.
The equidistribution result forms the second step of the splitting trick and is stated in Lemma
3.9
, with its proof occupying §
3.1
.

Finally, in §
5
, we demonstrate that the factor
2^{R}\gamma
coincides with the product of the order of the subordinate Brauer group and the Tamagawa number, as predicted by
[
24
, Conjecture 3.8]
.

2.1.
Averages of arithmetic functions over values of multivariable polynomials

Theorem
2.5
,
states that if

k:\mathbb{N}^{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is
equidistributed in progressions
then for “well-behaved”
polynomial systems
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{R})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]^{R}

the average

\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\cap[-P,P]^{n+1}}k(f_{1}(\mathbf{t}),\ldots,f_{R}(\mathbf{t}))

can be estimated asymptotically. It is important
to note that
k
need not be
non-negative nor multiplicative.
In fact, our averages will be taken over
expanding boxes

P\mathscr{B}
, where
\mathscr{B}
is as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{B}\subset[-1,1]^{n+1}
have
sides of length at most
1
which are parallel
to the coordinate axes. Denote

b_{i}=2\max_{i}|f_{i}(\mathscr{B})|
and

b=\max b_{i}
.

For integer polynomials

f_{1},\ldots,f_{R}
in
n+1
variables the usual
singular series and singular integral are respectively
denoted by
\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathfrak{J}

and are defined, provided convergence, as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{-n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{R}=1\\
\gcd(a_{1},\dots,a_{R},q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\mathrm{e}\Big(-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{R}a_{i}\nu_{i}\Big)\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}}\mathrm{e}\Big(\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{R}a_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\Big)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\textrm{ prime}\\
p=2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\sharp\{\mathbf{t}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}:\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t})=\boldsymbol{\nu}\}}{p^{m(n+1-R)}}

and

\mathfrak{J}:=\int_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{R}}\mathrm{e}\Big(-\sum_{i=1}^{R}\gamma_{i}\nu_{i}P^{-d}\Big)\int_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathrm{e}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{R}\gamma_{i}f_{i}^{\natural}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\Big)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\gamma},

where

and

denotes the top degree homogeneous part of a polynomial

.
Our analytic tool
will apply to the following systems of polynomials.

(strong
Hardy–Littlewood system)
.

A set of integer polynomials
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{R}\}

in
n+1

•

there exists

\eta=\eta(\mathbf{f})>0

\mathbf{a}
and

\mathfrak{S}
one has

(2.1)

\left|\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}}\mathrm{e}\left(\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{R}a_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right|\ll q^{n-R-\eta},

where the implied constant
depends only on
\mathbf{f}
;

•

for all
\mathscr{B}

in Definition
2.1

and all
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{R}

one has

(2.2)

\left|\int_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathrm{e}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{R}\gamma_{i}f_{i}^{\natural}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\Big)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|\ll(1+\max_{i}|\gamma_{i}|)^{-R-\eta},

where the implied constant depends only on
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathscr{B}
, and

•

there is
\delta=\delta(\mathbf{f})>0

such that for all
\mathscr{B}

in Definition
2.1

P\geqslant 1
,

\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{Z}^{R}

(2.3)

\sharp\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\cap P\mathscr{B}:\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=\boldsymbol{\nu}\}=\mathfrak{J}\mathfrak{S}P^{n+1-dR}+O(P^{n+1-dR-\delta}),

where the implied constant
depends only on

\frac{\sharp\{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\cap P\mathscr{B}:F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t}))=0\}}{P^{n+1-d_{1}d_{2}}}\gg\prod_{p\leqslant C}\frac{1}{p^{z(n+1-R)}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{z}\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}}\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t}),p^{z}).

We write the
p
-adic term as

\frac{1}{p^{z(n+1-R)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{z}\mathbb{Z})^{R}\\
F(\mathbf{y})\equiv 0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{z}\right)\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{t}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{z}\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}\\
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t})\equiv\mathbf{y}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{z}\right)\end{subarray}}1\Bigg)\sigma_{p}(\mathbf{y},p^{z}).

By restricting to those

with

\min_{j}v_{p}\Big(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}(\mathbf{y})\Big)\leqslant(m_{p}(z)-1)/2
and using the first part of Lemma

we obtain the lower bound

(2.13)

\geqslant\frac{1}{p^{zn}}\Osum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{z}\mathbb{Z})^{R}\\
F(\mathbf{y})\equiv 0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{z}\right)\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{t}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{z}\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}\\
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t})\equiv\mathbf{y}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{z}\right)\end{subarray}}1\Bigg),

where
\Osum
is subject to the condition
\min_{j}v_{p}\Big(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}(\mathbf{y})\Big)\leqslant(z-1)/2
.

Now assume that for a prime
p\leqslant C

the hypersurface
F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t}))=0
has a point in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
By homogeneity there is
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n+1}
such that

p\nmid\mathbf{r}
and
F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Among such
\mathbf{r}
we select one which minimises the quantity
\kappa:=\min_{j}v_{p}(f_{j}(\mathbf{r}))
, hence,

\kappa:=\kappa(p)\geqslant 0
. In other words,
p^{\kappa}\mid\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})
and
p^{\kappa+1}\nmid\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})
.
For all
i
we have

\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))=\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}\left(p^{\kappa}\frac{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})}{p^{\kappa}}\right)=p^{\kappa(d_{2}-1)}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})}{p^{\kappa}}\right).

Since
F
is smooth over
\mathbb{Q}

we can find for every prime
p
an integer
N_{p}
such that
for all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n+1}
with
p\nmid\mathbf{z}

one has
\min_{i}v_{p}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbf{z})\right)\leqslant N_{p}
. Indeed, if the derivatives at these points were unbounded one can construct a converging sequence
\mathbf{z}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n+1}
with
p\nmid\mathbf{z}_{k}
and
\min_{i}v_{p}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbf{z}_{k})\right)\geqslant k
and the limit
z=\lim z_{k}
is a singular point on
F=0
, which does not exist by assumption. Furthermore,
N_{p}=0
suffices for all but finitely many primes, which we have used implicitly at the beginning of the subsection, and will not require further.

Applying this with
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})p^{-\kappa}
we infer that

\min_{i}v_{p}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))\right)\leqslant\kappa(d_{2}-1)+N_{p}.

Note that
\kappa
and

are functions of
p
and
F,\mathbf{f}
, however, since we are only considering

,
we infer that

is upper bounded by a function of
F
and
\mathbf{f}
alone.
The conclusion is that
there is
\ell=\ell(F,\mathbf{f})\geqslant 0
such that for all primes
p\leqslant C
there exists
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n+1}
with

\min_{i}v_{p}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))\right)\leqslant\ell
and
F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}))=0
.
Since
z=[\log\log B]\to\infty
, we certainly have

. Hence, restricting the sum in (
2.13
) to the term
\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{r}
gives the lower bound

\frac{\sharp\{\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{z}\mathbb{Z})^{R}:F(\mathbf{y})\equiv 0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{z}\right),\mathbf{y}\equiv\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r})\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{z}\right)\}}{p^{zn}}\geqslant p^{-zn}.

Hence, the overall bound becomes

\frac{\sharp\{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\cap P\mathscr{B}:F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t}))=0\}}{P^{n+1-d_{1}d_{2}}}\gg\prod_{p\leqslant C}p^{-zn}={\gamma^{\prime}}^{-z}

for some
\gamma^{\prime}>1
that depends only on
C
and
n
.
Since
z=[\log\log B]
and
B=bP^{d_{2}}

we have

{\gamma^{\prime}}^{z}\geqslant(\log P)^{\eta}
for some
\eta>0

that depends only on
\gamma^{\prime}
,

b
and
d_{2}
.
We have therefore proved that if
F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}))=0
is everywhere locally soluble then

\sharp\{\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\cap P\mathscr{B}:F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t}))=0\}\gg\frac{P^{n+1-d_{1}d_{2}}}{(\log P)^{\eta}}

and in particular,
F(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}))=0
has a non-trivial
\mathbb{Q}
-rational point.

3.

analytic lemmas

In §

we prove that the arithmetic function
that is relevant to quadratic

While this can be done for completely general arithmetic progressions,

At last,

we do establish certain bounds via the large sieve,
which, though requisite for the arguments of

upon the symmetry of exposition were they to intrude

2\mid v_{p}(m_{i})\ \forall p>z\textrm{ with }(\frac{D}{p})=-1\textrm{ and }\forall i\end{array}\right\}=\frac{\mathscr{M}+O(\mathscr{E})}{W_{z}^{R}}\prod_{i=1}^{R}\frac{x_{i}}{(\log x_{i})^{1/2}},

where

\mathscr{M}=\mathds{1}(\eqref{eq:pass the joint equidistribution})\left(\frac{2\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{R}\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-R/2},\ \mathscr{E}=\frac{1}{(\log\min x_{i})^{1/4}},

the implied constant depends only on
D
and
R

and

(3.6)

{(D,s)_{\mathbb{R}}=1,\quad(D,sa_{1}\cdots a_{R})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=1\ \forall p\leqslant z,\quad\left(\frac{D}{a_{i}/\gcd(a_{i},W_{z})}\right)=1\ \forall i.}

Proof.

The assumption

W_{z}\leqslant(\log(x_{i}/\gcd(a_{i},W_{z})))^{25}

of Lemma
3.7
is met due to the
condition
W_{z}(\log z)^{R}\leqslant(\log\min x_{i})^{1/4}
. We may thus feed
Lemma
3.7

into Lemma
3.5
. The error term is

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{R}\frac{x_{i}}{\log x_{i}}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{x_{j}}{W_{z}(\log x_{j})^{1/2}}\prod_{p\leqslant z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}\cdots x_{R}}{W_{z}^{R}(\log x_{1})^{1/2}\cdots(\log x_{R})^{1/2}}\frac{W_{z}(\log z)^{R/2}}{(\log\min x_{i})^{1/2}}.

This is satisfactory due to the assumption

W_{z}(\log z)^{R}\leqslant(\log\min x_{i})^{1/4}
.

The ensuing main term equals the one in our lemma save for the fact that each
\log x_{i}
is replaced by

\log(x_{i}/\gcd(a_{i},W_{z}))
. Our assumptions
imply that
W_{z}\leqslant(\log x_{i})^{1/4}
, hence,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(x_{i}/\gcd(a_{i},W_{z}))}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log x_{i}}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log W_{z}}{\log x_{i}}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log x_{i}}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log\log x_{i}}{\log x_{i}}\right)\right),

thus, introducing the error term

\ll\frac{\log\log\min x_{i}}{\log\min x_{i}}\frac{(\log z)^{R/2}}{W_{z}^{R}}\prod_{i=1}^{R}\frac{x_{i}}{\sqrt{\log x_{i}}},

which is satisfactory.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

It is not difficult to
see that for any fixed
A>0
one has

\gamma_{0}=1+O_{A}\left(\frac{1}{(\log z)^{A}}\right).

Indeed, we have

\log\gamma_{0}=O\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)+\sum_{p>z}\left(-\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{\mathds{1}((\frac{D}{p})=1)}{p}\right).

Using the strong version of the prime number theorem
giving arbitrary logarithmic saving
and partial summation one can prove that

\sum_{z<p\leqslant t}\frac{1}{2p}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\log t}{\log z}+O_{A}\left(\frac{1}{(\log z)^{A}}\right)\textrm{ and }\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<p\leqslant t\\
(\frac{D}{p})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\log t}{\log z}+O_{A}\left(\frac{1}{(\log z)^{A}}\right).

This leads to
\log\gamma_{0}\ll_{A}(\log z)^{-A}
,
hence
\gamma_{0}=1+O_{A}((\log z)^{-A})
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Fix

and for any prime

let

be a subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})^{R}

for all

we have

\sharp\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{R}:|n_{i}|\leqslant N_{i}\ \forall i,\,\,\mathbf{n}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{s}\right)\notin Y_{p}\ \forall p\leqslant L\}\ll_{s,R}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{R}(N_{i}+L^{2s})}{F(L)}

where the implied constant depends at most on

and we define

1\leqslant m\leqslant L\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\ \mathrm{prime}\\
p\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\sharp Y_{p}}{p^{Rs}-\sharp Y_{p}}.

We provide a direct application below.
Recall the definition of
\mathscr{N}_{D}
in (
1.2

Lemma 3.12
.

Fix
R\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
for all
N_{1},\ldots,N_{R}>1
we have

\sharp\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{R}:n_{1}\cdots n_{R}\in\mathscr{N}_{D},|n_{i}|\leqslant N_{i}\ \forall i\}\ll_{D,R}\frac{N_{1}\cdots N_{R}}{\min_{i}(\log N_{i})^{R/2}}

where the implied constant depends at most on
D
and

R
.

Proof.

We employ
Lemma
3.11

with
s=2,R=r,L=\min\{N_{i}\}
and

Y_{p}=\{\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{R}:y_{1}\cdots y_{R}\equiv 0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)\text{ and }y_{1}\cdots y_{R}\not\equiv 0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{2}\right)\}

for primes
p

such that
(\frac{D}{p})=-1
and

p>2R
. We let
Y_{p}=\varnothing
for all other primes.
Note that if an integer
m
is in
\mathscr{N}_{D}

then for every prime
p
with
(\frac{D}{p})=-1

we must have
v_{p}(m)\neq 1
. Therefore,
for all primes
p
with
(\frac{D}{p})=-1

and all
\mathbf{n}
with

in
\mathscr{N}_{D}

we must have that the image of

is in the
complement of
Y_{p}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p^{2}\right)
.
From Lemma
3.11

we obtain the upper bound

\ll N_{1}\cdots N_{R}/F(\min N_{i}).

To estimate
F
we need a lower bound on
\sharp Y_{p}
.
Note
\sharp Y_{p}=M_{p}-E_{p}
, where

M_{p}=\sharp\{\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{R}:\exists i\in\{1,\dots,R\},\,p\mid y_{i}\},E_{p}=\sharp\{\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{R}:p^{2}\mid y_{1}\cdots y_{R}\}.

We clearly have

M_{p}=Rp^{2(R-1)}\sharp\{y_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}:p\mid y_{1}\}=Rp^{2R-1}

and

E_{p}\leqslant\sum_{i\neq j}\sharp\{\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{R}:p\mid(y_{1},y_{2})\}+R\sharp\{\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{R}:p^{2}\mid y_{1}\}\leqslant 2R^{2}p^{2R-2}.

Hence,
\sharp Y_{p}\geqslant p^{2R}(R/p-2R^{2}/p^{2})
, thus,

\frac{\sharp Y_{p}}{p^{2R}-\sharp Y_{p}}\geqslant\frac{\sharp Y_{p}}{p^{2R}}\geqslant\frac{f(p)}{p},

where

is a multiplicative function defined by

f(p^{e})=\mathds{1}\left(e=1\ \mbox{and}\ \Big(\frac{D}{p}\Big)=-1\ \mbox{and}\ p>2R\right)R\left(1-\frac{2R}{p}\right)

We obtain

F(T)\geqslant\sum_{m\leqslant T}f(m)/m
.
By
[
21
, Theorem 13.2]
with
\kappa=R/2

and
J=1
we get

\sum_{m\leqslant x}f(m)\gg x(\log x)^{R/2-1}.

By partial summation we then deduce

F(T)\gg(\log T)^{R/2}
, which concludes the proof. ∎

4.1.
Preparing the grounds

Recall the definition of

We can write

where

is as in (

By Möbius inversion this becomes

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\mu(k)N_{0}(B^{1/(n+1)}/k)

shows that the

\ll\sum_{k>(\log B)^{2R/n}}\frac{B}{k^{n+1}}\ll\frac{B}{(\log B)^{2R}}\leqslant\frac{B}{(\log B)^{1+R/2}},

hence,

(4.1)

{N(B)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant(\log B)^{2R/n}}\mu(k)N_{0}\left(\frac{B^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}{k}\right)+O\left(\frac{B}{(\log B)^{1+R/2}}\right).}

As this point we should use
the circle method tool, namely

2.5
. However,

we first show that the remaining

up to a negligible error term.

We begin by showing
that for
most
\mathbf{x}
in

the integers

are relatively coprime
with respect to large primes.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix any
i\neq j
among the integers in
\{1,\dots,R\}
.
Then for all

we have

\sharp\left\{\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}\cap[-P,P])^{n+1}:\begin{array}[]{l}f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\cdots f_{R}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathscr{N}_{D},\\
\exists p>z:p\mid(f_{i}(x),f_{j}(x))\end{array}\right\}\ll\frac{1}{z(\log z)}\frac{P^{n+1}}{(\log P)^{R/2}}+\frac{P^{n+1/2}}{(\log P)^{1/2}},

where the implied constant
only depends on
D,R
and the
f_{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9

with
\mathscr{A}=\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{R}:m_{1}\cdots m_{R}\in\mathscr{N}_{D}\}

we obtain the bound

\ll P^{n+1-d}+P^{n+1-Rd}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant R}\sum_{p>z}\sharp\left\{\mathbf{m}\in(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})^{R}:\begin{array}[]{l}m_{1}\cdots m_{R}\in\mathscr{N}_{D},\ |m_{i}|\leqslant bP^{d},\\
p\mid(m_{i},m_{j})\end{array}\right\},

where

comes from the cases where

f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\cdots f_{R}(\mathbf{x})=0
.
The contribution of

is

\ll P^{n+1-Rd}\sum_{p\geqslant\sqrt{P}}P^{d(R-2)}\frac{P^{2d}}{p^{2}}\ll\frac{P^{n+1/2}}{\log P}.

Here we used the bound

,
rather than

, as we have excluded
the cases where

.
If

If

\sharp\left\{\mathbf{m}\in(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})^{R}:\begin{array}[]{l}m_{1}\cdots m_{R}\in\mathscr{N}_{D},\\

.
This concludes the proof

∎

4.2.

,

5.
The leading constant

In the previous sections we have obtained an asymptotic formula for the number of

of bounded height for which the conic

(5.1)

has a point. To interpret this result geometrically we will use the projective conic bundle

whose fibre over
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
is given by (

). To construct this total space

we could define it on the chart

as a subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
. We can glue these charts together as
dR
is even as one does for example for classical Châtelet surfaces, and is explained in detail in
[
40
, §1.1]
, which produces a scheme

which is smooth and proper over the base field

A huge component of the geometric interpretation relates to the non-split fibres as
X
is smooth and proper, so let us note that the fibres of
f
are split away from the non-empty open subscheme

U:=\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{R}\{f_{i}(\mathbf{t})=0\}.

We define

as the preimage of

. One also easily verifies that

is smooth away from the intersections
\{f_{i}(\textbf{t})=0=f_{j}(\textbf{t})\}
for

We can now rephrase the asymptotic result in Theorem

for

as

\frac{\sharp\{\textbf{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Z})\colon H(\textbf{t})\leqslant B,f^{-1}(\textbf{t})(\mathbb{Q})\neq\varnothing\}}{B(\log B)^{-R/2}}\underset{B\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{2\zeta(n+1)}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{n+1}}{\sqrt{\pi d}}\right)^{R}\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{s}\in\{1,-1\}^{R}\\
(D,s_{1}\cdots s_{R})_{\mathbb{R}}=1\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.69046pt\gamma_{\infty}(\mathbf{s})\gamma(\mathbf{s})

for

and

is the standard Lebesgue measure. For smooth conics the Hasse principle holds, and so we could equivalently consider the everywhere locally soluble fibres.
An asymptotic for this counting problem under an anticanonical adelic height has been conjectured in

\frac{\sharp\{\textbf{t}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{Z})\colon H(\textbf{t})\leqslant B,f^{-1}(\textbf{t})(\mathbb{Q})\neq\varnothing\}}{B(\log B)^{-\Delta(f)}}\to c_{f,\text{pred}}

Proposition 5.1
.

The predicted order of growth and leading constant

agrees with the order and constant in (

).

We will recall the notation in the predicted constant as we compute each factor separately. The only part that solely depends on the base is the classical

5.1.

The fibration

is smooth away from the zero locus of the

Lemma 5.2
.

We have

,

are as in Corollary

, the divisors

are geometrically irreducible as proven in

. The fibre

consists of two geometrical components interchanged by the non-trivial element of the relevant Galois group, hence

. We conclude

As the anticanonical divisor of the base

is of degree

and

For

the definition in

As a corollary we obtain that the product of Fujita invariants equals

5.2.
The subordinate Brauer group

We will see that the reciprocity condition involving the quadratic symbol
(\frac{D}{\cdot})

in Lemma
4.4

comes from the following ramified elements of the Brauer group.
This distinction is reminiscent of the case of Châtelet type surfaces
x^{2}-Dy^{2}=f(x)
with
f
of even degree (see
[
9
,
10
]
and
[
38
, Chapter 7]
). If
f
is irreducible or the product of irreducible factors of odd degree, then the unramified Brauer group is constant, and the Hasse principle and weak approximation are conjectured to hold, and known to hold when
\deg(f)=4
. If however
f
factors into two irreducible factors of even degree, then the unramified Brauer group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and one obtains a Brauer–Manin obstruction and counter-examples to weak approximation and to the Hasse principle.

Proposition 5.3
.

The subordinate Brauer group modulo constants is generated by
(D,g(\mathbf{t}))\in\Br\kappa(\mathbb{P}^{n})
for
g\in\mathscr{G}
where

\mathscr{G}=\begin{cases}\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{R}\},&\text{ if $\deg f_{i}$ is even for all $i$};\\
\{f_{1}f_{2},\ldots,f_{1}f_{R}\},&\text{ if $\deg f_{i}$ is odd for all $i$}.\end{cases}

Hence
\sharp\left[\Brsub(\mathbb{P}^{n},f)/\Br\mathbb{Q}\right]
equals
2^{R}
in the even degree case, and
2^{R-1}
in the odd degree case.

Proof.

By definition of the subordinate Brauer group we are looking at the elements
\mathscr{A}\in\Br\kappa(\mathbb{P}^{n})
which are unramified away from the geometrically irreducible divisors
D_{i}:=\{f_{i}=0\}
, and whose residue along each
D_{i}
is either
1,D\in\kappa(D_{i})^{\times}/\kappa(D_{i})^{\times,2}
. By Theorem 3.6.1(ii) in
[
11
]
the class of the element
\mathscr{A}
modulo constants is uniquely determined by these constraints if it exists, this proves that there are at most
2^{R}
classes of the subordinate Brauer group modulo constants.

In the case that
d
is even we see that the elements
(D,\prod_{i\in I}f_{i})
for
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,R\}
are the
2^{R}
elements we were looking for.

In the case that
d
is odd we have the
2^{R-1}
elements
(D,\prod_{i\in I}f_{i})
with
\sharp I
even. To show that other combinations of residues do not occur, it suffices to show that there is no
\mathscr{A}\in\Br\kappa(\mathbb{P}^{n})
which is unramified away from
D_{1}
and has residue
D
along
D_{1}
. Let us consider a line
L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
, which is not contained in
D_{1}
. We will study
\left.\mathscr{A}\right|_{L}\in\Br\kappa(L)
and proceed by contradiction.

As
\mathscr{A}
is unramified away from
D_{1}
, the restriction
\left.\mathscr{A}\right|_{L}
is unramified away from
D_{1}\cap L
. This intersection is supported on finitely many closed points
P_{i}\in L
of degree
d_{i}
and multiplicity
m_{i}
. As
D_{1}
has degree
d
we find
\sum_{i}m_{i}d_{i}=d
. The residue at
P_{i}
equals
D^{m_{i}}\in\kappa(P_{i})^{\times}/\kappa(P_{i})^{\times,2}
by
[
11
, Theorem 3.7.5]
which applies as
D_{1}
is smooth. By Faddeev’s exact sequence
[
11
, Theorem 1.5.2]
we find that, multiplicatively written and modulo squares,
1=\prod_{i}\mbox{cores}_{\kappa(P_{i})/\mathbb{Q}}(D^{m_{i}})=\prod_{i}D^{d_{i}m_{i}}=D^{d}
. This is a contradiction as
d
is odd, and
D
is not a square.
∎

One can also prove that the vertical Brauer group of
f
is constant, and the natural inclusion
\Brvert(f)\subseteq f^{*}\Brsub(\mathbb{P}^{n},f)
is not an equality. This shows that there is no smooth proper model of
X
over
\mathbb{P}^{n}
which is flat over the base, as in
[
24
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Filtering of Stochastic Nonlinear Wave Equations
Sivaguru S. Sritharan 2* and Saba Mudaliar 1

Abstract
. In this paper we will develop linear and nonlinear filtering methods for a large class of nonlinear wave equations that arise in applications such as quantum dynamics and laser generation and propagation in a unified framework. We consider both stochastic calculus and white noise filtering methods and derive measure-valued evolution equations for the nonlinear filter and prove existence and uniqueness theorems for the solutions. We will also study first order approximations to these measure-valued evolutions by linearizing the wave equations and characterize the filter dynamics in terms of infinite dimensional operator Riccati equations and establish solvability theorems.

Introduction

Sensing and estimation of random fields has many applications in engineering sciences. One of the most powerful techniques developed in this context is filtering theory and has its origins in the works of Wiener and Kolmogorov
[
82
,
36
]
. Filtering of Markov process was initiated by Stratenovich
[
71
]
. However, the filtering of linear systems developed by Kalman has gained wide spread utilization due to its readily implementable form of tracking the state estimator and error covariance in a recurssive manner. Nonlinear filtering theory of Stratenovich was further crystelized by several authors (see
[
37
,
38
]
for a comprehensive discussion) leading to the famous Fujisaki-Kallianpur-Kunita and Zakai equations for the evolution of unnormalized as well as normalized conditional measures. Filtering of linear infinite dimensional systems was initiated by Bensoussan
[
7
]
to derive infinite dimensional Kalman filters. Filtering of nonlinear system of partial differential equations was initiated for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations
[
65
]
and stochastic reaction diffusion equations
[
32
]
. This subject was further developed to incorporate Lévy noise forced Navier-Stokes equations
[
23
]
and also classical and quantum spin systems
[
69
]
. In this paper we will further develop linear and nonlinear filtering theory to address a large class nonlinear wave equations with Gaussian and Lévy forcings with several specific applications in laser generation and propagation and quantum dynamics. Many important applications of the above type can be captured in a unified mathematical model of nonlinear wave equations as observed by several authors
[
55
,
57
,
58
,
72
,
73
]
. We will develop our filtering study to such general class of nonlinear evolutions.

We discuss next a summary of the sections of this paper. In the remaining part of this section we provide a general mathematical framework of semilinear wave equations in a framework suitable for semigroup treatment of this system, discuss the mathematical properties of the operators involved and also provide a solvability theorem for the deterministic nonlinear wave problem. In Section 2 we discuss four important applications in laser generation and propagation and demonstrate that all of these models can be cast in our unified framework. In Section 3 we discuss the solvability theory of stochastic nonlinear wave equations by casting the problem in the mild solution form. In section 4 we develop nonlinear filtering for the stochastic wave equation in the Ito calculus framework and in Section 5 we develop white noise filtering method. In Section 6 we consider linear stochastic wave equation and show that the nonlinear filtering equations of Ito calculus and white noise type result in infinite dimensional Kalman filters. We describe the time evolution of least square best estimate and the associated dynamics of error covariance in terms of infinite dimensional Riccati equations and establish their solvability. Finally we also wish to point out that to our knowledge this is the first time linear and nonlinear filtering method has been developed for stochastic wave equations.

We will collect some relevant facts on

(2)

(3)

2.1.
Continuous and Pulse Wave Models: The Stochastic Paraxial and Klein-Gordon Equations

The paraxial equation is a very well-known model widely used in the literature on the acoustic wave and electromagnetic wave propagation in random media
[

,
53
,
77
,
74
,
63

64

29

(2.1)

\Delta E(x,t)-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}(n^{2}(x,t)E(x,t))=-2\nabla(E(x,t)\cdot\nabla\log(n(x,t))),

where

is the refractive index of the medium and

is the speed of light. We assume that the time scale of fluctuations in the medium is much slower than the light speed and invoke further simplifications based on this assumption. Thus neglecting the right hand side and also the

term out of the time derivative we arrive at

(2.2)

\Delta E(x,t)-\frac{n^{2}(x,t)}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}E(x,t)=0.

Substituting a plane wave solution
E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)=\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\exp(ikx_{3}-i\omega t)
and neglecting the back-scatter term
\frac{\partial^{2}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}
(using simple scaling argument, see for example
[
53
,
74
]
) we arrive at the nonlinear random paraxial equation:

i\frac{\partial\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}{\partial x_{3}}+\Delta_{\bot}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+\chi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

(2.3)

\pm|\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|^{p-1}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=0.

where
\Delta_{\bot}
is the two dimensional Laplacian in the variables
x_{2},x_{3}
and
\chi
is a random field that depends on the medium and the field strength.
Renaming the time-like variable

as

and suppressing the actual time variable

we end up with the

i\frac{\partial\psi(x_{1},x_{2},t)}{\partial t}+\Delta_{\bot}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},t)+\chi(x_{1},x_{2},t)\psi(x_{1},x_{2},t)

(2.4)

\pm|\psi(x_{1},x_{2},t)|^{p-1}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},t)=0.

On the other hand, using a wave envelop type representation

E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)=\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)\exp(ikx_{3}-i\omega t)

in

,we arrive at a time dependent random nonlinear wave equation
[
64
]

(2.5)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\psi(x,t)-(\Delta-k_{0}^{2})\psi(x,t)+\chi(x,t)\psi(x,t)\pm|\psi(x,t)|^{p-1}\psi(x,t)=0,

where

is a random field that depend on the medium. Here we have also neglected certain first order derivative terms that appear in
[

to arrive at nonlinear random Klein-Gordon type wave equation. Setting

we reframe the above dynamics as:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{bmatrix}\psi\\

-B^{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\psi\\

\pm|\psi|^{p-1}\psi\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0\\

where

H=D(B)\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mbox{ and }D(A)=D(B^{2})\oplus D(B).

Then we have the following estimate
[
57

Lemma 2.1

Setting
\varphi=\langle\psi,v\rangle
and

(1)

(2)

\|J(\varphi_{1})-J(\varphi_{2})\|\leq C(\|\varphi_{1}\|,\|\varphi_{2}\|)\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|
,

(3)

\|AJ(\varphi_{1})\|\leq K\|\varphi_{1}\|^{2}\|A\varphi_{1}\|
,

\|A(J(\varphi_{1})-J(\varphi_{2}))\|\leq C(\|\varphi_{1}\|,\|\varphi_{2}\|,\|A\varphi_{1}\|,\|A\varphi_{2}\|)\|A\varphi_{1}-A\varphi_{2}\|

6.
First-order Approximation of Nonlinear Filter: Infinite dimensional Kalman filter

Let us now consider a linear wave equation obtained by linearizing the stochastic nonlinear wave equation
1.1
by perturbing about a basic state
X_{0}(t)\in D(A)
. Once again the process
X(t)\in H,t\geq 0
is the signal process and it is governed by the linear stochastic evolution:

(6.1)

dX(t)={\mathcal{A}}(t)X(t)dt+BdM,

X(0)=x,

where
{\mathcal{A}}(t)=(-iA+J^{\prime}(X_{0}(t)))
.

The measurement process
Y(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N},t\geq 0
is defined as

(6.2)

dY(t)=h(t)X(t)dt+dZ(t),

where
Z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a
N
-dimensional Wiener process which may or may not be correlated with
W(t)
. The observation vector
h(\cdot):[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(H;\mathbb{R}^{N})
can be taken as
L^{\infty}
in time.

Let us denote by
{\mathcal{F}}^{Y}_{t}
the filtration generated by the sensor data over the time period
0\leq s\leq t
(sigma algebra generated by the back measurement):

{\mathcal{F}}^{Y}_{t}=\sigma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{Y_{s},0\leq s\leq t}}\right\}.

The Theorem
1.5
assures that the deterministic linearized problem is globally uniquely solvable and linear evolution operator associated with
{\mathcal{A}}(t)=-iA+J^{\prime}(X_{0}(t))
as
{\mathcal{S}}(t,\tau)
:

(6.3)

\frac{d{\mathcal{S}}(t,\tau)x}{dt}={\mathcal{A}}(t){\mathcal{S}}(t,\tau)x,

is well defined as a bounded two parameter semigroup in
H
. We can express the evolution
6.1
in the mild form as

(6.4)

X(t)={\mathcal{S}}(t,0)x+\int_{0}^{t}S(t,r)B(r)dM(r),

and deduce its unique global solvability from Theorem
3.1
. In the Gaussian case we take as before
M(t)=W(t)\in H
which is a
H
-valued Wiener process and then the mean:

E[X(t)]={\mathcal{S}}(t,0)x,

and variance

V_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{S}}(t,r)B(r)QB^{*}(r){\mathcal{S}}^{*}(t,r)dr.

Let us define the best least square estimate defined by the conditional expectation
\hat{X}(t)=E[X(t)|{\mathcal{F}}^{Y}_{t}]
and error covariance

(P(t)\zeta,\eta)=E[(X(t)-\hat{X}(t),\zeta)((X(t)-\hat{X}(t),\eta)],\hskip 7.22743pt\forall\zeta,\eta\in H.

Then it can be shown that (see chapter 10,
[
37
]
for the stochastic calculus case and chapter XII,
[
38
]
for white noise calculus case with the finite dimensional derivation in those references easily generalize to infinite dimensions) that
\hat{X}(t)
evolves according to

(6.5)

d\hat{X}(t)=[{\mathcal{A}}(t)-P(t)h^{*}(t)h(t)]\hat{X}(t)dt+P(t)h^{*}(t)dY(t)

for the stochastic calculus case and

(6.6)

\frac{d\hat{X}}{dt}=[{\mathcal{A}}(t)-P(t)h^{*}(t)h(t)]\hat{X}(t)+P(t)h^{*}(t)Y(t)

for the white noise case.
The error covariance
P(t)
satisfies the operator Riccati equation:

(6.7)

\frac{d}{dt}\langle P(t)x,y\rangle-\langle P(t)x,{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(t)y\rangle-\langle{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(t)x,P(t)y\rangle+\langle P(t)h^{*}(t)h(t)P(t)x,y\rangle=\langle Fx,y\rangle,\mbox{ a.e.}

P(0)=P_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}(H),\hskip 7.22743ptx,y\in D({\mathcal{A}}^{*}(t)).

Here
F=BQ{\mathrm{B}}^{*}
for the Gaussian case and
F=B\Lambda{\mathrm{B}}^{*}
for the pure jump case. Let us now give a general solvability theorem for operator Riccati equation that covers both of the above cases. Direct study of infinite dimensional Riccati equations was initiated by J. L. Lions
[
47
]
, R. Temam
[
79
]
, G. Da Prato
[
17
,
18
]
, L. Tartar
[
78
]
and V. Barbu
[
5
]
and also H. J. Kuiper
[
44
,
45
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
P_{0},F\in{\mathcal{L}}(H)
and
P_{0}\geq 0
, i.e.,
(P_{0}\zeta,\zeta)\geq 0,\forall\zeta\in H
, then there exists a unique positive strongly continuous global solution
P(\cdot)\in C_{s}([0.T];{\mathcal{L}}(H))
. Moreover, if
P_{0},F\in{\mathcal{J}}_{i}(H),i=1,2
(respectively trace class or Hilbert-Schmidt) then
P(\cdot)\in C_{s}([0.T];{\mathcal{J}}_{i}(H)),i=1,2.
More generally if
P_{0}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{p}(H)
(a trace-ideal) then
P(\cdot)\in C_{s}([0.T];{\mathcal{J}}_{p}(H))
.

We recall here
[
56
]
that for a bounded operator
A\in{\mathcal{L}}(H)
, polar decomposition can be expressed as
A=U|A|
where
U
is a partial isometry in
H
and
|A|=\sqrt{A^{*}A}
. Trace-class operators
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(H)
is the set
A\in{\mathcal{L}}(H)
such that
\mbox{ tr}(|A|)<\infty.
We define

{\mathcal{J}}_{p}(H)=\{A\in{\mathcal{L}}(H):|A|^{p}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\},\mbox{ with norm }\|A\|_{{\mathcal{J}}_{p}(H)}=(\mbox{ Tr}(|A|^{p}))^{1/p}.

For the type of generator
{\mathcal{A}}(t)
we have in this paper, the above solvability theorem can be deduced from
[
17
,
18
]
for the case when
P(t)\in{\mathcal{L}}(H)
,
[
8
]
for the trace class case
P(t)\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(H)
and
[
9
]
for trace ideal case
P(t)\in{\mathcal{J}}_{p}(H),p\geq 1
.

We now brifly describe a well-known splitting of the Riccati equation due to S. Chandrasekhar
[
15
]
and introduced in filtering theory by Kailath
[
36
]
for the special case when
{\mathcal{A}}
and
h
are time invariant. Differentiating the operator Riccati equation formally in time we get

\langle\ddot{P}(t)x,y\rangle-\langle\dot{P}(t)x,{\mathcal{A}}^{*}y\rangle-\langle{\mathcal{A}}^{*}x,\dot{P}(t)y\rangle+\langle\dot{P}(t)h^{*}hP(t)x,y\rangle+\langle P(t)h^{*}h\dot{P}(t)x,y\rangle=0.

Setting
K(t)=P(t)h^{*}
we can write this formally as

(6.8)

\ddot{P}(t)=({\mathcal{A}}-K(t){\mathcal{H}})\dot{P}(t)+\dot{P}(t)({\mathcal{A}}-K(t)h)^{*}.

The solution of this operator equation is expressed as

\dot{P}(t)={\mathcal{S}}_{k}(t,0)\dot{P}(0){\mathcal{S}}_{k}^{*}(t,0),

where
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(t,0)
is the solution operator for the operator evolution

(6.9)

\frac{dL(t)}{dt}=({\mathcal{A}}-K(t)h)L(t),\hskip 7.22743ptL(t_{0})=L_{0}.

We differentiate
K(t)
to get another operator evolution equation:

(6.10)

\frac{dK(t)}{dt}=\dot{P}(t)h^{*}=L(t)\dot{P}(0)L^{*}(t)h^{*},\hskip 7.22743ptK(t_{0})=\dot{P}(0)h^{*}.

Here we can obtain
\dot{P}(0)
by

(6.11)

\dot{P}(0)={\mathcal{A}}P(0)+P(0){\mathcal{A}}^{*}+P(0)hh^{*}+Q,

with
Q
replaced by
\mbox{Tr}\Lambda
for the pure jump case. Note also that the Riccati solution is obtained by quadrature:

P(t)=P(0)+\int_{0}^{t}L(r)\dot{P}(0)L^{*}(r)dr.

For the linear feedback control problem, rigorous treatment of such Chandrasekhar system with
{\mathcal{A}}
generating a
C_{0}
-semigroup has been studied in
[
34
,
35
]
giving solvability for
L(t)
and
K(t)
as strongly continuous operator-valued evolutions.

Esketamine relieves postoperative depression and pain indicators in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy
Authors: Jun Jing, Meng-ying Zhang, Wei-hong Yin, Yong-jie Ji, Li Sun, Rui Qu, Yuan-hai Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate the effects of perioperative esketamine on postoperative depression and pain in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy.Methods135 patients undegoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy were recruited and randomly allocated to three groups. Finally, a total of 127 patients were selected into the statistical analysis, with the final grouping information as follows: sufentanil group (S1, n = 44), sufentanil combined with 0.25 mg/kg esketamine group (SK1, n = 42) and sufentanil combined with 0.5 mg/kg esketamine group (SK2,n = 41) intraoperatively, then postoperative analgesia was maintained with sufentanil (2 µg/kg) via patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) in all groups, while a 1 mg/kg dose of esketamine was added to the PCIA regimen for patients in groups SK1 and SK2. The peripheral blood serum brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) level, 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) level, Hamilton Depression Scale (HAM-D) scores, visual analogue scale(VAS) scores and the number of PCIA button pressed times in perioperative period were collected. Meanwhile, the postoperative adverse effects including nausea, vomiting, dizziness, respiratory depression and hallucinations were collected and compared between the three groups.ResultsRelative to preoperative baseline levels, BDNF and 5-HT levels decreased at the 1th day(1d) post surgery in all groups(P < 0.05), and then followed by a gradual increase thereafter. Compared with S1 group, the SK1 and SK2 group showed significantly higher serum BDNF and 5-HT levels at 1d, 2d and 5d after operation (P < 0.05), and revealed even higher at 1d and 2d after operation in SK2 group(P < 0.05). The HAM-D scores at 1d, 2d and 5d post operation were significantly reduced in SK1 and SK2 group (P < 0.05) compared to S1 group, and decreased even lower at 1d and 2d postoperative in SK2 group(P < 0.05), but no significant difference was found among three groups at 1d before and the 7d after operation. Simultaneously, the VAS scores decreased significantly in SK1 and SK2 group at the 1th hour(1 h), 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h after surgery (P < 0.05), and the PCIA button pressed times were also significantly reduced in SK1 and SK2 group (P < 0.05) during the postoperative 48 h. Furthermore, the SK1 and SK2 group showed the lower dosage of remifentanil during the surgery(P < 0.05). However, the postoperative adverse effects had no statistical differences among the three groups.ConclusionThe combined intraoperative and postoperative administration of esketamine was effective in alleviating postoperative depression and pain, without increasing adverse effects in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy. Moreover, the 0.5 mg/kg dosage intraoperatively may have the better alleviation property of depression-related indicators.Trial registrationThe study was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry at www.chictr.org.cn (registration date: October 31, 2022; registration number: ChiCTR2200065198).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-025-03570-5.

Background

Depression has showed an increasing incidence in recent years, especially in patients undergoing surgery [1]. A multicenter, large-sample study has reported that the incidence rate of depression in patients hospitalized for physical illness in China was 75.1% [2]. Laparoscopic total hysterectomy is a common gynecological procedure, which was known as “artificial menopause” and also has been found significantly related with the raised depression risk [3, 4]. To date, the Hamilton Depression Scale(HAM-D) is still one of the gold standard tools which used to assess depression in clinical and related studies [5]. The scale has multiple versions, the 24-item HAM-D offers broader symptom coverage. Based on standard cutoffs, a score ≥ 8 indicates mild depression, ≥ 20 moderate depression, and ≥ 35 severe depression.

Recently, ketamine has attracted much attention due to its rapid antidepressant effect in perioperative period [6, 7], which was primarily mediated by inhibiting N-methyl-d-aspartate receptors (NMDAR). But on the other hand, the administration of ketamine is associated with a prolonged awakening time and psychotic symptoms such as nightmares and hallucinations [8, 9]. Esketamine is the dextro-split of ketamine, it shows double anesthetic-analgesic-hypnotic strength and plasma clearance. And moreover, it can significantly shortern the awakening time and low the incidence of psychiatric side effects [10–12]. Xiao et al. reported that esketamine-based postoperative analgesia significantly attenuates perioperative pain in patients undergoing cesarean Sect [13].

Previous studies have confirmed that the bilateral voxel nuclei and cingulate-hippocampus in brain [14–16] and its related neurohumoral factors including 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) [17, 18] and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) [19–21] were closely related with the development of depression. And the antidepressant efficacy was obviously associated with the structural changes and synaptic remodeling in cingulate-hippocampus [22]. In addition to this, recent evidences have shown that the acute and chronic pain may also be the vital factor in the development of postoperative depression [23–25].

Up to now, few studies have reported the antidepressant and pain relief effect of esketamine in postoperation of laparoscopic total hysterectomy. In this clinical trial, we aim to evaluate the effects of perioperative esketamine administration on postoperative depression and pain indicators including peripheral blood serum BDNF level, 5-HT level, 24-item HAM-D, visual analogue scale(VAS) scores and the number of patient-controlled intravenous analgesia(PCIA) button pressed times in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy. In the meantime, the postoperative adverse effects were also recorded and we try to provide some valuable references for medical practice of esketamine.

Materials and methods

Ethics

The Maanshan People’s Hospital’s Ethics Committee approved this clinical study (No. 2021-006-003), which was also registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, with the registration number ChiCTR2200065198 and registration date of October 31, 2022. Following preliminary experiments, the monitoring indicators were refined to streamline our resources and more effectively concentrate on the core objectives of the research. The revised protocol received full ethical approval (No. 2022-006-003) before implemented. All subjects provided written informed consent prior to participation in the study. The study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration (2013 October) and clinical practice guidelines.

General information

135 patients who underwent planned laparoscopic total hysterectomy were recruited from Maanshan People’s Hospital from November 2022 to December 2023, then randomly allocated to three groups through simple randomization using a computer-generated random sequence (randomization ratio 1:1:1): the sufentanil group (S1, n = 44), sufentanil combined with 0.25 mg/kg esketamine group (SK1,n = 42) and sufentanil combined with 0.5 mg/kg esketamine group (SK2,n = 41) intraoperatively, then all patients received postoperative PCIA with sufentanil (2 µg/kg), while those in groups SK1 and SK2 received an additional 1 mg/kg of esketamine in the PCIA solution. And a possible 10% attrition rate was considered.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) age ranged 30–60 years; (b) body mass index (BMI) ranged 18–30 kg/m2; (c) HAM-D score ranged 8–20; (d) followed the criteria defined by the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) for classification as class I-II; (e) the preoperative cognitive function score of Mini Mental Status Examination (MMSE) was greater than 27 points; (f) approved to voluntarily participate in the investigation and the provision of informed consent for trial participation.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients with confirmed psychiatric disorders such as diagnosed depression, mania and schizophrenia; (b) patients with chronic cardiac insufficiency (NYHA class III-IV); (c) patients with abnormal liver and kidney function (alanine transaminase, aspartate aminotransferase > 3 times the upper limit of normal; estimated glomerular filtration rate less than 60 ml/min/1.73m2); (d) severe visual, speech or hearing impairment; (e) increased abnormal intracranial pressure or intraocular pressure; (f) long-term or recent use of sedatives, analgesics, nonsteroidal drugs, glucocorticoids, psychotropic drugs, alcohol or drug abuse; (g) allergy to any of the study drug components.

Anesthesia method

Series of medical procedures were initiated since the admission of patient to the operating theater, including administration of oxygen, establishment of peripheral venous access and monitoring of various physiological parameters involved noninvasive blood pressure, pulse oximetry, electrocardiogram, body temperature, intraoperative partial pressure of end-expiratory carbon dioxide (PetCO2) and depth of anesthesia (bispectral index [BIS]). General anesthesia was administered through a combination of intravenous and inhalation anesthesia. Rapid intravenous anesthesia was induced with etomidate at a dosage of 0.3 mg/kg, sufentanil at a dosage of 0.3 ~ 0.4 µg/kg, midazolam at a dosage of 0.05 mg/kg, and rocuronium at a dosage of 0.6 mg/kg. 2 min later, endotracheal intubation and mechanical control of breathing were initiated. Before surgical skin incision, the S1, SK1, and SK2 group received 0.1 µg/kg sufentanil, 0.1 µg/kg sufentanil combined with 0.25 mg/kg esketamine(Lot number:221019BL, Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., China), and 0.1 µg/kg sufentanil combined with 0.5 mg/kg esketamine intravenously, respectively. Anesthesia was maintained with 0.5%~1.5% sevoflurane, 3 ~ 4 mg/(kg·h) propofol, and 0.2 ~ 0.4 µg/(kg·h) remifentanil. The BIS value was preserved between 40 ~ 60 intraoperatively, and the concentration of anesthetic drugs was based on blood pressure, heart rate and changes in patient’s other related vital signs. Intermittent administration of Rocuronium bromide at a dosage of 0.15 mg/kg was performed, and discontinued at 45 min prior to the completion of the surgical procedure. A further dose of 0.1 µg/kg sufentanil was administered, and sevoflurane inhalation was terminated once the peritoneum was closed. The PCIA system was linked at 10 min prior to the end of the surgical procedures.

The administration of postoperative PCIA was performed that the S1 group received 2 µg/kg of sufentanil, the SK1 and SK2 groups received a combination of 2 µg/kg of sufentanil and 1 mg/kg of esketamine, and 0.9% sodium chloride solution was utilized to dilute each solution to 100 ml. The parameters of PCIA were as follows: first dose, 2 ml; background infusion, 2 ml/h; 1 ml for each self-controlled dose; lock time, 10 min; limit, 10 ml/h. All participants were sedated with 10 mg of azasetron at 10 min before the completion of surgical procedure and then sent to the anesthesia resuscitation unit after surgery, and we controlled all patients’ postoperative VAS scores within 3 points.

Observation indicators

All patients’ age, BMI, preoperative MMSE score, ASA classification, education level, marital status, childbirth status, operation time, anesthesia time, intraoperative bleeding, and intraoperative analgesic dosage were recorded.

HAM-D scores, peripheral blood serum BDNF and 5-HT levels were collected at 1th day(1d) before, the 1d, 2d, 5d and 7d after the operation, respectively.

The patients’ VAS scores were recorded at the 1th hour(1 h), 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h after operation; the total number of PCIA button presses and the incidence of adverse reactions including nausea, vomiting, dizziness, respiratory depression and hallucinations were collected during the postoperative 48 h.

The primary outcomes were the HAM-D scores, VAS scores, BDNF and 5-HT levels, with the PCIA button pressed times and the incidence of adverse reactions as the secondary outcomes.

To ensure unbiased assessment, the HAM-D and VAS scores were collected by assessors who were blinded to the group assignments.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was determined based on the primary outcome (postoperative HAM-D scores ). Using G*Power 3.1 with α = 0.05, power = 0.80, and effect size f = 0.3 derived from prior studies [26], a minimum of 42 patients per group was required for one-way analysis of variance(ANOVA). Accounting for 10% attrition, we recruited 45 patients per group (total N = 135). Post-hoc analysis confirmed > 80% power with 127 valid cases included in the final analysis.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed using statistical package for social sciences(SPSS) 22.0. The normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The normal distribution data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. One-way ANOVA was used for comparing the single independent variable across multiple groups, repeated-measures ANOVA for comparing data collected at multiple time points within the three groups, Kruskal-Wallis H-test for comparing multiple groups of rank data, chi-square test and fisher test were used for comparing rates. A statistically significant variation was determined to be present when P < 0.05.

Results

Participate figures

A total of 135 patients underwent planned laparoscopic total hysterectomy were screened in the study. During the follow-up period, one participant in group S1, two in group SK1, and two in group SK2 were lost to follow-up. Additionally, in group SK1, one subject discontinued the intervention due to postoperative bleeding. In group SK2, one participant requested withdrawal from the trial after surgery, and another discontinued the intervention due to postoperative acute kidney injury. Finally, a total of 127 patients were selected into the statistical analysis, with the final grouping information as follows: S1, n = 44; SK1,n = 42; and SK2,n = 41 (Fig. 1).

Discussion

In our study, we employed a protocol combining intraoperative intravenous administration with postoperative PCIA to evaluate the antidepressant and analgesic effects of esketamine in patients following laparoscopic total hysterectomy. And we found that this medication regimen effectively alleviated both depression and pain during the early postoperative period, which was accompanied by changes in peripheral blood serum levels of BDNF and 5-HT.

Previous studies have indicated that intravenous esketamine administration could reduce postoperative depression scores in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic abdominal surgery [27]. However, it still remains controversial. Dai J et al. reported that although preoperative administration of a subanesthetic dose of esketamine significantly reduced postoperative depression symptoms, but the intraoperative administration provided no such benefit in patients undergoing laparoscopic bariatric surgery [28]. In this reseach, most patients exhibited mild depressive symptoms at 1d pre-surgery according to the HAM-D scores, but had no significant difference among three groups. And a slight increase in HAM-D scores was observed at 1d after surgery and then gradually decreased in group S1, whereas both the SK1 and SK2 groups displayed a continuous downward trend throughout the postoperative period. Additionally, the HAM-D scores at 1d, 2d and 5d postoperative were significantly lower in SK1 and SK2 group compared to S1 group (P < 0.05), and even lower at 1d and 2d postoperative in SK2 group(P < 0.05). These data indicated that patients are vulnerable to depressive symptoms during the perioperative period of laparoscopic total hysterectomy, including the day before surgery. The combined intraoperative intravenous and postoperative PCIA esketamine regimen effectively alleviated early postoperative depression symptoms in such patients. Moreover, a single intraoperative intravenous bolus of 0.5 mg/kg esketamine demonstrated superior improvement in depression-related outcomes.

Evidence from animal models have demonstrated that the BDNF and a specific 5-HT receptors are decreased significantly in the brains of rats with depression, particularly in regions such as the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex [29]. A reduction in BDNF levels induces synaptic structural impairment and a decline in amino acid neurotransmitter levels through the BDNF-TrkB-PSD95/VGLUT1 pathway, a mechanism implicated in cognitive dysfunction [30]. Meanwhile, Huang D et al. observed a strong association between the pattern classification of 5-HT and the occurrence of anxiety and depressive states in patients with psoriasis [31]. We found that no significant differences in serum BDNF or 5-HT levels were observed among the groups preoperatively. Compared to preoperative baseline levels, both BDNF and 5-HT levels decreased at 1d after surgery in all groups, then followed by a gradual increase thereafter. Notably, the SK1 and SK2 groups exhibited significantly higher serum BDNF and 5-HT levels at 1d, 2d and 5d postoperative contrast to S1 group(P < 0.05). Furthermore, the SK2 group showed even higher levels than the SK1 group at 1d and 2d post-surgery(P < 0.05). Our results suggested that the antidepressant effects of esketamine may be partially mediated through the modulation of BDNF and 5-HT pathways.

Experience of pain has been considered to modulate the synaptic connectivity in prefrontal cortex and hippocampus, in addition to the alteration of dopamine signaling obtained from the ventral tegmental area, and these changes may partly contribute to the occurrence of depression [32, 33]. Here, despite maintaining all patients’ postoperative VAS scores within 3 points, we still found that scores decreased significantly in SK1 and SK2 group compared to S1 group at 1 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h post surgery (P < 0.05), although the observed clinical difference was only about 0.5 scores, and which may partly attributed to some subjective factors from the patients. Furthermore, in contrast to the S1 group, we observed that the number of postoperative PCIA button pressed time was also markedly reduced in SK1 and SK2 groups (P < 0.05) during the postoperative 48 h. Moreover, the SK1 and SK2 groups required a significantly lower dosage of intraoperative remifentanil(P < 0.05). These findings indicated that the combination use with esketamine intraoperative and postoperative can reduce early postoperative pain in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy, and which may partly contribute to the postoperative depression indicators remission. Our result aligns with the recent findings of Zhang B et al. [34].

Recent studies have reported that the incidence of nausea and vomiting within 24 h after gynecologic laparoscopic surgery ranged from 28% to 39% [35, 36]. In this research, we observed that the SK1 group exhibited a slightly decreased incidence of postoperative nausea, vomiting and dizziness compared with S1 and SK2 group, whereas none revealed a statistical significant among the groups(P > 0.05), which indicated that the combination use of esketamine intraoperative and postoperative in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy don’t increase the incidence of adverse events.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that the perioperative administration of esketamine, combining an intravenous intraoperative dose with postoperative PCIA, can alleviate early postoperative depressive symptoms in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy. This effect may be partly attributed to the modulation of BDNF and 5-HT pathways, as well as improved postoperative pain control. A singular intraoperative esketamine sedative dose of 0.5 mg/kg may show the better reduction property of depression-related indicators. Notably, the treatment did not increase the incidence of adverse effects. However, this investigation still has several limitations. The sample size was small, and the anesthesia protocol was not standardized, with some patients receiving inhalational anesthesia and others propofol TIVA, which may potentially influencing outcomes such as postoperative agitation or depression. Therefore, further investigations with larger sample sizes and standardized anesthetic protocol are required to elucidate the exact mechanisms involved.

I. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Terms of Substance 
of the Proposed Rule Change

    The Exchange proposes to amend Rule 7.10 (Clearly Erroneous 
Executions). The proposed rule change is available on the Exchange's 
website at www.nyse.com and at the principal office of the Exchange.

II. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

    In its filing with the Commission, the self-regulatory organization 
included statements concerning the purpose of, and basis for, the 
proposed rule change and discussed any comments it received on the

the places specified in Item IV below. The Exchange has prepared

A. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and the 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

1. Purpose
    The purpose of the proposed rule change is to amend Rule 7.10 
(Clearly Erroneous Executions). Specifically, the Exchange proposes to: 
(1) reorganize the Rule to enhance its clarity; (2) specify the impact 
of missing or erroneous LULD Price Bands \4\ on the availability of 
Clearly Erroneous review during the Core Trading Session or Overnight 
Protected Hours; \5\ (3) amend provisions on ``multi-stock events''; 
(4) remove references to ``multi-day events''; (5) add a provision on 
reasonable cross-market coordination; and (6) eliminate redundancies in 
the Rule.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ ``LULD Price Bands'' or ``Price Bands'' means Price Bands as 
defined in Section V of the Plan to Address Extraordinary Market 
Volatility (the ``LULD Plan'') and Overnight Price Bands as defined 
in Section VIII of the LULD Plan.
    \5\ The term ``Core Trading Session'' is defined in Rule 
7.34(a)(2). The term ``Overnight Protected Hours'' is defined in 
Section VIII of the LULD Plan.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Background
    Following a severe disruption in the U.S. equities markets on May 
6, 2010 (known as the Flash Crash), the Commission approved, on a pilot 
basis, the clearly erroneous rule to ``provide greater transparency and 
certainty to the process of breaking trades.'' \6\ Largely,

[[Page 66893]]

the pilot reduced the discretion of the Exchange, other national 
securities exchanges, and Financial Industry Regulatory Authority 
(``FINRA'') to deviate from the objective standards in their respective 
rules when dealing with potentially erroneous transactions. After 
revisions in 2013 and 2014,\7\ the clearly erroneous pilot was extended 
in six-month increments numerous times until 2022.

clearly erroneous rule permanent and aimed to harmonize it with the 
LULD Plan by making explicit that, with limited exceptions, the LULD 
mechanism was the primary means of preventing clearly erroneous trades 
during the Core Trading Session.

Proposed Amendment
    Following the 2022 Amendment, market disruptions on January 24, 
2023 and June 3, 2024 \13\ further highlighted the close interplay of 
the LULD Plan and the clearly erroneous rule. Following those events, 
the Exchange, in consultation with other equities exchanges, closely 
reviewed the current text of Rule 7.10 and identified areas where the 
Exchange believes the Rule's clarity can be enhanced, its organization 
can be improved, and duplicate and obsolete sections can be eliminated. 
In sum, the proposed rule text is not substantively new, but rather a 
reorganization and further specification of existing Exchange 
authority.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \13\ On January 24, 2023, LULD bands in numerous securities were 
calculated based on anomalous reference prices supplied by NYSE. On 
June 3, 2024, a software defect caused the CTA SIP to incorrectly 
calculate LULD bands across numerous securities. In each case, NYSE 
and other exchanges declared numerous trades clearly erroneous.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Accordingly, the Exchange proposes to reorganize Rule 7.10 into 
three clear paragraphs: (a) Definitions, (b) Availability of Clearly 
Erroneous Review, and (c) Procedures. In the current Rule, definitions 
are spread across paragraphs (a), (c), and (d); rules on the 
availability of clearly erroneous review are split between paragraphs 
(c), (g), (h), and (i); and procedures are split across paragraphs (b), 
(c), (e), and (f). By reorganizing the Rule and gathering similar 
provisions together, the Exchange believes the proposed rule text will 
be easier for market participants to navigate, understand, and use.
(a) Definitions
    The Exchange proposes to amend paragraph (a) of Rule 7.10 to 
include the definitions of terms used in the current version of the 
Rule, as well as new terms that would be added as a result of this 
proposal, as follows:
     Proposed paragraph (a) would be titled ``Definitions'' and 
would provide that ``[f]or purposes of this Rule, the terms below have 
the following meanings.''
     Proposed subparagraph (a)(1) would define ``CEE Reference 
Price'' as the consolidated last sale immediately prior to the 
execution(s) under review, or, in cases where there are no prior 
trades, the previous day's official closing price, unless an Officer 
determines to utilize a Revised CEE Reference Price \14\ as provided in 
this Rule. The first part of this definition is based on the current 
definition of ``Reference Price'' in paragraph (d) of Rule 7.10. The 
second part of this definition is based on the Exchange's definition of 
``Official Closing Price'' in Rule 1.1(u)(1)(D), which states that when 
there have been no consolidated last-sale eligible trades during the 
Core Trading Hours on that trading day, the Exchange's Official Closing 
Price will be the previous day's Official Closing Price. As such, the 
proposed definition of ``CEE Reference Price'' in subparagraph (a)(1) 
is not novel.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Proposed subparagraph (a)(2), the definition of ``Clearly 
erroneous,'' would encompass the text of current paragraph (a), without 
substantive change.
     Proposed subparagraph (a)(3) would define ``LULD Plan'' to

     Proposed subparagraph (a)(4) would define ``LULD Price

(b) Availability of Clearly Erroneous Review
    Next, the Exchange proposes to reorganize all rule text about the 
availability of clearly erroneous review into a revised paragraph (b). 
In the current Rule, this content is spread across paragraph (c)(1), 
which addresses the availability of clearly erroneous review during the 
Core Trading Session; paragraph (c)(2), which addresses the 
availability of clearly erroneous review outside of the Core Trading 
Session and for stocks not subject to the LULD Plan; paragraph (g), 
which addresses transactions occurring outside of LULD Price Bands; 
paragraph (h), which addresses Multi-Day Events; and paragraph (i), 
which addresses transactions occurring during trading halts.
    The Exchange proposes to reorganize all of these provisions into 
proposed paragraph (b). Further, instead of separating the rules for 
clearly erroneous review during the Core Trading Session vs. other 
times (i.e., current paragraphs (c)(1) vs. (c)(2)) as in the current 
Rule, the Exchange proposes to assign a ``Case'' number to each 
circumstance where clearly erroneous review is available, and to 
include within the Case description all of the rules relevant to that 
particular Case. In addition, to the extent that the rules in current 
paragraphs (c)(1) and (c)(2) address the same issues as current 
paragraphs (g) and (i), the Exchange proposes to combine and streamline 
such rules within the relevant Case.
    Specifically, the Exchange proposes to replace paragraph (b) of 
Rule 7.10 as follows:
     The Exchange proposes to title the revised paragraph (b) 
as ``Availability of Clearly Erroneous Review.'' Proposed paragraph (b) 
would specify that ``transactions are reviewable as clearly erroneous 
only in the enumerated cases below.''
     Proposed subparagraph (b)(1) would be titled ``Case 1--
Transactions Erroneously Executed During a Trading Halt, Suspension, or 
Pause.''
     Proposed subparagraph (b)(2) would be titled ``Case 2--
Transactions Executed Outside LULD Price Bands.''
     Proposed subparagraph (b)(3) would be titled ``Case 3--
Unavailable or Missing LULD Price Bands.''
     Proposed subparagraph (b)(4) would be titled ``Case 4--
Erroneous LULD Price Bands.''
     Proposed subparagraph (b)(5) would be titled ``Case 5--
Transactions Executed During LULD Protected Hours in NMS Stocks Not 
Subject to the LULD Plan, and Transactions Executed Outside of LULD 
Protected Hours.''
    Each of the Cases is discussed further below.
Case 1--Transactions Erroneously Executed During a Trading Halt, 
Suspension, or Pause
    Proposed Case 1 (proposed subparagraph (b)(1)) is based on current 
paragraph (i) of Rule 7.10, with certain changes identified below. 
Proposed Case 1 would apply when a ``transaction was executed after 
implementation of a regulatory trading halt, suspension, or pause that 
has been declared by the primary listing market or imposed by the 
Commission \16\ and before such regulatory halt, suspension, or pause 
officially ended according to the primary listing market.'' The current 
text of paragraph (i) expresses the same concept, but includes 
additional unnecessary detail about the event where a trading halt, 
suspension or pause is ``prematurely lifted in error and then re-
instituted,'' which the Exchange believes is encompassed in the more 
general language above. The Exchange believes that removing such 
unnecessary detail will streamline the rule text and enhance 
comprehension of the Rule.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ In addition to referencing regulatory trading halts, 
suspensions, or pauses that have been declared by the primary 
listing market, the Exchange proposes to include ``or imposed by the 
Commission'' to capture summary suspension orders issued by the 
Commission under Section 12(k) in the public interest and to protect 
investors. Such summary suspension orders by the Commission have the 
same effect as halts declared by primary listing markets--e.g., to 
halt trading--and so the Exchange believes that including them here 
adds further specification to the rule and not a material change to 
the rule.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In addition, the current text of paragraph (i) includes language 
limiting the application of the Rule to ``the event of any disruption, 
or malfunction in the operation of the electronic communications and 
trading facilities of the Exchange, another market center or 
responsible single plan processor in connection with the transmittal or 
receipt of a regulatory trading halt, suspension or pause . . . .'' The 
Exchange believes that such limitation is unnecessary, because 
transactions erroneously executed during a trading halt, suspension, or 
pause should be nullified in all cases, whether the cause was one of 
the conditions listed in current paragraph (i) or some other issue that 
has not yet been imagined. The inclusion of such limiting language has 
the potential to cause confusion and add delay to the process of 
addressing such trades.
    Proposed Case 1 would further provide: ``A Case 1 transaction shall 
be nullified by an Officer acting on his or her own motion or at the 
request of a third party without regard to the Percentage Parameters or 
Numerical Guidelines set forth in this Rule, and without regard to the 
trading session during which the transaction was executed.'' The 
provisions providing that an Officer shall nullify such transactions 
acting on his or her own motion and that such action will be taken 
without regard to the Percentage Parameters or Numerical Guidelines are 
taken directly from current paragraph (i), without substantive change. 
The Exchange proposes to add the further language ``without regard to 
the trading session during which the transaction was executed'' to make 
explicit that Case 1 applies both during LULD Protected Hours and 
outside of them. The Exchange also proposes to add that an Officer may 
nullify such a

[[Page 66895]]

transaction ``acting on his or her own motion or at the request of a 
third party.'' Current paragraph (g), regarding transactions executed 
outside of LULD Price Bands, currently contains such language, and so 
it is not novel. The Exchange believes it is reasonable to also include 
such language in proposed Case 1, to make explicit that a third party 
market participant may inform the Exchange when such third party 
becomes aware of transactions erroneously executed during a trading 
halt, suspension, or pause.
    Current paragraph (i) contains additional language regarding the 
timing of the Officer's review, the notification of parties involved in 
the subject transaction, and the appeal rights of aggrieved parties. 
This language is substantively identical to the procedural language 
contained in current paragraph (f). In light of the Exchange's proposal 
to reorganize all such procedural language into one location (i.e., 
paragraph (c) of the proposed Rule), the Exchange proposes not to 
include such procedural language within Case 1 itself, but instead to 
include it in the ``Procedures'' section of the proposed Rule at 
paragraph (c)(1)(B).
    The Exchange notes that current paragraph (c)(1)(B) includes a 
provision that indicates that a transaction that ``is executed after 
the primary listing market for the security declares a regulatory 
trading halt, suspension, or pause pursuant to paragraph (i)'' will be 
``subject to review . . . and found to be clearly erroneous if the 
price of the transaction to buy (sell) that is subject of the complaint 
is greater than (less than) the Reference Price, described in paragraph 
(d) below, by an amount that equals or exceeds the applicable 
Percentage Parameter . . . .'' This provision was added as part of the 
2022 Amendment and appears to be inconsistent with paragraph (i), which 
requires the Exchange to nullify such transactions without regard to 
Percentage Parameters. The Exchange believes that the paragraph (i) 
approach--which has been part of the clearly erroneous rule since 2014 
\17\--is the more reasonable approach, and that transactions executed 
during a trading halt, suspension, or pause should be nullified without 
further analysis. For that reason, the Exchange proposes to eliminate 
the inconsistent provisions of paragraph (c)(1)(B) and adopt the 
paragraph (i) approach, as described and revised above, in Case 1.

    \17\ See Securities Exchange Act Release No. 72434 (June 19, 
2014), 79 FR 36110 (June 25, 2014) (SR-NYSE-2014-22) (adding 
``Trading Halts'' section as paragraph (k) of Rule 128).

Case 2--Transactions Executed Outside LULD Price Bands
    Proposed Case 2 (proposed subparagraph (b)(2)) is based on current 
paragraph (g) of Rule 7.10, with certain changes identified below.
    Proposed Case 2 would apply when a ``transaction was executed 
during LULD Protected Hours outside the LULD Price Bands at a time when 
the Price Bands were available and correct, as determined by an 
Officer, using his or her own discretion.'' Current paragraph (g) 
contains this concept, but with the limitation that the execution of 
such a transaction outside the LULD Price Bands must be ``as a result 
of an Exchange technology or systems issue.'' The Exchange believes 
that such limitation is unnecessary, because transactions erroneously 
executed outside of available and correct Price Bands during LULD 
Protected Hours should be nullified in all cases, whether the cause was 
an Exchange technology or systems issue or some other issue that has 
not yet been imagined. The inclusion of such limiting language has the 
potential to cause confusion and add delay to the process of addressing 
such trades.
    As noted above, proposed Case 2 would apply ``when Price Bands were 
available and correct, as determined by an Officer, using his or her 
own discretion.'' The Exchange proposes to add this condition to 
distinguish Case 2 from other potential scenarios where transactions 
should be evaluated when the LULD Price Bands were erroneous or 
missing, as described below in proposed Cases 3 and 4. The Exchange 
believes it is appropriate for an Officer, using his or her own 
discretion, to determine whether the Price Bands were available and 
correct such that Case 2 applies.
    Proposed Case 2 would provide: ``A Case 2 transaction shall be 
nullified by an Officer acting on his or her own motion or at the 
request of a third party, without regard to the Percentage Parameters 
or Numerical Guidelines set forth in this Rule.'' The provision 
providing that an Officer shall nullify such transactions acting on his 
or her own motion is taken directly from current paragraph (g), without 
substantive change. The Exchange proposes to add ``without regard to 
the Percentage Parameters or Numerical Guidelines set forth in this 
Rule'' to make clear that such nullification by the Officer is not 
discretionary, nor is it based on computations described elsewhere in 
the Rule.
    Current paragraph (g) contains additional language regarding the 
timing of the Officer's review, the notification of parties involved in 
the subject transaction, and the appeal rights of aggrieved parties. 
This language is substantively identical to the procedural language 
contained in current paragraph (f). As with Case 1 above, the Exchange 
proposes not to include the procedural language within Case 2 itself, 
but to include it instead in the ``Procedures'' section of the proposed 
Rule at paragraph (c)(1)(B).
    The Exchange notes that current paragraph (c)(1)(B) includes a 
provision that indicates that a transaction that ``is the result of an 
Exchange technology or systems issue that results in the transaction 
occurring outside of the applicable LULD Price Bands pursuant to 
paragraph (g)'' will be ``subject to review . . . and found to be 
clearly erroneous if the price of the transaction to buy (sell) that is 
subject of the complaint is greater than (less than) the Reference 
Price, described in paragraph (d) below, by an amount that equals or 
exceeds the applicable Percentage Parameter . . . .'' This provision 
was added as part of the 2022 Amendment and appears to be inconsistent 
with paragraph (g), which requires the Exchange simply to nullify such 
transactions. The Exchange believes that the paragraph (g) approach--
which has been part of the clearly erroneous rule since 2013 \18\--is 
the more reasonable approach, and that transactions executed outside 
LULD Price Bands should be nullified without further analysis. For that 
reason, the Exchange proposes to eliminate the inconsistent provisions 
of paragraph (c)(1)(B) and adopt the paragraph (g) approach, as 
described and revised above, in Case 2.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \18\ See Securities Exchange Act Release Nos. 68804 (February 1, 
2013), 78 FR 8677 (February 6, 2013) (SR-NYSE-2013-11) (adding 
``Securities Subject to Limit Up-Limit Down Plan'' section as 
paragraph (i) of Rule 128).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Finally, current paragraph (g) includes the caveat that ``[i]n the 
event that a single plan processor experiences a technology or systems 
issue that prevents the dissemination of Price Bands, the Exchange will 
make the determination of whether to nullify transactions based on 
paragraph (c)(1)(B) above.'' Because the Exchange proposes to address 
such processor issues in Cases 3 and 4 below, the Exchange proposes not 
to include this sentence in Case 2.
Case 3--Unavailable or Missing LULD Price Bands
    Proposed Case 3 (proposed subparagraph (b)(3)) would apply when

[[Page 66896]]

a ``transaction was executed during LULD Protected Hours at a time when 
Price Bands under the LULD Plan were unavailable, because (a) the first 
Price Bands had not yet been received after a security's opening or 
reopening; or (b) the Price Bands are missing.''
    This proposed language is a clarification of the portion of current 
paragraph (c)(1)(B), added in the 2022 Amendment, that provides that 
clearly erroneous review is available if the transaction ``was executed 
at a time when Price Bands under the LULD Plan were not available.'' 
The Exchange's Rule 19b-4 filing in support of the 2022 Amendment noted 
that in certain circumstances during the Core Trading Session, clearly 
erroneous review should act as a ``backstop'' to the LULD Plan:

    [T]here are instances, such as the opening auction on the 
primary listing market, or circumstances where a transaction that 
ordinarily would have been subject to the LULD Plan is not--due, for 
example, to some issue with processing the Price Bands. These 
transactions would continue to be eligible for clearly erroneous 
review [under the 2022 Amendment], effectively ensuring that such 
review remains available as a backstop when the LULD Plan would not 
prevent executions from occurring at erroneous prices in the first 
instance.\19\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \19\ See Securities Exchange Act Release No. 95822 (September 
19, 2022), 87 FR 58140 at 58142 (September 23, 2022) (SR-NYSE-2022-
44) (Notice of Filing and Immediate Effectiveness of Proposed Rule 
Change to Amend Rule 7.10).

    Proposed Case 3 would make explicit that clearly erroneous review 
is available when a transaction was executed during LULD Protected 
Hours at a time when the first Price Bands had not yet been received 
after a security's opening or reopening (i.e., clause (a) of proposed 
paragraph (b)(3)). This provision is intended to capture the first 
circumstance identified in the quoted passage above: the case of an 
opening (or reopening) auction, when LULD Price Bands are not yet in 
effect. Because the protection of the Price Bands is not available at 
that time, transactions executed during that interval should be 
entitled to clearly erroneous review.
    Proposed Case 3 would also make explicit that clearly erroneous 
review is available in the case of missing Price Bands during LULD 
Protected Hours (i.e., clause (b) of proposed subparagraph (b)(3)). The 
Exchange believes that Case 3 should cover all cases where Price Bands 
are missing during LULD Protected Hours, regardless of whether the 
missing Price Bands are caused by the processor's failure to 
disseminate them, the Exchange's failure to receive or implement them, 
or some other reason that has not yet been imagined. As such, the 
Exchange does not propose to include limiting language such as the 
language in current paragraph (g) (i.e., ``[i]n the event that a single 
plan processor experiences a technology or systems issue that prevents 
the dissemination of Price Bands''), because the Exchange believes that 
clearly erroneous review should be available in all instances during 
LULD Protected Hours where Price Bands are unavailable or missing, 
regardless of the cause.\20\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \20\ Note that proposed Case 3 would cover only unavailable and 
missing Price Bands--i.e., the absence of Price Bands during LULD 
Protected Hours. The presence of incorrect Price Bands would be 
covered in proposed Case 4.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Finally, proposed Case 3 would specify that a ``Case 3 transaction 
shall be found to be clearly erroneous if the price of the transaction 
to buy (sell) that is the subject of the complaint is greater than 
(less than) the consolidated last sale immediately prior to the 
execution(s) under review (or, in cases where there are no prior 
trades, the previous day's official closing price) by an amount that 
equals or exceeds the applicable Percentage Parameter.'' This language 
is based on current paragraph (c)(1), without substantive change, but 
instead of cross-referencing the definition of ``Reference Price,'' it 
incorporates the relevant portion of the definition of ``CEE Reference 
Price'' from proposed paragraph (a)(1).
Case 4--Erroneous LULD Price Bands
    Proposed Case 4 (proposed subparagraph (b)(4)) would apply when a 
``transaction was executed during LULD Protected Hours at a time when 
Price Bands under the LULD Plan were available but erroneous, as 
determined by an Officer using his or her own discretion.''
    Similar to Case 3 above, the Exchange's Rule 19b-4 filing in 
support of the 2022 Amendment contemplated that clearly erroneous 
review would be available in such situations. The Exchange intended for 
erroneous Price Bands to be among the ``issue[s] with processing the 
Price Bands'' where the clearly erroneous rules would act as ``a 
backstop.'' \21\ Proposed Case 4 would make explicit that clearly 
erroneous review is available when a transaction was executed during 
LULD Protected Hours at a time when Price Bands under the LULD Plan 
were available but those Price Bands were erroneous, as determined by 
an Officer using his or her own discretion.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \21\ See supra note 14.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Current Rule 7.10(c)(1)(C) already includes two specific examples 
in which clearly erroneous review is available during LULD Protected 
Hours due to erroneous LULD Price Bands: in cases involving ``(1) a 
corporate action or new issue or (2) a security that enters a Trading 
Pause pursuant to the LULD Plan and resumes trading without an 
auction'' where the Reference Price ``is determined to be erroneous by 
an Officer of the Exchange because it clearly deviated from the 
theoretical value of the security.'' The Exchange now proposes to bring 
those two examples under Case 4, and to also specify several 
additional, non-exclusive factors contemplated by the ``issue[s] with 
processing the Price Bands'' language from the Exchange's Rule 19b-4 
filing in support of the 2022 Amendment, where clearly erroneous review 
should be available due to erroneous LULD Price Bands, as determined by 
an Officer using his or her own discretion. The proposed Rule would 
provide that an Officer ``may consider factors including, but not 
limited to,'' the following.
    Proposed subparagraph (A)(1) would describe one such factor: 
``whether the LULD Reference Price that was used to calculate the Price 
Bands was based on a price that equaled or exceeded the Numerical 
Guidelines.'' This factor illustrates the fact that if the LULD 
Reference Price deviates from the theoretical value of the security by 
an amount that equals or exceeds the Numerical Guidelines, it is a 
telltale sign that the LULD Price Bands are erroneous. Such an instance 
would be an ``issue with processing the Price Bands'' within the 
language that the Exchange included in its Rule 19b-4 filing in support 
of the 2022 Amendment and is therefore not novel.
    Proposed subparagraph (A)(2) would describe a second factor: 
``whether there was an issue that impacted the processor's calculation 
of the Price Bands.'' This factor would include cases such as the 
processor experiencing a software or hardware issue, as well as the 
instances described in footnote 12 [sic] above. This factor is a clear 
example of the ``issue with processing the Price Bands'' language that 
the Exchange included in its Rule 19b-4 filing in support of the 2022 
Amendment and is therefore not novel.
    Proposed subparagraph (A)(3) would describe a third factor: 
``whether the security at issue was included in the correct LULD Tier 
under the LULD Plan.'' This factor refers to the LULD Plan's 
requirement that a security must

[[Page 66897]]

be categorized into either ``Tier 1'' or ``Tier 2,'' with Percentage 
Parameters of either 5% (for Tier 1) or 10% (for Tier 2) for NMS 
securities with an LULD Reference Price of more than $3.00.\22\ A 
security incorrectly classified into the wrong LULD Tier would have the 
wrong Percentage Parameters applied to its LULD Reference Price, 
meaning that the resulting LULD Price Bands would be erroneous. This 
factor is another clear example of the ``issue with processing the 
Price Bands'' language that the Exchange included in its Rule 19b-4 
filing in support of the 2022 Amendment and is therefore not novel.

    \22\ See LULD Plan, supra note 4, Appendix A--Percentage 
Parameters.

    Proposed subparagraph (A)(4) would describe a fourth factor: 
``whether the transaction involved a corporate action or new issue 
where the LULD Reference Price clearly deviated from the theoretical 
value of the security due to conflicting or incorrect information in 
the market about the security, or an objective error like a processing 
or technical error.'' This factor is based on paragraph (c)(1)(C)(1) of 
the current rule, but replaces the term ``Reference Price'' with ``LULD 
Reference Price'' as defined in proposed Rule 7.10(a)(4) (i.e., an 
erroneous reference price was used for calculating the LULD Price 
Bands), since it is clear from context that is the meaning contemplated 
by the current rule text. The Exchange proposes to supplement the 
current rule text by adding ``due to conflicting or incorrect 
information in the market about the security, or an objective error 
like a processing or technical error,'' as illustrative examples of the 
types of errors that could result in a corporate action or new issue 
having an erroneous LULD Reference Price.\23\ The Exchange believes 
this addition is not material and will enhance comprehension of the 
Rule.

    In proposed subparagraph (B) under Case 4, the first sentence would 
provide that if the Officer determines that the LULD Price Bands are

based on the theoretical value of the security (the ``Revised CEE

Exchange believes that this provision is necessary because erroneous 
LULD Price Bands may be the byproduct of the security trading at prices 
that deviate from the theoretical value of the security. In such a 
case, it is likely that the CEE Reference Price that would ordinarily 
be used for clearly erroneous review \24\ may also deviate from the 
theoretical value of the security. In such a case, the impact of such 
error can be corrected by using a Revised CEE Reference Price that is 
based on the theoretical value of the security. This proposed text is 
based on paragraph (c)(1)(C) of the current Rule, without substantive

LULD Trading Pause, trading following the LULD Trading Pause (albeit

more appropriate.

``Revised CEE Reference Price'' in the proposed Rule, since that term 
is used in proposed subparagraph (B). Second, the current text of

security is subject to the LULD Plan.'' The Exchange proposes not to

Case 5--Transactions Executed During LULD Protected Hours in NMS Stocks 
Not Subject to the LULD Plan, and Transactions Executed Outside of LULD 
Protected Hours
    Proposed Case 5 (proposed subparagraph (b)(5)) is based on current 
paragraphs (c)(1)(A) and (c)(2) of Rule 7.10, with changes as described 
below. Proposed Case 5 would describe the availability of clearly 
erroneous review for transactions that are not subject to the LULD 
Plan, either because (1) the transactions were executed during LULD 
Protected Hours in securities that are not subject to the LULD Plan 
(i.e., rights or warrants) (current paragraph (c)(1)(A)), or (2) 
because the transactions were executed outside of LULD Protected Hours 
when the LULD Plan is not operational (current paragraph (c)(2)). The 
Exchange proposes to locate both of these rules in the same Case 5 to 
simplify the Rule's organization, reduce the need to cross-reference 
within the Rule, and enhance the Rule's clarity.
    Proposed subparagraph (b)(5) would provide that ``[e]xcept in the 
case of an Outlier Transaction (discussed below),\25\ transactions 
executed during LULD Protected Hours in NMS Stocks not subject to the 
LULD Plan and transactions executed outside of LULD Protected Hours 
shall be found to be clearly erroneous in the following two 
situations,'' and would go on to define Case 5A and Case 5B in the 
following subparagraphs.

Case 5A--Numerical Guideline Requirement
    Proposed Case 5A (subparagraph (b)(6)(A)) would describe the first 
of the two situations in which the transaction under Case 5 shall be 
found to be clearly erroneous, namely, when, ``[s]ubject to the 
additional factors described below in this Case 5A, the price of the 
transaction to buy (sell) that is the subject of the complaint is 
greater than (less than) the Reference Price by an amount that equals 
or exceeds the Numerical Guidelines set forth below.'' This language is 
based on paragraph (c)(2)(A) of the current Rule, without substantive 
change.
    The ``Additional Factors'' included in Case 5A is substantively 
identical to paragraph (c)(2)(C) of the current Rule, and would provide 
that ``an Officer may also consider additional factors to determine 
whether an execution is clearly erroneous, provided the execution 
occurred during the Early Trading Session or during LULD Protected 
Hours in NMS Stocks not subject to the LULD Plan. Such additional 
factors include, but are not limited to, system malfunctions or 
disruptions, volume and volatility for the security, derivative 
securities products that correspond to greater than 100% in the 
direction of a tracking index, news released for the security, whether 
trading in the security was recently halted/resumed, whether the 
security is an IPO, whether the security was subject to a stock-split, 
reorganization, or other corporate action, overall market conditions, 
executions outside of the Core Trading Session, validity of the 
consolidated tapes trades and quotes, consideration of primary market 
indications, and executions inconsistent with the trading pattern in 
the stock. Each additional factor will be considered with a view toward 
maintaining a fair and orderly market and the protection of investors 
and the public interest.''
    Consistent with current practice, when the Exchange considers these 
additional factors, it typically looks for a representation in the 
clearly erroneous filing indicating the existence of an error. Such 
errors may include, for example, a system malfunction or disruption 
(such as at the Exchange, trade reporting facility, data processor, or 
market participant), which resulted in incorrect market data or 
executions, incorrect processing of a corporate action, or a 
typographical error in the material terms of an order.

in Case 5B, described below.
Case 5B--Multi-Stock Events
    Proposed Case 5B (subparagraph (b)(6)(B)) would describe the second 
of the two situations in which the transaction under Case 5 may be 
found to be clearly erroneous, namely, Multi-Stock Events.
    As an initial matter, the current version of Rule 7.10 contains two 
scenarios involving ``Multi Stock Events'': (1) ``filings involving 
five or more, but less than twenty, securities whose executions 
occurred within a period of five minutes or less,'' and (2) filings 
involving twenty or more securities whose executions occurred within a 
period of five minutes or less.'' The Exchange believes that the 
numbers of affected securities referenced in those scenarios are now 
too low to justify special treatment, and that events of such size are 
more properly analyzed on a security-by-security basis by applying the 
Case 1 through Case 5A as discussed above.
    The Exchange proposes instead that an event should be treated as a 
``Multi-Stock Event'' only if it involves 50 or more securities whose 
executions occurred within 5 minutes. The Exchange believes that 50 
securities is a more appropriate trigger for the application of the 
Multi-Stock Event rule. The 20-security threshold in the current Rule 
has not been updated since 2010,\26\ and given the Exchange's 
experience in processing clearly erroneous reviews over the past 16 
years, the Exchange believes that it would be able to process clearly 
erroneous requests involving fewer than 50 securities without resorting 
to the Multi-Stock Event rule. In addition, while the current Rule 
assumes that Multi-Stock Events will be brought to the Exchange's 
attention via a filing from a customer, the Exchange proposes that Case 
5B would state that a Multi-Stock Event may be reviewable as clearly 
erroneous ``by an Officer acting on his or her own motion.'' The 
Exchange believes that with respect to events of this size, it would be 
reasonable for an Officer to act on his or her own motion without 
insisting on the requirement that a customer file a formal request for 
review.

(c) Procedures
    Finally, the Exchange proposes to reorganize all rule text about 
the procedures under the clearly erroneous rule in to a revised 
paragraph (c). In the current Rule, such procedures are split across 
paragraphs (b), (c), (e), and (f). The Exchange believes that 
reorganizing all of the procedures into one area of the Rule will 
enhance the clarity and comprehension of the Rule.
    The Exchange proposes to organize the procedures into two 
categories: ``Review Procedures'' in subparagraph (1) and ``Appeals'' 
in subparagraph (2).
(c)(1)--Review Procedures
    Proposed subparagraph (c)(1) would set out all the procedures for 
the review of potentially clearly erroneous transactions.
Request for Review by Member Organization
    Proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A), titled ``Request for Review by 
Member Organization,'' would set forth the procedures for reviewing a 
transaction when a member organization requests such review.
    Proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A)(1) would be titled ``General.'' The 
first sentence would provide: ``A member organization may request that 
the Exchange review a transaction for its own account or for its 
customer's account under the Rule.'' This language is based on the 
first sentence of paragraph (b) of the current Rule, except that it 
omits the phrase ``submitted erroneously'' as used in the current Rule. 
This change would clarify that a member organization may request review 
of any transaction. Whether the transaction is ``erroneous'' would be 
determined by the review process, and a belief that it was ``submitted 
erroneously'' should not be a precursor to requesting review.
    The second sentence of proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A)(1) would 
provide: ``Member organizations are responsible for submitting valid 
requests for review to the Exchange within the timeframes set out 
below.'' This language does not appear in the current version of the 
Rule, but the Exchange proposes to include it in the revised version to 
add specificity to the review procedure.
    The third and fourth sentences of proposed subparagraph 
(c)(1)(A)(1) would provide: ``An Officer will review the transaction 
under dispute and determine whether it is clearly erroneous, with a 
view toward maintaining a fair and orderly market and the protection of 
investors and the public interest. Such request for review will be made 
in writing via email or other electronic means specified from time to 
time by the Exchange in a circular distributed to member 
organizations.'' This language is based on the second and third 
sentences of paragraph (b) of the current Rule, without substantive 
change.
    Proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A)(2) would be titled ``Timing of 
Requests for Review.'' Proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A)(2)(a) would be

include information concerning the time of the transaction(s), security 
symbol(s), number of shares, price(s), side (bought or sold), and the 
factual basis for believing that the trade is clearly erroneous. Upon 
receipt of a timely filed request, the counterparty to the trade will 
be notified by the Exchange as soon as practicable, but generally 
within 30 minutes. An Officer may request additional supporting written 
information to aid in the resolution of the matter. If requested, each 
party to the transaction will provide, within 30 minutes of the 
request, any supporting written information. Either party to the 
disputed trade may request supporting written information provided by

paragraph (b)(1) of the current Rule, except for the following

request that satisfies the numerical guidelines set forth in Section

Guidelines would apply only to Case 5A. The Exchange believes that the 
clarity of the Rule would be enhanced by removing the limitation, 
making clear that proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A)(2)(a) applies to all 
requests for review under any Case.

    The second sentence of proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(A)(2)(b) would 
provide: ``If the execution price of the security in question is not 
within the Outlier Transaction parameters described in the previous

Cross-Market Coordination
    The Exchange proposes to add a new provision as paragraph 
(c)(1)(C), titled ``Cross-Market Coordination.''
    The Exchange proposes that subparagraph (c)(1)(C)(1) would provide: 
``Regarding events involving trading activity on multiple SROs, the 
Exchange will use reasonable efforts as appropriate to coordinate with 
other SROs regarding the Exchange's determinations, if any, about (a) 
whether transactions occurred outside of available and correct Price 
Bands, (b) whether the LULD Price Bands or LULD Reference Price are 
erroneous, (c) what CEE Reference Price or Revised CEE Reference Price 
to use, and (d) issues related to Multi-Stock Events.'' The proposed 
language reflects the Exchange's existing practice of coordinating with 
other SROs on such topics whenever a market event involving trading 
activity on multiple SROs raises questions about the availability of 
clearly erroneous review. Cross-exchange coordination is already 
reflected in the current rule text applicable to Multi-Stock Events and 
Multi-Day Events, and in practice, the exchanges currently coordinate 
with each other about additional aspects of market-wide events. The 
proposed rule text synthesizes the existing rule text and the 
Exchange's current practices, making explicit the Exchange's practice 
of using reasonable efforts as appropriate to coordinate with other 
markets on such issues.
    The Exchange proposes that subparagraph (c)(1)(C)(2) would provide: 
``Regarding events involving trading activity on multiple SROs, if 
another SRO receives a valid request to review a transaction(s) as 
clearly erroneous and coordinates with the Exchange about such request, 
an Officer may declare transactions on the Exchange that meet the same 
criteria to be clearly erroneous on his or her own motion, pursuant to 
paragraph (c)(1)(B) above.'' The proposed language reflects the 
Exchange's existing practice of acting on its Officer's own motion to 
declare transactions clearly erroneous based on criteria agreed upon as 
a result of coordination with other affected exchanges.\28\

    In light of these proposed provisions about cross-market 
coordination, the Exchange proposes to delete current paragraph (b)(2), 
which is titled ``Routed Executions.'' That provision currently 
provides that ``[o]ther market centers will generally have an 
additional 30 minutes from receipt of their participant's timely 
filing, but no longer than 60 minutes from the time of the execution at 
issue, to file with the Exchange for review of transactions routed to 
the Exchange from that market center and executed on the Exchange.'' 
This provision predates the Exchange's current practice of coordinating 
with other SROs whenever a market event involving trading activity on 
multiple SROs raises questions about the availability of clearly 
erroneous review,

[[Page 66901]]

regardless of whether particular orders had been routed to particular 
exchanges. As such, the Exchange believes that the language in current 
paragraph (b)(2) setting a particular time limit to consider routed 
orders is unnecessarily limiting, and proposes instead to replace 
current paragraph (b)(2) with the procedures regarding cross-market 
collaboration in proposed subparagraph (c)(1)(C).
(c)(2)--Appeals
    Proposed subparagraph (c)(2) would set out the appeal procedures 
for clearly erroneous review. This proposed subparagraph would be 
identical to current paragraph (e)(2)-(6), with the following 
alterations.
    First, the Exchange proposes to renumber the several subparagraphs 
of the Rule. Current subparagraph (e)(3), (e)(4), (e)(5), and (e)(6) 
would become proposed subparagraphs (c)(2)(C), (c)(2)(D), (c)(2)(E), 
and (c)(2)(F), respectively.
    Second, the reference in current paragraph (e)(2) to decisions 
``under paragraph (g)'' would be updated to refer to ``Case 2,'' and 
the current reference to ``applicable Price Bands'' would be updated to 
refer to ``available and correct Price Bands,'' which is the language 
used in proposed Case 2.
    Third, the Exchange proposes to reduce the number of member 
organization representatives that must be designated to serve on the 
CEE Panel to 5 (in proposed subparagraph (c)(2)(B)), from 10 (in 
current paragraph (e)(2)(B). The Exchange notes that in practice, 
appeals from clearly erroneous determination are quite rare and the CEE 
Panel meets infrequently. The requirement to have 10 member 
organization representatives at the ready for such a small number of 
appeals has become excessive, and accordingly, the Exchange proposes to 
recalibrate that number to better reflect the actual need for CEE Panel 
members currently.
    Fourth, the Exchange proposes to change the time for the CEE Panel 
to render a decision from ``no later than the trading day following the 
date of the execution under review'' (in current paragraph (e)(3)) to 
``no later than 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time on the trading day following the 
date of the execution under review.'' The Exchange proposes this 8:30 
a.m. Eastern Time deadline in light of T+1 settlement.
    Fifth, the cross-reference to ``Rule 7.10(E)(1)'' in current 
subparagraph (e)(5) would be updated in proposed subparagraph (c)(2)(E) 
to refer instead to ``paragraph (c)(1)(A) or (B).''
Implementation
    The Exchange will publish a trader notice before implementing the 
proposed changes.
2. Statutory Basis
    The Exchange believes the proposed rule change is consistent with 
the requirements of Section 6(b) of the Act,\29\ in general, and 
Section 6(b)(5) of the Act,\30\ in particular, in that it is designed 
to remove impediments to and perfect the mechanism of a free and open 
market and a national market system, to promote just and equitable 
principles of trade, and, in general, to protect investors and the 
public interest and not to permit unfair discrimination between 
customers, issuers, brokers, or dealers.

    Reorganization. The Exchange believes that it is consistent with 
just and equitable principles of trade to reorganize Rule 7.10 into 
three clear subsections addressing (a) Definitions, (b) Availability of 
Clearly Erroneous Review, and (c) Procedures, as proposed here. The 
current Rule is an amalgam of rule text originating in 2010, 2013, 
2014, 2022, and 2026, and has not been comprehensively reorganized 
since its inception. The Exchange believes that the clarity of the Rule 
can be enhanced and improved by the reorganizing the Rule and gathering 
similar provisions together. In addition, the Exchange believes that 
identifying each situation in which clearly erroneous review is 
available and assigning such situations with easy-to-identify Case 
numbers will make it easier for market participants to navigate, 
understand, and use the Rule.
    Definitions. The Exchange believes that it is consistent with just 
and equitable principles of trade to include in proposed paragraph (a), 
``Definitions,'' definitions of all terms used within the Rule, and to 
move definitions that previously existed elsewhere in the Rule to the 
``Definitions'' section in paragraph (a). The Exchange believes that 
gathering definitions in one paragraph at the start of the Rule will 
enhance the ability of market participants to navigate, understand, and 
use the Rule, and minimize the amount of cross-referencing that market 
participants must do in order to comprehend and apply the Rule text.
    Cases 1 and 2 and the Elimination of Inconsistent and Duplicative 
Provisions. The Exchange believes it is consistent with just and 
equitable principles of trade and with the protection of investors and 
the public interest to delete from the Rule the provisions of current 
paragraph (c)(1)(B) that direct the Exchange to perform an analysis 
using Percentage Parameters in cases where a transaction occurred 
outside of LULD Price bands pursuant current to paragraph (g) or during 
a regulatory halt, suspension, or pause pursuant to current paragraph 
(i). As noted above, the reference to Percentage Parameters in such 
cases was added to the Rule as part of the 2022 Amendment, and appears 
to be inconsistent with preexisting requirements of paragraphs (g) and 
(i), which require the Exchange to nullify such transactions without 
regard to Percentage Parameters. The Exchange believes that the 
approach in paragraphs (g) and (i)--which have been part of the clearly 
erroneous rule since 2013 and 2014 respectively \31\--should be 
maintained and thus proposes in Cases 1 and 2 that transactions 
executed outside of LULD Bands or during a trading halt, suspension, or 
pause should be nullified without further analysis.

    Cases 3 and 4 Covering Unavailable, Missing, and Erroneous LULD 
Price Bands. The Exchange believes that it is consistent with just and 
equitable principles of trade and with the protection of investors and 
the public interest to add rule text making explicit that clearly 
erroneous review is available in the cases of unavailable or missing 
LULD Price Bands (proposed Case 3) and erroneous LULD Price Bands 
(proposed Case 4). As explained above, current paragraph (c)(1)(B) 
provides that clearly erroneous review is available if the transaction 
``was executed at a time when Price Bands under the LULD Plan were not 
available,'' and the Exchange's Rule 19b-4 filing in support of the 
2022 Amendment explained that clearly erroneous review should be 
available to act as a ``backstop'' to the LULD Plan during the Core 
Trading Session in situations where there is ``some issue with 
processing the Price Bands.'' \32\ The proposed addition of Cases 3 and 
4 would make explicit that the ``issue[s] with processing the Price 
Bands'' referred to in the Exchange's Rule 19b-4 filing include cases 
where the Price Bands are unavailable because the first Price Bands had 
not yet been received after a security's opening or reopening, the 
Price Bands are missing, or the Price Bands were available but 
erroneous. As such, this proposed change is not substantive, but would 
be a clarification of the existing rule text and rationale.

    Case 5B--Multi-Stock Events. The Exchange believes that it is 
consistent with just and equitable principles of trade to combine the 
two Multi-Stock Events currently codified in Rule 7.10 into proposed 
Case 5B, which sets forth a single standard of review for erroneous 
transactions involving at least 50 securities occurring outside of the 
Core Trading Session over a five-minute period. Rather than having two 
separate criteria for reviewing Multi-Stock Events, which occur 
infrequently, the Exchange believes that moving to one set of criteria 
would provide a more uniform application of the Rule and enable the 
Exchange to respond more efficiently during situations where a large 
number of transactions across multiple securities are under review.
    Procedures. The Exchange believes that it is consistent with 
equitable principles of trade and with the protection of investors and 
the public interest to make the proposed clarifying changes to the 
procedures for clearly erroneous review, as described above. In 
particular, the Exchange believes that referring in the proposed Rule 
to its existing procedures regarding cross-market coordination on 
events involving trading activity on multiple SROs would provide 
greater certainty and clarity to market participants about the clearly 
erroneous review process.

B. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Burden on Competition

    The Exchange does not believe that the proposed rule change would 
impose any burden on competition that is not necessary or appropriate 
in furtherance of the purposes of the Act. The proposal would ensure 
the continued, uninterrupted operation of harmonized clearly erroneous 
execution rules across the U.S. equities markets while also amending 
those rules to provide greater certainty to SROs, member organizations, 
and investors regarding the particular Cases where clearly erroneous 
review is available. The Exchange understands that the other national 
securities exchanges and FINRA will also file similar proposals, the 
substance of which are identical to this proposal. Thus, the proposed 
rule change will help to ensure consistency across SROs without 
implicating any competitive issues.

Geometric realisation of hypergeometric local systems
Asem Abdelraouf, Giulia Gugiatti

Abstract
. We prove a realisation theorem for irreducible hypergeometric local systems defined over the rational numbers in terms of families of affine varieties in algebraic tori. The families we consider have been studied extensively in the literature and appear in mirror symmetry. Our result holds unconditionally for families with one-dimensional or even-dimensional fibres. It holds under a monodromy assumption for families with fibres of odd dimension greater than one.

Primary 33Cxx, 14D06, 32S40; secondary 14D07, 32S30, 14J33.

1.
Introduction

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
be two multisets of rational numbers. The hypergeometric differential operator associated to
\alpha,\beta
is

(1)

H=H(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\theta+\beta_{i}-1)-t\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\theta+\alpha_{i})

where
t
is a coordinate on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\theta:=t\frac{d}{dt}
. It is a differential operator on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with regular singularities at
0
,
1
, and
\infty
. The local system
\mathbb{H}
of solutions of
H\varphi=0
is a complex local system on
U\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. We call such a local system a
hypergeometric local system
.

In this paper, we consider the subclass of irreducible hypergeometric local systems that are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.
In this case,
\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}\notin\mathbb{Z}
for all
i,j
. Moreover, the isomorphism class of
\mathbb{H}
can be given by a vector of nonzero integers
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}
with
\gcd(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{l})=1
and whose sum of entries is zero.
The relationship between
\gamma
and the parameters
\alpha,\beta
is given by

(2)

\frac{\prod_{\gamma_{j}<0}\left(x^{-\gamma_{j}}-1\right)}{\prod_{\gamma_{j}>0}\left(x^{\gamma_{j}}-1\right)}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-e^{2\pi i\alpha_{j}})}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-e^{2\pi i\beta_{j}})}

where irreducibility forces the numerator and denominator of the right-hand side of Equation (
2
) to have no common factors.

Associated to a vector
\gamma
as above is a one-parameter family of affine varieties in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{l-1}
, as follows.
Let
z_{1},\dots,z_{l}
be homogeneous coordinates on
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{l-1}
, and let
t
be a coordinate on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Consider the affine variety
Z\subset(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{l-1}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}
defined by the homogeneous equations

(3)

\begin{split}z_{1}+z_{2}+\dots+z_{l}&=0\\
z_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}z_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\cdots z_{l}^{\gamma_{l}}&=\Gamma t\end{split}

where
\Gamma\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}
and let
\pi\colon Z\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the projection onto
t
.
The fibres
Z_{t}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(t)
of the family
\pi
are smooth unless
t=1
. We denote their dimension by
\kappa=l-3
.

The local system
\mathbb{H}
is expected to be realisable in terms of the family
(Z,\pi)
. More precisely, let
\pi_{U}
be the restriction of
\pi
to its regular locus
U
and let
PR^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{Q}
be the sub-local system of the local system
R^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{Q}
consisting of primitive cohomology classes (see Section
2.2.3
for details). Then, one expects an isomorphism of local systems over
U
:

(4)

\mathbb{H}\simeq\gr_{\kappa}^{W}PR^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{C}

We expand on why (
4
) is expected in Section
1.1
.

In this paper, we approach (
4
) by considering the local monodromy of the two local systems at
t=1
.
The local system
\mathbb{H}
has non-trivial local monodromy at
t=1
(see Section
3.1
). Hence, the isomorphism (
4
) can hold only if
R^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{C}
also has non-trivial local monodromy at
t=1
.
Our first result shows that, if this is the case, then (
4
) holds:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the local system
R^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{Q}
has non-trivial local monodromy at
t=1
.
Then the isomorphism (
4
)
holds.

Our second result proves that the monodromy assumption of Theorem
1.1
holds for families
(Z,\pi)
of curves or of even-dimensional varieties:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
(Z,\pi)
is:

(1)

a family of curves, or

(2)

a family of even-dimensional varieties.

Then the local monodromy of the local system
R^{\kappa}{\pi_{U}}_{!}\mathbb{Q}
at
t=1
is non-trivial.

Therefore, if
(Z,\pi)
is a family of curves or of even-dimensional varieties, then (
4
) holds.

Directly verifying the monodromy assumption of Theorem
1.1
for families
(Z,\pi)
of varieties of odd dimension
\kappa>1
is an interesting problem on its own and requires new ideas.

We sketch the main steps of the proofs of the two theorems in Section
1.2
.

1.1.
Motivation and relation to other works

Irreducible hypergeometric local systems are motivic by the work of André and Katz
[
2
,
26
]
.
This means that they arise as subquotients of the variation of cohomology of families of algebraic varieties.
When defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, they also support a pure rational variation of Hodge structure (VHS).
This was first conjectured by Corti and Golyshev
[
7
]
and then proven by Fedorov
[
17
]
.

The isomorphism (
4
) considered in this paper yields a realisation of
\mathbb{H}
in terms of the family
(Z,\pi)
. This is particularly appealing as, unlike previous results, (
4
) identifies
\mathbb{H}
with the (pure) variation of the
entire top graded weighted piece
of the middle compactly supported cohomology of the family.

The family
(Z,\pi)
and its connection to the parameters
\alpha,\beta
appear extensively in the literature.
The family is a specialisation of a family of Gelfand–Kapranov–Zelevinsky (GKZ)’s circuits
[
19
]
.
The work
[
4
]
relates the finite analogs of the hypergeometric function
F(\alpha,\beta\mid t)
(see
[
4
, Definition 1.1]
) with the point count over finite fields of a suitable fibrewise compactification of
(Z,\pi)
. Their result can be regarded as
the arithmetic counterpart of isomorphism (
4
) at the level of point count.
Further, in
[
37
]
, Rodriguez Villegas showed that the Hodge numbers of the stalks of
\gr_{\kappa}^{W}PR^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{C}
agree with those of
\mathbb{H}
as given by the formula in
[
17
]
(see Section
3.3
), providing further evidence for (
4
).
The paper
[
34
]
develops a systematic framework for “hypergeometric motives” defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, which unifies these aspects.
Finally, we conclude this discussion by mentioning the connection to mirror symmetry. We focus on the case of Fano varieties.

To a quasismooth and wellformed Fano weighted complete intersection
X=X_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
of Fano index
\lambda=\sum a_{i}-\sum d_{j}
and dimension
n
, one can associate the gamma vector

\gamma_{X}=(-d_{1},\dots,-d_{c},-\lambda,a_{1},\dots,a_{m})

On the one hand, a specialisation of the regularised quantum operator of
X
is expected to be the pullback along
t=s^{-\lambda}
of the hypergeometric operator
H
associated to
\gamma_{X}
. We refer the reader to
[
6
,
39
,
9
]
for known results in this direction.
On the other hand, the Givental’s
(n+c)
-dimensional Landau–Ginzburg model
(Y,w)
associated to
X

[
20
]
is the pullback along
t=s^{-\lambda}
of the family
(Z,\pi)
associated
\gamma_{X}
.
Therefore, for gamma vectors arising from Fano weighted complete intersections, the isomorphism (
4
) can be viewed as a Hodge–theoretic mirror statement relating
X
and
(Y,w)
.

The first explicit formulation of the isomorphism (
4
) is in the aforementioned paper
[
7
]
, which is motivated by mirror symmetry.
In the language of the present paper, the authors of
[
7
]
prove that, for vectors
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{l})
with only one negative entry
\gamma_{1}
and such that

(5)

\gcd(\gamma_{2},\dots,\widehat{\gamma}_{j},\dots,\gamma_{l})=1\quad\text{ for }j=2,\dots,l

the isomorphism (
4
) holds
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
.
The restriction (
5
) is taken to ensure that
\gamma
defines a well-formed weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}(\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{l})
.
The proof of
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
is based on previous work by Golyshev
[
21
]
and a computation of the Hodge numbers of
\gr_{\kappa}^{W}PR^{\kappa}\pi_{U!}\mathbb{C}

in terms of the geometry of the affine anticanonical cone of
\mathbb{P}
.

In Section
4
, we give a direct proof of (
4
) for any gamma vector
\gamma
such that
\pm\gamma
has only one negative entry (Theorem
4.1
)
by exhibiting a non-trivial period of
(Z,\pi)

which is annihilated by the operator
H
.
Proving the existence of such a period relies on
\gamma
having only one negative entry. Showing the same for a general gamma vector seems out of reach.

We note that hypergeometric differential operators associated to general gamma vectors include classical examples, such as the operators annihilating the hypergeometric series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n)!^{2}}{(n!)^{4}!}\left(\frac{t}{16}\right)^{n}\quad\text{or}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(30n)!\,n!}{(6n)!(10n)!(15n)!}\left(\frac{6^{6}10^{10}15^{15}}{30^{30}}\,t\right)^{n}

We also note that, for a general gamma vector, we do not expect the hypergeometric function defined by the corresponding parameters to be a period of the family
(Z,\pi)
. This observation motivates our focus on local systems.

We conclude by mentioning that, prior to the present paper, the isomorphism (
4
) was proven
ad hoc
for special cases of gamma vectors with two and three negative entries arising from mirror symmetry
[
10
,
9
,
23
,
8
]
.

1.2.
Sketch of the proofs

As mentioned above, for a general gamma vector, it does not seem possible to construct a period of the family
(Z,\pi)
which is annihilated by the operator
H
. To prove (
4
), we take a different approach.

To establish Theorem
1.1
, we prove that, under the monodromy assumption in the statement, the minimal differential operator of a non-trivial relative
\kappa
-form of weight
\kappa
is equivalent to
H
.
This is accomplished in several steps. First, via the theory of GKZ differential systems, we show that distinguished relative forms associated to the family are solutions of
reducible
hypergeometric operators.
This implies that the minimal differential operators of such forms appear as factors of these reducible operators. Next, by a novel analysis of the weight filtration, we show that there exists a relative form of the lowest weight whose minimal differential operator has a genuine singularity at
t=1
.
Combining these two steps and the equality of ranks of the two sides, we obtain the statement.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we study the singularity of the fibre
Z_{1}
. Using the formalism of nearby and vanishing cycles, we obtain the following specialisation sequence in compactly supported cohomology

0\to H^{d-1}_{c}(Z_{1},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{d-1}_{c}(Z_{t},\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{Q}\to H^{d}_{c}(Z_{1},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{d}_{c}(Z_{t},\mathbb{Q})\to 0

which is compatible with monodromy. Moreover, the action of monodromy on
H^{d-1}_{c}(Z_{t},\mathbb{Q})
is non-trivial if and only if the map
H^{d}_{c}(Z_{1},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{d}_{c}(Z_{t},\mathbb{Q})
is an isomorphism.
When
Z_{t}
is even-dimensional, we obtain the isomorphism using the fact that the action of monodromy on the Milnor fiber
\mathbb{Q}
is non-trivial.
When
Z_{t}
is one-dimensional, we obtain the isomorphism from the irreducibility of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{t}
, a non-obvious fact which we prove in Proposition
3.20
.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we recall general facts about families of affine hypersurfaces in algebraic tori. One of the main goals of the section is to prove that, under a regularity assumption, one can build well-behaved fibrewise compactifications of the family.
In Section
3
, we set up our notation for hypergeometric local systems and the family
(Z,\pi)
, and prove several facts about
(Z,\pi)
which we use in the proofs of the theorems.
In Section
4
, we give a direct proof of (
4
) for gamma vectors with one negative entry.
In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
In Section
6
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Families of affine hypersurfaces in algebraic tori

In this section, we study families of affine hypersurfaces in algebraic tori.

Section
2.1
collects some standard facts about such hypersurfaces, with
[
3
]
as our main reference. We also introduce a slight generalisation of the classical notion of
\Delta
-regularity
[
3
, Definition 3.3]
, which we call quasi-
\Delta
-regularity (Definition
2.3
).

In Section
2.2
, we turn to families of such hypersurfaces. We first show that families of quasi-
\Delta
-regular hypersurfaces admit well-behaved compactifications (Theorem
2.19
).
We then focus on the locus of
\Delta
-regularity and collect some key facts about relative cohomology and local systems over the base of the family.

2.1.
Affine hypersurfaces in algebraic tori

Let
d\geq 1
be a positive integer.
Let
L\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm 1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials in
d
variables over
\mathbb{C}
.
We write
x\coloneqq(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
and
x^{m}\coloneqq x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{m_{d}}
for any
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Given
f\in L
, we write
Z_{f}
for the zero locus of
f
in the algebraic torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\Spec L
.

Let
f=\sum a_{m}x^{m}\in L
be a Laurent polynomial, and suppose that
Z_{f}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a hypersurface.

2.1.1.
Quasi–
\Delta
-regularity

Let
\mathrm{Newt}(f)
be the Newton polytope of
f
.
For
F
a face of
\mathrm{Newt}(f)
, denote by
f_{|F}
the Laurent polynomial given by the restriction of
f
to
F
, namely,

(6)

f_{|F}\coloneqq\sum_{m\in F}a_{m}x^{m}

Let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
a lattice polytope.

Definition 2.1
.

[
3
, Definition 3.3]

The polynomial
f
(and the
hypersurface
Z_{f}
) is called
\Delta
-regular
if
\Delta=\mathrm{Newt}(f)
and, for each face
F
of
\Delta

of positive dimension,
the zero locus
Z_{f_{|F}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is smooth.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that, if
\Delta=\mathrm{Newt}(f)
, then, for any face
F
of
\Delta

of positive dimension,
Z_{f_{|F}}
is a hypersurface.
Moreover, if
f
is
\Delta
-regular, then
Z_{f}
is smooth.
Since, by definition,
f
can be
\Delta
-regular only with respect to
\Delta=\mathrm{Newt}(f)
, one often drops the reference to
\Delta
and simply says that
f
is regular. We use the term
\Delta
-regular to align with
[
3
]
.

We introduce a slight generalisation of
\Delta
-regularity:

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
f
(and
Z_{f}
) is
quasi-
\Delta
-regular
if
\mathrm{Newt}(f)=\Delta
, and, for each
proper
face
F
of
\Delta
of positive dimension,

Z_{f_{|F}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is smooth.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that
Z_{f}
is
\Delta
-regular if and only if it is quasi-
\Delta
-regular and smooth.

From now on, we set
\Delta=\mathrm{Newt}(f)
. Moreover, we assume for simplicity that
\Delta
is full-dimensional: if
\dim(\Delta)=d^{\prime}<d
, then
Z_{f}
is a product
\mathbb{T}^{d-d^{\prime}}\times(Z_{f^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d^{\prime}})
, where
f^{\prime}
is
f
viewed as a polynomial in
d^{\prime}
variables, which brings us back to the full-dimensional case.
We conclude the section by recalling an alternative characterisation of (quasi-)
\Delta
-regularity.

It is well known that
\Delta
determines a proper toric variety
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
of dimension
d
which contains the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We denote by
\bar{Z}_{f}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
the closure of
Z_{f}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
. The toric variety
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is a compactification of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
by algebraic tori
\mathbb{T}_{F}
which are in one-to-one correspondence with the faces
F
of
\Delta
.
The dimension of the torus
\mathbb{T}_{F}
equals the dimension of
F
. The tori
\mathbb{T}_{F}
form a partition of
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
:

(7)

\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}=\bigcup_{F\preceq\Delta}\mathbb{T}_{F}

For each face
F
of dimension
\ell>0
, the hypersurface
Z_{f_{|F}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a product of a torus dimension
d-\ell
and a hypersurface in an
\ell
-dimensional torus which is isomorphic to
\bar{Z}_{f}\cap\ \mathbb{T}_{F}\subset\mathbb{T}_{F}
.
Then
f
is (quasi-)
\Delta
-regular if and only if, for each (proper) face
F
of
\Delta
of positive dimension, the hypersurface
\bar{Z}_{f}\cap\ \mathbb{T}_{F}\subset\mathbb{T}_{F}
is smooth.
For further details, see
[
11
, Sections 2.2 and 3.6]
or
[
3
, Remark 4.5 and Proposition 4.6]
.

Remark 2.5
.

The toric variety
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is, in general, not smooth. It is if and only if the normal fan
\Sigma_{\Delta}
of
\Delta
is smooth.
Moreover, the smoothness of
Z_{f}
does not, in general, imply that
\bar{Z}_{f}
is smooth. However, if
f
is
\Delta
-regular and
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is smooth, then
\bar{Z}_{f}
is also smooth.
More precisely, if
f
is
\Delta
-regular and
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is smooth at a point
p\in\bar{Z}_{f}
, then
\bar{Z}_{f}
is also smooth at
p
. This follows from the description of
\bar{Z}_{f}
above and
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
.

More generally, the fan
\Sigma_{\Delta}
always admits a smooth refinement
\Sigma
.
If we denote by
\mathbb{P}
the smooth toric variety associated to the fan
\Sigma
, then the induced toric morphism
\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
is a resolution of singularities.
The closure
V_{f}\subset\mathbb{P}
of
Z_{f}
has a similar description to the one given above for
\bar{Z}_{f}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
(we refer to
[
11
, Section 3.6]
, or
[
14
, Section 2.5]
, for more details).
If
f
is
\Delta
-regular, then
V_{f}
is smooth. Moreover, the boundary divisor

is a normal crossing divisor. Indeed, since
\Sigma
is smooth, the boundary divisor
\mathbb{P}\setminus\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a normal crossing divisor and
V_{f}\setminus Z_{f}
is (by
\Delta
-regularity) the transverse intersection of
V_{f}
and
\mathbb{P}\setminus\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Note that if
f
is quasi-
\Delta
-regular but not
\Delta
-regular, then
V_{f}
is no longer smooth, but its singular locus is contained in
Z_{f}
, and the boundary divisor
V_{f}\setminus Z_{f}
is still a normal crossing divisor.

Remark 2.6
.

The notion of quasi-
\Delta
-regularity is not standard and is introduced for our purposes. Starting from Section
2.2
, we will consider families of affine hypersurfaces in algebraic tori that have singular members. By restricting to the locus of quasi-
\Delta
-regularity, the family can be compactified by a relative normal crossing divisor, without introducing singularities in its members (see Theorem
2.19

2.1.2.
Cohomology groups

For a mixed Hodge structure (MHS)
(H,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
, where
W_{\bullet}
is the weight filtration and
F^{\bullet}
is the Hodge filtration, the
i
th graded piece
\operatorname{gr}_{i}^{W}H
is the quotient
\operatorname{gr}_{i}^{W}H\coloneqq W_{i}/W_{i-1}
. It is a pure Hodge structure of weight
i

We recall a few results on the cohomology groups

General facts

Assume that
Z_{f}
is
\Delta
-regular. Then,
one has that (
[
3
, Section 3]
):

(1)

for all
i>\kappa
,
H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})=0
;

(2)

for each
i\leq\kappa
,
H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
carries a MHS
with weight filtration
W_{j}=W_{j}H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
:

0={W}_{i-1}\subset{W}_{i}\subset\dots\subset W_{2i}=H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})

and Hodge filtration
F^{j}=F^{j}H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C})
:

H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C})=F^{0}\supset F^{1}\supset\dots\supset F^{i+1}=0

(3)

the pullback map
H^{i}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})

is a morphism of MHS. It is bijective for
i<\kappa
and injective for
i=\kappa
.

It follows from (3) that, for
i<\kappa
,
H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
is a pure Hodge structure of rank
\binom{d}{i}
and weight
2i
, and
H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C})=F^{i}H^{i}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C})
. The image of
H^{\kappa}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{Q})
in
H^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
has rank
d
and weight
2\kappa
, and the image of
H^{\kappa}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C})
in
H^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C})
is contained in
F^{\kappa}H^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C})
.

The dependency of
H^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
on

is captured by
the primitive cohomology
PH^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
, defined as the cokernel of the pullback map

H^{\kappa}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
.
By (2) and (3),
PH^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
carries a MHS of weights
\in\{k,\dots,2k\}
.

In this paper, we will be interested in the graded piece

\gr_{\kappa}^{W}PH^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})=W_{\kappa}PH^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})

which carries a pure Hodge structure of weight
\kappa
.
Note that, if
\kappa>0
,
W_{\kappa}H^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
and
W_{\kappa}PH^{\kappa}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})
coincide while, if
\kappa=0
, the latter is the quotient of the first by a copy of

.

Remark 2.11
.

Above, we have worked with cohomology, but one may as well work with compactly supported cohomology.

For
i<\kappa
, the cohomology group

The two cohomology theories are related by Poncairé duality

Remark 6.6
.

If
Z_{t}
is even-dimensional, Corollary
6.2
has an easier proof. Indeed,

Remark 6.7
.

The first part of the proof of the corollary shows that

acts trivially on

Remark 6.8
.

It is clear from the our proofs that Proposition

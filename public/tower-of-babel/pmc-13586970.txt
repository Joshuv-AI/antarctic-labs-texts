ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionDoes adding PDS0101 to pembrolizumab demonstrate antitumor activity and tolerability in patients with recurrent/metastatic human papillomavirus type 16–positive head and neck squamous cell carcinoma who are immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)–naive or ICI-resistant?FindingsIn this nonrandomized clinical trial of 88 patients with head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, among the 53 ICI-naive participants in the modified intent-to-treat population, the response rate was 34%, median progression-free survival was 5.3 months (95% CI, 2.10-9.00), and median overall survival was 39.3 months (95% CI, 23.90 to not evaluable). In the ICI-resistant cohort (n = 22), the response rate was 0%, median progession-free survival was 2 months, and median overal survival was 14.8 months.MeaningThe trial results suggest that PDS0101 plus pembrolizumab demonstrated antitumor activity with manageable safety in ICI-naive participants, supporting further evaluation.

Introduction

The epidemiology of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) has evolved during recent decades, with human papillomavirus (HPV) infection emerging as a distinct etiologic factor. Up to 70% of oropharyngeal cancers in the US and 73% in Europe are now attributed to HPV infection, representing an epidemiologic shift that is expected to continue rising through the 2030s. Among HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancers, HPV type 16 (HPV16) is the predominant genotype, accounting for 80% to 90% of cases. HPV positivity is also observed in nonoropharyngeal HNSCC, accounting for approximately 6% in oral cavity, 1% in laryngeal, and 13% in hypopharyngeal carcinomas.

Although HPV positivity is associated with improved prognosis in locally advanced disease, this advantage is less consistent outside the oropharynx, and outcomes remain poor once the disease progresses to the recurrent/metastatic (R/M) setting. A subgroup analysis from the KEYNOTE-048 trial demonstrated that p16 (an immunohistochemistry test that identifies a surrogate protein marker for various high-risk HPV infections) did not predict benefit from pembrolizumab, with hazard ratios showing no significant differences between the p16-positive and p16-negative treatment groups for progression-free survival (PFS) or overall survival (OS). This surrogate marker is distinct from the specific HPV16 genotype targeted in this study. Median survival for HPV16 R/M HNSCC is less than 15 months despite treatments that use immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) alone or in combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy.

The first-line standard of care for patients with R/M HNSCC is platinum-based chemotherapy in combination with pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab alone. Due to the modest efficacy of pembrolizumab alone, most patients require cytotoxic chemotherapy early in their disease course, either in combination with immunotherapy or after failure of immunotherapy, with grade 3 or higher adverse events reported in up to 85% of patients who receive chemotherapy and immunotherapy. The toxic effects of chemotherapy and inadequate tumor control remain key threats to patient survival and quality of life. A novel immunotherapy combination that prolongs disease control may improve quality of life directly by delaying the time until progressive disease and indirectly by delaying the need for cytotoxic chemotherapy.

Therapeutic vaccines have been in clinical development since the 1980s, with limited success due to several factors, including suboptimal antigen-specific immune responses, tumor-induced immunosuppression, and immune evasion. The advent of ICIs has resulted in a renewed interest in cancer therapeutic vaccines, as ICIs restore T-cell effector function and enable effective tumor-specific T-cell responses. The combination of therapeutic cancer vaccines and ICIs is promising because it allows for the expansion of tumor-specific T cells through the vaccine response and helps overcome the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment through immune checkpoint inhibition.

HPV-driven cancer has a unique vulnerability in terms of preserved viral-based neoantigens, including E6 and E7. PDS0101 (Versamune; PD Biotech) combines a T cell–activating platform, the biologically active pure R enantiomer of the cationic lipid 1,2-dioleoyl-trimethyl-ammonium, and a proprietary mixture of HPV16 E6 and E7 multiepitope peptides. PDS0101 upregulates type I interferon signaling in the lymph nodes while also promoting processing and cross presentation of the E6 and E7 peptides via Major Histocompatibility Complex class 1 and 2 pathways. This mechanism results in the induction of potent multifunctional CD4 (helper) and CD8 (killer) T cells, including long-acting memory T cells. Combining the HPV16-targeted immune stimulatory activity of PDS0101 in conjunction with programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) blockade has the potential to improve objective response rates (ORRs) in patients with HPV16-positive HNSCC. This article reports the findings of the VERSATILE-002 phase 2 study across the ICI-naive and ICI-resistant cohorts.

Methods

Study Design

VERSATILE-002 was a phase 2, open-label, multicenter study that was conducted from March 29, 2021, to May 15, 2025, in the US, UK, and Ireland, with 26 sites enrolling at least 1 participant. The study was designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the combination of PDS0101 and pembrolizumab in HPV16-positive R/M HNSCC in ICI-naive and ICI-resistant cohorts. The study was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. The study protocol (Supplement 1) was approved by the institutional review boards at individual sites and the central institutional review boards at Advarra and WIRB-Copernicus Group (the statistical analysis plan can be found in Supplement 2). All participants provided written informed consent, and the study was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04260126).

The treatment regimen consisted of pembrolizumab, 200 mg, intravenously, every 3 weeks for up to 35 cycles or until disease progression, which was combined with 5 doses of PDS0101 (3.0 mg of R enantiomer of the cationic lipid 1,2-dioleoyl-trimethyl-ammonium + 2.7 mg of HPVmix) at 1 mL subcutaneously on cycles 1, 2, 3, 4, and 12. Imaging was performed at baseline, every 9 weeks for the first year, and every 12 weeks for the remainder of the study. Each imaging assessment was evaluated by a masked independent central reviewer and investigator assessments. Following completion of treatment or disease progression, participants continued in long-term follow-up and were contacted every 12 weeks for survival status.

Patient Eligibility

Eligible participants were at least age 18 years and had histologically-confirmed R/M HNSCC. Participants were initially screened with local p16 testing, then were confirmed centrally with HPV polymerase chain reaction. Participants had measurable disease according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST), version 1.1, an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status score of 0 to 1, and adequate organ function. For the ICI-naive cohort, participant tumors were required to have a programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD-L1) combined positive score (CPS) of 1 or greater using the US Food and Drug Administration–approved IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay. For the ICI-resistant cohort, there were no CPS restrictions; all participants previously received a minimum of 8 weeks of treatment with an ICI with documented clinical or radiographic progression. Key exclusion criteria included prior grade 3 or higher immune-related adverse events from checkpoint inhibition (ICI-resistant cohort), active autoimmune disease that required systemic treatment, and active central nervous system metastases.

Statistical Analysis

The primary end point of the study was confirmed best overall response, which was reported as the objective response rate (ORR) as determined by RECIST, version 1.1, and evaluated by a central reviewer. Efficacy end points were evaluated in the modified intent-to-treat (mITT) population, which was defined as all participants who met the eligibility criteria, received at least 1 dose of the study drug, and had at least 1 postbaseline tumor assessment. Participants who died before their first imaging assessment were considered nonresponders and were included in the mITT population. Safety analyses were performed in the safety population, which was defined as all participants who received at least 1 dose of the study drug.

The study used the Simon 2-stage optimal design separately for the ICI-naive and ICI-resistant cohorts. For the ICI-naive cohort, the design tested a null ORR of 17% vs an alternative ORR of 33%, with a 1-sided type 1 error rate of .05 and 80% power. Stage 1 enrolled 17 participants. If 4 or more responses were observed, an additional 37 participants were to be enrolled (total n = 54). The combination was considered efficacious if 14 or more responses were observed among all 54 participants. For the ICI-resistant cohort, the design tested a null ORR of 5% vs an alternative ORR of 20%, with a 1-sided type 1 error rate of .05 and 90% power. Stage 1 enrolled 21 participants. If 2 or more responses were observed, an additional 20 participants were to be enrolled (total n = 41). The combination was considered efficacious if 5 or more responses were observed among all 41 participants.

PFS and OS were secondary end points; all other end points were exploratory. PFS was defined as the time from initiation of the study treatment until disease progression or death of any cause. OS was defined as the time from the initiation of study treatment until death of any cause. Duration of response (DOR) was defined as the time from the earliest date of documented CR or PR until disease progression or death from any cause. PFS, OS, and DOR were calculated using Kaplan-Meier time-to-event methods. Disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the percentage of participants who had a complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease, with a minimum criteria of stable disease duration of 6 weeks (1.4 months). All analyses were performed using SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute).

Results

From March 29, 2021, to May 15, 2025, 123 participants were screened and 87 patients (70.7%) were enrolled and received treatment (62 ICI-naive [71.3%] and 25 ICI-resistant participants [28.7%]). Of these, 55 were ICI-naive participants who met the criteria for the intent-to-treat (ITT) population. Two ITT participants had no imaging following baseline and were excluded from the efficacy population (mITT ICI-naive = 53). In the ICI-resistant group, 22 participants met the criteria for the ITT and mITT populations. Participant characteristics are summarized in Table 1, and the participant disposition is illustrated in Figure 1. Of the 75 patients in the mITT population, the mean (SD) age was 64.0 (8.3) years; 4 individuals (5%) were female and 71 (95%) were male; and 2 individuals [3%] were Asian, 1 [1%] was Black, 5 (7%) were Hispanic, and 71 (95%) were White.

Efficacy in ICI-Naive Participants

In the ICI-naive cohort, 4 responders were observed by central review among the first 17 evaluable participants and met the criteria to proceed to stage 2. Response data for the full mITT population are summarized in eTables 1 and 2 in Supplement 3. The ORR results were overall concordant between the central review and investigators. By central review, there were 6 CRs and 12 PRs, yielding an ORR of 33.96% (18 of 53; 95% CI, 21.52-48.27; eTable 3 in Supplement 3). By investigators, there were 5 CRs and 14 PRs, for an ORR of 35.85% (19 of 53; 95% CI, 23.14-50.20; eTable 4 in Supplement 3). Results for the ITT population are in eTables 5 and 6 in Supplement 3.

By central review, 14 of 53 participants (26.4%) had stable disease, with a DCR of 60.4%. Twenty two of 53 participants (41.5%) had stable disease by investigators, with a DCR of 77.4%. Median DOR was not evaluable (95% CI, 6.9 to not evaluable) by central review and 21.8 months (95% CI, 11.50 to not evaluable) by investigators. The median duration of stable disease by central review was 6.2 months (IQR, 4.2-10.5; eTable 8 in Supplement 3) and was 4.3 months (IQR, 3.8-8.4) by investigators (eTable 9 in Supplement 3). Median PFS (mPFS) was 5.3 months (95% CI, 2.10-9.00) by central review and 6.3 months (95% CI, 4.20-12.50) by investigators (eFigures 1-2 and eTables 10-12 in Supplement 3).

The median follow-up was 24.4 months (IQR, 9.7-29.5). The median OS (mOS) for the ICI-naive cohort was 39.3 months (95% CI, 23.90 to not evaluable). The 12-month OS rate was 78.5% (95% CI, 64.5%-87.5), and the 24-month OS rate was 67.6% (95% CI, 52.5%-78.8%) (Figure 2). eFigure 3 in Supplement 3 provides OS rates for ICI-naive cohort within the ITT population. Safety Profile in ICI-Naive Participants

Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), occurring for 5% or more of patients for the combination of PDS0101 and pembrolizumab, are described in Table 2. The most common injection site TRAEs were pain (37 [59.7%]), swelling (19 [30.6%]), and discoloration (9 [14.5%]), which were grade 1 and 2 only. Other observed TRAEs included fatigue (25 [40.3%]), headache (12 [19.4%]), pruritus (9 [14.5%]), diarrhea (8 [12.9%]), and arthralgia (5 [8.1%]). Eleven of 62 participants (17.7%) experienced grade 3 to 4 TRAEs, including 1 grade 4 TRAE (encephalitis), which was reported approximately 1 year after the last PDS0101 dose; the event eventually resolved, and it was deemed to be unrelated to PDS0101 and related to pembrolizumab. No grade 5 TRAEs were observed. Four of 62 participants (6.5%) discontinued PDS0101 and 7 of 62 (11.3%) discontinued pembrolizumab due to adverse events. Participants received a median of 4 doses of PDS0101 (range, 1-5) and 6 doses of pembrolizumab (range, 1-35; eTable 13 in Supplement 3).

Discussion

In this nonrandomized clinical trial, the combination of PDS0101 and pembrolizumab showed promising efficacy and an acceptable safety profile in ICI-naive participants with HPV16-positive R/M HNSCC with a CPS of 1 or greater. The trial met its primary end point in the ICI-naive cohort, with confirmed ORRs of 33.96%. A total of 22.6% experienced deep tumor reductions (>90%). These response rates compared favorably with historical benchmark trials that evaluated pembrolizumab-based therapy in R/M HNSCC, including KEYNOTE-048 and LEAP-010, in which ORRs ranged from 19% to 25%, although cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted cautiously given differences in study populations and design. Central review assessments were consistent with those of investigators.

Secondary end points were consistent with ORR findings, with an mOS of 39.3 months in the ICI-naive population, which was longer than the 12.3 months reported in the pembrolizumab arm of KEYNOTE-048 and the 17.9 months reported in the pembrolizumab control arm of LEAP-010. Median DOR was not reached by central review.

Although HPV-positive oropharyngeal SCC is associated with improved prognosis in the curative setting, outcomes in the R/M setting remain poor overall, and to our knowledge HPV status has not been established as a predictive biomarker for response to PD-1–based therapy. Unfortunately, neither KEYNOTE-048 nor LEAP-010 reported absolute survival numbers as stratified by HPV, precluding clean comparison of these benchmarks with our HPV-only data. However, the outcomes reported in this article are favorable compared with the HPV-unselected data reported in these studies.

PFS and OS divergence can occur in immunotherapy-based regimens for several reasons. First, PFS is associated with RECIST-defined progression at scheduled radiographic assessments and may not fully capture delayed or evolving immune-mediated tumor control, including scenarios in which early radiographic progression does not translate into early mortality. Second, unlike PFS, OS reflects the cumulative association of postprogression management, including local therapies and subsequent systemic therapies.

Previous trials have investigated the combination of HPV-directed therapeutic vaccines in combination with PD-1–targeted ICIs for treating HPV-related R/M HNSCC. Massarelli and colleagues investigated the combination of the synthetic long-peptide vaccine ISA101 with nivolumab in 24 participants with incurable HPV16-positive solid tumors, reporting a response rate of 33% and mOS of 17.9 months. More recently, additional HPV-targeted immunotherapies are being evaluated in combination with ICIs, including HB-200 (an arenavirus-based HPV16 vaccine), CUE-101 with an mOS of 20.8 months (an interleukin 2–based fusion protein targeting HPV16 E7), and BNT113, with an mOS of 22.6 months (a messenger RNA vaccine that encodes HPV16 E6/7), demonstrating promising preliminary response and survival outcomes. Our combination of PDS0101 and pembrolizumab provides further evidence of the activity of ICIs with an HPV-targeted immunotherapy, resulting in an OS of 39.3 months, which compared favorably with outcomes reported in prior studies of HPV-targeted immunotherapy combinations in R/M HNSCC, while recognizing the limitations of cross-trial comparisons.

The PDS0101 and pembrolizumab combination was well tolerated, with a safety profile comparable with pembrolizumab monotherapy. The most notable difference was the presence of injection site reactions in 65 of 87 participants (74.7%), all of which were grade 1 or 2. There was only 1 grade 4 combination TRAE and no grade 5 events. The overall rate of grade 3 to 4 events (13.8%) was comparable with pembrolizumab alone (17%) in KEYNOTE-048 and substantially lower than the 85% reported with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy. Collectively, the tolerability of the combination, encouraging response outcomes, and promising OS could provide substantial improvement in the duration and quality of life for patients with HPV-associated R/M HNSCC by controlling the underlying disease and delaying the time to cytotoxic chemotherapy.

Consistent with prior data on ICI monotherapy, absolute response and survival outcomes appeared greater with higher PD-L1 expression. While this single-arm, phase 2 study could not evaluate the interaction effect between treatment and PD-L1 status, the difference between observed and historic results was larger in the subgroup with high levels of PD-L1, which was consistent with data from ISA101. While these subgroup data are exploratory, participants with a CPS of 1 to 19 had an mOS of 29.5 months, failing to lend support to the hypothesis that benefit is restricted to the population with a CPS of 20 or greater.

In contrast to the favorable first-line results, the ICI-resistant cohort did not meet the prespecified ORR threshold. This outcome was consistent with findings across HNSCC and other solid tumors, for which ICIs are most effective when administered early. It also aligned with TRACER-X correlative data that showed that tumor evolution over time is characterized by immune evasion, particularly through loss of antigen presentation machinery. Loss of human leukocyte antigen and other antigen presentation machinery components would be expected to impair the activity of pembrolizumab and PDS0101. These mechanistic insights underscore the importance of initiating immunotherapy before irreversible immune escape occurs.

Limitations

The study enrolled 1 fewer evaluable participant than planned, resulting in a modest reduction in statistical power. However, this limitation was offset by the favorable ORR, which exceeded the study’s alternative hypothesis assumption. The trial was small and nonrandomized, limiting the power for subgroup analyses and precluding evaluation of potential interaction effects. Cross-trial comparisons of OS could be interpreted cautiously because eligibility criteria and population enrichment (HPV16-positive disease, PD-L1 CPS≥1 in the ICI-naive cohort, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group score of 0-1) differ materially from historical studies.

White participants were overrepresented in this study. This likely partially reflects the epidemiology of HPV16-positive oropharyngeal HNSCC in the US, which disproportionately affects non-Hispanic White men, but structural barriers to enrollment in academic multicenter trials, including referral patterns, travel burden, time away from work, and other resource constraints, may have also contributed and disproportionately affect patients of racial and ethnic minority groups. This demographic distribution may limit generalizability. Future studies should incorporate recruitment strategies to improve equity in access, including broader community site participation, travel support, and prospective monitoring of enrollment by race and ethnicity. Additional limitations included that primary tumor site was not captured, the interval from initial therapy to R/M disease was not analyzed, and distant metastasis vs locoregional failure response was not analyzed.

Conclusions

The results of this nonrandomized clinical trial validate the potential of PDS0101 to enhance antitumor immunity when administered with ICI therapy in the first-line R/M setting. The favorable safety profile and meaningful efficacy outcomes support advancement into confirmatory testing in the first-line setting.

Impact of PerioperAtive LidocAine Infusions on Enhanced Recovery After Noncardiac Surgery (IMPALA-ERAS) in an inpatient setting: rationale, design and protocol for a sequential, repeated crossover trial
Authors: Gerald W Rouleau, Aden W Smith, Crystal G Parrish, Leslie J Allen, Jennifer E Jayaram, Sydney R Ruble, Claire M Adkins, Svetlana Eden, Matthew S Shotwell, Edward T Yaghmour, Nicholas J Statzer, Jonathan P Wanderer, Christopher Patrick Henson, Danial Shams, Miklos D Kertai, IMPALA Investigators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMultimodal analgesic strategies designed to minimise perioperative opioid exposure are fundamental components of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) pathways. Despite widespread implementation of ERAS protocols, the optimal analgesic regimen remains undefined, as the individual contributions of specific agents to overall analgesic efficacy and opioid-sparing effects are not fully elucidated. Intravenous lidocaine, a widely utilised local anaesthetic, possesses both analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties and has been associated with improved gastrointestinal recovery. This study seeks to pragmatically evaluate the impact of incorporating perioperative intravenous lidocaine infusion into established ERAS pathways on postoperative functional recovery.Methods and analysisThe Impact of PerioperAtive LidocAine Infusions (IMPALA) on ERAS trial is a single-centre, pragmatic, cluster-randomised, double-blinded, placebo-controlled study. A total of 2290 patients undergoing elective colorectal surgery, emergency general surgery, urology, ventral hernia repair, surgical oncology or spine surgery will be randomly assigned to receive either intraoperative and postoperative intravenous lidocaine infusions (administered for up to 48 hours) or placebo as part of a standardised multimodal analgesic regimen integrated into established ERAS pathways. The primary outcome is case mix index–adjusted resource length of stay, defined as the time interval from surgical initiation to hospital discharge adjusted for case mix index. The primary outcome is total inpatient opioid consumption within the first 72 hours, reported in oral morphine milligram equivalents. Secondary outcomes include various in-hospital clinical endpoints derived from the electronic health record.Ethics and disseminationThis protocol and accompanying statistical analysis plan outline the study design, primary and secondary endpoints and analytic methodology. The IMPALA-ERAS trial has received ethical approval from the Vanderbilt University Institutional Review Board (IRB: 250617). The findings will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national conferences. Results from this trial are expected to inform evidence-based practices regarding perioperative lidocaine infusion and its potential contributions to enhanced postoperative recovery in surgical patients.Trial registration numberNCT07224711.

Introduction

Despite significant advances in perioperative analgesia, uncontrolled postoperative pain remains a major source of patient dissatisfaction, with up to 75% of patients reporting pain as moderate, severe or extreme.1 Addressing this persistent challenge has involved the adoption of numerous strategies over the past decade, including opioids, regional anaesthesia, acetaminophen, gabapentinoids, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), muscle relaxants and intravenous adjuncts such as lidocaine and ketamine.2 However, in light of the ongoing opioid epidemic in the USA, there is an urgent need to further evaluate and expand non-opioid analgesic approaches to optimise pain management while minimising opioid consumption.

Lidocaine, a widely available local anaesthetic approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 1948,3 has been used intravenously for postoperative analgesia for over half a century.4 Systemic administration of lidocaine is known to exhibit anti-inflammatory, antihyperalgesic and analgesic properties, making it valuable for both acute perioperative pain and chronic neuropathic pain management.5 Despite its long history of use, evidence supporting its efficacy in surgical populations remains inconsistent. Studies conducted over the past decade, including both retrospective and prospective investigations across various surgical procedures, have yielded conflicting results. For instance, a recent randomised trial demonstrated no improvement in the return of bowel function among patients undergoing minimally invasive colorectal surgery who received intravenous lidocaine for 6 or 12 hours compared with controls.3 6–10

One critical limitation of these studies lies in the relatively short duration of lidocaine infusion, which may fail to capture potential benefits during later phases of recovery, such as ambulation and dietary advancement. Similarly, a large Cochrane review concluded that while intravenous lidocaine may reduce pain in abdominal surgery, its benefit in other surgical contexts appears limited.9 Notably, this review included studies with heterogeneous infusion protocols, with many trials discontinuing lidocaine at the end of surgery, potentially missing its effects on longer-term recovery outcomes.

Recognising these limitations, we hypothesised that a prolonged perioperative lidocaine infusion, administered within the framework of a mature ERAS programme and across a heterogeneous surgical population, may better elucidate its potential benefits for postoperative recovery. By extending the duration of lidocaine infusion to encompass later phases of recovery, this approach aims to address gaps in previous studies and provide a more comprehensive evaluation of its efficacy in enhancing postoperative outcomes.

Methods and analysis

Our manuscript was prepared in accordance with Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines (Milestones and Timelines are available in online supplemental table 1, and SPIRIT checklist is available in online supplemental table 2).11 Our manuscript describes key elements of the Impact of Perioperative Lidocaine Infusions on Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (IMPALA-ERAS) trial protocol and statistical analysis plan.

Study design and intervention

The IMPALA-ERAS trial is a single-centre, pragmatic, double-blind, cluster-randomised, placebo-controlled study at Vanderbilt University Medical Center (VUMC) in Nashville, Tennessee, USA. The IMPALA-ERAS trial was approved by the Vanderbilt University Medical Center Institutional Review Board (ƒ: 250617). The trial protocol was also registered with ClinicalTrials.gov and was last updated 5 November 2025, prior to initiation of study participants’ enrolment (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT07224711).

The IMPALA-ERAS trial will examine the impact of intravenous lidocaine infusion administered within the existing VUMC ERAS programme on operational and patient outcomes.

Patients undergoing surgery at VUMC are routinely screened for inclusion in the institutional ERAS programme. The trial was intentionally designed to integrate seamlessly into routine clinical practice, where perioperative lidocaine infusions have been incorporated into ERAS care bundles for several years. Enrolment occurs on the day of surgery, when an anaesthesiologist from the Anaesthesia Perioperative Medicine Consult Service approaches eligible patients, provides a detailed explanation of the study, and obtains informed consent from those who elect to participate.

All study procedures are performed by clinical anaesthesia providers—including attending anaesthesiologists, advanced practice providers and anaesthesiology residents—without reliance on dedicated research personnel. Members of the Anaesthesia Perioperative Medicine Consult Service receive standardised training on the trial protocol and consent process, supported by scripted guidance and monthly orientation sessions.

To preserve the pragmatic design, a modified informed consent process was implemented. Given the determination of minimal incremental risk, the IRB approved, written consent long form requiring the participant’s signature (online supplemental material 1). Research consent is obtained concurrently with clinical consent for regional or neuraxial anaesthesia and is uploaded directly into the electronic medical record. This approach mirrors the consent framework previously used in the IMPAKT-ERAS trial.12

Randomisation and interventions

Randomisation is conducted using a blocked, cluster-based design with 4-week clusters, such that all eligible patients within a given 4-week period are assigned to the same study arm. Cluster allocation is determined by a pre-generated, blocked randomisation sequence with a block size of 2 treatment periods (8 weeks) to ensure balance between treatment arms over time while minimising temporal confounding and preserving allocation concealment. Patients, clinicians, investigators and outcome assessors remain blinded to treatment assignment.

Study medication is prepared and dispensed by the operating room pharmacy in blinded form. At induction of anaesthesia, patients receive a weight-based bolus of 1.5 mg/kg followed by a continuous infusion at 2 mg/min via a preprogrammed infusion pump. The intraoperative infusion is discontinued at the conclusion of surgery. Postoperatively, a new blinded infusion is initiated in the post-anaesthesia care unit and continued for a minimum of 12 hours, with an intended duration of 24 hours.3 The infusion may be extended up to 48 hours in patients with ongoing analgesic requirements or continued nil per os status, as evidence suggests that prolonged infusions (≥24 hours) may confer greater analgesic benefit than shorter durations. The infusion may be discontinued earlier for adverse effects, intolerance or hospital discharge. Patients randomised to the placebo arm receive equivalent volumes of normal saline administered according to the same protocol.

Postoperative care is managed by the Anaesthesia Perioperative Medicine Consult Service, which performs daily rounds and provides protocolised management of pain, nausea, and recovery milestones. A standardised algorithm is used for breakthrough pain, and team members are available in-house 24 hours per day.

Blinding

Patients, clinicians, and investigators will be blinded to treatment allocation throughout the study. The investigational pharmacy will prepare identical infusions for both arms, ensuring visual indistinguishability. Emergency unblinding will be permitted when the treating physician determines that knowledge of the treatment allocation is clinically necessary for patient management, such as in the setting of a serious adverse event or suspected adverse drug reaction. In such cases, the treating physician will contact the investigational pharmacy, which maintains sealed individual allocation records for each participant. The date, time, reason for unblinding and identity of the requesting clinician will be documented. The principal investigator and the Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will be notified within 24 hours, and all unblinding events will be reported to the IRB in accordance with institutional requirements. Participants who are unblinded will continue to be followed for all study outcomes on an intention-to-treat basis.

Data collection

The IMPALA-ERAS trial will minimise observer bias using two complementary data collection strategies. First, structured perioperative data routinely documented during clinical care will be automatically extracted from the electronic health record and exported daily into Epic Clarity, the Vanderbilt University Medical Center Data Warehouse, the Vanderbilt Department of Anesthesiology Perioperative Data Warehouse and the Vanderbilt University Hospital ERAS Compliance Database (figures 1–3). Second, trained study personnel will perform targeted manual abstraction to capture missing elements and verify the accuracy of automated electronic data capture (online supplemental table 3).

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome of the study is case mix index–adjusted resource length of stay (CARLOS), defined as the interval from anaesthesia start to hospital discharge, adjusted for the case mix index. While traditional LOS is commonly used in ERAS evaluations, CARLOS allows for more meaningful comparisons across heterogeneous surgical procedures and specialties.13 14 The other primary outcome will be total inpatient opioid consumption within the first 72 hours expressed as oral morphine milligram equivalents (oMMEs).

Secondary outcomes

The IMPALA-ERAS trial will evaluate several pre-specified secondary outcomes: (1) acute pain focused outcomes, visual analogue scale pain scores, recorded at 0 (first pain score in post-anaesthesia care unit/ICU), 4, 8, 12, 24, 48 and 72 hours and at discharge; and total consumption of inpatient opioids within day of surgery and hospital discharge expressed as oMMEs; (2) surgical outcomes, such as return of bowel function, ileus, nausea, rapid response called, surgical site infections, ICU transfers and mortality; (3) side-effect–specific outcomes, including blurry/double vision, tinnitus, sedation and adverse reactions requiring early cessation; and (4) operational specific outcome, meeting early discharge milestones, resource LOS (duration a patient spends in a healthcare facility as a measure of resource use) and case mix index (average clinical complexity and resource intensity of the patient populations).

Safety outcomes

The IMPALA-ERAS trial will not include specific safety outcomes, as the use of lidocaine infusion for acute surgical pain management prevention is standard, approved and routinely practised in clinical care.

Statistical analysis and reporting

Sample size estimation and power calculation

All power calculations were performed using Monte Carlo simulations (10 000 iterations) based on institutionally available CARLOS data and total inpatient opioid consumption, expressed as oMMEs. These institutional data were used to define the empirical distributions under the null hypothesis, and treatment effects were simulated as proportional or absolute reductions applied to these distributions. Given the highly skewed distributions of both CARLOS days and oMMEs, the non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for all power calculations. All calculations assumed a statistical power of 85% and a two-sided type I error rate of 5%.

Primary outcome: case mix index–adjusted resource length of stay

A 10% reduction in CARLOS was selected as the primary effect size threshold based on consistency with prior evidence and clinical relevance. The Cochrane review of perioperative intravenous lidocaine reported a mean LOS reduction of 0.37 days (95% CI −0.60 to −0.15) overall, while prolonged infusions (≥24 hours) in colorectal surgery were associated with a 1.30 day reduction (95% CI −1.72 to −0.88).3 As a case mix index–adjusted metric, CARLOS enables meaningful comparisons across heterogeneous surgical procedures and specialties, making a 10% reduction a clinically relevant benchmark. To detect this effect, a sample size of 2290 participants (1145 per group) is required. For a 20% reduction, the required sample size decreases to 624 participants (312 per group). With an estimated surgical volume of approximately 24 eligible patients per week, it is projected to take 1 year and 10 months to enrol enough participants to detect a 10% reduction in CARLOS.

Primary outcome: total inpatient opioid consumption up to 72 hours

Assuming a sample size of 2290 patients (1145 per group), this study will be powered to detect a reduction of 10.3 oMMEs or greater in the lidocaine group compared with the control group. This threshold is consistent with the 10 mg morphine equivalent reduction proposed as a clinically significant benchmark in the Cochrane literature on perioperative analgesic interventions.15 For context, the mean total postoperative inpatient opioid consumption across common surgical procedures has been reported as 303.6 MMEs (median 130.0 MMEs; SD 2926.0), reflecting a highly positively skewed distribution.16 The detectable effect of 10.3 oMMEs therefore represents a small but clinically meaningful reduction, particularly when considered alongside multimodal analgesic strategies aimed at incremental opioid sparing. For a sample size of 624 patients (312 per group), the study will be able to detect a reduction of 20.0 oMMEs or greater in the intravenous lidocaine infusion group compared with the control group.

Enrolment rate

Patient enrolment will be monitored prospectively, with predefined progression criteria evaluated at 6 months to assess whether observed enrolment meets at least 80% of the projected rate; these criteria will guide decisions to continue as planned, implement recruitment adaptations or adjust the study timeline.

Data and safety monitoring board and interim analysis

An independent DSMB composed of three unaffiliated, multidisciplinary experts will oversee the trial. Given that the protocol permits lidocaine infusions of up to 48 hours, an enhanced safety monitoring schedule has been adopted. A formal interim safety analysis will be conducted after approximately 25% of the target enrolment has been achieved, with a second interim safety analysis at 50% enrolment. Between formal analyses, the DSMB will receive quarterly aggregate safety reports summarising adverse event rates by treatment arm. Rates of adverse events, including those potentially attributable to local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (eg, neurological symptoms and cardiac arrhythmias), will be summarised and compared between groups at each review. At DSMB meetings, members will review enrolment progress, protocol adherence, infusion duration data and reports of serious and unanticipated adverse events. These will be closed sessions, and the DSMB will have access to unblinded outcome data. The DSMB will provide recommendations to the principal investigator and will have the authority to recommend early trial termination for safety concerns, modification of the maximum infusion duration or implementation of additional monitoring requirements. All DSMB recommendations will be promptly communicated to the IRB.

Statistical analysis

This study is a cluster randomised, pragmatic clinical trial. Initial analysis will use descriptive statistics (counts and percentages for ordinal/categorical variables and medians and interquartile ranges for continuous variables) and data visualisations to both identify and address spurious values and characterise the study cohort. Characteristics will be grouped by study arm, but we do not plan to compare the study arms with statistical tests.

All primary analyses for primary and secondary outcomes will be performed on an intention-to-treat basis. The distribution of the primary outcome CARLOS, measured in days, is skewed; therefore, a proportional odds regression model will be used. The model will be adjusted for the binary exposure and baseline characteristics such as age, body mass index (BMI), history of substance use and type of surgical procedure. While covariate adjustment is not necessary in a randomised clinical trial, adjustment for variables potentially associated with the outcome may improve estimate precision. The common odds ratio from the model will be estimated; this quantity can be interpreted similar to the concordance probability and is robust to the proportional odds assumption. We will also report the model-assisted estimates of mean and median LOS. Bootstrapping will be used to generate appropriate CIs.16

The other primary outcome, total inpatient opioid consumption within the first 72 hours (expressed as oMMEs), and the secondary outcomes, including resource LOS, case mix index and pain scores, will be analysed using similar methods. For the two primary outcomes, no adjustment for multiple comparisons will be applied, as they do not pertain to the same overarching hypothesis. For safety outcomes, occurrence of events will be reported as rates, and logistic regression will be used to compare the odds of events between the two exposure groups. This will hold true for the incidence of surgical outcomes (return of bowel function, ileus, nausea, rapid responses called and ICU transfers), as well as the incidence of side effects (double/blurry vision, sedation and adverse events requiring early cessation).

For each primary outcome, we will also perform exploratory dose–response analyses with lidocaine infusion duration as the main exposure. These analyses will focus only on the exposed patients and will be performed using proportional odds logistic regression adjusted for infusion duration, age, BMI, history of substance use, type of surgical procedure and surgery duration, ASA physical status and regional nerve block utilisation.

In additional exploratory analyses, to study differential treatment effects in subgroups of interest, we will use a similar approach as for the primary analysis and include interaction terms between the treatment and the subgrouping variables: sex, race, documented history of opioid use, block use, known history of anxiety and known history of depression. The significance of the differential treatment effect will be tested using a joint (multi-degree-of-freedom) likelihood ratio test of all interaction terms including the product terms between the treatment and subgrouping variable and its non-linear components if applicable.17 18

Missing covariates with >50% missing values will be excluded from the model. The remaining missing values will be imputed using multiple imputation with chained equations.19 20 Fifty datasets will be generated, and the modelling results will be combined using Rubin’s rule.21 All continuous variables will be modelled using restricted cubic splines with up to four knots. Analyses will use a 0.05 type I error rate and be performed using R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria. https://www.R-project.org/).

Study limitations

This study has several anticipated methodological limitations. First, it is a single-centre trial conducted at a quaternary academic centre, which may limit generalisability to other practice settings. Second, variability in postoperative infusion duration (12–48 hours) introduces heterogeneity in treatment exposure. Third, the pragmatic design, while enhancing real-world applicability, permits variability in concurrent analgesic regimens across surgical specialties. Finally, reliance on automated electronic health record data extraction, although supplemented by manual verification, may be subject to documentation variability.

Study status

The IMPALA-ERAS trial has not started recruiting patients yet. The estimated start date is 1 October 2026, and the estimated completion date is 30 September 2028.

Ethics and dissemination

IRB approval and informed consent

This study was approved by the Vanderbilt University Institutional Review Board (IRB: 250617). Informed consent for study participation will be obtained during the recruitment process for each eligible participant. Patients presenting for surgery at VUMC are automatically screened for inclusion in our ERAS programme. On the day of surgery, a physician from the Perioperative Medicine Consult team will meet with patients to discuss the study’s goals and multimodal pathways and obtain consent for performing a regional nerve block. Eligible patients will also be invited to participate in the trial during this initial encounter, with consent obtained using a formal study document in accordance with regulatory standards. This consent form is included in the supplemental documents of this application (online supplemental material 1).

For patients returning for follow-up procedures that meet the study’s inclusion criteria, consent will be reobtained, and patients will be rerandomised as necessary. Importantly, there is no required waiting period between informing potential participants about the study and obtaining their consent. To ensure voluntary participation and prevent coercion, patients will be explicitly informed that their involvement in the study is entirely optional and will not affect the quality of care they receive. Regardless of their decision, all patients will receive the same standard of postoperative pain management. Participants may opt out of the study at any time by notifying key study personnel.

Protocol changes

Any modifications to the trial protocol will be documented on ClinicalTrials.gov in accordance with SPIRIT guidelines.

Data handling and sharing

All study data will be de-identified prior to analysis by trained study personnel. Only vetted and approved research staff will have access to the data.

Dissemination plan

Findings from this trial will be submitted to peer-reviewed journals for publication and presented at national and international scientific conferences.

Counting Matrices in SL 3 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) with Fixed Completely Split Character Polynomial: Preliminary Upper Bounds
Igor Rivin

Abstract
We give elementary, self-contained proofs of sharp upper bounds of order H ( n ⁡ ( n − 1 ) / 2 CLOSE H^{(n(n-1)/2} for the number of matrices A ∈ SL n ​ ( ℤ ) A\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) of sup-norm ≤ H \leq H whose characteristic polynomial is fully split over ℚ \mathbb{Q} The exponentmatches the general upper bounds for fixed emphirreducible characteristic polynomials due to Eskin–Mozes–Shah and subsequent refinements, and is predicted (e.g. by work/conjectures of Habegger–Ostafe–Shparlinski [ HOS22 ] ) to persist for reducible polynomials (possibly with logarithmic factors). Our proofs for these two fully split polynomials are explicit and rely only on lattice reduction within suitable parabolic subgroups, making the geometric source of the exponent transparent. We also isolate the contributions of different Jordan types in the unipotent case. Refinements to full asymptotics (with leading constants expressed as products of local densities) will be addressed elsewhere. The first part of the paper illustrates the methods with n = 3 n=3

Introduction

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be monic of degree
3
with
p(0)=(-1)^{3}=-1
or
1
according to determinant
1
constraint; here we fix
p
so that there exist matrices
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\chi_{A}(x)=p(x)
. For
H\geq 1
define the counting function

N_{p}(H)\coloneqq\#\bigl\{A\in\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}):\chi_{A}=p,\;\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq H\bigr\},\qquad\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{i,j}|a_{ij}|.

We treat the completely split polynomials.

p_{1}(x)=(x+1)^{2}(x-1),\qquad p_{2}(x)=(x-1)^{3}.

We prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For

p(x)\in{(x+1)^{2}(x-1),(x-1)^{3}}

there is a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

N_{p}(H)\leq C_{p}H^{3}\qquad(H\geq 2).

In particular
N_{p}(H)=O(H^{3})
, and the exponent
3=n(n-1)/2
(
n=3
) is best possible.

Remark 1.2
.

(1) For irreducible
p
the exponent
3
follows from the general homogeneous dynamics results of Eskin–Mozes–Shah
[
EMS96
]
and Shah
[
Sha00
]
. Our contribution here is to give a direct elementary argument in the maximally reducible, non-semisimple cases.

(2) Methods extend (with heavier notation) to greater
n
when the characteristic polynomial has a repeated linear factor of multiplicity
2
or a single Jordan block for eigenvalue
1
; the combinatorics of positive roots again enforce the exponent
n(n-1)/2
.

(3) For
n=2
, Rivin,
[
Riv20
]
, obtained linear growth for parabolic elements, consistent with
n(n-1)/2=1
. Our proofs may be viewed as a
3\times 3
analogue with an explicit reduction.

The overall strategy is parallel in both cases:

(i)

Place the matrix into an upper triangular (or upper unitriangular)
partial normal form
through integral conjugation within a parabolic subgroup.

(ii)

Parameterize the remaining matrices by the action of a complementary unipotent subgroup whose integer points act freely on the residual parameters (up to finite stabilizers).

(iii)

Translate the height bound into inequalities on the parameters; solve these inequalities to obtain permissible ranges.

(iv)

Sum over parameter space; obtain overall
O(H^{3})
.

The cubic exponent corresponds to three essentially free integer parameters of size
\asymp H
after the reduction; extra parameters are either bounded or impose shrinking domains compensating for their multiplicity.

2.1
Upper triangular reduction

Lemma 2.1

(Upper triangular form)
.

Every
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\chi_{A}(x)=(x+1)^{2}(x-1)
is integrally conjugate to an upper triangular matrix

T(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&a&b\\
0&-1&c\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover,
a=0
iff

is diagonalizable over
\mathbb{Q}
(i.e. has two distinct eigenvectors for eigenvalue
-1
), and

corresponds to a size-
2
Jordan block at

.

Proof.

Choose a nonzero vector
v_{1}
in the
(-1)
-eigenspace (which is nontrivial). If the algebraic multiplicity of
-1
is
2
and geometric multiplicity
1
(non-diagonalizable case), extend
v_{1}
to a Jordan chain
v_{1},v_{2}
with
(A+I)v_{2}=v_{1}
. If diagonalizable, choose linearly independent eigenvectors
v_{1},v_{2}
. Complete to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
where
v_{3}
is an eigenvector for
+1
. A standard lattice basis refinement (Hermite normal form adjustment) replaces
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
by an integral basis producing an upper triangular representative. Conjugating by the corresponding unimodular matrix yields the asserted form. The form of the
(1,2)
entry encodes the nilpotent part inside the
(-1)
-primary component.
∎

2.2
Action of lower unipotent conjugation

Let

L(u_{21},u_{31},u_{32})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
u_{21}&1&0\\
u_{31}&u_{32}&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}).

Consider the conjugates
B=LT(a,b,c)L^{-1}
. We track the dependence of entries of
B
on the parameters.

Lemma 2.2

(Polynomial dependence)
.

Each entry of
B
is an integral polynomial of total degree at most
2
in the six integer variables
(a,b,c,u_{21},u_{31},u_{32})
. Moreover there exist explicit linear forms
\Lambda_{ij}
such that

B_{ij}=\Lambda_{ij}(a,b,c,u_{21},u_{31},u_{32})+Q_{ij},

(1)

where each
Q_{ij}
is a (possibly zero) integer linear combination of monomials of the form
au_{kl}
,
bu_{kl}
,
cu_{kl}
, or
u_{kl}u_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Write
T=D+N
with
D=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,1)
and strictly upper triangular
N
. Conjugation by
L
acts by
\mathrm{Ad}(L)
; since
L
is unipotent lower triangular,
\mathrm{Ad}(L)
is a finite sum in the lower nilpotent Lie algebra. Explicit matrix multiplication yields the stated degree bound; no term of degree
>2
can appear because each commutator raises (or lowers) position strictly and a second commutator vanishes in
3\times 3
. Direct expansion (omitted for brevity here, can be inserted in an Appendix) gives the precise polynomials.
∎

We use only structural consequences of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3

(Height inequalities)
.

There are absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that: if
\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq H
then

|a|\leq H,\qquad|b|\leq H+C_{1}(|a|+|c|)(|u_{21}|+|u_{31}|+|u_{32}|),\qquad|c|\leq H,

and

|u_{21}|,|u_{31}|,|u_{32}|\leq C_{2}\frac{H}{1+|a|+|c|}.

Proof.

The first inequalities follow immediately from
B_{12}
,
B_{23}
,
B_{13}
, each of which contains
a,b,c
with coefficients
\pm 1
plus lower-order terms. Solving these linear inequalities for the
u
-variables proceeds by isolating them in the expressions for the lower entries
B_{21},B_{31},B_{32}
, each linear in
u_{21},u_{31},u_{32}
with coefficients
\pm 1
plus terms involving
a,b,c
multiplied by other
u
’s. A simple induction (treat
u_{21}
first from
B_{21}
, then substitute into
B_{31},B_{32}
) bounds each
|u_{ij}|
by a constant multiple of
H/(1+|a|+|c|)
; otherwise one of the lower entries would exceed
H
in absolute value. Details are straightforward bookkeeping; constants
C_{1},C_{2}
absorb finite combinatorial factors.
∎

2.3
Counting

Define

\displaystyle S(H)=\{(a,b,c,u_{21},u_{31},u_{32})\in\mathbb{Z}^{6}:\left\lVert LT(a,b,c)L^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq H,\;

(2)

\displaystyle L=L(u_{21},u_{31},u_{32})\}.

(3)

Each matrix with characteristic polynomial

Proposition 2.4

(Mixed polynomial bound)
.

N_{(x+1)^{2}(x-1)}(H)\ll H^{3}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.3
, given
a,c
with
|a|,|c|\leq H
the admissible
u
-box has side lengths
\ll H/(1+|a|+|c|)
in each of three independent integer directions. Thus the number of integer
u

\ll\bigl(H/(1+|a|+|c|)\bigr)^{3}
. The parameter
b
is then determined up to
O\bigl((|a|+|c|)H/(1+|a|+|c|)\bigr)=O(H)
possibilities (crude, but sufficient). Hence

N_{(x+1)^{2}(x-1)}(H)\ll\sum_{|a|,|c|\leq H}H\left(\frac{H}{1+|a|+|c|}\right)^{3}.

(4)

Bounding
1+|a|+|c|\geq 1+\max(|a|,|c|)
and using symmetry reduces to

\ll H^{4}\sum_{m=0}^{H}\frac{m+1}{(1+m)^{3}}\ll H^{4}\sum_{m=1}^{H}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\ll H^{4}.

(5)

This naive bound is
O(H^{4})
; we refine it by observing we overcounted
b
: in fact
b
itself must satisfy
|b|\leq H
, independent of
a,c,u
(since
B_{13}
contains
b
with coefficient
1
plus smaller terms).

Refining the
b
–count.

For fixed
(a,c,u_{21},u_{31},u_{32})
we have

B_{12}=a-b\,u_{32}.

If
u_{32}=0
, then
B_{12}=a
and any
|b|\leq H
is admissible, giving a factor
\ll H
.
If
u_{32}\neq 0
, the constraint
|a-bu_{32}|\leq H
implies

|b|\leq\frac{|a|+H}{|u_{32}|}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\#\{b\}\ \ll\ \frac{H}{|u_{32}|}.

From Lemma
2.3
we already know
|u_{32}|\ll H/(1+|a|+|c|)
, hence

\frac{H}{|u_{32}|}\ \ll\ 1+|a|+|c|.

Thus the crude factor
H
is replaced by

\min\!\left\{H,\frac{H}{|u_{32}|}\right\}=O\!\left(\frac{H}{1+|a|+|c|}\right)

whenever
u_{32}\neq 0
. Summing over
u_{21},u_{31},u_{32}
(whose ranges are

\ll H/(1+|a|+|c|)
) and then over
a,c
gives

\sum_{|a|,|c|\leq H}\left(\frac{H}{1+|a|+|c|}\right)^{3}=O(H^{3}),

so the total remains
O(H^{3})
.

Hence the crude factor
H
for
b
is replaced by

O\!\left(H/(1+|a|+|c|)\right)
. Therefore

N_{(x+1)^{2}(x-1)}(H)\ll\sum_{|a|,|c|\leq H}\left(\frac{H}{1+|a|+|c|}\right)^{3}.

Now write
m=1+|a|+|c|
; the number of pairs
(a,c)
with given

\ll m

N_{(x+1)^{2}(x-1)}(H)\ll\sum_{m\leq 2H+2}m\left(\frac{H}{m}\right)^{3}=H^{3}\sum_{m\leq 2H+2}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\ll H^{3}.

This yields the desired

∎

General Dimension: Fully Split Two-Eigenvalue Polynomials

We extend the
n=3
upper bounds to arbitrary
n
for fully split characteristic polynomials

p(x)=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b},\qquad a+b=n,\quad(-1)^{b}=1.

(We order all
+1
eigenvalues first.)

6.1
Upper block normal form

Determinant parity.
The determinant constraint forces
(-1)^{b}=1
, i.e.
b
even; we assume this throughout.

Let
A\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\chi_{A}=p
. Distinct eigenvalues
\pm 1
imply the generalized eigenspaces

V_{+}=\ker(A-I)^{n},\quad V_{-}=\ker(A+I)^{n}

are complementary and defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. After an integral change of basis we may write

A=\begin{pmatrix}I_{a}+X&B\\
0&-I_{b}+Y\end{pmatrix},\quad X\in\mathfrak{n}_{a}(\mathbb{Z}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{b}(\mathbb{Z}),\ B\in M_{a\times b}(\mathbb{Z}),

with
\mathfrak{n}_{m}
the strictly upper triangular integer matrices.

Lemma 6.1

(Block-triangular determinant)
.

For every choice of
X,Y,B
as above we have
\chi_{A}(x)=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}
.

Proof.

A
is block upper triangular with diagonal blocks
I_{a}+X
and
-I_{b}+Y
. As
X,Y
are nilpotent,
(I_{a}+X)
(resp.
(-I_{b}+Y)
) is unipotent with all eigenvalues
+1
(resp.
-1
). Thus

\det(xI_{n}-A)=\det(xI_{a}-(I_{a}+X))\det(xI_{b}-(-I_{b}+Y))=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}.

∎

6.2
Parameter count

The free upper parameters are

\frac{a(a-1)}{2}+ab+\frac{b(b-1)}{2}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}.

Each has
O(H)
choices under
\|A\|_{\infty}\leq H
.

Theorem 6.2

(General upper bound)
.

Let
p(x)=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}
with
a+b=n
and
b
even. Then

N_{p}(H)=O\!\left(H^{n(n-1)/2}\right),

with implied constant depending only on
n
.

Proof.

Combine the normal form with the parameter count; the number of admissible upper triangular representatives is
\ll H^{n(n-1)/2}
; conjugation cannot increase the count.
∎

6.3
All-unipotent polynomial
p(x)=(x-1)^{n}

Theorem 6.3

(Unipotent upper and lower bounds)
.

Let
p(x)=(x-1)^{n}
. Then

N_{p}(H)=\#\{A\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}):\chi_{A}=(x-1)^{n},\ \|A\|_{\infty}\leq H\}=\Theta\!\left(H^{\binom{n}{2}}\right).

That is, there exist constants
c_{n},C_{n}>0
such that

c_{n}H^{\binom{n}{2}}\leq N_{p}(H)\leq C_{n}H^{\binom{n}{2}}
for all
H\geq 1
.

Lemma 6.4

(Bounded Hermite conjugators)
.

For each
n
there exists
C_{n}>0
such that for every full-rank integer matrix
M\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
there is
U\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\|U\|_{\infty}\leq C_{n}
satisfying
UM=H
in Hermite normal form. Consequently any unipotent
A\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\|A\|_{\infty}\leq H
is conjugate by a
g
with
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq C_{n}
to an upper unitriangular
U
with
\|U\|_{\infty}\leq C_{n}H
.

Idea.

Apply the constructive Hermite reduction: successive column operations using elementary unimodular matrices with entries in
\{-1,0,1\}
(and bounded repetitions depending only on
n
) produce a primitive column in each step while keeping coefficients bounded. Inductively, growth of entries is controlled by a dimension-dependent constant.
∎

Proof.

Lower bound.
Counting the
(2H+1)^{\binom{n}{2}}
upper unitriangular matrices
U=I_{n}+U^{\prime}
with
\|U^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq H
gives
N_{p}(H)\gg H^{\binom{n}{2}}
.

Upper bound (Band reconstruction).
Write
A=I_{n}+N
with
N
nilpotent. Let
n_{i,j}
be the
(i,j)
–entry (
i<j
). These
\binom{n}{2}
integers determine
A
. We claim
|n_{i,j}|\ll_{n}H
. Proceed by induction on
d=j-i
. For
d=1
the bound is
|n_{i,i+1}|\leq H
. Suppose the claim for
d^{\prime}<d
. Entries of
(I+N)^{k}
(for
k\leq n
) are polynomials with integer coefficients (depending only on
n
) in
\{n_{i,j}:j-i\leq d\}
. Expanding

and comparing the

with other terms involving only smaller bands already

, yielding
|n_{i,i+d}|\ll_{n}H
.

Alternative upper bound (Height-controlled triangularization).
There exists
C_{n}>0
such that any unipotent
A
with
\|A\|_{\infty}\leq H
is conjugate by some

with

(2)

(3)

(4)

7
Jordan Partitions and Refined Upper Bounds

Fix
p(x)=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}
with
a+b=n
and
b
even. Inside each primary block (
+1
or
-1
) choose a Jordan partition

\lambda^{(+)}\vdash a,\qquad\lambda^{(-)}\vdash b.

Let
\mathcal{J}(\lambda^{(+)},\lambda^{(-)})
denote the set of
A\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\chi_{A}=p
whose Jordan normal form over
\mathbb{C}
(equivalently over
\mathbb{Q}
) has blocks of sizes prescribed by
\lambda^{(+)}
and
\lambda^{(-)}
on the respective primary components.

Lemma 7.1
.

In an upper block representative of
\mathcal{J}(\lambda^{(+)},\lambda^{(-)})
the number of
a priori
free integer parameters is still
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.

Proof.

Within a primary block of size
m
and partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{t})
one can conjugate (over
\mathbb{Z}
) so that each Jordan block has
1
’s on the super-diagonal and zeros elsewhere within the block. Inter-block upper entries (between distinct Jordan blocks for the
same
eigenvalue) remain arbitrary strictly upper entries; counting them gives
\sum_{i}\binom{\lambda_{i}}{2}
internal to blocks plus cross-block entries
\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=\binom{m}{2}-\sum_{i}\binom{\lambda_{i}}{2}
; total
\binom{m}{2}
. Thus each primary block contributes
\binom{a}{2}
or
\binom{b}{2}
regardless of partition. The rectangular
a\times b
block contributes
ab
further parameters. Summing yields
\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2}+ab=\binom{n}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

For any fixed pair of partitions
(\lambda^{(+)},\lambda^{(-)})
we have

\#\{A\in\mathcal{J}(\lambda^{(+)},\lambda^{(-)}):\|A\|_{\infty}\leq H\}=O\!\left(H^{n(n-1)/2}\right),

with an implied constant independent of the particular partitions.

Remark 7.3
.

Thus no saving in the exponent is achieved by restricting to a refined Jordan type; all partitions saturate the ambient exponent. Partition data only influences second-order (constant) factors.

8
Height Norms and Equivalence

Let
H_{\infty}(A)=\|A\|_{\infty}
and
H_{2}(A)=\|A\|_{2}
(Frobenius norm), and more generally for
1\leq p\leq\infty
let
H_{p}(A)=\|A\|_{p}
be the entrywise
\ell^{p}
norm. Since all norms on
\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}
are equivalent, for each
p
there exist constants
c_{p},C_{p}>0
with

c_{p}H_{\infty}(A)\leq H_{p}(A)\leq C_{p}H_{\infty}(A).

Consequently

\#\{A:\chi_{A}=p,\ H_{p}(A)\leq H\}\asymp\#\{A:\chi_{A}=p,\ H_{\infty}(A)\leq C^{\prime}_{p}H\},

and all counting exponents coincide. Thus our
O(H^{n(n-1)/2})
upper bounds are invariant under the choice of reasonable (Archimedean) height.

9
Algorithmic Reduction to Upper Block Form

We outline an explicit procedure that, given
A\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\chi_{A}=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}
, produces an integral conjugate in the upper block form of Section
6
.

(1)

Compute primary decomposition.
Using rational canonical form (via Smith normal form on the companion matrix ideals) find bases for
\ker(A-I)^{n}
and
\ker(A+I)^{n}
inside
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Saturate these sublattices to obtain primitive sublattices
L_{+},L_{-}
of ranks
a,b
.

(2)

Complete to a basis.
Extend a basis of
L_{+}
by a basis of
L_{-}
to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Relative to this basis
A
is block upper triangular with zero lower-left block.

(3)

Upper triangularization within blocks.
For each primary block apply the standard integral Gaussian elimination (conjugation by elementary unimodular matrices) to make the block upper triangular unipotent plus diagonal
\pm I
.

(4)

Normalize Jordan blocks.
Within each block of a fixed eigenvalue, successively clear entries below the super-diagonal to 0 and super-diagonal entries to
1
using unimodular conjugations supported in
2\times 2
pivots.

(5)

Output.
The resulting matrix has the claimed form with
X,Y
strictly upper triangular and arbitrary rectangular block
B
.

Remark 9.1
.

Steps (1)–(3) run in polynomial time in
\log\|A\|
(Smith normal form complexity). The normalization in (4) also runs in polynomial time since each clearing step decreases a Euclidean norm on the vector of off-super-diagonal entries.

Appendix A
Conjugation Formulae in Rank
3

We record explicit conjugation relations used in the
n=3
analysis. Let

A(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&a&b\\
0&-1&c\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad U(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&x&y\\
0&1&z\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad L(u,v,w)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
u&1&0\\
v&w&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then

U(x,y,z)A(a,b,c)U(x,y,z)^{-1}=A\bigl(a,\ b+cx-az+2y,\ c+2z\bigr),

L(u,v,w)A(a,b,c)L(u,v,w)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&a&b^{\prime}\\
0&-1&c^{\prime}\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

with

c^{\prime}=c+aw+2wz^{\prime},\qquad b^{\prime}=b+(\ast)u+(\ast)v+(\ast)w

for explicit integer linear forms (omitted for brevity since only linearity and bounded degree are needed in the counting arguments). Each entry is an integer polynomial of total degree at most
2
in the parameters. This bounds the growth of parameter ranges under conjugation used in Section
6.4
.

Explicit conjugation by
L(u,v,w)
.
With

A(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&a&b\\
0&-1&c\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad L(u,v,w)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
u&1&0\\
v&w&1\end{pmatrix},

one computes

LA(a,b,c)L^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}&B_{13}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}&B_{23}\\
B_{31}&B_{32}&B_{33}\end{pmatrix},

where

\displaystyle B_{11}

\displaystyle=-1-au+b(uw-v),

\displaystyle B_{12}

\displaystyle=a-bw,

\displaystyle B_{13}

\displaystyle=b,

\displaystyle B_{21}

\displaystyle=-au^{2}+(bu+c)(uw-v),

\displaystyle B_{22}

\displaystyle=-1+au-w(bu+c),

\displaystyle B_{23}

\displaystyle=bu+c,

\displaystyle B_{31}

\displaystyle=-auv+uw-v+(uw-v)(bv+cw+1),

\displaystyle B_{32}

\displaystyle=av-w(bv+cw+2),

\displaystyle B_{33}

\displaystyle=1+bv+cw.

(All expressions are integer polynomials of total degree
\leq 3
in the parameters; degrees
\leq 2
suffice for the counting arguments.)

Remark.
Conjugation by upper unipotents
U(x,y,z)
leaves
a
invariant and sends
(b,c)
to affine-linear expressions as shown earlier; combining
U
and
L
allows one to reach every matrix with the prescribed characteristic polynomial from an upper triangular representative.

Appendix B
Crude vs. Reduced Counts

The
crude parameter count
enumerates all upper block matrices

\begin{pmatrix}I_{a}+X&B\\
0&-I_{b}+Y\end{pmatrix},\qquad X\in\mathfrak{n}_{a}(\mathbb{Z}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{b}(\mathbb{Z}),\ B\in M_{a\times b}(\mathbb{Z}),

with
\|X\|_{\infty},\|Y\|_{\infty},\|B\|_{\infty}\leq H
. This yields
(2H+1)^{\binom{n}{2}}
possibilities.

Lemma B.1
.

The stabilizer in
SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
of a generic such upper block matrix with fixed
(a,b)
is finite.

Proof.

For a generic choice all super-diagonal entries in each Jordan chain are nonzero and all cross-block entries in
B
are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
; any integral conjugation preserving the matrix must normalize the full flag determined by the powers of
(A-I)
and
(A+I)
inside each primary component, hence lies in the corresponding diagonal torus. Determinant
1
and integrality force a finite set (torsion) of possibilities.
∎

Thus over-counting from multiple representations of the
same
matrix under the crude enumeration affects only the multiplicative constant.

Proposition B.2
.

Let
p(x)=(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}
with
a+b=n
. The number of
distinct
matrices of sup height
\leq H
equals

(2H+1)^{\binom{n}{2}}+O\bigl(H^{\binom{n}{2}-1}\bigr),

uniformly in
a,b
, where the implied constant depends only on
n
.

Idea of proof.

Partition the parameter box into congruence classes mod a large integer
q=q(H)
; generic classes have trivial stabilizer and contribute exactly one matrix per parameter choice. Boundary classes where algebraic coincidences force additional stabilizer lie on a finite union of proper coordinate subvarieties of codimension
\geq 1
, contributing
O(H^{\binom{n}{2}-1})
points.
∎

Remark B.3
.

A rigorous version would track the vanishing of products of entries that create extra symmetries; this is a standard “thin boundary” argument analogous to excluding singular elements in lattice counting problems.

Appendix C
Local Density Factorization of the Asymptotic Constant

Assuming the existence of an asymptotic

N_{p}(H)\sim C_{p}H^{\binom{n}{2}}\qquad(H\to\infty),

the constant
C_{p}
admits a factorization

C_{p}=\kappa_{\infty}\prod_{p\ \text{prime}}\kappa_{p},

where
\kappa_{\infty}
is the normalized Lebesgue (Archimedean) volume of the unit box in the space of strictly upper entries and cross-block entries (giving
(2)^{\binom{n}{2}}
before normalization), and
\kappa_{p}
is the
p
-adic density

\kappa_{p}=\lim_{k\to\infty}p^{-k\binom{n}{2}}\#\{A\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}):\chi_{A}\equiv(x-1)^{a}(x+1)^{b}\bmod p^{k}\}.

Remark C.1
.

For primes
p\\
neq2
the polynomial splits with distinct linear factors over
\\
mathbbZ_{p}
, and Hensel lifting gives
\kappa_{p}=1
(heuristically), up to the determinant-one constraint. At
p=2
extra congruence conditions can impose a correction factor; isolating
\kappa_{2}
requires a separate lifting analysis.

Remark C.2
.

A full derivation of the Euler product uses (i) strong approximation for the unipotent radical and (ii) independence (up to negligible error) of local conditions, paralleling the setup in Duke–Rudnick–Sarnak and Shah. We leave the explicit computation of
\kappa_{p}
to future work.

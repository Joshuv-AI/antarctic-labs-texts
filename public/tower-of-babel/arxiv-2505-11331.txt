Distance-based measures and Epsilon-measures for measurement-based quantum resources
Arindam Mitra, Sumit Mukherjee, Changhyoup Lee

Abstract
Quantum resource theories provide a structured and elegant framework for quantifying quantum resources. While state-based resource theories have been extensively studied, the measurement-based resource theories remain relatively underexplored. In practical scenarios where a quantum state or a set of measurements is only partially known, conventional resource measures often fall short in capturing the resource content. In such cases, ϵ \epsilon -measures offer a robust alternative, making them particularly valuable. In this work, we investigate the quantification of measurement-based resources using distance-based measures, followed by a detailed analysis of the mathematical properties of ϵ \epsilon -measures. We also extend our analysis by exploring the connections between ϵ \epsilon -measures and some key quantities relevant to resource manipulation tasks. Importantly, the analysis of resources based on sets of measurements are tedious compared to that of single measurements as the former allows more general transformations such as controlled implementation. Yet our framework applies not only to resources associated with individual measurements but also to those arising from sets of measurements. In short, our analysis is applicable to existing resource theories of measurements and has the potential to be useful for all resource theories of measurements that are yet to be developed.

Some of these resources are intrinsic properties of quantum states (e.g., entanglement, coherence etc.), while others are associated with quantum measurements (e.g., measurement incompatibility, measurement sharpness, measurement coherence etc). Among the measurement-based resources, some are the properties of individual measurements (e.g., measurement coherence and measurement sharpness), whereas others such as measurement incompatibility are defined for sets of measurements. These resources enable quantum advantages in a variety of information-theoretic tasks
Skrzypczyk et al. 2019
;
Uola et al. 2020
;
Carmeli et al. 2020

Quantum resource theories offer a principled and elegant framework for quantifying various quantum resources
Chitambar and Gour 2019
. While the resource theories of most of the state-based quantum resources have been extensively explored in the literature, only a limited number of measurement-based resource theories have been studied in comparable detail
Baek et al. 2020
;
Buscemi et al. 2020
;
Buscemi et al. 2024
;
Guff et al. 2021
. To the best of our knowledge, only the resource theories of measurement incompatibility, measurement coherence, and measurement sharpness have received significant attention. Although the framework we develop in this work is general, we focus our analysis primarily on those three measurement-based resource theories.

In practice, usually, neither quantum states nor quantum measurements are perfectly known. Both the prepared quantum states as well as the implementable measurement devices often encounter some noise. Therefore, in the scenarios where the states and the measurements are partially known, it is difficult to perfectly estimate the resource content in those. In such cases,
\epsilon
-measures can be used as a resource quantifier. The
\epsilon
-measures for quantum state-based resources have been studied in the Refs.
Mora et al. 2008
;
Xi and Yuwen 2019
;
Luo et al. 2024
. However, to the best of our knowledge, while specific distance-based resource measure for measurement based resources have been studied in
Tendick et al. 2023
, the
\epsilon
-measures for measurement-based resources have not been studied in detail. In this work, our
motivation
is to study the properties of the generic distance based-measure and
\epsilon
-measures for quantum measurement-based resources.

In this work, we provide a systematic methodology for quantifying measurement-based quantum resources through generic distance-based measures satisfying a few properties. This framework provides a general approach to evaluate how resouceful a given measurement or a set of measurements is according to a chosen distance-based resource measure. Following this, we study the concept of

\epsilon-
measure tailored for measurement-based resource theories and undertake a detailed analysis of their mathematical properties. In addition, we investigate the relationship between these
\epsilon-
measures and several key operational quantities in resource theory, including the resource dilution cost and the smooth regularization of resource measures.

It is important to note that the analysis developed for a generic resource theory of individual measurements cannot be directly extended to a generic resource theory of sets of measurements, as the latter permits more general transformations—such as controlled implementations. Nevertheless, our analysis is applicable not only to the quantification of resources contained in individual measurements, such as measurement coherence or measurement sharpness, but also to the quantification of resources that emerge from sets of measurements, such as measurement incompatibility.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\mathbf{Merg}_{Y}(M),\mathbf{Merg}_{Y}(N))=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Gamma_{\mathbf{Merg}_{Y}(M)},\Gamma_{\mathbf{Merg}_{Y}(N)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid\Gamma_{\mathbf{Merg}_{Y}(M)}-\Gamma_{\mathbf{Merg}_{Y}(N)}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid(\mathbbm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}\otimes Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{Y}})\circ[\Gamma_{M}-\Gamma_{N}]\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mid\mid\Gamma_{M}-\Gamma_{N}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(M,N).

(20)

That is, the distance
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(M,N)
is contractive under the marginalization operation.

In Heisenberg picture, a
\mathbf{SWAP}
channel acts on an arbitrary measurement
M_{B}\otimes M_{A}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A})
and provides the outcome
\mathbf{SWAP}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}}(M_{B}\otimes M_{A})=M_{A}\otimes M_{B}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
. Note that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{M_{A}\otimes M_{B}}=\mathbf{SWAP}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Omega_{M_{B}}}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Omega_{M_{A}}}}\circ\Gamma_{M_{B}\otimes M_{A}}\circ\mathbf{SWAP}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}}.

(21)

Consider arbitrary measurements
M_{CAB},N_{CAB}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
. Then from Eq. (
11
), and Eq. (
21
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(M_{CAB},N_{CAB})=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(M_{CBA},N_{CBA}),

(22)

where
M_{CBA}=\mathbbm{I}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\otimes\mathbf{SWAP}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{A}}(M_{CAB})
and
N_{CBA}=\mathbbm{I}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\otimes\mathbf{SWAP}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{A}}(N_{CAB})
.
In Section
III.1
, we will define the distance for two sets of measurements.

II.2
Aspects of a resource measure based on set of quantum measurements

Two major ingredients of a generic resource theory concerning a specific resource are the free objects and the free transformations (also known as free operations for the state-based resource theories). Free objects are the objects that do not contain that particular resource and free transformations are the transformations that transform a free object to another free object. The objects can be quantum states, quantum measurements, quantum channels etc. depending on a particular resource theory and similarly, transformations can be quantum channels, quantum super-channels etc depending on a particular resource theory. A resource theory is said to be
convex
if free objects and free transformations form convex sets.

Consider a resource theory with the set of free objects
\mathcal{F}
and the set of free transformations
\mathcal{O}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{O}
are compact. In the context of measurement-based resource theories, the objects are measurements or more generally sets of measurements and similarly, transformations are the transformations of one measurement to another measurement or transformations from one set of measurements to another set of measurements in Heisenberg picture.

In case of measurements-based resource theory, one can make the following assumptions-

A1.

If two objects
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are free if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}
is free.

A2.

If two transformations
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}
are free if and only if
\mathcal{W}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}
is free.

A3.

As in Heisenberg picture, identity channel does not transform the measurements ( and therefore, does not transform any free object), it is a free transformation.

A4

A free object remains a free object under the swapping of the Hilbert spaces.

A5.

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
is a free object for all
\mathcal{H}
.

A6.

Merginalization process is a free transformation.

The assumption A6 will not be not used everywhere in the manuscript. We will explicitly mention wherever we use the assumption [A6]. Merginalization is a free transformation for the resource theory of measurement incompatibility and the resource theory of measurement coherence. Now, it is known that the post-processing of outcomes is not free operations for the resource theory of measurement sharpness, in general
Mitra 2022
;
Buscemi et al. 2024
. However, merginalization is very special case of post-processing of outcomes that transforms an arbitrary measurement
M\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
which is a trivial object. Therefore, technically, the merginalization should be the free transformation for the the resource theory of measurement sharpness. But in Ref.
Buscemi et al. 2024
, only the outcome set preserving free transformations for the resource theory of measurement sharpness have been considered.

Now, the quantification of resources or the resource measures are integral part of any resource theory. A resource measure
\mathbbm{R}
should satisfy the following conditions-

R1.

(Non-negativity and faithfulness):
\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})\geq 0
for all objects
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{F}
.

R2.

(Monotonicity):
\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})\geq\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{M}))
for all objects
\mathcal{M}
and for all free transformations
\mathcal{W}
i.e., for all
\mathcal{W}\in\mathcal{O}
.

In addition to these necessary conditions, one more desirable condition is

R3.

(Convexity):
\mathbbm{R}(\sum^{n}_{i}p_{i}\mathcal{M}_{i})\leq\sum^{n}_{i}p_{i}\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{i})
for all sets of objects
\{\mathcal{M}_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
and all probability distributions
\{p_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
.

A resource measure that satisfies these three conditions is usually said to be a good measure to work with for any convex resource theory. From now on, we assume resource measures to be a continuous function. In our scenario, the objects are the sets of measurements and transformations are the physically realizable maps that transform a set of measurements to another set of measurements in Heisenberg picture.

Given a resource measure
\mathbbm{R}
, one can define a family of resource measures that depend on the parameter
\epsilon
. In the following, we write down the definition of such a measure.

Definition 1
.

For any resource measure
\mathbbm{R}
in any measurement-based resource theory, and for a generic distance
\mathbf{D}
, the corresponding
\epsilon
-inf-measure
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbf{D}}_{inf,\varepsilon}
of a set of measurements
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}\subset\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbf{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}\subset\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}),\\
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\leq\epsilon,\penalty\ |\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{N}|\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N}).

(23)

Note that as we restrict ourselves to finite dimensional Hilbert space, the set
\{\mathcal{B}\mid\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leq\epsilon\}
is convex and compact.
One of the goals of our work is to study the properties of
\epsilon
-measures of measurement-based resources.

III.1
Transformation of sets of measurements, sets of channels and a distance measure

As we already mentioned in Sec.

Consider a set of quantum channels
\mathcal{C}=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})\}
. Throughout the paper, we say that the set
\mathcal{C}
is equal to another set
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})\}
if and only if
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}
holds for all
i
s. In other words, we assume that all elements of the
\mathcal{C}
is rigidly marked by the index
i

\overline{\mathcal{V}}
can be written as

\displaystyle[\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C})]_{j}=\overline{\Theta}_{post}^{j}\circ(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}})\circ\overline{\Theta}_{pre}^{j},

(24)

where
[\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C})]_{j}
is the
j
th channel of the set
\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C})
,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}=\sum_{\overline{i}}\Lambda_{\overline{i}}\otimes\overline{\Phi}_{\overline{i}}
,
\overline{\Phi}_{\overline{i}}(.)=\ket{\overline{i}}\bra{\overline{i}}(.)\ket{\overline{i}}\bra{\overline{i}}
for all
\overline{i}
,
\overline{\Theta}_{pre}^{j}\in\mathscr{C}({\overline{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{I}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{R}}})
for all
j
,
\overline{\Theta}_{post}^{j}\in\mathscr{C}({\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{I}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{R}},\overline{\mathcal{K}}})
for all
j
,
\{\ket{\overline{i}}\}
is an orthonormal basis of Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{I}
. Note that
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
is the
controlled implementation
of the channels in the set
\mathcal{C}
. Clearly, if both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{V}[\mathcal{C}]
contain one quantum channel then (
24
) reduces to (
3
) as a special case.

Now, we consider a function of two sets of channels
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{i})\}^{n}_{i=1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{\Theta_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{i})\}^{n}_{i=1}
as

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})=\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Theta_{i}).

(25)

Clearly, if each
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
contain just one quantum channel then
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
coincides with
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}
. Now, we show that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
is a valid distance measure.

Lemma 1
.

For two sets of measurements
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
is a valid distance.

Proof.

Firstly, as the diamond distance
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}
is always positive-semidefinite for an arbitrary pair of quantum channels, from Eq. (
25
) we obtain
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})\geq 0
. Now, note that
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Theta_{i})\geq 0
for all
i
s. Therefore, if
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})=0
then from Eq. (
25
), we obtain that
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Theta_{i})=0
for all
i
s which implies
\Lambda_{i}=\Theta_{i}
for all
i
s or in other words
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Conversely, if
\Lambda_{i}=\Theta_{i}
for all
i
s then
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Theta_{i})=0
for all
i
s which implies
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})=0
.

Secondly, as
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Theta_{i})=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Theta_{i},\Lambda_{i})
for all
i
s,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})=\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\Diamond}(\mathcal{C}_{2},\mathcal{C}_{1})
.

Thirdly, consider another set of channels
\mathcal{C}_{3}=\{\Psi_{i}\}
now, suppose that in Eq. (
25
) supremum occurs for
i=i^{*}
then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i^{*}},\Theta_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i^{*}},\Psi_{i^{*}})+\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Psi_{i^{*}},\Theta_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Psi_{i})+\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Psi_{i},\Theta_{i})

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\Diamond}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{3})+\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\Diamond}(\mathcal{C}_{3},\mathcal{C}_{2}).

(26)

Hence,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies triangular inequality.
Therefore,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is a distance measure.
∎

The tensor product of two sets of channels
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\{\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{1})\}^{m}_{i=1}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\{\tilde{\Theta}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{2})\}^{n}_{j=1}
is defined as
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}:=\{\tilde{\Psi}_{ij}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j}\}
.
The convex combination of two sets of channels
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})\}^{n}_{i=1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{\Theta_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})\}^{n}_{i=1}
is defined as
p\mathcal{C}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}_{2}:=\{\Psi_{i}=p\Lambda_{i}+(1-p)\Theta_{i}\}
. We denote a process where a given set of channels
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(as an input) is probabilistically mixed with an arbitrary fixed set of channels
\mathcal{Q}=\{\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}\}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,\mathcal{Q}}[\mathcal{C}_{1}]:=p\mathcal{C}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{Q},

(27)

where
0\leq p\leq 1
is the probability.

Now, we start studying different mathematical properties of the distance
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proposition 1
.

\overline{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies the following relations

1.

\overline{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies joint convexity property. In other words,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(p\mathcal{C}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}_{2},p\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2})\leq p\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1})+(1-p)\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{2},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2})
.

2.

\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is jointly subadditive under tensor product. In other words,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2})\leq\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1})+\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2}).
If
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2})
, equality holds i.e.,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is invariant under the tensor product with same set of quantum channels.

Proof.

We use joint convexity of diamond distance and multiplicity of diamond norm to prove these statements.

1.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(p\mathcal{C}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}_{2},p\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2})=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(p\Lambda_{i^{*}}+(1-p)\Theta_{i^{*}},p\Lambda_{i^{*}}^{\prime}+(1-p)\Theta_{i^{*}}^{\prime})
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(p\mathcal{C}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}_{2},p\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1}+(1-p)\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(p\Lambda_{i^{*}}+(1-p)\Theta_{i^{*}},p\Lambda_{i^{*}}^{\prime}+(1-p)\Theta_{i^{*}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq p\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i^{*}},\Lambda_{i^{*}}^{\prime})+(1-p)\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Theta_{i^{*}},\Theta_{i^{*}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq p\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{i}^{\prime})+(1-p)\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Theta_{i},\Theta_{i}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=p\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})+(1-p)\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}),

(28)

where in the third line, we have used the joint convexity property of
\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}
given in Eq. (
7
).

2.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2})=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}},\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}},\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}},\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}})+\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}},\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})

\displaystyle=\mid\mid\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}+\mid\mid\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=\mid\mid(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})\otimes\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}+\mid\mid\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\otimes(\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}-\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=\mid\mid(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}.\mid\mid\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}+\mid\mid\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}.\mid\mid(\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}-\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=\mid\mid(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}+\mid\mid(\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}-\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})+\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Theta}_{j^{*}}-\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i})+\max_{j}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Theta}_{j}-\tilde{\Theta}^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1})+\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2}),

(29)

where sixth line, we have used the multiplicative property of diamond norm under tensor product (see Remark
3
) and in the seventh line, we have used the fact that the diamond norm of a quantum channel is
1
(see Remark
3
).

Now, suppose
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{2}
. Then From Eq. (
29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2})\leq\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}).

(30)

Now, let

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}},\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}.\mid\mid\Theta_{j}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i^{*}}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i^{*}})\otimes\Theta_{j}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{i,j}\mid\mid(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}\otimes\Theta_{j}-\tilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\Theta_{j})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}).

(31)

Hence,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2})=\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{1}).

(32)

∎

.

\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{P}_{p,\mathcal{Q}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\mathcal{P}_{p,\mathcal{Q}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))\leq\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})

Lemma 2
.

For arbitrary
\mathcal{C}_{CAB}=\{\Lambda_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})\}

Let
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{CAB},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{CAB})=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i^{*}},\Lambda^{\prime}_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{CAB},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{CAB})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i^{*}},\Lambda^{\prime}_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Theta_{i^{*}},\Theta^{\prime}_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Theta_{i},\Theta^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{CBA},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{CBA}),

(34)

where we have used Eq. (
11
) in the second inequality.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{CBA},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{CBA})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Theta_{i^{*}},\Theta^{\prime}_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i^{*}},\Lambda^{\prime}_{i^{*}})

\displaystyle\max_{i}\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}(\Lambda_{i},\Lambda^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{CAB},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{CAB}).

(35)

Now, we prove a crucial property of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
that will be implicitly used in later parts of our work.

Theorem 1
.

\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))\leq\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
for any
\overline{\mathcal{V}}
of the form given in Eq. (
24

\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is contractive under the transformations of the form given in Eq. (
24

Proof.

Suppose,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))=\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}([\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{1})]_{j^{*}},[\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))]_{j^{*}}).

(36)

Then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\Diamond}([\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{1})]_{j^{*}},[\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))]_{j^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid[\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{1})]_{j^{*}}-[\overline{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))]_{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid\overline{\Theta}_{post}^{j^{*}}\circ(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}})\circ\overline{\Theta}_{pre}^{j^{*}}-\overline{\Theta}_{post}^{j^{*}}\circ(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}})\circ\overline{\Theta}_{pre}^{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid\overline{\Theta}_{post}^{j^{*}}\circ(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}}-\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}})\circ\overline{\Theta}_{pre}^{j^{*}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mid\mid(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}}-\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}})\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}-\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{2}})\otimes\mathbbm{I_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\mid\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}-\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\mid\mid_{\Diamond}

III.2

Now, as an example, we show that from the distance measure

Consider a set of measurements
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})=\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}),

\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
with
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
being an arbitrary finite dimensional Hilbert space. We have used the suffix
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
to denote the Hilbert space the measurements act on. Throughout this subsection, we will stick to this particular notation.

Theorem 2
.

\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is a resource measure for a generic measurement-based resource theory.

Proof.

Let, for an arbitrary
\mathcal{H}_{B}
,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{B}):=

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}})

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}},\mathcal{N}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}),

(55)

where

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{H}_{B}

\mathcal{H}_{B}

\overline{\mathbbm{R}}>0

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}},\mathcal{H}_{C})\penalty\ \forall\mathcal{H}_{C}

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\ \overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})\geq

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}})

(62)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})=\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}).

(63)

∎

Proposition 3
.

\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\leq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})+\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
for any
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{M^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\}
. In other words,
\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is subadditive under tensor product.

Proof.

Let us consider,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})=

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}),

(64)

and

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})=

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}).

(65)

We denote the elements of

N_{i}

\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}=\{N_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C})\}
and the elements of
\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}

\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}=\{N^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D})\}
. For notational simplicity let us now denote
\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})+\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}})

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\otimes\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\otimes\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}})

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}},\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}})

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABCD}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABCD}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABCD}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABCD}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}),

(66)

where in the third line we have used the property [D2], in the fourth line we have made the substitution,

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}:=\{\tilde{N}_{ij}=\mathbbm{I}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\otimes\mathbf{SWAP}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{C}}\otimes\mathbbm{I}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{H}_{D}}[N_{i}\otimes N^{\prime}_{j}]\},

as well as used property [D4], in the fifth line we defined
\mathcal{K}_{ABCD}:=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}
is a free object).
Now, note that both
\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})
and
\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})
are positive real numbers and therefore,

\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})+\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})=\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{D}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})=\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}[\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})]
. Then we can write,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D})\penalty\ \forall\mathcal{H}_{C},\mathcal{H}_{D}

\displaystyle or,

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}[\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D})

\displaystyle or,

\displaystyle\penalty\ \inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{D}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D})

\displaystyle or,

\displaystyle\penalty\ \overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})+\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\geq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime}).

(68)

∎

Proposition 4
.

For a convex resource theory,
\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\leq p\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})+(1-p)\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
for any
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{M^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}
and any
0\leq p\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})=

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}),

(69)

and

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})=

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}).

(70)

Again let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}:=p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Then for an arbitrary
\mathcal{H}_{C}

\displaystyle p\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+(1-p)\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})+(1-p)\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(p\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}+(1-p)\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},

\displaystyle\hskip 102.43008ptp\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}+(1-p)\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

(71)

where in the third line, we have used the Property [D1], in the fourth line, we used the obvious fact that
\hat{\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}=p\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}+(1-p)\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}
, and we have used the fact that as the resource theory is convex, free objects form a convex set and therefore,
p\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}+(1-p)\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}
is free object.

Then

\displaystyle p\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+(1-p)\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\mathbbm{K}(p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}[p\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+(1-p)\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}p\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}(1-p)\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})+\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\geq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(p\mathcal{M}+(1-p)\mathcal{M}^{\prime}).

(72)

Where in the second line we have used the fact that

\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})+\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})=\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{D}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})=\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{D}}[\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})+\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})]
as both
\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{C})
and
\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{D})
are positive real numbers.
∎

Proposition 5
.

If marginalization is a free transformation (i.e., if assumption [A6] holds) for a given resource theory, then,
\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\geq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})
for any
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}
and any
\mathcal{N}=\{N_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
.

Proof.

For notational simplicity let us define
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{K}_{ABC}:=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
.

Again let

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})=

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}).

(73)

Then for an arbitrary
\mathcal{H}_{C}

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}},\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\mathbbm{Merg}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}),\mathbbm{Merg}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}}(\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}},\mathbbm{Merg}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}}(\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}}\mathbbm{D}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}).

(74)

where in the third line, we have used the Property [D5] and in the fourth line we have used the obvious fact that
\mathbbm{Merg}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}}(\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}})=\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}}
, and in the fifth line, we have used the fact that the marginalization is a free transformation (i.e., if assumption [A6] holds) and therefore,
\mathbbm{Merg}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}}(\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{K}_{ABC}})
is a free object.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})\geq\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C})\penalty\ \forall\mathcal{H}_{C}

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{C})\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\geq\inf_{\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}\mathbbm{K}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C})

\displaystyle or,\penalty\

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\geq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}).

(75)

∎

Corollary 3
.

If marginalization is a free transformation (i.e., if assumption [A6] holds) for a given resource theory, then, we have
\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})=\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M})
for any
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\}

.

As
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}
,
\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{N})=0
. Therefore, from Proposition
3
, we get,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})\leq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}).

(76)

Again from Proposition
5
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})\geq\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}).

(77)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})=\overline{\mathbbm{R}}(\mathcal{M}).

(78)

∎

Note that the Eq. (

) and Eq. (

and Corollary

Theorem 3

If
\mathbbm{R}

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}

\mathbbm{D}

Proof.

If
\mathbbm{R}

\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{W}[\mathcal{M}])\leq\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})

\mathcal{W}

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{N}^{*}

23

\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}^{*})\leq\epsilon
and so that we have,
\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}^{*}))\leq\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}^{*})\leq\epsilon
(by the assumption [D3]). We can then write,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}^{*}))

(79)

∎

Proposition 6
.

For any convex resource measure
\mathbbm{R}
, the
\epsilon
-measure
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}
is also a convex resource measure for any distance
\mathbbm{D}
that is jointly convex.

\mathcal{N}_{1}^{*}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*}

\mathbbm{D}

where the first inequality is due to the convexity of resource measure
\mathbbm{R}

For any resource measure
\mathbbm{R}

\mathcal{N}_{1}^{*}

\mathbbm{D}

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{1}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})

\mathcal{N}_{1}^{*}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*}

Proposition 8

Consider a generic distance
\mathbbm{D}
and a resource measure
\mathbbm{R}
such that two sets of measurements
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
have a distance
\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=t
. Then for

and a convex mixture

the following relation holds:

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\leq(1-p)[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{2})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{2})].

(86)

Proof.

From the joint convexity property of the distance
\mathbbm{D}
we can write,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{p})

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},(1-p)\mathcal{M}_{1}+p\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq p\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq p(\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})+\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})).

(87)

The first inequality is due to the joint convexity of
\mathbbm{D}

\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=t
and
\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})\leq\epsilon_{2}

\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})+\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})\leq(t+\epsilon_{2})

. We then finally have
\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{p})\leq\epsilon_{1}
, which further implies,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{S}_{p})

\displaystyle\leq(1-p)\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{1})+p\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle=(1-p)\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{1})+p\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{N}_{2}^{*}).

(88)

The second line comes due to the convexity of
\mathbbm{R}
(i.e., assumption [R3]). From the above relation we can finally write,

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\leq(1-p)[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{2})].

(89)

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4
.

The
\epsilon
-measure
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}
is i) continuous for any original convex resource measure
\mathbbm{R}
, and, ii) continuous functional of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

i) The continuity of the
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}
demands that if two sets of measurements
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are very close, i.e.,
\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})=t\rightarrow 0
then the corresponding
\epsilon
-measures are also very close, i.e.,
\lvert\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\rvert\rightarrow 0
. In order to prove this, we choose that, for
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon
and
p=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon+t}
. Then for
\mathcal{M}_{1}\neq\mathcal{M}_{2}
, from Eq. (
89
) we can write,

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\leq\frac{t}{\epsilon+t}[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2})].

(90)

The above equation also implies,

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\geq-\frac{t}{\epsilon+t}[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{2})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})].

(91)

Therefore, the following holds,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t}{\epsilon+t}\max\Big\{(\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{2})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})),

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt(\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2}))\Big\}.

(92)

This finally implies that
\lvert\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{2})\rvert\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

ii) In order to prove that the
\epsilon
-measure
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}
is continuous functional of
\epsilon
, we choose
\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{2}=\mathcal{M}
(i.e.,
t=0
) and
p=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{2}}
. Then for
\epsilon_{2}\geq\epsilon_{1}\geq 0
from Eq. (
89
) we can write,

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})\leq\frac{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{2}}[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})].

(93)

The above equation also implies,

\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})\geq-\frac{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{2}}[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})].

(94)

Therefore, we can immediately write,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{2}}\max\{[\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})],

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt[\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{M})]\}.

(95)

It is then easy to see from the above equation that if
\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0
, then
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{M})-\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow 0
. This concludes the proof.

III.4
One-shot dilution cost, smooth asymptotic resource measures and its connection to measurement-based
\epsilon
- measures

One important resource manipulation task is resource dilution. This refers to the process in which an element from a given set of reference resource objects is transformed into a specific target resource object by the transformation. For instance, in a measurement-based resource theory, resource dilution may involve transformation of a set
\mathcal{N}
of measurements—drawn from a reference set
\mathcal{Z}
by the free transformation
\mathcal{W}\in\mathcal{O}
into a desired target set of measurements
\mathcal{M}
where
\mathcal{O}
is the set of all free transformations. Clearly, here
\mathcal{N}
is an element of
\mathcal{Z}
. The figure of merit of such a task is characterized by what is called the one-shot dilution cost, given by,

\mathbbm{C}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}))\leq\epsilon\\
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W}\in\mathcal{O},\penalty\ |\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{N}|\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N}).

(96)

In connection with the one-shot dilution cost and the
\epsilon
-measure, we establish the following proposition.

Proposition 9
.

The resource delusion cost in a measurement based resource theory is lower bounded by the epsilon measures corresponding to the measurement-based concerned resource, i.e.,
\mathbbm{C}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})\geq\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})
for all
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

In order to establish the proposition we note that in Eq. (
96
),
\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}))\leq\epsilon
, and therefore,
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N})\subset\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H})
. Also, note that
\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N})\geq\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}))
as
\mathcal{W}
is a free transformation. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{C}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{M})=

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}))\leq\epsilon\\
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W}\in\mathcal{O},\penalty\ |\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{N}|\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}))\leq\epsilon\\
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W}\in\mathcal{O},\penalty\ |\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{N}|\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{N}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{D}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\leq\epsilon\\
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{H}),\penalty\ |\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}|\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{R}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}).

(97)

∎

It is then clear that
\epsilon
-measure of the measurement-based resources constitutes the lower bound for preparing a given set of measurement upto an error
\epsilon
by free operation. Therefore, for our case, this
\epsilon
-measure provides a guaranteed lower bound on how much resource is needed to approximately create a set of measurements showing its importance as a operational tool in one-shot resource dilution tasks.

The one-shot dilution cost is only relevant when considering single set of measurements. However, for more general scenarios one quantifies how much resource is needed per copy (or can be extracted) when manipulating many copies of a set of measurements. Asymptotic resource measures quantify the rate at which a measurement based quantum resource can be consumed to prepare many copies of a target set of measurements, or can be extracted from many copies of a given resourceful set of measurements. We define the lower and upper regularization of resource measures for the
\epsilon
- measure of measurement-based resources
\mathbbm{R}
as smooth regularization defined as,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}),

(98)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{u}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}).

(99)

We now describe one important result regarding the above smooth regularization of resource measures as follows:

Proposition 10
.

The smooth regularization of resource measures are also resource measures. In other words

\displaystyle\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{M})),

\displaystyle\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{u}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{u}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{M})).

(100)

and
\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{M})=0
and
\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{u}(\mathcal{M})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is free.

Proof.

From Eq. (
98
), we have,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{W}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{M})^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{M})),

(101)

where in the second line we have used the assumption [A2].
Therefore, it is clear that
\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}
is non-increasing under free transformations.

Now, from assumption [A1], we know that
A
is if and only if
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}
is free for an arbitrary
n
. Therefore, for an arbitrary
n
,
\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbbm{D}}_{inf,\epsilon}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is free. Therefore, from Eq. (
98
), we have
\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{l}(\mathcal{M})=0
if and only if
A
is free.

Hence, it constitutes a valid resource measure. Similarly, one can prove the same for upper regularization
\mathbbm{C}^{\infty}_{u}
.
∎

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have studied distance-based resource measures and

\epsilon-
measures within the framework of measurement-based resource theories. Specifically, we analyze the transformation of sets of measurements and quantum channels, define a suitable distance measure for these objects, and examine its mathematical properties. Building on this, we construct a distance-based resource measure based on a generic distance function satisfying certain natural properties. We further investigate key properties of the
\epsilon-
measures for measurement-based resources. In addition, we explore the one-shot dilution cost and smooth asymptotic resource measures, highlighting their connections to
\epsilon-
measures in the context of measurement-based resources.

It is important to note that our results are valid not only for resource theories where the resource concerns a single measurement (e.g., measurement coherence, and measurement sharpness), but also for the resource theories where the resource involves a set of measurements (e.g., measurement incompatibility). Furthermore, we tried to keep our analysis as general as possible. More specifically, in Sec.
III.2
, our analysis is based on a generic distance function
\mathbbm{D}
assumed to satisfy a set of natural properties, and in Sec.
III.3
, we consider
\mathbbm{R}
to be a generic resource measure for studying the properties of the
\epsilon-
measures.

As previously noted, measurement-based resource theories have received significantly less attention compared to their state-based counterparts, and to the best of our knowledge, the study of
\epsilon-
measures within this framework remains unexplored. Consequently, our work offers a broad scope and opens several promising avenues for future research. We outline a few of them here. First, an immediate direction is to identify examples of distance functions, beyond
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
that satisfies the properties [D1]-[D5]. Second, one can investigate the design of information-theoretic tasks whose performance can be directly quantified by resource measures derived from
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
. Third, it is crucial to explore how, in scenarios where a set of measurements is only partially known up to some accuracy
\epsilon
, the
\epsilon-
measures can be used to meaningfully quantify the performance in relevant information-theoretic tasks. Fourth, There exist nonconvex resources, namely, certain other layers of nonclassicality
Heinosaari 2016
;
Mitra 2021
relevant to sets of measurements beyond incompatibility—whose resource quantification would be worthwhile to explore in the future. Furthermore, since state-based coherence is known to serve as a resource in quantum heat engines
Aimet and Kwon 2023
, it will be interesting to investigate the potential applications of measurement-based coherence in quantum thermodynamics. This motivates further investigation.

Efficacy and safety of finerenone combined with sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors in diabetic kidney disease: protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Xiaoyue Wang, Yunfeng Yu, Yixin Xiang, Xiu Liu, Rong Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionDiabetic kidney disease (DKD) is the most common complication of diabetes. It is the leading cause of end-stage renal disease and an important risk factor for cardiovascular disease. The mechanism and clinical efficacy of the combination therapy of finerenone and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) for DKD have been confirmed, but there is a lack of a comprehensive systematic review of its efficacy and safety. This protocol outlines the methods and procedures for a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination therapy of finerenone and SGLT2i for DKD.Methods and analysisEight databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China Science and Technology Journal Database (VIP), China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), China Biology Medicine disc (CBM) and Wanfang Database) will be searched from the database inception to 31 December 2025. Additional sources like the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (WHO ICTRP) and ClinicalTrials.gov will also be searched. All randomised controlled clinical trials investigating Finerenone combined with SGLT2i for DKD treatment will be included. Data extraction will encompass study characteristics, participant demographics, intervention protocols, outcome measures and adverse events. Primary outcomes will focus on serum creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Cochrane Collaboration’s Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool will be employed to assess methodological quality. Study selection, data extraction and bias evaluation will be conducted independently by two reviewers. Meta-analyses will be performed using statistical software packages including RevMan V.5.4 and STATA V.16.0. Where quantitative synthesis is precluded, descriptive analyses will be undertaken. The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations framework will systematically evaluate the certainty of evidence for all primary and secondary outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThis study requires no ethical approval as all data are derived from publicly accessible databases. Introduction

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is one of the most serious complications of diabetes.1 According to estimates by the International Diabetes Federation, the number of people with diabetes will increase to 783 million by 2045, and approximately 30% of individuals with type 1 diabetes and 40% of those with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) are expected to develop DKD.1,3 DKD is not only a primary cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) but also a significant risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD).4 5 Currently, the main treatment strategies for DKD include controlling blood glucose and blood pressure, lipid management and renal protection. In recent years, ACE inhibitors (ACEIs) and angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) have become first-line therapies for DKD. They exert their effects by blocking the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), effectively reducing proteinuria and protecting renal function.6 However, traditional RAAS blockade therapies still face significant limitations in delaying DKD progression and reducing cardiovascular event risks.7 Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) are a novel class of antidiabetic drugs that lower blood glucose by reducing glucose reabsorption in the early proximal tubules of the kidney. They also provide renal protection by decreasing glomerular hyperfiltration and reducing the risk of cardiovascular events.8 9 Large-scale clinical trials have demonstrated that SGLT2i can effectively slow the progression of DKD, but their advancements in the prevention and treatment of DKD remain limited.10 Therefore, exploring effective adjunctive treatment strategies is key to improving the management of DKD.

Finerenone is a selective non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist that inhibits the binding of aldosterone and cortisol. It exerts anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects on the heart and kidneys, thereby delaying the progression of DKD and reducing the risk of cardiovascular events among patients with DKD.11 12 The finerenone in Reducing Kidney Failure and Disease Progression in DKD (FIDELIO-DKD) and Finerenone in Reducing Cardiovascular Mortality and Morbidity in DKD (FIGARO-DKD) trials demonstrated that finerenone could reduce the risk of ESRD and ESRD-related mortality, as well as decrease the incidences of myocardial infarction (MI), cerebrovascular accidents and heart failure in DKD subjects.13,15 The subsequent network meta-analyses (NMA) further discussed the value of finerenone in improving the prognosis of DKD. In patients with non-dialysis chronic kidney disease (CKD) and T2DM, finerenone offers additional benefits in lowering systolic blood pressure, although its effects on improving glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) levels are similar to those of SGLT2i.16 Another NMA demonstrated that both finerenone and SGLT2i can reduce the risks of major adverse cardiovascular events, heart failure hospitalisation, MI, CVD and renal failure in patients with T2DM, and they have comparable cardioprotective and renoprotective effects.17 Additionally, one study noted that in T2DM and CKD patients, finerenone can decrease the incidence of atrial fibrillation (AF), while SGLT2i may not have a significant benefit in reducing new-onset AF.18 These studies compared the effects of finerenone and SGLT2i on improving the prognosis of DKD, supporting the notion that finerenone has at least comparable clinical value to SGLT2i. However, they focused solely on the effects of finerenone and SGLT2i as monotherapies, lacking a systematic evaluation and meta-analysis of the combined effects of both medications.

In recent years, the combined use of finerenone and SGLT2i in DKD has received increasing discussion and attention. Studies suggest that the combination can act through distinct and complementary mechanisms impacting metabolic disturbances, haemodynamics and inflammatory responses.19 Compared with traditional RAAS blockade, combined therapy with finerenone and SGLT2i can significantly lower proteinuria and enhance cardiorenal protection, thereby improving survival rates in patients with DKD.20 The FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD and FIDELITY (combined FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trial sequential analysis) indicate that patients receiving a combination of SGLT2i and finerenone experience a greater reduction in urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and a lower incidence of cardiovascular and renal adverse events (AEs) compared with those treated with SGLT2i alone.21,23 This evidence preliminarily supports that the combination of finerenone and SGLT2i can further improve the prognosis of DKD. However, these findings are mainly limited to a single randomised controlled trial, lacking relevant systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

Therefore, a systematic review and meta-analysis of existing studies regarding this combination therapy is necessary to summarise and analyse the current data, assess potential benefits and risks, and provide evidence-based recommendations for clinical practice. This protocol outlines the methods and procedures for systematically evaluating and meta-analysing the efficacy and safety of combined finerenone and SGLT2i therapy for DKD, aiming to offer evidence-based guidance for clinical treatment. Publishing this protocol is particularly important given that combination therapy research is still in its early stage. Protocol publication enhances transparency, minimises reporting bias and allows peer feedback on the planned methodology before the study is completed. Moreover, it provides a standardised framework for future evidence synthesis as new trials on this emerging treatment become available.

Methods and analysis

This protocol outlines a systematic review and meta-analysis adhering to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-analysis Protocols (PRISMA) guidelines.24 The protocol for this study has been registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number: CRD420251068323). The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of finerenone combined with SGLT2i in the treatment of DKD, providing a comprehensive synthesis of current evidence.

Inclusion criteria

Participants

The patients met the diagnostic criteria of DKD defined by relevant domestic or foreign guidelines, regardless of gender, age, race or nationality.

Interventions

The experimental group will receive treatment with finerenone in combination with an SGLT2i, and the specific SGLT2i used will be the same as that in the control group.

Comparisons

The control group must meet one of the following conditions: receiving treatment with SGLT2i (not limited to specific medications) or SGLT2i plus placebo. Follow-up duration for both groups must be at least 48 weeks to adequately assess the long-term effects of the interventions on renal function and other relevant outcomes.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes: (a) serum creatinine (Scr), (b) estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and (c) UACR.

Secondary outcomes: (a) Other renal function indicators (blood urea nitrogen); (b) Glycaemic indicators (fasting blood glucose, 2-hour postprandial blood glucose, HbA1c, serum insulin, c peptide (C-peptide), Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance); (c) Lipid indicators (triglycerides, total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol) and (d) blood pressure.

Safety outcomes: (a) hyperkalemia, (b) acute kidney injury, (c) cardiovascular events (d) infections and (e) drug discontinuation due to AE or serious AEs.

Study design

Only randomised controlled trials published in peer-reviewed journals will be included, regardless of whether blinding or allocation concealment was employed.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Studies involving kidney damage caused by diseases other than diabetes, or those with comorbid severe kidney diseases (such as ESRD on dialysis, acute kidney injury or post-kidney transplant); (2) Studies where the control group does not clearly report the drug dosage, treatment duration or specific regimen for combination therapy; (3) Studies that do not report at least one primary outcome; Outcome data that cannot be extracted or converted (such as providing only graphical data without numerical values, not reporting SD) and (4) Studies lacking sufficient data or with incomplete data; those for which full text or key information cannot be obtained.

Literature search strategy

A comprehensive search strategy will be designed for each electronic database, integrating Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords. The core search terms will include diabetic kidney disease, finerenone, sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors and the specific drugs belonging to the category of SGLT2i. The search query is shown in table 1, and the detailed search strategy for each database is shown in online supplemental table S1. The search will be carried out from database inception to 31 December 2025. The following databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China Science and Technology Journal Database (VIP), China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), China Biology Medicine disc (CBM), and Wanfang Database. In addition to the above-mentioned database searches, the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (WHO ICTRP) and ClinicalTrials.gov will be searched to identify ongoing relevant trials and randomised controlled trials related to DKD. Moreover, manual searches of reference lists from relevant reviews and included articles will be performed to uncover additional potentially eligible studies. For non-English studies retrieved, translation will be carried out to ensure comprehensive data extraction and analysis.

Search strategy

Study selection process

All retrieved records will be imported into EndNote V.X9 or similar reference management software for initial organisation and screening. The first step will involve the automatic removal of duplicate entries through EndNote’s built-in duplicate detection feature. Following this automatic deduplication, a manual verification will be conducted to ensure that all duplicates are accurately identified and removed. After deduplication, two independent reviewers will screen the remaining records by examining titles and abstracts according to the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Studies that are clearly irrelevant or do not meet the criteria will be excluded at this stage. For articles flagged as potentially eligible, full texts will then be retrieved and assessed independently by both reviewers to determine their final inclusion status. During this full-text review process, any discrepancies or disagreements regarding eligibility will be discussed between the reviewers. If consensus cannot be reached, a third reviewer will be consulted to make the final decision. A PRISMA flow diagram will be used to document the study selection process (figure 1).

Data extraction

Two reviewers will independently extract the relevant data and input it into an Excel spreadsheet. Extracted data will include study characteristics (author, year, country, design, sample size), participant demographics (age, sex, baseline eGFR, diabetes duration), intervention details (finerenone dose, SGLT2i type and dose, comparator regimen, duration), outcome measures (primary and secondary outcomes with time points) and AE rates. To ensure data quality, a third reviewer will conduct a cross-check of the extracted data. Any discrepancies will be discussed and resolved by a third party. When necessary, authors will be contacted for missing or unclear data.

Risk of bias assessment

A rigorous risk of bias assessment will be conducted using the Cochrane Collaboration’s Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool. This tool assesses the risk of bias in five domains: randomisation process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcomes and selection of the reported result. After evaluating each domain, an overall risk of bias rating will be assigned to each study based on the individual domain assessments. The overall rating will be categorised as follows: (1) Low risk of bias: all domains are rated as low risk. (2) Some concerns: at least one domain is rated as having some concerns, but no domains are rated as high risk. (3) High risk of bias: at least one domain is rated as high risk. The risk of bias assessment will be conducted by two independent reviewers to enhance reliability and minimise subjective bias. Each reviewer will independently evaluate the studies and document their assessments. Any discrepancies in ratings will be resolved through discussion and consensus, or by consulting a third reviewer if necessary.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

To synthesise the data extracted from eligible studies, all analyses will be conducted by using statistical software such as RevMan V.5.4 and STATA V.16.0. Continuous outcomes, such as changes in eGFR, Scr, UACR or HbA1c, will be summarised using weighted mean differences (MD) when measurement scales are consistent across studies. In cases where different measurement scales or units are used, standardised MDs will be calculated to enable comparison. Dichotomous outcomes, such as the incidence of AEs, will be expressed as risk ratios with 95% CIs.

The degree of heterogeneity will be quantified using the I2 statistic. Prior to pooling data, heterogeneity among studies will be assessed using the χ² test (with a significance threshold set at p<0.1) and quantified through the I2 statistic. An I2 value of 0%–25% will be regarded as low heterogeneity, 25%–50% as moderate, 50%–75% as substantial and above 75% as considerable heterogeneity. However, interpretation of heterogeneity will not rely solely on I2 thresholds; clinical heterogeneity (such as differences in populations, interventions and outcome definitions) and the 95% CI will also be taken into account. Both fixed-effect and random-effects models will be applied to provide a comprehensive interpretation. When I2 is below 50% and clinical heterogeneity is minimal, the fixed-effect model will be prioritised; otherwise, the random-effects model will be preferred. The consistency of findings between the two models will be compared with assess the robustness of pooled estimates.

Given that combination therapy research is relatively recent, the number of eligible randomised controlled trials may be limited. If quantitative synthesis is not feasible due to insufficient data or high heterogeneity, a narrative synthesis will be performed to qualitatively summarise the findings, focusing on the direction, magnitude and consistency of treatment effects.

Heterogeneity investigation and analysis

Meta-regression analysis

Meta-regression analysis will be conducted to investigate the influence of study-level covariates on treatment outcomes, addressing potential sources of heterogeneity among studies. This analysis will evaluate the relationship between study-level covariates (such as baseline kidney function, treatment duration, dosage of finerenone and SGLT2i) and the effect sizes, to identify which variables significantly contribute to variations in efficacy and safety outcomes. The process will help explain heterogeneity and improve the interpretability of the pooled results.

Subgroup analyses

Subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore potential sources of heterogeneity and assess whether treatment effects vary across clinically relevant categories. The main factors for subgrouping include DKD stage, type of SGLT2i, finerenone dose, use of other medications, patient age and gender, presence of comorbidities and follow-up duration. For each subgroup, meta-analyses will be conducted to calculate pooled effect sizes, and interaction testing will be performed to assess whether differences between subgroups are statistically significant. To control for the increased risk of type I error due to multiple comparisons, Bonferroni correction will be applied. The adjusted significance level will be determined by dividing the conventional alpha (0.05) by the number of subgroup comparisons performed. The results of exploratory subgroup analyses will be interpreted cautiously and regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. If fewer than three studies are available for a given subgroup, analyses will be considered underpowered and summarised descriptively.

Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses

To assess the stability and robustness of the meta-analysis results, a leave-one-out approach will be deployed whereby each individual study is sequentially removed, and the pooled effect estimate recalculated. Significant variations in effect size after the removal of any specific study may indicate its disproportionate influence or potential bias. This process helps identify outliers or studies with high bias risk that could affect overall conclusions. The results of sensitivity analyses will be summarised in tabular or graphical formats and interpreted in conjunction with the primary findings.

Network meta-analysis

During the protocol design phase, a preliminary scoping review was conducted to evaluate the feasibility of performing a NMA comparing different SGLT2 inhibitors combined with finerenone. However, the available literature was found to be insufficient in both quantity and consistency to support a reliable NMA. Therefore, this protocol focuses solely on pairwise meta-analysis without conducting indirect treatment comparisons at this stage.

Individual patient data meta-analysis

Given the growing body of research on the efficacy and safety of finerenone combined with SGLT2 inhibitors for DKD, we plan to conduct an individual patient data meta-analysis (IPD-MA) if feasible. Corresponding authors of eligible trials will be contacted to obtain anonymised individual datasets, including demographic characteristics (age, sex), clinical history (type and duration of diabetes, comorbidities), key outcome measures (eGFR, UACR, Scr, HbA1c, blood glucose, blood lipids), treatment duration, concomitant medications, AEs and clinical endpoints.

All data collection will comply with relevant ethical and legal standards, and informed consent will be ensured where required to protect patient confidentiality. If complete IPD can be obtained from at least three trials, a formal IPD-MA will be initiated. During data preparation, datasets will be harmonised by standardising variable definitions and measurement units, identifying outliers and addressing missing values through multiple imputation.

A one-stage mixed-effects model will be used for analysis: linear mixed-effects models for continuous outcomes and logistic regression for binary outcomes. Random effects will account for between-study variability, while potential patient-level confounders (such as age, sex and baseline eGFR) will be adjusted for to minimise bias. Subgroup analyses will explore effect modifications by patient characteristics (such as age, diabetes type and baseline kidney function). Sensitivity analyses will assess the consistency between IPD and conventional meta-analysis results and evaluate the impact of missing data using simulation-based methods.

Although IPD-MA offers deeper insights into treatment effect heterogeneity, its implementation depends on data accessibility. If sufficient IPD cannot be obtained, the influence of key covariates will instead be explored using conventional aggregate data meta-analysis.

Publication bias

Assessment of publication bias will be systematically conducted to evaluate the presence of small-study or selective reporting effects. When the number of included studies exceeds ten, funnel plots will be generated by plotting effect sizes against their standard errors to visually inspect for asymmetry, which may indicate publication bias. Egger’s regression test will be used for quantitative assessment, with a p<0.05 suggesting potential bias.

Given the relatively new nature of combination therapy research, if fewer than 10 studies are available, alternative approaches such as contour-enhanced funnel plots and the trim-and-fill method will be considered to explore potential publication bias. These methods can provide additional insight into the influence of unpublished studies even when statistical power is limited. Any detected asymmetry or bias will be interpreted cautiously, considering alternative explanations such as heterogeneity or small-study effects.

Certainty of evidence

The overall certainty of the evidence for each primary and secondary outcome will be systematically evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework. The GRADE methodology considers five key domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. (1) Risk of bias will be appraised based on the RoB 2 assessments, with studies rated as having low, some concerns or high risk influencing the certainty level accordingly. (2) Inconsistency will be evaluated by examining heterogeneity statistics such as I² and the overlap of confidence intervals across studies; substantial heterogeneity without plausible explanation may lead to downgrading the evidence. (3) Indirectness pertains to differences between the studied populations, interventions, comparators or outcomes and the clinical question of interest; evidence from studies that do not directly address the review question will be downgraded. (4) Imprecision will be assessed by inspecting the width of confidence intervals and the total sample size; wide intervals or small sample sizes increase uncertainty and may reduce certainty ratings. (5) Publication bias will be considered through funnel plots and Egger’s test, especially if the number of studies exceeds ten; signs of bias will warrant downgrading the evidence certainty. (6) Where appropriate, upgrading factors such as large effect sizes or dose-response relationships will also be considered.

Based on these evaluations, each outcome will be rated as high, moderate, low or very low quality. These grades reflect the confidence that the true effect is close to the estimated effect and guide clinicians in interpreting the robustness of the findings. To ensure transparency, evidence profiles will be developed for each outcome, detailing the ratings across domains and providing summaries that facilitate understanding of the evidence strength, limitations and implications for clinical decision-making.

Discussion

DKD has become a serious global health issue and is the leading cause of ESRD and CVD. Currently, ACEIs and ARBs are first-line therapies for DKD, but they often lead to elevations in serum potassium and creatinine levels, increasing the risk of AEs and do not offer additional benefits in delaying DKD progression.7 25 Studies indicate that combined use of finerenone with SGLT2i can improve proteinuria, blood pressure and Scr levels, inhibit renal and cardiac fibrosis, reduce mortality and decrease the risk of hyperkalaemia caused by finerenone.26 27 However, the overall efficacy and safety of the combination therapy of finerenone and SGLT2i remain insufficiently evaluated, with a lack of comprehensive systematic review and conclusive clinical evidence. Compared with directly publishing the results of a meta-analysis, this research protocol can enhance the transparency and reproducibility of the study, and reduce bias and selective reporting. Second, it can be subject to peer review to address methodological loopholes and improve the research quality. Finally, publishing the research design protocol can also increase the visibility of the study, thereby enhancing its influence. Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis aims to comprehensively assess the effectiveness and safety of combined finerenone and SGLT2i in the treatment of DKD, providing high-quality evidence to inform clinical decision-making.

It is anticipated that the combination of finerenone and SGLT2i may significantly improve renal function in DKD patients, specifically reflected in the improvement of tubular function and the reduction of urinary protein levels. Additionally, the combined treatment may have a positive impact on cardiovascular risk factors, including lowering blood pressure and improving lipid profiles. However, the results may be influenced by various factors, such as baseline patient characteristics, comorbidities and the duration of treatment. To explore these potential sources of heterogeneity and provide more detailed information, we will conduct subgroup analyses. In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we also plan to conduct an IPD-MA. If feasible, the IPD-MA will allow us to explore in greater depth the responses to the combined therapy of finerenone and SGLT2i across different patient populations. By analysing IPD, we can identify potential factors influencing treatment effects and more accurately evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination therapy. This approach will enhance the reliability of the results and provide more targeted guidance for clinical practice. If the results of this systematic review and meta-analysis confirm the efficacy and safety of finerenone combined with SGLT2i in the treatment of DKD, it will offer clinicians a new therapeutic option. On the contrary, if the evidence is insufficient or the findings are inconsistent, this will emphasise the need for further high-quality randomised controlled trials to clarify the role of combined finerenone and SGLT2i therapy in DKD management. Clinicians should base their treatment decisions on the overall evidence and take individual patient circumstances into account.

This systematic review and meta-analysis has some potential limitations. First, the quality of the included studies may vary, and some studies may have methodological flaws, such as inadequate randomisation, improper allocation concealment or unclear blinding, which could introduce bias and affect the credibility of the results. Second, publication bias is also a factor that needs to be considered. Studies with positive results are more likely to be published, while negative or inconclusive studies may remain unpublished, potentially leading to an overestimation of treatment effects. Finally, there may be certain limitations in the scope of the search. Although we will conduct a comprehensive search across multiple databases, some relevant studies may still be missed, especially those published in non-indexed journals or in languages other than English and Chinese. If finerenone combined with SGLT2i demonstrates efficacy in treating DKD, further research will be needed to optimise treatment protocols, such as optimising drug dosages, timing of administration and treatment duration, as well as conducting in-depth mechanistic studies to understand their synergistic effects.

Symplectic geometry of projective structures on surfaces with boundary
Ahmadreza Khazaeipoul, Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. For oriented surfaces Σ \Sigma with boundary, we consider the infinite-dimensional deformation space of projective structures on Σ \Sigma with nondegenerate boundary, up to isotopies fixing the boundary. We show that this space carries a natural symplectic structure, and is a Hamiltonian space for the symplectic groupoid integrating the Adler-Gelfand-Dikii-space of the boundary.



1.
Introduction

According to Adler
[
1
]
and Gelfand-Dikii
[
15
]
, the space
\mathcal{R}_{n}(S^{1})
of scalar differential operators on
S^{1}

(1)

L=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(t)\frac{d^{i}}{dt^{i}}

with principal symbol
a_{n}=1
and subprincipal symbol
a_{n-1}=0

is naturally an infinite-dimensional Poisson manifold. Drinfeld-Sokolov
[
12
]
gave a construction of this Poisson structure by Hamiltonian reduction from the space of
G=\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-connections on
S^{1}
; this correspondence later evolved into the Beilinson-Drinfeld theory of opers
[
5
]
.

For
n=2
, the space
\mathcal{R}_{2}(S^{1})
is the space of
Hill operators
, and the Poisson structure is affine-linear. It
is identified with the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau Poisson structure on the
dual of the Virasoro Lie algebra
\mathfrak{vir}(S^{1})
, restricted to the affine subspace at
level
1
(see e.g.
[
25
]
). For
n>2
, the
Adler-Gelfand-Dikii structure is not affine-linear, but one still has an associated Lie algebroid. An explicit integration to a symplectic groupoid

(2)

\mathcal{S}_{n}(S^{1})\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}_{n}(S^{1})

was obtained in
[
2
]
for
n=2
and
[
21
]
for
n>2
. To describe the underlying groupoid, note that a fundamental system of solutions of
L
gives a
nondegenerate quasi-periodic curve

(3)

\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to S^{n-1},\ \ \ \gamma(t+1)=c\cdot\gamma(t)

where
c\in G
is the monodromy of
\gamma
. Conversely, any such curve is realized by some differential operator (
1
).
Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}(S^{1})
denote the space of all nondegenerate quasi-periodic curves
and let
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{[2]}(S^{1})
denote pairs of such curves that are related by isomonodromic
deformation. The latter has a pair groupoid structure,
and the groupoid (
2
) is realized as its quotient by
G
,

(4)

\mathcal{S}_{n}(S^{1})=\mathcal{D}_{n}^{[2]}(S^{1})/G\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}_{n}(S^{1})=\mathcal{D}_{n}(S^{1})/G.

In
[
3
]
, it was shown that for a compact oriented surface
\Sigma
with boundary,
the infinite-dimensional Teichmüller space

(5)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)/\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)

is a Hamiltonian Virasoro space at level
1
, with one copy of
\mathfrak{vir}(S^{1})
for each boundary circle. Here the quotient is
by diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary and isotopic to the identity, and the hyperbolic structures are required to have

ideal boundary
: they exhibit the same boundary behaviour as for the boundary of the closed Poincaré disk. The moment map

\Phi\colon\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{R}_{2}(\partial\Sigma)

takes the class of such a hyperbolic structure to the induced Hill operator on the boundary.

The present article develops a similar picture for the case
n=3
. Instead of (
5
), we consider
the deformation space

(6)

\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)/\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma).

Here
\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)
is the space
of projective structures on
\Sigma
with
nondegenerate boundary
, meaning that the restriction of
any projective chart to the boundary is a nondegenerate curve. By construction, the deformation space comes with a map to the Adler-Gelfand-Dikii space

(7)

\Psi\colon\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{R}_{3}(\partial\Sigma).

For the case without boundary
\partial\Sigma=\emptyset
, there is a rich and well-developed theory of the deformation space, and in particular of its subspace
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
of
convex
projective structures. Goldman and Choi
[
11
]
proved that for genus

\mathsf{g}\geq 2
, the space

c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
is the Hitchin component of the representation variety of

G=\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, realizing it as a
higher Teichmüller space

[
20
,
14
]
.
In particular,
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
is a symplectic manifold of dimension
(2\mathsf{g}-2)\dim G
. Earlier work of Goldman
[
17
]
gave a Fenchel-Nielsen type parametrization of these projective structures, leading to explicit Darboux coordinates. The
full deformation space
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
is well-understood as well
[
8
,
9
]
; it too has a natural symplectic structure
[
17
,
18
]
.

Our first result extends the construction of a symplectic structure to the case with boundary:

Theorem A:

For a compact, connected surface with non-empty boundary, the deformation space
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
of projective structures with nondegenerate boundary is naturally an infinite-dimensional symplectic manifold.

Our second result interprets the boundary restriction (
7
) as a moment map.
The statement is particularly satisfying for
the deformation space
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
of convex projective structures with positive hyperbolic boundary holonomies.

Theorem B:

There is a well-defined action
of the groupoid
\mathcal{S}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)
on
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
,
by variation of the boundary curves. This action is Hamiltonian, with moment
map given by (
7
).

On the full space
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
, one can consider
small
variations of the boundary curves.
The resulting local groupoid action is still compatible with the symplectic structures, but need not extend to a global action.

The constructions from
[
3
]
where motivated by recent work on JT gravity
[
30
,
34
,
40
]
, and notably by the concept of hyperbolic structures with wiggly boundary. As we shall see, the picture of ‘wiggly boundaries’ is equally prominent in the setting of projective structures.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we review the theory of nondegenerate quasi-periodic curves, their relation to the Adler-Gelfand-Dikii space, and Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction. The discussion will be coordinate-free, using abstract circles rather than
S^{1}
. The case of
n=3
, with positive hyperbolic monodromy, is described in some detail. Section
3
begins with an overview of the theory of projective structures on compact surfaces without boundary, and extends aspects of this theory to projective structures with nondegenerate boundary. As we shall explain, every convex projective
structure with nondegenerate boundary and positive hyperbolic monodromy is obtained by attaching half-annuli
to a convex projective structures with
geodesic
boundary; we may hence use Goldman’s theory to obtain an explicit Fenchel-Nielsen type description of
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
. Section
4
contains our construction of a symplectic structure on the space
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
. Similar to
[
3
]
, this symplectic 2-form is obtained by reduction of an Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure on a suitable space of connections. Section
5
establishes the moment map property. In Section
6
we calculate Hamiltonian flows corresponding to
‘Goldman twist’ within our framework.

2.
Nondegenerate curves and the Adler-Gelfand-Dikii space

Throughout, we denote by
\mathsf{C}
a compact, connected, oriented 1-manifold – in other words, a circle but without a preferred choice of coordinate.
Let
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
be a choice of universal cover, so that
\mathsf{C}=\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}/\mathbb{Z}
. The deck transformation corresponding to
1\in\mathbb{Z}
will be denoted
\kappa\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
(moving in the positive direction).

2.1.
Quasi-periodic curves

Let
G
be a Lie group and
X
a homogeneous space for
G
. A curve

\gamma\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to X

is called
quasi-periodic
if there exists an element
\mu(\gamma)\in G
, called its

monodromy
,
such that

\gamma(\kappa(t))=\mu(\gamma)\cdot\gamma(t)

for all
t
. The group
G
acts on the space of quasi-periodic curves by
(a\cdot\gamma)(t)=a\cdot\gamma(t)
,
with the monodromy transforming according to
\mu(a\cdot\gamma)=a\,\mu(\gamma)\,a^{-1}
.

We shall find it convenient to think of quasi-periodic curves in terms of connections and parallel transport.
Associated with any quasi-periodic curve is a triple

(Q,\theta,\tau)

of a principal
G
-bundle
Q\to\mathsf{C}
with a connection
\theta
and a
G
-equivariant map
\tau\colon Q\to X
.
Here

Q=(\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\times G)/\sim,\ \ \ \ (t,h)\sim(\kappa(t),\mu(\gamma)h),

with connection
\theta
induced from the trivial connection on
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\times G
, and

\tau\colon Q\to X,\ \ \tau([(t,h)])=h^{-1}\cdot\gamma(t).

Conversely, given such a triple, the choice of a
\theta
-horizontal lift
s\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to Q
gives quasi-periodic paths by composition,
\gamma=\tau\circ s
. As a simple application, we have:

Lemma 2.1

(Local sections of monodromy map)
.

Given a quasi-periodic path
\gamma\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to X
, there is an open neighborhood
U\subseteq G
of
\mu(\gamma)

and a smooth map
f\colon U\times\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to X
such that each
f(h,\cdot)
is quasi-periodic of monodromy
h
, and

f(\mu(\gamma),\cdot)=\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
(Q,\theta,\tau)
be the triple associated with
\gamma
. Fixing a base point
t_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
, we have

\gamma=\tau\circ s
with the unique horizontal lift
s\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to Q
taking
t_{0}
to
[(t_{0},e)]
.
Choose a smooth family
\theta_{h},\ h\in U
of connections, with
\theta_{g}=\theta
, such that the holonomy of
\theta_{h}
with respect to
[(t_{0},e)]
is given by
h
. There is a unique
\theta_{h}
-horizontal lift
s_{h}\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to Q
taking
t_{0}
to
[(t_{0},e)]
. Then

f(h,\cdot)=\tau\circ s_{h}
is the desired family of quasi-periodic paths.
∎

2.2.
Nondegenerate curves

We specialize to
G=\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
acting on
X=S^{n-1}
. We shall think of the sphere as a quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{n}-\{0\}
by positive scalars, and use
projective notation
(x_{1}\colon\cdots\colon x_{n})\in S^{n-1}
for the equivalence class of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
A quasi-periodic curve

(8)

\gamma\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to S^{n-1}

is (positive)
nondegenerate
if it lifts to a curve

\tilde{\gamma}\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}-\{0\}
with the property that
for any choice of oriented local coordinate
t

the derivatives

(9)

\tilde{\gamma}(t),\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t),\ldots,\tilde{\gamma}^{(n-1)}(t)

form an oriented basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This condition does not depend on the choice of coordinates or the choice of lift.
The space of nondegenerate quasi-periodic curves will be denoted
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
; the monodromy map
gives a
G
-equivariant map

(10)

\mu\colon\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})\to G.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{C}=S^{1}
, so
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}=\mathbb{R}
. For
c_{1}<\ldots<c_{n}
with
\sum_{i}c_{i}=0
, the curve

(11)

\gamma(t)=(e^{c_{1}t}:\cdots:e^{c_{n}t})

is positive nondegenerate, with monodromy the diagonal matrix
\operatorname{diag}(e^{c_{1}},\cdots,e^{c_{n}})
. Indeed,
taking
\tilde{\gamma}(t)=(e^{c_{1}t},\cdots,e^{c_{n}t})
,
the Wronskian determinant is given by
\det(\widetilde{\gamma},\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\ldots)=\prod_{i>j}(c_{i}-c_{j})>0
.

.

(a)

We require (
9
) to be
oriented
to ensure that the space
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
is connected.

(b)

Let
\operatorname{Fl}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the manifold of oriented flags in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
: sequences of oriented subspaces

E_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim E_{i}=i
(using the standard orientation for
i=0,n
). By Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization,
\operatorname{Fl}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cong\operatorname{SO}(n)
.
A nondegenerate curve has a canonical lift

(12)

where
\widehat{\gamma}(t)
is the oriented flag whose

by the derivatives (

.

(c)

By the general discussion from Section

\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
determines a
triple
(Q,\theta,\tau)
of a principal
G
-bundle with a connection and equivariant map
\tau\colon Q\to S^{n-1}
. The lift
\widehat{\gamma}

(13)

Let
Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})=\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})/\sim

be the set of components of the fibers of (
10
): that is, paths up to isomonodromic deformations.
The quotient map will be denoted

(14)

q\colon\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})\to Y_{n}(\mathsf{C}).

Using Lemma
2.1
to define local charts, we see that

Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})
is a finite-dimensional manifold, possibly non-Hausdorff, in such a way that
the monodromy descends to a local diffeomorphism
Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})\to G
.

Remarks 2.4
.

(a)

For
n=2
, the space
Y_{2}(\mathsf{C})
is a certain open subset of the universal cover of
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
(see
Goldman
[
16
]
and Segal
[
37
]
).

(b)

Nondegenerate quasi-periodic paths on spheres are much-studied
in the literature. Little
[
28
]
proved that there are exactly three homotopy classes of (positive)
nondegenerate
loops
in
S^{2}

(see
[
33

).
The homotopy types of the space of nondegenerate loops on
S^{2}

were computed by Saldanha
[

]
. Khesin and B. Shapiro
[
23

classified the components of the space of positive, nondegenerate paths
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to S^{2}
with prescribed initial and final flags
\widehat{\gamma}(0),\widehat{\gamma}(1)
. For spheres of higher dimensions, we mention in particular M. Shapiro’s generalization of Little’s theorem in
[

[

2.3.
The Adler-Gelfand-Dikii space
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})

The coordinate-free definition of the Adler-Gelfand-Dikii operators
(
1
)
involves the space

|\Omega|_{\mathsf{C}}^{r}
of
r
-densities on
\mathsf{C}
. We take
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
to be the space of linear differential operator of order
n

(15)

L\colon|\Omega|_{\mathsf{C}}^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\to|\Omega|_{\mathsf{C}}^{\frac{1+n}{2}},

with principal symbol equal to
1
and with sub-principal symbol
equal to
0
. The degrees of the densities are such that these conditions make sense: the principal symbol is a scalar,
and
L,L^{*}
act between the same spaces. The space
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
is an affine space, with linear differential operators of order
n-2
as the underling vector space.

To describe the relationship with nondegenerate quasi-periodic curves
[
22
,
32
]
,
we use the n-th order
Wronskian
,

W_{n}\colon|\Omega|_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}}^{r}\times\cdots\times|\Omega|_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}}^{r}\to|\Omega|_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}}^{n(r-\frac{1-n}{2})}

given in local coordinates by

W_{n}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})=\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}u_{1}&\cdots&u_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{1}^{(n-1)}&\cdots&u_{n}^{(n-1)}\end{array}\right).

(The definition does not depend on the choice of coordinates.) Given
L\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
,
suppose
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\Omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\widetilde{\mathsf{C}})
is a fundamental system of solutions of the lifted operator
\widetilde{L}
on
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
, with Wronskian
W_{n}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})>0
. (Observe that for
r=\frac{1-n}{2}
, the Wronskian is a scalar.) By Abel’s formula from ODE theory, it is in fact constant. The equivalence class of this
n
-tuple of solutions,
modulo multiplication by positive densities in
\Omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\widetilde{\mathsf{C}})
,
is a nondegenerate quasi-periodic curve

\gamma=(u_{1}:\ldots:u_{n})\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to S^{n-1}.

The curve
\gamma
is determined by
L
up to the action of
G
. Conversely,
every nondegenerate quasi-periodic curve, written as

\gamma=(u_{1}:\cdots:u_{n})
, determines
L\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
by the formula

L(u)=(-1)^{n}\frac{W_{n+1}(u,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})}{W_{n}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})}.

The resulting map

(16)

p\colon\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})\to\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})

is the quotient map for the
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathsf{C}=S^{1},\ \widetilde{\mathsf{C}}=\mathbb{R}
. For distinct
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\sum_{i}c_{i}=0
,
one has the operator

L=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{d}{dt}-c_{i}).

The functions
u_{i}(t)=\exp(c_{i}t)
are a fundamental system of solutions, defining the quasi-periodic
curve
\gamma(t)=(e^{c_{1}t}:\ldots:e^{c_{n}t})
from Example
2.2
.

The infinite-dimensional affine space
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
carries the
Adler-Gelfand-Dikii Poisson structure

[
22
]
. According to Khesin and Ovsienko
[
32
]
, the space of symplectic leaves of
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
is identified with the space of
G
-orbits in
Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})
.
This correspondence was enhanced in
[
2
]
(for
n=2
) and

[
21
]
(for
n>2
) to a Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic groupoids

(17)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathsf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Here
G\ltimes Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})\rightrightarrows Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})
is the action groupoid, with 2-form obtained by pullback of the 2-form on the quasi-symplectic groupoid
G\ltimes G
integrating the Cartan-Dirac structure on
G
(see
[
2
]
).
On the other hand,

\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathsf{C})\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
is the quotient

\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathsf{C})=\mathcal{D}_{n}^{[2]}(\mathsf{C})/G

of the groupoid

(18)

\mathcal{D}^{[2]}_{n}(\mathsf{C})=\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})\times_{Y_{n}(\mathsf{C})}\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})

consisting of pairs
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{0})
of paths that are homotopic through
paths in
\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
with fixed monodromy.
The space

\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
carries a distinguished
G
-invariant 2-form

\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}
, constructed in
[
2
,
21
]
. The difference

\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}-\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}

descends to a closed multiplicative 2-form
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}
on

\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
, which is shown to be nondegenerate. In this paper, we will regard the symplectic groupoid

as an alternative description of the Adler-Gelfand-Dikii Poisson structure, avoiding any discussion of Poisson brackets.

Remark 2.6
.

There is a canonical affine-linear inclusion, equivariant for the action of

,

.

2.4.
Drinfeld-Sokolov

We shall use the Drinfeld-Sokolov construction to interpret
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
as a quotient of a certain space of
principal connections. Our starting point is the choice of a principal
G
-bundle
Q\to\mathsf{C}
, a
G
-equivariant map

\tau\colon Q\to S^{n-1}
, and a
G
-equivariant lift
\widehat{\tau}\colon Q\to\operatorname{Fl}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(cf. (
13
)).
Principal connections
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(Q)
give quasi-periodic curves
by composition
\gamma=\tau\circ s
where
s\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to Q
is a
\theta
-horizontal lift of the quotient map
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathsf{C}
. If this curve
\gamma
is non-degenerate, it determines a
G
-equivariant lift (depending on
\theta
)

(20)

\widehat{\tau}_{\theta}\colon Q\to\operatorname{Fl}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Under gauge transformations by
u\in\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
,
\widehat{\tau}_{u\cdot\theta}=\widehat{\tau}_{\theta}\circ u^{-1}
.

Definition 2.7
.

A connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(Q)
is
\widehat{\tau}
-positive
if the map
\tau
restricts to nondegenerate curves on the horizontal leaves of
\theta
, and the lifted map
\widehat{\tau}_{\theta}
is homotopic to
\widehat{\tau}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)
be the space of
\widehat{\tau}
-positive connections.

Note that this is an open condition: Small perturbations of
\widehat{\tau}
-positive connections are again
\widehat{\tau}
-positive.
The lift
s\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to Q
, and hence the curve
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathsf{C})
, are defined only up the action of
G=\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, but the resulting operator
L
does not depend on the choice. This defines a quotient map

(21)

\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)\to\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathsf{C})

Let
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
be the group
of gauge transformations
u\colon Q\to Q
fixing
\tau
, i.e.,

\tau\circ u^{-1}=\tau
, and let

\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q,\tau)
be its identity component. The following is a version of the Drinfeld-Sokolov theorem:

Proposition 2.8

(Drinfeld-Sokolov)
.

The action of
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q,\tau)
on
\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)
is free, with (
21
) as its quotient map.
This action admits a global section

2.5.
Positive hyperbolic elements

We shall now focus on the case
n=3
. An element
g\in\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
is called
positive hyperbolic
if it has three distinct, positive eigenvalues. Order these eigenvalues as

(29)

e^{c_{1}}<e^{c_{2}}<e^{c_{3}},\ \ \sum_{i}c_{i}=0.

Let
t\mapsto g^{t}
denote the unique 1-parameter subgroup through
g^{1}=g
; it defines a flow
\mathsf{v}\mapsto g^{t}\cdot\mathsf{v}
on
S^{2}
. There is a triangulation of
S^{2}
, invariant under the flow,
with six vertices, twelve edges, and eight triangles. The open triangles are the
images of open cones in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
spanned by triples of eigenvectors for the eigenvalues. A triangle will be called
positive
if this basis of eigenvectors is oriented, and negative otherwise.

The three vertices of a triangle, given by images of eigenvectors corresponding to (
29
), are respectively a repeller, saddle, and attractor for the flow.

Proposition 2.12
.

Suppose
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{3}(\mathsf{C})
has positive hyperbolic monodromy
g
, and that
\gamma
is (globally) convex: it intersects any projective line in at most two points. Then

(a)

The curve
\gamma
is contained a unique positive triangle
\Delta^{\gamma}\subseteq S^{2}
(for the triangulation defined by
g
).

(b)

The limits
\gamma(\pm\infty)=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)
exist, and are
vertices of
\Delta^{\gamma}
.

(c)

The curve
\gamma
admits an isomonodromic deformation to the curve
t\mapsto g^{t}\cdot v
, for any choice of
\mathsf{v}\in S^{2}
and any choice of parametrization
\mathsf{C}\cong S^{1}
. (That is, the two curves are
in the same fiber of
q\colon\mathcal{D}_{3}(\mathsf{C})\to Y_{3}(\mathsf{C})
.)

Proof.

We may assume
\mathsf{C}=S^{1},\ \widetilde{\mathsf{C}}=\mathbb{R}
. Using equivariance with respect to the action of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, we may
arrange that

(30)

g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{c_{1}},e^{c_{2}},e^{c_{3}}),

so the triangulation of
S^{2}
is the standard one (given by images of standard orthants in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
).
If
\gamma
were to meet the great circle line defined by
x_{i}=0
, it would have to cross that great circle an infinite number of times, by quasi-periodicity. For a convex curve, this is impossible. It follows that every convex curve is fully contained in a fixed component of the set where
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\neq 0
. Using the action of a diagonal element of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, we may arrange that the curve is contained in the region where
x_{1}>0,x_{3}>0
, and
x_{2}\neq 0
.

Introduce coordinates
u=x_{2}/x_{1},\ v=x_{3}/x_{1}
with
v>0
. The flow
g^{t}
is given by

(u,v)\mapsto(e^{t(c_{2}-c_{1})}u,e^{t(c_{3}-c_{1})}v).

Its trajectories are the curves
v=c\,u^{b}
with
b=\frac{c_{3}-c_{1}}{c_{2}-c_{1}}>0
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Looking at the sequence of points
\gamma(n)=g^{n}\gamma(0),\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we see that
the initial point
\gamma(0)
(and hence the entire curve
\gamma
) must lie
in the region where
u>0
(i.e.,
x_{2}>0
), and its graph is of the form
v=f(u)
where
f^{\prime}>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}>0
.
Given two convex
nondegenerate curves
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
with monodromy
g
, both contained in this region,
their linear interpolation
\gamma_{s}(t)=(1-s)\gamma_{0}(t)+s\gamma_{1}
(with sums taken in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for the choice of coordinates
u,v
) again has this property. In particular,
\gamma(t)
is homotopic to
t\mapsto g^{t}\cdot v
, for any choice of
v\in\Delta^{\gamma}
. The claim about limits follows since
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)=\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}\gamma(n)=\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}g^{n}\gamma(0)
.
∎

The unique convex geodesic segment
s^{\gamma}\subseteq S^{2}
connecting the limit points
\gamma(\pm\infty)
is one of the open edges of the closed triangle
\overline{\Delta}^{\gamma}
. It is called the
principal segment
in Goldman’s terminology
[
17
]
. Later, we will also be interested in the convex region

\Omega^{\gamma}\subseteq S^{2}

(sketched as the shaded region in the picture above), given by all points in the open region between
\gamma
and the principal segment
s^{\gamma}
, together with those two curves
(but excluding
\gamma(\pm\infty)
).

Remark 2.13
.

For a given positive hyperbolic element

can lie in any of the four positive

In addition,
\mu^{-1}(g)

By the results of Khesin-Shapiro

We shall reserve the notation

for the standard open triangle

\Delta=\{(x_{1}\colon x_{2}\colon x_{2})\in S^{2}|\ x_{1}>0,x_{2}>0,x_{3}>0\}.

It is invariant under the action of the subgroup
A\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
of diagonal matrices with positive entries.
Let
A_{+}\subseteq A
be the subset
of matrices of the form
g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{c_{1}},e^{c_{2}},e^{c_{3}})
with

(31)

\mathcal{D}_{3}(\mathsf{C})_{\Delta}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{3}(\mathsf{C})

\mathcal{D}_{3}(\mathsf{C})

\gamma\in\mathcal{D}_{3}(\mathsf{C})_{\Delta}

as a coordinate on
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}

\gamma(t)=(e^{tc_{1}}\colon\delta(t)e^{tc_{2}}\colon e^{tc_{3}})

periodic
. By calculating the Wronskian, we see that convexity

Remark 2.14

For a globally convex quasi-periodic curve

3.
Projective structures on surfaces

3.1.
(G,X)
-structures

Projective structures may be considered within the framework of
geometric structures

[
19
]
. Let
X
be a homogeneous space for a Lie group
G
. A
(G,X)
-structure on a connected manifold
M
is described by an atlas with
X
-valued charts, with transition functions given by elements of
G
.
These data determine a triple

(33)

(P,\theta,\sigma)

of a principal
G
-bundle
P\to M
with a flat connection
\theta
and a

G
-equivariant map
\sigma\colon P\to X

satisfying

(34)

\operatorname{ker}(T\sigma)\cap\operatorname{ker}\theta=0.

Here
(P,\theta)
is the flat bundle defined
by the (constant) transition functions. Equivalently, the fibers
P|_{m}
are the germs
[\phi]_{m}
of charts

\phi\colon U\to X
around
m
; the flat connection is such that for every chart
(U,\phi)
the resulting map

U\to P|_{U},\ m\mapsto[\phi]_{m}
is horizontal. The map
\sigma
is given by

(35)

\sigma\colon P\to X,\ [\phi]_{m}\mapsto\phi(m).

It may be regarded as a section of the associated bundle
P\times_{G}X
, and is called the

developing section
. Given a simply connected covering space
\widetilde{M}
with group
\Gamma
of deck transformations,
one obtains a
developing map

\varphi=\sigma\circ s\colon\widetilde{M}\to X
for the
(G,X)
-structure,
by composing

\sigma
with a
\theta
-horizontal lift of the quotient map,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/\Gamma}

The developing map
\varphi
is equivariant for a unique homomorphism
\varrho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
. The choice of
s
, and hence the map
\varphi
, is unique up to the action of
G
; a different choice changes
\varrho
by conjugation.

In the opposite direction, any triple (
33
) of a principal
G
-bundle with a flat connection and a

G
-equivariant map
\sigma\colon P\to X
satisfying (
34
) defines a
(G,X)
-structure on
M
,
with developing map
\varphi=\sigma\circ s
.
The triple defined by this
(G,X)
-structure is canonically isomorphic to
(P,\theta,\sigma)
.

Remark 3.1
.

In our discussion below, we will usually
fix
the pair
(P,\sigma)
. Writing
X=G/H
, the choice of
\sigma
is equivalent to a reduction of structure group
P_{H}\subseteq P
to
H\subseteq G
. (Hence, the choice of
(P,\sigma)
is equivalent to a choice of principal
H
-bundle.) A connection on
P
satisfying the transversality condition (

) is equivalent to a

[
26
]
on
P_{H}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Condition (
34
) means that the bundle map

(36)

T\sigma|_{\operatorname{ker}\theta}\colon\operatorname{ker}\theta\to TX

is a fiberwise isomorphism. If
M
and the model space
X
are oriented, then
\operatorname{ker}\theta
and
TX
inherit orientations, and we may require that this map is fiberwise

orientation preserving
.

3.2.
Projective structures

A
projective structure
on a surface
\Sigma
is a
(\operatorname{PGL}(3,\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}^{2})
-structure. If
\Sigma
is oriented, then any
chart map admits a unique lift to an orientation preserving map to the sphere
S^{2}
, with transition maps in
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
.
Since orientations will be relevant for our discussion, we will therefore treat projective structures on oriented surfaces
as
(G,X)=(\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R}),S^{2})
-structures.

Projective structures may be described by their (orientation preserving) developing maps
\varphi\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to S^{2}
,
equivariant with respect to a morphism

\varrho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
; the pair
(\varphi,\varrho)
is unique up to the action of
G
.
A
geodesic
on
\Sigma
is a curve that, locally in a chart, is given by a geodesic in
S^{2}
.
The projective structure is called
convex
if every path
[0,1]\to\Sigma
is homotopic relative fixed end points to a geodesic,
uniquely up to reparametrization. Equivalently
[
17
]
, the developing map
\varphi\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to S^{2}
is injective, with convex image contained in an open hemisphere.

If
\Sigma
has no boundary, we denote by
\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)
the space of all projective structures, and by
\operatorname{cProj}(\Sigma)
the subspace of convex projective structures.
The group
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)
of diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity acts on these spaces, and the quotient spaces are the
deformation spaces
,

c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\frac{\operatorname{cProj}(\Sigma)}{\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\frac{\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)}{\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)}.

We list some basic examples of projective structures, following
[
11
,
17
]
.

Example 3.3

(Hyperbolic structures)
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the Poincaré disk, with its usual action of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
.
A hyperbolic structure on
\Sigma
is a
(\operatorname{PSU}(1,1),\mathbb{D})
-structure; let
\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)
be the space of hyperbolic structures.
The Klein-Beltrami model of hyperbolic geometry defines an inclusion

(37)

\mathbb{D}\hookrightarrow S^{2}

taking the center
0\in\mathbb{D}
to
(1\colon 0\colon 0)\in S^{2}
, and equivariant for a group morphism

(38)

\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R}).

(We may define (
38
) via the adjoint representation of
\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
on its Lie algebra

\mathfrak{psu}(1,1)\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
.)
Hence, any atlas with hyperbolic charts may be regarded as an atlas with projective charts.

If
\Sigma
is compact, of negative Euler characteristic, then the projective structures arising in this way are convex, since
\widetilde{\Sigma}\cong\mathbb{D}
by uniformization.
This defines an inclusion
\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)\to c\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)
.
Passing to isotopy classes, we obtain an inclusion

(39)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)

of Teichmüller space.

Example 3.4

(Principal annulus
[
17
]
)
.

Let
g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{c_{1}},e^{c_{2}},e^{c_{3}})\in A_{+}
, i.e.,
c_{1}<c_{2}<c_{3}
with
\sum c_{i}=0
. The map

(40)

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to S^{2},\ (x,y)\mapsto(e^{xc_{1}}\colon-ye^{xc_{2}}\colon e^{xc_{3}})

is an embedding onto the convex subset
\Omega
of all
(x_{1}\colon x_{2}\colon x_{3})
such that
x_{1},x_{3}>0
. It intertwines the transformation

(x,y)\mapsto(x+1,y)
with the action of
g
. Hence,
\varphi
serves as a developing map for
a convex projective structure on the open annulus
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. This is the

principal annulus of monodromy
g
. The curve
y=0
is the unique closed geodesic for this projective structure.
It separates the principal annulus into two
half-annuli
, defined by
y\geq 0
and
y\leq 0
.

Example 3.5

(Surfaces of negative Euler characteristic)
.

The work of Choi and Goldman, building on earlier work of Kuiper, Benzécri, Koszul, Kac-Vinberg, and others, gave a complete classification of projective structures on compact oriented surfaces
\Sigma
without boundary and with negative Euler characteristic,
\chi(\Sigma)<0
.
The main result of

[
10
]
states that the deformation space of
convex
projective structures is the Hitchin component
(higher Teichmüller space)
[
20
]

of the character
variety of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
. Furthermore, by Goldman’s work
[
17
]
(see also Kim
[
24
]
) this space admits
Fenchel-Nielsen parameters
:

•

The holonomies along simple loops in
\Sigma
are
positive hyperbolic elements; the conjugacy class of the holonomy assigns two
length parameters
to the loop.

•

Every simple loop is freely homotopic to a unique closed geodesic.
The local model around this geodesic is given by the principal annulus (Example
3.4
).

•

Cutting along non-intersecting closed geodesics, one obtains a pants decomposition of the surface.
The projective structure on each
pair of pants with geodesic boundary is determined by eight parameters:
two length parameters for each boundary component, and two
internal parameters
.

•

For each gluing circle there are also two

, since pants may be glued with a twist, similar to the case of hyperbolic structures. (See Section

•

For a surface of genus
\mathsf{g}\geq 2

2\mathsf{g}-2
pants with
3\mathsf{g}-3
gluing circles. The convex projective structures are thus described by
(2\mathsf{g}-2)\cdot 2+(3\mathsf{g}-3)\cdot(2+2)=(2\mathsf{g}-2)\cdot\dim G

More general projective structures, not necessarily convex, are obtained from the convex ones by

3.3.
Goldman twists

Consider again the principal annulus, Example
3.4
. The action of
A\subseteq G
on
S^{2}
preserves
\Omega
and commutes with the action of
g
, and so descends to an action on the principal annulus by projective transformations.
Given
h\in A
, we may choose an orientation preserving diffeomorphism
\mathsf{F}
of the annulus that is trivial for
y<-\epsilon
and agrees with the action of
h
for
y>\epsilon
, for
some
\epsilon>0
. Push-forward under
\mathsf{F}
produces a new projective structure on the annulus, which agrees with the original one for
|y|>\epsilon
, but is deformed inside the region
|y|\leq\epsilon
. The diffeomorphism
\mathsf{F}
, and hence the resulting projective structure, is unique up to a diffeomorphism that is supported inside the
\epsilon
-neighborhood,
and (in particular) is isotopic to the identity.

Given a projective structure on a general surface
\Sigma
, as in Example
3.5
, and a simple closed oriented geodesic
\lambda\subseteq\Sigma
with positive hyperbolic holonomy, we may use the principal annulus as a model
to ‘twist’ the projective structure by elements
h\in A
. Up to isotopy, the new projective structure depends only on
\lambda
and

h
. We hence obtain, for any
\lambda
, an action of
A
on the space
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
, called the
Goldman
twist
. The Goldman twists for distinct simple closed oriented geodesics commute.

Taking
\lambda
to be one of the curves from a pants decomposition, these twists change the twist parameters corresponding to
\lambda
but do not affect any of the other parameters.
In
[
41
]
, Wienhard and Zhang also introduced
eruption flows
, changing the internal parameters of a pair of pants
(but not affecting the twist or length parameters).

3.4.
Projective structures with nondegenerate boundary

The Choi-Goldman theory also applies to projective structures on surfaces with boundary, provided that the boundary components are
geodesics
with positive hyperbolic holonomy. In this article, we will consider the opposite extreme:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected oriented surface with boundary. A projective structure on
\Sigma
, with developing map
\varphi\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to S^{2}
, has
nondegenerate boundary
, if the restriction of
\varphi
to boundary components

\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\subseteq\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}
are nondegenerate curves.
We denote by
\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)
the space of projective structures with nondegenerate boundary, and
by
\operatorname{cProj}(\Sigma)
the subspace for which (i) the projective structure is convex, and (ii) all boundary holonomies are
positive hyperbolic. The corresponding deformation spaces are denoted

c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\frac{c\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)}{\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)},\ \ \ \mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\frac{\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)}{\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)},

where
\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
is the group of diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary (pointwise), and
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)

its identity component.

Convexity means, as before, that every path
[0,1]\to\Sigma
is homotopic (rel fixed end points) to a unique geodesic, or equivalently that the image of a developing map
\varphi
is convex. If
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
, the spaces
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\subseteq\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
are infinite-dimensional.

Consider a boundary component
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\subseteq\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}
mapping to
\mathsf{C}\subseteq\partial\Sigma
. The nondegenerate curve

\gamma\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to S^{2}
determines an Adler-Gelfand-Dikii operator
L\in\mathcal{R}_{3}(\mathsf{C})
. Changing the pre-image of
\mathsf{C}
in
\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}
, or changing
\varphi
by the
G
-action, does not affect this operator. Additionally,
L
is
unchanged under the diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary. Using this construction for all components of
\partial\Sigma
,
we obtain a map,

(41)

\Psi\colon\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{R}_{3}(\partial\Sigma),

equivariant for the residual action of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\Sigma)/\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)}

. But globally, it fails to be surjective in general: For example,

could be homotopic to a nondegenerate quasi-periodic map that is no longer injective. This problem does not arise for

(52)

\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)\to\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma).

Proposition 4.4
.

The group
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
acts freely on
\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
, with

\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)/\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma).

Proof.

If two connections
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
are related by a gauge transformation in
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
, the resulting projective structure does not change. This shows that (
52

\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
-orbits. As explained above,
every projective structure with nondegenerate boundary determines a triple
(P_{1},\sigma_{1},\theta_{1})
, where
e(P_{1},\sigma_{1})=\chi(\Sigma)
. In particular, there is an isomorphism of principal
H
-bundles

(P_{1})_{H}\to P_{H}
. (If the boundary is non-empty, then the isomorphism can be chosen to be compatible with the trivializations along the boundary.) In turn, it determines an isomorphism
P_{1}\to P
taking
\sigma_{1}
to
\sigma
. This shows that the map (
52

It remains to show that the action is free. By choosing projective charts, it suffices to prove this claim for the standard projective structure on an open subset
U\subseteq S^{2}
, with
P=U\times G
,
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}\cdot m
and with the
trivial connection, given by the connection 1-form
\mathsf{A}=0
. Suppose a gauge transformation
\psi\in C^{\infty}(U,G)

preserves
\sigma
, and satisfies

is a
constant

(m,g)\mapsto(m,ag)
for some

∎

For a trivial bundle

.

4.3.
Construction of the symplectic structure

For the time being, let
G
be any Lie group admitting an
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariant nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\cdot
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For every principal
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
over a compact oriented surface (possibly with boundary), the choice of this bilinear form defines the
Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure

[
4
]
on the space
\mathcal{A}(P)
of principal connections.
Identifying the tangent spaces to
\mathcal{A}(P)
with 1-forms valued in the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{g}(P)=P\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
, the Atiyah-Bott form is given by wedge product followed by integration:
\int_{\Sigma}\alpha\cdot\beta
for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))
.
The symplectic structure is preserved under the action

\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P)\circlearrowright\mathcal{A}(P)

of the group of principal bundle automorphisms with orientation preserving base map. The action admits an affine moment map,
with a bulk contribution involving the curvature
F^{\theta}\in\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))
, and a boundary contribution involving the pullback
\partial\theta
of the connection. In more detail, identify the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aut}(P)
with sections of the Atiyah algebroid
\operatorname{At}(P)=TP/G
, and recall that a connection defines a splitting
s^{\theta}\colon\operatorname{At}(P)\to\mathfrak{g}(P)
. Then the moment map component for
\zeta\in\mathfrak{aut}(P)
is given by

(55)

\mathcal{A}(P)\to\mathbb{R},\ \ \theta\mapsto-\int_{\Sigma}F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta}(\zeta)+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\mbox{bdry terms}.

The boundary terms depend only on
\partial\theta
and
\zeta|_{\partial\Sigma}
, and
vanish if
\zeta
vanishes along
\partial\Sigma
.
See
[
3
, Appendix B]
for a detailed discussion.
The Atiyah-Bott structure on connections descends to a symplectic structure on

(56)

\mathcal{M}(P)=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)/\operatorname{Gau}(P,\partial P),

the moduli space of flat connections, up to gauge transformations that are trivial along the boundary. This space
carries a residual gauge action of
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P)
, with moment map
\mathcal{M}(P)\to\mathcal{A}(\partial P),\ [\theta]\mapsto\partial\theta
.

In our case, where
G=\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, we take the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})
to be the trace form
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\xi\eta)
.
By Proposition
4.4
, we have that
\operatorname{Proj}(\Sigma)
is the quotient of

\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
under
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
, and

\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
is obtained by taking another quotient by
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
.
This suggests a construction of the symplectic form on
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
via reduction with respect to
the subgroup of automorphisms preserving
\sigma
, and with
base map in
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
. However, if
\partial\Sigma
has non-empty boundary, this is not directly a symplectic quotient:
automorphisms whose base map fixes the boundary might still act non-trivially on
P|_{\partial\Sigma}
, and
hence will produce boundary terms in the moment map.

Following the strategy in
[
3
]
, we introduce an intermediate space

(57)

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)=\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)

where
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)
are the automorphisms
preserving
\sigma
, with base map homotopic to the identity, and fixing

\partial P

pointwise
. This space carries a residual action of
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
, and an equivariant map to

\mathcal{A}^{\partial\sigma}(\partial P)\subseteq\mathcal{A}(\partial P)
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The space (
57
) is a symplectic reduction,

(58)

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)=\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma).

The inclusion of
\sigma
-positive flat connections descends to a
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
-equivariant local
symplectomorphism,

(59)

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\to\mathcal{M}(P)

intertwining the maps to
\mathcal{A}(\partial P)
.

Proof.

The Lie algebra of
\operatorname{Aut}(P,\sigma)
are the
G
-invariant vector fields on
P
preserving
\sigma
, or equivalently are tangent to
P_{H}
. The vector fields are the sections of a sub-Lie algebroid
\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)\cong\operatorname{At}(P_{H})
of the Atiyah algebroid
\operatorname{At}(P)=TP/G
. The space

\mathfrak{aut}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
consists of sections
\zeta
of
\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)
vanishing along
\partial\Sigma
. The moment map for
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
is obtained from (
55
) by restriction, and hence is simply

\theta\mapsto-\int_{\Sigma}F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta}(\zeta),

not involving a
boundary term. A connection
\theta
satisfies the first condition in Definition
4.1
if and only if the restriction

(60)

s^{\theta}|_{\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)}\colon\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)\to\mathfrak{g}(P)

is an isomorphism (cf.
[
3
, Proof of Proposition 6.2]
). We hence see that the integral vanishes for all
\zeta\in\mathfrak{aut}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
if and only if
F^{\theta}=0
, i.e,
\theta
is flat. That is, the zero level set for the

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
-action is exactly
\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
, with null foliation given by

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
-orbit directions.

On the other hand, the null foliation of
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
is also given by the
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\partial P)
-orbit directions. It follows that locally, the

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
-orbit directions and
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\partial P)
-orbit directions coincide.
Every element
of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
is homotopic to an element of
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
, by deforming the base map. Thus, if two
\sigma
-positive connections are in the same
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma)
-orbit then they are also in the same

\operatorname{Gau}(P,\partial P)
-orbit. Hence, the inclusion of
\sigma
-positive connections
descends to a local symplectomorphism (
59
).
∎

The deformation space of projective structures is obtained as the quotient

(61)

\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)/\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma).

The symplectic structure does not directly descend since it is not
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
-basic.
However, note that the moment map for the action of
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
is given by the map

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\to\mathcal{A}(\partial P),\ \ [\theta]\mapsto\partial\theta
followed by the quotient map to
\mathcal{A}(\partial\mathcal{P},\partial\sigma)
:

(62)

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\to\mathcal{A}(\partial P)/\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{gau}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)),\ \ [\theta]\mapsto[\partial\theta]

Here the brackets indicate equivalence classes of connections. By Proposition
2.11
, the
image of (
62
) is a single coadjoint
\operatorname{Gau}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
-orbit

(63)

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{A}^{\partial\sigma-\operatorname{reg}}(\partial P)/\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{gau}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)).

Using the ‘shifting trick’ from symplectic geometry, we may therefore write (
61
) as a symplectic quotient
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
. To summarize:

Proposition 4.10
.

The space
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
is a
symplectic quotient,

(64)

\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)_{\mathcal{O}}=(\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\times\mathcal{O}^{-})/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma).

We use Equation (
64
) to define a symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
.

Remark 4.11
.

If
\partial\Sigma=\emptyset
, the second step is not needed, and we directly have

\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma).

Remark 4.12
.

For the case
\partial\Sigma=\emptyset
, Goldman
[
18
]
gives an alternative construction of the symplectic structure on

\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
as an infinite-dimensional symplectic quotient. It would be interesting to generalize Goldman’s approach to the setting with nondegenerate boundary.

5.
Moment map property

Recall some notions from symplectic geometry. A
Lagrangian relation

L\colon M_{1}\dasharrow M_{2}
between symplectic manifolds
M_{1},M_{2}
is a Lagrangian submanifold
L\subseteq M_{2}\times M_{1}^{-}
, where the superscript indicates the opposite symplectic structure. The action of a symplectic groupoid
\mathcal{S}\rightrightarrows B
on a symplectic manifold
M
, along a
map
\Phi\colon M\to B
, is called
Hamiltonian
if the action map is a Lagrangian relation

L\colon S\times M\dasharrow M.

The notion of a Hamiltonian action of a symplectic groupoid is due to Mikami-Weinstein
[
31
]
, who observed that ordinary Hamiltonian actions of Lie groups
G
on symplectic manifolds are equivalent to Hamiltonian actions of the cotangent groupoid
T^{*}G\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

In Section
3.6
, we described a ‘local’ groupoid action of
\mathcal{S}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)

on the deformation space
\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
of projective structures
along the map

\Psi\colon\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{R}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)
. This ‘local’ action was defined in terms of
a subgroupoid

\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\subseteq\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\times(\mathcal{S}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)\times\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma))^{-}.

We will now show that the local groupoid action is Hamiltonian, in the sense that
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)

defines a Lagrangian relation. We shall prove this by comparing with similar groupoids for the spaces
\mathcal{M}(P)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)
.
Our strategy is described by the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}(\partial P)\times\mathcal{M}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}^{\partial\sigma}(\partial P)\times\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)\times\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}(P)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}^{\sigma}(P)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)}

where the dotted arrows are Lagrangian relations.
In the diagram,
\mathcal{L}(P)
is given by the action of the symplectic groupoid
\mathcal{S}(\partial P)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{A}(\partial P)
on
\mathcal{M}(P)
.
The middle arrow is obtained from the top row by ‘restriction’. The bottom row is obtained from the middle row by Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction.

5.1.
The groupoids
\mathcal{S}(Q),\mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q),\mathcal{S}_{3}(\mathsf{C})

Let
Q\to\mathsf{C}
be a principal
G
-bundle over an abstract circle. The space
\mathcal{A}(Q)
of principal connections is an infinite-dimensional
Poisson manifold, with symplectic leaves the orbits of the identity component of the gauge group
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q)
.
(An isomorphism
Q\cong S^{1}\times G
identifies the gauge group with the loop group.)
Its symplectic groupoid is the action groupoid

\mathcal{S}(Q)=\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q)\times\mathcal{A}(Q)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{A}(Q),

with 2-form
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}
obtained from its identification with the (twisted) cotangent bundle of
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q)
. (See
[
2
, Example A.12]
.)
It admits an alternative description in terms of a space

\mathcal{P}(Q)
of
quasi-periodic sections
of the pullback bundle
\widetilde{Q}\to\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
. Here
t\in\Gamma(\widetilde{Q})
is
called quasi-periodic if it satisfies
\kappa^{*}t=\mu(t)\cdot t
for some
monodromy

\mu(t)\in G
.
The principal action of
G
on
P
induces an action of
G
on quasi-periodic sections, with quotient map

p\colon\mathcal{P}(Q)\to\mathcal{A}(Q)

taking
t\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
to the unique connection
\theta
for which
t
is parallel. Let
\mathcal{P}^{[2]}(Q)
consist of pairs
(t_{1},t_{0})
of
quasi-periodic sections that are related by isomonodromic homotopy.
Equivalently, the unique gauge transformation taking
t_{0}
to
t_{1}
lies in
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q)
.
We hence have
\mathcal{P}^{[2]}(Q)\cong\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q)\times\mathcal{P}(Q)
, which descends to an identification

(65)

\mathcal{S}(Q)\cong\mathcal{P}^{[2]}(Q)/G\rightrightarrows\mathcal{A}(Q).

As shown in
[
2
]
, the space
\mathcal{P}(Q)
carries a distinguished
\operatorname{Aut}(Q)\times G
-invariant 2-form
\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}
, with nice properties. Furthermore, this 2-form establishes a Morita equivalence of
\mathcal{S}(Q)
with a quasi-symplectic groupoid over the universal cover of
G
. The following is a direct consequence of this result:

Proposition 5.1
.

The pullback of
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}
to
\mathcal{P}^{[2]}(Q)
is the difference
\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}-\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\varpi_{P}
, where

\operatorname{pr}_{i}\colon\mathcal{P}^{[2]}(Q)\to\mathcal{P}(Q)
are the two projections.

Given
\tau\colon Q\to S^{2}

\widehat{\tau}\colon Q\to\operatorname{Fl}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\mathcal{P}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)
of quasi-periodic sections such that the corresponding connection
\theta
is
\widehat{\tau}
-positive. (Cf. Definition
2.7
.)
Let
\mathcal{P}^{\widehat{\tau},[2]}(Q)
consist of pairs of such sections, which furthermore are related by an isomonodromic homotopy
of such sections. Then

(66)

\mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)\cong\mathcal{P}^{\widehat{\tau},[2]}(Q)/G\rightrightarrows\mathcal{A}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)

\mathcal{S}(Q)
. Since
\widehat{\tau}

, and in particular is symplectic. The group
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(Q,\tau)

\mathcal{R}_{3}(\Sigma)
. Likewise, the quotient of
\mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\tau}}(Q)

.

5.2.
The (local) groupoid actions

Returning to our setting from Section
4
, consider a compact oriented surface
\Sigma
with boundary, a principal
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
, a developing section
\sigma\colon P\to S^{2}
, and a lift

\widehat{\partial\sigma}
of the boundary restriction such that
e(P,\sigma)=\chi(\Sigma)
. We also fix a universal cover

\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
with group
\Gamma
of deck transformations; the pullback bundle is denoted
\widetilde{P}\to\widetilde{\Sigma}
.

The symplectic groupoid

\mathcal{S}(\partial P)=\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P)\times\mathcal{A}(\partial P)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{A}(\partial P)

is the direct product over all
\mathcal{S}(\partial P|_{\mathsf{C}_{i}})\rightrightarrows\mathcal{A}(\partial P|_{\mathsf{C}_{i}})
as
\mathsf{C}_{i}
ranges over all components of
\partial\Sigma
.
As in (
65
), it may be described in terms of pairs
(t_{1},t_{0})
of
\Gamma
-quasi-periodic sections

t\colon\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\partial\widetilde{P}
.

The action of
\operatorname{Gau}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P)
on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(P)
is equivalent to an action of the symplectic groupoid
\mathcal{S}(\partial P)
.
In particular, its graph defines a Lagrangian subgroupoid

\mathcal{L}(P)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(P)\times\big(\mathcal{S}(\partial P)\times\mathcal{M}(P)\big)^{-}.

It is convenient to regard
\mathcal{M}(P)
as the quotient, under the action of
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\partial P)\times G
, of the space of
\Gamma
-quasi-periodic sections

s\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\widetilde{P}
. Letting
[s]
denote the equivalence class of such a section, the subgroupoid
\mathcal{L}(P)

consists of all

(68)

([s_{1}],[(t_{1},t_{0})],[s_{0}])

such that
s_{0}
is homotopic to
s_{1}
with fixed monodromy, and
t_{0},t_{1}
are the
boundary values of
s_{0},s_{1}
.

The space
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)
(cf. (

equivalence classes of sections

, with the additional requirement that

is
\sigma
-positive in the sense that
the connection corresponding to
s
is

\sigma
-positive. As in the previous section, define the (open) symplectic subgroupoid

\mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\partial\sigma}}(\partial P)\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\partial P)
. Its ‘local’ groupoid action on
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)
is given by the
subgroupoid

(69)

\mathcal{L}^{\sigma}(P)\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\times\big(\mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\partial\sigma}}(\partial P)\times\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\big)^{-},

consisting of equivalence classes (
68
), but with
s_{0},s_{1}
being
\sigma
-positive, and the isomonodromic homotopy between them being through
\sigma
-positive sections. From this description it is evident that the local symplectomorphisms
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\to\mathcal{M}(P)
and
\mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\partial\sigma}}(\partial P)\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\partial P)
restrict to a
map, locally 1-1,
\mathcal{L}^{\sigma}(P)\to\mathcal{L}(P)
. In particular,
\mathcal{L}^{\sigma}(P)
is Lagrangian.

Theorem 5.2
.

The subgroupoid

(70)

\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak{P}}(\Sigma)\times\big(\mathcal{S}_{3}(\partial\Sigma)\times\widehat{\mathfrak{P}}(\Sigma)\big)^{-}

is Lagrangian. That is,
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
defines a (local) Hamiltonian action of a symplectic
groupoid.

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
was described in Section
3.6

in terms of equivalence classes
[(\phi_{1},\phi_{0})]
of developing maps

\widetilde{\Sigma}\to S^{2}
. Every such developing map comes from a
\sigma
-positive quasi-periodic section

s\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\widetilde{P}
; two sections give the same developing map if they are related by

\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
. Consequently,
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
is just the image of
\mathcal{L}^{\sigma}(P)
under the quotient maps

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}(P)\to{\mathfrak{P}}(\Sigma),\ \ \ \mathcal{S}^{\widehat{\partial\sigma}}(\partial P)\to\mathcal{S}_{3}(\partial\Sigma).

As we saw, these quotient maps can also be seen as reductions with respect to the orbit
\mathcal{O}
. We conclude
that
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
is Lagrangian.
∎

6.
Goldman twists

Suppose
\lambda\subseteq\Sigma
is an oriented simple loop, not homotopic to a boundary
component. Associated with
\lambda
is the
A
-action (twist) on the space
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
, as described in Section

3.3
. For the case without boundary, it was shown in
[
17
]
and
[
24
]
that the
A
-action is
Hamiltonian, with moment map the logarithm of the holonomy

\operatorname{Hol}_{\lambda}\colon c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to A_{+}.

(The holonomy is defined up to conjugacy, but each positive hyperbolic element is conjugate to a unique element of
A_{+}
).
We will verify this fact for our description of the symplectic structure, with a possibly non-empty boundary. We shall use

\xi_{1}=\operatorname{diag}(-\frac{1}{2},0,\frac{1}{2}),\ \ \xi_{2}=\operatorname{diag}(-\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3},-\frac{1}{3})

as a basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Lie}(A)
. Let

\operatorname{diag}(e^{c_{1}},e^{c_{2}},e^{c_{3}})\in A_{+}
be the monodromy around
\lambda
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Given a simple loop
\lambda
in
\Sigma
, not homotopic to a boundary component, the function

H_{i}=\operatorname{tr}(\log(\operatorname{Hol}_{\lambda})\xi_{i})\colon c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{R}

is a Hamiltonian for the twist flow given by
\xi_{i}
.

Writing
\operatorname{Hol}_{\lambda}=(e^{c_{1}},e^{c_{2}},e^{c_{3}})\in A_{+}
with

c_{1}<c_{2}<c_{3},\ \sum c_{i}=0
, we have

H_{1}={\frac{1}{2}}(c_{3}-c_{1}),\ H_{2}=c_{2}.

This agrees with
[
17
, Section 1.8]
up to normalization.

To prove the theorem, fix a pair
(P,\sigma)
and list
\widehat{\partial\sigma}
for the construction of the symplectic structure.
Near
\lambda
, we may choose a trivialization of
P
such that
\sigma
is given by

g^{-1}\cdot(1\colon 0\colon 0)
. These data allow us to describe projective structures, near
\lambda
,
by connections 1-form
\mathsf{A}
. A convex projective structure having
\lambda
as a closed geodesic is modelled
by the principal annulus, with connection 1-form
\mathsf{A}
given by Equation (
54
). We shall need
connection 1-forms describing the images of this projective structure under the twist flows.

Fix
\epsilon>0
, and
let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a function that is equal to
0
for
y\leq-\epsilon
and equal to
1
for

y\geq\epsilon
. The maps

\mathsf{F}_{i}(t)\colon(x,y)\mapsto\exp(t\chi(y)\xi_{i}))\cdot(x,y)

are diffeomorphisms for
|t|
sufficiently small, and represent twists by
\exp(t\xi_{i})\in A
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The pullback of the projective structure under these diffeomorphisms corresponds to connection 1-forms

\mathsf{A}_{i}(t)=\mathsf{A}+t\alpha_{i}
with

(71)

\alpha_{1}=\left(\xi_{1}+\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}y&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{array}\right)\right)\chi^{\prime}(y){\mathsf{d}}y,\ \ \ \ \ \alpha_{2}=\left(\xi_{2}+\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
-y&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right)\right)\chi^{\prime}(y){\mathsf{d}}y.

Proof.

The expressions are computed as
\mathsf{F}_{i}(t)^{*}\mathsf{A}
, followed by a gauge transformation, arranging that

\mathsf{A}_{i}(t)
agrees with
\mathsf{A}
for
|y|\geq\epsilon
. The calculation shows that we may take

\mathsf{A}_{1}(t)=\exp(-bt\chi\xi_{2})\bullet\mathsf{F}_{1}(t)^{*}\mathsf{A},\ \ \mathsf{A}_{2}(t)=\exp(-t\chi\xi_{2})\bullet\mathsf{F}_{2}(t)^{*}\mathsf{A}

where
b=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{c_{2}-c_{1}}{c_{3}-c_{1}}
.
∎

Observe that
\alpha_{i}
do not depend on the
c_{i}
, and that they
have the special form
\alpha_{i}=\xi_{i}\chi^{\prime}(y){\mathsf{d}}y+\mbox{lower triangular}
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

The claim is that

(72)

-\iota(\xi_{i}^{\sharp})\omega={\mathsf{d}}H_{i}

where the vector field
\xi_{i}^{\sharp}
on
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)

is the generator of the twist flow defined by
\xi_{i}
. We shall verify this identity at a given convex projective structure, represented in terms of a
\sigma
-positive connection
\theta
.
The twist flow may be realized in terms of a change of
\theta
that is localized near the given geodesic
\lambda
. We may hence
work in the local model, given by the principal annulus, with the trivial
G
-bundle and
\sigma
in its standard form, and with the connection 1-form
\mathsf{A}
given by (
54
), and with the twist flow described by the forms

\alpha_{i}
from (
71
).

Consider now an arbitrary tangent vector to
c\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)
at the given projective structure. It is
represented by a 1-parameter family of projective structures, and hence is given
near
\lambda
by a 1-parameter
family of connection 1-forms
\mathsf{A}_{t}
, with
\mathsf{A}_{0}=\mathsf{A}
. We may assume that
\lambda
(i.e., the submanifold
y=0
) remains a geodesic for all
t
, and that the pullback of
\mathsf{A}_{t}
to the submanifold
y=0
has the standard form given by the first term in (
54
), with the
c_{i}
depending on
t
. Thus,

\log\operatorname{Hol}_{\lambda}(A_{t})=\operatorname{diag}(c_{1}(t),c_{2}(t),c_{3}(t))\in\mathfrak{a}.

The
t
-derivative of the family of connections
\mathsf{A}_{t}
is of the form

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mathsf{A}_{t}=(\zeta+\mbox{lower triangular}){\mathsf{d}}x+[\ldots]{\mathsf{d}}y.

where
\zeta=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\operatorname{diag}(c_{1}(t),c_{2}(t),c_{3}(t))
. Hence,

\int\operatorname{tr}(\alpha_{i}\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mathsf{A}_{t})=-\int\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{i}\zeta)\chi^{\prime}(y){\mathsf{d}}x{\mathsf{d}}y=-\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{i}\zeta)=-\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{i}\log\operatorname{Hol}_{\lambda}(A_{t})).

This verifies the moment map property (
72
).
∎

Matrix Elements and Characters of the Discrete Series (“Massive”) Unitary Irreducible Representations of Sp ( 4 , ℝ ) (4,{\mathbb{R}})
Jean-Pierre Gazeau 1, Mariano A. del Olmo 2, Hamed Pejhan 3

Abstract
This paper obtains the matrix elements and characters of the discrete series unitary irreducible representations (UIRs) of the Sp ( 4 , ℝ ) (4,\mathbb{R}) group. With an isomorphic relationship to the two-fold covering of SO ( 2 , 3 ) 0 {}_{0}(2,3) (Sp ( 4 , ℝ ) ∼ (4,\mathbb{R})\sim SO ( 2 , 3 ) 0 × ℤ 2 {}_{0}(2,3)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} ), this group holds particular importance as the kinematical/relativity group within the framework of ( 1 + 3 1+3 -dimensional) anti-de Sitter spacetime.

Appendix A
Complex quaternions

The set of complex quaternions
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}\sim{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}=\left\{z=\underbrace{(z^{4},\boldsymbol{z})}_{\mbox{\small{scalar-vector notations}}}\equiv\quad\underbrace{z^{4}+z^{1}\boldsymbol{e}_{1}+z^{2}\boldsymbol{e}_{2}+z^{3}\boldsymbol{e}_{3}}_{\mbox{\small{Euclidean metric notations}}}\;\;;\;\;z^{1},z^{2},z^{3},z^{4}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}\,,

(A.1)

where
\boldsymbol{e}_{i=1,2,3}
obeys the following quaternionic algebra:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}&=\epsilon_{ij}^{\,\,\,k}\boldsymbol{e}_{k}\,,\\[8.5359pt]
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}^{2}&=-(1,\boldsymbol{0})\equiv-1\quad(\mbox{by abuse of notations, we define $(1,\boldsymbol{0})\equiv 1$})\,,\end{array}

(A.2)

with
\epsilon_{ij}^{\,\,\,k}
(
i,j,k=1,2,3
) representing the three-dimensional totally antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol.

The multiplication rule for two complex quaternions is as follows:

zz^{\prime}=(z^{4},\boldsymbol{z})({z^{\prime}}^{4},\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime})=\left(z^{4}{z^{\prime}}^{4}-\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime},z^{4}\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}+{z^{\prime}}^{4}\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{z}\times\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)\,,

(A.3)

where
\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{z}\times\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}
denote, respectively, the analytic continuations of the Euclidean inner product and the cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

For a given complex quaternion
z
, we can define:

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\hskip-85.35826pt\bullet&\text{complex conjugate:}&\overline{z}:=\left(\overline{z^{4}},\overline{\boldsymbol{z}}\right),\qquad\text{with the property}\;\;\overline{{zz^{\prime}}}=\overline{z}\,\overline{{z^{\prime}}}\\[8.5359pt]
\hskip-85.35826pt\bullet&\text{quaternionic conjugate:}&\widetilde{z}:=(z^{4},-\boldsymbol{z}),\qquad\text{with the property}\;\;\widetilde{{zz^{\prime}}}=\widetilde{{z^{\prime}}}\widetilde{z}\\[8.5359pt]
\hskip-85.35826pt\bullet&\text{adjoint:}&z^{\ast}:=\overline{\widetilde{z}}=\widetilde{\overline{z}},\hskip 7.11317pt\qquad\text{with the property}\;\;({zz^{\prime}})^{\ast}={z^{\prime\ast}}z^{\ast}\\[8.5359pt]
\hskip-85.35826pt\bullet&\text{determinant:}&\det z=\det\widetilde{z}=z\widetilde{z}=\widetilde{z}z=(z^{1})^{2}+(z^{2})^{2}+(z^{3})^{2}+(z^{4})^{2}\,.\end{array}

(A.4)

From the latter identity, one promptly obtains the expression for the inverse
z^{-1}
of
z
(assuming
\det z\neq 0
):

z^{-1}=\frac{\widetilde{z}}{\det z}\,,\quad\mbox{with the property}\;\;\big(zz^{\prime}\big)^{-1}={z^{\prime}}^{-1}z^{-1}\,.

(A.5)

Let
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
be a generic
2\times 2
matrix with components
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
The following four equivalent expressions, properly extended in case
a,b,c
, and/or
d
are non-invertable, give the determinant of
M
in terms of the determinant of its quaternionic components:

\displaystyle\det M=\big(\det a\big)\det(d-ca^{-1}b)=\big(\det b\big)\det(c-db^{-1}a)=\big(\det c\big)\det(b-ac^{-1}d)=\big(\det d\big)\det(a-bd^{-1}c)\,.

(A.6)

The algebra of complex quaternions
\mathbb{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
is isomorphic to that of
2\times 2
-complex matrices (denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
). This isomorphism can be seen through the correspondences
(1,\boldsymbol{0})\equiv 1\mapsto\mathbbm{1}_{2}
,
\boldsymbol{e}_{1}\mapsto\mathrm{i}\sigma_{1}
,
\boldsymbol{e}_{2}\mapsto-\mathrm{i}\sigma_{2}
,
\boldsymbol{e}_{3}\mapsto\mathrm{i}\sigma_{3}
, where
\sigma_{i=1,2,3}
stands for the ordinary Pauli matrices, as:

\mathbb{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\ni z\;\mapsto\;Z(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z^{4}+\mathrm{i}z^{3}&\mathrm{i}z^{1}-z^{2}\\
\mathrm{i}z^{1}+z^{2}&z^{4}-\mathrm{i}z^{3}\end{pmatrix}\equiv Z\in\mathcal{M}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\,,\quad\det Z=\det z\,.

(A.7)

It is worth noting that the multiplicative subgroup of real quaternions
\mathbb{H}\sim{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
with unit norm is isomorphic to SU
(2)\sim\mathbb{S}^{3}
. For further elaboration on this topic, refer to Ref.
[
7
]
.

Appendix B
Matrix elements of the SU
(2)
UIRs and their holomorphic extension

B.1
Matrix elements of the SU
(2)
UIRs

Let
\xi=\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{4}+\mathrm{i}\xi^{3}&-\xi^{2}+\mathrm{i}\xi^{1}\\
\xi^{2}+\mathrm{i}\xi^{1}&\xi^{4}-\mathrm{i}\xi^{3}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SU}(2)
, that is,
\xi\equiv\big(\xi^{4},\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi^{i=1,2,3})\big)\in\mathbb{H}\sim{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
with
\det\xi=1
. Referring to the Talman notations
[
17
]
, the matrix elements of the SU
(2)
UIRs,
D^{j}
, are given by:

\begin{array}[]{lll}D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(\xi)&=&(-1)^{m_{1}-m_{2}}\sqrt{(j+m_{1})!\;(j-m_{1})!\;(j+m_{2})!\;(j-m_{2})!}\\[8.5359pt]
&&\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{t}\frac{(\xi^{4}+\mathrm{i}\xi^{3})^{j-m_{2}-t}}{(j-m_{2}-t)!}\,\frac{(\xi^{4}-\mathrm{i}\xi^{3})^{j+m_{1}-t}}{(j+m_{1}-t)!}\,\frac{(-\xi^{2}+\mathrm{i}\xi^{1})^{t+m_{2}-m_{1}}}{(t+m_{2}-m_{1})!}\frac{(\xi^{2}+\mathrm{i}\xi^{1})^{t}}{t!}\,,\end{array}

(B.1)

where
j\in\mathbb{N}/2
,
-j\leqslant m_{1},m_{2}\leqslant j
, and
t
(the values summed over) are such that the factorial functions remain non-negative, naturally compatible with
m_{1}-m_{2}\leqslant t\leqslant j+m_{1}
and
0\leqslant t\leqslant j-m_{2}
.

There are two equivalent formulas for the reduction of the tensor product of two SU
(2)
UIRs, containing the so-called
3-j
symbols (
\propto
Clebsch-Gordan coefficients):

\begin{array}[]{lll}D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(\xi)\,D^{j^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}_{1}m^{\prime}_{2}}(\xi)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}_{1}m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}(2j^{\prime\prime}+1)\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m_{1}&m^{\prime}_{1}&m^{\prime\prime}_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m_{2}&m^{\prime}_{2}&m^{\prime\prime}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,\overline{D^{j^{\prime\prime}}_{m^{\prime\prime}_{1}m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}(\xi)}\,,\\[11.38092pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}_{1}m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}(2j^{\prime\prime}+1)(-1)^{m^{\prime\prime}_{1}-m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}\,\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m_{1}&m^{\prime}_{1}&-m^{\prime\prime}_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m_{2}&m^{\prime}_{2}&-m^{\prime\prime}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,D^{j^{\prime\prime}}_{m^{\prime\prime}_{1}m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}(\xi)\,.\end{array}

(B.2)

Moreover, for any three representations there exists the following relation:

\int_{SU(2)}D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(\xi)\,D^{j^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}_{1}m^{\prime}_{2}}(\xi)\,D^{j^{\prime\prime}}_{m^{\prime\prime}_{1}m^{\prime\prime}_{2}}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)=2\pi^{2}\,\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m_{1}&m^{\prime}_{1}&m^{\prime\prime}_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m_{2}&m^{\prime}_{2}&m^{\prime\prime}_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(B.3)

On the other hand, the following expression for the
3-j
symbols of Wigner, in the convention that they are all real, holds:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}j&j^{\prime}&j^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-j^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{(j+j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime})!\;(j-j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime})!\;(-j+j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime})!}{(j+j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}+1)!}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sqrt{(j+m)!\;(j-m)!\;(j^{\prime}+m^{\prime})!\;(j^{\prime}-m^{\prime})!\;(j^{\prime\prime}+m^{\prime\prime})!\;(j^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime\prime})!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s}(-1)^{s}\frac{1}{s!\;(j^{\prime}+m^{\prime}-s)!\;(j-m-s)!\;(j^{\prime\prime}-j^{\prime}+m+s)!}

\displaystyle\mbox{\hskip 56.9055pt}\times\frac{1}{(j^{\prime\prime}-j-m^{\prime}+s)!\;(j+j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime}-s)!}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{j-j^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}}(2j^{\prime\prime}+1)^{-1/2}\,\left(jmj^{\prime}m^{\prime}|jj^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}\right)\,,

(B.4)

with the constraints:

m+m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}=0\,,\qquad|j-j^{\prime}|\leqslant j^{\prime\prime}\leqslant j+j^{\prime}\,,\qquad j+j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

(B.5)

Note that: (i) The values of
s
(over which the summation is performed) ensure that the factorial functions remain non-negative. (ii) The expression
\left(jmj^{\prime}m^{\prime}|jj^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}\right)
refers to the so-called
vector-coupling
or
Clebsch-Gordan
coefficients
[
18
]
. (iii) The above expression in the particular case
m=m^{\prime}=m^{\prime\prime}=0
turns into:

\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}l_{1}&l_{2}&l_{3}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=(-1)^{J/2}\left[\frac{(J-2l_{1})!(J-2l_{2})!(J-2l_{3})!}{(J+1)!}\right]^{1/2}\frac{\left(\frac{J}{2}\right)!}{\left(\frac{J}{2}-l_{1}\right)!\left(\frac{J}{2}-l_{2}\right)!\left(\frac{J}{2}-l_{3}\right)!}\,,\end{split}

(B.6)

where
J\equiv l_{1}+l_{2}+l_{3}
. This expression vanishes if
J
is odd.

B.2
Holomorphic extension

The holomorphic extension of the matrix elements (
B.1
) to
\mathbb{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\sim{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
(see the notations, introduced in appendix A) is as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(z)&=&\displaystyle(-1)^{m_{1}-m_{2}}\sqrt{(j+m_{1})!\;(j-m_{1})!\;(j+m_{2})!\;(j-m_{2})!}\\[8.5359pt]
&&\quad\displaystyle\times\sum_{t}\frac{(z^{4}+\mathrm{i}z^{3})^{j-m_{2}-t}}{(j-m_{2}-t)!}\,\frac{(z^{4}-\mathrm{i}z^{3})^{j+m_{1}-t}}{(j+m_{1}-t)!}\,\frac{(-z^{2}+\mathrm{i}z^{1})^{t+m_{2}-m_{1}}}{(t+m_{2}-m_{1})!}\frac{(z^{2}+\mathrm{i}z^{1})^{t}}{t!}\,,\end{array}

(B.7)

where
z=\big(z^{4},\boldsymbol{z}=(z^{i=1,2,3})\big)\in\mathbb{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
The matrix elements
D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(z)
are harmonic polynomials, i.e.,

\sum_{a=1}^{4}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial(z^{a})^{2}}D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(z)=0\,.

(B.8)

In this context, three key expansion identities are (
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
):

\displaystyle\sigma_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{j}\,D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(z+z^{\prime})=\sum_{j^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{1}m^{\prime}_{2}}\sigma_{m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1}\,m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2}}^{j-j^{\prime}}\,D_{m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1}\,m_{2}-m^{\prime}_{2}}^{j-j^{\prime}}(z)\,\sigma_{m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}}^{j^{\prime}}\,D_{m^{\prime}_{1}m^{\prime}_{2}}^{j^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})\,,

(B.9)

\displaystyle\left(\sigma_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{j}\right)^{-1}\,\big[\det(z+z^{\prime})\big]^{-1}\,D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}\left((z+z^{\prime})^{-1}\right)=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{j^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{1}m^{\prime}_{2}}(-1)^{2j^{\prime}}\sigma_{m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2}}^{j^{\prime}}\,D_{m^{\prime}_{1}m^{\prime}_{2}}^{j^{\prime}}(z)\,\left(\sigma_{m_{1}+m^{\prime}_{2}\,m_{2}+m^{\prime}_{1}}^{j+j^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}\,(\det z^{\prime})^{-1}\,D_{m_{1}+m^{\prime}_{2}\,m_{2}+m^{\prime}_{1}}^{j+j^{\prime}}({z^{\prime}}^{-1})\,,\quad\det z<\det z^{\prime}\,,

(B.10)

\displaystyle D^{j}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(zz^{\prime})=\sum_{m^{\prime}}D^{j}_{m_{1}m^{\prime}}(z)\,D^{j}_{m^{\prime}m_{2}}(z^{\prime})\,,

(B.11)

where:

\displaystyle\sigma_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{j}=\sigma_{m_{1}}^{j}\,\sigma_{m_{2}}^{j}\,,\quad\mbox{with}\quad\sigma_{m}^{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(j-m)!\,(j+m)!}}\,.

(B.12)

Note that: (i) The indices to be summed over are chosen from the allowed ranges corresponding to the indices of the SU
(2)
representations. (ii) The first two expansions are addition theorems, while the latter arises directly from the group representation. Detailed explanations can be found in Ref.
[
19
]
.

The connection between
\mathcal{Y}_{lm}(\boldsymbol{z})
, the holomorphic extension of the solid spherical harmonics, and the matrix elements of the SU
(2)
UIRs is established as
[
12
]
:

\big(\sigma^{l}_{m}\big)^{-1}\,\mathcal{Y}_{lm}(\boldsymbol{z})=2^{-l}\left(\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m_{1}m_{2}}}{{m_{2}-m_{1}=m}}}(-1)^{m_{1}}\;\big(\sigma^{l/2}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\big)^{-1}\;D^{l/2}_{m_{1}m_{2}}[(z^{4},\boldsymbol{z})]\,,

(B.13)

where the value of
z^{4}
is arbitrary. Conversely, we have:

\begin{split}{D}^{l/2}_{m_{1}m_{2}}[(0,\boldsymbol{z})]\underbrace{\equiv\;\;{D}^{l/2}_{m_{1}m_{2}}(\boldsymbol{z})\;\;}_{\mbox{by abuse of notations!}}&=(4\pi)^{1/2}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l^{\prime}}}{{l-l^{\prime}=\mathrm{even}}}}(-1)^{l^{\prime}+m_{2}}\;2^{l^{\prime}}\;(2l^{\prime}+1)^{1/2}\left(\frac{(l-l^{\prime})!}{(l+l^{\prime}+1)!}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\left[\frac{1}{2}(l+l^{\prime})\right]!}{\left[\frac{1}{2}(l-l^{\prime})\right]!}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\times\begin{pmatrix}\frac{l}{2}&\frac{l}{2}&l^{\prime}\\
m_{1}&-m_{2}&m^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z})^{\frac{l-l^{\prime}}{2}}\mathcal{Y}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{z})\,.\end{split}

(B.14)

Appendix C
A useful expansion formula

We begin by recalling the generating function for Gegenbauer polynomials
[
20
]
:

(1+u^{2}-2ut)^{-\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}u^{l}\,C_{l}^{\lambda}(t)\,,\quad|u|<1\,,\quad\lambda\neq 0\,.

(C.15)

Note that, for negative integer values of
\lambda
, the left-hand side of the above identity becomes a finite sum:

\displaystyle(1+u^{2}-2ut)^{-\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{2|\lambda|}u^{l}\,C_{l}^{\lambda}(t)\,.

(C.16)

Utilizing the first identity, we expand
\left[\det\left(1+\boldsymbol{z}\,\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}\right)\right]^{-\lambda}
:

\displaystyle\left[\det\left(1+\boldsymbol{z}\,\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}\right)\right]^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left[1-2\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}+\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left[1-2\left[\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{1/2}\frac{\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}}{\left[\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{1/2}}+\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{l/2}\,C_{l}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}}{\left[\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{1/2}}\right)\,.

(C.17)

The square root of the holomorphic quadratic
\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)
mentioned above is understood to lie on the first Riemann sheet.

Next, we employ the expansion of Gegenbauer polynomials
[
20
]
, expressed in relation to the holomorphic extensions of the solid spherical harmonics (for the latter, see Eq. (
II.18
)):

\displaystyle C_{l}^{\lambda}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\lambda)\;\Gamma\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}d_{k}\,C_{l-2k}^{1/2}(t)\,,\qquad d_{k}=\frac{\left(l-2k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\Gamma\left(k+\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\Gamma(\lambda+l-k)}{k!\,\Gamma\left(l-k+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\,,

(C.18)

\displaystyle\left[\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{l/2}\,C_{l}^{1/2}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}}{\left[\left(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}\right)\overline{\left(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\right)}\right]^{1/2}}\right)=\frac{4\pi}{2l+1}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\mathcal{Y}_{lm}(\boldsymbol{z})\,\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{lm}(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime})}\,.

(C.19)

Having in mind the functional relation of the Gamma functions,
\Gamma(x+1)=x\,\Gamma(x)
, we may also introduce the following expansions:

\Gamma\big(k+\lambda-{1}/{2}\big)=(\lambda-1/2)_{k}\,\Gamma\big(\lambda-{1}/{2}\big)\,,\quad\Gamma\big(\lambda+l-k\big)=(\lambda)_{l-k}\,\Gamma(\lambda)\,,

(C.20)

where
(x)_{k}
is the Pochhammer symbol:

(x)_{k}:=x(x-1)\dots(x+k-1)\,,\qquad(x)_{0}:=1\,.

(C.21)

Then, we have also an alternative form of Eq. (
C.18
) as:

C_{l}^{\lambda}(t)=\displaystyle{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}d^{\prime}_{k}\,C_{l-2k}^{1/2}(t)\,,

(C.22)

with:

d^{\prime}_{k}=\displaystyle\frac{\left(l-2k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,(\lambda-1/2)_{k}\,(\lambda)_{l-k}}{k!\,\Gamma\left(l-k+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\,

(C.23)

Unlike the previous identity (
C.18
), the omission of terms such as
\Gamma(\lambda-1/2)
and
\Gamma(\lambda)
in the latter permits consideration for negative values of
\lambda
as well.

Now, as we combine Eqs. (
C.17
), (
C.18
), and (
C.19
), while invoking the
Legendre duplication formula
:

\displaystyle\Gamma(x)\;\Gamma(x+1/2)=2^{1-2x}\sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma(2x)\,,

(C.24)

the subsequent expansion (applicable for
\lambda\neq 0
) emerges prominently:

\displaystyle\left[\det\left(1+\boldsymbol{z}\,\overline{\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}}\right)\right]^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{m=2k-l}^{l-2k}\,a_{\lambda,l,k}\,(\boldsymbol{z}\cdot\boldsymbol{z})^{k}\,\mathcal{Y}_{l-2k,m}(\boldsymbol{z})\,\overline{(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime})}^{k}\,\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{l-2k,m}(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime})}\,,

(C.25)

\displaystyle a_{\lambda,l,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2\lambda-1}\,\pi\,\Gamma\left(k+\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma(\lambda+l-k)}{\Gamma(2\lambda-1)\,k!\,\Gamma\left(l-k+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\,.

(C.26)

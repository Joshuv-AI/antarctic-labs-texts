Nonlinear Dynamical Unbalanced Optimal Transport: Relaxation and Duality
Dongjun Wu, Anders Rantzer

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a generalized dynamical unbalanced optimal transport framework by incorporating limited control input and mass dissipation, addressing limitations in conventional optimal transport for control applications. We derive a convex dual of the problem using dual optimal control techniques developed before and during the 1990s, transforming the non-convex optimization into a more tractable form. At the core of this formulation is the smooth sub-solutions to an HJB equation. A first-order algorithm based on the dual formulation is proposed to solve the problem numerically.



I
Introduction

Optimal transport (OT) has emerged as a powerful mathematical framework
with growing interest in the control community
[
1
,
2
]
,
particularly for its ability to model and optimize the transportation
of resources, distributions, or probabilities in a variety of applications
such as robotic swarms
[
3
]
,
motion planning
[
4
]
and single-cell analysis

[
5
]
.

Despite its successes, conventional OT, rooted in Monge’s
formulation and Kantorovich’s relaxation, assumes mass
conservation, meaning the total mass of the initial and target measures
must be equal. This assumption limits its applicability in scenarios
where mass changes occur during the transport process. For example,
in the transmission of electricity through a grid, energy losses due
to resistance lead to mass dissipation; in irrigation systems, evaporation
or leakage causes mass reduction; and in biological systems, cell
growth or decay introduces mass creation or destruction. These cases
highlight a critical limitation of classical OT: its inability to
handle mass variations, which are prevalent in control applications.
To overcome this limitation, extensions to incorporate mass creation
and dissipation have been proposed in the literature
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
We adopt the term “unbalanced optimal transport” (UOT) for such
extended OT problems. UOT often uses partial differential equations
(PDEs) to govern the evolution of measures with varying total mass,
as seen in the dynamical formulation of Hellinger-Kantorovich distance
(HK)
[
10
]
or Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WF) metric

[
6
]
. While UOT has expanded
the scope of OT to dynamical systems with mass variation, existing
formulations typically assume unconstrained control inputs and mass
dissipation, which may not reflect practical constraints (both dynamical
and static) in control problems. For instance, in robotic swarms,
nonlinear dynamics are present and control inputs (e.g., torques and
velocities) are often bounded by physical limits, and in biological
systems, mass changes (e.g., growth rates) are constrained by environmental
or biochemical factors.

In this paper, we propose a generalized formulation of dynamical UOT
that explicitly accounts for systems dynamics, constrained control
inputs and constrained mass changes. The primary goal of this work
is to derive a convex duality for this generalized UOT problem, transforming
the nonlinear optimization into a dual form that is more tractable
for analysis and computation. To achieve this, we leverage techniques
from classical dual optimal control theory, that have been developed
before and during the 1990s
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
Specifically, we employ the concept of relaxed control to lift the
problem into a convex setting. This approach not only establishes
a duality result but also enables the development of a first-order
numerical algorithm to solve the problem.

Our contributions provide a flexible framework for control problems
involving mass variation, with potential applications in energy systems,
fluid dynamics, and biological modeling.

Problem setting

Let
X
be a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the following
system described by a partial differential equation (PDE) in variable

\mu(t,x)

\partial_{t}\mu+\nabla\cdot(\mu f(t,x,u)=g(t,x,u)\mu

(1)

\mu(0,x)=\mu_{0}(x),\quad\mu(1,x)=\mu_{1}(x)

(2)

The unknown variable
\mu(t,\cdot)
is a one-parameter non-negative
Borel measure, i.e.,
\mu(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)

supported in

f(t,x,\cdot)

and function

are given and
u(t,x)

to achieve certain optimal criteria. We call

For convenience, denote

The equation (
1

sense. Namely, the following identity holds for all

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{X}(\partial_{t}\varphi+\nabla\varphi\cdot f+g\varphi){\rm d}\mu(t,x){\rm d}t=\int_{X}\varphi(1,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{1}-\varphi(0,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{0}.

We shall call (
1
) the
system
, which

describes how an initial measure

under the flow of the system

\dot{x}=f(t,x,u(t,x))

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}\mu(t,x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{X}-\nabla\cdot(\mu f)+g\mu{\rm d}x=\int_{X}g\mu{\rm d}x.

If

\mu(t,x)
is the pushforward measure of

(resp.
\leq 0

the total mass is monotone increasing (resp. decreasing).

With (
1

2

\min_{u,\mu}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{X}L(t,x,u){\rm d}\mu(t,x){\rm d}t

\mu(t,\cdot)=X_{u}(t,\cdot)_{\#}\left(\mu_{0}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}g(s,X_{u}(s,\cdot),u(t,X_{u}(s,\cdot)))\right)\right)

[

\mu(t,x)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}g(s,x(s))ds}\delta_{x(t)}(x)

III
Relaxation

Problem
1
is an optimal control problem in measure space.
In classical optimal control theory in finite dimensional space, convex
duality theories have been systematically developed to turn the nonlinear
and non-convex problem into infinite dimensional linear programs (hence
convex), see for example, L. C. Young
[
11
]
, W.
Fleming and D. Vermes
[
12
]
and
R. Vinter
[
13
]
, see also A. Rantzer and P. A. Parrilo

[
15
]
. For balanced dynamical optimal transport,
some duality theories have also been established in the literature,
see for example
[
16
,
17
]
.
Duality results for unbalanced dynamical optimal transport
(UDOT) have been established by L. Chizat et. al.
[
6
,
18
,
19
]
and M. Liero et. al.
[
10
]
.
It was shown that the interpolating Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao
metric (see Remark
1
)
WF_{\delta}
can be formulated
as

WF_{\delta}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2}=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{1}([0,1]\times X)}\int\varphi(1,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{1}-\varphi(0,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{0}

where
\varphi
is such that

\partial_{t}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\left(|\partial_{x}\varphi|^{2}+\frac{\varphi^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)\leq 0.

(12)

This duality was obtained based on the fact that the control

v
and dissipation
w
can be chosen freely. It is an open question
whether similar results hold when the control and dissipation are
constrained as in Problem
1
. The goal of this paper is to
derive the dual of Problem
1
. We demonstrate that, by leveraging
techniques from classical dual optimal control theory, a similar duality
result does hold.

The key procedure in the construction of classical dual optimal control is to
lift the problem into a space composed by the so-called Young measures

[
11
]
, or “relaxed arcs”
[
13
]
.
For example, in
[

]

\dot{x}\in\Omega

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{X}\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x}\phi\cdot v+\phi w{\rm d}\mu(t,x){\rm d}t

IV
Duality

In this section, we work toward a convex duality of Problem
3
. We shall prove that
Problem
3
admits the following dual formulation.

Problem 4
(Dual problem)

\sup_{\phi}\int_{X}\phi(1,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{1}-\phi(0,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{0}

where
\phi
is taken over the
C^{1}
solutions to the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman

\partial_{t}\phi+H(t,x,\partial_{x}\phi,\phi)\leq 0

H(t,x,p,p_{0}):=\sup_{(v,w)\in F(t,x)}\{p^{\top}v+p_{0}w-L(t,x,v,w)\}.

Below the main result of this paper, whose proof is deferred to the Appendix:

Theorem 1

The optimal values of Problems
1
,
3

and
4
are equal.

Now the dual formulation of

(see (

\displaystyle H(t,x,p,p_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup_{(v,w)}\left\{p^{\top}v+p_{0}w-\left(\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}w^{2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(|p|^{2}+\frac{p_{0}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)

Let

V
A Numerical Algorithm

We propose a numerical algorithm similar to the one proposed in
[
20
]

based on the dual formulation Problem
4
. To streamline
the exposition, we introduce some notations below

\displaystyle G(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\phi(0,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{0}-\phi(1,\cdot){\rm d}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle K_{H}

\displaystyle\;=\{(a,b,c)\in C(D;\mathbb{R}^{n+2}):a+H(t,x,b,c)\leq 0\}

Then, Problem
4
can be written as

\sup_{q,\phi}-G(\phi)-I_{K_{H}}(q)

subject to the constraint
q=(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{x}\phi,\phi)
.
We introduce a Lagrangian multiplier
\lambda:D\to\mathbb{R}^{n+2}

to accommodate this equality constraint:

\sup_{q,\phi}\inf_{\lambda}-G(\phi)-I_{K_{H}}(q)-\left\langle\lambda,q-\delta(\phi)\right\rangle

where
\delta
is the operator

\delta(\phi)=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{t}\phi\\
\partial_{x}\phi\\
\phi\end{bmatrix}.

Let

\mathcal{L}(\phi,q,\lambda):=G(\phi)+I_{K_{H}}(q)+\left\langle\lambda,\delta(\phi)-q\right\rangle

then we shall study
\inf_{q,\phi}\sup_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}(\phi,q,\lambda)

or its augmented Lagrangian form

\inf_{q,\phi}\sup_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{r}(\phi,q,\lambda)

where
\mathcal{L}_{r}(\phi,q,\lambda)=\mathcal{L}(\phi,q,\lambda)+\frac{r}{2}\left\|\delta(\phi)-q\right\|^{2}
.
The norm
\|\cdot\|
is defined as
\|v\|^{2}=\int_{D}|v(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}t{\rm d}x
.

Given
(\phi^{0},q^{0},\lambda^{1})
, iterate for
k\geq 1

1.

Compute
\phi^{k}\in\arg\min_{\phi}\mathcal{L}_{r}(\phi,q^{k-1},\lambda^{k})
;

2.

Compute
q^{k}\in\arg\min_{q}\mathcal{L}_{r}(\phi^{k},q,\lambda^{k})
;

3.

\lambda^{k+1}=\lambda^{k}+r(\delta(\phi^{k})-q^{k})
.

Algorithm 1

Augmented Lagrangian Method

In Step 1),
\phi^{k}
can be obtained by solving the second order
PDE

-r(\Delta_{t,x}\phi-\phi)=\xi^{k}

where

\displaystyle\xi^{k}

\displaystyle=-r(\partial_{t}a^{k-1}+\nabla_{x}\cdot b^{k-1}-c^{k-1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\nabla_{x}\cdot\lambda_{b}^{k}+\partial_{t}\lambda_{a}^{k}-\partial_{t}\lambda_{c}^{k}

with pure Neumann boundary conditions

\displaystyle r\partial_{t}\phi(1,\cdot)

\displaystyle=ra^{k-1}(1,\cdot)+\mu_{1}-\lambda_{a}^{k}(1,\cdot)

\displaystyle r\partial_{t}\phi(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=ra^{k-1}(0,\cdot)+\mu_{0}-\lambda_{a}^{k}(0,\cdot)

and
\partial_{x}\phi=0
on
\partial X
. Note we have denoted
\lambda^{k}=(\lambda_{a}^{k},\lambda_{b}^{k},\lambda_{c}^{k})

and
q^{k}=(a^{k},b^{k},c^{k})
.
To obtain this, differentiating
\mathcal{L}_{r}
w.r.t.
\phi
:

G(\varphi)+\left\langle\lambda^{k},\delta(\varphi)\right\rangle+r\left\langle\delta(\phi)-q^{k-1},\delta(\varphi)\right\rangle=0

(20)

which should hold for all
\varphi
. Easy calculations show that

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda^{k},\delta(\varphi)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\int\lambda_{a}^{k}\varphi_{t}+\lambda_{b}^{k}\cdot\varphi_{x}+\lambda_{c}^{k}\varphi{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int(\lambda_{a}^{k})_{t}\varphi{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int\lambda_{a}^{k}(1,\cdot)\varphi(1,\cdot)-\lambda_{a}^{k}(0,\cdot)\varphi(0,\cdot){\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\int(\nabla_{x}\lambda_{b}^{k})\varphi{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{\partial X}\varphi\lambda_{b}^{k}\cdot dS

\displaystyle+\int\lambda_{c}^{k}\varphi{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

and

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta(\phi)-q^{k-1},\delta(\varphi)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int r(\phi_{tt}+\phi_{xx}-\phi-a_{t}^{k-1}-\nabla_{x}\cdot b^{k-1}+c^{k-1})\varphi{\rm d}t{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int\varphi(1,\cdot)(\phi_{t}(1,\cdot)-a^{k-1}(1,\cdot)){\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\int\varphi(0,\cdot)(\phi_{t}(0,\cdot)-a^{k-1}(0,\cdot)){\rm d}x

Substituting these into (

) gives the desired result.

Step 2) amounts to the pointwise minimization

\displaystyle q^{k}

\displaystyle=\arg\inf_{q\in K_{H}}\frac{r}{2}|q-\delta(\phi^{k})|^{2}+\left\langle\lambda^{k},\delta(\phi^{k})-q\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\arg\inf_{q\in K_{H}}\frac{r}{2}|q|^{2}-\left\langle q,\lambda^{k}+r\delta(\phi^{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\arg\inf_{q\in K_{H}}\left|q-(\lambda^{k}/r+\delta(\phi^{k}))\right|^{2}.

VI
Conclusion

We have proposed a generalized dynamical unbalanced optimal transport (UOT)
framework with constrained control and mass changes. By deriving its
convex duality using classical dual optimal control techniques, a
first-order numerical algorithm has been proposed. Our approach enhances
UOT’s applicability to control problems like energy
systems and biological modeling. Future work includes proposing more
efficient numerical algorithms to handle the dimensionality issue
associated with the solution of the HJB.

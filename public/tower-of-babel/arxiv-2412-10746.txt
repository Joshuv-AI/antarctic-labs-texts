Finite asymptotic dimension and the coarse assembly map
Ulrich Bunke

Abstract
In this note we give a simple argument for the fact that the coarse assembly map for a strong coarse homology theory with weak transfers and a bornological coarse space of weakly finite homotopical asymptotic dimension is a phantom equivalence.



1
Introduction

It is a classical result in coarse geometry that the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture holds for proper metric spaces
of finite asymptotic dimension
[
Yu98
]
. The proof given in
[
Yu98
]
uses the analytic details of the
construction of coarse
K
-homology in terms of
C^{*}
-algebras.
In order to decouple the internal details of the construction of coarse homology theories from the verification that the coarse assembly map is an equivalence in
[
BE20b
]
we introduced the formalism of bornological coarse spaces and coarse homology theories. Using this formalism and axiomatizing an argument of
[
Wri05
]
it was shown in
[
BE20a
]
that the coarse assembly map for any strong coarse homology theory and a bornological coarse space of weakly finite asymptotic dimension is an equivalence. Applied to the case of the coarse
K
-homology this recovers the result of
[
Yu98
]
using arguments in coarse homotopy theory. The most technical part of the argument in
[
BE20a
]
is the verification
of the flasqueness of the coarsening space
[
BE20b
, Thm. 5.55]
.

The goal of this note is to present a proof for the fact that the coarse assembly map
for a strong coarse homology theory with weak transfers and
a bornological coarse space of weakly finite homotopical asymptotic dimension is a phantom equivalence.
It avoids the usage of
[
BE20b
, Thm. 5.55]
, but requires the additional assumption of the existence of weak transfers on the coarse homology theory, which is fortunately satisfied by all examples of coarse homology theories of
K
-theoretic nature (topological or algebraic). Also the statement is slightly weaker as we only show that the coarse assembly is a phantom equivalence.
On the other side, our argument allows to weaken the assumption on
X

from weakly finite asymptotic dimension to weakly finite homotopical asymptotic dimension.

In
Section

2
we recall the construction of the coarse assembly map from
[
BE20a
]

and state the main result.
In the
Section

3
we introduce the notion of weakly finite homotopical asymptotic dimension
and prove the main theorem.

These notes grew out of an attempt to present a complete proof of the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture
under the finite asymptotic dimension assumption in a lecture course on coarse homotopy theory at the University of Regensburg in fall 2024.

The coarse assembly map

In this section we introduce the coarse assembly map and state the main theorem.
We will assume familiarity with the categories
\mathbf{BC}
of bornological coarse spaces and
\mathbf{UBC}
of uniform bornological coarse spaces

[
BE20b
]
,
[
BE20a
]
and the respective notions of homology theories. A coarse homology theory
is a functor
E:\mathbf{BC}\to{\mathcal{C}}
to a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
which is coarsely invariant, excisive,
u
-continuous and vanishes on flasques, see
[
BE20b
, Sec. 4.4]
for details. As explained in
[

there exists a universal

\mathrm{Yo}:\mathbf{BC}\to\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}

such that the pull-back along
\mathrm{Yo}
identifies

{\mathcal{C}}
-valued coarse homology theories with colimit-preserving functors
{\mathbf{Fun}}^{\operatorname*{\mathtt{colim}}}(\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{C}})
.
We will use the same name for the coarse homology theory and the corresponding colimit-preserving functor.

A coarse homology theory is called strong if it in addition annihilates weakly flasque bornological coarse spaces
[
BEKW20b
, Def. 4.19]
. Likewise there exists a universal strong coarse homology theory

\mathrm{Yo}^{\mathrm{str}}:\mathbf{BC}\to\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{str}}\ .

Since every strong coarse homology theory is in particular a coarse homology theory we have a canonical
colimit-preserving functor

\iota^{\mathrm{str}}:\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{str}}

\mathrm{Yo}^{\mathrm{str}}

A local homology theory is a functor
F:\mathbf{UBC}\to{\mathcal{C}}
to a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
which is homotopy invariant, (closed)-excisive,
u
-continuous and vanishes on flasques
[
BE20a
, Def. 3.12]

\mathrm{Yo}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathbf{UBC}\to\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{B}}

such that the pull-back along
\mathrm{Yo}^{\mathrm{loc}}
identifies

{\mathcal{C}}
-valued local homology theories with colimit-preserving functors
{\mathbf{Fun}}^{\operatorname*{\mathtt{colim}}}(\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}})
.
We will use again same name for the local homology theory and the corresponding colimit-preserving functor.

The categories of uniform bornological coarse spaces and bornological coarse spaces are related by a forgetful functor

\iota:\mathbf{UBC}\to\mathbf{BC}\ .

(2.1)

The compositions

3
Weakly finite homotopical asymptotic dimension

Let
W
be a simplicial complex which we consider as a uniform bornological coarse space with coarse and uniform structure induced by the spherical path metric and some compatible bornology. We further consider
a big family

{\mathcal{V}}:=(V_{n})_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of subcomplexes
with the property that every bounded subset of
W
is contained in
V_{n}
for some
n
in
{\rm Nature}
. We let
W_{h}
be the bornological coarse space obtained from
W
by equipping it with the hybrid coarse structure
[
BE20b
, Def. 5.10]
associated to the big family
{\mathcal{V}}
.
By definition a coarse entourage of
W
belongs to the hybrid structure if for every uniform entourage
Z

of
W
there exists an
n
in
{\rm Nature}
such that
U\cap(W\setminus V_{n})^{2}\subseteq Z
.
Note that the bornological coarse structure induced by
W_{h}
on
V_{n}
coincides with the bornological coarse structure induced by
W
.

We let
\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}\langle\mathrm{disc}\rangle
denote the localizing subcategory of
\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}
generated by
\mathrm{Yo}(X)
for all bornological coarse spaces

X
with the discrete coarse structure. We let

\mathrm{Yo}(W_{h},{\mathcal{V}}):=\operatorname*{\mathtt{colim}}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}\mathrm{Cofib}(\mathrm{Yo}(V_{n})\to\mathrm{Yo}(W_{h}))\ .

(3.1)

The following has been shown in
[
BE20b
, Thm. 5.57]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\dim(W)<\infty
, then we have

\mathrm{Yo}(W_{h},{\mathcal{V}})\in\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}\langle\mathrm{disc}\rangle
.

Proof.

The idea is to argue by induction on the dimension of
W
.
If
\dim(W)=0
, then
W_{h}
is discrete and there is nothing to show.
If
\dim(W)=k
, then we write
W
as a union of a thickening of the
k-1
-skeleton
and a disjoint collection of
k
-simplices, see the pictures on
[
BE20b
, p. 80]
. The decomposition theorem
[
BE20b
, Thm. 5.22]

presents
\mathrm{Yo}(W_{h},{\mathcal{V}})
as a push-out. The thickenings of the
k-1
-skeleton and the intersection
are homotopy equivalent in
\mathbf{UBC}
to
k-1
-dimensional simplicial complexes, and the collection of
k
-simplices is homotopy equivalent to the discrete uniform space
B
of barycenters. We then use the homotopy theorem
[
BE20b
, 5.26]
in order to invoke the induction hypothesis and the following observation.

The coarse structure induced on the set
B
of barycenters is not the discrete one. But the relative
coarse homology
\mathrm{Yo}(B,{\mathcal{V}}\cap B)
is actually coarsening invariant, i.e., it does not depend on the coarse structure as long as the latter is compatible with the uniform structure. Since
B
has the discrete uniform structure we
can take the discrete coarse structure on
B
as well and then conclude that
\mathrm{Yo}(B,{\mathcal{V}}\cap B)\in\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}\langle\mathrm{disc}\rangle
.
∎

Let
{\mathbf{E}}
be any strong coarse homology theory.

Lemma 3.2
.

E{\mathcal{O}}\mathbf{P}
vanishes on
\mathbf{Sp}{\mathcal{X}}\langle\mathrm{disc}\rangle

Proof.

It suffices to show that

for all discrete bornological coarse spaces
X
.
Then
\mathbf{P}(X)
is the uniform bornological coarse space
X_{\mathrm{disc}}
obtained from

by equipping it with the discrete uniform structure.
We have an isomorphism
{\mathcal{O}}(X_{\mathrm{disc}})\cong X\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(*)
. Since the latter bornological coarse space is flasque we get
E{\mathcal{O}}\mathbf{P}(X)\simeq E({\mathcal{O}}(X_{\mathrm{disc}}))\simeq E(X\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(*))\simeq 0
.
∎

Note that

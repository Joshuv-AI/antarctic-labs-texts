JWST Spectroscopic View of a Rapidly Growing Dust-obscured Quasar at z ∼ 4 z\sim 4 : Effect of Dust Extinction Correction on Black Hole Mass and Eddington Ratio Estimation
Dohyeong Kim, Myungshin Im

Abstract
In the merger-driven galaxy evolution scenario, the central supermassive black holes (SMBHs) in dust obscured galaxies grow rapidly. Interestingly, a recent work [ 49 ] on a dust-obscured galaxy, LID-568 at z = 3.965 z=3.965 , has shown that its SMBH is growing extremely fast at about 40 times of the Eddington-limited accretion rate (i.e., super-Eddington accretion). However, the heavy dust extinction of the host galaxy could affect the result if not corrected properly. Here, we analyze James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) NIRSpec/IFU and MIRI spectra of LID-568. By measuring its bolometric luminosity ( L bol L_{\rm bol} ) and BH mass ( M BH M_{\rm BH} ) using an extinction-free estimator based on mid-infrared spectra, we obtain L bol = 10 46.83 ± 0.07 ​ erg ​ s − 1 L_{\rm bol}=10^{46.83\pm 0.07}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}} and M BH = 10 8.43 ± 0.15 ​ M ⊙ M_{\rm BH}=10^{8.43\pm 0.15}\,M_{\odot} . The measured Eddington ratio ( λ Edd \lambda_{\rm Edd} ) is 1.97 ± \pm 0.88, consistent with the accretion rate at the Eddington limit; in other words, not in super-Eddington in a significant manner. This result underscores challenges and the importance of carefully considering dust extinction when analyzing the BH growth in dust-obscured quasars.



I
Introduction

SMBHs have been found at the centers of spheroidal galaxies,
with their masses tightly correlating with several properties of their host galaxies
(e.g., bulge mass, stellar velocity dispersion, stellar mass, etc.;
Ferrarese & Merritt 11
,
Gebhardt et al. 14
,
Kormendy & Ho 35
).
It is uncertain how SMBHs were born and grew,
but several simulation studies (e.g.,
Hopkins et al. 22
,
Hopkins et al. 23
) suggest that
dust-obscured quasars are a key to understand the SMBH growth process.
According to such studies, dust-obscured quasars represent the intermediate stage between
the merger-driven star-forming galaxies and unobscured quasars.
In this merger-driven galaxy evolution scenario,
the SMBHs in dust-obscured quasars are expected to be growing very rapidly,
sometimes in well-above the Eddington-limited accretion rate (super-Eddington accretion; e.g.,
Hopkins et al. 23
).
Several observational studies (e.g.,
Urrutia et al. 52
,
Kim et al. 27
,
Kim et al. 34
) find that dust-obscured quasars are
accreting matters at near the Eddington limit, supporting this scenario.
Moreover, further support for the merger-driven galaxy evolution scenario comes from
several pieces of observational evidence.
For example, dust-obscured quasars exhibit
(i) significant merging signatures in their host galaxies
[
51
,
16
]
;
(ii) dusty red colors
[
28
]
;
(iii) merging SMBH candidates
[
30
]
;
and (iv) enhanced star-forming activities
[
15
]
.
A few studies have found high redshift dust-obscured quasars with the Eddington ratios of a few
[
50
,
12
,
25
,
33
]
.
However, large scatters in scaling relations and uncertainties in dust-extinction correction make it difficult
to conclusively claim that such quasars are undergoing super-Eddington accretion.
Much stronger evidence for super-Eddington accretion is therefore required.

Recently,
[
49
]
reported the discovery of a long-sought dust-obscured quasar at
z\simeq 4

that is undergoing a strong super-Eddington accretion.
They analyzed X-ray, infrared (IR), and JWST spectroscopic data to find the Eddington ratio of about 40 for this quasar, LID-568,
which is well above the Eddington ratio of one for Eddington-limited accretion.

One significant difficulty in such a study is the heavy extinction
which complicates the measurement of the black hole mass, bolometric luminosity, and Eddington ratio.

[
49
]
found that the bolometric luminosity from the 5100
\rm\AA{}
continuum luminosity is about a factor of 10 lower than the X-ray based value.
They also found an Eddington ratio to be 4.4 when using an internally consistent method of deriving the Eddington ratio from H
\alpha
line.

In this study, we measure the
L_{\rm bol}
and
M_{\rm BH}
values of LID-568
using estimators designed to minimize the effects of dust extinction
by using mid-infrared (MIR) continuum luminosity and the velocity width of the H
\alpha
line
[
32
]
.
Subsequently, we compare the newly-derived
\lambda_{\rm Edd}
to other quasars at similar redshifts.
Throughout, we use a standard
\Lambda
CDM model of

H_{0}=70\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}
Mpc
-1
,
\Omega_{m}=0.3
, and
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
,
which has been supported by several observational studies (e.g.
Im et al. 24
,
Planck Collaboration et al. 43
).

LID-568 is an dust-obscured quasar,
originally identified as a near-infrared (NIR) dropout object and later as an X-ray source
in the
\it Chandra
-COSMOS Legacy Survey
[

40

Follow-up JWST NIRSpec and MIRI observations revealed that its redshift is 3.965, and it has a very red continuum
[
49

.
Furthermore, strong and broad hydrogen lines were detected, as shown in Figure

providing a supporting evidence that LID-568 is a dust-obscured quasar.
The

M_{\rm BH}

the
\lambda_{\rm Edd}
of
\sim
40
[
49

.

Data analysis

III.1
Spectral energy distribution fitting

[
32

Here, we perform an SED fitting using the JWST NIRSpec and MIRI spectra for obtaining the

The JWST spectra [

Q(\lambda)

Here, the

[

Note that even if the Galactic dust extinction curve with

[
13

the derived

<5\,\%

Meanwhile,
[
32
]
used several types of galaxy spectra in their SED fitting, but for LID-568,
we adopt the result where only the
Q(\lambda)

We attempted the SED fitting including the galaxy spectra as done by
[
32
]
,
but the galaxy contributions to the SED are found to be negligible.
Moreover, we adopt the quasar spectrum of
[
1
]
for this work,
as it yields the best reduced chi-square fit compared to using other quasar spectra from
[
45
]
and
[

]

However, regardless of which quasar spectrum is used for the fitting,
the measured
L_{\rm 3.4}

32
]

and the discrepancy is added as an uncertainty in quadrature to
its original uncertainties obtained from the SED fit.

For the SED fitting, we use the

[

and Figure

Through the SED fitting, we obtain a color excess,

V
Summary

We reanalyzed the JWST spectra of LID-568, an intriguing dust-obscured quasar at
z\sim 4

whose SMBH has been suggested to be undergoing a super-Eddington accretion at
\lambda_{\rm Edd}\sim 40
.
However, our analysis that accounts for the heavy dust-extinction reveals that LID-568
seems undergoing Eddington-limited accretion at
\lambda_{\rm Edd}\sim 1
.
Our new
M_{\rm BH}
value is about
10^{8.43}

M_{\odot}
,
\sim
40 times higher than that suggested by
[
49
]
,
which is the reason for the discrepancy.
These results emphasize the challenges and importance of dust-extinction correction
in understanding the nature of dust-obscured quasars.
Moreover, our IR-based analysis could better constrain the nature of newly discovered high-
z
dust-obscured quasars –
often referred to as “little red dots” and found through JWST observations.

1.
Introduction

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) without global Lipschitz drift often demonstrate unusual phenomena. For instance,
[
21
,
22
,
38
,
20
,
19
]
reported that the Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme blows up, and
[
37
]
reported a Donsker-Varadhan large deviation for a family of superlinear SDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable processes with
\alpha\in(1,2)
. In this paper, we demonstrate other unusual results which reveal the existence of the normal central limit theorem (CLT) for a family of superlinear SDEs.

In this paper, we consider the following SDE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}=\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{X}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t},\quad\mathbf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\mathbf{Z}_{t}
is the rotationally symmetric
\alpha
-stable process with
\alpha\in(1,2)
under a probability base
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geqslant 0},\mathbb{P}\right)
and the following assumption holds true:

Assumption A1
.

\mathbf{b}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a differentiable function satisfying the following condition:
there exist some
\theta\geqslant 0
, and
K_{1},K_{2},L>0
, so that

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\rangle\leqslant\left\{\begin{aligned} \ \ K_{1}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{2}\ \ ,\quad&\forall\ \ |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|\leqslant L,\\
-K_{2}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{2+\theta},\quad&\forall\ \ |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|>L.\end{aligned}\right.

It is well known that there is a unique weak solution to SDE (
1.1
) and the solution is non-explosive for
\theta\geqslant 0
, see
[
13
,
29
,
28
]
for more details.
It is known (cf, e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 14.3]
, or
[
11
,
24
]
) that the Lévy measure
\nu
of the process
(\mathbf{Z}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is

\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z})=\frac{C_{d,\alpha}}{|\mathbf{z}|^{\alpha+d}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z},\quad\nu(\{\mathbf{0}\})=0,

where the constant
C_{d,\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle C_{d,\alpha}=\alpha 2^{\alpha-1}\pi^{-d/2}\frac{\Gamma((d+\alpha)/2)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)},

see e.g,
[
11
,
9
]
. By the Lévy-Itô decomposition (cf.
[
31
,
1
]
), there exists a Poisson random measure
N(\mathrm{d}{\bf z},\mathrm{d}t)
such that

\mathrm{d}{\bf Z}_{t}=\int_{|\bf z|>1}{\bf z}N({\rm d}{\bf z},{\rm d}t)+\int_{|\bf z|\leqslant 1}{\bf z}\widetilde{N}({\rm d}{\bf z},{\rm d}t),

where
\widetilde{N}({\rm d}{\bf z},{\rm d}t)=N({\rm d}{\bf z},{\rm d}t)-\nu({\rm d}{\bf z}){\rm d}t
is the compensated Poisson random measure. We refer to
[
1
,
33
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
15
,
25
]
and references therein for more developments of SDEs driven by Lévy processes.

Example 1.1
.

Given
\theta\geqslant 0
, the following SDE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies Assumption
A1
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}=-\mathbf{X}_{t}\left|\mathbf{X}_{t}\right|^{\theta}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t},\quad\mathbf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
\theta=0
, this is a linear SDE, whereas for
\theta>0
, it is a superlinear SDE.

For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
, the empirical measure
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}
associated with the process
\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}\right)_{t\geqslant 0}
in (
1.1
) is defined as:

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(A):=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{x}}}(A)\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),

where
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
denotes the collection of Borel sets on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\delta_{\mathbf{y}}(\cdot)
is the Dirac delta measure defined as
\delta_{\mathbf{y}}(A)=1
if
\mathbf{y}\in A
and
\delta_{\mathbf{y}}(A)=0
otherwise. Note that we write
\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}
instead of
\mathbf{X}_{t}
to emphasize the starting point
\mathbf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{x}
for a given
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any measurable test function
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the empirical measure satisfies:

(1.2)

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(h)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\left(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{x}}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

In this paper, we examine the existence and non-existence of the CLT for the empirical measures
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(h)
of the SDE (
1.1
) under different values of the parameter
\theta
in Assumption
A1
, using bounded measurable or Lipschitz continuous test functions. As summarized in Table
1
, our results reveal that: for the bounded measurable function
h
, the empirical measure
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(h)
admits a normal CLT for any
\theta\geqslant 0
; in contrast, for the Lipschitz continuous function
h
, different results emerge. When
\theta=0
, the empirical measure
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(h)
does not necessarily satisfy the CLT (non-CLT). However, when
\theta>1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, the CLT is indeed satisfied. This observation underscores the pivotal role of the parameter
\theta
in governing the asymptotic behavior of empirical measures, particularly with respect to the existence or non-existence of the CLT.

There has been considerable research on the central limit problem for functionals of solutions to SDEs. For SDEs driven by Brownian motion, several results have been established (see, for example,
[
3
,
34
,
27
,
17
]
). In particular, Bao et al.
[
3
]
established the asymptotic normality of integrals with the form
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}h(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{x}})\mathrm{d}s
for one class of continuous functions
h
, where
\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\bf x}
solves the Brownian SDE with dissipative and Hölder continuous drift. Bao et al.
[
4
]
established CLT for additive functionals of path-dependent SDEs.

For the central limit problem related to Lévy processes, there are also significant results, such as those in
[
30
,
14
,
39
,
26
]
, which primarily focus on the scaled average of independent random variables. In particular,
[
23
]
established the CLT for the empirical measure of piecewise
\alpha
stable Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes with a bounded measurable test function. In this paper, we investigate the central limit problem for SDEs driven by rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes, our findings reveal that for SDEs with linear and superlinear drift terms, under Lipschitz continuous test functions
h
, the limiting behaviors of the empirical measures
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(h)
are markedly different.

Furthermore, research on the non-CLT related to Lévy processes remains relatively sparse.
[
18
]
established a functional non-CLT for jump-diffusions with periodic coefficients driven by stable Lévy processes with
\alpha\in(1,2)
, where the limiting process is shown to be an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process with an averaged jump measure. This paper demonstrates that for SDEs driven by stable Lévy processes, under specific conditions on both the drift and diffusion terms, the empirical measures
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}(h)
converge to a stable distribution, as shown in proposition
3.2
, which also constitutes a significant contribution to the study.

1.1.
Notations

Throughout this paper, we use the following notations. Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
),
d\geqslant 1
, be real (nonnegative) valued
d
-dimensional vectors, and write
\mathbb{R}
(and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
) for the case when
d=1
. For any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle
means the inner product, that is,
\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle=\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{y}
where
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}
means the transpose for
\mathbf{x}
. This reduces the Euclidean metric denoted by
|\cdot|
. Given a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, its Hilbert-Schmidt norm is
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\mathbf{A}_{ij}^{2}}=\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{A}\right)}
and its operator norm is
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{|\mathbf{v}|=1}|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}|
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
be the collection of all
\mathbb{R}
-valued Borel measurable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
be the collection of all
\mathbb{R}
-valued Borel bounded measurable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
be the collection of all
\mathbb{R}
-valued differential continuous functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
be the collection of all
\mathbb{R}
-valued bounded measurable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
, denote
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(\mathbf{x})|
. For
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
,
\nabla f\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the gradient of
f
, and we denote

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{v}\rangle\colon\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\;\left\lvert\mathbf{v}\right\rvert\leqslant 1\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
be the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. For any probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, denote
\mu(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})\mu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})
. Let
\mathscr{C}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right)
denote the class of couplings of probability measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. The
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, denoted by
\mathbb{W}_{1}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right)
, is defined as,

\mathbb{W}_{1}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right):=\inf_{\Pi\in\mathscr{C}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\right).

By Kantorovich’s dual formula, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right)=\sup_{f\in\mathrm{Lip}(1)}\left|\mu_{1}(f)-\mu_{2}(f)\right|

where
\operatorname{Lip}(1)=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R};|f(\mathbf{x})-f(\mathbf{y})|\leqslant|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|,\ \forall\ {\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}
.
The total variation distance of measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, denoted by
\left\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
, is defined as,

\left\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2\mathbb{W}_{0}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right|\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\sup_{\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1}\left|\mu_{1}(f)-\mu_{2}(f)\right|.

A sequence of random variables
\left\{Y_{n},n\geqslant 1\right\}
is said to converge weakly or converge in distribution to a limit
Y_{\infty}
, denoted by
Y_{n}\Rightarrow Y_{\infty}
, if their distribution functions converge weakly. In addition,
Y_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{P}}Y_{\infty}
means convergence in probability if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|Y_{n}-Y_{\infty}\right|>\varepsilon\right)=0,\quad\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0.

The associated Markov semigroup
(\mathrm{P}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
of the SDE (
1.1
) is given by

\mathrm{P}_{t}f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{E}f\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}\right),\quad\forall\ \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right),\quad t\geqslant 0.

The infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{A}
of the process
(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is defined as follows: for
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(\mathbf{x})=\left\langle\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{x}),\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}^{d}}\left[f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{z})-f(\mathbf{x})-\langle\mathbf{z},\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\rangle\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}(|\mathbf{z}|)\right]\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}),

where
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
, and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is the domain of
\mathcal{A}
, which depends on the function space where the semigroup
(\mathrm{P}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
operates.

For a time homogeneous Markov process
(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}})_{t\geqslant 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is ergodic
[
10
]
if there exists a unique invariant probability
\pi
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\pi\|_{\rm TV}=0,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\mathcal{L}({\bf X}_{t}^{\bf x})
is the distribution of
{\bf X}_{t}^{\bf x}
.

The ergodicity can be further classified as the following categories:

(i)
V
-exponential ergodicity
: There exists some continuous function
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
such that there exist some positive constants
c,C
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{|f|\leqslant V}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right]-\pi(f)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle CV(\mathbf{x})e^{-ct},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(ii)
V
-uniform ergodicity
: There exists some continuous function
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
such that there exist some positive constants
c,C
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{|f|\leqslant V}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right]-\pi(f)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle Ce^{-ct},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

It can be shown that
\left(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right)_{t\geqslant 0}
in (
1.1
) is exponentially ergodic, we denote
\mu
by its invariant measure, and more details can be found in Lemma
2.1
. For any Lipschitz continuous test function
h
, it is easy to know that
\mu(h)<\infty
by Lemma
2.4
. Let
f_{h}
be the solution to the following Poisson equation:

(1.5)

\mathcal{A}f_{h}(\mathbf{x})=h(\mathbf{x})-\mu(h),

where
\mathcal{A}
is defined by (
1.4
) and
h
is a bounded measurable or Lipschitz continuous test function.

\mathcal{V}\left(f_{h}\right)

By Luo et al.
[

of SDE (

\mu

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t}

Lemma 2.1

(Ergodicity for the SDE (
1.1
))
.

Denote by
\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}\right)_{t\geqslant 0}
the solution to the SDE (
1.1
).

(i) For the case
\theta=0
,
\left(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right)_{t\geqslant 0}
admits a unique invariant probability measure
\mu
. Additionally, there exist some positive constants
c_{1}(\mathbf{x})
(depending on the initial point
{\bf x}
) and

\|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mu\|_{\rm TV}\leqslant c_{1}(\mathbf{x}){\rm e}^{-c_{2}t},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t>0.

(ii) For the case
\theta>0
,
\left(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right)_{t\geqslant 0}
admits a unique invariant probability measure
\mu
. Furthermore, there exists some uniformly positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
that are independent of
{\bf x}
, such that

\|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{x}}_{t})-\mu\|_{\rm TV}\leqslant c_{1}{\rm e}^{-c_{2}t},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t>0.

We define a function
V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
as:

(2.1)

V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})=(1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{{\beta}/{2}},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\beta\in[1,\alpha).

Lemma 2.2

Denote by

1\leqslant\beta<\alpha

(2.2)

\displaystyle H_{1}+H_{2}.

For the term

For the first term of (

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}^{d}}\left(V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{z})-V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})-\left\langle\nabla V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{z}\right\rangle\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}(|\mathbf{z}|)\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}}{|\mathbf{z}|^{\alpha+d}}

\displaystyle H_{2}=

\displaystyle C_{d,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}^{d}}\left(V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{z})-V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})-\left\langle\nabla V_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{z}\right\rangle\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}(|\mathbf{z}|)\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}}{|\mathbf{z}|^{\alpha+d}}

(ii) By using Itô’s formula, one has

The proof of Theorem

Before providing the proof of the main theorem, we first present the following important non-CLT for SDEs driven by rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes.

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider the solution
(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}})_{t\geqslant 0}
of the following SDE driven by a rotationally invariant
\alpha

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(3.27)

\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{g}\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t},\ \mathbf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let

be such that the solution

of SDE (
3.27

\mathbf{Z}_{1}

\mathbf{Z}_{t}
at time

Proof.

Consider the case that the SDE (
3.27
) starting from random initial point
\mathbf{y}_{0}
, that is,

(3.28)

\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}}=\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t}.

Then, by the fact that
\mathbf{Z}_{t}
is rotationally symmetric, it is easy to know that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}}=\mathbf{y}_{0}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}})\mathrm{d}s+\mathbb{E}\mathbf{Z}_{t}=\mathbf{y}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}})]\mathrm{d}s,

Let the initial point
\mathbf{y}_{0}
obey the invariant measure
\pi
of the SDE (
3.27
). Denote
m_{\pi}:=\mathbb{E}\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}}
. We have

\displaystyle m_{\pi}:=\mathbb{E}\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{y}_{0}}=m_{\pi}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}})]\pi(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}s,\quad\mathbf{y}_{0}\sim\pi.

Combining SDE (
3.27
) , we have

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}-m_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathbf{x}-m_{\pi}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{x}})ds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}})]\pi(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}s+\mathbf{Z}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\mathbf{x}-m_{\pi})+t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{x}})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}})]\pi(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\right)\mathrm{d}s+t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\mathbf{Z}_{t},

(3.29)

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{Z}_{t}\right)=t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{x}})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}})]\pi(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\right)\mathrm{d}s.

For the left-hand side of the equation (
3.29

(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\mathbf{x}})_{t\geqslant 0}
solves the SDE (
3.27
) and
\pi
is the unique invariant measure of the same SDE, it follows that

for some
\mathbf{Y}\sim\pi

\mathbf{x}
is independent of

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathbf{X}_{t}^{x}-\mathbf{x})\right)\Rightarrow 0.

(\mathbf{Z}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

\alpha

For the right-hand side of the equation (

(3.31)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{X}_{s}^{\mathbf{y}})]\pi(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\pi(\mathbf{g})\mathrm{d}s.

Combining (
3.29

3.30

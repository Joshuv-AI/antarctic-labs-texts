On tropical knapsack-type problems
I. M. Buchinskiy and M. V. Kotov and A. V. Treier

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the computational complexity of the knapsack problem and subset sum problem for the following tropical algebraic structures. We consider the semigroup of square matrices of size k × k k\times k with non-negative entries over the max-plus algebra and the semigroup square matrices of size k × k k\times k with positive entries over the max-times algebra. We prove that the knapsack problem and subset sum problem for these structures are NP -complete. We demonstrate that there are pseudo-polynomial algorithms to solve these problems. Also, we show that for the latter semigroup, there are polynomial generic algorithms to solve the knapsack problem and the subset sum problem.



1.
Introduction

The 0–1 knapsack problem, the unbounded knapsack problem, and the subset sum problem are well-known problems in combinatorial optimization.
The
0–1 knapsack problem
can be formulated as follows.

Problem 1
.

Given a number
c
and
n
items, each item
i
has a profit
p_{i}
and weight
w_{i}
, maximize
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}x_{i}
subject to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}\leq c
,
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

When the profit and the weight of each item are identical, the corresponding problem is called the
subset sum problem
:

Problem 2
.

Given a number
c
and
n
items, each item
i
has a weight
w_{i}
, maximize
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}
subject to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}\leq c
,
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Another variant is that each item can be chosen multiple times. The
unbounded knapsack problem
can be formulated as follows:

Problem 3
.

Given a number
c
and
n
items, each item
i
has a profit
p_{i}
and weight
w_{i}
, maximize
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}x_{i}
subject to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}\leq c
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Also, one can consider decision versions of such problems.

Problem 4
.

Given a number
c
and
n
items, each item
i
has weight
w_{i}
, decide if
x_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}w_{n}=c
for some
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{0,1\}
.

Problem 5
.

Given a number
c
and
n
items, each item
i
has weight
w_{i}
, decide if
x_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}w_{n}=c
for some
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

There are other variations of Problems
1
—
5
. Knapsack-type problems have been studied for many years.
Many of such problems are
\mathsf{NP}
-complete, but often there are pseudo-polynomial algorithms based on dynamical programming.
A good overview of the results in this area can be found in two papers by Cacchiani, Iori, Locatelli, Martello
[
7

8

[

Problem 6

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a group. Given
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n},c\in\mathcal{G}

w_{1}^{x_{1}}\ldots w_{k}^{x_{n}}=c

Problem 7

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a group. Given
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n},c\in\mathcal{G}

They used the following terminology.

Several interesting results were obtained on the decidability and the algorithmic complexity of the knapsack problem in solvable Baumslag-Solitar groups in

Problem 12

Given matrices

.

Problem 13

Given matrices

, and

W_{1}^{\otimes x_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes W_{n}^{\otimes x_{n}}=C

.

In this paper, we consider two algebraic structures: the set of matrices with non-negative integer entries of size

over the max-plus algebra with matrix multiplication and the set of matrices with positive integer entries of size

We denote these structures by

In this paper, we prove Theorem

,
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})

\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})

\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})

The remainder of this paper is structured into five parts. In Section 2, we recall some definitions from tropical algebra and generic-case complexity theory. Section 3 contains the proof of Theorem

The next section contains the proof of Theorem

The final section gives a conclusion to our work and a list of open questions.

2.
Preliminaries

In the first part of this section, we discuss tropical semirings and tropical matrix semirings, paying attention to the max-plus and max-times matrix algebras. In the second part of the section, we discuss how the size of an instance of a problem can be defined.
In the last part, we recall the definition of the asymptotic density of a set and the definition of a generic algorithm.

Let
S
be a set, and
a
be a number. In this paper, we denote the set
\{x\in S:x\geq a\}
by
S_{\geq a}
and the set
\{x\in S:x>a\}
by
S_{>a}
. For example,
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the set of all non-negative integers, and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is the set of all positive integers.
Also, we denote
S\cup\{\infty\}
by
\overline{S}
and
S\cup\{-\infty\}
by
\underline{S}
.

A
semiring
is a ring without the requirement that each element must have an additive inverse.
Two famous examples of semirings are the max-plus algebra and min-plus algebra. The
max-plus algebra
is the set
\underline{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the operations

x\oplus y=\max(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad x\otimes y=x+y.

The
min-plus algebra
is the set
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the operations

x\oplus y=\min(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad x\otimes y=x+y.

These semirings are idempotent and commutative.
These two semirings are known as tropical algebras. They have been widely studied and have many applications. For more information, we refer the reader to
[
6
]
.

Some researchers studied the min-times and max-times algebras, where one of the operations is the multiplication of numbers, and the other is either
\min
or
\max

[
38
,
39
,
12
,
17
]
. The domain of the
max-times
algebra is
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and the operations are

x\oplus y=\max(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad x\otimes y=x\cdot y.

The domain of the
min-times
algebra is
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and the operations are

x\oplus y=\min(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad x\otimes y=x\cdot y.

One can use other sets closed under
\oplus
and
\otimes
as domains to define similar structures. For example, in case of the max-plus algebra, one can consider
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
instead of
\underline{\mathbb{R}}
.

We will need the following algebras:
\mathcal{N}_{+}=\left<\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},{+}\right>
,
\mathcal{N}_{\times}=\left<\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},{\cdot}\right>
,
\mathcal{N}_{\max,{+}}=\left<\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\oplus,\otimes\right>
, where
\oplus
is
\max
and
\otimes
is
{+}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{\max,{\times}}=\left<\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\oplus,\otimes\right>
, where
\oplus
is
\max
and
\otimes
is
{\cdot}
.

Let
S
be a set. We denote the set of matrices of size
k\times k
with entries in
S
by
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(S)
.
In this paper, for a matrix
A
, we often use
a_{ij}
to refer to the element at the
i
-th row and the
j
-th column of the matrix
A
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\left\langle S,\oplus,\otimes\right\rangle
be an algebra with two binary operations, and let
k
be a positive integer. The set
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(S)
can be equipped with addition
\oplus
and multiplication
\otimes
using the following formulae:

(A\oplus B)_{ij}=a_{ij}\oplus b_{ij},

(A\otimes B)_{ij}=a_{i1}\otimes b_{1j}\oplus\dots\oplus a_{ik}\otimes b_{kj}.

If
\mathcal{S}
is an idempotent semiring, then the obtained algebra is also an idempotent semiring.

We denote an element
a
of the semiring
\mathcal{S}
raised to the
n
-th power by
a^{\otimes n}
.

In this paper, we consider the following two algebras:

\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}=\left<\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}),\otimes\right>
, where
\otimes
is defined using the maximum and the addition of numbers, and
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}=\left<\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}),\otimes\right>
, where
\otimes
is defined using the maximum and the multiplication of numbers.

Note that these semigroups do not have the identity matrices.
If
x_{i}=0
in an expression
W_{1}^{\otimes x_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes W_{n}^{\otimes x_{n}}
,
then it means that the matrix
W_{i}
is absent in the product.

We have a partial order on
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\geq 0}})
defined by the following rule:

A\leq B\text{ iff }a_{ij}\leq b_{ij}\text{ for all }i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}.

Let us describe how to solve a linear matrix equation

A\otimes X=B

(1)

over

\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}
.

We denote the set of all solutions to an equation
F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=G(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

over
\mathcal{S}
by
\V_{\mathcal{S}}(F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=G(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}))
or
just by
\V(F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=G(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}))
if this does not cause misunderstandings.

Every linear matrix equation (
1
) can be rewritten as a one-sided system of linear equations:

\begin{array}[]{lll}a_{11}\otimes x_{11}\oplus a_{12}\otimes x_{21}\oplus...\oplus a_{1k}\otimes x_{k1}&=&b_{11},\\
\qquad a_{11}\otimes x_{12}\oplus a_{12}\otimes x_{22}\oplus...\oplus a_{1k}\otimes x_{k2}&=&b_{12},\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\qquad\qquad a_{11}\otimes x_{1k}\oplus a_{12}\otimes x_{2k}\oplus...\oplus a_{1k}\otimes x_{kk}&=&b_{1k},\\
a_{21}\otimes x_{11}\oplus a_{22}\otimes x_{21}\oplus...\oplus a_{2k}\otimes x_{k1}&=&b_{21},\\
\qquad a_{21}\otimes x_{12}\oplus a_{22}\otimes x_{22}\oplus...\oplus a_{2k}\otimes x_{k2}&=&b_{22},\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\qquad\qquad a_{21}\otimes x_{1k}\oplus a_{22}\otimes x_{2k}\oplus...\oplus a_{2k}\otimes x_{kk}&=&b_{2k},\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
a_{k1}\otimes x_{11}\oplus a_{n2}\otimes x_{21}\oplus...\oplus a_{kk}\otimes x_{k1}&=&b_{k1},\\
\qquad a_{k1}\otimes x_{12}\oplus a_{k2}\otimes x_{22}\oplus...\oplus a_{kk}\otimes x_{k2}&=&b_{k2},\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\qquad\qquad a_{k1}\otimes x_{1k}\oplus a_{k2}\otimes x_{2k}\oplus...\oplus a_{kk}\otimes x_{kk}&=&b_{kk}.\end{array}

The matrix
X^{*}=X^{*}(A,B)
such that
x^{*}_{ij}=\min\{b_{lj}\otimes a_{li}^{\otimes{-1}}\}_{l}
is called the
principal solution
to the equation (
1
).
Note that
x^{*}_{ij}=\min\{b_{lj}-a_{li}\}_{l}
for
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
, and
x^{*}_{ij}=\min\{b_{lj}/a_{li}\}_{l}
for
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}
.
Actually, the principal solution may not be a solution to (
1
).
The following lemma is an adaptation of Theorem 3.1.1 in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
or
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}
.
Let
A,B,X
be matrices of size
k\times k
.

X
is a solution to
A\otimes X=B
over
\mathcal{S}
if and only if
X\leq X^{*}(A,B)
and

\bigcup_{(i,j)\;:\;x_{ij}=x^{*}_{ij}}M_{ij}(A,B)=\{1,\ldots,k\}\times\{1,\ldots,k\},

where
M_{ij}(A,B)=\{(l,j):x^{*}_{ij}=b_{lj}\otimes a_{li}^{\otimes{-1}}\}
.

This lemma gives us a way to enumerate all the solution to
A\otimes X=B
.

Now, let us define the size of an integer, a matrix, and an instance of a problem.

Let
a
be a non-negative number.
We can consider two sizes of
a
. The first one is the number of bits that we need to write this number using the binary numeral system:

\size_{2}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lfloor\log_{2}(a)\rfloor+1&\text{ if }a>0,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

For
a\geq 1
, if
\size(a)=s
, then
2^{s-1}\leq a<2^{s}
. Therefore,

|\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}:\size_{2}(a)=s\}|=2^{s-1}.

(2)

Note that for positive integers
a,b
,

\size_{2}(a)+\size_{2}(b)-1\leq\size_{2}(ab)\leq\size_{2}(a)+\mathrm{size}(b).

(3)

Indeed, let
\size_{2}(a)=s
and
\size_{2}(b)=t
. Then
2^{s-1}\leq a<2^{s}
and
2^{t-1}\leq b<2^{t}
. Therefore,
2^{s-1+t-1}\leq ab<2^{s+t}
. This implies
s+t-1\leq\size_{2}(ab)\leq s+t
.

The second size is the number of symbols that we need to write this number using the unary numeral system:

\size_{1}(a)=a.

Let
A
be a matrix of size
k\times k
.
The matrix can be represented as a list of
k^{2}
numbers separated with
k^{2}-1
delimiters.
Therefore,

\size_{b}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\size_{b}(a_{ij})+k^{2}-1.

(4)

Let
I=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n},c)
be an input of
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{S})
or
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{S})
.
Then the size of
I
is

\size_{b}(I)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\size_{b}(w_{i})+\size_{b}(c)+n.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a matrix semigroup, then the size of an input
I=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},C)
can be rewritten as

\size_{b}(I)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\size_{b}(w_{lij})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\size_{b}(c_{ij})+(n+1)k^{2}-1.

Note that we consider the number
k
as a parameter of a problem. Hence,
k
can occur in exponents in polynomials.

Let
U
be a set.
A stratification
of
U
is a sequence
\{U_{m}\}_{m}
of non-empty finite subsets
U_{m}\subseteq U
such that
\bigcup_{m}U_{m}=U
.
For a subset
A\subseteq U
and a stratification
\{U_{m}\}_{m}
, the limit

\rho(A)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap U_{m}|}{|U_{m}|}

(if it exists) is called the
asymptotic density
of
A
with respect to the stratification
\{U_{m}\}_{m}
.
If
\rho(A)=1
, we say that
A
is
generic
.
If
\rho(A)=0
, we say that
A
is
negligible
.

An algorithm
\mathcal{A}\colon U\to V\cup\{?\}
is called
generic
if
\mathcal{A}
stops on every input
I\in U
, and
\{I\in U:\mathcal{A}(I)\neq{?}\}
is a generic set.
Here, the answer “?” means “don’t know”.

A decision problem
A\subseteq U
is
decidable generically in polynomial time
if there is a polynomial generic algorithm computing the indicator function of
A
.

3.
\mathsf{NP}
-completeness of the problems

In this section, we prove the theorems about the
\mathsf{NP}
-completeness of
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
,
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
,
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
, and
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
.
In order to do this, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
is in
\mathsf{NP}
.

Proof.

To prove that a problem is in
\mathsf{NP}
, we need to show that for every yes-instance
I
, there is a certificate of size polynomial in
\size_{2}(I)
that can be checked in time polynomial in
\size_{2}(I)
.

It is easy to see that
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
is in
\mathsf{NP}
.
Indeed, let
I=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},C)
be an input of the problem. A vector
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
such that

W_{1}^{\otimes x_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes W_{n}^{\otimes x_{n}}=C

(5)

is a certificate. The size of this vector is polynomial in
\size_{2}(I)
. Also, the equality (
5
) can be checked in time polynomial in
\size_{2}(I)
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
is in
\mathsf{NP}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Lemma
2
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})
and
B=(b_{ij})
be matrices in
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\geq 0}})
.
Consider
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
. Note that

(A\otimes B)_{ij}=a_{i1}\otimes b_{1j}\oplus\dots\oplus a_{ik}\otimes b_{kj}\geq a_{ij}.

Therefore, the sequence
A,A^{\otimes 2},A^{\otimes 2},\ldots
is non-decreasing. Moreover, if
A\neq O
, then the maximal
x
such that
A^{\otimes x}\leq B
cannot be greater than
2\cdot\max\{b_{ij}\}_{ij}
.

Remark 2
.

Consider
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}
. Similarly, if
A=(a_{ij})
is in
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\geq 1}})
, then the sequence

A,A^{\otimes 2},A^{\otimes 2},\ldots
is non-decreasing. Moreover, if there is
a_{ij}>1
, then
the maximal
x
such that

and

In this section, we prove that there are polynomial generic algorithms to solve

and
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})

Let
U
be the set of all tuples of the form
(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},C)
,
where
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},C\in\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
When considering a generic algorithm, we need to define a stratification of
U
.
In this section, we consider the stratification
\{U_{m}\}_{m}
of
U
,
where
U_{m}=\{I\in U:\size_{2}(I)=m\}

Let
M_{l}=\{A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}):\size_{2}(A)=l\}
.
Each matrix in
M_{l}
can be encoded by a string with
k^{2}-1

of the following form:

1d_{11}d_{12}\ldots d_{1l_{1}}\text{\textvisiblespace}1d_{21}d_{22}\ldots d_{2l_{2}}\text{\textvisiblespace}\ldots\text{\textvisiblespace}1d_{k^{2}1}d_{k^{2}2}\ldots d_{k^{2}l_{k^{2}}},

(9)

where

Note that

. Hence,
when the positions of the delimiters are fixed, each pattern (

encodes
2^{l-2k^{2}-1}

matrices. The number of these patterns can be found using the stars and bars method and is equal to
\binom{l-k^{2}}{k^{2}-1}
. Therefore,

|M_{l}|=2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\binom{l-k^{2}}{k^{2}-1}.

Lemma 13
.

Let
X,Y\in\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})
.
Then,
\size_{2}(X)+\size_{2}(Y)\leq\size_{2}(X\otimes Y)+2k^{2}-1

Proof.

Let
A=X\otimes Y
. Then, for every
i
and
j

x_{i1}\otimes y_{1j}\oplus x_{i2}\otimes y_{2j}\oplus\cdots\oplus x_{ik}\otimes y_{kj}=a_{ij}.

In the other notation,

\max\{x_{il}\cdot y_{lj}\}_{l}=a_{ij}.

Therefore, for each index
l
,
x_{il}\cdot y_{lj}\leq a_{ij}.

This implies that for each
l
,

\size_{2}(x_{il}\cdot y_{lj})\leq\size_{2}(a_{ij})
.
Using (
3
), we get

\size_{2}(x_{il})+\size_{2}(y_{lj})\leq\size_{2}(a_{ij})+1.

Let us choose

l=(i+j)\bmod k+1.

(10)

Then,

\size_{2}(x_{i,(i+j)\bmod k+1})+\size_{2}(y_{(i+j)\bmod k+1,j})\leq\size_{2}(a_{ij})+1.

Summing this inequality for all
i
and
j
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\size_{2}(x_{i,(i+j)\bmod k+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\size_{2}(y_{(i+j)\bmod k+1,j})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\size_{2}(a_{ij})+k^{2}.

Note that Formula (
10

\{1,\ldots,k\}
for each fixed
i
and each fixed
j
. Therefore, the first double sum has all the elements of
X
,
and the second double sum has all the elements of
Y
:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\size_{2}(x_{ij})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\size_{2}(y_{ij})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\size_{2}(a_{ij})+k^{2}.

Using Formula (
4

\size_{2}(X)-k^{2}+1+\size_{2}(Y)-k^{2}+1\leq\size_{2}(A)-k^{2}+1+k^{2}.

This yields that

\size_{2}(X)+\size_{2}(Y)\leq\size_{2}(A)+2k^{2}-1.\qed

Lemma 14

Let

where

|S_{l,P(m)}|\leq\frac{m^{k^{2}+1}}{P(m)}\cdot|M_{l}|.

Proof.

Let

K_{l}=\{(X,Y)\in(\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}))^{2}:\size_{2}(X\otimes Y)=l\}.

Note that
P(m)\cdot|S_{l,P(m)}|\leq|K_{l}|
.
Indeed,

|K_{l}|=\sum_{A\in M_{l}}|\{(X,Y)\in(\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}))^{2}:X\otimes Y=A\}|=\sum_{A\in M_{l}}|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|=\\
\sum_{A\in M_{l}\,:\,|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|\leq P(m)}|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|+\sum_{A\in M_{l}\,:\,|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|>P(m)}|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|\geq\\
\sum_{A\in M_{l}\,:\,|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|>P(m)}|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|\geq\sum_{A\in M_{l}\,:\,|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|>P(m)}P(m)=\\
P(m)\cdot|\{A\in M_{l}\,:\,|\!\V(X\otimes Y=A)|>P(m)\}|.

It follows from Lemma
13
that

|K_{l}|\leq|L_{l}|
, where

L_{l}=\{(X,Y)\in(\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}))^{2}:\size_{2}(X)+\size_{2}(Y)\leq l+2k^{2}-1\}.

Therefore,

P(m)\cdot|S_{l,P(m)}|\leq|K_{l}|\leq|L_{l}|.

(12)

Let

N_{r}=\{(X,Y)\in(\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}))^{2}:\size_{2}(X)+\size_{2}(Y)=r\}.

Note that

|L_{l}|=\sum_{r=4k^{2}-2}^{l+2k^{2}-1}|N_{r}|\leq(l-2k^{2}+2)\cdot|N_{l+2k^{2}-1}|\leq m\cdot|N_{l+2k^{2}-1}|,

and, using the stars and bars method again, we have

|N_{l+2k^{2}-1}|=2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\cdot\binom{l}{2k^{2}-1}.

Hence, we have

|L_{l}|\leq m\cdot 2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\cdot\binom{l}{2k^{2}-1}=\frac{m\cdot 2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\cdot l!}{(2k^{2}-1)!\cdot(l-2k^{2}+1)!}=\\
\frac{m\cdot 2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\cdot l!}{(2k^{2}-1)!\cdot(l-2k^{2}+1)!}\cdot\frac{|M_{l}|}{|M_{l}|}=\frac{m\cdot 2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\cdot l!\cdot(k^{2}-1)!\cdot(l-2k^{2}+1)!}{2^{l-2k^{2}+1}\cdot(2k^{2}-1)!\cdot(l-2k^{2}+1)!\cdot(l-k^{2})!}\cdot|M_{l}|=\\
\frac{m\cdot l!\cdot(k^{2}-1)!}{(2k^{2}-1)!\cdot(l-k^{2})!}\cdot|M_{l}|\leq\\
\frac{m\cdot l!}{(l-k^{2})!}\cdot|M_{l}|\leq m\cdot l^{k^{2}}\cdot|M_{l}|\leq m^{k^{2}+1}\cdot|M_{l}|.

Combining this inequality and the inequality (
12
), we obtain (
11
).
∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
R_{m,P(m)}=\{(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},C)\in U_{m}:|\!\V(X\otimes Y=C)|>P(m)\}
, where
P(m)
is a polynomial.
Then,

\frac{|R_{m,P(m)}|}{|U_{m}|}\leq\frac{m^{k^{2}+1}}{P(m)}.

(13)

Proof.

Let
Q_{l}=\{(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})\in(\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}))^{n}:\size_{2}(W_{1})+\ldots+\size_{2}(W_{n})+n=l\}
. Note that
U_{m}
can be presented as

U_{m}=\bigcup_{l=2k^{2}-1}^{m}Q_{m-l}\times M_{l}.

Hence,

|U_{m}|
can be calculated using the following formula:

|U_{m}|=\sum_{l=2k^{2}-1}^{m}|Q_{m-l}|\cdot|M_{l}|.

Also, using Lemma
14
, we have

|R_{m,P(m)}|=\sum_{l=2k^{2}-1}^{m}|S_{l,P(m)}|\cdot|Q_{m-l}|\leq\sum_{l=2k^{2}-1}^{m}\frac{m^{k^{2}+1}}{P(m)}\cdot|M_{l}|\cdot|Q_{m-l}|=\\
\frac{m^{k^{2}+1}}{P(m)}\cdot\sum_{l=2k^{2}-1}^{m}|M_{l}|\cdot|Q_{m-l}|=\frac{m^{k^{2}+1}}{P(m)}\cdot|U_{m}|.

Using this inequality, we obtain the inequality (
13
).
∎

Theorem 3
.

\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
and
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
are decidable generically in polynomial time.

Proof.

Consider
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
. We modify Algorithm
1
as follows.
Let
I=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n},C)
and
m=\size_{2}(I)
.
In the beginning of the function
SolveSSP
,
we check the size of the dictionary
Memo
. If this size is greater than

m^{k^{2}+3}
, then we stop the process and the algorithm returns “?”.
Note that the time complexity of this algorithm is polynomial in
m
.

Consider the set

R=\bigcup_{m}{R_{m,m^{k^{2}+2}}}.

From Lemma
15
,
it follows that

\rho(R)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{|R_{m,m^{k^{2}+2}}|}{|U_{m}|}=0.

Therefore, it is enough to show that for every
I\in U\setminus R
,
the algorithm returns
true
or
false
.
Let us estimate how many different tuples
(W_{i},\ldots,W_{n},Y)
we can obtain for the input
I
.
First, note that the number of different tuples
(W_{i},\ldots,W_{n})

is
n+1
. Second, note that for every
Y
, there is a matrix
X
such that
X\otimes Y=C
. Therefore, the number of different
Y
is not greater than

|\!\V(X\otimes Y=C)|
. Since
I\in U\setminus R
, this number is not greater than

m^{k^{2}+2}
. Hence, the number of different tuples
(W_{i},\ldots,W_{n},Y)
is not greater than
m\cdot m^{k^{2}+2}=m^{k^{2}+3}
. Therefore, the algorithm considers all such tuples.

Algorithm
2
to solve
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k})
can be modified similarly.
∎

6.
Conclusion and questions for further study

In this paper, we considered the knapsack problem and the subset sum problem for the algebras
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}
. We proved that these problems are
\mathsf{NP}
-complete. We showed that
there are pseudo-polynomial algorithms to solve these problems. Also, we showed that there
are polynomial generic algorithms to solve the knapsack problem and the subset sum problem for
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{\times}}^{k}
. It would be interesting to know if there are such algorithms for
\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
.

Question 1
.

Does there exist polynomial generic algorithms to solve
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
and
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{M}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
?

We proved our results for matrices without negative numbers and
-\infty
. It would be interesting to change our results for the semigroup of matrices of size
k\times k
over the max-plus algebra

\left<\underline{\mathbb{Z}},\oplus,\otimes\right>
.
Denote this structure by
\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
.

Question 2
.

Is
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k})

\mathsf{NP}
-complete?
Are there pseudo-polynomial or polynomial generic algorithms to solve
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
and
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
?

Also, we can consider matrices over the min-plus algebra
\left<\overline{\mathbb{Z}},\oplus,\otimes\right>
. Denote this structure by
\mathcal{Q}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
.

Question 3
.

Is
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{Q}_{\max,{+}}^{k})

\mathsf{NP}
-complete?
Are there pseudo-polynomial or polynomial generic algorithms to solve
\mathbf{SSP}(\mathcal{Q}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
and
\mathbf{KP}(\mathcal{Q}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
?

Alhussaini et al. considered the tropical discrete logarithm problem with a shift and the tropical two-sided discrete logarithm problem with a shift
[
1
]
.
It would be interesting to consider a shifted version of the knapsack problem.

Problem 14
.

Given matrices
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
, and
C
of size
k\times k
over a tropical semiring, decide if
s\otimes W_{1}^{\otimes x_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes W_{n}^{\otimes x_{n}}=C
for some nonnegative integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Denote this problem for
\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k}
by
\textbf{SKP}(\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
.

Question 4
.

Is
\mathbf{SKP}(\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k})

\mathsf{NP}
-complete?
Is there a polynomial or a polynomial generic algorithm to solve
\mathbf{SKP}(\mathcal{R}_{\max,{+}}^{k})
?

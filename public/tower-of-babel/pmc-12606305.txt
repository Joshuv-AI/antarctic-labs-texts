AIR-GANet: multi-head attention integrated residual dense block based generative adversarial network for visible and infrared image fusion
Authors: J Bineeshia, B Vinoth Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractImage fusion is a sophisticated improvement technique aimed at integrating data from multiple sensors into a single, detailed and coherent image. This integrated representation supports and simplifies subsequent processing tasks. This article presents a well-crafted fusion method depending on Multi-Head Attention (MA) and Residual Dense Block (RDB) based Generative Adversarial Network (AIR-GANet) for infrared and visible image fusion. The framework utilizes RDB for extracting local features, Multi-Head Attention for capturing global dependencies, ensuring a comprehensive representation of both infrared and visible modalities. A dual discriminator mechanism is incorporated with separate discriminators to refine the fused image through adversarial training, preserving both thermal details and texture information. The fusion module incorporates local and global features into a unified feature map, which is then decoded into the final fused image. By applying AIR-GANet to input infrared (and visible () images, the resulting fused image maintains crucial informative data from both sources, thus improving overall image quality and enabling high-performance across multiple quantitative evaluation metrics. Extensive evaluations on the TNO, Road Scene and Multi-Spectral Road Scenarios (MSRS) datasets demonstrate that the model attains average Entropy (EN) of 7.4085 and Mutual Information (MI) of 3.1531 on TNO, corresponding values of 7.7086 and 3.6953 on Road Scene, 7.8260 and 3.8502 on MSRS confirming its superior fusion capability across diverse scenarios.Keywords: Multi head attention, Residual dense block, Generative adversarial network, Local feature extraction, Global feature extraction, Infrared image, Visible image, Image fusion, Deep learningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rising demand for advanced imaging systems capable of capturing comprehensive and accurate scene representations has emphasized the challenges of single-modal sensors and traditional imaging techniques1. Single-frame imaging is commonly limited by the constraints of the capture device and environmental conditions, resulting in incomplete scene representation. For instance, visible imaging captures surface information effectively but lacks the capability to reveal internal features in low-light conditions2. Similarly, infrared imaging offers thermal visions but lack fine spatial details. This gap necessitates innovative solution that harnesses the strengths of multiple imaging modalities. Image fusion methodologies have been developed to mitigate these limitations, however, accomplishing higher quality fused images which preserve crucial information from input sources remains challenging. This research motivates to address the limitations by establishing an efficient image fusion framework that has the potential to significantly improve visual perception and enables superior performance across various applications.

Image Fusion3 is a sophisticated process designed to merge data from multiple imaging modalities that capture the same scene, resulting in a single, enhanced image. The core purpose of the process is constructing composite image carrying more meaningful and comprehensive information, than any individual source image4. By utilizing the strengths and balancing characteristics of different modalities, image fusion supports to overwhelm the challenges in single sensor imaging5. Numerous image fusion techniques have undergone continuous refinement to address a range of demands of different applications. In the realm of computer vision, fusion plays a vital role in improving scene understanding, detecting object and classifying tasks6. Similarly, in photography, it is frequently employed in enhancing image quality and texture details by combining inputs from sensors optimized from different lightning conditions. Each fusion method7 is tailored to the particular characteristics of source data and objectives of application, ensuring that the resulting image preserves critical features, minimizes redundancy, and improves interpretability. As a result, image fusion has become an indispensable tool in many fields, continually advancing with the development of new technologies and algorithms.

Strategies for combining visible and infrared images are divided into two distinct classes. The first includes classical techniques that rely on predefined rules, while the second method is based on deep learning. In traditional approaches, source images are converted into a specific domain utilizing mathematical approaches, and the fusion process is controlled by manually designed fusion rules and activity level metrics. The initial focus of image fusion research is based on spatial domain algorithms, including subspace based8 and saliency computation-based approaches9, directly operate on pixel values to combine information from source images. While straightforward and computationally efficient, these methods are prone to spectral distortions, where the color and intensity relationships of the source images are altered, and spatial distortions, which lead to the loss of structural and textural details critical for accurate representation. To mitigate these issues, transform domain methods emerged as a more sophisticated alternative. The Pyramid Transformation involves decomposition of images into multiple layers, such as Gaussian or Laplacian pyramids10,11 for isolating features at different resolutions for more effective fusion. The wavelet transformation decompose the image into frequency components, making it easier to integrate features across scales, while maintaining both spectral and spatial information12. Moreover, the Contourlet Transformation13 extends wavelets by capturing features like textures and edges. By isolating features at different scales or frequencies, transform domain methods reduce distortions and enlarge the quality of fused images. However, the transformation and reconstruction processes often introduce noise, which degrades the visual fidelity of the output. Additionally, the computational complexity of these methods makes them less suitable resource-constrained applications. Further progressions in traditional techniques led to the development of Sparse representation with the aim of decomposing images into a sparse set of meaningful features, ensuring that only the most critical information is retained for fusion14. Dictionary learning15, on the other hand, involves constructing dictionaries of features that guide the fusion process. While these methods offer improved adaptability in certain scenarios, they face significant challenges which are rigid and struggle to generalize to dynamic inputs.

Despite the progress made by traditional methods, their inherent limitations—such as sensitivity to noise, lack of adaptability, and computational inefficiencies—highlight the need for more advanced solutions. These challenges have paved the way for development of deep learning-based methods, which offer data-driven, adaptive approaches to image fusion. Autoencoders (AEs)16 reduce redundancy by focusing on most informative features, enhancing flexibility for dynamic inputs, though their performance depends heavily on the quality of latent representation. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)17 uses multi-layer architecture to extract spatial and semantic features, effectively preserving fine-grained information and accomplish high spatial resolution, making them ideal for tasks necessitating structural accuracy. However, CNNs struggle with global context representation and require substantial computational resources. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)18, with their generator-discriminator framework, produce high-quality, realistic fused images and are specifically effective in complex circumstances, including low-light conditions; however, their training is unstable, with issues like mode collapse. Also, GAN-based image dehazing restores clear, haze-free images from hazy inputs by learning the complex mapping between degraded and clean image domains. In this framework, the generator attempts to reconstruct a dehazed image by removing haze effects such as reduced contrast, color distortion and blurred edges, while the discriminator evaluates the realism of the generated output compared to ground truth haze-free images. Through this adversarial training, the generator progressively improves its ability to produce visually natural and detail-preserving results19.

Review on existing fusion topologies.

Due to the limitations observed in existing image fusion techniques, a need for a more robust and adaptive fusion framework arises. While methods such as Autoencoder-based and Diffusion-based models enhance certain aspects like feature fidelity and denoising, they often struggle with computational overhead, generalization issues, or modality-specific deficiencies. Similarly, CNN-based and transformer-based methods, although effective at spatial feature extraction and capturing global dependencies, respectively, are prone to detail loss or noisy outputs under certain conditions. On the other hand, FusionGAN and Attention FGAN show considerable promise by capitalizing on adversarial learning to produce visually convincing results, combining infrared thermal cues and visible texture details effectively. However, even these GAN models face challenges like training instability, mode collapse and insufficient handling of spatial versus contextual features. While Attention FGAN primarily emphasizes attention-based feature selection, it often struggles to fully preserve fine-grained textures when strong thermal cues dominate. Similarly, DUGAN focuses on adversarial learning but lacks sufficient capability to capture long-range dependencies, leading to incomplete contextual representation. To overcome these constraints, this research suggests an enhanced GAN-based fusion framework designed to deliver both fine detail preservation and robust contextual understanding.

The novel AIR-GANet architecture is an infrared–visible image fusion framework combining Multi-Head Attention (MA) to capture long-range global dependencies with Residual Dense Blocks (RDB) for extracting rich local information. In order to preserve visual detail in the fused image, the RDBs first concentrate on extracting fine-grained local features including edges, textures and tiny structural patterns. RDBs’ dense connections ensure that detailed texture information is preserved throughout the network’s deeper levels while promoting effective feature reuse and steady gradient flow. Concurrently, the MA mechanism emphasizes long-range contextual linkages over the entire image by capturing global dependencies. As a result, the framework can preserve structural coherence across modalities while emphasizing important semantic regions, such pedestrians or cars in road scenes. A feature fusion module combines the outputs of RDB and MA to create a balanced fused feature map that preserves detail and ensures contextual accuracy by combining local and global representations. In contrast to traditional GAN-based techniques or conventional fusion methods, which frequently depend on either global structural consistency or local detail preservation, AIR-GANet integrates both through a single feature fusion module. The dual-discriminator approach, which uses two discriminators to independently enhance thermal and texture information, is another unique feature. This ensures that the fused image preserves both important infrared clues and subtle visual textures. Compared to single-discriminator frameworks, this dual refinement approach offers a more balanced adversarial learning process. By providing consistently high-quality, information-rich fused images that may be used for night vision and real-time autonomous driving scenarios, these improvements collectively distinguish the proposed work. This unified approach not only enhances the accuracy and visual clarity of fused images but also provides stability and adaptability across diverse fusion scenarios. Image fusion using AIR-GANet approach

AIR-GANet architecture for infrared-visible image fusion is shown in Fig. 2. The system begins by concatenating the input visible () and infrared (IR) images, processed through initial convolution layers to extract primary features. The generator network comprises two parallel modules, which are RDB for local feature extraction and MA mechanism for global feature extraction. The RDB captures fine-grained texture and structural details through dense connections and residual learning, while the MA module effectively models long-range dependencies to emphasize important contextual regions across the image. The outputs from both modules are integrated into the feature fusion block to form a unified fused feature map. This map is subsequently decoded in the generative layers to produce the final fused image, which exhibits both thermal and visual characteristics. To refine the output, the system incorporates a dual discriminator strategy. One discriminator evaluates the preservation of visible image properties and the other ensures retention of infrared information. These discriminators guide the generator via adversarial learning, progressively enhancing the quality. The inclusion of both local and global feature extraction, combined with adversarial feedback, allows the proposed AIR-GANet to achieve superior texture sharpness, edge preservation, and perceptual quality across varying image conditions.

GAN architecture

The proposed framework incorporates a generator and a dual discriminator mechanism, inspired by GANs. This mechanism consists of two separate discriminators including infrared discriminator and visible discriminator. An infrared discriminator ensures that the thermal characteristics from infrared image are well-preserved in fused output. Similarly, a visible discriminator focuses on maintaining high-frequency textures and detailed structures from the visible image. By employing adversarial training, network refines the fused image, leading to improved perceptual quality, sharper edges, and a better balance between the two modalities. The adversarial loss helps in generating images that are visually realistic and rich in both thermal and visible information. Training procedure of proposed AIR-GANet structure is represented in Fig. 3.

Training process of AIR-GANet in image fusion.

GAN has generator and discriminator, the former aims to synthesize data that closely resembles real-world samples, while the latter differentiates real and generated data. Through adversarial training, the generator improves its capability to generate realistic outputs by continuously minimizing its loss against the discriminator’s judgments. In the context of the proposed AIR-GANet framework, the generator is specifically modelled to perform visible and infrared image fusion by learning rich feature representations. It begins by concatenating the input IR and Vi images, which are then passed through a convolutional layer. Subsequently, local features are extracted using RDB, while global dependencies are captured via the MA mechanism. These features are integrated within a fusion module to form a unified feature map, which is finally decoded into a high-quality fused image. This architectural design ensures that both fine textures and contextual structures from the input modalities are effectively preserved, enabling superior fusion performance. In addition, the GAN creates a game between a discriminator and a generator that is adversarial in nature. For training the AIR-GANet model, 22 image pairs are selected from the TNO dataset. Additionally, 32 image pairs are chosen from the Road Scene dataset. To enhance the diversity of training data, each image is divided into 128128 patches with a stride of 32, generating11, 560 pairs of training patches. Spectral normalization is applied to the discriminator to enhance convergence stability, and gradient clipping is introduced to prevent exploding gradients. The adversarial loss is combined with a content loss that ensures preservation of structural detail, while the training dataset includes TNO and Road Scene image pairs, divided into patches using a sliding window approach to enhance training diversity.

The proposed AIR-GANet introduces additional computational layers due to the integration of RDBs for local feature extraction and MA for capturing global dependencies, while also employing a dual-discriminator setup for adversarial refinement. Quantitative analysis shows that AIR-GANet requires approximately 7.4 M parameters and 24.3 floating-point operations per second (FLOP) per fused image of size 256 × 256, leading to an average inference time of ~ 29 ms per image on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. Importantly, the sub-30 ms inference time ensures real-time suitability, as the framework can process around 34 frames per second (FPS), making it practical for applications like autonomous driving, surveillance, and night vision where timely fused outputs are critical.

Ablation studies

Three ablation configurations are formulated. Each experiment isolates a key architectural element, such as dual discriminators, MA and RDB, to assess their specific functional roles within the image fusion process.

1. With One and Two Discriminators.

This ablation evaluates the impact of incorporating dual discriminators (2D) instead of a single discriminator (1D). The dual-discriminator setup, consisting of a visible-specific and infrared-specific discriminator, independently guides the generator to preserve both texture and thermal details.

2. With and Without MA.

This study investigates the contribution of the MA module in capturing global contextual dependencies. The ablation conducted with and without the MA module shows that its removal leads to notable declines in MI, SF and , which are strongly correlated with global structure retention and edge integrity. The attention mechanism enables the fusion network to weigh relevant spatial features across modalities and suppress redundant background noise. Without MA, the generator tends to overfit to local cues and misses global structure alignment, leading to weaker fusion in complex scenes.

3. With and Without RDB.

Here, the role of RDB in extracting fine-grained local textures is assessed. Excluding RDB degrades the AG and EN, showing that RDB’s dense connections and residual learning are crucial for fine detail preservation and local feature representation, which directly improve fusion sharpness and information richness. RDBs enhance gradient flow during backpropagation and enable deeper layers to reuse low-level details by capitalizing on dense feature reuse. This is especially beneficial in fusing fine textures from visible images and local thermal blobs from IR inputs. Without RDBs, the model struggles to maintain structural coherence and edge sharpness, resulting in fused images with blurry transitions and underutilized local textures. Notably, 2D configurations outperform their 1D counterparts across datasets, confirming that richer spatial feature extraction contributes to better fusion quality. These results strongly validate the effectiveness of integrating both local and global feature extraction mechanisms to achieve superior functionality.

Conclusion

This paper proposes an advanced technique based on AIR-GANet, which combines RDB for local feature extraction and MA for capturing global dependencies. The dual discriminator setup ensures that both thermal and texture details are preserved during adversarial learning. To validate its effectiveness and robustness, the proposed framework is applied to the TNO dataset, containing multispectral nighttime imagery, the Road Scene dataset, comprising urban driving scenarios with challenging lighting conditions and MSRS dataset. Across both datasets, AIR-GANet consistently achieves results that exceed those of advanced fusion algorithms in terms of fidelity, sharpness and detail preservation. On TNO dataset, it records EN of 7.4085, MI of 3.1531, AG of 4.6414, and similarly leads in SD, SF, VIF and . Across all three datasets, AIR-GANet demonstrates consistent superiority compared to other advanced techniques. On the Road Scene dataset, it further improves results with EN of 7.7086, MI of 3.6953, AG of 6.0237 and the highest values for the remaining metrics. Also, with MSRS dataset, EN of 7.8260, MI of 3.8502, AG of 5.1533 is attained. These results demonstrate the model’s superior ability to retain texture details, improve edge sharpness and preserve structural information. The ablation studies also reflects that combination of RDB and MA provides significant performance gains over individual components, confirming the effectiveness of integrating local and global feature learning. AIR-GANet maintains consistently high performance across all test cases, validating its robustness and strong generalization capability under varied image conditions.

Sustained Remission Following Limited Dupilumab Exposure in Refractory Eosinophilic Esophagitis
Authors: Dain (Raina) Kim, Adam Bubelenyi, Milli Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a type 2 immune-mediated esophageal disorder that can be refractory to standard therapy. We present a case of a 49-year-old woman with asthma, chronic sinusitis, and nasal polyposis diagnosed with EoE refractory to proton pump inhibitors, dietary modification, and swallowed topical corticosteroids. She was treated with dupilumab in 2023 but discontinued it after 3 months due to side effects. Subsequent esophagogastroduodenoscopies demonstrated sustained histologic and endoscopic remission up to October 2025 (26 months later), after which recurrence of disease was confirmed. This case highlights 2-year sustained remission following limited exposure to dupilumab in refractory EoE.

INTRODUCTION

Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is an immune-mediated disorder of the esophagus, frequently associated with type 2 inflammatory conditions.1,2 While proton pump inhibitors (PPI), dietary therapy, and swallowed topical corticosteroids are first-line treatments, a subset of patients do not respond to these agents.3 Biologic therapies have emerged as the next step in managing this condition.4

Dupilumab is the first US Food and Drug Administration-approved monoclonal antibody that inhibits interleukins 4 and 13, both of which are central to the type 2 inflammatory cascade.5 This agent is effective in treating other type 2 inflammatory disorders5 and is currently recommended as the standard of therapy in individuals 1 year of age or older either whose conventional treatment has failed or who have concomitant type 2 inflammatory conditions in EoE.2 Guidelines suggest ongoing therapy to maintain the effectiveness of treatment in managing EoE.1,2 We present a case of treatment-refractory EoE with sustained histologic remission following a 3-month exposure to dupilumab.

CASE REPORT

A 49-year-old woman with a history of type 2 inflammatory disease (asthma, nasal polyposis, and chronic sinusitis) presented with food impaction in 2015 in the background of dysphagia and reflux symptoms. Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with biopsies was consistent with EoE, prompting initiation of PPI therapy. Multiple PPIs and elimination diets were completed from 2015 to 2023. Topical corticosteroids failed due to side effects. Chronologically, these included: 3 months of pantoprazole 40 mg twice daily; 3 months of ranitidine 150 mg once daily; 9 months of lansoprazole 30 mg sublingual twice daily with soy and dairy reduction. This was followed by complete soy, wheat, and egg elimination; 8 months of dexlansoprazole 60 mg twice daily with concurrent wheat and egg elimination diet; 5 years of esomeprazole 40 mg twice daily on no elimination diet; and, finally, 7 months of dexlansoprazole 60 mg twice daily. She trialed 1 week of swallowed budesonide 2 mg once daily but discontinued this due to intolerance. Her adherence to PPIs varied throughout this period, from consistent use to only a few days of the week. She was unwilling to complete a full 6 food elimination diet due to negative lifestyle implications and did not want to trial other formulations of topical steroids. These treatments were unsuccessful in adequately controlling endoscopic and histologic inflammation or symptoms of dysphagia and reflux. On serial EGDs, she demonstrated diffuse eosinophilic infiltration in the esophagus, with Eosinophilic Esophagitis Endoscopic Reference Score ranging from 3 to 7 and recurrent strictures needing dilation. Dupilumab was approved for EoE treatment in Canada in May 2023, and she was one of the first patients to receive it at our institution. After receiving 300 mg weekly for 3 months, she discontinued it due to side effects such as abdominal pain, fatigue, and worsening reflux symptoms. Follow-up EGD in August 2023 (Figure 1) revealed histologic remission with the absence of eosinophilic infiltration and endoscopic improvement of Eosinophilic Esophagitis Endoscopic Reference Score to 1 (compared to a score of 3 before starting dupilumab). Table 1 summarizes the histologic and endoscopic details of her scopes from 2022 to 2025. Surveillance EGDs at 15 months (Figure 1) after dupilumab showed no histologic or endoscopic inflammation. During this time, she restarted dexlansoprazole twice daily and partial wheat and egg elimination for reflux control. She trialed tapering off PPIs with concurrent diet elimination but resumed daily dexlansoprazole use due to worsening reflux. EoE-Quality of Life-Assessment in 2022, 2024, and 2025 showed improvement in all subscales (eating/diet, social impact, emotional impact, disease anxiety, and swallowing anxiety) and total scores (2.20 pre-dupilumab to 3.25 post-dupilumab).

Regarding her other type 2 diseases, she continued with her preexisting treatments including Advair 250–25 μg inhaler, 1 puff twice daily, salbutamol 100 mcg inhaler as needed, fluticasone-based nasal sprays, and antihistamines as needed. She was seen by respirology, who deemed her asthma poorly controlled without any true exacerbations. She was initiated on a budesonide-formoterol inhaler to use as needed and was referred to otolaryngology after Pulmicort rinses caused repeated sinus infections. Independently, her primary care practitioner had started her on montelukast 10 mg orally once daily within this time frame.

After 26 months off dupilumab, histologic and endoscopic recurrence were identified (Figure 1), with concurrent worsening of gastrointestinal reflux and symptoms of her other type 2 inflammatory disorders including progressive exertional dyspnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, and allergic rhinitis. At her last follow-up, the patient had returned to consistent twice daily PPI use with dexlansoprazole, which had improved reflux but not dysphagia symptoms.

DISCUSSION

This case demonstrates sustained histologic remission following brief dupilumab exposure in refractory EoE. This is a rarely reported finding for type 2 inflammatory diseases, where evidence shows >1-year remission usually requires ongoing treatment.6–9 Studies demonstrate rapid worsening of clinical outcomes and high rates of recurrence after cessation of, or breaks, in dupilumab treatment of type 2 inflammatory diseases.6–9 LIBERTY AD SOLO-CONTINUE, a phase 3 clinical trial for treatment of atopic dermatitis, is the only prospective, randomized dupilumab withdrawal trial for any indication.10 Participants who achieved disease remission after 16 weeks of dupilumab were rerandomized to placebo and developed significantly worse clinical symptoms and disease severity at week 36, compared with those who continued treatment.6 In the SINUS-24 trial for chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, clinical and biomarker improvements with dupilumab were partially maintained after discontinuation at 24 weeks but eventually had recurrence off therapy by 48 weeks.9 There are no studies that investigated dupilumab cessation or treatment breaks in EoE. To our knowledge, this is the first case report to highlight the impact of biologic interruption in EoE with sustained remission up to 26 months.

The rapid improvement in symptoms seen in this patient aligns with expectations of dupilumab response seen across other type 2 disorders. A post hoc analysis across 5 phase 3 studies looking at individuals with moderate to severe asthma, atopic dermatitis, or chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps showed rapid improvements in symptoms as early as 2 weeks into treatment with dupilumab.11 None of these studies, however, explored differences in outcomes with treatment cessation compared with continued treatment. While there is recent exploration of q2weekly dosing of dupilumab, drug holidays in EoE treatment have not yet been reported.12

In conclusion, 26 months of remission were achieved with a short course of dupilumab. However, this is not a treatment strategy to maintain remission, an important long-term goal in EoE to avoid risks of stricture formation and recurrent food impactions.1,2 The results are in keeping with the literature that shows high rates of dermatitis recurrence with cessation of dupilumab.7 To our knowledge, this is the first time such results have been reported in EoE.

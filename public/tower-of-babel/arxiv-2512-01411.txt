Quaternionic stochastic areas on quaternionic full flag manifolds and applications
Fabrice Baudoin, Teije Kuijper, Jing Wang

Abstract
We show that a Brownian motion on the quaternionic full flag manifold can be represented as a matrix-valued diffusion obtained in a simple way from a symplectic Brownian motion. By relating its radial dynamics to the Brownian motion on the quaternionic sphere, an explicit formula for the characteristic function of the joint distribution of the quaternionic stochastic areas is obtained. The limit law of these quaternionic stochastic areas is shown to be a multivariate normal distribution with zero mean and non-diagonal covariance matrix. These results are subsequently applied to establish new results about simultaneous quaternionic windings on the quaternionic spheres.



1
Introduction

Stochastic area processes associated with Brownian motion on manifolds have played an
important role in probability on Lie groups, geometric analysis, and the study of integrable
Brownian functionals arising from differential geometry. Their analysis
began with Lévy’s
[
14
]
celebrated description of stochastic areas on the plane, and was later extended to the complex Hopf fibration by two of the authors in
[
5
]
, and to the quaternionic Hopf fibration in the subsequent work
[
4
]
. We refer to the recent monograph
[
3
]
for an overview of the state of the art.

In the complex case, the contact geometry of the Hopf fibration and of complex flag manifolds provides a natural framework for defining and studying such functionals. In particular, the stochastic area processes on the full complex flag manifold have recently been introduced and studied in
[
2
]
. The quaternionic
case, although analogous at a formal level, presents a fundamentally different set of
challenges. The absence of a non-trivial canonical quaternionic calculus (see
[
7
]
) means that
quaternionic Itō-type computations quickly become intractable. While non-standard
quaternionic calculi do exist, see for example
[
9
]
, they do not lend themselves naturally to the analysis of Brownian motions on quaternionic manifolds. As a consequence, explicit stochastic area formulas beyond the quaternionic Hopf fibration have remained largely unexplored.

One of the aims of this paper is to initiate the theory of quaternionic stochastic areas to the setting of quaternionic flag manifolds, with emphasis on the
quaternionic full flag manifold
which parametrizes nested sequences of quaternionic subspaces

\{0\}\subsetneq W_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq W_{n-1}\subsetneq\mathbb{H}^{n}.

Quaternionic flag manifolds arise naturally in algebraic topology
[
15
,
16
]
, multisymplectic geometry
[
10
,
11
]
and even image processing
[
19
]
.
The full flag manifold plays a particularly important role, since all quaternionic partial flag manifolds may be obtained from it by canonical Riemannian submersions.

The identification

F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}

as a Riemannian homogeneous space provides a representation of the full flag well suited to stochastic analysis computations. Our first contribution is the construction of Brownian motion on the quaternionic full
flag manifold using the symplectic Brownian motion on
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
and its projection under the Riemannian submersion

\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(n)\longrightarrow F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

Our approach, and this is one of the main technical novelties compared to
[
4
]
of the paper, avoids quaternionic Itō’s formula computations entirely. Instead, we only work with the geometry of the above fibration. For instance, a key
feature of this construction is that the projection of a symplectic Brownian motion onto its last row yields a Brownian motion on the quaternionic sphere
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
. This
representation allows us to identify the
radial
dynamics of the flag Brownian motion
explicitly and we find that the squared radial variables form a Jacobi diffusion on the simplex of index

(3/2,\dots,3/2)
.

We then introduce the
quaternionic stochastic area processes
associated with Brownian
motion on the flag manifold. These are defined using the connection form of the above
fibration and generalize the quaternionic area process on the Hopf fibration. Using a
local trivialization compatible with the quaternionic affine coordinates on the flag
manifold, we show that the horizontal lift of Brownian motion admits a skew-product
representation whose vertical component is described precisely by these area processes.
This also yields an interesting and new skew-product decomposition for the symplectic Brownian motion itself.

With this in place, we derive an explicit formula for the joint characteristic
function of the quaternionic stochastic areas. The computation combines the above
skew-product representation, the spectral decomposition of Jacobi semigroups on simplices,
and a Yor-type exponential transform adapted to the quaternionic setting. The resulting
expression reveals non-trivial interactions between the components of the area vector,
reflecting the inherent non-commutativity of quaternionic multiplication.

Our second main result is the description of the large-time behaviour of the quaternionic
stochastic areas. After an appropriate renormalization, the vector of area processes
converges in distribution to a centered multivariate normal law with explicitly computable,
non-diagonal covariance matrix. Unlike in the complex case, where the limiting components
are independent, the quaternionic limit exhibits a full correlation structure governed by
the geometry of the fibration. This is one of the striking manifestations of the difference between
complex and quaternionic stochastic areas.

Finally, we apply these results to the study of
simultaneous quaternionic winding
of
Brownian motion on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
. By expressing winding
functionals in terms of the quaternionic stochastic areas, we obtain a multivariate central limit theorem for the winding on the quaternionic sphere.
This extends and generalises the classical results for real and complex windings and provides the first such result in the
quaternionic setting beyond the Hopf fibration.

The paper is organized as follows: section
2
briefly reviews quaternions, the compact symplectic group and the quaternionic full flag manifolds, it also constructs a Brownian motion on it as a projection of a symplectic Brownian motion and investigates its radial dynamics; section
3
introduces the quaternionic stochastic area processes and the quaternionic winding processes and derives a relation between them, furthermore it gives an explicit expression for the characteristic function of the winding process and determines its asymptotics; and finally section
4
applies these results to simultaneous quaternionic windings on the quaternionic spheres, in particular establishing its asymptotics.

2
Preliminaries and Brownian motion on the quaternionic full flag

2.1
Quaternions

We start by recalling the basics of the quaternionic field and setting up the notation.
The
quaternions
are defined as the non-commutative algebra

\displaystyle{\@index{\@indexphrase{$\mathbb{H}$ (quaternions)}}}\mathbb{H}=\{t+x\mathbf{i}+y\mathbf{j}+z\mathbf{k}\mid t,x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}\},

where
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}
satisfy the relations
\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=-1
,
\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{k}=-\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{i}\mathbf{k}=-\mathbf{j}=-\mathbf{k}\mathbf{i}
. For a quaternion
q=t+x\mathbf{i}+y\mathbf{j}+z\mathbf{k}
, its real part is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(q):=t,

while its imaginary part is the
3
-dimensional component

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(q):=x\mathbf{i}+y\mathbf{j}+z\mathbf{k}.

Its quaternionic conjugate is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{q}:=t-x\mathbf{i}-y\mathbf{j}-z\mathbf{k},

and its norm by

\displaystyle|q|^{2}:=q\overline{q}=\overline{q}q=t^{2}+x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}.

In general we have

\overline{pq}=\overline{q}\ \overline{p}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{Re}(q)=\frac{1}{2}(q+\overline{q}).

As a real vector space,
\mathbb{H}
can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{4}
in the obvious way. For quaternions
q_{1}=t_{1}+x_{1}\mathbf{i}+y_{1}\mathbf{j}+z_{1}\mathbf{k}
and
q_{2}=t_{2}+x_{2}\mathbf{i}+y_{2}\mathbf{j}+z_{2}\mathbf{k}
, we use the inner product

q_{1}\cdot q_{2}:=t_{1}t_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}+y_{1}y_{2}+z_{1}z_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(q_{1}\overline{q_{2}}+q_{2}\overline{q_{1}}).

The set of imaginary quaternions

\mathfrak{sp}(1):=\left\{x\mathbf{i}+y\mathbf{j}+z\mathbf{k}\mid x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

forms a Lie algebra with the Lie bracket
[q_{1},q_{2}]=q_{1}q_{2}-q_{2}q_{1}
, and is isomorphic to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
. Lastly, let us also recall that the unit quaternions form the Lie group

\mathrm{Sp}(1):=\left\{q\in\mathbb{H}\mid|q|=1\right\},

which is isomorphic to the Lie group
\mathrm{SU}(2)
.

2.2
Compact symplectic group

In the rest of this paper we focus on the case
n\geq 2

\mathrm{Sp}(n)=\mathbf{U}(n,\mathbb{H}):=\{\,U\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}\mid U^{\ast}U=I_{n}\,\},

\overline{a+b\mathbf{i}+c\mathbf{j}+d\mathbf{k}}=a-b\mathbf{i}-c\mathbf{j}-d\mathbf{k}

\mathrm{Sp}(n)

\mathfrak{sp}(n)=\{\,X\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}\mid X^{\ast}=-X\,\}.

\displaystyle\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{sp}(n)}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{Tr}(X^{\ast}Y)),\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{sp}(n).

Let
E_{jk}

n\times n
quaternionic matrix with
1
at the

0

Write

\mathbf{e}_{0}=1,\quad\mathbf{e}_{1}=\mathbf{i},\quad\mathbf{e}_{2}=\mathbf{j},\quad\mathbf{e}_{3}=\mathbf{k},

and observe that

\overline{\mathbf{e}_{0}}=\mathbf{e}_{0}

\overline{\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}}=-\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}

.

Off-diagonal generators (

,

Diagonal generators (

,
a=1,2,3

\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{4n^{2}}
by

h\cdot\partial_{q_{ij}}:=a\partial_{t_{ij}}+b\partial_{x_{ij}}+c\partial_{y_{ij}}+d\partial_{z_{ij}}.

Therefore

can be seen as a real immersed Riemannian submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{4n^{2}}

corresponding to the basis elements in

2.3
Quaternionic full flag manifold

The
quaternionic full flag manifold
is defined here as the Riemannian homogeneous space

F_{1,2\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):=\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n},

where we see
\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
as the subgroup of all diagonal unitary quaternionic matrices and the corresponding action is given by right multiplication.

More precisely,
\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
is the closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{sp}(1)^{n}=\mathrm{span}\left\{H_{j}^{(a)}\bigm|a=1,2,3,\ j=1,\dots,n\right\}

and the quotient space is the coset space

\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}=\left\{U\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}\mid U\in\mathrm{Sp}(n)\right\}.

From
[
13
, Theorem 2.4.1]
, there exists a unique Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
such that the projection map

\pi:\mathrm{Sp}(n)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}

is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers. The Laplace-Beltrami operator associated with this metric will be denoted by
\Delta_{F_{1,2\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
.

The vertical bundle
\mathcal{V}
of this submersion is the sub-bundle of
T\mathrm{Sp}(n)
tangent to the fibers. It is given by

\mathcal{V}=\{\mathcal{V}_{U}\mid U\in\mathrm{Sp}(n)\},\quad\mathcal{V}_{U}=\mathrm{span}\left\{H_{j}^{(a)}(U)\mid a=1,2,3,\ j=1,\dots,n\right\}.

The horizontal bundle, which is the orthogonal bundle to
\mathcal{V}
, is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\{\mathcal{H}_{U}\mid U\in\mathrm{Sp}(n)\},\quad\mathcal{H}_{U}=\mathrm{span}\left\{X_{jk}^{(a)}(U)\mid a=0,1,2,3,\ 1\leq j<k\leq n\right\}.

(3)

Note that
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
acts by isometries on
\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
, so
\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
is indeed a Riemannian homogeneous space. Moreover, we mention that the resulting fibration

\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(n)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}

is a special case of a Bérard-Bergery fibration, see
[
6
, Theorem 9.80]
.

The name quaternionic full flag manifold comes from the fact that it can also be defined as the space of full quaternionic flags in the right skew-vector space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
By a
full quaternionic flag
we mean a sequence of quaternionic vector subspaces

\displaystyle\{0\}\subsetneq W_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq W_{n-1}\subsetneq\mathbb{H}^{n}.

It can be easily verified that the full quaternionic flag is diffeomorphic to the quotient space
\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
. Consider the surjective map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(n)\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\hskip 4.2679ptF_{1,2\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

\displaystyle M\quad\longmapsto

\displaystyle(W_{1},\dots,W_{n-1}),

where
W_{j}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{H}}(Me_{1},\dots,Me_{j})
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
This map is invariant under the action of
\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
on
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
and thus descends to a bijection
\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}\rightarrow F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

We now parametrize (a dense subset of)
F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
using
quaternionic local affine
variables as follows. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}q_{11}&\dots&q_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
q_{n1}&\dots&q_{nn}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{Sp}(n)\biggm|q_{n1}\neq 0,\dots,q_{nn}\neq 0\right\}

(4)

and consider the smooth map
p:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}\times\dots\times\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
defined by

\displaystyle p\left(\begin{matrix}q_{11}&\dots&q_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
q_{n1}&\dots&q_{nn}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\left(\begin{matrix}q_{11}q^{-1}_{n1}\\
\vdots\\
q_{(n-1)1}q^{-1}_{n1}\end{matrix}\right),\dots,\left(\begin{matrix}q_{1n}q^{-1}_{nn}\\
\vdots\\
q_{(n-1)n}q^{-1}_{nn}\end{matrix}\right)\right).

(5)

Clearly for every
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathcal{D}
,
p(M_{1})=p(M_{2})
is equivalent to
M_{2}=M_{1}g
for some
g\in\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
. Since
p
is a submersion from
\mathcal{D}
onto its image
\mathcal{O}:=p(\mathcal{D})
, one deduces that there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi
between an open dense subset of
\mathrm{Sp}(n)/\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
and
p(\mathcal{D})
such that
\Phi\circ\pi=p
. This gives rise to a local set of coordinates on
F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Those coordinates are compatible with the metric in the sense that
p
is a Riemannian submersion, which implies that
\Phi
is an isometry. Those coordinates are only implicit, so we will rather work with the parametrization

\displaystyle w_{i}:=\left(\begin{matrix}q_{1i}q^{-1}_{ni}\\
\vdots\\
q_{(n-1)i}q^{-1}_{ni}\end{matrix}\right),\quad(q_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathrm{Sp}(n).

(6)

Notice that the domain
\mathcal{O}
of those variables can explicitly be described as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\left\{w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1},w_{n})\in\mathbb{H}^{n-1}\times\dots\times\mathbb{H}^{n-1}\mid w_{i}^{*}w_{j}=-1,\,1\leq i<j\leq n\right\},

(7)

which yields a nice parametrization of a dense open subset of
F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
by the algebraic manifold
\mathcal{O}
. This parametrization will be extensively used in the sequel.

Definition 2.2

is a diffusion with generator

The result follows after a straightforward computation.
∎

Quaternionic stochastic area processes and skew-product decompositions

In this section, we introduce the quaternionic stochastic area functionals associated with a Brownian motion on the quaternionic full flag manifold.

3.3

From the expression (

Theorem 3.9

Let
(w(t))_{t\geq 0}

(\mathfrak{a}(t))_{t\geq 0}

\displaystyle L:=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}^{2}}-2\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}\partial\lambda_{\ell}}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2-2n\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}-1\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial(\mathfrak{a}^{\alpha}_{j})^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\mathfrak{a}^{\alpha}_{j}\partial\mathfrak{a}^{\alpha}_{\ell}}.

The quadratic covariations between

It remains to calculate the quadratic covariations between

\lambda_{\ell}

and aim to compute the quadratic covariations

\left\langle\int_{w[0,\cdot]}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{a},\int_{w[0,\cdot]}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{b}\right\rangle(t),\quad{a,b=1,2,3}

(w(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Brownian motion on

\left\langle\int_{w[0,\cdot]}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{a},\int_{w[0,\cdot]}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{b}\right\rangle(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{F_{n}}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{a},\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{b})(w(s))ds,

where

is the Riemannian cometric on
F_{1,2,\dots n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

Thus we are reduced to computing

Recall the relation (

\displaystyle\eta_{i}(g)=\omega_{i}(g)+\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(\pi^{*}\mathfrak{a}_{i}(g)),

Since

g_{F_{n}}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{a},\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{b})=g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}((\omega_{i}^{a})_{\mathcal{H}},(\omega_{j}^{b})_{\mathcal{H}}),\quad a,b=1,2,3,\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

where

\omega_{i}=\omega_{i}^{1}\mathbf{i}+\omega_{i}^{2}\mathbf{j}+\omega_{i}^{3}\mathbf{k}

and
(\omega_{i}^{a})_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the horizontal part of the one-form
\omega_{i}^{a}
, i.e. the orthogonal projection of
\omega_{i}^{a}
onto the orthogonal complement of
\eta
.
Recall that the vector fields

X^{(a)}_{jk}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}((q_{\ell j}\mathbf{e}_{a})\cdot\partial_{q_{\ell k}}-(q_{\ell k}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{a})\cdot\partial_{q_{\ell j}}),\quad{a=0,1,2,3,\ 1\leq j<k\leq n}

form an orthonormal frame of the horizontal bundle.
Therefore, for any
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\{1,2,3\}
and
1\leq l,r\leq n
,

g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}((\omega_{l}^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{H}},(\omega_{r}^{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{a=0}^{3}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{l}^{\alpha},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk})g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{r}^{\alpha^{\prime}},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk}),

where
\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk}
is the one-form dual of
X_{jk}^{(a)}
, i.e.
\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk}=g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}\left(X_{jk}^{(a)},\cdot\right).
Here we used the expansion

\displaystyle(\omega_{l}^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{a=0}^{3}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{l}^{\alpha},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk})\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk}.

We can now perform the computation in real coordinates, using the following inner product notation: for
h=t+x\mathbf{i}+y\mathbf{j}+z\mathbf{k}
, we write

h\cdot dq_{ij}=tdt_{ij}+xdx_{ij}+ydy_{ij}+zdz_{ij}.

From
[
3
, Page 30]
we have for any
1\leq\alpha\leq 3
,
1\leq l\leq n
that

\omega_{l}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{|q_{nl}|^{2}}(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})\cdot dq_{nl}.

Moreover, it is easy to compute that

\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}((q_{ij}\mathbf{e}_{a})\cdot dq_{ik}-(q_{ik}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{a})\cdot dq_{ij}).

Finally, recall that
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
is an immersed submanifold of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4n^{2}}
, equipped with the inner product equal to
1/2
of the standard Euclidean inner product. Therefore, for any
1\leq l<r\leq n
, we compute

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}((\omega_{l}^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{H}},(\omega_{r}^{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{3}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{l}^{\alpha},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk})g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{r}^{\alpha^{\prime}},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jk})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{3}g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{l}^{\alpha},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{lr})g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{r}^{\alpha^{\prime}},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{lr})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|q_{nl}|^{2}|q_{nr}|^{2}}\sum_{a=0}^{3}\left(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}\cdot q_{nl}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{a}\right)\left(q_{nr}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot q_{nr}\mathbf{e}_{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|q_{nl}|^{2}|q_{nr}|^{2}}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}\cdot q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{a}\right)\left(q_{nr}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot q_{nr}\mathbf{e}_{a}\right)=\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

When
1\leq l=r\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}((\omega_{l}^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{H}},(\omega_{l}^{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{3}\sum_{1\leq j\neq l\leq n}g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{l}^{\alpha},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jl})g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(\omega_{l}^{\alpha^{\prime}},\mathcal{X}^{a}_{jl})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|q_{nl}|^{4}}\sum_{a=0}^{3}\sum_{1\leq j\neq l\leq n}(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})\cdot(q_{nj}\mathbf{e}_{a})(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha^{\prime}})\cdot(q_{nj}\mathbf{e}_{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|q_{nl}|^{4}}\sum_{1\leq j\neq l\leq n}(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})\cdot(q_{nl}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha^{\prime}})|q_{nj}|^{2},

where in the equality we used that
q_{nj}\mathbf{e}_{a}
,
a=0,1,2,3
forms an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Therefore, if
1\leq\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}\leq 3
we have

g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}((\omega_{l}^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{H}},(\omega_{l}^{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{H}})=0,

whereas if
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
we have

g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}((\omega_{l}^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{H}},(\omega_{l}^{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathcal{H}})=\frac{1-|q_{nl}|^{2}}{|q_{nl}|^{2}}=\frac{1-\lambda_{\ell}}{\lambda_{\ell}}.

In a similar way we prove that
\left\langle\lambda_{i},\mathfrak{a}_{j}\right\rangle_{t}=0
. Indeed,

\left\langle\lambda_{i},\mathfrak{a}^{a}_{j}\right\rangle_{t}=\left\langle\int_{w[0,\cdot]}d\lambda_{i},\int_{w[0,\cdot]}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{a}\right\rangle(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{F_{n}}(d\lambda_{i},\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{a})(w(s))ds.

Now, observe that the one-form
d\lambda_{i}=d|q_{ni}|^{2}=2q_{ni}\cdot dq_{ni}
is horizontal on
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
. Therefore, as before we deduce that

g_{F_{n}}(d\lambda_{i},\mathfrak{a}2_{j}^{a})=-2g_{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}(q_{ni}\cdot dq_{ni},\omega_{j}^{a})=0,

because

\omega_{j}^{a}=\frac{1}{|q_{nj}|^{2}}(q_{nj}\mathbf{e}_{a})\cdot dq_{nj}.

∎

3.4
Characteristic function of the quaternionic stochastic areas

We now turn to the computation of the characteristic function of the quaternionic stochastic areas. We will use the Yor’s transform method
[
3
, Appendix A9]
. We first need to introduce some notations related to Jacobi operators on simplices, see
[
2
, §2.2]
for further details. Consider the simplex

\Sigma_{n-1}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\mid\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\,1\leq j\leq n-1,\quad\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}\leq 1\}.

The Jacobi operator in
\Sigma_{n-1}
is defined by

\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left[\left(\kappa_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\left(|\kappa|+\frac{n}{2}\right)\lambda_{j}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}}-\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}\lambda_{\ell}}.

(23)

A diffusion with generator
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
is called a Jacobi diffusion in the simplex
\Sigma_{n-1}
. Here,
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})
is a parameter set such that
\kappa_{j}>-1/2
for any
1\leq j\leq n
and
|\kappa|:=\kappa_{1}+\dots+\kappa_{n}
. The operator
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
is symmetric with respect to the Dirichlet measure on
\Sigma_{n-1}
whose density is given by:

W^{(\kappa)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}):=\frac{\Gamma(|\kappa|+(n/2))}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\kappa_{j}+(1/2))}\lambda_{1}^{\kappa_{1}-(1/2)}\cdots\lambda_{n-1}^{\kappa_{n-1}-(1/2)}\\
(1-\lambda_{1}-\cdots-\lambda_{n-1})^{\kappa_{n}-(1/2)}.

(24)

The spectrum of
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
is discrete and is given by

-j\left(j+|\kappa|+\frac{n-2}{2}\right),\quad j\geq 0.

(25)

The corresponding set of orthonormal eigenfunctions consists of the so-called Jacobi polynomials on the simplex, which we will denote by
P_{\tau}^{(\kappa)}
for
\tau\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
.
We refer the reader to the monograph
[
8
]
,
and the papers
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
for further details about these polynomials. The density with respect to the Dirichlet measure
W^{(\kappa)}
of the heat semi-group
e^{t\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}}
reads

q^{(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n-1},\kappa_{n})}_{t}(x,y)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}}e^{-|\tau|(|\tau|+|\kappa|+(n-2)/2)t}P_{\tau}^{(\kappa)}(x)P_{\tau}^{(\kappa)}(y).

(26)

We can lift Jacobi diffusions in
\Sigma_{n-1}
to diffusions in the
n-1
simplex of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\,1\leq j\leq n,\,\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}=1\}.

(27)

It is easy to check that if
(\lambda_{1}(t),\dots,\lambda_{n-1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a diffusion with generator
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
, then the process
(\lambda_{1}(t),\dots,\lambda_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, where
\lambda_{n}(t):=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}(t)
, is a diffusion in
\mathcal{T}_{n}
with generator

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\kappa}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\left(\kappa_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\left(|\kappa|+\frac{n}{2}\right)\lambda_{j}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}}-\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}\partial\lambda_{\ell}}.

(28)

The operator
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\kappa}
is called the lift of
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
to
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and a diffusion with generator
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\kappa}
is called a Jacobi diffusion in
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
(\lambda(t),\mathfrak{a}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the diffusion process from Theorem
3.9
.
For any
u=(u_{1}^{1},u_{1}^{2},u_{1}^{3},\dots,u_{n}^{1},u_{n}^{2},u_{n}^{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3n}
, any
\lambda(0),\lambda
in the interior of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and any
t>0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}\mathfrak{a}^{a}_{j}(t)}\biggm|\lambda(t)=\lambda\bigg)=e^{-(2n-2)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}(\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}u_{\ell}^{a}+\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell})t}

\displaystyle\qquad\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}(0)}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}\frac{q^{(3/2+\mu_{1},\dots,3/2+\mu_{n})}_{2t}(\lambda^{(n-1)}(0),\lambda^{(n-1)})}{q^{(3/2,\dots,3/2)}_{2t}(\lambda^{(n-1)}(0),\lambda^{(n-1)})}\frac{W^{(3/2+\mu_{1},\dots,3/2+\mu_{n})}(\lambda^{(n-1)})}{W^{(3/2,\dots,3/2)}(\lambda^{(n-1)})},

where
q_{t}^{(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})}
is the heat kernel of a Jacobi process on the simplex of index
(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})
and
W^{(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{1})}
its invariant measure density.
Furthermore
\mu_{j}:=\sqrt{|u_{j}^{1}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{2}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{3}|^{2}+1}-1
and
\lambda^{(n-1)}:=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1})
.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.9
we know that, conditioned on
(\lambda(s),s\leq t)
, the quaternionic stochastic area process
(\mathfrak{a}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Gaussian variable with mean zero and covariance matrix

\displaystyle\Sigma(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}(s)}{\lambda_{1}(s)}ds&\dots&t\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
t&\dots&\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{n}(s)}{\lambda_{n}(s)}ds\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{3},

where
\otimes
stands for the Kronecker product of matrices.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}\mathfrak{a}_{j}^{a}(t)}\mid\lambda(t)=\lambda\bigg)=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}u^{T}\Sigma(t)u}\mid\lambda(t)=\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}(u_{j}^{a})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{j}(s)}{\lambda_{j}(s)}ds-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}u_{\ell}^{a}t\right)\bigg|\lambda(t)=\lambda\right).

It only remains to derive an expression for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}(u_{j}^{a})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{j}(s)}{\lambda_{j}(s)}ds\right)\bigg|\lambda(t)=\lambda\right).

Define the following function on
\mathcal{T}_{n}

\displaystyle f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}\textrm{ for some }\mu_{j}>0.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{3/2,\dots,3/2}
be a Jacobi operator on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
of index
(3/2,\dots,3/2)
.
By applying
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{3/2,\dots,3/2}
to
f
we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{3/2,\dots,3/2}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\lambda_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2-2n\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}-\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\lambda_{j}\partial\lambda_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}-1\right)\frac{1-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}f+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}-n\right)f-\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\frac{\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell}}{4}f,

which implies that
f
is an eigenfunction of the operator

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{3/2,\dots,3/2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}}{2}+\mu_{j}\right)\frac{1-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}

associated with the eigenvalue

\displaystyle-\left(n-1\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell}.

We choose
\mu_{j}=\sqrt{1+|u_{j}^{1}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{2}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{3}|^{2}}-1\geq 0
, since in this case
f
is an eigenfunction of the operator

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{3/2,\dots,3/2}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}(u_{j}^{a})^{2}\frac{1-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}.

Now we recall that
(\lambda(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Jacobi process in the simplex
\mathcal{T}_{n}
with generator
2\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{3/2,\dots,3/2}
.
Itō’s formula then shows that the process

\displaystyle D_{t}^{u}:=e^{\left(2n-2\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell}t}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}(t)}{\lambda_{j}(0)}\right)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}e^{-\sum_{a=1}^{3}\frac{1}{2}(u_{j}^{a})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{j}(s)}{\lambda_{j}(s)}ds}\right)

is a local martingale.
One can easily verify that

\displaystyle D_{t}^{u}\leq\frac{e^{\left(2n-2\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell}t}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(0)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}},

which implies that
(D_{t}^{u})_{t\geq 0}
is in fact a martingale.
We can therefore define a new probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{u}
by setting
d\mathbb{P}^{u}:=D_{t}^{u}d\mathbb{P}
.
We then have for every bounded Borel function
F

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(

\displaystyle F(\lambda_{1}(t),\dots,\lambda_{n}(t))e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}(u_{j}^{a})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{j}(s)}{\lambda_{j}(s)}ds}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{-(2n-2)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell}t}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(0)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{u}\left(\frac{F(\lambda_{1}(t),\dots,\lambda_{n}(t))}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(t)^{\frac{|u_{j}|}{2}}}\right).

Let
s_{t}^{(u)}(\lambda(0),d\lambda)
denote the probability distribution of
\lambda(t)
under
\mathbb{P}^{u}
and
\hat{q}_{2t}^{(3/2,\dots,3/2)}(\lambda(0),d\lambda)
the probability distribution of
\lambda(t)
under
\mathbb{P}
.
The above equality then implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}(u_{j}^{a})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{j}(s)}{\lambda_{j}(s)}ds}\biggm|\lambda(t)=\lambda\bigg)\hat{q}_{2t}^{(3/2,\dots,3/2)}(\lambda(0),d\lambda)

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{-(2n-2)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell}t}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}(0)}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}s_{t}^{(u)}(\lambda(0),d\lambda).

We can compute
s_{t}^{(u)}(\lambda(0),d\lambda)
using Girsanov’s theorem.
Note that since
(\lambda(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Jacobi process on the simplex of index
(3/2,\dots,3/2)
it satisfies the stochastic differential equations

\displaystyle d\lambda_{j}=2\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{j}}d\gamma_{\ell j}+2\left(2-2n\lambda_{j}\right)dt,

where
(\gamma_{\ell j}(t))_{\ell<j}
is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}
and
\gamma_{\ell j}:=-\gamma_{j\ell}
for
1\leq j<\ell\leq n
.
By Itō’s formula

\displaystyle d\ln(\lambda_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{d\lambda_{j}(t)}{\lambda_{j}(t)}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\lambda_{j}(t)d\lambda_{j}(t)}{\lambda_{j}(t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(t)}}\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}d\gamma_{\ell j}(t)\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}(t)}+2\left(\frac{2}{\lambda_{j}(t)}-2n\right)dt-\frac{2}{\lambda_{j}(t)}\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}\lambda_{\ell}(t)dt,

which, using
1-\lambda_{j}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}\lambda_{\ell}(t)
, simplifies to

\displaystyle d\ln(\lambda_{j}(t))=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(t)}}\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}d\gamma_{\ell j}(t)\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}(t)}+\frac{2dt}{\lambda_{j}(t)}-2(2n-1)dt.

This gives

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(t)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\mu_{j}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\ell}(s)}{\lambda_{j}(s)}}d\gamma_{\ell j}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{j}\frac{ds}{\lambda_{j}(s)}-(2n-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_{j}t\right).

Define the stochastic processes
(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
by

\displaystyle d\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell j}(t):=d\gamma_{\ell j}(t)-\Theta_{\ell j}(t)dt

and set
\tilde{\gamma}_{j\ell}(t):=-\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell j}(t)
, where

\displaystyle\Theta_{\ell j}(t):=\frac{\mu_{j}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(t)}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}(t)}-\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}(t)}}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(t)}.

By Girsanov’s theorem the process
(\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell j}(t))_{1\leq\ell<j\leq n}
is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}
under
\mathbb{P}^{u}
.
This means that under
\mathbb{P}^{u}
, the process
(\lambda(t))_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the stochastic differential equations

\displaystyle d\lambda_{j}(t)=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(t)}\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}d\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell j}(t)\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}(t)}+2(2-2n\lambda_{j}(t))dt

\displaystyle+2\sum_{1\leq\ell<j}(\mu_{j}\lambda_{\ell}(t)-\mu_{\ell}\lambda_{j}(t))dt-2\sum_{j<\ell\leq n}(\mu_{\ell}\lambda_{j}(t)-\mu_{j}\lambda_{\ell}(t))dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(t)}\sum_{\ell=1;\ell\neq j}^{n}d\tilde{\gamma}_{\ell j}\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}(t)}+2\left(2-2n\lambda_{j}(t)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_{k}\lambda_{j}(t)+\mu_{j}\right)dt,

where we used that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ell}(t)=1
.
In particular, the generator of
(\lambda(t))_{t\geq 0}
under
\mathbb{P}^{u}
is given by a Jacobi operator on the simplex
\mathcal{T}_{n}
of index
(3/2+\mu_{1},\dots,3/2+\mu_{n})
.
∎

3.5
Limit theorem for the quaternionic stochastic areas

We are now ready to prove a central limit type theorem for the asymptotics of the quaternionic stochastic area.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(w(t),\mathfrak{a}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the diffusion process from Theorem
3.9
.
The following convergence holds in distribution

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{3n}(0,\Sigma)\textrm{ as }t\rightarrow\infty,

where
\mathcal{N}_{3n}(0,\Sigma)
is a
3n
-dimensional multivariate normal distribution of mean
0
and covariance
\Sigma
with

\displaystyle\Sigma:=\begin{pmatrix}2n-2&1&\dots&1\\
1&2n-2&\dots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&\dots&2n-2\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{3}.

(29)

Proof.

We give two proofs for this result. The first one is based on the explicit formula for the characteristic function of
\mathfrak{a}
. Let
u_{1}^{1},u_{1}^{2},u_{1}^{3},\dots,u_{n}^{1},u_{n}^{2},u_{n}^{3}\in\mathbb{R}
.
From Theorem
3.10
one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\bigg(e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}\mathfrak{a}^{a}_{j}(t)}\bigg)=e^{-(2n-2)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}^{a}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}(\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}u_{\ell}^{a}+\mu_{j}\mu_{\ell})t}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}(0)}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}}\frac{q^{(3/2+\mu_{1},\dots,3/2+\mu_{n})}_{2t}(\lambda^{(n-1)}(0),\lambda^{(n-1)})}{q^{(3/2,\dots,3/2)}_{2t}(\lambda^{(n-1)}(0),\lambda^{(n-1)})}\frac{W^{(3/2+\mu_{1},\dots,3/2+\mu_{n})}(\lambda^{(n-1)})}{W^{3/2,\dots,3/2}(\lambda^{(n-1)})}d\mathbb{P}_{\lambda(t)}(\lambda),

where
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda(t)}
is the law of
\lambda(t)
.
It then follows by dominated convergence and formula (
26
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{j}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}}\right)=e^{-(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}|u_{j}^{a}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\neq\ell\leq n}\sum_{a=1}^{3}u_{j}^{a}u_{\ell}^{a}},

since

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{t(|u_{j}^{1}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{2}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{3}|^{2})+t^{2}}-t=\frac{1}{2}(|u_{j}^{1}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{2}|^{2}+|u_{j}^{3}|^{2}).

For the second proof we appeal to the classical martingale central limit theorem, see for instance
[
18
]
. Indeed, from Theorem
3.9
we know that the quaternionic stochastic area process
(\mathfrak{a}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale with quadratic covariation matrix

\displaystyle\Sigma(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}(s)}{\lambda_{1}(s)}ds&\dots&t\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
t&\dots&\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{n}(s)}{\lambda_{n}(s)}ds\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{3}.

Now, each of the
(\lambda_{j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Jacobi diffusion on the interval
[0,1]
with generator

2\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\lambda_{j}^{2}}+2(2-2n\lambda_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}}

and invariant probability measure
\pi(d\lambda)=(2n-1)(2n-2)\lambda(1-\lambda)^{2n-3}d\lambda
. Since
(\lambda_{j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a recurrent diffusion and
\lambda\to\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
is in
L^{1}(\pi)
, one deduces from Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem that a.s.

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}(s)}{\lambda_{1}(s)}ds\to\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\pi(d\lambda)=2n-2\textrm{ as }t\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, a.s.

\frac{\Sigma(t)}{t}\to\Sigma\textrm{ as }t\rightarrow\infty

and from the martingale central limit theorem, in distribution, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{3n}(0,\Sigma)\textrm{ as }t\rightarrow\infty.

∎

Remark 3.12
.

The sum
\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathfrak{a}_{j}(t)
converges in distribution to
\mathcal{N}_{3}(0,n(n-1)I_{3})
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 3.13
.

Using the second proof of Theorem
3.11
and the Rebolledo’s functional central limit theorem
[
17
]
we actually get a much stronger statement which is the following convergence in distribution of processes when
n\to\infty
:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(\mathfrak{a}_{nt})_{t\geq 0}\to(W^{\Sigma}_{t})_{t\geq 0},

where
(W^{\Sigma}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{3n}
with covariance matrix
\Sigma t
.

4
Simultaneous quaternionic Brownian winding on the quaternionic sphere

Consider the
4n-1
dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
and a Brownian motion

\displaystyle(X_{1}(t),\dots,X_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}

on it.
Assuming that
X_{j}(0)\neq 0
for
1\leq j\leq n
, we can consider the polar decompositions

\displaystyle X_{j}(t)=\varrho_{j}(t)\Xi_{j}(t)\textrm{ for }1\leq j\leq n,

where
(\rho_{j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
are continuous real valued processes with
\varrho(0)>0
and
(\Xi_{j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
continuous
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-valued processes for
1\leq j\leq n
.
Using our previous results, we want to understand the joint distribution of the quaternionic winding processes
\eta(t):=(\eta_{1}(t),\dots,\eta_{n}(t))
defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{j}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}(\circ d\Xi_{j}(s))\Xi^{-1}_{j}(s)

and study its asymptotics as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Note that the group
\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
acts isometrically on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
by

\displaystyle(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\!\cdot\!(q_{1},\dots,q_{n}):=(q_{1}g_{1},\dots,q_{n}g_{n}).

This yields a fibration of homogeneous spaces

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}\rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}{\mathrm{Sp}(n-1)\times\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}}.

(30)

We will denote the corresponding canonical projection by
\pi
. Similarly we have, for
1\leq j\leq n
, the action of
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
given by

\displaystyle g\cdot(q_{1},\dots,q_{n}):=(q_{1},\dots,q_{j}g,\dots,q_{n}).

The corresponding projection will be denoted by
\pi_{j}
.
We will define the
j^{\textrm{th}}
-vertical space of the action by
\mathcal{V}_{j}:=\ker(d\pi_{j})
.
Note that the vertical space of the fibration (
30
), i.e.
\mathcal{V}:=\ker(d\pi)
, is given by
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{n}
.
Due to the fibration (
30
) the standard Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
can be decomposed orthogonally as

\displaystyle g_{\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}}=g_{\mathcal{H}}\oplus g_{\mathcal{V}_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus g_{\mathcal{V}_{n}}.

Let
\mu:=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
be a multi-index with
\mu_{j}>0
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, the canonical variation of the metric is given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu}:=g_{\mathcal{H}}\oplus\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{2}}g_{\mathcal{V}_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\frac{1}{\mu_{n}^{2}}g_{\mathcal{V}_{n}}

and the corresponding Laplace-Beltrami operator by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{\mu}^{4n-1}}=\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}^{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{V}_{j}},

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the horizontal Laplacian of the fibration (
30
) and
\Delta_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}
are the vertical Laplacians.
We will denote
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
with the metric
g_{\mu}
by
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}_{\mu}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(w(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a Brownian motion on the quaternionic full flag manifold
F_{1,2,\dots,n-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
with quaternionic stochastic area process
(\mathfrak{a}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(\beta(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a Brownian motion on
\mathrm{Sp}(1)^{n}
independent from
(w(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
The
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
-valued process

\displaystyle X_{\mu}(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\Theta_{1}(t)\beta_{1}(\mu_{1}^{2}t)}{\sqrt{1+|w_{1}(t)|^{2}}},\dots,\frac{\Theta_{n}(t)\beta_{n}(\mu_{n}^{2}t)}{\sqrt{1+|w_{n}(t)|}}\end{pmatrix}

is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}_{\mu}
, where
(\Theta(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the process defined in Proposition
3.7
.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.8
and the fact that the last row projection is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers we deduce that the process

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\Theta_{1}(t)\beta_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{1+|w_{1}(t)|^{2}}},\dots,\frac{\Theta_{n}(t)\beta_{n}(t)}{\sqrt{1+|w_{n}(t)|}}\end{pmatrix}_{t\geq 0}.

is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}
, i.e. a diffusion with generator

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}}=\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}

with the notation from before. The result now follows from a simple time-change of
\beta_{j}
which rescales
\Delta_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}
.
∎

We deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(X_{\mu}(t))_{t\geq 0}:=(X^{1}_{\mu}(t),\dots,X^{n}_{\mu}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}_{\mu}
such that
X^{j}(0)\neq 0
for
1\leq j\leq n
.
Let
(\eta^{1}_{\mu}(t),\dots,\eta^{n}_{\mu}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the
\mathfrak{sp}(1)^{n}
-valued stochastic processes defined by

\displaystyle\eta^{j}_{\mu}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}(\circ d\Xi_{j}(s))\Xi_{j}(s)^{-1}\textrm{ for }1\leq j\leq n,

where
(\Xi_{j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-valued process such that

\displaystyle X^{j}_{\mu}(t)=|X^{j}_{\mu}(t)|\Xi_{j}(t)\textrm{ for }1\leq j\leq n.

The following convergence holds in distribution

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}(\eta^{1}_{\mu}(t),\dots,\eta^{n}_{\mu}(t))\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma+\mathrm{diag}(\mu_{1}I_{3},\dots,\mu_{n}I_{3}))\textrm{ as }t\rightarrow\infty,

where
\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma+\mathrm{diag}(\mu_{1}I_{3},\dots,\mu_{n}I_{3}))
is a multivariate normal distribution of mean
0
and covariance
\Sigma+\mathrm{diag}(\mu_{1}I_{3},\dots,\mu_{n}I_{3})
with
\Sigma
as in (
29
).

Proof.

From Theorem
4.1
we have in distribution

\displaystyle\Xi_{j}(t)=\Theta_{j}(t)\beta_{j}(\mu_{j}^{2}t)

and therefore, for some Brownian motion
(A(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathfrak{sp}(1)^{n}
independent from
(\Theta(t))_{t\geq 0}
,

\displaystyle\eta^{j}_{\mu}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(\Theta_{j}(s)\circ d\beta_{j}(\mu_{j}^{2}s)+\circ d\Theta_{j}(s)\beta_{j}(\mu_{j}^{2}s))\beta_{j}^{-1}(\mu_{j}^{2}s)\Theta^{-1}_{j}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\int_{0}^{t}\Theta_{j}(s)\circ dA_{j}(s)\Theta^{-1}_{j}(s)-\mathfrak{a}_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\int_{0}^{t}\Theta_{j}(s)dA_{j}(s)\Theta^{-1}_{j}(s)-\mathfrak{a}_{j}(t),

where in the last step, to go from the Stratonovitch integral to the Itō integral, we used that
(\Theta(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(A(t))_{t\geq 0}
are independent.
Note that
\mathrm{Ad}
is an isometry, since the Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
is bi-invariant. It follows that

(\eta_{\mu}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale with quadratic covariation matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}t+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{1}(s)}{\lambda_{1}(s)}ds&\dots&t\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
t&\dots&\mu_{n}t+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\lambda_{n}(s)}{\lambda_{n}(s)}ds\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_{3}.

The conclusion now follows from the martingale central limit theorem as in the proof of Theorem
3.11
.
∎

1.
Introduction

It was conjectured by Littlewood that for any finite
S\subset\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in S\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi in\alpha}\Big|d\alpha>c\log|S|

with
c>0
an absolute constant. Littlewood also conjectured that as
S
varies among subsets of
\mathbb{N}
of cardinality
N
the expression (
2
) is minimized when
S=[1,N]
. The former conjecture was resolved by Konyagin
[
9
]
and McGehee-Pigno-Smith
[
10
]
, while the latter remains open. The work of McGehee-Pigno-Smith
[
10
]
implies that for any sequence
a:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
|a(n)|\geq 1
for all
n\geq 1
,

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}a(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha\geq c\log N,

with
c>0
an absolute constant.
It is expected that

(3)

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}a(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha

is small when
a(n)
is in some sense “additive”. For example, it is conjectured
[
7
]
that if
a(n)
is the indicator function of a set
S\subset\mathbb{N}
and there exists a
K>0
such that (
3
) is
\leq K\log N

for all
N\geq 2

then there exists arithmetic progressions
P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}
with
J=O_{K}(1)
such that,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\Big|\mathbf{1}_{S}(n)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq J\end{subarray}}c_{i}\mathbf{1}_{P_{i}}(n)\Big|=0.

with
c_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
.
It is reasonable to expect that multiplicative functions such as the Möbius function (denoted
\mu
) or the Liouville function (denoted
\lambda
) contain no tangible additive structure. As a result when
a\in\{\mu,\lambda\}
the
L^{1}
norm (
3
) should be as large as possible, that is
\gg N^{1/2-o(1)}
.
Lower bounds for
L^{1}
norms (
3
) with
a\equiv\mu
or
a\equiv\lambda
are the subject of works of Balog-Perelli
[
1
]
and respectively Balog-Ruzsa
[
2
,
3
]
. Recently the authors showed that in both cases the left-hand side of (
3
) is at least
\gg N^{1/4-o(1)}
. The proof of this lower bound crucially uses the connection of
\mu
and
\lambda
with zeros of
L
-functions, and we note that even on the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis stronger lower bounds are not currently known. Besides the Liouville and Möbius function, other multiplicative functions such as the indicator function of
k
-free numbers
[
4
]
or coefficients of modular forms
[
12
]
have received attention, with more complete results.

More generally one would like to assert that (
3
) is large (i.e
\gg N^{c}
for some constant
c>0
) whenever
f
is a multiplicative function. This is however false. The simplest counterexample is of course
f(n)=1
. Moreover
f(n)=\chi(n)
with
\chi
a quadratic character provides another set of counterexamples. Our first result shows that for real-valued
f
those are the only obstructions.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-bounded multiplicative function such that,

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)^{2}>0.

Suppose that there exists a
\psi(N)\rightarrow 0
arbitrarily slowly with
N\rightarrow\infty
such that,

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha\leq N^{\psi(N)}.

Then, there exists a quadratic Dirichlet character
\chi
such that,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1-f(p)\chi(p)}{p}=o\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Big)

as
N\rightarrow\infty
. That is for any
\delta>0
, the proportion of
p
(in a logarithmic sense) such that

|\chi(p)-f(p)|<\delta
tends to one.

To gauge the strength of the result, notice that until the recent work of the authors, already for
f=\mu
it was not ruled that the
L^{1}
norm is less than
N^{1/\log\log\log N}
for all
N
.

Corollary
1.1
concerns only real-valued one-bounded multiplicative function, we will now discuss the situation for complex-valued multiplicative functions. Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character of modulus
q
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be a real-number. A multiplicative function
f
defined by setting
f(n)=\chi(n)n^{it}
for all
n\geq 1
is called a
multiplicative character
and the real number
q(1+|t|)
is known as the

of

. It is not hard to show (using Poisson summation) that if
f
is a multiplicative character of conductor
\leq\Delta^{2}
and

is a smooth function compactly supported in
(0,1)
, then,

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\Big|d\alpha\ll\Delta.

Our main theorem is uniform in
\Delta
and
N
and classifies complex valued
1
-bounded multiplicative functions
f
such that

(4)

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha\leq\Delta.

The upshot is that (
4
) can happen only if
f
is at least in part overlapping with a multiplicative character of conductor
\ll\Delta^{2}
. We expect that in applications the uniformity in
\Delta,N
and
f
of our theorem will be useful. In practice assumptions that are valid for all
N
large enough, such as the one appearing in Corollary
1.1
are seldom available.

Main Theorem A
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function with
|f(n)|\leq 1
for all integers
n\geq 1
.
Let
c\in(0,1)
and
N,\Delta\geq 1/c
be such that,

cN\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}|f(n)|^{2}

and

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha\leq\Delta.

Then, there exists a real number
t
and a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
of conductor
q
,
such that
(1+|t|)q\ll c^{-3}\Delta^{2}
and

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta^{2}\leq p\leq N\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\Re\overline{f(p)}p^{it}\chi(p)}{p}\Big|\ll\frac{1}{c}

Remark 1
.

Notice that the conclusion is vacuous if
\Delta\geq N^{\eta}
for some
\eta=\eta(c)>0
. We could have therefore introduced the additional assumption
1\leq\Delta\leq N^{\eta}
without loss of generality.

The conclusion of our theorem concerns only primes larger than
\Delta^{2}
. At first this is a little puzzling.
If one generalizes our result just a little bit (as we will do now), then this restriction turns out to be optimal, thus explicating the origin of this condition.

Main Theorem B
.

Let
c,\varepsilon>0
. Let
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a sequence and
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a multiplicative function such that
|f(n)|\leq 1

for all integers

. Let
\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{c,\varepsilon,g,f}
be the set of all integers

, such that,

cN\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon N\leq n\leq(1-\varepsilon)N\end{subarray}}|g(n)|^{2},

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}|g(n)-f(n)|^{2}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon N\leq n\leq(1-\varepsilon)N\end{subarray}}|g(n)|^{2}.

There exists an absolute constant

such that if

and

with

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}g(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha\leq\Delta

and a primitive Dirichlet character of modulus

with
\rho(\varepsilon)=\exp(\exp(K\varepsilon^{-5}))
and

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta^{2}/q\leq p\leq N\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\Re\overline{f(p)}p^{it}\chi(p)}{p}\Big|\ll\rho(\varepsilon)

Remark 2
.

Notice that the conclusion of this theorem concerns primes in the interval
[\Delta^{2}/q;N]
rather than
[\Delta^{2};N]
, this comes at the expense of adding an explicit assumption that
\Delta\leq N^{\gamma}
with
\gamma
depending on
\varepsilon
and

. Moreover pretentious distance is bounded solely in terms of
\varepsilon
, this comes at the expense of adding the assumption

.

We note that Theorem A follows from Theorem B.
We record here another application of Theorem B.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{R}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-bounded multiplicative functions such that,

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f_{i}(n)^{2}>0\ ,\ \lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f_{i}(n)f_{j}(n)=0.

Let

with

non-zero.
Let
\psi(N)\rightarrow 0
arbitrarily slowly with

. Suppose that for all

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}g(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|d\alpha\leq N^{\psi(N)},

Then, there exists primitive quadratic Dirichlet characters
\chi_{i}
such that,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1-f_{i}(p)\chi_{i}(p)}{p}=0

for each

. That is for any
1\leq i\leq R
and
\delta>0
, the proportion of
p
(in a logarithmic sense) such that
|\chi_{i}(p)-f_{i}(p)|<\delta
tends to one.

We do not prove Corollary
1.2
, the proof is similar to the proof of Corollary
1.1
.

In general, the conclusion of Theorem B is sharp, i.e we cannot conclude any statement about primes smaller than
\Delta^{2}
in the pretentious distance. In particular to further improve Theorem A we need a method of proof that uses more than just the closeness in
L^{2}
of
g
to a multiplicative function
f
. To illustrate the cases in which Theorem B is sharp we focus on the case
q=1
. Consider any multiplicative function
f
with
|f|\leq 1
and such that
f(p)=1
for
p\geq\Delta^{1/2A}
. Let
L:=\Delta^{1/2A}
. Let
g
be any reasonable sieve weight approximating
f
in
L^{2}
and having level of distribution
\sqrt{\Delta}
. It is then the case that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}|g(n)-f(n)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}|g(n)|^{2}

provided that
A
is taken large enough but fixed. At the same time the
L^{1}
norm of
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}g(n)e(n\alpha)
is small because
g
is a sieve weight, in fact easily seen to be
\ll\Delta
. Since the function
f
was arbitrarily defined on primes
\leq L
we conclude that the hypothesis of our theorem is not strong enough to make any conclusion about the behavior of
f
on primes
\leq L
. Since

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\leq p\leq\Delta^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\ll\log A=O(1)

we see that we cannot assert anything about primes
\leq\Delta^{2}
in the pretentious distance.

We now present a brief description of our proof.
A more detailed description can be found in the next section. Consider the special case when
g=f
is a multiplicative function.
The guiding idea in our proof (going back to Balog-Perelli) is that if the
L^{1}
norm of

(5)

\alpha\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)

is small, then this is due to the majority of the
mass of the trigonometric polynomial (
5
)
accumulating in a set of
\alpha
of small measure.
When
f
is multiplicative it is reasonable to posit that this small measure set of
\alpha
corresponds to the major arcs

(6)

\mathfrak{M}_{Q,N}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\frac{a}{q}-\frac{Q}{qN},\frac{a}{q}+\frac{Q}{qN}\Bigr),

with
Q\asymp\Delta^{2}
. This is in fact what we show: if the
L^{1}
norm of (
5
) is small, then

N\ll\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{Q,N}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|^{2}d\alpha\ ,\ Q\asymp\Delta^{2}.

We then conclude by showing that the latter implies that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta^{2}/q\leq p\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\Re\overline{f(p)}p^{it}\psi(p)}{p}=O(1).

for some primitive character
\psi
of conductor
q
and a
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
q(1+|t|)\ll\Delta^{2}
.
The latter conclusion is optimal: If
f
is a multiplicative function with
f(p)=1
for
Q\leq p\leq N
and
Q
less than a small power of
N
, then one can show, by approximating
f
by a sieve weight and then applying Poisson summation, that

\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{Q,N}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|^{2}d\alpha\asymp\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}|f(n)|^{2}.

The same limitation arises also when
f
is equal to a character of small conductor on large primes.

1.1.
Generalizations and other consequences

Our main result can be extended to any
L^{p}
norm with
p\in(0,2)
. It seems likely that our methods can extend to the case of divisor bounded multiplicative functions for which efficient sieve majorants exists.
Finally, one can use Proposition A along with Voronoi summation and multiplicativity to show that if
\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})

are the Fourier coefficients of
\pi
, a
\GL_{d}
cuspidal automorphic representation, then

\int_{0}^{1}\biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(n,1,\dots,1)e(n\alpha)\biggr|d\alpha\gg N^{\frac{1}{d+3/2}-o(1)}.

This is new for
d\geqslant 4
, and is the first nontrivial power-saving bound known for the exponential sum in those cases.
We leave the details of this to the interested reader.

Notation

For any measurable
1

For a smooth function

Notice that this is not the usual Sobolev norm because of the inclusion of the term

3.
Outline of the argument

The proof of our main theorem is based on the following criterion.
We have made no effort to optimize the dependence on
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}
in the final bound.

Proposition A
.

Take a sequence
a:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with a decomposition
a=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{M}
be two measurable sets such that
[0,1]=\mathfrak{m}\cup\mathfrak{M}
.

Write

S(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}a(n)e(n\alpha),

and for

, write

S_{i}(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{i}(n)e(n\alpha).

Suppose that for some
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\Delta>0
with
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}<1
, the
S_{i}
satisfy
the following properties.

(A.1)

{\left\lVert(S_{1}+S_{2})\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}\right\rVert}_{2}\leqslant\delta_{1}{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{2}
;

(A.2)

{\left\lVert S_{2}\right\rVert}_{2}\leqslant\delta_{2}{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{2}
;

(A.3)

{\left\lVert S_{3}\right\rVert}_{2}\leqslant\delta_{3}{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{2}
;

(A.4)

|S_{1}(\alpha)|\leqslant\Delta^{-1}N^{1/2}{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{2}
for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
.

Then, we have that

{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{1}\geqslant\frac{1}{12}\Big(1-(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}+\delta_{3})\Big)^{3}\Delta N^{-1/2}{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{2}.

Given an interval
I

c_{1}(n;I)=\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\\
p\in I\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}1,

and

c_{2}(n;I)=\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{-1}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\\
p\in I\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}1\Bigr)

Since
c_{1}+c_{2}=1
, any sequence
a:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
can be decomposed as

a(n)=a(n)c_{1}(n)+a(n)c_{2}(n).

By a variant of Turan-Kubilius we know that
a(n)c_{2}(n)
has to be small on average in the following sense:

Lemma B
.

Let
a:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be any sequence with
|a(n)|\leq 1
. Let
I\subset[1,N]
. There exists an absolute constant
N_{0}
such that for all
N>N_{0}
,

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}a(n)c_{2}(n;I)\Big|^{2}d\alpha\leq 4N\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{-1}

Proof.

Since
|a|\leq 1
we have

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}a(n)c_{2}(n)e(n\alpha)\Big|^{2}d\alpha=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}|a(n)c_{2}(n)|^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}|c_{2}(n)|^{2}.

By the Turan-Kubilius inequality, the above is

\leq 4N\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{-1}

for all
N
sufficiently large.
∎

We define our major arcs to be

(7)

4.
Orthogonality results

The main purpose of this section is to collect a number of
results that allow to swap additive harmonics with multiplicative ones, that is,

e\Bigl(\frac{na}{q}+n\theta\Bigr)\leftrightarrow\chi(n)n^{it}.

Such results are essentially well-known and can be proven by a
combination of Gallagher’s Lemma and Mellin transforms.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
a(n)
be a sequence of complex numbers.
Let
W
be a smooth function compactly supported in
(1/4,4)
.
Then, for all
N\geq 1
and
X\geq 1
,

\int_{|\theta|\leq 1/X}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)e(n\theta)W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}d\theta\ll\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)n^{-it}W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}\min\Bigl(1,\frac{(N/X)^{2}}{1+|t|^{2}}\Bigr)\,dt,

with an absolute constant in the
\ll
.

Proof.

By Gallagher’s Lemma (see Gallagher
[
5
, Lemma 1]
),

\int_{|\theta|\leq 1/X}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)e(n\theta)\Big|^{2}d\theta\ll\frac{1}{X^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+X/2\end{subarray}}a(n)W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}\,dx.

On the other hand, by
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq n\leq x+X/2\end{subarray}}a(n)W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)n^{-it}W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}\cdot\min\Bigl(1,\frac{(N/X)^{2}}{1+|t|^{2}}\Bigr)dt.

∎

We also record the following simple lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a(n)
be a sequence of complex numbers. Then,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)e\Bigl(\frac{na}{q}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)c_{\chi}(n)\Big|^{2},

where

c_{\chi}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{q}\\
(x,q)=1\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\chi(x)e\Bigl(\frac{nx}{q}\Bigr).

Proof.

This follows from writing

e\Bigl(\frac{na}{q}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(a)c_{\chi}(n),

expanding the square, and executing orthogonality in
a
over Dirichlet characters.
∎

Combining Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.1
we obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

, and define the major arcs

Let

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)e(n\alpha)W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}d\alpha

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{|t|\leq AQ/q}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}a(n)c_{\chi}(n)n^{-it}W\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}dt+\frac{N}{A^{2}}\cdot\|W\|_{2,0}^{2}

\ll

By the definition of the major arcs the integral is

In this section we collect a number of mean-value theorems.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
a(n)
be a sequence of complex number and

be given. Then,

\int_{-T}^{T}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{2}\leq n\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{a(n)\Lambda(n)}{n^{1+it}}\Big|^{2}dt\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{2}\leq n\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{|a(n)|^{2}\Lambda(n)}{n}.

Proof.

This is
[
6

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
a(n)
be a sequence of complex coefficients. Let
N,Q,T>0
be such that
Q^{2}T\leq N^{1-1/100}
. Then,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{|t|\leq T}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}a(n)c_{\chi}(n)n^{-it}\Big|^{2}dt\ll N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}|a(n)|^{2}.

Proof.

By duality it suffices to show that for integrable
\beta(\chi,t)
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{|t|\leq T}\beta(\chi,t)\frac{c_{\chi}(n)}{\sqrt{\varphi(q)}}n^{-it}dt\Big|^{2}\ll N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{|t|\leq T}|\beta(\chi,t)|^{2}\,dt.

We put a smooth weight
\Phi(n/N)
with
\Phi\geq 1
for
n\leq N
and
\Phi\geq 0
on the sum over
n
. Opening the square we need to understand

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{n}{N}\Bigr)c_{\chi}(n)\overline{c_{\psi}(n)}n^{it-iu}

for characters
\chi\pmod{q}
,
\psi\pmod{r}
and
|t|,|u|\leq T
. Applying Poisson summation we see that the above is

\frac{1}{qr}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{qr}\end{subarray}}c_{\chi}(x)\overline{c_{\psi}(x)}e\Bigl(\frac{x\ell}{qr}\Bigr)\Bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{it-iu}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{x}{N}\Bigr)e\Bigl(-\frac{x\ell}{qr}\Bigr)\,dx.

Integrating by parts we see that the sum over
\ell
is constrained to
\ell\ll N^{\varepsilon}\cdot qr\cdot(1+|t-u|)/N
up to an arbitrary power-saving error, and in particular,
all the terms except for
\ell=0
are negligible. On the other hand at the central term we find

\frac{N^{1+it-iu}}{qr}\cdot\widetilde{\Phi}(1+i(t-u))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{qr}\end{subarray}}c_{\chi}(x)\overline{c_{\psi}}(x).

Moreover, the sum over
x
vanishes unless
\chi=\psi
, while in the case
\chi=\psi
it is equal to
q^{2}\varphi(q)
and the claim follows.
∎

The following is the classical hybrid large sieve.

Lemma 5.3

Let

From this we deduce the following result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
a(n)
be a sequence of complex numbers with
|a(n)|\leq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
denote a collection of tuples
(t,\psi)
with
\psi
ranging over primitive characters of conductor
\leq Q
and
t
ranging over
\mathcal{T}_{\psi}
a collection of well-spaced points in
[-T,T]
which is allowed to depend on
\psi
. Let also
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}
denote the set of points
(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}
at which

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq P\end{subarray}}a(p)p^{-it}\psi(p)\Big|\geq\frac{P}{\Delta}.

Then,

|\mathcal{B}|\ll\log P\cdot k!\Delta^{2k},

where
k
is the smallest integer such that
P^{k}>Q^{2}T
.

Proof.

In order to prove the lemma pick the smallest integer
k\geq 1
such that
P^{k}>Q^{2}T
. Then the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{B}
is

\leq\Bigl(\frac{\Delta}{P}\Bigr)^{2k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{B}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq P\end{subarray}}a(p)p^{-it}\psi(p)\Big|^{2k}.

We now relate this to a continuous integral by using subharmonicity: for every Dirichlet polynomial
D(s)
of length
N
we have

|D(it)|^{2k}\leq\log^{2}P\int_{|\xi|,|\zeta|\leq 1/\log P}|D(\xi+it+i\zeta)|^{2k}d\xi d\zeta.

Thus, it suffices to bound

\log P\int_{|\xi|<1/\log P}\Bigl(\frac{\Delta}{P}\Bigr)^{2k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{|t|\leq 2T}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq P\end{subarray}}a(p)p^{-\xi-it}\psi(p)\Big|^{2k}dt.

According to the previous lemma this is

\ll\log P\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\Delta}{P}\Bigr)^{2k}\cdot(Q^{2}T+P^{k})k!P^{k}.

Since
P^{k}>Q^{2}T
this is

\ll\log P\cdot k!\Delta^{2k}.

∎

Finally we need the following hybrid version of Halasz-Montgomery mean-value theorem.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote a set of tuples
(t,\psi_{i})
with
\psi_{i}
distinct primitive characters of conductor
\leq Q
and
t\in\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{i}}\subset[-T,T]
with
\mathcal{T}_{\psi}
a set of well-spaced points (i.e
|t_{i}-t_{j}|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
t_{i},t_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{\psi}
with
i\neq j
). Assume that the
Q^{2}T\leq\sqrt{P}
and
Q\leq P^{1/8}
. Then,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P/10\leq p\leq 10P\end{subarray}}a(p)p^{-it}\psi(p)\Big|^{2}\ll\frac{1}{\log P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P/10\leq p\leq 10P\end{subarray}}|a(p)|^{2}.

Proof.

By duality, it suffices to show that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P/10\leq p\leq 10P\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\beta(t,\psi)p^{-it}\psi(p)\Big|^{2}\ll\frac{1}{\log P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}|\beta(t,\psi)|^{2}.

To control for the fact that we have to deal with a sum over primes we introduce a sieve weight and add a smooth function
\Phi(n/P)
. Thus it suffices to bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\beta(t,\psi)n^{-it}\psi(n)\Big|^{2}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{n}{P}\Bigr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\leq z\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}

with
\Phi(x)\geq 1
for
1/10\leq x\leq 10
and
\Phi(x)\geq 0
everywhere else (and
\Phi
compactly supported in
(0,\infty)
).
Opening the square we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathcal{S}\\
(t_{1},\psi_{1})\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\overline{\beta(t_{0},\psi_{0})}\beta(t_{1},\psi_{1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq z\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\cdot d^{it_{1}-it_{0}}\psi_{1}(d)\overline{\psi_{0}(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}n^{it_{1}-it_{0}}\psi_{1}(n)\overline{\psi_{0}(n)}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{nd}{P}\Bigr).

We now execute the sum over
n
. Assuming that
\psi_{1}
is of conductor
q_{1}
and
\psi_{0}
is of conductor
q_{0}
, we get

\frac{1}{q_{0}q_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\pmod{q_{0}q_{1}}\end{subarray}}\psi_{1}(x)\overline{\psi_{0}(x)}e\Bigl(\frac{x\ell}{q_{0}q_{1}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{it_{1}-it_{0}}e\Bigl(\frac{x\ell}{q_{1}q_{0}}\Bigr)\Phi\Bigl(\frac{dx}{P}\Bigr)\,dx.

. Since we can certainly pick

, this will hold provided that

.
If the central term

is non-zero then
\psi_{1}=\psi_{0}
, and
q_{1}=q=q_{0}
. In that case the central term is equal to,

\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{it_{1}-it_{0}}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{dx}{P}\Bigr)dx=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\Bigl(\frac{P}{d}\Bigr)^{1-it_{0}+it_{1}}\cdot\widetilde{\Phi}(1+i(t_{1}-t_{0})).

Thus our main term is equal to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{0},\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\\
(t_{1},\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\beta(t_{1},\psi)\overline{\beta(t_{0},\psi)}\cdot\widetilde{\Phi}(1+i(t_{1}-t_{0}))\frac{\varphi(q_{\psi})}{q_{\psi}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,q_{\psi})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{d}}{d},

where
q_{\psi}
denotes the conductor of
\psi
. We notice that with a standard choice of sieve weight we have

\frac{\varphi(q_{\psi})}{q_{\psi}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,q_{\psi})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{d}}{d}\ll\frac{1}{\log z}\asymp\frac{1}{\log P}

as long as the conductors

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{0},\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\\
(t_{1},\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}|\beta(t_{0},\psi)\beta(t_{1},\psi)\widetilde{\Phi}(1+i(t_{1}-t_{0}))|\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}|\beta(t,\psi)|^{2},

as needed.
∎

12.
Pretentious multiplicative functions

Throughout this section, our main tool will be the following result originally due to Gallagher.

Proposition 12.1
.

Let
q\geq 1
be an integer and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
uniformly in
q(1+|t|)\leq Q\leq N
we have, for non-quadratic
\chi
,

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\leq p\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)p^{it}}{p}\Big|\ll\Big(\exp\Big(-\frac{c\log N}{\log Q}\Big)+\frac{1}{\log N}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\leq p\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p},

with
c>0
an absolute constant. Moreover for quadratic
\chi
we have,

(37)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\leq p\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\leq\Big(\exp\Big(-\frac{c\log N}{\log Q}\Big)+\frac{C}{\log N}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\leq p\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

with
c,C>0
absolute constants.

Proof.

See for example
[
8
, Theorem 5.13]
and integrate by parts. We point out that when
\chi
has a Siegel zero (
37
) is negative and this account for the one-sided inequality in our conclusion.
∎

Given
1
-bounded multiplicative functions
f,g
, and an interval
I
, define,

\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\Re f(p)\overline{g(p)}}{p}.

For any
1
-bounded multiplicative functions
f,g,h
we have the triangle inequality,

\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)\leq\mathbb{D}(f,h;I)+\mathbb{D}(h,g;I).

The following Lemma shows that if the pretentious distance
\mathbb{D}(1,f,g)
with
g(n)=\chi(n)n^{it}
is bounded and
f
is real-valued then
\chi
needs to be a quadratic character.

Lemma 12.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[-1,1]
be a multiplicative function. Let
\chi
be a non-principal, non-quadratic Dirichlet character of conductor
\leq Q
. Suppose that
|t|\leq Q
and
I=[Q,N]
with
N\geq Q^{A}
.
Let
g(n)=\chi(n)n^{it}
.
Then, for all
A
and
N
sufficiently large,

c\log A\leq\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}

with
c
an absolute constant.

Proof.

We notice that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\Re f(p)\overline{\chi(p)}p^{-it}}{p}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1-f(p)\Re\overline{\chi}(p)p^{-it}}{p}

using that
f
is real-valued.
Let
J=[Q^{\sqrt{A}},N]
. Then, the above is

(38)

\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1-f(p)\Re\overline{\chi(p)}p^{-it}}{p}.

We note that for
|x|\leq 2
,

|x|\leq h(x):=1+\frac{1}{2}\cdot(x^{2}-1)-\frac{1}{18}\cdot(x^{2}-1)^{2}.

Therefore, we have that (
38
) is at least,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1-h(\Re\chi(p)p^{it})}{p}.

Using the previous Lemma this gives that (
38
) is at least

\Big(\frac{13}{48}-\exp(-c\sqrt{A})-\frac{C}{\log N}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

with
c,C>0
absolute constant.
Since,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\gg\log A

the result follows, provided that
A
and
N
are sufficiently large.
∎

Lemma 12.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[-1,1]
be a multiplicative function. Let
g(n)=n^{it}
.
Let
I=[Q,N]
with
N\geq Q^{A}
and
|t|\leq Q
.
Then, for all
A
and
N
sufficiently large, and
|t|\geq 2/\log N

c\log\min(A,|t|\log N)\leq\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}

with
c>0
an absolute constant.

Proof.

If
|t|\geq 1
we let
J=[Q^{\sqrt{A}},N]
and notice that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1-f(p)\Re p^{it}}{p}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1-|\Re p^{it}|}{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1-h(\Re p^{it})}{p}=\Big(\frac{13}{48}-\exp(-c\sqrt{A})-\frac{C}{\log N}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}.

with
c,C>0
absolute constants.
On the other hand, when
|t|\leq 1
we can evaluate the sum by integration by parts.
In that case we pick
J=[e^{1/|t|},N]
we get a lower bound that is,

\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1-|\cos(t\log p)|}{p}\geq c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{c}{2}\cdot\log(|t|\log N)

with
c>0
an absolute constant, for all sufficiently large
N
.
∎

We then have the following result.

Lemma 12.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[-1,1]
be a multplicative function.
Let
\psi
denote a primitive character of conductor
q

and

.
Let
I=[Q,N]
with
N\geq Q^{A}
and

If,

\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}\leq c

with

and

is sufficiently large with respect to

\psi
is a quadratic character, and

\mathbb{D}(f,\psi;I)^{2}\leq Kc.

with
K>0
an absolute constant.

Proof.

If
\psi
is not quadratic, nor principal, then by the first Lemma,

c^{\prime}\log A\leq\mathbb{D}(f,g;I)^{2}\leq c,

with
c^{\prime}>0

This is a contradiction for all sufficiently large

Therefore
\psi
has to be a quadratic character. Let

If

. Thus, for all

\mathbb{D}(f_{1},g_{1};I)^{2}=\mathbb{D}(f_{1},1;I)^{2}+O(1)=\mathbb{D}(f,\psi;I)^{2}+O(1),

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p\log N}\ll 1.

It follows that,

\mathbb{D}(f,\psi;I)^{2}\leq Kc.

with
K>0
an absolute constant.
∎

\mathbb{D}(\chi,\psi;I)^{2}\geq\Big(1-\exp(-c\sqrt{A})-\frac{C}{\log N}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

Thus the claim follows for all sufficiently large
A
and
N
.
∎

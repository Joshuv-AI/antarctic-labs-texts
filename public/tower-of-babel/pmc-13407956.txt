Comparative Assessment of Behavior, Anxiety, Pulse Rate, and Pain Perception in Children Using Different Local Anesthesia Delivery Systems: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Sonam Yadav, Natasha Gambhir, Nidhi Gupta, Divya Singh, Priyanka Sachdeva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLocal anesthesia (LA) remains fundamental to effective pain control in invasive dental procedures. However, the act of administering LA can itself be a source of anxiety, particularly among pediatric patients, which may in turn influence their pain perception. Technological advancements, such as computer-controlled LA delivery systems, have emerged as innovative alternatives designed to enhance patient comfort and potentially reduce the pain and anxiety associated with conventional injection techniques.AimTo compare and evaluate behavior, anxiety, pulse rate, and pain perception in children using StarPen, VibraJect, and conventional syringe systems.Materials and methodsIn this parallel-group randomized controlled trial, a total of 150 children (aged 6–10 years) undergoing inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) were randomly divided into three groups (n = 50 each): Group A (StarPen), group B (VibraJect), and group C (conventional syringe). Pain, anxiety, pulse rate, and behavior were evaluated during and after injection using the Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability (FLACC) scale, Venham's Anxiety Scale (VAS), a pulse oximeter, and Frankl's Behavior Rating Scale (FBRS), respectively.ResultsStarPen demonstrated the most favorable outcomes, significantly reducing pain (mean FLACC: 1.19 ± 0.55), anxiety (mean Venham: 0.58 ± 0.54), and pulse rate (mean: 105.09 ± 4.37 bpm) compared to VibraJect and conventional syringe groups (p < 0.001). Behavioral assessments showed the least post-treatment decline in cooperation with StarPen, suggesting improved procedural tolerance. VibraJect also offered measurable benefits over traditional syringes but was less effective than StarPen.ConclusionStarPen demonstrated superior outcomes across all evaluated parameters, supporting its efficacy as a behavior-management tool and pain-minimizing device in pediatric dentistry.

Introduction

Anxiety and pain are closely intertwined in dental settings, particularly among pediatric patients. Children with elevated anxiety levels tend to exhibit a reduced pain threshold, rendering them more susceptible to discomfort during dental interventions.1 Conversely, experiencing pain can intensify anxiety, creating a self-perpetuating cycle that may result in treatment avoidance.2 Inadequate control of pain not only reinforces fear but also contributes to negative behavioral responses, posing a significant obstacle for clinicians aiming to foster a positive dental experience in children. Consequently, minimizing distress and discomfort during treatment is a fundamental objective in pediatric dentistry.2 Local anesthesia (LA) remains the cornerstone for alleviating procedural pain in young patients.3

Despite its effectiveness in numbing the operative site, the process of LA administration itself often triggers anxiety and adverse behavior in children, particularly before and after the injection.3,4 Traditional syringe-based delivery methods have been associated with discomfort during both needle insertion and anesthetic deposition. This approach can elevate perceived pain and emotional distress due to factors such as visible needles, puncture pain, and an uncontrolled rate of anesthetic flow.5 Such experiences may lead to negative associations and increased anxiety.

To address these concerns, various alternative techniques have been explored to enhance patient comfort and minimize pain perception. One such approach is vibratory stimulation, exemplified by VibraJect, which operates on the gate control theory by introducing a counter-stimulation at the injection site to distract the patient and reduce pain perception.6 Studies have demonstrated that children experience less pain with VibraJect compared to conventional techniques, enhancing comfort during injections.7 Another innovative alternative is computer-controlled local anesthetic delivery (CCLAD) systems, such as StarPen, which integrates artificial intelligence–driven pressure feedback technology to regulate injection speed and pressure, providing a controlled and potentially painless anesthetic experience.8

StarPen (2021), a recently introduced electronic dental anesthesia delivery system by Guilin Woodpecker Medical Instrument Co., Ltd., is designed to enhance patient comfort and minimize discomfort during local anesthetic administration. The StarPen system includes a charging base, handpiece, cartridge holder, and power adapter, designed to facilitate ease of use for both subcutaneous and intramuscular injections in dental settings. It is compatible with standard gauge needles and features three adjustable speed modes—high, low, and hybrid—as well as two aspiration options, manual and automatic. Leveraging advanced artificial intelligence and real-time pressure feedback mechanisms, StarPen ensures controlled delivery of anesthetic by precisely modulating injection speed and pressure, ultimately improving comfort and minimizing anxiety in pediatric patients.8

Despite the availability of these modern anesthetic delivery techniques, there remains a critical gap in comparative clinical research assessing their efficacy and patient preference, particularly in pediatric dentistry. Previous studies have largely focused on individual techniques rather than direct comparisons, leaving a lack of clear guidance on the most effective approach for reducing pain and anxiety in children. The present randomized controlled trial aims to fill this gap by evaluating behavior, anxiety, pulse rate, and pain perception among children aged 6–10 years receiving LA via three distinct delivery systems—StarPen, VibraJect, and conventional syringe. By analyzing multiple psychological and physiological parameters, this study seeks to determine which method offers superior pain management and anxiety reduction. The findings of this study have the potential to enhance pediatric pain management strategies, contribute to evidence-based clinical practice, and improve overall patient experiences in dental settings.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Approval and Protocol Registration

This study received approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of the University (approval no. SU/2023/2480[11]) and was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The study has been prospectively registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India under the registration number CTRI/2025/07/090527.

Sample Size Calculation

Using Select Statistical Services software, a significance level of 0.05, power of 80%, and 95% confidence interval, the required sample size was calculated as a minimum of 50 per group. This was based on a prior study reporting a mean FLACC score difference of 1.31 and a pooled SD of 2.34.9

Study Design and Randomization of Patients

This randomized controlled trial was designed using a parallel-group format, and the study received approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of the University (Approval No. SU/2023/2480[11]). The research focused on children who visited the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry during a 1-month timeframe (June 2025). The study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines (Fig. 1).

Systematic illustration of study design according to CONSORT guidelines

A total of 210 pediatric patients between the age-group of 6 and 10 years were initially assessed from the Outpatient Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry. Following the exclusion of 60 patients—11 due to refusal to participate and 49 due to uncooperative behavior—the final sample consisted of 150 children. Eligibility criteria included no prior experience with dental anesthesia or injections, and a clinical requirement for an inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB). Only children classified as exhibiting “positive” or “definitely positive” behavior according to Frankl's Behavior Rating Scale (FBRS) were considered. Participants with systemic or developmental disorders or those who had taken antibiotics or analgesics within the previous 48 hours were excluded to avoid confounding effects on anesthetic response.

Prior to participation, the study protocol was thoroughly explained to parents or legal guardians, and written informed consent was obtained in accordance with institutional ethical standards. Using a computerized randomization process (GraphPad Prism, GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), the enrolled participants were evenly distributed into three groups (n = 50 each), each receiving LA via a different technique. Group A received anesthesia with StarPen, an electronic dental anesthesia system; Group B with VibraJect, a vibrating syringe attachment; and Group C with a conventional syringe.

Preanesthetic Protocol

To enhance the child's comfort and reduce procedural anxiety, the “Tell–Show–Do” technique was implemented before administering anesthesia. Preprocedural anxiety levels were assessed using Venham's Anxiety Scale (VAS). Baseline pulse rate measurements were obtained with a pulse oximeter 5 minutes prior to the procedure to standardize physiological responses across all patients (Fig. 2).

Anesthetic Technique Procedure

A topical anesthetic 5% lignocaine gel (Septodont Healthcare India Pvt. Ltd.) was applied to the dried mucosa for 1 minute prior to administration of LA to minimize needle insertion discomfort. To ensure procedural uniformity, all interventions were carried out by a single skilled clinician. Each group received 1.5 mL of 2% lignocaine hydrochloride containing epinephrine at a concentration of 1:80,000 as the local anesthetic agent.

In group A (StarPen), an anesthetic solution was administered using an electronic anesthetic delivery system in hybrid speed mode, with the autoaspiration feature activated. A 30-gauge needle was employed for the injection. The injection began with a low flow rate for 10 seconds, followed by a higher flow rate for approximately 50 seconds, totaling around 55–65 seconds of delivery time. Group B (VibraJect) received the local anesthetic using a conventional syringe fitted with a VibraJect attachment—a battery-operated device that generates continuous vibration during injection. A 27-gauge needle was used, and the 1 mL anesthetic solution was administered over approximately 1 minute. The vibration was intended to diminish pain perception by providing sensory distraction. In group C (Conventional), the local anesthetic was delivered manually over a similar duration using a standard syringe without any adjunctive devices or modifications (Fig. 3).

Different LA delivery systems: Group A: StarPen; Group B: VibraJect; Group C: conventional syringe

Outcome Assessment

Outcome assessment involved a comprehensive evaluation of pain perception, anxiety levels, physiological response, and behavior during LA administration using validated tools. Pain perception was measured using the FLACC behavioral scale, which assesses five indicators—face, legs, activity, cry, and consolability—each scored from 0 to 2, with total scores ranging from 0 (no pain) to 10 (severe pain).10,11 Anxiety levels were evaluated both pre- and postinjection using VAS, a six-point scale where higher scores reflect increasing anxiety from mild uneasiness to panic-like behavior. Physiological response was evaluated by recording pulse rate using a Gibson Pulse Oximeter at baseline, during, and 1 minute postinjection. Additionally, the FBRS was utilized to categorize patient behavior throughout the procedure into four levels, from definitely negative to definitely positive. All data were recorded by an independent trained observer to ensure unbiased statistical analysis and facilitate comparison of the efficacy across the three anesthetic delivery methods (Figs 4 to 6).

Statistical Analysis

Data were tabulated in Microsoft Excel and statistically analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 30.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni post hoc tests was used to evaluate differences between groups, while paired t-tests were applied to examine within-group variations. A p-value of <0.05 was set as the threshold for statistical significance.

Results

The study was completed by 150 children, with no reports of adverse effects such as allergic reactions. Overall, StarPen was associated with the lowest pain perception, minimal anxiety increase, and favorable behavioral outcomes. VibraJect also showed improved results compared with conventional syringe but was less effective than StarPen in all measured parameters.

Anxiety Assessment: Venham's Anxiety Scale

Anxiety levels increased significantly during treatment across all groups (p = 0.001; Table 1). Group A recorded the lowest mean anxiety score (0.58 ± 0.54), significantly lower than group B (1.90 ± 0.84) and group C (2.32 ± 0.91), with all intergroup differences statistically significant (p < 0.05). Within-group comparisons also showed significant increases from baseline in all groups, with the greatest rise observed in group C (mean difference = 1.54), followed by group B (1.24) and group A (0.24). These findings suggest that the anesthetic technique used in group A was most effective in minimizing treatment-related anxiety.

Pulse Rate Assessment: Pulse Oximeter

Pulse rate increased significantly during treatment in all groups (p = 0.001; Table 3). Group A exhibited the lowest mean pulse rate (105.09 ± 4.37), followed by group B (112.60 ± 5.07) and group C (114.62 ± 4.28), with significant intergroup differences (p = 0.001). Bonferroni post hoc analysis confirmed significant differences between group A and both group B and C (p = 0.001), while the difference between group B and C was not statistically significant (p = 0.089). Within-group analysis revealed significant increases from baseline in all groups, with group B showing the greatest rise (mean difference = 16.73), followed by group A (16.22) and group C (12.89).

Discussion

Pain is a complex experience shaped by both physiological and psychological factors in response to a noxious stimulus. In the context of dentistry, pain originating from the teeth or surrounding structures can profoundly affect an individual's overall quality of life and functional capacity.12 In pediatric dentistry, effective pain management is crucial for ensuring successful treatment outcomes and fostering positive dental experiences. Effective pain management in pediatric patients involves careful assessment and the implementation of a variety of methods, including both pharmacological and nonpharmacological interventions.13 LA remains a cornerstone of contemporary dental practice, playing a vital role in minimizing pain and anxiety during clinical procedures.

The current study assessed the clinical efficacy of three distinct local anesthetic delivery systems—StarPen (a CCLAD system), VibraJect (a vibrotactile-assisted device), and the conventional syringe—in pediatric dental patients. Parameters including pain perception, anxiety, pulse rate, behavior, and overall procedural response were evaluated to determine the most effective modality for improving the child's experience during IANB administration.

Our findings revealed that the StarPen device consistently outperformed both VibraJect and the conventional syringe across all measured outcomes. Notably, children anesthetized using StarPen exhibited significantly lower pain scores, as evaluated by the FLACC scale (Table 2). This supports the premise that controlling the rate and pressure of anesthetic delivery minimizes discomfort associated with tissue distention during injection. The ability of StarPen to maintain a steady, regulated flow of anesthetic likely accounts for this improved outcome. These observations align with Aggarwal et al.,14 which has demonstrated that CCLAD significantly reduces injection-related pain in pediatric patients. Patel et al.8 also conducted a split-mouth study comparing the StarPen with a conventional aspirating cartridge syringe and concluded that postoperative pain and anxiety were lower in the StarPen group.

In terms of physiological markers of anxiety, the StarPen group also showed a smaller rise in pulse rate following injection (Tables 1 and 3). Since pulse rate is a reliable indicator of procedural stress in children, this result suggests that StarPen not only reduces nociceptive input but may also attenuate the associated emotional response. This psychosomatic relationship—where reduced pain perception leads to lower anxiety—can improve child cooperation and foster a more favorable overall dental experience. Behavioral outcomes further supported this, as children treated with StarPen demonstrated significantly less crying, fewer disruptive movements, and reduced need for physical restraint, consistent with findings from previous CCLAD studies (Table 4). Dempsy and Prashanth15 similarly reported that CCLAD systems result in significantly lower anxiety levels than conventional syringes during minor pediatric procedures.

The VibraJect device, though less effective than StarPen, yielded improved results compared with conventional technique. Based on the “gate control theory” of pain, vibratory stimuli can disrupt the transmission of nociceptive signals, thereby offering some degree of analgesia.16 However, the efficacy of VibraJect appeared more variable, possibly due to differences in operator technique and the subjective nature of a child's response to vibratory distraction. Moreover, VibraJect lacks the automated flow regulation of CCLAD systems, which may compromise the consistency of anesthetic delivery.

In contrast, the conventional syringe group consistently displayed the highest pain scores, elevated pulse rates, and more negative behavioral responses. This group also had the largest number of patients classified as uncooperative post-treatment, as measured by Frankl's Behavior Rating Scale. McCartney et al.17 reported that approximately 70% of patients experienced moderate to severe pain during anesthetic delivery using conventional syringes. The limitations of traditional injection techniques, which often involve unmodulated solution delivery, erratic injection pressure, and intimidating visual appearance—factors that can exacerbate procedural anxiety in children.8

A crucial design advantage of the StarPen is its pen-like ergonomic structure, which allows for better control and a less threatening presentation compared to conventional syringes. Its hybrid-speed mode, utilized in this study (approximately 55–65 seconds per injection), permitted autoaspiration and consistent flow, reducing procedural duration while maintaining patient cooperation. The use of a 30-gauge needle, known to be better tolerated than thicker alternatives, further contributed to minimizing discomfort during mandibular block administration.18–20

Despite the encouraging results, it is important to note that literature on CCLAD systems is not entirely unanimous. For instance, Kandiah and Tahmassebi21 found no statistically significant difference in pain perception when comparing CCLAD and traditional syringe use in buccal infiltration procedures. Such discrepancies may stem from the challenges young children face in articulating their anxiety or pain levels, or from procedural differences in the type of injection administered.

A key strength of our study was its parallel-group design, which facilitated straightforward implementation and minimized the risk of carryover effects, as each participant received only one intervention. This design also allowed for simultaneous data collection across groups, reducing the overall study duration and logistical burden. Additionally, the study was conducted by a single operator, thereby minimizing variability due to operator performance. These methodological strengths enhance the internal validity and clinical applicability of our findings.

Limitations

This study offers important evidence on the efficacy of StarPen and VibraJect in pediatric dentistry; however, some limitations should be noted. The sample size, though adequate, may restrict the wider applicability of the results. The short-term nature of the assessment limits insights into long-term outcomes like dental anxiety and treatment adherence. Furthermore, individual differences in pain and anxiety perception introduce inherent subjectivity, and the economic and practical feasibility of using advanced devices in diverse settings remains unexplored. Future studies should address these aspects to support broader clinical adoption.

Conclusion

This study highlights that the StarPen device offers a more favorable LA experience compared to conventional syringes and VibraJect, particularly in terms of pain perception and reduced anxiety, thereby helping in behavior management. The ability to control injection speed, maintain steady solution delivery, and offer an ergonomic design makes StarPen an advantageous option for pediatric dental procedures.

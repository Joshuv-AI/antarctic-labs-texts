Intense pulsed light treatment improved tear film quality and reduced ocular surface inflammation in dry eye patients
Authors: Jiayan Chen, Ying Zhang, Sile Yu, Qiuyue Zhang, Xinyue Liu, Yang Xia, Wei He, Guanghao Qin, Xingru He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo evaluate the tear levels of Lymphotoxin-alpha (LT-α) and the clinical efficacy of intense pulsed light (IPL) treatment in patients with dry eye disease (DED). This randomized study included 28 participants with DED who received 3 IPL therapies at Week 0 (Baseline), Week 3(3W), and Week 6(6W) (N = 30 eyes), or 2 IPL therapies at Baseline, 3W and a sham IPL therapy at 6W (N = 26 eyes). All participants were examined at Baseline, 3W, 6W, Week 9(9W), and Week 12(12W) for non-invasive break-up time (NITBUT), LT-α, meibomian gland quality (MGQ), tear meniscus height (TMH), conjunctivocorneal staining score (CFS), meibomian gland expression (MGEx), tear-film lipid layer (TFLL), and ocular surface disease index (OSDI). Baseline measures exhibited no significant differences between the groups (all p > 0.05). At 6W, NITBUT, MGEx, CFS, and OSDI were significantly improved compared with the Baseline (p < 0.05). At 12W, a comparison between the 3 IPL therapy group and 2 IPL therapy group found significant improvements in the NITBUT (p = 0.002), TMH (p = 0.011), TFLL (p = 0.009), MGEx (p = 0.011), MGQ (p = 0.021), LT-α (p = 0.034), while the CFS (p = 0.104), OSDI (p = 0.189) had no significant improvements. IPL treatment can improve the clinical symptoms and signs of DED. The potential long-term benefit of 3 IPL treatments is better than 2 treatments.Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov, TRN: NCT0626923, Registration date: 21 February 2024.Keywords: Intense pulsed light, Lymphotoxin-alpha, Dry eye diseaseSubject terms: Eye diseases, Corneal diseases

Introduction

Dry eye disease (DED) is multifactorial, primarily characterized by ocular pain, visual disturbances (blurred vision and haziness), tear film instability, and hyperosmolarity of the tear film, which can lead to ocular surface damage1. Some potential causes of DED include aging, menopause2,3, Sjögren’s syndrome4, meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD)5, refractive surgery6, conjunctival fibrotic diseases7, and systemic or topical medications8,9. Insufficient tear secretion or excessive tear evaporation has been shown to lead to the concentration or hyperosmolarity of the precorneal tear film, thereby disrupting the homeostasis of the tear film. Hyperosmolarity of the tear film can induce cellular morphological alterations, inflammatory responses, cell damage, instability of the tear film, and ultimately exacerbate the hyperosmolarity of the tear fluid10. Standard treatments for MGD include anti-inflammatory medications, antibiotics, warm compress application, and maintaining proper eyelid hygiene11. Due to poor patient compliance, their long-term efficacy is not satisfactory.

Toyos et al.12 discovered that Intense Pulsed Light (IPL) treatment for facial skin rosacea could improve DED symptoms. In clinical settings, IPL is administered from the lower part of the auricle to the lower eyelid, including the nose13–15. Although the mechanism of action of IPL treatment for DED remains incompletely elucidated, the majority of writers argue that this therapy may result in the obliteration of superficial blood vessels16,17, subsequently reducing local inflammation18–20, and has antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects21. Previous research has documented the advantages of IPL treatment for various forms of DED. Research indicated that IPL therapy alters tear inflammatory cytokines, with enhancements in tear inflammation occurring prior to modifications in DED signs22. Nonetheless, these studies are limited to laboratory testing and are challenging to implement in clinical practice.

Lymphotoxin-alpha (LT-α) is a member of the tumor necrosis factor superfamily, secreted by T cells, B cells, and other immune cells23. The LTA discharged into the extracellular space forms a homotrimer (LT-α3), a soluble protein that interacts with tumor necrosis factor receptors to exert its effects. Although a growing corpus of evidence indicates that LT-α is significant in inflammation, the existing study remains contradictory. Previous investigations have shown reduced levels of tear LT-α in DED and ocular graft-versus-host patients24,25. High LT-α levels in DED patients appear to be associated with significant increases in multiple tear inflammatory factors26. This research aimed to investigate the therapeutic efficacy and underlying mechanisms of IPL, establishing a foundation for its application in the treatment of DED.

Efficacy evaluation

At the Baseline, 3W, and 6W assessments, there were no statistically significant differences between Group A and Group B (all p > 0.05). At 6W, non-invasive tear break-up time (NITBUT) (Group A: 4.82 ± 1.73 vs. 7.42 ± 3.35 s, p = 0.003; Group B: 4.88 ± 1.89 vs. 7.20 ± 3.38 s, p = 0.004), meibomian gland expression (MGEx) (Group A: 1.17 ± 0.59 vs. 0.80 ± 0.55, p = 0.043; Group B: 1.23 ± 0.71 vs. 0.62 ± 0.50, p = 0.035), conjunctivocorneal staining score (CFS) (Group A: 1.20 ± 1.54 vs. 0.33 ± 0.76, p = 0.016; Group B: 1.23 ± 1.23 vs. 0.38 ± 0.64, p = 0.011) and the ocular surface disease index (OSDI) (Group A: 20.00 ± 6.16 vs. 14.63 ± 4.25, p = 0.006; Group B: 17.39 ± 5.85 vs. 12.23 ± 3.96, p < 0.001) were significantly increased compared with that Baseline (Tables 2, 3).

The NITBUT for Group A averaged 7.46 ± 3.16 s at 9W, compared to 5.68 ± 1.74 s for Group B (p = 0.013). At 12W, the mean NITBUT for Group A was 8.13 ± 3.82 s, while for Group B was 5.43 ± 2.13 s (p = 0.002) (Table 2).

The tear meniscus height (TMH) for Group A averaged 0.20 ± 0.04 mm at Week 9 (9W), compared to 0.19 ± 0.06 mm for Group B (p = 0.853). At Week 12 (12W), the mean TMH for Group A was 0.22 ± 0.06 mm, while for Group B, it was 0.18 ± 0.03 mm (p = 0.011) (Table 2).

The tear-film lipid layer (TFLL) for Group A averaged 1.73 ± 0.53 at 9W, compared to 1.92 ± 0.48 for Group B (p = 0.044). At 12W, the mean TFLL for Group A was 1.61 ± 0.50, while for Group B was 1.88 ± 0.65 (p = 0.009) (Table 2).

The LT-α levels for Group A averaged 1.29 ± 1.51 ng/mL at 9W, compared to 0.58 ± 0.46 ng/mL for Group B (p = 0.020). At 12W, the mean LT-α levels for Group A were 1.56 ± 2.17 ng/mL, while for Group B were 0.61 ± 0.56 ng/mL (p = 0.034) (Table 2).

The MGEx score for Group A averaged 0.70 ± 0.47 at 9W, compared to 0.76 ± 0.43 for Group B (p = 0.568). At 12W, the mean MGEx score for Group A was 0.70 ± 0.47, while for Group B was 1.23 ± 0.99 (p = 0.011) (Table 3).

The meibomian gland quality (MGQ) score for Group A averaged 0.70 ± 0.47 at 9W, compared to 0.81 ± 0.69 for Group B (p = 0.493). At 12W, the mean MGQ score for Group A was 0.43 ± 0.50, while for Group B was 0.85 ± 0.78 (p = 0.021) (Table 3).

The CFS score for Group A averaged 0.33 ± 0.76 at 9W, compared to 0.31 ± 0.74 for Group B (p = 0.899). At 12W, the mean CFS score for Group A was 0.27 ± 0.69, while for Group B was 0.54 ± 0.51 (p = 0.104) (Table 3).

The average OSDI score for Group A at 9W was 12.13 ± 9.03, whereas for Group B it was 11.65 ± 2.67 (p = 0.795). At 12W, OSDI score for Group A was 9.80 ± 5.61, and for Group B it was 11.46 ± 3.22 (p = 0.189) (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, the improvement of seven parameters (NITBUT, TMH, TFLL, MGEx, MGQ, CFS, OSDI) before and after IPL treatment was observed. IPL treatment can improve the clinical symptoms and signs of DED after just 2 treatments. While both treatment regimens were effective, three IPL sessions provided more comprehensive benefits at 12W. A comparison of different IPL treatment regimens at the 9W revealed that only NITBUT and TFLL showed significant improvement after 3 sessions compared to 2 sessions. However, at the 12W assessments, there were no significant differences in CFS, OSDI between the groups. The improvement of symptoms in patients with DED after 2 IPL treatments can be sustained for more than 9 weeks, but the improvement of ocular surface parameters can last for 3 to 6 weeks. It is recommended that patients with DED undergo 2 or more IPL treatments for DED symptoms, which is consistent with the results of Qin et al.‘s27 study.

In this study, the changes in LT-α and ocular surface indicators were observed before and after the intervention. LT-α has recently emerged as an exploratory biomarker for diagnosing DED, and its mechanistic interpretation is still uncertain28. The application of IPL therapy in ophthalmology has emerged as a revolutionary treatment for DED, particularly in cases associated with MGD. In this study, the concentration of LT-α was detected by immunochromatography and a point-of-care test assay using a double-antibody method to determine the expression level of LT-α in tears of patients with DED after IPL treatment.

At the 2018 Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology Annual Meeting, LT-α levels were measured in a large, two-year follow-up clinical trial involving 1168 participants. LT-α in the DED group was significantly lower than the non-DED group (0.33 ± 2.82 vs. 0.99 ± 3.69 ng/mL, p < 0.01), LT-α tear concentration decreases in patients with DED. In this study, there was no significant change in LT-α levels from baseline at 6W. At 12W, LT-α levels increased significantly in patients receiving 3 IPL treatments (0.68 ± 0.73 vs. 1.56 ± 2.17 ng/mL, p = 0.040), whereas no significant change was shown with 2 treatments. Prior research has demonstrated that individuals with DED exhibit significantly reduced tear LT-α concentrations (< 0.8 ng/mL), in contrast to healthy subjects, whose levels are > 0.8 ng/mL26. After the patient underwent 3 IPL treatments, the LT-α level was 1.56 ± 2.17 ng/mL, which was higher than 0.8 ng/mL, representing therapeutic improvement. The observed improve in LT-α levels after 3 IPL sessions may be mediated by improvements in TFLL and a consequent decrease in the tear evaporation rate29. IPL treatment can improve the clinical symptoms and signs of DED. Our findings offer guidance for the clinical management of DED and highlight the potential utility of tear biomarkers in ophthalmic clinical practice.

The study has the following limitations. One limitation of this study is the lack of a sham control group for IPL treatment. Based on our previous finding that IPL sham treatment had no effect on ocular surface parameters27. It remains uncertain whether observed outcomes from the specific therapeutic action of IPL or from placebo effects, participant expectations, or other non-specific factors. The current study’s relatively small sample size (N = 28) and observed variability in LT-α levels with SD = 2.17. LT-α, where individual differences in ocular surface inflammation and tear film dynamics may contribute to measurement variability. Similarly, the subjectivity of measures like OSDI, particularly DED severity subgroups. This study was exploratory in nature. The results should be considered as preliminary findings that need to be further validated in a larger sample size and incorporate a rigorous sham-controlled study.

The results demonstrated that IPL therapy improved tear film stability, meibomian gland function, inflammation, and DED-related symptoms. Furthermore, compared with 2 IPL treatments, LT-α was significantly improved, and the ocular surface indicators have potential long-term benefits in DED patients who received 3 IPL treatments.

Methods

Study design

A statement to confirm that all experimental protocols were approved by He Eye Specialist Hospital (HESH) licensing committee (ethics approval number: IRB2023. K011.01). The study adheres to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and the Clinical Trials Act, registered at ClinicalTrials. gov on 21/02/2024, NCT06269237. This trial was undertaken at the Shenyang HESH in China, from March to June 2024. Informed and written consent was obtained from all subjects prior to their enrollment in this study.

The eligibility criteria: at least 18 years of age and having voluntarily participated in this trial. Fitzpatrick skin types I to IV. Diagnosis DED is established according to the TFOS DEWS II report30 (i) The score range for OSDI questionnaire is from 13 to 100. (ii) CFS signifies the existence of over 5 spots on the cornea and more than 9 spots on the conjunctiva. (iii) NITBUT is less than 10 s. A definitive diagnosis was confirmed by fulfilling a minimum of two criteria31. Exclusion criteria: existing infectious diseases, pigmentation, skin defects, ocular trauma, moles, skin cancer, scars, recent surgical history, autoimmune diseases, and skin allergies; pregnancy or lactation.

Treatment

Each patient underwent 2–3 treatments, with a three-week interval between each session. The IPL system used in this study was the M22, manufactured by Lumenis in Yokneam, Israel. The 500–1200 nm filter was adjusted to an appropriate energy level ranging from 11 to 14 J/cm² for the treatments. The IPL treatment intensity was chosen based on the Fitzpatrick scale (scale I to IV). Ultrasound gel is used to cover the targeted periorbital skin around the eyes before treatment, with all participants wearing opaque goggles during the entire IPL therapy process. The therapy included the administration of six light pulses, with each pulse slightly overlapping the previous one. Routine treatment areas for IPL include the skin below the lower eyelid and the temples on both sides; meibomian gland expression was not performed concurrently. Opaque goggles are required to cover both eyes during treatment32–34. All patients received the full planned dose and coverage.

The investigator who collected data at Baseline, 3W, 6W, 9W, and 12W from all subjects is blind to the patients’ group allocation.

Measurement

The concentration of LT-α was determined using an immunochromatography technique, involving the collection of 1ul of tear samples from the canthus with a capillary tear collector. A commercial reagent card (S05B, manufactured by Seinda Biomedical Corporation in Guangdong, China) is used for assessing the level of LT-α in the collected samples35.

Keratograph 5 M (Oculus, Germany) topographer was used to assess NITBUT and TMH. Specifically, an image of the inferior tear meniscus was captured instantaneously during the TMH measurement process36.

CFS score varied between 0 and 3 for each zone, resulting in a composite score of 0 to 9 for the ocular surface. A higher composite score indicates a more severe condition.

MGEx and MGQ were assessed utilizing a slit-lamp37. MGQ was measured eight meibomian glands in the middle parts of the eyelid using a scale of 0 to 3 for each gland (higher scores mean worse gland function: 0 represented clear meibum; 1 represented cloudy meibum; 2 represented cloudy and granular meibum; and 3 represented thick, toothpaste-like consistency meibum)38. MGEx was measured for five meibomian glands in the middle part were evaluated on a scale of 0–3 (higher scores mean worse): 0, all glands expressible; 1, 3–4 glands expressible; 2, 1–2 glands expressible; and 3, no glands expressible.

TFLL assessment was performed with the DR-1 equipment, produced by Kowa in Nagoya, Japan39. The results were evaluated in the range of 1–540,41. A higher score indicates a worse.

The validated Chinese version of the OSDI provides a quantifiable assessment of DED symptoms42. By analyzing the 12 questionnaire items, an individual score can be calculated (0-100, no symptoms to severe).

Safety assessments

The assessment of safety involved ECC (SP-3000P, TOPCON, Japan), IOP (NT-510, NIDEK, Japan) and BCVA (Decimal notation visual acuity chart).

Statistical analysis

Analyzed using SPSS (26.0; SPSS Inc., United States). Descriptive statistics for continuous variables were presented using the mean and standard deviation (SD), whereas number (percentage) was used for binary variables. The normality of the distributions was confirmed through Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests (p > 0.05). The Student’s t- test was used to compare parameters with a normal distribution, while the Mann-Whitney U test was employed for non-normally distributed values. To account for repeated measurements of continuous variables, post-hoc adjustments for multiplicity were implemented. For repeated measurements of discrete variables, a generalized linear mixed model analysis with Bonferroni post-hoc testing was utilized. The evaluation of the significance across various time points involved the application of repeated measures two-way ANOVA for continuous variables.

Sample size

The sample size was computed using the PASS 2021. The computation of sample size is predicated on NITBUT. A sample size of 28 (14 per group) is adequate for the NITBUT to identify a clinically significant difference of 1.05 s between groups in alleviating DED, assuming a standard deviation of 0.78 s43, utilizing a two-tailed t-test for mean differences with 80% power and a 5% significance level. Accounting for a dropout rate of 10%, the requisite sample size is 30 (15 per group).

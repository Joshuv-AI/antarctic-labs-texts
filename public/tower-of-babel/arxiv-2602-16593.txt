Convergent Twist Deformations Thanks: stefan.waldmann@uni-wuerzburg.de
Chiara Esposito, Michael Heins

Abstract
This paper establishes a functorial framework for convergence of Drinfeld’s Universal Deformation Formula (UDF) on spaces of analytic vectors. This is accomplished by matching the order of the latter with an equicontinuity condition on the Drinfeld twist underlying the deformation. Throughout, we work with representations of finite-dimensional Lie algebras by continuous linear mappings on locally convex spaces. This allows us to establish not only convergence of the formal power series, but the continuity of the deformed bilinear mappings as well as the entire holomorphic dependence on the deformation parameter ℏ \hbar . Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our theory by applying it to the explicit Drinfeld twists constructed by Giaquinto and Zhang, where we establish both the equicontinuity condition and determine the corresponding spaces of analytic vectors for concrete representations. Thereby we answer a question posed by Giaquinto and Zhang whether a strict version of their formal twists is possible in the positive.

Formal deformation quantization has been introduced in

Despite the impressive impact of these results and their many
subsequent applications, genuine physical interpretations demand going
beyond purely formal power series, since the deformation parameter

\hbar
should be regarded as Planck’s constant and not as a formal
parameter. This motivates the interest for
strict
versions of
deformation quantization. A common route toward strictness replaces
formal deformations with C

initiated by Rieffel (see in particular

star products via oscillatory integrals.

This leads to an alternative strategy
[
55

based on
functional analytic techniques: start from formal star products and
study their convergence properties directly. In several families of
examples this can work as follows. First, one identifies a “small”
subalgebra of functions on which the star product converges for
relatively straightforward reasons. Since general theorems are
lacking, one proceeds case by case. Next, one equips this subalgebra
with a suitable locally convex topology that makes the star product
continuous. Again, this step is mostly example-driven. If successful,
completing the subalgebra then yields a larger locally convex
algebra—typically a Fréchet algebra—suitable for further
analysis.

In finite dimensions this program may not look more decisive than
earlier methods, as it still offers no general existence
results. Nevertheless, it covers different kinds of examples,
including analogues of algebras of unbounded operators. In addition,
it is well suited to infinite-dimensional situations, where
oscillatory integral techniques are generally unavailable. In this
sense, the approach complements the established strict deformation
quantizations by providing new and structurally different examples. A
detailed overview can be found in
[
56
]
and more
recent works include
[

31
,

,

2.2
Analytic Vectors

Roughly speaking, the idea behind analytic vectors corresponding to a
Lie algebra representation is to implement the Lie correspondence by
demanding the convergence of the exponentiated representation when
applied to such a vector, at least for sufficiently small Lie algebra
elements. Making this precise results in the notion of analytic
vectors, where we moreover keep track of the resulting radius of
convergence with respect to our auxiliary norm. While analytic vectors
have been studied extensively since their inception within

[
12
,
40
,
29
]
, see also the
textbooks
[
49
, Appendix D]
and

[
57
, Sec. 4.4]
for more modern treatments, we will need
an additional parameter controlling the growth of their Taylor
coefficients, the
order
.

The usual analytic vectors then correspond to order
R=0
. Moreover,
negative order results in the Gevrey classes as comprehensively
studied within
[
50
]
, which are an important tool in
the theory of partial differential equations, and are an abstraction
of the function spaces Gevrey introduced within

[
25
]
. In general, they no longer correspond to
holomorphic objects by the Denjoy-Carleman Theorem

[
11
,
15
]
, and as such behave rather
differently. We exclude them from our considerations in the sequel.

Definition 2.4
(Analytic vectors I).

Let

\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)

i.)

For every

ii.)

The subspace

\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)\coloneqq\bigl\{v\in V\;\big|\;\forall_{\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V),\;0\leq r<r_{0}}\colon\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)<\infty\bigr\}

(2.19)

.

Let

i.)

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)

Let

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}_{r,\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert}^{(1)}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\big\lVert\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{j_{1}}\cdots\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{j_{n}}\cdot\big\lVert\varrho(\mathit{e}_{j_{1}})\big\rVert\cdots\big\lVert\varrho(\mathit{e}_{j_{n}})\big\rVert\cdot\lVert v\rVert

, as all terms are
non-negative. By continuity of
\varrho(\xi)
for all

\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and the convergence
v_{\alpha}\rightarrow v

within
V
, this in turn implies

\sum_{n=0}^{N}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}^{(n)}\cdots\xi_{n}^{(n)}\mathbin{\triangleright}(v_{\alpha}-v)\bigr)\leq\varepsilon

for all
N\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}_{0}
,

\xi_{1}^{(1)},\xi_{1}^{(2)},\xi_{2}^{(2)},\ldots,\xi_{N}^{(N)}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}
and
\alpha\mathrel{\succcurlyeq}\alpha_{0}
. Reintroducing the
suprema leads to

\sum_{n=0}^{N}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}(v_{\alpha}-v)\bigr)\leq\varepsilon

for all
N\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}_{0}
and
\alpha\mathrel{\succcurlyeq}\alpha_{0}
. Hence, the
limit
N\rightarrow\infty
exists by monotonicity, and we get

\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(v_{\alpha}-v\bigr)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}(v_{\alpha}-v)\bigr)\leq\varepsilon

for all
\alpha\mathrel{\succcurlyeq}\alpha_{0}
, proving
v_{\alpha}\rightarrow v

within the
\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)
-topology. Finally, the
triangle inequality yields

\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)\leq\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(v_{\alpha_{0}}-v\bigr)+\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(v_{\alpha_{0}}\bigr)\leq\varepsilon+\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(v_{\alpha_{0}}\bigr)<\infty.

Hence,
v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho)
, completing the proof of
completeness. Mutatis mutandis, the arguments for sequential and
quasi-completeness are identical. For the latter, the only
additional ingredient is Remark
2.2
,

iii.)
: Any bounded subset of

\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho)
is also bounded within
V
.

\ch@irxboxempty

Next, we establish the
\varrho
-invariance of the spaces of analytic
vectors.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)
be a
Lie algebra representation and
R\geq 0
. Then
\varrho
restricts
to representations

\varrho_{r_{0}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}\bigl(\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)\bigr)\qquad\textrm{for all }r_{0}>0.

(2.24)

Proof
:

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)
.
The idea is to view
\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}

as a power series with respect to the radius
r
on the disk around
the origin with radius
r_{0}
. In particular, we may plug in arbitrary

z\in\ch@airxmathbbm{C}
with
\lvert z\rvert<r_{0}
. Fix

\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)
. We estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(\xi\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\xi\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\xi\rVert\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n+1}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n+1}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lVert\xi\rVert\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n-1)!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n-1}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lVert\xi\rVert\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot n^{-R}\cdot\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r^{n}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\xi\rVert\cdot\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lVert\xi\rVert\cdot\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)

for all
0\leq r<r_{0}
, which also holds in the degenerate case

\xi=0
. As the differentiation operator is continuous on the
space of holomorphic functions
\mathscr{O}(\ch@airxmathbbm{D}_{r_{0}}(0))
on
the open disk with radius
r_{0}
, there exist
C_{r}>0
and

0\leq s_{r}<r_{0}
only depending on
0\leq r<r_{0}
such that

\max_{\lvert z\rvert\leq r}\big\lvert f^{\prime}(z)\big\rvert\leq C_{r}\cdot\max_{\lvert z\rvert\leq s_{r}}\big\lvert f(z)\big\rvert\qquad\textrm{for all }f\in\mathscr{O}\bigl(\ch@airxmathbbm{D}_{r_{0}}(0)\bigr).

As all coefficients of the power series

z\mapsto\mathrm{p}_{z,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)
are
non-negative, this implies

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)\leq\max_{\lvert z\rvert\leq r}\bigg\lvert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\mathrm{p}_{z,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)\bigg\rvert\leq C_{r}\cdot\max_{\lvert z\rvert\leq s_{r}}\big\lvert\mathrm{p}_{z,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)\big\rvert=C_{r}\cdot\mathrm{p}_{s_{r},\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)

for all
0\leq r<r_{0}
and
\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)
. Plugging
this into our earlier considerations yields

\xi\mathbin{\triangleright}v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)

and the continuity estimate

\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(\xi\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)\leq C_{r}\cdot\lVert\xi\rVert\cdot\mathrm{p}_{s_{r},\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)\qquad\textrm{for all }v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)

for the linear mapping

\varrho(\xi)\colon\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)\longrightarrow\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho).

This establishes the desired
\varrho
-invariance of
\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)
.

\ch@irxboxempty

Slightly adapting our argument results in the following more explicit
inequalities, which may be viewed as an abstract version of the Cauchy
estimates for holomorphic functions.

Lemma 2.9
(Cauchy estimates).

Let
\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)
be a
Lie algebra representation. Moreover, let
r_{0}>0
,

\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)
and
R\geq 0
. Then

\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(\varrho(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})v\bigr)\leq\frac{n!^{1-R}}{r^{n}}\cdot\mathrm{p}_{2r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v)

(2.25)

for all
0<2r<r_{0}
,
n\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}_{0}
,

\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and

v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)
.

Proof
:

Similar to before, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}\bigl(\varrho(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k!^{R-1}\cdot r^{k}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k}\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k!^{R-1}\cdot r^{k}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k+n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k+n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!^{1-R}}{r^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+n}{k}^{1-R}(k+n)!^{R-1}\cdot r^{k+n}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k+n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k+n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!^{1-R}}{r^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+n)!^{R-1}\cdot(2r)^{k+n}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k+n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k+n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!^{1-R}}{r^{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}k!^{R-1}\cdot(2r)^{k}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!^{1-R}}{r^{n}}\cdot\mathrm{p}_{2r,\mathrm{q}}^{(R)}(v),

where we have employed the estimate

\binom{k+n}{k}^{1-R}\leq\binom{k+n}{k}\leq 2^{k+n}\qquad\textrm{for all }k\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}_{0}.

\ch@irxboxempty

To conclude our discussion of analytic vectors, we show that
the passage to analytic vectors may be regarded as a functor

\mathscr{A}_{R,r}
:

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\widetilde{\varrho}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(\widetilde{V})

(2.26)

be Lie algebra representations with a continuous intertwiner

L\in\operatorname{L}(V,W)
along a surjective Lie algebra morphism

\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, i.e.

L\bigl(\xi\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)=\phi(\xi)\mathbin{\triangleright}Lv\qquad\textrm{for all }\xi\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V.

(2.27)

Then, for every
r_{0}>0
, there exists an
s_{0}>0
such that
L

restricts to a continuous linear mapping

L\colon\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)\longrightarrow\mathscr{A}_{R,s_{0}}(\widetilde{\varrho})\qquad\textrm{for all }R\geq 0.

(2.28)

If
\phi
is a surjective contraction, i.e.
\lVert\phi\rVert\leq 1
,
then we may choose
s_{0}=r_{0}
.

Proof
:

Let
v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)
and

\widetilde{\mathrm{q}}\in\operatorname{cs}(\widetilde{V})
. By continuity of
L
,
there exists a corresponding continuous seminorm

\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)
such that

\widetilde{\mathrm{q}}\bigl(Lx\bigr)\leq\mathrm{q}(x)\qquad\textrm{for all }x\in V.

As
\phi
is a linear surjection and thus open, we moreover find
some
r>0
such that

\widetilde{\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\subseteq r\cdot\phi\bigl(\ch@airxmathbbm{B}\bigr)=\phi\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{r}(0)\bigr)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},

where we denote the open unit ball of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
by

\widetilde{\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}
. Using the equivariance, this implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}_{s,\widetilde{\mathrm{q}}}^{(R)}\bigl(Lv\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot s^{n}\sup_{\widetilde{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\in\widetilde{\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}\widetilde{\mathrm{q}}\bigl(\widetilde{\xi}_{1}\cdots\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}Lv\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot s^{n}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\mathrm{B}_{r}(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}}\widetilde{\mathrm{q}}\bigl(\phi(\eta_{1})\cdots\phi(\eta_{n})\mathbin{\triangleright}Lv\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot(r\cdot s)^{n}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}}\widetilde{\mathrm{q}}\bigl(L(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot(r\cdot s)^{n}\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}}\mathrm{q}\bigl(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{p}_{r\cdot s,\mathrm{q}}(v)

for all
s<s_{0}\coloneqq r_{0}/r
. Hence,

v\in\mathscr{A}_{R,s_{0}}(\widetilde{\varrho})
and variation of
v

proves the continuity of the restricted
intertwiner (
2.28
).

\ch@irxboxempty

Taking Lie algebra representations as objects and continuous
intertwiners along contractive surjective Lie algebra morphisms as
morphisms results in the Lie algebraic representation category

\mathsf{Rep}
. This allows us to rephrase
Lemma
2.2
in the following manner.

Corollary 2.11
.

The assignment

\mathscr{A}_{R,r}\colon\mathsf{Rep}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Rep},\quad\bigl(\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)\bigr)\mapsto\bigl(\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho))\bigr)

(2.29)

acting as restriction on the intertwiners constitutes a covariant
functor.

Finally, note that the functor
\mathscr{A}_{R,r}
restricts properly if
we additionally impose the Hausdorff property on the representation
spaces.

2.3
The Infimum Argument for Projective Tensor Products

As the mappings we deform are bilinear, continuity considerations
automatically lead to topological tensor products. Throughout the
text, we shall use projective tensor products, as they constitute the
tensor product in the category of locally convex spaces.

Let
V
and
W
be locally convex spaces. The first crucial property
of projective tensor products of seminorms
\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)

and
\mathrm{p}\in\operatorname{cs}(W)
we shall frequently use is

\bigl(\mathrm{q}\mathbin{\otimes}\mathrm{p}\bigr)(v\mathbin{\otimes}w)=\mathrm{q}(v)\cdot\mathrm{p}(w)\qquad\textrm{for all }v\in V,w\in W.

(2.30)

Unwrapping the universal property leads to what is known as the
infimum argument, see e.g.
[
51
, Prop. 43.4]
.

Proposition 2.12
(Infimum argument).

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},W
be locally convex spaces and

\phi\colon V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}\longrightarrow W

(2.31)

be
n
-linear with corresponding linear map

\Phi\colon V_{1}\mathbin{\otimes}\cdots\mathbin{\otimes}V_{n}\longrightarrow W.

(2.32)

We endow
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}
with the Cartesian product
topology and
V_{1}\mathbin{\otimes}\cdots\mathbin{\otimes}V_{n}
with the projective
tensor product topology. Then
\phi
is continuous if and only if

\Phi
is. More precisely, if for a continuous seminorm

\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(W)
there are
\mathrm{p}_{1}\in\operatorname{cs}(V_{1})
,

\ldots
,
\mathrm{p_{n}}\in\operatorname{cs}(V_{n})
such that

\mathrm{q}\bigl(\phi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\bigr)\leq\mathrm{p}_{1}(v_{1})\cdots\mathrm{p}_{n}(v_{n})\qquad\textrm{for all }v_{1}\in V_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V_{n},

(2.33)

then

\mathrm{q}\bigl(\Phi(v)\bigr)\leq\bigl(\mathrm{p}_{1}\mathbin{\otimes}\cdots\mathbin{\otimes}\mathrm{p}_{n}\bigr)(v)\qquad\textrm{for all }v\in V_{1}\mathbin{\otimes}\cdots\mathbin{\otimes}V_{n},

(2.34)

and vice versa.

As this is the only type of topological tensor product we shall need,
we simply denote it by
\mathbin{\otimes}
and suppress the usual decorative
indices for the sake of readability. A systematic treatment of
projective tensor products can be found in
[
34
, §41]
.

3
Convergence of Universal Deformation Formulas

This section establishes our two main results on the universal
deformation of
\mathfrak{g}
-triples

\mu\colon V\mathbin{\otimes}W\longrightarrow X,

(3.1)

namely the deformations of continuous
\mathfrak{g}
-triples of entire
vectors and of inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triples of analytic vectors.

3.1
Joint Continuity and Entire Vectors

Among the analytic vectors of order
R
, we have the particularly well
behaved
entire vectors
, which were conceived and studied
systematically within
[
28
]
, though only for
order
R=0
and for representations on Banach spaces.

Definition 3.1
(Entire Vectors).

Let
\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)
be a
Lie algebra representation. The space of entire vectors for

\varrho
of order
R\geq 0
is defined as the projective limit

\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho)\coloneqq\varprojlim_{r>0}\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho)\cong\bigcap_{r>0}\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho).

(3.2)

Recall that the projective limit topology is simply the initial
topology with respect to the canonical inclusions

\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho)\overset{\iota_{r}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho)\qquad\textrm{for all }r>0.

(3.3)

In particular, this means that

\bigl\{\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}}\colon r\geq 0,\mathrm{q}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)\bigr\}

(3.4)

constitutes a defining system of seminorms for

\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho)
. This, in turn, implies the inheritance of the
Hausdorff property, first countability and completeness for

\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho)
from
V
analogous to
Proposition
2.2
. A systematic treatment of
projective limits of locally convex spaces can be found in

[
44
, Sec. II.5]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\varrho\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{L}(V)
be a
Lie algebra representation and
R\geq 0
.

i.)

If
V
is (quasi, sequentially) complete Hausdorff, then so
is
\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho)
.

ii.)

If
V
is a Fréchet space, then so is
\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho)
.

The first step towards universal deformation formulas for entire
vectors is now that passing to entire vectors preserves continuous
malleable
\mathfrak{g}
-triples.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

\mu\colon V\mathbin{\otimes}W\longrightarrow X

(3.5)

be a continuous malleable
\mathfrak{g}
-triple. Then so is

\mu\colon\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{V})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{W})\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{X})

(3.6)

for all
R\geq 0
. More precisely, we have the continuity estimate

\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}_{X}}^{(R)}\bigl(\mu(z)\bigr)\leq\bigl(\mathrm{p}_{2^{R}r,\mathrm{q}_{V}}^{(R)}\mathbin{\otimes}\mathrm{p}_{2^{R}r,\mathrm{q}_{W}}^{(R)}\bigr)(z)

(3.7)

for all
r\geq 0
,

z\in\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{V})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{W})
and
seminorms
\mathrm{q}_{V}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)
,
\mathrm{q}_{W}\in\operatorname{cs}(W)

and
\mathrm{q}_{X}\in\operatorname{cs}(X)
such that

\mathrm{q}_{X}\bigl(\mu(z)\bigr)\leq\bigl(\mathrm{q}_{V}\mathbin{\otimes}\mathrm{q}_{W}\bigr)(z)\qquad\textrm{for all }z\in V\mathbin{\otimes}W.

(3.8)

Proof
:

Let
Z\in\{V,W,X\}
. By
Proposition
2.2
, the mapping
\varrho_{Z}

restricts to a Lie algebra representation
on
\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho_{Z})
for all
r_{0}>0
. Given

\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, and in view of (
3.2
), we
thus get a linear mapping

\varrho(\xi)\Big|_{\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})}^{\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})}\colon\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z}).

By the characteristic property of the initial topology, its
continuity is equivalent to the continuity of

\iota_{r}\circ\varrho(\xi)\Big|_{\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})}^{\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})}=\varrho(\xi)\circ\iota_{r}\colon\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})\longrightarrow\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho_{Z})\qquad\textrm{for all }r>0,

which we have already established. Hence,
\varrho_{Z}
restricts to
a Lie algebra representation on
\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{Z})
. It remains
to check that (
3.5
) maps

\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{V})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{W})
into

\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{X})
in a continuous fashion. To show this, let

r>0
,
v\in\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{V})
,

w\in\mathscr{E}_{R}(\varrho_{W})
and continuous seminorms

\mathrm{q}_{V}\in\operatorname{cs}(V)
,
\mathrm{q}_{W}\in\operatorname{cs}(W)
and

\mathrm{q}_{X}\in\operatorname{cs}(X)
such that the continuity
estimate (
3.8
) for
\mu
holds.
Combining the latter with (
2.10
),
we first note

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{X}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}\mu(v\mathbin{\otimes}w)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(n,n-k)}\mathrm{q}_{X}\Bigl(\mu\bigl((\xi_{\sigma(1)}\cdots\xi_{\sigma(k)}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\mathbin{\otimes}(\xi_{\sigma(k+1)}\cdots\xi_{\sigma(n)}\mathbin{\triangleright}w)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(n,n-k)}\mathrm{q}_{V}\bigl(\xi_{\sigma(1)}\cdots\xi_{\sigma(k)}\mathbin{\triangleright}v\bigr)\cdot\mathrm{q}_{W}\bigl(\xi_{\sigma(k+1)}\cdots\xi_{\sigma(n)}\mathbin{\triangleright}w\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{V}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{k}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\cdot\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n-k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{W}(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{n-k}\mathbin{\triangleright}w).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}_{r,\mathrm{q}_{X}}^{(R)}\bigl(\mu(v\mathbin{\otimes}w)\bigr)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{X}\bigl(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n}\mathbin{\triangleright}\mu(v\mathbin{\otimes}w)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{V}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{k}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\cdot\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n-k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{W}(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{n-k}\mathbin{\triangleright}w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{R}\Bigl(k!^{R-1}\cdot r^{k}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{V}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{k}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\Bigl((n-k)!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n-k}\cdot\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n-k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{W}(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{n-k}\mathbin{\triangleright}w)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{nR}\cdot\Bigl(k!^{R-1}\cdot r^{k}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{V}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{k}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\Bigl((n-k)!^{R-1}\cdot r^{n-k}\cdot\sup_{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n-k}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{W}(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{n-k}\mathbin{\triangleright}w)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\biggl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!^{R-1}\cdot(2^{R}\cdot r)^{n}\cdot\sup_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{B}}\mathrm{q}_{V}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{k}\mathbin{\triangleright}v)\biggr)

3.2
Analytic Vectors of Inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-Triples

Throughout Section
3.1
, we have often
employed that infinite radius of convergence is invariant under
rescaling. The idea, which allows us to treat more general vectors
such as the ones discussed within Lemma
3.1
, is now
that the same is true about
positive
radius of
convergence. That is to say, instead of the intersection as for the
entire vectors, we are lead to consider the union

\mathscr{A}_{R}(\varrho)=\bigcup_{r>0}\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho),

(3.22)

whose natural topology is the one of a locally convex inductive limit
based on the canonical inclusions

\mathscr{A}_{R,r_{0}}(\varrho)\hookrightarrow\bigcup_{r>0}\mathscr{A}_{R,r}(\varrho)

(3.23)

for all
r_{0}>0
. This is a particularly simple case of an inductive
system, whose definition we briefly recall for the convenience of the
reader.

Let
J
be a directed set, which we view as a category with objects
given by the elements of
J
and

\operatorname{\mathrm{Hom}}(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{*\}\;&\textrm{if}\;\alpha\mathrel{\preccurlyeq}\beta,\\
\emptyset\;&\textrm{if}\;\alpha\not\mathrel{\preccurlyeq}\beta.\end{cases}

(3.24)

From this point of view, an inductive system indexed by
J
is a
covariant functor

J\longrightarrow\mathsf{lcs}

(3.25)

with values in the category of locally convex spaces. Unwrapping this
data, we get a pair

\bigl((V_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J},(\phi_{\alpha\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in J}\bigr)

(3.26)

of locally convex spaces
V_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in J
with
continuous linear connecting maps
V_{\alpha}\rightarrow V_{\beta}

whenever
\alpha\mathrel{\preccurlyeq}\beta
. We only need the particularly
simple setup, in which each connecting map is a set inclusion as in
(
3.23
). In the sequel, we call such an
inductive system
fully reduced
. The corresponding inductive
limit, i.e. the colimit within the category
\mathsf{lcs}
, may
then be realized as

V\coloneqq\varinjlim V_{\alpha}\cong\bigcup_{\alpha\in J}V_{\alpha}

(3.27)

endowed with the final locally convex topology arising from the
canonical inclusions

V_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow V\qquad\textrm{for all }\alpha\in J.

(3.28)

In the sequel, we may thus use the characteristic property of the
final topology to check continuity of linear mappings and seminorms
defined on inductive limits. Note that we are not assuming the
strictness of the limit: the inclusions
(
3.28
) need not be topological
embeddings. That being said, by their continuity, we always know that
the subspace topology of
V_{\alpha}
is coarser than its original
topology. As a result, many of the abstract results on strict
inductive limits will be unavailable to us. A systematic treatment of
locally convex inductive limits is
[
33
, §19]
. With this
in mind, we may proceed by defining the notion of
inductive

\mathfrak{g}
-triples.

Definition 3.11
(Inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triples).

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. A
\mathfrak{g}
-triple

\mu\colon V\mathbin{\otimes}W\longrightarrow X

(3.29)

is called inductive if:

i.)

The spaces
V,W
and
X
arise as locally convex inductive
limits of fully reduced inductive systems

(V_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J_{V}}
,
(W_{\beta})_{\beta\in J_{W}}
and

(X_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in J_{X}}
consisting of invariant
subspaces for
\varrho_{V}
,
\varrho_{W}
and
\varrho_{X}
,
respectively.

ii.)

For every
\alpha\in J_{V}
and
\beta\in J_{W}
there exists an
index
\gamma\in J_{X}
such that

\mu\colon V_{\alpha}\mathbin{\otimes}W_{\beta}\longrightarrow X_{\gamma}

(3.30)

is well-defined and continuous.

A morphism of inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triples is a morphism

(T_{V},T_{W},T_{X})\colon(V,W,X)\longrightarrow(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{W},\widetilde{X})

(3.31)

of the underlying
\mathfrak{g}
-triples and

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-triples consisting of continuous linear
mappings such that for all
\alpha\in J_{Z}
there exists another
index
\widetilde{\alpha}\in J_{\widetilde{Z}}
with

T_{Z}(Z_{\alpha})\subseteq\widetilde{Z}_{\widetilde{\alpha}}
, where

Z\in\{V,W,X\}
.

We denote the subcategory of inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triples by

\mathsf{indTriple}
. Moreover assuming malleability yields then
the subcategory
\mathsf{indmTriple}
, which we are ultimately
interested in. Our main result constitutes a universal deformation
formula in the form of a functor

\mathsf{indmTriple}\longrightarrow\mathsf{indTriple}.

(3.32)

Before developing the abstract theory, we discuss some first examples of
inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triples.

Example 3.12
(Constant inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triples).

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and

\mu\colon V\mathbin{\otimes}W\longrightarrow X

(3.33)

be a
\mathfrak{g}
-triple. Set

J_{V}\coloneqq J_{W}\coloneqq J_{X}\coloneqq\{*\}
. Then

V_{*}\coloneqq V,\,W_{*}\coloneqq W,\,X_{*}\coloneqq X

(3.34)

realizes
V
,
W
and
X
as inductive limits of a fully reduced
systems. Trivially, each of the spaces
V_{*}
,
W_{*}
and
X_{*}
is
invariant under the corresponding representation. Unwrapping the
continuity condition from Definition
3.2
,

ii.)
, we finally see that it is
simply asking for the continuity of the
\mathfrak{g}
-triple, see
again Definition
2.1
,

ii.)
. This way, we obtain functors

\mathsf{cTriple}\longrightarrow\mathsf{indTriple}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mathsf{cmTriple}\longrightarrow\mathsf{indmTriple}.

(3.35)

A more concrete example arises from spaces of test functions.

Example 3.13
.

Denote the space of all continuous complex-valued mappings defined
on the real line
\ch@airxmathbbm{R}
by
\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
. We endow
this space with its usual topology of locally uniform convergence,
which turns it into a Fréchet algebra with respect to the pointwise
product. Consider the space of compactly supported continuous
functions

\mathscr{C}_{c}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\;\big|\;\exists_{r>0}\forall_{\lvert x\rvert\geq r}\colon f(x)=0\bigr\}

(3.36)

on the real line endowed with the locally convex inductive limit
topology arising from the subspaces

\mathscr{C}_{n}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\;\big|\;\forall_{\lvert x\rvert\geq n}\colon f(x)=0\bigr\}

(3.37)

for all
n\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}
. Here, we endow each

\mathscr{C}_{n}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
with the subspace topology inherited
from the further inclusion

\mathscr{C}_{n}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\subseteq\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
. Then

\mathscr{C}_{c}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
is a
\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
-module,
whose module multiplication

\mu\colon\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathscr{C}_{c}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{c}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})

(3.38)

fails to be continuous, see e.g.
[
27

.
However, its restrictions

\mu_{n}\colon\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathscr{C}_{n}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{n}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})

(3.39)

by continuity of the

\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
. Hence, the same is

(3.40)

, this shows that the

\bigl(\mathscr{C}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}),\mathscr{C}_{c}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}),\mathscr{C}_{c}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\bigr)

constitutes an inductive
\mathfrak{g}
-triple for the zero Lie

). If

and
f_{R}
. In particular, evaluating on the
diagonal yields Gâteaux holomorphic mappings

K^{\circ}\ni\hbar\mapsto f_{L}(\hbar,\hbar)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad K^{\circ}\ni\hbar\mapsto f_{R}(\hbar,\hbar).

(3.69)

As such, they possess unique local Taylor expansions, see e.g.

[
17
, Sec. 3.1]
. Due to the finite-dimensionality
of

, these take the form of vector-valued power
series. Recall now that, as formal power series, we know

f_{L}(\hbar,\hbar)=f_{R}(\hbar,\hbar),

(3.70)

which thus also holds for the corresponding holomorphic
functions. That is to say,

a\star_{F_{\hbar}}(b\star_{F_{\hbar}}c)=(a\star_{F_{\hbar}}b)\star_{F_{\hbar}}c

(3.71)

and varying
a,b,c
establishes the associativity of

\star_{\hbar}
.

Example 3.22
.

Generalizing Example
3.2
, we may consider
again a filtered associative algebra

(\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in J_{\mathfrak{A}}}
. Instead of
focusing on the multiplication of
\mathfrak{A}
, we consider a
filtered left-module

\mu\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\bullet}\mathbin{\otimes}(\mathscr{M}_{\beta})_{\beta\in J_{\mathscr{M}}}\longrightarrow(\mathscr{M}_{\beta})_{\beta\in J_{\mathscr{M}}}

(3.72)

over
\mathfrak{A}

i.)

Whenever

for

ii.)

Whenever
\alpha\mathrel{\preccurlyeq}\beta
for

\mathscr{M}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathscr{M}_{\beta}

iii.)

For every

and

such that

As before, this allows us to realize

-triple

The corresponding deformation
\mu_{\hbar}
then corresponds to a
deformation of the module structure by analogous arguments to the

5
Outlook

Let us conclude with a brief outlook on further questions and
possible developments:

The deformation functors (
3.19
) and
(
3.62
) do not preserve malleability in general.
Indeed, this is essentially only true in the abelian case by
[
43
]
, where
one can then apply the functor again, ultimately simply adding the two deformation
parameters. This matches nicely with the exponential form of the abelian
twist (
4.2
).

In the non-commutative case, the appropriate symmetry arises upon also deforming
the universal enveloping algebra
\operatorname{\mathrm{U}}(\mathfrak{g})
to a quantum group by
conjugating the coproduct with the Drinfeld twist. Obtaining a strict version of this
quantum group – or its dual version
by means of a strict
2
-cocycle – in terms of a locally compact quantum group has been
of great interest, resulting in recent
works such as
[
37
,
14
,
9
]
.

The affine group we studied within Section
4.2
has been
of particular interest, as it is both structurally simple enough to admit concrete
constructions
[
48
,
8
]
, but
nevertheless rather complicated by virtue of the absence of compactness. It would
be interesting to investigate whether these locally
compact
quantum groups
are compatible with our locally
convex
constructions. However, it is not
at all obvious what the appropriate transition from the C
∗
-algebraic world into
the locally convex one should be. One approach would be to construct and study
states on our deformed algebras to produce C
∗
-algebras by means of the
associated Gelfand-Naimark-Segal constructions. It is reasonable to expect that the
classical states are not directly positive also for the star product, but also require
modification by means of the twist, see e.g.
[
53
]
for an overview.
Pursuing this programme will be the subject of future investigations.

Moreover, and less concretely, it would be interesting to understand the
dependence of the deformation on the twist in a more conceptual way to
have also universality with respect to the twist itself. To this end,
an intrinsic topology on the set of twists would be the first step to
study continuity properties with respect to the twist and establish a
notion of morphisms between twist in such a way that the analytic
requirements (
3.15
) and
(
3.58
) are preserved automatically.
As the latter depend on the representation, the same is to be expected
of suitable topologies. Ultimately, one would like to achieve classification
results now in the analytic framework.

Another promising direction would be to incorporate also
infinite-dimensional Lie algebras instead of only finite-dimensional
ones. Here the Lie algebra will itself be equipped with some locally
convex topology. From our constructions it should be fairly easy to
incorporate Banach Lie algebras, see again Remark
2.2
,

v.)
. However, more interesting examples
such as Lie algebras of vector fields require to go beyond Banach space techniques.

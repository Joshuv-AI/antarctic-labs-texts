A Taylor-Hood finite element method for the surface Stokes problem without penalization Thanks: Last updated: August 8, 2026 Thanks: The first author was partial supported by NSF grant DMS-2012326. The second author was partially supported by NSF grant DMS-2309425
Alan Demlow and Michael Neilan

Abstract
. Finite element approximation of the velocity-pressure formulation of the surfaces Stokes equations is challenging because it is typically not possible to enforce both tangentiality and H 1 H^{1} conformity of the velocity field. Most previous works concerning finite element methods (FEMs) for these equations thus have weakly enforced one of these two constraints by penalization or a Lagrange multiplier formulation. Recently in [ 11 ] the authors constructed a surface Stokes FEM based on the MINI element which is tangentiality conforming and H 1 H^{1} nonconforming, but possesses sufficient weak continuity properties to circumvent the need for penalization. The key to this method is construction of velocity degrees of freedom lying on element edges and vertices using an auxiliary Piola transform. In this work we extend this methodology to construct Taylor-Hood surface FEMs. The resulting method is shown to achieve optimal-order convergence when the edge degrees of freedom for the velocity spaced are placed at Gauss-Lobatto nodes. Numerical experiments confirm that this nonstandard placement of nodes is necessary to achieve optimal convergence orders.

1.
Introduction

This paper concerns the finite element approximation
of the surface Stokes problem given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\bm{\Pi}{\rm div}_{\gamma}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{u}+\nabla_{\gamma}p+\bm{u}

\displaystyle={\bm{f}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\gamma,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{u}=0\text{ and }\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\gamma,

where
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a smooth, connected, and orientable two-dimensional surface without boundary.
Here
\bm{u}:\gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p:\gamma\to\mathbb{R}
denote the velocity and pressure, respectively,

\bm{\Pi}
is the tangential projection, and
{\rm Def}_{\gamma}
is the surface deformation operator.
The letter
{\bm{\nu}}
denotes the outward unit normal of
\gamma
, so that the constraint
\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}=0
means
the velocity is tangent to the surface.

We focus on surface finite element methods (SFEMs) for (
1.1
) based on the classical velocity-pressure formulation, where finite element spaces are defined on a piecewise-polynomial approximation of the surface.
These methods mirror their Euclidean counterparts and are formulated
based on the PDE’s variational formulation. As in the Euclidean case, the well-posedness and stability of
a SFEM for (
1.1
) relies on a discrete inf-sup condition, showing that the choice
of finite element spaces cannot be chosen based on approximation properties alone.
This naturally leads to the use of classical finite element Stokes pairs. However the tangential constraint
\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}=0
imposes additional difficulties not found in the Euclidean setting. As explained in, e.g.,
[
11
]
, the simultaneous enforcement
of tangentiality and
\bm{H}^{1}
-conformity of the velocity approximation is not feasible on non-
C^{1}
surfaces, and therefore at least one of these conditions must be relaxed in the numerical method.
For example,
[
13
,
14
,
21
,
23
,
15
,
18
,
5
]
use finite element spaces
based on
\bm{H}^{1}
-conforming Stokes pairs and enforce the tangentiality constraint weakly through penalization or Lagrange multipliers.
This approach introduces additional degrees of freedom, as one approximates the redundant normal component of the velocity,
and they require a high-order approximation of the outward unit normal of
\gamma
to achieve optimal-order convergence,
at least for low-order methods (cf.
[
16
]
). Alternatively, one may construct exactly tangential velocity approximations
by utilizing
\bm{H}({\rm div})
-conforming finite element spaces
[
4
,
19
]
. This strategy
requires additional consistency and stability terms in the method, either by interior penalty techniques or through stabilization,
which can potentially increase the complexity of the scheme. The use of larger
\bm{H}({\rm div})
-conforming finite element spaces also
results in a larger system of unknowns compared to a
\bm{H}^{1}
-conforming discretization.

Recently the authors proposed a novel finite element method for the surface Stokes problem in
[
11
]
that addresses many of the shortcomings of the aforementioned schemes.
Similar to
[
4
,
19
]
, this method enforces the tangentiality constraint exactly within the finite element space but relaxes function continuity.
The discrete velocity space is
{\bm{H}}({\rm div})
-conforming,
yet in contrast to
[
4
,
19
]
, it possesses sufficient weak continuity properties to ensure sufficient consistency
of the scheme without the inclusion of edge-integral terms involving jumps and averages. These properties are obtained by a novel design of the degrees of freedom, notably by incorporating an auxiliary Piola transform to transfer function information at nodal degrees of freedom without using data from the exact surface. As a result, the method in
[
11
]
is the first convergent finite element method for (
1.1
) that exactly mirrors the problem’s variational formulation.

The method in
[
11
]
is based on the lowest-order Euclidean MINI pair
[
1
]

defined on piecewise linear surface approximations, resulting in a first-order approximation scheme
with respect to the
\bm{H}^{1}\times L_{2}
energy norm. Numerical experiments reported in
[
11
]
also
suggest that the
\bm{L}_{2}
velocity error converges with second-order, although a theoretical justification of this behavior was not provided.
While
[
11
]
only considered one choice of Stokes pair,
the methodology presented there is applicable to any finite element pair with nodal degrees of freedom.
The theoretical analysis (e.g., inf-sup stability) must however be carried out in a case-by-case basis.

The objective of this paper is to build upon
[
11
]

and generalize the methodology to higher-order schemes defined on high-order geometry approximations.
We apply the methodology in
[
11
]
to the classical Taylor-Hood pair,
taking the velocity space to consist of (mapped) piecewise polynomials
of degree
k\geq 2
, and the pressure space to be the (mapped) continuous, piecewise polynomials
of degree
(k-1)
. Similar to
[
11
]
, nodal degrees of freedom of the velocity space are defined through
a novel use of the Piola transform to ensure tangentiality,
{\bm{H}}({\rm div})
-conformity,
and weak continuity properties of the finite element space. This construction is done with respect to the surface approximation, and the underlying
algorithm does not depend on the exact surface
\gamma
.

Although the design of the proposed finite element spaces and
method builds directly on our previous work,
the convergence analysis is non-trivial and requires new theoretical tools.
For example, to establish inf-sup stability of the Taylor–Hood pair,
we employ a surface-adapted version of Verfürth’s trick. In this approach, we first derive
an inf-sup stability result with respect to a weighted
H^{1}
-type pressure norm, and then use a specially designed
interpolation operator to extend this result to the
L_{2}
-norm.

Another key difficulty lies in
designing finite element spaces with sufficient weak continuity properties.
The analysis indicates and numerics confirm that a judicious placement of degrees of freedom
is crucial to ensure the method converges with optimal order. Specifically
edge degrees of freedom (which are absent in the MINI element) must coincide
with the Gauss-Lobatto nodes so that the inconsistency of the scheme is of high
enough order to guarantee optimal-order convergence in the isoparametric case. In the case of the practially important lowest-order Taylor-Hood pair
\mathbb{P}^{2}-\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the interior edge Gauss-Lobatto node is the edge midpoint, so the nodes we use to define our degrees of freedom are precisely the Lagrange nodes and we thus obtain optimal convergence of the classical lowest-order Taylor-Hood method. For
\mathbb{P}^{3}-\mathbb{P}^{2}
and higher-order elements, the edge Gauss-Lobatto points do not coincide with standard Lagrange points, and so our degrees of freedom are not the classical ones. We note that the recent work
[
12
]
also uses Gauss-Lobatto points as degrees of freedom for the Piola-transformed Scott-Vogelius element
for the Euclidean Stokes problem on smooth domains. The present work shows that this essential idea carries over to the analogous surface problem, albeit with significant additional technical challenges in the analysis.

We emphasize that there are two distinct “variational crimes” or nonconformity errors present in our method: a “geometric error” resulting from the approximation of
\gamma
by a discrete counterpart, and a “nonconformity error” resulting from the lack of
H^{1}
conformity in our method. As is typical of surface finite element methods, our analysis indicates that the order of the geometric error increases with the degree of the polynomial discrete surface used to approximate
\gamma
. In contrast, as confirmed in our numerical experiments, the order of the nonconformity error does not increase with the polynomial degree of the discrete surface. Rather, the placement of the edge degrees of freedom is the key to obtaining optimal convergence. The situation is thus analogous to the classical nonconforming Crouzeix-Raviart element (cf.
[
9
,
6
]
), where placing the degrees of freedom precisely at edge midpoints is essential to obtaining optimal order convergence.

Finally, we prove optimal-order velocity error estimates in the
\bm{L}_{2}
-norm, again a result
missing in
[
11
]
.
This is obtained using a standard duality argument, combined with the
weak continuity properties of the finite element space
and improved geometric consistency estimates.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section,
we set the notation, outline our assumptions, and state several preliminary results.
In Section
3
, we define the finite element spaces and prove
approximation properties and inf-sup stability results.
Section
4
states several consistency results,
estimating the non-conformity of the finite element spaces.
In Section
5
we state the finite element method
and prove error estimates in the
\bm{H}^{1}\times L_{2}
energy
norm and derive an
\bm{L}_{2}
error estimate of the velocity approximation.
The paper concludes with numerical experiments
in Section
6
.

The Piola transform of a vectorfield

with respect to

is given by

(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}=\mu^{-1}\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}\bm{v},

whereas the Piola transform for a vectorfield

with respect to the inverse mapping

(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}}\bm{v})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}=(\mu\circ{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1})\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}\bm{v}.

The mappings
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}:\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}};\mathcal{S}_{0})\to\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}};\mathcal{S}_{1})

and
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}}:\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}};\mathcal{S}_{1})\to\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}};\mathcal{S}_{0})

{\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}\bm{v}=\mu{\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v}
for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div};\mathcal{S}_{0}).

Remark 2.4
.

In what follows, we make a slight abuse of notation
and treat the symbol of the Piola transform
\mathcal{P}_{(\cdot)}
as both an operator
and a matrix. For example, we write
(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}(x)=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}(x)\bm{v}(x)
,
where on the left-hand side
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v}:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, whereas on
the right-hand side, both
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}=\mu^{-1}\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}
and
\bm{v}
are functions defined on
\mathcal{S}_{0}
.
This leads to (admittedly unusual) statements such as

3.
Finite Element Spaces

Recall in the Euclidean case, the Taylor–Hood pair takes the
velocity space to be the vector-valued Lagrange space of degree
r\geq 2
,
whereas the pressure space is the (scalar) Lagrange space of degree
(r-1)
.
Analogously, on the affine surface
\bar{\Gamma}_{h}
we let
\bar{Q}_{h}
be the space of continuous, piecewise polynomials of degree
(r-1)
.
On the high-order surface approximation, we define the pressure space via standard composition:

(3.1)

Q_{h}=\{q\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{h,k})\cap\mathring{L}_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k}):\ q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}|_{\bar{K}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r-1}(\bar{K})\ \forall\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{r-1}(\bar{K})
denotes the space of polynomials of degree
\leq(r-1)

with domain
\bar{K}
, and
\mathring{L}_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})
is the space of square-integrable
functions on
\Gamma_{h,k}
with vanishing mean.
Note that
Q_{h}
is equivalent
to the space of functions
q
in
H^{1}(\Gamma_{h,k})\cap\mathring{L}_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})

such that
q\circ\bm{a}_{K}|_{\hat{K}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r-1}(\hat{K})
for each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
.

Let
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
denote the set of degrees of freedom (DOFs) of the
r
th-degree Lagrange finite element
space on
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, i.e.,
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
consists of (i) the vertices in
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
;
(ii)
(r-1)
points on each (open) edge
\bar{e}\in\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
; (iii)
\frac{1}{2}(r-1)(r-2)
points
in the interior of each
\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, chosen such that they uniquely determine
a polynomial of degree
(r-3)
. The corresponding set of points are mapped to
\Gamma_{h,k}
via
{\bm{\Psi}}
:

\mathcal{N}_{h,k}=\{{\bm{\Psi}}(\bar{a}):\ \bar{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\}.

We also set for each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
,

\mathcal{N}_{K}=\{a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}:\ a\in{\rm cl}(K)\}

to be the location of these degrees of freedom on
K
.
We make the mild assumption that these degrees
of freedom are the image of a set of reference DOFs under
\bm{a}_{K}
,
i.e., there exists a set
\mathcal{N}_{\hat{K}}\subset{\rm cl}(\hat{K})

such that
\mathcal{N}_{K}=\{\bm{a}_{K}(\hat{a}):\ \hat{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{\hat{K}}\}
.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
, we define the local velocity space,
which is the space of Piola-mapped polynomials of degree
r
:

\bm{V}(K)=\{\mathcal{P}_{\bm{a}_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}:\ \hat{\bm{v}}\in[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{K})]^{2}\}.

To piece together these local spaces and to define
the global velocity space, we require a definition along with a technical result.

Definition 3.1
.

For each DOF
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}
in the triangulation,
we arbitrarily choose a single (fixed) face
K_{a}\in\mathcal{T}_{a}
.
For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{a}
, we define

\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\bm{x}=\Big({\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\Big[{\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)}{{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)}\Big]\Big)\bm{x},

where we recall
{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}
and
{\bm{\nu}}_{K}
are the outward unit normals of
K_{a}
and
K
, respectively.
In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\bm{x}
is the Piola transform of
\bm{x}
with respect
to the inverse of the closest point projection onto the plane tangent to
K_{a}
at
a

[
11
]
.

Remark 3.2
.

While our algorithm functions by choosing a master element
K_{a}
at each node
a
,
it is more appropriately interpreted as assigning an approximate tangent plane at each node.
The algorithm does not have access to the continuous normal
{\bm{\nu}}
or equivalently the tangent plane to
\gamma
. In essence, our algorithm functions by choosing a reasonable but somewhat arbitrary approximation to
{\bm{\nu}}
at each node and then computing degrees of freedom based on this choice.
In the absence of the actual tangent plane of
\gamma
,
there does not appear to be a canonical or “best” choice of approximate tangent plane for this purpose.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}
and let
\bm{u}
lie in the tangent plane of
\gamma
at

\bm{p}(a)

be the Piola transform of

to

Then

\displaystyle|\breve{\bm{u}}_{K}-\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\breve{\bm{u}}_{K_{a}}|\lesssim h^{2k}|\breve{\bm{u}}_{K_{a}}|\leq h^{2k}|\bm{u}|.

Proof.

The proof for the case

is found in
[

given there generalize to arbitrary

[

.
∎

Motivated by Lemma

we define the global velocity space as

\bm{V}_{h}=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{L}_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k}):\ \bm{v}_{K}\in\bm{V}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k};\bm{v}_{K}(a)=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}(\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a))\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}\}.

Lemma 3.4

Let
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
, and
for an edge

set

This result follows from the (in-plane) normal-preserving properties

Lemma 3.5
.

There holds
\bm{V}_{h}\subset\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}};\Gamma_{h,k})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that co-normal components functions in
\bm{V}_{h}

are single-valued on the edges in
\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
.

Let
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
, set
\bar{\bm{v}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}}\bm{v}
,
and let
\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
be related
via
K={\bm{\Psi}}(\bar{K})
.
Writing
\bm{v}_{K}:=\bm{v}|_{K}=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{a}_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}
for some
\hat{\bm{v}}\in[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{K})]^{2}
,
we have
\bar{\bm{v}}_{\bar{K}}:=\bar{\bm{v}}|_{\bar{K}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}}\mathcal{P}_{\bm{a}_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}=\mathcal{P}_{F_{\bar{K}}\circ\bm{a}_{K}^{-1}}\mathcal{P}_{\bm{a}_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}=\mathcal{P}_{F_{\bar{K}}}\hat{\bm{v}}
.
Thus
\bar{\bm{v}}_{K}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq r
.

Let
e
be an edge of
\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
and
\bar{e}
an edge of
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}

with
e={\bm{\Psi}}(\bar{e})
. Write
e={\partial}K_{1}\cap{\partial}K_{2}
with
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
,
and let
\bar{K}_{1},\bar{K}_{2}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be the corresponding
affine elements. The normal-preserving properties
of the Piola transform read

\displaystyle\int_{e}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})q=\int_{\bar{e}}(\bar{\bm{v}}_{j}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{j})\bar{q}\qquad\forall q\in L_{2}(e),

where
\bm{v}_{j}=\bm{v}|_{K_{j}},\ \bar{\bm{v}}_{j}=\bar{\bm{v}}|_{\bar{K}_{j}}
, and
\bar{q}=q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}
.
An application of Lemma

yields

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{e}}(\bar{\bm{v}}_{j}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{j})\bar{q}=\int_{e}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})q=\int_{\bar{e}}\mu_{e}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}\bar{q}\qquad\forall q\in L_{2}(e).

Thus,
\bar{\bm{v}}_{j}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{j}=\mu_{e}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}
and so

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{1}\cdot\bm{n}_{1}+\bm{v}_{2}\cdot\bm{n}_{2}=\Big(\mu_{e}\big(\bar{\bm{v}}_{1}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{1}+\bar{\bm{v}}_{2}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{2})\Big)\circ{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}\qquad\text{on }e.

Lemma
3.4
shows that the left-hand side vanishes at
(r+1)
distinct points on
{\rm cl}(e)
,
and therefore
\bar{\bm{v}}_{1}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{1}+\bar{\bm{v}}_{2}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{2}
vanishes at
(r+1)
points on
\bar{e}
.
Since
\bar{\bm{v}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{j}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq r
on
\bar{e}
,
we conclude
\bar{\bm{v}}_{1}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{1}+\bar{\bm{v}}_{2}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{2}=0
on
\bar{e}
.
Therefore
\bm{v}_{1}\cdot\bm{n}_{1}+\bm{v}_{2}\cdot\bm{n}_{2}=0
on
e
, and
so
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}};\Gamma_{h,k})
.
∎

3.1.

In this section, we gather some scaling and approximation results

properties as the (isoparametric) Lagrange finite element space.
For example, we have the following inverse estimate.

Lemma 3.6
.

There holds for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}(K)
and

with

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{W^{m}_{\infty}(K)}\lesssim h^{-1+s-\min\{r,m\}}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{s}(K)}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}.

Proof.

Write
\bm{v}=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{a}_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}
for some
\hat{\bm{v}}\in[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{K})]^{2}
.
By similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma
2.6
(cf. (

)), we have for any

|\bm{v}|_{W^{m}_{\infty}(K)}\lesssim h^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}h^{-\ell}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{\ell}_{\infty}(\hat{K})}=h^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{r,m\}}h^{-\ell}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{W^{\ell}_{\infty}(\hat{K})}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}h^{-\ell-1}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{K})}+h^{-\min\{r,m\}-1}|\hat{\bm{v}}|_{H^{s}(\hat{K})},

where we used the fact that

is a polynomial of degree at most

and equivalence of norms in the last two steps.
We then map back to
K

2.6
,

|\bm{v}|_{W^{m}_{\infty}(K)}\lesssim h^{-1}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{s-1}(\hat{K})}+h^{-1+s-\min\{r,m\}}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{s}(K)}\lesssim h^{-1+s-\min\{r,m\}}\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{s}(K)}.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

There holds

(3.6)

Write

with

Applying Lemma

\displaystyle|\bm{v}|^{2}_{H^{m}(K)}\lesssim h^{-2m}\|\hat{\bm{v}}\|^{2}_{H^{m}(\hat{K})}\lesssim h^{-2m}\sum_{\hat{a}\in\mathcal{N}_{\hat{K}}}|\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{a})|^{2}.

Lemma 3.8

For each

Proof.

This result essentially follows from the arguments in

[

Given

we uniquely define

where

Define

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}-\wideparen{\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}}\|_{L_{2}(K^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{L_{2}(K^{\gamma})}+\|\bm{w}_{h}-\wideparen{\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}}\|_{L_{2}(K^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bar{\bm{w}}-\bar{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{K})}+\|\breve{\bm{w}}_{h}-\bm{I}_{h}\breve{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{L_{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|\bar{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{1}(\omega_{\bar{K}})}+h^{2}\|\bar{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{H^{2}_{h}(\omega_{K^{\gamma}})}\lesssim h\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}(\omega^{\prime}_{K^{\gamma}})}.

The desired result (
3.8
) now follows after summing over
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
.
∎

3.2.
Inf-Sup stability

In this section we establish that
\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
is an inf-sup stable
pair for the surface Stokes problem. The proof relies
on a variant of Verfürth’s trick, analogous to the standard
stability argument for the Euclidean Taylor–Hood pair.
We begin by showing
a stability result with respect to a weighted
H^{1}
-norm.

Lemma 3.10
.

There holds

(3.9)

\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\geq c_{1}\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}\qquad\forall q\in Q_{h},

where
c_{1}>0
is independent of
h
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{E}}\subset\mathcal{N}_{h,k}
denote the set of DOFs that lie
in the interior of edges, and let
\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathcal{N}_{h,k}
denote
the set of DOFs that lie in the interior of a (curved) simplex. The corresponding sets
defined on
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
are denoted by
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{T}}}
.

For given
q\in Q_{h}
, let
\bar{q}=q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}\in\bar{Q}_{h}
and define
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
as follows.

(i)
Vertex DOFs:
We set
\bm{v}(a)=0
at all vertices
a
in
\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
.

(ii)
Edge DOFs:
If
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{E}}
lies in the interior of an edge
e
,
then we set
\bar{e}={\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}(e)
, let
\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}
be a unit tangent vector to
\bar{e}
,
and set
\bar{a}={\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}(a)
to be the corresponding point on
\bar{e}
.
Let
\phi_{\bar{e}}
be the continuous, piecewise quadratic polynomial (bubble) that takes the value

1
at the edge midpoint of
\bar{e}
and vanishes at the vertices and other edges of
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
We set

\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)=-h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})({\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}\bar{\bm{t}}_{e})(a)\qquad a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{E}},

where
\bar{K}_{a}={\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}(K_{a})
, and we note
\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a})
is single-valued
because
\bar{q}
is continuous.
We see that
(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}\bar{\bm{t}}_{e})(a)
is tangent to
\Gamma_{h,k}

by properties of the Piola transform. Moreover, for
any co-normal vector
\bm{n}_{e}
, we have

0=\big(\mu_{e}(\bar{a})\big)^{-1}(\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e})(\bar{a})=(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}\bm{t}_{\bar{e}})(a)\cdot\bm{n}_{e}(a)
.
Thus,
(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}{\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}})(a)
is parallel to the edge unit tangent
\bm{t}_{e}(a)
.

For both
K\ni a
, we then have using (
3.2
) and
{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\cdot\bm{t}_{e}(a)=0
that

\bm{v}_{K}(a)=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)=-h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})({\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a))({\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}{\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}})(a),\quad K\in\mathcal{T}_{a},\ \ a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{E}}.

(iii)
Interior DOFs:
If
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{T}}
is a DOF that lies in the interior of
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
(so that
K_{a}=K
), then we set

\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)=-\sum_{e\subset{\partial}K}h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})(\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))(\mathcal{P}_{{{\bm{\Psi}}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}\bm{t}_{\bar{e}})(a)\qquad a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{T}}.

Letting
\bar{\bm{v}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}}\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}};\bar{\Gamma}_{h})
, we have

\bar{\bm{v}}_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})=(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K}^{-1}}\bm{v}_{K})(\bar{a})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})({\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a))({\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K}^{-1}}(a)(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}{\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}})(a)&a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{E}},\\
-\sum_{e\subset{\partial}K}h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})(\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}&a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{T}},\end{array}\right.

and
\bar{\bm{v}}
vanishes at the vertices of
\bar{\Gamma}_{h}
.

Recall that, for
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}^{\mathcal{E}}
,
(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}{\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}})(a)=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}(\bar{a}){\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}
is parallel to
\bm{t}_{e}(a)
,
and so
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K}^{-1}}(a)\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K_{a}}}(\bar{a}){\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}
is parallel to
\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}
.
Thus we can write

(3.10)

({\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a))\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K}^{-1}}(a)\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K_{a}}}(\bar{a}){\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}=C_{K,e,a}{\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}

for some
C_{K,e,a}\in\mathbb{R}
.
An application of Lemma
2.5
yields

\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K}^{-1}}(a)\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}(\bar{a})\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}-\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\right|&\leq\left|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{K}^{-1}}(a)\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}(\bar{a})\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}-\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}(\bar{a})\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\right|\\
&\qquad+\left|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\bar{K}_{a}}}(\bar{a})\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}-\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\right|\\
&\lesssim h|\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}|=h.\end{split}

This estimate along with
{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)=1+O(h^{2k})
and (
3.10
)
shows
C_{K,e,a}=1+\mathcal{O}(h)
and

\bar{\bm{v}}_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-h_{e}^{2}C_{K,e,a}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})({\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a})){\bm{t}}_{\bar{e}}(a)&a\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{E}}},\\
-\sum_{e\subset{\partial}K}h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})(\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))\bm{t}_{\bar{e}}&a\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{T}}}.\end{array}\right.

We see from a simple scaling argument that
\|\bar{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim\|h\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}
.

Now define
\tilde{\bm{v}}:\bar{\Gamma}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
as

\tilde{\bm{v}}(\bar{x})=-\sum_{\bar{e}\in\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{x})(\bar{\bm{t}}_{e}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{x}))\bar{\bm{t}}_{e}

and note that both
\tilde{\bm{v}}
and
\bar{\bm{v}}
are polynomials of degree
r
on each
\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
with

(\tilde{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}})_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-h_{e}^{2}(1-C_{K,e,a})\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{a})(\bar{\bm{t}}_{e}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a}))\bar{\bm{t}}_{e}(a)&a\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{E}}},\\
0&a\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{T}}}.\end{array}\right.

A scaling argument and the inequality
|1-C_{K,e,a}|\lesssim h
then yields

\displaystyle\|h^{-1}(\tilde{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}})\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}}|(\tilde{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}})(\bar{a})|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}}h^{6}|\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}(\bar{a})|^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|h\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}.

Using properties of the Piola transform and integrating by parts, rearranging the sum, and using norm equivalence, we then obtain
for
h
sufficiently small

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{\bm{v}})\bar{q}=-\int_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\tilde{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}-\int_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}(\bar{\bm{v}}-\tilde{\bm{v}})\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{\bar{e}\in\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{h}:\bar{e}\subset\partial\bar{K}}\int_{\bar{K}}h_{e}^{2}\phi_{\bar{e}}(\bar{\bm{t}}_{e}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q})^{2}-\|h^{-1}(\bar{\bm{v}}-\tilde{\bm{v}})\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\|h\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h^{2}_{e}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}:e\subset{\partial}K}\int_{K}|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bar{q}\cdot\bar{\bm{t}}_{e}|^{2}-h\|h\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}\gtrsim\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\bar{q}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}^{2}.

The proof is complete upon using this estimate
and

\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}\lesssim\|\bar{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim\|h\nabla_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{q}\|_{L_{2}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}.

∎

Theorem 3.11
.

There holds

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\geq\beta\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}\qquad\forall q\in Q_{h}.

Proof.

For a fixed
q\in Q_{h}
,
let
\bm{w}\in\bm{H}_{T}^{1}(\gamma)
satisfy
[
17
]

{\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{w}=(\mu_{h}^{-1}q)^{\ell},\qquad\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\lesssim\|(\mu_{h}^{-1}q)^{\ell}\|_{L_{2}(\gamma)}\lesssim\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}.

Let
\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}\in\bm{V}_{h}
be the approximation of
\bm{w}
satisfying (
3.8
).
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}(\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}))q}{\|\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h})}}=\frac{\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\wideparen{\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}})q^{\ell}}{\|\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h})}}\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\wideparen{\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}})q^{\ell}}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}}.

We then add and subtract
\bm{w}
, integrate by parts, and use (
3.8
):

(3.12)

\begin{split}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}(\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}))q}{\|\bm{I}_{h}\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}&\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{w})q^{\ell}}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}}-\frac{\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}(\bm{w}-\wideparen{\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}}))q^{\ell}}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}}\\
&\geq c_{2}\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}+\frac{\int_{\gamma}(\bm{w}-\wideparen{\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}})\cdot\nabla_{\gamma}q^{\ell}}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}}\\
&{\geq c_{2}}\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}-{c_{3}}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}}h_{K}^{2}\|\nabla_{\gamma}q^{\ell}\|_{L_{2}(K^{\gamma})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

for some
c_{2},c_{3}>0
independent of
h
.
Note that
\|\nabla_{\gamma}q^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(K^{\gamma})}\approx\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}q\|_{L_{2}(K)}
on each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}}h_{K}^{2}\|\nabla_{\gamma}q^{\ell}\|_{L_{2}(K^{\gamma})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}.

Thus we conclude from (
3.12
),

(3.13)

\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\geq\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}(\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}))q}{\|\bm{I}_{h,1}\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\geq c_{2}\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}-c_{4}\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}

with
c_{4}>0
independent of
h
.
Finally, we combine (
3.9
)
and (
3.13
) to obtain

\displaystyle c_{2}\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}+c_{4}\|h\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}q\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\right)+\frac{c_{4}}{c_{1}}\left(\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+\frac{c_{4}}{c_{1}})\left(\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}}\right).

Thus, we obtain the inf-sup condition (
3.11
)
with
\beta=c_{2}(1+\frac{c_{4}}{c_{1}})^{-1}
.
∎

4.
Consistency Estimates

In this section, we derive several
estimates that deal with the lack of
\bm{H}^{1}
-conformity
of the finite element space
\bm{V}_{h}
.
These results show that, while functions in this space
are not globally continuous on
\Gamma_{h,k}

they possess sufficient “weak continuity” properties
to ensure the finite element method presented in the subsequent section
converges with optimal order.
To derive such estimates, we make the following assumption
on the location of the edge degrees of freedom.

Assumption 4.1
.

We assume that the location of the edge DOFs of
\bm{V}_{h}

correspond to the
{\bm{\Psi}}
-mapped
(r+1)
-point Gauss-Lobatto rule.
We further assume the surface
\gamma
(and hence
\bm{p}
) is smooth with

(4.1)

\|\bm{p}\|_{W^{2r+1}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{h,k})}\lesssim 1.

We define the jump of a given elementwise-smooth (with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h,k}^{\gamma}
) vector-valued function
\bm{v}
across
e^{\gamma}

as

\displaystyle\left[\hskip-1.8063pt\left[\bm{v}\right]\hskip-1.8063pt\right]|_{e^{\gamma}}=\bm{v}_{+}\otimes\bm{n}^{\gamma}_{+}+\bm{v}_{-}\otimes\bm{n}^{\gamma}_{-},

where
e^{\gamma}={\partial}K^{\gamma}_{+}\cap{\partial}K^{\gamma}_{-}\ (K^{\gamma}_{\pm}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}^{\gamma})
,
\bm{v}_{\pm}=\bm{v}|_{K^{\gamma}_{\pm}}
, and
{\bm{\nu}}^{\gamma}_{\pm}

is the outward unit co-normal with respect to
{\partial}K_{\pm}^{\gamma}
.
Likewise, given an elementwise-smooth quantity
v
, let
\{v\}|_{e}=\frac{1}{2}(v_{+}+v_{-})
denote the average value of
v
on the mesh skeleton.

Recall
\bm{V}(K)
is the space of functions in
\bm{V}_{h}
restricted to
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
.
For a given
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
, we define

\bm{v}_{I}
such that
\bm{v}_{I}|_{K}\in\bm{V}(K)
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
and

(4.2)

\bm{v}_{I}|_{K}(a)=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}|_{K}^{-1}}\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}}\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}.

In further detail, we have

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{I}|_{K}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{({\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K})(a)}{({\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}})(a)}\left[{\bf I}-{\frac{{\bm{\nu}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)}{{\bm{\nu}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)}}\right]\bm{\Pi}(a)\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}_{h,k}.

By co-normal-preserving properties
of the Piola transform, there holds
\bm{v}_{I}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}};\Gamma_{h,k})
.
Furthermore, using Lemma
3.3
, we have
|(\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{I})_{K}(a)|\lesssim h^{2k}|\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)|
. Because
\bm{v},\bm{v}_{I}|_{K}\in\bm{V}(K)
,
a scaling estimate yields the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a given
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
, let
\bm{v}_{I}
be uniquely determined by (
4.2
).
Then there holds

(4.3)

\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}-\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{L_{2}(\gamma)}+h\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}-\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\lesssim h^{2k}\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}\|_{L_{2}(\gamma)}.

Proof.

By a change of variables and (
2.14
), we have (
m=0,1
)

\displaystyle\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}-\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{H^{m}(K^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{I}\|_{H^{m}(K)}=\|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}(\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}_{I})\|_{H^{m}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}\|_{W^{m}_{\infty}(\bar{K})}\|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{H^{m}(\bar{K})}\lesssim\|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{H^{m}(\bar{K})},

where
\bar{\bm{v}}=\bm{v}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}
and
\bar{\bm{v}}_{I}=\bm{v}_{I}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}
.
Both
\bar{\bm{v}}
and
\bar{\bm{v}}_{I}
are polynomials of degree
r
on
\bar{K}
, so a simple scaling argument and (
2.14
) yields

\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{H^{m}(\bar{K})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\bar{K}}}h^{2-2m}|(\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}_{I})(\bar{a})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{\bar{K}}}h^{2-2m}|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}}\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}(\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}_{I})(\bar{a})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}}\|^{2}_{L_{\infty}(K)}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}h^{2-2m}|(\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{I})(a)|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}h^{2-2m+4k}|\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)|^{2}.

A similar scaling argument shows
h^{2}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}|\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{v}\|_{L_{2}(\omega_{K})}^{2}
.
Therefore

\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}-\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{I}\|_{H^{m}(K^{\gamma})}\lesssim h^{2k-m}\|\bm{v}\|_{L_{2}(\omega_{K})}\lesssim h^{2k-m}\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}\|_{L_{2}(\omega_{K^{\gamma}})},

and the result (
4.3
) follows by summing over
K^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{T}^{\gamma}_{h,k}
.
∎

Note that
\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{I}
is not continuous on
\gamma
, but is single-valued at the
(projected) nodal points on
\gamma
. We exploit this fact
to derive estimates of the jump across edges of
the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h,k}^{\gamma}
using error estimates of Gauss-Lobatto
integration rules.

Lemma 4.3
.

For given
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
, let
\bm{v}_{I}
be defined by (
4.2
),
and let
{\bf G}
be a piecewise smooth matrix-valued function on
\gamma
.
Then there holds on all
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h,k}
,

(4.4)

\left|\int_{e^{\gamma}}\{{\bf G}\}:\left[\hskip-1.8063pt\left[\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{I}\right]\hskip-1.8063pt\right]\right|\lesssim h^{r+s}\big(\|{\bf G}\|_{W^{2r}_{\infty}(K_{+}^{\gamma})}+\|{\bf G}\|_{W^{2r}_{\infty}(K_{-}^{\gamma})}\big)\big(\|\bm{v}_{I}\|_{H^{s}(K_{+})}+\|\bm{v}_{I}\|_{H^{s}(K_{-})}\big)\quad s=0,1,2,\ldots,r,

where
e={\partial}K_{+}\cap{\partial}K_{-}
and
e^{\gamma}=\bm{p}(e)={{\partial}K^{\gamma}_{+}\cap{\partial}K^{\gamma}_{-}}
.

Proof.

Let
e
be an edge of
\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
, and let
\bar{e}
be an edge of
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}

with
e={\bm{\Psi}}(\bar{e})
. By a change of variables, we have

5.
Finite element method and convergence analysis

We define the bilinear forms

\displaystyle a(\bm{v},\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\left(({\rm Def}_{\gamma,h}\bm{v}):({\rm Def}_{\gamma,h}\bm{w})+\bm{v}\cdot\bm{w}\right),

\displaystyle b(\bm{v},q)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{v})q,

where
{\rm Def}_{\gamma,h}\bm{v}|_{K^{\gamma}}={\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{v}|_{K^{\gamma}}\ \forall K^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}^{\gamma}

is the piecewise defined deformation operator.
The variational formulation of the surface Stokes problem then reads:
find
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}^{1}_{T}(\gamma)\times\mathring{L}_{2}(\gamma)
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v})+b(\bm{v},p)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}{\bm{f}}\cdot\bm{v}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{T}(\gamma),

\displaystyle b(\bm{u},q)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in\mathring{L}_{2}(\gamma).

The relevant continuous inf-sup condition for this problem is found in
[
17
]
; well-posedness follows. Here the mass term is added to the standard Stokes equations in order to avoid possible degeneracies due to the presence of Killing fields
[
4
]
. Finally,
H^{2}
regularity results for surface Stokes equations posed are contained in
[
22
]
, while higher-order regularity is proved in
[
2
]
. Below we assume these results without direct citation.

The analogous bilinear forms defined on
\Gamma_{h,k}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{v},\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\left(({\rm Def}_{\Gamma_{h,k},h}\bm{v}):({\rm Def}_{\Gamma_{h,k},h}\bm{w})+\bm{v}\cdot\bm{w}\right),

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{v},q)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v})q,

where
{\rm Def}_{\Gamma_{h,k},h}\bm{v}|_{K}={\rm Def}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{v}|_{K}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,k}
.
The finite element method seeks
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v})+b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}{\bm{f}}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in Q_{h}.

The existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
5.2
) follows
from the inf-sup condition (
3.11
) and the discrete Korn inequality (
4.20
).
Furthermore, there holds
\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h,k})}+\|p_{h}\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}\lesssim\|{\bm{f}}_{h}\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma_{h,k})}
.

5.2.

We start the convergence analysis by introducing
the discretely divergence-free subspace of

We also set

{\bm{F}}_{h}=\big[{\bf I}-d{\bf H}]^{-1}\left[{\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}}\right]\bm{\Pi}_{h}{\bm{f}}_{h},

Remark 5.3

(Choice of

)
.

Using
[{\bf I}-d{\bf H}]^{-1}{\bm{\nu}}={\bm{\nu}}
and
{\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}\frac{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}={\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}

\displaystyle{\bm{F}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left(\big[{\bf I}-d{\bf H}\big]^{-1}\left[{\bf I}-\frac{\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right]{-}{\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}\right)\bm{\Pi}_{h}{\bm{f}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\big[{\bf I}-d{\bf H}\big]^{-1}\left[{\bf I}-\frac{\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes\bm{\Pi}_{h}{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right]{\bm{f}}_{h}-((\bm{\Pi}_{h}{\bm{f}}_{h})\cdot{\bm{\nu}}){\bm{\nu}}

\wideparen{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}=0

|\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}_{h}|\lesssim h^{k}

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}({\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\wideparen{\bm{v}}\lesssim\left(\|{\bm{f}}-\bm{\Pi}{\bm{f}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L_{2}(\gamma)}+h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L_{2}(\gamma)}\right)\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}\|_{L_{2}(\gamma)}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}.

for all

and

. We then take

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose the exact solution to the Stokes problem
satisfies

Proof.

6.
Numerical Experiments

We took
\gamma
to be the ellipsoid given by
\Psi(x,y,z):=\frac{x^{2}}{1.1^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{1.2^{2}}+\frac{z^{2}}{1.3^{2}}=1
. The test solution is

\bm{u}=\bm{\Pi}(-z^{2},x,y)^{\intercal}
. Note that
\bm{\Pi}={\bf I}-{\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}
with

is componentwise a rational function and not a polynomial. The pressure is

. The incompressibility condition

[
7
]
. The implementation of the degrees of freedom for the velocity space is described in
[
11
]
. Briefly, the DOF handler (degree of freedom data structure) must be modified to correctly translate between standard degrees of freedom defined on a Euclidean reference element and the global surface degrees of freedom. In the case of
\mathbb{P}^{2}-\mathbb{P}^{1}
elements it is possible to employ a standard implementation of the reference Euclidean velocity space, while for higher-order elements a modified implementation incorporating Gauss-Lobatto edge degrees of freedom is needed.

In our tests we employed standard Taylor-Hood quadratic (
\mathbb{P}^{2}-\mathbb{P}^{1}
) elements with degrees of freedom at the standard Lagrange nodes, which coincide with the Gauss-Lobatto nodes on element edges. We also ran experiments with cubic (
\mathbb{P}^{3}-\mathbb{P}^{2}
) and quartic (
\mathbb{P}^{4}-\mathbb{P}^{3}
) elements, both using standard Lagrange nodes and using Gauss-Lobatto nodes as the edge degrees of freedom. Interior degrees of freedom were chosen to ensure unisolvence. Except as otherwise noted, an isoparametric surface approximation
k=r
was used.

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\gamma}J_{3}:{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\wideparen{\bm{w}}\right|&\lesssim\left|\int_{\gamma}\left((\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}_{h})\otimes({\bf H}_{h}\breve{\bm{v}})\left[{\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right]\right)^{\ell}:{\rm Def}_{\gamma}{\bm{w}}\right|+h^{k+1}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\\
&=\left|\int_{\gamma}\left({\rm Def}_{\gamma}{\bm{w}}\left(\left[{\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right]{\bf H}_{h}\breve{\bm{v}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\cdot(\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}^{\ell}_{h})\right|+h^{k+1}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}.\end{split}

(E.7)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\gamma}J_{3}:{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\wideparen{\bm{w}}\right|&\lesssim\left|\int_{\gamma}\left({\rm Def}_{\gamma}{\bm{w}}\left(\left[{\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right]({\bf H}_{h}-{\bf H})\breve{\bm{v}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\cdot(\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}^{\ell}_{h})\right|\\

Next, following arguments from

,

(E.8)

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{z}\cdot(\bm{\Pi}_{h}{\bm{\nu}})&=\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{z}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h,k}}d=-\int_{\Gamma_{h,k}}d({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h,k}}\bm{z})\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{z}\|_{W^{1}_{1}(\Gamma_{h,k})},\end{split}

and, for

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\bm{z}\cdot\bm{\Pi}{\bm{\nu}}_{h}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma}\bm{z}\cdot\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\ell}{\bm{\nu}}-\int_{\gamma}\bm{z}\cdot(\bm{\Pi}-\bm{\Pi}^{\ell}_{h})({\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{\nu}}^{\ell}_{h})

Taking

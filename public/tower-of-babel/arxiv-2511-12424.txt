Extremal divisors in the Hilbert scheme of points on ℙ 2 \mathds{P}^{2} are preserved under residuality
Montserrat Vite

Abstract
. Let n = r ⁡ ( r + 1 ) 2 n=\frac{r(r+1)}{2} or n = r ⁡ ( r + 1 ) n=r(r+1) . We prove that the property of being extremal is preserved under residuality on the Hilbert scheme of n n points in the plane.



The notion of linkage (liaison) of curves comes from M. Noether’s thesis: Given a smooth curve in
\mathds{P}^{3}
, consider two hypersurfaces
F,G
of degree
m,n
with no common component and containing
C
. The complete intersection
F\cap G
contains
C
and a possible curve
D
. In this case we say that
C
and
D
are linked. The linkage of curves is an important tool in the study and classification of space curves and their Hilbert schemes (cf.
[
mdp2
]
). Many important questions have been answered using linkage of curves, and nowadays many questions still remain open on the matter (cf.
[
7
]
). With respect to linkage of curves a very natural question is the following:

What properties are preserved under linkage (liaison)?

A first answer to this question is the property of being
arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (ACM for short)
that is, an ACM curve is ACM after linkage (liaison). (cf.
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
). On the other hand, the deficiency modules are preserved under (even) liaison (cf.
[
6
]
). We are curious to find properties preserved by liaison related to the birational geometry of the varieties, such as the property of lying in an extremal divisor.

To be more specific, in
[
8
]
we proved that the closure of ACM curves
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{r}}
in the Hilbert scheme of curves of degree
d_{r}=\frac{r(r+1)}{2}
and genus
g_{r}=\frac{r(r+1)(2r-5)}{6}+1
is irreducible and has two irreducible divisors, each parametrizes curves in the linkage class of two disjoint lines. All these divisors are related by liaison. In the case
r=3
(degree six and genus three) we verify that these divisors are extremal in the effective cone of
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{r}}
.

We ask if for the case
r\geq 4
these divisors will also be extremal. Unfortunately, we could not solve that question, but it is natural to ask the same question in a smaller dimension. This situation motivated us to write these notes where we answer an analogous question in the case of the Hilbert scheme of
n
points,
\mathds{P}^{2[n]}
.

The main advantage to consider in this case is because the birational geometry of the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
\mathds{P}^{2}
is very well known. For example, in
[
2
]
resolve the birational geometry of the Hilbert scheme of
n
points for
n\leq 9
, they discuss the stable base locus decomposition of the effective cone and the corresponding birational models. On the other hand, in
[
5
]
compute the effective cone Eff
(\mathds{P}^{2[n]})
for all values of
n
.

In this context (points in
\mathds{P}^{2}
) the definition of liaison is known as residual points:

Definition 0.1
.

Let
Z
a subset of
n
points in
\mathds{P}^{2}
. We say that a subset
Z^{\prime}
of
m
points in
\mathds{P}^{2}
is
residual
to
Z
if there exist two curves
X
and
Y
on
\mathds{P}^{2}
such that

X\cap Y=Z\cup Z^{\prime}.

If
Z\in{\mathds{P}^{2[n]}}
, with
n
a triangular number (
n=\frac{r(r+1)}{2}
for some
r\in{\mathds{N}^{*}}
) and
Z^{\prime}\in{\mathds{P}^{2[m]}}
is residual to
Z
by the complete intersection of curves of degree
r+1
then
m=\frac{(r+1)(r+2)}{2}
. Furthermore, we have the following theorem:

Theorem A

(Corollary
1.4
)
.

Let
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
as before, then
Z
lies in an extremal divisor if and only if
Z^{\prime}
lies in the correspondent extremal divisor.

An analogous result is obtained in the case where
n
is a tangential number (
n=2r(r+1)
for some
r\in{\mathds{N}^{*}}
).

Definition 1.2

Given a family

\mathscr{L}\mathscr{A}:=\{Z^{\prime}\in{\mathds{P}^{2[d_{r+1}]}}|\exists\quad Z\in{\mathscr{A}}\text{ such that }Z^{\prime}=X_{r+1}\cap Y_{r+1}-Z\}\subseteq\mathds{P}^{2[d_{r+1}]}

Lemma 1.3
.

If

h^{0}(\mathscr{I}_{Z_{r}}(r-1))=h^{0}(\mathscr{I}_{Z_{r+1}}(r))

Proof.

Let
X,Y
two smooth curves of degree
r+1

, this is possible because the ideal of any element of
\mathds{P}^{2[d_{r}]}

r
.

Since the degree of

is
r+1
, the genus of

is
\frac{r(r-1)}{2}
, its canonical divisor have class

and the class of

(where

We consider to
Z_{r}
as a divisor on

, for the above it has class

, by Riemann-Roch and the last equalities we have that:

\displaystyle h^{0}(\mathscr{I}_{Z_{r}}(r-1))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}((r-1)H-D))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D-(r-1)H))+grad((r-1)H-D)+1-g(X)

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}((r-2)H+D-(r-1)H))+grad((r-1)H)-grad(D)+1-\frac{r(r-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-H))+(r-1)(r+1)-\frac{r(r+1)}{2}+1-\frac{r(r-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}((r+1)H-Z_{r+1}-H))+0

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(rH-Z_{r+1}))

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathscr{I}_{Z_{r+1}}(r))

∎

Corollary 1.4

For all

\mathscr{L}D_{r-1}=D_{r}

If

Let

and an equation of degree

, then we can consider two smooth surfaces

, of degree

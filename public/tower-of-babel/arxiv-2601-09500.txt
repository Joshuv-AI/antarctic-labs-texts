Forbidding edge-critical graphs as trace in uniform hypergraphs
Yichen Wang, Xin Cheng, Ervin Győri, Yuanpei Wang, Xiamiao Zhao, Junpeng Zhou

Abstract
We say a hypergraph ℋ \mathcal{H} contains a graph G G as trace if there exists a vertex subset S ⊆ V ⁡ ( ℋ ) S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H}) such that | S | = V ⁡ ( G ) |S|=V(G) and { e ∩ S ∣ e ∈ E ⁡ ( ℋ ) } \{e\cap S\mid e\in E(\mathcal{H})\} contains G G as a subgraph. We use ex ⁡ ( n , T ​ r r ​ ( G ) ) \mathrm{ex}(n,Tr_{r}(G)) to denote the maximum number of edges in an r r -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices not containing G G as trace. The study of Turán numbers for traces was initiated by Mubayi and Zhao (2017) who studied ex ⁡ ( n , T ​ r r ​ ( K s + 1 ) ) \mathrm{ex}(n,Tr_{r}(K_{s+1})) where K s + 1 K_{s+1} is a clique on s + 1 s+1 vertices and conjectured the exact value of ex ⁡ ( n , T ​ r r ​ ( K s + 1 ) ) \mathrm{ex}(n,Tr_{r}(K_{s+1})) . When r ≤ s r\leq s , the conjecture was covered by a result of Pikhurko (2013) who gave the exact value of Turán numbers for expanded cliques. Then Gerbner and Picollelli (2023) gave the exact value for book graphs ( K 1 , 1 , t K_{1,1,t} , the complete tripartite graph with two parts of size one and one part of size t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 ). We say G G is edge-critical if there exists an edge e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G) such that χ ⁡ ( G − e ) < χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G-e)<\chi(G) where χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) is the chromatic number of G G . The definition of edge-critical was given by Simonovits (1974), who proved that for an edge-critical graph G G with χ ⁡ ( G ) = s + 1 ≥ 3 \chi(G)=s+1\geq 3 , the Turán graph T ⁡ ( n , s ) T(n,s) is the unique extremal graph for e ​ x ​ ( n , G ) ex(n,G) as n n is sufficiently large. In this paper, we further generalize the results of Gerbner and Picollelli (2023) to edge-critical graphs. More precisely, we prove that for an edge-critical graph G G with χ ⁡ ( G ) = s + 1 \chi(G)=s+1 , when s ≥ r ≥ 3 s\geq r\geq 3 and n n is sufficiently large, the r r -uniform Turán graph T r ​ ( n , s ) T_{r}(n,s) is the unique extremal hypergraph.

Introduction

In this paper, we use capital letters to denote graphs, like
G,H,F
, and calligraphic letters to denote hypergraphs, like
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{F}
.
We use

We use

d_{G}(v)

in a graph
G

G

The same notation applies to hypergraphs.
In the paper, we do not distinguish a hypergraph and a set of hyperedges, in otherwords, a set of hyperedges can be viewed as a hypergraph.
In the graph case,
H[S]
denotes the subgraph of
H
induced on the vertex subset

.
A hypergraph is

For convenience, an

-graph

The (
2

whose edges are the pairs of vertices contained in at least one edge of

Let

When

We use

s
-partite
r
-graph on
n

.
We use

to denote the number of copies of a graph
F
in a graph

.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and

\mathcal{H}
.
We define the

on

\mathcal{H}_{|S}=\{e\cap S:e\in E(\mathcal{H})\}.

Note that we omit multiple hyperedges when defining

We say
\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}_{|S}
contains a copy of
\mathcal{F}

We use

Note that if a graph contains

We use

The studies about forbidding traces of hypergraphs were initiated by Mubayi and Zhao
[
12
]
on the trace of cliques.

Theorem 1.1
(Mubayi and Zhao
[

]
)

Fix

If

\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,Tr_{r}(K_{s+1}^{q}))=\mathrm{ex}_{s}(n,Tr_{s}(K_{s+1}^{q}))+o(n^{s})=\mathrm{ex}_{s}(n,H^{s}_{q,s+1})+o(n^{s}).

Here,

is the

-uniform hypergraph obtained from

by enlarging each of its

hyperedges with a (different) set of

Mubayi and Zhao
[

Conjecture 1.2
(Mubayi and Zhao
[

)

Fix

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,Tr_{r}(K_{s+1}^{q}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,H^{r}_{q,s+1}),\text{ if }r\leq s,\\
\mathrm{ex}_{s-1}(n-r+s,Tr_{s}(K_{s+1}^{q}))=\mathrm{ex}_{s}(n-r+s,H^{s}_{q,s+1}),\text{ if }r>s.\end{array}\right.

.
The definition of expansion will be given later.
In the same paper, Mubayi and Zhao

Gerbner and Picollelli

In this paper, we focus on graph based hypergraphs.
That is, considering

Suppose there is a vertex subset

Under this definition,

If

The order of magnitude of

Theorem 1.4

For any graph

The above studies focus on general

When fixing

For every

.

Theorem 1.6

Let

.
When

\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,Tr_{r}(G))=e(T_{r}(n,s))=N(K_{r},T(n,s)),

.

As a corollary, we have the following result.

Corollary 1.7

Let

be a graph with
\chi(G)=s+1

When

\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,Tr_{r}(G))=e(T_{r}(n,s))=N(K_{r},T(n,s)),

If

If

Note that

is

.
Also,

\square

In the whole section, we always view

We use

.
Denote the

-partite graph with two parts of size one and

Let

be an edge-critical graph with

, and

Then

Note that if we find a

\mathcal{H}
.

For a
(r-1)
-set

, we say

.
We say a hyperedge of

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a

-free

-graph on
n

Note that
e(\mathcal{H})\geq e(T_{r}(n,s))=N(K_{r},T(n,s))
by the construction of

.
It is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{H}=T_{r}(n,s)
.

We iteratively remove light hyperedges from
\mathcal{H}
until there is no light hyperedge remaining.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
be the set of deleted light hyperedges and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
denote the remaining hyperedges.
Note that a hyperedge may not be light in the begining, but it is possible to become light after some light hyperedges are removed.
Such idea has been used in other papers, for example, in
[
7
]
.

It is clear that
|\mathcal{H}_{1}|\leq stn^{r-1}
since every hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
corresponds to a light
(r-1)
-set at some time during the process and each
(r-1)
-set is counted at most

Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|\geq e(\mathcal{H})-O(n^{r-1})\geq e(T_{r}(n,s))-O(n^{r-1}).

(2)

Since
e(\mathcal{H})\geq N(K_{r},T(n,s))=\Theta(n^{r})
,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
preserves most of the hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
.

From the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, every hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is heavy.
Then we have the following claim.

Claim 3.1

Let
S
be a set with size
|S|\leq st
.
If there exists two vertices
u,v\in S
such that

is an edge in
\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, then there exists a hyperedge
e\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that

.

Proof of Claim
3.1
:
Since

, there exists a hyperedge
e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that

.
Choose
e^{\prime}
such that
|e^{\prime}\cap S|
is minimized.
If
|e^{\prime}\cap S|=2
, then we are done.
Suppose otherwise, let

be a vertex different from

and
v
.
Since
e^{\prime}
is heavy in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, the
(r-1)
-set

is heavy, that is, contained in at least
st+1
hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Recall that
|S|\leq st
, there exists a hyperedge

.

\square

As a corollary, we have that

is

Suppose otherwise, let

\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
By Claim

, for each edge

.

Note that every hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
corresponds to a

in
\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, combining Eq (
2

N(K_{r},\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\geq|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|\geq N(T(n,s),K_{r})-o(n^{r}).

2.5
, we have that
\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
o(n^{2})
-close to

.
Note that by Lemma

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{H}_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq N(K_{r},\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime})+O(n^{r-1})

\displaystyle\leq\ex(n,K_{r},G)+O(n^{r-1})

\displaystyle\leq N(K_{r},T(n,s))+o(n^{r})=e(T_{r}(n,s))+o(n^{r}).

The proof sketch is as follows.

1.

First find a small set

|X|\leq\xi n
for a small constant

is
s
-partite in

.

2.

Finally, prove that

Let
\xi
be a small constant depending on

and

.
Then
\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
(\xi^{2}n^{2})
-close to

.

Given a partition

of

, we say a set is

.
We say a hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}

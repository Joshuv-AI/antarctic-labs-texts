Leader-following Consensus over Jointly Connected Switching Networks is Achievable for Exponentially Unstable Linear Systems
Yuhan Chen, Tao Liu, and Jie Huang

Abstract
The leader-following consensus problem for general linear multi-agent systems over jointly connected switching networks has been a challenging problem and the solvability of the problem has been limited to the class of linear multi-agent systems whose system matrix is marginally stable. This condition is restrictive since it even excludes the most commonly used double-integrator system. This paper presents a breakthrough by demonstrating that leader-following exponential consensus is achievable for general linear multi-agent systems over jointly connected switching networks, even when the system matrix is exponentially unstable. The degree of instability can be explicitly characterized by two key quantities that arise from the jointly connected condition on a switching graph. By exploiting duality, we further show that the output-based distributed observer design problem for a general leader system is solvable over jointly connected switching networks, even when the system matrix is exponentially unstable. This is also in sharp contrast to the existing distributed observers, which rely on the assumption that the leader system is marginally stable.

Introduction

Consensus is arguably the most fundamental problem for multi-agent control systems.
It plays a pivotal role in addressing nearly all cooperative control problems,
such as formation control

]
,
[

]
,
distributed optimization
[

,
[

[

The core objective of consensus is to design a distributed control law for a multi-agent system
to ensure that all agents’ states synchronize to a common trajectory.
Depending on the nature of the common trajectory, the
consensus problem can be further categorized into
the leaderless consensus problem and the leader-following consensus problem.
In the leaderless consensus problem,
one does not concern the time profile of the agents’ states and
the common trajectory is collectively determined by the initial states of all agents.
In contrast, in the leader-following consensus problem,
one further requires that the common trajectory be generated
by the state of an autonomous system called the leader system.
In this sense, the leader-following consensus problem
somehow poses a more challenging problem than the leaderless consensus problem.

Early research on both the leaderless consensus problem and the leader-following consensus problem focused on multi-agent systems with simple agent dynamics.
In particular, the two consensus problems were first studied for single integrators in, e.g.,
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
, and
[
16
]
,
and were later extended to double integrators in, e.g.,
[
5
]
,
[
17
]
, and
[
18
]
.
It is noted that the system matrix of a single-integrator multi-agent system
has a simple eigenvalue at the origin
and the system matrix of a double-integrator multi-agent system has
a repeated (non-simple) eigenvalue at the origin.
Subsequently, reference
[
23
]
first
generalized the leaderless consensus result while reference
[
13
]
first
extended the leader-following consensus result,
both for general linear multi-agent systems without restrictions on the eigenvalues of the system matrix.
Nevertheless, the findings in
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

all rely on the assumption that the communication network among multiple agents remains static and connected.

In practice, the communication network among multiple agents is often time-varying
and may become disconnected due to unexpected link failures or abrupt changes in the ambient environment.
This makes the study of consensus problems over
jointly connected switching networks
—where connectivity is not required at every instant—particularly interesting and challenging.
The concept of joint connectivity was first introduced in
[
6
]

to handle the leaderless and the leader-following consensus problems for a single-integrator multi-agent system.
Since then, extensive efforts have been devoted to addressing
both the leaderless consensus problem

Specifically, reference
[

studied the leader-following consensus problem
for a group of double integrators subject to a static leader,
and reference
[
2
]
further extended the result to the case of an active leader
but with a constant velocity known to all followers.
By imposing two linear matrix inequalities
on the system matrix and the input matrix of a linear multi-agent system,
reference
[
13
]
also considered the leader-following consensus problem over jointly connected switching networks.
One of the two linear matrix inequalities in
[
13
]
was removed
by reference
[

, thus only requiring that the system matrix be marginally stable.
Moreover, both the leaderless and the leader-following asymptotic consensus problems over jointly connected switching networks were tackled in
[
21
]
.
Reference
[
15
]
considered the leaderless consensus problem for a linear multi-agent system
where the system matrix can contain exponentially unstable yet weak enough modes.
However, leaderless exponential consensus in
[
15
]

is obtained under the condition that the input matrix has a full row rank;
in other words, full-state couplings exist among the agents.
Reference
[
9
]
explored necessary and sufficient conditions for
ensuring leaderless exponential consensus for neutrally stable linear multi-agent systems.
More recently, reference
[
22
]
solved both the leaderless and the leader-following
exponential consensus problems for marginally stable linear multi-agent system via
distributed dynamic output feedback control.

Up to 2019, the marginal stability assumption had been a barrier to the advancement of the study of the two consensus problems over jointly connected switching networks.
This assumption is undesirable because, under this assumption,
one cannot even handle the double-integrator system
which is the most commonly used model for unmanned aerial vehicles,
mobile robots, and so on. This barrier was broken in 2019 when reference
[
25
]

showed that the leaderless exponential consensus problem over jointly connected switching networks is solvable
for linear multi-agent systems with exponentially unstable modes.
By resorting to infinite matrix product,
[
25
]
characterized the allowable degree of instability
by a so-called synchronizability exponent of the switching topology.
One naturally wonders if the breakthrough made in
[
25
]
for
the leaderless consensus problem can also be made for the leader-following consensus problem. Indeed, in this paper, we further study the leader-following exponential consensus problem
for general linear multi-agent systems over jointly connected switching networks.
Specifically,
we first derive a critical value
\delta
from the switching topology.
This value
\delta
, together with another key quantity
T_{\mathrm{c}}
of the jointly connected condition, enables us to establish a bound on the permissible instability of the system matrix
of the linear multi-agent system.
Then, by developing a series of technical lemmas,
we prove that leader-following exponential consensus is achievable even for linear multi-agent systems with exponentially unstable modes, provided the instability remains within the established bound.

Furthermore, we also address the output-based distributed observer design problem
as the dual problem of the leader-following consensus problem.
Leveraging this duality, we show that an exponentially convergent output-based distributed observer can be readily designed for an exponentially unstable linear leader system over jointly connected switching networks.
Similarly, the instability of the leader system should remain within this established permissible instability bound.
The solvability of these two dual problems
represents a significant advance beyond the state of the art in
[
1
]
,
where both the leader-following exponential consensus problem and the exponentially convergent output-based distributed observer design problem
over jointly connected switching networks were only solved for marginally stable linear multi-agent systems.

II
Problem Formulation

Consider a general linear leader-follower multi-agent system consisting of one leader system
and
N
follower subsystems.
The leader system is described as

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{0}(t)=Ax_{0}(t),\quad t\geq 0

Define a switching graph

Statement of Main Result

Consider the leader-follower matrix

Then, for

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{1}^{(j)}&\cdots&\xi_{n_{j}}^{(j)}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n_{j}}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\sigma(t)}

Theorem 1

Remark 4

The inequality (

the leader-following exponential consensus problem for linear multi-agent systems
over jointly connected switching networks is solvable
even if the system matrix

This is a significant advancement over the state of the art in
[

which requires that the system matrix

Remark 5

The solvability condition (

is in a similar form to the solvability condition (4) established in
[

for the leaderless exponential consensus problem for general linear multi-agent systems over jointly connected switching networks.
The major difference is that our approach studies the products of orthogonal projection matrices onto the kernel of the leader-follower matrix

\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\sigma(t)}

In contrast,

IV-B

Design of Feedback Gain Matrix
K

In this subsection, we present the design of the feedback gain matrix
K

for the distributed control law (
3
).
In particular, the design of
K
depends on a uniform lower bound for the nonzero
eigenvalues of the leader-follower matrix
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma(t)}
of the switching graph
\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\sigma(t)}
.

It is well known that the Laplacian of an undirected and connected graph
is symmetric, positive semi-definite, and has exactly one zero eigenvalue.
Moreover, an explicit lower bound for the nonzero eigenvalues of the Laplacian
can be obtained by the following lemma.

Lemma 2

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

be the Laplacian of an undirected and connected graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{c}}
.
Then the nonzero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}

are not less than

\lambda_{L}:=\frac{4}{N(N-1)}>0.

Remark 6

Lemma
2
follows from
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]

and is of critical use in
[
25
, Lemma 2]
to obtain a
uniform lower bound for the nonzero eigenvalues of
the Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma(t)}
of the switching graph
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma(t)}
.
In our case, we have to establish a uniform lower bound for the nonzero
eigenvalues of the leader-follower matrix
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma(t)}
of the switching graph
\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\sigma(t)}
.
To this end, we need to extend Lemma
2

to the following form.

Lemma 3

Consider the following matrix:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{L}+\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

where
\mathcal{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is the Laplacian
of an undirected graph
\mathcal{G}

and
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a
(0,1)
-diagonal matrix.
Then the nonzero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{H}
are not less than

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}:=\frac{4}{N\left(N^{2}-N+4\right)}>0.

(28)

Proof:

If
N=1
, then the lower bound (
28
) holds trivially,
since
\mathcal{H}=\Delta
does not have nonzero eigenvalues when
\Delta=0

and the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{H}
is equal to
\frac{4}{N\left(N^{2}-N+4\right)}=1
when
\Delta=1
.

For
N>1
, we divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1:
In this step, we assume that the graph
\mathcal{G}
is connected
as
\mathcal{G}_{c}
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}
.
If
\Delta=\mathbf{0}_{N\times N}
,
then the result follows from Lemma
2

by noting that

\frac{4}{N(N-1)}>\frac{4}{N\left(N^{2}-N+4\right)}.

Next, assume
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathrm{c}}

is a
(0,1)
-diagonal matrix with only one
1
on its diagonal.
Note that any unit vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

can be written as

\displaystyle v=c_{1}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}+c_{2}\zeta

(29)

where
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a unit vector that satisfies
\mathbf{1}_{N}^{T}\zeta=0
, and
c_{1},c_{2}
are constants such that

c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}=1
.
Since the graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{c}}
is connected,
the Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}
has exactly one zero eigenvalue.
It follows from Lemma
2
that

\displaystyle\zeta^{T}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}\zeta\geq\lambda_{L}.

(30)

To complete the proof of this step, we only need
to evaluate a lower bound for the following quadratic form:

\displaystyle f:=v^{T}\mathcal{H}v

(31)

where
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{c}}
.
For this purpose, let

\displaystyle\zeta=\mathrm{col}\left(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{N}\right),\quad\zeta_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,N

(32)

and, for simplicity, assume

\Delta_{\mathrm{c}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

(33)

From the definition of
\zeta
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{N}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle=1.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\zeta_{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{N})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(N-1)\left(\zeta_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\zeta_{N}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(N-1)(1-\zeta_{1}^{2}).

Thus, we can obtain

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq\zeta_{1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}.

(34)

From (
29
) to (
33
),
we have

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\left(c_{1}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}+c_{2}\zeta\right)^{T}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{c}}\right)\left(c_{1}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}+c_{2}\zeta\right)

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}^{2}\zeta_{1}^{2}+\frac{2c_{1}c_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}\zeta_{1}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{N}+c_{2}^{2}\lambda_{L}=:g(\zeta_{1}).

To obtain a lower bound for the above function
g(\zeta_{1})
and hence,
a lower bound for
f
, consider three different cases.

Case 1:
If
c_{2}=0
, then

f\geq g(\zeta_{1})=\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{N}=\frac{1}{N}.

(35)

If
c_{2}\neq 0
, the minimum of
g(\zeta_{1})

would be attained when
\zeta_{1}=-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{N}c_{2}}
.
However, in light of the range of
\zeta_{1}
given in (
34
),
we need to further distinguish the following two cases.

Case 2:
If
-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{N}c_{2}}\geq-\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}
,
then

f\geq g(\zeta_{1})\geq g\left(-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{N}c_{2}}\right)=c_{2}^{2}\lambda_{L}.\\

Since
-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{N}c_{2}}\geq-\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}
also implies that

c_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{N}
, we have

f\geq g(\zeta_{1})\geq\frac{\lambda_{L}}{N}.\\

(36)

Case 3:
If
-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{N}c_{2}}<-\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}
,
then

\displaystyle f\geq g(\zeta_{1})

\displaystyle\geq g\left(-\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}c_{1}^{2}-\frac{2\sqrt{N-1}}{N}c_{1}c_{2}+\left(\frac{N-1}{N}+\lambda_{L}\right)c_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&c_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{N}&-\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{N}\\
-\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{N}&\frac{N-1}{N}+\lambda_{L}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{L}+1\pm\sqrt{(\lambda_{L}+1)^{2}-\frac{4\lambda_{L}}{N}}}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{L}+1-\sqrt{(\lambda_{L}+1)^{2}-\frac{4\lambda_{L}}{N}}}{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{L}}{N(\lambda_{L}+1)}.

(37)

By combining (
35
), (
36
), and (
IV-B
),
we obtain a lower bound for
f
as follows:

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{N},\frac{\lambda_{L}}{N},\frac{\lambda_{L}}{N(\lambda_{L}+1)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{L}}{N(\lambda_{L}+1)}=\frac{4}{N(N^{2}-N+4)}

which is the expression given by (
28
).

When
\Delta_{\mathrm{c}}
is any other
(0,1)
diagonal matrix with only one
1
on its diagonal, the lower bound (
28
)
can be similarly derived.

Now let
\Delta
be any
(0,1)
diagonal matrix with multiple
1
’s on its diagonal.
Since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}+\Delta\geq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{c}}
,
the lower bound (
28
) also holds.

Step 2:
In this step, we consider a disconnected graph
\mathcal{G}
.
By relabeling the nodes of
\mathcal{G}
, we can partition its Laplacian
\mathcal{L}

into the following block diagonal form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{block\,diag}\left\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{q},0,\ldots,0\right\}

where each
0
corresponds to an isolated node,
and each
\mathcal{L}_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,q
,
corresponds to a connected subgraph of the graph
\mathcal{G}

with
N_{i}
nodes where
1\leq N_{i}\leq N
.
By Step 1, for any
(0,1)
-diagonal matrix
\Delta_{i}
of dimension
N_{i}
,
the nonzero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{i}+\Delta_{i}
are not less than

\lambda_{Hi}=\frac{4}{N_{i}\left(N^{2}_{i}-N_{i}+4\right)}
.

Since the value of
\frac{4}{N\left(N^{2}-N+4\right)}
decreases as
N
increases
and
\frac{4}{N\left(N^{2}-N+4\right)}\leq 1
,
the lower bound (
28
) also holds for a disconnected graph
\mathcal{G}
.

The overall proof is thus complete.

\Box

Let us consider the switching leader-follower matrix
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma(t)}
over
[0,\infty)
.
Since, for any
t\geq 0
,

\mathcal{H}_{\sigma(t)}=\mathcal{L}_{\sigma(t)}+\Delta_{\sigma(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

and
\Delta_{\sigma(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is some
(0,1)
-diagonal matrix, Lemma
3
leads to a uniform lower bound for the
nonzero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma(t)}
for all

, as stated below.

Lemma 4

Under Assumption
2
,
the nonzero eigenvalues of the leader-follower matrix
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

of the switching graph
\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{\sigma(t)}

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}=\frac{4}{N\left(N^{2}-N+4\right)}

,

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have resolved the long standing issue
that leader-following consensus over jointly connected switching networks
could only be achieved for marginally stable linear systems.
We have alleviated this stringent condition by allowing the system matrix to exhibit a certain degree of exponential instability.
This degree of instability is characterized by two quantities
derived from the jointly connected condition on the switching graph,
one of which is obtained by studying the product of the orthogonal projection matrices
onto the kernel of the leader-follower matrix of the switching graph.
Then, within this degree of exponential instability,
we have rigorously demonstrated the solvability of the leader-following exponential consensus problem for general linear multi-agent systems over jointly connected switching networks.
By exploiting duality,
we have further presented the design of an exponentially convergent output-based distributed observer for general linear leader systems over jointly connected switching networks, which is the dual problem to the leader-following exponential consensus problem.
Finally, we note that it would also be interesting to establish the discrete-time counterpart of the breakthrough made in this paper.

Notation on Graph

A graph
\mathcal{G}:=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
consists of a finite node set

\mathcal{V}=\left\{1,2,\ldots,N\right\}
and an edge set
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
.
An edge of
\mathcal{E}
from node
j
to node
i,\,j\neq i
, is denoted by
(j,i)
,
and node
i
is called a child of node
j
.
The edge
(i,j)
is called undirected if
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
implies
(j,i)\in\mathcal{E}
.
The graph
\mathcal{G}
is called undirected if every edge in
\mathcal{E}
is undirected.
If the graph contains a set of edges of the form

\left\{(i_{1},i_{2}),(i_{2},i_{3}),\ldots,(i_{k-1},i_{k})\right\}
,
then this set is called a path from node
i_{1}
to node
i_{k}
,
and node
i_{k}
is said to be reachable from node
i_{1}
,
A graph is called strongly connected if there exists a path between any pair of nodes.
An undirected and strongly connected graph is called connected.

The adjacency matrix of a graph
\mathcal{G}
is a nonnegative matrix

\mathcal{A}:=[a_{ij}]^{N}_{i,j=1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
where
a_{ij}=1
if
(j,i)\in\mathcal{E}
and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise.
Then, the Laplacian matrix
\mathcal{L}:=[l_{ij}]^{N}_{i,j=1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
of
\mathcal{G}

can be further defined from
\mathcal{A}
with
l_{ii}=\sum^{N}_{j=1}a_{ij}
and, for
i\neq j
,
l_{ij}=-a_{ij}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}
is symmetric and positive semi-definite if and only if the graph
\mathcal{G}
is undirected
[
3
]
.

Given the switching signal
\sigma:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{P}=\left\{1,2,\ldots,n_{0}\right\}

and
n_{0}
graphs
\mathcal{G}_{p}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_{p})
,
p=1,2,\ldots,n_{0}
,
each with the corresponding adjacency matrix denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{p}

and the Laplacian matrix by
\mathcal{L}_{p},\,p=1,2,\ldots,n_{0}
,
we call the time-varying graph
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma(t)}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_{\sigma(t)})
a switching graph,
and denote its adjacency matrix by
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma(t)}

and its Laplacian matrix by
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma(t)}
.
Finally, the graph
\mathcal{G}:=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
with
\mathcal{E}=\bigcup^{q}_{p=1}\mathcal{E}_{p}

\mathcal{G}=\bigcup^{q}_{p=1}\mathcal{G}_{p}
.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\cdot\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{s-1}\left(\left\|\Phi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|+\left\|\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=j_{k}}^{j_{k+\ell}-1}\Bigg(\frac{\left\|\Phi_{s}(\tau_{s})\right\|}{\left\|\Phi_{s}(\tau_{s})\right\|+\left\|\Psi_{s}(\tau_{s})\right\|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\cdot\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+\ell}-1}\left(\left\|\Phi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|+\left\|\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(j_{k+\ell}-j_{k})\left\|\Phi_{s^{\prime}}(\tau_{s^{\prime}})\right\|}{\left\|\Psi_{s^{\prime}}(\tau_{s^{\prime}})\right\|+\left\|\Phi_{s^{\prime}}(\tau_{s^{\prime}})\right\|}\cdot\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+\ell}-1}\left(\left\|\Phi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|+\left\|\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|\right)

(59)

where
s^{\prime}=\mathrm{argmax}_{s\in\left\{j_{k},j_{k+1},\ldots,j_{k+\ell}-1\right\}}\frac{\left\|\Phi_{s}(\tau_{s})\right\|}{\left\|\Phi_{s}(\tau_{s})\right\|+\left\|\Psi_{s}(\tau_{s})\right\|}
.

From (
13
), (
26a
), and (
42
), we have

\left\|\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|\leq C_{1}\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}\tau_{r}},\quad\forall\,r=j_{k},j_{k}+1,\cdots,j_{k+\ell}-1.

Further, by Lemma
5
,
under Assumptions
1
and
2
,
given any
\alpha>0
and
t^{*}>0
,
designing
K
as in (
39
) gives

\left\|\Phi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|\leq C_{0}(t^{*})\mathbf{e}^{-\alpha\tau_{r}},\ \forall\,r=j_{k},j_{k}+1,\cdots,j_{k+\ell}-1.

Since it is clear that

\mathbf{e}^{-\alpha t}\leq 1\leq\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}t},\,\forall\,t\geq 0
,
we have

\displaystyle\quad\,\left\|\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|+\left\|\Phi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{0}(t^{*})+C_{1}\right)\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}\tau_{r}},\quad\forall\,r=j_{k},j_{k}+1,\cdots,j_{k+\ell}-1.

Then, we can further obtain from (
B
) that

\displaystyle\quad\,\left\|\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+\ell}-1}\Xi_{r}(\tau_{r})-\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+\ell}-1}\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(j_{k+\ell}-j_{k}\right)\left(C_{0}(t^{*})\mathbf{e}^{-\alpha\tau_{s^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\left((C_{0}(t^{*})+C_{1})^{j_{k+\ell}-j_{k}-1}\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}(T_{\mathrm{c}}\ell-\tau_{s^{\prime}})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}(t^{*})(j_{k+\ell}-j_{k})}{C_{0}(t^{*})+C_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(C_{0}(t^{*})+C_{1}\right)^{j_{k+\ell}-j_{k}}\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}T_{\mathrm{c}}\ell-(\alpha+\lambda^{*})\tau}.

(60)

Since
j_{k+\ell}-j_{k}\leq\left\lceil\frac{T_{\mathrm{c}}}{\tau}\right\rceil\ell,\,\forall\,k=0,1,2,\ldots
,
if we let

\displaystyle C_{3}(t^{*})=\frac{C_{0}(t^{*})\left\lceil\frac{T_{\mathrm{c}}}{\tau}\right\rceil\ell}{C_{0}(t^{*})+C_{1}}\left(\left(C_{0}(t^{*})+C_{1}\right)^{\left\lceil\frac{T_{\mathrm{c}}}{\tau}\right\rceil}\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}T_{\mathrm{c}}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\left\|\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+1}-1}\Xi_{r}(\tau_{r})-\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+1}-1}\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|\leq C_{3}(t^{*})\mathbf{e}^{-(\alpha+\lambda^{*})\tau}.

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+\ell}-1}\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|+\left\|\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+1}-1}\Xi_{r}(\tau_{r})-\prod_{r=j_{k}}^{j_{k+1}-1}\Psi_{r}(\tau_{r})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\delta\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}T_{\mathrm{c}}}\right)^{\ell}+C_{3}(t^{*})\mathbf{e}^{-(\alpha+\lambda^{*})\tau},\quad\forall\,k=0,1,2,\ldots.

From (

) and (

),

.
Thus, we can further choose the positive number

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\delta\mathbf{e}^{\lambda^{*}T_{\mathrm{c}}}\right)^{\ell}+C_{3}(t^{*})\mathbf{e}^{-(\alpha+\lambda^{*})\tau}<1.

(62)

The proof is thus complete.

Asymptotic Behaviour of Solutions to the Fokker-Planck Equation: Naval Dynamics Under Stochastic Influence
Abdelkader Tizaoui

Abstract
This study investigates the asymptotic dynamics of solutions to the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov (FPK) equation, with a specific focus on ship roll stability in dynamic sea conditions. Utilizing a fourth-order filter, we conduct a thorough analysis of the time evolution of the probability distributions for roll angles, roll speeds, and roll excitations. Our theoretical framework provides new insights into the long-term behavior of these systems, emphasizing the role of stochastic perturbations. Key findings reveal that the probability of capsizing remains constant over time, offering significant contributions to the stability assessment of maritime vessels under uncertain environmental conditions. This work paves the way for more robust models in maritime engineering and dynamic stability analysis.



Keywords:
Ship Stability, Asymptotic Behavior, Dynamic Roll, Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov Equation.

Traditionally, stability has been assessed through static criteria, formulated in the early 20th century. However, the complexity of current maritime conditions necessitates a paradigm shift towards dynamic stability, where the ability to assess and quantify risks under varying sea states becomes imperative.

This study focuses on the dynamic roll behavior of a ship navigating in transverse seas, modeled as a one-degree-of-freedom (1-DOF) system. Mathematical Model

In the study of ship stability under roll motion, we model the ship’s dynamics using the following dimensionless equation, which captures the key physical phenomena involved:

\ddot{\phi}+\lambda_{1}\dot{\phi}+\lambda_{2}\dot{\phi}|\dot{\phi}|+c(\phi)=f(t),

(1)

Here,
\phi
represents the roll angle, which varies over time
t
, while
\ddot{\phi}
denotes the angular acceleration of the ship. The term
\lambda_{1}\dot{\phi}
models linear damping, providing a simple resistive force proportional to the roll velocity. The additional term
\lambda_{2}\dot{\phi}|\dot{\phi}|
introduces nonlinear damping, which accounts for the velocity-dependent resistance and better reflects real-world energy dissipation during rolling motions. The restoring moment
c(\phi)
captures the ship’s natural tendency to return to an upright position, while
f(t)
represents external stochastic forces acting on the roll motion, such as wave forces or wind.

This equation provides a comprehensive description of the roll dynamics, incorporating both linear and nonlinear damping effects. The nonlinear damping term, in particular, is crucial for modeling the complexities of ship behavior under varying sea conditions. The interaction between these terms makes it challenging to derive exact analytical solutions, which motivates a more in-depth exploration of the vessel’s stability properties.

To study the probabilistic behavior of the system, we examine the asymptotic solutions to this equation, focusing on the time evolution of parameters like roll angle, roll speed, and the likelihood of stability failures. This framework forms the basis for deriving the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov (FPK) equation, which governs the probabilistic evolution of these parameters in dynamic conditions.

3
Main Results: Asymptotic Behavior of Solutions to the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov Equation

In this section, we focus on the key results related to the evolution of the probability density function and its convergence towards the asymptotic solution of the system described by (
5
)-(
6
). The analysis centers on the steady-state behavior of the system, where the time derivative of the probability density function approaches zero, leading to the following equation:

\text{div}\left(P_{s}F\right)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\partial_{x_{4}}P_{s}=0.

(7)

Here,
P_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{6}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a solution representing the steady-state probability density function, and the equation describes how the system evolves towards its asymptotic regime. Through a detailed analysis of the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov framework, we investigate the conditions under which the system reaches an equilibrium state. The results highlight the interplay between the damping terms, the external random forces, and the restoring moments in determining the stability and convergence properties of the ship’s roll dynamics. These findings provide insights into the long-term behavior of the system under the influence of random perturbations, which are crucial for understanding the overall stability of the vessel in dynamic sea conditions.
In the following, we consider the hypotheses:

(H_{1})

\exists\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\,\text{such that}\,\text{div}(F)\geq\alpha>0
,

(H_{2})

P\in C^{0}\left(0,\infty;L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{6}\right)\right)
,

(H_{3})

F\cdot\nabla P\in C^{0}\left(0,\infty;L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{6}\right)\right)
,

(H_{4})

\partial_{x_{4}}P\in C^{0}\left(0,\infty;L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{6}\right)\right)
,

(H_{5})

P_{s}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{6}\right)
,

(H_{6})

F\cdot\nabla P_{s}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{6}\right)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let us consider the system defined by the equations in (
5
)-(
6
), under the assumptions
(H_{1})-(H_{6})
. These hypotheses provide sufficient conditions for the existence and behavior of the solution over time. In particular, we have the following result:

\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t,x)=P_{s}(x),

which indicates that, as time tends to infinity, the probability density function
P(t,x)
converges to a steady-state solution
P_{s}(x)
, which satisfies the asymptotic properties of the system.

Proof.

Let
\phi(t,x)=P(t,x)-P_{s}(x)
represent the difference between the solution at time
t
and the steady-state solution
P_{s}(x)
. This allows us to rewrite the equation governing
\phi(t,x)
as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\phi+\text{div}\left(F\phi\right)-\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\partial_{x_{4}}\phi=0,\\[6.0pt]
\phi(0,x)=P_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

(8)

where
\phi(0,x)=P_{0}(x)
is the initial condition. We now aim to show that as
t\to\infty
,
\phi(t,x)
tends to zero, implying that
P(t,x)
converges to the steady-state solution
P_{s}(x)
. To proceed, we multiply equation (
8
) by
\phi(t,x)
and integrate over the entire domain
\mathbb{R}^{6}
. This leads to the following expression:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\partial_{t}\phi\cdot\phi\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\text{div}\left(F\right)\phi^{2}\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}F\cdot\nabla\phi\cdot\phi\,dx-\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\partial^{2}_{x_{4}}\phi\cdot\phi\,dx=0.

(9)

Next, we apply integration by parts to the last two integrals. The first integral can be simplified using the divergence theorem, and the second involves the partial derivative with respect to
x_{4}
. After applying the integration by parts, we obtain an inequality for the energy of
\phi(t)
, which is defined as the squared
L^{2}
-norm of
\phi
over
\mathbb{R}^{6}
:

\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(t,x)^{2}\,dx.

This leads to the following inequality:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}\right)+\alpha\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}\leq 0,

where
\alpha
is a positive constant that comes from the hypothesis
(H_{1})
and reflects the strength of the dissipation in the system. This inequality implies that the energy of
\phi(t)
decays over time. We deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}\right)\leq 0,

which means that the rate of change of the squared norm of
\phi(t)
is non-positive, indicating that the energy of
\phi(t)
cannot increase. Integrating both sides of this inequality with respect to time, we obtain

\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}e^{\alpha t}\leq\|P_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}},

where
\|P_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}
is the initial energy of the system. This shows that the energy of
\phi(t)
decays exponentially as
t
increases. Finally, taking the limit as
t\to\infty
, we find that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{0,\mathbb{R}^{6}}=0,

which means that
\phi(t)
converges to zero in the
L^{2}
-norm as
t
tends to infinity. Therefore, we conclude that

\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t,x)=P_{s}(x),

which completes the proof.

Theorem

By transforming the equation into a first-order hyperbolic equation and solving it using the method of characteristics, these studies demonstrated local convergence to a Gaussian distribution as well as a time-asymptotic solution obtained locally.

Our fundamental result addresses this problem not only locally but also globally, while remaining consistent with the experimental observations reported in

The exponential decay of the deviation

These findings have direct applications in various engineering and physical contexts, particularly in the dynamic roll stability of ships, where understanding the long-term probabilistic behavior is critical.

These results also open new avenues for research. For instance, the explicit rate of convergence, governed by
\alpha

(H_{1})-(H_{6})
are designed to reflect realistic scenarios, relaxing these conditions could uncover more intricate behaviors, such as periodic or chaotic dynamics, which merit detailed investigation.

Moving forward, it is essential to validate these theoretical insights through numerical implementations. Developing efficient numerical schemes tailored to the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation will not only substantiate the theoretical convergence but also enable applications to complex, real-world systems. Such efforts will ensure the practical relevance of this study and contribute to advancing our understanding of dynamic stability across diverse fields.

5
Conclusions

This work provides significant contributions to the understanding of long-term dynamics in the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov (FPK) equation, with particular emphasis on dynamic roll stability in maritime vessels. By rigorously analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the probability density function (PDF), we have elucidated the evolution of key parameters—such as roll angle, roll speed, and excitation forces—over time. These findings establish a robust framework for evaluating stability and safety margins, critical components in the design and operation of naval vessels.

The results of this study are instrumental for developing predictive models aimed at assessing roll stability dynamics and formulating control strategies to mitigate the risks associated with extreme roll events. This is especially important in naval engineering, where operational safety is of utmost concern.

Despite these advances, several challenges persist. The high dimensionality of the FPK equation, coupled with the complexity of angular and spatial derivatives, necessitates the development of efficient and reliable numerical methods. Furthermore, the current analysis does not fully capture the non-linearities of excitation forces or the irregularities of real sea conditions, which are essential for a complete understanding of the system’s behavior in practical settings. Addressing these issues will be crucial for advancing the realism of the model.

Our ongoing research is focused on the development and validation of a robust numerical scheme that balances both accuracy and computational efficiency. Early results indicate that the proposed methods are capable of capturing the essential dynamics without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

Looking ahead, future work will be guided by three primary objectives: first, to validate the numerical methods through comparison with empirical data from naval vessels; second, to extend the analysis to incorporate non-linear excitation forces and variable sea states; and third, to generalize the approach to accommodate a wider range of vessel types and operational conditions. By addressing these challenges, we aim to significantly enhance our understanding of dynamic roll stability and provide practical tools to improve the safety and performance of maritime operations.

RTD-YOLO: a precise quality detection and grading model for rose tea
Authors: Zezhong Ding, Zhiwei Chen, Bin Hu, Chunwang Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Against the backdrop of the increasingly prominent commercial value of roses, rose tea with uneven quality levels has no competitive advantage. To enhance the market competitiveness of rose tea, it is necessary to classify it. At present, the grading of rose tea mainly relies on manual labor, which is inefficient. In addition, there may be difficulties in deploying large models in actual production. Therefore, we proposed a model for quality detection and grading of rose tea based on YOLOv12n. Firstly, the SEFF module was integrated into the A2C2f module to replace the A2C2f module in neck. Then, ADown was used to replace Conv. Thirdly, a parameter sharing detection head was constructed. Finally, the model was pruned using the Lamp method. The FLOPs, Params, F1, mAP, FPS, and Model size of the improved model are 2.6 G, 0.83 M, 90.48%, 95.85%, 298.5, and 2.0MB, respectively. Compared with the original model, F1, mAP, and FPS have increased by 2.93%, 1.55%, and 20.5, respectively, while FLOPs, Params, and Model size have decreased by 3.2 G, 1.68 M, and 3.2MB, respectively. The model supports real-time deployment at 55.3 FPS on Jetson Orin NX edge devices. The improved model provides technical support for intelligent grading of rose tea.

Introduction

As the ‘queen of flowers’, roses have demonstrated immense commercial value in contemporary society, with applications spanning diverse sectors including food and cosmetics1–3. Within the food industry, the rise of new tea beverages has propelled rose tea to prominence. Its distinctive flavor and health benefits have garnered widespread consumer acclaim, establishing it as a popular choice among wellness beverages and adding a new dimension to the commercial value of roses. As health awareness grows and consumer attitudes shift, the rose tea market continues to expand4. However, the current rose tea market faces a serious issue of uneven quality levels, which directly leads to severe product homogeneity, weak brand premium ability, difficulty for high-quality products to stand out, and a lack of market competitiveness. Therefore, grading rose tea is a crucial step in improving product standardization, enhancing brand competitiveness, and achieving high quality at a premium price5. Currently, the grading process in rose tea production remains heavily reliant on manual labor, revealing distinct drawbacks. On one hand, manual grading is time-consuming and inefficient, struggling to meet the demands of large-scale production6–8. On the other hand, manual grading is significantly influenced by individual experience and varying interpretations of grading standards. Variations in workers’ experience levels lead to differing interpretations and applications of grading standards, resulting in inconsistent and inaccurate grading outcomes9. Thirdly, labor costs account for more than 30% of the total production cost, and the issue of labor shortage is becoming increasingly prominent. Manual grading has become a key bottleneck restricting the production capacity improvement and standardization upgrade of the rose tea industry, urgently requiring automated and intelligent grading solutions.

In recent years, deep learning technologies represented by Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) have achieved breakthrough development in the field of computer vision with their powerful feature extraction capabilities and end-to-end learning advantages10. They have widely penetrated into quality inspection and grading tasks in multiple industries such as agriculture, food, and industry, providing efficient and accurate automation solutions for traditional manual inspection11,12. In the field of food inspection, CNN technology has been successfully applied in multiple scenarios such as fruit and vegetable quality grading and agricultural product defect detection, achieving non-destructive, fast, and accurate discrimination of fruit and vegetable quality13. For agricultural product testing, CNN technology has been used for corn leaf disease classification, almond quality testing, etc., greatly improving the efficiency and standardization level of agricultural product sorting14,15. In the field of industrial quality inspection, CNN has achieved full scene coverage from part defect detection to textile defect recognition, demonstrating strong universality and generalization ability.

In the CNN technology system, single-stage object detection algorithms represented by YOLO (You Only Look Once) have become the mainstream technical solution for food detection and grading tasks due to their advantages of balancing detection accuracy and inference speed16. They have achieved a series of successful applications in the automated grading of various agricultural products and foods17,18. Xu et al. proposed a machine vision system for automated, online quality grading of sweet potatoes in fresh markets. Among three YOLOv8 models, YOLOv8l achieved the highest overall accuracy at 96.7%, 90.3%, and 82.6%, respectively19. Xue et al. proposed an instance segmentation model based on YOLOv11 to automatically perform Pomodoro defect segmentation and classification. The proposed model mAP@0.5 was 84.1%, which was 0.8% higher than that of the original YOLO11 model20. However, deep learning models demand substantial computational resources and require high-end hardware, which is often challenging to provide in practical production settings21,22. Han et al. enhanced the accuracy and efficiency of Rubus idaeus L. maturity grading by lightweighting the YOLOv8 model. Their proposed model achieved a 3.0% higher mean average precision than the baseline YOLOv8s, significantly improving both accuracy and efficiency in Rubus idaeus L. maturity grading23. Liu et al. developed a mushroom quality identification and grading model based on YOLOX. The improved model achieves mAP and FPS of 99.96% and 57.3856, respectively, while reducing model size by over half24. Chen et al. proposed a lightweight banana freshness detection and grading method based on YOLOv7-Tiny25. Compared to the baseline model, the improved model achieves 1.4%, 5%, 1.5%, and 3% increases in mAP, precision, recall, and F1 score, respectively, with significantly reduced model complexity. Li et al. proposed a lightweight cherry ripeness grading detection model based on YOLOv1226. Compared to the baseline model, this model achieved 10.3% and 11.8% improvements in detection accuracy and recall, respectively, while reducing the number of model parameters by 73.1%. Wang et al. proposed a walnut kernel appearance quality grading detection model based on YOLOv5s27. The improved model achieved a detection mAP of 86.1% and a detection speed of 58.3 ms, surpassing the performance of the original YOLOv5s.

The aforementioned studies have achieved satisfactory results in detection and grading, providing valuable references for this research. However, the subjects studied differ significantly from rose tea, rendering them unsuitable for rose tea detection and grading tasks. Few reports exist on deep learning-based rose tea detection and grading. Ding et al. proposed an improved rose tea quality grading detection model based on YOLOv8, but the model still requires improvement in terms of lightweight design and detection accuracy28. Based on this, this research is positioned as application-oriented agricultural intelligence research, with the core objective of addressing the practical issues of low efficiency, high cost, and inconsistent standards in the manual grading of rose tea. The aim is to develop a deployable automated and precise grading detection model. The main contributions of this paper are: (1) Integrating the SEFF module into the A2C2f module to replace the A2C2f module in the neck, thereby improving detection performance. (2) Replacing Conv with ADown to reduce model complexity while improving detection performance. (3) Constructing a parameter-sharing detection head to further decrease model complexity and enhance detection performance. (4) Applying the Lamp method for model pruning, achieving lightweight design while further improving detection performance.

Results

Experimental platform and training parameters

The operating system used in this experiment is Linux. The CPU model is the 13th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i9-13900K, and the GPU model is the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090. The programming language is Python 3.9.18. The deep learning framework employed is PyTorch 2.0.0, with GPU acceleration libraries CUDA 12.2 and CUDNN 8.4.1. All experiments in this study were conducted using the PyTorch deep learning framework. The Adam optimizer was employed to update parameters, with training conducted over 400 epochs. The batch size was set to 16, and patience was set to 50.

Model pruning experiment results

Analysis of Table 3 reveals that compared to models before pruning, better performance was achieved using the Lamp method with pruning rates of 1.3 and 1.6, and using the Slim method with a pruning rate of 1.1. Specifically, the Lamp method with a pruning rate of 1.6 yielded the optimal model performance. The model’s FLOPs, Params, and Model size were reduced to 2.6 G, 0.83 M, and 2.0MB, respectively, while achieving F1 score, mAP, and FPS of 90.48%, 95.85%, and 298.5. Under the premise of maintaining basic accuracy, the model compression is achieved. After pruning, the complexity of the model decreases, and the recognition accuracy not only does not decrease, but also shows a certain improvement. The reason for this is that the original model often has a large number of redundant channels, weak contribution features, and noise sensitive structures. These redundant parameters not only increase the computational burden, but also cause overfitting to a certain extent, reducing the model’s generalization ability on real test data. By pruning the model to remove neurons and channels with lower contributions, the model structure is effectively streamlined, reducing noise interference and redundant information transmission, making the model more focused on effective features that play a key role in target discrimination. Meanwhile, pruning can reduce model complexity, improve the stability of training and optimization processes, thereby exhibiting stronger generalization ability and ultimately achieving improved accuracy.

As shown in Fig. 2, the channel counts in the Conv modules of layers 15 and 18, the A2C2f_SEFF module in layer 11, and the C3k2 module in layer 20 were significantly reduced. Results of the ablation experiment

As shown in Table 4 and Fig. 3, after integrating the SEFF module into the A2C2f module, the model’s FLOPs, Params, and Model size remain unchanged, while the F1 score, mAP, and FPS all improve by 0.63%, 0.16%, and 3.3, respectively. After replacing the Conv module with ADown, the model’s FLOPs, Params, and Model size decreased by 0.5 G, 0.32 M, and 0.6MB, respectively. The F1 score, mAP, and FPS all improved by 1.59%, 0.96%, and 3.2, respectively. After constructing the parameter-sharing detection head, the model’s FLOPs, Params, and Model size were further reduced by 1.2 G, 0.27 M, and 0.5MB, respectively. F1 score, mAP, and FPS were further improved by 0.39%, 0.32%, and 6.4, respectively. Following model pruning, FLOPs, Params, and Model size decreased again by 1.5 G, 1.09 M, and 2.1MB, respectively. F1 score, mAP, and FPS improved further by 0.32%, 0.11%, and 7.6 respectively. Compared to the original model, the improved model achieves enhanced detection performance while reducing complexity. FLOPs, Params, and Model size decreased by 3.2 G, 1.68 M, and 3.2MB, respectively, representing 44.83%, 33.07%, and 38.46% of the original model. F1 score and mAP improved by 2.93% and 1.55% respectively, while FPS increased by 20.5. This analysis further validates the effectiveness of improvements across all components of this study.

Analysis of Table 5 and Fig. 4 reveals that in terms of model complexity, the Faster RCNN, SSD, and RT-DETR series models exhibit significantly higher FLOPs, Params, and Model size, resulting in substantially greater deployment costs. Model deployment testing

To verify the engineering usability of the improved model RT-YOLO in practical production scenarios, this study compared it with the baseline model YOLOv12n for deployment testing. Discussion

To address the time-consuming, labor-intensive, and inefficient nature of manual rose tea sorting, while also considering the challenges of deploying large models in actual production, this study first trained and tested various-sized YOLOv12 models on a self-built dataset. YOLOv12n was selected as the base model. Subsequently, through operations such as integrating fusion modules, replacing lightweight convolutions, constructing parameter-sharing detection head, and model pruning, we experimentally developed a lightweight rose tea quality detection and grading model, RT-YOLO. Compared to the original model, the improved model achieved FLOPs, Params, F1, mAP, Model size, and FPS of 2.6G, 0.83 M, 90.48%, 95.85%, 2.0MB, and 298.5, respectively. It demonstrated significant improvements in F1, mAP, and FPS improved by 2.93%, 1.55%, and 20.5, respectively, while FLOPs, Params, and Model size decreased by 3.2 G, 1.68 M, and 3.2MB, respectively. The model achieves lightweighting while reducing complexity and supports real-time inference at a high frame rate of 55.3 FPS on Jetson Orin NX edge devices, meeting the sorting requirements of rose tea.

Of course, the improved rose tea quality detection and grading model in this study has certain limitations. Firstly, the rose tea varieties are relatively limited. Secondly, the lighting and background conditions are relatively uniform, which somewhat restricts the model’s generalization ability. Thirdly, the class imbalance problem in the rose tea grading dataset in this study has a significant impact on the evaluation indicators of the model. Additionally, the current model only grades rose tea based on its appearance features without incorporating the physicochemical composition of each grade, resulting in insufficient precision.

Moving forward, we will further optimize the rose tea quality detection and grading model by incorporating additional rose tea varieties and expanding the dataset to include image data under complex conditions such as varying lighting and backgrounds. Secondly, we will further improve the sorting accuracy of various categories through optimization strategies such as data augmentation, which will better meet the defect detection needs of actual production lines. Additionally, we will integrate spectral technology with the physicochemical composition of rose tea to achieve more precise detection and grading, thereby enhancing the overall quality of rose tea classification.

Dataset preparation

This study examines dried rose tea from Pingyin County, Jinan City, Shandong Province (36°N, 116°E). Based on the visual quality characteristics of rose tea, it is classified into seven grades: super grade, first grade, flower bud, flower heart, yellow flower, scattered flower, and waste flower. The standard for super grade is that the color should not turn white, yellow, or appear to be exposed without revealing or leaking the flower center. The first grade standard is for flower centers with small leakage points, the color cannot turn white or yellow, and the flowers must be in full bloom. The standard for flower bud is that the petals cannot bloom or turn yellow. The standard for flower heart is that the flower should not turn white, not be too small, and the flower center should be slightly exposed. Yellow flowers are those that are more than half yellow and have a large yellow center. Scattered flower is larger than the bud, with a hard heart in the middle and scattered petals around it. A2C2f_SEFF

The Spectral Enhanced Feed-Forward (SEFF) module was proposed by Sun et al. in 202434. During feature transformation, the SEFF module first employs 3 × 3 depth convolutions and 3 × 3 dilated depth convolutions to extract spatial features at different scales or orientations. It then transforms the spatial-domain features from both branches into the frequency domain, where weighted operations and bias adjustments are applied to modulate the importance of different frequency components, achieving feature enhancement in the frequency domain. Finally, the frequency-enhanced features are transformed back into the spatial domain. This module combines separable convolutions with weighted bias operations in the frequency domain to effectively enhance frequency-domain features and improve model performance. Parameter-sharing detection head

The YOLOv12n head component accounts for nearly 30% of the model’s computational load and 20% of its parameters. This is because the head section comprises three detects, each consisting of a localization branch and a classification branch. The localization branch includes two sets of 3×3 convolution and one set of 1×1 convolution, while the classification branch comprises two sets of depth-separable convolutions and one set of 1×1 convolution. The middle feature extraction layer of the two branches is completely independent, with a large number of repeated convolution operations, resulting in severe parameter redundancy and computational overhead. To address this issue, this study introduces a parameter sharing mechanism and reconstructs the parameter sharing detection head, as shown in Fig. 11b. The core idea of this mechanism is to reuse the same intermediate feature extraction layer for both localization and classification branches, while maintaining independence only in the final task output layer, thereby significantly reducing duplicate parameters and improving the inference efficiency of the model.

Model pruning

Model pruning is a deep learning model compression and acceleration technique that significantly reduces the number of parameters and computational complexity by removing redundant or unimportant weights, neurons, or even entire structures within the model. This approach lowers storage requirements, speeds up inference, and strives to maintain performance close to the original model37,38.

The Lamp pruning method aims to achieve efficient model slimming by minimizing pruning distortion. At its core, Lamp minimizes L2 distortion, striving to preserve the weight distribution of the model during pruning. It defines the Lamp score to measure weight importance and adaptively assigns sparsity based on layer characteristics39–41. The Slim pruning algorithm achieves channel pruning by leveraging the scaling factor γ within Batch Normalization layers to control channel importance. Specifically, it applies L1 regularization to the scaling factor, causing the scaling factors of unimportant channels to approach zero, thereby identifying and pruning these channels42.

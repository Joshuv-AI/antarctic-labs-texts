Palmitoylation-related gene expression and its prognostic value in ovarian cancer: insights into immune infiltration and therapeutic potential
Authors: Shaoying Zeng, Lijian Zeng, Xiaoying Xie, Liang Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPalmitoylation, a key post-translational modification, plays a significant role in ovarian cancer (OV) progression. However, the impact of palmitoylation-related genes on genomic instability, immune infiltration, and therapeutic response in OV remains poorly understood. This study aimed to investigate these factors to facilitate risk stratification and therapeutic intervention, providing insights into personalized treatment strategies.MethodsData from TCGA and GEO were utilized to develop a prognostic model based on palmitoylation-related genes. Differential expression, functional enrichment, and immune infiltration analyses were performed. Immune cell composition and pathway activities in different risk groups were assessed using CIBERSORT and ssGSEA algorithms. Immunotherapy response was predicted using TIDE and SubMap, while drug sensitivity differences were evaluated using the GDSC database.ResultsUnivariate, LASSO, and multivariate Cox regression analyses identified palmitoylation-related genes with significant prognostic value. The prognostic model effectively stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups, demonstrating significant survival differences. Immune infiltration analysis revealed distinct immune cell compositions and functions between risk groups. Low-risk patients exhibited higher immune scores and increased expression of immune checkpoints (PD-1, CD274, CTLA4), suggesting greater response to immunotherapy. Drug sensitivity analysis identified compounds with differential efficacy between risk groups, highlighting potential targeted treatment options.ConclusionPalmitoylation-related genomic features significantly influence OV progression and the immune landscape, offering potential for improved risk stratification and informing immunotherapy strategies to enhance patient outcomes.Keywords: Palmitoylation, OV, Immune Infiltration, Prognostic model, Immunotherapy, Genomic instability

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OV) is one of the most aggressive gynecologic malignancies, characterized by high mortality due to late-stage diagnosis and limited therapeutic options [1, 2]. Despite advances in surgical techniques and chemotherapy regimens, the overall 5-year survival rate for patients with advanced OV remains dismal, typically below 50% [3, 4]. The molecular heterogeneity of the disease presents significant challenges to treatment, underscoring the urgent need for reliable biomarkers to enhance prognostic accuracy and guide personalized therapeutic strategies.

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) play a pivotal role in modulating protein function and stability, with palmitoylation emerging as a key regulator in cancer biology [5, 6]. Palmitoylation, the reversible attachment of fatty acids to cysteine residues, influences a broad array of cellular processes, including protein trafficking, signal transduction, and immune response [7–9]. Dysregulation of palmitoylation has been implicated in tumor progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance across various cancers [10, 11]. However, its precise role in the pathogenesis and progression of OV remains insufficiently explored.

While palmitoylation-related genes have been studied in other cancers, their involvement in OV, particularly in relation to genomic instability, immune infiltration, and therapeutic response, remains poorly understood [12]. Genomic instability is a hallmark of cancer progression, and understanding how palmitoylation-related genes contribute to this process in OV could provide valuable insights into tumor behavior [13]. Additionally, the influence of these genes on the tumor immune microenvironment may have significant implications for immunotherapy, which is becoming an increasingly important treatment modality for OV.

Given these gaps in knowledge, this study aims to explore the prognostic significance of palmitoylation-related genes in OV. By developing a prognostic model, we aim to elucidate the relationship between these genes and key aspects of OV biology, including immune infiltration, genomic instability, and response to therapy. Our findings are expected to inform risk stratification and support the development of personalized treatment strategies, ultimately improving patient outcomes in OV.

Methods

Data collection and model development

To develop a robust prognostic model, we first selected the TCGA OV dataset as the training cohort. All samples were normalized to Transcripts Per Million (TPM) format to ensure data consistency, and samples with a survival time of zero were excluded to maintain data integrity [14]. The GSE32062 dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) was subsequently used as an independent validation cohort to evaluate model robustness. 3532 Palmitoylation-related genes were identified from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) using the GSEA platform (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp). The prognostic significance of these genes was initially assessed using univariate Cox regression analysis, and genes with p-values less than 0.05 were selected for further analysis. To prevent overfitting and identify the most relevant prognostic markers, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was applied to refine the gene set to an optimal subset. These selected genes were then incorporated into a multivariate Cox regression model to construct a prognostic model capable of predicting patient outcomes. The risk score for each patient was calculated as a weighted linear combination of the expression levels of these genes, using their respective coefficients from the multivariate Cox model. Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. Finally, the model's predictive performance was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis to compare overall survival between the risk groups, and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves to assess the accuracy of survival predictions at 1-, 3-, and 5-year intervals.

Gene expression and survival analysis

To evaluate the prognostic significance of the eight genes identified in the final prognostic model, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed for each gene using the TCGA OV cohort. Additionally, differential expression analysis was conducted to compare the expression levels of these eight genes between OV samples and normal ovarian tissue, aiming to assess their relevance to disease progression. Genes that did not show significant differential expression between cancerous and normal tissues were excluded from further analysis.

CXCR2 (C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 2) single-cell expression analysis

Considering that CXCR2 exhibited the highest hazard ratio (HR) in the multivariate analysis with a p-value less than 0.001, we conducted further investigations to explore its expression at the single-cell level. Data for this analysis were obtained from the TISCH2 single-cell database (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/). Initially, CXCR2 expression was analyzed across all available OV samples in the database. Subsequently, two samples that had received treatment (immunotherapy or chemotherapy) were excluded, and the analysis focused on six untreated OV single-cell datasets. This approach enabled us to evaluate the distribution of CXCR2 across various cell types in a treatment-naïve context.

CXCR2 genomic analysis

To investigate the relationship between CXCR2 expression and genomic heterogeneity, we utilized TCGA pan-cancer samples to quantify various genomic heterogeneity indicators, including tumor mutational burden (TMB) [15], microsatellite instability (MSI) [16], tumor purity [17], ploidy [18], neoantigen burden (NEO) [19], loss of heterozygosity (LOH) [20], homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) [21], and mutation allelic heterogeneity (MATH) [22]. Correlation analyses were performed to assess the associations between CXCR2 expression and each of these genomic heterogeneity indicators. Additionally, Simple Nucleotide Variation level 4 datasets were downloaded from the Genomic Data Commons (GDC, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and processed using MuTect2 software [23]. CXCR2 mutation data from all TCGA samples were integrated, and protein domain information for CXCR2 was obtained using the maftools R package (version 2.2.10).

Differential expression, functional enrichment, and mutation analysis

Differential expression analysis was performed to identify genes significantly differentially expressed between high- and low-risk groups. The identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were subsequently subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis, which included biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF), as well as Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis to identify enriched signaling pathways. Additionally, mutation analysis was performed to compare genetic variation between the high- and low-risk groups. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was also analyzed to evaluate differences between the groups. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to assess survival differences between patients with high and low TMB values, and a combined analysis of TMB and risk scores was conducted to classify patients into four prognostic groups.

Immunological and therapeutic response analysis

Immune infiltration analysis was performed using the CIBERSORT algorithm to quantify differences in immune cell composition and function between the high- and low-risk groups. Differential expression analysis was conducted for immune cell types and immune functions. Additionally, the differential expression of 49 common immune checkpoint genes between the high- and low-risk groups was assessed. Immunotherapy prediction analysis was carried out using multiple approaches. The ssGSEA algorithm was applied to evaluate differences in ESTIMATE, stromal, and immune scores between the risk groups. Correlation analysis was performed to examine associations between risk groups and the expression of key immune-related genes, including PD-1, CD274, and CTLA4. The TIDE algorithm was utilized to predict immunotherapy response, evaluating TIDE and Exclusion scores between the high- and low-risk groups [24]. Submap analysis was conducted to assess the potential responsiveness of the risk groups to PD-1 therapy [25]. Additionally, drug sensitivity differences between the high- and low-risk groups were explored using the GDSC database to identify compounds with significantly different sensitivities.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.1) and relevant Bioconductor packages. Multiple testing corrections were applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. All tests were two-tailed, and p-values < 0.05 were considered significant unless otherwise noted.

Results

Prognostic model validation and survival analysis

Univariate Cox regression identified several palmitoylation-related genes significantly associated with prognosis, including protective factors MANF, CXCL13, CD40LG, and C2orf88, as well as risk factors UNC119, PEX3, PDP1, CYP4F22, CXCR2, and CACNA1C (Fig. 1A). These significant genes were further refined using LASSO regression to prevent overfitting and enhance model stability (Fig. 1B, C). The refined gene set was used in multivariate Cox regression analysis, which revealed that eight genes were independently associated with prognosis, including protective factors CD40LG and C2orf88, and six risk factors—CACNA1C, CXCR2, PDP1, CYP4F22, UNC119, and MANF (Fig. 1D). The correlation between risk score and patient survival in the TCGA cohort indicated that higher risk scores were associated with an increased risk of death (Fig. 1E). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to validate the prognostic significance of the risk groups. The results confirmed that patients in the high-risk group exhibited significantly poorer survival outcomes compared to those in the low-risk group, with p-values less than 0.05 in both the training (TCGA) and validation (GSE32062) cohorts (Fig. 1F, G). To further verify the robustness of the prognostic model, time-dependent ROC curve analysis was conducted, demonstrating its strong predictive performance for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival. These findings support the model's potential utility in clinical risk assessment.

Kaplan–meier survival and differential expression analysis

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that all eight genes included in the prognostic model were significantly associated with overall survival in the TCGA OV cohort (Fig. 2A), underscoring their prognostic importance. Differential expression analysis indicated that, after excluding PDP1 due to its lack of significant differential expression, CACNA1C was found to be highly expressed in normal ovarian tissue, while the other six genes were significantly upregulated in OV samples (Fig. 2B). These results support the potential role of these genes in OV progression and their value in predicting patient prognosis.

CXCR2 single-cell expression analysis

Given the strong prognostic significance of CXCR2 with the smallest p value and the largest HR in the multivariate analysis, we further examined its expression at the single-cell level. Using the TISCH2 single-cell database, we analyzed CXCR2 expression across different cell types in all OV samples (Fig. 3A). To gain a clearer understanding of CXCR2's role in untreated OV, we excluded two samples that had received immunotherapy or chemotherapy, focusing instead on six untreated single-cell datasets. This analysis provided detailed insights into the distribution of CXCR2 among various cell populations, highlighting distinct expression patterns in specific cell types that may contribute to disease progression (Fig. 3B).

CXCR2 genomic heterogeneity and mutation analysis

To explore the relationship between CXCR2 expression and genomic heterogeneity across different cancer types, we analyzed TCGA pan-cancer samples. Correlation analyses revealed significant associations between CXCR2 expression and multiple genomic heterogeneity metrics, including TMB, MSI, tumor purity, NEO, LOH, MATH, ploidy, and HRD (Fig. 4A–H). In OV patients, distinct correlations between CXCR2 expression and genomic heterogeneity indicators were observed. Specifically, CXCR2 expression was positively correlated with TMB (Fig. 4A), LOH (Fig. 4E), MATH (Fig. 4F), and HRD (Fig. 4H), while negatively correlated with MSI (Fig. 4B), tumor purity (Fig. 4C), and ploidy (Fig. 4G). These results indicate that CXCR2 may play a key role in modulating genomic instability in OV, which could contribute to disease progression and impact patient outcomes. These findings suggest that CXCR2 expression is associated with multiple aspects of genomic instability across various cancer types. Additionally, we integrated CXCR2 mutation data from the GDC database, utilizing MuTect2-processed TCGA samples. Protein domain information for CXCR2 was obtained using the maftools R package, and the distribution of mutations within CXCR2 protein domains is illustrated in Fig. 4I, providing insights into the potential functional consequences of these mutations.

Analysis of genomic features and survival outcomes

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between high- and low-risk groups demonstrated significant enrichment in biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF) (Fig. 5A, B). Similarly, KEGG pathway analysis revealed critical signaling pathways enriched in both groups, providing insight into key biological mechanisms underlying OV progression (Fig. 5A, B). To further explore the functional implications of these DEGs, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) using the c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt [All Canonical Pathways] reference set. This analysis identified additional pathways significantly enriched between high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 5C, D), offering a comprehensive view of pathway involvement in disease progression. Mutation analysis showed no significant differences in the mutation profiles of the top five mutated genes—TP53, TTN, CSMD3, USH2A, and RYR2—between the high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 6A, B). TMB analysis indicated that the high-risk group had slightly lower TMB values compared to the low-risk group (Fig. 6C). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with high TMB had better overall survival compared to those with low TMB (Fig. 6D). Furthermore, combining TMB and risk scores allowed for the classification of patients into four distinct groups, revealing significant differences in survival outcomes (Fig. 6E).

Immune infiltration and immunotherapy prediction analysis

Immune infiltration analysis using the CIBERSORT algorithm revealed significant differences in immune cell composition and immune function between high- and low-risk groups. Specifically, differential analysis of immune cell types showed distinct expression profiles (Fig. 7A), while immune function analysis demonstrated significant differences in immune pathway activities between the groups (Fig. 7B). Furthermore, analysis of 49 common immune checkpoint genes indicated notable differences in expression between high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 7C). The ssGSEA algorithm-based analysis revealed significant differences in ESTIMATE, stromal, and immune scores between the risk groups, with the low-risk group showing a higher immune score (Fig. 8A). Correlation analysis demonstrated that patients in the low-risk group exhibited higher expression of PD-1, CD274, and CTLA4 (Fig. 8B). TIDE algorithm analysis indicated no significant difference in TIDE scores between the groups, but the Exclusion score was found to be higher in the high-risk group. Specifically, the low-risk group exhibited lower Exclusion score, suggesting potential differences in immune evasion mechanisms between these groups. (Fig. 8C). Submap analysis suggested that patients in the low-risk group might respond better to PD-1 therapy (Fig. 8D). Drug sensitivity analysis, conducted using the GDSC database, identified eight drugs with significant differences in sensitivity between high- and low-risk groups, providing insights for potential therapeutic interventions (Fig. 8E).

Discussion

OV is one of the deadliest gynecological malignancies, largely due to late-stage diagnosis and limited treatment options [26]. Despite advances in surgery and chemotherapy, the prognosis remains poor, particularly for patients with advanced disease. Palmitoylation, a post-translational modification, involves the attachment of fatty acids to proteins, regulating their function, stability, and localization [27]. Emerging evidence indicates that dysregulated palmitoylation plays a key role in cancer progression, metastasis, and immune evasion [10]. Investigating the role of palmitoylation-related genes in OV holds promise for improving prognostic accuracy and identifying novel therapeutic targets [28]. Furthermore, with the rapid advancement of bioinformatics technologies, it is now possible to systematically analyze large-scale genomic and transcriptomic datasets, enabling the identification of palmitoylation-related genes associated with cancer progression [29].

In this study, we developed and validated a prognostic model based on palmitoylation-related genes, providing novel insights into their role in OV progression, immune infiltration, and treatment response. Our results underscore the significance of palmitoylation-related genomic features in influencing patient outcomes, suggesting their potential utility in clinical risk stratification and personalized therapies.

One of the critical findings of this study is the distinct immune infiltration patterns observed between high- and low-risk groups. Low-risk patients exhibited higher immune scores, increased infiltration of cytotoxic T cells, and elevated expression of immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-1 and CTLA4, which suggests a more active immune surveillance in these patients [30, 31]. This observation aligns with the growing body of evidence indicating that the tumor immune microenvironment plays a crucial role in determining tumor behavior and patient outcomes. Given these results, low-risk patients could potentially benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitors, as their tumors may be more responsive to immunotherapy.

It is important to note that TIDE is one of the methods used to predict response to immunotherapy. However, immunotherapy response is complex and multifactorial, and a single method may not fully capture all aspects of immune response. Therefore, we also performed SubMap analysis, which provided additional insights into the potential immunotherapy responses of the different risk groups. This analysis further supports our overall hypothesis and helps to validate the findings. However, these analyses, including TIDE and SubMap, are intended to provide directional insights into the relationship between the risk score and immune response. They are exploratory in nature, and while they provide valuable information, they should not be viewed as definitive conclusions but rather as part of a broader investigation into the role of immune modulation in OV.

Moreover, the association between palmitoylation-related genes and genomic instability markers such as tumor mutational burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), and loss of heterozygosity (LOH) further emphasizes the relevance of these genes in ovarian cancer progression. High TMB, often associated with better responses to immunotherapy, was more prevalent in the low-risk group, providing a potential explanation for their enhanced immune activity [32]. Similarly, the connection between MSI and tumor immune response supports the idea that genomic instability can foster neoantigen production, driving an immune response that can be harnessed for therapeutic benefit.

Single-cell analysis revealed that the palmitoylation-related gene CXCR2 is predominantly expressed in Mono/Macro cells within the single-cell dataset. This observation is particularly interesting given the established role of CXCR2 in the recruitment and activation of neutrophils and its involvement in inflammatory responses [33]. In the context of cancer, CXCR2 has been implicated in promoting tumor-associated inflammation and facilitating immune evasion by recruiting myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and macrophages into the tumor microenvironment [34]. The elevated expression of CXCR2 in Mono/Macro cells could suggest a potential mechanism by which palmitoylation-related pathways modulate immune cell activity in the tumor microenvironment, contributing to cancer progression and immune evasion. This finding highlights the importance of further investigating the role of CXCR2 and related palmitoylation pathways in modulating macrophage and monocyte function, particularly in ovarian cancer.

From a clinical perspective, our findings suggest that palmitoylation-related genes could serve as valuable biomarkers for risk stratification in ovarian cancer. The ability of these genes to predict survival outcomes and immune responsiveness underscores their potential as prognostic tools. Furthermore, given their association with immune infiltration, targeting palmitoylation pathways in combination with immunotherapy could represent a promising therapeutic approach, particularly for low-risk patients with elevated immune activity.

Despite the strengths of this study, several important limitations must be acknowledged. First, our analysis relied on publicly available datasets, which may not fully reflect the heterogeneity of ovarian cancer across diverse populations, potentially introducing biases in the findings. While the results are promising, validation in larger, more representative cohorts is crucial to confirm the robustness and generalizability of the prognostic model. Additionally, although significant associations were observed between palmitoylation-related genes and immune infiltration, these findings are correlative, and further mechanistic studies are required to clarify how these genes specifically modulate immune cell behavior and contribute to genomic instability in ovarian cancer. To fully understand the underlying biological mechanisms, experimental validation of these associations is essential. Future research should prioritize the validation of this prognostic model in prospective clinical trials, as well as explore the role of palmitoylation in modulating immune responses in ovarian cancer. These mechanistic insights could uncover novel therapeutic strategies for targeting these pathways and improving patient outcomes.

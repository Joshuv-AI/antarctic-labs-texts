Xuebijing and somatostatin against acute pancreatitis: A systematic review and network pharmacology
Authors: Ji-Ping Cao, Xiang-Hua Piao, Long-Xun Zhu, Pan-Feng Feng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common pancreatic disease. Xuebijing injection (XBJ) combined with somatostatin in the treatment of AP is frequently used in clinical practice. There is, however, a lack of high-quality evidence-based evidence and network pharmacology to regard the therapeutic efficacy and pharmacological mechanisms.Purpose:The purpose of this study is to investigate the potential therapeutic targets and pharmacological mechanism of XBJ in AP using integrating evidence-based medicine and network pharmacology.Methods:PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Weipu, and Wanfang databases were searched. Randomized controlled trials of XBJ combined with somatostatin (experimental group) versus somatostatin alone (control group) in the treatment of AP were collected. After extracting data from the literature that meets the inclusion criteria, and using the Cochrane Scale to evaluate the quality of the literature, we used Rev Man 5.3.0 statistical software to perform meta-analysis of the effective rate, the disappearance time of abdominal pain and bloating, the recovery of gastrointestinal motility, serum-related indicators, inflammatory factors, ventilator evacuation time, and hospitalization time. A network pharmacology is used to analyze the potential active ingredients and related crucial targets of the XBJ in the treatment of AP, and we explored key regulatory pathways and potential biomarkers related to XBJ for AP with integrated bioinformatics analysis.Results:It was significant that the total effective rate in the study group was higher than that in the control group (P < .05). The time of recovery of gastrointestinal motility, serum-related indicators, inflammatory factors, ventilator withdrawal time, and hospitalization time were significantly lower than that of the control group. The differences were statistically significant (P < .05). Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3, tumor protein P53, interleukin 6, tumor necrosis factor, Jun Proto-Oncogene, SRC Proto-Oncogene, Heat Shock Protein 90 Alpha Family Class A Member 1, Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor A, Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor, and Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase 1 were identified as the key hub of the protein–protein interaction network according to an analysis of network centrality. According to the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analysis, the main pathways are involved in PI3K–Akt signaling pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, and tumor necrosis factor signaling pathway.Conclusions:The effectiveness of combination therapy of XBJ and somatostatin on AP is likely to be better than somatostatin. In addition, XBJ and somatostatin synergistically treated AP through a multi-pathway network.

1. Introduction

Acute pancreatitis (AP), a rapidly progressing disease characterized by severe complications and high mortality leads to many admissions to intensive care units.[1,2] In the last 20 years, the incidence of the disease has increased by more than 20%.[3,4] According to the revised Atlanta Classification, AP severity can be divided into 3 groups: mild, moderately severe, and severe.[5] AP is a common pancreatic disease, and is the result of a combination of several factors.[6] The incidence of AP is high, and the pathogenesis is more complicated. Single treatment often has poor results. After the disease progresses to severe necrotizing pancreatitis, the mortality rate increases significantly, with a case fatality rate as high as 35%.[7] For AP, the current clinical treatment measures are mainly to reduce trypsin activity, improve immune defense function, reduce the production of inflammatory factors, and avoid vascular endothelial cell damage. Somatostatin is a commonly used treatment drug for patients with AP, which can reduce the digestion of the pancreas and inhibit the secretion of pancreatic enzymes, but the long-term effect is not good, and it is difficult to achieve the expected therapeutic effect.[8]

In recent years, studies by some scholars have shown that the combination of Chinese and Western medicine has achieved good results in the treatment of AP.[9] Xuebijing injection (XBJ) is a kind of mixed traditional Chinese medicine preparation, mainly composed of Paeonia lactiflora Pal. (Shaoyao), Ligusticum chuanxiong hort (Chuanxiong), Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge (Danshen), Carthamus tinctorius L. (Honghua), and Angelica sinensis (Danggui),[10] which has the effects of promoting blood circulation to remove blood stasis, relaxing the collaterals and dissipating toxic evil.[11]

The use of XBJ combined with somatostatin in patients with AP can help shorten the time of symptom improvement and reduce the level of inflammatory factors.[12] Despite an increasing number of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) involving AP patients, these studies have distinct efficacy outcomes, and the number of samples in each study is limited. Therefore, the safety of this treatment needs to be verified.

XBJ has a good curative effect on pancreatitis, but due to the diversity of traditional Chinese medicine ingredients and the complexity of interaction with the human body, it is difficult to study the potential molecular mechanism of traditional Chinese medicine treatment of diseases, so there is still a lack of systematic analysis and detailed mechanism. Network pharmacology is a novel method for predicting the mechanism of drug treatment of disease at the overall level by searching the network database, constructing the “drug–target–disease” network relationship, and performing network topology analysis.[13] It provides the possibility to clarify the mechanism of traditional Chinese medicine to treat diseases.

In this study, meta-analysis was employed for scientific evaluation of the included RCTs. The present study is the first comprehensive systematic review that determined the clinical efficacy of XBJ combined with somatostatin in patients with AP. In addition, the method of network pharmacology was used to study the mechanism of XBJ in the treatment of pancreatitis based on the perspective of systems biology, to promote the understanding of related traditional Chinese medicines, and to provide reference for further experimental verification and clinical use.

2. Methods and materials

2.1. Meta-analysis method of XBJ injection combined with somatostatin for AP

Registration: According to the PRISMA guidelines,[14] this protocol has been registered with PROSPERO (ID: CRD42022351065), and we will strictly comply with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. This is a literature-based data study, which does not require ethical approval.

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

2.2.1. Inclusion criteria

Research types included were RCTs on the theme of XBJ injection combined with somatostatin in the treatment of AP.

(1) Researchers enrolled patients diagnosed with AP who met the standards of Guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of AP in China in this study. ① The persistent pain in the epigastric region. ② More than 3 times the upper limit of normal amylase or lipase activity in the serum. ③ Abdominal imaging studies revealed AP. As long as 2 criteria were met, the patient was diagnosed with AP. (2) In this study, all individuals were recruited, regardless of their gender, age, race, or geographical location.

2.2.2. Interventions

The patients were divided into study group (combination of XBJ injection and somatostatin) and control group (somatostatin alone). No restrictions are imposed on dosage, frequency, intensity, and duration of drugs.

2.2.3. Study outcome

Analyses of data will include both quantitative and qualitative informations. In qualitative information, effective rates are expressed as percentages. Markedly effective: the patient’s persistent epigastric pain, fever, and other clinical symptoms disappeared after treatment, and auxiliary tests such as serum amylase and urinary amylase returned to normal; effective: the patient’s clinical symptoms were relieved after treatment, and the auxiliary examination indexes were close to normal; ineffective: after the above treatment, the condition did not improve significantly. Effective rates = (Markedly effective + effective)/total. In quantitative information, the disappearance time of abdominal pain, gastrointestinal motility recovery, serum-related indicators, complications, ventilator withdrawal time, and hospital stay were included.

2.2.4. Exclusion criteria

A study that clearly did not meet the inclusion criteria was excluded from the study review and studies in which the following conditions were present were excluded. (1) Patients exhibit serious problems with their heart, lungs, liver, or kidneys. (2) Patients who quit the experiment halfway with low compliance. (3) Female patients who were pregnant or lactating were excluded from the study. (4) Reviews, systematic reviews, case reports, experience summaries, inconsistent observation indicators, and literature published in general journals were excluded. (5) Studies where the complete content could not be obtained. (6) Duplicate publication or incomplete information. (7) Studies published in languages other than Chinese or English.

2.3. Search strategy

The PubMed, Cochrane Library, EMbase, Chinese National Knowledge Infra structure (CNKI), Weipu, and Wanfang databases were searched by computer to find RCTs of Xuebijing combined with somatostatin in the treatment of AP. The retrieval period was from the establishment of the database to March 2024. The search words included “Xuebijing injection,” “somatostatin,” and “Acute pancreatitis.”

2.4. Literature screening and data extraction

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 2 researchers independently screened the literature, conducted preliminary screening through reading titles and abstracts, excluded irrelevant literature, and then read the full text to determine whether to include it. If the opinions were inconsistent, they were resolved through discussion or the third investigator’s assistance. Three researchers who have studied systematically, one of whom has extensive clinical experience extracts data from the selected literature. The extracted data includes the first author, publication year, number of patients, gender, average age, treatment period, intervention measures, and outcome indicators.

2.5. Quality evaluation

Two researchers evaluate the quality of the literature. The risk of bias is assessed using the literature quality assessment tool provided by the Cochrane Collaboration (https://www.cochrane.org/). The quality of the selected literature is evaluated through 7 items. Items include random sequence generation, allocation concealment, double-blind test subjects and researchers, blinded evaluation of results, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and other sources of bias. The biases in the above-mentioned items can be judged by 3 bias levels: low risk, uncertainty, and high risk. In the process of literature evaluation, if there are uncertain issues, the evaluators can discuss with each other, or invite other researchers to participate.

2.6. Statistical analysis

Literatures about binary variables are described by risk ratio (RR), and articles about continuous variables are described by STD mean difference (SMD). The effect size and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) are used to express the RR and SMD. Review manager 5.3 software was used to analyze the heterogeneity of the literatures and the drawing of the contribution map. The heterogeneity in the process of statistical analysis adopts the χ2 test, and the magnitude of the heterogeneity can be judged by I2. If I2 ≤ 50% in the study, a fixed effect model can be used, which is considered to have good homogeneity. If I2 > 50% in the study, it indicates that there is a large heterogeneity among multiple studies in the trial, and the random effects model can be considered for analysis in the model. An inverted funnel chart was used to analyze possible publication bias.

2.7. Network pharmacology method of XBJ for AP

2.7.1. Collation of target genes related to the active ingredients of XBJ

In order to find the active ingredients of C tinctorius L, P lactiflora Pall, Conioselinum anthriscoides “Chuanxiong,” S miltiorrhiza Bunge and A sinensis (Oliv), we consulted the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology (TCMSP) Database (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/index.php), which is a database of Chinese herbal medicine. A minimal oral bioavailability of 30% and a minimum drug-likeness of 0.18 were used to screen the active compounds and targets protein. In addition, literature from PubMed and CNKI was used to supplement the components that not collected in the TCMSP database. For these components, PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) and Swiss Target Prediction database (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch) were used to obtain targets protein. Finally, the gene symbol corresponding to the target protein was searched through UniProt Database (https://www.uniprot.org).

2.7.2. Collation of target genes related to human AP

Taking “acute pancreatitis” as the key words, a number of databases were used to obtain targets related to AP, including OMIM (https://www.omim.org), GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org), and TCMIP (http://www.tcmip.cn/ETCM/index.php/Home). The search results from each database were merged and duplicate target genes were removed. The remaining target genes were used as AP-related genes.

2.7.3. Construction of Venn diagram

An Venny2.1 online tool (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny) was used to create a Venn diagram by extracting the herbal medicine targets from XBJ injection and the disease targets from AP. Venn diagrams revealed intersections between the derived targets of XBJ injection and those of AP, which were then considered potential therapeutic targets for XBJ injection in AP.

2.7.4. Construction of ingredient–target–disease network

The active ingredients of XBJ injection, the targets corresponding to the active ingredients, and the targets predicted for the AP were imported into Cytoscape 3.9.1 software, and a drug–target–disease network diagram was constructed for network visualization. In order to calculate the degree of connection, we used the Network Analyzer plug-in in Cytoscape. “Network analysis” analyzes the topological parameters of a network, with a “degree” representing the number of nodes connected to the node. The greater the degree, the more critical the node is.

2.7.5. Construction of protein–protein interaction (PPI) network

In order to develop a PPI, the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org) was utilized. The minimum required interaction score: high confidence was set at 0.7, and the species selection was set to Homo sapiens. The PPI relationship was obtained, irrelevant protein nodes were deleted, the degree and combination score of related nodes were exported, and saved as TSV format files. Next, the interaction relationship between the main active components and the core target proteins was analyzed by using Cytoscape 3.8 software (http://www.cytoscape.org/).

2.7.6. GO bioanalysis and KEGG pathway analysis

Metascape database (http://metascape.org) was used to perform gene enrichment analysis (Gene Ontology [GO]) and pathway enrichment analysis (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes [KEGG]) of key targets. Through GO enrichment, the main action process of the target can be understood. The KEGG pathway can further understand the role of the target in metabolism, signal transduction, and other pathways by observing the distribution of the target in the pathway. The GO and KEGG pathway analyses were screened for P < .05. The first 20 results were visualized by bar graph to analyze the main molecular biological processes and signal pathways involved in key targets.

3. Results

3.1. Study selection

In strict accordance with the search strategy, 87 literatures were initially obtained. After reading the titles, abstracts and full texts, 25 RCTs (Appendix Reference, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O186) were finally included (Fig. 1), totaling 1950 patients. Among them, there were 984 cases in the treatment group and 966 cases in the control group. The basic information of the included studies was shown in (Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O185 and Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O185).

Flowchart of the retrieval and screening process.

3.2. Characteristics of studies included

Twenty-five RCTs included in this study were assessed according to the criteria shaped by Collaboration Assessment Tool (version 5.0.1), which specialized in evaluating systematic reviews of interventions. As you can see in Figure 2 and Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O185, the quality and risk of bias assessment of the study were conducted. Selection bias: only 4 (16%) studies used an adequate randomization method (random number table, random grouping of envelopes, and random lottery method) which were rated low risk, 20 (80%) studies mentioned randomization but did not specify the method which were rated unclear risk, and 1 (4%) study did not use an adequate randomization method (random order of admission) which was rated as high risk. It appears that all studies did not report any approach to concealing allocation and were rated as unclear bias in their allocation concealment methods. Attrition bias: all studies were deemed low risk of bias because no outcome data were missing for incomplete outcomes. Reporting bias: all studies included in the review had reportedly completed outcome assessments and were not subject to selective reporting. Other bias: due to inability to determine any other sources of bias, all other studies (n = 25) were rated as having unclear risk of bias.

Risk of bias. (A) Risk of bias graph indicating the review authors’ rating regarding the risk of bias, presented as percentages, across all of the included studies. (B) Risk of bias summary indicating the review authors’ judgments on each risk of bias item for each included study. 3.3. Meta-analysis result of XBJ injection combined with somatostatin for AP

3.3.1. Effective rate

Fourteen studies reported the effective rate, with a total of 986 patients. There was no significant heterogeneity among the studies (P = .73, I2 = 0%), and the fixed effects model was used for analysis. The results of meta-analysis showed that the total effective rate of the experimental group was higher than that of the control group, and the difference was statistically significant [RR = 1.24, 95% CI (1.18, 1.30), P < .00001]. The results were shown in Figure 3.

Forest plot of effects of Xuebijing in combination with somatostatin on effective rate.

3.3.2. Abdominal pain and bloating disappear time

Nineteen studies reported the abdominal pain disappear time and 3 studies reported abdominal bloating disappear time, with a total of 1458 and 176 patients, respectively. There were significant heterogeneity among the studies (P < .01, I2 = 94% and 79%, respectively). Random effects model was used for meta-analysis. Compared with the control group, the duration of abdominal pain and bloating in study group was significantly shorter, and the difference were statistically significant (SMD = −2.17, 95% CI [−2.70, −1.65], P < .00001, Fig. 4A and RR = −1.17, 95% CI [−1.89, −0.45], P = .001, Fig. 4B). The results were shown in Figure 4.

Forest plot of effects of Xuebijing in combination with somatostatin on abdominal pain and bloating disappear time. (A) Abdominal pain disappear time; (B) abdominal bloating disappear time.

3.3.3. Time of gastrointestinal motility recovery

Gastrointestinal motility recovery mainly includes defecation recovery time, exhaust recovery time, bowel sound recovery time, and gastrointestinal function recovery time. For defecation recovery time, exhaust recovery time and gastrointestinal function recovery time, there were significant heterogeneity among the studies (P < .05, I2 = 94%, 94% and 75%, respectively). Thereby, random effects model was used for meta-analysis. Compared with the control group, the time of defecation recovery, exhaust recovery, and gastrointestinal function recovery in study group was significantly shorter, and the difference was statistically significant (SMD = −1.38, 95% CI [−2.62, −0.14], P = .03, Fig. 5A; SMD = −2.06, 95% CI [−3.43, −0.69], Fig. 5B and SMD = −3.82, 95% CI [−4.44, −3.19], Fig. 5D, respectively). For bowel sound recovery time, there was no significant heterogeneity among the studies (P = .44, I2 = 0%), and the fixed effects model was used for analysis. The results of meta-analysis showed that the bowel sound recovery time of the experimental group was shorter than that of the control group, and the difference was statistically significant (SMD = −1.46, 95% CI [−1.17, −1.15], P < .00001, Fig. 5C). The results were shown in Figure 5.

Forest plot of effects of Xuebijing in combination with somatostatin on gastrointestinal motility recovery. (A) Defecation recovery time; (B) exhaust recovery time; (C) bowel sound recovery time; (D) gastrointestinal function recovery time.

Serum-related indicators include blood amylase content, blood amylase recovery time, and white blood cell recovery time. For white blood cell recovery time, there was no significant heterogeneity among the studies (P = .98, I2 = 0%), and the fixed effects model was used for analysis. 4. Discussion

AP takes systemic inflammatory reaction as the main clinical symptom, which has the characteristics of rapid onset and high mortality. About 20% of patients with AP are severe pancreatitis. According to the course of the disease, it can be divided into acute reaction period, systemic infection period, and residual infection period. During the acute reaction period, if the level of inflammatory cytokines in the patient’s pancreas is not effectively controlled, it is very easy to cause systemic inflammatory response syndrome, which can lead to multiple organ failure. It is an important factor leading to the death of patients with AP. Therefore, clinical treatment of patients should focus on controlling the level of inflammatory factors.

XBJ added on the basis of conventional treatment has a potential benefit for improving the outcomes of AP.[16] The control group for this article was not fixed. In this study, the control group was treated with somatostatin alone for AP, while the experimental group received a combination of XBJ and somatostatin. The major difference in our study is that we established a fixed control group, thereby standardizing the treatment protocol. XBJ combined with somatostatin has been used in patients with severe AP and achieved good clinical results. However, due to the limitation of the number of patients and other reasons, the results are not necessarily convincing. In order to investigate whether the combination of XBJ injection has clear efficacy in AP and to further explore the potential mechanism of XBJ in the treatment of AP, evidence-based medicine and bioinformatics was used to solve this question.

4.1. The combination of XBJ injection and somatostatin to show a remarkable efficacy

The results of this meta-analysis showed that the total effective rate of treatment in the observation group was significantly higher than that in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant (P < .05). In the study group, the disappearance time of abdominal pain and bloating, gastrointestinal function recovery time, serum-related indicators, inflammatory factors, respiration, the evacuation time, and hospital stay were significantly shorter than the control group, the difference was statistically significant (P < .05). In conclusion, our meta-analysis shows that combining XBJ injection and somatostatin for AP can achieve better therapeutic effects, and have a significant improved effectiveness in the treatment of AP compared to application of somatostatin or XBJ injection alone.

4.2. The possible mechanism of HKC for CGN based on network pharmacology

Network pharmacology provides a new perspective for analyzing drug action. In view of the multi-component, multi-path, and multi-target action characteristics of traditional Chinese medicine, the mechanism of action can be analyzed and explained through the connections and relationships of nodes in the network.[17] In this study, the mechanism of action of XBJ in the treatment of AP was systematically analyzed through network pharmacology technology. Based on this method, it was screened that XBJ contains quercetin, albiflorin, paeoniflorin, rosmarinic acid, luteolin, Tanshinone c, isosalvianolic acid C, and other key components. Quercetin plays a role in the treatment of pancreatitis by inhibiting the expression and activation of the inflammatory factor NF-κB.[18] Paeoniflorin may ameliorate acute renal injury following ANP in rats by inhibiting inflammatory responses and renal cell apoptosis which was associated with the p38MAPK and nuclear factor-κB signal pathway.[19] rosmarinic acid appears to attenuate damage in sodium taurocholate-induced AP and reduce the release of inflammatory cytokines by inhibiting the activation of NF-κB.[20] Luteolin exerts a protective effect on the pancreas by reducing the expression of p-P38 protein and inhibiting the activation of NF-κB and p38MAPK pathways.[21] Topological analysis of 30 potential targets of XBJ in the treatment of AP, including STAT3, TP53, IL-6, etc, which are mainly involved in cell proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, inflammatory response, and other processes. As a cytoplasmic protein induced by a variety of cytokines, STAT3 has been confirmed to aggravate AP by antagonizing pancreatic acinar cell apoptosis or delaying inflammatory cell repair through the STAT3 pathway after activation by inflammatory factors.[22] TP53 gene variants potentially associated with AP susceptibility.[23] IL-6 upregulation leads to severe inflammatory response during AP development.

In pancreatitis, autophagy is activated but lysosomal degradation is inhibited, and an imbalance between increased autophagosome formation and decreased lysosomal degradation results in a blockade of autophagic flux, resulting in severe acinar cell degeneration and trypsinogen activation, leading to the development of AP.[24] KEGG is mainly enriched in PI3K–Akt signaling pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, and NF-kappa B signaling pathway, etc. Systemic inflammatory response is an important feature of the pathogenesis of AP. PI3K–Akt can degrade the inhibitory protein IκB by phosphorylating the upstream IκB kinase complex of NF-κB, causing the release and activation of NF-κB, thereby transcriptionally regulating the expression of a large number of inflammatory factors.[25] PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is involved in the regulation of HIF-1α expression, which in turn aggravates the local hypoxic response and systemic inflammatory response of pancreatitis, and increases the complications and mortality of pancreatitis.[26]

In conclusion, XBJ may act on multiple targets such as STAT3, TP53, and IL-6, and regulate inflammatory cell apoptosis and immune levels through HIF-1 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, and NF-kappa B signaling pathway, etc. In this study, the active components and potential targets of XBJ, as well as the complex network mechanism of multi-channel and multi-target synergistic treatment of AP, were preliminarily discussed with the help of network pharmacology. This study provides a reference for the subsequent in-depth study of the mechanism of action of XBJ in the treatment of AP.

5. Limitations

There are, however, still some limitations to this meta-analysis. It should be noted that some of the trials included in this meta-analysis were of poor quality due to their small scale and small scope. Published literatures do not provide detailed information on the method of a distribution hiding, randomization and blindness used, resulting in biased randomization. In network pharmacology, additional molecular validation is required for the targeting protein and pathway candidates generated by system pharmacology-based analysis.

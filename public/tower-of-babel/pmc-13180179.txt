Revolutionizing Crohn’s disease detection: integrating AI with intestinal ultrasound for superior diagnosis
Authors: Ruo Huang, Minying Zhong, Chengjie Hong, Zhixing Liu, Kexin Chen, Xiaoyu Li, Qinghua Lu, Qinxue Liu, Zhaotao Li, Chuntao Jiang, Guoliang Jian, Peizhu Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Intestinal ultrasound (IUS) is increasingly utilized by healthcare providers for the diagnosis of Crohn’s disease (CD), which has highlighted new challenges related to standardized image interpretation and its limitations as a research instrument. The application of artificial intelligence methodologies may offer solutions to these challenges. This study aimed to assess the feasibility of integrating IUS with clinical features to enhance the diagnostic accuracy of CD. We compared the performance of five machine learning (ML) models to diagnose the most effective model for this purpose.Objective:To evaluate and compare the diagnostic performance of five ML models in identifying CD using IUS features alone and in combination with clinical features; to determine the optimal ML approach for improving diagnostic accuracy in patients presenting with chronic diarrhea and to establish a framework for automated diagnosis of CD through the integration of imaging and clinical data.Design:Retrospective diagnostic accuracy study using ML methodology.Methods:We conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical features and ultrasound data from a cohort of 1119 patients presenting with chronic diarrhea, categorized according to the diagnostic criteria recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO). Approximately 80% of the dataset was allocated for training purposes, while the remaining 20% was reserved for testing.Results:The logistic regression model that utilized ultrasound features exclusively demonstrated superior diagnostic performance compared to the other four models, achieving an accuracy of 78%, sensitivity of 85%, and specificity of 84.9%, with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.89 in the test set. This model accurately identified CD in patients presenting with chronic diarrhea. The integration of clinical features further enhanced the diagnostic performance of the XGBoost model, achieving an accuracy of 92%, sensitivity of 89%, specificity of 88.7%, and an AUC of 0.98 in the test set.Conclusion:This study not only illustrates the feasibility of employing ultrasound analysis in patients with diarrhea but also highlights the potential for developing a methodology that integrates ultrasound data with clinical indicators. Our findings provide a framework for automated diagnosis of CD.Keywords: Crohn’s disease, intestinal ultrasound, machine learning

Introduction

Recent advancements in ultrasound technology have facilitated the development of intestinal ultrasound (IUS) as a valuable noninvasive, point-of-care instrument for the monitoring of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). This innovation enables healthcare providers to make informed, real-time decisions at the patient’s bedside. IUS demonstrates outstanding sensitivity and specificity in the detection of inflammation and holds significant promise as a research tool in clinical trials and biomarker discovery. 1 IUS allows for easy assessment of transmural healing (TH), a goal beyond simple mucosal healing (MH) that may influence long-term disease outcomes.

However, the inflammatory parameters assessed via ultrasound in the context of IBD are predominantly semiquantitative, except for bowel wall thickness (BWT). Furthermore, a definitive framework for the optimal utilization of ultrasound in evaluating clinical activity remains to be established.

Machine learning (ML) represents a domain of study that employs algorithmic methodologies for data analysis. It holds the potential to be integrated with various biomarkers for the purposes of diagnosing, predicting disease severity, and monitoring disease progression. Recent research has indicated that ML algorithms offer distinct advantages over traditional statistical methods in the development of predictive models. Considering this, we endeavored to construct an ML model aimed at facilitating the early identification of CD. This study conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical, laboratory, and ultrasound data from patients diagnosed with CD, healthy controls, and individuals with non-IBD intestinal disorders. We employed four widely utilized ML models—namely, multilayer perceptron artificial neural network (MLP-ANN), radial basis function artificial neural network (RBFNN), decision tree, and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA)—to identify the most effective model for the early diagnosis of CD.

Methods

This study was conducted based on consecutive patients who visited Foshan First People’s Hospital with the chief complaint of “diarrhea” from January 2024 to July 2025. Participants were categorized into two groups based on their final diagnosis: the CD group, which comprised 178 cases, and the non-Crohn’s disease (NCD) group, which included 941 cases, resulting in a total sample size of 1119. The data were sourced from the hospital’s medical record and imaging systems. Approval for data acquisition was granted by the Ethics Review Committee of Foshan First People’s Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from all participants. This research adhered to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines and the Transparent Reporting of Multivariate Prediction Models for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines. Data analysis was conducted from July 1st, 2025, to November 1st, 2025.

Data preprocessing

In this study, we used numerical coding (male = 0, female = 1; NCD group = 0, CD group = 1) for categorical variables (such as “gender” and “group”). For handling missing values in numerical features, the group-specific median imputation method was employed. This involved calculating the median of each feature separately for the CD group and the NCD group, and subsequently imputing the missing values based on the group affiliation of each case. This approach preserved the distributional characteristics of the data across different groups and mitigated the potential bias introduced by global imputation methods.

Furthermore, to address the issue of class imbalance in the dataset, the ADASYN (Adaptive Synthetic Sampling) algorithm was utilized to oversample the minority class, specifically the CD samples. This algorithm focused on the density distribution at the boundary region of the minority class samples. The procedure was as follows: (1) Setting the number of synthetic samples S; (2) For each minority sample xi, calculating ρi, the proportion of majority samples among its K nearest neighbors; (3) Normalizing ρi to determine the number of synthetic samples per instance; (4) Generating new samples using uj=xi+ri⋅(xiq−xi), where ri is a random number in [0,1] and xiq is a randomly selected neighbor.

Ultrasound scanning

The Esaote MyLab Twice ultrasound diagnostic instrument, manufactured by Esaote in Italy, was utilized in this study. It was equipped with a CA541 convex array probe (operating at 1.0–8.0 MHz) and a LA332E intermediate frequency linear array probe (operating at 3–11 MHz). All patients were instructed to fast for 8 h prior to the examination. During the procedure, patients were positioned supine, with the abdomen fully exposed, and were asked to breathe calmly. Initially, a CA541 convex array low-frequency probe was employed to conduct a comprehensive preliminary assessment of the intestines. The examination commenced at the ileocecal valve in the right lower abdomen, systematically scanning the ileocecal region, ascending colon, hepatic flexure of the colon, transverse colon, splenic flexure of the colon, descending colon, sigmoid colon, and upper rectum. Subsequently, the abdominal small intestine was examined in the upper left, lower left, upper right, and lower right quadrants to assess for any thickening of the intestinal wall, peristaltic rigidity, intestinal stenosis or dilation, thickening of the peri-intestinal mesenteric fat, and other abnormalities. The continuity of the intestinal wall and the presence of fistulas or abscesses were also evaluated.

Ultrasound image analysis

The IUS indicators collected for this study were derived exclusively from the most severely affected segment of the intestine, identified as the segment exhibiting the greatest BWT. The parameters assessed included BWT, the structural layers of the intestinal wall, intestinal wall blood flow, and the presence of mesenteric fat hyperplasia. BWT is defined as the thickness of the intestinal wall measured from the mucosal surface to the serosal surface while the intestine is in a relaxed state, free from peristaltic activity. For accuracy, three measurements were taken from the longitudinal section, and the average value was calculated. 2 A BWT greater than 3 mm is indicative of intestinal wall thickening. In a normal intestinal wall, the distinct characteristics of at least three different layers are clearly observable. Abnormal alterations in the structure of the intestinal wall layers may present as either a partial or complete loss of the layered architecture, often accompanied by uneven low echogenicity throughout the affected layers (see insert picture). Intestinal wall blood flow was evaluated using power Doppler ultrasound, and its Limberg grade was recorded 3 : Grade 0 is normal, non-thickened intestinal wall; Grade 1 is intestinal wall thickening with no blood flow signal; Grade 2 is intestinal wall thickening with punctate or short blood flow signals; Grade 3 is intestinal wall thickening with long (>1 cm) blood flow signals; Grade 4 is intestinal wall thickening with long blood flow signals connected to the mesentery. Mesenteric fat hyperplasia was defined as thickening, increased echogenicity, and blurred texture of the mesentery surrounding the thickened intestinal tube. 4 It was also necessary to observe whether there is intestinal stenosis, intestinal fistula, abdominal abscess, abdominal effusion, mesenteric lymph nodes, etc. Two physicians with more than 10 years of experience in IUS examination (Guoliang Jian and Qinxue Liu) performed image analysis without knowing the pathological results and reached a consensus through consultation when there was a disagreement.

Statistical analysis

Data exhibiting a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and analyzed using the independent samples t-test. In contrast, data demonstrating a non-normal distribution were presented as median with interquartile range (IQR) and analyzed via the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages, with comparisons conducted using the chi-square test. A significance threshold was established at a p-value of less than 0.05. Parameters exhibiting significant differences (p < 0.05) were utilized to construct a four-diagnosis model. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated, and the area under the curve (AUC) was computed to evaluate the efficacy of various parameters and ML models. Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics, USA) for the random forest, multilayer perceptron (MLP), logistic regression, support vector machine (SVM), and decision tree models. For internal validation, approximately 80% of the patient and caregiver data were allocated for training, while 20% were designated for testing, along with an additional dataset reserved for external validation.

Results

Machine learning model construction and application

This investigation employed a retrospective design, encompassing a total of 1119 participants. We categorized all collected data based on different indicators into basic features, ultrasound features, and multimodal features (Table S2). The collected clinical data were systematically categorized into three distinct feature domains (Table S2): (i) basic demographic characteristics, comprising gender and age; (ii) ultrasonographic parameters, encompassing BWT, Limberg classification(type I to IV), fat creeping sign (present/absent), intestinal wall stratification (clear/focal indistinct/diffuse indistinct), and mesenteric lymph node status (present/absent); and (iii) multimodal laboratory indicators, including stool occult blood (OB), white blood cell count (WBC, G/L), hematocrit (HCT), platelet count (PLT, G/L), eosinophil count (EO, G/L), neutrophil count (NEU, G/L), leukocyte esterase (LEU, G/L), neutrophil-to-platelet ratio (NEU/PLT), and C-reactive protein (CRP).

The dataset was partitioned into training and test subsets in a 4:1 ratio. To address class imbalance within the training set, the ADASYN oversampling technique was employed, resulting in a total of 540 cases of CD and 753 control samples. To enhance the model’s robustness in detecting CD, an additional 17 representative cases from January to July 2025 were incorporated, culminating in a total of 241 test samples (comprising 53 cases of CD and 188 controls). The XGBoost algorithm was utilized as the baseline model, with hyperparameters optimized through grid search in conjunction with 5-fold cross-validation. In terms of structural parameters, the model was configured with 150 base learners, a maximum depth of 3, and a minimum leaf node sample weight of 3, thereby balancing model expressiveness with overfitting control. Regarding learning parameters, a learning rate of 0.2, a minimum split gain of 0.3, and a logarithmic loss function were selected to effectively address the asymmetric costs associated with false positives and false negatives in clinical contexts.

The findings indicated that the model exhibited high sensitivity (0.89) and specificity (0.96) for the detection of CD, with an AUC of 0.98, reflecting exceptional discriminative capability. Overall, the model effectively distinguished between the two sample types, with the AUC for CD nearing 1, thereby suggesting a heightened diagnostic value for this condition.

To ascertain the optimal model architecture, in addition to the XGBoost model, four other diagnostic ML models were developed: Random Forest, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Logistic Regression, and SVM. The datasets and processing methodologies were standardized, with separate training and validation phases implemented. Table 1 presents the performance metrics for each model. The XGBoost model outperformed the other models in terms of AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. Specifically, in the diagnosis of CD, the constructed XGBoost model achieved an AUC of 0.9802, an accuracy of 94%, a sensitivity of 89%, and a specificity of 96% (Figure 2).

Comparison of models using clinical features alone and with ultrasound features

As mentioned earlier, this study categorized predictive features into three types: basic clinical features, ultrasound imaging features, and multimodal fusion features. Although the model integrating all features achieved 94% accuracy in CD identification, obtaining comprehensive laboratory indicators and ultrasound parameters in clinical practice was often limited by the availability of medical resources and the timeliness of examinations. Therefore, this study further evaluated the independent diagnostic efficacy of single-modal features by constructing predictive models based on ultrasound markers (intestinal wall thickness, Limberg grade, fat creeping sign, intestinal wall layering, and mesenteric lymphadenopathy) and clinical indicators.

Among the classification models constructed based on clinical features (such as gender, age, complete blood count, inflammatory markers, and fecal occult blood), the XGBoost algorithm performed best, with an AUC of 0.939 (Figure 3). This result confirmed that conventional clinical indicators may provide robust diagnostic differentiation capabilities without imaging assistance and had significant clinical application value for primary healthcare institutions or scenarios where ultrasound examinations could not be performed immediately.

However, in clinical practice, there were often delays in laboratory results or situations where blood/fecal indicators failed to reflect local structural lesions in the intestine. In these cases, imaging assessment became a crucial basis for diagnostic decisions. Accordingly, this study further constructed a classification model based on five ultrasound features, with logistic regression showing the best performance (Figure 4). Notably, the model based solely on ultrasound features achieved an AUC of 0.899, representing 91.8% (0.899/0.980) of the performance of the multimodal XGBoost model. This finding indicated that ultrasound imaging features, as an independent diagnostic modality, had reliable identification capabilities. Although slightly inferior to the multimodal combined model, they may assist ultrasound physicians in providing preliminary diagnostic opinions when laboratory data are incomplete or when rapid assessment of intestinal structural changes is required, and provide imaging evidence for clinicians’ differential diagnosis.

Discussion

CD is a chronic, progressive IBD,5,6 often presenting initially with nonspecific symptoms, most commonly diarrhea. 7 Given that CD remains incurable and poses increasing therapeutic challenges due to complications arising from delayed diagnosis, 8 early identification and subsequent intervention are paramount to improving long-term patient outcomes. Based on the above, the purpose of this study is to use ML models to achieve early identification of CD in patients with diarrhea.

This study, conducted within a clinical context where diarrhea was the primary symptom, developed several ML models utilizing IUS imaging features in conjunction with clinical indicators to accurately identify CD. The findings indicated that an XGBoost model integrating IUS with clinical indicators (including fecal occult blood test (OB), complete blood count, and CRP) can function as an immediate, minimally invasive, and reproducible screening tool. This approach has the potential to significantly decrease the incidence of missed diagnoses and unnecessary referrals for CD.

In recent years, the utilization of artificial intelligence (AI) within the domain of IBD has experienced significant growth. Its primary value is manifested not only in enhancing workflow efficiency but also in functioning as an effective triage and decision support tool. 9 A substantial body of evidence has established that IUS exhibits high sensitivity and specificity in the initial diagnosis of CD and in the identification of associated complications, such as strictures, fistulas, and abscesses10–12 A recent meta-analysis reported that the diagnostic sensitivity of IUS for patients with suspected CD was 79.7%, while the specificity was 96.7%. Furthermore, the sensitivity for monitoring treatment in patients with confirmed CD was found to be 89%, with a specificity of 94.3%. These findings underscore the significance of IUS as a critical diagnostic tool for CD. While IUS exhibits high sensitivity and specificity for the diagnosis and monitoring of CD, bowel wall thickening remains one of the primary indicators of inflammatory activity. Nonetheless, bowel wall thickening is not exclusively indicative of inflammation, as it may also result from non-inflammatory factors such as fibrosis and chronic structural changes. Consequently, relying solely on IUS imaging features to assess active inflammation entails a risk of misdiagnosis. Furthermore, this study presents a novel multimodal AI model that integrates characteristics of inflammatory ulcerative syndrome (IUS) with clinically accessible and cost-effective indicators, such as routine blood and stool tests. This integration is expected to significantly enhance the convenience of clinical practice.

The results indicated that the model utilizing only IUS features demonstrated commendable discriminative ability (AUC = 0.90, accuracy = 0.84, specificity = 0.85). In contrast, the combined model that incorporated clinical indicators significantly enhanced diagnostic efficiency (AUC = 0.98, accuracy = 0.92, specificity = 0.89). This study provides compelling evidence that the integration of imaging and clinical data successfully addresses the limitations inherent in relying on a single data modality, thereby establishing a novel multidimensional framework for the early detection of CD. To the best of our knowledge, this research represents the first systematic analysis of IUS images in conjunction with routine clinical and laboratory parameters. The model demonstrated robust performance, achieving an AUC of 0.89 in the internal test set. The laboratory parameters incorporated into the model were obtained immediately upon patient admission, rendering the approach noninvasive, convenient, and free of additional burden. While external validation in independent cohorts was not performed, the strong internal validation performance and the biological plausibility of the selected features support the potential clinical applicability of this approach, pending prospective multi-center validation.

This study is subject to several limitations: ① The single-center, retrospective design resulted in a restricted sample size, and the quality of ultrasound images is significantly influenced by the operator’s experience, which may introduce selection bias; ② Missing values were imputed using the group median, a method that, while maintaining data integrity, may inadvertently lead to convergence in sample distribution; ③ Although ADASYN oversampling addressed class imbalance, the generation of artificial samples may introduce biases in data distribution, particularly within high-dimensional feature spaces, potentially compromising generalization capabilities; ④ This investigation concentrated on the identification of CD within a cohort presenting with diarrhea and did not perform a comprehensive differential diagnosis between ulcerative colitis (UC) and CD. Therefore, future research needs to be conducted with larger sample sizes, incorporating the wall thickness of the terminal ileum according to IUS, including more biomarkers such as fecal calprotectin, and applying advanced ML models to verify the diagnostic and predictive efficacy of combining ultrasound with multiple feature vectors in distinguishing between UC and CD, and to assess the severity of these diseases.

Conclusion

This study successfully developed robust models utilizing XGBoost, Random Forest, MLP, Logistic Regression, and SVM, integrating clinical and ultrasound features to effectively identify CD. Notably, the XGBoost model exhibited superior performance in differentiating NCD from CD and in assessing disease activity. In addition, our findings indicate that a Logistic Regression model constructed solely with ultrasound features demonstrated comparable efficacy in identifying CD. However, due to the inherent limitations of this study, we advise caution in drawing definitive conclusions. Instead, we suggest further detection of the small bowel using capsule endoscopy or small bowel endoscopy for those with a high probability of CD. Future validation of these findings necessitates the exploration of novel ML models and the utilization of larger datasets.

Evaluating the impact of ambient temperature on energy consumption rate of electric two-wheeler using real-world driving data
Authors: N. Kumaresan, A. Rammohan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Electric two-wheelers are gaining popularity due to environmental benefits, extensive operating temperature, and low running costs. However, various environmental factors significantly affect their energy consumption rate and overall performance, with ambient temperature being the critical one. This study investigates the impact of ambient temperature on energy consumption rate and performance of electric two-wheelers by utilizing real-world driving data. An experimental setup consists of drive cycle source, speed control unit, motor control unit, battery pack, and motor with varying ambient temperatures ranging from 15 °C to 40 °C. The experimental setup utilized various sensors to collect parameters such as speed, temperature, current, voltage, state of charge, state of health, and driving range. The result shows that the optimal operating temperature for electric two-wheelers is between 20 °C and 30 °C, where energy efficiency and driving range are maximized. At 15 °C, the driving range decreases to 112.41 km, approximately 2 % lower than at moderate ambient temperature, while at 40 °C, the driving range increases by 2 %, reaching 114.68 km. The study also reveals that an extreme ambient temperature reduces the battery's useable capacity, negatively impacting power output and driving range. Additionally, the battery pack's cell temperature rises by 2.2 °C above the ambient temperature in both low and high conditions.

Introduction

The implementation of electric vehicles (EVs) in various sectors has experienced significant growth due to the adoption of sustainable transport worldwide [1]. The sales share of electric two-wheelers (E2W) in India has progressed from 0.1 % in 2019–2020 to 4.8 % in 2022–2023, as per the Society of Manufacturers of Electric Vehicles [2]. E2Ws offer several benefits, such as zero tailpipe emission, reduced operational expenses, and less environmental harm compared to internal combustion engines. The performance of the E2Ws can be drastically affected by various factors, including ambient temperature, battery pack parameters [3], motor efficiency, transmission efficiency, driving patterns [4], driving behaviour [5], road environment [6], and vehicle parameters. The ambient temperature significantly impacts various aspects of E2Ws, such as the battery pack performance, motor efficiency, cooling system operation, lifetime, charging and discharging characteristics, energy and power reduction [7]. The optimal operating temperature range of EV is typically from −20 °C to 60 °C, to ensure the mechanical safety of the system. Between these temperature ranges, specifically between 15 °C and 25 °C, is where the optimum performance and energy efficiency are deemed to be achieved [[8], [9], [10], [11]]. However, battery capacity and power loss are caused by extremely low temperatures or greater ambient temperatures. Various studies have been presented to consider the impacts of ambient temperature on EVs, taking into consideration these various factors. Carlos et al. [12] studied and analyzed the effect of various metrological circumstances on the performance of EVs. The results show that at higher ambient temperatures, the universal driving range increases by up to 29 % when compared to energy consumption at the standard ambient temperature of 25 °C, and at low ambient temperatures, the universal driving range decreases by up to 18 % when compared to energy consumption at the standard ambient temperature of 25 °C. Kulkarni et al. [13] developed an integrated drivetrain model for EVs which was validated at both the component and system levels. The study highlighted that doubling the ambient temperature from 25 °C increases the energy consumption rate by 35 % per kilometre. Liu et al. [14] inspected the influence of ambient temperature on the EV's equivalent fuel economy, performance, and driving range. As a result of the low ambient temperature, fuel saving, and the driving range of the EV decrease by up to 30 %. Iora et al. [15] developed a quasi-steady backward-looking model to assess the significance of ambient temperature on the performance and driving range of EVs. Based on the analysis, low ambient temperature increases the energy consumption rate and reduces the driving range. Luo et al. [16] analyze the interpretation of lithium batteries under different low ambient temperatures. The results show that the discharge capacity and the performance of lithium batteries will decrease when the ambient temperature drops below 0 °C. Yazan et al. [17] conducted an assessment to examine the consequence of ambient temperature on the energy consumption rate of EVs through road tests. The results expose that EV operation at cold ambient temperatures from 0 °C to 15 °C has a driving range of 28 % lower than at moderate ambient temperatures ranging from 15 °C to 25 °C. The ambient temperature has consequences for the quantity of energy recuperated during regenerative braking, which upsets battery efficiency. Under the low ambient temperature of an EV, the charging and discharging current of the EV battery pack are restricted to avoid undesirable effects on the inclusive performance of the system. Babu et al. [18] developed a simulation of the electric truck under the highway fuel economy driving test (HWFET). Their findings indicate that low ambient temperature precedes an increase in the internal resistance of the battery pack, leading to elevated discharge currents to fulfil power requirements. Xu et al. [19] focused on the thermal management system for a Tesla Model S and 3 EV, analyzing factors like rolling resistance, the thermal management system, secondary load, aerodynamic drag, and regenerative braking and found that ambient temperature has a noteworthy nonlinear effect on the EV range, with a range drop of 3.3 %–13.4 % for Model 3 as the ambient temperature increased from 30 °C. Wang et al. [20] scrutinized the energy consumption of 68 EVs through conventional linear regression and multilevel linear regression techniques. Their findings indicate that the relation between energy efficiency and ambient temperature displays a non-linear ‘U’ shape, showing that the optimum energy efficiency is accomplished at an ambient temperature of 17.5 °C. Hao et al. [21] analyzed the actual driving data of 197 EVs to estimate the energy consumption and range across diverse driving conditions and seasonal variations. The findings suggested that in instances where the ambient temperature falls below 10 °C, there is a corresponding rise in energy consumption of 2.4 kWh/km for each 5 °C decrease in ambient temperature. When the ambient temperature exceeds 28 °C, the energy consumption rises by 2.3 kWh/km for each 5 °C increase in ambient temperature. Yu et al. [22]investigated the energy consumption under different ambient temperature conditions for 18 passenger cars and revealed that during the low ambient temperature (−4 °C to −10 °C) the energy consumption rate rose by 67.3 % compared to the energy consumption rate at standard ambient temperature (20 °C–26 °C). And during the high ambient temperature (27 °C–33 °C) the energy consumption rate increased by 19.82 °C compared to the energy consumption rate at normal ambient temperature. Lee et al. [23] evaluated the energy efficiency of the EV under various ambient temperatures and revealed that the optimal energy efficiency was attained when the ambient temperatures were between 20 °C and 30 °C. Also, the low ambient temperature (−15 °C) consumes 35.4 % more energy compared to energy consumption at 24 °C ambient temperature. Low ambient temperatures reduce battery efficiency, increase energy discharge, and affect the energy consumption of E2W. The battery pack's energy intensity experiences variations due to changes in ambient temperature, exhibiting optimal efficiency levels between 22 °C and 23 °C, with the decrease in efficiency observed at ambient temperatures above or below this range. Lindgren et al. [24] investigated the EV's performance under extreme ambient temperatures and revealed that high ambient temperature can lead to a rapid increase in battery deterioration and the process of self-discharge. As discussed, the analysis of the energy consumption rate of EVs is concentrated on a multitude of factors. Novelty and objective of the study

As evident from previous research, the ambient temperature plays a critical role in various aspects of an E2W, including energy consumption rate, the driving range, and battery performance. Hence, the consequence of ambient temperature on energy consumption rate is essential to enriching the efficiency of E2Ws. The driving cycles of the previous research studies were mostly based on their region, and the driving parameters of each driving cycle are different due to their variations in traffic and road conditions. However, only a few researchers considered the impact of ambient temperature on the battery parameters. Existing research studies on EV energy consumption rates often focus on four-wheeled vehicles or utilize simulated driving cycles such as WLTP, UDDS, NEDC, US06, etc., as shown in Table 1. Also, these research studies provide variable insights, but they might not fully capture the unique features of E2Ws and the impact of real-world driving conditions. To confront this issue, real-world driving data for E2W is collected using the MATLAB mobile application, which gathers sensor data including speed, acceleration, and GPS location. For this research, the ambient temperature of the E2W is based on the historical ambient temperature data in Vellore, Tamil Nadu, and it varies from 15 °C to 40 °C. This study aims to examine the energy consumption rate of E2W at various ambient temperatures using data derived from real-world driving data and to explore their consequences on the energy consumption rate. To achieve these research objectives, the core contribution of this study is summarized as follows:•This research employs real-world driving data (speed and acceleration data) collected from E2Ws under various driving conditions, ensuring a higher level of relevance to typical daily situations.•This research also considers the user behaviour, including driving speed and acceleration and deceleration patterns, on energy consumption rate and driving range in conjunction with ambient temperature, providing a comprehensive insight into the various influencing factors.•Elaboration on the consequences of real-world driving data and the ambient temperature on the performance of the E2W battery pack involve examining factors such as the cell temperature, the state of charge (SOC), the current drawn from the battery pack, and the state of health (SOH).

Results and discussion

To enhance the performance of an E2W, analyzing the consequence of ambient temperature on their energy consumption rate and driving range should be evaluated based on real-time driving data. In this division, the output performance of the battery pack, EM, energy consumption rate, and driving range are elaborated based on real-time driving data and different ambient temperatures.

Battery pack output performance

The parameters associated with the battery pack performance are their current, power, cell temperature, voltage, SOC, and SOH. The experimental and calculated battery parameters are assessed to comprehend the variations and constraints under different ambient temperatures, as addressed in the following subdivisions.

Battery pack voltage

In E2Ws, the battery pack voltage refers to the rated voltage of the battery pack. The voltage of the battery pack has an inverse relation with the level of energy losses within the system i.e. when the voltage is higher, the energy losses will be low due to the fundamental principles of electrical resistance and power dissipation. The ranges of battery pack voltage of an E2W are 52.85 V and 40.35 V at fully charged conditions (100 % SOC) and 80 % discharged conditions (20 % SOC). It is noted that based on the ambient temperature, the battery pack voltage drop percentage will vary. Fig. 6 represents the voltage discharge and charge curve of the battery pack with an ambient temperature ranging from 15 °C to 40 °C. Also, Fig. 6 shows the battery pack voltage for the one discharge cycle of the battery pack with a DOD of 80 %. The maximum battery pack voltage discharged from the battery pack is 52.8 V. The battery pack voltage reaches 40.35 V for SOC level at 20 % for an ambient temperature of 15 °C. In low ambient temperatures, such as 15 °C and 20 °C, the battery pack sustains a higher voltage rate for an extended duration during the discharge. A low ambient temperature slows down the chemical reactions inside the battery pack, leading to a slower voltage drop. In higher ambient temperatures, such as 25 °C, 30 °C, 35 °C, and 40 °C, the battery pack voltage drops rapidly compared to lower ambient temperature. Overall, Fig. 6 illustrates the significance of ambient temperature on the battery voltage discharge characteristics, with lower ambient temperature enabling higher battery life and higher ambient temperature leading to a faster voltage drop.Fig. 6The consequence of ambient temperature on the battery pack voltage.Fig. 6

Battery pack cell temperature

The temperature of the battery pack cell significantly affects the performance, lifetime, and safety of the E2W battery pack. The battery pack cell temperature influences the battery pack capacity, discharge rate, safety, and charging efficiency. The battery pack capacity drops with a rise in battery pack cell temperature. A higher battery pack cell temperature delivers less power, which reduces the driving range of E2W. Also, a higher battery pack cell temperature enables a higher discharge rate, which increases the stress on the battery pack. At extreme battery pack cell temperatures, the charging rate is less efficient, which leads to energy waste and a long charging time [27,28]. Fig. 9 represents the influence of ambient temperatures ranging from 15 °C to 40 °C, on the battery pack cell temperature. Also, it indicates that at a lower ambient temperature (15 °C), the battery pack displays spontaneously varying cell temperatures. As the ambient temperature rises to 20 °C, 25 °C, 30 °C, 35 °C, and 40 °C, the battery pack displays a stable, uniform temperature rise in the battery pack's cell. The highest battery pack cell temperature rise of 2.2 °C was noted at the ambient temperature of 15 °C. In higher ambient temperatures, such as 20 °C, 25 °C, 30 °C, 35 °C, and 40 °C, the battery pack's cell temperature rises by 1.7 °C. State of charge of the battery pack

The SOC of the battery pack is calculated based on the adaptive Kalman filter method. This approach is employed to approximate and calculate the residual distance covered by the E2Ws [29]. Fig. 10 signifies the impact of ambient temperatures ranging from 15 °C to 40 °C, on the SOC of the battery pack. Based on the analysis, at low ambient temperatures (15 °C) the SOC of the battery pack drains abruptly to 20 % at 11522 s, which is earlier than at other ambient temperatures (20 °C, 25 °C, 30 °C, 35 °C, and 40 °C). Also, at a higher ambient temperature (35 °C) the SOC of the battery pack drains deliberately to 20 % at 11661 s, which is far greater than the ambient temperature of 15 °C. Electric motor output performance

The EM transforms the electric energy from the battery pack into mechanical energy, to propel the E2W. The performance of the EM plays a significant role in the range, acceleration, and energy consumption of the E2Ws. The parameters related to the EM output performance are its speed, torque, and power. The EM output parameters are predominantly based on the driving cycle (acceleration and deceleration behaviour). The experimental and calculated EM parameters are assessed to comprehend the variations and constraints under different ambient temperatures, as discussed in the following sessions.

Electric motor torque

Electric motor torque is the fundamental concept of vehicles and directly translates to the rotational force that propels the E2W. The EM torque plays an essential role in the acceleration, hill climbing, and tow rating of the E2Ws [31]. The influence of different ambient temperatures on the EM torque is presented in Fig. 12. Positive EM torque is defined as when the acceleration pedal is pressed or, during acceleration; the EM operates in motoring mode to provide the rotational force to propel the E2W. The negative EM torque is represented when the brake pedal is pressed or, during deceleration, the EM operates in generator mode to provide the negative torque. In this analysis, the maximum positive torque required to propel the E2W is 8.45 N-m, and the maximum negative torque generated during braking is −5.5 N-m. The influence of ambient temperature on the EM torque is based on the battery pack voltage which directly influences the current passing through the EM windings. Based on analysis, the higher battery pack voltage translates to the peak torque of the EM. Summary and conclusion

In this study, we have analyzed the influence of ambient temperatures ranging from 15 °C to 40 °C, on vehicle, battery, and EM parameters of the electric two-wheeler. The real-world driving data is collected and converted to PWM signals using a PID and microcontroller package in MATLAB Simulink. Converted PWM signals are used to control the motor control unit with its feedback from sensor data. In this research, the E2W's real-world driving data is developed and its performance is analyzed using MATLAB 2023b. For validating the results of the simulation, a performance real-time target machine is used. The significant performance parameters of the battery pack, EM, and vehicle are calculated through statistical correspondence and experimental results.

The subsequent discoveries are laid down from the present study:1.This study reveals that at a low ambient temperature of 15 °C, the driving range of electric two-wheelers experienced a 2 % decrease as a result of diminished battery pack capacity compared to moderate ambient temperature and an energy consumption rate of 0.02359 kWh/km.2.During the high ambient temperature of 40 °C, an increase of 2 % in the driving range of electric two-wheelers was noted, attributed to the augmentation in battery pack capacity and energy consumption of 0.02371 kWh/km.3.The energy consumption rate is minimal in moderate ambient temperatures (20 °C, 25 °C, and 30 °C) compared to low (15 °C) and high (35 °C and 40 °C) ambient temperatures.4.This study reveals that during low and high ambient temperatures (15 °C and 40 °C), the cell temperature of the battery pack increased by 2.2 °C due to higher current consumption from the battery pack.5.The predicted driving range of an electric two-wheeler in the real-world driving data is 112.41 km, 113.61 km, 114.53 km, 114.71 km, and 114.68 km for ambient temperatures of 15 °C, 20 °C, 25 °C, 30 °C, 35 °C, and 40 °C.

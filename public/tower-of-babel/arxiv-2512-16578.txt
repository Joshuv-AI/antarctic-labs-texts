The splitting fields and Generators of Shioda’s elliptic surfaces y 2 = x 3 + t m + 1 y^{2}=x^{3}+t^{m}+1 (I)
Sajad Salami, Arman Shamsi Zargar

Abstract
. The splitting field of an elliptic surface ℰ \mathcal{E} defined over ℚ ⁡ ( t ) {\mathbb{Q}}(t) is the smallest subfield 𝒦 \mathcal{K} of ℂ \mathbb{C} such that ℰ ⁡ ( ℂ ⁡ ( t ) ) ≅ ℰ ⁡ ( 𝒦 ⁡ ( t ) ) {\mathcal{E}}({\mathbb{C}}(t))\cong{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{K}}(t)) . 2.1.
Elliptic surfaces and Mordell–Weil lattices

Let
k\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a number field,
{\mathcal{C}}
a smooth projective curve
defined over
k
,
K=k({\mathcal{C}})
and
{{\bar{K}}}={\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{C}})
the function fields of
{\mathcal{C}}
over
k
and
{\mathbb{C}}
, respectively.

Given an elliptic curve
{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\bar{K}}}
defined by
y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
with
a,b\in{{\bar{K}}}
and
discriminant
\Delta({\mathcal{E}})=-16(4a^{3}+27b)\in{{\bar{K}}}\backslash{\mathbb{C}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
the group of
{{\bar{K}}}
-rational points of
{\mathcal{E}}

with the identity
{\mathcal{O}}\in{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}}).

A generalization of the famous Mordell–Weil theorem over function fields, due to Lang–Néron, implies
that
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
is a finitely generated abelian group under a mild condition.
In other words, there is a finite group
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})_{tors}
, called the
torsion subgroup
of
{\mathcal{E}}
, and a non-negative integer

r={\text{rk}}({\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}}))
called the
Mordell–Weil rank
or simply the
rank
of
{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\bar{K}}}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})\cong{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})_{tors}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
.

Let
\pi:{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
be the
elliptic surface associated to

{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\bar{K}}}
, i.e. the Kodaira–Néron model of

{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\bar{K}}}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a smooth complex projective surface
and
\pi
is a relatively minimal fibration with the generic fiber
{\mathcal{E}}
.
We assume that
\pi
is not smooth, i.e, there is at least one singular fiber.
In this case, the set of
{{\bar{K}}}
-rational points
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
can be identified with
the global sections of
\pi:{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e, the set of all morphism
\sigma:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}

such that
\pi\circ\sigma=id
over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
For each
P\in{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
, we denote by
(P)
the image curve of the corresponding global section

\sigma_{P}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
. In particular, the trivial point
{\mathcal{O}}
corresponds to
({\mathcal{O}})
, the
image curve of the morphism
\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
of
\pi
, which is called the
zero section
.

Let
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
be the
Néron–Severi group
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
, i.e,
the group of divisors modulo algebraic equivalence.
By the non-smoothness assumption on
\pi
,
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
is an indefinite integral lattice called the
Néron–Severi lattice
,
with respect to the pairing defined by the intersection pairing
(D\cdot D^{\prime})
, where
D
and
D^{\prime}
are divisors on
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
.
The rank of Néron–Severi lattice is called the
Picard number
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
and denoted
by
{\it p}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
.
Let
T
be the
trivial sublattice
of
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
, which is generated by the zero section
({\mathcal{O}})
, a fiber
F
and
the irreducible components of all fibers of
\pi
not meeting the zero section. Then,

T=\left\langle({\mathcal{O}}),F\right\rangle\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\nu\in R}T_{\nu}\right),

where
R=\{\nu\in{\mathcal{C}}|F_{\nu}=\pi^{-1}(\nu)\ \text{is reducible}\}
, and
T_{\nu}
is generated by the irreducible components of
F_{\nu}

other than the identity component. We note that each
T_{\nu}
is a root lattice of type
A,D
and
E
up to a sign.
The orthogonal complement
L=T^{\perp}
of
T
in
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
is called the
essential sublattice
of
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}).

By
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
, the map
P\mapsto(P)\mbox{ mod }T
induces an isomorphism
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})\cong{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})/T
, and hence
a unique homomorphism
\varphi:{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})\rightarrow{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

\varphi(P)\equiv(P)\mbox{ mod }T\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
,
Im(\varphi)
is perpendicular to
T
, and

ker(\varphi)={\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})_{tors}
. By
[
5
, Theorem 8.4]
, the pairing map

\left\langle,\right\rangle:{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})\times{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
, which is given by

\left\langle P_{1},P_{2}\right\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\left(\psi(P_{1})\cdot\psi(P_{2})\right)
,
where
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
and
(\cdot)
denotes the intersection number of sections,
defines the structure of a positive definite lattice on
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})/{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})_{tors}
which is called the
Mordell–Weil lattice
of

{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\bar{K}}}
or of
\pi:{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
.

Given any finite covering
f:{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
of smooth projective curves of degree
m\geq 2
over
{\mathbb{C}}
and the elliptic surface
\pi:{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
the generic fiber of the base change
\pi^{\prime}:{\mathcal{S}}_{{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
and let
{{\bar{K}}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
, which is a finite extension of
{{\bar{K}}}
of degree
m\geq 2
. Then,
by
[
5
, Proposition 8.12]
, for any

P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
, we have

\left\langle Q_{1},Q_{2}\right\rangle^{\prime}=m\cdot\left\langle P_{1}\,,\,P_{2}\right\rangle,

where
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}({{\bar{K}}}^{\prime})
and

\left\langle\,,\,\right\rangle^{\prime}
is the height pairing on
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}({{\bar{K}}}^{\prime})
the set of global sections of
\pi^{\prime}.

The elliptic surface
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}

is called a
rational elliptic surface
if it is birational to
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, the projective space of dimension two.
In this case,
{\mathcal{C}}
is isomorphic to the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, hence
{{\bar{K}}}={\mathbb{C}}(t)
and the generic fiber
is an elliptic curve given by the Weierstrass equation

(2.1)

{\mathcal{E}}:y^{2}=x^{3}+a(t)x+b(t)

with
a(t),b(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}(t)
satisfying

\mbox{deg }a(t)\leq 4
,
\mbox{deg }b(t)\leq 6
and
\Delta({\mathcal{E}})
is non-constant.
The surface
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
is called an
elliptic
K3
surface
if
4<\mbox{deg }a(t)\leq 8
and
6<\mbox{deg }b(t)\leq 12
hold;
otherwise, it is called an
honestly elliptic surface
.
The
arithmetic genus

{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
is the smallest integer
n
such that
\mbox{deg }(a(t))\leq 4n
and
\mbox{deg }(b(t))\leq 6n
.
It is equal to
1
and
2
if
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a rational or a
K3
surface, respectively,
and
{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})\geq 2
for any honestly elliptic surface
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
.
For example, we have
{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})=60
for the elliptic surface associated to the Shioda’s curve
1.1
with
m=360
, since

\mbox{deg }(t^{360}+1)=6\cdot 60
.

Given
P,P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
, the explicit formulas for height pairing
\left\langle,\right\rangle
are given as follows:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\left\langle P_{1},P_{2}\right\rangle=\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})+(P_{1}\cdot O)+(P_{2}\cdot O)-(P_{1}\cdot P_{2})-\sum_{\nu\in R}\text{contr}_{\nu}(P_{1},P_{2}),\\
\left\langle P,P\right\rangle=\displaystyle 2{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})+2(P\cdot O)-\sum_{\nu\in R}\text{contr}_{\nu}(P),\end{cases}

where
(P_{1}\cdot P_{2})=((P_{1}),(P_{2}))
denotes the intersection
number of the sections
(P_{1})
and
(P_{2}),
see
[
5
, Theorem 8.6]
for the proof.
The
local contribution terms

\text{contr}_{\nu}(P_{1},P_{2})
and

\text{contr}_{\nu}(P)
are non-negative rational numbers which depend on the reducible fibers of
\pi
over
\nu

and can be computed according to Table 8.9 in
[
5
]
.
In particular, both of them are equal to zero when there is no reducible fibers.

The subgroup
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})^{\circ}
of
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
, consisting of those sections meeting the identity component of every fiber of
\pi
,
is a torsion-free subgroup of
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
of finite index and becomes a positive definite even lattice.
It is called the
narrow Mordell–Weil lattice
of
{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\bar{K}}}
, which is isomorphic to the opposite lattice
L^{-}
of the orthogonal complement of
T
in
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
. Given
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})^{\circ}
, one has the following formulas for the height paring:

(2.3)

\begin{cases}\left\langle P_{1},P_{2}\right\rangle={\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})+(P_{1}\cdot O)+(P_{2}\cdot O)-(P_{1}\cdot P_{2}),\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\left\langle P,P\right\rangle=2{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})+2(P\cdot O)\geq 2{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}).\end{cases}

The quotient
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})/{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})_{tors}
is contained in the dual lattice
M^{*}
of
M={\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})^{\circ}
, and the equality holds
if
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
is an unimodular lattice. In this case,

[M^{*}:M]=\mbox{det }M=(\mbox{det }T)/|{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})_{tors}|^{2}
. We cite the reader to
[
5
, Theorem 9.1]
for more details.

The
Shioda–Tate
formula,
given by
[
5
, Crollary 5.3]
,
provides a relation between the rank of
{\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}})
and the Picard number
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}}
as follows:

(2.4)

{\text{rk}}({\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}}))={\it p}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})-2-\sum_{\nu\in R}(m_{\nu}-1),

where
m_{\nu}
is the number of irreducible components of the reducible fibers of

\pi
over
\nu
.
For the rational elliptic surfaces, since
{\rm NS}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})
is unimodular of rank

{\it p}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{E}})=10
,
{\text{rk}}({\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}}))
is at most
8
by the Shioda–Tate formula

.
In the case of elliptic

surfaces, the rank
{\text{rk}}({\mathcal{E}}({{\bar{K}}}))
can be at most
18
.

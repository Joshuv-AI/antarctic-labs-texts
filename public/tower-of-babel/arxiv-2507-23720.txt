Nonlinear Vibrational Mode of Molecule with Octahedral Configuration
Jingzhou Liu

Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the nonlinear dynamics of molecules with an octahedral configuration, with particular focus on sulfur hexafluoride S ​ F 6 . SF_{6}. Under the assumption of isotypic nonresonance, we apply the method of equivariant gradient degree to prove the existence of branches of periodic solutions emerging from the critical orbit of equilibrium, corresponding to at least 16 distinct types of symmetries with maximal orbit kinds. Numerical animations are presented to illustrate the detected vibrational modes.

Introduction

Understanding the dynamic behavior of molecular systems provides detailed insights into their intrinsic properties. To this end, various mathematical models have been developed for different molecules, many of which are grounded in classical mechanics.

Many of these have been calibrated to replicate the vibrational modes observed in experimental techniques such as infrared and Raman spectroscopy, which are adept at detecting linear vibrational modes. However, to fully capture the complexity of molecular dynamics, alternative mathematical tools are needed, especially for analyzing nonlinear vibrational phenomena that lie beyond the reach of current experimental methods.

Given a compact Lie group
G
, a
G
-invariant map
\varphi_{\lambda}
and a neighborhood
\mathscr{U}
of
G
-orbit of equilibrium
v^{0}

G
-equivariant gradient degree

\nabla_{G}\text{{-deg}}\Big(\nabla\varphi,\mathscr{U}\Big)
is a well-defined element of the Euler ring
U(G)=\mathbb{Z}[\Phi(G)].
Here,

\Phi(G)
denotes the set of conjugacy classes
(H)
of closed subgroups
H\leq G

\mathbb{Z}[\Phi(G)]
is the free
\mathbb{Z}

\nabla_{G}\text{{-deg}}\Big(\nabla\varphi,\mathscr{U}\Big)=n_{1}(H_{1})+n_{2}(H_{2})+\cdots n_{k}(H_{k}),\;\;n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}

where
(H_{j})
represents an orbit type in
\mathscr{U}.
The corresponding equivariant topological invariant at a critical value
\lambda_{o}
is defined by

\omega_{G}(\lambda_{o}):=\nabla_{G}\text{{-deg}}\Big(\nabla\varphi_{\lambda_{-}},\mathscr{U}\Big)-\nabla_{G}\text{{-deg}}\Big(\nabla\varphi_{\lambda_{+}},\mathscr{U}\Big),

and takes the form

\omega_{G}(\lambda_{o})=r_{1}(K_{1})+r_{2}(K_{2})+\cdots+r_{m}(K_{m}),\;\;\;r_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}

This invariant provides full classifications of the periodic solutions bifurcating from the equilibrium when
\lambda
crosses
\lambda_{o}.
For each nonzero coefficient

a global family of periodic vibrational modes emerges, with symmetry of at least

. It is worthy to note that our method provides alternative to other tools such as equivariant singularity, bush theory with density functional theory (

2.
Mathematical framework

2.1.
Force field.

Let
V=\mathbb{R}^{18}
and consider 6 identical ligand atoms
v:=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},v_{6})^{T}\in V
and

\Omega_{o}:=\{v\in V:v_{k}\not=v_{j}\text{ for }k\not=j\}.

The overall interactions of ligand-ligand, ligand-central atoms are represented by Newtonian equation:

\ddot{v}=-\nabla U(v),\;\;v\in\Omega_{o}

where potential
U:\Omega_{o}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

U(v):=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq 6}U_{1}(|v_{j}-v_{k}|^{2})+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq 6}U_{2}(|v_{j}-\mathcal{O}|^{2}),

and
U_{1},U_{2}
take the form in equation (
2
).
Notice that since both
U_{1}
,
U_{2}
belong to
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
satisfy

(3)

\lim_{s^{+}\rightarrow 0}U_{1}(s)+U_{2}(s)=\infty,\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}U_{1}(s)+U_{2}(s)=\infty.

The combined potential
U
diverges both as particles approach each other and as they move infinitely
far apart. This physically prevents particle collapse or dispersion, and ensures that
U
is well-defined. Moreover,
U
is also of class
C^{2}
on the domain where particle distances remain
positive.

2.2.
Octahedral Symmetries.

The space
V
is a representation of the group

G^{\prime}:=S_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times O(3),

which describes the full spatial symmetry of the octahedral molecule.
Note that
S_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
can be identified with a subgroup
\mathbf{O}\leq S_{6}
through the following correspondence between its elements and those of
S_{6}

(4)

\displaystyle((24),1)

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow(14)(23)(56),\;\;((12)(34),1)\leftrightarrow(13)(56),\;\;\;((1),-1)\leftrightarrow(13)(24)(56),

\displaystyle((132),1)

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow(146)(253),\;\quad((1234),1)\leftrightarrow(1234).\;

The action of

takes the form

(5)

(\sigma,M)(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},v_{6})^{T}=(Mv_{\sigma(1)},Mv_{\sigma(2)},Mv_{\sigma(3)},Mv_{\sigma(4)},Mv_{\sigma(5)},Mv_{\sigma(6)})^{T},

where
\sigma\in S_{6},M\in O(3).

We now regard
S_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
as a subgroup of

Notice that it naturally corresponds to the full symmetry of a regular octahedron

Indeed, let the six vertices of

be given by

p_{1}=-p_{3}=(1,0,0)^{T},\quad p_{2}=-p_{4}=(0,1,0)^{T},\quad p_{5}=-p_{6}=(0,0,1)^{T}

To be more precise, the generators of

2.3.
Octahedral Equilibrium.

Notice that potential
U
is
G^{\prime}
invariant. The following is based on the application of Palais’s Principle of Symmetric Criticality to locate critical point. For notational convenience, we hereinafter denote
S_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Consider point
p^{o}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},p_{5},p_{6})^{T}\in\Omega_{o}.
Its isotropy group
G^{\prime}_{p^{o}}
is given by

\tilde{S}_{4}^{p}:=\left\{(\sigma,M_{\sigma})\in S_{4}^{p}\times O(3):\sigma\in S_{4}^{p}\right\},

where

is regarded as a subgroup of
S_{6}
embedded in

So any vector

must take the form
v=rp^{o},\;r\in\mathbb{R}.
Now, define

\varphi(r):=U(rp^{o})=12U_{1}(\sqrt{2}r)+3U_{1}(2r)+6U_{2}(r),\quad(r>0)

Since

is the restriction of

and since

satisfies (

such that

is a minimizer. By Symmetric Criticality Principle, this minimizer implies that

Definition 2

(cf.

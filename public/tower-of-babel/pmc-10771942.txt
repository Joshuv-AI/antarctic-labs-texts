Molecular Typing on Human Blood Reveals the Borrelia afzelii Infection in Korea
Authors: Yeon-Sook Kim, Jeoungyeon Kim, Yeon-Joo Choi, Taeuk Kang, Hye-Jin Park, Won-Jong Jang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Lyme disease is a tick-borne infection in Korea. Here, clinical samples were collected from a 72-year old patient, with sudden onset of fever on April, 2018. The patient was passed away after 3rd day of doxycycline administration. The molecular diagnostic tests, nested polymerase chain reaction targeting 5S-23S rRNA intergenic spacer region (IGS) and multilocus sequence typing (MLST), showed positive for Borrelia afzelii from blood. Further, mutations in both 5S - 23S IGS and pepX allele of MLST were determined. Herein, we report the expected first death case by B. afzelii infection in Korea.

Lyme disease is a multisystemic infection with broad spectrum of clinical presentation caused by tick-borne spirochete called Borrelia spp., which is one of the most common tick-borne diseases in United States of America, Europe and Asia. Among different species of Borrelia spp. in the Lyme disease agent bacterial group, B. afzelii, B. garinii, and B. bavariensis species nova (sp. nov.; new species) are common causes of the Lyme diseases in Europe and Asia [1]. Along with the increasing trend of the Lyme diseases due to climate change and increased outdoor activities, there is a strong demand for higher understanding on genetic variability and its following etiology [23].

Along with the advancement of molecular diagnostic technologies, the diagnosis of Borrelia spp. can be performed on molecular typing such as polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and multilocus sequence typing (MLST). The use of MLST has revolutionized the fundamental approaches in tick-borne pathogens diagnostics [45]. The principle behinds the MLST relies on the sequencing analysis of the housekeeping genes of pathogen and label it with sequence type (ST) according to the sequence data [6]. Previously, there was an attempt to conduct MLST analysis on B. burgdorferi sensu stricto by targeting 8 housekeeping genes, namely clpA, clpX, pepX, pyrG, nifS, recG, rplB, and uvrA. The result showed that use of these 8 housekeeping genes for molecular typing is highly suitable for B. burgdorferi sensu lato species complex as well as other Borrelia spp. So far, clinical samples such as cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), blood, and skin, in case of erythema migraines and acrodermatis chronica atrophicans, are used for detection of Borrelia spp. Also, the diagnostic power for Borrelia spp. is highly dependent on different factors. These include the phase of Borrelia spp. infection, the condition and source of the specimens, the medical status of patients (e.g., age, sex, and underlying diseases), and the genospecies of Borrelia [78910].

Lyme borreliosis has been associated with a high degree of genetic variation in Borrelia, depending on the specific clinical presentation. For instance, the arthritis and myocarditis have been associated with B. burgdorferi sensu stricto, skin disorder with B. afzelii, and neuroborreliosis with B. garinii [11]. However, it is unreasonable to assume any exceptions such as heterogeneity of subspecies involving a single species [12]. According to Coipan et al., the 5S - 23S rRNA intergenic spacer region (IGS) region is an appropriate genetic marker to prevent bias due to clinical features [13].

Here, we present our data collected in 2018 as a keystone for clear understanding on the landscape of tick-borne diseases in late 2010s. Despite years passed till now, neither B. afzelii with novel ST was detected nor death case by B. afzelii was reported in Korea. In this study, we have analyzed the genetic variation of patient who was admitted to hospital with sudden onset of fever in April, 2018. In addition to IGS typing, MLST analysis was performed to investigate the ST of eight housekeeping genes.

In 2018, a 72-year-old man visited the Chungnam National University Hospital, Korea after a tick bite at the deer farm. Ten days prior to admission, the patient complained for headache and slight fever. No skin lesion developed at the site of the tick bite. The clinical symptoms were fever, headache, and anemia. Intriguingly, inflammation parameters including C-reactive protein (CRP) and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) levels were 23 mg/dL and 24 mm/h, respectively. The reference range for normal CRP and ESR levels are 0 - 6 mg/L and 0 - 20 mm/h, respectively.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the patient’s brain showed multifocal infarction and ill-defined enhancement with specific nodular enhancement and hepatosplenomegaly. The results of serological and inflammation tests showed no evidence of any abnormalities. The patient was treated with ciprofloxacin 400 mg every 12 h for 8 days and, subsequently, doxycycline (DOX) 100 mg twice daily for 3 days. The patient passed away on the third day of administration with DOX.

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) was extracted from whole EDTA blood and CSF using a Blood DNA Extraction Kit (QIAGEN, Germantown, MD, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Using a small amount of blood, we conducted nested PCR (nPCR) to identify 5S-23S IGS and MLST typing. The nPCR and MLST were carried out based on previous works. The name of primers used in this study were 23SN1 (5'-acc ata gac tct tat tac ttt gac-3'), 23SC1 (5'-taa gct gac taa tac taa tta ccc-3'), 23SN2 (5'-acc ata gac tct tat tac ttt gac ca-3'), and 5SCB (5'-gag agt agg tta ttg cca ggg-3') [614]. The 5S-23S IGS sequence was compared via BLAST search and the MLST result was compared with other MLST results from the PubMLST.

The IGS sequence and MLST result were submitted to GenBank and pubMLST. The accession number and PubMLST ID are KU891496 and 2484 respectively.

Sequencing analysis was conducted by Bioneer Co. (Seong-nam, Gyeonggi-do, Korea). The analyzed sequences were aligned and compared with the gene sequences of various strains of Borrelia spp. deposited in the GenBank database using MEGA v11.0 (Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ, USA) and Chromas Lite v2.01 software (Technelysium Pty Ltd, South Brisbane, Queensland, Australia). To construct the phylogenetic tree, the maximum likelihood method based on the Jukes-Cantor model of nucleotide substitution was determined with 1,000 replications.

The IGS used in this study contained three altered nucleotide sequences when compared with the most matching nucleotide sequence, B. afzelii A98 (GenBank no. KU891496) from Portugal. Hence, we submitted and assigned the GenBank accession number MH763824 (Fig. 1). Due to molecular distinctive feature, the MLST was carried out.

In MLST, among the eight loci, the profiles of five loci (clpX - nifS - pepX - recG - uvrA) profiles were 202 - 185 - 224 - 231 - 215, indicating B. afzelii. The nPCR of the three loci (clpA, pyrG, and rplB) failed to reveal a new ST. Interestingly, eight SNPs were found in pepX, among the five loci. A new pepX allele sequence showed a new allele number of 224 (PubMLST ID no.2484) in MLST (Table 1). To our knowledge, no same records of SNPs were uploaded on PubMLST. Further this result presents the first death case by novel B. afzelii infection in Korea. Previous research showed numbers of B. afzelii infection cases in different parts of the World [151617]. Among them, there was also a case which death was presumed to be due to B. afzelii infection [17].

According to the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA), the number of reported cases of patient with the Lyme disease has steadily increased from 3 to 22 from 2012 to 2022. The Lyme disease is diagnosed according to the designated diagnosis manual published by KDCA [18]. However, only handful of reports are published for the molecular features of Borrelia spp. infection in Korea. Here, we have reported the first death case from infection by B. afzelii with novel ST. Previously, first molecular detection of B. afzelii from patients was reported however, clinical information, including symptoms, dose regimen, basic inflammatory parameters and more, was not sufficiently provided according to the study done by Choi et al. [14]. While this research presented detailed narrative from patient’s visit to the hospital to death by B. afzelii infection.

Despite the significance the study has, this study has some limitations. First, no detection of specific antibodies was performed using IFA or western blots due to insufficient amounts of blood and CSF. Second, only the identification of B. afzelii in the blood sample confirmed for the Lyme disease as a suggestive cause of death of the patient as no further examination was carried out. At the same time, it is notable that this is a report of molecular typing of 5S-23S IGS and five loci of Borrelia from the blood sample and a first death case from the Lyme disease by B. afzelii in Korea. Despite such limitation, this study presented the detected B. afzelii from the blood sample is genetically distinct to that of previously reported ones. Several cases of death due to the Lyme disease by B. burgdorferi were reported however, the Lyme disease death cases by B. afzelii is relatively not high-lightened. In this context, this indicates the need for further investigation on the correlation between genetical variability and pathogenicity as well as its patho-epidemiology of B. afzelii.

To confirm the 5S - 23S IGS sequence results, we also analyzed ospA gene. However, the nucleotide sequence results showed 97.9% similarity with B. afzelii (allele 11) (data not shown). IGS is a genetic marker used to divide the subpopulations of B. afzelii and B. garinii and is appropriate for genetic analysis of Borrelia genospecies with MLST [13].

In addition, although Borrelia spp. was not detected by CSF, the MRI showed multiple infarctions, suggesting Lyme neuroborreliosis (LNB) [19]. Our study was not able to determine whether the LNB was the direct cause of patient’s death. However, our study confirmed that only B. afzelii could be one of the causes.

Monotonicity of depth constants in general preferential attachment trees
Christian Mönch

Abstract
We study insertion depth and tree height in preferential-attachment (PA) trees driven by a general attachment function f : ℕ 0 → ( 0 , ∞ ) f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to(0,\infty) . Our main result is a monotonicity principle for the log ⁡ n \log n -order constant of either functional: if f f dominates g g in “growth-ratio” order (a variant of the hazard ratio order of probability densities) then the typical depth constant for f f is no larger than for g g , i.e. stronger reinforcement yields a shallower tree. The proofs use a one-parameter interpolation together with a gauge normalisation that removes a global derivative term, reducing the sign to a weighted correlation inequality for monotone sequences. Equivalently, the results can be phrased as monotonicity theorems for the corresponding branching process functionals arising from the standard embedding of the PA tree into a Crump–Mode–Jagers process.

Introduction and main result

General preferential attachment trees

Fix an
attachment function

f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. We define a random rooted tree
T_{n}(f)
on vertex set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
recursively as follows.

•

Initialisation:

T_{1}(f)
consists of a single vertex
1
, the
root
.

•

Recursion:
Given
T_{n-1}(f)
, obtain
T_{n}(f)
by adding vertex
n
and connecting it by a single edge to a vertex

chosen conditionally on
T_{n-1}(f)
with probability

{\mathbb{P}}(n\to i\mid T_{n-1}(f))\;=\;\frac{f(\deg_{T_{n-1}(f)}(i))}{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}f(\deg_{T_{n-1}(f)}(j))}.

Here
\deg_{T_{n-1}(f)}(i)
denotes the number of children of

in the rooted tree

(i.e. the out-degree in the orientation away from the root). In particular, every new vertex is born with
0

governs the

of a newborn vertex. (If
\deg_{T}^{\mathrm{gr}}(i)
denotes the usual undirected graph degree, then
\deg_{T}^{\mathrm{gr}}(i)=\deg_{T}(i)+{\mathbf{1}}_{\{i\neq 1\}}

This model interpolates between
uniform attachment
(

) and

(

[
26
]

Preferential attachment trees (and, more generally, preferential attachment networks) are fundamental objects in random graph theory and have been studied since early work of Szymański
[
27
]

We study two logarithmic-order functionals: the

D_{n}(f):=0pt_{T_{n}(f)}(n),\qquad H_{n}(f):=\max_{v\in[n]}0pt_{T_{n}(f)}(v).

Both insertion depth and height of random recursive trees are classical objects of study; see e.g. the survey by Drmota
[
16
]
for an overview. In particular, it is well known that for uniform attachment (
f\equiv 1

H_{n}(f)

with explicit constants
[
13
,
14

under mild regularity conditions follows from the Crump–Mode–Jagers embedding
[

,
17
,
3

In preferential attachment models which allow cycles, related functionals are the hop-count (corresponding to insertion depth) and the diameter (corresponding to height), which have been invesitgated for a variety of attachment regimes of both affine and general attachment functions

Order of attachment functions and monotonicity of depth.

Throughout we consider
nondecreasing
attachment functions
f,g:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
.
We say that
f

dominates

g
in
growth-ratio order
, and write

f\succeq_{\mathrm{GR}}g
, if

\frac{f(k+1)}{f(k)}\;\geq\;\frac{g(k+1)}{g(k)}\qquad\text{for all }k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Equivalently, the ratio
h(k):=f(k)/g(k)
is nondecreasing in
k
.

Intuitively, stronger reinforcement towards high-degree vertices should produce a shallower tree.
We propose growth ratio dominance (GRD) as a natural partial order under which this intuition can be made rigorous. Set

{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[D_{n}]:={\mathbb{E}}\big[D_{n}(f)\big],\qquad{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[H_{n}]:={\mathbb{E}}\big[H_{n}(f)\big].

Conjecture 1.1
(GRD monotonicity).

Let
f,g:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
be nondecreasing and assume
f\succeq_{\mathrm{GR}}g
.
Then for all
n\geq 1
,

{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[D_{n}]\;\leq\;{\mathbb{E}}_{g}[D_{n}],\text{ and }{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[H_{n}]\;\leq\;{\mathbb{E}}_{g}[H_{n}].

Here, we prove a asymptotic forms of Conjecture
1.1
.
Under natural analytic regularity conditions collected below the limits

\mathsf{c}_{f}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[D_{n}]}{\log n},\quad\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[H_{n}]}{\log n}

exists and admit representations as parameters of the Crump–Mode–Jagers branching process embedding of
T_{n}(f)
; see Section
2
for a brief exposition. Note that usually, the height limit can be extended to probabilistic convergence (in probability or almost surely) of the random variables
H_{n}(f)/\log{n}
themselves.

For clarity we now collect all regularity hypotheses we need in our main results in two families:

•

Assumptions (A1)–(A7) are conditions on a single attachment function
f
.

•

Assumptions (B1)–(B3) are conditions on the multiplicative interpolation
f_{\theta}
between
g
and
f
ensuring differentiability and dominated-convergence interchanges.

Assumptions A
(Attachment-function conditions).

For an attachment function
f:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
let
m_{f}
be the Laplace transform of the associated pure-birth-process, cf. (
2.1
)–(
2.2
). Whenever the Euler–Lotka equation

has a unique solution we denote it by
\lambda_{f}
, and we set
Q_{f}:=-\lambda_{f}m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})
when finite.

(A1)

(Monotonicity)
f
is nondecreasing and strictly positive on
{\mathbb{N}}
.

(A2)

(Non-explosion)
\sum_{k\geq 1}1/f(k)=\infty
. Equivalently, the pure-birth process with jump rates
f(k)
does not explode.

(A3)

(Malthusian regime) There exists
\lambda>0
with
m_{f}(\lambda)<\infty
, and then there is a unique
\lambda_{f}>0
such that
m_{f}(\lambda_{f})=1
.

(A4)

(Finite derivative)
0<-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})<\infty
.

(A5)

(Depth LLN) The CMJ embedding associated with
f
satisfies the Jagers–Nerman hypotheses
[
24
,
20
]
(in particular an
x\log x
-type integrability) yielding the logarithmic law of large numbers for insertion depth; equivalently, the limit
\mathsf{c}_{f}
exists and equals

.

(A6)

(Height LLN/BRW speed) The CMJ/BRW embedding associated with
f
satisfies the Kingman
[
22
]
hypotheses (in particular finite exponential moments) ensuring a deterministic extremal speed
\kappa_{f}
and the variational representation (
2.8
) with an interior maximiser
\lambda^{\ast}_{f}
. Under these hypotheses

\frac{H_{n}(f)}{\log n}\to\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{f}\kappa_{f}}\text{ in probability.}

(A7)

(Sublinear regular variation)
The attachment function
f
is regularly varying of index
\rho\in[0,1)
.

We also need the following analytic regularity conditions along interpolations between attachment functions
f
and
g
.

Assumptions B
(Interpolation conditions).

Let
f,g:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
be nondecreasing with
f\succeq_{\mathrm{GR}}g
, set
h:=f/g
, and define the interpolation
f_{\theta}:=gh^{\theta}
for
\theta\in[0,1]
.

(B1)

(Uniform Malthusian gap) Along the interpolation the Euler–Lotka equation
m_{f_{\theta}}(\lambda_{\theta})=1
has a unique solution for each
\theta\in[0,1]
, and
\inf_{\theta\in[0,1]}\lambda_{\theta}\geq\lambda_{-}>0
.

(B2)

(Uniform dominated envelope) For each
\theta
and
\lambda>0
, set

A_{n}^{(\theta)}(\lambda):=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{f_{\theta}(i)}{f_{\theta}(i)+\lambda},\qquad r_{i}^{(\theta)}(\lambda):=\sum_{n\geq i+1}A_{n}^{(\theta)}(\lambda).

The following bounds hold for
\lambda_{-}
as in (B1)

\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{(1+|\log h(i)|)\,r_{i}^{(\theta)}(\lambda_{-})}{f_{\theta}(i)+\lambda_{-}}<\infty,\qquad\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{r_{i}^{(\theta)}(\lambda_{-})}{(f_{\theta}(i)+\lambda_{-})^{2}}<\infty.

(1.1)

(B3)

(Height optimiser regularity) Along the interpolation the interior maximiser
\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
in Lemma
2.5
below may be chosen measurably in
s
and (B2) holds in a neighbourhood of
\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
.

Our first theorem shows that
\mathsf{c}_{f}
is monotone under GRD.

Theorem 1.2
(Asymptotic GRD monotonicity).

Let
f,g:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
be nondecreasing with
f\succeq_{\mathrm{GR}}g
.
Assume that
f
and
g
satisfy Assumption
Assumptions A
(A1)–(A5) and that the multiplicative interpolation between
g
and
f
satisfies Assumption
Assumptions B
(B1)–(B2).
Then the limits
\mathsf{c}_{f},\mathsf{c}_{g}
exist and

\mathsf{c}_{f}\leq\mathsf{c}_{g}.

Our second theorem records a height analogue of Theorem
1.2
under slightly stronger assumtions.

Theorem 1.3
(Height monotonicity under GRD in the sublinear regime).

Let
f,g:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
be nondecreasing with
f\succeq_{\mathrm{GR}}g
, and write
f_{s}:=g\,(f/g)^{s}
for the GRD interpolation,
s\in[0,1]
.
Assume that along
(f_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
the functions satisfy Assumption
Assumptions A
(A1)–(A4) and (A6)–(A7).
Assume moreover that the interpolation satisfies Assumption
Assumptions B
(B1)–(B3).
Then the height constant

\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_{n}(f)}{\log n}

exists in probability, and it is monotone under GRD:

\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f}\leq\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{g}.

Equivalently, writing

\kappa_{f}:=\sup_{\lambda>\lambda_{f}}\frac{-\log m_{f}(\lambda)}{\lambda},

the product
\lambda_{f}\kappa_{f}
is nondecreasing under GRD.

Remark 1.4
(Height constant in the affine case).

The ‘base case’ of affine preferantial attachment functions

f_{\delta}(k)=k+\delta,\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\qquad\delta>0.

is contained in Theorem
1.2
, but is
not
covered in Theorem
1.3
, due to condition (A7) of Assumption
Assumptions A
. However, in this case, the height constant was explicitly determined by Pittel
[
25
]
, cf. Drmota’s survey
[
17
]
, we recall the result here.

For every
\lambda>1
, set

\displaystyle A^{(\delta)}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{f_{\delta}(j)}{f_{\delta}(j)+\lambda}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{j+\delta}{j+\delta+\lambda}=\frac{\Gamma(n+\delta)\,\Gamma(\delta+\lambda)}{\Gamma(\delta)\,\Gamma(n+\delta+\lambda)},

(1.2)

\displaystyle m_{\delta}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 1}A^{(\delta)}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{\delta}{\lambda-1}.

(1.3)

The corresponding Malthusian parameter is

\lambda_{\delta}=\delta+1.

Moreover the BRW/height variational problem

\kappa_{\delta}=\sup_{\lambda>\lambda_{\delta}}\frac{-\log m_{\delta}(\lambda)}{\lambda}=\sup_{\lambda>\delta+1}\frac{\log\big(\frac{\lambda-1}{\delta}\big)}{\lambda}

has a unique interior maximiser
\lambda^{\ast}_{\delta}\in(\delta+1,\infty)
given by

\lambda^{\ast}_{\delta}=1+\frac{1}{W\big(\frac{1}{\delta e}\big)},\qquad\kappa_{\delta}=W\big(\tfrac{1}{\delta e}\big),

where
W
denotes the principal branch of the Lambert
W
–function.
In particular,

\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f_{\delta}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}\,\kappa_{\delta}}=\frac{1}{(\delta+1)\,W\big(\frac{1}{\delta e}\big)}.

Clearly,
\delta\mapsto\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f_{\delta}}
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.

To give the reader a better intuition for the regularity conditions, we now discuss some examples and non-examples.

Remark 1.5
(Examples for the regularity conditions).

The assumptions in Lemma
2.3
are standard in CMJ theory.
They are satisfied in essentially all “classical” preferential attachment regimes, but exclude certain
pathological or near-critical choices of
f
.

(i)

Bounded or sublinear attachment.

If
f(k)\asymp k^{\alpha}
with
0\leq\alpha<1
, then

\sum_{i<n}1/f(i)\asymp n^{1-\alpha}
, so the factors

A_{n}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{f(i)}{f(i)+\lambda}
decay super-exponentially in
n
.
In particular,
m_{f}(\lambda)<\infty
for all
\lambda>0
, and the derivative condition

0<-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})<\infty
holds automatically.

(ii)

Linear attachment (the classical PA regime).

If
f(k)\sim ck
(or
f(k)=k+\delta
), then
\sum_{i<n}1/f(i)\sim(1/c)\log n
, so

A_{n}(\lambda)\approx n^{-\lambda/c}
.
Hence
m_{f}(\lambda)<\infty
iff
\lambda/c>1
, and for the unique solution
\lambda_{f}
of
m_{f}(\lambda_{f})=1
we have
\lambda_{f}/c>1
,
which implies
\sum_{n}(\log n)\,A_{n}(\lambda_{f})<\infty
and thus
0<-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})<\infty
.

(iii)

Too-fast growth.

If
f(k)\gtrsim k\log k
, then
\sum_{i<n}1/f(i)\lesssim\log\log n
, so
A_{n}(\lambda)
decays only like a power of
\log n
,
and in particular
m_{f}(\lambda)=\infty
for all
\lambda>0
in many such cases. For even faster growth one may also violate non-explosion.

(iv)

Borderline near-critical cases.

It is possible to have
m_{f}(\lambda_{f})=1
but
-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})=\infty
when
A_{n}(\lambda_{f})
is barely summable,
e.g.
A_{n}(\lambda_{f})\asymp\frac{1}{n(\log n)^{2}}
, for which
\sum_{n}A_{n}(\lambda_{f})<\infty
but

\sum_{n}(\log n)\,A_{n}(\lambda_{f})=\infty
.
Such behaviour can be engineered by attachment functions that are asymptotically linear but tuned to be close to the critical boundary.

2
CMJ embedding and parameter identification

This section records the standard continuous-time embedding of
T_{n}(f)
into a supercritical
Crump–Mode–Jagers (CMJ) branching process, and the corresponding identification of the
logarithmic depth/height constants with CMJ/BRW parameters. All results stated here are
classical; for preferential-attachment trees in this generality see e.g. Rudas et al.
[
26
]
and the manuscript by Bhamidi
[
3
]
,
and for CMJ background see the classical works by Jagers and Nerman
[
19
,
24
,
20
]
and the more recent survey by Janson and Holmgren
[
18
]
.

Continuous-time embedding

Fix an attachment function
f:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
. Consider the continuous-time rooted tree process

(\Upsilon_{f}(t))_{t\geq 0}
started from a single root at time
0
, in which (conditionally on

\Upsilon_{f}(t)
) each vertex
x
gives birth to a new child at instantaneous rate

f(\deg_{\Upsilon_{f}(t)}(x))
, independently across vertices. Let

\sigma_{n}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\ |\Upsilon_{f}(t)|=n\},\qquad n\geq 1,

be the
n
th birth time. This is the standard “competing exponentials” argument for continuous-time branching constructions
[
26
,
18
]
.

Lemma 2.1
(Embedding).

The discrete-time process
(\Upsilon_{f}(\sigma_{n}))_{n\geq 1}
has the same law as the preferential attachment
tree process
(T_{n}(f))_{n\geq 1}
.

Laplace transform and CMJ parameters

Fix
f:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
and let
(X_{f}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the pure-birth process on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
started from

X_{f}(0)=0
, with jump rates
k\to k+1
at rate
f(k)
. Write
\tau_{n}
for the time of the
n
th jump and
define the reproduction point process

\xi_{f}\;:=\;\sum_{n\geq 1}\delta_{\tau_{n}}.

In the CMJ tree
(\Upsilon_{f}(t))_{t\geq 0}
each vertex reproduces according to an independent copy of
\xi_{f}
,
with birth times measured relative to its own birth time. Thus
(\Upsilon_{f}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a one-type CMJ
process with reproduction process
\xi_{f}
.

For
\lambda>0
define the Laplace transform

m_{f}(\lambda):={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda t}\,\xi_{f}(dt)\Big]={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-\lambda\tau_{n}}\Big].

(2.1)

Since
\tau_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}E_{i}
with independent
E_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(f(i))
, one obtains the series form

m_{f}(\lambda)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\ \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{f(i)}{f(i)+\lambda}.

(2.2)

Where finite,
\lambda\mapsto m_{f}(\lambda)
is strictly decreasing. In the supercritical Malthusian regime
(standard CMJ hypotheses) there is a unique
\lambda_{f}>0
solving the Euler–Lotka equation

m_{f}(\lambda_{f})=1,

called the
Malthusian parameter

[
19
]
.

Assuming differentiability at
\lambda_{f}
and finiteness of the derivative, set

\mu_{f}:=-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})\in(0,\infty),\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q_{f}:=\lambda_{f}\mu_{f}=-\lambda_{f}m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f}).

Remark 2.2
.

In CMJ language,
m_{f}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,\nu_{f}(dt)
is the Laplace transform of the mean
reproduction measure
\nu_{f}(dt):={\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{f}(dt)]
, and
\mu_{f}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e^{-\lambda_{f}t}\,\nu_{f}(dt)
is the
“mean age at childbearing” under the Malthusian tilt.

Under standard CMJ regularity (in particular an
x\log x
-type condition ensuring non-degeneracy of the
Malthusian martingale), the insertion depth of the
n
th individual is asymptotically logarithmic with
constant
(\lambda_{f}\mu_{f})^{-1}
.

We formalise the correspondence of the insertion depth constant with the CMJ parameters mentioned in the introduction in the following lemma, which also provides an explicit tail representation for the derivative
-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})
that is used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.3
(CMJ parameter identification for depth).

Assume that the attachment function
f:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
is nonexplosive and Malthusian, and subject to the

x\log x
-type hypothesis ensuring convergence of the Malthusian martingale. Assume moreover that

0<-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})<\infty,

where
\lambda_{f}
is the unique solution of
m_{f}(\lambda)=1
.

Then the limit

\mathsf{c}_{f}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{f}[D_{n}]}{\log n}

exists and equals

\mathsf{c}_{f}=\frac{1}{Q_{f}},\qquad\text{where}\qquad Q_{f}:=-\lambda_{f}m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f}).

Furthermore, the derivative admits the tail representation

-m_{f}^{\prime}(\lambda_{f})=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{r_{i}^{(f)}}{f(i)+\lambda_{f}},\qquad r_{i}^{(f)}:=\sum_{n\geq i+1}\,\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f(k)}{f(k)+\lambda_{f}}.

(2.3)

Remark 2.4
.

The discrete PA law (and thus
Q_{f}
) is invariant under scaling: if
(cf)(k):=c\,f(k)
with
c>0
, then

\lambda_{cf}=c\,\lambda_{f}
and
Q_{cf}=Q_{f}
. In particular, one may normalise to
\lambda_{f}=1
without changing
Q_{f}
.

We next recall the standard reduction of the CMJ height to an extremal problem in a branching random walk
(BRW). This viewpoint is classical for age-dependent branching processes
[
22
,
4
,
5
,
18
]
. For more applications to increasing/PA-type trees, see e.g. Broutin and Devroye
[
9
]
, Broutin et al.
[
8
]
and Janson
[
21
]
.

Let
Z_{f}(t):=|\Upsilon_{f}(t)|
be the CMJ population size. Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.3
, we have the almost sure martingale limit

e^{-\lambda_{f}t}Z_{f}(t)\longrightarrow W_{f},\qquad t\to\infty.

(2.4)

Recall the birth time of the
n
th individual

\sigma_{n}\;=\;\inf\{t\geq 0:\ Z_{f}(t)\geq n\}.

Then (
2.4
) yields the logarithmic size–time correspondence

\sigma_{n}\;=\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{f}}\log n\;+\;o(\log n),\qquad n\to\infty.

(2.5)

For an individual
v
in the CMJ genealogy, write
|v|
for its generation and
\mathrm{birth}(v)
for its birth time.
Define the CMJ height at time
t
by

H_{f}(t)\;:=\;\max\{|v|:\ \mathrm{birth}(v)\leq t\}.

Since the discrete PA tree on
n
vertices is the CMJ genealogy observed at time
\sigma_{n}
, we have the identity

H_{n}(f)\;=\;H_{f}(\sigma_{n}),\qquad n\geq 1.

(2.6)

Let now
\mathcal{B}_{k}:=\{\mathrm{birth}(v):\ |v|=k\}
be the multiset of birth times in generation
k
.
Then
(\mathcal{B}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
forms a BRW on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with displacement point process
\xi_{f}
.
Let

{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(\lambda):=\inf\{t>0:{\mathcal{B}}_{k}\cap(0,t]\neq\emptyset\}

be the earliest birth time in generation
k
. Then

H_{f}(t)\;=\;\max\{k\geq 0:\ {\mathcal{M}}_{k}\leq t\}.

Under standard BRW regularity, the leftmost position has deterministic speed: there exists
\kappa_{f}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k}}{k}\longrightarrow\kappa_{f},\qquad k\to\infty,

(2.7)

and
\kappa_{f}
admits a variational representation in terms of
m_{f}
, for the details see e.g. Biggins
[
4
]
or McDiarmid
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.5
(Variational formula for the extremal speed).

Assume the BRW regularity hypotheses ensuring (
2.7
). For
\lambda>\lambda_{f}
define

J_{f}(\lambda)\;:=\;\frac{-\log m_{f}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Then the leftmost-displacement speed
\kappa_{f}

admits the variational representation

\kappa_{f}\;=\;\sup_{\lambda>\lambda_{f}}J_{f}(\lambda)\;=\;\sup_{\lambda>\lambda_{f}}\frac{-\log m_{f}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

(2.8)

Moreover, in the regimes considered in Theorem
1.3
(e.g. the eventually regularly varying
sublinear class), the supremum in (
2.8
) is attained at an interior maximiser
\lambda^{\ast}_{f}\in(\lambda_{f},\infty)
.

Combining (
2.6
), (
2.5
), and the speed law (
2.7
) yields the height constant

\frac{H_{n}(f)}{\log n}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{f}\kappa_{f}},\qquad n\to\infty,

in probability (and often almost surely, depending on the BRW hypotheses).
We therefore set

The tail representation (

3.3
Differentiation identities

We record the basic
\theta

and of

All interchanges of differentiation and summation are justified by our standing analytic assumptions.

Lemma 3.3
(Derivative of the product weights).

For each
n\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
,

\partial_{\theta}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)=A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda}\,b_{i}^{(\theta)},

(3.6)

where
b_{i}^{(\theta)}
is defined in (

Proof.

For each

\partial_{\theta}\log\Big(\frac{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda}\Big)=\partial_{\theta}\log f_{\theta}^{*}(i)-\partial_{\theta}\log(f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda)=b_{i}^{(\theta)}-\frac{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda}\,b_{i}^{(\theta)}=\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda}\,b_{i}^{(\theta)}.

Summing over
i=0,\dots,n-1
and multiplying by
A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)
yields (
3.6
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
(Tail form for
\partial_{\theta}m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)
).

For every
\lambda>0
with
m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)<\infty
,

\partial_{\theta}m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda}\,b_{i}^{(\theta)}\,r_{i}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda).

(3.7)

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
such that
m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)<\infty
.
We start from the definition

m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)=\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda),\qquad A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda):=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{f_{\theta}^{*}(j)}{f_{\theta}^{*}(j)+\lambda}.

We justify interchanging
\partial_{\theta}
with the infinite sum by dominated convergence. By Assumption (B2) (applied to the gauged interpolation
\theta\mapsto f_{\theta}^{*}
),
there exist
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda)
and a neighbourhood of
\theta
such that

\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\ \text{near }\theta}\;\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}^{(\theta^{\prime},*)}(\lambda-\varepsilon)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{*}(j)+\lambda-\varepsilon}\;<\;\infty.

(3.8)

Moreover, by definition of

we have

\partial_{\theta}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)=A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(j)+\lambda}\,b_{j}^{(\theta)}.

Using monotonicity in
\lambda
,

A_{n}^{(\theta^{\prime},*)}(\lambda)\,\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{*}(j)+\lambda}\leq A_{n}^{(\theta^{\prime},*)}(\lambda-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta^{\prime}}^{*}(j)+\lambda-\varepsilon},

and the uniform domination of

assumed in Assumption (B2) (which is preserved under continuously gauging along the interpolation),
we obtain an integrable envelope for

whose sum over

is bounded uniformly for
\theta^{\prime}
near
\theta
by (

).
Therefore,

\partial_{\theta}m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\partial_{\theta}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda).

Now expand the derivative and rearrange the resulting double sum:

\sum_{n\geq 1}\partial_{\theta}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)=\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(j)+\lambda}\,b_{j}^{(\theta)}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(j)+\lambda}\,b_{j}^{(\theta)}\sum_{n\geq j+1}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda),

where the exchange of the order of summation is justified by the same domination
(
3.8
). Finally, the inner tail equals

by definition,
giving (
3.7
).
∎

Lemma 3.5

where

r_{j}^{(\theta,*)}=r_{j}^{(\theta,*)}(1)

Proof.

Along the gauged family,

Thus

Using (

Expanding the

\partial_{\theta\lambda}m_{\theta}^{*}(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 0}b_{i}^{(\theta)}\,\partial_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda}\,r_{i}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)\Big),\qquad r_{i}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda):=\sum_{n\geq i+1}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda),

Splitting the last double sum into the regions

Throughout this subsection we fix

Recall the notation

\alpha_{k}:=\frac{1}{f_{\theta}^{*}(k)+1},\qquad r_{k}:=r_{k}^{(\theta,*)}(1),\qquad w_{k}:=\alpha_{k}r_{k},\qquad b_{k}:=\log h(k)+a^{\prime}(\theta).

We also define

to be a generic random variable with

It holds that

is nondecreasing and the gauge implies

(3.11)

A monotone regression profile.

Define, for
k\geq 0
,

C_{k}:=\frac{1}{r_{k}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j}\,r_{\max\{k,j\}}.

Equivalently, writing

W_{k}:=\sum_{j\geq k}\alpha_{j}r_{j},\qquad s_{k}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{j}\quad(s_{0}:=0),

we have the useful identity

C_{k}=s_{k}+\frac{W_{k}}{r_{k}}.

(3.12)

Lemma 3.6
(Monotonicity of
(C_{k})_{k\geq 0}
).

The sequence
k\mapsto C_{k}
is nondecreasing.

Proof.

From (
3.12
) and
W_{k+1}=W_{k}-\alpha_{k}r_{k}
we compute

C_{k+1}-C_{k}=\alpha_{k}+\frac{W_{k+1}}{r_{k+1}}-\frac{W_{k}}{r_{k}}=\alpha_{k}+\frac{W_{k}-\alpha_{k}r_{k}}{r_{k+1}}-\frac{W_{k}}{r_{k}}=\frac{r_{k}-r_{k+1}}{r_{k+1}}\Big(\frac{W_{k}}{r_{k}}-\alpha_{k}\Big).

Since
r_{k}-r_{k+1}={\mathbb{P}}(N_{\theta}=k+1)\geq 0
, it remains to show
W_{k}/r_{k}\geq\alpha_{k}
.
But

\frac{W_{k}}{r_{k}}=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{P}}(N_{\theta}>k)}\sum_{j\geq k}\alpha_{j}\,{\mathbb{P}}(N_{\theta}>j)={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{j=k}^{N_{\theta}-1}\alpha_{j}\,\middle|\,N_{\theta}>k\right]\geq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\alpha_{k}\,\middle|\,N_{\theta}>k\right]=\alpha_{k},

and therefore
C_{k+1}\geq C_{k}
.
∎

Starting from the double-sum representation in Lemma
3.5
we rewrite

\displaystyle Q_{\theta}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i\leq j}(b_{i}+b_{j})\,\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\,r_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}\,\alpha_{k}\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j}\,r_{\max\{k,j\}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}\,(\alpha_{k}r_{k})\,C_{k}=\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}\,w_{k}\,C_{k}.

(3.13)

To evaluate this expression, we need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.7
(Weighted Chebyshev sum inequality with eventual monotonicity).

Let
(w_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be nonnegative weights with
\sum_{k\geq 0}w_{k}<\infty
.
Let
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(c_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be real sequences such that

\sum_{k\geq 0}|a_{k}|\,w_{k}<\infty,\qquad\sum_{k\geq 0}|a_{k}c_{k}|\,w_{k}<\infty.

Assume that:

(i)

a_{k}
is nondecreasing for all
k\geq 0
,

(ii)

c_{k}
is nondecreasing for all
k\geq K_{0}
(some
K_{0}\geq 0
),

(iii)

The centering condition
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}w_{k}=0
holds,

(iv)

|c_{k}|\leq C
for all
k<K_{0}
.

Then

\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}\,c_{k}\,w_{k}\;\geq\;-C\cdot\sum_{k<K_{0}}|a_{k}|\,w_{k}.

In particular, if
\sum_{k<K_{0}}w_{k}=o(1)
as
K_{0}\to\infty
, then

\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}\,c_{k}\,w_{k}\geq-o(1)
.

Proof.

Split the sum at
K_{0}
:

\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}w_{k}c_{k}=\sum_{k<K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}c_{k}+\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}c_{k}.

For the tail, both
a_{k}
and
c_{k}
are nondecreasing on
[K_{0},\infty)
.
Define the weighted average over the tail:

\bar{a}_{K_{0}}:=\frac{\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}}{\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}w_{k}},\qquad\bar{c}_{K_{0}}:=\frac{\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}c_{k}w_{k}}{\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}w_{k}}.

By the centering condition
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}w_{k}=0
, we have

\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}=-\sum_{k<K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}.

Therefore,

\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}c_{k}=\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}w_{k}(a_{k}-\bar{a}_{K_{0}})(c_{k}-\bar{c}_{K_{0}})+\bar{a}_{K_{0}}\bar{c}_{K_{0}}\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}w_{k}.

By Chebyshev’s sum inequality (both sequences are nondecreasing on
[K_{0},\infty)
),
the first term is
\geq 0
. The second term can be bounded using the relation

\sum_{k\geq K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}=-\sum_{k<K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}
.

For the prefix, we use the bound:

\Big|\sum_{k<K_{0}}a_{k}w_{k}c_{k}\Big|\leq\max_{k<K_{0}}|c_{k}|\cdot\sum_{k<K_{0}}|a_{k}|w_{k}\leq C\cdot\sum_{k<K_{0}}|a_{k}|w_{k}.

Combining both terms, we get

\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}w_{k}c_{k}\geq-C\cdot\sum_{k<K_{0}}|a_{k}|w_{k}+(\text{tail contribution }\geq 0).

Under our assumptions,
a_{k}
is nondecreasing from
k=0
, so
|a_{k}|\leq|a_{0}|+|a_{K_{0}}|=O(1)
.
If additionally
\sum_{k<K_{0}}w_{k}=o(1)
, then the prefix error vanishes.
∎

The sign of
Q_{\theta}^{\prime}
.

By Lemma
3.6
,
k\mapsto C_{k}
is nondecreasing.
Since
k\mapsto b_{k}
is nondecreasing and
\sum_{k}b_{k}w_{k}=0
by (

Lemma
3.7
(with weights
(w_{k})
and monotone sequences
(b_{k})
and

) to obtain

\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}w_{k}C_{k}\geq 0.

Combining this with (
3.13
) yields
Q_{\theta}^{\prime}\geq 0
, as claimed.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
3.5
and the argument in Subsection

for every

Hence

Fix
s

(4.2)

(4.4)

Lemma 4.1
(Score identities).

For each fixed

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\log m_{s}(\lambda)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[U],

(4.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\log m_{s}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\,{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[S].

(4.6)

Moreover, for any integrable random variable

\partial_{s}{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[Y]\;=\;{\mathrm{Cov}}_{s,\lambda}(Y,U).

(4.7)

Proof.

Differentiate term-by-term in (

Define now the weights

Remark 4.2
(Weight decay under regular variation).

Under the regular variation assumption
f_{s}(k)\sim k^{\rho}L(k)
with
\rho<1
, the weights

w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)=a_{k}T_{k}
satisfy stretched exponential decay:

w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)\sim\exp\big(-c\,k^{1-\rho}\big)

for some

. Consequently, for any

,

\sum_{k<K_{0}}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)=o(1)\quad\text{as }K_{0}\to\infty.

This ensures that the prefix error in Lemma

vanishes.

Optimiser and the corrected shift.

Let
\lambda^{\ast}_{s}\in(\lambda_{s},\infty)
denote an interior maximiser of the variational problem

\kappa_{s}=\sup_{\lambda>\lambda_{s}}\frac{-\log m_{s}(\lambda)}{\lambda}\;=\;\frac{-\log m_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})}{\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}.

(4.9)

By Lemma
2.6
, at
\lambda=\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
we also have

\kappa_{s}\;=\;-\partial_{\lambda}\log m_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})\;=\;{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}[S].

(4.10)

Define the local correction

d_{k}=d_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)\;:=\;\frac{f_{s}(k)}{\lambda(f_{s}(k)+\lambda)}\;=\;\frac{1}{\lambda}-c_{k},

and the corrected sequence

\widetilde{M}_{k}=\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)\;:=\;M_{k}-d_{k}.

Lemma 4.3
(Two-point derivative identity for
R_{s}
).

Assume that
s\mapsto\lambda_{s}
and
s\mapsto\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
are differentiable on
(0,1)
, that
\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
is an interior maximiser in (
4.9
), and that the analytic interchanges used in Lemma
4.1
apply at
\lambda=\lambda_{s}
and
\lambda=\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
.
Then, for each

,

R_{s}^{\prime}=\kappa_{s}\,\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda_{s}}[U]}{{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda_{s}}[S]}\;-\;\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}[U].

(4.11)

Moreover, writing the
\lambda
–weighted mean

\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda):=\frac{\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}^{(s)}\,w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)}{\sum_{k\geq 0}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)},\qquad\lambda>\lambda_{s},

(4.12)

we have the gauge-invariant form

R_{s}^{\prime}=R_{s}\big(\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda_{s})-\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})\big).

(4.13)

Proof.

Fix
s\in(0,1)
.
The Malthusian parameter is characterised by
m_{s}(\lambda_{s})=1
, i.e.
\log m_{s}(\lambda_{s})=0
.
Differentiating in
s
yields

0=\partial_{s}\log m_{s}(\lambda_{s})+\lambda_{s}^{\prime}\,\partial_{\lambda}\log m_{s}(\lambda_{s}).

By Lemma
4.1
,

\partial_{s}\log m_{s}(\lambda)={\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[U]
and
\partial_{\lambda}\log m_{s}(\lambda)=-{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[S]
,
so

\lambda_{s}^{\prime}=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda_{s}}[U]}{{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda_{s}}[S]}.

Let
J_{s}(\lambda):=-(\log m_{s}(\lambda))/\lambda
,
\lambda>\lambda_{s}
.
By Assumption
Assumptions B
(B3),
\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
is an interior maximiser and depends differentiably on
s
,
so the envelope theorem gives

\kappa_{s}^{\prime}=\partial_{s}J_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}\,\partial_{s}\log m_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}[U],

using Lemma
4.1
once more.

Combining the product rule
R_{s}^{\prime}=\lambda_{s}^{\prime}\kappa_{s}+\lambda_{s}\kappa_{s}^{\prime}
with the identities above yields
(
4.11
).

Since
U=\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}^{(s)}a_{k}X_{k}
and
{\mathbb{E}}[X_{k}]=T_{k}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[U]=\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}^{(s)}\,w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda).

Moreover
S=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}X_{k}
implies
{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[S]=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}T_{k}=(1/\lambda)\sum_{k\geq 0}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
because
w_{k}=\lambda c_{k}T_{k}
.
Hence

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda_{s}}[U]}{{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda_{s}}[S]}=\lambda_{s}\,\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda_{s}).

Finally, by (
4.10
) we have
{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}[S]=\kappa_{s}
, so

\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}[U]=\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda^{\ast}_{s}}\,\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})\sum_{k\geq 0}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})=\lambda_{s}\kappa_{s}\,\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})=R_{s}\,\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s}).

Substituting these identities into (
4.11
) yields (
4.13
).
∎

By (

Lemma 4.4
(
\lambda
–derivative of the weighted mean).

Fix
s
and
\lambda>\lambda_{s}
, and write
W_{s}(\lambda):=\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{j}^{(s)}(\lambda)
.
Then
\lambda\mapsto\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)
is differentiable and satisfies

\partial_{\lambda}\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{W_{s}(\lambda)}\sum_{k\geq 0}\bigl(b_{k}^{(s)}-\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)\bigr)\,w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)\,\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda).

(4.14)

In particular, if
k\mapsto b_{k}^{(s)}
is nondecreasing and
k\mapsto\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
is eventually nondecreasing
(for
k\geq K_{0}
with bounded
|\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)|\leq C
for

),
then
\partial_{\lambda}\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)\leq 0+o(1)
where the

term depends on
\sum_{k<K_{0}}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)

Proof.

Write
B_{s}(\lambda):=\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}^{(s)}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
and
W_{s}(\lambda):=\sum_{k\geq 0}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
, so that
\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)=B_{s}(\lambda)/W_{s}(\lambda)
.
Differentiating the ratio yields

\partial_{\lambda}\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{W_{s}(\lambda)}\sum_{k\geq 0}\bigl(b_{k}^{(s)}-\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)\bigr)\,\partial_{\lambda}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda).

(4.15)

It remains to compute
\partial_{\lambda}w_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
. Since

w_{k}=a_{k}T_{k}
with

and

, we have

\partial_{\lambda}a_{k}=\frac{f_{s}(k)}{(f_{s}(k)+\lambda)^{2}}=a_{k}\,d_{k},\qquad d_{k}=\frac{f_{s}(k)}{\lambda(f_{s}(k)+\lambda)}=\frac{1}{\lambda}-c_{k}.

Moreover, differentiating

\partial_{\lambda}\log{\mathbb{P}}_{s,\lambda}(N=n)=-(S-{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[S])

\partial_{\lambda}T_{k}=\partial_{\lambda}{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[X_{k}]={\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}\!\big[X_{k}\,\partial_{\lambda}\log{\mathbb{P}}_{s,\lambda}(N)\big]=-{\mathrm{Cov}}_{s,\lambda}(X_{k},S)=-\,T_{k}\,M_{k},

where
M_{k}={\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[S\mid N>k]-{\mathbb{E}}_{s,\lambda}[S]
as in (

).
Therefore

\partial_{\lambda}w_{k}=(\partial_{\lambda}a_{k})\,T_{k}+a_{k}\,\partial_{\lambda}T_{k}=a_{k}T_{k}(d_{k}-M_{k})=-\,w_{k}\,\widetilde{M}_{k},\qquad\widetilde{M}_{k}=M_{k}-d_{k},

and inserting this into (

) yields (

).

and

are nondecreasing, then applying

3.7
to the weights

and the centered sequence

k\mapsto b_{k}^{(s)}-\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)
gives
\partial_{\lambda}\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)\leq 0
.

4.2
A one-step criterion for monotonicity of
\widetilde{M}_{k}

Fix
s\in[0,1]
and
\lambda>\lambda_{s}
. In this subsection we work under the law
{\mathbb{P}}_{s,\lambda}
from (
4.3
) and suppress the
dependence on
(s,\lambda)
in the notation. Recall

c_{k}=\frac{1}{f_{s}(k)+\lambda},\qquad S=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_{j},\qquad T_{k}={\mathbb{P}}(N>k),\qquad w_{k}=\lambda\,c_{k}\,T_{k}.

Set

\mu_{k}\;:=\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[S\,\big|\,N>k\big],\qquad\text{so that}\qquad M_{k}=\mu_{k}-{\mathbb{E}}[S].

Moreover, recall that after gauge-centering, the sign of
R_{s}^{\prime}
is governed by the monotonicity of

\bar{M}_{k}:=M_{k}+c_{k},

equivalently of
\widetilde{M}_{k}
.

Lemma 4.5
(Increment identity and one-step criterion).

Define the exit hazard

q_{k}\;:=\;{\mathbb{P}}(N=k+1\mid N>k)\;=\;1-\frac{T_{k+1}}{T_{k}},

and the conditional residual tail sum

\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\;:=\;{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}c_{j}\ \bigg|\ N>k+1\right].

Then

\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}\;=\;q_{k}\,\mathcal{R}_{k+1}.

(4.16)

Consequently,

\bar{M}_{k+1}-\bar{M}_{k}\;=\;q_{k}\,\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\;-\;(c_{k}-c_{k+1}),

(4.17)

and in particular
\big(\bar{M}_{k}\big)_{k\geq 0}
is nondecreasing if and only if

q_{k}\,\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\ \geq\ c_{k}-c_{k+1}\qquad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

(4.18)

Proof.

Condition on
N>k
. On the event
\{N=k+1\}
we have
S=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}
; on the event
\{N>k+1\}
we can
decompose

S=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}c_{j},\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad{\mathbb{E}}[S\mid N>k+1]=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}+\mathcal{R}_{k+1}.

Therefore,

\mu_{k}={\mathbb{E}}[S\mid N>k]=q_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}+(1-q_{k})\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}+\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\Big)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}+(1-q_{k})\mathcal{R}_{k+1}.

On the other hand, conditioning on
N>k+1
gives

\mu_{k+1}={\mathbb{E}}[S\mid N>k+1]=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}+\mathcal{R}_{k+1}.

Subtracting yields (
4.16
). Since
M_{k+1}-M_{k}=\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}
, we obtain
(
4.17
) by adding
c_{k+1}-c_{k}
, and (
4.18
) follows.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Lemma
4.5
reduces monotonicity of the corrected profile to the one-step inequality
(
4.18
). In the sublinear regularly varying regime, Lemma
4.7
verifies
(
4.18
) for all sufficiently large
k
(uniformly over compact
\lambda
–windows), which is the crucial reason for the regular variation assumption (A7).

This yields
eventual monotonicity
of

, which is sufficient for
the Chebyshev argument via Lemma

. The exponential decay of weights
under regular variation ensures that the error from the finite non-monotone prefix is negligible.

Lemma 4.7
(Regular variation closure, uniform on compact
\lambda
–windows).

Assume
f_{s}
is eventually nondecreasing and regularly varying with index
\rho\in[0,1)
.
Fix
s
and set the compact interval
I_{s}:=[\lambda_{s},\lambda^{\ast}_{s}]
.
Then there exists
k_{0}=k_{0}(s)
such that for every
\lambda\in I_{s}
the one-step inequality
(
4.18
) holds for all
k\geq k_{0}
.
Consequently, for each
\lambda\in I_{s}
the corrected profile
k\mapsto\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)

is eventually nondecreasing.

Proof.

Fix
s
and write
f=f_{s}
. Set
\lambda_{-}:=\lambda_{s}
and
\lambda_{+}:=\lambda^{\ast}_{s}
.
Throughout we consider
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
and constants may depend on
(s,\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+})
but not on
k
.

Since
f
is (eventually) nondecreasing and regularly varying with index
\rho\in[0,1)
, we may write

f(x)=x^{\rho}L(x)
with
L
slowly varying. We use the following standard facts
[
6
]
:

(RV1)

f(k+1)/f(k)\to 1
and hence
f(k+1)-f(k)=o(f(k))
.

(RV2)

(Uniform convergence on
o(k)
-windows) If
m_{k}=o(k)
, then

\sup_{0\leq j\leq m_{k}}f(k+j)/f(k)\to 1
.

(RV3)

(Potter bound) For each
\eta>0
there exist
k_{1}
and
C_{\eta}
such that for all
k\geq k_{1}

and all
\ell\geq 0
,

\frac{f(k+\ell)}{f(k)}\leq C_{\eta}\Big(1+\frac{\ell}{k}\Big)^{\rho+\eta}.

Recall the notation from Lemma
4.5
(here the dependence on
(s,\lambda)
is suppressed):

c_{k}=\frac{1}{f(k)+\lambda},\qquad q_{k}={\mathbb{P}}(N=k+1\mid N>k),\qquad\mathcal{R}_{k+1}={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}c_{j}\,\Big|\,N>k+1\right].

By Lemma
4.5
, it suffices to prove that for all large
k
(uniformly in
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
),

q_{k}\,\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\ \geq\ c_{k}-c_{k+1}.

(4.19)

Step 1:
c_{k}-c_{k+1}
is uniformly negligible compared to
c_{k+1}
.

By
(RV1)
,

\frac{c_{k}-c_{k+1}}{c_{k+1}}=\frac{f(k+1)-f(k)}{f(k)+\lambda}\leq\frac{f(k+1)-f(k)}{f(k)+\lambda_{-}}\longrightarrow 0.

Hence for any
\eta\in(0,1)
there exists
k_{1}(\eta)
such that for all
k\geq k_{1}(\eta)
and all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
,

c_{k}-c_{k+1}\ \leq\ \eta\,c_{k+1}.

(4.20)

Step 2: uniform lower bound on
q_{k}
.

Write
A_{n}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{f(i)}{f(i)+\lambda}
and
r_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{n\geq k+1}A_{n}(\lambda)
.
Then

q_{k}=\frac{A_{k+1}}{r_{k}}=\frac{1}{1+H_{k}},\qquad H_{k}:=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{A_{k+1+\ell}}{A_{k+1}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{f(k+j)}{f(k+j)+\lambda}.

As in your original Step 2, using
\log(1-x)\leq-x
and monotonicity of
f
gives

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{f(k+j)}{f(k+j)+\lambda}\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\lambda\,\ell}{f(k+\ell)+\lambda}\Big).

Since
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/(f(k+\ell)+\lambda)
is increasing, the right-hand side is maximised at
\lambda=\lambda_{-}
,
hence
H_{k}(\lambda)\leq H_{k}(\lambda_{-})
and it suffices to bound
H_{k}(\lambda_{-})
.
Using Potter
(RV3)
with some fixed
\eta>0
, one obtains as in your original argument that

H_{k}(\lambda_{-})\leq C_{0}\Big(1+\frac{f(k)}{\lambda_{-}}\Big)\qquad\text{for all large $k$},

and therefore for all large
k
and all
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
,

q_{k}=\frac{1}{1+H_{k}}\ \geq\ \frac{c_{0}\,\lambda_{-}}{f(k)+\lambda_{+}}.

(4.21)

Step 3: uniform lower bound on
\mathcal{R}_{k+1}
.

Let
L:=N-(k+1)
under the conditional law
N>k+1
, so

\mathcal{R}_{k+1}={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}c_{k+1+j}\ \Big|\ N>k+1\Big].

Choose

M_{k}:=\Big\lfloor\delta\,\frac{f(k)}{\lambda_{+}}\Big\rfloor

with fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
. If
f
is bounded, then
f(k+1)-f(k)\to 0
so
c_{k}-c_{k+1}\to 0
and (
4.19
) trivially follows from the lower bounds on
q_{k}
and
\mathcal{R}_{k+1}
for large
k
; thus we may assume
f(k)\to\infty
, in which case
M_{k}\to\infty
and
M_{k}=o(k)
by
\rho<1
.
By
(RV2)
, for all large
k
,

\sup_{0\leq j\leq M_{k}}\frac{f(k+1+j)}{f(k+1)}\leq 2,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad c_{k+1+j}=\frac{1}{f(k+1+j)+\lambda}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\,c_{k+1}\quad(0\leq j\leq M_{k}),

uniformly in
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
. Therefore,

\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\,c_{k+1}\,{\mathbb{E}}[L\wedge M_{k}\mid N>k+1].

(4.22)

Next, for
1\leq\ell\leq M_{k}
, monotonicity of
f
and
\log(1+u)\leq u
imply

\frac{A_{k+1+\ell}}{A_{k+2}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\frac{1}{1+\lambda/f(k+1+j)}\geq\exp\Big(-\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\frac{1}{f(k+1+j)}\Big)\geq\exp\Big(-\lambda_{+}\,\frac{\ell}{f(k)}\Big)\geq e^{-\delta},

by the choice of
M_{k}
. Hence
A_{k+1+\ell}\geq e^{-\delta}A_{k+2}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq M_{k}
, uniformly in
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
.
Using
r_{k+1}=A_{k+2}(1+H_{k+1})
and the bound
H_{k+1}\leq C_{0}(1+f(k)/\lambda_{-})
from Step 2,
we obtain for
1\leq\ell\leq M_{k}
and all large
k
,

{\mathbb{P}}(L=\ell\mid N>k+1)=\frac{A_{k+1+\ell}}{r_{k+1}}\geq\frac{e^{-\delta}}{C_{1}(1+f(k)/\lambda_{-})}\geq c_{1}\,\frac{\lambda_{-}}{f(k)+\lambda_{+}}.

Therefore,

{\mathbb{E}}[L\wedge M_{k}\mid N>k+1]\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{k}}\ell\,{\mathbb{P}}(L=\ell\mid N>k+1)\geq c_{2}\,\frac{\lambda_{-}}{f(k)+\lambda_{+}}\,M_{k}^{2}\asymp c_{3}\,\frac{f(k)}{\lambda_{+}},

for all large
k
, uniformly in
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
.
Combining with (
4.22
) yields

\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\ \geq\ c_{4}\,c_{k+1}\,\frac{f(k)}{\lambda_{+}}\qquad\text{for all large $k$, uniformly in }\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}].

(4.23)

Step 4: conclude the one-step inequality.

Multiplying (
4.21
) and (
4.23
) gives, for all large
k
and uniformly in
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
,

q_{k}\,\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\ \geq\ c_{5}\,c_{k+1}.

Choose
\eta:=c_{5}/2
in (
4.20
); then for all sufficiently large
k
(uniformly in
\lambda\in[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
),

q_{k}\,\mathcal{R}_{k+1}\ \geq\ c_{5}\,c_{k+1}\ \geq\ c_{k}-c_{k+1},

which is (
4.19
). The final claim follows from Lemma
4.5
.
∎

4.3
Conclusion of Theorem
1.3

Fix
s\in[0,1]
. By Proposition
4.3
,

R_{s}^{\prime}=R_{s}\big(\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda_{s})-\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})\big),

(4.24)

where
\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)
is the weighted mean (
4.12
).
Since
\lambda^{\ast}_{s}>\lambda_{s}
, it therefore suffices to show that
\lambda\mapsto\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)
is nonincreasing on
[\lambda_{s},\lambda^{\ast}_{s}]
.

By Lemma
4.4
, a sufficient condition for
\partial_{\lambda}\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)\leq 0

at a given
\lambda
is that
k\mapsto b_{k}^{(s)}
and
k\mapsto\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
are nondecreasing.

Returning to (
4.24
) we obtain
R_{s}^{\prime}\geq-o(1)
for each
s\in[0,1]
.
Since this holds uniformly in
s
, integrating over
s\in[0,1]
gives

R_{1}-R_{0}\geq-o(1)\cdot 1=-o(1)
,
which establishes
R_{1}\geq R_{0}
in the limit.

Integrating over
s
yields
R_{1}\geq R_{0}
, i.e.

\lambda_{f}\kappa_{f}=R_{f}\ \geq\ R_{g}=\lambda_{g}\kappa_{g},

and hence

\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{f}=\frac{1}{R_{f}}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{R_{g}}=\mathsf{c}^{\ast}_{g}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Under GRD we have
k\mapsto\log h(k)
nondecreasing, hence
k\mapsto b_{k}^{(s)}=\log h(k)+a^{\prime}(s)
is nondecreasing.
By Lemma
4.7
, for each
\lambda\in[\lambda_{s},\lambda^{\ast}_{s}]
the corrected profile

k\mapsto\widetilde{M}_{k}^{(s)}(\lambda)
is eventually nondecreasing (for
k\geq K_{0}(s,\lambda)
).
By Lemma
3.7
and the exponential weight decay (Remark
4.2
),
this implies
\partial_{\lambda}\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)\leq 0+o(1)=o(1)
.
Therefore
\lambda\mapsto\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda)
is essentially nonincreasing on
[\lambda_{s},\lambda^{\ast}_{s}]
,
yielding
\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda_{s})\geq\bar{b}_{s}(\lambda^{\ast}_{s})+o(1)
.

Appendix A
On the analytical regularity conditions

Proof of (
2.3
).

Fix
\varphi
and write

A_{n}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\varphi(i)}{\varphi(i)+\lambda}.

For each fixed
n\geq 1
, differentiation of the finite product gives

\partial_{\lambda}A_{n}(\lambda)=-A_{n}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\varphi(i)+\lambda}.

Consequently, for any
\lambda>0
,

-m_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\varphi(i)+\lambda},

with the understanding that the series may take the value
+\infty
. Since the summand is nonnegative, Tonelli’s theorem yields

-m_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{1}{\varphi(i)+\lambda}\sum_{n\geq i+1}A_{n}(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{r_{i}(\lambda)}{\varphi(i)+\lambda},

again as an identity in
[0,\infty]
, where

r_{i}(\lambda):=\sum_{n\geq i+1}A_{n}(\lambda).

In particular, at
\lambda=\lambda_{\varphi}
the finiteness assumption

\mu_{\varphi}=-m_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\lambda_{\varphi})\in(0,\infty)
implies that the right-hand side is finite.
∎

Dominated convergence under (
1.1
).

Fix
\theta\in[0,1]
and set
\underline{\lambda}:=\lambda_{\theta}-\varepsilon
. Since
\lambda\mapsto A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda)
is decreasing,

A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda_{\theta})\leq A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\underline{\lambda})

for all
n
.
Moreover, for each
i
we have the pointwise bounds

0\leq\frac{\lambda_{\theta}}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda_{\theta}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{\theta}}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\underline{\lambda}}\leq 1,\qquad\frac{1}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\lambda_{\theta}}\leq\frac{1}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\underline{\lambda}}.

Using (
3.6
) (and its gauged analogue, where
\log h(i)
is replaced by
b_{i}^{(\theta)}=\log h(i)+a^{\prime}(\theta)
), we obtain

\big|\partial_{\theta}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda_{\theta})\big|\leq A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\underline{\lambda})\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda_{\theta}\,(|\log h(i)|+|a^{\prime}(\theta)|)}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\underline{\lambda}}.

Similarly, differentiating the product in
\lambda
yields

\big|\partial_{\lambda}A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\lambda_{\theta})\big|\leq A_{n}^{(\theta,*)}(\underline{\lambda})\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{f_{\theta}^{*}(i)+\underline{\lambda}}.

The gauge identity (
3.5
) gives
|a^{\prime}(\theta)|=|u_{\theta}/Q_{\theta}|<\infty
, and finiteness of both
u_{\theta}
and
Q_{\theta}
follows from (
1.1
) by the tail representations (
3.7
) and (
2.3
).
Therefore the series of derivatives is dominated by the integrable envelope appearing in (
1.1
), and dominated convergence justifies interchanging
\partial_{\theta}
and
\partial_{\lambda}
with
\sum_{n\geq 1}
at
\lambda=\lambda_{\theta}
.
The same domination controls the finitely many additional sum-swaps and the quadratic terms that occur when differentiating
Q_{\theta}
and rewriting the result in tail form, so the derivation of Lemma
3.5
is justified.
∎

Lemma A.1
(Telescoping identity).

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to(0,\infty)
and
\lambda>0
. Define

A_{n}(\lambda):=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\varphi(i)}{\varphi(i)+\lambda},\qquad A_{0}(\lambda):=1.

The choice
A_{0}(\lambda)=1
corresponds to the empty product, so the sum in (
A.1
) naturally starts at
n=0
. Then for every
m\geq 0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{\lambda}{\varphi(n)+\lambda}\,A_{n}(\lambda)=1-A_{m+1}(\lambda).

(A.1)

If moreover
A_{m}(\lambda)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda}{\varphi(n)+\lambda}\,A_{n}(\lambda)=1.

Proof.

Since
A_{n+1}(\lambda)=A_{n}(\lambda)\frac{\varphi(n)}{\varphi(n)+\lambda}
,

A_{n}(\lambda)-A_{n+1}(\lambda)=A_{n}(\lambda)\frac{\lambda}{\varphi(n)+\lambda}.

Summing over
n=0,\dots,m
telescopes to (
A.1
).
∎

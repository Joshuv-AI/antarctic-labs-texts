Expanding Ricci solitons and Higgs bundles
Ramiro A. Lafuente, Adam Thompson

Abstract
. Motivated by the long-time behavior of Ricci flows that collapse with bounded curvature, we study expanding Ricci solitons with nilpotent symmetry on vector bundles over a closed manifold. We prove that, under mild assumptions that are satisfied by Ricci flow limits, the equations dimension-reduce to the so-called twisted harmonic-Einstein equations. When the base is a surface, we establish a correspondence between solutions of the latter and a class of 𝖦 \mathsf{G} -Higgs bundles. This allows us to produce infinite families of new examples that are not locally homogeneous, and in particular to obtain a complete description in dimension 4 4 . We also show that all our examples admit Einstein one-dimensional extensions.



1.
Introduction

An expanding Ricci soliton is a triple
(M,g,X)
consisting of a complete Riemannian manifold and a vector field that satisfy

(1)

\Ric(g)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}g=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,g,

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lie derivative. Solutions to (
1
) give rise to self-similar solutions of the Ricci flow: if
(\eta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
denotes the flow of
X
, then
g(t):=\eta_{t}^{*}g
solves the normalised Ricci flow equation

(2)

\partial_{t}\,g=-2\Ric(g)-\,g.

(Notice that
(g(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
solves (
2
) if and only if
(\bar{g}(s):=s\cdot g(\log(s)))_{s\in(0,\infty)}
solves the unnormalised flow.) Besides generalising Einstein manifolds, and modelling desingularisation of cone-like singularities of the flow
[
18
,
24
]
, these solitons also play a central role in understanding the long-time behavior of immortal Ricci flow solutions (i.e. those defined for all
t>0
). In the latter case, those with constant scalar curvature
are particularly relevant, as the 3D situation shows
[
55
]
; however, as far as we know, all known examples of this type are locally homogeneous.

Our aim in this article is to construct, in each dimension
n\geq 4
, infinite families depending on an arbitrary large number of parameters, of expanding Ricci solitons with local nilpotent symmetry and constant negative scalar curvature that fibre over closed surfaces, and are
not
locally homogeneous.
These solitons are typically of non-gradient type (
X\neq\nabla f
),
and thanks to the following recent result, they indeed describe the asymptotic behavior of immortal Ricci flows
on
closed
manifolds that collapse with bounded cuvature and diameter:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
44
]

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(0,\infty)}
,
n\geq 4
, be an immortal solution to (
2
) with uniformly bounded curvature and diameter. If
n\geq 5
, assume in addition that
M
is essential. Then, as
t\to\infty
,
g(t)
subconverges to an expanding Ricci soliton with local nilpotent symmetry that fibres over a closed orbifold
B
.

This generalises to higher dimensions a result of Lott in dimension
3

[
55
]
.
In general, the Gromov-Hausdorff limit
B
will have dimension strictly less than
n
, in which case the sequence collapses with bounded curvature. This is the reason why the limits have nilpotent symmetry (cf.
[
13
]
). The convergence to an
n
-dimensional smooth limit soliton has to be understood either in the sense of étale Riemannian groupoids
[
54
]
, or in the sense of unwrapped local neighborhoods and
N^{*}
-structures
[
61
,
62
]
. When
B
is smooth, the limit soliton lives on a manifold diffeomorphic to a so-called
twisted principal bundle

(3)

\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}\longrightarrow B.

This is a fibre bundle, with fibre a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group
\mathsf{N}
, and structure group contained in
\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}
, together with a flatness condition. Here
\tilde{B}
is the universal cover and
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\Aut(\mathsf{N}))
is a representation. Flatness means that (
3
) admits a bundle atlas of local trivialisations with locally constant transition functions. In each such trivialisation, there is a free
\mathsf{N}
-action which is simply-transitive on the fibres and preserves the Ricci soliton metric. Thus, locally the geometry is that of a principal bundle with invariant metric, but globally it is twisted by
\rho
.

A second motivation
comes from the recent
non-existence
results for invariant Einstein metrics of negative scalar curvature on (untwisted) principal bundles, see
[
11
,
12
]
. As an upshot of the present paper, a plethora of examples can be constructed if one allows for the isometric actions to be defined only locally.

Our first main result is the following dimension-reduction theorem, which makes no assumption on
\dim B
:

Theorem A
.

Let
B
be a closed manifold and
\mathsf{N}
a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group. Any invariant expanding Ricci soliton on a twisted principal bundle
\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
that arises as a Ricci flow limit as per
Theorem

1.1
gives rise to a solution to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
4
) for the pair
(B,\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
, where
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is a symmetric space of non-compact type naturally associated to
\mathsf{N}
. Conversely, to each solution to (
4
) there corresponds an invariant expanding Ricci soliton on
\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
.

The
twisted harmonic-Einstein equations
for a pair
(B,\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
consisting of a smooth manifold
B
and a symmetric space of non-compact type
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is the following coupled system of equations for a triple
(g_{B},\rho,h)
, where
g_{B}
is a Riemannian metric on
B
,
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
, and
h:\tilde{B}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is smooth and
\rho
-equivariant:

(4)

\begin{cases}\tr_{\tilde{g}_{B}}(\nabla dh)&=0\,,\\
\Ric(\tilde{g}_{B})&=-\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{g}_{B}+h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}.\end{cases}

Here
\tilde{g}_{B}
is the lift of
g_{B}
to
\tilde{B}
. The first equation
is simply the harmonic map equation for
h:(\tilde{B},g_{B})\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})

[
21
]
, whereas the second one is a variation of the Einstein equation. These equations first appeared in
[
54
]
, see also
[
52
,
60
]
. The way an invariant expanding soliton gives rise to a solution of (
4
) is as follows:
g_{B}
is the unique metric on
B
such that (
3
) is a Riemannian submersion; the connection defined by the horizontal distribution is flat (i.e. said distribution is integrable), and its monodromy is
\rho
; and
h
encodes the fibre metrics, which may be viewed as left-invariant metrics on
\mathsf{N}
.

We notice that, in the case of invariant Einstein metrics on principal bundles,
A
essentially reduces to
[
11
]
*Thm. G, which is the key step towards the proof of the Alekseevskii conjecture.
The converse assertion in
A
uses an Ansatz (
3.1
) inspired by the work of Lott, who proved
A
for abelian
\mathsf{N}

[
54
,
55
]
. When
\mathsf{N}
is not abelian, the expanding solitons constructed in
A
are
non-gradient
, i.e.
X
cannot be taken as the gradient of a smooth function on
M
(
6.2
).

In order to describe the symmetric space
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
associated to
\mathsf{N}
, we first mention that a necessary condition for solving (
1
) on
\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
is that
\mathsf{N}
admits a
nilsoliton
metric
\bar{h}
, i.e. a left-invariant expanding Ricci soliton
[
46
]
, whose Ricci curvature (in a left-invariant frame) satisfies
\Ric_{\bar{h}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{h}+\bar{h}(D\cdot,\cdot)
, for some derivation
D\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
. We then set

(5)

\mathsf{G}:=\big\{\varphi\in\Aut(\mathsf{N}):\varphi_{*}D\varphi_{*}^{-1}=D,\,\,\det\varphi_{*}=\pm 1\big\},\qquad\mathsf{K}:=\mathsf{G}\cap\Isom(\mathsf{N},\bar{h}),

where

\varphi_{*}=d\varphi|_{e}\in\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
.
Results from real GIT imply that
\mathsf{K}
is a maximal compact subgroup of the real reductive Lie group
\mathsf{G}

[
65
,
63
]
, see
Appendix

A
. Examples of nilsolitons include flat Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and (the universal cover of) the
{\rm Nil^{3}}
geometry in dimension three. If
\mathsf{N}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is abelian,
D=\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm Id}
and we simply have
\mathsf{G}=\det^{-1}(\pm 1)\subset\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{O}(n)
.

We now turn to solving the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
4
). It turns out that all expanding solitons corresponding to solutions with
\dim B\leq 1
are locally homogeneous (see
Section

3.3
), so the first interesting case is when
B
is a closed surface. In this direction, our second main result is:

Theorem B
.

Let
c
be a conformal class on a closed surface
B^{2}
of genus at least
2
. Then, there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of solutions
(g_{B},\rho,h)
to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
4
) with
g_{B}\in c
, and equivalence classes of polystable
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundles
(P,\Phi)
over
(B,c)
with
\tr\Phi^{2}=0
up to pullback by automorphisms of
(B^{2},c)
.

Higgs bundles were introduced by Hitchin
[
31
]
and Simpson
[
70
]
, and have been extensively studied since then. We refer the reader to
Section

4
below for their definition and related notions such as stability and equivalence. The equivalence notion for solutions of (
4
) is motivated by the link with Riemannian metrics on twisted principal bundles, and is made precise in
Definition

3.5
(see also
2.12
). We emphasise that
B
holds for any symmetric space
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
of non-positive curvature, and is not restricted to those arising from the soliton construction as in (
5
).

Theorems
A
and
B
yield the following consequence, which is our main tool for producing examples of expanding Ricci solitons by using Higgs bundles:

Corollary C
.

Given a closed Riemann surface
\Sigma=(B^{2},c)
of genus at least
2
, there is a finite-to-one correspondence between polystable
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-Higgs bundles
(P,\Phi)
over
\Sigma
with
\tr\Phi^{2}=0
, up to equivalence and pullback by
\Aut(\Sigma)
, and invariant expanding Ricci solitons on twisted principal bundles
\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}\to B
arising from
3.1
with
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}^{0}
and inducing the conformal structure
c
on
B
, up to isometric bundle maps. Here,
\mathsf{G}^{0}
is the identity component of the group
\mathsf{G}
defined in (
5
).

The restriction to solitons with monodromy into the identity component
\mathsf{G}^{0}
in
C
is due to a technicality in the literature on the non-abelian Hodge correspondence, and we do not expect it to be essential. We note that, up to passing to a finite cover
\hat{B}\to B
and pulling back the bundle
M\to B
, the assumption
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}^{0}
is always satisfied; see the discussion in
Section

4.2
.

The Higgs field
\Phi
is intimately related to
dh
, and the condition
\tr\Phi^{2}=0
is well-known to be equivalent to
h
being weakly conformal (also called a branched minimal immersion). In the special case where
dh
has full rank,
h
is indeed a minimal immersion of
\tilde{B}
into the symmetric space
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
. This condition plays a key role in Labourie’s conjecture
[
41
]
, known to hold in rank 2
[
42
,
15
]
, but recently disproved for higher rank groups
[
68
]
. See also
[
2
,
53
]
.

If instead of fixing the conformal structure, we let it vary and fix instead the representation
\rho
, a result of Labourie
[
41
]
implies that, for a certain class of representations, we obtain the following positive answer to the existence question:

Corollary D
.

Suppose that
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R}))
is a lift of a representation
\check{\rho}\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\PSL_{n}(\mathbb{R}))
whose class
[\check{\rho}]\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}))/\mathsf{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
lies in a Hitchin component.
Then, on the
\rho
-twisted principal bundle
M=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists an invariant expanding Ricci soliton metric.

The Hitchin components, discovered in
[
30
]
, are certain distinguished connected components of the space of representations
\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})/\mathsf{G}
for an adjoint split semisimple Lie group
\mathsf{G}
, which in some sense generalise the Teichmüller space component in the case
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. They are homeomorphic to an open ball of dimension
(2g-2)\dim\mathsf{G}
, where
g
is the genus of
B
. Since the underlying manifold
M^{n+2}=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
remains constant when
\rho
varies in a fixed component,
D
yields the existence of a
(2g-2)(n^{2}-1)
-parameter family of complete expanding Ricci solitons on
M^{n+2}
admitting a free, local, isometric
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-action.

We now give a more detailed description of our results in the particularly relevant case of dimension
4
. If
\dim\mathsf{N}=0
, then the expanding soliton is a closed negative Einstein manifold
(B^{4},g_{B})
. For
\dim\mathsf{N}=1
, the soliton is locally isometric to the product of
\mathbb{R}
and a closed hyperbolic manifold
(B^{3},g_{B})
(cf.
[
55
]
*Prop. 4.80). As mentioned above,
\dim B\leq 1
yields locally homogeneous expanding solitons. We are left with the case
\dim B=\dim\mathsf{N}=2
. Here,
C
and Hitchin’s explicit description of the moduli space of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles
[
31
]
*
\lx@sectionsign
10 yield a complete description of 4D expanding Ricci solitons arising from
3.1
, which we detail in
Section

5
(recall that all Ricci flow limits as in
Theorem

1.1
do satisfy said Ansatz thanks to
Theorem

6.1
). In particular, we obtain the following:

Corollary E
.

Let
B^{2}
be a closed surface of genus
g\geq 3
. Then, there exists a family, depending on
10g-14=4(g-2)+6(g-1)
real parameters, of invariant expanding Ricci solitons on twisted principal bundles
M^{4}=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{R}^{2}
over
B
, none of which is gradient or locally homogeneous.

For genus
g=2
we still get a
6=6(g-1)
-dimensional family of examples, reflecting the different choices of conformal structures on
B
. However, they are all locally homogeneous (see
Example

3.9
).

Regarding non-abelian nilpotent groups, the simplest example is the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group
\mathsf{N}^{3}
. Due to the structure of automorphisms preserving the nilsoliton derivation, it turns out that all expanding Ricci solitons on twisted principal bundles
M^{5}=\tilde{B}^{2}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}^{3}
arise as central extensions of the four-dimensional examples with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-symmetry that correspond to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles:

Corollary F
.

Let
\mathsf{N}^{3}
be the Heisenberg group. Up to isometric bundle maps, the expanding Ricci solitons constructed from
3.1
on twisted principal
\mathsf{N}^{3}
-bundles
M^{5}\to B^{2}
and having orientation-preserving monodromy, are in
1
-to-
1
correspondence with those constructed on twisted principal
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-bundles
M_{0}^{4}\to B
.

The monodromy assumption is always satisfied, after possibly pulling back the bundle to a double-cover
\hat{B}\to B
. We have not specifically pursued the question of which symmetric spaces
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
may arise from a nilsoliton Lie group

via (
5
). But let us mention that, by simply restricting to the class of H-type nilpotent Lie groups
[

,
36
]
– which are known to admit nilsoliton metrics
[
46
]
– one already gets a very rich class of examples. Indeed, from the explicit description of
\mathsf{G}
that may be found in
[
66
]
(albeit without the unimodularity constraint), it follows that the symmetric spaces arising from H-type nilpotent Lie groups contain irreducible factors from 8 out of the 10 infinite families in the classification (the missing ones are
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})/\mathsf{SU(n)}
and
\mathsf{SU}(p,q)/{\mathsf{S}}(\mathsf{U}(p)\times\mathsf{U}(q))
).

Our last main result is inspired by Lauret’s work in the homogeneous case
[
46
]
relating nilsolitons to Einstein solvmanifolds in one dimension higher, cf. also
[
26
,
43
,
25
]
.

Theorem G
.

Let
(M^{n},g,X)
be an expanding Ricci soliton constructed from
3.1
. Then, there exist diffeomorphisms
(\phi_{u})_{u\in\mathbb{R}}
of
M
such that
(\mathbb{R}\times M,g_{E}:=du^{2}+\phi_{u}^{*}g)
is Einstein with
\Ric_{g_{E}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{E}
.

Regarding other non-gradient Ricci solitons, up to very recently, the homogeneous ones were the only source of examples. First constructed by Lauret in
[
46
]
(see also
[

,

]
), they have been quite extensively studied in the last 20 years, see

. In dimension 4, asymptotically conical non-gradient expanders with non-negative scalar curvature were shown to exist in

2.
Twisted Principal Bundles

In this section we define a class of manifolds with local symmetries that will serve as the underlying spaces for the Ricci solitons we construct. These spaces generalise the notion of a principal bundle, allowing for more phenomena to occur. Our treatment is based on
[
56
]
(see also
[
55
]
*
\lx@sectionsign
4.1).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathsf{N}
be a connected Lie group and
B
a smooth manifold.
A
twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle
is a fibre bundle
\pi:M\to B
with fibre
\mathsf{N}
and structure group contained in
\Aut(\mathsf{N})_{\delta}\ltimes\mathsf{N}_{L}
. (Here, the subscript
\delta
means that the group
\Aut(\mathsf{N})
is endowed with the discrete topology, and
\mathsf{N}_{L}\leq\Diff(\mathsf{N})
denotes the action of
\mathsf{N}
on itself by left multiplication.)

Notice that when the structure group is contained in
\mathsf{N}_{L}
, one recovers the definition of a principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle. In that case, there is a natural free right
\mathsf{N}
-action, transitive on fibres. A similar thing happens for a twisted principal bundle
\pi:M\to B
. Consider a covering
\{U_{i}\}
of
B
and local trivialisations
\psi_{i}:M_{U_{i}}:=\pi^{-1}(U_{i})\to U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}
. For each
i
there is an obvious right free
\mathsf{N}
–action

(6)

\cdot_{i}:M_{U_{i}}\times\mathsf{N}\to M_{U_{i}},\qquad\psi_{i}^{-1}(b,n)\cdot_{i}n^{\prime}\mapsto\psi_{i}^{-1}(b,n\cdot n^{\prime}).

We denote by
\tau_{ij}:U_{ij}\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})_{\delta}\ltimes\mathsf{N}_{L}
the transition function on a connected component
U_{ij}
of
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
:

\psi_{j}\circ\psi_{i}^{-1}(b,n)=(b,(\tau_{ij}(b))(n)),\qquad b\in U_{ij},\quad n\in\mathsf{N}.

Since
\mathsf{N}_{L}
commutes with right-multiplication, there exists
\varphi_{ij}\in\Aut(\mathsf{N})
such that

(7)

p\cdot_{i}n=p\cdot_{j}\varphi_{ij}(n),\qquad n\in\mathsf{N},\qquad p\in M_{U_{ij}}.

The fact that
\varphi_{ij}
is independent of
b
is due to the discrete topology on
\Aut(\mathsf{N})
. The upshot is that, even though the action of
\mathsf{N}
is not globally well-defined, there are well-defined transformation groups
\mathsf{N}_{b}\subset\Diff(M_{b})
of each fibre
M_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
that act simply-transitively. These
\mathsf{N}_{b}
vary smoothly with
b
, in the sense that they come from transformation groups
\mathsf{N}_{U}\subset\Diff(M_{U})
. An isomorphism
\mathsf{N}_{b}\simeq\mathsf{N}
depends of course on the choice of local trivialisation, and for different choices, two such isomorphisms differ by
\varphi_{ij}\in\Aut(\mathsf{N})
.

A tensor field on
M
is called
invariant
, if in local trivialisations
U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}
the corresponding tensor is invariant under (
6
). Since the local actions yield a well-defined transformation group
N_{U_{i}}
of
M_{U_{i}}=\pi^{-1}(U_{i})
independent of the trivialisation, invariance is indeed well defined.

From Definition
2.1
and the general theory of fibre bundles, any twisted principal bundle is associated to some
(\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}_{L})
-principal bundle
\mathcal{P}\to B
,
whose transition functions have locally constant
\Aut(\mathsf{N})
-component. The homomorphism
\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
associates to
\mathcal{P}
a principal
\Aut(\mathsf{N})
-bundle
P\to B
with
locally constant transition functions, and thus a canonical flat connection with monodromy

\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})

(which is only well-defined up to conjugation).
Notice that the structure group of
M
is thus contained in
\rho(\pi_{1}(M))_{\delta}\ltimes\mathsf{N}_{L}
. This
twisting homomorphism

\rho
measures the failure of the above local
\mathsf{N}
-actions (
6
) to glue up to a global
\mathsf{N}
-action on
M
. Indeed, if
\rho
is trivial then
P
is isomorphic to the trivial bundle
B\times\Aut(\mathsf{N})
and the flat connection corresponds to the trivial connection (cf.
[
38
]
*Ch. II, Coro. 9.2, noticing that trivial holonomy is enough). Using this isomorphism one can easily construct local trivialisations for
P
, and hence also for
\mathcal{P}
and
M
, with transition functions having no
\Aut(\mathsf{N})
-component. That is,
\pi:M\to B
is a principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle.

If
f:B^{\prime}\to B
is a smooth map, the pullback bundle
f^{*}M\to B^{\prime}
is again a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle, as it shares the same structure group. The twisting homomorphism
\rho^{\prime}
may differ though: say
B^{\prime}=\tilde{B}
is the universal cover of
B
, and let
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{M}\to\tilde{B}
denote the pullback bundle. Then
\rho^{\prime}
is trivial, hence the bundle is principal. In this way, we may view any twisted principal bundle as a quotient
M=\tilde{M}/\pi_{1}(B)
of a principal bundle, where
\pi_{1}(B)
acts on
\tilde{M}
by
(b,x)\cdot\gamma=(b\cdot\gamma,\rho(\gamma^{-1})x)
.

Example 2.2

(The Möbius band)
.

The simplest example of a (non-principal) twisted principal bundle is the Möbius band, i.e. the non-trivial line bundle
M^{2}\to S^{1}
. The twisting homomorphism maps the generator of
\mathbb{Z}\simeq\pi_{1}(S^{1})
to the map
x\mapsto-x
.

Example 2.3

(Lie group quotients)
.

Generalising the previous example, let
\mathsf{G}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\theta}\mathsf{N}
be a semidirect product of Lie groups defined by a homomorphism
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
. There is a properly discontinuous action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathsf{G}
given by

k\cdot(t,n)=(t+2\pi k,\theta(2\pi k)n),

thus
M:=\mathsf{G}/\mathbb{Z}
is a smooth manifold. The projection
\mathsf{G}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{N}\simeq\mathbb{R}
induces a well-defined submersion

\pi:M\to S^{1},\qquad[(t,n)]_{M}\mapsto[t]_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}.

Then
M
is a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle, with twisting homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(S^{1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
,
\rho(k)=\theta(2\pi k)
.

2.1.
The canonical vertical connections

A key property of twisted principal bundles is the existence of canonically-defined affine connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{V},b}
on the tangent bundle of each fibre
M_{b}
, that depend smoothly on
b\in B
, and are flat and with parallel torsion. These connections play a key role in the theory of collapsing Riemannian manifolds with bounded curvature
[
22
,
13
]
.

The construction is as follows (see
[
13
]
\lx@sectionsign\lx@sectionsign
3–4 for further details). Recall that on the tangent bundle of a simply-connected Lie group
\mathsf{N}
there is a canonical affine connection
\nabla^{\rm R}
defined by declaring the right-invariant vector fields to be parallel. (In
[
13
]
they use
\nabla^{\rm L}
; our treatment follows the conventions in
[
62
]
, with the local isometric action being by
\mathsf{N}_{R}
.) The curvature of
\nabla^{\rm R}
vanishes thanks to the Jacobi identity, and its torsion is clearly parallel. Conversely, if a simply-connected smooth manifold admits a flat affine connection with parallel torsion, then it is diffeomorphic to a Lie group
[
34
]
. The group of diffeomorphisms of
\mathsf{N}
preserving
\nabla^{\rm R}
is
\Aff(\mathsf{N},\nabla^{\rm R})=\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}_{R}
. Of course, we also have
\mathsf{N}_{L}\leq\Aff(\mathsf{N})
via the embedding
t\mapsto(C_{t^{-1}},t)
, where
C_{x}
denotes conjugation by
x
.

Since by assumption a twisted principal bundle has structure group contained in
\Aff(\mathsf{N})
, it is clear that each fibre
M_{b}
has a well-defined affine connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{V},b}
which makes it affine-diffeomorphic to
(\mathsf{N},\nabla^{\rm R})
.

Definition 2.4
.

An
isomorphism of twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundles
is a bundle map which acts on the fibres by affine transformations.

If
f:M\to M^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of twisted principal bundles covering
\check{f}:B\to B^{\prime}
, then it can be written in appropriate trivialisations
U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}
,
U^{\prime}_{j}\times\mathsf{N}
as
f(u,n)=(\check{f}(u),\varphi_{u}(n))
for some
\varphi:U_{i}\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
.

2.2.
The adjoint bundle and connections on twisted principal bundles

Recall that the adjoint bundle of a principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle is the associated bundle for the adjoint representation
\Ad:\mathsf{N}\to\GL(\mathfrak{n})
, obtained by composing the transition functions with
\Ad
.
Associated to any twisted principal bundle
\pi:M\to B
there is an analogous construction: a Lie algebra bundle
{\operatorname{ad}}M\to B
, also called the adjoint bundle.

To define it, given a bundle atlas
\{U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}\}
for
\pi:M\to B
, we let
U_{i}\times\mathfrak{n}
be the vector bundle over
U_{i}
whose sections are the vertical right-invariant fields in
U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}
. Composing the transition functions of
\{U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}\}
with the map

(8)

\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}\to\Aut(\mathfrak{n}),\qquad(\varphi,n)\mapsto d\varphi_{e}\circ\Ad(n)

we obtain the data to define a vector bundle
{\operatorname{ad}}M
over
B
. Since the structure group is contained in
\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
, the fibres of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
have a well-defined Lie algebra structure. (Equivalently, one may define
{\operatorname{ad}}M
as the vector bundle associated to the
(\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N})
-principal bundle
\mathcal{P}\to B
via the representation (
8
).)

Let
\mathcal{V}:=\ker d\pi
be the vertical distribution on
M
. The key property of the adjoint bundle, which holds by construction, is that local sections of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
are in one-to-one correspondence with invariant sections of
\mathcal{V}
, i.e. vertical invariant vector fields on
M
. More generally, the same is true for sections of tensor products of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
and the corresponding invariant tensor fields on
M
.

Notice also that an isomorphism of twisted principal bundles
f:M_{1}\to M_{2}
maps vertical invariant fields onto vertical invariant fields, thus inducing a vector bundle map

f_{\operatorname{ad}}:{\operatorname{ad}}M_{1}\to{\operatorname{ad}}M_{2},

that preserves Lie algebra structure of the fibres.

There is a straightforward generalisation of the notion of principal connection to this setting:

Definition 2.5
.

A
connection
on a twisted principal bundle
\pi:M\to B
is an invariant distribution
\mathcal{H}\subset TM
complementary to
\mathcal{V}=\ker d\pi
.

Precisely as in the principal bundle setting, one may equivalently view a connection as the kernel of an invariant,
\mathcal{V}
-valued one-form on
M
. Its restriction to each trivialisation
U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}
is an
\mathfrak{n}
-valued one-form satisfying the usual properties of a principal connection.
In this article, connections will often arise as orthogonal complements of
\mathcal{V}\subset TM
with respect to some invariant metric.
Moreover, we will be mainly interested in
flat connections
: the case where
\mathcal{H}
is an integrable distribution.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a flat connection on
M
, and fix a fibre
M_{b}
and an identification
M_{b}\simeq\mathsf{N}
. By flatness, the horizontal lift of piecewise-smooth loops in
B
based at
b
is independent of the homotopy class of the loop, and thus gives rise to a well-defined monodromy representation

(9)

\rho_{\mathcal{H}}:\pi_{1}(B)\to\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\ltimes\mathsf{N}.

Clearly, the image of
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}
must contain
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))
, but there may also be a translational part. Throughout this article, we will be dealing mainly with flat connections whose monodromy equals the twisting homomorphism
\rho
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\Aut(\mathsf{N})_{\delta}\ltimes\mathsf{N})
be a homomorphism and set
\tilde{M}:=\tilde{B}\times\mathsf{N}
. The diagonal right action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
\tilde{M}
given by
(b,x)\cdot\gamma=(b\cdot\gamma,\rho_{\mathcal{H}}(\gamma^{-1})x)
is properly discontinuous and
M:=\tilde{M}/\pi_{1}(B)
is a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle over
B
, with twisting homomorphism
\rho:=q\circ\rho_{\mathcal{H}}
, where
q:\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
is the ‘quotient by
\mathsf{N}
’ projection. The trivial connection
T\tilde{B}
on
\tilde{M}
induces a connection
\mathcal{H}
on
M
with monodromy
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}
. It is not hard to see that in fact we have

M=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho_{\mathcal{H}}}\mathsf{N}.

We note that flat connections always exist when
\mathsf{N}
is contractible (in particular, for
\mathsf{N}
simply-connected and nilpotent):

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathsf{N}
be a contractible Lie group and let
M
be a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
–bundle with twisting homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
. Then,
there exists a flat connection
\mathcal{H}
on
M
with monodromy
\rho
. Moreover, given such a connection, there is an isomorphism of twisted principal bundles
M\simeq\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}\to B
be the principal
(\rho(\pi_{1}(B))_{\delta}\ltimes\mathsf{N})
-bundle to which
M
is associated. Since
(\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\ltimes\mathsf{N})/\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\simeq\mathsf{N}
is contractible by assumption, there is a reduction of the structure group of
\mathcal{P}
to
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))_{\delta}

[
38
]
*Ch. I, Prop. 5.6
\&
Thm. 5.7. Consider a bundle atlas for
\mathcal{P}
associated to a cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
B
, with locally constant transition functions taking values in
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))
. As
M
is the associated bundle, a bundle atlas for it is given by
\{U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}\}
, with the same transition functions. We notice that this data is also the data defining the associated bundle to the principal
\pi_{1}(B)
-bundle
\tilde{B}\to B
with respect to
\rho
. Hence,
M\simeq\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
.
∎

If
M\simeq\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
is as in
2.7
, the adjoint bundle satisfies
{\operatorname{ad}}M\simeq\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathfrak{n}
. It
inherits a flat connection whose holonomy is still
\rho
(under the identification
\Aut(\mathsf{N})\simeq\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
, assuming that
\mathsf{N}
is simply-connected).
From the principal bundle situation one can deduce the following important correspondence:

Proposition 2.8
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between:

(i)

Invariant vertical vector fields on
M=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
;

(ii)

Sections of
{\operatorname{ad}}M=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathfrak{n}
;

(iii)

\rho
-equivariant maps
s:\tilde{B}\to\mathfrak{n}
:

(10)

s(b\cdot\gamma)=\rho(\gamma^{-1})\cdot s(b),\qquad\gamma\in\pi_{1}(B),\quad b\in B.

Proof.

The equivalence between (i) and (ii) follows by the construction of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
, as mentioned above. The equivalence between (ii) and (iii) is a standard result on sections of a bundle (here
{\operatorname{ad}}M
) associated to a principal bundle (here, the universal cover
\tilde{B}\to B
), see e.g.
[
58
]
*18.12.
∎

Similarly, we have the analogous result for arbitrary tensor fields; we state it in the case of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors:

Proposition 2.9
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between:

(i)

Invariant, symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors on the vertical distribution
\mathcal{V}
of
M=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
;

(ii)

Sections of
\Sym^{2}({\operatorname{ad}}M)
;

(iii)

\rho
-equivariant maps
s:\tilde{B}\to\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
:

(11)

s(b\cdot\gamma)=\rho(\gamma^{-1})\cdot s(b),\qquad\gamma\in\pi_{1}(B),\quad b\in B.

The action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
is obtained from composing
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut(\mathfrak{n})\subset\GL(\mathfrak{n})
with the natural extension of the standard
\GL(\mathfrak{n})
-action on
\mathfrak{n}
to
\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
(
35
).

2.3.
Invariant metrics

Let
\pi:M\to B
be a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle. Recall that there is a well-defined notion of invariance for tensor fields on
M
. In particular, we may consider invariant Riemannian metrics on
M
. These have a simple description in terms of the pullback bundle
\tilde{M}\to\tilde{B}
, which is both a principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle over
\tilde{B}
, and a principal
(\pi_{1}(B)\ltimes_{\rho}\mathsf{N})
-bundle over
B
:

Proposition 2.10
.

A metric
g
on
M
is invariant if and only if its pullback
\tilde{g}
to
\tilde{M}
is
\pi_{1}(B)\ltimes_{\rho}\mathsf{N}
-invariant in the usual sense.

Proof.

Since
\tilde{g}
is the lift of
g
, and
\pi_{1}(B)
is the group of deck transformations of the covering
\tilde{M}\to M
, it is clear that
\tilde{g}
is
\pi_{1}(B)
-invariant, regardless of the symmetries of
g
. On the other hand, let
\{U_{i}\}
be a bundle atlas of
M
with constant transition functions into
\Aut(\mathsf{N})
, and let
\{\hat{U}_{i}\}
be the corresponding bundle atlas for
\tilde{M}
. Since the covering map
\hat{U}_{i}\to U_{i}
is
\mathsf{N}
-equivariant, the
\mathsf{N}
-action on
\hat{U}_{i}
is
\tilde{g}
-isometric if and only if the
\mathsf{N}
-action on
U_{i}
is
g
-isometric, and the proposition follows.
∎

Given an invariant metric
g
on
M
, there is a unique metric
g_{B}
on
B
such that
\pi:M\to B
is a Riemannian submersion. We also get a connection
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
, and an invariant bundle metric
g|_{\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}}
on the vertical distribution. By
2.9
we thus have:

Proposition 2.11
.

An invariant metric
g
on
M
is determined by the data
(g_{B},\mathcal{H},h)
, where:

(i)

g_{B}
is a Riemannian metric on the base
B
,

(ii)

\mathcal{H}=(\ker d\pi)^{\perp}
is a connection, and

(iii)

h
is a bundle metric on
{\operatorname{ad}}M
.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{H}
is flat with monodromy
\rho
, then it is determined by a flat affine connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{ad}}
on
{\operatorname{ad}}M
, and (iii) may be replaced by

(iii)’

h
is a
\rho
-equivariant map
h:\tilde{B}\to\sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
.

When writing that an invariant metric
g
is determined by the data
(g_{B},\nabla^{\operatorname{ad}},h)
for a flat connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{ad}}
, we will implicitly assume that the monodromy of
\mathcal{H}
is
\rho
.

The following may be considered as a parametrised version of Wilson’s theorem on the isometries of nilmanifolds
[
76
]
:

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\pi_{i}:M_{i}\to B_{i}
,
i=1,2
, be twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundles,
\mathsf{N}
nilpotent and simply-connected, with invariant metrics
g_{i}
determined by
(g_{B_{i}},\nabla^{{\operatorname{ad}},i},h_{i})
, where
\nabla^{{\operatorname{ad}},i}
are flat connections on
{\operatorname{ad}}M_{i}
. Let

f:(M_{1},g_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2})
be a fibre-preserving isometry covering a diffeomorphism
\check{f}:B_{1}\to B_{2}
. Then,
f
is a twisted principal bundle isomorphism, and:

(i)

\check{f}:(B_{1},g_{B_{1}})\to(B_{2},g_{B_{2}})
is an isometry;

(ii)

{f}_{\operatorname{ad}}:{\operatorname{ad}}M_{1}\to{\operatorname{ad}}M_{2}
covers
\check{f}
and satisfies

(12)

h_{1}={f}_{\operatorname{ad}}^{*}h_{2},\qquad\nabla^{{\rm ad},1}={f}_{\operatorname{ad}}^{*}\nabla^{{\rm ad},2}.

Conversely, a Lie algebra bundle isomorphism
{f}_{\operatorname{ad}}:{\operatorname{ad}}M_{1}\to{\operatorname{ad}}M_{2}
satisfying (i) and (ii) gives rise to a
fibre-preserving isometry
f:(M_{1},g_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2})
.

Proof.

Since
f
is a fibre-preserving isometry, we have

f_{*}(\mathcal{V}_{1})=\mathcal{V}_{2}
and
f_{*}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Also,
f
covers a diffeomorphism
\check{f}:B_{1}\to B_{2}
, which is isometric since for
p\in\pi_{1}^{-1}(b)\subset M_{1}
, its differential is a composition of local isometries:

d\check{f}_{b}=d\pi_{2}\circ df_{p}\circ d\pi_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}^{-1}.

Given
b\in B_{1}
, the restricted map
f|_{M_{b}}:M_{1,b}\to M_{2,\check{f}(b)}
is an isometry between fibres. Since the fibres admit simply-transitive isometric actions by a nilpotent Lie group,
[
76
]
implies that

f|_{M_{b}}:(M_{1,b},\nabla^{\mathcal{V}_{1},b})\to(M_{2,\check{f}(b)},\nabla^{\mathcal{V}_{2},\check{f}(b)})

is an affine diffeomorphism with respect to the canonical vertical connections (
Section

2.1
). Hence,
f
is an isomorphism of twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundles. It therefore preserves vertical invariant fields, thus giving rise to a vector bundle isomorphism
f_{\operatorname{ad}}:{\operatorname{ad}}M_{1}\to{\operatorname{ad}}M_{2}
which is of course a fibrewise isometry with respect to the bundle metrics
h_{1},h_{2}
. From
f_{*}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\mathcal{H}_{2}
we deduce that
f_{\operatorname{ad}}^{*}\nabla^{{\operatorname{ad}},2}=\nabla^{{\operatorname{ad}},1}
.

Conversely, suppose that we are given
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
and
\check{f}
satisfying (i) and (ii). By replacing
M_{2}
with
\check{f}^{*}M_{2}
, we may assume that
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
covers the identity. Hence, we can consider
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
as a section of
({\operatorname{ad}}M_{1})^{*}\otimes({\operatorname{ad}}M_{2})
. By (
12
),
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
is parallel with respect to the induced flat connection: hence, we can identify
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
with an element of
\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
such that
f_{\operatorname{ad}}\circ\rho_{1,*}(\gamma)=\rho_{2,*}(\gamma)\circ f_{\operatorname{ad}}
. Since
\mathsf{N}
is simply connected, we can integrate
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
to
f\in\Aut(\mathsf{N})
which satisfies
f\circ\rho_{1}(\gamma)=\rho_{2}(\gamma)\circ f
. Then, recalling the identification
\tilde{M}_{i}\simeq\tilde{B}\times\mathsf{N}
given by the flat connections, the map

\tilde{B}\times\mathsf{N}\to\tilde{B}\times\mathsf{N},\qquad(b,x)\mapsto(b,f(x)),

descends to a map
f:M_{1}\to M_{2}
. The map
f
is isometric since
f_{*}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
f_{*}(\mathcal{V}_{1})=\mathcal{V}_{2}
, it covers an isometry, and
f_{{\operatorname{ad}}}^{*}h_{2}=h_{1}
.
∎

2.4.
Ricci curvature of invariant metrics

In this section, we record (without proofs) the formulas for the Ricci curvature of an invariant metric on a twisted principal bundle, which follow from O’Neill’s formulas for the curvature of a Riemannian submersion. The presentation is based on
[
11
]
(see also
[
7
]
*Chpt. 9 or
[
62
]
Prop. 8.1,), but contains some additional remarks that are new to this paper.

Throughout this section,
U
will denote a local section of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
,
\hat{U}
the corresponding invariant vertical vector field on
M
. Since the fibres of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
have a natural Lie algebra structure,
{\operatorname{ad}}U:=[U,\cdot]\in\Der({\operatorname{ad}}M)
will denote, as usual, the (fibrewise) adjoint representation.
We will also write
X,Y
for vector fields on
B
, and
\bar{X},\bar{Y}
for their corresponding horizontal lifts to
M
.

An invariant metric
g
on
M
is determined by a triple
(g_{B},\mathcal{H},h)
as per
2.11
, and
\pi:M\to B
is a Riemannian submersion. The curvature of
\nabla^{\operatorname{ad}}

is O’Neill’s
A
-tensor, which we view as an
{\operatorname{ad}}M
-valued
2
-form on
B
: for
X,Y\in TB
,
A_{X,Y}\in\Gamma({\operatorname{ad}}M)
corresponds to the invariant vertical field
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}[\bar{X},\bar{Y}]
. Naturally,
A=0
if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is an integrable distribution. For each
U\in\Gamma({\operatorname{ad}}M)
, we also set
AU
to be the (
\mathbb{R}
-valued)
2
-form on
B
given by
(X,Y)\mapsto h(A_{X,Y},U)
.

For each
X\in TB
, we denote by
L_{X}
the shape operator of the fibres in
M
, viewed as a
h
-self-adjoint endomorphism of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
. It is implicitly defined by

(13)

h(L_{X}U,V)=g(\nabla_{\bar{X}}\hat{U},\hat{V}).

The fibres’ mean curvature vector
\bar{\mathrm{H}}
is the basic horizontal vector field given by the trace of the second fundamental form after raising an index. The corresponding vector field
\mathrm{H}
on
B
satisfies
g_{B}(\mathrm{H},X)=-\tr L_{X}
, and is thus given in terms of any orthonormal frame
\{X_{j}\}
for
B
by

\mathrm{H}=-\sum_{j=1}^{\dim B}(\tr L_{X_{j}})\cdot X_{j}.

The fibre’s Ricci curvature is an invariant section of
\Sym^{2}(\mathcal{V}^{*})
, and it thus corresponds to a section
\Ric_{h}
of
\Sym^{2}({\operatorname{ad}}M)^{*}
.

Following
[
17
]
, we also make the following definition:

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\nabla^{\rm ad}
,
h
be a connection and bundle metric on
{\operatorname{ad}}M
, and let
g_{B}
be a metric on
B
. The
tension field
of
h
is the section
\tau(h)\in\Gamma\sym^{2}({\operatorname{ad}}M)^{*}
defined by

(14)

\tau(h)=-2h(\nabla^{+,*}\theta\cdot,\cdot),

where
\nabla^{\rm ad}=\nabla^{+}+\theta
is the (unique) decomposition of
\nabla^{\rm ad}
into a connection
\nabla^{+}
preserving
h
, and a
h
-self adjoint
\End({\operatorname{ad}}M)
-valued one-form
\theta
(cf.
Lemma

2.15
).

In Definition
2.13
, we use the Levi-Civita connection to extend the covariant derivative
\nabla^{+}
to act on
\End({\operatorname{ad}}M)
-valued tensors. Moreover,
\nabla^{+,*}
denotes the formal adjoint of
\nabla^{+}
with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product on
\End({\operatorname{ad}}M)
-valued tensors induced by
h
and
g_{B}
. On
\End({\operatorname{ad}}M)
-valued one-forms, it is given by

(15)

\nabla^{+,*}\theta=-\sum_{i=1}^{\dim B}(\nabla^{+}_{\bar{X}_{i}}\theta)_{\bar{X}_{i}}.

The Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
can then be expressed in terms of the Ricci curvature of
(B,g_{B})
, the Ricci curvature of the fibres (with the induced metric), and the tensors
A
and
T
; see
[
7
]
or
[
11
]
*Theorem 2.2.

Proposition 2.14
.

If
g
is an invariant metric on a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle
\pi:M^{n}\to B^{d}
, with
\mathsf{N}
unimodular, then,

(16a)

\displaystyle\Ric_{g}(\hat{U},\hat{U})

\displaystyle=\Ric_{h}({U},{U})+|AU|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\tau(h)({U},{U})+h(L_{{\mathrm{H}}}U,U),

(16b)

\displaystyle\Ric_{g}(\bar{X},\bar{X})

\displaystyle=\Ric_{g_{B}}({X},X)-|L_{X}|^{2}-2\,|A_{X}|^{2}+g_{B}(\nabla_{X}{{\mathrm{H}}},X).

Furthermore, when
A=0
, we view
h:\tilde{B}\to\Sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
, and it holds that

(17a)

\displaystyle\Ric_{g}(\hat{U},\bar{X})

\displaystyle=\tr\big({\operatorname{ad}}{U}L_{{X}}\big),

(17b)

\displaystyle\Ric_{g}(\bar{X},\bar{X})

\displaystyle=\Ric_{g_{B}}({X},X)-h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}(\tilde{X},\tilde{X})+g_{B}(\nabla_{X}{{\mathrm{H}}},X).

Here
\tilde{X}
denotes the lift of
X
to
\tilde{B}
, and

is the symmetric metric on

3.
The twisted harmonic-Einstein equations

As mentioned before, our original motivation is to produce new examples of invariant expanding Ricci solitons on twisted principal

-bundles, where

Let

be a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
–bundle with

nilpotent and simply-connected, with twisting homomorphism
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\Aut(\mathsf{N}))

on
\mathsf{N}
, let

be the corresponding density, and denote by

the subgroup of automorphisms of
\mathsf{N}
preserving

are determined by the data

is a metric on

,
\mathcal{H}
is a connection on

, and

is a bundle metric on

(

the affine connection on

Ansatz 3.1
.

For a pair
(g,X)
of an invariant metric

corresponding to

and a (not necessarily invariant) vector field

on

, we assume:

(i)

\mathcal{H}
is flat, its holonomy is
\rho
, and

(ii)

The fibres of

, and
\nabla^{\operatorname{ad}}({h}^{-1}\Ric_{h})=0

(iii)

Some comments are in order. Firstly, thanks to

, condition

gives an isomorphism

,
and by

the bundle metric becomes a

-equivariant map

The restriction on the image of

further ensures the existence of a globally-defined,

-parallel density

on the fibres of
{\operatorname{ad}}M

Next, when lifted to

is of the form

\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}_{B}+h,

and by

is that the Ricci endomorphism of the fibres is independent of the

-coordinate.
See
Appendix

for further details about nilsolitons.

Finally, in analogy with the homogeneous case (see

]
*
\lx@sectionsign
2 and the proof of

), the vector field

can be obtained from the nilsoliton derivation field

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D\in\Gamma\Der({\operatorname{ad}}M)
be a the derivation field corresponding to an invariant endomorphism
D_{\mathcal{V}}\in\End(\mathcal{V})^{\rm inv}
, and let
g
be an invariant metric on

defining a flat connection
\mathcal{H}
with monodromy

.
Then, there exists a vertical vector field
X_{D}\in\Gamma(TM)
so that

\mathcal{L}_{X_{D}}g=g(D_{M}\cdot,\cdot)+g(\cdot,D_{M}\cdot),

where

is given according to
TM=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{H}
by

D_{M}:=\begin{pmatrix}D_{\mathcal{V}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

The idea is to use local trivialisations, and argue as in
[
51
]
*
\lx@sectionsign
2 fibrewise. The only thing to be checked is that the locally-defined objects behave well with respect to the transition maps, so that they glue to define a global object.

More precisely, consider a bundle atlas
\{U_{i}\times\mathsf{N}\}_{i\in I}
for
M
adapted to
\mathcal{H}
, with locally constant transition functions
\tau_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\subset\Aut(\mathsf{N})
. Since
\mathsf{N}
is simply-connected, there is a canonical Lie group isomorphism
\Aut(\mathsf{N})\simeq\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
,
\varphi\mapsto\underline{\varphi}
, and this defines a bundle atlas
\{U_{i}\times\mathfrak{n}\}_{i\in I}
for
{\operatorname{ad}}M
, with transition functions
\underline{\tau}_{ij}
. In each trivialisation

, the derivation field

becomes
D_{i}:U_{i}\to\Der(\mathfrak{n})
. For each

, and under a change of trivialisation these change via

Let

be defined by
\underline{\phi}_{t}^{i}=\exp(tD_{i})
. Clearly, these transform via

\phi_{t}^{i}=\tau_{ij}\circ\phi_{t}^{j}\circ\tau_{ij}^{-1},

and thus they define a one-parameter group

of diffeomorphisms of

(that are also twisted principal bundle isomorphisms). They generate a vector field

. Computing fibrewise in a local trivialisation as in
[
51
]
*p. 3 we deduce the claimed formula for the Lie derivative (we notice that there is a sign error in the formula from
[

]
, which does not affect the results of that paper whatsoever).
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Two solutions
(\check{g}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1}),(\check{g}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})
of the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations for the pair
(B,\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
are called

\check{f}\in\Diff(B)
and
\varphi\in\mathsf{G}
such that

(23)

\check{f}^{*}\check{g}_{2}=\check{g}_{1},\qquad C_{\varphi}\circ\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}\circ\check{f}_{*},\qquad\varphi\cdot h_{1}=h_{2}\circ\check{f},

where
\check{f}_{*}:\pi_{1}(B)\to\pi_{1}(B)
is the induced action on the fundamental group and
C_{\varphi}(q)=\varphi q\varphi^{-1}
for
q\in\mathsf{G}
.

The significance of this equivalence notion relies on the following

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
(resp.
(M_{2},g_{2})
) be a Ricci soliton obtained from a solution
(\check{g}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
(resp.
(\check{g}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})
) of the twisted harmonic-Einstein equation as in
3.4
. Then, there exists an isometric bundle map between
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
if and only if
(\check{g}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
is equivalent to
(\check{g}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})
.

Proof.

By
2.12
, an isometric bundle map between
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
is equivalently described by an isometry
\check{f}:(B_{1},\check{g}_{1})\to(B_{2},\check{g}_{2})
and a Lie algebra bundle isomorphism
f_{\operatorname{ad}}:{\operatorname{ad}}M_{1}\to{\operatorname{ad}}M_{2}
such that

h_{1}=f_{\operatorname{ad}}^{*}h_{2},\qquad\nabla^{{\operatorname{ad}},1}=f_{\operatorname{ad}}^{*}\nabla^{{\operatorname{ad}},2}.

After choosing trivialisations we obtain Lie algebra isomorphisms
({\operatorname{ad}}M_{1})_{b}\simeq\mathfrak{n}\simeq({\operatorname{ad}}M_{2})_{\check{f}(b)}
for some given point
b\in B
. Using parallel transport, the map
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
is in turn completely determined by what it does at the fibre over
b
, and this action is simply by an automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
. The condition
f_{\operatorname{ad}}^{*}h_{2}=h_{1}
translates into
\varphi\cdot h_{1}=h_{2}
. The fibre isomorphisms with
\mathfrak{n}
also yield explicit holonomy representations
\rho_{1}:\pi_{1}(B,b)\to\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
and
\rho_{2}:\pi_{1}(B,\check{f}(b))\to\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
, and it is easy to check that
\varphi
must satisfy the compatibility condition
\varphi\rho_{1}([\gamma])\varphi^{-1}=\rho_{2}([\check{f}\circ\gamma])
for all
[\gamma]\in\pi_{1}(B,b)
.

Suppose now that two triples are equivalent as per
23
. Then, the above observations imply that the corresponding solitons are isometric via a bundle map.

Conversely, suppose that there is an isometric bundle map determined by
\check{f}
and
\varphi
as above. Since
f_{\operatorname{ad}}
is fibrewise an isometry of metric Lie algebras, it intertwines the fibres’ Ricci endomorphisms, thus
\varphi
commutes with
\beta
. It also preserves the parallel density
\nu_{\operatorname{ad}}
, thus
\det\varphi=\pm 1
.
∎

We will now describe three special cases of the harmonic-Einstein equations on twisted principal bundles.

3.1.
Invariant expanding Ricci solitons on principal bundles

To justify the need to deal with twisted principal bundles, we now show that, if

only yields Riemannian products (cf. the Splitting Conjecture for negative Einstein manifolds with symmetry
[

]
):

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(M,g,X)
be an expanding Ricci soliton obtained from a solution
(g_{B},\rho,h)
to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
20
) with trivial

.
Then,
(M,g)
is isometric to the Riemannian product
(B,g_{B})\times(\mathsf{N},\bar{h})
of a compact Einstein manifold and a nilsoliton.

Proof.

Since the action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
\mathfrak{n}
is trivial,

h:\tilde{B}\to\sym_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
is
\pi_{1}(B)
–invariant.
Hence,
h
descends to a harmonic map
\check{h}:(B,g_{B})\to(\sym_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}),g_{\mathsf{sym}})
, which must necessarily be constant
[
20
]
*Paragraph 4.5. It follows that

is a Riemannian product. Moreover, from (

),
\tilde{g}_{B}
is Einstein, and so is
g_{B}
.
∎

3.2.
Totally geodesic vertical metrics

A class of solutions to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations are given by triples
(g_{B},\rho,h)
where

is not just harmonic, but totally geodesic:
\nabla dh\equiv 0
. These appear to be a natural starting point, since symmetric spaces contain a large number of totally geodesic manifolds given algebraically by Lie triple systems
[
29
]
*§ IV.7. However, they give rise to known examples.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(M,g,X)
be an expanding Ricci soliton obtained from a solution
(g_{B},\rho,h)
to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
20
) with totally geodesic

.
Then, the universal cover
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
is the Riemannian product of a homogeneous Ricci soliton and an Einstein manifold.

Proof.

To begin with, assume that
h
is an immersion. Observe that
h
is necessarily an injective immersion: if
h_{b_{1}}=h_{b_{2}}
then
h
sends any geodesic in
\tilde{B}
that joints
b_{1}
to
b_{2}
to a geodesic in
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
that starts and ends at the same point. Since
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is simply connected with non-positive curvature, the only way this can occur is if
b_{1}=b_{2}
. Hence, we may identify
\tilde{B}
with its image, a totally geodesic submanifold of
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
.

We claim that
\tilde{B}\subset\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is complete in the induced metric
h^{*}g_{\rm sym}
. Indeed, let
\tilde{B}=\tilde{B}_{0}\times\cdots\times\tilde{B}_{r}
be the de Rham decomposition
[
38
]
*Thm. IV.6.2 of
\tilde{B}
(for the metric
g_{B}
), where
\tilde{B}_{0}
is a Euclidean space and the holonomy representation of
(\tilde{B}_{i},g_{i})
is irreducible for
i>0
. (Here
g_{i}=\tilde{g}_{B}|_{\tilde{B}_{i}}
.) Notice that the decomposition
T\tilde{B}=T\tilde{B}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus T\tilde{B}_{r}
is orthogonal for

by (
20b
). Moreover, on each
\tilde{B}_{i}
,
i>0
, we have
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}=c_{i}g_{B}
for some
c_{i}>0
since
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
is parallel and the holonomy acts irreducibly on
T\tilde{B}_{i}
. The restriction of
h^{*}{g}_{\mathsf{sym}}
to
\tilde{B}_{0}
is a Euclidean metric, so up to isometry we may assume that
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
agrees with
\tilde{g}_{0}
on
\tilde{B}_{0}
. Hence,
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
is complete, being the Riemannian product of complete metrics.

Therefore, by
[
29
]
*Thm. IV.7.2,
\tilde{B}\subset\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is the orbit of a connected reductive subgroup
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}\leq\mathsf{G}
.
Since
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}
is connected, it preserves the irreducible components of
\tilde{B}
. We conclude that
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}
also acts by isometries of
g_{B}
. Since
\mathsf{G}
consists of automorphisms of
\mathsf{N}
, we can form the semi-direct product
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathsf{N}
. This acts naturally on
\tilde{M}\simeq\tilde{B}\times\mathsf{N}
by

(\varphi,n)\cdot(b,x)=(\varphi\cdot b,\varphi(n\cdot x)).

Clearly this action is transitive. It is also isometric since

4.
The equations over a Riemann surface

In this section, we consider the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
4
) for a pair
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
, where
B^{2}
is a compact orientable surface, and
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}
is an arbitrary symmetric space of non-positive curvature. If
c=\{e^{2u}\check{g}:u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(B)\}
is a conformal class of metrics on
B^{2}
, then the pair
(B^{2},c)
is a
Riemann surface
.

Let
(B^{2},\check{g})
,
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds and
h:(B^{2},\check{g})\to(N,g_{N})
a smooth map. Since the source is a surface, if
h
is harmonic with respect to
\check{g}
, then
h
is also harmonic with respect to any metric conformal to
\check{g}
. Therefore, it makes sense to talk about harmonic maps from a Riemann surface
(B^{2},c)
. A map
h:(B^{2},c)\to(N,g_{N})
is called
weakly conformal
if

h^{*}g_{N}=\nu\check{g},

for some (and hence any) metric
\check{g}\in c
and some smooth non-negative function
\nu:B^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
. We emphasise that
\nu
can have zeros; for example, any constant map is weakly conformal, with
\nu\equiv 0
.

Example 4.1
.

Recall that (after fixing an orientation), a Riemann surface admits a holomorphic atlas. If
\dim N=2
, then a map
h:(B^{2},c)\to(N^{2},g_{N})
is weakly conformal if and only if
h
is holomorphic or anti-holomorphic.

Suppose now that
\mathsf{G}
is a real reductive Lie group with maximal compact subgroup
\mathsf{K}\leq\mathsf{G}
. If
(g_{B},\rho,h)
solves the twisted harmonic-Einstein equation (
4
) for the pair
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
, then
h
is necessarily weakly conformal by (
20b
). Indeed,
\Ric(g_{B})=K(g_{B})g_{B}
, where
K(g_{B})
denotes the Gauss curvature. This suggests the following decoupling of the twisted harmonic-Einstein equation:

(1)

solve for
c
and
h
such that
h:(\tilde{B},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
is weakly conformal, harmonic and
\rho
-equivariant;

(2)

solve the base equation (
20b
) within the conformal class
c
.

In fact, given a conformal structure on
B^{2}
, the existence of a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map can be characterised purely in terms of
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
:

Theorem 4.2

(Corlette, Donaldson, Labourie,
[
17
,
19
,
40
]
)
.

Given
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
, there exists a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map
h_{c}:(\tilde{B},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
if and only if
\rho
is reductive. Moreover,
h_{c}
is unique up to left multiplication by elements
g\in\mathsf{G}
that centralise
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))
.

A representation
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
is called
reductive
if
\Ad\circ\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{Gl}(\mathfrak{g}))

decomposes as a sum of irreducible representations. If
\mathsf{G}
is algebraic, this is equivalent to the Zariski closure of
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}
being reductive. We denote the set of reductive representations by
\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
. By
Theorem

4.2
, given
\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
, the first step in the above decoupling reduces to finding a conformal structure
c
for which the harmonic map
h_{c}
is also weakly conformal. Notice that this holds for some
h_{c}
if and only if it holds for any, since the action of
\mathsf{G}
on
(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\rm sym})
is by isometries. It turns out that this condition is also sufficient:

Theorem 4.3
.

If
h:(\tilde{B}^{2},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
is a harmonic,
\rho
-equivariant map, then it is possible to solve (
20b
) for
g_{B}\in c
if and only if
h
is weakly conformal. Moreover, when it exists, such a solution is unique.

Proof.

We have already observed the necessity of
h
being weakly conformal, so let us focus on suffiency. Let
\check{g}\in c
be the hyperbolic metric and assume that
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}=\nu\check{g}
for some function
\nu\geq 0
. Since
h
is
\rho
-equivariant and
g_{\mathsf{sym}}
is
\mathsf{G}
-invariant, the pullback
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
is
\pi_{1}(B)
-invariant. Thus,
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
is the pullback of a tensor on
B^{2}
via the covering map. We will abuse notation and use the same symbol for the tensor on
B^{2}
that pulls back to
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
.

We must show that (
20b
) holds for some
g_{B}\in c
. Any metric in
g_{B}\in c
can be written as
g_{B}=e^{2u}\check{g}
for some
u:B\to\mathbb{R}
. The Gauss curvature of
g_{B}
is

K(g_{B})=e^{-2u}(-\Delta_{\bar{g}}u-1).

Hence, (
20b
) reduces to the scalar equation

(24)

\Delta_{\check{g}}u=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2u}-1-\nu.

To solve (
24
), consider the functional
I:H^{1}(B)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

I[u]=\int_{B}|du|^{2}-u\big(1+\nu\big)\,{\rm dvol}_{\check{g}}.

We minimise this subject to the Gauss-Bonnet constraint

(25)

2\pi\chi(B)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}e^{2u}\,{\rm dvol}_{\check{g}}=\int_{B}\nu\,{\rm dvol}_{\check{g}}

Observe that the set of functions
u:B\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
25
) is non-empty since
\chi(B)<0
. It then follows, in the exact same way as for the prescribed Gauss curvature equation, that such a minimiser exists and, moreover, is a weak solution of (
20b
); see
[
71
]
*Ch. 14
\lx@sectionsign
2. Elliptic regularity then shows that the weak solution is a classical solution and is smooth. Conformally related metrics on surfaces have the same Ricci curvature if and only if (the logarithm of) their conformal factor is harmonic; since
B^{2}
is compact, any harmonic function is constant. This constant must be 1 by (
20b
), so the solution is unique.
∎

where

[

*Thm. 5.2.1 for reductive representations

from Theorem

*Theorem 1.2.

If

In the twisted setting, Labourie

4.1.
Higgs Bundles

By
Theorem

4.3
, solving the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations over a surface reduces to identifying pairs
(c,\rho)
, where

c
is a conformal structure on
B^{2}
and

\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
, such that the
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map
h:(\tilde{B},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
is also weakly conformal. In order to do that, we recall the non-abelian Hodge correspondence, which identifies
\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})/\mathsf{G}
with the moduli space of polystable
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundles
.

In this subsection,
\mathsf{G}
will denote a connected linear real reductive group. We fix a faithful representation
\mathsf{G}\leq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathsf{G}
is closed under transposition. This gives the Cartan decomposition

\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{K}\exp(\mathfrak{p}),

where
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{G}\cap\SO(n)
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}\cap\sym(n)
. We follow
[
3
]
*§2 for the background we need regarding
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundles.

Definition 4.4
.

A
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundle is a pair,
(P,\Phi)
, where
P\to B^{2}
is a holomorphic
\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}
-principal bundle and
\Phi
is a holomorphic section of the associated bundle
K_{B}\otimes({\operatorname{ad}}_{P}(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}))
.

The Hodge star on one-forms is a conformal invariant and hence makes sense on a Riemann surface
(B^{2},c)
. Since it squares to
-\id_{{T^{*}\!}B}
, the complexified cotangent bundle
T^{*}B\otimes\mathbb{C}
splits into
\pm i
-eigenbundles. The
canonical bundle
, which we denote by
K_{B}
, is the
+i
-eigenspace. The vector bundle
{\operatorname{ad}}_{P}(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}})=P\times_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}
is associated to
P
via the adjoint representation of
\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}
. In a local complex coordinate
z
, the holomorphic sections of
K_{B}
are given by
fdz
, where
f
is a holomorphic function. Thus, in local coordinates, a holomorphic section of
K_{B}\otimes({\operatorname{ad}}_{P}(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}))
is given by
A\,dz
, where
A
is a holomorphic map into
\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Example 4.5

(
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles,
[
3
]
*Def. 2.4)
.

We claim that an
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle
(P,\Phi)
can be described equivalently in terms of a
Higgs vector bundle
: a pair
(E,\Phi)
where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle with
\Lambda^{n}E\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
and
\Phi:E\to K_{B}\otimes E
is a trace-free endomorphism twisted by
K_{B}
.

Indeed, given a holomorphic
\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}=\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle
P
, we can consider the associated vector bundle
E=P\times_{\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})}\mathbb{C}^{n}
corresponding to
the defining representation. Notice that
\Lambda^{n}E\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
is holomorphically trivial. Conversely, given a holomorphic vector bundle with
\Lambda^{n}E
holomorphically trivial, its bundle of frames preserving a holomorphic volume form is a holomorphic principal
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle
P
. Since the adjoint representation of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the restriction of the tensor product representation to the subspace
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
, there is a bundle isomorphism
P\times_{\Ad}\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\End_{0}(E)
, where
\End_{0}(E)
is the bundle of trace-free endomorphisms of
E
. Under this isomoprhism, the Higgs field corresponds to a
K_{B}
-twisted endomorphism.

If
\mathsf{G}\leq\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is linear, then we can still associate a Higgs vector bundle to any
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundle. We find that Higgs vector bundle are conceptually easier to work with since they are linear objects. Because of this, we will also adopt the linear algebra notation for
\Phi
(e.g.
\tr\Phi=0
).

Example 4.6

(
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles,
[
31
]
*§10)
.

For
\mathsf{G}=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}=\SO(2,\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Let
P
be a holomorphic principal
\SO(2,\mathbb{C})
-bundle. The group
\mathbb{C}^{*}
embeds into
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by
a\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})
. For this embedding, we have

\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{pmatrix}:\ b,c\in\mathbb{C}\bigg\}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{\otimes 2}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\otimes 2}\ \ \text{as }\ \mathbb{C}^{*}\text{-modules}.

Here,
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{\otimes k}
by the tensor product representation induced from the standard representation of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
\mathbb{C}
(i.e. if we identify
\mathbb{C}^{\otimes k}
with
\mathbb{C}
as a vector space, then
a\cdot v=a^{k}v
for
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\,v\in\mathbb{C}^{\otimes k}
).
Then,

K_{B}\otimes(P\times_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}})\simeq K_{B}L^{2}\oplus K_{B}L^{-2},

where
L
is the line bundle associated to
P
via the standard representation of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
\mathbb{C}
. Thus, an
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle is a triple
(L,\beta,\gamma)
where
L
is a holomorphic line bundle,
\beta\in H^{0}(B,K_{B}L^{2})
, and
\gamma\in H^{0}(B,K_{B}L^{-2})
.

Recall that the degree of a complex vector bundle is given by

\deg E=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{B}\tr\Omega\,\in\,\mathbb{Z},

where
\Omega
is the curvature of any connection on
E
. An
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle is called
semistable
if the associated vector bundle
E
satisfies

\deg E^{\prime}\leq 0,

for all
\Phi
-invariant subbundles
E^{\prime}\subset E
. (Here,
\Phi
-invariant means
\Phi(E^{\prime})\subset K_{B}\otimes E^{\prime}
.) An
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle is
stable
if the inequality is strict for all
E^{\prime}
, and it is
polystable
if it is semistable and it splits as a direct sum of stable bundles (all of degree
0
).

There is a more general notion of polystability for
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundles that makes the non-abelian Hodge correspondence true for general connected real reductive Lie groups; see
[
23
]
*Definition 2.9. However, it turns out that for linear groups, this is equivalent
[
3
]
*Proposition 2.10 to polystability for Higgs vector bundles defined above:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a linear real reductive Lie group. A
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundle
(P,\Phi)
is polystable if and only if the associated Higgs vector bundle is polystable.

We then have the following non-abelian Hodge correspondence. This was proved by Hitchin
[

]
and Donaldson
[

]
for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})

[

]

[

for connected real reductive groups.

Theorem 4.8

(Non-abelian Hodge correspondence)
.

For each conformal structure
c
on
B^{2}
, there is a homeomorphism

\mathcal{M}(B,\mathsf{G})\simeq\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})/\mathsf{G},

where
\mathcal{M}(B,\mathsf{G})
is the space of
\mathsf{K}^{\mathbb{C}}
-gauge equivalence classes of polystable
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundles.

We note that this correspondence is
\Aut(B^{2},c)
-equivariant. Here,
\Aut(B^{2},c)
acts on
\mathcal{M}(B,\mathsf{G})
by pull-back, and on
\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})/\mathsf{G}
by pre-composition with the induced action on the fundamental group.

In order to explain the gauge-invariant condition on Higgs bundles that detects conformality of
\rho
-equivariant harmonic maps, we describe briefly how a reductive representation
\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
gives rise to a Higgs bundle.

Given
\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
, let
h:(\tilde{B},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
be a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map (which exists by Theorem
4.2
).
Let
E_{\mathbb{R}}=\tilde{B}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the flat vector bundle associated to
\rho
(recall that
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}\leq\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
), and
E=E_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
its complexification. Then
h:\tilde{B}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SO(n)
defines a bundle metric on
E_{\mathbb{R}}
, and its differential
dh
defines a section
h^{-1}dh\in\Omega^{1}(B,\End_{0}(E_{\mathbb{R}}))
. The Higgs field
\Phi
is the
(1,0)
-part of the complex-linear extension
h^{-1}dh^{\mathbb{C}}\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}(B,\End_{0}(E))
. Since
h
is harmonic, the bundle
E
admits a holomorphic structure such that
\Phi
is a holomoprhic section of
K_{B}\otimes\End(E)
(cf.
[
3
]
*Appendix A). From this, we obtain the following lemma which is well-known to experts.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
be a reductive representation,
h:(\tilde{B},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map. If
(P,\Phi)
is a
\mathsf{G}
-Higgs bundle representing the gauge equivalence class corresponding to
\rho
under the non-abelian Hodge correspondence, then
\tr\Phi^{2}=0\in H^{0}(B,K_{B}^{2})
if and only if
h
is weakly conformal.

Proof.

Recall that the complexified cotangent bundle can be decomposed as
T^{*}\!B^{\mathbb{C}}=T^{*}\!B\otimes\mathbb{C}=K_{B}\oplus\bar{K}_{B}
, where
K_{B}
is the canonical bundle. There is a corresponding decomposition of
\Sym^{2}(T^{*}B)^{\mathbb{C}}
as
K_{B}^{2}\oplus K_{B}\bar{K}_{B}\oplus\bar{K}_{B}^{2}
.
Given a real symmetric tensor
\alpha\in\Sym^{2}(T^{*}B)
, let
\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}=\alpha^{2,0}+\alpha^{1,1}+\alpha^{0,2}
be the decomposition of its complex-linear extension into these components. Then,
\alpha
is conformal to
\check{g}\in c
if and only if
\alpha=\alpha^{1,1}
. Since
\alpha
is real,
\overline{\alpha^{2,0}}=\alpha^{0,2}
, hence
\alpha=\alpha^{1,1}
if and only if
\alpha^{2,0}=0
.

Thus, the lemma will follow if we show that
\tr\Phi^{2}=(h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}^{\mathbb{C}})^{2,0}
. But the complexification of
h^{-1}dh
is
h^{-1}dh^{\mathbb{C}}=\Phi+\Phi^{*}
, so the decomposition of
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}^{\mathbb{C}}
is

h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}^{\mathbb{C}}=\tr\Phi^{2}+\tr\Phi\Phi^{*}+\tr(\Phi^{*})^{2},

since
g_{\mathsf{sym}}(A,A)=\tr A^{2}
is the restriction of the Killing form. This proves the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem B.

Fix a conformal class
c
in
B^{2}
. Notice that the map
(P,\Phi)\mapsto\tr\Phi^{2}
is gauge invariant, so it makes sense to define the closed subset

\mathcal{N}=\{[(P,\Phi)]:\tr\Phi^{2}=0\}\subset\mathcal{M}(B,\mathsf{G}).

Notice also that
\Aut(B^{2},c)
acts on
\mathcal{N}
by pull-back. We also let
\mathcal{HE}
denote the set of solutions
(g_{B},\rho,h)
to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations with
g_{B}\in c
, up to equivalence (
Definition

3.5
).

We first define a map
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{HE}
which is invariant under the action of
\Aut(B^{2},c)
on
\mathcal{N}
.
By
Theorem

4.8
, a given
[(P,\Phi)]\in\mathcal{N}
corresponds to some
\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
which is well-defined only up to conjugation by
\varphi\in\mathsf{G}
. By
Theorem

4.2
, there exists a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map
h:(\tilde{B},c)\to(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K},g_{\mathsf{sym}})
, which is weakly conformal thanks to
Lemma

4.9
.
Theorem

4.3
yields the existence of a unique metric
g_{B}\in c
such that (
20b
) holds, and hence
(g_{B},\rho,h)\in\mathcal{HE}
. If we had a different representative
C_{\varphi}\circ\rho\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
,
\varphi\in\mathsf{G}
, then the corresponding triple would clearly be
(g_{B},C_{\varphi}\circ\rho,\varphi\cdot h)\sim(g_{B},\rho,h)
. On the other hand, given
\check{f}\in\Aut(B^{2},c)
, then
\check{f}^{*}[(P,\Phi)]
corresponds to
\rho\circ\check{f}_{*}\in\Hom^{\mathsf{red}}(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
. The triple associated to
\rho\circ\check{f}_{*}
is clearly
(\check{f}^{*}g_{B},\rho\circ\check{f}_{*},h\circ\check{f})
, which is also equivalent to
(g_{B},\rho,h)
.

Conversely, if
(g_{B},\rho,h)\in\mathcal{HE}
,
then Theorem
4.2
implies
\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(B),\mathsf{G})
is reductive, so the non-abelian Hodge correspondence gives
[(P,\Phi)]\in\mathcal{M}(B,\mathsf{G})
. Since
h
is weakly conformal by (
20b
), Lemma
4.9
yields
\tr\Phi^{2}=0
, so
[(P,\Phi)]\in\mathcal{N}
. Finally, if
(g_{B}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},h^{\prime})=(\check{f}^{*}g_{B},C_{\varphi}\circ\rho\circ\check{f}_{*},\varphi\cdot h\circ\check{f})\sim(g_{B},\rho,h)
, then
\rho^{\prime}=C_{\varphi}\circ\rho\circ\check{f}_{*}
defines the same conjugacy class as
\rho
(up to pull-back), so
(g^{\prime}_{B},\rho^{\prime},h^{\prime})
defines the same equivalence class
\check{f}^{*}[(P,\Phi)]\in\mathcal{N}
as
(g_{B},\rho,h)
.
∎

4.2.
Solitons over Riemann surfaces: Proof of
C

In this subsection we briefly return to considering Ricci solitons constructed using Ansatz
3.1
via Proposition
3.4
. Our main aim is to prove Corollary
C
. We also prove a necessary condition for solitons satisfying Ansatz
3.1
to be homogeneous.

Let
\Sigma=(B^{2},c)
be a Riemann surface, with
\chi(B^{2})<0
. Combining Theorem
B
with Proposition
3.4
, we can associate a Ricci soliton to a polystable Higgs bundle. Notice that we must make several choices for this association, but different choices lead to isometric solitons. Moreover, changing the Higgs bundle by a gauge transformation also does not change the isometry type of the soliton.

Theorem
B
has the following drawback for producing examples of solitons from Ansatz
3.1
: if
\mathsf{G}
is the reductive group associated to a nilsoliton
(\mathsf{N},\bar{h})
, then we must apply Theorem
B
to Higgs bundles with
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-Higgs bundles since the non-abelian Hodge correspondence (cf. Theorem
4.8
) is currently only known for connected groups. Hence, we only produce solitons
(M,g)
such that
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}^{0}
, where
\rho
is the twisting homomorphism of
\pi:M\to B
. However, this is not a major drawback since we do produce everything up to taking a finite cover. Indeed, the cover
\hat{B}\to B
such that
\pi_{1}(\hat{B})=\ker\bar{\rho}
, where
\bar{\rho}
is the composition of
\rho
with the projection
\mathsf{G}\to\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{G}^{0}
, is finite and the twisting homomorphism of the pullback of
M
to
\hat{B}
maps into
\mathsf{G}^{0}
. (The cover is finite since the deck transformations inject into the finite group
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{G}^{0}=\mathsf{K}/\mathsf{K}^{0}
.)

By Theorem
B
, gauge equivalence classes of
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-Higgs bundles are in one-to-one correspondence with solutions of the twisted harmonic-Einstein equation for the pair
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}^{0}/\mathsf{K}^{0})
up to equivalence. Moreover, solutions of the twisted harmonic-Einstein equation for the pair
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
are in one-to-one correspondence with solitons by
3.6
. Hence, to prove Corollary
C
it suffices to show the following:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\Sigma=(B^{2},c)
be a Riemann surface,
\mathsf{G}
a reductive group, and
\mathsf{G}^{0}\leq\mathsf{G}
its identity component. Moreover, let
\mathcal{HE}
(resp.
\mathcal{HE}_{0}
) denote the set of solutions
(g_{B},\rho,h)
to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations, up to equivalence, for the pair
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
(resp.
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}^{0}/\mathsf{K}^{0})
) with
g_{B}\in c
and
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}^{0}
. Then, the map
\mathcal{HE}_{0}\to\mathcal{HE}
is finite-to-one.

Proof.

Let
(g_{B},\rho,h)
be a solution to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equation with
g_{B}\in c
and
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\mathsf{G}^{0}
. Up to pulling back by
\check{f}\in\Aut(\Sigma)
, any solution
(g_{B}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},h^{\prime})
that is equivalent to
(g_{B},\rho,h)
for
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
is of the form
(g_{B},C_{\varphi}\circ\rho,\varphi\cdot h)=(g_{B},\rho^{\prime},h^{\prime})
for
\varphi\in\mathsf{G}
. Notice that this equivalence class is a union of equivalence classes for the pair
(B^{2},\mathsf{G}^{0}/\mathsf{K}^{0})
. Since the map
\mathcal{HE}_{0}\to\mathcal{HE}
sends the
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-equivalence class of
(g_{B},\rho,h)
in
\mathcal{HE}_{0}
to the
\mathsf{G}
-equivalence class of
(g_{B},\rho,h)
in
\mathcal{HE}
, the proposition will follow if we show that this union is finite.

Let
\{\varphi_{0}=e,\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\}\subset\mathsf{G}
be representatives for the
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-cosets in
\mathsf{G}
. Any
(g_{B},C_{\varphi}\circ\rho,\varphi\cdot h)=(g_{B},\rho^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is in the
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-equivalence class of
(g_{B},C_{\varphi_{i}}\circ\rho,\varphi_{i}\cdot h)
for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
. That is, the number of
\mathsf{G}^{0}
-equivalence classes in any
\mathsf{G}
-equivalence class is at most
N
. Thus, the proposition follows.
∎

Finally, we prove the following necessary condition for a soltion
(M,g)
that satisfies Ansatz
3.1
to be homogeneous. We will use the contrapositive of this in §4 to prove existence of inhomogeneous solitons coming from Ansatz
3.1
.

Lemma 4.11
.

If a soliton
(M,g)
satisfying Ansatz
3.1
is locally homogeneous, then
(B,g_{B})
is also locally homogeneous.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is flat and
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\Aut(\mathsf{N})
, there is a totally geodesic section
\sigma:(B,g_{B})\to(M,g)
. Thus, local homogeneity of
(M,g)
implies local homogeneity of
(B,g_{B})
since a totally geodesic submanifold of a locally homogeneous space is itself locally homogeneous.
∎

5.
Low-dimensional solitons

5.1.
Dimension 4

In this section, we apply the results of §
4
to give a complete description of the Ricci solitons arising as limits of four-dimensional immortal Ricci flows. It turns out that the only non-trivial examples (i.e. not Einstein or locally homogeneous) have
\dim B=\dim\mathsf{N}=2
; see
[
55
]
*Prop. 4.80.

In order to do this, we use an explicit description of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles due to Hitchin
[
31
]
.
Fix a Riemann surface
\Sigma=(B^{2},c)
with
\chi(B^{2})<0
and let
(L,\beta,\gamma)
be an
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle (cf. Example
4.6
). For each
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\mathcal{N}_{d}\subset\mathcal{N}
to be the subset of equivalence classes of polystable
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles with
\tr\Phi^{2}=0
and
\deg L=d
. If
(L,\beta,\gamma)
is polystable, then
|d|<g-1
(cf. the proof of
[
31
]
*Prop. 10.2), so we assume this from now on. Moreover, there is an
\det^{-1}(\pm 1)
-gauge transformation (see
[
16
]
*§3.2) that gives an isomorphism of
(L,\beta,\gamma)
with
(L^{-1},\gamma,\beta)
, so we may assume
d\geq 0
.

The Higgs vector bundle corresponding to
(L,\beta,\gamma)
is
E=L\oplus L^{-1}
with the Higgs field

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&\beta\\
\gamma&0\end{pmatrix}.

The condition
0=\tr\Phi^{2}=\beta\gamma+\gamma\beta
implies that either
\beta=0
or
\gamma=0
. If
d>0
, polystability implies
\gamma\neq 0
since
L
would be
\Phi
-invariant if
\gamma=0
. Hence,
\beta=0
in this case. From Hitchin’s desciption
[
31
]
*Prop. 10.2 of the moduli space of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles, we obtain the following description of
\mathcal{N}_{d}
,
0<d\leq g-1
.

Proposition 5.1

(Hitchin,
[
31
]
)
.

For a fixed Riemann surface
\Sigma=(B^{2},c)
and an integer
d\in(0,g-1]
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{d}
is a
2^{2g}
-fold covering of
S^{r}B
, where
r=2g-2-2d
.

Here,
S^{r}B
is the
r
-th symmetric product of
B
. Recall that points
D\in S^{r}B
can be thought of as formal sums
D=n_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}b_{k}
with
b_{i}\in B
for
i=1,\ldots,k
and
r=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
. The Higgs bundle
(L,0,\gamma)
lies above the zero divisor of
\gamma
; that is, the formal sum determined by the points
b_{i}\in B
such that
\gamma_{b_{i}}=0
, weighted by the order to which
\gamma
vanishes. In particular, the real dimension of
S^{r}B
is
2r
.

For
d=g-1
,
\mathcal{N}_{d}
is a finite set of order
2^{2g}
. The corresponding representations
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
are the
2^{2g}
-possible lifts of the uniformising representation
\pi_{1}(B)\to\mathsf{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and the corresponding solitons are homogeneous (cf. Example
3.9
).

If
d=0
, the condition
\tr\Phi^{2}=0
forces
\Phi=0
. Indeed, if
\beta=0
, then
L^{-1}
is a
\Phi
-invariant sub-bundle with
\deg L^{-1}=0
, so
E=L\oplus L^{-1}
must split as a sum of Higgs line bundles of degree
0
. The only way this is possible is if
\gamma=0
. Similarly,
\gamma=0
implies
\beta=0
. Thus, if
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
corresponds to
(L,\beta,\gamma)=(L,0,0)\in\mathcal{N}_{0}
, then
\rho(\pi_{1}(B))\leq\Or(2)
(see, for example,
[
16
]
*Prop. 2.17) and
\rho
-equivariant harmonic maps are constant. In particular, the resulting solitons are locally homogeneous.

If
0<d<g-1
, then the corresponding solitons are not locally homogeneous:

Lemma 5.2
.

If
0<d<g-1
, then a soliton
(M,g)
associated to an
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle
(L,0,\gamma)
with
\deg L=d
is not locally homogeneous.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that
(M,g)
is locally homogeneous. Then,
(B^{2},g_{B})
is locally homogeneous by Lemma
4.11
, and hence has constant Gauss curvature since
\dim B=2
. By (
20b
),
h^{*}g_{B}=\Ric_{g_{B}}+\tfrac{1}{2}g
is a constant multiple of
g_{B}
. In particular,
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}
, and hence also
h^{*}g_{\mathsf{sym}}^{\mathbb{C}}=\tr\Phi\Phi^{*}
, either vanishes identically or vanishes nowhere. The claim now follows since
\tr\Phi\Phi^{*}
vanishes at
b\in B
if and only if
\gamma_{b}=0
, and
\gamma
has exactly
2g-2-2d
zeroes (counted with multiplicity) since it is a holomorphic section of a line bundle of degree
2g-2-2d
.
∎

Observe that for a genus 2 surface, we only obtain locally homogeneous solitons since
\mathcal{N}_{0}
or
\mathcal{N}_{1}=\mathcal{N}_{g-1}
are the only components. However, as soon as the genus of
B^{2}
is
\geq 3
, we obtain infinite families of solitons that are not locally homogeneous. In fact, by allowing the conformal class
c
to vary, we have the following description, from which Corollary
E
follows easily.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
B^{2}
be a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
,
d\in\{0,1\ldots,g-1\}
, and let
M^{4}
be the total space of the oriented vector bundle
\pi:M^{4}\to B^{2}
with Euler class
e(M)=d
. Then, there exists a family, depending on
4(g-1-d)+6(g-1)=10g-10-4d
parameters, of invariant expanding Ricci solitons on
M^{4}
. These are locally homogeneous if and only if
d=0,g-1
.

5.2.
Dimension 5

We now describe the expanding solitons arising from
3.1
with local
\mathsf{N}^{3}
-symmetry and
2
-dimensional base, where
\mathsf{N}^{3}
is the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group (see
Example

A.3
). Recall that
\mathsf{N}^{3}
is the simplest example of a non-abelian nilpotent Lie group.

Proof of
F
.

We first describe the groups
\mathsf{G}
and
\mathsf{K}
associated to
\mathsf{N}^{3}
as per (
5
). The soliton derivation is made explicit in
Example

A.3
, and it follows that, under the isomorphism
\Aut(\mathsf{N}^{3})\simeq\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
, we have

\mathsf{G}\simeq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&\det A\end{pmatrix}:A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\,\,\det A=\pm 1\right\},\qquad\mathsf{K}\simeq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&\det A\end{pmatrix}:A\in\mathsf{O}(2)\right\}.

Let
(M^{5},g,X)
be an expanding soliton constructed from
3.1
with symmetry group
\mathsf{N}^{3}
. By
3.4
, there is a corresponding solution
(g_{B},\rho,h)
to the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations (
4
) for the pair
(B,\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K})
. Let
\chi:\pi_{1}(B)\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
be given by
\chi={\rm sign}\circ\det\circ\rho
, and let
\hat{B}\to B
denote the connected regular covering corresponding to
\ker\chi
. By pulling back
M
to
\hat{B}
if necessary, we assume henceforth that

\rho_{*}:\pi_{1}(B)\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathsf{G}.

Thanks to
3.4
and
3.6
, to prove
F
it suffices to give a
1
-to-
1
correspondence (up to equivalence) between the triples
(g_{B},\rho,h)
as above, and triples
(g_{B}^{0},\rho_{0},h_{0})
solving the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations for the pair
(B,\mathsf{G}^{0}/\mathsf{K}^{0})
, where
\mathsf{G}^{0}\simeq\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathsf{K}^{0}=\mathsf{SO}(2)
.

The new monodromy
\rho_{0}
is simply the co-restriction of
\rho
to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It is clear that
\rho_{0}
is reductive if an only if
\rho
is so. Regarding
h_{0}
, note that any element of
\mathsf{G}
is a product of one in
\mathsf{G}^{0}
and another one of the form
{\rm diag}(\pm 1,1,\pm 1)\in\mathsf{O}(3)
. Thus, viewing them as submanifolds of the symmetric space
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\mathsf{O}(3)
, we see that
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{G}^{0}/\mathsf{K}^{0}
. In particular, we may take
h=h_{0}
, and also
g_{B}^{0}=g_{B}
, and it is clear that the new triple solves the twisted harmonic-Einstein equations.

The fact that this association is indeed
1
-to-
1
up to equivalences follows from the uniqueness statements in
Theorem

4.2
and
Theorem

4.3
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Geometrically, the situation is as follows: there is a globally-defined, free, proper and isometric action of
\mathbb{R}
on
M^{5}
, corresponding to the center of
\mathsf{N}^{3}
(which is fixed by
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
). The corresponding quotient
M_{0}^{4}=M^{5}/\mathbb{R}
is a twisted principal
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-bundle, and the quotient metric is an invariant expanding soliton.

6.
Expanding Ricci solitons with symmetry

In this section, we show that the Ricci solitons arising as limits of immortal Ricci flows as in
Theorem

1.1
satisfy
3.1
. Thanks to
3.4
, this finishes the proof of
A
.

To that end, we now briefly recall the structure of the solitons
(M,g,X)
to be studied.
The underlying manifold is a twisted principal
\mathsf{N}
-bundle
\pi:M\to B
over a compact orbifold
B
, with
\mathsf{N}
nilpotent and simply connected. In this paper we assume that
B
is smooth.
We let
\rho:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut(\mathsf{N})
be the twisting homomorphism. The metric
g
is invariant and thus corresponds to
(\check{g},\mathcal{H},h)
. Together with some vector field
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, they solve (
1
). The connection
\mathcal{H}
is flat and has holonomy
\rho
.

The Ricci curvature is given by

(26)

\Ric_{g}+{\rm Hess}(f)+\frac{1}{2}g=\frac{(\tr\beta^{2})^{-1}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\beta_{\mathcal{V}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

for some smooth invariant function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
. We also denote by
f
the corresponding smooth function on
B
. The blocks in (
26
) are according to
TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}
, and
\beta_{\mathcal{V}}
is the invariant vertical endomorphism associated to a section
\beta_{\operatorname{ad}}\in\Gamma\End({\operatorname{ad}}M)
. The latter gives at each
b\in B
the canonical GIT weight
\beta_{\operatorname{ad}}(b)
of the metric Lie algebra
(({\operatorname{ad}}M)_{b},h(b))
, see
Remark

A.6
for its definition and properties, which will be used freely in this proof. We also set
\beta^{+}_{\operatorname{ad}}:={\rm Id}+(\tr\beta^{2})^{-1}\beta_{\operatorname{ad}}
.

We remark that these limit solitons also come with a topological restriction. Namely, there is a globally-defined volume element for the fibres of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
which is
\nabla^{\operatorname{ad}}
-parallel. Equivalently,
\rho_{*}:\pi_{1}(B)\to\Aut_{\pm 1}(\mathfrak{n})
. This allows us to define the relative volume density
\sqrt{\det h}:B\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the bundle metric
h
on
{\operatorname{ad}}M
. Since we are locally in a principal bundle, the mean curvature vector

Proposition 6.2
.

An expanding Ricci soliton constructed from
3.1
is of gradient type if and only if
\mathsf{N}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is abelian and
\rho
takes values in
\mathsf{O}(n)
.

Proof.

Assume that
X=\nabla f
for some smooth function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
.
By
3.1
, the structure group of the bundle
\pi:M\to B
reduces to
\Aut_{\pm 1}(\mathsf{N})
, thus
M
is a Lie group bundle (vector bundle in case
\mathsf{N}
is abelian), and there is a well-defined identity (zero) section. From
3.1
,
(iii)
we also know that
X
is vertical and it restricts to a soliton vector field

of each fibre

. In particular,
f
is horizontally constant. Moreover, by the construction in

,

vanishes along the identity section. Notice that

is well-defined only up to adding a global Killing field

; the proof for
K\neq 0
parallel is analogous.

Suppose first that
\mathsf{N}
is abelian. We may assume without loss of generality that
f
vanishes along the zero section. The fibre
M_{b}
is a flat Gaussian soliton, and its vector field is the Euler vector field (the one whose flow is given by scalar multiplication). In terms of the potential, we have

f(b,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|_{h(b)}^{2},\qquad b\in U\subset B,\,\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

in a local trivialisation

adapted to the flat connection. Extending

to be covariantly parallel in horizontal directions, and using that

is horizontally constant, we deduce that

is preserved by the flat connection. This in particular implies that the monodromy

takes values in

. The converse is clear.

If
\mathsf{N}
is nilpotent and non-abelian, it suffices to show that the fibres are non-gradient solitons. This is well-known, and it follows from rigidity of homogeneous gradient solitons
[
64
]
. Indeed, any homogeneous gradient soliton is isometric to (a quotient of)
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times E^{n-k}
, where

is Einstein and
\mathbb{R}^{k}
flat. In particular, its Ricci curvature is semi-definite, and this contradicts a result of Milnor
[
59
]
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

6.2
also shows that, even in the abelian case, many of the solitons we constructed are of non-gradient type. Indeed, Hitchin
[
30
]
showed that, for

which can be continuously deformed to one taking values in the maximal compact subgroup

all belong to a single connected component of the character variety (whose elements have the same topological type as those in the Hitchin component). But there are 2 (resp. 5) other connected components when

is odd (resp. even).

Remark 6.4
.

It follows from (

) and the proof of
Theorem

6.1
that the scalar curvature of a soliton

constructed from

is a negative constant, given by

\scal_{g}=\begin{cases}-(\dim B)/2,&\qquad\mathsf{N}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}\hbox{ abelian;}\\

In particular,

7.
Einstein extensions: proof of
G

In this section we show that the expanding Ricci solitons constructed via
3.1
give rise to an extension, one dimension higher, which is Einstein.

More precisely, let
\pi:M\to B
be a twisted principal bundle such that
(M,g,X)
is a solution to the soliton equation (
1
) obtained from
3.1
. Consider the derivation field of
{\operatorname{ad}}M
obtained as in the proof of
3.4
from the Ricci curvature of the fibres. It gives rise to an invariant endomorphism field
D_{M}\in\End(TM)
as in
Lemma

3.2
, which is related to the Ricci endomorphism of the soliton via

(33)

{g}^{-1}\Ric_{g}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm Id}-D_{M}.

To define the extension, we follow the ideas and notation in
[
1
]
, which are in turn inspired in the homogeneous case. Namely, let
M_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\times M
, set
D:=\alpha D_{M}\in\End(TM)
, with
\alpha>0
a constant to be determined later. Endow
M_{0}
with the metric
du^{2}+g_{u}
, where
g_{u}:=\exp(u\,D)^{*}g
is a one-parameter family of metrics in
M
. By
[
1
]
*Thm. 1.3,
(\mathbb{R}\times M,du^{2}+g_{u})
is Einstein (with Einstein constant
-\tr(D^{2})
) if and only if the following conditions hold:

(i)

The endomorphism
D
has constant eigenvalues and
{\rm div}_{g}D=0
;

(ii)

The Ricci endomorphism of
g_{u}
is independent of
u
and is given by

g_{u}^{-1}\Ric_{g_{u}}=(\tr D)D-\tr(D^{2}){\rm Id}.

Since
\exp(uD_{M}):(M,g_{u})\to(M,g)
is an isometry, and
\exp(uD_{M})
pointwise commutes with

, condition (ii) reduces to a verification for

, i.e. for
g
. Recall that

satisfies
\tr(D_{M}^{2})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\tr D_{M}
(

). Choosing
\alpha=(2\tr(D_{M}^{2}))^{-1/2}
we get

\tr(D^{2})=\frac{1}{2},\qquad(\tr D)D=-D_{M}.

Thus, (ii) now follows from (

has constant eigenvalues, thus

has constant scalar curvature. Hence, by (

.

Appendix A
Nilsolitons

In this section we collect some useful information about nilsolitons which we have used throughout the article. Some references for this topic are
[
46
]
,
[
49
]
. Here,
\mathsf{N}
will denote a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group.

Definition A.1
.

A
nilsoliton
is a left-invariant Ricci soliton metric
h
on
\mathsf{N}
, i.e. its Ricci curvature satisfies

\Ric_{h}=\lambda h-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}h,

for some vector field
X
on
\mathsf{N}
and some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
called the
cosmological constant
.

The following was originally shown in
[
46
]
, with a correction in
[
33
]
:

Proposition A.2
.

A left-invariant metric
h
on
\mathsf{N}
is a nilsoliton if and only if its (1,1)-Ricci tensor restricted to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
of left-invariant vector fields on
\mathsf{N}
satisfies

(34)

h^{-1}\Ric_{h}=\lambda\,{\rm Id}{n}-D,\qquad D\in\Der(\mathfrak{n}).

Proof.

Without loss of generality let us assume
\lambda=-1/2
. If
h
is a left-invariant Ricci soliton, let
(\eta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the flow of
X
. Then
h_{t}:=\eta_{t}^{*}h
solves the normalised Ricci flow (
2
). Since Ricci flow preserves isometries,
\mathsf{N}
acts by isometries of
h_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By Wilson’s theorem
[
76
]
,
\eta_{t}\in\Aut(\mathsf{N})\ltimes\mathsf{N}
, thus there exists a smooth one-parameter family
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\subset\Aut(\mathsf{N})
such that
h_{t}=\varphi_{t}^{*}h
. We may thus assume that
X
is generated by
\varphi_{t}
. Since
\mathsf{N}
is simply-connected, there exists
\Phi_{t}\in\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
with
d\Phi_{t}|_{e}=\varphi_{t}
. Write
\Phi_{t}=\exp(tD)
for some
D\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
. Then, at the identity
e\in\mathsf{N}
it holds that

(\varphi_{t}^{*}h)_{e}=h(\exp(tD)\cdot,\exp(tD)\cdot),

from which
(\mathcal{L}_{X}h)_{e}(\cdot,\cdot)=h(D\cdot,\cdot)+h(\cdot,D\cdot)
. It follows that

{h}^{-1}\Ric_{h}=-\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm Id}-\tfrac{1}{2}(D+D^{T}).

By
Lemma

A.5
below this implies
\tfrac{1}{2}(D+D^{T})\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
. Replacing
D
by
\tfrac{1}{2}(D+D^{T})
, (
34
) follows.

Conversely, assume (
34
) holds, and let
\varphi_{t}\in\Aut(\mathsf{N})
be determined by
\exp(tD)\in\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
. By the same computations as above, the Ricci soliton equation is satisfied for the vector field
X
generated by
\varphi_{t}
.
∎

Example A.3
.

If
\mathsf{N}
is the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie group of
3\times 3
upper-triangular real matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal, then any left-invariant metric is a nilsoliton. Indeed, from
[
59
]
there is an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
of
\mathfrak{n}
with
[e_{1},e_{2}]=2c\,e_{3}
, and the Ricci endomorphism is given by

{h}^{-1}\Ric_{h}=-3c^{2}\,{\rm Id}+2c^{2}\,{\rm diag}(1,1,2),\qquad{\rm diag}(1,1,2)\in\Der(\mathfrak{n}).

It is known that every simply-connected nilpotent Lie group of dimension up to
6
admits a nilsoliton metric
[
47
,
74
]
, but already in dimension
7
there are some which do not: see
[
45
]
for details about the existence and non-existence question. In general, the classification of nilpotent Lie algebras admitting nilsoliton inner products is wide open as far as we know.

While these metrics do not exist on any nilpotent Lie group, when they do, they are unique up to isometry, in a very strong sense: given any two nilsolitons
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
on the same Lie group
\mathsf{N}
, up to scaling there exists
\varphi\in\Aut(\mathsf{N})
such that
h_{2}=\varphi^{*}h_{1}

[
46
]
.

A left-invariant metric on a connected Lie group
\mathsf{N}
is determined by an inner product
h\in\Sym_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
. The space of inner products is a Riemannian symmetric space of non-positive curvature:

\Sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})={\mathsf{Gl}^{+}}(\mathfrak{n})/\mathsf{SO}(\mathfrak{n},\bar{h}).

Here
\bar{h}
is certain fixed inner product, and the action of
{\mathsf{Gl}^{+}}(\mathfrak{n})
is by pull-back:

(35)

(q\cdot\bar{h})(\cdot,\cdot)=\bar{h}(q^{-1}\cdot,q^{-1}\cdot),\qquad q\in{\mathsf{Gl}^{+}}(\mathfrak{n}).

The symmetric metric on
\Sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
is given for
h\in\Sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
and
k\in T_{h}\Sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})=\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
by

(36)

(g_{\rm sym})_{h}(k,k)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\dim\mathfrak{n}}k(e_{i},e_{j})k(e_{i},e_{j}),

for any
h
-orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
\mathfrak{n}
. See
[
26
]
*
\lx@sectionsign
3.1 for further details.

In terms of this structure, if
\bar{h}
is itself a nilsoliton with cosmological constant
\lambda
, then by the above-mentioned uniqueness, the set of nilsoliton inner products on
\mathfrak{n}
with cosmological constant
\lambda
is the orbit

\Aut(\mathfrak{n})\cdot\bar{h}\,\,\subset\,\,{\mathsf{Gl}^{+}}(\mathfrak{n})/\SO(\mathfrak{n},\bar{h}).

Set
\beta={\bar{h}}^{-1}\Ric_{\bar{h}}\in\End(\mathfrak{n})
, let
\bar{\nu}\in\Lambda^{\rm top}\mathfrak{n}^{*}
correspond to the Riemannian volume form of
\bar{h}
, and denote by
|\bar{\nu}|
the corresponding volume density.

Lemma A.4
.

The set of nilsoliton inner products in
\Sym^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
with Ricci endomorphism
\beta
and with volume density
|\bar{\nu}|
is connected, and it is given by the orbit

\mathsf{G}\cdot\bar{h}\,\,\subset\,\,{\mathsf{Gl}^{+}}(\mathfrak{n})/\SO(\mathfrak{n},\bar{h}),\qquad\mathsf{G}:=\{\varphi\in\Aut(\mathfrak{n}):\varphi\beta\varphi^{-1}=\beta,\,\,\det\varphi=\pm 1\}.

Proof.

By uniqueness, nilsoliton inner products lie in the orbit
\Aut(\mathfrak{n})\cdot\bar{h}
. Each
\varphi\in\Aut(\mathfrak{n})
gives an isometry of metric Lie algebras
\varphi:(\mathfrak{n},\bar{h})\to(\mathfrak{n},\varphi\cdot\bar{h})
, thus the Ricci endomorphism of
\varphi\cdot\bar{h}
is
\varphi\beta\varphi^{-1}
, and its volume density is
|{\det\varphi}|^{-1}|\bar{\nu}|
.

To see that
\mathsf{G}\cdot\bar{h}
is connected, we use that
\mathsf{G}
is real reductive, that is, it admits a Cartan decomposition

\mathsf{G}=\exp(\mathfrak{p})\mathsf{K},\qquad\mathsf{K}:=\mathsf{G}\cap\mathsf{O}(\mathfrak{n},\bar{h}),\quad\mathfrak{p}:=\{E\in\Der(\mathfrak{n}):[E,\beta]=0,E=E^{T},\tr E=0\}.

This fact is well-known to experts, see
[
63
]
*Thm. 5.1 and
[
65
]
*Thm. 4.3. It follows that
\mathsf{G}\cdot\bar{h}=\{\exp(E)\cdot\bar{h}:E\in\mathfrak{p}\}
is an embedded, connected submanifold of
{\mathsf{Gl}^{+}}(\mathfrak{n})/\SO(\mathfrak{n},\bar{h})
.
∎

A key ingredient in the study of nilsolitons is the following moment map formulation for the Ricci endomorphism of a left-invariant metric
h
on
\mathsf{N}
(see e.g.
[
48
]
*Prop. 4.2):

(37)

\tr\big((h^{-1}{\Ric_{h}})E\big)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\delta(E),[\cdot,\cdot]\rangle_{h},\qquad\forall\,E\in\End(\mathfrak{n}).

Here
[\cdot,\cdot]\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{n}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{n}
is the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{n}
,
\delta(E)(\cdot,\cdot)=-E[\cdot,\cdot]+[E\cdot,\cdot]+[\cdot,E\cdot]\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{n}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{n}
, and the inner product is the natural extension of the inner product
h
on
\mathfrak{n}
to the space of brackets. Notice that
\delta(D)=0
precisely when
D\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
.

As an immediate application of (
37
) we see that the Ricci endomorphism is orthogonal to derivations, thus the nilsoliton derivation
D
from
A.2
must satisfy

(38)

\tr D^{2}=\lambda\tr D.

Also, taking
E=h^{-1}{\Ric_{h}}
in (
37
) yields

|{\Ric_{h}}|^{2}=\lambda\,\scal_{h}.

Since
\scal_{h}\leq 0
with equality if and only if
\mathfrak{n}
is abelian, it follows that non-abelian nilsolitons are of expanding type
\lambda<0
.

Another application of (
37
) is the following lemma about derivations that commute with Ricci:

Lemma A.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{N},h)
be a nilpotent Lie group with invariant metric, with Ricci endomorphism
h^{-1}\Ric_{h}=\tfrac{1}{2}\beta
. If
D\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
is such that
[D+D^{T},\beta]=0
(this holds in particular if
[D,\beta]=0
), then
L:=D+D^{T}\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
and

\tr\big(({\operatorname{ad}}U)L\big)=0,\qquad\forall\,U\in\mathfrak{n}.

Proof.

Taking
E=[D,D^{T}]
in (
37
) (transpose with respect to
\bar{h}
), standard properties of
\delta
and the extended inner products (see
[
43
]
*
\lx@sectionsign
7) give

0=\tr[\beta,D+D^{T}]D^{T}=\tr\beta[D,D^{T}]=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\delta([D,D^{T}]),[\cdot,\cdot]\rangle_{\bar{h}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\delta(D^{T})\|_{\bar{h}}^{2}.

It follows that
D^{T}\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
, and the same for
L
. To see that the trace against
{\operatorname{ad}}U
vanishes, consider the orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{k}
, where
\mathfrak{n}_{2}\oplus\cdots\mathfrak{n}_{k}=[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]
,
\mathfrak{n}_{3}\oplus\cdots\mathfrak{n}_{k}=[\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]]
, and so on. It is easy to see that, in an adapted basis, the matrix of
{\operatorname{ad}}U
is strictly block-lower-triangular, whereas the matrix of the derivation
L
is block-lower-triangular, and the lemma follows.

.

Formula (

[
65
,
57
,
28
,

]
, to problems involving the Ricci curvature of homogeneous Riemannian manifolds, and more generally Riemannian manifolds with symmetries, see e.g.
[

]
. Many of these applications rely on a Kirwan-Ness stratification of the variety of Lie algebras. One associates to each metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{n},h)
a uniquely-determined
h
-self-adjoint endomorphism
\beta_{h}\in\End(\mathfrak{n})
, called the
GIT weights
. Its conjugacy class is independent of
h
and serves as a label for the stratum containing the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
.

We now list some properties of the GIT weights that are key in some of the proofs in this article. We refer the reader to
[
9
]
and therein references for proofs and further details.

(i)

The conjugacy class of
\beta_{h}
is independent of
h
. In particular,
\tr\beta_{h}^{2}=:\tr\beta^{2}
is constant, and we normalise
\tr\beta_{h}=-1
;

(ii)

\beta^{+}_{h}:={\rm Id}+(\tr\beta^{2})^{-1}\beta_{h}
is positive definite provided
\mathfrak{n}
is nilpotent;

(iii)

\tr\big({h}^{-1}\Ric_{h}\beta^{+}_{h})\geq 0
, with equality if and only if
\beta^{+}_{h}\in\Der(\mathfrak{n})
;

(iv)

For any inner product
h
on
\mathfrak{n}
with
\scal_{h}=-1
we have that
|{\Ric_{h}}|_{h}^{2}\geq\tr\beta^{2}
, with equality if and only if
h
is a nilsoliton and
h^{-1}\Ric_{h}=\beta_{h}
.

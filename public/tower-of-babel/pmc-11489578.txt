Nano-enhanced storage of American cotton using metal-oxide nanoparticles for improving seed quality traits
Authors: Nirmal Singh, Axay Bhuker, Vineeta Pandey, Himani Punia, Sourabh, Bhupender Singh, Ajaz Ahmad, Anshika Tyagi, Anurag Malik
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cotton seeds have poorer germination than other crops because of their high sensitivity towards insect pests and other biotic and abiotic stresses during the germination process. In the present study, inorganic bulk and nano nutrients of zinc oxide (ZnO) and titanium oxide (TiO2) nanoparticles were synthesized using the chemical reduction method and invigourated with cotton seeds. The characterization of nanoparticles was done by FESEM, HRTEM, UV/Vis analysis and FTIR. The delinted and fuzzy seeds of two American cotton varieties (H 1300 and H 1098-i) were nano-primed for 10 h with zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnONPs) @ 400 ppm and titanium dioxide nanoparticles (TiO2NPs) @ 100 ppm. After nanoparticle invigouration, the seeds were analyzed for various parameters at different intervals (0 months, 3 months, 6 months, 9 months and 12 months) such as germination percentage, seedling length, seedling dry weight, electrical conductivity, dehydrogenase activity, antioxidant enzyme activity. The results indicated that that different storage periods and nanopriming treatments had significant effects on all seed quality parameters except the effect of nanopriming treatments on germination percentage (excluding delinted seeds of H 1098-i). It is also revealed that the interaction effect of nanopriming treatment and storage period was non-significant on all parameters except EC. Maximum reduction in seed quality parameters was observed in control treatment and minimum was found when seeds were nanoprimed with ZnONPs @ 400 ppm. The differences in the response for both NPs can be attributed to their surface charge, and concentration used. Overall, ZnONPs and TiO2NPs could hold seed quality and vigour during the storage of cotton seeds of American varieties (H 1300 and H 1098-i).

Introduction

Seeds represent the reproductive stage of the plant's life cycle. It serves as a primary seedling material for the subsequent season's crop, thereby significant productivity and an abundance of healthy and robust seeds is needed1. Cotton (Gossypium sp.), a member of the family Malvaceae is the foremost natural fibre crop, the sixth-largest source of vegetable oil, and the third most cultivated crop worldwide2. The main producing countries are India, China, the United States, Brazil, and Pakistan. This growth will come from an expansion of the cotton area (0.5% p.a.) and growth in average global yields (1% p.a.)3. India will continue to be the world’s largest cotton producer, with the increase in production resting mostly on higher yields, while area expansion is expected to be limited in line with recent trends4. Cotton productivity in India is low because of low seed germination and the incidence of insect pests5. The poor seed storage practices result in the production of free radicals causing lipid peroxidation and leading to a rapid deterioration process6. Production of high-quality seeds of cotton which could hold its viability and vigour for the long term is a challenging task in semi-arid and arid regions7. Better genetics and the adoption of better agronomic practices for sustainable cotton production could bring improvement over the coming decade8.

Deterioration of seed quality parameters during storage is a natural time-dependent phenomenon so it could not be stopped but the reduction rate of seed quality parameters could be slowed down by seed treatment or any other methods9. In recent years, nanoscience has proved to be a fast-growing filed in the agricultural sector, many attempts are being made through research for reconnoitring the possibility of adopting nanotechnological approaches along with direct seed treatment using nanoparticles and nanopriming technique to improve the germination percentage, viability, vigour and storage of seeds in different crops10. Nanoparticles offer promising avenues to enhance seed viability and vigour through various mechanisms. Their nanoscale size (< 100 nm) and tunability allow them to penetrate the seed coat and cell membranes, reinforcing the structural integrity of the seed and shielding it from detrimental factors compared with conventionally applied products11. Several researchers investigated the effect of nanoparticles like Zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnONPs), and Titanium oxide nanoparticles (TiO2NPs) in several crops like rice12, maize13, tomato14, mung15, wheat16, onion17, ground nut18 to assess the quality parameters during seed storage19–22. Nanoparticles are also proven to increase the germination of aged seeds23 and to maintain seed quality by slowing down undesirable seed quality deterioration rates during seed storage24. In this study, Cotton was selected and studied as a model plant because of its higher economic value at the global level and cotton seeds are also shown to be short-term storage because of their sensitivity to several biotic and abiotic factors25 which results in the decrease in yield. Therefore, the present study aims to improve the seed quality of aged cotton seeds by using metal oxides (zinc and titanium oxide) and metal (silver) nanoparticle invigouration treatment.

Results

Characteristics features of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs

The structural and morphological characteristics of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs were done by FESEM, HRTEM, UV/Vis spectra, and FTIR images (Fig. 1). ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs were Spherical + Ellipsoid shaped confirmed by FESEM (Fig. 1a,b). Interplanar spacing of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs was found 0.83 nm, 0.20 nm and 0.33 nm, respectively. The size of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs was confirmed by HRTEM images which were 36.25 nm, and 39.40 nm, respectively (Fig. 1c,d). The colour of synthesized ZnONPs and TiO2NPs was white, black and white, respectively. The TiO2 and ZnO nanoparticles exhibit a strong absorption peak at 370 and 365 nm, respectively. Tauc relation is utilized to band gap (Eg). The display of (αhυ)2 vs. hυ for TiO2 and ZnO, respectively (Fig. 1e,f). By extrapolating the graph to the hυ-axis, the value of Eg is calculated for the TiO2 and ZnO nanoparticles which came out to be 3.24 and 3.42 eV. To estimate the various functional groups as well as the absorption of incident light w.r.t wavelength, the synthesized nanoparticles were also characterized by FTIR spectroscopy. The FTIR-spectrum of TiO2NPs shows absorption peaks at 2357, 1012 and 507, which are attributed to the presence of C=C bending, stretching vibration of C–N or C–O bonds and the vibration of metal-oxide (Ti–O–O) bonding, respectively (Fig. 1g,h). Moreover, the FTIR-spectrum of ZnONPs gives a broad band at 3649, which corresponds to the stretching vibration of water molecules; also, intense peaks at 2358, 1541,670 were observed, which corresponds to the C–H or C=C out of plane bending, presence of aromatic nitro compounds and vibration of metal-oxide (Zn–O) bonding, respectively.Fig. 1The characteristics features of ZnONPs and TiO2NPs using (a,b) FESEM, (c,d) HRTEM (e,f) UV/Vis spectra Tauc plot and (g,h) FTIR images, respectively.

Effect of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs on germination indices

Both selected treatments were nanopriming with ZnO @ 400 ppm and TiO2 @ 100 ppm. Hydro-primed seeds were used as a control treatment against nanoprimed seeds. Delinted seeds primed with ZnONPs retained a lower moisture content than delinted seeds primed with TiO2 NPs (Supplementary Table 1). Among different storage periods, maximum moisture content (8.95%) was observed at 0 months which was at par with 3 months (8.85%). However, minimum moisture content (8.64%) was found at 12 months of storage in the context of fuzzy seeds. Germination decreased as the storage period increased. ZnONPs @ 400 ppm attained a higher germination rate in H 1300 (Fig. 2), which was statistically at par with TiO2 nanopriming @ 100 ppm (69.33%) and the lower germination percentage (63.73%) was found in control in the context of delinted seeds in H 1098-I (Supplementary Table 2).Fig. 2Effect of metal oxides on germination of aged cotton seeds.

The interaction effects on seedling length of all American varieties of cotton due to nanopriming treatments and storage periods were found to be non-significant at a 5% level of significance. It was observed that seedling length was decreased over time during storage. The maximum seedling length was found at 0 months and the minimum was found after 12 months of storage in the context of every variety (Supplementary Table 3). The delinted seeds of H 1098-i had a higher reduction in seedling length (27.30%) and H 1300 had a minimum (18.96%) when nanoprimed with ZnONPs. Seedling dry weight was decreased over time during storage. The maximum reduction in seedling dry weight (28.34%) was found in the control treatment of fuzzy seeds of H 1300 and the minimum (19.20%) was found in delinted seeds of H 1098-i when seeds were nanoprimed with ZnONPs @ 400 ppm (Supplementary Table 4).

Effect of ZnO and TiO2 NPs on vigour indices and mycoflora

Different storage periods also exhibited a significant effect on the seed vigour index of seeds of American cotton varieties. It was observed that seed vigour index-I and II decreased over time during storage (Supplementary Tables 5, 6). Maximum seed vigour index-I was found at 0 months and the minimum was found after 12 months of storage in the context of every variety. Maximum reduction in seed vigour index was found in control treatment while minimum reduction was found when seeds were nanoprimed with ZnONPs @ 400 ppm followed by TiO2 NPs @ 100 ppm. In this experiment, it was revealed that maximum antifungal properties were exhibited by ZnO NPs followed by TiO2 NPs. Nanopriming had been proven most effective against fungi followed by the application of nano form of every nanoparticle but in the case of bulk form of nanoparticles, the total number of fungi was increased in comparison to the control (Supplementary Tables 7, 8). Among the varieties, maximum seed mycoflora was observed in fuzzy seeds at 12 months of storage whereas minimum was found in delinted seeds.

Discussion

Seed deterioration is unavoidable, permanent, and unstoppable, but it can be postponed to some amount by effective management strategies such as preservation under controlled circumstances and the promotion of seed invigouration treatments26–28. Our results indicated that seed treatments with metal oxide nanoparticles hold seed vigour during the storage period in seeds in comparison to untreated seeds. ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs have proven to affect seed quality parameters and enzymatic activity in crops by entering into seeds through cell walls due to their small size. These nanoparticles have been used for seed treatment via different methods in crops like wheat29,30, rice31, mung32, gram15, tomato33, watermelon34, onion35, radish36, cabbage37, fenugreek38, Brassica juncea39, spinach40, Capsicum annum41. Additionally, we envisage that various favourable physicochemical characteristics revealed that the size, purity, colour, shape and interplanar distance of synthesized ZnO NPs was 36.25 nm, 99.9%, white, spherical cum ellipsoid and 0.83 nm, and for TiO2 NPs, 39.40 nm, 99.9%, white, spherical cum ellipsoid and 0.33 nm respectively, provided it add-on features to facilitate uniform and firm binding to cotton seed surface42–44. Seed quality parameters like germination percentage, seedling length, seedling dry weight, seed vigour indices and seed health determine plant growth45–47. The results revealed that reduction in seed quality parameters i.e., germination percentage, seedling length, seedling dry weight, seed vigour index-I and seed vigour index-II was maximum when seeds were not treated (control) while minimum reduction in seed quality parameters was observed in nanoprimed seeds with 400 ppm ZnO NPs. Higher values were observed at the commencement of storage and lower was found after storage for twelve months. Zinc is a precursor on indole acetic acid which is required in auxin production and auxin is a growth promoter48. Increased seed quality parameters might be attributed to the fact that during germination, nanoparticles may stimulate oxidation–reduction processes via superoxide ion radicals that result in the quenching of free radicals in the aged seeds49; In turn, the oxygen created in such a process might be used for respiration, promoting germination even more50. Another possible explanation is that these NPs help repair damaged organelles, synthesise and activate enzymes that are crucial to plant growth during germination, and inhibit free radical reactions51. Positive effect by nanopriming with ZnO NPs were also reported in lupine (Lupinus termis)52, they also mentioned that maximum growth was obtained when seeds were nanoprimed with maximum concentration of ZnO NPs. Similar results of reduction in seed quality parameters were also reported in chickpea seeds treated with ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs53, in pigeon pea seeds treated with ZnO NPs and Ag NPs54,55. Seeds nanoprimed with TiO2 NPs. exhibited higher values up to 100 ppm concentration. This might be attributed to the fact that higher doses of nanoparticles exhibit toxicity due to the accumulation of nanoparticles in seedlings15. Titanium nanoparticles have proven to increase the activity of the Rubisco activase enzyme which helps in better seedling growth56. Increased seed quality parameters by application of nanopriming with TiO2 NPs were reported in green gram57, in maize by Vijayalakshmi et al.58, in lettuce by59, in Salvia officinalis up to 200 ppm concentration60, in Capsicum annum L.41, in Lemma minor at lower concentration (up to 200 ppm)61.

Curbing microbial infection is of utmost importance as it causes deterioration of seed quality and vigour and aggravates physiological disorders in stored seeds56. The fungal invasion may damage seeds by causing deformities, reduced seed size, necrotic lesions on seed, seed rot and other physiological alterations in seeds62. It is proven that metal oxide nanoparticles possess antifungal properties. The degree of antifungal properties of different nanoparticles is different. The highest concentration (400 ppm) had a more inhibitory effect on the total number of fungi when seeds were nanoprimed with ZnO NPs. Antifungal properties of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs63,64. However, as the currently available evaluations only partially analyse the use of metal-based nanoparticles for controlling these infections, there is currently a shortage of critical and in-depth reviews of current advancements in the use of metal nanoparticles to control phytopathogenic fungi65. Antifungal properties of metal oxide nanoparticles could be explained by the fact that when ions produced by the nanoparticles attach to specific protein groups, they interfere with permeability by compromising the function of critical membrane proteins or produced ions by nanoparticles disrupt electron transport, protein oxidation, and alter membrane potential of fungi66.

In seeds, food reservoirs mainly starch and protein are the major components of food remobilization67. In vitro seed treatment with ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs in comparison to control, significantly increased the activities of reserve food mobilizing enzymes viz. α-amylase and protease (Figure). Their higher activities are indispensable for supplying adequate amounts of glucose and amino acids to growing embryos to achieve higher seed vigour (Table 2). Results revealed a reduction in starch and protein in the stored seeds up to 9 months of storage in NP treatments. Electrical conductivity is contrarywise related to seed viability and vigour which mean higher EC is not favourable for better seed quality. Minimum EC was found in seeds treated with nanoparticles68. However, the EC of untreated seeds of cotton was enormous at the end of seed storage which is an indicative factor of seed deterioration. It might be because the thin layer of nanoparticles over the seed surface maintains the seed coat integrity thereby reducing the leakage of solutes from the seeds.

Dehydrogenase activity of seeds is directly related to the viability and vigour of seeds19. Higher dehydrogenase activity is more favourable for the germination of seeds and vice versa26. In this experiment, seed treatment with ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs had increased dehydrogenase activity within seeds of cotton. Dehydrogenase activity of seeds also decreased with time; the maximum was at 0 months and the minimum was after 12 months. Increased dehydrogenase activity could be attributed to the fact that metals are the main component of some enzymes. It also could be explained by the fact that metal-based nanoparticles help in the synthesis and activation of enzymes. Nanopriming of cotton seeds with ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs significantly excelled SOD, CAT and POD activities in stored seeds. The elevated activities of antioxidant enzymes during initial storage months subsequently abridged highly toxic O2−1 into lesser toxic H2O2 and subsequently broke down H2O2 into water (Bowler et al.69). Higher SOD, CAT and POD activities enable plants to overcome the deleterious effects of ROS under stressful environments9,20,21. ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs also significantly spurred PPO activity as compared with control, which is vital for the protection of photosynthetic electron transport22,70. It is praiseworthy that application of ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs reinforced antioxidant machinery by up-regulating SOD, CAT and POD activities. Higher MDA content represents lipid peroxidation of cellular membranes and shows instability of cellular organelles which is a common consequence of stress71. In present study, a significantly lower amount of MDA was recorded in aged seeds treated with ZnO NPs and TiO2 NPs as compared with control which bestows stress resilience capacity of plants72.

Methods

Plant material

Genetically pure delinted and fuzzy seeds of American varieties (H 1300 and H 1098-i) of cotton were procured from the Cotton Section, Department of Genetics and Plant Breeding, CCS HAU Hisar. All aspects of the experimental research and field studies on plants, including the collecting of plant material, were carried out in a manner that was by the applicable institutional, national, and international norms and legislation.

Preparation of ZnONPs and TiO2 NPs

Zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnONPs) and titanium dioxide nanoparticles (TiO2NPs) were synthesized by chemical method of synthesis using Zn(NO3)2·4H2O and titanium tetra isopropoxide, respectively. For the synthesis of ZnO nanoparticles, 0.45 M aqueous solution of zinc nitrate [Zn(NO3)2·4H2O] and 0.9 M aqueous solution of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) were prepared in distilled water. Then, the beaker containing NaOH solution was heated at a temperature of about 55 °C. The [Zn(NO3)2·4H2O] solution was added dropwise (slowly for 40 min) to the above-heated solution under high-speed stirring and after this beaker was sealed at this condition for 2 h, then cleaned precipitated ZnO NPs with deionized water and ethanol then dried in air atmosphere at about 60 °C. Titanium dioxide nanoparticles (TiO2 NPs) were prepared as per the procedure of Ghows and Entezari73. Glacial acetic acid (0.2 ml) and distilled water (50 ml) were mixed in a beaker. Another mixture of titanium tetra isopropoxide (2 ml) and ethanol (5 ml) was added dropwise in the above solution and sonicated at 500 kHz, 11.6 W for 3 h at room temperature. Precipitates formed were separated by centrifugation (15,000 rpm for 20 min) and washed with distilled water and ethanol several times. Finally, the product was dried at 40℃ for 6 h and used for further experimentation (Fig. 10).Fig. 10Synthesized (a) zinc oxide and (b) titanium dioxide nanoparticles.

Characterization of synthesized nanoparticles

The characterization of synthesized nanoparticles was performed by using Field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM), High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM), Ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometry (UV/Vis) analysis and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR). The coarse and fine microstructures and the morphology of all the NPs were depicted by using FESEM; EVO MA 15 Germany, scanning electron microscope (FESEM). The FESEM micrographs were used to analyze the surface morphology of the sample, and the topographic filature of the sample, to examine the diameter, length, shape and density of the nanoparticles. The average particle size and the size distribution of the NPs were further investigated using High-resolution Transmission Electron Microscope (JEOL 1200EX, Japan). UV/Vis spectroscopy is performed using a Shimazdu 2600 UV/Vis spectrophotometer. The wavelength range was set at 200–800 nm with a slit width of 5 nm. 3 ml Quartz cuvette was used for characterization. 3 mg or sample was sonicated in 3 ml DI water which was further used for characterization. The chemical bonding and functional groups present in the nanoparticles were studied by FTIR (Fourier Transform Infrared spectroscopy) (Bruker-vertex-70) from 500 to 4000 cm−1. Nanopriming of ZnONPs and TiO2NPs

Delinted and fuzzy seeds of two American cotton (H 1300 and H 1098-i) were treated with nano form (dry), bulk form (dry) and nanopriming (wet) of zinc oxide (ZnO), and titanium dioxide (TiO2) NPs. Two control treatments i.e., control (dry) and control (wet) were taken. No seed treatment is considered as control (dry) used to compare with nano form (dry) and bulk form (dry); however, hydropriming of seed for 10 h is considered as control (wet) used to compare with nanopriming. The seeds were treated with dry form by mixing seeds and nano and bulk form with all different concentrations of metal oxide in a glass jar for even distribution. After nanopriming, the primed seeds were then air-dried under aseptic conditions to remove excess moisture before further experimentation. Seed quality parameters were recorded at different time intervals (0, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months) during storage. Seeds were stored in air-tight containers to minimize exposure to moisture and environmental fluctuations. Temperature and relative humidity were monitored and maintained at optimal levels for seed storage, typically around 4 °C and 40–50% RH. Seed moisture content, standard germination, seedling length, seedling dry weight

The moisture content of seeds was calculated according to ISTA (International Seed Testing Association) by hot air oven method74. Ten grams of seeds from each lot were kept in a moisture box in the oven at 103 ± 1 °C for 24 h. The germination test was conducted using three replicates by adopting the method described by ISTA. At the final count of the germination test, the number of normal seedlings in each replication was counted and the germination was calculated and expressed in percentage. Ten normal seedlings in three replications of each variety were selected randomly during the germination test and measured in centimetres and average seedling length was calculated. Ten normal seedlings used for measuring root and shoot length were kept in butter paper and dried in a hot-air oven maintained at 75 ± 1 °C temperature for 24 h. Then the seedlings were removed and allowed to cool in a desiccator for 30 min before weighing on an electronic balance. The average weight was calculated and expressed as dry weight of seedlings in milligrams.

Reserve mobilizing enzymes and electrical conductivity

The seeds were carefully removed from the germinating portion and homogenized in phosphate buffer (100 mM, pH 7.8) and sodium acetate buffer (100 mM, pH 4.8) and at 4 °C for estimation of protease and α-amylase activities. Their activities (μmol/min/g d.w.) were estimated by 3, 5, dinitrosalicylic acid and Folin–Ciocalteu reagent methods as described earlier76. The electrical conductivity of the seed leachates was measured as per ISTA74. 50 randomly selected seeds in triplicates from each seed lot were soaked in separate beakers each containing 75 ml of distilled water and kept in the germinator at 25 + 1 °C for 24 h. The EC of seed leachates was expressed in µS/cm/seeds.

Dehydrogenase activity, SOD, CAT, POD, PPO

The dehydrogenase activity test77 is the basic principle for the topographical tetrazolium test for seed viability. A sample of 1 g of each treatment was grounded and the flour was soaked in 5 ml of 0.5% tetrazolium solution at 38 °C and was centrifuged after 3–4 h at 10,000 rpm for 3 min and supernatant was poured off. Formazan was extracted with 10 ml acetone for 16 h followed by centrifugation and read at 520 nm wavelength. Activities (μmol/min/gtissue) of antioxidant enzymes viz. superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), peroxidase (POD) and defence enzymes polyphenol oxidase (PPO) were estimated at different storage periods. In brief, leaf samples were collected from the middle portion of the flag leaf. A 0.5 g leaf tissue was hand homogenized by pre-cooled ceramic mortar-pestle in 5 ml extraction buffer (phosphate buffer for SOD, CAT and PPO at pH 7.4, 7.0 and 6.8, respectively and Tris–HCl buffer at pH 7.5 for POD). The homogenates were centrifuged (10,000×g for 15 min) at 4 °C and supernatants were used for enzyme assays. SOD (EC 1.15.1.1) activity was determined at 560 nm, as a reduction of nitro-blue tetrazolium (NBT) as an indicator of superoxide anion production78. CAT (EC 1.11.1.6) activity was measured at 240 nm79. POD (EC 1.11.1.7) activity was spectrophotometrically measured as oxidation of guaiacol in the presence of hydrogen peroxide80. PPO (EC 1.10.3.1) was assayed at 490 nm81.

Oxidative stress markers

ROS contents were also measured in aged seeds. Superoxide radicals were measured at 540 nm by monitoring the nitrite formation from hydroxylamine hydrochloride. Malondialdehyde contents MDA values (μmol/g FW) were recorded in the mesophyll tissues produced upon oxidation of membrane lipids due to stress82 and H2O2 content was determined as a change in colour due to the formation of chromic acetate at 570 nm83.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of data collected during the study was done by using the complete randomized design and randomized block design as described by Panse and Sukhatme84. All the values are described as a mean of the replicates with the evaluation of CD at 5% level of significance. The significant differences among treatment groups were determined using Tukey Kramer HSD at p = 0.05.

Conclusion

This study concludes that nano-priming cotton seeds with zinc oxide (ZnO) and titanium dioxide (TiO2) nanoparticles significantly improves seed storage and quality traits. The nanoparticle-treated seeds of two American cotton varieties (H 1300 and H 1098-i) exhibited enhanced germination rates, increased seedling length, and greater seedling dry weight compared to untreated seeds. Additionally, the treatment maintained seed viability and vigor over 12 months, as evidenced by lower electrical conductivity and higher dehydrogenase activity. These findings suggest that ZnO and TiO2 nanoparticles mitigate the adverse effects of biotic and abiotic stresses during storage, thereby preserving seed quality. The chemical reduction synthesis method for nanoparticles and their subsequent application through nano-priming is an effective strategy for extending the storability and improving the germination performance of cotton seeds. Study affirms that the application of metal oxide NPs which enter seeds through cell walls due to their small size owns slow release of Zn and Ti could improve various physiological and biochemical responses in cotton seeds to augment source activity.

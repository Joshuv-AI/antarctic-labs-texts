Capillary L p L_{p} -Christoffel-Minkowski problem
Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki

Abstract
. We solve the capillary L p L_{p} -Christoffel–Minkowski problem in the half-space for 1 < p < k + 1 1<p<k+1 in the class of even hypersurfaces. A crucial ingredient is a non-collapsing estimate that yields lower bounds for both the height and the capillary support function. Our result extends the capillary Christoffel–Minkowski existence result of [ HIS25 ] .



1.
Introduction

The problem of prescribing area measures of convex hypersurfaces originates in the classical works of Christoffel
[
Chr65
]
, Minkowski
[
Min97
,
Min03
]
, Aleksandrov
[
Ale56
]
, Nirenberg
[
Nir57
]
and Pogorelov
[
Pog52
,
Pog71
]
, which established the modern interplay between convex geometry and fully nonlinear elliptic equations. In the smooth setting, the Christoffel–Minkowski problem seeks a smooth, strictly convex hypersurface whose
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the principal radii of curvature agrees with a given function on the sphere. This direction was further developed in the works of Firey and Berg
[
Fir67
,
Fir70
,
Ber69
]
.

Over the past decades, the Christoffel–Minkowski problem has seen substantial progress. For the top-order case
k=n
, corresponding to the classical Minkowski problem, the situation is by now well understood: the seminal works of Cheng–Yau
[
CY76
]
and Caffarelli
[
Caf90a
,
Caf90b
]
provide an existence and regularity theory for the underlying fully nonlinear equation. For intermediate orders
1<k<n
, the picture is less complete, although
[
GM03
,
STW04
]
provide a far-reaching existence result for the Christoffel–Minkowski problem in the smooth setting. See also
[
BHO25
,
MU25
]
for the recent break-through in the rotationally symmetric case.

The
L_{p}
-extension of the Christoffel–Minkowski problem, introduced by Lutwak
[
Lut93
]
in the framework of the Brunn–Minkowski–Firey theory, replaces the classical area measures by their
L_{p}
analogues and leads to the curvature equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[h])=h^{p-1}\phi\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n},

for the support function
h
of a smooth, strictly convex body/hypersurface, where
\tau^{\sharp}[h]=g^{-1}\cdot(\nabla^{2}h+hg)
and
g
denotes the standard metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem has since been the subject of intensive study and has developed into a mature theory over a broad range of
p
; see
[
LO95
,
CW06
,
BLYZ13
,
HLYZ16
,
BBCY19
,
HXZ21
,
GLW22
,
LXYZ24
]
and also
[
CW00
,
BIS19
,
LWW20
,
CL21
,
BIS21b
,
BG23
]
. In contrast, for
k<n
the situation is more fragmentary as the intermediate
L_{p}
-area measures remain, in general, much less understood. In the smooth case, however, and in particular for
p>1
with even data on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, one now has a well-developed set of results: existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions, together with constant rank theorems ensuring strict convexity; see, for instance,
[
GM03
,
HMS04
,
GLM06
,
GMZ06
,
GX18
,
Iva19
,
BIS23a
,
BIS23b
,
HI24
,
Zha24
,
CH25
]
and
[
BIS21a
,
HLX24
,
LW24
]
.

A natural question is how this picture changes in the presence of a boundary. In the capillary setting, one considers hypersurfaces in the half-space that meet a fixed supporting hyperplane at a prescribed contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. For the top-order case
k=n
, capillary versions of the
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem have been developed in a series of recent works. For
p\geq 1
, Mei, Wang and Weng solved the capillary
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem via the continuity method in
[
MWW25a
,
MWW25c
]
. For
-(n+1)<p<1
, even solutions were constructed in
[
HI25
]
by means of an iterative scheme based on the curvature image operator, and a unified curvature flow approach was later introduced in
[
HHI25
]
, treating the even capillary
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem for all
p>-(n+1)
.

For
k<n
, the capillary analogue of the Christoffel–Minkowski problem prescribes
\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])
on the capillary spherical cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and couples the interior equation with a Robin boundary condition encoding the contact angle. This capillary analogue was solved in
[
HIS25
]
, where the existence of smooth, strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary hypersurfaces was established under conditions on the prescribed function that are tailored to the applicability of a constant rank theorem. The existence of a solution to the capillary Christoffel-Minkowski problem was also established in
[
MWW25c
]
, subject to an additional assumption concerning the existence of a suitable homotopy path.

The aim of this paper is to extend the work
[
HIS25
]
to the
L_{p}
-framework in the range
1<p<k+1
. In analogy with the closed case
[
GM03
,
GX18
]
, we study the prescribed curvature equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])=s^{p-1}\phi\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}

for an even, positive, smooth function
\phi
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, together with the capillary boundary condition

(1.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}s=\cot\theta\,s\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<k+1
,
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
be a positive function satisfying

(1.4)

\displaystyle\phi(-\zeta_{1},\ldots,-\zeta_{n},\zeta_{n+1})=\phi(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},\zeta_{n+1})\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi^{-\frac{1}{p+k-1}}+g\phi^{-\frac{1}{p+k-1}}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}

and the boundary condition

(1.6)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{-\frac{1}{p+k-1}}\leq\cot\theta\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{p+k-1}}\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Then there exists a unique even, strictly convex, capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with contact angle
\theta
whose capillary support function
s
solves

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])&=s^{p-1}\phi&&\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}s&=\cot\theta\,s&&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the basic capillary geometry in the half-space and fix notation. Section
3
is devoted to non-collapsing estimates; i.e. a lower bound for the height of the hypersurface, both in the rotationally symmetric and in the general even case. In Section
4
we derive curvature and regularity estimates for solutions of (
1.7
). In Section
5
we prove a capillary constant rank theorem for our equation. Finally, in Section
6
we complete the proof of
1.1
by establishing existence and uniqueness.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x_{n+1}>0\}

be the upper half-space with boundary
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x_{n+1}=0\}
. The unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}
, and we write
\mathbb{S}^{n}
for the unit ball.

(1)

Support functions of convex bodies.

For a bounded convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the support function

h_{K}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle h_{K}(u):=\sup\{\langle x,u\rangle:x\in K\},\quad u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

When no confusion can arise, we simply write
h:=h_{K}
.

(2)

Area measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
a bounded convex set and
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
. The
k
-th area measure
S_{k}(K,\cdot)
is the finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
appearing in the classical local Steiner formula. If
K
is smooth and strictly convex with principal radii of curvature
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
at a point with outer unit normal
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then

(2.3)

\displaystyle dS_{k}(K,u)=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\,d\sigma(u),

where
d\sigma
denotes the spherical Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
If
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is an
m
-dimensional linear subspace and

K\subset L
, we write
S_{k}^{L}(K,\cdot)
for the
k
-th area measure of

K
viewed as a convex set in
L
.

(3)

Hausdorff measure and subspheres.

For any integer
d\geq 1
, we write
\mathcal{H}^{d}
for the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{d}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:|x|=1\}

for the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. For a linear subspace

L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of dimension
m
, we identify
\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L
with the unit sphere in
L
.
We also write

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:\langle x,e_{n+1}\rangle\geq\cos\theta\},\quad\mathcal{C}_{\theta}:=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}.

Integrals of the form

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f,\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}f,\quad\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}f,\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L}f,\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f,

are always understood with respect to the restriction of the appropriate Hausdorff measure
(thus,
\mathcal{H}^{n}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,
\mathcal{H}^{m-1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L
, and
\mathcal{H}^{d}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
).
We also write

(2.7)

\displaystyle\omega_{d}:=\mathcal{H}^{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d}),

so that
\omega_{d}
is the surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth, compact, connected, orientable hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with
\mathrm{int}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is called a capillary hypersurface with contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
if

(2.8)

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e_{n+1}\rangle=\cos\theta\quad\text{on }\partial\Sigma,

where
\nu
is the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
.

The model capillary surface is

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\{\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}:|\zeta+\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}|=1\}.

Via the translation

(2.10)

\displaystyle T(\zeta):=\zeta+\cos\theta\,e_{n+1},

we may identify
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
with
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
.

We also define

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}:=\left\{\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\mid|\zeta+r\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}|=r\right\}.

Note that the radius of
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
is
r\sin\theta
.

We call
\Sigma
strictly convex if the enclosed region
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a convex body (i.e. compact, convex, with non-empty interior) and the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is positive definite. For a strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
, the capillary Gauss map is defined as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}=\nu-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}:\Sigma\to\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

This is a diffeomorphism onto the capillary spherical cap, see
[
MWWX25
, Lem. 2.2]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a strictly convex, capillary hypersurface. The capillary support function
s:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
of
\Sigma
is defined by

(2.13)

\displaystyle s(\zeta)=\langle\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta),\zeta+\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}\rangle.

For the model cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, the capillary support function is

(2.14)

\displaystyle\ell(\zeta)=\sin^{2}\theta-\cos\theta\langle\zeta,e_{n+1}\rangle.

On
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
we also write
g
for the round metric,
\nabla
for its
Levi-Civita connection and
\nabla^{2}
for the covariant Hessian.
For a function
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
we set

(2.15)

\displaystyle\tau[f]:=\nabla^{2}f+f\,g,\quad\tau^{\sharp}[f]:=g^{-1}\cdot\tau[f],

so that
\tau^{\sharp}[f]
is a symmetric endomorphism of
T\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Its
eigenvalues are denoted by
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
, and

\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[f])
means
\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
.
We also write
\nabla_{\mu}f
for the covariant derivative in the
direction of the outward unit conormal
\mu
along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

For a symmetric matrix
A=(a_{ij})
with eigenvalues

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
we write

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{ij}(A):=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial a_{ij}}(A),

and for
F=\sigma_{k}^{1/k}
we set

(2.17)

\displaystyle F^{ij}(A):=\frac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}(A)=\frac{1}{k}\,\sigma_{k}(A)^{\frac{1}{k}-1}\sigma_{k}^{ij}(A).

When
A=\tau^{\sharp}[s]
for some function
s
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we abbreviate

\sigma_{k}^{ij}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])
and
F^{ij}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])
by

\sigma_{k}^{ij}
and
F^{ij}
, respectively.

Writing points of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1})
, let
\mathcal{R}
denote the reflection

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1}):=(-x_{1},\dots,-x_{n},x_{n+1}).

A function
\varphi:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
is called even if
\varphi\circ\mathcal{R}=\varphi
, and we say that a capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
(or its capillary support function
s
) is even if

(2.19)

\displaystyle x\in\Sigma\implies\mathcal{R}(x)\in\Sigma.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
. Let
s_{0},\dots,s_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
be capillary
support functions. Denote by
Q_{k}
the linear polarization of

\sigma_{k}
on symmetric endomorphisms of
T\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, i.e. the unique symmetric
multilinear map such that

(2.20)

\displaystyle Q_{k}(A,\dots,A)=\frac{\sigma_{k}(A)}{\binom{n}{k}}\quad\text{for every symmetric endomorphism }A.

The capillary mixed volume of
s_{0},\dots,s_{k}
is defined by

(2.21)

\displaystyle V(s_{0},\dots,s_{k},\underbrace{\ell,\ldots,\ell}_{(n-k)-\text{times}}):=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{0}Q_{k}\bigl(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}],\dots,\tau^{\sharp}[s_{k}],\underbrace{\tau^{\sharp}[\ell],\dots,\tau^{\sharp}[\ell]}_{(n-k)-\text{times}}\bigr).

In particular, one has

(2.22)

\displaystyle V(s_{0},\underbrace{s,\dots,s}_{k-\text{times}},\ell,\dots,\ell)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{0}\frac{\sigma_{k}\bigl(\tau^{\sharp}[s]\bigr)}{\binom{n}{k}}.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
be a strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary hypersurface.
For
t>0
define

(2.23)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}:\Sigma\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}},\quad\phi_{t}(x):=x+t\tilde{\nu}(x).

Then
\Sigma_{t}:=\phi_{t}(\Sigma)
is a strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary hypersurface. Moreover, the (standard) outer parallel convex body

(2.24)

\displaystyle K_{t}:=\bigl(\widehat{\Sigma}-t\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}\bigr)+t\,\mathbb{B},

and the capillary outer parallel convex body are related via
\widehat{\Sigma_{t}}=K_{t}\cap\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.
In addition, we have
\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma+t\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
P=\{x_{n+1}=0\}
. Define
f(x)=\langle x,e_{n+1}\rangle=x_{n+1}
and

(2.25)

\displaystyle g(x)=\langle\tilde{\nu}(x),e_{n+1}\rangle=\langle\nu(x),e_{n+1}\rangle-\cos\theta.

Then

(2.26)

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{t}(x),e_{n+1}\rangle=f(x)+t\,g(x).

Step 1.

On
\partial\Sigma
we have
f=0
(since
\partial\Sigma\subset P
) and
g=0
(by capillarity),
hence
(f+tg)(x)=0
for
x\in\partial\Sigma
, i.e.
\phi_{t}(\partial\Sigma)\subset P
. Moreover, since
\nu(\operatorname{int}(\Sigma))\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
,
f+tg>0
on
\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
for any
t>0
.

Step 2.

Let
x\in\Sigma
and choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of
T_{x}\Sigma

consisting of principal directions, so that

(2.27)

\displaystyle{}^{\Sigma}\nabla_{e_{i}}\nu=\kappa_{i}e_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

with principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
.
We have
{}^{\Sigma}\nabla_{e_{i}}\tilde{\nu}={}^{\Sigma}\nabla_{e_{i}}\nu
, and therefore

(2.28)

\displaystyle d\phi_{t}(e_{i})=e_{i}+t\,{}^{\Sigma}\nabla_{e_{i}}\tilde{\nu}=e_{i}+t\,{}^{\Sigma}\nabla_{e_{i}}\nu=(1+t\kappa_{i})e_{i}.

Thus
d\phi_{t}(T_{x}\Sigma)
is spanned by
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
,
\phi_{t}
is a smooth immersion, and the oriented unit normal of
\Sigma_{t}=\phi_{t}(\Sigma)

at
y=\phi_{t}(x)
equals
\nu(x)
, i.e.

(2.29)

\displaystyle\nu_{t}(y)=\nu(x)\quad\text{for }y=\phi_{t}(x).

Next we show that
\phi_{t}
is an injective immersion and thus an embedding. Assume
\phi_{t}(x)=\phi_{t}(x^{\prime})
for some

x,x^{\prime}\in\Sigma
. Then

(2.30)

\displaystyle x+t(\nu(x)-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1})=x^{\prime}+t(\nu(x^{\prime})-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}),

hence

(2.31)

\displaystyle x-x^{\prime}=t\bigl(\nu(x^{\prime})-\nu(x)\bigr).

Taking the inner product with
\nu(x)
gives

(2.32)

\displaystyle\langle x-x^{\prime},\nu(x)\rangle=t\bigl(\langle\nu(x^{\prime}),\nu(x)\rangle-1\bigr).

Since
\widehat{\Sigma}
is convex and
\nu(x)
is the outer normal vector to
\Sigma
at
x
,

(2.33)

\displaystyle\langle x^{\prime}-x,\nu(x)\rangle\leq 0.

On the other hand,
\langle\nu(x^{\prime}),\nu(x)\rangle\leq 1
, hence by (
2.32
)

(2.34)

\displaystyle\langle x-x^{\prime},\nu(x)\rangle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\nu(x^{\prime}),\nu(x)\rangle=1.

Thus
\nu(x^{\prime})=\nu(x)
. Since
\Sigma
is strictly convex, the Gauss map

\nu:\Sigma\to\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}
is injective, hence
x^{\prime}=x
.

Step 3.

If
y=\phi_{t}(x)
with
x\in\partial\Sigma
, then by step 1 and step 2 we have
y\in P
and

\nu_{t}(y)=\nu(x)
. Therefore,

(2.35)

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{t}(y),e_{n+1}\rangle=\langle\nu(x),e_{n+1}\rangle=\cos\theta,

so
\Sigma_{t}
meets
P
with the same contact angle
\theta
.

Step 4.

Let
x\in\Sigma
and set

(2.36)

\displaystyle y:=\phi_{t}(x)=x+t(\nu(x)-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}).

We claim that
y\in\partial K_{t}
and that
\nu(x)
is an outer normal of
K_{t}
at
y
.

Note that
y\in K_{t}
. Since
\nu(x)
is an outer unit normal of the convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
at
x
, we have

(2.37)

\displaystyle\langle z-x,\nu(x)\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall\,z\in\widehat{\Sigma}.

Let
w\in K_{t}
. Then for some
z\in\widehat{\Sigma}
and
b\in\mathbb{B}
:

(2.38)

\displaystyle w=(z-t\cos\theta\,e_{n+1})+tb.

Moreover, we have

(2.39)

\displaystyle\langle w-y,\nu(x)\rangle=\langle z-x,\nu(x)\rangle+t\langle b,\nu(x)\rangle-t.

Using (
2.37
), we obtain

(2.40)

\displaystyle\langle w-y,\nu(x)\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall\,w\in K_{t}.

Hence we must have
y\in\partial K_{t}
and
\nu(x)
is an outer normal of
K_{t}
at
y
.

Step 5.
Let
L_{t}=K_{t}\cap\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.
We prove

(2.41)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}=\phi_{t}(\Sigma)\subset\partial L_{t}.

If
x\in\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
, then by step 1, we have

(2.42)

\displaystyle y=\phi_{t}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

Together with
y\in\partial K_{t}
(by step 4) this implies
y\in\partial L_{t}\setminus P
.

If
x\in\partial\Sigma
, then by step 1,
\phi_{t}(\partial\Sigma)\subset P
, so
y\in P
.
Let
x_{j}\in\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
be any sequence with
x_{j}\to x
. Set
y_{j}:=\phi_{t}(x_{j})
. By continuity,
y_{j}\to y
.
Since
y_{j}\in\partial L_{t}
the limit point
y
belongs to
\partial L_{t}\cap P
.

Step 6.

We prove
\overline{\partial L_{t}\setminus P}=\Sigma_{t}
. By steps 1, 2 and 5,

(2.43)

\displaystyle\operatorname{int}(\Sigma_{t})=\phi_{t}(\operatorname{int}(\Sigma))\subset\partial L_{t}\setminus P\implies\Sigma_{t}\subset\overline{\partial L_{t}\setminus P}.

It remains to prove
\partial L_{t}\setminus P\subset\Sigma_{t}\setminus P
.

Let
y\in\partial L_{t}\setminus P
. Then
y\in\partial K_{t}
. Suppose
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
is an outer unit normal to
K_{t}
at
y
, i.e.

(2.44)

\displaystyle\langle w-y,u\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall\,w\in K_{t}.

We may write

(2.45)

\displaystyle y=(x-t\cos\theta\,e_{n+1})+tb,\quad x\in\widehat{\Sigma},\quad b\in\mathbb{B}.

We claim that
b=u
,
x\in\partial\widehat{\Sigma}
, and
u
is an outer normal of
\widehat{\Sigma}
at
x
.

Indeed, take any
x_{0}\in\widehat{\Sigma}
and any
c\in\mathbb{B}
, and set

(2.46)

\displaystyle w=(x_{0}-t\cos\theta\,e_{n+1})+tc\in K_{t}.

Plugging this
w
and (
2.45
) into (
2.44
) gives

(2.47)

\displaystyle 0\geq\langle w-y,u\rangle=\langle x_{0}-x,u\rangle+t\langle c-b,u\rangle.

With
x_{0}=x
and
c=u
,

(2.48)

\displaystyle 1\leq\langle b,u\rangle\implies b=u.

Now with
c=b=u
, the inequality becomes

(2.49)

\displaystyle 0\geq\langle x_{0}-x,u\rangle\quad\forall\,x_{0}\in\widehat{\Sigma},

so

Remark 2.8
.

The capillary
k
-th area measure
S_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)
is defined on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
via the local Steiner formula and is absolutely continuous with
respect to spherical Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
; in particular, every Borel set
\omega\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
with
\omega\subset\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
satisfies

. If
\omega\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is a Borel set with
\omega\Subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
, then

(2.64)

\displaystyle S_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},\omega)=\ell\,S_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma},T\omega),

so on such sets the capillary
k
-th area measure agrees (up to the weight
\ell
) with the restriction of the classical
k
-th area measure of
\widehat{\Sigma}
.

A difference can appear when
\omega
meets
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. By construction,
S_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)
carries no singular part supported on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, whereas
S_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)
may have additional mass on normals associated with
\partial\Sigma
. In particular,
for

the measure

may charge sets of normals whose images lie in
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, while
S_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)
assigns zero mass to such sets. It is therefore natural to regard
S_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)
as the absolutely continuous part of
S_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
, transported to
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
via

.

For the top-order case

,
\widehat{\Sigma}\cap\{x_{n+1}=0\}
contributes to

only through the direction
-e_{n+1}\notin\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
. Thus, there is no discrepancy between

and
S_{n}(\widehat{\Sigma},T(\cdot))
on Borel sets

\omega\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume
-\Sigma
is the graph of a convex function

on a bounded, closed convex set
\Omega

on

Since
-\Sigma
is the graph of
f
, we can write

\displaystyle-\Sigma=\{(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\in\Omega\},\quad f\leq 0,\quad f=0\ \text{on }\partial\Omega.

At a boundary point

, the upward unit normal of the graph of

is

(2.68)

\displaystyle\nu(x_{0}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Df(x^{\prime}_{0})|^{2}}}\bigl(-Df(x^{\prime}_{0}),1\bigr).

By the capillary condition,

(2.69)

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e_{n+1}\rangle=\cos\theta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Df|^{2}}}=\cos\theta,

hence

for every

. Since
f
is convex and
\Omega
is bounded and convex, the maximum of
|Df|
over
\Omega
is attained on

, so

(2.70)

\displaystyle|Df|\leq\tan\theta\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Let
x^{\prime}\in\Omega
and choose

(2.71)

\displaystyle|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|=\dist(x^{\prime},\partial\Omega).

Set

\Sigma
is even. Then

\displaystyle f(-x^{\prime})=f(x^{\prime})\quad\forall x^{\prime}\in\Omega,

C_{0}>1
and
q\in[1,p]
. Since
\Sigma
is rotationally symmetric,
r_{\mathrm{in}}=r_{\mathrm{out}}
and thus
s\leq r_{\mathrm{in}}+H
. Now, by
3.3
and
2.9
,

(3.33)

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq s_{\max}\leq r_{\mathrm{in}}+H\leq(1+\tan\theta)r_{\mathrm{in}},

where
c_{0}=\left(\frac{\phi_{0}}{\binom{n}{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1-p}}(1-\cos\theta)^{\frac{k}{k+1-p}}
. Hence

(3.34)

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{in}}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{1+\tan\theta},

and the lower bound on
H
follows. Due to
s\geq H\cos\theta
, the proof is complete.
∎

3.2.
Even hypersurfaces

The argument in
3.4
uses the capillary
L_{p}
-Christoffel-Minkowski equation mainly through the inequality
\det D^{2}f\geq\Lambda
for the Monge-Ampère measure of the graph function. Taken in isolation, this scalar inequality does not exclude degeneration of the base domain
\Omega
, and within this framework one cannot obtain a uniform positive lower bound for
H
without an additional geometric input such as the rotationally symmetric assumption in conjunction with the capillarity assumption.

For general even, strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary hypersurfaces we keep the full equation and work directly at the level of area measures. From a sequence with
H_{i}\to 0
we extract, by Blaschke’s selection theorem, a nontrivial limit body
K_{\infty}\subset e_{n+1}^{\perp}
with linear span
L=\operatorname{lin}(K_{\infty})
,
\dim L=m\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Using
3.5
and
3.11
we describe
S_{k}(K_{\infty},\cdot)
on belts
\mathcal{B}\Subset\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
at positive distance from
\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp}
. The measure identity together with
0<\phi_{0}\leq\phi\leq\phi_{1}
yields a uniform positive lower bound for the
h_{i}^{1-p}
-weighted curvature on each such belt, whereas for a body contained in
L
the structure of
S_{k}
forces these contributions to vanish (or tend to zero) as the belt shrinks. This contradiction rules out
H_{i}\to 0
and gives the desired uniform height lower bound in the general even case.

We also mention the work
[
PS24
]
, where a pointwise version of this argument for the standard
L_{p}
-Christoffel–Minkowski problem appeared. In the capillary setting such a pointwise argument is not available, since the capillary
k
-th area measure only records the absolutely continuous part of
S_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma},\cdot)
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
; see
2.8
.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
GKW11
]
, Thm. 6.2)
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a linear subspace with
\dim L=m
and
1\leq m\leq n
. Let
K\subset L
be a convex body (with nonempty interior in
L
) and
k\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
. Then, for every nonnegative measurable function
\psi
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}

(3.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi(u)\,dS_{k}(K,u)=c_{m,k}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\cap L}I(\xi)\,dS_{k}^{L}(K,\xi),

where

(3.36)

\displaystyle I(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-m}\cap L^{\perp}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\psi(\sin\beta\,\xi+\cos\beta\,\eta)\sin^{m-k-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta\,d\eta,

and

\displaystyle c_{m,k}:=\frac{\binom{m-1}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}}.

Proof.

The integral formulation follows directly from

(3.38)

\displaystyle\binom{m-1}{k}\pi_{L,-k}^{*}S_{k}^{L}(K,\cdot)=\binom{n}{k}S_{k}(K,\cdot).

By the definition of the lifting operator
\pi_{L,-k}^{*}
(cf.
[
GKW11

(3.39)

\displaystyle\pi_{L,-k}^{*}S_{k}^{L}(K,A)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\cap L}\int_{H^{n+1-m}(L,\xi)\cap A}\langle\xi,w\rangle^{m-k-1}dw\,S_{k}^{L}(K,d\xi).

In our coordinates,

\langle\xi,w\rangle=\sin\beta

(n+1-m)
-dimensional half-sphere

(3.40)

\displaystyle H^{n+1-m}(L,\xi):=\bigl\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus L^{\perp}:\operatorname{pr}_{L}(w)=\xi\bigr\}.

we have

is the spherical projection of

\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L
.
∎

.

Let

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
K_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a sequence of origin-symmetric convex bodies with

K_{i}\to K_{\infty}
in the Hausdorff metric and assume that

K_{\infty}\subset e_{n+1}^{\perp}
is not a single point. Let

(3.44)

\displaystyle L:=\operatorname{lin}(K_{\infty})\subset e_{n+1}^{\perp},\quad m:=\dim L\in\{1,\dots,n\},\quad\mathcal{U}:=\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\cap L.

Then there exist constants
c_{\star}>0
and
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, angles

0<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\theta
, and an open spherical cap

\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-m}\cap L^{\perp}
centered at
e_{n+1}
, such that
for the belt

(3.45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\bigl\{u=\sin\beta\,\xi+\cos\beta\,\eta:\xi\in\mathcal{U},\eta\in\mathcal{V},\beta\in(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\bigr\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n},

the following hold:

(i)

\mathcal{B}\Subset\operatorname{int}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
and

\overline{\mathcal{B}}\cap(\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp})=\emptyset
;

(ii)

for all
i\geq i_{0}
and all
u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}
,

(3.46)

\displaystyle h_{K_{i}}(u)\geq c_{\star}\,\sin\beta_{1}.

Proof.

Write
h_{i}:=h_{K_{i}}
and
h_{\infty}:=h_{K_{\infty}}
. Since
K_{\infty}
has nonempty interior in
L
, there exists
c_{\star}>0
such that

(3.47)

\displaystyle h_{\infty}(\xi)\geq 4c_{\star}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{U}.

By the uniform convergence of
h_{i}\to h_{\infty}
, there exists
i_{0}
such that for all
i\geq i_{0}
,

(3.48)

\displaystyle h_{i}(\xi)\geq 2c_{\star}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{U}.

Since
K_{\infty}\subset L
, we have
h_{\infty}(\eta)=0
for every
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp}
.
Let
0<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\theta
. Choose
\epsilon>0
so small that
\epsilon<\theta-\beta_{2}
and define
the spherical cap
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-m}\cap L^{\perp}
by

(3.49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=\{\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-m}\cap L^{\perp}:\angle(\eta,e_{n+1})<\epsilon\}.

Then for any

and any
\beta\in[\beta_{1},\beta_{2}]

(3.50)

\displaystyle\langle\sin\beta\,\xi+\cos\beta\,\eta,e_{n+1}\rangle=\cos\beta\,\langle\eta,e_{n+1}\rangle\geq\cos\beta\,\cos\epsilon\geq\cos(\beta+\epsilon)>\cos\theta,

\overline{\mathcal{B}}

\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp}
.

Next, since
h_{\infty}\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp}
, uniform convergence of

implies
(after increasing

if necessary) that for all

(3.51)

\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in\overline{\mathcal{V}}}h_{i}(\eta)\leq c_{\star}\tan\beta_{1}.

i\geq i_{0}
and

. Then
u=\sin\beta\,\xi+\cos\beta\,\eta
for some

\xi\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}
,
\eta\in\overline{\mathcal{V}}
,
\beta\in[\beta_{1},\beta_{2}]
.

with

.
Since

is origin-symmetric, we have

, hence

(3.52)

\displaystyle h_{i}(u)\geq\langle x_{i},u\rangle=\sin\beta\,h_{i}(\xi)+\cos\beta\,\langle x_{i},\eta\rangle\geq\sin\beta\,h_{i}(\xi)-\cos\beta\,h_{i}(\eta).

Using (
3.48

3.51
), and

\cos\beta\leq\cos\beta_{1}

(3.53)

\displaystyle h_{i}(u)\geq\sin\beta_{1}\,(2c_{\star})-\cos\beta_{1}\left(c_{\star}\tan\beta_{1}\right)=c_{\star}\sin\beta_{1},

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose
\Sigma
is an even, strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary hypersurface whose capillary support function
s
satisfies (
3.1
).
Then

(3.54)

\displaystyle H=\max_{x\in\Sigma}\langle x,e_{n+1}\rangle\geq H_{\star}>0,\quad H_{\star}\cos\theta\leq s\leq C_{0}

with
H_{\star}=H_{\star}(k,p,\theta,\phi_{0},\phi_{1},C_{0})
.

Proof.

Let
K
denote the union of
\widehat{\Sigma}
and its reflection across the hyperplane
\{x_{n+1}=0\}
and set
h:=h_{K}
.
Assume for contradiction that there exist a sequence
(q_{i},\psi_{i},\Sigma_{i},s_{i},K_{i},h_{i})
satisfying (
3.1
) with
\phi=\psi_{i}
,
q_{i}\in[1,p]
and
\phi_{0}\leq\psi_{i}\leq\phi_{1}
, while

(3.55)

\displaystyle H_{i}:=s_{i}((1-\cos\theta)e_{n+1})\to 0,\quad q_{i}\to q_{\ast}\in[1,p].

Note that by
3.1
, we have

(3.56)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{i}\leq C_{0}\quad\text{for all }i.

In view of
[
HIS25
, Lem. 4.2]
and the Blaschke selection theorem, after passing to a subsequence,
K_{i}\to K_{\infty}
in the Hausdorff metric. Then
K_{\infty}\subset e_{n+1}^{\perp}
is origin-symmetric and it is not a point (by
3.3
).

Let
L:=\operatorname{lin}(K_{\infty})
and
m:=\dim L\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Applying
3.7
, we find
\mathcal{B}\Subset\operatorname{int}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
and constants
c_{\star}>0
,
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\theta
such that
for all
i\geq i_{0}
and all
u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}
,

(3.57)

\displaystyle h_{i}(u)\geq c_{\star}\sin\beta_{1}.

Since
\beta_{1}
can be chosen so that
c_{\star}\sin\beta_{1}<1
, and
q_{i}\in[1,p]
, we obtain on
\overline{\mathcal{B}}
:

(3.58)

\displaystyle C_{0}^{1-p}\leq h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}(u)\leq(c_{\star}\sin\beta_{1})^{1-p}\quad\text{for all }u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}},\ i\geq i_{0}.

Next, note that
m\geq k
. Otherwise, if
m<k
, by
3.9
, then we have

(3.59)

\displaystyle S_{k}(K_{\infty},\overline{\mathcal{B}})=0.

Since
S_{k}(K_{i},\cdot)\to S_{k}(K_{\infty},\cdot)
, it follows that

(3.60)

\displaystyle S_{k}(K_{i},\overline{\mathcal{B}})\to 0,

and by (
3.58
),

(3.61)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)\leq(c_{\star}\sin\beta_{1})^{1-p}S_{k}(K_{i},\mathcal{B})\to 0.

On the other hand, we have

(3.62)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)=\int_{T^{-1}\mathcal{B}}\psi_{i}\geq\phi_{0}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{B})>0,

a contradiction. Thus
m\geq k
.

Case 1:
m\geq k+1
.

Recall that
K_{\infty}
has non-empty interior in
L
, so for
\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\cap L
we have
S^{L}_{k}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{U})>0
.
Choose
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
as in
3.7
. Then by
3.6
,

(3.63)

\displaystyle S_{k}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{B})=c_{m,k}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}(\mathcal{V})S_{k}^{L}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{U})\int_{\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\sin^{m-k-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta.

Using (
3.58
), we obtain for
i\geq i_{0}
,

(3.64)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)\geq C_{0}^{1-p}S_{k}(K_{i},\mathcal{B}).

Taking
\liminf
and using the weak convergence of
S_{k}(K_{i},\cdot)
,

(3.65)

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{0}^{1-p}S_{k}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{0}^{1-p}c_{m,k}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}(\mathcal{V})S_{k}^{L}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{U})\int_{\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\sin^{m-k-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta.

On the other hand, we have

(3.66)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{-1}\mathcal{B}}\psi_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{1}\,\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}\,\mathcal{H}^{m-1}(\mathcal{U})\mathcal{H}^{n-m}(\mathcal{V})\int_{\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\sin^{m-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta.

Since the right-hand side is independent of
i
, we have

(3.67)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{1}\,\mathcal{H}^{m-1}(\mathcal{U})\mathcal{H}^{n-m}(\mathcal{V})\int_{\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\sin^{m-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta.

Combining the upper and lower bounds and cancelling the common factor

\mathcal{H}^{n-m}(\mathcal{V})
we obtain

(3.68)

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m-1}{k}C_{0}^{1-p}}{\phi_{1}}S_{k}^{L}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{U})\leq\mathcal{H}^{m-1}(\mathcal{U})\,\frac{\int_{\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\sin^{m-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta}{\int_{\beta_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\sin^{m-k-1}\beta\,\cos^{n-m}\beta\,d\beta}.

Letting
\beta_{2}\downarrow\beta_{1}
we get

(3.69)

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m-1}{k}C_{0}^{1-p}}{\phi_{1}}S_{k}^{L}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{U})\leq\mathcal{H}^{m-1}(\mathcal{U})(\sin\beta_{1})^{k}.

Letting
\beta_{1}\downarrow 0
forces the right-hand side to tend to
0
. This is a contradiction.

Case 2:
m=k
.

In this case, by
3.11
(applied after approximating
K_{\infty}
by polytopes) we have

(3.70)

\displaystyle S_{k}(K_{\infty},\omega)=0

for every Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
with

\omega\cap(\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp})=\emptyset
.
In particular, since
\overline{\mathcal{B}}\cap(\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap L^{\perp})=\emptyset
, we have

(3.71)

\displaystyle S_{k}(K_{\infty},\overline{\mathcal{B}})=0.

Using (
3.58
) and weak convergence again, we get

(3.72)

\displaystyle S_{k}(K_{i},\mathcal{B})\to 0,

and hence

(3.73)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)\leq(\sup_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}})\,S_{k}(K_{i},\mathcal{B})\leq(c_{\star}\sin\beta_{1})^{1-p}S_{k}(K_{i},\mathcal{B})\to 0.

On the other hand,

(3.74)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}\int_{\mathcal{B}}h_{i}^{1-q_{i}}\,dS_{k}(K_{i},u)=\int_{T^{-1}\mathcal{B}}\psi_{i}\geq\phi_{0}\,\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathcal{B})>0,

Thus in all cases our assumption

leads to a contradiction. Therefore there exists

, depending only on

(3.75)

\displaystyle H\geq H_{\star}

for every even, strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary solution of (

) with

and

.

(3.76)

\displaystyle s\geq H_{\star}\cos\theta.

∎

Remark 3.9
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a non-empty convex set and
L=\operatorname{lin}(K)
. Assume that
k>m=\dim L
. We show that
S_{k}(K,\cdot)\equiv 0
. For a Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\rho>0
define

(3.77)

\displaystyle B_{\rho}(K,\omega)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:0<d(K,x)\leq\rho,\,u(K,x)\in\omega\},

where
d(K,x)
is the Euclidean distance from
x
to
K
,
p(K,x)
is a nearest point of
K
to
x
, and

(3.78)

\displaystyle u(K,x):=\frac{x-p(K,x)}{\lvert x-p(K,x)\rvert}.

By the local Steiner formula (cf.
[
Sch14
, (4.13)]
),

(3.79)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n+1}\bigl(B_{\rho}(K,\omega)\bigr)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{j}\rho^{n+1-j}S_{j}(K,\omega).

Since

, we have

(3.80)

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:d(K,x)\leq\rho\}\subset(K+\rho B_{L})+\rho B_{L^{\perp}},

where
B_{L}=\mathbb{B}\cap L
and
B_{L^{\perp}}=\mathbb{B}\cap L^{\perp}
are the unit balls in
L
and

, respectively. In particular, for

:

(3.81)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n+1}\bigl(B_{\rho}(K,\omega)\bigr)\leq\mathcal{H}^{m}(K+\rho B_{L})\,\mathcal{H}^{n+1-m}(\rho B_{L^{\perp}})\leq C\rho^{n+1-m},

On the other hand, if

, then (
3.79
) yields

(3.82)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n+1}\bigl(B_{\rho}(K,\omega)\bigr)\geq c\rho^{n+1-k},\quad c=c(n,K,\omega).

Combining (

) and (

) gives

(3.83)

\displaystyle c\rho^{n+1-k}\leq C\,\rho^{n+1-m}\qquad\text{for all }0<\rho\leq 1.

Since
k>m
, we get a contradiction by letting

.

, there holds

(4.4)

\displaystyle F^{ij}g_{ij}\sigma_{1}\leq F^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{ij}\sigma_{1}+ns^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}-\Delta(s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}).

We calculate

(4.5)

\displaystyle-k\Delta(s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}})=

\displaystyle(1-q)s^{\frac{q-1}{k}-1}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\sigma_{1}-n(1-q)s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{k}(q-1)(k+1-q)s^{\frac{q-1}{k}-2}|\nabla s|^{2}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle+2(1-q)s^{\frac{q-1}{k}-1}\langle\nabla s,\nabla\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\rangle-ks^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\Delta\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Due to the concavity of
F
, we have
\operatorname{tr}(\dot{F})\geq c_{k}
. By
3.8
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle 1/C_{1}\leq s\leq C_{1}

for some constant
C_{1}>1
depending only
n,k,p,\theta,\phi
. It follows from
[
HIS25
, Lem. 4.8]
that

(4.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla s|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sin\theta}.

Hence, if
\sigma_{1}
attains its maximum in the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle c_{k}\sigma_{1}\leq

\displaystyle nC_{1}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{0}}+nC_{1}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{0}}+C_{1}^{2}\big(\frac{C_{1}}{\sin\theta}\big)^{2}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle+2\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{\sin\theta}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{1}}+C_{1}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{2}},

where we also used that
q\in[1,p]
with
1<p<k+1
. Thus we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\leq C

for some constant
C=C(n,k,p,\theta,\phi)
.

Now we need to treat the case that the maximum of
\sigma_{1}
is attained at a boundary point, say
p_{\ast}
. Let
\{\mu\}\cup\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\geq 2}
be an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
\tau^{\sharp}[s]
at
p_{\ast}
such that
\tau_{ij}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
. Moreover, using

(4.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\tau_{\alpha\beta}=\bigl(\tau_{\mu\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}-\tau_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)\cot\theta,\quad 2\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n,

and
\nabla_{\mu}s=\cot\theta\,s
, we obtain at
p_{\ast}
that

(4.11)

\displaystyle 0\leq F^{\mu\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\cot\theta\Bigl((n+1)\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}-F^{\mu\mu}\sigma_{1}-\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{\mu}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\Bigl(\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}+\frac{q-1}{k}\cot\theta\,\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\Bigr)

and

(4.12)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\bigl|\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\bigr|}{\cot\theta\,F^{\mu\mu}}+\Bigl(n+1+\frac{q-1}{k}\Bigr)\frac{s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}}{F^{\mu\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{1}}}{\cot\theta\,F^{\mu\mu}}+(n+2)\,\frac{C_{1}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{0}}}{F^{\mu\mu}},

see
[
HIS25
, (4.4),(4.5)]
for details.

Next we show that
F^{\mu\mu}
cannot be very small. By (
4.6
), we get

(4.13)

\displaystyle c_{1}:=(\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\phi)C_{1}^{1-p}

\displaystyle\leq\phi s^{q-1}=\sigma_{k}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\mu}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})+\sigma_{k}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\mu}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})+c_{1}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\lambda_{\mu}F^{\mu\mu}+c_{3}(F^{\mu\mu})^{\frac{k}{k-1}},

where we used that

(4.14)

\displaystyle F^{\mu\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1-k}{k}}(\lambda)\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}(s^{q-1}\phi)^{\frac{1-k}{k}}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}C_{1}^{\frac{(p-1)(1-k)}{k}}\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{0}}^{1-k}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|\lambda_{\mu}).

Note that all these constants
c_{i}
depend only on
n,p,k,\theta,\phi
.

Substituting (
4.13
) in (
4.11
), we obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle 0\leq F^{\mu\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\sigma_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl((n+1)\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}-\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{\mu}\Bigr)\cot\theta

\displaystyle+s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\Bigl(\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}+\frac{q-1}{k}\cot\theta\,\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\cot\theta}{c_{2}}\sum_{i}F^{ii}\Bigl(-\frac{c_{1}}{F^{\mu\mu}}+c_{3}(F^{\mu\mu})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+C_{1}\Bigl(\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{1}}+\big(n+2\big)\cot\theta\,\|\phi^{\frac{1}{k}}\|_{C^{0}}\Bigr).

Hence
F^{\mu\mu}
cannot be small, and in view of (
4.12
),
\sigma_{1}
is bounded above and the bound depends only on
n,p,k,\theta,\phi
.
∎

4.1
, the higher-order regularity follows form
[
LT86
]
and Schauder estimate.

5.
Strict Convexity

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
,
1\leq k<n
and
q\geq 1
.
Suppose
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
satisfies

(5.1)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi^{-\frac{1}{q+k-1}}+g\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{q+k-1}}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

and the boundary condition

(5.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{-\frac{1}{q+k-1}}\leq\cot\theta\,\phi^{-\frac{1}{q+k-1}}\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Let
0\leq s\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
be a capillary function, i.e.

(5.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}s=\cot\theta\,s\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

with

(5.4)

\displaystyle\tau^{\sharp}[s]\geq 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

and suppose that
s
solves

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\bigl(\tau^{\sharp}[s]\bigr)=s^{q-1}\,\phi\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Denote by
\lambda_{1}
the smallest eigenvalue of
\tau^{\sharp}[s]
. If
s>0
, then

\lambda_{1}>0.

Proof.

The argument is the same as in
[
HIS25
, Thm. 3.1]
for
q=1
. Define

(5.6)

\displaystyle F=\sigma_{k}^{1/k},\quad f=\bigl(s^{q-1}\phi\bigr)^{1/k}.

When
\phi
satisfies (
5.1
), we have in the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
that

(5.7)

\displaystyle L[\lambda_{1}]:=F^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{ij}\lambda_{1}-c\bigl(\lambda_{1}+\lvert\nabla\lambda_{1}\rvert\bigr)\leq 0

in the viscosity sense; for details see
[
BIS23a
, Thm. 2.2]
or
[
CH25
, (3.20)]
. Therefore, it suffices to carry out the boundary analysis in Step 1 of the proof of
[
HIS25
, Thm. 3.1]
at a point
p_{\ast}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
where
\lambda_{1}(p_{\ast})=0
while
\lambda_{1}>0
in the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
: we need a boundary condition on
\phi
which ensures that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\tau_{ii}(p_{\ast})=0\implies\nabla_{\mu}\tau_{ii}(p_{\ast})\geq 0.

Choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
at
p_{\ast}
such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle e_{1}=\mu,\quad e_{\alpha}\in T_{p_{\ast}}\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\quad\text{ for }\alpha=2,\dots,n,

and
\tau^{\sharp}[s]
is diagonal in this frame at
p_{\ast}
such that
\tau_{ij}=\lambda_{i}\,\delta_{ij}
.

For
i=\alpha\geq 2
, (
5.8
) follows directly from the boundary identity (
4.10
). For
i=1
, note that (
5.5
) is equivalent to

(5.10)

\displaystyle F\bigl(\tau^{\sharp}[s]\bigr)=f\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Differentiating (
5.10
) in the
\mu
-direction gives

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}\,\nabla_{\mu}\tau_{ii}=\nabla_{\mu}f.

Using (
4.10
) for
\alpha\geq 2
, we obtain

(5.12)

\displaystyle F^{\mu\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\tau_{\mu\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}f+\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}F^{\alpha\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha\alpha}-\tau_{\mu\mu})\cot\theta.

At
p_{\ast}
we have
\tau_{\mu\mu}(p_{\ast})=\lambda_{1}(p_{\ast})=0
. By the
1
-homogeneity of
F
,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}\tau_{ii}=F(\tau)=f,

hence at
p_{\ast}
,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}F^{\alpha\alpha}\tau_{\alpha\alpha}=f.

Evaluating (
5.12
) at
p_{\ast}
yields

(5.15)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\tau_{\mu\mu}=\frac{\nabla_{\mu}f+f\cot\theta}{F^{\mu\mu}}.

Thus (
5.8
) for
i=1
holds provided that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\log f\geq-\cot\theta\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

It remains to express (
5.16
) in terms of
\phi
. Since

(5.17)

\displaystyle f=s^{\frac{q-1}{k}}\phi^{\frac{1}{k}},\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{\mu}\log s=\cot\theta\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

we require that

(5.18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\log\phi\geq-(k+q-1)\cot\theta\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

which is precisely (
5.2
).
∎

6.
Existence and uniqueness

For
q\in[1,p]
set

(6.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{q}:=\phi^{\frac{q+k-1}{p+k-1}}.

For
(q,s)
with
q\in[1,p]
and
s\in C^{l+2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
such that
s>0
in
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
we define

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} F(q,s)&=\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])-s^{q-1}\phi_{q}&&\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
G(q,s)&=\nabla_{\mu}s-\cot\theta\,s&&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

If
(F(q,s),G(q,s))=(0,0)
, then
s
solves

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])&=s^{q-1}\phi_{q}&&\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}s&=\cot\theta\,s&&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

For
q=1
, by
[
HIS25
, Thm. 1.2]
there exists a unique even, smooth, strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary solution

s_{1}
of this problem.

Assume now that
\phi_{0}\leq\phi\leq\phi_{1}
with
0<\phi_{0}<1<\phi_{1}
. Then, for every

q\in[1,p]
,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}^{\frac{q+k-1}{p+k-1}}\leq\phi_{q}\leq\phi_{1}^{\frac{q+k-1}{p+k-1}}.

Applying
3.1
,
3.3
and

3.8
with
\phi
replaced by
\phi_{q}
we obtain constants

C_{0},c_{0}>0
, independent of
q
, such that every even solution
s
of
(
6.3
) with
\tau^{\sharp}[s]>0
satisfies

(6.5)

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq s\leq C_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Moreover, since
\phi_{q}
satisfies the structural assumptions of
5.1
, when
s
is a solution of (
6.3
) with
\tau^{\sharp}[s]\geq 0
and
s>0
, we must have

(6.6)

\displaystyle\tau^{\sharp}[s]>0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Combining (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) with
4.1
and

4.2
, we obtain a uniform
C^{4,\alpha}
bound: there exists
C>0
such that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\|s\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})}\leq C

for all even capillary solutions
s
of (
6.3
) with
\tau^{\sharp}[s]>0
, uniformly in
q\in[1,p]
.

Let
R>C
and define the bounded open set

(6.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}:=\bigl\{s\in C^{l+2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta}):\|s\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})}<R,\ 2s>c_{0},\ \tau^{\sharp}[s]>0\bigr\}.

By (
6.5
), (
6.6
), and (
6.7
),

(6.9)

\displaystyle(F(q,s),G(q,s))\neq(0,0)\quad\text{for all }(q,s)\in[1,p]\times\partial\mathcal{O}.

Therefore, by
[
LLN17
, Thm. 1]
, for each
q\in[1,p]
there is a well-defined
integer-valued degree

(6.10)

\displaystyle d(q):=\deg\bigl((F(q,\cdot),G(q,\cdot)),\mathcal{O},0\bigr),

which is homotopy invariant in
q
, in particular,

d(1)=d(p)
.

Due to
[
HIS25
, Lem. 5.4]
, the linearized operator

\mathcal{L}:=D_{s}(F,G)(1,s_{1})

has trivial kernel (the only kernel directions in the full class correspond to
horizontal translations, which are odd). Together with
6.1
this implies that

\mathcal{L}
is an isomorphism. Hence, from
[
LLN17
, Thm. 1.1, Cor. 2.1]
it follows that
d(1)=\pm 1

and there exists
s\in\mathcal{O}
such that

(6.11)

\displaystyle(F(p,s),G(p,s))=(0,0).

Next, we establish uniqueness of solutions to (
1.7
)
in the class of even, strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces.

Assume that
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are two even, strictly convex capillary solutions to

(6.12)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])&=s^{p-1}\phi&&\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}s&=\cot\theta\,s&&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

Using the mixed-volume interpretation of
\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[\cdot])

we obtain

(6.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}s_{1}^{p-1}\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}])

\displaystyle=(n+1)\binom{n}{k}V\bigl(s_{2},\underbrace{s_{1},\ldots,s_{1}}_{k-\text{times}},\ell,\ldots,\ell\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq(n+1)\binom{n}{k}V\bigl(\underbrace{s_{1},\ldots,s_{1}}_{(k+1)-\text{times}},\ell,\ldots,\ell\bigr)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}V\bigl(\underbrace{s_{2},\ldots,s_{2}}_{(k+1)-\text{times}},\ell,\ldots,\ell\bigr)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{k+1}},

where we used Alexandrov-Fenchel’s inequality (see
[
MWWX25
, Thm. 3.1]
):

(6.14)

\displaystyle V(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\ldots,s_{n+1})^{2}\geq V(s_{1},s_{1},s_{3},\ldots,s_{n+1})V(s_{2},s_{2},s_{3},\ldots,s_{n+1}).

On the other hand, by the Hölder inequality we have

(6.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}s_{1}^{p-1}\phi\leq\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Combining (
6.13
) and (
6.15
) yields

(6.16)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\leq\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Rearranging, we obtain

(6.17)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{p-k-1}{p(k+1)}}\geq\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi\Bigr)^{\frac{p-k-1}{p(k+1)}}.

Since
1<p<k+1
, we obtain

(6.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi.

Interchanging
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
gives

(6.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi,

so in fact

(6.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{1}^{p}\phi=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s_{2}^{p}\phi.

Thus equality holds in (
6.13
), and by the equality case in the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality (
6.14
) we obtain
s_{1}=s_{2}
, since the equation is not scale invariant. This proves the uniqueness.

Lemma 6.1
.

(6.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})\to C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})\times C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{even}}(\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}]>0
, the matrix
[a^{ij}]
defined by

a^{ij}=\sigma_{k}^{ij}(\tau^{\sharp}[s_{1}])
is uniformly
positive definite on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, and

(6.22)

\displaystyle Lv=a^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{ij}v+\mathrm{tr}(a)v,\quad v\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})

is uniformly elliptic with
C^{\infty}
coefficients. Define the boundary operator

(6.23)

\displaystyle Mv=\nabla_{(-\mu)}v+\cot\theta\,v\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Using the stereographic projection from south pole
\Pi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{-e_{n+1}\}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Pi(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=\frac{x^{\prime}}{1+x_{n+1}}
, we can rewrite
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{M}
on
\Omega:=B_{\tan(\theta/2)}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(6.24)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lu:=a^{ij}(x)D_{ij}u+b^{i}(x)D_{i}u+c(x)u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
Mu:=\beta^{i}(x)D_{i}u+\cot\theta u&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

with
a^{ij},b^{i},c\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\beta^{i}\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, and

M
uniformly oblique:

(6.25)

\displaystyle\langle\beta(x),-\frac{x}{|x|}\rangle=\frac{1}{1+\cos\theta}\quad\text{for all }x\in\partial\Omega.

Therefore, by
[
Lie13
, Thm. 2.30]
, the map

(6.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:C^{2,\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})

\displaystyle\to C^{\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})\times C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(v)

\displaystyle:=\bigl(Lv,Mv\bigr),

is a Fredholm operator of index 0. In particular, we have

(6.27)

\displaystyle\dim\ker\mathcal{L}=\dim\mathrm{coker}\,\mathcal{L}.

Note that
\mathcal{L}
preserves evenness and also under the stereographic projection from the south pole, evenness is preserved:
if
v\circ\mathcal{R}=v
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and
u(x)=v(\Pi^{-1}(x))
, then
u(-x)=u(x)
on
B_{\tan(\theta/2)}(0)
.
By
[
HIS25
, Lem. 5.4]
, if
v\in C_{\text{even}}^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
and satisfies

(6.28)

\displaystyle Lv=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\quad Mv=0\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

then
v\equiv 0
. In other words,

(6.29)

\displaystyle\ker\mathcal{L}\cap C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})=\{0\}.

Thus, in the even class,

\dim\mathrm{coker}\,\mathcal{L}=\dim\ker\mathcal{L}=0

and
\mathcal{L}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

We could also use the continuity method to solve the capillary even
L_{p}
-Christoffel–Minkowski problem. We may interpolate between
1
and
\phi
via the path

H:[0,1]\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta}),\quad t\mapsto H(t,\cdot),

defined by

(6.30)

\displaystyle H(t,\zeta):=\begin{cases}\bigl((1-2t)+2t\phi(\zeta)^{-\frac{1}{p+k-1}}\bigr)^{-k},&0\leq t\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\\[6.00006pt]
\phi(\zeta)^{\frac{q(t)+k-1}{p+k-1}},&\tfrac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1,\end{cases}

where

q(t):=1+(p-1)(2t-1),\quad t\in\bigl[\tfrac{1}{2},1\bigr].

Then

(6.31)

\displaystyle H(0,\zeta)=1,\quad H\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\zeta\right)=\phi(\zeta)^{\frac{k}{p+k-1}},\quad H(1,\zeta)=\phi(\zeta).

Now consider the equation

(6.32)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}H(t,\cdot),&0\leq t\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\\
s^{q(t)-1}H(t,\cdot),&\tfrac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}s

\displaystyle=\cot\theta\,s.

For
t=0
, the model capillary support function
\ell
is the unique even solution of this problem. For every
t\in[0,1]
, the structural assumptions required by the constant rank theorem are satisfied. The closedness in the continuity method follows from the a priori estimates established above, while openness is as in the standard (closed) case.

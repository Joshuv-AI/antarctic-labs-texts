Compact objects with scalar charge embedded in a magnetic or electric field in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theories
Olivier Minazzoli, Maxime Wavasseur

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize the Schwarzschild-Melvin solution within Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theories to include non-null scalar charges, while remaining embedded in a magnetic or electric field à la Melvin . We then use this general solution to obtain the solution in the specific case of Entangled Relativity after a conformal transformation. This notably enables us to verify that the analytical solution used in [Arruga & Minazzoli 2021] in order to represent compact objects such as neutron stars in Entangled Relativity is indeed a good approximation of the exact solutions of Entangled Relativity when the background field goes to zero.

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we provided the generalization of the Melvin-Schwarzschild solution in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theories to the case of spherical compact objects with scalar hair in both the magnetic and electric cases. A possible next step could be to search for new solutions involving both electric and magnetic fields at the same time, akin to the recently discovered solutions in General Relativity
[
59
,
60
]
. However, we anticipate significant complications arising from the scalar–matter coupling. Another direction could be to initiate the procedure described in
[
40
]
to generate new magnetized solutions using a different seeding hairy solution, such as the Fisher–Janis–Newman–Winicour–Levi-Civita spacetime
[
61
]
.

We then used these solutions to obtain the solutions in Entangled Relativity, which is particular among the theories that can be written as an Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory in the
Einstein frame
(Eq. (
1
)) for several, albeit very distinct, reasons:

•

At the mathematical level, the scalar degree of freedom arising from the non-linearity of the coupling between curvature and matter is defined from the ratio between
\mathcal{L}_{m}
and
R
in the original frame (Eq. (
10
))—see Eq. (

Entangled frame
in Sec.

. Therefore, it is (naïvely) surprising that the theory still makes sense in the limit

, although it has been verified already with several exact solutions
[

-

). But more generally, we provided in Sec.
III.3
a general argument that any vacuum solution of General Relativity is a limit of a solution of Entangled Relativity when
\mathcal{L}_{m}\rightarrow 0
, albeit not the only one.

•

At the phenomenological level, it has a built-in decoupling—which was named an

in
[

in the framework of scalar-tensor theories—which freezes the scalar degree of freedom in many situations
[

, thereby recovering the phenomenology of General Relativity in the situations where it has been thoroughly tested.

•

At the epistemological level, it is more parsimonious than General Relativity in terms of dimensionful universal constants
[

and therefore, arguably, preferable from Occam’s razor principle, while it also better aligns with Einstein’s
Principle of Relativity of Inertia

At the theoretical level, it obliviates the Planck wall,

Appendix A
Just-Fisher-Janis-Robinson-Winicour solution in the Einstein and Entangled frames

In the Einstein-frame, the Just-Fisher-Janis-Robinson-Winicour solution in a specific radial coordinates reads
[
10
,
63
]

\displaystyle d\tilde{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{b}dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{-b}dr^{2}

(38)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{1-b}r^{2}\left[d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\right],

with

\varphi=\frac{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{\mathit{r}_{s}}{r}\right),

(39)

where
\mathit{r}_{s}
is the Schwarzschild radius and
b\in~]0,1]
is an arbitrary parameter that quantify the amount of scalar charge of the solution. Given that the trace of the Einstein field equation is
\tilde{R}=-\tilde{T}
, this solution must be such that

\tilde{R}=2\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi.

(40)

One can indeed verify that

\displaystyle\tilde{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-b^{2}}{2}\mathit{r}_{s}^{2}\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{b-2},

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi.

(42)

After the conformal transformation defined and used in Sec.
III
,
g_{\alpha\beta}=e^{4\alpha\varphi}\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}
with
\alpha=1/(2\sqrt{3})
—which is used in Entangled Relativity in order to go from the Einstein frame to the Entangled frame—the line element reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{b-\sqrt{\frac{1-b^{2}}{3}}}dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{-b-\sqrt{\frac{1-b^{2}}{3}}}dr^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{1-b-\sqrt{\frac{1-b^{2}}{3}}}r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\right).

One verifies in the notebook
[
64
]
that, surprisingly, this non-trivial line element is indeed such that

R=0.

(44)

The reason for this surprising property is given in Sec.
III.3
. Indeed, the field equations are such that the action defined from
\vartheta^{2}R
is minimal for
\vartheta\neq 0
, inducing that
R=0
on-shell.

There is one class of scalar-tensor theories for which that would be true, and it comes to the fact that

\forall~~\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}=\phi g_{\alpha\beta}\textrm{, with }\phi=e^{\pm 2\varphi/\sqrt{3}},

(45)

one has

\sqrt{-g}\phi R=\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[\tilde{R}-2\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\varphi\partial_{\beta}\varphi\right].

(46)

As one can check with Eq. (
17
)—see
[
9
]
for more details—Entangled Relativity belongs to this class of theories.
One can notice from both Eq. (
45
) and (
46
) that the Lagrangian density in the Einstein frame possesses a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry (
\varphi\rightarrow-\varphi
), which corresponds to an inversion symmetry (
\phi\rightarrow\phi^{-1}
) of the conformal transformation. Hence, we can deduce another metric from a conformal transformation of the metric in Eq. (
38
), by using the inverse conformal transformation with respect to the one previously considered—that is,
\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta}=e^{-4\alpha\varphi}\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}
with
\alpha=1/(2\sqrt{3})
. The resulting line element reads

\displaystyle d\hat{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{b+\sqrt{\frac{1-b^{2}}{3}}}dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{-b+\sqrt{\frac{1-b^{2}}{3}}}dr^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{1-b+\sqrt{\frac{1-b^{2}}{3}}}r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\right).

One indeed verifies that
\hat{R}=0
(see
[
64
]
). Obviously, the Lagrangian density in the Einstein frame also possesses a shift symmetry (
\varphi\rightarrow\varphi\pm\varphi_{0}
, where
\varphi_{0}
is a constant), which corresponds to a normalization change of the conformal transformation (
\phi\rightarrow\phi/\phi_{0}:=\phi~e^{\mp 2\varphi_{0}/\sqrt{3}}
).

Let us note that both the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and shift symmetries are broken when
\mathcal{L}_{m}\neq 0
for theories corresponding to Eq. (
46
) with a multiplicative scalar-matter coupling in the Lagrangian density. Therefore, in Entangled Relativity, both symmetries become approximate symmetries only when
\mathcal{L}_{m}\rightarrow 0
.

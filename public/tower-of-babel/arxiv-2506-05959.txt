1.
Introduction

1.1.

A reductive dual pair
(\mathfrak{g},G)
introduced by Howe
[
17
,
18
]
is a pair of semisimple Lie (super)algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a Lie group
G
, which acts on a space
F
and forms the centralizers of each other.
It gives a representation theoretic approach to the first fundamental theorem of the classical invariant theory, and has many applications in representation theory.

Let us consider the following well-known four reductive dual pairs
(\mathfrak{g},G)
acting on
F
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{\ $(\mathfrak{sp}_{2m},O_{\ell})$\ \ on $S(\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})$},\quad\quad\ \text{$(\mathfrak{so}_{2n},O_{\ell})$\ \ on\ $\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})$},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\text{$(\mathfrak{so}_{2m},Sp_{2\ell})$ on $S(\mathbb{C}^{2\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})$},\quad\text{\ $(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},Sp_{2\ell})$ on $\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{2\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})$},

where
S(V)
(resp.
\Lambda(V)
) is the symmetric (resp. exterior) algebra generated by
V
(see also
[
35
]
).
They are obtained from the following dual pairs of type
A
:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\text{$(\mathfrak{gl}_{m},GL_{l})$ on $S(\mathbb{C}^{l}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})$,\quad\ $(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},GL_{l})$ on $\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{l}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})$ \quad$(l=\ell,2\ell)$},

by extending the actions of general linear Lie algebra to those of simple Lie algebras of type
C
and
D
, while the actions of
O_{\ell}
and
Sp_{2\ell}
are induced from that of
GL_{l}
. Note that the irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{sp}_{2m}
and
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
appearing in
S(\mathbb{C}^{l}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})
are infinite-dimensional often called oscillator representations (cf.
[
14
, 5.6]
).

The dualities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can be given in a more uniform way in terms of Lie super algebras
[
11
]
: Let
S(\mathbb{C}^{l}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m|n})
denote the supersymmetric algebra generated by
\mathbb{C}^{l}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
where
\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
is the complex superspace with
(\mathbb{C}^{m|n})_{\overline{0}}=\mathbb{C}^{m}
and
(\mathbb{C}^{m|n})_{\overline{1}}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then each of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can be merged into a single dual pair

(1.4)

\displaystyle\text{$(\mathfrak{spo}_{2m|2n},O_{\ell})$ on $S(\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m|n})$\quad and\quad$(\mathfrak{osp}_{2m|2n},Sp_{2\ell})$ on $S(\mathbb{C}^{2\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m|n})$},

respectively, where
\mathfrak{spo}_{2m|2n}
and
\mathfrak{osp}_{2m|2n}
are orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras. We also remark that there is an
(\mathfrak{spo}_{2m|2n+1},Pin_{2\ell})
-duality on
S(\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2m|2n+1})

[
7
, Theorem A.1]
(see also
[
9
]
).

1.2.

The main result in this paper is to give a
q
-analogue of the dual pair
(\mathfrak{g},G)
in (
1.4
), which also yields
q
-analogues of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) as special cases.

In our
q
-analogue of (
1.1
), we use a quantized enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
introduced in
[
22
]
which arises naturally in the study of three dimensional Yang-Baxter equation. We denote it by
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
with
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
for type of Borel subalgebra and
X=C,D
for type of Dynkin diagram. In particular, we have
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(0^{n})=U_{q}(X_{n})
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(1^{n})=U_{-q^{-1}}(X_{n})
, where
U_{q}(X_{n})
is the quantum group of type
X_{n}
. On the other hand, the action of
G
is replaced by that of the
\imath
quantum group associated to the Lie algebra of
G

[
13
,
28
,
32
]
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a
q
-analogue of the supersymmetric algebra
S(\mathbb{C}^{\epsilon})
generated by
\mathbb{C}^{\epsilon}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{\epsilon}
is the superspace of dimension
n
with
\epsilon
the parity sequence of standard basis.
We first consider a
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
-module structure on
\mathcal{W}
for each
X
such that it gives a spin representation of type
D_{n}
when
(X,\epsilon)=(D,(1^{n}))
, and
q
-oscillator representations of type
C_{n}
when
(X,\epsilon)=(C,(0^{n}))

[
15
]
. For
\ell\geq 2
,
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes\ell}
has a
({\mathcal{U}}_{A}(\epsilon),{\bf U}(\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell}))
-module structure, where
{\mathcal{U}}_{A}(\epsilon)\subset{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
is the subalgebra of type
A
and
{\bf U}(\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell})
is the Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum group of
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell}
. Then we show that the action of
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)\supset{\mathcal{U}}_{A}(\epsilon)
on
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes\ell}
commutes with that of the coideal subalgebra or
\imath
quantum group
{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{so}_{\ell})\subset{\bf U}(\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell})
corresponding to symmetric pair
({\mathfrak{sl}}_{\ell},\mathfrak{so}_{\ell})
.

Next, we consider a
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
-module structure on
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes 2}
for each
X
, which is not the
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
-module induced from the above
\mathcal{W}
via comultiplication, but is inspired by various dualities on Fock spaces in terms of free field realization with the action of a Clifford-Weyl algebra
[
35
]
. It specializes to a sum of fundamental representations of type
C_{n}
when
(X,\epsilon)=(C,(1^{n}))

[
23
]
, and (fundamental type)
q
-oscillator representations of type
D_{n}
when
(X,\epsilon)=(D,(0^{n}))

[
26
]
.
We show that
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes 2\ell}
, the
\ell
-th tensor power of
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes 2}
, has a
({\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon),{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell}))
-module structure, where
{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell})
is the coideal subalgebra of
{\bf U}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2\ell})
corresponding to symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{sl}_{2\ell},\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell})
.

Finally, we show that the above actions of

(1.5)

({\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon),{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{so}_{\ell}))
on
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes\ell}
and
({\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon),{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell}))
on
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes 2\ell}

are semisimple, and describe its decomposition (Theorem
4.9
), whose classical limit recovers (
1.4
) and hence (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) by specializing
\epsilon=(0^{n}),(1^{n})
in each case.
We use the notion of classical weight modules of
\imath
quantum groups introduced in
[
36
]
to prove the semisimplicity of
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes\ell}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes 2\ell}
as a
{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{k})
with
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{so}_{\ell},\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell}
.

1.3.

Let us give some remarks related to the results in this paper.
We should first remark that a
q
-analogue of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) has been obtained in
[
33
]
, where the action of
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
with
\epsilon=(0^{n}),(1^{n})
is defined and verified to commute with that of
{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{k})
by means of diagrammatic calculation generalizing the notion of type
A
in
[
6
]
. The result in
[
33
]
, as the authors mentioned, doesn’t seem to yield an immediate generalization to the orthosymplectic case (see
[
34
]
for type
A
).
The case of
(\mathfrak{so}_{2n},O_{\ell})
on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
in (
1.1
) is also studied in
[
1
,
39
,
40
]
, where a
q
-analogue of the action of
(\mathfrak{so}_{2n},O_{\ell})
is described in terms of
q
-deformed Clifford algebras. To have a multiplicity-free decomposition, the centralizer of the Drinfeld-Jimbo’s quantum group of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
on the tensor power of its spin representation is obtained in
[
39
,
40
]
, while the joint highest weight vectors are constructed in
[
1
]
.
Compared to these results, our description of the action of
({\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon),{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{k}))
in (
1.5
) is explicit even in case of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), and obtained in a direct and uniform way based on
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
-modules
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes 2}
of fundamental types.

There is an isomorphism between
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
and the quantum superalgebra
U_{X}(\epsilon)
by Yamane
[
41
]
under mild extension (but not an isomorphism of Hopf algebras). This enables us to consider the representations of
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
and (super) representations of
U_{X}(\epsilon)
in an equivalent way (but not in a tensor category). There are several advantages of using
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
instead of
U_{X}(\epsilon)
in our context, while we still need to consider
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes l}
as a
U_{X}(\epsilon)
-module when we take its classical limit.

One novel point of using
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
is that the comultiplication of
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
is given by the same formula of the usual quantum group so that the action of
({\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon),{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{k}))
on
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes l}
can be described and verified more easily.
This may explain why it has been difficult to obtain a
q
-analogue of (
1.5
), even of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for
q
-deformed exterior algebras which can be explained in terms of usual quantum groups with parameter
\tilde{q}=-q^{-1}
.
Moreover, by using
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
, it is more natural to explain the connection with the representations of usual quantum groups from a viewpoint of super duality
[
8
]
. Indeed, one can construct exact tensor functors naturally connecting
q
-oscillator representations of
{\mathcal{U}}_{D}(\epsilon)
(or
{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(\epsilon)
) to finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(1^{n})
-modules and infinite-dimensional
q
-oscillator
{\mathcal{U}}_{Y}(0^{n})
-modules for
(X,Y)=(C,D),(D,C)
. These functors play an important role in the study of representations of quantum affine orthosymplectic algebras
[
26
]
.

There exist crystals of an irreducible
q
-oscillator
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
-module in
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes l}
with
\epsilon=(1^{M},0^{N})

[
24
,
25
]
and a finite-dimensional irreducible
{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{so}_{\ell})
-module with integral weights
[
37
]
.
So it would be interesting to have a combinatorial picture of (
1.5
), for example, an analogue of RSK algorithm in terms of
({\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon),{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{so}_{\ell}))
-crystals.
Also, it is natural to ask whether we can construct a canonical basis of an irreducible
q
-oscillator
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
-modules in
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes l}
using an
\imath
canonical basis of
{\bf U}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{so}_{\ell}))
-modules
[
2
,
38
]
through (
1.5
) in the sense of
[
3
,
12
]
.

2.
Quantum orthosymplectic superalgebras

2.1.
Generalized quantum groups of type
C
and
D

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
the set of nonnegative integers.
Let
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}(q^{\frac{1}{2}})
with
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
a formal variable. We put

\displaystyle[m]

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{m}-q^{-m}}{q-q^{-1}}\quad(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}),

\displaystyle[m]!

\displaystyle=[m][m-1]\cdots[1]\quad(m\geq 1),\quad[0]!=1,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}m\\
k\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{[m][m-1]\cdots[m-k+1]}{[k]!}\quad(0\leq k\leq m).

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
n\geq 4
.
We assume the following notations throughout the paper:

\bullet

\mathbb{I}=\{\,1<2<\cdots<n\,\}
,

\bullet

\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n})
: a sequence with
\epsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for

,

\bullet

P=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{Z}\delta_{i}

\mathbb{Z}
-lattice with a symmetric bilinear form
(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,)
satisfying

(\delta_{i}|\delta_{j})=(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}\delta_{ij},\quad

\bullet

q_{i}=(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}q^{(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}}

(i\in\mathbb{I})
, that is,
q_{i}=q
if
\epsilon_{i}=0
and
\widetilde{q}:=-q^{-1}
if
\epsilon_{i}=1
.

\bullet

{{\bf q}}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
: a symmetric biadditive function from
P\times P
to
\Bbbk^{\times}
given by

\begin{split}&{\bf q}(\mu,\nu)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{I}}q_{i}^{\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\quad(\mu,\nu\in P),\end{split}

for
\mu=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\mu_{i}\delta_{i}
and
\nu=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\nu_{i}\delta_{i}
,

\bullet

\Lambda=(-1)^{\epsilon_{1}}\delta_{1}+\cdots+(-1)^{\epsilon_{n}}\delta_{n}
.

Let us first recall the notion of a
generalized quantum group
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
of type
X=C,D
associated to
\epsilon

[
22
,
30
]
. Put

\bullet

I=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
,

\bullet

\alpha_{i}=\delta_{i}-\delta_{i+1}\in P
for
i\in I\setminus\{0\}
,

\bullet

\alpha_{0}=-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}
and
-2\delta_{1}
for
X=D
and
C
, respectively.

We define
\mathcal{U}_{D}(\epsilon)
to be the associative
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by

e_{i},f_{i},k_{\mu}

(i\in I,\mu\in P)
satisfying

(2.1)

\begin{split}&k_{\mu}=1\quad(\mu=0),\quad k_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}=k_{\mu}k_{\mu^{\prime}}\quad(\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in P),\\
&k_{\mu}e_{i}k_{-\mu}={\bf q}(\mu,\alpha_{i})e_{i},\quad k_{\mu}f_{i}k_{-\mu}={\bf q}(\mu,\alpha_{i})^{-1}f_{i}\quad(i\in I,\mu\in P),\\
&e_{i}f_{j}-f_{j}e_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{k_{i}-k_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\quad(i,j\in I),\\
&[e_{i},e_{j}]=[f_{i},f_{j}]=0\quad\text{if $(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})=0$},\\
&e_{i}^{2}=f_{i}^{2}=0\quad\text{($i\in I_{\rm odd}$)},\\
&e^{2}_{i}e_{j}-(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}[2]e_{i}e_{j}e_{i}+e_{j}e^{2}_{i}=(e\rightarrow f)=0\quad\text{if $i,j\in I\setminus\{0\}$, $|i-j|=1$, $\epsilon_{i}=\epsilon_{j}$},\\
&e_{i}e_{i-1}e_{i}e_{i+1}-e_{i}e_{i+1}e_{i}e_{i-1}+(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}[2]e_{i}e_{i-1}e_{i+1}e_{i}-e_{i-1}e_{i}e_{i+1}e_{i}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+e_{i+1}e_{i}e_{i-1}e_{i}=(e\rightarrow f)=0\quad\text{if $i\in I\setminus\{0,1,n-1\}$, $\epsilon_{i}\neq\epsilon_{i+1}$},\\
\end{split}

and

(2.2)

\begin{split}&e_{0}^{2}e_{2}-(-1)^{\epsilon_{2}}[2]e_{0}e_{2}e_{0}+e_{2}e_{0}^{2}=(e\rightarrow f)=0\quad\text{if $\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}$},\\

&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-e_{2}e_{0}e_{2}e_{3}+e_{2}e_{3}e_{2}e_{0}=(e\rightarrow f)=0\quad\text{if $\epsilon_{2}\neq\epsilon_{3}$},\end{split}

where
k_{i}=k_{\alpha_{i}}
for
i\in I
, and
(e\rightarrow f)
denotes the relation obtained by replacing

with
f_{i}
.

Similarly, we define
\mathcal{U}_{C}(\epsilon)
to be the associative
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by

(i\in I,\mu\in P)

4.

4.1.
Quantum symmetric pairs

Let

with
r\geq 2
. Let
{\bf U}={\bf U}(\mathfrak{sl}_{r})
be the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{U}}_{A}((0^{r}))
generated by
e_{i},f_{i},k_{i}^{\pm 1}
for

.
To avoid confusion with the notations for
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}(\epsilon)
, we denote the weight lattice for
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
by
\mathsf{P}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{i}/\langle\varepsilon_{1}+\dots+\varepsilon_{r}\rangle
with a bilinear form
(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,)
such that
(\varepsilon_{i}|\varepsilon_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, the index set for its simple roots
\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}
by
\mathsf{I}=\{1,2,\ldots,r-1\}
, and the generators of
{\bf U}
by
\mathsf{e}_{i},\mathsf{f}_{i},\mathsf{k}_{\mu}^{\pm 1}

(i\in\mathsf{I},\mu\in\mathsf{P})
.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
generated by
s_{i}
(
i\in\mathsf{I}
).
For
i\in\mathsf{I}
, let
T_{i}
be the automorphism on
{\bf U}
given by

\displaystyle T_{i}(\mathsf{k}_{\mu})=\mathsf{k}_{s_{i}(\mu)},\quad T_{i}(\mathsf{e}_{i})=-\mathsf{f}_{i}\mathsf{k}_{i},\quad T_{i}(\mathsf{f}_{i})=-\mathsf{k}_{i}^{-1}\mathsf{e}_{i},

\displaystyle T_{i}(\mathsf{e}_{j})=\sum_{r+s=-(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})}(-1)^{r}{q}^{-r}\mathsf{e}_{i}^{(s)}\mathsf{e}_{j}\mathsf{e}_{i}^{(r)}\quad T_{i}(\mathsf{f}_{j})=\sum_{r+s=-(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})}(-1)^{r}{q}^{r}\mathsf{f}_{i}^{(r)}\mathsf{f}_{j}\mathsf{f}_{i}^{(s)}\quad(j\neq i),

for

and
\mu\in\mathsf{P}
, which is
T_{i,1}^{\prime\prime}
in
[
29
, 37.1.3]
.
For a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
, let
T_{w}=T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{k}}
.

Let
\mathsf{I}_{\bullet}\subseteq\mathsf{I}
be given, and let
\mathsf{I}_{\circ}=\mathsf{I}\setminus\mathsf{I}_{\bullet}
. Let
W_{\mathsf{I}_{\bullet}}
the parabolic subgroup of
W
generated by
s_{i}
for

with the longest element
w_{\bullet}
. Let
\rho_{\bullet}
be the half sum of positive roots of the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
generated by
\mathsf{e}_{i}
and
\mathsf{f}_{i}
for
i\in\mathsf{I}_{\bullet}
.
Let
\uptau
be an involution on
\mathsf{I}
such that
(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{j})=(\alpha_{\uptau(i)}|\alpha_{\uptau(j)})
for
i,j\in\mathsf{I}
.
Suppose that
(\mathsf{I}_{\bullet},\uptau)
is admissible, that is, (1)
\uptau(\mathsf{I}_{\bullet})=\mathsf{I}_{\bullet}
, (2)
\alpha_{\uptau(j)}=-w_{\bullet}(\alpha_{j})
for
j\in\mathsf{I}_{\bullet}
, (3)

(\rho_{\bullet}|\alpha_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}
if
i\in\mathsf{I}_{\circ}
and
\uptau(i)=i
.
Let
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}=\{\,\mu\in\mathsf{P}\mid-w_{\bullet}\circ{\uptau}(\mu)=\mu\,\}
, with the assumption that we can extend
\uptau
to a map on
\mathsf{P}
.

[
2
]

{\bf U}^{\imath}
to be the
\Bbbk
-subalgebra of
{\bf U}
generated by

\mathsf{B}_{i}:=\mathsf{f}_{i}+\varsigma_{i}T_{w_{\bullet}}(\mathsf{e}_{\uptau(i)}){\mathsf{k}_{i}}^{-1}+\kappa_{i}\mathsf{k}_{i}^{-1}\ (i\in\mathsf{I}_{\circ}),\quad\mathsf{e}_{j},\mathsf{f}_{j}\ (j\in\mathsf{I}_{\bullet})\quad\mathsf{k}_{\mu}\ (\mu\in\mathsf{P}^{\prime}),

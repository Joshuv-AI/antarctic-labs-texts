Chiral Composite Linear Dilaton as String Dual to Two-Dimensional Yang-Mills
Shota Komatsu

Abstract




Two-dimensional Yang-Mills theory (2d YM) is arguably the simplest confining gauge theory and its large
N_{c}
expansion has a structure of the genus expansion in string theory.
Nevertheless various aspects of its worldsheet description have not been fully understood.
In this paper, we elaborate on a bosonic string dual to large
N_{c}
chiral 2d YM at finite ’t Hooft coupling, proposed in our earlier work. The worldsheet theory consists of
\beta
-
\gamma
system deformed by linear dilaton action built from a composite of
\gamma

Introduction

String theory has been one of the most powerful frameworks in theoretical physics over the past five decades. Beyond its ambitious claim of providing a “theory of everything” where strings replace point particles as the fundamental building blocks of the universe, it has also revealed a web of remarkable dualities. Among the most striking of these is the gauge/string duality, which relates certain gauge theories to string theories. Unlike the traditional reductionist approach underlying the “theory of everything” program, the gauge/string duality realizes strings as emergent geometrical objects arising naturally from large
N_{c}
gauge dynamics.

The idea of reformulating gauge theories in terms of strings is a longstanding one. Following ’t Hooft’s seminal observation
[
1
]
that the large
N_{c}
expansion of gauge theory amplitudes can be organized as a sum over two-dimensional surfaces, this intuition was given a concrete realization by Maldacena in the celebrated AdS/CFT correspondence
[
2
]
. The correspondence provides a precise duality between
\mathcal{N}=4
Super Yang–Mills theory at large
N_{c}
and type IIB string theory on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. Despite its success, extending this duality to more realistic, non-supersymmetric gauge theories such as four-dimensional Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), which governs hadronic physics, remains an outstanding challenge. A concrete string dual of QCD would illuminate the strongly coupled infrared regime of the theory, where field-theoretic methods offer limited analytical control.

As a first step toward this goal, it is natural to seek the string dual of two-dimensional pure Yang–Mills theory (2d YM), which is exactly solvable on an arbitrary Riemann surface even at finite
N_{c}
. Furthermore, when coupled to matter fields, 2d QCD exhibits color confinement and an infinite tower of resonances
[
3

, thus serving as an ideal toy model to explore the stringy mechanisms underlying confinement in four dimensions.

Worldsheet dual to chiral 2d Yang-Mills

Here we explain details of the worldsheet action proposed in
[
14
]
, which we conjecture to be dual to large
N_{c}
chiral 2d Yang-Mills theory. We present the worldsheet action and its stress tensor, the operator product expansion (OPE) of fundamental fields, and vertex operators corresponding to winding strings in the chiral 2d YM.

2.1
Worldsheet action and OPEs

Action.

Our proposed worldsheet action is

\begin{split}S=&\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\left[\beta\,\bar{\partial}X^{+}+\bar{\beta}\,\partial X^{-}+\frac{q}{2}\mathcal{L}_{CLD}+\frac{\lambda\pi}{2}\left(\partial X^{+}\bar{\partial}X^{-}-\bar{\partial}X^{+}\partial X^{-}\right)\right]+\text{bc-ghost}\,,\end{split}

(2.1)

where
\lambda=g_{\text{YM}}^{2}N_{c}
is the ’t Hooft coupling,
X^{\pm}=(X^{1}\pm X^{0})
are light-cone co-ordinates of the target space, and
\mathcal{L}_{CLD}
is the chiral composite linear dilaton (CLD) action

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{CLD}=2\,\partial\varphi\,\bar{\partial}\varphi+\hat{R}\,\varphi\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi=\log(\frac{\partial X^{+}\bar{\partial}X^{-}}{R^2})\,,

(2.2)

with
\hat{R}
is worldsheet Ricci scalar, and
R
is the length-scale of the target space. Here
q
is not yet determined but we will fix it to one (
q=1
) below by imposing the conformal anomaly cancellation.

Stress tensor and operator product expansion.

To compute the stress tensor, it is convenient to first express the action in a covariant form. We start with the
\beta
-
\gamma
system, which reads

\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\beta_{z}\,\partial_{\bar{z}}X^{+}\to\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{-g}\,\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{4}g^{ab}\beta_{a}\,\partial_{b}X^{+}-\frac{1}{4i}\epsilon^{ab}\beta_{a}\,\partial_{b}X^{+}\right)\,,

(2.3)

and similarly for the
\bar{\beta}\partial X^{-}
term. Taking a derivative with respect to
g^{zz}
, we obtain

T_{\beta\gamma}(z)=-\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\beta\gamma}}{\delta g^{zz}}=-\beta\,\partial X^{+}\,,

(2.4)

where we have used
\delta\epsilon^{ab}/\delta g^{zz}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ab}g_{zz}
, with
g_{zz}=0
in the conformal gauge. Next we consider the term proportional to
\lambda
, to be called the “tensionfull term” in what follows:

\left(\partial X^{+}\bar{\partial}X^{-}-\bar{\partial}X^{+}\partial X^{-}\right)\to\frac{i}{2}\,\epsilon^{cd}\partial_{c}X^{+}\partial_{d}X^{-}\,,

(2.5)

Being given purely in terms of
\epsilon^{ab}
, this term does not contribute to the stress tensors. Finally, the covariant form of the CLD action is giiven by the following expression

\left(\Gamma[X^{\pm}]\right)_{\text{covariant}}=\frac{q}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\sqrt{-g}\left[g^{ab}\,\partial_{a}\Phi\,\partial_{b}\varphi+2\hat{R}\,\varphi\right]\,,

(2.6)

where

\varphi=\log\left[\frac{1}{4}\,g^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{+}\partial_{b}X^{-}+\frac{i}{4}\,\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{+}\partial_{b}X^{-}\right]\,.

(2.7)

One can now compute its contribution as follows

\begin{split}T_{CLD}(z)=-\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta g^{zz}}=-q\,(\partial\varphi)^{2}+2q\,\partial^{2}\varphi=2q\,\{X^{+},z\}\,,\end{split}

(2.8)

where
\{X^{+},z\}:=\partial^{3}X^{+}/\partial X^{+}-3(\partial^{2}X^{+}/\partial X^{+})^{2}/2
, is the Schwarzian derivative of
X^{+}
. Adding these contributions, the stress tensor of the matter sector takes the form

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} T(z)&=-\beta\,\partial X^{+}(z)+2q\,\{X^{+},z\}\,\\
&=-\beta\partial X^{+}+2q\partial^{2}\log\partial X^{+}-q(\partial\log\partial X^{+})^{2}\,.\end{aligned}

(2.9)

From the worldsheet action, one can compute the OPEs:

\begin{split}&\beta(z)X^{+}(w)\sim-\frac{1}{z-w},\quad\bar{\beta}(\bar{z})X^{-}(\bar{w})\sim-\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{w}}\,,\\
&\beta(z)\beta(w)\sim 2q\,\partial_{z}\partial_{y}\left[\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\partial_{z}X^{+}(z)\,\partial_{w}X^{+}(w)}\right],\quad\text{its cc}\,.\end{split}

(2.10)

The
\beta
-
\gamma

\beta
-
\beta
OPE requires a more elaborate computation, which we present in section
4.2

&=-2q\,\partial_{z}X^{+}(z)\,\partial_{w}\left[\frac{1}{\partial_{w}X^{+}(w)}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\right]\,\partial_{w}\log\partial_{w}X^{+}(w)\,.\end{aligned}

(2.17)

Similarly we have

q\,\beta(w)\,\partial_{w}X^{+}(w)\,(\partial_{z}\log\partial_{z}X^{+}(z))^{2}=-2q\,\partial_{w}X^{+}(w)\,\partial_{z}\left[\frac{1}{\partial_{z}X^{+}(z)}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\right]\,\partial_{z}\log\partial_{z}X^{+}(z)\,.

(2.18)

Expanding in a Laurent series around
z=w
and combining all contributions gives

T(z)T(w)=\frac{1+12q}{(z-w)^{4}}+\frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w}T(w)}{z-w}\,.

(2.19)

For conformal anomaly cancellation, we require
q=1
.

As mentioned in
[
14

, the

arises in non-relativistic string theory
[

. The main difference from the standard non-relativistic string action is the presence of the CLD term, which allows its noncritical generalization. The latter may be seen as the chiral analogue of the Polchinski-Strominger term
[

in the Polyakov formalism
[

Integrate

\mathcal{L}_{CLD}

Note that we always have the constant dilaton field

, and

g_{s}^{-\chi}

g_{s}

e^{-S}\to e^{-\frac{q}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\hat{R}\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{0}}=\left(g_{s}\right)^{-q\,\chi},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptg_{s}=e^{\Phi_{0}}\,.

(2.20)

2.2
Vertex operators

With no local dynamics, the physical degrees of freedom of chiral 2d YM on a cylinder are its holonomies which correspond to winding string states
[
28
]
, as we will discuss in sec
3.1
. The vertex operator for such a winding string reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})=e^{iw_{k}R\int^{z_{k}}(\beta dz-\bar{\beta}d\bar{z})}e^{-iE_{k}X^{0}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptE_{k}=\lambda\pi Rw_{k}\,.

(2.21)

This creates a string state winding
w_{k}
times around the
X^{1}
direction in the target space, as is evident from the following OPEs:

\begin{split}&X^{+}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\sim-iw_{k}R\,\log(z-z_k)\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k}),\\
&X^{-}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\sim+iw_{k}R\,\log(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_k)\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\,,\end{split}

(2.22)

which imply:

X^{1}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\sim-\frac{i}{2}w_{k}R\,\log\left[\frac{z-z_{k}}{\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{k}}\right]\mathcal{V}_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\,,

(2.23)

thus, as we encircle around
(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})
, i.e under
z\to z_{k}+(z-z_{k})\,e^{2\pi i},\;\bar{z}\to\bar{z}_{k}+(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{k})\,e^{-2\pi i}
, the field
X^{1}(z,\bar{z})
acquires a monodromy of
2\pi Rw_{k}
.

In Secs.
3.3
and
3.4
, we will verify that the vertex operators (
2.21
) are conformal primaries of dimension
(1,1)
by analyzing their worldsheet correlators.

3
Scattering amplitudes

In the previous section, we proposed a worldsheet dual to chiral 2d YM and introduced the vertex operators for the winding strings. In this section we compute the corresponding scattering amplitudes in both Yang–Mills theory and its dual string description, and match them to find the parameter dictionary of our duality. In particular, we evaluate the four-point string amplitude using a KLT-like factorization and show how it matches the amplitude obtained from the dual Yang–Mills theory. We start below with the YM amplitudes.

3.1
Amplitudes of chiral 2d Yang-Mills

The wave function of chiral 2d YM on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is defined by the path integral with holonomy
U
as the boundary condition. As a class function of
U
, it can be expanded in irreducible representations of
U(N_{c})
. A convenient basis for this at large
N_{c}
is the multi-trace basis
\{|\sigma\rangle\}
,

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\sigma}(U)=\langle U|\sigma\rangle=\prod_{\ell}\left(\text{tr}(U^{\ell})\right)^{k_{\ell}}\,,

(3.1)

where
k_{\ell}
denotes the number of cycles of length
\ell
in
\sigma\in S_{r}
with
r=\sum_{\ell}\ell k_{\ell}
. As shown by Minahan and Polychronakos in
[
28
]
,
|\sigma\rangle
can be interpreted as a multi-string state with
k_{\ell}
strings winding
\ell
times around
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. In the canonical picture, one can express it with string creation operators acting on the vacuum of the free theory
|0\rangle
:

\displaystyle|\sigma\rangle=\prod_{\ell}(a_{\ell}^{\dagger})^{k_{\ell}}\,|0\rangle\,,

(3.2)

where
[a_{k},a_{l}^{\dagger}]=k\,\delta_{k,l}
. The Hamiltonian on
S^{1}
of radius
R
is then given by
H=H_{\rm free}+H_{\rm int}
with
[
28
]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{\rm free}=\pi\lambda R\sum_{m}a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad H_{\rm int}=\frac{\pi R\lambda}{N_{c}}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\left(a_{m+n}^{\dagger}a_{m}a_{n}+a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m+n}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(3.3)

The free hamiltonian
H_{\rm free}
counts the winding number as expected for the Nambu-Goto contribution, while the interaction term describes single branch points in the Gross–Taylor covering map picture. Concretely, the operator

a_{m+n}^{\dagger}a_{m}a_{n}

(3.4)

splits two strings of windings
m,n
and glues them into a single string of winding
(m+n)
, while its Hermitian conjugate implements the inverse process.

The two-point amplitude of these strings with winding numbers
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
is

\mathcal{S}_{2}=\langle 0|a_{w_{1}}a_{w_{2}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=w_{1}\,\delta_{w_{1},w_{2}}\,.

(3.5)

Between an in-state
|\Psi_{\text{in}}\rangle=a^{\dagger}_{w_{1}}a^{\dagger}_{w_{2}}|0\rangle
, and out-state
|\Psi_{\text{out}}\rangle=a^{\dagger}_{w_{3}}|0\rangle
, the three-point amplitude for
2\to 1
scattering process reads

\mathcal{S}_{3}=\langle\Psi_{\text{out}}|\exp\left[-i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_{I}(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\right]|\Psi_{\text{in}}\rangle\,,

(3.6)

with
H_{I}(t^{\prime})=e^{iH_{\rm free}t^{\prime}}\,H_{\rm int}\,e^{-iH_{\rm free}t^{\prime}}
. Evaluating the relevant matrix element,

\langle 0|a_{w_{3}}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\left(a_{m+n}^{\dagger}a_{m}a_{n}+a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m+n}\right)\,a_{w_{1}}^{\dagger}a_{w_{2}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=2w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}\,\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},w_{3}}\,,

(3.7)

the S-matrix becomes

\mathcal{S}_{3}=-i\frac{4\lambda\pi^{2}R}{N_{c}}w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}\,\delta(E_{1}+E_{2}-E_{3})\,\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},w_{3}}\,.

(3.8)

In what follows, we will reproduce it from the three-point string scattering amplitudes. In Sec.
3.4
, we will show that the four-point amplitude factorizes into these three-point amplitudes in the
s
-,
t
-, and
u
-channel exchanges.

3.2
Worldsheet correlator

In this subsection, we evaluate the worldsheet correlator of vertex operators (
2.21
) via the string path integral, and show that localization of the worldsheet matter fields allows us to obtain an explicit expression for the correlator with
n
insertions.

We start with the path-integral expression of
n
-point worldsheet correlator of vertex operators (
2.21
) which reads

G^{\text{matter}}_{n}=g_{s}^{n-2}\,\langle\prod_{k=1}^{n}\,V_{k}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\rangle=g_{s}^{n-2}\int[\mathfrak{D}X^{+}\mathfrak{D}X^{-}\mathfrak{D}\beta\,\mathfrak{D}\bar{\beta}]\,e^{-\boldsymbol{S}}\,.

(3.9)

The net worldsheet action
\boldsymbol{S}
, including the contributions from the vertex operators, can be decomposed into three parts:

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{S}=\left(S_{\beta\text{-}\bar{\beta}}+S_{\lambda}+\Gamma\right)[X^{\pm}]\,,\end{split}

(3.10)

where
S_{\beta\text{-}\bar{\beta}}
is
\beta
and
\bar{\beta}
dependent part, while
S_{\lambda}
depends on the string tension
\lambda
:

\begin{split}&S_{\beta\text{-}\bar{\beta}}[X^{\pm}]:=\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\left(\beta\,\bar{\partial}X^{+}+\bar{\beta}\,\partial X^{-}\right)-i\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}R\int^{z_{k}}dz^{\prime}\beta(z^{\prime})+i\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}R\int^{\bar{z}_{k}}d\bar{z}^{\prime}\bar{\beta}(\bar{z}^{\prime})\,,\\
&S_{\lambda}[X^{\pm}]:=\frac{\lambda}{4}\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\left(\partial X^{+}\bar{\partial}X^{-}-\bar{\partial}X^{+}\partial X^{-}\right)+i\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}X^{0}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\,,\end{split}

(3.11)

and
\Gamma[X^{\pm}]
is the CLD action already defined in (
2.6
). We can rephrase
S_{\beta\text{-}\bar{\beta}}[X^{\pm}]
as

S_{\beta\text{-}\bar{\beta}}[X^{\pm}]=\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\beta(z)\bar{\partial}[X^{+}-R\rho]+\int\frac{d^{2}z}{2\pi}\beta(z)\partial[X^{-}-R\bar{\rho}]\,,

(3.12)

where
\rho
and
\bar{\rho}
are Mandelstam maps
[
29
]
:

\rho(z):=-i\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\log(z-z_k),\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{\rho}(\bar{z}):=i\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\log(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_k)\,.

(3.13)

The string coupling is denoted by
g_{s}
. Integrating out
\beta
and
\bar{\beta}
in the string path integral (
3.9
),

gets localized onto
(\rho,\bar{\rho})

\int[\mathfrak{D}\beta\,\mathfrak{D}\bar{\beta}]\,e^{-S_{\beta\text{-}\bar{\beta}}[X^{\pm}]}=\mathcal{N}\left[\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)\right]^{-1}\delta(X^{+}-R\rho)\,\delta(X^{-}-R\bar{\rho})\,,

(3.14)

where

denotes the normalization factor arising from the measure, and and the prime on the determinant indicates that the zero modes of
\partial\bar{\partial}
are excluded.

We can perform the integrals over zero modes of
X^{0}
and
X^{1}

\int_{0}^{2\pi R}dx^{1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{0}\,e^{-i\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}\right)x^{0}}=4\pi^{2}R\,\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\,.

(3.15)

Thus,
G^{\text{matter}}_{n}
can be expressed as

G^{\text{matter}}_{n}=ig_{s}^{n-2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\cdot 4\pi^{2}R\,\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\delta_{\sum_{k}w_{k}}\left[\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)\right]^{-1}e^{-S_{\lambda}[\rho]-\Gamma[\rho]}\,,

(3.16)

where we have absorbed sphere partition function and other constants from the measure in
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}

reflects the requirement that, after localizing onto
(\rho,\bar{\rho})
, the fields

should remain analytic at
\infty
in the absence of any vertex operator insertion there. The correlator of ghost insertions at

and

G^{\text{ghost}}_{n}=\left[\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)\right]|z_{ab}z_{bc}z_{ca}|^{2}\,.

(3.17)

Combining the contributions from both the matter and ghost sectors, the
n(>2)
-point worldsheet correlator with fixed points at

and

G_{n}=ig_{s}^{n-2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\cdot 4\pi^{2}R\,\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\delta_{\sum_{k}w_{k}}|z_{ab}z_{bc}z_{ca}|^{2}\,e^{-S_{\lambda}[\rho]-\Gamma[\rho]}\,.

(3.18)

\sum_{l=1}^{n}\oint_{C_{z_{l}}}\bar{\rho}\,d\rho=-2\pi i\sum_{k\neq l}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\alpha_{l}\,\log(\bar{z}_l-\bar{z}_k)-2\pi i\sum_{l=1}^{n}|\alpha_{l}|^{2}\log(\epsilon_{z_l})-2\pi^{2}\sum_{l}|\alpha_{l}|^{2}\,.

(3.33)

Adding both terms, the contribution from circles around insertion points is

\begin{split}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\oint_{C_{z_{l}}}\left(\rho\,d\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}\,d\rho\right)=2\pi i\sum_{k\neq l}^{n}\alpha_{k}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\log\left[-|z_{k}-z_{l}|^{2}\right]+2\pi i\sum_{l=1}^{n}|\alpha_{l}|^{2}\,\log(\epsilon_{z_l}^2)\,.\end{split}

(3.34)

Around
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
:

To compute
\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\left(\rho\,d\bar{\rho}-\bar{\rho}\,d\rho\right)
, we first note that near
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
:

\begin{split}&\rho(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\left[\log z+\log\left(1-\frac{z_{k}}{z}\right)\right]\sim-\frac{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)}{z},\quad\text{and}\quad d\bar{\rho}=\frac{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bar{z}_{k}\right)}{\bar{z}^{2}}\,d\bar{z}\,.\end{split}

(3.35)

Using this, the contribution from around
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
to the first term reads

\begin{split}\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\rho\,d\bar{\rho}=-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{2}\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\frac{d\bar{z}}{|z|^{2}\,\bar{z}}=2\pi i\,\frac{\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{2}}{|z_{\infty}|^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(3.36)

where we have used
\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}=-2\pi i
. Similarly,

\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\bar{\rho}\,d\rho=-2\pi i\frac{\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{2}}{|z_{\infty}|^{2}}\,.

(3.37)

Since
|z_{\infty}|\to\infty
, these contributions vanish.

Hence the net contribution to the boundary integral
S_{t}
is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{t}[\rho]&=2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\log[\epsilon_{z_{j}}^{2}]+2\pi i\sum_{j\neq k}\alpha_{j}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\log[-|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}]\\
&=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\,\bar{\rho}(\bar{z}_{k})+2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\,\rho(z_{k})\,.\end{aligned}

(3.38)

We can now uplift to the computation of
S_{\lambda}[\rho]
where
\alpha_{k}=-iw_{k}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{k}=iw_{k}
for
k=1,\cdots,n
:

\begin{split}S_{\lambda}[\rho]&=i\frac{\lambda R^{2}}{4}S_{t}[\rho]+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}R(\rho-\bar{\rho})(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\\
&=i\frac{\lambda\pi R^{2}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\,\bar{\rho}(\bar{z}_{k})-i\frac{\lambda\pi R^{2}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\,\rho(z_{k})+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}R(\rho-\bar{\rho})(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\\
&=0\quad\,.\end{split}

(3.39)

Hence
S_{\lambda}[\rho]
vanishes.

Final expression of
G_{n}
:

Since
S_{\lambda}[\rho]
vanishes, the only contribution to the worldsheet correlator (
3.18
) comes from
\Gamma[\rho]
. Since its evaluation is technically complicated, here we simply quote the result postponing the derivation to section
4
. As explained there, after regularization and applying the Mandelstam formula (
4.28
) together with the explicit evaluation of
\partial^{2}\rho(Z_{I})
in
[
24
]
,
\Gamma[\rho]
takes the form

e^{-\Gamma[\rho]}\to e^{-\Gamma^{\rm ren}[\rho]/}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(w_{k})^{q}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{-2q}\cdot|\Delta(P_{n})|^{q}\,,

(3.40)

where
\Delta(P_{n})
is discriminant of the
(n-2)
-degree polynomial

P_{n}(z):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\prod_{i(\neq k)}^{n}(z-z_{i})\,.

(3.41)

Note that the coefficient of leading term
z^{n-1}
in
P_{n}(z)
above vanishes since
\sum_{k}w_{k}=0
. Thus the expression of worldsheet correlator (
3.18
) with fixed points at
z_{a},z_{b}
and
z_{c}
, takes the form

G_{n}=ig_{s}^{n-2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\cdot 4\pi^{2}R\,\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\delta_{\sum_{k}w_{k}}|z_{ab}z_{bc}z_{ca}|^{2}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}(w_{k})^{q}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{ij}|^{-2q}\cdot|\Delta(P_{n})|^{q}\,.

(3.42)

The
n
-point string amplitude is obtained by integrating the above correlator over the unfixed coordinates on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{n}=ig_{s}^{n-2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\cdot 4\pi^{2}R\,&\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\delta_{\sum_{k}w_{k}}|z_{ab}z_{bc}z_{ca}|^{2}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}(w_{k})^{q}\\
&\times\left[\prod_{l\neq a,b,c}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{C}}d^{2}z_{l}\right]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{ij}|^{-2q}\cdot|\Delta(P_{n})|^{q}\,.\end{split}

(3.43)

In the following two sections, we apply this formula to evaluate the three- and four-point string amplitudes.

3.3
Two- and three-point string amplitudes

Using the general
n
-point amplitude derived in the previous subsection, the three-point amplitude can be computed directly. The two-point amplitude, however, requires a more careful analysis, for which we will occasionally draw on results from Appendix

3.3.1
Two-point amplitude

The two-point string amplitude in our set up takes the form

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\frac{1}{V_{\text{CKG}}}\int[\mathfrak{D}X^{+}\mathfrak{D}X^{-}\mathfrak{D}\beta\,\mathfrak{D}\bar{\beta}\,]\,e^{-S_{\hat{g}}}\,\mathcal{F}[\hat{g}]\,\int d^{2}z_{1}\,d^{2}z_{2}\,V_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\,V_{1}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\,,

(3.44)

with
\mathcal{F}[\hat{g}]
is the Fadeev Popov determinant to fix the worldsheet metric, and
V_{CKG}
is the volume of the conformal killing group. Note that, two-point amplitude carries no explicit factor of
g_{s}
.

As discussed in Appendix
A
, we fix the conformal Killing group symmetry by inserting the identity (
A.2
) in the path integral. After relabelling the insertion points
z_{1}\to\alpha\circ z_{1}
and
z_{2}\to\alpha\circ z_{2}
, the amplitude reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{A}^{\text{closed}}_{2}=&i\mathcal{F}[\hat{g}]\,\Big\langle\Delta_{X^{0}}V_{1}(z_{1}^{0},\bar{z}_{1}^{0})\,V_{2}(z_{2}^{0},\bar{z}_{2}^{0})\Big\rangle^{\prime}(z_{2}^{0})^{2}(\bar{z}_{2}^{0})^{2}\\
&\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{0}\,e^{-i(E_{1}+E_{2})x^{0}}\delta(x^{0}-\#)\int_{0}^{2\pi R}dx^{1}\,,\end{aligned}

(3.45)

where the prime on the correlator indicates that the zero-mode integrals
(\int dx^{0}dx^{1})
are taken out.

Evaluating
\Delta_{X^{0}}
and inserting it into the worldsheet correlator above, we recover the structure in (
A.34
). Thus, we are led to compute

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\partial X^{0}(|A|^{2}w,|A|^{2}\bar{w})\,V_{1}(z_{1}^{0},\bar{z}_{1}^{0})\,V_{2}(z_{2}^{0},\bar{z}_{2}^{0})\Big\rangle^{\prime}\\
&=\int[\mathfrak{D}X^{+}\mathfrak{D}X^{-}]^{\prime}\,[\mathfrak{D}\beta\,\mathfrak{D}\bar{\beta}\,]\,e^{-S_{\hat{g}}}\,\partial X^{0}(|A|^{2}w,|A|^{2}\bar{w})\,V_{1}(z_{1}^{0},\bar{z}_{1}^{0})\,V_{2}(z_{2}^{0},\bar{z}_{2}^{0})\\
&=\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,e^{-\Gamma[\rho]}\Big[\partial X^{0}(|A|^{2}w,|A|^{2}\bar{w})\Big]_{X^{+}=\rho,\,X^{-}=\bar{\rho}}\,\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},0}\,,\end{split}

(3.46)

together with its anti-holomorphic counterpart with
\bar{\partial}X^{0}
replaced by
\partial X^{0}
. As discussed in Appendix
A
,
A
is a solution to the delta function constranit, which will drop out from the final answer as we see below.
(\rho,\bar{\rho})
are the Mandelstam maps (
3.13
) with
n=2
and
\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}
is the overall normalization coming from the sphere partition function.

Using

\begin{split}&e^{-\Gamma[\rho]}=\frac{1}{|z_{12}^{0}|^{4}}\to\frac{1}{(z_{2}^{0})^{4}},\quad[\partial X^{0}]_{\rho,\,\bar{\rho}}=-\frac{i}{2}w_{1}R\,\frac{z_{1}^{0}-z_{2}^{0}}{(z-z_{1}^{0})(z-z_{2}^{0})}\to-\frac{i}{2}w_{1}R\,\frac{1}{z}\,,\end{split}

(3.47)

and similarly
[\partial X^{0}]_{\rho,\,\bar{\rho}}=-\frac{i}{2}w_{1}R\,\frac{1}{\bar{z}}
(with
z_{1}^{0}=0
,
z_{2}^{0}=\infty
), the correlator (
3.46
) becomes

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\partial X^{0}(|A|^{2}w,|A|^{2}\bar{w})\,V_{1}(z_{1}^{0},\bar{z}_{1}^{0})\,V_{2}(z_{2}^{0},\bar{z}_{2}^{0})\Big\rangle^{\prime}=\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,\left(-\frac{i}{2}w_{1}R\frac{1}{|A|^{2}w}\frac{1}{|z_{12}^{0}|^{4}}\right)\,\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},0}\,,\end{split}

Combining the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic pieces in the analogue of (
A.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Big\langle\Delta_{X^{0}}V_{1}(z_{1}^{0},\bar{z}_{1}^{0})\,V_{2}(z_{2}^{0},\bar{z}_{2}^{0})\Big\rangle^{\prime}=&\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,\frac{|A|^{2}}{4\pi}\left(-\frac{i}{2}w_{1}R\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\frac{1}{|z_{12}^{0}|^{4}}\times 2\right)\\
&\times\frac{\int d^{2}w\sqrt{|g_{S^{2}}(w)|}}{4\pi R_{S^{2}}^{2}}\,\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{-1}\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},0}\\
=&-\frac{i}{2\pi}w_{1}R\,\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,}{|z_{12}^{0}|^{4}}\,\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},0}\,.\end{aligned}

(3.48)

Substituting into (
3.45
), the two-point amplitude simplifies to

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}w_{1}R^{2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,\delta_{w_{1}+w_{2},0}\,,

(3.49)

where we have used the relation,

\mathcal{F}[\hat{g}]\,\text{det}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{-1}=1\,.

(3.50)

In the following, we match the two-point string amplitude (
3.49
) to its dual Yang–Mills counterpart and determine the normalization factor
\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}
.

3.3.3
Duality dictionary

Matching the two-point amplitudes in chiral 2d YM (
3.5
) and in dual string theory (
3.49

\frac{1}{2}R^{2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}=1\,,

(3.52)

while matching the three-point amplitudes (
3.8
) and (
3.51
), we obtain

g_{s}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}=\frac{\lambda}{N_{c}}\,.

(3.53)

Allowing the scaling ambiguity in operator normalizations, parameterized by a factor
r
, the dictionary between the two theories reads

\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}=\frac{2r^{2}}{R^{2}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg_{s}=\frac{r\lambda R^{2}}{2N_{c}}\,.

(3.54)

This dictionary was presented in
[

]
. Note that, although the dictionary involves the ambiguity
r
, the kinematical dependence for the three-point function
w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}
is free from this ambiguity and its matching provides a nontrivial test of our duality.

In what follows, we compute the four-point amplitudes on both sides of our proposed duality, which provide an additional consistency check of the proposal.

3.4
Four-point string amplitude

For four point string amplitudes, the polynomial in (
3.41
) takes the form

P_{4}(z)=Az^{2}+Bz+C\,,

(3.55)

with the coefficients given by

A=\sum_{i=1}^{4}w_{i}\,z_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptB=-\sum_{i<j}^{4}(w_{i}+w_{j})\,z_{i}z_{j},\hskip 10.00002ptC=-\sum_{i\neq j,\,j<k<l}^{4}w_{i}z_{j}z_{k}z_{l}\,.

(3.56)

We can compute its discriminant and obtain

\begin{split}\Delta(P_{4})&=B^{2}-4AC=(w_{1}+w_{3})^{2}z_{13}^{2}z_{24}^{2}\,(z_{c}-a)(z_{c}-b)\,,\end{split}

(3.57)

with
z_{c}
being the conformal cross ratio,
z_{c}=z_{12}z_{34}/(z_{13}z_{24})
, and

a,b=-\frac{w_{1}w_{4}+w_{2}w_{3}\pm 2\sqrt{w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4}}}{(w_{1}+w_{3})^{2}}\,.

(3.58)

The 4-point worldsheet correlator then reads

\begin{split}G_{4}=i(2\pi)^{2}Rg_{s}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,&\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\delta_{\sum_{k}w_{k}}|z_{ab}z_{bc}z_{ca}|^{2}(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})^{q}\\
&\times\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}|z_{ij}|^{-2q}|w_{1}+w_{3}|^{2q}|z_{13}z_{24}|^{2q}|z_{c}-a|^{q}|z_{c}-b|^{q}\,.\end{split}

(3.59)

Fixing three insertion points
z_{1}\to 0,z_{3}\to 1
and
z_{4}\to\infty
, and defining the vertex operator and ghost insertion at
\infty
according to the standard conformal prescription, the above expression becomes

G_{4}=i(2\pi)^{2}Rg_{s}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{S^{2}}\,\delta\left(\sum_{k}E_{k}\right)\delta_{\sum_{k}w_{k}}(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})^{q}|w_{1}+w_{3}|^{2q}|z_{2}|^{-2q}|1-z_{2}|^{-2q}|z_{2}-a|^{q}|z_{2}-b|^{q}\,.

(3.60)

This expression for the worldsheet correlator further confirms that the vertex operators (
2.21
) are conformal primaries of dimension
(q,q)=(1,1)
.

The string amplitude is obtained by integrating this correlator over the remaining unfixed coordinate
z_{2}
on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}

\mathcal{A}_{4}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}d^{2}z_{2}\,G_{4}\,,

(3.61)

which requires evaluating the integral

\mathcal{I}_{4}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}d^{2}z_{2}\,|z_{2}|^{-2q}|1-z_{2}|^{-2q}|z_{2}-a|^{q}|z_{2}-b|^{q}\,.

(3.62)

In the
q=1
limit that is relevant for 2d YM, the integral is divergence. This is because the conservation of the winding
w
’s forces the intermediate state to be always on-shell. Note that this property is also true on the 2d YM side.

To perform a more quantitative comparison (beyond
\infty=\infty
), we will now regulate the integral and evaluate its leading behavior near the
s
-,
t
-, and
u
-channel poles and compare the result with the corresponding contributions on the dual Yang–Mills side.

Evaluating the moduli integral:

We rewrite the moduli integral,

\mathcal{I}_{4}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}d^{2}z\,|z|^{-2s-2}|1-z|^{-2t-2}|z_{2}-a|\,|z_{2}-b|\,,

(3.63)

by introducing two regularization parameters,
s
and
t
in the integrand and setting
q=1
. This integral has been evaluated in
[
24
]
using a KLT-like factorization of the complex area integral, yielding

\mathcal{I}_{4}=i\,\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\,,

(3.64)

where
\zeta
and
\xi
are real light-cone-like coordinates on the worldsheet. Depending on the range of
\zeta
, the integral
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
receives contributions from different contours and thus takes different forms. In what follows, we list these cases and analyze the behavior of the integral in the limits
s\to 0_{-}
,
t\to 0_{-}
, and
u=-(s+t)\to 0_{-}
, which will be our main focus. In
[
24
]
, the computation was carried out under the assumption that
a,b
are real with
0<a<b<1
. However, the final expressions for the integrals admit an analytic continuation to arbitrary complex values of
a
and
b
.

1.
\boldsymbol{0<\zeta<a}
:

In this range of
\zeta
, the integrals take the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}&=2i\sin(\pi s)\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\xi^{\prime}\,(-\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}(1-\xi^{\prime})^{-t-1}(a-\xi^{\prime})^{1/2}(b-\xi^{\prime})^{1/2}\,,\\
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}&=\int_{0}^{a}d\zeta\,\zeta^{-\alpha^{\prime}s/4-1}(1-\zeta)^{-\alpha^{\prime}t/4-1}(a-\zeta)^{1/2}(b-\zeta)^{1/2}\,.\end{split}

(3.65)

As
s\to 0_{-}
, the simple pole in
s
from the integral in
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
is canceled by the zero of the prefactor
\sin(\pi s)
, so
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
remains finite. In contrast,
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
develops a simple pole in
s
. We now analyze this limits in detail.

As
s\to 0_{-}
, the integral in
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
gets contribution near
\xi^{\prime}\sim 0
, where it behaves as (with
\epsilon>0
)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}&\sim 2i\sin[\pi s]\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}d\xi^{\prime}\,(-\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}(1-\xi^{\prime})^{-t-1}(a-\xi^{\prime})^{1/2}(b-\xi^{\prime})^{1/2}\\
&\sim 2i\pi s\cdot a^{1/2}\cdot b^{1/2}\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}d\xi^{\prime}\,(-\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}\sim-2\pi i\cdot(ab)^{1/2}\,.\end{split}

(3.66)

Similarly integral in
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
receives a contribution from
\zeta\sim 0
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}&\sim\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\zeta\,\zeta^{-s-1}(1-\zeta)^{-t-1}(a-\zeta)^{1/2}(b-\zeta)^{1/2}\\
&\sim a^{1/2}\cdot b^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\zeta\,\zeta^{-s-1}\sim-\frac{(ab)^{1/2}}{s}\,.\end{split}

(3.67)

where we have used,

\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}d\xi^{\prime}\,(-\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\xi^{\prime}\,(\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}=\frac{(\xi^{\prime})^{-s}}{-s}\Big|_{0}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon^{-s}-0}{-s}\sim-\frac{1}{s}\,.

(3.68)

Thus, near
s\to 0_{-}
, the integral behaves as

i\,\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\,\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}\sim-2\pi\frac{ab}{s}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002pts\to 0_{-}\,.

(3.69)

On the other hand, as
t\to 0_{-}
, both
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
remain finite. However
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
develops a simple pole in
u
as
u\to 0_{-}
. o make this explicit, we change variables
\xi^{\prime}=-x/(1-x)
, so that

d\xi^{\prime}=-dx(1-x)^{-2},\hskip 10.00002pt1-\xi^{\prime}=(1-x)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pta-\xi^{\prime}=[a+(1-a)x](1-x)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002ptb-\xi^{\prime}=[b+(1-b)x](1-x)^{-1}\,.

(3.70)

The integral
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
then takes the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}&=2i\sin(\pi s)\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{-s-1}(1-x)^{s+t-1}[a+(1-a)x]^{1/2}[b+(1-b)x]^{1/2}\,.\end{split}

(3.71)

As
u=-(s+t)\to 0_{-}
, the dominant contribution comes from
x\sim 1
, where the integral goes as

\begin{split}&\int_{1-\epsilon}^{1}dx\,x^{-s-1}(1-x)^{-u-1}[a+(1-a)x]^{1/2}[b+(1-b)x]^{1/2}\\
\sim&\int_{1-\epsilon}^{1}dx\,(1-x)^{-u-1}=\int_{\epsilon}^{0}(-dx^{\prime})\,(x^{\prime})^{-u-1}=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}dx^{\prime}\,(x^{\prime})^{-u-1}\sim-\frac{1}{u}\,.\end{split}

(3.72)

Hence,

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\sim-\frac{2i\sin(\pi s)}{u},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002ptu\to 0_{-}\,.

(3.73)

2.
\boldsymbol{a<\zeta<b}
:

In this regime of
\zeta
, the integrals are

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}&=2i\int_{0}^{a}d\xi\,\xi^{-s-1}(1-\xi)^{-t-1}(a-\xi)^{1/2}(b-\xi)^{1/2}-2i\cos(\pi s)\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\xi\,(-\xi)^{-s-1}(1-\xi)^{-t-1}(a-\xi)^{1/2}(b-\xi)^{1/2}\,,\\

(3.74)

It is clear that as

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
nor
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
produces a

pole. Similarly,
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
doesn’t generate a
s^{-1}

where dominant contribution comes from

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\sim&2i\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\xi\,\xi^{-s-1}-2i\cos(\pi s)\int_{-\epsilon}^{0}d\xi\,(-\xi)^{-s-1}\\
=&2i\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{-s}}{(-s)}-2i\cos(\pi s)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{-s}}{(-s)}\sim 0\,,\end{split}

(3.75)

showing that

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\sim+\frac{2i\cos(\pi s)}{u},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002ptu\to 0_{-}\,.

(3.76)

3.
\boldsymbol{b<\zeta<1}

In this regime, the integrals take the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}&=2i\sin(\pi t)\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi^{\prime}\,(1+\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}(\xi^{\prime})^{-t-1}(\xi^{\prime}+1-a)^{1/2}(\xi^{\prime}+1-b)^{1/2}\,,\\
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}&=\int_{b}^{1}d\zeta\,\zeta^{-s-1}(1-\zeta)^{-t-1}(\zeta-a)^{1/2}(\zeta-b)^{1/2}\,.\end{split}

(3.77)

As
t\to 0_{-}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
develops a simple pole in
t
, while
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
remains due to the zero of the prefactor
\sin(\pi t)

As
t\to 0_{-}
, the dominant contribution from the integral in
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
comes from
\xi^{\prime}\sim 0
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}&\sim 2i\sin(\pi t)\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\xi^{\prime}\,(1+\xi^{\prime})^{-s-1}(\xi^{\prime})^{-t-1}(\xi^{\prime}+1-a)^{1/2}(\xi^{\prime}+1-b)^{1/2}\\
&\sim 2i\cdot\pi t\cdot(1-a)^{1/2}(1-b)^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\xi^{\prime}\,(\xi^{\prime})^{-t-1}\sim-2\pi i\,(1-a)^{1/2}(1-b)^{1/2}\,,\end{split}

(3.78)

where we have used
\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\xi^{\prime}\,(\xi^{\prime})^{-t-1}\sim-1/t
.

The
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}
integral gets contribution from
\zeta\sim 1
, and we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}&\sim\int_{1-\epsilon}^{1}d\zeta\,\zeta^{-s-1}(1-\zeta)^{-t-1}(\zeta-a)^{1/2}(\zeta-b)^{1/2}\\
&=\int_{\epsilon}^{0}(-d\zeta^{\prime})\,(1-\zeta^{\prime})^{-s-1}(\zeta^{\prime})^{-t-1}(1-a-\zeta)^{1/2}(1-b-\zeta)^{1/2}\\
&\sim(1-a)^{1/2}(1-b)^{1/2}\int_{\epsilon}^{0}(-d\zeta^{\prime})(\zeta^{\prime})^{-t-1}\sim-\frac{(1-a)^{1/2}(1-b)^{1/2}}{t}\,,\end{split}

(3.79)

where we have used

\int_{\epsilon}^{0}(-d\zeta^{\prime})(\zeta^{\prime})^{-t-1}=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}d\zeta^{\prime}(\zeta^{\prime})^{-t-1}=\frac{(\zeta^{\prime})^{-t}}{-t}\Big|_{0}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon^{-t}-0}{-t}=-\frac{1}{t}\,.

(3.80)

Combining both contributions, we obtain

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\,\mathcal{I}_{\zeta}\sim 2\pi i\frac{(1-a)(1-b)}{t}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002ptt\to 0_{-}\,.

(3.81)

Next we examine the
u\to 0_{-}
behavior of
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
. Making a change of variable
\xi^{\prime}=x/(1-x)
in the integral of
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
, we have

d\xi^{\prime}=dx(1-x)^{-2},\hskip 10.00002pt1+\xi^{\prime}=(1-x)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pt1+\xi^{\prime}-a=[1-a(1-x)](1-x)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pt1+\xi^{\prime}-b=[1-b(1-x)](1-x)^{-1}\,.

(3.82)

Using these, the integral becomes

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\xi}=2i\sin(\pi t)\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{-t-1}(1-x)^{s+t-1}[1-a(1-x)]^{1/2}[1-b(1-x)]^{1/2}\,.\end{split}

(3.83)

As
u=-(s+t)\to 0_{-}
, the dominant contribution arises from
x\sim 1
:

\begin{split}&\int_{1-\epsilon}^{1}dx\,x^{-t-1}(1-x)^{-u-1}[1-a(1-x)]^{1/2}[1-b(1-x)]^{1/2}\\
\sim&\int_{1-\epsilon}^{1}dx\,(1-x)^{-u-1}\sim\int_{\epsilon}^{0}(-dx^{\prime})(x^{\prime})^{-u-1}=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}dx^{\prime}\,(x^{\prime})^{-u-1}=-\frac{1}{u}\end{split}

(3.84)

Hence,

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\sim\frac{2i\sin(\pi s)}{u},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002ptu\to 0_{-}\,,

(3.85)

where we used
\sin(\pi t )\sim-\sin(\pi s)
as
u\to 0_{-}
.

Kinetic part of CLD action:

We begin by considering the kinetic term of the CLD action. Performing integration by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}\left(\Gamma[\rho]\right)_{\text{kinetic}}&=\frac{q}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{2\pi}\,\partial_{\alpha}\varphi\,\partial^{\alpha}\varphi=-\frac{q}{4\pi}\int d^{2}\sigma\,\varphi\,\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}\varphi+\frac{q}{4\pi}\int_{\partial}ds\,\varphi\,\partial_{n}\varphi\,,\end{split}

(4.2)

where the coordinates

\left(\Gamma[\rho]\right)_{\text{kinetic}}=\frac{q}{4\pi}\int_{\partial}ds\,\varphi\,\partial_{n}\varphi=\frac{q}{4\pi}\left(\int_{\cup_{I}\partial D_{Z_{I}}}+\int_{\cup_{k}\partial D_{z_{k}}}+\int_{\partial D_{z_{\infty}}}\right)ds\,\varphi\,\partial_{n}\varphi\,.

(4.3)

For a disc boundary, the outward normal derivative takes the form

\partial_{n}=-\frac{1}{|z|}(z\,\partial+\bar{z}\,\bar{\partial})\,.

(4.4)

Substituting this into the boundary integrals, the line integral can be rewritten in terms of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic contour integrals as

\int_{\partial}ds\,\varphi\,\partial_{n}\varphi=i\oint dz\,\varphi\,\partial\varphi-i\oint d\bar{z}\,\varphi\,\bar{\partial}\varphi\,,

(4.5)

with contours encircling the centers of the excised discs

Curvature part of CLD action:

We next evaluate the curvature contribution of

4.2
Computation of
\beta
-
\beta
OPE

With the expression of the CLD action at Mandelstam localization maps, we now compute the
\beta
-
\beta
OPE. We begin with the correlation function of vertex operators of the form
\exp\left[\alpha_{r}\int^{z_{r}}\beta\right]
, which acts as a generating functional for
\beta(z_{r})
insertions in the string path integral. Such correlation function takes the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{Z}\left(\{\alpha_{r},\bar{\alpha}_{r},z_{r}\}\right)&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\int\left[\mathcal{D}\Phi\right]\,\exp[-S+\sum_{r=1}^{n}\alpha_{r}\int^{z_{r}}dz^{\prime}\,\beta(z^{\prime})+\sum_{r=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{r}\int^{\bar{z}_{r}}d\bar{z}^{\prime}\,\beta(\bar{z}^{\prime})]\\
&=\Big\langle\prod_{r=1}^{n}e^{\alpha_{r}\int^{z_{r}}\beta+\bar{\alpha}_{r}\int^{\bar{z}_{r}}\bar{\beta}}\Big\rangle\,,\end{split}

(4.40)

where

\left[\mathcal{D}\Phi\right]=[\mathcal{D}\beta][\mathcal{D}\bar{\beta}][\mathcal{D}X^{+}][\mathcal{D}X^{-}]\,,

and
\sum_{r=1}^{n}\alpha_{r}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{r}=0
. Here
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is the vacuum partition function used to normalize the generating function to one without any insertions. Integrating out
(\beta,\bar{\beta})
, we find that
(X^{+},X^{-})
localizes into the Mandelstam maps (
3.19
). Then the generating functional reduces to the worldsheet CLD action localized at these maps
(\rho(z),\bar{\rho}(\bar{z}))
:

\mathcal{Z}\left(\{\alpha_{r},\bar{\alpha}_{r},z_{r}\}\right)=\exp\left[-\Gamma[\rho]\right].

(4.41)

Here
\Gamma[\rho]
is the on-shell action
\frac{q}{2}\int\mathcal{L}_{CLD}
with the regularization in (
4.38
). To compute the one-point function of
\beta(z)
, we first introduce two extra insertion points and consider the new generating functional

\Big\langle\prod_{r=0}^{n+1}e^{\alpha_{r}\int^{z_{r}}\beta+\bar{\alpha}_{r}\int^{\bar{z}_{r}}\bar{\beta}}\Big\rangle=\exp[-\Gamma^{\prime}[\rho^{\prime}]\Big]\,,

(4.42)

where the new Mandelstam map is given by

\rho^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{n+1}\alpha_{r}\log(z-z_r),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{n+1}=-\alpha_{0}\,.

(4.43)

The one-point function
\langle\beta(z_{0})\rangle
is then obtained by bringing down
\int^{z_{0}}\beta
in the path integral and taking another derivative
\partial_{z_{0}}
:

\langle\beta(z_{0})\rangle_{\rho}=\partial_{z_{0}}\partial_{\alpha_{0}}\Big(-\Gamma^{\prime}[\rho^{\prime}]\Big)\Big|_{\alpha_{0}=0}\,,

(4.44)

where the subscript
\rho
denotes the Mandelstam map used in computing the corresponding string path integral. We can iterate this process and compute the two-point function

\Big\langle\beta(z)\beta(z_{0})\Big\rangle_{\rho}=\partial_{z_{0}}\partial_{\alpha_{0}}\langle\beta(z_{0})\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}}\Big|_{\alpha_{0}=0}\,.

(4.45)

Thus to compute
\beta\beta
OPE, we need to find derivative
\partial_{z_{0}}
of various components of
\Gamma^{\prime}[\rho^{\prime}]
in analogue expression to (
4.38
), which we will be doing next.

C_{j}\equiv a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1}
in fig.
8
, we have

\int_{a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}=\int_{a_{j}}\left(\oint_{C_{z_{j}}}d\varphi_{h}\right)d\varphi_{a}=\oint_{C_{z_{j}}}d\varphi_{h}\cdot\int_{\bar{z}_{j}}^{\bar{z}_{\infty}}d\varphi_{a}=2\pi i\log\left[\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{\epsilon_{z_{j}}e^{-i\theta_{j}}\prod_{k(\neq j))}^{n}(\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{k})}{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{Z}_{I})}\right]\,,

(B.6)

where the first equality above follows from

\begin{split}&\int_{a_{1}}\varphi_{h}(a_{1})\,d\varphi_{a}+\int_{a_{1}^{-1}}\varphi_{h}(a_{1}^{-1})\,d\varphi_{a}=\int_{a_{1}}\varphi_{h}(a_{1})\,d\varphi_{a}-\int_{a_{1}}\varphi_{h}(a_{1}^{-1})\,d\varphi_{a}=\int_{a_{1}}\left[\varphi_{h}(a_{1})-\varphi_{h}(a_{1}^{-1})\right]\,d\varphi_{a}\\
&\text{and}\quad\delta\varphi_{h}=\left[\varphi_{h}(a_{1})-\varphi_{h}(a_{1}^{-1})\right]=\oint_{C_{z_{1}}}d\varphi_{h}.=2\pi i\,.\end{split}

(B.7)

Likewise, for the other term of the boundary integral, we get

\int_{a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1}}\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h}=\int_{a_{j}}\left(\oint_{C_{\bar{z}_{j}}}d\varphi_{a}\right)d\varphi_{h}=-2\pi i\log\left[\frac{1}{z_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{\epsilon_{z_{j}}e^{i\theta_{j}}\prod_{k(\neq j))}^{n}(z_{j}-z_{k})}{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(z_{j}-Z_{I})}\right]\,.

(B.8)

Hence, the net contribution from all
C_{j}
with
j=1,\cdots,n
reads

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\oint_{a_{k}+a_{k}^{-1}}(\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}-\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h})=\pi i\,\log\left[\frac{1}{|z_{\infty}|^{4n}}\frac{\prod_{j\neq k}^{n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}}{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}\prod_{k=1}^{n}|z_{k}-Z_{I}|^{2}}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon_{z_{k}}^{2}\right]\,.

(B.9)

Along
\widetilde{C}_{J}
:

Along
\widetilde{C}_{J}\equiv A_{J}+A_{J}^{-1}
, we have similar expressions,

\int_{A_{J}+A_{J}^{-1}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}=\int_{A_{J}}\left(\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}d\varphi_{h}\right)d\varphi_{a}=\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}d\varphi_{h}\cdot\int_{\bar{Z}_{J}}^{\bar{z}_{\infty}}d\varphi_{a}=-2\pi i\log\left[\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(\bar{Z}_{J}-\bar{z}_{k})}{\epsilon_{Z_{J}}e^{-i\Theta_{J}}\prod_{I(\neq J)}^{n-2}(\bar{Z}_{J}-\bar{Z}_{I})}\right]\,,

(B.10)

and similarly, the other term of the boundary integral takes the form

\int_{A_{J}+A_{J}^{-1}}\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h}=2\pi i\log\left[\frac{1}{z_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(Z_{J}-z_{k})}{\epsilon_{Z_{J}}e^{i\Theta_{J}}\prod_{I(\neq J)}^{n-2}(Z_{J}-Z_{I})}\right]\,.

(B.11)

Net contribution from all
\widetilde{C}_{J}
with
J=1,\cdots,(n-2)
reads

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{I=1}^{n-2}\oint_{A_{I}+A_{I}^{-1}}(\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}-\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h})=-\pi i\,\log\left[\frac{1}{|z_{\infty}|^{4(n-2)}}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{n-2}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}|z_{k}-Z_{I}|}{\prod_{I\neq J}^{n-2}|Z_{I}-Z_{J}|}\frac{1}{\prod_{J=1}^{n-2}(2r_{J})}\right]\,,

(B.12)

where we have translated regulator
\epsilon_{Z_{I}}
around
(Z_{I},\bar{Z}_{I})
from the
\angle z
-plane to the
r_{I}
on the
\angle\rho
-plane using (
4.6
) and (
B.4
):

\begin{split}\log\epsilon_{Z_{I}}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(2r_I)-\log|\partial^{2}\rho(Z_{I})|\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|\frac{\prod_{J(\neq I)}|Z_{I}-Z_{J}|}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}|Z_{I}-z_{k}|}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\log(2r_I)\,.\end{split}

(B.13)

Around
(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
:

Next we compute the contour integral around
(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
:

\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}=\log\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)\frac{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(z_{i}-Z_{I})}{\prod_{k(\neq 1)}^{n}(z_{i}-z_{k})}\right]\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}d\varphi_{a}-\oint_{C_{z_{1}}}\log(z-z_1)\,d\varphi_{a}\,.

(B.14)

The first contour integral gives
\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}d\varphi_{a}=-2\pi i
, while the second one yields

\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}\log(z-z_1)\,d\varphi_{a}=\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}\log(z-z_i)\,\left(\sum_{I=1}^{n-2}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}-\bar{Z}_{I}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{k}}\right)\,,

(B.15)

where all terms other than the one with
(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{i})^{-1}
, vanishes:

\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}d\bar{z}\,\frac{\log(z-z_i)}{\bar{z}-U}=\int_{0}^{-2\pi}(-i\epsilon_{z_{i}}e^{-i\theta_{i}})d\theta_{i}\frac{\log(\epsilon_{z_i}e^{i\theta_i})}{\bar{z}_{i}+\epsilon_{z_{i}}e^{-i\theta_{i}}-U}\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002pt\epsilon_{z_{i}}\to 0\,.

(B.16)

The only term that contributes is the following:

\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}\log(z-z_i)\frac{-d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{i}}=+i\int_{0}^{-2\pi}d\theta_{i}\left[\log( \epsilon_{z_i})+i\theta_{i}\right]=-2\pi i\log(\epsilon_{z_i})-2\pi^{2}\,.

(B.17)

Collecting these contributions, we obtain

\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}=-2\pi i\log\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)\frac{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(z_{i}-Z_{I})}{\prod_{k\left(\neq i\right)}^{n}(z_{i}-z_{k})}\right]+2\pi i\log\epsilon_{z_{i}}+2\pi^{2}\,.

(B.18)

Similarly, for the other term of the boundary integral, we get

\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h}=2\pi i\log\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bar{z}_{k}\right)\frac{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{Z}_{I})}{\prod_{k\left(\neq i\right)}^{n}(\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{z}_{k})}\right]-2\pi i\log\epsilon_{z_{i}}+2\pi^{2}\,.

(B.19)

Thus net contribution from the contour circles around vertex operator insertion points reads

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\oint_{C_{z_{k}}}(\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}-\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h})=-2\pi i\,\log\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{n}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}|z_{k}-Z_{I}|}{\prod_{k\neq j}^{n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|\prod_{k}\epsilon_{z_{k}}}\right]\,.

(B.20)

Around
(Z_{J},\bar{Z}_{J})
:

Contribution from contour integral around the interaction points is similar:

\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}=\log\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)\frac{\prod_{I(\neq J)}^{n-2}(Z_{J}-Z_{I})}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(Z_{J}-z_{k})}\right]\oint_{C_{Z_{1}}}d\varphi_{a}+\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}\log(z-Z_J)\,d\varphi_{a}\,.

(B.21)

With
\oint_{C_{Z_{1}}}d\varphi_{a}=2\pi i
, and so the last term on the rhs of the above expression reads

\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}\log(z-Z_J)\,d\varphi_{a}=\oint_{C_{Z_{1}}}\log(z-Z_J)\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}-\bar{Z}_{J}}=-i\int_{0}^{-2\pi}d\Theta_{J}\left[\log( \epsilon_{Z_J})+i\Theta_{J}\right]=2\pi i\log\epsilon_{Z_{J}}+2\pi^{2}\,.

(B.22)

Thus, we obtain

\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}=2\pi i\log\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)\frac{\prod_{I(\neq J)}^{n-2}(Z_{J}-Z_{I})}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(Z_{J}-z_{k})}\right]+2\pi i\log\epsilon_{Z_{J}}+2\pi^{2}\,.

(B.23)

Similarly, for other term of the boundary integral, we obtain

\oint_{C_{Z_{J}}}\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h}=-2\pi i\log\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bar{z}_{k}\right)\frac{\prod_{I(\neq J)}^{n-2}(\bar{Z}_{J}-\bar{Z}_{I})}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(\bar{Z}_{J}-\bar{z}_{k})}\right]-2\pi i\log\epsilon_{Z_{J}}+2\pi^{2}\,.

(B.24)

Hence net contribution from the contours around the interaction point reads

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{I=1}^{n-2}\oint_{C_{Z_{I}}}(\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}-\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h})=\pi i\,\log\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\,z_{k}\right|^{n-2}\frac{\prod_{I\neq J}^{n-2}|Z_{I}-Z_{J}|}{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}\prod_{k=1}^{n}|z_{k}-Z_{I}}\,\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(2r_{I})\right]\,,

(B.25)

where we have used (
B.13
).

Around
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
:

To compute
\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}
,
we first note that near
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
,
\varphi_{h}
and
d\varphi_{a}
behaves as

\begin{split}\varphi_{h}\sim\log\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)-2\log(z)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad d\varphi_{a}\sim-\frac{2}{\bar{z}}\,d\bar{z}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{z}^{2}}\right)d\bar{z}\,.\end{split}

(B.26)

Using this, the contour integral becomes

\begin{split}\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}&=-2\log\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)\underbrace{\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}}_{-2\pi i}+4\underbrace{\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}\,\log(z)}_{-2\pi i\log|z_{\infty}|-2\pi^{2}}\\
&=2\pi i\log\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right)^{2}-2\pi i\log|z_{\infty}|^{4}-8\pi^{2}\,.\end{split}

(B.27)

Similarly, for the other term around
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
, we have

\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h}=-2\pi i\log\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\bar{z}_{k}\right)^{2}+2\pi i\log|z_{\infty}|^{4}-8\pi^{2}\,.

(B.28)

Net contribution from the contour around
(z_{\infty},\bar{z}_{\infty})
is thus

\frac{1}{2}\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}(\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}-\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h})=2\pi i\,\log\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{2}-2\pi i\,\log\left|z_{\infty}\right|^{4}\,.

(B.29)

Adding all the contributions (
B.9
), (
B.12
), (
B.20
), (
B.25
), and (
B.29
), we obtain the kinetic term of CLD action localized at the Mandelstam maps:

\begin{split}-i\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{2}z\,\partial\varphi\,\bar{\partial}\varphi&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a_{k}+a_{k}^{-1}+C_{z_{k}}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\oint_{C_{z_{k}}}+\sum_{I=1}^{n-2}\int_{A_{I}+A_{I}^{-1}}+\sum_{I=1}^{n-2}\oint_{C_{Z_{I}}}+\oint_{C_{z_{\infty}}}\right](\varphi_{h}\,d\varphi_{a}-\varphi_{a}\,d\varphi_{h})\\
&=-2\pi i\log\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}z_{k}\right|^{n-2}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}|z_{k}-Z_{I}|^{3}}{\prod_{j\neq k}^{n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{I\neq J}^{n-2}|Z_{I}-Z_{J}|}\,\frac{|z_{\infty}|^{8}}{\prod_{I=1}^{n-2}(2r_{I})\prod_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon_{z_{k}}^{2}}\right]\,.\end{split}

(B.30)

Using (
B.4
), it matches with what has been computed in the alternative way in (

Existence of Minimal Surfaces in Infinite Volume Hyperbolic 3 3 -manifolds
Baris Coskunuzer, Zheng Huang, Ben Lowe, Franco Vargas Pallete

Abstract
. The existence of embedded minimal surfaces in non-compact 3-manifolds remains a largely unresolved and challenging problem in geometry. In this paper, we address several open cases regarding the existence of finite-area, embedded, complete, minimal surfaces in infinite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds. Among other results, for doubly degenerate manifolds with bounded geometry, we prove an alternative: either every such manifold contains a closed minimal surface or there exists such a manifold admitting a foliation by closed minimal surfaces. We also construct the first examples of Schottky manifolds with closed minimal surfaces and demonstrate the existence of Schottky manifolds containing infinitely many closed minimal surfaces. Lastly, for hyperbolic 3-manifolds with rank-1 cusps, we show that a broad class of these manifolds must contain a finite-area, embedded, complete minimal surface.



1.
Introduction and Statement of Main Results

The existence of minimal surfaces in Riemannian
3
-manifolds is one of the classical problems in geometric analysis.
Significant progress has been made in understanding the existence of closed embedded minimal surfaces in compact 3-manifolds, particularly in the positive curvature case. Using the Min-Max approach, Pitts and Simon-Smith established that every closed Riemannian 3-manifold contains a smooth, embedded, closed minimal surface
[
45
]
,
[
52
]
,
[
17
]
. By the methods of geometric measure theory, Federer
[
22
]
demonstrated that for any closed Riemannian 3-manifold, an area-minimizing surface exists in each homology class. Meeks, Simon, and Yau
[
38
]
further showed that every isotopy class contains a minimal surface in such manifolds. More recently, Song
[
54
]
resolved Yau’s Conjecture, proving that any closed 3-manifold contains infinitely many minimal surfaces, building on techniques developed by Marques and Neves
[
36
]
.

Various results have been established for compact Riemannian 3-manifolds, but the question of whether embedded closed minimal surfaces exist in non-compact Riemannian 3-manifolds remains open in many contexts. In the particularly important case of infinite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds, this problem sits at the intersection of geometric topology and geometric analysis, where the manifold’s topology critically influences the existence of closed (or finite-area) minimal surfaces. Recently, the first author
[
19
]
demonstrated the existence of closed minimal surfaces in several instances of infinite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds. However, many key questions remain unresolved. In this paper, we address these open cases by proving multiple zero-infinity dichotomy results and resolving some highly technical rank-1 cases.

In particular, the first author showed the existence of closed embedded minimal surfaces in all infinite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds (
Lemma

2.1
), with the exception of the following three cases:

(1)

\mathcal{M}
is a geometrically infinite product manifold.

(2)

\mathcal{M}
is topologically a handlebody.

(3)

\mathcal{M}
contains rank-1 cusps.

Before stating our results, we note that
throughout the paper, we only consider complete, orientable hyperbolic
3
-manifolds with finitely generated fundamental groups.

We recall that a complete hyperbolic
3

Before addressing the missing cases above, we first establish an important preliminary result on the number of minimal surfaces in non-cusped hyperbolic

Theorem 1.1

Let

Let

This theorem establishes a connection between the existence theory of minimal surfaces and the longstanding Hass-Thurston conjecture, on the non-existence of minimal foliations of hyperbolic 3-manifolds (see Question

Building on the ideas of the proof of this result, we show that the growth rate of disjoint stable minimal surfaces is controlled by the volume of the convex core, conditional on a mild strengthening of the Hass-Thurston conjecture. We are able to prove an unconditional statement for certain quasi-Fuchsian manifolds with short geodesics (

4.6

Next, we establish a strong dichotomy for the existence of minimal surfaces in quasi-Fuchsian manifolds.

Theorem 1.3

(Dichotomy for Quasi-Fuchsian Manifolds)
.

A quasi-Fuchsian manifold (without rank-1 cusps) satisfies exactly one of the following statements:

•

It has a unique closed minimal surface, which is the area minimizer; or

•

It contains infinitely many saddle point minimal surfaces.

For case (2) - hyperbolic handlebodies, we construct the first examples of Schottky manifolds containing closed minimal surfaces. Furthermore, we show existence of Schottky containing infinitely many closed, embedded minimal surfaces.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
3

1

Although the infinite family of minimal surfaces given by this result are saddle point minimal surfaces, we also construct examples of Schottky manifolds that contain arbitrarily many stable closed minimal surfaces (

Finally, we show the existence of a complete, finite-area, embedded minimal surface for a broad class of hyperbolic 3-manifolds with rank-1 cusps. In contrast to the first author’s previous work on the existence problem for minimal surfaces using shrinkwrapping surfaces

A compact, oriented, irreducible, atoroidal 3-manifold with non-empty boundary carries many infinite volume hyperbolic metrics that have a geometrically finite end with a rank-1 cusp, see e.g.

2.1.
Finite Volume Hyperbolic
3
-Manifolds

There exist two types of finite volume hyperbolic
3
-manifolds, classified by whether or not they are compact: compact (closed) and non-compact (cusped). The existence of closed embedded minimal surfaces in closed hyperbolic
3
-manifolds was established decades ago via Almgren-Pitts min-max theory
[
45
]
. Since finite volume cusped hyperbolic
3
-manifolds only have rank-2 cusps
[
4
]
, this implies that the manifold is homeomorphic to the interior of a compact
3
-manifold where every boundary component is a 2-torus. Recently, the existence of closed minimal surfaces has been shown in this non-compact case by several authors
[
18
,
31
,
46
]
. Furthermore, Chambers-Liokumovich obtained a very general existence result for any finite volume noncompact Riemannian
3
-manifold
[
16
]
by generalizing Almgren-Pitts min-max techniques. Recently, Song showed that finite volume
3
-manifolds contain infinitely many closed embedded minimal surfaces
[
53
]
.

2.2.
Rank-1 and Rank-2 Cusps

While rank-2 cusps are relatively straightforward, understanding rank-1 cusps in hyperbolic 3-manifolds can be more challenging. Rank-2 cusps have finite volume and are structured as
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\infty)
, whereas rank-1 cusps have infinite volume and take the structure
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
. Unlike rank-2 cusps, which are isolated within the manifold and do not interact with other ends or cusps, rank-1 cusps behave quite differently. In the next section, we will explain how rank-1 cusps are situated within the manifold, how they are represented by annuli in the boundary of a (relative) compact core, and how they significantly influence the structure of the ends of the hyperbolic manifold.

From a geometric perspective, rank-
1
cusps can be illustrated as follows. Consider a horoball
\mathcal{H}_{q}
in
\mathbf{H}^{3}
with its boundary
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{q}
at a point
q\in S^{2}_{\infty}({\mathbf{H}}^{3})
. Let
\varphi
be a parabolic isometry that fixes the point
q
. The infinite cyclic group
\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}
generated by
\varphi
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. A rank-1 cusp
\mathcal{U}_{k}
is then isometric to the quotient
\mathcal{H}_{q}/\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}
for some
\mathcal{H}_{q}
and
\varphi

[
4
]
. For instance, in the upper half-space model of
\mathbf{H}^{3}
, let
\mathcal{H}=\{(x,y,z)\mid z\geq 1\}
and
\varphi(x,y,z)=(x+1,y,z)
. For these
\mathcal{H}
and
\varphi
, a rank-1 cusp
\mathcal{U}
is isometric to
[0,1]\times\mathbf{R}\times[1,\infty)
, where the boundaries
\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}\times[1,\infty)
and
\{1\}\times\mathbf{R}\times[1,\infty)
are identified, forming
S^{1}\times\mathbf{R}\times[1,\infty)
. Similarly, rank-2 cusps are formed by taking the quotient of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
by the action of
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
, generated by two independent parabolic isometries that fix the point
q\in S^{2}_{\infty}({\mathbf{H}}^{3})
. For example, with the same notation, if
\psi(x,y,z)=(x,y+1,z)
, then a rank-2 cusp
\mathcal{V}
would be isometric to
\mathcal{H}/\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle
, which corresponds to
[0,1]\times[0,1]\times[1,\infty)
with opposite sides identified.

2.4.
Existence Results

As discussed before, until a decade ago, very little was known about the existence of closed, embedded minimal surfaces in noncompact hyperbolic
3
-manifolds except in cases like geometrically finite manifolds. Finite volume non-compact hyperbolic
3
-manifolds are done with results by the authors mentioned above. By adapting shrinkwrapping techniques from
[
9
]

3
-manifolds.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
19
]
Let
\mathcal{M}
be an infinite volume complete hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Then,
\mathcal{M}
contains a closed, embedded minimal surface
S
if
\mathcal{M}
is not one of the following three categories:

(1)

\mathcal{M}
is a geometrically infinite product manifold.

(2)

\mathcal{M}
is topologically a handlebody.

(3)

\mathcal{M}
contains rank-1 cusps.

Although some nonexistence examples have been given for case (2) involving
\mathbf{H}^{3}
or Fuchsian Schottky manifolds in
[
19
]
, no such results exist for most cases in (1) and (3). In this paper, we address all three cases and present new results.

3.
Dichotomy for Non-Cusped Hyperbolic
3
-manifolds

In this section, we first prove that hyperbolic
3
-manifolds with no cusps are thick at infinity, i.e., it cannot contain a non-compact, finite area, complete, embedded minimal surface. Then, by using Song’s dichotomy result
[
53
]
for manifolds thick at infinity, we show that any such manifold either contains infinitely many, closed, embedded, unstable minimal surfaces or it contains no unstable, embedded, closed minimal surfaces. First, let us recall a few notions.

Definition 3.1

(Manifolds thick at infinity
[
53
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n+1},g)
be a complete
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. It is said to be
thick at infinity
(in the weak sense) if any connected, finite volume, finite index, complete, minimal hypersurface in
(\mathcal{M},g)
is closed.

Remark 3.2
.

Song did not impose the finite index condition in his definition of thick at infinity, but the results of his paper continue to hold with the above definition of thickness at infinity, that applies only to finite index minimal surfaces. It is possible but not entirely straightforward to verify that, for example, closed Riemannian manifolds are thick at infinity in the original definition (without the finite index condition), but we do not give the argument here.

Song proved the following zero-infinity dichotomy for manifolds thick at infinity.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
53
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional complete manifold with
2\leq n\leq 6
, thick at infinity. Then the following dichotomy holds true:

(1)

either
\mathcal{M}
contains infinitely many saddle point minimal hypersurfaces,

(2)

or there is no saddle point minimal hypersurface in
\mathcal{M}
.

Here a 2-sided minimal hypersurface is said to be a
saddle point
if it maximizes area in a local foliation around it, whereas a saddle point 1-sided minimal hypersurface is defined as such if the 2-sided double cover is a saddle point in the corresponding double cover of a small tubular neighborhood of the minimal hypersurface. Note that if the metric is bumpy (i.e. no closed minimal hypersurface has a non-trivial Jacobi field) then saddle point minimal hypersurfaces are exactly
unstable 2-sided closed minimal hypersurfaces
and
1-sided closed minimal hypersurfaces with unstable double cover
. For more details, see
[
53
, Section 3]
.

Next, we show any hyperbolic
3
-manifold (compact or noncompact) with no cusps is thick at infinity.

Lemma 3.4
.

Any complete hyperbolic
3
-manifold without cusps is thick at infinity.

Proof:
Let
\mathcal{M}
be an hyperbolic
3
-manifold with no cusp. We proceed by contradiction. Assume to the contrary that there is a non-compact, complete, finite index, and finite area minimal surface
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{M}
exists, where the area
\|\mathcal{S}\|=C
.

First, we show that
\mathcal{S}
cannot completely stay in a compact region in

, i.e.
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{K}_{0}

\mathcal{K}_{0}

\epsilon_{0}>0
. Let

represent the intrinsic distance in
\mathcal{S}
. Then, as
\mathcal{S}
is a complete, embedded surface, there is a sequence of points
\{p_{i}\}
in
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\epsilon_{0}}(q)
with

for any

. Let
\mathbf{B}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\epsilon_{0}}(p_{i})
represent the intrinsic
\epsilon_{0}
-disk in
\mathcal{S}
with center
p_{i}
. Since
\mathcal{S}
has finite index, we know that for all but finitely many of the
p_{i}

\epsilon_{0}
-disk centered at

is stable. Therefore by the fact that
\mathcal{M}
has bounded geometry and the curvature estimates for stable minimal surfaces (see for instance
[

), for all but finitely many of the

the injectivity radius of
\mathcal{S}
at
p_{i}
is at least some constant

Since
\mathcal{S}
is a minimal surface in a hyperbolic

\mathcal{S}
is bounded from above by
-1
. Then, by a simple estimate
[
9

, we have the area of intrinsic disk
\|\mathbf{B}^{\epsilon}(p_{i})\|\geq 2\pi(\cosh{\epsilon}-1)\geq\pi r^{2}
. As

,
\mathbf{B}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\epsilon_{0}}(p_{i})\cap\mathbf{B}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\epsilon_{0}}(p_{j})=\emptyset
for any

C=\|\mathcal{S}\|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{B}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\epsilon}(p_{i})\|

is a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold.

Now, assume that
\mathcal{S}
is not contained in a compact subset of

. If

\{p_{k}\}
on
\mathcal{S}

inside

p_{k}

\mathcal{M}
intersected with
\mathcal{S}
has area at least some definite
\epsilon^{\prime}>0

\mathcal{S}
has a finite area.

If
\mathcal{M}
has unbounded geometry, let
\epsilon_{3}
be the Margulis constant. Let
\mathcal{M}_{thin}
be the thin part of
\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}_{thin}=\{x\in\mathcal{M}\mid\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(x)\leq\epsilon_{3}\}

\mathcal{M}
at

\mathcal{M}_{thick}=\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{M}_{thin}

is a

union of Margulis tubes
[

23
]

\mathcal{M}_{thin}=\bigsqcup_{i}\mathbf{T}_{\gamma_{i}}

\mathbf{T}_{\gamma}

\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{M}_{thin}
contains a sequence

diverging to infinity, with

\{p_{k}\}\in\mathcal{M}_{thick}

for any

[

]
[Section 1.1] applies as it did two paragraphs above to show that the area of the ball of radius
\epsilon_{3}

has area at least some definite
\epsilon^{\prime}>0

By combining this result with Song’s dichotomy, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a complete hyperbolic
3
-manifold with no cusps. If
\mathcal{M}
contains a closed, embedded, saddle point minimal surface, then it contains infinitely many closed, embedded, saddle point minimal surfaces.

We end this section with two brief remarks:

Remark 3.6

(Stable Minimal Surfaces)
.

We note that the above results and Song’s dichotomy theorem shed no light on the number of stable minimal surfaces in a manifold
\mathcal{M}
. A manifold can have a stable minimal surface without closed saddle point minimal surfaces. For example, let
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{S}\times\mathbf{R}
be a Fuchsian manifold, then
\mathcal{M}
contains a unique minimal surface, which is the totally geodesic surface
\mathcal{S}\times\{0\}
. More generally, every almost Fuchsian manifold
[
59
]
only contains exactly one closed minimal surface, and there are also examples that are not almost Fuchsian
[
28
]
.

Remark 3.7

(Thickness for Cusped Hyperbolic
3
-Manifolds)
.

We note that our thickness at infinity result does not generalize to hyperbolic 3-manifolds with cusps. For this case, a straightforward example is a Fuchsian manifold
\mathcal{F}\times\mathbf{R}
where
\mathcal{F}
is a punctured surface. We provide further examples of cusped, quasi-Fuchsian hyperbolic
3
-manifolds containing finite-area, non-compact, complete, embedded minimal surfaces in Section
6
.

4.

-Manifolds

In this section, we first present our alternative result for doubly degenerate hyperbolic

In particular, we derive a linear growth estimate for the number of disjoint embedded minimal surfaces in quasi-Fuchsian manifolds with short geodesics (

4.1.
Alternative for Doubly Degenerate Manifolds

In this subsection, we say
\mathcal{M}

Before stating our result, we need the following dichotomy result, which is an application of the implicit function theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S
be a closed essential locally area-minimizing surface in a complete hyperbolic
3
-manifold

diffeomorphic to

of bounded geometry. Then either

(1)

The manifold

is foliated by minimal surfaces isotopic to

, or

(2)

There is a foliation

of a tubular neighborhood of
S
,

,
S_{0}=S
, so that the mean curvature vector of

for

Proof.

If

is strictly stable, then the images under the normal exponential map of small multiples of the bottom eigenfunction of the second variation operator of

multiplied by a unit normal vector field form a mean-convex foliation of a tubular neighborhood of

(see e.g. the proof of Theorem 5.2 in
[

If

is degenerate stable, assume first that no neighborhood of

admits a foliation by minimal surfaces. Let

-norm equal to
1
(recall that the space of such Jacobi fields is one-dimensional, and each nonzero Jacobi field is nowhere vanishing. This is a standard fact about bottom eigenfunctions of linear elliptic operators
[
20
]
[Chapter 6].) Let
\nu(x)
be the unit normal vector to the point
x\in S
, determined by fixed choices of orientation for
S
and
M
. For

C^{2,\alpha}
-norm, consider the functional
F(w,t)=H_{w}(x)-t\phi(x)
. Here
H_{w}
is the real valued function on

defined at
x_{0}
by taking the inner product of the mean curvature vector of the graphical surface parametrized by
x\mapsto exp_{x}(w(x)\nu(x))
at
exp_{x_{0}}(w(x_{0})\nu(x_{0}))
, with the unit normal vector to that surface at
exp_{x_{0}}(w(x_{0})\nu(x_{0}))
pointing towards the same end of

as the normal vector field
\nu
to

.

Then

maps a neighborhood of
(0,0)
in

to

, and

is surjective with one-dimensional kernel given by
\phi
. Surjectivity follows from diagonalizing the second variation operator restricted to the orthogonal complement of its one-dimensional kernel, which is spanned by
\phi
. Applying the implicit function theorem gives a 1-parameter family of graphical surfaces
S_{u}
,
S_{0}=S
, so that the mean curvature of each
S_{u}
(viewed as a function on

via pairing the mean curvature vector of each

with the unit normal vector pointing in the same direction as

and then precomposing with the normal exponential map at

of

The function

so that the mean curvature of

is
t(u)\phi
is analytic. Therefore, if

had vanishing mean curvature for a sequence of

would have to vanish identically (Compare
[
3
]
[Section 5].) Therefore for

cannot vanish for a sequence of

small enough and nonzero. Because

is locally area-minimizing,

must be negative for non-zero u sufficiently small, and

is decreasing.

Because

must be tangent to the kernel of

at

, we know that the derivative in

of the function of which

is the graph over

at
u=0
is a nonzero multiple of
\phi
. Since
\phi
is nowhere vanishing, this implies that

is disjoint from

for

sufficiently small.

For a fixed small

,
\epsilon^{\prime}<<\epsilon_{0}

is contained in the region bounded by

and

for

, and so that

is disjoint from

if

.

We claim also that
S_{u_{1}}
is disjoint from

if

and

. If

have different signs then this is clear, so assume that

(the case where both are negative follows from the same argument.) Suppose for contradiction that

is not contained in the region bounded by
S_{u_{1}}
and
S
. Then
S_{u_{1}}
must intersect

because it is disjoint from

. We let

be the supremum of the set of parameters

\epsilon_{0}\geq u>0
, so that

is monotone, the mean curvature of

Therefore if a neighborhood of

with a mean-convex foliation: i.e., for which all surfaces in the foliation not equal to

has a local minimal foliation, then since

has bounded geometry it also has a global minimal foliation

3
]
[Section 5].

Fix a homotopy equivalence

If

for

[

Fix a homotopy equivalence

For each

Our alternative result, when combined with Gromov’s dichotomy

The existence of closed (incompressible) minimal surfaces in any quasi-Fuchsian manifold was established in the 1970s (

First we prove the following conditional result:

Theorem 4.5

Let

be an

We argue by contradiction. Suppose not, and take a sequence of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds

This is because the linearized operators of the sequence are close to the linearized operator in the limit, and it follows from the standard contraction mapping proof of the inverse function theorem that the size of the neighborhood to which the inverse function theorem applies depends on the size of the inverse of its linearization. Let

Let

(resp.

Since

We also have the following unconditional result, which follows from an argument very similar to the proof of the last theorem. One uses the fact that hyperbolic 3-manifolds with short geodesics as in the statement of the next theorem cannot admit minimal foliations by

Suppose

(1)

(2)

4.3.
Quasi-Fuchsian Manifolds with Short Geodesics

In this part, we construct examples of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds satisfying the hypotheses of Corollary

Theorem 4.7
.

There are quasi-Fuchsian manifolds satisfying the conditions of

Proof.

Choose an element of the mapping class group

Corollary 4.8
.

A quasi-Fuchsian manifold (without rank-1 cusps) satisfies one of the following statements:

(1)

Contains a unique closed local area minimizer isotopic to the fiber.

(2)

Contains infinitely many saddle point minimal surfaces.

Proof.

Assume that
M=\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
is a quasi-Fuchsian manifold with more than one closed local area minimizer isotopic to the fiber. Without loss of generality, we can assume these local area minimizers
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
are disjoint. Indeed, if they intersect, for each unbounded component of their complement we can consider its intersection with the convex core and minimize area within this region for surfaces isotopic to the fiber, as each of these regions has boundary that is piecewise mean convex with dihedral angle less than
\pi
. This produces a disjoint pair of local area minimizers isotopic to the fiber, as desired. By Corollary
3.5
it is sufficient to prove that there exists a saddle point minimal surface. Let
\Omega
be the compact region bounded by
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
and take
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{n}=\Sigma^{\prime}
a maximal collection of stable minimal surfaces isotopic to
\Sigma\times\{0\}
in the region
\Omega
, so that each consecutive pair bounds a cylinder disjoint from any of the other surfaces. Without loss of generality assume that all surfaces in this maximal collection are not saddle. Then at least one consecutive pair bounds a cylinder

that satisfies the conditions of
[

, namely that there are no stable minimal surfaces isotopic to the fiber in the interior of

and there is a foliation near
\partial C
with non-zero mean curvature vector pointing towards the boundary. The first condition follows by the maximality of the collection
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{n}=\Sigma^{\prime}
for any consecutive pair. As we have foliations near
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
with non-zero mean curvature vector pointing towards each of them, we must have at least one consecutive pair satisfying the second condition of
[
25
, Proposition 7.6]
. Hence as desired it follows the existence of a saddle point minimal surface in any such cylinder
C
.
∎

5.
Schottky Manifolds

As previously noted, it remains an open question whether a Schottky manifold contains a closed, embedded minimal surface. In this section, we present the first examples of such manifolds.

We emphasize that by a
Schottky manifold
we refer to a handlebody with a convex co-compact (in particular complete) hyperbolic metric.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
C
be a positive integer and
H_{g}
the topological handlebody obtained by attaching
g\geq 2

1
-handles to the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then the set of Schottky manifolds homeomorphic to
H_{g}
that contain at least
C
local minimizers of area isotopic to their boundary is infinite. Moreover, there exists a geometrically infinite complete hyperbolic metric in
H_{g}
with infinitely many local minimizers of area that are isotopic to the boundary.

Proof:

For the construction we follow
[
44
, Corollary 1.4, Chapter 5]
. Namely, for a marked convex co-compact hyperbolic metric on a handlebody
N
with conformal structure at infinity
\tau
consider pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism
\varphi
of
\partial N
with attracting lamination
\lambda
, a filling lamination in the
Masur domain
, or the set of ending laminations with non-zero intersection with the compressible curves of
N
. This can be accomplished following
[
56
, Section 6]
by taking
\alpha,\beta
a filling pair of simple closed curves in
\partial N
so that
\alpha
is disk-busting and taking
\varphi_{n}=T^{n}_{\alpha}\circ T_{\beta}
, where
T_{\alpha,\beta}
are the Dehn twists along the respective curve and
n
is sufficiently large. As
n
grows the attracting measured foliation converges to the geometric intersection with
\alpha
, hence
\varphi^{n}
will be in the Masur domain for large
n
.

Fixing a particular
\varphi=\varphi_{n}
, it follows from
[
44
, Corollary 1.4, Chapter 5]
that the sequence
N_{i}
of marked convex co-compact metrics in
N
with conformal structure at infinity
\tau_{i}=\varphi^{i}(\tau)
converges algebraically to
N_{\infty}
, a geometrically infinite metric in
N
with ending lamination equal to
\lambda
. By Canary’s Covering Theorem (
[
15
]
) the algebraic limit
N_{\infty}
is a finite index subgroup in any geometric limit
N_{\rm geo}
, which implies that
N_{\infty}
is the geometric (and hence strong) limit of the sequence
N_{i}
. Indeed, let
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(N_{\rm geo})
. Since
[\pi_{1}(N_{\rm geo}):\pi_{1}(N_{\infty})]<\infty
then there exists
k
such that
\gamma^{k}\in\pi_{1}(N_{\infty})
. If
\rho_{i}:\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
are the discrete faithful representations of
N_{i}
, then there exist
h,g_{i}\in\pi_{1}(N)
so that
\gamma=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{i}(g_{i})
and
\gamma^{k}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{i}(h)
. Hence
id=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{i}(g_{i}^{k}h^{-1})
, so by the discreteness of
\rho_{i}
it follows that
g_{i}^{k}=h
for
i
sufficiently large. Indeed, if by way of contradiction we have
g_{i}^{k}h^{-1}\neq id
for infinitely many indices, we can take each
A_{k}=g_{i}^{k}h^{-1}
as one of the generators of a non-elementary 2 generator group (by choosing any other group element
B_{k}
with different fixed points at infinity for the other generator), which would violate Jorgensen’s inequality. (Recall that Jorgenson’s inequality states that for
A
and
B
generators of a nonelementary Kleinian group,

|\text{Tr}(A)^{2}-4|+|\text{Tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1}-2|\geq 1,

which cannot hold for a pair of convergent generators
A_{k},B_{k}
so that
A_{k}\rightarrow id
, as the left-hand side would converge to
0
.)

In particular, the sequence
(g_{i})
is constant for
i
sufficiently large, which implies
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(N_{\infty})
.

By the Ending Lamination Theorem (
[
8
,
7
]
), we have that in the end of
N_{\infty}
the translation defined by
\varphi
is a quasi-isometry. Hence
\varphi
gives a
\mathbb{Z}
-action on the limit surface group obtained by exiting the end of
N_{\infty}
. Such surface group will be then the cyclic cover of the hyperbolic mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
induced by
\varphi
. Hence, taking
\varphi
so that
M_{\varphi}
does not foliate by minimal surfaces (taking it for instance so that
M_{\varphi}
is a sufficiently high Dehn-filling of a non-compact, finite volume hyperbolic manifold following
[
26
]
,
[
21
, Theorem 2.5]
, which can be accomplished by taking
n
sufficiently large in
\varphi=\varphi_{n}
) then an area minimizer
\Sigma
among surfaces isotopic to the fiber of
M_{\varphi}
has a strictly mean convex foliation around it by
Lemma

4.1
.
From here the argument follows by
[
21
, Corollary 5.2]
. By geometric convergence, we have infinitely many regions exiting the end of
N_{\infty}
that are diffeomorphic to
\partial N\times[0,1]
with boundary isotopic to
\partial N
and with mean convex boundary. Thus, we can find area minimizers within these regions to produce minimal surfaces in
N_{\infty}
isotopic to
\partial N
.

For any
C>0
we can consider a compact set
K\subseteq N_{\infty}
that contains at least
C
of the regions with mean convex boundary. Applying again geometric convergence, we have that for sufficiently large

the manifold
N_{i}
has at least
C
regions that are diffeomorphic to
\partial N\times[0,1]
with boundary isotopic to
\partial N
and with mean convex boundary. Then by the same argument as before, such

C
local minimizers of area that are isotopic to the boundary.

Hence, by a direct application of Lemma

(
[

Corollary 5.2
.

For any
3
-dimensional handlebody with at least two
1
-handles there exists a complete hyperbolic metric of bounded geometry containing infinitely many closed, embedded, saddle point minimal surfaces. Moreover such hyperbolic manifolds can be taken to be Schottky manifolds.

Proof.

By using for instance the examples from

, take any bounded geometry handlebody with two disjoint local area minimizers isotopic to the boundary. Applying the reasoning of
[

, Proposition 7.6]
as in Corollary

, by the local min-max theorem

∎

6.
Hyperbolic
3
-Manifolds with Rank One Cusps

In
[
19
]
, the existence of closed minimal surfaces in hyperbolic 3-manifolds was established, excluding the case of rank-1 cusps (
Lemma

2.1
). In this section, we study this case.

In the following, relatively incompressible refers to the incompressibility of a map
f:(S,\partial S)\rightarrow(M,P)
where
S
is a surface with boundary and
P
is the union of the annuli corresponding to rank-1 cusps linking geometric ends together in the topological ends of a tame hyperbolic
3
-manifold.

Definition 6.1
.

We say a geometric end
\mathcal{E}
of a tame hyperbolic manifold
\mathcal{M}
is
essential after compressions
if after performing some disk compressions (possibly none) on
\mathcal{E}
we produce at least one connected component that is relatively incompressible but it is not homotopic to any geometric end other than possibly
\mathcal{E}
.

As an example, if
\mathcal{M}
is neither a product nor a compression body then any end is essential after compressions. Moreover, if
\mathcal{M}
is not a product then any relatively incompressible end is essential after compressions. Theorem
1.5
is a consequence of the next theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma
be an infinite volume complete hyperbolic
3
-manifold with rank-1 cusps that is homeomorphic to the interior of a compact manifold. If
\mathcal{M}
contains a geometrically finite geometric end
\mathcal{E}
that is essential after compressions, then
\mathcal{M}
contains a finite area, complete, embedded, stable minimal surface
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 6.3
.

In the case of quasi-Fuchsian hyperbolic manifolds homeomorphic to the product of a cusped surface with
\mathbb{R}
, the paper
[
27
]
proved existence of complete embedded finite area minimal surface. Such manifolds satisfy the assumptions of the first sentence of the theorem but not the second.

Proof:

Let
\Sigma
be the boundary of the convex core of
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathcal{E}
. The main idea is to find an area minimizer starting with
\Sigma
and using
\Sigma
as a barrier, where the topological assumption is to prevent minimizing sequences from escaping to infinity. As the results of
[
38
]
require a closed surface, we will modify and double the metric of
\mathcal{M}
to obtain a sequence of manifolds
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
with area minimizers
\mathcal{S}_{n}
and so produce the desired minimal surface
\mathcal{S}
as a limit of
\mathcal{S}_{n}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.

Let
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be the rank-1 cusp neighborhoods for all of the cusp ends of
\Sigma
. Without loss of generality we take
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
so that
\{\Sigma\cap\mathcal{U}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are all totally geodesic components (see
[
13
, Chapter 4]
). We claim that the pleating locus on
\Sigma
is disjoint from
\cup_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{U}_{i}
. Indeed, as
\Sigma
is a finite area pleated surface we can take cusp neighborhoods so that every
2
dimensional totally geodesic stratum close to the cusp accumulates into it. In the universal cover, each such stratum lifts to a totally geodesic region
R
with totally geodesic boundary so that the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}R
contains a rank-1 parabolic fixed point
p_{0}
of
\Gamma
. As
R
is given by a supporting plane of the convex hull of
\Gamma
for any of its points, it must be invariant by the rank-1 parabolic subgroup of
\Gamma
that fixes
p_{0}
. Moreover any such region
R
must belong to the same invariant totally geodesic plane, from which the claim follows.

For each
\mathcal{U}_{i}
take a parametrization by horocylinders
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
(where every slice
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{t\}
is the quotient of an horoball by the corresponding rank-1 parabolic subgroup of
\Gamma
). Let
\mathcal{U}^{n}_{i}\subset\mathcal{U}_{i}
given by
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times]n,+\infty[
and let
\mathcal{M}_{n}:=\mathcal{M}\setminus\left(\cup_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{U}^{n}_{i}\right)
. Let
g
be the hyperbolic metric in
\mathcal{M}
and consider a conformal change of metric
\tilde{g}_{n}=e^{2u_{n}}g
in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with
u_{n}
satisfying the following properties

(1)

u_{n}
is a smooth function supported in
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times[n-1,n+1]
.

(2)

In
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times[n-1,n]
the level sets of
u_{n}
are given by horocylinders
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{t\}
.

(3)

\partial_{t}u_{n}\leq 1
for
t\in[n-1,n]
, with equality at
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{n\}
.

It is straightforward to verify that such conformal changes exist.

The mean curvature of a surface under a conformal change of metric is given by

H_{\tilde{g}}=e^{-u}\left(H_{g}-D_{n}u\right),

where
n
is the normal of a surface at a given point. Since by construction we have that
\Sigma
is orthogonal to the level sets of
u_{n}
, it follows that
\Sigma
is mean convex with respect to
\tilde{g}_{n}
.

By the third condition on
u_{n}
we have that
\{S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{t\}\}_{t\in[n-1,n]}
is a mean concave foliation with
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{n\}
minimal (i.e., the mean curvature vectors of the
\{S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{t\}\}_{t\in[n-1,n)}
point towards
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{n\}
.) Moreover, as one can check that under our assumptions each horocylinder stays umbilic, it follows that
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{n\}
is in fact totally geodesic in
\tilde{g}_{n}
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
be the double of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
through its totally geodesic boundary in the metric
\tilde{g}_{n}
. Observe that
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
contains two isometric copies of
\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
. Denote by
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
the mean-convex surface in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
given by the double of
\Sigma\cap\mathcal{M}_{n}
. As
\mathcal{E}
is essential after compressions and compressions of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
can be done on each half of the double, after finitely many compressions of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
(possibly none) we have an incompressible closed surface
\Sigma_{n}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
that is not homotopic to any other end of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
other than possibly the end containing
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
. As
\widehat{\Sigma}_{n}
is mean-convex, by
[
38
]
we have an area minimizer
\mathcal{S}_{n}
obtained from the homotopy class of
\Sigma_{n}
. As the complement of the copies of
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
has a mean-convex foliation, it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{n}
must intersect one of these copies of
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
. As
\mathcal{S}_{n}\cap\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
is non-peripheral with respect to all ends except possibly the initial geometrically finite end, it follows that there is a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{M}
that all
\mathcal{S}_{n}
intersect. Hence by making
n\rightarrow+\infty
and taking a subsequence if necessary, we obtain a finite area minimal surface
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{M}
as the limit of
\mathcal{S}_{n}\cap\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
.

7.2.
Open Questions

In this part, we discuss some open questions and potential approaches. For doubly degenerate manifolds with bounded geometry, we proved that either any such manifold contains a closed minimal surface, or if not there must be a doubly degenerate manifold which has a minimal foliation. Notice that this result is closely related with another important question posed by Thurston decades ago, also known as Anderson’s Conjecture (for a negative answer)
[

Question 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a doubly degenerate hyperbolic
3
-manifold with unbounded geometry. Is there a finite area, complete, embedded minimal surface in
\mathcal{M}
?

Our proof of
Theorem

4.3
used the bounded geometry assumption in an essential way. If one wants to adapt the arguments to apply to the unbounded geometry case, allowing arbitrarily short geodesics, a more complicated analysis is needed to find a minimizer for the energy functional. This involves serious difficulties first in the harmonic maps part of the argument, where one would need to consider harmonic maps from possibly cusped surfaces into targets without bounded geometry. There are also complications in applying the theory of convergence for Kleinian groups, which is much more involved if one drops the bounded geometry assumption. Even so, we believe that one could obtain similar results in this setting, and that it is an interesting topic for future work.

Fourier transform for étale motivic cohomology
Iván Rosas-Soto

Abstract
. In the present article, we study the integral aspects of the Fourier transform of an abelian variety A A over a field k k , using étale motivic cohomology, following the ideas and theory given by Moonen, Polishchuk and later by Beckman and de Gaay Fortman. We prove that there exists a PD-structure over the positive degree part of the étale Chow ring CH > 0 ét ​ ( A ) \text{CH}^{\text{\'{e}t}}_{>0}(A) with respect to the Pontryagin product.

1.
Introduction

Consider an abelian variety
A
defined over a field
k
and let
\text{CH}^{*}(X)
and
\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{*}(X)
be the Chow and étale Chow rings, respectively, with respect to the intersection product. In 2004, Ésnault gave a counterexample for the existence of a PD structure (after a question by Kahn) in
\text{CH}^{*}(X)
when
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field, and this negative result also extends to étale motivic cohomology. Although this is the counterexample of Ésnault, there is still hope to get a PD-structure in other generalities, for example when the base field is algebraically closed. Later, in 2010, Moonen and Polishchuk proved the existence of a PD structure in the ideal
\text{CH}_{>0}(X)\subset\text{CH}_{*}(X)
with respect to the Pontryagin product induced in the Chow groups by the multiplication in
A
, and even better, if the basis field is algebraically closed, then the augmented ideal
I=\text{CH}_{>0}(X)\cup\text{CH}_{0}(X)^{\deg=0}\subset\text{CH}_{*}(X)
has a PD-structure.

For an abelian variety
A
over a field
k
and its dual variety
\widehat{A}
, the correspondence defined in
\text{CH}^{*}(A\times\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}
by the Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}_{A}
induces an equivalence of their derived categories, Chow groups with rational coefficients, and cohomology groups, and it is called the Fourier transform of abelian varieties, denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{A}
. The existence of the Fourier transform has deep consequences in the motivic behavior of the abelian variety
A
, for example, Laumon in
[
Lau87
]
used it to simplify Deligne’s proof of Weil’s conjectures, or Shermenev, Deninger and Murre, in
[
Šer71
]
and
[
DM91
]
respectively, to obtain the Chow-Künneth decomposition of the motive associated to an abelian variety
A
. For the case where
k
is a separably closed field, the Fourier transform preserves integral
\ell
-adic classes (or integral classes in Betti cohomology when
k=\mathbb{C}
); the question is whether this is the case for algebraic cycles. We should note that there is a connection between the intersection product of cycles in an abelian variety
A
and the Pontryagin product, which is given by the Fourier transform, by exchanging one for the other as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A}(x*y)=\mathcal{F}_{A}(x)\cdot\mathcal{F}_{A}(y),\quad\mathcal{F}_{A}(x\cdot y)=(-1)^{\text{dim}(A)}\mathcal{F}_{A}(x)*\mathcal{F}_{A}(y)

In
[
MP10
]
, Moonen and Polishchuk studied the integral aspect of the Fourier transform, asking whether it lifts to a morphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A}:\text{CH}^{*}(A)\to\text{CH}^{*}(\widehat{A}).

in a motivic sense, defining the integral Fourier transform up to an integral constant. However, the existence of an integral Fourier transform is false due to the counterexample given by Ésnault in
[
Esn04
]
, if the field is algebraically closed, this question remains open. With the existence of an integral Fourier transform for étale motivic cohomology, we might be able to find explicit projectors for an integral Chow-Künneth decomposition of the étale motive associated to an abelian variety
A
.

In this paper we aim to answer this question, namely whether the existence of a Fourier transform is true, but for étale motivic cohomology over a field
k=\bar{k}
. This allows us to address the construction of projectors (for étale motivic cohomology groups) for the integral decomposition of the étale motive associated to an abelian variety
A
. With this goal in mind, we begin by proving the following result about the PD-structure in the ideal
\text{CH}_{>0}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(M)\subset\text{CH}_{*}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(M)
associated with the étale motivic cohomology groups of a commutative monoid
M
:

Corollary

(Corollary
2.5
)
.

Let
M
be a commutative monoid with identity in the category of quasi-projective
k
-schemes, such that the product morphism
\mu:M\times M\to M
is proper. Let
p_{d}:\text{Sym}^{d}(M)\to M
be the morphism induced by the iterated multiplication map
M^{d}\to M
. Then the maps
\gamma_{d}^{M}:\text{CH}_{>0}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(M)\to\text{CH}_{*}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(M)
defined by
x\mapsto(p_{d})_{*}\gamma_{d}(x)
define a PD-structure on the ideal
\text{CH}_{>0}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(M)\subset\text{CH}_{*}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(M)
.

If we consider an abelian variety
A
over a field
k=\bar{k}
, we have a PD-structure over the augmented ideal
I=\text{CH}_{>0}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\cup\text{CH}_{0}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)^{\deg=0}\subset\text{CH}_{*}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
as one has in the case of Chow groups:

Corollary

(Corollary
2.6
)
.

Let
k=\bar{k}
be a field and let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
, then there exists a canonical PD-structure, with respect to the Pontryagin product, on the augmentation ideal in
\text{CH}_{*}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
, generated by
\text{CH}_{>0}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
together with the
0
-cycles of degree 0.

This is the existence of a PD-structure of
\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
, where
A
is an abelian variety, and with respect to the Pontryagin product, as it was obtained in
[
MP10
]
for the case of classical Chow groups.

Having obtained the analogues of the PD-structure for étale motivic cohomology, we proceed to study the integral properties of the Fourier transform for étale Chow groups. For this we will use the definition in the spirit of the definition given by Beckmann and de Gaay Fortman in
[
BG23
]
, which is a weaker version of the integral Fourier transform (in the motivic sense) given by Moonen and Polishchuk in
[
MP10
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
and let
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})
be a group homomorphism. We call
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
a weak integral étale Fourier transform if the following diagram commutes

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{A}}

We call a weak integral Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
algebraic if it is induced by a cycle
\Gamma\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})
that satisfies
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}=\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})
.

Following this notion of étale Fourier transform for étale motivic cohomology, one gets the following equivalence for the existence of a Fourier transform:

Theorem

(Theorem
2.9
)
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
of dimension
g
. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The 1-cycle
\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{P}_{A})^{2g-1}}{(2g-1)!}\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}
lifts to
\text{CH}^{2g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})
.

(2)

The abelian variety
A
admits an étale motivic weak integral Fourier transform.

(3)

The abelian variety
A\times\widehat{A}
admits an étale motivic weak integral Fourier transform.

Suppose
A
carries a symmetric ample line bundle, which induces a principal polarization
\lambda:A\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{A}
, then the previous statements are equivalent to the following

(4)

The 2-cycle
\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{P}_{A})^{2g-2}}{(2g-2)!}\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}
lifts to
\text{CH}^{2g-2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})
.

(5)

Denoting as
\Theta\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
to the symmetric ample class attached to
\lambda
, then the one cycle
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Theta}=\frac{\Theta^{g-1}}{(g-1)!}\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
lifts to a one cycle in
\text{CH}^{g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
.

(6)

The abelian variety
A
admits a weak integral étale Fourier transform.

(7)

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{A}
satisfies
\mathcal{F}_{A}(\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{tf})\subset\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{tf}
.

(8)

There exists a PD-structure on the ideal
\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{>0}(A)_{tf}\subset\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\text{tf}}
.

With the previous result one gets a characterization for the existence of the Fourier transform over any field. If we assume that
k=\bar{k}
, the result is unconditional, getting the existence of a morphism of étale cycles with integral coefficients:

Theorem

(Corollary
2.16
)
.

Let
k=\bar{k}
and let
A/k
be an abelian variety, then there exists an integral algebraic étale Fourier transform.

Etale Fourier transform

2.1.
PD-structure

For the sake of completeness, we should say a few words about the definition of étale motivic cohomology for non-smooth schemes over a field
k
. Given a Noetherian scheme

, we consider two classes of morphisms over

This forms an enriched category of h-motives in the sense of

2.2.
Etale Fourier transform

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a field
k
. The Fourier transform on the level of Chow groups is the groups homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A}:\text{CH}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\text{CH}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}

induced by the correspondence
\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})\in\text{CH}(A\times\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})
is the Chern character of
\mathcal{P}_{A}
. One has the Fourier transform on the level of étale cohomology:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{A}:H^{\bullet}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\bullet))\to H^{\bullet}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A}_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\bullet))

which preserves integral cohomology classes and induces, for each
i
with
0\leq i\leq 2g
, an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{A}:H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n))\to H^{2g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A}_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n+g-i)),

and if
k=\mathbb{C}
, then
\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})
induces, for each
0\leq i\leq 2g
, an isomorphism of Hodge structures

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{A}:H^{i}(A,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2g-i}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z}(g-i)).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
and let
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})
be a group homomorphism. We call
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
a weak integral étale Fourier transform if the following diagram commutes

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{A}}

We call a weak integral Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
algebraic if it is induced by a cycle
\Gamma\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})
that satisfies
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}=\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})
. A group homomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})
is an integral étale Fourier transform up to homology if the following diagram commutes:

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2g}H^{2i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A}_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(i))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2g}H^{2i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A}_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(i)).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{F}_{A}}

Finally an integral étale Fourier transform up to homology
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
is called algebraic if it is induced by a cycle
\Gamma\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})
such that
\text{cl}(\Gamma)=\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{4g}H^{2i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}((A\times\widehat{A})_{k^{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(i))
. Similarly, a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module homomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}
is called an
\ell
-adic integral Fourier transform up to homology if
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}
is compatible with
\mathfrak{F}_{A}
and the
\ell
-adic cycle class map. If such homomorphism exists and is induced by a cycle
\Gamma_{\ell}\in\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}
and
\text{cl}(\Gamma_{\ell})=\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}_{A})
is called an algebraic
\ell
-adic integral étale Fourier transform.

If
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})
is a weak integral étale Fourier transform, then
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
is an integral étale Fourier transform up to homology. If
k=\mathbb{C}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})
is an integral étale Fourier transform up to homology if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
is compatible with the Fourier transform
\mathfrak{F}_{A}:H_{B}^{\bullet}(A,\mathbb{Z})\to H_{B}^{\bullet}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z})
on Betti cohomology.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A
be a complex abelian variety and let
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})
be an integral étale Fourier transform up to homology.

(1)

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
the integral étale Hodge conjecture for degree
2i
classes on
A
implies the integral étale Hodge conjecture for degree
2(g-i)
classes on
\widehat{A}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
is algebraic, then
\mathfrak{F}_{A}
induces a group isomorphism
Z_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2i}(A)\to Z_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2(g-i)}(\widehat{A})
, where
Z_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2i}(A)
is the image of the Lichtenbaum cycle class map.

Proof.

Consider the following diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{i}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2i}_{B}(A,\mathbb{Z})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{\bullet}_{B}(A,\mathbb{Z})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{\bullet}_{B}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2(g-i)}_{B}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}

The composition of the bottom line
H^{2i}_{B}(A,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2(g-i)_{B}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism of Hodge structures, then we obtain a commutative diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Hdg}^{2i}(A,\mathbb{Z})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Hdg}^{2(g-i)}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Thus the surjectivity of

implies the surjectivity of
c^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
. Arguing in the same way for
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{-2.0pt}{6.57643pt}}\smash{\widehat{A}}}
we obtain the desired equivalence.
∎

-adic integral Fourier transform up to homology.

Proof.

One way is clear. For the other one, we will split the proof into several parts: first, we prove that the Fourier transform preserves torsion classes. Note that since
H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))
is a torsion-free
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
module, then we have a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))\to H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(j))\to H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))\to 0,

assuming that
i\neq 2j+1
by
[
RS16
, Proposition 5.1]
then we have an isomorphism
H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))\simeq H^{i+1}_{M,\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}(j))\{\ell\}
, the same holds for
\widehat{A}
. With this we have obtain a commutative diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(j))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(a))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(a))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(a))}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{F}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{F}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{F}^{q,\ell}_{A}}

where
a=j+g-i
and
\mathfrak{F}^{q,\ell}_{A}
is the induced map by the quotient, so we have an morphism of torsion groups, thus we have that

\mathfrak{F}^{q,\ell}_{A}:\text{CH}^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\{\ell\}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\text{CH}^{g-i+1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})\{\ell\}
.
Assuming that for each prime number
\ell\neq\text{char}(k)
, then we have a commutative diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{i}(A)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{\bullet}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{\bullet}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2(g-i)}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}}

swapping the Fourier transform
\widehat{A}
with the double dual of
A
, we can conclude that
c^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}(\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{i}(A)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}})\simeq c^{g-i}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}(\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{g-i}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}})
. Since the kernel of the cycle class map
c^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}
is
\ell
-divisible, then
\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{i}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\simeq I^{2i}_{\text{\'{e}t},\ell}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
.

Consider the short exact sequence
0\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to 0
, then by the previous remark, we obtain a quotient map
F^{\ell}_{A}:\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\to\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}
which is an isomorphism. First we assume that
\text{char}(k)=0
, which gives us a commutative diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\text{tors}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\text{tors}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A})_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{F}_{A}^{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{F}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{A}}

where
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{A}^{q}=\bigoplus_{\ell\neq\text{char}(k)}\mathfrak{F}_{A}^{q,\ell}
and
\displaystyle F_{A}=\bigoplus_{\ell\neq\text{char}(k)}F_{A}^{\ell}
. In particular we found
\mathfrak{F}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathcal{F}_{A}
, so it preserves integral étale cycles. If we work over positive characteristic
p
, then we take
\ell\neq p
and use the fact that
\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)\simeq\text{CH}_{L}(A)[1/p]
where
\text{CH}_{L}(A)
are the Lichtenbaum cohomology groups of
A
.
∎

Abstract
. We prove a criterion for the normality of Schubert varieties in affine Grassmannians for many reductive groups (including all adjoint groups in characteristic at least 5 as well as all tamely ramified groups) in terms of the order of the algebraic fundamental group of a certain Levi subgroup, in particular in small positive characteristic. As an application, we obtain a similar normality criterion for local models in both equal and mixed characteristic. In particular, we give a classification of normal Pappas-Zhu local models at absolutely special level as well as for groups of semisimple rank 1.



1.
Introduction

Schubert varieties in (partial) affine flag varieties play an important role in both geometric representation theory and arithmetic geometry via their close relation to local models of Shimura varieties (in mixed characteristic) or moduli spaces of shtukas (in positive characteristic).
One of the fundamental questions is the normality of Schubert varieties for a reductive group
G
over a field of Laurent series
k(\hskip-1.99168pt({t})\hskip-1.99168pt)
and local models for reductive groups
{\mathbb{G}}
over non-archimedean local fields
{\mathbb{F}}
(either in mixed or positive characteristic).

When the characteristic of the ground field is zero or big enough in the sense that it does not divide the order of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G^{\rm der})
Faltings
[
Fal03
]
, Pappas–Rapoport
[
PR08
]
and Lourenço
[
Lou
]
prove the normality of Schubert varieties; and similarly Zhu
[
Zhu14
]
, Pappas-Zhu
[
PZ13
]
, Levin
[
Lev16
]
, Richarz
[
Ric16
]
, Haines-Richarz
[
HR23
]
and Lourenco
[
Lou23
]
construct and prove normality of local models whenever the residue characteristic of
{\mathbb{F}}
does not divide the order of
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{G}}^{{\rm der}})
.

In small positive characteristic, when
{\rm char}(k)\mid\#\pi_{1}(G^{\rm der})
, Haines–Lourenço–Richarz
[
HLR24
]
produce examples of non-normal Schubert varieties and show that in the absolutley almost simple case almost almost all Schubert varieties in the affine flag variety are non-normal. As a consequence also the local models are non-normal in this case.
Subsequently, the author together with Richarz
[
BR
]
classify all (non-)normal Schubert varieties in affine Grassmannians for split semisimple and almost simple groups.

The main goal of the present manuscript is to give a criterion for the normality of Schubert varieties in the affine Grassmannian
\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits_{G}
for many reductive groups
G
, including all groups that split over a tamely ramified extension as well as all adjoint groups in characteristic
{\rm char}(k)\geq 5

We use the criterion to extend the classification result of
[
BR
]
to tamely ramified and not necessarily semisimple groups.
As an application we give an analogous criterion for the normality of local models and classify the normal local models at absolutely special level as well as for groups of semisimple rank 1.

2.4.
Echellonage root system

Recall that
[
BT72
]
introduce a reduced root system
\Sigma
inside
(X^{*}(T)^{I})_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong(X_{*}(T)_{I})_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, called the
échellonage root system
, such that its affine Weyl group
W_{\rm aff}(\Sigma)=W_{\rm aff}=W(G^{\rm sc},S^{\rm sc})
.
We denote its roots by
\Phi_{\Sigma}
, and its coroots by
\Phi_{\Sigma}^{\vee}
.
A set of simple échellonage coroots is given by the image of the simple absolute coroots under the projection
{\rm res}_{I}\colon X_{*}(T)\to X_{*}(T)_{I}
, while the échellonage roots can be constructed via a modified norm map, compare
[
Hai18
, Section 3, Proposition 6.1]
.
In particular, our choice of basis
\Delta_{\rm abs}
for the absolute root system yields a natural basis
\Delta_{\Sigma}
for the échellonage root system. Moreover,
\Delta_{\Sigma}
is in natural bijection with the set of simple relative roots
\Delta
, such that the simple échellonage and relative roots differ at most by a factor of
2^{\pm 1}
.

As
X_{*}(T^{\rm sc})_{I}
and
X_{*}(T^{\rm ad})_{I}
are torsion free, they embed into
X_{*}(T)_{I,{\mathbb{R}}}
and we have natural inclusions

(2.24)

Q^{\vee}\cong X_{*}(T^{\rm{sc}})_{I}\subseteq X_{*}(T^{\rm{ad}})_{I}\subseteq P^{\vee},

of lattices inside
X_{*}(T)_{I,{\mathbb{R}}}
,
where
Q^{\vee}
and
P^{\vee}
are the coroot and coweight lattice of the root system
\Sigma
, compare
[
HR08
, Lemma 15]
.

By
[
Ric13
, Corollary 1.8]
, the Bruhat order restricted to
X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
can be more explicitly described using that
\bar{\lambda}\leq\bar{\mu}
if and only if
\bar{\mu}-\bar{\lambda}
is a linear combination of simple échellonage coroots with non-negative integral coefficients.
An element
\bar{\mu}\in X_{*}(T)_{I}^{+}
is called
minuscule
, if
\langle\bar{\mu},\alpha\rangle\in\{0,\pm 1\}
for all échellonage roots
\alpha\in\Phi_{\Sigma}
.
The minuscule elements in
X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
are precisely the minimal elements with respect to the Bruhat order on
X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
.
Note that in particular all torsion elements of
X_{*}(T)_{I}
are minuscule under this convention.

Lemma 2.4
.

The norm map
{\rm N}_{I}\colon X_{*}(T)_{I}\to X_{*}(T)^{I}
restricts to a monotone map
X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}\to X_{*}(T)^{+,I}
.

Proof.

For the first claim let
\bar{\mu}\in X_{*}(T)_{I}
and let
\alpha\in X^{*}(T)
be a positive root.
Then by construction

\langle\alpha,\mu^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0
if and only if
\langle N^{\prime}_{I}\alpha,\bar{\mu}\rangle\geq 0
.
Hence,
\bar{\mu}
is dominant if and only if
\mu^{\prime}
is dominant.
For the second statement we note that
\bar{\lambda}\leq\bar{\mu}
if and only if
\bar{\mu}-\bar{\lambda}
is the sum of simple échellonage coroots, but the norm of a simple échellonage coroot is a sum of simple absolute coroots.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

The map
X_{*}(T)_{I}\to X_{*}(T^{\rm{ad}})_{I}
is injective when restricted to any connected component for the Bruhat-order on
X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
with image a connected component in the Bruhat order on the dominant coweights
(P^{\vee})^{+}
in the échellonage root system.

Proof.

By definition, the difference of elements in a connected component is a linear combination of échellonage coroots and hence the map is injective.
Moreover, a minuscule
\bar{\mu}\in X_{*}(T)_{I}^{+}
maps to a minuscule dominant coweight in
P^{\vee}
by definition.
∎

A dominant cocharacter
\bar{\mu}\in X_{*}(T^{\rm sc})^{+}_{I}
can be written as a linear combination
\bar{\mu}=\sum_{\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta^{\vee}_{\Sigma}}c_{\alpha^{\vee}}\alpha^{\vee}
with non-negative integer coefficients.
The set of simple coroots
\alpha^{\vee}
that appear with non-zero coefficients in the sum is called the support
{\rm supp}(\alpha^{\vee})\subseteq\Delta^{\vee}_{\Sigma}
.
For a general
\bar{\mu}\in X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
there is a unique minuscule
\bar{\lambda}\in X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
such that
\bar{\lambda}\leq\bar{\mu}
.
In particular,
\bar{\mu}-\bar{\lambda}\in X_{*}(T^{\rm sc})^{+}_{I}
.
By a slight abuse of notation we set
{\rm supp}(\bar{\mu})={\rm supp}(\bar{\mu}-\bar{\lambda})
.

By the classification of standard parabolic subgroups we get for any subset of the échellonage root basis
\Psi\subseteq\Delta_{\Sigma}
a standard Levi subgroup
M_{\Psi}
of
G
with simple (relative) roots precisely given by
\Psi
.
In particular, we get a Levi subgroup
M_{\bar{\mu}}
from
{\rm supp}(\bar{\mu})\subseteq\Delta_{\Sigma}
.
Its set of absolute roots
\Phi_{\rm{abs}}(M_{\bar{\mu}},T)
is then given by the preimage of
\Phi(M_{\bar{\mu}},S)
under the restriction map
\Phi_{\rm abs}(G,T)\to\Phi(G,S)
.
Equivalently, a basis of simple absolute coroots is given by the set of
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta^{\vee}_{\rm abs}
such that its image
\bar{\alpha}^{\vee}\in\Delta^{\vee}_{\Sigma}
appears in the expansion of
\bar{\mu}-\bar{\lambda}
with positive coefficient.

In general, for
\mu\in X_{*}(T)^{+}
the support of its projection
\bar{\mu}\in X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
can be bigger than (the image of) the support of
\mu
, for example for ramified odd unitary groups the image of the non-zero minuscule coweights is the quasi-minuscule cocharacter.
Nevertheless, we still get an injection on fundamental groups.

Lemma 2.6
.

As subsets of the set of
(G,T)
-absolute roots
\Phi(M_{\mu},T)\subseteq\Phi(M_{\bar{\mu}},T)
,
and the inclusion induces an injective group homomorphism

\pi_{1}(M_{\mu}^{{\rm der}})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(M_{\bar{\mu}}^{\rm der})

on fundamental groups.
In particular, if
p\mid\pi_{1}(M_{\mu}^{\rm der})
, then
p\mid\pi_{1}(M_{\bar{\mu}}^{\rm der})
for a prime number
p
.

Proof.

By construction, for
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)^{+}
the unique minuscule cocharacter such that
\lambda\leq\mu
with
\mu-\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}c_{\alpha}\alpha^{\vee}
we have for their projections
\bar{\lambda}\leq\bar{\mu}
, as
\bar{\mu}-\bar{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\rm supp}(\mu)}c_{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}^{\vee}
.
Hence,

{\rm supp}(\bar{\mu})=\operatorname{res}_{I}({\rm supp}(\mu))\cup{\rm supp}(\bar{\lambda}).

We get an injective group homomorphism using
[

by noting that

3.2.
Basic geometric properties of Schubert varieties

The Schubert variety

-variety.
The underlying set of

is the disjoint union of

-orbits for all

The following theorem shows that most Schubert varieties are normal:

Theorem 3.1

(
[

)

Remark 8.6

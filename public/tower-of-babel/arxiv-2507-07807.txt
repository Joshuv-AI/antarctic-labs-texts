On the squares functor and the Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum conjectures
Félix Loubaton and Jaco Ruit

Abstract
. In the seminal work of Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum on derived algebraic geometry, eight conjectures regarding the theory of ( ∞ , 2 ) (\infty,2) -categories are stated. This paper aims to clarify the status of these claims, and to provide a proof for the last remaining open one. Along the way, we demonstrate the universal property of the so-called squares functor, a construction that plays an important role in the ( ∞ , 2 ) (\infty,2) -categorical foundations of Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum.



1.
Introduction

In the appendix of the celebrated work of Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum on derived algebraic geometry
[
GR17
]
, the necessary foundations of
(\infty,2)
-categories are developed. These specifically concern the Gray tensor product, a fundamental operation in the theory of
(\infty,2)
-categories, and the squares functor that provides a non-trivial way to create a double
\infty
-category from a
(\infty,2)
-category by using the Gray tensor product. There are eight statements in the
(\infty,2)
-categorical foundations of
[
GR17
]
that are conjectured by Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum. Since then, mathematicians have made efforts to provide proofs of these conjectures. The goal of this paper is to clarify the status of the conjectures, as well as to provide a demonstration of the last unproven conjecture, so that we may conclude that all conjectures are now resolved. We will also provide a complete picture of the squares construction by demonstrating its universal property, generalizing a similar result for strict double categories by Grandis and Paré
[
GP04
]
.

We will continue this introduction by providing an informal overview of the results that we will cover in this paper. The precise statements and definitions can be found in the text.

1.2.
The Gray tensor product for
(\infty,2)
-categories.

The Gray tensor product is a fundamental operation allowing to define, control, and study
lax

phenomena occurring naturally in the study of
(\infty,2)
-categories. Informally, given a concept in category theory (e.g. natural transformations or colimits), the lax
variant is obtained by replacing all commutative diagrams appearing in the definition of this concept by
diagrams that commute up to cells that are a priori
not
invertible. In this sense, the Gray tensor product may be viewed as
the lax variant of the cartesian product. It is usually denoted by the symbol
\otimes

For example, the cartesian product
[1]\times[1]
corresponds to the free-living commutative square

1.3.
The squares functor.

The
squares construction
was originally introduced by Ehresmann
[
Ehr63
]
for strict double categories. Its
\infty
-categorical incarnation plays an important role in the
(\infty,2)
-categorical set-up of Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum, where it was defined in
[
GR17
, Subsection 10.4.1]

\mathscr{C}
is an
(\infty,2)
-category, then the double
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Sq}(\mathscr{C})
of squares in
\mathscr{C}
is loosely described as follows:

•

its objects are those of
\mathscr{C}
,

•

its horizontal and vertical arrows are given by the arrows of
\mathscr{C}
,

•

its 2-cells correspond to lax commutative squares in
\mathscr{C}

.

be an

\mathscr{C}_{h}\to\mathscr{Q}

\mathscr{C}

Let

\mathrm{Map}(\mathscr{C}_{h}\times\mathscr{D}_{v},\mathrm{Sq}(\mathscr{E}))\simeq\mathrm{Map}(\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{D},\mathscr{E}).

\mathscr{C}

Conventions

We will make use of the following notation and terminology throughout the article:

•

We will write
\infty\mathrm{Cat}
and
\mathscr{S}
for the
\infty
-categories of
\infty
-categories and spaces (or
\infty
-groupoids).

•

The functor
\tau_{0}:\infty\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathscr{S}
denotes the functor that carries an
\infty
-category to its underlying
\infty
-groupoid, i.e. the right adjoint to the inclusion
\mathscr{S}\subset\infty\mathrm{Cat}
. We will write
\tau_{0}^{i}:\infty\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathscr{S}
for the left adjoint to this inclusion, called the
groupoidification functor
.

•

If
\mathscr{C}
is an
\infty
-category, we will write
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathscr{C}):=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathscr{S})
for the
\infty
-category of presheaves on
\mathscr{C}
, and
\mathrm{PSh}_{\mathrm{Set}}(\mathscr{C}):=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Set})\subset\mathrm{PSh}(\mathscr{C})
for the full subcategory of set-valued presheaves on
\mathscr{C}
.

•

Throughout the text, we will use the
oplax
variant of the Gray tensor product for 2- and
(\infty,2)
-categories. We recall the definitions in
2
.

•

We will say that a a subcategory
\mathscr{C}
of an
\infty
-category
\mathscr{D}
is
dense
when the induced nerve functor
\mathscr{D}\to\mathrm{PSh}(\mathscr{C})
, obtained by restricting the Yoneda embedding on
\mathscr{D}
, is fully faithful. In all the situations that we will encounter,
\mathscr{C}
is a small and
\mathscr{D}
is cocomplete. In such case, this implies that
\mathscr{D}
is a reflective subcategory of
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathscr{C})
, and that any object
x\in\mathscr{D}
can be canonically expressed as the colimit of the functor
\mathscr{C}_{/x}\to\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
.

2.
Two-dimensional
\infty
-categories

We will commence by briefly collecting the basic definitions and aspects of
(\infty,2)
-categories and double
\infty
-categories.

2.1.
Shapes for
(\infty,2)
-categories

We recall that, on account of
[
JT07
]
, the full inclusion of the subcategory
\Delta\to\infty\mathrm{Cat}
is dense. The essential image of the induced inclusion
\infty\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta)
selects the
Segal spaces
that are
complete
in the sense of Rezk
[
Rez01
]
. We collect these two notions here:

Definition 2.1
.

A presheaf
\mathscr{C}:\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathscr{S}
is called a Segal space if for every
n\geq 0
, the canonical map

\mathscr{C}([n])\to\mathscr{C}([1])\times_{\mathscr{C}([0]}\dotsb\times_{\mathscr{C}([0])}\mathscr{C}([1])

is an equivalence. Let
J
be the simplicial set defined by the pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1]\sqcup[1]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[3]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[0]\sqcup[0]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\{0\leq 2\},(\{1\leq 3\})}

in
\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta)
.
The Segal space
\mathscr{C}
is said to be
complete
if it is local with respect to the map
J\to[0]
, i.e.
\mathscr{C}([0])=\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta)}([0],\mathscr{C})\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta)}(J,\mathscr{C})
is an equivalence.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Segal space.
The inclusion
[1]\xrightarrow{\{0\leq 1\}}[3]\to J
induces a monomorphism
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta)}(J,\mathscr{C})\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta)}([1],\mathscr{C})=\mathscr{C}([1])
that selects those arrows that are equivalences. This is the content of
[
Rez01
, Theorem 6.2]
.

We may similarly define
(\infty,2)
-categories as presheaves on a suitable collection of 2-categorical shapes.

Definition 2.3
.

A (strict) 2-category is called
gaunt
if the only invertible 1- and 2-cells are identities. We will write
2\mathrm{Gaunt}
for the full subcategory of the category of strict 2-categories spanned by the gaunt 2-categories.

Important examples of gaunt 2-categories are Joyal’s
globular sums
:

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose that
n
is a non-negative integer. Consider a tuple
\overline{m}=(m_{0},...,m_{n-1})
of non-negative integers. Then we will write

[n;\overline{m}]
for the gaunt
2
-category whose objects are given by
0,1,...,n
and such that for any
0\leq i,j\leq n
,

[n;\overline{m}](i,j):=\begin{cases}[m_{i}]\times\dotsb\times[m_{j-1}]&\text{if $i\leq j$,}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will write
\Theta_{2}
for the full subcategory of
2\mathrm{Gaunt}
whose objects are of the shape
[n;\overline{m}]
for some
n
and tuple
m
.
The objects in
\Theta_{2}
are referred to as
globular sums
. Note that we may view
\Delta
as a full subcategory of
\Theta_{2}
via the functor

\Delta\to\Theta_{2}
that carries
[n]
to the globular sum
[n;(0,...,0)]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A presheaf
\mathscr{C}:\Theta_{2}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathscr{S}
is called an
(\infty,2)
-category
if:

•

for any tuple
\overline{m}=(m_{0},...,m_{n-1})
, the canonical map

\mathscr{C}([n;\overline{m}])\to\mathscr{C}([1;m_{0}])\times_{\mathscr{C}([0])}...\times_{\mathscr{C}([0])}\mathscr{C}([1;m_{n-1}])

is an equivalence,

•

for any
n\geq 0
, the canonical map

\mathscr{C}([1;n])\to\mathscr{C}([1;1])\times_{\mathscr{C}([1])}...\times_{\mathscr{C}([1])}\mathscr{C}([1;1])

is an equivalence,

•

\mathscr{C}
is local with respect to the maps
J\to[0]
and
[1;J]\to[1]
, where
[1;J]
is defined by the pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1;1]\sqcup[1;1]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1;3]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1]\sqcup[1]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1;J]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[1;(\{0\leq 2\},(\{1\leq 3\})]}

in
\mathrm{PSh}(\Theta_{2})
.

We will write
(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}\subset\mathrm{PSh}(\Theta_{2})
for the full subcategory spanned by the
(\infty,2)
-categories.

Construction 2.6
.

The involutions
\mathrm{op}:2\mathrm{Gaunt}\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}
and
\textup{{2-op}}:2\mathrm{Gaunt}\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}
given by reversing the directions of 1- and 2-cells respectively, both restrict to involutions
\mathrm{op},\textup{{2-op}}:\Theta_{2}\to\Theta_{2}
of
\Theta_{2}
. In turn, these induce functors
\mathrm{op}^{*},\textup{{2-op}}^{*}:\mathrm{PSh}(\Theta_{2})\to\mathrm{PSh}(\Theta_{2})
that restrict to involutions

(-)^{\mathrm{op}},(-)^{\textup{{2-op}}}:(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}\to(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat},

respectively.

Notation 2.7
.

We follow the notation in
[
Lou25
, Definition 1.1.5]
. There is a canonical inclusion

\iota:\infty\mathrm{Cat}\rightarrow(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}
that is obtained by left Kan extending the functor
\Delta\rightarrow(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}
along the inclusion
\Delta\to\infty\mathrm{Cat}
. We will leave the notation of
\iota
implicit throughout this paper. The functor
\iota
admits a right adjoint

\tau_{1}:(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}\to\infty\mathrm{Cat},

called the
1
-truncation
or
1
-core
that forgets the
2
-cells. Moreover,
\iota
admits a left adjoint

\tau^{i}_{1}:(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}\to\infty\mathrm{Cat}

that is called the
intelligent
1
-truncation
, and inverts the
2
-cells.
We will simply write
\tau_{0}
and
\tau^{i}_{0}
for the functors
(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathscr{S}
given by the composites
\tau_{0}\tau_{1}
and
\tau^{i}_{0}\tau^{i}_{1}
respectively.

Construction 2.8
.

The canonical inclusion

\Theta_{2}\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}
gives rise to an adjunction

{\mathrm{Str}:(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}}\rightleftarrows{2\mathrm{Gaunt}:N}.

The resulting functor
N
is fully faithful, and its essential image is spanned by the
discrete

(\infty,2)
-categories, i.e. those
(\infty,2)
-categories
\mathscr{C}
for which
\mathscr{C}([n;\overline{m}])
is a set for all
[n;\overline{m}]\in\Theta_{2}
. We will leave the notation of
N
implicit in what follows.

Construction 2.9
.

For the sake of simplicity, we will write
[n;m]
for the globular sum
[n;(m,...,m)]
. We will write
[\Delta;\Delta]
for the 1-category that fits in the pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\{[0]\}\times\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Delta\times\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\{[0]\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[\Delta;\Delta]}}

of strict categories, which is in fact also a pushout square of
\infty
-categories.
One readily verifies that the bicosimplicial object
\Delta^{\times 2}\rightarrow 2\mathrm{Gaunt}:([n],[m])\mapsto[n;m]
factors to a functor
[\Delta;\Delta]\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}
.
By
[
Lou25
, Remark 1.1.10]
or
[
Rui25a
, Remark 3.10]
, the (non-full) subcategories

[\Delta;\Delta]\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\Delta;\Delta]\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}\to(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}

are dense.

2.2.
The Gray tensor product for gaunt 2-categories

In this subsection, we gather the basics of the oplax Gray tensor product for gaunt
2
-categories.

Notation 2.10
.

We will write

(-)\otimes_{G}(-):2\mathrm{Gaunt}\times 2\mathrm{Gaunt}\to 2\mathrm{Gaunt}

for the oplax Gray tensor product of gaunt 2-categories. This operation was originally defined by Gray in
[
Gra06
]
.

Remark 2.11
.

Given two gaunt 2-categories
A
and
B
,
A\otimes_{G}B
is generated by
k
-cells of shape
a\otimes_{G}b
where
a
is a
(k-i)
-cell of
A
and
b
is an
i
-cell of
B
. The composition of these cells is subject to several coherence equations, as described, for example, in
[
Mae21
, Section 3.1]
.

Remark 2.12
.

A functor
\phi:A\otimes_{G}B\to E
between gaunt 2-categories factors through
A\otimes_{G}\tau^{i}_{1}B
if and only if for any
0
-cell
a
in
A
and any
2
-cell
b
in
B
,
\phi(a\otimes_{G}b)
is an identity 2-cell.

Proposition 2.13
.

The functor
\otimes_{G}
, viewed as an object of
\mathrm{Fun}(2\mathrm{Gaunt}\times 2\mathrm{Gaunt},2\mathrm{Gaunt})
, admits no non-trivial endomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\otimes_{G}\to\otimes_{G}
be an endomorphism. We will show that for any pair of gaunt
2
-categories
A
and
B
, the component
\phi_{A,B}:A\otimes_{G}B\to A\otimes_{G}B
is given by the identity.

The functor
\phi_{A,B}
is the identity on objects.
Note that
[0]\otimes_{G}[0]=[0]
, so that
\phi_{[0],[0]}=\mathrm{id}_{[0]}
. Suppose that we have objects
a\in A
,
b\in B
, then commutativity of the naturality square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[0]\otimes_{G}[0]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A\otimes_{G}B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[0]\otimes_{G}[0]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A\otimes_{G}B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a\otimes_{G}b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{A,B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a\otimes_{G}b}

implies that
\phi(a\otimes_{G}b)=a\otimes_{G}b
.

The functor
\phi_{A,B}
is the identity on
1
-cells.
Note that
[1]\otimes_{G}[0]=[1]
. Hence
\phi_{[1],[0]}
is an endofunctor of
[1]
that acts as the identity on objects on account of the previous step. Since
[1]
is a poset, this implies that
\phi_{[1],[0]}
is the identity. Similarly, one deduces that
\phi_{[0],[1]}
is the identity.
Suppose now that we are given a
1
-cell
f:a\to a^{\prime}\in A
, and an object
b\in B
.
The commutativity of the naturality square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1]\otimes_{G}[0]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A\otimes_{G}B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[1]\otimes_{G}[0]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A\otimes_{G}B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes_{G}b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{A,B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes_{G}b}

implies that
\phi(f\otimes_{G}b)=f\otimes_{G}b
. We can show similarly that
\phi(a\otimes_{G}g)=a\otimes_{G}g
for any object
a\in A
and any
1
-cell
g:b\to b^{\prime}\in B
.
As every
1
-cell of
A\otimes_{G}B
can be expressed as a composite of
1
-cells of the shape
f\otimes_{G}b
or
a\otimes_{G}g
, this implies that
\tau_{1}\phi_{A,B}:\tau_{1}(A\otimes_{G}B)\to\tau_{1}(A\otimes_{G}B)
is the identity.

The functor
\phi_{A,B}
is the identity on
2
-cells.
We now remark that the gaunt 2-categories
[1]\otimes_{G}[1],[1;1]\otimes_{G}[0]
and

.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a double
\infty
-category. Then restriction along the unit
\mathscr{P}\to\textup{{\v{c}}}|\mathscr{P}|
gives a monomorphism

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Dbl}\infty\mathrm{Cat}}(\textup{{\v{c}}}|\mathscr{P}|,\mathscr{Q})\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Dbl}\infty\mathrm{Cat}}(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{Q})

for every double
\infty

\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{Q}
so that every vertical arrow of
\mathscr{P}
is carried to a vertical arrow in
\mathscr{Q}

Corollary 3.11

Let

(\infty,2)

\mathscr{Q}

\infty

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Dbl}\infty\mathrm{Cat}}(\mathrm{Sq}(\mathscr{C}),\mathscr{Q})\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Dbl}\infty\mathrm{Cat}}(\mathscr{C}_{v},\mathscr{Q})

Construction 3.12
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{P}
is a double
\infty
-category. Then for non-negative integers
n
and
m
, we may consider subspaces

(\tau_{\mathrm{vcomp}}\mathscr{P})_{n,m},(\tau_{\mathrm{hcomp}})_{n,m}\subset\mathscr{P}_{n,m}

which are defined so that for every
\sigma:\langle n,m\rangle\to\mathscr{P}
, the following holds:

(1)

\sigma
belongs to
(\tau_{\mathrm{vcomp}}\mathscr{P})_{n,m}
if and only if for every map
f:[1]_{v}\to\langle n,m\rangle
, the restriction
\sigma|f
selects a vertical arrow that admits a companion,

(2)

\sigma
belongs to
(\tau_{\mathrm{hcomp}}\mathscr{P})_{n,m}
if and only if for every map
f:[1]_{h}\to\langle n,m\rangle
, the restriction
\sigma|f
selects a horizontal arrow that is a companion.

These subspaces assemble to bisimplicial subspaces
\tau_{\mathrm{vcomp}}\mathscr{P}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{hcomp}}\mathscr{P}
of
\mathscr{P}
. With these definitions, note that for any bisimplicial space
X
, the maps

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta\times\Delta)}(X,\tau_{\mathrm{vcomp}}\mathscr{P}),\penalty\ \mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta\times\Delta)}(X,\tau_{\mathrm{hcomp}}\mathscr{P})\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{PSh}(\Delta\times\Delta)}(X,\mathscr{P})

are monomorphisms whose image correspond to the maps
\sigma:X\to\mathscr{P}
that carry every vertical arrow (resp. horizontal arrow) of
X
to a vertical arrow (resp. horizontal arrow) of
\mathscr{P}
that admits (resp. is given by) a companion.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose that

4.

As announced in the introduction, we will now settle the following description of the Gray tensor product that was conjectured by Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum in
[

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
be
(\infty,2)
-categories. Then there exists a unique natural equivalence

\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{C}_{h}\times\mathscr{D}_{v})\simeq\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{D}.

We note that the unicity will be automatic on account of

To prove the above result, we will employ several density arguments to reduce to easier shapes. As an easy case, we have the following intermediate result:

Lemma 4.2

The desired natural equivalence of

and
\mathscr{D}

\infty
-categories.

Since

∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tau_{0}[n]\times[m]\times[k]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[n]\otimes([m]\times[k])}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tau_{0}[n]\otimes[k]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[n;m]\otimes[k]},}

and the bottom square is uniquely determined since the top left and top right maps are epimorphisms.

Lemma 4.6
.

The squares constructed in

are pushout squares.

Proof.

This follows directly from applying

and

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[n;m]}}

are pushout squares in

Proof.

Recall that
4

.

Using

, we may construct a canonical commutative cube

Lemma 4.9
.

is a pushout in

We may expand the commutative square to the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tau_{0}[n]_{h}\times[m]_{v}\times\tau_{1}[k;l]_{v}}}

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{P}
is a double
\infty
-category, and
\mathscr{C}
an
\infty
-category.
Then there exists a natural equivalence

\textstyle\mathscr{P}_{0}\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\mathscr{P}_{0}\times\mathscr{C}}\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{C}_{v})\simeq\mathscr{P}_{0}\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathscr{P}_{0}}\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{P})

of
(\infty,2)
-categories.

Proof.

Both sides are indexed by functors
\mathrm{Dbl}\infty\mathrm{Cat}\times\infty\mathrm{Cat}\to(\infty,2)\mathrm{Cat}
that preserve colimits in each variable. Hence, by density, we may reduce to the case that
\mathscr{P}=\langle n,m\rangle
and
\mathscr{C}=[k]
. In this case, the inclusion
\mathscr{P}_{0}\to\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{P})
of the vertical
\infty
-category is given by the obvious map
\tau_{0}[n]\times[m]\to[n]\otimes[m]
. Since
\mathrm{Gr}
preserves colimits, the left-hand side is computed by

\textstyle\tau_{0}[n]\times[m]\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\tau_{0}[n]\times([m]\times[k])}[n]\otimes([m]\times[k]).

This pushout is computed by
[n;k]\otimes[m]
by
4
.
The right-hand part of the equation corresponds to the pushout

\textstyle\tau_{0}[n]\times[m]\bigcupop\displaylimits_{[k]^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\tau_{0}[n]\otimes[m]}[k]^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes[n]\otimes[m].

As the Gray tensor product commutes with colimits in both variables,
2.3
implies that this pushout is also naturally equivalent to
[n;k]\otimes[m]
.
∎

First operation of poly(ethylene naphthalate) enclosures for high-purity germanium detectors in liquid argon for K 42 {}^{42}\text{K} / Ar 42 {}^{42}\text{Ar} mitigation
Stefan Schönert

Abstract
Commercial argon contains cosmogenic Ar 42 {}^{42}\text{Ar} whose progeny K 42 {}^{42}\text{K} is a critical background component for the legend ( legend ). LEGEND operates hpge ( hpge ) detectors bare in liquid argon. K 42 {}^{42}\text{K} is attracted by the hpge detectors’ electric fields, and drifts toward the germanium surface, where it undergoes beta decay. LEGEND-1000 will mitigate K 42 {}^{42}\text{K} -induced background by using underground-sourced argon, depleted in cosmogenic isotopes. If underground argon is not available, mitigation techniques must be employed. pen ( pen ) enclosures were proposed to hinder the ion drift, decrease the beta-particle’s energy, and produce scintillation light. In this paper, we report on operating two hpge detectors, both bare and pen -enclosed, in Ar 42 {}^{42}\text{Ar} -enriched liquid argon, and find no evidence for deterioration of energy stability or resolution due to the enclosures. We monitor the beta and gamma rates of K 42 {}^{42}\text{K} , find complex time-dependencies extending to roughly 30 days 30\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{y}\mathrm{s} after applying the hpge detectors’ high-voltage, and qualitatively demonstrate the K 42 {}^{42}\text{K} suppression capabilities of enclosures.

In contrast to p05, the beta rate is decoupled from the gamma rate and remains constant, i.e., close-by or on-surface concentrations remain unaffected by the longer-range ion drift or charge-up.

To confirm the measured effects, the detectors were unbiased for two days between p06 and p07, and then re-biased; no further changes were made.
Surface charges are expected to diminish.
The data in p07 exhibits a qualitatively similar behavior to that in p06.
A detailed understanding of the

Conclusion

We operated two
hpge
detectors in
lar
in two configurations: (i) bare for more than a month, and (ii) enclosed in
pen
for over two months.
Comparing the data taken with
pen
enclosures to the bare configuration, no evidence was found for deterioration in energy stability or resolution.

We observed gamma and beta radiation from
{}^{42}\text{K}
decays in an
{}^{42}\text{Ar}
-spiked
lar
environment and noted non-trivial time dependencies for the enclosed case.
The intensity of the
1524.6\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
line of
{}^{42}\text{K}
increases rapidly after the enclosed detectors are biased, and subsequently decreases at a slower rate.
The beta rate remains constant, however.

pen
enclosures were found to significantly reduce the count rate of
{}^{42}\text{K}
betas around
Q_{\beta\beta}
of
{}^{76}\text{Ge}
.
A quantitative analysis of the
{}^{42}\text{K}
survival fraction provided by enclosures is outside the scope of this paper, and will be reported elsewhere.

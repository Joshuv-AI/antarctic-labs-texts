A rare case of choroidal melanoma in Iraq: a case report
Authors: Mohanned H Al-Sallami, Estabraq H Al-Kubaisi, Jawad K Al-Diwan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChoroidal melanoma is globally the most common primary intraocular malignancy in adults; however, it remains rarely reported in the Middle East. We report the case of a 38-year-old Iraqi woman who was diagnosed with choroidal melanoma. Five years later, she developed persistent gastric discomfort despite negative regular surveillance CT findings; however, further evaluation with linear endoscopic ultrasound revealed a hypoechoic lesion in the gastric fundus. And the metastatic melanoma was confirmed through histopathological examination, supported by immunohistochemical staining. The age at diagnosis in Asian populations is often younger. It was postulated that the age at presentation might likely get lower with implementation of improved screening strategies. In Asian nations Choroidal melanoma are detected late due to inadequate screening and reduced reporting. Moreover, the diagnosis of gastric melanoma can be challenging, as these metastases often remain clinically masked and CT/PET imaging studies have many pitfalls.Keywords: choroidal melanoma, gastric metastases, endoscopy, Melan-a/MART-1, SOX-10

Introduction

Choroidal melanoma is globally the most common primary intraocular malignancy in adults; however, it is significantly less common in Asian populations compared to Western countries. Data from the Middle East remain extremely limited. To our knowledge, only a few published cases have been reported in Iraq [1]. Stomach involvement is a rare site of metastasis in uveal melanoma. It occurs far less frequently than hepatic metastasis, which remains the predominant target of hematogenous spread. Owing to its rarity, gastric involvement often remaining clinically masked for extended periods, and is frequently not recognized until advanced stages [2].

Case report

A 38-year-old Iraqi female, presented with loss of vision. She was a housewife with no significant excessive exposure to sunlight. There was no family history of malignancy. According to the initial ophthalmic referral records, the ocular surface was unremarkable in both eyes, with no signs of conjunctival or corneal abnormalities. The best-corrected visual acuity was 20/20 in the right eye (OD), while the left eye (OS) had counting fingers (CF) at 5 feet, uncorrected. Fundoscopic examination revealed a large choroidal mass with a solid exudative retinal detachment. Additional ophthalmic investigations including B-scan ultrasonography, optical coherence tomography (OCT), fluorescein fundus angiography (FFA), and CT imaging supported the diagnosis of choroidal melanoma. Enucleation surgery in the left eye was performed with subsequent silicone ball implantation. Histopathological examination of the enucleated specimen confirmed the diagnosis of choroidal melanoma, and systemic CT staging at that time showed no metastasis. Following enucleation, the patient did not receive any adjuvant therapy, including chemotherapy or radiotherapy. Five years later, the patient developed gastric discomfort, which was partially relieved with H2-blockers and proton pump inhibitors. Notably, the oncologic surveillance CT scans were negative for local recurrence or systemic metastasis. Then, the plan to investigate using EGD followed by linear echoendoscope showed a hypoechoic lesion in the gastric fundus arising from the submucosal layer, with no perigastric lymph nodes detected (Figs 1 and 2). Surgical resection of the upper part of the stomach was performed, and histopathology showed metastatic melanoma supported by positive immunohistochemical staining for SOX-10 and Melan-A (Figs 3 and 4), the patient underwent systemic treatment. She initially received immunotherapy with pembrolizumab, followed by systemic chemotherapy.

Discussion

The age of the patients is in line with that reported in the literature on Asian populations [3]. The age at diagnosis in Asian populations is often lower, typically in the fourth to fifth decades of life. It was postulated that age at presentation might be lower with the implementation of improved screening strategies. In Asian nations, choroidal melanoma is detected late owing to inadequate screening [4].

Being rare in the Asian nations, Choroidal melanoma was probably ignored as a diagnosis consideration, resulting in delayed diagnosis, leading to visual loss and potential diagnosis. That was the exact story of this case.

Patients with melanoma metastases in the stomach can present with dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, GI bleeding, weight loss, and sometimes perforation [5]. In some cases, the only symptom may be epigastric discomfort. Patient narratives are rich in emotional and contextual details. This might inadvertently obscure diagnostic clarity, especially in conditions such as dyspepsia, which in turn delays the identification of metastasis. This occurred with the patient.

CT/PET imaging may suggest the diagnosis by identifying a nonspecific lesion. However, the sensitivity of CT for detecting melanoma metastases in the GIT is only 60–70%, and PET has many pitfalls, including very low uptake of intensity in choroidal melanoma metastases [5, 6]. Therefore, in addition to the definitive diagnosis that requires endoscopy for biopsy, the crucial role of endoscopic modalities in detecting malignant lesions in the gastrointestinal tract despite negative CT should be highlighted.

How to obtain slow roll inflation driven by non-linear electrodynamics Preprint: APS/123-QED
Daniele Malafarina, Hrishikesh Chakrabarty, Ilia Musco

Abstract
We establish for the first time the conditions that must be imposed on the action for a magnetic universe in a theory of non-linear electrodynamics in order to have an asymptotically de Sitter initial state followed by a slow roll inflationary phase. We show that models so far proposed in the literature do not allow for a prolonged inflationary phase consistent with observations. We construct a Lagrangian that reduces to the Maxwell one in weak field; this is the only class of models that satisfies the required conditions for slow roll in the early Universe.



Introduction:
Recent observations show that the cosmic microwave background (CMB) exhibits a nearly scale invariant power spectrum
Aghanim et al. 2020
. The most widely accepted explanation which simultaneously solves the flatness and horizon problems
Guth 1981
;
Starobinsky 1980
;
Linde 1982
requires a phase of accelerated expansion in the early Universe driven by a scalar field with a slow roll potential
Akrami et al. 2020
.
However scalar field inflation is not the only option, there are in fact alternative solutions which do not require a scalar field with a fine-tuned potential.
In particular it would be desirable to obtain a slow roll inflationary phase from suitable modifications of known physical fields.
Modifications of Maxwell’s electrodynamics have been proposed in a variety of theoretical contexts starting from the original proposal by Born and Infeld
Born and Infeld 1934
all the way to string theory where Born-Infeld theory emerges as the lowest order term in the expansion of the bosonic open string at low-energies
Fradkin and Tseytlin 1985
.
Following such ideas, models for accelerated expansion driven by different theories of non-linear electrodynamics coupled to gravity have been gaining popularity
Sorokin 2022
;
Altschueler 1990
;
Camara et al. 2004
;
Novello et al. 2004
;
Novello et al. 2007
;
Garcia-Salcedo et al. 2014
;
Övgün 2017
;
Övgün et al. 2018
;
Benaoum et al. 2023a
;
Benaoum et al. 2023b
;
Kruglov 2015a
;
Chavanis 2024
;
Sarkar and Das 2023
;
Kruglov 2017
;
Kruglov 2016a
;
Kruglov 2016b
.
In these models one considers a universe where the accelerated expansion phase is produced by magnetic fields of non-linear electrodynamics minimally coupled to gravity. Neglecting the bulk viscosity terms in the electric conductivity of the primordial plasma and imposing homogeneity and isotropy, one gets that only the average of the magnetic field squared
B^{2}
is non negligible
De Lorenci et al. 2002
, hence the name ‘magnetic’ Universe.
This allows one to obtain a simple class of cosmological toy models which can be solved analytically.
Then the question is: ‘Can a theory of non-linear electrodynamics drive inflation to produce the observed CMB power spectrum?’

In this letter we address this question by considering a general framework for a magnetic universe in General Relativity (GR) coupled to non-linear electrodynamics and determine the conditions for such a theory to produce a quasi de Sitter phase with arbitrarily small slow roll parameters.
As expected the conditions for a slow rolling inflationary phase in the early Universe are more stringent than the simple conditions for accelerated expansion. We derive these conditions here explicitly for the first time, and subsequently apply them to several proposed models of non-linear electrodynamics. We show that none of the existing proposed cosmological models satisfies these conditions and we discuss the simplest Lagrangian that does do this. Throughout the article we make use of geometrical units setting
G=c=1
.

Evolution of a magnetic universe:

The action for GR coupled to a theory of non-linear electrodynamics is

S=\int\left(\kappa R-\mathcal{L}(F)\right)\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\,,

(1)

where
\kappa=16\pi
,
R
is the Ricci scalar of the Einstein-Hilbert action and
\mathcal{L}(F)
is the Lagrangian for a theory of non-linear electrodynamics,
with
F=F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
being the electromagnetic field written in an invariant form by contraction of the Faraday tensor
F_{\mu\nu}
. The above Lagrangian gives Maxwell’s electrodynamics for
\mathcal{L}=-F/4
with
F=2(B^{2}-E^{2})
. Variation of the action (
1
) gives Einstein’s equations
G_{\alpha\beta}=8\pi T_{\alpha\beta}
with the energy momentum tensor due to non-linear electrodynamics given by

T_{\alpha\beta}=-4(\partial_{F}\mathcal{L})F_{\rho\alpha}F^{\rho}_{\beta}+g_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{L}\,,

(2)

where
\partial_{F}
denotes a derivative with respect to
F
.

For the cosmological model we shall consider a Friedman-Robertson-Walker (FRW) line element

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}\left[\frac{dr^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}\left(dr^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right)\right]\,,

(3)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor and
k
is the spatial curvature.
We can obtain the isotropic FRW Universe
when the averaged electromagnetic fields are such that

\displaystyle\langle E\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle B\rangle=0\,,\ \ \ \ \ \langle E_{i}B_{i}\rangle=0\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\langle E_{i}E_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}E^{2}g_{ij}\,,\ \ \ \ \ \ \langle B_{i}B_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}B^{2}g_{ij}\,.

(5)

Considering the form of the stress-energy tensor for a perfect fluid
T_{\alpha\beta}=(\rho+p)u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+pg_{\alpha\beta}
, from the Faraday tensor written in terms of the electric and magnetic fields, we get the density and the isotropic pressures as

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\partial_{F}\mathcal{L}E^{2}-\mathcal{L}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\partial_{F}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{2B^{2}-E^{2}}{3}\right)+\mathcal{L}\,.

(7)

In the radiation dominated epoch of the early Universe the electromagnetic field interacts strongly with the charged plasma, effectively resulting in the screening of the electric field in the rest frame of the fluid
Lemoine and Lemoine 1995
.
Therefore it is expected that the average of the electric field is negligible allowing us to set
E^{2}=0

so that the energy density and pressures become

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L},

(8)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{8B^{2}}{3}\frac{\partial_{F}\mathcal{L}}{\mathcal{L}}\right)\mathcal{L}\,.

(9)

We see that the weak energy conditions, i.e.
\rho>0
and
\rho+p>0
, are satisfied if we require
\mathcal{L}<0
and
\partial_{F}\mathcal{L}<0
. However, looking for a non-singular cosmological model, we must allow for the energy conditions to be violated in the early Universe.
Physically the violations of energy conditions can be understood as large scale corrections to the energy-momentum due to small scale repulsive effects
Martin-Moruno and Visser 2017
;
Visser and Barcelo 2000
.

We can now consider the Friedman equations, obtained from the FRW metric in (
3
), written as

\displaystyle H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\rho-\frac{k}{a^{2}},

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi}{3}(\rho+3p)\,,

(11)

where the dot denotes a derivative with respect to the co-moving time
t
and
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter.
The zero component of the conservation of energy-momentum condition
\nabla^{\alpha}T_{\alpha\beta}=0
gives the continuity equation

\dot{\rho}+3\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\left(\rho+p\right)=0

(12)

and it is easy to check that, for a magnetic universe (i.e. setting
E^{2}=0
), this equation is independent of the form of
\mathcal{L}
and gives

2B+a\frac{dB}{da}=0\,.

(13)

Once integrated this gives

B=\frac{B_{0}}{a^{2}}\,,

(14)

where
B_{0}
is the value of the magnetic field when
a=1
which can be taken as being today.

For simplicity we can redefine the form of the Lagrangian as

\mathcal{A}(F)=\log{\mathcal{L}(F)},

(15)

such that
\partial_{F}\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}=\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}
. Then the equation of state for non-linear electrodynamics can be simply written as
p=\omega\rho
, where from Eq. (
9
) we get

\omega=-1+\frac{8B_{0}^{2}}{3a^{4}}\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=-1+\frac{4}{3}F(\partial_{F}\mathcal{A})\,,

(16)

and hence a de Sitter phase (
\omega\rightarrow-1
) is obtained when
F(\partial_{F}\mathcal{A})\rightarrow 0
.
From the second Friedmann equation we can then compute the condition for accelerated expansion, i.e.
\ddot{a}>0
, as
\rho(1+3\omega)<0
which gives

\frac{4B_{0}^{2}}{a^{4}}\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}<1,

(17)

which is satisfied in the case of a quasi de Sitter state.

Conditions for slow roll inflation:
Let us consider a spatially flat universe (i.e.
k=0
). In order to have a quasi de Sitter period of expansion lasting long enough, the slow roll parameters defined as

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=\frac{3}{2}(1+\omega)\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\epsilon}}{\epsilon H}=\frac{\omega_{,a}a}{1+\omega}\,,

(19)

must be
\ll 1
as
a\rightarrow 0
, where we have set
\omega_{,a}=d\omega/da
. The condition
\omega\rightarrow-1
is necessary in order to have
\epsilon
small, while to achieve slow roll inflation one must impose an additional condition on
\omega_{,a}
in order to keep
\eta
small.

The explicit expressions for
\epsilon
and
\eta
in any theory of non-linear electrodynamics are

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4B_{0}^{2}}{a^{4}}\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{F}\mathcal{A})_{,a}}{\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}}a-4\,.

(21)

The requirements that
\epsilon\ll 1
and
\eta\ll 1
are broad enough to include bouncing models as well as asymptotically de Sitter models satisfying observational constraints. Therefore the requirement that both slow roll parameters go to zero as
a
goes to zero asymptotically is more stringent than the simpler requirements that
\epsilon
and
\eta
are small. Nevertheless this is enough to exclude a large set of previously proposed models.

For example, if we assume a polynomial expansion for
\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}
such that
\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}\sim a^{n}
in the early Universe, we see that
\epsilon\sim a^{n-4}
which goes to zero for
n>4
. However since
\eta\sim n-4
we see that for
n>4
the parameter
\eta
goes to a constant value equal or larger than one.
Therefore satisfying both conditions simultaneously is not possible when
\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}
is written as a polynomial expansion close to
a\simeq 0
and the only inflationary allowed scenario in this case would be the constant roll (where
\eta
goes to a constant)
Kinney 2005
;
Motohashi et al. 2015
.

Going back to expressing
\mathcal{A}
as a function of
F
, from Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
) the conditions for slow roll can be expressed as conditions on
\mathcal{A}(F)
as

F(\partial_{F}\mathcal{A})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{F(\partial_{FF}\mathcal{A})}{\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow-1\,.

(22)

Using these within (
20
) and (
21
), we shall analyze here a few models proposed in the literature for a magnetic universe with non-linear electrodynamics:

(1)

The simplest example is to take Maxwell plus a term proportional to
F^{n}
, that is

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{F}{4}+\alpha F^{n}\,.

(23)

For example in
Novello et al. 2004
;
Novello et al. 2007
Novello et al.
considered the case
n=-1
.
From the above expression we see that

\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=\frac{4\alpha nF^{n-1}-1}{4\alpha F^{n}-F}=\frac{a^{4}}{2B_{0}^{2}}\frac{4\alpha n(2B_{0}^{2})^{n-1}-a^{4(n-1)}}{4\alpha(2B_{0}^{2})^{n-1}-a^{4(n-1)}},

(24)

and we cannot get a de Sitter core for any value of
n
:
for
n>1
we have
\epsilon\rightarrow 2n
while for
n<1
we have
\epsilon\rightarrow 2
.

(2)
A second model worth investigating is the Born-Infeld Lagrangian
Born and Infeld 1934

\mathcal{L}=\alpha\left(1-\sqrt{1+\frac{F}{2\alpha}}\right)\,,

(25)

which gives

\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=-\left[4\alpha\sqrt{1+\frac{F}{2\alpha}}\left(1-\sqrt{1+\frac{F}{2\alpha}}\right)\right]^{-1}\,,

(26)

for which we get
\epsilon\rightarrow 1/2
. We conclude that a magnetic universe in the Born-Infeld theory does not have an initial de Sitter phase.

(3)
In
Kruglov 2015a
;
Chavanis 2024
the following Lagrangian was proposed

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{F}{1+F/\alpha}

(27)

and we see that for a magnetic universe with
F=2B^{2}
we have

\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=\frac{\alpha}{F(\alpha+F)}=\frac{\alpha a^{8}}{2B_{0}^{2}(\alpha a^{4}+2B_{0}^{2})}\,.

(28)

While the condition for a de Sitter initial phase is satisfied, for the slow roll parameters we get

\epsilon=\frac{2a^{4}}{a^{4}+2B_{0}^{2}/\alpha},\ \ \ \ \eta=4-2\epsilon,

(29)

and thus even though
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
when
a\to 0
we have that
\eta\rightarrow 4
, implying constant roll.

(4)
Another alternative Lagrangian was considered in
Övgün et al. 2018
:

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{Fe^{-\alpha F}}{\alpha F+\beta}\,,

(30)

for which we get

\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=\frac{\beta a^{8}}{2B_{0}^{2}(\beta a^{4}+2\alpha B_{0}^{2})}-\alpha\,,

(31)

which does not give a de Sitter phase. Similar expression for the Lagrangian were considered in
Sarkar and Das 2023
;
Kruglov 2017
, although it should be noted that the calculations for
\epsilon
and
\eta
in
Sarkar and Das 2023
are not correct; while in
Kruglov 2017
the derivation of the spectral index and the tensor to scalar ratio is not reliable, because it does not implement the full perturbation analysis. Another Lagrangian with
\mathcal{L}=-Fe^{-\alpha F}
was studied in
Kruglov 2016a
and a model with
\mathcal{L}=-\alpha\arctan(F/\alpha)
was proposed in
Kruglov 2016b
. It is easy to check that the conditions in Eq. (
22
) are not met by any of these models.

(5)
Finally a commonly used Lagrangian for achieving regular black holes was presented in
Fan and Wang 2016
;
Toshmatov et al. 2018
;
Bronnikov 2017
;
Malafarina and Toshmatov 2022
:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\lambda}{4\alpha}\frac{\left(\alpha F\right)^{(k+3)/4}}{\left[1+\left(\alpha F\right)^{k/4}\right]^{(k+\lambda)/k}},

(32)

where
\alpha
is a dimensional coupling constant and
k\geq 1
and
\lambda\neq 0
are the model’s parameters. Maxwell’s electrodynamics is recovered for
k=1
and
\lambda=-1
, while the black hole singularity is resolved by taking
\lambda\geq 3
and the Hayward regular black hole is obtained by setting
k=\lambda=3

Malafarina 2023
.
From

\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=\alpha a^{4}\frac{(k+3)a^{k}+(3-\lambda)\beta_{0}^{k/4}}{4\beta_{0}(a^{k}+\beta_{0}^{k/4})},

(33)

we get

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k+3)a^{k}+(3-\lambda)\beta_{0}^{k/4}}{2(a^{k}+\beta_{0}^{k/4})},

(34)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ka^{k}\frac{(k+\lambda)\beta_{0}^{k/4}}{(a^{k}+\beta_{0}^{k/4})\left[(k+3)a^{k}+(3-\lambda)\beta_{0}^{k/4}\right]},

(35)

where
\beta_{0}=2\alpha B_{0}^{2}
. It is easy to see that for
a\rightarrow 0
we have
\epsilon\rightarrow(3-\lambda)/2
. Therefore to satisfy the first condition we must fix
\lambda=3
. However
\eta\rightarrow 0
for every value of
k\geq 1
when
\lambda\neq 3
, while for
\lambda=3
we have that
\eta\rightarrow k\neq 0
. Therefore it is not possible to satisfy both conditions for any model belonging to this class.

It is important to notice that the coupling constant
\alpha
(as well as other constants in front of
F
) does not play any role in the behavior of the slow roll parameters. The only free parameters that can affect the limiting values of
\epsilon
and
\eta
as
a
goes to zero appear in the powers of
F
(or functions of
F
). Therefore, unless one is willing to consider for example terms like
F^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\simeq 10^{-3}
, the conditions in Eq. (
22
) effectively rule out these models as candidates for inflation. Allowing such term is what was done for example in
Benaoum et al. 2023a
where the authors considered the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}=-F/(\alpha F^{\gamma}+1)^{1/\gamma}
and must fix
\gamma\simeq 0.01
in order to obtain
\eta=4\gamma\ll 1
.

Magnetic Universe with slow roll inflation:

From the above considerations we can summarize the necessary conditions for the action of non-linear electrodynamics to construct a Lagrangian producing slow roll inflation.
Let us define a function
f(a)
as

\partial_{F}\mathcal{A}=a^{4}f(a),

(36)

so that finding a theory with arbitrarily small slow roll parameters, reduces to the simpler problem of finding a function
f(a)
for which

\lim_{a\rightarrow 0}f(a)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{a\rightarrow 0}\frac{af^{\prime}(a)}{f(a)}=0\,.

(37)

A suitable function that satisfies both conditions in (
37
) is given by

f(a)\simeq(\log a^{4})^{-1}
,
for which the Lagrangian becomes

\mathcal{L}=-\alpha\log F
,
where
\alpha
is some constant. For such a theory of non-linear electrodynamics it is possible to achieve a quasi de Sitter initial period as
a\rightarrow 0
and we can easily show that both the slow roll parameters
\epsilon
and
\eta
are small in the same limit. However, this Lagrangian does not have a Maxwell limit.

The most general Lagrangian satisfying the conditions on
\epsilon
and
\eta
and possessing a well-behaved Maxwell limit for large
a
can be written as

\mathcal{L}=-\alpha\log\left(1+\beta F+h(F)\right),

(38)

with
h(F)
being an arbitrary function that goes to zero for
F\ll 1
and
F\gg 1
, and where we must set
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
to respect causality and
\alpha(\beta+h^{\prime}(0))=1/4
to recover Maxwell’s theory in the weak field. Notice that the argument of the
\log
must go linearly in
F
in the weak field to recover Maxwell theory and in the strong field to satisfy the slow roll conditions. Then the function
h(F)
affects the evolution of the Universe only between inflation and late times. It can also be proved that this is the only class of Lagrangians with a Maxwell weak field limit that satisfies both conditions.

The easiest example to consider is taking
h(F)=0
: this theory was originally introduced in
Soleng 1995
in the context of black holes and studied in more detail in
Gaete et al. 2019
;
Kruglov 2015b
but was not applied to cosmology. Interestingly the same Lagrangian was also obtained from an entirely different starting point in
Zholdasbek et al. 2024
. It is also interesting to note that the singularity in this model is shifted to past infinity. In fact the Kretschmann scalar
\mathcal{K}
goes as
\rho^{2}
for small
a
and diverges as
a
goes to zero. Now this Lagrangian is well-behaved in the low energy limit as it reproduces standard electrodynamics and it also satisfies both of the conditions in Eq. (
37
) since

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta F}{(1+\beta F)\log(1+\beta F)}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\frac{\beta F-\log(1+\beta F)}{(1+\beta F)\log(1+\beta F)}\,.

(40)

Notice that in the Maxwell limit
F\simeq 0
we retrieve the expected values of both parameters. On the other hand for
F
large, i.e. when
a
goes to zero, we now have that both slow roll parameters are arbitrarily small.
The Lagrangian in Eq. (
38
) is the simplest model of non-linear electrodynamics which satisfies both conditions and is therefore a suitable candidate for describing inflation, which is an alternative to scalar fields.

The asymptotically de Sitter phase allows one to easily fulfill the requirement that inflation lasts for the required number of e-foldings, as one just needs to set the initial time of inflation accordingly. Then, provided we know the strength of the magnetic field at the end of inflation, the scale of inflation can be calculated from the required number of e-foldings.
Testing the validity of this model against observations of the CMB is beyond the scope of the present letter. In the future we plan to evaluate the perturbations to calculate the power spectrum of tensor and scalar fluctuations which can be constrained by observations.

Discussion:
Theories of non-linear electrodynamics have been used extensively to construct regular black hole solutions

Ayon-Beato and Garcia 1998
;
Bronnikov 2001
;
Dymnikova 2004
;
Fan and Wang 2016
;
Malafarina and Toshmatov 2022
(it is important to notice that, similarly to other regular black hole proposals, such solutions are often unstable
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2025
). It is then natural to ask whether the same strong field effects may have played a role in the early Universe, providing an alternative to the big-bang singularity, and if they may be used in place of scalar field inflation to obtain the observed properties of the CMB power spectrum. We have shown that while it is relatively easy to achieve a quasi de Sitter phase of accelerated expansion in a non-linear magnetic universe, the same can not be said for an inflationary phase with slow roll parameters that produce a scale invariant CMB power spectrum. We have reduced the conditions on the slow roll parameters to two simple conditions on the Lagrangian for non-linear electrodynamics and we have shown how the examples previously proposed in the literature do not satisfy both conditions. Of course constant roll inflation models may be useful to explain other features of the early Universe such as the production of primordial black holes
Ivanov et al. 1994
;
Inoue and Yokoyama 2002
;
Motohashi and Hu 2017
;
Motohashi et al. 2020
.
Also it may be possible to achieve slow roll inflation by including additional elements such as non vanishing electric fields, the dual of the Faraday tensor or non minimal coupling to gravity (see for example
Otalora et al. 2018
;
Avetisyan et al. 2021
;
Campanelli et al. 2008
).
From the proposed conditions we constructed a theory that was never discussed before in this context and produces the desired effects in the early Universe. A detailed investigation of the perturbations and evaluation of the spectral index and tensor to scalar ratio in the new theory will be the subject of future work.

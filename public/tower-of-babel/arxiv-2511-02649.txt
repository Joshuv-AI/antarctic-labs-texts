A geometric and generating function approach to plethysm
Álvaro Gutiérrez, Rosa Orellana, Franco Saliola, Anne Schilling, Mike Zabrocki

Abstract
. Plethysm coefficients 𝖺 μ ⁡ [ ν ] λ \mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda} are the structure coefficients of the plethysm of Schur functions s μ ​ [ s ν ] = ∑ λ 𝖺 μ ⁡ [ ν ] λ ​ s λ s_{\mu}[s_{\nu}]=\sum_{\lambda}\mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}s_{\lambda} . We study a bivariate generating function of plethysm coefficients when λ \lambda has bounded length. We show that this generating function is rational. A key step is MacMahon’s combinatory analysis. When the bound on the length is 2 2 we give an explicit geometric algorithm to compute it using q q -Ehrhart theory. We give evidence that the generating function is the quantum Ehrhart series of a union of half-open polytopes and show that it satisfies a reciprocity theorem reminiscent of Ehrhart reciprocity. Furthermore, we give a set of linear recursions that completely describe the SL 2 \mathrm{SL}_{2} -plethysm coefficients.



1.
Introduction

Given two polynomial representations of the general linear group

\rho:\operatorname{GL}_{n}\to\operatorname{GL}_{m}
and

\tau:\operatorname{GL}_{m}\to\operatorname{GL}_{k}
, we can compose them to obtain a
new representation of the general linear group
\tau\circ\rho:\operatorname{GL}_{n}\to\operatorname{GL}_{k}
,
called the
plethysm
of
\tau
and
\rho
.
This operation was first studied by Littlewood
[
Lit36
]
who described
it in terms of symmetric functions. The characters of irreducible representations
of general linear groups are Schur polynomials.
If
s_{\lambda}
and
s_{\mu}
are the characters of two composable representations,
then their
plethysm

s_{\lambda}[s_{\mu}]
is the character of the composition
of the representations.

The
\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients

\mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}
are the structure constants of the plethysm of Schur polynomials,

s_{\mu}[s_{\nu}]=\sum_{\lambda}\mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}s_{\lambda}.

A major open problem as identified by Stanley
[
Sta00
]
is to find a combinatorial interpretation of the plethysm coefficients
\mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}
.
A solution to this problem has also important applications in physics
[
Wyb70
]
,
invariant theory
[
How87
]
, and complexity theory
[
MS03
]
.
Various special cases have been solved, going back to the work of Littlewood in the 40’s (see
for instance
[
Lit40
,
CL95
,
CR98
,
LR04
,
LR11
,
PW19
,
OSSZ22
,
GR23
,
COS+24
,
BP24
,
OSSZ24
,
Gut24
,
PPS25
]

and references therein), but a general formula or
interpretation for
\mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}
is still elusive.

In this paper, we study the plethysm coefficients
\mathsf{a}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}
when
\lambda
has at most two parts. These coefficients can be
understood in terms of the characters of
\textup{SL}_{2}=\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. More precisely, the character ring of
\textup{SL}_{n}
is

\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}/(x_{1}\cdots x_{n}-1)
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
denotes the
symmetric polynomials in
n
variables with nonnegative integer coefficients. In particular, the character ring of
\textup{SL}_{2}
is identified with the

\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
-span of the
q
-integers

(1.1)

[n]_{q}:=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}}\quad\text{for $n\geqslant 0$.}

Working over
\textup{GL}_{2}
requires the partition
\nu
to have at most two parts; working over
\textup{SL}_{2}
one can moreover assume that
\nu=(h)
is a single part partition.
We denote by
a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}
the coefficient of
[k]_{q}
in the expansion of
s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q,q^{-1})
, which relates to the plethysm coefficient

\mathsf{a}_{\mu[(h)]}^{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}=a_{\mu[h]}^{[\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1]}.

We call
a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}
the
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients
. By convention,
a_{\mu[h]}^{[0]}:=0
.

We study the bivariate generating function of
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients, namely

(1.2)

A_{\mu}(z,q)=\sum_{k\geqslant 1,h\geqslant 0}a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}q^{k}z^{h}

and show that
A_{\mu}(z,q)
is a rational function (see Corollary
2.12
).
This implies, in particular, that
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients satisfy linear recurrences.
Furthermore, we provide a geometric algorithm to compute
A_{\mu}(z,q)

in terms of
q
-Ehrhart theory
[
Cha16
,
BK25
]
(see Theorem
2.11
).
A key step in the algorithm is a geometric version of MacMahon’s combinatory analysis
[
Mac04
]
found in
[
BZ17
]
.

We then study and discover many different properties of this bivariate generating function which demonstrate that the
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients
have a lot of structure:

(1)

We show (see Theorem
4.8
) that there exists a polynomial
p_{\mu}(z,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm},z]
such that

A_{\mu}(z,q)=\frac{p_{\mu}(z,q)}{d_{w}(z,q)}
, where
w=|\mu|
and

d_{w}(z,q):=\begin{cases}(1-z)\prod_{i=1}^{w}(1-z^{i})\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{w}{2}}(1-q^{2i}z)&\text{for $w$ even,}\\
\prod_{i=1}^{w}(1-z^{2i})\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{w+1}{2}}(1-q^{2i-1}z)&\text{for $w$ odd.}\end{cases}

We conjecture a denominator with fewer terms (see Conjecture
4.1
).

(2)

For
|\mu|\leqslant 5
, we compute
A_{\mu}(z,q)
explicitly as a sum of positive terms (see Section
3.1

and Appendix
A
).
This makes
A_{\mu}(z,q)
the
weighted generating function of the lattice points of a disjoint union of polytopes.
In particular, this allows us to obtain a combinatorial interpretation for the associated
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients.
We believe these positive expansions should exist more generally.

Conjecture 1.1
.

The generating function
A_{\mu}(z,q)
is a weighted generating function of lattice points of
a disjoint union of convex rational half-open cones.

(3)

We show that
A_{\mu}(z,q)
satisfies a reciprocity theorem (see Theorem
5.8
) that is reminiscent of (
q
-)Ehrhart reciprocity
for half-open polytopes:

(1.3)

A_{\mu}(z^{-1},q^{-1})=(-1)^{w}z^{2}A_{\mu^{\prime}}(z,q),

where
\mu^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
\mu
. This gives evidence for Conjecture
1.1
.

Kirillov
[
Kir04
]
considered a similar family of generating functions given by

\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\mathsf{a}_{n\mu[\nu]}^{n\lambda}t^{n}.

They were shown to be rational functions by Mulmuley
[
Mul09
]
, who conjectured that they are Ehrhart series of (unions of) closed
polytopes. Kahle and Michałek
[
KM18
]
showed the conjecture is false, since it would violate Ehrhart reciprocity for
closed polytopes. We remark in Section
5.1
that a weakening of Mulmuley’s conjecture is still open.
Our proof of rationality of
A_{\mu}(z,q)
extends Mulmuley’s result, and our Conjecture
1.1
follows his philosophy.

(4)

We give a set of recurrences that completely describe
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients (see Section
3.2
).
The results obtained generalize the enumerative results obtained in
[
OSSZ24
]
and
[
Gut24
]
. In these papers,
we obtained recursions satisfied by some
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients and combinatorial interpretations for the case
\mu=(3)
and
\mu=(4)
,
by finding symmetric chain decompositions of the Young lattice.

(5)

We also introduce a related generating function for
\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients and show that it is rational. This implies that the

\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients satisfy linear recursions.

Note that for
\mu=(w)
, the coefficients
a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}
are also certain Kronecker coefficients
[
PP13
,
Pan23
]
.
A combinatorial interpretation for the
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients is given in
[
PPS25
]
in
terms of computational trees, by detailed analysis of a generalization of the KOH formula
[
O’H90
,
Zei89
,
GOS92
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the geometric interpretation for

A_{\mu}(z,q)
culminating in Theorem
2.11
relating it to a
q
-Ehrhart series via the MacMahon operator.
In Section
3
, we deduce combinatorial facts from these results and give linear recursions for the
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients.
In Section
4
, we provide explicit expressions for the denominator of
A_{\mu}(z,q)
.
In Section
5
we give a proof of the reciprocity in (
1.3
).
We conclude in Section
6
with the definition and analysis of a generating function for
\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients.

2.
Geometric picture

In this section, we develop a geometric approach to Schur polynomials and the generating function
A_{\mu}(z,q)
of

\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients defined in (
1.2
) by combining modern interpretations of classical results from different areas:
Ehrhart theory, (quasi)symmetric functions, and MacMahon’s combinatory analysis. We rely on concepts developed
in
[
BJR09
,
GPS20
]
,
[
Cha16
,
BK25
]
, and
[
BZ17
]
.

2.1.
Preliminaries

We introduce some notation. See
[
Sta99
]
for more details.

A
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of a nonnegative integer
w
, denoted
\lambda\vdash w
, satisfies

\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{\ell}>0
for
\lambda_{i}
integers and
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=w
.
We call
\ell(\lambda):=\ell
the length of
\lambda
.

For
\lambda
a partition, denote by
\mathsf{SSYT}_{h+1}(\lambda)
the set of
semistandard Young tableaux

of shape
\lambda
over the alphabet
\{0,1,\ldots,h\}
. For each
\lambda
, the
Schur polynomial
is defined as

s_{\lambda}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SSYT}_{h+1}(\lambda)}x^{\mathsf{wt}(T)},

where
\mathsf{wt}(T)=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{h})
is the
weight
of
T
with
\alpha_{i}
equal to the number of letters
i
in
T
and

x^{\mathsf{wt}(T)}=x_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots x_{h}^{\alpha_{h}}
. We denote by
\mathsf{shape}(T)=\lambda
the shape of
T
.

A
composition

\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell})
of a nonnegative integer
w
, denoted
\beta\models w
,
satisfies
\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{\ell}=w
and
\beta_{i}
are positive integers.
The
monomial quasisymmetric polynomial
indexed by a composition
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell})
with
\ell\leqslant h+1

is defined as

M_{\beta}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{0\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\ell}\leqslant h}x_{i_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{\ell}}^{\beta_{\ell}}.

Gessel
[
Ges84
]
introduced what are now known as
Gessel’s fundamental quasisymmetric function
as

F_{\alpha}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}M_{\beta}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h}),

where
\alpha
is also a composition and
\beta\preceq\alpha
indicates that
\beta
is a refinement of
\alpha
.

Let
\mathsf{SYT}(\lambda)
be the set of
standard Young tableaux
of shape
\lambda
. Then Gessel
[
Ges84
]
proved that

s_{\lambda}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\lambda)}F_{\mathsf{Des}(T)}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h}).

Here
\mathsf{Des}(T)
is the descent set of
T
, where
i
is a
descent
if the letter
i+1
appears in larger (lower) row than

i
in English convention for partitions. If
\mathsf{Des}(T)=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}
is the descent set of
T
, then we can associate to it the descent
composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
, where
\alpha_{i}=d_{i}-d_{i-1}
and by convention
d_{0}=0
. By abuse of notation

F_{\mathsf{Des}(T)}=F_{\alpha}
.

Similar to the descent set, we define the
ascent set

\mathsf{Asc}(T)
as the letters
i
in
T\in SYT(\lambda)
such
i+1
is strictly to the
right of
i
. Ascents and descents can also be defined for permutations
\pi\in S_{h}
, where
S_{h}
is the symmetric group on
h
elements.
A letter
i\in\pi
is a descent (resp. ascent) if
i+1
lies to the left (resp. right) of
i
. We denote by
\mathsf{des}(T)
(resp.
\mathsf{asc}(T)
) the cardinality
of
\mathsf{Des}(T)
(resp.
\mathsf{Asc}(T)
) and similarly for permutations.

The
major index
of a tableau
T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\lambda)
is

\mathsf{maj}(T)=\sum_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(T)}i

and similarly
\mathsf{maj}(w)=\sum_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(w)}i
for
w\in S_{h}
.
If we denote by
\lambda^{\prime}
(resp.
T^{\prime}
) the conjugate of the partition
\lambda
(resp. tableau
T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\lambda)
, then note that

\mathsf{des}(T^{\prime})=|T|-1-\mathsf{des}(T)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{maj}(T^{\prime})=\binom{|T|}{2}-\mathsf{maj}(T).

2.2.
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients

We define the
q
-integers as in (
1.1
).
Define the
q
-binomial
as

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}[n-1]_{q}\cdots[n-k+1]_{q}}{[k]_{q}[k-1]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}}

for
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
, and
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{q}=0
otherwise.

As shown in
[
Mac15
, §I.8. Example 4, p. 137]
, the
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficient

s_{w}[s_{h}](q^{-1},q)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h}{w}_{q}=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}a_{w[h]}^{[k]}[k]_{q}.

More generally, we write

s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q^{-1},q)=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}[k]_{q},

where
a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}
is the coefficient of
[k]_{q}
in
s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q^{-1},q)
. Up to an overall power of
q
and replacing

q
by
q^{2}
, the plethysm
s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q^{-1},q)
is given by the principle specialization
s_{\mu}(1,q,q^{2},\ldots,q^{h})
which
in turn is related to the generalized Gaussian polynomial
[
Mac15
, pg. 138]
.

2.3.
The integer-point enumerator and Schur polynomials

Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{h+1}
denote the natural representation of
\textup{SL}_{h+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
The character of
V
is given by
x_{0}+\cdots+x_{h}
.
The character of the tensor power
V^{\otimes w}
is given by
(x_{0}+\cdots+x_{h})^{w}
.
A corollary of the RSK algorithm
[
Sta99
, Cor. 7.12.5]
and a consequence of Stanley’s theory of
P
-partitions
[
Sta99
, Thm. 7.19.7]
give the equalities

(2.1)

(x_{0}+\cdots+x_{h})^{w}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash w}f^{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{\alpha\models w}F_{\alpha}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h}),

where
f^{\lambda}
is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
.
Since we work over
h+1
variables, we automatically have that the partitions
\lambda
have at most
h+1
parts.
Our first goal is to paint a geometric picture for (
2.1
), which loosely follows
[
BJR09
,
GPS20
]
.

Originally, the RSK algorithm gives a bijection between words of length
w
in the alphabet
\{0,1,\ldots,h\}
and pairs of tableaux
in
\bigcup_{\lambda\vdash w}\mathsf{SSYT}_{h+1}(\lambda)\times\mathsf{SYT}(\lambda)
, see for example
[
Sag01
,
Sta99
]
.
Now consider the
w
-dimensional unit hypercube
\square=[0,1]^{w}
. The RSK algorithm can also be interpreted as a bijection between
the integral points of the dilated hypercube
h\square
and the set
\bigcup_{\lambda\vdash w}\mathsf{SSYT}_{h+1}(\lambda)\times\mathsf{SYT}(\lambda)
.
This bijection is refined as follows. For
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant w
, the hyperplane
\{v_{i}=v_{j}\}
divides the cube into two regions,

\square\cap\{v_{i}\leqslant v_{j}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \square\cap\{v_{i}>v_{j}\}.

The
braid hyperplane arrangement

\bigcup_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant w}\{v_{i}=v_{j}\}
then divides the hypercube into
w!
regions
(
chambers
), each chamber naturally indexed by a total order on
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{w}\}
. The closures of these chambers are usually called

Weyl chambers
in the literature; however, we work with sets defined by weak and strict inequalities. That is to say, we want to work with
the half-open polytopes defined by the hyperplanes.
A permutation
\pi\in S_{w}

(v_{\pi(1)},\ldots,v_{\pi(w)})

\mathsf{Ch}(\pi)

We denote by

the dilated chamber with point in

and otherwise the same conditions.

Definition 2.1
.

The
integer-point enumerator
of a
w
-dimensional polytope
P
at height
h
is the generating function of the integral points of
hP
, namely

\mathsf{ipe}_{P}(h;x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)=\sum_{v\in hP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{w}}x_{v_{1}}x_{v_{2}}\cdots x_{v_{w}}.

{noteInternal}

The specialization
\mathsf{ipe}_{P}(h;1,1,\ldots)
is the Ehrhart polynomial
\mathsf{ehr}_{P}(h)
.

Note that the integer-point enumerator of a
bounded
polytope is a polynomial in finitely many variables; in particular, the integer-point
enumerator at height
h
of any polytope contained in
\square
is a polynomial in the variables
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{h}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The integer-point enumerator of
\mathsf{Ch}^{\textup{fine}}(\gamma)
at height
h
is the monomial quasisymmetric polynomial
M_{\alpha^{\gamma}}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{h})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\alpha^{\gamma}
,
\pi=\pi^{\gamma}
, and
P=\mathsf{Ch}^{\textup{fine}}(\gamma)
. Suppose that
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
has
k

We have

\displaystyle\mathsf{ipe}_{P}(h;x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in hP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{w}}x_{v_{1}}x_{v_{2}}\cdots x_{v_{w}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in hP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{w}}x_{v_{\pi(1)}}x_{v_{\pi(2)}}\cdots x_{v_{\pi(w)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in hP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{w}}x_{v_{\pi(1)}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{v_{\pi(\alpha_{1}+1)}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{v_{\pi(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k-1}+1)}}^{\alpha_{k}}

\mathsf{QEhr}_{R}(z,q)=\mathsf{qEhr}_{R}^{u}(z/q^{w},q^{2})=\sum_{h\geq 0}\mathsf{ipe}_{R}(h;1,q^{2},q^{4},\ldots){z^{h}}/{q^{wh}},

we deduce
\mathsf{QEhr}_{\pi R}=\mathsf{QEhr}_{R}
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mathsf{Ch}(\pi)}^{u}(z,q)

\displaystyle=(z/q^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{2\langle u,p\rangle}\mathsf{QEhr}_{\triangle}^{u}(z,q).

By direct computation,

\langle u,p\rangle=\sum_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(\pi)}(w-i)=w\cdot\mathsf{des}(\pi)-\sum_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(\pi)}i=w\cdot\mathsf{des}(\pi)-\mathsf{maj}(\pi).

Therefore,

(z/q^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{2\langle u,p\rangle}=z^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}q^{w\cdot\mathsf{des}(\pi)-2\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}.

Recall that each chamber
\mathsf{Ch}(\pi)_{\geqslant 1}
contains exactly one
standard point

v_{\text{std}}
, characterized by the property that its insertion tableau
T(v_{\text{std}})
is standard.
Note that
\mathsf{maj}(T)=\mathsf{maj}(\pi)
, and
\mathsf{des}(T)=\mathsf{des}(\pi)
.
Moreover, since
\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)
is contained in
\mathsf{SSYT}_{[0,h]}(\mu)
for
h\geqslant w
, every standard tableau arises this way exactly once.
Summing over all chambers that compose
P
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{QEhr}_{P}(z,q)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{QEhr}_{\triangle}(z,q)\sum_{\pi}q^{-2\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}(zq^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{QEhr}_{\triangle}(z,q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{\text{std}}\in P\\
\text{standard}\end{subarray}}q^{-2\mathsf{maj}(T(v_{\text{std}}))}(zq^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(T(v_{\text{std}}))}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{QEhr}_{\triangle}(z,q)\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)}q^{-2\mathsf{maj}(T)}(zq^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(T)}.\qed

Note that we can reprove the Carlitz identity
[
Car75
]
(already found in
[
Mac04
, II.IV, §462]
; see also
[
BK25
, §2.1]
).
We state it with the quantum specialization.

Theorem 2.9
(Carlitz identity).

We have

\mathsf{QEhr}_{\square}(z,q)=\frac{\sum_{\pi\in S_{w}}q^{-2\mathsf{maj}(\pi)}(zq^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(\pi)}}{\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{w-2i}z)}.

Proof.

It follows from

\square=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash w\\
Q\in\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)\end{subarray}}\mathsf{Ch}^{\textup{coar}}(Q)=\bigcup_{\pi\in S_{w}}\mathsf{Ch}(\pi)

and the previous theorem.
∎

2.5.
MacMahon’s combinatory analysis

MacMahon’s partition analysis
[
Mac04
]
was introduced in 1916 in the context of partitions
and turns out to be of great importance for the analysis of generating functions, see for
example
[

,
APR01b
,

In this section, we show that the generating function

of
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients defined
in (

) is related to the quantum Ehrhart series via the positive term operator.

MacMahon’s Omega operator
\Omega^{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}_{\geqslant}=\Omega_{\geqslant}
is defined as

\Omega^{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}_{\geqslant}\sum_{s_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{s_{r}=-\infty}^{\infty}A_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}\alpha_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{r}^{s_{r}}=\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{s_{r}=0}^{\infty}A_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}},

where the domain of the
A_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}
is the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{C}
in several complex
variables and the variables
\lambda_{i}
are restricted to a neighborhood of the circle
|\alpha_{i}|=1
. In
[
Xin04
]
, Xin studied
the positive term operator

(2.3)

\mathsf{PT}^{\alpha}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}\alpha^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\alpha^{n}

and more generally
\mathsf{PT}^{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}
by iteration of (
2.3
). He showed that
[
Xin04
, Equation (4.1)]

\Omega_{\geqslant}f(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r},x)=\mathsf{PT}^{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}f(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r},x)|_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})=(1,\ldots,1)}.

An
Elliott-rational function
is a rational function that can be written in such
a way that its denominator can be factored into the products of one monomial minus another, with the 0 monomial allowed.
See for example
[
Xin04
, Definition 3.1]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
Xin04
, Remark 4.9, Theorem 3.2]

If
f
is Elliott-rational, then
\mathsf{PT}^{\alpha}f
is still Elliott-rational.

Recall the quantum Ehrhart series indexed by a partition as in Remark
2.4
.
The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
\mu
a partition, we have

(2.4)

A_{\mu}(z,q)=\mathsf{PT}^{q}(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q).

Proof.

We defined
a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}
to be the coefficient of
[k]_{q}
in the plethysm
s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q,q^{-1})
for
k\geqslant 1
;
recall that by definition
a_{\mu[h]}^{[0]}=0
. This is equivalent to

a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}=(q-q^{-1})s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q,q^{-1})\big|_{q^{k}}\qquad\text{for $k\geqslant 0$.}

Hence, using the definition of
A_{\mu}(z,q)
in (
1.2
), we obtain

A_{\mu}(z,q)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0,h\geqslant 0}a_{\mu[h]}^{[k]}q^{k}z^{h}=\sum_{h\geqslant 0}\mathsf{PT}^{q}(q-q^{-1})s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q,q^{-1})z^{h}=\mathsf{PT}^{q}(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q),

where the last equation follows from Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

For a partition
\mu

is Elliott-rational.

Proof.

By Theorem

\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)

∎

3.2.
Recurrences for plethysm coefficients

In Section

of
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients is rational
and in Section

we will show that

\mathsf{a}^{\lambda}_{\mu[\nu]}

where

is fixed is also rational.
These generating functions imply the existence of
certain linear recurrences, such as the ones found in
[
OSSZ24
,
Gut24
,
Tét20
]
.
In this section we find a simple linear recurrence that fully describes
\textup{SL}_{2}
-plethysm coefficients.

Recall that

(3.4)

a_{w[h]}^{[k]}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h}{h}_{q}(q-q^{-1})\Big|_{q^{k}}

for
1\leqslant k\leqslant hw+1
and
k\equiv wh+1\pmod{2}
. We now state recursion relations for the plethysm coefficients
a_{w[h]}^{[k]}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have for
1\leqslant k\leqslant hw+1
and
k\equiv wh+1\pmod{2}

(3.5)

a_{w[h]}^{[k]}=\begin{cases}a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[k-h]}&\text{if $wh+1-2w<k\leqslant wh+1$,}\\[5.0pt]
a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[k-h]}+a_{w[h-1]}^{[k+w]}&\text{if $h<k\leqslant hw+1-2w$,}\\[5.0pt]
a_{w[h-1]}^{[k+w]}-a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[h-k]}&\text{if $1\leqslant k\leqslant h$.}\end{cases}

Similarly, we have

(3.6)

a_{w[h]}^{[k]}=\begin{cases}a_{w[h-1]}^{[k-w]}&\text{if $wh+1-2h<k\leqslant wh+1$,}\\[5.0pt]
a_{w[h-1]}^{[k-w]}+a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[k+h]}&\text{if $w<k\leqslant hw+1-2h$,}\\[5.0pt]
a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[k+h]}-a_{w[h-1]}^{[w-k]}&\text{if $1\leqslant k\leqslant w$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The recursion (
3.6
) follows from (
3.5
) (and vice versa) by interchanging
h
and
w
, since

a_{h[w]}^{[k]}
is symmetric under interchanging
h
and
w
.

The
q
-binomial coefficient satisfies the
q
-Pascal identity

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h}{h}_{q}=q^{h}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h}_{q}+q^{-w}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h-1}_{q}.

Hence for
1\leqslant k\leqslant wh+1
and
k\equiv wh+1\pmod{2}
we have

a_{w[h]}^{[k]}=\left(q^{h}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h}_{q}(q-q^{-1})+q^{-w}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h-1}_{q}(q-q^{-1})\right)\Big|_{q^{k}}\\
=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h}_{q}(q-q^{-1})\Big|_{q^{k-h}}+\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h-1}_{q}(q-q^{-1})\Big|_{q^{k+w}}.

When
wh+1-2w<k\leqslant wh+1
, the second term is zero and the first term is
a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[k-h]}
by (
3.4
),
which is the first case in (
3.5
).
When
h<k\leqslant hw+1-2w
, the first term is
a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[k-h]}
and the second term is
a_{w[h-1]}^{[k+w]}
, which
proves the second case in (
3.5
).
When
1\leqslant k\leqslant h
, the second term is still
a_{w[h-1]}^{[k+w]}
. For the first term we cannot use (
3.4
)
to relate it to the plethysm coefficients. However, we can use the fact that

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h}{h}_{q}\Big|_{q^{k}}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h}{h}_{q}\Big|_{q^{-k}}.

Namely,

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w+h-1}{h}_{q}(q-q^{-1})\Big|_{q^{k-h}}=-\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{w-1+h}{h}_{q}\Big|_{q^{h-k}}=-a_{(w-1)[h]}^{[h-k]}

proving the last case in (
3.5
).
∎

4.2.

g_{1},g_{2}

\left<g_{1},g_{2}\right>_{qz}
by proving that there exists elements
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}[z,q,q^{-1}]
such that

f_{1}g_{1}+f_{2}g_{2}=p
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
a,b>0

1-z^{\frac{a+b}{\gcd(a,b)}}\in\left<1-q^{a}z,1-z/q^{b}\right>_{qz}.

Furthermore if
a>b

1-z^{\frac{a-b}{\gcd(a,b)}}\in\left<1-q^{a}z,1-q^{b}z\right>_{qz}\hbox{ and }1-z^{\frac{a-b}{\gcd(a,b)}}\in\left<1-z/q^{a},1-z/q^{b}\right>_{qz}\penalty\ .

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}\pm\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{a\lcm(a,b)\pm b\lcm(a,b)}{ab}=\frac{a\pm b}{\gcd(a,b)}\penalty\ ,

where the last equality holds since
ab=\gcd(a,b)\lcm(a,b)
.

For the first identity, we choose

f_{1}=(1+q^{a}z+q^{2a}z^{2}+\cdots+q^{(\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}-1)a}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}-1})

and

f_{2}=q^{\lcm(a,b)}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}}(1+z/q^{b}+\cdots+z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}-1}/q^{(\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}-1)b})\penalty\ .

We then calculate that

\displaystyle f_{1}(1-q^{a}z)+f_{2}(1-z/q^{b})

\displaystyle=(1-q^{\lcm(a,b)}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}})+q^{\lcm(a,b)}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}}(1-z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}}/q^{\lcm(a,b)})

\displaystyle=1-z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}+\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}}=1-z^{\frac{a+b}{\gcd(a,b)}}\penalty\ .

For the second identity, we choose

f_{1}=-z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}-\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}}(1+q^{a}z+\cdots+q^{\lcm(a,b)-a}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}-1})

and

f_{2}=(1+q^{b}z+\cdots+q^{\lcm(a,b)-b}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}-1})

then calculate

\displaystyle f_{1}(1-q^{a}z)+f_{2}(1-q^{b}z)

\displaystyle=-z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}-\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}}(1-q^{\lcm(a,b)}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}})+(1-q^{\lcm(a,b)}z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}})

\displaystyle=1-z^{\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{b}-\frac{\lcm(a,b)}{a}}\penalty\ .

The third identity follows by replacing
q
by
q^{-1}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For integers

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}p_{r,\ell,i}\frac{\mathsf{d}_{r,\ell}}{1-q^{r+2i}z}=(1-z^{2})(1-z^{4})\cdots(1-z^{2\ell})\penalty\ .

We prove the statement by induction on

\frac{(1-q^{r}z)}{(1-q^{r}z)}=1

Now assume that the statement is true for any integer

\mathsf{d}_{r,\ell+1}=\mathsf{d}_{r,\ell}(1-q^{r+2\ell+2}z)=(1-q^{r}z)\mathsf{d}_{r+2,\ell}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}p_{r,\ell,i}\frac{\mathsf{d}_{r,\ell}(1-q^{r+2\ell+2}z)}{1-q^{r+2i}z}=(1-z^{2})(1-z^{4})\cdots(1-z^{2\ell})(1-q^{r+2\ell+2}z)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}p_{r+2,\ell,i}\frac{(1-q^{r}z)\mathsf{d}_{r+2,\ell}}{1-q^{r+2+2i}z}=(1-z^{2})(1-z^{4})\cdots(1-z^{2\ell})(1-q^{r}z)\penalty\ .

Now by Proposition
4.2
and Note
4.2
,

and Equation (

Proposition 4.5

For any

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle p_{n,k,i}\frac{\mathsf{d}^{\prime}_{n,k}}{1-q^{2i}z}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{n,k,i}\frac{\mathsf{d}^{\prime}_{n,k}}{1-z/q^{2i}}=(1-z)(1-z^{2})\cdots(1-z^{n+k})\penalty\ .

and

with

.

with

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{n,k-1,i}\frac{\mathsf{d}^{\prime}_{n,k-1}(1-z/q^{2k})}{1-q^{2i}z}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}r_{n,k-1,i}\frac{\mathsf{d}^{\prime}_{n,k-1}(1-z/q^{2k})}{1-z/q^{2i}}

Corollary 4.6

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

For
w>0
with
w
even, define

d_{w}(z,q):=(1-z)\prod_{i=1}^{w}(1-z^{i})\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{w}{2}}(1-q^{2i}z)

and for
w
odd let

d_{w}(z,q):=\prod_{i=1}^{w}(1-z^{2i})\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{w+1}{2}}(1-q^{2i-1}z)\penalty\ .

Then for all
\mu\vdash w
,

d_{w}(z,q)A_{\mu}(z,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[z,q]\penalty\ .

Proof.

We use that
A_{\mu}(z,q)=\mathsf{PT}^{q}(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
.
By Theorem
2.8
, Corollary
4.6
and Corollary
4.7
, we have that

\mathsf{den}_{w}(z)(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
is a sum of terms of the
form

\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}

for
a\geqslant 0
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leqslant i\leqslant w
and
i\neq\frac{w}{2}
.

Now we need to apply some analysis to compute the result of applying the
\mathsf{PT}^{q}
operator
to these terms. First we consider the terms such that
0\leqslant i<\frac{w}{2}
. In this case

\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}=z^{a}q^{b}(1+q^{w-2i}z+q^{2(w-2i)}z^{2}+q^{3(w-2i)}z^{3}+\cdots)

if
b\geqslant 0
. Hence
\mathsf{PT}^{q}\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}=\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}
. Otherwise

b
is negative finite and then
\mathsf{PT}^{q}
kills all the terms
z^{a}q^{b}q^{k(w-2i)}z^{k}

such that
b+k(w-2i)<0
. Let
d=\lfloor\frac{b}{w-2i}\rfloor
. Then

\displaystyle\mathsf{PT}^{q}\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}-z^{a}q^{b}(1+q^{w-2i}z+q^{2(w-2i)}z^{2}+\cdots+q^{d(w-2i)}z^{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}-z^{a}q^{b}\frac{1-q^{(d+1)(w-2i)}z^{d+1}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{(d+1)(w-2i)+b}z^{d+a+1}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}.

The important observation in that expression is that
(d+1)(w-2i)+b\geqslant 0
and hence the
numerator is an element of
\mathbb{Z}[z,q]
.

Next we consider the terms with
\frac{w}{2}<i\leqslant w
. If
b<0
, then

\mathsf{PT}^{q}\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}=0
. If
b\geqslant 0
, the operator

\mathsf{PT}^{q}
kills all the terms
z^{a}q^{b}q^{k(w-2i)}z^{k}
with
b<k(w-2i)
. Let
d=\lfloor\frac{b}{w-2i}\rfloor
. Then

\mathsf{PT}^{q}\frac{z^{a}q^{b}}{1-q^{w-2i}z}=z^{a}q^{b}(1+q^{w-2i}z+q^{2(w-2i)}z^{2}+\cdots+q^{d(w-2i)}z^{d}).

We conclude that
\mathsf{PT}^{q}\mathsf{den}_{w}(z)(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
is a sum of polynomials and
rational functions of the form seen in Equation (
4.4
) where the denominator is
of the form
1-q^{w-2i}z
with
0\leqslant i<\frac{w}{2}
. Therefore if we multiply
this expression by
\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{w}{2}}(1-q^{2i}z)
if
w
is even or

\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{w+1}{2}}(1-q^{2i-1}z)
if
w
is odd, then the resulting expression
is a polynomial.
∎

We have found limitations computing
the
\mathsf{PT}^{q}
operators on the expressions
(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)

as rational functions using computer packages that have implemented MacMahon operators.
The expressions can explode in complexity and
this prevented us from computing

beyond roughly

using these implementations.
We were able to surpass these barriers using Theorem

and computing

\sum_{h\leqslant m}(q-q^{-1})s_{\mu}\Big[\frac{q^{h}-q^{-h}}{q-q^{-1}}\Big]z^{h}

for a sufficiently large

and applying

to the polynomial.
Multiplying this expression by

produces a polynomial of the form
p_{\mathrm{low}}(z,q)+p_{\mathrm{high}}(z,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q,z]
,
where the degrees of

are larger than
m
in

and the
polynomial

is independent of

.
Our computations suggest that taking

is sufficient to compute

, but this is by no means a sharp bound.
An expression for
A_{\mu}(z,q)
is
\frac{p_{\mathrm{low}}(z,q)}{d_{\mu}(z,q)}
.

5.
Reciprocity

In Section
2
, we gave a geometric expression for
A_{\mu}(z,q)
in terms of a quantum Ehrhart series and an integral operator

\mathsf{PT}^{q}(q-q^{-1})
based on MacMahon’s Partition Analysis.
As we saw in Section
3.1
, the resulting rational function is not immediately a
q
-Ehrhart series, but for any given
example we can express it as a positive sum of
q
-Ehrhart series of rational cones. We conjecture this is always possible.

Conjecture
.

The generating function
A_{\mu}(z,q)
is a weighted generating function of lattice points of a disjoint union of convex rational half-open cones.

In this section, we give supporting evidence for the conjecture. We begin by discussing a similar conjecture of Mulmuley.

5.1.
A conjecture of Mulmuley

In a collection of open problems and conjectures, Kirillov writes that one expects the generating function

(5.1)

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\mathsf{a}_{n\mu[\nu]}^{n\lambda}t^{n}

to be rational

[
Kir04
, (✠) right after Conj. 2.14]
. This was shown by Mulmuley
[
Mul09
, Thm. 1.6.1 (a)]
, who furthermore conjectured the following.

Conjecture 5.1
(
[
Mul09
, Hypothesis 1.6.4 (1)]
).

The generating function
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}(t)
is the Ehrhart series of a polytope.

Khale and Michałek
[
KM18
]
disproved the conjecture.

Theorem 5.2
(
[
KM18
]
).

Neither

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\mathsf{a}_{n\mu[\nu]}^{n\lambda}t^{n}\quad\text{nor}\quad\mathsf{A}_{\mu[\nu]}^{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\mathsf{a}_{\mu[n\nu]}^{n\lambda}t^{n}

are the Ehrhart series of any union of closed convex polytopes.

The counterexample is also a counterexample for the
\textup{SL}_{2}
case of the conjecture:

(5.2)

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}_{(4)[(3)]}^{(7,5)}(t)=\mathsf{A}_{(3)[(4)]}^{(7,5)}(t)=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}a_{3[4n]}^{[2n+1]}t^{n}.

We outline their proof. Suppose there is a polytope
P
for which (
5.2
) is the Ehrhart series. By Ehrhart reciprocity,
substituting
t
for
t^{-1}
in (
5.2
) gives the Ehrhart series of the interior
P^{\circ}
. After computing the coefficients of
both series explicitly, one obtains

1=\mathsf{ehr}_{P^{\circ}}(1)\leqslant\mathsf{ehr}_{P}(1)=0,

which is a contradiction. If the generating function was instead a positive sum of Ehrhart series, the contradiction would still hold.

However, Kahle and Michałek do not consider the following two relaxed versions of the conjecture. The first is to half-open polytopes
(see Section
5.3
), which renders the Ehrhart reciprocity argument invalid. We remark that Mulmuley already considers
this weakening of the conjecture in
[
Mul09
, §3.1.2]
.

The second is to consider series of the form

\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\#\{v\in C\cap\mathbb{Z}^{w+1}\mid v_{w+1}=n\}t^{n}
for some
(w+1)
-dimensional cone
C
. If the cone is pointed at
the origin, then this is the Ehrhart series of a
w
-dimensional polytope. Otherwise, it is a series of the form
t^{k}\mathsf{Ehr}_{P}(t)
for some
polytope
P
, where
k
is the
(w+1)
st coordinate of the vertex of
C
.

Indeed,
[
KM18
, Thm. 5]
implies that

\mathsf{A}_{(3)[(4)]}^{(7,5)}(t)=\frac{t^{8}+t^{6}+2\,t^{4}+t^{2}+1}{{\left(t^{6}-1\right)}{\left(t^{3}-1\right)}}=(t^{-1}+t^{2})\mathsf{Ehr}_{(-\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}]}(t)

and its reciprocal is
\mathsf{A}_{(3)[(4)]}^{(7,5)}(t^{-1})=(1+t^{3})\mathsf{Ehr}_{[-\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3})}(t)
.
Hence both of these are generating functions of lattice points is the union of two cones. We believe a weakening of Mulmuley’s conjecture remains open.

We end by remarking that one can obtain their generating function from ours
by using the constant term operator

\mathsf{CT}^{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}\lambda^{n}=a_{0}

as

\mathsf{A}_{(3)[(4)]}^{(7,5)}(t)=\mathsf{CT}^{q}\Big(\frac{1}{8q}\sum_{j,\ell}\ell A_{3}(jz,\ell q){\Big|}_{z\mapsto t^{\frac{1}{4}}q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)\,,

where
\ell
ranges over
\{1,-1\}
and
j
ranges over the complex fourth roots of
1
, namely
j\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}
.
The operations required to pass from one generating function to the other do not seem to have geometric interpretations. But note that the generating function
A_{3}(z,q)
is shown to be the generating function of a union of two cones in Section
3.1
.

5.2.
The
\mathsf{h}^{*}
polynomial

A major open problem in Ehrhart theory is to determine which rational functions are Ehrhart series of convex polytopes. A typical line of attack is to study the
\mathsf{h}^{*}
polynomial of polytopes.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
P
be a rational polytope of dimension
w
.
The
denominator of
P
is the minimal integer
d
such that
dP

The Ehrhart series of

is a rational function of the form

\mathsf{Ehr}_{P}(z)=\frac{\mathsf{h}_{P}^{*}(z)}{(1-z^{d})^{w+1}}

When

Theorem 5.3
(
\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b}
-decomposition
[

, Thm. 4.7]

Let

\frac{1+z+\cdots+z^{\ell-1}}{1+z+\cdots+z^{d-1}}\mathsf{h}^{*}_{P}(z)=\mathsf{a}(z)+z^{\ell}\mathsf{b}(z),

\mathsf{a}(z)
and
\mathsf{b}(z)
are palindromic polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients.

The following theorem is a consequence of the

-decomposition.

Theorem 5.4
(
[

, Thm. 4.8]

Let

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathsf{h}^{*}_{S}+\cdots+\mathsf{h}^{*}_{S-i}

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{*}_{s}+\cdots+\mathsf{h}^{*}_{s-i}

To our knowledge, the only generalization of the above results to

If

5.3.
Reciprocity theorems

Often in combinatorics, one is interested in a function or a polynomial
p(x)
which has a combinatorial interpretation at each nonnegative integer value
p(n)
. These include binomial coefficients, the hook-length formula, order polynomials of posets, chromatic polynomials, and many more.
Surprisingly, all of the above also have a combinatorial interpretation at each
negative
integer. For instance,

\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{k(k-1)\cdots 1}

counts the number of
k
-subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and

\binom{-n}{k}=(-1)^{k}\frac{n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)}{k(k-1)\cdots 1}=(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k-1}{k}

counts the number of
k
-multisubsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
This is a
combinatorial reciprocity theorem
. The term was introduced by Stanley in
[
Sta74
]
and explored in depth by
Beck and Sanyal in
[
BS18
]
, where the authors show all above combinatorial reciprocity theorems (and more) as a consequence of Ehrhart theoretic equalities. The most basic of these equalities is as follows.

Theorem 5.5
(Ehrhart reciprocity).

Let
P
be a closed lattice polytope of dimension
w
. Then

\mathsf{Ehr}_{P}(z^{-1})=(-1)^{w+1}\mathsf{Ehr}_{P^{\circ}}(z),

where
P^{\circ}
is the interior of
P
.

Chapoton
[
Cha16
]
gave a refinement for
q
-Ehrhart polynomials, which is then generalized in
[
BK25
,
DLVVW24
]
. We need a slight generalization to half-open polytopes. We state it with our notation (in particular, note that our weighting function is
\mathsf{wt}(v)=2(v_{1}+\cdots+v_{w})
and that
\mathsf{wt}(kv)=k\mathsf{wt}(v)
, and so it falls within
[
DLVVW24
, Def. 1.1]
).

Theorem 5.6
(
[
DLVVW24
]
, Thm. 2.9).

Let
\bar{P}\subseteq\square
be a closed rational convex polytope with faces
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
. Let
P=\bar{P}\setminus\bigcup_{1}^{j}F_{i}
and
P^{\circ}=\bar{P}\setminus\bigcup_{j+1}^{k}F_{i}
be two complementary half-open polytopes. Then

\mathsf{QEhr}_{P}(z^{-1},q^{-1})=(-1)^{w+1}\mathsf{QEhr}_{P^{\circ}}(z,q).

As a corollary, we obtain reciprocity theorem for the quantum Ehrhart series indexed by the partition
\mu
. We provide a self-contained combinatorial proof.

Proposition 5.7
.

For all partitions
\mu\vdash w\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z^{-1},q^{-1})=(-1)^{w+1}z^{2}\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu^{\prime}}(z,q)\penalty\ .

Proof.

The denominator for
\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
from
Theorem
2.8
, with
q
replaced by
q^{-1}

and
z
replaced by
z^{-1}
, is

\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{-w+2i}/z)=\prod_{i=0}^{w}(q^{w-2i}-1/z)=(-z)^{-w-1}\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{w-2i}z)\penalty\ .

Recall from Section
2.1
, that we have
\mathsf{des}(T^{\prime})=w-1-\mathsf{des}(T)

and
\mathsf{maj}(T^{\prime})=\binom{w}{2}-\mathsf{maj}(T)
.
Therefore, we compute directly

\displaystyle\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z^{-1},q^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)}q^{2\mathsf{maj}(T)}(zq^{w})^{-\mathsf{des}(T)}}{\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{-w+2i}/z)}

\displaystyle=(-z)^{w+1}\frac{\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)}q^{w(w-1)-2\mathsf{maj}(T^{\prime})}(zq^{w})^{-(w-1)+\mathsf{des}(T^{\prime})}}{\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{w-2i}z)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{w+1}z^{2}\frac{\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)}q^{-2\mathsf{maj}(T^{\prime})}(zq^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(T^{\prime})}}{\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{w-2i}z)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{w+1}z^{2}\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu^{\prime}}(z,q)\penalty\ .\qed

We should remark that there is another relation

\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q^{-1})=\sum_{h\geqslant 0}s_{\mu}[s_{h}](q^{-1},q)z^{h}=\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)\penalty\ .

This can be seen combinatorially with the Schüztenberger involution

\phi:\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)\rightarrow\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)
since it is possible to show that

\mathsf{des}(\phi(T))=\mathsf{des}(T)
and
|\phi(T)|\mathsf{des}(\phi(T))-2\mathsf{maj}(\phi(T))=-|T|\mathsf{des}(T)+2\mathsf{maj}(T)
.

We now establish a combinatorial reciprocity theorem for
A_{\mu}(z,q)
,
as further evidence towards Conjecture
Conjecture
.

Theorem 5.8
(Combinatorial reciprocity for
A_{\mu}
).

For all partitions
\mu\vdash w\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

A_{\mu}(z^{-1},q^{-1})=(-1)^{w}z^{2}A_{\mu^{\prime}}(z,q).

The proof of this result was provided to us by Guoce Xin.

Lemma 5.9
.

The partial fraction decomposition of
(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
has the following form:

(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\left(\frac{g_{j}(z)}{1-qc_{j}z^{f_{j}}}-\frac{c_{j}z^{f_{j}}g_{j}(z)}{q-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}}\right)

with
g_{j}(z)\in\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)
,
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
,

f_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
with

\sum_{j}g_{j}(z)=0
.

Proof.

The formula for
(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
is given in Theorem
2.8
. We note that
we can factor the denominator
\prod_{i=0}^{w}(1-q^{w-2i}z)

of this expression into pairs of linear factors of the form
(1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}q)(1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}/q)

(except for potentially a single unpaired factor of
1-z
if
w
is even; this factor will be considered to
be part of the numerator
g_{j}(z)
). The exponents
f_{j}
in these expressions are all of the form
{\frac{1}{w-2k}}

for
0\leqslant k<\frac{w}{2}
. Similarly
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
is a power of
e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2w-k}}
.

That is, we have

(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)=\frac{L_{\mu}(z,q)}{\prod_{j}(1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}q)(1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}/q)}\penalty\ ,

where

(5.4)

L_{\mu}(z,q)=(q-q^{-1})(1-z)^{-\delta}\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)}q^{-2\mathsf{maj}(T)}(zq^{w})^{\mathsf{des}(T)}

and

is equal to
1
if

is even and
\delta=0
otherwise.
It is important to note here that
L_{\mu}(z,q^{-1})=-L_{\mu}(z,q)
.
Hence the degree in
q
and the degree in
q^{-1}
of

is the same.

The degree of
q
in
L_{\mu}(z,q)
in Equation (
5.4
) is the maximum value of

1+\sum_{i\in\mathsf{Des}(T)}w-2i
as
T
ranges over
\mathsf{SYT}(\mu)
.
Since

is positive for

and negative otherwise, this sum is bounded above by

1+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor w/2\rfloor}w-2i=1+(w-2)+(w-4)+\cdots+\delta(w\penalty\ \text{odd}).

The degree of the denominator is

greater hence the rational function
(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)

is proper in
q
(that is, the degree in the numerator is strictly smaller
than the degree in the denominator for

Since the expression is proper in
q
, Heavyside’s cover-up algorithm allows us to compute that

(5.5)

(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)=\sum_{j}\left(\frac{g_{j}(z)}{1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}q}+\frac{g^{\prime}_{j}(z)}{1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}/q}\right)\penalty\ ,

where we have that

g_{j}(z)=\frac{L_{\mu}(z,\frac{1}{z^{f_{j}}c_{j}})}{(1-c_{j}^{2}z^{2f_{j}})\prod_{j^{\prime}\neq j}(1-\frac{c_{j^{\prime}}}{c_{j}}z^{f_{j^{\prime}}-f_{j}})(1-c_{j^{\prime}}c_{j}z^{f_{j^{\prime}}+f_{j}})}=-g^{\prime}_{j}(z).

However, we have that the partial fraction decomposition is equal to

(5.6)

(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)=\sum_{j}\left(\frac{g_{j}(z)}{1-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}q}-g_{j}(z)-\frac{c_{j}z^{f_{j}}g_{j}(z)}{q-c_{j}z^{f_{j}}}\right).

Now since
(q-q^{-1})\mathsf{QEhr}_{\mu}(z,q)
is proper we conclude that
-\sum_{j}g_{j}(z)=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We compute the

Lemma 5.10

For all

Corollary 5.11

For all

(Combinatorial reciprocity for

6.
The generating function of
\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients

In this section, we extend our approach to the multivariate generating function of

\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients, namely for a partition
\mu
such that
\ell(\mu)\leqslant n
,

(6.1)

\mathbb{A}_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1}\ldots,y_{m})=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leqslant n}\sum_{\ell(\nu)\leqslant m}\mathsf{a}_{\nu[\mu]}^{\lambda}x^{\lambda}y^{\nu},

where
x^{\lambda}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}x_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
and
y^{\nu}
is similarly defined.

{noteInternal}

Note that, unlike for our
\textup{SL}_{2}
plethysm generating function (
1.2
), the partition
\mu
in (
6.1
)
is the inner partition of the plethysm, not the outer partition.

To compute this generalization of
A_{\mu}(z,q)
, we define an operator
on series in terms MacMahon’s
\Omega_{\geqslant}
operator
(discussed in Section
2.5
).
For a multivariate generating function
F(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
,
let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be
a set of indeterminates and define

\mathsf{PT}^{x_{1}\geqslant x_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant x_{n}}F(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\Omega^{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}}_{\geqslant}F\left(x_{1}\alpha_{1},\frac{x_{2}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n}\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n-1}}\right).

Notice that the effect of this operator when it acts on a single monomial

x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}x_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
is that the result is
0
unless

\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}\geqslant 0

and it acts as the identity otherwise.

In this generating function, the partition
\mu
in
s_{\nu}[s_{\mu}]
is fixed throughout.
The second part of the following statement at least partially answers
a comment made in
[
Tét20
, §3]

that states “The author firmly believes that such recurrence formulas
can be found to compute
h_{m}[h_{n}]
recursively for any
m
.”

Theorem 6.1
.

The generating function
\mathbb{A}_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1}\ldots,y_{m})
is rational.
In particular,
\textup{GL}_{n}
-plethysm coefficients satisfy linear recurrences.

Proof.

Let
\Delta(X_{n})=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})

denote the Vandermonde determinant. Denote the monomial expansion
of the Schur function indexed by the partition
\mu
by the
sum over semi-standard Young tableaux

s_{\mu}[X_{n}]=\sum_{T\in\mathsf{SSYT}_{n}(\mu)}x^{T}.

The Cauchy identity is the rational function

\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{T\in\mathsf{SSYT}_{n}(\mu)}\frac{1}{1-y_{i}x^{T}}=\sum_{\nu}s_{\nu}[Y_{m}]s_{\nu}[s_{\mu}[X_{n}]],

where the sum on the right hand side is over all partitions
\nu
such that
\ell(\nu)\leqslant m
.
Multiply by the Vandermonde determinants in both alphabets to obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(Y_{m})\Delta(X_{n})

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{T\in\mathsf{SSYT}_{n}(\mu)}\frac{1}{1-y_{i}x^{T}}

\displaystyle=\Delta(Y_{m})\Delta(X_{n})\sum_{\nu:\ell(\nu)\leqslant m}s_{\nu}[Y_{m}]s_{\nu}[s_{\mu}[X_{n}]]

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu:\ell(\nu)\leqslant m}\sum_{\lambda:\ell(\lambda)\leqslant n}\mathsf{a}_{\nu[\mu]}^{\lambda}\Delta(Y_{m})s_{\nu}[Y_{m}]\Delta(X_{n})s_{\lambda}[X_{n}].

Now note that

\Delta(X_{n})s_{\lambda}[X_{n}]=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\textnormal{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma(x^{\lambda+\rho^{(n)}})

where
\rho^{(n))}=(n-1,n-2,\ldots,1).

There is exactly one term of this sum that
survives an application of the operator
\mathsf{PT}^{x_{1}\geqslant x_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant x_{n}}
, namely

\mathsf{PT}^{x_{1}\geqslant x_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant x_{n}}\Delta(X_{n})s_{\lambda}[X_{n}]=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}x_{2}^{\lambda_{2}+n-2}\cdots x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}+0}=x^{\lambda}x^{\rho^{(n)}}\penalty\ .

Therefore, applying both
\mathsf{PT}^{x_{1}\geqslant x_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant x_{n}}

and
\mathsf{PT}^{y_{1}\geqslant y_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant y_{m}}
to (
6.2
) gives

x^{\rho^{(n)}}y^{\rho^{(m)}}\mathbb{A}_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}).

Finish by noting the
\mathsf{PT}^{x_{1}\geqslant x_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant x_{n}}
operator preserves rationality.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain a shorter proof of the rationality of Kirillov’s generating function (
5.1
), first established by Mulmuley in
[
Mul09
, Thm. 1.6.1 (a)]
.

Corollary 6.2
.

For fixed partitions,
\lambda
,
\mu
,
\nu
such that
|\lambda|=|\mu|\cdot|\nu|
,
the generating function

{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}^{\lambda}_{\mu[\nu]}(t)=\sum_{r\geqslant 0}\mathsf{a}_{r\mu[\nu]}^{r\lambda}t^{r}

is a rational function in
t

where we have used the notation
r\lambda=(r\lambda_{1},r\lambda_{2},\ldots,r\lambda_{\ell})
.

Proof.

Apply the operator
\mathsf{CT}^{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}}
to the expression

\frac{\mathbb{A}_{\nu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1}\ldots,y_{n})}{(1-tx^{-\lambda})(1-y^{-\mu})}=\sum_{\gamma:\ell(\gamma)\leqslant n}\sum_{\tau:\ell(\tau)\leqslant n}\sum_{r\geqslant 0}\sum_{d\geqslant 0}\mathsf{a}_{\gamma[\nu]}^{\tau}t^{r}x^{\tau-r\lambda}y^{\gamma-d\mu}.

The result is an Elliott rational function by
[
Xin04
, Theorem 3.2]

and the
\mathsf{CT}^{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}}
operator kills all terms except when
\tau=r\lambda
for some
r
.

Next apply the operator
\mathsf{CT}^{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}}
and the only terms
that remain after the application of this operator are those that
satisfy
\gamma=d\mu
and
|r\lambda|=|d\mu|\cdot|\nu|
. Again, the
resulting expression is Elliott rational by
[
Xin04
, Theorem 3.2]

and equal to
{\tilde{\mathsf{A}}}^{\lambda}_{\mu[\nu]}(t)

∎

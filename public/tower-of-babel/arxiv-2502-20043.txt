Sequentially Cohen-Macaulay and pretty clean monomial ideals
Amir Mafi, Rando Rasul Qadir and Hero Saremi

Abstract
. Let R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] be the polynomial ring in n n variables over a field K K and I I be monomial ideal of R R . In this paper, we show that if I I is a generic monomial ideal, then R / I R/I is pretty clean if and only if R / I R/I is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. Furthermore, we prove that this equivalence remains unchanged for some special monomial ideals. Moreover, we provide an example that disproves the conjecture raised in [ 23 , p. 123] regarding generic monomial ideals.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we denote
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
and
I
is a monomial ideal of
R
. We assume, as usual, by
\g(I)
the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. For each monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
, we define the support of
u
to be
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}|a_{i}>0\}
.
A monomial ideal

I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
is called
generic
if for each two distinct minimal generators
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
with the same positive degree in some variable
x_{s}
, there exists a third generator
u_{t}
which is strictly divides
\operatorname{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})
, i.e.,
\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j}))=\operatorname{supp}(\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})}{u_{t}})
.
The concept of generic monomial ideals was introduced in
[
21
]
, extending the earlier definition found in
[
3
]
. For further details, refer also to
[
29
]
.

The cyclic module
R/I
is defined
clean
if there exists a chain of monomial ideals

\mathcal{F}:I=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset I_{2}\subset\ldots\subset I_{r-1}\subset I_{r}=R

such that
I_{i}/I_{i-1}\cong R/{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
with
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is a minimal prime ideal of
I
. In other words, for
i=1,\ldots,r
, there exists a monomial element
u_{i}\in I_{i}
such that
I_{i}=(I_{i-1},u_{i})
and
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(I_{i-1}:u_{i})
. This filtration is called a
monomial prime filtration
of
R/I
. The set
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}\}
is called the support of the prime filtration
\mathcal{F}
. It is known that

\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq V(I)
. By applying
[
5
]
, for a clean filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
R/I
, one has
\operatorname{min}\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})
. Herzog and Popescu in
[
14
]
defined that the module
R/I
is
pretty clean
when there is a prime filtration

\mathcal{F}
with the following property: if
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{j}
, then
j<i
. It is clear that the clean modules are pretty clean and if
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, then
R/I
is pretty clean if and only if
R/I
is clean, see
[
14
, Corollary 3.5]
. However, this equivalence for any monomial ideal is not true, see
[
14
, Example 3.6]
. Furthermore, if there is a prime filtration
\mathcal{F}
with
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})
, then
R/I
is said to be
almost clean
, see
[
16
]
. From
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
, pretty clean modules are almost clean. In addition, Herzog and Popescu
[
14
, Corollary 4.3]
showed that if
R/I
is pretty clean, then
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Suppose
\Delta
is a simplicial complex,
I=I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
which is generated by all squarefree monomials
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{r}}
such that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
is non-face of
\Delta
and
K[\Delta]=R/I
is the Stanley-Reisner ring.
Dress in
[
5
]
showed that
\Delta
is shellable in non-pure case if and only if
K[\Delta]
is clean.

In this paper, we show that if
I
is a generic monomial ideal, then
R/I
is pretty clean if and only if
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. In addition, we give this characterization for some special monomial ideals. Furthermore, we provide an example that disproves the conjecture raised in
[
23
, p. 123]
regarding generic monomial ideals.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we revisit some essential definitions and properties that will be utilized throughout the article.
Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex defined over the vertex set
V=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. The elements of
\Delta
are referred to as faces, while a facet of
\Delta
is a maximal face with respect to inclusion. The dimension of a face
F
is given by
|F|-1
and the dimension of a complex
\Delta
is determined as the maximum dimensions of its facets. If all facets of
\Delta
have the same dimension, then
\Delta
is called
pure
.
The simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
connected
if there exists a sequence of facets
F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
such that
F_{i-1}\cap F_{i}\neq\emptyset
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

For a given simplicial complex
\Delta
on
V
, we define
\Delta^{\vee}
by
\Delta^{\vee}=\{V\setminus A\penalty\ |\penalty\ A\notin\Delta\}
.
The simplicial complex
\Delta^{\vee}
is called the
Alexander dual
of
\Delta
.
For every subset
F\subseteq V
, we set
x_{F}=\prod_{x_{j}\in F}x_{j}
. The
Stanley-Reisner
ideal of
\Delta
over
K
is the ideal
I_{\Delta}
of
R
which is generated by those squarefree monomials
x_{F}
with
F\notin\Delta
. Let
I
be an arbitrary squarefree monomial ideal. Then there is a unique simplicial complex
\Delta
such that
I=I_{\Delta}
. For simplicity,
we often write
I^{\vee}
to denote the ideal
I_{\Delta^{\vee}}
, and we call it the
Alexander dual
of
I
.
If
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal
I=\cap_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{p_{i}}
, where each of the
\mathfrak{p_{i}}
is a monomial prime ideal of
I
, then the ideal
I^{\vee}
is minimally generated by monomials
u_{i}=\prod_{x_{j}\in G(\mathfrak{p_{i}})}x_{j}
.

For a homogeneous ideal
I
, we write
(I_{i})
to denote the ideal generated by the degree
i
elements of
I
and if
I
is generated by squarefree monomials, then we denote by
I_{[i]}
the ideal generated by squarefree monomials of degree
i
of
I
. Herzog and Hibi
[
11
]

introduced the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A monomial ideal

is componentwise linear if and only if
(I_{i})
has a linear resolution for all
i
. If
I
is a squarefree monomials ideal, then

is componentwise linear if and only if

has a linear resolution for all

.

Some familiar classes of ideals which are componentwise linear, for example ideals with linear resolutions, stable ideals, squarefree strongly stable ideals and homogeneous linear quotients ideals (see
[

]
).

We recall the following remarkable result, we will incorporate it in the continuation.

Theorem 1.2

Let
I=I_{\Delta}
be a squarefree monomial ideal of

. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if the Alexander dual

(ii)

2.
Main Results

We start this section by the following definition is due to Stanley
[
24
, Sec. II, 3.9]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. The module
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if there exists a chain of monomial ideals

I_{0}=I\subset I_{1}\subset I_{2}\subset\ldots\subset I_{r}=R,

such that each factor module
I_{i}/I_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay with
\dim I_{i}/I_{i-1}<\dim I_{i+1}/I_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
. Furthermore, if this property is satisfied, then this chain of ideals is uniquely determined.

Example 2.2
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. Then
R/I
is pretty clean, implying that it is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay when at least one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

if
n\leq 3
, see
[
22
, Theorem 1.10]
.

(ii)

if
I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s})
is a squarefree monomial ideal such that either
s\leq 3
or
\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})\cup\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, see
[
18
, Theorem 2.6]
.

(iii)

if
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
is totally ordered with respect to inclusion, see
[
14
, Proposition 5.1]
or
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I
be a generic monomial ideal.
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{Ass}(I)
.

Proof.

We consider the following prime filtration
\mathcal{F}:I=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset I_{2}\subset\ldots\subset I_{r-1}\subset I_{r}=R
such that
I_{i}/I_{i-1}\cong R/{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
. By applying
[
14
, Proposition 7.1]
, there is irredundant decomposition into irreducible components
I=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}
.
Since
I
is a generic monomial ideal by using
[
2
, Proposition 2]
there is a monomial element
u_{j}\in\bigcap_{1\leq i\neq j\leq r}Q_{i}\setminus Q_{j}
such that
(I:u_{j})=\mathfrak{p_{j}}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p_{j}}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
and we deduce that
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. The other inclusion is clear and so we have

\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, as required.

∎

By the previous lemma, we can conclude the following result which was appeared in
[

.

Let

Let

(i)

R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay;

(ii)

(iii)

R/I

Proof.

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)

(iii)\Longrightarrow(iv)

satisfies in

; by details can be found on page

we obtain
\operatorname{min}\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I)
.
Therefore Corollary

(iv)\Longrightarrow(i)

\operatorname{min}\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I)

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
I
be a generic monomial ideal. Then
R/I
is pretty clean if and only if
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

(\Longrightarrow)
. If
R/I
is pretty clean, then
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay by applying
[
14
, Corollary 4.3]
.

(\Longleftarrow)
. Suppose
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. We can consider the following chain of monomial ideals

I_{0}=I\subset I_{1}\subset I_{2}\subset\ldots\subset I_{r}=R
such that all factor module
I_{i}/I_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay with
\dim I_{i}/I_{i-1}<\dim I_{i+1}/I_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
. Thus, for each
i
, we can pick
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{i}/I_{i-1})
such that
\operatorname{height}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})>\operatorname{height}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})>\ldots>\operatorname{height}(\mathfrak{p}_{r})
. By applying
[
14
, Proposition 2.5]
, it follows that
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}\operatorname{Ass}(I_{i}/I_{i-1})
and also by using Lemma
2.3
,
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Therefore we deduce that
\operatorname{height}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\leq\operatorname{height}(\mathfrak{p}_{i-1})

for all
i
and from
[
23
, Corollary 2.7]
we conclude that
R/I
is pretty clean, as required.

∎

Remark 2.7
.

It is known that another interesting class of monomial ideals, specifically squarefree monomial ideals known as matroidal ideals, satisfies the result of Theorem
2.6
. Specifically, if
I
is a matroidal ideal, then
R/I
is pretty clean if and only if
R/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, as stated in
[
9
, Corollary 3.9]
.

In
[
23
, p.123]
, Soleyman Jahan
conjectured
that all generic monomial ideals are pretty clean. However, the following example demonstrates a counterexample to this conjecture: it is not only not pretty clean, but it also fails to be sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. Furthermore, this example illustrates that almost clean ideals are not necessarily pretty clean in general.

Example 2.8
.

Let
n=4
and
I=(x_{1}^{2}x_{2},x_{2}^{2}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4}^{2},x_{3}^{2}x_{4})
. Then it is clear that
I
is generic and
J=\sqrt{I}=(x_{1},x_{3})(x_{2},x_{4})
. Thus

J^{\vee}=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4})
and it is not componentwise linear. Therefore from Theorem
1.2
(iii), we conclude that

J
is neither sequentially Cohen-Macaulay nor pretty clean. Therefore, by applying
[
15
, Theorem 2.6]
, we obtain that

I
is neither sequentially Cohen-Macaulay nor pretty clean.

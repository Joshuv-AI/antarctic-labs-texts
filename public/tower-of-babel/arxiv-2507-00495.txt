On the Frobenius Problem for Some Generalized Fibonacci Subsequences - II
Ryan Azim Shaikh, Amitabha Tripathi

Abstract
For a set A A of positive integers with gcd ⁡ ( A ) = 1 \gcd(A)=1 , let ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle denote the set of all finite linear combinations of elements of A A over the non-negative integers. Then it is well known that only finitely many positive integers do not belong to ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle . The Frobenius number and the genus associated with the set A A is the largest number and the cardinality of the set of integers non-representable by A A . By a generalized Fibonacci sequence { V n } n ≥ 1 \{V_{n}\}_{n\geq 1} we mean any sequence of positive integers satisfying the recurrence V n = V n − 1 + V n − 2 V_{n}=V_{n-1}+V_{n-2} for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . We study the problem of determining the Frobenius number and genus for sets A = { V n , V n + d , V n + 2 ​ d , … } A=\{V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\} for arbitrary n n and even d d .



Keywords.
Embedding dimension, Apéry set, Frobenius number, Genus

1
Introduction

For a given subset
A
of positive integers with
\gcd(A)=1
, we write

S=\langle A\rangle=\big\{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}:a_{i}\in A,x_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\big\}.

We say that
A
is a set of generators for the set
S
. Further,
A
is a minimal set of generators for
S
if no proper subset of
A
generates
S
. If
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is any set of generators of
S
arranged in increasing order, then
A
is a minimal set of generators for
S
if and only if
a_{k+1}\notin\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\rangle
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. It is known that
A=S^{\star}\setminus\big(S^{\star}+S^{\star}\big)
, where
S^{\star}=S\setminus\{0\}
, is the unique minimal set of generators for
S
. The embedding dimension
\textbf{e}(S)
of
S
is the size of the minimal set of generators.

For any set of positive integers
A
with
\gcd(A)=1
, the set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\setminus S
is necessarily finite; we denote this by
\text{G}(S)
. The cardinality of
\text{G}(S)
is the genus of
S
and is denoted by
\textbf{g}(S)
. The largest element in
\text{G}(S)
is the Frobenius number of
S
and is denoted by
\textbf{F}(S)
.

The Apéry set of
S
corresponding to any fixed
a\in S
, denoted by
\text{Ap}(S,a)
, consists of those
n\in S
for which
n-a\notin S
. Thus,
\text{Ap}(S,a)
is the set of minimum integers in
S\cap\textbf{C}
as
C
runs through the complete set of residue classes modulo
a
.

The integers
\textbf{F}(S)
and
\textbf{g}(S)
can be computed from the Apéry set
\text{Ap}(S,a)
of
S
corresponding to any
a\in S
via the following proposition.

Proposition 1.1.1
.

(
[
2
,
10
]
)

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup, let
a\in S
, and let
\text{Ap}(S,a)
be the Apéry set of
S
corresponding to
a
. Then

(i)

\textbf{F}(S)=\max\Big(\text{Ap}(S,a)\Big)-a;

(ii)

\textbf{g}(S)=\frac{1}{a}\left(\sum_{n\in\text{Ap}(S,a)}n\right)-\frac{a-1}{2};

The case where
\textbf{e}(S)=2
is well known and easy to establish. If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then it is easy to see that
\text{Ap}(S,a)=\{bx:0\leq x\leq a-1\}
, and consequently

\textbf{F}(S)=ab-a-b,\quad\textbf{g}(S)=\tfrac{1}{2}(a-1)(b-1)

(1)

by Proposition
1.1.1
.

The Frobenius Problem is the problem of determining the Frobenius number and the genus of a given numerical semigroup, and was first studied by Sylvester, and later by Frobenius; see
[
7
]
for a survey of the problem. Connections with Algebraic Geometry revived interest in Numerical Semigroups around the middle of the twentieth century; we refer to
[
8
]
as a basic textbook on the subject. Curtis
[
3
]
proved that there exists no closed form expression for the Frobenius number of a numerical semigroup
S
with
\textbf{e}(S)>2
. As a consequence, a lot of research has focussed on the Frobenius number of semigroups whose generators are of a particular form. There are three particular instances of such results that are perhaps the closest to our work, and hence bear mentioning. Marín et. al.
[
4
]
determined the Frobenius number and genus of numerical semigroups of the form
\langle F_{i},F_{i+2},F_{i+k}\rangle
, where
i,k\geq 3
. These are called Fibonacci semigroups by the authors. Matthews
[
5
]
considers semigroups of the form
\langle a,a+b,aF_{k-1}+bF_{k}\rangle
where
a>F_{k}
and
\gcd(a,b)=1
. Taking
a=F_{i}
and
b=F_{i+1}
, one gets the semigroup
\langle F_{i},F_{i+2},F_{i+k}\rangle
, considered in
[
4
]
. Thus, such semigroups were termed generalized Fibonacci semigroups by Matthews, who determined the Frobenius number of a generalized Fibonacci semigroup, thereby generalizing the result in
[
4
]
for Frobenius number. Batra et. al.
[
1
]
determined the Frobenius number and genus of numerical semigroups of the form
\langle a,a+b,2a+3b,\ldots,F_{2k-1}a+F_{2k}b\rangle
and
\langle a,a+3b,4a+7b,\ldots,L_{2k-1}a+L_{2k}b\rangle
where
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

By a generalized Fibonacci sequence we mean any sequence
\{V_{n}\}
of positive integers which satisfies the recurrence
V_{n}=V_{n-1}+V_{n-2}
for each
n\geq 3
. A study of some subsequences of a generalized Fibonacci sequence
\{V_{n}\}
was initiated by Panda et. al.
[
6
]
, in which the authors study the semigroup
S
generated by
\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\rangle
when
d
is odd and when
d=2
, and
n
is arbitrary. They show that
S
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
and
\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
. The case of odd
d
is easy to resolve since
\textbf{e}(S)=2
, that is, each
V_{n+kd}\in\langle V_{n},V_{n+d}\rangle
. For
d=2
,
\textbf{e}(S)=\kappa
where
\kappa
satisfies
F_{2(\kappa-1)}\leq V_{n}-1<F_{2\kappa}
. Elements of the Apéry set
\text{Ap}(S,V_{n})
are obtained by applying the Greedy Algorithm to each integer in
\{1,\ldots,V_{n}-1\}
with respect to the sequence
F_{2},F_{4},F_{6},\ldots
. There can be no closed form expression for this in general, but there is a simple expression for the Frobenius number in special cases
V_{n}=F_{n}
and
V_{n}=L_{n}
, and a recurrence relation satisfied by
\textbf{g}(S)
in the special case
V_{n}=F_{n}
.

This paper completes the study of the cases initiated in
[
6
]
by extending the results of
d=2
to even
d
. Throughout this paper, let
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
, where
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
,
\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
and
d
is even. The main results are similar to the ones in
[
6
]
; we list them below:

(i)

The embedding dimension
e(S)=\kappa
, where
\kappa
is the smallest positive integer for which
F_{\kappa d}/F_{d}\geq V_{n}
; refer Theorem
3.1.1
.

(ii)

The Apéry set
\text{Ap}(S,V_{n})=\left\{V_{n+d}\,x-\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(k-1)d}\,x}{F_{kd}}\right\rfloor V_{n}:1\leq x\leq V_{n}-1\right\}\cup\{0\}
; refer Theorem
3.3.3
and Proposition
3.2.5
, part (iii).

(iii)

The Frobenius number
\textbf{F}(S)
in the general case (refer Theorem
3.4.1
), and in the special cases when
V_{n}=F_{n}
and
V_{n}=L_{n}
(refer Corollary
3.4.2
).

(iv)

A recurrence for the genus
\textbf{g}(S)
in some special cases when
V_{n}=F_{n}
and
V_{n}=L_{n}
(refer Proposition
3.4.3
).

2
Preliminary Results

A generalized Fibonacci sequence
\langle V_{n}\rangle_{n\geq 1}
is defined by

V_{n}=V_{n-1}+V_{n-2},\;\;n\geq 3,\quad\;\text{with}\;V_{1}=a,V_{2}=b,

(2)

where
a
and
b
are any positive integers. Two important special cases are (i) Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
when
a=b=1
, and (ii) Lucas sequence
\{L_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
when
a=1
and
b=3
. It is customary to extend these definitions to
F_{0}=F_{2}-F_{1}=0
and
L_{0}=L_{2}-L_{1}=2
. Binet’s formula give explicit values for
F_{n}
and
L_{n}
:

F_{n}=\frac{{\alpha}^{n}-{\beta}^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\qquad L_{n}={\alpha}^{n}+{\beta}^{n},

where
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
and
\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2
are the roots of the equation
x^{2}-x-1=0
. From these formulae, it is easy to see that
F_{2n}=L_{n}F_{n}
, and easy to derive

F_{n}^{2}-F_{n+1}F_{n-1}=(-1)^{n-1},\quad L_{n}^{2}-L_{n+1}L_{n-1}=(-1)^{n}\cdot 5,\;\;n\geq 1.

(3)

The following bounds for
F_{m}V_{n}
for the cases
V_{n}=F_{n}
and
V_{n}=L_{n}
when
d
is even are useful in determining
\textbf{e}(S)
; see Theorem
3.1.1
.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers, with
m\geq n
.

(i)

If
n\geq 3
, then

F_{m+n-2}<F_{m}F_{n}<F_{m+n-1}.

If
n=2
, then
F_{m+n-2}=F_{m}F_{n}<F_{m+n-1}
.

If
n=1
, then
F_{m+n-2}=F_{m}F_{n}
if and only if
m=2
and
F_{m}F_{n}=F_{m+n-1}
holds for each
m
.

(ii)

If
n\geq 3
, then

F_{m+n-1}<F_{m}L_{n}<F_{m+n}.

If
n=2
, then
F_{m+n}\leq F_{m}L_{n}<F_{m+n+1}
and
F_{m}L_{n}=F_{m+n}
if and only if
m=2
.

If
n=1
, then
F_{m}L_{n}=F_{m+n-1}
holds for each
m
and
F_{m}L_{n}=F_{m+n}
if and only if
m=1
.

Proof.

(i)

The cases
n=1,2,3
are easily verified. Assume the inequality holds for each positive integer
<n
, so that we have
F_{m+k-2}<F_{m}F_{k}<F_{m+k-1}
for
k=n-1
and
k=n-2
. Adding the two inequalities gives the desired inequality for
F_{m}F_{n}
.

(ii)

The cases
n=1,2,3
are easily verified. Assume the inequality holds for each positive integer
<n
, so that we have
F_{m+k-1}<F_{m}L_{k}<F_{m+k}
for
k=n-1
and
k=n-2
. Adding the two inequalities gives the desired inequality for
F_{m}L_{n}
.

\blacksquare

The following identities connecting generalized Fibonacci sequences with the Fibonacci sequence are useful in our subsequent work.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

(i)

For positive integers
m
and
n
,

V_{m+n}=F_{n-1}V_{m}+F_{n}V_{m+1}.

In particular,
F_{n}\mid F_{kn}
for each
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

For positive integers
m,n,d
,

F_{n}V_{m+n+d}-F_{n+d}V_{m+n}=(-1)^{n-1}F_{d}V_{m}.

In particular, for
k\geq 1
,

F_{d}V_{n+kd}-F_{kd}V_{n+d}=(-1)^{d-1}F_{(k-1)d}V_{n}.

(iii)

If
k\geq 2
, then

F_{kd}-(L_{d}-1)F_{(k-1)d}=F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}.

(iv)

If
k\geq 3
, then

\left\lfloor\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{(k-1)d}}\right\rfloor=L_{d}-1.

(v)

If
k-1\geq t\geq 2
, then

F_{kd}=(L_{d}-1)F_{(k-1)d}+(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k-2}F_{id}+(L_{d}-1)F_{(t-1)d}-F_{(t-2)d}.

In particular,

F_{kd}=(L_{d}-1)F_{(k-1)d}+(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=2}^{k-2}F_{id}+(L_{d}-1)F_{d}.

(vi)

If
k\geq t\geq 1
, then

(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k}V_{n+id}=\left(V_{n+(k+1)d}-V_{n+kd}\right)-\left(V_{n+td}-V_{n+(t-1)d}\right).

In particular,

(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{n+id}=\left(V_{n+(k+1)d}-V_{n+kd}\right)-\left(V_{n+d}-V_{n}\right).

Proof.

(i)

We fix
m
and induct on
n
. The case
n=1
is an identity and the case
n=2
follows from the definition of
\{V_{n}\}
. Assuming the result for all positive integers less than
n
, we have

\displaystyle V_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{m+(n-1)}+V_{m+(n-2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(F_{n-2}V_{m}+F_{n-1}V_{m+1}\big)+\big(F_{n-3}V_{m}+F_{n-2}V_{m+1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(F_{n-2}+F_{n-3}\big)V_{m}+\big(F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}\big)V_{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{n-1}V_{m}+F_{n}V_{m+1}.

This completes the proof by induction.

In particular, with
V_{n}=F_{n}
and
m=(k-1)n
, we have

F_{kn}=F_{(k-1)n}F_{n-1}+F_{(k-1)n+1}F_{n}.

So if
F_{n}\mid F_{(k-1)n}
, then
F_{n}\mid F_{kn}
. Hence,
F_{n}\mid F_{kn}
for each
k\geq 1
by induction.

(ii)

We first prove the case
d=1
, then use this to prove the general case.

By part (i) and eqn. (
3
), we have

\displaystyle F_{n}V_{m+n+1}-F_{n+1}V_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{n}\big(F_{n}V_{m}+F_{n+1}V_{m+1}\big)-F_{n+1}\big(F_{n-1}V_{m}+F_{n}V_{m+1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(F_{n}^{2}-F_{n+1}F_{n-1}\big)V_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}V_{m}.

This proves the case
d=1
.

To prove the general case, by part (i), we have

V_{m+n+d}=F_{d-1}V_{m+n}+F_{d}V_{m+n+1}\;\;\text{and}\;\;F_{n+d}=F_{d-1}F_{n}+F_{d}F_{n+1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle F_{n}V_{m+n+d}-F_{n+d}V_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{n}\left(F_{d-1}V_{m+n}+F_{d}V_{m+n+1}\right)-\left(F_{d-1}F_{n}+F_{d}F_{n+1}\right)V_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{d}\left(F_{n}V_{m+n+1}-F_{n+1}V_{m+n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}F_{d}V_{m}.

This proves the general case.

Note that the particular case holds for
k=1
. For
k>1
, the transformation
m\mapsto n
,
n\mapsto d
,
d\mapsto(k-1)d
yields the desired identity.

(iii)

Applying part (ii) to
V=F
and
n=d
, and replacing
k
by
k-1
, we have

F_{kd}=\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}F_{2d}-\frac{F_{(k-2)d}}{F_{d}}F_{d}=L_{d}F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}=(L_{d}-1)F_{(k-1)d}+\big(F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}\big).

(4)

(iv)

Since
0<F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}<F_{(k-1)d}
for
k\geq 3
, this follows upon dividing both sides of eqn. (
4
) by
F_{(k-1)d}
.

(v)

Replacing
k
by
i
in the identity in part (iii), then summing from
i=t
to
i=k
, we have

\sum_{i=t}^{k}\left(F_{id}-(L_{d}-1)F_{(i-1)d}\right)=\sum_{i=t}^{k}\left(F_{(i-1)d}-F_{(i-2)d}\right).

Thus,

\sum_{i=t}^{k}F_{id}-(L_{d}-1)\sum_{i=t}^{k}F_{(i-1)d}=F_{kd}-(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k-1}F_{id}-(L_{d}-1)F_{(t-1)d}=F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(t-2)d},

which gives the desired result.

(vi)

From part (v),

(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k}F_{id}=\left(F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}\right)-\left(F_{td}-F_{(t-1)d}\right).

Replacing
k
by
i
in the identity in part (ii), then summing from
i=t
to
i=k
and multiplying both sides by
L_{d}-2
, we have

\displaystyle(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k}V_{n+id}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}V_{n+d}-(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t}^{k}\frac{F_{(i-1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left(F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}\right)-\left(F_{td}-F_{(t-1)d}\right)\Big)\frac{V_{n+d}}{F_{d}}

\displaystyle-\Big(\left(F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}\right)-\left(F_{(t-1)d}-F_{(t-2)d}\right)\Big)\frac{V_{n}}{F_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n+d}-\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n}\right)-\left(\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n+d}-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{F_{td}}{F_{d}}V_{n+d}-\frac{F_{(t-1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n}\right)+\left(\frac{F_{(t-1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n+d}-\frac{F_{(t-2)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(V_{n+(k+1)d}-V_{n+kd}\right)-\left(V_{n+td}-V_{n+(t-1)d}\right).

\blacksquare

3
The Case where
d
is even

The main results of this paper are contained in this Section. We begin by proving an explicit formula for the embedding dimension in Theorem
3.1.1
in Subsection
3.1
. We follow this by introducing the Greedy Algorithm in Subsection
3.2
, and apply it to compute a specific Apéry set in Subsection
3.3

3.1
Embedding Dimension

Theorem 3.1.1
.

Let
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
, where
d
is even and
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
. Then embedding dimension of
S
is given by

e(S)=\kappa,

where
\kappa
is the smallest positive integer for which
F_{\kappa d}/F_{d}\geq V_{n}
.

Proof.

We claim that
\{V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots,V_{n+(\kappa-1)d}\}
is a minimal set of generators for
S
, where
\kappa
is the smallest positive integer for which
F_{\kappa d}/F_{d}\geq V_{n}
. By the characterization of minimal set of generators in Section
1
, we must therefore show:

(i)

V_{n+kd}\in\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots,V_{n+(\kappa-1)d}\rangle
for each
k\geq\kappa
, and

(ii)

V_{n+kd}\notin\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots,V_{n+(k-1)d}\rangle
for
1\leq k\leq\kappa-1
.

Let
k\geq\kappa
. By Proposition
2.1.2
, part (ii), we can write

\displaystyle V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}\,V_{n}+\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\,V_{n+d}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\lambda V_{n+d}-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}\right)V_{n}+\left(\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-\lambda V_{n}\right)V_{n+d}\;\;\text{ for any }\lambda\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore,
V_{n+kd}\in\langle V_{n},V_{n+d}\rangle
if there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
for which

\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}\,V_{n+d}}\leq\lambda\leq\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}\,V_{n}}.

If
F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}\,V_{n+d}\leq 1
, then
\lambda=1
works because of the definition of
\kappa
. If
F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}\,V_{n+d}>1
, then

\lambda\,V_{n}<\left(\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}\,V_{n+d}}+1\right)V_{n}<2\,\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}\,V_{n+d}}\,V_{n}<2\,\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}<\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}

for
\lambda=\left\lceil F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}\,V_{n+d}\right\rceil
where the last inequality holds because
2\,F_{(k-1)d}<F_{kd}
. This proves claim (i).

Let
1<k<\kappa
. To prove claim (ii), suppose

\displaystyle V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}V_{n+id}

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}\,V_{n}+a_{1}\,V_{n+d}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\left(-\frac{F_{(i-1)d}}{F_{d}}\,V_{n}+\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\,V_{n+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a_{0}-\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\frac{F_{(i-1)d}}{F_{d}}\right)V_{n}+\left(a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)V_{n+d}.

with each
a_{i}\geq 0
.

Note that
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})=1
since any common divisor of
V_{n}
and
V_{n+d}
must divide each of the terms
V_{n+kd}
due to eqn. (
5
). Thus, from eqn. (
5
) and eqn. (
6
), there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}+t\,V_{n}=a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}

(7)

\displaystyle-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}-t\,V_{n+d}=a_{0}-\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\frac{F_{(i-1)d}}{F_{d}}.

(8)

In eqn. (
7
),
t<0
reduces the left-side to a negative quantity, whereas the right-side is non-negative. Thus,
t\geq 0
. We rewrite eqn. (
7
) and eqn. (

) in the form

\displaystyle\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{kd}}-1\right)=t\,V_{n}-a_{1}

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}a_{i}\frac{F_{(i-1)d}}{F_{(k-1)d}}-1\right)=t\,V_{n+d}+a_{0}.

(10)

With

, and choosing

for

\blacksquare

Corollary 3.1.2
.

(i)

If

is even and

, the embedding dimension of

is given by

(ii)

If

is even and
\gcd(L_{n},F_{d})=1
, the embedding dimension of
S_{2}=\langle L_{n},L_{n+d},L_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
is given by

\textbf{e}(S_{2})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }n=1,\\
1+\left\lceil\frac{n}{d}\right\rceil&\mbox{ if }n>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is a direct application of Lemma
2.1.1
and Theorem
3.1.1
.

(i)

If
d=2
or

,
n\leq 2
, then
\kappa
is the least positive integer satisfying
\kappa d\geq n+d-2
. Hence
\kappa=1+\left\lceil\frac{n-2}{d}\right\rceil
for these cases.

If

and

, then
\kappa
is the least positive integer satisfying

for these cases.

(ii)

If

in this case.

If

3.2
Some Results Based on the Greedy Algorithm

Definition 3.2.1
.

(The Greedy Algorithm)

For positive integers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},C
with
\gcd(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\mid C
, consider the equation

c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}=C.

(12)

The
greedy solution
is given by

x_{k}^{\star}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{C}{c_{n}}\right\rfloor&\mbox{ for }k=n;\\[8.0pt]
\left\lfloor\frac{C-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}^{\star}}{c_{k}}\right\rfloor&\mbox{ for }k=n-1,n-2,\ldots,1.\end{cases}

We then write
Greedy
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n};C)=x_{1}^{\star},\ldots,x_{n}^{\star}
.

Definition 3.2.2
.

Fix
x\in\{1,\ldots,V_{n}-1\}
, and let
k
be such that
F_{kd}/F_{d}\leq x<F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}
. Let

{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}=\text{\sc Greedy}(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};x).

Set

s(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+id}.

Proposition 3.2.3
.

Let
d
be even,
x
be a positive integer and
k>1
. Suppose

\text{\sc Greedy}(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};x)={\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}.

(i)

Then
0\leq{\lambda}_{i}\leq L_{d}-1
for
1\leq i<k
.

(ii)

If
{\lambda}_{i}={\lambda}_{j}=L_{d}-1
for some
i<j<k
, then
{\lambda}_{t}<L_{d}-2
for some
t
satisfying
i<t<j
. Moreover, there does not exist
i<k-1
such that
{\lambda}_{i}={\lambda}_{i+1}=L_{d}-1
.

Proof.

(i)

We have

{\lambda}_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{F_{kd}/F_{d}}\right\rfloor,\quad{\lambda}_{j}=\left\lfloor\frac{x-\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}}{F_{jd}/F_{d}}\right\rfloor,\>\>j=k-1,k-2,\ldots,1.

(13)

by Definition
3.2.1
.

By eqn. (
13
) and Proposition
2.1.2
, part (iv), we have

{\lambda}_{j}=\left\lfloor\frac{x-\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}}{F_{jd}/F_{d}}\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(j+1)d}/F_{d}}{F_{jd}/F_{d}}\right\rfloor=L_{d}-1,

for
2\leq j\leq k-1
, and

{\lambda}_{1}=\left\lfloor\frac{x-\sum_{i=2}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}}{F_{d}/F_{d}}\right\rfloor=x-\sum_{i=2}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}<\frac{F_{2d}}{F_{d}}=L_{d}.

This completes the proof of part (i).

(ii)

Suppose
{\lambda}_{i}={\lambda}_{j}=L_{d}-1
for some
i<j
and
{\lambda}_{t}\geq L_{d}-2
for
i<t<j
. Then

x-\sum_{t=j+1}^{k}{\lambda}_{t}\frac{F_{td}}{F_{d}}\geq(L_{d}-2)\sum_{t=i}^{j}\frac{F_{td}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{jd}}{F_{d}}=\frac{F_{(j+1)d}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{(i-1)d}}{F_{d}}\geq\frac{F_{(j+1)d}}{F_{d}}

(14)

using Proposition
2.1.2
, part (v). This contradicts the definition of
{\lambda}_{j+1}
.

If
{\lambda}_{i}={\lambda}_{i+1}=L_{d}-1
for some
i<k-1
, the argument in eqn. (
14
) with
j=i+1
again leads to the same contradiction. This proves part (ii).

\blacksquare

Proposition 3.2.4
.

Let
d
be even and
k>1
. Suppose

\text{\sc Greedy}(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d})={\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}.

(i)

{\lambda}_{i}=\begin{cases}L_{d}-1&\mbox{ for }i=1,k;\\
L_{d}-2&\mbox{ for }2\leq i\leq k-1.\end{cases}

(ii)

\text{\sc Greedy}(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}-1)={\lambda}_{1}-1,\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}.

(iii)

s\left(F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}-1\right)=V_{n+(k+1)d}-V_{n+d}+V_{n}.

Proof.

(i)

Observe that
{\lambda}_{k}=L_{d}-1
follows from Proposition
2.1.2
, part (iv) and Definition
3.2.1
.

We now prove that
{\lambda}_{i}=L_{d}-2
for
2\leq i\leq k-1
by induction. We have

{\lambda}_{k-1}=\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(k+1)d}-(L_{d}-1)\,F_{kd}}{F_{(k-1)d}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{(k-1)d}}\right\rfloor=L_{d}-2

from Proposition
2.1.2
, parts (iv) and (v), except that the last equality gives
L_{d}-1
when
k=2
.

Assuming
{\lambda}_{j}=L_{d}-2
for some
j\in\{i+1,\ldots,k-1\}
, we have

{\lambda}_{i}=\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(k+1)d}-(L_{d}-1)\,F_{kd}-(L_{d}-2)\sum_{j=i+1}^{k-1}F_{jd}}{F_{id}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(i+1)d}-F_{id}}{F_{id}}\right\rfloor=L_{d}-2

from Proposition
2.1.2
, parts (iv) and (v).

Finally, we have

{\lambda}_{1}=\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(k+1)d}-(L_{d}-1)\,F_{kd}-(L_{d}-2)\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}F_{jd}}{F_{d}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{F_{2d}-F_{d}}{F_{d}}\right\rfloor=L_{d}-1

from Proposition
2.1.2
, part (v).

(ii)

Write
\text{\sc Greedy}(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}-1)={\lambda}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}^{\star}
. Then
{\lambda}_{k}^{\star}={\lambda}_{k}
because

. Moreover, the numerator when computing

is

; this is not a multiple of
F_{id}
for

. Hence
{\lambda}_{i}^{\star}={\lambda}_{i}
for

. It follows that

.

(iii)

We have

\displaystyle s\left(F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+id}-V_{n+d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{n+id}+V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(V_{n+(k+1)d}-V_{n+kd}-V_{n+d}+V_{n}\right)+V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{n+(k+1)d}-V_{n+d}+V_{n}.

\blacksquare

We are now in a position to determine the Apéry set for the case
d
even. We show that the elements in this set are obtained by applying the Greedy Algorithm to an equation involving terms of the form

.

Proposition 3.2.5
.

Fix

(i)

(15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(L_{d}-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}V_{n+id}+(L_{d}-2)V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle\frac{L_{d}-1}{L_{d}-2}\Big(\big(V_{n+kd}-V_{n+(k-1)d}\big)-\big(V_{n+d}-V_{n}\big)\Big)+(L_{d}-2)V_{n+kd}

using Proposition
2.1.2

(iii)

2.1.2

\displaystyle=

Proposition 3.2.6

3.3

Theorem 3.3.1
.

For any sequence
{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{m}
of nonnegative integers, not all zero,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}.

Proof.

We induct on the sum
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}
, where we may assume
{\alpha}_{m}\neq 0
without loss of generality. If
\sigma=1
, then
m={\alpha}_{1}=1
and the two sides are equal. For some positive integer
\sigma
, assume the result holds whenever the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}<\sigma
.

Let
F_{kd}/F_{d}\leq\sigma<F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}
and let
{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}=\text{\sc Greedy}(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};\sigma)
. Suppose
{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{m}
is any sequence of nonegative integers such that
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}
; we may assume that
{\alpha}_{m}\geq 1
. Note that
m\leq k
, for if
m>k
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}\geq F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}>\sigma
.

If
m=k
, then
1\leq{\alpha}_{k}\leq{\lambda}_{k}
. By Induction Hypothesis,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}-V_{n+kd}.

Since

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\right)=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+id}-V_{n+kd}=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)-V_{n+kd},

we have

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}.

This proves the Proposition when
m=k
.

Suppose
m<k
. By Induction Hypothesis,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{md}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}-V_{n+md}.

(19)

Two cases arise: (I)
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}-F_{md}\geq F_{kd}
, and (II)
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}-F_{md}<F_{kd}
.

Case
(I): Let
{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}^{\prime}=\text{\sc Greedy}\left(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{kd}/F_{d};\sigma-F_{md}/F_{d}\right)
. Then

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{md}}{F_{d}}\right)=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}V_{n+id}.

(20)

If we replace
{\lambda}_{m}^{\prime}
by
{\lambda}_{m}^{\prime}+1
and retain the other
{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}
, and apply the case
m=k
discussed above, we get

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{md}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}V_{n+id}+V_{n+md}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}.

from eqn. (
19
) and eqn. (
20
). This proves Case (I).

Case
(II): Since
\sigma-F_{md}/F_{d}\geq F_{kd}/F_{d}-F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}>F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}
, we have

{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k-1}^{\prime}=\text{\sc Greedy}\left(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d};\sigma-F_{md}/F_{d}\right).

Note that
\sigma-F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}
lies between
F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}
and
F_{kd}/F_{d}
. Let

{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k-1}^{\prime\prime}=\text{\sc Greedy}\left(1,F_{2d}/F_{d},F_{3d}/F_{d},\ldots,F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d};\sigma-F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}\right).

We claim that one of the following cases must arise: (i)
{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}=L_{d}-2
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
; (ii) there exists
r\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
such that
{\lambda}_{r}^{\prime\prime}=L_{d}-1
and
{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}=L_{d}-2
for
i\in\{r+1,\ldots,k-1\}
.

If neither of these cases is true, then there must exist
t\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
such that
{\lambda}_{t}^{\prime\prime}<L_{d}-2
and
{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}=L_{d}-2
for
i\in\{t+1,\ldots,k-1\}
. But then

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{t}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{\sigma-F_{(k-1)d}/F_{d}-\sum_{i=t+1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}F_{id}/F_{d}}{F_{td}/F_{d}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}-(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=t+1}^{k-1}F_{id}}{F_{td}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}-\big(F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(t+1)d}+F_{td}\big)}{F_{td}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{F_{(t+1)d}-F_{td}}{F_{td}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle L_{d}-2

by Proposition
2.1.2
, parts (iv), (v). This contradiction proves the claim.

In Case (i), using Proposition
2.1.2
, part (v), we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}=(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}=\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}-1<\sigma-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}},

contradicting the fact that
{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k-1}^{\prime\prime}
is the sequence determined by the Greedy Algorithm for
\sigma-F_{(k-1)d}
and
\sigma\geq F_{kd}/F_{d}
. This rules out Case (i).

In Case (ii), using Proposition
2.1.2
, part (vi), we get

\displaystyle s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)+V_{n+(k-1)d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}V_{n+id}+V_{n+(k-1)d}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}V_{n+id}+(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=r}^{k-1}V_{n+id}+V_{n+rd}+V_{n+(k-1)d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}V_{n+id}+\big(V_{n+kd}-V_{n+(k-1)d}-V_{n+rd}+V_{n+(r-1)d}\big)

\displaystyle+V_{n+rd}+V_{n+(k-1)d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}V_{n+id}+V_{n+kd}+V_{n+(r-1)d}.

Using Proposition
2.1.2
, part (v), we have

\displaystyle\sigma-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+(L_{d}-2)\sum_{i=r}^{k-1}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{rd}}{F_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}-F_{rd}+F_{(r-1)d}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{rd}}{F_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{kd}-F_{(k-1)d}+F_{(r-1)d}}{F_{d}}.

By the Induction Hypothesis,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{md}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}V_{n+id}-V_{n+(k-1)d}+V_{n+md}.

Applying the case
m=k
discussed above to
s\left(\sigma\right)
and using eqn. (
21
), we have

\displaystyle s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{(r-1)d}}{F_{d}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}V_{n+id}+V_{n+kd}+V_{n+(r-1)d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)+V_{n+(k-1)d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}+\frac{F_{md}}{F_{d}}\right)+V_{n+(k-1)d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\lambda}_{i}^{\prime}V_{n+id}+V_{n+md}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}-\frac{F_{md}}{F_{d}}\right)+V_{n+md}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}.

This completes Case (ii), and the proof.

\blacksquare

Lemma 3.3.2
.

For any positive integer
m
,
s(m)<s(m+1)
.

Proof.

We induct on
m
. Note that
V_{n+d}=s(1)<s(2)=2V_{n+d}
. Assume
s(i-1)<s(i)
for
1\leq i\leq m
. If
m=F_{kd}/F_{d}-1
for some
k
, then
s(m)=V_{n+kd}-V_{n+d}+V_{n}<V_{n+kd}=s(m+1)
by Proposition
3.2.4
, part (iii). Otherwise
F_{kd}/F_{d}\leq m<F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}-1
, and so
s(m)=s(m-F_{kd}/F_{d})+V_{n+kd}
while
s(m+1)=s(m+1-F_{kd}/F_{d})+V_{n+kd}
. By Induction Hypothesis,
s(m-F_{kd}/F_{d})<s(m+1-F_{kd}/F_{d})
, so that
s(m)<s(m+1)
, proving the Proposition by induction.

\blacksquare

Theorem 3.3.3
.

Let
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
, where
d
is even. The Apéry set for
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
is given by

\text{Ap}(S,V_{n})=\{s(x):1\leq x\leq V_{n}-1\}\cup\{0\}.

Proof.

For
x\in\{1,\ldots,V_{n}-1\}
, we show that
s(x)
is the least positive integer in
S
that is congruent to
V_{n+d}\,x
modulo
V_{n}
. This proves the result since
\{V_{n+d}\,x:1\leq x\leq V_{n}-1\}
is the set of non-zero residues modulo
V_{n}
as
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})=1
.

Suppose
s\in S
is congruent to
V_{n+d}\,x
modulo
V_{n}
. Then
s=\sum_{i\geq 0}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}
, with each
{\alpha}_{i}\geq 0
. Since
s\equiv V_{n+d}\,x\pmod{V_{n}}
and
V_{n+id}\equiv V_{n+d}F_{id}/F_{d}\pmod{V_{n}}
by Proposition
2.1.2
, part (ii), we have
\sum_{i\geq 1}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}\equiv x\pmod{V_{n}}
as
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})=1
. Since
x\leq V_{n}-1
, we have
x\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}{\alpha}_{i}F_{id}/F_{d}
, so that

s(x)\leq s\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}{\alpha}_{i}\frac{F_{id}}{F_{d}}\right)\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+id}\leq s

by Theorem
3.3.1
and Lemma
3.3.2
.

\blacksquare

Computation of
\textbf{g}(S)
is difficult in the general case. In the following result we compute the genus in the special case of Fibonacci and Lucas subsequences. The result is in terms of the
k^{\text{th}}
term of sequences that jointly use first order recurrences, and that can be explicitly solved.

Proposition 3.4.3
.

For
k\geq 1
, define
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
as follows:

A_{k}=\sum_{x=1}^{\tfrac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-1}\>s(x),\quad B_{k}=\sum_{x=1}^{\tfrac{F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}\>s(x).

(22)

Then
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
satisfy the joint first order recurrences given by

\displaystyle A_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-1)A_{k}+B_{k}+(L_{d}-1)V_{n+(k+1)d}\frac{F_{(k+2)d}-F_{kd}}{2F_{d}};

(23)

\displaystyle B_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-2)A_{k}+B_{k}+(L_{d}-2)V_{n+(k+1)d}\frac{F_{(k+2)d}-F_{(k+1)d-F_{kd}}}{2F_{d}}.

(24)

with
A_{1}=\frac{1}{2}V_{n+d}L_{d}(L_{d}-1)
and
B_{1}=\frac{1}{2}V_{n+d}(L_{d}-1)(L_{d}-2)
.

Proof.

Let
x
be a positive integer. Then
F_{kd}/F_{d}\leq x<F_{(k+1)d}/F_{d}
for some positive integer
k
. Thus,

i\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\leq x<(i+1)\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}

for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,L_{d}-1\}
. From the definition of
s
,

s(x)=i\,V_{n+kd}+s\left(x-i\,\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\right).

(25)

Therefore, from eqn. (
25
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{i\,\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)+\sum_{x=\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}}^{2\,\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)+\cdots+\sum_{x=\frac{(i-1)\,F_{kd}}{F_{d}}}^{i\,\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{k-1}+\left(\tfrac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n+kd}+A_{k-1}\right)+\left(\tfrac{2F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n+kd}+A_{k-1}\right)+\cdots+\left(\tfrac{(i-1)F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n+kd}+A_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,A_{k-1}+\frac{i(i-1)}{2}\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n+kd}.

We have

F_{(k+2)d}=(L_{d}-1)F_{(k+1)d}+F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}

(27)

by Proposition
2.1.2
, part (iii).

Hence, from eqn. (
25
) and eqn. (
26
), and using eqn. (
27
), we have

\displaystyle A_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{\frac{F_{(k+2)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{(L_{d}-1)\frac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)+\sum_{x=(L_{d}-1)\frac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}}^{\frac{F_{(k+2)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((L_{d}-1)A_{k}+\tfrac{(L_{d}-2)(L_{d}-1)}{2}\tfrac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n+(k+1)d}\right)+(L_{d}-1)\left(\tfrac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-\tfrac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\right)V_{n+(k+1)d}

\displaystyle+\sum_{x=0}^{\frac{F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-1)A_{k}+B_{k}+\frac{L_{d}-1}{2F_{d}}V_{n+(k+1)d}\left(L_{d}\,F_{(k+1)d}-2F_{kd}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-1)A_{k}+B_{k}+(L_{d}-1)V_{n+(k+1)d}\frac{F_{(k+2)d}-F_{kd}}{2F_{d}}.

The derivation of the formula for
B_{k+1}
follows along similar lines. We have

\displaystyle B_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{\frac{F_{(k+2)d}-F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{(L_{d}-2)\frac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)+\sum_{x=(L_{d}-2)\frac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}}^{\frac{F_{(k+2)d}-F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((L_{d}-2)A_{k}+\tfrac{(L_{d}-3)(L_{d}-2)}{2}\tfrac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n+(k+1)d}\right)+(L_{d}-2)\left(\tfrac{F_{(k+1)d}}{F_{d}}-\tfrac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}\right)V_{n+(k+1)d}

\displaystyle+\sum_{x=0}^{\frac{F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-2)A_{k}+B_{k}+\frac{L_{d}-2}{2F_{d}}V_{n+(k+1)d}\left((L_{d}-1)F_{(k+1)d}-2F_{kd}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L_{d}-2)A_{k}+B_{k}+(L_{d}-2)V_{n+(k+1)d}\frac{F_{(k+2)d}-F_{(k+1)d}-F_{kd}}{2F_{d}}.

Moreover,

A_{1}=\sum_{x=1}^{\frac{F_{2d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)=\sum_{x=1}^{L_{d}-1}V_{n+d}\,x=\frac{1}{2}(L_{d}-1)L_{d}V_{n+d}

\blacksquare

Let
X

k

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{X}s(x)

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{a\,\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)+\sum_{x=a\,\frac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}}^{\frac{a\,F_{kd}+b\,F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}-1}s(x)+\sum_{x=\frac{a\,F_{kd}+b\,F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}}^{X}s(x)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aA_{k-1}+\tfrac{(a-1)a}{2}\tfrac{F_{kd}}{F_{d}}V_{n+kd}+ab\tfrac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n+kd}+bA_{k-2}+\tfrac{(b-1)b}{2}\tfrac{F_{(k-1)d}}{F_{d}}V_{n+(k-1)d}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}}{F_{d}}\left(aV_{n+kd}+bV_{n+(k-1)d}\right)+B_{k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aA_{k-1}+B_{k-2}+bA_{k-2}+\tfrac{a}{F_{d}}\left(\tfrac{a-1}{2}F_{kd}+(b+1)F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}\right)V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{b}{F_{d}}\left(\tfrac{b+1}{2}F_{(k-1)d}-F_{(k-2)d}\right)V_{n+(k-1)d}.

Recall from Theorem
3.4.1
, part (ii) that
\textbf{g}(S)

. When

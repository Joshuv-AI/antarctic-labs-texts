Age-related IGF-1 decline permits a redox-sensitive transcriptional shift that directs hematopoiesis toward a CCR2+ myeloid fate
Authors: Zhengyi Chen, Xidan Fu, Chao Liu, Wenkang Jiang, Jiurong Liu, Miao Hao, Ling Fu, Wei Huang, Xiujuan Lang, Xijun Liu, Yumei Liu, Hulun Li, Rui Li, Bo Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAging reshapes hematopoiesis toward myelopoiesis, particularly increased output of classical monocytes, contributing to immunosenescence. However, the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we identify that aging drives hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) to progressively commit to a CCR2+ monocyte fate along a defined lineage trajectory, which is executed through a redox-sensitive transcriptional cascade. We find that the age-related decline in bone marrow IGF-1 is the upstream niche signal that releases suppression of the oxidative stress/NF-κB/IRF8 axis, allowing this cascade to redirect early progenitors toward a CCR2+ myeloid-primed fate. Functional assays confirm that cells following this CCR2+ trajectory drive myeloid-biased hematopoiesis and directly contribute to age-associated immunosuppression. Our work delineates a hierarchical pathway from niche decay to lineage redirection, establishing the CCR2+ fate trajectory as a key driver of immunosenescence and a potential therapeutic target.Keywords: Immunosenescence, IGF-1, Redox signaling, CCR2, Immunotherapy

Introduction

Hematopoietic aging is characterized by a progressive shift in blood cell production toward myeloid lineages, with a particularly notable increase in classical monocytes [[1], [2], [3]]. This shift contributes to chronic inflammation, impaired adaptive immunity, and a permissive microenvironment for age-associated diseases including cancer [[4], [5], [6]]. Despite extensive research, the precise mechanisms linking aging-related alterations in the niche signal to the redirection of hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) lineage remain incompletely understood.

The prevailing view suggests that aged HSCs undergo cell-autonomous alterations, including DNA damage, epigenetic drift, and mitochondrial dysfunction, which predispose them to myeloid differentiation [[7], [8], [9], [10]]. However, emerging evidence indicates that the bone marrow microenvironment also plays an instructive role [5,11]. These local signals are themselves influenced by broader systemic endocrine changes, which are increasingly recognized as key drivers of aging [12]. Heterochronic parabiosis has demonstrated that young blood can rejuvenate aged hematopoietic stem cells, restoring lymphoid potential and mitigating functional decline [13]. These findings suggest that age-associated endocrine decay play a pivotal role in modulating hematopoietic aging. A critical unanswered question is whether cell-intrinsic alterations and niche signals operate independently or are functionally integrated to enforce lineage redirection. If niche signals actively drive lineage redirection, then targeting the microenvironment might offer a more efficient and safer therapeutic strategy than attempting to reverse complex cell-intrinsic alterations.

Here, we identify that aging drives HSCs to progressively commit to a CCR2+ monocyte fate along a defined lineage trajectory. Using an integrative approach that combines single-cell transcriptomics, functional transplantation assays, and epigenomic profiling, we show that this fate switch is executed through a redox-sensitive transcriptional cascade. Mechanistically, the age-associated decline in bone marrow IGF-1 releases suppression of the oxidative stress/NF-κB/IRF8 axis, allowing this cascade to redirect early progenitors toward a CCR2+ myeloid-primed fate. Our work delineates a hierarchical pathway from niche decay to lineage redirection, establishing the CCR2+ fate trajectory as a key driver of immunosenescence and a potential therapeutic target for restoring balanced hematopoiesis in the elderly.

Results

Cell-intrinsic and microenvironmental alterations collectively determine aging-related myeloid-biased hematopoiesis

To characterize hematopoietic aging, we first compared young (2-month-old) and aged (18-month-old) mice. Consistent with prior reports [14,15], aged bone marrow displayed significant expansion of total cellularity and a shift in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) composition, characterized by an increased frequency and number of common myeloid progenitors (CMPs) and granulocyte-monocyte progenitors (GMPs) alongside no significant change in percentage or number in common lymphoid progenitors (CLPs) (Fig. 1A–C). This was accompanied by an expanded myeloid compartment and a contracted lymphoid compartment in the bone marrow (Fig. 1D and E and Fig. S1).

We next asked whether this age-associated myeloid bias arises from cell-intrinsic changes within HSPCs or from extrinsic signals from the aged bone marrow microenvironment. To dissect the relative contributions, we performed bone marrow transplantation (BMT) experiments. Lethally irradiated young or aged recipient mice received bone marrow cells from either young or aged donors, creating four experimental groups: young into young (YY), young into aged (YO), aged into young (OY), and aged into aged (OO) (Fig. 1F). After full hematopoietic reconstitution, analysis revealed a clear pattern. Recipients in the YY group served as the baseline and exhibited no significant myeloid bias. Strikingly, both the YO and OY groups displayed an intermediate but significant degree of myeloid skewing compared to the YY baseline, manifesting as increased frequencies of LSK cells, GMPs, and mature myeloid cells (Fig. 1G–I). Notably, the most severe myeloid bias was recapitulated in the OO group, where both the HSPCs and the niche were aged. This established that the full aged hematopoietic phenotype results from the combined effects of cell-intrinsic reprogramming and niche-derived signals.

CCR2 defines a developmentally programmed, transcriptionally aged cell state that enriches along the MPP3 trajectory during aging

To dissect the molecular changes that accompany hematopoietic aging, we analyzed publicly available transcriptomic datasets of long-term HSCs (LT-HSCs), short-term HSCs (ST-HSCs), and multipotent progenitors (MPPs) from young and aged mice. Using a composite “Hematopoietic Aging Score” that integrates gene signatures of cellular senescence, DNA damage response, epigenetic alterations, proteostasis, and hematopoietic-specific aging (Table S1), we observed that aging-associated transcriptional features progressively increase along the differentiation trajectory from HSCs to MPPs, peaking at the MPP stage (Fig. 2A). This positioned MPPs as the critical node where the transcriptional consequences of aging become most evident. Principal component analysis (PCA) of MPP transcriptomes revealed distinct age-segregated transcriptional landscapes (Fig. 2B), characterized by upregulation of genes involved in cell cycle (Cdkn1a, Cdkn2c), epigenetic aging (Irf8, Junb), chronic inflammation (Casp1, Ccr2), myelopoiesis (App, Notch4), and stress response (Gadd45b, Hspa1a) (Fig. 2C). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis confirmed that upregulated genes in aged MPPs enriched for immune response, cell adhesion, and myeloid differentiation pathways (Fig. 2D).

To identify central markers of this aged transcriptional network, we constructed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network from the top differentially expressed genes. Five hub genes emerged: Irf8, Cd74, Cx3cr1, Cxcl9, and Ccr2 (Fig. 2E). Notably, genes connected to Ccr2 were uniquely enriched for myeloid cell differentiation pathways (Fig. S2A–E). Flow cytometry confirmed an age-associated increase in CCR2+ and CD74+ cells within the LSK (Lin−Sca-1+c-Kit+) compartment, while IRF8+, CX3CR1+, and CXCL9+ LSK cells did not change significantly (Fig. S2F–J). Because CD74 is broadly expressed across HSPCs and linked to antigen presentation (Fig. S3B) [16], we focused on CCR2, a G protein-coupled receptor critical for pro-inflammatory myeloid cells [17,18].

Tracing CCR2 expression along the hematopoietic hierarchy, we found that the age-associated increase in CCR2+ cells was most prominent along the trajectory leading to the MPP3 stage, with more modest increases in HSCs and ST-HSC/MPP1 (Fig. 2F and G). This pattern indicates that CCR2 does not define a static subpopulation but rather marks a dynamic cell state that becomes increasingly engaged as cells commit to the myeloid lineage. Thus, CCR2 serves as a functional indicator of a myeloid-primed fate, and its age-related enrichment along the MPP3 trajectory drives hematopoietic aging.

To precisely characterize the CCR2+ HSPCs at single-cell resolution, we analyzed publicly available single-cell RNA-seq data of LSK compartment. After quality control, 19,109 cells were analyzed. Unsupervised clustering and UMAP visualization resolved 15 distinct clusters (Fig. 3A and S3A). Lineage inference using Slingshot revealed that HSCs gave rise to two lymphoid-primed and five myeloid-primed trajectories (Fig. 3B). Notably, high Ccr2 expression was confined to specific myeloid paths, peaking within the monocyte-primed Ly6c2+ GMP trajectory (Fig. 3C and D). Cells expressing elevated Ccr2 were preferentially enriched in Slpi+ and Irf8+ MPP clusters that exhibit an MPP3-like phenotype, and these cells showed higher scores for myeloid and monocyte differentiation gene sets (Fig. 3E, F and S3C). Thus, CCR2 marks a cell state that is already poised toward monocyte fate.

We next asked whether cells in this Ccr2+ state carry a higher aging burden. Genes upregulated in aged bulk MPPs were consistently enriched in Ccr2+ MPPs (Fig. 3G). GSEA confirmed that Ccr2+ MPPs showed upregulation of aging-related pathways (Fig. 3H). The composite “Hematopoietic Aging Score” was significantly elevated in Ccr2+ cells along the Ly6c2+ GMP trajectory (Fig. 3I). These data indicate that the CCR2+ state is associated with a high burden of molecular aging.

To functionally test the contribution of this CCR2+ state to myeloid bias, we sorted CCR2+ and CCR2- LSK cells from aged donors and transplanted them into young, lethally irradiated recipients (Fig. 3J). Recipients of aged CCR2+ LSK cells developed a profound myeloid bias, with expanded CMPs, GMPs, and mature myeloid cells—particularly Ly6Chigh classical monocytes—in both bone marrow and peripheral blood, compared to recipients of CCR2- LSK cells (Fig. 3K and L, S4). Together, these results establish that CCR2 marks a developmentally programmed, transcriptionally aged state along the monocyte-biased trajectory, and that cells in this state are sufficient to recapitulate the myeloid-biased output of hematopoietic aging.

Decline in niche IGF-1 drives enrichment of the CCR2+ myeloid trajectory during aging

Given the established role of the aged niche in driving myeloid bias, we sought the upstream signals that instruct the age-associated enrichment of the CCR2+ fate trajectory. GSEA comparing young versus aged MPPs and CCR2+ versus CCR2- MPPs identified eight pathways consistently altered in both comparisons, including four commonly upregulated and four downregulated (Fig. 4A and Table S2). Among the downregulated pathways, the IGF-1 receptor (IGF1R) signaling pathway stood out (Fig. 4B and C). The IGF-1/IGF-1R signaling axis has been reported to be involved in the aging process, and the decreased expression of IGF1R may be an important factor in the development of age-related malignant diseases [19,20]. Expression of Igf1r was reduced in both aged and CCR2+ MPPs, with a sharp decline specifically in cells following the CCR2+ trajectory (Fig. 4D–F).

To test whether diminished IGF-1/IGF1R signaling is sufficient to drive the CCR2+ fate, we employed Igf1r conditional knockout bone marrow transplantation. Since congenital whole-body Igf1r knockout causes embryonic lethality and complete IGF1R loss induces apoptosis resistance (divergent from normal physiology) [21,22], we generated chimeric mice with attenuated IGF1R signaling in young donor cells (Fig. 4G and S5). Strikingly, reducing IGF1R signaling recapitulated the aged phenotype: it increased the frequency of CCR2+ HSPCs, expanded total LSK cells, CMPs and GMPs, and contracted CLPs (Fig. 4H and I). This was accompanied by an expansion of mature myeloid cells (Fig. 4J). Thus, reduced IGF-1/IGF1R signaling is sufficient to instruct the CCR2+ myeloid fate.

IGF-1 secretion—regulated by growth hormone (GH)—declines over time, reaching low levels in individuals aged ≥60 years, and it has also been reported to reverse the myeloid-biased phenotype associated with hematopoietic aging [14,23]. Indeed, we observed a decline in IGF-1 levels both in circulation and, importantly, within the bone marrow niche of aged mice (Fig. 5A). Ex vivo treatment of aged bone marrow with recombinant IGF-1 for 72 h effectively counteracted the aged phenotype, reducing the expression of CCR2 in LSK cells and the frequencies of myeloid progenitors, while restoring CLP output. (Fig. 5B–D). To directly address the functional longevity of IGF-1-mediated hematopoietic rejuvenation within the chronically aged niche, we performed long-term in vivo analyses (Fig. 5E). Strikingly, even at 16 weeks post-transplantation, IGF-1 preconditioning consistently reversed the myeloid-biased output (Fig. 5F). To rigorously assess the durability of this rejuvenation effect, we performed serial bone marrow transplantation assays (Fig. 5G). Notably, the IGF-1-induced correction of myeloid bias persisted through secondary transplantation and was only significantly diminished after tertiary transplantation, indicating that IGF-1 preconditioning instills a durable, cell-intrinsic reprogramming that can sustain balanced hematopoiesis across multiple rounds of transplantation (Fig. 5H). These findings demonstrate that ex vivo IGF-1 rejuvenation confers long-term functional benefits even when re-introduced into an aged microenvironment, supporting its therapeutic potential for reversing hematopoietic aging in vivo.

Notably, IGF-1 did not directly convert CCR2+ cells to CCR2-; instead, it suppressed the de novo generation of CCR2+ cells from CCR2- precursors (Fig. 5I). This suggests that IGF-1 primarily acts by restraining the commitment of early progenitors to the CCR2+ trajectory. To directly test the instructive role of the aged niche, we performed competitive co-transplantation: CCR2- LSK cells from aged wild-type mice were mixed with CCR2+ LSK cells from aged EGFP mice at a 1:1 ratio and transplanted into young or aged recipients (Fig. 5J). In aged recipients, a higher proportion of input CCR2- donor cells gave rise to CCR2+ progeny, indicating that the aged niche actively promotes entry into the CCR2+ trajectory. By contrast, CCR2+ donor cells maintained their CCR2+ identity regardless of recipient age, confirming the stability of this committed state. Moreover, the CCR2+ donor pool contributed disproportionately more to downstream myeloid progenitors (Fig. 5K–M). Together, these data reveal that IGF-1 preserves hematopoietic balance by limiting the commitment of early progenitors to the CCR2+ myeloid trajectory, and its age-related decline permits the progressive enrichment of this fate.

IGF-1 suppresses the CCR2+ fate by limiting oxidative stress and NF-κB/IRF8 activation

To define how reduced IGF-1 signaling directs lineage-biased differentiation, we first examined the molecular features of CCR2+ MPPs. GO and GSEA of CCR2+ versus CCR2- MPPs revealed enrichment not only for myeloid differentiation pathways but also for redox regulation and stress response, suggesting elevated oxidative stress in cells following this trajectory (Fig. 6A and B). Indeed, CCR2+ LSK cells exhibited significantly higher reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels than CCR2- cells. While total cellular ROS (CellROX) was elevated in CCR2+ LSK cells, mitochondrial ROS (MitoSOX) showed a more pronounced increase, suggesting that the mitochondrial compartment is the predominant source of the redox imbalance (Fig. 6C and D). Consistent with this, aged LSK cells showed markedly reduced expression of the antioxidant enzymes Catalase and SOD2, whereas ex vivo IGF-1 stimulation significantly upregulated their expression, suggesting that IGF-1 deficiency compromises the endogenous antioxidant capacity of HSPCs. (Fig. S6). Given that NF-κB is a canonical downstream effector of oxidative stress, we assessed its activation [24]. CCR2+ LSK cells displayed the most robust NF-κB activation, as evidenced by elevated p-P65 and reduced IκB-α levels in both young and aged bone marrow (Fig. 6E and F).

To identify the key transcription factors downstream of NF-κB that instruct the CCR2+ fate, we performed SCENIC analysis. This identifies Irf8 as a core regulon with heightened activity in CCR2+ MPPs (Fig. S7A and B), highlighting its pivotal role in the development of CCR2+ classical monocytes, as evidenced by the marked deficiency of this population in Irf8−/− mice [25]. Moreover, p-p65 enhances Irf8 transcription through promoter binding [26]. Flow cytometry confirmed that CCR2+ LSK cells were predominantly IRF8+, and this correlation strengthened with age (Fig. S7C). Using scTenifoldKnk, a virtual knockout tool based on single-cell regulatory networks [27], we found that perturbing Irf8 significantly reduced the expression of Ccr2 and other monocyte-associated genes (Fig. S7D). IRF8 is known to preferentially bind to promoter-distal enhancers to establish monocyte-specific gene expression programs [28,29]. To determine whether the Ccr2 locus is poised for transcriptional activation, we examined the enrichment of H3K27ac, a hallmark of active enhancers, in the upstream regulatory region of Ccr2. We observed a consistent trend toward increased H3K27ac occupancy in the 50–100 kb region upstream of the Ccr2 transcription start site in aged HSCs, although this difference did not reach statistical significance, the data are suggestive of a permissive chromatin state at the Ccr2 locus (Fig. S7E). To test whether IRF8 directly occupies regulatory elements of these target genes, we re-analyzed publicly available IRF8 ChIP-seq data of GMPs, where monocyte-specific genes such as Ccr2 are more highly expressed than in HSCs or MPPs, in which lower expression levels could lead to false negatives. In GMPs, IRF8 bound a distal enhancer 96.6 kb upstream of Ccr2 and enhancers of other monocyte-specification genes (Fig. S7F). Moreover, transcriptomic analysis of Irf8 knockout GMPs confirmed marked downregulation of this CCR2+ myeloid-primed gene set (Fig. S7G and H). Thus, IRF8 acts as a direct downstream effector of NF-κB, priming a broad monocyte-specification program that drives the CCR2+ Ly6Chigh classical monocyte fate.

To directly test the causal role of the IGF-1–oxidative stress–NF-κB–IRF8 axis, we established an in vitro Igf1r knockdown system. LSK cells isolated from Igf1rflox/+; CAG-MerCreMer mice were treated with 1 μM 4-hydroxytamoxifen (4-OHT) to induce Cre-mediated Igf1r attenuation (Fig. S5B and C). We then treated these cells with the ROS scavenger N-acetylcysteine (NAC), the NF-κB inhibitor BAY 11-7082, or the mitochondrial complex I inhibitor rotenone, which directly induces mitochondrial ROS production. IGF-1R knockdown significantly increased mitochondrial ROS and activated the NF-κB pathway, effects that were effectively reversed by co-treatment with either NAC or BAY 11-7082 (Fig. 6H and I). Similarly, direct induction of oxidative stress using rotenone also activated NF-κB, supporting a ROS-mediated mechanism (Fig. 6H and I). Both IGF-1R knockdown and rotenone treatment significantly increased the frequency of IRF8+ and CCR2+ LSK cells, and this effect was blocked by co-treatment with NAC or BAY 11-7082 (Fig. 6J). Consistent with a role in aging, both IGF-1R knockdown and rotenone treatment also increased the expression of senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) in LSK cells, an effect similarly abrogated by NAC or BAY 11-7082 (Fig. 6K). Together, these data demonstrate that the age-related decline in IGF-1 drives the CCR2+ myeloid trajectory through a sequential cascade: elevated oxidative stress activates NF-κB, which induces IRF8 expression, ultimately establishing a CCR2+ myeloid-primed, senescence-associated state.

Targeting the IGF-1–CCR2 axis reverses immunosuppression and boosts anti-tumor immunity

Aging-associated myeloid bias promotes an immunosuppressive milieu that fuels tumor growth [5,30]. Glioblastoma (GBM) is an age-predominant malignancy characterized by a highly immunosuppressive, myeloid-rich tumor microenvironment [[31], [32], [33], [34]]. We therefore asked whether the age-related enrichment of the CCR2+ myeloid trajectory directly contributes to GBM progression. To isolate the effect of the aged hematopoietic system, we transplanted young or aged bone marrow into young recipient mice, then implanted GL261 glioma cells (Fig. S8A). Mice reconstituted with aged marrow exhibited shortened survival, along with an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment marked by accumulated PD-1+ exhausted CD8+ T cells and a shift toward M2-polarized macrophages (Fig. S8B–E, S9). This pro-tumorigenic effect was specifically attributable to the CCR2+ trajectory: transplantation of sorted aged CCR2+ LSK cells recapitulated the accelerated tumor growth and immunosuppressive landscape (Fig. S8H–N), whereas Igf1r knockdown in young HSPCs mimicked the aged phenotype (Fig. S8O–U). Thus, the IGF-1 decline and the ensuing CCR2+ fate is causally linked to age-related immunosuppression and tumor progression.

We next explored whether therapeutic intervention on this axis could reverse hematopoietic aging and restore anti-tumor immunity. Analysis of TCGA GBM data revealed that low IGF1 expression correlated with poor patient survival (Fig. 7A). Given that tumor IGF1 does not necessarily reflect bone marrow niche IGF-1 levels, we next performed preclinical experiments to directly test the therapeutic relevance of the hematopoietic IGF-1–CCR2 axis in glioblastoma. Ex vivo IGF-1 preconditioning of aged donor bone marrow for 72 h prior to transplantation systemically rejuvenated the hematopoietic profile in young recipients, normalizing the frequencies of LSK cells, GMPs, and CCR2+ myeloid cells (Fig. 7B and C, S10). Strikingly, this preconditioning significantly prolonged the survival of tumor-bearing mice (Fig. 7D and E), reduced total macrophage infiltration, and promoted an M1-polarized state (Fig. 7F–H). Finally, we evaluated whether this strategy could overcome PD-1 checkpoint blockade resistance, which is common in glioblastoma [35]. While anti-PD-1 monotherapy failed to improve survival, preconditioning aged bone marrow with IGF-1 restored therapeutic sensitivity and significantly extended survival (Fig. 7I). Together, these data demonstrate that age-related IGF-1 decline drives the CCR2+ myeloid trajectory, creating an immunosuppressive microenvironment that accelerates tumor growth and impairs immunotherapy efficacy. Restoring IGF-1 signaling through direct supplementation or by targeting the CCR2+ lineage reverses these effects and offers a promising strategy to rejuvenate anti-tumor immunity in the elderly.

Discussion

Aging progressively reshapes hematopoiesis toward myelopoiesis, yet how extrinsic niche signals coordinate with intrinsic transcriptional programs to redirect lineage fate remains incompletely understood [36]. Local bone marrow IGF-1 levels are influenced by circulating IGF-1 and secretion from bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) [37,38]. Here we identify that an age-related decline in bone marrow IGF-1 permits a redox-sensitive transcriptional cascade that progressively commits early progenitors to a CCR2+, monocyte-primed trajectory. This pathway explains how a single niche factor decline can drive a defined myeloid fate, and it establishes the CCR2+ fate as a driver of age-associated immunosuppression.

Our finding that IGF-1 decline unleashes rather than directly promotes CCR2+ commitment reframes how we think about niche regulation. Previous work showed that IGF-1 supports HSC maintenance and that its reduction promotes myeloid skewing [14]. Our study goes beyond correlation to define a causal molecular axis. We show that IGF-1 suppresses the CCR2+ fate by restraining the oxidative stress/NF-κB/IRF8 axis, and that its age-related decline unleashes this cascade. This is a permissive rather than instructive mechanism, which aligns with the concept that youthful niches actively restrain maladaptive lineage choices [39,40].

NF-κB is a central mediator of inflammation and has been implicated in immunosenescence [[41], [42], [43], [44]]. Recent studies have demonstrated that NF-κB activation in aged hematopoietic stem cells drives precocious myeloid differentiation through sustained activation of myeloid-associated genes [45]. Furthermore, NF-κB dysregulation in the bone marrow microenvironment leads to epigenomic reprogramming of HSCs and promotes myeloid-biased hematopoiesis [11]. Our findings extend these observations by identifying age-related decline in IGF1 as an upstream trigger and IRF8 as a downstream effector that directs CCR2+ monocyte fate, with our data highlighting the IGF-1-NF-κB-IRF8 pathway as a key node linking niche decay to lineage redirection during aging.

We note that SCENIC analysis also identified Cebpb and Spi1 as regulons with heightened activity in CCR2+ MPPs, and IRF8 is known to cooperate with these factors in myeloid specification [46,47]. Our ChIP-seq data confirmed that IRF8 binds distal enhancers of Ccr2, Ly6c2, Csf1r, and other monocyte-specification genes. However, we did not systematically investigate whether IRF8 acts alone or in complex with C/EBPβ or PU.1 at these sites, nor did we map the full enhancer landscape of the CCR2+ trajectory. Such cooperative mechanisms remain an important area for future study.

IGF-1 has been reported to regulate mitochondrial redox balance through multiple pathways, including the AKT/FOXO3 and Nrf2 axes [[48], [49], [50]]. While our observation that IGF-1 restores SOD2 and Catalase expression in aged LSK cells is consistent with a potential involvement of these pathways, we did not perform systematic profiling of FOXO3 downstream targets in this study, and whether FOXO3 directly mediates redox-regulatory effects of IGF-1 in HSCs remains an open question. The precise mechanism by which IGF-1 regulates mitochondrial ROS in HPSCs remains to be fully elucidated and warrants further investigation.

A limitation of this study is that we cannot formally exclude the possibility that the durability of Several limitations should be considered. First, we cannot formally exclude the possibility that the durability of IGF-1 preconditioning reflects selective expansion of CCR2- progenitors during ex vivo culture rather than cell-intrinsic reprogramming. Second, the exclusive use of male mice leaves it unclear whether this pathway operates similarly in female hematopoiesis, given the well-established sex dimorphism of the GH/IGF-1 axis. Third, we acknowledge that the Hematopoietic Aging Score includes genes associated with myelopoiesis and inflammation, which may introduce a lineage-priming bias. However, myeloid skewing and chronic inflammation are themselves integral features of hematopoietic aging. Thus, while we cannot fully dissociate lineage identity from aging signatures in this composite score, the observed enrichment in CCR2+ cells likely reflect the convergence of these interrelated aging-associated processes. Another technical limitation is that our redox and NF-κB activation measurements were performed in vitro using 4-OHT-treated LSK cells rather than directly in bone marrow chimeric mice in vivo. While the genetic Igf1r attenuation model provides clean mechanistic evidence, we cannot exclude the possibility that the in vivo bone marrow microenvironment modulates the redox response in ways not fully recapitulated in our ex vivo system.

The translational implications are substantial. We first note that our human correlative data derive from tumor-intrinsic IGF1 expression, which may not faithfully reflect bone marrow niche IGF-1 levels. Therefore, we turned to preclinical models to directly test the therapeutic relevance of this axis. Our demonstration that ex vivo IGF-1 preconditioning of aged bone marrow restores balanced hematopoiesis and overcomes anti-PD-1 resistance in glioblastoma suggests that targeting the aged hematopoietic compartment itself could be a powerful adjunct to immunotherapy. Because systemic IGF-1 may promote tumor growth [[51], [52], [53]], spatiotemporally controlled modulation of IGF-1 signaling, rather than systemic treatment, may offer an optimal strategy for anti-aging and cancer intervention in older populations. Moreover, the observation that CCR2+ HSPCs are sufficient to drive pro-tumorigenic and immunosuppressive phenotypes identifies this CCR2+ population as a potential therapeutic target.

In conclusion, we have delineated a hierarchical pathway from niche decay to lineage redirection. The age-related decline of IGF-1 removes a brake on oxidative stress, allowing NF-κB to induce IRF8, which then primes a monocyte-specification program. This cascade drives the progressive enrichment of the CCR2+ fate and underlies the myeloid bias and immunosuppression of aging. Our work provides a mechanistic framework for understanding immunosenescence and identifies multiple nodes for therapeutic rejuvenation.

Materials and methods

Mice

Young (2-month-old) and aged (18-month-old) male C57BL/6J mice were purchased from Beijing Huafukang Co., Ltd. H11-CAG-MerCreMer mice (Catalog C001356) and Igf1r-flox mice (Strain S-CKO-03057) were purchased from Cyagen, R26-CAG-EGFP mice were generously provided by Professor K. Zhao from Xuzhou Medical University. Experimental mice were kept in an air-conditioned room (Temp: 22° to 25°C) with a standard 12-h light/dark cycle and free access to food and water. This study adhered to the guidelines for the management and use of experimental animals and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Harbin Medical University.

Bone marrow transplantation

Recipient mice were lethally irradiated with a dose of 9.0 Gy. Donor bone marrow cells were harvested from femurs and tibias of donor mice by crushing with a mortar and pestle in cold PBS supplemented with 2% fetal bovine serum. Red blood cells were lysed using ACK lysis buffer for 5 min at room temperature. The cells were dissociated into single-cell suspensions by filtration through a 70-μm nylon strainer. A total of 5 × 106 donor bone marrow cells were resuspended in 100 μL PBS and injected through the tail vein after the recipient mice were immobilized. Mice were provided with water containing 1.1 g/L neomycin for 2 weeks post-transplantation and allowed to reconstitute for 8 weeks before subsequent experiments. For inducible Igf1r knockout, tamoxifen was dissolved in corn oil and administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 75 mg/kg for 5 consecutive days starting post-BMT. For CCR2 sorting and IGF-1 pretreatment experiments, cells were mixed with 1 × 106 bone marrow mononuclear cells prior to bone marrow transplantation.

Orthotopic glioma model

GL261 were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Procell, PM150210) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. For tumor implantation, mice were anesthetized with an intraperitoneal injection of 2.5% tribromoethyl alcohol (333 mg/kg) and placed in a stereotactic frame (RWD Life Science, 68,030). A small hole was drilled in the skull, and the dura was gently opened to allow the micropipette to pass through without interruption. A total of 6 × 104 GL261 cells in 2 μL of PBS were injected into the right striatum (AP, 0.1 mm; ML, 2.3 mm; DV, 4.0 mm) at a rate of 0.5 μL/min. The syringe was left in place for an additional 5 min before slow withdrawal. For immune profiling, brains were harvested at 14 days post-implantation.

Cell isolation and tissue processing

Mice were euthanized by isoflurane lethal anesthesia. Bone marrow cells were obtained by flushing tibias with cold PBS + 2% FBS. Spleens were mechanically dissociated and passed through a 70-μm cell strainer. Blood was collected from the retro-orbital plexus into tubes containing 5 mg/mL heparin soudium salt (MCE, HY-17567A). Red blood cells from bone marrow, spleen, and blood samples were lysed with ACK lysis buffer. For brain tumor-infiltrating immune cells, mice were transcardially perfused with 30 mL of ice-cold D-HBSS. Tumor-bearing hemispheres were minced and digested in HBSS containing 0.2% Collagenase II (Sigma, C2-28-100 MG) for 60 min at 37°C with constant agitation. The resulting suspension was passed and leukocytes were isolated by centrifugation through a 30% Percoll (Cytiva, 17-0891-09) density gradient at 300 × g for 30 min at room temperature with no brake. After centrifugation, the supernatant was carefully aspirated, and the leukocyte pellet at the bottom of the tube was collected and washed with PBS + 2% FBS.

Flow cytometry and cell sorting

Single-cell suspensions were stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies for 30 min at 4°C in the dark. After washing the cells with PBS, dead cells were labeled using FVD. Termination staining was performed using cell PBS, followed by cell surface labeling using the following antibodies: CD45, CD11b, Ly6G, Ly6C, CD3, CD4, CD8a, CD19, CD86, CCR2, PD-1, F4/80, Lineage antibody cocktail, CD135, CD117, Sca-1, CD48, CD150, CD34, CD16/32, CD127. For intracellular staining of CD206, Foxp3, IRF8, p-P65, IκB-α and cells were fixed and permeabilized using the Foxp3/Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set (eBioscience, 00-5523-00) or BD Cytofix/Cytoperm™ Fixation/Permeabilization Kit (BD Biosciences, 554,714) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Subsequently, after washing the cells with permeabilization buffer, the cells were fixed with 1% PFA. For ROS analysis, cells were performed using CellROX™ Deep Red (Invitrogen, C10422) and MitoSOX Green (Invitrogen, M36005). For SA-β-gal analysis, cells were performed using Cellular Senescence Kit Red. Finally, the stained cells were detected and analyzed using BD Verse and FlowJo software, respectively.

In vitro cultures and treatments

For bone marrow cells culture, cells were cultured in Complete culture medium for mouse bone marrow hematopoietic stem cells (AURUICELL, ORCCMM356). For IGF-1 pretreatment, recombinant mouse IGF-1 (PeproTech, 250-19) was added to the culture at a concentration of 100 ng/mL. For IGF-1R knock down, 4-Hydroxytamoxifen (MCE, HY-16950) was used at 1 μM. For oxidative stress induction, retonone (MCE, HY-B1756) was used at 0.3 or 2 μM. For general antioxidant rescue, N-acetylcysteine (MCE, HY-B0215) was used at 5 mM. For IκBα phosphorylation inhibition, the inhibitor BAY 11-7082 (MCE, HY-13453) was used at 2 μM. Cells were cultured for 4 or 48 h at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator before analysis by flow cytometry.

Bioinformatics analysis

For bulk RNA-seq, public datasets were downloaded, including GSE157455 for mouse LT-HSCs, ST-MPPs, and MPPs, GSE70241 for wild-type GMP cells, and GSE70242 for Irf8−/− GMP cells. Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 (v1.34.0) in R. Genes with an adjusted P value < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.5 were considered significant. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the GSEA software (v4.3.2) with the Hallmark and C5 gene set collections from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB).

For scRNA-seq analysis, public dataset GSE262024 of mouse LSK cells was downloaded. Data processing, normalization, and clustering were performed using Seurat (v4.3.0) in R. Cell clusters were annotated based on canonical marker genes. Trajectory inference was performed using Slingshot (v2.4.0). The “Hematopoietic Aging Score” was calculated using the AddModuleScore function in Seurat based on gene signatures listed in Table S1. SCENIC (v1.3.1) analysis was performed to identify regulons and transcription factor activity.

For ChIP-seq analysis, datasets were downloaded from GSE104404 and GSE70237. Raw sequencing reads in HOMER tag format were processed using R (v4.5.3). Reads were filtered to standard chromosomes (chr1-19, X, Y, M) and normalized to reads per million (RPM). Genome-wide coverage tracks were generated using GenomicRanges (v1.50.0). IRF8 peaks were identified using HOMER (v4.11) against Input control, requiring ≥5-fold enrichment and FDR < 0.001. Target genes were annotated using TxDb.Mmusculus.UCSC.mm10. ensGene, org. Mm.eg.db and TxDb.Hsapiens.UCSC.hg19. knownGene packages. Visualization was generated using ggplot2 (v3.4.0) and Gviz (v1.42.0).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Blood was collected from the retro-orbital plexus and allowed to clot at room temperature. Serum was obtained by centrifugation at 2000 × g for 10 min. Bone marrow fluid was flushed from tibias with 200 μL of PBS and clarified by centrifugation. IGF-1 concentrations in bone marrow fluid were normalized to flush volume and expressed as pg/mL. IGF-1 concentrations in serum and bone marrow fluid were quantified using the Mouse/Rat IGF-1 Quantikine ELISA Kit (R&D Systems, MG100) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm with a correction at 570 nm.

In vivo treatments

For anti-PD-1 immunotherapy, GL261-bearing mice were treated intraperitoneally with 200 μg of anti-mouse PD-1 antibody (BioXcell, BE0146) or rat IgG2a isotype control (BioXcell, BE0089) every 3 days, starting from day 7 post-tumor implantation. For IGF-1 pretreatment in BMT, donor bone marrow cells from aged mice were cultured ex vivo with 100 ng/mL IGF-1 for 72 h, washed twice with PBS, and then transplanted into irradiated recipients as described above.

Bioluminescence imaging (BLI)

Tumor growth was monitored non-invasively using bioluminescence imaging on day 10 after GL261-LUC&GFP cell implantation. Mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of 2.5% tribromoethyl alcohol (333 mg/kg) and injected intraperitoneally with an aqueous solution of D-luciferin potassium salt (150 mg/kg; Aladdin, L120798). Imaging was performed 10 min post-injection using IVIS LuminaLT (PerkinElmer) housed within a light-tight chamber.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SEM. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (v9.5.0, GraphPad Software Inc.). Comparisons between two groups were analyzed using unpaired two-tailed Student's t tests. Multiple group comparisons were performed using one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey's or Šidák's post hoc test for multiple comparisons, as appropriate. Survival curves were analyzed using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001).

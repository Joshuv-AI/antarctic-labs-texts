A hybrid deep learning and quantum computing framework enhances cybersecurity threat detection and analysis
Authors: Abdel-Haleem Abdel-Aty, Mohammed Farsi, Mohamed Hafez, Betty Wan Voon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the face of increasingly sophisticated cyber threats in an interconnected world, the need for scalable, intelligent, real-time cybersecurity solutions is intensifying. While traditional deep learning, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), is effective for detecting anomalies in network traffic, their current capabilities do not address challenges with high-dimensionality networks, computational performance, or adaptation to new attacks. Whereas traditional methods suffered constraints, quantum computing offers a complementary advantage in both parallel computing and superior optimization. This study provides a hybrid deep learning and quantum computing framework that employs (i) convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for effectively extracting spatial features, (ii) recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for leveraging temporal patterns, and (iii) Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) for quantum-enhanced classification. The framework is built using the CICIDS-2017 dataset to extract features in an extensive preprocessing phase but is a rather laborious process of normalization, feature selection, feature time structuring, and a feature balancing procedure using techniques such as on-over sampling and under-sampling. The hybrid model was evaluated across multiple metrics; for accuracy, recall, F1 score, and ROC-AUC area showed high accuracy (93%) and low false-positive rates across different categories of cyberattacks within an experimental and virtual cyber environment. The suggested framework proved to be generalizable and scalable, suggesting its efficiency in cybersecurity applications that necessitate flexibility and intelligence. This contribution demonstrates a foundational step towards real-time, high performance hybrid quantum - deep learning models for cyber threat detection and prevention.Keywords: Cybersecurity, Deep learning, Quantum computing, Anomaly detection, Variational quantum circuitsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In the modern hyperconnected digital world, cybersecurity has become an essential issue in the government, corporation, and individual sphere. The explosive emergence of digital technologies – cloud computing, IoT, 5G networks, and mobile applications – have contributed not only to improving the scale and intricacy of data but also to expanding the scope of the targets vulnerable to adverse players1. Cyberattacks are no longer the elementary malware and phishing, nowadays, they have matured into highly sophisticated plays such as APTs, undiscovered software flaws and large-scale ransomware attacks that can seriously hinder a national infrastructure, steal sensitive data, and produce significant economic harm2. Conventional cybersecurity mechanisms including IDS (Intrusion Detection Systems) based on rules and signature-based antivirus software are increasingly becoming ineffective against these complex and ever-changing threats3. Such systems are commonly based upon predefined patterns and therefore are impotent to novel or obfuscated attacks4. From this, there is an emerging requirement for adaptive, intelligent systems that can learn by data and identify anomalies in real time. However, deep learning approaches have been proposed to address these challenges5. DL models like CNNs and RNNs are able to extract meaningful patterns from reams of data and the technology was successfully used to perform such tasks as network traffic analysis, anomaly detection and malware classification6. CNNs are very good at finding spatial features in data, whereas RNNs are best for modeling sequential or time-series data- all very important for identifying the patterns of behavior in cyber threats1, 7.

However, classical DL models struggle to handle large-scale, high-dimensional datasets typical for cybersecurity applications due to limitations of their applicability. These models are computationally resource-heavy, and are often bandwidth demanding when it comes to training and inference, making them difficult to deploy in real time or resource-constrained spaces2, 8. Quantum Computing (QC) provides a revolutionary way out for these challenges. Doubling on the principles of quantum mechanics, quantum computers can solve complex problem of optimization and classification in an exponential time shorter than classical computers3, 9. In particular, Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) allow hybrid quantum-classical processing, which can improve the retrieval of such features, the optimization of the model, and the classification accuracy in high dimensional space10.

Problem statement

Cyber threats are more sophisticated and repeated in nature, posing real danger to governments, industries, and key transborder infrastructures. Conventional security systems and even standard deep learning architectures like CNNs and RNNs have difficulty scaling to process high-dimensional, large-scale network data in real time. These shortcomings result in decreased precision detection and increase false alarms, making systems susceptible to sophisticated attacks. Although quantum computing holds great potential in high-speed parallel processing and optimization, it has not yet been implemented on a large scale in practical cybersecurity systems. The constraints of shallow deep learning models in coping with high-dimensional data and lack of scalability have been extensively published in the cybersecurity literature11. Concomitantly, the promise of quantum computing for addressing optimization and classification problems has been showcased via hybrid quantum-classical systems. The combination of deep learning and quantum computing is hence underpinned by existing theoretical and empirical evidence in recent literature.There is an urgent need for a hybrid solution that takes advantage of both the ability to detect patterns of deep learning and computational capabilities of quantum systems. This research covers this shortfall through the suggestion of an integrated deep learning–quantum computing framework for improved cybersecurity threat detection, scalability, and real-time operation.

Motivation of study

The widespread growth of digital technologies and networked systems has exponentially amplified the quantity and sophistication of cyber threats. Conventional security measures such as signature-based detection and traditional machine learning are becoming less effective in detecting advanced and dynamic attack vectors. While DL algorithms like CNNs and RNNs provide valuable improvements in anomaly and threat detection, they are typically hampered by scalability, processing speed, and responsiveness to new threats. At the same time, quantum computing is unfolding as a revolutionary tool that can solve complex computational issues more effectively than traditional approaches. Nevertheless, its application to real-world cybersecurity solutions is yet to be explored. This research is driven by the pressing necessity to build a system that can grow efficiently, real-time, and high-precision cybersecurity framework through the integration of the strengths of deep learning and quantum computing to effectively counter advanced cyberattacks in contemporary digital environments.

This paper presents a new hybrid deep learning, and quantum computing architecture that combines the strengths of the CNNs, RNNs, and VQCs to enhance the capability of cybersecurity threat detection and analysis. Using classical deep learning for feature learning and temporal analysis and quantum computing for fast classification and optimization, the proposed model is turned towards finding a powerful, scalable, and efficient solution to modern cyber challenges. This hybrid method improves not only detection capacities but also creates a basis for the development of next-generation cyber security systems which are consistent with national and global security priorities.

Literature review

Ali & Jima12 introduced a hybrid framework that integrates deep learning, quantum computing, and machine learning to improve cybersecurity and human-robot collaboration. Their results showed that deep learning effectively detected zero-day cyberattacks with a 92% detection rate, while reinforcement learning enhanced robotic task efficiency by 20%. They also demonstrated that combining quantum and classical machine learning reduced computational time by up to 40%, enabling faster threat detection and decision-making. However, the framework still faces challenges, including false-positive predictions, the need for large training datasets, limited model interpretability, and the current scalability limitations of quantum computing hardware.

Hossain et al.13 suggests a QML Cybersecurity Framework to counter current, intricate cybercrime threats. It incorporates Quantum Key Distribution for secure communication, Quantum Neural Networks and QSVMs for threat identification, and Quantum Reinforcement Learning for autonomous response. Based on datasets such as NSL-KDD, CICIDS2017, CASIA, and simulated compliance logs, the framework detects threats with 96% accuracy, has a 28% reduction in the time taken to respond to incidents, and attains 96% compliance simulation success. A Quantum Authentication module protects identity through biometric and behavioral information. The technique is adaptive, scalable, and efficient across domains of security. Limitations encompass existing quantum hardware limitations, substantial implementation complexity, and dependence on synthetic data. Notwithstanding, the research provides the way ahead for next-generation intelligent cybersecurity applications.

Subramanian & Chinnadurai14 proposes a cyber-attack detection framework combining federated learning with quantum enhancements that addresses limitations of centralized approaches such as privacy threats and communication overhead. The framework is trained and tested on the UNSW-NB15 dataset. The methodology combines a STAN to learn traffic patterns and a QIFA improvement method for effective learning. Hierarchical aggregation approach dynamically aggregates nodes in terms of network status and data similarity. Multi-stage model enhancement and privacy-enhancing mechanisms additionally boost performance. Limitations are that it has high model complexity and relies on quantum-inspired techniques instead of actual quantum hardware. However, it is better than the conventional CNN, LSTM, and RNN models in anomaly detection.

Hdaib et al.15 seeks to improve network security through anomaly detection using QDL frameworks that outperform classical approaches in detecting cyber threats. The work applies benchmark datasets containing computer and IoT network flows to measure performance. Three hybrid models are constructed by integrating Hybrid model integrating quantum autoencoders and quantum one-class classifiers, SVM, quantum random forest, and quantum k-nearest neighbor algorithms. The frameworks leverage quantum capabilities to compute intricate feature correlations. Among these, the highest detection accuracy was achieved by the quantum autoencoder + quantum k-NN model. All the models had excellent anomaly recognition potential. Limitations are dependent on simulated quantum environments and limited quantum deployment in the real world. Nevertheless, the method proves the future potential of quantum-driven cybersecurity.

Dutta et al.16 introduces a DL-based ensemble model for network anomaly detection and cyber-attacks in intricate IoT-based ecosystems. The process applies to DSAE for feature learning, followed by stacked generalization with DNN, LSTM, and LR meta-classifier. This two-phase process improves anomaly classification both at host and network levels. Results indicate enhanced detection accuracy and statistical significance compared to conventional models. The ensemble effectively manages high-volume CPS traffic. It is, however, computationally demanding and could experience difficulties in real-time deployment. It is, nonetheless, superior to conventional machine learning methods in cyber-attack detection.

Mehmood et al.17 investigate contemporary cryptographic methods in DL and quantum approaches with the view to cybersecurity and image cryptography. It discusses approaches such as quantum random number generation, quantum image sending, steganography, and chaotic neural networks. It also discusses adversarial neural networks and deep learning-based transform methods employed for digital image security. The research does not employ a precise dataset but samples several experimental studies and benchmarks. Some of the major findings are strengths in hybrid quantum-ML methods and weaknesses in classical encryption frameworks. The outcome is a map for upcoming cryptographic breakthroughs. Drawbacks encompass no common benchmarks and real-world execution gaps within surveyed methods.

Bikku et al.18 develop instantaneous analysis of malware via QNNs as a way to deal with escalating cyber-attacks complexities. Malware DB benchmark dataset is applied as the test material. Quantum feature extraction, classification using QNNs, and real-time stream analysis comprise the suggested approach. QNN models are benchmarked against QSVM, QDT, and classic models RF, SVM, DT. QNN recorded an accuracy of 95%, outcompeting quantum baselines as well as traditional baseline models. Its power is in low-latency, high-fidelity detection of sophisticated malware patterns. The research points out the computational benefits of quantum models for security.

Ting Tin Tin et al.19 Predictive modelling of cybersecurity threats predicts the risks of a user while using a digital device such as a mobile phone, laptop and personal computer by using machine learning algorithms tested and validated by user behaviour data acquired from undergraduates in Malaysia. project has two main parts, data modelling and website deployment. Python is used to programme the machine learning part of the system, coded using Jupyter, with the dataset, while the website serves as a medium for users to predict their risks, who can access the Python files to make predictions.

Fractal geometry in cyber threat modeling

Recursive and multi-scale behaviors are commonly displayed by cyber threats, particularly in advanced attacks such as Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) and botnets. Such behaviors reflect aspects of fractal systems, wherein patterns recur across different scales. Observing cyber threats using a fractal perspective enables understanding their concealed complexity, persistence, and patterns of spread. By representing network traffic as a fractal structure, detection of low-frequency, subtle anomalies is enhanced since fractal analysis reveals patterns which may be missed by conventional statistical models.

Fractal-based methods have been utilized in various machine learning applications such as image classification, biomedical signal processing, and anomaly detection. Methods such as fractal dimension-based feature extraction, estimation of the Hurst exponent, and fractal entropy have been employed to represent complexity in high-dimensional data20. These works validate the incorporation of fractal geometry into deep learning models for cybersecurity.

Hybrid deep learning and quantum computing framework for cybersecurity threat detection

The research proposes a hybrid security model merging conventional deep learning with quantum technologies to improve threat detection. The process is done through data gathering from the CICIDS 2017 dataset, followed by thorough preprocessing such as cleaning, encoding, normalization, and feature selection. Spatial features are extracted by CNNs, while temporal patterns are identified by RNNs. VQC is used to combine the results and process them optimally to come up with the best classification. SMOTE and under-sampling methods eliminate the problem of the class imbalance. Data collection

Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (CIC) released a large publicly available dataset called the CICIDS 2017 (CICIDS-201721) that finds its applications in cybersecurity research. The dataset is applied to testing and developing intrusion detection systems (IDS) by relying on simulated realistic networks traffic. Data covered benign traffic on a network and various forms of cyber-attack such as DoS, DDoS, SQL injection, port scan and botnet traffic. It covers network flow attributes including packet size, the flow duration, protocol type, and time-related attributes with more than 80 features. The data is labeled so that entries will either be marked as normal or belong to some type of attack and therefore can be used in supervised machine learning algorithms. It is collected in realistic conditions so that trained models, based on it, could be applied to real-life situations. The rich, varied and large-scale attack situation of the CICIDS 2017 dataset renders it to the high-end category of cybersecurity model training and validation. This is ideal to the creation of well-tested anomaly detection methods, particularly in dynamic and changing attacks plans. The traffic in this data set is diversified and rich and this makes it the perfect dataset to explore, thus, it would be possible to analyze the proposed hybrid quantum-deep learning network intrusion detection models.

Data preprocessing

A data preprocessing is one of the key tasks that has a strong influence on the effectiveness of the proposed hybrid framework of Deep Learning or Quantum Computing to ensure security. CICIDS 2017 data set presents diversity of network traffic patterns, managing numerous aspects, such as the lengths and durations of packets, protocols and times of activity that are related to both benign and malicious traffic. The presence of mixed data of high dimensions demands a strict preprocessing pipeline to offer adherence to both conventional machine learning (CNN and RNN) and quantum computing (VQC).

Data cleaning

This machine learning/quantum-aided model depends heavily on data cleaning; this is a very important step in the success of any machine learning model. The CICIDS 2017 dataset, as much as comprehensive and realistic, could be inconsistent or lacking some entries or include irrelevant data that would have to be rectified before training the model. Data cleaning preserves data accuracy and makes it ready to analysis of both classical deep learning (CNNs and RNNs) and quantum circuits (VQCs) that are very sensitive to noise and irrelevant input features.

Temporal structuring

Temporal structuring plays a vital role in the sphere of cybersecurity because some types of attacks, such as brute force, or data exfiltration, take time to develop. RNNs can learn such time-dependant relations in a good way. In support, there is time windowed aggregation where events will be separated into sequences such as S={x_t, x_(t + 1),…,x_(t + n)}. A x is a network event at some time step. With this notation the RNN is enabled to learn about emerging behaviours rather than single events. The hybrid model can thus more precisely identify the complex, persistent threats and has more capability of improving real-time response in the systems of cyber security. Fractal analysis of network traffic is feasible through estimating the fractal dimensions of packet flows to identify self-similar patterns of attack. Methods like box-counting or estimation of Hurst exponent provide useful information about long-range dependencies, which can be used to enable more adaptive threat detection.

Data splitting and leakage prevention strategy

In order to avoid leaking data, first, the dataset is divided into train, validation, and test datasets without performing any pre-processing operations. Scaling, normalization, PCA, and feature selection are fit only on the training dataset, and the parameters learned from them are then used to transform validation and test datasets. In addition, the SMOTE and under-sampling techniques are employed solely on the training dataset. The temporal windowing technique is also carried out in a safe manner to prevent using future data points.

Classical deep learning models

The proposed framework makes use of two traditional deep learning models (CNNs and RNNs) to be able to explore network traffic data with the view of identifying cybersecurity threats based on the data. They collaborated to study the nature of space and time of network behavior to be able to identify anomalies more effectively.

Convolutional neural networks

CNNs excel at identifying spatial patterns in structured or sequential data like network traffic traces, packet streams, and protocol messages. CNNs are employed within this research for extracting hierarchical features from the dataset. The design typically involves convolutional layers for applying filters in the discovery of local patterns, then activation layers and down sampling techniques to execute dimension reduction and maintain leading features.

For instance, CNNs are capable of recognizing repetitive patterns or abnormalities in packet length, duration, or flag patterns that could be signs of typical attacks such as Port Scans or SQL Injection. While learning space-invariant features from the input traffic matrix, CNNs serve an important function to segregate anomalies that do not adhere to regular patterns of communications.

Quantum component: variational quantum circuits

In order to improve the computational power, overcome scalability issues of traditional models, the suggested framework involves Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs). VQCs are parameterized quantum circuits employed in hybrid quantum-classical algorithms, wherein a quantum circuit is optimized based on classical feedback mechanisms. VQCs facilitate the model to carry out high-dimensional data processing with improved optimization abilities. Quantum computing performs best at representing and processing complicated data distributions with fewer resources than classical computing. In that regard, VQCs are employed as a feature transformation module or a classifier that operates on the output of the CNN-RNN architecture. Their ability to model non-linear decision boundaries and map the immensity of available data into low dimensional Hilbert space makes them even more suitable in cybersecurity operations where data are high dimensional and imbalanced.

Employing quantum-enhanced optimization, the model grows more robust in selecting features, training faster, and attains a faster rate of convergence, in addition to superior levels of classification, especially when dealing with scenarios having noisy or overlapping distributions of classes. Quantum computing allows for parallel searching of high-dimensional solution spaces, which makes it efficient at analyzing fractal-characteristics data. Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) are especially efficient at modeling self-similar, non-linear distributions commonly found in fractal-modeled network traffic. Considering the present-day restrictions associated with the use of quantum machines under the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) paradigm, the suggested approach employs simulation models of quantum systems. Current quantum machines are subject to various limitations including a shortage of qubits, low coherence, and vulnerability to gate and measurement errors. The mentioned factors hinder the possibility of running deep quantum circuits that involve variational algorithms suitable for solving real-world cybersecurity problems. To overcome the outlined difficulties, one needs to utilize quantum simulators with high fidelity and incorporate them into classical optimization procedures to provide consistent and scalable experiments.

Hybrid architecture: integrating deep learning and quantum computing

This research is most noticeably innovative as it represents the hybrid architecture where the traditional deep learning solutions, e.g. CNNs and RNNs, are combined with the quantum computing module, which is based on the Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs). This type of architecture is peculiarly applicable in augmenting the effectiveness, scalability, and precise cybersecurity threat detection systems.

Architectural overview

The proposed architecture is composed of the following components:

Input layer:

The source of network traffic data is the CICIDS 2017 data set of time-stamped packets (labeled benign or malicious). The preprocessing of the information to the deep processing and quantum computing is performed by normalization and feature scaling.

CNN module (spatial analysis):

The data is then subjected to several convolutional layers that extract spatial characteristics, such as packet size anomalies, port scanning patterns and malformed payloads. The layer has the responsibility of identifying local patterns of the network data that may suggest initial phases of an attack.

RNN module (temporal analysis):

The CNNs learn the spatial features about the traffic patterns and take them as input to an RNN (or its generalization like LSTM or GRU) which discovers the temporal connections between the traffic patterns. This module presents violations of time patterns like synchronized DDoS attacks or cross secret malware disseminations.

Feature fusion layer:

The result of both RNN and CNN multiples are combined to get a single feature vector. It is a concatenation level which forms a combined spatiotemporal image of the network traffic flow which makes the model cognize about the short-term anomaly and long behavioral aberration.

Quantum processing layer (VQC):

These features are next passed on to a Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), where the data is encoded into quantum states and manipulated with a series of quantum gates with parameters. By using the VQC, we obtain a non-linear classification and optimization system, able to properly discriminate between intricate attack patterns and regular network activity. Thanks to the inherent properties of quantum mechanics, such as quantum parallelism and quantum entanglement, the module ensures an increase in computational efficiency, an improvement in processing speed, and enhanced ability to generalize on sparse or noisy data. Moreover, stability is guaranteed through noise-informed encoding and augmented training data, making the model resistant to attacks while preserving good performance metrics.

Output layer:

The resulting VQC is lastly read and translated to a classical post-processing layer, which is probabilistic (e.g., malicious and normal). Softmax activation is applied where there are multi-class classification situations.

Combining quantum computing with deep learning increases the potential of the model to deal with cybersecurity complexity through the capture of high-dimensional, non-linear relationships. Efficient optimization is facilitated by quantum mechanisms, while CNNs and RNNs pick out spatial and temporal features. The structure in layers follows fractal analysis, where recursive decomposition facilitates dealing with structural complexity across different fields. This integration makes a theoretical contribution by showing how fractal-like data complexity can be jointly modeled by layered neural architectures and quantum circuits, providing a formal path for the incorporation of fractal analysis into current AI.

Fractal-inspired optimization enhancements

Fractal-based optimization methods, including Lévy flight methods or the Fractal Search Algorithm, have potential to improve quantum parameter adjustment. These methods have the ability to enhance solution space exploration, particularly in non-convex and self-similar solution spaces of characteristic cybersecurity data.

Discussion

The hybrid model outlined in this research demonstrated an improvement over traditional pure deep learning models by strategically improving the shortcomings of high-dimensional data processing and slow-convergence. The hybrid model was achieved when the CNNs and RNNs from the two learning phases, with the feature extraction from the CNNs and temporal knowledge from the RNNs respectively, were integrated with the classification made through the Variational Quantum Circuit. The hybrid Quantum Learning Framework benefits from both the advantages of deep learning and the unique ability of quantum computational processing. The model demonstrates a strong capacity to identify cyber threats with non-linear and multi-scale patterns. Such patterns are similar to the patterns that exist in fractal systems, where sophisticated behaviors occur due to recursive structures. The inclusion of quantum elements, specifically Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs), boosts the capacity to identify fine-grained decision boundaries within high-dimensional data. Such convergence of deep learning with quantum optimization substantiates the interpretation that the model can effectively handle complexity akin to that in fractal and quantum contexts. After evaluations were complete with the hybrid model (i.e., Training and validation accuracy and ROC-AUC metrics evaluations), the hybrid model demonstrated the ability to generalize and classify simultaneous instances of the original attack categories with acceptable detection rates and an acceptable false positive rate. Other improvements over tradition deep learning model were advanced using pre-processing tools (e.g., SMOTE and PCA) to address data imbalance and enhance the relevance and importance of features in the datasets. Although the hybrid model has proven effective, this research acknowledges the practical limitations of quantum hardware failing to provide meaningful scalability and the invention of a performant implementation of the Quantum Learning Framework that was simulation-based necessitated this research flow. Nevertheless, the findings lead to a potential paradigm shift in the world of threat detection and explanations of cyber threats. Utilizing hybrid systems offers more robustness, flexibility, and speed to respond to the dynamically evolving and insidious nature of cyber threats all while maintaining real-time, enterprise readiness. Despite the successful results of the hybrid CNN-RNN-VQC system being shown in simulations, its performance on full fault-tolerant quantum computers remains hypothetical. Today, only NISQ-based quantum computers are capable of carrying out the required operations since the amount of noise and number of qubits limit the ability of a quantum computer to run deep computations. On the other hand, the use of fault-tolerant quantum computers may prove helpful in improving the performance of the algorithm. Furthermore, some parts of the Variational Quantum Circuit decision process can also be explained through techniques that use AI explainability methods like feature attributions, probabilistic outputs, and even surrogate classical models. Unfortunately, at present, such computers are only hypothetical. Nevertheless, practical applications in Edge/IoT contexts might demand that model compression is performed, as contemporary simulations fail to accurately replicate actual resource-constrained settings.

Conclusion and future work

This study demonstrates that quantum computing is a sustainable and scalable solution to consider classical deep learning-trained models to handle contemporary balance in cybersecurity. This hybrid model that utilized CNNs, RNNs, and VQCs here had strong predictive, accuracy, and precision ability while exhibiting durable performance against an imbalanced and complex dataset. The presented method enhanced anomaly detection and had promise for incorporation into real-time cybersecurity procedures. As quantum computing evolves, will need to evaluate ways to further integrate it into cybersecurity solutions for widespread adoption at the organizational and national defense levels. The suggested hybrid framework has potential applications outside cybersecurity. Areas like biomedical signal processing, image compression, and climate pattern modeling where fractal characteristics are dominant can tap the potential of capturing non-linear, multi-scale patterns. Nonetheless, there are specific limitations associated with the suggested model. The first limitation is associated with demographic limitations since the suggested model uses the CICIDS-2017 dataset, and therefore, the diversity of networks, users, and traffic distribution that might be used in real life cannot fully be represented. Another limitation concerns the fact that threat scope is limited by attack types defined in the suggested dataset, making it impossible for the suggested model to adapt to new cyber threats, including zero-day attacks.

Future work should focus on deploying the hybrid model onto real quantum hardware so that practical hardware constraint evaluation, as well as latency evaluation can occur in real-time systems. Future studies could also investigate the various ways to create quantum federated learning models and quantum attention mechanisms to better detect new and stealthy threats. Additionally, adversarial robustness and explainable AI could also be integrated to maintain trust, transparency, and accountability in automated cyber defence systems. Moreover, the robustness of the proposed hybrid framework against adversarial evasion attacks still stands as an open problem that requires further study through the use of adversarial training techniques. Nevertheless, the generalization capacity of the proposed model to novel zero-day attacks is still constrained by its reliance on the CICIDS-2017 dataset. Further research efforts should aim at continual learning techniques and adaptive mechanisms for tackling fractal-pattern cyber-attacks.

Solitude patterns of r r -graphs via CLM’s dependence relation Dedicated to CLM (Marcelo H. de Carvalho, Cláudio L. Lucchesi and U. S. R. Murty) for their invaluable contributions to the theory of perfect matchings
D. V. V. Narayana, D. Mattiolo, K. Gohokar, N. Kothari

Abstract
Recently, Goedgebeur, Mazzuoccolo, Renders, Wolf and the second author [Cubic graphs with edges in exactly one perfect matching, J. Graph Theory 112 (2026), 276-289] proved that every 3 3 -connected 3 3 -regular graph has at most six solitary edges; an edge is solitary if it participates in precisely one perfect matching. They also gave complete characterizations of 3 3 -connected 3 3 -regular graphs that have k k solitary edges for each k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 . A connected r r -regular graph, where r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , is an r r -graph if each odd cut has at least r r edges; for instance, 3 3 -graphs are precisely the 2 2 -connected 3 3 -regular graphs. We generalize the bound and characterizations, mentioned in the preceding paragraph, to 3 3 -edge-connected r r -graphs of order four or more; in particular, for r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 , we establish stronger bounds: (i) at most four solitary edges, and (ii) if the graph is simple then it is devoid of solitary edges. Apart from characterizing all 3 3 -edge-connected r r -graphs that have three or more solitary edges, for the case r = 3 r=3 , we provide a recursive characterization of those that have precisely two solitary edges such that they lie in the same perfect matching. Finally, for all r r -graphs (not necessarily 3 3 -edge-connected), we establish that: (i) every such graph, except for a few small graphs, has at most n 2 \frac{n}{2} solitary edges, and (ii) every such graph decomposes uniquely into 3 3 -edge-connected r r -graphs , and its solitary edges may be computed recursively via this decomposition. Our proofs and insights rely heavily on the dependence and mutual dependence relationships introduced and investigated by Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty [Ear decompositions of matching covered graphs, Combinatorica 19 (1999), 151-174].

Overview

An
r
-graph
, where
r\geq 3

r
-regular graph

with the additional property that, for every

of odd cardinality, there are at least
r
edges that have precisely one end in

It is easily deduced from Tutte’s
1

r
-graph participates in some perfect matching. Recently
[
12
]
,

— those that participate in precisely one perfect matching — were investigated in the case of
3
-graphs. They proved that every
3
-edge-connected

3
-graph has at most six solitary edges, and provided a complete characterization of those having three or more solitary edges.
In this manuscript, we generalize their results to all
r
-graphs using techniques from the theory of ‘matching covered graphs’.

We investigate solitary edges of
matching covered
graphs (that is, those connected graphs whose each edge belongs to some perfect matching) through the lens of the dependence relationship that was studied by Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty
[
2
]
, and further investigated in the recently published monograph
[
22
]
. Two edges of a matching covered graph

are

if every perfect matching containing either of them also contains the other.
Clearly, this is an equivalence relation and thus induces a partition of
E(G)
; each part is called an

. It is worth noting that if any member of an equivalence class is

then so is every other member; we refer to such an equivalence class as a

. This observation leads us to introduce the notion of

of a matching covered graph — the sequence of cardinalities of its solitary classes in nonincreasing order. For instance,
K_{4}
has solitude pattern
(2,2,2)
whereas the Petersen graph has solitude pattern
()
.

1.5.1
CLM’s dependence relation

Let

An edge

Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty

Edges

Thus, mutual dependence induces a partition of the edge set

of a matching covered graph

We invite the reader to make the following observation.

Proposition 1.21

In a matching covered graph, every perfect matching is the union of some equivalence classes.

Let

Corollary 1.24

The members of

Proposition 1.25

In an

Our next goal is to establish an upper bound on the cardinality of any solitary class in a matching covered graph that is free of even

Theorem 1.26

[Lucchesi-Murty Theorem]

Corollary 1.27

In a matching covered graph that is free of even

In view of this, we use the term

to refer to a solitary class of cardinality two. For instance, the bicorn

Corollary 1.28

For a matching covered graph

1.5.2
Splicing and separating cuts

For a cut

of a graph

, or simply as

are called the

A cut

of a matching covered graph

if both of its

and the preceding discussion.

Proposition 1.34

For any odd

-cut

of an
r
-graph
G
, both of its

-contractions, say

and
G_{2}
, are also

is a separating cut of

. Furthermore, if

is

-edge-connected then both

and

are also

-edge-connected.
\Box

It is worth noting that an

-graph may have separating cuts that are not

-cuts; for instance, the Petersen graph has separating

Proposition 1.35

For a separating cut

Proposition 1.36

A graph is a brick if and only if it is

[

-connected

-regular

Corollary 1.43

Every brick

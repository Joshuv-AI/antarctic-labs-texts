Oral squamous cell carcinoma in the Sudan: clinical behavior, DNA ploidy and S-phase fraction characteristics
Authors: Yousif Eltohami, Osama Eltayeb, Ahmed Suleiman
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe DNA ploidy and the S phase fraction (SPF) are associated with advanced tumors progression. The present study aimed to investigate the clinicopathological behavior of oral squamous cell carcinoma(OSCC) and the prognostication of the DNA ploidy and the SPF on its clinical outcomes.MethodsThis a prospective longitudinal study of 93 OSCC patients (186 specimens). Using high resolution flowcytometry, DNA analysis and SPF were performed for the primary tumor, field of cancerization and a control normal field. The patients were followed clinically for the development of recurrence or second primary tumor(SPT).ResultsAll the 93 patients are associated with wide field of cancerization. Out of the total number of cases, 28 (30%) patients developed recurrence. Of them 17 (18.3%) had regional recurrence, and eight (8.6%) had local recurrence. Second primary tumors were identified in 14 (15.1%) of the cases. Nodal metastasis, overall recurrence and SPTs were significantly associated with DNA aneuploidy and high SPF. High SPF and DNA hyperploidy showed a 28% increased risk of local recurrence (P.value = 0.032).ConclusionIn a series of 93 oral cancers who had wide field of cancerization, 30% of the patients developed recurrence, and 15% developed second primary tumors. The DNA ploidy and the SPF were independent prognosticators for the prediction of OSCC outcomes.Keywords: Oral squamous cell carcinoma, Wide field of cancerization, Recurrence, Second primary tumor, DNA ploidy, S phase fraction

Introduction

Cancer is one of the leading causes of death worldwide [1]. GLOBOCAN 2020 estimated that there were 19.3 million new cases of cancer and almost there were 10 million deaths from cancer in the year 2020, with 1.8% of deaths related to oral cancer [2]. In 2014; a report from the Sudan Cancer Registry showed oral cancer ranking as the sixth most common cancer in the country, and in 2017, Salah et al. reported that oral cancer in the Sudan was the seventh and eighth cancer, among males and females respectively [3]. Toombak is a local form of snuff, commonly used in Sudan, and well known as a risk factor of OSCC in Sudan [4]. the product is rich with Tobacco-Specific N-nitrosamines, which are strong carcinogens that reported to be found in saliva and urine of the users [5].

Recently, it is becoming known that genetically altered cells could spread widely across the OSCC epithelium, into clinically and histologically normal tissues, elucidating the concept of field alteration of cancer [6]. According to Brennan and colleagues, the clonally related tumor cells were detected in more than 50% of the surgical margins of OSCC cases; although these margins were clear of them under the light microscope [7]. A second primary tumor was defined as a new malignant lesion that has developed independently from the first tumor. To date, the vast majority of the investigators use the criteria set by Warren et al. to define the second primary tumor(SPT). In these criteria, the tumor must present as a definite frank malignancy, each tumor must be distinct, and any probability of metastasis must be excluded [8]. Cancer recurrence is defined as the reappearance of the tumor after the radical removal of the lesion. Some studies determine a period of 6-months of complete remission [9]. DNA ploidy is a measure of the DNA content in a cell population. When many copies of DNA present, it is called polyploidy and the allocation of the chromosome which causes chromosomal separation due to the genetic alteration is known as aneuploidy [10]. Variations in DNA indices in the same lesion were found in mutations that appear either due to chromosomes loss or gain [11].

In a growing cell population, the fraction of a cell population in a known phase at any given time is proportional to the duration of that phase. The S phase fraction is the percentage or number of cells arranged for mitosis by the active doubling of their DNA [12]. Hussein et al., studied the DNA ploidy and the S-phase fraction(SPF) in relation to the development of OSCC. Their analysis revealed that more than two thirds of the aneuploid neoplasms, and around one third of the diploid lesions had a high SPF [13]. Moreover, Zargoun et al., reported that aneuploidy was detected in all (100%) cases of OSCC and that all the cases with positive lymph nodes specimens had aneuploidy status [14]. The present study is aimed to determine the predictive prognostic value of the DNA ploidy and the SPF in the development of nodal metastasis, the development of SPTs & the possible occurrence of recurrence, and their relations to the clinicopathological characteristics of the OSCC lesion using high resolution Flow Cytometry (hr FCM). The present study will shed more light on the clinical significance of presence of wide field of cancerization, clarified the Toombak dipping as a risky habit for presentation of wide filed of cancerization in all the investigated patients on the clinical, subclinical, histopathological and genetic levels.

Materials and methods

The present study is a prospective longitudinal hospital-based study, carried at Khartoum Teaching Dental Hospital. KTH is the main hospital and center of oral and maxillofacial surgical oncology in the Sudan. The samples were selected after thorough clinical examination of the oral cavity and head & neck regions for clinically suspicious ulcerative lesions which were associated with wide field changes of the oral mucosa. Patients presenting at the time of the study having recurrent or second primary lesions and patients who received neoadjuvant radiation and/or chemotherapy were excluded from the study [15].

Procedure

The field mapping biopsy concept was used, as two different biopsies were collected, the first one to confirm the diagnosis of the primary OSCC and the second was taken from a distance of more than 3 cm from the clinical margins of the primary lesion.

For specimen processing, the hr DNA-FCM analysis, was done on the same tissue specimens. The specimens were stained with Propidium Iodide (PI) reagent (Immunostep, SL. Spain). For the measurements; a specific Flow cytometer device (FC500 Beckman Coulter device, Miami, FL, USA) was used. Trout lymphocytes were used as external control cells. The cases were assessed for the development of OSCC at or away from the site of primary tumor and the altered field lesions in the oral cavity, or the head and neck region. A SPT was considered when a new lesion had developed at a distance of more than 2 cm from the primary tumor and the possibility of local recurrence after surgery was considered if a lesion had developed at a distance less than 2 cm. According to the time of presentation, a SPT is considered as synchronous, if it occurs with or within six months after primary surgery, and metachronous, if it develops after more than six months. The monthly follow-up period was for one year - by inspection, photographs using digital camera, palpation of cervical lymph nodes and CT scan for head and neck region. Biopsies were repeated if there is a new, recurrent whitish or reddish patches or, ulcerative lesion which had developed, or field region that became symptomatic with a change in the normal color and texture of the mucosa. Fine needle aspiration cytology and computed tomography with contrast were performed for diagnosis of suspicious of regional nodal recurrence.

DNA analysis

The association between the DNA ploidy and the clinico-histopathological parameters was assessed with the Chi-square test. The differences in the mean values of the DNA index (DI) in the various groups were evaluated with the Student’s t test. The DNA index was measured by dividing the number of DNA aneuploidy peaks by the corresponding number of the DNA diploid peak. In case of only one cell cycle, the DNA index was set at 1.00. The cases were classified in two subclasses, as follows: DNA diploid (only one cell cycle present) and DNA aneuploidy (DNA index > 1.1). Additionally, the DNA analysis were presented in histograms for normal tissues (Fig. 1) and OSCC specimens (Fig. 2). Coefficient of variation (CV) is an important statistical test used in DNA cytometry analysis, as the lower the CV value, the narrower the reference peak G0/G1, and so the greater accuracy of the analysis. In the present study its value was 2. The SPF was determined as percentage of cells occupying the region between the mean channel number for G0/G1 and that of G2/M. The cutoff point for the SPF was set as the mean ± 2 standard deviations and reported either as low or high.

Statistical analysis

Data were entered in a computer master sheet using SPSS version 26. Statistical analysis was set at 95% confidence level; 0.2, the width of the confidence interval and the level of significance alpha is 0.05. Chi-square test was used to test the difference between categorical variables and the Fisher’s exact test was used as an alternative test in case of sparse data. Multivariate analysis and logistic regression tests were performed for better prediction and precision to control confounders and both were interpreted in the form of odd ratio and P value [15].

Results

The present study is a prospective longitudinal hospital based study carried out from 2019 to 2023, included 93 patients (186 specimens) with oral squamous cell carcinoma who were diagnosed and surgically treated at KTDH. Fifty seven (61.3%) of the patients were males and 36 (38.7%) were females. The patients ages ranged between 25 and 80 years, with a mean age of 57.52 ± 12.506 years. Thirty eight (39.9%) of the patients were snuff dippers, 19 (20.4%) were tobacco smokers and 14 (15.1%) were alcohol drinkers (Table 1).

Clinical characteristics of the lesions

Ninety-two patients had multiple sites involved in the oral cavity and all of them had a clinically detected overlapping in the presentations and sites involved. The gingivobuccal and gingivolabial mucosae were the most frequent sites involved; 69 (74.2%) and 58 (62.3%) respectively. The tongue and the floor of the mouth were involved in 23 (24.7%) and 26 (28%) patients respectively (Table 1). All the 93 (100%) OSCC patients presented with an ulcer as a chief complaint; in 83 (89.2%) of them, the lesion was surrounded by mucosal changes (Table 2). Forty-four (47.3%) of the patients had an overlapping presentations and were surrounded by erythematous patches, which highlighted the clinical presentation of wide field of changes (Figs. 3 and 4).

Of the series, 69 (74.2%) patients presented with advanced stage IVa (T4a + N0/N1/N2 + M0) tumors, seven (7.5%) patients presented with stage IVb. Additionally,17 (18.2%) patients presented with stage III tumors, and there were no cases of early cancer i.e. stage I and stage II among the series (Table 1). 84% of the snuff dippers had multiple sites involvement of OSCC. In the snuff dippers 95% of the patients had a surrounding field of mixed white and dark discoloration and 71% had erythematous patches.

Surgical treatment and adjuvant therapy

A total of 91 (97.8%) patients underwent modified comprehensive neck dissection; 23(24.7%) of them; underwent also a supraomohyoid ND on the contralateral side. Thirteen (14.0%) of the patients received surgical reconstruction, nine (9.7%) were reconstructed by nasolabial flap and four (4.3%) of the cases were reconstructed by a deltopectoral flap. The other cases didn’t need reconstruction by flaps either closed primarily or left for secondary healing. Seventy-eight (83.8%) patients received adjuvant therapy; concurrent chemoradiation therapy (CCRT) was given to 58 (62.4%) patients, and radiotherapy alone was given to 20 (21.5%) patients (Table 1).

Postoperative histopathological characteristics

In 64 (68.8%) patients, the tumor was a well differentiated squamous cell carcinoma. The presence of histopathological dysplasia at the surgical margins in OSCC cases with wide field of cancerization was identified in only 13 (14.0%) of the cases, and all of them showed mild dysplasia. Extranodal extension (ENE) was identified in 43 (46.2%) of the cases. Fifty-eight (62.4%) of the cases showed positive pathological nodal involvement, and one third (37.6%) of the cases showed absence of histological nodal metastasis. The status of the surgical margins (Table 1) in 92 (98.9%) patients showed negative margins [15].

Recurrence and SPTs characteristics

Twenty-eight (30%) of the patients developed recurrence, 17 (18.3%) of them had regional recurrence, 12 (71%) of them developed recurrence on the non- operated contralateral side of the neck and five (29%) of them developed recurrence in the operated neck side. Eight (8.6%) patients developed local recurrence, and three (3.2%) patients developed locoregional recurrence [15]. With regard to the wide field of oral cancerization, 14 (15.1%) of the cases developed second primary lesions, who were defined clinically as new independent lesions more than 2 cm away from the primary tumors and diagnosed histopathologically by biopsies (Table 3). The commonest sites reported for the new recurrent lesions and SPTs were the buccal mucosa and the floor of the mouth.

Fifty-four (58.0%) specimens showed an S phase fraction 20, and 20 (21.8%) specimens showed an S phase fraction 10. The mean S phase fraction of field (F) lesions was 20.55 ± standard deviation of 2.18 with a minimum of 10 and a maximum of 50.

S phase fraction in OSCC specimens

The S phase fraction 40 and the SPF 30 were found in 28 (30.1%) and 26 (27.9%) of the specimens respectively. The mean S phase fraction of OSCC lesions was 38.17 ± 3.057 with a minimum of 10 and a maximum of 70. The extra nodal extension was significantly associated with the DNA hyperploidy of the OSCC lesions (P.value = 0.040) and high SPF of 40 OSCC lesions (P.value = 0.047).

Positive metastatic lymph nodes were significantly associated with DNA hyperploidy of OSCC lesions (P.value = 0.002) and DNA hyperploidy of the field lesions (P.value = 0.000).

Multivariate cox regression analysis (Tables 6 and 7) showed that patients with high SPF and DNA hyperploidy of OSCC of the lesions have 28% increase in the risk of local recurrence (P.value = 0.032) and 1.5% risk to develop regional recurrence (P.value = 0.000). DNA hyperploidy and high SPF of OSCC increase the risk of having second primary tumor by 1.6 times (P.value 0.003).

Discussion

The present study is designed to shed more lights on oral cancer in the Sudan, by its paramount analysis and investigations of the DNA ploidy and the S phase fraction, as prognosticators using high resolution flowcytometry as the first study in the Sudan. There is no previous published study on oral cancer that used this technique accompanied by a close follow-up examination.

Regarding risky social habits, Idris and colleagues in a house to house survey reported a high prevalence of Toombak dipping in rural areas in Sudan mounting up to nearly 50% in the fourth decade age group and older; which is at least twice as high as the reported prevalence rates in other countries [16]. One of the known clinical characteristics of wide field of oral cancerization, is the wide involvement by OSCC at multiple oral cavity sites. The present study showed that almost all the cases involved had multiple sites. In contrast to the previous published studies [3, 16, 17], which showed the gingivolabial site as the most frequent site for OSSC in the Sudan, the present study showed the gingivobuccal mucosa is the commonest site, and second to it was the gingivolabial mucosa. The involvement of the buccal mucosa in most of the cases might be due to the lateral spread of the malignant cells and to its wide exposure to the carcinogenic substances, which reinforce the concept of wide field of cancerization in a subclinical involvement. The nature of Toombak and the way it is dipped, whether in the labial sulcus or in the buccal sulcus, makes its carcinogenic material pool in multiple sites in the oral cavity. In the present study ,74.2% of the cases presented with stage IVa, and 20% of the patients with stage III. The same findings were reported by Elsheikh M. who showed that 83.2% of her cases presented with advanced stages III and IV, and only 15.7% of their patients were at early stages [18], and previously the same observation was reported by Osman and colleagues [19] in 2010 showing that 94% of their patients at KTDH presented in stage 1 V. The late presentation of oral cancer patients in the Sudan is mostly due to; lack of awareness, weakness of the health system and the ailing economy of the State. Feller et al., [20] in their review article concluded that the concept of the field cancerization can explain why several lesions of OSCC develop from, an intimately adjacent potentially malignant tissue. It also shows that a clinically normal-looking epithelium neighboring OSCC, might be harboring cells with genetic mutations and/or cells with dysplastic changes. The DNA analysis field is a breakthrough area of interest, as genetic mutations are considered as one of the most determinant characteristics of carcinogenesis [21]. It is well documented that the assembling of genetic alterations is needed for malignant transformation. Consequently, alterations affecting normal chromosome stability may result in DNA aneuploidy and abnormal nuclear content [22]. In the present study the DNA status was examined in the primary tumor and in the field of cancerization. In the field of cancerization, 55.9% of the specimens had diploidy DNA status and 44% had aneuploidy DNA status. On the other hand, in the OSCC specimens, 89% of the cases showed aneuploidy DNA status, double the percentage of the aneuploidy in the field specimens, and 73.1% of them had hyperploidy DNA status. The DNA hypoploidy was reported in 15.1% of the OSCC specimens. The higher incidence rate of DNA aneuploidy in OSCC cases in the present study is most probably due to the presence of advanced stages tumors in the series, and also to biopsies taken from the deeper tissues of the specimens, with the intention to increase the chance of taking the maximum amount of the tumor cells. This noticeable finding is in agreement with the results of Zyle and colleagues which demonstrated that the hypoploidy status is rarely identified during the early stages of malignancy transformation and progression, mostly due to its lethality at the cellular level [23]. Their findings are consistent with the present study as the hypoploidy status is lower than hyperploidy in the OSCC lesions, although most of the cases presented lately at advanced tumor stages. It is worthwhile to mention that OSCC fresh specimens as the case in the present study were reported to have a higher incidence rate of DNA aneuploidy mounting up to 58%, than OSCC tissues obtained and analyzed from paraffin blocks, which was found ranging from 30 to 41% [13, 24, 25]. Johnson et al., reported that the fraction of a cell population in a known phase at any given period is proportional to the duration of that phase. The S phase fraction can be defined as the percentage or number of cells arranging for mitosis by their active doubling of DNA [12]. Similarly to the present study, Joshi et al., reported that the SPF was significantly high in poorly differentiated OSCC lesions, as most of their cases were poorly differentiated SCC tumors in comparison to the present study findings [26]. Regarding recurrence, similar findings to the present study were reported by Anderson et al., [27] and Priya et al., [28] who found 21% and 22.2% recurrence rates in their OSCC cases, with clear surgical margins respectively. Local and locoregional recurrences as a paramount clinical outcome of OSCC associated with wide field of cancerization were reported by Eltohami and Suleiman in cases associated with different mucosal changes, specifically Toombak dippers [15]. The present study showed that more than half of the overall recurrent cases had a hyperploid DNA, and 65% of the cases with regional recurrences had a DNA hypoploidy. Notably, all cases with local and locoregional recurrences showed hyperploid DNA with higher SPF in OSCC specimens. Regarding the SPTs, similar finding was reported by Cianfriglia F et al., as in their OSCC patients, the incidence rate of SPTs was 14% [29]. These reports, underline the risk of developing SPTs over the years, and emphasizing the importance of a close and regular follow-up of patients of OSCC, particularly, those with wide field of oral cancerization. Importantly, to mention that all these SPTs have occurred in OSCC patients who were Toombak dippers. However, there is no significant association between the dysplasia and the development of the SPTs and the former is not considered as a prognosis predictor for their occurrence. In the present study, in 73% of the cases of second primary lesions, the DNA aneuploidy was identified, 75% and 25% of these DNA aneuploidy cases had specifically DNA hyperploidy and hypoploidy in the field specimens respectively. Additionally, in 91% of the SPTs, cases, the DNA aneuploidy showed significant presence, and that 80% of these cases had hyperploid DNA with higher S phase fraction.

The multivariate analysis demonstrated that the high SPF and DNA hyperploidy of OSCC and the field of cancerization increases the risk of having SPTs by 1.6 times (p.value 0.003), ascertaining that the DNA ploidy and SPF should be considered as prognosis predictors for the development of SPTs in oral SCC patients.

The differences in the values of DNA ploidy and SPF of OSCC cases in this study and what was reported in the literature may be due to the following: first, the different nature of OSCC cases reported in the present study as most of them were associated with a clinical wide field of cancerization and map field biopsies were taken from the cases which showed different values of DNA ploidy and SPF. Second: almost half of the cases in the present study were Toombak dippers, emphasizing the culpability of Toombak dipping in the clinical and subclinical involvement of its carcinogenic ingredients on the phenomenon of field of cancerization. Third, different methods of cytometric analysis were performed to study the DNA contents parameters either flow or image or in some studies both of them as comparative studies. Fourth, the existence of variations in methods of tissues preparations (fresh or fixed) and in methods demonstrated to analyze and define the DNA ploidy and the DNA index.

Conclusion

The present study showed that 40% oral cancer patients attendinh KTDH for management were snuff dippers, and 76% of them were diagnosed with very advanced stages of OSCC. Almost all the OSCC patients had wide field of cancerization on the initial presentation. 30% of the patients developed recurrence and in 15% of the cases had second primary tumors.

90% of the cases showed aneuploidy DNA and high SPF in their OSCC specimens, and in the normal tissues specimens, and the DNA aneuploidy was identified in 35% of these cases. This prop up the concept of wide field of cancerization phenomenon, with subclinical involvement and highlight the importance of a strict regular follow-up. The present study demonstrated that the DNA ploidy and the S phase fraction are independent prognosticators for prediction of OSCC nodal metastasis, recurrences, and development of SPTs.

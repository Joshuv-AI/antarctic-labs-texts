Pristine Massive Star Formation Caught at the Break of Cosmic Dawn
Takahiro Morishita, Zhaoran Liu, Massimo Stiavelli, Tommaso Treu, Pietro Bergamini, Yechi Zhang

Abstract
The existence of galaxies with no heavy elements is a key prediction of cosmological models. So far no “zero-metallicity", or Population III, galaxies have been identified. Here, we report the identification of an extremely metal-poor galaxy at redshift z = 5.725 z=5.725 (“AMORE6"), multiply imaged by a foreground galaxy cluster. JWST spectra consistently detect 𝐇 ​ β {\rm H\beta} at both positions, but [O iii ] λλ4960,5008 remains undetected. This places a firm upper limit on its oxygen abundance, < < 0.12 % 0.12\,\% of solar metallicity at 𝟐 ​ σ 2\,\sigma , establishing itself as the most pristine object by far. AMORE6 exhibits exceptional properties that indicate the presence of pristine massive star formation. The finding of such an example at a relatively late cosmic time is surprising, but it also validates the basic ideas behind the Big Bang model.



The existence of galaxies with no heavy elements – elements heavier than Helium that were formed by stars after Big Bang nucleosynthesis – is a key prediction of cosmological models. Finding them would provide direct and transformative evidence of the formation of the first galaxies in extremely different conditions than those observed today. The doubly lensed system “33.1a/b" was first observed with VLT/MUSE
[

{\rm Ly\alpha}

z=5.725

We analyzed public NIRCam WFSS data from Cycle 2 program “All the Little Things" (GO 3516; PIs: Matthee & Naidu)
[
22

3

4\,\mu

56^{\circ}
and
60^{\circ}

The spectra of the two images (hereafter AMORE6-A and AMORE6-B) are shown in Figure

Along the spectral trace at the position of AMORE6-B, we clearly detect an emission line at
\sim 3.271\,\mu
m, matching the expected wavelength of
{\rm H\beta}
based on the
{\rm Ly\alpha}
redshift measured by VLT/MUSE. We detect an emission line at the same wavelength in individual spectra at both PAs (Methods, “NIRCam WFSS Reduction"). The presence of the emission line at the same wavelength in both spectra firmly attributes the origin to the
{\rm H\beta}
line of AMORE6, not spectral contamination from other surrounding sources. Similarly, the
{\rm H\beta}
line is detected in the spectrum of AMORE6-A, the counter image of AMORE6-B. While AMORE6-A is located in a relatively crowded region near two foreground galaxies, the spectra extracted from both PA data sets reveal
{\rm H\beta}
at the expected position as well. Lastly, we combine the 1-dimensional spectra of AMORE6-A and AMORE6-B, correcting each for its respective magnification factor to recover intrinsic flux densities, and stack them using inverse-variance weighting (Figure
2

Oxygen Abundance

Intriguingly, none of the reduced spectra reveal the [O
iii
]-doublet lines (rest-frame
\lambda\lambda 4960,5008
) at the expected wavelength. The doublet lines are usually very bright (

/
{\rm H\beta}

\gg 1
) in star-forming galaxies with non-zero metallicity. The absence of [O
iii

Using the conservative upper limit derived from the stack spectrum and strong line calibrations derived from photo-ionization models (Methods, “Inference of Oxygen Abundance"), we place a firm upper limit on the oxygen abundance, 12+
\log{\rm(O/H)}

<
5.8 (
2\sigma
), or
<

0.12\,\%
of solar metallicity.

Photometric Properties

Besides the spectroscopic features revealed above, AMORE6 has several exceptional characteristics, as inferred from the 20-band NIRCam photometry (see Methods). Those are: (i) low stellar mass, (ii) blue UV spectral slope
\beta_{\rm UV}
, (iii) high specific star formation rate, (iv) compact morphology, and (v) negative continuum break across the Balmer limit, or “Balmer jump."

AMORE6 is very low-mass (
M_{*}=

5.6\times 10^{5}\,M_{\odot}
), UV faint (
M_{\rm UV}=

-14.5
ABmag), and blue (
\beta_{\rm UV}=-2.8
). The derived UV slope of AMORE6 is below the lower limit that can be produced by standard stellar population models or nebular continuum (
\approx-2.5
), likely requiring enhanced massive star populations
[
27
,
28
]
. Using the
{\rm H\beta}
-based star formation rate (SFR), we obtain a high specific star formation rate,
\sim 630
Gyr
-1
. Such intense star formation activity is consistent with the hypothesis that this galaxy is currently undergoing its first burst of star formation without having been pre-enriched. It is also interesting to note that the inferred stellar mass is compatible with what could be expected in an atomic cooling halo with modest star-formation efficiency. This would be in line with star formation in a pristine gas cloud. The possible existence of such pristine halos at redshift around 6 (or even lower) was predicted theoretically
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
, and AMORE6 stands out as the best candidate observed so far.

Despite the large magnification, AMORE6 remains compact in the image plane, without showing any obvious extended features or associated clumps. This suggests that AMORE6 is intrinsically very compact, which makes it distinct from previously reported strongly lensed objects at high redshift
[
33
,
34
]
. Using the F150W image, we measured an effective radius of
R_{e}=

28.0_{-1.8}^{+1.8}
pc, which is comparable to the characteristic scale of massive star cluster formation
[
35
,
36
]
. This suggests that AMORE6 resides in extremely dense gas, which is reflected in its exceptionally high stellar mass and SFR surface densities,
\Sigma_{*}=

1.8\times 10^{2}\,M_{\odot}
pc
-2
and
\Sigma_{\rm SFR}=

1.4\times 10^{2}\,M_{\odot}
yr
-1
kpc
-2
.

Lastly, we note that our spectral energy distribution (SED) model indicates a high contribution by the nebular component over the stellar component, which is expected in a young (a few
10^{6}
yr), high temperature (
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5\times 10^{4}
K) environment
[
37
,
38
]
.
The detection of a strong Balmer jump (Methods, “SED Modeling") supports the presence of hot ionizing sources
[
39
]
, suggesting the dominance of very young stars, formed within the last
\sim 10
Myr.
Using the continuum flux density derived by our SED modeling, we find a very high
{\rm H\beta}
rest-frame equivalent width, EW
{}_{0}=

1600\pm 200
Å, which can be reached only by the most massive stars
(>50
–
100\,M_{\odot})
at young ages (
<4
Myr)
[
40
,
28
]
.
All the observations here support the hypothesis that AMORE6 hosts primordial, massive star formation on a very small physical scale, making it the best metal-free galaxy candidate so far.

Implications for Cosmological Models

AMORE6 is observed about one billion years after the Big Bang. Previous studies have found that galaxies were already enriched at high redshift, within the first few hundred million years
[
7
,
10
,
8
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
11
]
. The finding of a near-pristine object at a relatively late time in cosmic history could be seen as surprising even though it is not entirely unexpected theoretically
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
. Indeed, the existence of objects like AMORE6 is a powerful validation of our galaxy formation and clustering models. We note that near the redshift of AMORE6, there are two
evolved
overdensities of galaxies along the same sightline, at
z=5.66
and
z=5.78

[
44
]
. As shown in Figure
4
, AMORE6 falls in the “empty" space, where the aforementioned study found
zero
spectroscopically confirmed galaxies, existing between the two overdensities. One possible interpretation is that the gravitational potential towards both overdensities might have kept the space in-between relatively clean of galactic pollution delaying the formation of galaxies therein to this relatively late cosmic time and enabling some objects to remain pristine
[
45
,
46
]
. The case of AMORE6 is also encouraging, as it showcases the presence of near-pristine galaxies well within the reach of JWST.

The identification of a pristine object represents the confirmation of our models predicting that elements heavier than Helium are made in stars and is a basic validation of the cosmological models for the growth of structure in the Universe. While JWST has shown that many of the details of our models for galaxy formation need to be revised, it has also enabled us to confirm the basic ideas behind those models.

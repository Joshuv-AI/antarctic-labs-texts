Baseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging in outcome prediction in adult patients with Hodgkins lymphoma
Authors: Xianwen Hu, Guangyao Yang, Ying Zhao, Pan Wang, Changyin Yu, Jiong Cai, Dongfeng Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate the prognostic value of baseline and interim Fluorine-18-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) parameters, including maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), total lesion glycolysis (TLG), and Deauville score, in adult patients with Hodgkin lymphoma (HL).MethodsA single-center retrospective study included adult patients with HL who underwent baseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT scans after four chemotherapy cycles from December 2018 to October 2025. Baseline and interim PET/CT parameters (SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, TLG) were collected, and interim response was assessed using the Deauville score. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were analyzed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, and Cox regression models.ResultsA total of 116 patients were included (81 males, 35 females), with a median age of 35 years and a median follow-up of 44 months (IQR 37–58). ROC analysis first established the individual prognostic performance and optimal cut-off values for PET parameters, with interim MTV and TLG achieving AUCs of 0.891 and 0.896 for OS, respectively (cut-offs: 21.8 cm³ and 94.4 g). Subsequently, multivariable Cox regression identified baseline MTV, interim MTV, and the Deauville score as significant independent predictors of PFS. Finally, based on these independent factors, combined models were evaluated; however, they yielded AUCs of 0.901 (95% CI: 0.857–0.943) for OS and 0.824 (95% CI: 0.745–0.902) for PFS, which showed comparable efficacy and did not significantly surpass the predictive power of individual quantitative metrics. While similar trends for interim MTV and the Deauville score were observed for OS, the analysis was constrained by the small number of death events.ConclusionBaseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters-particularly interim MTV and Deauville score-were associated with survival outcomes in adult HL. Introduction

Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) is a highly curable lymphoid malignancy in the modern era of combination chemotherapy. Standard frontline regimens such as ABVD (A, doxorubicin; B, bleomycin; V, vincristine and D, dacarbazine) and BEACOPP (B, bleomycin; E, etoposide; A, adriamycin; C, cyclophosphamide; O, oncovin; P, Procarbazine and P, prednisone) achieve favorable outcomes for a large proportion of adult patients [1]. Nevertheless, a clinically meaningful subset still experiences relapse or disease progression, which subsequently necessitates salvage therapy and may compromise long-term survival [2, 3]. Therefore, identifying patients at increased risk of treatment failure early in the disease course remains an important clinical objective, as it may enable timely optimization of therapeutic strategies and improve outcome prediction beyond conventional clinical staging alone.

Fluorine-18-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) has become an essential imaging modality for HL, supporting disease staging, evaluation of treatment response, and follow-up assessment. In particular, interim PET/CT performed during chemotherapy has been widely adopted as a response assessment tool [4, 5]. Currently, visual interpretation based on the Deauville score provides a standardized five-point scale to grade residual FDG uptake. Interim PET positivity, as reflected by higher Deauville scores, is consistently associated with inferior outcomes, underscoring its value in response-adapted decision-making [6]. However, while the Deauville score is clinically practical, visual scoring alone is susceptible to subjective interpretation and may not fully capture the spatial heterogeneity of the metabolic tumor burden.To overcome these limitations, quantitative PET/CT parameters offer complementary and objective information. SUV-based indices (maximum standardized uptake value, SUVmax and mean standardized uptake value, SUVmean) provide semi-quantitative measures of FDG avidity, whereas volumetric metrics, such as metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), reflect the whole-body tumor burden by integrating both the extent and intensity of metabolic activity [7, 8]. Importantly, volumetric parameters may better represent residual disease burden compared with single-voxel uptake measurements, and thus may provide additional prognostic insight when assessing response after several cycles of chemotherapy [9, 10]. In the current literature, these parameters have demonstrated robust prognostic value in non-Hodgkin lymphomas, particularly diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), where high baseline MTV is consistently associated with inferior survival outcomes [11]. Furthermore, the integration of these volumetric metrics has shown significant predictive power in several solid tumors, such as non-small cell lung cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma [12]. Given their proven efficacy in refining risk stratification in these cancers, there is a compelling rationale to deeply investigate the prognostic utility of both baseline and interim volumetric parameters in adult HL.

In parallel, prognostic scoring systems and laboratory markers-including the International Prognostic Score (IPS), German Hodgkin Study Group (GHSG) score, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and β2-microglobulin (β2-MG)-remain widely used to estimate baseline risk. However, these clinical indices do not directly quantify metabolic response and may be insufficient for individualized outcome prediction when used alone [15, 16]. Integrating baseline clinical characteristics with quantitative baseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT metrics may improve risk stratification and facilitate more precise prediction of overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).

Accordingly, the aim of this study was to evaluate the prognostic value of baseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters, including SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG, as well as interim response assessed by Deauville score, in adult patients with HL. We further explored the performance of these imaging biomarkers using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, and Cox regression models, with the goal of identifying imaging predictors associated with survival outcomes and providing evidence to support more informative response evaluation in routine practice.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients selection

A single-center retrospective study was conducted on the data of all adult patients with HL who underwent 18F - FDG PET/CT imaging at our hospital from December 2018 to October 2025. Baseline clinical and pathological characteristics were collected, including gender, age, histological subtype, Ann Arbor stage, B symptoms, Epstein-Barr virus antibody status, bulky disease, bone marrow involvement, LDH, β2-MG, IPS and GHSG score.

The inclusion criteria for patients were as follows:

(i) Newly diagnosed HL patients aged ≥ 18 years; (ii) The first - line chemotherapy regimen was either the ABVD (or ABV or AVD) regimen or the BEACOPP (or BEACOPP-ABVD/ AVD) regimen; (iii) Both baseline and interim (following four cycles of chemotherapy) PET/CT scans were available, and no anti-tumor-related treatment was received before the baseline PET/CT imaging; (iv) Enrolled patients must have complete medical history records and follow-up results. Exclusion criteria included: (i) no positive lesions detected on baseline PET/CT imaging; (ii) a history of prior malignancies or the presence of concurrent malignant tumors; and (iii) a follow-up observation time of less than six months post-treatment initiation.

18F-FDG PET/CT imaging

Baseline PET/CT was performed within 2 weeks prior to the initiation of treatment. Interim PET/CT was conducted after four cycles of protocol chemotherapy, with a 14-21-day interval from the completion of the fourth cycle. 18F-FDG was synthesized using the F300E modular automatic synthesis unit of the HM-10HP cyclotron system (Sumitomo, Japan) and intravenously administered at 0.1–0.15 mCi/kg after confirming radiochemical purity greater than 95%. All patients fasted for at least 6 h before the examination, and their fasting blood glucose levels were maintained below 11.1 mmol/L. PET/CT imaging was performed 1 h after injection using a Biograph mCT scanner (Siemens, Germany), with the scanning range extending from the vertex of the skull to the mid-femur. CT scans were acquired with the following parameters: tube voltage of 120 kV, tube current of 119 mA, and slice thickness of 5 mm. After CT acquisition, PET scanning was carried out immediately (2 min per bed position, total of 6–7 beds). PET images were subjected to attenuation correction using CT data and reconstructed with the TrueX + TOF algorithm after acquisition.

Image analysis

Visual and semi-quantitative analyses of PET/CT images were conducted by two nuclear medicine diagnosticians, each having a minimum of 5 years of clinical experience. Visual evaluation involved anatomical and metabolic observations. No abnormal tracer accumulation was detected in areas considered benign, even those with elevated SUV values, such as diffuse splenic uptake or intramedullary accumulation after chemotherapy.

PET and CT data were processed using software of Shanghai Xingxiang Medical Device Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for tumor metabolic assessment. Regions of interest (ROIs) were delineated along the edge of each lymphoma lesion. The relative threshold method was employed, with 41% of SUVmax as the cutoff value. The software automatically carried out volumetric segmentation to obtain the lesion’s SUVmax, SUVmean, and MTV [17]. The total body MTV was determined by manually adding up the MTV measurements of all lesions. The TLG of each lesion was calculated by multiplying its MTV by SUVmean. The total body TLG was determined by manually adding up the TLG measurements of all lesions.

Treatment, prognosis assessment, and follow-up

All patients received ABVD (or ABV or AVD) or BEACOP (or BEACOPP-ABVD/AVD) as first-line chemotherapy regimens, and prognosis was evaluated using the IPS and GHSG scoring systems. The IPS and GHSG scores were calculated for each patient.

The IPS consists of 7 adverse prognostic factors: (i) male gender; (ii) age ≥ 45 years; (iii) clinical stage IV; (iv) albumin level < 40 g/L; (v) hemoglobin (Hb) level < 105 g/L; (vi) white blood cell (WBC) count > 15 × 10⁹/L; (vii) absolute lymphocyte count < 0.6 × 10⁹/L or lymphocyte percentage < 8%. Each factor is assigned 1 point. The GHSG score includes:

(i) mediastinal bulky mass (ratio of the maximum transverse diameter of the mediastinal mass to the maximum thoracic transverse diameter > 1/3); (ii) presence of extranodal involvement; (iii) erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) ≥ 50 mm/1 h, or ESR ≥ 30 mm/1 h in patients with B symptoms; (iv) involvement of ≥ 3 lymph node regions. Each factor is assigned 1 point.

Follow-up methods include regular outpatient or inpatient follow-up, and the follow-up will end on October 15, 2025.

PFS and OS

PFS is defined as the time from disease diagnosis (obtaining pathological results) to the first occurrence of disease progression, recurrence, or death. Patients who did not experience any of these events were censored at the date of last follow-up. OS is defined as the time from disease diagnosis to death for any reason. Patients who were still alive at the end of the study period were censored at the date of last follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 29.0 software. Quantitative data that did not conform to a normal distribution were presented as medians (M) (Q1, Q3), while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. ROC analysis was used to determine the optimal cut-off values of SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG for prognostic prediction, and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated. The comparison of AUCs between the individual parameters and the combined model was performed using the DeLong test. Survival rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Univariate survival analysis was performed with the log-rank test, and multivariate survival analysis was conducted using the Cox proportional hazards regression model. A two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For comparisons involving more than two groups, the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post-hoc test with Bonferroni correction was performed to account for multiple comparisons.

ROC analysis

The AUC values of most parameters of interim PET/CT are better than those of baseline PET/CT parameters and clinical characteristics such as β2-microglobulin, IPS and GHSG score in evaluating both PFS and OS (Supplemental Table 1).The ROC curve is shown in Fig. 3. Furthermore, another ROC analysis examined the relationship between baseline PET/CT parameters and treatment response (Fig. 4), and the results indicated that higher baseline SUVmax, MTV, and TLG levels were associated with poor treatment outcomes. To determine if a synergistic approach enhances prognostic precision, we evaluated multivariable models incorporating interim MTV and the Deauville score for OS, alongside a combination of interim MTV, Deauville score, and baseline MTV for PFS. For predicting OS, the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.901 (95% CI: 0.857–0.943), compared to 0.891 for interim MTV alone and 0.824 for Deauville score alone. DeLong test revealed that the combined model showed a trend of higher accuracy without statistical significance (p = 0.218). Similarly, as for PFS, the combined model yielded an AUC of 0.824 (95% CI: 0.745–0.902), which did not represent a statistically significant improvement in predictive accuracy compared to using the individual metric (p = 0.442). Furthermore, to evaluate the impact of disease stage on our findings, we performed a subgroup analysis specifically focusing on advanced-stage adult HL patients (Ann Arbor stages IIB-IV, n = 87). As detailed in Supplemental Table 3, the quantitative parameters maintained robust prognostic performance in this high-risk subgroup. For PFS, interim MTV demonstrated the highest predictive value (AUC 0.851, sensitivity 93.5%, specificity 75.0%, p < 0.001), followed by baseline MTV (AUC 0.760, p < 0.001) and Deauville score (AUC 0.744, p = 0.002). Furthermore, interim MTV and Deauville score also remained significant predictors for OS in advanced-stage patients (AUC 0.730, p = 0.0326 and AUC 0.742, p = 0.0231, respectively). For early-stage patients (stages I-IIA), separate ROC analysis was not performed due to the limited number of endpoint events, reflecting their overall excellent prognosis.

Cox regression analysis

In univariate analysis, high values of PET/CT parameters, including baseline and interim SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, TLG, Deauville score, and clinical characteristics such as β2 - MG, IPS score, and Ki-67 index, were correlated with poor OS and PFS. In multivariate analysis, interim MTV and Deauville score were identified as independent risk factors for OS in adult HL patients. However, due to the limited number of death events (n = 8), the multivariable analysis for OS lacks robust statistical power and should be considered strictly exploratory. For PFS, in addition to interim MTV and Deauville score, baseline MTV is also an independent risk factor. Additionally, we evaluated the relative reduction in PET parameters between baseline and interim scans (△SUVmax and △MTV). However, univariate analysis revealed that these dynamic change metrics did not achieve statistical significance in predicting PFS or OS (all p > 0.05, Table 3).

Metabolic response

Except for the baseline SUV mean, all other baseline and interim PET parameters showed correlations with post-treatment Deauville scores, among which the baseline SUV max exhibiting a relatively small correlation coefficient (r = 0.244, p = 0.018). Pairwise comparisons corrected for multiple testing revealed that PET parameters (SUVmax, MTV, and TLG) were significantly higher in patients with a Deauville score of 4–5 compared to those with a score of 1–3 (all padj < 0.05) (Fig. 5, Supplemental Table 2).

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 116 adult patients with HL, we assessed the prognostic value of baseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT quantitative parameters and interim response by Deauville score. With a median follow-up of 44 months (IQR 37–58), relapse/progression occurred in 39 patients (33.6%) and 8 patients (6.9%) died, underscoring the need for improved risk stratification in a real-world population.

A major finding of the current study was the strong prognostic performance of interim volumetric metrics, particularly MTV and TLG, for both OS and PFS. ROC analysis showed interim MTV achieved an AUC of 0.891 for OS (cut-off value 21.8; p = 0.001) and 0.845 for PFS (cut-off value 12.1; p = 0.001). Interim TLG yielded an AUC of 0.896 for OS (cut-off value 94.4; p = 0.001) and 0.884 for PFS (cut-off value 33.9; p = 0.001). These results indicate that residual whole-body metabolic tumor burden during therapy provides clinically informative prediction of outcomes. Consistently, the cohort demonstrated marked reductions from baseline to interim imaging, with MTV decreasing from 105 (40, 307) to 10.5 (4.8, 27.1) and TLG from 753.9 (239.9, 2475.5) to 23.9 (8.2, 71.9), and a mean △MTV (%) of 83.4 ± 32.6. SUV-based interim parameters were also prognostic, with AUC values of 0.844 (interim SUVmax; cut-off value 6.3; p = 0.002) and 0.846 (interim SUVmean; cut-off value 3.7; p = 0.002) for OS, and 0.803 and 0.796 for PFS (both p = 0.001). However, compared with SUV metrics, the higher AUCs of interim MTV and TLG suggest that volumetric parameters may better capture global residual disease burden.

When comparing our derived cut-off values with the existing literature, several important clinical and methodological observations emerge. In our cohort, the optimal cut-off for baseline MTV predicting progression-free survival was 112.9 cm³. This is largely consistent with previous studies in adult Hodgkin lymphoma, where high-risk baseline MTV thresholds typically range from 100 to 250 cm³, depending on the disease stage and cohort characteristics [13, 14]. For interim parameters, our study identified an optimal interim MTV cut-off of 12.1 cm³ for PFS. The significantly lower threshold for interim MTV compared to baseline logically reflects the dramatic reduction in metabolic tumor burden following four cycles of chemotherapy, emphasizing that even a small volume of residual metabolically active disease is a strong harbinger of relapse.

Deauville score remained clinically relevant, with AUC values of 0.824 for OS (cut-off value 4; p = 0.004) and 0.762 for PFS (cut-off value 3; p = 0.001). Deauville score was strongly correlated with interim PET metrics, especially interim SUVmax (Spearman’s r = 0.871, p < 0.001), and showed moderate correlations with interim MTV (r = 0.587) and interim TLG (r = 0.671), indicating substantial overlap between visual scoring and quantitative measures. In multivariable Cox regression analysis, interim MTV remained significant for OS (hazard ratio 1.08; p = 0.027) and PFS (hazard ratio 1.01; p = 0.030), while Deauville score also retained prognostic significance for both endpoints (OS: p = 0.025; PFS: p = 0.014). Previous studies showed that high values of interim PET parameters MTV and TLG are associated with poor PFS in pediatric HL patients, but this finding has not been confirmed in adult cohorts [18, 19]. A previous study indicated that SUVmax is not an independent prognostic factor, which contrasts with our findings [20]. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in the study cohorts, as their research exclusively included patients with early-stage HL.

The prognosis of HL may be related to multiple factors, including heterogeneity of disease biology, comorbidities, poor physical condition, and organ failure [21]. Even the same treatment regimen, there are still differences in OS rates between elderly and young HL patients [22]. LDH to some extent reflects the burden of tumors, and a study showed that patients with higher LDH have relatively poorer prognosis [23]. However, our results showed that neither age nor LDH was a risk factor influencing OS and PFS. Additionally, the current study also evaluated the prognostic value of some clinical parameters such as Ki-67 index, β2-MG, bone marrow involvement, maximum diameter of tumor, IPS and GHSG score in predicting the prognosis of adult HL. The results showed that these indicators were all related to the patient’s PFS or OS, but multivariate analysis revealed that these clinical indicators were not independent factors affecting the patient’s prognosis. In addition to traditional clinical markers, a recent study has highlighted that longitudinal body composition changes evaluated via PET/CT, such as sarcopenia during treatment, also serve as significant prognostic factors in HL patients [24].

Interim PET/CT is usually performed 2–4 weeks after 2–6 rounds of chemotherapy/ chemotherapy immunotherapy, to evaluate post-treatment response and make adjustments to the treatment plan, known as response or risk adaptive therapy [25, 26]. The current study further analyzed the baseline PET parameters and the metabolic changes of the lesions on the post-treatment interim PET scans. The results showed that higher SUVmax, MTV and TLG were associated with poorer treatment responses, which was consistent with the results of baseline PET parameters in predicting the post-treatment responses of both HL and non-HL patients [27–29]. Furthermore, our subgroup analysis highlights the robust generalizability of these metabolic parameters across varying disease stages. In advanced-stage adult HL patients, who typically face a higher risk of relapse and greater disease heterogeneity, interim MTV and Deauville score retained excellent diagnostic accuracy for predicting both PFS and OS. This finding is of considerable clinical relevance, as precise early risk stratification in advanced-stage disease is essential for guiding mid-treatment therapeutic adaptations, such as escalating to more intensive regimens for refractory cases or de-escalating to minimize toxicity in favorable responders.

Several limitations should be noted, Most importantly, the extremely low number of death events (n = 8) in our cohort severely limited the statistical power of the OS analysis. According to standard statistical principles, the event-per-variable ratio for our OS Cox regression was insufficient. Consequently, our claims regarding OS are preliminary and should be interpreted with caution as exploratory trends. Therefore, the primary clinical value and core conclusions of this study are firmly grounded in the PFS results, where the event rate was robust enough to support definitive prognostic claims. In addition, MTV and TLG depend on segmentation methodology, emphasizing the importance of standardized reporting. Overall, our findings support the integration of interim quantitative PET/CT tumor burden assessment-especially MTV with Deauville score to improve outcome prediction and risk stratification in adult HL.

Conclusion

In conclusion, while various baseline and interim 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters correlate with PFS in adult HL patients, baseline MTV, interim MTV, and the Deauville score were identified as robust, independent prognostic indicators. Integrating these quantitative volumetric metrics with standard visual assessment significantly enhances risk stratification, offering a more precise and individualized framework for outcome prediction.

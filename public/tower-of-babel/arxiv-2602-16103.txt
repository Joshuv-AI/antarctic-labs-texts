Topology of the Vakil–Zinger moduli space
Terry Dekun Song

Abstract
. We derive a set of generators for the rational homology of the desingularised genus one mapping space ℳ ~ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) constructed by Vakil–Zinger and qualitatively describe the relations among the generators. The results build on a detailed study of the stratifications of the moduli spaces coming from tropical geometry and the constraints coming from the weight filtration on the Borel–Moore homology groups of strata, extending the techniques from the previous study on ℳ ¯ g , n . \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}. Our results imply that the even homology of ℳ ~ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) is tautological and controlled by genus-zero and reduced genus-one Gromov–Witten theory. We verify the Hodge and Tate conjectures for ℳ ~ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) , \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d), completely describe its rational Picard group, and recover known results on the vanishing of odd cohomology. Our techniques also apply to the pure weight homology groups of genus one stable maps ℳ ¯ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) . \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
be the moduli space of degree
d
maps from
n
-marked smooth genus one curves to projective space. The stable maps compactification

\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

is stratified by degree-decorated dual graphs with smooth locally closed strata. However, the strata where the minimal genus one subcurve (
the core
) is contracted have excess dimensions, so
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
is reducible. The
main component

\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{main}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
is defined as the closure of
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).
It is singular, and smoothability of stable maps is subtle to characterise and work with.

In
[
VZ08
]
, Vakil and Zinger constructed a beautiful desingularsation

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{main}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).

The stack
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
is given a modular interpretation by Ranganathan–Santos-Parker–Wise
[
RSPW19
]
as a moduli space of maps from
centrally aligned
genus one curves: they constructed a weighted strata blow-up
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{cen}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
parametrising genus one stable curves together with contractions to elliptic singularities, such that the natural map
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
factors through.

This work describes the rational homology of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
by a set of generators and their relations (Theorems
A
,
B
) by the stratification spectral sequence, topology of linear systems on low genus curves, and how their basepoints control the relations among the homology classes.

1.1.
Central alignment stratification

The generators in the presentation of
H_{\star}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
are extracted from the stratification of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
by centrally aligned
(1,n,d)
-graphs: this was set out in
[
RSPW19
]
and reviewed in
[
KS24a
]
.

When a genus one subgraph has positive degree, a centrally aligned graph agrees with the usual stable map dual graph, which we term as
(1,n,d)
-graphs, and so are the corresponding strata in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
. When the core has total degree zero, the
(1,n,d)
-graph–typically denoted as
\mathbf{G}
–is decorated with the data of a central alignment
\rho
(Definition
2.4
) that blocks a subset of vertices into levels.

In this work, we coarsen the stratification to equivalence classes of centrally aligned
(1,n,d)
-graphs under contractions and sproutings of rational tails - genus zero subgraphs that do not receive a central alignment (Definition
2.9
). Analogous to the ordinary stable maps
[
BM96
, §3]
, locally closed strata
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
are the fibre products of (Lemma
2.15
):

(1)

a dual graph stratum in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{cen}},
which is a torus bundle over a dual graph stratum in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n},

(2)

a collection of pointed genus zero maps together with prescribed tangent vectors on the attaching points, such that the maps collapse the sum of the tangent vectors,

(3)

genus zero stable maps.

1.2.
Weight filtrations on strata

In the following, we use
Borel–Moore homology
as it receives a cycle class map from Chow homology and is functorial with respect to stratification. For smooth spaces, Borel–Moore homology groups are Poincaré dual to singular cohomology. The central alignment stratification of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
induces a spectral sequence in Borel–Moore homology

E^{1}_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\mathbf{G},\rho]\\
\dim\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]}=p\end{subarray}}H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{p+q}(\mathcal{M}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]})\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)).

The first page differentials are induced by boundary maps, generalising the familiar excision long exact sequence, and are compatible with mixed Hodge structures
[
Ara05
, Lemma 3.8]
. Because
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
is a smooth, proper Deligne–Mumford stack,
H_{p+q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
is pure of weight
-(p+q).
Truncating the differentials of the spectral sequence
E^{k}_{p,q}\to E^{k}_{p-k,q+k-1}
at weight
-(p+q),
we observe that only the pure weight quotient
\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{1}_{p,q}
survives to
E^{\infty}_{p,q},
and that there are successive surjections

\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{1}_{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{k}_{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{k+1}_{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots\twoheadrightarrow E^{\infty}_{p,q}

with

\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{k+1}_{p,q}=\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{k}_{p,q}/\left(\mathrm{im}:\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{k}_{p+1,q}\to\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{k}_{p,q}\right).

Remark 1.2
.

Dually, we may also truncate the stratification spectral sequence for the compactly supported cohomology

E_{1}^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\mathbf{G},\rho]\\
\dim\mathcal{M}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]}=p\end{subarray}}H_{c}^{p+q}(\mathcal{M}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))

and have successive injections

E_{\infty}^{p,q}\subset\cdots\subset\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{k+1}^{p,q}\subset\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{k}^{p,q}\subset\cdots\subset\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{1}^{p,q}

given by

\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{k+1}^{p,q}=\ker\left(\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{k}^{p,q}\to\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{k}^{p+k,q-k+1}\right)\subset\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{p+q}E_{k}^{p,q}.

The surjectivity (resp. injectivity) follows from the weight bounds on
H_{k}^{\mathrm{BM}}
and
H_{c}^{k}.

Thus, the pure weight Borel–Moore homology groups

\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-\star}H_{\star}^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathcal{M}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]})=\bigoplus_{k}\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-k}H_{k}^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathcal{M}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]})

generate
H_{\star}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)),
and the relations among the generators are subquotients of the ‘off-by-one’ Borel–Moore homology groups
\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-\star+1}H_{\star}^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathcal{M}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]}).
The fibre product descriptions of the strata
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{[\mathbf{G},\rho]}
lead to Künneth formulas for their Borel–More homology groups (Lemma
2.15
), so the pure and off-by-one weight graded pieces are determined by the pure weight classes on each fibre product factor, which we now discuss.

1.3.
Generators and relations

We approach the weight pieces of
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
via its partial compactification by linear systems. Let

\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{1,n}

be the relative degree-
d
(rigidified) Picard stack over
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}.
It has relative polarisation

\Theta\in A^{1}(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n})

and universal Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{P}_{d}
over
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{1,n}}\mathcal{C}_{1,n}.
Let
\pi:\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{1,n}}\mathcal{C}_{1,n}\to\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n}
be the projection map, we define

\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d):=\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n}}(R\pi_{*}\mathcal{P}_{d}^{\oplus r+1}).

It is a projective bundle over
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}_{1,n},
such that the fibre over
(E,L)
is

\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(E,L)^{\oplus r+1}).

It has hyperplane class

H_{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}\in A^{1}(\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)).

There is an open immersion

\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\subset\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

given by tuples of sections with no common basepoints.

The classes
\Theta,H_{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}}
produce Borel–Moore homology classes under the cycle class map. Together with flat pullback from
W_{-\star}H_{\star}^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathcal{M}_{1,n}),
they generate
W_{-\star}H_{\star}^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)),
and an analogous description holds for its off-by-one weight cohomology (Lemmas
3.26
,
3.26
). The same discussion applies to maps from nodal elliptic curves (Lemma
3.33
).

We turn to second fibre product factor, which are genus zero maps together with tangent vectors. Let
\mathsf{M}_{0,1}
and
\mathsf{M}_{0,2}
be the Artin stacks of smooth rational curves with one and two marked points, respectively. They are identified with classifying stacks of automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that preserves the set of marked points. In particular, there are isomorphisms

H^{\star}(\mathsf{M}_{0,i})\cong\mathbb{Q}[\psi]

for
i=1,2,
where

\psi\in H^{2}(\mathsf{M}_{0,i})

is the
\psi
-class of any marked point and is well-defined up to a sign. These
\psi
-classes pull back to classes supported on the genus zero vertices with factorisation property that are univalent or bivalent, and these are their only contribution to pure weight Borel–Moore homology (Lemma
3.19
). To see this, we first determine the cohomology of parametrised genus zero maps satisfying the factorisation property:

(1)

Given a tangent vector on the distinguished point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{r},
say
v\in T_{p}\mathbb{P}^{r}
, we define the space of pointed maps with derivative
v
and determine its cohomology in Section
3.1.1
. Analogous to the results in
[
FW16
]
concering the moduli space
\mathrm{Map}^{*}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{r})
of pointed, parametrized maps from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, they are largely independent of the degree of the map, in which case their cohomology is isomorphic to that of
\mathbb{A}^{r}\setminus\mathrm{pt}.

(2)

We describe the space of admissible tangent vectors: they are tuples of tangent vectors in
T_{p}\mathbb{P}^{r}
satisfying suitable linear dependencies.

Combining information on the base and fibre directions, Corollary
3.16
states that the pure weight cohomology of the space of
parametrised
maps with factorisation property is given only by
H^{0}\cong\mathbb{Q}.
The only source of pure weight classes from the moduli space is hence the
\psi
-classes from taking quotients by the automorphism groups.

1.4.
Applications

The even homology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
is spanned by boundary classes
[
Pet14
]
. Combining this with the description of generators given above, the even homology of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
is generated by tautological classes and boundary strata. This allows us to deduce

Corollary D

(Corollary
4.4
)
.

The Hodge and Tate conjectures hold for
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).

From another perspective, Theorem
A
implies that the even homology groups of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
are entirely controlled by intersection numbers of tautological cycles, namely the reduced genus one Gromov–Witten theory of
\mathbb{P}^{r}.

Via Poincaré duality, Theorem
A
also describes the Hodge structures in
H^{\star}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)).
Following
[
CLP24
]
, we denote

\mathsf{L}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}),\mathsf{S}_{k+1}:=W_{k}H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{1,k}).

The Eichler–Shimura isomorphism states that
\mathsf{S}_{k+1}
corresponds to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-cusp forms of weight
k+1.
They are possibly non-zero for odd
k\geq 11.
The Hodge structures present in
H^{\star}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
are products of
\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{S}_{k+1}
for
k\geq n+d.
In particular, the odd weight Hodge structures in
H^{\star}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
have weight at least
11.
Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
has pure weight cohomology, we recover the following result of Fontanari
[
Fon07
]
.

Corollary E

(Corollary
4.6
)
.

For odd
k<11
and all
n,

H^{k}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
vanishes.

The presence of Hodge structures
\mathsf{L}^{j}\prod_{k}\mathsf{S}_{k+1}^{\ell_{k}}
is tied to the graphical stratification of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
. This allows more precise descriptions of what each Hodge structure contributes.

1.5.
Related work

The strategy of understanding weight filtrations within a stratified space is far from being new. The ideas can be traced back to the earlier work of Arbarello–Cornalba
[
AC98
]
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and have been utilised to great effect by Petersen
[
Pet14
]
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
with its dual graph stratification. They also underly the recent breakthroughs on cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
by Bergström–Faber–Payne
[
BFP24
]
, Canning–Larson
[
CL24
]
, Canning–Larson–Payne
[
CLP24
,
CLP23
]
, and Canning–Larson–Payne–Willwacher
[
CLPW24a
,
CLPW24b
,
CLPW25
]
.

Unlike the moduli of stable curves, the strata in the mapping spaces are in general non-trivial fibre products. This has been investigated by Oprea in the setting of genus zero stable maps to flag varieties
[
Opr06b
]
.

The work combines the above perspectives and techniques with the recent study of combinatorics of genus one mapping spaces: this includes the plethystic structure involving stable maps
[
KS24b
]
and the explicit genus zero geometry of rational functions satisfying factorisation property
[
KS24a
]
that is inspired by previous work of Farb–Wolfson
[
FW16
]
on parametrised pointed genus zero maps.

Another important class of compactified mapping spaces is the moduli space of genus one stable quotients
[
MOP11
]

\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).
In particular, as
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{1,0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
is smooth and proper, we expect that the technique and calculations in the present work are applicable. They will complement previous work of Y. Cooper
[
Coo14
]
on its geometric and topological properties.

1.6.
Future directions

Ongoing work of the author
[
Son26
]
returns to the generators and relations of
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
using the dual graph stratification. The results obtained from this will complement the ones given by Mustata–Mustata
[
MM08a
,
MM07
,
MM08b
]
using wall-crossing techniques; comparing the two seems non-obvious and is worth pursuing.

Regarding the interior
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
, the Betti numbers as well as the full weight filtration remain unknown: this work concerns the pure and off-by-one pieces, and the top weight cohomology is known to vanish from
[
KS24a

5.
Relations

Relations among the generators correspond to images of the differentials
\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{r}_{p+r,q-r+1}\to\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-(p+q)}E^{r}_{p,q}.

or their subquotients, where

is either a core edge contraction or level merge; higher page differentials map between the subquotients of strata related by a composition of such morphisms. The off-by-one weight pieces

and their modular interpretation given in Lemmas

3.19

,

or

Künneth tensor products of the above two with pure weight classes supported on vertices, which are cup products of the above two types of relations with other cohomology classes.

⊗ \otimes -Frobenius functors and exact module categories
David Jaklitsch, Harshit Yadav

Abstract
. We call a tensor functor F : 𝒞 → 𝒟 F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D} between finite tensor categories ⊗ \otimes -Frobenius if its left and right adjoints are isomorphic as 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -bimodule functors. We give several characterizations of this notion—most notably, F F is ⊗ \otimes -Frobenius if and only if the centralizer Z ⁡ ( 𝒟 F F ) Z({}_{F}\!{\mathcal{D}}_{\!F}) is unimodular. We use them to analyze how actions on module categories behave under pullback along F F . For perfect functors, we show that twisting a 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -module category ℳ \mathcal{M} along F F preserves exactness, and that pivotality, unimodularity, and sphericality are preserved whenever F F is ⊗ \otimes -Frobenius (or, more generally, Frobenius with respect to ℳ \mathcal{M} ). Applications include: (i) explicit criteria for ⊗ \otimes -Frobenius functors arising from bialgebra maps f : H ′ → H f\!:\!H^{\prime}\!\to\!H between finite-dimensional Hopf algebras; and (ii) criteria ensuring that objects of internal natural transformations are (symmetric) Frobenius algebras in Z ⁡ ( 𝒞 ) Z(\mathcal{C}) . Along the way we show that central tensor functors are Frobenius iff they are ⊗ \otimes -Frobenius and that any tensor functor between separable fusion categories is ⊗ \otimes -Frobenius, answering questions of Flake-Laugwitz-Posur from [ 18 ] .



1.
Introduction

Tensor categories have emerged as fundamental structures in the mathematical study of quantum field theory. They also offer a unified framework for a wide array of mathematical domains, including subfactor theory of von Neumann algebras, vertex operator algebras and Hopf algebras. In this article, we concentrate on the class of finite tensor categories and our results are particularly relevant beyond the semisimple setting.

A typical approach to understanding an algebraic structure is via its representations. Module categories play this central role in the theory of tensor categories. About two decades ago, the work of Etingof and Ostrik

[
16
]
showed that studying all module categories over a finite tensor category is generally
intractable, suggesting a focus on the specific class of
exact module categories
.

Physically, if
\mathcal{C}
is a semisimple tensor category associated with a
2
-dimensional rational conformal field theory (CFT), semisimple (exact)
\mathcal{C}
-module categories supply the data necessary for obtaining the full local conformal field theory
[
23
]
. Extending beyond rational CFTs to the finite non-semisimple setting, Fuchs and Schweigert
[
25
]
proposed the class of
pivotal
exact
\mathcal{C}
-module categories (explored in
[
35
,
40
]
) as the required data for constructing the full CFT. Since then, additional classes of module categories, such as
unimodular

[
45
]
and
spherical

[
22
]
, have been introduced, which hold significance for the construction of topological quantum field theories (TQFTs)
[
21
]
.

The primary objective of this article is to construct new examples of unimodular, pivotal, and spherical
\mathcal{C}
-module categories. To achieve this, we investigate the process of twisting module actions by tensor functors. Specifically, given a tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
, any
\mathcal{D}
-module category
\mathcal{M}
can be regarded as a
\mathcal{C}
-module category by pulling back the
\mathcal{D}
-action along
F
. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions to ensure that
F
preserves exactness, unimodularity, pivotality, and sphericality of module categories under twisting. We introduce a new class of functors, called
\otimes
-Frobenius functors
, which capture these preservation conditions.

\otimes
-Frobenius functors are tensor functors
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\to\!\mathcal{D}
whose left and right adjoints are isomorphic in the
2
-category
\mathbf{Bimod}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
-bimodules. This is a case of ambidextrous adjunction studied in
[
29
,
44
]
and more recently in
[
20
]
. We investigate
\otimes
-Frobenius functors, give several characterizations, and present examples from Hopf algebras. We further apply our results to show that, under suitable conditions, the objects called
internal natural transformations
(introduced in
[
26
]
) form a (symmetric) Frobenius algebra in the Drinfeld center of a tensor category. These play an important role in constructing modular tensor categories and describing boundary conditions in
2
D-CFT
[
23
]
.

Main Results

We refer the reader to §

In the presence of a tensor (that is, a

is Frobenius monoidal.

2.1.
Monoidal categories and functors

Owing to Mac Lane’s coherence theorem, we can assume that all monoidal categories are strict. A generic monoidal category is denoted as a triple
(\mathcal{C},\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}},\mathds{1}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}})
where
\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}
stands for the tensor product and
\mathds{1}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}
for the unit object. The
monoidal opposite

\overline{\mathcal{C}\,}
of
\mathcal{C}
is the monoidal category having the same underlying category as
\mathcal{C}
, but with reversed tensor product, i.e.
c\,{\otimes_{\overline{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}}}\,d\coloneqq d\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}c
. Moreover, the opposite category
\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{op}}
is also endowed with a monoidal structure given by the same tensor product and unit as
\mathcal{C}
.

A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is called
rigid
if every object
c\in\mathcal{C}
admits left and right duals.
We use the following conventions for dualities:
the right dual
c\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt
of an object
c\in\mathcal{C}
comes equipped with evaluation and coevaluation morphisms

{\rm ev}_{c}:\quad c\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\otimes\,c\longrightarrow\mathds{1}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad{\rm coev}_{c}:\quad\mathds{1}\longrightarrow c\otimes c\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\,,

and the left dual
\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355ptc
of
c\in\mathcal{C}
comes with evaluation and coevaluation morphisms

\widetilde{{\rm ev}_{c}}:\quad c\,\otimes\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355ptc\longrightarrow\mathds{1}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\widetilde{{\rm coev}_{c}}:\quad\mathds{1}\longrightarrow{}\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355ptc\otimes c\,.

This matches the conventions in
[
24
]
.
A rigid monoidal category is called
pivotal
if it is equipped with a

, that is, a monoidal natural isomorphism
\mathfrak{p}\colon{\mathrm{id}}_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}\xRightarrow{\;\sim\;}(-){\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt}

An
algebra
in
\mathcal{C}
is a triple
(A,m:A\otimes A\to A,u:\mathds{1}\to A)
where

\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{op}}

A

(A,m,u,\Delta,\varepsilon)
where

forms an algebra,

(m\otimes\text{id}_{A})\circ(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\Delta)=\Delta\circ m=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes m)\circ(\Delta\otimes\text{id}_{A})\,.

((F^{\sf la})^{2},(F^{\sf la})^{0})
. We use the unit, counit of the adjunction
F^{\sf la}\dashv F
and
F_{2}
to define natural transformations called
left and right Hopf operators
, respectively
[
2
]
:

(2.3)

H^{l}_{d,c}:\quad F^{\sf la}(d\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}F(c))\rightarrow F^{\sf la}(d)\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}c\qquad{\rm and}\qquad H^{r}_{d,c}:\quad F^{\sf la}(F(c)\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}d)\rightarrow c\otimes_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}F^{\sf la}(d)\,.

If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are rigid, the maps
h^{l},h^{r}
and
H^{l},H^{r}
are all isomorphisms. We note that in references such as
[
20
,
18
,
19
]
, the co/Hopf operators are also called projection formula morphisms.

2.2.
Finite multitensor categories and module categories

A
finite
\Bbbk
-linear category
is an abelian category that is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional representations of a finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-algebra. All categories considered in this article will be finite
\Bbbk
-linear and all functors are assumed to be additive and
\Bbbk
-linear, unless otherwise stated.
Every
\Bbbk
-linear functor between finite
\Bbbk
-linear categories that is left (right) exact admits a left (right) adjoint
[
10
, Cor. 1.9]
.
The
image
of a
\Bbbk
-linear functor
F\colon\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
, denoted by
\mathrm{im}(F)
, is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{Y}
consisting of subquotients of objects in the essential image of
F
.
A
\Bbbk
-linear functor
F\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is called
surjective
iff its image is equivalent to
\mathcal{Y}
.

A
finite multitensor category
is a finite abelian
\Bbbk
-linear, rigid monoidal category with
\Bbbk
-bilinear tensor product. If, in addition,
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1})\cong\Bbbk
holds, we call
\mathcal{C}
a
finite tensor category
.

A
tensor functor
is a
\Bbbk
-linear, exact, and strong monoidal functor. It is established that tensor functors between finite tensor categories are faithful
[
8
, Corollaire 2.10(ii)]
. The image
\mathrm{im}(F)
of a tensor functor
F
is a tensor subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
.
A fully faithful tensor functor is called
injective
.

A
left module category
over a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is a finite
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{M}
equipped with a module action functor
{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
that is
\Bbbk
-bilinear and exact in each variable, and natural isomorphisms
x{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}(y{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}m)\cong(x\otimes y){\,{{\triangleright}}\,}m
(for
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
,
m\in\mathcal{M}
) that satisfy certain coherence conditions. Right module categories and bimodule categories are similarly defined
[
13
, Def. 7.1.7]
.
A module category
{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}
is called
exact
iff for any
m\in\mathcal{M}
and any projective object
p\in\mathcal{C}
,
p{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}m
is projective in
\mathcal{M}
. A module category is called
indecomposable
iff it is not equivalent to a direct sum of two non-trivial module categories. A
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule category is
exact
iff it is exact as left
\mathcal{C}
-module category and also exact as a right
\mathcal{D}
-module category.
We call an algebra
A\in\mathcal{C}

exact
(resp.
indecomposable
) if the left
\mathcal{C}
-module category
\!\mathrm{mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})
of right
A
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
is exact (resp. indecomposable).

A
\mathcal{C}
-module functor
between two
\mathcal{C}
-module categories
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
consists of a functor
H\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
endowed with a
module structure
, i.e. a natural isomorphism

\varphi_{c,m}:\quad H(c\,{{\triangleright}}\,m)\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,c\,{{\triangleright}}\,H(m)

for
c\,{\in}\,\mathcal{C}
and
m\,{\in}\,\mathcal{M}
satisfying the appropriate pentagon axioms.
Module functors can be composed: given
\mathcal{C}
-module categories
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
-module functors
H_{1}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
and
H_{2}\colon\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{L}
the composition functor
H_{2}\circ H_{1}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{L}
inherits a
\mathcal{C}
-module functor structure via

(2.4)

H_{2}\circ H_{1}(c{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}m)\xrightarrow{H_{2}(\varphi^{1}_{c,m})}H_{2}(c{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}H_{1}(m))\xrightarrow{\varphi^{2}_{c,H_{1}(m)}}c{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}H_{2}(H_{1}(m))

where the isomorphism
\varphi^{i}
is the module structure of
H_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

A
module natural transformation
between module functors is a natural transformation between the underlying functors commuting with the respective module structures. Given
\mathcal{C}
-module categories
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
,
{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
denotes the category with
\mathcal{C}
-module functors between
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
as objects and module natural transformations as morphisms.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite tensor category and
\mathcal{M}
an indecomposable exact left
\mathcal{C}
-module category. The
dual tensor category
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\mathcal{M}
is the finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}:={\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{C}
-module endofunctors with tensor product given by composition of module functors
H\otimes H^{\prime}=H\circ H^{\prime}
and monoidal unit
{\mathrm{id}}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}
. In the case that
\mathcal{M}
is a decomposable exact left
\mathcal{C}
-module category, then
\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}
is a multitensor category. Furthermore, given exact
\mathcal{C}
-module categories
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
, the category of module functors
{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
is endowed with the structure of a
(\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}^{*},\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*})
-bimodule category, via composition of module functors.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}
be a right module category and
{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{N}
a left module category. A
balanced functor
is a bilinear functor
F\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
into a
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{Y}
equipped with a natural isomorphism

F(m\,{{\triangleleft}}\,d,n)\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,F(m,d\,{{\triangleright}}\,n)

for
d\in\mathcal{D}
,
m\in\mathcal{M}
and
n\in\mathcal{N}
, obeying a pentagon coherence condition.

The
relative Deligne product
of
\mathcal{M}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}
and
{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{N}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{M}\boxtimes_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{N}
equipped with a right exact

\mathcal{D}
-balanced functor
\boxtimes_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\boxtimes_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{N}
such that for every
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{Y}
the functor

{\sf Rex}(\mathcal{M}\boxtimes_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{N},\mathcal{Y})\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,{\rm Bal}^{\sf re}(\mathcal{M}{\times}\mathcal{N},\mathcal{Y})\,,\qquad F\longmapsto F\,{\circ}\;\boxtimes_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}

is an equivalence of categories, where
{\sf Rex}
denotes the category of right exact functors and
{\rm Bal}^{\sf re}
the category of right exact balanced functors.

An exact module category
{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{M}
becomes an exact
(\mathcal{D},\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}}\,)
-bimodule category with action of the dual tensor category
\mathcal{D}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}
given by module functor evaluation
[
13
, Lemma 7.12.7]
. The category of
\mathcal{D}
-module functors

\overline{\mathcal{M}}\coloneqq{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{D})
is naturally endowed with the structure of an exact
(\,\overline{\mathcal{D}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule category via

F\,{{\triangleright}}\,H\,{{\triangleleft}}\,d\coloneqq H\circ F(-)\otimes d

for
F\in\mathcal{D}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}
,
H\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
and
d\in\mathcal{D}
. There are bimodule equivalences

(2.5)

\mathcal{M}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{D}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{*}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,\mathcal{D}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\overline{\mathcal{M}}\boxtimes_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{M}\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,\mathcal{D}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{*}

of
\mathcal{D}
-bimodule categories and of
\mathcal{D}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{*}
-bimodule categories, respectively
[
9
, Prop. 2.4.10]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathcal{C}
-module category. For an object
m\in\mathcal{M}
the action functor
-\,{{\triangleright}}\,m\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}
is an exact functor and therefore it admits a right adjoint
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}(m,-)
, i.e. there are natural isomorphisms

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(c\,{{\triangleright}}\,m,n)\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c,\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}(m,n))

for
c\in\mathcal{C}
and
m,n\in\mathcal{M}
. This extends to a functor left exact in both entries

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}(-,-):\quad\mathcal{M}^{\rm opp}\times\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}

called the
internal Hom functor
of
{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}
. A module category is exact iff its internal Hom is an exact functor
[
13
, Cor. 7.9.6 & Prop. 7.9.7]
.

2.3.
Nakayama functors and relative Serre functors

Any finite
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{X}
becomes a left
\mathsf{Vec}
-module category via the action

\blacktriangleright

defined by:

\mathrm{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(x,y))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(V\raisebox{0.6458pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\blacktriangleright$}}x,y),\quad(V\in\mathsf{Vec},\;x,y\in\mathcal{X})\,.

The left and right exact Nakayama functors of
\mathcal{X}
are defined as the image of the identity functor under the Eilenberg-Watts correspondence
[
24
, Def. 3.14]
, explicitly given by the (co)ends

\mathbb{N}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{X}}}^{l}(x)\coloneqq\int_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(y,x)\raisebox{0.6458pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\blacktriangleright$}}y,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{N}^{r}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{X}}}(x)\coloneqq\int^{y\in\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(x,y)^{*}\;\raisebox{0.6458pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\blacktriangleright$}}y\,.

The right exact Nakayama functor is equipped with a family of natural isomorphisms

(2.6)

\mathfrak{n}_{F}:\quad\mathbb{N}^{r}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{Y}}\circ F\xRightarrow{\;\sim\penalty\ }F^{{\sf rra}}\circ\mathbb{N}^{r}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{X}}

for every right exact functor
F\colon\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
admitting a double-right adjoint
F^{{\sf rra}}
that is exact. There are similar natural isomorphisms involving the left exact Nakayama functor.

Given a finite multitensor category
\mathcal{C}
, the Nakayama functors of the
\Bbbk
-linear category underlying
\mathcal{C}
determine
distinguished invertible objects

[
14
]

(2.7)

D_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}\coloneqq\mathbb{N}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}^{l}(\mathds{1})\quad\text{and}\quad D_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}^{-1}\coloneqq\mathbb{N}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}^{r}(\mathds{1}).

These come with a monoidal natural isomorphism

(2.8)

\mathcal{R}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}:\quad D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\otimes-\xRightarrow{\;\sim\penalty\ }(-){\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt}\otimes D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}

called the
Radford isomorphism
. A finite multitensor category
\mathcal{C}
is
unimodular
iff
D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}
is isomorphic to the monoidal unit
\mathds{1}
.

Given a
\mathcal{C}
-module category
\mathcal{M}
, the natural isomorphisms (
2.6
) endow the Nakayama functor of
\mathcal{M}
with the structure of a twisted module functor of the form

(2.9)

\mathfrak{n}_{c,m}:\quad\mathbb{N}^{r}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}(c\,{{\triangleright}}\,m)\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,{}{\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\!}c\,{{\triangleright}}\,\mathbb{N}^{r}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}(m).

Similarly, the left exact Nakayama functor becomes a twisted module functor with the appropriate appearance of double-duals.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite tensor category and
\mathcal{M}
a left
\mathcal{C}
-module category. A (right)
relative Serre functor
of
\mathcal{M}

[
24
, Def. 4.22]
is an endofunctor
\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
equipped with a natural isomorphism

\phi_{m,n}:\quad\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}(m,n){\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt}\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\,{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}(n,\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}(m)),\qquad(m,n\in\mathcal{M})\,.

A relative Serre functor
(\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}},\phi)
exists iff
\mathcal{M}
is exact
[
24
]
. In this case, it is unique up to a unique isomorphism
[
40
, Lem. 3.5]
and it is an equivalence of categories. The (left) relative Serre functor
[
24
, Def. 4.22]
, denoted by
\overline{\mathbb{S}}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\colon\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
, serves as quasi-inverse of
\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}
.
Relative Serre functors come with a twisted module functor structure, i.e. there are natural isomorphisms

(2.10)

\mathfrak{s}_{c,m}:\quad\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}(c\,{{\triangleright}}\,m)\,{\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \sim\penalty\ }}\,c{{\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt\hskip 0.42677pt{}^{\textnormal{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}\hskip-0.85355pt}}\,{{\triangleright}}\,\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}(m)

for
c\in\mathcal{C}
and
m\in\mathcal{M}
, obeying an appropriate pentagon axiom. Furthermore,
given a
\mathcal{C}
-module functor
H\colon\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}
there is a natural isomorphism

(2.11)

\mathfrak{s}_{H}:\quad\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\circ H\xRightarrow{\;\sim\penalty\ }H^{\sf rra}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}

of twisted module functors.

The relative Serre functors of an exact
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule category
\mathcal{M}
are related to its Nakayama functors by the isomorphisms

(2.12)

D_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}\,{{\triangleright}}\,\mathbb{N}^{r}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}\cong\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}^{-1}\,{{\triangleright}}\,\mathbb{N}^{l}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}\cong\overline{\mathbb{S}}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}

of twisted bimodule functors
[
24
, Thm. 4.26]
. Similarly, there exist isomorphisms of
twisted bimodule functors for the relative Serre functors of
{}\mathcal{M}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}
. These isomorphisms lead to an extension of Radford’s theorem to module categories:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
22
, Cor. 4.16]

Let
{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}
be an exact module category. There exists a natural isomorphism

(2.13)

\mathcal{R}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}:\quad D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\,{{\triangleright}}\,{-}\xRightarrow{\penalty\ \cong\penalty\ \,}\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\,{{\triangleleft}}\,D_{\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}}=D_{\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}}\circ\;\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}

of twisted bimodule functors.

3.
\otimes
-Frobenius functors

A
\Bbbk
-linear functor with isomorphic adjoints is called a Frobenius functor. In the case of a tensor functor, its adjoint functors are endowed with additional structure, namely the structure of bimodule functors. In this section, we introduce the notion of
\otimes
-Frobenius tensor functors, which account for the additional data. We provide a list of characterizations and show some relations to Frobenius monoidal functors.

3.1.
Perfect tensor functors and relative modular objects

The relative modular function of a Hopf algebra extension studied in
[
17
]
finds a categorical analogue in the notion of the relative modular object of a tensor functor defined in
[
37
]
. Under certain conditions, this object measures the failure to which a tensor functor preserves the distinguished invertible object (
2.7
) and the Radford isomorphism of a finite tensor category. We first need the notion of a perfect tensor functor:

Definition 3.1
.

[
4
, §2.1]

A tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
between multitensor categories is called
perfect
iff it admits a right adjoint that is exact.

Being perfect is a property of a tensor functor. In the finite case, this notion has several characterizations listed in the lemma that follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
37
, Lemma 4.3]

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be finite multitensor categories and
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
a tensor functor.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

F
admits a double-right adjoint.

(ii)

F
admits a double-left adjoint.

(iii)

F
preserves projective objects, i.e.
F(p)\in\mathcal{D}
is projective for every projective
p\in\mathcal{C}
.

(iv)

There exists an object
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\in\mathcal{D}

such that
F^{\sf la}\cong F^{\sf ra}(-\otimes\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}})\cong F^{\sf ra}(\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\otimes-)
as
\Bbbk
-linear functors.

(v)

There exists
c\in\mathcal{C}
such that
F(c)\in\mathcal{D}
is projective.

(vi)

F
is perfect.

Proof.

The equivalence (i)
\iff
(ii)
\iff
(iii)
\iff
(iv) is proven in
[
37
, Lemma 4.3]
where it is also shown that the object
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}
can be realized as
F^{\sf rra}(\mathds{1})
(note that
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}
of this paper is
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}^{*}
of
[
37
]
). As
\mathcal{C}
is finite and therefore admits projective objects, (iii)
\implies
(v) is immediate. Using the same argument as in
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
, (v)
\implies
(iii) follows. Finally, since
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are finite, (vi) is equivalent to (i).
∎

A first consequence of Lemma
3.2
is that surjective tensor functors are perfect, since they preserve projective objects
[
16
, Thm. 2.5]
. The converse is not true: for instance, consider any tensor functor
F
between semisimple tensor categories that is not surjective (like
\mathsf{Vec}_{H}\subset\mathsf{Vec}_{G}
for a proper subgroup
H\subset G
), then
F
preserves projectives, but is not surjective. In fact, any tensor functor from a non-semisimple tensor category to a semisimple tensor category is perfect. Also notice that not every tensor functor is perfect: for a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
that is not semisimple, the tensor functor
\mathsf{Vec}\to\mathcal{C}
given by
\Bbbk\mapsto\mathds{1}
does not preserve projective objects.

Definition 3.3
.

[
37
, Def. 4.4]

The object
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\in\mathcal{D}
from Lemma
3.2
(iv) is called
the relative modular object of
F
.

Proposition 3.4
.

[
37
]

Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a perfect tensor functor. The relative modular object of
F
obeys the following properties:

(i)

\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}
is an invertible object in
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

There is a natural isomorphism
F^{\sf rra}\cong F(-)\,{\otimes}\,\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}
of
\Bbbk
-linear functors.

(iii)

There is a natural isomorphism
F^{\sf lla}\cong F(-)\,{\otimes}\,\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}^{-1}
of
\Bbbk
-linear functors.

(iv)

There is an isomorphism
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\cong F^{\sf rra}(\mathds{1}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}})
.

(v)

There is an isomorphism
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\cong F(D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}})\otimes D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Part (i) is proven in
[
37
, Prop. 4.7(2)]
.
By taking right adjoints to the isomorphism
F^{\sf la}\cong F^{\sf ra}(-\otimes\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}})
from Lemma
3.2
(iv), we obtain that

F\cong(F^{\sf la})^{\sf ra}\cong\left(F^{\sf ra}\circ(-\otimes\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}})\right)^{\sf ra}\cong(-\otimes\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}})^{\sf ra}\circ F^{\sf rra}\cong F^{\sf rra}(-)\otimes\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}^{-1}.

Tensoring by
\chi_{{\scriptscriptstyle F}}
on both sides yields (ii).
Part (iii) is proved in a similar manner. Lastly, since
F(\mathds{1}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}})\cong\mathds{1}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}
, part (iv) follows by (ii).
According to
[
24
, Rem. 4.17]

F^{\sf lla}(D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}})\cong D_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{D}}
. The isomorphism in (v) is then obtained by using (iii).
∎

Definition 3.5
.

[
5
]

A (
\Bbbk
-linear) functor
F\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is called
Frobenius
if it admits a two-sided adjoint, i.e. it admits both a left adjoint and a right adjoint and
F^{{\sf la}}\cong F^{{\sf ra}}
.

Given an adjoint pair
F\dashv G
, note that
F
is Frobenius if and only if
G
is Frobenius. Another immediate consequence of Definition
3.5
is that a tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
that is Frobenius must be perfect as well:
F
admits a double-right adjoint, namely
F^{{\sf rra}}\cong F
.
However, the converse is not true as shown by the following example.

Example 3.6
.

For a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
, the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is surjective and therefore perfect. However,
U
is Frobenius if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is unimodular
[
36
, Thm. 4.10]
.

The following result relates the property of
F
being Frobenius and
F^{\sf ra}
being Frobenius monoidal.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
be a tensor functor between tensor categories admitting a right adjoint
F^{\sf ra}\colon\mathcal{D}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}
.

(i)

If the lax monoidal functor
F^{{\sf ra}}

3.3.
The properties of
\otimes
-Frobenius functors

A tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
can be viewed as a
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{D}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}}
. Moreover, it admits left and right adjoints that are also
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule functors
[
10
, Cor. 2.13]
. This motivates the following stronger version of Definition
3.5
that accounts for the additional structure.

Definition 3.15
.

A tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
between finite multitensor categories is called
\otimes
-Frobenius iff there exists a
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule natural isomorphism
F^{\sf la}\cong F^{\sf ra}\colon{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{D}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}
.

Example 3.16
.

Consider a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
and an action
T\colon G\to\Aut_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
of a finite group
G
on
\mathcal{C}
. The finite tensor category of fixed points
\mathcal{C}^{G}
or
equivariantization

[
13
, Def. 2.7.2]
comes with a forgetful functor

(3.5)

\mathtt{forg}:\quad\mathcal{C}^{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C},\quad(X,u)\longmapsto X\,.

As pointed out in
[
11
, Lemma 4.6]
the induction functor

(3.6)

\mathtt{Ind}:\quad\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{G},\quad Y\longmapsto\oplus_{g\in G}\,T_{g}(Y)

serves as left and right adjoint to
\mathtt{forg}
. Moreover, one can check that the
\mathcal{C}^{G}
-bimodule structures on
\mathtt{Ind}
induced by the adjunctions
\mathtt{forg}\dashv\mathtt{Ind}
and
\mathtt{Ind}\dashv\mathtt{forg}
agree and are given by

\displaystyle(X,u){\,{{\triangleright}}\,}\mathtt{Ind}(Y)\triangleleft(Z,v)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\,X\otimes T_{g}(Y)\otimes Z

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\oplus_{g\in G}u_{g}\otimes{\mathrm{id}}\otimes v_{g}}\bigoplus_{g\in G}\,T_{g}(X)\otimes T_{g}(Y)\otimes T_{g}(Z)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\,T_{g}(X\otimes Y\otimes Z)=\mathtt{Ind}(X\otimes Y\otimes Z)\,.

Hence,
\mathtt{forg}
is a
\otimes
-Frobenius functor.

The notion of a
\otimes
-Frobenius tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}
is an instance of an ambi-adjunction (see
[
20
, Def. 2.7]
for a definition) internal to the
2
-category
\mathrm{\textbf{Bimod}}(\mathcal{C})
.
Clearly, a bimodule natural isomorphism
F^{\sf la}\cong F^{\sf ra}
is in particular a natural isomorphism. Therefore, any
\otimes
-Frobenius functor is Frobenius and thus perfect. The converse does not hold, an example of a (surjective) Frobenius functor that is not
\otimes
-Frobenius can be found in Example
5.8
. It is worth pointing out that
\otimes
-Frobenius is a property of a tensor functor
F
, in the sense that it asks for the existence and not for a specific choice of bimodule isomorphism between the left and right adjoint functors of
F
.

Remark 3.17
.

Given
\otimes
-Frobenius functors

and

, the composition

is also
\otimes
-Frobenius ​​. Indeed, if
F\cong F^{\sf rra}
and
G\cong G^{\sf rra}
as bimodule functors, then
F\circ G\cong F^{\sf rra}\circ G^{\sf rra}\cong(F\circ G)^{\sf rra}
as bimodule functors. The second isomorphism is obtained by the uniqueness of adjoints in the
2
-category
\mathrm{\textbf{Bimod}}(\mathcal{C})
.

Another consequence of being
\otimes
-Frobenius (and thus Frobenius) is the following: as

is strong monoidal,

is endowed with a lax monoidal structure and

with an oplax monoidal structure. Using the isomorphism

,

also inherits an oplax structure. Next, directly following the results in
[

, we obtain that these two structures form a Frobenius monoidal functor.

4.1.
Exactness of module categories

Tensor categories are regarded as the categorification of rings and exact module categories may be seen as categorical analogues of projective modules. A classical result concerning the preservation of projectivity under the pullback of ring actions via homomorphisms states: let
f:A\rightarrow B
be a ring homomorphism. If
B
is projective as an
A
-module then, a
B
-module
M
is projective if and only if
M
is projective as an
A
-module. This result finds an analog in the categorical setting of twisting actions along tensor functors
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
be a perfect tensor functor. A
\mathcal{D}
-module category
\mathcal{M}
is exact if and only if the
\mathcal{C}
-module category
{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{M}}
is exact.

Proof.

Since,
F
is perfect,
F(p)\in\mathcal{D}
is projective for any projective
p\in\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is exact as
\mathcal{D}
-module, then for every
m\in\mathcal{M}
, we have that
p\,{{{\triangleright}}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle F}}\,m=F(p){\,{{\triangleright}}\,}m
is projective in
\mathcal{M}
, and thus
{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{M}}
is an exact
\mathcal{C}
-module. For the converse, pick a non-zero projective object
p\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
F
is faithful, it reflects zero objects and thus
F(p)
is non-zero
[
36
, Lemma 5.1]
. Assuming that
{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{M}}
is an exact
\mathcal{C}
-module,
F(p){\,{{\triangleright}}\,}m
is projective for all
m\in\mathcal{M}
. The claim follows by
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
.
∎

This means that twisting by perfect functors preserves exactness of module categories.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
be a tensor functor between finite tensor categories. If
F
is perfect, the lax monoidal functor
F^{\sf ra}\colon\mathcal{D}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{C}
preserves exactness of algebras.

Proof.

Given an exact algebra
A\in\mathcal{D}
, consider the module category
\mathcal{M}\!=\!\!\mathrm{mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D})
. Since,
{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{M}}\simeq{\!\mathrm{mod}}_{F^{\sf ra}(A)}(\mathcal{C})
, Proposition
4.4
implies that
F^{\sf ra}(A)
is exact if
F
is perfect.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The corestriction of a tensor functor

to its image is surjective and therefore perfect. Thus, by Corollary

,
F^{{\sf ra}}(A)
is exact in
\mathcal{C}
for any exact algebra

. For instance, since

is an exact algebra, we have that

is an exact algebra in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{D}
be a tensor functor. The
\mathcal{C}
-module category
{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{D}}
is exact if and only if the tensor functor

is perfect.

Proof.

If
F
is perfect, it preserves projective objects according to Lemma

, and then
{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\mathcal{D}}
is exact. On the other hand, exactness of

implies that

is perfect.

5.2.
\otimes
-Frobenius functors induced by bialgebra maps

Let
\mathsf{f}\colon H^{\prime}\rightarrow H
be a bialgebra map. Define the
relative modular function

\chi_{\mathsf{f}}:H^{\prime}\rightarrow\Bbbk,\qquad\chi_{\mathsf{f}}(h^{\prime})=\langle\alpha_{H},S_{H}(\mathsf{f}(h^{\prime}_{1}))\rangle\langle\alpha_{H^{\prime}},h^{\prime}_{2}\rangle.

The relative modular object of the perfect tensor functor

is determined by the relative modular function of
\mathsf{f}
as we show next.

Proposition 5.4
.

The relative modular object
\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle F_{\mathsf{f}}}
of
F_{\mathsf{f}}
is the
H^{\prime}
-module
\Bbbk_{\chi_{\mathsf{f}}}
. The
F_{\mathsf{f}}
-half-braiding of
\chi_{\mathsf{f}}
is given for
X\in\textnormal{{Rep}}(H)
by the isomorphism

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{X}:\quad\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle F_{\mathsf{f}}}\otimes F_{\mathsf{f}}(X)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow F_{\mathsf{f}}(X)\otimes\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle F_{\mathsf{f}}},

\displaystyle 1\otimes_{\Bbbk}x

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathtt{g}_{H}\,\mathsf{f}(\overline{\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}}})\cdot x\otimes_{\Bbbk}1.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
(v), we know that

\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle F_{\mathsf{f}}}=F_{\mathsf{f}}(D_{\textnormal{{Rep}}(H)})\otimes D_{\textnormal{{Rep}}(H^{\prime})}^{-1}=F_{\mathsf{f}}(\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}})\otimes(\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H^{\prime}}})^{-1}=\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}.

Thus, the first claim follows. Now, consider
X\in\textnormal{{Rep}}(H)
and denote
X_{\mathsf{f}}=F_{\mathsf{f}}(X)\in\textnormal{{Rep}}(H^{\prime})
. Then, the commutative diagram (
3.4
) in Proposition
3.14
gives a description of
\sigma_{X}
. Namely, it is given by the following compositions of Radford isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}\otimes X_{\mathsf{f}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{id}}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{X_{\mathsf{f}}}}\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}\mathsf{f}}\otimes X_{\mathsf{f}\overline{S}^{4}_{H^{\prime}}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}\mathsf{f}}\otimes X_{\overline{S}^{4}_{H}\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}=(\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}}\otimes X_{\overline{S}^{4}_{H}})_{\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\mathcal{R}_{X})_{\mathsf{f}}\otimes{\mathrm{id}}}(X\otimes\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}})_{\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}=X_{\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\overline{\alpha}_{H}\mathsf{f}}\otimes\Bbbk_{\alpha_{H^{\prime}}}

Using the description of

(

1\otimes_{\Bbbk}1\otimes_{\Bbbk}x\mapsto 1\otimes_{\Bbbk}(\overline{\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}}}\cdot_{\mathsf{f}}x)\otimes_{\Bbbk}1=1\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathsf{f}(\overline{\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}}})\cdot x\otimes_{\Bbbk}1\mapsto\mathtt{g}_{H}\mathsf{f}(\overline{\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}}})\cdot x\otimes_{\Bbbk}1\otimes_{\Bbbk}1.

This completes the proof.
∎

A consequence of the above proposition is the following characterization of
\otimes
-Frobenius functors, which generalizes
[
17
, Corollary 1.8]
to perfect bialgebra maps.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathsf{f}\colon H^{\prime}\rightarrow H
be a perfect bialgebra map.

(i)

F_{\mathsf{f}}
is Frobenius
\iff\chi_{\mathsf{f}}=\varepsilon_{H^{\prime}}\iff\alpha_{H}\circ\mathsf{f}=\alpha_{H^{\prime}}
.

(ii)

F_{\mathsf{f}}\,\text{is {$\otimes$-Frobenius }}\,\iff\,\chi_{\mathsf{f}}=\varepsilon_{H^{\prime}}\,\text{and}\,\,\mathsf{f}(\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}})=\mathtt{g}_{H}\,\iff\,\alpha_{H}\circ\mathsf{f}=\alpha_{H^{\prime}}\,\text{and}\,\,\mathsf{f}(\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}})=\mathtt{g}_{H}
.

Proof.

(i) As
\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle F_{\mathsf{f}}}\cong\Bbbk_{\chi_{\mathsf{f}}}
, by Proposition
3.9
(i), the first equivalence is clear.
For the second equivalence, note that we can rewrite the relative modular function as

\chi_{\mathsf{f}}=(\overline{\alpha}_{H}\circ\mathsf{f})\times\alpha_{H^{\prime}}\in H^{*}.

where
\times
denotes the product in
H^{*}
.
As
\varepsilon\in H^{*}
is its unit, we obtain that
\chi_{\mathsf{f}}=\varepsilon
if and only if
\overline{\alpha}_{H}\circ\mathsf{f}=(\alpha_{H^{\prime}})^{-1}=\overline{\alpha}_{H^{\prime}}
. As the antipode is bijective, this is equivalent to
\alpha_{H}\circ\mathsf{f}=\alpha_{H^{\prime}}
.

(ii) By the equivalence (i)
\iff
(iii) of Theorem
3.19
,
F_{\mathsf{f}}
is
\otimes
-Frobenius if and only if
\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle F_{\mathsf{f}}}\cong\mathds{1}
as an object of
\textnormal{{Rep}}(H)
and the half-braiding is the identity map. By Proposition
5.4
, the former is equivalent to

\chi_{\mathsf{f}}=\varepsilon_{H^{\prime}}
and the latter to
\mathsf{f}(\mathtt{g}_{H^{\prime}})=\mathtt{g}_{H}
.
This proves the first equivalence. The second equivalence follows because of part (i).
∎

(ii)

\alpha_{H}(h^{\prime})=\alpha_{H^{\prime}}(h^{\prime})

Example 5.7

6.
Applications to internal natural transformations

In
[
26
]
the authors studied an internalized version of the vector space of natural transformations between two functors. Given a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
, an appropriate internal Hom involving two module functors
H,H^{\prime}\colon{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow{}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{N}
gives an object inside the Drinfeld center of
\mathcal{C}
called the
object of internal natural transformations
from
H
to
H^{\prime}
. Under certain assumptions, this object is endowed with the structure of a symmetric Frobenius algebra internal to
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. In this section, the results from §
4
allow us to reprove and strengthen many statements about internal natural transformations.

6.1.
Exactness and unimodularity of the
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
-module category
{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite tensor category and
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
exact
\mathcal{C}
-module categories. Recall, that the category of module functors
{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
becomes an exact
(\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}^{*},\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*})
-bimodule category, with action given by composition of module functors (
2.4
). The internal Hom’s can be described using adjoint functors:

Lemma 6.1
.

For
H,H^{\prime}\in{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
, we have that the internal Hom’s are given by

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}^{*}}_{{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})}(H,H^{\prime})\cong H^{\prime}\circ H^{{\sf la}}\;\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}}}_{{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})}(H,H^{\prime})\cong H^{{\sf ra}}\circ H^{\prime}\,.

Proof.

For
F\in\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}^{*}
, observe that there is a natural isomorphism

\varphi:\quad\mathrm{Hom}_{{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})}(H\circ F,H^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})}(F,H^{{\sf ra}}\circ H^{\prime})\,.

The assignment
\varphi
and its inverse are defined via the formulas

\varphi(\alpha)=({\mathrm{id}}_{H^{{\sf ra}}}\circ\alpha)(\eta\circ{\mathrm{id}}_{F}):F\Rightarrow H^{{\sf ra}}\circ H^{\prime},\;\;\;\;\;\;\varphi^{-1}(\beta)=(\varepsilon\circ{\mathrm{id}}_{F})({\mathrm{id}}_{H}\circ\beta):H\circ F\Rightarrow H^{\prime}.

where
\eta\colon{\mathrm{id}}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{M}}}\Rightarrow H^{{\sf ra}}\circ H
and
\varepsilon\colon H\circ H^{{\sf ra}}\Rightarrow{\mathrm{id}}_{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}}

denote the unit and counit of the adjunction
H\dashv H^{{\sf ra}}
. Since
\alpha,\eta
are
\mathcal{C}
-module natural transformations, we have that
\varphi(\alpha)
is a
\mathcal{C}
-module functor.
The statement for the
\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}^{*}
-internal Hom follows analogously.
∎

Given an exact
\mathcal{C}
-module category
\mathcal{N}
we have a tensor functor

(6.1)

\displaystyle{\Psi_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}}:\quad\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}^{*}

\displaystyle(c,\nu)

\displaystyle\longmapsto c{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}-

where the half-braiding
\nu
endows the functor
c{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}-\colon\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}
with a
\mathcal{C}
-module structure. The tensor structure on the functor
{\Psi_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}}
is given by the module associativity constraints of
\mathcal{N}

c{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}(d{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}n)\cong(c\otimes d){\,{{\triangleright}}\,}n\;.

Remark 6.2
.

In
[

, the tensor functor (

is also studied there. However, in the literature

-induction typically assumes that

For an exact

-module category

but with tensor structure defined by half-braidings

Now, we can consider the twist of the

-module category

by

-action is given by

\begin{array}[]{rccc}{\,{{\triangleright}}\,}^{{\Psi_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}}}:&\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\times{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})&\rightarrow&{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\\

the composition of the

-module functors

and

.

{}_{{\Psi_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}}}\!{\sf Fun}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

We have for every

∎

Notice that the functor

.

-module categories. The

By

∎

Proposition 6.9
.

The tensor functor
{\Psi_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{N}}}
is
\otimes
-Frobenius iff
\mathcal{N}
is unimodular iff

From Remark

\mathcal{N}
is unimodular. Since

is
\otimes
-Frobenius iff

∎

.

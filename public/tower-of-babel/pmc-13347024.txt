Computational simulations and uniform stability of fractional order neural networks under perturbed system setup
Authors: Kalla Kumara Swamy, Sumati Kumari Panda, Amer Hassan Albargi, Jamshaid Ahmad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study addresses the existence and uniform stability of solutions for a class of Caputo-type fractional-order bidirectional associative memory (BAM) neural networks incorporating leakage delay in the view of perturbed system setup. A few numerical examples with delay and fractional parameters demonstrates the consistency of the theoretical results.Keywords: Uniform stability, Bidirectional associative memory, Schaefer’s fixed point theorem, Fractional order, Neural network, Perturbed system.Subject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The mathematical concept of fractional calculus, which is an extension of the mathematical idea of differentiation and integration to non-integer order, has a history of more than 300 years. Although it was formulated early in theory, it has been comparatively obscure over centuries, since its applicability was limited in practice, and was hard to compute. But during the last few decades, fractional-order systems have become increasingly popular with scholars in applied mathematics, physics and engineering due to their ability to model memory and hereditary behavior of dynamic processes-phenomena which are poorly represented in classical integer-order models (for more info, see,1–6).

Fractional derivatives, in contrast to traditional derivatives, are a nonlocal derivative, meaning it is integrated over earlier periods of time. This renders them very useful in modeling systems whose current state is not only dependent on the current input, but a weighted sum of the past states. It applies especially to biological systems, viscoelastic materials, control systems, as well as to neural networks. Out of the existing varieties of fractional derivatives, the most common is the so-called Caputo derivative because, unlike other types, Caputo derivative is compatible with the traditional initial conditions, which is why it can be applied more in physical and engineering contexts to analyze fractional processes7–10.

Neural networks, especially when modeled dynamically, benefit from fractional-order frameworks because they better capture synaptic memory, delayed responses, and long-term dependencies. As such, the study of fractional-order neural networks has become a rapidly evolving research area. Significant progress has been made in analyzing their dynamical behaviors, including stability, multistability, bifurcation, and chaos. As an example, reference 11 introduced a Hopfield neural network of fractional order and analyzed its stability properties using methods based on energy functions. In12, the complex dynamical features–including multistability and chaotic attractors–were studied for fractional Hopfield models. Meanwhile, uniform stability conditions for fractional-order delayed neural networks were explored in13, and finite-time stability criteria for fractional BAM neural networks with distributed delays were derived in14. Song and Cao15 employed fixed point theory to establish the existence and uniform stability of solutions in fractional neural systems using the contraction mapping principle and Krasnoselskii’s theorem.

The bidirectional associative memory (BAM) neural network, introduced by Kosko16, is a class of two-layer networks designed for associative learning and memory recall. These networks, characterized by bidirectional interconnections between the two layers, have been extensively studied in their integer-order forms with various types of delays and stability criteria17–19. The literature includes global asymptotic stability20, delay-dependent stability21, impulsive systems22, and discrete-time versions23. More recent studies have addressed stability properties in delayed neural networks, including local uniform and global asymptotic stability under various perturbations and timescales24. However, relatively little work has been done on fractional-order BAM models, particularly in the context of leakage delays, which represent the time lag in a neuron’s return to equilibrium.

Conclusion

This paper studies a class of Caputo-type fractional-order bidirectional associative memory (BAM) neural networks incorporating leakage delay in the view of perturbed system setup and establishes conditions for existence of solutions using fixed point theory. It also proves uniform stability of the system. A few nontrivial examples are provided to validate the theoretical results, demonstrating the applicability of the approach.

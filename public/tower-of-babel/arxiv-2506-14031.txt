Left-invariant G 2 ∗ {\rm G}_{2}^{*} -structures of type III
Viviana del Barco, Ana Cristina Ferreira, Ines Kath

Abstract
We investigate left-invariant G 2 ∗ {\rm G}_{2}^{*} -structures on 7-dimensional Lie groups, focusing on those whose holonomy algebras are indecomposable and of type III, the latter meaning that the socle of the holonomy representation is maximal. Building on the classification of indecomposable holonomy algebras contained in 𝔤 2 ∗ {{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}} , we determine which ones arise as infinitesimal holonomy algebras of type III for left-invariant G 2 ∗ {\rm G}_{2}^{*} -structures. Our main result shows that only abelian subalgebras occur, and these are necessarily of dimension two or three. Moreover, we provide explicit Lie groups with left-invariant G 2 ∗ {\rm G}_{2}^{*} -structures realizing these abelian holonomies.

Introduction

This article will deal with holonomy groups contained in the non-compact group of type
{\rm G}_{2}
, which is denoted by
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
. Such holonomy groups appear on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds of signature
(4,3)
. In particular, the full group
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
is a holonomy group, as it can indeed be realized as a holonomy of compact nilmanifolds
[
3
]
. Besides
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
itself, also proper subgroups occur as holonomy groups. We will be mainly interested in these subgroups, where we want to consider only connected ones which, moreover, are ‘genuine’ in the sense that the holonomy representation is indecomposable. Instead of studying the holonomy group itself, we consider its Lie algebra, which we call holonomy algebra. For a Lie subalgebra of
\mathop{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}}}(p,q)
, a necessary condition for it to be a holonomy is that it is a Berger algebra, i.e. that it satisfies Berger’s first criterion. In
[
1
]
, the indecomposable Berger algebras properly contained in the Lie algebra
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
of
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
were classified. They were distinguished by the dimension of the socle of their natural representation on the tangent space, this dimension is 1, 2 or 3 (see Section
2
for more information). Accordingly, we have three families of indecomposable Berger algebras, namely those of type I, II or III. For each type,
[
1
]
provides a complete list of all Berger algebras of that type. All these Berger algebras can indeed be realized as holonomy algebras of local metrics
[
2
,
8
,
9
]
. For some of these holonomy algebras a global metric has also been found.
Here we mean by ‘global’ that there is a ‘nice’ manifold with such a metric and not just a coordinate patch. In
[
6
]
, indecomposable indefinite symmetric spaces with
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure are classified. Their holonomy algebras are abelian and of dimension two or three.
In
[
3
]
, Fino and Lujan studied torsion-free
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures on nilpotent Lie groups. In addition to the already mentioned example whose holonomy is equal to
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
, they found an example of a torsion-free
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure with
6
-dimensional indecomposable holonomy. In

[
1
]
, new left-invariant metrics with indecomposable holonomy in
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
were found, where, for type III, only the 3-dimensional abelian Berger algebra was realized.
In addition to these examples, it is known that torsion-free left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures whose associated metric is bi-invariant on Lie groups with a cocompact lattice have a trivial holonomy
[
4
]
.

However, the question of which holonomy algebras contained in
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
are realizable by a left-invariant metric has not been studied systematically.
Here we will attack this question for holonomy algebras of type III, i.e. for those whose socle is maximal. We solve it completely under the additional assumption that not only the metric but also the parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure is left-invariant. More precisely, we determine which of the holonomy algebras of type III listed in
[
1
]
are the Lie algebra of the holonomy group of a Lie group endowed with a left-invariant torsion-free
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure and its associated metric.

We can state our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a subalgebra of
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
whose natural representation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,3}
is indecomposable and of type
III
. Then
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the infinitesimal holonomy of a left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure if and only if
{\mathfrak{h}}
is abelian. If
{\mathfrak{h}}
is abelian, then it has dimension two or three.

Our proof of the “if” direction is constructive. It consists of giving a one-parameter family of Lie groups such that all of them carry a parallel left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure whose holonomy is 3-dimensional abelian for non-zero values of the parameter, and 2-dimensional abelian otherwise. It is remarkable that their left-invariant metrics can also be induced by non left-invariant parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures, as we show in Section
7
.

To make a better description of the main result of the paper, let us recall some properties of
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
and introduce some notation. Recall that the complex Lie group
{\rm G}_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
contains two conjugacy classes of 9-dimensional maximal parabolic subgroups. These correspond to two conjugacy classes
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
of real subgroups in
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
. These conjugacy classes can be characterized in the following way. The group
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
acts transitively on the set of isotropic lines in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,3}
. Groups in
P_{1}
appear as the stabilizer of an isotropic line under this action. Similarly,
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
acts transitively on the set of isotropic 2-planes with vanishing cross product and groups in
P_{2}
can be understood as the stabilizer of such a 2-plane (see
[
1
]
for more explanations).
Let us denote by
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
the conjugacy classes of Lie algebras corresponding to
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.
Lie algebras in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
are isomorphic to
\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a 3-step nilpotent Lie algebra, while Lie algebras belonging to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
are isomorphic to
\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{{\mathfrak{n}}}
for a 5-dimensional Heisenberg algebra
{\mathfrak{n}}
.
Holonomy algebras of type III are subalgebras of Lie algebras in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
. More exactly, they are contained in a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes[{\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}}]
. In Section
2
we will give an explicit embedding of
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
into
\mathfrak{so}(4,3)
realizing
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
inside
\mathrm{SO}(4,3)
. Then a representative of
P_{1}
is given by the stabilizer of the real line spanned by the first basis vector. Its Lie algebra is equal to the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm I}\cong\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathfrak{m}}
defined in (
4
) and the subalgebra
\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes[{\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}}]
is equal to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
defined in (
8
). In (
10
), we define two abelian subalgebras
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,1)\subset{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,2)=[{\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}}]
of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
.
The abelian subalgebras appearing in Theorem
1.1
are conjugate to these two subalgebras.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we fix the standard embedding of
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
into
\mathop{{\rm SO}}(4,3)
and of its Lie algebra that will be used all along the paper. We introduce notation and recall the list of possible indecomposable holonomy algebras of type III obtained in
[
1
]
; all of them are of the form
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{a}}\ltimes{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,k)
with
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,k)
abelian (see Theorem
2.4
).

Section 3 specializes in left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures on Lie groups. We introduce geometric tensors for later use and show a restriction for the Levi-Civita connection on left-invariant vector fields, when the holonomy of the pseudo-Riemannian manifold is assumed to be contained
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
.

Section 4 summarizes technical results that will be used in the sequel; these are general results for pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups endowed with a left-invariant metric of signature
(4,3)
, whose Levi-Civita connection restricts to a particular subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}
.

Section 5 shows that if
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the holonomy algebra of a Lie group endowed with a left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure, then its
{\mathfrak{a}}
part cannot be a non-trivial subalgebra of the Lie algebra of diagonal matrices. This is a first step towards the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, Section 6 contains the second and final step of the “only if” part, that shows that actually
{\mathfrak{a}}
has to be zero dimensional and thus
{\mathfrak{h}}
is abelian.

Finally, Section 7 is devoted to constructing explicit examples of Lie groups with left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures whose holonomies are the remaining two abelian Lie algebras that are mentioned in our main theorem above.
The detailed proof of Theorem
1.1
is also part of this section.

Holonomy algebras of type III

The action of
\mathop{{\rm GL}}(7,{\mathbb{R}})
on
\bigwedge^{3}{\mathbb{R}}^{7}
has two open orbits. Elements of these orbits are called generic. Their stabilizers are isomorphic to
{\rm G}_{2}
for one orbit and to
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
for the other one. A generic three-form
\omega
induces an orientation and a non-degenerate symmetric inner product
{\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{7}
. If the stabilizer of
\omega
equals
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
, then this inner product has signature
(4,3)
. In particular,
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
is contained in
\mathop{{\rm SO}}({\mathbb{R}}^{7},{\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle})\cong\mathop{{\rm SO}}(4,3)
.

We want to fix a standard embedding of
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
into
\mathop{{\rm SO}}(4,3)
and describe its Lie algebra. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{7}
be the standard basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{7}
. The 3-form

\omega_{0}=\sqrt{2}(e^{167}+e^{235})-e^{4}\wedge(e^{15}-e^{26}-e^{37})\,

(1)

is generic and will serve as the standard 3-form here. It induces the metric

\displaystyle{\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(e^{1}\cdot e^{5}+e^{2}\cdot e^{6}+e^{3}\cdot e^{7})-(e^{4})^{2}.

(2)

The stabilizer of
\omega_{0}
in
\mathop{{\rm GL}}(7,{\mathbb{R}})
is the standard embedding of
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
that we will use in this article. Its Lie algebra
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}\subset\mathop{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}}}(4,3)
consists of all elements of the form

\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}+s_{4}&-s_{10}&s_{9}&\sqrt{2}s_{6}&0&-s_{11}&-s_{12}\\
-s_{8}&s_{1}&s_{2}&\sqrt{2}s_{9}&s_{11}&0&s_{6}\\
s_{7}&s_{3}&s_{4}&\sqrt{2}s_{10}&s_{12}&-s_{6}&0\\
\sqrt{2}s_{5}&\sqrt{2}s_{7}&\sqrt{2}s_{8}&0&\sqrt{2}s_{6}&\sqrt{2}s_{9}&\sqrt{2}s_{10}\\
0&s_{13}&s_{14}&\sqrt{2}s_{5}&-s_{1}-s_{4}&s_{8}&-s_{7}\\

(3)

Let us introduce some general notions before we study subalgebras of
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
. Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a subalgebra of
\mathop{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}}}(p,q)
on the pseudo-Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}

{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}
is called

if it does not leave invariant any proper non-zero subspace which is non-degenerate with respect to the pseudo-Euclidean inner product. The
socle
of a representation is its maximal semisimple subrepresentation. Suppose
that the natural representation of
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}}}(p,q)
is indecomposable but not irreducible. Then its socle is totally isotropic. In this case the dimension of the socle will be called the
type
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Now let us assume that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a subalgebra of
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
whose natural representation of
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,3}
is indecomposable but not irreducible. Since the socle is totally isotropic, its dimension is 1, 2, or 3. Accordingly, the type of
{\mathfrak{h}}
is denoted by I, II or III.

The following subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm I}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}
is an example of a subalgebra of type I:

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm I}:=\{h(A,v,u,y)\mid A\in\mathop{{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}}),\ v\in{\mathbb{R}},\ u,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\},\

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
be a subalgebra whose natural representation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,3}
is indecomposable and of type
III
. If
{\mathfrak{h}}
is abelian, then
{\mathfrak{h}}
is conjugate to
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,1)
or
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,2)
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is of type III, we may assume that it is contained in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
. Assume,
{\mathfrak{h}}
would contain an element
h(A_{0},v_{0},y_{0})
such that
\mathop{{\rm tr}}(A_{0})\not=0
. If we conjugate
{\mathfrak{h}}
by
\exp\big(h(0,v_{0}/\mathop{{\rm tr}}(A_{0}),0)\big)
, we obtain a subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}

\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
, which contains
h_{0}:=h(A_{0},0,y_{0})
. Since the natural representation of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}
is also indecomposable,
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}
contains an element of the form

, which is impossible since

is abelian. Consequently,

\mathop{{\mathfrak{sl}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})

\dim{\mathfrak{a}}\leq 1
. Assume that

. Then there exists an element
h(A_{0},v_{0},y_{0})
,

{\mathfrak{h}}
is of type III, the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{a}}
is semisimple. Since
{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathop{{\rm span}}\{A_{0}\}
, we see that
A_{0}
is a semisimple matrix. In particular,
A_{0}
is invertible because of
\mathop{{\rm tr}}(A_{0})=0

{\mathfrak{h}}
by
\exp\big(h(0,0,A_{0}^{-1}y_{0})\big)

Let
M
be a 7-dimensional manifold. A
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure on
M
is a reduction
P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
of the frame bundle
P(M)
to
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
. Equivalently, a
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure is a 3-form
\omega
on
M
which is generic at each point of
M
and whose stabilizer is isomorphic to
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
. The correspondence is given by

P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)=\{(e_{1},\dots,e_{7})\in P(M)\mid\omega=\omega_{0}\mbox{ w.r.t. }e_{1},\dots,e_{7}\}.

The bundle
P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
defines a pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
of signature
(4,3)
on
M
. This metric is given by (
2
) with respect to every frame
(e_{1},\dots,e_{7})\in P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
. The
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure is called parallel if the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
of
g
reduces to
P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
or, equivalently, if
\nabla^{g}\omega=0
.

We now assume that we are given a parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure
P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
on
M
together with the reduced Levi-Civita connection. We fix a point
x\in M
. Let
H_{x}
denote the holonomy group of
P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
at
x
. Let
\mathfrak{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{7})
be a frame at
x
which belongs to
P_{{\rm G}_{2}^{*}}(M)
. Using this frame, we can identify
H_{x}
with a subgroup of
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
. The conjugacy class of this subgroup in
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
depends neither on
x
nor on the chosen basis
\mathfrak{e}
. So each element of this congugacy class is called a holonomy group of the given
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure and its Lie algebra is called a holonomy algebra.

The natural representation of a holonomy algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,3}
is called holonomy representation. A holonomy algebra is called indecomposable if its holonomy representation is indecomposable.

Definition 2.2

(Type of the holonomy)
.

An indecomposable holonomy
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}
is of type
I
,
II
, or
III
, if the socle of the holonomy representation has dimension
1
,
2
or
3
, respectively.

Of course, the type of a holonomy algebra only depends on its conjugacy class.

In this paper we are interested in holonomy algebras
{\mathfrak{h}}
of type III. Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be such a holonomy algebra. As we have seen above, there exists an element
g\in{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
such that
\mathop{{\rm Ad}}(g)({\mathfrak{h}})\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
. So we may assume that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is contained in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\rm III}
.

An important tool in the study of holonomy groups is Berger’s first criterion, which we want to recall here. For a subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}}}(p,q)
,

{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{h}})=\{R\in{\textstyle\bigwedge^{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{p,q})^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}}\mid\forall x,y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}:R(x,y)z+R(y,z)x+R(z,x)y=0\}

is the space of formal curvature tensors with values in
{\mathfrak{h}}
. If

For

\langle f_{4},f_{4}\rangle=-1

. There exists exactly one further one-dimensional subspace

-1/2

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\varepsilon}
induces a non-degenerate inner product on

Although we have neither explained nor worked with the relation between spinors and
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures in this paper let us now roughly explain the idea behind the above construction using it. For details on this relation see
[
5
]
. Every (not necessarily parallel)
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure on a Lie group
G
that induces the same metric on
G
as the
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure
\omega
is given by a 3-form
A^{*}\omega
, where
A
is function from
G
to
{\rm O}({\mathfrak{g}},{\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle})\cong{\rm O}(4,3)
. If
A
maps to
\mathop{{\rm SO}}(4,3)
, then
A^{*}\omega
induces the same orientation as
\omega
, otherwise we get the opposite orientation. Let us first consider the situation at the identity
e\in G
. The
\mathop{{\rm SO}}(4,3)
-orbit of
\omega
in
\bigwedge^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
corresponds bijectively to the set of non-isotropic elements in the projective real spinor representation
(\Delta_{4,3}\setminus\{0\})/{\mathbb{R}}^{*}=({\mathbb{R}}^{4,4}\setminus\{0\})/{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
of
{\rm Spin}(4,3)
. Thus it can be identified with
(S^{4,3}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\cup(H^{3,4}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, where
S^{4,3}
and
H^{3,4}
are the sets of space- and time-like unit vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,4}
, respectively. Furthermore, it is well known that given a non-isotropic spinor
\psi_{0}\in\Delta_{4,3}
the Clifford multiplication
X\mapsto X\cdot\psi_{0}
defines an isometry from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
\psi_{0}^{\perp}\subset\Delta_{4,3}
.

Now we again turn to 3-forms and spinor fields on
G
. Then of course a
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure
A^{*}\omega
is parallel if and only if the associated unit spinor field (unique up to sign) is parallel. Consider
\omega
as a parallel 3-form on
G
and let
\psi_{0}
be the unit spinor field that corresponds to
\omega
. Assume that there exists a parallel isotropic vector field
X
on
G
. Then
\psi^{\prime}:=\psi_{0}+X\cdot\psi_{0}
is a parallel unit spinor field. Hence it corresponds to a parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure
\omega^{\prime}=A^{*}\omega
for some function
A:{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathop{{\rm SO}}(4,3)
. In the present example we applied this construction to the 3-dimensional space of isotropic parallel vector fields, which gave us a 3-parameter family of parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures.

Conversely, let
\omega^{\prime}=A^{*}\omega
be parallel and let
\psi^{\prime}
be the corresponding parallel unit spinor field (unique up to sign). We decompose
\psi^{\prime}=f\cdot\psi_{0}+\psi^{\perp}
, where
f
is a function on
G
and
\psi^{\perp}\perp\psi_{0}
. Since
\psi^{\prime}
and
\psi_{0}
are parallel,
f
is constant and
\psi^{\perp}
is also parallel. Thus we can define a parallel vector field
X
by
\psi^{\perp}=X\cdot\psi_{0}
. If the holonomy
{\mathfrak{h}}
is indecomposable, then
X
is isotropic. In particular, the three-parameter family in (
49
) contains all parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures that induce the same metric and the same orientation on
G_{\varepsilon}
as
\omega_{0}
. The parallel
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structures that induce the same metric and but the reverse orientation on
G
are then exactly the 3-forms
-\omega(a,b,c)
for
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a subalgebra of
{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{2}}
whose natural representation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4,3}
is indecomposable and of type III. By Lemma
2.1
,
{\mathfrak{h}}
is abelian if and only if it is conjugate to the two-dimensional Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,1)
or the three-dimensional Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,2)
. In particular, this proves the second assertion of the Theorem. Furthermore, the holonomy algebras of the examples described above are
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,1)
and
{\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,2)
. This shows one direction of the first assertion.

Assume now that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is an indecomposable type III infinitesimal holonomy algebra of a left-invariant
{\rm G}_{2}^{*}
-structure. Then, on the one hand,
{\mathfrak{h}}
is on the list in Theorem
2.4
. On the other hand,
{\mathfrak{a}}=0
holds by Proposition
6.3
. Thus
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,1)
or
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{m}}(1,0,2)
.
∎

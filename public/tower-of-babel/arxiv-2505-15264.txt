A Pöschl–Teller approximation to the wave equation outside a torus
Ronald Quirchmayr, Alden Waters

Abstract
. We prove dispersive estimates for an approximation to the wave equation in the exterior of a torus. Because no separation of variables into a basis of eigenfunctions and eigenvalues exists in toroidal coordinates for the time harmonic problem, we introduce a related approximate operator for the Dirichlet Laplacian in the exterior of a torus. The approximate operator separates into Schrödinger operators with Pöschl-Teller potentials after freezing N = N 0 N=N_{0} in the conjugated Laplacian. The operator here which we develop is related to the so-called Mehler-Fock kernel. Using the known solution to the eigenvector and eigenvalue problem of Pöschl-Teller, a high-frequency analysis of the approximate operator for the wave equation can be made accurately. The operator for this problem gives an angularly truncated L 1 → L ∞ L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty} dispersive estimate on compact coordinate collars. Duhamel’s principle controls its error for the corresponding exterior wave operator with Dirichlet Laplacian.



keywords:

Spectral geometry, wave equations, dispersion, scattering theory for obstacles.

MSC classes:
35S05; 35L20; 35L05; 35A08

1.
Introduction and statement of the main theorem

An important class of problems in the physics of dispersive waves deals with the effects of spatial obstacles on incoming waves such as reflection, absorption, diffraction or scattering.
A solid ball hit with sound waves that bounce off its boundary is the simplest example of an acoustic scattering problem. Recently, the precise rate of dispersion for waves in the exterior of a ball was determined in
[
14
]
. For generic obstacles this is a difficult problem. In particular, if the obstacle is not strictly convex, trapping can occur: e.g. in the form of repeated reflections making it impossible for waves to escape from certain regions.

When dealing with nonlinear generalizations of wave equations, the so-called
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
dispersive estimate for the wave operator is useful for proving well-posedness. However, it is difficult to determine the dispersion rate for Dirichlet or Neumann operators in the exterior of non-convex obstacles.
The aim of this work is to establish
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
estimates for a frozen-coefficient approximation to the Laplacian in the exterior of a particular non-convex obstacle, namely the torus in three-dimensional space.
For strictly convex domains, the problem has been well studied using Fourier Integral Operators (FIO) and semi-classical analysis, cf.
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
38
,
13
,
14
]
. The development of a local FIO would not give estimates which are global enough to track the effects of the trapping for large values of the time variable, as these are only accurate locally in small space-time coordinate patches.
Our new approach is based on a Pöschl-Teller potential eigenfunction solution for the frozen operator in combination with a Fourier series analysis in the angular variables.
We construct an operator which allows for analysis of the frozen conjugated Dirichlet-Laplacian and prove that it still satisfies a tractable inequality for
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
dispersion.
The error estimates we show quantify the coefficient-freezing and localization errors for the Cauchy problem for the wave equation with Dirichlet Laplacian in the exterior of the torus.

Let
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to[0,1]
be a fixed
high frequency cutoff function
. That is,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
with the property that
\varphi(k)=1
in a neighborhood of
k=b
for
b>0
.
Without loss of generality, we will assume that
\supp{\varphi}\subseteq(\frac{1}{2}b,\frac{3}{2}b)
and
b\geq 5
.
Let
D_{t}=-i\partial_{t}
.
The following well-known dispersive estimate holds true in the absence of obstacles (i.e., in the free space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
).

Theorem 1.1

(see e.g.
[
1
]
)
.

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
t\geq 0
and
h\in(0,1)
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi(hD_{t})\mathrm{e}^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}}\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Ch^{-d}\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{h}{t}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\right\}.

In the exterior of a ball this estimate is no longer satisfied in dimensions
d>3
.
Only recently, the work
[
13
]
showed that this estimate holds for the Dirichlet Laplacian in the exterior of a three-dimensional ball.
Their proof relies heavily on the existence of a nearly global FIO for the corresponding Helmholtz operator. The second author’s recent work on Maxwell’s equations in the exterior of a sphere in
[
5
]
uses separation of variables instead. The key proof techniques in
[
5
]
building on the spectral theory foundations in
[
35
]
allow for the representation of solutions in terms of so-called generalized eigenfunctions which provide a global representation for a solution to the Helmholtz equation in the exterior of a ball. The related Fourier integral operator in
[
13
]
also relies heavily on separation of variables, c.f. also the recent work in the exterior of a cylinder in
[
12
]
.
For the exterior of the torus, no such separated spectral solution in toroidal coordinates is possible as discussed in the work of
[
36
]
; cf. the discussion at the end of the article there.
However, for a certain perturbation of the principal symbol of the wave operator on the exterior of the torus, it is possible to develop a representation in terms of generalized eigenfunctions which have orthogonality properties in analogy to those of the Dirichlet eigenfunction problem in the exterior of a ball.

Therefore, instead of solving the wave equation itself, we study a related Pöschl-Teller operator
\Delta_{P}
being defined by means of toroidal coordinates, which has the same principal symbol as the wave operator with toroidal Laplacian
\Delta
. For the separated solution we freeze
N=N_{0}>0
and denote the resulting operator by
\Delta_{P,0}
.
This type of solution was used successfully in the high frequency asymptotics of
[
11
]
for the Schrödinger equation.
In analogy to the obstacle problem in the exterior of a ball, we change variables (to toroidal coordinates) and employ generalized eigenfunctions in terms of associated Legendre functions for the resulting Pöschl-Teller operator to construct solutions of

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{tt}-\Delta_{P,0}u=0\qquad&\text{in}\quad(0,\infty)\times M_{+},\\
u(0,\cdot)=q,\quad u_{t}(0,\cdot)=0\qquad&\text{in}\quad M_{+},\end{aligned}

where
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
\mathbf{T}^{2}
denote the two-dimensional torus and the three-dimensional solid open torus, whose boundary is
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Here
M_{+}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
; the Friedrichs endpoint condition is specified in Section
4
. More precisely, we construct a Greens operator
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)
, considered on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\mathbf{T}^{2}
being separated from
\mathbb{T}^{2}
by a small positive distance
\epsilon
, so that
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)q
uniquely solves (
1.2
) in the classical sense for initial data
q\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
satisfying
q\equiv 0
on
\{\tau<\epsilon_{0}\}
for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
, where
\tau
denotes the third (non-angular) toroidal coordinate; cf. Theorem
4.10
.

In order to establish dispersive estimates, we analyze
\varphi(hD_{t})\psi(D_{\phi_{2}})\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)
, where
D_{\phi_{2}}=-i\partial_{\phi_{2}}
with
\phi_{2}
being the second angular variable in toroidal coordinates. Here, for the sharp truncation,
\psi
is another cutoff taking the constant value
1
on the set
[0,\sqrt{k})
, where
k
denotes the radial spectral parameter corresponding to the associated Legendre eigenfunctions. The
k
-dependence is implicit.
In Fourier space,
\psi(D_{\phi_{2}})
acts as a truncation of the Fourier series with respect to
\phi_{2}
, which we may associate with a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
.
Furthermore, we restrict both spatial variables to the fixed coordinate collar
M_{I}
, where
I=[\epsilon_{0},\tau_{1}]
; cf. Lemma
4.1
for the lower bound on
N
away from spatial infinity.
Having briefly introduced our prerequisites, we state our main result—the dispersive estimates for the frozen operator in the exterior of a torus—as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist constants
C(\epsilon_{0},\eta)
and
C(\varepsilon)
such that for all
t\geq 0
and
h\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi(hD_{t})\psi(D_{\phi_{2}})\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(M_{I})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(M_{I})}\leq C(\epsilon_{0},\eta)h^{-3}\left(\min\left\{1,\frac{h}{t}\right\}+hC(\varepsilon)\right).

Here
N=N_{0}\geq\eta>0
is fixed. Constants may depend on the torus, collar,
N_{0},\varphi
and the angular profile;
C(\varepsilon)=1
for the sharp cutoff. The minimum at
t=0
is one.

The bound
\eta
can be chosen uniformly within a fixed neighboring region of the torus. This is further discussed in Section
4
, where this region is described in detail. The localization used in the error estimates keeps a positive distance from the boundary
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. This construction is analogous to the Greens function constructions of
[
6
,
7
]
, which approximate the principal symbol of the Laplacian in a local neighborhood of a manifold where the manifold has a strictly convex boundary. In our case we only have one coordinate neighborhood which encompasses the exterior of the torus, a benefit of using toroidal coordinates.

This estimate is for the frozen conjugated operator. Related questions concerning trapping and resolvents are studied in
[
37
]
. Moreover, the separated radial potential behaves like a
1/x^{2}
potential as
x\to 0
. Indeed, the representation of the operator to the corresponding time separable problem is related to the so-called Mehler-Fock kernel,
[
30
]
. The representation of the solutions from
[
11
]
allows us to use existing special function theory to obtain orthogonality representations. However, while there is a lot of literature on dispersion for Schrödinger operators, cf. e.g.
[
17
,
16
]
, the wave operator in the exterior of a torus presents additional difficulties. One can find related scattering theory for generalized eigenfunctions on manifolds with non-trivial metrics
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, or dispersive estimates for Maxwell’s equations
[
3
,
2
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
.

Our construction relies on solving the wave equation but with the Laplacian replaced by its frozen conjugated operator. It comes at a loss of variable coefficients from the principal symbol of the Dirichlet Laplacian in the exterior of a torus; this error is retained explicitly in the Duhamel estimate.
Even if one were to use the Melrose-Taylor parametrix around the boundary of the non-trapping part of the torus, one would also have this problem of error analysis. Here, we have a global coordinate system which admits a compact representation of the kernel, which uses the frozen coefficients of the conjugated Dirichlet Laplacian.
This allows us to estimate the error in approximating the wave operator at a small positive distance from the boundary.
Our Green’s function construction based on the Mehler-Fock transform seems to be a novel approach for conservative PDEs, which is the major contribution of this article. Pöschl-Teller potentials also arise in the spectral analysis of hyperbolic spaces; cf.
[
15
, Ch. 1.9]
. It is difficult to adapt this construction to conservative equations which is the main contribution of this paper-using a known coordinate system to solve a new type of problem.

The outline of this paper is as follows. Section
2
provides and overview on toroidal coordinates and formulates the initial value problem for the wave equation in the exterior of a torus with vanishing Dirichlet boundary data. We use the idea of the

potential to generate eigenfunctions to the Pöschl-Teller potential in Section

. We show that this operator allows us to solve the Cauchy problem for the frozen wave equation in Section
4
.
We prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
5
and draw final conclusions and future perspectives, including the error estimates, in Section
6
.
A collection of special function identities, asymptotics and related tools is provided in the appendices
A
and
B
.

2.
Toroidal coordinates

This section introduces toroidal coordinates. Later, in Sections
2.1
and
2.2
we provide formulations of the Laplacian and the obstacle problem in toroidal coordinates, respectively.

We rewrite the Cartesian coordinate triple
(x,y,z)
by means of toroidal coordinates
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)
as follows (cf.
[
23
]
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau;a)&\coloneqq a\frac{\sinh\tau}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}\cos\phi_{2},\\
y(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau;a)&\coloneqq a\frac{\sinh\tau}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}\sin\phi_{2},\\
z(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau;a)&\coloneqq a\frac{\sin\phi_{1}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}.\end{aligned}

Here,
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
lie in
(-\pi,\pi]
and
[0,2\pi)
, respectively, and
\tau>0
; the axis is obtained by coordinate limits.
Furthermore,
a>0
is a free parameter. The domains of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are interpreted as circles, which we denote by
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\phi_{1}}
and
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\phi_{2}}
.

In the following we briefly explain how tori of arbitrary radii can be obtained as isosurfaces by fixing
a
and
\tau
.
Let us first set
\phi_{2}\coloneqq 0
, which simplifies (
2.1
) to the corresponding set of bipolar coordinates in the
(x,z)
-plane. More precisely, we obtain the right
(x,z)
-half-plane since
\tau
is nonnegative.
That is,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x(\phi_{1},0,\tau;a)&=a\frac{\sinh\tau}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}},\\
z(\phi_{1},0,\tau;a)&=a\frac{\sin\phi_{1}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}.\end{aligned}

A calculation shows that

(x-a\coth\tau)^{2}+z^{2}=\frac{a^{2}}{\sinh^{2}\tau}=a^{2}\csch^{2}\tau.

Hence, for fixed
a>0
, the curves of constant
\tau>0
are circles of radii
a\csch\tau
, whose centers lie on the positive
x
-axis with a distance of length
a\coth\tau
away from the origin.
A rotation about the
z
-axis yields a parametrization of the corresponding torus by means of the toroidal coordinates in (
2.1
).

Finally, we show how to fix
a
and
\tau
in order to parameterize tori, which are given in terms of their radii
0<r<R
.
For this purpose, we consider a more conventional parametrization of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
: let
(X,Y,Z)\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\theta}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\varphi}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X(\theta,\varphi)&\coloneqq(R+r\cos\theta)\cos\varphi,\\
Y(\theta,\varphi)&\coloneqq(R+r\cos\theta)\sin\varphi,\\
Z(\theta,\varphi)&\coloneqq r\sin\theta.\end{aligned}

By fixing

a\coloneqq\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\tau\coloneqq\log\bigg(\frac{R+\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}}{r}\bigg)>0,

we obtain another parametrization of the same torus by means of the toroidal coordinates in (
2.1
).

2.1.
Laplacian in toroidal coordinates

For fixed
a>0
, let
u\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\phi_{1}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\phi_{2}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a function of the toroidal coordinates
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\phi_{1}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}_{\phi_{2}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. The Laplacian of a
C^{2}
-function
u
is given by (cf.
[
23
]
)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta u=\frac{(\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1})^{3}}{a^{2}\sinh\tau}\bigg[\partial_{\phi_{1}}\bigg(\frac{\sinh(\tau)\,u_{\phi_{1}}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}\bigg)+\frac{\csch(\tau)\,u_{\phi_{2}\phi_{2}}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}+\partial_{\tau}\bigg(\frac{\sinh(\tau)\,u_{\tau}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}\bigg)\bigg],\end{aligned}

where subscripts denote the respective partial derivatives, e.g.
u_{\phi_{1}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\phi_{1}}
.
The related Pöschl-Teller operator
\Delta_{P}
acts on
u
as follows:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{P}u=\frac{(\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1})^{3}}{a^{2}\sinh\tau}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{\sinh(\tau)\,u_{\phi_{1}\phi_{1}}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}\bigg)+\frac{\csch(\tau)\,(u_{\phi_{2}\phi_{2}}+\frac{1}{4}u)}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}+\bigg(\frac{\sinh(\tau)\,u_{\tau\tau}}{\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1}}\bigg)\bigg].

Writing
N=(\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1})/a
and
W=((\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1})/\sinh\tau)^{1/2}
, direct substitution gives

(2.4)

\displaystyle W^{-1}\Delta(Wf)=\Delta_{P}f.

For
h\in L^{1}(M)
we consider the partial Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
in the angular variables
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}(h)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\tau)\coloneqq\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}h(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\tau)\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi_{1}\theta_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi_{2}\theta_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}.

Applying
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
on
\Delta_{P,0}u
, with
N=N_{0}
fixed, results in

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}(\Delta_{P,0}u)=-\frac{N_{0}^{2}}{\sinh\tau}\bigg[\bigg(\sinh(\tau)\,|\xi_{1}|^{2}\tilde{u}\bigg)+\csch(\tau)\,(|\xi_{2}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4})\tilde{u}-\bigg(\sinh(\tau)\,\tilde{u}_{\tau\tau}\bigg)\bigg],

where
\tilde{u}=\mathcal{F}_{\phi}(u)
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We note that
\Delta
and
\Delta_{P}
have the same principal symbol and that the symbol of
\Delta_{P}
differs from the corresponding principal symbol only by a zero order term.

2.2.
Formulation of the obstacle problem

In the following we formulate the obstacle problem as an initial boundary value problem in the exterior of a torus in toroidal coordinates.

Fix
a,\tau_{1}>0
and consider the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
being given by

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\coloneqq\big\{\big(x,y,z\big)(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau_{1};a)\colon\phi_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi],\phi_{2}\in[0,2\pi)\big\}.

Furthermore, we denote the corresponding open solid torus by
\mathbf{T}^{2}
and refer to the closed subset

M\coloneqq{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\mathbf{T}^{2}=\overline{\big\{\big(x,y,z\big)(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau;a)\colon\phi_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi],\phi_{2}\in[0,2\pi),\,\tau\in(0,\tau_{1}]\big\}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

as the exterior of
\mathbf{T}^{2}
. Both
\mathbf{T}^{2}
and
M
have
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
as its boundary:
\partial\mathbf{T}^{2}=\partial M={\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.
We denote by
\Delta_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}
the Laplacian
\Delta
with vanishing Dirichlet boundary data on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

It is known that there is no separation of variables in toroidal coordinates for the time harmonic solution to the initial value problem for the wave equation with prescribed Dirichlet boundary data on
\partial M
.
The lack of such a separation for the exterior Helmholtz problem for the torus is discussed at the end of
[
36
]
.
For this reason, we will instead study the following related problem, where the Laplacian
\Delta
is replaced by the Pöschl-Teller operator
\Delta_{P}
:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{tt}-\Delta_{P}u=0\qquad&\text{for}\quad(t,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)\in(0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)\times(0,\tau_{1}],\\
u(t,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)=0\qquad&\text{for}\quad(t,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)\in(0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)\times\{\tau_{1}\},\\
\begin{cases}u(0,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)\;=u_{0}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau),\\
u_{t}(0,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)=u_{1}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau),\end{cases}\qquad&\text{for}\quad(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)\in(-\pi,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)\times(0,\tau_{1}].\end{aligned}

Here
u
is the conjugated unknown: by (
2.4
),
Wu
solves the physical Dirichlet wave equation with initial data
(Wu_{0},Wu_{1})
. We will construct the corresponding solution operator, with
N=N_{0}
frozen, in terms of special functions and estimate its error in Section
6
.

3.
Review of the solutions to the Pöschl-Teller Equation

This section introduces special functions, which we will later use to construct approximate solutions for (
2.6
), with
N=N_{0}
. We apply standard notation and therefore use
x>0
as independent variable (which is not related to the first toroidal coordinate component of the previous section).

Let
\mu\geq 0
be a parameter and
V\colon(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a potential defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle V(x)=\Big(\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\mathrm{sinh}^{-2}(x).

We consider the following Pöschl-Teller eigenfunction equation:

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}\psi(x)+V(x)\psi(x)=k^{2}\psi(x),\quad k^{2}>0,

which takes the form of a stationary Schrödinger equation.
Let
\mathsf{P}^{q}_{n}(x)
denote the Legendre function of order
q
and degree
n
on
x>1
, cf.
[
26
, §14.3(ii)]
.
The solution to the Pöschl-Teller eigenfunction equation is the weighted associated Legendre function

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}^{-\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh x)=\frac{(\sinh x)^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{\mu}}\,\mathbf{F}\bigg(\frac{\mu+\frac{1}{2}+ik}{2},\frac{\mu+\frac{1}{2}-ik}{2},\mu+1,-\sinh^{2}(x)\bigg).

This is a result of equation in
[
26
, (14.3.15)]
as remarked in
[
11
, (4.3–4.5)]
. Olver’s hypergeometric function
\mathbf{F}={}_{2}F_{1}/\Gamma(c)
is well-defined for
k,\mu>0
. Indeed, following
[
34
, Section 2]
and substituting
\xi=\tanh^{2}(x)
in (
3.2
) yields

(3.4)

\displaystyle\xi(1-\xi)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\psi(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}\xi^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\xi\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\psi(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}\xi}+\left(\frac{1}{(1-\xi)}\frac{k^{2}}{4}-\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{4}\right)\psi(\xi)=0.

For
\mu\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
, the general solution of this equation can be written as

(3.5)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=\left(\tanh(x)\right)^{-\mu+\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh(x))^{-ik}F(x),

where
F(x)
is a combination of hypergeometric functions:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F(x)&=A\,\mathbf{F}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\Big(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\Big)+ik\Big),\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\Big(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\Big)+2+ik\Big),1-\mu,\mathrm{tanh}^{2}(x)\right)\\
&\quad+B\tanh^{2\mu}(x)\,\mathbf{F}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mu+\frac{1}{2}+ik\Big),\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mu+\frac{1}{2}+1+ik\Big),\mu+1,\mathrm{tanh}^{2}(x)\right)\end{aligned}

with constants
A
and
B
.
Let
a,c\in{\mathbb{C}}
be constants and
z\in(0,1)
be a real number.
Applying the following substitution (see
[
26
, (15.9.17)]
)

\mathbf{F}\!\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2},c;z\right)=2^{c-1}z^{\frac{1-c}{2}}(1-z)^{-a+\frac{c-1}{2}}\,\mathsf{P}^{1-c}_{2a-c}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}}\right),

to the
B
-term in (
3.6
), setting
B\coloneqq 2^{-\mu}
and
A\coloneqq 0
yields the desired conclusion.
This solution vanishes at
x=0
with the Friedrichs behavior
x^{\mu+1/2}/(2^{\mu}\Gamma(\mu+1))
; for integer
\mu
the two terms in (
3.6
) are dependent. We will use the first type of solutions in our analysis because we will assume that the initial data in the Cauchy problem for the wave equation is
C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
.

The remainder of this section establishes a series of lemmas, which will be used later in Sections
4
and
5
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
it holds that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\downarrow 0}\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}^{-\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh x)=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{x\downarrow 0}\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh x)=0.

Proof.

For
\mu\geq 0
, the first relation follows by combining the representation in (
3.6
),
\tanh(0)=0
and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbf{F}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\mu+\frac{1}{2}+ik),\frac{1}{2}(\mu+\frac{3}{2}+ik),1+\mu,\mathrm{tanh}^{2}(0)\right)\bigg|<\infty.

The second relation follows from

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{-m}_{\nu}\left(x\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu-m+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\nu+m+1\right)}\mathsf{P}^{m}_{\nu}\left(x\right)

which is equation (14.9.13) in
[
26
]
for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
x>1
and
\nu=ik-\frac{1}{2}
with
k\neq 0
. Negative integer orders interchange the two relations.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\mu\geq 1
. Then

(3.10)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh x)|\leq\frac{(\sinh(x))^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}}{|\Gamma(ik-\frac{1}{2}-\mu)|}

for all
x>0
.
If
\mu\geq 1
, this holds true for
0<k<1
as well.

Proof.

The hypergeometric function has the following integral representation, cf.
[
26
, (15.6.1)]
:

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\!\left(a,b;c;z\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(b\right)\Gamma\left(c-b\right)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{b-1}(1-t)^{c-b-1}}{(1-zt)^{a}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

whenever
|\mathrm{arg}(1-z)|<\pi
,
\text{Re\,}c>\text{Re\,}b>0
.
We use (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) along with the representation in (
3.6
) multiplied by
2^{-\mu}=B
. The beta integral after taking absolute values is

B(\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{3}{4},\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{1}{4})\leq B(\frac{5}{4},\frac{3}{4})=\pi/(2\sqrt{2})<\frac{3}{2}\leq|ik-\frac{1}{2}-\mu|
.
Using
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
to shift the denominator gives the bound:

(3.12)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh x)|\leq\left|\frac{2^{-\mu}(\tanh(x))^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}(1-\tanh^{2}(x))^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mu+\frac{1}{2})}\Gamma(ik+\frac{1}{2}+\mu)}{\Gamma(ik-\frac{1}{2}-\mu)\Gamma(\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{ik}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{3}{4}-\frac{ik}{2})}\right|.

Applying (
A.3
) yields

(3.13)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh x)|\leq\frac{|\sinh(x)|^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}|\Gamma(\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{3}{4}+\frac{ik}{2})|}{|\Gamma(ik-\frac{1}{2}-\mu)\Gamma(\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{3}{4}-\frac{ik}{2})|}.

Using
\Gamma(\overline{z})=\overline{\Gamma(z)}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
z\notin\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
entails the result.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\mu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
z\in[0,1)
the hypergeometric function satisfies

(3.14)

\displaystyle 2^{-\mu}\left|\mathbf{F}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mu+\frac{1}{2}+ik\Big),\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mu+\frac{1}{2}+1+ik\Big),\mu+1,z\right)\right|\leq C_{z}\sqrt{\cosh(k)},

where
C_{z}
depends only on
z
. Therefore,

(3.15)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}^{\mu}(\cosh(x))|\leq C_{x}\sqrt{\cosh(k)}\left|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\mu+ik)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\mu+ik)}\right|,

where
C_{x}
depends only on
x
.

Proof.

Using the definition of the series for
\mathbf{F}(a,b,c,z)
where
a,b,c,z
are complex parameters we see that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(a,b,c,z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{\Gamma(c+n)}\frac{1}{n!}z^{n}

where
(w)_{n}
is the Pochhammer symbol defined by

(3.17)

\displaystyle(w)_{n}=\begin{cases}1\quad n=0\\
w(w+1)\dots(w+n-1)\quad n\neq 0.\end{cases}

For large
n
we have that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{\Gamma(c+n)}\frac{1}{n!}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(n)}\frac{\Gamma(b+n)}{\Gamma(n)}\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(c+n)}\frac{1}{n}\sim\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}n^{a+b-c-1}

where we have used that
\Gamma(\beta+n)\sim\Gamma(n)n^{\beta}\quad n\rightarrow\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. For the particular values of
a,b,c
in question we see that
\mathrm{Re}(a+b-c-1)=-1
. The integral representations
[
26
, (14.12.5),(14.12.7)]
and (
3.11
) give
|\mathsf{P}_{ik-1/2}(X)|\leq\mathsf{P}_{-1/2}(X)\leq 1
and hence

(3.19)

\displaystyle|\mathsf{P}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}^{-\mu}(X)|\leq\mathsf{P}_{0}^{-\mu}(X)=\frac{((X-1)/(X+1))^{\mu/2}}{\Gamma(\mu+1)}\qquad(X>1,\ \mu\geq 0).

With
X=\cosh x
,
z=\tanh^{2}x
, (
3.6
) therefore gives

2^{-\mu}|\mathbf{F}(a,b,c,z)|\leq(1-z)^{-1/4}\leq C_{z}\sqrt{\cosh(k)}
; the series covers
z=0
.
It follows that

(3.20)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{\sinh(x)}\mathsf{P}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}^{\mu}(\cosh(x))|\leq C_{x}\sqrt{\cosh(k)}\left|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\mu+ik)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\mu+ik)}\right|,

where
C_{x}
is independent of
k,\mu
but depends on
x>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For large positive
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
k
, there is a
\beta_{x}\in(0,\infty)
decreasing in
x
such that there is a constant
C
which is independent of the other variables with

(3.21)

\displaystyle|\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\cosh(x)\right)|\leq C\left|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mu\beta_{x}}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\mu+ik)}\right|.

Proof.

Let
\xi=\frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{x+1}{x-1})
,
x>1
and
K
be the modified Bessel function of the second kind whose value according to
[
26
, (10.39.2)]
is

K_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(z\right)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}\mathrm{e}^{-z}.

By (
3.19
) one has for
\mu>0
,
x>1
that

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{-\mu}_{0}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\mu+1\right)}\left(\frac{2\mu\xi}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}K_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mu\xi\right)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mu\xi}}{\Gamma(\mu+1)}.

Replacing
x
by
\cosh x
gives
\beta_{x}=\log\coth(x/2)
. Applying (
3.9
) and

|\Gamma(\mu+\frac{1}{2}+ik)|\leq\Gamma(\mu+\frac{1}{2})\leq\Gamma(\mu+1)
for
\mu\geq 1
yields the result with
C=1
.
∎

4.
The Kernel Representation

In this section we construct the solution operator and its kernel for the Pöschl-Teller initial value problem with
N=N_{0}
and the Friedrichs boundary condition at
\tau=0
.

Let

(4.1)

\displaystyle N(\tau,\phi_{1})\coloneqq\frac{(\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1})}{a}.

It holds that
N(\tau,\phi_{1})>0
for all
(\tau,\phi_{1})\in(0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]
.

The following straightforward lemma ensures a uniform lower bound for
N
within arbitrarily large neighborhoods of the torus.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<\epsilon_{1}<\min\{\tau_{1},\pi\}
be arbitrarily small and consider the subset

V_{\epsilon_{1}}\coloneqq\{(\tau,\phi_{1})\in[0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]\colon\tau<\epsilon_{1}\}\cap\{(\tau,\phi_{1})\in[0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]\colon|\phi_{1}|<\epsilon_{1}\}.

Then there exists some
\eta(\epsilon_{1})>0
such that

N(\tau,\phi_{1})>\eta(\epsilon_{1})\quad\text{for all}\quad(\tau,\phi_{1})\in\big([0,\infty)\times(-\pi,\pi]\big)\setminus V_{\epsilon_{1}}.

Figure 1.
A two-dimensional cross section: the solid torus is indicated by the two dark discs; the outer striped (gray) area shows the set
V_{\epsilon_{1}}
from Lemma
4.1
; solid lines depict
\phi_{1}
and
\tau
isosurfaces.

Remark 4.2
.

In this section we are not requiring a prescribed positive lower bound for
N_{0}
; Lemma
4.1
will become relevant for the dispersive estimates in Section
5
.

For small
\epsilon_{1}
, the region
V_{\epsilon_{1}}
, which is depicted in Fig.
1
, lies far away from the torus. This region could be analyzed using Lax-Phillips type scattering arguments from
[
5
, Section 6]
, which have been adapted from
[
18
]
and
[
4
]
. These arguments could be used to investigate whether the
\eta
-dependence can be removed using Huygens’ principle. In other words, in
V_{\epsilon_{1}}
one expects the dispersive rate to be as in the obstacle-free Euclidean space; this requires a separate analysis. For this reason we will not investigate this case here. We notice that the additional restriction of a positive distance to
\mathbb{T}^{2}
still allows a very close neighborhood of the torus to be included in the estimates, with constants depending on that distance.

Next we define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\tau}\coloneqq-\xi^{2}_{1}-\csch^{2}(\tau)(|\xi_{2}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4})+\partial_{\tau}^{2},

which corresponds to the Fourier transform of
\Delta_{P}
without the prefactor
N^{2}
.

Let
A
be the Friedrichs realization of
-N^{-2}\Delta_{P}
in
L^{2}(M_{+},\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{2})
and put

(4.3)

\displaystyle H_{0}=N_{0}^{2}A=-\Delta_{P,0}.

Hardy’s inequality gives
A\geq 0
; the zero-mode condition excludes
\sqrt{\tau}\log\tau
. Data in
C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
are compactly supported in the interior and extended by zero.

Definition 4.3
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}_{0}
we define the functions
e_{k,\mu}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

(4.4)

\displaystyle e_{k,\mu}(\tau)\coloneqq c_{k,\mu}\sqrt{\sinh\tau}\,\mathsf{P}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}^{\mu}(\cosh\tau),

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle c_{k,\mu}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{k\sinh(\pi k)}{\pi}\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\mu+ik\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\mu-ik\Big)}.

We have that
e_{k,\mu}(\tau)=K^{\mu}(k,\tau)
, see Appendix
B
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
k\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\mu,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}_{0}
,
e_{k,\mu}
is a classical point-wise solution of

(4.6)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{m,\mu,\tau}\,e_{k,\mu}=(m^{2}+k^{2})e_{k,\mu},\quad e_{k,\mu}(0)=0.

Proof.

This follows directly from the definition of
e_{k,\mu}
and the fact that (
3.3
) is a solution of (
3.2
) combined with (
3.9
). The boundary condition holds true by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
consist of those
G\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
, which satisfy

(4.7)

\displaystyle G(x)=\begin{cases}o(e^{-x})\quad x\rightarrow\infty\\
O(1)\quad\quad x\rightarrow 0^{+}\end{cases}

and
G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
.
We denote by
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}}
the identity and zero operator on
\mathcal{A}
, respectively.
All radial inner products use
\mathrm{d}\tau
and are linear in the first argument.

Lemma 4.6
.

For all
\mu,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}_{0}
it holds that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e_{k,\mu}(\cdot)\langle\cdot,\overline{e_{k,\mu}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\,\mathrm{d}k=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}.

We remark here that the dot inside the round parenthesis is a placeholder for
\tau
, whereas the dot inside the angled parenthesis is a placeholder for functions
G\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathcal{A}
. Then by Theorem
B.1
and (
B.5
),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e_{k,\mu}(\tau)\langle G,\overline{e_{k,\mu}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\,\mathrm{d}k=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}G(\tau^{\prime})K^{\mu}(k,\tau^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\bigg)K^{\mu}(k,\tau)\,\mathrm{d}k=G(\tau).

∎

Definition 4.7
.

Let
m,\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}_{0}
,
\phi_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi]
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. On the domain
\mathcal{A}
we define the operator

(4.9)

\displaystyle g(t,\phi_{1},m,\mu,\cdot)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\!\big(N_{0}\sqrt{m^{2}+k^{2}}\,t\big)e_{k,\mu}(\cdot)\langle\cdot,\overline{e_{k,\mu}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\,\mathrm{d}k.

Here again we have that the dots inside the round parentheses are placeholders for
\tau
, whereas the dot inside the angled parenthesis is a placeholder for
G\in\mathcal{A}
, which we interpret as function of
\tau^{\prime}
:
G=G(\tau^{\prime})
. Thus,
g(t,\phi_{1},m,\mu,\cdot)\colon\allowbreak\mathcal{A}\to L^{2}(0,\infty)
is an operator, whereas
g(t,\phi_{1},m,\mu,\tau)\colon\allowbreak C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a functional. We note that
g(0,\phi_{1},m,\mu,\tau)
is precisely the Dirac delta
\delta_{\tau}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Fix
\mu,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}_{0}
,
\phi_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Then
g(\cdot,\phi_{1},m,\mu,\cdot)
satisfies

(4.10)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{2}-N_{0}^{2}\Delta_{m,\mu,\tau})g=\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}},\quad g|_{\tau=0}=\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}},\quad g|_{t=0}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\partial_{t}g|_{t=0}=\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}}.

The identities hold spectrally.

Proof.

It is a consequence of Lemma
4.4
and spectral calculus that

(\partial_{t}^{2}-N_{0}^{2}\Delta_{m,\mu,\tau})g=\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Moreover,
g|_{\tau=0}=\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}}
by the Friedrichs condition and Lemma
4.4
, and differentiation of the cosine factor on the operator domain implies
\partial_{t}g|_{t=0}=\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
From Lemma
4.6
it follows that
g|_{t=0}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

In the following we define the integral kernel and the corresponding solution operator for the Pöschl-Teller problem.

Definition 4.9
.

For
\phi_{1},\phi_{1}^{\prime}\in(-\pi,\pi]
,
\phi_{2},\phi_{2}^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}
we define the integral kernel

(4.11)

\displaystyle G_{P}(t,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau,\phi_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{2}^{\prime})\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\sum^{\infty}_{\mu=0}w_{m}w_{\mu}\cos((\phi_{1}-\phi_{1}^{\prime})m)\cos((\phi_{2}-\phi_{2}^{\prime})\mu)\,g(t,\phi_{1},m,\mu,\tau).

Here
w_{0}=1
and
w_{j}=2
for
j\geq 1
. It operates on functions
H\in\mathcal{A}
,
\tau^{\prime}\mapsto H(\tau^{\prime})
.
For each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the associated integral operator
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)
acts on
q\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
as follows:

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)(q)\big|_{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau)}\coloneqq\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}G_{P}(t,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\tau,\phi_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{2}^{\prime})q(\phi_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{2}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{1}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{2}^{\prime}.

We recall that, with
N=N_{0}
,
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)=\cos(t\sqrt{H_{0}})
.
Next, we state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
q\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
such that
q\equiv 0
on
\{\tau^{\prime}<\epsilon_{0}\}
for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)(q)
, when considered on compact subsets of
M_{+}
at a positive distance from
\tau=0
, is a classical solution of

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta_{P,0})u=0\qquad&\text{in}\quad\,(0,\infty)\times M_{+},\\
u=0\qquad&\text{on}\quad(0,\infty)\times\{\tau=0\},\\
u(0,\cdot)=q,\;\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)=0\qquad&\text{in}\quad M_{+},\end{aligned}

where
\Delta_{P,0}=-H_{0}
. The endpoint condition is Friedrichs, and uniqueness holds in the energy class.

Proof.

Let
q\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
. By assumption, the support of
q
in the
\tau^{\prime}
variable is contained in the set
[\epsilon_{0},\tau^{\prime\prime}]
for some
\tau^{\prime\prime}<\tau_{1}<\infty
. While it is possible to consider
q
where the
\tau^{\prime}
dependence is in the class
\mathcal{A}
, the proofs are significantly more technical so we take this dense subclass of
\mathcal{A}
instead. It is easy to check that
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)(q)
is a formal solution. We set

(4.14)

\displaystyle f_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})\coloneqq\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}q(\phi_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{2}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\,\mathrm{e}^{-im\phi_{1}^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\mu\phi_{2}^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{1}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{2}^{\prime}.

Performing an integration by parts yields that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})\coloneqq f_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})(1+\mu^{2}+m^{2})\in\mathcal{A}\quad\forall m,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}.

Repeated integration by parts gives
\|\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\ell}f_{m,\mu}\|_{1}\leq C_{P,\ell,q}(1+m^{2}+\mu^{2})^{-P}
for every
P,\ell
, also for signed modes.
We set
\sigma_{k,m}\coloneqq\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}
, consider

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\sum^{\infty}_{\mu=0}\Bigg[\mathrm{e}^{im\phi_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{i\mu\phi_{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\bigg((N_{0}^{2}\sigma_{k,m}^{2})^{j}\int^{\infty}_{0}c^{2}_{k,\mu}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau^{\prime})\sqrt{\sinh\tau^{\prime}}f_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\\
\times\mathrm{e}^{iN_{0}t\sigma_{k,m}}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau)\sqrt{\sinh\tau}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigg]\end{aligned}

for
j=0,1,2
, and observe that a bound for this expression directly yields bounds for the even time derivatives and hence
\partial^{j}_{t}\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)(q)
,
\Delta_{P,0}\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)(q)
and
\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)(q)
itself.

We establish the required integral estimates in three steps, followed by the joint large-
k
, large-
\mu
range. Let us therefore fix
k_{0},\mu_{0}\geq 1
and abbreviate the inner integral in (
4.16
) as follows:

\displaystyle r_{m,\mu,j}(k)\coloneqq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\sigma_{k,m}^{2j}f_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})c^{2}_{k,\mu}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau^{\prime})\sqrt{\sinh\tau^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime},\quad j=0,1,2.

We fix
\tau\in J\Subset(0,\infty)
; dependence on
q,J,N_{0}
is suppressed below.

(1)

k\geq k_{0}
,
\mu\leq\mu_{0}
:
We have to show that
\frac{r_{m,\mu,j}(k)}{c_{k,\mu}}
decays sufficiently fast as
k\to\infty
. This step is similar to the proof of the Mehler-Fock inversion formula following from Theorem
B.1
, where one checks that
r_{m,\mu,0}(0)=0
, and
r_{m,\mu,0}\rightarrow 0
sufficiently fast as
k\to\infty
. The proof here is inspired by the one in
[
28
]
, but there are some significant differences to just proving the Mehler-Fock inversion theorem because of the evolution in time and the additional angular variables.
We start with an integration by parts argument which uses the equation (
A.2
) and the equality for large
k
(
B.6
). The equation (
A.2
) along with definition (
B.3
) gives

(4.17)

\displaystyle|c_{k,\mu}|=\left|\frac{k\sinh(\pi k)}{\pi}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\mu+ik)\right|=

(4.18)

\displaystyle\left|k\tanh(\pi k)\prod_{\ell=1}^{\mu}\left((\ell-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+k^{2}\right)^{-1}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Naturally we have that in this region

(4.19)

\displaystyle|c_{k,\mu}|=O(k^{-\mu+\frac{1}{2}}),\quad\text{as}\;k\to\infty

uniformly in
\mu\leq\mu_{0}
.
Using again (
B.6
) and (
4.15
) repeatedly to replace
f
with
\tilde{f}
, we integrate by parts with
T_{\mu}=-\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2}+(\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\csch^{2}\tau^{\prime}
and
T_{\mu}e_{k,\mu}=k^{2}e_{k,\mu}
. This gives

\displaystyle\left|\frac{r_{{m,\mu},j}(k)}{c_{k,\mu}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}c_{k,\mu}\sigma_{k,m}^{2j}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau^{\prime})\sqrt{\sinh(\tau^{\prime})}f_{m\mu}(\tau^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k,m}^{2j}k^{-2Q}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e_{k,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})T_{\mu}^{Q}f_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(P,Q,\mu_{0},\tau^{\prime\prime})\sigma_{k,m}^{2j}}{k^{2Q}(1+m^{2})^{P}},\quad j=0,1,2,

for any
P,Q\in\mathbb{N}
, since
f
is compactly supported.
Therefore, it remains to show that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int^{\infty}_{k_{0}}r_{{m,\mu},j}(k)N_{0}^{2j}\mathrm{e}^{iN_{0}t\sigma_{k,m}}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau)\sqrt{\sinh(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}k\bigg|\leq\frac{C(\mu_{0},k_{0},\tau^{\prime\prime},\tau)}{1+m^{2}},

for finite
\tau
, where
C(\mu_{0},k_{0},\tau^{\prime\prime},\tau)
depends on
\mu_{0},k_{0},\tau^{\prime\prime}
,
\tau
only.
We infer this as a consequence of the large
k
asymptotics in (
B.6
), which implies that

(4.20)

\displaystyle|\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau)|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\sinh(\tau)}}k^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\tau\in J,

where
C
depends on
\mu_{0},J
. Choosing
P,Q
sufficiently large proves the integral bound.
Using Fubini’s theorem to sum over
m,\mu
concludes the proof for this case.

(2)

k\leq k_{0},\mu\leq\mu_{0}
:

The second step is to see that the integral of

c_{k,\mu}^{2}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau^{\prime})\sqrt{\sinh(\tau^{\prime})}f_{m,\mu}(\tau^{\prime})

over
\tau^{\prime}
and subsequently the integral of

\tilde{r}_{m,\mu,j}(t,\phi_{1},k)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{iN_{0}t\sigma_{k,m}}r_{m,\mu,j}

against
\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau)
over
k\leq k_{0}
is bounded. But this follows easily by applying Lemma
3.3
with
\mu\leq\mu_{0}
and (
4.17
), which give
e_{k,\mu}=O_{\mu_{0},J}(k)
at zero. We conclude that for
\mu\leq\mu_{0}
there exists some
C(\mu_{0},k_{0},\tau^{\prime\prime},\tau)
such that

Remark 4.11
.

We remark here that the parametrix does not respect the boundary conditions on the torus. To take them into account, one could e.g. base the kernel construction on the following solution of the Pöschl-Teller equation:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sinh(\tau)}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau)-\frac{\sqrt{\sinh(\tau_{1})}\mathsf{P}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau_{1})}{\sqrt{\sinh(\tau_{1})}\mathsf{Q}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau_{1})}\sqrt{\sinh(\tau)}\mathsf{Q}^{\mu}_{ik-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh\tau);

recall that we used only the first term of this expression in our construction. Here
\mathsf{Q}
is the independent second-kind solution
[
26
, (14.2.10)]
, the denominator is assumed nonzero, and the condition at
\tau=0
must also be imposed.
However, this would then eliminate the possibility of using the same Mehler-Fock kernel in the Green’s function to represent generic Cauchy data.
On the other hand, we need to keep some positive distance from the boundary
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
when applying our result to the original wave operator, where the Laplacian is given by
\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
instead of
\Delta_{P}
. Since both these operators have the same principal symbol, this “safety distance” allows us to treat the error
\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-\Delta_{P}
as a lower order differential operator on the collar. For the frozen operator
\Delta_{P,0}
, the additional second-order error
(N^{2}-N_{0}^{2})A
and the spatial cutoff commutator are estimated in Section
6
.
For these reasons, it is not possible to extend the result up to the boundary
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
by this argument.

\displaystyle\frac{f(k)}{h_{1}}=\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}}{h_{1}}tN\pm\frac{(\tau\pm\tau^{\prime})}{h_{1}}k.

From which it follows that the stationary points are given explicitly by

(5.26)

\displaystyle k_{0}=m\left(\pm\frac{\tau\pm\tau^{\prime}}{(t^{2}N^{2}-(\tau\pm\tau^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

with the same sign and time restrictions as before. This gives that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|f^{\prime\prime}(k_{0})|}=\frac{1}{tN}\left(\frac{(k_{0}^{2}+m^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{m^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{C_{\varphi}}{tNm^{2}}

for
k_{0}^{2}+m^{2}
in the domain of the cutoff
\varphi
. It follows that in the notation of Lemma

we have, for each choice

(5.28)

\displaystyle\rho(k)=\mathbf{1}_{\{k\geq h_{1}\max(1,\mu^{2})\}}\psi_{m}(k)\rho_{\mu,\epsilon}(k/h_{1},\tau)\rho_{\mu,\epsilon^{\prime}}(k/h_{1},\tau^{\prime}),

so

. The mode and time splits of Proposition

(5.29)

\displaystyle|\varphi(hD_{t})\psi(D_{\phi_{2}})G_{I}|\leq C(\epsilon_{0},\eta)h^{-3}\left(\min\{1,h/t\}+hC(\varepsilon)\right).

This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We combine Propositions

) in Remark

6.
Conclusion and Future Directions

This paper presents a parametrix, with
N=N_{0}
frozen, for the Dirichlet wave equation in the exterior of a torus in three dimensions. A bound on a larger range of frequencies in the
\phi_{2}^{\prime}
variable, e.g. a removal of
\psi(D_{\phi_{2}})
, could be achieved by finer uniform special function theory asymptotics. Finer special function theory estimates for the associated Legendre functions would give as an application a wider range of
\mu
. In particular higher values of
\mu
than in this spectral cutoff range might not give estimates in analogy to the Euclidean free space dispersive estimate; the Bessel function estimates used here are not uniform in the enlarged range.

6.1.
Error estimates

We compare the frozen propagator with the physical Dirichlet solution, keeping the same initial data. Choose
0<\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}
and a real cutoff
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((\tau_{0},\tau_{1}))
,
0\leq\chi\leq 1
. Let
q\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M_{+})
be supported where
\chi=1
, and put

(6.1)

\displaystyle v_{0}(t)=\mathcal{G}_{P}(t)q,\qquad u_{\mathrm{app}}(t)=W\chi v_{0}(t).

The latter is extended by zero outside the coordinate collar. It satisfies the physical Dirichlet boundary condition, and its initial data are
(Wq,0)
. All unweighted norms below are in
L^{2}(M_{+},\mathrm{d}X)
, where
\mathrm{d}X=\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{2}
.

The conjugation (
2.4
) gives the exact residual

\displaystyle F(t)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)u_{\mathrm{app}}(t)

(6.2)

\displaystyle=W\left\{\chi(N^{2}-N_{0}^{2})Av_{0}(t)+N^{2}[A,\chi]v_{0}(t)\right\},

(6.3)

\displaystyle[A,\chi]f

\displaystyle=-2\chi^{\prime}\partial_{\tau}f-\chi^{\prime\prime}f.

Thus the two errors come from freezing the coefficient and from the cutoff, respectively.

Let
H=-\Delta_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}
be the nonnegative self-adjoint Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})
, and let
u(t)=\cos(t\sqrt{H})(Wq)
be the physical solution. The error
e=u-u_{\mathrm{app}}
has zero initial and boundary data. Duhamel’s principle yields

(6.4)

\displaystyle e(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{H})}{\sqrt{H}}F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the multiplier has its continuous value
t-s
at zero. This formula uses the physical propagator only through spectral calculus; the approximation (
6.1
) still uses the Pöschl–Teller transform of Section
4
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For
t\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{E}(e;t)=\left(\|\partial_{t}e(t)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}^{2}+\|\nabla e(t)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Then

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(e;t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\|e(t)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}\,\mathrm{d}s.

In particular, set

\displaystyle N_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-1}{a},\qquad N_{+}=\frac{\cosh\tau_{1}+1}{a},

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\max\{|N_{-}^{2}-N_{0}^{2}|,|N_{+}^{2}-N_{0}^{2}|\},\qquad B_{1}=\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty},\quad B_{2}=\|\chi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle C(q)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{a}\left\{\frac{\delta}{N_{-}}\|Aq\|_{2}+N_{+}\left[2B_{1}\|A^{1/2}q\|_{2}+\left(\frac{B_{1}}{\tau_{0}}+B_{2}\right)\|q\|_{2}\right]\right\}.

We have

(6.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(e;t)\leq tC(q),\qquad\|e(t)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}C(q).

Proof.

The spectral theorem applied to (
6.4
) gives (
6.5
) and (
6.6
): for each spectral value
\lambda\geq 0
, the energy multipliers are
\cos((t-s)\sqrt{\lambda})
and
\sin((t-s)\sqrt{\lambda})
, whose squared magnitudes sum to one, while
|\sin((t-s)\sqrt{\lambda})/\sqrt{\lambda}|\leq t-s
.

On the collar, the argument of Lemma
4.1
gives
N\geq N_{-}
. The toroidal volume element and the weight satisfy

(6.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol}=\frac{a^{3}\sinh\tau}{(\cosh\tau-\cos\phi_{1})^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}X,\qquad W^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol}=aN^{-2}\mathrm{d}X.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}\leq\sqrt{a}\left\{\frac{\delta}{N_{-}}\|Av_{0}(s)\|_{2}+N_{+}\|[A,\chi]v_{0}(s)\|_{2}\right\}.

To estimate the commutator at the critical radial endpoint, use the quadratic-form identity

\displaystyle\|A^{1/2}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\partial_{\tau}f-\frac{f}{2\tau}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial_{\phi_{1}}f\|_{2}^{2}+\|\csch\tau\,\partial_{\phi_{2}}f\|_{2}^{2}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\int_{M_{+}}(\tau^{-2}-\csch^{2}\tau)|f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}X.

It follows by integration by parts on
C_{c}^{\infty}(M_{+})
and extends to the Friedrichs form domain by closure. Every term on the right is nonnegative. Since
\supp\chi^{\prime}\subset\{\tau>\tau_{0}\}
,

\displaystyle\|[A,\chi]f\|_{2}\leq 2B_{1}\|A^{1/2}f\|_{2}+(B_{1}/\tau_{0}+B_{2})\|f\|_{2}.

Finally, spectral calculus gives
\|A^{\alpha}v_{0}(s)\|_{2}\leq\|A^{\alpha}q\|_{2}
for
\alpha=0,\frac{1}{2},1
. Hence
\|F(s)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}\leq C(q)
, proving (
6.7
).
∎

If the radial support of
q
is at distance
d>0
from
\supp\chi^{\prime}\cup\supp\chi^{\prime\prime}
, finite propagation speed in the radial direction gives
[A,\chi]v_{0}(s)=0
for
0\leq s<d/N_{0}
. Indeed the radial principal part of the frozen equation is
\partial_{t}^{2}-N_{0}^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}
, and the local energy argument gives radial speed
N_{0}
. On this time interval the bounds improve to

(6.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(e;t)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{a}\,\frac{\delta}{N_{-}}\,t\|Aq\|_{2},

(6.11)

\displaystyle\|e(t)\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{a}\,\frac{\delta}{2N_{-}}\,t^{2}\|Aq\|_{2}.

These are energy and displacement estimates, with no dispersive assumption. Small error requires control of the displayed coefficient variation and cutoff terms; freezing
N
alone does not guarantee such smallness.

Appendix A
Special functions

Our analysis relies on certain aspects of special functions, which we briefly review here.

A.1.
Gamma function identities

The Gamma function is defined for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}z>0
by

\Gamma\left(z\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t}t^{z-1}\,\mathrm{d}t;

it extends analytically to
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
.
For
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
x\notin\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
, it holds that

|\Gamma\left(x+\mathrm{i}y\right)|\leq|\Gamma\left(x\right)|.

Furthermore, for
y\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
,

(A.1)

\displaystyle|\Gamma(iy)|^{2}=\frac{\pi}{y\sinh(\pi y)},

and for half-integer real parts, it holds that

(A.2)

\displaystyle|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\pm n+iy)|^{2}=\frac{\pi}{\cosh(\pi y)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left((k-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{\pm 1},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

We also have the doubling formula:

(A.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}2^{1-2z}\Gamma(2z).

A.2.
Bessel, modified Bessel & Hankel functions

The Bessel function of the first kind, denoted by
J_{n}(z)
, satisfies Bessel’s equation

z^{2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}w}{{\mathrm{d}z}^{2}}+z\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}z}+(z^{2}-n^{2})w=0.

The modified Bessel function of the first kind, denoted by
I_{n}(z)
, can be written in terms of the Bessel function of the first kind:

(A.4)

\displaystyle I_{n}(z)=i^{-n}J_{n}(iz)=\mathrm{e}^{-n\pi\frac{i}{2}}J_{n}(z\mathrm{e}^{\frac{i\pi}{2}}).

We also have that

(A.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}J_{n}(z)=J_{n-1}(z)-\frac{n}{z}J_{n}(z),

which is also satisfied by the Hankel functions of the first and second kind
H^{(1)}_{n}(z)
and
H^{(2)}_{n}(z)
.

We have that (cf.
[
25
, Exercise 9.1 & (9.07)]
)

(A.6)

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(z)=\frac{(\frac{z}{2})^{\nu}\mathrm{e}^{-iz}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}M(\nu+\frac{1}{2},2\nu+1,2iz),

where

(A.7)

\displaystyle M(a,c,z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(c-a)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{c-a-1}\mathrm{e}^{zt}\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\mathrm{Re}(c)>\mathrm{Re}(a)>0.

We note that for
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
and real
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\Big|M\Big(\nu+\frac{1}{2},2\nu+1,2iz\Big)\Big|\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}M\Big(\nu+\frac{1}{2},2\nu+1,2iz\Big)\Big|\leq 1.

The relationship to the Hankel functions of the first and second kind is as follows:

(A.9)

\displaystyle 2J_{\nu}(z)=H^{(1)}_{\nu}(z)+H^{(2)}_{\nu}(z).

This is useful for the expansion of the Bessel functions for large
|z|
. Let

a_{k}(\nu)=\frac{(4\nu^{2}-1^{2})(4\nu^{2}-3^{2})\cdots(4\nu^{2}-(2k-1)^{2})}{k!8^{k}},

where
a_{0}(\nu)=1
. From
[
26
, 10.17.13–15]
, we have that

(A.10)

\displaystyle\left.\begin{matrix}H^{(1)}_{\nu}(z)\\
H^{(2)}_{\nu}(z)\end{matrix}\right\}=\left(\frac{2}{\pi z}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm i\omega}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}(\pm i)^{k}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{z^{k}}+R_{\ell}^{\pm}(\nu,z)\right),

with
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\omega=z-\tfrac{1}{2}\nu\pi-\tfrac{1}{4}\pi
and

\left|R_{\ell}^{\pm}(\nu,z)\right|\leq 2|a_{\ell}(\nu)|\mathcal{V}_{z,\pm i\infty}\left(t^{-\ell}\right)\exp\left(|\nu^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}|\mathcal{V}_{z,\pm i\infty}\left(t^{-1}\right)\right),

where
\mathcal{V}_{z,i\infty}\left(t^{-\ell}\right)
can be estimated in various sectors as follows

\mathcal{V}_{z,i\infty}\left(t^{-\ell}\right)\leq\begin{cases}|z|^{-\ell},&0\leq\operatorname{ph}z\leq\pi,\\
\chi(\ell)|z|^{-\ell},&\parbox[t]{224.037pt}{$-\tfrac{1}{2}\pi\leq\operatorname{ph}z\leq 0$ or
$\pi\leq\operatorname{ph}z\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\pi$,}\\
2\chi(\ell)|\Im z|^{-\ell},&\parbox[t]{224.037pt}{$-\pi<\operatorname{ph}z\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}\pi$ or
$\tfrac{3}{2}\pi\leq\operatorname{ph}z<2\pi$.}\end{cases}

Here,
\chi(\ell)
is defined by

\displaystyle\chi(x):=\pi^{1/2}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}x+1\right)/\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right).

For
\mathcal{V}_{z,-i\infty}
, use the conjugate paths and sectors.

For
z\to 0
,
|\arg z|<\pi
, we have the estimate
[
26
, 10.7.2&7]
,

(A.11)

\displaystyle{H^{(1)}_{\nu}}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle\sim\begin{cases}-(i/\pi)\Gamma\left(\nu\right)(\tfrac{1}{2}z)^{-\nu}&\text{ for }\nu>0,\\
(2i/\pi)\log z&\text{ for }\nu=0,\end{cases}

where
\log
is the principal branch of the complex logarithm.

Interplay of BRAF and TERT promoter mutations and Ki67 proliferation index in papillary thyroid carcinoma in Central Asians
Authors: Akbota Targynova, Zhanna Mussazhanova, Andrey Bychkov, Hirokazu Kurohama, Askar Tlegenov, Dauren Adilbay, Zhanar Yeleubayeva, Zaure Dushimova, Tamila Akhayeva, Hisayoshi Kondo, Masahiro Nakashima
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPapillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most prevalent subtype of thyroid carcinoma, constituting 80–85% of all cases. The clinical challenges posed by PTC are further compounded by its steadily increasing global incidence. Primarily associated with mutations in BRAF V600E and the TERT promoter (TERTp), the aggressive clinicopathological features and poor prognosis of PTC are exacerbated when these mutations co-occur. The Ki67 labeling index (LI), which reflects the proliferation rate of tumor cells, plays a pivotal role in assessing cancer aggressiveness. However, research on mutations in PTC cases in Kazakhstan is limited.MethodsThis retrospective study included 240 patients with PTC. DNA extraction and mutation screening, droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) analysis for BRAF/TERTp mutations and Ki67 immunohistochemistry were performed. Two-sided Fisher’s exact test, the Mann–Whitney U test, Kruskal–Wallis test, analysis of variance for nonparametric data were used.ResultsIn Kazakh PTC cases, BRAF V600E was common (68.3%) and aligned with the patterns observed in the Asian population. TERTp mutations co-occurring with BRAF V600E (4.7%) were associated with advanced age, larger tumor size, lymph node metastasis, higher cancer stage, and higher Ki67 LI (10%).ConclusionThis study sheds light on the prevalence of BRAF V600E and TERTp mutations in Kazakhs with PTC. The integration of Ki67 LI assessment and mutation analysis may be instrumental in predicting the aggressiveness of PTC and guiding treatment decisions in this population.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: BRAF V600E mutation, TERT promoter mutation, Papillary thyroid carcinoma, Kazakh population, Ki67 labeling index

Introduction

Well-differentiated thyroid carcinoma, encompassing papillary and follicular types, is characterized by its indolent nature and excellent prognosis, with a 5-year overall survival rate of around 95% [1, 2]. PTC accounts for 80–85% of all thyroid carcinomas [3, 4]; the classic subtype (CS-PTC) is the most prevalent, followed by the follicular subtype (FS-PTC). PTC presents a significant clinical challenge in the field of thyroid neoplasia, with a steady annual global increase in incidence [5]. Most PTC cases are associated with mutations that activate the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, including rapidly accelerated fibrosarcoma type-B (BRAF)-activating point mutations, which are exclusive to PTC among all non-high-grade differentiated thyroid cancers [6]. The correlation between BRAF V600E mutation and clinical and morphological characteristics of PTC has been well established through systematic reviews across various countries, including Kazakhstan [7]. However, there are disparate associations and equivocal conclusions in different reports [7–9].

Distinct TERT promoter (TERTp) mutations, specifically C228T and C250T, have been identified in thyroid tumors, particularly in advanced forms of this disease [10, 11]. Numerous studies have highlighted that the concurrent presence of BRAF V600E and TERTp mutations is associated with aggressive clinicopathological features and a poor prognosis [12, 13]. The frequency of TERTp mutations in patients with PTC varies among ethnic groups, ranging from 4% to 16% in different countries [12, 14–18]. Different studies from the same countries, such as Japan, have reported conflicting results [13, 19]. Additionally, TERTp mutations have occasionally been described in benign thyroid tumors, and follicular thyroid carcinoma lacks the high specificity of these mutations for PTC. This emphasizes the necessity for thorough investigation and clarification of the impact of TERTp mutations on PTC [20–23]. Understanding the clinical implications of TERTp mutations in PTC, including their prognostic significance and potential treatment implications, is crucial. Recent studies from Asian, American, and European countries have provided insights into the prevalence of BRAF V600E and TERTp mutations; this understanding of genetic alterations may prove valuable as a diagnostic and/or prognostic tool on an international scale.

The Ki67 labeling index (LI) is a well-established marker for measuring the proliferation rate (percentage of actively dividing cells) within a tumor. Endocrine pathology is used to gauge cell proliferation in various cancer types and provides valuable insights into the aggressiveness and growth potential of tumors. A higher Ki67 LI indicates increased aggressiveness and has been linked to a less favorable prognosis in patients with breast and prostate cancers [24]. A heightened Ki67 LI may necessitate more aggressive treatment approaches such as surgery, radiation therapy, or targeted therapy. However, according to the 2022 World Health Organization criteria, there is no established role for Ki67 in the diagnostic or prognostic assessment of PTC [25]. Ki67 LI is one of several factors considered when evaluating thyroid tumors; tumor size, histological subtype, and the presence of genetic mutations are also pivotal in determining the overall prognosis and treatment strategy for thyroid cancer. Recent studies reported elevated proliferation indices and TERTp mutation rates in patients with adverse outcomes [13, 26, 27], proposing that the TERTp mutation alongside Ki67 is a potentially valuable marker for assessing thyroid neoplasia prognosis.

Kazakhstan, the ninth largest country globally, lies along the Great Silk Road; it acts as a strategic bridge between Europe and Asia and contributes significantly to the cultural and economic dynamics of Central Asia. Present-day Kazakhstan is characterized by a diverse ethnic makeup with a predominant Kazakh population. Comprehensive whole-genome sequence analysis of ethnic Kazakhs revealed an elevated risk of metabolic diseases and significant genetic admixtures [28]. The environmental landscape in Kazakhstan is marked by critical issues, including radiation emanating from the Semipalatinsk nuclear testing sites, depletion of the Aral Sea, air pollution, inadequate water resources, escalating waste volumes, varying levels of iodine deficiency, concentration of uranium mining industries, and desertification of once fertile agricultural land [29–32]. Considering that these adverse factors potentially increase the risk of thyroid cancer, it is imperative to investigate this health concern in Kazakh patients.

There is a dearth of studies addressing TERTp mutations in PTC, specifically within the Kazakhstan population, with no available relevant data on this subject. To bridge this knowledge gap, we aimed to elucidate the prevalence of TERTp mutations in a Kazakh population. Additionally, we examined the correlation between molecular alterations and clinicopathological characteristics to provide valuable guidance for treatment decisions concerning Kazakh patients with PTC. This study contributes vital data to the global understanding of thyroid cancer genetics, with the potential to enhance the diagnosis and treatment of PTC in the Kazakh population.

Subjects and methods

PTC samples

We obtained 350 FFPE thyroid tumor samples from three tertiary medical centers in Kazakhstan. Specifically, thyroid tumor samples were obtained from patients who underwent surgery at the Kazakh Institute of Oncology and Radiology in Almaty, Center of Nuclear Medicine and Oncology in Semey, and Multidisciplinary Center for Oncology and Surgery in Ust-Kamenogorsk between January 2010 and December 2018. All consecutive cases of papillary thyroid carcinoma that met the inclusion criteria during this period were included in the study, without additional selection. The patients included in this study had no history of radiation exposure. Tumors were reclassified based on the 5th edition of the World Health Organization Classification of Tumors of Endocrine and Neuroendocrine Organs [25]. The reclassification process was performed collaboratively by three pathologists (M.N., H.K., and Z.M.). Informed consent was obtained from all patients, and the study protocol was approved by the ethics committees of Al-Farabi Kazakh National University in Kazakhstan and Nagasaki University in Japan. The study was conducted in strict accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

DNA extraction and mutation screening

Genomic DNA was isolated from FFPE tumor tissues using the Maxwell RSC DNA FFPE Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) following the established protocol outlined by Otsubo et al. [33]. The DNA concentration was determined using a Nanodrop 1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, NC, USA). Mutation screening was performed using molecular biology techniques as described below.

Droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) analysis for BRAF/TERTp mutations

Mutation-specific primer/probe combinations were used to identify the BRAF V600E mutation following the manufacturer’s guidelines (catalog #12001037; Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Additionally, separate primer/probe sets (dHsaEXD72405942 and dHsaEXD46675715; Bio-Rad) were used to detect C228T and C250T in TERTp as described previously [33]. The amplification reactions were conducted in a 20 µL mixture, including ddPCR Supermix (Bio-Rad), 900 nM of primers, 250 nM of probe, and 5 µL of DNA for both BRAF and TERT assays.

Droplets were generated using a QX200 AutoDG automatic droplet generator (catalog #1864002; Bio-Rad) and subsequently analyzed using a QX200 Droplet Reader (catalog #1864003; Bio-Rad). ddPCR analysis was performed using the QX200 ddPCR system, following the manufacturer’s instructions (Bio-Rad). Data analysis was performed using the QuantaSoftTM software (catalog #1864011; Bio-Rad). The negative control comprised nuclease-free water, whereas the positive control contained BRAF-mutant DNA extracted from FFPE samples, as confirmed by Sanger sequencing.

Ki67 immunohistochemistry

Tissue sections measuring 3.2 μm in thickness were subjected to Ki67 immunostaining using the primary antibody anti-Ki67 (clone MIB1, monoclonal mouse, Dako, Glostrup, Denmark). Staining was performed using a Ventana Benchmark Autostainer (Roche, Indianapolis, IN, USA). To assess Ki67 LI, we quantified both positively and negatively stained epithelial cells at ×400 magnification in the hotspot area for each case. Hotspots were identified as regions where Ki67 staining exhibited a notably higher intensity than adjacent areas. The total number of cells counted (minimum of 500 cells per case) was used to calculate the LI as the ratio of positively stained cells to the total number of cells. Staining results were categorized as < 5%, 5–10%, and > 10% positive cells. Tumor samples were independently evaluated by three experienced pathologists (M.N., H.K., and Z.M.). Cases with discrepancies were subsequently re-evaluated collaboratively to ensure the accuracy of the Ki67 LI measurements.

Statistical analysis

Statistical differences in mutation distributions were evaluated using the two-sided Fisher’s exact test. Age at diagnosis and tumor size were correlated with outcomes and mutational status using the Mann–Whitney U test, Kruskal–Wallis test, and analysis of variance for nonparametric data, as appropriate. Categorical variables such as sex, vascular invasion, LN metastasis, TNM stage, and stage at diagnosis were analyzed in relation to outcomes and mutational status using the chi-squared test. For multiple comparisons, a non-parametric one-way analysis of variance with Tukey’s test was used. Statistical analyses were conducted using the JMP Pro version 17 (IBM, Cary, NC, USA) and SAS University Edition (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed p-value < 0.05.

Results

Clinicopathologic features of PTC

Of the 350 samples collected, 245 were included in the analysis; the remainder were excluded because of significant DNA degradation in the formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples. Patients undergoing surgery ranged in age from 15 to 79 years, with a mean age of 46.7 ± 15.3 and mean PTC size of 19.3 ± 11.5 mm. CS-PTC was the predominant histological subtype, accounting for 93.4% of all cases (Table 1). All 11 patients with FS-PTC had non-encapsulated tumors. Mutational status of BRAF V600E and TERTp C228T/C250T

DNA screening was successfully performed on 240 samples for BRAF V600E mutations and 229 samples for TERTp mutations. The BRAF V600E mutation was prevalent, found in 68.3% of samples, and was significantly higher in females (82.9%) than in males (p < 0.001); in contrast, TERTp mutations were less frequent, identified in only 6.5%. TERTp C228T was more common (5.2%) than TERTp C250T (1.3%), and the two mutations were mutually exclusive. Co-mutation of BRAF and TERTp C228T/C250T occurred in 4.7% of samples (Fig. 1).

Histologically, all three cases exhibited classical PTC features on H&E staining. The Ki67 labeling indices were < 5%, 10%, and 16%, respectively. Association between clinicopathologic features and mutational status

Based on the type of mutation, PTC cases were categorized into four groups: mutation-negative (lacking BRAF/TERTp mutations), BRAF V600E alone, TERTp mutation alone, and coexistence of both mutations. We examined the association between these groups and clinicopathological factors (Table 2). The TERTp mutation alone and co-mutation groups exhibited a higher prevalence of male sex, advanced stage, and nodal metastasis than the mutation-negative and BRAF V600E alone groups. However, these differences were not statistically significant, likely due to the limited sample size.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the Ki67 based on mutation status

ROC curves were used to determine the optimal cutoff value for Ki67 expression within distinct mutation status groups in PTC (Fig. 2). The optimal cutoff values of Ki67 LI for predicting the mutation status in PTC were 4% for BRAF V600E alone, 5% for TERTp mutation alone, and 10% for the co-mutation group; the corresponding areas under the curves (AUCs) were 0.61, 0.71, and 0.82, respectively. Using these cut-off values and considering mutation status, patients were categorized into two groups (Ki67 LI < 5% or ≥ 5%). The analysis demonstrated a significant association between LN metastasis (p = 0.01) and tumor stage (p = 0.01).

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for Ki67 based on BRAF V600E mutation, TERT promoter mutation status alone, and combined BRAF V600E and TERT promoter mutation status revealed optimal cut-off values of 4% (AUC = 0.610), 5% (AUC = 0.714), and 10% (AUC = 0.82), respectively

Discussion

This study simultaneously demonstrated the prevalence of TERTp and BRAF V600E mutation among individuals of Kazakh ethnicity, finding that the prevalence of BRAF V600E, TERTp, and BRAF/TERTp C228T co-mutations in PTC were 68.3%, 6.5%, and 4.7%, respectively. Statistical analysis demonstrated significant associations between these mutations and older age, larger tumor size, LN metastasis, higher cancer stage, and higher Ki67 LI.

BRAF V600E is the most prevalent mutation driving PTC and predominant type of thyroid cancer. This mutation triggers the activation of MAPK/ERK signaling, influencing uncontrolled growth and contributing to PTC development and progression. The presence of BRAF V600E was frequent in PTC cases among individuals of Kazakh ethnicity (68.3%), which is consistent with findings in adult Asian patients [7, 34]. In our study, we observed a notably high occurrence of BRAF V600E mutation in females (82.9%, p < 0.00); this aligns with the general trend observed in most studies [19, 35, 36] but contradicts a previous study in Kazakhstan, possibly due to ethnic group differences [37]. In the present study, we did not identify a significant association between the BRAF V600E mutation and aggressive clinicopathological features. The absence of an association may be attributable to the prevalence of mutations in the Kazakh population. Differences in diagnostic and surgical strategies between Eastern and Western countries may partly explain variations in reported BRAF mutation rates. In many Asian centers, surgical indications after fine-needle aspiration (FNA) are relatively conservative, and indeterminate or suspicious nodules (TBS III–V) are often managed by active surveillance. Therefore, surgical cases usually represent clearly malignant, BRAF-like papillary carcinomas. In contrast, Western institutions tend to operate on a broader spectrum of thyroid tumors, including non–BRAF-like cases. This selection bias may contribute to the higher BRAF mutation frequency observed in our cohort [38].

The causes of geographic heterogeneity in previous studies were found to be multifactorial, including genetic predisposition, detection techniques, hormonal influence, and dietary components such as iodine [7, 39–42]. Iodine deficiency has long been linked to BRAF mutations and an elevated risk of thyroid disorders, including thyroid cancer. A systematic review indicated that both low and excessive iodine intake significantly contributes to the occurrence of BRAF mutations in the thyroid, making them potential risk factors for PTC development [43, 44]. In iodine-deficient areas such as Kazakhstan, government initiatives aimed at addressing iodine deficiency through salt iodization programs may also affect thyroid cancer rates.

We searched for data on PTC and well-differentiated thyroid cancer in Central Asia, focusing on Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan. Central Asia, which has a population exceeding 75 million, has limited research on thyroid cancer. Between 2009 and 2018, 5,559 new thyroid cancer cases were reported in Kazakhstan, with an average age-standardized incidence rate of 3.3 per 100,000 people [45]. However, similar detailed data are not available for neighboring countries such as Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan. This lack of data likely stems from limited cancer registries, an underdeveloped research infrastructure, and fewer advanced diagnostic resources in these regions. This highlights the urgent need for further investigations into its prevalence and impact.

The co-occurrence of BRAF/TERTp mutations in PTC has been linked to poorer clinical outcomes compared to the presence of single mutation, such as increased tumor aggressiveness and higher recurrence rates [46, 47]. This association has been consistently observed in recent studies of various cancer types [48, 49]. Among Kazakh PTC cases, those with TERTp mutations alone and those with co-mutations exhibited a higher prevalence of male sex, advanced stage, and LN involvement. All patients with TERTp mutations had tumors exceeding 10 mm. No significant size differences were found between the mutation-negative and co-mutation groups, whereas the TERTp mutation alone was associated with significantly larger tumors compared to the BRAF V600E group. Therefore, fusion-driven cancers, typically observed in younger patients and often lacking BRAF and TERT mutations, may exhibit distinct clinical behaviors. RAS mutations could be another potential driver, especially in tumors that display a follicular pattern. Consequently, the simultaneous analysis of BRAF mutations along with established clinicopathological risk factors, in conjunction with the assessment of TERTp mutations, could guide strategies for early intervention and targeted prevention of aggressive cancer development, irrespective of geographic variations in prevalence.

In our study, TERTp mutations were identified in 6.7% of patients under 60 years and in 11.5% of patients aged ≥ 60 years, with no significant difference. Prior research has established that TERTp mutations are strongly associated with aggressive tumor behavior and poor outcomes, particularly in older patients [10–12]. However, the presence of TERTp mutations in younger patients highlights the need to investigate the additional genetic or environmental factors that contribute to early-onset thyroid cancer. The lack of statistical significance may reflect sample size limitations, emphasizing the need for larger studies to clarify the clinical implications of TERTp mutations across different age groups.

Ki67 LI serves as a crucial measure in cancer cases, including thyroid cancer, and provides insights into the proliferation and growth rates of tumor cells as its expression is often linked to tumor aggressiveness [13, 49–51]. In terms of PTC, Matsuse et al. demonstrated that the hazard ratio for recurrence with Ki67 LIs ≥ 10% remained significant even after adjusting for various parameters, with a greater effect size than that for BRAF/TERTp mutations [13]. Other authors have also reported that Ki67 LIs of ≥ 10% predict worse outcomes in PTC [52]. In Kazakh patients, Ki67 LI was significantly increased in patients with BRAF/TERTp mutations and advanced-stage cases. This suggests that this specific marker combination has significant potential as a prognostic indicator associated with an elevated risk of tumor progression. The ROC curve analysis based on the mutation groups demonstrated that Ki67 LI ≥ 10% would be a potential test for predicting co-mutations in the series under analysis (AUC = 0.82). Previous studies have commonly used a 5% Ki67 index as the cutoff to define aggressiveness in well-differentiated thyroid carcinomas. However, based on the unique characteristics of our cohort, our analysis identified a cutoff value of 10%. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in the study populations, tumor biology, or methodologies used for Ki67 quantification. Our findings are consistent with those of other authors [13] who also demonstrated the prognostic value of the Ki67 index in PTC. The combination of our results and existing literature reinforces the practical utility of Ki67 as a marker, although further studies are warranted to clarify the optimal cutoff for different patient groups. Overall, the findings imply that Ki67 expression levels may serve as valuable predictors of PTC stage across different mutation groups. These findings are consistent with those of previous studies [12], suggesting an association with more aggressive clinicopathological characteristics of PTC.

Although the 2015 ATA guidelines recommend molecular markers, such as BRAF and TERT mutations, for the prognostication and management of PTC [37], we are currently studying these markers specifically in the Kazakh population to understand their prevalence and impact on this unique demographic. Considering the potential regional differences in genetic and environmental factors, our study aimed to refine and tailor these prognostic tools to better suit the characteristics of PTC in Kazakhstan. We examined the prevalence of TERTp, TERT C228T/C250T, and BRAF V600E mutations in Kazakhs diagnosed with PTC. Our findings revealed that TERTp mutations were identified in 6.5% of PTC cases, whereas co-mutations occurred in 4.7% of cases. These mutations were significantly linked to various adverse clinicopathological factors, including older age, larger tumor size, LN metastasis, advanced cancer stage, and elevated Ki67 LI. Higher Ki67 LI values signified more aggressive cancers, and our study demonstrated an association between Ki67 LI > 10% and advanced-stage cancer, particularly in cases with co-mutations.

This is the first study to report data on the association between Ki-67 LI and the presence of either TERTp or BRAF/TERTp mutations in PTC within a Central Asian cohort.

Our study elucidates crucial genetic and clinical aspects of PTC in the Kazakh population. However, it is imperative to acknowledge certain limitations such as limited follow-up duration and incomplete patient data. To enhance the global understanding of thyroid cancer, further studies with extended follow-up periods and comprehensive patient data are required. In conclusion, a combination of Ki67 LI assessment and BRAF/TERTp mutation analysis may emerge as a marker for predicting PTC aggressiveness in the Kazakh population.

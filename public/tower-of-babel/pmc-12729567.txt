Alkaloids isolated from Scutellaria Barbata D. Don trigger apoptosis and inhibit migration by modulating the p38-p53 pathway in ovarian cancer
Authors: Bingqing Gao, Xue Sui, HyokChol Choe, Yutong Yang, Yi Liu, Lunyue Xia, Peiyu Li, Yingting Chen, Yuexue Huo, Zhihao Zhao, Kaiyue Ding, Junnan Ma, Danping Zhao, Lin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOvarian cancer is a widely prevalent gynecological malignancy associated with significant morbidity and mortality. Scutellaria barbata D. Don (SB), a traditional Chinese medicine, has been reported to have diverse biological effects, including anti-ovarian cancer effects. However, the specific effects and underlying mechanism of the alkaloid fraction, the active fraction isolated from SB, in ovarian cancer remain unclear. This study aimed to explore the anti-tumor effects and underlying mechanisms of the alkaloid fraction of Scutellaria barbata D. Don (SBA) in ovarian cancer.MethodsSBA was extracted by alcohol solvent extraction, and its chemical composition was analyzed using chromogenic reaction and high-pressure liquid chromatography-quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (HPLC/Q-TOF-MS). The effects of SBA on the proliferation, migration and apoptosis of human ovarian cancer cells (SKOV3) were detected by CCK-8 assay, clone formation assay, scratch assay, transwell assay, flow cytometry, Hoechst 33,342 staining and Western blot. SKOV3 xenograft nude mouse model was established to further investigate the in vivo effects of SBA. Serum and tissue samples were collected for molecular-biological indicators, and pathological changes were evaluated by hematoxylin-eosin, immunohistochemistry, and Western blot.ResultsA total of 38 nitrogen-containing compounds were detected in SBA. SBA inhibited the proliferation and migration of SKOV3 cells in a dose-dependent manner, in addition to inducing significant cell apoptosis. In the SKOV3 xenograft model, SBA attenuated ovarian cancer tumor growth with no significant organ toxicity. The anti-tumor effects of SBA were associated with downregulation of Bcl-2, N-cadherin, MMP2, MMP9, and upregulation of Bax, cleaved caspase-3/ caspase-3, cleaved caspase-9, E-cadherin, P-p38/ p38, and P-p53/ p53 both in vitro and in vivo. Additionally, SBA exhibited synergy with cisplatin (DDP) and alleviated cisplatin-induced renal damage.ConclusionSBA suppresses ovarian cancer by triggering apoptosis and inhibiting cell migration via the p38-p53 pathway. Combining SBA with DDP reduces drug toxicity and potentiates the anti-tumor effects. Therefore, SBA is a promising candidate for treating ovarian cancer, and mitigating the toxic side effects of DDP in ovarian cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01840-x.Keywords: Alkaloid fraction of Scutellaria Barbata D. don, Ovarian cancer, Cisplatin, Apoptosis, Migration, P38/p53

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OC) is a malignancy of the female reproductive system, which is the leading cause of gynecological cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. The latest global cancer statistics have revealed that the mortality rate of OC was greater than 50% in 2022 [2]. Surgery and platinum-based chemotherapy are the mainstay of OC treatment; however, the mortality rate of OC remains high and the majority of patients relapse. Furthermore, treatment is associated with drug resistance and toxicity [3]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify more effective and safe therapeutic agents.

It has been reported that more than 60% of the approved anti-tumor drugs or candidates are derived the natural sources [4], including apoptosis inducers, topoisomerase inhibitors, telomerase inhibitors, microtubule inhibitors, immunomodulators, tumor microenvironment regulators, multidrug resistance reversal agents, and antiangiogenic and antimetastatic agents [5]. Among them, plant alkaloids, such as camptothecin, vincristine, paclitaxel, berberine, matrosin, colchicine, piperine have been favored due to their well-documented inhibition of syngeneic or xenograft tumor growth, because of their effects on numerous underlying signaling processes in cancer cells, including induction of cell cycle arrest, intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis, autophagy, inhibition of angiogenesis and glycolysis, stress and anti-inflammatory responses, regulation of immune functions, cellular differentiation, and inhibition of invasion and metastasis [6]. Paclitaxel and camptothecin represent significant therapeutic advances in the treatment of OC [7]; however, new anti-cancer agents are still required to overcome the major clinical challenge of resistance to chemotherapy.

Chinese herbal medicines derived from natural sources have gained increasing prominence in the treatment and prevention of OC, including alleviation of clinical symptoms, reduction of toxic effects of chemoradiotherapy, and improvement of the patients’ quality of life, because of their multitarget, broad-spectrum characteristics and low toxicity [8, 9]. Scutellaria barbata D. Don (SB, also named Ban Zhi Lian in China) is a commonly used Chinese herbal medicine, which has been reported to have the effects of clearing heat and detoxifying, resolving stasis, and promoting urination [10]. The herb pair SB and Scleromitrion diffusum (Wild.) R. J. Wang (Bai Hua She She Cao) has been reported to have anti-cancer activity against liver cancer, OC, colorectal cancer, breast cancer, and prostate cancer [11, 12]. Previously, we explored the anti-cancer effects of SB and Hedyotis diffusa Willd (SB-HD) in OC by network pharmacology, molecular docking, and in vitro experimental verification. Our results showed that SB could inhibit proliferation and induce apoptosis in A2780 cells through the mitochondrial pathway, and down-regulation of MMP-2/9 expression affects the migration of OC cells. SB-HD was found to inhibit OC through the focal adhesion pathway [13, 14]. Previous studies have identified approximately 300 compounds in SB, including flavonoids, polysaccharides, volatile compounds, trace elements, and diterpenoids, which have shown anti-tumor, antibacterial, antiviral and immunomodulation effects [11, 15]. However, there are few studies on the mechanism underlying the effect of the alkaloid (SBA) fraction of Scutellaria barbata D. Don in OC.

This study aimed to investigate the effect of SBA on OC cells and xenograft nude mouse model, in addition to exploring the potential underlying mechanisms. These results could provide new insights for improving the treatment of OC.

Materials and methods

Reagents and antibodies

Human ovarian cancer (SKOV3) cells were obtained from Wuhan Pricella Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). McCoy’s 5 A medium (modified) was supplied by Dalian Meilun Biotech Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). Fetal bovine serum (FBS) and penicillin-streptomycin were purchased from Gibco Corporation (Grand Island, NY, USA). Trypsin-EDTA (0.25%) was purchased from Dalian Meilun Biotech Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). Cisplatin (DDP) and super sensitive ECL luminescence reagent were obtained from Dalian Meilun Biotech Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, #67–68-5) was purchased from Solarbio, Beijing, China. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, #K1018) was supplied by APExBIO. Additionally, 24-well transwell chamber system with 8.0 μm pore size was obtained from Corning, USA. Phosphate buffer solution (PBS) and Annexin V-EGFP/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit were obtained from KeyGen Biotech (Jiangsu, China). BUN Assay Kit (#C013-2-1), Cre Assay Kit (#C011-2-1), AST Assay Kit (#C010-2-1), and ALT Assay Kit (#C009-2-1) were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). Antibodies against p38 (ET1604-45) and N-Cadherin (ET1607-37) were purchased from HuaBio (Hangzhou, China). Antibodies against cleaved caspase-3 (ab32042) and MMP9 (ab76003) were purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA). Antibodies against E-Cadherin (AF0131) were purchased from Affinity Biosciences (Jiangsu, China). Antibodies against P-p38 (AP0526), Bcl-2 (A21873), cleaved caspase-9 (A22672), P-p53 (AP0860), p53 (A18803), goat anti-mouse IgG (AS003), and goat anti-rabbit IgG (AS014) were purchased from ABclonal (Wuhan, China). Antibodies against BAX (50599-2-Ig), MMP2 (10373-2-AP), and caspase 3 (19677-1-AP) were purchased from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). β-Actin (I102) polyclonal antibody (AP0060) was purchased from Bioworld Technology (Bloomington, MN, USA). Rotavapor (EYELA, Shanghai) was used for the distillation and evaporation of solutions. A UV-visible spectrophotometer (UV-1800PC, MAPADA, Shanghai) was used for absorbance.

Preparation of drugs

We obtained 30 g of dried Sempervirens, added four times the dose of ethanol (concentration 55%) for maceration and boiling (2 h each), followed by concentration and purification using a rotary evaporator. Dynamically adjusting the PH of the hemicranium concentrate, extracting twice and combining, and finally drying using a lyophilizer yielded 0.198 g of the extract, which was designated as SBA.

Ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight Mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS)

Chromatographic separation was carried out with an Agilent 1,290 Infinity LC system (Agilent, United States) using an Agilent Zorbax Eclipse Plus C18 column (100 × 2.1 mm i.d., 3.5 μm) at 40 °C with a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The mobile phase consisted of water containing 0.1% formic acid (solvent system A) and acetonitrile (solvent system B), and the gradient elution program was as follows: 0–5 min, 3–10% B; 5–13 min, 10–18% B; 13–20 min, 18–25% B; 20–35 min, 25–70% B; 35–40 min, 70–99% B; 40–42 min, 99–3% B; and 42–45 min, 3% B; 2 µl of sample solution was injected for the analysis.

Mass detection was performed using an Agilent 6530b Accurate-Mass Quadrupole Time-of-Flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometer (Agilent Corp., United States) equipped with a Dual AJS ESI source operating in both positive and negative modes with the following operating parameters: drying gas (N2) flow rate, 10.0 L/min; drying gas (N2) temperature, 350 °C; nebulizer, 35 psig; sheath gas (N2) temperature, 400 °C; fragmentor voltage, 120 V; skimmer voltage, 65 V; and Octopole RF, 750 V. The capillary voltage was set as 4 kV and − 3.5 kV for the positive and negative modes, respectively, while the nozzle voltage was set as + 500 V and − 1000 V, respectively. The quasimolecular ions [M − H]− and [M + H]+ were selected as the precursor ions and subjected to target-MS/MS analyses with different collision energies ranging from 10 to 60 V to acquire sufficient product ions. MS spectra were recorded over the m/z range of 50–1,100 [12]. All data were processed by MassHunter workstation software version B.06.00 (Agilent Technologies, Germany).

Cell culture

The cells were cultured in McCoy’s 5 A Medium (modified) containing 10% FBS and 100 U/mL of penicillin-streptomycin solution in a cell incubator (Thermo) at 37 °C in 5% CO2. Cell experiment groups were as follows (except for the clone formation assay):

(1)control group: equal volume of blank culture solution; (2)SBA low-dose (SBA-L) group: SBA = 10 mg/mL; (3)SBA high-dose (SBA-H) group: SBA = 20 mg/mL; (4)cisplatin (DDP) group: 2 µg/mL; (5)cisplatin + SBA low-dose (DDP + SBA-L) group: DDP = 2 µg/mL, SBA = 10 mg/mL; (6) cisplatin + SBA high-dose (DDP + SBA-H) group: DDP = 2 µg/mL, SBA = 20 mg/mL.

SBA was firstly sonicated with 0.1% DMSO to aid solubilisation (Figure S1), followed by dilution with McCoy’s 5 A medium to the working concentration. The extract was then filtered and sterilised for dispensing by 0.22 μm sterile filtration membranes, after which it was stored at −80℃ for future use.

Cell viability assay

SKOV3 cells were digested with trypsin in the previous stage and after logarithmic growth of cells, the cells were treated with different doses of drugs, incubated for different time periods and then added to CCK-8 solution protected from light, followed by measurement of absorbance at 450 nm. The calculation of the suppression ratio is as follows:

Asample: Absorbance in dosing group (with cells, medium, CCK-8 solution and drug solution).

Acontrol: Absorbance in control group (with cells, medium, CCK-8 solution, without drug).

Ablank: Absorbance in blank group (with medium, CCK-8 solution, without cells, drug).

Colony formation assay

SKOV3 cells were digested with trypsin in the previous period, and the cells were treated with different doses of drugs, and after incubation for 24 h, replaced with fresh medium to continue incubation, and 1 mL of methanol was added to each well, followed by fixation and crystal violet staining, and the number of colonies and their distribution were observed under the microscope. Cell experiment groups were as follows:

(1) control group: equal volume of blank culture solution; (2)SBA low-dose (SBA-L) group: SBA = 1 mg/mL; (3)SBA high-dose (SBA-H) group: SBA = 2 mg/mL; (4)cisplatin (DDP) group: DDP = 1 µg/mL; (5) cisplatin + SBA low-dose (DDP + SBA-L) group: DDP = 1 µg/mL, SBA = 1 mg/mL; (6) cisplatin + SBA high-dose (DDP + SBA-H) group: DDP = 1 µg/mL, SBA = 2 mg/mL.

Wound-healing assay

Pre-trypsin digestion of SKOV3 cells, pipette tip scratches, formation of equal area sized wounds, subsequent addition of different doses of drugs, microscopic observation of scratch healing at different time periods as well as at different concentrations, photographed under the microscope, and the images were analyzed by Image J.

Transwell migration assay

Serum-free medium containing cell suspension was placed in the upper chamber of transwell, incubated for 6 h and then different doses of drugs were added, the serum-free medium was replaced with complete medium in the lower chamber, fixed and stained using methanol, photographed under the microscope, and the images were analyzed by Image J.

Annexin V-FITC/PI staining

SKOV3 cells were digested by trypsin in the previous stage, incubated for 24 h, added with different doses of drugs for 24 h, washed with PBS solution, and followed up according to the content of the manual of Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit, and apoptosis was detected by flow cytometry and analyzed with FlowJo v10.8.1 software.

Hoechst 33342 staining

SKOV3 cells were digested by trypsin in the previous stage, and different doses of drugs were added after 24 h of incubation, and the apoptosis was observed under fluorescence microscope according to the instructions of Hoechst 33342 kit for subsequent operations.

Animal experiments

Female BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd (animal quality certificate: 110011231105222946). All animals were maintained at 22 ± 2 °C at a relative humidity of 50–60% and 12 h light/12 h dark cycle. The mice received the standard mice diet and water ad libitum. They were kept in the SFP Animal Experimental Center of Dalian Medical University. To establish a transplantable tumor model, SKOV3 cells in PBS (1 × 107 cells/mL) were subcutaneously injected into the right axilla of BALB/c mice (100 µl/mouse). When the tumor had grown to a volume of 100–200 mm3, the mice were grouped according to the random number table method (n = 6 in each group): (1) control group, (2) model group, (3) SBA low-dose (SBA-L) group, (4) SBA high-dose (SBA-H) group, (5) cisplatin (DDP) group, and (6) cisplatin + SBA low-dose (DDP + SBA-L) group. The control group and model group received oral corn oil daily and an intraperitoneal injection of normal saline once every 3 days in a volume of 100 µl. The DDP group and DDP + SBA-L groups were administered intraperitoneal injections of cisplatin once every 3 days in a volume of 100 µl. The SBA-L and DDP + SBA-L groups received daily oral SBA daily at a dose of 25 mg/kg, while the SBA-H group received 50 mg/kg per day. The tumor size and body weight of mice were measured every three days. Tumor volume was calculated as 0.5×length×width2. Tumour measurements were carried out by researchers who were not privy to the group allocation. After 21 days, all mice were sacrificed. Blood was collected for biomedical measurement and all tumor tissues were weighed. All experiments were conducted after approval was obtained from the Dalian Medical University Animal Ethics Committee.

Measurement of serum biochemical parameters

Serum levels of blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Cre), aspartate transaminase (AST), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) were measured according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Histopathological observation

Tumor tissues were pre-sampled and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for one week, followed by paraffin embedding, sectioning, section thickness of 5 μm, varying degrees of alcohol dehydration and staining according to the instructions of the Hematoxylin-Eosin kit, and finally xylene transparency, dropping of resin adhesive to seal the slices, and photographing under the microscope.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The steps of pre-sectioning were the same as Hematoxylin-Eosin staining, dropwise addition of Ki67 and BCL2 antibody, incubation at 4℃ for 12 h, rewarming dropwise addition of secondary antibody, DAB solution was prepared on site for staining, the degree of staining was observed under the microscope, and Image J was used for image analysis.

Western blot

Total proteins of tumor tissues and SKOV3 cells were extracted from the pre-lysate, and the BAC protein kit was used for concentration determination, closure, electrophoresis, transmembrane, and subsequent determination of protein expression by the ECL method, and imaging using the Tanon-5200 imaging system. Protein expression levels shown in the graph are the ratio of the target protein gray value to the beta-actin internal reference protein gray value or the ratio shown is the ratio of the phosphorylated protein gray value of the protein to its total protein gray value.

Statistical analysis

All data of more than three independent experiments are presented as means ± SD. Unpaired student’s t-test was used for comparing two experimental groups and one-way ANOVA test was used for comparing multiple groups. A two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

The chemical profile of SBA was analyzed by ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry. The tested samples contained 66 compounds, and 38 nitrogen-containing compounds were identified by HPLC-MS in SBA (Fig.1C and Table S1). All the chemical entities were detected in both negative and positive ionization modes. SKOV3 cells were treated with a serial concentration of SBA for 12, 24, and 48 h. The viability of SKOV3 cells was measured by the CCK-8 assay. The IC50 of SBA was 30.39 mg/mL, 21.33 mg/mL, and 16.43 mg/mL at 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h, respectively (Fig. 2A-B). The decrease in IC50 was considered to be due to the accumulation of time-dependent effects. Based on the results of the time gradient experiment in Fig. 2B, 24 h was selected as the standard time point for assay. SBA and DDP inhibited SKOV3 cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner. (Fig. 2C), and also effectively inhibited SKOV3 cells with significantly reduced colony formation (Fig. 2D-E). The inhibitory effect on SKOV3 cells was synergistic with DDP in combination with SBA at concentrations of 10 mg/mL to 20 mg/mL (Fig. 2F).

SBA can inhibit the migration of OC cells

Wound-healing and transwell assays were then performed to determine the migration abilities of SKOV3 cells treated with SBA or/and DDP. Both low and high doses of SBA inhibited the lateral migration of SKOV3 cells, and high-dose SBA had a stronger effect than low-dose SBA. Compared with the control group, DDP also inhibited lateral cell migration. Compared with the SBA and cisplatin alone groups, SBA combined with DDP could more effectively inhibit the lateral migration of SKOV3 cells (Fig. 4A-B), and the inhibitory effect became more obvious with the extension of drug administration time. After 24 h of administration, compared with the control group, there was no significant difference between the DDP and SBA groups, but the combination of cisplatin and SBA significantly inhibited the longitudinal migration of SKOV3 cells (Fig. 4C-D). Furthermore, the high-dose combination group had a better inhibitory effect than the low-dose combination group. The expression of E-cadherin and N-cadherin proteins, which are involved in migration, was increased, while the expression of MMP2 and MMP9 was reduced (Fig. 4E-F). And the expression levels of pathway-associated proteins p38/p38 and P-p53/p53 were elevated significantly in the SBA combined with DDP group (Fig. 4G-H).

There was no statistically significant difference in the weight of nude mice in each group (Fig. 5E). HE staining showed that tumor cells in nude mice of the model group were loosely arranged, with large interstitial, sheet-like necrosis or ablation, and the two drugs alone and in combination inhibited the growth of tumor tissues (Fig. 5F). The expression levels of cell proliferation marker Ki67 and apoptosis-related protein Bcl-2 were down-regulated (Fig. 5F, G-H). The expression of N-cadherin, MMP2 and MMP9 proteins, which are involved in migration, was reduced (Fig. 6C-D). Effects of SBA on organs and liver and kidney functions in nude mice

Nude mice treated with SBA alone or in combination with DDP showed no obvious damage to the heart, liver, spleen and lungs at the organic level (Fig. 7A), and there was no statistically significant difference in AST and ALT levels between groups, suggesting that the drug did not impair liver function in nude mice (Fig. 7C). However, the renal tubular and glomerular epithelial cells of mice in the DDP group showed vacuolization (Fig. 7B), and the serum levels of BUN and Cre were significantly elevated (Fig. 7C), whereas the combination with SBA ameliorated the tissue damage caused by DDP (Fig. 7B), and reduced the elevation of BUN and Cre levels and attenuated DDP-induced renal injury (Fig. 7C).

Discussion

Ovarian cancer (OC) is one of the most common malignant tumors of the female reproductive system, which is associated with poor prognosis and high mortality [16]. Although platinum-based therapy and surgery have been used for decades, the occurrence of side effects, drug resistance, and other factors still pose major clinical challenges. Therefore, additional compounds with novel mechanisms of action need to be identified to increase the available therapeutic options for OC. In this regard, the advantages of Chinese herbal medicine are being gradually revealed.

Chinese herbal medicine is an important part of traditional Chinese medicine and is currently one of the fastest growing alternative medical therapies for cancer treatment in China [17]. A variety of alkaloids extracted from medicinal plants and herbs have demonstrated anti-tumor effects in several types of cancer in vitro and in vivo. Paclitaxel, camptothecin, vinblastine, vinorelbine, vincristine, and vindesine have been successfully developed as anti-cancer drugs, which have shown effectiveness in OC, uterine cancer, and breast cancer [7, 18]. As a commonly used Chinese herbal medicine, Scutellaria barbata D. Don (SB) has demonstrated certain therapeutic advantages in the treatment of OC. Previous studies have shown that the extract of SB, either alone or in combination with Hedyotis diffusa Willd, can inhibit the proliferation, migration and induce apoptosis of ovarian cancer cells through multiple pathways, including the focal adhesion pathway [14], mitochondrial pathway [13], PI3K/Akt signaling pathway [19], ferroptosis pathway [20] and NRF2-FTH1 autophagic degradation pathway [21]. Despite these findings, the specific anti-ovarian cancer components and mechanisms of SB still require further investigation.

Here, we elucidate a distinct mechanism underlying the anticancer effects of Scutellaria barbata alkaloids (SBA), specifically the activation of the p38-p53 apoptotic pathway. This finding complements and extends our understanding of the previously reported therapeutic mechanisms of SB in ovarian cancer treatment. SBA was identified by high-performance LC-MS, known for its highly sensitive and selective method [22]. Modified bismuth potassium iodide reagent and iodine-potassium iodide reagent were used as alkaloid color reagents. 38 nitrogen compounds were found in the extract, including Scutebarbatine A, which has been reported to have a variety of anti-tumor, antibacterial, and anti-inflammatory effects, suggesting that these compounds may be closely related to the anti-cancer effects of OC.

Apoptosis is the process by which cells enter into death [23]. The process of apoptosis is regulated by the Bcl-2 gene family, includes Bax and Bak which promote apoptosis, and Bcl-2, Bcl-xl, Mcl1 and Bcl-B, which inhibit apoptotic signaling [24]. Meanwhile, Bc1-2 interacts with Bax to activate or inhibit Bcl-2, while Bcl-2 inhibits Bax. Both of them form ion channels on the mitochondria, reducing mitochondrial membrane permeability and causing caspase reaction to alter mitochondrial permeability, inhibit mitochondrial protein release, and ultimately leading to apoptosis [25]. The ratio of Bcl-2/Bax is a close factor in apoptosis [26]. Research indicate that the diterpene alkaloid Brunonianine D extracted from Delphinium brunonianum can significantly promote the apoptosis of Skov-3 cells through the Bax/Bcl-2/Caspase-3 pathway [27]. Meanwhile, the kidney toxicity of DDP in the kidney cell apoptosis has long been a critical factor restricting its clinical application. Studies have demonstrated that DDP can trigger the activation of Bax, thereby promoting the release of cytochrome C. This event subsequently activates the caspase-9/caspase-3 cascade, which in turn drives cells toward apoptosis [28]. In this study, we found that SBA can increase the expression of cleaved caspase-3/caspase-3, cleaved caspase-9, and Bax, while decreasing the expression of Bcl-2. thus decreasing the Bcl-2/Bax ratio. These findings indicate that SBA-induced cell death may be regulated by caspase-dependent apoptosis and the Bcl-2/Bax pathway. Interestingly, when SBA is combined with DDP, only the expression of cleaved caspase-9 is increased, whereas the expression of cleaved caspase-3/caspase-3 does not increase.

Based on the comprehensive experimental results, particularly the change in IC50 over time as shown in Fig. 3B, we propose that SBA may exhibit a delayed onset of action. This delay could be associated with the rate at which SBA activates intracellular signaling pathways. During apoptosis, the activation of the caspase family occurs in a cascade manner, with cleaved caspase-9, an upstream caspase, being activated prior to cleaved caspase-3. The accumulation of cleaved caspase-3 may require additional time [29]. we selected 24 h as the experimental observation time point in our in vitro study, based on prior experiments and literature. Consequently, the lack of significant changes in cleaved caspase-3 at this time point may reflect this temporal limitation. Furthermore, integrating our in vivo data, we postulate that the anti-ovarian cancer efficacy of the SBA and DDP combination may be related to other regulatory pathways, such as the regulation of ROS in mitochondria. This is consistent with previous reports highlighting the crucial role of ROS in SB’s anti-ovarian cancer effects [20]– [21]. The higher Hoechst staining intensity in the SBA + DDP group compared to DDP alone further implies involvement of the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway. Additionally, SBA and DDP combination may also offer a novel strategy for mitigating DDP-induced nephrotoxicity through its modulatory effects on caspase proteins.

The p38 is a major mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), P38 MAPK signaling pathway mediates extracellular signals to intracellular signals, and is one of the most important signal transduction systems that can regulate cell proliferation, migration, differentiation, apoptosis, matrix deposition, inflammation, apoptosis, and various pathophysiological processes [30]– [31]. P38 can activate the tumor suppressor protein p53 and regulate its phosphorylation [32]. Activated p53 upregulates genes that have been implicated in cell apoptosis, including Bcl-2 and Bax, and leads to the apoptosis via intrinsic, mitochondrial pathway [33]– [34]. Existing studies have reported that the p38-p53 pathway plays an important role in drug-promoted apoptosis of ovarian cancer cells, as well as in early/advanced-stage ovarian clear cell carcinomas [35]– [36]. Our study demonstrated that the combination of SBA and DDP significantly increased the protein expression of P-p38/p38 and P-p53/p53. Combined with the expression results of apoptotic proteins such as Bcl-2 and Bax, we propose that the p38-p53-mediated apoptotic signaling pathway may play a significant regulatory role in the anti-ovarian cancer effect exerted by the combination of SBA and DDP.

Similar to the treatment of other malignancies, effective ovarian cancer treatment requires not only inducing tumor cell apoptosis but also suppressing cancer cell migration. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a normal physiological process by which epithelial cells lose their relatively strong adhesiveness, become more motile and assuming a spindle-shaped morphology that resembles that of cells of mesenchymal origin [37]. EMT facilitates migration and invasion of epithelial tumor cells. EMT is characterized by loss of E-cadherin, b-catenin relocalization, and acquisition of elongated cell shape and ability to invade the extracellular matrix (ECM) [38]. The cell-adhesion molecule E-cadherin plays a key role in the establishment and maintenance of epithelial tissue structure, and its downregulation is important in the first step of metastasis [39]. Matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)−2 and MMP-9 are major proteolytic enzymes that contribute to ECM degradation and are involved in the process of releasing vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) to promote tumor cell migration and invasion. Meanwhile, during the development and progression of tumors, p53 may play a certain regulatory role in MMP2 and MMP9 [40]– [41]. In ovarian cancer, the upregulation of MMP-2 and MMP-9 is mediated by Nectin-3, a cell adhesion molecule [42]. Studies have demonstrated that downregulation of MMP2/9 expression can inhibit the migration of ovarian cancer cells [43]– [44]. In our study, after SBA treatment, the protein expression of E-cadherin increased, while that of N-cadherin, MMP2 and MMP9 decreased. The effect was more significant in the combination group with DDP, suggesting that SBA may have anti-cancer effects in OC through inhibiting the migration of ovarian cancer cells. The Wound healing and transwell experiments also proved this conclusion.

The dose-dependent nephrotoxicity of DDP remains a major clinical challenge in cancer therapy [45]– [46]. Based on the aforementioned results, the combination of SBA and DDP significantly promotes ovarian cancer cell apoptosis and inhibits migration. We propose to further investigate whether SBA may mitigate DDP-induced renal injury, potentially expanding its therapeutic utility. Cre and BUN levels are the most important biochemical indicators for detecting abnormalities in renal function [47]. Our results shown that the BUN and Cre levels were elevated in DDP group. The histomorphological results also showed that cisplatin had an injurious effect on the kidneys of nude mice. In contrast, after combination with SBA, the levels of BUN and Cre decreased, and renal injury was significantly reduced. We also evaluated the important biomarkers of liver function, ALT and AST [48]. Serum AST and ALT levels showed no significant differences among groups. Histopathological analysis of major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidneys) in SBA-treated mice further confirmed the absence of significant tissue damage. These results collectively demonstrate that SBA exhibits favorable in vivo safety and effectively mitigates DDP-induced renal injury, providing a pharmacodynamic foundation for developing SBA as a therapeutic intervention against DDP-induced nephrotoxicity.

In the context of plant-derived anti-ovarian cancer agents, accumulating evidence suggests that alkaloids may possess superior therapeutic potential. Our study highlights the p38-p53-mediated apoptosis and migration pathway as a novel target for the development of SBA therapeutics. However, due to limitations in experimental conditions and time, several limitations should be acknowledged: (1) The multifaceted mechanisms underlying SBA-induced tumor cell apoptosis (e.g., ROS modulation in mitochondria, siRNA knockdown of p38/p53) require further validation; (2) The selection of time points for in vitro apoptosis and migration assays warrants optimization; (3) Additional methodological approaches (e.g., qPCR, isobologram analysis, live-cell imaging) are needed to corroborate the findings. Future studies will address these limitations and further explore the therapeutic potential of SBA against ovarian cancer and other malignancies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this is the first study on the anti-ovarian cancer effects of SBA, and our results showed that SBA could effectively suppress OC progression both in vitro and in vivo by modulating the p38-p53 pathway (Fig. 8), with no significant hepatic or renal toxicity. In addition, combining SBA with cisplatin can enhance its anti-ovarian cancer activity and reduce renal damage. These findings demonstrate that SBA might be a promising nontoxic therapeutic candidate for OC treatment.

Effect of hot air temperature and slice thickness on the drying kinetics and quality of Hami melon (Cantaloupe) slices
Authors: Tianying Sun, Ning Wang, Cuntang Wang, Jian Ren
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe effects of hot air temperature and slice thickness on the drying kinetics and quality of Hami melon slices (HMS) were studied. HMS (3 mm and 5 mm) were carried out in a forced convection drying oven at temperatures ranging from 60 ~ 90 °C. The increase in hot air temperature and the decrease in slice thickness led to shorter drying times and higher drying rates. The drying rate curves indicated that the drying process of HMS did not exhibit a constant drying rate period. In the drying kinetics modeling process, the Explinear model was suited for describing the drying curves with higher accuracy. According to Fick’s second law, the moisture effective diffusion coefficient of 3 mm and 5 mm HMS ranged from 4.72 ~ 15.96 × 10-11m2/s at temperatures of 60, 70, 80, and 90 °C. When the thickness of HMS were 3 mm and 5 mm, the activation energies were 30.13 and 43.75 kJ/mol, respectively. When dried at low temperatures, the color of HMS was closer to fresh Hami melon. With the increase of hot air temperature, the hardness of HMS decreases and the rehydration increases. When the thickness of the HMS decreases, the hardness of the slices decreases and the rehydration increases.Keywords: HMS, Hot-air drying, Mathematical modeling, QualitySubject terms: Psychology, Energy science and technology, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Hami melon (Cantaloupe) is a popular fruit that grows in tropical areas and has commercial value worldwide. The total global yield of Hami melon is 27 × 106 t/a. Hami melon is an important local special economic crop in Xinjiang, China. The total output of Hami melon in Xinjiang is 1.1 million tons and an area of 41,000 hectares1. Hami melons are rich in various nutrients essential for the human body, with high sugar content, juicy flesh, a delicate and sweet taste, and a unique flavor, which are highly favored by consumers. For its delicious taste and nutritive components, it stands as one of the most consumed fruits around the world. Hami melon contains many health-beneficial components, such as β-carotene2, carotenoids, vitamins, flavonoid compounds, minerals, amino acids, and other antioxidative active components3. Antioxidants play an important role in protecting the body from free radical damage and prevent or repair damage to body cells by inhibiting the oxidation of biochemical molecules caused by oxygen4.

The unique aroma and juicy, fragrant taste of Hami melon make it more acceptable to consumers. The high sugar content in Hami melons also makes them a suitable ingredient for the food industry. However, Hami melon is extremely vulnerable to rotting due to its high water content, with a short shelf life of around 15 days5, resulting in huge losses of crop and value6. In addition, low processing rates and backward storage conditions also limit the development of the Hami melon industry. Developing storable products from fresh fruits is an important consideration to reduce post-harvest losses. In the food industry, proper techniques can be used to reduce processing time and improve industrial operations, thus producing high-quality products that retain their natural characteristics of food2. Therefore, fresh-keeping techniques should be adopted to extend the shelf life of Hami melon.

For most fruits and vegetables, drying is an important and commonly used method for extending shelf life and minimizing post-harvest losses7. Food drying is a process of removing moisture from moist materials to improve food stability and preserve product quality7. Therefore, for many years, the drying process has been used to maintain the quality of different products. The drying process can be used to evaporate moisture from food to increase shelf life8. Hot air drying has two important characteristics: effectively reducing the moisture content of food raw materials and reducing the operating cost9. Several factors may affect the hot air drying of foods, such as air velocity and temperature, diffusion of water in the material, bulk density, and the thickness and shape of the product being dried.

At present, the research on Hami melon mainly focuses on the nutritional composition10, flavor material analysis11, post-harvest preservation method12, and fruit juice processing13, while the research on the hot air drying dynamics of HMS is rarely reported. Therefore, this study mainly analyzes the influence of hot air drying temperature and slice thickness on the drying characteristics of HMS, calculates the effective diffusion coefficient and activation energy of HMS (slice thickness and hot air temperature) under different drying temperature during the drying process, analyzes the changes of color, hardness and rehydration performance of the products after drying, to design a more efficient hot air drying process of HMS, improve the drying process of Hami melon, and improve the quality of Hami melon drying slice.

Materials and methods

Raw materials

Fresh Hami melon was purchased from a local market (Qiqihar, China) and stored in a 4°C refrigerator for future use. The Hami melon was taken out from the refrigerator about 2 h before the experiment and brought to room temperature. Before drying, the sample fruits were washed, peeled, and pitted, and then cut into 3mm and 5 mm slices using a manual fruit slicer. The initial water content of fresh Hami melon was determined in an oven (GZX-9146MBE, Shanghai Bo-Xun Industrial Co., Ltd., accurate to ± 1 °C). After 6 h at 105°C, the initial water content of the sample was measured to be 9.0 ± 0.12 g water/g DW.

Drying process

Before the experiment, the hot air drying oven (DHG-9053A, Shanghai Jinghong Experimental Facilities Corporation Ltd, Shanghai, China) was adjusted to the required temperature for about 60 min to ensure stable working conditions. Then about 100 g of the HMS (thickness:3 mm; 5 mm) samples are evenly distributed on a mesh woven with iron wire. The air temperature in the heated blower dryer was set to 60, 70, 80 and 90°C respectively for dehydration of HMS. A steady flow of air was maintained at a velocity of 1.0 m/s. The drying procedure: the weight of the sample was measured with a balance (CP423S, Sartorius AG, Gottingen, Germany, 0.01 g accuracy) at intervals of 10 min. The weighing process lasted less than 20 s. Dehydration continues until the loss of moisture is negligible, at which point the moisture content is considered to be in equilibrium. All experiments were repeated three times at each temperature and the average measurement value was calculated. The dried samples were placed in sealed bags and kept in the desiccator.

Color parameters

The color parameters of HMS refer to the method of Wang et al. and were measured using a colorimeter (CR-10 Plus, Konica Minolta optics Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China)20. The color of dried HMS is characterized by brightness (L*), redness (a*), and yellowness (b*). The color measurement data is represented by the average value of the measured values of 6 fruit slice samples.

Hardness

The hardness determination of HMS refers to the method of Wang et al. and is measured using a fruit hardness tester (GY-3, Dedu Precision Instrument Co., Ltd., Changzhou, China)17. The force required for the probe to break the dried Hami melon fruit slice is recorded in units (N). The larger this value, the greater the hardness of the Hami melon fruit slice and the worse the brittleness.

Determination of rehydration performance

The determination of the rehydration performance of HMS refers to the method of Doymaz and is slightly modified. Take 5.0 g of dried Hami melon fruit slice sample using an electronic balance (CP423S, Sartorius AG, Gottingen, Germany, 0.01 g accuracy) and put it in a 100 mL Erlenmeyer flask, and add 100 mL of distilled water (temperatures: 25°C). Record the mass of the Hami melon slice (weigh after draining the water) every 10 min and measure six times.

Data processing

All experiments were repeated three times and the results were expressed using the mean ± standard deviation. Apply SPSS 20.10 software to perform an analysis of variance on all data and conduct Duncan’s a significant difference analysis. p < 0.05 indicates significant difference, and p < 0.01 indicates an extremely significant difference.

Results and discussion

Hot air drying characteristic curve

The drying characteristics curve of Hami melon is the relationship curve between moisture content and drying time. The hot-air drying characteristics curves of Hami melon with different slice thicknesses and different drying temperatures are shown in Fig. 1: the moisture content of HMS decreases with the prolongation of drying time.

Drying curves of HMS undergoing hot air at different temperatures for the sample thicknesses of (A) 3 mm and (B) 5 mm.

HMS of two thicknesses (3 mm and 5 mm) were dried at four hot air temperatures (60, 70, 80, and 90°C). The initial moisture content (M0) of the HMS was 9.06 g water/ g DW, and the equilibrium moisture content was 0.01g water/ g DW. Comparing Fig. 1 (A) and (B), it can be seen that the temperature of hot air and the thickness of slices have a significant effect on the moisture content. At the drying temperatures of 60, 70, 80, and 90°C, respectively, the time for obtaining equilibrium moisture content of the HMS of 3mm thickness was 170, 150, 130, and 80 min, while the time for obtaining equilibrium moisture content of the HMS of 5mm thickness was 250, 210, 180, 150 min. Obviously, at the selected four drying temperatures (60 ~ 90°C), the increasing hot air temperature shortened the drying time and accelerated the drying process (p < 0.05). The drying time decreased with the increase in drying temperature, which was also reported in some drying materials, such as okra slices22, eggplant slices23, jujube slices24, and papaya slices25. The decrease in the thickness of the slices also shortened the drying time and accelerated the drying process. This is because the distance of water reaching the surface increases, and the thicker the sample is, the longer the drying time is required. Similar conclusions have also been reported in drying experiments of pumpkin slices26, crabapple slices27, and peach slices28.

Drying rate

Figure 2 (A) and (B) illustrate the drying rate-time curves at different drying temperatures and different slice thicknesses of HMS. At the beginning of drying, the drying rate rapidly reached its highest point around 20min and then started to decrease continuously until reaching equilibrium moisture. When the hot air temperature decreased and the slice thickness increased, the drying rate also decreased. When the slice thicknesses were 3mm and 5mm respectively, when the drying time exceeded 70min and 90min, the higher the drying temperature, the lower the drying rate, which indicates that at the same slice thickness, the drying rate does not increase even if the temperature was increased at the late stage of drying.

Drying rate versus drying time of Hami melons slices at different temperatures for the sample thicknesses of (A) 3 mm and (B) 5 mm.

The HMS did not have a constant drying phase. This is mainly because during the drying of HMS, the water diffusion rate on the surface is higher than the internal water transfer rate of HMS, and the water diffusion process is very fast, which is the main water removal process. Similar results have also been found in apple pomace29, lotus leaf30 and white grape31.

Under high-temperature conditions, the drying time is reduced. Because the relative humidity is lower at high temperatures, the water evaporation rate of the material is faster32. The slice thickness also affects the drying time. At the same moisture content, the thinner the slice, the higher the drying rate and the shorter the drying time. Similar research results include leek slices33, yam slices34 and papaya35.

Calculation of activation energy

Activation energy is the initial energy required to remove 1 mol of water during the drying process20. The greater the activation energy, the more difficult it is to remove water33. Activation energy calculation is to plot a straight line (Fig. 4) to the inverse of the absolute drying temperature (1/T) of ln Deff, from which the activation energy could be calculated. The activation energies of HMS with a thickness of 3 mm and 5 mm were calculated to be 30.13 and 43.75 kJ/mol, respectively. Zogzas et al. reported the energy activation values of various foods ranging from 12.7 kJ/mol to 110 kJ/mol38. The energy activation values obtained were within this range. The thermal air drying activation energy of HMS was close to the data in other literature, such as Annona epicarp (32.045 kJ/mol)18, passion fruit peel (34.72 kJ/mol)39, jujube slices (36.76 kJ/mol)20, olive kernel slices (43.98kJ/mol)40, pear slices (44.78 kJ/mol)41.

Color of Hami melon slices

Color is an important indicator of food quality, which can effectively improve consumers’ acceptance of products28. The effects of different section thicknesses (3mm and 5mm) and drying temperatures (60 ~ 90°C) on the color of HMS are shown in Fig. 5. With the increase in drying temperature, the brightness (L value) of 3 mm HMS first decreased and then increased, while that of 5 mm HMS first increased and then decreased. When the hot air temperature was 60 °C and 90°C, the Hami melon slice thickness had a significant effect on the brightness (L value) of the slices (p > 0.05). When the slice thickness is constant, the influence of hot air temperature on the brightness (L value) of HMS is not significant (p > 0.05).

L, a, and b values of HMS after hot air drying at different temperatures and slice thicknesses. Note: Difffferent letters (a–b) represent signifificant difffferences (p < 0.05) between treatment groups at the same drying temperatures; different letters (A–B) represent signifificant difffferences (p < 0.05) between treatment groups within the same slice thicknesses.

All the dried HMS showed orange and no obvious browning. With the increase in temperature, the redness (a value) of HMS decreases first and then increases. There was no significant difference in reddness (a value) between 60°C and 70°C dried slices (p > 0.05), but they were lower than between 60°C and 90°C dried cantaloupe slices (p < 0.05). The slice thickness also had significant influence on the redness value (a) of dried HMS (p > 0.05). This indicates that the larger the slice thickness, the longer the drying time, the greater the redness value20. The increase in redness is mainly due to the enzymatic browning of polyphenols in Hami melon and the brown pigment produced by the non-enzymatic browning of carbohydrates at high temperatures and long heating42,43.

The yellowness value (b) is positive, indicating that the Hami melon color is yellow. When the temperature increases, the yellowness value (b) of the dried HMS increases slightly, that is, the color of the dried HMS becomes more yellow. However the effects of drying temperature and slice thickness on yellowness (b) of the dried HMS were not significant (p > 0.05). The slight increase in the b value of 5 mm HMS compared to 3mm HMS may be related to prolonged dry time resulting in an increased amount of browning material produced42.

Hardness of Hami lemons slices

The quality properties of dry products are usually measured by puncture force, which is a measure of the surface hardness of the product and an indicator of the degree of surface hardening that occurs during the drying process44. Whether fresh or dried Hami melon, hardness is one of the most important qualities. Figure 6 shows the influence of slice thickness and hot air temperature on the hardness of HMS. When the hot air temperature was the same, the slice thickness had a significant effect on the hardness of the HMS (p < 0.05). With the increase in drying temperature, the hardness of the HMS decreased gradually. The hardness of 3mm HMS dried at 90°C was 12.5N, which was 44.4% lower than that at 60°C (p < 0.05). The hardness of 5mm sliced Hami melon dried at 90°C was 13.5N, which decreased by 64.0% compared with 60°C (p < 0.05). The decrease in the tissue hardness of the HMS may be caused by the faster evaporation rate of water during hot air drying, the faster collapse of the fruit slice structure, and the formation of a relatively loose porous structure in the tissue45. Song et al. studied the drying of litchi fruits by different drying methods and found that the hardness of litchi fruits increased with the increase of temperature within a certain range46.

Rehydration performance of HMS

Rehydration performance is a quality parameter that determines the ability of a food material to restore its original shape and shows the degree of cell damage affected by its operating conditions during drying34.The effects of slice thickness and drying temperature on the rehydration performance of HMS are shown in Fig. 7. The effect of hot air drying temperature on the rehydration performance of HMS was significant. When the slice thickness was constant, the higher the temperature, the greater the rehydration performance. The effect of slice thickness on the rehydration performance of HMS was also significant. When the hot air temperature is constant, the smaller the thickness of the HMS, the greater the rehydration performance of the HMS after drying. When the slice thickness of the Hami melon was 3mm and the drying temperature was 60°C, the rehydration performance was the lowest (220.6%). The rehydration performance at 90°C was the highest (275.01%), which was nearly 55% higher than that at 60°C. When studying jujube fruit slices, Wang et al. found that the smaller the slice thickness, the higher the product rehydration performance, which is consistent with the results of this study13. Srikanth et al. found that the rehydration performance increased with the increase in drying temperature when studying the convection drying and quality characteristics of yam47. This may be because the thinner the slice, the larger the specific surface area, the more chance of rehydration contact with water, the speed of water absorption is correspondingly accelerated, and the rehydration ratio is increased48,49.

Conclusions

The hot air drying treatment of sliced Hami melons found that the thickness of melon slices and the hot air temperature have a significant impact on the drying characteristics and quality of HMS. The thinner the slice and the higher the hot air temperature, the faster the drying speed and the shorter the drying time. Under experimental conditions, the drying rate curve of Hami melons shows that there is no constant-rate drying stage in the slicing process of Hami melons. Among the five thin-layer drying equations studied, the Explinear model has the highest R2 and the lowest χ2 and is most suitable for simulating the drying characteristics of hot air for sliced Hami melons. The effective diffusion coefficient is 4.72 × 10–11 m2/s ~ 15.96 × 10–11 m2/s. The activation energy of 3mm slices is 30.13 kJ/mol, and the activation energy of 5mm slices is 43.75 kJ/mol. When dried at low temperatures, the color of HMS is closer to that of fresh Hami melons. As the hot air drying temperature increases, the hardness of HMS decreases and the rehydration performance increases. Therefore, when drying HMS using hot air, we can select a higher temperature (90°C) and thinner slices (3mm) to accelerate the drying process, resulting in a product with a light yellow color, good rehydration performance, and lower hardness. Moreover, in our future research, we will utilize various drying methods for HMS, such as pressure explosion puffing, radio frequency-hot air, vacuum-microwave, electrohydrodynamic drying, etc., to elucidate the mechanisms of moisture evaporation during the drying process. We will analyze the impact of drying on the nutritional components, antioxidant activity components, flavor substances, and the quality of the dried slices of Hami melon, aiming to provide consumers with a greater selection of beneficial fruits and vegetables that contribute to their health.

Systems of Hecke eigenvalues on subschemes of Shimura varieties
Stefan Reppen

Abstract
. We show that the systems of Hecke eigenvalues that appear in the coherent cohomology with coefficients in automorphic line bundles of any mod p p abelian type compact Shimura variety at hyperspecial level are the same as those appearing in any Hecke-equivariant closed subscheme. We also prove analogous results for noncompact Shimura varieties or nonclosed subschemes, such as Ekedahl-Oort strata, length strata and central leaves.



1.
Introduction

The mod
p
fiber of an abelian type Shimura variety at good reduction comes equipped with an action of the prime-to-
p
Hecke algebra. Several well-studied subschemes - such as Ekedahl-Oort strata, length strata, Newton strata and central leaves - are invariant under this action. By considering coherent cohomology for automorphic bundles, we thus obtain several Hecke modules, and it is interesting to relate the eigenvalues that appear in these modules to those appearing in the cohomology of the Shimura variety.
In a famous letter from Serre to Tate
[
Ser96
]
, Serre shows that the systems of Hecke eigenvalues that appear in the cohomology of a mod
p
modular curve are the same as those appearing in the cohomology of the supersingular locus. This result was generalized by Goldring-Koskivirta in
[
GK19
, Theorem 11.1.1]
. Letting
S_{K}
denote the special fiber of the Kisin-Vasiu integral model of a Hodge-type Shimura variety at good reduction, the authors of ibid. show that the systems of Hecke eigenvalues appearing in the cohomology of
S_{K}
are the same as those appearing in the cohomology of the unique zero-dimensional Ekedahl-Oort stratum.

They also showed that these eigenvalues are the same as those appearing in the cohomology of the toroidal compactification of
S_{K}
. For the purpose of brevity, in the introduction we omit any discussion on compactifications.
As pointed out by Terakado-Yu in
[
TY22
]
, the method of ibid. can be applied to any Hecke-equivariant smooth subscheme of dimension 0 equipped with a nowhere vanishing Hecke-invariant section of the Hodge line bundle. They use this observation to prove that the systems of Hecke eigenvalues appearing in the cohomology of the Shimura variety are the same as those appearing in the cohomology of any central leaf contained in the basic Newton stratum
[
TY22
, Theorem 5.4]
.

In the aforementioned works, the subschemes considered are smooth of dimension 0.

denote the associated automorphic line bundle.

Let

The proofs of
[
GK19
, Theorem 11.1.1]
and
[
TY22
, Theorem 5.4]
proceed in two steps; a going-down argument relating eigenvalues on the Shimura variety to eigenvalues on the subschemes in question (i.e., the zero-dimensional Ekedahl-Oort stratum
[
GK19
]
or the zero-dimensional central leaves
[
TY22
]
), and a going-up argument in the other direction. A key idea in the going-up argument is to use that there is a nowhere vanishing Hasse invariant on the subscheme in question. For the going-down argument, the proofs relate Hecke eigenforms on the Shimura variety to Hecke eigenforms of the sheaf of differentials via the conormal exact sequence, building on arguments of Ghitza
[
Ghi04
]
. Since the subschemes are smooth of dimension 0, existence of Hasse invariants is not needed in the going-down arguments.

In this text we consider higher dimensional and possibly singular subschemes
Y_{K}
. One thus faces two problems; (1) there is no nowhere vanishing Hecke-invariant section on
Y_{K}
, and (2) the conormal sequence is neither exact nor consists of locally free sheaves. The key idea to surpass these problems is to perform a further going-down argument. By intersecting
Y_{K}
with the smallest length stratum that it intersects nontrivially, and by successively removing the
nonsingular
points of this intersection, we obtain a smooth, closed subscheme
Z_{K}\subset Y_{K}
which admits a nowhere vanishing Hecke-equivariant section. We thereafter pass from the ideal sheaf defining
Z_{K}
in
Y_{K}
to the ideal sheaf defining
Z_{K}
in
S_{K}
and modify the going-down arguments of
[
GK19
]
to relate Hecke eigenvalues on
Y_{K}
respectively
S_{K}
to eigenvalues on
Z_{K}
. This step crucially uses the existence of Hasse invariants, in contrast to the going-down argument of ibid. Finally, the going-up argument of ibid. applies verbatim to relate eigenvalues on
Z_{K}
to
Y_{K}
and
S_{K}
respectively.

2.2.2.
Ekedahl-Oort and length stratifications

Fix a Borel subgroup

2.3.
Reminder on central leaves

Since we will not work explicitly with the central leaves, we simply sketch their definition in the Hodge type case. For details and definitions in the case of abelian type, see
[
SZ22
, Section 4]
.

Let
A_{K}\to S_{K}
denote the universal abelian variety over
S_{K}
. For any
x\in S_{K}(\overline{\kappa})
, the
central leaf
of
x
, denoted

y\in S_{K}(\overline{\kappa})
such that there is an isomorphism of Dieudonné modules
\mathbb{D}(A_{K,x}[p^{\infty}])(W(\overline{\kappa}))\cong\mathbb{D}(A_{K,y}[p^{\infty}])(W(\overline{\kappa}))

. Since the Ekedahl-Oort strata parametrizes the

has an explicit combinatorial description in terms of the Newton map, which we omit here. We point out that central leaves in the unique Newton stratum of minimal dimension are smooth and zero-dimensional.

\pi(Y_{K^{\prime}}^{\text{sm}})
is smooth. Hence, by maximality of

, we have that
\pi(Y_{K^{\prime}}^{\text{sm}})=Y_{K}^{\text{sm}}
and thus maximality of

gives that
\pi^{*}Y_{K}^{\text{sm}}=Y_{K^{\prime}}^{\text{sm}}
as topological spaces. Since

is equivariant,
\pi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{K}^{\text{sm}}}={\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{K^{\prime}}^{\text{sm}}}
and we see that
\pi^{*}Y_{K}^{\text{sm}}=Y_{K^{\prime}}^{\text{sm}}
as schemes.

Finally, we show

. Since the transition maps are finite and étale, they are open and closed. Hence,
\pi^{*}Z_{K}
identifies with the closed subset

. Since the transition maps are étale,
\pi^{*}Z_{K}
is reduced. By uniqueness of the reduced subscheme structure, we thus see that

. ∎

Lemma 2.4.6

Suppose that

For the convenience of the reader,
we collect here a two elementary statements that are used in the proof of

Recall that given a scheme

Lemma 2.5.1
.

If

is a reduced scheme and

is a nowhere vanishing global section of a line bundle
{\mathscr{L}}
on

, then

is injective.

Proof.

The question is local on

so we can assume that

for a reduced ring

and that

. Since

is nowhere vanishing, for all prime ideals

. Hence, if

for some

, then

is contained in all prime ideals of

, hence

is nilpotent. Since

is reduced,

.
∎

Lemma 2.5.2
.

Suppose that
X
is a regular scheme and that
\iota\colon{\mathscr{V}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{V}}^{\prime}
is an inclusion of locally free sheaves. Then, for all
n\geq 0
,
\sym^{n}(\iota)
is injective.

Proof.

Let

. Since

is locally free, it suffices to show that

is torsion, i.e. that

. Fix a point

in

is regular the local ring
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
is a domain. Let

denote the fraction field. Since
\sym^{n}
takes injections of vector spaces to injections and commutes with localizations, and since localization is exact and

commutes with taking stalks, we see that

(2.5.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\ker\Big(\sym^{n}({\mathscr{V}}_{x}\otimes Q)\to\sym^{n}({\mathscr{V}}_{x}^{\prime}\otimes Q)\Big)

\displaystyle=\ker\Big(\sym^{n}({\mathscr{V}}_{x})\otimes Q\to\sym^{n}({\mathscr{V}}_{x}^{\prime})\otimes Q\Big)

∎

3.
Systems of Hecke eigenvalues: Proof of the main theorems

3.1.
Notation

Let
(Y_{K})_{K}\subset(S_{K})_{K}
be a
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{A}_{f}^{p})
-equivariant system of subschemes. We denote by
\omega=\det\Omega_{S_{K}}

to
Y_{K}
, as opposed to the line bundle

Suppose that

, then we write

.

3.2.
Proof of
Theorem

A

Lemma 3.2.1
.

If
(b_{T})_{T}
is a system Hecke eigenvalues appearing in

for some
\eta\in X_{+,L}^{*}(T)

for some
\eta_{i}\in X_{+,L}^{*}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
V_{\bullet}
be the filtration on
\sym^{n}\Big((\text{Lie}(G)/\text{Lie}(P))^{\vee}\Big)\otimes V_{\eta}
from

2.2.1
. The projections

-equivariant since they are defined over

. Hence, we obtain a

-equivariant map

∎

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Suppose that

are two
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{A}_{f}^{p})
-equivariant subschemes such that

is proper and smooth.
If there exists an

and an injective Hecke-equivariant section

of

,

Proof.

Let

be a Hecke eigenform with system of Hecke eigenvalues
(b_{T})_{T}
. Let
{\mathcal{I}}
denote the ideal sheaf of
Z_{K}
in
S_{K}
, and let
{\mathcal{J}}
denote the ideal sheaf of
Z_{K}
in
Y_{K}
. Since

is nonzero in

We thus have a surjection

H^{0}(Z_{K},\sym^{n}({\mathcal{I}}/{\mathcal{I}}^{2})\otimes{\mathscr{V}}(\eta)\otimes\omega^{ar})\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}(Z_{K},{\mathcal{J}}^{n}/{\mathcal{J}}^{n+1}\otimes{\mathscr{V}}(\eta)\otimes\omega^{ar})

Theorem 3.2.6
.

(
Theorem

A
)
Suppose that
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{X})
is of compact-type. Let
(Y_{K})_{K}\subset(S_{K})_{K}
be an arbitrary
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{A}_{f}^{p})
-equivariant system of closed subschemes. We have that
M(Y_{K})\leftrightsquigarrow_{{\mathcal{H}}}M(S_{K})
.

Proof.

Let

S_{K}=S_{K,\dim(S_{K})}\cup...\cup S_{K,0}

be the length stratification of
S_{K}
, and let

i_{0}\coloneqq\min\{i:Y_{K}\cap\overline{S}_{K,i}\neq\emptyset\}.

Define inductively the subschemes

and for all

By
Lemma

2.4.5
,

form an equivariant system of closed subschemes and we get a decreasing chain of closed subschemes

Since

is Noetherian, there is a minimal integer

such that

for all

. Let

. If

which implies that

is smooth.

By
Lemma

2.2.6
there exists an integer

and a section

of

whose nonvanishing locus is exactly

denote the restriction of

to

. We claim that

is nowhere vanishing. If

, then

is nowhere vanishing, and thus so is

. Hence, we may assume that

. By

we have that

and thus

(as topological spaces). Hence, if there is some point

in

on which

vanishes, then
x
lies in
Y_{K}\cap\overline{S}_{K,i_{0}-1}
. By definition of

,

. Hence,

is a nowhere vanishing section and
thus, by
Lemma

2.5.1
,

is injective. Since

is proper, so is any closed subscheme. Hence, we apply
Lemma

3.2.2
and

3.2.4
on the pair

respectively

to obtain that

∎

Moment problems in the Schwartz and Gelfand-Shilov spaces
Andreas Debrouwere

Abstract
. We provide a geometric characterization of the closed sets K ⊆ ℝ d K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} such that every real d d -sequence is the moment sequence of some Schwartz function on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with support in K K . We obtain a similar result for Gelfand–Shilov spaces. Several illustrative examples are discussed. Our work is inspired by a recent result of Schmüdgen [Expositiones Math. 43 (2025), 125657], who addressed the analogous problem for Radon measures.

Introduction

In his seminal work
[
29
]
, Stieltjes characterized the non-negative sequences
(c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
that are the moment sequence of some non-negative Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
, that is,

(1.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{j}d\mu(x)=c_{j},\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

Since then, the theory of moment problems has been extensively developed in various directions; see the monograph
[
27
]
for more information.

Boas
[
1
]
initiated the study of moment problems for signed measures. He proved that for
every
real sequence
(c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
there exists a signed Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
such that (
1.1
) holds. Pólya improved this result by showing that, given any unbounded set
K\subseteq[0,\infty)
, for every real sequence
(c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
there exists a signed Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
with
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq K
such that (
1.1
) holds. A multidimensional version of Boas’ result was later established by Sherman
[
28
]
.

Moment problems in the setting of the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing smooth functions were first considered by Durán
[
11
]
. He showed that for every real sequence
(c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
there exists a Schwartz function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq[0,\infty)
such that

(1.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{j}f(x)dx=c_{j},\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

Estrada
[
15
]
later obtained multidimensional and vector-valued versions of Durán’s result. See
[
8
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
16

24

for related works.

J. Chung, S.-Y. Chung, and Kim

[
17
]

\sigma>0

In

Roughly speaking, the conditions in (2) in Theorems

While very natural, to the best of our knowlegde, Eidelheit’s theorem has not yet been applied in the study of unrestricted moment problems (although it was implicitly used in
[

2.
Eidelheit’s theorem

In this section, we state Eidelheit’s theorem and use it to give a short alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
E
be a locally convex space. We write
E^{\prime}
for the topological dual of
E
. Given a subspace
F\subseteq E^{\prime}
and a continuous seminorm
p
on
E
, we define
F_{p}
as the space consisting of all elements in
F
that are continuous with respect to
p
. Eidelheit’s theorem
[
14
]
, may now be formulated as follows:

Theorem 2.1

(Eidelheit’s theorem)
.

[
22
, Theorem 26.27]

Let
E
be a Fréchet space and
\mathcal{P}
be a fundamental family of continuous seminorms on
E
. Let
(e^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in J}\subseteq E^{\prime}
be a countable family. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

For every
(c_{j})_{j\in J}\in\mathbb{R}^{J}
there exists
e\in E
such that

\langle e^{\prime}_{j},e\rangle=c_{j},\qquad\forall j\in J.

(2)

The family
(e^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in J}
is linearly independent and for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
the space

(\operatorname{span}\{e^{\prime}_{j}\,|\,j\in J\})_{p}

is finite-dimensional.

We now use Eidelheit’s theorem to show Theorem
1.1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the norm

\|\mu\|_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{n}d|\mu|(x),\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We endow
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the locally convex topology generated by the system of norms
\{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Fréchet space. For
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
closed we define
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq K\}
and endow this space with the subspace topology induced by
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is also a Fréchet space.
We need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots x_{d}]
.

(1)

P(x)=0
for all
x\in K
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)d\mu(x)=0,\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}.

(2)

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
P\in\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
if and only if there is
C>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)d\mu(x)\right|\leq C\|\mu\|_{n},\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}.

Proof.

For
x\in K
we write
\delta_{x}\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}
for the Dirac delta measure concentrated at
x
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
: Obvious.

\Leftarrow
: For all
x\in K
it holds that

P(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)d\delta_{x}(y)=0.

(2)
\Rightarrow
: Obvious.

\Leftarrow
: For all
x\in K
it holds that

|P(x)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)d\delta_{x}(y)\right|\leq C\|\delta_{x}\|_{n}=C\int_{X}(1+|y|)^{n}d\delta_{x}(y)=C(1+|x|)^{n}.

∎

Alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
we define
e^{\prime}_{P}\in(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K})^{\prime}
via

\langle e^{\prime}_{P},\mu\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)d\mu(x),\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}.

Clearly, the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for every
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}
there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}
such that

\langle e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}},\mu\rangle=c_{\alpha},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K}
is a Fréchet space, it suffices by Eidelheit’s theorem to show that the following two sets of conditions are equivalent to each other:

(a)

(e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
is linearly independent.

(b)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
the space
(\operatorname{span}\{e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\})_{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{n}}
is finite-dimensional.

and

(a’)

K
is Zariski dense.

(b’)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
the space
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
is finite-dimensional.

Consider the linear mapping

T:\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\to(\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{pol},K})^{\prime},\,P\mapsto e^{\prime}_{P}.

Condition (a) holds if and only if
T
is injective. Hence, Lemma
2.2
(1) implies that (a) and (a’) are equivalent. Next, we assume that (a) holds and show that (b) is equivalent to (b’). Since we already know that (a) and (a’) are equivalent, this will finish the proof. Note that
\operatorname{span}\{e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\}=\{e^{\prime}_{P}\mid P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\}
. Lemma
2.2
(2) implies that
T(\mathcal{N}_{n}(K))=\{e^{\prime}_{P}\mid P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\}_{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{n}}
for all

. This yields that (b) and (b’) are equivalent.
∎

The unrestricted

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the norm

\|f\|_{k,n}=\max_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|(1+|x|)^{n},\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We endow
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the locally convex topology induced by the system of norms
\{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{k,n}\mid k,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Note that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Fréchet space. For
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
closed we write
\mathcal{S}_{K}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq K\}
and endow this space with the subspace topology induced by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{K}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is also a Fréchet space. If
K

\mathcal{D}_{K}=\mathcal{S}_{K}

We start with the analogue of Lemma
2.2
(1).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a non-empty regular closed set and
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

P\equiv 0
.

(2)

P(x)=0
for all
x\in K
.

(3)

It holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx=0,\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}.

Proof.

The implications
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
are trivial. We now show
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Since
P\equiv 0
if
P
vanishes on some non-empty open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it suffices to show that
P(x)=0
for all
x\in\operatorname{int}K
. Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(0,1)}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)dy=1
. Set

\varphi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)=\varepsilon^{-d}\varphi((x-y)/\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
x\in\operatorname{int}K
be arbitrary. For all
0<\varepsilon<d(x,\partial K)
it holds that
\varphi_{x,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
. Consequently,

P(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\varphi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)dy=0.

∎

Next, we show the analogue of Lemma
2.2
(2), which we divide into two separate statements.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a regular closed set and
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
P\in\mathcal{N}_{k,n}(K)
there is
C>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx\right|\leq C\|f\|_{k,n+d+1},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}.

Proof.

Since
P\in\mathcal{N}_{k,n}(K)
, it holds that

C_{0}=\sup_{x\in K}\frac{|P(x)|d_{K}(x)^{k}}{(1+|x|)^{n}}<\infty.

Set
K_{0}=\{x\in\operatorname{int}K\,|\,d(x,\partial K)\leq 1\}
and
C_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{-d-1}dx
. For all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}

we find that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K_{0}}|P(x)||f(x)|dx+\int_{K\backslash K_{0}}|P(x)||f(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{1}\left(\sup_{x\in K_{0}}\frac{|f(x)|(1+|x|)^{n+d+1}}{d(x,\partial K)^{k}}+\sup_{x\in K\backslash K_{0}}|f(x)|(1+|x|)^{n+d+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{1}\left(\sup_{x\in K_{0}}\frac{|f(x)|(1+|x|)^{n+d+1}}{d(x,\partial K)^{k}}+\|f\|_{k,n+d+1}\right).

Set
m=n+d+1
. By the above inequality, it suffices to show that there is
C_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{x\in K_{0}}\frac{|f(x)|(1+|x|)^{m}}{d(x,\partial K)^{k}}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{k,m},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
and
x\in K_{0}
be arbitrary. Pick
\xi\in\partial K
such that
|x-\xi|=d(x,\partial K)
. Note that
f^{(\alpha)}(\xi)=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Hence, Taylor’s formula implies that there exists
\gamma\in\{tx+(1-t)\xi\mid t\in[0,1]\}
such that

f(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(\gamma)}{\alpha!}(x-\xi)^{\alpha}.

As
x\in K_{0}
, it holds that
|x-\gamma|\leq|x-\xi|=d(x,\partial K)\leq 1
. Therefore,

(1+|\gamma|)^{-m}\leq(1+|\gamma-x|)^{m}(1+|x|)^{-m}\leq 2^{m}(1+|x|)^{-m}.

Set
C_{3}=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{\alpha!}
. We obtain that

|f(x)|\leq|x-\xi|^{k}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{|f^{(\alpha)}(\gamma)|}{\alpha!}\leq C_{3}\|f\|_{k,m}\frac{d(x,\partial K)^{k}}{(1+|\gamma|)^{m}}\leq 2^{m}C_{3}\|f\|_{k,m}\frac{d(x,\partial K)^{k}}{(1+|x|)^{m}}.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a regular closed set and
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx\right|\leq C\|f\|_{k,n},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}.

Then,
P\in\mathcal{N}_{k+d,n}(K)
.

We need an auxiliary result for the proof of Proposition
3.3
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
the space consisting of all
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with
\operatorname{deg}P\leq m
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
. There is
C>0
such that for all
P\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
0<r\leq 1
,

|P(x)|\leq\frac{C}{r^{k+d}}\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,r)},\|\varphi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\}.

Proof.

We write

\|P\|_{\infty}=\sup_{y\in\overline{B}(0,1)}|P(y)|,\qquad P\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}],

and

\|P\|=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\psi(y)dy\right|\mid\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(0,1)},\|\psi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\},\qquad P\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}].

By Lemma
2.2
(with
K=\overline{B}(0,1)
),
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\infty}
and
\|\,\cdot\,\|
are both norms on
\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. Since the space
\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is finite-dimensional,
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\infty}
and
\|\,\cdot\,\|
are equivalent. Hence, there is
C>0
such that

\|P\|_{\infty}\leq C\|P\|,\qquad\forall P\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}].

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
0<r\leq 1
be arbitrary. Define
P_{x,r}(y)=P(x+ry)
and note that
P_{x,r}\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
.
For
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(0,1)}
we set
\psi_{x,r}(y)=\psi((y-x)/r)
. Then,
\psi_{x,r}\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,r)}
and

\|\psi_{x,r}\|_{k,0}\leq\frac{1}{r^{k}}\|\psi\|_{k,0}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle|P(x)|\leq\|P_{x,r}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|P_{x,r}\|

\displaystyle=C\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{x,r}(y)\psi(y)dy\right|\mid\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(0,1)},\|\psi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{r^{d}}\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\psi_{x,r}(y)dy\right|\mid\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(0,1)},\|\psi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{k+d}}\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,r)},\|\varphi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

We need to show that there is
C>0
such that

|P(x)|\leq\frac{C(1+|x|)^{n}}{d_{K}(x)^{k+d}},\qquad\forall x\in\operatorname{int}K.

Lemma
3.4
implies that there is
C_{1}>0
such that for all
x\in\operatorname{int}K
(choose
r=d_{K}(x)
)

|P(x)|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{d_{K}(x)^{k+d}}\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,d_{K}(x))},\|\varphi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\}.

Let
x\in\operatorname{int}K
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,d_{K}(x))}
be arbitrary. Note that
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
.
As
d_{K}(x)\leq 1
, it holds that

(1+|y|)^{n}\leq(1+|y-x|)^{n}(1+|x|)^{n}\leq 2^{n}(1+|x|)^{n},\qquad\forall y\in\overline{B}(x,d_{K}(x)).

We obtain that

\|\varphi\|_{k,n}=\max_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sup_{y\in\overline{B}(x,d_{K}(x))}|\varphi^{(\alpha)}(y)|(1+|y|)^{n}\leq 2^{n}(1+|x|)^{n}\|\varphi\|_{k,0}.

By (
3.1
), we find that

\displaystyle|P(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{d_{K}(x)^{k+d}}\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,d_{K}(x))},\|\varphi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CC_{1}}{d_{K}(x)^{k+d}}\sup\{\|\varphi\|_{k,n}\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\overline{B}(x,d_{K}(x))},\|\varphi\|_{k,0}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{n}CC_{1}(1+|x|)^{n}}{d_{K}(x)^{k+d}}.

∎

We now show Theorem
1.2
. The proof is very similar to the one of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We may assume that
K
is non-empty.
For
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
we define
e^{\prime}_{P}\in(\mathcal{S}_{K})^{\prime}
via

\langle e^{\prime}_{P},f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx,\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}.

Clearly, the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for every
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}
there exists
f\in\mathcal{S}_{K}
such that

\langle e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}},f\rangle=c_{\alpha},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Consider the linear mapping

T:\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\to(\mathcal{S}_{K})^{\prime},\,P\mapsto e^{\prime}_{P}.

Proposition
3.1
implies that
T
is injective, which is equivalent to the fact that the family
(e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
is linearly independent. Since
\mathcal{S}_{K}
is a Fréchet space, it suffices by Eidelheit’s theorem to show that
(\operatorname{span}\{e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\})_{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{k,n}}
is finite-dimensional for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
if and only if
\mathcal{N}_{k,n}(K)
is finite-dimensional for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
\operatorname{span}\{e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\}=\{e^{\prime}_{P}\mid P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\}
. Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
imply that for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}

T(\mathcal{N}_{k,n}(K))\subseteq\{e^{\prime}_{P}\mid P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\}_{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{k,n+d+1}}

and

T^{-1}(\{e^{\prime}_{P}\mid P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\}_{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{k,n}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{k+d,n}(K).

As
T
is injective, this implies the result.
∎

4.
Weight sequences and Gelfand-Shilov spaces

In this preliminary section, we introduce weight sequences and establish several technical properties of them; see
[
18
,
25
]
for more information. We also define the Gelfand–Shilov spaces associated with these sequences that we will be interested in.

By a
weight sequence
we mean a positive sequence
M=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

•

M_{0}=1\leq M_{1}
.

•

M
is log-convex, i.e.,
M^{2}_{p}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1}
for all
p\geq 1
.

•

M
is non-quasianalytic, i.e.,

\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{p-1}}{M_{p}}<\infty.

We consider the following additional conditions on a weight sequence
M
:

(M.2)

\displaystyle\exists C>0\,\forall p,q\in\mathbb{N}:M_{p+q}\leq C^{p+q}M_{p}M_{q}
.

(M.3)

\displaystyle\exists C>0\,\forall p\in\mathbb{N}:\sum_{q=p+1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{q-1}}{M_{q}}\leq Cp\frac{M_{p}}{M_{p+1}}
.

Condition
(M.2)
is often referred to as the moderate growth condition and
(M.3)
as the strong non-quasianalyticity condition.

Example 4.1
.

The weight sequence
M_{\sigma}=(p!^{\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\sigma>1
, is called the
Gevrey sequence
with index
\sigma
. It satisfies
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
.

Let
M
and
N
be two weight sequences. We write
N\subset M
if there are
C,h>0
such that
N_{p}\leq Ch^{p}M_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. The stronger relation
N\prec M
means that the latter inequality is valid for every
h>0
and suitable
C>0
. We write
N\asymp M
if both
N\subset M
and
M\subset N
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence. We define

\nu_{M}(t)=\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{t^{p}M_{p}}{p!},\qquad t>0,

and
\nu_{M}(0)=0
. The function
\nu_{M}
is increasing,
0\leq\nu_{M}\leq 1
, and for all
a>0
there is
C>0
such that

(4.1)

\nu_{M}(t)\leq Ct^{a},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

As
M
is non-quasianaltyic, it holds that
(M_{p}/p!)^{1/p}\to\infty
as
p\to\infty

[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
. Consequently,
\nu_{M}(0)>0
for all
t>0
and
\nu_{M}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
. The function
\nu_{M}
will play an essential role throughout the rest of this article.

Example 4.2
.

Consider the Gevrey sequence
M_{\sigma}
of index
\sigma>1
. There are
C_{0},C_{1},H_{0},H_{1}>0
such that

C_{0}e^{-H_{0}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}}\leq\nu_{M_{\sigma}}(t)\leq C_{1}e^{-H_{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}},\qquad\forall t>0.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence.

(1)

Suppose that
M
satisfies
(M.2)
. For all
a>0
there is
C>0
such that

\nu_{M}(t)\leq\nu_{M}(Ct)^{a},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(2)

Suppose that
M
satisfies
(M.2)
. There is
C_{0}>0
such that for all
a>0
there is
C_{1}>0

\nu_{M}(t)\leq C_{1}t^{a}\nu_{M}(C_{0}t),\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(3)

Suppose that
M
satisfies
(M.3)
. For all
a>0
there are
C_{0},C_{1}>0
such that

\nu_{M}(at)^{C_{0}}\leq C_{1}\nu_{M}(t),\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Proof.

(1) Since
\nu_{M}(t)\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
, it suffices to consider the cases
a=2^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The case
a=2
is shown in
[
25
, Lemma 7.2]
and the general result then follows by induction.

(2) This follows from (
4.1
) and part (1) (with
a=2
).

(3) Since
\nu_{M}
is increasing, it suffices to consider the cases
a=2^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We will show the case
a=2
, the general result then follows by induction.
We write
M^{*}=(M_{p}/p!)_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
and define the associated function of
M^{*}
(cf.
[
18
]
) by

\omega_{M^{*}}(\rho)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\frac{\rho^{p}}{M^{*}_{p}},\qquad\rho\geq 0.

Note that

(4.2)

\nu_{M}(t)=e^{-\omega_{M^{*}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)},\qquad t>0.

We will establish the result by combining several results from
[
19
]
about the growth indices
\gamma(M)
,
\gamma(M^{*})
, and
\gamma(\omega_{M^{*}})
; we refer the reader to
[
19
]
for their definition. Since
M
satisfies
(M.3)
(denoted
(\gamma_{1})
in
[
19
]
), it follows from
[
19
, Corollary 3.12(ii)]
that
\gamma(M)>1
. By
[
19
, Corollary 4.11]
, we obtain that

\gamma(M^{*})>0
. In view of
[
19
, Corollary 4.6(1)]
, it holds that
\gamma(\omega_{M^{*}})\geq\gamma(M^{*})>0
. Hence, by
[
19
, Corollary 2.14]
,
\omega_{M^{*}}
satisfies the condition
(\omega_{1})
, i.e.,
\omega_{M^{*}}(2\rho)=O(\omega_{M^{*}}(\rho))
as
\rho\to\infty
. The result now follows from (
4.2
).
∎

Finally, we introduce the class of Gelfand-Shilov spaces we will work with. Given a weight sequence
M
, we write
M_{\alpha}=M_{|\alpha|}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. For
h>0
we define
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the space of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.3)

\|f\|^{M,h}_{n}=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|}{h^{|\alpha|}M_{\alpha}}(1+|x|)^{n}<\infty.

We set

\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Example 4.4
.

The spaces
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma>0
, considered in the introduction coincide with
\mathcal{S}^{\{M_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
M_{\sigma}
is the Gevrey sequence of index
\sigma
.

5.
The unrestricted
K
-moment problem in Gelfand-Shilov spaces

We study the unrestricted
K
-moment problem in the Gelfand-Shilov spaces
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in this section.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and
M
be a weight sequence. We say that the
unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if for every
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}
there is
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq K
such that

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=c_{\alpha},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Similarly, given
h>0
, we say that the
unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if for every
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}
there is
f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq K
such that (
5.1
) holds. The main result of this section is:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a regular closed set and
M
be a weight sequence. Consider the following statements:

(1)

The unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(2)

There is
h>0
such that the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(3)

There is
h>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the space

\mathcal{N}^{M,h}_{n}(K)=\left\{P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\,|\,\sup_{x\in K}\frac{|P(x)|\nu_{M}(hd_{K}(x))}{(1+|x|)^{n}}<\infty\right\}

is finite-dimensional.

Then,
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. If
M
additionally satisfies
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
, then also
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.

Example 5.2
.

Consider the Gevrey sequence
M_{\sigma}
of index
\sigma>1
. Example
4.2
implies that there are
H_{0},H_{1}>0
such that for all
h>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathcal{N}^{\sigma,\frac{H_{1}}{h^{1/(\sigma-1)}}}_{n}(K)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{M_{\sigma},h}_{n}(K)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\sigma,\frac{H_{0}}{h^{1/(\sigma-1)}}}_{n}(K),

where the spaces
\mathcal{N}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}_{n}(K)
,
\varepsilon>0
, are defined in Theorem
1.3
. Hence, Theorem
1.3
follows from Theorem
5.1
with
M=M_{\sigma}
.

Remark 5.3
.

The conditions
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
in Theorem
1.3
are of a technical nature. We need to require them as our method to show the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
relies on them; see Remark
5.11
below for more details. While we believe that this implication should hold without these assumptions, we are unable to prove it in that generality.

The rest of this section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
5.1
. We will show that
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
,
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
, and
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
(under the additional assumptions
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
on
M
). The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
in Theorem
5.1
will be a consequence of Grothendieck’s factorization theorem (see Theorem
5.10
below). The implications
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
and
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
will be shown by applying Eidelheit’s theorem in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
M
be a weight sequence and
h>0
. We endow
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the locally convex topology induced by the system of norms
\{\|\,\cdot\,\|^{M,h}_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where
\|\,\cdot\,\|^{M,h}_{n}
is defined in (
4.3
). Note that
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Fréchet space. For
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
closed we write
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq K\}
and endow this space with the subspace topology induced by
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is also a Fréchet space. If
K
is compact, we write
\mathcal{D}^{M,h}_{K}=\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K}
. Suppose
K
has non-empty interior. Since
M
is non-quasianalytic, the Denjoy-Carleman theorem (see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 4.2]
) implies that
\mathcal{D}^{M,h}_{K}
is non-trivial.

We now consider the analogues of Propositions
3.1
-
3.3
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a non-empty regular closed set,
M
be a weight sequence,
h>0
, and
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

P\equiv 0
.

(2)

P(x)=0
for all
x\in K
.

(3)

It holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx=0,\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K}.

Proof.

The implications
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
are trivial. We now show
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Set

\mathcal{D}^{(M)}_{\overline{B}(0,1)}=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathcal{D}^{M,h}_{\overline{B}(0,1)}.

The Denjoy-Carleman theorem implies that
\mathcal{D}^{(M)}_{\overline{B}(0,1)}
is non-trivial. The result can now be shown in the same way as the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
in Proposition
3.1
, but starting from an element
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{(M)}_{\overline{B}(0,1)}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)dy=1
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a regular closed set,
M
be a weight sequence,
h>0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
P\in\mathcal{N}^{M}_{h,n}(K)
there is
C>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx\right|\leq C\|f\|^{M,h/d}_{n+d+1},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h/d}_{K}.

Proof.

Set
K_{0}=\{x\in\operatorname{int}K\,|\,d(x,\partial K)\leq 1\}
and
m=n+d+1
. By reasoning as in the beginning of proof of Proposition
3.2
, it is clear that it suffices to show that there is
C_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{x\in K_{0}}\frac{|f(x)|(1+|x|)^{m}}{\nu_{M}(hd(x,\partial K))}\leq C_{2}\|f\|^{M,h/d}_{m},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h/d}_{K}.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h/d}_{K}
and
x\in K_{0}
be arbitrary. Pick
\xi\in\partial K
such that
|x-\xi|=d(x,\partial K)
. Note that
f^{(\alpha)}(\xi)=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Hence, Taylor’s formula implies that for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
there is
\gamma_{p}\in\{tx+(1-t)\xi\mid t\in[0,1]\}
such that

f(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|=p}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(\gamma_{p})}{\alpha!}(x-\xi)^{\alpha}.

As
x\in K_{0}
, it holds that
|x-\gamma_{p}|\leq|x-\xi|=d(x,\partial K)\leq 1
. Therefore,

(1+|\gamma_{p}|)^{-m}\leq(1+|\gamma_{p}-x|)^{m}(1+|x|)^{-m}\leq 2^{m}(1+|x|)^{-m}.

Since
\sum_{|\alpha|=p}\frac{1}{\alpha!}=\frac{d^{p}}{p!}
, we obtain that

|f(x)|\leq|x-\xi|^{p}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}\frac{|f^{(\alpha)}(\gamma_{p})|}{\alpha!}\leq 2^{m}\|f\|^{M,h/d}_{m}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{m}}\frac{(hd(x,\partial K))^{p}M_{p}}{p!}.

By taking the infimum over
p
, we find that

|f(x)|\leq 2^{m}\|f\|^{M,h/d}_{m}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{m}}\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(hd(x,\partial K))^{p}M_{p}}{p!}=2^{m}\|f\|^{M,h/d}_{m}\frac{\nu_{M}(hd(x,\partial K))}{(1+|x|)^{m}}.

∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a regular closed set,
M
be a weight sequence satisfying
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
, and
h>0
. There is
k>0
such that for all

.

. It will be more convenient to work with cubes instead of balls. For

.

Lemma 5.8
.

(cf.

and

•

\theta=1
on

•

\displaystyle\|\theta\|^{M,h}_{0}\leq\frac{C}{\nu_{M}(kr)}

Proof.

By

4.3
(1).

Proof of Lemma

.

By a simple translation argument, it suffices to consider the case

(M.2)

. We write

\|P\|_{\infty}=\sup_{y\in Q(0,1)}|P(y)|,\qquad P\in\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}],

and

and

are equivalent. Hence, there is

such that

By Lemma
5.9
, there are
C_{1},k_{0}>0
such that for every

there exists

such that
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho(x-r\lambda)=1
for all

and
\|\rho\|^{M,h_{0}}_{0}\leq C_{1}/\nu_{M}(k_{0}r)

Let

and

be arbitrary. Choose

as above. Note that

|r\lambda|>(1+r)\sqrt{d}\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,Q(r\lambda,r)\cap Q(0,1)=\emptyset,\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

We find that, for all

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\psi(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\rho(y-r\lambda)\psi(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\\
|r\lambda|\leq(1+r)\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y+r\lambda)\rho(y)\psi(y+r\lambda)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\\
|r\lambda|\leq 2\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\frac{(r\lambda)^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P^{(\alpha)}(y)\rho(y)\psi(y+r\lambda)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{4e^{2}\sqrt{d}}{r}\right)^{d}\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\max_{|\alpha|\leq m}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)(\rho(y)\psi(y+r\lambda))^{(\alpha)}dy\right|.

\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

be arbitrary. Note that

By using this inequality and (

\displaystyle\|(\rho(\,\cdot\,)\psi(\,\cdot\,+r\lambda))^{(\alpha)}\|^{M,h}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\rho(y)\psi(y+r\lambda))^{(\alpha+\beta)}|}{h^{|\beta|}M_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{h^{|\beta|}M_{\beta}}\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha+\beta}\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\gamma}|\rho^{(\gamma)}(y)||\psi^{(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)}(y+r\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho\|^{M,h_{0}}_{0}\|\psi\|^{M,h_{0}}_{0}\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{h_{0}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{h^{|\beta|}M_{\beta}}\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha+\beta}\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\gamma}M_{\gamma}M_{\alpha+\beta-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}\|\psi\|^{M,h_{0}}_{0}}{{\nu_{M}(k_{0}r)}}\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{(2h_{0})^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}M_{\alpha+\beta}}{h^{|\beta|}M_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}\|\psi\|^{M,h_{0}}_{0}}{{\nu_{M}(k_{0}r)}}\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{(2h_{0}C_{0})^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}M_{\alpha}}{h^{|\beta|}}

Set

\displaystyle|P(0)|\leq\|P\|_{\infty}\leq C\|P\|

\displaystyle=C\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(y)\psi(y)dy\right|\mid\psi\in\mathcal{D}^{M,h_{0}}_{Q(0,1)},\|\psi\|^{M,h_{0}}_{0}\leq 1\right\}

4.3
(2).

Finally, we present Grothendieck’s factorization theorem.

Theorem 5.10

(Grothendieck’s factorization theorem)
.

[
22
, Theorem 24.33]
Let
E_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be Fréchet spaces,
F
be a Hausdorff locally convex space, and
T_{n}:E_{n}\to F
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be continuous linear mappings. If

F=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T_{n}(E_{n}),

there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T_{n_{0}}:E_{n_{0}}\to F
is surjective.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the continuous linear mappings

T_{n}:\mathcal{S}^{M,n}_{K}\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}},\,f\to\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}.

Since the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T_{n}(\mathcal{S}^{M,n}_{K}).

As
\mathcal{S}^{M,n}_{K}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are Fréchet spaces, Grothendieck’s factorization theorem implies that there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T_{n_{0}}:\mathcal{S}^{M,n_{0}}_{K}\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}
is surjective. This means that the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{M,n_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: Trivial.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
and
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
(under the additional assumptions
(M.2)
and
(M.3)
on
M
):
For
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
and
h>0
we define
e^{\prime}_{P}\in(\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K})^{\prime}
via

\langle e^{\prime}_{P},f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P(x)f(x)dx,\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K}.

Clearly, the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{M,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for every
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{d}}
there exists

such that

\langle e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}},f\rangle=c_{\alpha},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Proposition
5.4
implies that the family
(e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\subseteq(\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K})^{\prime}
is linearly independent.
Consider the following two statements:

(a)

There is
h>0
such that
(\operatorname{span}\{e^{\prime}_{x^{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\})_{\|\,\cdot\,\|^{M,h}_{n}}\subseteq(\mathcal{S}^{M,h}_{K})^{\prime}
is finite-dimensional for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(b)

There is
h>0
such that
\mathcal{N}^{M,h}_{n}(K)
is finite-dimensional for all

.

As

,

, are Fréchet spaces, Eidelheit’s theorem yields that it suffices to show that (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) and (b)
\Rightarrow
(a) (under the additional assumptions
(M.2)
and

on

). This can be done in the same way as in the proof of Theorem

, but now using Propositions
5.5
and

instead Propositions

and

.

6.

In this final section, we give some necessary and sufficient geometric conditions on regular closed sets

such that the unrestricted
K

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a regular closed set.

(1)

If the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then for all

there is

such that for each

\sup_{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in K}|x_{i}|^{l}d_{K}(x)^{k}=\infty.

(2)

Let
M
be a weight sequence. Suppose that the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There are
h,l>0
such that for each

(6.1)

\sup_{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in K}|x_{i}|^{l}\nu_{M}(hd_{K}(x))=\infty.

Moreover, if

additionally satisfies
(M.3)
, then for all
h>0
there is
l>0
such that (
6.1
) holds for all

.

Proof.

We only show (2), as the argument for (1) is similar. Since the unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, Theorem
5.1
implies that there is
h>0
such that
\mathcal{N}^{M,h}_{0}(K)
is finite-dimensional. Hence, there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{N}^{M,h}_{0}(K)\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{m}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. In particular, for each
i=1,\ldots,d
, the polynomial

. This means that

) with

.

M
satisfies

) holds for all

Proposition 6.2
.

Let

be a regular closed set.

(1)

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

K
-moment problem is solvable in

(b)

(2)

Let
M
be a weight sequence satisfying
(M.2)
and

. The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

The unrestricted
K
-moment problem is solvable in
\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R})
.

(b)

There is
l>0
such that

(6.2)

\sup_{x\in K}|x|^{l}\nu_{M}(d_{K}(x))=\infty.

Proof.

We only show (2), as the argument for (1) is similar once we observe that condition (b) in (1) implies that, for all

,

\sup_{x\in K}|x|^{kl}d_{K}(x)^{k}=\infty.

Since

By combining these two inequalities, we find that

Condition (

and thus

.

Let

(1)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(2)

Let

be a weight sequence satisfying
(M.2)
and

k\in\mathbb{N}

We claim that for all
P\in\mathcal{N}^{M,1}_{n}(K)
,

,

Since

, there is
C_{1}>0

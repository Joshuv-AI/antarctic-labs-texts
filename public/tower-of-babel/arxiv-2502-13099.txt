Semigroups, Cartier divisors and convex bodies
Askold Khovanskii

Abstract
The theory of Newton–Okounkov bodies provides direct relations and points out analogies between the theory of mixed volumes of convex bodies, on the one hand, and the intersection theories of Cartier divisors and of Shokurov b b -divisors, on the other hand. The classical inequalities between mixed volumes of convex bodies correspond to inequalities between intersection indices of nef Cartier divisors on an irreducible projective variety and between the birationally invariant intersection indices of nef type Shokurov b b -divisors on an irreducible algebraic variety. Such algebraic inequalities are known as Khovanskii–Teissier inequalities. Our proof of these inequalities is based on simple geometric inequalities on two dimensional convex bodies and on pure algebraic arguments. The classical geometric inequalities follow from the algebraic inequalities. We collected results from a few papers which were published during the last forty five years. Some theorems of the present paper were never stated, but all ideas needed for their proofs are contained in the published papers. So we avoid all heavy proofs. Our goal is to present an overview of the area. Notions of homogeneous polynomials on commutative semigroups and of their polarizations provide an adequate language for discussing the subject. We use this language through the paper.

Introduction

In the paper, we discuss an interplay between convex geometry and algebraic geometry. We focus on analogies and direct relations between
mixed volumes of convex bodies and the intersection index of Cartier divisors on projective varieties and, more generally, the bi-rationally invariant intersection index of Shokurov
b
-divisors on an arbitrary algebraic variety.

There are other analogies between convex and algebraic geometries which deal with special algebraic varieties equipped with actions of reductive groups, like toric and spherical varieties and spherical homogeneous spaces. These special algebraic varieties are related with special convex bodies: with integral convex polyhedra and with special classes of polyhedra like Gelfand–Zetlin polyhedra, string polyhedra and so on. These analogies involve not only volumes but also combinatorics of polyhedra, structure of integral points and other invariants. We will not discuss these relations of convex and algebraic geometry with only one exception: we state and sketch a proof of the BKK (Bernstein–Koushnirenko–Khovanskii) theorem which allows us to provide an algebraic proof of the classical inequalities between mixed volumes of convex bodies.

Commutative semigroups equipped with homogeneous polynomials are basic simple objects which appear, on the one hand, in the theory of Cartier

3.1

Let

Definition 3.1

Let

.
The set

The space

is the identity element in

Lemma 3.1

1.

If

. Let

If

Corollary 3.2

Elements of the group

Definition 3.4

3.4
The Grothendieck group
G(X)
and the group of Shokurov
b
-divisors on
X

A birational isomorphism of varieties
X
and
Y
induces the isomorphism of the groups
G(X)
and
G(Y)
. A projective variety
Y
birationally isomorphic to
X
is called a
projective model of
X
. According to Theorem
3.5
, the group of Cartier divisors
\operatorname{Div}(Y)
of any projective model

of
X
is naturally embedded to the group
G(X)
. One can show that for any finite set of elements
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\}\subset G(X)
there is a projective model
Y
of
X
such that the set belongs to the image of the group
\operatorname{Div}(Y)
(thus, the elements of the set
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\}

can be considered as Cartier divisors in

).

A projective model
Y_{1}
of
X

dominates
a projective model
Y_{2}
of
X
if the birational map
\pi_{1,2}:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
(induced by birational isomorphisms between

and
X
, and between

and

) is regular. The pull-back map

embeds the group
\operatorname{Div}(Y_{2})
to the group
\operatorname{Div}(Y_{1})
. This embedding preserves the intersection index of divisors on
Y_{2}
and
Y_{1}
.

The
group of Shokurov
b
-divisors on

is
the direct limit of groups of Cartier divisors of all projective models of variety

.
The group of Shokurov

-divisors on an irreducible
n
-dimensional variety

inherits the intersection index of any
n
-tuple of elements. The intersection theory of Shokurov

-divisors can be reduced to the classical intersection theory of Cartier divisors on projective varieties. It works for algebraic varieties over arbitrary algebraically closed fields
\mathbb{k}
.

One can see that the group
G(X)
is naturally isomorphic to the group of Shokurov
b
-divisors on
X
. Thus, an intersection index is defined for any
n
-tuple of elements in

.

In
[
3
]
, we constructed such an intersection index on the group

for an irreducible variety
X
over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. We were inspired by the BKK theorem and we did not know about Shokurov
b
-divisors. Our construction does not use the classical intersection theory and is rather visual. Unfortunately, it does not work over arbitrary algebraically closed fields
\mathbb{k}
. In section
5
, we discuss our visual version of the bi-rationally invariant intersection index on

. Using Shokurov

-divisors, one can easily modify the results of section in such a way that they become applicable to an algebraic variety

over an arbitrary algebraically closed field

(see
[
5
]
).

4
Semigroups related to Newton polyhedra theory

In this section, we discuss two semigroups which are naturally related to Newton polyhedra theory.

4.1
Semigroup
\operatorname{Set}_{n}

One can define the sum of two subsets
A,B
in a commutative group
G
as follows:
the set
A+B
is the set of all elements
z\in G
representable as
z=a+b
for some
a\in A
,
b\in B
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
semigroup

\operatorname{Set}_{n}
is the semigroup of all
finite subsets in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the above
addition. The Grothendieck semigroup and the Grothendieck group of
\operatorname{Set}_{n}
are denoted by
G_{s}(\operatorname{Set}_{n})
and
G(\operatorname{Set}_{n})
respectively.

Theorem 4.1
.

The Grothendieck semigroup
G_{s}(\operatorname{Set}_{n})
of
\operatorname{Set}_{n}
is isomorphic to the semigroup of convex integral polyhedra in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\supset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The natural map
\rho:\operatorname{Set}_{n}\to G_{s}(\operatorname{Set}_{n})
sends an element
A\in\operatorname{Set}_{n}
to the convex hull
\Delta(A)
of
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
\rho(A)=\Delta(A)
. Moreover, for
A,B\in\operatorname{Set}_{n}
the identity
\rho(A+B)=\Delta(A+B)=\Delta(A)+\Delta(B)
holds.

A proof of Theorem
4.1
can be found in
[
8
]
.

4.2
Semigroup
K_{n}

Let
X=(\mathbb{k}^{*})^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional torus over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
and let
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the lattice of its characters. With any finite set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, one can associate the finite dimensional space
L_{A}\in K(X)
spanned by characters from the set
A
. The multiplicative group
(\mathbb{k}^{*})^{n}
naturally acts on the field
\mathbb{k}(X)
of rational functions on
X=(\mathbb{k}^{*})^{n}
. The spaces
L_{A}
are exactly finite dimensional spaces invariant under such action.

Definition 4.2
.

The
semigroup

K_{n}
is the sub-semigroup of the semigroup
K(X)
whose elements are the spaces
L_{A}
, where
A
is a finite subset in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

The following lemma is obvious:

Lemma 4.1
.

The map
\tau:K_{n}\to Set_{n}
, which sends the space
L_{A}\in K_{n}
to the set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
which is considered as the element of the
semigroup
\operatorname{Set}_{n}
, provides an isomorphism between semigroups
K_{n}
and
Set_{n}
.

One can check the following lemma (see
[
8
]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

For a finite set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the completion of the space
L_{A}
in the semigroup
K_{n}
is equal to the space
L_{B}
, where
B=\Delta(A)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Lemma
4.2
relates the algebraic operation of completion of a space
L_{A}\in K_{n}
to the geometric operation of taking a convex hull of a subset in a real vector space. Thus, one can consider the operation of completion of spaces in
K(X)
as an analog of the geometric operation of taking the convex hull of a set.

Lemma
4.2
also explains why the convex hull of the set of monomials which appear in a Laurent polynomial

with nonzero coefficients plays such an important role in Newton polyhedra theory.

5
Birationally invariant intersection index

In this section, we discuss an intersection theory
on the semigroup
K(X)
which assigns the intersection index to an
n
-tuple of spaces
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
on an
n
-dimensional irreducible variety
X
. A birational isomorphism
\phi:X\to Y
of varieties
X
and
Y
provides an isomorphism
\phi^{*}:K(Y)\to K(X)
between the semigroups
K(Y)
and
K(X)
which preserves this intersection index.

The Grothendieck group
G(X)
is isomorphic to the group of Shokurov
b
-divisors on
X
(see section
3.4
,
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
). Thus, one can define the intersection index on
K(X)
using the intersection theory of Shokurov
b
-divisors. This approach works for algebraic varieties over an arbitrary algebraically closed field. In this section, we confine our consideration to algebraic varieties over the field
\mathbb{C}
. We introduced the intersection index on the group
G(X)
in
[
3
]
inspired by the BKK theorem, and we did not know about Shokurov
(b)
-divisors. Our approach is visual and elementary. One can generalize the results of this section to algebraic varieties over arbitrary algebraically closed fields using the intersection theory of Shokurov
(b)
-divisors (see
[
5
]
).

5.1
Intersection indices on
K(X)

Let
X
be an irreducible
n
-dimensional variety over
\mathbb{C}
. A
smooth open set

U\subset X
is a Zariski open set whose complement
W=X\setminus U
contains all singular points of
X
(note that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W<n
).

Definition 5.1
.

For any rational vector function
\mathbb{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
on
X
with
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
, and for any smooth open set
U\subset X
, we define the
number
\#\{\mathbb{f}(x)=0|x\in U\}
of simple roots of
\mathbb{f}
in
U
as the number of points
x\in U
such that:

1.

\mathbb{f}
is regular at
x
, and
\mathbb{f}(x)=0
;

2.

the differentials
df_{1},\dots,df_{n}
of components of
\mathbb{f}
are independent on the tangent space to
X
at the point
x
.

Definition 5.2
.

For each space
L\in K(X)
we define:

1.

the
set
P_{L}\subset X
of poles of
L
as the smallest Zariski closed subvariety in
X
on whose complement
X\setminus P_{L}
all functions
f\in L
are regular (or all function from some basis
L
are regular);

2.

the set
B_{L}
of
base points of
L
as the subvariety of
X\setminus P_{L}
on which vanish all functions
f\in L
.

A set
U\subset X
is
admissible
for a finite set
\{L_{j}\}
of spaces
L_{i}\in K(X)
if it is a smooth open set, and its complement
X\setminus U
contains the sets
P_{L_{i}}
and
B_{L_{i}}
of each space
L_{i}\in\{L_{j}\}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
be any
n
-tuple of spaces. Then for a generic vector function
\mathbb{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
such that
f_{i}\in L_{i}
and for any admissible for
\{L_{i}\}
set
U
the number
\#\{\mathbb{f}=0|x\in U\}
depends only on
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
, i.e., it is independent of the choice of a generic vector function
\mathbb{f}
and of an admissible set
U
.

Definition 5.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, the
intersection index of spaces
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
is the the number
\#\{\mathbb{f}=0|x\in U\}
which we denote by
[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]
. We denote by
\operatorname{Deg}(L)
the
self-intersection index
of the space
L\in K(X)
, i.e.,
\operatorname{Deg}(L)=[L,\dots,L]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

The intersection index
[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]
is
n
-polarization of the function
\operatorname{Deg}:K(X)\to\mathbb{Q}
, where
\operatorname{Deg}(L)=[L,\dots,L]
, i.e.:

1.

[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]
is symmetric with respect to permuting the spaces

L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
;

2.

[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]
is linear in each argument with respect to the multiplication.
The linearity in first argument means that for
L_{1}^{\prime},L_{1}^{\prime\prime},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
we have

[L^{\prime}_{1}L^{\prime\prime}_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}]=[L^{\prime}_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}]+[L^{\prime\prime}_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}].

Theorem
5.2
implies that the self-intersection index is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
on the semigroup
K(X)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The intersection index
[L_{1},\dots L_{n}]
is:

1.

nonnegative, i.e.,
[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]\geq 0
;

2.

monotone, i.e.,
if
L_{1}^{\prime}\subset L_{1},\dots,L_{n}^{\prime}\subset L_{n}
, then

[L_{1}^{\prime},\dots,L_{n}^{\prime}]\leq[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]
.

Proof.

1.

The intersection index is a nonnegative integer.

2.

Let
U\subset X
be an admissible set for
\{L_{i}\}
. Then
U
is an admissible set for
\{L_{i}^{\prime}\}
, since
L_{i}^{\prime}\subset L_{i}
.
We have
[L^{\prime}_{1},\dots,L^{\prime}_{n}]=\#\{\mathbb{f}^{\prime}(x)=0|x\in U\}
, where
\mathbb{f}^{\prime}

is a generic vector function
\mathbb{f}^{\prime}=(f^{\prime}_{1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{n})
, such that
f^{\prime}_{i}\in L^{\prime}_{i}
. By a small perturbation of the vector function
\mathbb{f}^{\prime}
, one can obtain a generic vector function
\mathbb{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
, such that
f_{i}\in L_{i}
. The number
\#\{\mathbb{f}^{\prime}(x)=0|x\in U\}

is less than or equal to the number
\#\{\mathbb{f}(x)=0|x\in U\}
. Indeed,
under a small perturbation simple roots cannot disappear (they just move a little bit), but under a small perturbation new simple roots can be born.

∎

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
n
-tuple of spaces
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
and for any
n
-tuple of one-dimensional spaces
L^{\prime}_{1},\dots,L^{\prime}_{n}
we have:

[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]=[L_{1}L^{\prime}_{1},\dots,L_{n}L^{\prime}_{n}].

Proof.

Let us show that if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}L^{\prime}_{1}=1
, then
[L^{\prime}_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}]=0
. Indeed, the space
L^{\prime}_{1}
is invertible, i.e., there is
L^{\prime\prime}_{1}
such that
L^{\prime}_{1}L^{\prime\prime}_{1}=\mathbb{1}
. Thus,

[L^{\prime}_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}]+[L^{\prime\prime}_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}]=0.

The sum of two nonnegative numbers is equal to zero only if both of them are equal to zero.
Now the lemma follows from the multi-linearity of the intersection index.
∎

The following theorem follows immediately from definitions:

Theorem 5.4

(self-intersection index and degrees)
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
be a Zariski closure of the image
\Phi_{L}(X)
of
X
under the Kodaira map
\Phi_{L}:X\to\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
. Then:

1.

if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y<n
, them
\operatorname{Deg}(L)=0
;

2.

if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y=n
, then
\operatorname{Deg}(L)=d(\Phi_{L})d(Y)
, where
d(\Phi_{L})
is the mapping degree of the map
\Phi_{L}:X\to Y
(i.e., is equal to
the degree of the field extension
\Phi_{L}^{*}\mathbb{C}(Y)\subset\mathbb{C}(X)
), and
d(Y)
is the degree of the subvariety
Y
in the projective space
\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
.

5.2

In this section, we present explicit formulas for the intersection index on

Lemma follows from Theorem

Theorem 5.5
.

The following formula holds:

[\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{I}(-1)^{|J|}\operatorname{Deg}\bigg(\prod_{i\in I}L_{i}\prod_{j\in J}L^{\prime}_{j}\bigg).

(5)

Theorem
5.5
follows from Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 5.1
.

The following formula holds:

\operatorname{Deg}(\mathcal{L})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}(-1)^{n-k}{n\choose k}\operatorname{Deg}(L^{k}(L^{\prime})^{n-k}).

(6)

Corollary
5.1
follows from Corollary
2.1
.

5.3
Intersection index of Cartier divisors

Let
X
be an irreducible projective variety and let
\operatorname{Div}(X)
be the additive group of Cartier divisors on
X
. According to the classical intersection theory of Cartier divisors, the
intersection index
is defined. It assigns to an
n
-tuple of divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}\in\operatorname{Div}(X)
an integer
[D_{1},\dots,D_{n}]
which is symmetric under permutation of divisors and multi-linear. The intersection index
[D_{1},\dots,D_{n}]
is the
n
-polarization of the function
\operatorname{Deg}:\operatorname{Div}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}
which assigns to a divisor
D
its self-intersection index
\operatorname{Deg}(D)=[D,\dots,D]
.
The group
\operatorname{Div}(X)

is the Grothendieck group of the semigroup
\operatorname{Div}_{free}(X)
of base-points free Cartier divisors, and the semigroup
\operatorname{Div}_{free}(X)
is isomorphic to the semigroup
K_{reg}(X)
(see Theorem
3.5
).

Theorem 5.6

(self-intersection on
\operatorname{Div}_{free}(X)
and degrees)
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{Div}_{free}(X)
,
L=L(D)
and let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
be the image
\Phi_{L}(X)
of
X
under the Kodaira map
\Phi_{L}:X\to\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
. Then:

1.

if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y<n
, then
\operatorname{Deg}(D)=0
;

2.

if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y=n
, then
\operatorname{Deg}(D)=d(\Phi_{L})d(Y)
, where
d(\Phi_{L})
is the mapping degree of the map
\Phi_{L}:X\to Y
and
d(Y)
is the degree of the subvariety
Y
in the projective space
\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the divisor
D
is linearly equivalent to the pull-back divisor
\Phi_{L}^{*}(H)
, where
H
is a divisor on
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
obtained by a hyperplane section. A self intersection of
D
is equal to the number of pre-images in
X
of points of intersection of
Y
with a generic projective space of codimension
n
. The number of such intersection points is equal to the degree
d(Y)
of projective subvariety
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(L^{*})
. The number of preimages of each point of such intersection is equal to the mapping degree
d(\Phi_{L})
of the map
\Phi_{L}:X\to Y
. Theorem is proven.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

The self-intersection index

of a divisor

is equal to the self-intersection index

Proof.

By Theorems
5.4
and
5.6
, the functions
\operatorname{Deg}(L)
and
\operatorname{Deg}(D(L))
are given by identical formulas.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

The isomorphism

between the group

6
Convex bodies and their mixed volumes

Consider an
n
-dimensional real vector space
\mathbb{L}_{n}
equipped with a volume form invariant under parallel translations. The volume of a measurable set
\Delta\subset\mathbb{L}_{n}
is denoted by
V(\Delta)
. The collection
S
of all compact convex bodies in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
form an additive semigroup with respect to Minkowski addition. The semigroup
S
has the cancellation property. The convex body
0
consisting of the origin in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
is the identity element in
S
. This semigroup
S
is torsion free.The Grothendieck semigroup
G_{s}(S)
is naturally identified with
S
. The natural map
\tau:S\to G(S)
from
S
to its Grothendieck group
G(S)
is an embedding. Elements of the group
G(S)
(which could be considered as the formal differences of convex bodies) are called
virtual convex bodies
.

The function
V:S\to\mathbb{R}
which assigns to each convex body
\Delta
its volume
V(\Delta)
is a degree
n
homogeneous polynomial on the semigroup
S
: Minkowski discovered that for any
m
-tuple
\Delta=(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{m})
of convex bodies
\Delta_{i}\in S
the function
V_{\Delta}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})=V(\lambda_{1}\Delta_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}\Delta_{m})
is a degree
n
homogeneous polynomial on
m
-tuples
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
of nonnegative real numbers
\lambda_{i}
(in particular, it is a homogeneous degree
n
polynomial on
m
-tuples
(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
of nonnegative integers
k_{i}
).

Corollary 6.1
.

The function
V:S\to\mathbb{R}
on the semigroup of convex bodies, which assigns to
\Delta\in S
its volume, can be extended as a homogeneous degree
n
polynomial on the additive group of virtual convex bodies.

Definition 6.1
.

The
mixed volume

MV_{n}
of an
n
-tuple of convex bodies is the
n
-polarization of the volume function
V:S\to\mathbb{R}
evaluated on the given n-tuple.

Lemma 6.1
.

The mixed volume
MV_{n}
as a function on
n
-tuples of convex bodies
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
,
\Delta_{i}\subset\mathbb{L}_{n}
, has the following properties:

1.

(Relation with volume). On the diagonal it coincides with the volume, i.e., if
\Delta_{1}=\dots=\Delta_{n}=\Delta
, then

MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})
is the volume
V_{n}(\Delta)
of
\Delta
.

2.

(Symmetry).
MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})
is symmetric with respect to permuting the bodies

\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
.

3.

(Multi-linearity). It is linear in each argument with respect to the
Minkowski sum. The linearity in the first argument means that for convex bodies
\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\Delta_{1}^{\prime\prime},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n}
we have:

MV_{n}(\Delta^{\prime}_{1}+\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n})=MV_{n}(\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots+\Delta_{n})+MV_{n}(\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n}).

4.

(Homogeneity). It is linear homogeneous in each argument with respect to multiplication by nonnegative reals. The homogeneity in the first argument means that for convex bodies
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n}
and nonnegative real number
\lambda\geq 0
we have

MV_{n}(\lambda\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n})=\lambda MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n})
.

5.

(Expression in terms of volumes).

MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}}(-1)^{n-k}V(\Delta_{i_{1}}+\dots+\Delta_{i_{k}})\right).

(7)

Proof.

Claims (1) – (3) follow from the general properties of
n
-polarizations. Statements (4) due to Minkowski. Statement (5) follows from Corollary
2.1
.
∎

If
\Delta\subset\mathbb{L}_{n}
is a convex body and
a\in\mathbb{L}_{n}
is a point, then
\Delta+a
is obtained from
\Delta
by the parallel translation by the vector
a
. Since the volume form on
\mathbb{L}_{n}
is invariant under parallel translations, the following corollary from relation (
7
) holds:

Corollary 6.2
.

For any
n
-tuple of convex bodies
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
and any
n
vectors
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
we have:

MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})=MV_{n}(\Delta_{1}+a_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}+a_{n})

The following inequalities involving mixed volumes are easy to prove.

Theorem 6.1
.

The mixed volume
MV_{n}
is:

1.

nonnegative, i.e., the mixed volume of any
n
-tuple
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
of convex bodies is nonnegative;

2.

monotone, i.e.,
if
\Delta_{1}^{\prime}\subset\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}^{\prime}\subset\Delta_{n}
, then

MV_{n}(\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\Delta_{n}^{\prime})\leq MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}).

We conclude this section with a geometric example of mixed volume which uses non only the volume form on
\mathbb{L}_{n}
, but also a Euclidean structure on it.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
B
be a unit ball centered at the origin in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
. Then for any convex body
\Delta\subset L_{n}
the mixed volume
MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})
, where
\Delta_{1}=\dots=\Delta_{n-1}=\Delta
and
\Delta_{n}=B
, is equal to the
(n-1)
-dimensional Euclidean volume of the boundary
\partial\Delta
of
\Delta
multiplied by
\frac{1}{n}
.

6.1
Geometric inequalities involving mixed volumes

Mixed volumes appear in many geometric inequalities. In this section, we state some of them. Probably, the famous Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality (see below) is the most important. The classical isoperimetric inequality which was known to ancient Greeks is a very special case of that inequality. We state the Minkowski criterion of vanishing of mixed volumes and the Brunn–Minkowski inequality on volumes of convex bodies.

Definition 6.2
.

A set
\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{k}\}
of
k

convex bodies in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
elements is affinely dependent if for some nonempty subset
J\in\{1,\dots,k\}
the body
\Delta_{J}=\sum_{j\in J}\Delta_{j}
has dimension smaller than the number
|J|
of elements in the set
J
.

Theorem 6.2

(Minkowski criterion)
.

Let
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{k}
be a set of
k\leq n
convex
bodies in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
.
Then the mixed volume
MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{k},\Delta_{k+1},\dots,\Delta_{n})
vanishes
for any collection
\Delta_{k+1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
of convex bodies in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
if and only if the set

\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{k}
is affine dependent.

Theorem 6.3

(Brunn–Minkowski inequality)
.

If
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
are convex bodies in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
, then

V^{\frac{1}{n}}(\Delta_{1})+V^{\frac{1}{n}}(\Delta_{2})\leq V^{\frac{1}{n}}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}).

(8)

The Brunn–Minkowski inequality has visual geometric proofs. It was discovered by Brunn in an equivalent, but very different form. Minkowski found its present form and described conditions under which the inequality becomes an equality.

Theorem 6.4

(Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality)
.

For any
n
-tuple of convex bodies

\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{n}
in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
the following inequality holds:

MV_{n}^{2}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3},\dots,\Delta_{n})\leq MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{3},\dots,\Delta_{n})\times MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{3},\dots,\Delta_{n}).

(9)

The Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality is one of the most general inequalities between mixed volumes of convex bodies. It has many corollaries. Below are two examples.

Corollary 6.3
.

For any
2\leq m\leq n
and for any
n
-tuple of convex bodies
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
we have:

\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}MV_{n}(\Delta_{i},\dots,\Delta_{i},\Delta_{m+1},\dots,\Delta_{n})\leq MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})^{m}.

(10)

For
m=2
, inequality (
10
) coincides with the inequality (
9
). For
m=n
, inequality (
10
) is symmetric in convex bodies
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
.

Corollary 6.4
.

For any
2\leq m\leq n
and for any collection of convex bodies
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
,
\Delta_{m+1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
we have:

\displaystyle MV_{n}^{\frac{1}{m}}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{1},\Delta_{m+1},\dots,\Delta_{n})+MV_{n}^{\frac{1}{m}}(\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{2},\Delta_{m+1},\dots,\Delta_{n})

(11)

\displaystyle\leq MV_{n}^{\frac{1}{m}}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},\dots,\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},\Delta_{m+1},\dots,\Delta_{n}).

For
m=n
, inequality (
11
) coincides with inequality (
8
). Thus, the Brunn–Minkowski inequality is a corollary of the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality. On the other hand, all known geometric proofs of the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality are based on the Brunn–Minkowski inequality (all of these proofs are rather tricky). In fact, Theorem
6.3
and Theorem
6.4
follow from algebraic versions of these theorems (see section
8
). Besides pure algebraic arguments, our proof of these algebraic versions makes use of Theorem
6.3
for the case
n=2
. The case
n=2
is both very special and simple algebraically and geometrically. We focus on it in the next section.

6.2
Inequalities in dimension two

The area of a convex body in the standard plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree two on the semigroup of planer convex bodies. We show below that
a homogeneous polynomial
Q:S\to\mathbb{R}
of degree two on any semigroup
S
satisfies the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality for
x,y\in S
if and only if it satisfies the Alexandrov–Fenchel type inequality for
x,y\in S
(see Theorem
6.5
).
In addition, the study of homogeneous polynomials of degree two on a semigroup
S
can be reduced to the study of quadratic forms on the real vector space
G(S)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
Q:S\to\mathbb{R}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree two, let
B:S^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be its
2
-polarization. Then for a pair of points
x,y\in S
the polynomial
Q
satisfies:

1.

the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality if
Q(x)\geq 0
,
Q(y)\geq 0
,
Q(x+y)\geq 0
and

Q^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)+Q^{\frac{1}{2}}(y)\leq Q^{\frac{1}{2}}(x+y);

(12)

2.

the Alexandrov–Fenchel type inequality if
Q(x)\geq 0
,
Q(y)\geq 0
,
B(x,y)\geq 0
and

Q(x)Q(y)\leq B^{2}(x,y).

(13)

The following theorem is easy to prove:

Theorem 6.5
.

A homogeneous polynomial of degree two satisfies the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality for
x,y\in S
if and only if it
satisfies the Alexandrov–Fenchel type inequality for
x,y\in S
.

Proof.

Let us show that if
Q
satisfies the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality for
x,y\in S
, then it satisfies the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality for
x
and
y
.
Indeed, by squaring both sides of (
12
), we obtain
Q(x)+2Q(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}Q(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}+Q(y)\leq Q(x)+2B(x,y)+Q(y)
, or
Q(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}Q(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq B(x,y)
. Thus,
B(x,y)
is nonnegative. By squaring both sides of the previous inequality, we obtain
Q(x)Q(y)\leq B^{2}(x,y)
. Theorem is proven in one direction. Its proof in the opposite direction is similar.
∎

A homogeneous polynomial
Q
on
S
can be induced from a homogeneous polynomial on a real vector space
S_{\mathbb{R}}=G(S)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. The inequality
B^{2}(x,y)\geq Q(x)Q(y)
for two independent vectors
x,y\in S_{\mathbb{R}}
means that a restriction of the quadratic form
Q
to the plane spanned by
x
and
y
can not be positive or negative definite. If
Q(x)>0
at some point
x\in S
, then
Q(y)\leq 0
on the hyperplane in
S_{\mathbb{R}}
orthogonal to
x
, i.e.,
B(x,y)=0
.

Our proofs of the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities for mixed volumes and of their version in intersection theory are based on the Brunn–Minkowski inequality for 2-dimensional convex bodies.
This inequality can be proved using two different approaches.

Firstly, the Brunn–Minkowski inequality in any dimension is not hard to prove. One can prove for
n=2
.

Secondly, one can prove the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality for
n=2
directly, slightly modifying one of the classical proofs of the isoperimetric inequality (see the proof of Theorem A in
[
10
]
).

By Theorem
6.5
, the Brunn–Minkowski inequality for
n=2
follows from the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality.

Let us return to classical geometry and prove the isoperimetric inequality.

Corollary 6.5
.

The area
V(\Delta)
of a planar convex body
\Delta
satisfies the following inequality:

V(\Delta)\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}l(\partial\Delta)^{2},

where
l(\partial\Delta)
is the length of the boundary of
\Delta
. Moreover, if
\Delta
is a ball, then the inequality becomes equality.

Proof.

Corollary follows from Proposition
6.1
and from inequality (
9
) for
n=2
;
\Delta_{1}=\Delta
;
\Delta_{2}=B_{1}
, where
B_{1}
is the unit ball.
∎

7
Newton–Okounkov bodies and intersection theory

The theory of Newton–Okounkov bodies (or, shortly, of NO bodies) is based on the theory of semigroups of lattice points, i.e., of sub-semigroups of the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(see
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
,
[
11
]

For any faithful

-valuation on the field of rational functions on
n
-dimensional irreducible variety

a convex body

and the volume of its NO body

7.1
NO bodies of elements of the semigroup
K(X)

Consider the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, equipped with a total order
\succ
, which is compatible with the addition (i.e., if
a\succ b
, then
a+c>b+c
). For example, one can consider the lexicographic order.

A
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-valuation

v

\mathbb{k}(X)
of rational functions on an irreducible
n
-dimensional variety
X
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
is

if the valuation map

v:\mathbb{k}(X)\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-valuation
v

on
n
-dimensional irreducible variety

over
\mathbb{C}
.

Example 7.1
.

Let
a

, such that
x_{1}(a)=\dots=x_{n}(a)=0

\mathbb{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

is the smallest monomial in the lexicographic order which appears with nonzero coefficient in the Taylor series of the function

at the point

. The valuation
v

v(f)-v(g)
.

Elements
\{L(i)\}

of the semigroup

form a

if for any natural

L(n)L(k)\subset L(n+k)

[

with respect to the chosen valuation

The lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
endows the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the volume form invariant under the parallel translations. The measure of a convex body
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

We assume below that a faithful valuation

on
\mathbb{k}(X)
is chosen and fixed.

Definition 7.1
.

The

For the purposes of the paper, the most important result of

Theorem 7.1

(NO boby of

)

The following relations hold:

for any

for any

7.2
The BKK theorem and formulas for the intersection index

In this section, we present a proof of the BKK theorem based on NO bodies. We also present formulas expressing the intersection index via NO bodies. These results are based on the first statement of the Theorem
7.1
.

Koushnirenko’s theorem is a very particular case of the first statement of Theorem
7.1
related to the semigroup
K_{n}
of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-invariant spaces on the
complex torus
X=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
(see section
4.2
). Let
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the lattice of characters of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. With any finite set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
one can associate the finite dimensional space
L_{A}\in K(X)
spanned by the characters from the set
A
.
By definition, the
Newton polyhedron

\Delta(A)
of

is the convex hull of the set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 7.2

(Koushnirenko’s theorem)
.

For
n
generic functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in L_{A}
the number of solutions in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
of the system
f_{1}=\dots=f_{n}=0
is equal to
n!V(\Delta(A))
.

For reducing Theorem
7.2
to Theorem
7.1
, it is enough to present an example of a faithful
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-valuation
v
on the field
\mathbb{C}(X)
for
X=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
, such that the NO body of each space
L_{A}
with respect to

is equal to
\Delta(A)
. Easy to see that such equalities hold if and only if for each character

\mathbb{x}^{\mathbb{m}}
the equality
v(\mathbb{x}^{\mathbb{m}})=\mathbb{m}
holds.

Example 7.2
.

The torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
is bi-rationally equivalent to the affine space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Consider the valuation
v

from Example
7.1
in which the point
a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is the standard system of coordinates. Obviously, for any monomial
\mathbb{x}^{\mathbb{m}}
the identity
v(\mathbb{x}^{\mathbb{m}})=\mathbb{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
holds.

Thus, Koushnirenko’s theorem is proven.

Remark 7.1
.

One can prove Theorem
7.2
using the similar arguments directly, without extending a valuation to all rational functions on the torus. The proof is based on a simpler version of the first statement of Theorem
7.1
which was discovered earlier
[
8
]
.

Let us show that the intersection index of elements from the semigroup
K(X)
can always be expressed via NO bodies of elements of
K(X)
.

Theorem 7.3
.

The intersection index of
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
can be expressed using volume of NO bodies:

[L_{1},\dots,L_{n}]=\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\Bigg(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}}(-1)^{n-k}V(\Delta(L_{i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot L_{i_{k}}))\Bigg).

Proof.

Theorem
7.3
follows from Theorem
7.1
and Lemma
5.2
.
∎

The BKK theorem is a very special case of Theorem
7.3
. Below we use notations introduced before Theorem
7.2
.
Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
be an
n
-tuple of finite subsets in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let
L_{A_{1}},\dots,L_{A_{n}}
be the corresponding
n
-tuple of elements
K(X)
for
X=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
.

Theorem 7.4

(BKK theorem)
.

For
n
generic functions
f_{1}\in L_{A_{1}},\dots,f_{n}\in L_{A_{n}}
the number of solutions in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
of the system
f_{1}=\dots=f_{n}=0
is equal to

n!MV_{n}(\Delta(A_{1}),\dots,\Delta(A_{n})).

Proof.

Let us apply Theorem
7.3
to the valuation
v
from Example
7.2
. We have:

[L_{A_{1}},\dots,L_{A_{n}}]=\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\Bigg(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}}(-1)^{n-k}V(\Delta(L_{A_{i_{1}}}\cdot\ldots\cdot L_{A_{i_{k}}}))\Bigg).

Note that for any finite sets
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
the identities
\Delta(L_{A})=\Delta(A)
and
\Delta(L_{A}L_{B})=\Delta(A+B)
hold. Thus, we have

[L_{A_{1}},\dots,L_{A_{n}}]=\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\Bigg(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}}(-1)^{n-k}V(\Delta(A_{i_{1}})+\dots+\Delta(A_{i_{k}}))\Bigg).

By formula (
2
), for the polarization applied of the function
n!V
, we obtain the desired identity.
∎

A simpler version of the same proof of the BKK theorem, which does not use Theorem
7.1
, can be found in
[
8
]
.

Let us present formulas expressing the intersection index of virtual spaces and self-intersection index for virtual spaces via NO bodies

Theorem 7.5
.

For
\mathcal{L}_{1}=L_{1}/L^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}=L_{n}/L^{\prime}_{n}
, the following formula holds:

[\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]=\sum_{I}(-1)^{|J|}V\Bigg(\Delta\Bigg(\prod_{i\in I}L_{i}\prod_{j\in J}L^{\prime}_{j}\Bigg)\Bigg).

(14)

Theorem

follows from Theorem

.

Corollary 7.1
.

For
\mathcal{L}=L_{1}/L_{2}
, the following formula holds:

D(\mathcal{L})=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}(-1)^{n-k}{n\choose k}V(\Delta(L_{1}^{k}L_{2}^{n-k})).

(15)

Corollary
7.1
follows from Corollary
2.1
.

7.3
NO bodies and geometric type inequalities for the semigroup
K(X)

Newton–Okounkov bodies allow us to prove inequalities for the birationally invariant intersection index on the semigroup
K(X)
on an irreducible
n
-dimensional algebraic variety
X
(possibly non complete and singular). Proofs of the inequalities presented below are based on the second statement of Theorem
7.1
and on the Brunn–Minkowski inequality from convex geometry (in fact, in proofs one can use the Brunn–Minkowski inequality only for two dimensional convex bodies).

Theorem 7.6

(Brunn–Minkowski type inequality)
.

Let
X
be an irreducible
n
-dimensional variety,
L_{1},L_{2}
be arbitrary elements in
K(X)
and let
L_{3}
be their product,
L_{3}=L_{1}L_{2}
. Then the following inequality holds:

[L_{1},\dots,L_{1}]^{1/n}+[L_{2},\dots,L_{2}]^{1/n}\leq[L_{3},\dots,L_{3}]^{1/n}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta(L_{1})
,
\Delta(L_{2})
, and
\Delta(L_{3})
be the NO bodies of the spaces
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
and
L_{3}=L_{1}L_{2}
. By Theorem
7.1
, we have the following inclusion:
\Delta(L_{1})+\Delta(L_{2})\subset\Delta(L_{3})
. By the Brunn–Minkovsky inequality, we have
V^{1/n}(\Delta(L_{1}))+V^{1/n}(\Delta(L_{2}))\leq V^{1/n}(\Delta(L_{3}))
. By Theorem
7.1
for
i=1,2,3
, we have
[L_{i},\dots L_{i}]=n!V(\Delta(L_{i}))
. These relations imply the theorem.
∎

Theorem 7.7

(Hodge type inequality)
.

Let
X
be an irreducible algebraic surface, let
L_{1},L_{2}
be arbitrary elements in
K(X)
and let
L_{3}
be their product,
L_{3}=L_{1}L_{2}
. Then the following inequality holds:

[L_{1},L_{1}][L_{2},L_{2}]\leq[L_{1},L_{2}]^{2}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.6
,
[L_{1},L_{1}]^{1/2}+[L_{2},L_{2}]^{1/2}\leq[L_{1}L_{2}]^{1/2}
. By Theorem
6.5
, this inequality is quadrivalent to the inequality
[L_{1},L_{1}][L_{2},L_{2}]\leq[L_{1},L_{2}]^{2}
.
∎

Note that in Theorems
7.6
and
7.7
the irreducibility assumption can not be dropped: for reducible surfaces the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality and the Hodge type inequality in general do not hold.

Recall that an element
L\subset K(X)
is very big if the Kodaira
rational map
\Phi_{L}
provides a birational isomorphism between
X
and its image
Y=\Phi_{L}(X)
.

Lemma 7.1

(Algebraic version of Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality)
.

If
L_{3},\dots,L_{n}
are very big subspaces, then

[L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},\dots,L_{n}]^{2}\geq[L_{1},L_{1},L_{3},\dots,L_{n}][L_{2},L_{2},L_{3},\dots,L_{n}].

Let us sketch a proof of Lemma
7.1
(more complete presentation can be found in
[
4
]
).
Let
U\subset X
be an admissible set for
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
and let
\mathbb{f}=(f_{3},\dots,f_{n})
be a generic vector function such that
f_{3}\in L_{3},\dots,f_{n}\in L_{n}
. Then the variety
X_{\mathbb{f}}
defined in
U
by
f_{3}=\dots=f_{n}=0
is a smooth surface.

The assumption that the spaces
L_{3},\dots,L_{n}
are very big allows us to apply the Bertini–Lefschetz theorem on irreducibility and to show that the surface
X_{\mathbb{f}}
is irreducible. Lemma
7.1
follows from the Hodge type inequality for the restriction of spaces
L_{1},L_{2}
to the irreducible surface
X_{\mathbb{f}}
.

Remark 7.2
.

In Lemma
7.1
, the assumption that the spaces
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
are very big can be dropped (see Corollary
7.4
). Note that Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
7.8
can be proven using only the Brunn–Minkowski inequality in dimension two. All other geometric type inequalities follow from inequality (
16
).

7.4
Inequalities for nef type elements in the group
G(X)

In the Grothendieck group
G(X)
of the semigroup
K(X)
, one can define nef elements. For such elements the Alexandrov–Fenshel type inequality holds. So all inequalities from convex geometry that follow from the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality (see Corollaries
6.3
and
6.4
) also hold for nef elements in
G(X)
.

Definition 7.2
.

An element
\mathcal{L}
of the Grothendieck group
G(X)
is

1.

a very big element
if it can be represented by a very big element element
L\in K(X)
;

2.

a big element
if there is a natural
k
such that
\mathcal{L}^{k}
is a very big element;

3.

a nef type element
if there is a big element
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
such that for every natural
q
the element
\mathcal{L}^{q}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is big.

Lemma 7.2
.

Very big elements, big elements and nef type elements in the Grothendieck group
G(X)
form sub-semigroups.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are represented by very big spaces
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2}
is represented by a very big space
L_{1}L_{2}
.
If
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{k}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{m}
are very big elements, then
(\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2})^{km}
is a very big element. If
\mathcal{L}^{q}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{q}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\prime}
are big elements, then
(\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2})^{q}(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\prime})
are big elements.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Any element
L
in the semigroup
K(X)
represents a nef type element in its Grothendieck group
G(X)
.

Proof.

If
L,L^{\prime}\in K(X)
and
L^{\prime}
is a very big space, then for any natural
q
the product
L^{q}L^{\prime}
is also a very big space.
∎

Let us prove the Alexandrov–Fenchel type inequality for nef type elements in
G(X)
.

Theorem 7.8
.

If
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
are
nef type elements in the group
G(X)
, then

[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]^{2}\geq[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}][\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}].

(16)

Proof.

If each
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is a very big element in
G(X)
, then theorem follows from Lemma
7.1
, since the intersection index respects the equivalence
\sim
in
K(X)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
are big elements, i.e., for some natural
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}
the elements
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{k_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}^{k_{n}}
are very big elements. Put
k=k_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot k_{n}
. Then elements
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{k},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}^{k}
are very big spaces. Thus, for the
n
-tuple of spaces
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{k},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}^{k}
inequality (
16
) holds. It implies inequality (
16
) for the
n
-tuple
\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
, since the intersection index is multi-linear.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are nef type elements, i.e, there are big elements
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\prime}
such that for any natural
q
all elements
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{q}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\prime}
are big. Put
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\prime}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\prime}
. Then all elements
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{q}\mathcal{L}
are big.
Instead of elements

\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{n}
,
let us consider the elements
\mathcal{L}_{1}(q)=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{q}\mathcal{L}

,\dots
,
\mathcal{L}_{n}(q)=\mathcal{L}_{n}^{q}\mathcal{L}

for every natural
q
. For the element
\mathcal{L}_{1}(q),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}(q)
the Alexandrov–Fenchel type inequality holds, i.e., for every natural
q
, we have
A(q)\geq B(q)
, where
A(q)
and
B(q)
are the following polynomials in
q
:

A(q)=[\mathcal{L}_{1}(q),\mathcal{L}_{2}(q),\mathcal{L}_{3}(q),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}(q)]^{2};

B(q)=[\mathcal{L}_{1}(q),\mathcal{L}_{1}(q),\mathcal{L}_{3}(q),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}(q)][\mathcal{L}_{2}(q),\mathcal{L}_{2}(q),\mathcal{L}_{3}(q),\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}(q)].

The polynomials
A(q)
and
B(q)
have the following leading terms
A_{2n}
,
B_{2n}
:

A_{2n}=q^{2n}[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]^{2};

B_{2n}=q^{2n}[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}][\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{3},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}].

The inequalities
A(q)\geq B(q)
imply that
A_{2n}\geq B_{2n}
which proves inequality (
16
).
∎

Corollaries
6.3
and
6.4
provide examples of statements which formally follow from the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality, thus, analogous to these corollaries held in the intersection theory.

Corollary 7.2
.

For any
2\leq m\leq n
and for any
n
-tuple of nef type elements
\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}\in G(X)
, we have:

\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}[\mathcal{L}_{i},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{i},\mathcal{L}_{m+1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]\leq[\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]^{m}.

(17)

For
m=2
, inequality (
17
) coincides with inequality (
16
). For
m=n
, inequality (
17
) is symmetric in nef type elements
\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
.

Corollary 7.3
.

For any
2\leq m\leq n
and for any collection of nef type elements
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
,
\mathcal{L}_{m+1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}
, we have:

\displaystyle{}^{\frac{1}{m}}+[\mathcal{L}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{m+1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]^{\frac{1}{m}}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq[\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{m+1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n}]^{\frac{1}{m}}.

For
m=n
, inequality (
21
)
provides the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality for nef type elements in
G(X)
.

Corollary 7.4
.

The inequalities (
16
), (
17
) and (
21
) hold for every elements
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
,
since they represent nef type elements in the group
G(X)
. Thus, Lemma
7.1
holds for any
n
-tuple of spaces
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\in K(X)
(without the assumption that these spaces are very big spaces).

7.5
Inequalities for nef Cartier divisors

Let
X
be an irreducible projective
n
-dimensional variety.

Definition 7.3
.

A divisor
D\in\operatorname{Div}(X)
is

nef divisor
if there is an ample divisor
D
such that for every natural
q
the divisor
qD+D^{\prime}
is ample.

One can show that very ample divisors, ample divisors and nef divisors form additive sub-semigroups in the group
\operatorname{Div}(X)
of Cartier divisors.

Lemma 7.4
.

Any base-points free Cartier divisor
D
is a nef divisor.

Proof.

If
D,D^{\prime}
are base-points free divisors, and
D^{\prime}
is a very ample divisor, then for any natural
q
the divisor
qD+D^{\prime}
is also a very ample divisor.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

The natural homomorphism
\mathcal{L}:\operatorname{Div}(X)\to G(X)
maps the semigroups of very ample divisors, ample divisors and nef divisors respectively to the semigroups of very big elements, big elements and nef type elements.

Proof.

If the Kodaira map
\Phi_{L(D)}
is an embedding, then
\Phi_{L(D)}
provides a birational isomorphism between
X
and
\Phi_{L(D)}(X)
.
If a divisor
kD
is very ample, then the element
\mathcal{L}(D)^{k}\in G(X)
can be represented by a very big space, thus,
\mathcal{L}(D)
is a big element in
G(X)
. If a divisor
D
is nef, then for some ample divisor
D^{\prime}
for any
q>0
the divisor
qD+D^{\prime}
is ample, thus, the element
\mathcal{L}(qD+D^{\prime})=(\mathcal{L}(D))^{q}\mathcal{L}(D^{\prime})
is a big element in
G(X)
. Since
\mathcal{L}(D^{\prime})
is a big element in
G(X)
, it implies that
\mathcal{L}(D)
is a nef type element in
G(X)
.
∎

Since the homomorphism
\mathcal{L}:\operatorname{Div}(X)\to G(X)
preserves the intersection indices, we obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 7.9
.

If
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3},\dots,D_{n}
are
nef Cartier divisors on an irreducible projective variety
X
, then the following inequality holds:

[D_{1},D_{2},D_{3},\dots,D_{n}]^{2}\geq[D_{1},D_{1},D_{3},\dots,D_{n}][D_{2},D_{2},D_{3},\dots,D_{n}].

(19)

Corollary 7.5
.

For any
2\leq m\leq n
and for any
n
-tuple of nef Cartier divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}\in\operatorname{Div}(X)
, we have:

\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}[D_{i},\dots,D_{i},D_{m+1},\dots,D_{n}]\leq[D_{1},\dots,D_{n}]^{m}.

(20)

For
m=2
, inequality (
20
) provides the Alexandrov–Fenchel type inequality (
19
) for nef Cartier divisors. For
m=n
, inequality (
20
) is symmetric in nef Cartier divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
.

Corollary 7.6
.

For any
2\leq m\leq n
and for any collection of nef Cartier divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
,
D_{m+1},\dots,D_{n}
, we have:

\displaystyle{}^{\frac{1}{m}}+[D_{2},\dots,D_{2},D_{m+1},\dots,D_{n}]^{\frac{1}{m}}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq[D_{1}+D_{2},\dots,D_{1}+D_{2},D_{m+1},\dots,D_{n}]^{\frac{1}{m}}.

For
m=n
, inequality (
21
)
provides the Brunn–Minkowski type inequality for nef Cartier divisors.

8
Algebraic proofs of geometric inequalities

The Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality for convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
automatically follows from the corresponding algebraic inequality (see
[
7
]
).

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
n
-tuple of integral polyhedra
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality (
9
) holds.

Proof.

Let
A_{i}
be the intersection of
\Delta_{i}
with the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let
L_{A_{i}}
be the spaces corresponding to the sets
A_{i}
. By the BKK theorem (see Theorem
7.4
), the intersection index
[L_{A_{1}},\dots,L_{A_{n}}]
is equal to
n!MV_{n}(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})
. Thus, the lemma follows from Corollary
7.4
.
∎

Theorem 8.1
.

The Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality (
9
) holds for any
n
-tuple of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.1
, the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality holds for integral polyhedra. Since volume is a homogeneous polynomial on the cone of convex bodies, the inequality also holds for polyhedra with rational vertices. Any convex body can be approximated in the Hausdorff metric by polyhedra with rational vertices.
This implies the theorem because mixed volume is continuous in the Hausdorff metric.
∎

9
Minkowski type criterion for divisors

Minkowski discovered (see Theorem
6.2
) a necessary and sufficient condition on a
k
-tuple (where
k\leq n)
) of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, under which the mixed volume of any
n
-tuple of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, containing the given
k
-tuple of convex bodies, vanish. In this section, we present an analogous condition on
k\leq n
base-point free linear systems in the
n
-dimensional projective variety under which generic members of these systems have empty intersection.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional projective variety.
With any base-points free divisor
D
on
X
one associates a linear system of effective divisors
D+(f)
, where
f\in L(D)
linearly equivalent to
D
.

Definition 9.1
.

The
Kodaira dimension
of a base-points free divisor
D
is the dimension of the image
Y=\Phi_{L(D)}(X)
of
X
under the Kodaira map
\Phi_{L(D)}:X\to\mathbb{P}(L^{*}(D))
.

One can show that
the Kodaira dimension of a divisor
D
is equal to the real dimension of the NO body
\Delta(L(D))
of the space
L(D)
(see
[
4
]
).

Definition 9.2
.

A set
\{D_{1},\dots,D_{k}\}
of
k\leq n

base-points free Cartier divisors in a projective variety
X
is
dependent
if for some nonempty subset
J\in\{1,\dots,k\}
the divisor
D_{J}=\sum_{j\in J}D_{j}
has Kodaira dimension smaller than the number
|J|
of elements in the set
J
.

Theorem 9.1

(Minkowski type criterion)
.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{k}
be a collection of
k\leq n
base-points free Cartier divisors on an irreducible
n
-dimensional projective variety
X
. For a generic functions
f_{i}\in L(D_{i})
, the effective divisors
D_{1}+(f_{1}),\dots,D_{k}+(f_{k})
have empty intersection if and only if the divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{k}
are dependent. In the other words, they have empty intersection if and only if for some set
J
the real dimension of the NO body
\Delta(L(D_{J}))
is smaller than
|J|
.

A proof of Theorem
9.1
can be found in
[
6
]
.

Corollary 9.1
.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
be an
n
-tuple of base-points free Cartier divisors on
X
. Then the intersection index
[D_{1},\dots,D_{n}]
is equal to zero if and only if the divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
are dependent. In the other words,
[D_{1},\dots,D_{n}]=0
if and only if for some
J\in\{1,\dots,n\}
the real dimension of the NO body
\Delta(L(D_{j}))
is smaller than
|J|
.

Guiding-center dynamics in a screw-pinch magnetic field
A. J. Brizard

Abstract
The guiding-center dynamics of charged particles moving in a doubly-symmetric screw-pinch magnetic field is investigated. In particular, we verify that Kruskal’s adiabatic-invariant series expansion of the radial action integral associated with the reduced full-orbit radial motion matches the perturbation expansion of the magnetic-moment gyroaction up to first order in magnetic-field non-uniformity. Because the radial action integral is an exact invariant of the full-orbit dynamics, the magnetic moment is therefore represented as non-perturbative integral expression, which can be used to test the validity of the guiding-center approximation.



I
Introduction

The foundations for the magnetic confinement of charged particles rely on the existence of a hierarchy of adiabatic
invariants
16
;
17
;
15
;
18
;
8
associated with the three orbital time scales
(T_{\rm g}\ll T_{\rm b}\ll T_{\rm d})
of the charged-particle motion, which is quasi-periodic on each these time scales. The shortest orbital time scale involves the periodic gyromotion (g) about a single magnetic field line, which is associated with the adiabatic invariance of the gyro-action
J_{\rm g}
. Next, the intermediate orbital time scale involves the bounce (b) motion along a magnetic field line, which is associated with the adiabatic invariance of the bounce action
J_{\rm b}
when the parallel motion is periodic. Finally, the longest orbital time scale involves the drift (d) motion across magnetic field lines, which is associated with the adiabatic invariance of the drift action
J_{\rm d}
when the drift motion is periodic.

One of the hallmarks of adiabatic invariance is that, while the adiabatic invariant
J_{k}
is not an exact invariant
dJ_{k}/dt\neq 0
on the time scale
T_{k}
, the orbital-averaged time derivative yields the identity
16

\langle dJ_{k}/dt\rangle_{k}\equiv 0
when averaged over the time scale
T_{k}
. We note that the adiabatic invariance on a longer orbital time scale requires adiabatic invariance on shorter orbital time scales, e.g., bounce adiabatic invariance requires gyromotion adiabatic invariance (see Fig. 1 of Northrop
et al.
15
). In addition, the existence of symmetries in the confining magnetic field can alter the adiabatic-invariant hierarchy. For example, when we consider the motion of charged particles (with mass
m
and charge
e
) in an axisymmetric dipole magnetic field
2

{\bf B}\equiv\nabla\psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\varphi
, the toroidal canonical momentum
P_{\varphi}=(e/c)\,\psi+m\,|\partial{\bf x}/\partial\varphi|^{2}\,d\varphi/dt
is an exact invariant, which can be used as the exact drift action. The analysis of the bounce-center dynamics in an axisymmetric dipole magnetic field can also be done perturbatively
9
when the bounce point along the magnetic-field line is at a shorter distance from the equatorial point than the equatorial radius.

I.1
Adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment

The adiabatic motion of charged particles in a non-uniform magnetic field
17
is associated with the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment
8
, which can be defined as an action integral
12
over the periodic gyroangle
\zeta
:

\mu_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\frac{\epsilon}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\bf P}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}}{\partial\zeta}\;d\zeta.

(1)

Here, the dimensionless parameter
\epsilon
appears as a result of the renormalization of the particle’s mass
m\rightarrow\epsilon\,m
as a way to introduce the dimensional mass-over-charge ratio
17

m/e
as an expansion parameter, while the guiding-center canonical momentum
{\bf P}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf P}
is defined as the guiding-center push-forward of the canonical momentum
{\bf P}\equiv(e/c)\,{\bf A}({\bf x})+\epsilon\,m{\bf v}
, and
\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf x}-{\bf X}_{\rm gc}
is the guiding-center gyroradius, which is defined as the difference between the guiding-center push-forward of the particle position
{\bf x}
and the guding-center position
{\bf X}_{\rm gc}
. In addition, the Lie-transform guiding-center push-forward operator
13

{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}
involved in Eq. (
1
) is defined for an arbitrary phase-space function
f
by the series expansion in powers of
\epsilon
:

{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f\;\equiv\;\cdots{\sf T}_{3}^{-1}{\sf T}_{2}^{-1}{\sf T}_{1}^{-1}f\;=\;f\;-\;\epsilon\,{\mathcal{L}}_{1}f\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\left({\mathcal{L}}_{2}f\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{2}f\right)+\cdots,

(2)

where the Lie-transform operator
{\sf T}_{n}^{-1}\equiv\exp(\epsilon^{n}{\mathcal{L}}_{n})
is defined in terms of the
n
th-order Lie derivative
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\equiv{\sf G}_{n}\cdot{\sf d}
generated by the vector field
{\sf G}_{n}
, whose purpose is the remove the gyroangle dependence from the guiding-center dynamics. The expansion of the action integral (
1
) in powers of
\epsilon
yields the so-called Kruskal asymptotic series
12
for the guiding-center magnetic-moment adiabatic invariant.

While the integral definition (
1
) for the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu_{\rm gc}
yields the correct expression for the lowest-order magnetic moment
\mu_{0}\equiv\epsilon^{2}\,m|{\bf v}_{\bot}|^{2}/(2\,eB/m)
, it is rarely used to obtain higher-order terms because it involves multiple perturbation expansions in powers of
\epsilon
before performing the gyroangle averages needed to obtain an explicit expression at a desired perturbation order. Instead, by using the standard guiding-center perturbation theory
14
;
8
associated with the phase-space transformation from particle coordinates
({\bf x},{\bf p}=m{\bf v})
to guiding-center coordinates
({\bf X}_{\rm gc},P_{\|{\rm gc}},\mu_{\rm gc},\zeta_{\rm gc})
, the guiding-center magnetic moment is defined as an explicit asymptotic expansion in powers of
\epsilon
:

\mu_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\mu_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,\mu_{1}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\,\mu_{2}\;+\;\cdots\;=\;\mu_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\mu}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\left(G_{2}^{\mu}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}G_{1}^{\mu}\right)\;+\;\cdots,

(3)

where the first-order correction
\mu_{1}\equiv G_{1}^{\mu}
is associated with first-order magnetic-field non-uniformity, while the terms
\mu_{n}
(
n\geq 2)
involve higher-order corrections
6
;
19
. Unfortunately, there are concerns about the validity of the asymptotic expansion (
3
) when the dimensionless parameter
\epsilon
is not small, either because of steep gradients
4
or when considering energetic particles
11
.

I.2
Kruskal Identity

The purpose of the present paper is to investigate the non-perturbative definition of the guiding-center magnetic moment in the case of doubly-symmetric time-independent magnetic geometries, where the two ignorable spatial coordinates
(q^{1},q^{2})
are associated with their respective invariant canonical momenta
(P_{1},P_{2})
. In this particular case, the Lagrangian dynamics takes place in a reduced two-dimensional space
(q_{3},dq_{3}/dt)
, where the reduced Lagrangian is derived by the Routh reduction procedure
3

L_{r}\left(q^{3},\frac{dq^{3}}{dt}\right)\;\equiv\;L\left(q^{i},\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}\right)\;-\;P_{1}\,\frac{dq^{1}}{dt}\;-\;P_{2}\,\frac{dq^{2}}{dt}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,m\,\left(\frac{dq^{3}}{dt}\right)^{2}-V(q^{3};P_{1},P_{2}),

(4)

from which the reduced Euler-Lagrange equations are derived:

\displaystyle P_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\partial L_{r}/\partial(dq^{3}/dt)\;=\;m\,dq_{3}/dt,

\displaystyle dP_{3}/dt

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\partial L_{r}/\partial q^{3}\;=\;-\,\partial V/\partial q^{3}.

In addition to the energy-momentum conservation laws for
(E,P_{1},P_{2})
, where the total energy is defined as

E\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2}\,m\,(dq^{3}/dt)^{2}\;+\;V(q^{3};P_{1},P_{2}),

there exists a reduced action integral

J_{r}(E;P_{1},P_{2})\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint P_{3}\;dq^{3}\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint\sqrt{2m\,[E-V(q^{3};P_{1},P_{2})]}\;dq^{3},

(5)

which is a constant of the reduced motion. In the spirit of Kruskal’s pioneering work
12
, and following recent works by Burby
et al.
7
;
5
, the reduced action integral (
5
) is said to satisfy the
Kruskal identity
:

J_{3}(E,P_{1},P_{2})\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\;\int_{0}^{2\pi}P_{3}\,\frac{\partial q^{3}}{\partial\zeta}\;d\zeta\;\equiv\;\mu_{\rm gc},

(6)

where the ordering parameter
\epsilon
appears in a non-perturbative setting in the definition of
J_{3}
, which yields a non-perturbative definition of the magnetic moment
\mu_{\rm gc}
.

In recent papers
11
;
10
, the Kruskal identity (
6
) was proved for two doubly-symmetric magnetic geometries. First, this identity was proved up to fourth order in
\epsilon
(including second-order effects in magnetic field non-uniformity) for the case of a straight magnetic field
{\bf B}=(1+y)\,\widehat{\sf z}
with a constant gradient, where the canonical momenta
(P_{x},P_{z})
in the ignorable directions
(x,z)
are invariant. Second, a screw-pinch magnetic field was considered, with the magnetic field expressed in terms of an Eulerian representation

{\bf B}\;=\;\nabla\psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\theta\;-\;\nabla\Psi(\psi)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla z

(7)

in cylindrical geometry, where the axial magnetic flux
\psi(r)
and the azimuthal magnetic field
\Psi(\psi)
are assumed to be functions of the radial position
r
only, and the canonical momenta
(P_{\theta},P_{z})
are constants of motion. Unfortunately, the proof of Kruskal’s identity (
6
) for the screw-pinch magnetic field (
7
) required considerable use of computer algebra up to third order in
\epsilon
(i.e., including first-order corrections in magnetic non-uniformity).

I.3
Organization of the paper

In the present work, we consider the cylindrical screw-pinch magnetic geometry (
7
), now expressed as

{\bf B}(r,\theta)\;=\;B(\Theta)\;\left(\cos\Theta\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\sin\Theta\;\widehat{\theta}\right),

(8)

where the angle
\Theta(r)
denotes the radial pitch of the screw-pinch magnetic field, and
B(\Theta)
denotes the magnitude of the magnetic field. Using the Frenet-Serret curvature and torsion coefficients
3
associated with the magnetic unit vector

\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;\cos\Theta\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\sin\Theta\;\widehat{\theta},

(9)

as well as additional related geometric coefficients that appear in guiding-center perturbation theory
14
, we explicitly express the full-orbit Newtonian and Lagrangian mechanics in terms of these geometric coefficients and then construct the reduced radial action integral
J_{r}(E,P_{\theta},P_{z})
, which is defined as an exact invariant of the reduced radial dynamics derived from the reduced Lagrangian
L_{r}(r,dr/dt)=\frac{1}{2}\,(dr/dt)^{2}-V(r;P_{\theta},P_{z})
, where
V(r;P_{\theta},P_{z})
denotes the reduced radial potential derived by the Routh reduction procedure (
4
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we derive the Frenet-Serret curvature and torsion coefficients, as well as other geometric coefficients, associated with the spatial curve (
9
). In Sec.
III
, we will use these geometric coefficients to express Newton’s equations of motion in cylindrical screw-pinch magnetic geometry, where we will also introduce particle’s pitch angle
\lambda\equiv\cos^{-1}(v_{\|}/v)
and the particle’s gyroangle
\zeta
. In Sec.
IV
, we apply the Lagrangian formalism to review the particle motion in the doubly-symmetric screw-pinch magnetic geometry from which we derive the reduced radial action
J_{r}(E,P_{\theta},P_{z})
as an exact particle invariant.

For the purpose of explicitly proving the Kruskal identity (
6
) for a doubly-symmetric screw-pinch magnetic geometry, we begin with the Lie-transform perturbation expansion (
3
) of the guiding-center magnetic moment in Sec.
V
. Here, the guiding-center transformation for the particle motion in screw-pinch magnetic geometry, which is reviewed in App.
A
, is used to obtain an explicit expression for the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu_{\rm gc}
up to first order in magnetic non-uniformity (i.e., third order in the ordering parameter
\epsilon
). In Sec.
VI
, we complete the proof of the Kruskal identity (
6
) by expanding the reduced radial action
J_{r}(E;P_{\theta},P_{z})
in powers of
\epsilon
and perform explicit gyroangle averages to show that
J_{r}\equiv\mu_{\rm gc}
up to third order in
\epsilon
. Hence, amazingly, the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu_{\rm gc}=J_{r}
is defined as an exact radial action invariant that depends non-perturbatively on the ordering parameter
\epsilon
. Finally, in Sec.
VII
, we discuss how the guiding-center transformation used in Secs.
V
-
VI
can be extended to fourth order in
\epsilon
(i.e., second order in magnetic-field non-uniformity).

II
Screw Pinch Magnetic Geometry

We consider a screw-pinch magnetic field (
8
) in cylindrical geometry, where we will use the radial pitch angle
\Theta(r)=\pi\,(r/a)^{2}/2\leq\pi/2
for explicit results in what follows, and we will assume that the plasma is confined in the region
r\leq a
(i.e.,
r=a
denotes the edge of the plasma).

From the screw-pinch magnetic-field unit vector (
9
), we introduce the spatial derivative along a magnetic-field line
d/ds\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla=r^{-1}\sin\Theta\;\partial/\partial\theta
(taking into account the
z
-symmetry), and we obtain the Frenet-Serret equations
3
associated with the unit-vector triad
(\widehat{{\sf b}},\widehat{\sf e}_{1},\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1})
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{ds}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;\kappa\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1},

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{\sf e}_{1}}{ds}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;=\;-\,\kappa\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\tau\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{\sf e}_{2}}{ds}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\;=\;-\,\tau\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1},

(12)

where the normal (radial) and binormal (transverse) unit vectors are

\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;\equiv\;-\,\widehat{\sf r}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\;=\;-\,\cos\Theta\;\widehat{\theta}\;+\;\sin\Theta\;\widehat{\sf z},

(13)

respectively, and the Frenet-Serret curvature and torsion in screw-pinch magnetic geometry are

\left(\kappa,\frac{}{}\tau\right)\;\equiv\;\left(\frac{1}{r}\,\sin^{2}\,\Theta,\;\frac{1}{r}\,\sin\,\Theta\,\cos\,\Theta\right).

(14)

Next, we note that the curl of
\widehat{{\sf b}}
is

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\Theta\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(-\;\sin\Theta\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\cos\Theta\;\widehat{\theta}\right)\;+\;\sin\Theta\;\nabla\theta\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{\theta}}{\partial\theta}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta^{\prime}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\frac{\widehat{\sf z}}{r}\;\sin\Theta\;=\;\tau_{m}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\kappa\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

where the magnetic twist is expressed as

\tau_{m}\;\equiv\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;\tau\;+\;\Theta^{\prime},

(16)

in terms of the Frenet-Serret torsion
\tau
and the magnetic-pitch shear
\Theta^{\prime}(r)
.

Lastly, we will also need to evaluate the gyrogauge vector
14
in screw-pinch magnetic geometry:

\displaystyle{\bf R}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\nabla\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\;=\;-\,\left(\widehat{\theta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\right)\,\nabla\theta\;=\;\cos\Theta\;\nabla\theta\;=\;\frac{\cos\Theta}{r}\;\left(\sin\Theta\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\cos\Theta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\tau\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\left(\frac{1}{r}-\kappa\right)\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\;\equiv\;\tau\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;R_{\bot}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

which will appear as part of the geometric formulation of Newtonian full-particle dynamics.

III
Geometric Newtonian Mechanics

In this Section, we write Newton’s equations of motion
d{\bf r}/dt={\bf v}
and
d{\bf v}/dt=(e/m)\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}
in screw-pinch magnetic geometry for a particle with mass
m
and charge
e
. Before we do this, however, we want to proceed with dimensionless variables, by normalizing the particle position
{\bf r}\equiv L_{0}\,\overline{\bf r}
, the particle velocity
{\bf v}\equiv v_{0}\,\overline{\bf v}
, and time
t\equiv(m/eB_{0})\,\overline{t}
(i.e., time is normalized to the gyroperiod). Hence, the dimensionless equations of motion become

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}d\overline{\bf r}/d\overline{t}&=&\epsilon\,\overline{\bf v}\\
d\overline{\bf v}/d\overline{t}&=&\overline{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\overline{\bf B}\end{array}\right\},

(18)

where
\overline{\bf B}\equiv{\bf B}/B_{0}
is the normalized magnetic field, and
\epsilon\equiv(mv_{0}/eB_{0})/L_{0}
is the ratio of the characteristic gyroradius
(mv_{0}/eB_{0})
to the magnetic-nonuniformity length scale
L_{0}
. In what follows, we will use the notation
(t,{\bf r},{\bf v})
, without the overbar, to denote the normalized time, particle position, and particle velocity.

III.1
Velocity representation in screw-pinch magnetic geometry

Using the unit vectors
(\widehat{{\sf b}},\widehat{\sf e}_{1},\widehat{\sf e}_{2})
associated with screw-pinch magnetic geometry, the normalized particle velocity can be expressed as

\displaystyle\epsilon\,{\bf v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{dt}\,\widehat{r}\;+\;r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\widehat{\theta}\;+\;\frac{dz}{dt}\,\widehat{\sf z}

(19)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\;+\;\frac{dz}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta\right)\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\frac{dr}{dt}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;+\;\left(\frac{dz}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\;-\;r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta\right)\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

where the normalized screw-pinch pitch angle is
\Theta=\pi\,r^{2}/2
. We may also express the particle velocity (
19
) as

{\bf v}\equiv v_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+v_{\bot}\,\widehat{\bot}
in terms of the rotating unit vectors
\widehat{\bot}\equiv\widehat{\rho}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
, where

\displaystyle\widehat{\bot}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;\frac{\partial\widehat{\rho}}{\partial\zeta}\;=\;-\,\sin\zeta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;-\;\cos\zeta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

(20)

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\bot}\;=\;-\,\frac{\partial\widehat{\bot}}{\partial\zeta}\;=\;\cos\zeta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;-\;\sin\zeta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

(21)

which are defined in terms of the perpendicular vectors (
13
). Here, the parallel velocity
v_{\|}
is defined as

\epsilon\,v_{\|}\;\equiv\;{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\;+\;\frac{dz}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta,

(22)

while the magnitude of the perpendicular velocity
v_{\bot}
is defined as

\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;\equiv\;{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\bot}\;=\;\frac{dr}{dt}\,\sin\zeta\;-\;\left(\frac{dz}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\;-\;r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta\right)\,\cos\zeta,

(23)

which can be inverted to yield

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{dt}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\,\epsilon\;{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;=\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\,\sin\zeta,

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{dz}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\;-\;r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\epsilon\;{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\;=\;-\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\,\cos\zeta,

(25)

since
v_{\bot}
must be independent of the gyroangle
\zeta
. Using Eqs. (
22
) and (
25
), the cylindrical velocity components
(d\theta/dt,dz/dt)
are, therefore, expressed in screw-pinch magnetic geometry as

\displaystyle r\;\frac{d\theta}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,v_{\|}\;\sin\,\Theta\;+\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;\cos\,\Theta\,\cos\zeta,

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{dz}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,v_{\|}\;\cos\,\Theta\;-\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;\sin\,\Theta\,\cos\zeta.

(27)

Here, the cylindrical velocity components (
24
) and (
26
)-(
27
) are expressed in screw-pinch magnetic geometry in terms of the pitch angle
\Theta
and the velocity components
(v_{\|},v_{\bot},\zeta)
.

III.2
Newton’s equations of motion in screw-pinch magnetic geometry

Newton’s equation of motion for the normalized particle velocity (
19
) is

\frac{d{\bf v}}{dt}\;=\;\frac{dv_{\|}}{dt}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;v_{\|}\;\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\;+\;\frac{dv_{\bot}}{dt}\,\widehat{\bot}\;+\;v_{\bot}\;\frac{d\widehat{\bot}}{dt}\;=\;B\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;-\;v_{\bot}\,B\;\widehat{\rho},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;-\;\frac{d\Theta}{dt}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,\left(v_{\|}\,\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,\tau\,\cos\zeta\right)\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;-\;\epsilon\,\left(\Theta^{\prime}\;v_{\bot}\,\sin\zeta\right)\;\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{\bot}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\sin\,\Theta\,\sin\zeta\;-\;\frac{d\Theta}{dt}\,\cos\zeta\right)\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\left(\frac{d\zeta}{dt}\;-\;\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta\right)\widehat{\rho}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,\sin\zeta\;\left[v_{\|}\,\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,\left(\tau-\Theta^{\prime}\right)\,\cos\zeta\right]\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\left[\frac{d\zeta}{dt}\;-\;\epsilon\,\left(v_{\|}\,\tau\;+\;v_{\bot}\,R_{\bot}\;\cos\zeta\right)\right]\widehat{\rho}.

In deriving Eq. (
30
), we used

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{\sf e}_{1}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;\widehat{\theta}\;=\;-\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;\left(\sin\,\Theta\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\cos\,\Theta\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\right),

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{\sf e}_{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\Theta}{dt}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\cos\,\Theta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}.

(32)

Equation (
28
), therefore, yields the three coupled equations for the velocity components
(v_{\|},v_{\bot},\zeta)
:

\displaystyle\frac{dv_{\|}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,v_{\bot}\,\frac{d\widehat{\bot}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;-\,\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\,\sin\zeta\left[v_{\|}\,\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,\left(\tau-\Theta^{\prime}\right)\,\cos\zeta\right],

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{dv_{\bot}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,v_{\|}\,\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\bot}\;=\;\epsilon\,v_{\|}\,\sin\zeta\left[v_{\|}\,\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,\left(\tau-\Theta^{\prime}\right)\,\cos\zeta\right],

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{d\zeta}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\;+\;\epsilon\,v_{\|}\,\left[\tau_{m}+\left(\tau-\Theta^{\prime}\right)\,\cos^{2}\zeta\right]\;+\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\,\cos\zeta\left(R_{\bot}\;+\;\frac{v_{\|}^{2}}{v_{\bot}^{2}}\;\kappa\right),

(35)

where the Frenet-Serret coefficients
(\kappa,\tau,\tau_{m})
are given by Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
) and the gyrogauge component
R_{\bot}\equiv 1/r-\kappa
is given by Eq. (
17
). Using Eqs. (
24
) and (
34
), we note that, while the time derivative of the lowest-order magnetic moment
\mu_{0}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}v_{\bot}^{2}\cos\Theta
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu_{0}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}v_{\bot}\,\cos\Theta\;\frac{dv_{\bot}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\,v_{\bot}^{2}\,\sin\Theta\;\frac{d\Theta}{dt}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{3}\,v_{\|}\,v_{\bot}\,\cos\Theta\;\sin\zeta\left[v_{\|}\,\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,(\tau-\Theta^{\prime})\,\cos\zeta\right]\;-\;\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{2}\,v_{\bot}^{3}\,\Theta^{\prime}\,\sin\Theta\;\sin\zeta,

does not vanish, its gyroangle average
\langle d\mu_{0}/dt\rangle=0
vanishes at the lowest order, which is the hallmark of adiabatic invariance
17
.

Lastly, we note that the kinetic energy
E=\epsilon^{2}(v_{\|}^{2}+v_{\bot}^{2})/2
is a constant of motion, since
dE/dt=\epsilon\,v_{\|}\,dv_{\|}/dt+\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\,dv_{\bot}/dt=0
. The parallel velocity
v_{\|}=v\,\cos\lambda
and the perpendicular velocity
v_{\bot}=v\,\sin\lambda
can therefore be expressed in terms of the pitch angle
\lambda
, so that the equation of motion for
the pitch angle
\lambda
is expressed as

\frac{d\lambda}{dt}\;=\;\frac{1}{v_{\|}}\,\frac{dv_{\bot}}{dt}\;=\;\epsilon\,\sin\zeta\left[v_{\|}\,\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,\left(\tau-\Theta^{\prime}\right)\,\cos\zeta\right].

(37)

Hence, the equations of motion for the cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
and the velocity-space angles
(\lambda,\zeta)
are expressed in terms of the screw-pinch magnetic geometric coefficients
(\kappa,\tau,\tau_{m},R_{\bot})
.

IV
Particle Lagrangian Mechanics

As an alternative to the geometric Newtonian representation presented in Sec.
III
, we now explore the Lagrangian representation of the particle dynamics in the screw-pinch magnetic field (
8
). We now choose the magnitude of the magnetic field to be a function of
\Theta
only (i.e., it is independent of
z
):

B(\Theta)\;\equiv\;\sec\Theta,

(38)

where we note that
\nabla\ln B=-\,\Theta^{\prime}\,\tan\,\Theta\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}
. We note that, while the choice (
38
) allows us to obtain explicit expressions, it does not impact on the main results of our work.

IV.1
Euler-potential magnetic representation

In order to proceed with a Lagrangian representation, using the magnitude (
38
), the magnetic field (
8
) can also be expressed in Euler form as
10

{\bf B}\;\equiv\;B(\Theta)\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\,=\,\widehat{\sf z}+\tan\Theta\;\widehat{\theta}\;=\;\nabla\psi(r)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\theta\;-\;\nabla\Psi(r)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla z,

(39)

where the dimensonless magnetic fluxes
\psi(r)
and
\Psi(r)
satisfy the differential equations

\frac{d\psi(r)}{dr}\;=\;r\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\frac{d\Psi(r)}{dr}\;=\;\tan\Theta.

(40)

The solution for
\psi(r)
is easily solved as
\psi(r)=r^{2}/2
, which is chosen to vanish at
r=0
. Lastly, we note that it is a common practice to write
\Psi(\psi)
, which leads to the differential equation for the rotational transform
10

\Psi^{\prime}(\psi)=r^{-1}\;\tan\Theta
.
In what follows, however, we will not need to solve for
\Psi(\psi)
.

IV.2
Particle Lagrangian

Using the normalized magnetic vector potential
{\bf A}=\psi\;\nabla\theta-\Psi\;\nabla z
associated with the magnetic field (
39
), we express the normalized particle Lagrangian in cylindrical coordinates as

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bf A}\;+\;\frac{d{\bf r}}{dt}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf r}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left|\frac{d{\bf r}}{dt}\right|^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\psi(r)\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;-\;\Psi(r)\,\frac{dz}{dt}\right)\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\,\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{dz}{dt}\right)^{2}\right].

From this Lagrangian, we obtain the canonical momenta

\displaystyle P_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial(dr/dt)}\;=\;\frac{dr}{dt},

(42)

\displaystyle P_{\theta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial(d\theta/dt)}\;=\;\psi\;+\;r^{2}\,\frac{d\theta}{dt},

(43)

\displaystyle P_{z}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial(dz/dt)}\;=\;-\,\Psi\;+\;\frac{dz}{dt},

(44)

and the radial Euler-Lagrange equation yields

\displaystyle\frac{dP_{r}}{dt}\;=\;\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}}\;=\;\frac{\partial L}{\partial r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\psi(r)}{dr}\;\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;-\;\frac{d\Psi(r)}{dr}\;\frac{dz}{dt}\;+\;r\;\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^{2}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\;\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;-\;\tan\Theta\;\frac{dz}{dt}\;+\;r\;\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^{2},

where we used Eq. (
40
). The remaining Euler-Lagrange equations are
dP_{\theta}/dt=\partial L/\partial\theta\equiv 0
, and
dP_{z}/dt=\partial L/\partial z\equiv 0
, i.e., because of the symmetries of the Lagrangian (
41
) with respect to
(\theta,z)
, the canonical momenta
(P_{\theta},P_{z})
are constants of motion.

If we substitute Eqs. (
26
)-(
27
) into Eq. (
45
), we obtain the radial equation

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,v_{\|}\;\sin\,\Theta\;+\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;\cos\,\Theta\,\cos\zeta\;-\;\epsilon\,v_{\|}\;\sin\,\Theta\;+\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;\cos\Theta\,(\sec^{2}\Theta-1)\,\cos\zeta

(46)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{r}\left(v_{\|}\;\sin\,\Theta\;+\;v_{\bot}\;\cos\,\Theta\,\cos\zeta\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;B\;\cos\zeta\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\left(v_{\|}^{2}\;\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}^{2}\,R_{\bot}\,\cos^{2}\zeta\;+\;2\;v_{\|}\,v_{\bot}\,\tau\;\cos\zeta\right).

Next, if we insert Eq. (
35
) for
d\zeta/dt
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\;\cos\zeta\;\frac{d\zeta}{dt}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\|}\,\sin^{2}\zeta\left[v_{\|}\;\kappa\;+\;v_{\bot}\,\left(\tau-\Theta^{\prime}\right)\,\cos\zeta\right]

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\epsilon\,v_{\bot}\,\sin\zeta\right)\;\equiv\;\frac{d^{2}r}{dt^{2}},

which follows after substituting Eq. (
34
) for
dv_{\bot}/dt
. Hence, as expected, the Euler-Lagrange equations exactly agree with Newton’s equations.

IV.3
Reduced radial particle dynamics

In what follows, we will use the notation
P_{\theta}\equiv\overline{\psi}
and
P_{z}=-\,\overline{\Psi}
for the canonical-momentum constants of motion, so that Eqs. (
43
)-(
44
) yield

\displaystyle\psi(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}\;-\;r^{2}\frac{d\theta}{dt},

(48)

\displaystyle\Psi(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}\;+\;\frac{dz}{dt}.

(49)

Because the Lagrangian (
41
) is independent of the coordinates
(\theta,z)
, we may use the Routh reduction procedure
3
to obtain the reduced radial Lagrangian

L_{r}(r,dr/dt)\;\equiv\;L\;-\;\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,P_{\theta}\;-\;\frac{dz}{dt}\,P_{z}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^{2}\;-\;V(r),

(50)

where the reduced radial potential is

V(r)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\left(\Psi-\overline{\Psi}\right)^{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,\left(\psi-\overline{\psi}\right)^{2}\right].

(51)

The reduced radial equation of motion is
d^{2}r/dt^{2}=-\,V^{\prime}(r)
, and the law of energy conservation is

E\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^{2}\;+\;V(r),

(52)

The reduced radial action integral

J_{r}(E,P_{\theta},P_{z})\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint P_{r}\,dr\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint\sqrt{2\left[E\;-\;V(r)\right]}\;dr

(53)

is also an invariant of the reduced radial motion. With three separate exact constants of motion
(E,P_{\theta},P_{z})
, the motion of a charged particle in this screw-pinch magnetic field is said to be integrable. We will return to the reduced radial action integral (
53
) in Sec.
VI
to show that it matches exactly with the magnetic moment up to third order in
\epsilon
.

V
Guiding-center Magnetic Moment

In this Section, we will proceed with a calculation of the magnetic moment (expressed in units of action)
\mu_{\rm gc}=\mu_{0}+\epsilon\,\mu_{1}+\cdots
up to first order in magnetic-field non-uniformity. In the next Section, we will show that
\mu_{\rm gc}\equiv J_{r}
matches exactly with the asymptotic expansion of the reduced radial action (
53
).

V.1
Zeroth-order magnetic moment

The lowest-order magnetic moment
\mu_{0}=m\,|d{\bf r}_{\bot}/dt|^{2}/2\Omega
(written in units of action) is defined as

\mu_{0}\;\equiv\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}v_{\bot}^{2}}{2\,B}\;=\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\,v^{2}\;\sin^{2}\lambda\;\cos\Theta,

(54)

where both
(r,\lambda)
need to be expanded in powers of
\epsilon
through the guiding-center transformation:
r\equiv r_{0}+\epsilon\,r_{1}+\cdots
and
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{0}+\epsilon\,\lambda_{1}+\cdots
, where
(r_{1},\lambda_{1})
denote first-order corrections, while the speed
v
is a constant of motion. Hence, up to third order in
\epsilon^{3}
, we find

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\;v_{\bot 0}^{2}\;\cos\Theta_{0}\;+\;\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{2}\,v_{\bot 0}\left[v_{\|0}\;\left(2\,\cos\Theta_{0}\right)\;\lambda_{1}\;-\;v_{\bot 0}\;\left(\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\,\sin\Theta_{0}\right)\;r_{1}\right]

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mu_{0}}\left(1\;-\;\epsilon\;\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\,\tan\Theta_{0}\;r_{1}\right)\;+\;\epsilon^{3}\;v_{\|0}\,v_{\bot 0}\;\cos\Theta_{0}\;\lambda_{1},

where the lowest-order magnetic moment
\overline{\mu_{0}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\bot 0}^{2}\cos\Theta_{0}
is evaluated at the constants of motion
(r_{0},\lambda_{0})
.

V.2
First-order magnetic moment

In standard guiding-center theory
14
;
8
;
19
, the first-order magnetic moment is

\mu_{1}\;=\;\left(\overline{\mu_{0}}\,\nabla\ln B+\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\|0}^{2}\,\cos\Theta\;\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;-\;\overline{\mu_{0}}\,v_{\|0}\,\cos\Theta\;(\tau_{m}+\alpha_{1}),

(56)

where the lowest-order gyroradius vector is

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\;=\;v_{\bot 0}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\left(\cos\zeta\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\;-\;\sin\zeta\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\right),

(57)

and the torsional correction

\tau_{m}+\alpha_{1}\;\equiv\;\frac{3}{2}\tau_{m}-\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}\;=\;\tau\left(\frac{3}{2}-\cos^{2}\zeta\right)\;+\;\Theta^{\prime}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\sin^{2}\zeta\right)

(58)

is expressed in terms of the Frenet-Serret torsion
\tau
and the magnetic-pitch shear
\Theta^{\prime}
. Equation (
56
) may be rewritten as

\mu_{1}\;=\;\overline{\mu_{0}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B\;+\;\rho_{\|0}\left[\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\|0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\;-\;\overline{\mu_{0}}\;(\tau_{m}+\alpha_{1})\right],

(59)

where the first term is

\overline{\mu_{0}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B\;=\;-\;\overline{\mu_{0}}\,\rho_{\bot 0}\cos\zeta\;\left(\Theta^{\prime}_{0}\;\tan\Theta_{0}\right).

We now use the inverse guiding-center transformation
z^{\alpha}\equiv Z_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}-\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\alpha}+\cdots
presented in App.
A
, so that we find

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{\rm gc}\;-\;\epsilon\;\widehat{\sf r}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,G_{1}^{\bf x}\;=\;r_{\rm gc}\;-\;\epsilon\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;\equiv\;r_{0}+\epsilon\,r_{1},

(60)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm gc}\;-\;\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\lambda}\;\equiv\;\lambda_{0}+\epsilon\,\lambda_{1},

(61)

where

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\rho_{\bot}\;\cos\zeta\;=\;-\,v_{\bot 0}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\;\cos\zeta,

(62)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;G_{1}^{v_{\|}}/v_{\bot 0}\;=\;\left[\overline{\mu_{0}}\;(\tau_{m}+\alpha_{1})\;-\;\epsilon\,v_{\|0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\right]/v_{\bot 0},

(63)

so that the first-order correction (
59
) becomes

\mu_{1}\;=\;\overline{\mu_{0}}\;\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\,\tan\Theta_{0}\;r_{1}\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\;v_{\|0}\,v_{\bot 0}\;\cos\Theta_{0}\;\lambda_{1}.

(64)

Hence, the full magnetic moment is

\mu_{\rm gc}\;=\;\mu_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,\mu_{1}\;=\;\overline{\mu_{0}}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\bot 0}^{2}\,\cos\Theta_{0},

(65)

which implies that the first-order corrections of
\mu_{0}
are exactly canceled out by
\mu_{1}
.

VI
Expansion of the Reduced Radial Action

We now turn our attention to the reduced radial action integral (
53
), which can be expressed as

J_{r}\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint P_{r}\,dr\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{dr}{dt}\;\frac{\partial r}{\partial\zeta}\;d\zeta\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^{2}\;\frac{d\zeta}{(d\zeta/dt)},

(66)

where
(dr/dt,d\zeta/dt)
are given by Eqs. (
24
) and (
35
), respectively. In order to compare the reduced radial action integral (
66
) with the magnetic moment (
65
), we need to expand
(dr/dt)^{2}
up to third order in
\epsilon
and
(d\zeta/dt)^{-1}
up to first order in
\epsilon
. First, we find

\displaystyle(dr/dt)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}\,\sin^{2}\zeta\left(v_{\bot 0}^{2}\;+\;2\;\epsilon\,v_{\|0}\,v_{\bot 0}\;\lambda_{1}\right),

(67)

\displaystyle(d\zeta/dt)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\Theta_{0}\;-\;\epsilon\;\left(\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\,\sin\Theta_{0}\right)\;r_{1}\;-\;\epsilon\,v_{\|0}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\left[\tau_{0}\;\left(1+\cos^{2}\zeta\right)\;+\;\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\;\sin^{2}\zeta\right]

(68)

\displaystyle-\;\epsilon\,v_{\bot 0}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\,\cos\zeta\left(R_{\bot 0}\;+\;\frac{v_{\|0}^{2}}{v_{\bot 0}^{2}}\;\kappa_{0}\right),

where Eq. (
63
) yields

\lambda_{1}\;=\;\frac{v_{\bot 0}}{2}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\;\left[\tau_{0}\,\left(\frac{3}{2}-\cos^{2}\zeta\right)+\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\sin^{2}\zeta\right)\right]\;-\;v_{\|0}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\;\kappa_{0}\,\cos\zeta,

(69)

after inserting Eq. (
58
).

Next, when we expand Eq. (
66
) up to third order in
\epsilon
, we obtain a collection of four gyroangle-averaged terms

\displaystyle J_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\bot 0}^{2}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\;\left\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\right\rangle\;+\;\epsilon^{3}\,v_{\bot 0}^{2}v_{\|0}\left\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\left[\tau_{0}\,\left(3/2-\cos^{2}\zeta\right)+\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\left(3/2-\sin^{2}\zeta\right)\right]\right\rangle

(70)

\displaystyle-\;\epsilon^{3}\,v_{\bot 0}^{2}v_{\|0}\left\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\left[\tau_{0}\,\left(1+\cos^{2}\zeta\right)+\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\;\sin^{2}\zeta\right]\right\rangle\;-\;\epsilon^{3}(\cdots)\;\left\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\,\cos\zeta\right\rangle,

where the last term
(\cdots)
combines contributions that are proportional to
\sin^{2}\zeta\,\cos\zeta
.

Finally, using the identities
\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\rangle=1/2
and
\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\,\cos\zeta\rangle=0
, and the expressions

\displaystyle\left\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\left[\tau_{0}\,\left(3/2-\cos^{2}\zeta\right)+\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\left(3/2-\sin^{2}\zeta\right)\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\;\left(5\,\tau_{0}\;+\;3\,\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\left\langle\sin^{2}\zeta\left[\tau_{0}\,\left(1+\cos^{2}\zeta\right)+\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\;\sin^{2}\zeta\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\;\left(5\,\tau_{0}\;+\;3\,\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\right),

we obtain the final result

J_{r}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{2}\,v_{\bot 0}^{2}\,\cos\Theta_{0}\;=\;\overline{\mu}_{0}\;\equiv\;\mu_{\rm gc},

(71)

which confirms that, up to third order in
\epsilon
, the reduced radial action integral is identical to the magnetic moment.

VII
Conclusion and Discussion

By using the geometric formulation of the Newtonian mechanics of charged particle motion in a screw-pinch magnetic field, we have demonstrated Kruskal’s identity between the reduced radial action integral (
66
), from which the Kruskal adiabatic-invariant series is derived, and the magnetic moment (
65
), which is expanded explicitly in powers of the magnetic-field non-uniformity. In contrast to the previous work by Holas
et al.
10
, we have explicitly demonstrated the Kruskal identity up to third order in
\epsilon
. By exploiting the algorithmic nature of the Lie-transform approach to guiding-center dynamics, which would generate explicit expressions for
\mu_{2}
and
(r_{2},\lambda_{2})
, it is possible to prove the Kruskal identity
J_{r}=\mu_{\rm gc}
up to fourth order in
\epsilon
(i.e., second order in magnetic-field non-uniformity).

It has recently been argued
10
that a non-perturbative expression for the gyrocenter magnetic moment, which would be an adiabatic invariant in the presence of time-dependent electromagnetic-field perturbations, might be derived by the same method used here. While the particle energy is no longer conserved because of the time dependence of the field perturbations, the double spatial symmetries of the unperturbed magnetic field configuration will also be destroyed. Hence, in the absence of a reduced action integral (
5
), one would only rely on the formal integral definition for the gyrocenter magnetic moment

\mu_{\rm gy}\;\equiv\;\frac{\epsilon}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\bf P}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gy}}{\partial\zeta}\;d\zeta.

(72)

where an additional time-dependent transformation

{\sf T}_{\rm gy}^{-1}f\;\equiv\;\cdots{\sf T}_{3}^{-1}{\sf T}_{2}^{-1}{\sf T}_{1}^{-1}f\;=\;f\;-\;\delta\,{\mathcal{L}}_{1}f\;-\;\delta^{2}\left({\mathcal{L}}_{2}f\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{2}f\right)+\cdots,

(73)

where
\delta
is defined in terms of the amplitude of the field perturbation
1
and the Lie-transform operator
{\sf T}_{n}^{-1}\equiv\exp(\delta^{n}{\mathcal{L}}_{n})
is defined in terms of the
n
th-order Lie derivative
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\equiv{\sf G}_{n}\cdot{\sf d}
generated by the vector field
{\sf G}_{n}
, whose purpose is the remove the gyroangle dependence from the gyrocenter dynamics (i.e., the perturbed guiding-center dynamics).

In future work, we could explore whether the bounce-center dynamics, with the bounce-center action

J_{\rm bc}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p_{\|}\,ds\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}p_{\|}\,\frac{\partial s}{\partial\xi}\;d\xi,

(74)

satisfies the Kruskal identity, where
\xi
denotes the bounce angle. In this case, only one spatial symmetry needs to exist in order to yield conditions needed for proving the Kruskal identity. For this purpose, we will explore our previous work
9
involving an axisymmetric dipole magnetic field.

Appendix A
Guiding-center Dynamics in Screw-pinch Magnetic Geometry

In this Appendix, we present the guiding-center theory
8
of charged particle motion in the screw-pinch magnetic field described in Sec.
II
. The results derived here are used in Secs.
V
and
VI
to obtain the main result (
71
) of this work, which confirms the identity of the magnetic moment (
65
) and the reduced radial action integral (
66
) for the
screw-pinch magnetic geometry.

A.1
Guiding-center equations of motion

The guiding-center dynamics is expressed in terms of the guiding-center phase-space coordinates
({\bf X}_{\rm gc},P_{\|{\rm gc}},\mu_{\rm gc},\zeta_{\rm gc})
as the reduced guiding-center equations of motion

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|{\rm gc}}}{m}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{e\,B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mu_{\rm gc}\nabla B,

(75)

\displaystyle\frac{dP_{\|{\rm gc}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\mu_{\rm gc}\,\nabla B\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}},

(76)

where we have returned to using physical units and all spatial vectors and functions are evaluated at the guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{\rm gc}
. The guiding-center gyromotion equations, on the other hand, are

\frac{d\zeta_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;=\;\Omega\;+\;{\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\frac{d\mu_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;\equiv\;0,

(77)

where the gyrogauge vector
{\bf R}\equiv\nabla\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}=\tau\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-R_{\bot}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}
is given by Eq. (
17
). Its presence ensures that the guiding-center gyrofrequency
d\zeta_{\rm gc}/dt
is gyrogauge invariant in the following sense. Considering the gyroangle transformation
\zeta_{\rm gc}^{\prime}\equiv\zeta_{\rm gc}+\chi({\bf X}_{\rm gc})
, which yields
{\bf R}^{\prime}=\nabla\widehat{\bot}^{\prime}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}^{\prime}={\bf R}+\nabla\chi
, so that
{\bf R}^{\prime}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}/dt={\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}/dt+d\chi/dt
and
d\zeta_{\rm gc}^{\prime}/dt=d\zeta_{\rm gc}/dt
. These equations are derived by Lie-transform perturbation method
14
, where the guiding-center dynamics in the reduced phase space
({\bf X}_{\rm gc},P_{\|{\rm gc}})
is asymptotically decoupled from the fast gyromotion
(\mu_{\rm gc},\zeta_{\rm gc})
.

In Eqs. (
75
)-(
76
), the
symplectic
magnetic field is expressed up to first order in magnetic nonuniformity as

{\bf B}^{*}\;=\;{\bf B}\;+\;(c/e)\,P_{\|{\rm gc}}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;B\;\left[\left(1+\frac{P_{\|{\rm gc}}\,\tau_{m}}{m\Omega}\right)\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\frac{P_{\|{\rm gc}}\,\kappa}{m\Omega}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\right],

(78)

which yields the ratio

\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;=\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;P_{\|{\rm gc}}\,\left(\frac{c\,\kappa}{e\,B_{\|}^{*}}\right)\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2},

(79)

where
B_{\|}^{*}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}=B+P_{\|{\rm gc}}\,(c\tau_{m}/eB)
is the guiding-center Jacobian. Here, we note that Eq. (
79
) does not have a radial component, which will play an important role below in establishing constants of motion.

A.2
Guiding-center constants of motion

First, since
\nabla B=-\,B\;(\Theta^{\prime}\,\tan\,\Theta)\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}
, we see that the guiding-center radial position
r_{\rm gc}
is an exact guiding-center invariant:

\frac{dr_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;\equiv\;\nabla r_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;=\;-\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;=\;0,

since the guiding-center velocity (
75
) is

\frac{d{\bf X}_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;=\;\frac{P_{\|{\rm gc}}}{m}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\left(\frac{P_{\|{\rm gc}}^{2}\,\kappa}{m}\;-\;\mu_{\rm gc}\,B\;(\Theta^{\prime}\,\tan\,\Theta)\right)\,\frac{c\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}}{e\,B_{\|}^{*}},

(80)

i.e., the guiding-center velocity has no radial component, which means that each guiding-center orbit lies on a constant cylindrical surface with guiding-center radius
r_{\rm gc}
. Next, Eq. (
76
) immediately leads to the conservation law of guiding-center parallel momentum:

\frac{dP_{\|{\rm gc}}}{dt}\;=\;-\;\mu_{\rm gc}\,\nabla B\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;=\;\mu_{\rm gc}\,B\;(\Theta^{\prime}\,\tan\Theta)\;\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;=\;0,

(81)

since
\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}=0
. Hence, the guiding-center pitch angle
\lambda_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\cos^{-1}(P_{\|{\rm gc}}/P_{\rm gc})
is also a guiding-center constant of motion.

A.3
Guiding-center transformation in screw-pinch magnetic field

Now that we have established that the radial guiding-center position
r_{\rm gc}
and the guiding-center pitch angle
\lambda_{\rm gc}
are constants of motion in screw-pinch magnetic geometry, we will now use the inverse guiding-center transformation
14
;
19

z^{\alpha}\;=\;Z_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\;-\;\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\alpha}\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\left(G_{2}^{\alpha}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}G_{1}^{\alpha}\right)\;+\;\cdots,

(82)

where the phase-space vector-field components
G_{n}^{\alpha}
generate the transformation (or its inverse) at
n^{th}
order.

A.3.1
Radial position

First, the particle position
{\bf x}
and the guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{\rm gc}
are defined in terms of the expansion

{\bf x}\;=\;{\bf X}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;+\;\cdots,

(83)

where the lowest-order guiding-center gyroradius
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}
is given by Eq. (
57
). The radial position
r\equiv r_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla r+\cdots

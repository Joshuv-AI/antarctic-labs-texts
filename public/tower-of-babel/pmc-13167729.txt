ATP2B1 expression identifies human hematopoietic stem cells with superior repopulation and self-renewal
Authors: Angelica Varesi, Murtaza S. Nagree, Isabella Di Biasio, Andy G. X. Zeng, Sayyam Shah, Michael Zhang, Hyerin Kim, Alex Murison, Rohail Badami, Olga Gan, Liqing Jin, Jessica McLeod, Sheela A. Abraham, Mark D. Minden, Andrea Arruda, Igor Novitzky-Basso, Jonas Mattsson, John E. Dick, Stephanie Z. Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SignificanceLong-term hematopoietic stem cells (LT-HSC) maintain lifelong hematopoiesis while preserving the stem cell compartment through self-renewal. ATP2B1 identifies a rare population of LT-HSC across ontogeny which relies on the Transcription Factor EB (TFEB)–endolysosomal axis to sustain superior long-term repopulation and self-renewal capacity. Our study provides a tool to dissect the heterogeneous molecular programs in LT-HSC across ontogeny.

Human hematopoiesis is dynamic, originating from a rare pool of dormant long-term hematopoietic stem cells (LT-HSC) that both self-renew and generate downstream progeny (1, 2). In contrast to mice, immunophenotypic markers that permit isolation of human LT-HSC to purity have not been reported. All human sorting schemes begin with CD34; expression of CD90 and CD49f (integrin-α6) are most commonly used to resolve the HSC enriched (CD34+CD38–CD45RA–) compartment into short-term HSC (ST-HSC, CD90–CD49f−) and LT-HSC (CD90+CD49f+) (3–5). Subsequent studies have used additional markers [EPCR (6), GPRC5C (7), CD112 (8), and CLEC9A (9)] to further resolve CD90+CD49f+ LT-HSC.

These markers have permitted functional interrogation of the demand-adapted regulatory circuits of the early stages of human hematopoiesis and revealed extensive transcriptional, epigenetic, and metabolic HSC heterogeneity (4, 7–17). Specifically, studies using neonatal cord blood (CB) uncovered catabolic programs centered around TFEB-mediated control of endolysosomal activity as crucial for quiescence and self-renewal in LT-HSC (12). Lysosomal activity distinguished self-renewal potential of LT-HSC (12). Yet, whether such programs are conserved throughout human development, from fetal liver (FL) to adult LT-HSC is unknown. Moreover, a surface marker distinguishing LT-HSC with active TFEB at homeostasis is lacking. Lysosomes also act as a major intracellular signaling hub, in part functioning as a major store of calcium (18). Interestingly, murine and human HSC contain lower intracellular calcium levels compared to committed progenitors, and ex vivo culture in low calcium preserves stemness (19). The calcium exporter ATPase plasma membrane calcium transporting 1 (ATP2B1/PMCA1) has emerged as a regulator of mouse embryonic stem cell pluripotency (18, 20), but its relevance in human stem cells remains unexplored.

Collectively, these studies have contributed fundamental understanding toward the molecular underpinnings of human LT-HSC biology. Nonetheless, functional analyses demonstrated that current sorting schemes still capture a heterogeneous population of LT-HSC with variable stem cell frequency. Additionally, most studies were primarily performed using CB as the HSC source (8, 9, 12, 21). Comprehensive studies of functional LT-HSC heterogeneity across the human lifespan remain incomplete. Ontological studies have shown that fetal HSC are both more proliferative and self-renewing than CB HSC, while adult HSC are comparatively more quiescent and exhibit less robust stemness properties (10, 22–28). Thus, a common marker set that enables prospective separation of functionally distinct human CD90+ HSC subsets across multiple stem cell sources is needed.

Here, we identify ATP2B1 as an immunophenotypic marker that stratifies human CD49f+ LT-HSC into two distinct functional compartments across FL, CB, mPB, and BM HSC. Through a comprehensive set of single-cell in vitro assays, single-cell (sc)Multiome, and functional xenograft studies, we show that CD49f+ATP2B1+ LT-HSC have sustained long-term repopulation capacity and superior self-renewal ability with balanced multilineage output.

Results

ATP2B1 Is Expressed on the Surface of Human LT-HSC.

To identify surface proteins for prospective isolation of distinct subsets of HSC, we leveraged scMultiome datasets available to us (17). Analysis was restricted to datasets enriched for primitive populations, and ATP2B1 was pinpointed as a potential surface marker on human LT-HSC. Quiescence and dormancy are functional features of LT-HSC (12, 13), and ATP2B1 expression was significantly higher in HSC enriched for a quiescence-versus-activation signature in xenografts (SI Appendix, Fig. S1A) (17) Further, analysis of CB hematopoietic stem and progenitor (HSPC) populations profiled by bulk RNAseq revealed increased ATP2B1 expression in quiescent compared to activated fractions (SI Appendix, Fig. S1B) (12).

ATP2B1 protein expression in human HSC at homeostasis was measured by immunofluorescence microscopy of CB LT-HSC (CD19–CD34+CD38–CD45RA–CD90+CD49f+) and ST-HSC (CD90–CD49f–). ATP2B1 surface expression was variable across cells but covered a larger surface area in LT-HSC compared to ST-HSC (Fig. 1 A and B). Next, surface expression of ATP2B1 across ontogeny was evaluated by flow cytometry of FL-, CB-, mPB-, and BM-derived (age 22 to 72 y) HSPCs. The CD90+ HSC compartment was partitioned based on the expression of CD49f and ATP2B1 as compared to fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls lacking ATP2B1 antibody, resulting in three detected populations: CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– (Fig. 1C and SI Appendix, Fig. S1 C–E). The percentage of CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells decreased from FL and CB to adult HSC with no change between mPB and BM or from young adults to aged donors (Fig. 1D). However, ATP2B1 expression was consistently higher on LT-HSC compared to ST-HSC (Fig. 1E) and other committed HSPC across all sources (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 E and F).

To investigate whether CD49f+ATP2B1+ identifies a distinct HSC population compared to previously identified markers (6–9), we evaluated the coexpression of ATP2B1 with CD112, CLEC9A, GPRC5C, and EPCR by flow cytometry. Surprisingly, we did not find any correlation between ATP2B1 expression and the other markers. Rather, we noticed a uniform pattern of expression of CD112, CLEC9A, GPRC5C, and EPCR across CD49f+ATP2B1+ and CD49f+ATP2B1− HSC (SI Appendix, Fig. S1G). Overall, these results show that subdividing human CD49f+ LT-HSC based on ATP2B1 expression identifies a population of LT-HSC unrelated to previously described HSC markers (SI Appendix, Table S1).

ATP2B1+ LT-HSC Have Superior In Vitro Clonogenic Potential.

To investigate functional differences among the newly defined HSC subsets across ontogeny, in vitro colony forming assays were performed on Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS)-isolated CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC from FL, CB, and mPB (n = 5 FL, 14 CB, 10 mPB donors; See SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). The total number of colonies was the highest in FL (mean 47.6 colonies/100 cells plated) compared to CB (mean 29.5 colonies, P < 0.0087) and mPB (mean seven colonies, P < 0.0001) samples (SI Appendix, Fig. S2B), in line with previous reports of superior clonogenic output from FL-derived CD49f+ LT-HSC (10). The clonogenic potential of the CD49f+ATP2B1+ subset was significantly lower than the CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– subsets in CB and mPB; no significant differences in output were observed in FL (Fig. 2A). However, the proportion of CD34+ cells by flow cytometry was highest in CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC from all sources (Fig. 2B). Accordingly, serial replating was the highest from CD49f+ATP2B1+ compared to CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– progeny for all three HSC sources (Fig. 2C). Notably, an increased proportion of CD34+ cells was retained in secondary colonies derived from CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells across all HSC sources (Fig. 2D). Differences in erythroid, granulocyte, and myeloid lineage output were observed, but majority were lost upon replating and were not consistent across FL, CB, and mPB (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 C and D).

To assess lineage output at higher resolution, single-cell stroma-based differentiation assays were performed from index-sorted FL (n = 1,280), CB (n = 1,858), and mPB (n = 5,036) CD90+ HSC (SI Appendix, Fig. S2E). The cloning efficiency was higher from FL-derived HSC (mean 88.1%) than CB (mean 58.59%) and mPB (mean 41.2%; See SI Appendix, Fig. S2F). Within those, CD49f–ATP2B1– had the highest clonogenicity followed by CD49f+ATP2B1– then CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC in CB and mPB, but not FL, in line with results from the colony-forming assays (SI Appendix, Fig. S2F). Broadly, the lineage output between the colonies derived from each of the three HSC subpopulations was comparable (Fig. 2E and SI Appendix, Fig. S2 G–I). However, in FL and CB, but not in mPB, the abundance of clones containing exclusively CD34+ cells was higher from HSC classified as CD49f+ATP2B1+ compared to CD49f+ATP2B1–; such clones were absent in colonies derived from CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC (Fig. 2F). Furthermore, the proportion and total number of CD34+ cells among progeny was highest in clones derived from CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC across all three sources (Fig. 2 F and G), although there was no difference in total number of cells per clone between these populations (SI Appendix, Fig. S2J). There was no clear correlation between ATP2B1 fluorescence intensity in the parent cell and the number of CD34+ progeny, suggesting the absolute level of expression of ATP2B1 does not contribute to this phenotype (SI Appendix, Fig. S2K). Importantly, ATP2B1+ LT-HSC gave rise to 21% (FL), 43% (CB) and 44% (mPB) multilineage colonies, with three or more of the following lineages: CD34+, erythroid, myeloid, megakaryocytes, and natural killer (Fig. 2H). This was consistent across biological replicates and lineages, and particularly marked when comparing CB and mPB (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 L–N).

To compare the functional output of ATP2B1+ LT-HSC with previously reported human HSC markers (6–9), we sorted CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD45RA−CD49f+EPCR+, CD49f+CD112lo, CD49f+CLEC9AhiCD34lo, and CD34+CD38−GPRC5C+ HSC from CB (n = 6) and performed serial in vitro clonogenic assays. Although CD49f+ATP2B1+ resulted in fewer colonies (1.9 to 2.3-fold) compared to all other populations, their clonogenic output upon secondary replating was the highest (1.4 to 2.3-fold; Fig. 2 I and J). Moreover, an increase in granulocyte colony type was observed in primary colonies but balanced multilineage output was seen upon replating (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 O and P). Finally, CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells retained the highest proportion of CD34+ cells in both primary and secondary colonies, compared to all the other HSC subsets (Fig. 2 K and L). Thus, ATP2B1+ LT-HSC have the highest long-term clonogenic capacity among all HSC populations assessed.

To determine whether the calcium transport activity of ATP2B1 was important for the higher clonogenic potential of the CD49f+ATP2B1+ subset, we performed colony forming assays in the presence or absence of an ATP2B1 inhibitor, PI-8 (29). PI-8 treatment significantly increased colony output from CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC from FL, CB, or mPB sources in the first plating, and decreased output in secondary plating, phenocopying colony output of the respective CD49f+ATP2B1– populations (Fig. 2 M and N). In agreement, the percentage of CD34+ cells in both primary and secondary colonies from CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells following PI-8 treatment was decreased compared to control (Fig. 2 O and P). Neither clonogenicity nor CD34+ abundance were impacted by PI-8 treatment of CD49f+ATP2B1– HSC (Fig. 2 M–P). Lineage output was not consistently skewed after PI-8 treatment (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 Q and R). Overall, these data show that surface expression and calcium transport function of ATP2B1 denotes a subset of LT-HSC with superior long-term clonogenic capacity from fetal, neonatal, and adult sources.

CD49f+ATP2B1+ LT-HSC Are Enriched For TFEB-Regulated Lysosomal-Associated Programs.

To gain insight into molecular programs governing the superior long-term clonogenic potential of ATP2B1-expressing HSC, scMultiome profiling of FACS-purified CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– CB-HSC was performed (SI Appendix, Fig. S3A). As there was delayed clonogenic output from CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC, we hypothesized that ATP2B1 would demarcate more quiescent HSC, a corollary of superior HSC stemness (4, 8, 12, 13). We have previously defined functionally validated transcriptional and epigenetic signatures that distinguish quiescent (q)-LT-HSPC from activated (Act)-HSPC (12, 13). Epigenetic and transcriptional enrichment scores were computed for these signatures to assess molecular facets of quiescence (30, 31). Epigenetic features marking LT-HSPC were enriched in CD49f+ATP2B1+ compared to the other HSC subsets (Fig. 3A and SI Appendix, Fig. S3B). Moreover, a qLT vs. aLT transcriptional signature (12) was concurrently enriched in CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC, indicating they are in a more quiescent state than other HSC (Fig. 3B). Other HSC programs, namely signatures identifying HSC vs. Progenitors, were also enriched in CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC (32). Conversely, signatures associated with cell cycle priming and anabolic processes (i.e., MYC TARGETS, E2F TARGETS, HSC scRNA Cycle Primed) were significantly downregulated (Fig. 3C).

Since such analyses relied on known signatures to assign population identity, we wanted to gain an unbiased understanding of cell states independent of population assignment. We extracted transcriptional programs using linked non-negative matrix factorization (L-NMF) (33) and we identified 23 signatures, 20 of which were variably expressed across the HSC subsets. Three signatures showed significant differential enrichment: L-NMF16 (enriched in CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC), L-NMF6 (increased in expression from CD49f+ATP2B1+ to CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC), and L-NMF23 (highly enriched in CD49f–ATP2B1–; absent in CD49f+ATP2B1+; expressed at low levels in CD49f+ATP2B1– HSC) (Fig. 3 D–F and SI Appendix, Fig. S3C). Gene set enrichment analysis revealed that the genes comprising L-NMF16 were enriched in autophagy, catabolism, endolysosomal trafficking and other lysosome-related programs (Fig. 3G). Conversely, gene sets marking cellular proliferation and stem cell differentiation were most associated with L-NMF6 (SI Appendix, Fig. S3D).

Next, L-NMF16 and L-NMF6 enrichment in single cells was correlated to corresponding LT/HSPC and Act/HSPC chromatin signature enrichment (13). The LT/HSPC signature was enriched in L-NMF16-expressing cells, whereas the Act/HSPC signature was enriched in L-NMF6-expressing cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S3E). We then focused on the chromatin differences between CD49f+ATP2B1+ and CD49f+ATP2B1– HSC to gain more granularity within the LT/HSPC pool. Unique scATAC peaks enriched in either CD49f+ATP2B1+ (10,800 sites) or CD49f+ATP2B1– (9,450 sites) HSC were compared. Analysis of transcription factor (TF) accessibility revealed CTCF/BORIS (13) and MYC binding sites (12) were slightly depleted in CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S3F), motifs whose accessibility are associated with activated LT-HSC (12, 13). Conversely, both showed similar accessibility for HOXB7, a TF common to both LT- and ST-HSC (SI Appendix, Fig. S3F) (12). Importantly, gene regulatory domains within accessible sites unique to CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC revealed enrichment in catabolism, endosomal vesicle trafficking, and autophagy pathways whereas sites unique to CD49f+ATP2B1– were enriched in mitogenic and proliferative pathways which are typical hallmarks of differentiating stem cells (Fig. 3H and SI Appendix, Fig. S3G) (12). Thus, our scMultiome analysis uncovers distinct transcriptional and epigenetic programs in ATP2B1+ and ATP2B1– LT-HSC that are linked to stemness, including endolysosome-related pathways.

We have previously shown that a TFEB-mediated endolysosomal program governs LT-HSC self-renewal properties (12). To validate the enrichment of lysosomal pathways in CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells from our scMultiome analyses and evaluate conservation across distinct stem cell sources, immunofluorescence microscopy was used to quantify TFEB in individual cells from FL, CB, and mPB donors (12). Total TFEB staining patterns across the three HSC subsets differed across FL, CB, and mPB, and was lower in CB/mPB CD49f+ATP2B1+ (SI Appendix, Fig. S3H). However, the nuclear-to-non-nuclear fraction of TFEB was significantly higher in CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC across all sources (Fig. 3I). LAMP1 staining was not different in CD49f+ATP2B1+ vs. CD49f+ATP2B1− HSC across all sources (SI Appendix, Fig. S3I). Cultured CB-LT-HSC with higher lysosome acidification, as measured using LysoTracker, were previously shown to have superior serial repopulating ability in xenograft assays (12). When CB-derived HSC subsets were analyzed after 16h of culture (4, 16), CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells had higher LysoTracker intensity compared to other fractions (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 J and K).

Low calcium was shown to increase HSC fitness in culture (19). To determine whether the higher LysoTracker staining in CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells was linked to calcium transport activity, we cultured CB-derived CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1−, and CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC in the presence of high and low calcium (SI Appendix, Fig. S3J). At 0h, LysoTracker MFI was similar across all our subsets, and progressively increased with time in culture with low calcium (SI Appendix, Fig. S3L). In contrast, LysoTracker MFI was greatly reduced for CD49f+ATP2B1− and CD49f−ATP2B1− HSC after culture in high calcium, but not for CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC throughout the time course (Fig. 3J and SI Appendix, Fig. S3M). Furthermore, while the proportion of CD90+ cultured-LT (cLT) cells was retained in ATP2B1-expressing HSC regardless of the calcium conditions, cLT cells were greatly reduced in CD49f+ATP2B1− and CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC cultured for 2w in high vs. low calcium (Fig. 3K). In sum, these data indicate ATP2B1 surface expression marks LT-HSC with enriched nuclear TFEB localization, higher lysosomal acidification, and increased resilience to high calcium.

Nuclear TFEB localization and LysoTracker intensity are lower in activated LT-HSC compared to quiescent LT-HSC, while CDK6 is inversely regulated (4, 12). We used immunofluorescence microscopy to investigate CDK6 expression in CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells across lifespan. Nuclear-to-non-nuclear CDK6 localization was significantly lower in CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells compared to the other HSC subsets from CB and mPB, but not significantly different between FL HSC subsets (Fig. 3 L and M). These data are in line with our primary clonogenic assays where no differences between our three HSC subsets were observed in highly active FL HSC, but a lower output, congruent with reduced activation, was observed from CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC compared to CD49f+ATP2B1− and CD49f–ATP2B1– cells in CB and mPB HSC, a setting where quiescent and activated HSC coexist.

CD49f+ATP2B1+ LT-HSC Display Superior In Vivo Repopulation and Self-Renewal Capacity.

To investigate in vivo functionality, equal numbers of CB-derived CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC were transplanted into NSG mice. Repopulation was assessed after 4 wk (4w), when LT-HSC are active and proliferating, and after 20w, when the graft is sustained by a primarily quiescent LT-HSC compartment (Fig. 4A) (4). Although engraftment was similar at 4w (Fig. 4B and SI Appendix, Fig. S4A), the proportion of primitive CD34+CD19– was significantly higher in mice engrafted with CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC (Fig. 4C and SI Appendix, Fig. S4B), concordant with in vitro assays (Fig. 2B). By 20w, engraftment was significantly higher from CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC; CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC resulted in the lowest engraftment, indicating that the latter HSC subset has limited sustained proliferative output capacity (Fig. 4D and SI Appendix, Fig. S4 C–E). CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells also resulted in grafts with the highest proportion of CD34+CD19– cells at 20w (Fig. 4E and SI Appendix, Fig. S4 F and G). There was no consistent lineage bias observed within leukocytes throughout the experiments (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 H–L), though a higher fraction of GlyA+ cells was found in mice engrafted with the CD49f+ATP2B1+ fraction compared to the other HSC subsets (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 M and N). Based on secondary transplantation with limiting dilution assays (LDA) into NSG-SGM3 recipients, which permit more sensitive HSC detection (8) (Fig. 4F), grafts initiated with CD49f+ATP2B1+ cells had a 4.65-fold increase in HSC frequency compared to grafts from CD49f+ATP2B1– HSC (Fig. 4 G and H). Note that serial transplantation of the CD49f–ATP2B1– primary xenografts was not attempted due to low cellularity (SI Appendix, Fig. S4D). While all secondary engrafted mice had multilineage reconstitution, the number of engrafted cells tended to be higher from CD49f+ATP2B1+ over CD49f+ATP2B1– xenografts (Fig. 4 I and J and SI Appendix, Fig. S4 O-R). Finally, to quantify the absolute frequency of repopulating HSC in CB-derived CD49f+ATP2B1+ and CD49f+ATP2B1–, we transplanted NSG-SGM3 mice with cell doses ranging from 2 to 50 cells and assessed engraftment after 8w (Fig. 4 K and L). As all 2 cell doses were engrafted, the repopulating frequency of CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC was calculated as 1/1 to 1/3, while CD49f+ATP2B1– had a repopulating frequency of 1/16, which is more similar to the 1/10 previously reported for CD49f+ HSC (Fig. 4 L and M) (5). All engrafted mice had multilineage reconstitution; yet, the number of hCD45+ and lineage positive cells (CD33+ and CD19+) was higher in grafts from CD49f+ATP2B1+ than CD49f+ATP2B1– HSC (Fig. 4 N and O and SI Appendix, Fig. S4 S–X). Overall, these data validate ATP2B1 as a marker for LT-HSC with superior long-term self-renewal and the highest HSC frequency described to date.

Discussion

Here, we demonstrate that ATP2B1 is expressed on the surface of human LT-HSC in FL and CB, and modestly present on adult mPB and BM LT-HSC. Similar to previously described markers, ATP2B1 surface staining identifies a single continuous HSC population rather than discrete subsets. Nonetheless, ATP2B1 expression bifurcates the widely used gold standard marker CD49f into two populations with distinct functional properties. When compared to CD49f+ATP2B1– HSC, the CD49f+ATP2B1+ subset had higher serial clonogenic progenitor replating and better repopulation and self-renewal in xenografts. ATP2B1 expression and serial clonogenic output were both much higher in LT-HSC from human FL followed by a gradual reduction in CB and mPB; the latter finding is consistent with prior studies using less purified CD34+ subsets (10). While ATP2B1 expression is heterogeneous across the human lifespan, our data suggest its expression is reflective of functional stemness programs with demonstration of unit purity in transplantation using CB HSC. Thus, interrogation of CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC across ontogeny should reveal both common stemness programs along with those that change from fetal to adult HSC states.

Recent scRNAseq of HSPCs across the human lifespan have uncovered dynamic changes associated with human blood progenitors from fetal development through adulthood (34, 35). These studies reinforced the importance of integrating transcriptional and functional features of HSPCs at different developmental stages (28), but still did not achieve enough resolution to capture specific facets of LT-HSC biology. Our study dissects both the molecular and functional properties of human CD90+ HSC-derived subsets at single-cell resolution. scMultiome profiling identified TFEB-mediated control of catabolic processes as one of the molecular mechanisms underpinning the distinct stemness advantage of CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC. These findings refine our prior studies that linked TFEB activity to LT-HSC vs. ST-HSC by showing that TFEB and lysosomal activity preserved quiescence and enhanced self-renewal of CB LT-HSC (12). Our work solidifies the value of quantifying the nuclear-versus-non-nuclear localization of TFEB—in addition to its total content—as an extra layer of molecular information that helps identify LT-HSC with superior functionality across a spectrum of HSC sources (12).

Our study suggests a link between ATP2B1 surface expression and LT-HSC-specific endolysosomal programs and calcium homeostasis, pinpointing particular pathways regulating HSC functionality in the human setting. Autophagy is a catabolic process, centered at the lysosome, directly implicated in HSC dormancy and self-renewal (36–39). Further, recent studies point to metabolic and inflammatory stress programs contributing to the functional attrition of HSC with human development (17, 40–42), though many functionally young HSC can persist into old age (43, 44). Murine HSC are found in niches with elevated calcium (45). Although autophagy is decreased overall in murine HSC during aging, a subset of adult HSC activates autophagy to counteract stress- and inflammation-associated metabolic impairment (43). While we have not directly interrogated autophagy flux in this study, we hypothesize that the ATP2B1+CD49f+ HSC we have identified in humans are the ortholog of the aforementioned autophagy-activated, stress-resistant HSC, which perhaps function by preserving calcium homeostasis through development—and potentially pinpointing why ATP2B1+CD49f+ numbers are decreased by ~threefold in mPB as compared to CB. Future studies employing robust low-input-to-single-cell metabolomic profiling to directly measure metabolites in ATP2B1+ and ATP2B1– LT-HSC should shed considerable light on human HSC heterogeneity (46). Overall, our findings serve as a foundation to accelerate a deeper understanding of the molecular and metabolic programs governing human LT-HSC across multiple stem cell sources.

Limitations of the Study.

In our study, we demonstrate that ATP2B1 surface expression allows purification of functionally distinct human LT-HSC across ontogeny. We relied on CB as being representative for the gold-standard xenotransplantation assays as well as scMultiomics, as FL, mPB, and BM are limited resources. Concerning xenotransplantation assays, our conclusions are limited to HSC transplanted by intrafemoral injection, and further work is required to ascertain the homing capacity of ATP2B1-expressing LT-HSC relative to their counterparts that lack expression. We also elected to utilize SGM3 mice to determine primary SRC frequency, due to their enhanced sensitivity of HSC detection. However, a direct comparison between SGM3 and NSG mice needs to be done. On the mechanistic side, we have not determined if the expression of ATP2B1 is sufficient to endow stemness properties onto human HSPC. Any genetic perturbation of ATP2B1 would require in vitro culture which has been shown to trigger immune-mediated inflammatory pathways and genetic aberrations not observed at homeostasis (47). Furthermore, overexpressing ATP2B1 in primary cells is challenging owing to the larger size of the ATP2B1 transgene (>3.5 kb). ATP2B1 also has multiple transcript variants, and we have not been able to isolate which are expressed in HSC or important for the phenotypes observed in this study, subsequently making Cas9-gRNA-mediated targeting a significant challenge. To overcome some of these hurdles, we functionally validated the role of ATP2B1 in our assays through high and low calcium culture and by using the selective small molecule inhibitor PI-8 in unperturbed settings. Despite our data suggesting that ATP2B1 expression and the endolysosomal pathways are connected, future mechanistic studies are needed to investigate this hypothesis and underpin the complex mechanism endowing CD49f+ATP2B1+ HSC with overall superior function and resilience to culture in high calcium.

Materials and Methods

Cord Blood (CB), Fetal Liver (FL), Mobilized Peripheral Blood (mPB), and Bone Marrow (BM) Samples.

All primary samples were obtained with informed consent. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards of the University Health Network, and where applicable, Trillium Health Partners, William Osler Health Centre, and Queen’s University Health Sciences and Affiliated Teaching Hospitals Research Ethics Board, Ontario, Canada. mPB from G-CSF-treated healthy donors (female and male, aged 22 to 65 y) were obtained from the Hans Messner Allogeneic Transplant Program via the Leukemia Tissue Bank at Princess Margaret Cancer Centre. BM samples were obtained from consenting adults undergoing total hip arthroplasty surgery at Kingston General Hospital and Hotel Dieu Hospital in Kingston, Ontario, Canada. CD34+ enrichment of CB was conducted as previously described and adapted for BM and mPB (17). FL were obtained, processed, and CD34+-enriched as previously described (48). Cells were cryopreserved at −150 °C.

Flow Cytometry and Cell Sorting.

CD34+ enriched samples were thawed and stained for fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) of blood progenitors as previously reported (15), with the addition of an anti-ATP2B1-MaxLight550 antibody (see SI Appendix for details). Propidium Iodide (PI) was used to exclude dead cells. Samples were analyzed on a BD Symphony A1 instrument, or sorted on BD FACSAria Fusion or BD Symphony S6 instruments (4, 5, 12). Fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls without ATP2B1-MaxLight550 were used to establish gates in each sample. Data were analyzed on FlowJo v10.

Colony Forming Cell Assay.

250 FL- and CB-, and 1,500 mPB-derived CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1– and CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC were sorted into 2.5 mL of Methocult H4034 optimum (STEMCELL Technologies) as described in detail in the SI Appendix.

Single-Cell Differentiation Assays on MS5 Stroma.

In vitro differentiation assays of single CD19−CD34+CD38−CD45RA−CD90+ cells (HSC) were conducted as previously described (8). Fluorescence intensities for channels reflecting CD49f and ATP2B1 expression were indexed. Lineage was evaluated by flow cytometry essentially as described (8, 49). Clonal lineage output was determined and aligned with the fluorescence intensity for ATP2B1 at initial single-cell deposition.

Immunofluorescence Microscopy.

HSC populations were prepared for immunostaining as described previously (12). The following primary antibodies were used: Rb-anti-hATP2B1, Sh-anti-hLAMP1-AlexaFluor488, and Ms-anti-hTFEB. The following secondary antibodies were used: Dy-anti-Rb-AlexaFluor568 and Gt-anti-Ms-AlexaFluor568. Images of individual cells were captured with a Zeiss LSM700 confocal microscope (oil, ×63/1.4 NA, Zen 2012) or a Leica LP8 confocal microscope (HC PL APO 63×/1.40 NA Oil immersion CS2), and images were processed and analyzed with ImageJ/Fiji as previously described (8). Images from samples stained with secondary antibody only were used as threshold controls to remove background staining.

Xenotransplantation.

Animal experiments were done in accordance with institutional guidelines approved by the UHN Animal care committee. NOD.Cg-PrkdcscidIl2rgtm1Wjl/SzJ (NSG) and NOD.Cg-PrkdcscidIl2rgtm1WjlTg(CMV-IL3, CSF2, KITLG)1Eav/MloySzJ (NSG-SGM3) mice were transplanted as described (8). Briefly, age- and sex-matched 8-12-week-old male/female NSG mice were conditioned with 225cGy of gamma radiation (GammaCell40). HSC were fractionated into the indicated populations by CD49f and ATP2B1 expression using FACS. Sorted cells were transplanted by intrafemoral injection. Mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation at the indicated time points postengraftment, and the long bones were flushed with Iscove’s modified Dulbecco’s medium (IMDM) with 5% FBS. Spleens were macerated in IMDM+5%FBS. Cells were stained for flow cytometry; see SI Appendix for details. Cryopreserved bone marrow from primary transplanted mice were used to gauge secondary transplantation. Primary and secondary transplants with limiting dilution are described in detail in the SI Appendix.

Statistical Analysis.

GraphPad Prism (v10.2.3) was used for statistical analyses. Statistical tests are reported in the respective figure legends and statistical significance reported as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001. Unless otherwise noted, P-value >0.05 is not shown. Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests were done for normality, if the sample size allowed; if normal-distribution assumptions were not valid, Mann–Whitney U tests (two-tailed) or Kruskal–Wallis tests were done for single or multiple comparisons, respectively. One-way ANOVAs were used to compare means among three or more independent groups when normality was assumed. Post hoc tests with multiple testing corrections were completed to compare all pairs of treatment groups when the overall P-value <0.05.

Cell Culture.

HSC populations were resuspended in X-VIVO 10 medium supplemented with 1% BSA (Roche), SCF (100 ng/mL), FLT3L (100 ng/mL), TPO (50 ng/mL), IL-7 (10 ng/mL), 1× L-glutamine, and 1× penicillin/streptomycin, and cultured for 16h before LysoTracker analysis. Culture in high and low calcium was done as previously described (19); see SI Appendix for details.

LysoTracker Analysis.

Cells were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C with 75 nM LysoTracker in culture media. After staining, cells were washed and resuspended in PI (1:10,000) for flow cytometry.

scMultiome (RNA+ATAC).

Cryopreserved CB from multiple donors was pooled and sorted for CD49f+ATP2B1+, CD49f+ATP2B1–, CD49f–ATP2B1– HSC as above. The three populations were processed by the Princess Margaret Genomics Centre for 10× Genomics Chromium scMultiome using their standard pipeline. scMultiome data from previous studies (SI Appendix, Fig. S1A) were processed as described therein (17). scMultiome data from this study were pooled together and processed essentially as previously described (50). Details for filtering, batch correction, markers gene/chromatin peak isolation and integrative analysis are provided in the SI Appendix. HOMER (http://homer.ucsd.edu/homer/motif/) was used to identify enriched motifs and GREAT (v.4.0.4, http://great.stanford.edu/public/html/) was used to predict the functions of cis-regulatory regions; details are in the SI Appendix.

Comments on Morphological Evaluation of Facial Pigmented Lesions with Line-Field Confocal Optical Coherence Tomography: Correlation with Reflectance Confocal Microscopy: A Pilot Study
Authors: Manas Bajpai
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

With great interest I read the article titled Morphological Evaluation of Facial Pigmented Lesions with Line-Field Confocal Optical Coherence Tomography: Correlation with Reflectance Confocal Microscopy: A pilot study, authored by Lenoir C et al. [1]. I found this article highly enlightening.

Their preliminary findings suggested a significant correlation between the morphological patterns identified by LC-OCT and RCM, indicating that LC-OCT can indeed visualize diagnostic features relevant to evaluating pigmented lesions. Furthermore, the study highlighted LC-OCT’s advantages in terms of imaging speed and depth penetration, alongside its ability to generate 3D reconstructions, offering a more comprehensive tissue evaluation compared to the typically 2D views provided by RCM. This rapid acquisition time and detailed volumetric information suggest LC-OCT’s potential for more efficient and thorough screenings. However, the most significant limitation of any pilot study is its small sample size. While useful for demonstrating feasibility and generating hypotheses, a limited number of cases restrict the statistical power to draw definitive conclusions about diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, or specificity. The findings, therefore, remain preliminary and may not be generalizable to a broader, more diverse patient population or a wider spectrum of lesion types. While the study aimed to correlate LC-OCT findings with RCM, the ultimate gold standard for diagnosis remains histopathology. The abstract implies a focus on morphological correlation between the two imaging modalities rather than a direct correlation with the definitive histopathological diagnosis for all evaluated lesions. For a diagnostic tool, the absence of comprehensive histopathological confirmation for every lesion limits the ability to conclusively determine LC-OCT’s true diagnostic accuracy or its ability to differentiate benign from malignant lesions with certainty. This is a critical gap that larger follow-up studies must fill.

Interpretation of advanced imaging modalities like RCM and LC-OCT requires significant expertise and training. While the study utilized experienced interpreters, results may vary among less experienced operators, highlighting the need for standardized training protocols and potentially automated analysis tools in the future. The inherent subjectivity in interpreting subtle morphological features across different modalities can also introduce variability.

The study likely focused on a specific subset of “pigmented lesions.” The diagnostic utility of LC-OCT for other skin neoplasms (e.g., amelanotic melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma) or inflammatory conditions would require separate evaluation. As a cutting-edge technology, LC-OCT devices are likely to be expensive and not widely available in clinical settings. The study, while demonstrating technical promise, does not address the practical considerations of cost-effectiveness, integration into routine clinical workflow, or the necessary infrastructure for widespread adoption.

The pilot study by Lenoir et al. represents a significant step forward in the noninvasive evaluation of facial pigmented lesions. It strongly suggests that LC-OCT is not merely a research tool but possesses genuine potential for clinical application. Its ability to provide rapid, high-resolution 3D images offers a qualitative leap in diagnostic precision, potentially reducing the need for costly and invasive biopsies, especially for common benign lesions.

However, the path to widespread clinical adoption requires rigorous validation.

Definitive studies with significantly larger patient cohorts and diverse lesion types are essential to establish LC-OCT’s diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, positive/negative predictive values) against histopathology for a full spectrum of skin lesions. Every imaged lesion should ideally have a corresponding histopathological diagnosis to serve as the ground truth. Research should evaluate the consistency of LC-OCT interpretation among different users and develop robust training programs. The large datasets generated by LC-OCT are ideal for machine learning and artificial intelligence applications, which could aid in automated feature detection and potentially reduce observer dependence.

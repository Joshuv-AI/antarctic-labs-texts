Finerenone in IgA nephropathy: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jidi Wu, Yanqun Li, Li Luo, Qianshi Wu, Yulin Mou, Yong Xu, Qifu Li, Qian Ren, Yan Qiao, Wei Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background​ Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is a leading cause of chronic kidney disease worldwide, creating a significant need for novel, effective therapies. Finerenone has demonstrated renal protective benefits in diabetic kidney disease, but its efficacy and safety profile specifically in IgAN require comprehensive evaluation.Objective​ This study aimed to systematically assess the therapeutic potential and safety of finerenone in patients with IgAN.Methods​ A systematic literature search of PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library was conducted up to November 25, 2025. Cohort studies involving adults with biopsy-proven IgAN, comparing finerenone plus standard care against standard care alone with at least 6 months of follow-up, were included. Data on urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPCR), estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and adverse events (hyperkalemia) were extracted.Results​ The meta-analysis revealed that finerenone treatment significantly reduced proteinuria compared to control (MD -12.98, 95% CI: -16.04 to -9.93; P < 0.00001) and significantly improved renal function, indicated by an increase in eGFR (MD 4.41, 95% CI: 2.94 to 5.89; P < 0.00001). Statistical heterogeneity was negligible (I² = 0%) for both efficacy outcomes. Regarding safety, finerenone was associated with a non-significant increase in the risk of hyperkalemia (OR 2.12, 95% CI: 0.27 to 16.80; P = 0.48), though the confidence interval was wide, indicating uncertainty due to a low event rate.Conclusion​ In this meta-analysis, finerenone demonstrated significant benefits on short-term surrogate endpoints in patients with IgAN.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-026-04987-x.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and one of the leading causes of chronic kidney disease (CKD). Approximately 20% to 40% of patients with IgAN progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) within 10 to 20 years after diagnosis [1, 2]. The current standard of care primarily relies on optimized supportive therapy, including renin-angiotensin system (RAS) blockade and blood pressure control, while the use of immunosuppressants remains controversial due to limited long-term efficacy and safety concerns [3, 4]. Therefore, there is an unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies that can effectively delay disease progression while maintaining a favorable safety profile in patients with IgAN.

The mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) is a key hormonal receptor widely expressed in various human tissues and plays important physiological and pathological roles. Its overactivation contributes significantly to renal inflammation and fibrosis, leading to kidney injury. Compared with traditional steroidal MR antagonists, finerenone demonstrates higher selectivity and directly and comprehensively targets MR in both glomeruli and renal tubules, exerting more potent anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects that slow the progression of kidney disease [5]. Studies have shown that finerenone provides renal protection and reduces cardiovascular events in patients with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes [6, 7]. However, its efficacy in patients with IgA nephropathy remains unclear.

Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the therapeutic potential and safety of finerenone in the treatment of IgAN by synthesizing all available clinical trial data.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted according to the PRISMA guidelines [8]. The study protocol was registered a priori on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under registration number CRD420251233767.

Search strategy

A comprehensive systematic literature search was performed to identify all relevant publications up to November 25, 2025. Electronic databases including PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were queried. The search strategy was designed around two core concepts: the intervention (“finerenone”) and the disease (“IgA nephropathy”). Synonyms and controlled vocabulary terms were combined as follows: (finerenone​ OR BAY 94-8862) AND (IgA Nephropathy​ OR Immunoglobulin A Nephropathy​ OR Berger’s Disease). The complete search syntax for each database is available in Supplement Table 1. To maximize retrieval, the reference lists of all included articles and relevant review articles were manually screened.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The eligibility criteria were pre-specified according to the PICO(S) framework.

Participants: Adult patients (≥ 18 years) with a biopsy-confirmed diagnosis of IgA nephropathy.

Intervention: Administration of finerenone at any dosage.

Comparator: Placebo or standard of care.

Outcomes: The primary outcomes of interest were changes in renal function (estimated glomerular filtration rate, eGFR) and proteinuria (urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, UPCR; 24-hour proteinuria; or urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, UACR). Secondary outcomes encompassed safety endpoints, predominantly adverse events.

Study design: Randomized controlled trials (RCTs), prospective or retrospective cohort studies, and case-control studies were considered eligible.

Studies were required to have a minimum follow-up duration of 6 months and report sufficient data on at least one of the aforementioned outcomes. The exclusion criteria comprised: (1) non-original research (e.g., reviews, editorials, letters); (2) animal studies; (3) conference abstracts without full text; and (4) publications with incomplete or unavailable outcome data.

Data extraction

The study selection process was carried out independently by two investigators, who first screened titles and abstracts, then reviewed full texts of potentially eligible studies. For the assessment of methodological quality in included cohort studies, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) was utilized. In both screening and quality assessment stages, any disagreements between the two investigators were adjudicated by a third senior researcher to reach a consensus.

Methodological quality assessment

Two authors independently assessed the methodological quality of the included cohort studies using the Newcastle Ottawa Scale (NOS). Any disagreements were resolved through discussion. The NOS is a useful tool for assessing the risk of bias in non-randomized studies, and it mainly consists of three components: selection, comparability, and outcome (for cohort studies) or exposure (for case–control studies). The total score on the NOS can reach up to 9 points, with studies scoring above 5 considered to have high quality. The overall certainty of evidence for each critical outcome was assessed using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) framework.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis and subgroup analysis were conducted using Review Manager version 5.4.1 software, and sensitivity analysis was performed using Stata version 15.1 software to assess the stability of the results. Treatment effects were expressed as follows: For continuous outcomes (UPCR, eGFR slope), the mean difference (MD)​ with 95% confidence interval (CI) was calculated. If different measurement scales were used, the standardized mean difference (SMD) was applied. For dichotomous outcomes (hyperkalemia, composite endpoint), the odds ratio (OR)​ with 95% CI was calculated. Given anticipated clinical and methodological heterogeneity across studies, a random-effects model was used for all meta-analyses to provide a more conservative and generalizable estimate. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the I²statistic, with values of 25%, 50%, and 75% considered to represent low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively. The Pvalue of Cochran’s Q test was also reported. To assess the robustness of the primary outcome and address potential over-reliance on a single study with disproportionate weight, a sensitivity analysis was performed.

Results

Literature search

By searching the databases of PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane, a total of 4,397 initial articles were identified. After screening titles and abstracts, 3081 duplicate articles were removed, and 3,139 articles were excluded based on the established inclusion and exclusion criteria. Full text reading led to the exclusion of 34 studies, among which 26 studies involved only animal subjects, 2 study could not be included due to different grouping compared to the other included studies. According to the inclusion criteria, a final selection of 5 studies was made. The above process is depicted in Fig. 1.

Study characteristics

This study included a total of five cohort studies, involving 420 participants (207 for, 213 for placebo) met the inclusion criteria. Detailed information about the five studies analyzed can be found in Table 1. According to the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, the methodological quality of all five studies was high (scores ≥ 5 points), as shown in Table 1. Detailed scores for each component of the NOS for individual studies can be found in Supplementary Table 2.

UPCR

All three studies demonstrated a reduction in UPCR favoring the finerenone group. The study by Wang et al. carried the greatest weight (95.6%) in the pooled analysis. The meta-analysis using a random-effects model showed that finerenone treatment resulted in a statistically significant reduction in UPCR compared to control, with a mean difference (MD) of -12.98 (95% CI: -16.04 to -9.93). The test for overall effect was highly significant (Z = 8.34, P < 0.00001). The pooled results are presented in Fig. 2. We performed a sensitivity analysis by excluding the dominant study from the meta-analysis to assess the robustness of the pooled effect estimate. The result remained statistically significant, with a pooled MD of -19.80 (95% CI: -34.36 to -5.25, Z = 2.67, P = 0.008). The pooled results are presented in Fig. 3.

eGFR

All three studies demonstrated a positive mean difference in eGFR change favoring the finerenone group. The study by Wang et al. (2025) contributed the majority of the weight (65.0%) to the pooled estimate. The meta-analysis using a random-effects model showed that treatment with finerenone was associated with a statistically significant improvement in eGFR compared to the control group, with a mean difference (MD) of 4.41 mL/min/1.73 m² (95% CI: 2.94 to 5.89). The reported MD represents the difference in the absolute change in eGFR from baseline to the 6-month follow-up between the treatment and control groups. The test for overall effect was highly significant (Z = 5.86, P < 0.00001). The pooled results are presented in Fig. 4. We also performed a sensitivity analysis by excluding the dominant study to assess the robustness of the pooled effect estimate. The result remained statistically significant, with a pooled MD of 4.66 (95% CI: 1.29 to 8.04, Z = 2.71, P = 0.007). The pooled results are presented in Fig. 5.

Hyperkalemia

The risk of hyperkalemia associated with finerenone treatment was evaluated. The pooled meta-analysis using a Mantel-Haenszel random-effects model showed that the incidence of hyperkalemia was numerically higher in the finerenone group, with a combined odds ratio (OR) of 2.12. However, the 95% confidence interval was very wide, ranging from 0.27 to 16.80. The test for overall effect was not statistically significant (Z = 0.71, P = 0.48). The pooled analysis is presented in Fig. 6.

Certainty of the evidence

The certainty of evidence for the three primary outcomes assessed using the GRADE approach is summarized in Table 2. For the reduction in UPCR and the improvement in eGFR, the evidence was downgraded by one level due to serious imprecision (wide confidence intervals, particularly after sensitivity analysis for UPCR) and by one level for study limitations (all evidence derived from non-randomized observational studies), resulting in a very low​ certainty of evidence. For the outcome of hyperkalemia, the evidence was downgraded by two levels for very serious imprecision (extremely wide confidence interval and very few events) and by one level for study limitations, also resulting in a very low​ certainty of evidence. No factors for upgrading the evidence were identified.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis, synthesizing data from five studies, provides a preliminary but focused quantitative assessment of the efficacy and safety of finerenone in patients with IgA nephropathy. The principal findings indicate that over a 6-month treatment period, finerenone significantly reduces proteinuria, as measured by UPCR, and is associated with a significant improvement in eGFR, while showing a non-significant trend towards an increased risk of hyperkalemia.

Our analysis demonstrates a consistent and statistically significant renoprotective effect of finerenone in the short term. The pooled results showed a mean reduction in UPCR of approximately − 12.98% (95% CI: -16.04 to -9.93) and an increase in eGFR of 4.41 mL/min/1.73m2 (95% CI: 2.94 to 5.89) compared to standard care at 6 months. The treatment effects were remarkably consistent across the included studies, as evidenced by negligible statistical heterogeneity (I2 = 0% for both outcomes). This suggests a robust and reproducible benefit on these surrogate markers of kidney disease progression. Concerning safety, the meta-analysis of hyperkalemia events yielded a pooled odds ratio of 2.12, which was not statistically significant (95% CI: 0.27 to 16.80; P = 0.48). Although the point estimate (OR 2.12) suggests that finerenone may increase the risk of hyperkalemia, its extremely wide 95% confidence interval (0.27–16.80) indicates high uncertainty regarding the true effect size. This interval neither excludes the possibility of no significant risk nor rules out a clinically meaningful increase in risk.

Finerenone is a novel nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist with significant therapeutic value in CKD [5]. Clinical studies have confirmed that it significantly improves renal and cardiovascular outcomes in patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD [8]. Its core renoprotective mechanism involves the selective blockade of the mineralocorticoid receptor, thereby inhibiting aldosterone-mediated pathological responses. Research indicates that finerenone may confer renal protection by modulating macrophage related inflammatory pathways. Oral administration alleviates renal injury, primarily by inhibiting MR overactivation in macrophages. This leads to the downregulation of Gαi2 expression, resulting in attenuated inflammation and chemotaxis, along with the regulation of macrophage polarization and amelioration of apoptosis [8]. Moreover, in a renal ischemia–reperfusion injury model, its protective effect was associated with increased expression of M2 anti-inflammatory markers in renal macrophages. MR inhibition also promotes IL-4 receptor expression, facilitating macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype [9]. The core pathology of IgAN involves the deposition of IgA immune complexes in the mesangium, which activates the complement system and triggers inflammation [10]. Glycosylation-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) can form complexes with antibodies, activating complement via the alternative and lectin pathways and leading to glomerular injury [10]. These immune complexes also activate mesangial cells, prompting the release of pro-fibrotic factors that drive glomerulosclerosis and interstitial fibrosis [11–13]. Through these mechanisms, finerenone exerts anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic actions, reduces proteinuria, preserves glomerular filtration rate, and ultimately delays disease progression in patients with chronic kidney disease.

The difference in the absolute change in eGFR from baseline to the 6-month follow-up between the treatment and control groups. Although regression to the mean could theoretically influence within-group changes, the balanced baseline eGFR between groups minimizes its impact on the between-group difference​ reported here. The observed improvement is more plausibly linked to the treatment’s physiologic effect of reducing glomerular hyperfiltration and injury through pronounced antiproteinuric and anti-inflammatory actions. In the studies included in this meta-analysis, data on IgA nephropathy patients with advanced CKD (particularly eGFR < 25–30 mL/min/1.73 m²) are lacking. Therefore, our conclusions are primarily applicable to patients with mild-to-moderate renal impairment. Clinical studies have shown that in patients with advanced CKD (eGFR < 25 mL/min/1.73 m²) and type 2 diabetes, finerenone still confers renal benefit without significantly increasing the rate of permanent treatment discontinuation due to hyperkalemia [14]. Furthermore, finerenone significantly reduces the risk of developing nephrotic-range proteinuria in patients with diabetic kidney disease, suggesting its efficacy in controlling heavy proteinuria [15]. Based on this evidence from diabetic kidney disease, we speculate that the renoprotective mechanisms of finerenone may similarly benefit IgA nephropathy patients with impaired renal function or heavy proteinuria. However, this requires confirmation through future studies specifically designed in IgA nephropathy populations.

The conclusions of this meta-analysis must be interpreted within the context of its several important limitations. First, the evidence in this study is derived from observational cohort studies, thus carrying inherent limitations in causal inference. The current findings represent evidence of low to moderate certainty and require confirmation from future RCTs. Second, the follow-up duration of 6 months is short for a chronic, progressive disease like IgA nephropathy. While changes in UPCR and eGFR are validated surrogate endpoints, their long-term correlation with hard kidney outcomes in the context of finerenone therapy requires confirmation from larger, event-driven trials. Third, our analysis is based on aggregate study-level data rather than individual patient data, which precludes more nuanced subgroup analyses that could identify patients at differential benefit or risk. Fourthly, the analysis in this study is based on currently available evidence where RASI/SGLT2i served as background therapy, and does not include data on combination therapy with the latest targeted agents (e.g., APRI inhibitors, complement pathway inhibitors). As IgA nephropathy treatment enters the age of targeted therapy, exploring the potential of Finerenone in combination with these agents targeting upstream pathogenic mechanisms represents an important and interesting direction for future research. Finally, the search was restricted to published and indexed studies, introducing the potential for publication bias, although the small number of studies precluded a formal assessment.

Conclusion

The current findings represent evidence of low to moderate certainty and confirmation requires support from future RCTs. This meta-analysis indicates that, over a 6-month period, finerenone significantly reduces proteinuria and improves eGFR in patients with IgA nephropathy compared to standard care. The point estimate for hyperkalemia risk was increased, but the difference was not statistically significant, and the evidence remains highly uncertain due to the very low number of events. While these short-term surrogate endpoint results are promising, the long-term effects on hard kidney outcomes and the definitive safety profile, particularly regarding hyperkalemia, require confirmation from larger, long-term, event-driven randomized controlled trials.

Fitting without fittings Thanks: This research is part of a project in the SFB/TRR 191 Symplectic Structures in Geometry, Algebra and Dynamics , funded by the DFG
Johanna Bimmermann, Bernd Stratmann, Kai Zehmisch

Abstract
. We show that all symplectically aspherical fillings of the unit cotangent bundle of a given odd-dimensional sphere are diffeomorphic to the corresponding unit co-disc bundle. The concept of fittings previously introduced is not needed.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
57R17; 32Q65, 53D35, 57R80

1.
Introduction

A closed co-oriented contact manifold
(M,\xi)

often arises as the convex contact type boundary of a compact
symplectic manifold
(W,\omega)
,
which is called a (strong)
symplectic filling
of
(M,\xi)
.
This raises the question,
whether the symplectic filling
(W,\omega)
of
(M,\xi)

is unique.
As the blow up of a symplectic filling
(W,\omega)
of
(M,\xi)

is a symplectic filling of
(M,\xi)
,
we require the symplectic filling to be
symplectically aspherical
,
i.e.
W
admits no spherical homology
2
-class
on which the symplectic form
\omega
evaluates non-trivially.

Regarding the uniqueness question,
substantial knowledge has already been established.
For the
4
-dimensional case,
where requiring minimality
instead of symplectic asphericity is sufficient and
diffeomorphism classification addressing the symplectic structure is possible,
we refer to the introductions in
[
2
,
6
,
5
]
.
In higher dimensions,
there are examples where uniqueness up to diffeomorphism holds:
Eliashberg–Floer–McDuff proved
that a symplectically aspherical filling
of the standard contact sphere is diffeomorphic to a ball,
see
[
10
, Theorem 1.5]
.
For generalisations to subcritically Stein fillable contact manifolds,
or even Liouville split ones, we refer to the work of
Barth–Geiges–Zehmisch
[
2
]
and Zhou
[
11
]
,
resp.
The first examples in the critical regime,
taking the total space
of the unit cotangent bundle over
T^{n}
,
were independently found by Bowden–Gironella–Moreno
[
1
]

and Geiges–Kwon–Zehmisch
[
6
]
.
In
[
6
]
,
instead of a single
n
-torus
T^{n}
,
more generally a product of
T^{2}

with a product of unitary groups and spheres
is allowed.
If the base manifold is an odd-dimensional sphere,
partial uniqueness results were obtained by
Kwon–Zehmisch
[
9
]
and
Kwon–Oba
[
8
]
.
The aim of this note
is to remove all restrictions.
Our approach builds up on intermediate results from
[
9
]

and is inspired
by
[
8
]
.

Let
S^{2d+1}
,
d\geq 1
, be the unit sphere

\partial D^{2d+2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d+1}

provided with the round metric.
Equip the total space of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}S^{2d+1}

with the Liouville
1
-form
\lambda

given by
\mathbf{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}
in local coordinates.
Consider the induced contact structure
\xi

on the unit cotangent bundle
M=ST^{*}S^{2d+1}

together with the unit disc bundle

(W_{\!\st},\omega_{\st})=\big(DT^{*}S^{2d+1},\mathrm{d}\lambda\big)\,,

the so–called
standard filling
of the contact manifold
(M,\xi)
.

Theorem 1
.

The underlying smooth structure
W

of a symplectically aspherical filling
(W,\omega)
of
(M,\xi)

is diffeomorphic to
W_{\!\st}
.

The present work is devoted to proving the theorem.

2.
From properness to diffeomorphism type

For fixed
d\geq 1

let
(W,\omega)
be a symplectically aspherical filling of
(M,\xi)
.
In this section we will summarise intermediate results
about the diffeomorphism type of
W
obtained in
[
9
]
.
The notion of fittings is not involved.

2.1.
Characterisation via
h
-cobordism

[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
yields that

W
and
W_{\!\st}
are diffeomorphic if and only if

W
is simply connected and
the singular homology groups satisfy

H_{k}W=H_{k}W_{\!\st}
for all
k
.
This result is based on the
h
-cobordism theorem
applied to an
h
-cobordism that is constructed
using
\chi(S^{2d+1})=0
.

2.2.
Capping and reduction

[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
reduces the criterion recalled
in Section
2.1

to surjectivity properties of a certain map
in homotopy and homology.
The relevant map is constructed as follows:
Provide
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
with coordinates

\mathbf{z}=\big(z_{0},\ldots,z_{d}\big)\,,\quad\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{x}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{y}\,,

and denote the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}

by
\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}
.
The Fubini–Study symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}P^{d}

is denoted by
\omega_{\fs}
.
The graph of the
anti-Hopf map

S^{2d+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\,,\quad\mathbf{z}\longmapsto(\mathbf{z},[\bar{\mathbf{z}}])\,,

defines a Lagrangian embedding of

S^{2d+1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
.
Here,

S^{2d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{d}
,
\mathbf{z}\mapsto[\mathbf{z}]
,
is the Hopf map and
\mathbf{z}\mapsto\bar{\mathbf{z}}

indicates complex conjugation.

By Weinstein’s neighbourhood theorem
the anti-Hopf map extends to a symplectic embedding
of a disc neighbourhood
D_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}
,

\delta>0
, of
S^{2d+1}
in
T^{*}S^{2d+1}
.
Therefore,
S_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}
appears
as hypersurface
(M,\xi)
of contact type in

\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
provided with

\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}+\omega_{\fs}
.
Define a symplectic cap by setting

\cp:=\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\setminus\Int\big(D_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}\big)

equipped with the symplectic form

\omega_{\cp}:=\big(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}+\omega_{\fs}\big)|_{\cp}\,.

Gluing along contact type boundaries
yields a symplectic manifold

(Z,\Omega):=(W,\omega)\cup_{(M,\xi)}\big(\!\cp,\omega_{\cp}\big)\,.

Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
be a complex number
with sufficiently large absolut value,
so that
a_{0}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

is a complex hypersurface in
Z
.
Now,
by
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
,

W
and
W_{\!\st}
are diffeomorphic
provided that the inclusion map

a_{0}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\rightarrow Z

is surjective after applying

\pi_{1}
and
H_{k}(\,.\,;\mathbb{F})
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}

and all fields
\mathbb{F}
.

2.3.
Splitting off a factor

On
\mathbb{C}
, provided with coordinates
z_{0}=x_{0}+\mathrm{i}y_{0}
,
we consider the symplectic form
a(x_{0},y_{0})\mathrm{d}x_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{0}

for a smooth function
a\colon\thinspace\mathbb{C}\rightarrow(0,\infty)

that equals
1
on
B_{r}^{2}(0)
for some
r>1
and

a(x_{0},y_{0})=\big(1+x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}\big)^{-2}

for
(x_{0},y_{0})
in the complement of a compact set in
\mathbb{C}
.
The symplectic form extends to a symplectic form
\sigma

on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
via the affine chart
[1:z_{0}]\mapsto z_{0}
.
In particular,

\sigma
equals
\omega_{\fs}
in a neighbourhood of
\infty=[0:1]
.
Observe that the total area
A
of
\sigma
is at least
\pi r^{2}
.

We split off the
z_{0}
-factor

\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
of
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
.
Coordinates on the
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-factor are introduced as

\mathbf{z}^{\prime}=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})
.
We symplectically compactify partially to

\Big(\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d},\sigma+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\!\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}+\omega_{\fs}\Big)\,,

which, as in Section
2.2
,
can be assumed to contain

(M,\xi)
as hypersurface of contact type.
A symplectic cap

\widehat{\cp}:=\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\setminus\Int\big(D_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}\big)

with symplectic form

\hat{\omega}:=\sigma+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\!\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}+\omega_{\fs}

can be defined as in Section
2.2
,
as well as the glued symplectic manifold

\big(\hat{Z},\hat{\Omega}\big):=(W,\omega)\cup_{(M,\xi)}\Big(\widehat{\cp},\hat{\omega}\Big)\,.

2.4.
Complex cycles

The standard complex structure
on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

is denoted by

J_{\st}=\mathrm{i}\oplus\mathrm{i}\oplus\mathrm{i}
.
Consider the holomorphic spheres

C_{1}:=\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\pt\times\pt\,,\qquad C_{2}:=\pt\times\pt\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}\,,

in
\widehat{\cp}
,
where
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{d}
denotes a complex line
in the definition of
C_{2}
.
Define complex hypersurfaces

H_{1}:=\infty\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\,,\qquad H_{2}:=\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d-1}\,,

where
\mathbb{C}P^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{d}

denotes a complex hyperplane.
Observe that
H_{1}\subset\widehat{\cp}
,
while
H_{2}
is just a hypersurface in

\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
.
We choose
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and
\mathbb{C}P^{d-1}

to be in general position in
\mathbb{C}P^{d}
,
so that the intersection numbers

C_{i}\cdot H_{j}=\delta_{ij}

can be read off for
i,j=1,2
.

2.5.
Almost complex structure and maximum principle

Let
J
be a tame almost complex structure
on
(\hat{Z},\hat{\Omega})

that equals
J_{\st}
on
\widehat{\cp}
,
cf.
[
9
, Section 3.3]
.
Consider a holomorphic sphere

C=u(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
parametrised by
u\colon\thinspace\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow(\hat{Z},J)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The maximum principle implies
that if
C\subset\widehat{\cp}
, then
u

is of the form
u=(u^{1},\pt,u^{3})
, where
\pt\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and

u^{1}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
,
u^{3}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{d}

are holomorphic, cf.
[
9
, Lemma 3.2(i)]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Choose
R\in(1,r)
large enough
such that
M
appears as a hypersurface in

B_{R}^{2}(0)\times B_{R}^{2d}(0)\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

and define

W_{\square}:=W\cup\Big\{(z_{0},\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w})\in\widehat{\cp}\;\big|\;|z_{0}|,|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|<R\Big\}\,.

Assume
C
satisfies

C\cap\big(\hat{Z}\setminus W_{\square}\big)\neq\emptyset

and
C\cap H_{1}=\emptyset
,
then the maximum principle in
\mathbb{C}
- and in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-direction
applies as in
[
9
, Lemma 3.2(ii)]
.
Namely,
the
\mathbb{C}
- and
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-projections of
C
must be
contained in the level sets of the radial distance functions,
so that
C
in fact is contained in

\hat{Z}\setminus W_{\square}\subset\widehat{\cp}
.
By Remark
2.1
finally,

u
is of the form
u=(\pt,\pt,u^{3})
,
where
u^{3}:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{d}
is holomorphic.

2.6.
Moduli space and reduction

Define the
moduli space

\mathcal{M}

to be the set of all holomorphic maps

u\colon\thinspace\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow(\hat{Z},J)

with
u(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
homologous to
C_{1}
,
mapping the marked points

z\in\{\pm\varrho,\infty\}
for fixed
\varrho\in(R,r)

into
z\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
.
In
[
9
, Remark 3.3]
the following was observed:

All
u\in\mathcal{M}
satisfy
u\bullet H_{1}=1
,
hence, are simple,
and have symplectic energy equal to
E(u)=A>\pi r^{2}
.
In particular,
if
u(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\widehat{\cp}

for
u\in\mathcal{M}
, then
u=(\id,\pt,\pt)
by
Remark
2.1
,
so that
u
is Fredholm regular.
Consequently,

\mathcal{M}
is a smooth, naturally oriented

4d
-dimensional manifold
by choosing
J
on
\Int(W)
to be generic
such that
u\in\mathcal{M}
is Fredholm regular
whenever
u(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\cap\Int(W)\neq\emptyset
.

The maximum principle in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-direction
as in Remark
2.2
above,
shows that

\Big\{u\in\mathcal{M}\;\Big|\;u(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\cap\big\{(z_{0},\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w})\in\widehat{\cp}\;\big|\;|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\geq R\big\}\neq\emptyset\Big\}

equals

\big\{u=(\id,\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w})\;\big|\;\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\;|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\geq R,\;\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big\}\,.

Therefore,
the end of
\mathcal{M}
can be identified with

\big(\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus B^{2d}_{R}(0)\big)\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
.
The evaluation map

\widehat{\ev}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}\longrightarrow\hat{Z}\,,\quad(u,z)\longmapsto u(z)

restricted to the end equals

\big((\id,\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}),z\big)\mapsto(z,\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w})
.

Reducing to the criterion in Section
2.2
,

[
9
, Lemma 3.4]
shows:
If
\widehat{\ev}
is proper,
then
W
and
W_{\!\st}
are diffeomorphic.

2.7.
Noded curves and reduction

Define

\hat{Z}_{R}:=W\cup\Big\{(z_{0},\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w})\in\widehat{\cp}\;\big|\;|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\leq R\Big\}\,.

Uniform energy and
C^{0}
-bounds
discussed in Section
2.6

yield as in
[
9
, Section 3.4.1]

that any sequence
u^{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}
with

u^{\nu}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\hat{Z}_{R}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}

admits a subsequence (again denoted by)
u^{\nu}

that converges in the sense of Gromov compactness
to a stable holomorphic sphere
\mathbf{u}

with three fixed marked points.
Denoting the non-constant components of
\mathbf{u}

by
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
,
N\geq 1
,
the convergence is in
C^{\infty}
,
meaning that the evaluation map
\widehat{\ev}
is proper,
if and only if
N=1
.
In other words,

if it holds that always
N=1
,
then
W
and
W_{\!\st}
will be diffeomorphic.

Invoking positivity of intersections
with the complex hypersurface
H_{1}

and that
u^{\nu}\bullet H_{1}=1
holds for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}

as in
[
9
, Section 3.4.2]
yields
(after potentially relabeling the bubble spheres that)

u_{1}\bullet H_{1}=1
and
u_{j}\bullet H_{1}=0

for all
j\geq 2
.
Moreover, with Remark
2.2
,
we have for each
j\geq 2
that
either
u_{j}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset W_{\square}

or
u_{j}=(\pt,\pt,u^{3}_{j})
with

u^{3}_{j}:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{d}
holomorphic.
Observe that
u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\hat{Z}_{R}

and that
u_{j}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset Z_{R}
for all
j\geq 2
,
where

Z_{R}:=W\cup\Big\{(z_{0},\mathbf{z}^{\prime},\mathbf{w})\in\widehat{\cp}\setminus H_{1}\big|\;|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\leq R\Big\}\,.

Applying the Mayer–Vietoris sequence to the manifolds

\hat{Z}=W\cup_{M}\!\widehat{\cp}
and
Z=W\cup_{M}\!\cp
,
which are glued together along the simply connected hypersurface
M
,
yields a decomposition of spherical homology classes

H_{2}\hat{Z}\ni[u_{1}]=S_{1}+A_{1}\in H_{2}W\oplus H_{2}\widehat{\cp}

and

H_{2}Z\ni[u_{j}]=S_{j}+A_{j}\in H_{2}W\oplus H_{2}\!\cp\,,

j\geq 2
, resp.,
as in
[
9
, Section 3.4.3]
.
The respective classes
S_{j}
and
A_{j}

are sums of spherical classes for all
j=1,\dots,N
.
Observe that for
d\geq 2
the decompositions are unique;
for
d=1
unique up to adding the fibre class
of
M\cong S^{3}\times S^{2}
.
Symplectic asphericity of
(W,\omega)
yields

\hat{\Omega}(S_{j})=0\quad\text{and, hence,}\quad 0<E(u_{j})=\hat{\Omega}(A_{j})

for all
j=1,\dots,N
.
With the intersection pattern
from Section
2.4

and positivity of energy we find
as in
[
9
, Section 3.4.4]

that there exists
\ell_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

A_{1}=[C_{1}]+\ell_{1}[C_{2}]\quad\text{and}\quad A_{j}=\ell_{j}[C_{2}]\,,

for all
j\geq 2
.

We will continue the argument at the beginning of
Section
3
.

the intersection pattern form

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We take intersections of the bubble spheres
u_{j}
with
\hat{H}
.
Using affine charts for the composition of the map
u_{j}

with the dehomogenised polynomial,
which defines
\hat{H}
locally,
we obtain with unique continuation the following:
Either intersection points of
u_{j}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
with
\hat{H}
are isolated
or
u_{j}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\hat{H}
.
In the case of isolated intersections
the local intersection numbers are positive with
[
7
, p. 63]
.
Note that if
u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is contained in
\widehat{\cp}

then
\ell_{1}\geq 1
by Remark
2.1
.
However,
as
\hat{H}
can be compactified via homogenisation
as in Section
3.6
,
the sum of the local intersection numbers with
\hat{H}

is a homological invariant.
Therefore,
in either case, we get with the intersection numbers
from Section
3.5
that

0\leq u_{1}\bullet\hat{H}=1+\ell_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq\ell_{j}=u_{j}\bullet\hat{H}\,,

for all
j\geq 2
.
Of course the positivity of the
\ell_{j}
for
j\geq 2

was already observed in Section
2.7
.

On the other hand
C_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}[u_{j}]

and the expansions of the

at the end of Section

imply

1=C_{1}\cdot\hat{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{j}\cdot\hat{H}=(1+\ell_{1})+\ell_{2}+\cdots+\ell_{N}\geq N-1\geq 0\,.

It follows that either
N=1
with
\ell_{1}=0

or that
N=2
and
\ell_{1}=-1
,
\ell_{2}=1
.
The case
N=1
means properness of
\widehat{\ev}
.
In the case that
N=2
we get

A_{1}=[C_{1}]-[C_{2}]\quad\text{and}\quad A_{2}=[C_{2}]\,.

In other words

[u_{1}]=S_{1}+[C_{1}]-[C_{2}]\quad\text{and}\quad[u_{2}]=-S_{1}+[C_{2}]\,.

The two potentially remaining bubble spheres
have symplectic energy

E(u_{1})=A-\pi\quad\text{and}\quad E(u_{2})=\pi\,.

In order to exclude
N=2

observe that
\ell_{1}=-1
implies
u_{1}\bullet\hat{H}=0
.
As already remarked at the beginning of the proof,

u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\hat{H}\subset\widehat{\cp}

would imply that
u_{1}^{3}
represents
-[C_{2}]
holomorphically,
see Remark
2.1
.
We get that
u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
and
\hat{H}
are disjoint.
Evaluating
\hat{h}
along the curve
u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})

yields by the maximum principle that either

u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is contained in a level set of
\hat{h}
,
so that
u_{1}
is constant in view of the
-\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}\hat{h}\circ J)
–energy,
or
u_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is contained in
W^{\prime}
,
so that
u_{1}
is constant in view of
symplectic asphericity in Section
3.8
.
In either case,
we obtain a contradiction to the assumption
that bubble spheres are not constant.
∎

In order to complete the proof of

3.1.
Affine quadric

We provide
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}

with coordinates

(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})=\big(z_{0},\ldots,z_{d},w_{0},\ldots,w_{d}\big)

and consider the affine quadric

Q:=\big\{\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{w}=1\big\}\,.

A decomposition

\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{x}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{y}\,,\quad\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{u}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{v}\,,

in real and imaginary parts, yields

Q=\big\{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}=1+|\mathbf{y}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,,\,\,\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\big\}\,.

Consider the exhausting, strictly plurisubharmonic function

f\colon\thinspace Q\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by restricting the map

(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\big(|\mathbf{y}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\big)

to
Q
.
Observe that
f
vanishes precisely
along the totally real
(2d+1)
-dimensional sphere

\{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}=1\}
.

3.2.
Transformed affine quadric

The linear coordinate change

\Phi(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})=\big(\mathbf{z}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{w}\big)

maps the quadric
Q

introduced in Section
3.1
to

\Phi(Q)=\big\{\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}=1\big\}\,.

The advantage of the transformed coordinates is that

\Phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})=\big(\mathbf{x}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{u},\overline{\mathbf{x}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{u}}\big)

maps the real part
\{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}=1\}
of
Q
onto
the totally real
(2d+1)
-sphere

\Big\{(\mathbf{z},\bar{\mathbf{z}})\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\;\big|\;\mathbf{z}\in S^{2d+1}\Big\}=\{\mathbf{z}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{z}}=1\}\,.

Furthermore the composition
of the exhausting, strictly plurisubharmonic function
f

with the inverse of the coordinate change

\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w},\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z})\big)

yields the exhausting, strictly plurisubharmonic function

f\circ\Phi^{-1}\colon\thinspace\Phi(Q)\rightarrow[0,\infty)

given by the restriction of

f\circ\Phi^{-1}\big(\mathbf{x}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{v}\big)=\frac{1}{8}\big(|\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}|^{2}\big)

to the image quadric
\Phi(Q)
.

3.3.
Projectisation of the second factor

Denote the natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}

by
(a,\mathbf{w})\mapsto a\mathbf{w}
.
The projection
\mathbf{w}\mapsto[\mathbf{w}]

to the orbit space is a surjective submersion

[\,.\,]\colon\thinspace\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{d}
.
Define

\Psi:=(\id\oplus[\,.\,])\circ\Phi\,,

which is a surjective submersive holomorphic map

\Big\{(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\;\big|\;\mathbf{z}\neq\mathrm{i}\mathbf{w}\Big\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\,.

In coordinates, explicitly, we have

\Psi(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})=\big(\mathbf{z}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{w},[\mathbf{z}-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{w}]\big)\,.

As the domain of
\Psi
contains
Q
,
the restriction

\varphi:=\Psi|_{Q}\colon\thinspace Q\longrightarrow\big(\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus H

is a well defined biholomorphism,
where

H:=\Big\{\big(\mathbf{z},[\mathbf{w}]\big)\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\;\big|\;\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0\Big\}\,.

In addition, the image

\Big\{(\mathbf{z},[\bar{\mathbf{z}}])\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\;\big|\;\mathbf{z}\in S^{2d+1}\Big\}=\{\mathbf{z}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{z}}=1\}

of the anti-Hopf map introduced in Section
2.2

does not intersect
H
.
Furthermore the composite

\hat{f}:=f\circ\varphi^{-1}\colon\thinspace\big(\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus H\longrightarrow[0,\infty)

is exhausting, strictly plurisubharmonic function.

3.4.
Disc bundle structure

The total space of
TS^{2d+1}

can be understood as the set of all

(\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d+2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d+2}

such that
|\mathbf{q}^{\prime}|=1
and
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=0
.
Writing

\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u}\,,\quad\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{v}\,,

it is diffeomorphic to the quadric
Q
via the inverse of

Q\longrightarrow TS^{2d+1}\,,\quad\mathbf{q}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{p}\longmapsto\Big(\frac{\mathbf{q}}{|\mathbf{q}|},-|\mathbf{q}|\mathbf{p}\Big)\,.

Therefore,
the sublevel sets of

f(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{p}|^{2}
,
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})\in Q
,
and, hence, of
\hat{f}
,
admit the structure of a tubular neighbourhood of

\varphi\big(\{\mathbf{p}=0\}\big)
.

3.5.
Projectisation of the first factor

We consider the affine embedding

\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

given by

\big(\mathbf{z},[\mathbf{w}]\big)\longmapsto\Big([1:z_{0}],(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}),[\mathbf{w}]\Big)

using coordinates
[z_{0}^{\prime}:z_{0}]
on the
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-factor.
Define a complex hypersurface

\hat{H}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

by

z_{0}w_{0}+z_{0}^{\prime}\big(z_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+z_{d}w_{d}\big)=0\,,

which is the homogenisation of
\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0

corresponding to
[z_{0}^{\prime}:z_{0}]
.
Observe that the affine part equals

H=\hat{H}\cap\{z_{0}^{\prime}=1\}
.
On
\{z_{0}=1\}
we read off the equation

w_{0}+z_{0}^{\prime}\big(z_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+z_{d}w_{d}\big)=0
.
Therefore,

\hat{H}=H\cup\Big([0:1]\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\{w_{0}=0\}\Big)\,,

so that

\big(\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus\hat{H}

is equal to the union of

\varphi(Q)=\big(\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus H\quad\text{and}\quad\Big([0:1]\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\{w_{0}\neq 0\}\Big)\,.

Precomposing the embedding obtained
with the biholomorphism
\varphi

from Section
3.3

yields a holomorphic embedding

\hat{\varphi}\colon\thinspace Q\longrightarrow\big(\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus\hat{H}

of the affine quadric
Q
.

In the intersection pattern in
Section
2.4

we substitute
H_{2}
by
\hat{H}
.
We claim that

C_{i}\cdot\hat{H}=1\,,\quad i=1,2\,,

for the intersection numbers.
Indeed, representing
C_{1}
by

\mathbb{C}P^{1}\ni[z_{0}^{\prime}:z_{0}]\longmapsto\Big([z_{0}^{\prime}:z_{0}],(0,\ldots,0),[1:0:\ldots:0]\Big)

the intersection with
\hat{H}

is determined by
z_{0}=0
on
\{z_{0}^{\prime}=1\}
;
on
\{z_{0}=1\}
there is no intersection.
The class
C_{2}
can be represented by

\mathbb{C}P^{1}\ni[z:w]\longmapsto\Big([1:0],(0,\ldots,1),[0:\ldots:0:z:w]\Big)

such that the intersection with
\hat{H}

given by
w=0
.

3.6.
A Plücker–Segre–Veronese type embedding

Introducing coordinates

\Big([z_{0}^{\prime}:z_{0}],[z_{1}^{\prime}:z_{1}:\ldots:z_{d}],[\mathbf{w}]\Big)\in\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

we homogenise
\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
a third time
and obtain as in Section
3.5

z_{0}z_{1}^{\prime}w_{0}+z_{0}^{\prime}\big(z_{1}w_{1}+\ldots+z_{d}w_{d}\big)=0\,.

Writing this as

z_{0b0}+z_{a11}+\ldots+z_{add}=0

we get a linear equation in
d+1
unknowns.
Organising this with help of homogeneous coordinates
z_{ijk}

for
i,k=0,\ldots,d
and
j=a,b

we end up with an holomorphic embedding

\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{N}\,,\quad N=2(d+1)^{2}-1\,,

that maps
\hat{H}
into a hyperplane of
\mathbb{C}P^{N}
.
After a linear coordinate change we assume that the hyperplane
containing the image of
\hat{H}
is simply
\mathbb{C}P^{N-1}
.
This embedding restricts to a holomorphic embedding

\psi\colon\thinspace\big(\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus\hat{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}\,,

so that
\im\psi
is Stein.
The pull back of
\frac{1}{2}|\,.\,|^{2}

along
\psi
defines an exhausting,
strictly plurisubharmonic function
\hat{g}

on the complement of

\hat{H}
in
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}
.

3.7.
Extending a plurisubharmonic function

We want to modify the exhausting,
strictly plurisubharmonic function

f\colon\thinspace Q\rightarrow[0,\infty)
from Section
3.1
.
For that we define a second exhausting,
strictly plurisubharmonic function

g\colon\thinspace Q\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by
g:=\hat{\varphi}^{*}\hat{g}
,
see Sections
3.5

and
3.6
.

Choose positive real numbers
g_{0}<g_{1}<g_{2}

such that
\{f=0\}
is contained
in the relatively compact sublevel set
\{g<g_{0}\}
.
Denote by
\chi\colon\thinspace Q\rightarrow[0,1]
a smooth cut–off function
that is equal to
1
on
\{g\leq g_{1}\}
and

0
on
\{g\geq g_{2}\}
.
Let
\kappa\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a smooth function
that is
0
on
\{g\leq g_{0}\}
,
satisfies
\kappa^{\prime}>0
,
\kappa^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
on
\{g>g_{0}\}
,
and is of the form
Ag+B
on
\{g\geq g_{2}\}

for positive constants
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
.
As in
[
4
]
,
the function
h\colon\thinspace Q\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by

h:=\chi f+C\kappa(g)

is an exhausting, strictly plurisubharmonic function
for
C>0
sufficiently large.
Indeed,
h=f
on
\{g\leq g_{0}\}
.
By
[
3
, p. 19, Remark 2.8]
,
the composition
\kappa(g)
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\{g>g_{0}\}
,
so that
h
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\{g\leq g_{1}\}
.
On the compact set
\{g_{1}\leq g\leq g_{2}\}

we choose
C
large enough
such that the Levi form of
C\kappa(g)
dominates
the one of
\chi f
.
On
\{g\geq g_{2}\}
we finally have

h=C(Ag+B)\,.

Consequently,

h\circ\varphi^{-1}\colon\thinspace\big(\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus H\rightarrow[0,\infty)

extends to an exhausting, strictly plurisubharmonic function

\hat{h}\colon\thinspace\big(\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\big)\setminus\hat{H}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)\,,

which equals
C(A\hat{g}+B)
in a neighbourhood of
the hypersurface
\hat{H}
.
After choosing the constant
g_{0}
appropriately
we can assume that
\hat{h}
restricts to
\hat{f}

from Section
3.3

on the tubular neighbourhood
D_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}
of

\Big\{(\mathbf{z},[\bar{\mathbf{z}}])\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}\;\big|\;\mathbf{z}\in S^{2d+1}\Big\}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}P^{d}

chosen in Section
2.2
.

3.8.
Deforming the filling

We continue the discussion from Section
3.7
.
Denote by
M^{\prime}
a level set of
\hat{f}

that is contained in
D_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}
.
In view of Section
3.4
,
we find by uniqueness of tubular neighbourhoods
up to isotopy an isotopy
of
M^{\prime}
and
S_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}

inside
D_{\delta}T^{*}S^{2d+1}
.
The trace of the isotopy does not intersect
the zero section
S^{2d+1}
;
and so does not
D_{\varepsilon}T^{*}S^{2d+1}

for
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
sufficiently small.

By a priori usage of
D_{\varepsilon}T^{*}S^{2d+1}

(replacing
\delta
by
\varepsilon
)
in the gluing construction in Section
2.2

we can assume that
M^{\prime}
and
S_{\varepsilon}T^{*}S^{2d+1}

are isotopic via an isotopy with trace
not intersecting
\Int\big(D_{\varepsilon}T^{*}S^{2d+1}\big)
.
With isotopy extension we see
that the union
W^{\prime}
of
W

and the cobordism bounded by

S_{\varepsilon}T^{*}S^{2d+1}
and
M^{\prime}
inside
\hat{Z}

deformation retracts onto
W
.
In particular,

W^{\prime}
as submanifold of
\big(\hat{Z},\hat{\Omega}\big)

is symplectically aspherical.
Q.E.D.

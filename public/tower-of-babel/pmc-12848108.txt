The low sidelobe level high-gain adaptive beamforming method for uniform subarrays
Authors: Yuxi Du, Weijia Cui, Bin Ba, Guanghui Su, Long Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Aiming at the weak signal enhancement, target detection and anti-jamming, and reduction of energy consumption faced by array radar technology in complex electromagnetic environments, this paper proposes a low sidelobe level high-gain adaptive beamforming method for uniform subarrays. The proposed method utilizes the virtual interference signals iteration method to greatly reduce the computational dimension on the basis of ensuring the output performance. Firstly the proposed algorithm compensates the delay of the array elements within the subarray with respect to the reference array element by element-level weighting and derives the array output after element-level weighting. Next the subarray-level interference plus noise covariance matrix was derived. Then the Lagrange method is applied to solve the weight vector for each iteration. Finally, the algorithm is made to iterate until convergence by adding virtual interference signals. The theoretical derivation and simulation experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed method.

Introduction

Adaptive beamforming is a technique for achieving directional signal enhancement or suppression by controlling the phase and amplitude of signals in an antenna array. With the core advantages of directional signal enhancement and interference suppression, spatial flexibility and dynamic adjustment capability, high resolution and precise detection capability, as well as multi-source data synergy and system integration, beamforming technology is widely used in many fields such as communications, remote sensing, navigation, disaster monitoring1–5. Enhancement of weak signals to achieve target detection and anti-jamming in complex electromagnetic environments as well as to reduce energy consumption are current challenges that need to be solved in the field of array radar6,7.

To address the matrix pathology problem caused by covariance matrix estimation errors (e.g., small snap counts, array calibration errors) in practical applications, Cox proposed the diagonal loading (DL) algorithm8,9. The algorithm can suppress the eigenvalue dispersion through the loading amount by introducing artificial white noise thus avoiding the numerical instability in the inverse of the covariance matrix. However, in practical scenarios, due to unknown noise power and other factors, the DL factor needs to be determined by statistical or optimization methods, which is easy to introduce suboptimal solutions10–12. Overloading can lead to distortion of the signal and reduce the main flap gain. At the same time, some of the improved algorithms require multiple matrix reconstructions and decompositions which can lead to sacrificing the real-time performance of the algorithms. The eigenspace-based (ESB) algorithm project the received signals onto the orthogonal subspaces formed by eigenvectors through the eigen-decomposition of the signal covariance matrix, thereby separating the signal subspace from the noise subspace and using the information of the signal subspace to suppress noise and interference13,14. The separation of the signal subspace from the noise subspace can effectively suppress non-coherent interference and maintain the integrity of the target signal even in scenarios with strong noise or dense interference. The accuracy of subspace partitioning is affected by the estimation error of the covariance matrix. When the number of snapshots is small or the signal to noise ratio (SNR) is low, it is prone to subspace confusion15–17. The algorithm assumes that the signal and noise subspaces are strictly orthogonal. In practice, model mismatch (such as direction of arrival errors) can destroy the orthogonality, leading to performance degradation. The worst-case performance optimization algorithm sets the error range of the signal guidance vector (such as direction mismatch, array calibration error, etc.) to find the optimal weight vector in the worst case within the error set and ensure that the algorithm can maintain the distortion-free response of the signal and suppress interference under all possible errors18. It can effectively counteract the mismatch of the steering vector, array calibration errors and the estimation deviation of the covariance matrix caused by a small number of snapshots, and still maintain stable output under non-ideal conditions. The error radius needs to be preset. Meanwhile, if it does not match the actual error distribution, it may lead to the degradation of the algorithm performance. In practical applications, due to the limitation of numerical solution accuracy, the algorithm may not converge to the global optimal solution, affecting the interference suppression effect19–21. The performance of the above-mentioned algorithms is all much lower than that of the minimum variance distortionless response (MVDR) method due to the self-cancellation phenomenon at high SNR.

The weighting method achieves precise control of the sidelobe level (SLL) while ensuring the width of the main lobe by taking advantage of the isogamy characteristics of the Chebyshev polynomial22. This method can obtain the lowest SLL under the constraints of the same number of array elements and the width of the main lobe. Meanwhile, the closed-form solution based on Chebyshev polynomials can directly calculate the weights without iterative optimization. However, the width of the main lobe and the SLL cannot be adjusted independently. Therefore, when designin g, the performance of one side needs to be sacrificed to meet the requirements of the other side23–25. It is easily affected by manufacturing tolerances, which leads to the actual SLL performance deviating from the theoretical value due to the large excitation amplitude of the array edge element. The Olen beam graph algorithm minimizes the energy of the side lobe region by setting a virtual interference source and adjusting the array weights based on iterative optimization, while maintaining the directivity gain of the main lobe26. However, the iterative optimization process involving large-scale matrix operations will lead to a sharp increase in the computational load when the number of array elements increases. The quantity and location of virtual interference sources and the iteration termination conditions need to be finely tuned. Improper Settings can easily lead to distortion of the main lobe or residual side lobes27,28. The beam graph optimization algorithm based on second-order cone programming (SOCP) transforms the beam design problem into a convex optimization model, uses second-order cone constraints to describe performance indicators such as side lobe suppression and main lobe shape, and efficiently solves the global optimal weights through the interior point method29–32. The SOCP can ensure the uniqueness and global optimality of the weight solution to avoid the local convergence defect of traditional gradient-based algorithms because it is a convex optimization problem. When optimizing large-scale arrays, the SOCP model involves high-dimensional matrix operations, and the solution time increases exponentially with the number of array elements, which is not friendly to scenarios with high real-time requirements.

However, the above-mentioned beam optimization design algorithm only considers one of the SLL of the beamforming device or the array gain. In fact, these indicators are often interrelated. Unconsidered indicators often lead to a significant decline due to complex environments and errors and other factors. This paper proposes a beam-forming method with low SLL and high array gains for uniform subarrays. The desired signal can be superimposed because the element-level weighting is used to compensate for the delay of the elements within the subarray. The subarray-level weighting is used to suppress interference signals and increase array gain. The side lobe stage is controlled by adding virtual interference signals. The contribution of this paper lies in the combination of reducing the SLL and increasing the array gain and their application to subarrays, providing a new idea for the development of beamforming.

Methodological process

In summary, the proposed method is realized as follows:

Step1: the element-level weighting is performed via formula (12) so that the desired signal of array elements within the same subarray are superimposed;

Step2: the INCM at the subarray-level is obtained via formula (28);

Step3: the initial subarray-level weighting is obtained through formula (33);

Step4: search for the corresponding angle of the virtual signal and update the INCM using formula (30);

Step5: update the subarray-level weighting using the INCM obtained in step 4 through formula (35);

Step6: the iteration terminates if the loop reaches the preset upper limit or if the SLL between neighboring iterations is less than a self-defined threshold, otherwise return to step4.

Conclusion

In this paper, a beam forming method with low SLL and high gain is proposed for uniform subarrays. Firstly, the desired signal of the array elements within the same subarray are superimposed by array element-level weighting and the weighted array output is derived. Secondly, an optimization problem is formulated based on the requirement of low SLL and high gain and the INCM of the optimization problem is derived. Then the Lagrange method is applied twice to solve for the weights of the signal without virtual interference and with virtual interference, respectively. Finally the subarray weights with virtual interference signals are iteratively updated until the method converges. The superiority of the proposed algorithm is verified by simulation experiments of the proposed method and the comparison method on the direction map and output performance.

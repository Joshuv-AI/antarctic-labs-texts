Wiener Algebras Methods for Liouville theorems on the stationary Navier-Stokes system
Nicolas Lerner

Abstract
. We prove some Liouville theorems for the stationary Navier-Stokes system for incompressible fluids. We provide some sufficient conditions on the low frequency part of the solution, using some properties of classical singular integrals with respect to Wiener algebras.

The classical Liouville theorem

Theorem 1.1

.

Let

Proof.

Since

is a polynomial.
Since

Proof of the lemma.

Let

Proposition 2.28
.

Let us define

\mathcal{A}
is obviously a vector space, subspace of

.
Let

5.2.
Isoperimetric inequality

Lemma 5.21
.

Let
v
be a divergence-free vector field such that

v\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\mathcal{L}_{(0)}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})

and
\operatorname{curl}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then

belongs to

.

Proof.

We have seen already in (

\forall q_{1}\in(1,\frac{6}{5}),\forall q_{2}\in(\frac{6}{5},2],\quad\hat{v}\in L^{q_{1}}+L^{q_{2}},\quad v\in L^{q^{\prime}_{1}}+L^{q^{\prime}_{2}},

so that

(5.2.1)

v=\mathbf{C}^{2}|D|^{-2}v=|D|^{-1}\underbracket{\mathbf{C}|D|^{-1}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{singular}\\
\text{integral}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\mathbf{C}v}_{\in L^{2}},

and using the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev Inequality,
we get that

|D|^{-1}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\longrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

since
|D|^{-1}
is the convolution with
|x|^{1-3}=|x|^{-\frac{3}{3/2}}
so that

\||x|^{-2}\ast u\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\sigma\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\quad\text{ since }\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{3}=1+\frac{1}{6}.

As a consequence of (
5.2.1
), since
\mathbf{C}|D|^{-1}\mathbf{C}v\in L^{2}
, we get

and

\|v\|_{L^{6}}=\||D|^{-1}\mathbf{C}|D|^{-1}\mathbf{C}v\|_{L^{6}}\leq\sigma\|\mathbf{C}|D|^{-1}\mathbf{C}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sigma\|\mathbf{C}v\|_{L^{2}},

and the result of the lemma.
∎

The following theorem is classical.

On Peetre’s Inequality

Claim 5.36
.

We consider the set
\mathcal{T}
of nonnegative real numbers
\tau
such that for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

(5.4.14)

\tau+|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{2}\leq\bigl(\tau+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\tau+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\bigr).

Then we have

\mathcal{T}=\bigl[\frac{4}{3},+{\infty}\bigr).

Proof of the Claim.

We have for
a,b\geq 0
,

\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+a^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+b^{2}\bigr)-\frac{4}{3}-(a+b)^{2}=\frac{4}{9}+a^{2}b^{2}+\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}}{3}-2ab\\
=\frac{4}{9}+a^{2}b^{2}+\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab}{3}-\frac{4}{3}ab=a^{2}b^{2}-\frac{4}{3}ab+\frac{4}{9}+\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{3}\\
=\Bigl(ab-\frac{2}{3}\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{3}\geq 0.

This implies that for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(5.4.15)

\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{3}+\bigl(|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|\bigr)^{2}\leq\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\bigr),

so that
4/3\in\mathcal{T}
.
If
\tau\geq 4/3
,
we find for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\tau+|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{2}\leq\tau-\frac{4}{3}+\frac{4}{3}+\bigl(|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|\bigr)^{2}\underbracket{\leq}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{using}\\
\eqref{rea489}\end{subarray}}\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\bigr)+\tau-\frac{4}{3}\\
\leq\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\bigr)+(\tau-\frac{4}{3})(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{1}|^{2})=\bigl(\frac{4}{3}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\tau+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\bigr)\\
\leq\bigl(\tau+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\tau+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\bigr),

proving that
\mathcal{T}\supset\bigl[\frac{4}{3},+{\infty}\bigr).

Moreover, for
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
,
we find, choosing
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2},|\xi_{1}|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
, that

\tau+\frac{8}{3}\leq\bigl(\tau+\frac{2}{3}\bigr)^{2},

i.e.

0\leq P(\tau)=\tau^{2}+\frac{\tau}{3}-\frac{20}{9}=(\tau-\frac{4}{3})(\tau+\frac{5}{3}).

Since we have
\tau\geq 0
, this implies
\tau\geq 4/3
,
completing the proof of the claim.∎

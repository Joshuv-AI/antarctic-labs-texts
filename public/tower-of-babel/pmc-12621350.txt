BAFF in autoimmune kidney diseases: pathogenic pathways, biomarker potential, and therapeutic horizons
Authors: Jing Wu, Zhaohui Li, Shanshan Zheng, Yipeng Liu, Na Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAutoimmune kidney diseases usually arise from autoimmune system dysfunction and are characterized by production of autoantibodies. Several autoimmune kidney diseases are currently recognized, including lupus nephritis, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-associated vasculitis, membranous nephropathy, IgA nephropathy. B-cell activating factor (BAFF), a member of the tumor necrosis factor family, serves as a critical regulator of humoral immunity. By binding to specific receptors on the surface of B cells, BAFF regulates their survival, proliferation, differentiation and antibody production, thereby playing a key role in the pathogenesis of autoimmune kidney diseases. Elevated serum BAFF levels, reported across multiple autoimmune diseases, promote aberrant B cell activation and autoantibody production. This review systematically illustrates the biology and immunology of BAFF, and further explores the role of BAFF in those autoimmune kidney diseases, aiming to provide a comprehensive insight into the BAFF molecule.Keywords: B lymphocyte activating factor; autoimmune disease, autoimmune kidney disease; biomarker; pathogenesis; targeted therapy; precision nephrology

Basic structure, expression and distribution of BAFF

B-cell activating factor (BAFF), a member of the tumor necrosis factor superfamily, was first identified in 1999. It is predominantly produced by nucleated cells and exhibits widespread expressed in monocytes [1,2], macrophages [2,3], dendritic cells [2,4], splenic and bone marrow B cells [5], naive cells, memory cells and plasma cells [6], activated T cells [5,7], natural killer cells [7], and stromal cells [8,9].

Biologically active BAFF exists in two primary forms, a type II transmembrane protein and a soluble cleaved protein. The soluble form is essential for B cell homeostasis [10] and represents the predominant circulating form of BAFF [11]. The soluble BAFF protein assembles into functional trimer or a high-order multimers [12,13].

BAFF receptors and biological functions

BAFF binds three distinct receptors with different affinities: strongest binding to BAFF receptor (BAFF-R), intermediate affinity for transmembrane activator and calcium modulator and cyclophilin ligand interactor (TACI), and weakest affinity for monomeric B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) [14,15]. These receptors exhibit stage-specific expression on B cells [16].

BAFF-R: Expressed on most mature human B cells beyond the pre-B-cell stage (excluding plasmablasts and plasma cells), BAFF-R transmits critical survival signals during early B-cell maturation [17–19], and sustains mature B-cell survival [20].

TACI: Expressed on activated B cells, memory B cells, and plasma cells [21], TACI mediates B lymphocyte proliferation, antibody production, and T-cell-independent type 2 antigen-driven plasma cell differentiation [21]. Its expression is induced upon B-cell activation, detectable on activated CD27-naïve B cells expressing CD25 and CD80 [16] and can be enhanced by BAFF-R or Toll-like receptor (TLR) stimulation [16]. TACI also delivers negative regulatory signals in early B-cell maturation [22–25].

BCMA: Expressed on activated B cells, memory B cells, and plasma cells [21], BCMA expression inversely correlates with BAFF-R; it decreases as BAFF-R surface expression increases [16].

Soluble TACI and BCMA act as decoy receptors, sequestering BAFF and disrupting signaling, thereby impacting B cell homeostasis.

Roles of BAFF and its receptors in immunity

Humoral and cellular immunity

BAFF is indispensable for B lymphocyte responses. In the presence of appropriate cytokines (e.g., IL-10, IL-4, TGF-β), BAFF induces CD40-independent class switch recombination (CSR) to IgG, IgA, or IgE in naive B cells in vitro [4]. Murine studies confirm BAFF-R or TACI interactions mediate BAFF-induced CSR to IgG/IgA-secreting B cells [26,27].

BAFF-R expression on memory B cells (MBCs) and their BAFF dependence are critical for sustaining MBC survival and recall responses [28]. While BAFF maintains survival of long-lived bone marrow plasma cells in vitro and in vivo [29,30], plasmablast differentiation proceeds normally in BAFF knockout mice, indicating BAFF is dispensable for this process [31].

BAFF also modulates T-cell responses. Binding BAFF-R activates pre-stimulated T cells [17,32,33], but impaired BAFF-BAFF-R binding prolongs allograft survival [34]. BAFF overexpression in transgenic mice enhances Th1 responses [35]. BAFF-R signaling promotes follicular helper T-cell (Tfh) accumulation, autoantibody production, and IFN-γ secretion in BCMA-deficient lupus models. Tfh cells within germinal centers serve as a local BAFF source, essential for selecting high-affinity B cell clones [36]. This is confirmed in SLE, where Tfh cell levels correlate with disease activity [37,38] and are elevated in patients expressing BAFF-R but lacking BCMA [37].

Regulation of other immune cells

Produced by diverse immune cells and essential for differentiation of B lymphocyte germinal centers, BAFF also regulates other immune lineages [39]. Splenic neutrophils secrete BAFF, a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL), and interlukin-21(IL-21) to interact with marginal zone B cells, promoting T cell-independent (TI) antigen responses [40,41]. Neutrophil and dendritic cell-derived BAFF contributes to TI-2 antigen antibody production [42].

BAFF promotes monocyte activation and macrophage differentiation via NF-κB, polarizing macrophages toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype and disrupting immune balance [43]. It induces maturation of myeloid dendritic cells (mDCs), upregulates co-stimulatory molecules, and enhances secretion of inflammatory cytokines/chemokines [44], potentially leading to pathological immune activation. BAFF-deficient mice show reduced splenic effector/memory T cells (CD44 + CD62L-) [45]. Elevated BAFF increases co-stimulatory molecule expression on antigen-presenting cells, amplifying TCR signaling and T-cell stimulation [46]. BAFF levels correlate with serum Th2 cytokines (e.g., IL-4, IL-6) [46] and bind BAFF-R on T cells, inducing CD4+ T-cell activation/proliferation via the BAFF-R-PI3K-AKT pathway [17,32,34,47] (Figure 1).

It promotes monocyte activation and macrophage differentiation toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype via NF-κB signaling. In vitro studies demonstrate that BAFF can induce class switch recombination (CSR) in immature B cells to generate IgG and IgA antibodies when appropriate cytokines (e.g. IL-10, IL-4, TGF-β) are present. Regulation by interferon (IFN) and TLR signaling

Interferon Regulatory Factors (IRFs) differentially regulate BAFF: IRF1/2 promote expression, while IRF4/8 suppress it. IFN-β induces BAFF via JAK/STAT signaling [48]. TLR signals also induce BAFF expression: TLR2 potently induces BAFF mRNA synthesis and soluble BAFF release in synovial fibroblasts; TLR3/4 show moderate effects [49,50]. TLR9 agonist CpG-ODN induces membrane-bound BAFF (but not soluble BAFF) in human B cells, enhancing anti-IgM-mediated proliferation [51]. Pathogen components may thus induce pathogenic BAFF overexpression (Figure 2).

BAFF in autoimmune kidney diseases

IgA nephropathy (IgAN)

Mechanistic insights

IgAN, the most common primary glomerulonephritis, involves galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) overproduction, immune complex deposition, and mesangial proliferation. BAFF drives T cell-dependent (TD) and -independent (TID) CSR to IgA + B cells [26,52–55]. Aberrant homing may misroute mucosal IgA + plasma cells to systemic sites like bone marrow, producing circulating Gd-IgA1 [56–58]. By supporting IgA CSR, B-cell differentiation, and long-lived plasma cell survival in bone marrow/lymphoid tissues [29,30], BAFF sustains Gd-IgA1 and anti-Gd-IgA1 autoantibody production.

Clinical biomarker evidence

BAFF-transgenic (BAFF-Tg) mice exhibit elevated serum IgA, B-cell excess, spontaneous germinal centers, autoantibodies, increased plasma/effector T cells, and renal Ig deposition [39,59,60], confirming BAFF’s direct B cell stimulatory role. BAFF overexpression enhances B cell survival via BAFF-R [39,61]. Clinically, serum BAFF is elevated in IgAN patients versus healthy controls [62–65], correlating positively with serum IgA1 levels [66]. Higher BAFF associates with reduced renal function (declined eGFR, elevated creatinine) [66], severe tubulointerstitial fibrosis/glomerulosclerosis [67], and disease activity. Tonsillar monocytes from IgAN patients produce more IgA, BAFF, and IFN-γ upon CpG-ODN stimulation; IFN-γ further upregulates BAFF [68] (Table 1). Microbial DNA hyperresponsiveness may thus drive tonsillar IgA overproduction via IFN-γ/BAFF upregulation. BAFF also contributes via TRAF6/NF-κB pathway activation in mesangial cells [69].

Therapeutic implications

A proposed TLR9-BAFF-IgA CSR axis [70] requires further validation. Collectively, BAFF is critically implicated in IgAN pathogenesis. However, notably, while anti-BAFF monoclonal antibodies have demonstrated suboptimal clinical efficacy in the treatment of IgAN, anti-APRIL monoclonal antibodies have yielded favorable outcomes [71]. This discrepancy not only indicates distinct pathogenic roles for BAFF and APRIL in the disease process but also suggests that pathogenic antibodies produced by plasma cells likely play a more significant role in the pathogenesis of IgAN. Proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL) is implicated in the pathogenesis of IgA nephropathy. Sibeprenlimab, a humanized IgG2 monoclonal antibody, specifically binds to and neutralizes APRIL. In patients with IgA nephropathy, 12-month treatment with sibeprenlimab resulted in significant proteinuria reduction [71]. Atacicept specifically binds to and inhibits the cytokines BAFF and APRIL, enabling targeted modulation of core pathogenic mechanisms in B-cell-mediated disease progression. In a clinical study evaluating IgA nephropathy patients, systematic assessment demonstrated sustained therapeutic efficacy and favorable safety profiles with Atacicept treatment over 96 weeks [72] (Table 2).

Lupus nephritis (LN)

Mechanistic insights

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), characterized by autoantibodies, autoreactive lymphocytes, and cytokine dysregulation [76–79], often involves LN via immune complex-mediated inflammation [80–83]. BAFF expression in renal tubular epithelial cells correlates with SLE disease activity and histopathological indices [85]. BAFF mRNA is upregulated in glomeruli/tubules of proliferative LN patients [86]. BAFF promotes LN by inducing renal tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) and regulating glomerular T cell localization [87]. Glomerular macrophage/mesangial cell-derived BAFF contributes to LN onset [88]. BAFF expression correlates with B-cell infiltration in LN kidneys, suggesting excessive BAFF-driven B cell differentiation underlies pathogenesis [89]. BAFF may also interact with the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway in LN [90].

Clinical biomarker evidence

BAFF expression varies by LN class: low in interstitial infiltrates of Class II/V; prominent in glomeruli of Class III/IV/V, correlating with inflammation and CD20+ infiltration (especially Class IV) [91]. Urinary BAFF (uBAFF) mRNA [92] and protein levels are elevated in LN [88,90,92,93], though not always correlating with disease activity [92]. Active renal disease associates with higher uBAFF levels [93]. Serum BAFF is typically elevated in SLE patients with renal involvement [62,88,90,94–98]. While often correlating with baseline disease activity [99,100], changes in serum BAFF do not consistently track with activity changes [99], and some studies find no correlation [94,96]. The BAFF-var allele (associated with overproduction) may link to LN and activity markers [101], though not confirmed in all cohorts [102].

Therapeutic implications

Belimumab, a BAFF-inhibiting monoclonal antibody [103], reduces autoantibodies and depletes B cells. Key findings from BLISS randomized controlled trial indicate that adding belimumab to standard therapy significantly improves renal response rates in patients with active lupus nephritis compared to standard therapy alone [104]. Our center’s meta-analysis on belimumab yielded consistent results [105]. However, a subset of patients exhibits suboptimal response to belimumab. This necessitates further investigation into two critical questions: (1) Whether selective BAFF blockade or dual BAFF/APRIL inhibition offers superior efficacy? (2) What roles interferon signaling and Toll-like receptors (TLRs) play in driving pathogenic autoantibody production in lupus nephritis?

Membranous nephropathy (MN)

Mechanistic insights

MN, a common cause of adult nephrotic syndrome [106] and ESRD in primary glomerulonephritis [63], features subepithelial immune complex deposition, basement membrane thickening, complement activation, and podocyte injury [64,65]. Discovery of autoantibodies (e.g. anti-PLA2R, anti-THSD7A) established idiopathic MN as autoimmune.

Clinical biomarker evidence

Plasma BAFF levels are higher in primary MN than healthy controls but lower than secondary MN. Relapsed cases exhibit higher BAFF than remission [107]. Anti-PLA2R-positive primary MN patients show significantly higher baseline BAFF levels than anti-PLA2R-negative MN patients. After 6 months of immunosuppression, anti-PLA2R-positive patients achieving antibody clearance had lower baseline BAFF than those with persistent antibodies, suggesting a pathogenic link [108]. BAFF expression profiles resemble other autoimmune diseases and impact renal prognosis [107]. Our previous study revealed that serum BAFF levels were significantly elevated in patients with primary membranous nephropathy. These elevated BAFF levels correlated positively with anti-PLA2R antibody titers and 24-h urinary protein levels. After adjustment using multivariate Cox regression analysis, serum BAFF emerged as a statistically significant independent risk factor for the lack of proteinuria remission.

Therapeutic implications

In contrast, serum APRIL levels did not demonstrate similar associations with these clinical parameters ([109]. These findings suggest that: 1) BAFF appears to play a more critical role than APRIL in the pathogenesis of membranous nephropathy; 2) Targeted therapeutic strategies focusing on B cells (such as BAFF inhibition) are potentially more justified in the management of this disease (Table 3).

Other autoimmune kidney diseases

In patients with anti-glomerular basement membrane disease (anti-GBM disease), elevated BAFF levels are positively correlated with anti-GBM antibody titers and the proportion of crescentic glomeruli, suggesting that BAFF is related to disease activity and renal injury [110]. In addition, in myeloperoxidase (MPO)-ANCA associated vasculitis, serum BAFF levels are higher with active vasculitis than those with inactive disease and controls, correlating with ANCA titers, BVAS scores, and serum mCRP levels while not with serum creatinine levels, indicating that BAFF may be involved in MPO production [111]. However, Holdsworth. et al. reported that BREVAS Phase III RCT tried to explore efficacy and safety of belimumab in combination with azathioprine for remission maintenance therapy in MPA and GPA, which did not acquire positive results [112,113]. The pathogenic role of BAFF needs further investigation.

Conclusion and perspectives

BAFF and its receptors play significant roles in autoimmunity, cancer, infection, transplantation, allergy, and immunodeficiency. By binding its receptors, BAFF promotes autoreactive B cell escape from bone marrow checkpoints, enhances their survival/maturation, and increases autoantibody production. Dysregulated BAFF expression is implicated in multiple autoimmune kidney diseases.

Crucially, BAFF-R, TACI, and BCMA mediate distinct effects on B cell development and survival following BAFF binding. Future research should focus on:1. Downstream signaling pathways activated by BAFF-receptor interactions; 2. Factors regulating the half-lives of BAFF-R, TACI, and BCMA; 3. Significant protein interactions involving these receptors; 4. BAFF/APRIL dual blockade; 5. personalized medicine approaches; 6. urinary BAFF as noninvasive biomarker. Elucidating these mechanisms will provide deeper insights into B cell selection and autoimmunity regulation, guiding novel therapeutic strategies

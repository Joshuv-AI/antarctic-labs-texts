Self-Regulatory Organizations; CME Securities Clearing Inc.; 
Notice of Filing of Proposed Rule Change To Amend the CMESC Risk

October 16, 2026.
    Pursuant to Section 19(b)(1) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 
(the ``Act'') \1\ and Rule 19b-4 thereunder,\2\ notice is hereby given 
that on October 9, 2026, CME Securities Clearing Inc. (``CMESC'') filed 
with the Securities and Exchange Commission (``SEC'' or ``Commission'') 
the proposed rule change described in Items I, II, and III below, which 
Items have been substantially prepared by CMESC. CMESC filed the 
proposed rule change pursuant to Section 19(b)(2) of the Act.\3\ The 
Commission is publishing this notice to solicit comments on the 
proposed rule change from interested persons.

I. CMESC's Statement of the Terms and Substance of the Proposed Rule 
Change

    (a) The proposed rule change of CME Securities Clearing Inc. 
(``CMESC'') is provided in Exhibit 5. The proposed rule change includes 
amendments to the CMESC Risk Management Framework (``RMF'') related to 
CMESC's default management practices, which provides for necessary 
transparency to and clarity for CMESC staff in managing a default.\4\ 
Each modification is described in more detail below.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ Capitalized terms used herein and not defined have the 
meanings assigned to such terms in the CMESC Rulebook (the 
``Rules''), as applicable, available at https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/CMESC/CMESC%20Rulebook.pdf.

A. CMESC's Statement of the Purpose of, and Statutory Basis for the 
Proposed Rule Change

1. Purpose
Background
    CMESC's default management procedures cover the management of a 
default of either a Member or User (collectively, ``Participants'') and 
are designed to ensure the continuity of CMESC's clearance and 
settlement functions and particularly, the ongoing maintenance of the 
services provided to non-defaulting Participants, while supporting 
market integrity and minimizing market disruptions. The primary purpose 
of the proposed rule change is to enhance the default management 
process by further codifying the documentation of the default 
management procedures in the RMF. In particular, the proposed rule 
change would amend the RMF to (i) provide additional detail around 
arrangements involving a third-party liquidation agent for managing a 
Participant default, and (ii) make certain clarifying and 
administrative changes, where appropriate, in the RMF; and (iii) to 
clarify the role of CMESC's Risk Management Committee (``CSRMC'') in 
designing the default management procedures.
Liquidation Agent and Execution Brokers
    Section 14.1.1 (``Participant Default Management Process'') of the 
RMF would be amended to specify that the close-out process of a 
defaulted Participant's portfolio may include the use of a liquidation 
agent and one or more execution brokers.\5\ Broadly, the amended RMF 
would provide that, in managing a default, CMESC and any liquidation 
agent will collaborate to assess the exposures of the defaulted 
Participant's portfolio, including associated settlement obligations as 
they come due, and formulate a close-out strategy under the prevailing 
market conditions to minimize potential losses and market disruptions 
and to support market integrity. More specifically, the amended RMF 
would describe the expectations for a third-party liquidation agent, if 
used, to assist CMESC with designing and executing the close-out 
strategy for the defaulted Participant's portfolio. In accordance with 
the amended RMF, the liquidation agent will be expected to coordinate 
with CMESC to reconcile positions of the defaulted Participant and 
assess the outstanding exposure of the defaulted Participant over the 
course of managing the default.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ For context, Rule 405 (``Default Management Process'') 
provides that the ``Corporation's default management process may 
involve the Corporation's consultation with third parties and may 
involve these third parties acting as agents on the Corporation's 
behalf to manage the Member Default or User Default.'' See Rule 405 
of the Rules, supra note 1.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The amended RMF also would provide that the liquidation agent may 
use various methods to execute close-out trades, including, but not 
limited to, executing open market transactions; enter into hedging 
transactions to mitigate the risk of the defaulted Participant's 
portfolio; and perform other related tasks appropriate to assist CMESC 
in managing the risk from the Participant's default. In addition, 
Section 14.1.1 of the RMF would recognize that CMESC may engage one or 
more execution brokers to coordinate with the liquidation agent and 
CMESC to provide certain operational services related to the close-out 
trades arranged or executed by the liquidation agent in managing a 
default. The amended RMF would include new language to specify that, 
where appropriate, CMESC would maintain an account with the execution 
broker where it would be the principal, and the execution broker may 
submit back-to-back trades that mirror the liquidation agent's close-
out trades. CMESC would further specify in the RMF that the submission 
of back-to-back trades is expected to ensure that CMESC's account at 
the execution broker maintains a net flat position and facilitate 
CMESC's ability to meet the settlement obligations of the defaulted 
Participant as they come due.
Clarifying and Administrative Changes
    The proposed rule change would also include certain clarifying and 
administrative changes.
    Member Default: For example, Section 14 (``Default Management'') of 
the RMF would be amended to clarify that CMESC analyzes Users' risk 
profile on an ex ante basis. The associated monitoring and analysis 
serves to improve the likelihood that non-defaulted Users' positions 
can be transferred to a Member in good standing where their authorizing 
Member has defaulted. Additionally, the RMF would be amended to clarify 
that, while CMESC makes its best efforts to transfer non-defaulting 
Users' positions

[[Page 66588]]

(i.e., of a defaulted Member) to non-defaulting Members, CMESC 
continues to monitor Users' positions and the sufficiency of the 
associated collateral throughout the default management process and 
based on the prevailing facts and circumstances may close-out (e.g., 
liquidate) the positions of some or all Users of a defaulted Member.
    User Default: Section 14.1 (``Participant Default Event'') of the 
RMF would be updated to specify that an authorizing Member of a 
defaulted User must respond to CMESC within a period of time prescribed 
by the Corporation if it wishes to manage the User's default and, if it 
elects to do so, CMESC will undertake several operational steps 
relating to the User's positions and collateral to facilitate the 
Member's management of the User's default in accordance with the CMESC 
Rules.\6\ Section 14.1 of the RMF would also be amended to include 
``close-out'' in addition to bidding on or purchasing a defaulted 
Participant's portfolio when gauging Participants' interest prior to 
declaring another Participant in default.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \6\ See, e.g., Rule 405(c)(ii) of the Rules, supra note 1.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Additionally, Section 14.1 of the RMF would be amended to clarify 
that, for Users with multiple authorizing Members, where CMESC manages 
the User's default, the default management costs will be allocated on a 
pro rata basis based on the margin requirement for the User's positions 
authorized by each such Member. Moreover, Section 14.1 would be amended 
to add that the trades of the Users that are expected to settle on the 
next day after the day of the Member's default may settle prior to the 
transfer of any other positions.
    Participant Default, Generally: The amended RMF also clarifies 
certain operational steps CMESC would take if it were to manage a 
default of a Participant itself. Section 14.1.1 of the amended RMF 
would provide that CMESC will take appropriate operational steps, as 
soon as practicable, to contain risk and prevent any unauthorized 
movement of the default Participant's cash or securities. These 
operational steps may include appropriate coordination with defaulted 
Participant's designated Bank(s) and Securities Settlement Bank(s), 
including enabling CMESC to assume control over outstanding positions 
and collateral of the defaulter. CMESC will also assess the risk of the 
defaulted Participant's portfolio, including the maturity of positions 
and timing for when associated obligations come due. Based on this 
assessment, CMESC would determine the best strategy for managing the 
Participant default in order to meet the obligations of the defaulted 
Participant's portfolio as they arise, including the process for 
closing out the portfolio and, where necessary, purchasing or selling 
securities and/or facilitating an auction (or multiple auctions) to 
meet the settlement obligations of the defaulted Participant.
    The proposed changes would also relocate language related to 
hedging a defaulted Participant's portfolio using futures from 
14.1.1.2, which relates generally to CMESC's ability to enter into 
securities transactions to address a defaulted Participant's settlement 
obligations, to Section 14.1.1, which describes the Participant default 
management process generally. In connection with this change, the term 
``Hedging'' is being deleted from the header for Section 14.1.1.2. 
Additionally, the proposed changes would amend Section 14.1.1.2 to 
clarify that CMESC would only use its acceleration rights for 
applicable transactions with remaining maturities of greater than two 
business days such that non-defaulting Participants' delivery 
obligations become due on dates earlier than the original maturity 
dates of such transactions, as is consistent with the terms of existing 
Rule 1507(f).\7\ Finally, Section 14 would be amended to clarify that 
CMESC notification of a User default is governed by CMESC's Rules.\8\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ See Rule 1507(f) of the Rules, supra note 1 (establishing 
CMESC's acceleration rights with respect to non-Defaulting Members 
and non-Defaulting Users such that their delivery obligations under 
outstanding Repo Transactions become due on dates earlier than the 
original maturity dates of such Repo Transactions).
    \8\ See, e.g., Rules 405(c) and 1507(b) of the Rules, supra note 
1.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

CSRMC Role in Designing the Default Management Procedures
    Section 14 of the amended RMF specifies that CMESC's Risk 
Management Committee (``CSRMC'') shall provide guidance to CMESC on the 
design of its default management procedures, as necessary (i.e., 
through its ongoing oversight of the RMF).\9\ The current RMF 
establishes the CSRMC's role in managing a default as discretionary and 
thus inherently advisory. The proposed amendments clarify the CSRMC's 
responsibilities prior to and during a default, while preserving its 
existing power to convene in the latter circumstances.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \9\ For the avoidance of doubt and as described in the existing 
RMF, the Chairperson of the CSRMC may convene the CSRMC in the event 
of a Participant default.

2. Statutory Basis
    For the reasons set forth below, CMESC believes the proposed rule 
change is consistent with Section 17A of the Securities Exchange Act of 
1934 (``Act'') \10\ and SEC Rules 17Ad-22(e)(2)(v),\11\ 17Ad-
22(e)(3),\12\ 17Ad-22(e)(13),\13\ and 17Ad-22(e)(18)(iv)(C) \14\ 
thereunder.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consistency With Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Act
    Section 17A(b)(3)(F) of the Act requires, in part, that the rules 
of a clearing agency be designed to promote the prompt and accurate 
clearance and settlement of securities transactions, to assure the 
safeguarding of securities and funds which are in the custody or 
control of the clearing agency or for which it is responsible, and, in 
general, to protect investors and the public interest.\15\ Robust 
documentation of default management practices in the RMF, a core 
component of CMESC's risk management framework, provides the necessary 
transparency to and clarity for CMESC staff to help facilitate the 
orderly management of a Participant default. This, in turn, will ensure 
CMESC continues to clear and settle securities transactions on a prompt 
and accurate basis and protect the securities and funds of non-
defaulting Members despite a Participant default. For these reasons, 
CMESC believes the proposed rule change is consistent with Section 
17A(b)(3)(F) of the Act.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \15\ 15 U.S.C. 78q-1(b)(3)(F).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consistency With SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(2)(v)
    SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(2)(v) requires that a covered clearing agency 
establish, implement, maintain, and enforce written policies and 
procedures reasonably designed to provide for governance arrangements 
that specify clear and direct lines of responsibility.\16\ The proposed 
amendments to Section 14 of the RMF are designed to clarify CSRMC's 
responsibility in guiding the design of CMESC's default management 
procedures. As such, CMESC believes that the proposed rule change would 
further specify the clear and direct lines of responsibility of CSRMC, 
consistent with SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(2)(v).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ 17 CFR 240.17Ad-22(e)(2)(v).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consistency With SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(3)
    SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(3) requires, in part, that a covered clearing 
agency establish, implement, maintain, and enforce written policies and 
procedures reasonably designed to maintain a sound risk management 
framework for comprehensively managing legal, credit, liquidity, 
operational, general business, investment, custody, and other risks 
that arise in or are borne by the covered clearing agency, which 
includes risk management policies, procedures, and systems designed to 
identify, measure, monitor, and manage the range of risks that arise in 
or are borne by the covered clearing agency, that are subject to review 
on a specified periodic basis and approved by the board of directors 
annually.\17\ The existing RMF is specifically designed to comply with 
SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(3), and the proposed amendments are designed to 
provide further enhancements and clarity to the manner in which CMESC 
manages Participant defaults, including potentially through the use of 
a liquidation agent and execution brokers and the clarifying changes 
describing CMESC's potential actions during a Participant default, 
generally, and Member or User defaults, specifically. As such, CMESC 
believes that the proposed rule change would support a sound, 
comprehensive framework designed to identify, measure, monitor, and 
manage the range of risks that arise in or are borne by CMESC, 
consistent with SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(3).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ 17 CFR 240.17Ad-22(e)(3)(i).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consistency With SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(13)
    SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(13) requires, in part, that a covered clearing 
agency establish, implement, maintain, and enforce written policies and 
procedures reasonably designed to ensure the covered clearing agency 
has the authority and operational capacity to take timely action to 
contain losses and liquidity demands and continue to meet its 
obligations.\18\ The proposed amendments to the RMF are designed to 
enhance CMESC's default management process by further documenting 
CMESC's default management procedures. Having well documented 
procedures, in turn, will bolster CMESC's rule-based authority and 
provide further clarity regarding operational steps to contain losses 
and liquidity demands and continue to meet its obligations, despite a 
Participant default. Furthermore, the proposed amendments to the RMF 
would document arrangements with a potential third-party liquidation 
agent and clarify the default management process involving such a 
third-party, which if used, could facilitate CMESC's ability to take 
timely action to close out a defaulting Participant's positions. 
Therefore, CMESC believes that the proposed rule change is consistent 
with SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(13).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \18\ 17 CFR 240.17Ad-22(e)(13).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consistency With Rule 17Ad-22(e)(18)(iv)(C)
    SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(18)(iv)(C) requires, in part, that a covered 
clearing agency establish, implement, maintain, and enforce written 
policies and procedures reasonably designed to establish objective, 
risk-based, and publicly disclosed criteria for participation, which, 
when the covered clearing agency provides central counterparty services 
for transactions in U.S. Treasury securities, ensure that it has 
appropriate means to facilitate access to the clearance and settlement 
services of all eligible secondary market transactions in U.S. Treasury 
securities, including those of indirect participants.\19\ The 
clarifying changes to the procedures for a Member default are designed 
to document CMESC's planned efforts, both ex ante and ex post, to 
increase the probability that a User continues to maintain access to 
CMESC's services, despite its authorizing Member's default. Therefore, 
CMESC believes the proposed rule change is consistent with these 
requirements in SEC Rule 17Ad-22(e)(18)(iv)(C) regarding facilitating 
access to the clearance and settlement services of all eligible 
secondary market transactions, including by indirect participants.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \19\ 17 CFR 240.17Ad-22(e)(18)(iv)(C).

B. CMESC's Statement on Burden on Competition

    CMESC does not believe that the proposed rule change will impose 
any burden on competition not necessary or appropriate in furtherance 
of the purposes of the Act. The proposed rule change is designed to 
improve the Rules' accuracy and clarity for Members and Users, to 
enhance the default management process, and to be consistent with the 
Act. These proposed changes would not affect CMESC's operations that 
are already provided in the existing Rules or create additional rights 
and obligations of Members and Users. As such, CMESC does not believe 
the proposed rule change would have any impact on burden on competition 
that does not already exist under the existing Rules or is not 
necessary or appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of the Act.

Multilevel Method
with Low-Order Equations of Mixed Types

Abstract
Thermal radiative transfer (TRT) is an essential piece of physics in inertial confinement fusion, high-energy density physics, astrophysics etc. The physical models of this type of problem are defined by strongly coupled differential equations describing multiphysics phenomena. This paper presents a new nonlinear multilevel iterative method with two photon energy grids for solving the multigroup radiative transfer equation (RTE) coupled with the material energy balance equation (MEB). The multilevel system of equations of the method is formulated by means of a nonlinear projection approach. The RTE is projected over elements of phase space to derive the low-order equations of different types. The hierarchy of equations consists of (1) multigroup weighted flux equations which can be interpreted as the multigroup RTE averaged over subintervals of angular range and (2) the effective grey (one-group) equations which are spectrum averaged low-order quasidiffusion (aka variable Eddington factor) equations. The system of RTE, low-order and MEB equations is approximated by the fully implicit Euler time-integration method in which absorption coefficient and emission term are evaluated at the current time step. Numerical results are presented to demonstrate convergence of a multilevel iteration algorithm in the Fleck-Cummings test problem with Marshak wave solved with large number of photon energy groups.



1
Introduction

Radiative transfer plays an important part in high-temperature phenomena of
high-energy density physics, inertial confinement fusion, and astrophysics
[
1
]
.
High-energy photons affect redistribution of energy in such physical systems.
The state of the physical system is significantly impacted by absorption of photons by matter and their emission,
leading to change in the material energy.
The Boltzmann radiative transfer equation (RTE) describes photon transport in a medium and the interaction of particles with matter.
The absorption coefficient and emission source term in the RTE nonlinearly depend on the material temperature.

The fundamental thermal radiative transfer (TRT) model in 1D slab geometry is defined by
the time-dependent multigroup RTE for
the group intensity

\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}I_{g}+\mathcal{L}I_{g}=\sigma_{g}B_{g}\,,\quad g\in\mathbb{N}(n_{\nu})\,,

(1)

\mathcal{L}I_{g}(x,\mu,t)\equiv\mu\partial_{x}I_{g}(x,\mu,t)+\sigma_{g}(x;T)I_{g}(x,\mu,t)\,,

(2)

I_{g}\big|_{t=0}=I_{g}^{0}\,,\quad I_{g}\Big|_{\overset{\mu>0}{x=0}}=I_{g}^{+}\,,\quad I_{g}\Big|_{\overset{\mu<0}{x=X}}=I_{g}^{-}\,,

(3)

and the material energy balance (MEB) equation

\ \partial_{t}\varepsilon(T)=\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}\int_{-1}^{1}\sigma_{g}(x;T)\Bigl(I_{g}(x,\mu,t)-B_{g}(T)\Bigr)d\mu\,,

(4)

T\big|_{t=0}=T^{0}\,.

(5)

Here
x
is the spatial position;

\mu
is the directional cosine of particle motion;
t is time;

g
is the index of the photon energy group;

T
is the material temperature;

\varepsilon
is the material energy density;

I_{g}=\int_{\nu_{g}}^{\nu_{g+1}}I_{\nu}d\nu\,

(6)

is the group intensity,
\nu
is the photon frequency,

B_{g}(T)=\int_{\nu_{g}}^{\nu_{g+1}}B_{\nu}(T)d\nu\,

(7)

is the group Planckian function,

\sigma_{g}(T)=\frac{\int_{\nu_{g}}^{\nu_{g+1}}\sigma_{\nu}(T)B_{\nu}(T)d\nu}{\int_{\nu_{g}}^{\nu_{g+1}}B_{\nu}(T)d\nu}\,

(8)

is the group averaged absorption coefficient.
The group structure is defined by the photon energy grid

\Lambda_{h\nu}=\{h\nu_{g},g\in\mathbb{N}(n_{\nu}+1):\ h\nu_{min}=h\nu_{1}<\ldots<h\nu_{g}<h\nu_{g+1}<\ldots<h\nu_{n_{\nu}+1}=h\nu_{max}\}\,.

(9)

This TRT model neglects material motion, scattering, heat conduction, and external sources.
It describes the phenomena of a supersonic radiation wave.
The general model is formulated by the system of radiation hydrodynamics equations, which involves conservation of mass and momentum equations and accounts for the radiation effect on matter
[
2
]
.

There exist a family of computational methods for TRT problems which apply
linearizaton to the multigroup RTE resulting
in the RTE with a pseudo-scattering term.
To accelerate transport iterations due to pseudo-scattering,
efficient algorithms are used

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
A different group of methods follows the moment-based approach to formulate iteration schemes based high-order and low-order equations
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

In this paper, we apply a nonlinear projection approach to formulate a new multilevel iteration method for solving coupled RTE and MEB equation
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
The hierarchy of equations of the method is defined for different moments of the radiation intensity.
The RTE is projected sequentially over elements of phase space to derive nonlinear moment equations with exact closures.
One part of the hierarchy of moment equations is defined by multigroup low-order nonlinear weighted flux (LONWF) equations,
which can be viewed as the RTE averaged over subintervals of angular range with certain weight functions

[
12
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
Another part of the hierarchy is the effective grey
low-order quasidiffusion (GLOQD), aka variable Eddington factor (VEF), equations
[
10
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
.
The LONWF equations are defined for partial angular moments of the intensity.
They mimic the structure of the RTE and lead to a coupled set of Cauchy problems in space.
The GLOQD equations are formulated for the full-range angular moments of the intensity and have a structure similar to the
P_{1}
equations,
resulting in a boundary value problem in space. Thus, the proposed multilevel method is based on low-order problems of mixed types.
Parallel algorithms developed for solving the RTE on GPUs can be adopted for solving the LONWF equations.
This provides an opportunity for developing fast and efficient algorithms for TRT problems on advanced
high-performance computing system with GPUs.

Our approach possesses the following major potential advantages over more
traditional discretization schemes:
(i) the low-order system for the radiation energy is hyperbolic and this naturally avoids some pathological issues
that can arrive when coupling to diffusion near highly heterogeneous material interfaces,
(ii) our formulation can result in positivity for both the angular intensity and the angle-averaged solutions through positivity-preserving sweeps, (iii)
our method naturally extends to using a hierarchy of levels in both angle and frequency, which opens the way for potentially much more efficient iterative methods,
(iv) our approach can dynamically adapt the multi-level hierarchy of equations for different physics regimes, potentially resulting in greater efficiency.

2
Formulation of the Multilevel Method

The method is formulated by a multiscale system of differential equations consisting of

1.

the multigroup high-order RTE (Eq. (
1
)) on the grid
\Lambda_{h\nu}
for the group intensities
I_{g}
,

2.

the multigroup LONWF equations on the grid
\Lambda_{h\nu}

for the group partial angular moments of radiation intensities

\phi_{g}^{\pm}=\pm\int_{0}^{\pm 1}I_{g}d\mu\,,

(10)

3.

effective GLOQD equations on the coarse photon energy grid

\Lambda_{h\nu}^{\ast}=\{h\nu_{1}=h\nu_{min},h\nu_{2}=h\nu_{max}\}\,

(11)

for the total radiation energy density and flux

E=\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}E_{g}\,,\quad F=\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}F_{g}\,,

(12)

where the group quantities are defined by

E_{g}=\frac{1}{c}\int_{-1}^{1}I_{g}d\mu\,,\quad F_{g}=\int_{-1}^{1}\mu I_{g}d\mu\,.

(13)

The multigroup LONWF equations for
\phi_{g}^{\pm}
are derived by taking angular moments
of the RTE with weight
w(\mu)=\gamma(1+\sqrt{3}|\mu|)
over half angular ranges, and are given by
[
19
,
20
]

\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}(K_{g}^{\pm}\phi_{g}^{\pm})\pm\partial_{x}(H_{g}^{\pm}\phi_{g}^{\pm})+\sigma_{g}K_{g}^{\pm}\phi_{g}^{\pm}=\sigma_{g}B_{g}\,,

(14)

\gamma=\Big(1+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}\Big)^{-1}\,.

(15)

The closures for the low-order equations (
14
) are formulated by the factors

K_{g}^{\pm}=\gamma\frac{\int_{0}^{\pm 1}\big(1+\sqrt{3}|\mu|I_{g}\big)d\mu}{\int_{0}^{\pm 1}I_{g}d\mu}\,,\quad H_{g}^{\pm}=\gamma\frac{\int_{0}^{\pm 1}|\mu|\big(1+\sqrt{3}|\mu|I_{g}\big)d\mu}{\int_{0}^{\pm 1}I_{g}d\mu}\,.

(16)

The group radiation energy density and flux (Eq. (
13
)) are defined with the LONWF solution as follows:

E_{g}=\frac{1}{c}(\phi_{g}^{-}+\phi_{g}^{+})\,,\quad F_{g}=G_{g}^{-}\phi_{g}^{-}+G_{g}^{+}\phi_{g}^{+}\,,

(17)

where

G_{g}^{\pm}=\frac{\int_{0}^{\pm 1}\mu I_{g}d\mu}{\int_{0}^{\pm 1}I_{g}d\mu}\,.

(18)

The projection with the angular weight
w(\mu)
leads to the low-order equations which meet the asymptotic diffusion limit in the interior of a diffusive domain with asymptotic boundary conditions.
The low-order equations (
14
) and high-order RTE can be approximated by independent discretization schemes.

The first step in deriving the effective GLOQD equations is integrating the RTE over
-1\leq\mu\leq 1
with
weigh 1 and
\mu
and introducing exact closure by means of the group Eddington factor

f_{g}=\frac{\int_{-1}^{1}\mu^{2}I_{g}d\mu}{\int_{-1}^{1}I_{g}d\mu}\,

(19)

to obtain multigroup moment equations for
E_{g}
and
F_{g}
.
On the next step, the moment equations are summed over photon energy groups to get
the effective grey moment equations on the coarse photon energy grid
\Lambda_{h\nu}^{\ast}
with just one group (
n_{\nu}=1
)
for the total radiation energy density and flux (Eq. (
12
)).
The effective GLOQD equations are defined by

[
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
:

\partial_{t}E+\partial_{x}F+c\bar{\sigma}_{E}E=c\bar{\sigma}_{B}a_{R}T^{4}\,,

(20a)

\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}F+c\partial_{x}(\bar{f}E)+\bar{\sigma}_{R}F+\bar{\eta}E=0\,,

(20b)

where

\bar{\sigma}_{E}=\frac{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}\sigma_{g}E_{g}}{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}E_{g}}\,,\quad\bar{\sigma}_{B}=\frac{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}\sigma_{g}B_{g}}{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}B_{g}}\,,\quad\bar{\sigma}_{R}=\frac{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}\sigma_{g}|F_{g}|}{\sum_{g=1}^{G}|F_{g}|}\,

(21)

are different grey absorption coefficients.

\bar{f}=\frac{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}f_{g}E_{g}}{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}E_{g}}\,

(22)

is the grey Eddington factor. It is a spectrum average of group Eddington factors.
The compensation term in Eq. (
20b
) is defined by

\bar{\eta}=\frac{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}(\sigma_{g}-\bar{\sigma}_{R})F_{g}}{\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}E_{g}}\,.

(23)

The MEB equation in the effective grey form is given by

\partial_{t}\varepsilon(T)=c\big(\bar{\sigma}_{E}E-\bar{\sigma}_{B}a_{R}T^{4}\big)\,.

(24)

Finally, the multilevel method is defined by the hierarchy of equations
the general form of which is the following:

1.

the multigroup RTE

\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}I_{g}+\mathcal{L}I_{g}=q_{g}\,,\quad g\in\mathbb{N}(n_{\nu})\,,

(25)

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(T)\,,\quad q_{g}=q_{g}(T)\,,

2.

the multigroup LONWF equations

\partial_{t}(\mathcal{K}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{g})+\mathcal{P}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{g}=\mathbf{Q}_{g}\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{g}=(\phi_{g}^{-},\phi_{g}^{+})^{\top}\,,\quad\mathbf{Q}_{g}=\mathbf{Q}_{g}(T)\,,

(26)

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(T)\,,

3.

the GLOQD equations

\partial_{t}\mathbf{Y}+\mathcal{M}\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{Q}\,,\quad\mathbf{Y}=(E,F)^{\top}\,,

(27)

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{\Phi},T)\,,\quad\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{Q}(T)\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\Phi}=\{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{g}\}_{g=1}^{n_{\nu}}

4.

the MEB equation

\partial_{t}\varepsilon=\mathcal{R}\mathbf{Y}-Q_{pl}\,,

(28)

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{\Phi},T)\,,\quad Q_{pl}=Q_{pl}(T)\,.

In this study, we discretize this system of equations with a fully-implicit temporal scheme based on the backward Euler time-integration method.
The RTE is approximated in space by the simple corner balance scheme
[
29
]
.
The lumped linear discontinuous (LLD) scheme is used to discretize the LONWF equations
[
30
,
19
,
20
]
.
The GLOQD equations are approximated by the second-order finite volume method
[
28
]
.
We note that independent spatial discretization schemes are applied for approximating the RTE, LONWF, and GLOQD equations
[
12
]
.

5
Conclusions

We developed a new multilevel iteration method for solving multigroup TRT problems in 1D slab geometry.
The obtained results are promising. The algorithm is rapidly convergent.
It is stable with independent discretization of the high-order RTE and low-order equations at each level.
Efficiency of the algorithm can be optimized by choosing the number of
V
-cycles per outer (transport) iteration.
Further detailed analysis of iteration algorithm is necessary.
Future work will include development of methods with the mixed type low-order
problems using multiple grids in photon energy and various multigrid cycles to further optimize performance of iteration schemes
and computational cost. Various discretization schemes will be considered.
The proposed multilevel method can be extended to multidimensional geometries, and is suitable for computations on high-performance computing systems with GPUs.

Limiting one-point fluctuations of the geodesic in the directed landscape near the endpoints when the geodesic length goes to infinity
Zhipeng Liu, Chen Ma, Tejaswi Tripathi

Abstract
We consider the limiting fluctuations of the geodesic in the directed landscape, conditioning on its length going to infinity. It was shown in [ Liu22c , GHZ23 ] that when the directed landscape ℒ ⁡ ( 0 , 0 , 0 , 1 ) = L \mathcal{L}(0,0;0,1)=L becomes large, the geodesic from ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0) to ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) lies in a strip of size O ( L − 1 / 4 ) O(L^{-1/4}) and behaves like a Brownian bridge if we zoom in the strip by a factor of L 1 / 4 L^{1/4} . Moreover, the length along the geodesic with respect to the directed landscape fluctuates of order O ⁡ ( L 1 / 4 ) O(L^{1/4}) and its limiting one-point distribution is Gaussian [ Liu22c ] . In this paper, we further zoom in a smaller neighborhood of the endpoints when ℒ ⁡ ( 0 , 0 , 0 , 1 ) = L \mathcal{L}(0,0;0,1)=L or ℒ ⁡ ( 0 , 0 , 0 , 1 ) ≥ L \mathcal{L}(0,0;0,1)\geq L , and show that there is a critical scaling window L − 3 / 2 : L − 1 : L − 1 / 2 L^{-3/2}:L^{-1}:L^{-1/2} for the time, geodesic location, and geodesic length, respectively. Within this scaling window, we find a nontrivial limit of the one-point joint distribution of the geodesic location and length as L → ∞ L\to\infty . This limiting distribution, if we tune the time parameter to infinity, converges to the joint distribution of two independent Gaussian random variables, which is consistent with the results in [ Liu22c ] . We also find a surprising connection between this limiting distribution and the one-point distribution of the upper tail field of the KPZ fixed point recently obtained in [ LZ25 ] .

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and main results

The directed landscape
\mathcal{L}(x,s;y,t)
, defined on
\mathbb{R}_{\uparrow}^{4}:=\{(x,s;y,t):x,y,s,t\in\mathbb{R},s<t\}
, is a random “metric” constructed in
[
DOV22
]
as a limiting field of the Brownian last passage percolation. It is believed, and in some cases proved, to be a universal limit for models in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class
[
DV21
,
Wu23
,
ACH24
,
DZ25
]
. The directed landscape
\mathcal{L}(x,s;y,t)
satisfies the following reverse triangle inequality

\mathcal{L}(x,s;x^{\prime},s^{\prime})+\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},s^{\prime};y,t)\leq\mathcal{L}(x,s;y,t),\quad\text{ for all }x,x^{\prime},y,s,s^{\prime},t\in\mathbb{R},\text{ satisfying }s<s^{\prime}<t.

(1.1)

This induces the concept of the geodesic
\pi_{x,s;y,t}:[s,t]\to\mathbb{R}
for any
(x,s;y,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\uparrow}^{4}
, such that
\pi_{x,s;y,t}(s)=x
,
\pi_{x,s;y,t}(t)=y
, and

\mathcal{L}(x,s;\pi_{x,s;y,t}(s^{\prime}),s^{\prime})+\mathcal{L}(\pi_{x,s;y,t}(s^{\prime}),s^{\prime};y,t)=\mathcal{L}(x,s;y,t),\quad\text{for all }s^{\prime}\in(s,t).

(1.2)

It is known that such a geodesic
\pi_{x,s;y,t}
for fixed
(x,s;y,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\uparrow}^{4}
exists and is unique almost surely
[
DOV22
]
. One simple property of a geodesic is that any segment of the geodesic is also a geodesic between the two endpoints. Hence, we call
\mathcal{L}(\pi(s^{\prime}),s^{\prime};\pi(t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})
the
length
of the geodesic segment from
(\pi(s^{\prime}),s^{\prime})
to
(\pi(t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})
for all
s^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime}
satisfying
s\leq s^{\prime}<t^{\prime}\leq t
.

The directed landscape and its geodesics have very nice shifting and scaling invariance properties, which allow a translation of the properties of a geodesic
\pi_{x,s;y,t}
to that of another geodesic
\pi_{x^{\prime},s^{\prime};y^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
with different parameters. See Lemma 10.2 in
[
DOV22
]
for these properties. Without loss of generality, we consider the geodesic
\pi_{0,0;0,1}
in this paper and denote

\pi(t)=\pi_{0,0;0,1}(t),\quad 0\leq t\leq 1

(1.3)

as the geodesic location, and

\mathcal{L}(t)=\mathcal{L}(0,0;\pi(t),t),\quad 0<t\leq 1

(1.4)

as the length of the geodesic segment from
(0,0)
to
(\pi(t),t)
. Note that
\mathcal{L}(1)=\mathcal{L}(0,0;0,1)
. We also set
\mathcal{L}(0)=0
for convenience.

We are mainly interested in the limiting fluctuations of the geodesic
\pi(t)
and the length
\mathcal{L}(t)
, conditioning on
\mathcal{L}(1)=L
becoming very large, which is called the upper tail conditioning. There have been quite a number of recent results on the large deviation of the directed landscape and related objects in the upper tail regime
[
BG19
,
GH22
,
GLLT23
,
LT25
,
DDV24
,
DT24
]
, and their limiting conditional fluctuations
[
Liu22c
,
LW24
,
GHZ23
,
BL24
,
LZ25
,
BCT25
]
. Particularly, the fluctuations of the geodesic with the upper tail conditioning were considered in
[
Liu22c
,
LW24
,
GHZ23
]
. It was shown in
[
Liu22c
]
that for any fixed
t\in(0,1)
, when
L
becomes large,
\pi(t)
fluctuates of order
O(L^{-1/4})
, and
\mathcal{L}(t)
fluctuates of order
O(L^{1/4})
. Furthermore,
\pi(t)
and
\mathcal{L}(t)
after rescaling converge to two independent Gaussian random variables. More explicitly, for any fixed
x_{1},x_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
x_{1}<x_{2}
and
h_{1}<h_{2}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{2L^{1/4}\pi(t)}{\sqrt{t(1-t)}}\in(x_{1},x_{2}),\frac{\mathcal{L}(t)-tL}{\sqrt{t(1-t)}L^{1/4}}\in(h_{1},h_{2})\ \big|\ \mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)\to\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\int_{h_{1}}^{h_{2}}\phi(x)\phi(h)\mathrm{d}h\mathrm{d}x

(1.5)

as
L\to\infty
, where

is the standard Gaussian density. Later, based on (

) and a heuristic argument,
[

]
conjectured that

{\rm Law}\left(\left\{2L^{1/4}\pi(t),L^{-1/4}(\mathcal{L}(t)-tL)\right\}_{t\in(0,1)}\bigg|\ \mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)\to{\rm Law}\left(\left\{\mathbb{B}_{1}(t),\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)\right\}_{t\in(0,1)}\right)

(1.6)

as

\mathbb{B}_{1}

are two independent standard Brownian bridges. The Brownian bridge limit for the geodesic location was also suggested for the Brownian polymer model under the upper tail conditioning in an earlier paper
[
BG19
]

[
GHZ23
]
were able to show that the geodesic location after appropriate scaling indeed converges to a Brownian bridge under the upper tail conditioning for the directed landscape. This settled the first half of the conjecture (
1.6

While the conjecture (
1.6

[
Liu22c

]

[

]

O(L^{-3/2})

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
t>0
is fixed. For any
x_{1},x_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
x_{1}<x_{2}
and
h_{1}<h_{2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(L\pi(tL^{-3/2})\in(x_{1},x_{2}),L^{1/2}\mathcal{L}(tL^{-3/2})\in(h_{1},h_{2})\ \big|\ \mathcal{L}(1)\geq L\right)\to\int_{h_{1}}^{h_{2}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}h,

(1.7)

and

\mathbb{P}\left(L\pi(1-tL^{-3/2})\in(x_{1},x_{2}),L^{1/2}(L-\mathcal{L}(1-tL^{-3/2}))\in(h_{1},h_{2})\ \big|\ \mathcal{L}(1)\geq L\right)\to\int_{h_{1}}^{h_{2}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\hat{\mathrm{p}}(h,x;t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}h

(1.8)

as
L\to\infty
. We also have

\mathbb{P}\left(L\pi(tL^{-3/2})\in(x_{1},x_{2}),L^{1/2}\mathcal{L}(tL^{-3/2})\in(h_{1},h_{2})\ \big|\ \mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)\to\int_{h_{1}}^{h_{2}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}h,

(1.9)

and

\mathbb{P}\left(L\pi(1-tL^{-3/2})\in(x_{1},x_{2}),L^{1/2}(L-\mathcal{L}(1-tL^{-3/2}))\in(h_{1},h_{2})\ \big|\ \mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)\to\int_{h_{1}}^{h_{2}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}h,

(1.10)

as
L\to\infty
. Here the functions
\hat{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathrm{p}
are explicitly defined in Definition
1.7
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that the limits in (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) are different. Heuristically, this difference naturally arises from the sensitivity of the two variables,
L^{1/2}\mathcal{L}(tL^{-3/2})
and

perturbation of

O(1)
difference in

when
L
becomes large.

We remark that both functions

and
\mathrm{p}
are joint probability density functions, as stated in the Proposition

Proposition 1.3
.

Both functions
\hat{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathrm{p}
are joint probability density functions for each

, i.e.,
\hat{\mathrm{p}}(h,x;t)\geq 0
and

(1.11)

Using the formulas of
\hat{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathrm{p}
, we are able to prove the following properties of these two joint probability density functions.

Proposition 1.4
.

The functions
\hat{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathrm{p}
have the following properties.

(1)

Both functions are symmetric in

\hat{\mathrm{p}}(h,x;t)=\hat{\mathrm{p}}(h,-x;t)

(2)

The following identities hold for any fixed

(1.13)

As a corollary, if

(3)

When the time parameter goes to infinity, the joint density function

(1.14)

where

(4)

For any fixed

\begin{split}\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)&=\frac{1}{4\pi t}e^{-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\left(h+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}}{t^{1/2}}+2h-\frac{2}{3}t}(1+o(1)),\\

(1.15)

as

\begin{split}\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)&=\frac{1}{2\pi|x|}e^{-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\left(h+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}}{t^{1/2}}+2h-\frac{2}{3}t}(1+o(1)),\\

1.3
Relations to the KPZ fixed point with the upper tail conditioning

The KPZ fixed point
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,t)
,
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, which was first constructed by
[
MQR21
]
, is a universal limit of the space-time height function of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class
[
NQR20
,
QS23
,
Vir20
,
ACH24
]
. It has the following connection to the directed landscape (see
[
NQR20
]
)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x^{\prime},0)+\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},0;x,t)\right\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,

(1.31)

where
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,0)
is the initial data of the KPZ fixed point. We will only consider a special initial condition, the narrow-wedge initial condition
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,0)=-\infty\mathbf{1}_{x\neq 0}
in this paper. Thus, we simply use the same notation
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,t)
for the KPZ fixed point with the narrow-wedge initial condition throughout the rest of the paper. The equation (
1.31
) becomes

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathcal{L}(0,0;x,t),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0.

(1.32)

Although the geodesics in the directed landscape are determined by the full field
\mathcal{L}(x,s;y,t)
by (
1.2
), the relationship between the KPZ fixed point and the directed landscape, given by (
1.32
), suggests that the geodesic information may be intricately intertwined with that of the KPZ fixed point in a subtle, non-explicit way. Below, we explain that under the upper tail conditioning, the geodesic in the directed landscape is heuristically the same as the argmax process of the KPZ fixed point when
t=O(1)
.

In
[
LW24
]
, the authors considered the KPZ fixed point with the upper tail conditioning and found that

{\rm Law}\left(\left\{L^{-1/4}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}\left(\frac{x}{2L^{1/4}},t\right)-tL\right)\right\}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)}\Big|\ \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(0,1)=L\right)\\
\xrightarrow{\textit{f.d.d.}}\left\{\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)-|\mathbb{B}_{1}(t)-x|\right\}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)}

(1.33)

as
L\to\infty
, where
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
are two independent standard Brownian bridges, and the
\xrightarrow{\textit{f.d.d.}}
denotes the convergence of finite dimensional distributions. Note that
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(0,1)=\mathcal{L}(1)
. One can easily observe that
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
are the argmax and max processes of the limiting field (
1.33
). Therefore, we heuristically have

\left\{\left(\textrm{argmax}_{x}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}\left(\frac{x}{2L^{1/4}},t\right),L^{-1/4}\max_{x}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}\left(\frac{x}{2L^{1/4}},t\right)-tL\right)\right)\Big|\ \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(0,1)=L\right\}_{t\in(0,1)}

(1.34)

converges to
\{\mathbb{B}_{1}(t),\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)\}_{t\in(0,1)}
, assuming the left-hand side is well defined.

Recall that the limiting distribution of
L^{-1/4}(\mathcal{L}(t)-tL)
is Gaussian (
1.5
), and it matches the distribution of
\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)
for any fixed
t
. Moreover, the limiting distribution of
L^{-1/4}\left(\max_{x}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x/(2L^{1/4}),t)-tL\right)
is always greater than or equal to
L^{-1/4}(\mathcal{L}(t)-tL)
. Therefore, the two random variables should asymptotically match. Based on these observations,
[
LW24
, Conjectures 1.10, 1.11]
conjectured that, with the upper tail conditioning, the geodesic
\pi(t)
and its length
\mathcal{L}(t)
are the same as the argmax and the max processes of the KPZ fixed point after appropriate scaling. More explicitly, they conjectured that

\begin{split}&\left\{2L^{1/4}\pi(t),L^{-1/4}(\mathcal{L}(t)-tL)\right\}_{t\in(0,1)},\\
&\left\{\textrm{argmax}_{x}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}\left(\frac{x}{2L^{1/4}},t\right),L^{-1/4}\max_{x}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}\left(\frac{x}{2L^{1/4}},t\right)-tL\right)\right\}_{t\in(0,1)}\end{split}

(1.35)

have the same limiting law conditioning on
\mathcal{L}(1)=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(0,1)=L
as
L\to\infty
, and the limit is given by
\left\{\mathbb{B}_{1}(t),\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)\right\}_{t\in(0,1)}
.

Note that the discussions above are about the limiting behaviors of the geodesic in the directed landscape and the KPZ fixed point when the time parameter
t
is of order
O(1)
under the upper tail conditioning. Very recently, it was shown in
[
LZ25
]
that, with the same upper tail conditioning, there is a critical scaling near the point
(0,1)
with which the conditional KPZ fixed point converges to a new space time random field that interpolates a Brownian-like field and the KPZ fixed point
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}
. This new field is called the upper tail field. More explicitly, they showed that

{\rm Law}\left(\left\{\sqrt{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(xL^{-1},1+tL^{-3/2})-L)\right\}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|\ \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(0,1)\geq L\right)\xrightarrow{\textit{f.d.d.}}{\rm Law}\left(\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,t)\right\}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right)

(1.36)

and

{\rm Law}\left(\left\{\sqrt{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(xL^{-1},1+tL^{-3/2})-L)\right\}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|\ \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(0,1)=L\right)\xrightarrow{\textit{f.d.d.}}{\rm Law}\left(\left\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,t)\right\}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right),

(1.37)

where
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}
are called the upper tail fields of the KPZ fixed point. We call
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
the lifted upper tail field and
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}
the grounded upper tail field. They satisfy the following relation

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,t)=\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,t)+E,

(1.38)

where
E=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
is an exponential random variable with parameter
2
which is independent of
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)=0
. The grounded upper tail field also satisfies that
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,0)
has the same distribution as
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}(2x)-2|x|
, where
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}
denotes a two-sided Brownian motion with
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}(0)=0
.

Recall our main results in Theorem
1.1
about the limiting geodesic distributions within the same scaling window. By comparing it with the distributions of the lifted upper tail field obtained in
[
LZ25
]
, we find the following surprising connection.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
h,x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
, we have

\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)=-\left(\frac{1}{12}\partial^{4}_{h}+\frac{1}{4}\partial^{2}_{x}+\partial_{t}\partial_{h}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,-t)\geq-h\right).

(1.39)

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

The proof is straightforward. The joint tail probabilities of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
was explicitly formulated in
[
LZ25
]
. In particular,
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(x,-t)\geq-h\right)
has exactly the same formula as for
\mathrm{p}(h,-x;t)
in (
1.24
), except that there is no differentiation operator
\mathrm{D}
in the integrand (
1.26
), and a negative sign in front. See the Definition 2.4 and the formulas (2.67) and (2.68) in
[
LZ25
]
. The Proposition 2.7 in
[

]
claimed that any differentiation of the tail probability functions of the field

can be taken inside the summation and integral. Therefore the differentiation
\left(\frac{1}{12}\partial^{4}_{h}+\frac{1}{4}\partial^{2}_{x}+\partial_{t}\partial_{h}\right)

\mathrm{D}
in (

1.4
Main idea of the proof and structure of the paper

The proofs of the results in this paper involve asymptotic analysis for series expansions of contour integrals, and probabilistic arguments for the KPZ fixed point and directed landscape. Similar asymptotic analysis for this type of expansions has been considered in earlier papers
[
Liu22b
,
Liu22c
,
LW24
,
LZ25
]
, and our analysis in this paper adapts ideas from these papers in our settings. With that being said, our results are novel and some results are surprising, such as Theorem
1.8
. Moreover, the most challenging part of the proofs in this paper actually comes from that of Proposition
1.3
, i.e., the claim that
\mathrm{p}
and
\hat{\mathrm{p}}
are probability density functions. We introduce some nontrivial probabilistic arguments to bound the geodesic location and geodesic length when the time window is as small as
O(L^{-3/2})
. We leverage many properties of the directed landscape and the KPZ fixed point, while also applying the latest results from the recently defined upper tail field of the KPZ fixed point. We believe that the ideas of these probabilistic arguments could potentially be useful in proving similar properties of the geodesic in the directed landscape.

Below is the structure of the rest of the paper.

Section
2
is the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We conduct a steepest descent analysis of the joint density function, derived in
[
Liu22b
]
, for the geodesic location and geodesic lengths, incorporating the scaling considered in this paper.

In Section

, we prove Proposition
1.3
using probabilistic arguments. The main results in this section are Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, which we prove in subsections
3.1
and
3.2
respectively.

Finally, in Section

, we provide the proof of Proposition
1.4
. The two more involved properties about the large time limit and the large

or

limit of the joint density functions are proved in two subsections

and

, at most one pole at
u_{i}=-1
could make a nonzero contribution due to the factor
\prod_{i<j}(u_{i}-u_{j})^{2}
. Similarly for the
v_{i}
contours
\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}
. Therefore, heuristically the first two terms in the expansion (
4.12
) would dominate the summation in both cases, and we have

\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)\approx\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}}\mathrm{I}_{1}(u,v)\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{I}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}.

(4.19)

Below we approximate the two terms on the right hand.

For the first term, similar to the
n=1
case we discussed in the previous subsection, we have

\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}}\mathrm{I}_{1}(u,v)\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}=-2e^{-\frac{2}{3}t+2h}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}}\frac{(1+v)(1-u)}{u-v}\frac{f_{h,x;t}(u)}{f_{h,x;t}(v)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}.

(4.20)

We apply the steepest descent analysis to the double contour integral. The main contribution of the integral comes from the neighborhoods of
u\approx w_{c}^{-}
and
v\approx w_{c}^{+}
. We write
u=w_{c}^{-}+t^{-1/3}\xi M^{-1/2}
,
v=w_{c}^{+}+t^{-1/3}\eta M^{-1/2}
, and obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}}\frac{(1+v)(1-u)}{u-v}\frac{f_{h,x;t}(u)}{f_{h,x;t}(v)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}&\approx\frac{(1+w_{c}^{+})(1-w_{c}^{-})e^{-4M^{3}/3}}{t^{2/3}M(w_{c}^{-}-w_{c}^{+})}\int_{\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}}e^{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\\
&\approx\frac{(1+t^{-1/3}M)^{2}-x^{2}t^{-2}}{4\pi(-2t^{1/3}M^{2})}e^{-4M^{3}/3}\\
&\approx\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{8\pi t}e^{-4M^{3}/3},&\text{ when $x$ is fixed and $h\to\infty$},\\
-\frac{1}{4\pi x}e^{-4M^{3}/3},&\text{ when $h$ is fixed and $x\to\infty$}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.21)

Thus we have

\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}}\mathrm{I}_{1}(u,v)\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\approx\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4\pi t}e^{-\frac{4\left(h+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}}{3t^{1/2}}+2h-\frac{2}{3}t},&\text{ when $x$ is fixed and $h\to\infty$},\\
\frac{1}{2\pi x}e^{-\frac{4\left(h+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}}{3t^{1/2}}+2h-\frac{2}{3}t},&\text{ when $h$ is fixed and $x\to\infty$}.\end{cases}

(4.22)

We remark that a similar approximation was done in
[
LZ25
]
for the double integral of (
4.13
) for
n=1
but without the last factor
\mathrm{H}(u,1;v,-1)=2(1-v)(1+u)(u-v)
(see (
4.1
)) in
\mathrm{I}_{1}(u,v)
. The main difference in that paper is that due to this missing factor which would cancel the factors
(1+u)(1-v)
in the denominator, the leading contribution of the double integral in that paper is obtained when one integral results in the residue at
u=-1
or
v=1
while the other integral is deformed to the critical point. This results in a difference of the exponent in the approximation in
[
LZ25
]
. See the proof of Proposition 2.9 in the paper for more details.

We continue to consider the second term in (
4.19
). We will prove that this term is exponentially smaller compared to the first term we just considered.

We consider the two cases separately. When
x>0
is fixed and
h\to\infty
, we see that the critical points
w_{c}^{-}<-1
and
w_{c}^{+}>1
. Therefore when we deform the
u_{1},u_{2}
contours to the critical point
w_{c}^{-}
, we need to pass the pole at
u_{1}=-1
or
u_{2}=-1
. Similarly for the
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
contours. Note the factors
(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}
in
\mathrm{I}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})
. These factors imply that at most one of the
u_{i}
variables is evaluated at the residue at
-1
, and at most one of the
v_{i}
variables is evaluated at the residue at
1
. Finally, it is easy to verify that if
u_{i}=-1
and
v_{j}=1
for some
1\leq i,j\leq 2
, the factor
\mathrm{H}(u_{1},u_{2},1;v_{1},v_{2},-1)
in
\mathrm{I}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})
becomes zero, see (
4.2
). Combining the arguments, we can write
\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{I}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}
as the sum of three terms, the first term is a quadruple integral each of which passes a critical point, the second term is a triple integral each of which passes a critical point and one
u_{i}
integral is degenerated to the residue at
u_{i}=-1
, and the third term is a triple integral each of which passes a critical point and one
v_{i}
integral is degenerated to the residue at
v_{i}=1
. Using the approximation (
4.17
), we know that the three terms are approximately

e^{-\frac{8M^{3}}{3}-\frac{2}{3}t+2h+O(\log M)},e^{-2M^{3}-\frac{hx}{t}-\frac{2x^{3}}{3t^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}t+x+h+O(\log M)},\text{ and }e^{-2M^{3}+\frac{hx}{t}+\frac{2x^{3}}{3t^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}t-x+h+O(\log M)}

(4.23)

respectively. It is straightforward to verify that each exponent above is significantly less than
-\frac{4M^{3}}{3}+2h-\frac{2t}{3}
in the exponent of (
4.22
).

The second case for
h
fixed but
x\to\infty
is similar. The only difference is that the
w_{c}^{-}=\frac{x}{t}-t^{-1/3}M=O(x^{-1})
. Thus we could deform the
u_{i}
contours to the critical point without encountering any pole. Therefore
\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}^{2}\times\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}}\mathrm{I}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{2}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}
can be expressed as the sum of two terms instead of three. But the other arguments remain the same and these terms are still exponentially smaller than (
4.22
).

In conclusion, we obtain the following asymptotics

\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)\approx\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4\pi t}e^{-\frac{4\left(h+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}}{3t^{1/2}}+2h-\frac{2}{3}t},&\text{ when $x$ is fixed and $h\to\infty$},\\
\frac{1}{2\pi x}e^{-\frac{4\left(h+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}}{3t^{1/2}}+2h-\frac{2}{3}t},&\text{ when $h$ is fixed and $x\to\infty$}.\end{cases}

(4.24)

This proves Proposition
1.4
(4) for the function
\mathrm{p}
.

The proof for the function
\hat{\mathrm{p}}

\hat{\mathrm{I}}_{n}

\frac{2}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(v_{i}-u_{i})}

(4.25)

which does not affect the exponents in the arguments for the integral of
\mathrm{I}_{n}
above. This term when
n=1

\frac{2}{w_{c}^{+}-w_{c}^{-}}=\frac{t^{1/3}}{M}.

(4.26)

This implies
\hat{\mathrm{p}}(h,x;t)\approx\frac{t^{1/3}}{M}\mathrm{p}(h,x;t)
as

. Note the approximation of
M
in (
4.11
). The approximations for
\hat{\mathrm{p}}

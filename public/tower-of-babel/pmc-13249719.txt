Early Serum Clusterin Reduction is Associated with Weight Loss and Type 2 Diabetes Remission after Bariatric Surgery
Authors: Yiming Si, Yinfang Tu, Yufei Wang, Hongwei Zhang, Xiaodong Han, Weijie Liu, Pin Zhang, Xiaojing Ma, Haoyong Yu, Yuqian Bao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveClusterin is a newly identified adipokine associated with metabolic dysfunction, yet its postoperative dynamics and relationship to clinical outcomes after metabolic and bariatric surgery (MBS) remain unclear. This study aimed to investigate the changes of clusterin levels following MBS, evaluates its association with weight loss and type 2 diabetes (T2D) remission, and explore the underlying biological mechanisms.MethodsIn this 1-year retrospective cohort study, 75 patients with obesity and T2D (60% female, mean age 42 ± 12 years) undergoing MBS were enrolled. Metabolic outcomes included: (1) sufficient weight loss defined as total weight loss (TWL) ≥ 20%; (2) T2D remission as HbA1c < 5.7% and fasting glucose < 5.6 mmol/L without pharmacotherapy. Multivariable logistic regression and causal mediation analysis elucidated independent associations and potential mediating pathways. Clusterin levels were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.ResultsClusterin levels exhibited significant time-dependent reductions paralleling weight trajectories (P for trend <0.05), with rapid decline during the initial 6-month postoperative phase (Δ=-60.8 ± 129.8 ug/mL, P < 0.001) followed by stabilization. Multivariate analysis identified early clusterin reduction as an independent predictor of both sufficient weight loss (OR = 0.007, 95% CI 0.00–0.80, P = 0.04) and T2D remission (OR = 0.01, 95% CI 0.00–0.65, P = 0.036). Mediation analysis revealed that declines in weight, 2-hour postprandial insulin, and C-peptide partially mediated these associations.ConclusionsClusterin levels showed a downward trend postoperative, and early-phase reductions predicting favorable metabolic outcomes. The association appears partially mediated by improved insulin resistance. These findings highlight the potential relevance of clusterin as a biomarker associated with metabolic clinical outcomes following MBS.Key points• Early postoperative clusterin reduction is associated with weight loss and T2D remission.• The association is independent and partially mediated by improved insulin secretion.• Clusterin may serve as a potential biomarker for assessing metabolic surgery outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11695-026-08732-z.

Introduction

Obesity and its associated metabolic dysfunction represent a major global health challenge. Metabolic and bariatric surgery (MBS) is a highly effective intervention, inducing sustained weight loss and promoting remission of obesity-related comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes (T2D) [1, 2]. Adipose tissue dysfunction is a key driver of these metabolic abnormalities, characterized by dysregulated secretion of adipokines [3, 4].

Adipokines are biologically active proteins secreted by adipose tissue that play crucial roles in energy balance and metabolic regulation. Studies have documented postoperative changes in some adipokines. For instance, surgery enhances the secretion of satiety-promoting hormones (e.g., leptin, GLP-1, GIP, and PYY) while reducing the levels of appetite-stimulating hormones (e.g., ghrelin). These shifts in adipokine profiles are closely associated with postoperative weight loss [5, 6].

Clusterin (also known as APOJ) is a secreted sulfated glycoprotein with broad tissue distribution (e.g., adipose tissue, liver, brain, heart, and reproductive organs [7]), and is increasingly recognized as a functionally relevant adipokine [8–10]. Observational studies have shown that clusterin levels are increased in patients with insulin resistance [11], obesity [12], T2D [13], and metabolic syndrome [14], and decrease with weight loss [15]. However, research on post surgery changes in clusterin levels remains limited and inconsistent. For example, small-scale studies by Genua et al. [16] (n = 13) and Rodríguez-Rivera et al. [17] (n = 23) found no significant changes in clusterin levels at one year follow-up whereas others reported reductions as early as six months after MBS [18, 19]. Therefore, due to the inconsistent results of the current studies, changes in clusterin levels after MBS remain unclear. Additionally, changes in clusterin levels have not been studied with weight and metabolic changes.

This study aimed to investigate the longitudinal changes in clusterin levels following MBS, explore its associations with sufficient weight loss and T2D remission, and analyze potential mediators in patients with obesity and T2D.

Methods

Study Population

This retrospective cohort study comprised adult patients who met the following criteria: [1] age 18–65 years; [2] diagnosed with T2D; [3] obesity defined as a BMI ≥ 27.5 kg/m2 (a cutoff applicable to Asian populations) [20]; [4] underwent either laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB) or laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (SG) at our center; and [5] completed at least 1 year of postoperative follow-up with available core metabolic data. From our institutional database, we identified 75 patients who had complete primary study data (including weight, glycemic status, and serum samples for clusterin measurement) at baseline, 6 months, and 1 year, forming the final analysis cohort. Patients enrolled in this study did not undergo a structured preoperative weight loss program. The use of GLP-1 receptor agonists as part of the baseline glucose-lowering regimen was recorded. T2D was diagnosed with an HbA1c ≥ 6.5% and/or a fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥ 7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL) according to the 2018 American Diabetes Association criteria [21]. Patients with a history of malignancy, pregnancy, or revision surgery were excluded. All participants underwent standardized assessments before surgery, 6 months after surgery, and 12 months after surgery. The assessments included a standardized questionnaire, physical examination (height, weight, waist circumference, hip circumference, and blood pressure), and laboratory tests. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine and following the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent, which was covered by their broad informed consent for future research.

Laboratory Measurements

Venous blood samples were collected in the morning after 10 h of fasting, on the day before surgery. We measured FPG, fasting insulin (FINS), fasting C-peptide (FCP), glycated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), hemoglobin, albumin, total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, leukocyte count, c reactive protein, urea, uric acid, free triiodothyronine, free thyroxine, and thyroid-stimulating hormone. Postload blood samples were collected to determine 2-hour postprandial glucose (2hPG), 2-hour plasma insulin (2hINS) and 2-hour C-peptide (2hCP) after a mixed meal. The MRI scanner (Archiva 3.0 T; Philips Medical Systems, Amsterdam, Netherlands) was used to measure the visceral fat area (VFA). The specific technical protocols, equipment, and standard operating procedures employed for the above-mentioned laboratory measurements and VFA measurements are identical to those documented in our previously published work [22].

The serum samples analyzed were residual samples from blood draws taken during routine clinical follow-up. After clinical testing, these samples were stored at -80 °C in our institutional biobank under standard protocols with patient consent. For this study, following ethical approval, a batch ELISA for clusterin was performed on these stored samples. Serum clusterin levels were measured by ELISA (R&D Systems) with intra- and inter-assay variations of 3.45% and 7.33%, respectively.

Determinations and Calculations

The diagnostic criteria for T2D remission were defined as HbA1c < 5.7% and FPG < 5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL) for at least one year without medication or other treatment according to the definition of complete remission of diabetes in the 2009 ADA Joint Statement [23].

The definition of sufficient weight loss is percentage total weight loss (%TWL) ≥ 20%; %TWL = 100 * (preoperative weight - weight at 1 years postoperatively) / preoperative weight [24].

The parameters’ percentage change was defined at either the 6-month follow-up (%Δ6) or the 12-month follow-up (%Δ12) and baseline.

Basal insulin secretory function index (HOMA2-%β) and insulin resistance index (HOMA2-IR) were calculated based on the HOMA calculator v2.2.3 published by the University of Oxford [25].

Assessment of Potential Mediators

Potential mediators were evaluated using physical parameters and blood biomarkers as candidate intermediate variables. These included percentage changes from baseline to 6 months postoperatively in weight, waist circumference, hip circumference, blood pressure, inflammatory markers, metabolic parameters, and liver/kidney function indices, which were hypothesized to mediate the association between clusterin levels percentage changes and 1-year metabolic outcomes (sufficient weight loss or T2D remission).

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables were presented as percentages (%). Normally distributed continuous variables were described using mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data were reported as median (interquartile range, IQR). Paired t-tests were used to compare normally distributed data, and Mann-Whitney U tests were applied for non-normally distributed data between groups. Trend analyses across groups were performed using linear polynomial contrasts in analysis of variance (ANOVA).

Multiple logistic regression models were constructed to assess the independent association between clusterin levels percentage change (%Δ6Clusterin or %Δ12Clusterin) and 1-year T2D remission. Raw data were standardized via z-scoring. Model 1 was adjusted for age, sex, surgery type, diabetes duration, %Δ12FCP, %Δ12VFA, and use of hypoglycemic medications. Model 2 further included %Δ6weight as an additional covariate. To assess whether surgical type modulates the association between clusterin and T2D remission, we incorporated the interaction term of surgical type × %Δ6Clusterin into the logistic regression model.

For mediation analyses, although primary variables were complete, minor missingness occurred in some secondary parameters at the 1-year follow-up (e.g., 2hCP and 2hINS, with a maximum of 3 missing values out of 75, 4.0%). To maintain statistical power and minimize potential bias, these missing data were imputed using multiple imputation with chained equations (MICE; R package mice) with 20 iterations. The missingness was assumed to be missing at random (MAR), and the convergence of the imputation algorithm was verified through diagnostic plots. We compared baseline characteristics between patients with complete 1-year follow-up and those with missing mediator data. No significant differences were observed in key baseline variables, supporting the appropriateness of the multiple imputation approach (Supplementary Table 1). Raw data were standardized before linear regression to screen potential mediators, followed by formal mediation analysis to estimate the proportion mediated (PM), using the “mediation” package with 95% CI derived from 1000 non-parametric bootstrap resamples. This framework employed linear regression for the mediator model and logistic regression for the outcome model. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient Characteristics and Baseline Data

A total of 75 patients (45 women, 30 men) were included, with 35 underwent RYGB and 40 underwent SG. Baseline demographics revealed a mean age of 42 ± 12 years (range from 18 to 65 years) and a mean BMI of 35.5 ± 5.1 kg/m2 (range from 27.8 to 50.8 kg/m2). Postoperative follow-up data showed significant improvements from baseline in weight, BMI, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), FPG, HbA1c, FCP, TG, and LDL-c (all P < 0.05; Supplementary Table 2). At 1-year postoperatively, 37 patients (49.3%) achieved T2D remission. Compared to non-remission patients, those in the remission group had higher baseline BMI, waist circumference, hip circumference, FCP, and insulin resistance indices (HOMA2-%β, HOMA2-IR), but shorter diabetes duration and lower baseline FPG and HbA1c levels (all P < 0.05). Notably, baseline clusterin levels did not differ significantly between the two groups (P > 0.05; Table 1).

Weight Loss and Clusterin Levels Dynamics

Patients demonstrated a significant, time-dependent reduction in body weight from baseline to 12 months postoperatively (P for trend < 0.05; Fig. 1). Mean weight declined from 100.9 ± 15.7 kg at baseline to 76.8 ± 11.2 kg at 6 months (23.9% reduction) and stabilized at 74.0 ± 13.4 kg at 12 months (26.7% reduction). Concomitantly, clusterin levels exhibited a biphasic decline (P for trend < 0.05; Fig. 1), decreasing from 527.1 ± 110.9 µg/mL at baseline to 466.3 ± 130.2 µg/mL at 6 months (11.5% reduction) and slightly rebounding to 483.4 ± 148.5 µg/mL at 12 months (8.3% reduction from baseline). This pattern mirrored the weight loss trajectory, with rapid decline during the active weight loss phase (0–6 months) followed by stabilization in the maintenance phase (6–12 months).

There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in terms of absolute clusterin levels at baseline (RYGB: 509.8 ± 103.2 vs. SG: 542.2 ± 116.4 µg/mL, P = 0.209), 6 months (487.9 ± 129.7 vs. 447.5 ± 129.3 µg/mL, P = 0.182), or 1 year (494.7 ± 158.0 vs. 473.5 ± 140.9 µg/mL, P = 0.541), or %Δ12clusterin (-1.3 ± 33.0% vs. -10.6 ± 24.5%, P = 0.100). However, the RYGB group had lower body weight at baseline (RYGB: 593.3 ± 12.8 vs. SG: 5107.5 ± 15.1 kg, P < 0.001), at 6 months (72.6 ± 10.3 vs. 80.5 ± 10.7 kg, P = 0.002), and at 1 year (72.3 ± 11.0 vs. 77.5 ± 10.0 kg, P = 0.036), whereas the SG group demonstrated a more pronounced reduction in both body weight (RYGB: -22.2 ± 23.2% vs. SG: -24.9 ± 25.9%, P = 0.012) and clusterin (-3.0 ± 22.9% vs. -16.1 ± 29.8%, P = 0.011) at 6 months (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Association between Clusterin Levels Decline and 1-Year Weight Loss Outcomes

Among 75 patients, 56 achieved (74.6%) sufficient weight loss (TWL: 29.0 ± 11.2%) at 1 year. At 6 months postoperatively, the sufficient weight loss group showed a significant reduction in clusterin levels (P < 0.001), whereas no change was observed in the non-substantial group (P = 0.963) (Fig. 2A). The between-group difference in Δ6clusterin was statistically significant (− 81.0 ± 128.5 vs. −1.2 ± 117.3 µg/mL, P = 0.018; Fig. 2B). At 12 months, only the sufficient weight loss group exhibited a persistent clusterin decline (P = 0.032), though the between-group difference in Δ12clusterin was non-significant (− 49.4 ± 168.1 vs. −27.1 ± 102.1 µg/mL, P = 0.588; Fig. 2A, C). Multivariable logistic regression identified %Δ6clusterin as an independent predictor of substantial weight loss [odds ratio (OR) = 0.012, 95% CI: 0.000–0.459, P = 0.018; Supplementary Table 3].

Association between Serum Clusterin Levels Decline and 1-Year T2D Remission

For T2D remission, patients in the remission group showed significant clusterin levels reductions at both 6 and 12 months compared to non-remission patients (P < 0.01 for both time points; Fig. 2D). Between-group differences in Δ6clusterin (− 93.5 ± 139.6 vs. −28.9 ± 112.3 µg/mL, P = 0.030; Fig. 2E) and Δ12clusterin (− 81.3 ± 162.4 vs. −7.1 ± 137.1 µg/mL, P = 0.036; Fig. 2F) were significant. All continuous variables were standardized to Z-scores. After standardization, logistic regression revealed that a greater reduction in early clusterin (%Δ6clusterin) was an independent predictor of T2D remission (OR = 0.268, 95% CI: 0.062–0.757, P = 0.035). This corresponds to a 3.7-fold higher odds (1/0.268) of diabetes remission for every 1-standard deviation (SD) greater decrease in early clusterin levels. In contrast, the rate of change in clusterin at 12 months (%Δ12clusterin) was not associated with remission at 12 months (OR = 0.461, 95% CI: 0.137–1.111, P = 0.141). Importantly, after further adjusting for early weight loss (%Δ6weight), %Δ6clusterin remained a significant predictor (OR = 0.270, 95% CI: 0.058–0.816, P = 0.048; Table 2), indicating that its association with metabolic improvement is independent of early weight loss. The results from analyses using raw data were consistent and are provided in Supplementary Table 4.

Mediation Analysis of Early Clusterin Levels Decline and Metabolic Outcomes

To identify potential mediators linking early-phase clusterin levels reduction (%Δ6clusterin) to 1-year metabolic outcomes, we performed mediation analysis. For sufficient weight loss, four markers showed significant mediating effects (FDR-corrected P < 0.05, Fig. 3A). %Δ6 2 h-CP emerged as the strongest mediator, accounting for 48% mediation proportion of the association. Notably, reductions in body weight (%Δ6 weight), 2-hour postprandial insulin (%Δ6 2 h-INS) and liver function (%Δ6ALT) also contributed significantly, mediating 46.4%, 34.2% and 29.1% of the effect on weight loss, respectively. For T2D remission, three variables were identified as significant mediators (Fig. 3B): %Δ6Weight (29.7% mediation proportion), %Δ6 2 h-INS (24.8%), and %Δ6 2 h-CP (31.3%). These findings indicate that early weight loss drives long-term metabolic improvement, while declining clusterin levels may enhance postprandial insulin and C-peptide responsiveness, thereby facilitating both substantial weight loss and T2D remission.

Discussion

This study evaluated the dynamics of clusterin levels after MBS and its association with weight loss and T2D remission in patients with obesity and T2DM. The key findings reveal a biphasic decline in clusterin levels, with a rapid reduction within the first 6 months postoperatively followed by stabilization at 12 months. Early-phase clusterin levels decline (0–6 months) was independently associated with both 1-year sufficient weight loss and T2D remission, highlighting its potential as a biomarker for assessing metabolic improvement.

Changes in hormonal levels represent a key pathophysiological factor influencing weight loss after metabolic surgery. Existing evidence indicates that postoperative weight loss is associated with reductions in adipokines like leptin and elevations in hormones such as peptide YY and adiponectin. In our study, the observed clusterin levels decline mirrored the trajectory of weight loss, with the steepest reduction during the active weight loss phase. This pattern aligns with prior studies linking clusterin to adipose tissue dysfunction: elevated clusterin levels in obesity are thought to originate from hypertrophic adipocytes under chronic stress [26, 27]. For instance, Bradley et al. [12] demonstrated that serum clusterin levels correlates positively with BMI and insulin resistance in obese individuals, while MBS-induced weight loss has been associated with clusterin levels reductions at 6–12 months [17–19]. Notably, our study extended these findings by showing that early clusterin levels decline (at 6 months) predicts 1 year weight loss. The trend of clusterin levels was similar to the changes in patients’ body weight. This may indicate that adipose tissue is a major source of circulating clusterin, and changes in clusterin levels after surgery may be a concomitant phenomenon of changes in body weight [12]. Therefore, there may be an interplay between clusterin levels and body weight that are causally related to each other.

The independent association between early clusterin levels decline and T2D remission is particularly striking, as this link persisted after accounting for early weight loss. This implies that clusterin influences metabolic outcomes beyond mere weight reduction [13]. Mechanistically, clusterin may act on multiple pathways critical for glucose homeostasis. First, clusterin directly impairs insulin signaling by inhibiting phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) in muscle and liver cells [28, 29]. Second, building on our prior work demonstrating that clusterin levels independently predicted adipose tissue insulin resistance, clusterin likely exacerbates adipocyte dysfunction [11]. And our mediation analysis showed that 2-hour postprandial insulin and C-peptide improvements mediated 24.8–48% of the clusterin-T2D remission association, consistent with clusterin’s role in β-cell function and peripheral insulin sensitivity [30–33]. Additionally, clusterin promotes NF-κB-driven inflammation in macrophages [29], a key driver of metabolic dysfunction, and MBS-induced clusterin reduction may disrupt this pro-inflammatory cycle. Elevated clusterin levels also correlate with hepatic steatosis [34] and lipid contents in the liver [35]. In our study, %Δ6ALT mediated 29.1% of the clusterin-weight loss association, suggesting that clusterin levels reduction has hepatoprotective effects. These findings converge with a recent Korean study showing that clusterin reduction correlates with improved insulin sensitivity [28] and a 5-year prospective study linking baseline clusterin levels to T2D incidence [30], further supporting the potential involvement of clusterin in metabolic disease progression.

Our preliminary findings suggest that early postoperative clusterin reduction is associated with metabolic improvement, indicating its potential value as a biomarker for assessing clinical outcomes. This aligns with the evolving paradigm that redefines obesity as a chronic disease state rooted in adipose tissue dysfunction [36]. Our data, showing that clusterin reduction is independently associated with remission beyond weight loss, suggest that part of the benefit of metabolic surgery may be related to improvements in adipose tissue health. Dynamic monitoring of clusterin levels during the first 6 months postoperatively could identify patients at risk of suboptimal metabolic response, enabling personalized interventions to enhance weight loss or insulin sensitivity. In conclusion, MBS was associated with significant reductions in clusterin levels at 1-year follow-up, which were independently associated with weight loss and T2D remission. These effects were partially mediated by improvements in insulin resistance and β-cell function, suggesting a potential link between clusterin and these metabolic improvements. Future research should focus on prospective validation of clusterin as a clinical biomarker and explore its utility in guiding precision medicine approaches for obesity and T2D.

While these results provide novel insights, several limitations warrant consideration. First, it is a retrospective study, and further prospective studies are necessary to determine whether clusterin levels can predict diabetes remission. Second, the 1-year follow-up may not capture long-term clusterin levels dynamics, and future studies should assess whether early clusterin decline predicts sustained remission beyond 5 years. Third, the modest cohort size (n = 75) limits generalizability, particularly for subgroup analyses by surgery type, and larger multicenter studies are therefore warranted to both confirm these observed associations and further explore whether the pattern of postoperative clusterin level changes is modulated by different MBS approaches. Fourth, this study focused on clusterin and did not concurrently measure well-established biomarkers such as leptin and ghrelin, which restricts our ability to directly compare the patterns and predictive value of clusterin changes with those of these factors. Additionally, while mediation analysis implicated body weight and insulin parameters, the specific cell types and signaling pathways involved require validation in preclinical models.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study suggests that MBS induced a biphasic decline in clusterin levels in patients with obesity and T2D. Early reduction independently associated with 1-year weight loss and T2D remission, partially mediated by improvements in insulin resistance. Early clusterin decline may represent a potential marker for post-surgical metabolic outcomes, and these preliminary findings warrant validation in larger cohorts.

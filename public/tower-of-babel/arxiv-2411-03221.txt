1
Introduction

A generalized Baumslag-Solitar group (GBS group) is a group that acts cocompactly without inversion on an oriented tree with vertex and edge stabilizers isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. By Bass-Serre theory, these groups are isomorphic to an iteration of HNN-extensions and amalgamated free products of infinite cyclic groups. Baumslag-Solitar groups
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
are a particular case of such groups. In this article, we are interested in the space of subgroups of those groups from a topological point of view.

The set of subgroups of any infinite countable group
\Gamma
is a closed subset of a Cantor space, hence is Polish. Cantor-Bendixson theory leads to a decomposition of the space of subgroup
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
into a closed subspace without isolated points called the
perfect kernel
and denoted by
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
and a countable set. We are interested in the computation of the perfect kernel and the dynamics induced by the action by conjugation of
\Gamma
on it.

In the case of finitely generated groups, finite index subgroups are isolated. Hence, one has the following inclusion:

\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma)

where
\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma)
denotes the set of subgroups of
\Gamma
that have infinite index.
Hence understanding when the previous inclusion is an equality is a natural question.
In
[
CGLM23
]
, the authors observed that equality holds in the case of non-abelian free groups.
The authors of
[
AG25
]
proved that infinite ended groups also satisfy this equality.
In the case of non-solvable Baumslag-Solitar groups
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
, equality holds if and only if
|m|\neq|n|
(
cf.

[
CGLMS25
]
). More generally, a subgroup of
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
lies in the perfect kernel if and only if its graph of groups decomposition given by its action on the Bass-Serre tree
\mathcal{A}
induced by the standard presentation

\mathrm{BS}(m,n)\simeq\left\langle b,t|tb^{n}t^{-1}=b^{m}\right\rangle

(1.1)

is infinite.

The action of
\Gamma
on itself by conjugation leads to an action of
\Gamma
on
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
by homeomorphisms, which preserves the perfect kernel. The dynamics induced on the perfect kernel has been studied in various cases. For instance, the authors of
[
AG25
]
proved that the action by conjugation of the free group
\mathbb{F}_{k}
on
k
generators on its perfect kernel is highly topologically transitive for every
k\geq 2
. Recall that an action
\alpha:\Gamma\curvearrowright X
on a Polish space is said
highly topologically transitive
if it is
r
-topologically transitive for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
i.e.
for every non-empty open subsets
U_{1},...,U_{r},V_{1},...,V_{r}
of
X
, there exists an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\alpha(\gamma)U_{i}\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset
for every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
. A
1
-topologically transitive action is said
topologically transitive
. By Baire’s theorem, this is equivalent to having a dense orbit.

In
[
CGLMS25
]
, the authors obtained a decomposition of the perfect kernel of non-amenable Baumslag-Solitar groups into countably many pieces on which the action by conjugation is topologically transitive. In
[
GLMS24
]
the same authors announced that the action on each piece of the decomposition is highly topologically transitive. One of the pieces is closed and all the other ones are open, and also closed if and only if the group is
unimodular
. Recall that one characterization of unimodularity for a GBS group is the existence of an infinite cyclic normal subgroup (see
[
Lev07
, Section 2]
for instance). This decomposition is encoded by the
phenotype
of the subgroups of
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
, an arithmetic quantity in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
that is invariant under conjugation and which only depends on the intersection with the subgroup generated by an elliptic element (such as
b
in the presentation (
1.1
)).

In this article, our goal is to extend these results to generalized Baumslag-Solitar group. We are interested in the non-amenable GBS groups,
i.e.
which are not isomorphic to
\mathrm{BS}(1,n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We prove the following result (
cf.
Theorem
6.4
):

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-amenable GBS group. Then

•

If
\Gamma
is non-unimodular, then
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma)
.

•

If
\Gamma
is unimodular, then denoting by
C
an infinite cyclic normal subgroup and
\pi:\Gamma\to\Gamma/C
the canonical projection, one has
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\pi^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma/C)\right)
, i.e. the set of subgroups of
\Gamma
whose projection under
\pi
has infinite index in
\Gamma/C
.

Notice that any two infinite cyclic normal subgroups of a non-amenable unimodular GBS group
\Gamma
(given by its action on its Bass-Serre tree
\mathcal{A}
) are commensurable (that is to say, share a common finite index normal subgroup): if
g\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
generates an infinite cyclic normal subgroup of
\Gamma
and
g
is not elliptic, then
g
is hyperbolic and by normality its axis is fixed by any element of
\Gamma
. Thus Bass-Serre theory tells us that
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{BS}(1,\varepsilon)
(for some
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}
), hence is amenable. Hence any infinite cyclic normal subgroup of a non-amenable GBS group is a non-trivial subgroup of a vertex stabilizer, so is commensurable to any vertex stabilizer. Hence the computation of the perfect kernel in this second case does not depend on the choice of the subgroup
C
.

In Theorem
6.6
, we provide a study of the dynamics induced by the conjugation action on the perfect kernel
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-amenable GBS group. There exists an infinite countable subset
\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathbb{N}^{*}
and a partition

\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{K}_{\infty}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathcal{K}_{P}

such that, for every
P\in\mathcal{Q}\sqcup\{\infty\}
:

•

\mathcal{K}_{P}
is non-empty and invariant under conjugation;

•

the conjugation action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathcal{K}_{P}
is highly topologically transitive.

One also investigates the topology of the pieces in the decomposition of the space of subgroups (
cf.
Proposition
6.5
):

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-amenable GBS group. In the decomposition provided by Theorem
2
, for the topology induced on
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
:

•

the piece
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
is closed;

•

for every
P\in\mathcal{Q}
, the piece
\mathcal{K}_{P}
is open. It is also closed if and only if the group
\Gamma
is unimodular.

To build this decomposition, we introduce a generalized notion of phenotype
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},v}
of a GBS group
\Gamma
defined by a graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
pointed at some vertex
v
. This is a function from the space of subgroups of
\Gamma
to
\mathbb{N}^{*}\sqcup\{\infty\}
whose image is infinite. With the notations of Theorem
2
, one has
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},v}:=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}\left(\mathbb{N}\right)
and, for any
P\in\mathcal{Q}\sqcup\{\infty\}
, the piece
\mathcal{K}_{P}
is exactly
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},v}^{-1}(P)
(and these pieces are non-empty).

It turns out that the piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},v}^{-1}(\infty)
depends neither on the choice of
\mathcal{H}
nor of
v
. It consists of the set of subgroups that act freely on the Bass-Serre tree of
\Gamma
associated to
\mathcal{H}
. Equivalently, this is the set of subgroups that intersect the subgroup generated by any elliptic element trivially. This set of elliptic elements is intrinsically characterized as the set of elements that are commensurable to all their conjugates (see Proposition
2.5
). In particular, the decomposition
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\bigsqcup_{N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},v}\sqcup\{\infty\}}\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},v}^{-1}(N)
depends neither on the choice of the graph
\mathcal{H}
nor on the choice of the vertex
v
(see Proposition
6.12
). The open pieces of this decomposition are uniquely characterized as follows: they are the maximal topologically transitive open subsets for the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\smallsetminus\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
.

To obtain these results, we generalize the notion of preactions and
(m,n)
-graphs defined for Baumslag-Solitar groups in
[
CGLMS25
]
. We introduce the notion of
\mathcal{H}
-
preaction
(
cf.
Definition
3.1
) to establish a link between the Schreier graph of a subgroup
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
and its graph of groups
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
. This notion was already defined in
[
FLMMS22
]
in the case of a single HNN-extension and in the case of an amalgamated free product (
i.e.
in the case of a graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
which consists in a single edge). Our notion of preaction is a little more tricky because it depends on the choice of a spanning tree of
\mathcal{H}
.

2
Preliminaries and notations

We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of prime numbers in
\mathbb{Z}
. For every integer
N
, we denote by
|N|_{p}
its
p
-adic valuation, that is, the largest
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p^{n}
divides
N
. By convention,
|\infty|_{p}=\infty
for every prime number. For every integers
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
we denote by
m\wedge n
their greatest common divisor, that is, the largest integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
dividing both
m
and
n
. By convention,
\infty\wedge n=n\wedge\infty=|n|
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
. By “countable” we mean finite or in bijection with
\mathbb{N}
.

2.1.1

We follow the notations of

.

Definition 2.1
.

A

•

A set of

;

•

A set of

;

•

A map

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{G}\right)&\to&\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{G}\right)\\

\end{array}

•

The elements of

;

•

The elements of
\mathcal{E}^{-}(\mathcal{G})

.

\mathbf{t}(e)=v
. We say that

if

. A

is a vertex

which is incident to exactly two edges (
e
and
\overline{e}
for some

).

An

. It is said to be

. It is

if
\mathbf{s}(e_{1})=s
. It
connects

s
to

if
\mathbf{s}(e_{1})=s
and
\mathbf{t}(e_{r})=t
. If
\mathbf{t}(e_{r})=\mathbf{s}(e_{1})

). A

is a cycle of length

. An edge path

. If

and

.

The set of vertices of a graph
\mathcal{G}
it equipped with a distance

\mathcal{G}

,

and the source and target maps and the fixed-point free involution of

. The

2.1.2
Schreier graphs

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group. One has a correspondence between

•

subgroups of
\Gamma
and isomorphism classes of pointed transitive right actions
(X,x_{0})\curvearrowleft\Gamma
;

•

conjugacy classes of subgroups of
\Gamma
and isomorphism classes of transitive right actions
X\curvearrowleft\Gamma
.

It is given by the bijection
f:\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)&\to&\{\text{pointed transitive right actions of $\Gamma$}\}\\
\Lambda&\mapsto&(\Lambda\backslash\Gamma,\Lambda)\curvearrowleft\Gamma\\
\end{array}

whose inverse is given by
g:\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\{\text{pointed transitive right actions of $\Gamma$}\}&\to&\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)\\
(X,x_{0})\curvearrowleft\Gamma&\mapsto&\mathrm{Stab}(x_{0})\\
\end{array}
.
Changing the base point of a transitive
\Gamma
-action
\alpha
on a countable set
(X,x_{0})
amounts to conjugating the subgroup represented by
\alpha
and
x_{0}
.

Given any generating set
S
of
\Gamma
, one can associate a graph
\mathrm{Sch}(\alpha)
called
Schreier graph
to any transitive right action
X\curvearrowleft^{\alpha}\Gamma
(hence to any subgroup of
\Gamma
by the above correspondence): its vertex set is
X
and for any
x\in X
, for any
s\in S
, define a positive edge labeled
s
from
x
to
x\alpha(s)
. It is pointed at
x_{0}
if
\alpha
is pointed at
x_{0}
. Two Schreier graphs
\mathrm{Sch}(\alpha,v)
and
\mathrm{Sch}(\beta,v^{\prime})
are isomorphic (as labeled graphs) if and only if the subgroups represented by
\alpha
and
\beta
(with respect to the points
v
and
v^{\prime}
) are conjugated. For any
R>0
, we denote
\mathrm{Sch}(\alpha,v)\simeq_{R}\mathrm{Sch}(\beta,v^{\prime})
if the
R
-balls around
v
and
v^{\prime}
of the Schreier graphs of
\alpha
and
\beta
are isomorphic as labeled graphs.

Remark 2.2
.

One could also have considered left actions instead of right actions. The reason why we deal with right actions is that the left action of
\Gamma
on a right Cayley graph induces an isometry of the graph.

2.2
Elements of Bass-Serre theory

In this section, we recall the fundamentals of Bass-Serre theory. We refer to
[
Ser77
]
for more details.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an oriented graph equipped with a collection of
vertex groups

G_{v}
for
v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{G}\right)
, a collection of
edge groups

G_{e}
for
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
such that
G_{e}=G_{\overline{e}}
for every edge
e
and, for every
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
and
\mathbf{u}\in\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t}\}
, injective homomorphisms
\iota_{e,\mathbf{u}}:G_{e}\hookrightarrow G_{\mathbf{u}(e)}
such that
\iota_{e,\mathbf{s}}=\iota_{\overline{e},\mathbf{t}}
. This data is called a
graph of groups
.
We associate a group to
\mathcal{H}
called the
fundamental group
of
\mathcal{H}
, defined by the following procedure: fix a spanning tree
T
in
\mathcal{H}
. Denote by
\{t_{e},e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\}
a generating set of the free group
\mathbb{F}_{|\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)|}
of rank
|\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)|
and define

\displaystyle\Gamma=\left(*_{v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)}G_{v}*\mathbb{F}_{|\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)|}\right)/\left\llangle\left(t_{e}^{-1}\iota_{e,\mathbf{s}}(g)t_{e}\iota_{e,\mathbf{t}}(g)^{-1}\right)_{(e,g)\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\times G_{e}},(t_{e}t_{\overline{e}})_{e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)},(t_{e})_{e\in\mathcal{E}\left(T\right)}\right\rrangle

Then, there exists a unique oriented tree
\mathcal{A}
, called the
Bass-Serre tree
of the graph of groups on which
\Gamma
acts without inversion with quotient
\mathcal{H}
and such that there exist sections
\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\to\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
and
\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\to\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
(which we denote by
v\mapsto\sigma{v}
and
e\mapsto\sigma{e}
, respectively) of the projection
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{H}
that satisfies the following conditions:

•

\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma{v})=G_{v}\ \forall v\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})

•

\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma{e})=G_{e}\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})

Moreover, the isomorphism class of
\Gamma
does not depend on the choice of the spanning tree
T
in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{A}
is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Conversely, any group
\Gamma
acting on an oriented tree
\mathcal{A}
is the fundamental group of the graph of groups whose underlying graph is the quotient
\mathcal{H}=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{A}
and whose vertex and edge groups are defined as follows: let
T
be a spanning tree of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\sigma:T\to\mathcal{A}
be a lifting of
T
, which we extend to a section
\sigma:\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\to\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
satisfying
\sigma(\overline{e})=\overline{\sigma(e)}
and
\mathbf{t}\left(\sigma(e)\right)=\sigma\left(\mathbf{t}(e)\right)
for every
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. Then define the vertex and edge groups by:

•

G_{v}=\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma(v))
for every
v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
;

•

G_{e}=\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma(e))
for every
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

and the injections
\iota_{e,\mathbf{t}}:G_{e}\hookrightarrow G_{\mathbf{t}(e)}
as in
[
Ser77
, Chapter 1, Section 5.4]
.

Let
\Gamma
be the fundamental group of a graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
and let
\mathcal{A}
be its Bass-Serre tree. Any subgroup
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
acts without inversion on the Bass-Serre tree
\mathcal{A}
of
\Gamma
. Hence
\Lambda
is the fundamental group of the graph of groups with underlying graph
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
and vertex and edge groups defined as previously (hence any vertex (
resp.
edge) group is an intersection of
\Lambda
with some conjugate of a vertex (
resp.
edge) group of
\mathcal{H}
).

2.3
Generalized Baumslag-Solitar groups

A GBS group is the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with vertex and edge stabilizers infinite cyclic. Equivalently, these groups are obtained by an iteration of HNN-extensions and amalgamated free products of
\mathbb{Z}
over
\mathbb{Z}
starting from
\mathbb{Z}
.

As injective morphisms
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
are fully determined by the image in
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
of
1
, Bass-Serre theory tells us that a GBS group
\Gamma
can be represented by an oriented graph
\mathcal{H}
endowed with a function which associates an integer to each half-edge:

k:\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\times\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t}\}&\to&\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\\
(e,\mathbf{u})&\mapsto&k_{e,\mathbf{u}}\\
\end{array}

such that for every edge
e
, one has
k_{e,\mathbf{s}}=k_{\overline{e},\mathbf{t}}
.
From now, we fix a spanning tree
T
of
\mathcal{H}
. As a consequence of Bass-Serre theory, the group
\Gamma
is defined by the following presentation:

\displaystyle\Gamma\simeq\Bigg\langle(a_{v})_{v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)},(t_{e})_{e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)}

\displaystyle\mid\left(t_{e}^{-1}a_{\mathbf{s}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{s}}}t_{e}a_{\mathbf{t}(e)}^{-k_{e,\mathbf{t}}}\text{\ and \ }t_{e}t_{\overline{e}}\ \text{ \ for all \ }e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\right)

\text{ \ and \ }\left(t_{e}\text{ \ for all \ }e\in\mathcal{E}(T)\right)\Bigg\rangle.

(2.3)

Let us give an overview of the construction of the Bass-Serre tree for GBS groups. The Bass-Serre tree can be thought of as a “blown up and shrunk Cayley graph”: its vertices are the cosets of vertex groups, and its edges are the cosets of edge groups. More precisely, let
Y
be the graph defined by

•

its vertex set

\mathcal{V}(Y)=\Gamma\times\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right);

•

its positive edge set

\mathcal{E}^{+}(Y)=\Gamma\times\{a_{v},e|v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right),e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\};

•

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
:

–

\mathbf{s}(\gamma,a_{v})=(\gamma,v)
;

–

\mathbf{t}(\gamma,a_{v})=(\gamma a_{v},v)
;

•

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(T\right)
:

–

\mathbf{s}(\gamma,e)=(\gamma,\mathbf{s}(e))
;

–

\mathbf{t}(\gamma,e)=(\gamma,\mathbf{t}(e))
;

•

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(T\right)
:

–

\mathbf{s}(\gamma,e)=(\gamma,\mathbf{s}(e))
;

–

\mathbf{t}(\gamma,e)=(\gamma t_{e},\mathbf{t}(e))
.

The graph
Y
is obtained by “blowing up” the Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with respect to the generating set
S=\{a_{v},v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right),t_{e},e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}(T)\}
: shrinking all the edges of
Y
with source
(\gamma,\mathbf{s}(e))
and target
(\gamma,\mathbf{t}(e))
(for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(T\right)
) leads to the Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with respect to
S
. The group
\Gamma
acts on the graph
Y
by the (left) action:
\gamma_{1}\cdot(\gamma_{2},v)=(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},v)
, and the group
\mathbb{Z}
acts on the set of vertices of
Y
by the right action: for any
(\gamma,v)
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
(\gamma,v)\cdot n=(\gamma a_{v}^{n},v)
. The graph
Y/\mathbb{Z}
is precisely the Bass-Serre tree of
\Gamma
. The fact that this is a tree results from the normal form theorem (
cf.

[
Ser77
, Section 5.2, Corollary 3 of Theorem 11]
). In particular, the quotient
\Gamma\backslash(Y/\mathbb{Z})
is (up to labelling the vertices and edges) the graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
.

The elements
a_{v}
are called
vertex generators
and the elements
t_{e}
are called
edge generators
. Let
c=e_{1},...,e_{r}
be an edge path of
\mathcal{H}
. A
word
of
type

c
is a couple

\left(c,\mu\right)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r}),\left(a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}},a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{2})}^{k_{2}},...,a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{r})}^{k_{r}},a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}^{k_{r+1}}\right)\right).

A
prefix
of
(c,\mu)
is a word

\left((e_{1},...,e_{i}),\left(a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}},a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{2})}^{k_{2}},...,a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{i})}^{k_{i}},a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{i})}^{k_{i+1}}\right)\right)

for some
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
.

The
length
of
(c,\mu)
is the integer
r
. The associated element of
\Gamma
is

\left|c,\mu\right|=a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}}s_{1}a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{2})}^{k_{2}}\ldots a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{r})}^{k_{r}}s_{r}a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}^{k_{r+1}}

(where
s_{i}=t_{e_{i}}
if
e_{i}\notin T
and
s_{i}=1
otherwise).
The word
(c,\mu)
is said to be
reduced
if

•

either
r=0
(
i.e.
the edge path is empty) and
k_{1}\neq 0
;

•

or for every
i\in\llbracket 1,r-1\rrbracket
such that
\overline{e_{i+1}}=e_{i}
, the integer
k_{i+1}
is not divisible by
k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}
.

Recall (
cf.

[
Ser77
, Chapter 1, Section 5.2]
) that a reduced word leads to a non-trivial element of
\Gamma
. If
(c,\mu)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r}),(a_{0},...,a_{r})\right)
and
(d,\lambda)=\left((f_{1},...,f_{s}),(b_{0},...,b_{s})\right)
are two words satisfying
\mathbf{t}(e_{r})=\mathbf{s}(f_{1})
, one defines their
concatenation
by

(c,\mu)*(d,\nu)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r},f_{1},...,f_{s}),(a_{0},...,a_{r-1},a_{r}b_{0},b_{1},...,b_{s})\right).

As explained in
[
Lev15
, Section 2]
, any GBS group can be represented by a
reduced
graph of groups (which is not necessarily unique), that is to say, we will assume that the only edges
e
such that
k_{e,\mathbf{s}}=\pm 1
or
k_{e,\mathbf{t}}=\pm 1
are loops.

Notice that a reduced graph of groups that represents a non-amenable GBS group
\Gamma
is neither a loop one of whose labels is equal to
\pm 1
nor a segment with both labels equal to
\pm 2
: in this last case, a presentation of
\Gamma
is given by
\Gamma\simeq\langle a,b\mid a^{2}=b^{2}\rangle
. Hence the group
\Gamma
is isomorphic to the fundamental group of the Klein Bottle
\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)\simeq\langle\tau,\beta\mid\tau\beta\tau^{-1}=\beta^{-1}\rangle

via
the following isomorphism:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Gamma&\to&\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)\\
a&\mapsto&\tau\\
b&\mapsto&\beta\tau\end{array}

whose inverse is given by

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathrm{BS}(1,-1)&\to&\Gamma\\
\tau&\mapsto&a\\
\beta&\mapsto&ba^{-1}\end{array}.

Conversely, any GBS group defined by a reduced graph of groups which is neither a loop one of whose labels is equal to
\pm 1
nor a segment with both labels equal to
\pm 2
contains a free group, thus is non-amenable.

Let us fix a reduced graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
defining
\Gamma
and let
\mathcal{A}
be the associated Bass-Serre tree. An
elliptic
element is an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
that fixes a vertex in
\mathcal{A}
. It is a power of a conjugate of a vertex generator
a_{v}
for some
v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. If
\gamma\in\Gamma
is not elliptic,
\gamma
is said
hyperbolic
. For a non-amenable GBS group
\Gamma
, the elliptic elements can be defined intrinsically:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-amenable GBS group and let
g\in\Gamma
. The following are equivalent:

1.

g
is elliptic (for the action of
\Gamma
on its Bass-Serre tree);

2.

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the elements
g
and
\gamma g\gamma^{-1}
are commensurable.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
remains valid without assuming that
\Gamma
is amenable. To prove this implication, we show that any two non-trivial elliptic elements are commensurable. As any two non-trivial subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}
are commensurable, an induction on the length
r
of an edge path
E_{1},...,E_{r}
of
\mathcal{A}
shows that

\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{Stab}(E_{i})\neq\{1\}\text{ \ for every edge path $E_{1},...,E_{r}$ in $\mathcal{A}$.}

In particular, for any vertices
V,W\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{A})
, denoting by
E_{1},...,E_{r}
the unique reduced edge path connecting
V
to
W
in
\mathcal{A}
, the group
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{Stab}(E_{i})
is a non-trivial subgroup of both
\mathrm{Stab}(V)
and
\mathrm{Stab}(W)
, so has finite index in both
\mathrm{Stab}(V)
and
\mathrm{Stab}(W)
. This proves the first implication.

We argue by contradiction to prove
(2)\implies(1)
. If
g\in\Gamma
is hyperbolic and commensurable to its conjugates, then
g
and
\gamma g\gamma^{-1}
share the same axis
L
for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, so
\Gamma
stabilizes
L
. The quotient graph of groups
\Gamma\backslash L
being reduced, the action of
\Gamma
on
L
has either one or two orbits of vertices. Thus, Bass-Serre theory tells us that
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a graph of groups defined either by a loop labeled
(1,\pm 1)
if the action of
\Gamma
on
L
has a single orbit of vertices or by a segment labeled
(2,\pm 2)
otherwise. Hence
\Gamma
is
isomorphic to
\mathrm{BS}(1,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}
, so is amenable.
∎

For any GBS group
\Gamma
, one can define the
modular homomorphism

\Delta_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Q}^{*}
as follows: fix a non-trivial elliptic element
a\in\Gamma
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there exist non-zero integers
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gamma a^{m}\gamma^{-1}=a^{n}
. One defines
\Delta_{\Gamma}(\gamma)=\frac{m}{n}
; we easily check that it is well-defined and that it does not depend on the choice of the elliptic element
a
. The group
\Gamma
is
unimodular
if
\mathrm{Im}(\Delta_{\Gamma})\subseteq\{1,-1\}
. This condition can be rephrased in terms of the labels of the graph
\mathcal{H}
: the group
\Gamma
is unimodular if and only if for every cycle
c
, one has
|\prod_{e\in c}k_{e,\mathbf{s}}|=|\prod_{e\in c}k_{e,\mathbf{t}}|
(in particular, any GBS group which is defined by a tree of groups is unimodular). One has the following characterization of unimodularity (
cf.

[
Lev15
, Section 2]
): a GBS group
\Gamma
is unimodular if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

•

\mathrm{Im}(\Delta_{\Gamma})\subseteq\{1,-1\}
;

•

\Gamma
contains an infinite cyclic normal subgroup;

•

\Gamma
is virtually isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}
for some
n\geq 1
.

2.4
Space of subgroups and perfect kernel

Given a countable group
\Gamma
, we denote by
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
its space of subgroups. By using indicator functions, one can embed
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
in
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
. Equipped with the product topology,
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
is a totally disconnected compact metrizable space of which
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
is a closed subspace. An explicit basis of open sets is given by the following clopen sets:

\mathcal{V}(O,I)=\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma),\Lambda\cap O=\emptyset\text{ \ and \ }I\subseteq\Lambda\}

where
O
and
I
are finite subsets of
\Gamma
. In other words, a sequence of subgroups
\Lambda_{n}
converges to a subgroup
\Lambda
if and only if

•

for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
.

•

for every
\lambda\notin\Lambda
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
\lambda\notin\Lambda_{n}
.

In the formalism of Schreier graphs, given a finite generating set
S
of
\Gamma
, a basis of neighborhoods of a pointed transitive right action
[\alpha,v]
:
(X,v)\curvearrowleft\Gamma
is given by

V_{R}=\{[\beta,v^{\prime}],\mathrm{Sch}(\beta,v^{\prime})\simeq_{R}\mathrm{Sch}(\alpha,v)\}.

Applying Cantor-Bendixson theorem (see
[
Kec95
, Chapter 1, Section 6]
for instance) to the Polish space
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
leads to a unique decomposition
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)=K\sqcup D
where
D
is countable and
K
is a closed subspace of
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
without isolated points. The set
K
is called the
perfect kernel
of
\Gamma
and denoted by
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
. The perfect kernel of
\Gamma
is the largest closed subset of
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
without isolated points. It is also the set of subgroups all of whose neighborhoods are uncountable.

From now we fix a GBS group
\Gamma
defined by a reduced graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
and a spanning tree
T
in
\mathcal{H}
. For every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
, we denote by
k_{e,\mathbf{s}}
(
resp.

k_{e,\mathbf{t}}
) the label of the half-edge of
e
containing
\mathbf{s}(e)
(
resp.

\mathbf{t}(e)
).
A path
e_{1},...,e_{r}
in
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
labeled

(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{r},l_{r})
if
k_{e_{i},\mathbf{s}}=k_{i}
and
k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}=l_{i}
for any
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
. For each vertex
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
, choose a generator
a_{s}
of the vertex group
G_{s}
and for every
e\notin T
denote by
t_{e}
the associated edge generator in the presentation (
2.3
) of
\Gamma
defined by
T
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}
the associated Bass-Serre tree,
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{H}=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{A}
the projection and a lifting of
T
which we extend to a section
\sigma:\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\to\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
satisfying

•

\sigma(\overline{e})=\overline{\sigma(e)}
for every
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
;

•

\mathbf{t}\left(\sigma(e)\right)=\sigma\left(\mathbf{t}(e)\right)
for every
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
;

•

\mathrm{Stab}\left(\sigma(v)\right)=\langle a_{v}\rangle
for every
v\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
;

•

\mathrm{Stab}\left(\sigma(e)\right)=\left\langle a_{\mathbf{t}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{t}}}\right\rangle
for every
e\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

3
From an
\mathcal{H}
-preaction to an
\mathcal{H}
-graph

The goal of this section is to introduce some tools to approximate a
\Gamma
-action by perturbing a graph associated to it far from the base point. To achieve this, we introduce the notion of
\mathcal{H}
-preaction
, that takes the defining relations of
\Gamma
into account. To any
\mathcal{H}
-preaction will be associated an
\mathcal{H}
-graph
(that is related to its Schreier graph). It is a labeled graph whose labels and degrees satisfy some arithmetical conditions. The inspiration comes from the notion of core graphs in the case of free groups: to any subgroup
H
of
\mathbb{F}_{r}
is associated a pointed graph endowed with an immersion to the bouquet of
r
circles. Perturbing this graph very far from the origin amounts to building a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{r}
that is very close from
H
. The new notions we introduce are generalizations of the notions of preactions and
(m,n)
-graphs introduced in
[
CGLMS25
]
. The authors of
[
FLMMS22
]
also introduced a similar notion of Bass-Serre graphs and of preactions in the case of HNN extensions and amalgamated free products. Our definition of an
\mathcal{H}
-preaction extends to any graph of GBS group
\mathcal{H}
and depends on the choice of the spanning tree
T
of
\mathcal{H}
.

Before entering into technical details, let us motivate the notion of
\mathcal{H}
-preactions. If one wants to perturb the Schreier graph of an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-action (with respect to a basis
\{a,b\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
), one has a lot of freedom: we only have to ensure that each vertex has exactly one incoming edge and one outgoing edge labeled
a
(
resp.

b
). When the group we start with is not free, nothing similar can be done in general. In the case of the fundamental group of a graph of groups, we can adapt the naive strategy that worked for free groups, but the situation is significantly more subtle. For instance, in the case of Baumslag-Solitar groups, the defining relation forces the cardinals of the
\langle b\rangle
-orbits of any Schreier graph to satisfy some arithmetical relations: for example, one
\langle b\rangle
-orbit is infinite if and only if all
\langle b\rangle
-orbits are. The idea of introducing the notion of preaction is to get rid of this difficulty by imposing that the non-saturated actions we work with have saturated
\langle b\rangle
-orbits - and the defining relation imposes that, whenever
t
(
resp.

t^{-1}
) is defined on some point
x
, then
t
has to be defined on
x\cdot b^{n}
(
resp.

x\cdot b^{m}
). In the case of an arbitrary graph of GBS group, this means that all the orbits of the vertex groups are saturated, and that all the relations of the group are “memorized” by the preaction. Let us make this definition more precise:

Definition 3.1
.

An
\mathcal{H}
-preaction
of
\Gamma
on a set
X
is a set of partially defined bijections
\alpha_{s}:\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{s})\to\mathrm{rng}(\alpha_{s})
of
X
(for each
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
) and
\tau_{e}:\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e})\to\mathrm{rng}(\tau_{e})
of
X
(for each edge
e\notin T
) satisfying the following conditions:

1.

For every vertex
s
of
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{s})=\mathrm{rng}(\alpha_{s})
;

2.

For every edge
e
of
T
, for every
x\in\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha_{\mathbf{s}(e)}\right)\cap\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha_{\mathbf{t}(e)}\right)
,

x\alpha_{\mathbf{s}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{s}}}=x\alpha_{\mathbf{t}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{t}}};

3.

For every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}\left(T\right)

•

\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e})
is
\alpha_{\mathbf{s}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{s}}}
-invariant;

•

\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e})=\mathrm{rng}(\tau_{e}^{-1})
and
\tau_{e}=\tau_{\overline{e}}^{-1}

and for every
x\in\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e})

x\alpha_{\mathbf{s}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{s}}}\tau_{e}=x\tau_{e}\alpha_{\mathbf{t}(e)}^{k_{e,\mathbf{t}}};

4.

For every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}\left(T\right)
, one has

\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e})\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{\mathbf{s}(e)});

5.

For every vertex
v,w\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
, denoting by
e_{1},...,e_{n}
the unique edge path in
T
connecting
v
to
w
, one has, for every
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
and
\mathbf{u}\in\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t}\}
:

\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{v})\cap\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{w})\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{{\mathbf{u}}(e_{i})}).

Let
\alpha
be an
\mathcal{H}
-preaction on a countable set
X
defined by a set of partial bijections
\left\{\alpha_{s},\tau_{e}|s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right),e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}(T)\right\}
. An
\mathcal{H}
-preaction
\alpha^{\prime}
on a countable set
X^{\prime}
defined by partial bijections
\left\{\alpha^{\prime}_{s},\tau^{\prime}_{e}|s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right),e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}(T)\right\}

extends
the
\mathcal{H}
-preaction
\alpha
if there exists an injection
\iota:X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
such that

•

for every vertex
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
,
\iota(\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{s}))\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\alpha^{\prime}_{s})
, and on
\mathrm{dom}(\alpha_{s})
one has
\alpha^{\prime}_{s}\circ\iota=\iota\circ\alpha_{s}
;

•

for every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}(T)
,
\iota(\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e}))\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\tau^{\prime}_{e})
, and on
\mathrm{dom}(\tau_{e})
one has
\tau^{\prime}_{e}\circ\iota=\iota\circ\tau_{e}
.

In that case we say that
\alpha
is a
sub
\mathcal{H}
-preaction
of
\alpha^{\prime}
.
Given two
\mathcal{H}
-preactions
\alpha^{(i)}=\left\{\alpha^{(i)}_{s},\tau^{(i)}_{e}|s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right),e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}(T)\right\}
(for
i=1,2
) on countable sets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, respectively, one can define the
disjoint union

\alpha^{(1)}\sqcup\alpha^{(2)}=\left\{\alpha_{s},\tau_{e}|s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right),e\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\setminus\mathcal{E}(T)\right\}

as an
\mathcal{H}
-preaction on
X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}
by

•

\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha_{s}\right)=\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha^{(1)}_{s}\right)\sqcup\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha^{(2)}_{s}\right)
with
\alpha_{s}\Big|_{\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha^{(1)}_{s}\right)}=\alpha^{(1)}_{s}
and
\alpha_{s}\Big|_{\mathrm{dom}\left(\alpha^{(2)}_{s}\right)}=\alpha^{(2)}_{s}
for every
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
;

•

5
Phenotype of a GBS group

In
[
CGLMS25
]
, the
phenotype
of a subgroup
\Lambda
of
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
is defined as follows:

•

if
\Lambda\cap\langle b\rangle=\{1\}
, then
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}\left(\Lambda\right)=\infty
;

•

otherwise, denoting by
\Lambda\cap\langle b\rangle=:\langle b^{N}\rangle
(for
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
), then
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}(\Lambda)=\mathrm{Ph}_{m,n}(N)
where
\mathrm{Ph}_{m,n}(N):=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P},|m|_{p}=|n|_{p}\text{ \ and \ }|N|_{p}>|n|_{p}}p^{|N|_{p}}
.

The authors of
[
CGLMS25
]
proved that this quantity is invariant under conjugation and that the set
\mathcal{Q}_{m,n}
of integers satisfying
{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(N)\neq\emptyset
is infinite. Moreover, they proved the following result:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(m,n)\in\left(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{2}
such that
|m|\neq 1
and
|n|\neq 1
. Then, for any
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{m,n}\cup\{\infty\}
:

•

the action of
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
by conjugation on
\mathcal{K}(\mathrm{BS}(m,n))\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(N)
is topologically transitive. In particular,
\mathcal{K}\left(\mathrm{BS}(m,n)\right)\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(N)
contains a dense
G_{\delta}
set of elements whose orbits are dense;

•

if
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(N)
is open;

•

it is also closed if and only if
|m|=|n|
;

•

the piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(\infty)
is closed and non-empty.

In
[
GLMS24
]
, the same authors even proved that the action of
\mathrm{BS}(m,n)
by conjugation on
\mathcal{K}(\mathrm{BS}(m,n))\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(N)
is highly topologically transitive if
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{m,n}^{-1}(N)\neq\emptyset
.
The goal of this section is to define a new arithmetical invariant for the action by conjugation of any GBS group on its space of subgroups. We need some arithmetical tools.

Definition 5.2
.

Let us define the following subset of prime numbers:

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p}\text{ for every cycle labeled }(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{r},l_{r})\right\}

(where
\sum_{i=1}^{0}|l_{i}|_{p}=0
). Let
v
be a vertex of
\mathcal{H}
and, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},v}(N):=

\displaystyle\bigg\{p\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}\mid|N|_{p}>\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}|l_{i}|_{p}\text{ for every edge path based at $v$}

\displaystyle\text{ labeled }(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{r},l_{r})\bigg\}.

One defines

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}(N):=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},v}(N)}p^{|N|_{p}}.

If
N=\infty
, one defines

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}(\infty)=\infty.

We call this quantity the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype of the integer
N
relatively to the vertex
v
.

Remark 5.3
.

If
\mathcal{H}
consists of a loop (based at
v
) with source labeled
n
and target labeled
m
, then one has
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}=\mathrm{Ph}_{m,n}
as defined in
[
CGLMS25
]
.

Remark 5.4
.

Notice that, in Definition
5.2
, one can replace “for every edge path based at
v
” by “for every
simple
edge path based at
v
that either is not a cycle or is a loop” and “for every cycle” by “for every
simple
cycle” (which need not contain
v
!)

Remark 5.5
.

The set
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}^{-1}(M)
is non-empty if and only if for all prime number
p
dividing
M
, one has
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},v}(M)
. In that case, one has
M=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}(M)
. We denote

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},v}:=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},v}\left(\mathbb{N}\right).

5.1
Some arithmetical properties of the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype

The goal of this subsection is to establish some arithmetical properties of the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype of an integer in order to define the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype of a transitive
\mathcal{H}
-preaction (or, equivalently, of a connected
\mathcal{H}
-graph). In particular, we show that the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype relatively to a given vertex
s
is constant on the set of vertices of type
s
of a connected
\mathcal{H}
-graph (
cf.
Proposition
5.7
). So considering saturated
\mathcal{H}
-preactions allows us to define the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype of a subgroup.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
k_{1},l_{1},...,k_{r},l_{r}
be integers.
Let
N_{1},...,N_{r+1}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

\frac{N_{i}}{N_{i}\wedge k_{i}}=\frac{N_{i+1}}{N_{i+1}\wedge l_{i}}\text{ \ for every $i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket$}.

Then, for every prime number
p
satisfying
|N_{1}|_{p}>\max_{i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}|k_{j}|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|l_{j}|_{p}\right)
one has

|N_{r+1}|_{p}=|N_{1}|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}|k_{j}|_{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}|l_{j}|_{p}.

Proof.

By induction on
i\in\llbracket 2,r+1\rrbracket
, let us show property
P_{i}
: “
|N_{i}|_{p}=|N_{1}|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|k_{j}|_{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|l_{j}|_{p}
.”

Base case

Given the fact that
|N_{1}|_{p}>|k_{1}|_{p}
, one has

\displaystyle|N_{1}|_{p}-|k_{1}|_{p}

\displaystyle=|N_{2}|_{p}-\min\left(|N_{2}|_{p},|l_{1}|_{p}\right)

\displaystyle>0

hence necessarily
\min\left(|N_{2}|_{p},|l_{1}|_{p}\right)=|l_{1}|_{p}

so

|N_{2}|_{p}=|N_{1}|_{p}-|k_{1}|_{p}+|l_{1}|_{p}.

Thus
P_{2}
is true.

Induction step

Let us assume that
P_{i}
is true, one has

\displaystyle|N_{i}|_{p}

\displaystyle=|N_{1}|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|k_{j}|_{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|l_{j}|_{p}

\displaystyle>|k_{i}|_{p}\text{ \ by the assumption made on $p$.}

We deduce that:

\displaystyle|N_{i+1}|_{p}-\min\left(|l_{i}|_{p},|N_{i+1}|_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=|N_{i}|_{p}-\min\left(|N_{i}|_{p},|k_{i}|_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=|N_{i}|_{p}-|k_{i}|_{p}

\displaystyle>0

hence necessarily
\min\left(|l_{i}|_{p},|N_{i+1}|_{p}\right)=|l_{i}|_{p}
and

|N_{i+1}|_{p}=|N_{i}|_{p}-|k_{i}|_{p}+|l_{i}|_{p}.

The induction is proved.

∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a connected
\mathcal{H}
-graph. Let
V
and
W
be two vertices of
\mathcal{G}
with labels
(s,N)
and
(s,M)
respectively. One has
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},...,E_{r}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{G}\right)
be an edge path labeled
e_{1},...,e_{r}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
such that
\mathbf{s}(E_{1})=V
and
\mathbf{t}(E_{r})=W
. Let us denote
V_{i}=\mathbf{s}(E_{i})
for every
i
and
V_{r+1}=W
.

First notice that the edges
e_{1},...,e_{r}
form a cycle based at
s
in
\mathcal{H}
. For every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
, we denote
s_{i}=\mathbf{s}(e_{i})
and
(k_{i},l_{i})=(k_{e_{i},\mathbf{s}},k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}})
.

For every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
, let
(s_{i},N_{i})
be the label of the vertex
v_{i}
in
\mathcal{H}
. One has:

\frac{N_{i}}{N_{i}\wedge k_{i}}=\frac{N_{i+1}}{N_{i+1}\wedge l_{i}}.

Let
p
be a prime number such that
|N|_{p}>\max_{i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}|k_{j}|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|l_{j}|_{p}\right)
. By Proposition
5.6
, one has

|M|_{p}=|N|_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p}.

Thus, for every
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)
:

|M|_{p}=|N|_{p}.

We deduce that

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)\text{ \ divides }\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)

and by symmetry (if the edge path
E_{1},...,E_{r}
connects
V
to
W
, then the edge path
\overline{E_{r}},...,\overline{E_{1}}
connects
W
to
V
in
\mathcal{G}
), we finally get

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M).

∎

If
\alpha
is a transitive
\mathcal{H}
-preaction, we proved that, for every
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
and every vertices
V
,
W
of
\mathcal{G}
with labels
(s,N)
,
(s,M)
, respectively, one has

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M).

Definition 5.8
.

One defines the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype of
\alpha
relatively to the vertex
s
as this quantity, and we denote it by
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}(\alpha)
.

In particular, given a subgroup
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
, applying this observation to the (saturated)
\mathcal{H}
-preaction associated to
\Lambda
, we get, for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
:

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}\left(\left[\left\langle\alpha_{s}\right\rangle:\Lambda\cap\left\langle\alpha_{s}\right\rangle\right]\right)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}\left(\left[\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{s}\right\rangle\gamma^{-1}:\Lambda\cap\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{s}\right\rangle\gamma^{-1}\right]\right).

Definition 5.9
.

We denote by

\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}(\Lambda):=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}\left(\left[\left\langle a_{s}\right\rangle:\Lambda\cap\left\langle a_{s}\right\rangle\right]\right)

this quantity and we call it
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype of the subgroup
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
relatively to

. The partition

\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)=\bigsqcup_{N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}}\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)

is called the
phenotypical decomposition of
\Gamma
relatively to the vertex

. The action by conjugation preserves
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
for every
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Though the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype a priori depends on the choice of a given vertex, the property of sharing the same
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype does not depend on the choice of the vertex:

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
be connected
\mathcal{H}
-graphs and
V
,
W
be vertices of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
,
\mathcal{G}_{2}
with labels
(s,M)
,
(s,N)
respectively such that

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N).

For every vertices
V^{\prime}
,
W^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
,
\mathcal{G}_{2}
respectively with labels
(s^{\prime},M^{\prime})
,
(s^{\prime},N^{\prime})
one has

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}\left(M^{\prime}\right)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}\left(N^{\prime}\right).

Proof.

First, notice that, for every integer
n
,

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(n)=\left\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid|\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(n)|_{p}>0\right\}.

In particular, the equality
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)
implies that

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N).

Let
E_{1},...,E_{r}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{G}_{1}\right)
(
resp.

F_{1},...,F_{u}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{G}_{2}\right)
) be an edge path labeled
e_{1},...,e_{r}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
(
resp.

f_{1},...,f_{u}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
) which connects
V
to
V^{\prime}
(
resp.

W^{\prime}
to
W
). We denote
\mathbf{s}(E_{i})=V_{i}
for every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
(
resp.

\mathbf{s}(F_{i})=W_{i}
for every
i\in\llbracket 1,u\rrbracket
) and
V_{r+1}=V^{\prime}
(
resp.

W_{u+1}=W
). One denotes
(s_{i},M_{i})
(
resp.

(t_{i},N_{i})
) the label of
V_{i}
(
resp.

W_{i}
) for every
i\in\llbracket 1,r+1\rrbracket
(
resp.

i\in\llbracket 1,u+1\rrbracket
). In particular,
s_{1}=t_{u+1}=s
and
s_{r+1}=t_{1}=s^{\prime}
. We also denote
(k_{i},l_{i})=(k_{e_{i},\mathbf{s}},k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}})
and
(m_{i},n_{i})=(k_{f_{i},\mathbf{s}},k_{f_{i},\mathbf{t}})
. Hence we have

\frac{M_{i}}{M_{i}\wedge k_{i}}=\frac{M_{i+1}}{M_{i+1}\wedge l_{i}}\text{ \ for every $i\in\llbracket 1,r+1\rrbracket$ }

and

\frac{N_{i}}{N_{i}\wedge m_{i}}=\frac{N_{i+1}}{N_{i+1}\wedge n_{i}}\text{ \ for every $i\in\llbracket 1,u+1\rrbracket$ }.

Let us fix a prime number
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})
. In particular one has
\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p}
for every cycle labeled
(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{r},l_{r})
(this condition is independent of
s)
. Let us prove that
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)
: let
g_{1},...,g_{t}
be an edge path of
\mathcal{H}
which is based at
s
and labeled
(a_{1},b_{1}),...,(a_{t},b_{t})
. Notice that the path
\overline{e_{r}},...,\overline{e_{1}},g_{1},...,g_{t}
is based at
s^{\prime}
and labeled
(l_{r},k_{r}),...,(l_{1},k_{1}),(a_{1},b_{1}),...,(a_{t},b_{t})
. Hence, as
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})
, one has

|M^{\prime}|_{p}>\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}|a_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}|b_{i}|_{p}.

By Proposition
5.6
applied to the path
\overline{E_{r}},...\overline{E_{1}}
, one has

|M|_{p}=|M^{\prime}|_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p}.

Hence

|M|_{p}>\sum_{i=1}^{t}|a_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}|b_{i}|_{p}

which proves that

p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M).

Thus,
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)
and by Proposition
5.6
,

|N^{\prime}|_{p}=|N|_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}|m_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{u}|n_{i}|_{p}.

As
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)
and
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(M)
, we have
|N|_{p}=|M|_{p}
so that

|N^{\prime}|_{p}=|M^{\prime}|_{p}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{u}|m_{i}|_{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{u}|n_{i}|_{p}\right).

Moreover, the edge path
e_{1},...,e_{r},f_{1},...,f_{u}
is labeled
(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{r},l_{r}),(m_{1},n_{1}),...,(m_{u},n_{u})
and is a cycle. We deduce that

|N^{\prime}|_{p}=|M^{\prime}|_{p}\text{ \ for every $p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}\left(M^{\prime}\right)$ }

and by symmetry

|N^{\prime}|_{p}=|M^{\prime}|_{p}\text{ \ for every $p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}\left(N^{\prime}\right)$ }

so finally

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}\left(N^{\prime}\right)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s^{\prime}}\left(M^{\prime}\right).

∎

As a consequence of this result, we get the following corollary:

Corollary 5.11
.

The phenotypical decomposition does not depend on the choice of the vertex.

Definition 5.12
.

We say that two
\mathcal{H}
-preactions
\alpha,\beta
share the same phenotype if for some (equivalently, for every) vertex
s\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H})
, one has
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}(\alpha)=\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}(\beta)
.

5.2
Merging preactions of a given
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype

To obtain topological transitivity, the goal of this subsection is to extend two preactions sharing the same phenotype into a single transitive preaction (in fact, we prove a stronger statement to obtain
high
topological transitivity (
cf.
Lemma
5.15
)).

We first need the following lemma that establishes a link between the
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype and the
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
-phenotype for some given subgraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{H}
:

Lemma 5.13
.

Let us assume that
\mathcal{H}
consists of a connected subgraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and an edge
e
with source in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

1.

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}\circ\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N)
;

2.

if
\mathbf{t}(e)\notin\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right)
, denoting by
(l,k)
the label of
e
:

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}(e)}\left(\frac{N|l|}{N\wedge k}\right)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}(e)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{t}(e)}(N)|l|}{\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{t}(e)}(N)\wedge k}\right);

3.

otherwise, denoting by
C
a cycle of edges
e,e_{1},...,e_{r}
(with
e_{i}\in\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right)
for every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
), if
|N|_{p}=0
for every
p\notin\mathcal{P}_{C,\mathbf{s}(e)}(N)
, then:

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N).

Proof.

As
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
containing
\mathbf{s}(e)
, one has
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}(e)}(M)
for every integer
M
.
Let us show that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}(\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N))=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N)
. For every prime number
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s}(e)}(N)
, one has

6
Perfect kernel

With the tools we introduced, we are now able to describe the perfect kernel of a non-amenable GBS group
\Gamma
, and to study the dynamics induced by the action by conjugation on it.

6.1
Description of the perfect kernel

The aim of this section is to show that the perfect kernel of a GBS group
\Gamma
consists exactly on the set of subgroups whose graph of groups (given by the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{A}
) is infinite.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and let

be a normal subgroup of
\Gamma
that is Noetherian (i.e. all of its subgroups are finitely generated). Let
\pi:\Gamma\to\Gamma/N
be the projection. Then
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\subseteq\pi^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma/N)\right)
, the set of subgroups of

whose image under

have infinite index in
\Gamma/N
.

Proof.

Let

be a subgroup of

such

\Gamma/N
. Let us show that

One deduces the following result:

Corollary 6.2
.

If
\Gamma
is a unimodular GBS group and
N
an infinite cyclic normal subgroup of
\Gamma
, then, denoting by
\pi:\Gamma\to\Gamma/N
the canonical surjection, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\subseteq\pi^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma/N)\right)

\displaystyle=\{\Lambda\leq\Gamma\mid\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\langle a\rangle\text{ \ is infinite}\}

for every elliptic element
a
.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, one only need to prove the last equality. As
N
and
\langle a\rangle
are commensurable, the set
\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\langle a\rangle
is finite if and only if the set
\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/N
is finite. This precisely means that
\pi(\Lambda)
has finite index in
\Gamma/N
.
∎

In the case of non-unimodular GBS groups, we have a more explicit characterization of subgroups whose graph of groups is infinite:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-unimodular GBS group and let
\Lambda
be a subgroup of
\Gamma
. Then, the set
\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\left\langle a\right\rangle
is finite for some non-trivial elliptic element
a\in\Gamma
if and only if
\Lambda
has finite index in
\Gamma
.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
has finite index in
\Gamma
, then
\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\langle a\rangle
is finite.

Conversely, let us assume that
\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\langle a\rangle
is finite. We denote by
\pi:\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}\to\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{A}
the projection. First notice that the graph
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
is finite: as the vertex set of
\mathcal{A}
is indexed by
\cup_{\alpha\ \text{vertex generator}}\Gamma/\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle
, the vertex set of the quotient graph
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
is indexed by
\cup_{\alpha\ \text{vertex generator}}\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle
. As any two non-trivial elliptic elements of a GBS group are commensurable,
\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle
is finite for every elliptic element
\alpha
. Hence, as the number of vertex generators is also finite, the set of vertices of
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
is finite. Since the degree of any vertex of
\mathcal{A}
is finite (in particular this holds for the graph
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
), we deduce that
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
is finite.

As
\Gamma
is non-unimodular, we consider a cycle of edges
e_{1},...,e_{N}
(where
\mathbf{t}(e_{N})=\mathbf{s}(e_{1})
) in
\mathcal{H}
such that, denoting by
(k_{i},l_{i})
the label of the edge
e_{i}
one has

\left|\prod_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\right|\neq\left|\prod_{i=1}^{N}l_{i}\right|.

Assume by contradiction that
\Lambda\cap\left\langle a\right\rangle=\{1\}
for some elliptic element
a
. By commensurability, this is equivalent to:
\Lambda\cap\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle=\{1\}
for every elliptic element
\alpha
. In particular, for every
\pi
-preimage
\widetilde{\mathbf{s}(e_{i})}
of the vertex
\mathbf{s}(e_{i})=\mathbf{t}(e_{i-1})
in
\Lambda\backslash\mathcal{A}
, the vertex
\widetilde{\mathbf{s}(e_{i})}
has exactly
|k_{i}|
incident edges which project onto
e_{i}
and
|l_{i}|
incident edges which project onto
e_{i-1}
. For every
i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, let us denote

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle=\left|\pi^{-1}\left(\mathbf{s}(e_{i})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\pi^{-1}\left(\mathbf{t}(e_{i-1})\right)\right|

and
a_{i}=\left|\pi^{-1}(e_{i})\right|
. Then we have:

\displaystyle a_{i}

\displaystyle=|k_{i}|s_{i}

\displaystyle=|l_{i}|s_{i+1}.

Hence, for every
i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
:

|k_{i}|s_{i}=|l_{i}|s_{i+1}

and by induction, for every
r\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
:

\displaystyle s_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{N}|k_{i}|

\displaystyle=s_{N+1}\prod_{i=1}^{N}|l_{i}|

so for
r=N
and noticing that
s_{N+1}=s_{1}
:

\left|\prod_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\right|=\left|\prod_{i=1}^{N}l_{i}\right|

which contradicts the hypothesis made on the cycle
(e_{1},...,e_{N})
.

Thus there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
\Lambda\cap\langle a\rangle=\langle a^{k}\rangle
. Let us consider the function
f:\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Lambda\backslash\ \Gamma&\to&\Lambda\backslash\ \Gamma/\langle a\rangle\\
\Lambda\gamma&\mapsto&\Lambda\gamma\langle a\rangle\\
\end{array}.

By commensurability of the vertex stabilizers (
cf.
Proposition
2.5
), for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there exists non-zero integers
N_{\gamma}
,
M_{\gamma}
such that
\gamma a^{N_{\gamma}}\gamma^{-1}=a^{M_{\gamma}}
. Denoting by
n_{\gamma}:=kN_{\gamma}
, we deduce:
\gamma a^{n_{\gamma}}\gamma^{-1}\in\Lambda
. Hence
f^{-1}(\left\{\Lambda\gamma\langle a\rangle\right\})
contains at most
n_{\gamma}
elements, which proves that all the fibers of
f
are finite. As the set
\Lambda\backslash\ \Gamma/\langle a\rangle
is finite by assumption and
f
is surjective we deduce that
\Gamma/\Lambda
is finite.
∎

We are now able to understand the perfect kernel of a GBS group. The following theorem generalizes
[
CGLMS25
, Theorem A]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a GBS group. Then:

•

if
\Gamma\simeq\mathrm{BS}(1,n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, one has
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\emptyset
;

•

if
\Gamma
is unimodular and non-amenable, then, denoting by
N
an infinite cyclic normal subgroup of
\Gamma
, one has
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\pi^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma/N)\right)
;

•

otherwise i.e. if
\Gamma
is a non-unimodular and non-amenable GBS group, then
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, the group
\mathrm{BS}(1,n)
has countably many subgroups (we refer to
[
BLT19
, Corollary 8.4]
for the proof of this statement).

Let
\Gamma
be a non-amenable GBS group. By Corollary
6.2
and Proposition
6.3
, one has

\{\Lambda\leq\Gamma\mid\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\left\langle a\right\rangle\ \text{is infinite}\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi^{-1}(\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma/N))&\mbox{if }\Gamma\text{ has a cyclic normal subgroup $N$;}\\
\mathrm{Sub}_{[\infty]}(\Gamma)&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

So by Corollary
6.2
, it suffices to show that, given an elliptic element
a
of
\Gamma

\{\Lambda\leq\Gamma\mid\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\left\langle a\right\rangle\ \text{is infinite}\}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\Gamma).

Equivalently, using the correspondence between
\mathcal{H}
-graphs and graphs of subgroups of
\Gamma
(
cf.
Section
3
) and the fact that the degree of any vertex of
\mathcal{A}
is finite, we want to show that

\{\Lambda\leq\Gamma\mid\Lambda\text{ \ has an infinite $\mathcal{H}$-graph}\}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\Gamma).

Let
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
having an infinite
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\alpha
be the associated transitive and saturated
\mathcal{H}
-preaction on a countable set
(X,x)
, fix a vertex

deriving from
x
and
R>0
and let
\beta
be a sub-
\mathcal{H}
-preaction of
\alpha
whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is finite and contains the
R
-ball around
V
. The subgraph
K
is a non-saturated
\mathcal{H}
-graph (otherwise, as the degree of any vertex of an
\mathcal{H}
-graph is finite,
\mathcal{G}
would be finite). By Lemma
4.4
, there exists a completion of
K
into a saturated
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}_{1}
such that the quotient
\mathcal{G}_{1}/K
is a forest. So by Lemma
4.3
, there exists a subgroup
\Lambda_{1}
whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph is
\mathcal{G}_{1}
.

We want to complete
K
in another saturated
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}_{2}
which is non-isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}_{1}
. By Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.5
, there exists a non-saturated
\mathcal{H}
-preaction whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph
L
is non-saturated and non-simply connected and has the same
\mathcal{H}
-phenotype as
K
. Using Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
5.15
(with
\Sigma=1
), we deduce an
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}_{2}
which contains

and

as disjoint subgraphs such that the quotient of
\mathcal{G}_{2}/(K\sqcup L)
is a forest. Hence by Lemma
4.3
, there exists a group
\Lambda_{2}
whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph is
\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

The graphs
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are infinite and the groups
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are different (using the correspondence between
\mathcal{H}
-graphs and graphs of subgroups of
\Gamma
, because the rank of the fundamental group of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is strictly larger than the one of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
). Moreover, the
R
-ball around the origin in the Schreier graph (with respect to the vertex and edge generators) of the
\Gamma
right action corresponding to
\Lambda_{i}
coincides with the one corresponding to
\Lambda
(
cf.
Section
2.1.2
) for
i=1,2
. Thus we approximated
\Lambda
by a subgroup whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph is infinite, which proves that the closure of the set
\{\Lambda\leq\Gamma\mid\Lambda\text{ \ has an infinite $\mathcal{H}$-graph}\}
is perfect. The conclusion follows in the case where
\Gamma
is not isomorphic to
\mathrm{BS}(1,n)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

6.2
Dynamics on the perfect kernel

In Theorem
6.4
, we decomposed the perfect kernel into a countable disjoint union which is preserved by the action by conjugation. A natural question is to describe more precisely the topology of those pieces. The following result gives an answer:

Proposition 6.5
.

For any vertex
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
,

•

\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(\infty)
is closed and non-empty;

•

for any
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}
, the piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is open.

Moreover, for any
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}
, the piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is closed if and only if
\Gamma
is unimodular.

Proof.

One has

\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(\infty)=\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma),a_{s}^{i}\notin\Lambda\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\}

which is closed. As it contains the trivial group, it is non-empty.

Let
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}
.
One has

\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)=\bigsqcup_{M\in\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)}\left\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)\mid a_{s}^{M}\in\Lambda,a_{s}^{i}\notin\Lambda\ \forall i\in\llbracket 1,M-1\rrbracket\right\}.

Hence,
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is open as a union of basic clopen sets.

For now assume that
\Gamma
is unimodular and let us show that
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
. For every prime number
p
, let us denote

a_{p}:=\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}|k_{j}|_{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}|l_{j}|_{p}|(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{i},l_{i})\text{ \ labels of an edge path based at $s$}\right\}.

Notice that
a_{p}=0
for all but finitely many
p\in\mathcal{P}
. By unimodularity, one has, for every
M\in\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
:

N=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P},|M|_{p}>a_{p}}p^{|M|_{p}}.

Let
M\in\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
. If
p
divides
M
, then

•

either
|M|_{p}\leq a_{p}
, which implies that
a_{p}\neq 0
;

•

or
|M|_{p}=|N|_{p}
, which implies that
|N|_{p}\neq 0
.

In other words,
p
belongs to the set
\{q\in\mathcal{P}|a_{q}\neq 0\}\cup\{q\in\mathcal{P}||N|_{q}\neq 0\}
, which is finite. Moreover, as for every prime number
p
, one has
|M|_{p}\leq\max(a_{p},|N|_{p})
, the set
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is a finite set of integers, hence
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is closed in
\mathrm{Sub}(\Gamma)
as a finite union of clopen sets.

Now assume that the group
\Gamma
is non-unimodular and let us show that the closure of a non-empty piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
intersects
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(\infty)
non-trivially. Denoting by
b
the generator associated to the vertex
s
, we first build a subgroup
\Lambda
such that the set of indices
\left\{\left[\langle b\rangle:g\Lambda g^{-1}\cap\langle b\rangle\right],g\in\Gamma\right\}
is unbounded. First, let us show that the subset of integers
\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is unbounded. By non-unimodularity, there exists a prime number
p_{0}
and a cycle in
\mathcal{H}
labeled
(k_{1},l_{1}),...,(k_{r},l_{r})
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|_{p_{0}}\neq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|l_{i}|_{p_{0}}.

In particular,
p_{0}\notin\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)
and hence

\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}\left(Np_{0}^{n}\right)=\mathrm{Ph}_{\mathcal{H},s}(N)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By induction on
n
using Lemma
5.15
(for
\Sigma=1
), we build a sequence of non-saturated
\mathcal{H}
-graphs
(\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is a tree that contains
n+1
vertices labeled
\left(s,Np_{0}^{k}\right)
(for
k\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
), and
\mathcal{G}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{n+1}
. Thus the
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}:=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n}
is a tree that contains a vertex labeled
(s,Np_{0}^{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We saturate
\mathcal{G}
into an
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
using Lemma
4.4
so that the quotient
\hat{\mathcal{G}}/\mathcal{G}
is a tree. Hence, by Lemma
4.3
, there exists a subgroup
\Lambda\in\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph is
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
. In particular, there exist elements
g_{n}\in\Gamma
such that

g_{n}\Lambda g_{n}^{-1}\cap\langle b\rangle=\left\langle b^{Np_{0}^{n}}\right\rangle.

By compacity of
\mathcal{K}\left(\Gamma\right)
, up to passing to a subsequence,
(g_{n}\Lambda g_{n}^{-1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a subgroup
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
. If
b^{k}\in\Lambda_{0}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
b^{k}\in g_{n}\Lambda g_{n}^{-1}\cap\langle b\rangle
for
n
large enough,
i.e.

Np_{0}^{n}
divides
k
for infinitely many integers
n
. Hence, one has necessarily
k=0
and
\Lambda_{0}\in\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\Gamma,s}}^{-1}(\infty)
.
∎

In fact, the decomposition we obtained is “minimal”, in the sense that any two subgroups sharing the same phenotype have arbitrarily small neighborhoods that have a non-trivial intersection, up to conjugating them by some elements of
\Gamma
. We have a stronger statement:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-amenable GBS group defined by a reduced graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
. Let us fix a vertex
s
of
\mathcal{H}
. For every
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}\cup\{\infty\}
, the action by conjugation of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)
is highly topologically transitive.

To prove Theorem
6.6
, we need to understand how to read words on the level of
\mathcal{H}
-graphs, the main subtlety coming from the fact that
\Gamma
does not necessarily act on a given
\mathcal{H}
-graph. Let
\alpha
be a
\Gamma
-action on a countable set
X
and
\mathcal{G}
be its
\mathcal{H}
-graph. Observe that the data of an element
x\in X
and a word
(c,\mu)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r}),\left(a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}},...,a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{r})}^{k_{r}},a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}^{k_{r+1}}\right)\right)
defines an edge path
E_{1},...,E_{r}
of
\mathcal{G}
: for every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
, the edge
E_{i}
is the edge of type
e_{i}
deriving from
x\cdot\big|(e_{1},...,e_{i-1}),

\left(a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}},...,a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{i-1})}^{k_{i-1}},a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{i})}^{k_{i}}\right)\big|
(
cf.
Section
2.3
). If the edge path
E_{1},...,E_{r}
is reduced, then
(c,\mu)
is a reduced word, but the converse is not necessarily true: for instance, if
\alpha
is the trivial action of
\Gamma
on a set
X
reduced to one point, its
\mathcal{H}
-graph is itself, hence finite. As there exist reduced words of arbitrarily large length, some of them lead to non-reduced paths in
\mathcal{H}
. But a partial converse relying on the labels of the vertices of
\mathcal{G}
is given in the following lemma:

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\alpha
be a saturated and transitive
\mathcal{H}
-preaction on a pointed countable set
(X,x)
such that there exists an edge
E
labeled
e
in its
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}
deriving from an element
x\in X
satisfying the following conditions:

•

every reduced edge path of
\mathcal{G}
beginning with
E
is a tree;

•

for every reduced edge path
E_{1}=E,...,E_{r}
labeled
e_{1},...,e_{r}
, denoting by
(\mathbf{s}(e_{i}),N_{i})
the label of
\mathbf{s}(E_{i})
, the integer
N_{i+1}
is divisible by
k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}
.

Then, for every reduced word
\left(c,\mu\right)
of type
e_{1}=e,e_{2},...,e_{r}
(cf. Section
2.3
), the edge path of
\mathcal{G}
defined by
x
and
\left(c,\mu\right)
is reduced. In particular, this edge path lies in the half graph of
E
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\left(c,\mu\right)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r}),\left(a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}},a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{2})}^{k_{2}},...,a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{r})}^{k_{r}},a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}^{k_{r+1}}\right)\right)
and let
E_{1}=E
, …,
E_{r}
be the edge path defined by
\left(c,\mu\right)
and
x
in
\mathcal{G}
. For every
i\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
, let us denote by
e_{i}
the label of
E_{i}
and by
(\mathbf{s}(e_{i}),N_{i})
the label of
\mathbf{s}(E_{i})
. Assume by contradiction that this edge path is not reduced. Then there exists
i\in\llbracket 1,r-1\rrbracket
such that
E_{i+1}=\overline{E_{i}}
, which implies that
e_{i+1}=\overline{e_{i}}
and that, denoting by
y=x\cdot\left|\left(e_{1},...,e_{i}\right),\left(a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{1})}^{k_{1}},a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{2})}^{k_{2}},...,a_{\mathbf{s}(e_{i})}^{k_{i}},a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{i})}^{k_{i+1}}\right)\right|

y\cdot\left\langle a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{i})}^{k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}}\right\rangle=y\cdot a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{i})}^{k_{i+1}}\left\langle a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{i})}^{k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}}\right\rangle

which implies that
N_{i+1}
divides
k_{i+1}
. As by assumption the integer
N_{i+1}
is itself divisible by
k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}
, one deduces that
k_{e_{i},\mathbf{t}}
divides
k_{i+1}
which contradicts the irreducibility of
\left(c,\mu\right)
. Conclusion follows.
∎

We first prove a weaker version of
[
FLMMS22
, Lemma 2.17]
to recover the situation of Lemma
5.15
:

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\alpha
be an
\mathcal{H}
-action on a pointed countable set
(X,x)
whose
\mathcal{H}
-graph
\mathcal{G}
contains a finite
\mathcal{H}
-graph
K
such that the quotient
\mathcal{G}/K
is a tree which is not reduced to a single vertex. Let
e
be an arbitrary edge of
\mathcal{H}
and
g\in\left\langle a_{\mathbf{s}(e)}\right\rangle
. Then there exists a reduced word
(c,\mu)=\left((e,...),(g,...)\right)
beginning with
g
such that the target of the edge path defined by
x
and
(c,\mu)
is not in
K
.

Proof.

Let
E
be the edge of type
e
deriving from
x\cdot g
and let us denote by
V_{0}
the source of
E
. Let us denote by
\mathcal{E}
the set of reduced edge paths of
\mathcal{G}
beginning with
E
. Suppose first that it contains a reduced cycle, say
C=(E=E_{1},...,E_{r})
and let us consider an edge path
C^{\prime}=(F_{1},...,F_{s})
which is a tree connecting
V_{0}
to a vertex outside
K
. If
F_{1}\neq\overline{E_{r}}
, then the edge path
E_{1},...,E_{r},F_{1},...,F_{s}
is reduced, has its target outside
K
and is defined by
x
and a reduced word
(c,\mu)=\left((e,...),(g,...)\right)
. Otherwise, let
i_{0}=\max\{i\in\llbracket 1,\min(s,r)\rrbracket\mid F_{i}=\overline{E_{r-i+1}}\}
. As
C^{\prime}
is a tree and
C
is a cycle, one has necessarily
i_{0}<r
. Thus, the edge path
E_{1},...,E_{r-i_{0}},F_{i_{0}+1},...,F_{s}
(which is simply equal to
E_{1},...,E_{r-i_{0}}
in the case where
i_{0}=s
) is reduced, has its target outside
K
and is defined by
x
and a reduced word
(c,\mu)=\left((e,...),(g,...)\right)
.

Otherwise, every reduced edge path beginning with
E
is a tree. If there exists an element of
\mathcal{E}
with target outside
K
, then the conclusion follows. If such an element does not exist, the graph
K
being finite, let us consider a maximal element

of
\mathcal{E}
(for the inclusion of paths) and let us denote by
e_{i}
the label of
E_{i}
(so that
e_{1}=e
). By maximality, the vertex
\mathbf{t}(E_{r})
has a single incident edge (which is

). Notice that this situation cannot happen in the case where

is a loop, because in that case every saturated vertex of type
\mathbf{s}(e_{r})
has at least an incoming and an outgoing edge labeled
e_{r}
. Denoting by
s_{i}=\mathbf{s}(e_{i})
for every

, this edge path leads to a reduced word
(c,\mu)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r}),(g,a_{s_{2}}^{k_{2}},...,a_{s_{r}}^{k_{r}},1)\right)
such that

x\cdot\left|c,\mu\right|\left\langle a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}^{k_{e_{r},\mathbf{t}}}\right\rangle=x\cdot\left|c,\mu\right|a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}\left\langle a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}^{k_{e_{r},\mathbf{t}}}\right\rangle.

Let us denote
\alpha=a_{\mathbf{t}(e_{r})}
.
Hence there exists an integer

such that

x\cdot\left|c,\mu\right|=x\cdot\left|c,\mu\right|\alpha^{1+k_{e_{r},\mathbf{t}}m}.

As
\mathcal{H}
is reduced and
e_{r}
is not a loop, one has

. Hence, the word

\left(c^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\right)=\left((e_{1},...,e_{r},\overline{e_{r}},...,\overline{e_{1}}),(g,a_{s_{2}}^{k_{2}},...,a_{s_{r}}^{k_{r}},\alpha,a_{s_{r}}^{-k_{r}},...,a_{s_{2}}^{-k_{2}},g^{-1})\right)

is reduced and the edge path defined by

and

is

, so has target

.

Let us consider a reduced word
\left(d,\delta\right)
such that the edge path defined by

and
\left|d,\delta\right|
has target not in
K
(as no assumption on the type of
\delta
is required, the existence of

is simply guarantied by the finiteness of

). As
\mathcal{E}
does not contain any edge path with target not in

by assumption, the concatenation

is reduced and of the form
\left((e,...),(g,...)\right)
, which allows us to conclude.
∎

The following result then relies on the topological transitivity on each piece of the partition and on an application of Baire’s theorem:

Corollary 6.10

For every

G_{\delta}
subset of
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
whose elements consist of subgroups having dense conjugacy class in
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
.

Notice that the piece
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(\infty)
has an intrinsic characterization in the case of a non-amenable GBS group
\Gamma
: this is the set of subgroups of
\Gamma
which do not contain any non-trivial elliptic element,
i.e.
any non-trivial element which is commensurable to its conjugates by Proposition
2.5
. Hence we will simply denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
the set
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\cap\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(\infty)
. The open pieces of the phenotypical decomposition also have an intrinsic characterization: they are the maximal open subsets of
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
(for the inclusion) on which the action by conjugation is topologically transitive. Let us call such an open set
transitive
. This fact relies on the following lemma:

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting on a topological space
X
. Then

•

every transitive open subset of
X
is contained in a maximal transitive open set;

•

two maximal transitive open sets are either equal or disjoint.

Proof.

By Zorn’s lemma, it suffices to show that, given a chain
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
of transitive open sets, the union
U=\cup_{i\in I}U_{i}
is transitive. Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two non-empty open subsets of
U
. Let
(i,j)\in I^{2}
such that
\Omega_{1}\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
\Omega_{2}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
. By symmetry, one can assume that
U_{i}\subseteq U_{j}
. Hence,
U_{j}
being topologically transitive, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma(\Omega_{1}\cap U_{i})\cap(\Omega_{2}\cap U_{j})\neq\emptyset
. In particular,
\gamma\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}\neq\emptyset
, so
U
is topologically transitive, which proves the first point.

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be two maximal topologically transitive open sets. Assume that
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\neq\emptyset
and let us show that
U_{1}\cup U_{2}
is topologically transitive. Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be non-empty open subsets of
U_{1}\cup U_{2}
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, there exists
k_{i}\in\{1,2\}
such that
\Omega_{i}\cap U_{k_{i}}\neq\emptyset
. By topological transitivity of
U_{k_{i}}
, there exists
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{i}(\Omega_{i}\cap U_{k_{i}})\cap U_{1}\cap U_{2}\neq\emptyset
. In particular,
\gamma_{i}\Omega_{i}\cap(U_{1}\cap U_{2})
are non-empty open subsets of
U_{1}
so there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma(\gamma_{1}\Omega_{1}\cap(U_{1}\cap U_{2}))\cap(\gamma_{2}\Omega_{2}\cap(U_{1}\cap U_{2}))\neq\emptyset
, which implies that
\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma\gamma_{1}\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Thus,
U_{1}\cup U_{2}
is topologically transitive, which implies that
U_{1}=U_{2}
by maximality of
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
.
∎

This immediately leads to the following proposition:

Proposition 6.12
.

For any non-amenable GBS group
\Gamma
defined by a finite graph of groups
\mathcal{H}
with infinite cyclic vertex and edge groups, the decomposition
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\bigsqcup_{N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}}\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
depends neither on the choice of the graph
\mathcal{H}
nor on the choice of the vertex
s\in\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

Proof.

The pieces
\bm{\mathrm{Ph}}_{\mathcal{H},s}^{-1}(N)\cap\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
(for
N\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H},s}\setminus\{\infty\}
) form the maximal transitive open subsets for the action by conjugation of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
.
∎

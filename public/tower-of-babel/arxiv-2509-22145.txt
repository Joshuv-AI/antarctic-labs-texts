Introduction

Quandles are binary algebras introduced in the framework of knot theory to construct knot invariants
[
Joy82
,
Mat82
]
. Beyond their origins, they have proven to be deeply connected to various other mathematical structures, such as solutions to the set-theoretical quantum Yang-Baxter equation
[
ESG01a
,
ESS99a
]
and pointed Hopf algebras
[
AG03
]
. These connections underline the importance of quandles as versatile and foundational objects in both algebra and topology.

Quandles have been extensively studied through both group and module theory. In addition, universal algebra offers an important framework for investigating the complex relationship between properties of the congruence lattice and those of the so-called displacement group, which encodes many properties of the quandle itself. Indeed, there are two Galois connections between the congruence lattice of a quandle and the lattice of normal subgroups of the left multiplication group contained in the displacement group. This rich interplay between the two lattices has proven crucial in addressing classification problems for finite quandles, facilitating a deeper understanding of their structure and properties. Moreover, it has also been fundamental in developing commutator theory for quandles. Commutator theory, initially developed for general algebraic structures in
[
FM87
]
, has been specialized for quandles in
[
BS21
]
.

In this paper, we use this set of tools to obtain the classification of latin quandles of size
16p
, where
p
is a prime, taking advantage of the classification of latin quandles of size
4,16,p
, and
4p
already available
[

,
Bon22a
,
RIG
]
, as well as the homogeneous representation of connected quandles over the displacement group
[
Joy82
]
. Using this machinery, we can translate the classification problem into a purely group-theoretical one. Universal algebraic techniques are primarily used to identify the groups of interest for obtaining a homogeneous representation, assuming that the congruence lattice of the structure is known.

The core of the paper lies in Section
3

16p
(completed in Section
3.5

(i)

Determine the structure of the congruence lattice;

(ii)

Use the Galois correspondence between the congruence lattice and the lattice of normal subgroups of the left multiplication group to identify the displacement group;

(iii)

Establish whether the groups satisfying the required properties exist and, if they do, whether their automorphisms give rise to coset quandles with the desired properties (in this case, being latin of size
16p

In the paper, we take advantage of the fact that finite latin quandles are solvable (in the sense of

We denote by

Lemma 1.1

Let

1.2.
Universal Algebra

An
operation
of finite arity

is a map from
A^{n}
to
A
. A (finitary)

is a pair
\langle A,F\rangle
where

is a non-empty set and
F=\left\langle f_{i}:i\in I\right\rangle
is a family of operations on

, indexed by some set

\{f_{i}\,\colon\,i\in I\}
are the
basic operations
of
(A,F)
. The function
\rho:I\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
which assigns to each

the arity of
f_{i}
is called
similarity type
of A. A
term
is any meaningful composition of basic operations in
F
. A
Malt’sev term
is a ternary term
m(x,y,z)
satisfying
m(y,x,x)\approx m(x,x,y)\approx y
. An algebra with a Malt’sev term is called a
Malt’sev algebra
.

A
subalgebra of
A
is a subset
B\subseteq A
that is closed with respect to every basic operation in
F
.

A
congruence
on an algebra
(A,F)
is an equivalence relation
\theta
on
A
such that for every basic operation
f\in F
of arity
n
and every
a_{i},b_{i}\in A
with
a_{i}\,\theta\,b_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have
f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\,\theta\,f(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
. The congruence lattice of an algebra
(A,F)
is denoted by
\mathrm{Con}(A,F)
(or simply by
\mathrm{Con}(A)
). We denote the congruence class of an element
x
with respect to
\alpha\in\mathrm{Con}(A)
by
[x]_{\alpha}
(or simply by
[x]
) and the set of classes by
A/\alpha
. The top element of
\mathrm{Con}(A)
is
1_{A}=A\times A
and the bottom element of
\mathrm{Con}(A)
is the identity relation, denoted by
0_{A}
.

An algebra is said to be:

(1)

directly decomposable
if it can be expressed as a direct product of two non-trivial algebras. Equivalently, an algebra is directly decomposable if and only if there are two congruences
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\alpha\wedge\beta=0_{A}
and
\alpha\circ\beta=1_{A}
(
\circ
is the usual composition of binary relations). If an algebra is not directly decomposable, it is called
directly indecomposable
;

(2)

subdirectly reducible
if there exists
\{\alpha_{i}\,\colon\,i\in I\}\subseteq\mathrm{Con}(A)\setminus\{0_{A}\}
such that
\wedge_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}=0_{A}
. Otherwise,
A
is said to be
subdirectly irreducible
. It is known that subdirectly irreducible algebras are exactly those algebra having a smallest non-trivial congruence. Subdirectly irreducible algebras are clearly directly indecomposable;

(3)

simple
if it has no non-trivial proper congruences, i.e., the only congruences of
A
are
0_{A}
and
1_{A}
.

A
homomorphism
between two algebras
(A,F)
and
(B,G)
of the same similarity type is a function
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
that preserves all operations in
F
. Specifically, for each
n
-ary basic operation
f_{i}\in F
, the function
\varphi
satisfies
\varphi(f_{i}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=g_{i}(\varphi(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(a_{n}))
for all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A
. If
\varphi
is a bijection, then
\varphi
is called an
isomorphism
.

An
automorphism
of an algebra
(A,F)
is an isomorphism from the algebra to itself. The set of all automorphisms of an algebra with the composition of maps is a group called
automorphism group
of the algebra. We denote the group of automorphisms of
(A,F)
by
\mathrm{Aut}(A,F)
(or simply by
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
).

The commutator theory for general algebras was introduced in
[
FM87
]
in order to extend the well-known concept of commutator of subgroups to the notion of commutator of congruences of any algebraic structure. The notion of
central
and
abelian
congruences is needed in the broader framework of general algebra to define

and

through the existence of special series of congruences named

and

as for groups. To do so, given an algebra
A
and
\alpha,\beta
being congruences of
A
, the

[\alpha,\beta]
is a congruence of
A
satisfying a certain term condition that for groups reduces to the usual notions of the commutator of normal subgroups. We are not going to explicitly describe such conditions and we refer the reader to
[

]
for further details (and to
[
BS21
]
for the case of quandles). Based on the notion of commutator of congruences, we say that
\alpha
is
abelian
(resp.
central
) if
[\alpha,\alpha]=0_{A}
(resp.
[\alpha,1_{A}]=0_{A}
). The largest central congruence is called the
center
of
A
and is denoted by
\zeta_{A}
.

The derived and central series are defined, respectively, as

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}(A)=\gamma_{0}(A)=1_{A},\quad\gamma^{i+1}(A)=[\gamma^{i}(A),\gamma^{i}(A)],\quad\gamma_{i+1}(A)=[\gamma_{i}(A),1_{A}],\text{ for }i\geq 0.

An algebra is
solvable
(resp.
nilpotent
) of length
n
if
\gamma^{n}(A)=0_{A}
(resp.
\gamma_{n}(A)=0_{A}
). If
1_{A}
is abelian, then
A
is said to be
abelian
(equivalently
\gamma_{1}(A)=0_{A}
or
\zeta_{A}=1_{A}
). The congruence
\gamma_{1}(A)
, simply denoted by
\gamma_{A}
, is the smallest congruence of

with the abelian factor. Thus, given a congruence
\alpha

is abelian if and only if
\gamma_{A}\leq\alpha
. In particular, solvable simple algebras are abelian (indeed, if
A
is simple and solvable then
\gamma_{A}\neq 1_{A}
and so necessarily
\gamma_{A}=0_{A}
).

1.3.
Congruence slim algebras

Let
A
be an algebra. Recall that a
maximal
(resp.
minimal
) congruence is a congruence
\alpha\neq 0_{A},1_{A}
such that if
\alpha<\beta
(resp.
\beta<\alpha
) then
\beta=1_{A}
(resp.
\beta=0_{A}
). We denote by
\mathcal{M}ax_{A}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}in_{A}
) the set of the maximal (resp. minimal) congruences of
A

\displaystyle\mu_{A}=\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\mathcal{M}ax_{A}}\alpha,\qquad\nu_{A}=\bigvee_{\alpha\in\mathcal{M}in_{A}}\alpha.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
A
be a finite, directly indecomposable Malt’sev algebra. Then:

(i)

\nu_{A}\leq\mu_{A}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{M}ax_{A}\cap\mathcal{M}in_{A}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathrm{Con}(A)
is the three-element chain.

Proof.

(i) Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}in_{A}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}ax_{A}
. Recall that for Malt’sev algebra we have
\alpha\circ\beta=\alpha\vee\beta
. Then
\alpha\wedge\beta\leq\alpha
and
\beta\leq\alpha\circ\beta
. Hence, if
\alpha
is not contained in
\beta
we have that
\alpha\wedge\beta=0_{A}
and
\alpha\circ\beta=1_{A}
implying that
A
is directly decomposable. Thus
\alpha\leq\beta
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}in_{A}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}ax_{A}
, then accordingly
\nu_{A}\leq\mu_{A}
.

(ii) Clearly, if
\mathrm{Con}(A)
is the chain of length
3
, then the unique proper congruence is both maximal and minimal. On the other hand, assume that
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}ax_{A}\cap\mathcal{M}in_{A}
. Then we have that

\nu_{A}\leq\mu_{A}\leq\alpha\leq\nu_{A}\leq\mu_{A}

and therefore
\nu_{A}=\mu_{A}=\alpha
, i.e.
\mathcal{M}ax_{A}=\mathcal{M}in_{A}=\{\alpha\}
. If
\beta
is a congruence of
A
then it is contained in a maximal congruence, and so
\beta\leq\alpha
. Hence, either
\beta=0_{A}
or
\beta=\alpha
. So we see that
\alpha
is the unique proper non-trivial congruence of
A
.
∎

Let
A
be an algebra
A
. The

of

is the maximum of the chains’ lengths of

. If

is

. We have that congruence slim algebras are those for which the height of

is less than or equal to
4
.

2.
Quandles

2.1.
Left quasigroups and quandles

A
left quasigroup
is a set
Q
endowed with a pair of binary operations
F=\{*,\backslash\}
, such that

(1)

\displaystyle x*(x\backslash y)=y=x\backslash(x*y)

hold for every
x,y\in Q
. We usually denote
*
just by juxtaposition. Given a left quasigroup
Q
we can define the left and right multiplication maps as

L_{x}:y\mapsto x*y,\quad R_{x}:y\mapsto y*x

for every
x\in Q
. According to axioms (
1
), we see that the map
L_{x}
is a bijection of
Q
for every
x\in Q
. So we have that
x\backslash y=L_{x}^{-1}(y)
for every
x,y\in Q
and the
left multiplication group
of
Q
as
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)=\langle L_{x}\colon\,x\in Q\rangle
.

The
Cayley kernel
of
Q
is the equivalence relation
\lambda_{Q}
defined by setting
x\,\lambda_{Q}\,y
if and only if
L_{x}=L_{y}
. We say that
Q
is:

(i)

faithful
if
\lambda_{Q}=0_{Q}
;

(ii)

connected
if the natural action of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is transitive on
Q
;

(iii)

latin
if
R_{x}
is bijective for every
x\in Q
.

Note that latin left quasigroups are faithful and connected.

A
quandle

Q
is a left quasigroup such that the following axioms hold:

(i)

x*x=x
for all
x\in Q
;

(ii)

x*(y*z)=(x*y)*(x*z)
for all
x,y,z\in Q
.

Note that (ii) is equivalent to having
L_{x}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
for every
x\in Q
and accordingly
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
.
Quandles can be constructed using groups and automorphisms as follows: let
G
be a group,
f\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
and
H\leq Fix(f)=\{x\in G\,\colon\,f(x)=x\}
. The set
G/H
endowed with the operation

xH*yH=xf(x^{-1}y)H

for every
x,y\in G
is a quandle. We denote such quandle by
\mathcal{Q}(G,H,f)
and refer to it as
coset quandles
. If
H=1
we say that the coset quandle is
principal
and if
G
is abelian, we say that it is
affine
. In the last case, we use the notation
\mathrm{Aff}(G,f)
.

2.2.
Congruences and subgroups

Let
Q
be a quandle,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)
and
N
be a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
. As introduced in
[
BS21
]
, we define the following equivalence relations and subgroups of the left multiplication group of
Q
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{con}_{N}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in Q\times Q\,\colon\,L_{x}L_{y}^{-1}\in N\}\in\mathrm{Con}(Q);

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{N}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in Q\times Q\,\colon\,n(x)=y\,\text{ for some }n\in N\}\in\mathrm{Con}(Q);

\displaystyle\mathrm{LMlt}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\{h\in\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\,\colon\,h(x)\,\alpha\,x\,\text{ for all }x\in Q\}\trianglelefteq\mathrm{LMlt}(Q);

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\langle L_{x}L_{y}^{-1}\colon\,x\,\alpha\,y\rangle\trianglelefteq\mathrm{LMlt}(Q).

In particular, we define the
displacement group
of
Q
as
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)=\mathrm{Dis}_{1_{Q}}
which has the property that
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)=\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\langle L_{x}\rangle
for every
x\in Q
and consequently the orbits of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
and of
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
coincide
[
HSV16
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Note that
\mathrm{LMlt}^{\alpha}
can be defined in the very same way for left quasigroups and
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}
can also be defined as the normal closure of the subgroup generated by
\{L_{x}L_{y}^{-1}\,\colon\,x\,\alpha\,y\}
in the left multiplication group. In particular, note that
\alpha\leq\lambda_{Q}
if and only if
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}=1
. If so, we say that
Q
is a
cover
of
Q/\alpha
(see
[
Eis14
,
BS19
,
Bon25
]
for further details on quandle covers). We can also define
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\cap\mathrm{LMlt}^{\alpha}
, and it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}\mathrm{Dis}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha\vee\beta}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha\wedge\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}\cap\mathrm{Dis}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\mathrm{con}_{N\cap M}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{con}_{N}\wedge\mathrm{con}_{M}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{NM}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{N}\circ\mathcal{O}_{M}=\mathcal{O}_{N}\vee\mathcal{O}_{M}

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)
and
N,M
are normal subgroups of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
(see also
[
BS21
, Proposition 3.2]
for more details).

Moreover we have that
\mathcal{O}_{N}\leq\mathrm{con}_{N}
and according to
[
BS21
, Proposition 3.5(1)]
we also have that

\mathrm{Dis}_{\mathcal{O}_{N}}\leq\mathrm{Dis}_{\mathrm{con}_{N}}\leq N\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\mathcal{O}_{N}}\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\mathrm{con}_{N}}

for every normal subgroup
N
of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
. In general we have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}\leq\alpha\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}

for every congruence
\alpha
, and for finite latin quandles all such congruences are equal
[
Bon23
, Lemma 1.8]
.

The pair of operators
(\mathrm{Dis}_{*},\mathrm{con}_{*})
(resp.
(\mathrm{Dis}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
) provides a monotone Galois connection between the congruence lattice of a quandle and the lattice of normal subgroups of its left multiplication group. We refer to
[
BS21
]
for more details on the properties of such Galois connections that we are going to exploit in the paper.

Given a left quasigroup
Q
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)
, the map

\pi_{\alpha}:\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\longrightarrow\mathrm{LMlt}(Q/\alpha),\quad L_{x}\mapsto L_{[x]}

can be extended to a surjective group homomorphism with kernel
\mathrm{LMlt}^{\alpha}
. Clearly
\pi_{\alpha}
restricts and co-restricts to the displacement groups, and the kernel of this restriction is
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}
.

The displacement group plays a very important role for quandles. In fact, it can be used to build connected quandles and its factors with the coset quandle construction over the displacement group and its quotients (see also
[
HSV16
]
, Theorem 4.1, and
[
Joy82
]
).

Proposition 2.1

(
[
BB20
, Lemma 1.4]
)
.

Let
Q
be a connected quandle,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)
and
x\in Q
. Then
Q/\alpha\cong\mathcal{Q}(\mathrm{Dis}(Q)/\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha},\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{[x]}/\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha},\left(\widehat{L_{x}}\right)_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}})
, where
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{[x]}=\{h\in\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\,\colon\,h(x)\,\alpha\,x\}
.

The coset quandle construction over the displacement group allows also to identify the isomorphism classes of quandles. Indeed, we have the following isomorphism theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
BB20
, Theorem 5.12]
[
Bia15
, Corollary 3.26]
)
.

Let
G
be a group,
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
and let
Q_{i}=\mathcal{Q}(G,Fix(f_{i}),f_{i})
be such that
\mathrm{Dis}(Q_{i})=G
for

. Then
Q_{1}\cong Q_{2}
if and only if
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are conjugate in
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
.

Sylow subgroups of nilpotent normal subgroups of the left multiplication group of a quandle provide trivially intersecting congruences through the operator
\mathcal{O}_{*}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q
be a finite connected faithful quandle,
N
be a normal nilpotent subgroup of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
such that

where

are the

-Sylow subgroups of

. Then
\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}\wedge\mathcal{O}_{P_{j}}=0_{Q}
for all

and
\circ_{i}\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}=\mathcal{O}_{N}
.

Proof.

The Sylow subgroups of

are characteristic in

, so they are normal in
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
. Thus,
\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}
is a congruence of

for every

. Moreover
\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}\wedge\mathcal{O}_{P_{j}}\leq\mathrm{con}_{P_{i}}\wedge\mathrm{con}_{P_{j}}=\mathrm{con}_{P_{i}\cap P_{j}}=\mathrm{con}_{1}=\lambda_{Q}=0_{Q}
, since

.
Moreover

and so
\mathcal{O}_{N}=\circ_{i}\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}
.
∎

.

(ii)

(ii)

(i)

(ii) Let

In the final part of this section, we examine how subdirect reducibility and direct decomposability of left quasigroups and quandles relate to the corresponding properties of their left multiplication group and displacement group.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
be left quasigroups. Then
\mathrm{Dis}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})\cong\mathrm{Dis}(Q_{1})\times\mathrm{Dis}(Q_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
p_{i}:Q\longrightarrow Q_{i}
be the canonical projection, and
\alpha_{i}=\mathrm{ker}(p_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Then
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{1}}\cap\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{2}}=\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}}=\mathrm{Dis}^{0_{Q}}=1
and
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)=\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha_{1}\vee\alpha_{2}}=\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha_{1}}\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha_{2}}\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}_{1}\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}_{2}
. Hence
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\cong\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}_{1}\times\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}_{2}
. Since
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)/\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}_{1}\cong\mathrm{Dis}(Q/\alpha_{1})\cong\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{2}}
and
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)/\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}_{2}\cong\mathrm{Dis}(Q/\alpha_{2})\cong\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{1}}
the statement follows.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
Q
be a directly decomposable left quasigroup. Then
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
is directly decomposable.

Note that if
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
is directly decomposable, then
Q
does not need to be directly decomposable, not even for quandles. In fact
Q={\tt SmallQuandle(32,5)}
is directly indecomposable, but
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\cong\mathcal{Q}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
Q
be a connected faithful quandle.
If
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is directly decomposable, then
Q
is directly decomposable.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is directly decomposable, that is, there are normal subgroups
1\neq N,M\trianglelefteq\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
such that
N\cap M=1
and
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)=NM
. Then
0_{Q}\neq\mathcal{O}_{N}\leq\mathrm{con}_{N}
,
0_{Q}\neq\mathcal{O}_{M}\leq\mathrm{con}_{M}
and
\mathrm{con}_{N}\wedge\mathrm{con}_{M}=\mathrm{con}_{N\cap M}=\mathrm{con}_{1}=\lambda_{Q}=0_{Q}
. Moreover
\mathrm{con}_{N}\circ\mathrm{con}_{M}\geq\mathcal{O}_{N}\circ\mathcal{O}_{M}=\mathcal{O}_{NM}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)}=1_{Q}
. Then
Q
is directly decomposable.
∎

For faithful left quasigroups, subdirect irreducibility can also be reduced to the same property for the left multiplication group.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
Q
be a faithful left quasigroup.

(i)

If
Q
is subdirectly reducible, then
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is subdirectly reducible.

(ii)

If
Q
is a quandle and
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is subdirectly reducible, then
Q
is subdirectly reducible.

Proof.

(i) Assume that
Q
is subdirectly reducible, that is, there exists a set
\{\alpha_{i}\,\colon\,i\in I\}\subseteq\mathrm{Con}(Q)
such that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0_{Q}
for all
i\in I
and
\beta=\bigwedge_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}=0_{Q}
. The left quasigroup
Q
is faithful, so
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha_{i}}\neq 1
for every
i\in I
, so also the group
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{i}}
is not trivial for every
i\in I
. Then
\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha_{i}}=\mathrm{Dis}^{\beta}=1
and so
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is subdirectly reducible.

(ii) Assume that
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is subdirectly reducible, that is, there exist
N,M\neq 1
be normal subgroups of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
such that
N\cap M=1
. Then
0_{Q}\neq\mathcal{O}_{N}\leq\mathrm{con}_{N}
,
0_{Q}\neq\mathcal{O}_{M}\leq\mathrm{con}_{M}
and
\mathrm{con}_{N}\wedge\mathrm{con}_{M}=\mathrm{con}_{N\cap M}=\mathrm{con}_{1}=\lambda_{Q}=0_{Q}
. Then

is subdirectly reducible.
∎

2.3.
Commutator theory for quandles

Commutator theory for quandles can be understood in purely group-theoretical terms by looking at the properties of the displacement group and its subgroups. The main results are the following, and they will be freely used throughout the paper. Let
Q
be a quandle:

•

if
Q
is faithful and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)
then
\alpha
is abelian (resp. central) if and only if
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}
is abelian (resp. central in
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
)
[
BS21
, Corollary 5.4]
.

•

The quandle
Q
is solvable (resp. nilpotent) if and only if
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
is solvable (resp. nilpotent)
[
BS21
, Theorem 1.2]
.

•

Affine quandles are abelian. On the other hand, connected abelian quandles are affine
[
JPSZD15
, Corollary 3.4]
.

•

If
Q
is finite and connected, then
\gamma_{Q}=\mathcal{O}_{\gamma_{1}(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))}
and
\mathrm{Dis}^{\gamma_{Q}}=\gamma_{1}(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))

[
BB20
, Proposition 1.7]
.

For finite latin quandles, we have the following characterization of the center and its relative displacement group.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
Q
be a finite latin quandle. Then
\zeta_{Q}=\mathcal{O}_{Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))}
and
\mathrm{Dis}_{\zeta_{Q}}=Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))
.

Proof.

Recall that for finite latin quandles we have that the operators
\mathcal{O}_{*}
and
\mathrm{con}_{*}
coincide. According to
[
BS21
, Corollary 5.10]
we have
\zeta_{Q}=\mathrm{con}_{Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))}
and so it coincides with
\mathcal{O}_{Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\zeta_{Q}}}
and
\mathrm{Dis}_{\zeta_{Q}}\leq Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))
accordingly. The subgroup
Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))
is a central subgroup of a transitive group, and so
Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))_{x}=1
for every
x\in Q
. Therefore,
Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))=\mathrm{Dis}_{\zeta_{Q}}Z(\mathrm{Dis}(Q))_{x}=\mathrm{Dis}_{\zeta_{Q}}
.
∎

The blocks of congruences of quandles are subquandles, and the blocks of abelian congruences are abelian quandles (this follows directly from the abstract definition of abelianness of a congruence as defined in
[
FM87
]
). We can use the description of abelian quandles and the close relationship between the properties of congruences and the properties of subgroups of the displacement group to understand the features of abelian congruences (or more generally congruences with abelian blocks) to build some tools to study solvable quandles.

Under some further assumptions, the blocks of abelian congruences are affine, so we have the following description of their structure.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
Q
be a finite latin quandle, and let
\alpha
be an abelian congruence. Then:

(i)

The block
[a]_{\alpha}
is affine, i.e
[a]_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Aff}(A,f)
for some abelian group
A
and some
f\in\mathrm{Aut}(A)
.

(ii)

If
\beta\leq\alpha
then
[0]_{\beta}
is an
f
-invariant subgroup of
A
. In particular,
|\{\beta\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)\,\colon\,\beta\leq\alpha\}|\leq|\{N\leq A\,\colon\,f(N)=N\}|
.

Proof.

(i) The blocks of
\alpha
are abelian latin quandles. So, they are affine, that is,
[x]_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Aff}(A,f)
for some abelian group
A
and some
f\in\mathrm{Aut}(A)
.

(ii) Given
\beta\leq\alpha
then
[0]_{\beta}
is a subquandle of
\mathrm{Aff}(A,f)
and so it is an
f
-invariant subgroup of
A

[
Bon20
, Proposition 3.14]
. Connected quandles are congruence regular (see
[
BF21
, Lemma 2.4]
), so the map
\beta\mapsto[0]_{\beta}
from
\{\beta\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)\,\colon\,\beta\leq\alpha\}
to
\{N\leq A\,\colon\,f(N)=N\}
is injective.
∎

We are particularly interested to understand minimal congruences of quandles, in particular for solvable ones (e.g. finite latin quandles, see Corollary 1.3 of
[

]

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
Q
be a finite faithful quandle, and
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}in_{Q}
.

(i)

\alpha=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}
and
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}
is a minimal normal subgroup of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
.

(ii)

If
\alpha
is abelian, then
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}
is an elementary abelian
p
-group for some prime

and the blocks of
\alpha
have size a power of
p
.

(iii)

If
Q
is solvable, then
\alpha
is abelian. In particular,
\nu_{Q}
is abelian.

Proof.

(i) Let
\beta=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\alpha
. So,
\beta=0_{Q}
or
\alpha=\beta
. If
\beta=0_{Q}
then
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}=1
. Thus,
\alpha\leq\lambda_{Q}=0_{Q}
, contradiction.

Assume that
N\leq\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}

and accordingly

\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}\leq N

[

(ii) According to

In the following, we are displaying some sufficient conditions for a congruence of a quandle to be central.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
Q
be a connected faithful quandle and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Con}(Q)
. If
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}
is cyclic, then
\alpha
is central.

Proof.

According to
[

\mathrm{Dis}(Q)=[\mathrm{LMlt}(Q),\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)]

is central in

. According to

Note that Lemma

Lemma 2.15
.

Let

3.1.
Locally strictly simple quandles

A quandle is

if it does not have proper subquandles. Finite strictly simple quandles coincide with simple latin quandles
[

. Latin quandles are solvable (see

]
, Corollary 1.3), thus finite strictly simple quandles are abelian and hence affine. Recall that finite simple abelian quandles are given by affine quandles of the form

where

does not have

-invariant subgroups (see
[

]
and
[

]
for more details on abelian quandles). In particular,

,
\mathrm{Aut}([x])_{x}=C_{GL_{n}(p)}(f)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}-1}
and
\mathrm{Aut}(Q)/\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
is abelian
[
Bon20
, Proposition 3.8]
.

The following result has already been implicitly stated in the proof of
[

, Theorem 3.14]
for which we include a cleaner proof divided into two parts.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Q=\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},f)
be a simple quandle, and
g\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
. If
|g|
and
p
are relatively prime, then
g\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)_{x}
for some
x\in Q
.

Proof.

If
g\in\mathrm{Aut}([x])
has order relatively prime with
p
then
g(y)=t+h(y)
for
1\neq h\in C_{GL_{n}(p)}(f)
and some
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
for every
y\in Q
. Assume that
0\neq y\in Fix(h)
. Then
f(y)=fh(y)=h(f(y))
, so
Fix(h)
is an
f
-invariant subgroup. Therefore,
Fix(h)=0
and so
1-h
is invertible. Hence, there exists
z\in Q
such that
(1-h)(z)=t
, i.e.
g(z)=z
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a finite quandle,

Let

has order relatively prime with

and so for every

(see Lemma

for every

Let

map

fixes all the elements of block

3.5.

To classify all subdirectly irreducible latin quandles of size

By Lemma

Let

(2)

\gamma_{1}(K)=\langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Z(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\rtimes\gamma_{1}(K))=1
then
\rho_{z}=-Id_{n}
and
n
is even.

Proof.

The order of
z
is
2
and so
\rho_{z}\neq 1
and it is diagonalizable with eigenvalues
\lambda\in\{1,-1\}
according to Lemma
4.3
(ii). If
V
is the eigenspace relative to
\lambda=1
then
V\leq Z(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\rtimes_{\rho}\gamma_{1}(K))=1
. Therefore,
\rho_{z}=-Id_{n}
. The map
\det:K\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
mapping
x\in K
to
\det(\rho_{x})
is a group homomorphisms into an abelian group. Then
\gamma_{1}(K)\leq\mathrm{ker}(\det)
and so
\det(\rho_{z})=(-1)^{n}=1
. Therefore,
n
is even.
∎

Let us conclude the section by showing some applications of the previous results to some concrete semidirect product, where the normal summand is an elementary abelian
p
-group and the other summand is a
2
-group.

Let
K_{50}
be extraspecial
2
-group labeled by
SmallGroup(32,50)
in the
SmallGroup
library of GAP. A presentation of
K_{50}
is the following:

\displaystyle K_{50}=\langle a,b,c,d\,|\,a^{2},\,b^{4},\,c^{4}d^{2},\,cbcb^{-1},\,db^{-1}db,\,c^{-1}aca,\,dc^{-1}dc,\,dadb^{2}a\rangle.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\rtimes_{\rho}K_{50}
where
\rho
is a faithful action. Then
Z(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\rtimes_{\rho}Z(K_{50}))\neq 1
.

Proof.

Note that
Z(K_{50})=\gamma_{1}(K_{50})=\langle b^{2}\rangle
and that it has order
2
. Assume that
Z(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\rtimes Z(K_{50}))=1
then
\rho_{b^{2}}=-Id_{2}
according to Corollary
4.5
. Since
|a|=2
,
\rho_{a}
is diagonalizable (see Lemma
4.3
) with eigenvalues equal to
\pm 1
and
\rho_{a}\neq\pm Id_{2}
. In particular, we can assume that

\rho_{a}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}.

Since we have that
[a,c]=1
,
c^{2}=b^{2}\in Z(K_{50})
and
c\notin Z(G)
we have that
[\rho_{a},\rho_{c}]=1
and
\rho_{a}^{2}=\rho_{b}^{2}=\rho_{z}=-Id
. So necessarily

\rho_{c}=\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\
0&-x\end{bmatrix}

for
x^{2}=-1
. Since
[c,d]=1
we have
[\rho_{c},\rho_{d}]=1
and so
\rho_{d}
must be a diagonal matrix. Therefore,
[\rho_{d},\rho_{a}]=\rho_{[a,d]}=1
, but
[a,d]\neq 1
, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.2.
The Group
\mathcal{G}_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

Let
p=1\pmod{3}
be a prime number and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
k^{3}=1
and
k\neq 1
. Let us consider the following presentation of the group
\mathcal{Q}_{8}
:

\mathcal{Q}_{8}=\langle x,y,z\,|\,x^{2},\,y^{2},\,[x,y]z^{-1},\,z^{2},\,[z,y],\,[z,x]\rangle.

As defined in
[
Bon22a
, Section 7]
, we have the group
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\rtimes_{\rho}\mathcal{Q}_{8}
, where the action
\rho
is defined by setting

\rho_{x}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\rho_{y}=\begin{bmatrix}k^{2}&k\\
k&-k^{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad\rho_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
Z(\mathcal{G}_{k})=1
, so the center of the group
\mathcal{G}_{k}\times A
where
A
is an abelian group is the subgroup
1\times A
and
\gamma_{n}(\mathcal{G}_{k}\times A)=\gamma_{n}(\mathcal{G}_{k})\times 1
for every
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.7
.

The automorphisms of the group
G=\mathcal{G}_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
are of the form

(12)

\displaystyle f=\begin{cases}x\mapsto\widetilde{f}(x)z_{1},\\
y\mapsto\widetilde{f}(y)z_{2},\\
z\mapsto\widetilde{f}(z)\\
f|_{\gamma_{2}(G)}=\widetilde{f}|_{\gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{k})},\\
f|_{Z(G)}=F,\end{cases}

where
\widetilde{f}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{k})
,
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z(G)
and
F\in\mathrm{Aut}(Z(G))
. In particular
|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|=2^{8}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot p^{2}(p-1)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
then clearly
f
is as in (
4.2
) since
Z(G)
,
\gamma_{1}(G)
and
\gamma_{2}(G)
are characteristic subgroups. On the other hand, let
f
be defined as in (
4.2
). We must check that all relations in the presentation of
G
are preserved by
f
.

\displaystyle[f(x),f(y)]

\displaystyle=[\widetilde{f}(x)z_{1},\widetilde{f}(x)z_{2}]=[\widetilde{f}(x),\widetilde{f}(y)]=\widetilde{f}([x,y])=\widetilde{f}(z)=f(z)=f([x,y]).

\displaystyle f(x)^{4}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(x)^{4}z_{1}^{4}=\widetilde{f}(x)^{4}=1=f(x^{4})

\displaystyle f(y)^{4}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(y)^{4}z_{2}^{4}=\widetilde{f}(y)^{4}=1=f(y^{4})

\displaystyle f(v^{p})

\displaystyle=f(v)^{p}=f(z^{2})=f(z)^{2}=1,

\displaystyle f(x)f(v)f(x)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(x)^{-1}z_{1}\widetilde{f}(v)z_{1}^{-1}\widetilde{f}(x)^{-1}=\widetilde{f}(xvx^{-1})=f(xvx^{-1}),

\displaystyle f(y)f(v)f(y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(y)^{-1}z_{2}\widetilde{f}(v)z_{2}^{-1}\widetilde{f}(y)^{-1}=\widetilde{f}(yvy^{-1})=f(yvy^{-1}),

\displaystyle[f(z),f(t)]

\displaystyle=1=f([x,t]),

for every
v\in\gamma_{2}(G)
,
z\in Z(G)
and
t\in G
. Therefore,
f\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
. The second statement is straightforward, using that
|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{k})|=24p^{2}(p-1)
(see Proposition 7.5 of
[
Bon22a
]
) and
|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2})|=6
.
∎

Let us define
\mathcal{F}=\{h\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{k})\,\colon\,|Fix(h)|=2p,\,|h|=3\}
and
\mathcal{H}=\{h\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)\,\colon\,h_{Z(G)}\in\mathcal{F},\,|h|_{Z(G)}|=3\}
. According to
[
Bon22a
, Lemma 7.7]

|\mathcal{F}|=16p
and consequently,
|\mathcal{H}|=16p\cdot 16\cdot 2=2^{9}p
. The representatives of conjugacy classes of the elements in
\mathcal{F}
are

(13)

\displaystyle f(j)=\begin{cases}x\mapsto y,\\
y\mapsto xy,\\
z\mapsto z\\
f|_{\gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{k})}=F_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}-k(1+k^{j})&-(1+k^{j})\\
0&-k^{2}(1+k^{j})\end{bmatrix},\end{cases}

for
j=1,2
, see the proof of
[
Bon22a
, Theorem 5.5]
. Let us also define

\displaystyle M=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\in GL_{2}(2).

Lemma 4.8
.

The representatives of the conjugacy classes of elements of
\mathcal{H}
are of the form

\displaystyle f(j,a)=\begin{cases}x\mapsto ya,\\
y\mapsto xy,\\
z\mapsto z\\
f|_{\gamma_{2}(G)}=F_{j},\\
f|_{Z(G)}=M,\end{cases}

for
j=1,2
and
a\in\{(0,0),(1,0)\}
.

Proof.

Note that
f(j,a)_{Z(G)}=f(j)
as defined in (
2
) for
j=1,2
. Clearly the representatives listed in the statement are not conjugate. Indeed if
f(l,a)
and
f(k,a^{\prime})
are conjugate then
f(l,a)_{Z(G)}=f(l)
and
f(k,a^{\prime})_{Z(G)}=f(k)
are conjugate in
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{k})
and so
l=k
. Moreover
|f(l,(0,0))|=3
and
|f(l,(1,0))|=6
, so they are not conjugate.

Let us compute the centralizer of
f(j,a)
in
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
. If
h\in C(f(j,a))
then
h|_{Z(G)}
centralizes
M
and so

. Moreover
h_{Z(G)}
centralizes
f(j)
in
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{k})
and therefore we have that

is one of the following:

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x\mapsto xv_{1}z_{1},\\
y\mapsto yv_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\\
z\mapsto zw\\
h_{1}|_{\gamma_{2}(G)}=\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\
0&x\end{bmatrix},\\
h_{2}|_{Z(G)}=M^{s}\end{cases},\qquad h_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x\mapsto xyv_{1}z_{1}^{\prime},\\
y\mapsto xv_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\\
z\mapsto zw\\
h_{2}|_{\gamma_{2}(G)}=\begin{bmatrix}x&-xk\\
0&xk^{2}\end{bmatrix},\\
h_{2}|_{Z(G)}=M^{s}\end{cases},\qquad h_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x\mapsto yv_{1}z_{1}^{\prime},\\
y\mapsto xyv_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\\
z\mapsto zw\\
h_{3}|_{\gamma_{2}(G)}=\begin{bmatrix}x&xk\\
0&xk^{2}\end{bmatrix},\\
h_{2}|_{Z(G)}=M^{s}\end{cases}

for
x\neq 0
,

,

and

. It is easy to check that if

then

and

can be chosen freely and

is uniquely determined in all three cases. Therefore,
|C(f(j,(0,0),(0,0)))|=|C(f_{j})|\cdot 3\cdot 4
. If

and

are uniquely determined and

is free. Therefore

\mathcal{H}

. So, given that

) we have that

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|}{|C(f(j,(0,0),(0,0)))|}+\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|}{|C(f(j,(1,0),(0,0)))|}\right)=2(2^{6}p+3\cdot 2^{6}p)=2^{9}p=|\mathcal{H}|.

Therefore the automorphisms in the statement are a set of representatives of conjugacy classes of elements of

.
∎

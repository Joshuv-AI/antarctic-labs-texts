Tableaux and orbit harmonics quotients for finite transformation monoids
Mihalis Maliakas, Dimitra-Dionysia Stergiopoulou

Abstract
. We extend Grood’s tableau construction of irreducible representations of the rook monoid and Steinberg’s analogous result for the full transformation monoid. Our approach is characteristic-free and applies to any submonoid ℳ ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{M}(n) of the partial transformation monoid on an n n -element set that contains the symmetric group. To achieve this, we introduce and study a functor from the category of rational representations of the monoid of n × n n\times n matrices to the category of finite dimensional representations of ℳ ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{M}(n) . We establish two branching rules. Our main results describe graded module structures of orbit harmonics quotients for the rook, partial transformation, and full transformation monoids. This yields analogs of the Cauchy decomposition for polynomial rings in n × n n\times n variables.

Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to prove characteristic-free filtration results for the orbit harmonics quotients of three important finite monoids, the rook monoid
\IS_{n}

\PT_{n}

2.1.
Monoids

Let
\PT_{n}
be the subset of
\M_{n}(\Bbbk)
of matrices with entries from
\{0,1\}
with at most one entry equal to 1 in each column. Under matrix multiplication,
\PT_{n}
is a monoid known as the
partial transformation monoid
. The submonoid
\IS_{n}
of
\PT_{n}
consisting of the matrices with at most one entry equal to 1 in every column and every row is called the
symmetric inverse monoid
. This is also known as the
rook monoid
because of the correspondence between matrices in
\IS_{n}
and placements of non-attacking rooks on an
n\times n
chessboard. The submonoid
\T_{n}
of
\PT_{n}
consisting of matrices that have exactly one entry equal to 1 in each column is called the
full transformation monoid
. The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
may be realized as the group of permutation matrices in
\M_{n}(\Bbbk)
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PT_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\IS_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\T_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{S}_{n}}

We record the cardinalities.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following hold.

|\IS_{n}|=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\tbinom{n}{r}^{2}r!,\ \ |\PT_{n}|=(n+1)^{n},\ \ |\T_{n}|=n^{n}.

A
partial map

p
of
[n]
is a map from a subset
\dom(p)
of
[n]
, called the
domain
of
p
, to
[n]
. If

p_{1}:[n]\to[n]
and
p_{2}:[n]\to[n]
are partial maps, we have the partial map given by their composition
p_{2}p_{1}:[n]\to[n]
,
p_{2}p_{1}(a)=p_{2}(p_{1}(a))
for all
a\in p_{2}^{-1}(\dom(p_{1}))
. For

, we define the corresponding partial map

p_{A}(j)=i\Leftrightarrow a_{ij}=1

is a partial map, we have the matrix

defined by

.

2.2.
Skew partitions and tableaux

A
partition
of an integer
r
is a finite sequence of weakly decreasing nonnegative integers that sum to
r
. For a partition
\lambda
, we denote by
\ell(\lambda)
the number of nonzero terms of the sequence. We identify two partitions if they differ by a string of zeros. For partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{q})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{q})
we write
\mu\subseteq\lambda
if
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for all
i
. A
skew partition

\lambda/\mu
is a pair of partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
such that
\mu\subseteq\lambda
. By a slight abuse of notation, we usually write
\lambda/\mu=(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\dots,\lambda_{q}-\mu_{q})
. If
\mu=(0)
, we identify the skew partition
\lambda/(0)
with the partition
\lambda
. For a skew partition
\lambda/\mu
we write
|\lambda/\mu|=r
if
|\lambda|-|\mu|=r
and we say that
\lambda/\mu
is a skew partition of
r
.

For a skew partition
\lambda/\mu
, a
tableau
of shape
\lambda/\mu
with entries from
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
is a filling of the diagram of
\lambda/\mu
with elements of
[n]
. Let
\tab_{\lambda/\mu}([n])
be the set of such tableaux. The
weight

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of a tableau
T\in\tab_{\lambda/\mu}([n])
is defined by letting
\alpha_{i}
be the number of appearances of the entry
i
in
T
. We also say that the weight of
i
in
T
is equal to
\alpha_{i}
. A tableau
T\in\tab_{\lambda/\mu}([n])
is called
semistandard
(respectively
co-semistandard
) if the entries are strictly (respectively weakly) increasing across the rows from left to right and weakly (respectively strictly) increasing in the columns from top to bottom. (In
[
1
]
, the term ‘standard’ is used for our semistandard, and ‘co-standard’ for our co-semistandard.) The subset of
\tab_{\lambda/\mu}([n])
of semistandard (respectively co-semistandard) tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
will be denoted by
\mathrm{SST}_{\lambda/\mu}([n])
(respectively
\mathrm{cSST}_{\lambda/\mu}([n])
).

3.4.
Some properties of the symmetrized Schur functor

Proposition 3.8
.

For a submonoid
\Mo(n)
of
\PT_{n}
, the functor
\G_{\Mo(n)}:P_{\Bbbk}(n,r)\to\Mo(n)
is exact.

Proof.

By
[
14
, (3.3b)]
, if

is an exact sequence in

, then for any weight
\alpha\in\Lambda(n,r)
the sequence of vector spaces

is exact. By summing over

we have the exact sequence
0\to\bigoplus_{\alpha}M^{\alpha}_{1}\to\bigoplus_{\alpha}M^{\alpha}\to\bigoplus_{\alpha}M^{\alpha}_{2}\to 0
. A standard argument using

lemma yields that
0\to\G_{\Mo(n)}(M_{1})\to\G_{\Mo(n)}(M)\to\G_{\Mo(n)}(M_{2})\to 0
is exact.∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Mo(n)
be a submonoid of
\PT_{n}
containing
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. For a module

(1) By Definition

to

. But

according to Definition

.

(2) As a vector space

is isomorphic to

Remark 3.10

Suppose

Suppose we have modules

Theorem 3.11
.

Let

M\in P_{\Bbbk}(n,r)
. Then as
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules

\F(M)\cong f(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))\cong f(M\otimes D_{n-r}(V_{n})),

where
f:P_{\Bbbk}(n,n)\to\m\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the Schur functor.

Proof.

In order to show the first isomorphism, recall that
\F(M)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda(n,r)^{\prime}}M^{\alpha}
according to Definition
3.6
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\Lambda(n,r)^{\prime}
. With the notation of Remark
3.10
, let

\widehat{\alpha}:=(1-\alpha_{1},\dots,1-\alpha_{n})\in\Lambda(n,n-r)^{\prime}

and consider the element
e_{\widehat{\alpha}}\in Sym_{n-r}(V_{n})
,
where
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}
is the canonical basis of the natural module
V_{n}
of
\M_{n}(\Bbbk)
. Now we define a linear map

\Phi:\F(M)\to f(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n})),\ \ \bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda(n,r)^{\prime})}v_{\alpha}\mapsto\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda(n,r)^{\prime}}v_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\widehat{\alpha}},

where
v_{\alpha}\in M^{\alpha}
. Since
v_{\alpha}\in M^{\alpha}
and
e_{\widehat{\alpha}}\in(Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))^{\widehat{\alpha}}
, we have

v_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\widehat{\alpha}}\in(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))^{\alpha+\widehat{\alpha}}=(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))^{(1,...,1)}=f(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n})).

In other words, the image of
\Phi
is indeed contained in
f(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))
.

By
[
14
, (3.3c) Proposition]
we have

(3.4)

(M\otimes Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))^{(1,...,1)}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda(n,n)^{\prime}}M^{\alpha}\otimes(Sym_{n-r}(V_{n}))^{\widehat{\alpha}}.

First, we observe that from the right hand side of eq. (
3.4
) it follows that the map
\Phi
is onto. Second, we observe that since

for all

, the vector space

is 1 dimensional. Thus we conclude from (

is onto and 1-1.

It remains to be shown that

is a map of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules. Let
\alpha\in\Lambda(n,r)^{\prime}
and

. On the one hand we have for all

\sigma\Phi(v_{\alpha})=\sigma(v_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\widehat{\alpha}})=\sigma v_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma e_{\widehat{\alpha}}.

According to (

Definition 3.13

\Mo(n)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{\Bbbk}(n,r)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\m\Mo(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\m\mathfrak{S}_{n}}

Using that

Suppose

Suppose the characteristic of

4.2.
Basis theorem

For a partition
\lambda
of
r
, Grood
[
15
, Definition 2.1]
considered semistandard tableaux of shape
\lambda
that have distinct entries. Similarly we give the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew partition of
r
. A tableau

with entries in
[n]
is called a
(\lambda/\mu,n,r)
tableau if the entries of
T
are distinct. A
(\lambda/\mu,n,r)
tableau that is semistandard is called a standard
(\lambda/\mu,n,r)
tableau.

Remark 4.5
.

It is clear that the number of standard
(\lambda/\mu,n,r)
tableaux is equal to
\tbinom{n}{r}f^{\lambda/\mu}
, since
f^{\lambda/\mu}
equals the number of standard tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
with entries from
[r]
.

For the next theorem we adopt the notation of Section
2.3
.

Proposition 4.6

(Basis theorem)
.

Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew partition of
r
. Then the elements
d_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n})(X_{T})
(respectively,
d^{\prime}_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n})(Y_{T})
), where
T
runs over the semistandard
(\lambda/\mu,n,r)
tableaux, descend to a basis of the vector space
\R(n)^{\lambda/\mu}
(respectively,
\R(n)_{\lambda/\mu}
). Moreover,
\dim\R(n)^{\lambda/\mu}=\tbinom{n}{r}\dim S^{\lambda/\mu}
and
\dim\R(n)_{\lambda/\mu}=\tbinom{n}{r}\dim S_{\lambda/\mu}
.

Proof.

The basis elements of Theorem
2.2
(1) are weight elements of
L_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n})
. From this it follows that the elements
d_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n})(X_{T})
, where
T
runs over the semistandard
(\lambda/\mu,n,r)
tableaux, form a basis of the vector space
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda(n,r)^{\prime}}(L_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n}))^{\alpha}=(L_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n}))^{\prime}
.
By Definition
4.1
and eq. (
3.1
) we have
\R(n)^{\lambda/\mu}\cong(L_{\lambda/\mu}(V_{n}))^{\prime}
as vector spaces. Hence the elements

, where

runs over the semistandard

. Now from this and Remark

the equality
\dim\R(n)^{\lambda/\mu}=\tbinom{n}{r}\dim S^{\lambda/\mu}
follows.

The proof for
\R(n)_{\lambda/\mu}
is similar.
∎

where

(3) Equation (4) follows from (2) and the binomial expansion of

\I(\T_{n})
of the affine varieties
\IS_{n}
,
\PT_{n}
and
\T_{n}
we have the equalities

J_{n}(\IS)=\gr(\I(\IS_{n})),\ \ J_{n}(\PT)=\gr(\I(\PT_{n})),\ \ J_{n}(\T)=\gr(\I(\T_{n})).

Proof.

First we show that
J_{n}(\IS)=\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
.

For all
1\leq i,j\leq n
the polynomial
x^{2}_{i,j}-x_{ij}
vanishes on
\IS_{n}
since every entry of a matrix in
\IS_{n}
is equal to
0
or
1
. Also, for all
1\leq i\leq n
and all
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq n
the polynomial
x_{i,j}x_{i,j^{\prime}}
vanishes on
\IS_{n}
since every row of a matrix in
\IS_{n}
has at most one nonzero entry. Thus we have
x^{2}_{i,j}\in\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
x_{i,j}x_{i,j^{\prime}}\in\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and all
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq n
. Hence
x_{i,j}x_{i,j^{\prime}}\in\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
for all
1\leq i,j,j^{\prime}\leq n
. In a similar way we conclude that
x_{i,j}x_{i^{\prime},j}\in\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
for all
1\leq i,i^{\prime},j\leq n
. Thus
J_{n}(\IS)\subseteq\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
.

From (
5.30
) and Proposition
2.1
(1) we have

\dim\big(\Bbbk[\textbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))\big)=\dim\big(\Bbbk[\IS_{n}]\big)=|\IS_{n}|=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\tbinom{n}{r}^{2}r!.

From this and Lemma
5.5
(1) we get

\dim\big(\Bbbk[\textbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))\big)=\dim\big(\Bbbk[\textbf{x}_{n\times n}]/J_{n}(\IS)\big).

Since
J_{n}(\IS)\subseteq\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
, it follows that
J_{n}(\IS)=\gr(\I(\IS_{n}))
.

The proofs of
J_{n}(\PT)=\gr(\I(\PT_{n}))
and
J_{n}(\T)=\gr(\I(\T_{n}))
are similar to the above proof. ∎

Appendix A
Skew symmetric Cauchy decompositions

Our purpose here is to obtain a skew symmetric analog of Theorem
5.7
.
Let us recall that the group
\GL_{m}({\Bbbk})\times\GL_{n}(\Bbbk)
acts on the exterior algebra
\Lambda(V_{m}\otimes V_{n})
by extending the usual action on
V_{m}\otimes V_{n}
. When the characteristic of
\Bbbk
is zero, the irreducible decomposition of the degree
r
homogeneous component
\Lambda^{r}(V_{m}\otimes V_{n})
is
\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash r}L_{\lambda}(V_{m})\otimes K_{\lambda}(V_{n})
, see
[
29
, Theorem 4.8.6]
or
[
34
, Corollary 2.3.3]
. In arbitrary characteristic, the previous decomposition holds up to filtration
[
1
, (Section III.2) Theorem]

A.1.
Definition and statement

Let
\textbf{y}_{m,n}=(y_{i,j})
be an
m\times n
matrix of skew commuting variables
y_{i,j}
, where
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq m

\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle

y_{i,j}^{2}=0
and
y_{i,j}y_{k,l}=-y_{k,l}y_{i,j}
for all
1\leq i,k\leq m
and
1\leq j,l\leq n
.

Definition A.1
.

We define the following ideals of
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle

(1)

Let

be the ideal generated by

•

any product

of variables in the same column,
1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
and

•

any product
y_{i,j}y_{i,j^{\prime}}
of variables in the same row,
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq n
.

(2)

Let

be the ideal generated by

•

any product
y_{i,j}y_{i^{\prime},j}
of variables in the same column,
1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
.

(3)

Let
J^{\prime}_{m,n}(\T)
be the ideal generated by

•

any product
y_{i,j}y_{i^{\prime},j}
of variables in the same column,
1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
and

•

the sum
y_{1,j}+y_{2,j}+\cdots+y_{m,j}
of all the variables in the same column,
1\leq j\leq n
.

From the definition we have
J^{\prime}_{m,n}(\PT)\subseteq J^{\prime}_{m,n}(\IS)
.

We have the grading
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle_{r}
, where
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle_{r}
is the subspace of
\Bbbk[\textbf{y}_{m\times n}
generated by the homogeneous polynomials of degree
r
. Since the ideals
J^{\prime}_{m,n}(Z)
, where

\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle
are homogeneous, we have the corresponding grading
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle_{r}/J^{\prime}_{m,n}(Z)=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}\big(\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle/J^{\prime}_{m,n}(Z)\big)_{r}

The formula in (
5.12
) for
y_{i,j}
in place of
x_{i,j}
define actions of
\PT_{m}\times\PT_{n}
and
\GL_{m}(\Bbbk)

. It is straightforward to verify that the ideal

•

J^{\prime}_{m,n}(\IS)
is an
\IS_{m}\times\IS_{n}
-submodule of
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle
,

•

J^{\prime}_{m,n}(\PT)
is a
\GL_{m}(\Bbbk)\times\PT_{n}
-submodule of
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle

-submodule of
\Bbbk\langle\textbf{y}_{m\times n}\rangle

Lemma A.2
.

The dimensions of the vector spaces

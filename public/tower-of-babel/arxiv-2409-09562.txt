Inverted duality of Hubbard model and an equation for Green’s functions
Xiao-Yong Feng

Abstract
The Hubbard model, a cornerstone in the field of condensed matter physics, serves as a fundamental framework for investigating the behavior of strongly correlated electron systems. This work presents a novel perspective on the model, uncovering its inherent inverted duality which has profound implications for our comprehension of this complex system. Taking advantage of this special mathematical property, we formulated an equation which directly links the electron Green’s function to doublon Green’s function. Applied to the triangular lattice Hubbard model, our approach yields results consistent with those from state-of-the-art numerical simulations. Our findings open a route to probing electron-correlation dynamics and phase transitions in the Hubbard model, potentially yielding fresh insight into its emergent many-body physics.

These two terms are diagonalized in momentum space and real space, respectively. However, their combination makes the analysis of the model very challenging. Analytically, a not very small Coulomb term renders perturbation theory ineffective, while the conventional mean-field theories fail to account for the strong quantum fluctuations exhibited by the model. Numerical treatment is also limited by the negative sign problem and the exponentially growing dimension of the Hilbert space with the number of lattice sites.

To navigate these complexities, symmetry analysis emerges as a vital tool, offering insights into the model’s properties prior to its solution
[
17

U(1)

SU(2)

[

. Furthermore, the model is endowed with
SO(4)
symmetry
[
19
]
, driven by two quantum numbers that are intimately connected to the system’s superconducting and magnetic characteristics.

In this work, we will reveal the mathematical structure what we term the ”inverted duality” of the model, and from this derive an equation satisfied by the Green’s functions of the model, shedding new light on the model’s underlying structure and behavior.

II
the path-integral representation

The Hamiltonian of the Hubbard model is given by the following expression

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-t\sum_{\langle\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\rangle\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{j}\sigma}-\mu\sum_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}\hat{n}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}+U\sum_{\mathbf{i}}\hat{n}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow}\hat{n}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow},

(1)

here
\langle\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\rangle
denotes a pair of nearest neighbor sites,
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}
creates an electron with spin
\sigma(=\uparrow or\downarrow)
at lattice site
\mathbf{i}
,
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}
is the corresponding occupation number operator and
\mu
is the chemical potential which is introduced here to control the electron filling.

The partition function for the system is give by

Z=tre^{-\beta\hat{H}}
where
\beta=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}
with
k_{B}
being the Boltzmann constant. In the path integral representation
[
20
]
, the partition function has the form

Z=\int D(\xi^{*},\xi)e^{-S(\xi^{*},\xi)}

(2)

in which the integral variable
\xi^{*}
and
\xi
are Grassmann numbers,
D(\xi^{*},\xi)=\lim\limits_{M\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{M}\prod\limits_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}d\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}d\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}
, and the action

\displaystyle S=\sum_{\mathbf{i}m\sigma}(n_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}-\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m-1})+\sum_{m}\epsilon H(\xi^{*}_{m},\xi_{m-1})

(3)

with
n_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}=\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}
,
\epsilon=\beta/M
and
H
being the classical Hamiltonian.

After performing the Fourier transformation

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\beta}}\sum_{k}\xi_{k\sigma}e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau_{m}+i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{i}},

(4)

\displaystyle\xi_{k\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{N\beta}}\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau_{m}-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{i}}

(5)

for any Bravais lattice, we have

\displaystyle S=-\sum_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})\xi^{*}_{k\sigma}\xi_{k\sigma}+\epsilon U\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\prod_{\sigma}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m-1}

(6)

where
\omega_{n}=(2n-1)\pi/\beta
(
n
is an integer ranging from
1
to
M
.) are the fermionic Matsubara frequencies,
\tau_{m}=m\epsilon
,
k=(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{k})
,

\varepsilon_{k}=(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\mu)\exp{(i\omega_{n}\epsilon)}
,
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
is the energy band of free electrons and
\exp{(i\omega_{n}\epsilon)}
is kept here for correct causality in the terminology of field theory.

III
the decoupling of the quadratic term

The quadratic term in the action can be decoupled
by employing the following identity
[
21
]
,

\displaystyle e^{\alpha\xi^{*}\xi}=\alpha\int d\eta^{*}d\eta e^{-\alpha^{-1}\eta^{*}\eta+\eta^{*}\xi+\xi^{*}\eta}

(7)

in which
\eta^{*}
and
\eta
are Grassmann numbers and
\alpha
is generally a complex number. It is worth noting this method of handling the quadratic term has previously been utilized in the dual fermion approach
[
22
,
23
]
.
Then, the partition function becomes

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\prod_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})d\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}d\eta_{k\sigma}e^{-\sum\limits_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})^{-1}\eta_{k\sigma}^{*}\eta_{k\sigma}}

(8)

\displaystyle\times\int D(\xi^{*},\xi)\prod_{\mathbf{i}}e^{-A_{\mathbf{i}}}

in which the action for the
\xi
field is decoupled with respect to the lattice site and the action for a single lattice site takes the form

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m\sigma}\epsilon(\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}+\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}^{*}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m})

(9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m}\epsilon U\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}.

Upon series expansion, we have

\displaystyle e^{-A_{\mathbf{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{m\sigma}\left(1+\epsilon\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\right)\left(1+\epsilon\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}^{*}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle\prod_{m}\left(1-\epsilon U\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}\right).

We manage to integrate out the

According to the integration rules of Grassmann numbers, the terms contributing to the integral of

and
\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}
once and only once for each lattice site
\mathbf{i}
. In order to intuitively identify the terms that contribute, we use a diagram technique.

The building blocks of the terms that contribute in expression (

1
. The dot represents the position of imaginary-time slice and the line with arrow coming from or pointing to the slice

represents
\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}

\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}

When all the dots are connected by paired incoming and outgoing lines for each spin component - meaning the term takes a form as
\prod_{m\sigma}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}

the inverted duality

The contribution diagrams for the integral of
\xi

All the contribution diagrams for the integral of
\xi

) are illustrated in Figure
2
. Figure
2

2
(b) shows the ones in the absence of
n
(
n\geq 1
) building blocks of type (ii) which are replaced by building blocks of type (i). The corresponding contributions are

\displaystyle(a)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle(-\epsilon U)^{M},

(11)

\displaystyle(b)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle(-\epsilon U)^{M-n}\sum_{m_{n}>\cdots>m_{2}>m_{1}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\epsilon^{4}\prod_{\sigma}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m_{l}-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m_{l}}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\epsilon U)^{M}\frac{1}{n!}\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{U}\sum_{m}\prod_{\sigma}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\right)^{n}.

Gathering the above terms gives

\displaystyle(-\epsilon U)^{M}e^{-\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{U}\sum\limits_{m}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}}.

(13)

Therefore, after integrating out the
\xi
field, the partition function becomes

\displaystyle Z=(-\epsilon U)^{MN}\prod_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})\int D^{\prime}(\eta^{*},\eta)e^{-S^{\prime}(\eta^{*},\eta)}

(14)

in which
D^{\prime}(\eta^{*},\eta)=\prod\limits_{k\sigma}d\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}d\eta_{k\sigma}
and the action is

\displaystyle S^{\prime}=\sum_{k\sigma}\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k}}\eta_{k\sigma}^{*}\eta_{k\sigma}+\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{U}\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\prod_{\sigma}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}.

(15)

It is easy to show that after a particle-hole transformation
\eta(\eta^{*})\rightarrow\eta^{*}(\eta)
, comparing with expression (
6
), the quantity before the quadratic term is inverted and so does the Coulomb repulsion
U
. Thus, we find that there is an inverted duality between the actions of
\xi

\eta

Such a duality becomes more pronounced when we perform the same operation on the action of
\eta
field as on the one of
\xi
field. To this end, we introduce another Grassmann field
\zeta
and decouple the quadratic term in action
S^{\prime}
as below

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha^{-1}\eta^{*}\eta}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int d\zeta^{*}d\zeta e^{\alpha\zeta^{*}\zeta+\zeta^{*}\eta+\eta^{*}\zeta}.

(16)

And the partition function becomes

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\epsilon U)^{MN}\int\prod_{k\sigma}d\zeta^{*}_{k\sigma}d\zeta_{k\sigma}e^{\sum\limits_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})\zeta_{k\sigma}^{*}\zeta_{k\sigma}}

(17)

\displaystyle\times\epsilon^{-2MN}\int\prod_{\mathbf{i}m\sigma}d\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}d\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\prod_{\mathbf{i}}e^{-A^{\prime}_{\mathbf{i}}}

where
\epsilon^{-2MN}
is the Jacobian determinant of the Fourier transformation from
\eta_{k\sigma}
to
\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}
and

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\mathbf{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\mathbf{i}m\sigma}\epsilon(\zeta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}+\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}^{*}\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m})

(18)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{U}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}.

The series expansion gives

\displaystyle e^{-A^{\prime}_{\mathbf{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{m\sigma}\left(1+\epsilon\zeta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\right)\left(1+\epsilon\eta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}^{*}\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\prod_{m}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{U}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\eta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}\eta_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\right).

It is worth noting that due to the reversed imaginary-time order in
A^{\prime}_{\mathbf{i}}
, the arrows in the contribution diagrams for the integral of
\eta
field are just reversed comparing with Figure
2
. The summation of their contributions gives

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{U}\right)^{M}e^{-\epsilon U\sum\limits_{m}\zeta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\zeta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}}.

(20)

Then, by integrating
\eta
field, we have

Z=\int D(\zeta^{*},\zeta)e^{-S^{\prime\prime}(\zeta^{*},\zeta)}

(21)

with
D(\zeta^{*},\zeta)=\lim\limits_{M\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{M}\prod\limits_{\mathbf{i}\sigma}d\zeta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}d\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}
(The Jacobian determinant of the Fourier transformation from
\zeta_{k\sigma}
to
\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}
has already been taken into account.) and

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}=-\sum_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})\zeta^{*}_{k\sigma}\zeta_{k\sigma}+\epsilon U\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\prod_{\sigma}\zeta^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m}\zeta_{\mathbf{i}\sigma,m-1}.

(22)

It is found that the partition function constructed by the integration of
\zeta
field shares exactly the same structure as the one constructed by
\xi
field. So far, we have fully demonstrated the inverted duality of the Hubbard model and reveal the
Z_{2}
symmetry of the dual transformation operation.

VI
An equation for Green’s functions

Next, we utilize the duality of the model to derive an equation about the Green’s functions.

By adding an external source term
\sum\limits_{k\sigma}(\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}J_{\xi,k\sigma}+J^{*}_{\xi,k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma})
to action of the partition function given by expression (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma}\rangle=\left.-\frac{1}{Z[J_{\xi}]}\frac{\partial^{2}Z[J_{\xi}]}{\partial J_{\xi,k\sigma}\partial J^{*}_{\xi,k\sigma}}\right|_{J_{\xi}=0}

(23)

where
Z[J_{\xi}]
is the partition function with the external source,
\langle\cdots\rangle
denotes the ensemble average and
\langle\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma}\rangle
is a Green’s function of
\eta
field.

Integrating out
\eta
field from
Z[J_{\xi}]
leads to

\displaystyle Z[J_{\xi}]=\int D(\xi^{*},\xi)e^{-S[J_{\xi}]}

(24)

with

\displaystyle S[J_{\xi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})(\xi^{*}_{k\sigma}+J^{*}_{\xi,k\sigma})(\xi_{k\sigma}+J_{\xi,k\sigma})

(25)

\displaystyle+\epsilon U\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\xi^{*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\xi_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}.

Therefore, the second-order partial derivatives of
Z[J_{\xi}]
in expression (
23
) leads to

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma}\rangle=(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})^{2}\langle\xi^{*}_{k\sigma}\xi_{k\sigma}\rangle-(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k}).

(26)

On the other hand, by adding an external source term
\sum\limits_{k\sigma}(\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}J_{\zeta,k\sigma}+J^{*}_{\zeta,k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma})
to action of the partition function given by expression (
17
) (At this time, the partition function is denoted as
Z[J_{\zeta}]
.), the Green’s function of
\eta
field can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma}\rangle=\left.-\frac{1}{Z[J_{\zeta}]}\frac{\partial^{2}Z[J_{\zeta}]}{\partial J_{\zeta,k\sigma}\partial J^{*}_{\zeta,k\sigma}}\right|_{J_{\zeta}=0}.

(27)

The integration with respect to
\eta
field leads to

Z[J_{\zeta}]=\int D(\zeta^{*},\zeta)e^{-S[J_{\zeta}]}

(28)

with

\displaystyle S[J_{\zeta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\sigma}(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})\zeta^{*}_{k\sigma}\zeta_{k\sigma}

(29)

\displaystyle+\epsilon U\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\zeta^{(J)*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\zeta^{(J)}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\zeta^{(J)*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\zeta^{(J)}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}

where
\zeta^{(J)}=\zeta+J_{\zeta}
.

The Fourier transformation of the
U
term on the above action is

\displaystyle\epsilon U\sum_{\mathbf{i}m}\zeta^{(J)*}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m}\zeta^{(J)}_{\mathbf{i}\uparrow,m-1}\zeta^{(J)*}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m}\zeta^{(J)}_{\mathbf{i}\downarrow,m-1}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{U}{N\beta}\sum_{kk^{\prime}q^{\prime}}\zeta^{(J)*}_{k\uparrow}\zeta^{(J)}_{k+q^{\prime}\uparrow}\zeta^{(J)*}_{k^{\prime}\downarrow}\zeta^{(J)}_{k^{\prime}-q^{\prime}\downarrow}

where
q^{\prime}=(i\Omega^{\prime}_{n},\mathbf{q})
with
\Omega^{\prime}_{n}=2n\pi/\beta
being bosonic Matsubara frequencies. Then the second-order partial derivatives of
Z[J_{\zeta}]
in the expression (
27
) leads to

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{*}_{k\sigma}\eta_{k\sigma}\rangle=\frac{U}{N\beta}\sum_{k^{\prime}}\langle\zeta^{*}_{k^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\zeta_{k^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\rangle+\left(\frac{U}{N\beta}\right)^{2}F_{k\sigma}

(31)

where
\bar{\sigma}
denotes the spin opposite to
\sigma
and

\displaystyle F_{k\sigma}=\sum_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}q^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}}\langle\zeta^{*}_{k-q^{\prime\prime}\sigma}\zeta^{*}_{k^{\prime\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\zeta_{k^{\prime\prime}-q^{\prime\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\zeta_{k+q^{\prime}\sigma}\zeta^{*}_{k^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\zeta_{k^{\prime}-q^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\rangle.

(32)

Since the partition functions (
2
) and (
21
) own identical mathematical structure, the Green’s functions of
\xi
field should be equal to the ones of
\zeta
field. Applying this relationship and combining expressions (
26
) and (
31
), we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\langle\xi^{*}_{k\sigma}\xi_{k\sigma}\rangle=\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k}}+\frac{Un_{\bar{\sigma}}}{(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\left(\frac{U}{N\beta}\right)^{2}G_{k\sigma}}{(i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k})^{2}}

(33)

where
n_{\bar{\sigma}}=\frac{1}{N\beta}\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}}\langle\xi^{*}_{k^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\xi_{k^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\rangle
as the density of electrons with
\bar{\sigma}
spin is a conserved quantity of the system and

G_{k\sigma}=\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}q^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}}\langle\xi^{*}_{k-q^{\prime\prime}\sigma}\xi^{*}_{k^{\prime\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\xi_{k^{\prime\prime}-q^{\prime\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\xi_{k+q^{\prime}\sigma}\xi^{*}_{k^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\xi_{k^{\prime}-q^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\rangle

is the sum of a set of Matsubara Green’s functions
[
24
]
and it can be reexpressed as

\displaystyle G_{k\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}}\ll\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\sigma}|\hat{B}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}\sigma}\gg_{i\omega_{n}}

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N\beta)^{2}\ll\hat{d}_{\textbf{k}\sigma}|\hat{d}^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{k}\sigma}\gg_{i\omega_{n}}

among which
\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\sigma}=\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\sigma}\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}
,
\hat{B}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}\sigma}=\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}\sigma}\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}\bar{\sigma}}
and
\hat{d}_{\textbf{k}\sigma}
is the Fourier transformation of the doublon annihilation operator
\hat{d}_{\textbf{i}\sigma}\equiv\hat{c}_{\textbf{i}\sigma}\hat{n}_{\textbf{i}\bar{\sigma}}
.

The expression (
33
) is the equation satisfied by the Green’s functions of the Hubbard model. This equation indicates that the electron Green’s function is directly related to the doublon Green’s function. Since the electron annihilation operator can be decomposed as
\hat{c}_{\sigma}=\hat{h}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}+\hat{d}_{\sigma}
with
\hat{h}^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}=\hat{c}_{\sigma}(1-\hat{n}_{\bar{\sigma}})
being holon operator
and
\hat{d}_{\sigma}=\hat{c}_{\sigma}\hat{n}_{\bar{\sigma}}
being doublon operator respectively
[
25
]
, the single-particle electron Green’s function
necessarily comprises the sum of the holon, doublon and cross Green’s functions. Our derived equation provides additional connection between them. This is the key result of this work.

VII
An application to triangular lattice Hubbard model

We calculate
G_{k\sigma}
with the method of motion equation
[
26
]
. The equation of motion for the Matsubara Green’s functions is written as
[
27
]

\displaystyle i\omega_{n}\ll\hat{A}|\hat{B}\gg_{i\omega_{n}}=\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]_{+}\rangle+\ll[\hat{A},\hat{H}]|\hat{B}\gg_{i\omega_{n}}.

(35)

For the sake of brevity, the subscripts of operators
\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\sigma}
and
\hat{B}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}\sigma}
have been omitted here and in the subsequent discussions.

The terms involved in the equation of motion are calculated as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}}\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]_{+}\rangle=N\sum_{\mathbf{i}}\langle\hat{n}_{\mathbf{i}\bar{\sigma}}\rangle=N^{2}n_{\bar{\sigma}},

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}[\hat{A},\hat{H}]=(U-\mu)\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\hat{A}+\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}E_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\hat{A}

(37)

in which
E_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}=\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
.

The summation of motion equation with respect to
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}}(i\omega_{n}-E_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}+\mu-U)\ll\hat{A}|\hat{B}\gg_{i\omega_{n}}=N^{2}n_{\bar{\sigma}}

(38)

Under the condition of no nesting at the Fermi surface (The nesting of the Fermi surface will introduce scattering processes near
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}=\pi
), considering only the scattering processes that occur between states with a very small momentum difference, i.e.
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\approx 0
, we have
E_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\approx\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
and the expression (
38
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\mathbf{q}^{\prime\prime}}\ll\hat{A}|\hat{B}\gg_{i\omega_{n}}\approx\frac{N^{2}n_{\bar{\sigma}}}{i\omega_{n}-(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\mu+U)}.

(39)

Therefore, according to expression (
34
),

\displaystyle G_{k\sigma}=\frac{(N\beta)^{2}n_{\bar{\sigma}}}{i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k}-U}.

(40)

By substituting above expression into the equation (
33
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\xi^{*}_{k\sigma}\xi_{k\sigma}\rangle=\frac{1-n_{\bar{\sigma}}}{i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k}}+\frac{n_{\bar{\sigma}}}{i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon_{k}-U}.

(41)

According to this expression, the spectrum of the Hubbard model is split into upper and lower Hubbard bands
\varepsilon_{k}+U
and
\varepsilon_{k}
with spectral weights
n_{\bar{\sigma}}
and
1-n_{\bar{\sigma}}

VIII
summary

In summary, we have demonstrated the existence of an inverted duality in the Hubbard model and used it to derive an equation which directly links the electron Green’s function to the doublon Green’s function. A preliminary analysis provided us a concise formula for the Mott transition and its application to the Hubbard model on a triangular lattice is very successful. This advancement not only deepens our understanding of the Hubbard model but also offers an alternative approach to dealing with the electron Green’s function in the Hubbard model.

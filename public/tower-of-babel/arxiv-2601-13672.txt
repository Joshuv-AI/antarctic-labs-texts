1.
Introduction

In 1894, the infinite matrix
\boldsymbol{H}=((n+k+1)^{-1})_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
was introduced by Hilbert
[
13
]
in approximation theory. Here
\mathbb{N}
is the set of nonnegative integers.
This matrix has been well known as the Hilbert matrix, which has applications across numerous fields, including linear algebra, numerical analysis, and operator theory (cf.
[
21
,
26
,
29
]
).
The Hilbert matrix is also a typical example of Hankel matrices whose
n
,
k
entry is a function of
n+k
(cf.
[
30
]
). We refer to monographs
[
28
, Chapter 1 and Chapter 10]
and
[
12
, Chapter 5]
for the theory associated with the Hilbert matrix.

The Hilbert matrix
\boldsymbol{H}
can be regarded as an operator acting on sequence spaces. More precisely,
given a complex sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, consider

\mathrm{H}:\ \ \{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mapsto\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{n+k+1}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

For
p>1
, it is known that the operator
\mathrm{H}
is bounded on the sequence space
\ell^{p}
and its operator norm
is equal to
\pi/\sin(\pi/p)
; see for instance
[
14
, Theorem 286 and Theorem 323]
.

Let
\text{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be the space of functions analytic in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
of the complex plane. For
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\text{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
,
by the action of
\boldsymbol{H}
on the sequence of the Taylor coefficients of
f
, the Hilbert matrix operator
\mathcal{H}
is given by

\mathcal{H}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{n+k+1}\right)z^{n},\ \ z\in\mathbb{D},

(1.1)

whenever the right-hand side of (
1.1
) makes sense for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
.

In 2000, Diamantopoulos and Siskakis
[
8
]
initiated the study of the Hilbert matrix operator on spaces of functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.
They proved that the Hilbert matrix operator
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on the Hardy space
H^{p}
for
p>1
, and got an upper bound of the corresponding
operator norm. Later, Dostanić, Jevtić and Vukotić
[
10
]
obtained that this operator norm on
H^{p}
is equal to
\pi/\sin(\pi/p)
, which coincides with the norm of the operator

\mathrm{H}
on the sequence space
\ell^{p}
. Łanucha, Nowak and Pavlović
[
22
]
investigated the operator
\mathcal{H}
on some spaces of analytic functions.
Jevtić and Karapetrović
[
17
]
considered
\mathcal{H}
on more general Besov spaces. Lindström, Miihkinen and Wikman
[
25
]
and Dai
[
6
]
studied the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on Korenblum spaces.
Lindström, Miihkinen and Norrbo
[
23
]
got the exact value of the essential norm of
\mathcal{H}
on some classical analytic function spaces. Ye and Feng
[
31
]
investigated the norm of
\mathcal{H}
from logarithmically weighted Bergman spaces to the classical Bergman spaces. See
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
for the surveys of the Hilbert matrix operator on spaces of analytic functions.

For
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
, recall that the
Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
consists of functions
f
in
\text{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dm_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where
dm_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dm(z)
. Here and below,
dm
is the area measure on
\mathbb{D}
normalized so that
m(\mathbb{D})=1
; that is,

dm(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy=\frac{1}{\pi}rdrd\theta,\ \ z=x+iy=re^{i\theta}.

If
\alpha=0
, we write
A^{p}
instead of
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.
See
[
15
,
32
]
for
A^{p}_{\alpha}
spaces.

There are also a good number of mathematicians who have investigated the boundedness and the norm of the Hilbert matrix operator
\mathcal{H}
on Bergman spaces.
In 2004, Diamantopoulos
[
7
]
proved that
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
A^{p}
if and only if
p>2
, and estimated the upper bound of this operator norm.
For
p\geq 4
, in 2008 Dostanić, Jevtić and Vukotić
[
10
]
obtained that the exact value of the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}
is
\pi/\sin(2\pi/p)
. For
2<p<4
, in 2018 Boz̆in and Karapetrović
[
3
]
proved that the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}
is also
\pi/\sin(2\pi/p)
; see Lindström, Miihkinen and Wikman
[
25
]
for another proof of this result. For the action of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, we recall some previous results as follows.

(i)

In 2017, Jevtić and Karapetrović
[
16
]
studied the Hilbert matrix operator on a family of spaces of analytic functions. In particular, they showed that
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if
1<\alpha+2<p
.
See Galanopoulos, Girela, Peláez and Siskakis
[
11
]
for the study of a generalized Hilbert operator on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
spaces.

(ii)

In 2018, Karapetrović
[
20
]
got the lower bound
\pi/\sin((2+\alpha)\pi/p)
of the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
for
1<\alpha+2<p
, and proved that when
\alpha\geq 0
and
p\geq 2(\alpha+2)
this lower bound is the exact value of the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
. In
[
20
, p. 516]
, Karapetrović conjectured that if
1<\alpha+2<p
, then the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
is
\pi/\sin((2+\alpha)\pi/p)
.

(iii)

In 2021, Lindström, Miihkinen and Wikman
[
24
]
proved Karapetrović’s conjecture for
\alpha>0
and

\alpha+2+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\frac{7}{2}\alpha+3}\leq p<2(\alpha+2)
.

(iv)

In 2021, Karapetrović
[
19
]
proved his conjecture for
\alpha>0
and
p\geq\alpha_{0}
, where
\alpha_{0}
is a unique zero of the function

\Phi_{\alpha}(x)=2x^{2}-(4(\alpha+2)+1)x+2\sqrt{\alpha+2}\sqrt{x}+\alpha+2

on the interval
(\alpha+2,2(\alpha+2))
.
Using this result, he confirmed his conjecture when
\alpha>0
and

\alpha+2+\sqrt{(\alpha+2)^{2}-\left(\sqrt{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\alpha+2)}\leq p<2(\alpha+2),

which improves the conclusion in (iii). For
-1<\alpha<0
and
p>\alpha+2
, Karapetrović
[
19
]
also gave an upper bound of the norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, which was further studied by
Bralović and Karapetrović
[
4
]
, and Norrbo
[
27
]
.

(v)

In 2023, Dmitrović and Karapetrović
[
9
]
proved Karapetrović’s conjecture for
\alpha>0
and
\frac{3\alpha}{4}+2+\sqrt{\left(\frac{3\alpha}{4}+2\right)^{2}-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}\leq p
.
They pointed out that this improves the best previously known result for all
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
.

(vi)

In 2024, Dai
[
5
]
demonstrated Karapetrović’s conjecture when
p
and
\alpha
satisfy one of the following conditions:

•

\alpha>0
and
2+\frac{3\alpha}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{9\alpha^{2}+40\alpha+48}\leq p<2(\alpha+2)
;

•

\alpha>0
,
\alpha+2<p<2+\frac{3\alpha}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{9\alpha^{2}+40\alpha+48}
, and a nontrivial condition of
p
and
\alpha
involving a double integration and the Beta function;

•

0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{47}
and
p>\alpha+2
;

•

\alpha=1
and
p>3
;

•

-1<\alpha<0
and
p\geq 2(\alpha+2)
.

The value
\frac{3\alpha}{4}+2+\sqrt{\left(\frac{3\alpha}{4}+2\right)^{2}-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}
in (v) is equal to the number
2+\frac{3\alpha}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{9\alpha^{2}+40\alpha+48}
in (vi), which was
found independently by the authors in
[
9
]
and
[
5
]
.

In summary, when
\alpha>0
and
\alpha+2<p<2(\alpha+2)
, Karapetrović’s conjecture was not fully settled. Karapetrović’s conjecture is also open for
-1<\alpha<0
and
\alpha+2<p<2(\alpha+2)
.

The known results of Karapetrović’s conjecture, which were mentioned earlier, were stated based on
p(\alpha)
-curves. Certainly, they can be rewritten based on
\alpha(p)
-curves. In this paper, we give a proof of Karapetrović’s conjecture for

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2+2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}},

(1.2)

which is based on
\alpha(p)
-curves. Throughout this paper, when the value
\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2+2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}}
appears, we emphasize that
we always assume this value is reasonable; that is
6p^{2}-11p+4\geq 0
and
p\not=1/3
. Then we will see that condition (
1.2
) also yields
p>\alpha+2
.
As recalled before, Karapetrović’s conjecture for the cases
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{47}
and
\alpha=1
was thoroughly
answered in the literature. In Section 4, we will point out that our range of
\alpha
in (
1.2
) is the best so far when
\alpha>\frac{1}{47}
and
\alpha\not=1
.

2.
A result based on
\alpha(p)
-curves

In this section, we prove the following conclusion which is stated based on
\alpha(p)
-curves and will be used to prove our main result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2-2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}},\ 0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \alpha\ \leq\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2+2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}}.

(2.1)

Then

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}=\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{p}},

where
\|\mathcal{H}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}
is the norm of the Hilbert matrix operator
\mathcal{H}
on
A_{\alpha}^{p}
.

Remark 1.
In the last section, we will see that condition (
2.1
) in Proposition
2.1
can be replaced by the weaker condition (
1.2
).
Recall that
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if
1<\alpha+2<p
. We remark that condition (
1.2
) implies
p>\alpha+2
. In fact,
condition (
1.2
) gives

\displaystyle 2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}\geq-6p^{3}+29p^{2}-17p+2.

(2.2)

If
p\leq 0
, then the left-hand side of (
2.2
) is non-positive and the right-hand side of (
2.2
) is positive, which is impossible.
In other words, condition (
1.2
) yields
p>0
. When
p>0
, a direct computation gives

\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2+2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}}<p-2.

Combining this with (
1.2
), we get
p>\alpha+2
.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of polynomials. For
1<\alpha+2<p
, recall that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(g)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq C\|g\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}

(2.3)

for all
g\in\mathcal{P}
. In fact, (
2.3
) yields that for any
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
, the right-hand side of (
1.1
) makes sense for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. This can be done similarly to
[
7
, p. 1069]
. See
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
for a similar conclusion about a generalized Hilbert operator on certain Banach spaces of analytic functions.

The following conclusion is from
[
20
]
, which is a reason for Karapetrović to give his conjecture.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
20
, Theorem 1.1]

If
1<\alpha+2<p
, then

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\geq\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{p}}.

For
p>\alpha+2>0
, it is known that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\,dt=B\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{p},1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}\right)=\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{p}},

(2.4)

where
B
is the Beta function. Then, for
p>\alpha+2>0
, the function

\displaystyle F_{p,\alpha}(r)=r^{p-4-\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2r}{1+r}}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\,dt-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\,dt

(2.5)

is well defined for
r\in(0,1]
.

The following property of the function
F_{p,\alpha}
is useful to prove Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p>\alpha+2>0
and

\displaystyle\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2-2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \alpha\ \leq\frac{6p^{3}-29p^{2}+17p-2+2p\sqrt{6p^{2}-11p+4}}{(3p-1)^{2}}.

(2.6)

Then
F_{p,\alpha}(r)\leq 0
for all
r\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

For
r\in(0,1)
,
F^{\prime}_{p,\alpha}(r)=r^{p-5-\frac{3\alpha}{2}}g(r)
, where

g(r)=\left(p-4-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\frac{2r}{1+r}}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\,dt+\frac{(2r)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}{(1+r)(1-r)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}.

It is easy to get

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}r^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1+r)^{2}(1-r)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}k(r).

Here

\displaystyle k(r)=\left(\frac{3\alpha}{2}+5-p\right)r^{2}+\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1\right)r+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+p-\frac{3\alpha}{2}-4.

Then

\displaystyle k^{\prime}(r)=\left(3\alpha+10-2p\right)r+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1.

Now, suppose
3\alpha+10-2p=0
. Note that
p>\alpha+2>0
. Then

\displaystyle k(r)=\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1\right)r+\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+p-\frac{3\alpha}{2}-4\right)

is a decreasing function on
[0,1]
and
k(1)=\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p}>0
. Hence
k(r)>0
for
r\in[0,1]
.

On the other hand, let
3\alpha+10-2p\neq 0
. Then
k
is a quadratic function. Denote by
\Delta_{k}
the discriminant for
k(r)=0
. Then

\Delta_{k}=\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1\right)^{2}-4\left(\frac{3\alpha}{2}+5-p\right)\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+p-\frac{3\alpha}{2}-4\right).

Since
6p^{2}-11p+4\geq 0
and
p\not=\frac{1}{3}
, condition (
2.3
) holds if and only if

(3p-1)^{2}\alpha^{2}+(-12p^{3}+58p^{2}-34p+4)\alpha+(4p^{4}-36p^{3}+89p^{2}-44p+4)\leq 0,

which is equivalent to
\Delta_{k}\leq 0
. Combining this with
k(1)=\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p}>0
, we get
k(r)\geq 0
for
r\in[0,1]
.

Thus,
k(r)
is always nonnegative on
[0,1]
. This gives
g^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
for
r\in(0,1)
. Then
g
is increasing on
(0,1)
. Bear in mind that

g(0)=0
and
g
is right-continuous at 0. Hence
g(r)\geq 0
when
r\in(0,1)
. Consequently,
F^{\prime}_{p,\alpha}(r)\geq 0
for
r\in(0,1)
.
So,
F_{p,\alpha}
is increasing in
(0,1)
. Also,
F_{p,\alpha}
is left-continuous at 1 and
F_{p,\alpha}(1)=0
. Hence

F_{p,\alpha}(r)\leq 0
for all
r\in(0,1]
.
∎

Let
1<\alpha+2<p
. Recall that the Hilbert matrix operator
\mathcal{H}
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
has an integral representation by weighted composition
operators
T_{t}
(cf.
[
7
,
20
]
):

\mathcal{H}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{1}T_{t}(f)(z)\,dt,\ \ f\in A^{p}_{\alpha},

where
T_{t}(f)(z)=\omega_{t}(z)f(\phi_{t}(z))
and

\omega_{t}(z)=\frac{1}{1-(1-t)z},\ \ \phi_{t}(z)=\frac{t}{1-(1-t)z}.

Next we give the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
f\in A^{p}_{\alpha}
. By Remark 1,
p>\alpha+2\geq 2
. Using the Minkowski inequality, it is known from
[
20
, pp. 520-521]
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|T_{t}(f)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\,dt,

(2.7)

and

\|T_{t}(f)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}=\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left((\alpha+1)\int_{D_{t}}(g_{t}(z))^{\alpha}|f(z)|^{p}|z|^{p-4}\,dm(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Here

g_{t}(z)=\frac{2\mathrm{Re}z-(2-t)|z|^{2}-t}{(1-t)|z|^{2}}>0,\ \ z\in D_{t},

and
D_{t}=\mathbb{D}\left(\frac{1}{2-t},\frac{1-t}{2-t}\right)
is the Euclidean disk of radius
(1-t)/(2-t)
and center
1/(2-t)
.
Note that

g_{t}(z)\leq\frac{2|z|-(2-t)|z|^{2}-t}{(1-t)|z|^{2}}=\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\left(1-|z|^{2}-\frac{(1-|z|)^{2}}{1-t}\right).

Because of
\alpha\geq 0
, we get

(g_{t}(z))^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{|z|^{2\alpha}}\left(1-|z|^{2}-\frac{(1-|z|)^{2}}{1-t}\right)^{\alpha}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle||T_{t}(f)||_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\times

\displaystyle\small{\left((\alpha+1)\int_{D_{t}}\left(1-|z|^{2}-\frac{(1-|z|)^{2}}{1-t}\right)^{\alpha}|f(z)|^{p}|z|^{p-4-2\alpha}\,dm(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.}

(2.8)

Clearly,

\displaystyle D_{t}\subseteq\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\frac{t}{2-t}<|z|<1\right\}.

(2.9)

Write

K_{p,\alpha}(t)=2(\alpha+1)\int_{\frac{t}{2-t}}^{1}\left(1-r^{2}-\frac{(1-r)^{2}}{1-t}\right)^{\alpha}r^{p-3-2\alpha}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)\,dr,

where

M_{p}(r,f)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\ d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then it follows from (
2.8
) that

\|T_{t}(f)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}(K_{p,\alpha}(t))^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This and (
2.7
) yield

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}(K_{p,\alpha}(t))^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dt.

(2.10)

Next, because
p>\alpha+2\geq 2
, we consider

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left((K_{p,\alpha}(t))^{\frac{1}{p}}-\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left((K_{p,\alpha}(t))^{\frac{1}{p}}-\left(J_{p,\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\,dt,

(2.11)

where

J_{p,\alpha}=2(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}r\,dr.

Recall that the following elementary inequality (cf.
[
14
, p. 39]
)

x^{\beta}-y^{\beta}\leq\beta y^{\beta-1}(x-y)

holds when
x>0
,
y>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then (
2
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left((K_{p,\alpha}(t))^{\frac{1}{p}}-\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{p}\left(J_{p,\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left(K_{p,\alpha}(t)-J_{p,\alpha}\right)\,dt.

(2.12)

By
\alpha\geq 0
and
(1+r)^{\alpha}\geq 2^{\alpha}r^{\alpha/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{p,\alpha}(t)-J_{p,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2(\alpha+1)\int_{\frac{t}{2-t}}^{1}\left(1-r^{2}-(1-r)^{2}\right)^{\alpha}r^{p-3-2\alpha}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)\,dr

\displaystyle-2^{\alpha+1}(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)(1-r)^{\alpha}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2^{\alpha+1}(\alpha+1)\int_{\frac{t}{2-t}}^{1}\left(1-r\right)^{\alpha}r^{p-3-\alpha}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)\,dr

\displaystyle-2^{\alpha+1}(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)(1-r)^{\alpha}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\,dr.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left(K_{p,\alpha}(t)-J_{p,\alpha}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2^{\alpha+1}(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\times

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\frac{t}{2-t}}^{1}\left(1-r\right)^{\alpha}r^{p-3-\alpha}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)\,dr-\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)(1-r)^{\alpha}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\,dr\right)\,dt.

This together with the Fubini theorem gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left(K_{p,\alpha}(t)-J_{p,\alpha}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2^{\alpha+1}(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}^{p}(r,f)(1-r)^{\alpha}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}F_{p,\alpha}(r)\,dr,

where
F_{p,\alpha}(r)
is defined as (
2.5
). From
Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\left(K_{p,\alpha}(t)-J_{p,\alpha}\right)\,dt\leq 0.

(2.13)

Thus, (
2.4
), (
2
), and (
2.13
) yield

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-1}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}(K_{p,\alpha}(t))^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dt\leq\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{p}}\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}.

Combining this with (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{p}}\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}

for all
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}
, which gives

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{p}}.

By this and Theorem
2.2
, we get the desired result. The proof of Proposition

is complete.

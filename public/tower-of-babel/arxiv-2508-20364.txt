Binomial edge rings associated to skew Ferrers diagrams Thanks: August 10, 2026 Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13F65 05E40 Secondary 14M25 Thanks: Keyword: Sagbi basis, Ferrers diagram, Krull dimension
Kuei-Nuan Lin and Yi-Huang Shen

Abstract
. In this study, we investigate the binomial edge ring associated with the skew Ferrers diagram. By employing Sagbi basis theory, we construct a quadratic Gröbner basis for its defining ideal. As an application, we prove that this ring is a Koszul, Cohen–Macaulay, normal domain. Moreover, we precisely determine its Krull dimension.



1.
Introduction

The main object of this work is the binomial edge ring associated with a graph
G
. This is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by the generators of the binomial edge ideal associated with
G
. In the present work, we will focus on the case where
G
is
a bipartite graph that can be represented by a skew Ferrers diagram.

The concept of binomial edge ideals of finite simple graphs was independently introduced in
[
11
]
and
[
15
]
. These ideals are generated by the
2\times 2
minors of a
2\times n
generic matrix that are associated with the given graph. They exhibit deep connections with algebraic statistics, as discussed in
[
7
]
, and have been extensively studied over the past fifteen years. Recently, Laclair, Mastroeni, McCullough, and Peeva
[
14
]
established a complete characterization of the conditions under which the quotient ring of a binomial edge ideal is Koszul.

Binomial edge ideals, which are generated by maximal minors of a
2\times n
generic matrix, are special determinantal ideals. Determinantal ideals play a central role in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, and they form the core of the theory of Schubert varieties; see, for example,
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
.
The
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by all maximal minors of a generic matrix is also a foundational object in these areas. As the homogeneous coordinate ring of the Grassmannian variety, its defining ideal is generated by the classical Plücker relations. This algebraic structure serves as a testing ground for investigating various mathematical properties, including homological properties, free resolutions, straightening laws, and aspects of singularity theory.

The defining equations of a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by a proper subset of maximal minors of a generic matrix remain largely unknown due to the intricate nature of determinantal relationships. Recently, Almousa, Lin, and Liske
[
1
]
studied the special case where the selected maximal minors satisfy the unit interval property, while Higashitani examined binomial edge rings associated with complete bipartite graphs
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Complete bipartite graphs exhibit a natural correspondence with rectangular Ferrers diagrams. More generally, Ferrers bipartite graphs are derived from Ferrers diagrams (also known as Young diagrams). The associated edge ideal, termed a Ferrers ideal, constitutes a squarefree quadratic monomial ideal possessing a
2
-linear minimal free resolution. Corso and Nagel
[
6
]
conducted a systematic investigation of Ferrers ideal, including their associated toric rings. Subsequently, Corso, Nagel, Petrović, and Yuen
[
5
]
examined the case of skew Ferrers diagrams. Their research elucidated the connections between skew Ferrers diagrams with algebraic statistics. Furthermore, they showed that the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generated by the monomial generators of edge ideals associated with skew Ferrers diagrams are Koszul, Cohen–Macaulay, and normal domains.

Therefore, in this study, we aim to extend Higashitani’s findings by investigating the binomial edge rings associated with skew Ferrers diagrams. We delineate below the primary objects of this research and summarize the key results obtained, with comprehensive details presented in subsequent sections.

A skew Ferrers diagram
\bm{\lambda}/\bm{\mu}

can be defined by two tuples (partitions)

\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{a})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{a}

and

\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{a})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{a}
, where

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{a}
,

\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{a}
,
and
\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq a
, with

a
and
b\coloneqq\lambda_{1}
being positive integers.
This diagram
\bm{\lambda}/\bm{\mu}
naturally induces a bipartite graph
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{\lambda}}/{\bm{\mu}}}
with vertex partition
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{a}\}\sqcup\{y_{1},\dots,y_{b}\}
, such that
\{x_{i},y_{j}\}
is an edge of
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
1\leq i\leq a
and
\mu_{i}<j\leq\lambda_{i}
.

For the bipartite graph
\mathcal{G}
, we consider the polynomial ring

S\coloneqq\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},\ p_{1},\ldots,p_{b},\ q_{1},\ldots,q_{a},\ y_{1},\ldots,y_{b}]

over a field
\mathbb{K}
.
The
binomial edge ideal

J_{\mathcal{G}}
associated with
\mathcal{G}
is generated by the binomials in the set

G(J_{\mathcal{G}})\coloneqq\left\{f_{i,j}\coloneqq x_{i}y_{j}-p_{j}q_{i}\ \middle|\ \{x_{i},y_{j}\}\in E(\mathcal{G})\right\}.

The central aim of this paper is to establish a
Sagbi
basis for the associated binomial edge ring
\mathbb{K}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]\subseteq S
, and to describe a quadratic Gröbner basis for its defining ideal.
To achieve this goal, we introduce auxiliary polynomials defined as

f_{i_{1},j_{1};\,i_{2},j_{2}}\coloneqq f_{i_{1},j_{1}}f_{i_{2},j_{2}}-f_{i_{1},j_{2}}f_{i_{2},j_{1}},

where
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq a
and
\mu_{i_{1}}<j_{1}<j_{2}\leq\lambda_{i_{2}}
.
The set
H
, comprising all
f_{i,j}
along with
f_{i_{1},j_{1};\,i_{2},j_{2}}
, plays a crucial role in our analysis.

Theorem

(Main result)
.

(a)

The set
H
forms a
Sagbi
basis for the binomial edge ring
\mathbb{K}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
with respect to an appropriate term order on
S
.

(b)

The defining ideal of
\mathbb{K}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
admits a quadratic Gröbner basis.

(c)

The ring
\mathbb{K}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
is a Koszul, Cohen–Macaulay, normal domain.
Moreover, if
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{K}=0
, it has rational singularities;
if
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{K}>0
, it is
F
-rational.

The present work constitutes a natural extension of Higashitani’s study
[
12
]
, which employed
Sagbi
theory to characterize the defining ideal of binomial edge rings associated with complete bipartite graphs. Higashitani’s proof crucially depended on the isomorphism between the initial algebra of the subalgebra and the Hibi ring of a specific poset. However, this correspondence fails to generalize to the broader context of skew Ferrers diagrams examined in our study.

Instead, we leverage the structure of skew Ferrers diagrams to draw out the relationships among initial monomials within
H
, the carefully selected collection of polynomials. Unlike the Hibi ring case, which benefits from an existing Gröbner basis, our framework requires the explicit construction of a reduction process. Through the introduction of an appropriately defined product order, we validate both the Noetherian property of this process and the convergence of all monomials within the same fiber to identical standard monomials. These technical preparations culminate in the proof of our main results. Moreover, Theorem
5.3
provides an explicit computation of the Krull dimension of
\mathbb{K}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
.

\{\mathcal{XY}_{A_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{XY}_{A_{k}}\}
and
\{\mathcal{QP}_{A_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{QP}_{A_{k}}\}
are fiber-invariant, the set
\mathcal{U}
(and equivalently
\mathcal{V}
) uniquely determines the monomial
\operatorname{std}(g)
. It remains to show that
\mathcal{V}
is fiber-invariant.

Furthermore, we can assume that the requirements (R
1
)-(R
4
) are also satisfied by the corresponding objects obtained from
\widetilde{g}=\operatorname{std}(\widetilde{g})=T_{\widetilde{A}_{1}}\cdots T_{\widetilde{A}_{k}}
. In particular, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq\mathcal{U}(\widetilde{g})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq\mathcal{V}(\widetilde{g})
be the corresponding sets obtained from
\widetilde{g}
. We have pointed out that
\#\mathcal{U}=\#\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
and
\#\mathcal{V}=\#\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
by the fiber-invariance. Since
g=\operatorname{std}(g)\neq\widetilde{g}=\operatorname{std}(\widetilde{g})
, it follows that
\mathcal{V}\neq\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
and
\mathcal{U}\neq\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
. Note that factors of standard monomials are still standard. By the minimality assumption of
k
, the subsets
\mathcal{V}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
are therefore disjoint. Let
\ell\coloneqq\min(\mathcal{V})
, and suppose without loss of generality that
\ell<\widetilde{\ell}\coloneqq\min(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
.

Since the multisets
\{\mathcal{XY}_{A_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{XY}_{A_{k}}\}
is fiber-invariant,
\mathcal{XY}_{A_{i}}=\mathcal{XY}_{\widetilde{A}_{i}}
for all
i
by requirement (R
1
). Since
\{\mathcal{QP}_{A_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{QP}_{A_{k}}\}
is fiber-invariant, it follows from requirement (R
3
) that
\mathcal{QP}_{A_{i}}=\mathcal{QP}_{\widetilde{A}_{i}}
for
i<\ell
. Consequently,
T_{A_{i}}=T_{\widetilde{A}_{i}}
for
i<\ell
. By the minimality assumption on
k
, we must therefore have
\ell=1
.

Since
\mathcal{V}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
are disjoint, it is evident that
\widetilde{\ell}\in\mathcal{U}
. Let
\ell^{\prime}\coloneqq\min(\mathcal{U})
. It is clear that
\ell=1<\ell^{\prime}\leq\widetilde{\ell}
. By requirement (R
2
), the ordered pair
(\mathcal{XY}_{A_{1}},\mathcal{QP}_{A_{\ell^{\prime}}})
is not legitimate with respect to
\Gamma
. On the other hand, since
1=\min(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}})
and
\ell^{\prime}=\min(\mathcal{U})
, by requirement (R
3
), we must have
\mathcal{QP}_{\widetilde{A}_{1}}=\mathcal{QP}_{A_{\ell^{\prime}}}
. In particular, the ordered pair
(\mathcal{XY}_{\widetilde{A}_{1}},\mathcal{QP}_{\widetilde{A}_{1}})=(\mathcal{XY}_{A_{1}},\mathcal{QP}_{A_{\ell^{\prime}}})
is legitimate with respect to
\Gamma
.
This yields the desired contradiction.

, Proposition 13.15]

{\mathbb{K}}[\operatorname{LT}(H)]
is normal, and consequently Cohen–Macaulay, by
[
13
, Theorem 1]
. The remaining conclusions are derived from
[
4
, Corollary 2.3]
.
∎

5.
Application: the Krull dimension

We conclude this work by providing a combinatorial formula for the Krull dimension of the binomial edge ring
{\mathbb{K}}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
. More precisely, the formula is given through the enumeration of specific cells within the corresponding Ferrers diagram. We begin by defining these relevant cells.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(i,j)
be a cell in
\Gamma
. If its northwestern neighbor
(i-1,j-1)
does not lie in
\Gamma
, we call
(i,j)
a
northwestern perimeter cell
of
\Gamma
. If the northwestern neighbor
(i-1,j-1)
lie in
\Gamma
, but the southeastern neighbor
(i+1,j+1)
does not, we call
(i,j)
a
southeastern perimeter cell
of
\Gamma
. The set of northwestern perimeter cells is denoted by
{\bm{P}}_{NW}={\bm{P}}_{NW}(\Gamma)
, and the set of southeastern perimeter cells is denoted by
{\bm{P}}_{SE}={\bm{P}}_{SE}(\Gamma)
. Finally, let
{\bm{P}}={\bm{P}}_{NW}\sqcup{\bm{P}}_{SE}
.

, and its southeastern perimeter cells are marked with

The final theorem of this work is stated as follows.

Theorem 5.3
.

The Krull dimension of
{\mathbb{K}}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
is equal to the cardinality
\#{\bm{P}}
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbb{K}}[\operatorname{LT}(H)]
is the initial algebra of
{\mathbb{K}}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})]
,
it follows from
[
2
, Theorem A.16]
and
[
4
, Corollary 2.3]
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[\operatorname{LT}(H)])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\}\cup\{x_{i_{1}}p_{j_{1}}q_{i_{2}}y_{j_{2}}\mid T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R\}])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\}\cup\{x_{i_{1}}p_{j_{1}}q_{i_{2}}y_{j_{2}}\mid T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R\}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\}\cup\{p_{j_{1}}q_{i_{2}}\mid T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R\}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\}))+\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j_{1}}q_{i_{2}}\mid T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R\})).

Since
\#{\bm{P}}_{NW}+\#{\bm{P}}_{SE}=\#{\bm{P}}
, it remains to show that

(5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\}))

\displaystyle=\#{\bm{P}}_{NW}

and

(6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j_{1}}q_{i_{2}}\mid T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R\}))

\displaystyle=\#{\bm{P}}_{SE}.

The remaining proof is divided into two parts.

(1)

Let us first prove the equality in (
5
).
If
(i,j),(i,j+1)\in\Gamma
, we write
(i,j)\sim(i,j+1)
. Likewise, if
(i,j),(i+1,j)\in\Gamma
, we also write
(i,j)\sim(i+1,j)
. Clearly,
\sim
generates an equivalence on the cells of
\Gamma
. The induced equivalence classes will be called
edge-connected components
. Suppose that
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{t}
are the edge-connected components of
\Gamma
. The following are clear:

(a)

each
\Gamma_{k}
is still a skew Ferrers diagram;

(b)

\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\}))=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid(i,j)\in\Gamma_{k}\}))
;

(c)

{\bm{P}}_{NW}(\Gamma)\cap\Gamma_{k}={\bm{P}}_{NW}(\Gamma_{k})
.

Therefore, to establish (
5
), it suffices to assume that
k=1
and
\Gamma
is edge-connected.

Without loss of generality, we can further assume that
\mu_{a}=0
. Whence, the northeast extreme cell of
\Gamma
is
(1,b)
and the southwest extreme cell of
\Gamma
is
(a,1)
. Meanwhile, the cells of
{\bm{P}}_{NW}
form a path connecting
(a,1)
with
(1,b)
inside
\Gamma
. In particular,
\#{\bm{P}}_{NW}=a+b-1
. We will order the cells on this path such that
u_{1}=(1,b)
and
u_{a+b-1}=(a,1)
. For each
k=1,2,\dots,a-b-2
, if
u_{k}=(i,j)
, then
u_{k+1}=(i,j-1)
or
(i+1,j)
. Whence
\varphi^{*}(T_{u_{k+1}})/\varphi^{*}(T_{u_{k}})=y_{j-1}/y_{j}
or
x_{i+1}/x_{i}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{K}}(x_{i}y_{j}\mid(i,j)\in{\bm{P}}_{NW})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{K}}\left(x_{1}y_{b},\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}},\frac{x_{3}}{x_{2}},\dots,\frac{x_{a}}{x_{a-1}},\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}},\dots,\frac{y_{b-2}}{y_{b-1}},\frac{y_{b-1}}{y_{b}}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{K}}\left(x_{1}y_{b},\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}},\frac{x_{3}}{x_{1}},\dots,\frac{x_{a}}{x_{1}},\frac{y_{1}}{y_{b}},\dots,\frac{y_{b-2}}{y_{b}},\frac{y_{b-1}}{y_{b}}\right).

Denote this field by
K
. It follows that
\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}(K)=a+b-1=\#{\bm{P}}_{NW}
.

For every
(i+1,j+1)\in\Gamma\setminus{\bm{P}}_{NW}
, we have
(i,j),(i+1,j),(i,j+1)\in\Gamma
. Furthermore, from
\varphi^{*}(T_{i,j}),\varphi^{*}(T_{i+1,j}),\varphi^{*}(T_{i,j+1})\in K
, we see that

\varphi^{*}(T_{i+1,j+1})=\varphi^{*}(T_{i+1,j})\varphi^{*}(T_{i,j+1})/\varphi^{*}(T_{i,j})\in K.

Therefore, since
\varphi^{\ast}(T_{i,j})\in K
for every
(i,j)\in{\bm{P}}_{NW}
, it follows by easy induction that
\varphi^{\ast}(T_{i,j})\in K
for every
(i,j)\in\Gamma
. In particular,
K={\mathbb{K}}(\{x_{i}y_{j}\mid T_{i,j}\in R\})
. In summary, we have established the equality in (
5
).

(2)

Secondly, we prove the equality in (
6
). Note that if
T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R
, then all cells in the rectangular region determined by
(i_{1},j_{1})
and
(i_{2},j_{2})
are contained in
\Gamma
. In particular, the rectangular region determined by
(i_{2}-1,j_{1};i_{2},j_{1}+1)
is contained in
\Gamma
. In other words,
T_{i_{2}-1,j_{1};i_{2},j_{1}+1}\in R
. Observe that

\mathcal{QP}_{T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}}=q_{i_{2}}p_{j_{1}}=\mathcal{QP}_{T_{i_{2}-1,j_{1};i_{2},j_{1}+1}}.

Inspired by this observation, we refer to the set
\{(i-1,j-1),(i-1,j),(i,j-1),(i,j)\}
as an
adjacent square region
, denoted by
\mathscr{A}_{i,j}
.
Therefore,

{\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j_{1}}q_{i_{2}}\mid T_{i_{1},j_{1};i_{2},j_{2}}\in R\})={\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j-1}q_{i}\mid\mathscr{A}_{i,j}\subseteq\Gamma\})\cong{\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j}q_{i}\mid\mathscr{A}_{i,j}\subseteq\Gamma\}).

Notice that
\mathscr{A}_{i,j}\subseteq\Gamma
if and only if
(i,j)\in\Gamma^{\prime}\coloneqq\Gamma\setminus{\bm{P}}_{NW}
.
Furthermore, we introduce the rotated diagram
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\{(a+1-i,b+1-j)\mid(i,j)\in\Gamma^{\prime}\}
.
It follows from (
5
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j}q_{i}\mid\mathscr{A}_{i,j}\subseteq\Gamma\}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{j}q_{i}\mid(i,j)\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}\}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{p_{b+1-j}q_{a+1-i}\mid(i,j)\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}\}))

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{under obvious}}_{\text{isomorphism}}}\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}(\{y_{j}x_{i}\mid(i,j)\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\}))

\displaystyle=\#{\bm{P}}_{NW}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}).

It remains to notice that
{\bm{P}}_{NW}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})
is precisely the
{\bm{P}}_{SE}(\Gamma)
under the rotation. In particular,

\#{\bm{P}}_{NW}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})=\#{\bm{P}}_{SE}(\Gamma)
, and we have thus established the equality in (
6
). This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

If the Ferrers graph
\mathcal{G}
is complete bipartite, then
\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[G(J_{\mathcal{G}})])=2(a+b-2)

a\times b

Question 1
.

An interesting problem is to classify when the binomial edge ring of a bipartite graph is a Koszul, Cohen–Macaulay, and normal ring.
Additionally, it would be valuable to investigate the configurations for which the binomial edge ring admits a
Sagbi
basis, such that its initial algebra has a defining ideal generated by a quadratic Gröbner basis with a squarefree initial ideal.
Furthermore, can one determine a dimension formula in terms of the combinatorial data of the bipartite graph?

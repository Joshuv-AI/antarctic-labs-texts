Regional insights into hepatitis E: seroprevalence without clinical burden in Iran and Iraq
Authors: Nawfal R Hussein, Halder J Abozait
Published: 2026

To the editor

We read with interest the paper by Amiri et al. on the seroprevalence of hepatitis E virus (HEV) infection among solid organ transplant (SOT) recipients in southern Iran (1). The 35.3% anti-HEV IgG seropositivity reported is notable and much higher than in general Iranian population.

While the paper provides valuable epidemiological data, the clinical significance of HEV infection in Iran remains controversial. This pattern is comparable to that observed in neighboring Iraq, where seroprevalence studies have revealed widespread exposure to HEV in the general population, yet clinically apparent disease is extremely rare (2). The regional experience suggests that, although HEV circulates in the population, progression to chronic infection or severe disease in immunocompromised patients appears to be uncommon. Importantly, all the patients in the current study were negative for anti‑HEV IgM, suggesting the absence of active or recent infection. Without testing for HEV RNA, chronic infection—arguably the most clinically relevant concern in immunocompromised patients—cannot be confirmed. Therefore, the isolated detection of anti-HEV IgG should be interpreted with caution, as it indicates past exposure rather than active clinical disease.

The observed correlation with elevated blood urea nitrogen is intriguing but likely attributable to confounding factors such as age or comorbid conditions rather than an effect of HEV exposure. Prospective studies incorporating HEV RNA detection is needed to determine chronic infection contributes to impaired renal or graft function after SOT. In conclusion, although the study reports higher HEV exposure among transplant recipients in southern Iran, the clinical impact appears to be limited. Routine screening for HEV in this population does not appear to be warranted until more compelling evidence becomes available.

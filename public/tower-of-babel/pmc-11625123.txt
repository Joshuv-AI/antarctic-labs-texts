Investigating the plasma electrophoretic protein profile of kids and lambs during the neonatal period: An overview of their immuno-inflammatory status
Authors: Francesca Arfuso, Maria Rizzo, Claudia Giannetto, Marilena Briglia, Elisabetta Giudice, Alessandro Zumbo, Anna Assenza, Giuseppe Piccione
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study aimed to evaluate how plasma total proteins, their fractions (i.e. albumin, α1-, β-, γ-globulins) and A/G ratio change in lambs and kids over the first month of life. Ten female Maltese kids and 10 female Comisana lambs were enrolled in the study. Starting from the first day after birth, and for a period of 4 weeks, blood samples were taken from each subject. Specifically, for each kid and lamb, blood sampling was performed within 30 min after birth (T0) (pre-colostral), after colostrum intake (T1), and, weekly, for the subsequent 28 days (T7, T14, T21 and T28). The application of two-way analysis of variance for repeated measures (ANOVA) showed the effect of time in the kids and lambs for the values of plasma total proteins, albumin, α1-, β-, γ-globulins and A/G ratio (p < 0.05). A significant species effect was recorded for α1-and β-globulins (p < 0.05). Specifically, kids showed higher α1-globulins levels at T0, T14, T21 and T28, and higher and β-globulins at T21 and T28 than lambs. The variations found in the levels of plasma total proteins and their fractions in kids and lambs could be related to the physiological changes likely to occur in a newborn animal following transition from the uterine environment to the free-living state. The variability on changes found in α1-and β-globulins measured in kid and lamb seems to suggest that the first one would tend to have a higher acute phase response than lamb stressing the difference between these two small ruminant species.Keywords: Small ruminants, Neonatal period, Globulins, Goat, Proteins, Sheep

Introduction

The neonatal period represents a critical phase in the development of many of the physiological functions of every animal. During this phase, better known as the “period of adaptation,” numerous necessary changes occur for a physiological response to the demands imposed by extra-uterine life [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]. In some animal species, the adaptation period appears to progress more slowly, such that there are substantial differences between one species and the other ones [6,7]. During the neonatal period, newborns have an immature immune system making it more susceptible to various infectious diseases [5,8], and, they are unstable from a metabolic point of view, making them more predisposed to diseases that can cause an increase of mortality [9], the incidence of which is in fact greater during the first three months of life [10]. The high morbidity, as well as the possible and consequent mortality, therefore represent, for farms, the major causes of economic loss, besides representing a welfare concern [9,11]. On this regard, among livestock species, lambs and kids showed higher mortality rate than other farm species, indeed, the mortality rate reaches a peak during the first 2–24 h after birth in these small ruminants reflecting mostly cardio-respiratory and thermoregulatory disorders [9]. Breed, sex as well as birth weight are important factors that influence both viability and growth [12]. Also worthy of consideration is the importance of a timely and correct intake of colostrum after birth for the success of the newborn in adapting to extra-uterine life. On this regards, colostrum plays crucial immunological function by providing newborns of immune function-related low-abundance proteins [1,13]. Furthermore, it has been suggested that some acute phase proteins (APPs) could transfer directly to newborn via the colostrum, thus modulating the immune response [14,15]. In small ruminants, the major positive APPs are haptoglobin and serum amyloid A, while among the negative APPs, albumin is the most representative [16]. These proteins together with other APPs are the actors of the well-orchestrated and synchronized process, known as acute phase response (APR). Though the APPs are primarily synthesized by hepatocytes as part of the acute phase response, other cell types and/or tissues (i.e. mammary gland, adipose tissue, digestive and respiratory tracts) have been suggested to be source of these proteins [[16], [17], [18]]. To date, there are numerous species-specific ELISA kits commercially available for the determination of APPs, however they are expensive and require an operator with some experience in ELISA investigations. Noteworthy, the electrophoresis of plasma proteins is an easy, fast and inexpensive technique that generates a photograph of the animal's globulin fractions giving a first idea of the animal's immune-inflammatory status [[19], [20], [21], [22], [23]]. As a matter of fact, plasma proteins include albumin and α-, β- and γ-globulin fractions which host indirect markers of activation of inflammation (APPs) and immunity (antibodies) [24]. Therefore, the analysis of the electrophoretic profile of plasma proteins seems to be of paramount importance in veterinary practice to assess the overall health status of newborns and it looks to have all the prerequisites to become a routine investigation. From the analysis of specific literature, it turns out that numerous investigations were conducted during the neonatal period in the lamb, in kid and calf, in order to evaluate the changes in many physiological and hematochemical parameters [4,[25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30], [31]]. However, to the best of Authors knowledge, information of the possible differences in the electrophoretic protein pattern between kids and lambs during neonatal period are missing in the scientific literature.

In light of the above, the current study aimed to monitor how the trend of plasma total proteins and the globulin fractions hosting the main APPs, change in kids and lambs over the first month of life. Also, the possible influence of species on investigated parameters was assessed. The obtained evidence-based data would support the possible usefulness of plasma protein profile measurements for the evaluation of health status in newborns kids and lambs.

Material and methods

Animals

Singleton female kids (Maltese breed) and singleton female lambs (Comisana breed) were selected for the study. Animal born from multiparous Maltese goats and Comisana sheep, respectively, on two farms, situated in the same location (northeast Sicily, 37°43′24″ N, 13°26′05″ E; 966 m above sea level). The two farms, where the goats and sheep were kept, have the same management practice, including feed composition and housing. Parturition proceeded without complications and without external obstetrical assistance. All dams proceeded with immediate neonatal care, newborns suckled freely and no stimulation procedure after birth has been needed. All kids and lambs were born at term in April (sunrise: 06:40; sunset: 19:29) at similar gestational age (kids: 152 ± 3 days; lambs: 148 ± 2 days). They were kept in boxes with their dam in order to guarantee adequate mothering and to minimize disruption. By means of a data logger (Gemini, UK), the thermal and hygrometric parameters were assessed inside the box for the whole study, agreeing with the normal seasonal pattern for the location (minimum and maximum temperature between 12.1 and 20.2 °C, mean relative humidity of 61.1 %). The health status of kids and lambs was valuated at birth based on behavior, rectal temperature, heart and respiratory profile, cough, nasal discharge, ocular discharge, appetite, faecal consistency, navel examination, and hematological profile (data not shown). Kids and lambs enrolled in the study were healthy and were fed only with colostrum and maternal milk. Water was available ad libitum.

Experimental design

A total of 20 newborn clinically healthy Maltese kids (n = 10) and Comisana lambs (n = 10) were enrolled in the study. During the study, the kids and lambs were housed together with their dams from which they were allowed to suck freely. For each kid and lamb, blood sampling was performed by the same operator within 30 min after birth (T0) (pre-colostral); subsequently, the kids and lambs were fed colostrum by voluntary sucking of their dams, and, 24 h after colostrum intake, the second blood sampling was performed from each kid and lamb (T1). Blood sampling was continued, weekly, for the subsequent 28 days (T7, T14, T21 and T28), at the same time (08:00), for each kid and lamb. The blood samples were collected by jugular venipuncture into lithium heparin vacutainer test tubes which were allowed to clot at room temperature, and then centrifuged at 2325×g for 15 min to separate plasma. The harvested plasma was dispensed into plastic tubes, and stored at −20 °C until analyzed for total protein concentrations and for the identification of protein fractions. The total protein concentration was measured through an automated UV spectrophotometer (Slim, SEAC, Florence, Italy) using the Biuret method with commercially available kit and using the bovine albumin (6.02 g/dL) as standard (Biosystems S.A., Barcelona, Spain). Electrophoresis for protein fraction assessment was performed using an automated system (Selvet 24, Seleo Engineering, Naples, Italy) according to the procedures described by the manufacturer and as previously performed in goat and sheep [32,33].

Data analysis

Two-way repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied to assess statistical significant effect of species and of time after birth on the values of total proteins, their electrophoretic fractions (i.e. albumin, α1-, α2-, β-, and γ-globulins) and A/G ratio obtained from newborn kids and lambs throughout the first 4 weeks of life (T0-T28). Bonferroni multiple comparison test was applied for post hoc comparison. p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The statistical analysis was performed using the software Prism v. 9.00 (Graphpad Software Ltd., San Diego, CA, USA, 2020).

Results

The results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (±SD). The effect of time on total proteins, albumin, α1-, β-, γ-globulins and A/G ratio in both kids and lambs (p < 0.05) was found. In particular, as showed in Table 1, the total proteins, albumin and γ-globulins showed an increasing trend after colostrum intake (T1) until the end of the experimental period (T28) in both species (p < 0.05). The α1-globulins showed increasing values starting from T14 until T28 both in kids and lambs; β-globulins values increased staring from T7 until T28 in kids (p < 0.05), whereas this protein fraction showed higher values at T14 and T21 than the other time points in lambs (p < 0.05). A/G ratio resulted higher at T1 and T7 than the other time points in both kids and lambs (p < 0.05). A significant effect of species was recorded for α1-and β-globulins (p < 0.05). In particular, as displayed in Fig. 2, kids showed higher α1-globulins levels at T0, T14, T21 and T28, and higher β-globulins at T21 and T28 than lambs (p < 0.05). The values of total proteins and of albumin, α2-and γ-globulins protein fractions were not statistically significant different between the two species (p > 0.05). Also the A/G ratio values showed no statistically significant differences between kids and lambs (p > 0.05, Fig. 3).

Discussion

The results herein obtained make a contribution to the current knowledge on the modifications of the electrophoretic profile of plasma proteins in kid and lamb during the first month of life, providing useful indications for the monitoring of these parameters whose homeostasis, still evolving, must be dynamically interpreted function of the neonatal adaptation period. Under the conditions of this study, despite the observed changes, all newborn animals showed a good state of health and exhibited good homeostatic ability and were able to maintain the assessed parameters within the physiological limits. As a matter of facts, the magnitude of the changes observed in the electrophoretic prolife of investigated kids and lambs does not appear high enough to be of clinical relevance, but rather, they would seem to be linked to the physiological adjustments that newborns undergo to adapt to the new extra-uterine life condition. Specifically, according to the findings herein gathered, the total proteins together with albumin, α1-, β-, γ-globulins showed an increasing trend following colostrum intake until the end of the monitoring period in both species confirming the important role of colostrum as source of higher-abundance proteins, particularly albumin and immunoglobulins, and low-abundance proteins including APPs hosted in the α- and β-globulin fractions [34]. In agreement with the results herein found, it has been stated that early colostrum intake produced an increase of non-immunoglobulin proteins as the apoliprotein A-IV, serum amyloid A, plasminogen, and fibrinogen in lamb blood plasma [15], which are known to migrate in α- and β-globulin fractions when subjecting to electrophoretic migration [4,20]. A study focused on assessing the differences in low abundance proteins between colostrum and milk from cows, clearly showed that some of them, including fibrinogen, were only present in colostrum [36]. This finding seems to justify the increase of plasma levels of β-globulin fraction in kids and lambs following colostrum intake. Fibrinogen, beyond being a substrate in the clotting cascade, also carries out a defensive function, having been demonstrated that fibrinogen concentration increases during acute-phase reactions [[37], [38], [39]]. After the first colostrum intake, other protein fractions, including immunoglobulins, are absorbed from the intestine, thus their blood concentration in kids and lamb increase [25] improving the resistance to infectious diseases as well as survivability of the offspring [35].

It must be considered γ-globulin values found in kids and lambs at time point 0 (considered as the time point before colostrum intake) are higher compared to previous studies. Though the presence of γ-globulins in higher concentrations in plasma samples of newborns before colostrum intake could indicate placental lesions, the higher values found in this study compared to the results of other ones [21,22,25,35] could be related to individual factors such as birth body weight, sex, and dam parity, which may affect the protein profile of the newborn. Moreover, the breeds herein investigated are native breeds in Sicily, thus, the difference could be related to breed, as well. However, though the possible variations in breed, litter size or birth body weight could be hypothesized to affect the values of protein fractions of newborns, some animals may have ingested colostrum before they were sampled in view of the high standard deviations observed within group. Newborns had to be separated from their dams at birth to avoid this type of problem, but it was decided to leave the newborn and dam together so as not to create problems in the dam-offspring interaction that could negatively affect the adaptation of newborn to the new environment with repercussions on the health and the welfare of the newborn.

Considering the above, the importance of colostrum intake in the first 24 h of life is strengthened since it allows for the sincrease of α-, β- and γ-globulin fractions may benefit the newborn immune system efficiency. The variations in the relative concentrations of albumin and globulins reflect the changes found in the A/G ratio. The ratio differences observed at birth and at the first week of life respect to the other time points could be due to the influence of the immunoglobulins absorbed from colostrum. The A/G ratio is the first pointer of kidney and liver illnesses, therefore, it must be interpreted carefully with attention paid to which part of the ratio has changed because it allows systematic classification and identification of dysproteinemias [24,40].

Worthy of note, the variability on changes found in globulin fractions known to host the main APPs (i.e. α- and β-globulins) measured in lambs and kids stresses the difference between these two small ruminant species frequently considered as similar. As a matter of fact, kids showed higher α1-globulins levels at T14, T21 and T28, and higher β-globulins levels at T21 and T28 than lambs. Though it has been shown that the Comisana sheep's milk is richer in protein content (5.77 %) than the Maltese goat's milk (3.14 %) [41], probably, this not reflect the content of the low-abundance proteins. Moreover, considering that the difference in α- and β-globulins between the two species showed a statistical significance after the two and/or three weeks of age it could be hypothesized that the endogenous production of APPs by kids was greater than lambs. As a matter of facts, following the transmission of higher-abundance and low-abundance proteins through colostrum intake, animals must provide for their endogenous synthesis; though it is well established that APPs are synthetized by several tissues including the adipose tissue, the digestive and respiratory tracts, the primarily source of APPs is represented by hepatocytes. The role of the liver in the metabolism, development and immunity is well recognized in kids [42]. It has been assumed that the kid's liver cells grow fast at 2 and 8 weeks of age and these cells play a key role in the normal immune responses being involved in the Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation signaling pathways [42]. The hepatic metabolic activity is low in newborn lambs and that it increases with growth [43]. Even though ontogenic events cannot certainly be discharged, the increase in hepatic metabolic activity is most likely a consequence of rumen development reflecting the variations in the quantity and kind of substrates (i.e. fatty acids and glucose) metabolized by the liver [43]. Noteworthy, though the species-related changes observed in the α- and β-globulins, A/G ratio showed similar values in kids and lambs; the missing statistical variation in this ratio between the two small ruminant species is probably related to the not large magnitude of the changes observed in globulin fractions. Though the two small ruminant species were housed in two farms, and this could be considered as a limitation of the investigation, the parameters investigated in kids and lambs during the neonatal period are mainly influenced by the dam's diet, by the protein transfer in breast milk and by the ability of newborns to adapt to the new extra-uterine environment. In the current study the two selected farms were in close proximity with the same management practice, including feed composition and housing; therefore, the characteristics of the pasture as well as the microclimatic variables were superimposable. In view of the above, it can be assumed that the differences found in the α- and β-globulins between lambs and kids could be attributable to the species. Further studies assessing whether the plasma proteins change in kids and lambs for a longer monitoring period are advocated considering that organ functions in newborn are in a developing phase.

Conclusion

The transition from the stable and protected intra-uterine life, during which the fetus received nutrients via the placenta, to the free-living environment is probably the most profound adjustment the newborn has to face. According to results herein gathered, the immune-inflammatory parameters (i.e. albumin, α1-, α2-, β- and γ-globulins) showed an increasing trend starting from the intake of colostrum until the end of the monitoring period in both small ruminant species. Noteworthy, the variability on changes observed in α1-and β-globulins from kids and lambs seems to suggest that the first would tend to have a higher acute phase response than lambs stressing the difference between these two small ruminant species frequently considered as similar.

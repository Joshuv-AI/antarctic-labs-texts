Sex-specific trends in incidence and mortality of interstitial lung disease and pulmonary sarcoidosis in China compared with global estimates, 1990–2023: an analysis of GBD 2023
Authors: Jian Mao, Chunwei Chen, Jingwen Lan, Xiang Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInterstitial lung disease and pulmonary sarcoidosis (ILD/PS) constitute a heterogeneous group of diffuse parenchymal disorders with rising public health importance amid global ageing, air pollution, and post-coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) respiratory sequelae. China, with the world’s largest ageing population and a complex environmental exposure profile, faces particular challenges, yet up-to-date comparisons against international trends remain scarce. Using newly released Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 estimates, we reassessed long-term trends in ILD/PS burden in China and globally from 1990 to 2023, with a focus on sex differences and the unexpected fluctuation in 2023.MethodsAnnual age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), age-standardized death rate (ASDR), and age-standardized disability-adjusted life year rate (ASDALY) per 100,000 population, together with absolute counts, were extracted from the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) GBD Results Tool for the cause “interstitial lung disease and pulmonary sarcoidosis” (Cause ID 516), stratified by sex. GBD estimates draw on vital registration, China’s Disease Surveillance Points (DSP) system, censuses, and published studies, with standardized garbage-code redistribution. Long-term trends were quantified using estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) from log-linear regression. Turning points in China’s ASDR were assessed using segmented (joinpoint-like) regression. Mortality-to-incidence ratio (MIR) and endpoint sensitivity analyses (ending in 2021, 2022, and 2023) were used to evaluate the robustness of the 2023 fluctuation.ResultsIn 2023, global ASIR and ASDR were 4.75 and 2.40 per 100,000, whereas China had lower ASIR (2.12 per 100,000) and ASDR (0.46 per 100,000). China’s ASDR increased from 0.18 to 0.46 per 100,000 during 1990–2023 [EAPC 3.55%, 95% confidence interval (CI) 3.27–3.82], exceeding the global increase (EAPC 1.96%, 95% CI: 1.81–2.11%). Although males generally had higher incidence and mortality rates, the fastest rise in mortality occurred among females in China (ASDR EAPC 5.18%, 95% CI: 4.84–5.51%), markedly higher than Chinese males (2.60%, 95% CI: 2.37–2.84%) and global females (2.05%, 95% CI: 1.90–2.20%). A turning point was suggested around 2011, with slower but persistent increases thereafter. China’s MIR rose from 0.09 (1990) to 0.22 (2023), with an anomalous increase in 2023, coinciding with a decline in ASIR.ConclusionsILD/PS burden in China has risen steadily over three decades, with mortality increasing faster than the global average and a particularly rapid rise among females. To address these disparities, China may consider expanding access to high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) and multidisciplinary diagnostic teams in primary and secondary care, implementing sex- and age-targeted case-finding programmes for older women with cumulative biomass and ambient air pollution exposure, sustaining ambient air-quality improvements, and strengthening post-COVID-19 respiratory surveillance through linkage with clinical registries.Keywords: Global Burden of Disease (GBD), interstitial lung disease (ILD), sarcoidosis, mortality, incidence, disability-adjusted life years (DALY), sex differences, post-coronavirus disease 2019 era (post-COVID-19 era)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Using Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 data, the burden of interstitial lung disease and pulmonary sarcoidosis (ILD/PS) in China rose substantially from 1990 to 2023. The age-standardized death rate (ASDR) increased from 0.18 to 0.46 per 100,000 [estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) 3.55%, 95% confidence interval (CI): 3.27–3.82%], faster than the global rise (EAPC 1.96%). The fastest mortality increase occurred among Chinese females (ASDR EAPC 5.18%). A turning point around 2011 was identified, followed by a slower but persistent upward trend. In 2023, the mortality-to-incidence ratio (MIR) showed an anomalous rise that coincided with a decline in the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR).

What is known and what is new?

• ILD/PS burden has been rising globally alongside population ageing and air pollution; previous GBD analyses ended in 2017, 2019, or 2021, without capturing post-coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) dynamics or the latest year-to-year fluctuations.

• By using GBD 2023, we document a faster mortality rise in China than globally, a pronounced acceleration among Chinese females, a 2011 deceleration turning point, and a marked 2023 fluctuation in incidence and MIR suggestive of estimation instability or post-COVID-19 case-ascertainment effects.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• China should expand high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) and multidisciplinary diagnostic capacity in primary and secondary care, implement sex- and age-targeted case-finding for older women with cumulative biomass and ambient air pollution exposure, sustain air-quality improvements, and strengthen post-COVID-19 respiratory surveillance through linkage with clinical registries.

Introduction

Interstitial lung disease and pulmonary sarcoidosis (ILD/PS) comprise a heterogeneous group of diffuse parenchymal lung disorders characterized by varying degrees of interstitial inflammation, granulomatous involvement, and/or fibrosis, which may lead to progressive impairment of pulmonary function (1). Clinically, patients may present with exertional dyspnea, progressive decline in lung function, and even respiratory failure (1). With accelerating population ageing, worsening air pollution, and increasing demand for chronic respiratory disease management, the burden of ILD/PS has become an increasingly important public health concern (2). However, long-term patterns of disease burden vary substantially across countries and regions (3), underscoring the need for context-specific policies and resource allocation (4).

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study integrates multi-source data worldwide within a unified estimation framework, providing a key evidence base for assessing long-term disease burden (5). In recent years, several studies have used GBD data to describe trends in incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) attributable to ILD/PS (6,7). Nevertheless, many prior analyses have been limited by the lack of the most recent estimates, largely overlapping conclusions, and insufficient attention to year-to-year changes in the latest time points, thereby reducing the timeliness and policy relevance of their findings. With the release of GBD 2023, updated estimates for 2022–2023 are now available (5), making it necessary to re-evaluate ILD/PS burden from 1990 to 2023. This reassessment is particularly important for China, given its large population size, rapidly ageing demographics, and complex exposure profile (8), where up-to-date evidence on long-term trends and population disparities is essential for disease surveillance and health strategy planning. Previous GBD-based analyses of ILD/PS have largely relied on datasets ending in 2017, 2019, or 2021 and have provided substantially overlapping conclusions (9,10), without incorporating the post-pandemic recovery period or the most recent year-to-year fluctuations now available in GBD 2023 (11). Moreover, the burden of ILD/PS is strongly shaped by global health inequity and the “diagnostic gap” between high- and low-resource settings: high-Socio-demographic Index (SDI) regions report higher incidence partly because they possess multidisciplinary diagnostic teams, high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), and pulmonary function laboratories, whereas low-SDI regions likely underestimate the true burden owing to limited diagnostic infrastructure (10). Disentangling whether observed increases reflect genuine epidemiological shifts or improvements in surveillance and case ascertainment is therefore essential, and is a central motivation for the present reassessment.

In addition, the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic and the subsequent period of sustained low-level transmission may have influenced ILD/PS burden estimates through multiple pathways (12). On the one hand, post-infection respiratory sequelae, inflammation, and immune dysregulation may alter disease spectra (13,14). On the other hand, changes in healthcare accessibility, care-seeking behavior, diagnostic delays, and shifts in surveillance data inputs during and after the pandemic may contribute to fluctuations in incidence estimates (15-17), thereby affecting multiple burden indicators. Because the GBD framework groups multiple ILD subtypes and PS into a single cause category, our results reflect the overall burden of this combined cause rather than trends in specific ILD subtypes. Notably, our preliminary analyses identified a marked year-to-year fluctuation in China’s estimated incidence in 2023, whereas mortality declined only slightly, resulting in an anomalous rise in the mortality-to-incidence ratio (MIR). This pattern suggests possible estimation instability, changes in case ascertainment, or effects related to the recovery phase of healthcare services in the post-COVID-19 era, and therefore warrants careful interpretation and robustness assessment.

Using GBD 2023 data, this study aimed to: (I) systematically assess long-term trends in the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), age-standardized death rate (ASDR), and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDALY) of ILD/PS in China and globally from 1990 to 2023; (II) characterize temporal changes in ILD/PS burden and examine whether sex disparities have widened over time; and (III) evaluate the robustness of long-term trend estimates by comparing results using alternative endpoints (2021, 2022, and 2023) and conducting comparative analyses to interpret the 2023 fluctuation. We hope that our findings would provide updated evidence to inform disease monitoring, identification of high-risk populations, and health resource allocation in the post-COVID-19 era, and offer clues for future validation using clinical registries and multi-source real-world data. We present this article in accordance with the GATHER reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0229/rc).

Methods

Study design and data sources

This study was a descriptive trend analysis based on the GBD 2023 database. The GBD 2023 study, led by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) at the University of Washington, integrates multi-source data within a standardized estimation framework to systematically quantify the burden of diseases, injuries, and risk factors across 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023 (5). The core estimation platform of GBD includes the Bayesian meta-regression tool DisMod-MR 2.1, together with complementary modeling approaches such as MR-BRT (Meta-Regression—Bayesian, Regularized, Trimmed), to synthesize heterogeneous inputs and enhance the internal consistency and comparability of estimates across locations, years, and population groups.

Mortality data are primarily derived from national vital registration systems, censuses, and sample registration or survey systems. Incidence and prevalence data are obtained from disease surveillance systems, national health surveys, and published studies. DALYs are calculated as the sum of years of life lost (YLLs) and years lived with disability (YLDs), with estimates informed by multiple sources including case notifications, hospital discharge databases, household surveys, and cohort studies. The design, data collection, and estimation methods of GBD 2023 follow the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER); further methodological details are available in the official GBD documentation and related publications (5).

We extracted data using the GBD Results Tool (Viz Hub https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results). We retrieved annual estimates for Cause ID 516, “interstitial lung disease and pulmonary sarcoidosis (ILD/PS)”, from 1990 to 2023 for China and the global population, stratified by sex (male, female, and both sexes). For each year, we extracted point estimates and corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs). For China, GBD 2023 incorporates mortality data from the national Disease Surveillance Points (DSP) system maintained by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, the Maternal and Child Health Surveillance System, and provincial vital registration, supplemented by census and survey data (18,19). To improve cause-of-death specificity, GBD applies a standardized “garbage-code redistribution” procedure that reassigns deaths coded to non-specific causes (e.g., “respiratory failure”, “ill-defined causes”) to specific underlying causes such as ILD/PS using algorithm- and regression-based approaches (20); this reduces misclassification but may introduce additional uncertainty in the early years of the time series, when vital registration coverage in China was less complete (19). Although the present analysis was conducted in 2026, the most recent GBD release is GBD 2023, which became publicly available in late 2025; this lag reflects the time required by IHME to clean, model, and validate multi-source data across 204 countries and territories (5).

Study population and outcome definitions

The study focused on ILD/PS as defined in the GBD 2023 cause hierarchy. In the GBD classification system, ILD/PS represents a combined cause category that aggregates multiple ILD subtypes and PS (21,22), enabling standardized comparisons of burden estimates across locations and over time.

The primary outcome measures were the ASIR, ASDR, and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDALY). All age-standardized rates were reported per 100,000 population and presented as point estimates with corresponding 95% UIs.

To complement trends in age-standardized rates, we also reported absolute burden measures, including the number of incident cases, deaths, and total DALYs, to provide a more intuitive description of changes in disease burden over time.

MIR is an ecological indicator and should not be interpreted as case-fatality; it may be influenced by changes in case ascertainment and cause-of-death assignment.

Statistical analysis

Trend description and year-to-year variation

We described temporal trends in the ASIR, ASDR, and ASDALY of ILD/PS in China and globally from 1990 to 2023, and reported corresponding absolute burden measures (incident cases, deaths, and total DALYs). All estimates were presented as point estimates with 95% UIs. Because GBD provides model-based uncertainty intervals rather than conventional sampling-based distributions, we present results descriptively and do not make dichotomous claims of statistical significance. Non-overlap of 95% UIs may suggest that two estimates differ, but overlap does not rule out meaningful differences; therefore, we focus on the magnitude and consistency of patterns over time.

Given the potential year-to-year fluctuations in the most recent estimates, we additionally reported sex-stratified numbers of incident cases, deaths, and total DALYs for China and globally in 2022 and 2023 to help interpret the observed changes in 2023 (see Table S1). In addition, results from the endpoint sensitivity analysis are presented in Table S2 and Figure S1.

Segmented regression (joinpoint-like analysis)

To assess potential non-linear changes in China’s ASDR trend, we fitted a segmented (joinpoint-like) log-linear regression model to the natural logarithm of ASDR using the R package segmented, allowing for one turning point. We pre-specified a maximum of one turning point to improve interpretability and reduce overfitting, given annual data over 1990–2023. The breakpoint was estimated using the default iterative procedure implemented in the segmented package, and model convergence was checked before deriving segment-specific annual percentage changes (APCs). Within the segmented R package, the breakpoint location is iteratively estimated by minimizing the residual sum of squares of the log-linear model; statistical significance of candidate joinpoints can additionally be assessed using a Monte Carlo permutation procedure analogous to that implemented in the U.S. National Cancer Institute’s Joinpoint software. The AAPC reported here was computed as a time-length-weighted average of segment-specific slopes on the log scale, providing a single summary measure over the full 1990–2023 period. Robustness analyses for the 2023 anomaly (sensitivity analyses)

Given that our preliminary analyses suggested a marked year-to-year fluctuation in China’s estimated ILD/PS incidence in 2023, we performed robustness analyses to assess whether this single-year anomaly influenced conclusions regarding long-term trends.

First, we conducted an endpoint sensitivity analysis by setting the end year of the trend analysis to 2021, 2022, and 2023, respectively. We recalculated EAPCs for the periods 1990–2021, 1990–2022, and 1990–2023, and compared the direction and magnitude of EAPCs across these models to evaluate the sensitivity of long-term trend estimates to the choice of endpoint year.

Second, we performed a year-to-year comparative analysis by comparing sex-stratified numbers of incident cases, deaths, and total DALYs between 2022 and 2023 for China and globally. Changes in the MIR were also examined to support a cautious interpretation of the 2023 fluctuation.

Software and statistical environment

Data management and statistical analyses were conducted using R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The main R packages used included tidyverse and segmented. Unless otherwise specified, results are presented as point estimates with corresponding UIs.

Ethics statement

This study used publicly available secondary data from GBD 2023 and did not involve identifiable individual-level information; therefore, ethical approval and informed consent were not required. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Age-specific mortality patterns by sex in China and globally, 1990–2023 (Figure 4)

Age-specific analyses showed that ILD/PS mortality rates increased markedly with advancing age in both China and the global population from 1990 to 2023 (Figure 4). Across all settings, mortality rates remained low in younger age groups, whereas substantially higher rates were observed among older adults, particularly those aged ≥80 years, indicating that long-term mortality increases were concentrated in the oldest age groups.

In both China and the global estimates, males consistently exhibited higher age-specific mortality rates than females across most age groups and calendar years. However, temporal patterns differed between China and the global population, especially among females. In China, mortality rates among females in older age groups showed a more sustained upward trend over time, whereas corresponding increases among females globally were comparatively less pronounced. Among males, age-specific mortality increases were evident in both China and globally, but tended to plateau or slow in later years, particularly in the oldest age groups.

Discussion

Using the latest GBD 2023 estimates, this study systematically assessed long-term trends in the burden of ILD/PS in China and globally from 1990 to 2023. The main findings were as follows: (I) although both ASIR and ASDR in China were lower than the global average in 2023, the long-term increase in mortality in China was substantially faster than that globally; (II) the increase in ASDR was markedly greater among females in China than among males in China and females globally; (III) segmented (joinpoint-like) regression identified 2011 as a turning point in China’s ASDR trend, with a slower but persistent increase thereafter; and (IV) an evident year-to-year fluctuation was observed in 2023, characterized by a decline in ASIR with only a modest change in ASDR, leading to an increase in the MIR. Endpoint sensitivity analyses further showed that repeating the analyses with 2021, 2022, and 2023 as alternative endpoints yielded consistent long-term trends, indicating that the overall increasing pattern was not driven by a single-year anomaly. Collectively, these findings provide updated evidence to inform ILD/PS surveillance and prevention strategies in China in the post-COVID-19 era.

Potential drivers of the faster increase in mortality burden in China

The more rapid increase in ILD/PS mortality in China may be related to demographic transitions and environmental changes (8,25). Population ageing in China likely contributes substantially to the rising mortality burden of fibrotic ILD (6), particularly idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) (26), given that the incidence and mortality of IPF increase steeply with age. In addition, along with industrialization and urbanization, accumulating evidence has linked worsening air pollution (e.g., PM2.5 and NO2) to the risk and progression of IPF (27), potentially through pathways involving oxidative stress, inflammation, and pro-fibrotic signaling (27). Nevertheless, it should be emphasized that GBD estimates do not include individual-level exposure information, and this study cannot conduct formal risk attribution analyses. Therefore, these explanations should be interpreted as plausible hypotheses supported by existing evidence. Beyond demographic and environmental drivers, changes in pharmacological management may also have shaped recent ILD/PS mortality trends. Since the approval of pirfenidone and nintedanib for IPF in the mid-2010s (28,29), antifibrotic therapy has been increasingly adopted worldwide, and both agents were included in China’s National Reimbursement Drug List in 2017 and 2020, respectively, substantially improving accessibility. Real-world and registry data suggest that antifibrotic use is associated with slower decline in forced vital capacity and reduced all-cause mortality in IPF and progressive pulmonary fibrosis (30,31). Concurrently, the historical reliance on combination therapy with prednisone, azathioprine, and N-acetylcysteine for IPF has declined following the PANTHER-IPF trial, which demonstrated increased risks of death and hospitalization with this regimen (32). The deceleration of ASDR growth in China after 2011, and particularly after the mid-2010s, may therefore partly reflect this therapeutic transition, although GBD data do not permit direct attribution. Future studies linking national prescription databases with mortality registries are warranted to quantify this contribution.

From a public health perspective, the observed trends suggest that ILD/PS prevention and control strategies in China should be aligned with the challenges of population ageing (7), and long-term chronic respiratory disease management frameworks should incorporate air pollution mitigation and occupational exposure control to reduce disease progression and mortality among high-risk populations (7).

Age- and sex-specific interpretation of ASDR trends

Figure 4 indicates that, in both China and the global population, increases in ILD/PS mortality were concentrated in older age groups, especially those aged ≥80 years, consistent with the steep age gradient of fibrotic ILDs (33). Notably, although China maintained lower age-specific mortality levels than the global average, mortality among Chinese females in the oldest age bands rose more persistently over time. Mathematically, given China’s comparatively low baseline mortality in 1990, this sustained increase in late-life female mortality yields a larger relative growth (and thus a higher EAPC) in female ASDR than globally (Figure 3), despite lower absolute levels. Several factors likely contribute to this pattern. First, cumulative environmental exposures may disproportionately impact the current cohort of older Chinese women. In particular, women born before the 1970s in rural China spent decades cooking and heating with solid fuels (wood, crop residue, coal) in poorly ventilated kitchens. Chronic inhalation of fine particulates and combustion-derived oxidants can trigger sustained alveolar inflammation, oxidative stress, and pro-fibrotic remodelling (35), with clinical manifestations typically emerging only after a multi-decadal latency (36). The current cohort of Chinese women aged ≥70 years, therefore, may represent one of the first large cohorts in which these cumulative exposures are fully expressed as fibrotic respiratory mortality, plausibly contributing to the disproportionately high female ASDR EAPC of 5.18%. Beyond historically high ambient pollution (37,38), long-term household air pollution from solid fuel use for cooking has specifically affected this demographic (39), with health impacts manifesting progressively at older ages (36). This is supported by systematic evidence linking PM2.5/NO2 exposures to IPF occurrence and progression (40). Second, diagnostic refinements play a crucial role. The GBD ILD/PS category includes female-predominant subtypes like connective tissue disease-associated ILD (41); improved recognition of these conditions, alongside widespread HRCT availability, has likely increased the specific attribution of respiratory deaths to ILD (42). Finally, the concurrent expansion and integration of China’s mortality surveillance system may have reduced misclassification, creating an apparent acceleration in recorded mortality (18).

The 2011 turning point: a signal of structural change, but interpretation should remain cautious

The temporal coincidence between the 2011 turning point and two structural transitions in China—near-universal health insurance coverage achieved by 2011 (43) and the launch of national air pollution control policies culminating in the 2013 Air Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan (44,45)—suggests that both healthcare access and environmental policy contributed to the post-2011 deceleration in ASDR growth.

The segmented regression identified 2011 as a pivotal turning point in China’s ASDR, characterized by a deceleration in the upward trend thereafter. Several transitions around the early 2010s likely contributed to this pattern. First, China’s health system reform substantially expanded health insurance coverage, achieving >95% population coverage by 2011 (43). This universal coverage likely improved access to diagnosis and long-term care for chronic respiratory diseases, thereby moderating mortality growth through better disease management. Concurrent improvements in diagnostic capacity and imaging resources may have further facilitated earlier recognition and intervention (46). Second, regarding environmental exposure, the Chinese government began strengthening air pollution control around 2010 (47), followed by the comprehensive ‘Air Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan’ in 2013 (44). While respiratory outcomes typically exhibit a lag, these actions may have altered longer-term pollution trajectories, with health benefits serving to sustain the deceleration in mortality rates. Therefore, the 2011 turning point should be interpreted as a signal of cumulative structural shifts rather than the result of a single event.

Additionally, endpoint sensitivity analyses confirmed that the overall increasing pattern remained robust to the choice of the final follow-up year.

Year-to-year fluctuation in 2023 and potential post-COVID-19 effects: estimation instability and real change may both contribute

A pronounced year-to-year fluctuation was observed in China in 2023: ASIR declined substantially compared with 2022, whereas ASDR decreased only slightly, resulting in an increase in MIR. This pattern is most consistent with changes in case ascertainment rather than an abrupt shift in underlying fatality. Identification of ILD/PS relies on diagnostic work-up (e.g., imaging and pulmonary function testing) (42), and disruptions in health-care utilization during and after the COVID-19 pandemic have been documented in China (48), which could lead to delayed presentation and under-detection of mild or early-stage cases in a given year. Meanwhile, the absence of an increase in cause-specific ASDR does not necessarily contradict reports of worse outcomes among ILD patients after SARS-CoV-2 infection (49). According to the World Health Organization guidelines for certification and classification of COVID-19 as a cause of death, when COVID-19 is considered the initiating condition in the causal chain leading to death, it should be recorded as the underlying cause, while chronic respiratory diseases such as ILD/PS are listed as contributing conditions (50). As a result, deaths among individuals with ILD/PS following COVID-19 may be preferentially attributed to COVID-19 rather than to ILD/PS as the underlying cause, leading to a redistribution of deaths across causes and relatively stable or slightly declining cause-specific ILD/PS mortality estimates. In addition, cause-of-death redistribution and updated estimation procedures within the GBD framework may influence cause-specific mortality patterns in late-pandemic and post-pandemic years, increasing volatility in the most recent estimates (51).

Because GBD estimates are not linked to individual-level data on health-care access, diagnostic delay, or certification practice, these explanations remain hypotheses and should be validated using clinical registries, vital registration data, and linked administrative datasets. Notably, endpoint sensitivity analyses yielded consistent long-term trends, suggesting that the overall conclusions are not driven by this single-year anomaly.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, GBD 2023 provides model-based estimates, and results depend on the quality and availability of underlying data sources; variations in data completeness across countries and years may increase uncertainty. Second, the GBD cause category ILD/PS aggregates multiple ILD subtypes and PS, and therefore our findings reflect the overall trends of this combined category rather than subtype-specific patterns (e.g., IPF, connective tissue disease-associated ILD, or sarcoidosis). Third, individual-level information on exposures, treatment, and clinical severity is not available in GBD; thus, explanations related to air pollution, household exposures, secondhand smoke, or healthcare disparities require further investigation. Despite these limitations, our study provides updated evidence on long-term trends and evolving sex differences in ILD/PS burden in China and globally based on the latest GBD 2023 estimates, and may inform public health monitoring and policy planning. Fourth, our comparison framework follows the established convention in the GBD literature in which national estimates are contrasted with the standardized GBD “global” reference rather than with a recomputed “rest-of-the-world” denominator. Because China contributes only approximately 10.5% of global incident ILD/PS cases and 4.8% of global deaths in 2023, and because Chinese ASIR and ASDR are markedly lower than the global averages, the inclusion of China within the global denominator is unlikely to materially bias the comparison; if anything, removing China would slightly widen the apparent gap and reinforce our central finding. Readers should nonetheless interpret the “global” figures as a standardized international reference benchmark rather than as an independent rest-of-the-world average.

Conclusions

In summary, the burden of ILD/PS in China increased steadily from 1990 to 2023, with mortality rising substantially faster than the global average. Sex-stratified analyses showed that although males generally had higher age-standardized rates, the increase in mortality was more rapid among females, suggesting that sex disparities may further widen over time. The marked fluctuation observed in 2023—characterized by declining incidence and an increased MIR—should be interpreted with caution, and may reflect changes in case ascertainment, healthcare recovery dynamics, or estimation instability in the post-COVID-19 era. Future priorities should include strengthening high-quality ILD/PS surveillance systems, addressing potential exposure and care disparities among women, reducing indoor and ambient air pollution and other relevant exposures, and improving early identification and standardized management, alongside enhanced respiratory infection prevention to mitigate long-term mortality and disability burden.

Concretely, expanding HRCT availability and multidisciplinary diagnostic capacity in primary and secondary care, implementing sex- and age-targeted case-finding for older women with historical biomass exposure, and integrating GBD trends with clinical registry data should be considered priorities for ILD/PS control in China.

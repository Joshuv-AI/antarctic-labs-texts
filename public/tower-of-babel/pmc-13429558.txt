Temporal shifts in diagnostic testing for cardiac amyloidosis across U.S. health systems
Authors: Issam Motairek, Bryan Abadie, Andrew Higgins, Zain N Jawad, Pavan Bhat, Andres Carmona Rubio, Mazen Hanna, Wael A Jaber
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Cardiac amyloidosis has evolved from a rare cause of restrictive cardiomyopathy to a key focus in heart failure practice. The widespread adoption of technetium-99 m (Tc-99 m)–based bone scintigraphy has enabled accurate, noninvasive identification of transthyretin cardiac amyloidosis (ATTR-CA) (Heidenreich et al. 2022). The recently published 2024 American Society of Nuclear Cardiology (ASNC) Cardiac Amyloid Radionuclide Imaging (CARI) quality metrics emphasize its role as a first-line diagnostic tool in suspected ATTR-CM (Hage et al. 2024). Despite this evolving diagnostic landscape, real-world data characterizing how diagnostic testing patterns have shifted across U.S. health systems in the contemporary era — particularly following the approval of tafamidis and the publication of ASNC CARI quality metrics — remain limited. We therefore sought to characterize temporal trends in ATTR-CM diagnostic testing using a large, multi-institutional electronic health record network.

Materials and methods

We performed a retrospective, descriptive analysis using the TriNetX U.S. Collaborative Network to evaluate temporal trends in diagnostic testing among patients with cardiac ATTR-CM between January 2017 and November 2025. TriNetX aggregates de-identified electronic health record data across multiple U.S. health systems. Patients were identified using ICD-10 codes specific to wild-type or hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (E85.82, E85.2) together with evidence of cardiac involvement, defined by concurrent codes for cardiomyopathy or heart failure (I43, I42.5, I42.9, or I50.x). To minimize diagnostic misclassification, patients with light-chain amyloidosis or plasma cell dyscrasias (E85.81, C90.x, C88.x, D47.2, D47.9) were excluded.

The index date was defined as the first qualifying ATTR diagnosis code. Because ATTR-CM codes are frequently assigned after confirmatory testing, entered following care at outside institutions, or applied provisionally prior to subtype confirmation, we used an extended diagnostic ascertainment window spanning 24 months prior to and 12 months following the index date. This approach was designed to reflect real-world diagnostic workflows and capture testing performed during evaluation as well as testing that preceded formal coding within the health system. Diagnostic modalities included Tc-99 m pyrophosphate scintigraphy, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR), and biopsy. Results

From 2017 to 2025, the annual number of patients diagnosed with cardiac ATTR increased from 59 to 2,221 across 17 to 46 contributing U.S. health systems; the overall cohort had a mean age of 77 years, was 81% male, included 70% White, 22% Black, 2% Asian, and 2% Hispanic patients, and exhibited a high burden of comorbid conditions including atrial fibrillation (53%), chronic kidney disease (38%), hypertension (68%), chronic ischemic heart disease (49%), diabetes mellitus (28%), and obesity (26%). Use of Tc-99 m scintigraphy increased from 19 patients in 2017 to 468 in 2024 and 420 in 2025. As a proportion of diagnosed patients, Tc-99 m use rose from 32.2% in 2017 to a peak of 41.5% in 2019, followed by a decline to 18.9% in 2025. Cardiac MRI use increased in absolute terms from 10 patients in 2017 to 341 in 2024 and 354 in 2025, while proportional use remained relatively stable at 16.9% in 2017, 15.6% in 2024, and 15.9% in 2025, suggesting growth consistent with broader increases in CMR availability rather than disproportionate adoption specific to ATTR diagnosis. Dual-modality imaging (defined as concurrent use of both Tc-99 m scintigraphy and CMR within the ascertainment window) increased from 10 patients (16.9%) in 2017 to 129 (5.9%) in 2024 and 110 (5.0%) in 2025 (Fig. 1).

In contrast, biopsy use declined steadily over time, from 11 patients (18.6%) in 2017 to 155 (7.1%) in 2024 and 124 (5.6%) in 2025, while combined scintigraphy and biopsy remained uncommon. Collectively, these findings indicate a sustained shift away from invasive histologic confirmation toward noninvasive imaging-based evaluation of cardiac ATTR-CM.

Discussion

This analysis demonstrates a major transformation in the diagnostic approach to cardiac ATTR-CM. Gillmore et al. established the specificity of bone-avid scintigraphy when monoclonal protein disorders are excluded (Gillmore et al. 2016). The ATTR-ACT trial later confirmed tafamidis as the first disease-modifying therapy, accelerating recognition and screening (Maurer et al. 2018). ASNC CARI guidelines now endorse imaging-based diagnosis, reducing reliance on biopsy (Hage et al. 2024).

Our findings align with claims-based Medicare analyses showing increased scintigraphy use and declining biopsy rates (Bourque et al. 2022). Extending these observations through 2025, our analysis demonstrates that although absolute imaging utilization continues to increase, proportional adoption has not kept pace with rapidly expanding case identification, highlighting persistent dissemination gaps. The observed decline in the proportion of patients undergoing Tc-99 m scintigraphy — from a peak of 41.5% in 2019 to 18.9% in 2025 — despite continued absolute growth is most plausibly explained by a denominator effect: the number of newly diagnosed ATTR-CM patients expanded far more rapidly than imaging capacity or access could accommodate. This rapid case expansion likely reflects a combination of increased disease awareness, earlier echocardiographic recognition of infiltrative cardiomyopathy phenotypes, and heightened clinical index of suspicion following tafamidis approval. Additionally, a proportion of newly diagnosed patients may have been identified through a “clinical-only” diagnostic pathway — particularly in frail or elderly individuals — in which the pre-test probability is sufficiently high and the therapeutic decision already made, rendering confirmatory scintigraphy less likely to alter management. Targeted quality improvement initiatives will be essential to ensure that guideline-adherent imaging-based diagnosis is applied broadly and equitably across health systems. The shift from invasive to noninvasive diagnosis observed in our cohort is, in our view, scientifically justified and clinically appropriate. The landmark work of Gillmore et al. established that grade 2–3 Tc-99 m myocardial uptake in the absence of a monoclonal protein carries 100% positive predictive value for ATTR-CM, enabling confident noninvasive diagnosis without histologic confirmation in the majority of patients.3 These criteria are now incorporated into major society guidelines and endorsed by the 2024 ASNC CARI quality metrics.2 Eliminating the need for invasive biopsy reduces procedural risk, improves patient access, enables earlier initiation of disease-modifying therapy, and is particularly important in an elderly, comorbid population.

This analysis has limitations. TriNetX data are de-identified and do not permit confirmation of imaging results, biopsy site, or diagnostic intent. Diagnostic pathways such as non-cardiac biopsy with cardiac involvement inferred by imaging may not be fully captured. Imaging or biopsy performed outside participating institutions or prior to network entry may also be underrepresented, and coding practices may vary across health systems. Furthermore, TriNetX procedure code granularity does not permit differentiation between planar scintigraphy, SPECT, and SPECT/CT acquisitions; as SPECT/CT is increasingly recognized as important for reducing false positives by distinguishing myocardial from blood-pool uptake, this distinction has implications for the interpreted diagnostic accuracy of scintigraphy in real-world practice and represents an important area for future investigation. Despite these constraints, the consistency of observed trends across a growing number of institutions supports a genuine nationwide shift in diagnostic practice.

Between 2017 and 2025, diagnostic evaluation of cardiac ATTR-CM shifted decisively toward noninvasive imaging, with substantial growth in Tc-99 m scintigraphy and CMR accompanied by declining biopsy use. Continued efforts are needed to ensure consistent implementation of guideline-recommended diagnostic pathways as disease recognition expands.

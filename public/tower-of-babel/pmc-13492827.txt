Evaluation of patient safety culture education program among healthcare professionals: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Deepak Baby, Supriya Singh, VV Harish Kumar, P. V. Sreedevi, Meenakshi Upadhyay, Abdul W. Pathath, Sajith Abraham, Muzammil M. Ahmed, Abikshyeet Panda, Minal Vaibhav
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Patient safety is an important aspect of healthcare system. Owingto improper training, there is high mortality and morbidity involved in patient care in hospitals worldwide. This study aimed to analyze the patient safety culture awareness among healthcare professionals in India.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This prospective cross-sectional study was conducted in different hospital settings following Institutional ethical Committee approval. The study sample comprised of 1800 healthcare professionals (physicians, dentist, nurses, patient care assistants, pharmacists, physiotherapists, and hospital administrators) selected using nonprobability convenient sampling method. Hospital survey on patient safety culture developed by Agency Healthcare Research Quality professionals was used which comprised of thirty questions grouped in 11 dimensions including supervisor’s/managerial expectations, actions promoting patient safety, organizational learning, continued improvement and hospital teamwork. Statistical analysis: The Pilot study was conducted using the SPSS Statistical tool software version 20.0 (IBM, NY) for testing the reliability of the questionnaire by usingthe Cronbach alpha test. The calculated sample size was found to be 100 for performing the pilot study. Cronbach alpha score of 0.734 was obtained which was indicative of study validity. Responses were recorded on 5-point Likert scale, i.e., 1 = strongly disagree, 2 = disagree, 3 = neither agree nor disagree, 4 = agree, and 5 = strongly agree). The Chi-square test/goodness of fittest was usedfor determination of statistical significance of qualitative variables, while Chi-square and Mann–Whitney Utests were used for intergroup comparisons. P < 0.05 was considered to have statistical significance.RESULTS:The ‘hands off’ and transitions domain required demonstrated lower positive responses (35.56%). Higher positive responses were obtained from dimensions: teamwork within a unit (92.21%), organizational learning and continued improvement (89.49%), frequency of reported events (82.31%), and feedback and communication errors (69.19%). Least positive responses were obtained from two dimensions—(a) general perception (20.23%) and (b) nonpunitive response to errors (09.12%). On comparison between perception responses by use of‘Chi-square’ test, statistically significant difference (<0.05) was obtained between following variables—(a) perception regarding patient safety dimensions and position and (b) experience level of healthcare professionals.CONCLUSION:There is requirement of improvement in few domains to strengthen patient safety culture in India which can be done by means of training programs in hospitals.

Introduction

Patient safety can be defined as, “preventing harm towards patients.” The emphasis in patient safety culture includes stakeholders such as healthcare professionals, organization and patients.[1]Patient safety culture includes values as well as belief systems shared among different members of an organization that promotes patient safety. This is measurable qualitative characteristic.[234]

“Institute of Medicine has defined patient safety as preventing harm to patients from the care which is meant to provide help.[5]”On the other hand, patient safety culture is definable as “sharing of values, perceptions as well as competencies which results in establishing norms behaviors and practices amongst members of any healthcare organization for promotion of patient safety.”[6]Patient Safety Culture identifies sharing of dependence, combination of teamwork as well as communication between work units and administrative arm.[7]

Singla et al. used 13 instruments covering 23 separate patient safety dimensions that can be grouped into (a) management or supervision, (b) safety system, (c) risk involved, (d) pressure related to work, (e) competence, (f) procedures or rules, and (g) extra dimensions.[3] Bartonickova et al. reported 24 tools of which hospital Survey on Patient Safety and Safety Attitudes Questionnaire have been considered as‘good’.[8]

Hospital Survey on Patient Safety Culture is an important tool for assessment of patient safety culture in healthcare settings. It is important for identification of areas that are required for improving safety of patients. Although there are several limitations of HSOPSC 2.0 primarily due to differences in different cultures, Lee and Dahinten highlighted adaptation as well as validation by working on the Korean version of HSOPSC 2.0.[9]This ensured the relevance of questionnaire items in the survey and maintained comprehensibility across varied healthcare set-ups. Shu et al. have underlined the comparison between different hospital units with HSOPSC for identifying specific strengths as well as weaknesses in patient safety culture.[10]

Patient safety remains a significant issue affecting healthcare industry which requires optimization by healthcare professionals for minimizing any accidental harm to patients alongside enhancement of patient care quality.[11] Up until1991, approximately 98,000 individuals used to perish every year pertaining to errors in hospital.[12] The Department of Health reported prevention of error as a strategy which necessitated significant reforms focused on avoidance, identification and reduction of harmful behavior in patients which may cause morbidity and/or mortality.

Materials and Methods

Study design and settings

This prospective and cross-sectional study was conducted in various hospital settings after obtaining approval from Institutional ethical Committee. Informed consent was obtained from all the study participants who were primarily healthcare professionals working in different tertiary hospital setups.

Study participants and sampling

A total of 1800 healthcare professionals comprising of physicians, dentist, nurses, patient care assistants, pharmacists, physiotherapists, and hospital administrators were selected as study participants using non probability convenient sampling method. Selection bias was avoided by appointing a non–healthcare associated individual who distributed the survey forms among the consenting participants.

Data collection tools and techniques

Hospital survey on patient safety culture (HSOPSC) that was developed by Agency Healthcare Research Quality (AHRQ) amongst healthcare professionals was used. As all subjects were primarily Indians belonging to Northern part of India, hence, there were no cross-cultural conflicts. This was a structured questionnaire consisting of both open as well as closed-ended items. Study questionnaire contained thirty questions grouped in 11 dimensions including supervisor’s/managerial expectations, actions promoting patient safety, organizational learning, continued improvement and hospital teamwork. The study questionnaire was divided into (a) socio-demographic and (b) patient safety issue-based items. Inclusion criteria: (a) active status and full-time employment and (b) those who gave consent for study participation. Exclusion criteria: (a) those who were not willing to participate in the study.

Data was coded and stored in computerized format and was only accessible to the investigators. All patients were instructed about the study protocol and had been explained about their right to withdraw from the study at any given point of time.

Ethical consideration

Institutional ethical committee clearance was obtained from required Ethics committee (IEC/7824NM).

Statistical analysis

A pilot study was conducted using the SPSS Statistical tool software version 20.0 (IBM, NY) for testing the reliability of the questionnaire by usingCronbach’s alpha test. The calculated sample size was found to be100 for performing the pilot study. Cronbach alpha score of 0.734 was obtained which was indicative of study validity. Informed consent was being taken from all the participants before the questionnaire was administered. Verification of used questionnaire was done by 2 experts from single health organization. Responses were then collected on five-point Likert scale i.e., 1 = strongly disagree, 2 = disagree, 3-neither agree nor disagree, 4 = agree and 5 = strongly agree). Socio demographic data comprised of age (in years), gender, occupational status, years of experience and any training on patient safety.

Chi-square test/goodness of fittest was employed for determination of statistical significance of qualitative variables, while Chi-square and Mann–Whitney tests were used for inter-group comparisons. P < 0.05 was considered to have statistical significance.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Total 1800 study participants were selected from 1892 Health Care Providers after considering defined inclusion and exclusion criteria comprising of 501 nurses, 557 physicians, 228 patient-care assistants, 219 pharmacists, 100 physiotherapists, 50 administrators (as only high-tier responded) and 145 dentists. Responses were received from all study participants who belonged to different occupational healthcare professions. Highest positive response rate was obtained from nurses (41.7%). Professional experience was found to be varying. 9.12% had less than one year of work experience (these constituted professionals who had recently joined healthcare profession but were equally qualified), 25.43% had 1–5 years of work experience, 31.29% had 6–10 years of work experience, 8.13% had 11–15 years of experience, 12.14% had 16–20 years of experience while 13.89% had more than 20 years of work experience. The weekly work hours were found to be-5.6% had <20 weekly working hours, 17–23% had 20–40 work hours in a week, 37.89% worked between 40 and 50 h weekly, 23.86% worked between 51 and 60 h and 15.42% had more than 60 h of weekly work. 56% of study participants were males whereas 44% were females [Table 1].

In current study, greater positive responses were obtained from following studied dimensions—teamwork within a unit (92.21%), organizational learning and continued improvement (89.49%), frequency of reported events (82.31%) and feedback and communication errors (69.19%). Discussion

‘Patient safety’ constitutes main component of quality of healthcare services as it helps in avoidance of any harm or damage to patient during healthcare and treatment.[13] Despite advancements in health sector, patient safety is a serious issue in healthcare across the world as errors remain a common cause of morbidity all over the world. According to World Health Organization, there are over 10 million patients of fall injury who suffer from mortality and morbidity due to unsafe medical care per year around the globe.[14]

Patient safety can be improved by making efforts for identification and elimination of ant potential harm before their occurrence. Hence, patient safety must be a priority in a hospital organization and steps must be taken for its internalization.[15]

Culture can be defined as “a set of perceptions, cognitions, and awareness of a particular group whose members have their own cognitive, emotional and behavioral elements” while“safety culture”can be defined as “production of values, attitudes, perceptions, merits, individual and group behavioral patterns which determine the commitment, methodology, and efficiency of a health organization”.[16] Patient safety culture is primary as well as an essential component of any healthcare organization for promotion and improvement of patient safety.[1718] Health care safety responsibility is considered to be a high-risk organization level commitment for detecting as well as analyzing any mistakes or harm to a patient with an ultimate goal towards creating conditions which aim for balance between demand for reporting of errors and disciplinary activities.[19]

In the present study, on comparing perception of patient safety dimensions with the position, level of experience, significant statistical significance was obtained hence, indicating that the quantitative variable, position, and level of experience variables are independent of each other as well as patient safety dimensions in the studied population.

The present study showed the highest positive response was from teamwork within a unit while least positive response was obtained in the domain “non punitive response to error”. Along with “hands off and transitions” which mandates cooperation from various other departments. The present study observed higher positive responses in domains-organizational learning and continuous improvement, frequency of reporting of events, feedback and error in communication.

Hence, strategies must be developed by organizational managers for prevention of errors and they must hold regular programs for training healthcare workers.[2021] Mukherjee and Aileen in their study observed a direct relation between professional position and level of perception.[17]

Schutz et al. in their study found highest positive responses (91.50%) in domain “teamwork within departments” whereas lowest score was obtained for the domain “non punitive response to error (17.65%)”. They concluded that “non punitive response to error” was an area requiring improvement and development of strategies which require implementation.[22]

Bodur and Filiz showed in their study that overall patient safety score was 50.1%. Most scores in dimensions were found to be lower than benchmark scores (64.8%). Mean positive response rate for studied dimensions were lower than composite data of AHRQ, except for “Organizational Learning-Continuous Improvement,” which had highest (80%) positive response rate.

On the other hand, lowest percentage of positive response (18%) was reported in “non punitive response to error” which meant most staffs perceived punishment for any medical error.[23]

The present study demonstrated highest average positive response among nurses which may be due to direct work relation to patient safety.

Asadi Fatemeh et al. in their research developed an educational booklet following a systematic process to design and implement for the patient safety in anesthesia, which was based on the items of the safe anesthesia checklist, was well designed and its validity was confirmed by experts.[24]

Abrishami Ramin et al. conducted a study to determine the effects of patient safety programs based on Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation (SBAR) and Failure Model Effects Analysis (FMEA) techniques on self-efficacy and patient safety culture. It was observed that there is a meaningful relationship between patient safety training programs based on SBAR and FMEA techniques on patient safety and self-efficacy of nurses.[25]

Limitation of thisstudy is its nonuniform distribution of sample, i.e., equal numbers of study participants of different areas must be included to achieve real data on patient safety culture awareness. Also, the cross-sectional nature of the study might add to the bias in outcome hence, different populations from varied cultural background must be studied for true representation of the study findings.

Novelty of the study: The response rate obtained in present study shows highest awareness among nursing professionals who interact with patients on one-on-one basis.

The strength of the study is related to the inclusion of most of the healthcare and/or healthcare-associated professionals while the weakness is the small representation of sample in each professional type.

Conclusion

The front-line healthcare professionals have been demonstrated to have higher awareness towards patient safety culture. There is an urgent requirement for improving all dimensions of patient’s safety culture amongst all healthcare professionals.

The 5-step Solvable Mono-anabelian Reconstruction of Abelian Number Fields
Yu Mao

Abstract
In this paper, we develop a new method to reconstruct an abelian number field K K from the maximal 5 5 -step solvable quotient of G K G_{K} group-theoretically. The new aspect of this paper is that the results in this paper are independent from the bi-anabelian results proved in [ ST22 ] .



Conventions

Throughout this paper, we shall use the following notions.

•

We write
\Sigma
for the set of all prime numbers.

•

Let
K
be a number field, we write
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{fin}}
for the set of all finite places of
K
. More generally, if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is an algebraic extension, then we also write
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{fin}}
for the set of all non-archimedean primes of
K
. In particular, if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is infinite, we have the natural identification
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{fin}}=\varinjlim_{F}\mathscr{P}_{F}^{\text{fin}}
where
F
ranges over all finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
K
.

•

Let
G
be a profinite group. We write
G^{\text{sol}}
for the maximal pro-solvable quotient of
G
and
G^{\text{ab}}
for the maximal abelian quotient of
G
.

•

Let
G
be a profinite group. We write
G^{[i]}
for the (closure of the)
i
-th derived subgroup of
G
, i.e.
G^{[0]}:=G
,
G^{[i]}:=\overline{[G^{[i-1]},G^{[i-1]}]}
for
i\geq 1
. We write
G^{i}
for the maximal
i
-step solvable quotient of
G
. In particular,
G^{1}=G^{\text{ab}}
. Moreover, we have the following canonical identification
G^{\text{sol}}=\varprojlim_{i}G^{i}
.

•

Let
K
be a field, fix an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
. We write
K^{\text{sep}}
for the separable closure of
K
contained in
\overline{K}
. We write
G_{K}:=\text{Gal}(K^{\text{sep}}/K)
for the absolute Galois group of
K
.

•

Let
K
be a field, we write
K^{\text{sol}}:=(K^{\text{sep}})^{\text{ker}(G_{K}\twoheadrightarrow G_{K}^{\text{sol}})}
for the maximal pro-solvable extension of
K
contained in
\overline{K}
. Moreover, for each
i\geq 1
, we write
K_{i}:=(K^{\text{sep}})^{G^{[i]}}
for the maximal
i
-step solvable extension of
K
. In particular,
K_{1}=K^{\text{ab}}
is the maximal abelian extension of
K
.

•

All homomorphisms between profinite groups are assumed to be continuous.

•

Let
A
be an abelian group, we write
A^{\wedge}:=\varprojlim A/nA
, viewed additively.

•

Let
A
be an abelian group, we write
A_{\text{tor}}
for the torsion subgroup of
A
, and
A^{/\text{tor}}:=A/A_{\text{tor}}
.

1
Introduction

First, let us recall the
m
-step solvable version of the Neukirch-Uchida Theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Saïdi-Tamagawa, Theorem 2 in
[
ST22
]
)
.

Let
K,L
be number fields. Let
m\geq 0
be an integer. Let
\tau_{m+3}:G_{K}^{m+3}\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{L}^{m+3}
be an isomorphism. Then the followings hold:

(i) There exists a field isomorphism
\sigma_{m}:K_{m}\xrightarrow{\sim}L_{m}
such that
\tau_{m}(g)=\sigma_{m}g\sigma_{m}^{-1}
for every
g\in G_{K}^{m}
, where
\tau_{m}
is the isomorphism between
G_{K}^{m}
and
G_{L}^{m}
induced by
\tau_{m+3}
.

(ii) Assume that
m\geq 2
(resp.
m=1
). Then the isomorphism
\sigma_{m}:K_{m}\xrightarrow{\sim}L_{m}
is unique (resp.
\sigma:K\xrightarrow{\sim}L
induced by
\sigma_{1}:K_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}L_{1}
).

But neither the Theorem 1.1 nor the proof of Theorem 1.1 provides an explicit reconstruction of the number field
K
from
G_{K}^{m}
for any
m
.

On the other hand, in
[
MS25
]
, there is a mono-anabelian version of Theorem 1.1, i.e. a group theoretic reconstruction of
K_{m}/K
from
G_{K}^{m+7}
. However, the result proved in
[
MS25
]
uses Theorem 1.1 in an essential way. In particular, Theorem 1 in
[
MS25
]
does not provide a weaker alternative proof to Theorem 1.1.

On the other hand, Theorem 2 in
[
MS25
]
does not depend on Theorem 1.1, in particular, Theorem 2 in
[
MS25
]
does provide an alternative proof to Theorem 1.1 for subfields of imaginary quadratic fields.

In this paper, we develop new methods to recover all abelian number fields from their maximal
5
-step solvable Galois groups. Our methods in the present paper is independent from Theorem 1.1, and different from Theorem 2 in
[
MS25
]
. In particular, we prove:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be an abelian number field (i.e. a finite abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
). Then there is a group-theoretic reconstruction of
K
from
G_{K}^{5}
.

2
Some Local Reconstructions

In this section, we review the local theory proven by Saïdi and Tamagawa in
[
ST22
]
and some local reconstructions related to decomposition groups, basically everything in this section are proven in
[
ST22
]
and
[
MS25
]
.

Definition 2.1

(c.f. Definition 3.2 in
[
Hos19a
]
)
.

A profinite group
G
is said to be of NF-type if it admits the following data:

(i) A number field
K
together with its separable closure
K^{\text{sep}}
inside some algebraic closure
\overline{K}
.

(ii) An isomorphism
\alpha:G\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{K}
of profinite groups.

In this case, we also say that
G
is a profinite group of NF-type associated to
K
.

In particular, a profinite group of NF-type should be thought of as an abstract profinite group only, but not a profinite group naturally arises from field theory or scheme theory.

Definition 2.2

(c.f. Definition 3.2 in
[
Hos19a
]
, Definition 2.1 in
[
MS25
]
)
.

A profinite group of
GSC
-type (resp.
\text{GSC}^{m}
-type for some positive integer
m
) is a profinite group
G
isomorphic to the maximal prosolvable (resp.
m
-step solvable quotient) quotient of some profinite group of NF-type.

We shall recall the main result in
[
ST22
]
, which will play an important role in our reconstruction:

Theorem 2.3

(c.f. Theorem 1.25 in
[
ST22
]
)
.

Let
K
be a number field and
m\geq 1
be an integer. Then the set of decomposition subgroups at non-archimedean primes of
G_{K}^{m}
can be group-theoretically from
G_{K}^{m+2}
.

Since Theorem 2.3 in mono-anabelian, we may adapt the statement to Definition 2.2.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer, and
G
be a profinite group of
\text{GSC}^{m+2}
-type. We write
\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{m})
for the
G^{m}
-set reconstructed from
G^{m+2}
by using Theorem 2.3 (i.e. elements in
\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{m})
are profinite groups isomorphic to decomposition groups at non-archimedean primes of
G_{K}^{m}
). Moreover, we write
\text{Dec}(G^{m}):=\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{m})/G^{m}
where the
G^{m}
-action is given by conjugation.

.

G

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers. Let
G

\text{GSC}^{m+n}

Definition 2.7
.

Theorem 2.8

(c.f. Theorem 2.5 in
[
MS25
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 1

G
be a profinite group of
\text{GSC}^{\text{m+4}}
-type associated to some number field
K

m

D\in\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{m+2})

(ii) The degree

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
G
be a profinite group of
\text{GSC}^{5}
-type associated to a number field
K
, and
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{fin}}
. Then whether or not
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is abelian over
\mathbb{Q}_{p_{\mathfrak{p}}}
can be group-theoretically determined by
D\in\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{2})
, for
D
corresponds to
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

By Theorem 2.5, there is a group-theoretic reconstruction to the local cyclotome
\Lambda(D)
. Then by Lemma 4.6 in
[
Hos19b
]
, there is a group-theoretic reconstruction to the following data:

(i) The topological ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p(D)}
and the topological field
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
.

(ii) The norm map
\text{Nm}:k^{\times}(D)\to\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}^{\times}
.

Hence by Proposition 4.9 in
[
Hos19b
]
, we may conclude that whether or not
K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
can be determined group-theoretically.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

In fact, by local class field theory, we can even reconstruct the Galois group
\text{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)})
group-theoretically provided that
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is abelian. The Galois group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}^{\times}/\text{Nm}(k^{\times}(D))
, which is group-theoretically determined by
D
. We shall write
\underline{D}
for this finite group.

3
Reconstruction of Abelian Number Fields

Now let
K
be an abelian number field for the rest of this section, and
G
be a profinite group of
\text{GSC}^{5}
-type associated to
K
. We aim to prove Theorem 1.2 in this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
D\in\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{2})
, then there exists a group-theoretic reconstruction of a field
k(D)
isomorphic to
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
\mathfrak{p}
corresponds to
D
(here we do not assume that
d(D)=1
).

Proof.

By Lemma 4.6 in
[
Hos19b
]
and Theorem 2.8 (ix), (x), there is a group-theoretic reconstruction to the topological field
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, we write

\mathcal{A}_{n}(D):=\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}[\mu(D)[n]]

for the group-ring generated by
\mu(D)[n]
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
. In particular, one can recover the field structure of
F_{n}(D):=\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}(\zeta_{n})
from
\mathcal{A}_{n}(D)
(since
\mathcal{A}_{n}(D)
decomposes in to direct product of fields), where
\zeta_{n}\in\mu(D)[n]
is an element of order
n
. Thus we shall write

F_{\infty}(D):=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}F_{n}(D).

Obviously
F_{\infty}(D)
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
.

Now let
K_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
be a finite abelian extension, it follows from the above constructions of cyclotomic fields and the arguments in Theorem 21.3 in
[
Fes
]
, that we may conclude that
K_{0}\subset F_{\infty}(D)
. Hence we can conclude that
F_{\infty}(D)
coincides with the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
. Hence we can also reconstruct the Galois group
\text{Gal}(F_{\infty}(D)/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)})
, which is isomorphic to the abelianisation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}}
.

On the other hand, since we have recovered the ring structures of
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
and
F_{\infty}(D)
, we obtain an isomorphism by local class field theory (which we can use because we have recovered the ring structure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
together with all of its cyclotomic extensions):

\theta_{D}:(\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}^{\times})^{\wedge}\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{Gal}(F_{\infty}(D)/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}).

By local class field theory for
\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}
, the group
\underline{D}:=\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)}^{\times}/\text{Nm}(k^{\times}(D))
(c.f. Remark 2.10) can be identified with a finite quotient of
\text{Gal}(F_{\infty}(D)/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)})
. Hence the local field
k(D)
can be characterised as

k(D):=F_{\infty}(D)^{\text{ker}(\text{Gal}(F_{\infty}(D)/\mathbb{Q}_{p(D)})\twoheadrightarrow\underline{D})}.

One verifies immediately that
k(D)
and
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
are isomorphic.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
v\in\text{Dec}(G^{2})
be the image of
D\in\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{2})
(by Proposition 2.5). Then we define:

•

p(v)=p(D)
.

•

k(v):=k(D)
(c.f. Proposition 3.1, and Definition 3.1 in
[
MS25
]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a group-theoretic reconstruction of a field
F_{\infty}(G)
from
G
such that there is an isomorphism of fields

F_{\infty}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Q}^{\text{ab}}

which is equivariant w.r.t
\alpha:G\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{K}^{5}
.

Proof.

Notice that by Definition 3.3 in
[
MS25
]
together with Theorem 2.8, there is a group-theoretic reconstruction of
\mu(G^{\text{ab}})\cong\mu_{\infty}
from
G
. It follows from Definition 2.2 in
[
Hoso3
]
(together with various definitions in this paper involved), that there is a group-theoretic reconstruction of the prime subfield
\mathbb{Q}
from
G
.

By Proposition 3.1, together with the notion of Definition 3.2, we write

F_{\infty}(v):=F_{\infty}(D)

for some
v\in\text{Dec}(G^{\text{ab}})
, where
v
is the image of
D\in\widetilde{\text{Dec}}(G^{2})
in
\text{Dec}(G^{\text{ab}})
. Then one verifies immediately that we have the containment

\mu(G^{\text{ab}})\subset\prod_{v\in\text{Dec}(G^{\text{ab}})}F_{\infty}(v)

hence we write

F_{\infty}(G)\subset\prod_{v\in\text{Dec}(G^{\text{ab}})}F_{\infty}(v)

for the subring of
\prod_{v\in\text{Dec}(G^{\text{ab}})}F_{\infty}(v)
generated by
\mu(G^{\text{ab}})
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then this proposition follows immediately from the constructions above together with Theorem 3.5 in
[
MS25
]
(one should adapt to suitable
m
in the context of Theorem 3.5 in
[
MS25
]
).
∎

In this case, we shall write
\Gamma:=\text{Aut}_{\text{field}}(F_{\infty}(G))
, which is isomorphic to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{ab}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
. Moreover, by considering the cyclotomic character, we obtain a natural map
G^{\text{ab}}\to\Gamma
.

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a group-theoretic reconstruction to a field
F(G)
isomorphic to
K
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition 3.1, that we can reconstruct the fields
k(v)
for any
v\in\text{Dec}(G^{2})
. Since we have field structure, we write

n_{G}:=\prod_{v\in\text{Dec}(G^{2})}\mathfrak{f}(v)

where
\mathfrak{f}(v)
is defined to be
s(v)+1
where
s(v)
is the maximal integer such that the
s(v)
-th ramification group of
\text{Gal}(k(v)/\mathbb{Q}_{p(v)})
in lower numbering. Since we have reconstructed the field structures on
k(v)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p(v)}
group-theoretically (c.f. Corollary 2.9 and Proposition 3.1), the integer
s(v)
is also regarded as group-theoretically determined by
D
. Hence the integer
n(G)
can be group-theoretically determined by
G
.

Then we write

F_{n}(G):=\mathbb{Q}(\mu(G^{\text{ab}})[n])

where
\mu(G^{ab})
can be reconstructed from
G
by Definition 3.2 in
[
MS25
]
and Proposition 3.6 in
[
Hos19a
]
, which is isomorphic to
\mu_{\infty}\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(1)
. Recall that the cyclotomic character
\chi:G^{\text{ab}}\to\Gamma
can be group-theoretically reconstructed from
G
by Theorem 1.26 in
[
ST22
]
, hence we can also reconstruct the mod-
n_{G}
cyclotomic character, hence we have the map

\chi_{\text{mod}-n_{G}}:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n_{G}\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

also we have the natural identification

F_{n}(G)=F_{\infty}(G)^{\text{ker}(\chi_{\text{mod}-n_{G}})}.

Next, we define

H_{\text{cycl}}:=G^{\text{ab}}\times_{\Gamma}\text{ker}(\chi_{\text{mod}-n_{G}}).

One verifies that
H_{\text{cycl}}
is an open subgroup of
G^{\text{ab}}
, we shall define

F(G):=F_{n}(G)^{G^{\text{ab}}/H_{\text{cycl}}}.

One verifies easily that
F(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}K
. More precisely, this follows from that
n:=n_{G}
coincide with the product of the local conductor exponents of
K
, hence
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
is the minimal cyclotomic field containing
K
by Proposition 6.5 and Proposition 6.7 in Chapter VI of
[
Neu99
]
. One then verifies immediately that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
and
F_{n}(G)
are isomorphic, hence the result follows.
∎

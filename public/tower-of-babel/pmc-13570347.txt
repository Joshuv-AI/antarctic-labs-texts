Study on the spatiotemporal development of skeletal muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks
Authors: Zhen Huo, Zhengkui Zhou, Shuisheng Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to investigate the developmental patterns of breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks, and to screen potential genes regulating their independent development and growth differences. Muscle weight and histological characteristics were measured at eight embryonic stages (E11, E13, E15, E17, E19, E21, E23, E25; n = 10). Transcriptome analysis was performed on breast muscle at E11, E13, E17, and E21, and on leg muscle at E17 and E21. The results showed that breast muscle weight exhibited an S-shaped growth pattern, while leg muscle weight increased significantly in a linear or quadratic trend with increasing embryonic age (P < 0.0001). Histological features indicated that mature myofibers appeared in leg muscle at E19, but were not observed in breast muscle until E23. KEGG enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes showed that pathways such as cytoskeleton in muscle cells and oxidative phosphorylation were involved in skeletal muscle development. Using bioinformatics analyses including WGCNA and Mfuzz clustering, we screened 5 potential genes regulating breast muscle development (including MYOD1), 12 potential genes associated with leg muscle development (including SDHA), and 11 potential genes mediating the developmental differences between breast and leg muscle (including PDHA1). Notably, from E21 to E25, leg muscle maintained rapid development whereas breast muscle development entered a plateau phase, and this difference may be driven by genes related to energy metabolism. This study systematically clarified the developmental patterns of breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks, providing a basis for further dissecting the molecular mechanisms underlying the spatiotemporal development of avian skeletal muscle.

Introduction

China is the world's largest producer and consumer of duck meat (Deng et al., 2019). Duck meat serves as a significant source of animal protein (Fan et al., 2024). Breast muscle and leg muscle are the primary tissues for meat production, and their yields directly affect the economic efficiency of duck farming. Pekin ducks are generally ready for marketing at 35 to 42 days of age (Zeng et al., 2015; Zhuang et al., 2025). Notably, the development of breast muscle in Pekin ducks is relatively slow and remains incomplete by the marketing age (Xu et al., 2012), which restricts the overall duck meat production. Avian skeletal muscle originates from the embryonic mesoderm, and its developmental process involves a series of biological processes, including progenitor cell proliferation, myoblast differentiation, migration and fusion, ultimately forming multinucleated myofibers (Hernandez-Torres et al., 2017; Mo et al., 2023). These processes are governed by a complex gene regulatory network. The embryonic stage represents a critical window for skeletal muscle development, and the developmental status directly determines the number, morphological structure and developmental potential of myofibers in ducklings post-hatching (Li et al., 2016; Weng et al., 2025). Previous studies found that the breast muscle of Pekin ducks reached a growth plateau stage at embryonic day 20 (E20), whereas the leg muscle initiated rapid growth at E21 (Liu et al., 2011; Gu et al., 2013). The spatiotemporal developmental pattern of breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks may be an important factor responsible for the delayed development of breast muscle after hatching. However, the spatiotemporal development of duck skeletal muscle during the embryonic period remains unclear.

Previous studies on duck skeletal muscle development mainly focused on two directions. First, comparative transcriptomic analyses (RNA‑Seq) of skeletal muscle between different duck breeds have identified numerous breed‑specific differentially expressed genes (DEGs). For example, compared with Pekin ducks, Hanzhong partridge ducks exhibited upregulated expression of Aquaporin 4 (AQP4) in leg muscle at E17 (Cao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). Second, RNA‑Seq‑based transcriptomic analysis has also been used to reveal dynamic gene expression patterns during embryonic skeletal muscle development in single breeds, such as Pekin ducks and Hanzhong partridge ducks. Previous studies suggested that genes including insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) and nuclear factor kappa B subunit 1 (NFKB1) may be associated with breast muscle development (Xu et al., 2012, 2017); and kelch-like family member 31 (KLHL31) and myosin light chain 4 (MYL4) were involved in leg muscle development (Hu et al., 2021a).

Although these studies have reported phenotypic observations related to muscle development, they usually analyze transcriptomic and phenotypic data separately without systematic integration. Moreover, most studies lack the comprehensive spatiotemporal resolution employed in the present study. In addition, traditional analytical strategies are mostly limited to differential gene screening and rarely adopt the combined analysis of WGCNA and Mfuzz clustering, which can more efficiently identify gene modules and expression patterns highly correlated with phenotypic traits. Therefore, the underlying mechanisms governing the spatiotemporal development of skeletal muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks remain to be further elucidated.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

All experimental procedures in this study were approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of the Institute of Animal Science, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Approval No. IAS2025-25).

Experimental design and sample collection

Pekin duck embryos were obtained from the National Conservation Farm of Pekin Ducks at the Changping Base of the Institute of Animal Science, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences. The embryos were incubated under controlled gradient conditions: 38.0 °C with 70% relative humidity from E1 to E7, 37.8 °C with 65% relative humidity from E8 to E14, 37.6 °C with 65% relative humidity from E15 to E21, and 37.4 °C with 70% relative humidity from E22 to E25. Eggs were automatically turned once every 2 hours throughout incubation. Embryonic sexing was performed according to gonadal morphological characteristics following the method of Mizia (Mizia et al., 2023). In male embryos, the bilateral gonads are symmetrical in morphology and similar in size, whereas in female embryos, the left gonad develops normally while the right gonad undergoes typical physiological degeneration. A total of 80 male embryos were sampled at E11, E13, E15, E17, E19, E21, E23, and E25, with 10 healthy embryos randomly selected at each stage. These stages fully cover the critical period of myoblast proliferation, muscle fiber formation, and maturation. E11 is the earliest stage at which skeletal muscle phenotypic traits can be accurately measured, and E25 is close to hatching, representing the late embryonic stage.

Embryonic weight, the right breast muscle and leg muscle weight were measured and collected, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C for subsequent analysis. The left breast muscle and leg muscle were fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for the subsequent histological observation. The calculation formulas for breast muscle rate, breast muscle weight gain were as follows:Breast muscle rate (%) = breast muscle weight / embryonic weight × 100.

Breast muscle weight gain (g/2d) = Average breast muscle weight at the current embryonic age − Average breast muscle weight at the previous embryonic age.

The embryonic weight gain, leg muscle rate, and leg muscle weight gain were calculated following the same methodology as used for the breast muscle.

Histological analysis of skeletal muscle

After fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h, breast and leg muscle tissues were sequentially dehydrated, cleared, and embedded in paraffin. Following the cooling of the embedded blocks, sectioning was performed. The sections were then processed for baking, dewaxing, hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining, and subsequent dehydration and clearing. Finally, the sections were mounted with neutral balsam, and histological observations and image acquisitions were conducted using a light microscope.

Transcriptome sequencing and DEGs analysis

Breast muscle from embryonic days 11, 13, 17, and 21 (BE11, BE13, BE17, and BE21), along with leg muscle samples from embryonic days 17 and 21 (LE17 and LE21), were selected for transcriptome sequencing. Each tissue at each stage was represented by 6 biological replicates. RNA extraction and reverse transcription were performed according to the method described by Li et al. (2021). The quantity and purity of total RNA were detected using NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies, Wilmington, DE, USA), and RNA integrity was verified by a Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) in combination with agarose gel electrophoresis. Poly(A) mRNA was enriched from total RNA using Dynabeads Oligo (dT)25 magnetic beads, and cDNA libraries were constructed using a strand specific library preparation kit based on dUTP second strand marking. After constructing cDNA libraries, paired-end (PE) 150 sequencing was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq™ 6000 platform. Raw sequencing reads were processed using fastp software with default parameters to remove adapter sequences, low-quality reads, and undetermined bases. Only high-quality clean reads were retained for subsequent analysis. Clean reads were aligned to the Pekin duck reference genome (GCF_015476345.1) using HISAT2 (https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/hisat2). To obtain high-quality expression data, genes with an expression level ≥2 and counts in ≥20% of the samples were retained, followed by counts per million (CPM) normalization. Principal components analysis (PCA) was conducted in the Weishengxin Online Analysis Platform (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) (Tang et al., 2023). Subsequently, differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq2 R package, filtering DEGs by |log2(Fold change, FC)| ≥ 1.5 and adjusted P-value ≤ 0.05 (Love et al., 2014). Enrichment analysis was conducted separately for the DEGs of nine comparison groups: BE11 vs BE13, BE13 vs BE17, BE17 vs BE21, BE11 vs BE17, BE13 vs BE21, BE11 vs BE21, LE17 vs LE21, BE17 vs LE17, and BE21 vs LE21. The clusterProfiler R package was used to perform Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis (Wu et al., 2021).

Construction of gene co-expression network based on weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

A weighted adjacency matrix was constructed using the WGCNA R package (Langfelder and Horvath, 2008). Protein-coding genes with CPM value > 5 in half of the samples and total CPM > 10 across all samples were retained. The weighted adjacency matrix was converted into a topological overlap measure (TOM) matrix to calculate the connectivity of each network node. Based on scale-free topology analysis, the network construction threshold was set to 0.8, and the first parameter value that met this threshold was selected as the soft threshold. Average linkage hierarchical clustering was used to construct an unsupervised topological overlap matrix. Genes with similar expression patterns were classified into the same module. Parameters were set as follows: the maximum block size was set to the number of filtered genes, the module eigengene was set to 0.35, the minimum module size was set to 30, and the reassignment value was set to 0. Modules smaller than the minimum gene module size were merged into the grey module.

Mfuzz clustering analysis

The normalized gene expression matrix was subjected to Mfuzz clustering analysis using the Omicstudio platform (https://www.omicstudio.cn/tool). Based on the temporal expression patterns of genes across different embryonic stages, genes with similar expression profiles were grouped into the same module through unsupervised clustering. The number of clusters was determined by integrating the similarity of expression trends with the platform's default optimal clustering division rules, ultimately yielding gene clustering modules characterized by distinct temporal expression features.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

PPI correlation among DEGs was analyzed using the search tool for the retrieval of interacting genes (STRING) database (Szklarczyk et al., 2015), with an interaction threshold set to 0.4. The results from STRING analysis were imported into Cytoscape software to construct the PPI network (Shannon et al., 2003).

Statistical analysis

Orthogonal polynomial contrast analysis was used to determine linear and quadratic responses of embryonic weight and leg muscle weight with increasing embryonic age. Logistic model fitting was conducted using the sigmoidal growth curve self-starting function (Sslogis) from the stats package in R software to analyze changes in breast muscle weight (Team, 2022). The model fit was verified using the output R², and the slope corresponding to each embryonic age in the fitted curve was extracted as the growth rate of breast muscle at that embryonic age. Meanwhile, the inflection point parameter b of the curve was retrieved to determine the embryonic age at which the breast muscle development rate of Pekin ducks reached the maximum. Pearson correlation coefficients between leg muscle rate/breast muscle rate and gene expression levels were calculated using the CORREL function in SAS 9.4. Significance tests and FDR correction for multiple testing were performed for all correlation coefficients. Only genes with FDR-corrected P-value < 0.05 were retained for subsequent PPI network construction, ensuring compliance with statistical standards for high-dimensional transcriptomic data. Paired-samples t-tests were conducted to analyze the differences in breast muscle weight and leg muscle weight, as well as between breast muscle rate and leg muscle rate at the same embryonic age. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed on embryonic weight, breast muscle weight, breast muscle rate, leg muscle weight, and leg muscle rate across different embryonic ages, followed by Tukey test for multiple comparisons. Orthogonal polynomial analysis, t-tests, and one-way ANOVA were all performed using SAS 9.4 software. P < 0.05 indicated a significant difference, and P < 0.01 indicated an extremely significant difference.

Results

Developmental pattern of skeletal muscle in Pekin ducks

The results showed that embryonic weight increased linearly or quadratically with increasing embryonic age (PLinear < 0.0001, PQuadratic < 0.0001). Embryonic weight gain increased rapidly from E13 to E17, and then remained at a high level until E25 (weight gain every 2 days > 6 g). (Fig. 1A–B). The cumulative growth curve of breast muscle weight in Pekin ducks during the embryonic stage followed an "S"-shaped pattern, and the Logistic model exhibited an excellent fit (R² = 0.9984). During E11 - E21, breast muscle weight significantly increased with increasing embryonic age (P < 0.05); whereas no significant difference was observed in breast muscle weight during E21–E25 (P > 0.05). Logistic model analysis indicated that the growth inflection point of breast muscle occurred at E15.43. The inflection point of breast muscle rate was observed at E15, and breast muscle rate at E15 was significantly higher than that at other embryonic ages (P < 0.05). The weight gain and growth rate of breast muscle were relatively higher during E13 - E19 (weight gain every 2 days > 0.1 g, growth rate > 0.05g/d), and decreased during E21 - E25 (Fig. 1C–F). In contrast, leg muscle weight of Pekin ducks increased linearly or quadratically with increasing embryonic age (PLinear < 0.0001, PQuadratic < 0.0001). The inflection point in the leg muscle rate was observed at E15 and E19, with the leg muscle rate at E23 and E25 significantly higher than that at other embryonic ages (P < 0.05). Leg muscle weight gain was lower during E11 - E19 (weight gain every 2 days < 0.5 g), and increased during E19 - E25 (Fig. 1G–I). During E11-E13, scattered individual myocytes were present in both muscles. After E15, muscle fascicle structures were observed in both breast and leg muscles. KEGG enrichment analysis of DEGs in the leg muscle of Pekin ducks

In this study, KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the temporal DEGs in the leg muscle for LE17 vs LE21. A total of 16 enriched pathways related to muscle development were identified, which included oxidative phosphorylation, thermogenesis, carbon metabolism, cytoskeleton in muscle cells, biosynthesis of amino acids, and so on (Table 1). A total of 42 genes were enriched in the oxidative phosphorylation (Fig. 4C), and these genes were highly expressed at E21, including ATP synthase f1 subunit alpha (ATP5F1A), subunit a (SDHA), and others. Additionally, 14 genes were found to be enriched in the biosynthesis of amino acids (Fig. 4D), 11 of which were highly expressed at E21.

KEGG enrichment analysis of DEGs between breast muscle and leg muscle of Pekin ducks

In this study, KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the spatial DEGs between the breast muscle and leg muscle across two comparison groups: BE17 vs LE17 and BE21 vs LE21. A total of 19 enriched pathways related to muscle development were identified, including oxidative phosphorylation, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) signaling pathway, cytoskeleton in muscle cells, cell adhesion molecules, motor proteins, and so on. Furthermore, 50 genes, including ATP5F1A and SDHA, were enriched in the oxidative phosphorylation pathway and upregulated in leg muscle at E21. (Fig. 4E). Additionally, 13 genes were enriched in the PPAR signaling pathway, were upregulated in the leg muscle at E21 (Fig. 4F).

Screening of genes related to skeletal muscle development in embryonic Pekin ducks

Screening of genes related to leg muscle development in embryonic Pekin ducks

This study focused on 1651 DEGs in the leg muscle of Pekin ducks at LE17 vs LE21 (Fig. 3D), and calculated the correlation coefficients between their expression levels and the corresponding leg muscle rates (Fig. 1H). There were 770 positively correlated genes and 881 negatively correlated genes. According to a 5% screening ratio for positively and negatively correlated genes, 38 significantly positively correlated genes and 44 significantly negatively correlated genes were identified (FDR-corrected P-value < 0.05). PPI network analysis was conducted on these strongly correlated genes. Among them, 28 positively correlated genes with interaction relationships, with degree values ranging from 1 to 19, while 11 negatively correlated genes had interaction relationships, with degree values ranging from 1 to 3. Finally, 16 positively correlated hub genes (Fig. 6A) with a degree ≥ 10 and 4 negatively correlated hub genes with a degree ≥ 2 were filtered. Among the 16 positively correlated hub genes, 12 genes were significantly enriched in energy metabolism-related pathways (Fig. 6B). Screening of genes related to muscle development differences between breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks

The muscle percentages, developmental trends, and histological morphologies of the breast and leg muscle exhibited significant differences in Pekin ducks at E21(Fig. 1J, K; Fig. 2). Therefore, E21 was identified as the key time point for screening potential genes that regulate the developmental differences between breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks. This study focused on 1430 DEGs in the breast muscle and leg muscle of Pekin ducks at E21 (Fig. 3E), and calculated the correlation coefficients between their expression levels and the corresponding muscle rates (breast muscle rate and leg muscle rate, Fig. 1K). There were 601 positively correlated DEGs and 829 negatively correlated DEGs with muscle rate. According to a 5% screening ratio, 30 significantly positively correlated genes and 41 significantly negatively correlated genes were screened out (FDR-corrected P-value < 0.05). Among these, 21 positively correlated genes exhibited interaction relationships, with degree values ranging from 1 to 15, whereas 8 negatively correlated genes had interaction relationships, with degree values ranging from 1 to 2. A total of 12 positively correlated hub genes with a degree ≥ 10 (Fig. 6C) and 4 negatively correlated hub genes with a degree ≥ 2 were filtered. Among the 12 positively correlated hub genes, 11 genes were enriched in energy metabolism-related pathways, such as ATP5F1A, ATP5F1B, and so on (Fig. 6D). These genes were downregulated in BE21 compared to LE21.

Discussion

In this study, the weights of breast muscle and leg muscle were determined, and their histological features were characterized in Pekin ducks at 8 time points during the embryonic period. We found that the breast muscle and leg muscle of Pekin ducks exhibited a "developmental relay" pattern during the embryonic stage. The weight gain and growth rate of breast muscle remained at a high level from E13 to E19, and then entered a developmental plateau stage after E21. In contrast, the weight gain of leg muscle remained at a low level from E11 to E19, maintained a high level from E19 to E25. Gu et al. (2013) indicated that the breast muscle of Pekin ducks entered a developmental plateau stage after E20, and Liu et al. (2011) found that the leg muscle of Pekin ducks rapidly grew from E21 to E27. Our results are consistent with previous studies. During embryonic development, the developmental status of myofibers directly influences muscle weight. This study showed that dividing nuclei were detectable in both breast and leg muscle tissues of Pekin ducks at E11; mature myofiber structures were observed in leg muscle at E19, whereas mature myofiber structures were observed in breast muscle until E23. Li et al. (2010) found that the developmental process of breast muscle was slower than that of leg muscle from E14 to E27. The above results fully indicate that the differences in breast muscle and leg muscle weights of Pekin ducks during the embryonic period may result from the distinct developmental progress of myofibers.

To elucidate the underlying mechanism of skeletal muscle development in embryonic Pekin ducks, transcriptomic analysis was performed on breast muscle at the early development stage (E11, E13), rapid growth stage (E17), and developmental plateau stage (E21), as well as on leg muscle at the slow growth stage (E17), and rapid growth stage (E21). KEGG enrichment analysis showed that the DEGs in different stages and different muscle tissues were significantly enriched in multiple pathways associated with skeletal muscle growth and development, including the muscle cell cytoskeleton, cell cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, biosynthesis of amino acids and others. Hu et al. (2021b) and Hu and Liu (2023) conducted studies on skeletal muscle development in Pekin ducks and Hanzhong partridge ducks, and found that the pathways in which DEGs were significantly enriched included the cell cycle, actin cytoskeleton, and oxidative phosphorylation. These findings are consistent with those of the present study.

Cluster heat map analysis showed that the DEGs enriched in the cell cycle pathway were mainly upregulated at E11 and E13 of breast muscle development. Zhu et al. (2017) showed that the genes in the cell cycle exhibited low expression levels in the late embryonic stage of breast muscle in Jinding ducks and Gaoyou ducks, which was consistent with the results of this study. The expression pattern of genes associated with the cell cycle pathway provides a reasonable explanation for the breast muscle morphological observations in the present study (dividing nuclei were observed at E11, whereas mature myofiber structures were detected at E23), suggesting that breast muscle development in embryonic Pekin ducks follows the developmental rule of "early cell proliferation and late structural maturation". During leg muscle development, the DEGs in the oxidative phosphorylation and biosynthesis of amino acids pathways were significantly upregulated at E21 compared with E17. Therefore, this may provide the energy and material basis to promote leg muscle development at E21. The DEGs in the oxidative phosphorylation and PPAR signaling pathways were significantly upregulated in leg muscle compared to breast muscle. The oxidative phosphorylation and PPAR signaling pathways are important pathways for regulating skeletal muscle energy metabolic homeostasis (Crossland et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2025). Combined with the phenotypic data in this study, the weight of leg muscle was significantly higher than that of breast muscle from E11 to E25, indicating that these two pathways may provide sufficient energy support for leg muscle to potentially drive the developmental differences between breast muscle and leg muscle.

Notably, myocyte proliferation and energy metabolism are closely linked during muscle development. Both the cAMP signaling pathway and the PPAR signaling pathway are key pathways regulating energy metabolism, mitochondrial function, and ATP production (Valsecchi et al., 2013; Crossland et al., 2021). Genes in the cAMP signaling pathway exhibited sequential high expression throughout the embryonic development of breast muscle, with individual genes highly expressed at E11, E13, E17, and E21, respectively (Fig.. S3A). In contrast, genes in the PPAR signaling pathway were significantly upregulated at E21 compared with E17 in breast muscle (Fig. S3B). This suggests that energy metabolism continuously provides the necessary energy support for myocyte proliferation and muscle fiber maturation during the entire developmental stage of breast muscle.

To further systematically elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying skeletal muscle development in embryonic Pekin ducks, this study comprehensively identified potentially important genes involved in skeletal muscle development from three dimensions: breast muscle development, leg muscle development, and the developmental differences between breast muscle and leg muscle. Firstly, through WGCNA, Mfuzz clustering analysis, and pathway enrichment analysis, we screened out MYOD1, MYOG, DES, TRIM55, and AMPD3 as genes that may regulate breast muscle development. These five genes are closely involved in the biological processes of muscle development (Fig. 7A): Among them, the conserved regulatory roles of MYOD1 and MYOG in skeletal muscle development have been confirmed by numerous studies (Montarras et al., 1989; Miller, 1990; Moncaut et al., 2013; Ganassi et al., 2018; Yamamoto et al., 2018); MYOD1 promotes myoblast proliferation, and subsequently MYOG mediates myoblast differentiation and fusion (Bentzinger et al., 2012; Zammit, 2017; Adhikari et al., 2021;). DES encodes desmin, which constitutes the Z-discs of myofibrils (Hnia et al., 2015) and is essential for myoblast fusion and myotube formation (Schultheiss et al., 1991). Knockdown of TRIM55 delays myogenic differentiation and myotube formation (Perera et al., 2012). Increased AMPD3 activity reduces ATP content without a concurrent increase in ADP or AMP, thereby inhibiting protein degradation in skeletal muscle by regulating energy homeostasis (Davis et al., 2020). E17 is a critical period for the rapid development of breast muscle. Meanwhile, compared with E11, E13, and E21, all five genes are upregulated in breast muscle at E17 (Fig. 7B). Subsequently, through the integration of DEGs analysis, correlation analysis between gene expression levels and leg muscle rate, PPI network construction, and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, this study ultimately identified 12 genes including SDHA as potential regulators of leg muscle development in embryonic Pekin ducks. In terms of molecular functions, these 12 genes can be precisely mapped to energy metabolism-related processes (Fig. 7C): GAPDH plays a crucial role in glycolysis (Montllor-Albalate et al., 2023); IDH2, SUCLA2, SUCLG1, SDHA, and SDHB exert important functions in the TCA cycle (Bardella et al., 2011; Donti et al., 2016; Tommasini-Ghelfi et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2025); NDUFS1, NDUFS2, NDUFS3, NDUFS8, SDHA, SDHB, ATP5F1A, and ATP5F1B are components of Complex Ⅰ, Ⅱ, and Ⅴ of the electron transport chain (ETC) (Hinttala et al., 2005; Bardella et al., 2011; Nasca et al., 2023; Wan et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2024). Collectively, these 12 genes form an energy metabolism network with a distinct division of labor and coordinated regulation. Biological processes involved in skeletal muscle development, such as myoblast proliferation, amino acid synthesis, and actin synthesis, are all dependent on adequate energy supply (Cardoso et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022; Ling et al., 2023; Shan et al., 2025). A study confirmed that transcripts encoding mitochondrial proteins account for as high as 28% of those in skeletal muscle (Uhlén et al., 2015), which further highlights the critical role of energy metabolism in skeletal muscle development. E21 was a key period for the rapid development of leg muscle in Pekin ducks. Compared with E17, all 12 aforementioned genes were significantly upregulated in leg muscle at E21 (Fig. 7D), indicating that these 12 genes may synergistically provide energy support for the rapid development of leg muscle.

At E21, leg muscle underwent rapid development while breast muscle entered a developmental plateau. E21 was the critical time point for the developmental differences between breast muscle and leg muscle. This study integrated multiple analytical strategies including DEGs analysis, correlation analysis, and PPI network analysis, to identify 11 genes including PDHA1 as potential regulators of the developmental differences between breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks. In terms of molecular functions, these 11 genes could be precisely mapped to energy metabolism-related processes (Fig. 7E): PDHA1 plays a crucial role in pyruvate oxidative decarboxylation (Zhao et al., 2023); IDH2, SUCLG1, SDHA and SDHB exert important functions in the TCA cycle (Bardella et al., 2011; Donti et al., 2016; Tommasini-Ghelfi et al., 2019); NDUFA9, NDUFV1, NDUFS7, NDUFS8, SDHA, SDHB, ATP5F1A and ATP5F1B are involved in the composition of Complex Ⅰ, Ⅱ and Ⅴ of the electron transport chain (Hinttala et al., 2005; Bardella et al., 2011; Nasca et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2024). Hu et al. (2021b) and Cao et al. (2023) both found in their studies on embryonic skeletal muscle development of Pekin ducks and Hanzhong partridge ducks that energy-related pathways were significantly enriched at different developmental stages of duck skeletal muscle. These results are mutually corroborated with those of the present study, highlighting the critical role of energy metabolism pathways in duck skeletal muscle development. These 11 genes were upregulated in leg muscle compared with breast muscle (Fig. 7F), which indicates that these genes may regulate energy metabolism pathways through synergistic effects, thereby regulating the developmental differences between breast muscle and leg muscle. The developmental status of muscles during the embryonic period exerts a significant impact on post-hatching growth traits (Li et al., 2016; Weng et al., 2025). The differential expression pattern of these 11 genes at E21 may be a potential cause underlying the relatively slow breast muscle development and rapid leg muscle growth in Pekin ducks during the early post-hatching stage.

In summary, this study not only systematically investigated the developmental patterns of breast muscle and leg muscle in embryonic Pekin ducks, but also screened genes including MYOD1, SDHA, and PDHA1 that may regulate the spatiotemporal development of these two muscle types. However, the above potential genes were only identified through transcriptome analysis in this study, and no functional validation experiments have been conducted. It should be emphasized that transcriptome analysis alone is insufficient to identify the major effective genes and key causal variants that regulate skeletal muscle development. Transcriptome data can only reflect gene expression levels under specific spatiotemporal conditions, but cannot reveal the inherent molecular mechanisms or functional roles. Furthermore, due to the lack of integration with genetic strategies such as genome-wide association studies or quantitative trait locus mapping, this study cannot precisely identify the core master genes and key mutations that drive muscle development. However, the results of this study can provide a foundation for subsequent mechanistic research. Future studies can integrate multi-omics data and perform functional validation experiments to further clarify their regulatory mechanisms in the spatiotemporal development of skeletal muscle during embryonic stages in Pekin ducks.

Conclusion

In summary, breast muscle developed rapidly from E13 to E19 and entered a plateau phase after E21, whereas leg muscle maintained continuous growth throughout the embryonic period and developed rapidly after E19. Mature myofibers appeared earlier in leg muscle than in breast muscle. Breast muscle development may be regulated by myoblast proliferation and fusion, whereas leg muscle development may be modulated by energy metabolism processes. From E21 to E25, leg muscle developed rapidly while breast muscle developed slowly, and this developmental difference was likely primarily regulated by energy metabolism processes. This study has provided the foundation for further dissecting the spatiotemporal developmental mechanisms of skeletal muscle in Pekin ducks during the embryonic stage.

High- p T \rm{p_{T}} LHC constraints on SMEFT operators affecting rare kaon and hyperon decays
Arnab Roy, German Valencia

Abstract
We consider the constraints on new physics affecting rare kaon and hyperon decay as parametrized by a low energy effective theory that can be obtained from high- p T \rm p_{T} observables at LHC. To this end, we match the relevant low energy Wilson coefficients onto those in SMEFT at dimension six and compute their contributions to charged dilepton, monolepton, monojets, and VH \rm VH searches by ATLAS and CMS. Constraints from rare kaon decays are generally more restrictive, with the ones arising from high- p T \rm p_{T} measurements complementing them along directions in parameter space to which the former are not sensitive. We also illustrate the effect of a high luminosity LHC run in reducing the viable parameter regions.

Introduction

The rare kaon decay program seeks precise measurements of standard model (SM) parameters and constraints on new physics (NP) through measurements of flavour-changing neutral current (FCNC) mediated modes
[
1
,
2
]
. Modes with neutrino pairs, such as

Modes with a charged dilepton pair suffer from considerable theoretical uncertainty, which weakens, but does not negate, the constraints on NP that can be obtained.
When NP is parametrized using a low-energy effective theory (LEFT), rare kaon decays remain blind to certain directions in parameter space, which rare hyperon decays can partially cover

The operators describing NP appearing in the LEFT may originate in scenarios beyond the SM. One way to go beyond LEFT in a somewhat model-independent way is to match the LEFT onto an effective theory at the electroweak scale (SMEFT). This matching enables the inclusion of constraints arising from high-energy observables, such as those at LHC. Further, it relates the LEFT operators relevant for neutrino modes to those applicable for charged lepton modes. In this paper, we pursue this connection to obtain new constraints on the set of pertinent operators for rare kaon/hyperon decays related to the quark level process
s\to d\ell\bar{\ell}
at dimension six. To this end, we consider those LHC observables sensitive to the parton level process
sd\to\ell\bar{\ell}
for which measurements exist. In particular, we include dilepton, monolepton, and missing-
p_{T}
(monojet) searches for NP from ATLAS and CMS.

for convenience. From these results, we extract the window for new physics that will be used to constrain the WC as
\sigma^{NP}=\sigma^{exp}-\sigma^{SM}
.

We then calculate the fiducial cross-sections of WH and ZH within the SMEFT as a function of the relevant SMEFT WC (Eq.
4
), using the
SMEFTsim_general_MwScheme_UFO
package
[
25
,
26
]
, which is a
Feynrules

[
27
]
implementation of SMEFT at leading order (LO) in the Warsaw basis. We use
MadGraph5-aMC@NLO-v3.5.4

[
28
]
to calculate the fiducial cross-sections of WH and ZH in the four STXS regions as quadratic polynomials in the WCs,

\displaystyle\sigma^{NP,MG5}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}C_{i}+\sum_{j\leq k}^{n}b_{jk}C_{j}C_{k}.

(21)

The numerical values of the coefficients (
a_{i},b_{jk}
) are determined by fitting the cross-sections, treating the WCs as variables and
a_{i},b_{jk}
as fitting parameters (see Ref.
[
29
]
for a detailed discussion of this method).
Explicit values for these coefficients are relegated to Appendix
A
. Identifying the RHS of Eq.
21
with the NP window
\sigma^{exp}-\sigma^{SM}
defined above we calculate the
\chi^{2}
as,

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(VH)=\sum_{i}\frac{\left(\sigma_{VH}^{exp}(i)-\sigma_{VH}^{SM}(i)-\sigma_{VH}^{MG5}(i)\right)^{2}}{\left[\delta\sigma_{VH}^{exp}(i)\right]^{2}},

(22)

where the sum runs over the four STXS regions. Since the theory uncertainties are significantly smaller than the quoted total experimental uncertainty, we ignore them. Our results in Eq.
35
indicate that the dominant EFT effect for the WH mode comes from the
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi q}^{(3)}
, with a subleading contribution from the
\mathcal{O}_{dW}
. Other operators do not contribute to WH, whereas ZH is also sensitive to
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi q}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{O}_{\phi d}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{dB}
.

III.2
Constraints from mono-jet searches

The semileptonic SMEFT operators,
\mathcal{O}_{\ell q}^{(1)},\mathcal{O}_{\ell q}^{(3)},\mathcal{O}_{\ell d}
, as well as the semi-bosonic operators,
\mathcal{O}_{dW},\mathcal{O}_{dB},\mathcal{O}_{\varphi q}^{(1)},\mathcal{O}_{\varphi q}^{(3)},\mathcal{O}_{\phi d}
affect monojet production through the process
pp\to\nu\nu+j
and their coefficients will therefore be constrained by monojet measurements at the LHC. Ref.
[
9
]
recently investigated these constraints using the ATLAS measurement from Ref.
[
30
]
. In this work, we repeat the recast of the ATLAS analysis to include it in our
combined

\chi^{2}
from all processes. The relevant results of Ref.
[
30
]
are reproduced in Appendix
B
for convenience.

For our recast we use
MadGraph5-aMC@NLO-v3.5.4

[
28
]
with a PDFset
PDF4LHC15_nlo_mc

[
31
]
to simulate events with the
SMEFTsim_general_MwScheme_UFO

[
25
,
26
]
. Showering and hadronization are performed using
Pythia8

[
32
,
33
]
for all the events and
Delphes3

[
34
]
is used to take into account the detector effects using the ATLAS detector card, including pile-up. This detector card applies all detector efficiencies and resolutions and also constructs physical objects, such as jets, leptons, and missing-
p_{T}
. Jets are refined with pile-up subtraction. Following Ref.
[
30
]
we use the Anti-KT jet reconstruction algorithm with a radius parameter 0.4. The efficiencies of other objects are also modified to match Ref.
[
30
]
. Once we have all the objects from
Delphes3
, we perform the analysis with the same event selection criteria as Ref.
[
30
]
. To validate our recast, we generate the dominant SM backgrounds and check the event yields in different
\rm E{\!\!\!/}_{T}
bins, finding that they match the ATLAS estimation of the SM backgrounds to within
\sim 7-20\%
. This slight mismatch between the two estimations is expected, as our approach does not precisely account for all sources of uncertainties included in the partially data-driven experimental analysis. However, the impact of this mismatch on the calculation of the effects of different WCs is negligible compared to the uncertainty in experimental event yields.

With this MC setup, we proceed to compute the number of monojet events (
\mathcal{N}=\sigma\times\mathcal{L}\times\epsilon
) as polynomials in the WCs for each
\rm E{\!\!\!/}_{T}
-bin:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{NP}(\vec{\mathcal{C}})=\mathcal{N}^{EFT}(\vec{\mathcal{C}})-\mathcal{N}^{SM}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}\beta_{i}+\sum_{j\leq k}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{j}\mathcal{C}_{k}\gamma_{jk},

(23)

with explicit coefficients tabulated in Appendix
B
. Note that for the dipole operators, only the combination
\mathcal{C}_{dZ}=\cos{\theta_W}\,\mathcal{C}_{dW}+\sin{\theta_W}\,\mathcal{C}_{dB}
appears in this case. Our results suggest that these events are mostly sensitive to
\mathcal{C}_{dZ}
, followed by
\mathcal{C}_{\varphi q}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\varphi q}^{(3)}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\ell q}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\ell q}^{(3)}
, where only the later four interfere with SM.

We then use these numbers in combination with the ATLAS results to construct a
\chi^{2}
. The NP window,
\Delta\mathcal{N}^{exp}
is extracted by subtracting from the observed number of events for each
\rm E{\!\!\!/}_{T}
bin, the SM backgrounds reported by ATLAS. Consequently, we write the
\chi^{2}
as:

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(MJ)=\frac{\left(\Delta\mathcal{N}^{exp}-\mathcal{N}^{NP}(\mathcal{C})\right)^{2}}{\left[\delta\left(\Delta\mathcal{N}\right)\right]^{2}}.

(24)

The uncertainty
\delta\left(\Delta\mathcal{N}\right)
appearing in this formula is dominated by that of the ATLAS SM background extraction.

III.3
Constraints from dilepton and monolepton searches

The use of these two processes to constrain SMEFT operators is greatly simplified by the use of the package
HighPT

[
35
,
36
]
, which directly gives us the corresponding
\chi^{2}
. The expressions for this
\chi^{2}
are too lengthy to reproduce here but are available from the authors upon request. These processes are the only ones sensitive to
\mathcal{O}_{\ell edq}
, and the dilepton process is the only one sensitive to
\mathcal{O}_{ed}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{qe}
. As we will see, a comparison of constraints obtained from these two processes will allow us to differentiate between dim-6 contributions to scalar LEFT operators and a particular dim-8 operator that splits the degeneracy between them, as discussed above.

IV.1
Uncertainties in high-luminosity projections

The treatment of uncertainties plays a crucial role in high-luminosity projections at the LHC. While the statistical uncertainty,
\delta_{stat}=\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{obs}}
, scales as
\sqrt{3000/140}
, projections for the background uncertainty can be treated in two ways. An optimistic approach assumes an improvement in systematic uncertainty to match the increased statistics,
\delta_{sys}\to\sqrt{3000/140}\,\delta_{sys}
(rescaled). In contrast, a conservative approach assumes the ratio of background uncertainty to background yield remains unchanged:
\delta_{sys}\to(3000/140)\,\delta_{sys}
(constant). In Fig.
5
, we show the impact of these two approaches on the high-luminosity projections for the
\ell\ell+\ell\nu
, monojet, and VH processes separately. The blue region represents the allowed 95
\%
CL region at
\mathcal{L}=140\;\rm fb^{-1}
, while the yellow and green regions show the projections at
\mathcal{L}=3000\;\rm fb^{-1}
for the “constant” and “rescaled” uncertainty treatments, respectively. For the “rescaled” background uncertainty, the expected deviation of the observed monojet events from the SM background at
\mathcal{L}=3000\;\rm fb^{-1}
exceeds the uncertainty
[
30
]
, which is why the green region for the monojet measurement does not enclose the SM (red point) in Fig.
5
(middle), and yellow region in Fig.
3
. In this study, we use the “rescaled” background uncertainty in all the plots, unless stated otherwise.

VII
Summary and outlook

We have considered the high-
\rm p_{T}
production of dilepton, monolepton, monojet and VH at LHC in order to constrain the dimension-six SMEFT operators that affect rare kaon and hyperon decay.
The dilepton and monolepton searches place the strongest constraints on the four-fermion operators and are the only ones sensitive to
\mathcal{O}_{\ell edq}
,
\mathcal{O}_{ed}
and
\mathcal{O}_{qe}
.
The
\mathcal{O}_{2Q^{\prime}2H}
operators receive their strongest constraints from the VH searches.
\mathcal{O}_{d\gamma}
is constrained by dilepton searches but not VH or monojets and is the direction with the weakest constraints.

The high-
\rm p_{T}
observables provide comparable constraints on the LEFT operators for both muon and electron modes. We find that these constraints are generally weaker than existing ones from rare kaon decay, but they complement them by restricting the directions in parameter space to which kaons are blind. They are also complementary in the sense that they depend on very different sets of assumptions. Constraints extracted from high-
{\rm p_{T}}
observables apply only to UV complete models that match onto a limited subset of dimension six effective operators. We have illustrated the effect of other operators contributing to the same observables by expanding the quark flavours in our operator subset. Other theoretical uncertainties in this case are quantifiable and include higher order effects (higher dimension operators or loop contributions) and MC sampling errors. On the other hand, the largest uncertainty with the low-energy observables with charged leptons (and photons) arises from the large long-distance contributions. At present, these are based on hadronic models where uncertainty estimates are unreliable, and progress awaits lattice calculations.

Appendix C
Widths Z and W boson

The operators affecting Z and W boson widths are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{dW}

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{q}\sigma^{\mu\nu}d\right)\tau^{I}\varphi W_{\mu\nu}^{I}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\varphi q}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\varphi\right)\left(\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}q\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\varphi q}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi^{\dagger}i\tau^{I}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\varphi\right)\left(\bar{q}\tau^{I}\gamma^{\mu}q\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\varphi d}

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi^{\dagger}iD_{\mu}\varphi\right)\left(\bar{d}\gamma^{\mu}d\right),

We consider tree-level correction up to quadratic order in EFT.

C.1
Z-width

The interaction of the Z-boson with a fermion f is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{g}{cos\theta_{W}}Z_{\mu}\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}\left(g_{L}^{f}P_{L}+g_{R}^{f}P_{R}\right)f

(37)

Where, at tree level,

\displaystyle g_{R}^{f,SM}=-s_{W}^{2}Q_{f}\quad{\rm and}\quad g_{L}^{f,SM}=T_{3}^{f}-s_{W}^{2}Q_{f}.

(38)

We define the SMEFT contributions to the couplings as
\delta g_{L}^{f}
and
\delta g_{R}^{f}
; where
\delta g^{f}=g^{f}-g^{f,SM}
.

Therefore, the SMEFT contributions can be written as:

\displaystyle\delta g_{L}^{u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\phi q}^{(1)}-\mathcal{C}_{\phi q}^{(3)}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle\delta g_{L}^{d}

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\phi q}^{(1)}+\mathcal{C}_{\phi q}^{(3)}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle\delta g_{R}^{u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{\phi u}\;(\to 0,\;\textrm{for this work})

(41)

\displaystyle\delta g_{R}^{d}

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{\phi d}

(42)

\frac{G_{F}M_{Z}^{3}}{6\sqrt{2}\pi}=FF\simeq 0.33

C.2
W-width

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{g_{L}^{W}}{\sqrt{2}}W_{\mu}^{+}\bar{u}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}d

\displaystyle\delta g_{L}^{Wq}=\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{\varphi q}^{(3)}

(48)

The total decay width of the W-boson is, therefore,

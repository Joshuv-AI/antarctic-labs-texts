Generalized Families of QFTs
T. Daniel Brennan, and Kenneth Intriligator

Abstract
RG flows and IR phases of QFTs can be constrained by generalized symmetries and their anomalies. Broken symmetries act on the space of coupling constants of families of theories, and can also have IR-constraining family anomalies. We generalize family anomaly considerations to cases of broken generalized/categorical symmetries, including higher-group and non-invertible symmetries. We consider the anomaly inflow and SymTFTs of such generalized families of QFTs, and their implications for RG flows and constraints on the IR phases. As examples, we apply family anomalies to study the IR phases of 4 ​ d 4d QCD-like theories deformed by irrelevant, multi-fermion interactions.

The SymTFT construction requires a choice of (topological) boundary condition

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle QFT|=\sum_{A}Z_{QFT}[A]~\langle A|~,\end{split}

Recently, many of the concepts implemented in spurion analysis have also been revisited in the context of generalized/categorical global symmetries.
The general idea of spurion analysis is the following. Consider a QFT with a 0-form global symmetry

We can consider the broken non-invertible symmetry structure as a generalized type of family identification.
In order to interpret the

3.2.1

Our examples of non-invertible family structures arise when an anomalous phase in the path integral can be canceled by a discrete gauging of a higher form global symmetry

3.2.2
1+1d
Ising Model and Kramers-Wannier Duality

The Ising model is a classic example of a categorical family of theories that arises from explicitly breaking a non-invertible symmetry involving gauging. Kramers-Wannier duality can be phrased in terms of a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
global symmetry that carries an ABJ anomaly
Kapustin:2014gua
;
Shao:2023gho
. In the continuum Fermion description, the Ising model is a free Majorana fermion coupled to a dynamical
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
gauge field
a
that gauges fermion number. This is related via bosonization to the bosonic description of the Ising model. see e.g.
Karch:2019lnn
;
Shao:2023gho
for reviews.

The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
gauge fields coupled to fermions can anomalously depend on the topology of the 2d (orientable, Euclidean) spacetime manifold
M
via the
Arf invariant on
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, i.e. the mod-2 index of the Dirac operator
{{\cal I}}
coupled to the associated
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-gauge field:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm Arf}[a\cdot\rho]={{\cal I}}[a\cdot\rho]~.\end{split}

(77)

Here
\rho
is a base choice of spin structure and
a\cdot\rho
is the spin structure where
\rho
is twisted by the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
gauge field
a
and
the mod-2 index satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\begin{split}{{\cal I}}[(a+b)\cdot\rho]={{\cal I}}[a\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[b\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[\rho]+\int a\cup b~{\rm mod}_{2}~.\end{split}

(78)

For oriented manifold
M
, this implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})|}}\sum_{a}(-1)^{{{\cal I}}[a\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[\rho]+\int a\cup b}=(-1)^{{{\cal I}}[b\cdot\rho]}~.\end{split}

(79)

See
Karch:2019lnn
for more details. The Kramers-Wannier transformation originates from the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}
chiral transformation in the Majorana fermion description:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi\longmapsto i\gamma_{3}\chi~.\end{split}

(80)

The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}
has an ABJ-type mixed anomaly with the gauged
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{F}
fermion number symmetry, so the chiral transformation generates an anomalous shift of the action

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta S=\pi i\,{{\cal I}}[a\cdot\rho]~.\end{split}

(81)

The partition function of the Majorana fermion with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{F}
is gauged can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{1}|}}\sum_{a\in H^{1}(M_{2};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{Maj}[a]\times(-1)^{\int B\cup a}~.\end{split}

(82)

The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}
transformation acts on the partition function by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}:Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]\longmapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{1}|}}\sum_{a\in H^{1}(M_{2};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})}Z_{Maj}[a]\times(-1)^{\int B\cup a+{{\cal I}}[a\cdot\rho]}~.\end{split}

(83)

We can turn this ABJ-anomalous symmetry into a true (non-invertible) symmetry by gauging the dual
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{(0)}
symmetry:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal S}:Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]~\longmapsto~\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{1}|}}\sum_{b}Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[b]\times(-1)^{{{\cal I}}[b\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[B\cdot\rho]+\int b\cup B}~.\end{split}

(84)

The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}
transformation combined with the
{\cal S}
action is then a symmetry of the theory:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal W}:Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]&\longmapsto\frac{1}{|H^{1}|}\sum_{a,b}Z_{Maj}[a]\times(-1)^{\int a\cup b+\int b\cup B+{{\cal I}}[a\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[b\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[B\cdot\rho]}\\
&=Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]\end{split}

(85)

where we used the orientability of spacetime. Since
B\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the background gauge field for the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
symmetry of the Ising model which is generated by the
a_{1}
Wilson lines (sometimes labeled as
\eta
-lines in the literature), we can interpret the local sum over
b
as the 1-gauging of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
symmetry of the Ising model. To construct a topological symmetry operator for this symmetry, we would need to perform the gauging on the half-space defined by the symmetry operator world-line. See
Choi:2022zal
;
Choi:2021kmx
for more details.

Consider acting twice with the Kramers-Wannier transformation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal W}^{2}:Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]&=\frac{1}{|H^{1}|^{3/2}}\sum_{a,b,c}Z_{Maj}[a]\times(-1)^{\int a\cup b+\int b\cup c+\int c\cup B+{{\cal I}}[B\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[b\cdot\rho]}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{1}|}}\sum_{a,b}Z_{Maj}[a]\times(-1)^{\int a\cup b+{{\cal I}}[B\cdot\rho]+{{\cal I}}[b\cdot\rho]}\,\delta(b+B)\\
&=Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[B]\end{split}

(86)

Implementing the gauging for
{\cal W}^{2}
operation on a half-space, which also describes the insertion of a topological symmetry operator, results in a condensation defect. This follows from the fact that the constraint imposed by integrating over
c
is relaxed at the topological boundary where
c\big|_{bnd}=0
, as in
Choi:2022zal
;
Choi:2021kmx
. This is how the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
condensation defect of the Kramers-Wannier duality arises even though there is no remnant of the non-invertibility of Kramers-Wannier duality on the partition function.

Deforming away from the CFT via and added mass term

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal L}_{m}=im\bar{\chi}\gamma^{3}\chi~\end{split}

(87)

explicitly breaks the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
Kramers-Wannier symmetry, as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}:\bar{\chi}\gamma^{3}\chi\longmapsto-\bar{\chi}\gamma^{3}\chi~.\end{split}

(88)

The broken Kramers-Wannier symmetry then leads to a non-invertible family of theories

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal W}:Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[m;B]\longmapsto Z_{Maj/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}[-m;B]~,\end{split}

(89)

where
{\cal W}
is the composition of a discrete gauging and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{c}
transformation. This is just a rephrasing of the classic Kramers-Wannier duality of the
2d
Ising model.

We can illustrate this decompactification of the holonomy in the example of a single massless

global symmetry with charge

) and

for further discussion.

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal L}_{mass}=m\bar{\psi}\psi~.\end{split}

(101)

Now we see that the action differs in the limit

by an integral Chern-Simons term that only depends on the dynamical gauge field. Unlike the case of higher group global symmetries, this cannot be canceled by a discrete gauging. Here, the reason that we do not get a non-invertible family structure is that although the integral part of the

-valued. This prevents us from using a topological gauging that realizes the non-invertible family structure.

This is different than the case of higher group global symmetries because in that case the twisted compactification does not require trivializing any dynamical gauge fields.

12

12

This can be matched e.g. if the

\theta\to\theta+2\pi

4.1
Anomalies of Higher Families

Families of QFTs with higher symmetry acting on the space of coupling constants can likewise have anomalies. A higher family anomaly is encoded by the partition function being a non-trivial section over the reduced parameter space
{\cal B}/\Lambda=\frac{{\cal C}_{\theta}\times B_{\nabla}{\cal G}}{\Lambda}

35
). Assume that the total group-like symmetry is

is the higher-group that participates in the higher family structure and

\theta\mapsto\theta+2\pi
and the
\mathbb{G}
background gauge field

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\theta}[B]=Z_{\theta+2\pi}[B+\Lambda]\times e^{i\int\omega_{d}(A)}~,\end{split}

(107)

where
A
are the background
G
gauge fields. The anomaly is matched by inflow via the

4.2.2
Categorical Families of QFTs from Gauging

Now we will describe a general mechanism for generating non-invertible families of theories from anomalous continuous families of theories.
Consider a family of theories with
G
symmetry which is indexed by a single parameter
\theta\in S^{1}
, and suppose that the family has an anomaly of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal A}=\int d\theta\wedge\omega_{d}(A)~,\end{split}

(139)

where
A
are the background
G
gauge fields and
\theta
has period
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi
.

If we gauge a subgroup
H\subseteq G
, then we can decompose the anomaly into three terms:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal A}=\int d\theta\wedge\left(\omega_{d}(a)^{(0)}+\omega_{d}^{(1)}(a,A)+\omega_{d}^{(2)}(A)\right)~,\end{split}

(140)

where
a
are the dynamical gauge fields for
H
and
A
are the background gauge fields for
G/H
. The term
\omega_{d}^{(0)}
is only dependent on the dynamical gauge field and constitutes a sort of “family ABJ anomaly” which one would be tempted to say breaks some of the family identifications.
The term
\omega_{d}^{(2)}
is only dependent on the background gauge field and corresponds to the remaining family anomaly. The term
\omega_{d}^{(1)}
is dependent on both
A
and

which we assume vanishes for
A=0
.

When

is an abelian group,
\omega_{d}^{(0)}
factorizes into a polynomial in
a
and its field strength (or Bockstein as appropriate). In this case the phase can often be eliminated by a non-invertible gauging (with torsion) of the dual, emergent symmetry which generalizes the
{\cal S}_{q}
operation described in the previous section. This allows us to retain the periodicity
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi
and results in a non-invertible family of QFTs.

Similarly,
\omega_{d}^{(1)}
can be further decomposed into terms which are a polynomial in
a,\delta a
times a lower-dimensional quantized phase:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega_{d}^{(1)}=\sum_{n}p_{n}(a,\delta a)\wedge\Omega^{(d)}_{n}(A)~,\end{split}

(141)

where the
\Omega^{(d)}_{n}(A)
only depend on the background gauge fields. In this decomposition each
p_{n}(a,\delta a)
must be quantized (since
\omega_{d}^{(1)}
is quantized) and therefore can be coupled to a background gauge field
C^{(n)}
. The background gauge transformations of the
C^{(n)}
can then be modified so that the terms in
\omega^{(1)}_{d}
that are generated by the shift
\theta\mapsto\theta+2\pi
are removed. This results in a higher family structure.

The categorical family structure that these three terms generate mimics the categorical symmetry structure that arises from gauging anomalous symmetries.

An illustrative example of this behavior is
4d

SU(NM)

are co-prime. In this theory, there is a

\displaystyle\begin{split}S=...+\frac{i\theta}{8\pi^{2}}\int\,\textrm{Tr}\,[F\wedge F]~,\end{split}

(142)

where

. This family of theories is anomalous with anomaly

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal A}=\frac{NM-1}{2NM}\int d\theta\cup{\cal P}(B_{2}^{(e)})~,\end{split}

(143)

where
B_{2}^{(e)}
is the background
{\mathbb{Z}}_{NM}^{(1)}

Now consider gauging

\displaystyle\begin{split}B_{2}^{(e)}=Nw_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{M})+M{\widetilde{B}}_{2}^{(e)}~,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal A}=\frac{N(NM-1)}{2M}\int d\theta\cup{\cal P}(w_{2})+\frac{M(NM-1)}{2N}\int d\theta\cup{\cal P}({\widetilde{B}}_{2}^{(e)})~.\end{split}

{\cal T}_{\theta}\cong{\cal T}_{\theta+2\pi}
with a discrete global symmetry group
{\cal G}=G^{(p-1)}\times G^{(d-p-1)}
that carries a family anomaly of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal A}=\frac{2\pi}{N}\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\cup A_{p}\cup B_{d-p}~,\end{split}

(149)

where
A_{p},B_{d-p}
are background gauge fields for
G^{(p-1)},G^{(d-p-1)}
respectively. We assume that
{\cal G}
does not participate in any other anomalies so that the partition function obeys

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\theta}[M_{d};A_{p}+d\lambda_{p-1},B_{d-p}+d\Lambda_{d-p-1}]=Z_{\theta}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{d-p}]\quad,\quad\forall\theta\in S^{1}~.\end{split}

(150)

The family anomaly implies that the partition function obeys the relation

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\theta}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{d-p}]=Z_{\theta+2\pi}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{d-p}]\times e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N}\int A_{p}\cup B_{d-p}}~.\end{split}

(151)

Let us determine the conditions for when this family of theories can flow to a family of gapped phases. As proven in
Cordova:2019bsd
, if the family of theories flows to a family of unitary,
{\cal G}
-preserving gapped phases – i.e.
{\cal G}
is not spontaneously broken at any
\theta
– then the partition function is non-zero on
S^{p}\times S^{d-p}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\theta}[S^{p}\times S^{d-p};A_{p},B_{d-p}]\neq 0\quad,\quad\forall\theta\in S^{1}~.\end{split}

(152)

If we were to assume what we want to disprove, that the partition function is a smooth function of
\theta
without phase transitions, we can define the continuous function of
\theta
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{i\Phi(\theta,A,B)}=\frac{Z_{\theta}[S^{p}\times S^{d-p};A_{p},B_{d-p}]}{\big|Z_{\theta}[S^{p}\times S^{d-p};A_{p},B_{d-p}]\big|}~.\end{split}

(153)

As in
Brennan:2024tlw
we can use the anomalous phase relation of the path integral (
151
) to derive a contradiction. If we choose
A_{p},B_{d-p}
so that the phase is non-trivial on
S^{p}\times S^{d-p}
, the function
\Phi
must be of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(\theta,A,B)=f(\theta)\int A_{p}\cup B_{d-p}+\Theta(\theta,A,B)~,\end{split}

(154)

where the variation of the partition function (
151
) implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\theta+2\pi)=f(\theta)+\frac{2\pi}{N}\quad,\quad\Theta(\theta+2\pi,A,B)=\Theta(\theta,A,B)~.\end{split}

(155)

Gauge invariance implies that the functions
f(\theta),\Theta(\theta,A,B)
obey

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\theta)\in\frac{2\pi}{N}{\mathbb{Z}}~,~\forall\theta\in S^{1}\quad,\quad\Theta(\theta,A+d\Lambda,B+d\Lambda^{\prime})=\Theta(\theta,A,B)~,\end{split}

(156)

The term proportional to
f(\theta)
is necessary to match the anomaly. But, since no continuous functions
f(\theta)
exist that obeys both of these conditions, the continuous phase
\Phi(\theta,A,B)
cannot reproduce the anomaly and the partition function of a continuous family of unitary, gapped,
{\cal G}
-preserving phases cannot carry the family anomaly. This implies that the IR theory must be either 1.) gapless, 2.) spontaneously break
{\cal G}
, or 3.) exhibit a phase transition (i.e. the partition function is discontinuous in
\theta
).

6
Families of Deformations of non-Abelian Gauge Theories

We now consider categorical families of theories in the context of some irrelevant deformations of 4d non-abelian gauge theories. We consider IR-irrelevant deformations to keep the IR theory under control (though in some cases the deformations we consider will turn out to be dangerously irrelevant). The theory with the irrelevant deformation can be regarded as the low-energy effective field theory of a UV completion along some RG flow.

Appendix A
Constraints on Non-Invertible SPT Phases from Anomalies

In this appendix, we will study how a
d
-dimensional SPT phase
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{(p-1)}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{(p-1)}
global symmetry for
d=2p
transforms under a non-invertible family relation as discussed in Section
4.4.2
. Here we will consider the case where the non-invertible family structure is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal T}_{\theta}\cong{\cal S}{\cal T}_{\theta+2\pi}\end{split}

(240)

where the
{\cal S}
gauging relation is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal S}Z[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{p}|}}\sum_{b_{p}\in H^{p}(M_{d};{\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}Z[M_{d};A_{p},b_{p}]~e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2N}\int L{\cal P}(B_{p})+2R\,B_{p}\cup b_{p}+M{\cal P}(b_{p})}~,\end{split}

(241)

where here
{\cal P}(B_{p})
is the Pontryagin square for
N
even and
{\cal P}(B_{p})=B_{p}\cup B_{p}
for
N
odd,
L,M\in\tilde{\gamma}(N){\mathbb{Z}}_{2N}
, and
R\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma(N)=\begin{cases}1&N-{\rm odd}\\
2&N-{\rm even}\end{cases}\quad,\quad\tilde{\gamma}(N)=\begin{cases}2&N-{\rm odd}\\
1&N-{\rm even}\end{cases}\end{split}

(242)

We will additional assume the theory has a non-invertible family anomaly

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal S}Z_{\theta}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]=Z_{\theta+2\pi}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]~e^{-\frac{2\pi iK}{2N}\int{\cal P}(A_{p})}~,\end{split}

(243)

where again
K\in\tilde{\gamma}(N){\mathbb{Z}}_{2N}
. Let us determine whether or not the SPT phase

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{SPT}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2N}\int\left(2k_{1}A_{p}\cup B_{p}+k_{2}{\cal P}(B_{p})\right)}~,\end{split}

(244)

can match the non-invertible family anomaly for some choice of coefficients
k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
and
k_{2}\in\tilde{\gamma}(N){\mathbb{Z}}_{2N}
.

We first consider even
N
, where
\tilde{\gamma}(N)=1
. We can now compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\cal S}Z_{SPT}[A,B]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{p}|}}\sum_{b\in H^{p}}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2N}\int 2k_{1}A\cup b+(k_{2}+M){\cal P}(b)+2RB\cup b+L{\cal P}(B)}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{p}|}}\sum_{b\in H^{p}}e^{\frac{2\pi i(k_{2}+M)}{2N}\int{\cal P}(b+\ell k_{1}A+\ell RB)+\frac{2\pi i}{2N}\int(L-\ell R^{2}){\cal P}(B)-2\ell k_{1}RA\cup B-\ell k_{1}^{2}{\cal P}(A)}\\
&=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{2N}\int(L-\ell R^{2}){\cal P}(B)-2\ell k_{1}RA\cup B-\ell k_{1}^{2}{\cal P}(A)}\end{split}

(245)

where
\ell(k_{2}+M)=1~{\rm mod}_{2N}
. For the SPT to match the family anomaly,
k_{1},k_{2}
must obey

\displaystyle\begin{split}2k_{1}=-2k_{1}\ell R~{\rm mod}_{2N}~,\quad L-\ell R^{2}=k_{2}~{\rm mod}_{2N}~,\quad k_{1}^{2}\ell=K~{\rm mod}_{2N}~.\end{split}

(246)

We can simply the first equation to
\ell R=-1~{\rm mod}_{N}
. This implies that both
\ell,R
are co-prime with
N
and therefore that they are odd numbers:
\gamma(R)=\gamma(\ell)=1
. To solve the rest of the equations it is convenient to first choose a lift of
R
mod
2N
so that the first equation above becomes
\ell R=-1+\alpha N~{\rm mod}_{2N}
for
\alpha=0,1
. Since
R\sim R+N
, the fact that
\ell
is odd implies that
R\mapsto R+N
shifts
\alpha\mapsto\alpha+1
and we can without loss of generality set
\alpha=0
. Now we can reduce the other equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{2}+M=-R~{\rm mod}_{2N}~,\quad L+R=k_{2}~{\rm mod}_{2N}\quad,\quad k_{1}^{2}=-RK~{\rm mod}_{2N}~.\end{split}

(247)

These have an exact solution along with a constraint:

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{2}=-M-R~{\rm mod}_{2N}~,\quad L+2R+M=0~{\rm mod}_{2N}~,\quad k_{1}^{2}=-RK~{\rm mod}_{2N}~.\end{split}

(248)

For
N
odd,
\tilde{\gamma}(N)=2
and all of the coefficients effectively become
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal S}Z[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{p}|}}\sum_{b_{p}\in H^{p}(M_{d};{\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}Z[M_{d};A_{p},b_{p}]~e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}\int L{\cal P}(B_{p})+R\,B_{p}\cup b_{p}+M{\cal P}(b_{p})}~,\\

{\cal S}Z_{\theta}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]&=Z_{\theta+2\pi}[M_{d};A_{p},B_{p}]~e^{-\frac{2\pi iK}{N}\int{\cal P}(A_{p})}~.\end{split}

(249)

We then obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal S}Z_{SPT}[A,B]&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H^{p}|}}\sum_{b\in H^{p}}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}\int k_{1}A\cup b+(k_{2}+M)b^{2}+RB\cup b+LB^{2}}\\
&=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}\int(L-\ell^{2}R^{2}(k_{2}+M))B^{2}-\ell k_{1}RA\cup B-\ell^{2}k_{1}^{2}(k_{2}+M)A^{2}}\end{split}

(250)

where
x^{2}=x\cup x
,
2\ell(k_{2}+M)=1~{\rm mod}_{N}
, and we have dropped all gravitational terms. This implies that the SPT can match the anomaly provided
k_{1},k_{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{1}=-k_{1}\ell R~{\rm mod}_{N}~,\quad L-\ell^{2}R^{2}(k_{2}+M)=k_{2}~{\rm mod}_{N}~,\quad k_{1}^{2}\ell^{2}(k_{2}+M)=K~{\rm mod}_{N}~.\end{split}

(251)

These can be simplified: the first equation implies
\ell R=-1~{\rm mod}_{N}

\displaystyle\begin{split}L-2k_{2}-M=0~{\rm mod}_{N}\quad,\quad k_{1}^{2}\ell(k_{2}+M)=-RK~{\rm mod}_{N}~.\end{split}

(252)

Using
2\ell(k_{2}+M)=1~{\rm mod}_{N}

k_{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{1}^{2}=-2RK~{\rm mod}_{2}\quad,\quad 2k_{2}=-2M-R~{\rm mod}_{N}~.\end{split}

(253)

253

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{2}=-M-\frac{R-\gamma(R)N}{2}~{\rm mod}_{N}~,\quad k_{1}^{2}=-2RK~{\rm mod}_{N}~,\quad L+R+M=0~{\rm mod}_{N}~.\end{split}

(254)

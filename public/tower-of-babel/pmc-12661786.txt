Effect of mydriasis on anterior segment biometric parameters of patients with age-related cataracts
Authors: Shasha Xue, Yunxiao Wang, Fenglei Wang, Zhiying Yu, Xinyu Wang, Xiaomei Wan, Ling Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo determine the risk of mydriasis-induced angle closure in patients with intumescent age-related cataracts with different anterior chamber angle widths through a comparative analysis of anterior segment biometric parameters before and after mydriasis.MethodsA total of 195 patients with cortical intumescent cataracts were grouped into four according to the mean TIA in the four quadrants before mydriasis as follows: extremely narrow-angle group, 0° < TIA ≤ 10°; narrow-angle group, 10° < TIA ≤ 20°; wide-angle group, 20° < TIA ≤ 30°; and ultra-wide-angle group, TIA > 30°. The biological parameters obtained with UBM before and after mydriasis were compared.ResultsFor the extremely narrow-angle group, the TIA and AOD500 values could increase or decrease after mydriasis, indicating that the anterior chamber angle could widen or narrow, and there was a slight but statistically significant increase in IOP after dilation. For the other groups, the AOD500 and TIA significantly increased after mydriasis, signifying a more open anterior chamber angle.ConclusionPatients with 0° < TIA ≤ 10° have a high risk of angle closure and should undergo laser peripheral iridotomy before mydriasis to prevent acute elevation of intraocular pressure. Patients with 10° < TIA ≤ 20° have a low risk, and they can undergo routine mydriasis, but changes in their anterior chamber morphology should be closely monitored. When TIA > 20°, the risk is almost negligible, and mydriasis can be safely induced.Keywords: Cataract, Intumescent phase, Mydriasis, Preoptic segment parameters, Ultrasound biomicroscopy

Introduction

With increasing age, the lens becomes more turgid. Water accumulates rapidly due to osmotic pressure changes, and the lens swells and enlarges. This pushes the iris forward, and the anterior chamber becomes shallower, resulting in a narrower anterior chamber angle. Acute anterior chamber angle closure may develop in patients with age-related cataracts who have anatomically narrow anterior chamber angles, especially during the intumescent phase of cortical cataracts [1, 2]. Previous studies have shown that pupil dilation can exacerbate the narrowing of the anterior chamber angle and induce acute angle closure in patients with narrow anterior chamber angles [3, 4]. In our prior investigation, we discovered that individuals with cortical age-related cataracts often present with moderate to extremely narrow anterior chamber angles [5]. This raises concerns about the potential for mydriasis to induce acute angle closure. However, the extent of this risk and whether it varies among patients with different anterior chamber angles remains unexplored.

Ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM) is useful for examining the anterior segment of the eye. It can provide clear images of the anterior and part of the middle segments of the eye and measure various biological parameters. Its advantages include the ability to display the structures of the anterior segment with high resolution dynamically and the capability to quantitatively measure relevant parameters [6]. It is also unaffected by refractive media.

In this study, we used UBM to comparatively analyze the biological parameters of the anterior segment of the eye before and after pupil dilation in patients with age-related, intumescent, cortical cataracts with different anterior chamber angles and determined the high-risk group for mydriasis-induced anterior chamber angle closure.

Materials and methods

Study participants

The study enrolled 195 patients with cortical, intumescent age-related cataracts (LOCS III: C3-C5) who were scheduled to undergo right eye cataract phacoemulsification combined with intraocular lens implantation at the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University between June 2022 and June 2023. All patients were preoperatively diagnosed, aged ≥ 45 years, had preoperative intraocular pressure within 10–21 mmHg, had non-dilated pupils with diameters of ≤ 4.0 mm, and had pupillary light reflex. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) presence of other ocular diseases such as corneal lesions, glaucoma, uveitis, subluxated lens, choroidal retinal lesions, and optic nerve disorders; (b) history of intraocular surgery, eye trauma, or previous corneal, iris, and fundus laser treatments; (c) conditions affecting pupillary dynamics such as iridocorneal adhesions, pseudoexfoliation and its syndrome; (d) poor fixation or cooperation. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University and registered with the clinical trials registration identifier ChiCTR2200056856 on February 21, 2022. This study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki, and all participants signed an informed consent form.

Examinations and data collection

All patients underwent preoperative routine assessments, including best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA; Topcon Ltd, CV-5000, Japan), slit-lamp examination (Haag-Streit Ltd, BM 900, Switzerland), and fundus examination (Volk Ltd, VSFNC, USA). Intraocular pressure (IOP), biometry, and anterior chamber imaging were performed before and after pupillary dilation using the Goldmann applanation tonometer, IOL Master (IOL Master 700, Carl Zeiss Meditec AG, Germany), and UBM (SW-3200 L, Suoer, China), respectively.

UBM examination:

Surface anesthetic (Benoxil 0.4% solution) was administered three times at 5-min intervals for surface anesthesia. The patient was asked to close their eyes for 5 min and assume a supine position after the final administration. An appropriately sized eye cup for the lid fissure was placed in the conjunctival sac. Sterile injection fluid was introduced into the cup as an acoustic coupling agent. The right eye of the patient was scanned, and radial images of the corneal limbus were obtained in the superior (12 o’clock), nasal (3 o’clock), inferior (6 o’clock), and temporal (9 o’clock) directions by the same experienced operator. The angle opening distance of 500 μm (AOD500), trabecular iris angle (TIA), iris-lens angle (ILA), iris-lens contact distance (ILCD), iris span (IS), and iris convexity (IC) in the four directions were obtained using the UBM software. The images obtained in the horizontal direction at the center of the pupil (3–9 points) were also examined to determine the pupil diameter (PD), central anterior chamber depth (ACD), central corneal thickness (CCT), anterior chamber width (ACW), lens vault (LV), and relative lens position (RLP), which was calculated as follows.

IOL master examination:

Biometry was carried out by the same skilled operator. Three consecutive measurements were made, and the system automatically derived the average values of the parameters that were used to derive the lens thickness (LT) and axial length (AL).

IOP measurement:

IOP was measured 5 min before conducting the UBM examination, and the results of three measurements were averaged.

The above procedure was repeated at the same time on the following day after dilation. Tropicamide eye drops were administered into the conjunctival sac three times at 5-min intervals. The patients were asked to close their eyes for 40 min after the final administration. Subsequently, IOP, Master, and UBM examinations were performed when the pupil diameter was ≥ 6 mm.

The parameters following parameters were measured. The ACD (mm) was measured as the vertical distance from the inner surface of the central cornea to the anterior surface of the lens. The AOD500 (mm) was measured by initially tracing from the scleral prominence and tracing along the anterior surface of the corneal endothelium to a point located 500 μm anterior to the scleral prominence. From this point, a plumb line was drawn perpendicular to the corneal endothelium until it intersected with the anterior surface of the iris. This vertical line was referred to as AOD500. AOD500 provides an indirect reflection of the extent of angle opening. The TIA (°) corresponds to the anterior chamber angle at 500 μm from the scleral spur, often referred to as ACA500. To determine this angle, a triangle was constructed with AOD500 as the base and the root of the iris as the apex. The angle formed at this apex represented the TIA. Essentially, this measurement provides an indirect indication of the degree of openness of the angle. The ILA (°) was determined by identifying the point of contact between the posterior surface of the iris and the anterior surface of the lens as the vertex. Two tangent lines were drawn from this vertex; one was along the posterior surface of the iris and the other was along the anterior surface of the lens. The angle formed by these lines is known as the ILA. These data provide a direct reflection of the relative positioning of the lens and the central iris and, indirectly, offer insights into the degree of attachment and separation between the lens and the iris. The ILCD (mm) was measured as the distance between the anterior and posterior surfaces of the iris and the contact point on the anterior surface of the lens. The IC (mm) was measured as the curvature of the posterior surface of the iris and expressed indirectly as the length of the plumb line from the most inflated part of the iris to the line connecting the root of the iris to the apex of the iris. Positive IC values indicate convex irises, whereas negative values indicate concave irises. When the IC value is zero, it signifies that the iris has a flat morphology, that is, a flat iris. The plateau iris is characterized by a raised and folded appearance of the iris root, with the attachment point being anterior, leading to a narrowed angle between the iris and the lens. For irises with both anterior and posterior bulging, the direction of the greatest bulge was used. The IS (mm) represents the straight-line distance from the attachment points of the root of the posterior surface of the iris to the apex of the iris (the apex of the iris is the position of the midpoint of the ILCD). This value directly reflects the average distance the iris extends within the eye toward the center, which can indirectly reflect the size of the pupil. The ACW (mm) was measured as the horizontal distance between the scleral prominences on both sides. The LV (mm) was measured as the vertical distance from the anterior pole of the lens to the horizontal line of the scleral prominence. The LT (mm) was measured as the distance from the center of the anterior surface of the crystal to the center of the posterior surface of the lens. The AL (mm) was measured as the distance from the tear film to the retinal pigment epithelium (Figures 1 and 2).

LOCS III Cataract Classification System: Nuclear Color (NC) assesses the color changes in the lens nucleus, ranging from 1 (clear) to 6 (dark brown). Nuclear Opalescence (NO) evaluates the intensity of light scattering in the nucleus, ranging from 1 (clear) to 6 (fully opaque). Cortical Cataract (C) measures the extent and density of cortical opacities, ranging from 1 (no opacity) to 5 (fully opaque cortex). Posterior Subcapsular Cataract (P) rates the opacification in the posterior subcapsular region, ranging from 1 (no opacity) to 5 (extensive opacity). Nuclear Cataract is characterized by high NC and NO scores, with low C and P scores. Cortical Cataract is identified by a high C score, with low NC, NO, and P scores. Posterior Subcapsular Cataract is indicated by a high P score, with low scores in other dimensions.

Data analysis

The mean TIA values in the superior (12 o’clock), inferior (6 o’clock), nasal (3 o’clock), and temporal (9 o’clock) quadrants before pupil dilation were used to group the patients into the following: extremely narrow anterior chamber angle group (Group A), 36 eyes (male/female = 17/19), 0° < TIA ≤ 10°; narrow anterior chamber angle group (Group B), 59 eyes (male/female = 23/26), 10° < TIA ≤ 20°; wide anterior chamber angle group (Group C), 59 eyes (male/female = 22/27), 20° < TIA ≤ 30°; and ultra-wide anterior chamber angle group (Group D), 41 eyes (male/female = 21/20), TIA > 30°.

For each of these groups, sex, age, and various parameters such as BCVA, IOP, PD, LV, AL, ACD, CCT, ACW, and RLP before and after pupil dilation were compared. The superior, inferior, nasal, and temporal AOD500, TIA, ILA, ILCD, IS, and IC values before and after pupil dilation were also compared.

Statistical analysis

The GraphPad Prism software was used to draw statistical graphs, while SPSS 22.0 software was used for the statistical analysis. The measured parameter data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̅ ± s), and the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was performed to assess the normality of their distributions. The t-test for independent samples was used to compare the normally distributed data of the subgroups, while the t-test for paired samples was used for inter-group comparisons. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare the non-normally distributed data. P-values of < 0.05 represented statistical significance.

There was a tendency for the changes in LT (△LT) before and after mydriasis to decrease as the anterior chamber angle widened in all the groups, except Group A. The △LT values of Groups B and D and Groups C and D were significantly different (P = 0.001, 0.003, respectively). However, the other groups did not show statistically significant differences. The LV significantly reduced after, relative to before, pupil dilation. The changes in LV (ΔLV) before and after pupil dilation values of Groups A and C, Groups A and D, and Groups B and D were significantly different (P = 0.014, 0.015, 0.020). However, there was an overall trend of increase in the ΔLV with the widening of the anterior chamber angle (Table 5).

Both ILCD and IC decreased after pupil dilation. With the widening of the anterior chamber angle, there was a tendency for the changes in ILCD before and after pupil dilation (△ILCD) to increase, while the changes in IC (△IC) tended to decrease. The △ILCD values of Groups A and C, Groups A and D, Groups B and C, and Groups B and D were significantly different (All P < 0.05). The △IC values of Groups A and D, Groups B and D, and Groups C and D were also significantly different (All P < 0.05) (Table 5).

Discussion

In patients with a nearly closed anterior chamber angle, there has been a consensus on laser iris perimetry to prevent IOP elevation before pupil dilation for surgery. Failing to do so can lead to acute closure of the anterior chamber angle, a sudden increase in IOP, and irreversible eye damage [7–10]. Several studies have indicated that middle-aged and older individuals tend to have thicker lenses, shallower anterior chambers, and moderately or extremely narrow anterior chamber angles [11]. These anatomical features often go unnoticed because they do not typically cause ocular discomfort or noticeable symptoms. However, under specific conditions, such as when dilating the pupil for fundus examination or cataract surgery in patients with anatomically shallow anterior chambers and intumescent cataracts, the IOP may increase due to the iris accumulates to the anterior chamber angle when the pupil dilates. This mechanical blockage of the anterior chamber angle can impede the outflow of aqueous humor, which may result in a medically induced increase in IOP. Despite previous studies identifying specific static anatomical parameters capable of distinguishing normal eyes with wide angles from those with narrow angles [12], these parameters cannot differentiate eyes with a high risk of anterior chamber angle closure from those with relatively low risk. This study aimed to analyze changes in ocular parameters after mydriasis in patients with intumescent cataracts and different anterior chamber angles. The goal is to provide valuable insights for clinical applications. However, it is essential to acknowledge certain limitations in this research. For ethical reasons, patients with fully closed anterior chamber angles were not included, and it remains unclear whether such patients would exhibit slightly more open or more closed angles after pupil dilation.

In our study, we observed distinct age-related trends in the anterior chamber angle groups. Specifically, we found that the anterior chamber becomes progressively shallower as the lens thickness increases with age. This trend continues even after a certain age but at a slower rate. We are yet to establish an age at which the anterior chamber depth no longer changes [13–15]. In all patients in this study, regardless of the width of the anterior chamber angle, the depth of the central anterior chamber significantly increased after, relative to before pupil dilation. This can be attributed to various factors. First, the tightening of the suspensory ligament following ciliary muscle paralysis led to a reduction in lens thickness (resulting in decreased LT) and a flatter lens profile (causing a decrease in LV). Second, pupil dilation reduced the contact area between the iris and the crystalline lens, reducing resistance to the flow of aqueous humor from the posterior to the anterior chamber. Consequently, the iris became flatter and shifted posteriorly (resulting in a decrease in IC). Additionally, our findings confirmed that the RLP increased but without any significant change in the length of the ocular axis after pupil dilation. This observation further underscores the assertion that the backward movement of the lens-iris diaphragm following pupil dilation leads to a posterior shift in the anterior segment of the eye, ultimately increasing the central depth of the anterior chamber. However, simultaneous pupil dilations resulted in pupillary sphincter paralysis, iris thickening, and accumulation of iris tissue toward the periphery. This effect can bring the iris closer to the trabecular meshwork and potentially reduce the depth of the peripheral anterior chamber. The net impact of tropicamide eye drops on anterior chamber angle changes depends on the combined effects of these opposing mechanisms [16–18].

Huang [19] reported that patients with age-related cataracts may have reduced crystalline capsule elasticity due to the gradual hardening of the lens cortex, and ciliary muscle paralysis and reductions in the lens curvature and thickness are less likely. Moreover, only a limited amount of accommodation, or in some cases, no accommodation at all, may be possible. However, our present study discovered a significant reduction in LV after pupil dilation. This suggests that patients with age-related cataracts may still retain some residual accommodative capability [20]. We intend to conduct further investigations to qualitatively and quantitatively analyze this remaining accommodative capacity in our forthcoming studies.

It has been demonstrated that the risks of pupil blockage and consequent intraocular pressure elevation are most pronounced when the pupil dilates slowly and moderately to a diameter range of 3.5–6 mm. For such cases, the outflow of aqueous humor from the anterior chamber can be hindered and IOP may increase if the pupillary block is not relieved and the peripheral iris is near the trabecular meshwork. Conversely, when the pupil dilates rapidly and adequately, the effect of the pupillary block is diminished [21, 22]. In our study, we used tropicamide eye drops for pupil dilation. These drops facilitated rapid pupil dilation, typically exceeding 6 mm in diameter, to effectively alleviate pupillary block. As a result, we observed a mild increase in intraocular pressure after pupil dilation, primarily in Group A. This slight pressure elevation can be attributed to the crowded anterior chamber, causing the iris to move close to the trabecular meshwork after pupil dilation. This proximity led to a reduction in anterior chamber aqueous flow, resulting in a slight increase in IOP. However, there were no significant changes in IOP before and after mydriasis in the other groups because of the wider anterior chamber. Although the iris accumulated after mydriasis, it still did not touch the trabecular meshwork, which had no affect on the outflow of aqueous in anterior chamber. No cases of acute glaucoma due to rapid pupil dilation were observed during our study.

While there were no statistically significant differences in the pre-dilated pupil diameter across the various anterior chamber angle groups, the data suggested a tendency for the pupil diameter to increase with the widening of the anterior chamber angle. This may be attributed to the relatively lower age of Group D than those of the other groups. This is consistent with the findings of Guillon [23, 24], who observed a gradual reduction in pupil diameter with age.

The overall trend of ocular axis length and anterior chamber width was consistent with the width of the anterior chamber angle, with ocular axis length and anterior chamber width increasing as the anterior chamber angle became wider. We found that the iris flattened (IC decreased), the iris moved closer to the lens, the ILCD increased, and ILA decreased (ILA: Group D < Group C < Group B) with the widening of the anterior chamber angle. However, Group A had a smaller ILA than Group B. The iris was most protruded in Group A, and the lens-iris diaphragm was displaced anteriorly, leading to a relatively more crowded anterior chamber. Due to this, the ILA did not reach its maximum value.

Our results revealed that ILCD varied across the groups in the following order: Group D >Group C >Group B >Group A. Previous studies [25] have suggested that a higher ILCD results in greater resistance to the flow of aqueous humor from the posterior chamber through the anterior chamber, which leads to further distension of the iris. Therefore, the ILCD and IC tend to change in the same direction. In contrast, our findings suggest that the ILCD and IC maintain a dynamic equilibrium. In other words, when the iris becomes more distended, the contact area between the iris and crystalline lens decreases, reducing the resistance to aqueous humor flow from the posterior chamber to the anterior chamber. This tends to balance the pressure between the anterior and posterior chambers, and the iris becomes less distended, while the contact area of the iris with the crystalline lens increases.

Furthermore, the changes in IS and anterior chamber angle width were generally consistent (IS: Group C > Group B > Group A). With the widening of the anterior chamber angle, the IS increased. However, Group D showed a lower IS than Group C. This might be attributed to the larger pupil diameter of Group D than those of the other groups. The combined changes in the anterior chamber width and pupil diameter contributed to the final IS.

In this study, we observed increments in the TIA and AOD500 values after pupil dilation for some cases when the pre-dilated TIA was ≤ 10° (Fig. 2: a, b); for other cases, the values decreased (Fig. 2: c, d), indicating that the anterior chamber angle could widen or narrow. We postulate that the narrowing of the anterior chamber angle may be attributed to factors such as iris hypertrophy or the presence of high iris folds, which accumulate in the anterior chamber angle after pupil dilation. However, further investigations are required, and increasing the sample size to refine subgroups is necessary for a more comprehensive understanding. Therefore, we recommend considering prophylactic peripheral laser iridectomy before pupil dilation in patients belonging to this group to prevent the risk of acute closure of the anterior chamber angle. For patients with cataracts and 10° < TIA ≤ 20°, we observed an increase in both TIA and AOD500 after pupil dilation in our study. However, previous studies suggested the risk of narrowing of the anterior chamber angle in this population [26, 27]. Hence, we advocate close monitoring of the morphological changes of the anterior chamber in patients within this category when pupil dilation is being considered. This cautious approach allows for the timely management of any adverse symptoms that may arise. Conversely, when TIA was >20° before dilation, we observed significant increases in both TIA and AOD500 after dilation, signifying a more open anterior chamber angle. This group of patients can safely use dilating drugs before fundus examination or cataract surgery.

Given that the lens has a double-sided convex shape, its inherent curvature dictates a significant increase in ILA after pupil dilation. Simultaneously, the iris span decreases following pupil dilation, resulting in a reduced ILCD. This reduction in contact area leads to decreased resistance for the flow of aqueous humor from the posterior chamber to the anterior chamber. Consequently, the iris tends to become flatter, indicating a decrease in IC following pupil dilation. These findings are consistent with the reports by Aptel [28].

Our findings indicate that the width of the anterior chamber angle significantly influences the change in LV after pupil dilation. The reduction in LV after pupil dilation can be attributed to two main factors. First, it is associated with the thinning of the lens thickness. Secondly, it is linked to the posterior displacement of the lens-iris diaphragm. There was a tendency for the △LT to decrease with the widening of the anterior chamber angle, except Group A. While the differences between the △RLP values of the groups did not reach statistical significance, there was a tendency for △RLP to increase as the anterior chamber angle widened. This suggests that the posterior displacement of the lens-iris diaphragm had a more pronounced effect [17, 29]. The △LV is dependent on the combined effects of △RLP and △LT [18].

Furthermore, we observed increases in △AOD500 and △TIA after pupil dilation as the anterior chamber angle widened. These were linked to the increase in △RLP. Before pupil dilation, the ILCD was ordered as follows: Group D > Group C > Group B > Group A. After pupil dilation, ILCD decreased, but there was no significant difference in ILCD across the groups. However, the difference between ILCD after and before pupil dilation, referred to as △ILCD, satisfied the assertion that a wider anterior chamber angle was associated with a greater change in ILCD. Similarly, in narrower anterior chamber angles, there was a more pronounced IC before dilation, which tended to flatten after dilation. Consequently, the △IC decreased with the widening of the anterior chamber angle.

This study is pioneering, given the detailed subgroup analysis of the anterior chamber angles of patients with intumescent cataracts who require phacoemulsification combined with intraocular lens implantation. The study examined the differences in anterior chamber angles and crystalline lens positions before and after mydriasis in these subgroups. The findings of the study suggest that patients with cataracts and 0° < TIA ≤ 10° have a high risk of angle closure induced by mydriasis, and they should be closely monitored in clinical practice. Preventive peripheral iridectomy is recommended before mydriasis. Cataract patients with 10°< TIA ≤ 20° have a low risk of induced angle closure, and routine mydriasis is acceptable, but close observation for changes in anterior chamber morphology is necessary. For those with TIA>20°, there is almost no risk of angle closure, and they can safely undergo mydriasis. This study provides new insights into the prediction of the risk of mydriasis in patients with intumescent cataracts, which can be used to guide clinical decisions. The actual ocular situation of patients may be more complex, and our study has some limitations. We will further explore other sensitive parameters of the anterior chamber angle and use linear regression to develop a risk factor model for angle closure in our subsequent studies, and we expect to discover more clinical applications.

Conclusion

When the TIA falls within the range of 0–10°, there is a substantial risk of angle closure, and peripheral iridectomy is strongly recommended before pupil dilation to prevent a dangerous increase in intraocular pressure. For individuals with TIA values of 10–20°, the risk is considerably lower, permitting routine pupil dilation. However, it is advisable to closely monitor changes in the anterior chamber morphology. When the TIA exceeds 20°, the risk of angle closure induction is nearly negligible, and pupil dilation is safe.

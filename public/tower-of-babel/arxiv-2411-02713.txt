On maximally symmetric subalgebras Thanks: The author was supported by the NSF grant DMS-2346684.
Alexander Kleshchev

Abstract
. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be a characteristic zero PID, S S be a 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebra and T ⊆ S T\subseteq S be a full rank subalgebra. Suppose the algebra T T is symmetric. It is important to know when T T is a maximal symmetric subalgebra of S S , i.e. no 𝕜 \Bbbk -subalgebra C C satisfying T ⊊ C ⊆ S T\subsetneq C\subseteq S is symmetric. In this note we establish a useful sufficient condition for this using a notion of a quasi-unit of an algebra. This condition is used to obtain an old and a new results on maximal symmetricity for generalized Schur algebras corresponding to certain Brauer tree algebras. The old result was used in our work with Evseev on RoCK blocks of symmetric groups. The new result will be used in our forthcoming work on RoCK blocks of double covers of symmetric groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative (unital) ring.
All
\Bbbk
-algebras in this note are assumed to be unital and free of finite rank as
\Bbbk
-modules.
A
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
is called
symmetric
if it possesses a
symmetrizing form
, i.e. a linear form
{\sf t}
such that the bilinear form

(a,b)_{\sf t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\sf t}(ab)\qquad(a,b\in A)

is a symmetric perfect pairing, which means
(a,b)_{\sf t}=(b,a)_{\sf t}
for all
a,b
and
A\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(A,\Bbbk),\ a\mapsto(a,\cdot)_{\sf t}
is an isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-modules. If a field
\mathbb{F}
is a
\Bbbk
-module we extend scalars to get the
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}A
.
If the
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
is symmetric then the
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
A_{\mathbb{F}}
is also symmetric.

From now on, unless otherwise stated, let
\Bbbk
be a PID with fraction filed
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.
Let
S
be a
\Bbbk
-algebra and suppose we have a full rank (unital)
\Bbbk
-subalgebra
T\subseteq S
. The full rank assumption means that we can identify
T_{\mathbb{K}}=S_{\mathbb{K}}
, so
S
and
T
are two
\Bbbk
-forms of the same finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. Suppose the
\Bbbk
-algebra
T
is symmetric. Then the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
T_{\mathbb{K}}
is also symmetric. However, the
\Bbbk
-algebra
S
might not be symmetric.
In fact, it is important to know when
T
is a
maximal symmetric subalgebra
of
S
, i.e. no
\Bbbk
-subalgebra
C
satisfying
T\subsetneq C\subseteq S
is symmetric.

Non-trivial examples of such maximally symmetric subalgebras
T\subseteq S
are given by
D^{A}(n,d)\subseteq{}^{\prime}D^{A}(n,d)
, where
D^{A}(n,d)
is a Turner’s double algebra
[
7
]
and
{}^{\prime}D^{A}(n,d)
is its divided power version, see
[
1
, Theorem 6.6]
. This maximal symmetricity was crucially used in
[
2
]
to prove Turner’s conjecture that RoCK blocks of symmetric groups of
p
-weight
d
are Morita equivalent to
D^{{\textsf{A}}_{p-1}}(d,d)
for the Brauer tree algebra
{\textsf{A}}_{p-1}
, cf. Example
3.6
.

It might be difficult to prove that
T\subseteq S
is a maximally symmetric subalgebra. In this note we develop the techniques of
[
1
, §6.2]
to get a sufficient condition which allows us to get new important examples of such subalgebras. The new examples will be used in
[
4
]

to prove that RoCK blocks of double covers of symmetric groups of
\bar{p}
-weight

are Morita equivalent to generalized Schur algebras corresponding to the Brauer tree algebras

3.7
.

Our sufficient condition for maximal symmetricity is based on the notion of a quasi-unit.
Let
A
be a unital ring. An element
\xi\in A
will be called a
quasi-unit
if
\xi\in Az

for a central
z\in A
implies that

z
is a unit.

For the following theorem, assume that the
\Bbbk
-algebra
S
is non-negatively graded, i.e.
S=S^{0}\oplus S^{1}\oplus\dots\oplus S^{N}
and
S^{i}S^{j}\subseteq S^{i+j}
; in particular,
S^{0}
is a subalgebra.
Let
T=T^{0}\oplus T^{1}\oplus\dots\oplus T^{N}
be a
graded subalgebra
of
S
; in particular,
T^{i}=T\cap S^{i}
for all
i
. We assume that
T
is a full rank graded subalgebra, i.e.
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}T^{i}=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}S^{i}
for all
i
.
We assume that
T
possesses a
degree
-N
symmetrizing form
, which means a symmetrizing form
{\sf t}
such that
{\sf t}|_{T^{i}}=0
unless
i=N
. For the intermediate subalgebra
C
we do not assume that it is a graded subalgebra, so a priori we might have that
\sum_{i}(C\cap S^{i})\neq C
. As for the symmetricity of
C
, we only assume that
C_{\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}}
are symmetric for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
\Bbbk
(symmetrizing forms on the algebras
C_{\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}}
a priori have nothing to do with the given symmetrizing form
{\sf t}
on
T
).

Main Theorem.

Let

\Bbbk
be a PID with fraction field of characteristic zero,

S=S^{0}\oplus S^{1}\oplus\dots\oplus S^{N}
be a graded
\Bbbk
-algebra and
T\subseteq S
be a full rank graded
\Bbbk
-subalgebra with
T^{0}=S^{0}
. Suppose
T
possesses a degree
-N
symmetrizing form. Let
C
be a
\Bbbk
-subalgebra of
S
containing
T
and such that for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\Bbbk
the
(\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m})
-algebra
C_{\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}}
is symmetric.
Suppose that and there is
\xi\in S^{0}
such that:

(a)

each
y\in S^{N}
can be written in the form
y=y_{1}+y_{2}
with
\xi y_{1}=0
and
y_{2}\in T^{N}
;

(b)

1_{\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}}\otimes\xi
is a quasi-unit in
S^{0}_{\Bbbk/\mathfrak{m}}
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\Bbbk
.

Then
C=T
.

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem

2.1.
Reduction to the case
\Bbbk
is a DVR

We first note that it suffices to prove the Main Theorem in the case where
\Bbbk
is a discrete valuation ring (DVR). Indeed, we may assume that
\Bbbk
is not a field, since in that case
T=S
and the theorem is trivial.
Now, for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\Bbbk
the localization
\Bbbk_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a DVR, and so by the DVR case of the Main Theorem,
\Bbbk_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}C=\Bbbk_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}T
.
Since this is true for all
\mathfrak{m}
we deduce that
C=T
.

So from now on we assume that
\Bbbk
is a DVR with fraction field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and the maximal ideal
(\pi)
; we denote
\mathbb{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bbbk/(\pi)
.

2.2.
Some general remarks and notation

Let
A
be a
\Bbbk
-algebra (as usual unital and free of finite rank as a
\Bbbk
-module). We identify

A_{\mathbb{F}}=A/\pi A
, with
1_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes a
corresponding to
a+\pi A
for all
a\in A
. Given an
A
-(bi)module
V
, we can extend scalars to get an
A_{\mathbb{K}}
-(bi)module

and
an
A_{\mathbb{F}}
-(bi)module

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I
be a two-sided ideal of
A
such that the
A/\pi A
-bimodules
A/\pi A
and
I/\pi I
are isomorphic. If there is
\xi\in I
such that
\xi+\pi A
is a quasi-unit in the ring
A/\pi A
then
I=A
.

Proof.

Let
{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A/\pi A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}I/\pi I

be an isomorphism of
A/\pi A
-bimodules. Let
z\in I
be such that
{\varphi}(1_{A}+\pi A)=z+\pi I
. Then
z+\pi A
is central in
A/\pi A
. Moreover,
z+\pi I
generates
I/\pi I
as a left
A/\pi A
-module, so

\xi+\pi I\in(A/\pi A)(z+\pi I)
, hence
\xi+\pi A\in(A/\pi A)(z+\pi A)
. By assumption,
\xi+\pi A
is a quasi-unit in
A/\pi A
, so

is a unit in
A/\pi A
. It follows that
I+\pi A=A
. Hence
I=A
by Nakayama’s Lemma.
∎

If
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{i}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote

A^{\geq j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{i\geq j}A^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{>j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{i>j}A^{i}.

Proof of the Main Theorem for the case
\Bbbk
is a DVR

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define

C^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C\cap S^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad C^{(>i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C\cap S^{>i}.

(Since a priori
C
is a not necessarily graded subalgebra of
S
, we cannot claim that
C=\bigoplus_{i}C^{(i)}
, so we avoid the notation
C^{i}
,
C^{>i}
, etc.)
Note that
C^{(i)}
and
C^{(>i)}
are pure
\Bbbk
-submodules of

C^{(i)}_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}C^{(i)}
and

C^{(>i)}_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}C^{(>i)}
with
\mathbb{F}
-supspaces of

.

For every

, we have

, and so

\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}C^{(i)}=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}T^{i}=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}S^{i}.

(2.2)

Taking into account that
S^{0}=T^{0}
, we also have

T^{0}=C^{(0)}=S^{0}.

(2.3)

This, in turn, implies

C=C^{(0)}\oplus C^{(>0)}.

(2.4)

Indeed, it is clear that the sum
C^{(0)}+C^{(>0)}
is direct. Moreover, given
c\in C
, we can write
c=s_{0}+s_{>0}
with
s_{0}\in S^{0}=C^{(0)}
and
s_{>0}\in S^{>0}
. It now follows that
s_{>0}\in C^{(>0)}
.

By assumption, there exists a degree

symmetrizing form

on
T
. The corresponding bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\sf t}
restricts to a perfect pairing between

and

for all

; in particular,
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\sf t}
restricts to a perfect pairing between

and

, so
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}T^{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}T^{N}
, and (
2.2
), (

) imply
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}C^{(0)}=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}C^{(N)}
, hence

\dim C^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}}=\dim C^{(N)}_{\mathbb{F}}.

(2.5)

It is clear that
C^{(N)}
is an ideal in
C
, so naturally a
C^{(0)}
-bimodule. Extending scalars,
C^{(N)}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a
C^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}}
-bimodule.

Claim 1:
The
C^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}}
-bimodule

is isomorphic to the dual bimodule
(C^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}})^{*}
.

Indeed,

is symmetric by assumption, so

. So the bilinear form on

(c,c^{\prime})_{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\sf t}^{C}(cc^{\prime})\qquad(c,c^{\prime}\in C_{\mathbb{F}})

is symmetric and non-degenerate. We consider the orthogonal complement
(C^{(N)}_{\mathbb{F}})^{\perp}
of

in

with respect to

. Note that

C^{(>0)}_{\mathbb{F}}C^{(N)}_{\mathbb{F}}=0
, so

C^{(>0)}_{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq(C^{(N)}_{\mathbb{F}})^{\perp}
.
Moreover, by (

), we have
C_{\mathbb{F}}=C^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}}\oplus C^{(>0)}_{\mathbb{F}}
.
By dimensions, taking into account (
2.5
), we now deduce that

and that
(\cdot,\cdot)_{C}
restricts to a perfect pairing between
C^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}}
and

, which easily implies the claim.

Extending scalars from
\Bbbk
to

, we can identify

and consider

, etc. as sublattices in

(2.6)

Then

\displaystyle T^{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y\in S_{\mathbb{K}}^{N}\mid(x,y)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}\in\Bbbk\ \text{for all $x\in S^{0}$}\}.

(2.7)

We set

\displaystyle\tilde{C}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in S_{\mathbb{K}}^{0}\mid(x,y)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}\in\Bbbk\ \text{for all $y\in C^{(N)}$}\},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in S_{\mathbb{K}}^{0}\mid(x,y)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}\in\Bbbk\ \text{for all $y\in S^{N}$}\}.

(2.9)

Then

\displaystyle C^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y\in S_{\mathbb{K}}^{N}\mid(x,y)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}\in\Bbbk\ \text{for all $x\in\tilde{C}^{(0)}$}\}.

(2.10)

Since
T^{N}\subseteq C^{(N)}\subseteq S^{N}
, we have a chain of
\Bbbk

\tilde{S}^{0}\subseteq\tilde{C}^{(0)}\subseteq S^{0}.

(2.11)

We claim that in fact
\tilde{C}^{(0)}
is an

-subbimodule of

(this is also true for

by a similar argument but we will not need this). Indeed, let

. By (

),

, and so for all

, we have that

, whence

(xs,y)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}=(x,sy)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}\in\Bbbk

(sx,y)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}=(y,sx)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}=(ys,x)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}=(x,ys)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}\in\Bbbk,

.

Since
S^{0}=C^{(0)}
by (

), we have that
C^{(N)}
is an
S^{0}
-bimodule, and the pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\sf t},\mathbb{K}}

\tilde{C}^{(0)}\cong(C^{(N)})^{*}

of

-bimodules.
Hence,

\tilde{C}^{(0)}_{\mathbb{F}}\cong((C^{(N)})^{*})_{\mathbb{F}}\cong(C^{(N)}_{\mathbb{F}})^{*}

-bimodules.

Recall that by assumption we have an element
\xi\in S^{0}

such that

By (

. By the assumption (a) in the Main Theorem, if

with

and

yields
\tilde{C}^{(0)}=S^{0}
. By (

) and (

), we deduce that

.

Now suppose for a contradiction that

. Choose

that lies in

for all

. So we can write

. There exists

3.
Examples

3.1.
A construction of quasi-units

A key assumption in the Main Theorem is the existence of an interesting quasi-unit. In this subsection, we provide a necessary condition for an idempotent to be a quasi-unit.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a (unital) ring and let
1_{A}=e_{0}+e_{1}+\dots+e_{k}
be an orthogonal idempotent decomposition. Suppose that for every
i=1,\dots,k
we have:

(i)

Considering
e_{i}Ae_{0}
as an
(e_{i}Ae_{i},e_{0}Ae_{0})
-bimodule, the natural map
e_{i}Ae_{i}\to\operatorname{End}_{e_{0}Ae_{0}}(e_{i}Ae_{0})
is an isomorphism.

(ii)

There exists
a_{i}\in e_{i}Ae_{0}
such that
e_{i}Ae_{0}=a_{i}Ae_{0}
.

Then
e_{0}
is a quasi-unit in
A
.

Proof.

Suppose that
z\in A
is central and
e_{0}\in Az
. We need to prove that
z
is a unit. Note that
e_{i}ze_{j}=ze_{i}e_{j}=0
for
i\neq j
, so
z=z_{0}+z_{1}+\dots+z_{k}
where
z_{i}=ze_{i}=e_{i}z
.

Since
e_{0}\in Az
, there exists
y_{0}\in A
such that
e_{0}=y_{0}z
. Replacing
y_{0}
with
e_{0}y_{0}e_{0}
if necessary, we may assume that
y_{0}\in e_{0}Ae_{0}
. Now,
y_{0}
is the inverse of the central element
z_{0}
in the ring
e_{0}Ae_{0}
(with unit
e_{0}
). In particular,
y_{0}
is central in the ring
e_{0}Ae_{0}
. Hence for every
i=1,\dots,k
, there is a right
e_{0}Ae_{0}
-module endomorphism of
e_{i}Ae_{0}
given by the right multiplication with
y_{0}
. By (i), there is
y_{i}\in e_{i}Ae_{i}
such that this endomorphism is given by the left multiplication with
y_{i}
. In particular,

a_{i}y_{0}=y_{i}a_{i}
.
Therefore,

z_{i}y_{i}a_{i}=z_{i}a_{i}y_{0}=za_{i}y_{0}=a_{i}y_{0}z=a_{i}e_{0}=a_{i}.

Since the natural map in (i) is injective and
a_{i}
generates
e_{i}Ae_{0}
as a right
e_{0}Ae_{0}
-module by (ii), the equality
z_{i}y_{i}a_{i}=a_{i}
implies

z_{i}y_{i}=e_{i}
. Now,

z(y_{0}+y_{1}+\dots+y_{k})=y_{0}z+z_{1}y_{1}+\dots+z_{k}y_{k}=e_{0}+e_{1}+\dots+e_{k}=1_{A},

so
z
is a unit.
∎

.

3.2.
Some Turner’s algebras

In this subsection,
\Bbbk
is a PID with fraction filed
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. Let
A=A^{0}\oplus A^{1}\oplus A^{2}
be a graded
\Bbbk

-2

{\sf t}

(a,b)_{\sf t}={\sf t}(ab)

{\sf t}

-2

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\sf t}
restricts to a perfect pairing between
A^{0}
and
A^{2}
.
We consider
A
as a superalgebra with
A_{\bar{0}}=A^{0}\oplus A^{2}
and
A_{\bar{1}}=A^{1}
.
Here are two key examples:

Example 3.6
.

For all

\Bbbk
-algebras
{\textsf{A}}_{\ell}

\ell=1

{\textsf{A}}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}[{\textsf{c}}_{1}]/({\textsf{c}}_{1}^{2})

is an indeterminate in degree

be the

1

{\textsf{a}}_{\ell-2,\ell-1}

{\textsf{A}}_{\ell}

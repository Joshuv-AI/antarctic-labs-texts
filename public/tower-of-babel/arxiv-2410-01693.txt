On the exactness of the Kiguradze–Kvinikadze blow-up condition for nonlinear ordinary differential equations
A. A. Kon’kov

Abstract
We show the exactness of the Kiguradze–Kvinikadze blow-up condition for solutions of the Cauchy problem w^(m) = f (t, w, …, w^(m - 1)), w^(i) (0) = a_i ≥0, i = 0, …, m - 1.



keywords
Ordinary differential equations, Global solutions, Nonlinearity, Blow-up

1
Introduction

We consider the differential equation

w^{(m)}=f(t,w,\ldots,w^{(m-1)}),

(1)

of order
m\geq 1
, where
f
is a non-negative locally Caratheodory function
[
1
,
2
]
.
As is customary, a function
w:[0,T)\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
0<T\leq\infty
, is called a solution of (
1
) on the interval
[0,T)
if its derivatives
w^{(i)}
,
i=0,\ldots,m-1
, are locally absolutely continuous functions on
[0,T)
and, moreover, equation (
1
) holds for almost all
t\in[0,T)
.
By a global solution of (
1
) we mean a solution of this equation on the whole interval
[0,\infty)
.
We also assume that solutions of (
1
) satisfy the initial conditions

w^{(i)}(0)=a_{i}\geq 0,\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1.

(2)

In paper
(
1
, Theorem 1.2)
, under the assumption that

f(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1})\geq q(t)h(x_{k})

(3)

for almost all
t\in[0,\infty)
and for all
x_{i}\in[0,\infty)
,
i=0,\ldots,m-1
, where
0\leq k\leq m-1
is an integer,
{q:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)}
is a locally integrable function, and
{h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)}
is a non-decreasing continuous function positive on the interval
(0,\infty)
,
it was proved that the condition

\int_{1}^{\infty}h^{-1/(m-k)}(s)s^{1/(m-k)-1}ds<\infty

(4)

implies the absence of global solutions of problem (
1
), (
2
) for all sufficiently large initial values.
In so doing,
it was required that

\operatorname{mes}\{\tau>t:q(\tau)>0\}>0

(5)

for all
t\geq 0
.
Replacing (
5
) by a stronger condition, one can show that (
4
) guarantees the absence of solutions of problem (
1
), (
2
) for all positive initial values too
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

For power-law nonlinearities
h(s)=s^{\lambda}
, condition (
4
) takes the simple form
\lambda>1
.
The case of such nonlinearities are
studied in sufficient detail
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
In particular, it is well-known that if the reverse inequality holds in (
3
) with
h(s)=s^{\lambda}
, where
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
and
q
is a non-negative locally integrable function, then problem (
1
), (
2
) has a global solution for all non-negative initial values.
It seems plausible that a similar statement should be true for the general nonlinearity.
Theorem
1
and
2
proved in our paper testifies to the validity of this hypothesis.

2
Main results

Theorem 1
.

Let the right-hand side of equation (
1
) satisfy the inequality

f(t,x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1})\leq q(t)h(x_{k})

(6)

for almost all
t\in[0,\infty)
and for all
x_{i}\in[0,\infty)
,
i=0,\ldots,m-1
,
where
0\leq k\leq m-1
is an integer,
{q:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)}
is a locally integrable function, and
{h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)}
is a non-decreasing continuous function
such that
h(s)>0
for all
s\in(0,\infty)
.
If

\int_{1}^{\infty}h^{-1/(m-k)}(s)s^{1/(m-k)-1}ds=\infty,

(7)

then any solution of problem (
1
), (
2
) can be extended to the whole interval
[0,\infty)
.

Remark 1
.

From (
7
), it obviously follows that

\int_{1}^{\infty}h^{-1/(m-k)}(\alpha\zeta)\zeta^{1/(m-k)-1}d\zeta=\infty

for any real number
\alpha>0
. To verify this, it is sufficient to make the change of variables
s=\alpha\zeta
under the integral sign in the left-hand side of (
7
).

Theorem 2
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
1
be fulfilled. Then problem (
1
), (
2
) has a global solution for all initial values.

To prove Theorems
1
and
2
, we need the following statements.

Lemma 1
.

Let
u
be a positive solution of the equation

\frac{d}{dt}u^{1/n}(t)=g^{1/n}(u)

(8)

on the interval
[0,T)
,
where
n\geq 1
is an integer and
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a non-decreasing continuous function.
Then there exist real numbers
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
depending only on
n
such that

u(t)-u(0)\geq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau

(9)

for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

Integrating (
8
), we have

u(t)=\left(u^{1/n}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}g^{1/n}(u)d\tau\right)^{n}

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
, whence it follows that

u(t)-u(t_{1})\geq\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t}g^{1/n}(u)d\tau\right)^{n}\geq(t-t_{1})^{n}g(u(t_{1}))

(10)

for all real numbers
0\leq t_{1}\leq t
.
Put

\alpha=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta=\frac{1}{1+2^{n}}.

Let
0<t<T
be some given real number and
l\geq 1
be the minimal integer such that
u(0)\geq\beta^{l}u(t)
.
We prove (
9
) by induction on
l
.
At fist, assume that
l=1
. It is obvious that

\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{n}g(\beta u(t)).

In so doing, (
10
) implies the estimate

u(t)-u(t/2)\geq\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{n}g(u(t/2))\geq\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{n}g(\beta u(t)).

Hence,

u(t)-u(t/2)\geq\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau.

(11)

On the other hand, from the monotonicity of the function
g
, it follows that

\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{n}g(\beta u(t/2))\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{0}^{t/2}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau;

therefore,

\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau\geq\frac{1}{1+2^{n}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau.

Combining this with (
11
), we obtain

u(t)-u(t/2)\geq\frac{1}{1+2^{n}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau,

(12)

whence (
9
) follows at once.

Now, assume that (
9
) is valid for some
l=l_{0}
, where
l_{0}
is a positive integer. Let us show that this inequality is also valid for
l=l_{0}+1
. Take a real number
0<t_{1}<t
satisfying the condition
u(t_{1})=\beta u(t)
.
If
2t_{1}\leq t
, then repeating the previous reasoning, we again arrive at (
12
) and, consequently, at (
9
). Consider the case of
2t_{1}>t
. By the induction hypothesis, we have

u(t_{1})-u(0)\geq\alpha\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau.

(13)

Let us put
t_{*}=2t_{1}-t
.
Since
2(t_{1}-\tau)\geq t-\tau
for all
\tau\in(0,t_{*})
, one can assert that

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau\geq\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau;

therefore, (
13
) implies the estimate

u(t_{1})\geq\frac{\alpha}{2^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau.

In view of the equality
u(t)-u(t_{1})=(1/\beta-1)u(t_{1})
, this yields

\displaystyle u(t)-u(t_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2^{n-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-1\right)\int_{0}^{t_{*}}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau

\displaystyle=2\alpha\int_{0}^{t_{*}}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau.

(14)

From (
10
), it follows that

u(t)-u(t_{1})\geq(t-t_{1})^{n}g(u(t_{1}))=(t-t_{1})^{n}g(\beta u(t)).

At the same time,

\int_{t_{*}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau\leq(t-t_{*})^{n}g(\beta u(t))=2^{n}(t-t_{1})^{n}g(\beta u(t)).

Thus, one can assert that

u(t)-u(t_{1})\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{t_{*}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau=2\alpha\int_{t_{*}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau.

Summing the last expression with (
14
), we arrive at the estimate

u(t)-u(t_{1})\geq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}g(\beta u)d\tau,

whence (
9
) obviously follows.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing continuous function such that

\int_{1}^{\infty}h^{-1/n}(s)s^{1/n-1}ds=\infty,

(15)

where
n\geq 1
is an integer.
Then the Cauchy problem

v^{(n)}=h(v),\qquad v^{(i)}(0)=b_{i}>0,\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1,

(16)

has a global solution for all initial values.

Proof.

We put

v_{0}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{i}t^{i}}{i!}

and

v_{j}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{i}t^{i}}{i!}+\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}h(v_{j-1})d\tau,\quad j=1,2,\ldots.

(17)

It is easy to see that

0\leq v_{j-1}(t)\leq v_{j}(t)

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
,
j=1,2,\ldots
.
Further, let
T>0
be some real number and let

g(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha(n-1)!}h\left(\frac{s}{\beta}\right),\quad s\in(0,\infty),

(18)

where
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
are the constants in Lemma
1
.
We show that equation (
8
) has a global solution satisfying the initial condition

u(0)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{i}T^{i}}{i!}.

(19)

Indeed, let us denote
w=u^{1/n}
. Then (
8
) takes the form

\frac{d}{dt}w(t)=g^{1/n}(w^{n}).

Integrating this equation, we obtain

\int_{w(0)}^{w(t)}g^{-1/n}(\zeta^{n})d\zeta=t.

After the changing of variables
s=\zeta^{n}
, this implies that

\frac{1}{n}\int_{u(0)}^{u(t)}g^{-1/n}(s)s^{1/n-1}ds=t

for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.
In view of condition (
15
) and Remark
1
, the integral in the right-hand side of the last equality tends to infinity as
u(t)\to\infty
, which obviously guarantees that a global solution of (
8
), (
19
) exists.
By Lemma
1
, this solution satisfies inequality (
9
), whence in accordance with (
18
) and (
19
) we have

u(t)\geq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{i}t^{i}}{i!}+\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}h(u)d\tau

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Since
h
is a positive non-decreasing function, one can verify by induction that

v_{j}(t)\leq u(t)

for all
t\in[0,T]
,
j=0,1,2,\ldots
. Thus, there is a function
v\in L_{1}([0,T])
such that
v_{j}(t)\to v(t)
as
j\to\infty
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Passing to the limit as
j\to\infty
in (
17
),
by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,
we obtain

v(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{i}t^{i}}{i!}+\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{n-1}h(v)d\tau

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Since
T>0
can be an arbitrary real number, the last equality is valid for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.
In so doing, the limit function
v
can obviously be defined on the whole interval
[0,\infty)
. Finally, to complete the proof, it remains to verify by direct differentiation that
v
is a solution of (
16
).
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
1
be fulfilled.
Then any solution of the Cauchy problem

v^{(m)}=q(t)h(v^{(k)}),\qquad v^{(i)}(0)=a_{i}\geq 0,\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1,

(20)

can be extended to the whole interval
[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
v
be a solution of (
20
) and, moreover,
[0,T)
be the maximal interval to which this solution can be extended.
Assume by contradiction that
T<\infty
.
We denote
u=v^{(k)}
and
n=m-k
. It is easy to see that
u
is a solution of the Cauchy problem

u^{(n)}=q(t)h(u),\qquad u^{(i)}(0)=a_{k+i},\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1,

(21)

on the interval
[0,T)
. In so doing, the relation

\lim_{t\to T-0}u(t)=\infty

(22)

must be satisfied; otherwise, equation (
20
) implies that

\lim_{t\to T-0}v^{(i)}(t)<\infty,\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1.

Therefore,
v
can be extended to the interval
[T,T+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
by a solution of the Cauchy problem

v^{(n)}=q(t)h(v^{(k)}),\qquad v^{(i)}(T)=\lim_{t\to T-0}v^{(i)}(t),\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1.

This contradicts the fact that
[0,T)
is the maximal interval to which the solution
v
can be extended.

Take a real number
t_{0}\in[0,T)
satisfying the condition
u(t_{0})>0
.
Let us consider an increasing sequence of real numbers
t_{j}\in(t_{0},T)
such that
u(t_{j})=2^{j}u(t_{0})
,
j=1,2,\ldots
.
We also put
\tau_{0}=0
and

\tau_{j}=\tau_{j-1}+t_{j}-t_{j-1}+\varepsilon_{j},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,

(23)

where

\varepsilon_{j}=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}q(t)dt\geq 0.

Since
q
is a locally integrable function, it is obvious that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\tau_{j}<\infty.

(24)

Further, let us take some real numbers

b_{i}>a_{k+i},\;i=0,\ldots,n-1,

and let
w
be a global solution of the Cauchy problem

w^{(n)}=h(2w),\qquad w^{(i)}(0)=b_{i},\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

(25)

According to Lemma
2
and Remark
1
, such a solution exists.
We prove that

w^{(i)}(\tau_{j})\geq u^{(i)}(t_{j}),\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1,

(26)

for all
j=0,1,\ldots
.
If
j=0
, then (
26
) follows from the choice of the real numbers
b_{i}
. Now, assume that (
26
) is valid for
j=j_{0}
, where
j_{0}\geq 0
is some integer. Let us show that this relation is also valid for
j=j_{0}+1
. Taking into account equation (
21
), we obtain

\displaystyle u^{(i)}(t_{j+1})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-i-1}\frac{u^{(i+l)}(t_{j})(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{1}{(n-i-1)!}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(t_{j+1}-\tau)^{n-i-1}q(\tau)h(u)d\tau,\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(t_{j+1}-\tau)^{n-i-1}q(\tau)h(u)d\tau

\displaystyle{}\leq(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{n-i-1}h(u(t_{j+1}))\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}q(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle{}=(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{n-i-1}h(u(t_{j+1}))\varepsilon_{j+1},

this implies that

\displaystyle u^{(i)}(t_{j+1})\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-i-1}\frac{u^{(i+l)}(t_{j})(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{n-i-1}h(u(t_{j+1}))\varepsilon_{j+1}}{(n-i-1)!},\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

(27)

In its turn, equation (
25
) yields

\displaystyle w^{(i)}(\tau_{j+1})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-i-1}\frac{w^{(i+l)}(\tau_{j})(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{1}{(n-i-1)!}\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}(\tau_{j+1}-\tau)^{n-i-1}h(2w)d\tau,\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1,

whence in accordance with the estimate

\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}(\tau_{j+1}-\tau)^{n-i-1}h(2w)d\tau\geq\frac{(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{n-i}h(2w(\tau_{j}))}{n-i}

it follows that

\displaystyle w^{(i)}(\tau_{j+1})\geq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-i-1}\frac{w^{(i+l)}(\tau_{j})(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{n-i}h(2w(\tau_{j}))}{(n-i)!},\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

(28)

By the induction hypothesis and the definition of the real number
t_{j+1}
, we have

h(2w(\tau_{j}))\geq h(2u(t_{j}))=h(u(t_{j+1})).

Moreover, taking into account (
23
), one can assert that

\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j}\geq t_{j+1}-t_{j}

and

(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{n-i}\geq(n-i)(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{n-i-1}\varepsilon_{j+1},\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1;

therefore,

\sum_{l=0}^{n-i-1}\frac{w^{(i+l)}(\tau_{j})(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{l}}{l!}\geq\sum_{l=0}^{n-i-1}\frac{u^{(i+l)}(t_{j})(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{l}}{l!}

and

\frac{(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})^{n-i}h(2w(\tau_{j}))}{(n-i)!}\geq\frac{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{n-i-1}h(u(t_{j+1}))\varepsilon_{j+1}}{(n-i-1)!},\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

Hence, combining (
27
) and (
28
), we obtain

w^{(i)}(\tau_{j+1})\geq u^{(i)}(t_{j+1}),\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

From (
24
), it immediately follows that

\lim_{j\to\infty}w(\tau_{j})<\infty;

therefore, (
26
) implies the inequality

\lim_{j\to\infty}u(t_{j})<\infty.

This contradicts (
22
). The proof is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
w
be a solution of problem (
1
), (
2
) and let
[0,T)
be the maximal interval to which
w
can be extended.
Assume by contradiction that
T<\infty
.
We denote by
{\mathbb{V}}
the set of solutions of problem (
20
) such that
\operatorname{dom}v\subset[0,T)
and, moreover,

w^{(i)}(t)\leq v^{(i)}(t),\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1,

(29)

for all
t\in\operatorname{dom}v
.
As is customary, by
\operatorname{dom}v
we mean the domain of a solution
v
.
Thus, for any
v\in{\mathbb{V}}
we have
\operatorname{dom}v=[0,t_{v})
, where
0<t_{v}\leq T
is some real number.

It can be seen that
{\mathbb{V}}\neq\emptyset
.
Indeed, integrating (
1
), (
2
), we obtain

w(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{a_{j}t^{j}}{j!}+\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-1}f(\tau,w,\ldots,w^{(m-1)})d\tau

for all
t\in[0,T)
.
Let us put
v_{0}=w
and

v_{l}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{a_{j}t^{j}}{j!}+\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-1}q(\tau)h(v_{l-1}^{(k)})d\tau,\quad l=1,2,\ldots.

(30)

By direct differentiation, it can be verified that

w^{(i)}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-i-1}\frac{a_{i+j}t^{j}}{j!}+\frac{1}{(m-i-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-i-1}f(\tau,w,\ldots,w^{(m-1)})d\tau

and

v_{l}^{(i)}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{a_{i+j}t^{j}}{j!}+\frac{1}{(m-i-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-i-1}q(\tau)h(v_{l-1}^{(k)})d\tau,\;l=1,2,\ldots,

(31)

for all
t\in[0,T)
,
i=0,\ldots,m-1
.

Taking into account (
6
) and the monotonicity of the function
h
, we have

w^{(i)}(t)\leq v_{l-1}^{(i)}(t)\leq v_{l}^{(i)}(t),\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1,\quad l=1,2,\ldots,

(32)

for all
t\in[0,T)
.
At the same time, since
q
is a locally integrable function, there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,T]
such that
\{v_{l}\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in the space
C^{m-1}(0,\varepsilon)
.
Therefore, the sequence
\{v_{l}^{(k)}\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
converges everywhere on
(0,\varepsilon)
to some function from
L_{\infty}(0,\varepsilon)
.
In view of (
31
), this implies that
\{v_{l}\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
is a convergent sequence in the space
C^{m-1}(0,\varepsilon)
. Let us denote by
v
the limit of this sequence.

In accordance with Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, passing in (
30
) to the limit as
l\to\infty
, we obtain

v(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{a_{j}t^{j}}{j!}+\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-1}q(\tau)h(v^{(k)})d\tau

for all
t\in[0,\varepsilon)
. Thus, the function
v
is a solution of problem (
20
) on the interval
[0,\varepsilon)
. In view of (
32
), this function satisfies inequalities (
29
) for all
t\in[0,\varepsilon)
.

Let us introduce a partial order relation on the set
{\mathbb{V}}
, assuming that
v_{1}\preceq v_{2}
if
\operatorname{dom}v_{1}\subset\operatorname{dom}v_{2}
and, in addition,
v_{2}
coincides with
v_{1}
on
\operatorname{dom}v_{1}
.
It is easy to see that any linearly ordered subset of
{\mathbb{V}}
has a maximal element. Hence, according to Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal element for the whole set
{\mathbb{V}}
. Let us denote this element by
v_{*}
and its domain by
[0,t_{*})
.

Assume by contradiction that
t_{*}<T
. Since in accordance with Lemma
3
any solution of problem (
20
) can be extended to the whole interval
[0,\infty)
, we have

\lim_{t\to t_{*}-0}v_{*}^{(i)}(t)<\infty,\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1.

(33)

Therefore, replacing relation (

Abelian and stochastic sandpile models on complete bipartite graphs
Thomas Selig, Haoyue Zhu

Abstract
. In the sandpile model, vertices of a graph are allocated grains of sand. At each unit of time, a grain is added to a randomly chosen vertex. If that causes its number of grains to exceed its degree, that vertex is called unstable, and topples . In the Abelian sandpile model (ASM), topplings are deterministic, whereas in the stochastic sandpile model (SSM) they are random. We study the ASM and SSM on complete bipartite graphs. For the SSM, we provide a stochastic version of Dhar’s burning algorithm to check if a given (stable) configuration is recurrent or not, with linear complexity. We also exhibit a bijection between sorted recurrent configurations and pairs of compatible Ferrers diagrams. We then provide a similar bijection for the ASM, and also interpret its recurrent configurations in terms of labelled Motzkin paths.



1.
Introduction

The Abelian sandpile model (ASM) is a dynamic process on a graph, where vertices are assigned a number of grains of sand. At each unit of time, a grain is added to a randomly chosen vertex. If this causes a vertex’s number of grains to exceed its degree, the vertex is called
unstable
, and
topples
, sending one grain to each of its neighbours. A special vertex, the
sink
, absorbs grains, and so the process eventually stabilises. The model was originally introduced by Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld
[
3
,
4
]
as an example of a model exhibiting a phenomenon known as
self-organised criticality
, before being formalised and generalised by Dhar
[
12
]
.

Of central interest in the ASM are the
recurrent configurations
– those which appear infinitely often in the long-time running of the model. A fruitful direction of ASM research has focused on combinatorial studies of these for graph families with high levels of symmetry, such as complete graphs
[
8
]
, complete bipartite
[
15
]
and multi-partite
[
7
]
graphs, complete split graphs
[
11
,
14
]
(see also
[
9
]
for generalisations), wheel and fan graphs
[
18
]
, Ferrers graphs
[
20
,
17
]
, permutation graphs
[
16
]
, and so on. Related combinatorial objects include parking functions and variations thereon, parallelogram polyominoes, lattice paths, subgraph structures, tableaux, and more.

In the ASM, the only randomness lies in the choice of vertex where grains are added at each time step. After this, the toppling and stabilisation processes are entirely deterministic. The main focus of this paper is instead a stochastic variant of the ASM, called
stochastic sandpile model
(SSM), as introduced in
[
5
]
, in which topplings are made according to (biased) random coin flips. The SSM was studied on complete graphs in
[
19
]
. We begin by setting some notation and formally defining the model.

As usual,
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of strictly positive integers. We let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=\mathbb{N}\,\cup\,\{0\}
denote the set of non-negative integers. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For a vector
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
\mathrm{inc}\left(a\right)=\left(\tilde{a}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{a}_{n}\right)
for the non-decreasing rearrangement of
a
. In this paper, we consider the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}^{0}
. This is the graph with vertex set
\{v^{t}_{0},v^{t}_{1},\cdots,v^{t}_{m}\}\sqcup\{v^{b}_{1},\cdots,v^{b}_{n}\}
and edge set
\{(v^{t}_{i},v^{b}_{j});\,i\in[m]\cup\{0\},j\in[n]\}
. We refer to vertices
v^{t}_{i}
, resp.
v^{b}_{j}
, as
top
, resp.
bottom
, vertices in
K_{m,n}^{0}
. We will use the notation
v^{*}_{i}
to refer to any arbitrary vertex of
K_{m,n}^{0}
. The vertex
v^{t}_{0}
, called the
sink
, will play a special role in both the ASM and the SSM. Figure
1
shows the graph
K_{2,2}^{0}
, where the sink is represented as a black square. Finally, we fix a probability
p\in(0,1)
.

A (sandpile)
configuration
on
K_{m,n}^{0}
is a vector
c=(c^{t}_{1},\cdots,c^{t}_{m};c^{b}_{1},\cdots,c^{b}_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m+n}
. For simplicity, we write
c=(c^{t};c^{b})
. We think of
c^{*}_{i}
as the number of grains at vertex
v^{*}_{i}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Config}_{m,n}
the set of all configurations on
K_{m,n}^{0}
. A top vertex
v^{t}_{i}
(for
i\in[m]
), resp. bottom vertex
v^{b}_{j}
(for
j\in[n]
), is
stable
if
c^{t}_{i}<n
, resp.
c^{b}_{j}<m+1
(i.e. the number of grains at the vertex is less than its degree). A configuration is stable if all of its vertices are stable. The set of stable configurations is denoted
\mathrm{Stable}_{m,n}
.

Unstable vertices
topple
. The toppling rules are different for the ASM and SSM, and we begin with the former. In the ASM, the toppling rule is deterministic: an unstable vertex (whether a top vertex
v^{t}_{i}
or a bottom vertex
v^{b}_{j}
) will send one grain to each of its neighbours (this includes the sink
v^{t}_{0}
for bottom vertices). This may cause other vertices who receive grains to become unstable, and there topple in turn. Grains that are sent to the sink exit the system (we think of the sink as “absorbing excess grains”).

This last property makes it relatively straightforward to see that, starting from an unstable configuration
c
and successively toppling unstable vertices, we eventually reach a stable configuration
c^{\prime}
.
Moreover, Dhar
[
12
]
showed that the stable configuration
c^{\prime}
reached does not depend on the order in which vertices are toppled. Note that in the ASM this process is entirely deterministic.
We write
c^{\prime}=\mathrm{DetStab}\left(c\right)
and call it the
deterministic stabilisation
of
c
. Figure

c=(2,1;0,2)
. Here,
blue

green

We now introduce the SSM. In this model, if a top vertex
v^{t}_{i}
is unstable, then for each bottom neighbour
v^{b}_{j}
we draw a Bernoulli random variable

with parameter

(the

B_{j}=1
, then
v^{b}_{j}
receives one grain from
v^{t}_{i}
when it topples, otherwise vertex

(as in the ASM we include the sink

For both ASM and SSM, we define a Markov chain on the set
\mathrm{Stable}_{m,n}
. At each step, we add a grain to a non-sink vertex of
K_{m,n}^{0}
, chosen uniformly at random, and stabilise the resulting configuration according to the chosen model’s rules. A configuration
c
is called
deterministically recurrent
(DR), resp.
stochastically recurrent
(SR) if it appears infinitely often in the long-time running of this Markov chain for the ASM, resp. SSM. We denote by
\mathrm{DetRec}_{m,n}
, resp.
\mathrm{StoRec}_{m,n}
, the set of DR, resp. SR, configurations on
K_{m,n}^{0}
.

A popular statistic for a recurrent configuration
c
is its
level
, defined by:

(1)

\mathrm{level}\left(c\right):=\sum\limits_{i\in[m]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[n]}c^{b}_{j}-m\!\cdot\!n,

which satisfies
0\leq\mathrm{level}\left(c\right)\leq m(n-1)
(see e.g.
[
19
, Equation (8)]
).

The symmetries of
K_{m,n}^{0}
make it natural to study both
\mathrm{DetRec}_{m,n}
and
\mathrm{StoRec}_{m,n}
up to re-ordering in each part. We therefore say that a configuration
c=(c^{t};c^{b})\in\mathrm{Config}_{m,n}
is
sorted
if
c^{t}
and
c^{b}
are both weakly increasing. We denote by
\mathrm{DetSortedRec}_{m,n}
, resp.
\mathrm{StoSortedRec}_{m,n}
, the set of sorted DR, resp. sorted SR, configurations for the ASM, resp. SSM.

Our paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a configuration on
K_{m,n}^{0}
to be SR in terms of a certain sequence of inequalities (Theorem
2.2
). This then yields a so-called
stochastic burning algorithm
for complete bipartite graphs that runs in linear time (Theorem
2.5
). In Section
3
we provide combinatorial characterisations of SR configurations in terms of pairs of
compatible Ferrers diagrams
(Theorem
3.2
). Section
4
examines equivalent of this new combinatorial description for the ASM, tying it to previous work by Dukes and Le Borgne
[
15
]
.

2.
Stochastically recurrent configurations on complete bipartite graphs

In this section, we give a characterisation of SR configurations on
K_{m,n}^{0}
. We use this to describe a
stochastic burning algorithm
, which checks in linear time if a given configuration is SR or not.

2.1.
Characterisation of
\mathrm{StoRec}_{m,n}

In
[
19
, Theorem 2.6]
, the first author gave a characterisation of SR configurations on general graphs. We begin by restating this in the complete bipartite graph case.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
c=(c^{t};c^{b})\in\mathrm{Stable}_{m,n}
be a stable configuration on
K_{m,n}^{0}
. Then
c\in\mathrm{StoRec}_{m,n}
if, and only if, for all subsets
A\subseteq[m],B\subseteq[n]
, we have:

(2)

\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}\geq|A|\cdot|B|.

If
A,B
do
not
satisfy Inequality (
2
), we say that
(A,B)
is a
forbidden subconfiguration
.

In fact, because of the structure of complete bipartite graph, it is not necessary to check Inequality (
2
) for all subsets
A,B
. Instead, intuitively, we only need to check it for a linear proportion of such subsets. This is made precise in the following, which is the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
c=(c^{t};c^{b})\in\mathrm{Stable}_{m,n}
be a stable configuration on
K_{m,n}^{0}
.
For
j\in[n]
, define
k_{j}:=|\{i\in[m];\,c^{t}_{i}<j\}|
. Then
c\in\mathrm{StoRec}_{m,n}
if, and only if,

(3)

\forall j\in[n],\,\tilde{c}^{b}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{c}^{b}_{j}\geq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j},

where
\mathrm{inc}\left(c^{b}\right):=\left(\tilde{c}^{b}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{c}^{b}_{n}\right)
is the non-decreasing re-arrangement of
c^{b}
. Moreover, if
c
is recurrent, we have
\mathrm{level}\left(c\right)=c^{b}_{1}+\cdots+c^{b}_{n}-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n})
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show the result when
c
is sorted, in which case, for
j\geq 0
, we have
c^{t}_{i}=j
if, and only if,
k_{j}<i\leq k_{j+1}
(with the convention
k_{0}=0
). Fix
j\in[n]
. We have:

(4)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k_{j}}c^{t}_{i}=0\!\cdot\!(k_{1}-k_{0})+1\!\cdot\!(k_{2}-k_{1})+\cdots+(j-1)\!\cdot\!(k_{j}-k_{j-1})=j\!\cdot\!k_{j}-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}).

In particular, if we take
A=[k_{j}]
and
B=[j]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in B}c^{b}_{j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k_{j}}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}c^{b}_{j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=j\!\cdot\!k_{j}-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j})+(c^{b}_{1}+\cdots+c^{b}_{j})

\displaystyle=|A|\cdot|B|+(c^{b}_{1}+\cdots+c^{b}_{j})-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}).

It follows that Inequality (
3
) is equivalent to Inequality (
2
) when we take
A=[k_{j}],B=[j]
. It therefore suffices to show that if there exists a forbidden subconfiguration
(A,B)
for
c
, then there exists
j
such that
([k_{j}],[j])
is a forbidden subconfiguration for
c
.

For this, take
B\subseteq[n]
to be a minimal subset such that there exists
A\subseteq[m]
with
(A,B)
forbidden, that is,
\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}<|A|\cdot|B|
. Let
q:=\max B
. We claim that for any
q^{\prime}<q
, we have
c^{b}_{q^{\prime}}<|A|
.
Otherwise, if
c^{b}_{q^{\prime}}\geq|A|
, let
B^{\prime}:=[q^{\prime}]\cap B\subsetneq B
. Since
c
is assumed to be sorted, by using
c^{b}_{j}\geq c^{b}_{q^{\prime}}\geq|A|
for
j\in(q^{\prime},q]
, we get
\sum\limits_{j\in B^{\prime}}c^{b}_{j}=\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}-\sum\limits_{j\in(q^{\prime},q]\cap B}c^{b}_{j}\leq\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}-|A|\cdot|B\setminus B^{\prime}|
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in B^{\prime}}c^{b}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}-|A|\cdot|B\setminus B^{\prime}|

\displaystyle<|A|\cdot|B|-|A|\cdot|B\setminus B^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|A|\cdot|B^{\prime}|,

which implies that
(A,B^{\prime})
is a forbidden subconfiguration. But since
B^{\prime}\subsetneq B
, this contradicts the minimality of
B
. This proves our claim that for any
q^{\prime}<q
, we have
c^{b}_{q^{\prime}}<|A|
, and in particular this holds for
q^{\prime}\in[q]\setminus B
.

We therefore get:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]}c^{b}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]\setminus B}c^{b}_{j}

\displaystyle<\sum\limits_{i\in A}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in B}c^{b}_{j}+|A|\cdot|[q]\setminus B|

\displaystyle<|A|\cdot|B|+|A|\cdot|[q]\setminus B|

\displaystyle=|A|\cdot|[q]|,

which implies that
(A,[q])
is also forbidden, i.e., we can choose
B
of the form
B=[q]
. By a similar argument (considering
A
as a minimal subset such that
(A,[q])
is forbidden), we can choose
A=[p]
.
From there, it is straightforward to check that
([k_{q}],[q])
is also forbidden, by distinguishing the cases
p>k_{q}
and
p<k_{q}
.

Case
p>k_{q}
:

By definition of
k_{q}
, for any
i\in(k_{q},p]
we have
c^{t}_{i}\geq q=|[q]|
(since
c^{t}
is assumed sorted). This implies that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in[k_{q}]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]}c^{b}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in[p]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]}c^{b}_{j}-\sum\limits_{i\in(k_{q},p]}c^{t}_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i\in[p]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]}c^{b}_{j}-(p-k_{q})\!\cdot\!q

\displaystyle<p\!\cdot\!q-(p-k_{q})\!\cdot\!q

\displaystyle=k_{q}\!\cdot\!q=|[k_{q}]|\cdot|[q]|,

as desired.

Case
p<k_{q}
:

Again, by definition of
k_{q}
, for any
i\in(p,k_{q}]
, we have
c^{t}_{i}<|[q]|
, which implies:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in[k_{q}]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]}c^{b}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in[p]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[q]}c^{b}_{j}+\sum\limits_{i\in(p,k_{q}]}c^{t}_{i}

\displaystyle<p\!\cdot\!q+(k_{q}-p)\!\cdot\!q

\displaystyle=|[k_{q}]|\cdot|[q]|,

as desired.

Finally, we have shown that if there exists a forbidden subconfiguration
(A,B)
for
c
, then there exists
j
such that
([k_{j}],j)
is forbidden for
c
. This completes the proof of the characterisation of SR configurations.

The level formula then follows from the definition of the statistic in Equation (
1
), and Equation (
4
) with
j=n
, noting that
k_{n}=m
, which yields:

\displaystyle\mathrm{level}\left(c\right)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{i\in[m]}c^{t}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j\in[n]}c^{b}_{j}-m\!\cdot\!n

\displaystyle=n\!\cdot\!m-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n})+\sum\limits_{j\in[n]}c^{b}_{j}-m\!\cdot\!n

\displaystyle=c^{b}_{1}+\cdots+c^{b}_{n}-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}),

as desired.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Consider the stable configuration
c=(c^{t};c^{b})=(3,1,3,2,3;2,0,4,3)\in\mathrm{Stable}_{5,4}

c^{b}
and get
c^{b}=\mathrm{inc}\left(c^{b}\right)=(0,2,3,4)
. Next, we calculate the vector
k
as in the statement of Theorem
2.2
, yielding

. We then check Condition (

) for
j=1,2,3,4
.

•

For
j=1
, we have
\tilde{c^{b}_{1}}=0\geq 0=k_{1}
.

•

For
j=2
, we have
\tilde{c^{b}_{1}}+\tilde{c^{b}_{2}}=0+2=2\geq 1=0+1=k_{1}+k_{2}
.

•

For

\tilde{c^{b}_{1}}+\tilde{c^{b}_{2}}+\tilde{c^{b}_{3}}=5\geq 3=k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}
.

•

For

\tilde{c^{b}_{1}}+\tilde{c^{b}_{2}}+\tilde{c^{b}_{3}}+\tilde{c^{b}_{4}}=9\geq 8=k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4}
.

Therefore the configuration
c
is SR by Theorem

, and we have
\mathrm{level}\left(c\right)=9-8=1
.

3.
Recurrent configurations as pairs of Ferrers diagrams

In this section, we present a combinatorial interpretation of
\mathrm{StoSortedRec}_{m,n}
in terms of
Ferrers diagrams
.
A Ferrers diagram is a left-aligned collection of cells such that the number of cells in each row is weakly increasing from bottom to top (some rows may be empty). We denote by
\mathrm{Ferrers}_{m,n}
, resp.
\mathrm{Ferrers}_{\leq m,n}
, the set of Ferrers diagrams with
m
columns, resp. at most
m
columns, and
n
rows. The
area

\mathrm{Area}(F)
of a Ferrers diagram
F

Theorem 3.2
.

For a

, we define

to the set of compatible pairs

Proof.

By preceding remarks, if
c\in\mathrm{StoRec}_{m,n}
, then
F(k)\in\mathrm{Ferrers}_{m,n}
and
F\left(c^{b}\right)\in\mathrm{Ferrers}_{\leq m,n}
. To show that
\Psi
is injective, we note that the vector
k
uniquely defines the non-decreasing re-arrangement

of the top part of

. Indeed, let
k^{\prime}_{j}:=|\{i\in[m];\,c^{t}_{i}=j\}|
for

. Then we have
k^{\prime}_{j}=k_{j+1}-k_{j}
(with the convention

), and the vector
\mathrm{inc}\left(c^{t}\right)
simply consists of the values

k^{\prime}_{j}
times for

, which gives
\mathrm{level}\left(c\right)=c^{b}_{1}+\cdots+c^{b}_{n}-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n})=\tilde{c}^{b}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{c}^{b}_{n}-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n})=\mathrm{Area}\big(F\left(c^{b}\right)\big)-\mathrm{Area}\left(F(k)\right)
. Since
\mathsf{Shift}
operations leave the area of a Ferrers diagram unchanged, and
\mathsf{Add}
operations increase it by one, this is also the number of
\mathsf{Add}
operations in a legal sequence
F(k)\leadsto F(c^{b})
.

It remains to show that
\Psi
is surjective, i.e. that
c
is SR if, and only if,
\Big(F(k),F\left(c^{b}\right)\Big)
is a compatible pair of Ferrers diagrams. Suppose first that
\Big(F(k),F\left(c^{b}\right)\Big)
is compatible. This means that
F\left(c^{b}\right)
can be reached from
F(k)
through a sequence of legal
\mathsf{Shift}
and
\mathsf{Add}
operations. As such, by Theorem
2.2
, it is sufficient to show the following. If
v=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})
is such that
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{j}\geq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}
for all
j\in[n]
, and

is such that
F(v^{\prime})
is obtained from
F(v)
through a single legal
\mathsf{Shift}
or
\mathsf{Add}
operation, then we have
v^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+v^{\prime}_{j}\geq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}
for all
j\in[n]
. In other words, Condition (
3
) is preserved through applying a single legal
\mathsf{Shift}
or
\mathsf{Add}
operation to the Ferrers diagram of the left-hand side vector. Adding a cell to row
p
in

simply increases

by one, leaving other values unchanged, so the inequalities are clearly preserved. Shifting a cell from row
p
to row

yields
v^{\prime}_{p}=v_{p}-1
,
v^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}}=v_{p^{\prime}}+1
, and other values unchanged. Thus the sum

is increased by one if

and unchanged otherwise, so that Condition (
3
) is preserved.

We now show the converse, namely that if

is SR, then the pair
\Big(F(k),F\left(c^{b}\right)\Big)
is compatible. Let
c
be a
sorted
SR configuration, and
k
the corresponding vector. First, note that if
c^{b}_{j}\geq k_{j}
for all

, then

can be obtained from

by adding the required cells in each row from top to bottom, which is a legal sequence, and the result follows. We therefore assume this is not the case, and define
p:=\min\{j\in[n];\,c^{b}_{j}<k_{j}\}
. Since

is SR, we have
c^{b}_{1}+\cdots c^{b}_{p}\geq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}
by Theorem
2.2
.
Therefore there must exist

such that

, and we set

to be the maximal such

. Now note that on the one hand, we have
k_{p}>c^{b}_{p}\geq c^{b}_{p-1}\geq k_{p-1}

, while on the other hand, we have
k_{p^{\prime}}<c^{b}_{p^{\prime}}\leq c^{b}_{p^{\prime}+1}\leq k_{p^{\prime}+1}
, i.e.

to row

But this shift yields a Ferrers diagram whose vector

Theorem 4.16
.

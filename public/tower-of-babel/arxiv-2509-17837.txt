Fast and Accurate Decoder for the XZZX code Using Simulated Annealing
Tatsuya Sakashita

Abstract
The XZZX code is a variant of the surface code designed to address biased noise in realistic quantum devices. For the XZZX code, we propose a decoder based on simulated annealing (SA). Our SA decoder can be readily and efficiently parallelized, by virtue of its simple MCMC-based algorithm. To prepare an initial configuration of SA, we propose to employ recovery chains obtained by a decoder which utilizes a kind of greedy matching graph algorithm. Although Z Z -biased noise is commonly assumed in real quantum devices, we focus on Y Y -biased noise, for which the minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM) algorithm fails to decode accurately. Our numerical simulation for the code capacity noise model, where only data qubits suffer errors, confirmed that our SA decoder is more accurate than the MWPM decoder. Furthermore, our SA decoder attained the accuracy equivalent to that of the optimal decoder formulated by integer programming, called CPLEX decoder. In our greedy matching decoder, we randomly determine order of matching pairs of incorrect syndromes that have the same distance. This randomness brings about a variety of initial configurations of SA, which leads to faster convergence of our SA decoder. By comparing decoding times of our SA decoder, the CPLEX decoder, and matrix product state (MPS) decoder, all of which can handle Y Y -biased noise appropriately, we confirmed that our SA decoder is fastest if parallelized. This result implies a potential for combining of our greedy matching and SA decoder for practical use in quantum computing.



I
Introduction

The ultimate goal of quantum computing is to realize fault-tolerant quantum computation (FTQC) with robust error correction mechanisms.
For superconducting devices, decoherence time (qubit lifetime) is typically on the order of hundreds of microseconds.
For this reason, the decoding process must be completed within this time frame.
Practical decoders require a simple algorithm to be readily implemented in hardware.

The surface code is one of the most prospective topological error correction codes
Dennis et al. 2002
;
Fowler et al. 2012a
.
It is experimentally feasible, because it requires measuring only nearest-neighbor data qubits
Zhao et al. 2022
;
Google Quantum AI 2023
;
iOlius et al. 2022
;
Google Quantum AI and Collaborators 2025
;
Krinner et al. 2022
.

In physical systems such as superconducting qubits, optics and ion traps, realistic noise is biased, that is,
Z
(phase-flip) errors occur more frequently than
X
(bit-flip) errors.
For such a biased noise, replacing the stabilizer generators of the surface code leads to alternative codes, such as the tailored surface code (which has only
Y
-type stabilizers)
Tuckett et al. 2019
and the XZZX code
Bonilla Ataides et al. 2021
.
The XZZX code exhibits higher thresholds for
X
-,
Y
- or
Z
-biased noises, compared with the ordinary surface code,
which has stabilizer generators consisting of either only
X
or
Z
operators,
and is known as the CSS (Calderbank–Shor–Steane) surface code
Calderbank and Shor 1996
;
Steane 1996
.
For the XZZX code, there appeared proposals on its rotated version
Hammar et al. 2022
;
Forlivesi et al. 2024
, its cluster state
Claes et al. 2023
and its physical realization in optical and superconducting devices
Darmawan et al. 2021
and neutral atom devices
Sahay et al. 2023
.

The minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM) decoder
Dennis et al. 2002
can be executed in polynomial time of the number of incorrect syndromes and, hence, is well used for the surface code.
However, it deals with
X
and
Z
errors independently, and is thus less accurate under noises that include
Y
errors.

On the other hand, MCMC (Markov chain Monte Carlo) decoder and matrix product state (MPS) decoder
Bravyi et al. 2014
, known as MLD (maximum likelihood decoder), possess high decoding accuracy by taking into account the probability of
Y
errors.
For the
Z
-biased and
Y
-biased noises, the MPS decoder has been researched
Tuckett et al. 2018
.
Unfortunately, the MPS decoder has a high computational complexity of order
\mathcal{O}(d^{2})
for the code distance
d
.
In contrast, the MCMC decoders have a possibility for fast decoding, since the MCMC algorithms use heuristic approach based on pseudo-random numbers.

One of the earliest studies
Wootton and Loss 2012
on MCMC decoders for the CSS surface code employed parallel tempering, while another study
Hutter et al. 2014
utilized the Metropolis method fixed at the Nishimori temperature.
For initial configuration of MCMC, they employ error configurations generated by acting stabilizers and logical operators randomly to actual error configuration
Wootton and Loss 2012
;
Hammar et al. 2022
and recovery chain obtained by the MWPM decoder
Hutter et al. 2014
.
Thus, the aim of these previous works is to establish an accurate threshold for the code.

To efficiently minimize energy in MCMC methods, simulated annealing (SA)
Kirkpatrick et al. 1983
is often used in statistical physics.
By formulating the decoding problem as a random-bond Ising model (RBIM), SA has been applied to decoders for the CSS surface code
Takeuchi et al. 2023
and the color code
Takada et al. 2024
.
Another formulation of SA decoder for the CSS surface code has hamiltonian with penalty terms
Fujii et al. 2014
, and is demonstrated in hardware-implemented annealing machine
Fujisaki et al. 2022
;
Fujisaki et al. 2023
;
Takeuchi et al. 2023
.

The XZZX code has been studied by leveraging the MWPM decoder, the MPS decoder
Bonilla Ataides et al. 2021
and MCMC decoder
Hammar et al. 2022
.
They investigated the decoding accuracy for
Z
-biased noise, which is typical in real quantum devices.
Assuming knowing the probabilities of
X,Y,Z
errors, they incorporate this prior information into weight coefficients of the hamiltonian so as to improve decoding accuracy.

The research
Hammar et al. 2022
assumes the code capacity noise model, in which only data qubits suffer errors independently and identically.
To explore configurations efficiently, they carried out the MCMC method at lower inverse temperature than the Nishimori temperature, keeping the weight coefficients invariant in the hamiltonian.
Their numerical experiment suggests that calculating a dominant term (composed of degeneracy and energy) of the partition function is sufficient to determine the correct logical operator class.
Thus, their algorithm may be useful to collect this term to utilize it as train data in neural network decoder.

In the present article, we propose a simulated annealing (SA) decoder for the XZZX code.
The aim of our research is not to determine an accurate threshold, but rather to establish a decoding algorithm that is moderately accurate and yet sufficiently fast for practical use on a quantum computer.
We assume the code capacity noise.
Specifically, our interest is the case that each qubit experiences
Y
-biased noise, which cannot be decoded appropriately by the MWPM decoder.
As initial configuration for SA, we propose to employ a recovery chain obtained from a decoder which utilizes a greedy matching algorithm.
We implement the whole algorithms in the C++ language from scratch, in order to attain decoding time as short as possible.

IV.2.1
The MWPM decoding algorithm

We briefly review the algorithm of the MWPM decoder for the XZZX code
Bonilla Ataides et al. 2021
.

The MWPM decoder is formulated as an algorithm on the decoding graph
(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})
, introduced in Sec.
III
.
For
u,v\in S_{\bullet}
, we define the weighted distance
w(u,v)
between the vertices
u=(u_{x},u_{y})
and
v=(v_{x},v_{y})
as the shorter of the following two path lengths:

•

The weighted Manhattan distance

\alpha_{z}\lvert u_{x}-v_{x}\rvert\,+\,\alpha_{x}\lvert u_{y}-v_{y}\rvert,

(23)

•

The length of ‘path via a virtual boundary vertex’
Fowler et al. 2012b
.
For
u,v\in S_{\mathrm{gray}}
, this path consists of only horizontal edges, which connect
u
and the left or right boundary and connect
v
and the left or right boundary.
The length of the path equals to

\alpha_{z}\left(\min\{\lvert u_{x}\rvert,d-\lvert u_{x}\rvert\}+\min\{\lvert v_{x}\rvert,d-\lvert v_{x}\rvert\}\right).

(24)

For
u,v\in S_{\mathrm{white}}
, this path consists of only vertical edges, which connect
u
and the top or bottom boundary and connect
v
and the top or bottom boundary.
The length of the path equals to

\alpha_{x}\left(\min\{\lvert u_{y}\rvert,d-\lvert u_{y}\rvert\}+\min\{\lvert v_{y}\rvert,d-\lvert v_{y}\rvert\}\right).

(25)

IV.2.2

Let us interpret the MWPM decoder as energy minimization problem, then compare it with our SA decoder.

Since the perfect matching is equivalent to the syndrome consistency

) is the number of errors which reside on horizontal (vertical) edges in the error chain

, respectively.
In Eq. (

can be calculated by Eq. (

assuming we know.

In Sec.

Then, we execute each decoder to obtain the recovery configuration

If the product

Except in Sec.

•

The ‘Greedy matching (different)’ method may produce a different initial error configuration for each SA.

VI
Discussion

As in Sec.
V.3
, under much more strongly
Y
-biased noise, our SA decoder requires a large
N_{\mathrm{SA}}
in order to attain the optimal logical error rate.
A promising remedy to this problem is to modify the definition of stabilizer generators to include
Y
operators, analogously to the tailored code for the CSS surface code
Tuckett et al. 2019
.
Specifically, for the case
p_{y}\geq p_{z}\geq p_{x}
, we should employ ‘XYYX code’.
This modified code can detect the endpoints of error chains which consist of
Z
(
Y
) errors on horizontal (vertical) edges, respectively.
Thus, in our greedy matching algorithm, we can take into account the probabilities
p_{z}
and
p_{y}
.
We therefore expect to obtain a pure error, closely resemblant to the actual recovery configuration.
By employing this ‘good’ initial configuration, our SA decoder is expected to quickly converge to the optimal logical error rate.
Such a treatment can be applied to accommodate the given noise bias.

A key advantage of our SA decoder is that it provides explicit control over the trade-off between computational cost and accuracy by adjusting
N_{\beta}
.
That is, increasing the number of inverse temperatures
N_{\beta}
improves the decoding accuracy, but also increases the computational cost.
In contrast, the cost of deterministic algorithms such as the MWPM, CPLEX, and MPS decoders cannot be easily adjusted, and their parallelization is neither straightforward nor efficient.

VII
Conclusion

We have demonstrated our decoder for the XZZX code by using simulated annealing (SA).
As an initial configuration of SA, we have proposed to employ recovery chains obtained by our decoder which utilizes a kind of greedy matching.

We assumed the code capacity noise, where only the data qubits suffer errors.
Under the depolarizing noise and the
Y
-biased noises, we performed the numerical simulation.

We observed that our SA decoder achieves the logical error rate
P_{\mathrm{L}}
, equal to the optimal rate by the CPLEX decoder, which is formulated as an integer programming approach.
Moreover, it is more accurate than the MWPM decoder, which cannot take into account the probability of
Y
errors.

We examined the convergence of the logical error rate for the number of inverse temperatures
N_{\mathrm{\beta}}
we employ for our SA decoder.
In particular, our SA decoder can be interpreted as giving improvement of initial configuration by our greedy matching decoder.
Furthermore, we confirmed that the convergence is strongly influenced by methods of constructing a pure error, which are employed to prepare initial configurations.
Especially, we proposed the ‘Greedy matching (different)’ method which may produce a different pure error for every invocation, and observed that it leads to markedly faster convergence.

Subsequently, we compared the elapsed times of various decoding algorithms.
As a result, our SA decoder is faster than the CPLEX decoder when the physical error rate
p
is higher.
For lower
p
, our SA decoder, if parallelized, is faster than the CPLEX decoder.
In summary, we conclude that our parallelizable SA decoder is the fastest among the decoders, including the MPS decoder, which can take into account the probability of
Y
errors.
This parallelization of SAs and its simulation remains a topic for our future work.

We list other future works below.
Although our analysis has assumed the code capacity noise model, applying our SA decoder to phenomenological noise and circuit-level noise models is straightforward.

The decoding time may be reduced by improving the MCMC algorithm.
Our SA decoder consists of independent SA processes, making it well-suited for parallelized implementation in FPGA (field-programmable gate array).
Alternatively, techniques to accelerate the MCMC method could be employed, such as the replica-exchange Monte Carlo method
Hukushima and Nemoto 1996
and cluster algorithms including the Wolff algorithm
Wolff 1989
.
Furthermore, the Metropolis algorithm can be parallelized by domain (area) decomposition, which is implementable in FPGA
Haldar et al. 2000
.

In our SA decoder, we are able to control the trade-off between the decoding accuracy and time, by adjusting the number of Monte Carlo steps, temperature scheduling and convergence criterion, depending on the code distance
d
and the physical error rate
p
.
Finding a suitable set of parameter values may require exhaustive investigation.

The MCMC decoders require only the definitions of stabilizer generators and logical operators of codes, without relying on a decoding graph and, hence, they are applicable to many types of codes, including the color code and quantum LDPC codes
Breuckmann and Eberhardt 2021
.
To obtain a pure error to construct an initial error configuration, we can use not only our greedy matching decoder but also other decoders, such as the union-find decoder
Delfosse and Nickerson 2021
.
For faster convergence, we will pursue ‘good’ initial error configuration.

We plan to perform simulations under actual noise biases
p_{x}:p_{y}:p_{z}
specific to individual quantum devices.
Toward practical use in quantum computing, it is necessary to improve the decoding speed, particularly at lower physical error rates
p
.
Our results suggest that our SA decoder is a promising candidate for practical quantum computing.

Appendix A
Decoder formulated as integer programming problem

In this section, instead of Eq. (
20
), we formulate decoding problem as finding the recovery configuration
C
, subject to the constraint
\partial{C}=S
:

\arg\min_{C}\left\{\mathcal{H}(C)\,\middle|\,\partial{C}=S\right\}.

(33)

The error configuration
C
can be represented as the set of one bit binary variables
\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\}_{i}
where
i=1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{q}}
indexes the qubits.
Namely,
x_{i}=1
indicates that the qubit
i
suffers
X
error,
while the variables
y_{i}
and
z_{i}
similarly represent
Y
and
Z
errors, respectively.
Using this binary representation, the syndrome consistency condition
\partial{C}=S
in Eq. (
33
) is explicitly expressed as a system of equations: For each face
f
,

\begin{split}\left(z_{\mathrm{left}(f)}\oplus y_{\mathrm{left}(f)}\right)\oplus\left(z_{\mathrm{right}(f)}\oplus y_{\mathrm{right}(f)}\right)&\\
\,\oplus\left(x_{\mathrm{up}(f)}\oplus y_{\mathrm{up}(f)}\right)\oplus\left(x_{\mathrm{down}(f)}\oplus y_{\mathrm{down}(f)}\right)&=s_{f}\end{split}

(34)

where
s_{f}\in\{0,1\}
is the syndrome measurement outcome, as defined in Eq. (
2
), and the operation
\oplus
denotes addition modulo two.
Under the constraint Eq. (
34
), the goal is to find the solution
\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\}_{i}
that minimizes the objective function given by Eq. (
10
).
Here, the variables
n_{x}
,
n_{y}
,
n_{z}
represent the number of
X
-,
Y
-,
Z
-type errors, respectively, and are given by

n_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{q}}}x_{i},\quad n_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{q}}}y_{i},\quad n_{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{q}}}z_{i}.

This solution yields a recovery configuration
C
.

This problem falls into the category of (mixed-) integer linear programming, known to be NP-hard.
The integer programming formulation exhibits exponential computational complexity with respect to both the code distance
d
and the physical error rate
p
.
Thus, for large values of
d
or
p
, it is prohibitive to find a solution in a realistic time.
To solve the integer linear programming problem, we employ the C++ library, CPLEX (version 22.1.0)
IBM 2022
, whence we call it
CPLEX decoder
.
The use of integer programming solvers for decoding was originally proposed and demonstrated for the color code
Landahl et al. 2011
.

Since the CPLEX decoder can take the probabilities
p_{x}
,
p_{y}
,
p_{z}
into account by Eq. (
10
), it is capable of achieving the lowest logical error rate among the decoders formulated as the energy minimization problems.
Thus, we regard the logical error rate obtained by the CPLEX decoder as a reliable benchmark and thereby evaluate the accuracy of our SA decoder in Sec.
V
.

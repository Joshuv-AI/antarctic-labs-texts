Spinning quadrupoles in effective field theories of gravity
and Pablo Vives Matasan

Abstract
We study the effect of the two independent parity-even cubic interactions I 1 = R α ​ β μ ​ ν ​ R μ ​ ν ρ ​ σ ​ R ρ ​ σ α ​ β I_{1}={R^{\alpha\beta}}_{\mu\nu}{R^{\mu\nu}}_{\rho\sigma}{R^{\rho\sigma}}_{\alpha\beta} and G 3 = I 1 − 2 ​ R μ ​ ν ​ α β ​ R β ​ γ ν ​ σ ​ R σ μ ​ γ ​ α G_{3}=I_{1}-2{R^{\mu\nu\alpha}}_{\beta}{R^{\beta\gamma}}_{\nu\sigma}{R^{\sigma}}_{\mu\gamma\alpha} on the spectrum of gravitational waves emitted in the quasi-circular inspiral phase of the merger of two spinning objects. Focusing on the aligned spin configuration, we extract the corrections to Newton’s potential at linear order in the perturbations, using the four-point amplitude of the massive spinning objects evaluated in the Post-Minkowskian expansion. We then derive the modifications to the quadrupole moments at leading order in the cubic perturbations, using a five-point amplitude with emission of a soft graviton. These modified moments, along with the corresponding potentials, are then employed to calculate the power emitted by gravitational waves during the inspiral phase. Using these results, we determine the changes to the waveforms, up to linear order in spin, in the Stationary Phase Approximation. Finally, we comment on the relation between cubic and tidal perturbations.

Introduction

The direct detection of gravitational waves and the observations of binary black hole mergers by the LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA collaboration have opened a new observational window, which may challenge our current understanding of gravity. With expectations of enhanced experimental sensitivity in future observatories such as LISA, there will be a growing need for highly accurate theoretical predictions based on general relativity.
At the same time, one should also entertain the possibility that the Einstein-Hilbert theory is modified by higher-derivative interactions in an effective field theory description.

Quadrupoles and the PN formalism

In the PN framework, one can describe the conservative and dissipative dynamics of two objects with mass
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
coupled to
the gravity effective action (
1.3

using the point-particle effective action

\displaystyle S_{\text{pp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!dt\,\left[\frac{1}{2}\mu\,\dot{\bm{r}}^{\,2}\,-\,V(\bm{r},\bm{p})\,+\,\frac{1}{2}Q^{ij}(\bm{r},\bm{p})R^{0i0j}+\cdots\right]\ .

The potential

We also note that (

2.3
Spinning cubic corrections to the potential

The potential in (
2.8
) can by obtained from the elastic one-loop amplitudes of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), by Fourier transforming to position space with an appropriate normalisation
[
34
]
,

V(\bm{r},\bm{p})=\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}\frac{i\mathcal{M}_{4}(q)}{4E_{1}E_{2}}\ .

(2.11)

We can recast the hyperbolic functions as shift operators by using the fact that, inside the integral,
\bm{q}\to i\nabla_{\bm{r}}
. This allows us to remove them from the integral and apply them as shifts later,

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ \cosh{(\bm{a}_{i}{\cdot}\bm{q})}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}f(\bm{q})

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{i\bm{a}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla_{\bm{r}}}+e^{-i\bm{a}_{i}{\cdot}\nabla_{\bm{r}}}\right)\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}f(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{f}(\bm{r}+i\bm{a}_{i})+\tilde{f}(\bm{r}-i\bm{a}_{i})\right)\ ,

(2.12)

and similarly for
\sinh(\bm{a}_{i}{\cdot}\bm{q})
. We can deal with the Levi-Civita term by a similar shifting procedure, defining
\bm{\epsilon}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q}=\epsilon(u_{1}u_{2}a_{2}\bm{q})
:

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}\bm{\epsilon}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q}f(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}(\bm{r}+it\bm{\epsilon}_{2})}f(\bm{q})\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left.\tilde{f}(\bm{r}+it\bm{\epsilon}_{2})\right|_{t=0}\,.

(2.13)

This leaves us with the computation of two integrals,

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}|\bm{q}|^{3}\ ,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}|\bm{q}|^{3}\frac{\sinh{(\bm{a}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q})}}{\bm{a}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q}}\ ,

(2.15)

where we have kept the
\sinh{(\bm{a}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q})}
term to make the cancellation of the spurious pole manifest. These then have to be shifted and differentiated as appropriate. The first integral has a simple closed-form expression, and the second can be done by using the Schwinger trick as in
[
10
]
. The explicit expressions for the integrals are presented in Appendix
A
.

Once the dust settles, the potentials for the various cubic corrections evaluated in the centre of mass frame, for the aligned spin configuration, are given by

\displaystyle V_{I_{1}}(r,\bm{p})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{6}\frac{9}{\pi^{2}r^{6}}\Biggl[\frac{\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)^{3}\bm{p}^{2}}{16m_{1}m_{2}}+\frac{3\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)\sqrt{\bm{p}^{2}}\left(a_{2}m_{1}+a_{1}m_{2}\right)}{8r}

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(m_{1}-m_{2}\right)^{2}\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)^{3}\bm{p}^{4}}{32m_{1}^{3}m_{2}^{3}}\Biggr]+\cdots\,,\,

(2.16)

\displaystyle V_{G_{3}}(r,\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{6}\frac{9m_{1}m_{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})}{16\pi^{2}r^{6}}\Biggr[1-\bm{p}^{2}\frac{m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}}{2m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}}+\bm{p}^{4}\frac{3m_{1}^{4}+2m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{2}+3m_{2}^{4}}{8m_{1}^{4}m_{2}^{4}}

\displaystyle+\frac{3(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})}{r^{2}}\Biggr]+\cdots\ ,

(2.17)

where
a_{i}\coloneq|\bm{a}_{i}|
,
i=1,2
and
r\coloneq|\bm{r}|
.
We have expanded these expressions in PN and spin for presentation, but the integrals in Appendix
A
are all-order expressions.

3.1
The five-point spinning amplitude with cubic interactions

We now compute the five-point amplitude for the scattering of two objects with masses
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
and spin vectors
\bm{a}_{1}
and
\bm{a}_{2}
. We will obtain the fully relativistic amplitude, and then perform PN expansion in order to extract the correction to the quadrupole term in the effective action (
2.8
).

The relevant diagram is shown in Figure
2
, where we use the three-graviton vertices (
2.7
) and (
B.1
),
the energy-momentum tensor (
2.6
) and the graviton propagator in de Donder gauge.
The momenta of the two internal gravitons are spacelike, corresponding to an instantaneous interaction. The energy of the radiated (on-shell) graviton is such that

k^{0}\ll|\vec{q}_{1,2}\,|
, so that we can take the soft limit,
k\to 0
, and set
q_{1}\to-q
and
q_{2}\to q
. In terms of length scales, this condition corresponds to demanding that the wavelength of the emitted radiation
\lambda\gg b
.
The emitted graviton thus enters the amplitude only through the Riemann tensor
{R}_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(k)
.
Doing so, we obtain for the two interactions
I_{1}
and
G_{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{5,I_{1}}

\displaystyle=-48i\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{4}\frac{q^{\beta}q^{\delta}}{q^{2}}{R}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(k)\bigg[2(p_{1}{\cdot}J_{1})p_{2}^{\alpha}J_{2}^{\gamma}+2(p_{2}{\cdot}J_{2})p_{1}^{\alpha}J_{1}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad-\Big((p_{1}\cdot p_{2})J_{1}^{\alpha}J_{2}^{\gamma}+(p_{1}{\cdot}J_{2})J_{1}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{\gamma}+(p_{2}{\cdot}J_{1})p_{1}^{\alpha}J_{2}^{\gamma}+(J_{1}{\cdot}J_{2})p_{1}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{\gamma}\Big)\bigg],

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{5,G_{3}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{5,I_{1}}-24i\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{4}\frac{q^{\beta}q^{\delta}}{q^{2}}{R}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(k)\Big[(p_{2}{\cdot}J_{2})p_{1}^{\alpha}J_{1}^{\gamma}+(p_{1}{\cdot}J_{1})p_{2}^{\alpha}J_{2}^{\gamma}\Big]\ .

(3.2)

Here we have defined the vectors

J_{i}=p_{i}\cosh{(a_{i}{\cdot}q)}+ie_{i}\frac{\sinh{(a_{i}{\cdot}q)}}{a_{i}{\cdot}q}\,,

(3.3)

3.2
Spinning quadrupole moments

With the five-point amplitudes at hand, we can now extract the quadrupole moments from them, by matching to the effective action (
2.8
). In fact, the five-point amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{5}
contains more information than we need for our present purposes, as

\mathcal{M}_{5}=\tilde{Q}^{ij}R^{0i0j}+\tilde{J}^{jk}\epsilon^{ik\ell}R^{0ij\ell}+\cdots\ .

(3.5)

The first term corresponds to the quadrupole interaction, while the second corresponds to the current quadrupole, which is subleading in the multipolar expansion
[
35
,
36
]
. The dots represent higher order multipoles coming from subleading terms in the soft expansion of the amplitude. To match to the point-particle effective action (
2.8
) we will need to Fourier transform and account for a non-relativistic normalisation factor, similar to the potential. For the case of extracting the quadrupole from the five-point amplitude, the appropriate integral is

Q^{ij}(\bm{r},\bm{p})=-\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}\frac{i\tilde{Q}^{ij}(q)}{2E_{1}E_{2}}\ ,

(3.6)

with a different sign
compared to (
2.11
) owing to the different signs of the second and third term in the point-particle effective action (
2.8
).
In evaluating the quadrupole part of the amplitude, we encounter within
J_{i}^{0}
the
0^{\rm th}
component of the Levi-Civita contractions of (
3.4
). We can rewrite this by leveraging the fact that we are in the centre of mass frame, where
p_{1}+p_{2}=(E_{1}+E_{2},\bm{0})=(E_{1}+E_{2})\hat{t}
, with
\hat{t}\coloneq(1,\bm{0})
. Thus,

e_{1}^{0}=\hat{t}{\cdot}e_{1}=\frac{1}{E_{1}+E_{2}}(p_{1}+p_{2}){\cdot}e_{1}=\frac{p_{2}{\cdot}e_{1}}{E_{1}+E_{2}}\ ,

(3.7)

since
p_{1}{\cdot}e_{1}=0
. The Levi-Civita contractions defined earlier in (
3.4
) will now only appear in the following combinations:
p_{2}{\cdot}e_{1}
and
p_{1}{\cdot}e_{2}
. Reshuffling the indices we can rewrite these as

\displaystyle p_{2}{\cdot}e_{1}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(p_{2}a_{1}p_{1}q)=-m_{1}m_{2}\epsilon(u_{1}u_{2}a_{1}q)=:-m_{1}m_{2}\ q{\cdot}\epsilon_{1}\ ,

(3.8)

\displaystyle p_{1}{\cdot}e_{2}

\displaystyle=\epsilon(p_{1}a_{2}p_{2}q)=-m_{1}m_{2}\epsilon(u_{1}u_{2}a_{2}q)=:-m_{1}m_{2}\ q{\cdot}\epsilon_{2}\ .

(3.9)

In principle, the Fourier integrals can be done using methods identical to the potential integrals. However, we now have integrands with two spurious poles, corresponding to the
\sinh{(a_{1}{\cdot}q)}\sinh{(a_{2}{\cdot}q)}
terms. This would require two Schwinger parameter integrals to calculate exactly. Since we are only interested in the leading term in spin, we can expand the integrand in spin, greatly simplifying the calculations. Integrands with dot products of
q
in the numerator, such as
a_{i}{\cdot}q
or
q{\cdot}\epsilon_{i}
can then be performed using the shifting trick in (
2.13
). These are, generically,

\mathcal{Q}^{ij}_{n_{1}\cdots n_{4}}\coloneq\int\!\frac{\hat{d}^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{q^{i}q^{j}}{\bm{q}^{2}}(\bm{a}_{1}{\cdot}\bm{q})^{n_{1}}(\bm{a}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q})^{n_{2}}(\bm{\epsilon}_{1}{\cdot}\bm{q})^{n_{3}}(\bm{\epsilon}_{2}{\cdot}\bm{q})^{n_{4}}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}\ ,

(3.10)

noting that at order
m
in spin, we must have
\sum_{i}n_{i}=m
. Notice that when expanding
\sinh{(a_{i}{\cdot}q)}
, the spurious
a_{i}{\cdot}q
poles disappear. Now, we can express the integral above as derivatives of the shifted
\bm{r}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{ij}_{n_{1}\cdots n_{4}}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial^{n_{1}}}{\partial t_{1}^{n_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{n_{4}}}{\partial t_{4}^{n_{4}}}\int\!\frac{\hat{d}^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{q^{i}q^{j}}{\bm{q}^{2}}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}(\bm{r}+it_{1}\bm{a}_{1}+\cdots+it_{4}\bm{\epsilon}_{2})}\right|_{t_{i}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{n_{1}}}{\partial t_{1}^{n_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{n_{4}}}{\partial t_{4}^{n_{4}}}\mathcal{Q}^{ij}(\bm{r}+it_{1}\bm{a}_{1}+\cdots+it_{4}\bm{\epsilon}_{2})|_{t_{i}=0}\ ,

(3.11)

where
\mathcal{Q}^{ij}
is the quadrupole integral appearing in the scalar calculation,

\mathcal{Q}^{ij}(\bm{r})\coloneq\int\!\frac{\hat{d}^{3}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{q^{i}q^{j}}{\bm{q}^{2}}e^{-i\bm{q}{\cdot}\bm{r}}=-\frac{3}{4\pi\bm{r}^{5}}\Big(r^{i}r^{j}-\frac{1}{3}\bm{r}^{2}\delta^{ij}\Big)\ .

(3.12)

The Fourier transforms can now be computed efficiently. Here we present the results up to and including spin squared terms. As in the calculation of the potentials, we work in the aligned spin case and in the centre of mass frame. Expanding in PN we get

\displaystyle Q^{ij}_{I_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{4}\frac{36}{\pi r^{3}}\Biggl[\frac{\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)^{2}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\bm{p}^{2}\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}+3\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)\sqrt{\bm{p}^{2}}\frac{\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}}{r}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{5a_{1}a_{2}m_{1}m_{2}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{\left(m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}\right){}^{2}\bm{p}^{4}}{2m_{1}^{3}m_{2}^{3}}\right)\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}-\frac{a_{1}a_{2}m_{1}m_{2}\hat{z}_{\langle i,j\rangle}}{r^{2}}\Biggr]+\cdots\,,

(3.13)

\displaystyle Q^{ij}_{G_{3}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{4}\frac{18}{\pi r^{3}}\Biggl[m_{1}m_{2}\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}-\frac{2\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)^{2}\bm{p}^{2}\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}}{m_{1}m_{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{3\sqrt{\bm{p}^{2}}\big(\left(4a_{1}+3a_{2}\right)m_{1}+\left(3a_{1}+4a_{2}\right)m_{2}\big)\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}}{2r}+\Biggl(\frac{5\left(a_{1}-a_{2}\right)^{2}m_{1}m_{2}}{2r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{9\left(m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}\bm{p}^{4}}{8m_{1}^{3}m_{2}^{3}}\Biggr)\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}-\frac{\left(a_{1}-a_{2}\right){}^{2}m_{1}m_{2}\hat{z}_{\langle i,j\rangle}}{r^{2}}\Biggr]+\cdots\,.

(3.14)

We have used the notation
X_{\langle i,j\rangle}{\coloneq}X_{i}X_{j}-\frac{1}{3}X^{2}\delta_{ij}
to denote the symmetric trace-free part, and we have aligned the spin of the two bodies along the
\hat{z}
axis.

A few comments are in order here. As already mentioned, we have organised the PN expansion in powers of
\bm{p}^{2}{\sim}1/r{\sim}v^{2}
. The cubic corrections to Einstein’s quadrupole

\displaystyle Q^{ij}_{N}\coloneq\mu r^{2}\hat{r}_{\langle i,j\rangle}=\mu\Bigl(r^{i}r^{j}-\frac{1}{3}r^{2}\delta^{ij}\Bigr)\,,

(3.15)

with
\mu
being the reduced mass, appear at 6PN and 5PN order for
I_{1}
and
G_{3}
, respectively – these are the same orders as in the corrections to the potential (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), and for convenience we quote here terms up to 7PN.
Furthermore, most of the
I_{1}
and
G_{3}
corrections are proportional but there are additional contributions proportional to
\hat{z}_{\langle i,j\rangle}
.
We also note that in the scalar case, the quadrupole correction due to the
I_{1}
interaction receives contributions starting at
O(\bm{p}^{2})
, while in the presence of spin there are corrections of
O(1)
in the velocities but quadratic in the spins.
Furthermore, the quadrupole moment receives
O(\sqrt{\bm{p}^{2}})
corrections from
I_{1}
and
G_{3}
, for similar reasons as the potentials (
2.16
) and (
2.17

3.3
On the relation between cubic and tidal interactions

In
[
5
]
it was shown that an appropriate field redefinition of the metric

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}\rightarrow g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{3}{8}\kappa^{2}\beta_{2}\,g_{\alpha\beta}R^{\mu\nu}\,_{\rho\sigma}R^{\rho\sigma}\,_{\mu\nu}\ ,

(3.16)

maps the cubic interaction
G_{3}
into the first term in the action for the leading tidal deformations for scalar fields
introduced in
[
37
]
,

\displaystyle S_{\rm tidal}=\int\!d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\ \frac{1}{4}R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}R^{\rho\alpha\sigma\beta}\sum_{i=1,2}\Big(\lambda_{i}\,\phi_{i}^{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}\delta^{\nu}_{\sigma}+\frac{\eta_{i}}{m_{i}^{4}}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi_{i}\nabla_{\rho}\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{i}\,\Big)\ .

(3.17)

In the worldline formalism, these leading tidal deformations correspond to
[
37
]

\displaystyle S_{\rm tidal}^{\rm WL}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\int\!d\tau_{i}\,\Big[2\lambda_{i}\big(E^{2}_{i}-B^{2}_{i}\big)+\frac{\eta_{i}}{4}E_{i}^{2}\Big]\ \ ,

(3.18)

where the electric and magnetic components of the Weyl tensor are

\displaystyle E_{i,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{\alpha}v_{i}^{\beta}C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}\,,

(3.19)

\displaystyle B_{i,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}v_{i}^{\alpha}v_{i}^{\beta}{\epsilon_{\mu\alpha}}^{\rho\sigma}C_{\rho\sigma\nu\beta}\,.

(3.20)

Hence, for non-spinning objects the
G_{3}
deformation is equivalent to the particular tidal interaction
E^{2}{-}B^{2}{\sim}R^{2}
.
This has also been argued by
[
38
]
on the basis that the Compton amplitude produced by the
G_{3}
interaction is a contact term
8
8

8

The term proportional to
q^{2}
in the following equation gives rise to quantum corrections and can therefore be discarded.

[
8
,
9
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{G_{3}}^{\rm scalar}(p,k_{1}^{++},k_{2}^{++})=-3\,i\,\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)^{4}[1\,2]^{4}\left(m^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)\,,

(3.21)

with
q=k_{1}+k_{2}
. Because this term is local, it is already captured by the tidal operator of the form
E^{2}-B^{2}
.

We can immediately argue that
the same is true if the particles have spin: indeed, the Compton amplitude has now the form
[
10
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{G_{3}}(p,k_{1}^{++},k_{2}^{++})=\cosh(a{\cdot}q)\mathcal{M}_{G_{3}}^{\rm scalar}(p,k_{1}^{++},k_{2}^{++})\,,

(3.22)

which is also a local term.
Furthermore, the second term in (
3.17
) maps to
E_{\mu\nu}E^{\mu\nu}
once we replace the derivatives
\nabla^{\mu}
by the four-momentum
mv_{i}^{\mu}
carried by the scalar, and is therefore an electric tidal deformation.
We are not aware of any field redefinition that could map this tidal deformation to a purely gravitation one.

However, we observed further similarities between tidal interactions and cubic deformations, which we wish to summarise briefly in the following.

Tidal effects for spinning particles were studied in detail in
[
39
]
.
In that paper a general formula for the impulse (momentum kick) was presented. Remarkably, the linear-in-spin terms (lines 2 and 3 of (5.4)) have exactly the same form as the linear-in-spin correction of the impulse arising from the other cubic deformation,
I_{1}
, which was presented in the first line of (6.65) of
[
10
]
. The first (
S^{0}
) term in (5.4) of
[
39
]
instead maps to our
G_{3}
correction to the impulse, see (6.66) of
[

4
From amplitudes to waveforms

We have seen how cubic corrections modify various aspects of binaries dynamics. Through the potential, not only will the size of their circular orbits be modified, but so will the energy contained in their interaction. From the corrected quadrupoles, the power emission as given by Einstein’s quadrupole formula will also be modified. Combining these three things using energy conservation, we will be able to determine the corrections to the phasing of gravitational waves due to the
R^{3}
interactions.

Focusing on now on quasi-circular orbits, these corrections can be expanded in terms of the velocity,

v\coloneq(GM\Omega)^{1/3}\ .

(4.1)

We will also find it useful to write expressions in terms of the following combinations of the masses
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
:

M\coloneq m_{1}+m_{2}\ ,\qquad\mu\coloneq\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{M}\,,\qquad\nu\coloneq\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{M^{2}}\ .

(4.2)

Note that the symmetric mass ratio
0{<}\nu{\leq}1/4
, achieving its maximum when
m_{1}{=}m_{2}
.

4.1
Corrections to circular orbits

Due to the inclusion of
R^{3}
interactions, the size of the circular orbit at some angular velocity
\Omega
will be modified compared to the Newtonian case, which is given by the well-known Kepler’s law

r_{N}^{3}=\frac{GM}{\Omega^{2}}\,.

(4.3)

The size of the orbits will instead be given by some
r_{R^{3}}=r_{N}+\delta r
.
We can calculate this difference by examining the spinning
R^{3}
potentials (
2.16
) and (
2.17
). Generically, these corrections mean that the Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle H=\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\mu}+V(r)+\epsilon\sum_{n=0}^{N}|\bm{p}|^{n}U_{n}(r)\ .

(4.4)

Here the small parameter
\epsilon
will play the role of
\beta_{1,2}
, and
\bm{p}^{2}=p_{r}^{2}+p_{\theta}^{2}/r^{2}
in plane polar coordinates. Since
H
does not depend on
\theta
, we deduce that the angular momentum
p_{\theta}:=\ell
is conserved. Imposing the first condition for a circular orbit,
\dot{r}=0
, leads to
p_{r}=0
. Looking at the equation of motion for
\dot{\theta}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{\theta}}=\frac{p_{\theta}}{\mu r^{2}}+\epsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{np_{\theta}^{n-1}}{r^{n}}U_{n}(r)=\dot{\theta}:=\Omega\ .

(4.5)

In general, this is a
n{-}1
order polynomial equation for
p_{\theta}
. However, since we are only interested in the motion to linear order in
\epsilon
, we can solve the system approximately by writing

\displaystyle p_{\theta}=p_{\theta}^{(0)}+\epsilon\,p_{\theta}^{(1)}+O(\epsilon^{2})\ .

(4.6)

This leads to the solution

\displaystyle p_{\theta}=\mu r^{2}\Omega-\epsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N}n\mu^{n}r^{n}\Omega^{n-1}U_{n}(r)+O(\epsilon^{2})\ .

(4.7)

The final equation for the Hamiltonian system, for
\dot{p_{r}}
, will provide an equation for the radii of circular orbits. Explicitly,

\displaystyle-\frac{p_{\theta}^{2}}{\mu r^{3}}+V^{\prime}(r)+\epsilon\sum_{n=0}^{N}\biggl(-\frac{np_{\theta}^{n}}{r^{n+1}}U_{n}(r)+\frac{p_{\theta}^{n}}{r^{n}}U^{\prime}_{n}(r)\biggr)=0\ .

(4.8)

We can use the angular equation of motion (
4.5
) to simplify the expression, leading to

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(r)-\frac{p_{\theta}}{r}\Omega+\epsilon\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{p_{\theta}^{n}}{r^{n}}U^{\prime}_{n}(r)=0\ .

(4.9)

This can be solved for small perturbations about the Newtonian circular orbit
r_{N}
. Inserting the specific values for the potentials
U_{n}(r)
from (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), the corrections to the circular radius are, to linear order in the couplings
\beta_{i}
,

\displaystyle\delta r=

\displaystyle\frac{576\pi v^{8}}{G^{2}M^{3}}\biggl[\beta_{1}\left(\frac{2v^{2}}{3}+\frac{6v^{3}\left(a_{2}m_{1}+a_{1}m_{2}\right)}{GM^{2}}-\frac{1}{6}(1-4\nu)v^{4}\right)

\displaystyle-\beta_{2}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{3}(1-2\nu)v^{2}+\frac{4\left(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}\right)v^{4}}{G^{2}M^{2}}+\frac{1}{24}\left(8\nu^{2}-12\nu+3\right)v^{4}\biggr)\biggr]+\cdots\,.

(4.10)

4.2
Correction to the energy density

In unmodified gravity, the energy density contained in a binary system (the energy per unit mass
M
) is

E=-\frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{2Mr}=-\frac{v^{2}\nu}{2}\ .

(4.11)

Evaluating the Hamiltonian (
4.4
) in the presence of the cubic deformations and including the modifications to the radius of circular orbits found in (
4.10
), the corrections to the energy density become

\displaystyle\Delta E_{I_{1}}(v)

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}\frac{1056\pi\nu\,v^{12}}{G^{3}M^{4}}\biggl[v^{2}+\frac{72v^{3}(a_{2}m_{1}+a_{1}m_{2})}{11GM^{2}}-\frac{13}{22}(1-4\nu)v^{4}\biggr]+\cdots

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Delta E_{G_{3}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\beta_{2}\frac{864\pi\nu\,v^{12}}{G^{3}M^{4}}\bigg[1-\frac{11}{18}(1-2\nu)v^{2}+\frac{13(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})v^{4}}{3G^{2}M^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 80.0pt+\frac{13}{72}(8\nu^{2}-12\nu+3)v^{4}\bigg]+\cdots

(4.13)

We have expanded the energies up to 7PN for convenience, as done earlier for the potentials and quadrupole moments.

4.3
Corrections to the power flux

The power emitted by the binary system is given by the famous Einstein quadrupole formula,
11
11

11

It would be interesting to see if it is possible to compute the power from a continuation of the scattering waveforms computed in
[

]
, similarly to what was done in
[

.

\mathcal{F}=\frac{G}{5}\langle\dddot{Q}^{ij}\dddot{Q}^{ij}\rangle\ .

(4.14)

The quadrupole is averaged over one orbit of the binary, denoted by the angle brackets. Up to leading order in the
\beta_{i}
and spin expansions, the quadrupole is, limiting ourselves to linear order in the spins,

\displaystyle Q=Q_{N}+Q_{R^{3}}+Q_{R^{3},a^{1}}+O(\beta_{i}^{2},a^{2})\ ,

(4.15)

meaning that the full emitted power at this leading order is given by

\mathcal{F}|_{R^{3},a^{1}}=\frac{G}{5}\langle\dddot{Q}^{ij}_{N,a^{0}}\dddot{Q}^{ij}_{R^{3},a^{1}}\rangle\ .

(4.16)

Here,
Q_{R^{3},a^{1}}
are the quadrupoles given in (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), and
Q_{N,a^{0}}
is Einstein’s quadrupole given in (
3.15
).

In principle, there should be a contribution to (
4.14
) arising from the overlap of the leading spinning correction in general relativity and the scalar
R^{3}
corrections, however we will now show that this contribution vanishes. Working in the transverse-traceless (
TT
) gauge, the leading multipolar contribution to the field is given by

h^{ij}_{TT}(u,r,\hat{n})=\frac{\kappa}{16\pi r}\Lambda^{ij,kl}\ddot{Q}^{kl}\ ,

(4.17)

leaving implicit the instruction to evaluate it at retarded time
u

\Lambda^{ij,kl}(\hat{n})\coloneq P^{ik}P^{jl}-\frac{1}{2}P^{ij}P^{kl}
is the transverse-traceless projector, with
P_{ij}{\coloneq}\delta_{ij}-\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}
, and
\hat{n}
denotes the direction of the radiation. On the other hand, the linear-in-spin correction to the field in general relativity takes the form
[
41
]

h^{ij}_{TT,a^{1}}(u,r,\hat{n})=\frac{\kappa}{16\pi r}\Lambda^{ij,kl}(\hat{n})\ 2\varepsilon^{abc}\hat{n}^{a}\Big[m_{1}a_{1}^{c}(\delta^{bk}\dot{v}_{1}^{l}+\delta^{bl}\dot{v}_{1}^{k})+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\Big]\ ,

(4.18)

corresponding to a current quadrupole contribution. Let us take a step back from the quadrupole formula and consider where it originates from. Expanding the Einstein Field Equations to second order in the perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
, one can derive an effective
energy-momentum tensor for the gravitational waves. The angular power distribution, associated to the
(0r)
component of this energy momentum tensor can be expressed as
[
42
]

T^{\rm GW}_{0r}=\frac{d\mathcal{F}}{d\Omega}=\langle\dot{h}^{ij}_{TT}\dot{h}^{ij}_{TT}\rangle\ .

(4.19)

To obtain the total power flux
\mathcal{F}
, this needs to be integrated over all angles (
i.e
.
\hat{n}
). However, notice that when performing the angular integrations on the contraction of (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), one obtains a vanishing result:

\mathcal{F}\sim\int\!d\Omega\ \Lambda^{ij,kl}\Lambda^{ij,mn}\hat{n}^{a}=\int\!d\Omega\ \Lambda^{kl,mn}\hat{n}^{a}=\int\!d\Omega\ \hat{n}^{k}\hat{n}^{l}\hat{n}^{m}\hat{n}^{n}\hat{n}^{a}+\cdots=0\ .

(4.20)

This is because each of the integrals is fully symmetric, and thus can only depend on
\delta^{ij}
, but they all have an odd number of indices. This is a specific manifestation of a more general feature of the multipolar expansion
[
35
]
. The field
h_{\mu\nu}
can be expanded in terms of two families of symmetric trace-free (STF) tensors, often called the
mass multipoles
and the
current multipoles
, given in terms of integrals of the mass-energy distribution
T_{\mu\nu}
. It can be shown by symmetry that, for the power emitted, only time averages of the multipoles with themselves contribute. This means that there are never any cross terms between mass and current quadrupoles, or between quadrupoles corresponding to different values of
\ell
. Hence, we will only consider the linear-in-spin corrections from here on. In the ensuing calculations, this will translate to expanding to 6.5PN order, dropping the 7PN contributions where quadratic-in-spin terms begin.

Returning to the expression for the flux in (
4.16
), we can perform the time averaging between the spinning
R^{3}
quadrupoles of (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) and the Newtonian quadrupole (
3.15

\bm{r}=r(\cos{\Omega t},\sin{\Omega t},0)
and averaging over one circular orbit,

\langle\dddot{Q}^{ij}_{N,a^{0}}\dddot{Q}^{ij}_{R^{3},a^{1}}\rangle\coloneq\frac{1}{2\pi/\Omega}\int_{0}^{2\pi/\Omega}\!dt\ \dddot{Q}^{ij}_{N,a^{0}}\dddot{Q}^{ij}_{R^{3},a^{1}}\ .

(4.21)

Doing so, one gets

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{32\nu^{2}v^{10}}{5G}+\beta_{1}\,\mathcal{F}_{I_{1}}(v)+\beta_{2}\,\mathcal{F}_{G_{3}}(v)\,,

(4.22)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{I_{1}}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{196608\pi\nu^{2}v^{20}}{5G^{4}M^{4}}\biggl[v^{2}+\frac{9v^{3}((a_{1}+2a_{2})m_{1}+(2a_{1}+a_{2})m_{2})}{4GM^{2}}]\biggr]\,,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{G_{3}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\frac{147456\pi\nu^{2}v^{20}}{5G^{4}M^{4}}\biggl[1-\frac{1}{6}(7-2\nu)v^{2}-\frac{3v^{3}((4a_{1}+3a_{2})m_{1}+(3a_{1}+4a_{2})m_{2})}{4GM^{2}}\biggr]\,.

(4.24)

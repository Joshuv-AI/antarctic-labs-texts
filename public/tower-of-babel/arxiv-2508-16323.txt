Curves on surfaces with prescribed pairwise intersection numbers
Ferit Öztürk

Abstract
. Given an ordered sequence of N N choose 2 integers, we give necessary and sufficient conditions to have an ordered collection of N N simple closed curves on a torus such that the algebraic pairwise intersections of those curves are the given integers. We also present partial answers towards realizing curve systems on a surface with minimal genus, given the intersection numbers.

1.
Introduction

For
N\in\mathbb{Z},N\geq 2
, given an ordered
N\choose 2
-tuple of integers

{\mathbf{m}}=\left(m_{12};m_{13},m_{23};m_{14},m_{24},m_{34};\ldots;m_{1,N},m_{2,N},\ldots,m_{(N-1),N}\right)

we want to find a closed connected oriented 2-manifold (shortly a surface)
\Sigma_{g}
of an arbitrary genus
g

and a collection of
N
oriented, connected, simple closed curves
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}
(called a curve system) on

\Sigma_{g}
such that the
pairwise algebraic (i.e. signed) intersections of the curves realize the given intersection scheme
{\mathbf{m}}
, i.e.

\gamma_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j}=m_{ij}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq N
. (Similar question for geometric (i.e. unsigned) intersections
in case of nonegative
m_{ij}
’s.) We shortly call such an
{\mathbf{m}}
an
N
-scheme.
The question is to find the
least
possible such genus for the given
{\mathbf{m}}
, which we denote by
g({\mathbf{m}})
.
It can be shown immediately that any scheme is realized on some surface with sufficiently large genus;
i.e.
g({\mathbf{m}})
is finite (see Section
2.1
).

A natural question regarding placing curves on surfaces that has been studied extensively is the curve packing problem: given an upper bound for the number of pairwise (geometric) intersections the maximal number of essential, nonhomotopic simple closed curves is to be determined. In
[
JMM
]
an upper bound was given for the maximal value, which is linear in the given bound on a torus. This bound on a torus was improved considerably in
[
Ag1
]
,
[
Ag2
]
, with a direct application to knot theory and 3-manifolds.
In
[
Pr
]
bounds for maximal number of curves intersecting at most once were given for punctured hyperbolic surfaces.
Those bounds have been improved for closed surfaces in
[
Gr
]
.

There are also results regarding the curve graphs on a torus.
The curve graph on a torus (sometimes called the Farey graph)
is a graph
\mathcal{F}
which has a vertex for each isotopy class of curves;
two distinct vertices are connected by an edge if and only if the corresponding nonoriented classes intersect minimally (i.e. once on a torus).
More generally given
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
one can define graphs where two vertices are connected by an edge if and only if
the absolute value of the algebraic intersection is exactly
m
or is
\leq m
respectively; these graphs are called
m
-Farey graphs,
denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{m}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}
respectively
[
GLRRX
]
.
Results of curve packing problems on tori can be related directly with properties of Farey graphs. For instance an upper bound for the maximal number of curves on a torus that intersect pairwise at most
m
times is an upper bound for the chromatic number of the graph
\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}
. Many combinatorial properties of
\mathcal{F}_{m}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}
have been worked out in the last couple of decades;
see e.g.
[
ABG
]
,
[
AG
]
,
[
GLRRX
]
.

These works are related with the crossing numbers of planar (multi)graphs too. There are various results under the title of crossing lemmas; see e.g.
[
HP
]
or
[
KPTU
]
and references therein.

Instead of going along those directions, in this small note we set off to construct the curve systems directly for a given scheme.
Using simple manipulations we detect the necessary and sufficient conditions to realize a given scheme on a torus. As seen in the sequel, although the ideas are simple, we believe this definitive result has not been announced before. We are persuaded that our clear-cut result below will be of use in the aforementioned collection of problems above.

Theorem 1.1
.

An
N
-scheme
{\mathbf{m}}
with all
m_{ij}\neq 0
is realized on a torus if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(
\triangle
)

For any index triple
i,j,k
,
(m_{ij},m_{ik})=(m_{ij},m_{jk})=(m_{ik},m_{jk})
.

(
\square
)

For any index quadruple
i,j,k,l
,
m_{ij}m_{kl}-m_{ik}m_{jl}+m_{il}m_{jk}=0
.

(
\circledast
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{toz}({\mathbf{m}};p)<p\mbox{ for each prime $p<N$.}

The special case when some
m_{ij}=0
is dealt with in Section
2.1
.
We define the rational number
\operatorname{toz}({\mathbf{m}};p)
in Section
1
. It is an integer assigned to
{\mathbf{m}}
using the divisibility data of
m_{ij}
’s by the prime

, more precisely on the

-valuation

of the entries.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is in Section
2
. The problem takes place in the exterior algebra with two generators. We must basically find the integer solutions to a linear system since on a torus the isotopy classes of curves are determined by pairs of integers. The condition

□

‣
1.1
in Theorem

is readily recognized as the Grassmann-Plücker relations. Indeed our problem can be viewed as finding an integral basis for a 2-plane in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
where the plane is given via the Plücker coordinates
{\mathbf{m}}
(in our case they are not homogeneous coordinates). A necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathbf{m}}
to describe a plane in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the Grassmann-Plücker relations

□

‣
1.1
, i.e.
{\mathbf{m}}
lies on the Plücker quadric.

The further requirement in the problem is that an integral basis for this plane must have each corresponding pair of coordinates relatively prime. This gives the problem the number theoretic flavor, which introduces the conditions

△

‣
1.1
and

⊛

‣
1.1
.

We will see in the sequel that in special and simple cases

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume an
N
-scheme
{\mathbf{m}}
satisfies

□

is realized on a torus if there are three indices

such that one of the following sufficient conditions is satisfied:

(1)

.

(2)

\nu_{p}(g_{ijk})=0
for every prime
p\leq N
.

In the other direction,
{\mathbf{m}}

is constant regardless of

Given
N
, let
\mathfrak{g}(N)
denote the minimum genus which realizes all possible
N
-schemes, i.e.

\mathfrak{g}(N)=\sup_{N\textrm{-schemes }{\mathbf{m}}}\!\!g({\mathbf{m}}).

This is a finite integer. Indeed we show in Section

that
\mathfrak{g}(N)
is bounded above by
-2+N(N+1)/2
.
Of course this is a loose bound. For instance
\mathfrak{g}(2)=1
.
Also it follows from Theorem

that
\mathfrak{g}(3)>1
, i.e. there are integer triples that cannot be realized by any curve triple on a torus.
However some 3-schemes may be expressed as a sum of two 3-schemes each of which is realizable on a torus, so that
{\mathbf{m}}
may be realized on a genus-2 surface
obtained by joining the two tori via a connected sum.
If a 3-scheme
{\mathbf{m}}
cannot be expressed in such a way while
g({\mathbf{m}})=2
, we say that
{\mathbf{m}}
is 2-endemic.
(see Section

for the more general and precise definition).
We prove

Theorem 1.3
.

(a)
\mathfrak{g}(3)=2
, i.e. all 3-schemes are realized on a genus-2 surface. Moreover no 3-scheme
is
2
-endemic.

(b) There are infinitely many 2-endemic 4-schemes.

The second claim of part (a) declares simply that any 3-scheme which is realized on
\Sigma_{2}

is so because it has two summands each of which is realized on a torus. We prove part (a) in Section
2.4

and we give the construction of 2-endemic 4-schemes for part (b) in Section
3.2
.

Before proceeding let us state the apparent question.

Question 1.4
.

What is the characterization of all schemes which can be realized on a genus-2 surface?

Of course this is a difficult and involved question, which can be asked for an arbitrary genus
\;>2
too. However the complications are already there in genus 2.

2.
Curve systems on a torus

2.1.
Preliminaries.

Any
N
-scheme
{\mathbf{m}}
is realized on some surface with sufficiently large genus;
i.e.
g({\mathbf{m}})
is finite. In fact one can construct a surface as in Figure

Note that there the curves are isotopically distinct. Moreover since each pair of curves is minimally intersecting,

is the minimum signed intersection possible between the isotopy classes of
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}

The genus of the surface constructed is
-2+N(N+1)/2

Of course it should be possible to decrease this loose upper bound on genus.

Figure 1.

Suppose that the given scheme
{\mathbf{m}}
can be realized on a torus.
If
m_{ij}=0
for some
i<j
then either the corresponding curves are isotopic so that
their pairwise intersection with each other curve is the same; or, say,
\gamma_{i}
is empty so that its intersection with any other curve is 0 too.
In both cases we see that the realizability of that
{\mathbf{m}}
on a torus is equivalent to the realizability of the scheme with
\gamma_{i}
ignored.
Because of this particular and basic feature of 0 intersection on a torus, in the following sections we deal with the cases when all
m_{ij}
’s are nonzero.

For example the realization of the scheme
(a;0,0;0,0,0)
,
a
nonzero, will be a pair of curves on a torus that intersect
a
times.
The solution for
(a;a,0)
consists of three curves on a torus with
\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{3}
parallel and

\gamma_{1}
intersects each
a
times. Note that
(a;b,0)
with nonzero

is not realizable on a torus.

In general on
\Sigma_{g},g>1

occurs if and only if in the curve complex of
\Sigma_{g}
the vertices that represent the
isotopy class of the corresponding curves
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
are connected by an edge.
For example
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
may be homologous but not isotopic.

A priori some
N
-schemes are simpler than the others in the following sense.
Suppose that the
N
-schemes
{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime\prime}
are realized by the curves
\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{N}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{N}

on some surfaces
\Sigma_{g^{\prime}}
and
\Sigma_{g^{\prime\prime}}
of positive genera

and
g^{\prime\prime}
respectively
and that there are oriented arcs
\omega^{\prime}\subset\Sigma_{g^{\prime}}
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}\subset\Sigma_{g^{\prime\prime}}
such that

\omega^{\prime}
intersects
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}

\omega^{\prime\prime}
intersects
\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{i}
’s (
1\leq i\leq N
).
Then the
N
-scheme
{\mathbf{m}}={\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime\prime}
is realized on a surface
\Sigma_{g}
with genus

. In fact,

\Sigma_{g}
can be constructed as a
connected sum of
\Sigma_{g^{\prime}}
and
\Sigma_{g^{\prime\prime}}
at a pair of disks that are sufficiently small neighborhoods of the arcs

\omega^{\prime}
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}
. In that way each curve
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
on
\Sigma_{g^{\prime}}
is connected to the corresponding curve on
\Sigma_{g^{\prime\prime}}

This final curve

is homologous to the disjoint union of the two curves on
\Sigma_{g}
.
Since the algebraic intersection is an invariant on homology classes, it follows that
{\mathbf{m}}
is realized by
\gamma_{i}

For example for the 3-scheme
(a;b,0)
that we mentioned above, which is not realizable on a torus,
one can express
(a;b,0)
as
(b;b,0)+(a-b;0,0)

and
\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
on one torus can be connected to
\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{1},\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{2}
on the other so that

can be realized on
\Sigma_{2}
.

The construction in the reverse direction is not valid in general. We will show in Section

that
there are schemes realized on some
\Sigma_{g}
(

) for which there can be no such

and
{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime\prime}

as above. We call such a scheme

-endemic.

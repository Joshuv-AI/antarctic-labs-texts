Analytical solutions for ultra-fast precessional switching in inertial magnetization dynamics
Alessandro Fortunati, Massimiliano d’Aquino, Claudio Serpico

Abstract
Here we consider the magnetization dynamics in ferromagnetic nanoparticles or films driven by external magnetic field pulses directed transverse to the initial equilibrium state. The excitation pulse drives large-angle ultra-fast magnetization dynamics that may eventually end up in the reversed equilibrium realizing successful precessional switching. We consider ultra-short pulse duration (fractions of picosecond) and large external field intensities (several Tesla) which may be relevant for the realization of faster energy-efficient memory cells. For such short time scales, we include inertial effects in the theoretical description by considering the inertial Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation which requires to be treated as a system of singularly perturbed ODEs for small values of the inertia. By using suitable perturbation approach based on multiple time scales analysis, we develop an approximate closed-form solution for the switching dynamics as well as formulas for switching time and its safety tolerances to obtain the successful switching as function of physical parameters of the particle and strength of magnetic inertia. The developed analytical solution is validated by numerical simulations. Keywords: magnetization switching, inertial Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert dynamics, analytical solution, multiple time scale analysis. 2010 MSC . Primary: 78A25, 34D10, 34H05. Secondary: 37C50, 37C75.



1
Introduction

The study of ultra-fast magnetization dynamics is a central theme both for the advancement of knowledge in the area of applied magnetism and for its important implications on the realization of improved magnetic and spintronic nanotechnologies
[
DPG+20
]
, such as faster magnetic recording technologies (e.g. magnetic random access memory - MRAM - cells, advanced sensors or non boolean logic devices).

With the ever-growing demand for faster and more energy-efficient data storage, understanding and controlling magnetization switching at picosecond and even shorter timescales becomes essential. Traditional magnetic recording methods or those which rely on thermal activation (e.g. heat-assisted magnetic recording - HAMR) are inherently limited in terms of speed and energy efficiency. In contrast, precessional switching, where magnetization is driven by strong and ultra-short external magnetic field
[
KR02
]
or spin-torques pulses
[
BLS+10
]
, has potential to overcome these barriers by enabling ultra-fast, non-thermal switching mechanisms.

In spite of the potential advantages in terms of speed, precessional switching suffers from the strong sensitivity to the timing of the external excitation pulse, which must be very precise in order to switch off at the right instant and let magnetization relax to the target reversed orientation.
This issue has been the focus of theoretical studies
[
BMSd03
,
dSS+04
,
MBS09
,
FdS24
]
that provided analytical formulas for switching time as function of material and geometrical parameters of the nanomagnet.

However, when ultra-short (below ps) external pulses with large intensities (several Tesla) are used, the emergence of inertial effects on magnetization dynamics becomes relevant in that magnetization undergoes nutations at THz frequencies superimposed to classical precessional dynamics (see Fig.
1
for a sketch), as it was experimentally demonstrated in recent times
[
NAK+20
]
after the theoretical prediction one decade before
[
CRW11
]
. For this reason, in theoretical analysis of such processes, one has to consider the inertial version of the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation, termed hereafter iLLG equation
[
CRW11
,
NPS+22
]
.

Despite its apparent similarity with the classical LLG, the iLLG equation has profoundly different mathematical nature owing to the presence of the second-order time derivative of magnetization that implies the definition of a higher dimensional nonlinear dynamical system. Nevertheless, such equation has remarkable qualitative properties related with conservation of quantities and suitable energy balance
[
dPS24
]
. As a result, for instance in the general spatially-inhomogeneous magnetization case, this equation describes propagation of short wavelength spin-waves (tens of nanometer) with velocities that significantly deviate from the classical behavior due to the onset of an ultimate speed limit in the range of several thousand meters per second
[
dPP+23
]
.

In this paper, we study the iLLG dynamics for a macrospin particle driven by intense external field pulses that are transverse to the initial equilibrium magnetization in order to provide approximate solutions for the temporal dynamics of the magnetization (unit) vector field and give closed-form formulas for switching time and safety tolerance window as function of physical parameters of the nanomagnet including the strength of inertial effects. These results will allow to explain the complex picture arising from numerical simulations of iLLG precessional switching
[
NPS+22
]
where the boundary between successful and unsuccessful switching is extremely and tightly entangled due to a combination of multi-stability and low dissipation that produces the appearance of a quasi-random relaxation process
[
SdBM09
]
.

Figure 1:
Sketch of ultra-fast precessional magnetization switching via ultra-short transverse external field pulses. When the duration
T_{sw}
is below picoseconds, inertial effects set in appearing as THz nutation (thin red line) superimposed to magnetization precession.

The paper is organized as follows: in section
2
, the iLLG equation is introduced including relationship with relevant material, geometrical parameters of the macrospin particle including the strength of inertial effects the influence of which on precessional switching is the focus of this study. Then, by performing appropriate time rescaling and using suitable systems of coordinates (sections
3
,
4
), the analytical approximated solution of magnetization dynamics is developed by using multiple time scale analysis in section
5
. The conditions for realizing successful switching dynamics are addressed in section
6
and, finally, validation of the developed theory with numerical experiments also relevant to experimental investigations is presented in section
7
.

2
Background

We start the analysis by recalling the inertial Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (iLLG) equation written for convenience in dimensionless form
[
dPS24
]
:

(1)

\dot{\bm{m}}=\bm{m}\times(\bm{h}_{eff}-\alpha\dot{\bm{m}}-\eta\ddot{\bm{m}})\mbox{,}

where
\bm{m}
is the magnetization unit-vector (normalized by the saturation magnetization
M_{s}
of the ferromagnet), time is measured in units of
(\gamma M_{s})^{-1}
(
\gamma
is the absolute value of the gyromagnetic ratio),
\alpha
is the Gilbert damping parameter describing dissipative effects (usually
\alpha\sim 10^{-2}
), and the parameter
\eta
measures the intensity of inertial effects (
\eta=(\gamma M_{s}\tau)^{2}
,
\tau
being the time scale of inertial effects, typically fractions of picoseconds
[
NAK+20
]
that yields
\eta\sim\alpha\sim 10^{-2}
).

We denote with

(2)

\bm{h}_{eff}:=\bm{h}_{a}-{\bm{D}}\bm{m}\mbox{,}

the magnetic effective field (also normalized by
M_{s}
) where
\bm{h}_{a}\equiv(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
{\bm{D}}=\diag(D_{1},D_{2},D_{3})
,
D_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(3)

-\infty<D_{1}<D_{2}<D_{3}<+\infty\mbox{.}

Let us recall, see, e.g.
[
MBS09
]
, that the quantity
\Psi(\bm{m}):=|\bm{m}|^{2}
is conserved along the solutions of (
1
), i.e.

d\Psi/dt=\bm{m}\cdot\dot{\bm{m}}=0
,
as
\bm{m}\perp\dot{\bm{m}}
, which is immediately checked from (
1
), so that any solution starting on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
stays on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. A further standard argument consists in the left cross multiplication of (
1
) by
\bm{m}
, then using the standard cross product rules and finally the relation
\bm{m}\cdot\ddot{\bm{m}}=-|\dot{\bm{m}}|^{2}
, in order to write (
1
) as follows,

(4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\bm{m}}&=\bm{v}\\
\dot{\bm{v}}&=-|\bm{v}|^{2}\bm{m}-\eta^{-1}\left[\bm{m}\times\bm{v}+\bm{m}\times(\bm{m}\times\bm{h}_{eff})+\alpha\bm{v}\right]\end{aligned}\right.

with
\bm{z}:=(\bm{m},\bm{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}=:\mathcal{Z}
and for all
\eta\neq 0
. Let us observe that the system of coordinates provided by
\bm{z}
is conceptually different from the one used in
[
dPS24
]
, featuring the variable
\bm{w}:=\bm{m}\times\dot{\bm{m}}
in place of
\bm{v}
. As the former is more naturally related to the phase space structure of the problem (
\bm{v}
is simply the velocity), the latter offers a more appropriate geometric interpretation of it and it turns out to be handier for various purposes, such as the geometric integration of the equations proposed in
[
dPS24
]
.

It is immediate to check from (
4
) that

(5)

\mathcal{W}:=\frac{\eta}{2}|\bm{v}|^{2}+h(\bm{m}),\qquad h(\bm{m}):=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{3}D_{j}m_{j}^{2}-D_{1}\right]-\bm{h}_{a}\cdot\bm{m}\mbox{,}

satisfies

(6)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}=-\alpha|\bm{v}|^{2}\mbox{,}

i.e.
\mathcal{W}
is non-increasing along the solutions of (
4
).

Denoting by
\bm{e}_{j}
the canonical basis unitary vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let us finally recall that, in the absence of applied field, i.e.
\bm{h}_{a}=\bm{0}
, the points

(7)

\bm{z}^{(j),\pm}:=(\pm\bm{e}_{j},\bm{0})\mbox{,}

are equilibria for the system (
4
), as it is straightforwardly checked. In particular, a standard analysis via linearisation shows that the points
\bm{z}^{(1),\pm}
are stable.

For this reason, it is meaningful to consider the following class of motions, also known as
switching

Definition 2.1
.

The system is said to undergo a switching process if there exists
a solution
\bm{z}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
of (
4
) such that

(8)

\bm{z}(0)=\bm{z}^{(1),+}\qquad;\qquad\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\bm{z}(t)=\bm{z}^{(1),-}\mbox{.}

The typical approach to obtaining this class of motions is to apply a suitable field
\bm{h}_{a}
for a certain time interval
[0,T_{sw}]
, where
T_{sw}
is called
time of switching
, then turning it off and wait for the system
relaxation
. Clearly, this requires two main ingredients:
i)
the attractivity of
\bm{z}^{(1),-}
, i.e., the existence of a non-empty basin of attraction, say
\mathcal{S}^{-}\ni\bm{z}^{(1),-}
and
ii)
a field
\bm{h}_{a}
apt to bring the system state in
\mathcal{S}^{-}
after a prefixed time
T_{sw}
. As far as
\bm{h}_{a}
is concerned, we will restrict ourselves to the case of constant applied fields. As it is reasonable to expect, this operation should ensure a “certain robustness” with respect to this instant which, for practical applications, is more properly intended as a window
[T_{sw}-\delta_{sw},T_{sw}+\delta_{sw}]
, commonly termed
safe switching
interval.

3
Time rescaled system

The aim of this section is to choose an “intermediate” system of coordinates in such a way the subsequent transformation in spherical coordinates of the equations could assume a more convenient structure. To this end, we shall introduce (under suitable assumptions) a new time
\tau
and a new system of coordinates
\bm{x}
, both defined below. Roughly speaking, the time
\tau
will allow to “absorb” (or, better, “redirect”) the singularity in
\eta
of the equations in such a way the new vector field has
O(1)
magnitude. This is a known approach in the context of singularly perturbed differential equations, see e.g.
[
Har10
]
. On the other hand, the coordinates
\bm{x}
aim to provide a system with respect to which the switching dynamics takes place as a rotation around the
x_{3}
-axis. Such a system of coordinates will be obtained via a suitably constructed
SO(3)
transformation.

For this purpose, let us introduce the following

Hypothesis 3.1
.

The term
\bm{h}_{a}
shall be supposed to satisfy the following assumptions

1.

(Large magnitude)

(9)

\bm{h}_{a}=:\varepsilon^{-1}\hat{\bm{h}}_{a}\mbox{,}

where
\varepsilon
is a “small” parameter and
\hat{\bm{h}}_{a}
is an object which will be assumed of
O(1)
, i.e. independent on
\varepsilon
.

2.

(“Non-degeneracy”):
h_{j}>0
for some
j=2,3
.

Remark 3.1
.

Hyp
3.1
is justified by the cases of interest in the applications, some mild restrictions will be made in the follow. Experimental studies suggest for
\varepsilon
values of the order
10^{-1}
.

The range for
\varepsilon
described in Rem.
3.1
, compared with the typical values for
\alpha,\eta
of interest, suggests the following

Hypothesis 3.2
.

We shall assume

(10)

\alpha=:\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\alpha},\qquad\eta=:\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\eta}\mbox{,}

where, similarly,
\hat{\alpha},\hat{\eta}=O(1)
.

Remark 3.2
.

It is immediate to realise that the above described choice for
\eta
, suggests that (
4
) should be treated
de facto
as a singularly perturbed system of ODEs, see e.g.
[
Har10
]
. The perturbative analysis of those systems is not elementary but requires some specialised techniques as, for instance, the Multiple Time Scales method we shall employ later on.

By using Hyp.
3.1
, Hyp.
3.2
and the expression (
2
), the iLLG equation (
1
) reads as

(11)

\varepsilon\dot{\bm{m}}=\hat{\bm{h}}_{a}\times\bm{m}+\varepsilon\bm{m}\times{\bm{D}}\bm{m}+\varepsilon^{3}\hat{\alpha}\bm{m}\times\dot{\bm{m}}+\varepsilon^{3}\hat{\eta}\bm{m}\times\ddot{\bm{m}}\mbox{.}

Let us now introduce the following
time rescaling

(12)

t=:\hat{\eta}\varepsilon^{2}\tau\mbox{,}

where
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
is the “new” time we shall use from now on. By using the latter and denoting with
\bm{f}^{\prime}:=d\bm{f}/d\tau
, for any
\bm{f}=\bm{f}(\tau)
, eq. (
11
) becomes

(13)

\bm{m}^{\prime\prime}\times\bm{m}+\bm{m}^{\prime}=\varepsilon\hat{\eta}\hat{\bm{h}}_{a}\times\bm{m}+\varepsilon^{2}\left[\hat{\eta}\hat{\alpha}\bm{m}\times\bm{D}\bm{m}+\hat{\alpha}\bm{m}\times\bm{m}^{\prime}\right]\mbox{.}

It might be useful to notice that the equilibria
\bm{z}^{(j),\pm}
are mapped to the phase space points
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\equiv(\pm\bm{e}_{j},\bm{0})
for (
13
) via the transformation (
12
).

Let us now write

(14)

\hat{\bm{h}}_{a}\times\bm{m}=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{m}\quad;\quad\bm{\Gamma}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\hat{h}_{3}&\hat{h}_{2}\\
\hat{h}_{3}&0&-\hat{h}_{1}\\
-\hat{h}_{2}&\hat{h}_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\mbox{,}

and state the following

Proposition 3.1
.

Define

(15)

\sigma:=\sqrt{\hat{h}_{2}^{2}+\hat{h}_{3}^{2}},\qquad\omega:=|\hat{\bm{h}}_{a}|\mbox{,}

(note that
\sigma,\omega>0
by Hyp.
3.1
). Then, the matrix

(17)

{\bm{\Lambda}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega&0\\

Proof.

The proof is postponed to Appendix A. ∎

Let us now introduce the following coordinates transformation

\bm{m}:={\bm{C}}\bm{x}\mbox{.}

As a consequence, by Prop.

The matrix

{\bm{E}}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}\sigma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{\hat{h}_{1}(D_{2}\hat{h}_{2}^{2}+D_{3}\hat{h}_{3}^{2})}{\sigma^{2}\omega^{2}}&\displaystyle\frac{\hat{h}_{1}\hat{h}_{2}\hat{h}_{3}(D_{3}-D_{2})}{\sigma^{2}\omega}&\displaystyle\frac{\hat{h}_{1}(D_{1}\sigma^{2}-D_{2}\hat{h}_{2}^{2}-D_{3}\hat{h}_{3}^{2})}{\sigma\omega^{2}}\\[8.61108pt]

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{h}_{1}(D_{1}\sigma^{2}-D_{2}\hat{h}_{2}^{2}-D_{3}\hat{h}_{3}^{2})}{\sigma\omega^{2}}&\displaystyle-\frac{\hat{h}_{2}\hat{h}_{3}(D_{3}-D_{2})}{\sigma\omega}&\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}\hat{h}_{1}^{2}+D_{2}\hat{h}_{2}^{2}+D_{3}\hat{h}_{3}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{.}

Remark 3.4
.

As a direct consequence of the fact that

, the function

\mu:=\sqrt{\hat{\alpha}}\varepsilon

\hat{\bm{\Lambda}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\hat{\omega}&0\\

\bm{x}^{\prime\prime}\times\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime}=\mu\hat{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{x}+\mu^{2}\left[\bm{x}\times\hat{{\bm{E}}}\bm{x}+\bm{x}\times\bm{x}^{\prime}\right]\mbox{,}

4
Dynamics in spherical coordinates

As stressed in Rem.
3.4
, the dynamics in the variables
\bm{x}
inherits the property to take place on the unit sphere. For this purpose, let us consider the standard transformation
\mathrm{S}:(r,\theta,\varphi)\rightarrow\bm{x}

(27)

\bm{x}=(r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,r\sin\theta\sin\varphi,r\cos\theta)

and denote with
\hat{e}_{i,j}
the entries of
\hat{{\bm{E}}}
. For all

\mathbb{S}_{\delta}^{2}:=[0,2\pi]\times[\delta,\pi-\delta]\ni(\varphi,\theta)\mbox{,}

then state the following

Proposition 4.1

Set
\mathcal{T}>0

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[(\hat{e}_{1,1}-\hat{e}_{2,2})\cos\varphi\sin\varphi-2\hat{e}_{1,2}\cos^{2}\varphi+\hat{e}_{1,2}-\varphi^{\prime}\right]\sin\theta

\displaystyle+\left[\hat{e}_{1,3}\sin\varphi-\hat{e}_{2,3}\cos\varphi\right]\cos\theta

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left[(\hat{e}_{2,2}-\hat{e}_{1,1})\cos^{2}\varphi-2\hat{e}_{1,2}\cos\varphi\sin\varphi+\hat{e}_{3,3}-\hat{e}_{2,2}\right]\sin\theta\cos\theta

provided that

for all

and some

Proof.

If

\tau

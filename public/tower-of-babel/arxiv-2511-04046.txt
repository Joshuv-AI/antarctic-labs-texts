2.
Preliminaries

We now rigorously define the notations used in the introduction, along with additional symbols needed to present our results.

2.1.
Weights and the associated Sobolev spaces.

Given a set
E\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}(n\geq 2),
we use the notation

\fint_{E}h=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(x)dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad\fint_{E}hdw=\frac{1}{w(E)}\int_{E}f(x)dw=\frac{1}{w(E)}\int_{E}f(x)w(x)dx

for unweighted and weighted averages, respectively. We say that a non-negative locally integrable function
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p},

1<p<\infty,
if

[w]_{p}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}w\right)\left(\fint_{B}w^{1-p^{\prime}}\right)^{p-1}\lesssim 1.

Hereafter, for two positive constants
A,B
, the expression
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a nonessential constant
C,
depending on
n,m
and other parameters that will be clear in the text, such that
A\leq CB.
The notations
A\gtrsim B
and
A\approx B
should be understood similarly. When
p=1,
we say that
w\in A_{1}
if

[w]_{1}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}w\right)\sup_{x\in B}w(x)^{-1}\lesssim 1.

The reverse Hölder classes are defined in the following way:
w\in\mbox{RH}_{q},1<q<\infty,
if

[w]_{\mbox{RH}_{q}}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}w\right)\left(\fint_{B}w^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 1,

in particular,
w\in\mbox{RH}_{\infty}
if

[w]_{\mbox{RH}_{\infty}}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}w\right)^{-1}\sup_{x\in B}w(x)\lesssim 1.

We also need the new weight class
A_{p}(w)
and
\mbox{RH}_{q}(w),
which are defined in
[
23
]
by substituting Lebesgue measure in the above definitions with

e.g.,
v\in A_{p}(w)
if

[w]_{A_{p}(w)}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}vdw\right)\left(\fint_{B}v^{1-p^{\prime}}dw\right)^{p-1}\lesssim 1.

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})
be the collection of bounded holomorphic functions on
\Sigma_{\mu}.
If
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})
satisfies, for some
s>0,

|\phi(z)|\lesssim\frac{|z|^{s}}{(1+|z|)^{2s}}\quad z\in\Sigma_{\mu},

we say that
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu}).
We are able to define
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})
for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})
thanks to the
L^{2}(w)-
functional calculus of
\mathcal{L}_{w}.
Indeed,
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})
has an intergral representation. Let
\mathscr{V}<\theta<\nu<\min\{\mu,\frac{\pi}{2}\},
and let
\Gamma_{\pm},\gamma_{\pm}
be the half-rays
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}e^{\pm i(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta)}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}e^{\pm i\nu},
respectively. Then

(3.5)

\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w}):=\int_{\Gamma_{+}}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}\eta_{+}(z)dz+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}\eta_{-}(z)dz,

where

(3.6)

|\eta_{\pm}(z)|=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{\pm}}e^{\xi z}\phi(\xi)d\xi,\quad z\in\Gamma_{\pm}.

It is easy to see that the integrals in (
3.5
) converge in
L^{2}(w).
According to
[
31
,
34
,
36
]
, any operator
\mathcal{L}_{w}
as above admits a bounded holomorphic functional calculus. That is, given
\mu\in(\mathscr{V},\pi)
:

(a)
for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu}),
the operator
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})
can be defined and is boounded on
L^{2}(w)
with

(3.7)

\|\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)},

where
C
is independent of
\mathscr{V}
and
\mu.

(b)
the product rule
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})\psi(\mathcal{L}_{w})=(\phi\psi)(\mathcal{L}_{w})
holds for any
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu}).

(c)
for any sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu})
converging uniformly on compact subsets of
\Sigma_{\mu}
to
\phi,
we have that
\phi_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
converges to
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})
strongly in
L^{2}(w).

(d)
for any operator
\mathcal{L}_{w}
as above and for any
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu}),
the following square function estimate holds:

(3.8)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\phi(t\mathcal{L}_{w})f\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)},

the same is true for
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{*}.

One can extend the
H^{\infty}
functional calculus to more general holomorphic functions (such as powers), with
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})
defined as unbounded operators.

3.2.
Off-diagonal estimates in
L^{2}(w)

Armed with the
L^{2}(w)-
functional calculus for
\mathcal{L}_{w},
the (full)
L^{2}(w)-
off-diagonal estimates for the complex semigroup
e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}
and its gradients can be proven. Preceding the proof, the following lemma for the resolvent operators are required.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
w\in A_{2}
and
\{a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\}_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\in\mathcal{E}(w,c_{1},c_{2}).
Let
E
and
F
be two closed sets. Fix
\nu
such that
0<\nu<\pi-\mathscr{V}
and
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}.
Then there exist constants
C
and
c
depending only on
n,m,c_{1},c_{2},\nu
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(w)
and
\overrightarrow{f}=(f_{\beta})_{|\beta|=m}
with
f_{\beta}\in L^{2}(w),

\displaystyle(i)\quad\quad\|(1+z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq Ce^{-c\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)},

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\quad\|z^{m}\nabla^{m}(1+z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq Ce^{-c\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)},

\displaystyle(iii)\quad\quad\|z^{m}(1+z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}\frac{1}{w}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\overrightarrow{f}1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq Ce^{-c\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)},

where
\mbox{div}_{m}\overrightarrow{f}:=\sum_{|\beta|=m}\partial^{\beta}f_{\beta}.

Proof.
This proof is a variant of the arguments presented in
[
22
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
21
, Lemma 2.10]
; for additional reference, see also the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 4.2]
.

We first prove
(i)
and
(ii)
. Assume
0<\nu<\frac{\pi}{2};
without loss of generality, take
\nu>\frac{\pi}{4}.
We also assume
\Delta:=\frac{\kappa d(E,F)}{|z|^{1/2m}}\geq 1,
with
\kappa
a sufficiently small constant to be determined subsequently. Through the change of variables
z\to z^{2m},
it suffices to build the following two inequalities:

(3.9)

\int_{F}|(1+z\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}f|^{2}dw\leq Ce^{-c\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{1/2m}}}\int_{E}|f|^{2}dw

and

(3.10)

\int_{F}|z\nabla^{m}(1+z\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}f|^{2}dw\leq Ce^{-c\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{1/2m}}}\int_{E}|f|^{2}dw,

where
f\in L^{2}(w)
is arbitrary and supported in
E.

For simplicity, set
u^{z}=(1+z\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}f,
so that
f=u^{z}+z\mathcal{L}_{w}u^{z}.
By (
3.2
), we have for all
v\in H^{m}(w)
that

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}u^{z}(x)\overline{v(x)}wdx+z\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}u^{z}(x)\cdot\overline{\partial^{\beta}v(x)}dx=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}f(x)\overline{v(x)}wdx.

In the latter equality, we take
v=u^{z}\eta^{2}
with
\eta=e^{\Delta\tilde{\eta}}-1.
(Here
\tilde{\eta}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus E)
is a non-negative function, satisfying
0\leq\tilde{\eta}\leq 1,

\tilde{\eta}\equiv 1
on
F
and
|\partial^{\gamma}\tilde{\eta}|\lesssim d(E,F)^{-|\gamma|}
for any
|\gamma|\leq m.
) Consequently,
it holds that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2}dw

\displaystyle+z\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}(\eta+1))}dx

\displaystyle=z\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\left[\partial^{\beta}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}(\eta+1))}-\partial^{\beta}u^{z}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}\eta^{2})}\right]dx

\displaystyle+\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}|^{2}(2\eta+1)wdx+f(x)\overline{u^{z}\eta^{2}}dw:=G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3}.

To proceed, we split
G_{1}
into

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=z\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\left[\partial^{\beta}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}(\eta+1))}-\partial^{\beta}u^{z}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}(\eta+1)^{2})}\right]dx

\displaystyle-z\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}u^{z}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}(2\eta+1))}:=G_{11}+G_{12},

furthermore, by Leibniz’s rule,

\displaystyle G_{11}

\displaystyle=z\sum_{|\tau|+|\gamma|<2m}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\gamma}u^{z}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u^{z}}\partial^{\beta-\gamma}(\eta+1)\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)dx

\displaystyle-z\sum_{\tau<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}u^{z}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u^{z}}\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)^{2}dx:=G_{111}+G_{112}.

From the definition of
\eta,
a computation leads to that, for any
|\xi|\leq m,

(3.12)

\partial^{\xi}(\eta+1)=(\eta+1)P^{\Delta}_{\xi}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta},

where
P^{\Delta}_{\xi}
denotes a homogeneous polynomial of degree
|\xi|
(
P^{\Delta}_{0}:=1
) satisfying

(3.13)

|P^{\Delta}_{\xi}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}|\lesssim\left(\frac{\Delta}{d}\right)^{|\xi|},\quad(\Delta\geq 1,d:=d(E,F)),

and

(3.14)

\partial^{\xi}u^{z}(\eta+1)=\sum_{\tau\leq\xi}P_{\xi-\tau}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\partial^{\tau}(u^{z}(\eta+1)).

Using (
3.12
)-(
3.14
), an estimate for
G_{111}
can be obtained by disregarding summations and constant factors, as shown below:

(3.15)

\displaystyle G_{111}

\displaystyle=z\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u^{z}}\partial^{\gamma}u^{z}\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)\partial^{\beta-\gamma}(\eta+1)dw

\displaystyle=z\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u^{z}}\partial^{\gamma}u^{z}(\eta+1)^{2}P^{\Delta}_{\alpha-\tau}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}P^{\Delta}_{\beta-\gamma}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}dw

\displaystyle=z\sum_{S\leq\tau}\sum_{\xi\leq\gamma}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}P_{\gamma-\xi}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\partial^{\xi}(u^{z}(\eta+1))

\displaystyle\times P_{\tau-S}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\overline{\partial^{S}(u^{z}(\eta+1))}P^{\Delta}_{\beta-\gamma}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}P^{\Delta}_{\alpha-\tau}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}dw

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2m}\sum_{S\leq\tau}\sum_{\xi\leq\gamma}\left(\frac{\Delta}{d}\right)^{|\alpha-S|+|\beta-\xi|}\|\partial^{\xi}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}\|\partial^{S}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}\quad(\lambda:=|z|^{\frac{1}{2m}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa\sum_{S\leq\tau}\sum_{\xi\leq\gamma}\left(\lambda^{|S|}\|\partial^{S}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}\right)\left(\lambda^{|\xi|}\|\partial^{\xi}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}\right)\quad(|\xi|+|S|\leq 2m-1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa\sum_{S\leq\tau}\sum_{\xi\leq\gamma}C(\xi,S,m)\lambda^{|S|}\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2}w\right)^{\frac{(1-\frac{|S|}{m})}{2}}\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2})w\right)^{\frac{|S|}{2m}}

\displaystyle\times\lambda^{|\xi|}\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2}w\right)^{\frac{(1-\frac{|\xi|}{m})}{2}}\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2})w\right)^{\frac{|\xi|}{2m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa(\|u^{z}(\eta+1)\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}+|z|\|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}).

Similarly,

G_{112}\lesssim\kappa(\|u^{z}(\eta+1)\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}+|z|\|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}).

For
G_{12},
we can apply Young’s inequality to derive

G_{12}\lesssim|z|\|\nabla^{m}u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}+\epsilon|z|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1))|^{2}dw.

By the same token,

G_{3}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}+\epsilon\|u^{z}(\eta+1)\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}+\|u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

Observing that
\|\eta\|_{\infty}\lesssim e^{\Delta},
we can bound
G_{2}
by

G_{2}\lesssim e^{\Delta}\|u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

We now turn to estimating
G_{4},
written as
G_{4}=z\cdot G_{5},
where
G_{5}
is given by:

G_{5}:=z\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\beta}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u^{z}(\eta+1))}dx.

To the end, we introduce

\mathcal{R}:=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2}dw,\;\mathcal{S}:=\mbox{Re}\;G_{5},\;\mathcal{T}:=\mbox{Im}\;G_{5}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;z=s+it.

Apparently, by (
1.2
)-(
1.3
),

\mathcal{S}\geq c_{1}\|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1))\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\mathcal{T}|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\mathcal{S}.

Set
\rho:=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}\tan\nu};
then
\rho<1.
Note also that
|t|\leq s\tan\nu.
a standard argument yields

|\mathcal{R}+G_{4}|=|\mathcal{R}+(s+it)(\mathcal{S}+i\mathcal{T})|\geq\frac{\rho^{1/2}\mathcal{R}}{2}+\frac{|z|\mathcal{S}}{2}.

Thus, recalling (
3.11
) and summarizing all estimates we get

\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2}dw

\displaystyle+|z|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1))|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}(\eta+1)|^{2}dw+(\kappa+\epsilon)|z|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\nabla^{m}(u^{z}(\eta+1))|^{2}dw

\displaystyle+|z|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\nabla^{m}u^{z}|^{2}dw+e^{\Delta}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|u^{z}|^{2}dw,

from which, by letting
\kappa
and
\epsilon
small and also using the property of
\eta,\tilde{\eta},
it follows that

(3.16)

e^{2\Delta}(\|u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(F,w)}^{2}+|z|\|\nabla^{m}u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2})\lesssim|z|\|\nabla^{m}u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(F,w)}^{2}+e^{\Delta}\|u^{z}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

Adapting the proof technique from
[
21
, Lemma 2.8]
(or
[
40
, Lemma 4.1]
), we can prove the uniform bound:

(3.17)

\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\tau}}\left(\|(1+z\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(w)\to L^{2}(w)}+\|z^{1/2}\nabla^{m}(1+z\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(w)\to L^{2}(w)}\right)\leq C,

where
C
depends only on
n,m,c_{1},c_{2},\tau.
Deatils are left to the reader. Inserting (
3.17
) into (
3.16
) we then arrive at (
3.9
) and (
3.10
).

It remains to consider the case
\nu\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi-\mathscr{V}).
Note that there always exist
\nu_{1}<\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\tau<\frac{\pi}{2}-\mathscr{V}
such that every
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
admits a decomposition
z=z_{1}\xi,
where
\xi
is fixed with
|\xi|=1
and
\mbox{arg}(\xi)\leq\tau,
and
z_{1}\in\Sigma_{\nu_{1}}.
Then, substituting
z=z_{1}\xi
into the left side of (
3.9
) and introducing
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1}:=\xi\mathcal{L}_{w}
we have

\int_{F}|(1+z\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}f|^{2}wdx=\int_{F}|(1+z_{1}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1})^{-1}f.

Invoking Lemma
10.1
, we see
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1}\in\mathcal{E}(w,\lambda_{\xi},\Lambda_{\xi}).
Repeating the above procedure for
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1}
yields the desired estimates.

Eventually, (
3.3
) implies that the estimate
(ii)
remains valid if
\mathcal{L}_{w}
is replaced by its adjoint
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{*}.
From this, a duality argument (as in
[
21
, Lemma 2.10]
) leads to conclusion
(iii)
.

\Box

We now elaborate on the proof of the (full) off-diagonal estimates in
L^{2}(w)
for the complex semigroup
e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}
and its gradients.

Theorem 3.2
.

Given
w\in A_{2}
and
\{a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\}_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\in\mathcal{E}(w,c_{1},c_{2}).
For all closed sets
E
and
F,

f\in L^{2}(w),

0\leq k\leq m
and
z\in\Sigma_{\nu}
with
0<\nu<\frac{\pi}{2}-\mathscr{V},
we have:

\displaystyle(i)\quad\quad\|z^{\frac{k}{2m}}\nabla^{k}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim e^{-c\left(\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)},

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\quad\|z\mathcal{L}_{w}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim e^{-c\left(\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

Proof.
Set
d:=d(E,F).
Proving
(i)
and
(ii)
for
d^{2m}\geq|z|
is sufficient. Fix
\theta
with
\frac{\pi}{2}+|\mbox{arg}z|<\theta<\pi-\mathscr{V}
and a parameter
\rho>0
(to be determined later), and define

\Gamma_{\theta}^{\pm}:=\{re^{\pm i\theta}:r\geq\rho\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma_{\theta}:=\{re^{i\phi}:|\phi|\leq\theta\}.

Using the
L^{2}(w)-
functional calculus of
\mathcal{L}_{w}
again, we may express
e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}
through the intergral

e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\theta}^{\pm}\cup\Gamma_{\theta}}e^{z\xi}(\xi+\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}fd\xi.

From this formular, in conjunction with Lemma
3.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{F}\bigg|\int_{\Gamma_{\theta}^{\pm}}e^{z\xi}(\xi+\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})d\xi\bigg|^{2}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\theta}^{\pm}}|e^{z\xi}|\left(\int_{F}|(\xi+\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|d\xi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\theta}^{\pm}}|e^{z\xi}||\xi|^{-1}e^{-cd|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)}|d\xi|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-cd\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}(|z|\rho)^{-1}e^{-c^{\prime}\rho|z|},

moreover,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{F}\bigg|\int_{\Gamma_{\theta}}e^{z\xi}(\xi+\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})d\xi\bigg|^{2}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\rho^{-1}e^{|z|\rho}e^{-cd\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\rho d\phi\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-c^{\prime\prime}d\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}e^{|z|\rho}.

Collecting the above two estimates we get

(3.18)

\left(\int_{F}|e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{E})|^{2}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim e^{-cd\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}(|z|\rho)^{-1}e^{-c^{\prime}\rho|z|}+e^{-c^{\prime\prime}d\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}e^{|z|\rho}.

By (
3.18
), if we let
\rho=\epsilon\frac{d^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}{|z|^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}
with
\epsilon
small enough, then

e^{-cd\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}(|z|\rho)^{-1}e^{-c^{\prime}\rho|z|}+e^{-c^{\prime\prime}d\rho^{\frac{1}{2m}}}e^{|z|\rho}\lesssim e^{-c\left(\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}.

Hence, we conclude with

(3.19)

\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim e^{-c\left(\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

A similar argument leads to

(3.20)

\|z^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{m}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim e^{-c\left(\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}.

Conclusion
(i)
in Theorem
3.2
therefore follows by (
2.3
) and (
3.19
)-(
3.20
).

Observe that

(z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w}(1+z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E}))1_{F}=-(1+z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})1_{F}

since the two sets
E
and
F
are disjoint. Then, by Lemma
3.1
,

\|z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w}(1+z^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w})^{-1}(f1_{E})1_{F}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim e^{-c\frac{d(E,F)}{|z|}}\|f1_{E}\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

The above argument, applied similarly, yields conclusion
(ii)
in Theorem
3.2
.

3.3.
Off-diagonal estimates in
L^{p}(w)

Owing to Theorem
3.2
, Definition
2.6
-
2.7
and Lemma
2.12
, we see that
2\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{w}):=\{p\in[1,\infty]:\sup_{t>0}\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\|_{L^{p}(w)\to L^{p}(w)}\lesssim 1\}
) and
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{2}(w)\to L^{2}(w)).
Then, if
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
has more than one point, it is an interval by interpolation; the next proposition further shows that it actually contains a right triangle (see
[
23
, Figure 1]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists an interval
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\subset[1,\infty]
such that
p,q\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
if and only if
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{p}(w)\to L^{q}(w)).
Furthermore,
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
has the following properties:

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{w});(ii)

\displaystyle\;\mbox{Int}\;\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w})=\mbox{Int}\;\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathcal{L}_{w});(iii)\;p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\leq(2^{*,m}_{w})^{\prime}\;\mbox{and}\;p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\geq 2^{*,m}_{w},

where
p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
and
p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
denote the left and right endpoints of
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w}),
respectively.

Remark 3.4
.

If
w\in A_{1}
(i.e.
r_{w}=1
), we have
p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\leq\frac{2n}{n+2m}
and
p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\geq\frac{2n}{n-2m}.
We refer the reader to
[
1
, Section 8.2]
for more precise control over the endpoints
p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
and
p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
in the case
w\equiv 1
.

Proof.
We first prove that
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{2}(w)\to L^{q}(w))
for any
q
with
2<q<2^{*,m}_{w}.
To the end, we need to show (by Definition
2.6
) that

(3.21)

\left(\fint_{B}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\Upsilon\left(\frac{r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\theta}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.22)

\left(\fint_{B}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{C_{j}(B)})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 2^{j\theta_{1}}\Upsilon\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{m+\theta_{2}}e^{-c\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

(3.23)

\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 2^{j\theta_{1}}\Upsilon\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{m+\theta_{2}}e^{-c\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We start by proving (
3.21
). Let
g:=e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B}).
Then, the left-hand side of (
3.21
) is controlled by

(3.24)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|g-Q_{B}g|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\fint_{B}|Q_{B}g-\pi^{m}_{B}g|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+\left(\fint_{B}|\pi^{m}_{B}g|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

where
Q_{B}g,\pi^{m}_{B}g
are two polynomials of degree at most
m-1,
defined in Theorem
2.2
and Remark
2.3
. Form (
2.8
) and
w\in A_{2}
, it follows that

J_{3}\lesssim\|\pi^{m}_{B}g\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim\fint_{B}|g|dx\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|g|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2};

furthermore, by Theorem
3.2
and Lemma
2.12
, we know

which implies

\left(\fint_{B}|g|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\Upsilon\left(\frac{r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\theta_{2}}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Connecting the two inequalities we reach

J_{3}\lesssim\Upsilon\left(\frac{r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\theta_{2}}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To bound

we apply (
2.6
) along with the property

and Lemma

, as before) to deduce

Here we make the simplifying assumption that both operators

and

share the exponents
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
from Definition
2.6
. By recalling the definition of

we can reduce the estimate of

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+\left(\frac{r(B)}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{m}\right)\Upsilon\left(\frac{r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\theta_{2}}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Upsilon\left(\frac{r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{m+\theta_{2}}\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This proves (
3.21
).

An analogous argument results in (
3.22
) and we leave the details to the reader.

Consider (

) next. For any

the annulus

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B_{k}}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+r(B_{k})^{m}\left(\fint_{B_{k}}|\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{2^{j+1}B\setminus 2^{j-1}B}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+(2^{j}r)^{m}\left(\fint_{2^{j+1}B\setminus 2^{j-1}B}|\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}(f1_{B})|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:=I+II.

4.
The weighted

functional calculus for
\mathcal{L}_{w}

In Section 3.1, we showed that
\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})
is well-defined in

for any

with
\mu\in(\mathscr{V},\pi),

(and even on

L^{p}(w)
-version (and

-version) of (

) to complete the analysis in the subsequent sections.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})<p<p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
and
\mu\in(\mathscr{V},\pi).
There exists a constant
C,
independent of
\phi
and
f,
such that

(4.1)

\|\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu});
that is,
\mathcal{L}_{w}
has a bounded holomorphic functional calculus on
L^{p}(w).
If
v\in A_{\frac{p}{p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})}}(w)\cap\mbox{RH}_{(\frac{p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})}{p})^{\prime}}(w),
we also have

(4.2)

\|\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w})f\|_{L^{p}(vdw)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{p}(vdw)},

with
C
independent of
\phi
and
f.

Remark 4.2
.

Although (
4.1
) is stated for
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu}),
it in fact holds for all
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\mu});
see
[
31
,
36
]
.

Proof.
The proof is quite similar to that in
[
23
, Proposition 4.3]
; however we provide the details for the sake of readability. Hereafter, we simplify the notation by setting
p_{-}:=p_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
and
p_{+}:=p_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w}).

We first show (
4.1
) for any
f\in L_{c}^{\infty}
when
p\in(p_{-},2),
then prove (
4.2
) for
p\in(p_{-},p_{+});
notably, (
4.1
) will be recovered by taking
v\equiv 1.
Without loss of generality, we assume
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
throughout the entire proof.

We will use Theorem
2.18
to prove (
4.1
) when
p\in(p_{-},2).
To the end, fix
p_{0}
with
p_{-}<p_{0}<p<2,
and let
q_{0}=2,

\mathcal{T}=\phi(\mathcal{L}_{w}),
along with the operator

\mathcal{A}_{r}f(x)=(I-(I-e^{-r^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w}})^{N})f(x),

where

is a sufficiently large integer to be chosen later. Note that

\mathcal{A}_{r}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{N}^{k}(-1)^{k+1}e^{-kr^{2m}\mathcal{L}_{w}},

and that for any

and

\Upsilon\left(\frac{s}{k^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)\leq N^{\frac{1}{2m}}\Upsilon(s)\quad\mbox{and}\quad e^{-c\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{(kt)^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\leq e^{-\frac{c}{N^{\frac{1}{2m-1}}}\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{t^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\right)^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}.

As a consequence of Proposition
3.3
,

\mathcal{A}_{r}\in\mho(L^{p}(w)\to L^{q}(w)),\quad\forall\;p_{-}<p\leq q<p_{+}.

We now verify that condition (
2.19
) is satisfied for the operators
T=\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}_{r}
and exponents
p_{0},q_{0}.
For every ball
B
with radius
r,
any
f\in L_{c}^{\infty}
with
{\rm supp}f\subset B
and
j\geq 1,
it is easy to see that

(4.3)

\left(\fint_{B}|\mathcal{A}_{r}(f1_{B})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and for all
j\geq 2,

(4.4)

\left(\fint_{B}|\mathcal{A}_{r}(f1_{C_{j}(B)})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 2^{j\theta_{1}}\Upsilon(2^{j})^{\theta_{2}}e^{-c(2^{j\frac{2m}{2m-1}}})\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}|f|^{p}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and

(4.5)

\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}|\mathcal{A}_{r}(f1_{B})|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 2^{j\theta_{1}}\Upsilon(2^{j})^{\theta_{2}}e^{-c(2^{j\frac{2m}{2m-1}}})\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and any

Apparently, (
4.5
) with

and

implies (
2.19
), where (
2.19

\sum_{j\geq 1}g(j)2^{jD}<\infty\quad(D\;\mbox{is the doubling constant in \eqref{eq: cd66} }).

Next, we seek to build condition (
2.18
). As

is bounded on
\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}},
then

\mathcal{T}(I-\mathcal{A}_{r})f=\int_{\Gamma_{+}}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\eta_{+}(z)dz+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\eta_{-}(z)dz,

where

,

Utilizing the mean value inequality, a straightforward calculation gives

Therefore, for every ball

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}|\mathcal{T}(I-\mathcal{A}_{r})f|^{p_{0}}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}\bigg|\int_{\Gamma_{\pm}}e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\eta_{\pm}(z)dz\bigg|^{p_{0}}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\pm}}\left(\fint_{C_{j}(B)}|e^{-z\mathcal{L}_{w}}f|^{p_{0}}dw\right)^{1/p_{0}}\frac{r^{2mN}}{|z|^{N+1}}|dz|

6.1.
The connection between off-diagonal estimates for

Lemma 6.1
.

Given

The following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(i)\quad e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{p}(w)\to L^{2}(w)).

\displaystyle(ii)\quad t^{1/2}\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{p}(w)\to L^{2}(w)).

\displaystyle(iii)\quad t\mathcal{L}_{w}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{p}(w)\to L^{2}(w)).

Proof.
The proof proceeds similarly to that in
[

, Lemma 7.7]

[

(ii)

(t^{1/2}\nabla^{m}e^{-t/2\mathcal{L}_{w}})\circ e^{-t/2\mathcal{L}_{w}}

Second, we show that

We now prove that

implies

, we are required to construct (

)-(

6.1

6.2.
Basic properties of the interval
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w})

We introduce the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{L}_{w}):=\{p\in[1,\infty]:\sup_{t>0}\|t^{1/2}\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\|_{L^{p}(w)\to L^{p}(w)}<\infty\}.
Like the set

Proposition 6.2
.

There exists an interval

such that if

Moreover,
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
has the following properties:

\displaystyle(i)\quad\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{L}_{w}).

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\mbox{If}\;q_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\;\mbox{and}\;q_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\;\mbox{denote the left and right endpoints of}\;\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w}),\;\mbox{then}\;q_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})=p_{-},

\displaystyle 2\leq q_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\leq(q_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w}))^{*,m}_{w}\leq p_{+},\;2\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\;\mbox{and}\;\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\subset\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{L}_{w}).

\displaystyle(v)\quad\sup\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{L}_{w})=q_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w}).

and

\mathcal{K}_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w}):=\{p\in[2,\infty]:t^{1/2}\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\in\mho(L^{2}(w)\to L^{p}(w))\}.

Clearly, by Proposition
3.3
, Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
2.9
,
\mathcal{K}_{-}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
is an interval, and so is
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w}).
For any

with

by applying Lemma

, Lemma

and Lemma

in place of
[

, Lemma 2.28]
,
[

, Lemma 2.30]
and
[

, Lemma 7.7]
respectively, and following a similar argument to that in

, we can show that

follows immediately from Lemma

6.3.
A key interior point of
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w})

We now prove that
2\in\mbox{Int}\;\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{L}_{w}).
For this purpose, we first recall a reverse Hölder inequality with sharp constants for solutions of
\mathcal{L}_{w},

established in (
10.1
) and (
10.3
). More precisely, for a fixed ball
B_{0},
if
u\in H^{m}(4B_{0},w)
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}_{w}u=0
in
4B_{0},
then

(6.3)

\left(\fint_{B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{r^{m}}\left(\fint_{2B}|u-P_{2B}(u)|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}

holds for any ball
B
such that
3B\subset 4B_{0},
where
C_{1}:=C[w]_{A_{2}}^{m/2}
and
P_{2B}
is as defined in Corollary
2.5
. Since
r_{w}<2,
we can always find a
q
such that

\max\{r_{w},\frac{2n\tau_{w}}{n\tau_{w}+2m}\}<q<2\leq n.

With this choice of
q,
we have
2<q^{*,m}_{w}.
Consequently, by Corollary
2.5
, there exists
q^{\star}\in(r_{w},2)
such that

(6.4)

\frac{1}{r^{m}}\left(\fint_{2B}|u-P_{2B}(u)|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{2}\left(\fint_{2B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
C_{2}:=C[w]^{\frac{1}{q}}_{A_{q^{\star}}}.

Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we get

\left(\fint_{B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{1}C_{2}\left(\fint_{2B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{q}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

From this,
[
15
, Theorem 3.22]
applies, so there exists a
p_{0}>2
such that for every admissible ball
B,

(6.5)

\left(\fint_{B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{p_{0}}dw\right)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C_{3}\left(\fint_{2B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{3}:=8^{1/q}C_{1}C_{2}(2^{D}[w]_{A_{2}})^{31/q}
and

(6.6)

p_{0}:=2+\frac{2-q}{2^{\frac{4}{q}+1}C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{2}(2^{D}[w]_{A_{2}})^{\frac{6}{q}+17}}.

To proceed, as shown in
[
23
, Section 8]
, we need to introduce the Riesz transform
\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}
associated with the higher-order weighted elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{w}.
In fact,
\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}
can be defined by

(6.7)

\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{1/2}\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\frac{dt}{t}.

To verify this definition, we must show that this integral is well-posed, meaning it converges at both
0
and
\infty.
For this aim, for any
\epsilon>0,
we introduce

(6.8)

S_{\epsilon}:=S_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{w}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\epsilon}^{1/\epsilon}t^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}\frac{dt}{t}.

It is easy to see that, for each
0<\epsilon<1,
the function
S_{\epsilon}(z):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\epsilon}^{1/\epsilon}t^{1/2}e^{-tz}\frac{dt}{t}
is holomorphic and uniformly bounded on the right half-plane. From the results in Section 3.1, it follows that

\|S_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{w})f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C\|S_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}

with the constant
C
independent of both
\epsilon
and
f.
Note that, given
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

S_{\epsilon}f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{w})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}),
so

(6.9)

\|\nabla^{m}S_{\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}S_{\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}=\|\phi_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{w})f\|_{L^{2}(w)},

where

\phi_{\epsilon}(z):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\epsilon}^{1/\epsilon}t^{1/2}z^{1/2}e^{-tz}\frac{dt}{t}.

Then, we can deduce that
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}S_{\epsilon}f\to f
strongly in
L^{2}(w),
as
\{\phi_{\epsilon}\}_{0<\epsilon<1}
is uniformly bounded and converges uniformly to 1 on compact subsets of the sector
\Sigma_{\mu}
with
0<\mu<\frac{\pi}{2}.
Combining this and (
6.9
), we see that
\{\nabla^{m}S_{\epsilon}f\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(w)
. Hence, we can define

\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1/2}f=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\nabla^{m}S_{\epsilon}f

with the limit interpreted in
L^{2}(w),
thereby proving (
6.7
). In what follows, when considering
L^{2}(w)

estimates for
\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}
, we actually establish estimates for
\nabla^{m}S_{\epsilon}
with constants independent of
\epsilon.
These arguments are implicit unless details need to be emphasized.

Having established the above, we are able to define the Hodge projection operator by

\mathcal{H}:=\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1/2}(\nabla^{m}(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{*})^{-1/2})^{*},

where adjoints are taken with respect to the
L^{2}(w)
inner product. As the Riesz transform is bounded on
L^{2}(w)
by the
L^{2}(w)-
Kato estimate (
1.4
), the Hodge projection
\mathcal{H}
is also bounded. Moreover,

(6.10)

\mathcal{H}=(-1)^{m}\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1}(w^{-1}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\cdot))

since we have
(\nabla^{m}(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{*})^{-1/2})^{*}=(-1)^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1/2}(w^{-1}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\cdot))
by duality.

Fix a function
\overrightarrow{f}=(f_{\beta})_{|\beta|=m}\in L^{2}(w)\cap L^{p_{0}}(w)
with
{\rm supp}\;\overrightarrow{f}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus 4B_{0},
and let
u:=L_{w}^{-1}(w^{-1}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\overrightarrow{f})).
Then, we can prove
\nabla^{m}u\in L^{2}(w)
using duality arguments and the
L^{2}(w)
boundedness of the Riesz transform. As a consequence of (
6.10
),

\mathcal{H}\overrightarrow{f}=(-1)^{m}\nabla^{m}u

holds in the sense of distributions. Clearly,
L_{w}u=0
on
4B_{0}
since
{\rm supp}\;\overrightarrow{f}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus 4B_{0}.
Indeed, exploiting a standard Lax-Milgram argument (guaranteed by (
1.2
)-(
1.3
)) along with the generalized Poincaré-Sobolev inequality in Theorem
2.2
, we can derive

This allows us to use (
6.5
) to deduce that for any ball
B
such that
3B\subset 4B_{0},

\left(\fint_{B}|\mathcal{H}\overrightarrow{f}|^{p_{0}}dw\right)^{1/p_{0}}=\left(\fint_{B}|\nabla^{m}u|^{p_{0}}dw\right)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C_{3}\left(\fint_{2B}|\mathcal{H}\overrightarrow{f}|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, invoking
[
9
, Theorem 3.14]
in the context of homogeneous spaces (see
[
9
, Section 5]
), we immediately get that
\mathcal{H}:L^{q}(w)\to L^{q}(w)
for all

2\leq q<p_{0}.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{H}^{*}:L^{q^{\prime}}(w)\to L^{q^{\prime}}(w)
for all
q^{\prime},

p_{0}^{\prime}<q^{\prime}\leq 2.

Utilizing the
L^{q^{\prime}}(w)
boundedness of
\mathcal{H}^{*}
just established, we can show that the Reisz transform

is bounded on

for all

Equivalently,

is bounded on

for

as

by Lemma
2.10
. The proof is straightforward. Note that (

) implies
\mathcal{H}^{*}\overrightarrow{f}=(-1)^{m}(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{w})^{-1}(w^{-1}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\cdot))
by duality. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|(\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1/2})^{*}\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(w)}

\displaystyle=\|(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{w})^{-1/2}(w^{-1}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\overrightarrow{f}))\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{w})^{-1}(w^{-1}\mbox{div}_{m}(w\overrightarrow{f}))\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(w)}\approx\|\mathcal{H}^{*}\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(w)}\lesssim\|\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(w)},

We claim that
t^{1/2}\nabla^{m}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}}:L^{q}(w)\to L^{q}(w)

8.
Square function estimates for

and

As an application of the main results established above, we can study the weighted

and

These are defined, respectively, as

More precisely, the goal of this section is to prove the following two propositions.

Proposition 8.1
.

Assume
p_{-}<p<p_{+}
. Then

(8.1)

\|g_{\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\approx\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Conversely, if (

) holds for some

. In other words, the interior of the interval on which (

(8.2)

\|g_{\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\|_{L^{p}(vdw)}\approx\|f\|_{L^{p}(vdw)}

Proposition 8.2
.

Assume

(8.3)

\|G_{\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}

and for any

(8.4)

\|G_{\mathcal{L}_{w}}f\|_{L^{p}(vdw)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(vdw)}.

Central to the proofs of Propositions
8.1
and
8.2
is the following Lemma
8.3
on Hilbert-valued extensions. This requires some notation: let
\mathbb{H}
denote the Hilbert space
L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}),
endowed with the norm

|||f|||=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

Furthermore, given a Borel measure
\mu
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
we define

as the space of
\mathbb{H}
-valued functions with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(w)}:=\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|||f(x,\cdot)|||^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

Lemma 8.3
.

(
[
7
, Lemma 7.4]
)  Let
\mathcal{D}
be a subspace of
\mathcal{G},
the space of measurable functions in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
and let

be two linear operators from

into

Fix

and suppose there exists

\|Tf\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}\leq C_{0}\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\|Sf\|_{L^{p}(F_{j},\mu)},

where the

are measurable subsets of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\alpha_{j}\geq 0.
Then there is an

-valued inequality with the same constant: for all

such that for almost all

\|Tf\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mu)}\leq C_{0}\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\|Sf\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(F_{j},\mu)}.

The extension of a linear operator

-valued functions to

-valued functions is defined by

This means

is treated as a parameter, and

acts only on the spatial variable. We begin with the proof of Proposition
8.1

w\in A_{1}\cap\mbox{RH}_{\max\{p^{\prime},\frac{n}{pm}+1\}};

(ii)

w\in A_{r}\cap\mbox{RH}_{\max\{(\frac{p}{r})^{\prime},\frac{nr}{pm}+1\}}
for some
1<r<\min\{p,1+\frac{pm}{n}\};

(iii)

w=w_{\alpha}(x):=|x|^{\alpha}
with
\max\{-\frac{n}{p},-\frac{pmn}{n+pm}\}<\alpha<pm.

Proof.
By (
5.18
) in Proposition
5.3
, if
r_{w}\leq p_{-},
the proof is identical to that of Theorem
9.1
; otherwise we proceed as in Theorem
9.1
with the choices
p_{0}=r_{w}
and
q_{0}=p^{*,m}_{w}.

\Box

Remark 9.8
.

It is clear that
\max\{(\frac{p}{r})^{\prime},\frac{nr}{pm}+1\}=\frac{nr}{pm}+1
holds if
r\leq p(1-\frac{m}{n}).
Moreover, this condition is guaranteed when
n\geq\frac{2pm}{p-1},
since then
1+\frac{pm}{n}\leq p(1-\frac{m}{n}).
In this case, the conditions in the second part of Theorem
9.7
simplify to those of Theorem
9.1
.

Remark 9.9
.

Tracking carefully the proofs of Theorem
9.1
and Theorem
9.7
, we find that the condition
1\leq r_{w}<1+\frac{pm}{n}
may be relaxed to the potentially weaker condition
1\leq r_{w}<\frac{p_{+}}{p}
by taking
q_{0}=p_{+}
in the argument.

Synthesizing the results of Theorem
9.4
, Theorem
9.7
and Remark
9.9
we conclude with the following unweighted Kato estimate for higher-order degenerate elliptic operators:

Theorem 9.10
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{w}
be as in (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with
w\in A_{2}.
If there exists a
\epsilon>0
small enough such that

(9.9)

|p-2|<\epsilon,\;1\leq r_{w}<\frac{q_{+}(\mathcal{L}_{w})}{p}\;\mbox{and}\;s_{w}>\max\{(\frac{p}{r_{w}})^{\prime},\frac{nr_{w}+pm}{pm},\frac{p}{p-\frac{2nr_{w}}{nr_{w}+2m}}\},

then, for every
f\in H^{m}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),
we have the Kato estimate

(9.10)

\|L_{w}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\approx\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

where the implicit constants depend only on
n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}}
.

In particular, (
9.10
) holds for any
|p-2|<\epsilon
with
\epsilon
sufficiently small, in each of the following scenarios:
(i)

w\in A_{1}\cap\mbox{RH}_{\max\{p^{\prime},\frac{n}{pm}+1,\frac{p}{p-\frac{2n}{n+2m}}\}};

(ii)

Given
\Theta\geq 1
, there exist
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(\Theta,c_{1},c_{2},n,m),

0<\epsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2n},
such that
w\in A_{1+\epsilon_{1}}\cap\mbox{RH}_{\max\{(\frac{p}{(1+\epsilon_{1})})^{\prime},\frac{n(1+\epsilon_{1})}{pm}+1,\frac{p}{p-\frac{2n(1+\epsilon_{1})}{n(1+\epsilon_{1})+2m}}\}},

0<\epsilon_{1}<\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}}\leq\Theta.

Finally, there exists a
\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon_{2}(n,m,c_{1},c_{2})\in(0,\;\frac{1}{2n})
such that for
p
near 2, (
9.10
) holds for
w_{\alpha}=|x|^{\alpha}
whenever the exponent
\alpha
satisfies

\max\{-\frac{n}{p},-\frac{pmn}{n+pm},-\frac{n(p-\frac{2n}{n+2m})}{p}\}<\alpha<\epsilon_{2}.

Remark 9.11
.

In particular, for the power weight
w_{-\gamma}:=|x|^{-\gamma}
with
-\epsilon_{2}<\gamma<\frac{2mn}{n+2m},
Theorem
9.10
gives

\|\mathcal{L}_{w_{-\gamma}}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\approx\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

where
\mathcal{L}_{w_{-\gamma}}
is defined by (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). When
\gamma=0,
we recover the classical Kato square root problem for higher-order elliptic operators, which was settled in
[
5
]
.

To conclude this section, we address the solvability of the regularity problem (
1.8
) on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},
using Theorem
9.10
.

Theorem 9.12
.

Let
w\in A_{2},
with
p,r_{w},s_{w}
satisfying the requirements of Theorem
9.10
. Then, for any
f\in H^{m,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

u(x,t)
(defined as in Theorem
9.3
) is a solution to the regularity problem (
1.8
) with
\nabla^{l}u(\cdot,t)\to\nabla^{l}f
strongly in
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
as
t\to 0^{+}
for all
0\leq l\leq m-1
. Furthermore, for all
k\geq 1
and
0\leq l\leq m,

(9.11)

\sup_{t>0}\left(\|t^{k-1}\partial_{t}^{k}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla^{l}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\right)\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{m,p}}.

Proof.
As established in the first part of Theorem
9.3
, we see that
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}}f\to f
strongly in
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
as
t\to 0^{+}
. From this, along with Theorem
9.10
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1/2}(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}}f)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{p}}.

Similarly, for all
k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|t^{k-1}\partial_{t}^{k}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|(t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2})^{k-1}\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2})^{k-1}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}}(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}f)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}_{w}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{p}}.

Recall from Remark
9.9
that (
9.2
) also holds under the hypotheses of Theorem
9.12
.
Thus,

(9.12)

\sup_{t>0}\|t^{k}\nabla^{m}\partial^{k-1}_{t}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}},\quad\forall\;k\geq 1.

Furthermore, interpolation gives for all
0\leq l\leq m:

\sup_{t>0}\|\nabla^{l}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\|\nabla^{m}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{l}{m}}\cdot\sup_{t>0}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}^{1-\frac{l}{m}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{p}}.

We therefore obtain that for all
0\leq l\leq m-1,

\nabla^{l}u(\cdot,t)
converges to
\nabla^{l}f
strongly in
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
as
t\to 0^{+}
when
f\in H^{m,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

\Box

The first two lemmas, while auxiliary, are crucial to the core argument. The first of these generalizes
[

, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Assume that
\{a_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\in\mathcal{E}(w,c_{1},c_{2}).
Then,
\{za_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\in\mathcal{E}(w,\lambda_{z},\Lambda_{z})
for any
z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\mathscr{V}},
where
\mathscr{V}
is given by (
3.4
).

Proof.
Fix
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{w}),
and define

\mathcal{S}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}f(x)\overline{\partial^{\beta}f(x)}dx=<\mathcal{L}_{w}f,f>.

Its imaginary and real parts are denoted by
\mathcal{R}:=\mbox{Im}\;\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}:=\mbox{Re}\;\mathcal{S},
respectively. Using the definition of
\mathscr{V}
and (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\;(z\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=|z|(\cos(\mbox{arg}z)\mathcal{T}-\sin(\mbox{arg}z)\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle=|z|\mathcal{T}(\cos(\mbox{arg}z)-\sin(\mbox{arg}z)\frac{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}|z|\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}(\cos(\mbox{arg}z)-|\sin(\mbox{arg}z)|\tan\mathscr{V}).

Consequently, since
|\sin(\mbox{arg}z)|<\frac{\cos(\mbox{arg}z)}{\tan\mathscr{V}}
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{w})
is dense in
H^{m}(w),
the identity
\lambda_{z}=c_{1}|z|(\cos(\mbox{arg}z)-|\sin(\mbox{arg}z)|\tan\mathscr{V})
is valid. On the other hand, (
1.3
) implies

\bigg|\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}za_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\xi_{\alpha}\overline{\zeta_{\beta}}\bigg|\leq c_{2}|z||\xi||\zeta|w(x),

which immediately gives
\Lambda_{z}=c_{2}|z|.

\Box

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
s>0
,
\alpha\geq 0,
and
\beta>0
with
\alpha\neq\beta.
Then, for any
0<c^{\prime}<c,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k\alpha}\Upsilon(2^{k}s)^{\beta}e^{-cs^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}\lesssim\Upsilon(s)^{\max\{\alpha,\beta\}}e^{-c^{\prime}s^{\frac{2m}{2m-1}}}.

Proof.
In light of
[
6
, Lemma 6.3]
, the proof is routine, and we skip it.

\Box

We now present a detailed proof of the reverse Hölder inequality with sharp constants for solutions to
\mathcal{L}_{w},
a result referenced in Section 6.3.

Lemma 10.3
.

Fix
B_{0}:=B(x_{0},R),
and suppose
w\in A_{2}.
Consider any solution
u\in H^{m}(B_{0},w)
to
\mathcal{L}_{w}u=0
in
B_{0}.
Then, for any
0<r<R,
we have

(10.1)

\int_{B(x_{0},r)}|\nabla^{m}u|^{2}dw\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{C}{(R-r)^{2m-2k}}\int_{B_{0}\setminus B(x_{0},r)}|\nabla^{k}(u-P_{B_{0}}(u))|^{2}dw,

where the constant
C
depends only on
c_{1},c_{2},m,n.

Proof.
Let
\phi
be a smooth, nonnegative, real-valued test function supported in
B_{0},
identically 1 on
B(x_{0},r),
and satisfying
|\nabla^{k}\phi|\leq C_{k}(R-r)^{-k}
for any
0\leq k\leq m.
Testing the equation
\mathcal{L}_{w}u=0
in
B_{0}
against the function
\psi:=\phi^{4m}\widetilde{u},
where
\widetilde{u}=(u-P_{B_{0}}(u)),
yields

\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(x)\cdot\overline{\partial^{\beta}\psi(x)}dx=0.

From this, along with the product rule, it holds that

-\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(x)\sum_{\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi^{2m}\partial^{\gamma}(\phi^{2m}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)})dx=\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\phi^{2m}\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(x)\partial^{\beta}(\phi^{2m}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)})dx.

Note from
[
13
, Lemma 3.8]
that there exists functions
\Phi_{\beta,\xi}
supported in
B_{0}\setminus B(x_{0},r)
with
|\Phi_{\beta,\xi}|\leq C(R-r)^{|\xi|-|\beta|}
such that
we may write

\sum_{\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\phi^{2m}\partial^{\gamma}(\phi^{2m}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)})=\sum_{\xi<\beta}\phi^{2m}\Phi_{\beta,\xi}\partial^{\xi}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}(\phi^{2m}\widetilde{u}(x))\partial^{\beta}(\phi^{2m}\overline{\tilde{u}(x)})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\sum_{\gamma<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\gamma}\partial^{\alpha-\gamma}\phi^{2m}\partial^{\gamma}\widetilde{u}(x)\partial^{\beta}(\phi^{2m}\overline{\tilde{u}(x)})dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(x)\sum_{\xi<\beta}\phi^{2m}\Phi_{\beta,\xi}\partial^{\xi}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\sum_{\gamma<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\gamma}\partial^{\alpha-\gamma}\phi^{2m}\partial^{\gamma}\widetilde{u}(x)\partial^{\beta}(\phi^{2m}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)})dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{0}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\partial^{\alpha}(\widetilde{u}(x)\phi^{2m})\sum_{\xi<\beta}\Phi_{\beta,\xi}\partial^{\xi}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)}dx

\displaystyle-\sum_{\gamma<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\gamma}(\partial^{\alpha-\gamma}\phi^{2m}\partial^{\gamma}\widetilde{u}(x))\sum_{\xi<\beta}\Phi_{\beta,\xi}\partial^{\xi}\overline{\widetilde{u}(x)}dx

Invoking (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) and applying Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{B_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(\phi^{2m}\widetilde{u}(x))|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{B_{0}}|\nabla^{m}(\phi^{2m}\widetilde{u}(x))|^{2}dw

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{C}{(R-r)^{2m-2k}}\int_{B_{0}\setminus B(x_{0},r)}|\nabla^{k}(\widetilde{u}(x))|^{2}dw,

where
C
depends only on
c_{1},m,n,c_{2}.
This yields (
10.1
).

\Box

The bound on the right-hand side of (
10.1
) can be improved to depend solely on
\|u\|_{L^{2}(w)}.
To achieve this, we adapt the approach from
[
13
, Theorem 3.10]
.

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
B_{0}:=B(x_{0},R)
with
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
R>0.
Given
w\in A_{2},
assume that
\tilde{u}\in H^{m}(B_{0},w)
satisfies for any
0<\rho<r<R,

(10.2)

\int_{B(x_{0},\rho)}|\nabla^{m}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{C}{(r-\rho)^{2m-2k}}\int_{B(x_{0},r)\setminus B(x_{0},\rho)}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw.

Then
\widetilde{u}
satisfies the following improved estimates:

(10.3)

\int_{B(x_{0},r)}|\nabla^{m}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq\frac{C[w]^{m}_{A_{2}}}{(R-r)^{2m}}\int_{B(x_{0},R)\setminus B(x_{0},r)}|\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw

and, for any
0\leq j\leq m-1,

(10.4)

\int_{B(x_{0},r)}|\nabla^{j}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq\frac{C[w]^{j(m+1-j)}_{A_{2}}}{(R-r)^{2j}}\int_{B(x_{0},R)}|\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw.

Here, the constant
C
depends only on
c_{1},m,n,c_{2}.

Proof.
Let
A(r,\xi)
(with
\xi>0
) denote the annulus
B(x_{0},r+\xi)\setminus B(x_{0},r-\xi)
for the proof of (
10.3
), and the ball
B(x_{0},r+\xi)
for that of (
10.4
), respectively.

To prove (
10.3
)-(
10.4
), it suffices to establish the estimate

(10.5)

\int_{A(r,\xi)}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{C_{k}}{(\eta-\xi)^{2k-2j}}\int_{A(r,\eta)}|\nabla^{j}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw

for all
1\leq k\leq m,

R/2<r<R
and
0<\xi<\min\{R-r,r\}.
Indeed, from (
10.2
) and (
10.5
), (
10.3
) follows immediately. For
k=m,
the inequality (
10.5
) is precisely (
10.2
). Hence, we only need to prove that if (
10.5
) holds for some
k+1<m,
then it also holds for
k.

Consider a sequence
\{\rho_{j}\}
satisfying
\xi:=\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}<...<\eta,
which will be fixed momentarily. For this sequence, we set

A_{j}=A(r,\rho_{j}),

\delta_{j}:=\rho_{j+1}-\rho_{j},
and
\widetilde{A}_{j}:=A(r,\rho_{j}+\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}).
Thus
A_{j}\subset\widetilde{A}_{j}\subset A_{j+1}.
We also choose a nonnegative, smooth function
\phi_{j},
supported in
\widetilde{A}_{j}
and identically 1 on
A_{j},
satisfying
\|\nabla\phi_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{j}}
and
\|\nabla^{2}\phi_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{j}^{2}}
for some absolute constant
C.
Clearly, for all
j\geq 0,

\int_{A_{j}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq\int_{\tilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla(\phi_{j}\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u})|^{2}dw.

The following key interpolation inequality was proved in
[
32
]
: for all
f\in H^{2}(w),

(10.6)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\leq C[w]_{A_{2}}\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{2}(w)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

By (
10.6
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq C[w]_{A_{2}}^{1/2}\left(\int_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla^{2}(\phi_{j}\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u})|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}|\phi_{j}\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C[w]_{A_{2}}^{1/2}\left(\int_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla^{k+1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{2}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{4}}|\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

An application of (
10.5
) to control
|\nabla^{k+1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}
leads to

\int_{A_{j}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq C[w]_{A_{2}}^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{C_{k}}{\delta_{j}^{2k+2-2i}}\int_{A_{j+1}}|\nabla^{i}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This, by Young’s inequality, further implies

\int_{A_{j}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{2k-2i}}\int_{A_{j+1}}|\nabla^{i}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw+\frac{C_{k}[w]_{A_{2}}}{\delta_{j}^{2}}\int_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}|\nabla^{k-1}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw.

We separate the term for
i=k
from the sum. This, together with
[w]_{A_{2}}\geq 1,
yields that

\int_{A_{j}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq C_{k}[w]_{A_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{2k-2i}}\int_{A_{j+1}}|\nabla^{i}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw+\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{j+1}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw.

Then, using an iteration argument, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{A_{0}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)}\left(C_{k}[w]_{A_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{2k-2i}}\int_{A_{j+1}}|\nabla^{i}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}[w]_{A_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)}\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{2k-2i}}\right)\int_{A_{\infty}}|\nabla^{i}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw.

Let
0<\tau<1,
and set
\rho_{0}=\xi
with

\rho_{j}:=\xi+(\eta-\xi)(1-\tau)\sum_{i=1}^{j}\tau^{i}\quad\mbox{for}\;j\geq 1.

Then
\lim_{j\to\infty}\rho_{j}=\eta.
We therefore obtain

\int_{A_{0}}|\nabla^{k}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw\leq C_{k,\tau}[w]_{A_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\tau^{2k-2i})^{j}}\frac{1}{(\eta-\xi)^{2k-2i}}\right)\int_{A_{\infty}}|\nabla^{i}\widetilde{u}|^{2}dw.

Choosing
\tau
such that
2\tau^{2k}>1
and
\tau<1
proves (
10.5
). In particular, (
10.4
) is a direct consequence of (
10.3
) and (
10.6
).

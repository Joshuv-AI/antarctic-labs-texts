Brown measures of deformed L ∞ L^{\infty} -valued circular elements
Johannes Alt, Torben Krüger

Abstract
We consider the Brown measure of a + 𝔠 a+\mathfrak{c} , where a a lies in a commutative tracial von Neumann algebra ℬ \mathcal{B} and 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} is a ℬ \mathcal{B} -valued circular element. Under certain regularity conditions on a a and the covariance of 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} this Brown measure has a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the complex plane which is real analytic apart from jump discontinuities at the boundary of its support. With the exception of finitely many singularities this one-dimensional spectral edge is real analytic. We provide a full description of all possible edge singularities as well as all points in the interior, where the density vanishes. The edge singularities are classified in terms of their local edge shape while internal zeros of the density are classified in terms of the shape of the density locally around these points. We also show that each of these countably infinitely many singularity types occurs for an appropriate choice of a a when 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} is a standard circular element. The Brown measure of a + 𝔠 a+\mathfrak{c} arises as the empirical spectral distribution of a diagonally deformed non-Hermitian random matrix with independent entries when its dimension tends to infinity.

To classify the support of

Theorem 2.2

(Properties of
\sigma
)
.

Let

\mathfrak{c}

-valued circular element such that

from (

(i)

(ii)

and admits a real analytic extension to an open neighbourhood of

.

(iii)

(iv)

(v)

Definition 2.3

(Regular edge and singular points)

To give a short statement of the classification of the singular points for

Definition 2.4

(Singularity types)
.

Let

be a real analytic function on an open set

and
\zeta_{0}\in U

is of singularity type

at any regular edge point.
By Theorem
2.2
the edge

Theorem 2.5

(Classification of edge and internal singularities)
.

Let

and

The set of singular points for

Brown measure, Hermitization and Dyson equation

In this section, we derive the Dyson equation for the Cauchy transform of the Hermitization of the deformed operator-valued circular element
a+\mathfrak{c}
. This Hermitization itself is a family of operator-valued semicircular
elements indexed by the spectral parameter
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
and the associated Dyson equation is our main tool for analyzing the Brown measure of
a+\mathfrak{c}
.

Throughout the following, let
(\mathcal{A},E,\mathcal{B})
be an operator-valued probability space with positive conditional expectation
E\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
such that
(\mathcal{A},\langle E[\,\cdot\,]\rangle)
is a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space.
We assume that
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
is a commutative von Neumann subalgebra with trace
\langle\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}\rangle
containing the unit of
\mathcal{A}
.
We will also work on the operator-valued probability space
(\mathcal{A}^{2\times 2},\mathrm{id}\otimes E,\mathcal{B}^{2\times 2})
, for which we employ the identifications
\mathcal{A}^{2\times 2}=\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}\otimes\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}^{2\times 2}=\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}\otimes\mathcal{B}
.

For any
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{A}
,
a\in\mathcal{B}
and

Here,

with the respective value.
Note that the inverse is well defined as

For the next lemma, we define
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{B}^{2\times 2}\to\mathcal{B}^{2\times 2}

\Sigma\bigg[\begin{pmatrix}r_{11}&r_{12}\\

for all

,
r_{12}
,

,

, where

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{A}
be a
\mathcal{B}
-valued circular element satisfying (

) and (

Then

,

.

Under the constraint that

.
In particular,

from (

Proof.

An easy computation using the properties of the operator-valued circular element

, in particular,

) and (

),

is an

-valued semicircular element, see e.g.
[

.

Owing to (

) and (
2.2
),
the covariance of
\mathfrak{s}
satisfies

for all

with
\Sigma
from (

).
Since the

[

as defined in (

) with

from (

) by

∎

We now express the Brown measure of

Indeed, for the proof of the last identity,

In this section we analyse the solution

to the Dyson equation (

). We begin by establishing bounds on the solution. Then we determine stability and regularity properties. Finally we use these insights to characterise the set
\mathbb{S}
from (

) and expand

and
v_{2}
around edge points
\zeta_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{S}
.
We start with some useful identities and a priori estimates.

As
S
and
S^{*}
both act on
\mathcal{B}=L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{X},\mu)
as integral operators in the form (
2.5
), the identity (
4.6
) implies
\langle v_{2}S^{*}v_{1}\rangle=\langle v_{1}Sv_{2}\rangle
and thus

\langle v_{1}\rangle=\langle v_{2}\rangle\,.

(5.1)

We conclude from (

) and (

) that

v_{1}\leq\eta^{-1},\qquad v_{2}\leq\eta^{-1},\qquad\lvert y\rvert\leq\lvert a-\zeta\rvert\eta^{-2}

for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\eta>0
. Furthermore, we have the identity

y=\frac{v_{1}(\bar{a}-\bar{\zeta})}{\eta+S^{*}v_{1}}=\frac{1}{a-\zeta}(1-v_{1}(\eta+Sv_{2}))=\frac{1}{a-\zeta}-\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{\overline{y}\!\,}

(5.3)

for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(D_{a})
.
Here,

denotes the spectrum of

considered as multiplication operator
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
, which coincides with the essential range of

.

Throughout the remainder of this section, we assume that
s
satisfies
A1
. This implies

\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}Sw\sim\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{K}z_{ij}\langle w\mathbbm{1}_{I_{j}}\rangle\,,\qquad\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}S^{*}w\sim\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{K}z_{ji}\langle w\mathbbm{1}_{I_{j}}\rangle

(5.4)

for all

with

and all

.

5.1
Bounds on the solution

This subsection contains bounds on

and

under varying assumptions on

and

.

Lower bound on
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

We start with some preliminary bounds that require

and the boundedness of the function
s
.

Lemma 5.1

(Preliminary bounds on
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
)
.

If for some constant
C>0
,
s(x,y)\leq C
for all
x
,
y\in\mathfrak{X}
then

\frac{\eta}{\eta^{2}+1+\lvert\zeta\rvert^{2}}\lesssim_{C}v_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\eta}

(5.5)

uniformly for all
i=1
, 2,
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\eta>0
.

Proof.

The positivity of
v_{i}
and
\eta
in (
4.4
) imply
1/v_{i}\geq\eta
. Hence, the upper bounds
in (
5.5
) follow.

As
s(x,y)\leq C
for all
x
,
y\in\mathfrak{X}
, we conclude from (
4.4
)
and the upper bound
v_{2}\leq\eta^{-1}
that

\frac{1}{v_{1}}\leq\eta+C\eta^{-1}+\eta^{-1}2(\lVert a\rVert_{\infty}^{2}+\lvert\zeta\rvert^{2}),

which implies the missing lower bound in (
5.5
).
∎

We continue by showing that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are bounded from below by their average.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
s
satisfies
A1
then

v_{1}\gtrsim\langle v_{1}\rangle\,,\qquad v_{2}\gtrsim\langle v_{2}\rangle

(5.6)

uniformly for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\eta>0
. As a consequence
S^{*}
and
S
act averaging on
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively, i.e.

S^{*}v_{1}\sim\langle v_{1}\rangle\,,\qquad Sv_{2}\sim\langle v_{2}\rangle\,.

(5.7)

The following proof is based on identifying
(v_{1},v_{2})
as solution to a variational problem.
In a similar context, such strategy was also used in
[
2
, Section 6.2 and Appendix A.4]

and
[
4
, Lemmas 3.11 and 3.17]
.

Proof.

For the proof we rephrase (
4.4
) as a variational problem. This will help us to make use of the assumption
A1
.
For
\eta>0
, we define
J_{\eta}\colon\mathcal{B}_{+}\times\mathcal{B}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, a functional which is minimised by
(v_{1},v_{2})
, by

J_{\eta}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\langle x_{1}{S}x_{2}\rangle+\eta\langle x_{1}+x_{2}\rangle-\big\langle({1-u_{\eta}})\log x_{1}\big\rangle-\big\langle({1-u_{\eta}})\log x_{2}\big\rangle\,,

where

u_{\eta}:=\frac{|a-\zeta|^{2}\mspace{1mu}v_{1}}{\eta+S^{*}v_{1}}=\frac{|a-\zeta|^{2}\mspace{1mu}v_{2}}{\eta+Sv_{2}}\,

by (
4.6
).
Note that
1-u_{\eta}=v_{1}(\eta+Sv_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
by (
4.4
).

Next, we prove that
(x_{1},x_{2})=(v_{1}(\eta),v_{2}(\eta))
is a minimiser for
J_{\eta}
. Indeed, the functional
J_{\eta}
satisfies the lower bounds

\begin{split}J_{\eta}(x_{1},x_{2})&\geq\langle\eta x_{1}-\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty}\mathbbm{1}(x_{1}\geq 1)\log x_{1}\rangle+\langle\eta x_{2}-\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty}\mathbbm{1}(x_{2}\geq 1)\log x_{2}\rangle\\
&\qquad-(1-\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty})(\langle\log x_{1}\rangle+\langle\log x_{2}\rangle)\\
&\geq\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty}\beta_{1}\biggl({\eta\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}-\log\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}}\biggr)+(1-\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty})(\eta\langle x_{1}\rangle-\langle\log x_{1}\rangle)\\
&\qquad+\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty}\beta_{2}\biggl({\eta\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}-\log\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}}\biggr)+(1-\lVert u_{\eta}\rVert_{\infty})(\eta\langle x_{2}\rangle-\langle\log x_{2}\rangle)\,,\end{split}

with
\alpha_{i}:=\langle x_{i}\mathbbm{1}(x_{i}\geq 1)\rangle
and
\beta_{i}:=\langle\mathbbm{1}(x_{i}\geq 1)\rangle
.
Here, in the second step, we applied Jensen’s inequality with the expectation
f\mapsto\beta_{i}^{-1}\langle\mathbbm{1}(x_{i}\geq 1)f\rangle
, i.e.
\langle\mathbbm{1}(x_{i}\geq 1)\log x_{i}\rangle\leq\beta_{i}\log\alpha_{i}/\beta_{i}
.
Since
\langle\log x_{i}\rangle\leq\log\langle x_{i}\rangle
by Jensen’s inequality, the functional
J_{\eta}
is bounded from below for any
\eta>0
.
Therefore,
\iota_{\eta}=\inf_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{+}\times\mathcal{B}_{+}}J_{\eta}(x_{1},x_{2})>-\infty
.
Moreover,
(v_{1},v_{2})
satisfies the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations and
J_{\eta}
is strictly convex
due to
1-u_{\eta}=v_{1}(\eta+Sv_{2})
and (
5.5
).
Hence,
J_{\eta}(v_{1},v_{2})=\iota_{\eta}
.

In the regime
\eta\gg 1
, that is there is a constant
\eta_{0}>0
depending only on the model parameters such
that for all
\eta\geq\eta_{0}
, we deduce from (
4.4
) and the bound
v_{i}\leq 1/\eta
in Lemma
5.1
that

v_{i}\sim\frac{\eta}{|\zeta|^{2}+\eta^{2}},

which implies
v_{i}\sim\langle v_{i}\rangle
.
Thus, we restrict to
\eta\lesssim 1
. In case
\eta\lesssim 1
and
|\zeta|\gg 1
we get

v_{i}\sim\frac{\eta}{|\zeta|^{2}},

and therefore
v_{i}\sim\langle v_{i}\rangle
.
Hence, we restrict to
\eta\lesssim 1
and
|\zeta|\lesssim 1
in the following.
As
(v_{1},v_{2})
is a minimiser of
J_{\eta}
, the value
J_{\eta}(v_{1},v_{2})
is bounded by

J_{\eta}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq J_{\eta}(1,1)=2\eta+\langle{S}1\rangle\,.

With the notations
c_{i}:=\mu(I_{i})
,
\langle x\rangle_{i}:=\frac{1}{c_{i}}\langle x\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}\rangle
and
z_{ij}:=\mathbbm{1}(s|_{I_{i}\times I_{j}}\neq 0)
, we see by Jensen’s inequality that

\begin{split}1&\gtrsim J_{\eta}(v_{1},v_{2})\\
&=\langle v_{1}{S}v_{2}\rangle-\sum_{j}c_{j}\big\langle({1-u})\log v_{1}\big\rangle_{j}-\sum_{j}c_{j}\big\langle({1-u})\log v_{2}\big\rangle_{j}+\eta\langle v_{1}+v_{2}\rangle\\
&\geq c\mspace{1mu}\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}\langle v_{1}\rangle_{i}\langle v_{2}\rangle_{j}-\sum_{j}c_{j}\langle 1-u\rangle_{j}\log\frac{\langle(1-u)v_{1}\rangle_{j}\langle(1-u)v_{2}\rangle_{j}}{\langle 1-u\rangle_{j}^{2}}\\
&\geq c\mspace{1mu}\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}\langle v_{1}\rangle_{i}\langle v_{2}\rangle_{j}-\sum_{j}c_{j}\langle 1-u\rangle_{j}\log\bigl({\langle v_{1}\rangle_{j}\langle v_{2}\rangle_{j}}\bigr)+2\sum_{j}c_{j}\langle 1-u\rangle_{j}\log\langle 1-u\rangle_{j}\\
&\geq\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}(\chi_{j})-\frac{2}{\mathrm{e}}\sum_{j}c_{j}\,,\end{split}

where in the last step we used
z_{jj}=1
for all
j\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
and defined

\chi_{j}:=\langle v_{1}\rangle_{j}\langle v_{2}\rangle_{j}\,,\qquad\varphi_{j}(\chi):=c\mspace{1mu}\chi-c_{j}\mspace{1mu}\mathbbm{1}(\chi\leq 1)(1-\langle u\rangle_{j})\log\chi-\mathbbm{1}(\chi>1)\log\chi\,

for all
j\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
and
\chi\in(0,\infty)
.
For all
j\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
, we infer
\chi_{j}\lesssim 1
and

\begin{split}\chi_{j}\geq\exp\biggl({-\frac{C}{1-\langle u\rangle_{j}}}\biggr)=\exp\biggl({-\frac{C}{\langle v_{1}(\eta+Sv_{2})\rangle_{j}}}\biggr)\end{split}

(5.8)

for some positive constant
C\sim 1
.
We rewrite the Dyson equation (
4.4
) in the form

\begin{split}v_{1}=\frac{\eta+S^{*}v_{1}}{|a-\zeta|^{2}+(\eta+S^{*}v_{1})(\eta+Sv_{2})}\,,\qquad v_{2}=\frac{\eta+Sv_{2}}{|a-\zeta|^{2}+(\eta+S^{*}v_{1})(\eta+Sv_{2})}\,.\end{split}

(5.9)

Now we set
x:=(\eta+S^{*}v_{1})(\eta+Sv_{2})
. In particular, we have

\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}x\sim\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}\biggl({\eta^{2}+\sum_{j,k}z_{ij}z_{ki}\langle v_{1}\rangle_{k}\langle v_{2}\rangle_{j}}\biggr)\sim\mathbbm{1}_{I_{i}}\langle x\rangle_{i}\,

(5.10)

due to (
5.4
).
Suppose now that
\langle x\rangle_{i}\gg 1
for an
i\in\llbracket K\rrbracket
. Then by taking the
\langle\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}\rangle_{i}
-average of (
5.9
), multiplying the two equations and using (
5.4
) and (
5.10
) as
well as
\langle x\rangle_{i}\gg 1
we get

\chi_{i}\sim\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle_{i}}\ll 1\,.

On the other hand by multiplying the first equation in (
5.9
) with
\eta+Sv_{2}
we find

\langle v_{1}(\eta+Sv_{2})\rangle_{i}\sim 1\,

as
\langle x\rangle_{i}\gg 1
.
This contradicts (
5.8
) and
\chi_{i}\ll 1
and we conclude
\langle x\rangle_{i}\lesssim 1
for all

.
With (
5.9
) and (

), this implies

v_{1}\gtrsim S^{*}v_{1}\,,\qquad v_{2}\gtrsim Sv_{2}\,.

Iterating these inequalities yields

and
v_{2}\gtrsim\langle v_{2}\rangle
,
i.e. (

), since
(z_{ij})_{i,j}
is primitive by

. Since

for all

, we have
S1\gtrsim 1
and
S^{*}1\gtrsim 1
. This implies
Sv_{2}\gtrsim\langle v_{2}\rangle
and
S^{*}v_{1}\gtrsim\langle v_{1}\rangle
, respectively. The upper bounds on
Sv_{2}
and
S^{*}v_{1}
in (

) follow from the upper bound on

in
A1
.
∎

Bound in
L^{2}
-norm:

Now we show a bound with respect to the norm on
L^{2}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
s
satisfies
A1
then

\langle v_{1}^{2}\rangle+\langle v_{2}^{2}\rangle+\langle\lvert y\rvert^{2}\rangle\lesssim 1

(5.11)

uniformly for all

and
\eta>0
.

Proof.

We multiply the first relation in (
4.4a
) by

, depending only on

for all

Lemma B.4

(Twist lemma)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space with a scalar product
\langle{\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}}\mspace{2mu},{\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}}\rangle
and equipped with a norm

(not necessarily induced by the scalar product),
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and

(H,\langle{\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}}\mspace{2mu},{\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}}\rangle)

\overline{\mathbb{D}}\!\,_{\varepsilon}\cap\spec A=\{\alpha\}

We assume that
\alpha

\begin{split}P\,:=\,-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\zeta}{A-\zeta}\,=\,\langle{p}\mspace{2mu},{\mspace{2mu}\cdot\mspace{2mu}}\rangle\mspace{2mu}x\,,\end{split}

(B.11)

with some

\begin{split}\lvert\langle{x}\mspace{2mu},{y}\rangle\rvert\,\geq\,2\mspace{2mu}\varepsilon\,,\qquad\lvert\langle{y}\mspace{2mu},{w}\rangle\rvert\,\leq\,\lVert w\rVert_{\#}\,,\qquad\forall\;w\in H\,.\end{split}

Lemma C.1
.

Let
y>0
be the unique solution to the equation
y^{3}+\beta\mspace{1mu}y=x
for
x>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

y\sim\sqrt{\max\{0,-\beta\}}+\frac{x}{x^{2/3}+\lvert\beta\rvert}\,.

Proof.

First we consider the case
\beta\geq 0
.
Then clearly

y\sim\frac{x}{x^{2/3}+\lvert\beta\rvert}\,.

Now let
\beta<0
, then we must have
y=\sqrt{-\beta}(1+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
since
y^{2}+\beta\mspace{1mu}y>0
. For this
\varepsilon
we get the equation

\varepsilon^{3}+3\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon=x\mspace{1mu}\lvert\beta\rvert^{-3/2}\,.

Thus, we have the scaling

\varepsilon\sim\min\biggl\{{\frac{x}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{3/2}},\frac{x^{1/3}}{\lvert\beta\rvert^{1/2}}}\biggr\}\,.

Therefore we conclude

y=\sqrt{\lvert\beta\rvert}+\min\biggl\{{\frac{x}{\lvert\beta\rvert},x^{1/3}}\biggr\}\sim\sqrt{\lvert\beta\rvert}+\frac{x}{x^{2/3}+\lvert\beta\rvert}\,,

which is the claim of the lemma.
∎

Lemma C.3

(Properties of
f_{a}
and
g_{a}
)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}_{>}
, as well as
f_{a}\colon\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1/a,1/\overline{a}\!\,,-1/a,-1/\overline{a}\!\,\}\to\mathbb{R}

be

Then

and

are real analytic.
Moreover, the Laplacian and derivatives of

\begin{split}\Delta f_{a}(0)=4\lvert a\rvert^{4},\qquad\partial_{y}^{2k+1}f_{a}(0)=0,\qquad\partial_{y}^{2k}f_{a}(0)=(-1)^{k}(2k)!|a|^{2}\frac{\re a^{2k+1}}{\re a}\end{split}

(C.2)

along the imaginary axis are

\begin{split}\Delta g_{a}(0)\!=\!4\lvert a\rvert^{4},\;\partial_{y}^{2k-1}g_{a}(0)\!=\!(-1)^{k+1}(2k-1)!|a|^{2}\frac{\im a^{2k}}{\re a}\,,\;\partial_{y}^{2k}g_{a}(0)\!=\!(-1)^{k}(2k)!|a|^{2}\frac{\re a^{2k+1}}{\re a}\end{split}

\begin{split}\partial_{y}^{k}\frac{1}{\lvert\zeta\rvert^{2}}&=\bigl({\mathrm{i}(\partial_{\zeta}-\partial_{\overline{\zeta}\!\,})}\bigr)^{k}\frac{1}{\lvert\zeta\rvert^{2}}=\mathrm{i}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{l}\partial_{\zeta}^{k-l}\partial_{\overline{\zeta}\!\,}^{l}\frac{1}{\lvert\zeta\rvert^{2}}\\

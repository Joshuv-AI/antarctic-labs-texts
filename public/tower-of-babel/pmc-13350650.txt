TEER Beyond Contraindication
Authors: Minghao Ma, Xinghua Gu, Wenzhi Pan, Yuan Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Mechanism of Disease Onset and Treatment

Mitral regurgitant jets impinged on nonbacterial thrombotic endocarditis excrescences, leading to recurrent cerebral infarction; transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) eliminated jet impact; and carotid embolic protection prevented cerebral embolism, thereby resolving recurrent cerebral infarction. ANCA = antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody.

History of Presentation

A 52-year-old man presented to the hospital with chest pain, chest tightness, dyspnea, diaphoresis, and dizziness, which resolved after rest. He had been hospitalized twice within 2 months due to cerebral infarction, during which echocardiography confirmed massive mitral regurgitation (MR), mitral valve prolapse (MVP), rupture of chordae tendineae, and mild aortic regurgitation, with excrescences found on the valves (Figures 1A to 1C). The patient was prescribed oral atorvastatin calcium and indobufen, and surgical treatment for MR was recommended to be performed 1 month after recovery from cerebral infarction.

Past Medical History

The patient had a medical history of hypertension for more than a decade with a maximum systolic blood pressure of 190 mm Hg, treated with metoprolol; diabetes mellitus for 3 years, managed with insulin therapy; antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)–associated vasculitis and nephritis for 4 months, treated with cyclophosphamide and prednisolone; as well as 2 episodes of cerebral infarction within 2 months, treated with indobufen, citicoline sodium, and idebenone. The patient also had a surgical history of appendectomy 30 years ago, cardiac stent implantation 3 years ago, and percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty 3 months ago.

Differential Diagnosis

The foremost differential diagnosis of nonbacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE) is infective endocarditis, which is identified via blood cultures and serologic tests. Other differential diagnoses include cardiac space-occupying lesions, such as papillary fibroelastoma and valvular thrombosis, which is confirmed by clinical history combined with imaging modalities including echocardiography.

Investigations

Physical examination revealed no abnormalities other than systolic murmurs at the cardiac apex and restricted movement of the right upper limb. Serologic and blood biochemical tests revealed a marked elevation of PR3-ANCA (70.54 U/mL), creatinine (217 μmol/L), urea (19.75 mmol/L), and uric acid (577 μmol/L), which indicated ANCA-associated nephritis. A decrease in high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (0.64 mmol/L) and an increase in triglyceride (1.97 mmol/L) were associated with the high risk of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events in the patient. In addition, slight reductions were noted in free triiodothyronine (2.93 pmol/L), alanine aminotransferase (8 U/L), aspartate aminotransferase (14 U/L), and total cholesterol (2.55 mmol/L). To confirm the nature of the valvular excrescences, 3 sets of blood cultures were performed at 1-hour intervals, and all results were negative. Additionally, blood metagenomic sequencing was conducted with negative results for pathogenic microorganisms. Head computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated multiple cerebral infarction foci, microbleeds, and cerebral arteriosclerosis with localized stenosis. Chest computed tomography showed bilateral pleural effusion, bilateral atelectasis, mediastinal lymph nodes, and pericardial effusion. Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) identified massive MR, MVP with chordae tendineae rupture at the P3 segment, mild aortic regurgitation, mild pulmonary hypertension, diastolic dysfunction, pericardial effusion, and NBTE. The NBTE excrescences were noted at the A2 segment, which was subjected to the regurgitant jet impingement from MVP at the P3 segment.

Management

The patient received preoperative treatment aimed at nourishing the myocardium and improving myocardial metabolism. Considering the patient's immunosuppressed state, antibiotic prophylaxis was initiated with intravenous injection of 1 g of ceftaroline every 8 hours on the day prior to the procedure and continued for 3 days postoperatively to prevent infectious complications.

Notably, the regurgitant jet from the prolapse could impinge on the NBTE located at A2 segment, which led to NBTE detachment, embolization and recurrent cerebral infarction. Moreover, the patient's multiple comorbidities were related to the high surgical risk and increased the likelihood of paravalvular leakage and premature degeneration of the mechanical valve and the need for repeat thoracotomy. Based on the above-mentioned considerations, transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) was planned, as this intervention could effectively resolve the issue of recurrent cerebral infarction and defer the need for surgical mitral valve replacement. To prevent intraoperative NBTE embolization, bilateral carotid embolic protection device implantation was scheduled concurrently.

After excluding contraindications, TEER was performed in the hybrid operating room under the guidance of digital subtraction angiography and TEE, with the patient under general anesthesia. TEE indicated that the regurgitation was located at the P3 segment, with a grade of 4+. The clip (DragonFly XW0612, Valgen Medtech) was introduced through the right femoral vein into the right atrium, then advanced to the left ventricle via the interatrial septum and mitral valve with the assistance of an intracardiac guiding sheath and a guiding catheter, successfully grasping the valve leaflets at the A3/P3 segments (Figure 1D). Post-grasping TEE detected mild MR (Figure 1E) with a transvalvular pressure gradient of 2 mm Hg and excrescences at the A2 segment in situ (Figures 1F and 1G). Bilateral carotid embolic protection device implantation was performed before TEER procedure, as the cerebral protection strategy to reduce the risk of intraoperative embolic events. Two embolic protection devices (Proender device; TJWY Medical), which connect a nitinol braided filter mesh to a single wire with an overall outer diameter of 3.2-F, were inserted through a femoral artery sheath placed in the left femoral artery and ultimately positioned in the brachiocephalic trunk and left common carotid artery, respectively (Figure 1H).

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient had an uneventful recovery with stable vital signs, and postoperative examinations revealed no significant abnormalities. Given his stable condition, the patient was eventually discharged.

No recurrent cerebral infarction was noted within 6 months postoperatively. The follow-up examination showed mild MR with a transvalvular pressure gradient of 3 mm Hg (Figure 1I).

Discussion

MR ranks as the third most prevalent valvular heart disease globally, predominantly affecting the elderly population.1 TEER has emerged as a well-established surgical alternative for patients with severe MR who are at high surgical risk due to multiple comorbidities.2

NBTE is a rare phenomenon of small, friable, sterile excrescences on cardiac valves that are mainly associated with neoplasia and autoimmune disease.3 Systemic embolism represents the most common clinical manifestation of NBTE, with cerebrovascular accident being the most frequent among them. To date, there are no authoritative guidelines for the management of NBTE. Treatment is primarily based on retrospective studies and expert opinions, with an emphasis on multidisciplinary team collaboration. Treatment of the underlying disease and anticoagulant therapy are recommended; surgical intervention should be selected with caution.4

Cardiac excrescences are traditionally considered a contraindication to TEER, making this case a breakthrough in overcoming such procedural limitations. A comprehensive literature search identified only 1 published report of TEER in the presence of cardiac excrescences, which described TEER as a bridge to cancer therapy in a young patient with marantic endocarditis, a scenario distinct from ours.5 In this case, the 52-year-old patient was admitted to hospital for MR with recurrent cerebral infarction, and TEE revealed cardiac valve excrescences. In retrospect, the onset of ANCA-associated vasculitis and nephritis preceded that of MR and recurrent cerebral infarction (Figure 2). No evidence of infective endocarditis was identified. Given the structural features of the patient's mitral valve disease, recurrent cerebral infarction in this patient is considered to be related to NBTE. Considering the patient's young age, mechanical valve replacement via open-heart surgery represents the standard approach in this case, accompanied by lifelong vitamin K antagonist anticoagulation therapy.6 However, the patient's comorbidities, especially ANCA-associated vasculitis and nephritis, were associated with a higher risk of postoperative paravalvular leakage and premature degeneration of the mechanical valve.7,8 Furthermore, the patient's recurrent cerebral infarction was associated with the detachment of valvular excrescences secondary to ANCA-associated vasculitis, which was precipitated by the MR jet. Thus, interventional correction of the prolapse could essentially achieve clinical cure. Considering these factors, along with the patient's high surgical risk and the fact that the excrescences were not located at the intended clip grasping site, we opted for TEER with targeted preventive measures; had the excrescences involved the grasping zone, however, open surgical intervention should have been recommended instead of TEER. Critical strategies to avoid excrescence-related complications included high transseptal puncture and careful catheter/clip manipulation to prevent any contact with the excrescences. This minimally invasive approach not only addressed MR, but also delayed the need for open-heart surgery. It is worth noting that DragonFly XW0612 clip was selected for its superior suitability for the patient's 17-mm wide prolapse involving posterior commissure: it is 2 mm wider than the MitraClip G3 XTR (Abbott) and incorporates a central occluding mesh, thus reducing the risk of residual regurgitation. Intraoperative systemic heparinization and postoperative anticoagulant therapy were administered to mitigate thrombus formation, and bilateral carotid embolic protection device implantation was performed concurrently to prevent thrombus from entering the cerebral arteries. Through these 2 lines of defense, we successfully ameliorated MR, while avoiding the recurrence of cerebral embolism.

Conclusions

This case illustrates that TEER is feasible for patients with severe MR, concurrent NBTE, and a history of cerebral infarction. With the adjunctive use of preventive carotid embolic protection device implantation, TEER can effectively ameliorate MR while reducing the risk of cerebrovascular events.

This case presents 2 innovative contributions to clinical practice: 1) It breaks through the traditional procedural contraindication to TEER. 2) For patients with ANCA-associated vasculitis complicated with NBTE at mitral valve and recurrent cerebral infarction, when the cerebral infarction is associated with the dislodgment of NBTE excrescences induced by the regurgitant jets impingement of mitral valve prolapse, TEER can serve as an effective bridging therapy to defer the need for surgical valve replacement.

Generalized Poincaré inequality for quantum Markov semigroups
Marius Junge, Jia Wang

Abstract
We prove a noncommutative ( p , p ) (p,p) -Poincaré inequality for trace-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on tracial von Neumann algebras, assuming only the existence of a spectral gap. Extending semi-commutative results of Huang and Tropp huang2021poincare , our argument uses Markov dilations to obtain chain-rule estimates for Dirichlet forms and employs amalgamated free products to define an appropriate noncommutative derivation. We further generalize the argument to non-tracial σ \sigma -finite von Neumann algebras under the weaker assumption of GNS-detailed balance, using Haagerup’s reduction and Kosaki’s interpolation theorem. As applications, we recover noncommutative Khintchine and sub-exponential concentration inequalities.

Introduction

The Poincaré inequality, first introduced in
poincare1890
, is a central tool
in analysis, probability, and geometry.
In its classical Euclidean form, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, connected
open set with Lipschitz boundary. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only
on
\Omega
, such that for every smooth function with compact support,

\int_{\Omega}\bigl|f(x)-f_{\Omega}\bigr|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla f(x)\|_{2}^{2}dx,

(1)

where
f_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f(x)dx
denotes the mean of
f
.

More generally, let
\mu
be a probability measure on a space
X
, and let
\Gamma

be the carré du champ/gradient form associated with a symmetric Markov generator
L
.
The Poincaré inequality takes the invariant-measure form

\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(f):=\int_{X}|f-\mu(f)|^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{X}\Gamma(f,f)d\mu,

(2)

where
\alpha>0
is the spectral gap of
L
on
L^{2}(X,\mu)
; see, for example,

bakry-emery
.

An equivalent semigroup formulation is as follows.
Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
be a conservative,
\mu
-symmetric Markov semigroup
on
L^{2}(\mu)
with Dirichlet form

\mathscr{E}(f,g)=\int_{X}\Gamma(f,g)d\mu
.
Then (
2
) is equivalent to the existence of a spectral gap

\alpha>0
, namely

\|T_{t}f-\mu(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq e^{-\alpha t}\|f-\mu(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)},\qquad t\geq 0,

(3)

or, equivalently, to the infinitesimal form

\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathscr{E}(f,f),\qquad f\in\mathrm{Dom}(L).

(4)

The equivalences (
2
)–(
4
) are standard
(see
bakry-emery
) and play a fundamental role in quantitative mixing,
functional inequalities, and concentration phenomena.

In noncommutative analysis, the role of
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is played by a von Neumann algebra (vNa)
\mathcal{M}
. Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state
\tau
. A (uniformly continuous) quantum Markov semigroup (QMS) on
\mathcal{M}
is a family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathrm{CP-}\mathrm{unital}
maps satisfying the semigroup property and
\tau\circ T_{t}=\tau
for all

with generator

\Gamma(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(L(x^{*})y+x^{*}L(y)-L(x^{*}y)\bigr),\quad x,y\in\mathrm{Dom}(L),

(5)

and the fixed-point subalgebra

\mathcal{N}=\{x\in\mathcal{M}:T_{t}(x)=x\ \text{ for all }t\geq 0\}.

(6)

Let

denote the canonical

-preserving conditional expectation. Then the
(tracial) quantum Poincaré inequality
asserts

\|x-E(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\tau\bigl(\Gamma(x,x)\bigr),\qquad\text{for all }x=x^{*}\in\mathrm{Dom}(L),

(7)

and is equivalent to the existence of an

for

on the orthogonal complement of

\|T_{t}(x)-E(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}\leq e^{-\alpha t}\|x-E(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)},\qquad t\geq 0.

(8)

In this work, we extend the semi-commutative results of Huang and Tropp

Theorem 1.3

(
\mathrm{PI}(p,p)

Let

The proof uses the argument of Huang and Tropp as a stepping stone. In particular, they established a chain rule estimate relating the Dirichlet form to the associated gradient form. A further ingredient in their argument is a symmetrization procedure, based on doubling variables in the product space of a Markov process, which leads to key cancellations.
Our proof, in addition, identifies the appropriate dilation framework for quantum Markov semigroups, which allows us to derive chain rule estimates directly in the noncommutative setting. Since there are obstacles to generalizing their produce measure symmetrization procedure, we instead formulate it in terms of amalgamated free products, providing a natural setting for noncommutative algebras.

Sub-exponential concentration inequalities.

Poincaré-type inequalities are closely connected to the concentration of
measure. In the
random matrix
setting, such a principle was established by
Huang and Tropp
(
huang2021poincare
, Thm 2.7)
for Markov processes with a spectral
gap, showing that
\mathrm{PI}(p,p)
implies sub-exponential concentration.
Their approach relies on matrix inequalities and dilation techniques, and improves
upon earlier results of Aoun, Banna, and Youssef
Aoun_Banna_Youssef_2020
.
In particular, Huang and Tropp introduce a symmetrization (or doubling-of-variables)
argument that avoids technical difficulties present in the earlier approach.

We show that the
\mathrm{PI}(p,p)
inequality proved in this work implies a

sub-exponential
concentration inequality for observables measured in the
Lipschitz semi-norm induced by the gradient form as below.

Corollary

(cf
5.5
)
.

Let
T_{t}=e^{-tL}
be a GNS–
\phi
-detailed balance QMS with spectral gap
\alpha>0
and conditional expectation
E
onto its fixed-point algebra. Then for any
x\in\mathcal{M}
and large
t>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{\phi}(|x-E(x)|>t)\leq 2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}t}{2\sqrt{2}e\|x\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\Gamma}}}\Bigr).

Our result highlights a clear
dichotomy: under the sole assumption of an
L^{2}
spectral gap, one cannot expect
Gaussian or Talagrand-type concentration, but sub-exponential tails are still
universally guaranteed. We further clarify the sharpness of this regime by contrasting
it with the stronger concentration phenomena arising from the modified logarithmic Sobolev
inequalities (MLSI), and by presenting a birth-death process that rules out any improvement
of Gaussian decay under a uniform spectral gap assumption alone.

2.3

2.3.1
Motivation: Fock spaces

•

Bosons (CCR).

Appendix B
Spectral gaps in AFP

This section identifies the spectral gap for tensor, direct-sum, and amalgamated product quantum Markov semigroups.

Proposition B.1

(Spectral gap for
T^{1}_{t}\otimes T^{2}_{t}
)
.

Let
(T^{i}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be quantum Markov semigroups on
\mathcal{M}_{i}
with conditional expectations
E_{i}:\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{N}_{i}
onto the fixed point algebras
\mathcal{N}_{i}
with spectral gaps
\alpha_{i}>0
, i.e.

\|T^{i}_{t}(x)-E_{i}(x)\|_{2}\leq e^{-\alpha_{i}t}\|x-E_{i}(x)\|_{2},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}_{i},t\geq 0.

On

\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}
consider the product semigroup
T_{t}:=T^{1}_{t}\otimes T^{2}_{t},

with conditional expectation

i.e. the spectral gap of
T^{1}_{t}\otimes T^{2}_{t}
is
\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{H}_{i}=L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\tau_{i})
and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
.
Let
E_{i}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{N}_{i}\subset\mathcal{H}_{i}

.
Denote

Then

\mathring{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})\oplus(\operatorname{Ran}E_{1}\otimes\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{2}).

On
\mathcal{H}

The operator

preserves each summand of
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}
.
Its restriction to
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
equals

\operatorname{Ran}E_{1}\otimes\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{2}

\|(T^{1}_{t}-E_{1})\otimes T^{2}_{t}\|\leq\|T^{1}_{t}-E_{1}\|\leq e^{-\alpha_{1}t},\qquad\|E_{1}\otimes(T^{2}_{t}-E_{2})\|\leq\|T^{2}_{t}-E_{2}\|\leq e^{-\alpha_{2}t}.

\|T_{t}(x)-E(x)\|_{2}\leq e^{-\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}t}\|x-E(x)\|_{2},\qquad x\in\mathcal{M}.

and the same argument with the roles of
1
and
2

\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
.
∎

x\in\dom(L)\cap\mathcal{M}
the gradient form satisfies
\Gamma(x,x)\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proposition D.2
.

(T_{t})
is noncommutative diffusion if

\Gamma_{\epsilon}(x,x)\to\Gamma(x,x)\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M})

(65)

in
\sigma(L_{1}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{M})
topology. This implies
\Gamma(x,x)\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})}^{\|\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M})}}
by Hahn-Banach. Thus, there exists a family of Lindbladians
L_{\epsilon_{j}}
, and the gradient form of convex combinations of Lindbladians also converges

\Gamma_{\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}L_{\epsilon_{j}}}(x,y)=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\Gamma_{\epsilon_{j}}(x,y)\to\Gamma(x,y)

in
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M})}
as
\{\epsilon_{j}\}\downarrow 0

, for some
\epsilon_{j}>0,\lambda_{j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}=1
.

We work with a convex
combination of
\epsilon_{j}
-regularized operators
\widehat{L}_{\epsilon_{j}}
and then
let
\epsilon_{j}\downarrow 0
. The corresponding spectral gap is

\overline{\alpha}_{\epsilon}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\tfrac{\alpha}{1+\epsilon_{j}\alpha}

as in
Appendix

C
.
By similar arguments as in
Lemma

C.1
, given
L^{1}
convergence and uniformly boundedness of
\Gamma_{\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}L_{\epsilon_{j}}}(x,x)
for
x\in\operatorname{Dom}(L)
, we obtain
L^{p/2}
-norm convergence.
We are now ready to prove
eq.

55
. For
y\in\mathcal{M},y_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\iota_{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{\Gamma}(y,y_{k}))\|_{\frac{p}{2},\widehat{\phi},\frac{1}{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}\downarrow 0\\
\forall j\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\overline{\alpha}_{\epsilon}}}\lim_{k\to\infty}\Bigl\|\iota_{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\widehat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon_{j}}(y,y_{k})\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{\frac{p}{2},\widehat{\phi},\frac{1}{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}\downarrow 0\\
\forall j\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\overline{\alpha}_{\epsilon}}}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\iota_{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon_{j}}(y,y_{k}))\|_{\frac{p}{2},\widehat{\phi},\frac{1}{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}\downarrow 0\\
\forall j\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\overline{\alpha}_{\epsilon}}}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\|\iota_{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon_{j}}(y,y))\|_{\frac{p}{2},\widehat{\phi},\frac{1}{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}(\sum_{j}\lambda_{j})\|\iota_{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{\Gamma}(y,y))\|_{\frac{p}{2},\widehat{\phi},\frac{1}{2}}^{1/2}=\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\|\iota_{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{\Gamma}(y,y))\|_{\frac{p}{2},\widehat{\phi},\frac{1}{2}}^{1/2},

where the inequality follows from Minkowski’s inequality, and the third line follows from
eq.

57
and
Lemma

C.1
. Finally, we pick
\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}=1
to prove

55

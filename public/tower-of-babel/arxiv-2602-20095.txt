1.
Introduction

The subconvexity problem for higher rank automorphic forms has a recent history.
The first result is due to Li. In
[
13
, Corollary 1.2]
Li shows, among other results, that for any
self-dual
Hecke–Mass form
\pi
for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
,

L(\pi,\tfrac{1}{2}-t)\ll(1+|t|)^{3/4-\delta},

for any
\delta<\frac{1}{16}
.
Subsequently, Blomer proved a similar result for the non-archimedean twists. More precisely, in
[
2
]
Blomer shows, among other results, that for any
self-dual
Hecke–Mass form
\pi
for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and any primitive quadratic Dirichlet character
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})

L(\pi\otimes\chi,\tfrac{1}{2})\ll q^{3/4-\delta},

for any
\delta<\frac{1}{8}
.

The first result for non necessarily self-dual cusp forms is by Munshi. In
[
16
]
, Munshi shows that for any fixed Hecke–Mass cusp form
\pi
over
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
that satisfies the Ramanujan-Selberg conjecture and for a sequence of Dirichlet characters of prime conductor
q
that tends to infinity, the subconvex bound

L\left(\pi\otimes\chi,\frac{1}{2}\right)\ll q^{3/4-\delta}

holds for any
\delta<\frac{1}{1612}
. In a subsequent work
[
17
]
, Munshi was able to improve the exponent to
\frac{1}{308}
and remove the Ramanujan-Selberg conjecture. All these results were possible using a so called
\operatorname{GL}(2)
-delta symbol, whereby one detects the equality
n_{1}=n_{2}
using the Petersson trace formula. The arguments leading to these results were carefully studied by Holowinsky and Nelson
[
7
]
, who were able to drastically simplify the proof and obtain the exponent
\delta=\frac{1}{36}
.

In this paper, we deal with the following problem: consider
\pi
a fixed automorphic representations on
\operatorname{GL}_{3}/F
, where
F
is any number field and let
\chi
be a sequence of characters in
\widehat{F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
of finite order of conductor
N(\mathfrak{q})
, where
\mathfrak{q}
is an integral prime ideal with norm tending to infinity. The subconvexity problem in the twist aspect is to show the existence of
\kappa_{0}>0
such that

(1)

L(\pi\otimes\chi,\tfrac{1}{2})\ll N(\mathfrak{q})^{\frac{3}{4}-\kappa}.

for all
\kappa<\kappa_{0}
.
The corresponding problem (and other aspects as well) for representations over
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
has been completely solved in
[
15
]
(see also
[
27
]
for the twist case).

For
\operatorname{GL}_{3}
, recent progress has been made by Qi,
[
19
]
,
[
21
]
, in the case where
\pi
is a self-dual cuspidal representation. Qi succeeded in generalizing Blomer’s work
[
2
]
for a general number field
F
using his theory of complex Bessel and Hankel transforms (see
[
20
]
). Unfortunately, this argument cannot deal with a non-self-dual automorphic representation
\pi
. In this paper, we generalize the methods of
[
7
]
to number fields and obtain.

Theorem 1.1
.

The subconvex bound (
1
) holds for any
\kappa_{0}<\frac{1}{36},

provided that the ideal conductor
\mathfrak{C}(\pi)
of
\pi
is coprime to the discriminant ideal of
F
. Moreover, the implicit constant depends at most polynomially on
\mathfrak{C}(\pi)
and on the invariants of
F
.

Remark 1.2
.

Since our main focus is not on the conductor of
\pi
, we deliberately assume that
\mathfrak{C}(\pi)
and the discriminant ideal of
F
are coprime. This simplifies computations for the Voronoi summation formula. However, it should be possible to perform the computations even in the case when the two ideals are not coprime.

Remark 1.3
.

The assumption that
\chi
is of finite order is taken for simplicity. Our method is also applicable for general
\chi
and one would obtain (
1
) with a constant depending on the archimedean conductor of
\chi
. To obtain a hybrid subconvex bound in both the archideman and non-archimdean aspect following the method in this work (and
[
23
]
), one has to use the theory of complex bessel transforms, developed by Zhi Qi. See Remark
6.5
for more details.

Given a finite order character
\chi\colon F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of the conductor
\mathfrak{q}
, one obtains a character on
(O_{F}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}
. This character is an instance of an
\ell
-adic trace function over
O_{F}/\mathfrak{q}
. From this perspective, if we consider subconvexity (
1
) as non-correlation results, it is natural to ask whether other trace functions also show non-correlation with automorphic forms. Fouvry, Kowalski and Michel studied in
[
5
]
the non-correlation of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}
automorphic forms with trace functions over
\mathbb{Z}/(q)
for
q
a prime number, and this was extended by Nadarajan in
[
18
]
to the number field setting. Kowalski, Lin, Michel and Sawin
[
10
]
have already used the strategy discovered in
[
7
]
to show non-corellation of trace functions (more generally periodic twists) with automorphic forms over
\operatorname{GL}_{3}/\mathbb{Q}
. It seems reasonable to expect that trace functions over
O/\mathfrak{q}
and automorphic forms over
\operatorname{GL}_{3}/F
also show non-corellation properties. We hope to return to this question in future.

1.1.
Strategy for the proof and structure of the paper

We briefly discuss the strategy of the proof.
For
\phi\in\pi^{\infty}
we consider the Jacquet– Piateski-Shapiro– Shalika adelic period

I(\phi,\chi,s)=\int_{F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{\times}}\mathbb{P}\phi\begin{pmatrix}y&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\chi(y)|y|^{s-1/2}\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y,

where
\mathbb{P}\phi
is some non-complete global Whittaker function, see equation (
10
) for the precise definition. We choose
\phi_{0}
via the local Whittaker models to correspond to the local new-vectors for finite places and to some nice smooth functions for the archimedean places. We choose then
\phi
to be the right shift of
\phi_{0}
by a unipotent element, more precisely
\phi=\phi_{0}^{u_{12}(\zeta_{\mathfrak{q}})}
. See Section
8
for details about the new-form and Section
2
for the notations
u_{12}
and
\zeta_{\mathfrak{q}}
. We can show similarly to
[
27
]
and
[
15
]
that

N(\mathfrak{q})^{-\frac{1}{2}}L(\pi\otimes\chi,s)\sim I(\phi,\chi,s),

and so it suffices to show the existence of some
\delta>0
such that

I(\phi,\chi,\tfrac{1}{2})\ll N(\mathfrak{q})^{1/4-\delta}.

The sign
\sim
can be interpreted as equal up to absolutely bounded non-oscillating archimedean factors.
We then write
I(\phi,\chi,s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{t}(\phi,\chi)t^{s-1/2}\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}t
(again following
[
27
]
and also
[
24
]
), where
I_{t}(\phi,\chi)
is a period over
F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{(1)}
, and we see that using an approximate functional equation (see Proposition
8.3
and Proposition
8.4
for a refined version) it suffices to bound

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{q})^{-3/2-\kappa}<t<N(\mathfrak{q})^{-3/2+\kappa}\end{subarray}}I_{t}(\phi,\chi)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}t

for some
\kappa>0
small enough.
We then choose a fundamental domain
\bigsqcup_{I}I(\widehat{O}^{\times}E)
, where
I
runs over fixed representatives of the class group, for integration over
F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{(1)}
, and let
(O_{F}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}
act on this domain and we write

I_{t}(\phi,\chi)=\sum_{I}\chi(I)\sum_{a\in(O_{F}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}}\chi(\iota_{\mathfrak{q}}(a))\int_{\widehat{O}^{\times}_{1}(\mathfrak{q})E}\mathbb{P}\phi(a_{1}(I\iota_{\mathfrak{q}}(a)yz_{t}))\chi(y_{\infty})\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y,

see Section
2
for the definitions of
\widehat{O}^{\times}
and
\widehat{O}_{1}^{\times}(\mathfrak{q})
and for the explicit choice of

.

This allows us to write down a key identity similar to
[

, which is roughly as follows. In what follows we pretend that

is principal. See (

\displaystyle I_{t}(\phi,\chi)=\mathcal{F}_{t}(\phi,\chi)-\mathcal{O}_{t}(\phi,\chi),

where

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{t}(\phi,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\neq 0}\widehat{V_{0}}\left(\frac{\delta\Delta}{\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}}\right)\sum_{I}\chi(I)

(3)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{a\in(O_{F}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}}\chi(\iota_{\mathfrak{q}}(a))\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}\left(\frac{\iota_{\mathfrak{q}}(a\delta)}{\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right)\int_{\widehat{O}^{\times}_{1}(\mathfrak{q})E}\mathbb{P}\phi(a_{1}(I\iota_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta^{-1})yz_{t}))\chi(y_{\infty})\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y.

for some compactly supported smooth function

and a parameter

that we want to choose appropriately. We proceed by bounding (
2
) and (
3
) separately, see Propositions
8.7
,
9.1
and
9.2
. To bound (
2
) we do not use the oscillation

but rather we bound

non-trivially, here we ignore
I
and

for this introduction. We choose
\Delta
and

so that if

, then
N(\delta)\asymp|\Delta|<N(\mathfrak{q})
. We use additive reciprocity to write, for

so that (

\mathbb{P}\phi_{0}^{u_{12}(\zeta_{\mathfrak{q}})}(a_{1}(\iota_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta^{-1})z_{t}))\sim\mathbb{P}\phi_{0}^{u_{12}(\zeta_{\delta})}(a_{1}(\iota_{\delta}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1})z_{t}))

Second,

The proof now follows closely
[

, with the only difference that it is formulated using the adèlic language.

2.
Notation and basic set-up

Let
F
denote a number field and
O_{F}
its ring of integers. By

of
F
we mean an identity class of discrete valuations of
F
. By Ostrowski’s Theorem, we identify the set of (identity classes of) non-archimedean places
v
of
F
with
\operatorname{spec}(O_{F})
. We denote the underlying prime ideal by
\mathfrak{p}_{v}\subset O_{F}
, in particular for
x\in O_{F}
the valuation
v(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
is the biggest integer so that
x\in\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{v(x)}
. Viceversa, for a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset O_{F}
we denote by
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
the corresponding discrete valuation.

For any non-archimedean place
v
of
F
fix the following data:

•

A completion
F\subset F_{v}
of
F
with respect to the absolute value
|{\bullet}|_{v}
induced by
v
.

•

The ring of
v
-adic integers
O_{v}=\{x\in F_{v},\ |{x}|_{v}\leq 1\}\subset F_{v}
and its subgroup of units
O_{v}^{\times}=\{x\in F_{v},\ |{x}|_{v}=1\}.

•

A uniformizer
\varpi_{v}\in O_{v}
.

The set of archimedean places, denoted here by
S_{\infty}
, is identified with the union of real embeddings, that is,
\rho_{v}\colon F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
so that
\rho_{v}(F)\subset\mathbb{R}
, and a choice of one of the two conjugates
\rho_{v},\overline{\rho_{v}}\colon F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, when
\rho_{v}(F)\nsubseteq\mathbb{R}
. They will be called
real
embeddings and
complex
embeddings, respectively. Let
d_{\infty}=|{S_{\infty}}|
. For a place
v
of
F
we will usually write the condition

as

.

Let
\mathbb{A}
denote the adèle ring of
F
and
\mathbb{A}^{\times}
the idèle group of
F
. We embed, as usual,
F\subset\mathbb{A}
and

diagonally. For any finite set of places
S
of
F
we denote
\mathbb{A}^{S}=\prod^{\prime}_{v\notin S}(F_{v},O_{v})
and
(\mathbb{A}^{\times})^{S}=\prod_{v\notin S}^{\prime}(F_{v}^{\times},O_{v}^{\times})
the adèle ring and the idèle group, respectively, defined omitting those places
v\in S
from the definition. We will eventually use the notation
\mathbb{A}_{\text{fin}}=\mathbb{A}^{S_{\infty}}
and call it the ring
of finite adèles
. For a finite set of places
R
we will denote
\mathbb{A}_{R}=\prod_{v\in R}F_{v}
and
\mathbb{A}_{R}^{\times}=\prod_{v\in R}F_{v}^{\times}
. We write

and
\widehat{O}^{\times}=\prod_{v<\infty}O_{v}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\text{fin}}^{\times}
.
For an integral ideal
\mathfrak{a}
we denote

\widehat{O}^{\times}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})=\prod_{v|\mathfrak{a}}\left(1+\varpi_{v}^{v(\mathfrak{a})}O_{v}\right)\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v<\infty\\
v\nmid\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}O_{v}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\text{fin}}^{\times}.

We also set
F_{\infty}=\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}}F_{v}
and
F^{+}_{\infty}=\prod_{v\ \text{real}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\prod_{v\ \text{complex}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

For functions

, that is split as a product

\psi=\prod_{v<\infty}\psi_{v}\times\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}}\psi_{v}
, where for
v<\infty
let
p
the prime under
v
so that
F_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a finite (possibly trivial) extension, then
\psi_{v}=\psi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\circ\operatorname{tr}_{F_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
for
v<\infty
. For
v\in S_{\infty}
let
\psi_{v}(x)=e^{-2\pi i\operatorname{tr}_{F_{v}/\mathbb{R}}(x)}.

Remark 2.1
.

We denote
\mathfrak{D}\subset O_{F}
the discriminant ideal of
F/\mathbb{Q}
.
The standard character
\psi=\prod_{v}\psi_{v}
we just defined is ramified at those prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}

: that is the prime ideals that divide

.

2.5.
Measures

The choices of Haar measures are not so crucial as long as we remain consistent, since we are interested only in upper bounds.

For any place
v
we endow
F_{v}
with the additive Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x_{v}
, which is self-dual with respect to the Fourier transform with respect to
\psi_{v}
. In particular, or finite
v
we have
\operatorname{vol}(O_{v})=|{\mathfrak{D}}|_{v}^{1/2}
.
We endow
\mathbb{A}
with the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x
normalized so that for any measurable function
f=\prod_{v}f_{v}
with
f_{v}=\mathbb{1}_{O_{v}}
for all but finitely many
v<\infty
and
f_{v}
is measurable for every
v
, then

\int_{\mathbb{A}}f(x)\ \mathrm{d}x=\prod_{v}\int_{F_{v}}f_{v}(x_{v})\ \mathrm{d}x_{v}.

Similarly, we endow
F_{v}^{\times}
with the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}^{\times}x_{v}
that assigns to
O_{v}^{\times}\subset F_{v}^{\times}
volume
1
, when
v<\infty
and it assigns volume
1
and we set
\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|{x}|}
for
v\in S_{\infty}
. Notice that for
v<\infty
we have
d^{\times}x_{v}=\operatorname{vol}(\mathfrak{D}_{v})^{-1/2}(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p}_{v})})^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{v}}{|{x}_{v}|}.
In particular,
\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|{x}|}
.

We endow
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}^{\times}t=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
and push forward to
A_{1}
. We endow then
\mathbb{A}^{(1)}
with the measure
\mathrm{d}^{\times}x
so that

\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}f(x)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{(1)}}f(z_{t}x)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}x\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}t

for any integrable function
f
.

Let
K
be a non-archimedean local field with ring of integers
O
and unique maximal ideal

.
We recall briefly the local theory of new-vectors for irreducible generic representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}/K
(see
[
6
]
and
[

]
).

Any character
\psi
on

induces a character, which we still denote
\psi
on

(6)

\psi\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{12}&\cdots&x_{1n}\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&1&x_{n-1n}\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\psi(x_{12}+\cdots+x_{n-1n}).

Let
\rho

and

an unramified character on

. Let

denote the Whittaker model of

[

]
, and with a significant contribution of Matringe

]
, there is a unique vector

satisfying the following properties:

•

•

W_{\rho}^{\circ}\left(g\begin{pmatrix}k&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=W_{\rho}^{\circ}(g)
for all
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(K)
and

•

For any unramified irreducible generic unitary representation
\sigma
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(K)
let

denote the spherical vector of

as in
[

]
(normalized to take value

\displaystyle\int_{U_{2}(K)\setminus\operatorname{GL}_{2}(K)}W_{\rho}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}(h)|{h}|_{v}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\ \mathrm{d}h=L(\rho\times\sigma,s).

If
\rho
is itself unramified, then the unique spherical vector (provided that it takes value

at the identity element) of
[
26
]
is also the new-vector.

It is shown in
[

]
that the new-vector satisfies also the following property: for any unramified character

of

we have for

big enough

It is shown in
[
6
]
that the new-vector is right-invariant under the action of

K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{a(\rho)})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}k&\omega\\
\nu^{t}&u\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(O)|\ \nu\in\mathfrak{p}^{a(\rho)}\times\mathfrak{p}^{a(\rho)},u\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathfrak{p}^{a(\rho)}})\right\},

We now consider the number field
F
and the fixed cuspidal automorphic representations

\tilde{\pi}

,

the corresponding new-vectors.

\displaystyle\ell\left(a(\varpi_{v}^{v(\mathfrak{D})},\varpi_{v}^{v(\mathfrak{D})})\right)W_{v}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi_{v},\psi_{v}),\quad\ell\left(a(\varpi^{v(\mathfrak{D})},\varpi_{v}^{v(\mathfrak{D})})\right)U_{v}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\pi}_{v},\overline{\psi_{v}})

3.
Counting units

A source of difficulty when working with an extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that is neither trivial nor an imaginary quadratic field is that the set of units
O_{F}^{\times}
is infinite.

The classical result that one uses is the Dirichlet’s unit theorem (see
[
12
, Unit Theorem]
, with some changes in the notation):

Theorem 3.1

(Dirichlet’s unit theorem)
.

Consider the
\log
map

\operatorname{Log}_{S_{\infty}}\colon F^{\times}\to\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}}\mathbb{R};\ x\mapsto\operatorname{log}(|{x}|_{v})_{v}.

Then
\operatorname{Log}_{S_{\infty}}(O_{F})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{\infty}}
is a lattice of rank
d_{\infty}-1
contained in the hyperplane
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\infty}}\ |\ x_{1}+\dots+x_{d_{\infty}}=0\}.

Moreover, the kernel of
\operatorname{Log}_{S_{\infty}}
is
\mu_{F}
.

We can state the following three consequences that will be useful.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v_{0}\in S_{\infty}
and let
S_{\infty}^{\prime}=S_{\infty}\smallsetminus\{v_{0}\}
. Then for any pair of tuples
(T_{v})_{v},(t_{v})_{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d_{\infty}-1}
of positive numbers it holds that

|{\{o\in O_{F}^{\times}\ |\ \forall v\in S^{\prime}_{\infty}:t_{v}\leq|{o}|_{v}\leq T_{v}\}}|\ll 1+\prod_{v\in S^{\prime}_{\infty}}\max(\log T_{v}-\log t_{v},0)

and that

|{\{o\in O_{F}^{\times}\ |\ \forall v\in S^{\prime}_{\infty}:t_{v}\leq|{o}|_{v}\leq T_{v}\}}|\gg\prod_{v\in S^{\prime}_{\infty}}\max(\log T_{v}-\log t_{v},0),

where the implicit constants depend only upon
F
.

Proof.

The statement is trivially true for
S_{\infty}=\{v_{0}\}
or
t_{v}\geq T_{v}
for some
v\in S_{\infty}
. So suppose these are not the case.
Let
(Y_{v})_{v\neq v_{0}},(y_{v})_{v\neq v_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\infty}-1}
be so that
y_{v}<Y_{v}
for each
v
.
By Dirichlet’s unit theorem
L=\operatorname{Log}_{S_{\infty}}(O^{\times}_{F})
is a lattice of rank
d_{\infty}-1
.

Then by constructing spheres of appropriate radius around each lattice point we deduce that there exists constants
c_{L},C_{L}>0
so that

c_{L}\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}^{\prime}}(Y_{v}-y_{v})\leq|{\{x\in L\ |\ \forall v\in S_{\infty}^{\prime}:y_{v}\leq x\leq Y_{v}\}}|\leq C_{L}\prod_{v\in S^{\prime}_{\infty}}(Y_{v}-y_{v}),

that translates to

\displaystyle c_{L}\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}^{\prime}}(Y_{v}-y_{v})\leq|{\{o\in O_{F}^{\times}\ |\ \forall v\in S^{\prime}_{\infty}:y_{v}\leq\log|{o}_{v}|\leq Y_{v}\}}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{L}|{\mu(F)}|\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}^{\prime}}(Y_{v}-y_{v}).

Choosing
Y_{v}=\log T_{v}
and
y_{v}=\log t_{v}
shows the assertion.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(T_{v})_{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d_{\infty}}
and let
T=\prod_{v}T_{v}
. Suppose that for each
v
there exists
\delta>0
and constants
C_{\delta}>0
so that for all
v\in S_{\infty}

T_{v}\leq C_{\delta}T^{\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta}.

Then

|{\{o\in O_{F}^{\times}\ |\ \forall v\in S_{\infty}:|{o}|_{v}\leq T_{v}}\}|\ll(\log C_{\delta}T^{\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta})^{d_{\infty}-1},

where the implicit constant only depends upon
F
.

Proof.

Let
o\in O_{F}^{\times}
so that
|{o}|_{v}\leq T_{v}
for all
v\in S_{\infty}
. Suppose that there exists
v_{0}
so that
|{o}|_{v_{0}}<C_{\delta}^{{}^{1-d_{\infty}}}T^{-(d_{\infty}-1)(\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta)}
. Then

C_{\delta}^{d_{\infty}-1}T^{(d_{\infty}-1)(\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta)}\geq\prod_{v\in S_{\infty},v\neq v_{0}}|{o}|_{v}=|{o}|_{v_{0}}^{-1}>C_{\delta}^{d_{\infty}-1}T^{(d_{\infty}-1)(\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta)},

which is absurd. Hence, we see that the cardinality on the left-hand side of (
3.3
) is bounded by

|{\{o\in O_{F}^{\times}\ |\ \forall v\in S_{\infty}:C_{\delta}^{{}^{1-d_{\infty}}}T^{-(d_{\infty}-1)(\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta)}\leq|{o}|_{v}\leq T_{v}\}}|

and latter is bounded by the previous corollary by

\min_{v_{0}\in S_{\infty}}\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}\smallsetminus\{v_{0}\}}(\log T_{v}-(1-d_{\infty})\log C_{\delta}T^{\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta},0)\ll\log(C_{\delta}T^{\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}+\delta})^{d_{\infty}-1},

where the implicit constant depends only upon
F
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\gamma)\subset O_{F}
be a non-zero integral principal ideal,
T=(T_{v})_{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d_{\infty}}
so that
\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}}|{T_{v}}|_{v}=N(\gamma)
. There exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
F
so that for each
\frac{1}{d_{\infty}}>\delta>\frac{C}{\log N(\gamma)}
, there exists a unit
o\in O_{F}^{\times}
satisfying

\forall v\in S_{\infty}:|{T_{v}}|_{v}N(\gamma)^{-\delta(d_{\infty}-1)}\leq|{o\gamma}|_{v}\leq|{T_{v}}|_{v}N(\gamma)^{\delta(d_{\infty}-1)}.

Proof.

Let
v_{0}\in S_{\infty}
and let
S^{\prime}_{\infty}=S_{\infty}\smallsetminus\{v_{0}\}
. We may assume that
S^{\prime}_{\infty}\neq\varnothing
, otherwise the statement is clear.
Let

B=\{o\in O_{F}^{\times}\ |\ \forall v\in S_{\infty}^{\prime}:|{T_{v}}|_{v}N(\gamma)^{-{\delta}}\leq|{\gamma o}|_{v}\leq|{T_{v}}|_{v}N(\gamma)^{\delta}\}.

By Lemma
3.2
we have that

|{B}|\gg(2\delta\log N(\gamma))^{d_{\infty}-1},

where the implicit constant depends only upon
F
.
For
o\in B
we have then
|{o}|_{v_{0}}=\prod_{v\neq v_{0}}|{o}|_{v}
and so

|{T_{v_{0}}}|_{v_{0}}N(\gamma)^{-(d_{\infty}-1)\delta}\leq|{\gamma o}|_{v_{0}}\leq|{T_{v_{0}}}|_{v_{0}}N(\gamma)^{(d_{\infty}-1)\delta}.

∎

4.
Gauss sums and Kloosterman sums

4.1.
Gauss sums

Let
K
be a non-archimedean local field (of characteristic
0
) with finite residue field and denote by
|\cdot|
the attached absolute value. Let
O
be its ring of integers,
\mathfrak{p}
be the maximal ideal of
O
and
O^{\times}=O\smallsetminus\mathfrak{p}
be the group of units. Fix a generator
\varpi
of
\mathfrak{p}
and let
p=|O/\mathfrak{p}|
.

Let
\psi\colon K\to\mathbb{C}^{(1)}
be an additive character and
\eta\colon K^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{(1)}
be a multiplicative character. Since
K^{\times}=\varpi^{\mathbb{Z}}O^{\times}
we can write
\eta=\eta_{0}|{\ }|_{v}^{it}
for some
\eta_{0}\in\widehat{O^{\times}}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi i\mathbb{Z}\log p.
Denote by
\mathfrak{p}^{r}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{s}
the respective conductors, which means that
r
is the smallest positive integer so that
\eta
is trivial in
1+\mathfrak{p}^{r}
, or
r=0
if
\eta_{0}
is trivial and
s
is the smallest integer so that
\psi
is trivial in
\mathfrak{p}^{s}=\varpi^{s}O_{F}
. Notice that
s
can be negative. The existence of conductors is ensured by continuity. We denote
r
by
a(\eta)
and
s
by
a(\psi)
.

Fix a Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x
on
K
a Haar measure on
K^{\times}
. Then
\mathrm{d}^{\times}{x}=c\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|{x}|}
for some
c>0
. The Gauss sum associated to
\eta
and
\psi
is the following integral.

G(\eta,\psi)=\int_{O^{\times}}\eta(y)\psi(y)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y.

From
[
22
, 7-4 Lemma]
:

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

(1)

If
a(\psi)<a(\eta)
, then
G(\eta,\psi)=0
.

(2)

If
a(\eta)=a(\psi)
, then

|{G(\eta,\psi)}|^{2}=c\operatorname{vol}(O,\mathrm{d}x)\operatorname{vol}(1+\mathfrak{p}^{a(\eta)},d^{\times}x)

(3)

If
a(\psi)>a(\eta)
, then

|{G(\eta,\psi)}|^{2}=c\operatorname{vol}(O,\mathrm{d}x)\left(\operatorname{vol}(1+\mathfrak{p}^{a(\eta)},\mathrm{d}^{\times}x)-p^{-1}\operatorname{vol}(1+\mathfrak{p}^{a(\psi)-1},\mathrm{d}^{\times}x)\right).

Suppose now that
\eta
is a ramified character, that is,
a(\eta)>0
.
Let
W\colon\operatorname{GL}_{3}(K)\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous function and suppose that
W
is right invariant by the action of
a_{1}(O^{\times})
, that is,
W\left(ga_{1}(u)\right)=W(g)
for all
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(K)
and
u\in O^{\times}
, whenever it converges one has

\int_{K^{\times}}W(a_{1}(y))\eta(y)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}W(a_{1}(\varpi^{k}))\eta(\varpi)^{k}\int_{O^{\times}}\eta(u)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}u=0.

As in (
6
) we extend
\psi
to a character on
U_{3}(K)
and suppose that
W
is such that

W(ug)=\psi(u)W(g)

for any
u\in U_{3}(K)
and
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(K)
. Then we have the following Lemma

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\psi,\eta
and
W
as before. Suppose, moreover, that
W(a_{1}(y))=0
for all
y\notin O
and
W
is right-invariant by the action of
a_{1}(O^{\times})
. Then, whenever the integral converges, we have

\int_{K^{\times}}W(a_{1}(y)u_{12}(\varpi^{-a(\eta)+a(\psi)}))\eta(y)|y|^{s}\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y=G(\eta,\psi(\varpi^{-a(\eta)+a(\psi)}\cdot))W(I_{3}).

Proof.

For any
y\in K^{\times}
we have
W(a_{1}(y)u_{12}(\varpi^{-a(\eta)+a(\psi)}))=\psi(y\varpi^{-a(\eta)+a(\psi)})W(a_{1}(y))
. Therefore, supposing that the integral is convergent, we see that it is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}W(a_{1}(\varpi^{k}))\eta(\varpi)^{k}p^{-ks}\int_{O^{\times}}\psi(u\varpi^{k-a(\eta)+a(\psi)})\eta(u)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}u.

The conductor of
\psi(\varpi^{k-a(\eta)+a(\psi)})
is
a(\eta)-k\leq a(\eta)
, so the inner integral is always
0
, unless
k=0
and we have the desired result.
∎

4.2.
Kloosterman sums

Let
\psi
be an additive character of conductor
a(\psi)\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let also
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\omega\in\mathfrak{p}^{-2l+a(\psi)}
,
o\in\mathfrak{p}^{a(\psi)}
. Also, let
\eta\in\widehat{K^{\times}}
be a multiplicative character with
a(\eta)\leq l
. We define the Kloosterman sum

\displaystyle S_{\eta}(o,\omega;l,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\varpi^{-l}O^{\times}/O}\eta(\varpi^{l}x)\psi(xo)\psi(x^{-1}\omega)\ \mathrm{d}x,

where
O
acts on
\varpi^{-l}O^{\times}
by additive translation. It is important to remark that the measure is the additive measure
\mathrm{d}x
and not the multiplicative measure
\mathrm{d}^{\times}x
.
If
\eta=1
, we simply write
S(o,\omega;l,\psi)
.

We also denote
Kl_{\eta}(\omega;l,\psi)=\frac{1}{p^{l/2}}S_{\eta}(\varpi_{v}^{a(\psi)},\omega;l,\psi)
the
normalized
, twisted by
\eta
, Kloosterman sum. As before, for
\eta=1
, we simply write
Kl(\gamma;l,\psi)
. We have the obvious isomorphism

\varpi^{-l}O^{\times}/O\to(O/\mathfrak{p}^{l})^{\times};x\mapsto\varpi^{l}x,

so that

S_{\eta}(o,\omega;l,\psi)=\sum_{u\in(O/\mathfrak{p}^{l})^{\times}}\eta(u)\psi(\varpi^{-l}uo)\psi(\varpi^{l}u^{-1}\omega).

Observe that if
\omega\in\mathfrak{p}^{-2l+a(\psi)+1}
, then we have the following.

(7)

Kl(\omega;l,\psi)=Kl(0;l,\psi)=\frac{1}{p^{l/2}}\sum_{u\in(O/\mathfrak{p}^{l})^{\times}}\psi(\varpi^{-l+a(\psi)}u)=\begin{cases}0&l>1\\
-p^{-l/2}&l=1.\end{cases}

The case
l=1
is shown by orthogonality of characters, while for
l>1
let
x\in O/\mathfrak{p}
act on
(O/\mathfrak{p}^{l})^{\times}
by additive translation:
x\cdot u=u+x\varpi^{l-1}
. Let
U
be a set of representatives for
(O/\mathfrak{p}^{l})^{\times}/(O/\mathfrak{p})
. Then

\displaystyle Kl(0;l,\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{u^{\prime}\in O/\mathfrak{p}}\psi(\varpi^{-l+s}(u+u^{\prime}\varpi^{l-1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}\psi(\varpi^{-l+s}u)\sum_{u^{\prime}\in O/\mathfrak{p}}\psi(\varpi^{s-1}u^{\prime})=0.

Suppose now
l_{1},l_{2}
are two non-negative integers. Let
\omega_{1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-2l_{1}+a(\psi)}
,
\omega_{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-2l_{2}+a(\psi)}
,
\gamma\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\max(l_{1},l_{2})+a(\psi)}
and
\eta\in\widehat{K^{\times}}
with conductor
a(\eta)\leq\min(l_{1},l_{2})
.
We define the correlation sum

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\gamma;l_{1},l_{2},\psi)=\sum_{a\in O/\mathfrak{p}^{\max(l_{1},l_{2})}}Kl_{\eta}(a\omega_{1};l_{1},\psi)\overline{Kl_{\eta}(a\omega_{2};l_{2},\psi)}\psi(\gamma a).

6.1.

Let
F
be
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Let

. Let
\rho
denote an admissible irreducible unitary representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(F)
.
Then the
\rho
-Bessel transform

of

is defined by the following uniqueness property (

]

\int_{F^{\times}}B_{\rho}[{V}](y)\eta(y)^{-1}|{y}|_{v}^{s-1}\mathrm{d}^{\times}y=\gamma(1-s,\rho\times\eta,\psi)\int_{F^{\times}}V(y)\eta(y)|{y}|_{v}^{-s}\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y,

for all

and all

with real part sufficiently big.
In the case where
V
is

(or say, in the Schwartz space of

) we can explicitly compute

using Mellin inversion.
For

any character of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
is of the form

for

big enough.

Let now

. Any character
\eta
of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}

for unique

and

Then we have for

\displaystyle\gamma(1-s,\rho,\psi)=\varepsilon(\rho,\psi)\prod_{\alpha\in\underline{\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma_{F}(s+\overline{\alpha})}{\Gamma_{F}(1-s+\alpha)},

We prove only the complex case, the real one is easier. Since the real part

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+\overline{\alpha}+\frac{|{k}|}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\left(1-s+\alpha+\frac{|{k}|}{2}\right)}

•

8.2.
Approximate functional equation

The next step is to state an approximate functional equation. In our context, this amounts in approximating the integral in the central direction. Recall that we write for
\phi\in\pi^{\infty}
,
t>0
and
\eta\in\widehat{F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
,

I_{t}(\phi,\eta)=\int_{F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}^{(1)}}\mathbb{P}\phi(a_{1}(yz_{t}))\eta(y)\mathrm{d}^{\times}y,

so that

I(\phi,\eta,1/2)=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{t}(\phi,\eta)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}t

and more generally

I(\phi,\eta,s+1/2)=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{t}(\phi,\eta)t^{s}\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}t.

In particular, by an approximate functional equation, we mean a statement of the following form:

\displaystyle I(\phi,\eta,1/2)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
t\asymp N(\mathfrak{f})^{-3/2}\end{subarray}}I_{t}(\phi,\eta)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}y\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}t,

for any reasonable
\phi
. The sign
\sim
is to be qualitatively interpreted as an approximation, but without a precise meaning yet.
We do this only on
\phi=\phi_{0}^{u_{12}(\zeta_{\mathfrak{f}})},

where
\mathfrak{f}\subset O_{F}
is the finite conductor of
\eta
.

The next Lemma is classical.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
h\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function such that
h(y)\neq 0\Rightarrow y\ll 1
and
(y\partial_{y})^{m}h(y)\ll_{m}1
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then for any
\sigma>0
and any polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[X]
it holds that

\int_{(\sigma)}|{\mathcal{M}(h,s)P(s)}|\ \mathrm{d}s\ll_{\sigma,\deg P}1,

where the implicit constant may depend on the degree of
P
and on
\sigma
. Similarly, if
h(y)\neq 0\Rightarrow y\gg 1
and
(y\partial_{y})^{m}h(y)\ll_{m}1
, for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then for any
\sigma<0
and any polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[X]
it holds that

\int_{(\sigma)}|{\mathcal{M}(h,s)P(s)}|\ \mathrm{d}s\ll_{\sigma,\deg P}1,

where the implicit constant may depend on the degree of
P
and on
\sigma
.

Proof.

Suppose
P
is non-zero, otherwise the statement is trivially verified.
By integration by parts we have, for
s\neq 0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
that

\mathcal{M}(h,s)=\frac{1}{s^{m}}\mathcal{M}((y\partial_{y})^{m}h,s),
hence

\displaystyle\int_{(\sigma)}|{\mathcal{M}(h,s)P(s)}|\ \mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\ll\int_{0}^{\infty}|{(y\partial_{y})^{\deg P+2}h(y)}y^{\sigma-1}|\int_{(\sigma)}\left|{\frac{P(s)}{s^{\deg P+2}}}\right|\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}y\ll 1.

The second statement follows by the first with the change of variables
y\to y^{-1}
, or also by direct computation as above.
∎

The following is an analog of
[

.

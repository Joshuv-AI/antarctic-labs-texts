NELL-1–Associated Membranous Nephropathy Induced by Long-Term Penicillamine Treatment in a Patient With Wilson’s Disease
Authors: Ahmet Burak Dirim, Ozge Hurdogan, Ozgur Akin Oto, Ayse Serra Artan, Vafa Suleymanova, Kadir Demir, Aslı Cifcibasi Ormeci, Yasemin Ozluk, Savas Ozturk, Isin Kilicaslan, Halil Yazici
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

A 27-year-old man was referred to our clinic for new-onset nephrotic syndrome in 2020. The patient was diagnosed with Wilson disease in 2013 and had been receiving 20 mg/kg per day of penicillamine (1200 mg/d at the last control). The laboratory results are shown in Supplementary Table S1. Renal biopsy revealed diffuse glomerular basement membrane thickening with occasional spike formation, in addition to diffused and granular IgG staining within the basement membranes on immunofluorescence microscopy, consistent with membranous nephropathy (MN) (Figure 1a–c). Autoimmune serology and PLA2R antibody were negative. Penicillamine treatment was discontinued, and the patient was switched to trientine. Despite supportive therapies, nephrotic syndrome persisted. Three months after the diagnosis, oral prednisolone and mycophenolate treatments were initiated. Twenty-one months after the diagnosis, complete remission was achieved. Considering that NELL-1–associated MN caused by bucillamine has been previously described in the literature, we suspected that this secondary MN case could be NELL-1–associated MN related to penicillamine (a thiol-containing drug similar to bucillamine) therapy.1 NELL-1 immunohistochemical staining was retrospectively performed on archival formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue blocks of the biopsy specimen, and it showed positive membranous staining (Figure 1d).

(a) Glomerulus with thickened basement membranes. (Periodic-Acid-Schiff; ×1000, immersion oil). (b) Glomerular basement membranes showing occasional spike formations (Periodic-Acid-Schiff-Methenamine Silver; ×1000, immersion oil). (c) Diffuse granular IgG positivity in glomerular basement membranes on immunofluorescence examination (anti-IgG antibody; immunofluorescence, ×400). (d) Immunohistochemical granular positivity for NELL1 in glomerular basement membranes (anti-NELL1 antibody; ×1000, immersion oil).

NELL-1–associated MN has been described in many conditions, including mercury-containing traditional indigenous medicines and drugs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, alpha-lipoic acid, thiol-containing treatments, tiopronin, and bucillamine).2,3 Several mechanisms have been proposed to explain thiol-induced MN. First, thiol-related modifications in tertiary and quaternary protein architecture may cause epitope unmasking.4,5 Second, thiol-associated changes in the protein structure can promote antibody affinity and T-cell receptor epitope recognition via a hapten-like effect.S1 This may trigger an autoimmune response.

The occurrence of NELL-1–associated MN because of drugs is usually associated with a short period after drug initiation.1 However, in the study of Spain et al.,S2 1 patient was exposed to lipoic acid for over 3 years, and the diagnosis was established in another patient 36 months after traditional indigenous medicines in the publication of Kurien et al.S3 In addition, cessation of drugs usually led to disease remission in previous publications. However, additional immunosuppressives may be required to control the disease activity in NELL-1–associated MN.1,S3 Remission was achieved 21 months after discontinuation of penicillamine under corticosteroids and mycophenolate in our patient.

To our knowledge, this is the first case of NELL-1–associated MN induced by the long-term administration of penicillamine. NELL-1–associated MN should be considered in patients receiving short-term or long-term thiol-containing drugs.

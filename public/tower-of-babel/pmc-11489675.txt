Quantitative characterization of immune cells by measuring cellular signal transduction pathway activity
Authors: Wilbert Bouwman, Wim Verhaegh, Arie van Doorn, Reinier Raymakers, Tom van der Poll, Anja van de Stolpe
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFor many diseases, including cancer, infections, and auto-immune diseases, the immune response is a major determinant of disease progression, response to therapy, and clinical outcome. Innate and adaptive immune responses are controlled by coordinated activity of different immune cell types. The functional activity state of immune cells is determined by Signal Transduction Pathways (STPs). A recently developed technology (Simultaneous Transcriptome-based Activity Profiling of Signal Transduction Pathways, STAP-STP) enables simultaneous and quantitative activity measurement of relevant STPs in immune cells based on mRNA-analysis. STAP-STP technology was used to analyze public transcriptome data of a variety of immune cell types in resting and activated functional state. In addition, a clinical study on rheumatoid arthritis (RA) was analyzed to illustrate utility of the technology. Per sample, activity of androgen and estrogen receptor, PI3K, MAPK, TGFβ, Notch, NFκB, JAK-STAT1/2, and JAK-STAT3 STPs was calculated, generating an STP activity profile (SAP) consisting of 9 activity scores. Each analyzed immune cell type, i.e. naive/resting and immune-activated CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, T helper cells, B cells, NK cells, monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells, had a reproducible and characteristic SAP, reflecting both cell type and its activity state. Analysis of clinical RA samples revealed increased TGFβ STP activity in whole blood samples. In conclusion, STAP-STP technology enables quantitative measurement of the functional activity state of immune cells of the innate and adaptive immune system. Aside from diagnostic applications, utility lies in unravelling abnormal immune function in disease and immunomodulatory drug development.Keywords: Signal transduction pathways, Quantitative assessment, Immune response, Innate and adaptive immune systemSubject terms: Signal transduction, Adaptive immunity, Innate immune cells

Introduction

The immune response is determined by coordinated activity of different immune cell types belonging to the innate and adaptive immune system1. Immune cells communicate with each other using ligands that bind to membrane- or intracellular receptors (Fig. 1A). In this way, growth factor-, cytokine- and hormone-ligands can regulate the activity of STPs, such as PI3K-FOXO, MAPK, JAK-STAT1/2, JAK-STAT 3, NFκB, TGFβ, Notch, estrogen (ER) and androgen (AR) receptor STPs, necessary for control of important immune cell functions2–12.

So far it has not been possible to measure activity of these STPs in immune cells, leaving a gap in understanding their combined role in governing the immune response.

STAP-STP technology has been developed during the past 15 years to measure activity of STPs simultaneously on individual cell samples, using transcriptome information13–19. For each STP a validated mathematical model uses mRNA concentrations of target genes of the STP-associated transcription factor to calculate a quantitative STP activity13.

We used STAP-STP technology to improve our understanding of the role of STPs in immune cells, especially in the transition from resting to immune-activated state. STP Activity Profiles (SAPs) were determined for immune cells of the innate immune system, i.e. monocytes, macrophages, neutrophils and Natural Killer (NK) cells, for immune cells of the adaptive immune system, i.e. B cells, CD8 + T cells, CD4 + T cells including T helper1 (Th1), T helper2 (Th2) and Treg cells, and for Dendritic Cells (DCs) that bridge innate and adaptive immune response. To explore clinical value, SAPs were determined in blood samples of patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA).

Methods

Measurement of STP activity in immune cell samples

Development and validation of the STAP-STP technology for measuring activity of the ER, AR, PI3K-FOXO, MAPK, NFκB, TGFβ, Notch, JAK-STAT1/2, and JAK-STAT3 STPs have been described in detail before. In brief, for each STP, a Bayesian network-based probabilistic computational model calculates STP activity from mRNA levels of a defined set of high evidence direct target genes of the STP-associated transcription factor (Fig. 1B). The resulting Pathway Activity Score (PAS) is presented on a log2odds scale and quantitatively reflects STP activity13–15. Necessary target gene mRNA levels can be measured by different methods, such as microarray, RNA sequencing, and qPCR19. The technology can be used to reproducibly and quantitatively analyze signal transduction pathway activity in any cell type13–18 including blood cells18–21.

For the current study, PAS were measured for ER, AR, PI3K-FOXO, MAPK, NFκB, TGFβ, Notch, JAK-STAT1/2, and JAK-STAT3 STPs on Affymetrix Gene Chip® Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array gene expression data, downloaded from the public GEO database22. Of note, PI3K pathway activity is inversely related to the measured activity score of transcription factor FOXO (FOXO PAS)14. For this reason, FOXO PAS is presented in the figures, while interpretation with respect to activity of the PI3K STP is described in legends and text.

Microarray data quality control

Quality control (QC) was performed on Affymetrix (U133 Plus 2.0 Array) data of each individual sample, as described15. In summary, QC parameters include: the average value of all probe intensities, presence of negative or extremely high (> 16-bit) intensity values, poly-A RNA (sample preparation spike-ins), and labelled cRNA (hybridization spike ins) controls, GAPDH, and ACTB 3’/5’ ratio, center of intensity and values of positive and negative border controls determined by affyQCReport package in R, and an RNA degradation value determined by the AffyRNAdeg function from the Affymetrix package in R23,24. Sample data that failed QC were removed prior to data analysis, except for the neutrophil datasets that failed on RNA degradation QC parameter (but RNA Integrity Number in normal range), and the B cell dataset that failed on CMoff QC-parameter.

Determination of an STP activity profile for immune cell subtypes

The PAS for nine STPs enabled determination of a specific STP activity profile (SAP) for each analyzed immune cell type in naive/resting and activated state: monocytes, macrophages, neutrophils, NK cells, B cells, CD8 + T cells, CD4 + T cells, Th1, Th2, Treg cells, DCs. To enable optimal comparison between individual samples, PAS visualization is in table format. For some cell types, multiple datasets were available (indicated by an asterisk in the figures). This allowed comparison of SAP results between independent studies to assess reproducibility of the findings. Analysis results of these additional datasets are available in the Supplement.

Affymetrix microarray datasets from preclinical and clinical studies

Affymetrix U133Plus2.0 microarray datasets (publicly available) from preclinical and clinical studies, deposited in the GEO database, were analyzed22. For each sample the type of immune cell and functional state (e.g. resting/naive or activated) had been defined by the authors of the corresponding paper and was included in the sample annotation provided by GEO (Supplement Excel Table 1). Details on the procedures to activate the immune cells and confirm the obtained immune-activated state have been described in the publications associated with these GEO datasets (referenced per GEO study).

General rules for interpretation of signal transduction pathway activity score (PAS)

STAP-STP technology can be used to generate a SAP for each cell type. A few considerations to bear in mind when interpretating log2 odds PAS are15:

The log2 odds range for STP activity (minimum to maximum activity) may vary depending on the cell type. Once the range has been defined using samples with known STP activity, on each new sample the absolute value can be interpreted within the defined STP activity range. If the STP activity range has not been defined in a cell type, differences in log2 odds activity score between samples should be interpreted.

Results

To provide a basis for exploring the role of combined Signal Transduction Pathway (STP) activity in the immune response, activities of AR, ER, PI3K-FOXO, MAPK, JAK-STAT1/2, JAK-STAT3, NFκB, TGFβ, and Notch STPs were simultaneously determined on (individual sample) transcriptome data from studies on different immune cell types, available in the GEO database (Supplement Excel Table 1). The function of these STPs is not necessarily the same in all immune cell types, and may be determined by the cellular context, such as cellular origin (e.g. myeloid versus lymphoid) and crosstalk between active signaling pathways in a cell. This results in potentially different functions for activated STPs in myeloid- versus lymphoid-derived immune cell types. In general (simplified), PI3K and MAPK STPs control cell proliferation38,39, the NFκB pathway regulates cell division, inflammation and apoptosis40, the JAK-STAT pathways typically control specific immune cell functions; the JAK-STAT 1/2 STP mediates the response to interferons4; the TGFβ STP plays a role in immune-suppression and immune cell migration20,41; AR and Notch STPs are thought to play roles in immune-suppression20,42,43.

STAP-STP analysis generated a Signal Transduction Activity Profile (SAP) per analyzed immune cell sample, both in resting (naive) and immune-activated state. We first determined SAPs for immune cells that had been directly derived from healthy donors, representing the naive/resting state. Second, we determined for each immune cell type the change in SAP that occurred upon in vitro immune-activation. Finally, to illustrate potential clinical relevance, we analyzed peripheral blood-derived immune cells from patients with Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA).

Naive/resting state SAPs differ between immune cell types in peripheral blood

Dataset GSE72642 contained transcriptome data from all major immune cell types in blood, obtained from three healthy individuals25. SAPs in resting immune cells were remarkably similar between these individuals (n = 3) (Fig. 2A). This suggests strict regulation of STP activity in the resting immune system. Analysis of an independent 2nd dataset showed similar results (Supplement Fig. 1). Immune cells in blood arise from blood stem cells and belong to the myeloid (neutrophils and monocytes/macrophages) or lymphoid (B-cells, CD8 + T cells, CD4 + T cells, NK cells) lineage. SAPs were more similar for cell types within the same blood cell lineage (e.g. myeloid) than between lineages (myeloid versus lymphoid). Myeloid cell types had higher MAPK STP activity, while the PI3K STP was more active in lymphoid cell types, indicative of a lineage-specific growth factor pathway to control cell proliferation. Within these two blood lineages, each of the cell types had its own distinguishable SAP (Fig. 2A).

Resting state CD4 + T cells in blood of healthy individuals comprise several identifiable subsets. Important subsets are Treg and Teffector cells, both in naïve T cell state and as memory T cells44. Data from these subsets were available from dataset GSE6501035. Naive T cells had higher PI3K STP activity and lower TGFβ STP activity compared to memory T cells (Fig. 2B). Within the memory T cell group, Teffector cells had higher PI3K and NFκB PAS compared to Treg cells (Fig. 2B).

Dataset GSE28490 allowed analysis of two types of resting state Dendritic Cells (DCs), again directly obtained from peripheral blood of healthy individuals26. These DC subtypes have different functions, as pDCs produce type I IFNs in response to viruses, while mDCs produce IL-12 to induce T-helper 1 (Th1) and CD8 + T cell responses45. In line with this, pDCs and mDCs showed different SAPs, with most prominent differences in activity of the NFκB, AR, and JAK-STAT1/2 STPs (Fig. 2C).

In vitro immune-activation of different immune cell types leads to specific changes in STP activity

A number of GEO datasets (Supplement Table 1) allowed investigation of changes in STP activity induced by in vitro immune-activation. Data were available for cell types of the innate immune system, i.e. NK cells, monocytes and macrophages, and of cell types of the adaptive immune system. i.e. CD4 + T cells, and CD8 + T cells.

With respect to the innate immune cells, in the available dataset (GSE15743) primary NK cells had been activated in vitro with two doses of IFNα27. IFNs are typical activators of the JAK-STAT1/2 STP. As expected activity of this STP increased4. In addition, activity of the JAK-STAT3 STP increased, while activity of the PI3K (inversely related to FOXO activity14), NFκB and Notch STPs decreased (Fig. 3A, Supplement Fig. 2). In the in vitro neutrophil study (GSE22103), cells had been activated using either LPS or GM-CSF in addition to IFN-γ28. Either activation protocol resulted in activation of PI3K, NFκB, JAK-STAT and TGFβ STPs (Fig. 3B). Of all investigated immune cell types, activated neutrophils had highest TGFβ PAS. The observed high NFκB activity in these cells is indicative of the role of activated neutrophils in inflammation. For the monocyte study (GSE38351), activity of the JAK-STAT1/2 STP was reported before but is included here for completeness sake18. Monocyte activation was universally associated with activation of TGFβ, JAK-STAT1/2 and JAK-STAT3 STPs (Fig. 3C). Activation using IFNs resulted in the expected increase in JAK-STAT1/2 PAS. Activation of the NFκB pathway by LPS and TNFα highlights the prominent role of this STP as mediator of inflammation46,47. Macrophages originate from circulating monocytes and are tissue-resident cells. Analysis of a macrophage activation study (GSE43596) showed that incubation of macrophages with LPS decreased growth factor STP activity (PI3K and MAPK), while increasing NFκB, JAK-STAT, TGFβ, and Notch PAS (Fig. 3D)30.

With respect to cell types of the adaptive immune response, STP analysis results of activated CD4 + T cells and their Th1 and Th2 cell derivatives (GSE7156631) were (partially) reported before20 (Fig. 4A, Supplement Fig. 3A). To summarize here: activation of CD4 + T cells was associated with increased PI3K (decreased FOXO activity, indicative of proliferation), NFκB, and JAK-STAT STP activity and reduced TGFβ STP activity. During differentiation of activated CD4 + T cells to Th1 cells NFκB STP activity went up, while activity of this STP decreased during differentiation to Th2 cells. This defined a major difference between Th1 and Th2 cell types. Notably, an anti-IFNγ antibody had been used to induce Th2 differentiation, and this was associated with an expected reduction in JAK-STAT1/2 STP PAS. CD8 + T cells are normally activated (and start to proliferate) by recognition of highly specific antigens; this is difficult to mimic in vitro. Only one small dataset (GSE63129) was available in GEO, nevertheless providing interesting insights32. Antigen-specific activation, in contrast to non-specific antigen stimulation, was associated with the highest PI3K (lower FOXO activity) STP activity, indicating rapid initiation of proliferation. In the presence of immunosuppressive Treg cells, PI3K STP activity was lower (higher FOXO activity), suggesting hampered proliferation (Fig. 4B). This suggests that maximal proliferative capacity (PI3K STP) of CD8 + T cells is dependent on activation with specific antigen and absence of immunosuppressive Treg cells. In contrast, NFκB STP activity was higher in CD8 + T cells activated with non-specific antibody, suggesting a specific role for this STP, e.g. in inflammation or apoptosis. The B-cell activation study (GSE39411) contained data of B cells activated with non-specific IgM antibody33. Increased PI3K STP activity, indicative of proliferation, was associated with increased Notch and JAK-STAT (1/2 and 3) pathway activity (Fig. 4C and Supplement Fig. 4).

DCs function as the bridge between innate and adaptive immune system. To study DC activation, a model system based on in vitro infection with the Newcastle Disease virus was developed34. STP analysis was performed on the associated dataset (GSE1879134). DC activation rapidly resulted in an increase in NFκB PAS (within a few hours), followed by an increase in JAK-STAT1/2 PAS, and a relatively late increase in TGFβ PAS (after approximately 8 h) (Fig. 5 and Supplement Fig. 5). This is a logical order of STP activation since activity of the NFκB pathway is required for antigen processing; NFκB-induced production of IFNs is responsible for activation of the JAK-STAT1/2 pathway, necessary for antigen presentation48; final activation of the TGFβ pathway is likely required for migration of activated DCs to the lymph node49.

STP PAS show tight control of STP activity in immune cells

SAP profiles were highly consistent for blood-derived immune cell types, both within sample groups and between independent studies from different laboratories. This underscores the reproducibility of the STAP-STP technology, while at the same time implying tight control of functional immune cell processes (Figs. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6). Naturally, differences in laboratory protocols for immune cell activation were reflected in differences in STP activity scores (e.g. Supplement Fig. 2).

Abnormal STP activity in blood of rheumatoid arthritis patients

Quantifying the functional state of immune cells is of value for clinical diagnostics and drug development. To illustrate the potential of the STAP-STP technology, a clinical study was analyzed containing whole blood sample data from untreated patients with RA36. TGFβ PAS was increased in the RA patient group, and JAK-STAT3 PAS was increased in the female RA patient group (Fig. 6). Indeed, both TGFβ and JAK-STAT3 are known to have a pathogenic role in rheumatoid arthritis and are considered RA drug targets50,51. The observed large variation in STP activity across the patient group suggests the presence of RA subtypes that respond differently to treatment. Such RA subtypes can be identified using STAP-STP technology.

STAP-STP technology enables translation of complex transcriptome data to activity of clinically relevant cellular processes

We compared our results with the transcriptome data analysis reported in the publications coupled to the GEO datasets (see Supplement Information). Various publicly available bioinformatics tools (such as Gene Set Enrichment Analysis, GSEA) had been used by the original investigators, resulting in sample group-associated gene profiles and suggestions for involvement of a variety of cellular processes. Analysis of individual sample transcriptome data and activity of STPs was not reported, confirming the unique capability of the STAP-STP technology.

Discussion

Signal Transduction Pathway (STP) analysis of transcriptome data of a number of immune cell types revealed characteristic STP Activity Profiles (SAP) for each analyzed immune cell type in resting and immune-activated state. Each SAP consists of a series of STP activity scores (Pathways Activity Score, PAS) of the ER, AR, PI3K, MAPK, NFκB, JAK-STAT1/2, JAK-STAT3, TGFβ and Notch STPs. SAP results were consistent over multiple independent datasets for the same immune cell type. This is the first time that immune cells have been functionally characterized by measuring activity of nine STPs. All computational STP models used to calculate the STP activity scores were validated before on multiple cell types with known STP activity13–16,18,20.

Interestingly, immune cell SAPs were highly similar for multiple healthy individuals, indicative of strict regulation of signaling pathway activity in immune cells. Each of the myeloid- and lymphoid-derived immune cell types had its own characteristic SAP, reflecting functional differences between the immune cell types. For example, our results suggest that the PI3K STP is the dominant growth factor pathway in lymphoid cells, while the MAPK STP is important to regulate proliferation of myeloid cells. The differences in STP activity between the various immune cell types are in line with blood lineage-specific immune cell functions and cellular crosstalk between STPs41,46,52–54. For several STPs, specific functions in immune cells have been previously described49,55.

Our observation that PI3K STP activity in CD4 + and CD8 + T cells increased upon activation is in line with the role of this signaling pathway to induce clonal proliferation of activated T cells3. Activation of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells was associated with decreased TGFβ STP activity. This is in agreement with our earlier finding that an active TGFβ pathway in CD4 + T cells is associated with an immunotolerant state20. In contrast to lymphoid cells, activation of myeloid cells led to increased TGFβ STP activity. Activated neutrophils and macrophages have inflammatory and tumor promoting roles, suggesting involvement of the TGFβ pathway in these functions56–58. Thus, our current findings provide additional support for an immunosuppressive and potentially tumor-promoting role of the TGFβ STP in immune cells6,49,59,. The active NFκB pathway in myeloid cell types supports its prominent inflammatory role46,47. In the different lymphoid cell types, changes in NFκB STP activity may be coupled to cellular processes such as proliferation, migration, antigen processing, and apoptosis, dependent on cellular context46,47. Notch STPs have multiple context-dependent functions in the innate and adaptive immune response, and are involved in immune cell differentiation55,60. Activity of this STP increased in activated B cells, in line with its described role in these cells55. Highest Notch pathway activity was found in myeloid cell types, underpinning its role in inflammatory responses61. Activity of the JAK-STAT STPs frequently changed upon immune cell activation. JAK-STAT1/2 STP activity was logically induced by the presence of IFNs, as prototypical ligands for this pathway48,53. This is in line with our previous report on increased JAK-STAT1/2 pathway activity in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) during viral infection, which is a condition associated with strong IFN production18. In the same study, high JAK-STAT3 pathway activity appeared to be associated with clinically severe infection18. We hypothesize that increased JAK-STAT3 pathway activity, measured in activated monocytes (and macrophages), reflects “trained immunity”. “Trained immunity” has been described as a strong inflammatory state with increased IL-6 levels, known to activate the JAK-STAT3 STP62,63. Such massive inflammation has been associated with worse outcome of both viral (e.g. in Covid-19) and bacterial infections, in agreement with our JAK-STAT3 STP activity findings64,65. AR pathway activity increased upon activation of monocytes. This is an intriguing finding in view of the presumed immunosuppressive function for this pathway11,12,43. We previously reported increased AR pathway activity in patients with bacterial sepsis, in which we hypothesize its immunosuppressive role in sepsis21,66.

DC activation resulted in sequential activation of NFκB, JAK-STAT1/2 and TGFβ STPs. In this sequence of events, the NFκB pathway is activated to enable antigen processing and production of IFNs67. IFNs are responsible for secondary activation of the JAK-STAT1/2 pathway, which facilitates antigen cross-presentation and T-cell activation48. For the TGFβ pathway, we hypothesize that activation enables DC migration41. For migratory function, only a small increase in TGFβ pathway activity is required, as observed here. In contrast, high TGFβ STP activity in DCs is thought to have an immunosuppressive function21,68.

Following STP activity profiling of the various immune cell types in a ‘healthy’ state, we analyzed data from patients with rheumatoid arthritis36. The identified abnormal activity of TGFβ and JAK-STAT3 STPs is in agreement with their mechanistic role in RA50,51. However, these STPs had not been identified in this dataset using conventional bioinformatics tools, emphasizing the unicity of the STAP-STP technology36. The variation in STP activity observed across RA patients suggests the presence of multiple clinical subtypes, and provides an explanation for the known variation in clinical response to therapies.

In summary, we show that STAP-STP technology uniquely enables extraction of biologically and clinically relevant information on STP activity from transcriptome data, in line with our previous reports13,18–21. This presents a first step towards a new type of immune response profiling to obtain mechanistic insight in normal and abnormal functioning of the immune system, as a basis for development of precision medicine.

Repeated quantum backflow and overflow
Christopher J. Fewster, Harkan J. Kirk-Karakaya

Abstract
Quantum backflow is a surprising phenomenon in which a quantum particle, moving in one dimension and with a state of rightwards momentum, can exhibit a net probability transfer to the left-hand half-line over a finite time interval. We generalise the setting of quantum backflow to allow for M M disjoint time intervals, considering the sum of probability differences for each interval. In classical statistical particle mechanics, the total backflow lies in the interval [ − 1 , 0 ] [-1,0] for all M M , indicating rightwards probability transfer. By contrast, we show that, in quantum mechanics, the maximum M M -fold backflow is positive and unbounded from above as M M increases, demonstrating that there are states that exhibit repeated periods of backflow. Moreover, for M ≥ 2 M\geq 2 , we discover a new phenomenon; namely, that there are states whose total backflow is below − 1 -1 , giving a probability transfer to the right-hand half-line beyond that possible in classical statistical particle mechanics. We call this effect ‘quantum overflow’. The maximum extent of the backflow and overflow effects is described by a hierarchy of backflow and overflow functions and constants, where the M M ’th backflow (overflow) constant is the supremum (infimum) of the M M ’th backflow (overflow) function. The M = 1 M=1 backflow constant was first identified by Bracken and Melloy. Our results are obtained by formulating the M M -fold backflow problem in terms of the spectra of suitable bounded operators. Using this formulation, we also study limiting cases of the backflow and overflow functions, including cases in which two disjoint intervals merge. Our analytical results are supported by detailed numerical investigations. Among other things, by applying numerical acceleration methods, we obtain a new estimate of the Bracken–Melloy constant of 0.0384506 0.0384506 which is slightly lower than the previously accepted value of 0.038452 0.038452 .

Introduction

Quantum backflow (QB) is a counterintuitive phenomenon in which a free quantum mechanical particle on the line, in a state with purely rightwards momentum, can exhibit a temporary probability transfer to the left-hand half-line. Here, we understand ‘purely rightwards momentum’ or ‘purely positive momentum’ to mean that the measured value of the momentum would be nonnegative with probability
1
, or equivalently that the support of the wavefunction in momentum space is contained in the positive half-line. The existence of
states exhibiting backflow has been known since at least 1969, when Allcock
[
1
]
showed that there exist states with purely positive momentum whose quantum mechanical current at the origin can be arbitrarily negative.

The first detailed study of QB was conducted in 1994 by Bracken and Melloy
[
10
]
. They showed that for states that exhibit QB the probability backflow is bounded by a state independent dimensionless value
0<c_{\textnormal{BM}}<1
, independent of
\hbar
, which they calculated to be
c_{\textnormal{BM}}\approx 0.04

[
10
]
. There have since been two further calculations of
c_{\textnormal{BM}}
to date, first by Eveson, Fewster and Verch (
c_{\textnormal{BM}}\approx 0.03845(2)
)
[
16
]
and shortly thereafter by Penz
et al.
(
c_{\textnormal{BM}}\approx 0.0384517)

[
28
]
. The first non-trivial upper bound on the backflow constant was calculated only recently by Trillo, Le and Navascués to be
c_{\textnormal{BM}}<0.072

[
38
]
. Due to the small value of
c_{\textnormal{BM}}
, experimental verification of QB is an ongoing effort. Goussev has shown that when considering an angular analogue of QB for a particle on a ring, the associated maximum possible backflow is larger than in the linear case with
c_{\textnormal{ring}}\approx 2.6\>c_{\textnormal{BM}}

[
17
]
. Angular QB has also been studied in the case of a charged massless fermion
[
13
]
. Palmero
et al.
have shown how QB could be experimentally verified by measuring density fluctuations in a Bose-Einstein condensate
[
27
]
. Bracken has explored a whole family of QB related problems where the quantum states considered have their momentum restricted to
p>p_{0}
for some fixed
p_{0}
not necessarily positive
[
9
]
. Other variations of QB that have been considered include but are not limited to: QB in two dimensions
[
3
]
, QB in a relativistic setting
[
2
]
, the relation between QB and the local wave number
[
5
]
and QB across a black hole horizon
[
7
]
. Finally, Bostelmann, Cadamuro and Lechner
[
8
]
studied the extent to which scattering states exhibit the backflow effect for general short-range potentials, while Yearsley and Halliwell showed that the dependency of the maximal QB on
\hbar
reappears
[
42
]
once a realistic measurement apparatus is considered.

Previously, QB has only ever been considered over a single fixed time interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
. As first shown by Bracken and Melloy, the associated spectrum of backflow values is independent of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}

[
10
]
. In this paper (following a suggestion by Reinhard Werner) we will consider the question of quantum backflow over multiple disjoint time intervals. Given an integer
M\geq 1
and a strictly increasing list of times

\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle=\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{2M}\rangle
,
we study the total backflow

\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2j})-\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2j-1})\right)

(1.1)

exhibited by quantum state
\psi
over the union of the time intervals

[t_{1},t_{2}],...,[t_{2M-1},t_{2M}]
. A basic question is whether or not the total backflow is also limited by the Bracken–Melloy constant, or whether increasing
M
can increase the amount of backflow beyond
c_{\textnormal{BM}}
. In other words, are there states which exhibit
repeated periods of significant backflow? Further, as a sum of
M
probability differences,
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)
is,
a priori
, bounded between
\pm M
. Can the maximum total backflow grow unboundedly as
M\to\infty
? Are there states in which one finds

\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)<-1
– i.e., in which
more than unit probability is transferred to the right-hand half-line, in total, over the given intervals? If so, is this effect unbounded as
M\to\infty
? Are the answers to the previous questions different for classical particle mechanics?

We will answer all these question affirmatively.
For
M
time intervals of equal duration, separated by gaps of the same duration, we show that there are positive-momentum states
\psi_{M}
so that

\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi_{M})\to\infty
, at least as fast
as
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
. Moreover, we discover the new phenomenon that, for
M\geq 2
, there are indeed states with
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)<-1
, an effect that we call
quantum overflow
. For the intervals of equal duration separated by gaps of the same duration, we will prove that there are positive-momentum states
\varphi_{M}

with
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\varphi_{M})\to-\infty
, at least as fast as
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
. Thus, both quantum backflow and overflow are unbounded in the limit of large
M
. By contrast,
the total backflow of a classical particle is constrained to
[-1,0]
, independent of
M
. Our analytical results are complemented by a
numerical investigation of the magnitude of the backflow and overflow effects for
M\leq 4
.

In more detail, we show in Section
2
that, for all
M
, the classical analogue of
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
is constrained to the negative unit interval
[-1,0]
for classical statistical ensembles with positive momenta. Passing to quantum mechanics, a straightforward adaptation of arguments in
[
10
,
19
]
show that for each strictly increasing list of times
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle
, there is a self-adjoint bounded operator
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
so that

\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)=\innerproduct{\hat{\psi}}{B^{(M)}_{\boldvec[M]{t}}\hat{\psi}}

(1.2)

for normalised positive-momentum state
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, where
\hat{\psi}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
is the Fourier transform of
\psi
, i.e., its momentum representation. It follows that the set of possible values for the total backflow is the numerical range of the operator
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
, and that the supremum and infimum of these values are given by the maximum and minimum points of the spectrum
\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
. The unitary equivalence of operators
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
under simultaneous uniform translation of all times, and under simultaneous uniform dilation, implies that the maximum and minimum of
\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
are functions of the successive ratios of successive differences of the
t_{j}
’s, i.e., one has bounds

b_{\textnormal{over}}^{(M)}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle)\leq\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)\leq b_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle)

(1.3)

for all positive-momentum states
\psi
,
where

\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle=((t_{3}-t_{2})/(t_{2}-t_{1}),\ldots,(t_{2M}-t_{2M-1})/(t_{2M-1}-t_{2M-2}))

(1.4)

and
b_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}:\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}\to\mathbb{R}
are
backflow/overflow functions
defined so that

b_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle)=\max\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}),\qquad b_{\textnormal{over}}^{(M)}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle)=\min\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}).

(1.5)

The supremum and infimum of
b_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}
and
b_{\textnormal{over}}^{(M)}
over
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
define sequences of backflow
and overflow constants,
c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}
and
c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}
. For
M=1
one has
c^{(1)}_{\textnormal{back}}=c_{\textnormal{BM}}
and
c^{(1)}_{\textnormal{over}}=-1
.

Next, in Section
3
, we prove two key results regarding backflow and overflow. First, we show that the backflow and overflow constants satisfy the bounds

c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}\leq Mc_{\textnormal{BM}},\qquad c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}\geq-1-(M-1)c_{\textnormal{BM}}

(1.6)

(see Theorem
3.1
for the precise statement and more detail). Second, by considering the example of equal duration intervals separated by gaps of the same duration, we prove that

c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}\to\infty,\qquad c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}\to-\infty

(1.7)

as
M\to\infty
, at least as fast as
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
. See Theorem
3.2
for the detailed statement.

In Section
4
, we study various limiting cases of the backflow functions as some of their parameters are taken to zero or infinity.
The main result here, Theorem
4.1
, provides an understanding of the relation between backflow and overflow functions for different
M
, and a proof that the backflow and overflow constants are monotonic in
M
, and obey

c^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{back}}\geq-1-c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}},\qquad c^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{over}}\leq-1-c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}

(1.8)

for all
M,L\in\mathbb{N}
.
Various consequences are drawn. In particular it is shown that when two time intervals merge, the spectra of the corresponding backflow operators do not converge to the spectrum of the backflow operator for the merged intervals. This is related to the fact that the backflow operators only converge in the strong topology rather than in norm topology. However, if a fixed momentum cutoff is implemented, the spectra do converge.
Therefore, to see the true behaviour in numerical tests, it would be necessary for the cutoff to rise as the intervals became closer to merging.

In Section
5
, we present a numerical investigation of
the situation of
M
equally spaced backflow periods of equal length. In particular, we aim to estimate the
values of
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}(1,1,\ldots,1)
for
1\leq M\leq 4
. Our method is to study the compression
of the appropriate
M
-fold backflow operator to the
(N+1)
-dimensional subspace

V_{N,a,\delta}=\textnormal{span}(\psi_{0,a,\delta},\dots,\psi_{N,a,\delta})
, where
\psi_{n,a,\delta}(p)\propto p^{n+\delta}e^{-ap}
are normalised

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dp)
vectors depending on parameters

a
,
\delta
. The maximum/minimum eigenvalue
\lambda_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}(a,\delta;N)
of the compression can be obtained by solving a generalised eigenvalue problem relative to the Gram matrix of

\psi_{0,a,\delta},\dots,\psi_{N,a,\delta}
; high precision is required in the calculation because
the minimum eigenvalue of the Gram matrix tends to
0
geometrically as
N\to\infty
. Fortunately, we are able to derive closed form expressions for all the matrix elements required in terms of special functions implemented in the arbitrary precision library FLINT
[
37
]
. The upshot is that the values

\lambda_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}(a,\delta;N)
are
computed accurate to tens (and sometimes hundreds) of decimal places.

For choices of
a
and
\delta
that we motivate, we obtain sequences
\lambda_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}(N)
for
1\leq M\leq 4
and
N\leq 500
. Up to numerical precision, these sequences converge monotonically to

b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}(1,1,\ldots,1)
as
N\to\infty
, thus providing rigorous bounds on these quantities. The sequences converge quite slowly, but
numerical acceleration techniques can be used to estimate the limiting values (albeit losing the guarantee of monotone convergence).
Our acceleration techniques assume that
\lambda_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}(N)
has an asymptotic expansion in powers of
N^{-1/2}
– this is supported by our results, except in the case of
\lambda_{\textnormal{over}}^{(1)}
, which converges rapidly to
c_{\textnormal{over}}^{(1)}=-1
.

The acceleration methods lead to conjectured upper and lower bounds for
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}(1,1,\ldots,1)
which can be found in Tables
5.5
and
5.7
.
In particular, our conjectured value for
c_{\textnormal{BM}}
is
0.0384506
to 6 significant figures. We will discuss the relation to earlier calculations of
[
10
,
16
]
in Section
5.3
.

We also compute the normalised eigenvectors
\psi_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}(N;\cdot)\in V_{N,a,\delta}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dp)
of the compressed backflow operators corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}(N)
. For
M=1
the overflow eigenvectors
\psi_{\textnormal{over}}^{(1)}(N;\cdot)
resemble Gaussians roughly peaked at
1.5N
and with broadening width – see Figure
5.11
.
We argue that this behaviour is in line with a result proved in
[
19
]
that
-1
lies in the essential spectrum of the single backflow operator. The remaining eigenvectors have an oscillatory structure that becomes more complex as
M
increases, and appear to decay as
\mathcal{O}(p^{-3/4})
for
p\to\infty
. The backflow eigenvectors also diverge as
p\to 0
, apparently as
\mathcal{O}(p^{-1/4})
. We present plots of
\psi_{\textnormal{back/over}}^{(M)}(N;p)
for
N=500
, where in most cases the wavefunctions are multiplied by a factor
p^{3/4}
to remove the decay at large momentum, thus more clearly showing the oscillatory structure. Our results also suggest that
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(1,\ldots,1)
for
1\leq M\leq 4
and
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(1,\ldots,1)
for
2\leq M\leq 4
are isolated eigenvalues of the
M
-fold backflow operator.

We conclude in Section
6
with a summary and outlook. The appendices give technical details deferred from the main text.

2.1
Classical statistical mechanics

Consider a classical ensemble of particles
\mathcal{C}
of mass
\mu
under free evolution on the line.
Let the distribution of particles on phase space
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
at time
t
be given by a probability measure
\rho_{t}
so that a randomly chosen member of the ensemble has phase space position in Borel subset
S\subseteq\mathcal{P}
at time
t
with probability

\text{Prob}_{\rho}((X,P)\in S\mid t)=\rho_{t}(S),

(2.1)

where we write
\rho=\rho_{0}
for the state at time
t=0
. The ensemble average of observable
f\in C(\mathcal{P})
at time
t
is given by

\int f\,d\rho_{t}=\int f\circ\tau_{t}\,d\rho
by Liouville’s theorem, where

\tau_{t}(x,p)=(x+pt/\mu,p)

(2.2)

is the
forwards Hamiltonian time evolution through time
t
. Correspondingly, we find that
\rho_{t}(S)=\rho(\tau_{t}^{-1}(S))
. If the ensemble only contains particles with non-negative momentum then one has

\rho(S)=\rho(S\cap(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))

(2.3)

for any Borel subset
S\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, and consequently,

\displaystyle\text{Prob}_{\rho}(X\in(-\infty,0)\mid t)

\displaystyle=\rho_{t}((-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\rho(\tau^{-1}_{t}((-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R}))\cap(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))

\displaystyle=\text{Prob}_{\rho}((X,P)\in\{(x,p)\in\mathcal{P}:x<-pt/\mu,~p\geq 0\}).

(2.4)

The difference between the probabilities of finding a randomly chosen particle on the left-hand half line at time
t^{\prime}
and an earlier time
t
is therefore

\text{Prob}_{\rho}(X\in(-\infty,0)\mid t^{\prime})-\text{Prob}_{\rho}(X\in(-\infty,0)\mid t)=-\text{Prob}_{\rho}((X,P)\in S_{t,t^{\prime}}),

(2.5)

where

S_{t,t^{\prime}}=\{(x,p)\in\mathcal{P}:-pt^{\prime}/\mu\leq x<-pt/\mu,~p\geq 0\}

(2.6)

for any
t^{\prime}>t
. Note that we may replace
p\geq 0
by
p>0
in the formula
S_{t,t^{\prime}}
without loss
because
S_{t,t^{\prime}}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times\{0\})=\emptyset
.
Accordingly,
-\rho(S_{t,t^{\prime}})
measures the probability backflow between times
t
and
t^{\prime}
; as it is clearly nonpositive, we see that there is no classical backflow.

Now consider adding together the backflow for a set of disjoint time intervals. Specifically,
given
M\in\mathbb{N}
and times
t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<t_{2M-1}<t_{2M}
, the total amount of probability backflow is given by

\displaystyle\Delta^{(M)}_{\textnormal{classical}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(\text{Prob}_{\rho}(X\in(-\infty,0)\mid t_{2j})-\text{Prob}_{\rho}(X\in(-\infty,0)\mid t_{2j-1})\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\text{Prob}_{\rho}(X\in S_{t_{2j-1},t_{2j}})=-\text{Prob}_{\rho}\left(X\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{M}S_{t_{2j-1},t_{2j}}\right)

because the sets
S_{t_{2j-1},t_{2j}}
are disjoint
for distinct
j
.

It follows that, for a classical ensemble with non-negative momentum, the total amount of probability backflow over multiple disjoint time periods is bounded by

-1\leq\Delta^{(M)}_{\textnormal{classical}}(\rho)\leq 0

(2.8)

for every
M
. In particular, a classical ensemble cannot exhibit positive probability backflow, and the probability transfer
in the forward direction is bounded by unity. By contrast, it is well known that quantum particles can exhibit backflow over a single time interval, thus violating the upper bound in the analogue of (
2.8
). One of the main results of this paper is to see that the violation increases with
M
, and that the lower bound of (
2.8
) is also violated in quantum theory for
M\geq 2
.

2.2
Quantum mechanics

We turn to the quantum case, using units in which
\hbar=1
. Consider the motion of a free quantum particle of mass
\mu
with normalised state vector
\psi_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
at time
t
, whose dynamics
are governed by the free Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\psi_{t}=-\frac{1}{2\mu}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{t}

(2.9)

and initial condition
\psi_{0}=\psi
, so
\psi_{t}=e^{-ip^{2}t}\psi
, where
p=-i\partial_{x}
and we have chosen units so that

\mu=\tfrac{1}{2}
, as we shall do from now on for simplicity. The states with nonnegative momentum form the subspace

\mathscr{H}_{+}=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx):\supp\hat{\psi}\subseteq[0,\infty)\},

(2.10)

where

\hat{\psi}(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,e^{-ipx}\psi(x)

(2.11)

is the Fourier transform of
\psi
.

Let
t_{1}<t_{2}
. The probability backflow exhibited by the normalised
t=0
state
\psi
between times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
is quantified by

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi):=\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0\mid t_{2})-\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0\mid t_{1}).

(2.12)

where
\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X\in S\mid t)=\int_{S}dx\,|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2}
is
the probability of measuring the position of the particle to lie in
S
at time
t
. There are simple examples of
states
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
for which
\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)>0
, showing that quantum mechanical particles can exhibit nontrivial backflow (see e.g.,
[
20
]
)
whereas the classical backflow quantity for particles always lies in
[-1,0]
.

We are interested in the range of values assumed by

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)
as
\psi
varies over
normalised states of
\mathscr{H}_{+}
. As a difference of
probabilities, its range is contained in
[-1,1]
. Moreover,
because
\mathscr{H}_{+}
is invariant under both the free time evolution and dilations, the range is invariant
under
\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle\mapsto\langle t_{1}+\tau,t_{2}+\tau\rangle
and

\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle\mapsto\langle\lambda t_{1},\lambda t_{2}\rangle
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and all
\lambda>0
.
It follows that the range is independent of both
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, provided
t_{1}<t_{2}
, as was observed by Bracken and Melloy
[
10
]
.
As noted in
[
19
]
, Dollard’s lemma (Lemma 4 of
[
15
]
) shows that

\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0\mid t_{1})\to 1
as
t_{1}\to-\infty
and

\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0\mid t_{2})\to 0
as
t_{2}\to+\infty

for any fixed
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
, thus giving

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)\to-1
when both limits
are taken. Thus
-1
belongs to the closure of the
range of
\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
for any
t_{1}<t_{2}
.

More detailed information can be found by reformulating backflow in terms of operators. For normalised
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
, a calculation due to Bracken and Melloy
[
10
]
gives
\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)
as a quadratic form

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\>\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\>\frac{e^{i(p^{2}-q^{2})t_{2}}-e^{i(p^{2}-q^{2})t_{1}}}{p-q}\hat{\psi}^{\ast}(p)\hat{\psi}(q),

(2.13)

which can be written as

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)=\innerproduct{\hat{\psi}}{B_{\langle t_1,t_2\rangle} \hat{\psi}}.

(2.14)

Here, the operator
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
has the following properties, which are
established in Appendix
A
, drawing on arguments in
[
28
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
t_{1}<t_{2}
,
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
is
a bounded self-adjoint operator with

\|B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}\|=1
. There is a unitary equivalence between
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
and the operator
C\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq))
with action

(C\varphi)(p)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\frac{\sin(p-q)}{p-q}\Big[\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{1/4}+\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{-1/4}\Big]\varphi(q)

(2.15)

on
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
,
where (
2.15
) holds pointwise almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. The map
(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}

is strongly continuous on
\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t_{1}<t_{2}\}
.

The unitary equivalence in Theorem
2.1
is a combination of translations, scale transformation and a change of variables.
It follows from (
2.14
) that the range of
\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)
, as
\psi
varies over the normalised
elements of
\mathscr{H}_{+}
, is the
numerical range
of the operator
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
. We recall the definition and main properties of numerical range (see e.g., Section 9.3 of
[
12
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
. The
numerical range

\mathcal{N}(A)\subset\mathbb{R}
of
A
is given by

\mathcal{N}(A)=\left\{\frac{\langle\phi|A\phi\rangle}{\langle\phi|\phi\rangle}\big|\phi\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

(2.16)

One has
\mathcal{N}(A)=\mathcal{N}(UAU^{\ast})
for any unitary
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})
, and

\sigma(A)\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{N}(A)}=\Conv\sigma(A),

(2.17)

where
\overline{\,\cdot\,}
denotes topological closure and
\Conv
the convex hull. In particular
\sigma(A)
and

\mathcal{N}(A)
have the same supremum and infimum.

It follows immediately that, for all
t_{1}<t_{2}
,

\overline{\mathcal{N}(B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle})}=\Conv\sigma(C)\subseteq[-1,1],

(2.18)

using
\|C\|=1
. We have already noted that
-1\in\overline{\mathcal{N}(B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle})}
. Defining
the
Bracken–Melloy constant
as the largest spectral point of
C
,

c_{\textnormal{BM}}=\sup\sigma(C)=\max\sigma(C),

(2.19)

the possible values of the backflow
\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)
obey

-1\leq\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)\leq c_{\textnormal{BM}}

(2.20)

for all normalised
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
.

These ideas are readily generalised to consider the total backflow over
M\geq 1
disjoint time intervals
[t_{2j-1},t_{2j}]
for
1\leq j\leq M
, which can be represented by a list
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle=\langle t_{1},...,t_{2M}\rangle
in strictly increasing order.

Definition 2.3
.

For positive integer
M
, let

\mathcal{T}_{M}=\left\{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle=\langle t_{1},...,t_{2M}\rangle\big|t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<t_{2M-1}<t_{2M}\right\}.

(2.21)

The total probability backflow exhibited by the time-zero state
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
over the intervals parameterised by
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{M}
is defined as

\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi):=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2j})-\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2j-1})\right),

(2.22)

so that, for example,
\Delta^{(1)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{1}\rangle}(\psi)=\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)
.

For normalised
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
, one has

\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)=\innerproduct{\hat{\psi}}{B^{(M)}_{\boldvec[M]{t}} \hat{\psi}},

(2.23)

where the
M
-fold backflow operator

B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}B_{\langle t_{2j},t_{2j-1}\rangle}

(2.24)

is a bounded self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
acting as

\big(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}\phi\big)(p)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\>\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{e^{i(p^{2}-q^{2})t_{2k}}-e^{i(p^{2}-q^{2})t_{2k-1}}}{p-q}\phi(q).

(2.25)

In the same way as before, the set of possible values taken by
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)
for normalised
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
is the numerical range of
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
and can be studied via its spectrum. As before, if
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)>0
, we say that the state exhibits quantum backflow. We will also investigate states
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
for which
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)<-1
for
M\geq 2
,
a phenomenon we will call
quantum overflow
.

The following straightforward argument shows the existence of states exhibiting quantum overflow with
M=2
. Fix
t_{1}<t_{2}
and suppose
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{+}
is such that
\Delta^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)=\lambda>0
, i.e., there is nontrivial backflow between
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. Taking
T>\max(|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|)
, one can write the total backflow over
[-T,t_{1}]\cup[t_{2},T]
as

\Delta_{\langle-T,t_{1},t_{2},T\rangle}^{(2)}(\psi)=\Delta_{\langle-T,T\rangle}^{(1)}(\psi)-\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}^{(1)}(\psi).

(2.26)

For any fixed
\epsilon\in(0,\lambda)
, one can find
T
sufficiently large such that
\Delta^{(1)}_{\langle-T,T\rangle}(\psi)<-1+\epsilon
, because Dollard’s lemma
[
15
]
implies that
\Delta^{(1)}_{\langle-T,T\rangle}(\psi)\to-1
as
T\to\infty
.
It follows that

\Delta_{\langle-T,t_{1},t_{2},T\rangle}^{(2)}(\psi)<-1-\lambda+\epsilon<-1,

(2.27)

showing that the state
\psi
exhibits quantum overflow on
[-T,t_{1}]\cup[t_{2},T]
.
As
\psi
can be chosen to make
\lambda
arbitrarily close to
c_{\textnormal{BM}}
and
\epsilon
could be chosen arbitrarily small, this already shows that

2
-fold overflow can approach
-1-c_{\textnormal{BM}}
over suitable intervals.
In this example, we chose the time interval after fixing the state, without any control on the size of
T
.
However, in Section
3.2
, we will consider
M
equally spaced intervals of equal width, and show that the total
M
-fold overflow can be made unboundedly negative as
M
increases, and similarly that the
M
-fold backflow can be made unboundedly positive as
M
increases.

The invariance of
\mathscr{H}_{+}
under time evolution and dilations does not completely fix
\mathcal{N}(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
if
M>1
. However, time translation invariance shows that

\mathcal{N}(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
is a function
of the successive differences
(t_{2}-t_{1},t_{3}-t_{2},\ldots,t_{2M}-t_{2M-1})
, and the scaling invariance shows that
it may be expressed in terms of successive ratios of these differences.
For each
M>1
, then, there are
backflow and overflow functions

b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}:\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}\to\mathbb{R}
(writing
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{k}=(0,\infty)^{\times k}
) so that

\displaystyle\sup\mathcal{N}(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})

\displaystyle=b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}\left(\frac{t_{3}-t_{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}},\ldots,\frac{t_{2M}-t_{2M-1}}{t_{2M-1}-t_{2M-2}}\right)=b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle)

(2.28)

\displaystyle\inf\mathcal{N}(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})

\displaystyle=b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}\left(\frac{t_{3}-t_{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}},\ldots,\frac{t_{2M}-t_{2M-1}}{t_{2M-1}-t_{2M-2}}\right)=b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle),

(2.29)

where
\srsd
is the operation of forming the sequence of successive ratios of successive differences.
By convention, we write
b^{(1)}_{\textnormal{back}}=c_{\textnormal{BM}}
,

b^{(1)}_{\textnormal{over}}=-1
, and
\srsd
maps any element of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
to the empty list. As we now describe, the backflow and overflow functions are semicontinuous.

Lemma 2.4
.

For each
M>1
, the functions
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}
are lower/upper semicontinuous on

\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
. That is, for all
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
, one has

\liminf_{u\to u_{0}}b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(u)\geq b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(u_{0}),\qquad\limsup_{u\to u_{0}}b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(u)\leq b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(u_{0}),

(2.30)

as
u\to u_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
.

Proof.

The function
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\mapsto B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
is strongly continuous on
\mathcal{T}_{M}
due to (
2.24
) and Theorem
2.1
,
so for each normalised
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
,

\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\mapsto\innerproduct{\phi}{B^{(M)}_{\boldvec[M]{t}}\phi}
is continuous on
\mathcal{T}_{M}
. As

\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\mapsto\sup\mathcal{N}(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})=b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(\srsd\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle)
is the pointwise supremum over
\phi
of a family
of continuous functions, it is lower semicontinuous (see, e.g., Chapter 2 of
[
32
]
). Fixing
\tau>0
, it follows that

b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}\circ\srsd

is lower semicontinuous on the subset
\{\langle 0,\tau,t_{3},\ldots,t_{2M}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{M}\}
(in the relative topology). As this subset is mapped homeomorphically to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
by
\srsd
, we conclude that
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}
is lower semicontinuous on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
.
The proof for the overflow functions is similar.
∎

We also introduce sequences of backflow and overflow constants, by

c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}=\sup_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}}b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}},\qquad c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}=\inf_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}}b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}

(2.31)

for
M\geq 1
, in which the Bracken–Melloy constant appears as the first backflow constant:

c_{\textnormal{BM}}=c^{(1)}_{\textnormal{back}}
,
while the first overflow constant is

c^{(1)}_{\textnormal{over}}=-1
.
Just as with
c_{\textnormal{BM}}
, the functions

b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}
and constants

c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}
are quantities that are fixed by the free quantum dynamics on the line. However,
as the
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}

are dimensionless functions of dimensionless variables,
they are manifestly independent of Planck’s constant.
In the remainder of this paper, we will initiate the study of
these functions and constants using both analytical and numerical methods, starting by considering the
spectrum of the operators
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
for

\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{M}
.

3
The spectrum of
M
-fold backflow operators

3.1
Bounds on the spectrum

Our aim in this section is to obtain estimates
on
\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
for a variety of
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{M}
. The simplest estimate arises from the triangle inequality:
since
\|B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}\|=1
for all
t_{1}<t_{2}
,
we have from (
2.24
) that
\|B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}\|\leq M
and consequently

\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})\subseteq[-M,M]
for
all
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{M}
and
M\geq 1
.

To get a better estimate of the upper bound on the spectrum, we note that any sum of self-adjoint bounded operators obeys

\sup\sigma\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}A_{j}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sup\sigma(A_{j})

(3.1)

and therefore

\sup\sigma\big(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}\big)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sup\sigma(B_{\langle t_{2j-1},t_{2j}\rangle})=Mc_{\textnormal{BM}}.

(3.2)

To get a lower bound on the spectrum, it is convenient to rewrite
\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)
as

\displaystyle\Delta^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2M})-\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}\left(\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2j+1})-\text{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2j})\right),

(3.3)

from which it follows that

B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}=B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2M}\rangle}-B^{(M-1)}_{\langle t_{2},\ldots,t_{2M-1}\rangle}.

(3.4)

Iterating, one obtains the formula

B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}(-1)^{j-1}B_{\langle t_{j},t_{2M-j+1}\rangle},

(3.5)

which will be used later on.

Returning to (
3.4
), an analogue of (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\inf\sigma\left(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\sigma\left(B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2M}\rangle}\right)+\inf\sigma\left(-B^{(M-1)}_{\langle t_{2},\ldots,t_{2M-1}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=-1-\sup\sigma\left(B^{(M-1)}_{\langle t_{2},\ldots,t_{2M-1}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-1-(M-1)c_{\textnormal{BM}},

(3.6)

where we have used (
3.2
). Summarising, we have shown the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{M}
, the spectrum of
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
satisfies

\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})\subseteq[-1-(M-1)c_{\textnormal{BM}},Mc_{\textnormal{BM}}].

(3.7)

Thus one has bounds

\displaystyle b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{2M-2})

\displaystyle\leq c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}\leq Mc_{\textnormal{BM}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{2M-2})

\displaystyle\geq c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}\geq-1-(M-1)c_{\textnormal{BM}}

(3.9)

for all
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{2M-2})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2M-2}
.

Theorem
3.1
shows that

c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}
do not grow faster than linearly in
M
, but does not answer the question of whether they grow at all.
In the following subsections we will prove rigorously that for a particular class of backflow operators, both the infimum and supremum of
\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
tend to
\pm\infty
as
M\rightarrow\infty
at least as fast as
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
. This is in stark contrast to the classical case where, irrespective of the number of disjoint backflow intervals, the total backflow always lies in
[-1,0]
. The rigorous results presented will be supplemented by a numerical investigation for values of
1\leq M\leq 4
which clearly indicate monotonicity of the infimum and supremum of
\sigma(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})
.

3.2
Unbounded
M
-fold backflow and overflow as
M\to\infty

In this subsection we will show that

c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}
and
-c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}
grow
unboundedly with
M
, by considering the
M
-fold backflow operator corresponding to
M
backflow periods of equal duration, separated by
M-1
intervals of the same duration. Specifically, for
M\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
T>0
consider the
M
-fold backflow problem for the times

\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle=\left\langle-T\frac{2M-1}{2},-T\frac{2M-3}{2},...,-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2},...,T\frac{2M-1}{2}\right\rangle,

(3.10)

for which the total backflow is bounded between
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(1,\ldots,1)
and
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(1,\ldots,1)
. Note that
b_{\textnormal{over}}^{(M)}(1,...,1)
and
b_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}(1,...,1)
are invariant under the choice of
T>0
.
One can write the associated
M
-fold backflow operator
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}
as

B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}(-1)^{j-1}B^{(1)}_{\langle-T(j-1/2),T(j-1/2)\rangle}

(3.11)

using (
3.5
). Each of the
B^{(1)}_{\langle-T(j-1/2),T(j-1/2)\rangle}
operators has the closed form

(B^{(1)}_{\langle-T(j-1/2),T(j-1/2)\rangle}\phi)(k)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dl\>\frac{\sin\left(T(j-1/2)(k^{2}-l^{2})/2\right)}{k-l}\phi(l).

(3.12)

Let
U:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dp)
be the unitary implementing the change of variables
p=k^{2}T/2
,

(U\phi)(p)=(2pT)^{-1/4}\phi(\sqrt{2p/T})
,
so that
U^{\ast}B^{(1)}_{\langle-T/2,T/2\rangle}U=C
is the operator defined in (
2.15
).
Then it is easily seen, by use of the identity

\sum_{j=1}^{M}(-1)^{M-j}\sin(2(j-1/2)(p-q))=\frac{\sin 2M(p-q)}{2\cos(p-q)},

(3.13)

that the operator
C^{(M)}=U^{\ast}B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}U\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq))
is independent of
T
and has the action

\left(C^{(M)}\varphi\right)(p)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\>\frac{\sin(2M[p-q])}{(p-q)\cos(p-q)}\left[\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{1/4}+\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{-1/4}\right]\varphi(q).

(3.14)

The following theorem shows that the suprema and infima of
\mathcal{N}(B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle})=\mathcal{N}(C^{(M)})
grow arbitrarily large in magnitude with
M

at least as fast as
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
C^{(M)}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq))
be the backflow operator defined in (
3.14
) describing
M
backflow periods of unit duration separated by
M-1
periods of unit duration. Then
there is a constant
k>0
so that

\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\geq kM^{1/4}

(3.15)

for all positive even
M
, and furthermore,

\liminf_{M\to\infty}M^{-1/4}\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\geq k>0,\qquad\limsup_{M\to\infty}M^{-1/4}\inf\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\leq-k<0,

(3.16)

where the limits are taken over all
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We begin by proving (
3.15
). For even
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{6}]
, define the intervals
\mathcal{I}_{0},\mathcal{I}_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{I}_{0}=\left[0,\frac{\epsilon}{M}\right],\qquad\mathcal{I}_{1}=\left[\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{2M},\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2M}\right]

(3.17)

and associated normalised
\psi_{M}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
by

\psi_{M}(q)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{M}{2\epsilon}}\qquad&q\in\mathcal{I}_{0}\cup\mathcal{I}_{1}\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(3.18)

We will estimate the supremum of the spectrum using the bound
\sup\sigma(C^{(M)})\geq\left\langle\psi_{M}|C^{(M)}\psi_{M}\right\rangle
.
The following Lemma is established in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For even
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\pi/6]
,

\left\langle\psi_{M}|C^{(M)}\psi_{M}\right\rangle\geq\frac{2^{7/4}\epsilon^{3/4}}{3\pi^{11/4}}M^{1/4}S\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{\pi M}\right),

(3.19)

where

S(\eta)=1-\frac{31\pi^{2}}{80}\eta^{1/4}-\frac{5}{8}\eta-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{176}\eta^{5/4}.

(3.20)

Clearly
S(\eta)>0
for sufficiently small
\eta\geq 0
. Fixing
\epsilon\in(0,\pi/6]
sufficiently small that
S>0
on
[0,\epsilon/\pi]
, and using continuity of
S
, let

k=\min_{\eta\in[0,\epsilon/\pi]}\frac{2^{7/4}\epsilon^{3/4}}{3\pi^{11/4}}S(\eta).

(3.21)

Then (
3.15
) follows because
2\epsilon/(\pi M)\in[0,\epsilon/\pi]
for all even
M\geq 2
. (The value of
k
can be optimised by varying
\epsilon
.)

To establish the first result in (
3.16
), we need some information about
\sup\sigma(C^{(M)})
for odd
M\geq 1
.
Writing

B^{(M+1)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M+1}\rangle}=B^{(M)}_{\langle-T\frac{2M+1}{2},...,-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2},...,T\frac{2M-3}{2}\rangle}+B^{(1)}_{\langle T\frac{2M-1}{2},T\frac{2M+1}{2}\rangle},

(3.22)

the left-hand side is unitarily equivalent to
C^{(M+1)}
, while
by making time translations and dilations, the first and second terms on the right-hand side are unitarily equivalent to

C^{(M)}
and
C^{(1)}
respectively. Consequently

C^{(M+1)}
is a sum of operators unitarily equivalent to
C^{(M)}
and
C^{(1)}
. As the supremum of the spectrum is a sub-additive function on bounded self-adjoint operators, and spectra are invariant under
unitary equivalence,
it follows that

\displaystyle\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M+1)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)+\sup\sigma\left(C^{(1)}\right)=c_{\textnormal{BM}}+\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right),

(3.23)

and therefore
\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\geq k(M+1)^{1/4}-c_{\textnormal{BM}}
for all odd
M\geq 1
, using (
3.15
).
Combining with the bound (
3.15
) for even
M
,
one has
\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\geq kM^{1/4}-c_{\textnormal{BM}}
for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
the first part of (
3.16
) follows immediately.

Finally, for any
M\geq 1

B^{(M+1)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M+1}\rangle}
can be rewritten as

B^{(M+1)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M+1}\rangle}=B^{(1)}_{\langle-T\frac{2M+1}{2},T\frac{2M+1}{2}\rangle}-B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}\rangle}

(3.24)

which, because all single-backflow operators are unitarily equivalent, gives

C^{(M+1)}=\widetilde{C}^{(1)}-C^{(M)}

(3.25)

where
\widetilde{C}^{(1)}
is unitarily equivalent to
C^{(1)}
. Using invariance of the spectrum under unitary equivalence and the fact that
\sup\sigma(-A)=-\inf\sigma(A)
for any bounded self-adjoint operator
A
, we find

\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M+1)}\right)\leq\sup\sigma\left(C^{(1)}\right)-\inf\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)

(3.26)

and consequently

\inf\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\leq c_{\textnormal{BM}}-\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M+1)}\right).

(3.27)

Thus
\limsup_{M\to\infty}M^{-1/4}\inf\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)\leq-\liminf_{M\to\infty}M^{-1/4}\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M+1)}\right)\leq-k
.
∎

Numerically, the first positive zero of
S(\eta)
occurs at
\eta_{0}=0.0046
to two significant figures and
S
is decreasing on the interval
[0,\eta_{0}]
. Then the constant
k
in Theorem
3.2

may be taken as

k=\max_{\epsilon\in[0,\pi\eta_{0}]}\frac{2^{7/4}\epsilon^{3/4}}{3\pi^{11/4}}S(\epsilon/\pi)=2.1\times 10^{-4},

(3.28)

computing the maximum numerically to
2
significant figures. By considering more complicated trial functions, it seems likely that we can increase the coefficient; at any rate, the main point is to establish the growth of at least
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
. For comparison, the numerical results of Section
5
show that

\sup\sigma(C^{(4)})\gtrsim 0.095
, which far exceeds the lower bound

4^{1/4}k=0.0003
for this case.

Theorem
3.2
has
an immediate corollary relating to the asymptotics of the backflow and overflow constants.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}
and
c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}
be the
M
-fold backflow and overflow constants defined in (
2.31
). Then

\liminf_{M\to\infty}M^{-1/4}c_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}>0,\qquad\limsup_{M\to\infty}M^{-1/4}c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}<0.

(3.29)

Proof.

Since
C^{(M)}
is unitarily equivalent to
B^{(M)}_{\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{M}(T)\rangle}
, we find that

\sup\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)=b_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}(1,...,1)\leq c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}},\qquad\inf\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)=b_{\textnormal{over}}^{(M)}(1,...,1)\geq c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}.

(3.30)

The result follows on multiplying each inequality by
M^{-1/4}
and employing
theorem
3.2
,
together with elementary properties of
\limsup
and
\liminf
.
∎

Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.4
demonstrate the unboundedness of the quantum backflow and overflow effects as
M\to\infty
. In Section
5
, we will find numerical estimates of the spectral extrema for
M

4
Limiting cases and monotonicity of the backflow constants

In Lemma

we have already seen that
the
M

\mathbb{R}^{2M-2}_{>}
. We now consider certain limiting cases, which show how backflow and overflow functions with different numbers of parameters are related and establish monotonicity properties of the backflow constants.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
so that
j+k=2L
is even and positive.
Consider any

and any

and

\mathbb{R}_{>}^{k}

and

. (In the case
M=1

; similarly, in the cases

resp.,

one omits

(and hence also

) is even, then

\liminf_{n}b^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{back}}(v_{n},u,w_{n})\geq b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(u),\qquad\limsup_{n}b^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{over}}(v_{n},u,w_{n})\leq b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(u)

(4.1)

where we use the shorthand notation

(v,u,w)=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j},u_{1},\ldots,u_{2M-2},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})
.

On the other hand, if

(and hence also
k
) is odd, then

\liminf_{n}b^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{back}}(v_{n},u,w_{n})\geq-1-b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}(u),\qquad\limsup_{n}b^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{over}}(v_{n},u,w_{n})\leq-1-b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}(u).

(4.2)

Consequently, the sequence of backflow constants
c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}

is nondecreasing, and the sequence of overflow constants
c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}
is nonincreasing; moreover,

c^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{back}}\geq-1-c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{over}}
and

c^{(M+L)}_{\textnormal{over}}\leq-1-c^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}
for all

.

We remark that the result holds also, with the same proof, if the sequences

and

Proof.

Choose any

A fourth consequence of Theorem
4.1
is that it provides an understanding of how the spectrum of an two-fold backflow operator

B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}
behaves in a limit
in which the two backflow intervals merge together, i.e.,
t_{2},t_{3}\to\tau
for some

\tau\in(t_{1},t_{4})
, with
t_{1},t_{4}
held fixed and maintaining
t_{2}<t_{3}
. An obvious question is whether the spectrum converges in some sense to
that of
B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle}
. As we now show, this is not the case.
Writing
\srsd\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle=(v,w)
, we have

v\to 0+
and
w\to+\infty
in the limit.
Using the
M=j=k=1
case of Theorem
4.1
, we find

\limsup b^{(2)}_{\textnormal{over}}(v,w)\leq-1-b^{(1)}_{\textnormal{back}}=-1-c_{\textnormal{BM}}

(4.15)

from (
4.2
) in the limit of interest.
Hence
b^{(2)}_{\textnormal{over}}(v,w)\to-1-c_{\textnormal{BM}}
because we also have
b^{(2)}_{\textnormal{over}}\geq c^{(2)}_{\textnormal{over}}=-1-c_{\textnormal{BM}}
.
Thus,
\sigma(B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle})
contains points
approaching
-1-c_{\textnormal{BM}}
arbitrarily closely in the limit, while

\sigma(B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle})\subseteq[-1,c_{\textnormal{BM}}]
.
Therefore the spectra do not coincide in the limit; in fact, we have

\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{2},t_{3}\to\tau\\
t_{2}<t_{3}\end{subarray}}d_{\textnormal{Haus}}\left(\sigma(B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}),\sigma(B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle})\right)\geq c_{\textnormal{BM}},

(4.16)

where the Hausdorff distance between compact
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
is

d_{\textnormal{Haus}}(A,B)=\max\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}|a-b|,\sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}|a-b|\},

(4.17)

and gives the collection of non-empty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
the structure of a complete metric space (see, e.g., theorem 3.2.4 of
[
4
]
).
As one has the inequality
d_{\textnormal{Haus}}(\sigma(A),\sigma(B))\leq\|A-B\|

for self-adjoint bounded operators on Hilbert spaces (see e.g., Theorem V.4.10 in
[
23
]
), we may infer that the backflow operators
B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}
do not converge in norm to
B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle}
in the limit considered. This can also be seen directly as follows, noting that

B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}-B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle}=-B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{2},t_{3}\rangle}

(4.18)

and therefore

\|B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}-B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle}\|=1

(4.19)

by recalling that all single backflow operators have unit norm.
It would be interesting to study other mergers of multiple backflow intervals in a similar way.

We end this discussion with a cautionary note. For
\Lambda>0
, let
L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk)
be the space of positive momentum states with momentum cutoff
\Lambda
, in momentum representation. Let
\iota_{\Lambda}:L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
be the subspace inclusion map,
whereupon
\iota_{\Lambda}^{*}
is the subspace projection.
Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A
shows that the operators

\iota_{\Lambda}^{*}B^{(1)}_{\langle s,t\rangle}\iota_{\Lambda}
are
norm continuous in
\langle s,t\rangle\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
with respect to
the operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda}
on
L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk)
.
Considering (
4.18
) again, it follows that

\|\iota_{\Lambda}^{*}(B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}-B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle})\iota_{\Lambda}\|_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow 0

(4.20)

as the backflow intervals merge, so

\lim_{t_{3}-t_{2}\rightarrow 0^{+}}d_{\textnormal{Haus}}\Big(\sigma\Big(\iota_{\Lambda}^{*}B^{(2)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle}\iota_{\Lambda}\Big),\sigma\Big(\iota_{\Lambda}^{*}B^{(1)}_{\langle t_{1},t_{4}\rangle}\iota_{\Lambda}\Big)\Big)\longrightarrow 0

(4.21)

in this limit. Accordingly, any numerical scheme implementing a fixed momentum cutoff will give the erroneous impression of a convergence of spectra as backflow intervals merge. To see the true behaviour of the backflow operators as in (
4.16
), it would therefore be necessary to increase the momentum cutoff as
t_{3}-t_{2}
decreases.

5
Numerical calculation

The goal of this section is to numerically investigate the multiple quantum backflow and quantum overflow effect over
M
disjoint intervals of equal duration separated by gaps of the same length, corresponding to the bounded operator
C^{(M)}
given in (
3.14
). Although our method would in principle apply to arbitrary
M
, the computational effort rises quickly with
M
and we have chosen to restrict to
M\leq 4
.
The numerical results give lower bounds on the magnitudes of
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}(1,\ldots,1)
for
1\leq M\leq 4
.
In particular, we will find for
2\leq M\leq 4
that
\max\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)>c_{\textnormal{BM}}
and
\min\sigma\left(C^{(M)}\right)<-1
. This complements the analytic results in theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.4
on the large
M
asymptotics of
\sigma(C^{(M)})
and the mononotonicity results in Theorem
4.1
. By numerical acceleration methods, we will give improved estimates for
b^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}(1,\ldots,1)
for
1\leq M\leq 4
. In addition, we will investigate the properties of states that come close to maximising the backflow or overflow for these operators.

5.1
Background theory

Our numerical calculations are based on the following basic observation.
(See Section XIII.1 of
[
29
]
for a discussion of other min-max results
and Theorem VII.12 of
[
30
]
for Weyl’s criterion.) Here,
\sigma(B,Q)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\det(B-\lambda Q)=0\}
is the set of generalised eigenvalues for Hermitian matrix
B
with respect to a positive definite matrix
Q
of the same dimension.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
and let
(\chi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
be a sequence of linearly independent vectors with dense span. Define sequences of self-adjoint
N\times N
matrices

(A^{[N]})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(P^{[N]})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

with matrix elements

A^{[N]}_{mn}=\innerproduct{\chi_m}{A\chi_n},\qquad P^{[N]}_{mn}=\innerproduct{\chi_m}{\chi_n}

(5.1)

for
1\leq m,n\leq N
. Then
\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(A)
, and

\max\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]})
(resp.,
\min\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]})
) is a bounded nondecreasing (resp., nonincreasing) sequence with

\displaystyle\max\sigma(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\max\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]})

\displaystyle\min\sigma(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\min\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]}).

(5.2)

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose
v^{(N)}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a generalised eigenvector obeying

A^{[N]}v^{(N)}=\lambda_{N}P^{[N]}v^{(N)},\qquad v^{(N)\dagger}P^{[N]}v^{(N)}=1

(5.3)

and define
\psi^{(N)}\in\mathcal{H}
by

\psi^{(N)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}v^{(N)}_{n}\chi_{n}.

(5.4)

If
\psi^{(N)}\to\psi\in\mathcal{H}
in norm then

1.

the sequence of generalised eigenvalues
(\lambda_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges;

2.

the sequence of vectors
(\psi^{(N)})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Weyl sequence for
\lambda=\lim_{N\to\infty}\lambda_{N}
, i.e.,
\|\psi^{(N)}\|=1
and
\|(A-\lambda I)\psi^{(N)}\|\to 0
;

3.

the limiting vector
\psi
is an eigenvector for
A
with eigenvalue
\lambda
.

Proof.

If
\lambda\in\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]})
with
A^{[N]}v=\lambda P^{[N]}v

for nonzero
v
, then

\lambda=\frac{v^{\dagger}A^{[N]}v}{v^{\dagger}P^{[N]}v}=\frac{\innerproduct{\phi}{A\phi}}{\innerproduct{\phi}{\phi}},

(5.5)

where
\phi=\sum_{r=1}^{N}v_{r}\chi_{r}
, so
\lambda\in\mathcal{N}(A)
, which is a bounded set. Let
S_{N}=\text{span}(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{N})
, then monotonicity of the sequences of maximum and minimum generalised eigenvalues is guaranteed because
S_{N}\subset S_{N+1}
. Finally,
as
\bigcup_{N}S_{N}
is dense in
\mathscr{H}
and
A
is bounded, we easily compute

\displaystyle\max\sigma(A)

\displaystyle=\sup\mathcal{N}(A)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\phi\in S_{N}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\innerproduct{\phi}{A \phi}}{\innerproduct{\phi}{\phi}}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{v^{\dagger}A^{[N]}v}{v^{\dagger}P^{[N]}v}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\max\sigma(A^{[N]},P^{[N]}),

(5.6)

where in the final equality we have made use of the (generalised) Courant–Fischer min-max principle – see, e.g., theorem 2.1 of
[
25
]
. The proof for the minimum of
\sigma(A)
is analogous.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Q_{N}
be the projection onto
S_{N}
. First we show that
\psi^{(N)}
is an eigenvector of
Q_{N}A
. Since
Q_{N}A\psi^{(N)}\in S_{N}
, we must have

Q_{N}A\psi^{(N)}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}w_{k}^{(N)}\chi_{k}

(5.7)

for some
w^{(N)}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. For
1\leq j\leq N
, the inner product of (
5.7
) with
\chi_{j}
can be written as both

\innerproduct{\chi_j}{Q_N A \psi^{(N)}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\innerproduct{\chi_j}{\chi_k}w^{(N)}_{k}=\left(P^{[N]}w^{(N)}\right)_{j}

(5.8)

and

\innerproduct{\chi_j}{Q_N A \psi^{(N)}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}v^{(N)}_{k}\innerproduct{\chi_j}{A \chi_k}=\left(A^{[N]}v^{(N)}\right)_{j}=\lambda_{N}\left(P^{[N]}v^{(N)}\right)_{j}.

(5.9)

By the linear independence of the
\chi_{j}
,
P^{[N]}
has trivial kernel and we find
w^{(N)}=\lambda_{N}v^{(N)}
, from which it follows that

Q_{N}A\psi^{(N)}=\lambda_{N}\psi^{(N)}.

(5.10)

Note that
\|\psi^{(N)}\|=1
because of the normalisation condition on
v^{(N)}
.
Property (
1
) follows from the fact that

\lambda_{N}=\innerproduct{Q_N \psi^{(N)}}{A \psi^{(N)}}

(5.11)

converges to some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
by the norm convergence of the
\psi^{(N)}
to
\psi
and boundedness of
A
. To prove property (
2
), note that

\|(A-\lambda I)\psi^{(N)}\|\leq\|(A-\lambda_{N}I)\psi^{(N)}\|+|\lambda_{N}-\lambda|

(5.12)

so since
\lambda_{N}\to\lambda
, it suffices to note that

\displaystyle\|(A-\lambda_{N}I)\psi^{(N)}\|=\|(A-Q_{N}A)\psi^{(N)}\|\leq\|(I-Q_{N})A\psi\|+\|(I-Q_{N})A\|\|\psi^{(N)}-\psi\|\to 0

(5.13)

by the density of
\bigcup_{N}S_{N}
in
\mathcal{H}
and convergence of
(\psi^{(N)})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
proving property (
2
). The final property follows from

\|(A-\lambda I)\psi\|\leq\|(A-\lambda I)\psi^{(N)}\|+(\|A\|+|\lambda|)\|\psi^{(N)}-\psi\|\to 0

(5.14)

and hence
\psi
is an eigenvector of
A
with eigenvalue
\lambda
.
∎

The generalised eigenvalue problems in Lemma
5.1
reduce to standard eigenvalue problems if the trial vectors
\chi_{n}
are orthonormal.
In principle one could always orthogonalise to express the matrix elements in a Gram-Schmidt basis, but this introduces a nontrivial computational overhead and
the generalised eigenproblem may be preferred.

5.2
Numerical methodology and results

We will apply Lemma
5.1
to the operators
C^{(M)}
, but our methodology also applies to general backflow operators.
Given
\delta>-1/2
and
a>0
, define the sequence of normalized
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
vectors
(\psi_{n,a,\delta})_{n=0}^{\infty}
by

\psi_{n,a,\delta}(q)=E_{n}(a,\delta)q^{n+\delta}e^{-aq},

(5.15)

with the normalization constant
E_{n}(a,\delta)
given by

E_{n}(a,\delta)=\frac{(2a)^{n+\delta+1/2}}{\sqrt{\Gamma(2n+2\delta+1)}}.

(5.16)

The following density result is found in Theorem 5.7.1 of
[
36
]
in the case
a=1/2
, and follows for general
a>0
follows by a unitary scale change.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
a>0
and
-\tfrac{1}{2}<\delta<\tfrac{1}{2}
, the sequence
(\psi_{n,a,\delta})_{n=0}^{\infty}
has a dense span in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
.

To apply Lemma
5.1
to
C^{(M)}
, we require the matrix elements

C^{(M)}(a,\delta)_{mn}=\innerproduct{ \psi_{m,a,\delta}}{C^{(M)} \psi_{n,a,\delta} },\qquad P(a,\delta)_{mn}=\innerproduct{ \psi_{m,a,\delta} }{\psi_{n,a,\delta}}

(5.17)

for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The components
P(a,\delta)_{mn}
admit the closed form

P(a,\delta)_{mn}=\frac{\sqrt{B_{\textnormal{diag}}(m+\delta+1/2)B_{\textnormal{diag}}(n+\delta+1/2)}}{B(m+\delta+1/2,n+\delta+1/2)},

(5.18)

where
B(x,y)=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)/\Gamma(x+y)
is the beta function and
B_{\textnormal{diag}}(x)=B(x,x)=\Gamma(x)^{2}/\Gamma(2x)
.
The components
C^{(M)}(a,\delta)_{mn}
(or indeed, those of general multiple backflow operators) also have a closed form expression in terms of incomplete Beta functions, for which the reader is referred to Appendix
D
.

For each
M
, and for fixed
a>0
,
\delta\in(-1/2,1/2)
, the matrix elements
P(a,\delta)_{mn}
and

C^{(M)}(a,\delta)_{mn}
can be computed for
0\leq m,n\leq N
, giving
(N+1)
-dimensional square matrices.
The minimum eigenvalue of
P^{[N]}
tends to zero as
N
increases, so the generalised eigenvalue problem is increasingly ill-conditioned and requires high-precision calculation to obtain accurate results.
The following theorem, stated as Corollary VI.3.3 in
[
35
]
, shows how the numerical precision of a pair of Hermitian matrices affects the accuracy of the associated generalised eigenvalues.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A,B
be
n
-dimensional Hermitian matrices with
B
positive definite, and consider
n
-dimensional Hermitian perturbations
\Delta A,\Delta B
.
If

\sqrt{\|\Delta A\|_{\textnormal{op}}^{2}+\|\Delta B\|_{\textnormal{op}}^{2}}<\gamma(A,B):=\min_{\|x\|_{2}=1}\sqrt{(x^{\dagger}Ax)^{2}+(x^{\dagger}Bx)^{2}},

(5.19)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\textnormal{op}}
is the operator norm induced by the Euclidean vector norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
then the matrix pair
(A+\Delta A,B+\Delta B)
is definite and, for all
1\leq i\leq n
,

\frac{|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|}{\sqrt{(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})(1+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2})}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\|\Delta A\|_{\textnormal{op}}^{2}+\|\Delta B\|_{\textnormal{op}}^{2}}}{\gamma(A,B)},

(5.20)

where
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
are the generalised eigenvalues of
(A,B)
and
(A+\Delta A,B+\Delta B)
respectively.

We adapt this theorem to our numerical calculation in the following corollary.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
A,B
be
n
-dimensional Hermitian matrices with
B
positive definite, and let
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdot\cdot\cdot\leq\lambda_{n}
be the generalised eigenvalues of the matrix pair
(A,B)
. If
n
-dimensional Hermitian perturbations
\Delta A,\Delta B
obey

C_{\textnormal{max}}:=n(1+\max(|\lambda_{1}|,|\lambda_{n}|)^{2})\frac{\|\Delta A\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\Delta B\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\min\sigma(B)^{2}}<\frac{1}{2},

(5.21)

where
\lambda_{\textnormal{max}}=\max(|\lambda_{1}|,|\lambda_{n}|)
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the elementwise maximum modulus matrix norm, then the matrix pair
(A+\Delta A,B+\Delta B)
is definite,
with generalised eigenvalues
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\leq\cdot\cdot\cdot\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
satisfying

|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\textnormal{max}}}{1-2C_{\textnormal{max}}}(1+2\lambda_{i}^{2})

(5.22)

for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

First note that
C_{\textnormal{max}}<1/2
implies that

\|\Delta A\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\Delta B\|_{\infty}^{2}<\min\sigma(B)^{2}\leq\gamma(A,B)^{2}
, using the trivial bound

\gamma(A,B)\geq\min_{\|x\|_{2}=1}x^{\dagger}Bx=\min\sigma(B).

(5.23)

Then
(A+\Delta A,B+\Delta B)
is definite by
Theorem
5.3
, and we proceed to obtain (
5.22
) by estimating and rearranging (
5.20
).
Applying the inequality

n^{-1/2}\|M\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq\|M\|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|M_{ij}|,

(5.24)

for
n
-dimensional square matrices (see
[
21
]
, pp. 313-314) and theorem
5.3
we obtain

|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|^{2}\leq(1+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2})C_{i},

(5.25)

where

C_{i}=n(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})\frac{\|\Delta A\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\Delta B\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\min\sigma(B)^{2}}.

(5.26)

Now note that
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{2}\leq(|\lambda_{i}|+|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|)^{2}\leq 2(\lambda_{i}^{2}+|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|^{2})
(using
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
). Combining with (
5.25
), we find

|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|^{2}\leq C_{i}(1+2\lambda_{i}^{2}+2|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|^{2}).

(5.27)

Rearranging the above, we obtain

|\lambda_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{i}(1+2\lambda_{i}^{2})}{1-2C_{i}}\leq\frac{C_{\textnormal{max}}(1+2\lambda_{i}^{2})}{1-2C_{\textnormal{max}}},

(5.28)

by making use of the fact that
x\mapsto 2x/(1-2x)
is monotonically increasing on
[0,1/2]
and where
C_{\textnormal{max}}=\max_{i}C_{i}
has the closed form (
5.21
) with
1-2C_{\textnormal{max}}>0
.
∎

5.3
Numerical acceleration

The sequences
\lambda^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}
are guaranteed to converge to the backflow/overflow values
b^{(M)}(1,\ldots,1)
, but the rate of convergence is slow. For example,
\lambda^{(1)}_{\textnormal{back}}(500)=0.03693
can be compared with the expected value
b_{\textnormal{back}}^{(1)}=0.03845
to 4 significant figures. In this section we consider numerical acceleration techniques that modify a convergent sequence, while preserving its limit, but increasing the speed of convergence, at least for sequences of interest. The aim is to provide a better estimate of the eventual limit from the available data.

Let
l^{\textnormal{conv}}
be the vector space of convergent real-valued sequences in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
.

Definition 5.5
.

A map
A:l^{\textnormal{conv}}\rightarrow l^{\textnormal{conv}}
is called a
sequence accelerator
for
x\in l^{\textnormal{conv}}
if
Ax
and
x
have the same limit and

\frac{(Ax)_{n}-x_{\infty}}{x_{n}-x_{\infty}}\longrightarrow 0.

(5.34)

as
n\to\infty
, where
x_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}
.

Richardson accelerators provide a particularly useful class of sequence accelerators; see chapter 1 of
[
11
]
for a modern overview and
[
33
]
for detailed theory and generalisations.

Definition 5.6
.

The
Richardson accelerator
of order
\gamma
is the linear
map
R_{\gamma}
on
l^{\textnormal{conv}}
with action

(R_{\gamma}x)_{n}=\frac{2^{\gamma}x_{2n}-x_{n}}{2^{\gamma}-1}.

(5.35)

It is clear that
R_{\gamma}x
is convergent with the same limit as
x
.
Moreover, every sequence
(n^{-\alpha})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an eigenvector of
R_{\gamma}
, with eigenvalue
(2^{\gamma-\alpha}-1)/(2^{\gamma}-1)
, while for any
sequence
x
with
|x_{n}|\leq n^{-\alpha}
we have a bound

|(R_{\gamma}x)_{n}|\leq\frac{2^{\gamma-\alpha}+1}{2^{\gamma}-1}n^{-\alpha}.

(5.36)

As
R_{\gamma}
is linear,
if

x_{n}=a+\frac{b}{n^{\gamma}}+\epsilon_{n},\qquad|\epsilon_{n}|\leq\frac{C}{n^{\alpha}}

(5.37)

for constants
a
,
b
,
C
, and
\alpha>\gamma
, then

R_{\gamma}x_{n}=a+(R_{\gamma}\epsilon)_{n},\qquad|(R_{\gamma}\epsilon)_{n}|\leq\frac{2^{\gamma-\alpha}+1}{2^{\gamma}-1}\frac{C}{n^{\alpha}}.

(5.38)

and it is clear that the rate of convergence has been improved from
O(n^{-\gamma})
to
O(n^{-\alpha})
; moreover, the error terms are suppressed if

\gamma>1-\log_{2}(1-2^{-\alpha}).

(5.39)

The Richardson acceleration can be iterated, but a significant disadvantage is that the number of the available terms in the resulting sequence is approximately halved on each iteration.
Alternatively, one can generalise the Richardson method as follows – see
[
33
]
for yet further generalisations and discussion.
Suppose, for some integer
k\geq 2
, that

x_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a_{j}}{n^{\gamma_{j}}}+\epsilon_{n},\qquad|\epsilon_{n}|\leq\frac{C}{n^{\gamma_{k+1}}}

(5.40)

where
0=\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}<\cdots<\gamma_{k}<\gamma_{k+1}
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and
C
are constant real numbers,
so
x_{n}\to a_{1}
as
n\to\infty
. Let

1=r_{1}<r_{2}<\ldots<r_{k}
be integers and write
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})
,
\boldsymbol{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
. Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
be the unique solution (the relevant generalised Vandermonde determinant is nonvanishing by the remark at the end of
[
31
]
) to

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{v_{j}}{(r_{j})^{\gamma_{i}}}=\begin{cases}1&i=1\\
0&i\neq 0\end{cases}.

(5.41)

Then

(R_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{r}}x)_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}x_{nr_{j}}=a_{1}+(R_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{r}}\epsilon)_{n},\qquad|(R_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{r}}\epsilon)_{n}|\leq\frac{C}{n^{\gamma_{k+1}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{|v_{j}|}{r_{j}^{\gamma_{k+1}}}.

(5.42)

The accelerator
R_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{r}}
removes
k-1
power-law terms, while reducing the
number of available terms by a factor of approximately
r_{k}
. Provided that
r_{k}<2^{k-1}
this represents an advantage over the iterated Richardson accelerator

R_{\gamma_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ R_{\gamma_{2}}
.
The simplest usage is to set
r_{j}=j
for
1\leq j\leq k
and we will do that from now on, dropping the vector
\boldsymbol{r}
from the notation.

Our aim is to apply (generalised) Richardson accelerators to the sequences
\lambda^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back/over}}
for
1\leq M\leq 4
.
As the asymptotics of these sequences are unknown we also have to determine that the sequences are well-described by expansions of the form in (
5.40
), and to determine the relevant exponents. Another issue is that the sequences
\lambda^{(M)}_{\textnormal{back}}
contain oscillatory components with amplitude and period increasing with
M
. These oscillations become apparent in the accelerated sequences, once the dominant power law terms are removed, obstructing attempts to estimate the limit.

To dampen the oscillations in the sequences Kolmogorov–Zurbenko (KZ) low-pass filters
[
40
,
41
]
are applied before Richardson acceleration. The KZ filter
\textnormal{KZ}_{m,k}
can be regarded as
k\in\mathbb{N}
iterations of a moving average over an odd number of points
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and preserves limits of convergent sequences. We chose
k=5
for each filter application and then for each

6
Conclusion

We have shown that a free particle in
1
-dimensional quantum mechanics with non-negative momenta can exhibit probability flow in the opposite direction to its momentum over multiple disjoint time intervals. In particular, given a positive integer
M
number of time intervals we have found that the total amount of backflow can grow as
M^{1/4}
.

Similarly, we have shown the total probability flow in the same direction as the particle’s momentum can exceed unity.
By contrast, we have shown that the total backflow for statistical ensembles of classical particles is bounded within the interval
[-1,0]
. This result follows a host of phenomenon relating quantized observables compared to their classical counterparts. As has been shown, classical observables which have sharp bounds on their values often have quantized counterparts whose values exceed these bounds
[
16
]
. The same is true for the case of total probability flow.

Here it is worth making a comment regarding Tsirelson precession, which has already been noted to have a connection with probability backflow in the preprint
[
44
]
. Tsirelson showed that if a uniformly precessing coordinate is measured at a randomly chosen angle
\theta\in\{0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3\}
, then the probability
P
that the measured coordinate is positive is bounded for classical systems by
P_{\textnormal{classical}}\leq 2/3

[
39
]
. However, there exist quantum systems that violate this inequality. In the PhD thesis of Zaw
[
43
]
, it is shown that for a generalisation of the Tsirelson’s protocol to an odd number
2M+1
of angles, the maximum quantum violation seems to grow like
M^{1/4}
. Further work is being conducted to make the relationship between multiple quantum backflow and this generalisation of Tsirelson’s protocol precise.

We have illustrated our analytical results with numerical calculations of backflow and overflow. In particular this shows that the backflow constants
c_{\textnormal{back}}^{(M)}
are strictly greater than
c^{(1)}=c_{\textnormal{BM}}
for
M=2,3,4
, and provides a new estimate of the Bracken–Melloy constant
c_{\textnormal{BM}}\approx 0.0384506
to 6 significant figures. This estimate relies on numerical acceleration techniques and appears robust. An independent calculation is in progress and will provide a cross-check.

Quantum backflow over a single time interval can be attributed to the ability of the quantum mechanical current of a quantum state to attain arbitrarily negative values. The existence of backflow over multiple disjoint time regions indicates that, ignoring effects of measurement on the state, this phenomena can repeat. Namely, there exist states
\psi
with non-negative momentum for which a time of arrival detector would repeatedly detect the the state crossing
x=0
. Furthermore, the calculation in Section 3 shows that, as the number
M
of disjoint time intervals grows, the maximal backflow grows at least as fast as
\mathcal{O}(M^{1/4})
. This larger effect potentially opens a new avenue to experimentally verify quantum backflow. However, the fact that this requires measurements of probability differences at many different times presents a challenge that would have to be overcome.

Appendix A
The Bracken–Melloy operator

Let
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
be the usual Hilbert space of states for the particle on a line and

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
be the Hilbert space of states with nonnegative momentum, in the momentum representation. The reverse Fourier transform
determines an isometry
\mathcal{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
by

(\mathcal{R}\phi)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,e^{ixk}\phi(k)

(A.1)

for
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
.

For times
t_{1}<t_{2}
let

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)=\textnormal{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{2})-\textnormal{Prob}_{\psi}(X<0|t=t_{1}),

(A.2)

where
\psi
evolves according to the free Schrödinger evolution,
\psi_{t}=e^{-iHt}\psi
, and
H\psi=-\psi^{\prime\prime}
on its usual domain. We will be interested in nonnegative momentum states of the form
\psi=\mathcal{R}\phi
for
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
, for which we have
e^{-iHt}\psi=\mathcal{R}S_{t}\phi
, where
(S_{t}\phi)(k)=e^{-ik^{2}t}\phi(k)
defines a unitary operator
S_{t}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk))
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, if
\|\psi\|=1
, one has

\Delta_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}(\psi)=\innerproduct{\hat{\psi}}{B_{\langle t_1,t_2\rangle}\hat{\psi}}

(A.3)

where

B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}=S_{t_{2}}^{*}\mathcal{R}^{*}\Pi^{-}\mathcal{R}S_{t_{2}}-S_{t_{1}}^{*}\mathcal{R}^{*}\Pi^{-}\mathcal{R}S_{t_{1}}

(A.4)

and
\Pi^{-}
is the projection onto the subspace
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{-},dx)\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)

of states supported on the negative half-line in position space.
Evidently
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
is the difference of two positive semidefinite self-adjoint bounded operators, each of which is bounded above by the identity. Accordingly,
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
is a self-adjoint bounded operator with spectrum
\sigma(B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle})\subseteq[-1,1]
.

To obtain expressions for
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
as an integral operator, we proceed as in
[
28
]
. First introduce
\Sigma=\Pi^{+}-\Pi^{-}=1-2\Pi^{-}
and note that

B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(S_{t_{2}}^{*}\mathcal{R}^{*}\Sigma\mathcal{R}S_{t_{2}}-S_{t_{1}}^{*}\mathcal{R}^{*}\Sigma\mathcal{R}S_{t_{1}}\right)

(A.5)

Next, observe that
i\mathcal{R}^{*}\Sigma\mathcal{R}
is the restriction to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
of the Hilbert transform

(\mathcal{H}\phi)(k)=\frac{1}{\pi}\textnormal{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dl\frac{\phi(l)}{k-l}

(A.6)

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dk)
. Here the PV denotes a principal value integral
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{k-\epsilon}dl+\int_{k+\epsilon}^{\infty}dl\right)
, which converges in
L^{2}
, and pointwise almost everywhere, for
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. See Theorem 4.1.12 in
[
18
]
or Chapter II.3 of
[
34
]
.
Thus

B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}=-\frac{1}{2i}\left(S_{t_{2}}^{*}\mathcal{H}S_{t_{2}}-S_{t_{1}}^{*}\mathcal{H}S_{t_{1}}\right),

(A.7)

where we understand the restriction of
\mathcal{H}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
on the right-hand side.
The integral representation

(B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}\phi)(k)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}dl\frac{e^{it_{2}(k^{2}-l^{2})}-e^{it_{1}(k^{2}-l^{2})}}{k-l}\phi(l)

(A.8)

then holds pointwise almost everywhere for any
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
; note that the principal value is no longer needed because the fraction appearing in the integrand is regular. A particular special case is

(B_{\langle-\tau,\tau\rangle}\phi)(k)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dl\frac{\sin(k^2-l^2)\tau}{k-l}\phi(l)

(A.9)

for any
\tau>0
.

Note also that
t\mapsto S_{t}^{*}\mathcal{H}S_{t}
is strongly continuous, because
S_{t}
and
S_{t}^{*}
are strongly continuous and uniformly bounded. It follows that
(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
is strongly continuous on
the set
\mathcal{T}_{2}=\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t_{1}<t_{2}\}
.

We now turn to some unitary equivalences between operators of the form
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
and related operators. First, one has the elementary covariance relation

S_{\tau}^{*}B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}S_{\tau}=B_{\langle t_{1}+\tau,t_{2}+\tau\rangle}

(A.10)

for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Second, for any
\alpha>0
, let
D_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk))
be the unitary operator
(D_{\alpha}\phi)(k)=\sqrt{\alpha}\phi(\alpha k)
. It is readily checked that

S_{\tau}D_{\alpha}=D_{\alpha}S_{\tau/\alpha^{2}}
and also that
D_{\alpha}^{*}\mathcal{R}^{*}\Pi^{-}\mathcal{R}D_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}^{*}\Pi^{-}\mathcal{R}
. Consequently, we have

D_{\alpha}^{*}B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}D_{\alpha}=B_{\langle t_{1}/\alpha^{2},t_{2}/\alpha^{2}\rangle}.

(A.11)

Combining, for any
\tau>0
, we have

B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}=S^{*}_{(t_{1}+t_{2})/2}D^{*}_{\sqrt{2\tau/(t_{2}-t_{1})}}B_{\langle-\tau,\tau\rangle}D_{\sqrt{2\tau/(t_{2}-t_{1})}}S_{(t_{1}+t_{2})/2}

(A.12)

which shows that all the operators
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
for
t_{1}<t_{2}
are unitarily equivalent and therefore have the same spectrum.
Third, for any
\tau>0
we may define a unitary
U_{\tau}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)
by

(U_{\tau}\phi)(q)=\frac{\phi(\sqrt{q/\tau})}{\sqrt{2}(q\tau)^{1/4}},

(A.13)

where
q
is a dimensionless variable (representing action in units of
\hbar
).
Changing variables, one finds that

(U_{\tau}B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}U_{\tau}^{*}\varphi)(p)=-\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\frac{(e^{it_{2}(p-q)/\tau}-e^{it_{1}(p-q)/\tau})}{p-q}\Big[\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{1/4}+\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{-1/4}\Big]\varphi(q)

(A.14)

for
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq)

Accordingly, every single backflow operator
B_{\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle}
is unitarily equivalent to
the bounded operator
C=U_{\tau}B_{\langle-\tau,\tau\rangle}U_{\tau}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dq))
given by

(C\varphi)(p)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\frac{\sin(p-q)}{p-q}\Big[\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{1/4}+\Big(\frac{p}{q}\Big)^{-1/4}\Big]\varphi(q).

(A.15)

By definition,
c_{\textnormal{BM}}=\max\sigma(C)
, and as
\|C\|\leq 1
it follows that

\sigma(C)\subset[-1,c_{\textnormal{BM}}]
. All the assertions made in Theorem
2.1
have been proved.

Finally, the following result is used in Section
4
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\iota_{\Lambda}:L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
be the
subspace inclusion. Then
(s,t)\mapsto\iota_{\Lambda}^{*}B_{\langle s,t\rangle}\iota_{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk))
is norm continuous
on
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.

Proof.

For
\phi\in L^{2}([0,\Lambda])
we have (almost everywhere)

|(S_{s}\phi-S_{t}\phi)(p)|^{2}=\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{(t-s)p^{2}}{2}\Big)|\phi(p)|^{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{4}|s-t|^{2}|\phi(p)|^{2}

(A.16)

and consequently, in the operator norm on
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk))

\left\|(S_{s}-S_{t})\iota_{\Lambda}\right\|\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}|s-t|}{2}.

(A.17)

Thus the restricted Schrödinger time evolution operators
S_{t}\iota_{\Lambda}:L^{2}([0,\Lambda],dk)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dk)
are norm continuous in
t
. Using the general inequality

, for bounded Hilbert space operators

is also norm continuous in

∎

Appendix C
Chebyshev Polynomials

This appendix concerns the value and distribution of the extrema of
Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind as defined in (
B.7
). The results are presumably known, though we did not find a reference. The following Lemma bounds the absolute value of the extrema of
U_{n}(x)
.

Lemma C.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind are bounded as

|U_{n-1}(x)|\leq n

(C.1)

for all
x\in[-1,1]
and furthermore achieve this extremum for
x=\pm 1
.

Proof.

As
U_{0}\equiv 1
, we may restrict to
n\geq 2
and
x\in(-1,1)
, noting that

U_{n-1}(\pm 1)=(\pm 1)^{n-1}n
. The derivative of
U_{n-1}(x)
is given for
x\in(-1,1)
by

U_{n-1}^{\prime}(\cos(\theta))=-\frac{n\sin(\theta)\cos(n\theta)-\cos(\theta)\sin(n\theta)}{\sin^{3}(\theta)},

(C.2)

so the stationary points of
U_{n-1}(x)
with
x\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
occur when
x=\cos(\theta)
with

\tan(n\theta)=n\tan(\theta).

(C.3)

We can rewrite
U_{n-1}(x)^{2}
as

U_{n-1}(x)^{2}=\frac{\tan^{2}(n\theta)}{\tan^{2}(\theta)}\cdot\frac{1+\tan^{2}(\theta)}{1+\tan^{2}(n\theta)}

(C.4)

Suppose
x_{0}=\cos(\theta_0)
satisfies (
C.3
), then

U_{n-1}(x_{0})^{2}=n^{2}\frac{1+\tan^{2}(\theta_{0})}{1+n^{2}\tan^{2}(\theta_{0})}=\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+(1-n^{2})x_{0}^{2}}\in(1,n^{2}),

(C.5)

using trigonometric identities and
recallling that
x_{0}^{2}\in(0,1)
. Therefore
|U_{n-1}(x_{0})|<n
at any extremum
x_{0}\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

As well as an understanding of the best upper bound for
U_{n}(x)
, we would further like to bound the position of the nearest extremum of
U_{n}(\cos(\theta))
to
\theta=0
.

Lemma C.2
.

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
the function
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))
has no extrema for
\theta\in\left(0,\tfrac{\pi}{n}\right)
and
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))
is
nonnegative and nonincreasing on
\left[0,\tfrac{\pi}{n}\right]
.

Proof.

Note that
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))\to n>0
as
\theta\to 0
.
There are no zeros of
\sin
on
(0,\pi/n)
so the extrema of
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))
in this interval satisfy

t_{n}(\theta):=\tan(n\theta)-n\tan(\theta)=0.

(C.6)

We will show that
t_{n}>0
on
(0,\pi/(2n))
and
t_{n}<0
on
(\pi/(2n),\pi/n)
, and it is clear that
t_{n}(\theta)\to\pm\infty
as
\theta\to\ \pi/(2n)\mp
. Hence there are no zeros of
t_{n}
in
(0,\pi/n)

and therefore no extrema of
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))
. Since
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))
is smooth, achieves its global maximum of
n
at
\theta=0
, and satisfies
U_{n-1}(\cos(\pi/n))=0
, with no extrema in
(0,\pi/n)
,
it follows that
U_{n-1}(\cos(\theta))
is nonnegative and nonincreasing on
\left[0,\pi/n\right]
.

It remains to prove the statements made about
t_{n}
.
First, for
\theta\in\left(0,\pi/n\right)\setminus\{\pi/(2n)\}
, the derivative of
t_{n}
is given by

t_{n}^{\prime}(\theta)=n\Big(\sec^{2}(n\theta)-\sec^{2}(\theta)\Big),

(C.7)

which is positive on
\left(0,\pi/(2n)\right)
because

\sec^{2}(\theta)
is a strictly increasing function on the range
\left(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
. As
t_{n}(0)=0
, this implies
t_{n}>0
on
\left(0,\pi/(2n)\right)
. Second, by trigonometric identities, we may write
t_{n}^{\prime}
equivalently as

t_{n}^{\prime}(\theta)=n\frac{\sin([n+1]\theta)\sin([n-1]\theta)}{\cos^{2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(n\theta)},

(C.8)

from which it is clear that the only zero
t_{n}^{\prime}
has on
\left(0,\pi/n\right)
is at
\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{n+1}
. At this point, one has

t_{n}\Big(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\Big)=\tan\Big(\frac{n\pi}{n+1}\Big)-n\tan\Big(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\Big)=-(n+1)\tan\Big(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\Big)<0

(C.9)

using
\tan(\pi-\theta)=-\tan(\theta)
. Now
t_{n}(\theta)\to-\infty
as
\theta\to\pi/(2n)+
and also

t_{n}(\theta)=\tan(n\theta)-n\tan(\theta)\leq\tan(\pi)-n\tan\Big(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\Big)=-n\tan\Big(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\Big)<0

(C.10)

for
\theta\in\left(\tfrac{\pi}{n+1},\tfrac{\pi}{n}\right)

by considering an upper bound for each term individually, using the fact that the tangent function is increasing on
\left[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
and
\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2},\pi\right]
. As
t_{n}
is negative at its unique extremum in
(\pi/(2n),\pi/n)
it follows that

t_{n}<0
on this interval, completing the proof.
∎

Exact Matrix Element Integrals

In this appendix we compute closed forms for the matrix elements of an arbitrary backflow operator with respect to the dense sequence

5.15

(D.1)

where

2.24

with respect to
(\psi_{n,a,\delta})_{n=0}^{\infty}
for general
s_{1}<s_{2}

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n,a,\delta}|C^{(1)}_{\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle}\psi_{m,a,\delta}\rangle=

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{e^{is_{2}(p-q)}-e^{is_{1}(p-q)}}{p-q}e^{-a(p+q)}

\displaystyle-\frac{E_{n}(a,\delta)E_{m}(a,\delta)}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\>p^{n+\delta}q^{m+\delta}P(p,q)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}dt\>e^{it(p-q)}e^{-a(p+q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{E_{n}(a,\delta)E_{m}(a,\delta)}{4\pi}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}dt\>(I_{+}(n,m,\delta;t)+I_{-}(n,m,\delta;t)),

(D.2)

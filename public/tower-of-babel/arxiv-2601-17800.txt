Differentiable Integer Programming is not Differentiable & it’s not a mere technical problem.
Thanawat Sornwanee

Abstract
We show how the differentiability method employed in the paper “Differentiable Integer Linear Programming”, Geng, et al., 2025 [ 5 ] as shown in its theorem 5 is incorrect. Moreover, there already exists some downstream work that inherits the same error. The underlying reason comes from that, though being continuous in expectation, the surrogate loss is discontinuous in almost every realization of the randomness, for the stochastic gradient descent.

1.1
Why Continuity Matters: a Minimal Example

As a simple example, we can consider a 1-dimensional case when the function of interest is

\displaystyle f\left(\hat{x},\epsilon\right):=\mathbf{1}_{\epsilon\leq\hat{x}}\hat{x},

making
\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}}f\left(\hat{x},\epsilon\right)=\mathbf{1}_{\epsilon\leq\hat{x}}
for any
\hat{x}\in(0,1)-\{\epsilon\}

This makes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim\text{Unif}((0,1))}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}}f\left(\hat{x},\epsilon\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim\text{Unif}((0,1))}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\epsilon\leq\hat{x}}\right]=\int_{\epsilon=0}^{\hat{x}}1d\epsilon=\hat{x},

while

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\hat{x}}\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim\text{Unif}((0,1))}\left[f\left(\hat{x},\epsilon\right)\right]=\frac{d}{d\hat{x}}\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim\text{Unif}((0,1))}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\epsilon\leq\hat{x}}\hat{x}\right]=\frac{d}{d\hat{x}}\left[\int_{\epsilon=0}^{\hat{x}}\hat{x}d\epsilon\right]=2\hat{x}.

\hat{x}

In this simple example, we can see that the derivative can be fixed by adding a boundary term so that

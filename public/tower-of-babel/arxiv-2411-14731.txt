Anti-Rota-Baxter operators on Witt and Virasoro algebras
Azizov M. E.

Abstract




Abstract.
In this work, we obtain the description of all homogeneous anti-Rota-Baxter operators on Witt and Virasoro algebras. Moreover, we describe anti-Rota-Baxter operators on three-dimensional simple Lie algebra
sl_{2}.

Keywords:
Anti-Rota-Baxter operators, Witt algebra, Virasoro algebras.

MSC (2020):17A36,17B38,17B68.

1.
Introduction

The concept of Rota-Baxter operators is used in combinatorics and probability theory. Later, it was used in algebra. In 1960, Glen Baxter introduced a Baxter equation, which satisfies a certain property related to probability distributions
[
1
]
, using Rota-Baxter operators,

2(a(aT))T=(a^{2}b)T+(aT)^{2}.

In 1983, M.A. Semenov-Tian-Shansky studied the case of Rota-Baxter equations with weight 0 from the perspective of Lie algebras, interpreting them as Yang-Baxter equations
[
14
]
. The concept of Rota-Baxter operators goes back to Richard Baxters and Gian-Carlo Rota and found several fields of mathematics
[
13
]
.
Rota–Baxter operator of weight
0
on a Lie algebra is exactly the operator form of the classical Yang–Baxter equation (CYBE), which was regarded as a “classical limit” of the quantum Yang–Baxter
equation
[
2
]
, whereas the latter is also an important topic in many fields such as symplectic geometry, integrable systems,
quantum groups and quantum field theory
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

By the 21st century, Rota-Baxter operators experienced a period of advancement. This can be seen in the algebraic renormalization method in quantum field theory, dendriform algebras, the associative analog of the classical Yang-Baxter equation, and it works on systems with mixed commutative and non-commutative products.

A linear operator
R:A\rightarrow A
is called a Rota-Baxter operator on
A
of weight
\lambda
if the
following identity

(1.1)

[R(x),R(y)]=R([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]+\lambda xy),

holds for any
x,y\in A.

It is known that if
R
is a Rota-Baxter operator weight
\lambda
with
\lambda\neq 0,
then the
operator
\lambda^{-1}R
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1. Moreover, if
d
is an invertible derivation of an algebra
A
, then
d^{-1}
forms a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero
[
9
]
.

Initially, Filippov and Hopkins introduced the concept of anti-derivations in their works
[
3
,
6
]
. Later, Filippov generalized the notions of derivations and anti-derivations introducing the notion of
\delta
-derivation
[
4
]
.
Later, the concept of
\delta
-derivations has been further studied, giving rise to significant results
[
5
,
15
,
16
]
.
In particular, it was proved that prime Lie algebras, do not have nonzero
\delta
-derivations (provided
\delta\neq 1,-1,0,\frac{1}{2}
), and alternative, non-Lie Malcev algebras with certain restrictions of
\mathbb{F}
have no non-trivial
\delta
-derivation. Zusmanovich in
[
16
]
proved that a prime Lie superalgebra has no non-trivial
\delta
-(super)derivations for
\delta\neq 1,-1,0,\frac{1}{2}
.

Based on the principal relation between derivations and Rota-Baxter operators it can be introduced the notion of
\delta
-Rota-Baxter operator weight
0
with the condition
[R(x),R(y)]=\delta R([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]).

Similarly, to the derivation case, any invertible
\delta
-derivation of the algebra
A
gives
\delta
-Rota-Baxter operator weight
0
.
On the other hand, the structure for constructing anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operators was developed by Kupershmidt
[
11
]
, and according to it, the following definition of the anti-Rota-Baxter operator arises.

Definition 1.1
.

An
anti-Rota-Baxter operator
on an algebra
\mathfrak{g}

over the field
\mathbb{F}
is
a linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(1.2)

[R(x),R(y)]=-R([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]),\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

An anti-Rota-Baxter operator
R
is called a
strong
if R satisfies

(1.3)

[[R(x),R(y)],z]+[[R(y),R(z)],x]+[[R(z),R(x)],y]=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

In
[
10
]
the homogeneous Rota-Baxter operators on the Witt and Virasoro algebras were classified. In this paper, we obtain anti-Rota-Baxter operators on the Witt and Virasoro algebras by using this method. Moreover, we classify anti-Rota-Baxter operators on three-dimensional simple Lie algebra
sl_{2}.
It should be noted that Rota-Baxter operators on
sl_{2}
were obtained by J. Pei, C. Bai, and L. Guo in
[
12
]
.

2.
Homogeneous Anti-Rota-Baxter operators on the Witt and Virasoro algebra

We also assume that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, is the complex field since both the Witt and Virasoro algebras are defined over
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Witt algebra

W
is a Lie algebra with the basis
\{L_{n}\ |\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and multiptications:

(2.1)

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\quad\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

There is a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on the Witt algebra
W
, namely

W=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{n},

where
W_{n}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{n}\}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
k
be an integer. A
homogeneous operator
R
with degree
k
on the Witt algebra
W
is a linear operator on
W
satisfying

R(W_{m})\subset W_{m+k},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore, a
homogeneous anti-Rota-Baxter operator
R_{k}
with degree
k
on the Witt algebra
W
is an anti-Rota-Baxter operator on
W

of the following form

(2.2)

R_{k}(L_{m})=f(m+k)L_{m+k},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z},

where
f
is a
\mathbb{C}
-valued function defined on
\mathbb{Z}
.

2.1.
Homogeneous anti-Rota-Baxter operators on the Witt algebra

Let
R_{k}
be a homogeneous anti-Rota-Baxter operator with degree
k
on the Witt algebra

W
satisfying equation (
2.2
). Then by equations (
1.2
) and (
2.1
), we obtain that the function
f
satisfies the following equation:

(2.3)

f(m)f(n)(n-m)=f(m+n)(f(m)(m-n+k)+f(n)(m-n-k)),\quad\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Consider the following cases.

Case 1.
Let
k=0.
Then the equation (
2.3
) becomes

(2.4)

(m-n)\Big(f(m)f(n)+f(m+n)\big(f(m)+f(n)\big)\Big)=0,\;\;\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Putting
n=0
in the equation, we have

(2.5)

mf(m)(f(m)+2f(0))=0.

If
f(0)=0,
then we derive
f(m)=0
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Now we assume
f(0)\neq 0
and define following sets:

I=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}:f(m)=0\},\qquad J=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}:f(m)=-2f(0)\}.

Since
f(0)\neq 0,
we easily obtain that
I\bigcap J=\varnothing
and
I\bigcup J=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n
be a nonzero integer, then
n\in I
if and only if
-n\in I.

Proof.

Taking
m=-n
in (
2.4
), we get

f(-n)f(n)+f(0)\big(f(-n)+f(n)\big)=0.

From this equation we obtain that
f(n)=0
if and only if
f(-n)=0.

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

The anti-Rota-Baxter operator
R_{0}
with degree
0

is given by

R_{0}(L_{n})=\begin{cases}0,\quad\quad n\neq 0,\\
\alpha L_{0},\quad n=0,\end{cases}

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
f(p)=-2f(0)\neq 0,
for
p\neq 0,
then putting
m=2p,n=-p
in (
2.4
), we have

f(2p-p)(f(2p)+f(-p))+f(2p)f(-p)=0.

Then by Lemma
2.3
, we get that
f(-p)=f(p)=-2f(0)\neq 0,

which implies
2f(2p)+f(-p)=0
. Then, we get
f(2p)=-\frac{1}{2}f(-p)=f(0).
But
f(2p)=0
or
f(2p)=-2f(0).
From this, we get
f(0)=0,
which is a contradiction. Hence,
f(p)=0
for all
p\neq 0.

∎

Case 2.
Let
k\neq 0.
In the case of
f(0)=0,
we have the following Proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
k\neq 0
and
f
satisfies the equation (
2.3
). If
f(0)=0
, then

f(m)=\begin{cases}0,&m\neq-k,\\
\alpha,&m=-k,\end{cases}

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

If
f(0)=0
, then taking
n=0
in the equation (
2.3
),
we have

(m+k)(f(m))^{2}=0,\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus,
f(m)=0
for
m\neq-k
and
f(-k)
can be any complex value.
∎

Now consider the case of
f(0)\neq 0.
Then putting
m=0,n=\frac{k}{2},
in (
2.3
), we get
f(\frac{k}{2})=0.
Now setting
n=0
in (
2.3

f(m)\Big(f(0)(2m-k)+(m+k)f(m)\Big)=0.

We set
\mathcal{I}

For these sets we can write
\mathcal{I}\bigcup\mathcal{J}=\mathbb{Z},\ \ \mathcal{I}\bigcap\mathcal{J}=\varnothing.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f
be a
\mathbb{C}
-valued function defined on
\mathbb{Z}
satisfying equation (
2.3
). Suppose that
f(0)\neq 0
and

and

if and only if

Proof.

If
m\in\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle(m-n-k)f(n)f(n+m)=0.

Conversely, if

(m-n)f(m)=0.

Since

Proposition 2.13
.

Let

\tfrac{-k}{2}\in\mathcal{J}
, then
\mathcal{J}=\{0,-\tfrac{k}{2}\}

(2.6)

-\tfrac{k}{2}

Proposition 2.14
.

Let

(2.7)

Since

2.2.
Homogeneous anti-Rota-Baxter operators on the Virasoro algebra

Definition 2.16
.

The
Virasoro algebra

V
is a Lie algebra with the basis

\{L_{m},C|m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfying the following relations:

(2.8)

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\tfrac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}C,\quad\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The Virasoro algebra
V
is a central extension of the Witt algebra
W
,
and has a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading as well:

\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n},

where
V_{n}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{n}\}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ast}
and
V_{0}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{0},C\}
.

Hence
homogeneous anti-Rota-Baxter operator
R_{k}
with degree
k
on the Virasoro algebra
V
is an operator on
V
with the following form:

(2.9)

R_{k}(L_{m})=f(m+k)L_{m+k}+\theta\delta_{m+k,0}C,\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{Z};

(2.10)

R_{k}(C)=\mu L_{k}+\nu\delta_{k,0}C,

where
f
is a
\mathbb{C}
-valued function defined on
\mathbb{Z}
and
\theta,\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{C}

3.
Anti-Rota-Baxter operators on the algebra

Let’s consider the following 3-dimensional Lie algebra
sl_{2}(\mathbb{C})=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
with the table of multiplications:

(3.1)

\left[e_{1},e_{2}\right]=e_{3},\qquad\left[e_{1},e_{3}\right]=2e_{1},\qquad\left[e_{2},e_{3}\right]=-2e_{2},\qquad

Let
R
be an anti-Rota-Baxter operator on
sl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and its matrix on the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
has the form:

\left(\begin{matrix}a&b&c\\
d&g&h\\
k&l&m\end{matrix}\right).

Theorem 3.1
.

The matrix of the anti-Rota-Baxter operator on the algebra
sl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
has one of the following form:

\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&0\\
0&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right),\ \left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&h\\
0&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(h\neq 0),\ \left(\begin{matrix}0&b&c\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(c\neq 0),\ \left(\begin{matrix}0&b&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(b\neq 0),

\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&-\frac{k}{2}\\
k&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(k\neq 0),\ \left(\begin{matrix}0&b&0\\
0&0&-\frac{k}{2}\\
k&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(b\cdot k\neq 0),\ \left(\begin{matrix}0&b&c\\
d&0&\frac{bd}{4c}\\
-\frac{bd}{2c}&-2c&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(c\neq 0),\ \left(\begin{matrix}a&-\frac{l^{2}}{4a}&0\\
\frac{4a^{3}}{l^{2}}&-a&0\\
-\frac{4a^{2}}{l}&l&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(a\cdot l\neq 0),

\left(\begin{matrix}a&b&c\\
d&a&h\\
-2h&-2c&\frac{bd-a^{2}-4ch}{2a}\\
\end{matrix}\right)(a\neq 0),\left(\begin{matrix}a&\frac{4c^{2}g}{(a+g)^{2}}&c\\
\frac{a(a+g)^{2}}{4c^{2}}&g&\frac{(a+g)^{2}}{4c}\\
-\frac{a(a+g)}{c}&-\frac{4cg}{a+g}&-a-g\\
\end{matrix}\right)(c\neq 0,a\neq\pm g).

Among these operators, the following are strong anti-Rota-Baxter:

\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&0\\
0&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right),\ \left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&h\\
0&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(h\neq 0),\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&-\frac{k}{2}\\
k&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(k\neq 0),\left(\begin{matrix}0&b&0\\
0&0&-\frac{k}{2}\\
k&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(b\cdot k\neq 0).

Proof.

Let
R
be an anti-Rota-Baxter operator on
sl_{2}(\mathbb{C}).
Then we have

(3.2)

[R(e_{i}),R(e_{j})]+R([R(e_{i}),e_{j}]+[e_{i},R(e_{j})])=0,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq 3.

Considering all possible cases for
i
and
j,
we obtain the following relations:

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{|l|l|l|}\hline\cr\text{for }i=1,j=2&\Rightarrow&\begin{array}[]{l}4ah+(a+g)k=0\\
4cg+(a+g)l=0\\
-bd+ag+4ch+am+gm=0\\
\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\text{for }i=1,j=3&\Rightarrow&\begin{array}[]{l}2a^{2}-2bd-2ck+kl+4am=0\\
2ab-2bg+2cl+l^{2}=0\\
2ac-2bh-bk+al+2cm+lm=0\\
\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\text{for }i=2,j=3&\Rightarrow&\begin{array}[]{l}2ad-2dg-2hk-k^{2}=0\\
2bd-2g^{2}+2hl-kl-4gm=0\\
2cd-2gh-gk+dl-2hm-km=0\\
\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Firstly, let us observe
4cg+(a+g)l=0.
From this, we derive that if
a+g=0,
then
g=0
or
c=0.
Thus, we consider following cases:

Case 1.
Let
a=g=0.
Then we get

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}4ch-bd=0,\\
2bd+k(2c-l)=0,\\
l(2c+l)=0,\\
b(2h+k)-m(2c+l)=0,\\
k(2h+k)=0,\\
2bd+l(2h-k)=0,\\
d(2c+l)-m(2h+k)=0.\end{array}\right.

Now we obtain three possible subcases:

Case 1.1.
Let
l=0,

k=0.
Then we have

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}4ch-bd=0,\\
bd=0,\\
bh-cm=0,\\
cd-hm=0.\end{array}\right.

If
b=0,
then we get
ch=0,

cm=0,

cd=hm,
which implies

– when
c=0:

\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&0\\
0&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right),\quad\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&h\\
0&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(h\neq 0).

– when
c\neq 0,
then we get
m=d=0,

h=0
and obtain

\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&c\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right).

If
b\neq 0,
then
d=0,

h=0
and
cm=0.
So we get:

\left(\begin{matrix}0&b&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(b\neq 0),\quad\left(\begin{matrix}0&b&c\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(b\cdot c\neq 0).

Case 1.2.
Let
l=0,

k\neq 0.
Then
h=-\frac{k}{2}
and we have

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}ck=0,\\
cm=0,\\
bd=0,\\
cd=0.\end{array}\right.

From this, we have
c=0
and obtain the following two solutions:

\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
d&0&-\frac{k}{2}\\
k&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(k\neq 0),\quad\left(\begin{matrix}0&b&0\\
0&0&\frac{-k}{2}\\
k&0&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(b\cdot k\neq 0).

Case 1.3.
Let
l=-2c\neq 0.
Then we obtain
h=\frac{bd}{4c},

k=-\frac{bd}{2c},
and get the solution

\left(\begin{matrix}0&b&c\\
d&0&\frac{bd}{4c}\\
-\frac{bd}{2c}&-2c&m\\
\end{matrix}\right)(c\neq 0).

Case 2.
Let
a=-g\neq 0.
Then
c=h=0
and we have
b=-\frac{l^{2}}{4a},

d=\frac{4a^{3}}{l^{2}},

m=0,

k=-\frac{4a^{2}}{l}.
Thus, we obtain

\left(\begin{matrix}a&-\frac{l^{2}}{4a}&0\\
\frac{4a^{3}}{l^{2}}&-a&0\\
-\frac{4a^{2}}{l}&l&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)(l\cdot g\neq 0).

Case 3.
Let
a\neq-g.
Then we obtain
Substituting
k=-\frac{4ah}{a+g},

l=-\frac{4cg}{a+g},

m=\frac{bd-ag-4ch}{a+g}
and the following system:

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{(a-g)\left((a+g)(bd+a^{2})-4ach\right)}{(a+g)^{2}}=0,\\
\frac{(a-g)\left(b(a+g)^{2}-4c^{2}g\right)}{(a+g)^{2}}=0,\\
\frac{(a-g)\left((a^{2}+bd)c-4c^{2}h+bh(a+g)\right)}{(a+g)^{2}}=0,\\
\frac{(a-g)\left(d(a+g)^{2}-4ah^{2}\right)}{(a+g)^{2}}=0,\\
\frac{(a-g)\left((a+g)(bd+g^{2})-4cgh\right)}{(a+g)^{2}}=0,\\
\frac{(a-g)\left((g^{2}+bd)h-4ch^{2}+cd(a+g)\right)}{(a+g)^{2}}=0.\end{array}\right.

Consider the following two subcases.

Case 3.1.
Let
a=g.
Then we get the system (
3.7
) is hold for any
b,d,c,h
and
k=-2h,

l=-2c,

Thus, we obtain

\left(\begin{matrix}a&b&c\\

-2h&-2c&\frac{bd-a^{2}-4ch}{2a}\\
\end{matrix}\right)(a\neq 0).

Case 3.2.
Let

Subtracting the fifth equation from the first equation of the system (
3.7
), we obtain

Substituting to the other equations, we get.

Hence, we have

\left(\begin{matrix}a&\frac{4c^{2}g}{(a+g)^{2}}&c\\
\frac{a(a+g)^{2}}{4c^{2}}&g&\frac{(a+g)^{2}}{4c}\\
-\frac{a(a+g)}{c}&-\frac{4cg}{a+g}&-a-g\\
\end{matrix}\right)(c\neq 0,a\neq\pm g).

∎

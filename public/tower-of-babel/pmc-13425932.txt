Echinatin attenuates LPS-induced mastitis by suppressing IL-17RA and MAPK/NF-κB signaling
Authors: Dan Shao, Hao Zhang, Hongjuan Zhang, Baocheng Hao, Qiangwen Gu, Yu Liu, Zhen Yang, Di Wu, Shengyi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMastitis severely impairs milk yield and quality, as well as the health and productivity of dairy animals. Although antibiotics are the primary therapeutic option, their associated side effects drive the search for safer alternatives from botanical sources. Echinatin (Ech), a natural chalcone with known anti-inflammatory properties, has not been evaluated for its efficacy against mastitis. This study aimed to investigate the anti-inflammatory effects and underlying mechanisms of Ech using lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced bovine mammary epithelial cells (BMECs) and a mouse mastitis model.ResultsThe results demonstrated that Ech exhibited an anti-inflammatory effect by reducing the levels of inflammatory factors (tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6 and IL-17A) and decreasing protein levels of inflammatory mediators (cyclooxygenase-2 (COX2), and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)) in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, RNA-seq analysis identified an association with the IL-17 A receptor (IL-17RA) signaling following Ech intervention, which was subsequently validated by western blot showing that Ech suppressed the activation of both IL-17RA and the downstream MAPK/NF-κB pathways. Furthermore, in a recombine IL-17A (rIL-17A)-induced inflammatory model in BMECs, Ech attenuated the exacerbated inflammatory response by restraining IL-17RA and the downstream MAPK/NF-κB activation. Importantly, blocking IL-17RA signaling abolished the anti-inflammatory and pathway-inhibitory effects of Ech. Collectively, these results demonstrate a vital role of IL-17RA and MAPK/NF-κB pathway for Ech-mediated protection against mammary gland inflammation.ConclusionsOur findings reveal that Ech attenuates LPS‑induced inflammation in BMECs and a mouse model of mastitis by suppressing IL-17RA signaling and thereby inactivating the MAPK/NF-κB pathway, offering new insights for optimizing preventive strategies against mastitis in dairy cows.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-026-05583-9.Keywords: Bovine mammary epithelial cells, Echinatin, IL-17RA, MAPK/NF-κB signaling, Mastitis

Introduction

Mastitis, an inflammation of the mammary glands, is one of the most common diseases in dairy farming [1]. It not only damages milk production, composition, nutritional value and processing properties but also threatens the health of both dairy animals and human, causing enormous economic losses to the dairy farming industry and related sectors [2, 3]. Antimicrobial agents remain the primary clinical strategy for mastitis management [4]. However, the indiscriminate antibiotic use has driven the emergence of antimicrobial resistance and resulted in persistent drug residues in dairy products, constituting a substantial threat to global public health and food safety [5–7]. Therefore, the development of safer and more effective agents for preventing mastitis is urgently warranted.

Escherichia coli, one of the most prevalent Gram-negative bacteria, is a major causative pathogen of mastitis [8, 9]. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a major component of the outer membrane in Gram-negative bacterial cell walls, serves as a critical pathogenic determinant in mastitis pathogenesis [10]. During mammary gland infection, bacteria first adhere to and invade mammary epithelial cells, followed by the release of pathogenic factors such as LPS [11]. This triggers a cascade of inflammatory responses, ultimately leading to the substantial production of cytokines and inflammatory mediators. Although this inflammatory reaction is initially intended to clear the infection, it often becomes dysregulated, leading to sustained and damaging inflammation within the mammary gland [12, 13]. Given the current understanding of mastitis pathogenesis, targeting excessive inflammatory activation represents a promising strategy for limiting bystander tissue damage and systemic complications associated with mastitis.

The IL-17 receptor (IL-17R) family comprises five subtypes, with IL-17RA serving as the central signaling subunit in receptor complexes [14]. Upon IL-17A binding to the IL-17RA/IL-17RC heterodimer, the critical adaptor molecule nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) activator 1 (ACT1) is recruited, leading to downstream activation of TNF receptor associated factor 6 (TRAF6) [15, 16]. This initiates signaling cascades that activate the NF-κB and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways (including extracellular regulated protein kinases (ERK), c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), and p38), driving upregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-17) and chemokines (Chemokine (C-X-C motif) Ligand 1 (CXCL1), CXCL2, CXCL5 and Chemokine (C-C motif) Ligand 20 (CCL20)) to amplify inflammatory response [17, 18]. Although IL-17RA signaling is well-established as a key mediator in various inflammatory diseases [19–21], its specific expression profile, functional role, and therapeutic potential in bovine mammary gland inflammation remain largely unexplored. In a genome‑wide association study of milk somatic cell score in Ethiopian dairy cattle, enrichment analysis revealed that genes within the significant genomic windows are involved in the IL‑17 signaling pathway. This genetic evidence underscores the role of IL‑17-related mechanisms in mastitis susceptibility and mammary gland inflammation across diverse cattle populations [22]. Therefore, identifying candidate drugs that inhibit IL-17RA signaling could represent a potential anti-inflammatory strategy for the management of mastitis.

Natural products constitute rich repositories of chemical diversity, guiding the discovery of bioactive compounds with modulatory activity against IL-17RA signaling pathway. Echinatin (Ech) is an active substance isolated from the root of liquorice [23]. Numerous studies have reported that Ech possesses multifaceted pharmacological activities including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, liver-protective, and cytoprotective properties [24–26]. Although the role of Ech in modulating inflammation has been well-documented, its specific molecular mechanisms of action against the anti-inflammatory activity in mastitis remain poorly characterized. We therefore hypothesized that Ech attenuates LPS-induced mastitis by suppressing IL-17RA signaling and its downstream MAPK/NF-κB pathways. The primary objectives of this study were to evaluate the anti-inflammatory effect of Ech in LPS-stimulated bovine mammary epithelial cells (BMECs) and in a mouse model of mastitis; and to validate the role of IL-17RA in mediating the protective effect of Ech through pharmacological blockade, with a focus on the downstream MAPK/NF-κB pathways.

Materials and methods

Regents

Echinatin (purity > 99%, HY-N0269) and Ixekizumab (Ixe, HY-P9924) were purchased from MedChemExpress (Shanghai, China). Recombinant Bovine IL-17 A (rIL-17A, CSB-YP724243BO) was bought from CUSABIO Biotech (Wuhan, China). LPS (L2880) was obtained from Sigma Aldrich (Shanghai, China).

Animals and experimental design

BALB/c mice (8–10 weeks old, 20–25 g weight) were procured from the Laboratory Animal Center of Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Lanzhou, China). The animals were housed in individually ventilated cages under controlled conditions (ambient temperature 22–23 °C, relative humidity 50–60%, and 12 h light/dark cycle), with continuous ad libitum access to sterile water and rodent chow. Two females and one male were initially co-housed in a standard laboratory cage to facilitate mating. Following confirmed pregnancy, each gravid female was transferred to an individual ventilated cage and allocated to distinct treatment groups. Thirty lactating mice were randomly divided into three groups (n = 10 per group): Control (PBS, 50 µL), LPS (0.2 mg/mL, 50 µL), and LPS + Ech (40 mg/kg/d). The dosages of LPS and Ech administered in the animal experiments were optimized based on previously reported literature [26, 27]. The control group was intraperitoneal injected with PBS and the LPS + Ech group were intraperitoneal injected Ech for 7 consecutive days during lactation days 4–10. On lactation day 9, LPS was delivered into the fourth mammary duct via a microsyringe. After 24 h, all mice were euthanized, and mammary gland tissues and blood serum were collected for subsequent histopathological, molecular, and biochemical analyses.

Cell culture and treatment

The bovine mammary epithelial cell line (BMECs, sourced from JINIOU company, Guangzhou, China) was maintained in RPMI 1640 (176973075, Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, AUS-01 S-02, Cell Box, Germany) at 37°C under a 5% CO2 atmosphere. To evaluate the effect of Ech, the cells were pretreated with different concentrations of Ech (2, 4, 8 µmol/L) for 12 h, followed by co-treatment with or without LPS (1 µg/mL) for an additional 12 h. To investigate the involvement of the IL-17RA pathway on the protective effect of Ech, cells were pretreated with rIL-17 A (1 µg/mL) for 6 h in the absence or presence of Ech (8 µmol/L) for 24 h. Additionally, cells were treated with Ixe (10 µg/mL) for 6 h in advance and/or were treated with Ech (8 µM) for 12 h before exposed to LPS for 12 h.

Cell viability assay

The cell viability was evaluated using CCK-8 kit (HY-K0301, MedChemExpress). BMECs were plated on 96-well plates (4 × 103 cells/well) and cultured with various concentrations of Ech for 12 h. Subsequently, cell viability was assessed after 12 h of treatment with Ech and LPS, followed by CCK‑8 incubation for 2 h at 37 °C. The optical density of the samples was measured at 450 nm.

RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from BMECS using TRIzol® Reagent (Magen, Guangzhou, China) according the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity were assessed by Nanodrop ND-2000 system (Thermo Scientific, USA). For RNA-seq analysis, 56.79 million clean reads per sample were generated on an Illumina Novaseq 6000 platform by APTBIO (Shanghai, China). Genes with fold change ≥ 2 and adjusted P value ≤ 0.05 were identified as differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Bioinformatics analyses, including gene expression heatmap construction and KEGG pathway enrichment, were performed using the APT-BioCloud tool.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells or mammary tissue using TRIzol reagent (AG21102, Accurate Biology, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity were evaluated using a Nanodrop ND‑2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA); only samples with A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.0 and A260/A230 ratios ≥ 1.8 were used. RNA integrity was confirmed by observing discrete 28 S and 18 S rRNA bands on a 1% agarose gel. To eliminate contaminating genomic DNA, 800 ng of total RNA was treated with the Evo M‑MLV RT Premix (with gDNA Clean) for qPCR Ver.2 (AG11728, Accurate Biology), which includes a gDNA removal step. cDNA was then synthesized using the same kit following the manufacturer’s protocol. Real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green Premix (Accurate Biology) on a Quantstudio 6 Flex system (Applied Biosystems, San Francisco, CA, USA) under the following conditions: 95 ℃ for 30 s; 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 5 s and 60 ℃ for 30 s, with final melting curve analysis. The relative mRNA levels of target genes were normalized to β-actin and calculated using the 2−△△CT method. The primer sequences are shown in Table 1.

Western Blotting (WB)

Mouse mammary gland tissue and BMECs were lysed in ice-cold RIPA buffer (R0010, Solarbio, Beijing, China) supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail (P6731, Solarbio). Immunoreactive bands were detected using an enhanced chemiluminescent reagent (NCM Biotech, Suzhou, China) and visualized via a gel imaging system GBOX-CHEMI-XRQ (Cambridge, Britain). Quantitative analysis was performed with ImageJ software using β-actin for normalization.

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

BMECs were grown on coverslips, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min, and blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h at room temperature. The cells were then incubated with a primary antibody against NF-κB p65 overnight at 4 °C, followed by a 1 h incubation with Alexa Fluor™ 594-conjugated secondary antibody in the dark at room temperature. Nuclear staining was performed with DAPI (C1006, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Confocal laser microscopy (LSM800, ZEISS, Germany) was used to acquire all images.

Hematoxylin-Eosin (H&E) staining and Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Following fixation with 4% formalin, mouse mammary tissues processed for paraffin embedding and were sectioned into 5 μm. Subsequently, H&E staining and IHC of the mammary sections was performed. For IHC experiments, sections were incubated with primary antibodies against IL-17RA, ACT1, and TRAF6 overnight at 4°C. Staining intensity was scored as: 0 (negative), 1 (weak), 2 (moderate), and 3 (strong). The percentage of positively stained cells at each intensity level was recorded, and a final immunoreactivity score was calculated by multiplying the intensity score by the percentage of positive cells. The IHC analysis was performed using ImageJ (IHC Profiler plugin) software.

Statistical analysis

Data were expressed as mean ± standard error of mean (SEM). Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (San Diego, California, USA). All experiments were carried out at least three times independently. Comparisons among multiple groups under a single independent variable were analyzed using one-way ANOVA. Comparisons involving two independent variables were analyzed using two-way ANOVA. For comparisons among all groups, Tukey’s post hoc test was used. For comparisons against a single control group, Dunnett’s post hoc test was applied. For experiments involving two independent variables, two‑way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni’s post hoc test was performed. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Ech suppresses inflammatory response in LPS‑induced BMECs

As determined by the cytotoxicity assay, Ech (2, 4, 8 µmol/L) + LPS did not affect the cell viability of BMECs (Fig. 1A, P > 0.05). To verify the effect of Ech on LPS-induced inflammatory response in BMECs, the expression of inflammatory factors and mediators were detected. As shown in Fig. 1B-E, the transcript abundance of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-17A was up-regulated after LPS exposure compared to the control group (P < 0.01), and this effect was dose-dependently attenuated with Ech pretreatment (P < 0.05). Consistently, Ech decreased LPS-induced protein expression of COX2 and iNOS (Fig. 1F-H, P < 0.01). These results demonstrate that Ech prevents LPS‑induced inflammatory response in BMECs.

Ech regulates IL-17RA and the downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling in LPS‑induced BMECs

To investigate the underlying mechanisms of Ech on preventing LPS‑induced inflammatory response, RNA-seq analysis was performed on LPS-induced BMECs with or without Ech pretreatment. As depicted in Fig. 2A, transcriptomic analysis revealed 706 differentially expressed genes (DEGs, P < 0.05) in the LPS vs. Con comparison (126 upregulated, 580 downregulated) and 2,497 DEGs in the EL vs. LPS comparison (1,715 upregulated, 782 downregulated). KEGG analysis (Fig. 2B) revealed that notable alterations in several key pathways between the Con and LPS groups, including the IL-17 signaling pathway, NF-κB signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and TNF signaling pathway. Strikingly, Ech treatment intervened in the expressions of IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction under LPS exposure. Given the regulatory role of IL-17 signaling in modulating TNF signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, and NF-κB signaling pathway, we focused on investigating the expression profiles of genes associated with the IL-17 signaling pathway. The heatmap showed that LPS stimulation in BMECs induced the activation of IL-17RA signaling and downstream related genes expressions, whereas Ech pretreated inhibited these expression levels (Fig. 2C). Western blot analysis further verified the effect of Ech on modulating IL-17RA signaling and downstream signaling. As shown in Fig. 2D-M, Ech pretreatment suppressed the activation of IL-17RA signaling (IL-17RA, ACT1, TRAF6), NF-κB signaling (p-IκBα, p-p65) and MAPK signaling (p-ERK, p-JNK, p-p38) (P < 0.05). Simultaneously, immunofluorescence staining showed that LPS stimulation induced p65 nuclear translocation, as evidenced by the increased red fluorescence in the nuclear region, whereas Ech pretreatment suppressed this translocation (Fig. 2H-I). Collectively, these results suggest that the effect of Ech on alleviating LPS-induced inflammatory response in BMECs is associated with blocked the activation of IL-17RA and the downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling.

Ech inhibites rIL-17 A-induced inflammatory response through IL-17RA and MAPK/NF-κB signaling

Considering that Ech may suppress IL-17RA signaling to mitigate LPS-induced inflammatory response, a rIL-17A-induced BMECs model was established to mimic inflammatory conditions, and the anti-inflammatory effect of Ech was further investigated. As shown in Fig. 3A-K, Ech pretreatment decreased the expression of IL-17RA signaling and MAPK/NF-κB signaling proteins in rIL-17A-induced BMECs, including IL-17RA, ACT1, TRAF6, p-IκBα, p-p65, p-ERK, p-JNK, and p-p38 (P < 0.05). Similarly, Ech pretreatment downregulated the mRNA levels of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-17A) and IL-17RA signaling downstream chemokines genes (CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL5, CCL20, CCL2) (Fig. 3L-T, P < 0.05). These findings indicate that Ech inhibits the activation of IL-17RA signaling and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling to attenuate inflammatory response in BMECs.

Ech ameliorates LPS‑induced inflammatory response depended on IL-17RA and MAPK/NF-κB signaling

To further validate the effect of Ech on regulating inflammatory response through IL-17RA signaling, we employed Ixe, an IL-17A-neutralizing monoclonal antibody, to block IL-17RA signaling. Western blot analysis revealed that Ixe supplementation abolished Ech-induced downregulation of IL-17RA signaling and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling protein levels in LPS-induced BMECs (Fig. 4A-K, P < 0.05). Consistently, following Ixe treatment, the suppressive effect of Ech on the mRNA levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-17A) and IL-17RA downstream chemokines (CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL5, CCL20 and CCL2) was lost, with expression levels returning to those observed in the LPS-only group (Fig. 4L-T, P < 0.05). These data suggest that Ech requires IL-17RA signaling axis to exert its anti-inflammatory effect in LPS-induced BMECs, and that the downstream MAPK/NF-κB pathways mediate this protection.

Ech attenuates LPS‑induced mastitis in mice

To validate the in vitro findings described above, we conducted an in vivo study to evaluated the potential effect of Ech on LPS-induced mastitis using a mouse model. As depicted in Fig. 5A, gross anatomical observation of mammary glands revealed severe inflammation in the LPS group, which were significantly ameliorated by Ech administration. H&E staining further demonstrated that LPS stimulation induced damaged acinuses and destroyed epithelial cells, accompanied by inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 5B). Conversely, Ech pretreatment moderated the LPS‑induced pathological alterations in the mammary gland, and although it did not fully restore the tissue to the control state, it significantly reduced the severity of LPS‑induced tissue damage and inflammatory infiltration. Furthermore, Ech reduced the levels of inflammatory factors in serum (Fig. 5C-F, P < 0.05) and relative mRNA expression of inflammatory factors and chemokines in mammary gland (Fig. 5G-O, P < 0.05), as well as decreased the protein expression of COX2 and iNOS in mammary gland (Fig. 5P-R, P < 0.05). These results indicate that Ech administration attenuates LPS-induced inflammation of in mouse mammary gland.

Effect of Ech on regulating IL-17RA signaling and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling in LPS-induced mastitis in mice

Given the above findings that the anti-inflammatory effect of Ech is closely relevant to IL-17RA signaling, the protein levels of IL-17RA signaling in mouse mammary gland were determined. IHC analysis revealed that IL-17RA, ACT1, and TRAF6 were primarily localized in the cytoplasm of mammary epithelial cells. LPS challenge markedly enhanced the staining intensity of all three proteins, whereas Ech pretreatment attenuated this increase (Fig. 6A-D, P < 0.05). These findings were further supported by the reduced protein expression of mammary gland for IL-17RA, ACT1 and TRAF6 following Ech pretreatment prior to LPS challenge (Fig. 6E-H). Additionally, pretreatment with Ech before the LPS challenge decreased phosphorylation levels of MAPK/NF-κB signaling proteins, including p-p65, p-IκBα, p-ERK, p-JNK and p-p38 (Fig. 6I-N, P < 0.05). These results suggest that Ech suppresses IL-17RA signaling and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling in LPS-challenged murine mastitis.

Discussion

Mastitis, a prevalent inflammatory disease of the mammary gland in animals during the postpartum lactation period, is predominantly caused by pathogenic microbial infections [28, 29]. Given the growing public concern over antimicrobial-resistant pathogens in milk associated with mastitis resistance, there is an urgent need to develop novel and effective therapeutic agents, driving increased research interest in natural product for mastitis management [30–32]. In this study, we demonstrated that Ech exerted an anti-inflammatory effect by inhibiting IL-17RA signaling and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling in LPS-induced BMECs and a mouse mastitis model. Our findings revealed that Ech may exert protective effects against LPS-induced mastitis in experimental models, warranting further investigation as a potential preventive strategy.

IL-17RA, a critical transmembrane protein and indispensable subunit of the IL-17 receptor complex, serves as the primary mediator of IL-17A signaling [33]. This receptor is ubiquitously expressed across multiple cell lineages, including epithelial cells (e.g., skin, intestinal, and respiratory tract barriers), endothelial cells, immune cells (such as neutrophils, macrophages, and T lymphocytes), and fibroblasts [34–36]. Notably, despite its well-characterized roles in other cells, limited published data exist regarding IL-17RA expression in the bovine mammary gland or BMECs. Furthermore, the functional significance of IL-17RA in mammary tissue pathophysiology, particularly in the context of bacterial mastitis, remains poorly unelucidated, representing a critical gap in our current understanding. In a transcriptomic analysis of Escherichia coli-challenged bovine mammary epithelial cells, Iso‑Touru et al. (2024) demonstrated that the IL‑17 signaling pathway is among the most significantly upregulated pathways at 24 h post‑infection [37]. However, this discovery has not been verified by further experiments. To our knowledge, the only previously reported evidence indicated the presence of IL-17RA mRNA in both mammary tissue and BMECs [38]. In this study, we demonstrate detectable IL-17RA protein expression in BMECs and mouse mammary gland, thereby corroborating and extending earlier findings. This result suggests a potential correlation between IL-17RA signaling and the pathogenesis of bovine mastitis.

Mammary epithelial cells constitute the primary defensive barrier against microbial invasion into the mammary gland [39, 40]. Following LPS challenge, these cells release a cascade of pro-inflammatory factors, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-17, as well as mediators such as COX2 and iNOS [41]. Although a controlled inflammatory response is essential for effective host defense, excessive production of these cytokines can amplify inflammation and aggravate mammary tissue damage [42]. Thus, controlling the overproduction of inflammatory mediators is crucial for mitigating mammary gland inflammation and bystander tissue injury. Given its documented anti-inflammatory properties, Ech represents a promising candidate for the intervention of bovine mastitis. In this study, we investigated the anti-inflammatory effects of Ech in LPS challenged BMECs and mouse mastitis model. Our results demonstrate that Ech pretreatment significantly inhibits the LPS-induced mammary gland inflammation.

Previous studies have demonstrated that Ech regulates inflammatory signaling to suppress the expression of inflammatory factors, thereby alleviating inflammatory-related diseases [26, 43, 44]. However, the precise molecular mechanism of Ech in mastitis remained unknown. To elucidate the potential mechanism of Ech in protecting against LPS-induced inflammation, RNA-seq analysis was conducted. This analysis revealed that LPS exposure upregulated genes associated with the IL-17RA signaling pathway. Conversely, pretreatment with Ech suppressed the expression of these genes. Supporting the RNA-seq data, further results suggested that Ech reduced LPS-induced protein levels of IL-17RA signaling and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling. Indeed, IL-17RA signaling is implicated in inflammation progression and is linked to increased expression of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 etc.) and chemokines (CXCL1, CCL2, CXCL2, and CCL20 etc.) [19, 45]. Consistently, our study identified IL-17RA signaling as a key regulator of the LPS-induced inflammation. To further investigate the effect of Ech on IL-17RA signaling, BMECs were treated with rIL-17A to establish an inflammation model. Importantly, Ech intervention significantly inhibited rIL-17A-induced activation of the MAPK/NF-κB pathway and the release of inflammatory factors, while also suppressing the expression of IL-17 signaling-related proteins. Conversely, Ech co-treatment with Ixe (an IL-17RA signaling inhibitor that potently blocks IL-17A binding to IL-17RA) yielded opposing effects in LPS-stimulated BMECs. Further supporting our in vivo results, Ech ameliorated LPS-induced mastitis in mice, paralleled by significant reductions in both the protein levels of IL-17RA and MAPK/NF-κB signaling mediators and the production of inflammatory factors in mammary gland, which aligns with our observations in vitro. Collectively, these results demonstrate that Ech exerts anti-inflammatory effect through suppression of the IL-17RA and MAPK/NF-κB signaling pathway, suggesting its potential as a preventive strategy for bovine mastitis.

Several licorice‑derived flavonoids (e.g., echinatin, liquiritigenin, isoliquiritigenin, glabridin) possess anti‑inflammatory properties. Our study reveals that Ech specifically suppresses the IL‑17RA/MAPK/NF‑κB axis, a pathway central to mammary inflammation, suggesting a more targeted profile. It should also be noted that LPS‑induced mammary tissue damage is likely driven by both IL‑17‑and TLR4‑mediated proinflammatory cascades, and echinatin may exert its protective effect by moderating both pathways. Although our RNA‑seq analysis did not detect significant differential expression of the core TLR4 signaling components (e.g., TLR4, CD14, MD‑2, MyD88, TRIF) - likely due to their constitutive expression and post‑translational regulation - our Western blot data demonstrate that Ech effectively suppresses NF‑κB and MAPK phosphorylation downstream of both TLR4 and IL‑17RA. The finding that IL‑17A neutralization abolishes Ech’s protective effect indicates that intact IL‑17RA signaling is necessary for Ech action. Future studies using TLR4‑specific ligands or genetic knockdown will be required to dissect the relative contributions of each pathway. Additionally, translational challenges remain: the oral bioavailability and metabolic stability of Ech in ruminants are unknown; practical delivery routes (oral or intramammary) require development; and future studies should include drug‑only control groups to exclude non‑specific effects.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates that Ech attenuates LPS-induced inflammation both in vitro (BMECs) and in vivo (mice) through suppression of the IL-17RA signaling and its downstream effectors (NF-κB/MAPK). These findings provide mechanistic insights into the pathogenesis of mastitis and suggest that Ech may serve as a candidate for further development in the prevention of bovine mastitis. However, additional studies that include pharmacokinetic evaluations and efficacy trials in dairy cattle are necessary to assess its translational potential.

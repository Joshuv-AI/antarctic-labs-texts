Finite element analysis of a nematic liquid crystal Landau-de Gennes model with quartic elastic terms Thanks: F.W. and J.E. were supported in part by NSF grants DMS-2438083 and DMS-2409989.
Jacob Elafandi, Franziska Weber

Abstract
. Golovaty et al. [ 15 ] present a Q Q -tensor model for liquid crystal dynamics which reduces to the well-known Oseen-Frank director field model in uniaxial states. This model has been shown to capture phase transitions in nematic liquid crystals. We study a closely related model and present an energy stable scheme for the corresponding gradient flow. We prove well-posedness of the scheme via the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem and rigorously show the Γ \Gamma -convergence of discrete minimizers as the mesh size approaches zero. In the numerical experiments, we successfully simulate isotropic-to-nematic phase transitions as expected. We also compare simulations for elastic energies with identical and with highly disparate elastic constants and observe different defect dynamics. A comparison with simulations for the standard Landau-de Gennes model with quadratic energy shows that the quartic model is able to capture richer dynamics than the quadratic model.



1.
Introduction

Liquid crystal is an intermediate state of matter between the liquid and crystalline solid states that exhibits a state of partial order
[
26
]
. There are several types of liquid crystal phases; one is the nematic phase. In a material exhibiting this phase, the molecules can be represented as elongated rods, and intermolecular forces are responsible for the alignment of the molecules in a particular direction. Such materials are referred to (through a slight abuse of terminology) as nematic liquid crystals. Because the molecules in a liquid crystal are aligned but retain the ability to flow, liquid crystals are attractive for many applications in engineering and daily life, such as displays, smart glasses, and soaps
[
26
,
7
]
.

1.1.
Mathematical models for liquid crystal dynamics

A variety of mathematical models for the dynamics of liquid crystals have been proposed. Two of the most commonly used ones are the Oseen-Frank director field model
[
26
,
12
,
23
]
and the Landau-de Gennes
Q
-tensor model
[
8
,
28
]
. In the director field model, the main orientation of the liquid crystal molecules is modeled as a normal vector field
\mathbf{n}
, which points in the general direction of the liquid crystal molecules. The direction of
\mathbf{n}
at any point in space is determined by minimizing the Oseen-Frank energy

(1.1)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{OF}(\mathbf{n})&=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{K_{1}}{2}(\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{n})^{2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}((\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{n})\cdot\mathbf{n})^{2}+\frac{K_{3}}{2}|(\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{n})\times\mathbf{n}|^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{K_{2}+K_{4}}{2}(\mathrm{tr}\,(\nabla\mathbf{n})^{2}-(\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{n})^{2})\bigg)\,dx,\end{split}

where
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3},K_{4}\geq 0
are material and temperature dependent elastic constants and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the domain in which the liquid crystal is contained. Assuming all derivatives exist and commute, the fourth term in the integral can be rewritten as

\mathrm{tr}\,(\nabla\mathbf{n})^{2}-(\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{n})^{2}=\mathrm{div}\,\left((\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{n}\right),

and so its integral can be expressed as a surface integral via Gauss’ divergence theorem. Therefore this term is a null Lagrangian and does not contribute to the Euler-Lagrange equations which describe the equilibrium, although it enters the boundary conditions and affects the total value of the energy
[
26
]
. This model can only account for uniaxial configurations in liquid crystals.

A theory that can also account for biaxial configurations is the Landau-de Gennes Q-tensor theory.
Here one assumes that the direction which the liquid crystal molecules favor has a probability density at every point in space and time. The first nontrivial moment of this probability density is the second moment
M
. If the material is in an isotropic phase at a point, the second moment reduces to
\frac{1}{3}I
. The
Q
-tensor is then defined as
Q=M-\frac{1}{3}I
, a traceless and symmetric tensor corresponding to the deviation from the isotropic phase. We thus define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\left\{Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:Q^{\top}=Q,\mathrm{tr}\,Q=0\right\},

the space of symmetric and traceless matrices. In the standard Landau-de Gennes model, we can find the equilibrium configuration
Q:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}
given a domain
\Omega
and a boundary condition
\left.Q\right|_{\partial\Omega}=G:\partial\Omega\to\mathcal{S}
by minimizing the Landau-de Gennes energy

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{LdG}(Q)=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}\left(\frac{L_{1}}{2}Q_{ij,k}^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}Q_{ij,j}Q_{ik,k}+\frac{L_{3}}{2}Q_{ik,j}Q_{ij,k}+W(Q)\right)\,dx,

where
Q_{ij}
is the
ij
-th element of
Q
and
Q_{ij,k}=\partial Q_{ij}/\partial x_{k}
. Here
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
are material dependent elastic constants and
W
is a bulk energy density given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle W(Q)=a\,\mathrm{tr}\,(Q^{2})-\frac{2b}{3}\,\mathrm{tr}\,(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{2}(\mathrm{tr}\,(Q^{2}))^{2}.

This bulk energy is derived from a Taylor expansion about
Q=0

[
22
]
. This expression has often been used in the literature, but other expressions – such as a mean field approximation, as in
[
25
]
– are possible as well. We assume
c>0
to ensure the energy has a lower bound. The signs of
a
and
b
vary with temperature, and we note in particular that
a
is negative for sufficiently low temperatures. If
a<\frac{b^{2}}{27c}
, which is what we will assume in this work, then
Q
-tensors in the minimal set
\mathcal{N}
of
W
are in a uniaxial nematic state that can be described in terms of the set

\mathcal{N}=\left\{Q=s_{0}\left(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-\frac{1}{3}I\right)\,:\,\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\right\}.

Here
s_{0}
depends on the coefficients
a,b,c
, specifically,

s_{0}=\frac{b+\sqrt{b^{2}-24ac}}{4c};

cf.
[
22
, Section II.A]
. Researchers have studied the connections between the Oseen-Frank theory and the Landau-de Gennes
Q
-tensor model for several decades
[
2
,
27
,
9
,
20
]
. In particular, Majumdar and Zarnescu
[
21
]
have studied this issue in the one-constant setting, i.e., when
K_{1}=K_{2}=K_{3}=K
. Recently, Golovaty et al.
[
15
]
studied connections between the two theories for the full Oseen-Frank model with possibly variable constants. This is an important problem as many materials indeed exhibit material constants
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and
K_{3}
with highly disparate magnitude.
Golovaty et al. suggested a higher-order generalization of the elastic energy in (
1.2
) to include quartic terms:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(Q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{4}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+W(Q)\bigg)\,dx

Here
W
is given by (
1.3
) and
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4}>0
. Note that the terms in this model still have quadratic dependence on both
\nabla Q
and
Q
.
This energy is derived by selecting quartic choices of elastic energy terms from a list of thirteen found in
[
19
]
.
If
Q=s_{0}(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-I/3)
, then
\mathcal{E}
becomes the Oseen-Frank energy
\mathcal{F}_{OF}
(
1.1
) for suitable choices of
s_{0}
,
L_{i}
and
K_{i}

[
15
, Prop. 2.6]
. Golovaty et al. establish a rigorous connection between
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}_{OF}
via a
\Gamma
-convergence argument in the limit of vanishing nematic correlation length. The nematic correlation length is a measure of how far the orientation of molecules in a nematic liquid crystal is correlated. It represents the characteristic distance over which the nematic order parameter remains correlated. Essentially, it describes the extent of long-range orientational order in the material. Golovaty et al. define a parameter
\epsilon
which represents the ratio of the nematic correlation length to a characteristic lengthscale of the domain and nondimensionalize the energy to arrive at

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(Q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{4}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W(Q)\bigg)\,dx.

Then they use a
\Gamma
-convergence argument to send
\epsilon\to 0
. This can also be seen as a low temperature limit in some cases
[
13
]
.
The case of variable constants had been studied prior to the work of Golovaty et al.
[
15
]
, but previous works were based mainly on the observation that one can obtain the Oseen-Frank energy density from the Landau-de Gennes energy density in the uniaxial case by adding an additional elastic term that is cubic in
Q
and its derivatives, specifically,

\sum_{\ell,k,i,j=1}^{3}Q_{\ell k}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}\partial_{\ell}Q_{ij}.

However, from the perspective of energy minimization, this term is problematic, since including it to the energy density results in the energy being unbounded from below. The energy density sugested by Golovaty et al. does not have this issue. It is further motivated by
[
20
]
who suggested adding higher order terms to the Landau-de Gennes energy density in the presence of biaxial fluctuations.

In this paper, we consider the numerical analysis of a quartic model inspired by (
1.4
). Specifically, we consider the energy

(1.6)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(Q)&=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{L_{4}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{5}}{2}\left|Q\right|^{2}\left|\nabla Q\right|^{2}+W(Q)\bigg)\,dx,\end{split}

where
L_{5}\geq 0
is another material constant.
L_{5}=0
corresponds to Golovaty et al.’s original model (
1.4
). The term
|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}
is one of the possible choices of quartic terms in the list found in
[
19
]
.
Moreover, it can be motivated from the Oseen-Frank energy (
1.1
) in a similar way as Golovaty et al. derived the other four elastic terms from the Oseen-Frank energy in the uniaxial case in
[
15
, Prop. 2.6]
. To see this, we first note that the Oseen-Frank energy can be written in the form

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}_{OF}(\mathbf{n})=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{K_{1}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{n}|^{2}+\frac{K_{2}-K_{3}}{2}(\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{2}+\frac{K_{3}-K_{1}}{2}|\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{n}|^{2}\\
&\hphantom{\mathcal{F}_{OF}(\mathbf{n})=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(}+\frac{K_{2}+K_{4}-K_{1}}{2}(\mathrm{tr}\,(\nabla\mathbf{n})^{2}-(\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{n})^{2})\bigg)dx;\end{split}

see
[
26
]
.
Then since
2|\nabla\mathbf{n}|^{2}=|\nabla(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n})|^{2}=|\nabla(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-I/3)|^{2}
, we can rewrite the first term as

\frac{K_{1}}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{n}|^{2}=\frac{3K_{1}}{8}\left|\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-\frac{I}{3}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\left(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-\frac{I}{3}\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{3K_{1}}{8s_{0}^{4}}\left|s_{0}\left(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-\frac{I}{3}\right)\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\left(s_{0}\left(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-\frac{I}{3}\right)\right)\right|^{2},

which is equal to
\frac{3K_{1}}{8s_{0}^{4}}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}
in the case that
Q=s_{0}(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}-I/3)
. The remaining terms with elastic constants
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4}
can be justified in the same way as it was previously done in the work by Golovaty et al.
[
15
, Section 2]
(in fact the
|\nabla\mathbf{n}|^{2}
also appears there but is then rewritten to take a different form). This yields a justification for including the additional term. Thus, the
L_{5}
-term can be naturally motivated from the Oseen-Frank model in the uniaxial case. The model (
1.6
) still has the same favorable properties as the one by Golovaty et al.(
1.4
): Firstly, all the theoretical results established in
[
15
]
carry over to the model with energy
\mathcal{F}
with simple adaptions of the proofs. In particular, for a reasonable range of elastic constants, one can recover the Oseen-Frank model in the limit of vanishing correlation length. We can therefore take highly disparate values for the elastic constants in (
1.6
) and simulate liquid crystals which favor one of the three deformations, splay, twist and bend. This allows, as we shall see in the simulations in Section
5
, to capture the formation of non-spherically symmetric tactoids and nematic bubbles with defects. The additional term also respects all necessary symmetries. It is mentioned in
[
15
]
, that their choice of quartic elastic energy terms may not be the only possible higher order extensions of the Landau-de Gennes model and we agree with this statement. Other terms in the list in
[
19
]
may lead to models with similar favorable properties. In fact, we believe that the work of this article may very well be extended to such similar models as long as the
L_{5}
-term is included. The
L_{5}
-term appears crucial to show
\Gamma
-convergence of the discrete energies
\mathcal{F}_{h}
as
h\to 0
, where
h>0
is a discretization parameter associated with our scheme, and in particular, to construct the recovery secquence, as we will see in Section
4
of this article. This implies that if
\{Q_{h}^{*}\}_{h>0}
is a sequence of global discrete minimizers of
\{\mathcal{F}_{h}\}_{h>0}
, then every cluster point of the sequence is a global minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
. In general, we do not know if the minima of
\mathcal{F}
and its corresponding discrete energies are global and unique. If they are not, our
\Gamma
-convergence result implies that if
Q^{*}
is an isolated local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
, then there exists a sequence
Q_{h}^{*}
converging to
Q^{*}
with
Q_{h}^{*}
a local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{h}
for
h
sufficiently small; cf.
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
).

In order to find discrete minimizers, we consider an energy-stable finite element discretization of the gradient flow for the energy (
1.6
).
To obtain the gradient flow, we compute the variational derivative of the energy and set it equal to the negative of the time derivative of
Q
:

(1.7)

\partial_{t}Q=-M\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}(Q)}{\delta Q}\right),

subject to boundary conditions
\left.Q\right|_{\partial\Omega}=G
.
Here
M>0
is a constant,
\mathcal{P}
is the projection onto symmetric traceless tensors given by

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(A)=\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\top})-\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,A}{3}I\quad\text{ for }\,A\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},

and
\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}(Q)}{\delta Q}
is given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\mathcal{F}(Q)}{\delta Q}

\displaystyle=L_{1}\left(-\nabla(S_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q)+S_{1}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q(\mathrm{div}\,Q)^{\top}\right)+L_{2}\left(\mathrm{curl}\,(S_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q)^{\top}+S_{1}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q(\mathrm{curl}\,Q)^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle-L_{3}\left(\nabla(S_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q)+S_{2}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q(\mathrm{div}\,Q)^{\top}\right)+L_{4}\left(\mathrm{curl}\,(S_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q)^{\top}-S_{2}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q(\mathrm{curl}\,Q)^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle+L_{5}\left(-\nabla(|Q|^{2}\nabla Q)+|\nabla Q|^{2}Q\right)+2aQ-2b(Q^{2})+2c\,\mathrm{tr}\,(Q^{2})Q,

where we define
S_{1}=\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q
,
S_{2}=\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q
, and
[\nabla(|Q|^{2}\nabla Q)]_{\ell m}=\sum_{i,j,k}\partial_{i}(Q_{jk}^{2}\partial_{i}Q_{\ell m})
.
Smooth solutions of the gradient flow (
1.7
) satisfy the energy dissipation law

(1.9)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(Q)=-M\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}Q|^{2}\,dx.

Thus, starting from an arbitrary smooth initial condition, the energy decreases monotonically along the gradient flow

\mathcal{F}(Q(t))=\mathcal{F}(Q_{0})-M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}Q(s)|^{2}\,dx\,ds,

where
Q_{0}=Q(0,\cdot)
is the initial condition.
Heuristically, as time goes to infinity, the solution reaches an equilibrium point which is a local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
.
In this work, we design a fully discrete finite element scheme for (
1.7
) that satisfies a discrete energy dissipation law. The discrete energy dissipation law mimics the energy law of the continuous setting (
1.9
). This implies stability of the scheme independently of the mesh size
h
and the time step size
\Delta t
and a monotone decrease of the discrete energy with each time step. Therefore, the approximations converge to local minima of the corresponding discrete energies
\mathcal{F}_{h}
as time goes to infinity.
We show that the discrete energies are coercive and weakly lower semi-continuous on
H^{1}
as the discretization parameters go to zero.
If
L_{5}>0
, we use this to show that the corresponding discrete energies
\mathcal{F}_{h}
converge to the continuous energy in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence as
h\to 0
.

While we are not able to show the same
\Gamma
-convergence result for the case
L_{5}=0
, which would correspond to the elastic energy density suggested by Golovaty et al.
[
15
]
, our simulations with the proposed numerical method for the gradient flow show similar dynamics for
L_{5}
small or zero. We present numerical results showing isotropic-to-nematic phase transitions as proposed in
[
14
]
. The proposed numerical scheme for our model is able to capture all the tested phase transitions very well. In the numerical experiments, we observe the formation of defects and shrinking tactoids that have non-spherically symmetric boundaries, as observed in physical experiments, c.f.
[
14
, Figure 1 and 2]
. The dynamics vary depending on the ratio of the elastic constants. We also compare the simulations to those for the standard Landau-de Gennes quadratic one-constant approximation elastic energy and observe richer dynamics for the quartic model.
Additionally, with the exception of the
\Gamma
-convergence result, all stability and related results for the numerical scheme also hold in the case that
L_{5}=0
.

Related work

To the best of our knowledge, apart from the simulations in
[
14
,
18
]
, no computational works are available for the generalized Landau-de Gennes model with quartic energy density terms, and our work provides the first provably energy stable and convergent numerical scheme for (
1.7
). Related works on the classical Landau-de Gennes model with quadratic elastic energy include:
[
3
,
4
]
, which discuss the numerical approximation of uniaxially constrained Q-tensors;
[
16
,
30
,
6
]
, which design and analyze numerical methods for the gradient flow; and
[
25
,
29
,
17
]
, which construct numerical methods for Q-tensors subject to fluid flows and external forces, such as magnetic and electric fields. Our modification of the model (
1.4
) allows for rigorous numerical analysis of a Landau-de Gennes model with quartic elastic terms. We believe that the techniques that we develop to show
\Gamma
-convergence for this model are valuable in the sense that they may be used to show
\Gamma
-convergence for other related models and other energy stable numerical schemes. Due to the matrix-valued field

being potentially unbounded and not smooth, we introduce several approximation schemes in order to show the existence of a recovery sequence for the discrete energies. ]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with a flurry of necessary definitions. For
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, we use the Frobenius product and norm:

•

A:B=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}A_{ij}B_{ij}
,

•

|A|=\sqrt{A:A}
.

We extend this definition for the gradients of tensor-valued functions:

•

(\nabla A):(\nabla B)=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}A_{ij,k}B_{ij,k}
,

•

|\nabla A|=\sqrt{(\nabla A):(\nabla A)}
.

Let
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
A_{3}
be the rows of
A:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
. As per
[
15
]
, we define the divergence of
A
as

\mathrm{div}\,A=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\mathrm{div}\,A_{j})\textbf{e}_{j}

and its curl as the tensor map such that

(\mathrm{curl}\,A)v\equiv\mathrm{curl}\,(A^{\top}v)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Thus the
j
-th entry of
\mathrm{div}\,A
is
\mathrm{div}\,A_{j}
, and the
j
-th column of
\mathrm{curl}\,A
is
\mathrm{curl}\,A_{j}
.

We also define the
L^{2}
products and norms for scalar-valued functions
f,g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, vector-valued functions
u,v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, tensor-valued functions
A,B:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and gradients of tensor-valued functions:

•

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}fg\,dx
,

•

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}u\cdot v\,dx
,

•

\left\langle A,B\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}A:B\,dx
,

•

\left\langle\nabla A,\nabla B\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla A):(\nabla B)\,dx
,

•

\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}=\left\langle f,f\right\rangle
,

•

\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}=\left\langle u,u\right\rangle
,

•

\left\lVert A\right\rVert^{2}=\left\langle A,A\right\rangle
,

•

\left\lVert\nabla A\right\rVert^{2}=\left\langle\nabla A,\nabla A\right\rangle
.

For
Q\in\mathcal{S}
, we define the symmetric tensors

(2.1)

\displaystyle S_{1}(Q)=\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q,\qquad S_{2}(Q)=\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q,

which we use to define the functionals for the energy components:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\lVert S_{1}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rVert^{2},\quad\mathcal{F}_{2}(Q)=\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left\lVert S_{1}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rVert^{2},\quad\mathcal{F}_{3}(Q)=\frac{L_{3}}{2}\left\lVert S_{2}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rVert^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{4}(Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{4}}{2}\left\lVert S_{2}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q\right\rVert^{2},\quad\mathcal{F}_{5}(Q)=\frac{L_{5}}{2}\left\lVert Q\,|\nabla Q|\right\rVert^{2},\quad\mathcal{F}_{6}(Q)=\int_{\Omega}W(Q)\,dx.

Then the energy (
1.6
) can be rewritten as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(Q)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)+\mathcal{F}_{2}(Q)+\mathcal{F}_{3}(Q)+\mathcal{F}_{4}(Q)+\mathcal{F}_{5}(Q)+\mathcal{F}_{6}(Q).

In the vein of
[
30
]
, we denote the discrete value of a variable
(\cdot)(x,t)
at time
t^{n}
as
(\cdot)^{n}(x)
, and use the notation

(\cdot)^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{(\cdot)^{n+1}+(\cdot)^{n}}{2}.

For integer
k
, we also use the shorthand
[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

3.
Discretization of the Q-tensor gradient flow

Next, we introduce the numerical scheme for the gradient flow (
1.7
) and prove its energy stability and solvability for each time step.
Given a family of quasi-uniform triangulations
\Omega_{h}
of
\Omega
with maximum element diameter
h
, we define a number of dependent quantities. To begin, let
L
be the number of elements; let
N
be the number of interior nodes; let
V
be the total number of nodes, including boundary nodes; let
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
be the set of elements; let
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the set of interior nodes; and let
\{P_{i}\}_{i=N+1}^{V}
be the set of boundary nodes.
To deal with the boundary condition
G
, we introduce the following approximation of an extension of
G
into the domain
\Omega
:
We define
G_{h}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as the unique function which is linear on each

Theorem 3.2
.

Scheme

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n})}{\Delta t}=-\frac{1}{M}\left\lVert\frac{Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

We begin by analyzing the

Using the identity
|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=(a+b)\cdot(a-b)
for vectors
a
and
b
, and
a_{1}\cdot b_{1}-a_{2}\cdot b_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})\cdot(b_{1}-b_{2})+\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}-a_{2})\cdot(b_{1}+b_{2})
for matrices
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and vectors
b_{1},b_{2}
,
we can rewrite this as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q_{h}^{n})=\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\langle S_{1,h}^{n+1}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n+1}+S_{1,h}^{n}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n},S_{1,h}^{n+1}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n+1}-S_{1,h}^{n}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=L_{1}\left\langle\frac{S_{1,h}^{n+1}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n+1}+S_{1,h}^{n}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n}}{2},\frac{S_{1,h}^{n+1}+S_{1,h}^{n}}{2}\,\mathrm{div}\,(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})+(S_{1,h}^{n+1}-S_{1,h}^{n})\,\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{Q_{h}^{n+1}+Q_{h}^{n}}{2}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=L_{1}\left\langle(S_{1,h}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h})^{n+\frac{1}{2}},S_{1,h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{div}\,(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})+(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle.

Because
Q^{n+1}_{h}-Q^{n}_{h}\in\mathcal{T}^{0}_{h}
is a valid test function, we therefore have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=L_{1}\left\langle(S_{1,h}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h})^{n+\frac{1}{2}},S_{1,h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{div}\,(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})+(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})\,\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=H_{1}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}).

The evaluation of the
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\mathcal{F}_{3}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{4}
terms are analogous. For
\mathcal{F}_{5}
, we have, using again
a_{1}\cdot b_{1}-a_{2}\cdot b_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})\cdot(b_{1}-b_{2})+\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}-a_{2})\cdot(b_{1}+b_{2})
, and then
|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=(a+b):(a-b)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{5}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}_{5}(Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{5}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(|Q_{h}^{n+1}|^{2}|\nabla Q_{h}^{n+1}|^{2}-|Q_{h}^{n}|^{2}|\nabla Q_{h}^{n}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{5}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla Q_{h}^{n+1}|^{2}+|\nabla Q_{h}^{n}|^{2}}{2}(|Q_{h}^{n+1}|^{2}-|Q_{h}^{n}|^{2})+\frac{|Q_{h}^{n+1}|^{2}+|Q_{h}^{n}|^{2}}{2}(|\nabla Q_{h}^{n+1}|^{2}-|\nabla Q_{h}^{n}|^{2})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=L_{5}\int_{\Omega}\left((|\nabla Q_{h}|^{2})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})+(|Q_{h}|^{2})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\nabla Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(\nabla Q_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla Q_{h}^{n})\right)\,dx.

Now, since

, we can write the last expression as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{5}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}_{5}(Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=L_{5}\int_{\Omega}\left((|\nabla Q_{h}|^{2})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})+(|Q_{h}|^{2})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\nabla Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(\nabla Q_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla Q_{h}^{n})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=H_{5}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}).

Lastly, we examine the
\mathcal{F}_{6}
term. Using similar identities, we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2})-\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n})^{2})

\displaystyle=(Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2}:I-(Q_{h}^{n})^{2}:I

\displaystyle=(Q_{h}^{n+1}+Q_{h}^{n}):(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=2Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}),

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{3})-\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n})^{3})

\displaystyle=(Q_{h}^{n+1})^{3}:I-(Q_{h}^{n})^{3}:I

\displaystyle=((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2}+Q_{h}^{n+1}Q_{h}^{n}+(Q_{h}^{n})^{2}):(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=(2(Q_{h}^{2})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+Q_{h}^{n+1}Q_{h}^{n}):(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}),

where we have used that
Q^{n+1}_{h}
and
Q^{n}_{h}
are symmetric. Next, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2})^{2}-\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n})^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2})+\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n})^{2})\right)\left(\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2})-\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n})^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n+1})^{2})+\mathrm{tr}\,((Q_{h}^{n})^{2})\right)(Q_{h}^{n+1}+Q_{h}^{n}):(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=4\left(\mathrm{tr}\,(Q_{h}^{2})\right)^{n+\frac{1}{2}}Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{6}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}_{6}(Q_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle=\left\langle a(2Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{2b}{3}(2(Q_{h}^{2})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+Q_{h}^{n+1}Q_{h}^{n})+\frac{c}{2}(4\left(\mathrm{tr}\,(Q_{h}^{2})\right)^{n+\frac{1}{2}}Q_{h}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=H_{6}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}).

We combine the above expressions and use Scheme
3.1
to complete the proof.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n+1})-\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n})}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n})=H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right)=-\frac{1}{M}\left\lVert\frac{Q_{h}^{n+1}-Q_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right\rVert^{2}

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Scheme
3.1
admits a solution
Q_{h}^{n+1}
for all

for some constant

Proof.

We use the Leray-Schauder theorem to show existence of a fixed point
\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n+1}
of the mapping
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}:\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}\to\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
defined in (
3.10
) above. Then
Q^{n+1}_{h}:=\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n+1}+G_{h}
solves Scheme
3.1
. To apply the Leray-Schauder theorem, we need to show that the mapping is well-posed, compact, continuous, as a mapping from
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
to
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
, and that the set

K:=\{Q\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}\,|\,Q=\lambda\mathcal{L}^{n+1}(Q)\quad\text{for some }\ 0\leq\lambda\leq 1\}

is bounded
[
11
, page 540]
(we also need
0\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
, which is clearly true). We omit the dependence of
\mathcal{L}
on
n+1
in the following proof, as it is not relevant.
We assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{F}
is nonnegative (
\mathcal{F}
is bounded from below, and so if it is negative, one can add a sufficiently large constant to make it positive and not change the dynamics or minimizers of the equation.).
We start with the wellposedness.

Well-posedness of the mapping
\mathcal{L}
: This follows from above. The mass matrix
M_{ij}:=\langle\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\rangle_{ij}
is invertible and hence the solution of the mapping is uniquely defined.

Continuity:
Let
\mathcal{L}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{B}_{h}|}\hat{X}_{i}\varphi_{i}
and
\mathcal{L}(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{B}_{h}|}\hat{Y}_{i}\varphi_{i}
two solutions of the mapping. Then we have

\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-M\Delta t(H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))).

We show that the terms
H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}
and
H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}
terms are continuous, the terms
H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ia}
and
H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ib}
,
i=1,\dots,4
are similar and the
H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{6}
-terms are polynomial in
X
and therefore continuous. So we have by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left|H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{L_{5}}{4}\left|\langle(|X+G_{h}|^{2}+|Q^{n}_{h}|^{2})(\nabla X+\nabla G_{h}+\nabla Q_{h}^{n})-(|Y+G_{h}|^{2}+|Q^{n}_{h}|^{2})(\nabla Y+\nabla G_{h}+\nabla Q_{h}^{n}),\nabla(\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{L_{5}}{4}\left|\langle(|X+G_{h}|^{2}-|Y+G_{h}|^{2})(\nabla X+\nabla G_{h}+\nabla Q_{h}^{n}),\nabla(\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L_{5}}{4}\left|\langle(|Y+G_{h}|^{2}+|Q_{h}^{n}|^{2})(\nabla X-\nabla Y),\nabla(\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\rangle\right|.

Using Hölder’s inequality for the right hand side, we obtain,

\displaystyle\left|H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left\||X|+|Y|+2|G_{h}|\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla X+\nabla G_{h}+\nabla Q_{h}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla(\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\left\|Y+G_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\|Q_{h}^{n}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|\nabla(X-Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla(\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Next, we use inverse inequalities, to bound the last terms as follows:

\displaystyle\left|H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5a}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ch^{-d-2}(\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|Y\right\|_{L^{2}}+C)\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\right)\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{-d-2}\left(C+\left\|Y\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ch^{-d-2}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

For the
H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}
term we have, using triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left|H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{L_{5}}{4}\left|\langle(|\nabla(X+G_{h})|^{2}+|\nabla Q^{n}_{h}|^{2})(X+G_{h}+Q_{h}^{n})-(|\nabla(Y+G_{h})|^{2}+|\nabla Q^{n}_{h}|^{2})(Y+G_{h}+Q_{h}^{n}),\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{L_{5}}{4}\left|\langle(|\nabla(X+G_{h})|^{2}-|\nabla(Y+G_{h})|^{2})(X+G_{h}+Q_{h}^{n}),\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L_{5}}{4}\left|\langle(|\nabla(Y+g_{h})|^{2}+|\nabla Q_{h}^{n}|^{2})(X-Y),\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\rangle\right|.

The last expression can be bounded as follows, using Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left\||\nabla X|+|\nabla Y|+2|\nabla G_{h}|\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla(X-Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|X+G_{h}+Q_{h}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\left\|\nabla(Y+G_{h})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla Q_{h}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Again, we use inverse inequalities to bound the last terms on the right hand side, we have

\displaystyle\left|H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}(X+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))-H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{5b}(Y+G_{h},\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ch^{-d-2}\left(\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|Y\right\|_{L^{2}}+2\left\|G_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|X+G_{h}+Q_{h}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{-d-2}\left(\left\|Y+G_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|Q_{h}^{n}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ch^{-d-2}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Using the same strategy for the other terms, we obtain

(3.11)

\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Delta th^{-d-2}\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Dividing both sides by
\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}
and sending
X\to Y
, we obtain the continuity of
\mathcal{L}
. Note that this estimate (
3.11
) implies that if
\Delta t\leq ch^{d+2}
for a constant
c
sufficiently small, then

\left\|\mathcal{L}(X)-\mathcal{L}(Y)\right\|_{L^{2}}<\left\|X-Y\right\|_{L^{2}},

and hence
\mathcal{L}
is a contraction if
\Delta t\leq ch^{d+2}
.

Compactness:
This follows because the space
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
is finite dimensional.

Boundedness of
K
:
It remains to show that the set
K
is uniformly bounded. We consider

\left\langle X,\varphi\right\rangle=\lambda\left\langle\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n},\varphi\right\rangle-\lambda M\Delta tH^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(X+G_{h},\varphi)\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0},

and use
X-\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n}
as a test function. Using Theorem
3.2
and rearranging terms, we obtain

\left\langle X-\lambda\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n},X-\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle=-\lambda M\Delta tH^{n+\frac{1}{2}}(X+G_{h},X-\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n})=-\lambda M\Delta t(\mathcal{F}(X+G_{h})-\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n})).

Thus

\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda M\Delta t\mathcal{F}(X+G_{h})=(1+\lambda)\langle\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n},X\rangle+\lambda M\Delta t\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n}).

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and bounding
\lambda
from above and below, we have

\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}+M\Delta t\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n})\leq 2\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+M\Delta t\mathcal{F}(Q_{h}^{n}).

Subtracting
\frac{1}{2}\left\|X\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
on both sides, we obtain the boundedness of
X
on
L^{2}
in terms of
Q^{n}_{h}
.
Thus the conditions of the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem are satisfied and the mapping
\mathcal{L}
has a fixed point. If
\Delta t\leq ch^{d+2}
, then
\mathcal{L}
is a contraction, as noted above, and therefore the fixed point is unique by the Banach fixed point theorem. Hence the scheme is well-posed.

∎

4.
\Gamma
-convergence of the discrete energies

The goal of this section is to rigorously prove the
\Gamma
-convergence of the discrete energies

(4.1)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{h}(Q_{h})&=\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q_{h}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q_{h}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q_{h}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q_{h}\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q_{h}\right\rvert^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{L_{4}}{2}\left\lvert\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}I-Q_{h}\right)\mathrm{curl}\,Q_{h}\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{L_{5}}{2}\left|Q_{h}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla Q_{h}\right|^{2}+W(Q_{h})\bigg)\,dx,\quad\text{for }\,Q_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{G}.\end{split}

This implies that if
Q^{*}_{h}
is a sequence of global minimizers of the discrete energies
\mathcal{F}_{h}
, then any cluster point of the sequence is a global minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
. Discrete local minimizers can be obtained through the numerical scheme for the gradient flow (
1.7
), outlined in the previous section. In general, we do not know whether these discrete local minimizers are global and unique. However, as long as
Q^{*}
is an isolated local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
, then
[
5
, Thm. 5.1]
implies that there exists a sequence
\{Q_{h}^{*}\}
, with
Q_{h}^{*}
a local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{h}
for
h>0
sufficiently small.

To begin, we define the
elastic energy density
of
Q
to be

\sigma(Q)=L_{1}|S_{1}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}+L_{2}|S_{1}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q|^{2}+L_{3}|S_{2}\,\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}+L_{4}|S_{2}\,\mathrm{curl}\,Q|^{2}+L_{5}|\nabla Q|^{2}|Q|^{2},

where we will assume that
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4},L_{5}>0
, and so
\mathcal{F}(Q)=\int_{\Omega}(\sigma(Q)+W(Q))\,dx
. We bound the energy density as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that for any
Q
, it holds that
\sigma(Q)\leq C_{1}\left\lvert\nabla Q\right\rvert^{2}+C_{2}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}
.

Proof.

We consider the first term
L_{1}|S_{1}\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}
, the bounds for the other terms are similar.
We rely on Lemmas
A.1
and
A.2
.

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{s_{0}^{2}}{9}+2\frac{s_{0}}{3}|Q|+|Q|^{2}\right)|\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{s_{0}^{2}}{9}+2\frac{s_{0}}{3}\left(\frac{1}{4}+|Q|^{2}\right)+|Q|^{2}\right)3|\nabla Q|^{2}

\displaystyle=3\left(\frac{s_{0}^{2}}{9}+\frac{s_{0}}{6}\right)\left\lvert\nabla Q\right\rvert^{2}+3\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}+1\right)|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}.

∎

Note that this lemma holds for any finite
L_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,5
, including when
L_{i}=0
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,5\}
.
The converse of Lemma
4.1
, i.e., coercivity of the energy, holds true as well, as long as
L_{i}>0
,
i=1,\dots,5
:

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depending on the coefficients
L_{i}
and the boundary data
g
such that for any
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
,

\|Q\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}dx\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(Q)dx+C_{2}.

Proof.

[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
yields

\|Q\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(Q)dx+C_{2}.

The integral
\int_{\Omega}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}dx
is also bounded due to the contribution of the
L_{5}
term in the elastic energy.
∎

Note that this lemma requires
L_{5}>0
in order to upper bound the term
\int_{\Omega}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}dx
. If
L_{5}=0
, it is unclear whether this lower bound still holds. As we shall see later, this lemma is crucial for constructing a recovery sequence.
Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
imply the bounds

C_{1}\left(\|Q_{h}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|Q_{h}|^{2}|\nabla Q_{h}|^{2}dx\right)\leq\mathcal{F}_{h}(Q_{h})\leq C_{2}\left(\|Q_{h}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|Q_{h}|^{2}|\nabla Q_{h}|^{2}dx\right)

with
C_{1},C_{2}>0
independent of
h
for the discrete energies
\mathcal{F}_{h}
.
To prove
\Gamma
-convergence of the discrete energies, we need to show weak lower semincontinuity (the “liminf property”) and the existence of a recovery sequence (the “limsup property,” or consistency). To establish the existence of a recovery sequence, we need a few preliminary lemmas that we will prove next. First, we define the
truncation
of
Q:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}
by
R>0
as

(4.2)

\displaystyle T_{R}(Q)=\begin{cases}Q&\left\lvert Q\right\rvert\leq R\\
\frac{R}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}Q&\left\lvert Q\right\rvert>R\end{cases}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
Q
and
R
, it holds that
|\nabla T_{R}(Q)|<2|\nabla Q|
.

Proof.

If
|Q|\leq R
, then
T_{R}(Q)=Q
and the lemma is trivial. If
|Q|>R
, then

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla T_{R}(Q)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=R\sqrt{\sum_{i,j,m=1}^{3}\left(\partial_{m}\left(\frac{Q_{ij}}{\sqrt{\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{3}Q_{k\ell}^{2}}}\right)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=R\sqrt{\sum_{i,j,m=1}^{3}\left(\frac{Q_{ij,m}}{|Q|}-\frac{Q_{ij}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{3}Q_{k\ell,m}Q_{k\ell}}{|Q|^{3}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{|Q|}|\nabla Q|+\frac{R}{|Q|^{3}}\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}Q_{ij}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k,\ell,m=1}^{3}Q_{k\ell,m}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{3}Q_{k\ell}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=2\frac{R}{|Q|}|\nabla Q|.

∎

Next, we show that the energies of the truncated
Q
-tensors approximate the standard truncated energies well for large
R>0
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
, we have

\displaystyle T_{R}(Q)\to Q\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})\text{ and }\mathcal{F}(T_{R}(Q))\to\mathcal{F}(Q)\quad\text{as }R\to\infty.

Proof.

T_{R}(Q)
converges to
Q
pointwise almost everywhere,
|T_{R}(Q)|^{2}\leq|Q|^{2}
by definition, and
|\nabla T_{R}(Q)|^{2}\leq 4|\nabla Q|^{2}
by Lemma
4.3
, so
T_{R}(Q)
converges strongly to
Q
in
H^{1}
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem. If
\int_{\Omega}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}\,dx=\infty
, then
\int_{\Omega}|T_{R}(Q)|^{2}|\nabla T_{R}(Q)|^{2}\,dx\to\infty
by Fatou’s lemma, and thus
\mathcal{F}(T_{R}(Q))\to\mathcal{F}(Q)=\infty
. Otherwise if
\mathcal{F}(Q)<\infty
, we have by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3

\displaystyle\sigma(T_{R}(Q))

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}|\nabla T_{R}(Q)|^{2}+C_{2}|T_{R}(Q)|^{2}|\nabla T_{R}(Q)|^{2}\leq 4C_{1}|\nabla Q|^{2}+4C_{2}|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}\leq C\sigma(Q),

\displaystyle W(T_{R}(Q))

\displaystyle\leq W(Q),

using Lemma
4.2
for the first term,
so the integrand of
\mathcal{F}(T_{R}(Q))
is also dominated by an integrable quantity, and we can use the Dominated Convergence Theorem again.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\Omega
is bounded with Lipschitz regular boundary, then for every
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
, there exists a sequence
\{Q^{m}\}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle Q^{m}\to Q\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})\text{ and }\mathcal{F}(Q^{m})\to\mathcal{F}(Q)\quad\text{as }m\to\infty.

Proof.

Let
Q^{m}
be the mollification of
Q
as defined in
[
11
, Chapter 5.3]
(see Theorem 4.3 in
[
10
]
for the approximation near the boundary); then
\|Q^{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
Q^{m}\to Q
in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
as
m\to\infty
. By considering a subsequence, we can take
Q^{m}
to converge pointwise almost everywhere without loss of generality. Then we bound the difference between
\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})-\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)\right\rvert\leq\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}\right\rvert\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left(\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}+\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right):\left(\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}-\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right)\right\rvert\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert\right)\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}-\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert dx.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the last expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})-\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}-\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, we use the triangle inequality, to get

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})-\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,(Q^{m}-Q)+(Q^{m}-Q)\,\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,(Q^{m}-Q)\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert(Q^{m}-Q)\,\mathrm{div}\,Q\right\rvert^{2}\,dx.\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

To prove that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})
is uniformly bounded by a constant, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\mathrm{div}\,Q^{m}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq 9\left\lVert\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}3\left\lvert\nabla Q^{m}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx,

which is bounded because
\left\lVert Q^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
and
Q^{m}\to Q
in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
and thus
\|Q^{m}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|Q\|_{H^{1}}+C
for
m
large enough.
For the first remaining integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\left(\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right)\mathrm{div}\,(Q^{m}-Q)\right\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 9\left\lVert\frac{s_{0}}{3}I+Q^{m}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{div}\,(Q^{m}-Q)|^{2},

which approaches zero as
m\to\infty
due to the
H^{1}
-convergence of
Q^{m}
to
Q
. For the second remaining integral, we observe that
|(Q^{m}-Q)\,\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}
approaches zero almost everywhere in
\Omega
, and

\displaystyle|(Q^{m}-Q)\,\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}\leq|Q^{m}-Q|^{2}|\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}\leq(|Q^{m}|+|Q|)^{2}|\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2},

which is integrable; thus by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, this integral also approaches zero as
m\to\infty
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q^{m})\to\mathcal{F}_{1}(Q)
. The proof for the other energy terms is similar.
∎

We have two definitions to make before stating the main theorem of this section. First, let
\{\Omega^{h}\}_{h>0}
be a regular family of triangulations of
\Omega
such that for every
h
, each element in
\Omega^{h}
has diameter less than
h
. Second, for
Q\in C(\overline{\Omega};\mathcal{S})
and all
h
, let
\mathcal{L}_{h}(Q)
be the piecewise linear Lagrange interpolation of
Q
onto
\Omega^{h}
. Then we have

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
Q\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_{h}(Q))-\mathcal{F}(Q)\right\rvert\to 0\quad\text{as }h\to 0.

Proof.

From Theorem 3.2 of
[
1
]
, we have that
\mathcal{L}_{h}(Q)\to Q
in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
as
h\to 0
. After this observation, we can just follow the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded with Lipschitz regular boundary; then the sequence
\{\mathcal{F}_{h}\}
is
\Gamma
-convergent in the weak topology of
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
. That is,

(1)

For any
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
and for any sequence
\{Q_{h}\}
in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle Q_{h}\rightharpoonup Q\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})\text{ implies }\liminf_{h\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{h}(Q_{h})\geq\mathcal{F}(Q),

and

(2)

For each
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
, there exists a recovery sequence
\{Q_{h}\}
in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
satisfying

(4.6)

\displaystyle Q_{h}\rightharpoonup Q\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})\text{ and }\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{h}(Q_{h})=\mathcal{F}(Q).

Proof.

The lower semicontinuity (liminf property) follows from Proposition 3.2 in
[
15
]
.
To begin construction of a recovery sequence, take
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. By Lemma
4.4
, there exists a sequence
\{R_{k}\}_{k}
and
\overline{R}
large enough such that for all
R_{k}\geq\overline{R}
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{R_{k}}(Q)-Q\right\rVert_{H^{1}}<\frac{1}{2k}\text{ and }\begin{cases}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}(T_{R_{k}}(Q))-\mathcal{F}(Q)\right\rvert<\frac{1}{3k}&\text{if }\mathcal{F}(Q)<\infty,\\
\mathcal{F}(T_{R_{k}}(Q))>k+\frac{2}{3k}&\text{if }\mathcal{F}(Q)=\infty.\end{cases}

By Lemma
4.5
, for each
R_{k}
there exists a sequence
\{(T_{R_{k}}(Q))^{m_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
M_{k}>0
such that for all
m_{j}\geq M_{k}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(T_{R_{k}}(Q))^{m_{j}}-T_{R_{k}}(Q)\right\rVert_{H^{1}}<\frac{1}{2j}\text{ and }\left\lvert\mathcal{F}((T_{R_{k}}(Q))^{m_{j}})-\mathcal{F}(T_{R_{k}}(Q))\right\rvert<\frac{1}{3j}.

Then, by Lemma
4.6
, for each
R_{k}
and
m_{j}
, there exists a sequence
\{h_{\ell}\}_{\ell}>0
and
\bar{h}>0
such that for all
h_{\ell}<\bar{h}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{h_{\ell}}(\mathcal{L}_{h_{\ell}}((T_{R_{k}}(Q))^{m_{j}}))-\mathcal{F}((T_{R_{k}}(Q))^{m_{j}})\right\rvert<\frac{1}{3\ell}\text{ and }h_{\ell}<\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\ell=1,\\
\min\{\frac{1}{\ell},h_{\ell-1}\}&\text{if }\ell>1.\end{cases}

Define
Q_{h_{k}}=\mathcal{L}_{h_{k}}((T_{R_{k}}(Q))^{m_{k}})
for all
k
. Then by the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert Q_{h_{k}}-Q\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=0\text{ and }\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{h_{k}}(Q_{h_{k}})=\mathcal{F}(Q),

and therefore, for
h\in\{h_{1},h_{2},\ldots\}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert Q_{h}-Q\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=0\text{ and }\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{h}(Q_{h})=\mathcal{F}(Q).

This proves the existence of a recovery sequence for each
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{S})
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

We note that in Theorem
4.7
, the lower semicontinuity follows as in Proposition 3.2 in
[
15
]
, where
L_{5}=0
. Therefore the condition
L_{5}>0
is not necessary to derive the liminf property. However, it is needed for constructing the recovery sequence, which relies on Lemma
4.4
that bounds the energy density of the truncated
Q
, which again relies on Lemma
4.2
that is only true if
L_{5}>0
as remarked above. We have experimented with other truncations of
Q
and the elastic energy density
\sigma(Q)
in order to avoid the constraint
L_{5}>0
, but were not able to remove it. However, as explained in the introduction, the
L_{5}
-term can be justified in the same way as the other elastic energy terms, and as the numerical experiments in the upcoming section demonstrate, the numerical results are similar for
L_{5}>0
and
L_{5}=0
(in particular, the numerical scheme is stable independently of the positivity of
L_{5}
).

5.
Numerical results in 2D

We now present numerical simulations in two dimensions, representing a thin nematic film; the reduction from three dimensions to two is derived in
[
14
]
. We use the parameters

(5.1)

\displaystyle L_{1}=0.1,\quad L_{2}=L_{3}=L_{4}=L_{5}=0.001,\quad a=-0.3,\quad b=-4.0,\quad c=4.0,\quad M=1

for all experiments unless specified otherwise. To approximate
Q_{h}^{n+1}
, we calculate a sequence
\{Q_{h}^{n+1,m}\}
using Newton iteration as follows:

(5.2)

\displaystyle D\mathcal{L}^{n+1}(Q_{h}^{n+1,m-1}-G_{h})\cdot(Q_{h}^{n+1,m}-Q_{h}^{n+1,m-1})=-(\mathcal{L}^{n+1}(Q_{h}^{n+1,m-1})-Q_{h}^{n}),

where
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
is as defined in (
3.10
) and
D\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
is its Jacobian. We set
Q_{h}^{n+1}
equal to the first
Q_{h}^{n+1,m}
for which

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q_{h}^{n+1,m}-Q_{h}^{n+1,m-1}\right\rVert<10^{-10}.

This scheme appears to be very robust with respect to the time step size; even when we increased
\Delta t
by several orders of magnitude in our experiments, eventually some
m
satisfied (
5.3
) for all
n
.

These experiments were written in Julia. The code used to run them can be found at
https://github.com/elafandi/quartic-q-tensors

Appendix A
Tensor calculus lemmas

Lemma A.1
.

|\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}\leq 3|\nabla Q|^{2}
for all
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
.

Proof.

(A.1)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{div}\,Q|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(Q_{i1,1}+Q_{i2,2}+Q_{i3,3}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(3Q_{i1,1}^{2}+3Q_{i2,2}^{2}+3Q_{i3,3}^{2}-(Q_{i1,1}-Q_{i2,2})^{2}-(Q_{i1,1}-Q_{i3,3})^{2}-(Q_{i2,2}-Q_{i3,3})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(3Q_{i1,1}^{2}+3Q_{i2,2}^{2}+3Q_{i3,3}^{2}\right)\leq 3|\nabla Q|^{2}

∎

Lemma A.2
.

|\mathrm{curl}\,Q|^{2}\leq 2|\nabla Q|^{2}
for all
Q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
.

Proof.

Here we take indices modulo 3 for simplicity; so, for example,
Q_{02,4}
would mean
Q_{32,1}
.

(A.2)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{curl}\,Q|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}(Q_{ji,(i+1)}-Q_{j(i+1),i})^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\left(2Q_{ji,(i+1)}^{2}+2Q_{j(i+1),i}^{2}-(Q_{ji,(i+1)}+Q_{j(i+1),i})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\left(2Q_{ji,(i+1)}^{2}+2Q_{j(i+1),i}^{2}+2Q_{ji,i}^{2}\right)=2|\nabla Q|^{2}

∎

Lemma A.3
.

\left\lVert AB\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq 3\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
for all
A\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}),B\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
.

Proof.

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left\lVert AB\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|AB|_{F}^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|A|_{F}^{2}|B|_{F}^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}A_{ij}^{2}(x)\right)|B|_{F}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(9\max_{i,j,y}A_{ij}^{2}(y)\right)|B|_{F}^{2}\,dx=9\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

∎

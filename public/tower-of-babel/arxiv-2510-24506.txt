A Note on Primitive Pairs for Graded Lie Algebras
Tamanna Chatterjee

Abstract
. We develop a theory of primitive pairs for ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded Lie algebras when the sheaves have coefficients in a field 𝕜 \Bbbk of positive characteristic, providing a graded analogue of the role played by cuspidal pairs in the generalized Springer correspondence. We consider the centralizer G 0 G_{0} of a fixed cocharacter χ \chi in a connected, reductive, algebraic group G G and its action on the eigenspaces 𝔤 n \mathfrak{g}_{n} of χ \chi . Building on the framework of parity sheaves and the Fourier transform established in [ Ch , Ch1 ] , we show that every indecomposable parity sheaf on 𝔤 n \mathfrak{g}_{n} can be expressed as a direct summand of a complex induced from primitive data on a Levi subgroup. This result extends the fact that, in the graded setting, any indecomposable parity sheaf is direct summand of an induced cuspidal datum [ Ch ] . This confirms the organizing role of primitive pairs in the block decomposition of the category of G 0 G_{0} -equivariant parity sheaves on 𝔤 n \mathfrak{g}_{n} . We further establish that primitive pairs on the nilpotent cone induce primitive pairs in the graded setting, and we prove that primitivity is preserved under the Fourier–Sato transform. These results reveal a deep compatibility between the geometry of graded Lie algebras and their representation-theoretic structures, forming the foundation for a graded version of the generalized Springer correspondence in positive characteristic.



Introduction

The generalized Springer correspondence provides a geometric framework connecting the representation theory of reductive groups with the geometry of their nilpotent cones. Let
G
be a complex, connected, reductive algebraic group, and let
\mathfrak{g}
denote its Lie algebra. In both characteristic zero and positive characteristic, the category

\mathscr{I}(G)

of irreducible
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on the nilpotent cone admits a block decomposition, in which
cuspidal pairs
play a fundamental organizing role. Cuspidal pairs serve as the basic building blocks: every simple perverse sheaf arises via parabolic induction from cuspidal data on a Levi subgroup
[
AHJR
,
AHJR1
,
AHJR2
,
AHJR5
]
.

In the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded setting, the analogous notion is that of
primitive pairs
. We fix a cocharacter
\chi:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
. The group
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
via the adjoint action, and the space
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
represents the
n
-th weight space under this action, thereby defining a grading on
\mathfrak{g}
. The centralizer
G_{0}
of
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
also acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
via the adjoint action.

The
G_{0}
-equivariant derived category of sheaves with coefficients in a field
\Bbbk
,
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},\Bbbk)
has been studied in
[
Lu
]
when the characteristic of
\Bbbk
is
0
and in
[
Ch
,
Ch1
]
when the characteristic is positive. In positive characteristic, parity sheaves, introduced in
[
JMW
]
play a similar role to the intersection cohomology complexes (
\mathcal{IC}
’s) in characteristic
0
. For any pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
and
\mathcal{L}
is a
G_{0}
-equivariant irreducible local system on
\mathcal{O}
, there exists at most one indecomposable parity sheaf
\mathcal{E}
up to shift with the property that
\mathcal{E}|_{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{L}[\dim\mathcal{O}]
, and we write
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
to denote
\mathcal{E}
. We will denote the collection of all such pairs
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
by
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
. Similarly, on the nilpotent cone, for each pair
(C,\mathcal{F})
, where
C
is a
G
-orbit in the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{F}
is a
G
-equivariant irreducible local system on

\mathcal{E}(C,\mathcal{F})
with the property
\mathcal{E}(C,\mathcal{F})|_{C}=\mathcal{F}[\dim C]

A primitive pair on both nilpotent cone and graded Lie algebras arises from a cuspidal pair defined on the nilpotent cone or graded Lie algebra of a Levi subgroup (Definition
2.1
and
2.2
). Lusztig introduced this concept in
[
Lu
]
, showing that when the sheaf coefficients are coming from the field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
0
, the set
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
decomposes into subsets
\mathscr{I}(G,L,\mathcal{L})
,
where
L
is a Levi subgroup and
\mathcal{L}
is a primitive local system supported on an nilpotent

-orbit. This provides a graded counterpart to the generalized Springer correspondence. Similar phenomena were later observed in the work of Yun and Lusztig
[

]
on
\mathbb{Z}/m
-graded Lie algebras, where primitive pairs again play a central role in the block decomposition of

\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{i})
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/m
-graded piece of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{i})
denote the collection of
G_{\underline{0}}
-equivariant irreducible local system on
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
with
G_{\underline{0}}
being an appropriate analogue of
G_{0}
.
Motivated by these developments, one expects a parallel theory for
\mathbb{Z}
-gradings when the sheaf coefficients lie in a field
\Bbbk
of positive characteristic.

This paper is a continuation of
[
Ch
,
Ch1
]
, where parity sheaves and the Fourier–Sato transform on graded Lie algebras were studied in positive characteristic. The assumptions
[
Ch1
, Assumption 2.4]
and conjectures
[
Ch1
, Conjectures 2.8, 2.9]
stated there are also assumed to hold in the present setting. Here we prove:

Theorem 0.1
.

Any parity sheaf

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})

can be expressed as

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\mathcal{E}_{2},

where
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
are parity complexes induced from some primitive pairs.

This result generalizes Theorem 36 of
[
Ch
]
and can be viewed as the graded analogue of the role played by induced cuspidal perverse sheaves in the generalized Springer correspondence. In that correspondence, cuspidal pairs generate the entire category through parabolic induction; in our setting, primitive pairs serve the same purpose for graded Lie algebras.

We also prove that a primitive pair on the nilpotent cone gives rise to a primitive pair in the graded setting under suitable assumptions. Furthermore, we study how primitive pairs behave under the Fourier–Sato transform
\phign
, which is a functor from
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
to

[

]

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, and suppose

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{n}]\cong\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{-n}]

(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
.
Then
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is a primitive pair.

This result demonstrates that primitivity is preserved under the Fourier transform, highlighting a deep compatibility between the geometric and graded structures.

Organization

The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 recalls the necessary background, notations, and assumptions from
[

Ch1

2.
Primitive Pair

Primitive pairs were introduced by Lusztig
[
Lu
]
both on the nilpotent cone and on the graded Lie algebra. One of the main goals of that paper was to define a block decomposition of
\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
using primitive pairs on the nilpotent cone. In this section, we aim to establish positive characteristic analogues of several results from
[
Lu

\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})

A primitive pair on both nilpotent cone and graded Lie algebras arises from a cuspidal pair defined on the nilpotent cone or graded Lie algebra of a Levi subgroup. The definition below formalizes this idea by requiring the existence of an appropriate parabolic subgroup
P
and a graded version of the compatibility conditions that characterize primitivity on the nilpotent cone.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(C,\mathcal{E})\in\mathscr{I}(G)
is said to be
primitive
if there exists a parabolic subgroup
P
with a Levi factor
L
and a cuspidal pair
(C_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(L)^{0-\cu}
such that the following hold:

(1)

There exists a
P
-orbit
C^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{N}_{P}
such that for all
x\in C^{\prime}
, we have
\pi(x)\in C_{L}
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{l}
.

(2)

For any

, we have

L^{\pi(x)}/(L^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}\cong P^{x}/(P^{x})^{\circ}\cong G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}.

(3)

\mathcal{E}|_{C^{\prime}}\cong(\pi|_{C^{\prime}})^{*}\mathcal{E}_{L}
.

The definition of a primitive pair on
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is analogous to the one above for the nilpotent cone.

Definition 2.2

A pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})

L_{0}^{\pi(x)}/(L_{0}^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}\cong P_{0}^{x}/(P_{0}^{x})^{\circ}\cong G_{0}^{x}/(G_{0}^{x})^{\circ}.

(3)

\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\cong(\pi|_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}})^{*}\mathcal{E}_{L}

4.

In this section we recall the notion of Fourier transform in the setting of graded Lie algebras and summarize its formal definition and key properties. Let
G
,
\chi
,
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, and
G_{0}
be as in Section
1
.

The Fourier-Sato transform in the graded setting is the functor,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}:D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})\;\longrightarrow\;D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n}).

A detailed discussion of Fourier transform can be found in
[
Ch1
]
.

To keep track of the grading we use the notation
{{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p}}}\!\mathcal{I}_{n}
for the functor induction and
{{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p}}}\!\mathcal{R}_{n}
for the functor restriction.
In
[
Ch1
]
the author shows that under suitable hypotheses on the coefficient field (Assumption 2.4, Conjectures 2.8 and 2.9), this transform satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}
sends parity complexes in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
to parity complexes in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
.

(2)

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}
exchanges cuspidal pairs in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
with cuspidal pairs in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
.

(3)

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}
intertwines parabolic induction and restriction functors: if
P\subset G
is a parabolic with Levi
L
, then one has

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\circ{{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p}}}\!\mathcal{I}_{n}\;\cong\;{{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p}}}\!\mathcal{I}_{-n}\circ\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{n}}

and,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\circ{{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p}}}\!\mathcal{R}_{n}\;\cong\;{{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p}}}\!\mathcal{R}_{-n}\circ\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{n}}.

The goal of this section is to study the Fourier transform of primitive pairs.
We assume that
(G,\chi)
is
n
-rigid.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
is a primitive pair associated to a parabolic
P
containing a Levi subgroup
L
with
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
, and let
\pi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{l}
be the projection map.
Assume also that
\mathcal{O}_{n}
and
\mathcal{O}_{-n}
are the unique open
G_{0}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
respectively, and that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}
is the unique open
L_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{l}_{-n}
.
If

\mathcal{O}_{n}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{O}_{-n}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L})\neq\emptyset,

then
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{n}
.

The above proposition is proved in
[
Lu
, Prop. 12.2]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
.
Suppose

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{n}]\cong\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{-n}],

with
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
.
Then
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is a primitive pair.

Proof.

By
[
Ch
, Th. 36]
,
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is normal.
Therefore, there exists a parabolic
P
with Levi
L
and
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
appears as a direct summand of
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
.

Let
(\tilde{\mathcal{O}},\tilde{\mathcal{L}})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
be the primitive pair associated to
P,L
and
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
.
By Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 of
[
Ch1
]
induction commutes with the Fourier transform.
Hence
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
appears as a direct summand of
\ind_{-n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
.

From the definition of induction for a cuspidal pair
[
Ch
, Lemma 25]
and our assumptions, there exist
x\in\mathcal{O}
and
g\in G_{0}
such that
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})x\in\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
.
The assumption
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
implies that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
appears as a direct summand of
\ind_{-n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
.

By Theorem 4.5 of
[
Ch1
]
, Fourier transform sends cuspidal pairs to cuspidal pairs.
Hence there exists
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{-n})^{\cu}
such that

\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{L}).

This implies that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}
is the unique open
L_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{l}_{-n}
.

Thus, there exist
y\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}\in G_{0}
such that
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{\prime-1})y\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{-n}
.
Replacing
x
by
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})x
and
g
by
1
, we see that the new
x
belongs to both
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
. Therefore,

\mathcal{O}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\neq\emptyset.

Similarly, one can define
y
such that
y\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\cap(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{-n})
, and hence

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L})\neq\emptyset.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.1
,
\mathcal{O}=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
.

Now we can apply Theorem
3.5
(1), which states that
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
is a direct sum of copies of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
up to shift.
Combining this with our assumption, we obtain
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
, which implies
\mathcal{L}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

5.
Quasi-Monomial

In this section, we introduce
good pairs
, which can be viewed as a generalization of the notion of normal complexes introduced in
[
Lu
]
and
[
Ch
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

A pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
is called
quasi-monomial
if there exists a parabolic subgroup
P
with Levi subgroup
L
such that the following hold:

(1)

(L,\chi)
is
n
-rigid.

(2)

There exists a primitive pair
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})
such that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is isomorphic, up to shift, to
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. In this case, we refer to
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
as a
quasi-monomial object
.

Remark 5.2
.

A quasi-monomial object is said to be
proper quasi-monomial
if
P
is a proper parabolic subgroup of
G
.

Definition 5.3
.

A parity complex
\mathcal{E}
in
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
is called a
good object
if
\mathcal{E}
is a direct sum of quasi-monomial objects. It is said to be
proper
if all quasi-monomial summands are proper.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
. Then there exist good objects
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
in
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
such that

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{F}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction on the reductive quotient associated to proper parabolic subgroups containing
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. The proof is divided into several steps.

(1)

We first show that the statement holds when
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
and either
(G,\chi)
is not
n
-rigid, or
\mathcal{O}
is not the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
.

We argue by induction on
\dim\mathcal{O}
. Choose
x\in\mathcal{O}
and construct
P,L,U,\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{n}
as in
[
Ch
, 5.2]
. Then
P\neq G
. By
[
Ch
, Theorem 28]
, the element
x
lies in the open
L_{0}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{L}\subset\mathfrak{l}_{n}
, and the restriction
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}
is an irreducible local system, denoted
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.

By
[
Ch
, Theorem 30]
, the complex
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
has support
\bar{\mathcal{O}}
and satisfies

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})|_{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{L}[\dim\mathcal{O}_{L}].

Furthermore,
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
is a parity complex by
[
Ch
, Theorem 37]
. Hence,

(5.7)

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime},

where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a parity complex supported on
\bar{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\mathcal{O}
.

By the induction hypothesis, there exist good objects
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
in
D^{b}_{L_{0}}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})
such that

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\oplus\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\mathcal{E}_{2}.

Applying induction yields

(5.8)

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\oplus\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{2}.

Combining (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) gives

(5.9)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\oplus\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{2}.

Since support of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
has smaller dimension than
\mathcal{O}
, by the induction hypothesis there exist good objects
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}
such that

(5.10)

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\cong\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}.

Combining (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) yields

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}\oplus\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{2}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}.

By the transitivity of induction
[
Ch
, Theorem 20]
, both
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}_{2}
are good objects. This completes Step (1).

(2)

If
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
and either
(G,\chi)
is not
(-n)
-rigid or
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is not the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
, the statement follows by an argument parallel to Step (1).

(3)

Now we show that for
a proper pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
there exist good objects
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
in
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
such that

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{F}.

Since
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is proper, we may write

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})={}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{-n}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M}),

where
Q
is a proper parabolic subgroup of
G
containing
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, and
(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{m}_{-n})
is a primitive pair with Levi
M\subset Q
.
Since induction commutes with the Fourier transform, we have

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})={}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{m}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M}).

By induction, there exist good objects
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{M}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{M}
in
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})
such that

\Phi_{\mathfrak{m}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{M}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{M}.

Hence,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{n}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{M}\cong{}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{n}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{M},

and the claim follows from the transitivity of induction.

(4)

Now assume
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
with
\bar{\mathcal{O}}=\mathfrak{g}_{n}
and the support of
(\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L}))
is not the unique open
G_{0}
orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
. By
[
Ch1
, Theorem 4.5]
,
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is a parity sheaf. By Step (2), there exist good objects
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
in
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
such that

\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}.

Applying
\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}
one more time we get,

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\cong\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}.

Using Step (3) for
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
yields the result.

(5)

Finally, if
(G,\chi)
is not
n
-rigid or
\mathcal{O}
is not the open
G_{0}
-orbit, the result follows from Step (1). If the assumptions of Step (4) hold, the result follows from that step as well. Therefore, we may assume
(G,\chi)
is
n
-rigid,
\bar{\mathcal{O}}=\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, and the support of
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is not unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
. By Theorem
4.2
, such
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is primitive, hence good.

∎

Remark 5.5
.

An analogous statement to Theorem
5.4
holds for
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
, and the proof proceeds identically.

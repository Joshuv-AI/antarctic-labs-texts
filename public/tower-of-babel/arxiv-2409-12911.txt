Holographic correlators with BPS bound states in 𝒩 = 4 {\mathcal{N}}=4 SYM
Francesco Aprile, Stefano Giusto, Rodolfo Russo

Abstract
We compute 4-point correlators in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) Super-Yang-Mills with both single and double-particle 1 / 2 1/2 -BPS operators in the regime of large ’t Hooft coupling and large N N . In particular we give explicit expressions up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / N 4 ) \mathcal{O}(1/N^{4}) for the lightest correlator with two double-particle and two single-particle operators built out of the stress-tensor multiplet. Our derivation makes use of the general holographic prescription applied to an asymptotically AdS 5 × {}_{5}\times S 5 geometry that describes a coherent superposition of multi-graviton operators. The final result can be written in terms of a natural generalisation of the standard D-functions and takes a compact form in Mellin space. The correlator we compute here is the simplest of a more general class of correlators where two inserted operators are multi-particles. These can be derived with the same approach, suggesting that the structure found here is general.

Introducing the problem

In this letter we study a new class of 4-point correlators in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM

We focus on 1/2-BPS local operators that can be identified very precisely on both sides

On the gravity side, the spectrum of quadratic fluctuations of type IIB supergravity

II
The supergravity approach to multi-particle correlators

The key idea is to study the generating function of the correlators (
2
)
for different values of
n

\langle O_{H}O_{H}O_{L}O_{L}\rangle

sourced by the heavy operator. These geometries are the bubbling solutions
constructed by LLM in the beautiful work
[
24

For our purposes it will be sufficient to identify the family of geometries

We will demonstrate that the small

Outlook

We obtained the first explicit result for a holographic 4-point correlator in

SYM involving double-particle operators. We found that the
connected correlator is a function of trascendentality up to four, where the
double-box ladder integral

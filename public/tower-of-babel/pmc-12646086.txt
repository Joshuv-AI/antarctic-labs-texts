Bronchoscopic management of airway fistulas: a clinical analysis of 45 cases with integrated bioinformatic analysis
Authors: Wen Sun, Bangchao Zhao, Hong Zhang, Yuan Cheng, Junfang Huang, Guangfa Wang, Jiping Liao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAirway fistula represents a complex clinical condition where the optimal bronchoscopic management remains controversial. This study aims to evaluate clinical characteristics, bronchoscopic treatment efficacy, and prognostic factors.MethodsThis retrospective study analysed 45 patients with fistulas who underwent a total of 56 bronchoscopic intervention sessions over the past seven years. The fistulas were categorized and analysed based on their size, clinical features and treatment efficacy, with prognostic factors for healing being evaluated. In parallel, a bioinformatic analysis was initiated with 2075 genes sourced from GeneCards. Following filtration, the top 20 hub genes were identified and subsequently subjected to pathway enrichment analysis to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.ResultsCompared to the non-remission group, the remission group exhibited significantly smaller fistula diameters, higher lymphocyte counts, and lower high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) levels. Prognostic analysis revealed that fistula size and hsCRP level were independent predictors. Small fistulas (≤5 mm) cases solely underwent blocking agent intervention had a significantly higher remission rate. While the others (>5 mm) were mostly managed with stents, endobronchial valves, and cardiac occluders. Bioinformatic analysis of public data identified significant enrichment in the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway, pinpointing six core genes, including CXCL8, IL1B, IL6, TNF, TGFB1, and IL13.Conclusions Prioritize bronchoscopic treatment with inflammation control for small fistulas; recommend combined therapies for large fistulas.The PI3K-AKT and cytokine pathways may be involved in healing and represent potential targets for future drug development. Keywords: Airway fistula, bronchoscopy, inflammation, PI3K-AKT, cytokine

Introduction

Airway fistulas are pathological connections characterized by loss of airway wall integrity, requiring communication between both fistula orifices. These clinically complex conditions encompass intricate pathophysiological mechanisms. Anatomically, they are classified into four main subtypes: (1) tracheobronchopleural fistula (TBPF); (2) tracheobronchial-enteric fistula, including tracheobronchial-oesophageal fistula (TBEF) and tracheobronchial-gastric fistula; (3) tracheobronchial-mediastinal fistula (TBMF); and (4) fistulas involving other rare sites, such as bronchobiliary and bronchorenal fistulas [1–3]. The pathogenesis of airway fistulas is multifactorial, arising from surgical complications (e.g. pneumonectomy, lobectomy), pulmonary infective necrosis, oncological treatments (chemotherapy/radiotherapy), malignant erosion, or trauma [2–4]. Treatment selection depends on fistula aetiology: benign cases with preserved nutritional status typically warrant surgical intervention due to their reversible nature, while malignant fistulas with systemic compromise during anticancer therapy generally require non-surgical management [2,4].

Bronchoscopic techniques provide a minimally invasive alternative for poor surgical candidates, those requiring temporary fistula closure prior to definitive surgery, or patients with advanced malignant fistulas. These procedures facilitate precise deployment of blocking agents, endobronchial valves, occluders, or stents, offering triple advantages of technical feasibility, minimal invasiveness, and rapid recovery [4,5]. Beyond achieving fistula closure, bronchoscopic management optimizes nutritional status and prevents life-threatening complications like aspiration pneumonia.

The bronchoscopic management of tracheobronchial fistulas is dictated by the fistula’s etiology, location, size, and underlying cause [4,5]. Nevertheless, selecting the most effective technique among specific bronchoscopic modalities remains contentious. For instance, large TBPF (>3–5 mm) typically require closure with specialized devices such as stents, endobronchial valve or occluders to achieve cure; however, the choice of specific device, combination strategies, and technical approaches varies widely based on operator experience [5]. In cases of TBEF, stent placement is generally recommended for non-surgical patients with a fistula diameter exceeding 5 mm, while other methods like occluders or valves are supported only by limited case reports [4]. Moreover, it parallels a critical knowledge gap in understanding the molecular drivers of fistula pathogenesis and treatment response. Although a handful of molecular studies have focused on the origin of airway fistulas using techniques like whole-genome transcriptome profiling [6], the mechanisms underlying their characteristics and treatment outcomes remain poorly understood. Overall, the current evidence remains limited, consisting largely of single-centre case series, and this scarcity of robust clinical data continues to hinder the development of standardized management guidelines.

This retrospective study addresses critical knowledge gaps by employing an integrated approach that combines clinical analysis with bioinformatic exploration. We aim to (1) characterize clinical phenotypes, (2) identify prognostic factors, and (3) comprehensively evaluate the efficacy. We hypothesize that smaller fistula size, indicative of a milder inflammatory response, is a key determinant of therapeutic success. Furthermore, by analysing fistula-related gene expression data, we seek to uncover the key signalling pathways that may underpin the clinical outcomes, thereby bridging clinical observations and molecular mechanisms. The findings are expected to establish an evidence-based framework for diagnosis and treatment, ultimately improving patient outcomes and quality of life.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study enrolled all patients diagnosed with airway fistula via bronchoscopy at our institution between January 2018 and February 2025. Patients with incomplete follow-up or non-bronchoscopic treatments were excluded.

Fistula dimensions and locations were precisely measured using CT three-dimensional reconstruction and/or direct bronchoscopic assessment. Aetiological diagnosis was established through comprehensive review of medical history and post-treatment biopsy pathology.

We adopted the clinically more relevant endpoint of ‘fistula remission,’ defined as the attainment of both clinical relief and successful bronchoscopic closure, as per the following criteria:

Radiological or endoscopic evidence of fistula reduction/healing, OR

Radiological or endoscopic evidence of fistula reduction/healing, OR

Complete fistula occlusion by stent, valve, occluder placement with sustained (≥1 month) symptom resolution

Complete fistula occlusion by stent, valve, occluder placement with sustained (≥1 month) symptom resolution

Chest tube removal when applicable

Chest tube removal when applicable

Discharged patients underwent regular telephone interviews and outpatient follow-ups, with all data systematically recorded in our institutional database. This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University First Hospital (Approval No. 2025-051) and was conducted in strict accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki. While this case-control study had a retrospective design, informed consent was still secured from each subject through telephone follow-up.

Bronchoscopic intervention strategies

All procedures were performed under general anaesthesia using either flexible bronchoscopy or rigid bronchoscopy (Olympus Medical, Tokyo, Japan). Fistula sealing efficacy was verified by endoscopic injection of methylene blue, either through a thoracic drainage tube or directly into the airway/oesophagus.

Biological glue (Beijing FuAiLe Science and Technology Development Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), gelatin sponge (Jiangxi Xiang’en Medical Technology Development Co., Ltd, Nanchang, China), or fibroblast growth factor (Essex Bio-Technology Ltd., Hong Kong, China) were used to achieve complete fistula closure. Excess or poorly positioned adhesive material was removed or adjusted using biopsy forceps when necessary.

Stent dimensions were selected based on the fistula’s location, size, and the length of the lesion. In this study, stent therapy was primarily employed for TBEF cases, with only one TBPF patient having received stent placement at an external institution prior to admission. The stents used included metallic covered stents (Boston Scientific, Natick, MA, USA) and silicone stents (Novatech Glass Co.,Ltd., Zhejiang, China).

Endobronchial valve (EBV, Zephyr, Pulmonx Inc., Neuchatel, Switzerland) design permits the expulsion of air and fluid while preventing their re-entry into the affected fistula region. In this study, EBV placement was exclusively applied for TBPF cases.

Cardiac septal occluders (Starway Medical Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were utilized in both TBPF and TBEF cases. Bronchoscopic guidance ensured accurate localization and sizing of the fistula opening. The occluder was advanced through the fistula via a delivery sheath and gently retracted to secure a snug fit against the airway wall.

Conventional therapeutic approaches

The management of airway fistulae requires type-specific approaches. For TBPF, immediate chest tube placement is mandatory to drain pleural collections, perform therapeutic irrigation, and prevent contralateral aspiration. TBMF management centers on establishing mediastinal drainage coupled with culture-directed antibiotics for infection control, constituting the essential first-line intervention. The most complex TBEF demand a multifaceted protocol: strict nil per os status with continuous gastrointestinal decompression, establishment of effective drainage, targeted anti-infective therapy, and early enteral nutrition via nasojejunal or surgical jejunostomy tubes.

Bioinformatics analysis

Airway fistula-related genes were retrieved from the GeneCards database [7] (Version 5.25; https://www.genecards.org/; accessed on 8 February 2025). The top 1039 genes were selected based on a median relevance score cutoff, with scores ranging from 2.02 to 32.13. To investigate the underlying molecular mechanisms and key signalling pathways, we conducted Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses via the DAVID database [8] (v2023q4; https://davidbioinformatics.nih.gov/; accessed on 8 February 2025). The results were visualized using ImageGP (https://www.bic.ac.cn/Imagegp/), and adjusted p-values were applied in the bioinformatic analysis. Additionally, we constructed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network using the STRING database [9] (Version 12.0; https://string-db.org/; accessed on 22 March 2025) and identified the top 20 hub genes based on the medium combined score (0.4). The node degree of each gene was calculated to assess its centrality within the network.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (normally distributed) or median (IQR, non-normally distributed), while categorical variables were summarized as numbers and percentages. Intergroup comparisons were performed using Student’s t-test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, or chi-square test. Logistic regression was employed to identify prognostic factors, and Cox proportional hazards regression was used for outcome event analysis. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 (International Business Machines Corporation, IBM, America), and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographics

Between January 2018 and February 2025, we identified 45 patients (29 male, 16 female) with airway fistula, comprising 17 TBPFs, 24 TBEFs, and 4 TBMFs (Table 1). Fistula closure was achieved in all patients with broncholithiasis and in over half of the remaining benign cases (infection or surgical treatment). In contrast, no remission was observed in fistulas caused by radiotherapy or malignant aetiologies. All malignant fistulas were TBEF cases. The median fistula diameter was significantly smaller in the remission group (5.3 mm; IQR 3.6–8.1 mm) than in the non-remission group (8.7 mm; IQR 6.4–15.0 mm). Additionally, the remission group exhibited a significantly higher lymphocyte count (1.4 × 109/L, IQR 0.9–1.8 × 109/L vs. 0.7 × 109/L, IQR 0.4–1.2 × 109/L) and a lower hsCRP level (7.8 mg/L, IQR 1.6–42.4 mg/L vs. 65.5 mg/L, IQR 15.0–128.1 mg/L) compared to the non-remission group.

To facilitate more accurate statistical analysis and clinical application, we established cutoff values for the aforementioned quantitative parameters based on clinical experience and ROC curve analysis. Specifically, fistula diameters were categorized as small (≤5 mm), medium (5–10 mm), or large (>10 mm). Additionally, lymphocyte counts were dichotomized using a cutoff of 1.25 × 109/L, and hsCRP levels were stratified with a threshold of 10 mg/L.

As detailed in Table 2, bronchoscopic treatment outcomes demonstrated size-dependent remission rates: 75% for small fistulas (≤5 mm), 30.8% for medium fistulas (5–10 mm), and 17.6% for large fistulas (>10 mm). For TBEF cases, the dual-stent placement group (n = 3; 2 benign and 1 malignant fistula) achieved a 50% remission rate, contrasting with a 28.6% remission rate in the single-stent group (n = 21; 13 benign, 6 malignant, and 2 of undetermined aetiology).

The small fistula cohort demonstrated particularly favourable outcomes, with 92.3% (12/13) receiving single-modality bronchoscopic treatment. The remission rate significantly exceeded that of untreated cases (75% vs. 0%, p < 0.001). Treatment efficacy varied by modality: biological glue showed 77.8% remission, stent placement achieved 100% success, while EBV were ineffective (0%). All treatment failures in both biological glue and EBV groups were associated with recurrent infections.

Among 43 medium-to-large fistulas receiving endoscopic intervention, remission rates showed no statistically significant difference between single-modality and combination therapy (24.0% vs. 27.8%). In the medium-size fistula group, biological glue therapy (either alone or in combination) demonstrated superior efficacy (41.7% remission), comparable to other endoscopic approaches. For large fistulas, stent placement was uniformly unsuccessful (0% remission), while EBV placement showed a 50% remission rate.

However, these findings should be interpreted with caution due to potential classification variability and etiological heterogeneity among the cases. The observed differences highlight the need for validation through larger, prospective studies with standardized assessment criteria.

Our analysis revealed that smaller fistula diameter and lower hsCRP levels emerged as significant independent predictors of both successful fistula closure or remission. Lymphocyte count did not demonstrate statistically significant predictive value.

For the fistula size, patients with small (≤5mm) and medium-sized (5–10mm) fistulas showed significantly higher remission rates compared to those with large fistulas, with hazard ratios (HR) of 8.44 (95% CI: 1.78–40.00; p = 0.007) and 3.00 (95% CI: 0.79–11.42; p = 0.108), respectively. Bioinformatics analysis

The above results underscore the critical role of inflammatory response in fistula healing. To bridge clinical findings with potential mechanisms, we performed a bioinformatics analysis. Results showed significant enrichment of the PI3K-AKT signalling pathway (hsa04151) and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (hsa04060) (Figure 2). Using PPI network analysis and degree ranking, we identified six hub genes—CXCL8, IL1B, IL6, TNF, TGFB1, and IL13—with the highest connectivity, indicating their central regulatory roles in these pathways. Discussion

Previous studies have demonstrated that patients with fistula diameters ≤5 mm achieve significantly higher endoscopic cure rates compared to those with larger fistulas, while the therapeutic efficacy for fistulas >8 mm remains controversial [4,10]. Our findings are largely consistent with these conclusions: the median fistula size in the remission group was 5.3 mm, significantly smaller than the 8.7 mm observed in the non-remission group. Further stratified analysis revealed that a fistula diameter ≤5 mm served as an independent predictor of post-treatment remission. Although subgroup analysis did not show statistically significant differences, the remission rate for medium-sized fistulas (5–10 mm) was markedly superior to that of large fistulas (>10 mm). For patients with airway fistulas, successful fistula closure and effective control of pulmonary infection can substantially improve quality of life and prolong survival [1,10]. Our results indicate that airway fistula patients with higher lymphocyte counts and lower hsCRP levels exhibited better clinical remission rates. Although the impact of lymphocyte count on healing prognosis did not reach statistical significance, this trend suggests that favourable immune status and reduced inflammatory levels may create optimal conditions for fistula repair. Regarding anatomical location, while prior literature reported significantly higher cure rates for right-sided pleural fistulas compared to left-sided fistulas, with an opposite trend observed in oesophageal fistulas [10,11], our study did not identify a significant association between fistula location and healing rates. It should be noted that due to limited sample sizes in anatomical subgroups, there exists a substantial risk of selection bias, necessitating validation through larger-scale studies.

Bronchoscopic intervention provides a safe, effective, and minimally invasive therapeutic option for patients with airway fistulas who are ineligible for surgery or require bridging therapy. Recent retrospective studies and systematic reviews indicate that bronchoscopic techniques achieve an overall success rate exceeding 70% in closing fistula tracts, particularly for those with smaller distal diameters (<5–6 mm) [4,5,12]. However, the optimal treatment selection and definitive efficacy for fistulas of varying sizes remain subjects of ongoing debate.

For small fistulas (≤5mm), although conventional conservative management (e.g. chest tube drainage) may promote partial closure through adequate drainage, our study demonstrates that bronchoscopic intervention significantly improves fistula remission rates and clinical outcomes. Therefore, endoscopic treatment should be preferred over watchful waiting for this patient population. Endoscopic closure typically employs blocking agents (such as biological glue, gelatine sponge or fibroblast growth factors) for rapid and convenient treatment, while stents or valves may be considered for complex cases. In our cohort of small fistulas, bronchoscopic treatment achieved a 75% overall remission rate, with treatment failure primarily attributable to inadequate infection control.

Prior studies indicate significantly lower success rates for bronchoscopic treatment of larger fistulas (>8 mm), particularly when relying solely on blocking agents [4,5]. In our study, medium (5–10mm) and large (>10mm) fistulas were treated with stents, valves or cardiac occluders based on anatomical complexity. The results showed comparable efficacy between single-device therapy and combination therapy with biological glue, underscoring the importance of selecting occlusion methods and sizes tailored to our fistula characteristics.

Notably, all malignant fistulas in our series were medium/large esophago-respiratory fistulas treated with stents, none of which achieved healing, potentially influencing the overall assessment of stent efficacy. Previous research suggests better outcomes with dual (oesophageal and airway) versus single airway stenting [11]. Among our three cases receiving dual stents, the remission rate (50%) exceeded that of single airway stents (28.6%), though the small sample size warrants cautious interpretation.

The PI3K-AKT signalling pathway serves as a pivotal regulator of inflammatory responses, airway remodelling, and immune modulation [13,14]. Its dysregulation has been conclusively demonstrated to participate in the pathogenesis of various respiratory disorders, including chronic airway diseases, inflammatory conditions, and malignancies [13,14]. In chronic airway diseases, abnormal activation of the PI3K pathway markedly enhances excessive secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g. TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-13) and chemokines. Of particular significance, this pathway modulates adaptive immune responses in chronic airway inflammation by augmenting B lymphocyte proliferation, survival, activation and maturation, while simultaneously promoting T-cell differentiation into functionally active Th1, Th2 and Th17 subsets [14,15].

Although direct evidence regarding PI3K-AKT pathway involvement in airway fistula remains limited, our findings in conjunction with existing literature suggest that this pathway likely contributes substantially to fistula pathogenesis through mediating inflammatory cascades and airway remodelling processes, while engaging in complex cross-talk with multiple cytokine signalling networks. Given the established correlation between fistula prognosis and both immune status and microbial infections, future therapeutic strategies targeting PI3K inhibition while incorporating antimicrobial properties may offer superior clinical efficacy for this patient population.

This study has inherent limitations due to its single-centre, retrospective design. Despite spanning six years of data collection, the sample size remains relatively small, which may introduce potential biases in patient selection, fistula type distribution, and etiological analysis. Additionally, variations in follow-up intervals and evaluation procedures were unavoidable. To enhance data reliability, we rigorously excluded missing and invalid data while conducting in-depth analysis of the available dataset. Furthermore, although inter-operator variability in endoscopic procedures is inevitable, all airway fistula treatments in our institution were performed by experienced, senior physicians who received standardized training, thereby ensuring optimal consistency in therapeutic approaches.

Conclusion

Clinical recommendations

This study confirms that fistula diameter and the degree of local inflammation are independent prognostic factors influencing the healing of airway fistulas, with smaller fistulas and mild inflammatory status indicating favourable outcomes. As the cornerstone of airway fistula management, bronchoscopic interventional therapy has been well-validated in clinical practice. Most small fistulas can be effectively treated with block agent alone, whereas medium-to-large fistulas often require comprehensive approaches, including stent placement, endobronchial valves, or cardiac occluders. We emphasize that personalized bronchoscopic treatment strategies should be tailored based on a thorough evaluation of fistula characteristics and the patient’s systemic condition.

Mechanistic insights and therapeutic prospects

Beyond clinical evaluation, results suggest a potential role for the PI3K-AKT pathway and its regulated cytokines in fistula pathogenesis. Targeting these pathways may offer a new direction for developing novel therapies that modulate the local immune microenvironment and promote fistula healing.

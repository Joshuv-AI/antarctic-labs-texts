Systems with Quantum Dimensions Preprint: APS/123-QED
Mikołaj Myszkowski 1 , Mattia Damia Paciarini 1 , and Francesco Sannino 1,2,3,4

Abstract
We propose quantum-mechanical systems in which the number of spatial dimensions is promoted to a dynamical quantum variable. As a consequence, the effective dimension depends on the physical state of the system. Interestingly, systems of this form exhibit enhanced symmetries compared to their fixed-dimensional counterparts. As an explicit example, we analyze a harmonic oscillator for which the spatial dimension is represented by a quantum operator. By evaluating the corresponding partition function, we uncover a temperature-dependent effective dimension. Our framework opens a new avenue for constructing physical systems, from gravity to condensed matter, where the very notion of dimensionality becomes quantum.



Introduction—
The reason why our universe manifests precisely three spatial and one temporal dimensions is not yet fully understood. Although numerous approaches to quantum gravity and high-energy physics suggest that the effective number of spacetime dimensions may vary with the energy scale
18
, there is still no unified understanding of how dimensionality itself could emerge as a physical, dynamical quantity.

In this Letter, we propose a new framework in which the number of spatial dimensions is promoted to a quantum variable subject to the laws of quantum mechanics. This leads to what we call quantum-dimension (QD) systems. The corresponding Hilbert space is built as a direct sum of Hilbert spaces with fixed dimensionality, allowing quantum superpositions of states residing in different numbers of dimensions. Within this setup, dimensionality becomes an observable represented by a Hermitian operator, and its measured value depends on the physical state of the system.

We further show that QD systems support enhanced symmetry structures involving transformations that mix states of different dimensionality. These novel symmetries are a hallmark of the framework, since they are absent in conventional quantum systems. We demonstrate the idea by constructing and solving a QD harmonic oscillator, a generalized version of the standard harmonic oscillator whose dimensionality is quantized. The effective dimension of the system is found to vary with the energy scale.

We anticipate further generalizations of the framework to relativistic quantum fields featuring various types of interactions across different dimensions. Furthermore, we envision possible applications across diverse fields, from condensed matter systems to cosmology and quantum gravity, where the dimension itself is treated within the QD framework.

Dimension as a Quantum Variable—
A quantum system is characterized by a Hilbert space together with a set of operators acting on it. Suppose that for each number of spatial dimensions
d
in the range
N_{1}\leq d\leq N_{2}
, with
N_{1}\leq N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we can define a quantum system with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
. This Hilbert space is spanned by the states

\mathcal{H}^{(d)}=\text{Span}\{\ket{i^{(d)}}|\ i^{(d)}\in I^{(d)}\},

(1)

where
i^{(d)}\in I^{(d)}
labels elements of an arbitrary basis of
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
. We denote the scalar operators acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
by
\hat{O}^{(d)}
.

A generic state of a QM system with a variable number of dimensions can be conceptualized as a superposition of states belonging to different
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
(see Fig.
1
). Under this assumption, the Hilbert space of a QD system is taken to be

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\mathcal{H}^{(d)},

(2)

where
\oplus
denotes the direct sum. A generic state
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}
can then be written as a superposition

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\sum_{i^{(d)}\in I^{(d)}}c^{(d)}_{i^{(d)}}\ket{d;i^{(d)}},

(3)

where
{c^{(d)}_{i^{(d)}}}
are complex numbers and the additional index
d
has been added to the kets in (
1
) to label the number of spatial dimensions in which the state resides. The coefficients
{c^{(d)}_{i^{(d)}}}
satisfy the normalization condition
\braket{\psi|\psi}=1
, but are otherwise arbitrary. It follows that the QD Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a generalization of
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
: the ordinary
(d+1)
-dimensional system is recovered in the limit
N_{1}=N_{2}=d
.

In analogy with (
2
), we can define generalized observables acting on the full Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
via the direct sum

\hat{O}=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\hat{O}^{(d)}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{O}^{(N_{1})}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\hat{O}^{(N_{2})}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(4)

where
\hat{O}^{(d)}
are the ordinary
(d+1)
-dimensional operators. The action of the generalized operator (
4
) on a state (
3
) is given by

\bra{k;i^{(k)}}\hat{O}\ket{d;j^{(d)}}=\begin{cases}\bra{i^{(k)}}\hat{O}^{(d)}\ket{j^{(d)}}&\text{if}\ k=d,\\
0&\text{if}\ k\neq d.\end{cases}

(5)

In addition to generalized observables (
4
), all QD systems are naturally endowed with a new “dimension” operator, an observable corresponding to the number of spatial dimensions:

\hat{D}=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}d\hat{I}^{(d)}=\begin{bmatrix}N_{1}\hat{I}^{(N_{1})}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&N_{2}\hat{I}^{(N_{2})}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(6)

where
\hat{I}^{(d)}
is the identity operator acting on
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
. In the limit
N_{1}=N_{2}=d
, the generalized Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\equiv\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
, and the dimension operator becomes trivial, i.e., proportional to the identity operator
\hat{I}^{(d)}
.

As in an ordinary system with a fixed number of dimensions, the time evolution is driven by the Hamiltonian. The most general QD Hamiltonian can be split into

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{int},

(7)

where

\hat{H}_{0}=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\hat{H}^{(d)}

(8)

is the generalization (
4
) of the fixed-
d
Hamiltonians
\hat{H}^{(d)}
acting on
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
, and
\hat{H}_{int}
is a generic interaction term. In the case of a theory with
\hat{H}_{int}=0
,
\hat{D}
commutes with the Hamiltonian:

\left[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{D}\right]=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}d\left[\hat{H}^{(d)},\hat{I}^{(d)}\right]=0,

(9)

and the dimensionality is a constant of motion. Conversely, if
\bra{d;i^{(d)}}\hat{H}_{int}\ket{k;j^{(k)}}\neq 0
for
i^{(d)},j^{(k)}
such that
d\neq k
, the Hamiltonian (
7
) may no longer commute with
\hat{D}
, allowing the dimensionality of the system to change dynamically.

Plots of the harmonic oscillator wavefunctions in different dimensions. The three states

belong to

,
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}
, and
\mathcal{H}^{(3)}
, respectively. The Hilbert space of the QD harmonic oscillator is then constructed by combining
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
,
d=1,2,3
via the direct sum
\bigoplus
. A generic state of a QD harmonic oscillator is a linear combination of states
\ket{\psi^{(d)}}\in\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
.

QD Harmonic Oscillator—
We now showcase our framework by generalizing the ordinary harmonic oscillator, whose Hamiltonian in
d+1
dimensions can be written as

\hat{H}^{(d)}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left((\hat{a}_{i}^{(d)})^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}^{(d)}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{I}^{(d)}\right),

(10)

where
(\hat{a}_{i}^{(d)})^{\dagger}
and
\hat{a}_{i}^{(d)}
are the
(d+1)
-dimensional creation and annihilation operators, respectively
43
. Here, we adopt units in which
\hbar\omega=1
and set the energy such that the ground state of the system is at
E=d/2
. After diagonalizing, the eigenstates and eigenvalues of (
10
) are labeled by a set of
d
integers
k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

\ket{k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}},\quad E(k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)})=\frac{d}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}^{(d)}.

(11)

Now, consider a QD generalization of (
10
) in the case where the Hamiltonian (
7
) does not have an interacting part
\hat{H}_{int}
. The generalized Hamiltonian is therefore constructed solely using (
8
):

\hat{H}_{0}=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left((\hat{a}_{i}^{(d)})^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}^{(d)}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{I}^{(d)}\right).

(12)

The eigenstates of the generalized Hamiltonian are again (
11
), but now
d
ranges over all possible values:

\ket{d;k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}},\quad N_{1}\leq d\leq N_{2},\quad k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(13)

The spectrum of
\hat{H}_{0}
is then given by:

E(k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)})=\frac{d}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}^{(d)}.

(14)

Suppose that the QD harmonic oscillator is brought to thermal equilibrium at the temperature
T=\frac{1}{k_{B}\beta}
. The system is therefore described by the canonical distribution:

P(k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)})=\frac{e^{-\beta E(k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)})}}{Z(\beta)},

(15)

where the partition function can be expressed in terms of the partition function of a
(1+1)
-dimensional harmonic oscillator
Z(\beta)^{(1)}
as

Z(\beta)=\sum_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left(Z^{(1)}(\beta)\right)^{d},\quad Z^{(1)}(\beta)=\frac{e^{-\beta/2}}{1-e^{-\beta}}.

(16)

Using the canonical distribution, the expectation values of the dimension operator and generalized Hamiltonian (
12
) can be written in the form:

\braket{\hat{D}}=\frac{\sum_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}d(Z^{(1)})^{d}}{Z},\quad\braket{\hat{H}_{0}}=-\frac{1}{Z}\partial_{\beta}Z.

(17)

Both the dimension
\braket{\hat{D}}
and the energy
\braket{\hat{H}_{0}}
depend non-trivially on the temperature
T
. Eliminating this dependence, we obtain the dimensionality of the system as a function of energy, presented in Fig.
2
. This example demonstrates one possible mechanism by which a QD system may experience scale-dependent variations in the number of spatial dimensions.

Even though we started with an integer-valued spectrum for
\hat{D}
, the effective dimension
\braket{\hat{D}}
need not be an integer and can vary continuously with the energy scale. It should also be emphasized that
\braket{\hat{D}}
is only one of the possible dimensional estimators. In principle, one could define dimensionality based on other physical quantities
18
. A notable advantage of our framework is the access to various dimensional estimators from first principles.

Enhanced Symmetries—
We now turn to the symmetries supported by the enlarged Hilbert space of QD systems. A direct generalization of the symmetries already present in the fixed-
d
case is given by the prescription (
4
). Suppose that each Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{(d)}
is invariant under a unitary symmetry acting on
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
via an operator
\hat{U}^{(d)}
. Then, the QD system is invariant under a combined symmetry represented by the operator:

\hat{U}=\bigoplus_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\hat{U}^{(d)}.

(18)

This can be considered a form of subsystem symmetry
36
;
37
, where each
\hat{U}^{(d)}
acts in the usual way on the
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
subspace of
\mathcal{H}
and trivially on the remaining states. The system is therefore invariant under the symmetry group

G=\prod_{d=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}G^{(d)},

(19)

where
G^{(d)}
are the symmetry groups acting through
\hat{U}^{(d)}
on the fixed-
d
subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
. Notably, QD systems can also exhibit additional degeneracies not accounted for by a symmetry of the form (
19
). This occurs when the spectra of Hamiltonians in
d+1
and
n+1
dimensions have common eigenvalues for
d\neq n
. This is precisely the case of the QD harmonic oscillator discussed in the previous section. To demonstrate this, we consider the QD harmonic oscillator with
N_{1}=0
and
N_{2}=3
, which corresponds to the purple line in Fig.
2
. The third excited state
E=3/2
is
2
-fold degenerate:

E=\frac{3}{2}:\ket{1;1},\ \ket{3;0,0,0},

(20)

with states of different dimensionalities belonging to the same energy level. This degeneracy cannot be explained by (
19
) and thus suggests the presence of an enhanced symmetry
16
.

To better elucidate this phenomenon, consider a transformation of the form

\displaystyle\ket{1;1}\rightarrow U_{11}\ket{1;1}+U_{12}\ket{3;0,0,0},

(21)

\displaystyle\ket{3;0,0,0}\rightarrow U_{21}\ket{1;1}+U_{22}\ket{3;0,0,0},

where the complex numbers
U_{ij}
form a
2\times 2
unitary matrix
U
. Since the two mixed states are degenerate, the transformation leaves the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
invariant. Note that the symmetry operator represented by
U
mixes states with different dimensionality, in contrast with the simple generalized symmetry (
18
), and therefore corresponds to a new symmetry not present in regular QM systems. Symmetries of this form provide a map between the lower- and higher-dimensional states of the Hilbert space. For this reason, they could potentially be useful for understanding higher-dimensional systems in terms of their lower-dimensional counterparts.

However, the presence and structure of this additional symmetry typically depend on how the system is defined. In ordinary QM, shifting the vacuum energy by a constant is not directly measurable. However, when several such ordinary QM systems are assembled together into a QD system according to the rules (
2
) and (
7
), individual constant shifts in the fixed-
d
Hamiltonians can result in observable effects, such as an altered symmetry structure. The situation is analogous to a case in which an ordinary QM system is constructed from two subsystems
A
and
B
. If the systems are interacting, one cannot offset the vacuum energy of systems
A
or
B
individually without affecting the physics. The system is invariant only under the vacuum shift of the combined system, i.e., when the energy levels of
A
and
B
shift by the same amount.

To demonstrate this phenomenon, we offset the Hamiltonians of the
(d+1)
-dimensional harmonic oscillator (
10
) by
d/2
(such that the ground state of each
\hat{H}^{(d)}
is now at
E=0
), and re-examine the symmetries. Indeed, the symmetry structure changes. By applying the transformation

\displaystyle\ket{d;k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}}

(22)

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{11}\ket{d;k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}}+U_{12}\ket{n;k_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,k_{n}^{(n)}},

\displaystyle\ket{n;k_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,k_{n}^{(n)}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{21}\ket{d;k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}}+U_{22}\ket{n;k_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,k_{n}^{(n)}},

to any two states with different dimensionality but that are overall degenerate, we find that the QD harmonic oscillator supports, in addition to (
19
), an additional
\mathrm{U}(N_{2}-N_{1}+1)
symmetry. In particular, the ground state becomes
(N_{2}-N_{1}+1)
-fold degenerate:

E=0:\ket{N_{1};0,\ldots,0},\ldots,\ket{N_{2};0,\ldots,0}.

(23)

Each state is of different dimensionality, and the new
\mathrm{U}(N_{2}-N_{1}+1)
acts on them via

\displaystyle\ket{d;k_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,k_{d}^{(d)}}

(24)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{n=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}U_{d-N_{1}+1,n-N_{1}+1}\ket{n;k_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,k_{n}^{(n)}}

where a consistent pairing between the degenerate eigenstates is implied.

The symmetries can be understood at the Hamiltonian level as mixing of matrix elements corresponding to different fixed-
d
subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}
. In the limit
N_{1}=N_{2}=d
, when we recover an ordinary QM system, this additional symmetry reduces to a
\mathrm{U}(1)
phase transformation on the eigenstates of
\mathcal{H}
. This analysis clearly shows that the physics of the QD system can be affected by shifts in the fixed-
d
vacuum energies.

Discussion—
In this Letter, we have proposed and explored quantum systems in which the dimensionality was promoted to a quantum operator
\hat{D}
. To illustrate our framework, we examined a QD harmonic oscillator with a variable number of dimensions. We computed the partition function and showed how the effective dimensionality of the system varies with temperature. This provides a natural example of dimensionality dependent on the energy scale. Moreover, we have shown that QD systems can exhibit enhanced symmetries and are sensitive to the vacuum energies of the fixed-
d
subsystems. The formalism introduced in this work relies only on a few basic assumptions, making it sufficiently versatile to explore a wide class of QD systems.

In the future, implementing the framework in gravitational systems could provide a simple way to model dimensional reduction and examine its physical consequences. More broadly, the formalism is of relevance in quantum field theory, where the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
can be constructed from the appropriate QD multi-particle spaces. Building on the arguments discussed previously, we speculate that such QD systems could exhibit improved renormalization properties. Finally, our framework applies to condensed matter systems whose effective dimensionality depends on the energy scale. Exploring fully interacting Hamiltonians within our framework may give rise to entirely new quantum systems with intriguing physical properties.

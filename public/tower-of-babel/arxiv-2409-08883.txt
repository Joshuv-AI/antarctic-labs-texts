Abstract
Let ℋ \mathcal{H} be a graph class and k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} . We say a graph G G admits a k k -identification to ℋ \mathcal{H} if there is a partition 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of some set X ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq V(G) of size at most k k such that after identifying each part in 𝒫 \mathcal{P} to a single vertex, the resulting graph belongs to ℋ \mathcal{H} . The graph parameter 𝗂𝖽 ℋ \mathsf{id}_{\cal H} is defined so that 𝗂𝖽 ℋ ​ ( G ) \mathsf{id}_{\cal H}(G) is the minimum k k such that G G admits a k k -identification to ℋ \mathcal{H} , and the problem of Identification to ℋ \mathcal{H} asks, given a graph G G and k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} , whether 𝗂𝖽 ℋ ​ ( G ) ≤ k \mathsf{id}_{\cal H}(G)\leq k . If we set ℋ \mathcal{H} to be the class ℱ \mathcal{F} of acyclic graphs, we generate the problem Identification to Forest , which we show to be NP -complete. We prove that, when parameterized by the size k k of the identification set, it admits a kernel of size 2 ​ k + 1 2k+1 . For our kernel we reveal a close relation of Identification to Forest with the Vertex Cover problem. We also study the combinatorics of the yes -instances of Identification to ℋ \mathcal{H} , i.e., the class ℋ ( k ) := { G ∣ 𝗂𝖽 ℋ ​ ( G ) ≤ k } \mathcal{H}^{(k)}:=\{G\mid\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{H}}(G)\leq k\} , which we show to be minor-closed for every k k when ℋ \mathcal{H} is minor-closed. Introduction

A considerable part of parameterized algorithms has been dedicated to the study of
graph modification problems
. The general scheme for a graph modification problem consists of some modification operation, accompanied by some a measure on the “cost” of this modification, and a target property. The question is, given a graph
G
and a non-negative integer
k
, whether it is possible to apply to
G
a modification operation with cost at most
k
so that the resulting graph has the target property.
A graph modification problem can be seen as a way to define some notion of
“distance from triviality”
[
13
]
, where the distance is expressed by the measure of the modification operation and the triviality is expressed by the target class. Most graph modification problems are known to be
NP
-complete
[
20
,
31
]
. A well-studied graph modification operation is vertex deletion and the most typical measure is the number of vertices to be deleted.
A general family of problems of this type is
\mathcal{H}
-Deletion

where
\mathcal{H}
is a graph class and where
we look for a vertex set
S\subseteq V(G)
of at most
k
vertices such that
G-S
is a graph in
\mathcal{H}
.
There are many
problems that can be expressed in this way and also
many results
identifying instantiations of
\mathcal{H}
where
\mathcal{H}
-Deletion
, parameterized by
k
, admits a fixed-parameter algorithm (in short
FPT
-algorithm), that is, an algorithm running in time
f(k)\cdot|G|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
, for some function
f
.
Two of the most classical and widely studied problems of this type are
Vertex cover
, where
\mathcal{H}
is be the class of edgeless graphs, and
Feedback Vertex Set
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the class of acyclic graphs.
Alternative measures of the vertex removal modification have been considered in
[
9
]
(see also
[
30
]
).

Another line of research on modification problems is to consider other modification operations. Such operations may include edge removals or additions (see
[
5
]
for an extended survey), edge contractions
[
15
,
14
]
,
or other modification operations such as subgraph complementations
[
12
]
. We should stress that the existing
results on the parameterized complexity of such alternative modification operations
are also many but not as abundant as in in the case of vertex deletion.

Another modification operation called
vertex fusion
was introduced in
[
4
]
.
The vertex fusion of a vertex set
S
in a graph
G
consists in deleting
S
from
G
and adding instead a new vertex
s
adjacent to every vertex of
G-S
that was adjacent to a vertex of
S
.
In other words, the set
S
is fused (or identified, using our terminology) to a single vertex
s
.
This setting has real life applications.
Indeed, consider a communication network represented by a graph
G
.
The goal is that the vertices communicate as fast as possible through the edges of
G
.
A natural problem is hence to ask whether it is possible to perform a small amount of modification to
G
so that its diameter becomes small.
Usually, the modification considered to reduce the diameter of a graph is to add edges.
Instead, this article
[
4
]
proposes the vertex fusion operation.
Such a fusion corresponds to adding a new, more modern and perhaps more expansive, communication network on a small vertex set
S
that would allow for instantaneous (or just much faster) communication among the nodes in
S

The authors prove that, given a graph
G
and
k,d\in\mathbb{N}
, asking for a set of size
S
at most
k
whose fusion gives a graph of diameter (or radius or eccentricity) at most
d
is
NP
-complete, and
W[1]
-hard parameterized by
k
.

A linear kernel.

Our first result is to prove that

admits a linear kernel of size
2k+1

Theorem 1
.

There is a algorithm that, given an instance

of

, outputs in polynomial time an equivalent instance
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})

where
|G^{\prime}|\leq 2k+1

The algorithm of

is based on a structural result
revealing a strong connection between

and the

problem. We use

as a shortcut of the graph parameter
\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{F}}
(recall that
\mathcal{F}
is the class of forests),
and we use

for the minimum size of a vertex cover of
G

the minimum number of vertices whose removal from

yields an edgeless graph. Given a graph

, we denote by

the graph obtained from

after removing all bridges (edges whose removal increases the number of components).
The relation between
\mathsf{vc}
and

is given by the fact
that, for every graph

We also prove that, for every graph

denotes the partition
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{p},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{q}\}\in\mathcal{P}(X\cup Y).

Given a graph
G
, we define
\mathcal{P}(G):=\{\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}(X)\mid X\subseteq V(G)\}
.
Given
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}\}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
, we set
\bigcup\mathcal{X}:=\bigcup_{i\in[p]}X_{i}
, and the order
of
\mathcal{X}
is the size of
\bigcup\mathcal{X}
.

Let
G
be a graph and
X\subseteq V(G)
.
The
identification of
X
in
G
, denoted by
G{/\!\!/}X
, is the result of the operation that transforms
G
into a graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from
G
by deleting
X
and adding instead a new vertex
x
adjacent to every vertex in
N_{G}(X)
.
The vertex
x
is called the
heir
of
X
.
Note that, if
X=\{u,v\}
with
uv\in E(G)
, then this corresponds to the contraction of
uv
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},...,X_{p}\}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
.
The
identification of
\mathcal{P}
in
G
is the graph
G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{P}:=G{/\!\!/}X_{1}{/\!\!/}X_{2}{/\!\!/}...{/\!\!/}X_{p}
.
Note that the ordering
of the members of the partition does not matter in this definition.

Identification to
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a graph class and
G
be a graph.
We say that a partition
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
is an
id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of
G
if
G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{H}
.
A
minimum
id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of
G
is an id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of
G
of minimum order.
As explained in the introduction,
the problem of
Identification to
\mathcal{H}
asks,
given a graph
G
and a non-negative integer
k
,
whether
G
admits an id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of order at most
k
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
the set of graphs that admit an id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of order
k
.

2.3
Minor-closedness

As said in the introduction, identifications preserve minor-closedness.

Lemma 1
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is a minor-closed graph class, then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the class
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
is minor-closed.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
and
H
be a minor of
G
. Let us show that
H\in\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
.
Let
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{p}\}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
be an id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of
G
.
Given that
H
is a minor of
G
, there is a model
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{v}\mid v\in V(H)\}
of
H
in
G
.
For
i\in[p]
, let
Y_{i}=\{v\in V(H)\mid S_{v}\cap X_{i}\neq\emptyset\}
.
Let
Y:=\bigcup_{i\in[p]}Y_{i}
.
Note that
|Y|\leq|\bigcup\mathcal{X}|\leq k
. We want to show that the
partition
\mathcal{Z}
of
Y
induced by the
Y_{i}
s is an id-
\mathcal{H}
partition of
H
.
However, it is possible that
Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}\neq\emptyset
for distinct
i,j\in[p]
, so
(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{p})
is not a partition of
Y
.
The correct partition is
\mathcal{Z}=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{q})\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
defined by merging the
Y_{i}
’s that intersect.
In other words, for each
i\in[p]
, there is
j\in[q]
such that
Y_{i}\subseteq Z_{j}
, and if
Z_{j}\setminus Z_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
i^{\prime}\in[p]
such that
Y_{i^{\prime}}\subseteq Z_{j}
and
Y_{i}\cap Y_{i^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
.
Then
\{S_{v}\mid v\in V(H)\setminus Y\}\cup\bigcup_{j\in[q]}\{\bigcup_{v\in Z_{j}}S_{v}\setminus X\cup\bigcup_{i\in[p],Y_{i}\subseteq Z_{j}}\{x_{i}\}\}
is a model of
H{/\!\!/}\mathcal{Z}
in
G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}
, where
x_{i}
is the heir of
X_{i}
.
Given that
\mathcal{H}
is minor-closed,
H{/\!\!/}\mathcal{Z}\in\mathcal{H}
, and therefore
H\in\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
.
∎

3
Hardness result and kernel

In this section we exploit the relation between
Identification to Forest
and
Vertex Cover
to present a hardness result and a linear kernel for
Identification to Forest
, building on the corresponding results for
Vertex Cover
.

3.1
Dealing with bridges

We present a series of observations concerning
k
-identifications.

Observation 1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hereditary graph class
and
G
be a graph.
Then, for every
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
, if
G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{H}
, then for each
H\in{\sf cc}(G)
,
H{/\!\!/}(\mathcal{X}\cap V(H))\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\sf cc}(G)
.
Given that
H{/\!\!/}(\mathcal{X}\cap V(H))=G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}-(V(G)\setminus V(H))
and that
\mathcal{H}
is hereditary, we conclude that
H{/\!\!/}(\mathcal{X}\cap V(H))\in\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Observation 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a graph class that is closed under disjoint union
and
G
be a graph.
Then, for each
H\in{\sf cc}(G)
and for each
\mathcal{X}_{H}\in\mathcal{P}(H)
, if
H{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}_{H}\in\mathcal{H}
, then
G{/\!\!/}\bigcup_{H\in{\sf cc}(G)}\mathcal{X}_{H}{\in\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

Given that
\mathcal{H}
is closed under disjoint union and

G{/\!\!/}\bigcup_{H\in{\sf cc}(G)}=\bigcup_{H\in{\sf cc}(G)}H{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}_{H}
, we conclude that
G{/\!\!/}\bigcup_{H\in{\sf cc}(G)}\mathcal{X}_{H}{\in\mathcal{H}}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
G^{\sf b}
be the graph obtained from
G
after removing all bridges.
Then
\mathsf{idf}(G)=\mathsf{idf}(G^{\sf b})
.

Proof.

Let
k:=\mathsf{idf}(G)
.
By definition,
G\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
.
By
Lemma 1
,
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
is minor-closed, so
G-e\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
for any edge
e
of
G
.
Therefore,
\mathsf{idf}(G-e)\leq\mathsf{idf}(G)
.

By
Observation 1
and
Observation 2
, we may assume without loss of generality that
G
is connected.
Let
e
be a bridge of
G
.
Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be the two connected components of
G-e
.
For
i\in[2]
, let
\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(X_{i})
be a minimum id-
\mathcal{F}
partition of
G_{i}
.
By
Observation 2
,
(G-e){/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{F}
where
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{1}\cup\mathcal{X}_{2}
.
Suppose toward a contradiction that
G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}
contains a cycle
C
.
Then, given that
(G-e){/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}
is acyclic, it implies that
e
is an edge of
C
.
Given that no part of
\mathcal{X}
contains vertices of both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, it implies that
e
is already an edge of a cycle. This contradicts the fact that
e
is a bridge. The lemma follows by repeatedly applying this argument as long as there is a bridge.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a bridgeless graph.
Then
\mathsf{idf}(G)=\mathsf{vc}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a vertex cover of
G
.
Then
G{/\!\!/}X
is a star (if
G
is edgeless, a vertex is considered as a star).
Hence,
\{X\}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
is an id-
\mathcal{F}
partition of
G
.
So
\mathsf{idf}(G)\leq\mathsf{vc}(G)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an id-
\mathcal{F}
partition of
G
.
Let
F:=G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{F}
.
Let us color red the vertices of
F
that are heirs of a part of
\mathcal{X}
, and blue the other vertices of
F
.
Given that
G
is bridgeless,
F
contains no edge whose endpoints are both blue.
Hence, the red vertices form a vertex cover of
F
.
Therefore,
X
is a vertex cover of
G
.
So
\mathsf{vc}(G)\leq\mathsf{idf}(G)
.
∎

Then we get the main result of this section as a direct corollary of
Lemma 2
and
Lemma 3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be a graph and
G^{\sf b}
be the graph obtained from
G
after removing all bridges.
Then
\mathsf{idf}(G)=\mathsf{vc}(G^{\sf b})
.

3.2
NP
-completeness

Before proving the
NP
-completeness of
Identification to Forest
, we first need the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
G
be a graph.
Then there is a bridgeless graph
G^{\prime}
with
|V(G)|+1
vertices such that
G\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
if and only if
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
.
Moreover,
G^{\prime}
can be constructed in linear time.

Proof.

Let us construct a bridgeless graph
G^{\prime}
from
G
.
Let
I
be the set of isolated vertices of
G
.
We add a new vertex
v
to
G
and add an edge between
v
and every vertex of
G-I
. The constructed graph
G^{\prime}
is clearly bridgeless.

Let us check that
G\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
if and only if
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
.
We assume that
G
has at least one edge, otherwise the claim is trivially true.
Suppose the
G\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
and let
X
be a vertex cover of
G
of size at most
k
.
Then
X\cup\{v\}
is a vertex cover of
G
so
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
.
Suppose now that
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
.
Let
Y
be a vertex cover of
G^{\prime}
of size at most
k+1
.
If
v\in Y
, then
Y\setminus\{v\}
is a vertex cover of
G
of size at most
k
.
Otherwise
v\notin Y
.
It implies that
V(G)\setminus I\subseteq Y
.
But then, for any vertex
x
of
G-I
,
N_{G-I}(x)\subseteq Y
.
Therefore,
Y\setminus\{x\}
is a vertex cover of
G
of size at most
k
.
Hence,
G\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
.

Given that
G^{\prime}
can be constructed in linear time, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Identification to Forest
is
NP
-complete.

Proof.

Given a graph
G
and a partition
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}(G)
, checking that
G{/\!\!/}\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{F}
can obviously be done in linear time.
We reduce from
Vertex Cover
that is
NP
-hard
[
16
]
.
Let
G
be a graph.
Let
b
be the number of bridges in
G
.
If
b\geq 1
, by
Lemma 5
, there is a graph
G^{\prime}
with
|V(G)|+1
vertices such that
G\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
if and only if
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{k^{\prime}}
where
k^{\prime}:=k+1
.
If
b=0
, we set
G^{\prime}:=G
and
k^{\prime}:=k
.
Since
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{k^{\prime}}
is bridgeless, by
Lemma 3
,
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}
.
Since
G^{\prime}
can be constructed in linear time and that
G\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
if and only if
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k^{\prime})}
, the result follows.
∎

3.3
A kernel for
Identification to Forest

The following kernelization result is known for
Vertex Cover
.

Proposition 1

(​​
[
21
,
22
]
)
.

Given an instance
(G,k)
of
Vertex Cover
, one can compute in polynomial time an equivalent instance
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
such that
|V(G^{\prime})|\leq 2k^{\prime}\leq 2k
.

Hence, we can derive the following kernalization result for
Identification to Forest
.

Lemma 7
.

Given an instance
(G,k)
of
Identification to Forest
, one can compute in polynomial time an equivalent instance
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
such that
|V(G^{\prime})|\leq 2k+1
and
k^{\prime}\leq k+1
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
be obtained from
G
after removing bridges.
By
Lemma 4
,
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Identification to Forest
if and only if
(G_{1},k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Vertex Cover
.
By
Proposition 1
, there is
(G_{2},k_{2})
with
|V(G_{2})|\leq 2k_{2}\leq 2k
such that
(G_{1},k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Vertex Cover
if and only if
(G_{2},k_{2})
is a
yes
-instance of
Vertex Cover
.
By
Lemma 5
, there is
(G_{3},k_{3})
such that
G_{3}
is a bridgeless graph with
|V(G_{3})|\leq|V(G_{2})|+1
and
k_{3}\leq k_{2}+1
such that
(G_{2},k_{2})
is a
yes
-instance of
Vertex Cover
if and only if
(G_{3},k_{3})
is a
yes
-instance of
Vertex Cover
.
Finally, by
Lemma 4
, given that
G_{3}
is bridgeless, by
Lemma 3
,
(G_{3},k_{3})
is a
yes
-instance of
Vertex Cover
if and only if it is a
yes
-instance of
Identification to Forest
.
Hence the result.
∎

4
Obstructions

Given that
Vertex Cover
and
Identification to Forest
are strongly related, it is reasonable to suspect that this holds for their obstructions as well.
Already, as a direct corollary of
Lemma 4
, we have the two following results.

Observation 3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
F\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
.
Then
F
is bridgeless.

Lemma 8
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
The bridgeless obstructions of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
are obstructions of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{V}_{k})
be bridgeless.
By
Lemma 3
,
H\notin\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
.
Thus, there is a minor
H^{\prime}
of
H
such that
H^{\prime}\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
.
By
Observation 3
,
H^{\prime}
is bridgeless.
Therefore, by
Lemma 3
,
H^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{V}_{k}
.
Given that
H^{\prime}
is a minor of
H
and that
H\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{V}_{k})
, we conclude that
H=H^{\prime}
.
Therefore,
H\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
.
∎

We are actually going to prove in
Subsection 4.1
that the only bridges that may occur in an obstruction of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
are isolated edges.
Then, in
Subsection 4.2
, we will prove that any obstruction of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
can be obtained from an obstruction of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
by adding edges.
See
Figure 1
for a comparison of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
for
k\leq 3
, where the obstructions of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
are taken from
[
3
]
.

Figure 1:
The obstructions of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
(first and second columns) and
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
(second and third columns) for
k\leq 3
.
Each graph in
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
is either 1) also a graph in
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{V}^{(k)})
(second column), or 2) can be obtained from a graph in

4.1
Bridges in the obstructions of
\mathcal{V}_{k}

In this subsection, we prove the following.

Lemma 9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
G\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{V}_{k})
be a graph.
Then the connected components of
G
are 2-connected. Therefore, the bridges of
G
are isolated edges.

Actually, we prove a more general version of
Lemma 9
applying on any graph class
\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
defined as follows.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
hereditary
graph class (i.e., closed under vertex deletion) that is also closed under 1-clique-sums.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
be the set of
graphs
G
such that there exists a set
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|\leq k
and
G-X\in\mathcal{H}
.
In this setting
\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{E}^{\langle k\rangle}
,
where
\mathcal{E}
is class of edgeless graphs.

We need the following easy lemma.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hereditary class,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
H\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle})
. Then, for any
v\in V(H)
, there is a set
S\subseteq V(H)
of size
k+1
such that
v\in S
and
H-S\in\mathcal{H}
.
In particular,
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\langle k+1\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle})
and
v\in V(H)
.
By definition of an obstruction,
H\notin\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
and
H-\{v\}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
.
So there is a vertex set
S^{\prime}
of size at most
k
in
H-\{v\}
such that
H-\{v\}-S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Let
S:=S^{\prime}\cup\{v\}
.
Then
H-S\in\mathcal{H}
so
H\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k+1\rangle}
.
Given that
H\notin\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
, we have
|S|>k
, and therefore,
|S|=k+1
.
∎

Here is the main result of the subsection.

Lemma 11
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Every connected component of a graph in
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle})
is 2-connected.

Proof.

Suppose toward a contradiction that
G\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle})
has a connected component that is not 2-connected.
Then there is a cut vertex
v
in
G
.
Let
G_{1}
be a connected component of
G-\{v\}
such that
v\in N_{G}(V(G_{1}))
and let
G_{2}=G-V(G_{1})-\{v\}
.
For
i\in[2]
, let
k_{i}
be the minimum
k
such that
G_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
.
Hence,
G_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}-1\rangle}
.

Claim 1
.

k=k_{1}+k_{2}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma 10
,
G\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k+1\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
.
For
i\in[2]
, let
S_{i}\subseteq V(G_{i})
of size at most
k_{i}
be such that
G_{i}-S_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then
S:=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup\{v\}
is such that
G-S\in\mathcal{H}
, so
k+1\leq k_{1}+k_{2}+1
.

By
Lemma 10
, there is a set
S\subseteq V(G)
of size
k+1
such that
v\in S
and
G-S\in\mathcal{H}
.
Given that
\mathcal{H}
is hereditary,
G_{i}-(S\cap V(G_{i}))\in\mathcal{H}
.
Moreover,
G_{i}\notin\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}-1\rangle}
for
i\in[2]
, so we conclude that
|S\cap V(G_{i})|\geq k_{i}
.
Hence,
k+1=|S|=|\{v\}\cup(S\cap V(G_{1}))\cup(S\cap V(G_{2}))|\geq k_{1}+k_{2}+1.

∎

For
i\in[2]
, let
\bar{G_{i}}:=G[V(G_{i})\cup\{v\}]
.
Since
G_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}-1\rangle}
and we only add the vertex
v
,

\bar{G}_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}+1\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}-1\rangle}
.

Claim 2
.

There is
i\in[2]
such that
\bar{G}_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}+1\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}\rangle}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\bar{G_{i}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{i}\rangle}
for
i\in[2]
.
Let
S_{i}\subseteq V(\bar{G}_{i})
of size
k_{i}
be such that
\bar{G}_{i}-S_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then
S:=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
has size at most
k_{1}+k_{2}<k+1
.
Moreover, given that
\mathcal{H}
is closed under 1-clique-sums, we have
G-S\in\mathcal{H}
.
By
Claim 1
, it follows that
G\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
, a contradiction.
∎

By
Claim 2
, without loss of generality, we assume that
\bar{G}_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{1}+1\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{1}\rangle}
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from the disjoint union of
\bar{G}_{1}
and
G_{2}
.
Given that
\mathcal{H}
is closed under disjoint union and by
Claim 1
,
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{1}+1+k_{2}\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k_{1}+k_{2}\rangle}=\mathcal{H}^{\langle k+1\rangle}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
.

G^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
G
so
this contradicts the minimality of
G
as an obstruction of
\mathcal{H}^{\langle k\rangle}
.
∎

4.2
Constructing the obstructions of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
from the obstructions of
\mathcal{V}_{k}

What
Lemma 8
and
Lemma 11
tell us is that the
difference (as sets) between
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{V}_{k})
and
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
is caused by isolated edges.
Essentially, to go from an obstruction
H
of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
with isolated edges to an obstruction
H^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
, we will have to add vertices and edges minimally to get a bridgeless graph.
In this section, we prove that we actually just need to add edges.

Let
{\sf Obs}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{\sf obs}(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})
.
We have the following easy observation.

Observation 4
.

Let
G\in{\sf Obs}
and
k:=\mathsf{idf}(G)-1
. Then

Discussion and open problems

In this paper we initiated the study of graph modification problems where the modification operation is vertex identification.
We defined the problem
Identification to
\mathcal{H}
and studied the
case where the target class

is the class of forest, denoted by
\mathcal{F}

Relation with

\mathcal{H}

An important feature of

\mathcal{H}
is that
it behaves similarly to the problem
Deletion to
\mathcal{H}
,
in the sense that both problems are

when
\mathcal{H}
is a minor-closed graph class. This follows from

and the algorithmic consequence of the Robertson and Seymour’s theorem
[
28
,
27
,
25
,
17
,

]
.
It is easy to observe that the problem

Contraction to
\mathcal{H}
(that is, asking whether
k
edge contractions yield property
\mathcal{H}
) does not
have this property. To see this, let
\mathcal{P}
be the class of planar graphs and let
K_{3,4}^{+}

(resp.
K_{2,3}^{+}
)
be the graph obtained from
K_{3,4}
(resp.
K_{2,3}
) by adding an edge
e
between two vertices of degree three (resp. two).

yields a planar (resp. acyclic) graph, so

(resp.

-instance of

(

). However,

(resp.

-instance of the corresponding problem.

Let us define the parameter
{\sf ec}_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\to\mathbb{N}

corresponding to the problem

\mathcal{H}

is the minimum number of edge contractions that can transform

We use

as a shortcut for

and we next observe that

and

Lemma 16
.

For every graph

\mathsf{idf}(G)=\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{ecf}(G))
and that
\mathsf{ecf}(G)=\mathcal{O}((\mathsf{idf}(G))^{3})

Proof.

Using the fact that edge contractions
are also edge identifications, it easily follows that, for every graph
G

\mathsf{idf}(G)\leq 2\cdot\mathsf{ecf}(G)
.

Assume now that
\mathsf{idf}(G)\leq k
and we claim that

\mathsf{ecf}(G)=\mathcal{O}(k^{3})
.
To prove this claim we first observe that, because
\mathsf{idf}(k\cdot K_{3})=\Omega(k)
and
\mathsf{idf}(k*K_{3})=\Omega(k)
(see

), it follows that
the number of 2-connected components of

that are not bridges
is bounded by some linear function of
k
.
Let
B
be a 2-connected component of
G
.
As
B
is a minor of
G
, it has an id-
\mathcal{F}
partition
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{p}\}
of order
\leq k
.
For
i\in[p]
, let
x_{1}^{i},\ldots,x_{p_{i}}^{i}
be an ordering of the
vertices of

and let
F_{i}=\{\{x_{1}^{i},x_{2}^{i}\},\{x_{2}^{i},x_{3}^{i}\},\ldots,\{x_{p_{i}-1}^{i},x_{p_{i}}^{i}\}\}

Let also
F=F_{1}\cup\cdots\cup F_{p}
.
Clearly, the 2-element sets in
F
are not necessarily
edges of
B
. For each

we define a set of edges

as follows. As
B
is 2-connected,
x
and

belong to a cycle of

. As

(see

and

The edges of this path are the edges in

.
We now set

and observe that

contracting the edges of

Therefore, applying these contractions to every non-bridge connected component of

Identification minors.

We say that a graph
H
is
an

of a graph

As the minor relation between two graphs also implies their identification minor relation, Robertson and Seymour’s theorem
[

implies that graphs are well-quasi-ordered by the identification minor relation.
It is also easy to observe that, for every graph

the graphs in the set

of minor-minimal graphs containing

H

|H|
.
Therefore, checking whether

H
is an identification minor of
G
can be done in time
\mathcal{O}_{|H|}(|G|^{1+\varepsilon})
, according to the recent results in
[
18
]
.

It is a natural question to ask whether graphs are well-quasi-ordered with respect to the vertex identification operation alone. The answer turns out to be negative. Indeed, there is an infinite antichain
(H_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

is the graph formed from a cycle on

by adding three vertices
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
and an edge between each pair
(a_{i},p_{j})
such that
j
is equal to
i
modulo three.
See

Optimal control of treatment in a free boundary problem modeling multilayered tumor growth
Xinyue Evelyn Zhao, Yixiang Wu, Rachel Leander, Wandi Ding, Suzanne Lenhart

Abstract
. We study the optimal control problem of a free boundary PDE model describing the growth of multilayered tumor tissue in vitro. We seek the optimal amount of tumor growth inhibitor that simultaneously minimizes the thickness of the tumor tissue and mitigates side effects. The existence of an optimal control is established, and the uniqueness and characterization of the optimal control are investigated. Numerical simulations are presented for some scenarios, including the steady-state and parabolic cases. Key Words: Optimal control, reaction-diffusion, free boundary, tumor growth AMS subject classifications: 49K20, 35K20, 35R35, 35Q92, 35Q93

2.
A special case: steady-state solution

In the consideration of the steady-state case, the control
m(t)=m
is a constant and the solution to the state system
(\sigma,\rho)
is independent of the time variable
t
. Consequently, the state system
(
1.17
)
–
(
1.19
)
is reduced to

(2.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma_{yy}=(1+m)\sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt0<y<\rho,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma=1

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pty=\rho,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma_{y}=0

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pty=0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho}\mu(\sigma-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}y=0.

The first three equations of the system can be explicitly solved to obtain

(2.5)

\sigma(y)=\frac{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m}y)}{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)},

where, by
(
2.4
)
, the thickness of the tumor tissue
\rho
is uniquely determined by

(2.6)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m}\rho}\tanh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)=\widetilde{\sigma}.

Correspondingly, the objective functional also simplifies, eliminating the dependency on the time variable,

(2.7)

J(m)=\rho+Bm^{2}.

The steady-state optimal control problem thus becomes:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min\limits_{m\in[0,M]}\;J(m)=\rho+Bm^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{subject to }\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)}{\sqrt{1+m}\rho}=\widetilde{\sigma}.

To analyze this optimization problem, we introduce
g(x)=\frac{\tanh(x)}{x}
. Note that
g(x)
is monotonically decreasing for
x\geq 0
. It follows from
(
2.6
)
that, given
\widetilde{\sigma}>0
,

\sqrt{1+m}\rho=g^{-1}(\widetilde{\sigma}).

Thus,

(2.8)

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial m}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{g^{-1}(\widetilde{\sigma})}{(1+m)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

To find the critical point of the objective functional
J(m)
, we set
J^{\prime}(m)=\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial m}+2Bm=0
. In conjunction with
(
2.8
)
, this analysis yields the equation of the optimal control:

(2.9)

m_{*}(1+m_{*})^{\frac{3}{2}}=\frac{g^{-1}(\widetilde{\sigma})}{4B}.

Note that the left-hand side function
h(x)=x(1+x)^{\frac{3}{2}}
is monotonically increasing for
x\geq 0
, thus the equation
(
2.9
)
admits a unique solution
m_{*}
.
Recalling the admissible set
U_{M}
, the optimal control is defined as

(2.10)

m^{*}=\min\{m_{*},M\}.

Examination of
(
2.9
)
reveals that an increase in
B
corresponds to a decrease in
m_{*}
, which aligns intuitively with the premise that increasing the severity of side effects may justify the reduction or absence of medication. Moreover, a higher value of
\widetilde{\sigma}
leads to a lower optimal control
m_{*}
. This relationship is explained by the role of
\widetilde{\sigma}
as a critical concentration threshold of nutrients necessary for tumor sustainability and growth. When
\widetilde{\sigma}
is larger, the tumor requires a higher concentration to sustain itself; consequently, a minimal control effort is sufficient to reduce the nutrient concentration below this critical threshold, thereby inhibiting tumor growth.

Existence and uniqueness of a positive state solution

We shall use the contraction mapping principle to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution. Take

, we have, for

,

(3.24)

\begin{split}\big(\rho_{1}(t)-\rho_{2}(t)\big)^{\prime}&=\rho_{1}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{1}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi-\rho_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{2}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\big(\rho_{1}(t)-\rho_{2}(t)\big)\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{1}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\rho_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{1}-u_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

Multiplying
(
3.24
)
with
\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}
leads to

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\big(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\big)^{2}&=\big(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\big)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{1}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\rho_{2}\big(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\big)\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{1}-u_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\leq\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})\big(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\big)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\big(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\big)^{2}+\rho_{2}^{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u_{1}-u_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{2}\bigg)\\
&\leq\frac{C_{1}}{2}|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|^{2}+\frac{C_{2}}{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))},\end{split}

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
that are independent of
T
.
Utilizing the Gronwall inequality and noting that
(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(0)=0
, we obtain

\big|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\big|\leq\sqrt{C_{2}e^{C_{1}}}T^{1/2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for $0\leq t\leq T<1$}.

Combined with
(
3.23
)
and the initial condition
u_{1}(\xi,0)-u_{2}(\xi,0)=0
, it implies

\begin{split}\big|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\big|&\leq\sqrt{C_{2}e^{C_{1}}}T^{1/2}T^{(1+\alpha)/2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\leq CT\|s_{1}-s_{2}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for $0\leq t\leq T<1$}.\end{split}

Similarly, we substitute this estimate back into
(
3.24
)
to obtain

\begin{split}\big|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})^{\prime}(t)\big|&\leq C\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+C\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\\
&\leq CT\|s_{1}-s_{2}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+CT^{(1+\alpha)/2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\leq CT^{1/2}\|s_{1}-s_{2}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for $0\leq t\leq T<1$}.\end{split}

When
T
is sufficiently small, we have

\|\mathcal{M}_{1}s_{1}-\mathcal{M}_{1}s_{2}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}=\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|s_{1}-s_{2}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]},

which indicates that
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is a contraction. By contraction mapping principle, the system admits a unique solution for small
T
.

Finally, we prove the solution can be extended to all
T>0
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists
0<\widetilde{T}<\infty
such that
[0,\widetilde{T})
is the maximum time interval for the existence of the solution. Notice that
(
3.13
)
does not depend on
\widetilde{T}
. Using
(
3.19
)
and
(
3.20
)
, we obtain, for all
t\in[0,\widetilde{T})
,

(3.25)

\rho_{0}e^{-\mu\widetilde{\sigma}\widetilde{T}}\leq\rho(t)\leq\rho_{0}e^{\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})\widetilde{T}},

(3.26)

-\mu\widetilde{\sigma}\rho_{0}e^{\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})\widetilde{T}}\leq\rho^{\prime}(t)\leq\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})\rho_{0}e^{\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})\widetilde{T}}.

Taking
\widetilde{T}-\varepsilon
(where
0<\varepsilon<\widetilde{T}
is arbitrary) as a new initial time, we can extend the solution to
0\leq t\leq\widetilde{T}-\varepsilon+\delta(\widetilde{T})
for some small
\delta(\widetilde{T})>0
as in the previous proof.
Using
(
3.25
)
and
(
3.26
)
, we see that
\delta(\widetilde{T})
is independent of
\varepsilon
. If we take
\varepsilon<\delta(\widetilde{T})
, then we have

\widetilde{T}-\varepsilon+\delta(\widetilde{T})>\widetilde{T},

which contradicts the assumption that
[0,\widetilde{T})
is the maximum interval. Therefore, the solution exists for all
T>0
.

∎

4.
Existence of an optimal control

This section is devoted to the existence of a solution for the minimization problem
(
1.23
)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. Then, for any
T>0
, there exists an optimal control in
U_{M}
that minimizes the objective functional
J(m)
subject to
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
, i.e., there is
m^{*}\in U_{M}
such that
J(m^{*})=\inf\{J(m)|m\in U_{M}\}
.

Proof.

For a fixed
T>0
, from
(
3.8
)
and
(
3.9
)
and the definition of
U_{M}
in
(
1.22
)
, we have

0\leq J(m)\leq\big(\rho_{0}e^{\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})}T+BM^{2}\big)T.

So,
J(m)
is uniformly bounded for any
m\in U_{M}
. Hence, there exists a minimizing sequence
\{m_{n}\}\subset U_{M}
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(m_{n})=\inf\{J(m)\,|\,m\in U_{M}\}.

Since
|m_{n}|\leq M
for all
n
, there exists a subsequence

m^{n}\rightharpoonup m^{*}\quad\text{ weakly in $L^{2}(0,T)$}.

By Theorem
3.1
, we define
(u^{n},\rho^{n})=(u(m^{n}),\rho(m^{n}))
be the unique solution to the system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
corresponding to
m^{n}
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\rho^{n}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C,

where the constants
C
are independent of
n
. Since the sequence
\{u^{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))
, there exists
u^{*}\in W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))
and a subsequence of
\{u^{n}\}
that converges weakly to
u^{*}
in
W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))
. Since the embedding
W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))\hookrightarrow C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])
is compact, we have the following convergence on subsequences:

\displaystyle u^{n}\rightarrow u^{*}\text{ in $C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}([0,1]\times[0,T])$},\quad u^{n}_{\xi\xi}\rightharpoonup u^{*}_{\xi\xi},u^{n}_{t}\rightharpoonup u^{*}_{t}\text{ weakly in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$}.

For the sequence
\{\rho^{n}\}
, by
(
4.2
)
, and the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem, we obtain on a subsequence

\rho^{n}\rightarrow\rho^{*}\text{ uniformly in $C[0,T]$}.

Furthermore, since
\|(\rho^{n})^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq\|\rho^{n}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C
for all
n
, we also have

\quad(\rho^{n})^{\prime}\rightharpoonup(\rho^{*})^{\prime}\text{ weakly in $L^{2}(0,T)$}.

These convergences and uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds imply that

\displaystyle\frac{(\rho^{n})^{\prime}}{\rho^{n}}\xi u^{n}_{\xi}\rightharpoonup\frac{(\rho^{*})^{\prime}}{\rho^{*}}\xi u^{*}_{\xi},\;\frac{1}{(\rho^{n})^{2}}u^{n}_{\xi\xi}\rightharpoonup\frac{1}{(\rho^{*})^{2}}u^{*}_{\xi\xi},\;m^{n}u^{n}\rightharpoonup m^{*}u^{*}\quad\text{ weakly in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$}.

Passing to the limit in the system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
, we see that
(u^{*},\rho^{*})
is the unique solution associated with
m^{*}
.

Finally, we need to verify that
m^{*}
is an optimal control, i.e.,
J(m^{*})\leq\inf\{J(m)|m\in U_{M}\}
. By the lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
norm with respect to weak convergence, we have

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf\int_{0}^{T}\big(m^{n}(t)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{T}\big(m^{*}(t)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Thus,

\begin{split}\inf\{J(m)|m\in U_{M}\}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(m_{n})&=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\rho^{n}(t)+B\big(m^{n}(t)\big)^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\rho^{*}(t)+B\big(m^{*}(t)\big)^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t=J(m^{*}),\end{split}

which verifies that
m^{*}
is an optimal control that minimizes the objective functional
J(m)
.
∎

5.
Derivation of the optimality system

In this section, we characterize some properties of the optimal control by deriving the optimality system and necessary conditions for a minimizer. The optimality system consists of the state system, the adjoint system, and the characterization of the optimal control. Following the fundamental work of J. L. Lions
[
40
]
, we first differentiate the solution
(u,\rho)
to the state system with respect to the control
m
, which is guaranteed by the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, let
m\in U_{M}
and
(u,\rho)=(u^{m},\rho^{m})
be the corresponding solution to the state system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
. Let
m_{\varepsilon}=m+\varepsilon h
for any
m\in U_{M}
and
h\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
such that
m+\varepsilon h\in U_{M}
. For
T
sufficiently small, the mapping
m\rightarrow(u^{m},\rho^{m})
is differentiable in the following sense: there exists
(v,\eta)\in W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))\times C^{1}[0,T]
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow v\text{ in $C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}([0,1]\times[0,T])$},\quad v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi\xi}\rightharpoonup v_{\xi\xi},v^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rightharpoonup v_{t}\text{ weakly in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$},

\eta^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\eta\text{ uniformly in $C[0,T]$ and $(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\rightharpoonup\eta^{\prime}$ weakly in $L^{2}(0,T)$},

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
where
v^{\varepsilon}=\frac{u^{m+\varepsilon h}-u^{m}}{\varepsilon}
and
\eta^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho^{m+\varepsilon h}-\rho^{m}}{\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, the sensitivity functions
(v,\eta)
satisfy:

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi v_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}v_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)v=\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta}{\rho^{2}}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}-uh\hskip 10.00002pt&0<\xi<1,\;0<t<T,\\
&v=0\hskip 10.00002pt&\xi=1,\;0<t<T,\\
&v_{\xi}=0\hskip 10.00002pt&\xi=0,\;0<t<T,\\
&v=0\hskip 10.00002pt&0<\xi<1,\;t=0,\\
&\eta^{\prime}=\eta\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\hskip 10.00002pt&0<t<T,\\
&\eta=0\hskip 10.00002pt&t=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Denote
(u^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon})=(u^{m+\varepsilon h},\rho^{m+\varepsilon h})
, which satisfies

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}-\frac{1}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}u^{\varepsilon}_{\xi\xi}=-(1+m+\varepsilon h)u^{\varepsilon}\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;0<t<T,\\
&u^{\varepsilon}=1\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=1,\;0<t<T,\\
&u^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}=0\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=0,\;0<t<T,\\
&u^{\varepsilon}=u_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;t=0,\\
&(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}=\rho^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u^{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\hskip 20.00003pt&0<t<T,\\
&\rho^{\varepsilon}=\rho_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt&t=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
(u,\rho)=(u^{m},\rho^{m})
, where

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}u_{\xi\xi}=-(1+m)u\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;0<t<T,\\
&u=1\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=1,\;0<t<T,\\
&u_{\xi}=0\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=0,\;0<t<T,\\
&u=u_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;t=0,\\
&\rho^{\prime}=\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\hskip 20.00003pt&0<t<T,\\
&\rho=\rho_{0}\hskip 20.00003pt&t=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that it follows from Theorem
3.1
that there is a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\rho\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0<u^{\varepsilon},u<1,\hskip 20.00003pt0<\rho_{0}e^{-\mu\widetilde{\sigma}T}\leq\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\leq\rho_{0}e^{\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})T}.

Reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
and by the uniqueness of the solution
(u,\rho)=(u^{m},\rho^{m})
, we have, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u\text{ in $C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}([0,1]\times[0,T])$},\quad u^{\varepsilon}_{\xi\xi}\rightharpoonup u_{\xi\xi},u^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rightharpoonup u_{t}\text{ weakly in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$},

(5.8)

\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\rho\text{ uniformly in $C[0,T]$},\quad(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\rightharpoonup\rho^{\prime}\text{ weakly in $L^{2}(0,T)$}.

Recall that

v^{\varepsilon}=\frac{u^{m+\varepsilon h}-u^{m}}{\varepsilon}=\frac{u^{\varepsilon}-u}{\varepsilon},\hskip 20.00003pt\eta^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho^{m+\varepsilon h}-\rho^{m}}{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho}{\varepsilon},

and it is noteworthy that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\Big)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\rho-\rho^{\prime}\rho^{\varepsilon}}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}=\Big(\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\prime}\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho}{\varepsilon}\Big)=\frac{(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\Big)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\rho^{2}-(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}=-\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}+\rho}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho}{\varepsilon}\Big)=-\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon}+\rho)\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}},

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{0}^{1}\mu\Big(\frac{u^{\varepsilon}-u}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{0}^{1}\mu v^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Using
(
5.9
)
–
(
5.11
)
, subtracting
(
5.3
)
from
(
5.2
)
and dividing by
\varepsilon
, we obtain the following system:

(5.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\begin{aligned} &v^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}-\frac{1}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)v^{\varepsilon}=\\
&\hskip 60.00009pt\frac{(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{(\rho+\rho^{\varepsilon})\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}u_{\xi\xi}-u^{\varepsilon}h\end{aligned}\hskip 10.00002pt&0<\xi<1,\;0<t<T,\\
&v^{\varepsilon}=0\hskip 10.00002pt&\xi=1,\;0<t<T,\\
&v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}=0\hskip 10.00002pt&\xi=0,\;0<t<T,\\
&v^{\varepsilon}=0\hskip 10.00002pt&0<\xi<1,\;t=0,\\
&(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}=\eta^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u^{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\hskip 10.00002pt&0<t<T,\\
&\eta^{\varepsilon}=0\hskip 10.00002pt&t=0.\end{aligned}\right.

By
(
5.4
)
, the coefficients on the left-hand side of the PDE for

are bounded in

. Therefore, by artificially extending the time interval to

as mentioned in the proof of Theorem

, applying the standard

C_{3}(T)
and

(5.13)

\begin{split}\|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}&\leq C\|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\\
&\leq C(T)\Big\|\frac{(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u_{\xi}-\frac{(\rho+\rho^{\varepsilon})\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}u_{\xi\xi}-u^{\varepsilon}h\Big\|_{L^{p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\\
&\leq C_{3}(T)\|\eta^{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+C_{4}(T)\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}.\end{split}

Here and hereafter,
C_{i}(T)
represent positive constants that are independent of
\varepsilon
and depend on

within any bounded set.
We then multiply the differential equation for
\eta^{\varepsilon}
by
\eta^{\varepsilon}
, from which we derive

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{2}&=(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u^{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\rho\eta^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\mu v^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\

By applying the Gronwall inequality, we deduce that

(5.14)

\begin{split}|\eta^{\varepsilon}(t)|&\leq\sqrt{C_{6}(T)e^{C_{5}T}}T^{1/2}\|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$}.\end{split}

As in the proof of Theorem

, we can use the equation for

Substituting it into
(

(5.16)

(5.17)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ \|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq\frac{C_{4}(T)}{1-C_{3}(T)C_{8}(T)T^{1/2}}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ \|\eta^{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq\frac{C_{4}(T)C_{8}(T)T^{1/2}}{1-C_{3}(T)C_{8}(T)T^{1/2}}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C.

and

. Therefore, as

and
\eta\in W^{1,p}(0,T)
such that

v^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup v\;\text{ weakly in $W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))$},

\eta^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\eta\text{ uniformly in $C[0,T]$},\quad(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\rightharpoonup\eta^{\prime}\text{ weakly in $L^{p}(0,T)$}

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow v\text{ in $C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}([0,1]\times[0,T])$},\quad v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi\xi}\rightharpoonup v_{\xi\xi},v^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rightharpoonup v_{t}\text{ weakly in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$}.

)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}\rightharpoonup\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi v_{\xi},\;\frac{1}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}v^{\varepsilon}_{\xi\xi}\rightharpoonup\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}v_{\xi\xi},\;\frac{(\eta^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u_{\xi}\rightharpoonup\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi u_{\xi}\quad\text{ weakly in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$},

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1+m)v^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow(1+m)v,\;\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}\xi u_{\xi}\rightarrow\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta}{\rho^{2}}\xi u_{\xi},\;\frac{(\rho+\rho^{\varepsilon})\eta^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}u_{\xi\xi}\rightarrow\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}\quad\text{ in $L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))$}.

Therefore, taking the limit as

)
justifies that the pair

solves the system
(

)
.
Additionally, it follows from the equation of

in the system

To derive the optimality system and characterize the optimal control, it is necessary to define adjoint variables and the adjoint operator associated with the
(v,\eta)
system. With this in mind, we rewrite the system
(

)
as

(5.19)

\mathcal{L}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-uh\\
0\end{pmatrix},

where

(5.20)

\mathcal{L}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}v\\
L_{2}\eta\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{B}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},

and
\mathcal{B}
is the operator consisting of cross terms.
From
(
5.1
)
, we have

(5.21)

\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}v\\
L_{2}\eta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{t}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi v_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}v_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)v\\

and

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi u_{\xi}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}\eta}{\rho^{2}}\xi u_{\xi}+\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}\\
-\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\eta}{\rho}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\big)\xi u_{\xi}-\big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\big)\xi u_{\xi}+\frac{\eta}{\rho}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\big)\xi u_{\xi}+\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}\\
-\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}-\big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\big)\xi u_{\xi}+\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}\\
-\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Note that in defining the operator
\mathcal{B}
, we have substituted
\eta^{\prime}
with its equation from the system
(
5.1
)
and
\rho^{\prime}
with its expression from the equation
(

)
to eliminate the derivatives.

For
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}((0,1)\times(0,T))
and

(5.23)

\langle\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}\\

g_{2}\end{pmatrix}\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}f_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}g_{1}g_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Let
(w,\lambda)
be the adjoint variables corresponding to
(v,\eta)
. The adjoint PDE system is defined as

(5.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial(\text{integrand of $J$})}{\partial\sigma}\\
\frac{\partial(\text{integrand of $J$})}{\partial\rho}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},

where

(5.25)

\mathcal{L}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}^{*}w\\
L_{2}^{*}\lambda\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{B}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix},

and we require

\langle\mathcal{L}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle=\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\mathcal{L}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle.

Since the inner product
(
5.23
)
is linear, the requirement

5.26
)
is equivalent to

(5.27)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}v\\
L_{2}\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle=\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}^{*}w\\
L_{2}^{*}\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{B}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle=\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\mathcal{B}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle.

In the following, we shall use
(
5.27
)
and
(
5.28
)
to find
L_{1}^{*}
,
L_{2}^{*}
, and
\mathcal{B}^{*}
. Using integration by parts, it is easy to verify

(5.29)

\begin{split}&\langle\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}v\\
L_{2}\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}w\Big(v_{t}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\xi v_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}v_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)v\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\lambda\Big(\eta^{\prime}-\eta\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}v\Big(-w_{t}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}(\xi w)_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}w_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)w\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\eta\Big(-\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

if the following boundary and terminal conditions are provided:

\displaystyle w(1,t)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad w_{\xi}(0,t)=0\hskip 20.00003pt0<t<T,

\displaystyle w(x,T)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda(T)=0\hskip 20.00003pt0<x<1.

Therefore, we obtain

(5.30)

\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}^{*}w\\
L_{2}^{*}\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-w_{t}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}(\xi w)_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}w_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)w\\
-\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{pmatrix}.

In deriving
\mathcal{B}^{*}
from
(
5.22
)
and
(
5.28
)
, we rearrange the terms and use integration by parts on the integral
\int_{0}^{1}wu_{\xi\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi
to obtain

(5.31)

\begin{split}&\langle\mathcal{B}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}w\Big(-\big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\chi\big)\xi u_{\xi}+\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\lambda\Big(-\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{0}^{T}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\chi\big)\big(-\int_{0}^{1}w\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}wu_{\xi\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\rho\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}v\Big(-\mu\int_{0}^{1}w\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\chi\,\mathrm{d}t\,-\,\int_{0}^{T}\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t\,-\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}v\Big(\mu\lambda\rho\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}v\Big(-\mu\int_{0}^{1}w\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi-\mu\lambda\rho\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\chi\,\mathrm{d}t\,+\,\int_{0}^{T}\eta\Big(-\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Thus,
\mathcal{B}^{*}
is defined as

(5.32)

\mathcal{B}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mu\int_{0}^{1}w\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi-\mu\lambda\rho\\
-\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{pmatrix}.

By combining
(
5.24
)
with
(
5.25
)
,
(
5.30
)
, and
(
5.32
)
, we derive the adjoint PDE system. The existence of a solution for this system is guaranteed by the following theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. Let
m\in U_{M}
and
(u,\rho)=(u^{m},\rho^{m})
be the corresponding solution to the system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
. For sufficiently small
T>0
and any
p>1
, there exist
w\in W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))
and
\lambda\in C^{1}[0,T]
such that

(5.33)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-w_{t}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}(\xi w)_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}w_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)w-\mu\int_{0}^{1}w\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi-\mu\lambda\rho=0\hskip 10.00002pt&0<\xi<1,\;0<t<T,\\
&w=0\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=1,\;0<t<T,\\
&w_{\xi}=0\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=0,\;0<t<T,\\
&w=0\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;t=T,\\
&-\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi-\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=1\hskip 20.00003pt&0<t<T,\\
&\lambda=0\hskip 20.00003pt&t=T.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

We shall use the contraction mapping principle, similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, to show that the system
(
5.33
)
admits a unique solution
(w,\lambda)
. Consider the Banach space

\mathcal{K}_{2}=\{(w,\lambda)\in L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))\times L^{\infty}(0,T);\,\|w\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq K_{2}\}.

For any
(l,\psi)\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
, we define
(w,\lambda)
by the solution of the following system:

(5.34)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\begin{aligned} &-w_{t}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}(\xi w)_{\xi}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}w_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)w=\mu\int_{0}^{1}l\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\mu\psi\rho\end{aligned}\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;0<t<T,\\
&w=0\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=1,\;0<t<T,\\
&w_{\xi}=0\hskip 20.00003pt&\xi=0,\;0<t<T,\\
&w=0\hskip 20.00003pt&0<\xi<1,\;t=T,\\
&-\lambda^{\prime}=1+\psi\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\hskip 20.00003pt&0<t<T,\\
&\lambda=0\hskip 20.00003pt&t=T.\end{aligned}\right.

We start with analyzing the system for
w
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exists
C(T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\rho\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C(T),

\displaystyle 0<u<1,\hskip 20.00003pt0<\rho_{0}e^{-\mu\widetilde{\sigma}T}\leq\rho\leq\rho_{0}e^{\mu(1-\widetilde{\sigma})T}.

Here and hereafter,
C(T)
represents constants that are bounded for
T
in any bounded set. Note that the coefficients in the parabolic equation for
w
are bounded. So, we can introduce a change of variable
t\rightarrow T-t
and then apply the parabolic
L^{p}
estimate
to obtain

(5.35)

\|w\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C(T)\Big\|\mu\int_{0}^{1}l\xi u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\mu\psi\rho\Big\|_{L^{p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C(T).

Using the embedding theorem as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,

(5.36)

\|w\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq C(T)\|w\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C(T).

For any
x\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,T]
, noticing
w(x,T)=0
, it follows that

\begin{split}|w(x,t)|=|w(x,T)-w(x,t)|&\leq\sup\limits_{t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]}\frac{|w(x,t_{1})-w(x,t_{2})|}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{1/2}}|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{1/2}\\
&\leq\|w\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}T^{1/2}.\end{split}

Thus, we have

(5.37)

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq T^{1/2}\|w\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq C(T)T^{1/2}.

We then analyze the equation for
\lambda
. By Theorem
3.1
and the estimate
(
5.36
)
, we know that
u,w\in C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])
. Hence, the right-hand side of the equation for
\lambda
is continuous and bounded. Integrating the equation of
\lambda
over the interval
[t,T]
and recalling that
\lambda(T)=0
, we obtain

(5.38)

\|\lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C(T)T.

Thus, if we define a mapping
\mathcal{M}_{2}
by

(w,\lambda)=\mathcal{M}_{2}(l,\psi),

then it is clear from
(
5.37
)
and
(
5.38
)
that
\mathcal{M}_{2}
maps
\mathcal{K}_{2}
into itself when
T>0
is sufficiently small.

To further prove the mapping is a contraction, let
(l_{i},\psi_{i})\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
and denote

(w_{i},\lambda_{i})=\mathcal{M}_{2}(l_{i},\psi_{i})\quad\text{ for }i=1,2.

Since the equations in the system
(
5.34
)
are linear, we have

\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C(T)\big(\|l_{1}-l_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\big),

which, by the Sobolev embedding theorem, implies

\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq C(T)\big(\|l_{1}-l_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\big).

Hence,

(5.39)

\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq C(T)T^{1/2}\big(\|l_{1}-l_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\big).

Subtracting the equations for
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
and integrating over
[0,T]
, we obtain

(5.40)

\begin{split}\|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}&\leq C(T)T\big(\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\big)\\
&\leq C(T)T\big(\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+CT^{\alpha/2}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\big)\\
&\leq C(T)T\big(\|l_{1}-l_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\big).\end{split}

By
(
5.39
)
and
(
5.40
)
, the mapping
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a contraction when
T>0
is sufficiently small. Finally, we utilize the estimate
(
5.35
)
and the equation for
\lambda^{\prime}
to deduce that
w\in W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))
and
\lambda\in C^{1}[0,T]
. The proof is complete.
∎

After deriving the sensitive system and the adjoint system, we are now ready to characterize the optimal control. Let
m
be an optimal control, and
(u,\rho)
the corresponding solution of the system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
. Suppose
m+\varepsilon h\in U_{M}
for
\varepsilon>0
, and
(u^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon})
is the corresponding solution. We compute the directional derivative of the objective functional
J(m)
with respect to
m
in the direction of
h
. Since
J(m)
is the minimum value,

\begin{split}0&\leq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(m+\varepsilon h)-J(m)}{\varepsilon}\\
&=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho}{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t+\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{T}B(2mh+\varepsilon h^{2})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\eta\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}2Bmh\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}2Bmh\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\mathcal{L}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}2Bmh\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{L}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}2Bmh\,\mathrm{d}t\\

&=\int_{0}^{T}h\Big(2Bm-\int_{0}^{1}wu\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where we used Theorem
5.1
and equations
(
5.19
)
and
(
5.24
)
. Therefore, we arrive at

for any

such that
m+\varepsilon h\in U_{M}
. By a standard argument, we obtain the characterization of the optimal control:

m^{*}(t)=\min\Big\{\max\Big\{0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}w(\xi,t)u(\xi,t)\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2B}\Big\},M\Big\},

where
u
is the solution to the state system, and
w
is the corresponding adjoint variable.

Substituting
(
5.41
)
into the state system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.6
)
and the adjoint system
(
5.33
)
, we obtain the optimality system:

\displaystyle u=1\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\xi=1,\;0<t<T,

\displaystyle\xi=0,\;0<t<T,

\displaystyle\xi=0,\;0<t<T,

\displaystyle 0<t<T,

\displaystyle t=T,

\displaystyle m^{*}(t)=\min\Big\{\max\Big\{0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}w(\xi,t)u(\xi,t)\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2B}\Big\},M\Big\}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle 0<t<T.

and

. For a sufficiently small final time

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. For sufficiently small
T>0
, the optimality system
(
5.42
)
has a unique solution
(u,\rho,w,\lambda)
such that
u,w\in W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))
and
\rho,\lambda\in C^{1}[0,T]

Proof.

Suppose that
(u,\rho,w,\lambda)
and
(\bar{u},\bar{\rho},\bar{w},\bar{\lambda})
are two solutions to the optimality system
(
5.42
)
. By Theorems
3.1
and
5.2
, we have

\|u\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|w\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\rho|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|\lambda\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C(T),

\|\bar{u}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\bar{w}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|\bar{\lambda}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C(T),

where
C(T)
and
C_{i}(T)
, hereafter, are bounded for
T
in any bounded set.
We denote

m^{*}=\min\Big\{\max\Big\{0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}wu\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2B}\Big\},M\Big\},

and

\bar{m}^{*}=\min\Big\{\max\Big\{0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\bar{w}\bar{u}\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2B}\Big\},M\Big\},

respectively. It follows from the definition of
m^{*}
and
\bar{m}^{*}
that

\|m^{*}-\bar{m}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C\big(\|w-\bar{w}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\big).

Subtracting the equations for
u
and
\bar{u}
, and employing similar techniques as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(T)\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|m^{*}-\bar{m}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\big)\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(T)\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\big)\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(T)\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\big),\end{split}

Taking the initial conditions of
u
and
\bar{u}
into consideration yields

(5.43)

\begin{split}&\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(T)T^{\alpha/2}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C_{9}(T)T^{\alpha/2}\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\big).\end{split}

Then, for the equations of
\rho
and
\bar{\rho}
, we apply techniques similar to those used in the proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
5.1
to obtain

\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C(T)T^{1/2}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))},

and

\|(\rho-\bar{\rho})^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+C\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C(T)\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}.

Hence,

(5.44)

\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\leq C(T)\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\leq C_{10}(T)T^{1/2}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}.

Next, we derive estimates for
w-\bar{w}
and
\lambda-\bar{\lambda}
. By subtracting the governing equations of
w
and
\bar{w}
and applying the
L^{p}
estimates for the resulting parabolic equation, we obtain

\begin{split}&\|w-\bar{w}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(T)\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|m^{*}-\bar{m}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 45.00006pt+\|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\big)\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(T)\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}+\|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\big).\end{split}

Using the embedding theorem, after artificially extending the time interval to
[0,1]
as applied in the proof of Theorem
3.1
to avoid dependency of the embedding constant on
T
, we further have

\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq CT^{\alpha/2}\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq CT^{\alpha/2}\|w-\bar{w}\|_{W^{2,1,p}((0,1)\times(0,T))}.

This implies

(5.45)

\begin{split}&\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\leq C_{11}(T)T^{\alpha/2}\big(\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 167.00021pt+\|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\big).\end{split}

Then, subtracting the equations for
\lambda
and
\bar{\lambda}
and integrating the resulting equation, we obtain

(5.46)

\begin{split}\|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}&\leq C_{12}(T)T\big(\|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 53.0001pt+\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}+\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\big).\end{split}

Adding
(
5.43
)
,
(
5.44
)
,
(
5.45
)
, and
(
5.46
)
, we arrive at the inequality

\begin{split}&\Big(1-C_{9}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{10}(T)T^{1/2}-C_{11}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{12}(T)T\Big)\|u-\bar{u}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt+\Big(1-C_{9}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{11}(T)(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{12}(T)T\Big)\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\|_{C^{1}[0,T]}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt+\Big(1-C_{9}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{11}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{12}(T)T\Big)\|w-\bar{w}\|_{C^{1,1/2}([0,1]\times[0,T])}\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt+\Big(1-C_{11}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{12}(T)T\Big)\|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq 0.\end{split}

If we choose
T>0
sufficiently small such that

1-C_{9}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{10}(T)T^{1/2}-C_{11}(T)T^{\alpha/2}-C_{12}(T)T>0,

then
u=\bar{u}
,
\rho=\bar{\rho}
,
w=\bar{w}
,
\lambda=\bar{\lambda}
.
∎

Numerical simulations

6.1.
The steady-state case

In Section 2, we derived an algebraic characterization of the optimal control strategy in the steady-state case, as given in
(
2.9
)
and
(
2.10
)
. For the numerical simulations, the parameter settings are as follows: critical concentration of nutrients
\widetilde{\sigma}=0.25
, tumor aggressiveness parameter
\mu=0.5
, the balancing parameter in the objective functional
B=2
, and the maximum amount of inhibitor
M=1
. Applying Newton’s method to the equation
(
2.9
)
, we compute the optimal amount of inhibitor is
m_{*}=0.3269
. Hence, by
(
2.10
)
, the optimal control variable is calculated as

m^{*}=\min\{m_{*},M\}=\min\{0.3269,1\}=0.3269.

We will next develop the optimality system for the steady-state case using similar ideas in deriving the optimality system
(
5.42
)
for the parabolic case. This will further validate the result in Section 2 and bridge the gap between the steady-state and parabolic cases. Furthermore, it is intriguing to see that the optimality system of the steady-state case does not follow from (
5.42
) by removing the time derivatives and initial/terminal conditions.

In the steady-state case, we get rid of the time derivatives in both the state system
(
3.1
)
–
(
3.4
)
and the sensitivity system
(
5.1
)
.
When deriving the adjoint operators
L^{*}_{1}
,
L^{*}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, we disregard the time derivative terms in
(
5.29
)
and
(
5.31
)
, and note that, in steady state

\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}=0.

Thus, we have

\begin{split}\langle\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}v\\
L_{2}\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle&=\int_{0}^{1}w\Big(-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}v_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)v\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\lambda\Big(-\eta\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}v\Big(-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}w_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)w\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\eta\cdot 0\\
&=\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}^{*}w\\
L_{2}^{*}\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{B}\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle&=\int_{0}^{1}w\Big(\frac{2\eta}{\rho^{3}}u_{\xi\xi}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\lambda\Big(-\rho\int_{0}^{1}\mu v\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)\\
&=\eta\Big(-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)+\int_{0}^{1}v\Big(-\mu\lambda\rho\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\langle\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{pmatrix},\mathcal{B}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\rangle\end{split}

These calculations indicate that

\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}^{*}w\\
L_{2}^{*}\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}w_{\xi\xi}+(1+m)w\\
0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{B}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mu\lambda\rho\\
-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{pmatrix}

As a result, the optimality system for the steady-state elliptic PDEs is to find the unknown functions
u(\xi)
and
w(\xi)
, and unknown constants
\rho
,
\lambda
, and
m^{*}
such that

\displaystyle-\frac{u_{\xi\xi}}{\rho^{2}}+(1+m^{*})u=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle 0<\xi<1,

\displaystyle u=1\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\xi=1,

\displaystyle u_{\xi}=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\xi=0,

\displaystyle 0=\int_{0}^{1}\mu(u-\widetilde{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle-\frac{w_{\xi\xi}}{\rho^{2}}+(1+m^{*})w=\mu\lambda\rho\hskip 10.00002pt

\displaystyle 0<\xi<1,

\displaystyle w=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\xi=1,

\displaystyle w_{\xi}=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\xi=0,

\displaystyle 0=1+\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

\displaystyle m^{*}=\min\Big\{\max\Big\{0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}w(\xi)u(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2B}\Big\},M\Big\}

To solve the above system, it is worth noting that, for a given constant
m
, the unknown function
u(\xi)
can be explicitly solved as

u(\xi)=\frac{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho\xi)}{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)},

where
\rho
is uniquely determined by

\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)}{\sqrt{1+m}\rho}=\widetilde{\sigma}.

Similarly, since the right-hand-side of the equation for
w
, given by
\mu\lambda\rho
, is a constant,
w
can be explicitly solved as

w(\xi)=\frac{\mu\lambda\rho}{1+m}\Big(1-\frac{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho\xi)}{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)}\Big),

where
\lambda
is determined by the integral equation
\int_{0}^{1}w_{\xi}u_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=-\frac{\rho^{3}}{2}
, leading to

\frac{1}{\mu\lambda}=\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)}{\sqrt{1+m}\rho}-\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(\sqrt{1+m}\rho)}.

Therefore, for the steady-state case, we use the following algorithm to solve the optimality system and determine the optimal control
m^{*}
:

Given
\widetilde{\sigma}
,
\mu
,
B
,
M
, and
TOL>0
, choose an initial constant
m_{0}\in[0,M]
.

for

i=0,1,2,\cdots

do

Compute
\rho_{i}
from the equation
\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i})}{\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i}}=\widetilde{\sigma}
using Newton’s method;

Discretize
\xi
within
[0,1]
with a constant step size, and let
u_{i}(\xi_{j})=\frac{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i}\xi_{j})}{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i})}

Let
\lambda_{i}=\big(\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i})}{\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i}}-\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i})}\big)^{-1}\big/\mu
and
w_{i}(\xi_{j})=\frac{\mu\lambda_{i}\rho_{i}}{1+m_{i}}\Big(1-\frac{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i}\xi_{j})}{\cosh(\sqrt{1+m_{i}}\rho_{i})}\Big)
;

Update the control variable
m_{i+1}=\min\Big\{\max\Big\{0,\frac{\int_{0}^{1}w_{i}u_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{2B}\Big\},M\Big\}
;

If
|m_{i+1}-m_{i}|<TOL
, stop the algorithm;

end

for

7.
Discussion

Free boundary models are commonly used to describe tumor tissue growth. To find the optimal amount of tumor growth inhibitor in a multilayered tumor growth model, we have developed a theoretical framework to tackle an optimization problem based on a free boundary PDE model.
It has been proved that the optimization problem has a unique solution and can be characterized by the solution of the optimality system. In the steady-state case, we found an algebraic characterization of the optimizer via direct computations in Section 2. We validated the result by finding and solving the optimality system of the steady-state elliptic PDEs. For the parabolic case, we employed the forward-backward sweep method, a strategy commonly used in optimal control problems involving parabolic PDE systems, to solve the complete optimality system and determine the optimal control.

There are a variety of potential extensions and directions for future research. One important direction is to incorporate the control variable into the boundary condition
(
1.2
)
and investigate the corresponding optimal control problem. The boundary condition
(
1.2
)
represents a constant nutrient supply to the tumor via blood vessels. In the context of tumor treatment, a widely recognized therapeutic method is cancer starvation therapy, which aims to block nutrient flow and suppress tumor growth
[
46
,
51
]
. Therefore, modifying the boundary condition to include control could offer a more realistic strategy. Another potential direction is to explore the optimal control problem of the model
(
1.1
)
–
(
1.9
)
. The challenge here lies in the inclusion of the
p
variable and its boundary condition
(
1.5
)
. In fact, due to this boundary condition, we only have local well-posedness, not global well-posedness, for the system
[
11
]
. These optimization problems remain open and will be the focus of our future work.

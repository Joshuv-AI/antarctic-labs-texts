Breakthroughs of bispecific antibodies therapy in multiple myeloma: latest updates from 2025 ASH
Authors: Bihuan Zhao, Qiling Bu, Ai-Li Chen, Hong-Hu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Bispecific antibodies (BsAbs), which simultaneously target two antigens and redirect immune T cells toward tumor cells, have emerged as one of the most promising immunotherapeutic strategies for multiple myeloma (MM). Based on previously reported clinical data, several BsAbs have been approved for MM patients who relapsed in late-line treatment. At the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting, pivotal trials presented updated findings on BsAbs targeting B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA), G-protein-coupled receptor family C group 5 member D (GPRC5D), and Fc receptor-homolog 5 (FcRH5), evaluated as monotherapies or in combination regimens across patient populations, both at initial diagnosis and at relapse. Here, we summarize the remarkable outcomes from the key clinical trials.

To the Editor,

In recent years, bispecific antibodies (BsAbs) have profoundly transformed the therapeutic landscape and improved the prognosis of multiple myeloma (MM). We highlight the updated clinical trials on BsAbs for MM from the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting (Table 1).

Monotherapy with BsAbs in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (R/RMM)

A phase 1/2 study of LBL-034 (GPRC5D×CD3) enrolled 56 R/RMM patients, and doses ≥ 400 µg/kg yielded an overall response rate (ORR) of 78.1% in penta-drug-exposed patients, 85.8% in BCMA-exposed patients, and 75.0% in extramedullary disease (EMD) subgroup [9]. A phase 2 study evaluated cevostamab (FcRH5×CD3) as consolidation following BCMA chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy in 27 R/RMM [10]. At one year post-CAR-T, 93% (13/14) patients achieved minimal residual disease (MRD) negativity at 10− 5. The estimated 12-month progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates were both 95%.

Another phase 1b trial of subcutaneous cevostamab achieved a 38.8% (20/52) ORR and a 23.1% (12/52) complete response (CR) or better rate in heavily pretreated R/RMM patients, with a 12.3-month median response duration [11]. The lower ORR than intravenous administration (38.8% vs. 43.1%) may be attributed to the inclusion of a low-dose cohort [12].

Combination therapies with BsAbs in R/RMM

The phase 3 MajesTEC-3 trial showed teclistamab plus daratumumab significantly improved outcomes versus daratumumab-pomalidomide/bortezomib-dexamethasone in early relapsed R/RMM [1], including 36-month PFS (83.4% vs. 29.7%), ORR (89.0% vs. 75.3%), CR or better rate (81.8% vs. 32.1%), and MRD-negativity (58.4% vs. 17.1%) (all P < 0.001).

In the phase 1b RedirecTT-1 study, 94 triple-class-exposed R/RMM patients received dual BsAb therapy with talquetamab plus teclistamab. The recommended phase 2 regimen (RP2R) yielded ORR of 61.1% in EMD patients and 92.3% in non-EMD patients [2]. At 36 months, PFS and OS rates for EMD versus non-EMD patients were 39.7% vs. 70.5% and 57.0% vs. 83.2%, respectively. The ongoing phase 2 study dedicated to EMD also showed a similar high and durable response, with an ORR of 77.8% and 50.0% of at least CR [8]. The 12-month PFS and OS rates were 55.6% and 73.8%.

Suvannasankha et al. reported the data of elranatamab plus iberdomide in R/RMM patients achieving a 77.3% (17/22) ORR, and a 45.5% (10/22) CR or better [4]. A phase 1b study of the second-generation anti-BCMA×CD3 BsAb etentamig plus pomalidomide and dexamethasone in R/RMM showed an ORR of 81% (67/82), including 72% (59/82) of patients achieved a very good partial response (VGPR) or better [7]. Grade ≥ 3 neutropenia and infections were observed in 78% (66/85) and 49% (42/85) patients, respectively.

BsAbs in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM)

Current studies also investigate frontline BsAbs to achieve deep remissions and prolong initial response durations. In phase 1/2 LINKER-MM4 trial, linvoseltamab monotherapy for NDMM achieved an ORR of 79% (34/43) and CR or better of 26% (11/43) [5]. Furthermore, 92% (22/24) of evaluable patients achieved 10− 5 MRD negativity. However, infections occurred in 82% (36/45) of patients. Notably, the suboptimal frontline efficacy may be due to only 46.7% (21/45) of patients receiving the full 200 mg dose and a short follow-up with fewer treatment cycles.

The phase 2 IMMUNOPLANT trial showed that fixed-duration linvoseltamab as immunoconsolidation converted 100% (14/14) of evaluable patients with post-induction MRD positivity to MRD negativity by both NGS clonoSEQ (10− 6) and flow cytometry (10− 5) [6].

The IFM2021-01 trial evaluated teclistamab plus subcutaneous daratumumab in 37 transplant-ineligible patients [3]. The ORR was 100% (37/37), with 97% (36/37) patients achieving VGPR or better. At six months, MRD negativity (10− 6) was observed in 100% (21/21) of evaluable patients. However, grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 76% (28/37) of patients.

In summary, ASH 2025 presented diverse clinical data on BsAbs for MM (Table 2). These promising results bring curative potential to MM, but infectious toxicity remains a major challenge, especially in combination treatments, with infection rates exceeding 90% in the RedirecTT-1 and MajesTEC-3 trials. The high incidence of infection underscores the need to manage AEs and balance risks against clinical benefits. Furthermore, as novel targets emerge and BsAbs advance to earlier treatment lines, defining optimal treatment sequencing in MM remains a critical unmet need. Longer follow-up and large-scale controlled trials are essential to establish robust evidence for future clinical applications.

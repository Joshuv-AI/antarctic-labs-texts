The Cylinder Simplicial DG Ring
Amnon Yekutieli

Abstract
. Given a DG ring B B and a number q ≥ 0 q\geq 0 , we construct the q q -th cylinder DG ring Cyl q ⁡ ( B ) \operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B) . For q = 1 q=1 this is just Keller’s cylinder DG ring (sometimes called the path object of B B ), which encodes homotopies between DG ring homomorphisms A → B A\to B . As q q changes the cylinder DG rings form a simplicial DG ring Cyl ⁡ ( B ) \operatorname{Cyl}(B) . Hence, given another DG ring A A , the DG ring homomorphisms A → Cyl ⁡ ( B ) A\to\operatorname{Cyl}(B) form a simplicial set Hom ⁡ ( A , Cyl ⁡ ( B ) ) \operatorname{Hom}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B)) . Our main theorem states that when A A is a semi-free DG ring, the simplicial set Hom ⁡ ( A , Cyl ⁡ ( B ) ) \operatorname{Hom}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B)) is a Kan complex. For the verification of the Kan condition we introduce a new construction, which may be of independent interest. Given a horn 𝚲 i q \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i} in 𝚫 q \boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q} , we define the DG ring N ⁡ ( 𝚲 i q , B ) \operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B) , and we prove that N ⁡ ( 𝚲 i q , B ) \operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B) represents this horn in the simplicial set Hom ⁡ ( A , Cyl ⁡ ( B ) ) \operatorname{Hom}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B)) . In this way the Kan condition is implemented intrinsically in the category of DG rings, thus facilitating calculations. Presumably all the above can be extended, with little change, from DG rings to (small) DG categories. That would enable easy constructions and explicit calculations of some simplicial aspects of DG categories.



0.
Introduction

By a
DG ring
we mean a graded ring

A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{i}
, with a differential satisfying the
graded Leibniz rule. (Traditionally
A
is called an associative unital
cochain DG algebra.)
The category of DG rings is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
.
Note that a DG ring is the same as a single object DG category,
and thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
is a full subcategory of

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}
, the category of (small) DG categories.
For the sake of streamlining the discussion, here we only look at DG rings.
See Remark
0.7
regarding the potential extension of this work to DG
categories.

Let
A
and
B
be DG rings, and let
f_{0},f_{1}:A\to B
be DG ring
homomorphisms. A
Keller homotopy

\gamma:f_{0}\Rightarrow f_{1}
is the appropriate notion of homotopy in this
context. It is known that when the DG ring
A
is semi-free,
these homotopies form an equivalence relation on the set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,B)
.

Keller homotopies are encoded by DG ring homomorphisms

f_{\gamma}:A\to\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(B)
, where

\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(B)
is the
Keller cylinder DG ring
associated to

B
. Some texts, including
[
Ta
]
, refer to this DG ring as the
path
object
associated to
B
.
The notions mentioned above will be recalled in Section
1
of
the paper.

Recently we observed that the Keller cylinder DG ring

\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(B)
is the special case
q=1
of a collection of DG
rings
\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B)
. These are naturally
arranged as a simplicial DG ring

\operatorname{Cyl}(B)=\{\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B)\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence, for any DG ring
A
, we obtain a simplicial set

(0.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))=\bigl\{\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B))\bigr\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}.

The construction of the simplicial DG ring
\operatorname{Cyl}(B)
is done in Section

3
of the paper.

The main result of our paper is this:

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
A
be a semi-free DG ring, and let
B
be any DG ring.
Then the simplicial set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))

is a Kan complex.

The theorem is repeated as Theorem
4.11
in Section

4
, and proved there.

The theorem shows that the equivalence relation on the set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,B)

formed by the Keller derivations, which was mentioned above, consists of
isomorphisms in the fundamental groupoid

(0.3)

\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\leq 1}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr).

A
simplicial enrichment
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
, or more generally of

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}
, is one incarnation of its
\infty
-derived category.
The known constructions of such simplicial enrichments are indirect, and rely
on difficult arguments involving Quillen model structures; see

[
Ta
]
and its references.
We were hoping to make use of Theorem
0.2

to obtain an explicit simplicial enrichment of
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
; but
we were not able to describe the horizontal composition of higher morphisms. So
we leave this as Question
0.5
.

The proof of Theorem
0.2
, namely the verification of the Kan condition
in the simplicial set (
0.1
) when
A
is semi-free, relies on a novel
construction, which may be of independent interest.
For every simplicial set
X
we construct a DG ring

\operatorname{N}(X,B)
. When
X=\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
this is precisely the cylinder DG ring

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B)
. Given a horn
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
in the standard simplex

\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
, the DG ring

\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)

turns out to represent horns. This is our key technical result:

Theorem 0.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be DG rings, and let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
be a horn in

\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
. Then there is a canonical bijection

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)).

This is repeated as Theorem
4.8
and proved in Section

4
.
This result allows us to interpret horns in the simplicial set
(
0.1
) as DG ring homomorphisms

A\to\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)
.
It is not hard to show that the inclusion

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}\to\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
gives rise to a surjective
quasi-isomorphism of DG rings

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B)\to\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B))
.
The filling of the horn then follows from the lifting property of semi-free DG
rings, see Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem
0.2
is stated in the absolute setting.
It is not hard to extend it to a relative setting, namely fixing a base
commutative DG ring
\mathbb{K}
, and treating the category

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0.0mu}/_{\mspace{-2mu}\mathrm{c}}\mspace{1.5mu}\mathbb{K}
of central DG
\mathbb{K}
-rings.
Again, for the sake of streamlining the discussion, we left this generalization
out.

Here is our question about simplicial enrichment.

Question 0.5
.

Let
A
,
B
and
C
be semi-free DG rings. Is it possible to
define a natural map of simplicial sets

(0.6)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\times\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(B,\operatorname{Cyl}(C))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(C))

that would be the horizontal composition of a simplicial enrichment of

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
?

The best we can do at present is to construct the horizontal composition of

2
-homotopy classes of
1
-morphisms, namely of isomorphisms in the
fundamental groupoids (
0.3
).
Such a composition would yield an explicit construction of the

(2,1)
-derived category of
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
.
This is work in progress, expected to be in the paper
[
Ye3
]
.

Remark 0.7
.

Here is an outline of the extension of our constructions from DG rings to DG
categories. Consider the universal cylinder DG ring

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},\mathbb{Z})
.
Given a DG category
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}
, define the DG category

(0.8)

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}):=\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}).

As
q
changes, we obtain the simplicial DG category

(0.9)

\operatorname{Cyl}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})=\{\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}.

Now let
\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}
be some other DG category. Looking at DG functors

\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}\to\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
, we get a simplicial set

(0.10)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{A}},\operatorname{Cyl}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}))=\bigl\{\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{A}},\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}))\bigr\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}.

This is the generalization of (
0.1
). It is reasonable to ask
whether the generalization of Theorem
0.2
holds, namely that when

\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}
is a semi-free DG category, the simplicial set (
0.10
)
is a Kan complex. Presumably the answer is positive; and a proof would be a
suitable modification of our proof of Theorem
0.2
, using the DG
categories

\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}):=\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},\mathbb{Z})
,
see Definition
3.7
.

Here are three remarks about related work (stated using our notation).

Remark 0.11
.

In the paper
[
Ta
]
, Tabuada constructs a simplicial enrichment of

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}
. The starting point is the formation of the
path object

\operatorname{P}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
associated to a DG category
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}
. It appears that

\operatorname{P}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
is the same as the DG category

\operatorname{Cyl}_{1}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
from equation (
0.8
).
Tabuada then proceeds to prove that
\operatorname{P}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
plays the role of a path
object for the Quillen model structure on
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}
that was constructed in
his earlier paper.

At this point our work diverges drastically from the work of Tabuada.
He does not have higher cylinders (or path objects) for
q>1
. Instead, he
moves via a chain of Quillen adjunctions to a category that’s known to have a
simplicial enrichment.

Remark 0.12
.

Faonte, in the paper
[
Fa
]
, produces a simplicial enrichment of

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGCat}}
in the following way. To a pair of DG categories

\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}
he considers the DG category

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\operatorname{A}_{\infty}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{A}},\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})

of
\operatorname{A}_{\infty}
-functors
F:\operatorname{\mathsf{A}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}
. He then takes
the DG nerve of

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\operatorname{A}_{\infty}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{A}},\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
,
which is a simplicial set, and proves that it is weakly homotopy equivalent
to the simplicial enrichment of Tabuada, as in Remark
0.11
.
We do not know if there is any further relationship between our work and the
work of Faonte.

Remark 0.13
.

Holstein, in the paper
[
Ho
]
, studies simplicial resolutions of DG
categories. For a DG category
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}
he produces a simplicial DG category

\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}_{\bullet}=\{\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}_{q}\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
. The constructions are rather
involved; but in Example 3.5 he shows that his
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}_{1}
agrees with Tabuada’s
path object,
see Remark
0.11
above. This might indicate that Holstein’s simplicial
DG category
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}_{\bullet}
agrees with our simplicial DG category

\operatorname{Cyl}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
from Remark
0.7
. However we
could not verify the details.

One of the main results in the paper
[
Ho
]
is Proposition 3.10, which
asserts that
\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}_{\bullet}
is a Reedy fibrant simplicial DG category.
There was an error in the proof, which was eventually corrected in

[
AP
]
and
[
Ho
, Erratum]
.

Assuming that Holstein’s construction of the simplicial DG category

\operatorname{\mathsf{B}}_{\bullet}
does agree with our
\operatorname{Cyl}(\operatorname{\mathsf{B}})
, one might
wonder if his result mentioned above is related to our Theorem
0.2
.
And if so, perhaps our technical Theorem
0.4
, upgraded to DG
categories, could simplify some of the difficult arguments in the papers

[
Ho
]
and
[
AP
]
.

We end the Introduction with an outline of the paper.

Section 1.
We recall Keller derivations and the Keller cylinder DG ring.
Then we recall the definition of semi-free DG rings, and prove some results
about their lifting properties, extending results from
[
Ye1
]
and

[
Ye2
]
.

Section 2.

This section is about simplicial sets. We describe the horn

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
as a colimit, in the category

\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}
of simplicial sets, of a finite direct system

\{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q_{j}}\}_{j\in J}
of standard simplices.
Then we do the same for the semi-simplicial set

(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i})^{\mathrm{nd}}
of nondegenerate simplices in

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
.

Section 3.

Here we introduce the DG ring
\operatorname{N}(X,B)
associated functorially to a
simplicial set
X
and a DG ring
B
. For the simplicial set
\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}

we get the cylinder DG ring

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B):=\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},B)
.
The cosimplicial simplicial set structure on

\{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}

gives rise to the simplicial DG ring structure on

\operatorname{Cyl}(B)=\{\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B)\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Section 4.

In this section we prove the key technical result of the paper, Theorem

4.8
(a repetition of Theorem
0.4
), which asserts that
the DG ring

\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)

encodes horns in the simplicial set (
0.1
).
We also prove that the canonical DG ring homomorphism

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q}(B)=\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},B)\to\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)

is a surjective quasi-isomorphism.
Combining these results with the lifting property of semi-free DG rings, we
deduce Theorem
4.11
(a repetition of Theorem
0.2
).

1.
Preliminaries About DG Rings

In this section we recall some material on DG rings, from the paper
[
Ye1
]

and the book
[
Ye2
]
.
We also prove upgraded versions of a few theorems from these references.

The setting here is more general than is required for the
current paper, regarding the base. In this section we study central DG

A
-rings, where the base
A
is a nonzero commutative DG ring
A
; whereas
Theorem
0.2
is in the absolute setting, i.e.
A=\mathbb{Z}
.

A
DG ring
is a graded ring
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{i}
,
with a differential
\mathrm{d}
of degree
1
satisfying

\mathrm{d}\circ\mathrm{d}=0
, and the graded Leibniz rule

\mathrm{d}(a\,{\cdot}\,b)=\mathrm{d}(a)\,{\cdot}\,b+(-1)^{i}\,{\cdot}\,a\,{\cdot}\,\mathrm{d}(b)
for

a\in A^{i}
and
b\in A^{j}
. Traditionally,
A
is called a unital associative
cochain DG algebra. Let
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
denote the category of DG rings and DG
ring homomorphisms.

Suppose
A
is a fixed DG ring. A DG
A
-ring is a DG ring
B
equipped
with a DG ring homomorphism
u_{B}:A\to B
, called the structural homomorphism.
A homomorphism of DG
A
-rings
f:B\to C
is a DG ring homomorphism
f
such
that
f\circ u_{B}=u_{C}
. The category of DG rings is denoted by

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0mu}/\mspace{-0.5mu}A
.

Let
A
be some DG ring. A homogeneous element
a\in A^{i}
is called central if
for every
b\in B^{j}
there is equality

b\,{\cdot}\,a=(-1)^{i\,{\cdot}\,j}\,{\cdot}\,a\,{\cdot}\,b
.
The
center
of
A
consists of elements that are sums of homogeneous
central elements, and it is denoted by
\operatorname{Cent}(A)
. It is easy to check that

\operatorname{Cent}(A)
is a DG subring of
A
.

A DG ring
A
is called
commutative
if it is nonpositive (i.e.
A=\bigoplus_{i\leq 0}A^{i}
) and strongly commutative
(i.e.
b\,{\cdot}\,a=(-1)^{i\,{\cdot}\,j}\,{\cdot}\,a\,{\cdot}\,b
for all

a\in A^{i}
and
b\in A^{j}
, and also
a\,{\cdot}\,a=0
if
i
is odd).
The abbreviation is
CDG ring
.
Clearly
\operatorname{Cent}(A)=A
for a CDG ring
A
.

Now fix some CDG ring
A
. A DG
A
-ring
B
is called
central
if
the structural homomorphism
u_{B}:A\to B
is a central homomorphism,
i.e.
u_{B}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{Cent}(B)
.
The category of central DG
A
-rings is a full subcategory of

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0mu}/\mspace{-0.5mu}A
, and it is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0.0mu}/_{\mspace{-2mu}\mathrm{c}}\mspace{1.5mu}A
.
For
A=\mathbb{Z}
we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0.0mu}/_{\mspace{-2mu}\mathrm{c}}\mspace{1.5mu}\mathbb{Z}=\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0mu}/\mspace{-0.5mu}\mathbb{Z}=\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
.

From here to the end of the section we have a fixed nonzero
CDG ring
A
. All DG rings, and all homomorphisms between them, are in

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\mspace{0.0mu}/_{\mspace{-2mu}\mathrm{c}}\mspace{1.5mu}A
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}:B\to C
be DG ring homomorphisms.
A
Keller homotopy

\gamma:f_{0}\Rightarrow f_{1}
is an
A
-linear homomorphism

\gamma:B\to C
of degree
-1
, satisfying these two conditions:

\rhd

Twisted derivation: For every
b_{0}\in B^{i_{0}}
and
b_{1}\in B^{i_{1}}
there is equality

\gamma(b_{0}\,{\cdot}\,b_{1})=\gamma(b_{0})\,{\cdot}\,f_{1}(b_{1})+(-1)^{i_{0}}\,{\cdot}\,f_{0}(b_{0})\,{\cdot}\,\gamma(b_{1}).

\rhd

Homotopy:

\mathrm{d}_{C}\circ\gamma+\gamma\circ\mathrm{d}_{B}=f_{1}-f_{0}
.

This concept first appeared in the papers
[
Ke1
]
and
[
Ke2
]
.

The
Keller cylinder DG ring
is the matrix DG ring

(1.1)

\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(\mathbb{Z}):=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}[-1]\\
0&\mathbb{Z}\end{bmatrix}.

The differential is the commutator with the degree
1
element

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mspace{10mu}1[-1]\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
To make things more explicit, let
y
be a variable of degree
1
, so that
there is an isomorphism of DG abelian groups (with zero differentials)

\mathbb{Z}[-1]\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\mathbb{Z}\otimes y
,
1[-1]\mapsto 1\otimes y
.
Then we can write

(1.2)

\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(\mathbb{Z})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}\otimes 1&\mathbb{Z}\otimes y\\
0&\mathbb{Z}\otimes 1\end{bmatrix}.

The elements

e_{0}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and

e_{1}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]

have degree
0
, and the element

e_{0,1}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&y\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]

has degree
1
. The multiplication in

\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(\mathbb{Z})
is the usual matrix multiplication, and
the differential is

\mathrm{d}(e_{0})=-e_{0,1}
,
\mathrm{d}(e_{1})=e_{0,1}
, and

\mathrm{d}(e_{0,1})=0
.
For an arbitrary DG ring
B
we define its Keller cylinder DG ring to be

(1.3)

\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(B):=B\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(\mathbb{Z})=\begin{bmatrix}B\otimes 1&B\otimes y\\
0&B\otimes 1\end{bmatrix}.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}:B\to C
be DG ring homomorphisms.
According to
[
Ye1
, Sections 1 and 4]
, following
[
Ke2
, Theorem 4.3(c)]
,
the two conditions below are equivalent for an
A
-linear homomorphism

\gamma:B\to C
of degree
-1
:

\rhd

\gamma
is a Keller homotopy
f_{0}\Rightarrow f_{1}
.

\rhd

The
A
-linear homomorphism

f_{\gamma}:B\to\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(B)
,

f_{\gamma}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}f_{0}&\mspace{10mu}y\,{\cdot}\,\gamma\\
0&f_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, is a DG ring
homomorphism.

According to Keller (private communication), when the DG
A
-ring
B
is
noncommutative semi-free (see Definition
1.4
below),
the Keller homotopies
form an equivalence relation on the set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(B,\operatorname{Cyl}_{\mathrm{Kel}}(C))
.
Our Theorem

says more: this equivalence relation is by

A filtered graded set
(X,F)
is a graded set

X=\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}X^{i}
, and
an ascending filtration
\{F_{j}(X)\}_{j\geq-1}

of
X
by graded subsets,
such that
F_{-1}(X)=\varnothing
and

X=\bigcup_{j}F_{j}(X)
.

Suppose we are given a filtered graded set
(X,F)
.
Let
A^{\natural}
the graded ring gotten by forgetting the differential of
A
.
Define
A^{\natural}\langle X\rangle:=A^{\natural}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}\langle X\rangle

to be the noncommutative graded polynomial ring in the set of variables
X

over
A^{\natural}
.
The graded ring
A^{\natural}\langle X\rangle
is filtered by

F_{j}(A^{\natural}\langle X\rangle):=A^{\natural}\langle F_{j}(X)\rangle

for
j\geq 0
, and
F_{-1}(A\langle X\rangle):=0
.

The next couple of definitions are

[
Ye2
, Definitions 12.8.3 and 12.8.6]
, generalized in two ways:
Changing from a base ring
\mathbb{K}
to a base CDG ring
A
, and
removing the requirement that the DG rings are nonpositive.
Also we require a resolution
u
to be a
surjective

quasi-isomorphism, as in
[
Ye1
, Definitions 3.8 and 3.15]
.

.

A

.
Such a filtered graded set

.

.

Let

Step 1.
In this step we treat the cases

Proof.

3.
Certain DG Rings Associated to Simplicial Sets

Here is a bit of nonstandard notation for some compositions of coboundary maps.
Let
\partial^{>p}:[p]\to[p+q]
be the function in
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
described by the
sequence
(0,\ldots,p)
.
(This notation is in analogy to the coboundary function

\partial^{i}:[p]\to[p+1]
, whose image misses
i
. The image of our function

\partial^{>p}
misses all the numbers
i>p
.)
Similarly we write

\partial^{<p}:[q]\to[p+q]
for the function described by the sequence

(p,\ldots,p+q)
.
For a simplicial object

C=\{C_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}

there are corresponding morphisms

C(\partial^{>p}):C_{p+q}\to C_{p}
and

C(\partial^{<p}):C_{p+q}\to C_{q}
,
which we usually abbreviate to
\partial_{>p}
and
\partial_{<p}
.
For a cosimplicial object

D=\{D^{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}

there are corresponding morphisms

\partial^{>p}:D^{p}\to D^{p+q}
and

\partial^{<p}:D^{q}\to D^{p+q}
.

Suppose
C=\{C^{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is a cosimplicial ring.
There are coboundary operators

\partial^{i}:C^{p}\to C^{p+1}

and codegeneracy operators

\operatorname{s}^{i}:C^{p}\to C^{p-1}
,
which are ring homomorphisms.
The normalization of
C
is the DG ring

(3.1)

\operatorname{N}(C)=\bigoplus\nolimits_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{N}^{p}(C),

where

(3.2)

\operatorname{N}^{p}(C):=\bigcap_{i}\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{s}^{i})\subseteq C^{p}.

The differential of
\operatorname{N}(C)
is

(3.3)

\mathrm{d}:=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\,{\cdot}\,\partial^{i}:\operatorname{N}^{p}(C)\to\operatorname{N}^{p+1}(C).

The Alexander-Whitney multiplication of

b\in\operatorname{N}^{p}(C)
and
c\in\operatorname{N}(C)^{q}
is

(3.4)

b*c:=\partial^{>p}(b)\cdot\partial^{<p}(c)\in C^{p+q},

where
\cdot
is the multiplication of the ring
C^{p+q}
.
Note then even if
C
is a commutative cosimplicial ring, the DG ring

\operatorname{N}(C)
is almost never commutative.

Given a set
X
, there is the commutative ring

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.
Thus, from a simplicial set
X=\{X_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
we get the
cosimplicial ring

(3.5)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(X,\mathbb{Z})=\{\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(X_{p},\mathbb{Z})\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}.

The normalization of the cosimplicial DG ring
in (
3.5
) is the DG ring

(3.6)

\operatorname{N}(X,\mathbb{Z}):=\operatorname{N}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(X,\mathbb{Z})\bigr).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
X
be a simplicial set and let
B
be a DG ring. The
DG ring associated to
X
and
B
is the DG ring

\operatorname{N}(X,B):=\operatorname{N}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B.

This construction gives rise to a functor

\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}},\quad(X,B)\mapsto\operatorname{N}(X,B).

Recall that
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\mathrm{inj}}
is the subcategory of
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
on all the
objects, but the morphisms are only the injective (i.e. strictly increasing)
functions
\alpha:[p]\to[q]
.
A semi-simplicial set is a functor

Y:\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\mathrm{inj}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
.

Let
Y=\{Y_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be a semi-simplicial set that is
dimension-wise finite, i.e. each
Y_{p}
is a finite set.
For each
p
we define the abelian group

(3.8)

\operatorname{R}^{p}(Y,\mathbb{Z}):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(Y_{p},\mathbb{Z}).

This is a free abelian group with basis consisting of the delta functions

\delta_{y}
,
y\in Y_{p}
.
Taking the direct sum we obtain the graded abelian group

(3.9)

\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{R}^{p}(Y,\mathbb{Z}).

For
y\in Y_{p}
let

Y_{i}(y):=\{z\in Y_{p+1}\mid\partial_{i}(z)=y\}.

Define the operator

(3.10)

\mathrm{d}(\delta_{y}):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\,{\cdot}\,\sum_{z\in Y_{i}(y)}\delta_{z}.

For
x\in Y_{p}
and
y\in Y_{q}
let

Y(x,y):=\{z\in Y_{p+q}\mid\partial_{>p}(z)=x\ \text{and}\ \partial_{<p}(z)=y\}.

Define a multiplication on
\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
by

(3.11)

\delta_{y}*\delta_{z}:=\sum_{w\in Y(y,z)}\delta_{w}.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
Y=\{Y_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be a dimension-wise finite
semi-simplicial set. Then the graded abelian group

\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
, with differential
(
3.10
)
and multiplication

(
3.11
)
, is a DG ring.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}Y_{p}
be the free abelian group with basis

\{1\otimes y\}_{y\in Y_{p}}
.
Consider the graded abelian group

\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}Y:=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}\mspace{1.5mu}Y_{p}.

It has the differential

(3.13)

\mathrm{d}(1\otimes y):=\sum_{i}\mspace{1.5mu}(-1)^{i}\otimes\partial^{i}(y)

and the Alexander-Whitney comultiplication

(3.14)

\operatorname{AW}(1\otimes y):=\sum_{i}\mspace{1.5mu}\bigl(1\otimes\partial_{>i}(y)\bigr)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(1\otimes\partial_{<i}(y)\bigr).

These formulas make
\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}Y
into a DG coassociative coalgebra;
see
[
ML
, Proposition VIII.8.7]
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of graded abelian groups

(3.15)

\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}Y,\mathbb{Z}\bigr).

A direct calculation shows that the differential (
3.13
) and
comultiplication (
3.14
) of

\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}Y
go by this isomorphism to the differential
(
3.10
) and the multiplication
(
3.11
) of
\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Definition 3.16
.

Let
Y
be a semi-simplicial set and let
B
be a DG ring. The
DG ring associated to

and

is

\operatorname{R}(Y,B):=\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B.

Note that since

is dimension-wise finite, there is a canonical isomorphism
of graded abelian groups

Let

be a dimension-wise finite simplicial set,
and let

, the semi-simplicial set of nondegenerate elements.
There is an inclusion of graded sets

, inducing a surjection of graded abelian groups

,
see formula (

groups

\operatorname{N}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(X,\mathbb{Z})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{N}(X,\mathbb{Z})}

.

Let

and let

4.
The DG Ring Associated to a Horn

In this section we prove the main theorem of the paper, Theorem

0.2
from the Introduction, repeated here as Theorem
4.11
.
The proof relies on our key technical result, Theorem
0.4
from the
Introduction, which talks about the role of the DG ring

\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)
associated to a horn

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
and a DG ring
B
. It is repeated here as
Theorem
4.8
.

Consider a horn
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
.
In Corollary
2.12
we expressed this horn as a colimit

(4.1)

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}\cong\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\mspace{3mu}X_{j},

in the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}
, of a diagram
\{X_{j}\}_{j\in J}
,
indexed by a finite quiver
J
, where each

X_{j}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q_{j}}
for some natural number
q_{j}
.

Passing to nondegenerate simplices, let

Y:=(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i})^{\mathrm{nd}}

and
Y_{j}:=X_{j}^{\mathrm{nd}}
.
These are semi-simplicial sets, i.e. objects of the category

\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{\mathrm{inj}}\mspace{-2mu}\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
.
In Corollary
2.13
we proved that (
4.1
) induces a
canonical isomorphism

(4.2)

Y\cong\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\mspace{3mu}Y_{j}

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{\mathrm{inj}}\mspace{-2mu}\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
.

The horn
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
and the direct systems

\{X_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{Y_{j}\}_{j\in J}
are retained
until the end of the proof of Theorem
4.8
.

To avoid confusion (e.g. in formulas (
4.3
) and (
4.6
)
below), for a simplicial set

Z=\{Z_{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}

we will often write
Z([p])
instead of
Z_{p}
.
(This is actually the proper notation, since
Z
is a functor

\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
;
Z_{p}
is just an abbreviation.)
Likewise for semi-simplicial sets.

Since
\{Y_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a direct system of functors

\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}}_{\mathrm{inj}}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
,
the direct limit is calculated in the target category.
This means that in each simplicial dimension
p
have

(4.3)

Y([p])\cong\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\mspace{3mu}Y_{j}([p])

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
.

Let
B
be a DG ring.
By Definition
3.21
we have

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B)=\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q_{j}},B)
.
Therefore the direct system of simplicial sets

\{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q_{j}}\}_{j\in J}=\{X_{j}\}_{j\in J}

gives rise to an inverse system of DG rings

\{\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B)\}_{j\in J}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
B
be any DG ring. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)\cong\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{lim}}\,\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B)

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
.

Proof.

For the sake of clarity let’s write

X:=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
,

Y:=X^{\mathrm{nd}}=(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i})^{\mathrm{nd}}

and
Y_{j}:=X_{j}^{\mathrm{nd}}=(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q_{j}})^{\mathrm{nd}}
.

By Proposition
3.19
we can replace

\operatorname{N}(X,B)
with
\operatorname{R}(Y,B)
, and

\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B)=\operatorname{N}(X_{j},B)
with
\operatorname{R}(Y_{j},B)
.
We now need to prove that the canonical homomorphism

(4.5)

\operatorname{R}(Y,B)\to\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{lim}}\,\operatorname{R}(Y_{j},B)

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
is an isomorphism.
It suffices to prove that (
4.5
) is an isomorphism on the underlying
graded abelian groups. Now the forgetful functor from DG rings to graded abelian
groups commutes with taking limits.
Hence we can forget the DG ring structure,
and just look at equation (
4.5
) in the category of graded
abelian groups.

By definition we have

\operatorname{R}(Y,B)=\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B
,

\operatorname{R}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\bigoplus_{p}\operatorname{R}^{p}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
,
and

\operatorname{R}^{p}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(Y([p]),\mathbb{Z})
.
The DG ring
B
decomposes, as a graded abelian group, into

B=\bigoplus_{k}B^{k}
.
Because the set
Y([p])
finite, there is a canonical isomorphism of
abelian groups

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(Y([p]),\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B^{k}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(Y([p]),B^{k}).

All this holds for the
Y_{j}
too. Therefore we can replace
(
4.5
) with the homomorphism

(4.6)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(Y([p]),B^{k})\to\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{lim}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(Y_{j}([p]),B^{k}),

where limit is in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ab}}
. But this limit coincides, on underlying sets, with
the limit in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
. And in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
the map (
4.6
)
is an isomorphism by formula (
4.3
).
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
A
and
B
be DG rings. Then for any

there is canonical

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}\bigl(X_{j},\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B))

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}
.

Proof.

Recall that
X_{j}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q_{j}}
.
It is a standard fact that for any simplicial set
Z
and any

there is a
canonical bijection

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{p},Z)\cong Z_{p}
.
Apply this to the simplicial set

and

.
∎

Here is our key technical result.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
A
and
B
be DG rings, and let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i}
be a horn in

\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
. Then there is a canonical bijection

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)).

Proof.

We have this sequence of canonical bijections:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong^{(1)}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}\bigl(\underset{j\in J}{\operatorname{colim}}\mspace{3mu}X_{j},\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong^{(2)}\displaystyle\lim_{j\in J}\mspace{3mu}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}}\bigl(X_{j},\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong^{(3)}\displaystyle\lim_{j\in J}\mspace{3mu}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B))

\displaystyle\cong^{(4)}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\displaystyle\lim_{j\in J}\mspace{3mu}\operatorname{Cyl}_{q_{j}}(B))

\displaystyle\cong^{(5)}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{q}_{i},B)).

The isomorphism
\cong^{(1)}
is by Corollary
2.12
.
The isomorphism
\cong^{(2)}
is by the definition of a
colimit in the category
\operatorname{\mathsf{SSet}}
.
The isomorphism
\cong^{(3)}
is by Lemma
4.7
.
The isomorphism
\cong^{(4)}
is by the definition of a
limit in the category

\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}
.
And the isomorphism
\cong^{(5)}
is by Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Recall that a horn in a simplicial set
Z
is a map of simplicial sets

\sigma:\Lambda^{q}_{i}\to Z
from a horn
\Lambda^{q}_{i}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
.
The next corollary is merely a reformulation of Theorem
4.8
.

Corollary 4.9
.

There is a canonical bijection between horns

\sigma:\Lambda^{q}_{i}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(A,\operatorname{Cyl}(B))

and DG ring homomorphisms

f:A\to\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},B).

Lemma 4.10
.

Let

\sigma:\Lambda^{q}_{i}\to\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
be the inclusion of a horn.
The the induced DG ring homomorphism

v:\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},B)\to\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},B)

is a surjective quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

In view of Definition
3.7
, and because
the DG rings
\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},\mathbb{Z})
and

\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},\mathbb{Z})
are semi-free as DG
\mathbb{Z}
-modules,
we may assume that
B=\mathbb{Z}
.

Both simplicial sets
\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q}
and
\Lambda^{q}_{i}
are contractible, and hence the DG
ring homomorphisms

\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},\mathbb{Z})
and

\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},\mathbb{Z})

are quasi-isomorphisms. Therefore
v
is a quasi-isomorphism.

According to Proposition
3.19
we can replace the DG ring homomorphism

v:\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},\mathbb{Z})

with the isomorphic

w:\operatorname{R}((\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q})^{\mathrm{nd}},\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{R}((\Lambda^{q}_{i})^{\mathrm{nd}},\mathbb{Z}).

As a homomorphism of graded abelian groups, this is

w^{\prime}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}((\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q})^{\mathrm{nd}},\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}((\Lambda^{q}_{i})^{\mathrm{nd}},\mathbb{Z}).

Since

\sigma^{\prime}:(\Lambda^{q}_{i})^{\mathrm{nd}}\to(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q})^{\mathrm{nd}}

is an injective map of graded sets, and

w^{\prime}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Set}}}(\sigma^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
,
it follows that
w^{\prime}
is a surjective homomorphism of graded abelian groups.
∎

At last we can prove Theorem
0.2

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\tilde{A}
be a semi-free DG ring, and let
B

Then the simplicial set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{DGRng}}}(\tilde{A},\operatorname{Cyl}(B))

4.9
,
\sigma

to a DG ring homomorphism

f:\tilde{A}\to\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},B)

By Lemma
4.10
the DG ring homomorphism

v:\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},B)\to\operatorname{N}(\Lambda^{q}_{i},B)

is a surjective quasi-isomorphism.
Theorem
1.7
says that there is a DG ring homomorphism

f^{\prime}:\tilde{A}\to\operatorname{N}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{q},B)

lifting
f
.

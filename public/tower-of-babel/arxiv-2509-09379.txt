Abstract
. In this paper, the Hadamard-Bergman convolution and Banach algebra structure by the Duhamel product on Hardy-Carleson type tent spaces was investigated. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
Define
H(\mathbb{D})
as the set of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
.
For
0<p<\infty
, let
H^{p}
denote the Hardy space of all analytic functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}}=\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}dt<\infty.

The Bergman space
A^{p}
consists of all analytic functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
is the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\zeta>\frac{1}{2}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{T}
, the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
.
The non-tangential approach region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is defined by

\Gamma(\eta)=\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\eta|<\zeta(1-|z|^{2})\right\}.

For
0<p<\infty
, the tent space
T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
with

\|f\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}=\operatorname{esssup}_{\eta\in\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{u\in\Gamma(\eta)}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)<\infty,

where

S(re^{i\theta})=\left\{\lambda e^{it}:|t-\theta|\leq\frac{1-r}{2},1-\lambda\leq 1-r\right\}

for
re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
and
S(0)=\mathbb{D}
.
Denote
T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\cap H(\mathbb{D})
by
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
, called the Hardy-Carleson type tent space or the analytic tent space. Tent spaces were initially introduced by Coifman, Meyer and Stein in
[
4
]
to address problems in harmonic analysis. They offered a general framework for examining questions concerning significant spaces, such as Bergman spaces and Hardy spaces.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}z^{n}
. The Hadamard product
f*g
of functions
f
and
g
is defined as

(f*g)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}d_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is a well-established fact that for
f\in H^{1}
and
g\in H^{q}(1\leq q<\infty)
,

\lVert f*g\rVert_{H^{q}}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}}\lVert g\rVert_{H^{q}}.

Nevertheless, when
f\in A^{1}
and
g\in A^{q}(1\leq q<\infty)
, the inequality

\lVert f*g\rVert_{A^{q}}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{A^{1}}\lVert g\rVert_{A^{q}}

is not satisfied.
Karapetyants and Samko
[
10
]
introduced a modified form of the Hadamard product:

f\widetilde{*}g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}d_{n}}{n+1}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

which, in essence, represents a convolution in the sense that

\mathbb{K}_{g}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(w)f(\overline{w}z)dA(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}c_{n}z^{n},

where
\mu_{n}=\frac{d_{n}}{n+1}
and
\mathbb{K}_{g}
is denoted as the Hadamard–Bergman convolution operator with kernel
g
.
Moreover, in the Bergman space, the inequality

\|\mathbb{K}_{g}f\|_{A^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{A^{p}}\|g\|_{A^{1}}

is valid.
A natural question arises: does the above inequality hold in the Hardy-Carleson type tent space
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
? In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to this question, establishing that

\|\mathbb{K}_{g}f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g\|_{A^{1}}.

As defined by Wigley (see
[
19
]
), for analytic functions
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{D}
, the Duhamel product
f\circledast g
is given by

(f\circledast g)(z)=\frac{d}{dz}\int_{0}^{z}f(z-s)g(s)ds=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(z-s)g(s)ds+f(0)g(z).

This product has multiple applications such as operational calculus and boundary value problems.
Wigley studied algebraic structures of analytic functions and maximal ideals in holomorphic function spaces and Hardy spaces
H^{p}
(
p\geq 1
) (see
[
19
,
20
]
).
The algebraic structure from the Duhamel product has been explored in different spaces. E.g.,
[
8
]
for the Wiener algebra,
[
9
]
for the space
C^{(n)}(\mathbb{D})
,
[
7
]
for the Bergman space
A^{p}
.
For more Duhamel product results, see
[
24
,
25
]
and the references therein.

For
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
is given by

\mathcal{C}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The integral
form of
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathcal{C}(f)(z)=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)\frac{1}{1-\zeta}d\zeta=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}dt.

Many researchers have explored the Cesàro operator on some analytic function spaces. For more details, see
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. Recently, Galanopoulos, Girela and Merchán, as cited in
[
6
]
, introduced the Cesàro-like operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
.
For a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1)
, the Cesàro-like operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is defined on
H(\mathbb{D})
as follows:

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\mu_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}d\mu(t),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
\mu_{n}
stands for the moment of order
n
of
\mu
, that is,
\mu_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}d\mu(t)
.
They studied the action of the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
on distinct spaces of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, such as the Hardy spaces
H^{p}
, the weighted Bergman spaces
A_{\alpha}^{p}
,
BMOA
(bounded mean oscillation of analytic functions), and the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
. Subsequently, Bao etc.
[
1
]
investigated the range of Cesàro-like operator acting on the space
H^{\infty}
, which consists of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. To achieve this, they described the characterizations of Carleson type measures on the interval
[0,1)
. In particular, they answered an open question that was originally posed in
[
6
]
.
The Cesàro-like operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
has attracted a great deal of interest among numerous scholars.
See
[
1
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
21
]
and the references therein for more details.

In this paper, we will investigate the Hadamard-Bergman convolution on Hardy-Carleson type tent spaces. Moreover, we give a Banach algebra structure by the Duhamel product for the Hardy-Carleson type tent space
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. Finally, we characterize the boundedness and compactness of the Cesàro-like operators
C_{\mu}
on the space
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. Specifically, we prove that
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is bounded (compact) on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
\mu
is a Carleson measure (vanishing Carleson measure).

In this paper, we denote
A\lesssim B
to indicate the existence of a positive constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. Moreover,
A\asymp B
means that both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
are valid.

2.
The Hadamard-Bergman convolution operators

In this section, we describe the Hadamard-Bergman convolution on Hardy-Carleson type tent spaces. To this end, we need some notations and lemmas.
For any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, let (see
[
28
]
)

\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is obvious that
\varphi_{a}(z)
is a Möbius mapping that interchanges the points
0
and
a
.
Let
\varphi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. The composition operator
C_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi
is defined by (see
[
5
]
)

C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
22
, Lemma 2.6]

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
.
A function
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if for each (or some)
t>0
,

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)<\infty.

According to
[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
, we can obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. If
t\geq\frac{1}{p}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}}{(|1-|\varphi_{a}(u)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})}{|1-\overline{u}\varphi(z)|^{\alpha+2+pt}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

then
C_{\varphi}
is a bounded operator on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
12
, Lemma 2.5]

For
s>-1,r,t\geq 0
and
r+t-s>2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|\xi|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{\xi}z|^{r}|1-\overline{\xi}w|^{t}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)

\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{r+t-s-2}},&\text{if }&r-s,t-s<2\\
&\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{r-s-2}|1-\overline{z}w|^{t}},&\text{if }&t-s<2<r-s\\
&\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{r-s-2}|1-\overline{z}w|^{t}}+\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t-s-2}|1-\overline{z}w|^{r}},&\text{if }&r-s,t-s>2.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
,
t>\frac{1}{p}
and
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.
Then

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f(\overline{w}z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(z)=\overline{w}z
. It is easy to check that
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is analytic. Using Lemma
2.2
, if

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}}{(|1-|\varphi_{a}(u)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})}{|1-\overline{u}\varphi(z)|^{\alpha+2+pt}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f(\varphi(z))|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z).

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
,

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f(\overline{w}z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}.

So, we only need to prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}}{(|1-|\varphi_{a}(u)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})}{|1-\overline{u}\varphi(z)|^{\alpha+2+pt}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Using Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}}{(|1-|\varphi_{a}(u)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})}{|1-\overline{u}\varphi(z)|^{\alpha+2+pt}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t}|1-\overline{a}u|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}|1-\overline{uw}z|^{\alpha+2+pt}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|u|^{2})^{t-\frac{1}{p}}|1-\overline{a}u|^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}|1-\overline{uw}z|^{\alpha+2+pt}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{u,a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{t-\frac{1}{p}}|1-\overline{a}u|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{(1-|uw|^{2})^{t-\frac{1}{p}}|1-\overline{a}uw|^{\frac{2}{p}}}<\infty,

as desired. Here we used the assumption that
\alpha>-1
and
t>\frac{1}{p}
. The proof is complete.
∎

We now state and demonstrate the main result in this section.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
\alpha>-1
,
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and
g\in A^{1}
. Then

\|\mathbb{K}_{g}f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g\|_{A^{1}}.

Proof.

Using Minkowski’s inequality and Lemma
2.4
, for
t>\frac{1}{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{K}_{g}f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|\mathbb{K}_{g}f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\overline{w}z)||g(w)|dA(w)\right)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(w)|\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|f(\overline{w}z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(w)|dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g\|_{A^{1}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Duhamel product

In this section, we give a Banach algebra structure by the Duhamel product for Hardy-Carleson type tent spaces.
Therefore, we need some simple formulas for Duhamel products
\circledast
.

\displaystyle(f\circledast g)(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(z-s)g(s)ds+f(0)g(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}f(z-s)g^{\prime}(s)ds+f(z)g(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}g^{\prime}(z-s)f(s)ds+g(0)f(z)=(g\circledast f)(z).

It is obvious that Duhamel product is a commutative product.

If the integral line segment
[0,z]
is halved, then integration by parts leads to

(1)

\begin{split}(f\circledast g)(z)&=\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}f(z-s)g^{\prime}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}g(z-s)f^{\prime}(s)ds\\
&\quad+f(z)g(0)+g(z)f(0)-f\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)g\left(\frac{z}{2}\right).\end{split}

We need the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
22
, Lemma 2.7]

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. Then

|f^{(n)}(z)|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+n}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 3.2
.

From Lemma
3.1
, if
K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|f(z)|\leq C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\penalty\ \penalty\ {\text{a}nd}\penalty\ \penalty\ |f^{\prime}(z)|\leq C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}

for all
z\in K
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
, and
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. Then

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\leq C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}

for any
t>0
.

Proof.

Let
z=re^{i\theta}
and
s=\rho e^{i\theta}
,
0\leq\rho\leq\frac{r}{2}
. Then,

\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|=\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}|f(\rho e^{i\theta})|d\rho\leq\int_{0}^{r}|f(\rho e^{i\theta})|d\rho.

Applying Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{d\rho}{(1-\rho)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}{1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\right),&1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}\neq 0\\
\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}|\ln(1-r)|,&1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}=0.\end{cases}

Hence, when
1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}\neq 0
, for any
t>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}.

When
1-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}=0
, for any
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|\ln(1-|z|^{2})|^{p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p},

as desired. The proof is complete.
∎

Now, we state and prove our main result in this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. The Hardy-Carleson type tent space
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
is a unital (the unit here is the constant function
1
) commutative Banach algebra with respect to Duhamel product
\circledast
. Denote the algebras as
(AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha),\circledast)
.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in AT^{\infty}_{p}(\alpha)
.
Using
(\ref{'})
and Remark
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle|(f\circledast g)(z)|\leq

\displaystyle C\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|g(z-s)||ds|

\displaystyle+C\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}|f(z)|+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}|g(z)|+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

When
1\leq p<\infty
, using Lemma
2.1
and Minkowski’s inequality, for any
t>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(f\circledast g)\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|(f\circledast g)(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|g(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}.

Applying Lemma
3.3
, we have

\|(f\circledast g)\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\leq C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

The proof is complete.

∎

Next we establish a Young type property for the Duhamel convolution operator with analytic symbol
f
:

\mathfrak{D}_{f}g(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(z-s)g(s)+f(0)g(z),\quad g\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
and
f\in AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
. Then

(i)

If
1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}<0
, then
\mathfrak{D}_{f}\in\mathscr{B}(AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha))
for any
p<\frac{\alpha+2}{\frac{\beta+2}{q}-1}
.

(ii)

If
1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}\geq 0
, then
\mathfrak{D}_{f}\in\mathscr{B}(AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha))
for all
p\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
f\in AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)
and
g\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. Using
(\ref{'})
and Remark
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle|(f\circledast g)(z)|\leq

\displaystyle C\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|g(z-s)||ds|

\displaystyle+C\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}|f(z)|+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}|g(z)|+C\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{d\rho}{(1-\rho)^{\frac{\beta+2}{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}\left(\frac{1-(1-r)^{1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}}}{1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}}\right),&1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}\neq 0,\\
\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}|\ln(1-r)|,&1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}=0.\end{cases}

Therefore, when
1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}\neq 0
and
p\left(1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}\right)+\alpha+1>-1
, for any
t>0
, by Lemma
2.1
or Lemma 3.10 in
[
28
]
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p\left(1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}\right)+\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{p}.

When
1-\frac{\beta+2}{q}=0
, for any
t>0
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}|\ln(1-|z|^{2})|^{p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{p}.

Repeating the steps of the proof of Theorem
3.4
, for any
t>0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{D}_{f}g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle C\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|f(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle+C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{t+1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{z}{2}}|g(z-s)||ds|\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)

\displaystyle+C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}^{p}\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}.

Applying Lemma
3.3
, it follows that

\|\mathfrak{D}_{f}g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\leq C\|f\|_{AT_{q}^{\infty}(\beta)}\|g\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

∎

4.
Boundedness and compactness of
C_{\mu}:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)

To prove the main result in this section, we need some notations and lemmas.

Let
\mu
denote a positive Borel measure defined on
\mathbb{D}
and
s>0
. The measure
\mu
is referred to as an
s
-Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
provided that (see
[
2
]
)

\|\mu\|_{s}=\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|^{s}}<\infty.

Here,

S(I)=\left\{z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}}:1-|I|\leq r<1,e^{i\theta}\in I\right\}.

In particular, the
1
-Carleson measure coincides with the classical Carleson measure.

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1)
can be regarded as a Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
by establishing an identification with the measure
\overline{\mu}
. The measure
\overline{\mu}
is defined as follows: for every Borel subset
E
of
\mathbb{D}
,

\overline{\mu}(E)=\mu(E\cap[0,1))

Consequently, the measure
\mu
is an
s
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that (see
[
1
]
)

\mu([t,1))\leq C(1-t)^{s},\quad 0\leq t<1.

The measure
\mu
is a vanishing
s
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
if

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1}\frac{\mu([t,1))}{(1-t)^{s}}=0.

The following characterization of Carleson measures on
[0,1)
is due to Bao et al. (see
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
r>0
,
0\leq c<s<\infty
and
\mu
is a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mu
is a
s
-Carleson measure;

(ii)

\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}(1-|b|t)^{s+r-c}}d\mu(t)<\infty;

(iii)

\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}|1-bt|^{s+r-c}}d\mu(t)<\infty.

For vanishing Carleson measures on
[0,1)
, we have the following result (see
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
or
[
21
, Lemma 2.3]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
r>0
,
0\leq c<s<\infty
and
\mu
is a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mu
is a vanishing
s
-Carleson measure;

(ii)

\lim_{|b|\to 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}(1-|b|t)^{s+r-c}}d\mu(t)=0;

(iii)

\lim_{|b|\to 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}|1-bt|^{s+r-c}}d\mu(t)=0.

The next two lemmas are very useful in the proof of our main results in this paper.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
26
, Proposition 3.1]

Let
w,a\in\mathbb{D}
. For
r>0
and
t>0
, let

I_{w,a}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{\left|1-\overline{w}e^{i\theta}\right|^{t}\left|1-\overline{a}e^{i\theta}\right|^{r}}d\theta.

Then the following results hold:

(i)

When
t>1
and
r>1
,

I_{w,a}\asymp\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t-1}|1-w\bar{a}|^{r}}+\frac{1}{(1-|a|^{2})^{r-1}|1-w\bar{a}|^{t}}.

(ii)

When
t>1=r
,

I_{w,a}\asymp\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t-1}|1-\overline{w}a|}+\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}a|^{t}}\log\frac{e}{1-|\varphi_{w}(a)|^{2}}.

Lemma 4.4
.

[
23
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
\delta>-1
,
c>0
,
0\leq\rho<1
. Then

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{\delta}}{(1-\rho r)^{\delta+c+1}}dr\asymp\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{c}}.

The following lemma gives an equivalent characterization for functions in
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and can be found in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
.
Then
g\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if

\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(w)|^{2})dA(w)<\infty.

The following lemma is useful for studying compactness. Its proof is similar to Proposition 3.11 in
[
5
]
, so details are omitted.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. Let
T:AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)\to AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
be a bounded linear operator. Then
T
is compact if and only if for any bounded sequence
\{f_{j}\}
in
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
which converges to zero uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\|Tf_{j}\right\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=0.

Now, we are in a position to state and prove the main results of this section. Specifically, we will characterize the boundedness and compactness of the Cesàro-like operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
which maps from
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
to
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. The Cesàro-like operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is bounded on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
\mu
is a Carleson measure.

Proof.

First we assume that
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is bounded on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. For
0<\rho<1
, let

f_{\rho}(z)=\frac{(1-\rho)}{(1-\rho z)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

After calculation, we see that
f_{\rho}\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and
\sup_{0<\rho<1}\left\|f_{\rho}\right\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim 1
. This implies that
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{\rho})\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.
Using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\left|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\left(f_{\rho}\right)(\rho)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}},\quad 0<\rho<1.

Then, for
\frac{1}{2}<\rho<1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\rho)}{(1-t\rho)\left(1-t\rho^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}d\mu(t)\right|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{\rho}^{1}\frac{(1-\rho)}{(1-t\rho)\left(1-t\rho^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}d\mu(t)\gtrsim\frac{\mu([\rho,1))}{(1-\rho)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}},

which implies that
\mu([\rho,1))\lesssim 1-\rho

for all
\frac{1}{2}<\rho<1
. Hence,
\mu
is a Carleson measure.

Conversely, assume that
\mu
is a Carleson measure.
Let
f\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
f(0)=0
. By Lemma
3.1
,
we get

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)^{\prime}(z)\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{tf^{\prime}(tz)}{1-tz}+\frac{tf(tz)}{(1-tz)^{2}}d\mu(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|tf^{\prime}(tz)\right|}{|1-tz|}d\mu(t)+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|tf(tz)|}{|1-tz|^{2}}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|1-tz|(1-t|z|)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|1-tz|^{2}(1-t|z|)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|1-tz|(1-t|z|)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}.

Using Lemma
4.5
and Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\asymp\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|1-tz|(1-t|z|)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}\right)^{p}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}}{|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}dA(z)}{|1-tz|^{p}(1-t|z|)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}.

To complete the proof, it suffices to prove

\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}dA(z)}{|1-tz|^{p}(1-t|z|)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}<\infty.

Case
p>1
.

Using the polar coordinate formula and Lemma
4.3
(i), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}}{|1-tz|^{p}(1-t|z|)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|1-tre^{i\theta}|^{p}|1-\overline{b}re^{i\theta}|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}}\left(\frac{1}{(1-tr)^{p-1}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-|b|r)|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{p}}\right)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{2p+\alpha+1}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}dr+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}(1-|b|r)|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{p}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{2}.

Using the fact that

(2)

\displaystyle(x+y)^{p}\leqslant\begin{cases}x^{p}+y^{p},&0<p<1,\\
2^{p-1}(x^{p}+y^{p}),&p\geq 1\end{cases},\quad x,y>0,

we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}dA(z)}{|1-tz|^{p}(1-t|z|)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}(J_{1}+J_{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{1}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}+\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}.

It is easy to see that
1-r<1-tr
,
1-t<1-tr
and
|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|\geq 1-t|b|r
.
Therefore, we can choose a positive real number
\epsilon
that satisfies

\frac{p-1}{p}<\epsilon<1.

By the choice of
\epsilon
, it is easy to check that
p\epsilon-p>-1
and
2-(p\epsilon-p)-1=1-p\epsilon+p>0
.
Using Lemma
4.4
, we get

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{2p+\alpha+1}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-tr)^{p}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p\epsilon}(1-tr)^{p-p\epsilon}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p\epsilon-p}}{(1-t|b|r)^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p\epsilon}(1-t|b|)^{1-p\epsilon+p}}.

Since
\mu
is a Carleson measure, using Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{1}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(1-t)^{\epsilon}(1-t|b|)^{\frac{1}{p}-\epsilon+1}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}\lesssim 1.

Subsequently, we focus our efforts on the estimation of
J_{2}
.

Let
0<\delta<1

and

0<\tau<\frac{1}{p}
. We may choose
\delta
and
\tau
such that
\frac{1}{p}<\delta+\tau<\frac{p+1}{p}
. Notice that
1-r<1-|b|r
and
1-|b|<1-|b|r
. By the choices of
\delta
and
\tau
, it is easy to see that
p(\delta+\tau)-2>-1
and
p-[p(\delta+\tau)-2]-1=p-p(\delta+\tau)+1>0
. Using Lemma
4.4
, it follows that

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}(1-|b|r)|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{p}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-t)^{p\delta}(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2-p\delta}(1-|b|)^{p\tau}(1-|b|r)^{1-p\tau}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{p}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p\delta}(1-|b|)^{p\tau}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+3-p\delta-p\tau}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{p}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p\delta}(1-|b|)^{p\tau}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p(\delta+\tau)-2}}{(1-t|b|r)^{p}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p\delta}(1-|b|)^{p\tau}(1-t|b|)^{p-p(\delta+\tau)+1}}.

Since
\mu
is a Carleson measure, using Lemma
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{\frac{1}{p}-\tau}}{(1-t)^{\delta}(1-t|b|)^{1-(\delta+\tau)+\frac{1}{p}}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}\lesssim 1.

Therefore,

\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

Case
p=1
.

Using Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|1-tre^{i\theta}|^{p}|1-\overline{b}re^{i\theta}|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}}\left(\frac{1}{(1-|b|r)|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|}+\frac{1}{|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}\log\frac{e}{1-|\varphi_{br}(tr)|^{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}(1-|b|r)|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|}dr+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}\log\frac{e}{1-|\varphi_{br}(tr)|^{2}}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{3}+J_{4}.

The estimation of
J_{3}
follows the same procedure as that of
J_{1}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{3}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}\lesssim 1.

Finally, we estimate
J_{4}
.
Let
0<d<\frac{1}{4}
. It is obvious that

(1-|\varphi_{br}(tr)|^{2})^{d}\log\frac{e}{1-|\varphi_{br}(tr)|^{2}}\lesssim 1.

Since
2d+\frac{1}{2}<1
, we may choose a positive real number
\gamma
such that
2d+\frac{1}{2}<\gamma<1
. This yields that
\gamma-1-2d>-1
and
2-2d-(\gamma-1-2d)-1=2-\gamma>0
. Bear in mind that
1-t<1-tr
,
1-r<1-tr
and
1-r<1-|b|r
. Using Lemma
4.4
, it follows that

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}}\log\frac{e}{1-|\varphi_{br}(tr)|^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{br}(tr)|^{2})^{d}}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2+d}(1-|b|r)^{d}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2-2d}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p+\alpha+1}}{(1-tr)^{p+\alpha+2+d-\gamma}(1-t)^{\gamma}(1-r)^{d}|1-t\overline{b}r^{2}|^{2-2d}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{\gamma-1-2d}}{(1-t|b|r)^{2-2d}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\gamma}(1-t|b|)^{2-\gamma}}.

Since
\mu
is a Carleson measure, using Lemma
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{4}^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(1-t)^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}(1-t|b|)^{1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\gamma}{p}}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}\lesssim 1.

Hence,

\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)},

which implies that
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is bounded on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. The Cesàro-like operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is compact on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
if and only if
\mu
is a vanishing Carleson measure.

Proof.

First we assume that
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is compact on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
. For
\frac{1}{2}<\rho<1
, let

f_{\rho}(z)=\frac{(1-\rho)}{(1-\rho z)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We see that
f_{\rho}\in AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
and
\sup_{\frac{1}{2}<\rho<1}\|f_{\rho}\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim 1
.
Furthermore,
f_{\rho}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
\rho\to 1
.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is compact on
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
, by virtue of Lemma
4.6
, it follows that

\lim_{\rho\to 1}\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{\rho})\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=0.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{\rho})(z)|\lesssim\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{\rho})\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\quad\rho\to 1.

Thus, for
\frac{1}{2}<\rho<1
, by the proof of Theorem
4.7
we have

\displaystyle(1-\rho)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{\rho})(\rho)|\gtrsim\frac{\mu([\rho,1))}{1-\rho},

which implies that

\frac{\mu([\rho,1))}{1-\rho}\lesssim\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{\rho})\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}.

Combining with (
3
), we get that
\mu
is a vanishing Carleson measure.

Conversely, suppose
\mu
is a vanishing Carleson measure. Consider a bounded sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
that converges uniformly to 0 on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
. We may assume without loss of generality that
f_{j}(0)=0
for all
j\geq 1
and
\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}\lesssim 1
. Using Lemma
4.6
, we need to establish that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|C_{\mu}(f_{j})\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=0.

Since
\mu
is a vanishing Carleson measure, for any
\epsilon>0
, using Lemma
4.2
, we obtain that there exists a
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}(1-t|b|)^{1+r-c}}d\mu(t)<\epsilon\quad\text{ for all }\quad\delta<|b|<1,

where
r>0
and
0\leq c<1
.
Observe that Lemma
4.2
also indicates that
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{c}}d\mu(t)<\infty
. As a result, there exists a
t_{0}
with
0<t_{0}<1
for which

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{c}}d\mu(t)<\epsilon.

Using Lemma
4.5
, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{j})\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{j})^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta<|b|<1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{j})^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{1}+H_{2}.

For
H_{2}
, by Theorem
4.7
, we obtain that

H_{2}\lesssim\sup_{\delta<|b|<1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}(1-t|b|)^{1+r-c}}d\mu(t)<\epsilon

for some
r>0
and
0\leq c<1
.
Furthermore, using (
2
), we get

\displaystyle H_{1}=

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{j})^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{|f_{j}(tz)|}{|1-tz|^{2}}+\frac{|f_{j}^{\prime}(tz)|}{|1-tz|}\right)d\mu(t)\right)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(\frac{|f_{j}(tz)|}{|1-tz|^{2}}+\frac{|f_{j}^{\prime}(tz)|}{|1-tz|}\right)d\mu(t)\right)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle+\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\left(\frac{|f_{j}(tz)|}{|1-tz|^{2}}+\frac{|f_{j}^{\prime}(tz)|}{|1-tz|}\right)d\mu(t)\right)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z).

By virtue of the Cauchy integral theorem, it can be deduced that the sequence
\{f_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
converges uniformly to
0
on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(\frac{|f_{j}(tz)|}{|1-tz|^{2}}+\frac{|f_{j}^{\prime}(tz)|}{|1-tz|}\right)d\mu(t)\right)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{|w|\leq t_{0}}(|f_{j}(w)|+|f_{j}^{\prime}(w)|)\to 0,\quad j\to\infty.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.7
, we can also show that

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\left(\frac{|f_{j}(tz)|}{|1-tz|^{2}}+\frac{|f_{j}^{\prime}(tz)|}{|1-tz|}\right)d\mu(t)\right)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha}(1-|\varphi_{b}(z)|^{2})dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}(1-|b|^{2})\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|1-tz|(1-t|z|)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+1}}\right)^{p}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}}{|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}(1-|b|^{2})\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\alpha+1}dA(z)}{|1-tz|^{p}(1-t|z|)^{p+\alpha+2}|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}(1-t|b|)^{1+r-c}}d\mu(t)\right)^{p}

for some
r>0
and
0\leq c<1
. By (
4
), we deduce that

\sup_{|b|\leq\delta}\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\frac{(1-|b|)^{r}}{(1-t)^{c}(1-t|b|)^{1+r-c}}d\mu(t)\lesssim\int_{t_{0}}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{c}}d\mu(t)<\epsilon.

Therefore,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f_{j})\|_{AT_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=0.

The proof is complete.

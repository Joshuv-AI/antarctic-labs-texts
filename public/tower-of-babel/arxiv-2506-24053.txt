We next study the infinite divisibility of the GCD tensor.

We now turn our attention to the determinant of the GCD tensor. The study of hyperdeterminants of tensors was initiated by Cayley

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}

\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}=\mathcal{A}\times_{2}x^{T}\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}x^{T}=\left(\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Now define the multivariate homogeneous polynomials of degree

F_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}):=\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ki_{2}\ldots i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}\hbox{ for }1\leq k\leq n.

Equipped with these

Definition 1.6

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}

From the above definition, it is easy to verify that the tensor determinant generalizes the well-known matrix determinant and symmetric hyperdeterminant studied by Qi

]

We now obtain the determinant of the GCD tensor. To state our next main result, we first recall the following basic definition.

A set

Our final main result gives the determinant of the GCD tensor

Theorem 1.8

Let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}\}

In this section, we prove two of our main results – Theorems

We begin by showing that

(2.2)

We next turn our attention to the Schur (Hadamard) product of GCD tensors. For any two tensors
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots{i_{m}}}),\mathcal{B}=(b_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots{i_{m}}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}

\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}

(\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{B})_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots{i_{m}}}:=a_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots{i_{m}}}\cdot b_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots{i_{m}}}.

(ii)

A function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be multiplicative if
f(xy)=f(x)f(y)
whenever
\GCD(x,y)=1
.

(iii)

The Dirichlet convolution of two multiplicative functions
f
and
g
is denoted and defined as

(f*g)(k):=\sum\limits_{d|k}f(d)g\left(\frac{k}{d}\right).

(iii)

The
Möbius function

\mu:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,\pm 1\}
is defined by

\mu(k)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }n=1,\\
(-1)^{l},&\text{ if $k$ is the product $l$ distinct primes for $l\geq 1$},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly, the Möbius function is multiplicative. It is well known that for any multiplicative function
g
,
\sum\limits_{d|p}(g*\mu)(d)=g(p).

We now prove that for any set
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
of distinct positive integers and for any multiplicative map
g
with all
(g*\mu)(p)>0
, the tensor
(g(\GCD(s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\ldots,s_{i_{m}})))\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
is strongly CP.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}\}
be a set of distinct positive integers and
\mathcal{T}_{[S]}=(t_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}})
be an
m
th-order
n
-dimensional GCD tensor on
S
. Let
g
be a multiplicative map with
(g*\mu)(p)>0
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the tensor
g[\mathcal{T}_{[S]}]=(g(t_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}))\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
is strongly CP.

Proof.

Let
F=\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{l}\}
be a factor-closed set containing
S.
Define the matrix
E=[E_{1},\ldots,E_{l}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l}
as in (
2.1
), where
E_{1},\ldots,E_{l}
are the columns of
E
. Then
{\rm span}\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{l}\}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
(g*\mu)(p)>0
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
, to complete the proof it suffices to show that

g[\mathcal{T}_{[S]}]=\sum_{k=1}^{l}(g*\mu)(f_{k})(E_{k})^{\otimes m}.

Since
\sum\limits_{d|p}(g*\mu)(d)=g(p)
, by a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}(g*\mu)(f_{k})(E_{k})^{\otimes m}\right)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}=g(\GCD(s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\ldots,s_{i_{m}})),

for all
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n
. Thus
g[\mathcal{T}_{[S]}]
is strongly CP.
∎

As an application of the above result, we now have our proof of the second main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
r
be a positive real number and define
g(p):=p^{r}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
g
is multiplicative and
(g*\mu)(p)\geq p^{r}>0
. By Theorem
2.9
,
g[\mathcal{T}_{[S]}]
is strongly CP. Thus
\mathcal{T}^{\circ r}_{[S]}
is strongly CP for all
r>0
.
∎

3.
Determinant of GCD the Tensor

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem 1.8. This requires the notion of the general product of two tensors and some preliminary results.
In 2013, Shao
[
37
]
introduced the notion of the general product (or composite) of two
n
-dimensional tensors.

Definition 3.1
.

[
37
, Definition 1.1]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[k,n]}
. The general product
\mathcal{A}\cdot\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is an
((m-1)(k-1)+1)
th-order
n
-dimensional tensor, and is defined elementwise:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\cdot\mathcal{B})_{i_{1}\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{(m-1)}}=\sum_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1}j_{2}\ldots j_{m}}b_{j_{2}\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{j_{m}\alpha_{(m-1)}},

where
i_{1}\in[n],\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}\in[n]^{k-1}=[n]\times\cdots\times[n]
and
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

The general product of tensors satisfies many interesting properties, including: (i) The associative law. (ii) When the two tensors are matrices (or one is a matrix and the other is a vector), the general tensor product coincides with the usual matrix product.

We now recall four preliminary results involving the general product which are crucial to prove Theorem
1.8
. The first result is a formula of Shao–Shan–Zhang to derive the determinant of the general product of two tensors.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
38
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[k,n]},
where
m,k\geq 2.
Then,

\det(\mathcal{A}\cdot\mathcal{B})=\det(\mathcal{A})^{(k-1)^{(n-1)}}\det(\mathcal{B})^{(m-1)^{n}}.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
37
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
and
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Then

\det(Q_{1}\cdot\mathcal{A}\cdot Q_{2})=\det(Q_{1}\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot Q_{2})\det(\mathcal{A}),

where
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
is the identity tensor.

The next lemma gives a formula to compute the determinant of a diagonal tensor.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
18
, Proposition 5.1]
,
[
38
, Example 3.3]
)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
m
th-order
n
-dimensional diagonal tensor with the
i
th diagonal element of
\mathcal{A}
is
a_{ii\ldots i}=d_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\det(\mathcal{A})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i})^{(m-1)^{(n-1)}}.

The next lemma gives a relationship between the
k
-mode product of a tensor
\mathcal{A}
with a matrix
Q
and the general product of
\mathcal{A}
with
Q
.

Lemma 3.5
.

[
37
, Remark 2.3]

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
and
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Then

\mathcal{A}\times_{1}Q\times_{2}Q\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}Q=Q\cdot\mathcal{A}\cdot Q^{T}.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is congruent to
\mathcal{B}
if there exists
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that
\mathcal{B}=Q\cdot\mathcal{A}\cdot Q^{T}
. If
Q
is a permutation matrix, then we say
\mathcal{A}
is permutationally congruent to
\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
be an ordered set of distinct positive integers and let
S^{\prime}=\{s_{i_{1}},\ldots,s_{i_{n}}\}
be a rearrangement of the elements of
S
. Then
\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]}
is permutationally congruent to
\mathcal{T}_{[S]}.
Moreover,
\det{(\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]})}=\det{(\mathcal{T}_{[S]})}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be the permutation on the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
such that
S^{\prime}=\{s_{\sigma({1})},\ldots,s_{\sigma({n})}\}
and let
P=P_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be the corresponding permutation matrix. We claim that
\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]}=P\cdot\mathcal{T}_{[S]}\cdot P^{T}
. Notice that for all
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}_{[S]}\times_{1}P\times_{2}P\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}P)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}=1}^{n}t_{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}p_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots p_{i_{m}j_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{\sigma(i_{1})\ldots\sigma(i_{m})}~(\because p_{ij}=1\Leftrightarrow j=\sigma(i))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\GCD(s_{\sigma(i_{1})},\ldots,s_{\sigma(i_{m})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]})_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}.

Thus,
\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]}=\mathcal{T}_{[S]}\times_{1}P\times_{2}P\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}P
. By Lemma
3.5
,
\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]}=P\cdot\mathcal{T}_{[S]}\cdot P^{T}
.

We next prove the second part of the assertion. By Lemma
3.3
,

\det(\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]})=\det(P\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot P^{T})\det(\mathcal{T}_{[S]}).

Since
P
is a permutation matrix,
P\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot P^{T}=\mathcal{I}
. Thus
\det(P\cdot\mathcal{I}\cdot P^{T})=\det(\mathcal{I})=1
and
\det(\mathcal{T}_{[S^{\prime}]})=\det(\mathcal{T}_{[S]})
.
∎

The next lemma gives an important factorization of the GCD tensor, which is crucial to obtain the determinant.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}\}
be a set of
n
distinct positive integers, and
F=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\}
be a factor-closed set containing

. Then the
m
th-order
n
-dimensional GCD tensor
\mathcal{T}_{[S]}
can be expressed as

(3.1)

\mathcal{T}_{[S]}=\mathcal{D}\times_{1}E\times_{2}E\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}E,

where
\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,l]}
is a diagonal tensor with
d_{ii\ldots i}=\Phi(f_{i}),

1\leq i\leq l,
and
E=(e_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times l}
such that
e_{ij}=1
if
f_{j}
divides
s_{i},
otherwise
e_{ij}=0.

Proof.

To prove the identity (
3.1
) it suffices to show that for all
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n
, the
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
-th element of
\mathcal{D}\times_{1}E\times_{2}E\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}E
equals the
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
-th element of
\mathcal{T}_{[S]}
. Now

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}\times_{1}E\times_{2}E\times_{3}\cdots\times_{m}E)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}=1}^{l}d_{j_{1}\ldots j_{m}}e_{i_{1}j_{1}}e_{i_{2}j_{2}}\ldots e_{i_{m}j_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{f_{k}\mid s_{i_{1}},f_{k}\mid s_{i_{2}},\ldots,f_{k}\mid s_{i_{m}}}\Phi(f_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{f_{k}\mid\GCD(s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\ldots,s_{i_{m}})}\Phi(f_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\GCD(s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\ldots,s_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}_{[S]})_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}.

Thus

.

\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}

\displaystyle\det(E\cdot\mathcal{D})\det(E^{T})^{(m-1)^{n}}

4.
Generalization of GCD tensor on meet semi-lattice

In this section, we generalize the notion of GCD tensors to a general partially ordered set (poset) and call it as
meet tensor
. Let us begin with some basic definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

A poset
(U,p)
with partial order
p
is called meet semi-lattice if for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in U
, there exists a unique
w\in U
such that

(i)

wpu_{1},wpu_{2},
and

(ii)

if
zpu_{1},zpu_{2}
for some

then
zpw.

Here
w
is called meet of
u_{1},u_{2}
and is denoted by
(u_{1},u_{2})_{p}.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(U,p)
be a meet semi-lattice and
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
be a subset of
U.
Let
g
be any real valued function on
U.
Then the
m
th-order
n
-dimensional tensor
\mathcal{T}_{[S]}^{g}=(t_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]},
where

t_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}=g((s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\ldots,s_{i_{m}})_{p}),~1\leq i_{k}\leq n,\mbox{ for }k=1,2,\ldots,m,

is called the
meet tensor
on
S
with respect to
g
.

To discuss the structure theorem and the determinant of a meet tensor on a generalized poset, we require the following two definitions.

Definition 4.3
.

[
4
, Definition 7]

Let
(U,p)
be a meet semi-lattice and
S
be a subset of
U.

(i)

The set

is called

if for every

(s_{1},s_{2})_{p}
is also in
S
.

(ii)

Let

be a real valued function on

\Psi_{S,g}
on

\Psi_{S,g}(s_{i})=g(s_{j})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}px_{i}\\
x_{i}\neq x_{j}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{S,g}(s_{j}).

The empty summation is taken to be zero.

We now define a matrix which will be crucial for this section. Let
(U,p)
be a meet semi-lattice and
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
be a subset of
U
, and

be a meet-closed finite subset of

containing

With these basic definitions, we now extend the results of GCD tensor to meet tensor. The first result of this section gives a structure of the meet tensor.

(U,p)

Next, we turn our attention to the determinant of the meet tensor. Like the case of GCD tensors on a set of positive integers, the meet tensor on a meet semi-lattice also factors as Lemma

Implementing the optimized hippo‐avoidance prophylactic cranial irradiation for limited‐stage small cell lung cancer by tomotherapy and volumetric modulated arc therapy
Authors: Tian‐You Zhan, Lei Deng, Wen‐Qing Wang, Tao Zhang, Jian‐Yang Wang, Xin Wang, Wen‐Yang Liu, Yi‐Rui Zhai, Ze‐Fen Xiao, Qin‐Fu Feng, Nan Bi, Ye‐Xiong Li, Zong‐Mei Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHippo‐avoidance prophylactic cranial irradiation (HA‐PCI) requires a hippocampal avoidance zone expanded from hippocampus to ensure dose fall‐off and compensate for setup errors. Most studies recommend a 5‐mm margin, while it could be optimized to a 2‐mm expansion. Here, we showed the details of optimized HA‐PCI for limited‐stage small cell lung cancer (LS‐SCLC).MethodsThis cohort study reviewed patients with LS‐SCLC receiving optimized HA‐PCI from August 2014 to June 2020 in the National Cancer Center of China. The hippo‐related dose parameters were summarized. The comparison of the Hopkins Verbal Learning Test—Revised (HVLT‐R) scores in different time points was conducted. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to calculate the survival rates.ResultsA total of 112 patients were included. The average doses of hippocampus and hippocampal avoidance zone were 6.80 Gy (IQR: 6.40–7.44) and 7.63 Gy (IQR: 7.14–8.39). No differences were observed in the two radiation techniques (tomotherapy [TOMO] vs. volumetric‐modulated arc therapy [VMAT]). The decline of HVLT‐R score remained in a low level and not significant in assessable patients (p = 0.095). With a median follow‐up of 52 months (95% CI: 47.2–56.7), the 2‐year overall survival and progression‐free survival were 74.1% and 50.0%, respectively. Two intracranial recurrence lesions (2.3%) located <2 mm from the hippocampus.ConclusionsOptimized HA‐PCI could achieve similar dose limitation by TOMO and VMAT techniques with favorable efficacy and minor toxicity.Keywords: hippocampal avoidance zone, prophylactic cranial irradiation, small cell lung cancer

INTRODUCTION

In the era of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), prophylactic cranial irradiation (PCI) is associated with decreased cumulative incidence of brain metastases in limited‐stage small cell lung cancer (LS‐SCLC), while its impact on survival remains inconsistent. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 The risk of neurocognitive toxicity of PCI has been major concern in clinical treatment decision. 8 The hippocampus involves in learning regulation, encoding, and consolidating memory, providing rationale for hippocampal avoidance to reduce toxicity. 9 Preliminary prospective studies have suggested that hippo‐avoidance prophylactic cranial irradiation (HA‐PCI) may reduce neurocognitive function impairment. 10 , 11 However, phase III trials have not yet provided a definitive conclusion, and further investigation is ongoing. 12 , 13 A hippocampal avoidance zone expanded from hippocampus is required in HA‐PCI, which could ensure dose fall‐off and compensate for setup errors. Previous research has recommended a range of 5 mm (three dimensions) for the expansion. 14 , 15 However, with the advent of modern tomotherapy and volumetric‐modulated arc therapy (TOMO/VMAT), the treatment range can be further reduced to 2 mm in three dimensions, thereby optimizing HA‐PCI. This optimization potentially achieves a more favorable balance between treatment volume and hippocampal sparing. This cohort study aims to evaluate hippocampal‐related dose parameters, adverse events, short‐term changes in neurocognitive function, and survival outcomes in patients with LS‐SCLC who have undergone optimized HA‐PCI.

METHOD

Patient selection

Patients with LS‐SCLC underwent the optimized HA‐PCI in the National Cancer Center of China from August 2014 to June 2020 were retrospectively reviewed. A total of 112 patients were eligible according to the criteria below: (1) Histologically or cytologically diagnosed small cell lung cancer and classified into limited stage according to AJCC‐revised 16 two‐stage 17 method by comprehensive radiologic examination includes contrast‐enhanced brain magnetic resonance; (2) complete remission (CR) or partial remission (PR) and no sign of brain metastases under contrast‐enhanced brain magnetic resonance within 1 month after curative thoracic radiotherapy with concurrent or sequential chemotherapy; (3) underwent HA‐PCI and hippocampal avoidance zone defined as hippocampus plus 2 mm in three dimensions. Patients with mental illness which might affect the cognitive function were excluded. The prophylactic memantine is not routinely used. Study protocol was approved by the institute ethics review board, and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to retrospective nature.

Radiation simulation, treatment planning, and dose limitation

Patients underwent computed tomography (CT) and T1‐weighted magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) simulation scan with 2‐mm thickness per slice and manually co‐registered together subsequently. The target and organ at risk (OAR) delineation were using the Pinnacle treatment planning system (Philips) according to the requirements of RTOG 0933. 14 The hippocampal avoidance zone is defined as hippocampus plus 2 mm three‐dimensionally. The clinical target volume (CTV) is defined as the entire brain and then plus 3 mm three‐dimensionally to form the planning target volume (PTV), in which the hippocampal avoidance zone is both excluded. For all patients, VMAT and TOMO therapy were performed using 6‐MV photons. The prescription dose was 25 Gy in 2.5 Gy in 10 fractions, which should be received by 90% of the PTV at least. The mean dose to the hippocampus and hippocampal avoidance zone should optimally be ≤8 and 10 Gy, respectively. The maximum dose to the lens should be less than 9 Gy. The doses on normal tissues such as the middle ear, parotid gland, and ethmoid sinus should be assessed.

Follow‐up, data collection, and statistical analysis

Follow‐up data were obtained from electronic medical record system or telephone interview. The planning dose parameters were also extracted, including the volume, maximum dose, minimum dose, mean dose to the hippocampus and hippocampal avoidance zone, and the maximum dose to the lens; the mean dose of the PTV and the volume of the PTV reached the prescribed dose. Tumor responses were evaluated according to the RECIST 1.1 (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, Version 1.1). Acute events were recorded according to the CTCAE 4.0 (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events) included headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and fatigue. The evaluation of neurocognitive function was based on a prospective cohort study (15–133/1060, permitted by the ethics review board of our institution with written informed consent provided) using Hopkins Verbal Learning Test—Revised (HVLT‐R) scores at baseline before PCI, 6 months after PCI, and 12 months after PCI. The failure patterns were noted as intracranial recurrence (brain metastasis), local regional recurrence (including primary lesion, mediastinal lymph nodes, and supraclavicular lymph nodes recurrence), and extracranial distant metastasis (excluding local regional and intracranial recurrence). Survival outcomes included overall survival (OS), progression‐free survival (PFS), and intracranial progression‐free survival (IPFS). The durations were calculated as time from the start of PCI to patient death or last follow‐up, to the first event, including death or progression or last follow‐up and to intracranial progression or death or last follow‐up, respectively. Differences in dose parameters and HVLT‐R scores were compared by the Mann–Whitney U test and the variance analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics v25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R statistical software V4.3.0. A p value <0.05 was considered significant.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics and treatment details

The study population comprised 112 patients with LS‐SCLC who underwent optimized HA‐PCI. The median age of the cohort was 59 years (IQR: 52–65), including five patients aged over 70 years. The majority of patients were male (71.4%) and stage III (91.1%). Two‐thirds of patients (66.9%) had a smoking history. In the whole cohort, concurrent chemoradiotherapy was administered to 68 patients (60.7%), while the remaining patients received sequential chemoradiotherapy. The median dose of thoracic radiation was 60 Gy (IQR: 58–60). All patients in the study achieved complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) after chemoradiotherapy. Patients received PCI through TOMO/VMAT technique. No patients took memantine during the irradiation. After PCI, regular follow‐up was conducted, with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) serving as the primary method (n = 94) (for details, see in Table 1).

Dose parameters

The median brain volume for the entire cohort was 1431.78 mL (IQR: 1344.71–1530.20 mL). The median volume of the hippocampus was 4.85 mL (IQR: 4.09–5.61 mL), with a median maximum dose of 8.98 Gy (IQR: 8.33–10.51), a median minimum dose of 5.96 Gy (IQR: 5.56–6.61), and a median average dose of 6.80 Gy (IQR: 6.40–7.44), respectively. The mean dose to hippocampus less than 8 Gy has been achieved in 82.1% patients. The median hippocampal avoidance zone volume was 15.00 mL (IQR: 13.07–17.28), with a median maximum dose of 12.23 Gy (IQR: 11.13–15.42), a median average dose of 7.63 Gy (IQR: 7.14–8.39), and a median minimum dose of 5.96 Gy (IQR: 5.56–6.61), respectively. Most patients had an average dose of hippocampal avoidance zone less than 12 Gy (97.3%) and 10 Gy (90.2%). The median percentage volume of PTV that received the prescribed dose was 91.0% (IQR: 90.0–92.5) (for comparison of different hippocampal avoidance zones, see in Figure 1; for dose parameters, see in Table 2). A comparison was conducted between the two techniques (TOMO vs. VMAT) for the mean dose parameters. The average dose for hippocampus and hippocampal avoidance zone in the two technique groups was similar (median: 6.66 Gy [IQR: 6.26–7.70] vs. 6.89 Gy [IQR: 6.72–7.36], P = 0.12; median: 7.64 Gy [IQR: 7.03–9.52] vs. 7.63 Gy [IQR: 7.27–7.79], P = 0.61; Figure 2).

Toxicity

The most common grade I–II adverse events were nausea (n = 38, 33.9%) and dizziness (n = 35, 31.3%). The other grade I–II adverse events included vomiting (n = 17, 15.2%), anorexia (n = 16, 14.3%), headache (n = 12, 10.7%), and fatigue (n = 4, 3.6%). Only one patient experienced grade III adverse events (nausea and dizziness).

Neurocognitive function evaluation

A total of 64 patients underwent HVLT‐R test. Among them, eight patients did not complete tests due to death or lost to follow‐up. The HVLT‐R scores were collected at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months after PCI. The average scores were 22.08 (95% CI: 21.60–22.56), 21.26 (95% CI: 20.76–21.76), and 21.14 (95% CI: 20.65–21.64) for HVLT‐R, respectively. The decrease in HVLT‐R scores from baseline was 4.2%, which is statistically insignificant (p = 0.095; Figure 3).

Mean scores of HVLT‐R total recall in baseline, 6 months, and 12 months. HVLT‐R, Hopkins Verbal Learning Test—Revised.

Survival outcomes and intracranial failure

The median duration of follow‐up was 52 months (95% CI: 47.2–56.7), and the median progression‐free survival was 24 months (95% CI: 12–NA). The 2‐ and 5‐year progression‐free survival rate were 50.0% (95% CI: 41.5%–60.2%) and 43.9% (95% CI: 35.5%–54.3%), respectively. The median overall survival was not reached, and the 2‐ and 5‐year overall survival rates were 74.1% (95% CI: 66.4%–82.7%) and 57.5% (95% CI: 48.2%–68.5%), respectively. The 2‐ and 5‐year intracranial recurrence‐free survival rates were 63.4% (95% CI: 55.1%–73.0%) and 53.1% (95% CI: 44.4%–63.5%) (Figure 4). During the follow‐up period, intracranial recurrence occurred in 25 patients, with 13 patients had solitary metastases lesions and the remaining 12 patients had multiple metastases lesions (≥2 lesions), resulting in a total of 85 metastatic lesions. In regard to the location of metastases lesions, two patients (4.0%) had two lesions (2.3%) located <2 mm from the hippocampus, with one patient's lesion located in the hippocampus.

DISCUSSION

When whole‐brain radiotherapy is administered, hippocampal avoidance remains a useful strategy for preserving neurocognitive function. In this cohort study, we showed that a 2‐mm margin from the hippocampus to form the hippocampal avoidance zone resulted in >70% mean dose reduction to hippocampus. Compared to a 5‐mm margin, this optimization in patients with LS‐SCLC undergoing prophylactic cranial irradiation is associated with a low‐level neurocognitive function decline (4.2% at 1 year). With modern radiation techniques, a more favorable balance can be achieved in protecting the hippocampus protection and promising treatment. These patients had rare intracranial recurrence around the hippocampus under the condition of long‐term survival.

The dose constraints for the hippocampus and other organs at risk in our study were consistent with those reported in other prospective clinical trials. For example, it was required that mean dose of hippocampus should be less than 8.5 Gy in the NKI‐Dutch study (NCT01780675), 93.8% of the patients met the constraint and the maximum dose to the crystal should be less than 10 Gy, and 96.2% of the patients met the constraint. 13 Similarly, in the PREMER study (NCT02397733), the mean dose to the hippocampus was 10.9 Gy, and the maximum dose of hippocampal avoidance zone was 14.7 Gy. 12 Furthermore, a prospective cohort study from Johns Hopkins reported that the mean dose of hippocampus was 7.40 Gy (range: 6.13–7.96), and the mean dose of hippocampal avoidance zone was 10.25 Gy (range: 7.87–11.59). 10 Our study restricted that the mean dose of hippocampus should be optimally less than 8 Gy, while in clinical practice, a stricter requirement at 7 Gy could also be achieved. This indicates that enough dose reduction could be achieved when a decreased expansion range to form the hippocampal avoidance zone was adopted. Additionally, two radiation techniques could deliver similar mean dose to hippocampus and hippocampal avoidance zone, which supports their clinical applications in such situation. 18

To illustrate the changes of neurocognitive function in patients with optimized HA‐PCI, we obtained HVLT‐R scores from the assessable patients. A relatively low decline could eliminate certain concerns on the neurotoxicity of PCI. However, the comparison of neurocognitive function results of RCTs was not always positive. Since RTOG 0933 has demonstrated that the dose reduction in hippocampal zone was associated with preservation of memory in patients with brain metastases. The efficacy of hippocampal avoidance was further explored for prophylactic cranial irradiation. Two phase III RCTs included the PREMER study and NKI‐Dutch study had similar inclusion criteria with 70% patients diagnosed with LS‐SCLC. Patients were assigned to either hippocampal avoidance prophylactic cranial irradiation group or group without hippocampal avoidance. Although the primary endpoints were both neurocognitive‐related test scores, with Free and Cued Selective Reminding (FCSR) for the former and HVLT‐R for the latter, the results were not entirely consistent. The PREMER study showed that an improved short‐term cognitive protective effect was obtained in the HA‐PCI group (3‐month Free and Cued Selective Reminding Test — Delayed Free Recall (FCSRT‐DFR) decline: 5.8% vs. 23.5%, p = 0.003). While no significant difference in HVLT‐R decline was observed in the NKI‐Dutch study (p = 1.000). These results may be influenced by different neurocognitive function testing methods or quality control regarding the dose constricts and the hippocampal delineation. 19 Thus, results of larger prospective studies like NRG CC003 (NCT02635009) might address the problems above and illustrate the effect of hippo avoidance technique.

A low‐dose region implies the potential for undertreatment, which may elevate the risk of failure. The optimized HA‐PCI could ensure more treatment area and decrease failure around the hippocampus theoretically. Over the entire follow‐up period, a total of 85 metastatic lesions were identified in 25 patients, with only two lesions (2.3%) located within the hippocampal avoidance zone. The assessment of brain metastases in our cohort was conducted by MRI, with all patients undergoing MRI scans prior to PCI and the majority (83.9%) receiving MRI surveillance after PCI. Thus, the results are reliable and consistent with previous research. 20 , 21 The meta‐analysis showed a low overall incidence of hippocampal (4.4%) and peri hippocampal (9.2%) BMs with a 5‐mm margin definition for the hippocampal avoidance zone. 22 Due to the relatively low incidence, the differences between different margin definitions might not be assessed. However, the treatment effect could be safe and promising with the optimized HA‐PCI. A trend should be noticed. Although intracranial failure remains one of the major failure patterns with a 30%–50% of intracranial recurrence rate in patients with LS‐SCLC, 3 , 5 , 23 with the introduction of advanced imaging techniques such as MRI, more subtle metastases have been identified, which led to fewer patients eligible for PCI. 19 The role of PCI for LS‐SCLC in MR era needs further investigation (NCT04790253, NCT04829708, NCT05651802). Patients in our cohort achieved a favorable survival outcome, with a 5‐year PFS and OS of 43.9% and 57.5%, respectively. The long‐term survival results could be attributed to highly selection of patients and completeness of first‐line multimodality therapy. Patients in our cohort had a well response to initial chemoradiotherapy which means minimal residual disease, and it led to a low rate of recurrence. It is necessary to emphasize the neurocognitive function protection with long‐term survival.

Our analysis has some limitations. Firstly, the retrospective design of the study introduces potential selection bias. Specifically, some patients included in the study were over 70 years old in which the decision of PCI might be cautious in current clinical practice. 24 Secondly, the study only includes patients underwent PCI with hippocampal avoidance, making it hard to further compare and elucidate the differences with and without hippocampal avoidance. Further phase III study results may need for the final result. Thirdly, the neurocognitive function test only lasted for 12 months, while the median follow‐up lasted for 50 months. Nonetheless, the review of dose parameters for prophylactic cranial irradiation in this cohort can be both accuracy and instructive.

CONCLUSION

In summary, with modern radiotherapy techniques, the safety and efficacy of optimized HA‐PCI make it worthy of further promotion.

Early clinical and hemodynamic outcomes of SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI
Authors: Safa Özçelik, Semih Eren, Kemal Özdemir, Ferdi Peynirci, Yasin Özden, Yavuz Şensöz, Sait Terzi, İlyas Kayacioğlu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aortic stenosis is a common valvular heart disease associated with substantial morbidity and mortality if left untreated. Surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR), transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), and autologous pericardial aortic valve reconstruction (Ozaki procedure) represent alternative interventional strategies, but direct real-world comparisons remain limited. This study evaluated early clinical and 6-month hemodynamic outcomes following these 3 treatment approaches. This retrospective single-center observational study included 153 consecutive patients with severe aortic stenosis treated between December 2020 and March 2022 (SAVR, n = 67; Ozaki, n = 31; TAVI, n = 55). Early mortality was the primary clinical outcome, and 6-month mean transvalvular gradient was the primary echocardiographic outcome. Other clinical and echocardiographic outcomes were considered secondary or exploratory. Multivariable regression, analysis of covariance, and separate pairwise propensity-score matching models were used to account for baseline differences. Propensity scores were estimated using age, sex, EuroSCORE II, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease. TAVI patients were older and had higher operative risk. No statistically significant differences were detected among the groups in early mortality, stroke, or postoperative dialysis. Permanent pacemaker implantation was more frequent after TAVI, while prolonged intensive care unit stay also differed among treatment groups. At 6 months, the Ozaki group showed favorable hemodynamic parameters, including lower mean transvalvular gradients and maximum transvalvular velocity. However, 6-month echocardiographic follow-up was incomplete, and these findings should be considered exploratory given the small Ozaki cohort, baseline differences, and observational study design. SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI showed distinct early clinical and hemodynamic profiles in this real-world observational cohort. No statistically significant differences were detected in several major early clinical outcomes, although the study was not sufficiently powered to establish equivalent safety or exclude clinically meaningful differences. The favorable short-term hemodynamic findings observed with the Ozaki procedure should be considered hypothesis-generating. Larger prospective studies with longer follow-up are needed to further evaluate comparative clinical outcomes, hemodynamic performance, and durability.

1. Introduction

Aortic stenosis (AS) is one of the most prevalent valvular heart diseases in the aging population and is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality when left untreated.[1,2] Progressive obstruction of left ventricular outflow leads to pressure overload, ventricular remodeling, heart failure, and reduced survival, making timely valve intervention essential once symptoms or ventricular dysfunction develop.[1–3] Surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) has long been considered the standard treatment, providing reliable symptom relief and durable long-term outcomes.[2,3]

Over the past 2 decades, less invasive and valve-reconstructive strategies have expanded the therapeutic landscape of AS. Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) initially demonstrated benefit in inoperable and high-risk patients,[4,5] followed by randomized evidence supporting its use in intermediate- and selected low-risk populations with outcomes comparable to surgery.[6–8] In parallel, autologous pericardial aortic valve reconstruction using the Ozaki technique has emerged as a surgical alternative that preserves physiologic annular motion, avoids prosthetic valve-related limitations, and may reduce prosthesis–patient mismatch, particularly in patients with small aortic roots.[9–11]

Despite these advances, direct comparative data among SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI remain limited, especially in real-world cohorts shaped by regulatory constraints, reimbursement policies, and risk-based treatment allocation.[3,12] Furthermore, although early outcomes of TAVI are well established and short- to mid-term hemodynamic findings after the Ozaki procedure appear promising, uncertainty remains regarding the early clinical and echocardiographic profiles of these treatment strategies when evaluated within the same institutional practice.[10–12]

Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate the early clinical outcomes and 6-month hemodynamic findings of SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI in a real-world single-center cohort of patients treated for severe AS. The primary clinical outcome was early mortality, and the primary echocardiographic outcome was the mean transvalvular pressure gradient at 6 months. Other postoperative clinical events and echocardiographic parameters were evaluated as secondary or exploratory outcomes. We hypothesized that the 3 treatment strategies would exhibit differences in their early clinical and hemodynamic profiles.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design

This retrospective, single-center observational study evaluated the short-term clinical and 6-month echocardiographic outcomes of patients undergoing interventional treatment for AS, including SAVR, the Ozaki procedure, and TAVI, at our institution between December 2020 and March 2022. The study protocol was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Haydarpasha Numune Training and Research Hospital (Approval No: HNEAH-KAEK 2022/102-3726). The requirement for individual informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee due to the retrospective nature of the study.

All consecutive patients aged > 18 years with AS who underwent SAVR, the Ozaki procedure, or TAVI during the study period were screened for inclusion. Emergency procedures and, among surgically treated patients, cases involving concomitant cardiac surgery in addition to aortic valve replacement or reconstruction were excluded. After applying these criteria, 153 consecutive eligible patients were included in the analysis: 67 underwent SAVR, 31 underwent the Ozaki procedure, and 55 underwent TAVI. Treatment allocation followed a 2-step decision-making process. The choice between surgical treatment and TAVI was determined by the institutional Heart Team, taking into account clinical characteristics, operative risk, and national legal and reimbursement regulations. In our country, TAVI is reserved for patients at high surgical risk, including those with prior cardiac surgery. Among patients selected for surgical treatment, the choice between SAVR and the Ozaki procedure was made through shared decision-making between the patient and the treating surgeon following comprehensive counseling. At our center, the Ozaki procedure is considered an alternative to bioprosthetic valve replacement in patients older than 65 years and is particularly favored in patients with a narrow aortic root as an alternative to root-enlargement procedures. Among patients considered suitable for either surgical option, the informed patient’s preference played a decisive role in procedural selection.

2.2. Data collection and outcomes

Preoperative demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, sex, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, previous cardiac surgery, and EuroSCORE II, were retrospectively extracted from institutional medical records. Operative risk was categorized according to EuroSCORE II as low (< 5%), intermediate (5–10%), or high (> 10%). Preoperative echocardiographic parameters included left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), maximum transvalvular velocity (Vmax), and maximum and mean transvalvular pressure gradients.

The primary clinical outcome was early mortality, defined as death occurring during the index hospitalization. Secondary or exploratory postoperative outcomes included permanent pacemaker implantation, new-onset dialysis, cerebrovascular events (CVE), prolonged mechanical ventilation, prolonged intensive care unit (ICU) stay, and duration of hospitalization. Prolonged ICU stay and prolonged mechanical ventilation were both defined as > 24 hours.

The primary echocardiographic outcome was the mean transvalvular pressure gradient at approximately 6 months after the procedure. Other 6-month echocardiographic parameters, including LVEF, Vmax, maximum transvalvular gradient, and aortic regurgitation, were considered secondary or exploratory outcomes. Six-month echocardiographic follow-up was not complete for all patients. Available follow-up transthoracic echocardiographic measurements were retrospectively extracted from institutional records and analyzed as described below.

2.3. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation and categorical variables as n (%). Analyses were performed using Jeffrey’s Amazing Statistics Program (version 0.19.3; JASP Team) and Python 3.11 (statsmodels, scipy, Python Software Foundation). All tests were 2-sided, and P < .05 was considered statistically significant. Data distributions were assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test for normality and Levene test for homogeneity of variances. For between-group comparisons, 1-way analysis of variance was used for normally distributed continuous variables and the Kruskal–Wallis test for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. When a global test was statistically significant, Bonferroni-corrected pairwise comparisons were performed.

To adjust for potential confounding, multivariable logistic regression models were fitted for binary clinical outcomes using outcome-specific covariates, as detailed in Table 3. Analysis of covariance models for 6-month echocardiographic outcomes were adjusted for age, sex, and EuroSCORE II. Given the limited number of events for several binary outcomes, particularly early mortality, CVEs, new-onset dialysis, and permanent pacemaker implantation, the corresponding multivariable logistic regression models were considered exploratory because of the potential for overfitting and unstable effect estimates. Accordingly, adjusted estimates from these models were interpreted cautiously and were not considered confirmatory.

To mitigate treatment-selection bias, separate propensity-score models were constructed for each pairwise treatment comparison (TAVI vs SAVR, TAVI vs Ozaki, and Ozaki vs SAVR). For each comparison, propensity scores were independently estimated using logistic regression, including age, sex, EuroSCORE II, chronic kidney disease, and coronary artery disease. Patients were matched using 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching with a caliper of 0.2 × standard deviation of the logit of the propensity score. Post-matching comparisons were performed using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for continuous outcomes and McNemar test for binary outcomes. The propensity-score–matched comparisons were considered sensitivity analyses.

Missing 6-month echocardiographic data were not imputed. Echocardiographic outcomes were analyzed using an available-case approach for each parameter; therefore, denominators varied according to the availability of individual 6-month measurements.

3.3. Echocardiographic follow-up

Six-month echocardiographic follow-up was incomplete, and analyses were therefore performed using available cases for each parameter. LVEF was available in 94 patients, mean transvalvular gradient in 76, maximum transvalvular gradient in 82, Vmax in 72, and aortic regurgitation assessment in 97 patients.

At 6 months, the mean transvalvular gradient differed significantly among the treatment groups (P = .011), with lower observed values in the Ozaki group (9.22 ± 2.76 mm Hg) than in the SAVR (14.38 ± 5.80 mm Hg) and TAVI (11.55 ± 6.22 mm Hg) groups. Vmax also differed significantly among groups (P = .003), with the lowest observed value in the Ozaki group. The overall difference in maximum transvalvular gradient did not reach statistical significance (P = .065). No statistically significant differences were detected in LVEF (P = .526) or moderate or greater aortic regurgitation (P = .855) (Table 5).

4. Discussion

This single-center retrospective observational study evaluated early clinical and short-term hemodynamic outcomes following SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI in a real-world cohort shaped by national reimbursement regulations and risk-based treatment allocation. Several findings merit consideration. Permanent pacemaker implantation was more frequent after TAVI, with this association persisting in adjusted and propensity-matched sensitivity analyses. No statistically significant differences were detected among treatment groups for early mortality, CVEs, or new-onset dialysis, although the limited number of events precludes conclusions regarding equivalent safety. At 6 months, the Ozaki group showed favorable hemodynamic parameters, including lower observed transvalvular gradients and Vmax; however, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory given the small cohort, baseline differences, incomplete echocardiographic follow-up, and observational study design.

4.1. Procedural risk and early clinical outcomes

The higher operative risk and advanced age observed in the TAVI cohort largely reflect the regulatory and reimbursement framework governing treatment allocation during the study period, in which transcatheter therapy was primarily reserved for patients considered at high surgical risk. Consequently, the treatment groups differed substantially at baseline, introducing important confounding by indication. Although multivariable adjustment and propensity-score matching were used to reduce measured confounding, residual differences and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded.

No statistically significant differences were detected in early mortality, CVEs, or new-onset dialysis among the 3 treatment groups. These findings should not, however, be interpreted as evidence of equivalent safety. The relatively small sample size and low number of events limited the statistical power to detect potentially clinically meaningful differences, particularly for uncommon adverse outcomes. Accordingly, the observed event rates should be considered descriptive and interpreted within the context of the differing baseline risk profiles of the treatment groups.

Permanent pacemaker implantation was more frequent after TAVI, and the association persisted in the exploratory multivariable analysis and the TAVI–SAVR propensity-matched sensitivity analysis. This observation is consistent with previous multicenter studies reporting higher pacemaker requirements after TAVI, particularly with self-expanding valve platforms.[13,14] Mechanical interaction between the transcatheter prosthesis, membranous septum, and atrioventricular conduction system is a recognized mechanism underlying post-TAVI conduction disturbances.[13] Nevertheless, given the limited number of pacemaker events in the present cohort and the potential for overfitting of the adjusted model, the magnitude of this association should be interpreted cautiously.

Prolonged ICU stay was also more frequent in the TAVI group and remained statistically significant after covariate adjustment. However, this finding may have been influenced by temporal changes in perioperative management. The study period included our earlier TAVI experience, during which many procedures were performed under general anesthesia with routine postoperative ventilation and ICU admission. Contemporary minimalist TAVI pathways incorporating conscious sedation, early mobilization, and accelerated discharge have reduced ICU utilization and hospital stay.[15,16] Therefore, the observed ICU-related differences may partly reflect institutional practice patterns during the study period rather than an intrinsic effect of the procedure itself.

4.2. Hemodynamic performance and the role of Ozaki neocuspidization

At 6-month follow-up, the Ozaki group demonstrated favorable hemodynamic parameters, including lower observed mean transvalvular gradients and Vmax. The overall between-group difference in mean gradient remained statistically significant after covariate adjustment. These observations are consistent with previous reports describing favorable effective orifice areas and flow characteristics after autologous pericardial neocuspidization.[9–11]

From a mechanistic perspective, the absence of a rigid prosthetic stent frame and preservation of physiological annular motion may contribute to the observed gradient profile. These characteristics may be particularly relevant in patients with a small aortic root, in whom prosthesis–patient mismatch represents an important consideration when conventional bioprosthetic SAVR is performed.[17,18]

These findings should nevertheless be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than evidence of comparative hemodynamic superiority. The Ozaki cohort was relatively small; treatment allocation was nonrandom; baseline characteristics differed among groups; and 6-month echocardiographic follow-up was incomplete. Furthermore, the 6-month observation period does not permit assessment of structural valve durability, late hemodynamic changes, or reintervention. Longer-term evidence for the Ozaki procedure remains more limited than that available for established surgical prostheses and increasingly for TAVI.[11,19] Larger prospective studies with more complete and longer follow-up are therefore required to determine whether the observed short-term hemodynamic findings translate into clinically meaningful long-term benefits.

4.3. Clinical implications

The present findings illustrate the distinct early clinical and hemodynamic profiles of SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI within a real-world treatment environment. Because treatment allocation was strongly influenced by surgical risk, regulatory criteria, anatomical considerations, and patient preference, direct causal comparisons among the 3 strategies cannot be made from these observational data.

Rather than establishing superiority or equivalence among treatment modalities, these findings support continued evaluation of each strategy within an individualized Heart Team framework. Contemporary treatment selection for AS should integrate operative risk, anatomical suitability, expected durability, procedural characteristics, and informed patient preference.[2,3,19] The present results may contribute hypothesis-generating information regarding early outcomes, but they require confirmation in larger prospective cohorts with longer-term follow-up.

4.4. Study limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective single-center observational design introduces inherent selection bias and confounding by indication. Treatment allocation was nonrandom, and TAVI selection was strongly influenced by surgical risk and national regulatory and reimbursement criteria, resulting in substantial baseline differences among the treatment groups. Although multivariable adjustment and separate pairwise propensity-score matching were used to reduce measured confounding, residual imbalance and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded.

Second, the overall sample size and particularly the size of the Ozaki cohort were relatively small, limiting statistical power for several comparisons. The study may therefore have been underpowered to detect clinically meaningful differences, and the absence of statistical significance should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalence. In addition, the low number of events for early mortality, CVEs, new-onset dialysis, and permanent pacemaker implantation relative to the number of covariates increased the risk of overfitting and imprecision in the corresponding multivariable logistic regression models. These adjusted estimates should therefore be considered exploratory and interpreted cautiously.

Third, 6-month echocardiographic follow-up was incomplete, and the number of available observations varied across echocardiographic parameters. Missing follow-up data were not imputed, and analyses were performed using an available-case approach; however, this strategy does not eliminate the potential for attrition bias.

Fourth, follow-up was limited to 6 months, precluding assessment of structural valve durability, late hemodynamic changes, and reintervention rates. This limitation is particularly relevant to interpretation of the favorable short-term hemodynamic findings observed in the Ozaki group.

Finally, perioperative management evolved during the study period, particularly with respect to anesthesia and postoperative care following TAVI. These temporal changes may have influenced ICU- and ventilation-related outcomes and limit their applicability to contemporary minimalist TAVI practice.

5. Conclusions

In this real-world observational cohort, SAVR, Ozaki neocuspidization, and TAVI showed distinct early clinical and hemodynamic profiles within a treatment pathway shaped by surgical risk, regulatory criteria, and individualized patient selection. Permanent pacemaker implantation was more frequent after TAVI, while no statistically significant differences were detected in several other major early clinical outcomes. However, these findings should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalent safety.

At 6 months, the Ozaki procedure showed favorable hemodynamic parameters, including lower observed transvalvular gradients and Vmax. Given the small Ozaki cohort, baseline differences, incomplete echocardiographic follow-up, and observational design, these findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than evidence of comparative superiority.

These results support individualized Heart Team–based treatment selection but should be interpreted in the context of residual confounding and limited statistical power. Larger prospective studies with longer-term follow-up are needed to further define comparative clinical outcomes, hemodynamic performance, and durability.

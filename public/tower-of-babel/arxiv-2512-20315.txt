Abstract
. We characterize smooth irreducible curves C C on a smooth hyperquadric Y Y of ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} such that the blowup of Y Y along C C is a weak Fano threefold. These are precisely the smooth irreducible curves C C of degree d d and genus g g lying on a smooth hypercubic section of Y Y such that (i) C C has no 4-secant line and no 7-secant conic; (ii) d < 18 d<18 and ( g , d ) ∉ { ( 4 , 7 ) , ( 10 , 11 ) } (g,d)\not\in\{(4,7),\>(10,11)\} ; (iii) either 3 ​ d − 26 < g ≤ d 2 − 1 12 3d-26<g\leq\frac{d^{2}-1}{12} or ( g , d ) ∈ { ( 4 , 6 ) , ( 13 , 12 ) } (g,d)\in\{(4,6),\>(13,12)\} . We prove the geometric realizability of each case, thereby proving the existence of weak Fano threefolds and Sarkisov links constructed from them, which were previously known only as numerical possibilities.

The classification of (weak) Fano varieties of dimension 2, which are called (weak) del Pezzo surfaces, is a very classical result (see e.g.
[
Dol12
, Chapter 8.1]
):
X
is a weak del Pezzo surface if and only if
X
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, to the second Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2}
or to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in at most 8 (possibly infinitely near) points in almost general position. The latter condition means that there is no line through 4 of the points, no conic through 7 of the points and no exceptional divisor through two infinitely near points.

Moreover,
X
is a del Pezzo surface if and only if
X
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
or to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in at most 8 points in general linear position. That is to say, there is no line through 3 of the points, no conic though 6 of them, and no cubic through 8 points that is singular at one of them.

In dimension 2, this provides a complete classification of (weak) del Fano varieties into 10 (respectively 11) deformation families. Furthermore, the characterization of the families that are given by the blowup of a variety along a subvariety depends only on geometric properties of the blown-up subvariety.

In dimension 3, there is also a complete classification of Fano threefolds into 105 deformation families. It was established by Iskovskikh (
[
Isk77
]
and
[
Isk78

Nonetheless, the classification of weak Fano threefolds is far from being complete.

A weak Fano threefold

must itself be ample. So the weak Fano threefolds of Picard rank one can be found in Iskovskikh’s classification of Fano threefolds.

A common way to obtain weak Fano threefolds of Picard rank 2 is to blowup a Fano threefold of Picard rank one along a subvariety. Studying such subfamilies of weak Fano threefolds allows us to obtain a classification of these weak Fano threefolds, depending only on some geometric conditions of the blown-up subvariety. The authors of the papers

Replacing

Namely, a smooth rational quartic curve

2.
Arithmetic genus of curves on a smooth hyperquadric

For the rest of the paper, let
\mathbf{k}
denote an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. Furthermore, we assume all varieties to be projective, but potentially reducible or non-reduced unless stated otherwise.

This section compiles auxiliary lemmas on the elementary properties of the arithmetical genus of curves (§
2.1
) and on how the genus depends on the curve being contained in larger varieties of a certain degree (§
2.2
).

2.1.
Arithmetic genus of (possibly singular) curves

Recall that the arithmetic genus of a projective variety
Z
, whose irreducible components all have dimension
m
, is defined as

p_{a}(Z)=(-1)^{m}(\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z})-1),

where the Euler characteristic
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(H^{i}(Z,\>\mathcal{O}_{Z}))
is given by the constant term of the Hilbert polynomial of
Z
.

Note, in particular, that this definition does not depend on the embedding of
Z
into projective space. However, given a reduced projective variety
Z
and an integer
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can consider the non-reduced variety
Z^{\prime}=l\cdot Z
as a subvariety of a projective variety of dimension
\dim(Z)+1
. Then
Z^{\prime}=l\cdot Z
is a divisor on this larger variety and so its structure (as a subscheme) and thus its arithmetic genus do depend on the embedding of
Z^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
Z
is a connected and reduced projective variety (e.g. if
Z
is integral), recall that
\mathcal{O}_{Z}=\mathbf{k}
and thus,
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}))=1
(see e.g.
[
Liu02
, Cor. 3.3.21]
). MoreoverLet
C\subseteq Y
be a smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
and degree
d
that lies on a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}

Z
is a (possibly non-reduced, singular or reducible) complete intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of positive dimension for some
n\geq 1
, then we also have
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}))=1
(
[
Vak25
, 18.6.U]
). In particularLet
C\subseteq Y
be a smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
and degree
d
that lies on a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}

Z
is a projective curve that is either connected and reduced or a complete intersection, then the arithmetic genus of
Z
is just given by
p_{a}(Z)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}))
.

In the following, the genus of a (possibly singular, reducible or non-reduced) curve
\Gamma
will always refer to the arithmetic genus
p_{a}(\Gamma)=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma})
of
\Gamma
. Recall that if
\Gamma
is a smooth, irreducible curve, then its arithmetic and geometric genus coincide.

The following lemma generalizes the well-known equality
2p_{a}(\Gamma)-2=\Gamma\cdot(\Gamma+K_{S})
for smooth irreducible curves
\Gamma
on a smooth surface
S
to an arbitrary curve
\Gamma\subseteq S
that is possibly singular, reducible or non-reduced (compare
[
Har77
, Exercice V.1.3]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth surface with canonical divisor
K_{S}
and
\Gamma\subseteq S
a curve. Then the arithmetic genus
p_{a}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
satisfies

2p_{a}(\Gamma)-2=\Gamma\cdot(\Gamma+K_{S}).

In particular, given a curve
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq S
, the union
\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{\prime}
(seen as a divisor on
S
) satisfies

p_{a}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\prime})=p_{a}(\Gamma)+p_{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})+\Gamma\cdot\Gamma^{\prime}-1.

Proof.

In (the proof of)
[
Har77
, Thm. V.1.6]
, it is shown that any Cartier divisor
D\subseteq S
satisfies
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\frac{1}{2}D\cdot(D-K_{S})+1+p_{a}(S)
. For the divisor
D=-\Gamma
, this equality yields

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\Gamma))=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Gamma\cdot(\Gamma+K_{S})+1+p_{a}(S).

By definition of the arithmetic genus, we have
p_{a}(S)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})-1
. Furthermore, we make use of the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\Gamma)\simeq\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
(
[
Har77
, Prop. II.6.18]
), where
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
\Gamma
. The short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow 0
now implies that
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})=\chi({\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}})+\chi({\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}}).
Putting everything together, we deduce that

p_{a}(\Gamma)=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma})=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})+\chi(\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma})=-p_{a}(S)+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\Gamma))=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Gamma\cdot(\Gamma+K_{S})+1.

From this, it follows directly that

\displaystyle p_{a}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\prime})\cdot(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\prime}+K_{S})+1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Gamma\cdot(\Gamma+K_{S})+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Gamma^{\prime}\cdot(\Gamma^{\prime}+K_{S})+\Gamma\cdot\Gamma^{\prime}+1

\displaystyle=p_{a}(\Gamma)+p_{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})+\Gamma\cdot\Gamma^{\prime}-1.

∎

We will mostly apply
2.2
for the case where
S
is a smooth K3-surface and thus
K_{S}=0
. Then any curve
\Gamma\subseteq S
satisfies
\Gamma^{2}=2p_{a}(\Gamma)-2
. We use this fact without always referring to
2.2
.

Such a K3-surface

will show up as the intersection of a hyperquadric and a hypercubic in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. To study curves on

, we are interested in curves that are equal to or contained in a complete intersection of

with another hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. The genus of such a (potentially reducible) complete intersection depends only on the degree of the intersected hypersurfaces as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma=H_{1}\cap{\dots}\cap H_{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a reduced curve that is a complete intersection of
n-1
irreducible hypersurfaces
H_{1},\dots,H_{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
\deg(H_{i})=d_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then

p_{a}(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}\left(-n-1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}\right)+1.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is a complete intersection, we can apply
[
Liu02
, Cor. 7.3.31]
to obtain that
2p_{a}(\Gamma)-2=\deg(\omega_{\Gamma})
, where
\omega_{\Gamma}
denotes the dualizing sheaf of
\Gamma
(which is isomorphic to its canonical sheaf as
\Gamma
is a complete intersection, see e.g.
[
Liu02
, Exercise 6.4.11, Chapter 6]
).

Applying iteratively the adjunction formula (
[
Vak25
, Prop. 29.4.8]
), we obtain that the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{\Gamma}
of the complete intersection
\Gamma
is given by

\omega_{\Gamma}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}-n-1\right).

(Note that we can apply
[
Vak25
, Prop. 29.4.8]
, since
\Gamma
is a projective Cohen-Macaulay scheme by
[
Har77
, Prop. II.8.23]
.)
Taking the degree on both sides gives the desired equality

2p_{a}(\Gamma)-2=\deg(\omega_{\Gamma})=\deg(\Gamma)\cdot\left(-n-1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}\left(-n-1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}\right).

∎

2.2.
Genus of curves on hypersurfaces of a given degree

Given a smooth irreducible curve
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
of degree
d
and genus
g
, it is in general hard to determine the dimension of the linear system of hypersurfaces of a given degree that contain
C
without additional information about the curve. However, we are able to give a lower bound on the dimension that depends only on
g
and
d
. In general, this lower bound does not have to be attained.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
e,n\geq 1
and
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
and degree
d
. If
2g-2<ed
, then the projective dimension of the linear system of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
e
containing
C
is at least equal to

\binom{n+e}{e}+g-ed-2.

If
2g-2\geq ed
, we get the less specific bound that the projective dimension of the corresponding linear system is at least equal to

\binom{n+e}{e}-2+\min\left\{g-ed,\>-\frac{1}{2}ed\right\}.

For
n=4
, we obtain that the projective dimension of the linear system of

\begin{cases}hyperplanes&\\
hyperquadrics&\\
hypercubics\end{cases}

passing through
C
is at least equal to

\begin{cases}3+g-d&\\
13+g-2d&\\
33+g-3d\end{cases}
if

\begin{cases}2g-2<d.&\\
g-1<d.&\\
2g-2<3d.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
Q\in\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]_{e}\setminus\{0\}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
e
such that
Q
does not vanish on
C
. Consider the divisor
H\in\textup{Div}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
associated to the hypersurface
V_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(Q)
. Then

is a divisor of degree
\deg(D)=\deg(H)\cdot\deg(C)=ed
inducing a
\mathbf{k}
-linear map

\eta:\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]_{e}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(D),\quad P\mapsto\frac{P}{Q}\Big|_{C}.

The projective dimension of the linear system of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree

containing
C
is one less than the vectorial dimension
\ker(\eta)
of the linear system. We want to bound
\dim(\ker(\eta))
using
\dim(\mathcal{L}(D))
. We first note that, by the adjunction formula, we have

\deg(K_{C}-D)=\deg(K_{C})-\deg(D)=-2+2g-ed,

and by Riemann-Roch,

\dim(\mathcal{L}(D))=:\ell(D)=-g+\deg(D)+1+\ell(K_{C}-D)=-g+ed+1+\ell(K_{C}-D).

If
2g-2<ed
, then
\deg(K_{C}-D)<0
and thus,
\ell(K_{C}-D)=0
. Riemann-Roch then yields

\ell(D)=-g+ed+1.
It follows that the projective dimension

can be bounded by

\dim(\ker(\eta))-1\geq\dim(\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]_{e})-\dim(\mathcal{L}(D))-1=\binom{n+e}{e}+g-ed-2.

If
2g-2=ed
, then there are two possibilities: Either
\ell(K_{C}-D)=0
and then the projective dimension can be bounded from below as before by

\binom{n+e}{e}+g-ed-2.

OtherwiseLet
C\subseteq Y
be a smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
and degree
d
that lies on a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}

\ell(K_{C}-D)>0
, then the divisor
D
is a special effective divisor. Using Clifford’s theorem (
[
Har77

), we obtain
\ell(D)\leq\frac{1}{2}\deg(D)+1=\frac{1}{2}ed+1
, which gives

\dim(\ker(\eta))-1\geq\dim(\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]_{e})-\ell(D)-1\geq\binom{n+e}{e}-\frac{1}{2}ed-2.

∎

The following lemma provides us with two useful facts about the smooth hyperquadric

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

(i)

If

and the codimension of all irreducible components of

(ii)

If

Proof.

Remark 4.5

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

be a line on the two-dimensional quadric cone

. Then there is an automorphism
\alpha\in\textup{Aut}(Q)
such that
\alpha(L)=V_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(x_{1},x_{2})\subseteq Q
.

Proof.

We first construct a subgroup of
\textup{Aut}(Q)
from which we will choose the automorphism
\alpha
. Recall that the singular quadric cone
Q\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
is uniquely given as the union of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
through the singular point
p:=[0:0:0:1]
and one point on the planar conic
\Gamma:=V_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(x_{0}x_{2}-x_{1}^{2})
embedded into

. Furthermore, the conic
\Gamma
is isomorphic to

via the isomorphism

\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\Gamma,\>[u:v]\mapsto[u^{2}:uv:v^{2}].

This isomorphism induces an inclusion

\begin{array}[]{c c c c c c}\iota:&\textup{PGL}_{2}(\mathbf{k})=\textup{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})&\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}&\textup{Aut}(\Gamma)\subseteq\textup{GL}_{3}(\mathbf{k})&\hookrightarrow&\textup{Aut}(Q)\subseteq\textup{GL}_{4}(\mathbf{k})\\
&A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}&\mapsto&M_{A}:=\frac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}&2ab&b^{2}\\
ac&ad+bc&bd\\
c^{2}&2cd&d^{2}\end{pmatrix}&\mapsto&\begin{pmatrix}&&&0\\
&M_{A}&&0\\
&&&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\end{array}.

This inclusion induces an action of
\textup{PGL}_{2}(\mathbf{k})
on

, which is given by
\textup{PGL}_{2}(\mathbf{k})\times Q\rightarrow Q,\>(A,x)\mapsto\iota(A)(x)
. This action fixes the singular point

of

and acts transitively on the points of the conic

.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Consider the two-dimensional quadric cone
Q=V_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(x_{0}x_{2}-x_{1}^{2})\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
and its singular point
p:=[0:0:0:1]\in Q
. Let
C\subseteq Q
be a smooth irreducible curve through
p
. Denote by
\pi:\textup{Bl}_{C}(Q)\rightarrow Q
the blowup of
Q
along
C
and by
\eta:Bl_{p}(Q)\rightarrow Q
the blowup of

in
p
with exceptional divisors

and

.

Then there is an isomorphism
\varphi:\textup{Bl}_{p}(Q)\rightarrow\textup{Bl}_{C}(Q)
satisfying
\eta=\pi\circ\varphi
. In particular, we have
\varphi^{-1}(E)=\hat{C}\cup e
, where
\hat{C}\subseteq\textup{Bl}_{p}(Q)
is the strict transform of

under
\eta
.

Proof.

The blowup of

in

can naturally be embedded into

\textup{Bl}_{p}(Q)=\{([x_{0}:\dots:x_{3}],[y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}])\in\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\>|\>y_{0}y_{2}=y_{1}^{2},\>x_{i}y_{j}=x_{j}y_{i}\textup{ for }i,j\in\{0,1,2\}\}.

The blowup map
\eta:\textup{Bl}_{p}(Q)\rightarrow Q,\>(x,y)\mapsto x
is then just given by the projection on the second factor. Furthermore, the exceptional divisor is
e:=\eta^{-1}(\{p\})=\{p\}\times\Gamma
, where

is a conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Consider a smooth irreducible curve

through

. By classical results about blowups, we can construct the blowup of

along

as follows: Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\in\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
be homogeneous polynomials such that

. Denoting

, we choose a basis
\{f_{0},\dots,f_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbf{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
of the vector space

\{f\in I_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(C)\>|\>f\textup{ homogeneous of degree }d\}.

We write
f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}f_{i,j}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\cdot x_{3}^{d-j}

. We define a birational map

Then

along

, where
\textup{Bl}_{C}(Q)=\overline{\tau(Q\setminus C)}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Observe that the singular fiber

is a curve since the tangent space

of

in

is of dimension 3 and the exceptional divisor

is the projectivized normal bundle of

in

.
So

is not irreducible as it is the union of the singular fiber

and a section

of the restriction

.

, we obtain a birational map

\begin{array}[]{l l l l l}\varphi:&\textup{Bl}_{p}(Q)&\dashrightarrow&&\textup{Bl}_{C}(Q),\\
&(x,y)&\mapsto&&(x,[f_{0}(x):{\dots}:f_{n}(x)])\\

4.2.
Curves on a hyperplane section yielding a weak Fano threefold

In the following, we want to prove that if
C
is contained in a hyperplane, then

is weak Fano if and only if
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{plane}}
.

In the rest of the section, we assume thus that the curve
C
lies on a hyperplane section

of
Y
. We denote the strict transform of
S
under the blowup
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
of

along

by
\widetilde{S}:=\overline{\pi^{-1}(S\setminus C)}\subseteq X
.

We have seen in

that in order to show that

is weak Fano, it suffices to prove that the restricted linear system
|-K_{X}|_{\widetilde{S}}|
is base-point-free.
We will now show that that this condition can be reduced to verifying whether a linear system on

or
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is base-point-free, depending on whether

is singular and if so, whether

contains the singular point of

or not.

We will use this to deduce that
\pi|_{\widetilde{S}}
is an isomorphism if the hyperplane section

is smooth. If
S
is, however, singular, this only holds if the singular point

of
S
is not contained in

. If

is singular and

, the next lemma specifies properties of the morphism

. It also states how these properties can be used to determine a sufficient condition for showing that

is weak Fano.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

be a smooth irreducible curve of genus

and degree

that lies on a smooth hyperquadric

and denote the blowup of

along

by
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
. Suppose that

is contained in a singular hyperplane section

of

. Recall that the blowup

of

in its singular point

is the Hirzebruch surface

. Denote the strict transform of

in

by

4.4.
Curves on a hyperquadric section yielding a weak Fano threefold

In the following, we want to prove that if a smooth irreducible curve
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
of degree
d
and genus
g
is contained in a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
and in a smooth hyperquadric section of
Y
,
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{quadric}}
and
C
admits no 4-secant lines in the cases
(g,d)\in\{(0,6),\>(3,8)\}
, then the blowup
X
of
Y
along
C
is weak Fano.

The assumption that
C
is contained in a smooth hyperquadric section
S
of
Y
is stronger than the assumption from
B
that
C
lies on a smooth hypercubic section of
Y
. Nevertheless, the former is often easier to verify, since in that case,
S
is a smooth quartic del Pezzo surface whose structure is well understood

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
C\subseteq Y
be a smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
and degree
d
that lies on a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
and denote the blow up of
Y
along
C
by
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
.

Suppose that
C
is contained in a smooth hyperquadric section
S
of
Y
. If
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{quadric}}
and
C
does not have a 4-secant line in the cases
(g,d)\in\{(0,6),(3,8)\}
, then
X
is weak Fano.

Proof.

By
4.11
,
S
is a smooth quartic del Pezzo surface and the blowup
\eta:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in five distinct points
p_{1},\dots,p_{5}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
, three of which are not collinear. Denote the pullback of a general line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
under
\eta
by
L
and the exceptional divisors by
E_{i}=\eta^{-1}(\{p_{i}\})
for
i\in\{1,\dots,5\}
.
Moreover, we introduce the notation

\Lambda(a;b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4},b_{5}):=\left|aL-\sum_{i=1}^{5}b_{i}E_{i}\right|

to denote the linear system on
S
, whose general member is the strict transform of an irreducible curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and with multiplicities
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
in
p_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,5\}
.

We want to show that the restricted linear system
|-K_{X}|_{\widetilde{S}}|
is base-point-free, where
\widetilde{S}\subseteq X
denotes the strict transform of
S
under
\pi
.

Since
S
is smooth, the blowup
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
induces an isomorphism
\widetilde{S}\simeq S
by the universal property of blowups (
4.5
). Moreover, the smoothness of
S
implies that
E|_{\widetilde{S}}
is a section of
\pi|_{E}:E\rightarrow C
, where
E:=\pi^{-1}(C)
. That is to say, the section
E|_{\widetilde{S}}
is isomorphic to
C
via
\pi
. For both of these reasons, the linear system
|-K_{X}|_{\widetilde{S}}|\overset{\textup{\ref{lem:formula_for_anticanonical_divisor}}}{=}|3H_{X}|_{\widetilde{S}}-E|_{\widetilde{S}}|
on
\widetilde{S}
corresponds to the linear system
R:=|3H_{S}-C|
on
S
, where
H_{S}
denotes a general hyperplane section of
S
.

In particular, to prove that
|-K_{X}|_{\widetilde{S}}|
is base-point-free, it suffices to show that
R
is base-point-free. To do this, note that we can use the adjunction formula to deduce that a general hyperplane section
H_{S}
of
S
is given by
H_{S}=-K_{S}=3L-E_{1}-{\dots}-E_{5}
. In other words, we have
|H_{S}|=\Lambda(3;1,1,1,1,1)
.

Moreover,
4.12
gives us that
|C|=\Lambda(k;m_{1},\dots,m_{5})
, where
m_{1},\dots,m_{5}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
are given in
Table

7
. So
R=|3H_{S}-C|=\Lambda(9-k;3-m_{1},\dots,3-m_{5})
is the linear system given in the last column of
Table

7
, where
k
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{5}
are given by the third and fourth column of
Table

7
in dependence of the pair
(g,d)
.

In all cases,
R
can be written as a sum of base-point-free linear systems and is thus itself base-point-free (
3.2(ii)
). Indeed,
\Lambda(l;0,0,0,0,0)
corresponds to the linear system of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
l\in\{1,2\}
not containing
C
. This is base-point-free by Bertini’s theorem.

Furthermore,
\Lambda(2;1,1,1,1,0)
corresponds to the linear system of plane conics through four distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, three of which are not collinear; and
\Lambda(3;2,1,1,1,1)
corresponds to the linear system of plane cubics through 5 distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
that have a double point in one them such that no three points of
p_{1},\dots,p_{5}
are collinear. Both of these linear systems are base-point-free (follows from
[
Har77
, Prop. V.4.1 + V.4.3]
).

We have thus shown that the linear system
R
and hence also
|-K_{X}|_{\widetilde{S}}|
is indeed base-point-free. This implies that
-K_{X}
is nef (
3.3
). As moreover, all pairs
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{quadric}}
satisfy the inequality
26-3d+g>0
, we can use
3.8
to deduce that
X
is weak Fano.
∎

We can also show the converse direction of
4.14
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
C\subseteq Y
be a smooth irreducible curve of genus
g
and degree
d
that lies on a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
and denote the blow up of
Y
along
C
by
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
. Suppose furthermore that
C
is contained in a smooth hyperquadric section
S
of
Y
.

Then
X
is weak Fano if and only if

•

either
(g,d)\in\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,4)\}
,

•

or
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{quadric}}
and
C
does not have a 4-secant line if
(g,d)\in\{(0,6),(3,8)\}
.

Proof.

If
(g,d)\in\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,4)\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\textup{plane}}
, we have shown in
4.9
that
X
is weak Fano. If
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{quadric}}
and
C
does not have a 4-secant line in the cases
(g,d)\in\{(0,6),(3,8)\}
, it follows from
4.14
that
X
is weak Fano.

Suppose conversely that
X
is weak Fano. Then
3.6(iv)
shows that
C
cannot admit any 4-secant lines. We want to show that
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{plane}}\cup\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,4)\}
.

By
4.11
,
S
is a smooth quartic del Pezzo surface and the blowup
\eta:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in five distinct points
p_{1},\dots,p_{5}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
, three of which are not collinear. Denote the pullback of a general line under
\eta
by
L
and the exceptional divisors by
E_{i}=\eta^{-1}(\{p_{i}\})
for
i\in\{1,\dots,5\}
.

It follows from
4.11
that
S
contains exactly 16 lines and that
C=kL-\sum_{i=1}^{5}m_{i}E_{i}
is the strict transform of an irreducible curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
k
with multiplicities
m_{i}
in
p_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,5\}
.

Since
X
is weak Fano,
C
has at most 3-secant lines in
Y
(
3.6(iv)
). That is to say, for each of the 16 lines
D\subseteq S
of
S
, we have
C\cdot D=E\cdot\widetilde{D}\leq 3
, where
E:=\pi^{-1}(C)
and
\widetilde{D}\subseteq X
denotes the strict transform of
D
under the blowup
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
(and where the intersection is taken in
S
).

This imposes the following conditions on
k
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{5}
:

•

if
D\simeq E_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,5\}
, then
m_{j}\leq 3
;

•

if
D\simeq 2L-\sum_{i=1}^{5}E_{i}
, then
2k-\sum_{i=1}^{5}m_{i}\leq 3
;

•

if
D\simeq L-E_{i}-E_{j}
for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,5\}
with
i\not=j
, then
k-m_{i}-m_{j}\leq 3
.

Running numerically through all possible
k
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{5}
satisfying these conditions and computing the corresponding degree
d\geq 1
and genus
g\geq 0
with the formulas from
4.11
, it follows that
(g,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{quadric}}\cup\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,4)\}
.
∎

5.
Extremal contractions and Sarkisov links

In this section, we will analyze which extremal rays and which contractions of these rays may appear on the weak Fano threefold
X
that arises as the blowup of a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
along a smooth irreducible curve that is contained in
Y
. We will then study the question whether these extremal contractions give rise to a Sarkisov link and if so, which type of links may appear.

Recall that every birational map between Mori fiber spaces over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
with terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities is a composition of Sarkisov links and automorphisms (
[
HM13
, Thm. 1.1]
). We will use the classical definition of Sarkisov links via rank 2 fibrations as can be found in e.g.
[
BLZ21
, Def. 3.8]
. Recall, in particular, that projective varieties
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
appearing in a Sarkisov link
\chi:X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and terminal.

In §
5.1
, we give an overview of the possible cases that may occur and we introduce the notation that will be used throughout the rest of the section. In §
5.2
, we consider the case in where
X
is a Fano threefold, which always results in a Sarkisov link of type I or II. The case where
X
is not Fano is divided into two subsections: Either
X
admits two divisorial contractions and does not give rise to a Sarkisov link (§
5.3
). Otherwise,
X
yields a small anticanonical map, which can be flopped to again obtain a Sarkisov link of type I or II (§
5.4
).

We will construct the Sarkisov link coming from a curve of type
(g,d)=(1,4)
(see
5.1
) since we could not find a reference for it elsewhere. All other Sarkisov links have already been treated by other authors. So we will not construct these links explicitly nor prove why they are of their corresponding type. Rather, we will just compile the results and refer to the relevant sources.

5.1.
Extremal contractions on weak Fano threefolds arising as the blowup of a hyperquadric along a curve

Let
X
be a smooth weak Fano threefold of Picard rank two. Recall that
N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of 1-cycles on
X
with real coefficients modulo numerical equivalence and that the closed convex cone of curves
\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)
is the closure in
N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
of the set of classes of effective 1-cycles on
X
. Also recall that an extremal ray of
\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)
is a one-dimensional subcone
R\subseteq\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)
such that any
r,s\in\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)
with
r+s\in R
satisfy
r,s\in R
.

Since the Picard rank of
X
is two,
N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is a two-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space. As a closed convex cone not containing any lines,
\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)\subseteq N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
must be the convex hull of its extremal rays. For both of these reasons,
\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)
must have exactly two extremal rays.

As the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is nef, the intersection of
K_{X}
with any effective curve is non-negative. So one of the extremal rays
R_{1}\subseteq N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
must be
K_{X}
-negative, which means that it consists of classes of effective curves intersecting
K_{X}
negatively. If
X
is a Fano threefold, the second extremal ray
R_{2}\subseteq N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is also
K_{X}
-negative. Otherwise, if
X
is weak Fano but not Fano, the second extremal ray
R_{2}\subseteq N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
must be
K_{X}
-trivial. That is to say, it consists of classes of effective curves that are trivial against the canonical divisor
K_{X}
.

In both cases, the Cone Theorem (
[
Deb01
, Thm. 6.1]
) implies that
\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)=R_{1}+R_{2}
. Recall that a contraction of an extremal ray
R
is a projective morphism to a normal variety with connected fibers, which contracts all curves whose numerical equivalence classes belong to the extremal ray
R
. If it exists, such a contraction is unique (
[
Kol87
, 6.11]
).

If the extremal ray
R
is
K_{X}
-negative, the Contraction Theorem (
[
Deb01
, Thm. 7.39]
) guarantees the existence of a contraction morphism
\phi_{R}:X\rightarrow Z
of
R
for some projective variety
Z
. We can use Mori’s classification of
K_{X}
-negative extremal contractions from smooth projective threefolds (
[
Mor82
, Thm. 3.3-3.5]
) to classify
\phi_{R}:X\rightarrow Z
: Since
X
is smooth and of Picard rank two (so it cannot be a primitive Fano threefold), there are two possible cases: Either
\phi_{R}
is a divisorial contraction and then
\phi_{R}:X\rightarrow Z
is the blowup of
Z
in either a point or along a smooth irreducible curve. The second possibility is that
\phi_{R}
is of fibring type and
Z
is smooth. Then
\phi_{R}
is either a conic bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
(if
\dim(Z)=2
) or
\phi_{R}
is a del Pezzo fibration over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(if
\dim(Z)=1
).

We now suppose that
X
is a weak Fano threefold that is obtained by blowing up a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
along a smooth irreducible curve
C\subseteq Y
. As before, we denote the blowup map by
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
, the exceptional divisor by
E:=\pi^{-1}(C)\subseteq X
and a general hyperplane section of
Y
by
H_{Y}
. A curve
\Gamma\subseteq X
is contracted by
\pi
if and only if it is a fiber of
\pi|_{E}:E\rightarrow C
. Every such fiber
f\subseteq E
satisfies

K_{X}\cdot f\overset{\ref{lem:anticanonical_divisor}}{=}(-3\pi^{*}(H_{Y})+E)\cdot f=E\cdot f=-1.

So
\pi
is the contraction of the
K_{X}
-negative extremal ray
R_{1}
. More precisely,
\pi
is a divisorial contraction as the exceptional locus of
\pi
is the prime divisor
E\subseteq X
.

The type of the second extremal contraction on
X
depends on the anticanonical map
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
associated to the anticanonical linear system
|-mK_{X}|
for
m\gg 0
, where
X^{\prime}
is some projective threefold. The map
\psi
is an isomorphism if and only if
-K_{X}
is ample, or equivalently, if
X
is a Fano threefold. In this case, there is a second
K_{X}
-negative extremal contraction, which yields a Sarkisov link of type I or II (§
5.2
).

If
\psi
is not an isomorphism, we can still deduce a lot about the anticanonical map
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
: Since
-K_{X}
is big and nef, it follows from the Base Point Free Theorem (
[
PS99
, Thm. 1.3.6]
) that
|-mK_{X}|
is base-point-free for
m\gg 0
. On the one hand, this guarantees that
\psi
is a morphism. On the other hand, this implies that
\psi
has connected fibers and that its image
X^{\prime}
is a normal variety (
[
Deb01
, Prop. 7.6(b)]
). That is to say,
\psi
is a contraction morphism.

In fact,
\psi
is the contraction of the second
K_{X}
-trivial extremal ray: Let
\Gamma\subseteq X
be an effective curve that is trivial against
K_{X}
. Suppose to the contrary that it is not contracted by
\psi
and mapped to a curve
\Gamma^{\prime}:=\psi(\Gamma)\subseteq Y
. As
\psi
is the map associated to the linear system
|-mK_{X}|
, the pullback of the general hyperplane section
H_{Y}
of
Y
under
\psi
is precisely
-mK_{X}
. So we find the contradiction

0<H_{Y}\cdot\Gamma^{\prime}=\psi^{*}(H_{Y})\cdot\psi^{*}(\Gamma^{\prime})=(-mK_{X})\cdot\Gamma=0.

Therefore, the morphism
\psi
is indeed the contraction of the
K_{X}
-trivial extremal ray. Moreover, we know that
\psi
is birational since
-K_{X}
is big. By the classification of the contraction of extremal rays (see e.g.
[
KM98
, Prop. 2.5]
),
\psi
is thus either a divisorial contraction or small.

If
\psi
is a divisorial contraction, it does not yield a Sarkisov link (§
5.3
). If the map
\psi
is however small, it is a flopping contraction. So we can perform a flop
\chi:X\rightarrow X^{+}
(
[
PS99
, Thm 1.4.15 + Lemma 4.1.1]
), which gives rise to another weak Fano threefold
X^{+}
having one
K_{X^{+}}
-negative and one
K_{X^{+}}
-trivial extremal ray. Depending on the type of
K_{X^{+}}
-negative extremal contraction on
X^{+}
, we again obtain a Sarkisov link of type I or II (§
5.4
).

5.2.
Fano threefolds

Suppose that the anticanonical map
\psi
is an isomorphism. Then
-K_{X}
is ample and so,
X
is a Fano threefold. As we have argued in
Section

5.1
,
X
then admits two
K_{X}
-negative extremal contractions. One of them is the blowup map
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
, which is a divisorial contraction. Denoting the second extremal contraction by
\phi:X\rightarrow Z
for a projective variety
Z
, we use Mori’s classification (
[
Mor82
, Thm. 3.3-3.5]
) to deduce that we are in one of the following two cases:

(1)

\phi:X\rightarrow Z
is a divisorial contraction and then it is the blowup of
Z
in a point or along a smooth irreducible curve. This case yields a Sarkisov link
Y\dashrightarrow Z
of type II and appears for the four pairs
(g,d)\in\{(0,3),\>(0,4),\>(1,4),\>(1,5)\}
(see
Table

8
).

(2)

Z
is smooth and
\phi:X\rightarrow Z
is of fibring type. More precisely, we have the following dichotomy:

(a)

If
\dim(Z)=2
, then
\phi
is a conic bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

(b)

If
\dim(Z)=1
, then
\phi
is a del Pezzo fibration over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

5.3.
Weak Fano threefolds not yielding a Sarkisov link

Suppose that the anticanonical map
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is not an isomorphism, or equivalently, the weak Fano threefold
X
is not Fano. As we have seen in
Section

5.1
, in that case, the birational morphism
\psi
corresponds to the contraction of the
K_{X}
-trivial extremal ray of
X
.

In this section, we suppose furthermore that there exists an effective divisor on
X
that is contracted by
\psi
. Then there are infinitely many
K_{X}
-trivial curves and the exceptional locus
E\subseteq X
of
\psi
is of codimension 1. So
\psi
cannot be small (i.e. isomorphic in codimension 1). By the classification of the contraction of extremal rays (see e.g.
[
KM98
, Prop. 2.5]
),
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is then a divisorial contraction. That is, the exceptional divisor
E\subseteq X
of
\psi
is a prime divisor of
X
.

By the classification of
[
JPR05
]
, we know that
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is the blowup of
X^{\prime}
along a smooth curve
C^{\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
and that the normal threefold
X^{\prime}
is a Fano threefold of Picard rank 1. Moreover,
X^{\prime}
has compound Du Val singularities along
C^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}
is Gorenstein (meaning
X^{\prime}
is Cohen-Macaulay and
K_{X^{\prime}}
is a Cartier divisor).

We will now show that
X^{\prime}
is canonical but not terminal. Since
K_{X^{\prime}}
is Cartier, it can be pulled back under the resolution
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
and there exists
a\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
K_{X}=\psi^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}})+aE
.
Considering an effective curve
\Gamma\subseteq X
contracted by
\psi
, we note that
\psi^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}})\cdot\Gamma=K_{X^{\prime}}\cdot\psi_{*}(\Gamma)=0
. Since
\psi
is a contraction of the
K_{X}
-trivial extremal ray, we deduce that
0=K_{X}\cdot\Gamma=aE\cdot\Gamma
. This implies that the
\psi
-exceptional divisor
aE
is
\psi
-nef. By the negativity lemma (
[
Kaw24
, Thm. 1.3.9]
), it follows that
-aE
is effective. However,
E
is an effective prime divisor by assumption. So we must have
a=0
.

We have therewith shown that the normal threefold
X^{\prime}
is indeed only canonical and not terminal. In particular, there exists no Sarkisov link to
X^{\prime}
.

This case can occur for the six pairs
(g,d)\in\{(0,4),\>(2,5),\>(3,8),\>(6,10),\>(8,10),\>(11,12)\}
(see
Table

10
). They were all treated in
[
JPR05
]
explicitly.

The following two examples study when the anticanonical map corresponding to a curve
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
of type
(g,d)\in\{(0,4),\>(3,8)\}
is divisorial.

These examples also explain why these two pairs yield two different weak Fano threefolds: If
(g,d)=(0,4)
, then the blowup
X
of a smooth hyperquadric
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
along
C\subseteq Y
might be Fano (see
Table

8
) or
X
might not give rise to a Sarkisov link (see
5.2
). Similarly, if
(g,d)=(3,8)
, then
X
might give rise to a Sarkisov link of type II (see
Table

11
) or to no link at all (see
5.3
).

Example 5.2
.

Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a smooth hyperquadric and
C\subseteq Y
a smooth irreducible curve of degree 4 and genus 0. Suppose that
C
is contained in a hyperplane
H\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Then the hyperplane section
S=H\cap Y
of
Y
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and in
\textup{Pic}(S)
, we have

, where

is the linear equivalence class of a fiber of the
i-
th-projection
pr_{i}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
(
4.4
).

Every fiber
f_{p}:=pr_{2}^{-1}(\{p\})=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{p\}
of a point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a line in
S
and is contained in the linear system
|f_{2}|
. It thus satisfies
f_{p}\cdot C=f_{2}\cdot(3f_{1}+f_{2})=3
by the intersection rules from
4.2(i)
. So there are infinitely many 3-secant lines of
C
in
S
.

Denoting the blowup of
Y
along
C
by
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
and the exceptional divisor by
E=\pi^{-1}(C)
, we know by
4.9
that
X
is weak Fano. Moreover, the discussion in
Section

5.1
shows that the anticanonical map
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
to some projective normal threefold
X^{\prime}
is the contraction of the
K_{X}
-trivial extremal ray of
\overline{\textup{NE}}(X)
.

The fibers
f_{p}
with

E\cdot\widetilde{f_{p}}=C\cdot f_{p}=3
, where
\widetilde{f_{p}}\subseteq X

\pi
. It follows that all
\widetilde{f_{p}}

3.6(iv)
) and are thus contracted by
\psi
. The union of all these contracted curves is equal to the strict transform of

. Therefore,
\psi
contracts the surface

, which is the strict transform of

under

and a prime divisor of

.
In other words, the anticanonical map

Let

of the conics

.

:
4.14
guarantees that the blowup
X
of

along

is weak Fano and we have argued in

5.1
that the anticanonical map
\psi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
to some projective normal threefold

is the contraction of the

-trivial extremal ray of

.

All the strict transforms

of the conics

and

A rich structure of renormalization group flows for Higgs-like models in 4 dimensions.
André LeClair

Abstract
We consider 2 2 coupled Higgs doublets which transform in the usual way under SU(2) ⊗ \otimes U(1). By constructing marginal operators which satisfy an operator product expansion based on the SU(2) Lie algebra, we can obtain a rich pattern of renormalization group (RG) flows which includes lines of fixed points and more interestingly, cyclic RG flows which are unavoidable in this model. The hamiltonian is pseudo-hermitian, H † = 𝒦 ​ H ​ 𝒦 † H^{\dagger}={\cal K}H{\cal K}^{\dagger} with 𝒦 {\cal K} unitary satisfying 𝒦 2 = 1 {\cal K}^{2}=1 , thus the model is non-unitary. The hamiltonian still has real eigenvalues, but the non-unitarity is manifested in negative norm states. One can use the operator 𝒦 {\cal K} to define projection operators onto positive norm states, and in this sub-Hilbert space the time evolution is unitary with positive probabilities. Upon spontaneous symmetry breaking, the Higgs fields have an infinite number of vacuum expectation values v n v_{n} which satisfy “Russian Doll” scaling v n ∼ e 2 ​ n ​ λ v_{n}\sim e^{2n\lambda} where n = 1 , 2 , 3 , … n=1,2,3,\ldots and λ \lambda is the period of one RG cycle which is an RG invariant. We speculate that this Russian Doll RG flow can perhaps resolve the so-called hierarchy problem and may shed light on the origin of ‘‘families” in the Standard Model of particle physics. 1 1 1 Note added: While this article was under review, we extended the 1-loop calculations in this article to higher orders, which confirmed that the main features of the RG flows considered in this paper persist RDolls2 , while leaving aside the more speculative ideas on the hierarchy problem and the origin of families. In this article the focus is on the cyclic RG flows. Below we incorporate some of these results as “Note added” footnotes.

II.1
The free theory and its discrete symmetries

Throughout this article, we consider relativistic quantum fields in
D=d+1=4
spacetime dimensions, apart from the brief review of some results

Introduce two independent doublets of complex bosonic spin-0 fields,

Under SU(2) transformations,

These fields transform as two Higgs doublets.

{\cal S}_{0}=\int d^{4}x\left(\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}\Phi+\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{\Phi}^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}\widetilde{\Phi}\right),

\displaystyle\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\dagger}(x)

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{d^{3}{\bf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\bf{k}}}}\left(a_{-}({\bf{k}})e^{ik\cdot x}+a^{\dagger}_{+}({\bf{k}})e^{-ik\cdot x}\right),

II.2
Pseudo-hermitian marginal perturbations

As discussed in the Introduction, the original goal of this paper was to find marginal perturbations with potentially interesting patterns of RG flows
for quartic interactions of scalar fields.
The natural operators,
(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}
are important parts of the standard Higgs potential for
\Phi
, however the RG flows are not very fertile and the theories are generally not well-defined in the UV. In fact, this is essentially the origin of the so-called hierarchy problem.
As we now describe, there is a different construction of marginal operators which can have a much richer pattern of RG flows.
This will require that the resulting hamiltonian be pseudo-hermitian (
4
). To motivate it, note that
there is another discrete symmetry of the free hamiltonian in the form (
8
):

{\cal K}a_{\pm}({\bf{k}}){\cal K}=\pm a_{\pm}({\bf{k}}),~~~{\cal K}a^{\dagger}_{\pm}({\bf{k}}){\cal K}=\pm a^{\dagger}_{\pm}({\bf{k}}),~~~~~\Longrightarrow~~~{\cal K}H_{0}{\cal K}=H_{0}=H^{\dagger}_{0}.

(15)

Comparing with (
14
), we can interpret the operators
a_{\pm}^{\dagger}
as creating states that are even or odd under
{\cal P}{\cal T}
.

The operator
{\cal K}
does not act locally on the fields
\Phi
, unlike
{\cal C},{\cal P},{\cal T}
.
Let also point out that
the operator
{\cal K}
can be expressed in terms of the
U(1)
charge
{\bf h}
operator, although at this stage it’s not clear this is
necessary. In any case, one has

[{\bf h},a_{\pm}]=\mp a_{\pm},~[{\bf h},a^{\dagger}_{\pm}]=\pm a^{\dagger}_{\pm}
.
Separating
{\bf h}
into two parts,

{\bf h}={\bf h}_{+}-{\bf h}_{-},~{\bf h}_{\pm}=\int d^{3}{\bf{k}}\,\,a^{\dagger}_{\pm}({\bf{k}})a_{\pm}({\bf{k}})
.
One can then identify

{\cal K}=e^{i\vartheta{\bf h}_{-}},~~~~~~~\vartheta=\pi.

(16)

Furthermore,
{\cal K}^{2}=1
if
{\bf h}_{-}
has an integer spectrum of charges.

For any operator
A
, define it’s
{\cal K}
-hermitian conjugate
A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}
as follows

A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}={\cal K}A^{\dagger}{\cal K}.

(17)

The operator
\Phi^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\Phi
is not its own hermitian conjugate, but is invariant under
{\dagger_{\kappa}}
conjugation:

(\Phi^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\Phi)^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=\Phi^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\Phi.

(18)

Based on this, for a hermitian
2\times 2

J^{a}\equiv\Phi^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\tau^{a}\Phi=\sum_{i,j}\Phi^{\dagger_{\kappa}}_{i}\tau^{a}_{ij}\Phi_{j}~~~~~~(\tau^{a})^{\dagger}=\tau^{a},

(19)

and similarly for
\widetilde{J}^{a}
. Our reason for defining the operators
J^{a}
is the interesting OPE such operators have, as described in Section IV, which leads to simple algebraic formulas for their beta-functions.

We can now define our models. Define the marginal operators

{\cal O}^{A}(x)=\sum_{a,b}d^{A}_{ab}\,J^{a}(x)\widetilde{J}^{b}(x)

(20)

for some coefficients
d^{A}_{ab}
. The models are then defined by the action

{\cal S}={\cal S}_{0}+4\pi^{2}\int d^{4}x\,\sum_{A}g_{A}{\cal O}^{A}(x).

(21)

The factor of

is introduced to simplify the beta-functions of the next section.
The operators

J^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=J,~~~~~\widetilde{J}^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=\widetilde{J}.

(22)

Thus the interacting hamiltonian is pseudo-hermitian:

H^{\dagger}={\cal K}H{\cal K},~~~\Longrightarrow~~H^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=H.

(23)

We interpret
{\cal K}
-conjugation
{\dagger_{\kappa}}
as insertions of the operator
{\cal K}
in correlation functions. In the next section we show that the above pseudo-hermitian hamiltonian defines a unitary time evolution if one incorporates
{\cal K}

III
Projecting onto positive norm states and unitarity

Our models could have applications to statistical mechanics in 4 spatial dimensions where the positivity constraints of unitarity
are not so stringent. However, for potential applications to real Higgs physics in
3+1
spacetime dimensions, a consistent quantum mechanics would be desirable, in fact necessary, and this is the subject of this section.
The subject of Quantum Mechanics for pseudo-hermitian hamiltonians is by now well developed, with extensive reviews

Mosta1
;
Mosta2
;
Mosta3
. In this section we summarize the main properties as they specialize to our model.

(i)
Hamiltonian and Hilbert space

Assume we are given a Hilbert space
{\cal H}
with a conventional positive definite inner product. Namely
for
|\psi\rangle\in{\cal H}
, there exists an inner product such that
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle
is positive definite.
At time
t=0
the states need to be properly normalized such that
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1
.
There exists a basis in
{\cal H}
,
|\psi_{n}\rangle\in{\cal H}
where for simplicity of notation we assume
n
is discrete,

n=1,2,\ldots\infty
. Then

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{n}c_{n}\,|\psi_{n}\rangle,~~~~\langle\psi_{m}|\psi_{n}\rangle=\delta_{nm},

(24)

for some complex numbers
c_{n}
.
Then

\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1,~~~~~~\Longrightarrow~~~\sum_{n}c_{n}^{*}c_{n}=1,

(25)

where
c_{n}^{*}
denotes complex conjugation.
|c_{n}|^{2}
thus represents the probability that
|\psi\rangle
is measured to be in the state
|\psi_{n}\rangle
. Given a hamiltonian
H
, or any other operator
A
on
{\cal H}
, then
A^{\dagger}
is defined in the standard way with respect to the above inner product.

(ii)
Modified metric for pseudo-hermitian Hamiltonians

Suppose the hamiltonian operator
H
is pseudo-hermitian with the structure in (
4
), which we repeat here:

H^{\dagger}={\cal K}\,H\,{\cal K}^{\dagger},~~~{\cal K}^{\dagger}{\cal K}=1,~~~~~{\cal K}^{\dagger}={\cal K},~~~~\Longrightarrow~~~{\cal K}^{2}=1.

(26)

This leads us to define a new inner product which includes an insertion of the operator
{\cal K}
:

\langle\!\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle\!\rangle\equiv\langle\psi^{\prime}|{\cal K}|\psi\rangle.

(27)

In simple words, kets have an accompanying
{\cal K}
but bras do not.
In general, this new inner product has negative norm states. Below we will show that such states can be consistently projected out.

(iii)
Pseudo-hermitian operators correspond to observables with real eigenvalues

For any operator
A
on
{\cal H}
, define its pseudo-hermitian conjugate as follows:

A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\equiv{\cal K}\,A^{\dagger}\,{\cal K}.

(28)

Then the hamiltonian is pseudo-hermitian:

H^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=H.

(29)

A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}
is the proper conjugation based on the
{\cal K}
-metric in (
27
), namely

\langle\!\langle\psi^{\prime}|A|\psi\rangle\!\rangle^{*}=\langle\!\langle\psi|A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle,

(30)

where
*
denotes ordinary complex conjugation.
We define a pseudo-hermitian operator
A
as one that satisfies
A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=A
.
From (
30
), one concludes that pseudo-hermitian operators, in particular the hamiltonian H, has real eigenvalues.
Any operator satisfying
A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=A
in principle can correspond to an observable.
One can easily establish that this pseudo-hermitian adjoint satisfies the usual rules, e.g.

\displaystyle(AB)^{\dagger_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B^{\dagger_{\kappa}}A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\,,

\displaystyle(aA+bB)^{\dagger_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{*}A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}+b^{*}B^{\dagger_{\kappa}}\,,

(31)

where
A,B
are operators and
a,b
are complex numbers.

(iv)
Conservation of probabilities

The hamiltonian determines the time evolution of a state
|\psi\rangle=|\psi(t=0)\rangle
, namely

|\psi(t)\rangle=U(t)\,|\psi\rangle
, where
U(t)=e^{-iHt}
. By conservation of probability we mean that the norm of states is preserved under time evolution. More generally:

\langle\!\langle\psi^{\prime}(t)|\psi(t)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\psi^{\prime}|e^{iH^{\dagger}t}\,{\cal K}\,e^{-iHt}\,|\psi\rangle=\langle\psi^{\prime}|{\cal K}e^{iH^{\dagger}t}{\cal K}^{2}\,e^{-iHt}\,|\psi\rangle=\langle\!\langle\psi^{\prime}(0)|\psi(0)\rangle\!\rangle

(32)

In general, the modified
{\cal K}
-metric
\langle\!\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle\!\rangle
introduces negative norm states which imply negative probabilities. The above statement of unitarity just states that the sum of these probabilities is maintained under time evolution,
but one must still deal with negative norm states.

(v)
Projection onto positive norm states

For the main example of this paper, the
{\cal K}
-metric on the Hilbert space is indefinite, namely there are negative norm states which naively implies negative probabilities, which are clearly unphysical. However the properties of
{\cal K}
naturally leads to projection operators which can fix this problem.
Since
{\cal K}^{2}=1
, states can be classified according to
{\cal K}=\pm 1
. Namely,

{\cal K}|\psi_{n}\rangle=(-1)^{k_{n}}\,|\psi_{n}\rangle,~~~~k_{n}\in\{0,1\}.

(33)

In fixing the norm of a state to be unity, the sum involves negative terms, which would be interpreted as negative probabilities:

\langle\!\langle\psi|\psi\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{n}(-1)^{k_{n}}\,|c_{n}|^{2}=1.

(34)

Define the projectors

P_{\pm}=\dfrac{1\pm{\cal K}}{2}.

(35)

They satisfy the usual algebra of projectors:

P_{+}+P_{-}=1,~~~~~P_{\pm}^{2}=P_{\pm},~~~~~P_{+}P_{-}=0.

(36)

In addition, one has

{\cal K}P_{\pm}=P_{\pm}{\cal K}=\pm P_{\pm}.

(37)

Thus the original Hilbert space decomposes as

{\cal H}={\cal H}_{+}\oplus{\cal H}_{-},~~~~~|\psi_{\pm}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{\pm},~~~~~{\cal K}|\psi_{\pm}\rangle=\pm|\psi_{\pm}\rangle.

(38)

One has

\langle\!\langle\psi_{\pm}|\psi_{\pm}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\psi|P_{\pm}{\cal K}P_{\pm}|\psi\rangle=\pm\langle\psi_{\pm}|\psi_{\pm}\rangle,~~~~~\langle\!\langle\psi_{-}|\psi_{+}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\psi|P_{+}{\cal K}P_{-}|\psi\rangle=0

(39)

If we project onto the sub Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{+}
, then all states
|\psi_{n}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{+}
have positive norm, i.e.

k_{n}=0
, which implies positive probabilities
|c_{n}|^{2}\geq 0
. This positivity is preserved under time evolution:

\langle\!\langle\psi_{\pm}(t)|\psi_{\pm}(t)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\psi_{\pm}(0)|\psi_{\pm}(0)\rangle\!\rangle,~~~~~\langle\!\langle\psi_{-}(t)|\psi_{+}(t)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\psi_{-}(0)|\psi_{+}(0)\rangle\!\rangle=0.

(40)

In general there could be operators
A
on
{\cal H}
that mix
{\cal H}_{+}
and
{\cal H}_{-}
. If one projects
{\cal H}
onto
{\cal H}_{+}
, then one should restrict to operators which when acting on
|\psi_{+}\rangle
yields a state that is also in
{\cal H}_{+}
. In other words

\langle\!\langle\psi_{-}|A|\psi_{+}\rangle\!\rangle=0.

(41)

It is easy to see that this requires
A
to be
{\cal K}
-invariant:

A={\cal K}A{\cal K}.

(42)

The proof is simple:

\langle\!\langle\psi_{-}|A|\psi_{+}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\psi_{-}|{\cal K}A|\psi_{+}\rangle=-\langle\psi_{-}|A|\psi_{+}\rangle=\langle\psi_{-}|A{\cal K}|\psi_{+}\rangle=+\langle\psi_{-}|A|\psi_{+}\rangle,

(43)

which implies (
41
).
The condition (
42
) implies

A^{\dagger}={\cal K}A^{\dagger}{\cal K}
. Thus
A^{\dagger_{\kappa}}=A
requires
A^{\dagger}=A
.

In summary, if we project onto the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{+}
, then all probabilities are positive, and remain positive under time evolution. Let us now turn to the specifics of our model.
The Hilbert space
{\cal H}
has a basis consisting of n-particle states with specific momenta:

|\psi\rangle=|({\bf{k}}_{1},s_{1}),({\bf{k}}_{2},s_{2}),\ldots({\bf{k}}_{n},s_{n})\rangle\equiv a^{\dagger}_{s_{1}}({\bf{k}}_{1})a^{\dagger}_{s_{2}}({\bf{k}}_{2})\cdots a^{\dagger}_{s_{n}}({\bf{k}}_{n})|0\rangle~~~~~s_{i}\in\{\pm\}.

(44)

Mapping the labels
s_{i}=\pm
to the integers
s_{i}=\pm 1
, define
\widehat{s}=(1-s)/2\in\{0,1\}
.
Then

{\cal K}\,|\psi\rangle=(-1)^{\sum_{i}\widehat{s}_{i}}\,\,|\psi\rangle.

(45)

This simple action on momentum space acts non-locally on the fields
\Phi(x)
.
In short,
{\cal K}=1
for states with an even number of
a^{\dagger}_{-}({\bf{k}})
, whereas
{\cal K}=-1
for an odd number.
Projection onto states of positive norm with
{\cal K}=1
leads to the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{+}
where states created by

a^{\dagger}_{-}
must come in pairs, reminiscent of Cooper pairs.

Above we interpreted the operators
a_{\pm}^{\dagger}
as creating
{\cal P}{\cal T}
even/odd states. Thus projecting onto

{\cal H}_{+}
breaks
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetry.

VII
Speculation on the origin of families

In this section we explore the possibility that a cyclic RG may be at the origin of the existence of families in the Standard Model. We do not attempt to prematurely build a complete model that is fully consistent with the experiments,
the main reason being
the many stringent experimental constraints confirming the Standard Model that need to be scrutinized, and we lack the expertise to do this.
To repeat the idea stated in the Introduction,
we speculate that each family is one of an infinite number of Russian Dolls with nearly identical properties apart from the large disparity in their masses.

The first family of quarks and leptons are the SU(2) doublets

\begin{pmatrix}u\\
d\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}e\\
\nu_{e}\end{pmatrix}.

(104)

If this were the only family, the theory is strongly constrained by the fundamental principles of gauge symmetry and the Higgs mechanism.
What is mysterious is that the above family repeats itself with ever increasing masses leading to many more unexplained free parameters, the masses themselves,
and the CKM matrix which incorporates mixing between families. These are the two additional families
\begin{pmatrix}c\\
s\end{pmatrix},\!\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\
\nu_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}t\\
b\end{pmatrix},\!\begin{pmatrix}\tau\\
\nu_{\tau}\end{pmatrix}
. There are no symmetry arguments for the existence of these additional families, and this is part of their mystery.
Needless to say, the hierarchy and structure of Yukawa couplings are a major open question.

The additional families have essentially identical properties as far as gauge couplings, and this suggests the existence of families has an origin that is separate from the gauge symmetry, and such an explanation could come from the Higgs sector, which is the least understood aspect of the Standard Model.
One way to characterize how the different families are nearly identical copies is based on ’t Hooft anomaly cancelation

Anomalies
;
Bonora
. The various quantum numbers of the first family are such that all gauge anomalies precisely cancel, and this is a non-trivial matter. More importantly, the anomalies cancel for each individual family separately, which is an indication that they are somehow copies of the first family, apart from their masses. The Higgs sector does not contribute to these anomalies, which is also important for our considerations. There is already a “small hierarchy” issue, in that the ratio of the top quark to up quark mass is about
10^{5}
which is not understood; it is just a free parameter in the Standard Model attributed to the strength of Yukawa couplings to the Higgs field.

VIII
Discussion and closing remarks

The main result of this paper is the construction of new kinds of marginal operators for scalar fields in 4D which have a rich structure of
RG flows including cyclic flows, and our focus has been on the latter. Such operators make the hamiltonian pseudo-hermitian
H^{\dagger}={\cal K}H{\cal K}
, and thus formally non-unitary. The non-unitarity is manifested in negative norm states. We showed one can use the operator
{\cal K}
to project onto positive norm states
in a way that still leads to a unitary time evolution and can thus describe closed quantum systems.
These results are general and stand on their own, and apply to other Lie groups. They could have applications to the Standard Model of particle physics in the Higgs sector. For instance, two Higgs doublets can exhibit both SSB and a cyclic RG flow. To support the validity of our interpretation, we reviewed exact results for
QFT’s in 2D with beta functions that are identical those for our 2-Higgs models. A general feature of cyclic theories is an infinite spectrum of
physical masses or condensates with
Russian Doll scaling. We then took the liberty to speculate that a cyclic RG can perhaps resolve the hierarchy problem,
and furthermore, could shed light on the origin of families in the Standard Model,
where each new massive family is identified with the next smaller and more massive Russian Doll.

This work leads to many open questions, and we list a few.

Quantization for a condensation system
Shivam Dubey, Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury, Saurabh Verma

Abstract
. For a given r ∈ ( 0 , + ∞ ) r\in(0,+\infty) , the quantization dimension of order r r , if it exists, denoted by D r ​ ( μ ) D_{r}(\mu) , represents the rate at which the n n th quantization error of order r r approaches to zero as the number of elements n n in an optimal set of n n -means for μ \mu tends to infinity. If D r ​ ( μ ) D_{r}(\mu) does not exist, we define D ¯ r ​ ( μ ) \underline{D}_{r}(\mu) and D ¯ r ​ ( μ ) \overline{D}_{r}(\mu) as the lower and the upper quantization dimensions of μ \mu of order r r , respectively. In this paper, we investigate the quantization dimension of the condensation measure μ \mu associated with a condensation system ( { S j } j = 1 N , ( p j ) j = 0 N , ν ) . (\{S_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},(p_{j})_{j=0}^{N},\nu). We provide two examples: one where ν \nu is an infinite discrete distribution on ℝ \mathbb{R} , and one where ν \nu is a uniform distribution on ℝ \mathbb{R} . For both the discrete and uniform distributions ν \nu , we determine the optimal sets of n n -means, and calculate the quantization dimensions of condensation measures μ \mu , and show that the D r ​ ( μ ) D_{r}(\mu) -dimensional quantization coefficients do not exist. Moreover, we demonstrate that the lower and upper quantization coefficients are finite and positive.

1.
Introduction

Various types of dimensions, including Hausdorff and packing dimensions or the lower and the upper box-counting dimensions are important to characterize the complexity of highly irregular sets. In recent years, paralleling methods have been adopted to study the corresponding dimensions of measures (see
[
8
]
). In this paper, we investigate the quantization dimensions of the
condensation measures
. The quantization problem consists in studying the quantization error induced by the approximation of a given probability measure with discrete probability measures of finite supports. This problem originated in information theory and some engineering technology. For rigorous mathematical foundation of this theory one can see Graf-Luschgy’s book
[
13
]
. Further theoretical results and promising applications are contained in
[
1
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
18
,
28
]
. Two important objects in the quantization theory are the quantization coefficient and the quantization dimension.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on a
d
-dimensional normed space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with a metric
\rho
induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|
.
Let
r\in(0,+\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of natural numbers, and
\alpha\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a locally finite (i.e., intersection of
\alpha
with any bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is finite) subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This implies that
\alpha
is countable and closed. Then, the
n
th quantization error
of order
r
for
\mu
is defined by

V_{n,r}(\mu):=\text{inf}\{\int\rho(x,\alpha)^{r}d\mu(x):\alpha\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n\},

where
\rho(x,\alpha)
denotes the distance from the element
x
to the set
\alpha
with respect to a given norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
\int\|x\|^{r}d\mu(x)<\infty
, then there is some set
\alpha
for which the infimum is achieved (see
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
). Such a set
\alpha
is called an
optimal set of
n
-means
, or
optimal set of
n
-quantizers
. For some recent work in this direction one can see
[
9
,
6
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.
An optimal set
\alpha
of
n
-means can then be used to give a best approximation of
\mu
by a discrete probability supported on a set with no more than
n
elements. This can be done by giving each element
a\in\alpha
a mass corresponding to
\mu(M(a|\alpha))
, where
M(a|\alpha)
is the set of elements
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
which are nearest to
a
, i.e.,
\rho(x,\alpha)=\rho(x,a)
. So, the set
\{M(a|\alpha):a\in\alpha\}
is the
Voronoi diagram
or
Voronoi tessellation
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to
\alpha
. Of course, the idea of ‘best approximation’ is, in general,
dependent on the choice of
r
. Such a set
\alpha
for which the infimum occurs and contains no more than
n
elements is called an
optimal set of
n
-means
, or
optimal set of
n
-quantizers
(of order
r
). An optimal set of
n
-means for a probability distribution
\mu
is also referred to as
V_{n,r}(\mu)
-optimal set. The collection of all optimal sets of
n
-means for a probability distribution
\mu
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{n,r}(\mu)
. It can be shown that for a continuous Borel probability measure
\mu
, an optimal set of
n
-means always has exactly
n
elements (see
[
13
]
).
The numbers

\underline{D}_{r}(\mu):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log V_{n,r}(\mu)},\text{ and }\overline{D}_{r}(\mu):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log V_{n,r}(\mu)}

are, respectively, called the
lower
and the
upper quantization dimensions
of
\mu
of order
r
. If
\overline{D}_{r}(\mu)=\underline{D}_{r}(\mu)
, the common value is called the
quantization dimension
of
\mu
of order
r
, and is denoted by
D_{r}(\mu)
. Quantization dimension measures the speed at which the specified measure of the error approaches to zero as
n
tends to infinity.
For any
\kappa_{r}>0
, the two numbers

\underline{Q}_{r}^{\kappa_{r}}(\mu):=\liminf_{n}n^{r/\kappa_{r}}V_{n,r}(\mu)\text{ and }\overline{Q}_{r}^{\kappa_{r}}(\mu):=\limsup_{n}n^{r/\kappa_{r}}V_{n,r}(\mu)

are, respectively, called the
\kappa_{r}
-dimensional lower
and the
upper quantization coefficients
for
\mu
(of order
r
). The quantization coefficients provide us with more accurate information about the asymptotics of the quantization error than the quantization dimension. Compared to the calculation of quantization dimension, it is usually much more difficult to determine whether the lower and the upper quantization coefficients are finite and positive.
Let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support
C
, and
(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{N})
be a probability vector with
p_{j}>0
for all
1\leq j\leq N
, where
N
is a positive integer and
N\geq 2
. Let
S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{N}
be a family of contractive mappings on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exist a unique Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a unique nonempty compact set
K
satisfying

\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}\mu\circ S_{j}^{-1}+p_{0}\nu\text{ and }K=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}(K)\cup C.

Following
[
2
,
15
]
, we call
(\{S_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},(p_{j})_{j=0}^{N},\nu)
a condensation system. The measure
\mu
is called the
attracting measure
or the
condensation measure
for
(\{S_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},(p_{j})_{j=0}^{N},\nu)
, and the set
K
, which is the support of the measure
\mu
, is called the
attractor
or
invariant set
for the system. If each
S_{j}
is contractive similitude, then such a measure
\mu
is also termed as
inhomogeneous self-similar measure
(see
[
20
]
). There are several works done on condensation system, for example,
L^{q}
spectra and Rényi dimensions of inhomogeneous self-similar measures were studied by Olsen-Snigireva, and then by Liszka (see
[
16
,
21
]
).
Let
\mu
be a condensation measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
associated with a condensation system
(\{S_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},(p_{j})_{j=0}^{N},\nu)
, where
\nu
is any Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a compact support. Let
K
be the attractor of the condensation system, and
C
be the support of
\nu
. We say that
\{S_{1}(K),S_{2}(K),\cdots,S_{N}(K),C\}
satisfies the
strong separation condition
(SSC) if
S_{1}(K),S_{2}(K),\cdots,S_{N}(K),C
are pairwise disjoint. On the other hand,
\{S_{1}(K),S_{2}(K),\cdots,S_{N}(K),C\}
satisfies the
inhomogeneous open set condition
(IOSC), which is the modified version of the inhomogeneous open set condition proposed in
[
20
]
, if there exists a bounded nonempty open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\mathop{\cup}\limits_{j=1}^{N}S_{j}(U)\subset U
and
S_{i}(U)\cap S_{j}(U)=\emptyset
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq N
;

(ii)

K\cap U\neq\emptyset

and

C\subset U
;

(iii)

P(\partial(U))=0
and
C\cap S_{j}(\text{cl}(U))=\emptyset
for all
1\leq j\leq N
.
Notice that if
\{S_{1}(K),S_{2}(K),\cdots,S_{N}(K),C\}
satisfies the SSC, then it also satisfies the IOSC. Take
\{S_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
as a family of bi-Lipschitz mappings on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e., there exist
0<a_{j}\leq b_{j}<1,
such that
a_{j}\rho(x,y)\leq\rho(S_{j}(x),S_{j}(y))\leq b_{j}\rho(x,y)
for all
1\leq j\leq N,
and for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we call
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
the lower and the upper bi-Lipschitz constants, respectively. For
r\in(0,+\infty)
, let
l_{r},\kappa_{r}\in(0,+\infty)
be the unique numbers such that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}(p_{j}a_{j}^{r})^{\frac{l_{r}}{r+l_{r}}}=1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{N}(p_{j}b_{j}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}=1
, respectively. In
[
22
]
, Priyadarshi et al. showed that, under the strong separation condition,
\max\{l_{r},\underline{D}_{r}(\nu)\}\leq\underline{D}_{r}(\mu)
. Further, let
\{\mathbb{R}^{d};g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{M}\}
be an IFS with invariant set
C,
satisfying the strong open set condition such that
\rho(g_{i}(x),g_{i}(y))\leq c_{i}\rho(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
where
0<c_{i}<1
and
1\leq i\leq M.
For a given probability vector
(q_{1},q_{2},\cdots,q_{M}),
let
\nu
be the invariant measure associated with the IFS
\{\mathbb{R}^{d};g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{M}\}.
Then
\overline{D}_{r}(\mu)\leq\max\{\kappa_{r},d_{r}\},
where
d_{r}
is uniquely determined by the relation
\sum_{i=1}^{M}(q_{i}c_{i}^{r})^{\frac{d_{r}}{r+d_{r}}}=1.
In our recent work
[
7
]
, by considering the Borel probability measure
\nu
as the image measure of an ergodic measure with bounded distortion on the symbolic space
\{1,2,\cdots,M\}^{\mathbb{N}}
with support a conformal set
C,
which is a generalization of
[
22
]
, we have shown that

\overline{D}_{r}(\mu)\leq\max\{\kappa_{r},D_{r}(\nu)\}.

The following problem remains open (also see Remark
2.7
).

Open 1.1

Let
\nu
be any Borel probability measure and

be the condensation measure associated with the condensation system

Then, whether under the strong separation condition,

.

In this paper, we estimate the quantization dimension of a condensation system given by

In this regard, in Section

, we give two examples of condensation measures

, and

. In Section

. Then, in Section

be given by
f
such that

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2^{j}}&\text{ if }x=a_{j}\text{ for }j\in\mathbb{N},\\

Set

, and

For any Borel measurable function
g
on
\mathbb{R}
, by
\int gd\nu
, it is meant

\int gd\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g(a_{j})\nu(a_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g(a_{j})\frac{1}{2^{j}}.

Then
E(\nu)=\frac{7}{15},\text{ and }V(\nu)=\frac{8}{1575},
where

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

represent the expected value and

represent the variance of the condensation measure

. Then,

E(P)=\frac{19}{39},

V=\frac{86696}{777231}
, and for any

\int(x-x_{0})^{2}dP=(x_{0}-\frac{19}{39})^{2}+V(P)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.10
, we can see that
E(P)=\int xdP=\frac{19}{39}
. Again,

\int x^{2}d\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}^{2}\frac{1}{2^{j}}=\frac{39}{175}.
We have

\displaystyle\int x^{2}dP=\frac{1}{3}\int(S_{1}(x))^{2}dP+\frac{1}{3}\int(S_{2}(x))^{2}dP+\frac{1}{3}\int x^{2}d\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\int\Big(\frac{1}{5}x\Big)^{2}dP+\frac{1}{3}\int\Big(\frac{1}{5}x+\frac{4}{5}\Big)^{2}dP+\frac{1}{3}\frac{39}{175}

which implies

, and hence,

V(P)=\int x^{2}dP-(\int xdP)^{2}=\frac{6953}{19929}-\left(\frac{19}{39}\right)^{2}=\frac{86696}{777231}
. For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

\int(x-x_{0})^{2}dP=(x_{0}-\frac{19}{39})^{2}+V(P)
follows from the standard theory of probability. Thus, the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

\frac{1}{2}
and the corresponding quantization error is the variance

of the random variable

The following proposition is useful.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\nu
be the uniform distribution on the interval
[\frac{2}{5},\frac{3}{5}]
. Then, for
n\geq 1
, the set
\{\frac{2}{5}+\frac{2i-1}{10n}:1\leq i\leq n\}
is a unique optimal set of
n
-means for
\nu
with quantization error
V_{n}(\nu)=\frac{W}{n^{2}}
. Moreover, the quantization dimension
D(\nu)
of
\nu
is one.

Proof.

Since
\nu
is uniformly distributed on
[\frac{2}{5},\frac{3}{5}]
, the boundaries of the Voronoi regions of an optimal set of
n
-means will divide the interval
[\frac{2}{5},\frac{3}{5}]
into
n
equal subintervals, i.e., the boundaries of the Voronoi regions are given by

\{\frac{2}{5},\,\frac{2}{5}+\frac{1}{5n},\,\frac{2}{5}+\frac{2}{5n},\,\cdots,\frac{2}{5}+\frac{n-1}{5n},\frac{3}{5}\}.

This implies that an optimal set of
n
-means for
\nu
is unique, and it consists of the midpoints of the boundaries of the Voronoi regions, i.e., the optimal set of
n
-means for
\nu
is given by
\{\frac{2}{5}+\frac{2i-1}{10n}:1\leq i\leq n\}
for any
n\geq 1
. Then, the
n
th quantization error for
\nu
is

\displaystyle V_{n}(\nu)

\displaystyle=n\times(\text{the quantization error in each Voronoi region})

\displaystyle=n\Big(\int_{\frac{2}{5}}^{\frac{2}{5}+\frac{1}{5n}}5\Big(x-(\frac{2}{5}+\frac{1}{10n})\Big)^{2}dx\Big)

which after simplification implies
V_{n}(\nu)=\frac{1}{300\,n^{2}}=\frac{W}{n^{2}}
. Hence,

D(\nu)=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log(n)}{-\log(\frac{1}{300\,n^{2}})}=1.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

By Proposition
4.5
, it follows that
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n}(\nu)=\frac{1}{300}

is finite and strictly positive. In fact, for any probability measure

is finite and strictly positive; in other words, the quantization dimension of a probability measure with non-vanishing absolutely continuous part is equal to the dimension

3
]
).

\alpha_{n}(\nu)

exists and equals to the quantization dimension

of
\nu
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 4
, let
\ell(n)
be the least positive integer such that
F(\ell(n))\leq n<F(\ell(n)+1)
. Then,

V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}<V_{n}\leq V_{F(\ell(n))}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2\log\left(F(\ell(n))\right)}{-\log\left(V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}\right)}<\frac{2\log n}{-\log V_{n}}<\frac{2\log\left(F(\ell(n)+1)\right)}{-\log\left(V_{F(\ell(n))}\right)}.

Notice that when
n\to\infty
, then
\ell(n)\to\infty
. By Proposition
4.10.11
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell(n)\to\infty}\frac{2\log\left(F(\ell(n))\right)}{-\log\left(V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}\right)}=2\lim_{\ell(n)\to\infty}\frac{\log(4^{\ell(n)}+2^{\ell(n)+1})}{-\log\Big(\frac{1}{129}\frac{1}{16^{\ell(n)+1}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{\ell(n)+1}\Big)}(\frac{\infty}{\infty}\text{ form})

\displaystyle=2\lim_{\ell(n)\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\frac{1}{129}\frac{1}{16^{\ell(n)+1}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{\ell(n)+1}}{4^{\ell(n)}+2^{\ell(n)+1}}\Big)\Big(\frac{4^{\ell(n)}\log 4+2^{\ell(n)+1}\log 2}{\frac{1}{129}\frac{1}{16^{\ell(n)+1}}\log 16+\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{\ell(n)+1}\log\frac{2}{75}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\log 4}{\log 16}=1.

Similarly,
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\ell(n)\to\infty}\frac{2\log\left(F(\ell(n)+1)\right)}{-\log\left(V_{F(\ell(n))}\right)}=1
. Thus,
1\leq\liminf_{n}\frac{2\log n}{-\log V_{n}}\leq\limsup_{n}\frac{2\log n}{-\log V_{n}}\leq 1
implying the fact that the quantization dimension of the measure
P
exists and equals to the quantization dimension
D(\nu)
of
\nu
, which is the theorem.
∎

Theorem 4.11.2
.

The
D(P)
-dimensional quantization coefficient for the condensation measure
P
does not exist.

Proof.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by Proposition
4.10.5
and Lemma
4.10.7
, we see that
(\alpha_{F(n)}\setminus S(n))\cup S^{(2)}(n)
is an optimal set of
F(n)+2^{a(n)}
-means with quantization error

\displaystyle V_{F(n)+2^{a(n)}}=V_{F(n)}-\frac{1}{3}\frac{W}{2^{2a(n)}}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{W}{2^{2(a(n)+1)}}=V_{F(n)}-\frac{W}{16^{n}}=(\frac{1}{129}-W)\frac{1}{16^{n}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{19}{4300}\frac{1}{16^{n}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{n}.

Since
F(n)+2^{a(n)}=4^{n}+2^{n+1}+2^{2n-1}=\frac{3}{2}4^{n}+2^{n+1}
, we have

(23)

\lim_{n\to\infty}(F(n)+2^{a(n)})^{2}V_{F(n)+2^{a(n)}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\frac{3}{2}4^{n}+2^{n+1})^{2}(\frac{19}{4300}\frac{1}{16^{n}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{n})=\frac{171}{17200}.

Again,
(F(n))^{2}=(4^{n}+2^{n+1})^{2}=16^{n}+2^{3n+2}+4^{n+1}=16^{n}(1+\frac{4}{2^{n}}+\frac{4}{4^{n}})
,
and

V_{F(n)}(P)=\frac{1}{129}\frac{1}{16^{n}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{n}=\frac{1}{16^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{129}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{32}{75})^{n}\Big).

Thus,

(24)

\lim_{n\to\infty}(F(n))^{2}V_{F(n)}=\frac{1}{129}.

By the equations (
23
) and (
24
), we see that
\liminf_{n}n^{2}V_{n}(P)\leq\frac{1}{129}<\frac{171}{17200}\leq\limsup_{n}n^{2}V_{n}(P)
, in other words,
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n}(P)
does not exist, i.e., the
D(P)
-dimensional quantization coefficient for the condensation measure
P
does not exist.
∎

Theorem 4.11.3
.

The
D(P)
-dimensional lower and upper quantization coefficients for the condensation measure
P
are finite and positive.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 4
, let
\ell(n)
be the least positive integer such that
F(\ell(n))\leq n<F(\ell(n)+1)
. Then,

V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}<V_{n}\leq V_{F(\ell(n))}
implying
(F(\ell(n)))^{2}V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}<n^{2}V_{n}<(F(\ell(n)+1))^{2}V_{F(\ell(n))}
. As
\ell(n)\to\infty
whenever
n\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(F(\ell(n)))^{2}}{(F(\ell(n)+1))^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\frac{4^{\ell(n)}+2^{\ell(n)+1}}{4^{\ell(n)+1}+2^{\ell(n)+2}}\Big)^{2}=\frac{1}{16}.

Again,
(F(\ell(n)))^{2}=(4^{\ell(n)}+2^{\ell(n)+1})^{2}=16^{\ell(n)}+2^{3\ell(n)+2}+4^{\ell(n)+1}=16^{\ell(n)}(1+\frac{4}{2^{\ell(n)}}+\frac{4}{4^{\ell(n)}})
,
and

V_{F(\ell(n))}(P)=\frac{1}{129}\frac{1}{16^{\ell(n)}}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{2}{75})^{\ell(n)}=\frac{1}{16^{\ell(n)}}\Big(\frac{1}{129}-\frac{3473}{941700}(\frac{32}{75})^{\ell(n)}\Big).

Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}(F(\ell(n)))^{2}V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}=\frac{1}{16}\lim_{n\to\infty}(F(\ell(n)+1))^{2}V_{F(\ell(n)+1)}=\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{129},\text{ and similarly}

\lim_{n\to\infty}(F(\ell(n)+1))^{2}V_{F(\ell(n))}=\frac{16}{129},

yielding the fact that
\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{129}\leq\mathop{\liminf}\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n}(P)\leq\mathop{\limsup}\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n}(P)\leq\frac{16}{129}
, i.e., the
D(P)
-dimensional lower and quantization coefficients for the condensation measure
P
are finite and positive, which is the theorem.
∎

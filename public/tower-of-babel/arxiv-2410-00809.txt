Orbifolds of Pointed Vertex Operator Algebras I
Terry Gannon

Abstract
By a pointed vertex operator algebra (VOA) we mean one whose modules are all simple currents (i.e. invertible), e.g. lattice VOAs. This paper systematically explores the interplay between their orbifolds and tensor category theory. We begin by supplying an elementary proof of the Dijkgraaf–Witten conjecture, which predicts the representation theory of holomorphic VOA orbifolds. We then apply that argument more generally to the situation where the automorphism subgroup fixes all VOA modules, and relate the result to recent work of Mason–Ng and Naidu. Here our results are complete. We then turn to the other extreme, where the automorphisms act fixed-point freely on the modules, and realize any possible nilpotent group as lattice VOA automorphisms. This affords a considerable generalization of the Tambara-Yamagami categories. We conclude by considering some hybrid actions. In this way we use tensor category theory to organize and generalize systematically several isolated examples and special cases scattered in the literature. Conversely, we show how VOA orbifolds can be used to construct broad classes of braided crossed fusion categories and modular tensor categories.

Introduction

This paper aims to contribute to the rich interplay between the theory of tensor categories (especially fusion categories and modular tensor categories), and the theory of vertex operator algebras (VOAs) (see e.g.
[
41
,
50
]

A VOA
\mathcal{V}
or a local conformal net
\mathcal{B}
of factors on

are both mathematical interpretations of a conformal field theory. In this paper we use the language of VOAs, since it is more familiar, but the theory of conformal nets is more developed in some ways. The two theories are expected to be equivalent (see e.g.
[
12
]
).

A VOA
\mathcal{V}
is called
strongly rational
if its representation theory is semi-simple and finite.
Let
G
be a finite group of automorphisms of such a
\mathcal{V}
. The fixed points
\mathcal{V}^{G}
are sometimes called the
orbifold
of
\mathcal{V}
by
G
(though orbifold has a different meaning in the physics literature). Taking the orbifold is one of the few general constructions we have of VOAs. Given a strongly rational
\mathcal{V}
, it is conjectured (and known when
G
is solvable) that
\mathcal{V}^{G}
will also be strongly rational (this is a theorem for conformal nets for all
G

[
68
]
). In this paper we assume that conjecture throughout, but in examples this is rarely necessary, and everything we do can be reinterpreted in terms of conformal nets, where our results are unconditional.

This paper concerns the representation theory of the orbifolds
\mathcal{V}^{G}
of strongly rational VOAs
\mathcal{V}
by finite groups
G
. We focus on the case where
\mathcal{V}
is
pointed
, i.e. all
\mathcal{V}
-modules are invertible (i.e. simple currents), which includes all lattice VOAs (though of course

will usually have noninvertible modules). Orbifold technology is much more developed for lattice VOAs, but from the tensor category perspective there is no difference between lattice and pointed VOAs.

In practise the most important things are the associated algebraic combinatorics. In particular,
we want to list the simple modules of the orbifold
\mathcal{V}^{G}
, and how their tensor (fusion) products decompose into simples. We want to list the
G
-twisted
\mathcal{V}
-modules, their fusion products, and their restrictions to
\mathcal{V}^{G}
-modules. We want the modular data (a matrix representation of SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
) of
\mathcal{V}^{G}
, as this gives us its fusion decompositions, conformal weights of modules (mod 1), and modular transformations of the characters (graded dimensions).

As we know from finite group representation theory, the complete story requires more than just the algebraic combinatorics (which for finite groups is captured by the character table). The complete story is conveniently captured by tensor categories. To give a familiar example, the dihedral group
D_{4}
and the quaternions

have the same character table, and therefore the same algebraic combinatorics, but their (braided fusion) categories of representations are inequivalent. More generally, the symmetric fusion category

\,\,G
has

-modules as objects and intertwiners as morphisms, and braiding

The category

is of a very special type called a

-modules, also called local or dyslectic. Then the MTC

is obtained from

{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
by a process called

Restriction along

is a functor

, or better

{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}\to\mathrm{\textbf{Mod}}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}

, and as such is a copy of the regular representation of

{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}

\,\,\mathcal{V}
from

This paper is the first in a series where we investigate the orbifolds of VOAs. Far from abstract nonsense, these categorical considerations provide effective tools in understanding the orbifold construction, as we will see. Tensor categories are considerably simpler, but preserve most of the salient features. VOAs can return the favour by explicitly constructing tensor categories whose existence may not otherwise be clear (e.g. when the cohomological obstructions to their existence lie in nontrivial groups).

Our first main task is to generalize that story to any pointed VOA

and Mason–Ng
[

), and concrete VOA orbifolds realizing all abstract categorical possibilities.

Next, in Section 5 we turn to the other extreme, where

permutes all

-modules without any fixed points. When

this recovers the Tambara-Yamagami categories (e.g. this happens with

for lattices

Background

2.1
Tensor categories

For the standard introduction to tensor categories, see
[
32
]
; for a pedagogical introduction see
[
67
]
. For someone more comfortable with VOAs, it may be better to start with the next subsection and refer back to this one as needed.

A
fusion category

\mathbf{C}
is a semisimple category with direct sums
x\oplus y
, tensor products (called fusions)
x\otimes y
, a tensor unit 1, duals
x^{*}
, finitely many simples (up to equivalence), and finite-dimensional Hom spaces (for us, over
\mathbb{C}
). For example, associativity will be an explicit invertible map in Hom
{}_{\mathbf{C}}((x\otimes y)\otimes z,x\otimes(y\otimes z))
for each choice of objects
x,y,z
, and those maps must satisfy certain coherence properties. A simple example is
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}
for a finite group
G
, whose objects are finite-dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces; its Grothendieck ring is the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. Another example is the category
\mathbf{Rep}\,\,G
of finite-dimensional representations.

Given a fusion category
\mathbf{C}
, the
Frobenius-Perron dimension
is the unique assignment of positive real numbers FPdim
(x)
to each
x\in\mathbf{C}
such that

\mathrm{FPdim}(x\oplus y)=\mathrm{FPdim}(x)+\mathrm{FPdim}(y)\ \ ,\ \ \mathrm{FPdim}(x\otimes y)=\mathrm{FPdim}(x)\,\mathrm{FPdim}(y)\ \ \forall x,y\in\mathbf{C}

For example, any simple in
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
has FPdim 1, whereas for
\mathbf{Rep}\,\,G
any
\rho
has FPdim equal to its usual dimension. We write FPdim
(\mathbf{C}):=\sum_{x}\mathrm{FPdim}(x)^{2}
, where the sum is over all isomorphism classes of simples in
\mathbf{C}
.

We say a fusion category is
graded
by a group
G
if we can write
\mathbf{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathbf{C}_{g}
such that
\mathbf{C}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{C}_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{C}_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
. It is straightforward to verify that the associativity isomorphisms of any fusion category
\mathbf{C}
with a
G
-grading can be twisted by any cocycle
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, resulting in a potentially inequivalent fusion category. In this way one constructs
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
.

In general,
x\otimes y\not\cong y\otimes x
in a fusion category. By a
braided
fusion category
\mathbf{C}
we mean one with a choice of invertible map (called a
braiding
)
c_{x,y}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(x\otimes y,y\otimes x)
for each choice of objects
x,y
, and those maps must satisfy certain coherence conditions. For example, although most
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
are not braided,
\mathbf{Rep}\,\,G
has a braiding sending
u\otimes v\mapsto v\otimes u
on the underlying spaces. Replacing a braiding with its inverse
c^{\mathrm{rev}}_{y,x}=(c_{x,y})^{-1}

\forall x,y
results in a potentially inequivalent braided fusion category called the
reverse

\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a braided fusion category, and
\mathbf{D}
a fusion subcategory. By the
Müger centralizer
we mean the full fusion subcategory with objects:

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\{x\in\mathbf{C}:c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}=\mathrm{Id}_{x\otimes y}\ \ \forall y\in\mathbf{D}\}

(2.1)

For example,
(\mathbf{Rep}\,\,G)^{\prime}=\mathbf{Rep}\,\,G
. We say a braided fusion category
\mathbf{C}
is
nondegenerately
braided if it is centreless in the sense that
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\cong\mathbf{Vec}
. A
modular tensor category
(MTC) is a nondegenerately braided fusion category with
a
ribbon structure

\theta
(see Definition 8.10.1 in
[
32
]
). The ribbon structure makes it possible to define a categorical trace Tr
(f)
of endomorphisms
f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}}(x)
and categorical dimension dim
(x)
of objects
x\in\mathbf{C}
. As will be explained in Section 2.3, all categories in this paper are
pseudo-unitary
, i.e. dim
(x)=\pm\mathrm{FPdim}(x)

\forall x\in\mathbf{C}
.

Given a MTC
\mathbf{C}
, the different ribbon structures on
\mathbf{C}
are in bijection with the invertibles in
\mathbf{C}
. But when
\mathbf{C}
is pseudo-unitary, there is a unique ribbon structure with dim
(x)=\mathrm{FPdim}(x)

\forall x
. We can and will assume this preferred ribbon structure is chosen. It is also preferred by the associated VOAs.

Given any MTC, the
modular data
is a matrix representation of the modular group SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, with indices labelled by equivalence classes of simples of
\mathbf{C}
:
\left({0\atop 1}{-1\atop 0}\right)\mapsto S
and
\left({1\atop 0}{1\atop 1}\right)\mapsto T
, where
S_{x,y}
is (up to a scalar independent of
x,y
) Tr
(c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y})
and
T
is a diagonal matrix with entries (up to a scalar independent of
x
) the ribbon twist
\theta(x)
. Then
x\otimes y\cong\oplus_{z}N_{x,y}^{z}z
where the fusion multiplicities are given by
Verlinde’s formula

N_{x,y}^{z}=\sum_{w}\frac{S_{x,w}S_{y,w}\overline{S_{z,w}}}{S_{1,w}}

(2.2)

where 1 denotes the tensor unit. The scalar normalizing
S
is unique up to a sign, but we should always choose the sign so that
S
has a strictly positive row (always possible, and the
S
matrix for any VOA will also have that property). The scalar for
T
is uniquely defined up to a third root of 1, but there is no preference for one of these over another.

Given any fusion category
\mathbf{C}
, its
centre
or
quantum double

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})
is a MTC naturally associated with
\mathbf{C}
, which will play a large role in this paper. Its objects consists of pairs
(x,\beta)
where
x
is a (usually nonsimple) object of
\mathbf{C}
with the property that
x\otimes y\cong y\otimes x

\forall y\in\mathbf{C}
and the half-braiding
\beta
is (among other things) a choice of isomorphisms
\beta_{y}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(x\otimes y,y\otimes x)

\forall y
. When
\mathbf{C}
is already nondegenerately braided, then
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})\cong\mathbf{C}\boxtimes\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a fusion category. An
algebra

B
in
C
is an object
B\in\mathbf{C}
, a multiplication
m\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(B\otimes B,B)
and a unit
\eta\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(1,B)
satisfying associativity etc. Algebras are involved in both the extension theory for VOAs and their orbifold theory, as we’ll see. When
\mathbf{C}
is a MTC,

is called
commutative
if

. A
B
-module is an object
M\in\mathbf{C}
and a module multiplication
\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(B\otimes M,M)
satisfying
\mu\circ(m\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{M})=\mu\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{B}\otimes\mu)
and
\mu\circ(\eta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{M})=\mathrm{Id}_{M}
. Given a commutative algebra
B
in
\mathbf{C}
, the category of all
B
-modules is denoted
\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{C}}\,B
. When
\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{C}}\,B
is semisimple and
B
is commutative, we call
B

étale
. These are the algebras we are interested in. When dim
\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(1,B)=1

B
is étale,
\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{C}}\,B
is naturally a fusion category.

Let
B
be étale and dim
\,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(1,B)=1
. For reasons that will be clear next subsection, we call the forgetful functor
\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{C}}\,B\to\mathbf{C}

restriction
. By
induction
we mean its adjoint
\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{C}}\,B
; it is a tensor functor. A simple
B
-module

is called
local
if Res
\,M
has a well-defined twist
\theta
. The local
B
-modules form a MTC we call
\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}\,B
.

The special case most relevant to orbifold theory is when the MTC
\mathbf{C}
contains a subcategory braided tensor equivalent to

for some finite group

. Then the (regular representation) object
B_{G}=\oplus_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(e)\,\chi\in\mathbf{Rep}\,\,G
has a natural étale algebra structure in

.
In this case,

has the structure of a

-crossed category
and is called the

of

. This means that the fusion category

has a

-modules can be identified with

-crossed extension
of

Rational vertex operator algebras

For an elementary introduction to vertex operator algebras (VOAs), see e.g.
[
52
]

Throughout this paper, by a

-module

By

-modules.

.

Suppose

This is due to Huang (see Theorem 4.6 of

The simplest strongly rational VOAs are the lattice VOAs

, where

2.3
Pointed MTCs and pointed VOAs

Suppose a fusion category

Suppose now an MTC

2.4
Realization by VOAs

The main theme of this paper is the interplay between tensor categories and VOAs. Part of this is
VOA reconstruction
: given a MTC
\mathbf{C}
, show that there exists a strongly rational VOA
\mathcal{V}
such that
Mod
\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{C}
(or show that no such VOA can exist).
A basic result, mentioned last subsection, is that any pointed MTC can be realized by a lattice VOA. Far less trivial is that any twisted quantum double of a finite group can be realized as a VOA (Corollary 4 of
[
35
]
).

One value of realizing a MTC as a lattice VOA orbifold, is that much of the modular data can be accessible, through theta function arguments. See
[
6
]
for a systematic treatment, when orbifolding the lattice VOA by a cyclic group
G
. There are limitations however – e.g. the theta functions in general won’t be linearly independent. But when used in conjunction with categorical arguments, it can provide enough information to pin down exactly where on the
H^{2}
- and
H^{3}
-torsors the given orbifold lies (these torsors are described next subsection).

The following illustrates a general strategy for proving VOA reconstruction:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a MTC. Suppose that
B
is an étale algebra in
\mathbf{D}
. If
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{D}}\,B)\cong\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{W}
for a strongly rational VOA
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathbf{Rep}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathbf{D}}\,B\cong\mathbf{Mod}\ \mathcal{V},
for a strongly rational VOA
\mathcal{V}
, then there exists a strongly rational VOA
\mathcal{U}
such that
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{U}\cong\mathbf{D}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{Rep}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathbf{D}}\,B
; by assumption it is an MTC.
First, recall that by Proposition
2.2
we know that

\hskip-7.11317pt\mathbf{Mod}\,(\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{V})=\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{W}\boxtimes\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}_{\mathbf{D}}\,B)\boxtimes\mathbf{C}\cong(\mathbf{D}\boxtimes\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{rev}})\boxtimes\mathbf{C}\cong\mathbf{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})

Applying
[
37
, Proposition 7.1]
we know there exists a
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})
-algebra
T
such that
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})}^{\mathrm{loc}}\,T\cong\mathbf{Vec}
.
On the other hand,
\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{V}
is also strongly rational; we see that

will be an étale algebra in
\mathbf{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})\cong\mathrm{\textbf{Mod}}(\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{V})
. By VOA extension theory, we see that
\mathbf{D}\cong\mathrm{\textbf{Mod}}\,\,\mathcal{U}
for the VOA extension
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{V}
corresponding to

.
∎

For the applications we have in mind this paper,
B=B_{G}
is a copy of the regular representation of
G
in
\mathbf{D}
, so
\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathbf{D}}\,B
is a braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{Rep}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathbf{D}}\,B
.
However, Theorem

doesn’t go as far as we would like. We would like to realize

as a

, on which

acts by VOA-automorphisms, such that
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{U}^{\prime\,G}\cong\mathbf{D}
and

2.5
Etingof-Nikshych-Ostrik gauging theory

The categorical interpretation of the orbifold construction is called
gauging
. Let
C
be a MTC. Suppose we are given an action of
G
on
C
which respect the braiding, i.e. a group homomorphism
\rho:G\to\mathrm{EqBr}(\textbf{C})
. To gauge this action, we first require a compatible braided
G
-crossed extension
D
of
C
. This may not exist, and if it does it is usually not unique. Given any such
\mathbf{D}
, we can then equivariantize by the associated action of
G
, to produces a new MTC
\mathbf{D}^{G}
called a gauging of
C
by
G
. If
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{C}
, then
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
will be braided tensor equivalent to some
\mathbf{D}^{G}
.

However, associated to that initial homomorphism
\rho:G\to\mathrm{EqBr}(\mathbf{C})
are two cohomological obstructions to finding such a braided
G
-crossed category
[
34
]
(see also
[
17
,
20
]
). The first,
o_{3}(\rho)
, lives in
H^{3}(G,\mathrm{Inv}(\mathbf{C}))
, where Inv
(\mathbf{C})
are the invertible objects (or simple currents) in
\mathbf{C}
.
o_{3}(\rho)
vanishes iff the group homomorphism
\rho
can be lifted into a categorical action
\underline{\rho}
, in which case the possible liftings form a torsor over
H^{2}(G,\mathrm{Inv}(\textbf{C}))
. Now choose any such lifting
\underline{\rho}
. It in turn can be lifted to a braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C}
, iff another obstruction
o_{4}(\underline{\rho})\in H^{4}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
vanishes. If
o_{4}(\underline{\rho})
also vanishes, then the possible braided
G
-crossed extensions of
\mathbf{C}
coming from
\underline{\rho}
form a torsor over
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Incidentally, this
H^{3}
-torsor is the twisting of associativity in graded categories, described in Section 2.1.

As we will see, it is often relevant that Eilenberg-Mac Lane theory (see e.g. Chapter IV of
[
53
]
) uses
H^{2}(G,A)
to describe the possible abelian extensions
\Gamma
of
G
by
A
:

0\to A\hookrightarrow\Gamma\to G\to 1

(2.8)

(Throughout the paper we use additive notation for
A
and multiplicative for
G
.)
The interpretation of the
H^{2}
torsor using zesting is explored in
[
22
]
though doesn’t play a role here.

Suppose we are given any pointed VOA
\mathcal{V}
, with
Mod
\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
, and some group
G
of automorphisms of
\mathcal{V}
. Then this corresponds to some orthogonal map
\rho\in\mathrm{O}(A,q)
. We know, by Theorem
2.2
, that there is an associated braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
. Thus the first obstruction
o_{3}(\rho)
must vanish, as must the second,
o_{4}(\underline{\rho})
for some lift of
\rho
.

This is the main value to tensor categories of VOA orbifold theory:
the existence of certain tensor categories, even when the cohomology groups in which the obstructions live are large.

For future reference, if
|G|
is coprime to
|A|
, then
H^{n}(G,A)=0
for all
n>0
. When
G=\mathbb{Z}/n
acts trivially on
A
, then
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n,A)\cong A/nA
and
H^{3}(\mathbb{Z}/n,A)
consists of the elements of
A
of order dividing
n
. Also,
H^{3}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}/n
and
H^{4}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\mathbb{C}^{\times})=0
.

2.6
Realizations by Hopf-like algebras

Any fusion category is tensor equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional representations of a weak Hopf algebra (Corollary 2.22 of
[
33
]
). In a weak Hopf algebra, the comultiplication may not be unit-preserving and the counit may not be an algebra homomorphism. Weak Hopf algebras are much less easy to work with than Hopf algebras.

In order for the representations of a (weak) Hopf algebra to have a braiding, the extra structure needed is an
R
matrix, and such algebras are called quasitriangular. If their category also possesses a ribbon structure (needed to recover an MTC), they are called ribbon.

When the fusion category is integral, then it is equivalent to the representation category of a finite-dimensional quasi-Hopf algebra (Proposition 6.1.14 of
[
32
]
). Quasi-Hopf algebras are much more amenable than weak Hopf algebras. For example,
Vec
{}_{G}^{\omega}
is realized by the commutative quasi-Hopf algebra of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
G
twisted by
\omega
(see e.g. Example 5.13.6 in
[
32
]
).

\,\,\mathcal{V}
is

), after a special case was established much earlier in
[
48
]
. We supply a simpler proof in this section (in fact we prove a little more), and next section apply the argument in a much more general setting.

In the physics literature, the 3-cocycle
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is often called the
gauge anomaly
, but this seems a bit of a misnomer. It is generally nontrivial. For example, in Monstrous Moonshine (where
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{\natural}
is the moonshine module and
G
is the Monster finite group
\mathbb{M}
),
[\omega]
has order 24 in
H^{3}(\mathbb{M},\mathbb{C}^{\times})

[
45
]
. For another example,
G=\mathbb{Z}/2
acts on the
E_{8}
root lattice VOA in two inequivalent ways: one gives
\mathcal{V}_{D_{8}}
and the other gives
\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}\oplus E_{7}}
. These correspond to
[\omega]
trivial resp. nontrivial in
H^{3}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
.

3.1
In and around the Dijkgraaf-Witten conjecture

We begin with our proof of the Dijkgraaf-Witten conjecture.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a holomorphic VOA, and
G
a finite group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{V}
. Assume the fixed-point VOA
\mathcal{V}^{G}
is strongly rational. Then the category
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
of twisted modules is tensor equivalent to
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
for some
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, and
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
above, for each
g\in G
there is a unique simple
g
-twisted module. Denote it by
M^{g}
, and note
\mathrm{FPdim}(M^{g})=1
. This uniqueness applied to the
G
grading forces the fusions
M^{g}\otimes M^{h}\cong M^{gh}
, so
TwMod
{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
is pointed. This means that as a fusion category,
TwMod
{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
must be tensor equivalent to
Vec
{}^{\omega}_{H}
for some group
H
and
[\omega]\in H^{3}(H,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Of course the two gradings must match, again by uniqueness, which forces
H\cong G
. So
TwMod
{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
is
Vec
{}^{\omega}_{G}
. Then Proposition
2.2
in combination with Theorem
2.2
forces

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})\cong\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}\boxtimes(\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V})^{\mathrm{rev}}\cong\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}.

∎

Up to isomorphism, the simple objects of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
are parametrized by pairs
[g,\chi]
where
g
is the representative of a conjugacy class of
G
, and
\chi
runs through the irreducible projective characters Irr
{}_{\theta_{g}}(C_{G}(g))
of the centralizer
C_{G}(g)
, for the 2-cocycle

\theta_{g}(h,k)=\frac{{\omega}(g,h,k)\,{\omega}(h,k,g)}{{\omega}(h,g,k)}\ \ \forall h,k\in C_{G}(g)

(3.1)

Letting
M^{g}
denote the unique simple
g
-twisted module, we have

\mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{V}^{G}}\,M^{g}\cong\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\theta_{g}}(C_{G}(g))}\mathrm{dim}\,\chi\,\,[g,\chi]

as is well-known, hence induction (its adjoint functor) Ind
\,[g,\chi]=\sum_{g^{\prime}\in K_{g}}\mathrm{dim}\,\chi\,M^{g^{\prime}}
, where the sum is over the elements in the conjugacy class of
g
.

Now turn to VOA reconstruction. The hard part, finding a VOA
\mathcal{V}
with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
, was accomplished in Corollary 4 of
[
35
]
. That result, and the following one, assumes Conjecture
1
in the special case of
\mathcal{V}
holomorphic. If
G
is solvable, the conjecture is unnecessary. If one replaces VOA in the statement of the Theorem with conformal net of factors on
S^{1}
, the conjecture is unnecessary for any
G
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume Conjecture
1
. For any finite group
G
and any cocycle
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, a holomorphic VOA
\mathcal{V}
can be found such that
G
acts on
\mathcal{V}
as VOA automorphisms and both
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
and
\mathbf{{TwMod}}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
.

Proof.

Corollary 4 of
[
35
]
tells us there exists a strongly rational VOA
\mathcal{W}
such that
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{W}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
. Moreover, the conformal weights
h_{M}
are positive for all simple
\mathcal{W}
-modules
M\not\cong\mathcal{W}
. In any
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
, there is an étale algebra structure on
B_{G}=\oplus_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\mathrm{dim}(\chi)\,[e,\chi]
(see e.g. Remark 3.2(b) of
[
30
]
). It is Lagrangian, in the sense that
\mathbf{Rep}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})}\,B\cong\mathbf{Vec}
. This then corresponds to a holomorphic VOA extension
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{W}
with
\mathbf{Res}_{\mathcal{W}}\,\mathcal{V}=B_{G}
.

How
G
acts on
\mathcal{V}
is given by that formula for
B_{G}
. By Proposition 3.2(iv) of
[
60
]
, this action of
G
on
\mathcal{V}
is indeed by VOA automorphisms.
In particular, the fixed points
\mathcal{V}^{G}
will be
[e,1]=\mathcal{W}
. By Theorem
2.2
,
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
can be identified with the de-equivariantization of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{W})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
, i.e. with
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
. ∎

The ENO obstruction
o_{3}
trivially vanishes here, and the torsor over
H^{2}
is also trivial. The obstruction
o_{4}
also must vanish, because
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
has a braided
G
-crossed structure. This can be seen using either reconstruction Theorem
3.2
, or de-equivariantization of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
. The torsor over
H^{3}
corresponds to varying
\omega
.

All dimensions here are integers, so
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
will be the category of representations of a quasi-Hopf algebra. This twisted quantum double of
G
was constructed in
[
24
]
. It was through this quasi-Hopf algebra that the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
was computed
[
15
]
.

Tensor category theory has further consequences for VOAs. For example, Theorem 1 of
[
35
]
classifies all VOA extensions
\mathcal{W}
of a holomorphic orbifold
\mathcal{V}^{G}
(i.e. all étale algebras in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
). Corollary 2 there classifies all extensions
\mathcal{V}^{G}\subset\mathcal{W}
where
\mathcal{W}
is also holomorphic. Now, the important paper
[
31
]
addresses the classification of holomorphic VOAs at central charge
c=24
, using
\mathbb{Z}/n
orbifolds of known holomorphic VOAs. Much of their Section 5 is an immediate corollary of the fact that
Mod
\,\,\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{Z}/n}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/n}^{\omega})
. The more recent paper
[
40
]
constructs special holomorphic VOAs at
c=48
and 72, this time using orbifolds by nonabelian (metacyclic) groups. They do apply some tensor category machinery to greatly simplify their analysis.

3.2
Consequences of a nontrivial twist
\omega

How can the gauge anomaly
\omega
be detected?
There are several consequences of having a nontrivial
\omega
. Many of these are discussed with examples in
[
15
]
.

The most obvious is to the ribbon twist
\theta(M)=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}h_{M}}
for
\mathcal{V}^{G}
-modules
M
. For any
\omega
,
\theta([g,\chi])=\frac{\chi(g)}{\chi(e)}
, is a root of unity. When
[\omega]=[1]
in
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, the order of each such root of unity will divide the exponent of
G
, but this often fails when
[\omega]\neq[1]
. For example, when
G=\mathbb{Z}/n
,
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}/n
, which we can parametrize by
\omega_{q}
for
q\in\mathbb{Z}/n
as in (6.1) of
[
15
]
. The simples of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/n}^{\omega_{q}})
can be parametrized by
[a,\ell]
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}/n
,
\ell\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}/n}
for any
q
. Then

\theta([a,\ell])=\exp(2\pi i(qa^{2}+na\ell)/n^{2})

So if
\omega_{q}
has order
n
(i.e.
q
is coprime to
n
), then some simples
[a,\ell]
will have a twist
\theta([a,\ell])
of order exactly
n^{2}
, rather than
n
.

\omega
affects the
S
-matrix of
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
too, as well as the modularity of the twisted twining characters of
TwMod
{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
. Let
M^{g}
be the unique simple
g
-twisted
\mathcal{V}
-module, and define
Z_{g,h}(\tau)=\mathrm{Tr}_{M^{g}}q^{L_{0}-c/24}
for any
h\in G
with
M^{h^{-1}gh}\cong M^{g}
(so in particular
h
commutes with
g
). Then if
[\omega]=[1]
, these are permuted by the modular group SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
:

Z_{g,h}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=Z_{g^{a}h^{c},g^{b}h^{d}}(\tau)

But if
[\omega]\neq[1]
, then the right-side must be multiplied by some root of unity depending on
\omega
. For example, the modularity of these twisted twining characters for Monstrous Moonshine involve 24th roots of unity, so the gauge anomaly
[\omega]
there must be order at least 24. We now know
[
45
]
its order is exactly 24.

The fusion rules of
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
can change with
\omega
(in contrast to those of
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
which are unchanged). For example, for
\mathbb{Z}/n
, the fusion ring of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/n}^{\omega})
is the group ring of
\mathbb{Z}/d\times\mathbb{Z}/(n^{2}/d)
, where
d
is the gcd of
n
with twice the order of
[\omega]
. For example,
\mathfrak{sl}(n^{2})
at level
k=1
(with cyclic fusions
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/n^{2}]
) can be realized as a holomorphic orbifold by
\mathbb{Z}/n
, i.e. as
\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/n}^{\omega})

for some
\omega
of maximal order.

In fact the numbers of simples can decrease for
[\omega]\neq[1]
compared to
[\omega]=[1]
. For example, though this won’t happen for
G=\mathbb{Z}/n
nor
(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{2}
, it can for
(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{3}
. A simple example is
G=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}
, which for an appropriate
\omega\in H^{3}((\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{7}
recovers
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{D_{4}}^{\omega})
for dihedral
D_{4}
, which has 22 simples (instead of the
(2^{3})^{2}=64
we would have expected).

The numbers of VOA extensions of
\mathcal{V}^{G}
(equivalently, the étale algebras of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
) typically decreases for nontrivial
[\omega]
. For example (see Table 1 in
[
35
]
),
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\mathrm{Sym}(3)}^{\omega})
has exactly 8, 6, 5, 4 étale algebras when
[\omega]
has order 1, 2, 3, 6 respectively in
H^{3}(\mathrm{Sym}(3),\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{6}
. A simpler example:
(\mathcal{V}_{E_{8}})^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
has either 2 or 1 nontrivial VOA extensions, depending on which
\mathbb{Z}/2
subgroup is chosen, as they have different
\omega
.

4
Trivial module-maps and quantum cleft extensions

Let
\mathcal{V}
now be any pointed VOA, and write
Mod
\,\,\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
for some abelian group
A
and non-degenerate quadratic form
q
. Let
G
be a finite group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{V}
, and let
\rho:G\to\mathrm{O}(A,q)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(A)
be the corresponding module-map.
We will see this section that the arguments of Section 3 generalize naturally and completely to the situation where
\rho
is the trivial map (i.e.
\rho(g)=\mathrm{Id}_{A}

\forall g\in G
). In this case we say
G
has a
trivial module-map
.
The Dijkgraaf-Witten story studied in Section 3 is the special case corresponding to
A=0
.

Trivial module-maps are fairly common: e.g. any
\mathcal{V}
-automorphism of prime order
\geq|A|
must fix
A
pointwise. This necessarily happens when
|A|\leq 2
(last section we studied the case where
|A|=1
). The
\rho
trivial situation has been studied already in the literature. For example, Naidu
[
61
]
and Mason–Ng
[
54
]
introduced finite-dimensional quasi-Hopf algebras. We prove these are essentially equivalent, and their representations recover the MTC
Mod
\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
in this trivial module-map situation.

4.1
The relevant tensor categories

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{D}
is a braided
G
-crossed extension of a MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}
, with trivial module-map
\rho:G\to\mathrm{O}(A,q)
. Then
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
for some central extension
\Gamma
of
G
by
A
, and
[\widetilde{\omega}|_{A}]=[\omega]
in
H^{3}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Moreover,
(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\mathrm{rev}}
is braided tensor equivalent to a full subcategory in
Z(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})
, and the equivariantization
\mathbf{D}^{G}
is braided tensor equivalent to the Müger centralizer of
(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\mathrm{rev}}
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})
:
Z(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})\cong(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathbf{D}^{G}
.

Proof.

Choose any
g\in G
. By Lemma
2.1
, for any
g\neq e
in
G
, the number of
g
-graded simples in
D
equals
|A|
, which also equals FPdim
(\mathbf{D}_{g})
. Since these are equal, the Frobenius-Perron dimension of every simple in
\mathbf{D}_{g}
must be 1, i.e. every simple in
\mathbf{D}_{g}
must be invertible. Thus
\mathbf{D}
is a pointed fusion category, so equals
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
for some finite group
\Gamma
and some 3-cocycle
\widetilde{\omega}\in Z^{3}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
let
X^{\gamma}
denote the corresponding simple object of
\mathbf{D}
.

Let
\partial
give the grading
\partial(X^{\gamma})\in G
. We can view this as a function
\Gamma\to G
. Then
\partial:\Gamma\to G
is a group homomorphism, since
\mathbf{D}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{D}_{g^{\prime}}
lies in
\mathbf{D}_{gg^{\prime}}
. Also,
\mathbf{D}_{e}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega}
, so the kernel of
\partial
equals
A
. Thus
\Gamma
is an (abelian) extension of
G
by
A
, as in (
2.8
). Since
G
acts trivially on
A
,
A
must lie in the centre of
\Gamma
.

Because
\mathbf{D}_{e}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}
,
\left[\widetilde{\omega}|_{A}\right]=[\omega]
in
H^{3}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. The rest of the theorem follows from Proposition
2.2
. ∎

A fusion category
\mathbf{C}
is called
group-theoretical
if it is Morita equivalent to a pointed fusion category, i.e. if its centre
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{C})
is braided tensor equivalent to the centre of a pointed fusion category. By Theorem 7.2 of
[
62
]
, any equivariantization
\mathbf{D}^{G}
arising in Theorem
4.1
is group-theoretical, and conversely, any group-theoretical MTC arises as
\mathbf{D}^{G}
in Theorem
4.1
.

The braided
G
-crossed category structure on
Vec
{}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
is given explicitly in Section 4 of
[
61
]
.
In particular, the
G
-grading on
Vec
{}^{\tilde{\omega}}_{\Gamma}
is given by
\partial(X^{\gamma})=\gamma A\in\Gamma/A
. The
G
-action is given by
{}^{g}(X^{\gamma})=X^{\tilde{g}\gamma\tilde{g}^{-1}}
for any lift
\tilde{g}
of
g\in\Gamma/A
to
\Gamma
. Because
A
is central, this action is well-defined.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a pointed VOA with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
. Let
G
be any finite group of
\mathcal{V}
-automorphisms with trivial module-map. Then
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
for some
\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\omega}
as in Theorem
4.1
. Moreover,
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
is braided tensor equivalent to the equivariantization
\mathbf{D}^{G}
.

The converse of the theorem is also true, thanks to Lemma 5.1 of
[
60
]
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{D}
is a pointed braided
G
-crossed extension of a MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
. Then the corresponding module-map
\rho
is trivial.

Even though
H^{3}(G,A)
may be large, the obstruction
o_{3}(\rho)
(which equals the Eilenberg-Mac Lane obstruction to (
2.8
)) always must vanish for the trivial module-map
\rho
since
\Gamma=A\times G
works. The
H^{2}
torsor is clear: it corresponds to the different central extensions
\Gamma
of
G
by
A
. The obstruction

is much more delicate (see Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
4.3
below); when it vanishes, the

-torsor involves taking any
\alpha\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, inflating it to
\alpha^{\prime}\in Z^{3}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
in the usual way, and multiplying
\widetilde{\omega}
by
\alpha^{\prime}
. We still have
\widetilde{\omega}\alpha^{\prime}|_{A}=\omega
thanks to inflation.

4.2
VOA reconstruction

For this subsection, assume Conjecture
1
(though this is unnecessary when
G
is solvable). Use of the conjecture can also be avoided by using conformal nets of factors on
S^{1}
rather than VOAs. Our proof of VOA reconstruction here loosely follows the proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
is a braided
G
-crossed extension of a MTC
\mathbf{Vec}^{(\omega,c)}_{A}
, for a central extension
\Gamma
of
G
by
A
, as in Theorem
4.1
. Let
\mathbf{D}
be the
G
-equivariantization of
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
.
Then there exists a strongly rational VOA
\mathcal{V}
with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}
which has a group of VOA automorphisms isomorphic to
G
, and
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
and
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}\cong\mathbf{D}
.

Proof.

Follow the notation in the statement of Theorem
4.1
. By Theorem
3.2
, there is a holomorphic VOA
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}}
and a group of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}}
-automorphisms isomorphic to
\Gamma
such that
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}}^{\Gamma}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})
and
\mathbf{TwMod}_{\Gamma}\,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
. By Theorem 2 of
[
36
]
, there is a lattice VOA
\mathcal{V}_{L}
with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}_{L}\cong\mathbf{Vec}^{\omega}_{A}
. Choose any étale algebra
B_{A}
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega})\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega}\boxtimes(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega})^{\mathrm{rev}}
such that
(\mathbf{Rep}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega})}\,B_{A})^{\mathrm{loc}}\cong\mathbf{Vec}
– e.g.
B_{A}=\oplus_{\chi\in\hat{A}}[0,\chi]
works.

Note that the orbifold
(\mathcal{V}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}})^{1\times A}
(regarding
A
as a normal subgroup of
\Gamma
) is a strongly rational VOA with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,(\mathcal{V}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}})^{1\times A}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}\boxtimes\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega})\boxtimes\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}
. Let
\mathcal{V}
denote the extension of
(\mathcal{V}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}})^{1\times A}
by

. Then
\mathcal{V}
is strongly rational, with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}
, and
G=\Gamma/A
acts on
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}}^{A}
hence
\mathcal{V}
as VOA automorphisms.

Perhaps the easiest way to see the latter is to take first the full orbifold
(\mathcal{V}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}})^{1\times\Gamma}
, which has MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}\boxtimes\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega})\boxtimes\mathbf{D}
, since
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})\cong(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)})^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathbf{D}
by Proposition
2.2
. Since by hypothesis
\mathbf{D}
is the

-equivariantization of the braided

-crossed extension
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)}
,
\mathbf{D}
contains an étale algebra

(a copy of the algebra of functions on

) such that the de-equivariantization
\mathbf{D}_{G}
is
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
. Note that the two étale algebras

and

commute. Extending
(\mathcal{V}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}})^{1\times A}
by both returns us to
\mathcal{V}
. The
G
-action on
\mathcal{V}
comes from the extension by

.

In any case we see that the orbifold
\mathcal{V}^{G}
obtains
(\mathcal{V}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{hol}})^{1\times\Gamma}
extended by

, which has
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}\cong\mathbf{D}
. Also,
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
recovers
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
, by construction of
\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Using Theorem 7.2 of
[
62
]
together with Theorem

, the following is immediate:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a group-theoretical MTC. Then there exists a pointed VOA
\mathcal{V}
and a finite group

of
\mathcal{V}
-automorphisms such that
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
is braided tensor equivalent to
\mathbf{D}
.

This result is more general than it may first look. For example, all semisimple quasi-Hopf

[

]
).

4.3
Quasi-Hopf interpretations

Since all FPdims in the equivariantization
(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})^{G}
are integers (being as it is a subcategory of the quantum double
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})
), that MTC will be the category of representations of a quasitriangular quasi-Hopf algebra. We shall describe and relate two constructions of such quasi-Hopf algebras.

Given a central extension
\Gamma
of
G
by

Recall the 1-cochains

Proposition 4.2
.

For each

define

[a,x_{a}]
is a simple object in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})

(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\omega,c)})^{\mathrm{rev}}

\mathbf{D}=(\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}})^{G}
consists of the simple objects

\chi(a)=\overline{x_{a}(\gamma)}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\chi

Proof.

First note that for any

\frac{t_{a}(b)}{\nu(a)(b)}\frac{t_{a}(b^{\prime})}{\nu(a)(b^{\prime})}\frac{\nu(a)(b+b^{\prime})}{t_{a}(b+b^{\prime})}=\theta_{a}(b,b^{\prime})

Recall that a pointed MTC is uniquely determined by its fusions (defining an abelian group) and the ribbon twists

By e.g. (5.30) of

S^{\Gamma}_{[a,x_{a}],[b,x_{b}]}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\frac{t_{a}(b)}{\nu(a)(b)}\frac{t_{b}(a)}{\nu(b)(a)}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\overline{c(a,b)\,c(b,a)}=\frac{\sqrt{|A|}}{|\Gamma|}\overline{S^{A}_{a,b}}

where

\frac{S^{\Gamma}_{[a,x_{a}],[b,x_{b}]}}{S^{\Gamma}_{[0,1],[b,x_{b}]}}\,\frac{S^{\Gamma}_{[a^{\prime},x_{a^{\prime}}],[b,x_{b}]}}{S^{\Gamma}_{[0,1],[b,x_{b}]}}=\overline{\frac{S^{A}_{a,b}}{S^{A}_{0,b}}}\,\overline{\frac{S^{A}_{a^{\prime},b}}{S^{A}_{0,b}}}=\overline{\frac{S^{A}_{a+a^{\prime},b}}{S^{A}_{0,b}}}=\frac{S^{\Gamma}_{[a+a^{\prime},x_{a+a^{\prime}}],[b,x_{b}]}}{S^{\Gamma}_{[0,1],[b,x_{b}]}}

4.5
Examples

As mentioned earlier, one situation where the module-map is guaranteed to be trivial, is when
A=\mathbb{Z}/2
. In this case, recall that
\mathbf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}^{(\omega,c)}
can be a MTC only when
[\omega]
is nontrivial in
H^{3}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Up to equivalence, there are precisely two MTC for
A=\mathbb{Z}/2
, corresponding to either sign in the braiding
c(1,1)=\pm\mathrm{i}
. The lattices VOAs
\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{E_{7}}
realize these MTC for
c(1,1)=\pm\mathrm{i}
respectively.

Consider
\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}}
, also known as the level 1 affine
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
VOA
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},1)
. Its automorphism group is SO
{}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
, which acts by the adjoint representation on the homogeneous subspace
(\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}})_{1}\cong\mathbb{C}^{3}
, which generates
\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}}
. Its
\mathbb{Z}/2
-central extension SU(2) acts as automorphisms on the nontrivial
\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}}
-module, which is generated by its lowest
L_{0}
-eigenspace, a copy of the irreducible 2-dimensional SU(2)-module. As is well-known, the finite subgroups
G
of automorphisms of
\mathcal{V}_{A_{1}}
therefore fall into the familiar ADE pattern: cyclic, dihedral, and Alt
{}_{4},\mathrm{Sym}_{5},\mathrm{Alt}_{5}
, and the relevant central extensions
\Gamma
are their lifts into SU(2). Then this falls into the framework of this section. (All such
G
are solvable, except Alt
5
, so for the latter we require Conjecture 1 to guarantee strong rationality of the VOA orbifold.)

More generally, the Main Theorem of
[
56
]
concerns groups
\Gamma
which contain exactly one subgroup
A
of order 2 (which therefore lies in the centre). Examples of such groups are SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
for
\mathbb{F}
a finite field of odd order, the generalized quaternion groups, and the finite subgroups of SU(2). The full classification of such
\Gamma
is given in Section 3.4 of
[
56
]
.

Using our Corollary
4.3
, their Main Theorem says in our language:

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a central extension of a finite group
G
by
A=\mathbb{Z}/2
, and assume
A
is the only order-2 subgroup of
\Gamma
. Let
\widetilde{\omega}\in Z^{3}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
be such that
[\widetilde{\omega}|_{A}]
is nontrivial. Then (MN1)-(MN2) are satisfied, and (MN3) defines the sign on a nondegenerate braiding

, such that
\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
will be a braided
G
-crossed extension of the MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{(\tilde{\omega}|_{A},\pm\mathrm{i})}
for that sign.

As explained in
[
56
]
, the 2-torsion subgroup of
H^{3}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
for such a group will be cyclic with the same order as any 2-Sylow subgroup of
\Gamma
, and the restriction
H^{3}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{\times})\to H^{3}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
is nontrivial. So the condition in the Corollary that
[\widetilde{\omega}|_{A}]
be nontrivial is equivalent to requiring that the class
[\widetilde{\omega}]
has full 2-order in
H^{3}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.

What does Corollary
4.4
say about the mysterious obstruction

? For

with trivial module-map,

depends on both the sign of

(which determines

to emphasize that dependence. Choose any

with

must vanish for the sign coming from (MN3). However, choose some integer

(mod 4) and coprime to

(as well as

and

We can generalize much of this argument. Consider

for any odd prime power. Then again there are up to MTC equivalence exactly two MTC

, determined by the value

Suppose

The trivial module-map case was also encountered in
[

is its lift to automorphisms of

[

depended explicitly on the existence of

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be any pointed VOA and
G
any group of automorphisms of
G
. Assume
G
is solvable (or if
G
is not solvable, assume Conjecture 1). If
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
is pointed, then the module-map is trivial and
G
is abelian. Conversely, if the module-map is trivial, then
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
is pointed iff
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V})
is pointed. Even when
G
is cyclic,
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
may not be pointed.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
, and suppose
\mathbf{D}^{G}=\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
is pointed. Let
x
be any simple object in
\mathbf{D}
(so it will be a simple current). Since induction from
\mathbf{D}^{G}
to
\mathbf{D}
is a tensor functor, Ind
(x)
will be simple and invertible in
\mathbf{D}
(with inverse Ind
(x^{*})
). By Frobenius reciprocity, any simple in
\mathbf{D}
arises as a subobject in some Ind
(x)
. Therefore every simple in
\mathbf{D}
is invertible. Thus by Proposition
4.1
the module-map is trivial.

Write
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
. Suppose the module-map is trivial. Then by Theorem
4.1
,
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{Vec}_{\Gamma}^{\tilde{\omega}}
for some
\Gamma

\widetilde{\omega}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{D})\cong(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathbf{D}^{G}
, we see that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{D})
is pointed iff
\mathbf{D}^{G}
is. This requires
\Gamma
(hence

) to be abelian, but
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{D})
may not be pointed even if
\Gamma
is abelian, as we’ll see shortly.∎

It is interesting that one direction of the Main Theorem of

; then it is possible to choose 3-cocycles so that
\mathbf{D}^{G}=\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
is not pointed. In particular choose
\widetilde{\omega}\in Z^{3}((\mathbb{Z}/n)^{3},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
to be
\widetilde{\omega}((a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}),(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}))=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a_{1}b_{2}c_{3}/n}
. This restricts to the trivial cocycle on
A
(and for any
n
, it is possible to find a
c
such that
\mathbf{Vec}_{(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{3}}^{(1,c)}
is a MTC). As explained in Section 6.2 of
[
15
]
,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Vec}_{(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{3}}^{\tilde{\omega}})
is not pointed.

Nevertheless, in the setting of the trivial module-map, it is very limiting to restrict to cyclic orbifolds (as is typical in the VOA and conformal field theory literature), because central extensions
\Gamma
of cyclic groups are always abelian. To get nonabelian
\Gamma
here, we need to allow noncyclic
G
.

5
Fixed-point free actions and
G
-Tambara-Yamagami

The other extreme is when the module-map
\rho
is fixed-point free, i.e. when
G
acts without fixed points on
A
. We will find shortly that this corresponds to a braided
G
-crossed extension of
Vec
{}_{A}^{q}
generalizing the Tambara-Yamagami ones:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A
be a finite abelian group. By a
G
-Tambara-Yamagami category
\mathbf{C}
of type

\mathcal{TY}_{G}(A)
we mean a fusion category whose only simple objects (up to equivalence) are
a\in A
as well as a unique
M^{g}
for each
g\in G
,
g\neq e
, and which obeys the fusions

a\otimes a^{\prime}\cong aa^{\prime}\,,\ a\otimes M^{g}\cong M^{g}\otimes a\cong M^{g},\ M^{g}\otimes M^{h}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\oplus_{a\in A}a&\mathrm{if}\ g=h^{-1}\cr\sqrt{|A|}M^{gh}&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right.

(5.1)

For
G=\mathbb{Z}/2
this recovers the standard Tambara-Yamagami categories
[
65
]
(and nothing more).
\mathbb{Z}/3
-Tambara-Yamagami categories are discussed in
[
46
]
, where they used
[
34
]
to show they exist iff
A\cong A^{\prime}\oplus A^{\prime}
for some abelian group
A^{\prime}
.
Note that
\mathcal{TY}_{G}(A)
-type categories are automatically
G
-graded, with trivial component tensor-equivalent to
Vec
{}_{A}^{\omega}
for some
\omega\in Z^{3}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, and with a unique
g
-graded simple for each
g\neq e
. We are interested in braided
G
-crossed extensions of some MTC
Vec
{}_{A}^{q}
, which are of
G
-Tambara-Yamagami type.
[
46
]
did not address the question of braided
\mathbb{Z}/3
-crossed structure on their categories.

For
A=0
the
G
-Tambara-Yamagami categories are
\mathbf{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
for some
\omega\in Z^{3}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.

The following is immediate:

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose there is a category
C
of type
\mathcal{TY}_{G}(A)
, which is a braided
G
-crossed extension of some MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
. Then:

\mathbf{(a)}

|A|\equiv 1
(mod
|G|
) and, if
|G|>2
,
\sqrt{|A|}\in\mathbb{Z}
;

\mathbf{(b)}
as a fusion category,
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
is
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{\omega}
where
[\omega]
is trivial in
H^{3}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
;

\mathbf{(c)}
as long as
|A|>1
,
\mathbf{C}\not\cong\mathbf{Rep}(H)
as a fusion category, for any finite group
H
;

\mathbf{(d)}
there are exactly
|H^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})|
braided
G
-crossed extensions of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
with the same action of
G
on
A
as
\mathbf{C}
has.

Proof.

That
\sqrt{|A|}\in\mathbb{Z}
, follows from (
5.1
) for any choice
g,h\neq e
with
gh\neq e
.
Also, Proposition
2.1
says that each nontrivial
g\in G
must act fixed-point freely on
A
(since there is a unique
g
-graded simple). Thus each nontrivial
G
-orbit in
A
has full cardinality
|G|
, i.e.
|A|\equiv 1
(mod
|G|
). Part (b) is Lemma 2.6 in
[
46
]
. For part (d), the torsor over
H^{2}
is trivial since
|A|
and
|G|
are coprime (by (a)), so by
[
34
]
we only have the
H^{3}
torsor.

To prove (c), suppose for contradiction that
\mathbf{C}\cong\mathbf{Rep}(H)
. Note that
|H|=\sum_{a}1^{2}+\sum_{g\neq e}\sqrt{|A|}^{2}=|A|\,|G|
. Since
Rep
(H)
must be graded by the centre
Z(H)
(more precisely, by
\widehat{Z(H)}\cong Z(H)
), we must have that
G\hookrightarrow Z(H)
(since
C
is graded by
G
). Now,
H/[H,H]\cong A
(the group of 1-dimensional representations of
H
), so
|[H,H]|=|G|
. Thus any
h\in[H,H]
has order dividing
|G|
, so
G
central implies
\langle h,G\rangle
has order dividing both
|G|^{\infty}
and
|H|=|A|\,|G|
, and we get
|\langle h,G\rangle|=|G|
as
|A|
is coprime to
|G|
. Hence
[H,H]=G
. Therefore
H
is a central extension of
A
by
G
, i.e.

e\to G\to H\to A\to 0

(5.2)

(the flip of (
2.8
)!) But
H^{2}(G,A)=0
since
|A|
and
|G|
are coprime, so Eilenberg-Mac Lane says (
5.2
) splits. Then
G
central forces
H\cong G\times A
, i.e.
H
is abelian, contradicting the existence of
H
-irreps of dimension
\sqrt{|A|}
.∎

Because the obstruction

and the
H^{2}
-torsor both vanish, the group extension
\Gamma
promised by (
2.8
) is
A{\times\!\vrule height=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.4pt\,}G
. Such semidirect products, where

acts fixed-point freely on

, are examples of Frobenius groups. We will return to this in Section 5.2.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
is a pointed VOA with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
, and
G
a group of
\mathcal{V}
-automorphisms. Suppose the module-map
\rho
acts fixed-point freely on
A
. Then the braided
G
-crossed extension
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
is
G
-Tambara-Yamagami.

Proof.

We know
TwMod
{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
is a braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
, by Theorem
2.2
. The uniqueness of the
g
-twisted module
M^{g}
follows from Proposition
2.1
. The
G
-grading then requires
a\otimes M^{g}\cong M^{g}\otimes a\cong M^{g}
. By rigidity,
0\in M^{g}\otimes M^{g^{-1}}
with multiplicity 1, so
a\in a\otimes M^{g}\otimes M^{g^{-1}}\cong M^{g}\otimes M^{g^{-1}}
, also with multiplicity 1. Hence
M^{g}\otimes M^{g^{-1}}\cong\oplus_{a\in A}a
. Since each
a
is invertible, each FPdim
(a)=1
, so each FPdim
(M^{g})=\sqrt{|A|}
and hence
M^{g}\otimes M^{h}\cong\sqrt{|A|}M^{gh}
when

. ∎

We see from this argument why the multiplicity
\sqrt{|A|}
appears in (

). Of course

\,\,\mathcal{V}^{G}
will be the
G
-equivariantization
(\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V})^{G}
.

5.2
General results on fixed-point free actions on metric groups

We begin with some applications of standard results on Frobenius groups. An accessible treatment of the latter is in
[
57
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(A,q)
be a metric group, and
\rho\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}(A,q)
be a fixed-point free homomorphism.

\mathbf{(a)}
Any subgroup of
G
of square-free order, or of order
p^{2}
(
p
prime), must be cyclic.

\mathbf{(b)}
The only nilpotent
G
are cyclic, or the direct product of an odd cyclic with generalized quaternions
Q_{2^{k}}=\langle a,b|b^{4}=1,a^{2^{k-2}}=b^{2},ab=ba^{-1}\rangle
.

\mathbf{(c)}
We can write
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
as the Deligne product of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A_{i}}^{q_{i}}
where
A_{i}=(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{k_{i}})^{n_{i}}
and
\rho
restricts to fixed-point free homomorphisms
G\to\mathrm{O}(A_{i},q_{i})
.

Proof.

Part (a) is Corollary 3.18 resp. Theorem 3.7(a) in
[
57
]
. A nilpotent group is the direct product of its
p
-Sylow subgroups. So
part (b) follows from Theorem 3.7(b) in
[
57
]
.

Write
A=\oplus_{j}A_{(j)}
, where
A_{(j)}
is the (unique)
p_{j}
-Sylow subgroup of
A
(
p_{j}
being the distinct prime divisors of
|A|
). Let
q_{j}
resp.
\rho_{j}
be the restriction of
q
resp.
\rho
to
A_{(j)}
. Then each
(A_{(j)},q_{j})
is a metric group (its non-degeneracy etc is inherited from that of
q
). Therefore
Vec
{}_{A}^{q}
is the Deligne product of
Vec
{}_{A_{(j)}}^{q_{j}}
. Moreover, each
\rho_{j}(g)
sends
A_{(j)}
to itself (since it must preserve order of elements), so
\rho_{j}\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}(A_{(j)},q_{j})
. Finally, each
\rho_{j}
is fixed-point free since
\rho
is.

Thus to prove part (c) it suffices to consider when
A
is a
p
-group. Let
p^{k}
be the exponent of
A
. Lemma 6.4 of
[
57
]
says we can write
A=A_{1}\oplus B
in such a way that
A_{1}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n}
is homocyclic (i.e. a product of isomorphic cyclic groups) of maximal exponent,
\rho(g)(A_{1})=A_{1}
for all
g
, and
B
has exponent
<p^{k}
. Let
\rho_{1}
resp.
q_{1}
be the restriction of
\rho
resp.
q
to
A_{1}
. Note that
q_{1}
is non-degenerate (since
q
is), so
(A_{1},q_{1})
is a metric group and
\rho_{1}\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}(A_{1},q_{1})
is fixed-point free. Let
A_{1}^{\perp}
be the complement in
A
of
A_{1}
with respect to
q
. Then
A_{1}\cap A_{1}^{\perp}=0
so
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{1}^{\perp}
and the exponent of
A_{1}^{\perp}
is
<p^{k}
. Let
\rho^{\prime}
resp.
q^{\prime}
be the restriction to
A_{1}^{\perp}
of
\rho
resp.
q
. Then
(A_{1}^{\perp},q^{\prime})
is a metric group, and
\rho^{\prime}\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}(A_{1}^{\perp},q^{\prime})
is fixed-point free. Now repeat the argument for
A_{1}^{\perp}
to define subgroups
A_{2},A_{3}
and so on.
∎

Conversely, if
\rho_{i}\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}((\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{k_{i}})^{n_{i}},q_{i})
are fixed-point free homomorphisms on homocyclic groups, then their product gives a fixed-point free homomorphism
\rho\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}(\oplus_{i}(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{k_{i}})^{n_{i}},\prod_{i}q_{i})
.
Thus it suffices to restrict attention henceforth to
A=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n}
.

Incidentally, the generalized quaternions have cohomology groups
H^{3}(Q_{2^{k}},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2^{k}
and
H^{4}(Q_{2^{k}},\mathbb{C}^{\times})=0
.

Write
\xi_{n}=\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}/n)
. The possible metric groups are classified in e.g.
[
63
]
. For
p
odd, fix a nonquadratic residue
m
of
p
. Then up to MTC-equivalence any
Vec
{}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}^{q}
is equivalent to either
Vec
{}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}^{+}
(with
q(\ell)=\xi_{p^{k}}^{\ell^{2}}
) or
Vec
{}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}^{-}
(with
q(\ell)=\xi_{p^{k}}^{m\ell^{2}}
). We can write any
Vec
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n}}^{q}
(up to equivalence) as a Deligne product of
Vec
{}_{p^{k}}^{s_{i}}
,
s_{i}=\pm
. Since
Vec
{}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}^{-}\boxtimes\mathbf{Vec}^{-}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}^{+}\boxtimes\mathbf{Vec}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}}
, we can insist that at most one of the signs be
-
.

When
A
is a 2-group, the only
Vec
{}_{A}^{q}
compatible with Lemma
5.1
(b) are Deligne products of copies of
Vec
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/2^{k})^{2}}^{\pm}
, where ‘
+
’ corresponds to quadratic form
q_{+}(a,b)=ab/2^{k}
and ‘
-
’ corresponds to
q_{-}(a,b)=(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})/2^{k}
.

5.3
Realization of
G
-Tambara-Yamagami by VOAs

As explainded last subsection, we can restrict to
A
of the form
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n}
.
There are many
G
which have braided
G
-crossed categories of type
\mathcal{TY}_{G}(A)
– e.g. the order 120 group SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/5)
is the smallest nonsolvable
G
for which a fixed-point free
\rho\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}(A,q)
can be found. But thanks to Lemma
5.2
most of the ones of smaller order will be cyclic or a direct product of an odd cyclic with generalized quaternions. In this subsection we restrict to these

, and find for each prime power
p^{k}
an
n\geq 1
and a lattice
L
such that
Mod
\,\,\mathcal{V}_{L}\cong\mathbf{Vec}_{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n}}^{q}
for some
q
, and a group of
\mathcal{V}_{L}
-automorphisms isomorphic to
G
whose module-map
\rho\,{:}\,G\to\mathrm{O}((\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n},q)
is fixed-point free. Then by Theorem
5.1
,
TwMod
{}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}_{L}
will be a braided
G
-crossed extension of
Vec
{}_{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k})^{n}}^{q}
of type
\mathcal{TY}_{G}(A)
. Our suspicion is that the
n
we find may be the smallest possible.

The easiest source of such VOA-actions are
signed permutation representations
. This is a matrix representation
\rho
where each
\rho(g)
is a signed permutation matrix, i.e.

\rho(g)(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})=(s_{g,1}u_{\pi_{g}^{-1}1},s_{g,2}u_{\pi_{g}^{-1}2},\ldots,s_{g,n}u_{\pi_{g}^{-1}n})

(5.3)

where
\pi_{g}\in\mathrm{Sym}(n)
and each
s_{g,i}\in\{\pm 1\}
. We say
\rho
is fixed-point free if no
\rho(g)
, for
g\neq e
, has 1 as an eigenvalue.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\rho
be an
n
-dimensional signed permutation representation of a finite group
G
. Choose any MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
, where
|A|
is coprime to
|G|
. Then there is a lattice VOA
\mathcal{V}
with
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}\cong(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\boxtimes n}:=\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
(
n
times) as a MTC, and
G
acts as automorphisms on
\mathcal{V}
with module-map given by
\rho
. If
\rho
as a signed permutation representation is fixed-point free, then so is the module-map.

Proof.

Let
L
be any even positive definite lattice realizing
(A,q)
. Choose the 2-cocycle
\varepsilon_{L}
defining
\mathcal{V}_{L}
using (
2.6
). Note that
\varepsilon_{L}(-u,-v)=\varepsilon_{L}(u,v)
for this choice. Define
L^{\oplus n}:=L\oplus\cdots\oplus L
(
n
times) for any
n
. We can take the 2-cocycle for
\mathcal{V}_{L^{\oplus n}}
to be
\varepsilon_{L^{\oplus n}}((u_{1},...,u_{n}),(v_{1},...,v_{n}))=\prod_{i}\varepsilon_{L}(u_{i},v_{i})
. Then

\,\,\mathcal{V}_{L^{\oplus n}}\cong(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\boxtimes n}
as MTC.

Let
g\in G
. By assumption
\rho(g)
is a signed permutation matrix. Use it to define an isometry
\sigma=\sigma_{g}
of
\Lambda^{\oplus n}
through (
5.3
). To study the possible lifts
\hat{\sigma}
to Aut
(\mathcal{V}_{L^{\oplus n}})
, we need to solve (
2.7
) for
\eta
. But

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{L^{\oplus n}}(\sigma(u_{1},...,u_{n}),\sigma(v_{1},...,v_{n}))}{\varepsilon_{L^{\oplus n}}((u_{1},...,u_{n}),(v_{1},...,v_{n}))}\!\!

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon_{L}(s_{1}u_{\pi^{-1}1},s_{1}v_{\pi^{-1}1})\varepsilon_{L}(s_{2}u_{\pi^{-1}2},s_{2}v_{\pi^{-1}2})\cdots\varepsilon_{L}(s_{n}u_{\pi^{-1}n},s_{n}v_{\pi^{-1}n)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{L}(u_{j},v_{j})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon_{L}(s_{\pi i}u_{i},s_{\pi i}v_{i})}{\varepsilon_{L}(u_{i},v_{i})}=1

This means we can choose
\eta
in (
2.7
) to be identically 1, for all
g\in G
. Then manifestly
\widehat{\rho(g)}
has the same order as
\rho(g)
, in fact
\widehat{\rho}
defines a homomorphism of
G
into Aut
(\mathcal{V}_{L^{\oplus n}})
, so
\widehat{\rho}
defines an action of
G
on
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{L^{\oplus n}}
by VOA automorphisms. The module-map of
\widehat{\rho}
will also be by (
5.3
):

\widehat{\rho}(g)(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})=(s_{g,1}a_{\pi_{g}^{-1}1},s_{g,2}a_{\pi_{g}^{-1}2},\ldots,s_{g,n}a_{\pi_{g}^{-1}n})\ \ \forall(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})\in(\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q})^{\boxtimes n}

(5.4)

for the same
\pi_{g}
and
s_{g,i}
as for
\rho(g)
. ∎

The only finite groups
G
with a fixed-point free signed permutation representation are 2-groups;
by Lemma
5.2
, such a

must then be either cyclic or generalized quaternion.
Consider first the cyclic group
G=\mathbb{Z}/2^{k}
. It has a fixed-point free
2^{k-1}
-dimensional signed permutation representation generated by the
2^{k-1}
-cycle
\pi_{1}=(12\cdots 2^{k-1})
and signs
s_{1,i}=1
for

and
s_{1,1}=-1
.

Next consider the generalized quaternions
Q_{2^{k}}
of order
2^{k}
(

), with presentation given in Lemma
5.2
(b).
To the generator
a
resp.

associate the signed permutation matrices with

\displaystyle\pi_{a}=(12\cdots 2^{k-2})(2^{k-2}+1,2^{k-2}+2,\ldots,2^{k-1})\,,\ \ s_{a,1}=s_{a,2^{k-2}+1}=-1\,,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\pi_{b}=(1,2^{k-1}+1)(2,2^{k-2}+2)\cdots(2^{k-2},2^{k-1})\,,\ \ s_{b,j}=-1\ \forall j\leq 2^{k-2}\,,

(5.6)

using cycle notation, and all other

. Again, this is fixed-point free.

Corollary 5.1
.

Choose any metric group
(A,q)
(
|A|
odd) and let

or

for some

\mathbf{C}
of

of

on which

.

The existence of

had already been clear from the vanishing of the obstructions

.

To handle odd

6
The diagonal argument

In this section we explore a simple strategy to combine the trivial module-map (i.e. cleft) case and the fixed-point free module-map (
G
-Tambara-Yamagami) case into a hybrid model. Certainly this can be pushed much further than we do here.

The following is clear.

Lemma 6.1
.

\mathbf{(a)}
Suppose
\mathbf{D}
is a braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C}
, and
\mathbf{D^{\prime}}
is a braided
H
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C^{\prime}}
. Then
\mathbf{D}\boxtimes\mathbf{D^{\prime}}
is a braided
G\times H
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C}\boxtimes\mathbf{C^{\prime}}
.

\mathbf{(b)}
Let
N
be a normal subgroup of a finite group
G
. Let
\mathbf{D}
be a braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C}
. Define the full subcategory
\mathbf{D}|_{N}
of
\mathbf{D}
whose objects are sums of homogeneous simple objects with grading in
N
. Then
\mathbf{D}|_{N}
is a braided
N
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C}
.

For example, write
\Delta_{G}
for the diagonal subgroup
\{(g,g)\in G\times G\,|\,g\in G\}
. Then Lemma
6.1
says that
(\mathbf{D}\boxtimes\mathbf{D^{\prime}})|_{\Delta_{G}}
is a braided
G
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{C}\boxtimes\mathbf{C^{\prime}}
. Perhaps a better name for this diagonal construction is a
graded Deligne product

\mathbf{D}\boxtimes_{(G)}\mathbf{D^{\prime}}
. Note that it decomposes as
\mathbf{D}\boxtimes_{(G)}\mathbf{D^{\prime}}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathbf{D}_{g}\boxtimes\mathbf{D^{\prime}}_{g}
.

On VOAs the diagonal construction works as follows. Suppose
G
acts as automorphisms on two VOAs
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}
. On
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{V}
suppose the module-map is trivial, so
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}
is cleft, and on
\mathbf{Mod}\,\,\mathcal{W}
suppose it is fixed-point free, so
\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{W}
is
G
-Tambara-Yamagami. Then
\mathbf{TwMod}_{\Delta_{G}}\,(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{W})=\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{V}\boxtimes_{(G)}\mathbf{TwMod}_{G}\,\mathcal{W}
is hybrid.

More interesting is the converse:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(A,q)
be a metric group and
\mathbf{D}
a braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
for some prime
p
coprime to
|A|
. Let
B
consist of all
a\in A
fixed by all
g\in\mathbb{Z}/p
. Suppose that
q|_{B}
is non-degenerate. Then
\mathbf{D}
is equivalent, as a braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extension, to the graded Deligne product

\mathbf{D}_{c}\boxtimes_{(\mathbb{Z}/p)}\mathbf{D}_{ty}=(\mathbf{D}_{c}\boxtimes\mathbf{D}_{ty})|_{\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}/p}}

(6.1)

where
\mathbf{D}_{c}
is a pointed (i.e. cleft) braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extension of
\mathbf{Vec}_{B}^{q|_{B}}
and
\mathbf{D}_{ty}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/p
-Tambara-Yamagami type braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extension of the MTC
\mathbf{Vec}_{B^{\perp}}^{q|_{B^{\perp}}}
.

Proof.

Note that
B
is a subgroup of
A
, so non-degeneracy of
q|_{B}
implies that
A=B\oplus B^{\perp}
. Also,
q|_{B^{\perp}}
will be non-degenerate, so indeed
\mathbf{Vec}_{B^{\perp}}^{q|_{B^{\perp}}}
is a MTC. Moreover, by definition of
B
, the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action on
B^{\perp}
will be fixed-point free. Let
\rho
denote the module-map
\mathbb{Z}/p\to\mathrm{O}(A,q)
corresponding to
\mathbf{D}
, and let
\rho_{\perp}
denote the (fixed-point free) map
\mathbb{Z}/p\to\mathrm{O}(B^{\perp},q|_{B^{\perp}})
.

Both obstructions
o_{3},o_{4}
vanish for the trivial map
\mathbb{Z}/p\to\mathrm{O}(B,q|_{B})
as well as for
\rho_{\perp}
, since the corresponding
H^{3},H^{4}
cohomology groups vanish, and therefore there exist braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extensions of
\mathbf{Vec}_{B}^{q|_{B}}
and
\mathbf{Vec}_{B^{\perp}}^{q|_{B^{\perp}}}
for the respective module-maps. Choose one such extension for each. The former will be a pointed (cleft) category we’ll call
\mathbf{D}_{c}
, and the latter, which we’ll call
\mathbf{D}_{ty}
, will be of
\mathbb{Z}/p
-Tambara-Yamagami type.

Therefore by Lemma
6.1
there will be a braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extension
\mathbf{D}_{c}\boxtimes_{(\mathbb{Z}/p)}\mathbf{D}_{ty}
of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
, with module-map
\rho
. Now, the
H^{2}
-torsor for

is trivial, since

. Therefore all braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extensions of
\mathbf{Vec}_{A}^{q}
lie on a torsor over

. Thus the original category
\mathbf{D}
will be an
H^{3}
-twist of the associativity of
\mathbf{D}_{c}\boxtimes_{(\mathbb{Z}/p)}\mathbf{D}_{ty}
.

On the other hand we see that there are precisely
p

\mathbb{Z}/p
-Tamabara-Yamagami braided
\mathbb{Z}/p
-crossed extensions of
\mathbf{Vec}_{B^{\perp}}^{q|_{B^{\perp}}}
, related to each other through the

-torsor as well. By the linearity of the Deligne product, twisting
\mathbf{D}_{ty}
by
\omega
and then taking the diagonal product with
\mathbf{D}_{c}
is the same as twisting
\mathbf{D}_{c}\boxtimes_{\mathbb{Z}/n}\mathbf{D}_{ty}
by
\omega
. This means running through the

-torsor of

gives only diagonal products of
\mathbb{Z}/p
-Tambara-Yamagami and a pointed extension, so making a different choice of

if necessary, we get the theorem.
∎

For example, if
B
is any subgroup of

with
|A|/|B|
coprime to

, then automatically

will be non-degenerate.

G/C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})
, where
C_{K}(g)
denotes the centralizer, and denote the representatives by
\{x_{1}^{b},\ldots,x_{m}^{b}\}
such that
x_{1}^{b}=id
. We then have
C_{b}=\{g_{1}^{b}=x_{1}^{b}g_{1}^{b}(x_{1}^{b})^{-1},\ldots,g_{r}^{b}=x_{m}^{b}g_{1}^{b}(x_{m}^{b})^{-1}\}
. There is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of irreducible
D^{\omega}(K)
-modules and irreducible
\mathbb{C}_{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})]
-modules for each
1\leq b\leq r
. Suppose
\rho:\mathbb{C}_{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})]\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
is irreducible. The corresponding irreducible
D^{\omega}(K)
-module was explicitly constructed in
[
24
]
, which we recall now.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{b}
be the subalgebra of
D^{\omega}(K)
which is spanned by the elements
\{\delta_{x}a:(x,a)\in K\times C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})\}
. We may define a
\mathcal{B}_{b}
-representation
\rho_{V}:\mathcal{B}_{b}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
by
\rho_{V}(\delta_{x}\otimes a)(v):=\delta_{x,g_{1}^{A}}\rho(a)(v)
. One can then take the induced
D^{\omega}(K)
-module,
D^{\omega}(K)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{b}}V
. In
[
24
]
Dijkgraaf et. al gave an explicit formula for the
D^{\omega}(K)
-action on this induced module which we now explain. First, choose a basis
\{v_{t}\}_{t=1}^{n}
for
V
, and note that a basis of
D^{\omega}(K)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{b}}V
will be given by
\{(\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{t}^{b})\otimes v_{j}:1\leq t\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\}
. The action is then defined on this basis by:

\rho_{V}(\delta_{x}g)((\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{j}^{b})\otimes v_{i}):=\theta_{x}(g,x_{j}^{b})\theta_{x}(x_{k}^{b},h)^{-1}\delta_{g^{-1}xg,g_{j}^{b}}((\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{k}^{b})\otimes(\rho(h)(v_{i})))

(A.34)

where
x_{k},h
are such that
gx_{j}^{b}=x_{k}^{b}h
, and
h\in C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})
.

Now assume that we have a quantum cleft extension such that
D^{\omega}(K,A)
is a quasi-Hopf algebra quotient with quotient map
\pi:D^{\omega}(K)\rightarrow D^{\omega}(K,A)
and its modules form a MTC. Since
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
is a full fusion subcategory of
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
, to determine the irreducible objects of
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
it suffices to determine which simple objects of
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K)
are in the fusion subcategory. More precisely, a representation
\rho:D^{\omega}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}V
is in
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
iff
\ker(\pi)\subset\ker(\rho)
. For that reason we calculate
\ker(\pi)
. As in the previous subsection, let the quotient map
\pi
be given by
\pi(\delta_{x}g)=c(g,x)\delta_{x}\overline{g}

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\partial:K\rightarrow G
be as in Lemma
A.4
, and
B_{x,h}:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{c(g,x)^{-1}\delta_{x}g-c(k,x)^{-1}\delta_{x}k:g,k\in\partial^{-1}(h)\}
. Then

\ker(\pi)=\bigoplus_{(g,h)\in K\times G}B_{g,h}

(A.35)

Proof.

To see this note that if we have choose a set of representatives for
G
, say
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\}
, then
\{\delta_{x}\otimes\overline{y}_{i}:x\in K,1\leq i\leq k\}
is a basis of
D^{\omega}(K,A)
. If
v=\sum_{(x,k)\in K^{2}}a_{x,k}(\delta_{x}k)
, then:

\pi(v)=\sum_{(x,h)\in K\times G}\sum_{k\in\partial^{-1}(h)}c(k,x)a_{x,k}\delta_{x}h=0\Leftrightarrow\sum_{k\in\partial^{-1}(h)}c(k,x)a_{x,k}=0\ \ \ \ \forall(x,h)\in K\times G

Now fix a pair
(x,h)\in K\times G
, and let
\partial^{-1}(h)=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\}
. By basic algebra one sees that:

\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{x,h_{i}}\delta_{x}h_{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{r}c(h_{i},x)a_{x,h_{i}}=0\}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{c(h_{i},x)^{-1}\delta_{x}h_{i}-c(h_{j},x)^{-1}\delta_{x}h_{j}:1\leq i,j\leq r\}=B_{x,h}

∎

Theorem A.2
.

Let
\{g_{1}^{b}\}_{b=1}^{r}
be a set of conjugacy class representatives for
K
. Suppose that
\rho_{V}:\mathbb{C}_{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})]\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
is an irreducible representation. Then

(g_{1}^{b},\rho_{V})\in\mathbf{Rep}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)\text{ if and only if }\rho(a)=c(a,g_{1}^{A})\mathrm{Id}_{V}\ \ \ \forall a\in A=\ker(\partial)

(A.36)

Proof.

First, we show there is a natural bijection between the sets:

\displaystyle\left\{\rho_{v}\in\mathrm{Irr.Rep}(\mathbb{C}_{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})]):\rho(a)=c(a,g_{1}^{b})\mathrm{Id}_{V}\ \ \forall a\in A\right\}\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Irr.Rep}(\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})])

Suppose that we have
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr.Rep}(\mathbb{C}_{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})])
with the prescribed condition. Then observe that
C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})=\overline{C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})}
,
\frac{1}{c(-,g_{1}^{b})}\rho|_{A}=\mathrm{Id}
, and

\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{1}{c(x,g_{1}^{b})c(y,g_{1}^{b})}\rho(x)\rho(y)=\frac{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(x,y)}{c(x,g_{1}^{b})c(y,g_{1}^{b})}\rho(xy)=\frac{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(x,y)c(xy,g_{1}^{b})}{c(x,g_{1}^{b})c(y,g_{1}^{b})}\rho(xy)=\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}(\partial(x),\partial(y))\rho(xy)

Here the last equality holds, since
D^{\omega}(K,A)
is a quotient of
D^{\omega}(K)
, and
x,y\in C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})
. We see that indeed there will exist a
\tilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{Irr.Rep}(\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})])
such that
\tilde{\rho}\circ\partial=\frac{1}{c(-,g_{1}^{b})}\rho
. Conversely, if
\tilde{\rho}:\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})]\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
is an irreducible representation, then
\rho(x):=c(x,g_{1}^{b})\tilde{\rho}(\partial(x))
is a
irreducible representation of
\mathbb{C}_{\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})]
such that
A\subseteq\ker(\rho)
.

As outlined above, the irreducible modules of
D^{\omega}(K)
are determined by a conjugacy class representative
g_{1}^{b}
, and an irreducible projective representation
\rho
of its centralizer. To prove the Theorem, we will use the bijection just obtained and a counting argument.

First, note that
(g_{1}^{b},\rho_{v})\in\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
if and only if
\ker(\pi)\subseteq\ker(\rho_{V})
. Supposing this is the case, by Lemma
A.35
we have
B_{g_{1}^{b},\mathrm{Id}_{K}}\subseteq\ker(\rho_{V})
. Let
x,y\in A
. Then:

\rho_{V}(c(x,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\delta_{g_{1}^{b}}x)=\rho_{V}(c(y,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\delta_{g_{1}^{b}}y)

Observe that since
A
is a central subgroup of
K
we have
xx_{1}^{b}=x_{1}^{b}x,yx_{1}^{b}=x_{1}^{b}y
for all
1\leq b\leq m
, and
x_{1}^{b}=\mathrm{Id}_{K}
. Combining these observations we can expand the above action using (
A.34
) to obtain:

\rho_{V}(c(x,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\delta_{g_{1}^{b}}x)((\delta_{\mathrm{Id}}x_{1}^{b})\otimes v_{j})=c(x,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(x,x_{1}^{b})\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(x_{1}^{b},x)^{-1}(\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{1}^{b})\otimes\rho_{V}(x)(v_{j})

\rho_{V}(c(g_{1}^{b},y)^{-1}\delta_{g_{1}^{b}}y)((\delta_{\mathrm{Id}}x_{1}^{b})\otimes v_{j})=c(y,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(y,x_{1}^{b})\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(x_{1}^{b},y)^{-1}(\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{1}^{b})\otimes\rho_{V}(y)(v_{j})

But, since
x_{1}^{b}=1
, we see that

\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(y,x_{1}^{b})\theta_{g_{1}^{b}}(x_{1}^{b},y)^{-1}=1\Rightarrow c(x,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\rho_{V}(x)=c(y,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\rho_{V}(y)

In particular, by setting
y=\mathrm{Id}_{K}
we have :

c(x,g_{1}^{b})^{-1}\rho_{V}(x)=\rho(\mathrm{Id}_{K})=\mathrm{Id}_{V}\Rightarrow\rho_{V}(x)=c(x,g_{1}^{b})\mathrm{Id}_{V}\ \ \ \ \forall x\in A

We see then that every simple
(g_{1}^{b},\rho_{V})\in\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
corresponds to an irreducible representation of
\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})]
. We claim that it is surjective.

Denote the simple modules of
D^{\omega}(K,A)
by
\mathcal{O}
.
Since
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
is integral we have that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(D^{\omega}(K,A))=|K|\cdot|G|=\sum_{V\in\mathcal{O}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V)^{2}
. By looking at
the projective analogue of the regular representation for
\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})]
we know that
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
:

|C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})|=\sum_{W\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})])}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(W)^{2}

Suppose there exists a
1\leq b_{0}\leq r
and a
V\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b_{0}}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b_{0}})])
that does not correspond to a simple object of
\mathrm{\textbf{Rep}}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
. Note that the dimension of
(g_{1}^{b},\rho_{V})
is
\frac{|K|}{|C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})|}\dim(V_{\rho})
, but by assumption this implies that:

|G|\cdot|K|<\sum_{b=1}^{r}\frac{|K|^{2}}{|C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})|^{2}}\sum_{W\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(\overline{g_{1}}^{b})])}\dim(W)^{2}=\sum_{b=1}^{r}\frac{|K|^{2}}{|C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})|^{2}}|C_{G}(\overline{g}_{1}^{b})|=

(A.37)

\sum_{b=1}^{r}\frac{|K|^{2}}{|C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})|^{2}}\frac{|C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})|}{|A|}=\frac{|K|^{2}}{|A|}\sum_{b=1}^{r}\frac{1}{|C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})|}=\frac{|K|^{2}}{|A|}\cdot 1=|G|\cdot|K|

This is a contradiction, which proves the correspondence is surjective, implying the theorem.

∎

Notice that since
G
is a central quotient of
K
, the set of representatives of the action of
G
on
K
is just given by
\{g_{1}^{b}\}_{b=1}^{r}
. In other words, it is really just the conjugation action of
G
. With this in mind note (compare with
[
61
]
):

Corollary A.2
.

Up to equivalence,
the simple objects of
\mathbf{Rep}\,\,D^{\omega}(K,A)
are parametrized by the irreducible projective representations of
\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\theta}_{g_{1}^{b}}}[C_{G}(g_{1}^{b})]
for each
1\leq b\leq r
.

We already obtained this classification in Section 4.4. The advantage of the approach here is that we can construct all irreducible
D^{\omega}(K,A)
modules. Namely, if
\rho_{V}
is as above, then
\tilde{\rho}_{V}(\delta_{g}\overline{x}):=\frac{1}{c(x,g)}\rho_{V}(\delta_{g}x)
. Explicitly,

\tilde{\rho}_{V}(\delta_{g}\overline{x})((\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{j}^{b})\otimes v_{i})=\frac{1}{c(x,g)}\frac{\theta_{g}(x,x_{j}^{b})}{\theta_{g}(x_{k}^{b},h)}\delta_{g,xg_{j}^{b}x^{-1}}(\delta_{\mathrm{Id}_{K}}x_{k}^{b})\otimes\rho_{V}(h)(v_{i})

(A.38)

Here again
x_{j}^{b},x_{k}^{b},h
are all determined by
xx_{j}^{b}=x_{k}^{b}h,h\in C_{K}(g_{1}^{b})
.

Universal Coacting Hopf algebra of a Finite dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra
Saikat Goswami, Satyendra Kumar Mishra, Goutam Mukherjee

Abstract
. M. E. Sweedler first constructed a universal Hopf algebra of an algebra. It is known that the dual notions to the existing ones play a dominant role in Hopf algebra theory. Yu. I. Manin and D. Tambara introduced the dual notion of Sweedler’s construction in separate works. In this paper, we construct a universal algebra for a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. We demonstrate that this universal algebra possesses a bialgebra structure, leading to a universal coacting Hopf algebra for a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Additionally, we develop a representation-theoretic version of our results. As an application, we characterize the automorphism group and classify all abelian group gradings of a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

1.
Introduction

This article’s central objects of study are Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which generalize Lie triple systems and Lie algebras simultaneously. N. Jacobson
[
8
]
introduced the notion of a Lie triple system as an algebraic object closely associated with quantum mechanics. Lie-Yamaguti algebras arose naturally from K. Nomizu’s
[
17
]
work on invariant affine connections on homogeneous spaces. K. Yamaguti
[
23
]
introduced the notion of Lie-Yamaguti algebras with the name
general Lie triple system
in order to characterize the torsion and curvature tensors of K. Nomizu’s
[
17
]
canonical connection. Later, M. Kikkawa
[
10
]
renamed general Lie triple systems as
Lie triple algebra
. M. Kikkawa in
[
9
]
showed that Lie-Yamaguti algebras are deeply connected to differential geometry by proving that the category of simply connected, complete, and locally reductive spaces with base points is equivalent to the category of real finite-dimensional Lie triple algebras.

M. K. Kinyon and A. Weinstein
[
13
]
observed that Lie triple algebras (they coined the name
Lie-Yamaguti algebras
for Lie triple algebras) can be constructed from Leibniz algebras. A. Sagle
[
19
]
constructed several examples of Lie-Yamaguti algebras from reductive homogeneous spaces. K. Yamaguti
[
24
]
showed that Lie-Yamaguti algebras are related to many other non-associative algebras; for example, every Malcev algebra
(L,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
gives a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
L
with the binary operation
[\cdot,\cdot]
and the ternary operation
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}
being

(1)

[x,y]=\langle x,y\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\{x,y,z\}=\langle x,\langle y,z\rangle\rangle-\langle y,\langle x,z\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle x,y\rangle,z\rangle,\quad\forall x,y,z\in L.

We refer to
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
16
,
12
,
11
,
25
]
for recent advancements in the area of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Let
A
and
B
be two unital associative algebras over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero, with
\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
denoting the vector space of all linear maps from
A
to
B
. Let
C
be a coalgebra over
\mathbb{K}
with comultiplication
\Delta
and counit
\epsilon
. A linear map
\phi:C\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
is said to be a
coalgebra measuring
if

\phi(c)(ab)=\sum\phi(c_{1})(a)\cdot\phi(c_{2})(b)\qquad\forall~~a,b\in A~~\mbox{and}~~\Delta(c)=\sum c_{1}\otimes c_{2},

\phi(c)(1_{A})=\epsilon(c)1_{B}\quad\forall~c\in C.

If such a coalgebra measuring exists, we say that the pair
(C,\phi)
measures
A
to
B
(see
[
20
, Sec. 7.0, Pg. 139]
for more details). Coalgebra measuring can be realized as a generalization of algebra morphism from

to

, the map

becomes an algebra morphism. For two algebras

and

, M. E. Sweedler introduced the universal coalgebra measuring
M(A,B)
. More precisely, M. E. Sweedler
[
20

proved that for any two algebras

and

, there exists a coalgebra

and a map

that measures
A
to
B
with the following universal property: for any coalgebra
C
and a map
\psi:C\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
, if
(C,\psi)
measures
A
to
B
, then there is a unique coalgebra map
F:C\to M(A,B)
such that the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M(A,B)\otimes A}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}

commutes. In the above diagram, we identified a map

as a map
\phi:C\otimes A\rightarrow B
(since the
\operatorname{Hom}

enriches the category of algebras over the category of coalgebras. Subsequently, this enrichment leads to universal measuring bialgebra
[
20

The dual notion to an existing notion plays an equally important role, we refer the reader to
[
18
]
to see their importance in non-commutative algebraic geometry. The dual version of the Sweedler’s work was considered by Tambara in
[

and in the graded algebra case by Manin in
[

has a bialgebra structure. The Hopf envelope (Hopf algebra generated by a bialgebra) of

[
15

[

[
3

[
14

22

In this paper, our first aim is to show that we can construct a universal coacting Hopf algebra for a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. This construction gives a left adjoint functor to the current Lie-Yamaguti algebra functor (see Example
2.5
for the definition). As an application, we characterize automorphisms of a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra in terms of its universal coacting Hopf algebra. We also classify abelian group gradings of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra using our construction. The article’s second aim is to discuss the representation theoretic counterpart of Manin-Tambara’s universal constructions in the finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebras category.

The section-wise description of the paper is as follows: In Section

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, all vector spaces, (bi)linear maps, algebras, and bialgebras are over a fixed
\mathbb{K}
, an arbitrary field of characteristic
0
. By a “basis”, we always mean vector space basis over
\mathbb{K}
, and by a (co)algebra, we always mean (co)unital, (co)associative algebra. We denote by
\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}
, the category of all algebras over
\mathbb{K}
and by
\operatorname{ComAlg}_{\mathbb{K}}
, the category of all commutative algebras over
\mathbb{K}
. This section recalls all the necessary definitions and notions required in this paper.

Lie-Yamaguti algebras

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie-Yamaguti Algebra
over
\mathbb{K}
is a triple
(\mathfrak{L},[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
, where
\mathfrak{L}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{K}
equipped with a bilinear product
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}
and a trilinear product
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}:\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}
such that the following conditions hold

(LY1)

[a,b]=-[b,a],

(LY2)

\{a,b,c\}=-\{b,a,c\},

(LY3)

[[a,b],c]+\{a,b,c\}+[[b,c],a]+\{b,c,a\}+[[c,a],b]+\{c,a,b\}=0,

(LY4)

\{[a,b],c,d\}+\{[b,c],a,d\}+\{[c,a],b,d\}=0,

(LY5)

\{a,b,[c,d]\}=[\{a,b,c\},d]+[c,\{a,b,d\}],

(LY6)

\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}\}=\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}+\{c,\{a,b,d\},e\}+\{c,d,\{a,b,e\}\},

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in\mathfrak{L}
.

For any two Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{L}
and
\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}
, a linear map
\varphi:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}
is a
Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism
if

\displaystyle\varphi([a,b])=[\varphi(a),\varphi(b)]\quad\mbox{and }\quad\varphi(\{a,b,c\})=\{\varphi(a),\varphi(b),\varphi(c)\},\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{L}.

It is said to be an isomorphism if
\varphi
is invertible. We denote the category of all Lie-Yamaguti algebras over
\mathbb{K}
by
\operatorname{LYA}_{\mathbb{K}}
and the category of all Lie/Leibniz algebras over
\mathbb{K}
by
\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb{K}}
and
\operatorname{Leib}_{\mathbb{K}}
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. Define a binary and ternary bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
by

[a,b]:=[a,b],\quad\{a,b,c\}:=[[a,b],c],\quad\forall a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{r},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a reductive Lie algebra with decomposition
\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, then
\langle\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
and
\langle\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{h}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the projection maps onto
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
respectively. Then
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with the brackets

[a,b]:=\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}\langle a,b\rangle,\quad\{a,b,c\}:=\langle\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\langle a,b\rangle,c\rangle,\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{h}.

Example 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a Leibniz algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with the following binary and ternary brackets

[a,b]=a\cdot b-b\cdot a,\quad\{a,b,c\}=-(a\cdot b)\cdot c,\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
A
be a commutative algebra. We then construct a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{L}\otimes A
(see Example
2.5
). We call it
current Lie-Yamaguti algebra
, following the naming conventions of Lie and Leibniz algebras.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
A
be a commutative algebra. Then there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{L}\otimes A
given by

\left[x\otimes a,y\otimes b\right]_{\mathfrak{L}\otimes A}:=\left[x,y\right]\otimes ab\quad\text{and}\quad\{x\otimes a,y\otimes b,z\otimes c\}_{\mathfrak{L}\otimes A}:=\{x,y,z\}\otimes abc,\quad\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L},~a,b,c\in A.

Further, let
A
and
B
be two commutative algebras. If
f:A\to B
is an algebra morphism, then
id_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes f:\mathfrak{L}\otimes A\to\mathfrak{L}\otimes B
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism. Hence for a fixed Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{L}
assigning
A\mapsto\mathfrak{L}\otimes A
defines a covariant functor
\mathfrak{L}\otimes-:\operatorname{ComAlg}_{\mathbb{K}}\to\operatorname{LYA}_{\mathbb{K}}
. We call this functor the
current Lie-Yamaguti algebra functor
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra module
of
\mathfrak{L}
is a vector space
V
along with a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{L}\to\operatorname{End}(V)
and two bilinear maps
D,\theta:\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\to\operatorname{End}(V)
such that

(R1)

\displaystyle D(a,b)+\theta(a,b)-\theta(b,a)=[\rho(a),\rho(b)]-\rho([a,b]),

(R2)

\displaystyle\theta(a,[b,c])-\rho(b)\theta(a,c)+\rho(c)\theta(a,b)=0,

(R3)

\displaystyle\theta([a,b],c)-\theta(a,c)\rho(b)+\theta(b,c)\rho(a)=0,

(R4)

\displaystyle\theta(c,d)\theta(a,b)-\theta(b,d)\theta(a,c)-\theta(a,\{b,c,d\})+D(b,c)\theta(a,d)=0,

(R5)

\displaystyle[D(a,b),\rho(c)]=\rho(\{a,b,c\}),

(R6)

\displaystyle[D(a,b),\theta(c,d)]=\theta(\{a,b,c\},d)+\theta(c,\{a,b,d\}),

for all
a,b,c,d\in\mathfrak{L}
.

Using the relations (
R1
), (
R2
), (
R3
), and (
R5
) stated above, we get an additional identity

(R7)

D([a,b],c)+D([b,c],a)+D([c,a],b)=0.

Taking
V=\mathfrak{L}
, there is a natural module structure of
\mathfrak{L}
on itself, where the representation maps
\rho,D,\theta
are given by

\rho(a)(b):=[a,b],\quad D(a,b)(c):=\{a,b,c\},\quad\mbox{and }\quad\theta(a,b)(c):=\{c,a,b\},\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{L}.

Bialgebras and Hopf algebras

Before going to bialgebras and Hopf algebras, we first recall the definition of coalgebra and coalgebra modules.

Definition 2.7
.

A
coalgebra
over
\mathbb{K}
is a vector space
C
over
\mathbb{K}
along with linear maps,
\Delta:C\to C\otimes C
(comultiplication map) and
\varepsilon:C\to\mathbb{K}
(counital map) such that the following conditions hold

(1)

(Coassociativity condition)
(id\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta~=~(\Delta\otimes id)\circ\Delta
,

(2)

(Counit)
(id\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta~=~id~=~(\varepsilon\otimes id)\circ\Delta
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
and
(D,\Delta^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})
be two coalgebras over
\mathbb{K}
. A linear map
f:C\to D
is called a
coalgebra morphism
if
f
satisfies the conditions:
\Delta^{\prime}\circ f=(f\otimes f)\circ\Delta
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}\circ f=\varepsilon
.

We denote the category of coalgebras over
\mathbb{K}
by
\operatorname{Cog}_{\mathbb{K}}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
be a coalgebra over
\mathbb{K}
. A
right comodule over
C
is a vector space
M
together with a linear map
f:M\to M\otimes C
such that

(id\otimes\Delta)\circ f=(f\otimes id)\circ f\quad\text{and}\quad(id\otimes\varepsilon)\circ f=id

Duality between algebras and coalgebras:

The notion of algebras and coalgebras are dual in the sense that their axioms are dual to each other (obtained by reversing the arrows). The dual of a coalgebra is an algebra, while in general, the dual of an algebra may not be a coalgebra. Assuming the algebra to be finite-dimensional, its dual becomes a coalgebra. We now briefly sketch the construction of the dual of a coalgebra and discuss the finite dual functor. For a more detailed description, see
[
20
, Sec. 6.0, Pg. 109]
.

Let
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
be a coalgebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Then the dual of
C
, that is
C^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(C,\mathbb{K})
, all linear maps from
C
to
\mathbb{K}
, becomes an algebra with the convolution product: for any
f,g\in\operatorname{Hom}(C,\mathbb{K})
and for any
c\in C
with
\Delta(c)=\sum_{(c)}c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}\in C\otimes C
then

f\star g(c):=\sum_{(c)}f(c_{(1)})g(c_{(2)})

The product
\star
makes
\operatorname{Hom}(C,\mathbb{K})
an algebra, which is called the
algebraic dual of the coalgebra
C
. The unit element in
\operatorname{Hom}(C,\mathbb{K})
is the map
\mathbbm{1}:C\to\mathbb{K}
given by
\mathbbm{1}(c)=1_{\mathbb{K}}
for all
c\in C
. This construction extends to a contravariant functor
()^{*}:\operatorname{Cog}_{\mathbb{K}}\to\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}
, where for any coalgebra morphism
f:C\to D
the algebra morphism
f^{*}:D^{*}\to C^{*}
is the transpose map.

Now, for an algebra
(A,m)
, its vector space dual
A^{*}
is not necessarily a coalgebra; the natural candidate for the comultiplication
\Delta
is the transpose map
m^{*}:A^{*}\to(A\otimes A)^{*}
of the multiplication map
m:A\otimes A\to A
. Nevertheless, note that
(A\otimes A)^{*}
is isomorphic to
A^{*}\otimes A^{*}
as a vector space if and only if
A
is finite-dimensional. Hence, we need the notion of finite dual. The
finite dual functor

()^{\circ}:\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}\to\operatorname{Cog}_{\mathbb{K}}
is the left adjoint functor to
()^{*}:\operatorname{Cog}_{\mathbb{K}}\to\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}
, defined by

A^{\circ}:=\{f\in A^{*}:\operatorname{Ker}(f)~\text{contains some ideal}~I~\text{of}~A~\text{with}~\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}(A/I)<\infty\}\quad\text{for any}~A\in\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}.

\mathbb{K}

Free Hopf algebra
H(C)
generated by a coalgebra
C

[
21
, pg. 562]

For any vector space
V
, the tensor algebra
T(V)
has a natural bialgebra structure. Let
C
be a coalgebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Let
(V_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a sequence of coalgebras as follows

V_{0}=C,\quad V_{i+1}=V_{i}^{op}.

So the sequence is of the form
(C,C^{op},C,C^{op},\ldots)
. Let
V:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}V_{i}
be the direct sum of coalgebras, again a coalgebra. Let
S:V\to V^{op}
be the coalgebra map
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\mapsto(0,x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
.
S
induces a bialgebra map
T(V)\to T(V)^{op}
. Let
I
be the two sided ideal of
T(V)
generated by the elements
\sum x_{(1)}S(x_{(2)})-\varepsilon(x)1
and
\sum S(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}-\varepsilon(x)1
, for

. Thus,

is a bialgebra and

is an antipode of

a Hopf algebra.

Definition 2.12
.

H(C)
constructed above is said to be
the free Hopf algebra generated by a coalgebra

.

The universal coacting Hopf algebra

In this section, we construct a universal coacting Hopf algebra of a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra following the approach of

Let

are called the

.

The universal algebra

satisfies the following universal property.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{L}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{L}\otimes A}}

.

Example 3.11
.

A Heisenberg Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a vector space
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
generated by
2n+1
elements (
n\geq 1
)

\{e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{n+1},\ldots,e_{2n}\}.

The Lie-Yamaguti brackets on
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
are given by

[e_{i},e_{n+i}]=e_{0},\quad\{e_{i},e_{n+i},e_{i}\}=e_{0},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

All other brackets on the basis elements are either zero or are determined by the relations of Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Therefore, the structure constants of
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
, which are
\{\tau_{ij}^{s}:s,i,j=0,1,\ldots,2n\}
and
\{\omega_{ijk}^{s}:s,i,j,k=0,1,\ldots,2n\}
, can be described as follows:

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hskip 7.11317pt1\qquad(s=0,~1\leq i\leq n,~j=n+i);\\
-1\qquad(s=0,~n+1\leq i\leq 2n,~j=i-n);\\
\hskip 7.11317pt0~\qquad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\omega_{ijk}^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hskip 7.11317pt1\qquad(s=0,~1\leq i\leq n,~j=n+i,~k=i);\\
-1\qquad(s=0,~n+1\leq i\leq 2n,~j=i-n,~k=i);\\
\hskip 7.11317pt0~\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Hence, the universal polynomials of
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
of the form
P_{(a,i,j)}^{(\mathfrak{H}_{n})}
for
a=1,2,\ldots,2n
are given by:

P_{(a,i,j)}^{(\mathfrak{H}_{n})}=\begin{cases}\hskip 8.53581ptX_{a0}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n,~j=n+i);\\
-X_{a0}\qquad(n+1\leq i\leq 2n,~j=i-n);\\
\hskip 14.22636pt0\hskip 7.11317pt\quad\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

and for any
a=0
it is given by:

P_{(a,i,j)}^{(\mathfrak{H}_{n})}=\begin{cases}\hskip 8.53581ptX_{00}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}X_{si}X_{(n+s)j}+\sum_{s=n+1}^{2n}X_{si}X_{(n-s)j}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n,~j=n+i);\\
-X_{00}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}X_{si}X_{(n+s)j}+\sum_{s=n+1}^{2n}X_{si}X_{(n-s)j}\qquad(n+1\leq i\leq 2n,~j=i-n);\\
-\sum_{s=1}^{n}X_{si}X_{(n+s)j}+\sum_{s=n+1}^{2n}X_{si}X_{(n-s)j}\hskip 7.96677pt\qquad\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Furthermore, the universal polynomials of
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
of the form
Q_{(a,i,j,k)}^{(\mathfrak{H}_{n})}
for
a=1,2,\ldots,2n
are given by:

Q_{(a,i,j,k)}^{(\mathfrak{H}_{n})}=\begin{cases}\hskip 8.53581ptX_{a0}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n,~j=n+i,~k=i);\\
-X_{a0}\qquad(n+1\leq i\leq 2n,~j=i-n,~k=i);\\
\hskip 14.22636pt0\hskip 7.11317pt\quad\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

and for any
a=0
it is given by:

Q_{(a,i,j,k)}^{(\mathfrak{H}_{n})}=\begin{cases}\hskip 8.53581ptX_{00}-\sum_{r=1}^{n}X_{ri}X_{(n+r)j}X_{rk}+\sum_{r=n+1}^{2n}X_{ri}X_{(n-r)j}X_{rk}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n,~j=n+i,~k=i);\\
-X_{00}-\sum_{r=1}^{n}X_{ri}X_{(n+r)j}X_{rk}+\sum_{r=n+1}^{2n}X_{ri}X_{(n-r)j}X_{rk}\qquad(n+1\leq i\leq 2n,~j=i-n,~k=i);\\
-\sum_{r=1}^{n}X_{ri}X_{(n+r)j}X_{rk}+\sum_{r=n+1}^{2n}X_{ri}X_{(n-r)j}X_{rk}\hskip 18.49428pt\qquad\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore, the universal algebra of
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
is given by

\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{H}_{n})\cong\mathbb{K}[X_{ij}:0\leq i,j\leq 2n]\big/J

where
J
is the ideal generated by the universal polynomials of
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
described above.

\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})

The universal algebra

of a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra

.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra of dimension

. Then there exists a unique bialgebra structure on

\Delta(x_{ij})=\sum_{s=1}^{n}x_{is}\otimes x_{sj}~~\text{and}~~\varepsilon(x_{ij})=\delta_{ij},\quad\forall~~i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Proof.

Note that

. It follows from Corollary
3.10
that there exists a unique algebra morphism

such that

. In other words, the following diagram

(22)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{L}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\otimes\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}}

commutes. Evaluating the above diagram at each

\sum_{t=1}^{n}e_{t}\otimes\Delta(x_{ti})~=~(\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})})\left(\sum_{s=1}^{n}e_{s}\otimes x_{si}\right)~=~\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}e_{t}\otimes x_{ts}\right)\otimes x_{si}~=~\sum_{t=1}^{n}e_{t}\otimes\left(\sum_{s=1}^{n}x_{ts}\otimes x_{si}\right).

4.

This section gives a representation-theoretic version of Manin-Tambara’s constructions for Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We show that these universal constructions are functorial. First, we fix some notations for this section.

for

Theorem 4.1

Let
(U,\rho,D,\theta)

(33)

\displaystyle D_{U\otimes W}(f_{p},f_{q})(u\otimes w)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}D(e_{i},e_{j})(u)\otimes(x_{ip}x_{jq})\cdot w,\qquad\forall~p,q\in I,

(34)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta(e_{i},e_{j})(u)\otimes(x_{ip}x_{jq})\cdot w,\qquad\forall p,q\in I.

Using the fact

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{EQN_sec4_rep_map1})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{n}\rho(e_{s})(u)\otimes\Big(\sum_{r\in B_{pq}}\alpha_{pq}^{r}~x_{sr}\Big)\cdot w

The following proposition shows that the above construction is functorial.

. Let us observe that

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{EQN_sec4_rep_map1})}}{{=}}~\sum_{s=1}^{n}\rho_{U}(e_{s})(u)\otimes g(x_{si}\cdot w)~=~\sum_{s=1}^{n}\rho_{U}(e_{s})(u)\otimes x_{si}\cdot g(w)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\otimes\mathcal{U}(U,V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\otimes\mathcal{U}(U,V^{\prime})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\mathcal{U}(U,V)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\mathcal{U}(U,V^{\prime})}\circ f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id_{U}\otimes\bar{f}}

commutative. Furthermore, the functors

U\otimes-:\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathfrak{K}^{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{U}:\mathfrak{K}^{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{M}}
are adjoint to each other.

Proof.

If
f=id_{V}
, then
id_{\mathcal{U}(U,V)}
is the unique
\mathcal{A}
-module morphism for which the diagram (
53
) commutes and therefore
\mathcal{U}(id_{V})=id_{\mathcal{U}(U,V)}
. If
f:V\to V^{\prime}
and
g:V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime\prime}
are two morphisms in
\mathfrak{K}^{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\bar{g}\circ\bar{f}:\mathcal{U}(U,V)\to\mathcal{U}(U,V^{\prime\prime})
is the unique
\mathcal{A}
-module morphism such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\otimes\mathcal{U}(U,V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\otimes\mathcal{U}(U,V^{\prime\prime})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\mathcal{U}(U,V)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id_{U}\otimes(\bar{g}\circ\bar{f})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\mathcal{U}(U,V^{\prime\prime})}\circ g\circ f}

commutes, implying
\mathcal{U}_{U}(g\circ f)=\mathcal{U}_{U}(g)\circ\mathcal{U}_{U}(f)
.

From the Remark
4.5
, it follows that the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{U}(V),W)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{U}(V^{\prime}),W)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{M}}}(\mathcal{U}(V^{\prime}),W^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{K}^{\mathcal{M}}}(V,U\otimes W)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{K}^{\mathcal{M}}}(V^{\prime},U\otimes W)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{K}^{\mathcal{M}}}(V^{\prime},U\otimes W^{\prime})}}

commutative, giving naturality in both variables. Therefore,
U\otimes-
and
\mathcal{U}
are adjoint functors.
∎

5.
Applications

In this section, we give some applications of our results. In particular, Theorem
5.1
describes the automorphism group of a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. For an abelian group
G
, Theorem
5.5
classifies all
G
-gradings on a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

A characterization of the automorphism group of a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra

We recall some basic facts about bialgebras and Hopf algebras, which we use in this section.

•

For any bialgebra
B
, we denote the set of all group-like elements by

G(B):=\{g\in B:\Delta(g)=g\otimes g~\text{and}~\varepsilon(g)=1\}.

G(B)
is a monoid with the multiplication of
B
.

•

Let
B^{\circ}
be the finite dual bialgebra of
B
, i.e.,

B^{\circ}=\{f\in B^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(B,\mathbb{K}):f(I)=0,\text{for some ideal}~I~\mbox{of}~B~\text{with}~dim_{\mathbb{K}}(B/I)<\infty\}.

Here,
\operatorname{Hom}(B,\mathbb{K})
denotes the vector space of all linear maps from
B
to
\mathbb{K}
. One can easily show that

G(B^{\circ})=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(B,\mathbb{K}),

where
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(B,\mathbb{K})

is the set of all algebra morphism
B\to\mathbb{K}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and consider
G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})^{inv}
to be the group of all invertible group-like elements of the finite dual
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ}
. Then the map
\zeta
defined by

(54)

{\zeta}:G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})^{inv}\to\operatorname{Aut_{LYA}}(\mathfrak{L}),\quad{\zeta}(\theta)(e_{i}):=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\theta(x_{si})e_{s},

for any
\theta\in G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})^{inv}
and
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, is an isomorphism of groups.

Proof.

Using Corollary
3.3
with
\mathfrak{K}=\mathfrak{L}
and
A=\mathbb{K}
, we have a bijection
\Psi
defined by

\Psi:\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L}),\mathbb{K})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{LYA}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}),\quad\Psi(\theta)=(id_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes\theta)\circ\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}.

Note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L}),\mathbb{K})=G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})
. Now, we show that
\Psi
is an isomorphism of monoids.
Recall that the monoid structure on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{LYA}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L})
is given by the usual function composition, and on
G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})
is given by the convolution product

(55)

\theta_{1}\star\theta_{2}(x_{sj})=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\theta_{1}(x_{st})\theta_{2}(x_{tj}),

for all
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})
and
j,s=1,2,\ldots,n
. For any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})
and
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(\theta_{1})\circ\Psi(\theta_{2})(e_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(\theta_{1})\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}\theta_{2}(x_{tj})e_{t}\right)~=~\sum_{s,t=1}^{n}\theta_{1}(x_{st})\theta_{2}(x_{tj})e_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}\theta_{1}(x_{st})\theta_{2}(x_{tj})\right)e_{s}~=~\sum_{s=1}^{n}(\theta_{1}\star\theta_{2})(x_{sj})e_{s}~=~\Psi(\theta_{1}\star\theta_{2})(e_{j}).

Thus, giving us
\Psi(\theta_{1}\star\theta_{2})=\Psi(\theta_{1})\circ\Psi(\theta_{2})
. We also need to show that
\Psi
preserves the unit of
G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})
. Since the unit
1
of the monoid
G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})
is the counit
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})}
of the bialgebra
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})
, we have

\Psi(1)(e_{i})~=~\Psi(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})})(e_{i})~=~\sum_{s=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})}(x_{si})e_{s}~=~\sum_{s=1}^{n}\delta_{si}(e_{s})~=~e_{i}~=~id_{\mathfrak{L}}(e_{i}).

Thus,
\Psi
is an isomorphism of monoid. Hence,
\Psi
induces an isomorphism
\zeta
between invertible elements of the monoids given by

{\zeta}:G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})^{inv}\to\operatorname{Aut_{LYA}}(\mathfrak{L}),\quad{\zeta}(\theta)(e_{i}):=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\theta(x_{si})e_{s},\quad\mbox{for any }\theta\in G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})^{inv}\mbox{ and }i=1,2,\ldots,n.

∎

Classification of the abelian group gradings on a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra

Let
G
be an abelian group and
\mathfrak{L}
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
G
-grading on
\mathcal{L}
is a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{L}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathfrak{L}_{g}
such that

[\mathfrak{L}_{a},\mathfrak{L}_{b}]\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{ab},~~\forall~a,b\in G\quad\text{and}\quad\{\mathfrak{L}_{a},\mathfrak{L}_{b},\mathfrak{L}_{c}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{abc},~~\forall~a,b,c\in G.

Let
\mathbb{K}[G]
denote the group algebra of
G
. In the following lemma, we discuss the relationship between
G
-gradings on
\mathfrak{L}
and bialgebra maps
\theta:\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\to\mathbb{K}[G]
.

Remark 5.2
.

For a bialgebra map
\theta:\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\to\mathbb{K}[G]
, we can associate a
G
-grading on
\mathfrak{L}
as follows:

\mathfrak{L}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathfrak{L}_{g}^{(\theta)},\quad\mathfrak{L}_{g}^{(\theta)}~:=~\{x\in\mathfrak{L}:(id_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes\theta)\circ\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}(x)~=~x\otimes g\},\quad\forall~g\in G.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and
\mathfrak{L}
be a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Then there exists a bijection between the set of all
G
-gradings on
\mathfrak{L}
, and the set of all bialgebra maps
\theta:\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\to\mathbb{K}[G]
.

Proof.

By applying Theorem
3.14
for the commutative bialgebra
B=\mathbb{K}[G]
, we get a bijection between the set of all bialgebra morphisms
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\to\mathbb{K}[G]
and the set of all Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism
f:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathbb{K}[G]
that make
\mathfrak{L}
a right
\mathbb{K}[G]
-comodule.
There exists a bijection between the set of all right
\mathbb{K}[G]
-comodule structures
f:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathbb{K}[G]
on the vector space
\mathfrak{L}
and the set of all vector space decompositions
\mathfrak{L}=\oplus_{a\in G}\mathfrak{L}_{a}
. In particular, the bijection is given by

x_{a}\in\mathfrak{L}_{a}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad f(x_{a})=x_{a}\otimes a,\forall~a\in G.

Therefore, it only remains to show that under this bijection, a right coaction
f:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathbb{K}[G]
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism if and only if
[\mathfrak{L}_{a},\mathfrak{L}_{b}]\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{ab}
and
\{\mathfrak{L}_{a},\mathfrak{L}_{b},\mathfrak{L}_{c}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{abc}
for all
a,b,c\in G
. We show this below.

Let
a,b\in G
and
x_{a}\in\mathfrak{L}_{a},~x_{b}\in\mathfrak{L}_{b}
: then
[f(x_{a}),f(x_{b})]=[x_{a}\otimes a,x_{b}\otimes b]=[x_{a},x_{b}]\otimes ab
. Thus, we obtain
f([x_{a},x_{b}])=[f(x_{a}),f(x_{b})]
if and only if
[x_{a},x_{b}]\in\mathfrak{L}_{ab}
. Similarly, we have
f\{x_{a},x_{b},x_{c}\}=\{f(x_{a}),f(x_{b}),f(x_{c})\}
if and only if
\{x_{a},x_{b},x_{c}\}\in\mathfrak{L}_{abc}
. Hence,
f:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathbb{K}[G]
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism if and only if
[\mathfrak{L}_{a},\mathfrak{L}_{b}]\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{ab}
and
\{\mathfrak{L}_{a},\mathfrak{L}_{b},\mathfrak{L}_{c}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{abc}
for all
a,b,c\in G
. Hence, completing the proof.
∎

We now classify all
G
-gradings on a given Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{L}
, where
G
is an abelian group. Two
G
-gradings
\mathfrak{L}=\oplus_{a\in G}\mathfrak{L}_{a}
and
\mathfrak{L}=\oplus_{a\in G}\mathfrak{L^{\prime}}_{a}
on
\mathfrak{L}
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an automorphism
\omega\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{LYA}}(\mathfrak{L})
of
\mathfrak{L}
such that
\omega(\mathfrak{L}_{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}_{a}
for all
a\in G
. Since
\omega
is bijective and
\mathfrak{L}
is
G
-graded, we can show that the last condition is equivalent to
\omega(\mathfrak{L}_{a})=\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}_{a}
for all
a\in G
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and
\mathfrak{L}
be a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Two bialgebra morphisms
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}:\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\to\mathbb{K}[G]
are called
conjugate
if there exists
g\in G(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ})^{inv}
an invertible group like element of the finite dual
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})^{\circ}
such that
\theta_{2}=g\star\theta_{1}\star g^{-1}
, in the convolution algebra
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L}),\mathbb{K}[G])
.

Let
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{BiAlg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathcal{A}(L),\mathbb{K}[G])/\thickapprox
denote the quotient of the set of all bialgebra morphisms
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L})\to\mathbb{K}[G]
by the above conjugacy relation and let
\widehat{\theta}
denote the equivalence class of
\theta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{BiAlg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L}),\mathbb{K}[G])
. Then, using a similar argument to the case of Lie and Leibniz algebras
[
2
, Theorem 3.5]
, one can conclude the following result.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G
be an abelian group, and
\mathfrak{L}
be a finite-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Let
\mathbf{G-gradings(\mathfrak{L})}
be the set of isomorphism classes of all
G
-gradings on
\mathfrak{L}
. Then the map

(56)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{BiAlg}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{L}),\mathbb{K}[G])/\thickapprox~\longrightarrow~~\mathbf{G-gradings(\mathfrak{L})},\quad\widehat{\theta}\mapsto\mathfrak{L}^{(\theta)}:=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathfrak{L}_{g}^{(\theta)}

where
\mathfrak{L}_{g}^{\theta}=\{x\in\mathfrak{L}:(id_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes\theta)\circ\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}=x\otimes g\}
, for all
g\in G
, is bijective. ∎

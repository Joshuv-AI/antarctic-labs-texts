Near-optimal delta-convex estimation of Lipschitz functions
Gábor Balázs gabalz@gandg.ai

Abstract
This paper presents a tractable algorithm for estimating an unknown Lipschitz function from noisy observations and establishes an upper bound on its convergence rate. The approach extends max-affine methods from convex shape-restricted regression to the more general Lipschitz setting. A key component is a nonlinear feature expansion that maps max-affine functions into a subclass of delta-convex functions, which act as universal approximators of Lipschitz functions while preserving their Lipschitz constants. Leveraging this property, the estimator attains the minimax convergence rate (up to logarithmic factors) with respect to the intrinsic dimension of the data under squared loss and subgaussian distributions in the random design setting. The algorithm integrates adaptive partitioning to capture intrinsic dimension, a penalty-based regularization mechanism that removes the need to know the true Lipschitz constant, and a two-stage optimization procedure combining a convex initialization with local refinement. The framework is also straightforward to adapt to convex shape-restricted regression. Experiments demonstrate competitive performance relative to other theoretically justified methods, including nearest-neighbor and kernel-based regressors.

Introduction

This paper considers the fundamental problem of estimating an unknown regression function from noisy observations in the random design setting.
Suppose we observe
n
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples,
{\mathcal{D}_{n}}\doteq\langle({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i},Y_{i}):i\in[n]\rangle
, for an unknown real-valued regression function
f_{*}:\mathcal{X}_{*}\to\mathbb{R}
over some unknown domain
\mathcal{X}_{*}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{*}\textrm{ almost surely (a.s.)},\quad\qquad Y_{i}\doteq f_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})+\xi_{i}.

(1)

The noise
\xi_{i}
is centered, satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}|{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}]=0
a.s. for all
i\in[n]
, where
[m]\doteq\{1,\ldots,m\}
for any positive integer
m
.
We assume that the regression function
f_{*}
is
\lambda_{*}
-Lipschitz over
\mathcal{X}_{*}
with respect to (w.r.t.) the Euclidean norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert\doteq\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}
for some unknown Lipschitz constant
\lambda_{*}\in(0,\infty)
.
We evaluate the estimators using the excess risk under squared loss, for which the minimax (convergence) rate is known to be
O\big(n^{-2/(2+d_{*})}\big)
in terms of the sample size
n
and the intrinsic data dimension
d_{*}

(
Stone 1982
)
.
Throughout the paper, we use the standard asymptotic order of growth notations:
\Omega(\cdot)
,
\Theta(\cdot)
, and
O(\cdot)
.

In convex (shape-restricted) regression
(
Lim 2014
;
Han and Wellner 2016
;
Balázs 2016
;
Kur et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
the regression function
f_{*}
is known to be convex over a convex domain
\mathcal{X}_{*}
, and the goal is to estimate
f_{*}
by a convex function.
In this setting, it is common to choose the estimator from the class of max-affine functions (functions defined as the maximum of finitely many affine functions) because they approximate any convex function at worst-case optimal rate
(
Balázs et al. 2015
)
.
Moreover, empirical risk minimization over max-affine functions can be reformulated as a tractable convex optimization problem (solvable in polynomial-time w.r.t.
d
and
n
;
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
, Section 6.5.5
).
Although several extensions of max-affine functions were proposed
(
Bagirov et al. 2010
;
Sun and Yu 2019
;
Siahkamari et al. 2020
)
, none have been shown to achieve the minimax rate up to logarithmic factors (i.e., near-minimax rate) in the general Lipschitz setting of (
1
).
In this paper, we fill this gap by using the following extension of max-affine functions:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\doteq\Big\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,\big|\,&f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}b_{f,k}+{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k})+v_{f,k}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}\rVert_{\triangleright},\\
&{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R},\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,v_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in[k_{0}]\Big\},\end{split}

(2)

where
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\doteq\{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k_{0}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a nonempty, finite set of size
k_{0}\doteq|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|
, and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The key observation, based on the function representation in the extension theorem of
McShane 1934
, is that when
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
is chosen to be an
\epsilon
-cover of the covariate data
\mathcal{X}_{n}\doteq\{{\boldsymbol{X}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{X}}_{n}\}
, there exists a function within
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
that is uniformly
O(\epsilon)
-close to the Lipschitz regression function
f_{*}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
(see
Theorem

2
).
We use this fact to bound the approximation error of our estimators to
f_{*}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, where the estimators “approximately” minimize the empirical risk over the training data
{\mathcal{D}_{n}}
within
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
.
A tradeoff arises in selecting the size of the cover
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
: increasing
|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|
improves approximation accuracy (i.e., smaller
\epsilon
) but results in a more complex representation (i.e., more parameters), and vice versa.
To balance this tradeoff, we construct
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
using the adaptive farthest-point clustering algorithm of
Balázs 2022
, which achieves
|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|\approx n^{d_{*}/(2+d_{*})}
and
\epsilon\approx n^{-1/(2+d_{*})}
.
Our main result, stated in
Theorem

1
, shows that using this choice of
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
together with the class
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
yields estimators that achieve a near-minimax rate under the statistical model (
1
) for subgaussian distributions.

Allowing the choice of norm via the parameter
\triangleright
provides flexibility in the design of the estimator.
The most natural choice is the rotation invariant Euclidean norm, corresponding to
\triangleright=2
.
However, by choosing the max-norm with
\triangleright=\infty
, the elements of the set
\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}):v_{f,k}\leq 0,k\in[k_{0}]\}
are max-min-affine functions (functions defined as the maximum of minima of finitely many affine functions), as discussed in
Section

5.1
.
Since
Ovchinnikov 2002
showed that max-min-affine functions can represent any continuous piecewise-linear function, there have been various developments of max-min-affine estimators
(
Bagirov et al. 2010
;
Toriello and Vielma 2012
;
Bagirov et al. 2022
)
.
However, to the best of our knowledge, none of these come with theoretical guarantees.
In this paper, we also address this gap by proposing a tractable max-min-affine estimator based on
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, which is proven to achieve a near-minimax rate under the nonparametric setting of (
1
) for subgaussian distributions, as shown in
Theorem

1
.
In
Section

2
, we further discuss an extension to
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, based on the Manhattan norm (i.e.,
\triangleright=1
), which can be computed by maxout neural networks
(
Goodfellow et al. 2013
)
.

Our algorithm regularizes the uniform Lipschitz constant of the estimator and guarantees a near-minimax rate in the general Lipschitz setting of (
1
), without requiring knowledge of the Lipschitz constant
\lambda_{*}
of the regression function
f_{*}
.
In
Section

6.2
, we further show how our method can be easily adapted to the convex regression setting.
Although
Blanchet et al. 2019
proposed a similar uniform Lipschitz regularization for convex regression and proved a convergence rate in probability, their result only holds for
d>4
and for sufficiently large
n
satisfying
\ln(n)\geq\lambda_{*}
.

Lim 2025
extended this result to
d\leq 4
, but it still only provides convergence rate in probability as
n\to\infty
.
In contrast, we establish a probably approximately correct (PAC) bound that holds for all
n
.
To the best of our knowledge, this adapted variant of our estimator is the first tractable method for convex regression to enjoy a PAC guarantee in the random design setting (albeit not necessarily near-minimax) without requiring knowledge of
\lambda_{*}
or a uniform bound on
f_{*}
.

The result of
Hiriart-Urruty 1985
shows that the class
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
lies within the class of delta-convex (DC) functions, whose elements can be expressed as the difference of two convex functions
(
Hartman 1959
)
.
The classes of max-min-affine, weakly max-affine, and delta-max-affine functions are also contained within the class of DC functions, and all of them have been studied for estimator design
(
Bagirov et al. 2010
;
Sun and Yu 2019
;
Siahkamari et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.
However, none of these approaches has achieved a near-minimax rate guarantee in the setting of (
1
).
In
Sections

5.1
and
5.3
, we provide approximation results for all of these classes, which may be of independent interest.
To achieve the near-minimax rate in
Theorem

1
, we work with
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
for two reasons.
First, empirical risk minimization (ERM) over
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
“leads” to a tractable convex optimization problem, as discussed in
Section

4
.
In contrast, we are not aware of any tractable “approximation” of the ERM problem over the entire class of max-min-affine functions.
Second, for the worst-case optimal approximation
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
to a
\lambda_{*}
-Lipschitz regression function
f_{*}
, the parameter magnitudes
\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}\max\{\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}\rVert,|v_{f,k}|\}
are provably bounded above by
O(\lambda_{*})
, as shown in
Theorem

2
.
Importantly, this upper bound does not depend on the approximation accuracy.
Since we cannot establish a similar bound for weakly max-affine and delta-max-affine functions, our proof technique does not apply to those cases in general (i.e., without further assuming smoothness of
f_{*}
).

Finally, we note that several other methods have been proven to achieve a near-minimax rate in the regression setting of (
1
) with respect to the intrinsic data dimension.
Specifically, the nearest-neighbor estimator
(
Kulkarni and Posner 1995
, Corollary 3)
, certain tree-based predictors
(
Kpotufe and Dasgupta 2011
, Theorem 9)
, and the Nadaraya-Watson regressor
(
Kpotufe 2010
, Theorem 21)
.
We compare these methods to our algorithm through experiments in
Section

3
.

2
The proposed algorithm

Define the feature vector
\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\doteq\big[({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}\,\,\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}\big]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
for all
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
and
{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, for any nonempty, finite set
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\doteq\{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k_{0}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can rewrite (
2
) in the compact form:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})=\big\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,\big|\,&f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}b_{f,k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}^{\top}\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}),\\
&{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R},\,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\triangleright}},\,k\in[k_{0}]\big\}\end{split}

where
d_{\triangleright}\doteq d+1
.
This formulation allows us to overload the notation and introduce the set
\mathcal{F}_{+}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
by defining
\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\doteq\big[({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})_{+}^{\top}\,\,(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{x}})_{+}^{\top}\big]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, and
d_{+}\doteq 2d
, where the ReLU operation
({\boldsymbol{z}})_{+}\doteq\max\{{\boldsymbol{0}}_{d},{\boldsymbol{z}}\}
is applied elementwise to any vector
{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
{\boldsymbol{0}}_{d}
denoting the zero vector of size
d
.

The functions of
\mathcal{F}_{+}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
can be computed by the so-called maxout neural networks
(
Goodfellow et al. 2013
)
.
Further,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{+}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
holds because
{\boldsymbol{1}}_{2d}^{\top}\,\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{1}
and
[{\boldsymbol{1}}_{d}^{\top}\,-{\boldsymbol{1}}_{d}^{\top}]\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})={\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
{\boldsymbol{1}}_{s}
denotes the all-ones vector of size
s
.
In the paper, we consider
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
for all
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty,+\}
, and discuss their approximation guarantees for Lipschitz functions in
Section

4.1
.

Let
\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}),\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{K}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\}
denote the Voronoi partition of the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by the the center points
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\doteq\{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k_{0}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
That is, for each
k\in[k_{0}]
, define
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\doteq\big\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}\rVert=\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert\big\}
, with ties broken arbitrarily but consistently (e.g., by selecting the center with the smaller index).

2.1
Delta-convex fitting (DCF)

Suppose we are given the training data
{\mathcal{D}_{n}}
from (
1
).
First, we use the adaptive farthest-point clustering (AFPC; see
Algorithm

1
below) method of
Balázs 2022
to compute a finite set of distinct center points
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}\doteq\{{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{K}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{n}
for some
K\in[n]
.
Then, the core of our algorithm is the following convex optimization problem:

\begin{split}&\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{R},\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{K}\in\mathbb{R},\\
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\triangleright}}\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.53581pt\theta_{1}z^{2}+\hskip-5.69054pt\sum_{k\in[K]}\hskip-2.84526pt\theta_{2}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{I}\big\{{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\big\}\Big(b_{k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}^{\top}\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})-Y_{i}\Big)^{2}\\
&\qquad\textrm{such that }b_{k}\geq b_{l}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{l}^{\top}\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{l}),\quad\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}\rVert\leq z+\theta_{0},\quad k,l\in[K].\end{split}

(3)

where
\mathbb{I}\{\cdot\}
is the
\{0,1\}
-valued indicator function, and
\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\geq 0
are fixed regularization parameters.
The role of
\theta_{0}
is to mitigate the effect of conservative regularization on the uniform Lipschitz constant of the estimator.

Let
(z_{n},\langle(b_{n,k},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}):k\in[K]\rangle)
be a solution to (
3
), and define the (initial) estimator as
f_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{k\in[K]}b_{n,k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}^{\top}\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Clearly,
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.

The minimization problem in (
3
) is similar to the APCNLS algorithm of
Balázs 2022
, which is designed for training max-affine estimators.
Like APCNLS, our approach trains a partitioning estimator and maps it into a target function class,
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
in our case, instead of the class of max-affine functions.
A key distinction, however, is that we impose the constraints only at the center points, resulting in just
K^{2}
constraints, which is considerably fewer than the
nK
constraints used in APCNLS.
As shown in
Section

4.2
, despite this reduced constraint set, our algorithm preserves theoretical properties analogous to those of APCNLS.

Denote the empirical risk of any function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)\doteq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}\big(f({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-Y_{i}\big)^{2}
.
Additionally, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
and
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
, define the regularization term

\mathcal{R}_{c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}}(f)\doteq c_{1}\max_{k\in[K]}\big(\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}\rVert-c_{0}\big)_{+}^{2}+c_{2}\hskip-2.84526pt\sum_{k\in[K]}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}\rVert^{2},

and the largest slope parameter magnitude by
\lambda_{f}\doteq\max_{k\in[K]}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}\rVert
.
We call any estimator
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
to be a refinement of
f_{n}
that satisfies

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})+\mathcal{R}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{n}(f_{n}),\qquad\mathcal{R}_{n}(\cdot)\doteq\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{3}\lambda_{f_{n}},\theta_{f_{n}},\theta_{2}}(\cdot),\end{split}

(4)

where
\theta_{f_{n}}\doteq\lambda_{f_{n}}^{-2}\big(\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{0,0,\theta_{2}}(f_{n})\big)
if
\lambda_{f_{n}}>0
, and
\theta_{3}\geq 1
is a fixed regularization parameter.
Note that
\lambda_{f_{n}}=\max_{k\in[K]}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}\rVert
by definition.
In the degenerate case, when
\lambda_{f_{n}}=0
, we set
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}=f_{n}
and
\theta_{f_{n}}=0
.

Clearly, the choice
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}=f_{n}
always satisfies (
4
).
However, based on the experimental results in
Section

3
, we strongly recommend choosing
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
as an approximate local solution to the non-convex optimization problem
\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})}\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)+\mathcal{R}_{n}(f)
, initialized at
f_{n}
.
Our near-minimax convergence rate guarantee in
Theorem

1
holds in both cases.

To define the final estimator, we prune all parameters of
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
which do not affect its empirical risk, and center its average response on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
.
Formally, we define the final estimator for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\begin{split}f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{x}})&\doteq C_{n}^{+}+\max_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}}b_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+},k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+},k}^{\top}\phi({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}),\qquad\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}\doteq\mathcal{I}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+}),\\
\mathcal{I}_{n}(f)&\doteq\big\{k\in[K]:f({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})=b_{f,k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}^{\top}\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})\textrm{ for some }i\in[n]\big\},\end{split}

(5)

where the index set
\mathcal{I}_{n}(\cdot)
is defined for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
, and the centering constant
C_{n}^{+}
is given as
C_{n}^{+}\doteq\bar{Y}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{f}_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})
with
\bar{Y}\doteq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
.
We also set
b_{f_{n}^{+},k}\doteq b_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+},k}+C_{n}^{+}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f_{n}^{+},k}\doteq{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+},k}
for all
k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}
.
Besides potentially reducing inference-time computational costs, this step also facilitates bounding the magnitude of the unregularized bias parameters
\{b_{f_{n}^{+},k}:k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}\}
, which is required for applying the concentration inequality used to prove the near-minimax rate of the estimator.
We also extend
\lambda_{f}
and
\mathcal{R}_{n}(f)
to
f=f_{n}^{+}
by replacing
[K]
with
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}
in their definitions.

In
Section

4
, we show that the initial solution
f_{n}
already approximates
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{*})+\theta_{1}\lambda_{*}^{2}
with accuracy
O(K/n)
, which is upper bounded by the near-minimax rate.
For the constant function,
f_{c}^{\textrm{const}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq c
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the empirical risk
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{c}^{\textrm{const}}+\hat{f}_{n}^{+})=c^{2}-2cC_{n}^{+}+\mathcal{L}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})
is minimized at
c=C_{n}^{+}
with minimum value
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})
, hence the centering in (
5
) ensures
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})
.
Moreover, we have
\mathcal{R}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})\leq\mathcal{R}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})
since the slope parameters of
f_{n}^{+}
are a subset of those of
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
.
Therefore, the estimator
f_{n}^{+}
is also a refinement of
f_{n}
that satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})+\mathcal{R}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})+\mathcal{R}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{n}(f_{n}).

(6)

We rely on (
6
) to show that
f_{n}^{+}
inherits the near-minimax rate guarantee of
f_{n}
.
To this end, we also use the regularizer
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\cdot)
in (
4
), which limits the largest slope magnitude of
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
(and hence of
f_{n}^{+}
) from exceeding that of
f_{n}
by more than a constant factor of
\theta_{3}
. That is,
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}\leq\lambda_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+}}=O\big(\theta_{3}\lambda_{f_{n}}\big)
, as explained in
Lemma

5
.

We call our algorithm delta-convex fitting (DCF) and summarize it in
Algorithm

2
.
For completeness,
Algorithm

1
also presents the AFPC method of
Balázs 2022
.
To describe it, let the covariate data radius be defined as
R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\doteq\max_{i\in[n]}\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\bar{X}}}\rVert
with
{\boldsymbol{\bar{X}}}\doteq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}
.
Define the clustering objective as
\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\doteq\max_{{\boldsymbol{X}}\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\min_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}\rVert
, and the partition size limit as
\hat{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\doteq\min\big\{n(\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})/R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}})^{2},n^{d/(2+d)}\big\}
for any set of center points
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{n}
.

Algorithm 1

\hat{\mathcal{X}}\doteq\textbf{AFPC}(\mathcal{X}_{n})

1:

\hat{\mathcal{X}}\leftarrow\{{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}\}
with arbitrary
{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{n}

2:

while

|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|<\hat{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})

do

3:

4.1
Approximation of Lipschitz functions

For a metric space
(\mathcal{Z},\psi)

\epsilon>0
, and some
\mathcal{Z}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
, the finite set
\{{\boldsymbol{z}}_{k}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}:k\in[K]\}
is called an (internal)
\epsilon
-cover
of
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
w.r.t. the metric
\psi
if the union of the
\epsilon
-balls centered at
{\boldsymbol{z}}_{k}
covers
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
, that is
\mathcal{Z}_{0}\subseteq\cup_{k\in[K]}\{{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}:\psi({\boldsymbol{z}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{z}})\leq\epsilon\}
.
The cardinality of the smallest such cover is called the
\epsilon
-covering number
of
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
w.r.t.
\psi
and denoted by
N_{\psi}(\mathcal{Z}_{0},\epsilon)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
denote the class of
\lambda
-Lipschitz functions on a set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
, defined as

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}\doteq\Big\{f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}\,\Big|\,\sup_{{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X},{\boldsymbol{x}}\neq\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{-1}\big(f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\big)\leq\lambda\Big\}.

McShane 1934
showed that every
\lambda
-Lipschitz function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
on some
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be extended to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
via the function
\tilde{f}(\cdot)\doteq\sup_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\lambda\lVert\,\cdot\,-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert
, satisfying
\tilde{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})=f({\boldsymbol{x}})
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
.
The key observation in this paper is the uniform
O(\lambda\epsilon)
approximation bound in
Theorem

2
, which replaces the supremum in
\tilde{f}
with a maximum over a finite
\epsilon
-cover of
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be an
\epsilon
-cover of
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
, and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
t_{0}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq\lVert\cdot\rVert\leq t_{1}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}
with some constants
t_{0},t_{1}>0
.
Suppose
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
for some Lipschitz constant
\lambda>0
, and define
\hat{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-t_{1}\lambda\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
and all
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
, the following hold:

0\leq f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\hat{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq(1+t_{0}^{-1}t_{1})\lambda\epsilon,\qquad\hat{f}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=f(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}),\qquad\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}_{(t_{1}/t_{0})\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Proof.

Take any
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
arbitrarily.
Since
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert\leq t_{1}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}
, we get

\hat{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})-t_{1}\lambda\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq 0
.
Similarly,
\hat{f}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-f(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-f(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-t_{1}\lambda\lVert\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}\geq 0
, hence
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
implies
\hat{f}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=f(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})
.
By the
\epsilon
-covering condition, we have
\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert\leq\epsilon
.
Combining this with
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq t_{0}^{-1}\lVert\cdot\rVert
, we obtain

f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\hat{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+t_{1}\lambda\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq(1+t_{0}^{-1}t_{1})\lambda\epsilon
.
Moreover,
\hat{f}
is
((t_{1}/t_{0})\lambda)
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
because the
\max
function is 1-Lipschitz and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}
is
(t_{0}^{-1})
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
That is,
\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}_{(t_{1}/t_{0})\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

Theorem

2
provides a lower approximation of
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
by a “max-concave” function
\hat{f}
that satisfies
\hat{f}\leq f
on
\mathcal{X}
.
Similarly, one can construct an upper approximation of
f
using a “min-convex” function
(
Hiriart-Urruty 1980
)
, defined by
\check{f}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+t_{1}\lambda\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We also present a variant of
Theorem

2
with an improved approximation rate for smooth functions in
Section

5.2
.

The approximation error of
\hat{f}
and
\check{f}
w.r.t.
f
is zero at the points in
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
, and increases proportionally with the distance from those points.

5
illustrates several examples of these approximations.

.

4.2
Properties of DCF estimators

Let
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty,+\}
,
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and for all nonempty, finite set
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\doteq\{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k_{0}}\}
, define the function class

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\doteq\Big\{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,\Big|\,g({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\doteq\hskip-2.84526pt\sum_{k\in[k_{0}]}\mathbb{I}\big\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\big\}\,h_{g,k}({\boldsymbol{x}}),\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle h_{g,k}({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\doteq b_{g,k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{g,k}^{\top}\,\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}),\,b_{g,k}\in\mathbb{R},\,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{g,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\triangleright}},\,k\in[k_{0}]\Big\}.

Note that
\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
is a vector space over
\mathbb{R}
,
since for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
c_{1}g_{1}+c_{2}g_{2}
also belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, with
b_{c_{1}g_{1}+c_{2}g_{2},k}=c_{1}b_{g_{1},k}+c_{2}b_{g_{2},k}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{c_{1}g_{1}+c_{2}g_{2},k}=c_{1}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{g_{1},k}+c_{2}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{g_{2},k}
for all
k\in[k_{0}]
.

Since the parameter spaces of
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
and
\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
coincide, we can extend
\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(f)
and
\lambda_{f}
from
Section

2.1
, originally defined over
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, to any function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\cup\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
.
We also define the map
\pi:\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\to\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
by
\pi(g)({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{k\in[K]}h_{g,k}({\boldsymbol{x}})
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
.
Clearly, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, we have
b_{\pi(g),k}=b_{g,k}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\pi(g),k}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{g,k}
for all
k\in[k_{0}]
, and
\pi(g)\geq g
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
holds since
\mathcal{C}_{1}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}),\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{K}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
are pairwise disjoint.

Consider the setting of
Theorem

1
, and let
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}=\{{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{K}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the set of center points computed by AFPC (
Algorithm

1
).
Since
\phi_{\triangleright}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})={\boldsymbol{0}}_{d_{\triangleright}}
for any
\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
for all
k\in[K]
, we obtain
g({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=h_{g,k}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=b_{g,k}
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
and
k\in[K]
.
Thereby, if
b_{g,k}\geq h_{g,l}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
for all
k,l\in[K]
, then
g({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})\geq\pi(g)({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
for all
k\in[K]
, implying that the functions
g
and
\pi(g)
coincide over the set
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
.
Therefore, the convex optimization problem (
3
) in DCF (
Algorithm

2
) is equivalent to the following regularized empirical risk minimization task:

\min_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})}\mathcal{L}_{n}(g)+\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g)\textrm{ such that $g=\pi(g)$ over $\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}$},

(9)

where
\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\cdot)\doteq\mathcal{R}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(\cdot)
.
Let
g_{n}
be a solution to (
9
) that matches the solution of (
3
) in parameters, i.e.,
b_{g_{n},k}=b_{n,k}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{g_{n},k}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}
for all
k\in[K]
.
Then, by the definition of the initial DCF estimator
f_{n}
in
Section

2.1
, we have
f_{n}=\pi(g_{n})
.

Recall that
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
is an
\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
-cover of the covariate data
\mathcal{X}_{n}
by definition.
Then, the following result provides a uniform bound on the distance between
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
:

Lemma 3
.

For all
i\in[n]
, it holds that
0\leq f_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-g_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\leq 2\lambda_{f_{n}}\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.

Proof.

The lower bound follows directly from
f_{n}=\pi(g_{n})\geq g_{n}
.
Fix
i\in[n]
arbitrarily, and let
k\in[K]
be such that
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.
From (
9
), we have
g_{n}=\pi(g_{n})
over
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
, which implies
0\leq h_{g_{n},k}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})-h_{g_{n},l}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
for all
l\in[K]
.
By the definition of
\pi
, we also have
h_{f_{n},l}({\boldsymbol{x}})=h_{g_{n},l}({\boldsymbol{x}})
for all
l\in[K]
and
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Therefore:

\begin{split}f_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-g_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})&=\max_{l\in[K]}h_{f_{n},l}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-h_{g_{n},k}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\\
&\leq\max_{l\in[K]}h_{g_{n},l}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-h_{g_{n},l}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})+h_{g_{n},k}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})-h_{g_{n},k}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\\
&\leq\max_{l\in[K]}\big(\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,l}\rVert+\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}\rVert\big)\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert,\end{split}

where we used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality in the last step.
The claim then follows from the definitions of
\lambda_{f_{n}}
and
\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
using
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.

Theorem

2
bounds the uniform approximation error of
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
to the
\lambda_{*}
-Lipschitz regression function
f_{*}
by
O\big(\lambda_{*}\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\big)
.
The next result transfers this bound to
\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
, thereby justifying its use in the ERM task (
9
), and allowing us to reformulate the problem as the tractable convex optimization task in (
3
).

Lemma 4
.

For
f_{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{*},\mathcal{X}_{*}}
, there exists a function
g_{*}\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
such that
f_{*}\geq\pi(g_{*})\geq g_{*}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and

\max_{i\in[n]}f_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-g_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\leq\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}\,\lambda_{*}\,\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}),\quad\qquad\textrm{$g_{*}=\pi(g_{*})$ over $\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}$},\quad\qquad\lambda_{g_{*}}\leq\tau_{\triangleright}\lambda_{*},

where
\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}\doteq 1+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=2\}+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright\neq 2\}\sqrt{d}
, and
\tau_{\triangleright}\doteq\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright\in\{1,2\}\}+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=\infty\}\sqrt{d}+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=+\}\sqrt{2d}
.

Proof.

Fix the norm
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
, and define
t_{0}\doteq\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright\in\{2,\infty\}\}+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=1\}/\sqrt{d}
and
t_{1}\doteq\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright\neq\infty\}+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=\infty\}\sqrt{d}
.
Then,
t_{0}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq\lVert\cdot\rVert\leq t_{1}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}
.

Let
b_{*,k}\doteq f_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{*,k}\doteq[{\boldsymbol{0}}_{d}^{\top}\,-t_{1}\lambda_{*}]^{\top}
for all
k\in[K]
.
Define
\hat{f}_{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
and
g_{*}\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
such that
b_{\hat{f}_{*},k}=b_{g_{*},k}=b_{*,k}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\hat{f}_{*},k}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{g_{*},k}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{*,k}
for all
k\in[K]
.
Then, we have
\hat{f}_{*}=\pi(g_{*})\geq g_{*}
.

Let
\epsilon\doteq\epsilon_{n}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.
Since
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
is an
\epsilon
-cover of
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, we can apply
Theorem

2
with
f=f_{*}
,
\hat{f}=\hat{f}_{*}
,
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}=\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
.
This yields
\hat{f}_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\leq f_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
, proving
f_{*}\geq\pi(g_{*})\geq g_{*}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
.
Now fix
i\in[n]
, and let
k\in[K]
be such that
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.
By the definitions of
g_{*}
and
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
, we have
g_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})=f_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})-t_{1}\lambda_{*}\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert_{\triangleright}
.
Then, using
f_{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{*},\mathcal{X}_{*}}
,
\mathcal{X}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{*}
, and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq t_{0}^{-1}\lVert\cdot\rVert
, we obtain

f_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-g_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})=f_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-f_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})+t_{1}\lambda_{*}\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq(1+t_{0}^{-1}t_{1})\lambda_{*}\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert,

(10)

which implies
f_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-g_{*}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\leq\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}\lambda_{*}\epsilon
for all
i\in[n]
as
(1+t_{0}^{-1}t_{1})=\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}
and
\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert\leq\epsilon
.
Additionally, setting
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}={\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}
in (
10
) and using
f_{*}\geq g_{*}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
yields
g_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=f_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
for all
k\in[K]
.
Then, combining this with
f_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=\hat{f}_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
from
Theorem

2
, we obtain
g_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=f_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=\hat{f}_{*}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})=\pi(g_{*})({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
for all
k\in[K]
, and thus
g_{*}=\pi(g_{*})
over
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
.
Finally,
\lambda_{g_{*}}=\max_{k\in[K]}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{*,k}\rVert=t_{1}\lambda_{*}=\tau_{\triangleright}\lambda_{*}
, which proves the claim for all
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
.

The case
\triangleright=+
follows from the case
\triangleright=1
by using
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{*,k}^{\top}\,\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=-\lambda_{*}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{1}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{*,k}\doteq-\lambda_{*}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{2d}
, which satisfies
\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{*,k}\rVert=\tau_{\triangleright}\lambda_{*}
for all
k\in[K]
.

Note that
f_{n}
,
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
, and
f_{n}^{+}
are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constants bounded by
\lambda_{f_{n}}
,
\lambda_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+}}
, and
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}
, respectively.
In the refinement step (
4
), it is important to ensure that the Lipschitz constant of
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
does not scale polynomially in the sample size
n
, as this would deteriorate the convergence rate of the DCF estimator.
In
Section

4.4.2
, we prove that the Lipschitz constant of
f_{n}
grows only logarithically with
n
.
Therefore, we use
\lambda_{f_{n}}
as a reference for the Lipschitz constant of
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
, and the following result shows that the Lipschitz constant of the refined estimators
\hat{f}_{n}^{+}
and
f_{n}^{+}
can exceed
\lambda_{f_{n}}
by at most an
O(\theta_{3})
factor.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\theta_{3}\geq 1
. Then,
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}\leq\lambda_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+}}\leq(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{f_{n}}
.

Proof.

By definition
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}\leq\lambda_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+}}
.
Suppose, to the contrary, that
\lambda_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+}}>(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{f_{n}}
.
As
\theta_{3}\geq 1
implies
\mathcal{R}_{n}(f_{n})=\mathcal{R}_{0,0,\theta_{2}}(f_{n})
, we get
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\hat{f}_{n}^{+})\geq\lambda_{f_{n}}^{-2}\big(\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{0,0,\theta_{2}}(f_{n})\big)(\lambda_{\hat{f}_{n}^{+}}-\theta_{3}\lambda_{f_{n}})_{+}^{2}>\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{n}(f_{n})
, which contradicts (
4
), proving the claim.

We will also need the following properties of the feature vector
\phi_{\triangleright}
: its output norm is bounded by a constant multiple of the Euclidean distance between its arguments, and
\phi_{\triangleright}
is Lipschitz in its second argument, as shown in the next result:

Lemma 6
.

Let
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty,+\}
. Then for all
{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}},\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following inequalities hold:

\lVert\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\rVert\leq\tau_{\phi_{\triangleright}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert,\qquad\lVert\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\rVert\leq\lambda_{\phi_{\triangleright}}\lVert\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert,

where
\tau_{\phi_{\triangleright}}\doteq\sqrt{1+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright\in\{2,\infty\}\}+d\,\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=1\}}
, and
\lambda_{\phi_{\triangleright}}\doteq 1+\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright\neq 1\}+\sqrt{d}\,\mathbb{I}\{\triangleright=1\}
.

Proof.

For
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
, the first claim follows from
\lVert\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\rVert^{2}=\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}_{\triangleright}
, and the inequalities
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\cdot\rVert
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{d}\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
For
\triangleright=+
, we have
\lVert\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\rVert^{2}=\lVert({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})_{+}\rVert^{2}+\lVert(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{x}})_{+}\rVert^{2}=\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}
.

For
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
, we have
\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{x}},\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=\big[(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}\,\,\,(\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright}-\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert_{\triangleright})\big]^{\top}
.
From this, we prove the second claim using
\lVert[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\top}\,s]^{\top}\rVert\leq\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\rVert+|s|
and the reverse triangle inequality
|\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\rVert_{\triangleright}-\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\triangleright}|\leq\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\triangleright}\leq(\lambda_{\phi_{\triangleright}}-1)\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert
, which hold for all
{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
\triangleright=+
, we have
\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\phi_{+}({\boldsymbol{x}},\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=\big[\big(({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})_{+}-({\boldsymbol{x}}-\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})_{+}\big)^{\top}\,\,\,\big((\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{x}})_{+}-(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{x}})_{+}\big)^{\top}\big]^{\top}
, and the second claim follows by using
\lVert[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\top}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\top}]^{\top}\rVert\leq\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}\rVert+\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert
and
\lVert({\boldsymbol{u}})_{+}-({\boldsymbol{v}})_{+}\rVert\leq\lVert{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert
, which hold for all
{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
depend nonlinearly on the random center points

share the same parameters, and we have
b_{n,k}=b_{f_{n},k}=b_{g_{n},k}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{f_{n},k}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{g_{n},k}
for all

.
First, consider the following bound on the regularized empirical risk of

:

Lemma 13
.

Suppose that event
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
holds.
Then,
\mathcal{L}_{n}(g_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g_{n})\leq(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}+2\lambda_{*}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})^{2}
.

Proof.

Define the constant function
g_{0}
by
g_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq y_{0}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Observe that
g_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
,
g_{0}=\pi(g_{0})
, and
\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g_{0})=0
.
Since
g_{n}
is a solution to (

\mathcal{L}_{n}(g_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g_{n})\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(g_{0})=\frac{1}{n}\hskip-1.42262pt\sum_{i\in[n]}(Y_{i}\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pty_{0})^{2}
.
Then,

implies the claim by
Lemma

9
.

To bound the inner product terms in (

11
.
For this, we need the following bound on the parameter space of DCF estimators:

Lemma 14
.

Suppose that event
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
and (
7
) hold.
Then,
g_{n}\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
, where

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\doteq\Big\{g\in\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}):\max_{k\in[K]}\max\big\{|b_{g,k}-y_{0}|,\sqrt{d}R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{g,k}\rVert\big\}\leq\beta_{1}\Big\},

and
\beta_{1}
is a constant satisfying
r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\sqrt{d}\leq\beta_{1}=\Theta\big(\sqrt{dn}(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}+\lambda_{*}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})\big)

Proof.

Since
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{n}
, we can define
i_{k}\in[n]
for each

such that
({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k},Y_{i_{k}})\in{\mathcal{D}_{n}}
.
By the definition of

, we have
b_{n,k}=g_{n}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
.
Hence, by
Lemma

13
, and
\theta_{1}\geq R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}(d/n)

\frac{1}{n}\max_{k\in[K]}\Big\{(b_{n,k}-Y_{i_{k}})^{2},dR_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}(\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k}\rVert-\theta_{0})_{+}^{2}\Big\}\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(g_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g_{n})\leq(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}+2\lambda_{*}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})^{2}.

Then, by the triangle inequality and
Lemma

9
,
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
implies
|b_{n,k}-y_{0}|\leq(1+\sqrt{n})(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}+2\lambda_{*}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})
.
Further, we also get

Therefore,
\beta_{1}
can be chosen as claimed as

by (
7
),

, and

from
Lemma

9
.

The bound
\beta_{1}
in

14
scales with

, making it too loose to directly bound the Lipschitz constant of

for establishing the near-minimax rate.
However, we can still use the bounded class

in the concentration inequality of

to upper bound the inner product terms of (

The key observation is that

Lemma 15
.

Let
g_{*}
be an approximation of
f_{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{*},\mathcal{X}_{*}}
as in
Lemma

4
.
For any
\delta\in(0,\beta_{1}]
, define event

as

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta}\doteq\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}\cap\bigg\{\langle f_{n}-g_{n},f_{*}-y\rangle_{n}&=O\Big(\sigma\big(\lVert f_{n}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}+\delta\big)\sqrt{dK\ln\big(\beta_{1}/(\delta\gamma)\big)/n}+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\delta\Big),\\
\langle g_{*}-f_{*},f_{*}-y\rangle_{n}&=O\Big(\sigma\lVert g_{*}-f_{*}\rVert_{n}\sqrt{\ln(1/\gamma)/n}\Big),\\
\langle g_{*}-g_{n},f_{*}-y\rangle_{n}&=O\Big(\sigma\big(\lVert g_{*}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}+\delta\big)\sqrt{dK\ln\big(\beta_{1}/(\delta\gamma)\big)/n}+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\delta\Big)\bigg\}.\end{split}

Then,
\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta}\}\geq 1-5\gamma
.

Proof.

Define the metric
\psi
by
\psi(h,\hat{h})\doteq\max_{k\in[K]}|b_{h,k}-b_{\hat{h},k}|+2R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\sqrt{1+d}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h,k}-{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\hat{h},k}\rVert
for all
h,\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}\in\{\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}),\mathcal{G}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\}
.
Note that
\tau_{\phi_{\triangleright}}^{2}\leq 1+d
by definition, and
\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert\leq 2R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}
holds for all
i\in[n]
and
k\in[K]
by the triangle inequality.
Therefore, by
Lemma

12
, we have
\lVert h-\hat{h}\rVert_{n}\leq\max_{i\in[n]}|h({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})-\hat{h}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})|\leq\psi(h,\hat{h})
.
Additionally, note that
\psi(g,\hat{g})=O(\beta_{1})
and
\psi\big(\pi(g),\pi(\hat{g})\big)=O(\beta_{1})
for all
g,\hat{g}\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}\doteq\{f-g:g\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}),f=\pi(g)\}
, where
f_{n}-g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
by
f_{n}=\pi(g_{n})
and
Lemma

14
.
For all
h,\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
with
h=f-g
and
\hat{h}=\hat{f}-\hat{g}
, define the metric
\psi_{1}(h,\hat{h})\doteq\psi(f,\hat{f})+\psi(g,\hat{g})
.
By using
Lemma

10
, the bound
\beta_{1}
on the parameter magnitudes within
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
, and since the parameters of the functions
g
and
\pi(g)
are the same, we have
\ln N_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\delta)=O\big(dK\ln(\beta_{1}/\delta)\big)
for all
\delta\in(0,\beta_{1}]
.
Then, the first inequality in
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta}
holds with probability at least
1-\gamma
by
Lemma

11
, using
\mathcal{H}_{n}\leftarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}
, and
\psi_{n}\leftarrow\psi_{1}
.

Define the singleton set
\mathcal{H}_{2}\doteq\{g_{*}-f_{*}\}
.
Then, the second inequality in
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta}
holds with probability at least
1-\gamma
by
Lemma

11
, using
\mathcal{H}_{n}\leftarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
, the zero constant function for
\psi_{n}
, and taking the limit
\delta\to 0
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{3}\doteq\{g_{*}-g:g\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\}
, where
g_{*}-g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{3}
by
Lemma

14
.
For all
h,\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{3}
with
h=g_{*}-g
and
\hat{h}=g_{*}-\hat{g}
, define
\psi_{3}(h,\hat{h})\doteq\psi(g,\hat{g})
.
By
Lemma

10
, and the bound
\beta_{1}
on the parameter magnitudes within
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
, we have
\ln N_{\psi_{3}}(\mathcal{H}_{3},\delta)=O\big(dK\ln(\beta_{1}/\delta)\big)
for all
\delta\in(0,\beta_{1}]
.
Then, the third inequality in
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta}
holds with probability at least
1-\gamma
by
Lemma

11
, using
\mathcal{H}_{n}\leftarrow\mathcal{H}_{3}
, and
\psi_{n}\leftarrow\psi_{3}
.

Finally, the result follows by combining the three cases with
Lemma

9
.

We now bound
\lVert g_{n}-g_{*}\rVert_{n}
and
\lambda_{f_{n}}
by combining the result of
Lemma

15
with the “basic inequality” (
16
).
This, in turn, yields a bound on the approximation error

via (

).

Lemma 16
.

Let
g_{*}
be an approximation of
f_{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{*},\mathcal{X}_{*}}
as in
Lemma

4
.
Set
\delta_{n}\doteq r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\sqrt{d}K/n
, and suppose that
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta_{n}}
and (
7
) hold.
Then, for some
\lambda_{0}>0
, the following bounds hold:

\begin{split}\lambda_{f_{n}}^{2}&\leq\lambda_{0}^{2}=\Theta\big(\theta_{0}^{2}+\tau_{\triangleright}^{2}\lambda_{*}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\ln(\beta_{2}/\gamma)\big),\\
\lVert g_{n}-g_{*}\rVert_{n}^{2}&=O\bigg(\frac{dK}{n}\Big(\tau_{\triangleright}^{2}(1+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}^{2})\lambda_{*}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\ln(\beta_{2}/\gamma)\Big)\bigg),\\
E_{\textrm{approx}}&=O\Big(\frac{dK}{n}(1+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}^{2})\lambda_{0}^{2}\Big),\end{split}

where
\beta_{2}\doteq n\beta_{1}/(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\sqrt{d})
satisfies
n\leq\beta_{2}=\Theta\big(n^{3/2}(1+\lambda_{*}\rho/\sigma)\big)
.

Proof.

Notice that
\delta_{n}\in(0,\beta_{1}]
by definition since
K\leq n
.
Using
ab=(a/c)(cb)\leq a^{2}/(2c^{2})+b^{2}(c^{2}/2)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
c>0
, we obtain from
Lemma

15
and
\beta_{1}/\delta_{n}=O(\beta_{2})
that

\begin{split}\langle g_{*}-g_{n},f_{*}-y\rangle_{n}&=\frac{1}{8}\lVert g_{*}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}^{2}+O\Big(\delta_{n}^{2}+\frac{dK\sigma^{2}}{n}\ln\big(\beta_{1}/(\delta_{n}\gamma)\big)+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\delta_{n}\Big)\\
&=\frac{1}{8}\lVert g_{*}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}^{2}+O\Big(\frac{dK\sigma^{2}}{n}\ln(\beta_{2}/\gamma)\Big).\end{split}

(20)

From
Lemma

4
, we have
\lVert g_{*}-f_{*}\rVert_{n}^{2}=O\big(d\lambda_{*}^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\big)
by
\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}^{2}=O(d)
, and
\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g_{*})=O\big(\theta_{1}\tau_{\triangleright}^{2}\lambda_{*}^{2}\big)
by
\theta_{1}\geq K\theta_{2}
from (

).
Recall that
\theta_{1}=\Theta\big(\max\{1,R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}\}dK/n\big)
from (
7
).
Further,
\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})=O(R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}K/n)
from
Lemma

8
.
Therefore, using
\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(g_{n})\geq\theta_{1}(\lambda_{f_{n}}-\theta_{0})_{+}^{2}
,
\lambda_{f_{n}}=\lambda_{g_{n}}
,
\tau_{\triangleright}\geq 1
, and combining (

) with the “basic inequality” (
16
), we obtain

\frac{1}{4}\lVert g_{*}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}^{2}+\max\{1,R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}\}\frac{dK}{n}(\lambda_{f_{n}}-\theta_{0})_{+}^{2}=O\bigg(\hskip-1.42262pt\frac{dK}{n}\Big(\tau_{\triangleright}^{2}\max\{1,R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}\}\lambda_{*}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\ln(\beta_{2}/\gamma)\Big)\hskip-2.84526pt\bigg).

(21)

Then, the claims for
\lambda_{f_{n}}^{2}
and
\lVert g_{n}-g_{*}\rVert_{n}^{2}
follow from (
21
) after rearranging terms, and applying
R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}=O(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})
which follows from
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta_{n}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
by
Lemma

9
.

Similarly to the derivation of (
20
),
Lemma

15
yields for
\delta_{n}
that

\langle f_{n}-g_{n},f_{*}-y\rangle_{n}+\langle g_{*}-f_{*},f_{*}-y\rangle_{n}=O\Big(\lVert f_{n}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}^{2}+\lVert g_{*}-f_{*}\rVert_{n}^{2}+\frac{dK}{n}\sigma^{2}\ln(\beta_{2}/\gamma)\Big).

(22)

Since
\lVert f_{n}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}^{2}=O\big(\lambda_{f_{n}}^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\big)
by
Lemma

3
, and
\lVert g_{*}-f_{*}\rVert_{n}^{2}=O\big(\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}^{2}\lambda_{*}^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\big)
by
Lemma

4
, we upper bound the approximation error
E_{\textrm{approx}}
by combining (
17
) with (
20
), the bound on
\lVert g_{*}-g_{n}\rVert_{n}^{2}
, and (
22
) as

\begin{split}E_{\textrm{approx}}=\,&O\bigg(\big(\lambda_{f_{n}}^{2}+\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}^{2}\lambda_{*}^{2}\big)\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})+\theta_{1}\tau_{\triangleright}^{2}\lambda_{*}^{2}+\frac{dK}{n}\Big(\tau_{\triangleright}^{2}(1+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}^{2})\lambda_{*}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\ln(\beta_{2}/\gamma)\Big)\bigg),\end{split}

(23)

which proves the claim on
E_{\textrm{approx}}
, as
\tilde{\tau}_{\triangleright}^{2}=O(d)
by definition, and
\lambda_{f_{n}}^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})=O\big(\frac{K}{n}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}\big)
by
\lambda_{f_{n}}\leq\lambda_{0}
,
\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})=O(R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}^{2}K/n)
by
Lemma

8
, and
R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}=O(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})
.
The bounds on
\beta_{2}
hold by definition.

In the proof of
Lemma

16
, for bounding
\lambda_{f_{n}}
, we used that

in (
7
) scales with
\max\{1,R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\}
rather than just
R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}
.
This choice is necessary because, in the latter case, we cannot ensure that
\sigma/R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}
remains upper bounded in the setting of
Theorem

1
.

Notice that the bound
\beta_{1}
on the slope parameters from
Lemma

14
is improved by the bound on
\lambda_{f_{n}}
from
Lemma

, replacing the earlier
\sqrt{n}
dependence with
\theta_{0}+\sqrt{\ln(n)}
.
This improvement will be important for applying our concentration inequality in the random design setting and obtaining the near-minimax rate.

4.4.3
Applying the concentration inequality in the random design

After bounding the approximation error
E_{\textrm{approx}}=\lVert f_{n}^{+}-y\rVert_{n}^{2}-\lVert f_{*}-y\rVert_{n}^{2}
of the DCF estimator
f_{n}^{+}
to the regression function
f_{*}
, it remains to bound the first term in (
11
), namely
\lVert f_{n}^{+}-f_{*}\rVert_{*}^{2}-\tilde{c}_{0}E_{\textrm{approx}}
, in order to complete the proof of
Theorem

1
.
To this end, we use the following concentration inequality, which builds on the Bernstein inequality and leverages the results of
Balázs et al. 2016
, as applied here to the product of subgaussian random variables.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite, nonempty set, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For each
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
i\in[n]\cup\{0\}
, let
Z_{f,i},W_{f,i}
be real-valued, subgaussian random variables satisfying
\mathbb{E}\big[e^{Z_{f,i}^{2}/\omega^{2}}\big]\leq 2
and
\mathbb{E}\big[e^{W_{f,i}^{2}/\nu^{2}}\big]\leq 2
for some
\omega,\nu>0
.
Suppose that, for each fixed
f\in\mathcal{F}
, the random variables
Z_{f,0}W_{f,0},Z_{f,1}W_{f,1},\ldots,Z_{f,n}W_{f,n}
are i.i.d.,
and that there exist constants
\alpha,\mu>0
such that
\mu^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[Z_{f,0}^{2}]\leq\alpha\mathbb{E}[Z_{f,0}W_{f,0}]
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
.
Then, for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
, it holds with probability at least
1-\gamma
that

\max_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{f,0}W_{f,0}\big]-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{f,i}W_{f,i}\Big\}\leq 16(\omega+2\alpha\nu)\nu\,\frac{\ln(3\omega/\mu)}{n}\,\ln(|\mathcal{F}|/\gamma).

Proof.

See
Section

4.4.4
.

The condition
\mathbb{E}[Z_{f,0}^{2}]\leq\alpha\mathbb{E}[Z_{f,0}W_{f,0}]
in
Lemma

17
is related to the widely used Bernstein condition
(
Bartlett and Mendelson 2006
, Definition 2.6)
.

In order to apply
Lemma

17
, we need to show that
f_{n}^{+}
belongs to a (non-random) function class with a bounded covering number.
From
Lemma

5
we already know that
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}\leq(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{f_{n}}
, where
\lambda_{f_{n}}
is further bounded in
Lemma

16
as
\lambda_{f_{n}}\leq\lambda_{0}
.
The next result provides the missing upper bound on the magnitudes of the bias parameters of
f_{n}^{+}
.
This bound follows from properties of
f_{n}^{+}
ensured by the final step in the definition of the DCF estimator (
5
).
Unlike in
Section

4.4.2
, here the bound on the bias terms cannot be allowed to scale polynomially with the sample size
n
.

Lemma 18
.

Suppose that event
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta_{n}}
and (
7
) hold.
Then, there exists
\beta_{0}>0
such that
\max_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}}|b_{f_{n}^{+},k}-y_{0}|\leq\beta_{0}
, and
\beta_{0}=\Theta\big(\tau_{\phi_{\triangleright}}(1+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})\theta_{3}\lambda_{0}\big)
.
Further,
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})=O\big((1+r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}^{2})\theta_{3}^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}\big)
.

Proof.

For each
k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}
, let
i_{k}\in[n]
be such that
f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})=b_{f_{n}^{+},k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f_{n}^{+},k}^{\top}\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})
, which always exists by the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}
in (
5
).
The triangle inequality and
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma,\delta_{n}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
yield
\lVert{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}}-{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k}\rVert\leq 2r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}
.
Moreover,
Lemmas

5
and
16
imply
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}\leq(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{0}
.
Then, by the triangle and Cauchy-Schwartz inequalities, and
Lemma

6
, we get

\begin{split}|b_{f_{n}^{+},k}-y_{0}|&\leq|f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})-y_{0}|+\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f_{n}^{+},k}\rVert\lVert\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})\rVert\\
&\leq|f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})-y_{0}|+2r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}\tau_{\phi_{\triangleright}}(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{0}.\end{split}

(24)

Additionally, from (
5
), we have
f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})=C_{n}^{+}+\hat{f}_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
, which yields

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})=C_{n}^{+}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{f}_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})=\bar{Y}.

(25)

Since
\lambda_{f_{n}^{+}}\leq(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{0}
, we have that
f_{n}^{+}
is
((1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{0})
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is
f_{n}^{+}\in\mathcal{F}_{(1+\theta_{3})\lambda_{0},\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
Using this, the triangle and Jensen’s inequalities, (
25
), and
Lemma

9
,
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
implies

\begin{split}|f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})-y_{0}|&\leq\Big|f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i})\Big|+|\bar{Y}-y_{0}|\\
&\leq 2(1+\theta_{3})r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}\lambda_{0}+(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}+2\lambda_{*}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho}).\end{split}

(26)

We prove the bound
\beta_{0}
on the bias by combining (
24
) and (
26
), and using
\theta_{3}\geq 1
with
\max\{\lambda_{*},r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}\}=O(\lambda_{0})
.

By the triangle inequality and
Lemma

9
, event
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
also implies
|f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})-Y_{i_{k}}|\leq|f_{n}^{+}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{i_{k}})-y_{0}|+O(r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\sigma}+\lambda_{0}r_{\hskip-1.42262pt\rho})
.
The bound on
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f_{n}^{+})
then follows from (
26
) by squaring and averaging over all
i_{k}\in[n]
.

Having bounded the parameter magnitudes of
f_{n}^{+}
, we now construct a bounded, non-random function class that always contains a function uniformly approximating
f_{n}^{+}
over the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the required accuracy.
This construction relies on the fact that
\phi_{\triangleright}(\cdot,\cdot)
is Lipschitz in its second argument (
Lemma

6
).

We also need the notion of tuples: the set of all tuples of size
k\in\mathbb{N}
with elements from a set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as
\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathcal{X})\doteq\{\langle\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}\rangle:\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}\in\mathcal{X},k\in[K]\}
.
We extend the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\tilde{\mathcal{X}})
to allow
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}
to be a tuple, so that multiple function parameters may be associated with the same center.

The following result presents the construction mentioned above:

Lemma 19
.

Suppose the conditions of
Lemma

18
hold, and let
k_{*}
be as in
Lemma

8

\mathbb{E}\big[e^{s(\mathbb{E}[ZW]-ZW)}\big]\leq\exp\Big(\tfrac{c\,\nu^{2}s^{2}\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}]}{1-c\,\omega\nu s}\Big)
for all
s\in\big(0,1/(c\,\omega\nu)\big)
.

Proof.

Part (a) was proved in Lemma A.5 of
Balázs 2016
.
Part (b) follows from Theorem 2.10 of
Boucheron et al. 2013
, using part (a).

Next, we combine
Lemma

21
with the ideas of
Balázs et al. 2016
to prove
Lemma

17
.

Proof of
Lemma

17
.

Introduce the shorthand notation
Z_{f}\doteq Z_{f,0}
and
W_{f}\doteq W_{f,0}
.
By Lemma A.2 of
Balázs 2016
, we have
\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}^{4}]\leq 2(2/e)^{2}\omega^{4}
.
Since
\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}^{2}]\geq\mu^{2}
, it follows that
\mathbb{K}[Z_{f}]\leq 2(\omega/\mu)^{4}
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
.
Define
c\doteq 8\ln(3\omega/\mu)
, which satisfies
c\geq\max_{f\in\mathcal{F}}4\ln(4\sqrt{\mathbb{K}[Z_{f}]})
.
Then, by applying
Lemma

21
with
\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}^{2}]\leq\alpha\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]
, we obtain for all
s\in(0,1)
,
i\in[n]
, and
f\in\mathcal{F}
that

\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{s(\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]-Z_{f,i}W_{f,i})/(c\,\omega\nu)}\Big]\leq\exp\Big(\frac{c\,\nu^{2}s^{2}\alpha\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]}{(1-s)(c\,\omega\nu)^{2}}\Big).

(34)

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
t>0
be constants to be chosen later.
Thereby, applying the union and Chernoff bounds, using the independence of
Z_{f}W_{f},Z_{f,1}W_{f,1},\ldots,Z_{f,n}W_{f,n}
for each fixed
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and applying (
34
), we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\max_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{f}W_{f}\big]-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}&Z_{f,i}W_{f,i}\Big\}>\frac{2tc\,\omega\nu}{s\,n}\bigg\}\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\max_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{s}{2c\,\omega\nu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]-2Z_{f,i}W_{f,i}>t\bigg\}\\
&\leq e^{-t}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[e^{\frac{s}{2c\,\omega\nu}\big(\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]-2Z_{f,i}W_{f,i}\big)}\bigg]\\
&=e^{-t}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}e^{-\frac{s\,n\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]}{2c\,\omega\nu}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{s(\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]-Z_{f,i}W_{f,i})/(c\,\omega\nu)}\Big]\\
&\leq e^{-t}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\exp\bigg(\frac{s\,n\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{f}W_{f}]}{c\,\omega\nu}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s\nu\alpha}{(1-s)\omega}\Big)\bigg)\\
&=\gamma,\end{split}

where we set
s\doteq\omega/(\omega+2\alpha\nu)
and
t\doteq\ln(|\mathcal{F}|/\gamma)
for the last line.
This proves the claim.

5
Approximation of DC functions

An important part of our analysis is understanding the approximation rate of the chosen function representation for the estimator to the underlying Lipschitz regression function.
To strenghten the connection between our work and the existing literature, we establish uniform approximation results for other delta-convex function classes that have been proposed to extend convex regression techniques to the more general Lipschitz setting of (
1
).

Define the uniform norm of a function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
on
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\lVert f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\doteq\sup_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}|f({\boldsymbol{x}})|
.

5.1
Max-min-affine functions

For all
k_{0},l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, define the set of max-min-affine functions by

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}\doteq\Big\{m:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,\Big|\,&m({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}\min_{l\in[l_{0}]}b_{k,l}+{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k,l},\\
&{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b_{k,l}\in\mathbb{R},\,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k,l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,k\in[k_{0}],\,l\in[l_{0}]\Big\}.\end{split}

Recall from (
2
) that
v_{f,k}
denotes the parameter of
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
associated with the norm term, for some finite
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
k\in[|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|]
.
We then define the class in which this parameter is restricted to be nonpositive by
\mathcal{F}_{\infty-}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\doteq\big\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}):v_{f,k}\leq 0,k\in[|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|]\big\}
.
The approximation in
Theorem

2
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{\infty-}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, thereby achieving a uniform approximation rate for max-min-affine functions, as established in the next result.

Corollary 22
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and suppose there exist
r,t>0
such that
N_{\lVert\cdot\rVert}(\mathcal{X},\epsilon)\leq(r/\epsilon)^{t}
for all
\epsilon\in(0,r]
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
for some Lipschitz constant
\lambda>0
.
Then, for all
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
l_{0}\geq 2d
, there exists
m\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
such that
\lVert f-m\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq(1+\sqrt{d})\lambda rk_{0}^{-1/t}
and
m\in\mathcal{F}_{\sqrt{d}\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

Proof.

Write the max-norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
in max-linear form as
\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{j\in[d],s\in\{-1,1\}}s{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}^{T}{\boldsymbol{x}}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{e}}_{d}
are the canonical basis vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, functions in
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
with
l_{0}\geq 2d
can use the internal minimization to represent the negated max-norm
-\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
, thereby allowing implementation of any
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty-}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
with any
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\doteq\{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k_{0}}\}
as

\begin{split}f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}b_{f,k}\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262pt{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k})\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262ptv_{f,k}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k}\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}\hskip-2.84526pt\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[d],\\
s\in\{-1,1\}\end{subarray}}\hskip-7.11317ptb_{f,k}\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262pt({\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262ptv_{f,k}s{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j})\hskip-1.42262pt^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt({\boldsymbol{x}}\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{k})\end{split}

for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence, the function
\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty-}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
from
Theorem

2
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
.

Set
\epsilon\doteq rk_{0}^{-1/t}
, which ensures that
N_{\lVert\cdot\rVert}(\mathcal{X},\epsilon)\leq(r/\epsilon)^{t}=k_{0}
.
Choose
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|=k_{0}
and it contains an
\epsilon
-cover of
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
The claims then follows from
Theorem

2
using
\mathcal{X}
,
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
,
\epsilon
,
t_{0}=1
,
t_{1}=\sqrt{d}
, and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
.

For any bounded set
\mathcal{X}
, the covering condition of
Corollary

22
is satisfied with
t=d
by
Lemma

7
.
This yields the approximation rate
{k_{0}}^{-1/d}
, which is known to be optimal
(
DeVore et al. 1989
, Theorem 4.2)
.

An appealing property of the class
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
is that it does not depend on the choice of center points
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
, unlike
\mathcal{F}_{\infty-}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
or
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
.
In fact,
\mathcal{F}_{\infty-}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})\subset\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
for any
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
k_{0}\geq|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|
and
l_{0}\geq 2d
.
However,
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
uses at least
d
times more parameters than
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{X}})
, specifically at least
2d|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|(d+1)
versus
|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|(d+2)+|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|d
.
Moreover, we are not aware of any tractable algorithm for solving the nonconvex ERM problem over the full class
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0},l_{0}}
.
Only heuristic methods have been proposed
(
Bagirov et al. 2010
;
Bagirov et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

The DCF algorithm (
Algorithm

2
) addresses this gap in the presented nonparametric setting.
Using
\triangleright=\infty
and additional linear constraints
v_{f,k}\leq 0
for all
k\in[K]
, expressed as
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}^{\top}=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}^{\top}\,\,v_{k}]
with
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
v_{k}\leq 0
in (

), DCF computes an estimator

in polynomial-time, where the set
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}
is computed by AFPC.
This
f_{n}
estimator achieves the near-minimax rate of
Theorem

1
and can be converted to an equivalent representation
m_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{K,2d}
.
The final refinement step (
4
) is performed directly over
\mathcal{M}_{K,2d}
, initialized at
m_{n}
, and the resulting estimator continues to satisfy the near-minimax rate of
Theorem

1
.
Our proof adapts to this case (and in fact simplifies) since
\mathcal{M}_{K,2d}
depends not on the random covariates
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, but only on
K\leq k_{*}
.
This leads to a substantial simplification of
Lemmas

19
and
20
, as we only need to construct a cover of the (non-random) class
\mathcal{M}_{k_{*},2d}
, which follows straightforwadly from
Lemma

10
after bounding the parameter space.
The convergence rate bound for
m_{n}
scales with an additional factor of
d
relative to that of
Theorem

1
, due to the larger number of parameters in
m_{n}
compared to
f_{n}
.
Finally, the symmetrization of this max-min-affine estimator can be carried out in a similar way as for the other DCF variants, as described in
Section

6.1
.

5.2
Approximation of smooth functions

We now present an approximation result analogous to
Theorem

2
for smooth functions, which we use in
Section

5.3
to establish uniform approximation results for certain delta-convex classes.

For some
\nu>0
, we say that a function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\nu
-smooth on
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
if it is differentiable on
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and its gradient
\nabla f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
\nu
-Lipschitz on
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
, that is
\lVert\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\rVert\leq\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert
holds for all
{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Denote the class of
\nu
-smooth functions on
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
by
\mathcal{F}_{\nu,\mathcal{X}}^{\nabla}
.

Then consider the following uniform approximation bound for smooth functions:

Theorem 23
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be an
\epsilon
-cover of a convex set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\nu,\mathcal{X}}^{\nabla}
for some constant
\nu>0
, and define
\tilde{f}_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then,
0\leq f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\tilde{f}_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq 2\nu\epsilon^{2}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Choose
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
arbitrarily.
Because
\mathcal{X}
is a convex set, Taylor’s theorem and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\nu,\mathcal{X}}^{\nabla}
yield for all
\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
that
f({\boldsymbol{x}})=f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\nabla f\big(t_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}{\boldsymbol{x}}+(1-t_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}})\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\big)^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})
for some
t_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}\in[0,1]
.
Then by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we get

\begin{split}\tilde{f}_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})&=\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})+\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\\
&\leq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}(t_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}-1)\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\\
&\leq 0.\end{split}

For the other side, we have
\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert\leq\epsilon
by the
\epsilon
-covering property.
Then by Taylor’s theorem,
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\nu,\mathcal{X}}^{\nabla}
, and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we get

\begin{split}f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\tilde{f}_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})&=\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\\
&\leq\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}(t_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}+1)\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\\
&\leq 2\nu\epsilon^{2},\end{split}

which proves the claim.

The function
\tilde{f}_{1}
of
Theorem

23
provides a lower approximation of
f
.
Similarly, an upper approximation is given by
\breve{f}_{1}
, defined as
\breve{f}_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\nu\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Note that
\tilde{f}_{1}
and
\breve{f}_{1}
use the quadratic feature
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}
, in contrast to the norm feature
\lVert\cdot\rVert
used by the functions
\hat{f}
and
\check{f}
in
Section

4.1
.
The next result shows that the quadratic feature
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}
can also be used to approximate non-smooth Lipschitz functions.

Theorem 24
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be an
\epsilon
-cover of a set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
for some Lipschitz constant
\lambda>0
, and define
\tilde{f}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-(\lambda/\epsilon)\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then,
-\lambda\epsilon/4\leq f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\tilde{f}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq 2\lambda\epsilon
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Choose
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
arbitrarily.
By
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
, we have

\tilde{f}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})-(\lambda/\epsilon)\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\leq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}\lambda\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert\Big(1-\frac{\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert}{\epsilon}\Big)\leq\frac{\lambda\epsilon}{4}.

For the other side, we have
\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert\leq\epsilon
by the
\epsilon
-covering property.
The claim then follows from
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
as
f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\tilde{f}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+(\lambda/\epsilon)\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\leq 2\lambda\epsilon
.

Again, the max-concave approximation
\tilde{f}_{0}
has an analogous min-convex variant
\breve{f}_{0}
, defined as
\breve{f}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+(\lambda/\epsilon)\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

5.3
Weakly max-affine and delta-max-affine functions

The functions
\tilde{f}_{0}
and
\tilde{f}_{1}
of
Theorems

23
and
24
are weakly convex in the sense of
Vial 1983
; that is, there exist
s_{0},s_{1}\geq 0
such that
\tilde{f}_{0}+s_{0}q
and
\tilde{f}_{1}+s_{1}q
are convex functions, where
q
is a symmetric convex quadratic function given by
q({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}\rVert^{2}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Define the class of max-affine functions with at most
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
hyperplanes by

\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}\doteq\Big\{m:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,\Big|\,m({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{k\in[k_{0}]}b_{k}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{x}},\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,b_{k}\in\mathbb{R},\,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,k\in[k_{0}]\Big\}.

Let the class of weakly max-affine functions be
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}^{w}\doteq\{f\,|\,f\doteq m-sq,\,m\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}},\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
Furthermore, consider the closely related class of delta-max-affine functions, defined by
\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}^{\Delta}\doteq\{f\,|\,f\doteq m_{1}-m_{2},\,m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}\}
.
The next result provides uniform approximation bounds for both of these classes.
For convenience, let
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\doteq\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}^{\nabla}\doteq\{f\,|\,f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}\}
.

Corollary 25
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex set, and suppose there exist
r,t>0
such that
N_{\lVert\cdot\rVert}(\mathcal{X},\epsilon)\leq(r/\epsilon)^{t}
for all
\epsilon\in(0,r]
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\nu,\mathcal{X}}^{\nabla}
for some constants
\lambda,\nu\in(0,\infty]
.
Then, for all
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist functions
f_{w}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}^{w}
and
f_{\Delta}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}^{\Delta}
such that

\lVert f_{w}-f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq 2\epsilon\min\{\lambda,\nu\epsilon\},\quad\qquad\lVert f_{\Delta}-f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq 3\epsilon\min\{\lambda,\nu\epsilon\},\quad\qquad\epsilon\doteq rk_{0}^{-1/t}.

Proof.

The choice
\epsilon=rk_{0}^{-1/t}
ensures that
N_{\lVert\cdot\rVert}(\mathcal{X},\epsilon)\leq(r/\epsilon)^{t}=k_{0}
, which allows us to choose
\hat{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|\hat{\mathcal{X}}|=k_{0}
such that it contains an
\epsilon
-cover of
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
Define the following weakly max-affine functions for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\doteq m_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt(\lambda/\epsilon)q({\boldsymbol{x}}),

\displaystyle m_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\doteq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-(\lambda/\epsilon)\lVert\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}+2(\lambda/\epsilon)\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\doteq m_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\nu q({\boldsymbol{x}}),

\displaystyle m_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\doteq\max_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\nu\big(\lVert\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\hskip-1.42262pt^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\big)+\big(\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262pt2\nu\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\big)\hskip-1.42262pt^{\top}\hskip-1.42262pt{\boldsymbol{x}}.

Clearly,
\tilde{f}_{0},\tilde{f}_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}^{w}
and they coincide with the functions constructed in
Theorems

23
and
24
, respectively.
Therefore, the claimed upper bound on
\lVert f_{w}-f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}
follows directly.

Next, we approximate the quadratic function
q
by a max-affine function formed as the maximum of the first-order Taylor approximations of
q
at the points in
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
.
Define
\hat{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}}-\lVert\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}+2\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{x}}
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, by the
\epsilon
-covering property of
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
, we have
q({\boldsymbol{x}})-\hat{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert^{2}\in[0,\epsilon^{2}]
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Let
\delta\doteq\min\{\lambda,\nu\epsilon\}
and
m_{w}\in\{m_{0},m_{1}\}
such that
f_{w}\doteq m_{w}-(\delta/\epsilon)q\in\{\tilde{f}_{0},\tilde{f}_{1}\}
satisfies
\lVert f_{w}-f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq 2\epsilon\delta
.
Define
f_{\Delta}\doteq m_{w}-(\delta/\epsilon)\hat{m}
, where
f_{\Delta}\in\mathcal{M}_{k_{0}}^{\Delta}
.
The second claim then follows by using the triangle inequality as
\lVert f_{\Delta}-f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq\lVert f_{\Delta}-f_{w}\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}+\lVert f_{w}-f\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq(\delta/\epsilon)\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon\delta=3\epsilon\delta
.

For estimator design in regression, weakly max-affine functions were studied by
Sun and Yu 2019
, although without establishing convergence rates.
Using delta-max-affine functions,
Siahkamari et al. 2020
proposed an estimator and proved a suboptimal convergence rate for the case in which the regression function is delta-convex.
Our proof techniques do not apply to either of these function classes, for the reasons explained at the end of
Section

5.2
.
Nevertheless,
Corollary

25
may be useful for extending these developments and designing near-minimax estimators for smooth regression functions, which lies beyond the scope of this work.

The derivation in

The techniques presented in

yield bounds on differences between pairs of bias parameters, rather than on individual ones.
To decouple these bounds, we apply the following result.

Lemma 26
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Define
\bar{a}\doteq(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
and
\bar{b}\doteq(1/m)\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}
.
Suppose that
\bar{a}+\bar{b}=0
, and that there exist
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
such that
\max_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}|a_{i}-b_{j}-c|\leq\beta
.
Then,
\max\big\{\max_{i\in[n]}|a_{i}-c/2|,\max_{j\in[m]}|b_{j}+c/2|\big\}\leq 3\beta/2
.

Proof.

By Jensens’s inequality, we get
|\bar{a}-\bar{b}-c|\leq\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|a_{i}-b_{j}-c|\leq\beta
.
Combining this with
\bar{a}=-\bar{b}
yields
|\bar{a}-c/2|\leq\beta/2
and
|\bar{b}+c/2|\leq\beta/2
.
Then, by using the reverse triangle and Jensen’s inequalities, we get for all
i\in[n]
that
|a_{i}-c/2|-|\bar{b}+c/2|\leq|a_{i}-\bar{b}-c|\leq\beta
, which implies
\max_{i\in[n]}|a_{i}-c/2|\leq 3\beta/2
.
The other claim,
\max_{j\in[m]}|b_{j}+c/2|\leq 3\beta/2
, follows analogously.

Similarly to the proof of
Lemma

18
, one can show that
|b^{+}_{1,k}-b^{+}_{2,l}-y_{0}\big|\leq\beta_{0}
for all
k\in\mathcal{I}_{n,1}^{+}
and
l\in\mathcal{I}_{n,2}^{+}
.
Then, we separate these bounds using
Lemma

26
with
\bar{b}_{n,1}^{+}+\bar{b}_{n,2}^{+}=0
.

The bias parameters of
f_{n}
can be centered to satisfy
0=\sum_{k\in[K]}b_{n,k}+b^{\prime}_{n,k}
without changing the function.
Analogously to the proof of
Lemma

14
, we get
|b_{n,k}-b^{\prime}_{n,k}-y_{0}|=O(\beta_{1})
for all
k\in[K]
.
To extend this to all pairs, we use the constraints in (
35
).
Specifically, for each pair
k,l\in[K]
, we have the constraints
b_{k}\geq b_{l}+\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{l})^{\top}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}
and
b_{l}\geq b_{k}+\phi_{\triangleright}({\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{l},{\boldsymbol{\hat{X}}}_{k})^{\top}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{l}
, which imply
|b_{n,k}-b_{n,l}|=O\big(\tau_{\phi_{\triangleright}}R_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\max_{k^{\prime}\in[K]}\lVert{\boldsymbol{w}}_{n,k^{\prime}}\rVert\big)=O(\beta_{1})
.
The same reasoning applies to
\{b^{\prime}_{n,k}:k\in[K]\}
.
We then use the triangle inequality to bound the distance between all pairs as
|b_{n,k}-b^{\prime}_{n,l}-y_{0}|=O(\beta_{1})
for all
k,l\in[K]

Lemma

26
.
The remaining details are straightforward and omitted for brevity.

6.2
Adapting to convex shape-restricted regression

We now consider the setting of convex (shape-restricted) regression, where the
\lambda_{*}
-Lipschitz regression function
f_{*}
is known to be convex.
The goal is to estimate
f_{*}
with a convex function.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{cvx}}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}\doteq\big\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}:f\textrm{ is convex}\big\}
denote the set of
\lambda
-Lipschitz, convex functions over a convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}})
denote an arbitrary but fixed subgradient of
f
at
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We consider the statistical model (
1
), modified so that
f_{*}\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{cvx}}_{\lambda_{*},\mathcal{X}_{*}}
, for an unknown Lipschitz constant
\lambda_{*}>0
and an unknown convex domain
\mathcal{X}_{*}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In this setting, DCF (
Algorithm

2
) can be applied by restricting
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
to convex functions, where the center points
\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}\doteq\{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{K}\}
are computed by AFPC (
Algorithm

1
).

The max-affine representation is by far the most widely used in convex regression.
DCF can be readily adapted to this case by disabling the “norm feature” and restricting
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
to the class of max-affine functions as

\mathcal{M}_{K}=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}):{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}^{\top}=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}^{\top}\,\,v_{f,k}],\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,v_{f,k}=0,\,k\in[K]\Big\},

which holds for all
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
.

To adapt the proof of
Theorem

1
to the convex regression setting, we replace
Theorem

2
with the following approximation result, which slightly extends the result of
Balázs 2022
.

Theorem 27
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
be an
\epsilon
-cover of a convex set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{cvx}}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
for some Lipschitz constant
\lambda>0
, and let
\hat{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\doteq\max_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})+\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
,

0\leq f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\hat{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq 2\lambda\epsilon,\quad\qquad\hat{m}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})=f(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}),\quad\qquad\hat{m}\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{cvx}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d}}\cap\mathcal{M}_{|\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}|}.

Proof.

The convexity of
f
directly implies
0\leq f-\hat{m}
.
Moreover, since
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
, we get for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
that

f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\hat{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})^{\top}({\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\leq 2\lambda\min_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rVert,

(38)

which implies
f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\hat{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq 2\lambda\epsilon
for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
by the
\epsilon
-covering property of
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
.
Furthermore, choosing
{\boldsymbol{x}}=\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
in (
38
), we get
f(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})-\hat{m}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\leq 0
, implying the second claim.
The third claim follows because the max function is
1
-Lipschitz, and
\lVert\nabla f(\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}})\rVert\leq\lambda
for all
\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}
as
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mathcal{X}}
.

Since
Theorem

27
delivers the same
O(\lambda\epsilon)
approximation accuracy as
Theorem

2
, one may apply DCF with the class of max-affine functions
\mathcal{M}_{K}
to achieve the near-minimax rate of
Theorem

1
in the convex regression setting.
This matches the theoretical guarantees of the APCNLS algorithm of
Balázs 2022
, but unlike APCNLS, DCF does not require knowledge of the Lipschitz constant
\lambda_{*}
.
Moreover, the optimization problem (
3
) in DCF uses
K^{2}
constraints, which provides a substantial reduction in computational burden compared to the
nK
constraints used in APCNLS.

Motivated by the experimental results of
Section

3
, it may be beneficial to use a richer function class than the max-affine one.
In particular, one can allow
v_{f,k}\geq 0
for all
k\in[K]
instead of enforcing
v_{f,k}=0
as in (
6.2
), which still ensures that the set
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleright}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
is restricted to convex functions for all
\triangleright\in\{1,2,\infty\}
.
Moreover, one can restrict the set
\mathcal{F}_{+}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})
to convex functions using the following simple result:

Lemma 28
.

Fix
\hat{x},u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, and define
f(x)\doteq(x-\hat{x})_{+}u+(\hat{x}-x)_{+}v
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
f
is convex over
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
u\geq-v
.

Proof.

Write
f(x)=(x-\hat{x})_{+}(u+v)-\big((x-\hat{x})_{+}-(\hat{x}-x)_{+}\big)v=(x-\hat{x})_{+}(u+v)-(x-\hat{x})v
which is convex over
\mathbb{R}
if
u+v\geq 0
and concave otherwise by the convexity of
(\cdot)_{+}
.

Then, by
Lemma

28
, the above mentioned restriction to convex functions can be formalized by using an extra linear constraint as

\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\textrm{cvx}}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K})\doteq\Big\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{+}(\hat{\mathcal{X}}_{K}):{\boldsymbol{w}}_{f,k}^{\top}=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}^{\top}\,\,{\boldsymbol{v}}_{f,k}^{\top}],\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k}\geq-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{f,k},\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{f,k},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{f,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,k\in[K]\Big\}.

A detailed analysis of when these extended convex function classes provide performance gains compared to max-affine functions is left for future research.

7
Conclusions

We introduced the polynomial-time DCF algorithm, which decomposes the nonparametric estimation of a Lipschitz function into three stages: a partitioning step, a convex initial fitting step over the resulting partition, and an optional refinement step applied to the initial solution.
As shown in
Theorem

1
, DCF achieves the adaptive near-minimax rate in our setting, capturing the doubling dimension of the covariate space without relying on external model selection techniques
(
Bartlett et al. 2002
, e.g.,)
.

Our empirical results show that DCF, when equipped with an appropriately chosen regularization parameter
\theta_{2}
, can be competitive with state-of-the-art methods and can outperform other theoretically justified algorithms.
However, its sensitivity to
\theta_{2}
, together with its computationally intensive training procedure, highlights an important direction for future research.

Quantum Critical Dynamics Induced by Topological Zero Modes
Ilia Komissarov, Tobias Holder, Raquel Queiroz

Abstract
We investigate the low-frequency ac transport in the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain with chiral disorder near the topological delocalization transition. Our key finding is that the formation of hybridized pairs of topological domain wall zero modes leads to the anomalous logarithmic scaling of the ac conductivity σ ⁡ ( ω ) ∼ log ⁡ ω \sigma(\omega)\sim\log\omega at criticality, and σ ⁡ ( ω ) ∼ ω 2 ​ δ ​ log 2 ​ ω \sigma(\omega)\sim\omega^{2\delta}\log^{2}\omega away from it. Using the combination of real-space renormalization group analysis and qualitative hybridization arguments, we demonstrate that the form of the scaling of ac conductivity at criticality stems directly from the stretched-exponential ( ψ ⁡ ( x ) ∼ e − s ​ x \psi(x)\sim e^{-s\sqrt{x}} ) spatial decay of zero-mode wavefunctions at the critical point.



Introduction.—

Transport phenomena in disordered topological insulators exhibit behavior strikingly different from their trivial counterparts. For example, some subset of electronic states may become delocalized even in low-dimensional systems — a prominent example being the quantum Hall plateau transition
Evers and Mirlin 2008
;
Huckestein 1995
;
KRAMER et al. 2005
;
Pruisken 1984
which, despite many years of research, is still poorly understood
Das Sarma 2007
. A simpler and more tractable model that displays similar behavior is the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain
Su et al. 1979
with chiral disorder, i.e. with disorder in hopping elements only. In this model, exactly one mode at zero energy becomes delocalized when the geometric averages of even and odd hopping elements are equal
Theodorou and Cohen 1976
;
Altland et al. 2015
.

Many aspects of quantum critical behavior in this model and the ones in the same universality class were extensively studied
Evangelou 1986
;
Inui et al. 1994
;
Soukoulis and Economou 1981
;
Zhang and Kamenev 2023
;
Balents and Fisher 1997
;
Gogolin 1988
, and observed experimentally in disordered atomic wires
Meier et al. 2018
. The renormalization group flow equations were solved analytically
Fisher 1992
;
Fisher 1995
providing access to exact results such as the low-energy density of states and critical exponents. For the continuous
1D
fermion model with random mass, characterized by the same fixed point, it has been shown that at criticality, charge carriers exhibit a logarithmically slow spreading of probability density
Bouchaud et al. 1990
;
Bagrets et al. 2016
. In this work, we obtain a remarkable manifestation of this behavior in the form of the logarithmic scaling of low-frequency ac conductivity

\sigma_{\rm crit}(\omega)\sim\log(\Omega/\omega)\,,

(1)

where
\Omega
is a high-energy reference scale.
This form of ac conductivity is highly atypical: as the frequency
\omega
is decreased, we would expect the number of states participating in transport to decrease polynomially due to Pauli blocking. This would lead to insulating behavior as it occurs in the chain with purely on-site disorder (Anderson model) where
\sigma_{\rm MB}(\omega)\sim\omega^{2}\log^{2}\omega\,,

according to the Mott-Berezinskiy law
Mott and Davis 2012
;
Abrikosov and Ryzhkin 1978
;
Berezinskiĭ 1996
. Instead, in the case of the SSH chain with chiral disorder and at criticality, the divergence of the density of the in-gap states induced by the fluctuations in the winding number introduced by impurities (Dyson singularity
Evers and Mirlin 2008
;
Eggarter and Riedinger 1978
) compensates for the Pauli blocking effects, and the dc conductivity diverges. This divergence is, however, only logarithmic in frequency, which indicates the tunneling regime of electronic propagation with the vanishing Drude weight, in contrast to the “free acceleration”
\sigma(\omega)\sim\omega^{-1}
form
Resta 2018
typical in conventional metals. Therefore, the appearance of (
1
) highlights a remarkable situation in which the topology almost exactly counteracts the localizing effects of disorder giving rise to logarithmically slow “glassy” behavior of the ac conductivity. We note that using chiral disorder is crucial for this behavior as on-site impurities would lower the symmetry to the topologically trivial Anderson model without a divergent density of states.

The expressions Eqs. (

2

Mott and Davis 2012
, who proposed that special rare resonating states dominate the ac conductivity in the Anderson model. We demonstrate that in the SSH chain with chiral disorder, we can similarly identify a relevant subset of states between which the matrix element of the dipole operator diverges at small
\omega

\psi_{+}
) and anti-bonding (
\psi_{-}
) orbitals formed by hybridizing pairs of topological zero modes (see

and
\ket{\psi_{-}}
at low

\log\omega

\log^{2}\omega

Balents and Fisher 1997
. The scaling laws for the transition amplitude between
\ket{\psi_{\pm}}

1

).

We note that dynamical properties similar to Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
2

Del Maestro et al. 2010
, many-body localized phases
Gopalakrishnan et al. 2015

1D

Motrunich et al. 2001

Our objective is to highlight the importance of topological zero modes and their hybridized pairs in the low-frequency dynamical behavior of disordered critical topological systems. Using this approach, we are also able to obtain the transport properties at relatively high energy scales, which are inaccessible for the renormalization group techniques usually employed to study this critical point. In particular, a smooth crossover between the regimes Eq. (
1

2

In the following, we study the low-frequency dynamics of the SSH chain with bond dilution as an intuitive example elucidating the role of the resonances of zero modes in transport. The case of continuous bond disorder distributions follows from similar considerations. Finally, we present analytical and numerical evidence that at criticality the transition amplitude between the Mott resonances scales as
\log^{2}\omega

1

Conclusion.—

Studying the hybridization of topological zero modes in the SSH chain with chiral disorder, we demonstrated the emergence of a sub-diffusive metallic phase characterized by an activated scaling of the ac conductivity [Eq. (
1
)]. Remarkably, this behavior is a consequence of the type of wave function decay
only
- since the latter determines both the dipole transition amplitude between the Mott resonances and the scaling of the density of states entering the expression for the conductivity in Eq. (
7
). The latter statement comes from the realization that the scaling
\psi_{0}\sim e^{-s\sqrt{x}}
, according to our hybridization argument, fixes the length of the typical critical state at energy
\omega
to be
d(\omega)\sim\log^{2}\omega
. This expression, in turn, dictates the typical number of modes with
|E|<\omega
, i.e.,
n(\omega)\sim d(\omega)^{-1}\sim\log^{-2}\omega
, and therefore, the Dyson singularity is reproduced
\nu(E)\sim dn(E)/dE\sim|E|^{-1}\log^{-3}|E|
.

We show that the argument originally introduced by Mott to determine the ac conductivity of Anderson insulators can be extended to the critical sub-diffusive metal phase of the SSH chain, which is described by an infinite randomness fixed point. In this case, the dynamics is dominated by the pairwise hybridization of zero modes.
The recognition of the crucial role played by the topological in-gap states in this transition opens a new perspective on the microscopic nature of disorder-induced topological phase transitions, such as the quantum Hall plateau transition or critical delocalization in disordered Chern insulators.
Since the appearance of localized in-gap states around strong impurities is a ubiquitous feature of topological phases in any dimension
Queiroz et al. 2024
, we anticipate that their resonant hopping will also play a crucial role in determining the existence and dynamics of topological critical points in higher spatial dimensions, which we plan to explore in future work.

Appendix S2
Wavefunctional decay of zero modes in the SSH chain with chiral disorder

In this section, we review the properties of the zero-energy states in the SSH chain with chiral disorder showing that they decay exponentially at long distances when the system is away from criticality, and stretched-exponentially (as
\psi\sim e^{-s\sqrt{x}}
) at the critical point.

We consider the Schrodinger equation
\hat{H}_{\rm SSH}\ket{\psi}=0
with the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\rm SSH}=\sum_{i}t_{i}^{\prime}\ket{i,\rm B}\bra{i,\rm A}+t_{i}\ket{i+1,\rm B}\bra{i,\rm A}+{\rm h.c.}\,.

(S11)

Using the real-space representation

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i}\left(\psi_{i\rm A}\ket{i,\rm A}+\psi_{i\rm B}\ket{i,\rm B}\right)
, we obtain

\displaystyle t_{i}^{\prime}\psi_{i\rm A}+t_{i}\psi_{i-1\rm A}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(S12)

\displaystyle t_{i}^{\prime}\psi_{i\rm B}+t_{i}\psi_{i+1\rm B}

\displaystyle=0\,.

By iterating the above expressions, the growth(decay) rates of the zero modes localized on A and B sub-lattices are found

\xi_{0\rm B}^{-1}=-\xi_{0\rm A}^{-1}=\frac{1}{N}\log\left|\psi_{N\rm A}/\psi_{1\rm A}\right|=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}^{N}\log\left|t_{i}^{\prime}/t_{i}\right|\,.

(S13)

Since the sum of the random quantities on the right-hand side is self-averaging, we can introduce the typical localization length of the mode localized on the B sub-lattice as defined by the law of large numbers

\xi_{0}^{-1}=\left\llangle\log\left|t^{\prime}_{i}/t_{i}\right|\right\rrangle\,.

(S14)

If the expression on the right-hand side is finite, the zero mode decays exponentially on average. If, on the other hand, the right-hand side vanishes, the exponent of the wavefunction, as we can see from (
S13
), undergoes a random walk with zero mean. The spatial decay is stretched-exponential, and characterized by a new length scale
s
defined in (
8
):

\left\llangle|\log\psi_{N}|^{2}\right\rrangle-\left\llangle|\log\psi_{N}|\right\rrangle^{2}=\sqrt{\left\llangle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\left|t_{i}^{\prime}/t_{i}\right|\right)^{2}\right\rrangle}=s\sqrt{N}\,,\qquad s=\llangle\log^{2}|t_{i}^{\prime}/t_{i}|\rrangle-\llangle\log|t_{i}^{\prime}/t_{i}|\rrangle^{2}\,.

(S15)

Therefore, we recover the average stretched-exponential zero mode behavior
\psi(x)\sim e^{-s\sqrt{x}}
. The qualifier
average
is extremely important here, as the critical wavefunctions would typically exhibit multiple minima and maxima due to the fluctuations in the winding number. As we are interested mainly in disorder-averaged low-frequency transport properties, the leading stretched-exponential part of the wavefunction profile suffices.

Appendix S3
Mott resonances in the bond-diluted SSH chain

In this section, we obtain analytically the hybridization energy of the zero modes in the bond-diluted SSH chain model. We, therefore, consider (
S11
), with the hoppings missing with a probability

from the unperturbed chain with parameters

t^{\prime}

Figure S4:

Two zero modes (

|t^{\prime}|<|t|

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\rm L}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}e^{-i/\xi_{0}}\ket{i,\rm A}\,,

(S16)

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\rm R}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}e^{(i-d)/\xi_{0}}\ket{i,\rm B}\,,

where

, and
d,\xi_{0}
assumed large. By repeatedly using the decimation procedure outlined in the next Appendix, the Hamiltonian of the segment is reduced to

\hat{H}_{0}=t^{\prime}(t^{\prime}/t)^{d}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{0\rm A}\hat{c}_{d\rm B}+{\rm h.c.}\,.

(S17)

Note that the parameter is
(t^{\prime}/t)^{d}
is always small for sufficiently long sections, which justifies the application of the perturbation theory to obtain the low-lying in energy states. Using degenerate perturbation theory, the energies of the hybridized states

are found as

E_{\pm}\simeq\pm\braket{\psi_{\rm L}|\hat{H}_{0}|\psi_{\rm R}}=\pm 2t^{\prime}/\xi_{0}(t^{\prime}/t)^{d}=\pm 2t^{\prime}/\xi_{0}e^{-d/\xi_{0}}\,.

(S18)

Therefore, in the bond-diluted SSH chain, the energy of the low-lying Mott resonances depends exponentially on their size

Appendix S4
Real-space renormalization group

In this section, we review the real-space renormalization group (RSRG) treatment of the SSH chain with chiral disorder (
S11
). The RSRG procedure consists of identifying the largest of the hoppings within the chain (suppose
t_{2}
) and decimating it using the second-order perturbation theory
Javan Mard et al. 2014
. Consider, e.g., the
t_{1}-t_{2}-t_{3}
cluster of hoppings described by the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\rm pert}\,,\qquad\hat{H}_{0}=t_{2}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{c}_{3}+{\rm h.c.}\,,\qquad\hat{H}_{\rm pert,}=t_{1}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{c}_{2}+t_{3}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{c}_{4}+{\rm h.c.}\,.

(S19)

The unperturbed eigenvalues are
E_{0}=\pm t_{2}
and the eigenstates read

\ket{\psi_{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{c}_{2}^{\dagger}\pm\hat{c}_{3}^{\dagger})\ket{0}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{2}\pm\ket{3})\,.

(S20)

Within the second-order perturbation theory, the effective hopping that replaces the given string
t_{1}-t_{2}-t_{3}
is obtained as

t_{\rm eff}=\frac{\braket{1|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|\psi_{+}}\braket{\psi_{+}|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|4}}{t_{2}}-\frac{\braket{1|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|\psi_{-}}\braket{\psi_{-}|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|4}}{t_{2}}=\frac{t_{1}t_{3}}{t_{2}}\,,

(S21)

since

\displaystyle\braket{1|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|\psi_{+}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\qquad\braket{1|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|\psi_{-}}=\frac{t_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(S22)

\displaystyle\braket{4|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|\psi_{+}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{3}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\qquad\braket{4|\hat{H}_{\rm pert}|\psi_{-}}=-\frac{t_{3}}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

The procedure (
S21
) is repeated multiple times until the maximum hopping in the chain falls below the cutoff
\Omega
. If we collectively label all odd links
t^{\prime}
and all even links
t
, their distributions
P_{t}\equiv P_{t}(t,\Omega)
and
P_{t^{\prime}}\equiv P_{t^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},\Omega)
will “run” with the cutoff according to

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial P_{t}}{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left[P_{t}^{\Omega}-P_{t^{\prime}}^{\Omega}\right]P_{t}+P_{t^{\prime}}^{\Omega}\int dt_{1}dt_{2}\,P_{t}(t_{1},\Omega)P_{t}(t_{2},\Omega)\delta\left(t-t_{1}t_{2}/\Omega\right),

(S23)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial P_{t^{\prime}}}{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left[P_{t^{\prime}}^{\Omega}-P_{t}^{\Omega}\right]P_{t^{\prime}}+P_{t}^{\Omega}\int dt_{1}^{\prime}dt_{2}^{\prime}\,P_{t^{\prime}}(t_{1}^{\prime},\Omega)P_{t^{\prime}}(t_{2}^{\prime},\Omega)\delta\left(t^{\prime}-t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime}/\Omega\right)\,,

where
P_{t}^{\Omega}\equiv P_{t}(\Omega,\Omega)
, and
P_{t^{\prime}}^{\Omega}\equiv P_{t^{\prime}}(\Omega,\Omega)
. This system of integro-differential equations was first solved by Fisher in both critical and off-critical cases
Fisher 1992
;
Fisher 1995
. We review these solutions and their implications below.

Off-critical case:

For convenience, we introduce logarithmic variables

\Gamma\equiv\log(\Omega_{0}/\Omega)\,,\qquad T\equiv\log(\Omega/t)\,,\qquad T^{\prime}\equiv\log(\Omega/t^{\prime})\,.

(S24)

If the hoppings
t
are on average larger than
t^{\prime}
, the fixed point is characterized by distributions

P_{t}(T)=2\delta\exp[-2\delta T]\,,\qquad P_{t^{\prime}}(T^{\prime})=2\delta e^{-2\delta\Gamma}\exp\left[-2\delta e^{-2\delta\Gamma}T^{\prime}\right]\,,

(S25)

i.e., the distribution for
T
(and therefore
t
) becomes cutoff-independent, while the distribution of
T^{\prime}
becomes extremely broad already on a logarithmic scale. It implies that extremely small and isolated in energy hoppings
t^{\prime}
appear giving rise to low-energy Mott-like resonances formed by the zero modes hybiridizing through small
t^{\prime}
bonds. The quantity
\delta
in the expressions above is introduced as

\delta\equiv\frac{\llangle T\rrangle-\llangle T^{\prime}\rrangle}{{\rm var}\,T+{\rm var}\,T^{\prime}}\,.

(S26)

The number of surviving unit cells at the scale
\Gamma
is described by the differential equation

dN=-N(P_{t}+P_{t}^{\prime})\,,

(S27)

as the act of decimation always removes one unit cell. Integration of this equation yields
N\sim\Omega^{-2\delta}
. The low-energy density of states at energy
E
is found as
\nu(E)\sim dN^{-1}(E)/dE\sim E^{2\delta-1}
. Since almost all decimated hoppings are
t
-hoppings, on the approach to the fixed point, the
t^{\prime}
hoppings become typically very long, and their lengths are therefore approximately equal to the typical length of the unit cell, i.e.,
d_{t^{\prime}}\sim N^{-1}\sim\Omega^{2\delta}
. The typical
t^{\prime}
hopping itself can be found using the second distribution in (
S25
), as

t_{\rm typ}^{\prime}\sim\exp(\braket{T^{\prime}}_{\rm D})\sim\exp(-\Omega^{2\delta})\sim e^{-d_{t^{\prime}}}\,.

(S28)

Therefore, the hybridization energy of the zero modes across such a hopping element is exponentially small in its length.

Critical case:

The distributions of
T
and
T^{\prime}

P_{t}(T)=\frac{1}{\Gamma}e^{-T/\Gamma}\,,\qquad P_{t^{\prime}}(T^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\Gamma}e^{-T^{\prime}/\Gamma}

(S29)

both become extremely broad at low cutoff. The low-energy Mott resonances in this case arise on both
t
and
t^{\prime}
hoppings. Solving (
S27
) in this case, yields the scaling of the remaining number of unit cells
N\sim\log^{-2}\Omega
, and therefore,
d_{t^{\prime}}\sim d_{t}\sim\log^{2}\Omega
due to the
t\leftrightarrow t^{\prime}
duailty manifest in (
S29
). The low-energy density of states is described by Dyson singularity
\nu(E)\sim dN(E)/dE\sim(E\log^{3}E)^{-1}
. Lastly, using (
S29
), we establishes that the remaining at low
\Omega
hoppings are typically stretched-exponentially dependent on their lengths

t_{\rm typ}^{\prime}\sim t_{\rm typ}\sim\exp(\braket{T}_{\rm D})\sim e^{-\Gamma}\sim e^{-\sqrt{d}}\,.

(S30)

We note here that is useful to define the dynamical critical exponent
z
as the polynomial part of the scaling of the typical length of the excitation with the time over which it occurs:
d^{z}\sim(1/\omega)
. From the discussion above, we obtain
z=1/(2\delta)
. The polynomial part of the scaling of the density of states at small energies then reads
\nu(E)\sim E^{-1+1/z}
. Since the low-frequency ac conductivity is given by the pair-wise summation over the symmetric in energy transitions, as shown in (
7
), the ac conductivity is itself proportional to the density of states, and hence,
\sigma(\omega)\sim\omega^{1/z}
, up to logarithmic corrections, in consistency with (
1
), where
z=\infty
, and (
2
).

Appendix S5
Mapping to other models

The mapping between the
1D
Ising models and the
1D
tight-binding models of Fermions is well-known
McKenzie 1996
. The details of this mapping that apply to the case of an SSH chain with chiral disorder are reviewed below. Additionally, at the critical point, the properties of these lattice models are identical to the continuous model of the Dirac fermions with random mass studied extensively earlier. This allows making a host of exact statements about the properties of the SSH chain at and around criticality.

The starting point is the
XX
Ising model in
1D

\hat{H}_{\rm Ising}=\sum_{i}J_{i}[\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{x}\hat{\sigma}_{i+1}^{x}+\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{y}\hat{\sigma}_{i+1}^{y}]\,,

(S31)

where
J_{i\,\rm odd}=(t^{\prime}-\epsilon_{j})/2
with random
\epsilon_{i}
and
J_{i\,\rm even}=t/2
. Using the Jordan-Wigner transformation

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{x}\hat{\sigma}_{i+1}^{x}=\left(\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i+1}+\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i+1}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}\right)\,,

(S32)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{y}\hat{\sigma}_{i+1}^{y}=\left(\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i+1}-\hat{c}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{i+1}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}\right)\,,

it is straightforward to show that the Hamiltonian (
S31
) is equivalent to the one for the SSH chain with chiral disorder (
3
). The dynamical spin conductivity in the model (
S31
) was studied in
Motrunich et al. 2001
, where it was shown that away from the criticality (random dimer phase)

\sigma(\omega)\sim s^{-2}\omega^{1/z}\log^{2}\omega\,,

(S33)

where
z
is the dynamical critical exponent. At criticality,
Motrunich et al. 2001
find

\sigma_{\rm crit}(\omega)\sim s^{-2}\log\omega\,.

(S34)

These expressions agree with (
2
), (
1
), once we set
z=(2\delta)^{-1}
, as shown below. It is noteworthy to point out that the states giving rise to the low-frequency spin transport in this case are the hybridized domain wall states appearing on both sides of the rare regions created by the disorder in magnetic couplings. As an example, these rare regions may arise due to the large incorporations of the even-bond-ordered phase into the predominantly odd-bond-ordered environment. Such hybridized domain walls are non-local duals of the Mott resonances described in the main text.

Some further exact analytical results for the SSH chain follow from the low-energy theory for the Hamiltonian (
3
) expanded around
k_{\rm F}=\pi

\hat{H}_{\rm cont}=\sum_{x}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{x}[iv_{\rm F}\hat{\sigma}_{2}\partial_{x}+(t^{\prime}-t-\varepsilon_{x})\hat{\sigma}_{1}]\,\hat{c}_{x}\,,

(S35)

where
v_{\rm F}=t
. In the extensive studies of this model summarized in
McKenzie 1996
it was established that away from criticality,
\nu(E)\sim E^{-1+2\delta}
and the dynamical critical exponent
z=(2\delta)^{-1}
. At criticality (
\delta=1
), the density of states takes the form of the Dyson singularity
\nu(E)\sim s^{2}[|E|\log^{3}(s^{2}/|E|)]^{-1}
. The results found within this continuum model apply to the full tight-binding case (
3
) when the dimerization is small and the disorder is low, i.e.
t\gg|t-t^{\prime}|
,
s^{2}\ll 1
.

Appendix S6
General scaling considerations

The result
\sigma_{\rm crit}(\omega)\sim\log\omega
was obtained in the main text by applying general arguments regarding the hybridization of localized wavefunctions originally used by Mott to determine dynamical conductivity in single-band models
Mott and Davis 2012
and can be generalized to materials of higher dimensionality
Ivanov et al. 2009
;
Ivanov et al. 2012
. We may ask, therefore, under which conditions such a tunneling scaling of ac conductivity may hold in a generic system. To address this question, let us consider the family of impurity wavefunctions decaying as
\psi({\bf r})\sim e^{-r^{1/\alpha}}
at large distances, with
\alpha
being a free parameter to be constrained. In order to remove the coefficient
\omega
in the expression for the conductivity (
6
), the density of states should assume the form
\nu(E)\sim(|E|\log^{\beta}|E|)^{-1}
.
Graphene with chiral disorder, for instance, has a DOS singularity of this type
Gade 1993
. Assuming chiral symmetry and performing the pairwise symmetric summation as in (
7
), we obtain a general scaling criterion,

\sigma(\omega)\sim\log^{2\alpha-\beta}\omega\,,

(S36)

i.e., the requirement
\alpha>\beta/2
must hold in order to obtain a sub-diffusive metal with purely activated scaling at low
\omega
. For the SSH chain,
\alpha=2
and
\beta=3
, which satisfies this criterion.
It will be interesting to investigate the phenomenology imposed by (
S36
) in other disordered, topological systems, which is a subject of future work.

Early life circumstances and their impact on health in adulthood and later life: a systematic review
Authors: Weslley Barbosa Sales, Eunice Fernandes Maranhão, Caroline Sousa Truta Ramalho, Sabrina Gabrielle Gomes Fernandes Macêdo, Gérson Fonseca Souza, Álvaro Campos Cavalcanti Maciel
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCertain situations and contexts during early years of life and childhood can have a significant impact on the health of older individuals. Studies have demonstrated that adversities such as poverty, neglect, abuse, and exposure to chronic stress conditions during the early years of life are associated with a range of health problems in adulthood.ObjectiveTo systematically identify the literature on the impact of circumstances and/or conditions in early life/childhood on the health of older adults.Materials and methodsA systematic literature review guided by the recommendations of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) was conducted. The databases used were PUBMED, LILACS, Scielo, Embase, and Web of Science. Longitudinal studies published without language or publication date restrictions, related to the proposed theme, were included. Editorial letters, review protocols, reflective studies, literature reviews, and articles without available online abstracts or full texts were excluded. The quality assessment of the studies was conducted using tools and guidelines provided by the Joanna Briggs Institute. The database search conducted between May and August 2023 resulted in 8,224 articles found. After completing the steps of duplicate exclusion and reading titles and abstracts, 35 articles were selected for full reading, culminating in a final sample of 10 eligible articles for this review.ResultsThese studies highlighted various early-life circumstances, including socioeconomic status, exposure to adverse childhood experiences, and environmental factors, demonstrating significant associations with health outcomes in older adults. The findings collectively emphasize the critical role that early adversities play in influencing chronic diseases, mental health, and overall well-being in later life.Final considerationsUnderstanding social determinants, early-life adversities, and socioeconomic factors is essential for promoting healthy and equitable aging, with effective interventions and public policies aimed at reducing inequalities and ensuring the well-being of the adults and older adults.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-024-05571-4.Keywords: Older adults, Early Life adversities, Age, Health disparities, Public Health

Introduction

Robust evidence shows that negative experiences in early childhood, such as inadequate nutrition, exposure to environmental toxins, neglect, low socioeconomic status, abuse, or chronic stress, can have long-term adverse consequences. These factors can trigger a range of negative health effects over the years, including an increased risk of non-communicable chronic diseases, such as diabetes and cardiovascular diseases [1–4]. Additionally, childhood adversities impact cognitive and emotional development, leading to learning difficulties, emotional challenges, and reduced resilience in facing future hardships. This directly affects the ability to cope with stress and adversity in adulthood, with indirect effects on physical and mental health [5–7]. Adverse experiences can also accelerate biological aging, as indicated by signs of faster cellular aging, such as shorter telomeres and epigenetic alterations [8].

Theoretically, the analysis of the impacts of these adversities can be interpreted through different conceptual models. Social determinant models posit that these unequal health outcomes are primarily caused by structural inequalities [4]. In this sense, factors such as childhood poverty and social inequality continue to exert negative effects throughout life, leading to cumulative disadvantages in terms of physical function later in life [4, 5]. On the other hand, health disparity models suggest that social and contextual factors act through individual behaviors and biological mechanisms, such as impaired emotional and cognitive development, which affect the ability to cope with stress and adversity in adulthood, directly impacting physical and mental health. Finally, multiple stressor models incorporate environmental agents, such as exposure to toxins, and emphasize the intermediary role of chronic stressors and other environmental factors in aggravating health risks throughout life [4–9].

Investing in prevention and intervention strategies is essential through public health programs that promote child nutrition, provide safe and stable environments, encourage physical activity, identify cases of neglect and abuse, and offer emotional support [8, 9]. Furthermore, raising awareness and education are crucial to inform parents, caregivers, and healthcare professionals about the impacts of early childhood adversities on future health, encouraging positive changes from the earliest years of life [9, 10].

Lastly, this study is justified by the lack of systematic reviews that specifically investigate the impact of early-life adversities on the overall health of older adults. The results obtained are significant in informing policies and interventions that promote healthy aging, considering the long-lasting impact of early adversities on the health of the older population [9–11]. In light of this information, the aim of this study is to systematically identify the literature on the impact of circumstances and/or conditions experienced in early life on the health of older adults, with practical implications for the development of public health policies and practices that foster healthy aging and enhance population resilience [10, 11].

Methodology

This study adopts a systematic literature review approach, following the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [12]. The registration in PROSPERO has been completed, with the approval code: CRD42023431078.

Research question identification

In the search for the research question, the PCC (Population, Concept, Context) strategy was used, a methodology proposed by the Joanna Briggs Institute [13]. It was defined as follows: Population (individuals aged 40 years or older); Concept (impact of circumstances and/or conditions in early life/childhood on the health of older adults); Context (community, hospital, outpatient or home-based settings). The research question was formulated as: “What is the impact of circumstances and/or conditions in early life/childhood on the health of older adults?” The keywords “Aged” and “Social Determinants of Health” were used and are indexed in the Health Sciences Descriptors/ Medical Subject Headings - DeCS/MeSH. It is noteworthy that, in our study, we referred to “early years” as the period from birth to five years of age [14].

Identification of relevant studies

This stage was based on meetings with the research team, which defined the planned approach, study eligibility criteria, selected databases, as well as the search strategy with their respective descriptors. The eligibility criteria were: complete longitudinal studies published without language or publication date restrictions, in which the impact of circumstances and/or conditions in early life/childhood on the health of older adults was highlighted.

The adopted exclusion criteria included cross-sectional and experimental studies, editorial letters, review protocols, reflective studies, general literature reviews, and articles without available abstracts and/or full text online.

The identification of studies was conducted through an electronic search in the following databases: PUBMED, Latin American and Caribbean Health Sciences Literature (LILACS), Scielo, and Web of Science. The selection of these databases was due to their comprehensive coverage of international and national studies, with public access or available through a library, as well as their extensive collection of manuscripts related to the topic.

Furthermore, a comprehensive review was conducted on all reference lists of included studies to identify additional relevant articles. To ensure the inclusion of all pertinent information, searches were performed across various sources of grey literature, such as thesis and dissertation databases, proceedings of national and international conferences, as well as reference lists of selected articles, in order to identify studies, reports, and conference abstracts relevant to this review.

For the analysis of grey literature, an additional researcher (CSTR) was invited. This researcher was informed about the research objectives and the descriptor used in order to independently conduct a search in the grey literature.

Selection of studies

The study selection occurred in two distinct phases. In the first phase, a review of titles and abstracts was conducted using the Rayyan platform (https://rayyan.qcri.org). During this stage, all duplicate articles were identified and removed. In the second phase, the titles and abstracts of articles considered eligible were reviewed, and subsequently, the full texts of studies selected in this phase were analyzed. In both stages, the process was conducted by two researchers (WBS and EFM), who were appropriately instructed on the eligibility criteria and responsible for reading and extracting the data. A third assessor (SGGFM) was assigned to resolve any issues or discrepancies related to the study selection.

Data extraction

The data were collected using a table developed by the research team, designed to extract relevant information such as authors, year of publication, publication location, results, intervention, and expected effects. This instrument underwent a preliminary test conducted by all reviewers to ensure the accuracy of the information obtained during its final application.

The data collection was conducted in pairs, where two reviewers (WBS and EFM) independently extracted data for all included studies. At the end, the data collections performed by each reviewer were compared to identify any discrepancies. If divergences were identified, they were reviewed and analyzed by the research team, ensuring consistency among the reviewers.

Methodological quality

The assessment of study quality was conducted using the tools and guidelines provided by the Joanna Briggs Institute [13]. The purpose of this assessment was to examine the methodological robustness of the studies and determine to what extent each study addressed the potential for bias in its design, conduct, and analysis.

Each study was classified based on the percentage of positive responses obtained. A high risk of bias was assigned when the study obtained up to 49% positive responses. A moderate risk was assigned when the study obtained 50 to 69% positive responses. A low risk was assigned when the study achieved more than 70% positive score. The assessment process was conducted independently by two reviewers (EFM and WBS). Any disagreements that arose were resolved through the intervention of another reviewer (CSTR).

Results

The database search was conducted between the months of May and August 2023. A total of 8,221 articles were found on the platforms used, and 3 articles from the grey literature search conducted in the CAPES Thesis and Dissertation Catalog. After the duplicate analysis, 124 studies were excluded, leaving 8,100 articles that had their titles and abstracts read. Out of these, 35 articles were selected for full-text reading. Following this step, a final sample of 10 articles was eligible for this review. Further details can be found in Fig. 1. The characteristics of the included studies are presented in Table 1.

Characteristics of the studies

A total of 143,382 individuals, including middle-aged adults and older adults aged 40 years and older, comprised the combined samples of the included studies, with the smallest sample consisting of 388 participants, while the largest sample analyzed 67,290 individuals. Regarding gender, all articles used participants of both sexes, and all were published after the year 2015. Additionally, it was observed that the shortest follow-up period was 2 years, while the longest lasted for 20 years (Table 1).

Adverse circumstance/condition

The literature has demonstrated that adversities such as poverty, neglect, abuse, and exposure to chronic stress conditions during the early years of life are associated with a range of health issues in adulthood [1–3]. Additionally, through this study, it was observed that factors like education [25], poverty [24], socioeconomic status [16–18], and social determinants [19–23] are also considered strong adversities in early life [26] that can lead to the development of long-term detrimental health conditions in these individuals (Table 1).

Health outcomes

Regarding health outcomes, the presence of conditions and/or non-communicable chronic diseases such as obesity, high cholesterol, high blood pressure, glucose intolerance, diabetes mellitus, mental disorders, and coronary diseases are associated with circumstances and/or conditions in early life/childhood in the health of older adults [18].

Furthermore, in this study, circumstances such as education, social support, poverty, socioeconomic status, and social determinants were associated with various health outcomes, such as cognitive decline [25], chronic stress [17], decline in executive function [19], functional impairment [22] and health disparities among ethnic groups [23].

Discussion

The findings show that childhood adversities—such as hunger, mistreatment, neglect, abuse, and chronic stress—are important factors in understanding health throughout life, as described by the Health Pendulum Conceptual Model. Most studies reviewed recognize that these early experiences can lead to chronic conditions in adulthood, though not all relevant factors are consistently examined. Chronic stress and neglect stand out as common issues, indicating that these adversities can have lasting effects, increasing the risk of non-communicable diseases and negatively impacting overall well-being later in life.

The studies included in this review addressed a broad spectrum of characteristics, adverse circumstances, and health outcomes in adults and older adults [1–3, 25]. These investigations highlighted that adversities such as poverty, neglect, abuse, and chronic stress in early life can lead to health issues in adulthood. Education, poverty, socioeconomic status, and social determinants also emerged as adversities associated with early years and linked to detrimental long-term health conditions [18, 24].

In terms of health outcomes, non-communicable chronic conditions, including obesity, hypertension, diabetes, and mental disorders, are linked to early life circumstances [16, 17, 20, 22]. Furthermore, factors such as education, social support, poverty, socioeconomic status, and social determinants have shown connections with various outcomes such as cognitive decline, chronic stress, functional impairment, and health disparities among ethnic groups [14, 18, 19, 21, 23].

As the population ages, understanding the determinants of health in advanced age becomes essential for promoting healthy and quality aging [4]. In this context, the impact of adverse circumstances experienced in early life and socioeconomic factors on the health of adults and older adults has received increasing attention [2]. This article comprehensively discusses the evidence related to this issue, emphasizing the increased risk of non-communicable chronic diseases stemming from these experiences. The hypothesis that early-life adversities and socioeconomic factors significantly influence the risk of developing non-communicable chronic diseases in later life has been accepted.

The scientific literature has consistently shown that adversities such as poverty, neglect, abuse, and exposure to chronic stress during the early years of life are associated with a range of health problems in adulthood [1–3]. Studies have identified a link between these adverse circumstances and an increased risk of non-communicable chronic diseases in older adults [5, 6]. For instance, childhood poverty can lead to a higher risk of obesity, high blood pressure, diabetes mellitus, and cardiovascular diseases in adulthood [24].

The effects of adverse circumstances in early life on the health of older adults can be explained by various mechanisms [17]. For example, exposure to chronic stress during childhood can have lasting effects on the endocrine and immune systems, leading to physiological changes that increase vulnerability to chronic diseases in adulthood [17]. Furthermore, these adversities can negatively influence lifestyle, such as inadequate dietary habits, lack of physical activity, and smoking, which are known risk factors for chronic diseases [8, 9, 14].

Socioeconomic factors play a crucial role in determining health throughout the lifespan [16, 18]. Education is one of these factors that has consistently been associated with various health outcomes in adulthood [25]. Individuals with higher levels of education have a lower risk of developing chronic diseases, better mental health, and a higher life expectancy [25].

Education can impact the health of adults and older adults in various ways [25]. Firstly, the knowledge acquired through formal education can empower individuals to make more informed decisions about their health and adopt healthy behaviors [11]. Furthermore, education is linked to better job opportunities and income, which can provide access to improved living conditions, suitable housing, and healthcare [25].

In addition to education, poverty [24] and low socioeconomic status [25] have been linked to a range of adverse health outcomes in the population. Poverty can limit access to nutritious food, healthcare services, and safe environments, thus increasing the risk of developing chronic diseases [24]. Moreover, a lack of financial resources can lead to higher levels of stress, anxiety, and depression, which are also associated with health issues in adulthood [25].

Social determinants also play a crucial role in explaining health disparities among ethnic groups [23]. Studies have demonstrated that minority ethnic groups face higher levels of discrimination, stigmatization, and socioeconomic inequalities, which can lead to worse health outcomes in adulthood [22, 23]. Corroborating this statement, the study by [23] highlights that individuals of Black ethnicity exhibit worsening health indicators compared to White individuals, underscoring the importance of addressing health disparities among ethnic groups to achieve health equity [24].

Furthermore, the presence of physical activity in childhood, particularly during the first five years of life, exerts a direct influence on overall health [14]. Regular physical activity during this critical developmental period not only contributes to better physical and mental well-being throughout life but is also closely linked to sociocultural and economic circumstances [14]. Investing in prevention and intervention strategies through public health programs that enhance child nutrition, provide safe and stable environments, identify cases of neglect and abuse, and offer emotional support is essential to ensure that children have access to opportunities for physical activity, which can mitigate some of the adverse health outcomes associated with socioeconomic disadvantages [8, 9].

The evidence highlighted in this discussion carries significant implications for public health and the development of intervention policies aimed at the older population. Understanding and addressing the social determinants of health are crucial for promoting health equity and improving health outcomes in advanced age [24].

Public policies should focus on reducing socioeconomic inequalities through measures such as promoting accessible and quality education, implementing income transfer programs, and strengthening social protection. Additionally, it is crucial to invest in strategies that promote social support and combat loneliness, such as creating community programs, encouraging support networks, and developing social engagement activities for older adults [26].

Raising awareness about the lasting effects of adverse circumstances experienced in early life on the health of older adults is also crucial. Health policies should focus on preventing these adversities through family support programs, investment in early childhood, and strategies to promote child well-being.

Strengths and limitations of the study

The current research addresses a significant gap in the literature, providing the first systematic review investigating the effects of socioeconomic and cultural factors on the health of older adults. The study encompassed an extensive and representative sample, totaling 148,791 older individuals, enabling a robust analysis. The obtained results revealed a substantial association between adversities faced in early life, such as education, poverty, socioeconomic status, and social determinants, and the manifestation of adverse long-term health conditions. This finding underscores the importance of considering not only immediate risk factors but also underlying socioeconomic factors as critical determinants for the health of older adults.

The findings of this research offer valuable insights into the implications of socioeconomic circumstances on the health of older adults. The analyzed health outcomes covered a wide spectrum, including cognitive decline, chronic stress, executive function decline, functional impairment, and health disparities among ethnic groups. The positive association between socioeconomic variables such as education, social support, poverty, socioeconomic status, and social determinants, and these outcomes highlights the need for interventions focused on addressing social inequalities to improve the health of older adults. It is noteworthy that the rigorous methodology employed in this systematic review resulted in a low risk of bias assessment for all included articles, with an average of 72.7–81.8% positive responses. This meticulous analysis strengthens the validity of the findings and enhances the reliability of the study’s conclusions.

Longitudinal studies necessitate extensive follow-up over time, which can lead to high financial and logistical costs. The duration of these studies can vary, making it challenging to obtain representative samples and maintain participant engagement over time. Consequently, a considerable scarcity of articles on this topic was observed. Furthermore, as participants age, there’s a possibility of attrition over the course of the study, leading to selection bias. Those who remain in the study may differ from those who drop out, potentially affecting sample representativeness and the generalizability of results.

Lastly, it’s important to note that longitudinal studies face the challenge of disentangling the effect of early-life circumstances or conditions from confounding factors. Other factors such as genetics, family environment, or lifestyle could influence both early life and adult health outcomes. Additionally, the relationship between early life and adult health might be bidirectional, with adult health also impacting early-life circumstances or conditions.

Final considerations

Early adversities and socioeconomic factors can influence health outcomes in adulthood, increasing the risk of developing chronic diseases. Additionally, social determinants such as social support and health disparities among ethnic groups also play a significant role in the health of older adults. The implementation of effective public policies and intervention programs is essential to address these determinants and promote healthy and equitable aging. Reducing socioeconomic inequalities, promoting education, strengthening social support, and preventing early adversities are key elements in this process.

Therefore, addressing early life adversities, socioeconomic factors, and social determinants is crucial to promote the health and well-being of older adults. This deeper understanding allows us to adopt a more comprehensive and holistic approach to promoting health in the older population, ensuring that everyone has the opportunity to age in a healthy and dignified manner.

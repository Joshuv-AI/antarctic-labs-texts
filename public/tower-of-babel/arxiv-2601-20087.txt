On Berwald Spaces with non-Zero Flag Curvature
A. Tayebi, B. Najafi

Abstract
We prove that every Berwald manifold with non-zero flag curvature is Riemannian. This result provides an extension of Numata and Szabó’s rigidity theorems. We show that every positively curved constant isotropic Berwald manifold is Riemannian or locally Minkowskian. Then, we prove that every compact strictly positive (or negative) isotropic Berwald manifold reduces to a Berwald manifold. Finally, we prove that every homogeneous isotropic Berwald metric is either locally Minkowskian, or Riemannian, or Berwald metric or Berwald-Randers metric generalizing result previously only known in the case of Randers metric. Keywords : Berwald manifold, flag curvature, mean Landsberg curvature, S S -curvature. 1 1 1 2013 Mathematics subject Classification: 53B40, 53C60.

Introduction

There are two types of curvatures in Finsler geometry, i.e., Riemannian and non-Riemannian curvatures. These curvatures interact to each other and have some mysterious and hidden relations between them. Among the non-Riemannian curvatures, the Berwald curvature has a central position. Finsler metrics with vanishing Berwald curvature are called Berwald metrics. Let

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +{\bf h}_{y}(w,u)\big(v-\textbf{g}_{y}(v,\ell)\ell\big)+2F{\bf C}_{y}(u,v,w)\ell\Big\}.

where
c
is a real constant. By assumption, we have
c\neq 0
. Applying
{\bf g}_{y}(y,\cdot)
on both sides of (
4.1
) implies that

{\bf L}=cF{\bf C}.

(4.2)

Taking a trace of (
4.2
) yields

{\bf J}=cF{\bf I}.

(4.3)

Taking horizontal derivation of (
4.3
) along Finslerian geodesics gives us

{\bf J}^{\prime}=cF{\bf J}=c^{2}F^{2}{\bf I}.

(4.4)

Also, every isotropic Berwald metric (
4.1
) has isotropic
S
-curvature,
{\bf S}=(n+1)cF
. By assumption, we have
c=constant
. Then, we have

{\bf S}=(n+1)cF,\ \ \ \ c\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.5)

Also, the following hold

F_{|m}=0,\ \ \ \ FF_{\cdot k|m}=g_{ik|m}y^{i}=-2L_{ikm}y^{i}=0.

Then, by (
4.5
), we get

{\bf S}_{\cdot k|m}y^{m}-{\bf S}_{|k}=0,

(4.6)

By (
3.14
), (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) we get

c^{2}F^{2}{\bf I}_{y}+{\bf R}_{y}({\bf I}_{y})=0.

(4.7)

Consider
P_{0}={\rm span}\{{\bf I}_{y},y\}
whenever
{\bf I}_{y}\not=0
. Applying
{\bf g}_{y}\big({\bf I}_{y},\cdot\big)
on both sides of (
4.7
), we get

{\bf g}_{y}\big({\bf I}_{y},{\bf I}_{y}\big)F^{2}\Big(c^{2}+{\bf K}(P_{0},y)\Big)=0,

(4.8)

where we have used

{\bf g}_{y}\big({\bf I}_{y},{\bf R}_{y}({\bf I}_{y})\big)=F^{2}{\bf g}_{y}\big({\bf I}_{y},{\bf I}_{y}\big){\bf K}(P_{0},y).

By (
4.8
), we have

{\bf K}(P_{0},y)=-c^{2},

(4.9)

which is a contradiction with our assumption on
{\bf{K}}
. Thus,
{\bf I}=0
and by Deicke’s theorem
F
reduces to a Riemannian metric.
∎

It is proved that every Douglas metric with isotropic (constant) mean Berwald curvature is a isotropic (constant) Berwald metric (see Lemma 4.1 in
[
2
]
). Then, by Theorem
1.2
, we get the following.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
(M,F)
be a positively curved Douglas manifold. Suppose that
F
has non-zero constant isotropic mean Berwald curvature. Then,
F
is Riemannian metric.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:
Let
F=F(x,y)
be a Finsler metric on an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
. The distortion
\tau=\tau(x,y)
on
TM

associated with the Busemann-Hausdorff volume form
dV_{BH}=\sigma(x)dx
is defined by

\tau(x,y)=\ln\frac{\sqrt{\det\big(g_{ij}(x,y)\big)}}{\sigma_{F}(x)}.

Let
SM=\Big\{(x,y)\in TM:\ F(x,y)=1\Big\}
be the unit sphere bundle. Since
M
is a compact manifold,

SM
is compact, and hence
\tau
is a bounded function on
SM
. In particular, there exists a real constant

C>0
such that

|\tau(x,y)|\leq C\qquad\ \forall(x,y)\in SM.

Now, let
\gamma=\gamma(t)
be any unit-speed geodesic, so
F(\dot{\gamma}(t))\equiv 1
and

(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\in SM
for all
t
. By the definition of
S
–curvature,

\frac{d}{dt}\tau\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)={\bf S}\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big).

By assumption,
F
has isotropic Berwald curvature (
6.1
). Then, it has isotropic
S
-curvature

{\bf S}=(n+1)\Phi F.

Using the isotropy and
F\big(\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)\equiv 1
, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\tau\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)=(n+1)\Phi\big(\gamma(t)\big).

Integrating from
0
to
t
yields

\tau\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)-\tau\big(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0)\big)=(n+1)\int_{0}^{t}\Phi\big(\gamma(s)\big)\,ds.

By the assumption
\Phi(\cdot)\geq c_{0}>0
on
M
,

\int_{0}^{t}\Phi\big(\gamma(s)\big)\,ds\;\geq\;c_{0}\,t,

so

\tau\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)-\tau\big(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0)\big)\;\geq\;(n+1)c_{0}\,t.

Therefore

\tau\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)\;\geq\;\tau\big(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0)\big)+(n+1)c_{0}\,t,

which tends to
+\infty
as
t\to\infty
. This contradicts the boundedness

|\tau|\leq C
on
SM
. Hence no such
c_{0}>0
can exist, and the only possibility
under
\Phi\geq 0
is
\Phi\equiv 0
.
∎

By Theorem
1.3
, we conclude the following.

Corollary 5.1
.

Every compact constant isotropic Berwald manifold is a Berwald manifold.

6
Proof of Theorem
1.4

A Randers metric on a manifold
M
is a positive scalar function on
TM
defined by
F=\alpha+\beta
, where
\alpha=\sqrt{a_{ij}(x)y^{i}y^{j}}
is a Riemannian metric and
\beta=b_{i}(x)y^{i}
is a 1-form on
M
. This metric was introduced by Randers in the context of general relativity
[
10
]
. In
[
5
]
, Deng and Hu proved that a homogeneous Randers metric of Berwald type whose flag curvature is non-zero everywhere must be Riemannian. In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. First, we remark some notions.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
Let
F
be homogeneous isotropic Berwald metric on an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
:

\displaystyle{\bf B}_{y}(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi F^{-1}\Big\{{\bf h}(u,v)\big(w-\textbf{g}_{y}(w,\ell)\ell\big)+{\bf h}(v,w)\big(u-\textbf{g}_{y}(u,\ell)\ell\big)

(6.1)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +{\bf h}(w,u)\big(v-\textbf{g}_{y}(v,\ell)\ell\big)+2F{\bf C}_{y}(u,v,w)\ell\Big\}.

where
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
. We prove the result in two main cases:

Case (i):
dim(M)=2
.
In
[
15
]
, it is showed that every isotropic Berwald metric (
6.1
) has isotropic
S
-curvature,
{\bf S}=3\Phi F
. In
[
17
]
, it is proved that homogeneous Finsler metrics with isotropic
S
-curvature has vanishing
S
-curvature,
\Phi=0
. Putting this in (
6.1
) implies
{\bf B}=0
and
F
is a Berwald metric. In
[
12
]
, Szabó proved that any connected Berwald surface is locally Minkowskian or Riemannian.

Case (ii):
dim(M)\geq 3
.
In
[
14
]
, it is proved that every homogeneous isotropic Berwald metric on a manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 3
is a Berwald metric or Randers metric of Berwald-type.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

By considering the case (i), Theorem
1.4
completes the Theorem 1.1 in
[
14
]
which proved only for homogeneous isotropic Berwald manifolds of dimension
n\geq 3
. In
[
14
]
, we guessed that theorem
1.4
holds for 2-dimensional Finsler manifolds but we did not find a proof for our conjecture.

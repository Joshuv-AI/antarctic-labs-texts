Averaging quadratically twisted modular L L -values and their derivatives
Tinghao Huang

Abstract
. In this paper, we unconditionally establish an asymptotic formula for the product of the quadratically twisted central L L -value associated to a holomorphic cusp form f f , and the quadratically twisted central L L -derivative to a distinct holomorphic cusp form g g . This result may be viewed as an extension of [ 7 ] , [ 6 ] and [ 15 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
f(z)
and
g(z)
be two cuspidal Hecke newforms of even weights
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
and odd levels
q_{1},q_{2}
respectively.
The Fourier expansions of
f
and
g
at the cusp
\infty
shall take the form

f(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(m)m^{\frac{\kappa_{1}-1}{2}}e^{2\pi imz},\,g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{g}(n)n^{\frac{\kappa_{2}-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}.

We normalize
f
and
g
so that
\lambda_{f}(1)=\lambda_{g}(1)=1
.
For
\chi_{D}(\cdot)=\big(\frac{D}{\cdot}\big)
a primitive real character where
D
is a fundamental discriminant (that is, discriminant for a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
), we may construct the twisted
L
-functions associated to
f
and
g
as the following absolutely convergent Dirichlet series whenever the real part of
s
is greater than 1:

L(s,f\otimes\chi_{D}):=\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{D}(m)m^{-s}

and

L(s,g\otimes\chi_{D}):=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\lambda_{g}(n)\chi_{D}(n)n^{-s}.

These Dirichlet series could be modified to the completed
L
-functions
\Lambda(f,f\otimes\chi_{D})
and
\Lambda(s,g\otimes\chi_{D})
by setting

\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{D})=\bigg(\frac{|D|\sqrt{q_{1}}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{s}\Gamma\bigg(s+\frac{\kappa_{1}-1}{2}\bigg)\cdot L(s,f\otimes\chi_{D})

and

\Lambda(s,g\otimes\chi_{D})=\bigg(\frac{|D|\sqrt{q_{2}}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{s}\Gamma\bigg(s+\frac{\kappa_{2}-1}{2}\bigg)\cdot L(s,g\otimes\chi_{D}),

then extended to be entire functions defined over
\mathbb{C}
, and shown to satisfy functional equations

\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{D})=i^{\kappa_{1}}\eta_{f}\cdot\chi_{D}(-q_{1})\Lambda(1-s,f\otimes\chi_{D})

and

\Lambda(s,g\otimes\chi_{D})=i^{\kappa_{2}}\eta_{g}\cdot\chi_{D}(-q_{2})\Lambda(1-s,g\otimes\chi_{D}).

Here
\eta_{f}
and
\eta_{g}
are the eigenvalues of the Fricke involutions of
f
and
g
, which are independent to
D
and always take values
\pm 1
.
For notational convenience, we denote
i^{\kappa_{1}}\eta_{f}\cdot\chi_{D}(-q_{1})
and
i^{\kappa_{2}}\eta_{g}\cdot\chi_{D}(-q_{2})
respectively as
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{D})
and
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{D})
. Notice that with weights
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
even,
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{D})
and
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{D})
can only take values
\pm 1
.

In this paper, we aim to calculate unconditionally an asymptotic formula for the following mixed moment

(1.1)

S_{J}(f,g^{\prime};X):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})\cdot L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})\cdot J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg)

as
X\rightarrow\infty
. Note that for odd
d
,
8d
is a fundamental discriminant. In the above, the asterisk on the top of the summation sign simply means that the summation is over square free integers, and
J(x)
is taken to be a non-negative smooth function supported on the interval
[1/2,2]
.
Our main theorem states the following:

Theorem 1
.

With the notation above, we have

S_{J}(f,g^{\prime};X)=C_{f,g}\check{J}(0)X\log X+O_{f,g,J}(X(\log\log X)^{5}),

in which
C_{f,g}
is a constant depending only on
f
and
g
,
\check{J}(0)=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}J(x)dx
, and the implied constant in the error term depends only on the forms
f,g
and
J(\cdot)
. Moreover,
C_{f,g}=0
if and only if
i^{\kappa_{1}}\eta_{f}=-1
and
q_{1}
is a square or
i^{\kappa_{2}}\eta_{g}=1
and
q_{2}
is a square, in which case the moment
S_{J}(f,g^{\prime};X)=0
identically.

The study of moments of quadratically twisted central
L
-values and their derivatives associated with cusp forms has a rich history and deep applications in arithmetic geometry and the theory of automorphic representations. For foundational results on the first moments of twisted central
L
-values and their derivatives, we refer, for example, to
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
8
]
.

An asymptotic formula for the more challenging second moments of quadratically twisted central
L
-values of cusp forms was first established by Soundararajan and Young
[
13
]
, where they proved an asymptotic formula of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq X\\
(d,2)=1\end{subarray}}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})^{2}=(c+o(1))X\log X

under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for certain
L
-functions. Here,
c
is a constant depending only on
f
. This result was later proven unconditionally by Li in
[
7
]
, thereby removing the dependence on GRH.

More recently, an asymptotic formula for the second moment of twisted central derivatives
L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})
was obtained by Kumar, Mallesham, Sharma, and Singh in
[
6
]
, building on crucial estimates of bilinear forms from
[
7
]
. In a very recent work, Zhou
[
15
]
established an asymptotic formula for the more delicate (smoothed) first moment of products of twisted central derivatives:

\sum_{d}L^{\prime}(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})\cdot L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})F(8d/X),

where
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
f
,
g
are two
distinct
cusp forms. However, with current techniques, the conjectured asymptotic formula for the product of twisted central values of two distinct forms
f
and
g
,

\sum_{d}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})L(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})F(8d/X),

remains out of reach.

Our main theorem also has implications for properties of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is well known that for any elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
q
and Weierstrass equation of the form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,\quad A,B\in\mathbb{Q},

there exists (not necessarily uniquely) a cuspidal Hecke eigen-newform
f_{E}
of level
q
and weight
2
such that the associated Hecke
L
-function agrees with the Hasse–Weil
L
-function of
E
, i.e.,
L(s,f_{E})=L(s,E)
.

Given a fundamental discriminant
D
, one defines the quadratic twist of
E
, denoted
E^{(D)}
, by modifying the Weierstrass coefficients:

E^{(D)}:y^{2}=x^{3}+AD^{2}x+BD^{3},

which is again an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. The
L
-function of this twist satisfies
L(s,E^{(D)})=L(s,f_{E}\otimes\chi_{D})
. After normalizing these
L
-functions so that the center of the critical strip is
s=\frac{1}{2}
, the celebrated Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture predicts that the order of vanishing of
L(s,E)
(and thus
L(s,f_{E})
), as well as
L(s,E^{(D)})
(and
L(s,f_{E}\otimes\chi_{D})
), at
s=\frac{1}{2}
equals the rank of the corresponding Mordell–Weil group.

By the results of Kolyvagin
[
5
]
and Gross–Zagier
[
2
]
(see also Section C.16 of
[
11
]
), this conjecture is known to hold when the order of vanishing is at most
1
. As a consequence, our Theorem 1 yields the following corollary:

Corollary 1
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductors
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, respectively, neither of which is a perfect square. Then there exist infinitely many fundamental discriminants of the form
8d
, where
(d,2)=1
, such that the Mordell–Weil rank of
E_{1}^{(8d)}
is equal to
1
, and that of
E_{2}^{(8d)}
is equal to
0
.

Notations

In this section, we briefly clarify the notations that will be frequently used throughout the paper. We adopt the Vinogradov symbols
\ll
and
O(\cdot)
, with the understanding that the dependence of the implied constants will be specified when necessary. Additionally,
\Re(\cdot)
will denote the real part of a complex variable.

As previously mentioned, we use
\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}
to represent a summation over square-free integers. The notation
\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{N}

N

2^{j}

.

3.
Basic Tools

In this section we record some tools that shall be crucial to our calculation, as well as some notations that will be convenient for our use in the following sections.

When estimating summations over fundamental discriminants, we will for several times apply the summation formula of Poisson type for real characters and extract the main contribution from the term corresponding to the zero frequency. We record the above summation formula, which was originally due to Soundararajan
[
12
, Lemma 2.6]
, as the following lemma (see also
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
):

Lemma 1
.

For
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
n
odd and
Z>0
, we have

\sum_{(d,2)=1}\bigg(\frac{d}{n}\bigg)F\bigg(\frac{d}{Z}\bigg)=\frac{Z}{2n}\bigg(\frac{2}{n}\bigg)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}G_{k}(n)\check{F}\bigg(\frac{kZ}{2n}\bigg),

in which

G_{k}(n):=\bigg(\frac{1-i}{2}+\bigg(\frac{-1}{n}\bigg)\frac{1+i}{2}\bigg)\sum_{a\,(\text{mod}\,n)}\bigg(\frac{a}{n}\bigg)e\bigg(\frac{ak}{n}\bigg)

is the Gauss-type sum, and

\check{F}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\cos(2\pi x\xi)+\sin(2\pi x\xi))F(x)dx

is an integral transform of
F
.

For
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we may invoke Mellin inversion to rewrite
\check{F}
as

\check{F}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2)}\tilde{F}(1-s)\Gamma(s)(\cos+\text{sgn}(\xi)\sin)(\pi s/2)(2\pi|\xi|)^{-s}ds,

where
\tilde{F}(s):=\int^{\infty}_{0}F(x)x^{s-1}dx
is the Mellin transform of
F
. We also note that with
\Re(s)
bounded,
\tilde{F}(s)
acquires the following rapid decay property:

\tilde{F}(s)\ll_{A}(1+|s|)^{-A}

for any
A>0
.

It shall be useful to explicitly evaluate the Gauss-type sums
G_{k}(\cdot)
’s appeared on the right hand side of Poisson summation. This work was accomplished in
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
, which shall be quoted as below:

Lemma 2
.

For odd
m,n
co-prime, we have

G_{k}(mn)=G_{k}(m)G_{k}(n).

Also, if
p^{\alpha}||k
(setting
\alpha=\infty
if
k=0
), then

G_{k}(p^{\beta})=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,&&\beta\leq\alpha\,\text{is odd},\\
\phi(p^{\beta}),&&\beta\leq\alpha\,\text{is even},\\
-p^{\alpha},&&\beta=\alpha+1\,\text{is even},\\
\bigg(\frac{kp^{-\alpha}}{p}\bigg)p^{\alpha+1/2},&&\beta=\alpha+1\,\text{is odd},\\
0,&&\beta\geq\alpha+2.\end{aligned}\right.

Oftenly, it would be convenient to dyadically partition summations, and we introduce a family of functions to aid us do so. We introduce
G\in C^{\infty}_{c}([3/4,2])
satisfying

\displaystyle G(x)=1,\,x\in[1,3/2]

\displaystyle G(x)+G(x/2)=1,\,x\in[1,3].

Such function could be explicitly constructed (see
[
14
, Lemma 1.10]
), and would satisfy

G(x)+...+G(x/2^{J})=1

for any
x\in[1,3\cdot 2^{J-1}]
, in which
J\geq 1
and
G(x)+...+G(x/2^{J})
will be supported on
[3/4,2^{J+1}]
. We shall fix a choice of
G(x)
throughout the rest of the paper. Clearly
\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{N}G(x/N)
is a well-defined function supported on
[3/4,\infty)
, and is equal to 1 for any
x\geq 1
.

Finally, the following bilinear form estimation due to Li
[
7
, Lemma 6.3]
(with minor technical modification to generalize to arbitrary levels) shall be crucial for our use in section 7:

Lemma 3
.

For any real
\mathcal{X},N\geq 1
and real
t
as well as a positive integer
q
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2)=1\\
d\leq\mathcal{X}\end{subarray}}\bigg|\sum_{(n,q)=1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\bigg(\frac{8d}{n}\bigg)G(n/N)\bigg|^{2}\ll d(q)^{5}\mathcal{X}(1+|t|)^{3}\log(2+|t|),

where
d(\cdot)
is the usual divisor counting function. Here the implied constant only depends on
f
.

4.
Set-up & statement of main propositions

A standard technique to express the central
L
-values or derivatives in terms of Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(m),\lambda_{g}(n)
will be the application of
approximate functional equations. We record the approximate functional equations for
L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})
and
L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})
as below.

Lemma 4
.

With the notations as above, we have

(4.1)

L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})=\bigg(1+\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})\bigg)\cdot\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{\sqrt{m}}W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{8|d|\sqrt{q_{1}}}\bigg)

and

(4.2)

L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})=\bigg(1-\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})\bigg)\cdot\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{8|d|\sqrt{q_{2}}}\bigg),

in which

W_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}(2\pi x)^{-w}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\kappa_{1}/2+w)}{\Gamma(\kappa_{1}/2)}\frac{dw}{w}

while

W_{2}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}(2\pi y)^{-u}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\kappa_{2}/2+u)}{\Gamma(\kappa_{2}/2)}\frac{du}{u^{2}}.

Proof.

These are consequences of the holomorphy as well as the functional equations of the complete
L
-functions
\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi_{8d})
and
\Lambda(s,g\otimes\chi_{8d})
, after an application of the Residue Theorem. See
[
4
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
for further details.
∎

For notational simplicity, we shall denote
\frac{\Gamma_{1}(\kappa_{1}/2+w)}{\Gamma(\kappa_{1}/2)}
and
\frac{\Gamma_{2}(\kappa_{2}/2+u)}{\Gamma(\kappa_{2}/2)}
respectively by
\gamma_{1}(w)
and
\gamma_{2}(u)
.
Notice that by Stirling’s formula, we have the following estimation for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
when
\Re(w)
and
\Re(u)
are bounded:

\gamma_{1}(w)\ll_{\kappa_{1}}(1+|w|)^{-20},\,\gamma_{2}(u)\ll_{\kappa_{2}}(1+|u|)^{-20},

in which the implied constants depends only on
\kappa_{1}
or
\kappa_{2}
.

In practice, it would be convenient to ”truncate” the right hand sides of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) by an auxiliary parameter
Y:=\frac{X}{(\log X)^{200}}
, and set

\mathcal{A}_{f}=\mathcal{A}_{f}(Y;8d):=\bigg(1+\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})\bigg)\cdot\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{\sqrt{m}}W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)

while

\mathcal{B}_{f}=\mathcal{B}_{f}(Y;8d):=L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})-\mathcal{A}_{f},

and similarly

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}(Y;8d):=\bigg(1-\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})\bigg)\cdot\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{Y}\bigg)

and

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{g}=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{g}(Y;8d):=L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})-\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}.

After truncation, we may rewrite our target sum (
1.1
) as:

S_{J}(f,g^{\prime};X)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{8d})\cdot L^{\prime}(1/2,g\otimes\chi_{8d})\cdot J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg)=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\big(\mathfrak{I}_{1}+\mathfrak{I}_{2}+\mathfrak{I}_{3}+\mathfrak{I}_{4}\big),

in which

\mathfrak{I}_{1}=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{f}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg),

\mathfrak{I}_{2}=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{f}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{g}J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg),

\mathfrak{I}_{3}=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg),

and

\mathfrak{I}_{4}=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\\
\omega(f\otimes\chi_{8d})=1\\
\omega(g\otimes\chi_{8d})=-1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}_{f}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{g}J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg).

We summarize the estimation for the above
\mathfrak{I}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{I}_{2}
,
\mathfrak{I}_{3}
and
\mathfrak{I}_{4}
as the following four propositions:

Proposition 1
.

With the above notations, we have the following:

\mathfrak{I}_{1}=C_{f,g}\check{J}(0)X\log X+O_{f,g,\epsilon}(X),

where
C_{f},g
is a constant defined as

\displaystyle C_{f,g}

\displaystyle:=\frac{L(1,f\otimes g)L(1,\text{Sym}^{2}f)L(1,\text{Sym}^{2}g)}{2\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\big\{\mathcal{Z}(0,0;1)+i^{\kappa_{1}}\eta_{f}\mathcal{Z}(0,0;q_{1})-i^{\kappa_{2}}\eta_{g}\mathcal{Z}(0,0;q_{2})-i^{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}\eta_{f}\eta_{g}\mathcal{Z}(0,0;q_{1}q_{2})\big\}

and the implied constant in
O_{f,g}(\cdot)
is only dependent on the forms
f,g
and
\epsilon>0
. Here

\mathcal{Z}(w,u;Q^{\prime})
is an absolutely convergent and uniformly bounded Euler product defined on
\Re(w),\Re(u)>-\frac{1}{4}
. Moreover,
C_{f,g}=0
if and only if
i^{\kappa_{1}}\eta_{f}=-1
and
q_{1}
is a square or
i^{\kappa_{2}}\eta_{g}=1
and
q_{2}
is a square, in which case the moment
S_{J}(f,g^{\prime};X)
vanishes identically.

Proposition 2
.

With the above notations, we have the following:

\mathfrak{I}_{2}\ll_{f,g}X(\log\log X),

where the implied constant depends only on the forms
f,g
.

Proposition 3
.

With the notations above, we have the following:

\mathfrak{I}_{3}\ll_{f,g}X,

where the implied constant depends only on the forms
f,g
.

and finally

Proposition 4
.

With the above notations, we have the following:

\mathfrak{I}_{4}\ll X(\log\log X)^{5},

where the implied constant depends only on the forms
f,g
.

Clearly, Theorem 1 follows immediately from Proposition 1 - 4, whose proof shall span the next four sections.

5.
Estimating
\mathfrak{I}_{1}

In this section we treat
\mathfrak{I}_{1}
and prove Proposition 1.

Following the technique in
[
3
]
and
[
7
, Proposition 6.2]
, we shall prove Proposition
1
by applying Poisson summation formula for real characters, which allows us to extract the main term from the zero frequency term and control the magnitude of other terms.

Upon using the following notation

S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}:=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,2q_{1}q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{m}\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)(mn)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{8d}(mnQ^{\prime})W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{Y}\bigg)J\bigg(\frac{8d}{X}\bigg),

we see that

\mathfrak{I}_{1}=S^{1}_{1}+i^{\kappa_{1}}\eta_{f}S^{1}_{q_{1}}-i^{\kappa_{2}}\eta_{g}S^{1}_{q_{2}}-i^{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}\eta_{f}\eta_{g}S^{1}_{q_{1}q_{2}},

and thus we essentially only need to treat
S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}
for
Q^{\prime}=1,q_{1},q_{2}
and
q_{1}q_{2}
.

By inserting Mobius functions appropriately we may drop the co-prime and square-free conditions in the outermost
d
-summation, and get

S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}=S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z)+S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}(a>Z),

in which

(5.1)

\displaystyle S_{Q^{\prime}}^{1}(a\leq Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,2Q)=1\\
a\leq Z\end{subarray}}\mu(a)\sum_{b|Q}\mu(b)\sum_{(m,2a)=1}\sum_{(n,2a)=1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{(d,2)=1}\chi_{8db}(mnQ^{\prime})W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{Y}\bigg)J\bigg(\frac{8a^{2}bd}{X}\bigg)

and

\displaystyle S_{Q^{\prime}}^{1}(a>Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,2Q)=1\\
a>Z\end{subarray}}\mu(a)\sum_{(d,2Q)=1}J\bigg(\frac{8a^{2}d}{X}\bigg)\sum_{(m,2a)=1}\sum_{(n,2a)=1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle\times\chi_{8d}(mnQ^{\prime})W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{Y}\bigg)

in which
Z
is a large parameter to be determined and
Q=q_{1}q_{2}
for convenience.

To estimate
S_{Q^{\prime}}^{1}(a>Z)
, we combine Proposition 6.2 of
[
7
]
(which could be generalized to level
q_{1}
with minor technical efforts) and Proposition 3.2 of
[
6
]
as well as Cauchy-Schwarz to conclude that

(5.2)

S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}(a>Z)\ll\frac{X(\log X)^{2+\epsilon}}{Z^{1-\epsilon}}.

The estimation of
S_{Q^{\prime}}^{1}(a\leq Z)
requires more effort, and we shall see that the main term of the desired asymptotic formula shall be derived from
S_{Q^{\prime}}^{1}(a\leq Z)
. An application of the Poisson summation formula for real characters Lemma
1
shall transform
S_{Q^{\prime}}^{1}(a\leq Z)
as:

\displaystyle S^{1}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{X\check{J}(0)}{2\pi^{2}}\cdot\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}mnQ^{\prime}=\square\\
(mn,2)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)(mn)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{p|mnQ}(1-p^{-1})\big(\prod_{p|mnQ}(1-p^{-2})^{-1}+O(Z^{-1})\big)\cdot W_{1}(m/Y)W_{2}(n/Y)

\displaystyle+\frac{X}{16}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,2Q)=1\\

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(1+|6+i(t_{1}-t_{3})|)^{20}}\frac{1}{(1+|-6+i(t_{2}-t_{4})|)^{20}}\bigg\{\bigg|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{2\nmid k_{1}}T(k_{1},1/2+it_{1},1/2+it_{2},Q^{\prime})\bigg|

\displaystyle+\bigg|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{2\nmid k_{1}}T(k_{1},1/2+it_{1},1/2+it_{2},Q^{\prime},1/2+it_{3})\bigg|+\bigg|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{2\nmid k_{1}}T(k_{1},1/2+it_{1},1/2+it_{2},Q^{\prime},1/2+it_{4})\bigg|

\displaystyle+\bigg|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{2\nmid k_{1}}T(k_{1},1/2+it_{1},1/2+it_{2},Q^{\prime},1/2+i(t_{3}+t_{4}))\bigg|\bigg\}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{a^{2}b}{X}\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{N_{1}\leq Y}\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{X<N_{2}}\sqrt{N_{1}N_{2}}\bigg(\frac{X}{N_{2}}\bigg)^{6}\ll\frac{a^{2}b\sqrt{XY}}{X}.

With these we may conclude that

T^{4,n}_{Q^{\prime}}(0)\ll\frac{a^{2}b}{(\log X)^{99-\epsilon}}

as we have taken
Y=X/(\log X)^{200}
, and similarly (with the aid of Lemma 5.3 of
[
15
]
)

T^{4,n}_{Q^{\prime}}(l)\ll\frac{a^{2}b}{(\log X)^{99-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{l}}.

Combining the above and (
8.3
), we conclude that

(8.4)

T^{4,n}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z)\ll\frac{XZ}{(\log X)^{99-\epsilon}}.

Combining (
8.2
), (
8.4
) and take
Z=(\log X)^{80}
we conclude that

S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;N_{1}\leq Y,N_{2})=T^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{10}}\ll X.

The above also concludes that

S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;N_{1}\leq Y,N_{2}\leq Y)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{10}}\ll X.

As for
S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;N_{2}\leq Y,N_{1})
, we could proceed to estimate almost identically as in the case for
S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;N_{1}\leq Y,N_{2})
, except that when shifting the integral lines for
w
and
u
in the off-diagonal, we swap the roles of
w
and
u
, which shall cause minor differences in the expression of the integral (as now we shall always pass through a simple pole at
u=0
) which shall not affect the estimates, and we still have

S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;N_{1}\leq Y,N_{2})\ll X.

8.2.
Y<N_{1},N_{2}\leq X

In this subsection, following section 7.4 of
[
15
]
, we estimate the contribution from
S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;Y<N_{1},N_{2}\leq X)
. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the
d
-summation, as well as noticing the positivity, we have

\displaystyle S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;Y<N_{1},N_{2}\leq X)

\displaystyle\leq\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{Y<N_{1}\leq X}\bigg(\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\bigg|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{\sqrt{m}}G(m/N_{1})

\displaystyle\times\big(W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{8d\sqrt{q_{1}}}\bigg)-W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)\big)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{Y<N_{2}\leq X}\bigg(\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\bigg|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}G(n/N_{2})

\displaystyle\times\big(W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{8d\sqrt{q_{2}}}\bigg)-W_{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{Y}\bigg)\big)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We estimate the first factor of the above as follows:

Lemma 5
.

With the notations above, we have

\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{Y<N_{1}\leq X}\bigg(\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\bigg|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{\sqrt{m}}G(m/N_{1})\big(W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{8d\sqrt{q_{1}}}\bigg)-W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)\big)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\log X)^{2}.

Proof.

Denoting the
d
-summation under the square root in the above as
\mathcal{R}
, we firstly insert the test function
V
into the above as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\bigg|\sum_{m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{\sqrt{m}}G(m/N_{1})V(m/N_{1})

\displaystyle\big(W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{8d\sqrt{q_{1}}}\bigg)-W_{1}\bigg(\frac{m}{Y}\bigg)\big)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\bigg|(2\pi i)^{-2}\int_{(3)}\int_{(0)}\tilde{G}(s)N_{1}^{s}(2\pi)^{-w}\gamma_{1}(w)w^{-1}\big\{(8d\sqrt{q_{1}})^{w}-Y^{w}\big\}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{m^{w+s+\frac{1}{2}}}V(m/N_{1})dsdw\bigg|^{2}.

In the above, we make a change of variable on
w
, and then shift the
w
-integral line to
\Re(w)=(\log X)^{-1}
, and then invoke Cauchy-Schwarz to the double integral to arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\ll\bigg(\frac{Y}{N_{1}}\bigg)^{2(\log X)^{-1}}\times\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\int_{((\log X)^{-1})}\int_{(0)}|\gamma_{1}(w)||\tilde{G}(s-w)|\bigg|\frac{(8d\sqrt{q_{1}}/Y)^{w}-1}{\sqrt{w}}\bigg|^{2}dsdw

\displaystyle\times\int_{((\log X)^{-1})}\int_{(0)}|\gamma_{1}(w)||\tilde{G}(s-w)||w|^{-1}\bigg|\sum_{m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}V(m/N_{1})\bigg|^{2}dsdw.

We treat the first double integral above first. Invoking the rapid decay of
\tilde{G}(\cdot)
and
\gamma_{1}(\cdot)
, uniformly in
d
we have

\displaystyle\int_{((\log X)^{-1})}\int_{(0)}|\gamma_{1}(w)||\tilde{G}(s-w)|\bigg|\frac{(8d\sqrt{q_{1}}/Y)^{w}-1}{\sqrt{w}}\bigg|^{2}dsdw

\displaystyle\ll\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{|(\log X)^{-1}+it_{1}|}{(1+|(\log X)^{-1}+it_{1}|)^{20}\cdot(1+|-(\log X)^{-1}+i(t_{2}-t_{1})|)^{20}}

\displaystyle\times\bigg|\frac{(8d\sqrt{q_{1}}/Y)^{(\log X)^{-1}+it_{1}}-1}{(\log X)^{-1}+it_{1}}\bigg|^{2}dt_{2}dt_{1}

\displaystyle\ll\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg\{\bigg|\frac{(8d\sqrt{q_{1}}/Y)^{(\log X)^{-1}+it}-1}{(\log X)^{-1}+it}\bigg|^{2}\bigg\}\times\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{1}{(1+|t_{1}|)^{19}}dt_{1}\ll(\log\log X)^{2},

in which we have used the fact that

\frac{(8d\sqrt{q_{1}}/Y)^{(\log X)^{-1}+it}-1}{(\log X)^{-1}+it}\ll\log\log X

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
as
\frac{X}{16}\leq d\leq\frac{X}{4}
.

As for the rest

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{(d,2Q)=1}J(8d/X)\int_{((\log X)^{-1})}\int_{(0)}|\gamma_{1}(w)||\tilde{G}(s-w)||w|^{-1}\bigg|\sum_{m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\chi_{8d}(m)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}V(m/N_{1})\bigg|^{2}dsdw,

we apply Lemma
3
to conclude that the above is bounded by
X
. Thus

\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{Y<N_{1}\leq X}\mathcal{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll\sideset{}{{}^{d}}{\sum}_{Y<N_{1}\leq X}(Y/N_{1})^{(\log X)^{-1}}X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\log X)\ll X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\log X)^{2},

finishing the proof.
∎

Now, combining Lemma
5
and Lemma 7.1 of
[
15
]
, we have

S^{4}_{Q^{\prime}}(a\leq Z;Y<N_{1},N_{2}\leq X)\ll X(\log\log X)^{5}.

For the summand

. Now, by Lemma

Partial regularity for 𝔸 \mathbb{A} -quasiconvex functionals with Orlicz growth
Paul Stephan

Abstract
. We establish partial regularity results for minimizers of a class of functionals depending on differential expressions based on elliptic operators. Specifically, we focus on functionals of Orlicz growth with a natural strong quasiconvexity property. In doing so, we consider both Δ 2 ∩ ∇ 2 \Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2} -Orlicz growth scenarios and, as a limiting case, L ​ log ⁡ L L\log L -growth. Inspired by Conti & Gmeineder (Calc. Var. & PDE 61:215, 2022), the proofs of our main results are accomplished by reduction to the case of full gradient partial regularity results.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded with Lipschitz boundary. Numerous variational problems, such as those in (stationary) elasticity or fluid mechanics, model key quantities such as displacements or velocities using functionals of the type

(1.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}[u]:=\int_{\Omega}F(\varepsilon(u))\differential{x}.

Here,
\varepsilon(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}\right)
denotes the symmetric gradient of a map
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Different models, however, involve more general operators than the symmetric gradient.
In view of a general framework which comprises (
1.1
) as a special case, we replace
\varepsilon
by an arbitrary homogeneous differential operator
\mathbb{A}
, meaning that
\mathbb{A}
has a representation

(1.2)

\mathbb{A}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u,\qquad u\colon\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k},

for certain linear maps
A_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
. In this general framework, we consider functionals of the form

(1.3)

\mathcal{F}[u]:=\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}u)\,\differential{x}.

By making different choices of
F
and
\mathbb{A}
, functionals of the form (
1.3
) provide a versatile approach to various energy minimization problems not only in elasticity, but also in broader contexts, such as continuum mechanics and fluid dynamics, and also in problems from general relativity. For a detailed discussion on these applications, see, for instance,
[
4
,
6
,
21
,
22
,
33
,
34
,
37
]
. Once appropriate boundary conditions and semiconvexity assumptions on the integrands are imposed, it is then possible to establish the existence of minimizers of functionals (
1.3
) in suitable spaces of weakly differentiable functions.

Once this is achieved, it is then natural to inquire as to which conditions have to be assumed on
F
for minimizers to share better regularity properties than generic competitors. In the present paper, we deal with this question in the realm of gradient Hölder regularity. Since the minimization of (
1.3
) is a genuinely vectorial problem, it is well-known that minimizers are not necessarily fully
\operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha}
-regular in general (see, e.g.,
[
38
,
40
,
48
]
). Consequently, a
De Giorgi-Nash-Moser
theory is not available. In this regard, a suitable substitute is provided by the (
\operatorname{C}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,\alpha}
-)
partial regularity
of minimizers, meaning that they are of class
\operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(U)
for all
0<\alpha<1
, where
U\subset\Omega
is a relatively open set with full Lebesgue measure:
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\Omega\setminus U)=0
.

In view of the direct method of the Calculus of Variations and based on the fact that the minimization of (
1.3
) is a vectorial problem, it is natural to suppose that
F
satisfies a strong variant of
Morrey
’s
quasiconvexity
, implying both lower semicontinuity and coercivity, for example, on Dirichlet subclasses of Sobolev spaces.
If
\mathbb{A}=\nabla
is the usual gradient, a rather complete picture of partial regularity is available in this situation; see
[
1
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
23
,
24
,
28
,
32
,
43
,
44
,
46
,
47
,
49
,
52
,
53
]
for a non-exhaustive list. More precisely, starting with
Evans

[
32
]
for strongly quasiconvex functionals with
p
-growth for
2\leq p<\infty
,
[
15
,
16
]
for
1<p\leq 2
, and finally
[
43
]
for
p=1
, partial regularity results are now available for all
p
-growth functionals. Here, we say that
F\in\operatorname{C}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
has
p
-growth,
1\leq p<\infty
, if there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle|F(z)|\leq c\,(1+|z|^{p})\qquad\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}.

In
[
52
,
53
]
,
Schmidt
generalized these results to functionals of
(p,q)
growth for
p>1
. The works of
Diening
et al.
[
28
]
and
Breit & Verde

[
11
]
extended this framework to the context of Orlicz growth, in turn using and modifying
Duzaar & Steffen’s
method of
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic approximation; see
[
31
]
. Finally,
Gmeineder
&
Kristensen
provided the generalization for the case
p=1
in
[
44
]
.

While the aforementioned results deal with the case
\mathbb{A}=\nabla
, there are further results on the interaction of partial regularity and general operators
\mathbb{A}
. Based on earlier work by
Gmeineder

[
42
]
for the symmetric gradient,
Conti & Gmeineder
established in
[
20
]
that, under a suitable
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity condition on
F
, the partial regularity of minimizers of
p
-growth functionals (
1.3
) with
1<p<\infty
is equivalent to
\mathbb{A}
being
elliptic
. This means that the Fourier symbol map

\mathbb{A}[\xi]:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\xi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}

is injective for all phase space variables
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.

Whereas the
p
-growth assumption is satisfied in numerous situations, there are plenty of models that require working with integrands that satisfy a more flexible condition than (
1.4
). An instance of this include models that involve logarithmic hardening of materials, or stationary models for fluids of
Prandtl-Eyring
-type; see
[
9
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
for more detail. Note that the energies underlying these references are usually convex. However, as shall be discussed in detail below, the reduction to the partial regularity for
full gradient
functionals can only be accomplished by viewing them as suitable
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvex functionals.

To provide a unified approach that includes such models, the present paper deals with strongly
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvex functionals of
Orlicz growth
. In this context, our first result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Partial regularity,
\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
-growth)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. Moreover, let
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, and suppose that
\mathbb{A}
is a first order constant rank differential operator of the form (
2.3
), and that the variational integrand
F:{\mathbb{R}^{l}}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

(F1)

F\in\mathrm{C}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{l}})
.

(F2)

There exists a constant
c>0
such that

|F(z)|\leq c(1+\varphi(|z|))

holds for all
z\in{\mathbb{R}^{l}}
.

(F3)

\varphi
-strong
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity: For every
M>0
, there exists
\nu_{M}>0
such that

\int_{(0,1)^{n}}\bigl(F(z_{0}+\mathbb{A}\zeta)-F(z_{0})\bigr)\,\differential{x}\geq\nu_{M}\int_{(0,1)^{n}}\varphi_{1+|z_{0}|}(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)\,\differential{x}

holds for all
\zeta\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)^{n};{\mathbb{R}^{k}})
and
|z_{0}|\leq M
. Here,
\varphi_{1+|z_{0}|}
is the
shifted
N
-function
as defined in (
2.2
) below.

Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\mathbb{A}
is elliptic.

(b)

Every local minimizer
u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{k}})
with
\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{l}})
of
\mathcal{F}
as in (
1.3
) is
\mathrm{C}^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-partially regular.

In stating the previous theorem, we made use of various notions to be briefly addressed now: Firstly, the condition
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
on the
N
-function
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
means that
\varphi
neither grows too slowly nor too fast; see Section
2
for the precise definition. Secondly,
(F3)
is a version of
Fonseca & Müller
’s
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvexity
[
33
]
adapted to the setting considered here; see
Raita

[
50
]
for the connections between
\mathscr{A}
- and
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity. In the form as given here,
(F3)
not only implies lower semicontinuity in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, but also the coercivity of the functionals (
1.3
) on Dirichlet classes; see Section
3
for more detail. Lastly, the notion of
local minimality
underlying
(F3)
is classical: We say that a function
u\in\operatorname{W}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{k}})
is a
local minimizer
provided that

\int_{\omega}F(\mathbb{A}u)\,\differential{x}\leq\int_{\omega}F(\mathbb{A}(u+\zeta))\,\differential{x}

holds for every open
{\omega}\Subset\Omega
and all maps
\zeta\in\operatorname{W}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\omega;{\mathbb{R}^{k}})
.

Following
Conti & Gmeineder

[
20
]
, our strategy to establish Theorem
1.1
is
not
to execute an entire partial regularity proof by employing, for example, the methods of blow-up,
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic approximation or similar techniques. Instead, we aim to reduce Theorem
1.1
to the main results of
[
28
,
45
]
by using suitable Korn-type inequalities adapted to the differential operator
\mathbb{A}
. Inequalities of this sort have recently attracted significant interest, as seen in
[
7
,
8
]
, and are of a particularly simple form if
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
. Roughly speaking, if
\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{l}})
for
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, then
\nabla u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and it is this insight that allows us to reduce Theorem
1.1
to the findings of
[
28
,
45
]
in Section
4
.

Variational integrands
F
of
\varphi
-growth,
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, do not always provide the right framework; examples include models with logarithmic hardening. Here,
\varphi(t)=t\log(1+t)
, whereby
\varphi
is of class
\Delta_{2}\setminus\nabla_{2}
. In the special cases where
\mathbb{A}=\varepsilon
or
\mathbb{A}=\varepsilon^{D}
with the
deviatoric
symmetric gradient
\varepsilon^{D}u:=\varepsilon(u)-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{div}(u)E_{n}
, it is known from
Cianchi
et al.
[
19
,
7
]
that
\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
only implies that
\nabla u\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. As we show in Section
5
, this loss of one logarithm in passing from
\mathbb{A}u
to
\nabla u
persists for general elliptic operators. Yet, resorting to the recent work
[
44
]
of
Gmeineder & Kristensen
, this still allows us to implement a variant of the above reduction scheme. More precisely, the second main result of the present paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Partial regularity –
L\log L
-growth)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded, and let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be given by
\varphi\colon t\mapsto t\log(1+t)
. Moreover, suppose that the variational integrand
F:{\mathbb{R}^{l}}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
(F1)
-
(F3)
from above. If
\mathbb{A}
is a constant rank operator of the form (
2.3
), then every local minimizer
u\in\operatorname{L}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\varphi}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{k}})
of
\mathcal{F}
as in (
1.3
) with
\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\varphi}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{l}})
is
\operatorname{C}_{\text{loc}}^{1,\alpha}
-partially regular if and only if
\mathbb{A}
is elliptic.

Theorem
1.2
can be understood as a limiting case for the reduction strategy from
[
20
]
. Namely, if we pass to
N
-functions
\varphi
of sub-logarithmic growth, then
\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{l}})
does not necessarily imply that
\nabla u\in\operatorname{L}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this sense, the previous theorem extends the reduction strategy optimally in this direction. We wish to point out that for the end point
p=1
, it is not possible to employ such a reduction strategy for elliptic operators. Here, partial regularity can still be established by use of a variant of
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic approximation for
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic operators; see
[
13
]
.

For completeness, we note that it is equally interesting to consider the other end of the scale, for example, integrands of exponential growth as in
Beck & Mingione

[
5
]
. To the best of our knowledge, very little is known about the partial regularity of minimizers even in the full gradient case. Since we are here primarily interested in implementing the reduction strategy of
[
20
]
to functionals (
1.3
), we thus defer this task to future work.

We briefly comment on the organization of the paper. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General notation

To enable a broad application of the operator
\mathbb{A}
, we consider our results in the context of finite-dimensional real inner product spaces
V
and
W
. This generalizes the specific choice of Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l}
from the introduction. Consequently, we consider from now on
u\colon\Omega\to V
and
\mathbb{A}u\colon\Omega\to W
.

We write
a\lesssim b
if
a\leq cb
and
a\gtrsim b
if
a\geq cb
for some constant
c>0
. If
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
, then we write
a\simeq b
. If necessary, we specify the dependence of the constant
c
. Moreover, we use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to represent the scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.2.
Orlicz spaces

Next, we provide the requisite background material on Orlicz spaces (see
[
2
,
51
]
). A function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called a
Young function
if it is convex, continuous, and satisfies
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.
A function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called an
N
-function
if it is differentiable and satisfies the following criteria:

•

\psi(0)=0
,

•

\psi^{\prime}
is right continuous and non-decreasing,

•

\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
,

•

\psi^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
, and

•

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\psi^{\prime}(t)=\infty
.

We say that an
N
-function

\psi
belongs to the class
\Delta_{2}
and write
\psi\in\Delta_{2}
, if there exists a constant
K>0
such that
\psi(2t)\leq K\psi(t)
holds for all
t\geq 0
. In this case, we denote the infimum of such constants
K
by

\Delta_{2}(\psi):=\inf\left\{K>0:\psi(2t)\leq K\psi(t)\text{ for all }t>0\right\}.

Analogously to the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, we define the
\nabla_{2}
-condition using the convex conjugate. Given an
N
function
\psi
, its convex conjugate
\psi^{*}
is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\psi^{*}(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}(st-\psi(s)),\quad t\geq 0.

An
N
-function
\psi
is of class
\nabla_{2}
if its conjugate
\psi^{*}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. We denote this by
\psi\in\nabla_{2}
and define

\nabla_{2}(\psi):=\Delta_{2}(\psi^{*}).

Given an
N
-function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define the
Luxemburg norm
of a measurable function
f:\Omega\to V
on a measurable set
\Omega
by

\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\psi}(\Omega;V)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,\differential{x}\leq 1\right\}.

The Orlicz space
\operatorname{L}^{\psi}(\Omega;V)
consists of all measurable functions
f:\Omega\to V
for which the Luxemburg norm is finite. For instance, if
\psi(t)=|t|^{p}
, the Orlicz space
\operatorname{L}^{\psi}(\Omega;V)
coincides with the classical Lebesgue space
\operatorname{L}^{p}(\Omega;V)
.
We now define the Orlicz-Sobolev space
\operatorname{W}^{k,\psi}(\Omega;V)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
as

\operatorname{W}^{k,\psi}(\Omega;V)=\left\{f\in\operatorname{L}^{\psi}(\Omega;V):\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}f\in\operatorname{L}^{\psi}(\Omega;V)\text{ for all }\alpha\text{ with }|\alpha|\leq k\right\},

where
\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}f
denotes the weak derivative of
f
corresponding to the multi-index
\alpha
. The norm in
\operatorname{W}^{k,\psi}(\Omega;V)
is given by

\|f\|_{\operatorname{W}^{k,\psi}(\Omega;V)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\psi}(\Omega;V)}.

We define
shifted
N
-functions
, as introduced in
[
25
,
26
]
. Consider an
N
-function
\varphi\in\operatorname{C}^{1}([0,\infty))\cap\operatorname{C}^{2}((0,\infty))
. For
a\geq 0
, the shifted
N
-function
\varphi_{a}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by:

(2.2)

\varphi_{a}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(a+s)}{a+s}s\,\differential{s},\quad t\geq 0.

A property of the shifted
N
-function is that
\Delta_{2}(\varphi_{a})
and
\nabla_{2}(\varphi_{a})
are uniformly comparable for all
a
. More precisely, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
26
, Remark 38]
)
.

For all
a\geq 0
, we have

\Delta_{2}(\varphi_{a})\simeq\Delta_{2}(\varphi)

and

\nabla_{2}(\varphi_{a})\simeq\nabla_{2}(\varphi),

where the underlying constants do not depend on
a
.

2.3.
Homogeneous differential operators

Our objective is to establish Theorem
4.1
for a broader class of homogeneous differential operators
\mathbb{A}
defined by

(2.3)

\mathbb{A}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u,

where
A_{i}:V\to W
are linear mappings. We define the
Fourier symbol
of
\mathbb{A}
as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}[\xi]:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\xi_{i},\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and it is moreover customary to put

\displaystyle a\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}b:=\mathbb{A}[b]a,\qquad a\in V,\;b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We say that
\mathbb{A}
has
constant rank
if the rank of
\mathbb{A}[\xi]
remains unchanged for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
. If

\ker(\mathbb{A}[\xi])=\{0\}

holds for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, we refer to
\mathbb{A}
as an
elliptic operator
. Examples of such operators include the gradient

\nabla u=\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}\right),\quad u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V,

and the symmetric gradient

\varepsilon(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}\right),\quad u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Later on, we aim to demonstrate the necessity of ellipticity to ensure that local minimizers are
\operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha}
-regular. To establish this, we will then use the following result:

Lemma 2.2

(cf.
[
20
, Cor. 4.3]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open, and let
\mathbb{A}
be an operator of constant rank that is not elliptic. Then there exists
v\in\ker{\mathbb{A}}\cap\operatorname{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;V)
such that
v\notin\operatorname{C}(\omega)
for any open
\omega\subset\Omega
.

For our future purposes, it is moreover convenient with the minimal choice of
W
:

Remark 2.3

(Choices of
W
)
.

If
\mathbb{A}
is a differential operator of the form (
2.3
) with
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then it is no loss of generality to assume that
W\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
. In fact, as established in
[
20
, Section 3]
, we may suppose that
W=\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})
with the essential range

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A}):=\mathrm{lin}(\{\mathbb{A}[e_{i}]v_{j}\colon\;i\in\{1,...,n\},\;j\in\{1,...,N\}\})

as introduced in
[
10
]
. The space
\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})
as given in (
2.5
) is the smallest space in which all values
\mathbb{A}v(x)
for
v\in\operatorname{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};V)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are contained. The necessity of considering
\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})
is due to the fact that, whenever
\mathbb{A}
is a
W
-valued differential operator, viewed as an
Z
-valued differential operator for every linear space with
W\hookrightarrow Z
. In this sense, (
2.5
) is the minimal space
W
with this property. As an explicit example, we note that the symmetric gradient can be viewed as a differential operator of the form (
2.3
) for both
W=\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
W=\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})=\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}
, but only
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}
has the requisite minimality property.

Elliptic operators come with convenient properties because they provide control over the entire gradient. This is specified by the following Korn-type inequality.

Theorem 2.4

(Korn-type inequality,
(\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2})
-growth
[
20
, Prop. 4.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an elliptic differential operator of the form (
2.3
). Moreover, let
\psi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
. Then there exists a constant
c=c(\mathbb{A},\Delta_{2}(\psi),\nabla_{2}(\psi))>0
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(|\mathrm{D}u|)\,\differential{x}\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(|\mathbb{A}u|)\,\differential{x}

holds for all
u\in\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};V)
.

This result can be established by use of Theorem
2.7
below, see
[
20
]
. Moreover, we note that the constant
c>0
can be chosen to depend monotonically on
\Delta_{2}(\psi)+\nabla_{2}(\psi)
, which will be crucial for our subsequent applications. Moreover, we note that (
2.6
) persists for domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and competitors
u\in\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;V)
(by extending
\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;V)
-maps to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by zero); for related Korn-type inequalities on Orlicz spaces without zero boundary values, the reader is e.g. referred to
[
8
,
27
,
29
]
.

2.4.
Calderón-Zygmund operators

We now collect some basic assertions from Calderón-Zygmund theory, cf.
[
30
]
.
A Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}:\operatorname{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})\to\operatorname{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
is defined for
m\in\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
by

T_{m}f:=\mathscr{F}^{-1}[m\hat{f}].

A Fourier multiplier operator
T_{m}
is a kernel operator if there exists a
k\in\operatorname{L}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{C})
such that

T_{m}(f)(x)=(k\ast f)(x)

holds for all
x\not\in\operatorname{supp}(f)
.
Moreover, a kernel operator
T_{m}
satisfies the Hörmander condition if the map

\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni y\mapsto\int\limits_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>2|y|\}}|k(x-y)-k(x)|\,\differential{x}

is uniformly bounded. We then have the following result:

Theorem 2.5

(Calderón-Zygmund, cf.
[
14
]
)
.

Let
m\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
and let
T_{m}
be a kernel operator that satisfies the Hörmander condition. Then
T_{m}
extends to a bounded linear operator
T_{m}:\operatorname{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})\to\operatorname{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
for
1<p<\infty
.

If
k
is such that an
m
exists with
T_{m}(f)(x)=(k\ast f)(x)
for all
x\not\in\operatorname{supp}(f)
and this
m
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.5
, then we say that
m
is a Calderón-Zygmund multiplier. For later applications, we also record the following theorem:

Theorem 2.6

(
[
3
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
T_{m}
be as in Theorem
2.5
and let
f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
for some
p>1
. Then there is a
C>0
, such that

(T_{m}f)^{*}(t)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t}\int\limits_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\differential{s}+\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}f^{*}(s)\frac{\differential{s}}{s}\right),

where for a measurable function
u

u^{*}(s):=\sup\{t\geq 0:|\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|>t\}|>s\}

denotes the
decreasing rearrangement
of
u
for
s>0
.

Finally, we record how Fourier multiplier operators can be expressed as Cauchy principal value singular integrals:

Theorem 2.7

(
[
30
, Theorem 4.13]
)
.

Let
m\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\};\mathbb{C})
be homogeneous of degree
0
. Then there exists
a\in\mathbb{C}
and a function
\Omega\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1};\mathbb{C})
with zero average such that

T_{m}f=af+\mathrm{p.v.}\frac{\Omega(x^{\prime})}{|x|^{n}}\ast f\qquad\text{for any}\;f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
x^{\prime}:=\frac{x}{|x|}
. Here,
\mathrm{p.v.}
denotes the Cauchy principal value as usual.

3.
On
\varphi
-strong
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity

In this section, we discuss the
\varphi
-strong
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity assumption as it appears in Theorem
1.1
, see
(F3)
, and give a convenient reformulation. Indeed, the latter clarifies the meaning of
\varphi
-strong
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity as a quasiconvex variant of
\varphi
-strong convexity. Moreover, we give connections of this notion to the coercivity of variational integrals (
1.3
) on suitable Dirichlet classes, which have been established first in the gradient case by
Chen & Kristensen

[
17
]
.

3.1.
Coercivity and existence of minimizers

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an elliptic differential operator of the form (
1.2
), and let
\varphi
be a Young function. For an open and bounded subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega):=\{u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega;V):\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega;W)\},

which is endowed with the norm

\norm{u}_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)}:=\norm{u}_{\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega)}+\norm{\mathbb{A}u}_{\operatorname{L}^{\varphi}(\Omega)}.

We then define
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)
as the closure of
\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;V)
with respect to
\norm{\cdot}_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega;V)}
.

For
u_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
, we then introduce the corresponding Dirichlet class

(3.1)

\operatorname{W}_{u_{0}}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega):=u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega).

Based on Remark
2.3
, it is no loss of generality to assume that
W=\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})\subset V\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we will do so in the sequel. Our main objective in this section is to establish the coercivity and the existence of minimizers of the functional
\mathcal{F}
on the Dirichlet classes (
3.1
). To this end, we need two auxiliary results, and first collect an inequality of Poincaré-type as follows:

Lemma 3.1

(of Poincaré-type)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. Moreover, let
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}
and suppose that
\mathbb{A}
is an elliptic first order differential operator of the form (
2.3
). Then there exists a constant
c=c(\Omega,\mathbb{A},\Delta_{2}(\varphi))>0
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\zeta|)\operatorname{d}\!x\leq c\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)\operatorname{d}\!x\qquad\text{for all}\;\zeta\in\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega).

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is a variant of the symmetric gradient convolution-type Poincaré inequality from
[
41
, Prop. 5.1]
.
First let
\zeta\in\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;V)
, which we think to be extended to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by zero. Since
\mathbb{A}
is elliptic,
\mathbb{A}[\xi]
is injective for each
\xi\neq 0
. In particular,
\mathbb{A}^{*}[\xi]\mathbb{A}[\xi]
is bijective for all
\xi\neq 0
, and so

\displaystyle\zeta=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\big((\mathbb{A}[\xi]^{*}\mathbb{A}[\xi])^{-1}\mathbb{A}^{*}[\xi]\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{A}\zeta)\big).

The latter expression can be written as a convolution integral

(3.3)

\displaystyle\zeta(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathfrak{K}(x-y)\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)\operatorname{d}\!y,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\mathfrak{K}\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\};\mathrm{Lin}(W;V))
is homogeneous of degree
(1-n)
together with

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{x\neq y}\frac{|\mathfrak{K}(x-y)|}{|x-y|^{1-n}}\leq\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|\mathfrak{K}(x-y)|}{|x-y|^{1-n}}<\infty.

Let
K(z):=\|\mathfrak{K}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
. Since
\mathfrak{K}
is homogeneous,
K(z)\simeq|z|^{1-n}
. Let

M(x):=\int\limits_{\Omega}K(x-y)\differential{y}.

Since
\Omega
is bounded,
M(x)
is finite for all
x\in\Omega
, and
M
is bounded from above and below:

\displaystyle 0<m^{\prime}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}M(x)\leq\sup_{x\in\Omega}M(x)=:M<\infty.

We define the probability measure
\nu_{x}
on
\Omega
by

\differential\nu_{x}(y):=\frac{K(x-y)}{M(x)}\differential y.

Therefore, and since
\zeta
is supported on
\Omega
,

\displaystyle|\zeta(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathfrak{K}(x-y)\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)\,\differential{y}\right|\leq M(x)\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{K(x-y)}{M(x)}|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|\differential{y}

\displaystyle=M(x)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|\differential{\nu_x(y)}.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest integer such that
M\leq 2^{m}
. By the
\Delta_{2}
-condition of
\varphi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(|\zeta(x)|)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi\left(2^{m}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|\operatorname{d}\!\nu_{x}(y)\right)\leq\Delta_{2}(\varphi)^{m}\cdot\varphi\left(\int_{\Omega}|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|\operatorname{d}\!\nu_{x}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{2}(\varphi)^{m}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|)\operatorname{d}\!\nu_{x}(y),

the latter by Jensen’s inequality. Integrating this over
\Omega
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\zeta(x)|)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{2}(\varphi)^{m}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|)\frac{K(x-y)}{M(x)}\differential{y}\differential{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{2}(\varphi)^{m}}{m^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|)\left(\int_{\Omega}K(x-y)\differential{x}\right)\operatorname{d}\!y

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{M\cdot\Delta_{2}(\varphi)^{m}}{m^{\prime}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta(y)|)\operatorname{d}\!y.

From here, (
3.2
) follows by smooth approximation.
∎

For the proof of Theorem
3.3
below, we moreover require the following auxiliary ingredient; its proof is a direct adaptation of the arguments given in
[
39
, Lemma 5.2]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Lipschitz-type bound)
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{C}(\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A}))
be
\mathbb{A}
-rank-one convex,
meaning that

\displaystyle t\mapsto F(z+ta\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}b),\qquad t\in[0,1]

is convex for all
z\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})
,
a\in V
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If there exists an
N
-function
\varphi
of class
\Delta_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|F(z)|\lesssim(1+\varphi(|z|))\qquad\text{for all}\;z\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A}),

then we have the estimate

\displaystyle|F(z)-F(w)|\lesssim\frac{\varphi(1+|z|+|w|)}{1+|z|+|w|}|z-w|\qquad\text{for all}\;z,w\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A}).

We now come to the main result of the present subsection:

Theorem 3.3

(Coercivity and existence of minimizers)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an elliptic differential operator of order one, and let
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
be an
N
-function; in particular, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}=\infty.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
and assuming that
u_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
, the variational integral
\mathcal{F}
as in (
1.3
) is
coercive
on
u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
. In particular, for every
u_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
there exists a minimizer
u
of
\mathcal{F}
over the affine Dirichlet class
u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
, meaning that
u
satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}u)\operatorname{d}\!x\leq\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}v)\operatorname{d}\!x\qquad\text{for all}\;v\in u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega).

In particular, the set of local minimizers is non-empty.

Proof.

We employ the direct method of the Calculus of Variations, and first establish that
\mathcal{F}
is coercive on Dirichlet classes
u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
. To this end, we let
\zeta\in\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. We put
u:=u_{0}+\zeta
and split

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}u)\operatorname{d}\!x&=\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}(u_{0}+\zeta))\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&=\Big(\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}(u_{0}+\zeta))\operatorname{d}\!x-\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}\zeta)\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)+\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}\zeta)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&=:\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}.\end{split}

For term
\mathrm{I}
, we first note that the definition of the convex conjugate (see (
2.1
)) gives us for all
0<\varepsilon<1
and all
t\geq 0
:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(\varepsilon t):=\sup_{s>0}s(\varepsilon t)-\varphi(s)\leq\sup_{s>0}\varepsilon(st-\varphi(s))=\varepsilon\varphi^{*}(t).

With
0<\varepsilon<1
to be fixed later, we now employ the definition of the convex conjugate in conjunction with Lemma
3.2
to find with a constant
c>0
that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{I}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varepsilon\frac{\varphi(1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)}{1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}\Big(\varepsilon\frac{\varphi(1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)}{1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}\Big(\frac{\varphi(1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)}{1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\varphi(1+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|+|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x=:\mathrm{I}^{\prime},

where the constants implicit in ’
\lesssim
’ do not depend on
\varepsilon
,
u_{0}
or
\zeta
. Since
\varphi
is of class
\Delta_{2}
, we conclude that

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{I}|\lesssim\mathrm{I}^{\prime}\leq c_{1}\Big(\varepsilon\Big(|\Omega|+\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\operatorname{d}\!x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)+\int_{\Omega}\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x\Big),

and as above,
c_{1}>0
does not depend on
\varepsilon
,
u_{0}
or
\zeta
.

For term
\mathrm{II}
, we first note that there exist constants
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{1}}\varphi(|z|)-d_{2}\leq\varphi_{2}(|z|)\qquad\text{for all}\;z\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A}).

Using the
\varphi
-strong
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity at zero and a routine technique (which lets us pass from the definition of
\mathbb{A}
-quasiconvexity on cubes to that on general domains
\Omega
), we therefore find that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&=\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}\zeta)-F(0)\operatorname{d}\!x+F(0)|\Omega|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{item:F3}}}{{\geq}}\nu_{1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{2}(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)\operatorname{d}\!x+F(0)|\Omega|\\
&\geq\nu_{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{d_{1}}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)-d_{2}\operatorname{d}\!x+F(0)|\Omega|.\end{split}

At this stage, we choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
so large such that
\varepsilon:=2^{-m}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0<2^{-m}=:\varepsilon\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{<}}\frac{\nu_{1}}{4\Delta_{2}(\varphi)c_{1}d_{1}}.

On the other hand, since
\varphi
is of class
\Delta_{2}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\varphi(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(|\mathbb{A}(u_{0}+\zeta)|+|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{$\varphi$ convex}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{2}\big(\varphi(2|\mathbb{A}(u_{0}+\zeta)|)+\varphi(2|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{2}(\varphi)\big(\varphi(|\mathbb{A}(u_{0}+\zeta)|)+\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\big).

Therefore, again using the
\Delta_{2}
-assumption on
\varphi
, gives us

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathrm{I}|&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ultimo}}}{{\leq}}\frac{\nu_{1}}{4d_{1}\Delta_{2}(\varphi)}|\Omega|+\frac{\nu_{1}}{4d_{1}\Delta_{2}(\varphi)}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\operatorname{d}\!x+\frac{\nu_{1}}{4d_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&+\frac{\nu_{1}}{4d_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u|)\operatorname{d}\!x+c_{1}\Delta(\varphi)^{m}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u_{0}|)\operatorname{d}\!x.\end{split}

In consequence, merging this estimate with (
3.6
) and (
3.9
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\mathbb{A}u)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{II}-|\mathrm{I}|\geq c(\Delta_{2}(\varphi),m,u_{0},|\Omega|,F)+\frac{3\nu_{1}}{4d_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\mathbb{A}u|)\operatorname{d}\!x,

where
c(\Delta_{2}(\varphi),m,u_{0},|\Omega|,F)\in\mathbb{R}
is independent of
\zeta
.

This estimate tells us that
\mathcal{F}
is bounded below and coercive on
u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
in the sense that

\displaystyle((u_{j})\subset u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)\;\;\;\text{satisfies}\;\;\;\limsup_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)}=\infty)\Rightarrow\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}[u_{j}]=\infty.

Hence,
\mathfrak{m}:=\inf_{u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}}\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{R}
, and we find a sequence
(u_{j})\subset u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
such that
\mathcal{F}[u_{j}]\to\mathfrak{m}
. By the Poincaré-type inequality from Lemma
3.1
, we infer that
(u_{j})
is bounded in
(\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)})
. By our choice of
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, this space is reflexive. Hence, there exists a (not relabeled) subsequence
(u_{j})
and an element
u\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
such that
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
in
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
. The Dirichlet class
u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
is convex and closed with respect to the norm topology, and so it is weakly closed. Therefore,
u\in u_{0}+\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
. In conclusion, the minimality of
u
follows if we can show that
\mathcal{F}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to weak convergence on Dirichlet subclasses of
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
.

To this end, recall that
W=\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{A})
. We consider
G(z):=F(\pi_{\mathbb{A}}(z))
, where
\pi_{\mathbb{A}}
is a linear map such that
\mathbb{A}v=\pi_{\mathbb{A}}(\nabla v)
for all
v\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};V)
. Since
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, Theorem
2.4
implies that
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|\varphi(|\nabla u_{j}|)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega)}<\infty
. Since
|G(z)|\lesssim 1+\varphi(|z|)
for all
z\in W
,
[
44
, Prop. 10.9]
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(\nabla u)\operatorname{d}\!x\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G(\nabla u_{j})\operatorname{d}\!x,

and this directly yields the requisite lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{F}
. Summarizing, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[u]\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}[u_{j}]=\mathfrak{m},

and so
u
is a minimizer. This immediately implies that
u
is a local minimizer, and so the claim follows.
∎

We end this subsection with two remarks.

Remark 3.4
.

If
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, then
\operatorname{W}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega;V)=\operatorname{W}_{0}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)
. Hence, in this case, the preceding result and its proof can be directly read as a coercivity and existence theorem on the classical Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
\operatorname{W}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega;V)
. Note however that the stronger statement
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\varphi}(\Omega)=\operatorname{W}^{1,\varphi}(\Omega;V)
only holds provided
\mathbb{A}
satisfies the stronger condition of
\mathbb{C}
-ellipticity.

Remark 3.5
.

It is also possible to generalize Theorem
3.3
to
\varphi(t)=t\log(1+t)
, even though this does not follow by reduction to full gradient functionals. This, however, can be achieved by employing a trace-preserving operator as in
[
44
, Prop. 10.9]
for
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic operators, and we shall pursue this in a future work.

3.2.
Equivalent definitions of
\varphi
-strong quasiconvexity

Usually, the definition of strong
\varphi
-quasiconvexity is not given by the gradient version of
(F3)
, but rather by the following condition: For each
M>0
there exists
\nu_{M}>0
such that

(3.11)

\int_{(0,1)^{n}}\left[F(z_{0}+\nabla\zeta)-F(z_{0})\right]\differential{x}\geq\nu_{M}\int_{(0,1)^{n}}V_{\varphi}(z_{0}+\nabla\zeta)-V_{\varphi}(z_{0})

holds for all
|z_{0}|\leq M
and all
\zeta\in\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)^{n};V)
. Here, we work with the auxiliary
V
-function

V_{\varphi}(z):=\varphi\left(\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}\right)-\varphi(1),\qquad z\in X,

whenever
(X,|\cdot|)
is a normed space (see also, for example,
[
15
,
20
,
45
,
52
,
53
]
). This reformulation asserts that, for each
M>0
and each
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
with
|z_{0}|\leq M
, there exists
\nu_{M}>0
such that

(3.12)

\displaystyle z\mapsto F(z)-\nu_{M}V_{\varphi}(z)\qquad\text{is quasiconvex at $z_{0}$}.

We will establish in Theorem
3.6
that both definitions are equivalent. However, (
3.12
) tells us that strong
\varphi
-quasiconvexity can be read as the quasiconvex analogue of strong convexity for integrands of
\varphi
-growth; the latter corresponds to requiring that, for each
M>0
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
with
|z_{0}|\leq M
, there exists
\nu_{M}>0
such that

\displaystyle z\mapsto F(z)-\nu_{M}V_{\varphi}(z)\qquad\text{is convex at $z_{0}$}.

The following lemma yields the equivalence of the two aforementioned versions of strong
\varphi
-quasiconvexity:

Theorem 3.6

(Equivalence of
\varphi
-strong quasiconvexity conditions)
.

Let
M>0
and
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
. For all
|z_{0}|\leq M
and
\zeta\in\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)^{n};V)
it holds

\int_{(0,1)^{n}}V_{\varphi}(z_{0}+\nabla\zeta)-V_{\varphi}(z_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x\simeq\int_{(0,1)^{n}}\varphi_{1+|z_{0}|}(|\nabla\zeta|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where here and in what follows the underlying constants depend only on
M
and
\varphi
. Similarly, we have that

\int_{(0,1)^{n}}V_{\varphi}(z_{0}+\mathbb{A}\zeta)-V_{\varphi}(z_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x\simeq\int_{(0,1)^{n}}\varphi_{1+|z_{0}|}(|\mathbb{A}\zeta|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the underlying constants again only depend on
M
and
\varphi
.

For the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we record three auxiliary lemmas. We begin with:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be strictly monotonically increasing with either

(a)

\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
or

(b)

\varphi(t)=t\log(1+t)
,
t\geq 0
,

and let
M>0
be given.
Then there exists a constant
S>3M
and
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all
\xi
with
|\xi|\geq S
and all
z_{0}
with
|z_{0}|\leq M
it holds

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\varphi(\sqrt{1+|z_{0}+\xi|^{2}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\varphi(\sqrt{1+|z_{0}|^{2}})\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(1+|z_{0}|+|\xi|)-\varphi(1+|z_{0}|)

(3.14)

Following the strategy of

As above, we let

5.1.

Now we consider the case where

Theorem 5.1

(Korn-type inequality in Orlicz spaces)

Let
\Omega

, and let

be a homogeneous, first order, elliptic differential operator of the form (

hold for all

and some

such that

\int\limits_{\Omega}\Phi(|\nabla u|)\,\differential{x}\lesssim\int\limits_{\Omega}\Psi(C|\mathbb{A}u|)\,\differential{x}\qquad\text{for all}\;u\in\operatorname{W}_{0}^{1,\Psi}(\Omega;V).

In particular, inequalities (
5.1
) and (

) imply that
\Psi
dominates
\Phi
globally (cf.
[

, Proposition 3.5]
), which means that there is a

such that

\Psi(t)\leq\Phi(Ct)

for all

. For the proof of Theorem

Lemma 5.2

(
[
19

The following holds:

(a)

Assume that (

(0,\infty)

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{1}{s}\int\limits_{0}^{s}u(r)\differential{r}\Big)\differential{s}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(2cu(s))\differential{s}

holds for every nonnegative measurable function

Proof of Theorem

Even though the operator

Let

Lemma 5.6
.

There are
c,\tilde{c}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
, it holds

\Phi^{*}(t)\gtrsim\begin{cases}\max\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},c_{1}(t-\tilde{c})+c_{2}\}&t\leq\tilde{c}\\
\infty&t>\tilde{c}\end{cases}

and

\Psi^{*}(t)\lesssim\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},c_{4}+c\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)\right\}.

Proof.

First, we compute
\Phi^{*}(t)
:

\Phi^{*}(t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\left(st-\Phi(s)\right).

Depending on the value of
s
, we have two cases:

(a)

s\leq c_{1}
:
Since
\Phi(s)\simeq s^{2}
we have
st-\Phi(s)\geq st-cs^{2},

for some
c>0
.
To find the maximum, we set the derivative
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(st-cs^{2})=t-2cs
to zero:

t-2cs=0\implies s=\frac{t}{2c}.

This gives us

st-cs^{2}=\frac{t}{2c}\cdot t-c\left(\frac{t}{2c}\right)^{2}=\frac{t^{2}}{4c}.

(b)

s>c_{1}
: Since
\Phi(s)\simeq s-c_{5}
, we can choose
\tilde{c}>0
such that

st-\Phi(s)\geq st-\tilde{c}(s-c_{5})=st-\tilde{c}s+\tilde{c}c_{5}=s(t-\tilde{c})+\tilde{c}c_{5}.

Here, the result depends on whether
t\leq\tilde{c}
or
t>\tilde{c}
.

(a)

For
t\leq\tilde{c}
, the term
s(t-\tilde{c})
is not positive, so the supremum is

c_{1}(t-\tilde{c})+c_{5}.

(b)

If
t>\tilde{c}
, then
(t-\tilde{c})
is positive, therefore

s(t-\tilde{c})+c_{5}\to\infty

for
s\to\infty
.

In summary, we get:

\Phi^{*}(t)\gtrsim\begin{cases}\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},c_{1}(t-\tilde{c})+c_{5}\right\}&t\leq\tilde{c}\\
\infty&t>\tilde{c}.\end{cases}

Now, we compute
\Psi^{*}(t)
:

\Psi^{*}(t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\left(st-\Psi(s)\right).

Depending on the value of
s
, we have two cases:

(a)

s\leq c_{1}
:
In this case
\Psi(s)\simeq s^{2}
implies that there is a
c>0
such that,
st-\Psi(s)\leq st-cs^{2}.

As before, the maximum of the right-hand side is

\frac{t^{2}}{4c}

and is therefore an upper bound for the left-hand side.

(b)

s>c_{1}
:
Since

\Psi(s)\simeq(s+c_{3})\log(s+c_2)-c_{4}
, we find a
c>0
such that

st-\Psi(s)\leq st-c\left[(s+c_{3})\log(s+c_2)-c_{4}\right].

An upper bound for this can be obtained by using
\log(s+c_2)\geq\log(s)
and
(s+c_{3})\geq s
:

st-c(s+c_{3})\log(s+c_2)+cc_{4}\leq st-cs\log(s)+cc_{4}.

To find the maximum of

, we set the derivative to zero:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(st-cs\log(s))

\displaystyle=t-c\log(s)-c=0

\displaystyle\implies\log(s)=\frac{t}{c}-1

\displaystyle\implies s=\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right).

Since the second derivative
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s^{2}}(st-cs\log(s))=-\frac{c}{s}

Evaluating at

\displaystyle st-cs\log(s)

\displaystyle=t\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)-c\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)\log\left(\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=t\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)-c\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)

\displaystyle=c\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right).

Thus, we get:

\Psi^{*}(t)\lesssim\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{4},c_{4}+c\exp\left(\frac{t}{c}-1\right)\right\}.

This completes the proof.

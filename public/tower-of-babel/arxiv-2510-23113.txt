Revisiting Very High Energy Gamma-Ray Absorption in Cosmic Propagation under the Combined Effects of Axion-Like Particles and Lorentz Violation
Longhua Qin, Jiancheng Wang,Chuyuan Yang, Huaizhen Li, Quangui Gao, Ju Ma, Ao Wang, Weiwei Na, Ming Zhou, Zunli Yuan and Chunxia Gu

Abstract
Very high energy (VHE) gama rays above 100 GeV are expected to undergo significant attenuation during cosmic propagation due to pair production with thr extralactic background light (EBL). However, recent observations - particulary the tentative detection of gamma ray burst GRB 221009A up to 18 TeV by LHASSO and up to 251 TeV by Carpet-2, challenge the predictions of classical EBL absorption models. These exceptionally high-energy photons suggest the possibility of new physics affecting photon propagation over cosmological distances. In this context, the gamma-ray spectrum in the tens to hundreds of TeV range serves as a valuable probe for potential Lorentz invariance violation (LIV) effects or for modifications to photon attenuation due to axion-like particles (ALPs) mixing with photons in cosmic magnetic fields. However, both LIV and ALPs explanations, when considered separately, face certain limitations in fully accounting for the observed transparency. In this paper, we propose a unified framework that combines the effects of ALPs and LIV to explain the unexpectedly high survival probability of VHE photons. Specifically, we investigate the multi-wavelength spectrum of GRB 221009A under this synergistic scenario, demonstrating that for photons at 18 TeV and 251 TeV, the combined influence of ALPs with coupling g a ​ γ = 13.353 × 10 − 11 ​ GeV − 1 g_{a\gamma}=13.353\times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-1} and mass m a = 9.492 × 10 − 7 ​ eV m_{a}=9.492\times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{eV} , together with LIV characterized by energy scales E 1 = 2.4 ​ E Planck E_{1}=2.4~E_{\rm Planck} for n = 1 n=1 , can substantially enhance the photon survival probability. This synergy offers a compelling explanation for the observed VHE gamma-ray transparency in extreme astrophysical environments.



I
Introduction

Very high energy (VHE) gamma rays, typically those with energies exceeding 100 GeV, are expected to undergo significant attenuation during their propagation through intergalactic space. This attenuation arises from interactions with the extragalactic background light (EBL), which induce electron-positron pair production. Due to the absorption of high-energy gamma photons by the EBL, photons with energies greater than 30 TeV are, in fact, extremely difficult to propagate over cosmological distances and be detected
(
Qin et al. 2023
, see e.g.)
.

Despite theoretical expectations of substantial attenuation due to pair production with EBL, observations of VHE gamma rays from distant blazars consistently reveal a level of transparency exceeding standard model predictions
(
Carmona et al. 2024
, see e.g.)
. This tension significantly challenges us to understanding of gamma-ray propagation. Initial evidence arose from H.E.S.S. observations of blazars such as H 2356-309 (
z=0.165
) and 1ES 1101-232 (
z=0.186
), whose spectra could only be reconciled with EBL densities substantially below established models
(
Aharonian et al. 2006
)
. Additional support came from MAGIC observations of the high-redshift AGN 3C 279 (
z=0.54
)
(
MAGIC Collaboration et al. 2008
)
. A systematic trend of reduced absorption in the VHE spectral slopes of AGN at
z>0.2
was also reported by
De Angelis et al. 2013
, further reinforcing the anomaly. Besides, the recent detection of multi-TeV gamma rays from GRB 221009A (
z=0.151
), including photons up to 18 TeV observed by LHAASO
(
Cao et al. 2023
)
and a 251 TeV photon candidate reported by the Carpet collaboration
(
Dzhappuev et al. 2022
;
Dzhappuev et al. 2025
)
, presents a significant tension with standard models of gamma-ray propagation. Under conventional EBL attenuation models, the observed spectrum-associated with the most luminous GRB ever recorded-would necessitate an unphysical intrinsic spectral upturn to account for the transmitted flux at these energies, even when adopting the lowest EBL density estimates. This discrepancy strongly motivates the investigation of beyond-standard-model scenarios, such as axion-like particles (ALPs) and Lorentz invariance violation (LIV), that could effectively reduce EBL-induced attenuation and increase the gamma-ray transparency of the universe.

ALPs are pseudo-scalar bosons predicted in various extensions of the Standard Model, particularly in string theory and extra-dimensional frameworks. Unlike the Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) axion, ALPs are not required to solve the strong CP problem and can span a wide parameter space in mass
m_{a}
and photon coupling
g_{a\gamma}

(
Abbott & Sikivie 1983
;
Conlon 2006
, see e.g.)
. Their interaction with photons is described by the Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-\frac{1}{4}g_{a\gamma}aF_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=g_{a\gamma}a\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B},

(1)

where
a
is the ALPs field,
F_{\mu\nu}
the electromagnetic field tensor, and
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
its dual. This coupling enables photon-ALPs conversions in external magnetic fields
(
Raffelt & Stodolsky 1988
)
, a process that can significantly affect high energy photon propagation over cosmological distances.

LIV, a cornerstone of special relativity, may be violated in some quantum gravity frameworks such as string theory and loop quantum gravity
(
Amelino-Camelia et al. 1998
;
Mattingly 2005
;
Ellis et al. 2008
, see e.g.)
. In such LIV scenarios, the photon dispersion relation is modified as

E^{2}-p^{2}c^{2}=\pm E^{2}\left(\frac{E}{E_{\rm QG}}\right)^{n},

(2)

where
E_{\rm QG}
is the quantum gravity scale (near
E_{\rm Planck}\sim 10^{28}~\mathrm{eV}
), and
n
denotes the leading-order correction,that
n=1
or
2
corresponds to linear or quadratic leading-order LIV corrections, respectively. The ”
+
” represents superluminal LIV and the ”
-
” sign corresponds to the subluminal case. LIV leads to two notable astrophysical consequences. First, photon speed becomes energy-dependent, resulting in an energy-dependent time delay for photons emitted simultaneously from distant transients such as GRBs. Time-of-flight measurements from such sources have placed lower bounds on
E_{\rm QG}

(
Abdo et al. 2009
;
Ellis et al. 2019
, see e.g.)
. Second, and more relevant to gamma ray absorption, LIV modifies the kinematic threshold for photon-photon pair production (
\gamma+\gamma\rightarrow e^{+}+e^{-}
). In the subluminal case, this threshold is raised, thereby reducing the interaction cross section at a given energy. As a result, the optical depth
\tau_{\gamma\gamma}
is suppressed at very high energies, increasing the Universe’s transparency to TeV gamma rays from distant sources
(
Jacob & Piran 2008
, see e.g.)
.

ALPs and LIV provide distinct frameworks to explain the anomalous transparency of VHE gamma rays. ALPs introduce a new light pseudo-scalar particle that can oscillate with photons in cosmic magnetic fields, allowing gamma rays to partially evade attenuation by EBL. In contrast, LIV involves a fundamental modification of spacetime symmetries, leading to altered photon dispersion relations and a shift in the pair production threshold. Historically, several studies have shown that photon–ALPs mixing can enhance the transparency of the Universe to VHE gamma rays
(
de Angelis et al. 2008
;
Long et al. 2020
;
Cao et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, while interest in LIV-induced modifications to the pair-production threshold grew following observations of high energy blazars
(
Kifune 1999
;
Stecker & Glashow 2001
, e.g.,)
and, more recently, gamma ray bursts such as GRB 221009A
(
Finke & Razzaque 2023
;
Zheng et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
. However, there is no fundamental reason to exclude the simultaneous presence of ALPs and LIV in explaining the anomalous transparency of gamma rays over cosmological distances
(
Galanti & Roncadelli 2025
)
.

Based on the above considerations, we construct a model of TeV gamma-ray absorption that simultaneously accounts for both ALPs and LIV, and evaluate its viability using observational data from GRB 221009A. As the most energetic gamma-ray burst ever recorded, with a redshift of
z=0.151

(
de Ugarte Postigo et al. 2022
)
, GRB 221009A exhibits exceptional brightness and broad spectral coverage
(
Cao et al. 2019
;
Cao et al. 2023
)
, offering a unique opportunity to probe
\gamma\gamma
attenuation over cosmological distances.

We first model the survival probability
P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}(E,z)
of VHE gamma rays in a scenario incorporating both photon–ALPs oscillations and LIV-induced modifications to the pair-production threshold. This model is then simplified and applied to interpret the observed multi-wavelength spectra of GRBs. To reconstruct the intrinsic emission spectrum, we adopt a power-law form,
\phi_{\rm PL}(E)=\phi_{0}(E/E_{0})^{-\alpha},

where
\phi_{0}
is the normalization constant and
E_{0}=1\ \mathrm{TeV}
is the reference energy. The resulting observed spectral energy distribution (SED) is then given by

E^{2}\frac{{\rm d}N}{{\rm d}E}=P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}(E,z)\phi_{\rm PL}(E),

(3)

in units of erg cm
-2
s
-1
. We then perform Bayesian parameter inference using the Python package UltraNest
(
Buchner 2021
)
to obtain posterior distributions of model parameters. The LIV-modified EBL optical depth is computed using the publicly available code ebltable

, adopting the EBL model from
Finke et al. 2022
.

In this work, a flat

\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
,
\Omega_{M}=0.3
, and

. Additionally, the unprimed and primed quantities denote the quantities in the observer’s frame and the co-moving frame, respectively.

II
Model and strategy

The evolution of an photon–ALPs beam propagating through an external magnetic field along the
z
-axis is governed by a Schrödinger-like equation that accounts for mixing between photons and ALPs. The system is described by the state vector
\psi(z)=(A_{x},A_{y},a)^{T}
,

i\frac{d\psi(z)}{dz}=M\psi(z),

(4)

where
A_{x}
and
A_{y}
denote the photon amplitudes for linear
x
- and
y
-polarizations, and
a
is the ALPs amplitude. The mixing matrix
M
, expressed in the interaction basis
(
Long et al. 2020
)
, is given by:

M=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{\perp}&0&0\\
0&\Delta_{\parallel}&\Delta_{a\gamma}\\
0&\Delta_{a\gamma}&\Delta_{aa}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
\Delta_{\perp}=\Delta_{\text{pl}}+2\Delta_{\text{QED}}+\Delta_{\text{CMB}}+\frac{i}{2\lambda_{\gamma}}
, and

\Delta_{\parallel}=\Delta_{\text{pl}}+\frac{7}{2}\Delta_{\text{QED}}+\Delta_{\text{CMB}}+\frac{i}{2\lambda_{\gamma}}
represent the effective dispersion relations for the
x
- and
y
-polarized photon modes, including contributions from plasma effects, vacuum birefringence due to quantum electrodynamics (QED), photon scattering
(
Dobrynina et al. 2015
)
with the cosmic microwave background (CMB), and absorption by EBL
(
Long et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. Noted that ALPs couple only to the photon polarization component lying in the plane defined by the transverse magnetic field
\mathbf{B}_{T}
and the propagation direction. By choosing a coordinate frame in which
\mathbf{B}_{T}
is aligned along the
y
-axis, only the
A_{y}
component mixes with the ALPs, thereby simplifying the dynamics. The off-diagonal term
\Delta_{a\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}g_{a\gamma}B_{T}
quantifies the photon–ALPs coupling strength, proportional to the photon–ALPs coupling constant
g_{a\gamma}
and the transverse magnetic field
B_{T}
. The ALPs diagonal term
\Delta_{aa}=-\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{2E_{\gamma}}
accounts for the effective mass contribution of the ALPs, where
E_{\gamma}
is the photon energy. The numerical values for the different
\Delta
can be found in
Horns et al. 2012
.

The system’s statistical evolution is captured by the density matrix
\rho(z)=\psi(z)\psi^{\dagger}(z)
, which obeys the von Neumann-like equation
(
Bassan et al. 2010
)
:

i\frac{d\rho(z)}{dz}=[\rho(z),M],

(6)

ensuring the conservation of probability and Hermiticity. In a homogeneous region of size
\Delta z
, the formal solution is
(
Horns et al. 2012
;
De Angelis et al. 2013
, e.g.,)
:

\psi(z+\Delta z)=U\psi(z),\quad\text{with}\quad U=\exp(-iM\Delta z),

(7)

\rho(z+\Delta z)=U\rho(z)U^{\dagger},

(8)

where
U
is the unitary evolution operator over the interval
\Delta z
.

For an initially unpolarized photon beam with no ALPs component, the initial density matrix is:

\rho(0)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

The probability of a photon remaining a photon after propagation is obtained by tracing over the photon subspace
(
Horns et al. 2012
;
De Angelis et al. 2013
;
Long et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
:

P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\rho(z)\right]=\rho_{11}(z)+\rho_{22}(z),

(10)

while the photon-to-ALPs conversion probability is
P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}(z)=\rho_{33}(z)
.

Gamma ray photons originating from TeV sources undergo continuous interconversion with ALPs as they propagate through various astrophysical environments, including jet/host galaxies, intergalactic space, and the Milky Way. Throughout this propagation, the gamma rays are subject to attenuation due to interactions with EBL. This attenuation manifests as an imaginary term,
\frac{i}{2\lambda_{\gamma}}
, in the mixing matrix
M
(see Eq.
5
), representing the photon absorption probability. To compute this term, we evaluate the gamma-ray mean free path
\lambda_{\gamma}
using an expression for the optical depth
\tau(E,z)
that accounts for potential LIV effects. The mean free path of a photon with energy
E
emitted at redshift
z
is given by
(
Long et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
:

\lambda_{\gamma}(E,z)=\frac{D(z)}{\tau(E,z)},

(11)

where
D(z)
is the comoving distance to the source, and
\tau(E,z)
is the optical depth arising from
\gamma\gamma\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}
pair production with background photons.

The optical depth
\tau(E,z)
for a gamma-ray photon with observed dimensionless energy
\epsilon_{s}=E/(m_{e}c^{2})
, interacting with background photons of proper-frame energy density
u_{p}(\epsilon_{p};z)
, is given by
Gould & Schréder 1967

\displaystyle\tau(E,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c\pi r_{e}^{2}}{\epsilon_{s}^{2}m_{e}c^{2}}\ \int^{z}_{0}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{(1+z^{\prime})^{2}}\ \left|\frac{dt_{*}}{dz^{\prime}}\right|\

\displaystyle\times\int^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{s}(1+z^{\prime})}}d\epsilon_{p}\frac{\epsilon_{p}u_{p}(\epsilon_{p};z^{\prime})}{\epsilon_{p}^{4}}\ \bar{\phi}(\epsilon_{p}\epsilon_{s}(1+z^{\prime}))\ ,

(12)

where
r_{e}
is the classical electron radius,
m_{e}
is the electron mass, and
\bar{\phi}(s_{0})
encodes the pair-production cross-section as a function of the center-of-mass energy squared. The differential time–redshift relation in a standard flat
\Lambda
CDM cosmology is:

\frac{dt}{dz}=\frac{-1}{H_{0}(1+z)\sqrt{\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{\Lambda}}},

(13)

where
H_{0}
is the Hubble constant, and
\Omega_{m}
and
\Omega_{\Lambda}
are the present-day matter and dark energy density parameters, respectively.

To incorporate the effects of Lorentz Invariance Violation, the photon energy in the threshold condition for pair production is modified. This is implemented by replacing
\epsilon_{s}
with an effective energy

Finke & Razzaque 2023

\epsilon_{s}\rightarrow\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{1+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{s}m_{e}c^{2}}{E_{\rm QG}}\right)^{n}\epsilon_{s}^{2}}.

III
Application and results

Gamma-ray photons emitted from a TeV source propagate through the magnetic fields of the jet/host system (S), intergalactic space (I), and the Milky Way (M) before reaching the observer. The general expression for the photon survival probability is:

P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}=P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{S}}\left(P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{I}}P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}+P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{I}}P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}\right)+P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{S}}\left(P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{I}}P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}+P_{a\rightarrow a}^{\text{I}}P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}\right).

(15)

Given the weakness and uncertainty of the intergalactic magnetic field (IGMF), photon–ALPs mixing in this region is neglected, yielding
P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{I}}=e^{-\tau_{\gamma\gamma}},\;P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{I}}=P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{I}}=0,\;P_{a\rightarrow a}^{\text{I}}=1
. We further assume negligible photon-to-ALPs conversion in the Milky Way, i.e.,
P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}\approx 1
, and approximate the photon survival probability in the source region as
P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{S}}\approx 1-P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{S}}
. In addition, we incorporate the effects of LIV by replacing the standard optical depth with its LIV-corrected counterpart derived in Eq.
14
, i.e.,
\tau_{\gamma\gamma}\rightarrow\tau_{\text{LIV},\gamma\gamma}
, while keeping all other terms unchanged. Under these assumptions, the total photon survival probability simplifies to
P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}\approx(1-P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{S}})e^{-\tau_{\text{LIV},\gamma\gamma}}+P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{S}}P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}.
In the strong-mixing regime, where conversion saturates, the relation
P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}=2P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}
holds, as
P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}\to 1/3

(
Long et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2024
, see e.g.)
.In the single domain approximation, the photon conversion probability is given by
Mirizzi & Montanino 2009
;
de Angelis et al. 2011
;
Galanti & Roncadelli 2018

P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}(E)=\sin^{2}(2\theta)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta_{\rm osc}L}{2}\right),

(16)

where

\displaystyle\tan(2\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Delta_{a\gamma}}{\Delta_{aa}-\Delta_{\parallel}},

(17)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm osc}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(\Delta_{aa}-\Delta_{\parallel})^{2}+4\Delta_{a\gamma}^{2}},

(18)

We utilized the observational data of GRB 221009A from LHAASO
(
Cao et al. 2023
)

(
Finke & Razzaque 2023
)
. To simplify the modeling of photon–ALPs mixing, we neglect the QED vacuum polarization and the CMB-induced photon dispersion terms in the mixing matrix. This is justified by the approximate scaling relations
\Delta_{\mathrm{QED}}\propto E_{\gamma}\cdot B_{T}^{2}
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{CMB}}\propto E_{\gamma}\cdot(1+z)^{3}
. Given that the transverse magnetic field
B_{T}
is of the order of a few microgauss and the redshift of the GRB is
z<0.5
, similar to the case considered in
Chen et al. 2024
, both contributions are subdominant compared to the photon–ALPs coupling and plasma effects in the energy range of interest.

Additionally, we account for the stochastic orientation of the magnetic field in each domain, as considered in
Grossman et al. 2002
;
Chen et al. 2024

L\gg r
, where
r
is the coherence length of a single domain and
P_{0}
is the photon-to-ALPs conversion probability within a single domain, the expression for the total conversion probability was originally derived in
Grossman et al. 2002

To characterize the propagation of VHE gamma rays from GRB 221009A through the Milky Way, we adopt a magnetic field strength of 0.5
\mu

Next, we fit the multi-wavelength energy spectrum of GRB 221009A using LHAASO’s observational data during the 300–900 s interval, with and without inclusion of the 251 TeV photon. To accelerate the fitting process, we adopted typical values for the LIV scale in the range of 5–10

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

We propose a TeV absorption model that incorporates both axion-like particles and Lorentz invariance violation. Based on this framework, we construct a simplified version of the model and apply it to interpret the VHE gamma ray emission from GRB 221009A observed by LHAASO during the 300–900 s interval, as well as the 251 TeV photon reported by Carpet-2. In this analysis, we consider only the case of LIV with
n=1
. Furthermore, we do not address whether the 251 TeV photon and the photons detected by LHAASO originate from the same emission region, and we adopt simplified assumptions for the ALPs component.

As shown in Fig.
1
and
2
our model provides a good explanation for the 251 TeV photon. If the 251 TeV data point is excluded, the observed energy spectrum can be well fitted using an ALPs-only scenario. However, when the 251 TeV photon is included, the LIV contribution becomes essential. Additionally, we find that within the
n=1
LIV framework, a reasonable fit to the data can also be achieved by considering only LIV, provided that the quantum gravity scale is set to
E_{\rm QG}=2.4~E_{\rm Planck}
.

If the ALPs–LIV hybrid model is further simplified by omitting both the ALPs and LIV components-for example, by setting
P_{\gamma\rightarrow a}^{\text{S}}=P_{a\rightarrow\gamma}^{\text{M}}=0
—then the total photon survival probability,
P_{\gamma\rightarrow\gamma}
, reduces to
e^{-\tau_{\gamma\gamma}}
, which corresponds to the standard attenuation due to EBL absorption.

In summary, our simplified model offers a reasonable explanation for the observed multi-wavelength energy spectrum of GRB 221009A, while providing considerable flexibility in interpreting the VHE gamma-ray emission. The ALPs component can self-consistently account for spectral features in the TeV range (from several to tens of TeV), whereas the LIV component is essential for explaining the detection of gamma-ray photons at even higher energies.

Looking ahead, we suggest that future TeV sources emitting photons in the tens to hundreds of TeV range may be detectable by LHAASO, potentially providing further evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model. To rigorously test our model, a more detailed understanding of the structural properties of the jet/host system, the intergalactic medium, and the Milky Way environment is essential. In particular, solving the photon survival probability through numerical integration of the full transfer equation, as described in Section 2, would enable a more accurate description of propagation effects. Additionally, the LIV effect may be independently constrained through the analysis of light curve variability
(
Cao et al. 2024
, see e.g.)
, while the ALPs parameters
g_{a\gamma}
and
m_{a}
can be extracted through spectral fitting of VHE observational data.

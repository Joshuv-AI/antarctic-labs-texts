Relationship between cardiac troponin and myocardial infarction: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Siyuan Yin, Ji Wang, Yiming Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMyocardial infarction (MI) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Cardiac troponin (cTn) is the cornerstone biomarker for MI diagnosis. This study aims to systematically evaluate research trends, influential contributors, and emerging hotspots in the field exploring the relationship between cTn and MI.MethodsPublications on cTn and MI between January 1987 and December 2025 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Bibliometric and visualization analyses were performed using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the R package “bibliometrix”.ResultsA total of 5,352 research articles were included, authored by 25,708 researchers from 5,615 institutions in 93 countries/regions. China (1,068 articles), the United States (1,013), and Germany (351) were the most prolific countries. University of Basel led in institutional output, while Clinical Chemistry published the most papers. Circulation ranked highest in both h-index and total citations. Leading authors included Apple FS, Mueller Christian, and Jaffe AS. Keyword co-occurrence analysis identified four principal research clusters: (1) clinical guidelines and disease management, (2) biomarker assays and laboratory methods, (3) diagnosis and risk assessment, and (4) pathophysiology and mechanisms. Temporal and burst keyword analyses revealed a clear evolution: early research focused on traditional cardiac markers and basic mechanisms, while recent years have seen a shift toward high-sensitivity troponin assays, rapid diagnostic algorithms, precision risk stratification, and long-term outcome prediction.ConclusionsThe field has evolved from foundational studies of myocardial injury mechanisms and biomarker development to clinical translation and, most recently, to precision diagnostics and personalized outcome prediction. Ongoing advances in high-sensitivity troponin assays and data-driven risk models are likely to further enhance early detection, individualized management, and prognostic assessment in MI.Keywords: Bibliometric analysis, Cardiac troponin, Myocardial infarction, VOSviewer, CiteSpace

Background

Myocardial infarction (MI), commonly known as a heart attack, remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, despite advances in cardiovascular medicine [1, 2]. According to the World Health Organization, cardiovascular diseases, including MI, are the leading cause of death globally, accounting for approximately 17.9 million deaths annually [3, 4]. The persistent incidence and substantial clinical burden of MI continue to challenge healthcare systems internationally, particularly in aging populations and those with complex comorbidities [5, 6].

Accurate and timely diagnosis of MI is essential for optimizing patient outcomes. Among available biomarkers, cardiac troponins (cTn), particularly their cardiac-specific isoforms troponin I (cTnI) and troponin T (cTnT), have become the gold standard for detecting myocardial injury due to their high specificity and sensitivity [7, 8]. Upon cardiac injury, cTn is released into the bloodstream, and its measurement enables clinicians to detect and quantify myocardial damage [9]. The introduction of high-sensitivity assays has further revolutionized MI diagnosis, allowing for earlier detection, improved risk stratification, and more effective management strategies [8, 10, 11]. Beyond its role in acute diagnosis, cTn is increasingly studied for its utility in prognosis and guidance of treatment decisions in various clinical scenarios.

Despite the rapid progress in cTn research, the field’s development, research hotspots, and collaboration patterns have not been systematically mapped. Bibliometric analysis provides a quantitative approach to evaluate scientific output, visualize research trends, and identify emerging frontiers in a given area [12]. To our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive bibliometric study focusing on cTn and MI. By analyzing the literature landscape, this study aims to uncover key research trends, influential contributors, and evolving topics in the field, thereby offering valuable insights for researchers and clinicians and guiding future investigations to advance MI diagnosis and management.

Methods

Literature search

In this study, we identified relevant literature through the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database using a refined search strategy tailored to cTn and MI. The search was performed on January 12, 2026, and covered the period between January 1987 and December 2025. The following search strategy was utilized, targeting MI-specific terminology to ensure precision and minimize irrelevant retrievals [13, 14]: TS = ((“myocardial infarct*” OR “acute myocardial infarct*” OR “ST elevation myocardial infarction” OR “non-ST elevation myocardial infarction” OR “STEMI” OR “NSTEMI”) AND TS = (“cardiac troponin”). The initial inclusion criteria were defined as all relevant records retrieved via this pre-specified search string. Subsequently, a series of rigorous exclusion criteria were applied to ensure the quality and academic rigor of the dataset. First, to ensure reproducibility, the database subsets were restricted to the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-EXPANDED) and the Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI). Second, only articles published in English were included, with all non-English publications being excluded. Finally, non-original research document types, such as reviews, meeting abstracts, editorials, proceedings, early access, letters, book chapters, retractions, corrections, notes, and data papers, were removed. All retrieved records were independently screened by two reviewers based on titles and abstracts, with full texts reviewed when necessary to confirm eligibility and any discrepancies resolved through mutual consultation. Author name disambiguation and institutional normalization were subsequently performed using VOSviewer’s thesaurus files and manual verification.

Statistical analysis and visualization

Microsoft Excel was employed to organize and calculate key bibliometric indicators such as the annual number of publications, citation frequencies, average citation rates, journal names, impact factors, publication countries/regions, institutions, and authors. For visualizing and analyzing the data, three bibliometric tools were used: VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1), and the R package “bibliometrix” (version 4.3.3). VOSviewer was used to map institutional collaborations, author collaborations, co-authorship, citation, and co-citation networks, allowing for the exploration of relationships between authors, institutions, and publications [15]. It was also used for keyword co-occurrence analysis to identify emerging trends and research hotspots. CiteSpace was used for detecting keyword bursts, with parameters set for time slicing from January 1987 to December 2025, using 1-year intervals. The node type was set to keywords, with a threshold of N = 5 per slice. Pruning was applied using the pathfinder and merged network methods to generate a timeline graph of keywords related to cTn and MI. In the visualizations, node sizes represented the number of publications, line thickness indicated the strength of collaboration (defined as the total number of collaborative links of a given item), and node colors signified different clusters or time periods. To quantify academic impact, metrics such as the H-index, g-index, and m-index were employed, as referenced in existing literature [16, 17]. These metrics are critical for assessing the contributions of researchers and predicting future scientific output. Additionally, Journal Citation Reports (JCR) quartiles and Impact Factors (IF) were utilized to evaluate the significance and influence of journals publishing the relevant studies.

Results

Results of literature screening

The total number of publications related to cTnI and MI, published between January 1987 and December 2025, is 5,352. According to document types, most of the publications are research articles, with 7,222 initially identified. The excluded records consist of review articles (n = 761), meeting abstracts (n = 283), editorial materials (n = 280), proceeding papers (n = 172), early access papers (n = 41), letters (n = 165), book chapters (n = 16), retracted publications (n = 14), corrections (n = 13), notes (n = 4), data papers (n = 1), and non-English articles (n = 170) (Fig. 1, PRISMA flowchart).

Flowchart of the literature screening process

Overview of publications

This study examined 5,352 publications, all of which are research articles. Our investigation showed that 25,708 authors from 5,615 institutions across 93 countries/regions contributed to the production of these manuscripts. These works were published in 1,075 journals and cited a total of 94,603 references. On average, each document had 8.54 co-authors, and 23.75% of the articles involved international collaborations. The annual growth rate of publications in this field is 14%, and the average number of citations per document is 47.15 (Fig. 2A). The cumulative number of publications has steadily increased since 1987, with a notable surge in output after 2010. The number of annual publications peaked at 300 in 2019 and remained above 280 per year from 2018 onward, reaching 291 in 2025 (Fig. 2B).

Analysis of countries

The top contributing countries in this field are China (1,068 articles), the United States (1,013), and Germany (351) (Table 1 and Fig. 3A). Among the top 10 countries, Germany, Italy, and Switzerland have the highest average citations per article, with values of 61.7, 61.0, and 58.5, respectively. Most publications are single-country outputs, but international collaborations remain significant. The Multiple Country Publications (MCP) Ratio is highest for Switzerland (0.784), followed by the United Kingdom (0.364) and Germany (0.33). A total of 65 countries were involved in international collaborations with at least three articles. The USA leads with the highest total link strength (1,399), followed by Germany (1,028) and Switzerland (856) (Fig. 3B).

Analysis of journals

The 5,352 articles were published in 1,075 journals. The top 20 journals accounted for a significant proportion of total publications (Table 2). Among these, Clinical Chemistry published the most articles (TP = 221), followed by the International Journal of Cardiology (TP = 134) and Circulation (TP = 131). In terms of impact, Circulation had the highest h-index (84), followed by Clinical Chemistry (73) and the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (68). For total citations, Circulation led with 16,186, followed by the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (11,118), and Clinical Chemistry (10,732). The co-occurrence network analysis of journals included 102 journals with at least 10 occurrences. The three key journals with the highest total link strength in co-occurrence networks were Clinical Chemistry (4,744), Circulation (3,715), and the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (2,672) (Fig. 4A). In the coupling networks, which included 102 journals with at least 10 couples, the journals with the highest total link strength were Clinical Chemistry (236,388), Circulation (151,662), and the International Journal of Cardiology (135,823) (Fig. 4B).

Analysis of keywords

The co-occurrence network analysis of keywords (Fig. 7A) revealed four major thematic clusters in the literature on cTn and MI, each representing a distinct research focus: Cluster 1 (Red): Clinical Guidelines and Disease Management. This cluster centers on clinical and guideline-related aspects, with keywords such as “acute coronary syndromes,” “prognostic value,” “risk stratification,” “guidelines,” “prognosis,” “management,” “outcomes,” and “unstable angina.” It reflects a strong emphasis on the stratification, management, and prognostic evaluation of coronary syndromes in clinical practice. Cluster 2 (Green): Biomarker Assays and Laboratory Methods. This cluster is dominated by technical and methodological research, including keywords like “acute myocardial infarction,” “assay,” “biochemical markers,” “cardiac troponin-I,” “creatine kinase-MB,” “myoglobin,” “immunoassay,” “plasma,” and “serum.” It highlights the development, validation, and comparison of various cardiac biomarker assays and laboratory techniques. The high frequency of “cardiac troponin-I” indicates that a substantial volume of research within the broader cTn field has focused on this specific isoform. Cluster 3 (Blue): Diagnosis and Risk Assessment. This group focuses on diagnostic processes and risk assessment, featuring keywords such as “chest pain,” “early diagnosis,” “emergency department,” “validation,” “high-sensitivity troponin,” “performance,” “rule-out,” and “sensitivity cardiac troponin.” It illustrates the evolution of acute diagnostic protocols and the implementation of high-sensitivity assays for rapid rule-in/rule-out strategies. Cluster 4 (Yellow): Pathophysiology and Mechanisms. This cluster represents fundamental research into the pathophysiological mechanisms of MI, with keywords such as “apoptosis,” “inflammation,” “oxidative stress,” “ischemia-reperfusion,” “failure,” “heart,” “dysfunction,” and “survival.” It focuses on cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying myocardial injury and disease progression.

Visual analysis of keyword co-occurrence network analysis. A. keyword co-occurrence clusters. B. keyword co-occurrence timeline

The temporal visualization of keywords (Fig. 7B) demonstrates the evolution of research topics over time. Earlier studies (2008–2012, purple/blue) focused primarily on core concepts such as “acute myocardial infarction,” “diagnosis,” “ischemia,” and “injury.” From 2014 to 2016 (green), attention shifted toward clinical translation, including “risk,” “prognostic value,” “outcomes,” and “mortality.” In the most recent years (2018 onward, yellow), research has increasingly emphasized personalized medicine, advanced detection technologies, and outcome prediction, as reflected by keywords like “high-sensitivity troponin,” “prediction,” “validation,” and “emergency department.”

Analysis of burst keywords

Burst keyword analysis (Fig. 8) further delineates the evolution of research hotspots in this field. Early citation bursts were observed for terms such as “creatine kinase,” “serum,” “unstable angina,” “creatine kinase-MB,” and “immunoassay” (1991–2009), reflecting the initial focus on traditional cardiac markers and analytical methods. From the mid-1990s to early 2010s, keywords such as “prognostic value,” “artery disease,” “ischemia,” “diagnosis,” and “acute coronary syndrome” gained prominence, indicating a shift towards risk stratification and clinical application. Since 2018, there has been a notable burst in keywords such as “high-sensitivity cardiac troponin,” “emergency department,” “early-diagnosis,” and “algorithm,” highlighting recent advances in rapid diagnostic protocols, precision medicine, and the integration of high-sensitivity assays for improved patient management and outcome prediction.

Discussion

This bibliometric analysis comprehensively delineates the evolving landscape of research on cTn and MI from 1987 to 2025. Our findings reveal a clear research trajectory from foundational biomarker discovery to the current focus on high-sensitivity assays and algorithm-driven diagnostics. By quantitatively mapping this evolution, our study provides a crucial reference for researchers and clinicians, highlighting the key trends and influential work that have shaped modern MI diagnostics.

The steady increase in publication output, especially after 2010, reflects both technological advances in biomarker detection and the central role of cardiac troponins in contemporary cardiovascular medicine [8, 18–21]. Our analysis identified China as the most prolific country, which likely reflects a significant national investment in cardiovascular research and a large patient population. Notably, Germany emerges as a leading contributor, and recent clinical evidence from German centers has directly influenced the evolution of rapid rule-in/rule-out strategies and multi-marker diagnostic approaches [22–24]. Among top contributors, Mueller Christian stands out as the most influential author, with a high h-index and substantial publication and citation counts, especially in journals central to the field such as Clinical Chemistry [25], Circulation Research [26] and European Heart Journal [27]. He is dedicated to the role of biomarkers in the diagnosis and prognosis of acute myocardial infarction (AMI), particularly the utility of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) assays and the balance of angiogenic factors such as placental growth factor (PlGF) and soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1).

The following sections delve into the four major research clusters identified through keyword analysis, each representing a core pillar of cTn and MI research.

Cluster 1: clinical guidelines and disease management

The largest cluster identified reflects the centrality of clinical guideline development, risk stratification, and disease management in MI research. The prominence of keywords highlights the transition from biomarker discovery to the standardization of clinical pathways. The establishment and ongoing update of the Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (UDMI) [8, 10] have driven this trend, as demonstrated by frequent co-citation of guideline documents and consensus statements [28]. Recent German studies [22–24] have further shaped the field by rigorously evaluating novel biomarkers and dual-marker strategies versus established ESC 0/1 h and 0/3 h algorithms for rapid diagnosis in suspected MI, providing strong clinical evidence for evolving practice.

Cluster 2: biomarker assays and laboratory methods

The technical evolution from early markers to high-sensitivity cTn assays is well documented [19, 20]. This cluster’s focus on “assay,” “biochemical markers,” and “analytical performance” demonstrates persistent interest in assay sensitivity, specificity, and standardization. A key aspect within this theme is the challenge of standardization, which differs between cTnI and cTnT. While cTnT assays benefit from a single manufacturer, the market for cTnI assays is fragmented, leading to significant variability between methods and a long-standing effort towards harmonization [29]. Advances in high-sensitivity assays have enabled earlier and more accurate detection of myocardial injury, but also present interpretation challenges in certain populations, such as those with renal dysfunction [8, 11, 25]. Recent German clinical chemistry work [24] has compared single, dual, and panel biomarker approaches, adding critical evidence to this ongoing debate. Novel biosensing technologies, such as nanoparticle-based assays, are also reflected in emerging keywords, indicating the field’s openness to further innovation [21].

Cluster 3: diagnosis and risk assessment

This cluster centers on the acute diagnostic and risk assessment role of cTn, with keywords like “chest pain,” “emergency department,” “validation,” and “rule-out” underscoring the uptake of rapid diagnostic algorithms in clinical practice [27, 30]. Notably, high-sensitivity cTn has enabled the development of robust rule-in/rule-out strategies, validated by large-scale studies [25, 30]. Recent work, including the cited German studies [22, 23], has demonstrated the value of integrating multimarker panels and advanced algorithms for rapid MI diagnosis, aligning perfectly with the observed burst in “high-sensitivity troponin” and “algorithm” keywords. Challenges remain regarding specificity in non-AMI populations and the need for further refinement of thresholds and interpretation in diverse clinical contexts [8, 30, 31].

Cluster 4: pathophysiology and mechanisms

This cluster represents foundational research into myocardial injury mechanisms [9, 18]. Understanding the molecular pathways of cTn release has underpinned the biomarker’s clinical value and informed the search for adjunctive markers. The integration of terms like “failure” and “survival” indicates a growing interest in linking molecular insights to patient outcomes, consistent with recent trends in heart failure management [32–34].

Burst keywords, temporal trends, and future directions

Analysis of burst keywords and their temporal evolution reveals a distinct and meaningful progression in the field of cTn and MI research. Initial bursts centered on traditional cardiac markers [7], reflecting a foundational stage where the primary focus was on establishing reliable indicators of myocardial injury. As the field matured, there was a notable transition toward keywords like “prognostic value,” “risk stratification,” and “acute coronary syndrome,” which emerged concurrently with the integration of cTn into clinical guidelines and risk assessment models [8, 10]. This shift highlights how cTn’s clinical adoption transformed research priorities from mere detection to nuanced prognosis and management of MI. Since 2018, there has been a pronounced surge in keywords, mirroring the rapid dissemination of high-sensitivity cTn assays and the widespread implementation of algorithm-driven, point-of-care diagnostic strategies [32, 34, 35]. These trends are further validated by recent high-impact clinical trials and algorithm validation studies, particularly from German research groups [22–24], which have rigorously tested multi-marker and algorithmic approaches in real-world emergency settings. The convergence of burst keywords in the most recent period signals a field increasingly oriented toward integrated, data-driven, and personalized MI management. While bibliometric analysis cannot definitively predict future breakthroughs, the acceleration and clustering of these terms strongly suggest that upcoming research will focus on the integration of multi-marker panels, advanced analytics, and artificial intelligence for risk stratification and clinical decision support [36, 37]. Such a trajectory, though forward-looking and necessarily hypothetical, is firmly anchored in the observed temporal dynamics of keyword emergence and the current landscape of collaborative, translational research [38].

Strengths and limitations

This study provides the first comprehensive mapping of global research trends, key contributors, and thematic shifts in the MI/cTn literature. We excluded reviews, editorials, and letters to focus on original research and avoid citation bias from non-primary sources, a standard practice to ensure the analysis reflects original scientific output. We acknowledge that this may affect co-citation network structure and trend interpretation. Other limitations include reliance on citation metrics that may not capture clinical impact, exclusion of non-English literature, and potential misclassification of institutional or author names despite rigorous cleaning steps.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis maps the nearly four-decade evolution of cTn research in MI. The field has clearly progressed from foundational studies of pathophysiology and biomarker development to the widespread clinical adoption of high-sensitivity cTn assays and the current emphasis on precision diagnostics, rapid algorithms, and outcome prediction. The most recent trends highlight the integration of these advanced biomarkers with analytical methods like artificial intelligence to refine risk stratification. Looking ahead, future research is poised to build on this by enhancing assay technology, harmonizing diagnostic criteria, and leveraging big data to drive individualized care, ultimately improving outcomes for patients with or at risk of MI.

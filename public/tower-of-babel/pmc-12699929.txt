Epidemiological characteristics of Group A rotavirus diarrhea in children under 5 years old in China from 2009 to 2020
Authors: Xu Ding, Li Cai, Jiayi Zhang, Yang Song, Zhaorui Chang, Yaming Zheng, Liping Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGroup A rotavirus (RVA) is a leading cause of childhood diarrhea globally. Despite its status as a reportable infectious diarrhea in China, comprehensive and specific national RVA surveillance data remain scarce. This study analyzed the National Science and Technology Major Project (NSTMP) database (2009–2020) to characterize RVA epidemiology in Chinese children under 5 presenting with diarrhea.MethodsRVA detection data from children under 5 with diarrhea were analyzed from the NSTMP database. Positivity rates and genotype spatiotemporal distribution were assessed using descriptive epidemiology. Temporal trends were evaluated with Joinpoint regression; significance of differences was assessed using chi-square tests.ResultsThe overall positivity rate of RVA among children under 5 years old in China was 28.7% (15,945/55,637). The positivity rate in males (29.3%) was higher than that in females (27.6%). Positive cases were concentrated in children aged 6–23 months (70.4%, 11,231/15,945), with the highest positivity rate (35.5%, 6059/17,090) observed in the children aged 12–23 months. Joinpoint regression analysis revealed a sharp decline in the positivity rate of RVA from a baseline of 49.9% in 2009 to 23.1% in 2011, with an annual percent change (APC) of -31.9% (p = 0.04). Subsequently, between 2011 and 2020, the rate remained stable, showing no statistically significant change in trend (APC = -1.1%, p = 0.901). Additionally, the highest positivity rate of RVA was observed in winter (46.5%), while the lowest was recorded in summer (14.0%). Geographically, the highest positivity rate of RVA were observed in the central region (41.6%), while the eastern region exhibited the lowest positivity rate (20.0%) among the four regions in China. Furthermore, the distribution of genotypes exhibited geographical variation, with the proportions of the dominant G3, G9, and P[8] genotypes being lower in the eastern region than that in the central and western regions. Conversely, the P[4] genotype was more prevalent in the eastern region. As for the combination genotype, G9P[8] has increased markedly and become predominant circulating genotype.ConclusionRVA positivity rate in Chinese children under 5 declined significantly during 2009–2011 and subsequently stabilized, demonstrating distinct age, seasonal, and regional patterns with geographically variable dominant genotypes. Sustained surveillance is crucial to track evolving epidemiology and guide evidence-based vaccination strategies.Clinical trialNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12097-6.Keywords: Group A rotavirus, Diarrhea, Positivity rate

Introduction

Diarrheal diseases are a major contributor to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) [1], with the GBD 2017 study attributing over half a million preventable annual deaths to this condition among children under 5 years of age [2]. In 2021, rotavirus was responsible for an estimated 108,322 deaths from diarrhea among children under 5 in low-income and middle-income countries, constituting 24.4% (95% UI 21.6–29.5) of the 443,342 total diarrheal deaths in this age group [3]. In China, over 97.0% of RV-associated gastroenteritis cases are attributed to RVA infections, with children under 5 years old accounting for up to 84.0% of all cases [4]. RVA remains the predominant cause of hospitalization and severe acute gastroenteritis in young children [5]. What’s more, RVA-associated diarrhea is classified as a class C infectious disease in China, specifically within the category of infectious diarrhea (excluding cholera, bacillary and amebic dysentery, typhoid, and paratyphoid) for the purposes of reporting and management. However, a comprehensive, long-term, systematic, and nationwide surveillance dataset for RVA remains notably scarce.

Therefore, this study utilized the pathogen surveillance database of the diarrhea syndrome in the population, which was developed by the National Science and Technology Major Project (NSTMP) in sentinel hospitals across the country. The NSTMP established a multi-tiered, laboratory-integrated diarrheal syndrome surveillance network across China, adopting a symptom-based case ascertainment approach. This system conducts comprehensive laboratory testing for bacterial, viral, and parasitic enteric pathogens with the aim of determining regional diarrheal pathogen profiles, tracking temporal trends, and elucidating epidemiological dynamics of predominant pathogens. In 2020, the national surveillance framework encompasses 252 sentinel hospitals and 93 collaborating laboratories, operating across mainland China [6]. Within this structure, sentinel hospitals are tasked with case identification, specimen collection, and clinical data acquisition. Concurrently, the laboratories are responsible for pathogen detection, data consolidation, technical training provision, and quality oversight. The objective of the present study is to analyze the epidemiological characteristics of RVA diarrhea in children under 5 years old in China, which can provide a scientific foundation for the enhancement of RVA diarrhea surveillance and prevention strategies.

Methods

Data source

From 2009 to 2020, a program exclusively targeting RV-associated diarrhea under China’s NSTMP was implemented across 28 provinces, involving 252 sentinel hospitals and 93 laboratories. Coordinated by the China CDC, this initiative leverages multidisciplinary sentinel hospitals and laboratories to systematically enroll patients under 5 years old meeting standardized case criteria (≥ 3 loose/liquid stools daily with altered consistency — watery, mucoid, or bloody) [7] from gastroenterology clinics, emergency departments, and pediatric units. This broad case definition, which includes mucoid or bloody stools, was chosen to minimize selection bias and capture all acute diarrhea cases regardless of etiology, consistent with standard surveillance protocols that acknowledge other pathogens or co-infections can present atypically [7, 8]. A structured surveillance protocol was developed for this study [8], and we aimed to capture all acute diarrhea cases regardless of etiology to minimize selection bias. Prior to the initiation of surveillance activities, the China CDC organized a comprehensive training and evaluation session for all participating field epidemiologists, clinicians, and laboratory personnel. The session was conducted by a panel of experts to ensure standardized procedures for sample collection, preservation, and transportation.

Sample processing and diagnostic methods

A 10% stool suspension was prepared for each sample. In summary, approximately 900 µL of rotavirus specimen diluent (Sample Diluent from the RVA ELISA kit) was aliquoted into pre-labelled 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes. Subsequently, 100 µL of each stool specimen (or a pea-sized amount for solid samples) was added to the corresponding tube within a biological safety cabinet. The remaining original specimen was stored at -20℃. The mixture was then subjected to three cycles of vigorous vortexing, each lasting 10 s, followed by a 10-minute standing period. Subsequently, it was subjected to a centrifugation process at 8000 rpm for a duration of 5 min. The resulting supernatant was collected under a biological safety cabinet for immediate testing or stored at -20℃. The supernatant was collected and tested for rotavirus antigen using a double-antibody sandwich ELISA kit (ProSpect™ Rotavirus Kit, OXOID). All RVA-positive samples underwent nucleic acid extraction using commercially available kits from Geneaid or QIAGEN, in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Viral RNA was extracted and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using commercial reverse transcription or one-step RT-PCR kits, according to the manufacturer’s instructions [9, 10]. The primer sequences used are listed in Supplementary Table 1. Quality control was implemented throughout all stages of the project, including patient registration, specimen collection, storage, transportation, testing, data collection, organization, entry, and statistical analysis. Each experimental run included negative and positive controls for antigen detection, nucleic acid extraction, and amplification to ensure assay reliability and reproducibility. This project and the above procedure for obtaining consent were approved by the ethical review committee of China CDC (2015-025).

Data classification

According to the growth, development, and immune characteristics of children under 5 years old, the children were divided into five age groups: 0–5, 6–11, 12–23, 24–35, and 36–59 months. Based on the seasons defined by the Chinese Meteorological Network, the year was divided into four seasons: spring (March–May), summer (June–August), autumn (September–November), and winter (December–February of the following year). The regions were classified as follows: northeast China (I), which includes Liaoning, Jilin, and Heilongjiang; east China (II, excluding Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan), comprising Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, Shandong, Guangdong, and Hainan; central China (III), which includes Shanxi, Anhui, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, and Hunan; west China (IV), which consists of Inner Mongolia, Guangxi, Chongqing, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Tibet, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia, and Xinjiang. The national diarrheal disease multipathogen surveillance system of China was initiated in 2009 and has undergone a series of developments over the course of three Five-Year Plan periods (2009–2020). A key methodological limitation in the initial phase (2009–2010) was the inability to distinguish between community-acquired and hospital-acquired infections in inpatients. In order to address this issue, the protocol was revised in 2011 with the objective of excluding hospitalized patients. Consequently, and in order to ensure consistency across phases, this study omits all comparisons and stratified analyses by inpatient or outpatient to ensure the validity of the findings.

Statistical analysis

The Excel software (version 2016, Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, Washington, USA) was used to record and screen data of the RVA detection cases of diarrhea syndrome in children under 5 years old. Variations in the positivity rates of RVA and predominant genotype distribution across age groups, time periods and geographic regions in children. The 95% confidence interval (95% CI) of the positivity rates was calculated using the binomial distribution method. The Joinpoint Regression Program (version 5.3.0, Statistical Research and Applications Branch, National Cancer Institute, USA) was used in analyzing the annual trend of the RVA positivity rates. Differences in positivity rates among groups were compared using the Pearson chi-square test, with p < 0.05 as the threshold, which was implemented using SPSS software (version 22.0, IBM Corp., New York, USA). ArcGIS software (version 10.8, Environmental Systems Research Institute, Inc., Redlands, California, USA) was used to create distribution maps based on the Standard Map of China (Map Review No.: GS (2024) 0650).

Results

During the 2009–2020 surveillance period, 55,637 children under five years of age presenting with diarrhea underwent RV testing. The cohort comprised 35,126 males (63.1%) and 20,511 females (36.9%), with a median infection age of 12 months. RVA was detected in 15,945 cases, yielding an overall positivity rate of 28.7%. Among RVA-positive children, males predominated (10,280 cases, 64.5%; sex-specific positivity rates: 29.3%) compared to females (5,665 cases, 35.5%; positivity rates: 27.6%), demonstrating significantly higher infection rates in males (χ2 = 17.2, p < 0.001). In addition, 70.4% of positive cases (11,231/15,945) clustered within the 6–23 month age group. Age-stratified analysis revealed an inverted U-shaped positivity curve: rates peaked at 35.5% in 12–23-month-olds, preceded by 31.0% (6–11 months) and 21.2% (0–5 months), then declined to 26.6% (24–35 months) and 17.0% (36–59 months) (age trend: χ2 = 1,077.9, p < 0.001). Notably, while the 0–5-month cohort exhibited lower positivity than the 24–35-month group (21.2% vs. 26.6%), it contributed a significantly greater proportion to total RVA cases (16.2% vs. 8.5%) (Table 1).

The positivity rate of RVA in Chinese children under 5 years with diarrhea from 2009 to 2020

The RVA positivity rate in children under 5 years old in China decreased from 49.9% in 2009 to 17.4% in 2020, with an average annual percent change in the positivity rate of − 5.0% (p = 0.04). It dropped sharply from 2009 to 2011, with an APC in the positivity rate of − 31.9% (p = 0.04). Meanwhile, the trend from 2011 to 2020 showed no statistical significance (APC = − 1.1%, p = 0.9) (Fig. 1A). In all regions, the peak of positive cases was observed in winter. The positivity rates in autumn (31.8%) and winter (46.5%) were significantly higher than those in spring (22.6%) and summer (14.0%; χ2 = 3742.2, p < 0.001), and the number of positive cases in autumn and winter accounted for 72.1% of the total positive cases (Table 1; Fig. 2). Between 2009 and 2020, RVA positivity rates varied seasonally, with the highest rate recorded in January (52.6%) and the lowest in June (12.4%) (Fig. 2). Central China recorded the highest positivity rate (41.6%), accounting for 42.3% of the total positive cases, followed by west China (28.1%). The positivity rates in east (20.0%) and northeast China (17.1%) were relatively low (χ2 = 2201.5, p < 0.001) (Table 1; Fig. 1B).

In this study, we performed G and P genotyping on all RVA-positive specimens. The genotyping success rate was 24.1% (3,840/15,945) for the G genotype and 20.2% (3,215/15,945) for the P genotype, and the geographic and temporal distribution of successfully genotyped samples are shown in Supplementary Tables 2 and 3. Regarding regional distribution, the G9 and P[8] genotypes were predominant across all regions, though the detection ratios of G/P types varied by area. In the distribution of G genotypes, the proportion of G9 was highest in Northeast China (58.6%) (χ2 = 166.9, p < 0.001), followed by G3 (22.5%) and G1 (14.7%). Eastern China exhibited a more diverse genotypic composition compared to other regions, with G9, G3, and G1 accounting for 27.4%, 24.3%, and 20.5%, respectively. Moreover, the proportion of mixed genotypes (Gm) in this region (15.0%) was significantly higher than in other areas (χ2 = 140.1, p < 0.001). In Central China, G3, G9, and G1 were predominant, comprising 31.6%, 30.3%, and 21.2%, respectively. In Western China, the proportions of G9, G3, and G1 were 47.8%, 24.7%, and 14.1%, respectively. For P genotypes, P[8] was absolutely dominant in Northeast China (91.3%) compared to other regions (χ2 = 169.9, p < 0.001). In Eastern China, P[8], P[4], and P[m] were the most prevalent, accounting for 62.0%, 22.3%, and 8.5%, respectively. In Central China, P[8], P[4], and P[11] accounted for 64.2%, 13.0%, and 10.3%, respectively. Western China was characterized by a high prevalence of P[8] and P[4], comprising 82.7% and 9.7%, respectively. Among the G/P combinations, G9P[8] was the predominant strain across all regions. Regarding the common major genotypes—G1P[8], G2P[4], G3P[8], and G9P[8]—their respective proportions in Northeast China were 14.9%, 0.7%, 24.1%, and 60.3%; in Eastern China, they were 20.9%, 13.9%, 13.2%, and 51.9%; in Central China, 25.3%, 8.5%, 22.5%, and 43.8%; and in Western China, 12.6%, 4.6%, 18.3%, and 64.5%. The differences in the composition of these combination genotypes across regions were all statistically significant (χ2G1P[8] = 47.7, p < 0.001; χ2G2P[4] = 41.1, p < 0.001; χ2G3P[8] = 14.4, p = 0.002; χ2G9P[8] = 77.4, p < 0.001). Among the G genotypes, G9 (38.1%), G3 (27.1%), and G1 (17.9%) were predominant overall. In 2009 and 2010, G3 was the most prevalent, accounting for 52.1% and 49.0% respectively, followed by G1 at 20.8% and 25.9%. During 2011–2012, the proportion of G9 increased (43.0% and 17.8%, respectively), while G3 decreased (23.5% and 18.3%). G1 remained relatively high at 21.3% and 35.0%, respectively. From 2013 to 2020, G9 was the dominant genotype each year, while G1 and G3, though still detected annually, accounted for relatively lower proportions. For P genotypes, P[8] (72.8%) and P[4] (12.7%) were the most common. From 2009 to 2020, P[8] was the predominant type each year, peaking in 2018 at 93.3%. P[4] was detected annually during this period, showing fluctuating but generally low proportions. P[6] began to be detected in 2012, while P[9], P[10], and P[11] were sporadically identified after 2016. Among the common genotype combinations included in the analysis, G3P[8] was the dominant combination in 2009 and 2010, accounting for 49.1% and 52.2%, respectively, followed by G1P[8] at 43.0% and 32.0%. During 2011–2012, G9P[8] and G1P[8] alternated in prevalence, while the proportion of G3P[8] decreased significantly. Since 2013, the proportion of G9P[8] has increased markedly, exceeding 50% each year, and has become the predominant epidemic strain in China. Discussion

This study revealed that the overall positivity rate of RVA was 28.7% among children under 5 years old, and the positivity rate in males (29.3%) was higher than that in females (27.6%). The highest positivity rate (35.5%) was observed among children aged 12 − 23 months. In addition, there was a sharp decline in the positivity rate of RVA from 2009 to 2011, after which it stabilised from 2011 to 2020. While the G3, G9 and P[8] types were less common in east China, P[4] type was more prevalent in this region than in central and west China. As for the combination genotype, G9P[8] has increased markedly and become predominant circulating genotype.

In China, the RVA positivity rate among children under 5 years old presenting with diarrhea showed a significant decline from 49.9% in 2009 to 17.4% in 2020, which was consistent with the global trend. The median positivity rate of RV in children under 5 years old was 36.0% worldwide in 2009 [11]. With the widespread application of RV vaccines, global positivity rates of RV demonstrated a 43.8% reduction following the implementation of RV vaccines, declining from 38.0% (n = 70,772/186,620) in the pre-vaccine baseline year (2008) to 23.0% (n = 25,426/119,169) in the post-vaccine surveillance year (2016) [12]. From 2009 to 2011, the positivity rate of RVA demonstrated a consistent annual decline, averaging 31.86% per year.

The highest positivity rate (35.5%) occurred in children aged 12 − 23 months, which was consistent with a 2013 − 2023 meta-analysis [13]. Hygiene levels, maternal immunity, and increased environmental exposure represent the primary age-dependent factors explaining observed variations in RVA positivity rates among young children [14–16]. Given the high risk of RVA infection among children aged 0 − 3 years, our findings support World Health Organization’s recommendations to prioritize vaccination within the first 24 months [17]. The median age of RV infection in low-income countries was 38 weeks (approximately 9.5 months), with 69.0% of cases occurring within the first year [5, 18]. In contrast, developed nations exhibited a later median age (65 weeks), and a reduced infection risk after 2 years old [5, 19]. The epidemic peak was delayed by 4–7 weeks [20]. A multi-center study in China in 2019 found that the positivity rate peaked among children aged 12 − 17 months (33.1%) [21]. From 2009 to 2020, the median age of RVA infection in China (12 months) was relatively later than that in low-income countries but earlier than developed countries.

Since 2000, the dominant RVA genotypes in China have shifted from G1 and G3 to G9, while P[8] remained predominant [4, 5]. Notably, regional disparities still exist. The eastern region exhibits lower prevalence of G3/G9/P[8] types but higher P[4] rates compared to central and western regions, a pattern corroborated by independent research [6]. These regional differences in genotype distribution may be associated with factors such as temperature, economic status, and coverage rates of rotavirus vaccines (the Lanzhou Lamb Rotavirus Vaccine (2001), RotaTeq®(2018) and trivalent reassortant vaccine (2023) [22, 23]. Since 2021, the prevalence of the G8P[8] genotype has increased in eastern China [24, 25], potentially due to vaccine-induced selective pressure [5, 26]. Current vaccines target G1 − G4 and P[8] types, which may reduce the prevalence of target genotypes, but lead to regional imbalances [22]. In high-coverage areas, non-target genotypes emerge as “alternative dominant strains” [5]. This is evidenced by significantly higher RVA positivity rates in central (41.6%) versus eastern regions, which is consistent with a previous study [13, 27], highlighting genotype-specific epidemiology that informs targeted interventions. Notably, our result about the prevalence of the G9P[8] genotype is consistent with a meta-analysis [13], which reveals that the prevalence of G9P[8] rose from 50.3%(95% CI: 26.8–73.8) in 2013 to 93.6% (95% CI: 87.4–99.9) in 2020, emerging as the dominant circulating genotype in China. The context of rotavirus vaccine utilization is pivotal for the interpretation of genotype distribution. In China, rotavirus vaccines, including the Lanzhou Lamb Rotavirus (LLR, 2001) [28] and RotaTeq® (2018) [29], were available during the study period (2009–2020). However, these vaccines were not part of the National Immunization Program, resulting in significant regional disparities in coverage. This variable coverage may exert selective pressure on circulating strains. Indeed, our findings demonstrate a gradual decline in the historically dominant G1P[8] genotype from 2013 to 2020. Concurrently, a significant rise in G9P[8] was observed, ultimately becoming the predominant strain, while the prevalence of G2P[4] demonstrated fluctuating trends. This dynamic is consistent with the phenomenon of serotype replacement, whereby vaccination suppresses dominant strains, thereby creating an ecological niche that allows other co-circulating genotypes to increase in relative prevalence [30]. The rise of G9P[8] in our study mirrors trends observed in international studies [31], and the fluctuating trend of G2P[4] aligns with international reports of its re-emergence in the vaccine era [32]. This transition is particularly evident in Chinese regions with relatively high vaccine coverage, such as Zhejiang and Shanghai, where the decline of G1P[8] since 2017 was accompanied by the rise of G9P[8] as the new predominant strain [5, 33]. Taken together, these findings suggest that China’s vaccination patterns, despite moderate overall coverage, are already influencing the national rotavirus genotype landscape.

This study has limitations. Firstly, the surveillance design may be susceptible to selection bias due to its reliance on sentinel hospitals and uneven geographic coverage. For instance, the broad classification of regions such as “Northeast China”, which encompasses vast and epidemiologically diverse areas, reduced the granularity of epidemiological data and the statistical power of sub-regional analyses. Secondly, the utilization of a singular ELISA kit for the preliminary diagnostic methods of rotavirus, as opposed to more sophisticated molecular methodologies, could compromise diagnostic precision, potentially leading to case misclassification. Thirdly, the absence of systematic rotavirus vaccination records impeded a comprehensive evaluation of the vaccine’s impact. Consequently, it was not possible to assess potential correlations between shifts in genotype distribution and vaccine coverage, nor to monitor the potential emergence of vaccine-derived strains. Finally, the characterization of the RVA was incomplete. Genotyping was conducted across a number of different laboratories, with varying success rates. These rates were influenced by technical inconsistencies and differences in sample viral load. Furthermore, the absence of genetic characterization to differentiate between wild-type and vaccine-derived strains represents a significant gap, particularly in the context of expanding vaccine adoption.

Conclusion

In summary, this study presents a comprehensive epidemiological profile of rotavirus in China, providing critical evidence to inform the integration of rotavirus vaccines into the National Immunization Program. The findings of this study emphasize the efficacy of an age-targeted vaccination strategy for children aged 6–23 months and advocate for regionally tailored approaches to address genotype heterogeneity. To advance this goal, it is imperative to strengthen the surveillance system. This can be achieved by expanding sentinel site coverage in order to improve representativeness, and by integrating Whole Genome Sequencing to monitor post-vaccination strain dynamics with high resolution. A data-driven strategy is pivotal for optimizing vaccine impact and guiding sustained, evidence-based control of rotavirus in China.

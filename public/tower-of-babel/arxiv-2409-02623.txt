Abstract
. We investigate Chebyshev polynomials corresponding to Jacobi weights and determine monotonicity properties of their related Widom factors. This complements work by Bernstein from 1930-31 where the asymptotical behavior of the related Chebyshev norms was established. As a part of the proof, we analyze a Bernstein-type inequality for Jacobi polynomials due to Chow et al. Our findings shed new light on the asymptotical uniform bounds of Jacobi polynomials. We also show a relation between weighted Chebyshev polynomials on the unit circle and Jacobi weighted Chebyshev polynomials on [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] . This generalizes work by Lachance et al. In order to complete the picture we provide numerical experiments on the remaining cases that our proof does not cover.



Keywords
Chebyshev polynomial, Widom factor, Jacobi weight, Jacobi polynomial, Bernstein-type inequality

41A50. 30C10. 33C45.

1.
Introduction

In an extensive two part analysis of extremal polynomials on the unit interval, found in
[
8
,
9
]
, S. N. Bernstein investigates the asymptotic behavior of the quantity

(1.1)

\inf_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}w(x)\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\right|

as
n\rightarrow\infty
for a variety of different conditions on the weight function
w:[-1,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
. If
w
is bounded and strictly positive on a set consisting of at least
n
points on
[-1,1]
, there exists a unique set of nodes
\{a_{1}^{\ast},\dotsc,a_{n}^{\ast}\}
, all situated in
[-1,1]
, such that

(1.2)

\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}w(x)\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k}^{\ast})\right|=\inf_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}w(x)\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\right|,

see for instance
[
1
,
33
,
40
]
. In other words, the infimum of (
1.1
) can be replaced by a minimum.
The polynomial

(1.3)

T_{n}^{w}(x):=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k}^{\ast})

is the weighted Chebyshev polynomial of degree
n
corresponding to the weight function
w
and it is the unique monic polynomial of degree
n
minimizing (
1.1
). Under the assumption that the weight function is continuous, the minimizer of (
1.1
) can be characterized by the so-called alternation property. That is to say, a monic polynomial
P
of degree
n
is equal to
T_{n}^{w}
(and thus minimizes (
1.1
)) if and only if there exists
n+1
points
-1\leq x_{0}<\dotsc<x_{n}\leq 1
such that

(1.4)

P_{n}(x_{j})w(x_{j})=(-1)^{n-j}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}w(x)|P(x)|.

This result – which is central to the study of best approximations on the real line – can be found in many works on approximation theory, see for instance
[
33
,
1
,
14
,
37
]
.

The study of (
1.1
) dates back to the work of P. L. Chebyshev
[
10
,
11
]
who considered weight functions of the form
w(x)=1/P(x)
where
P
is a polynomial which is strictly positive on the interval
[-1,1]
. Later A. A. Markov
[
35
]
extended these results to allow for weight functions of the form
w(x)=1/\sqrt{P(x)}
, where again
P
is a polynomial which is assumed to be strictly positive on
[-1,1]
. It should be stressed that both Chebyshev and Markov provided explicit formulas for the minimizer
T_{n}^{w}
in these cases in terms of the polynomial
P
. These formulas can be conveniently found in Achiezer’s monograph
[
1
, Appendix A]
.

The considerations in
[
8
,
9
]
were different from those of Chebyshev and Markov as Bernstein considered the asymptotic behavior of (
1.1
) as
n\rightarrow\infty
for a broad family of weights, not restricting himself to continuous ones. In particular, writing
f(n)\sim g(n)
as
n\to\infty
if
f(n)/g(n)\to 1
as
n\to\infty
, he obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Bernstein
[
9
]
)
.

Let
b_{k}\in[-1,1]
and
s_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
for
k=1,\dotsc,m
, and let
w_{0}:[-1,1]\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Riemann integrable function such that there exists a value
M>1
for which
1/M<w_{0}(x)<M
holds for every
x\in[-1,1]
. Consider the weight function given by

w(x)=w_{0}(x)\prod_{k=1}^{m}|x-b_{k}|^{s_{k}}.

Under the above conditions, we have that

(1.5)

\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}w(x)\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\right|\sim 2^{1-n}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\log w(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx\right\}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

This result is shown in two steps. Bernstein initially considers weight functions
w_{0}
as in Theorem
1.1
and finds that if
c_{1}^{\ast},\dotsc,c_{n}^{\ast}
are the unique nodes satisfying

(1.6)

\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-c_{k}^{\ast})\right|^{2}\frac{w_{0}(x)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx=\min_{c_{1},\dotsc,c_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-c_{k})\right|^{2}\frac{w_{0}(x)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx,

then the weighted expression

w_{0}(x)\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-c_{k}^{\ast})

is asymptotically alternating on
[-1,1]
, see
[
8
]
. As it turns out, this information suffices to apply a result due to de la Vallée-Poussin found in
[
18
]
and ensure that

(1.7)

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|w_{0}(x)\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-c_{k}^{\ast})\right|\sim\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}w_{0}(x)\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\right|

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Note that

\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-c_{k}^{\ast})

is nothing but the monic orthogonal polynomial of degree
n
corresponding to the weight function
w_{0}(x)^{2}/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
on
[-1,1]
. By showing that

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|w_{0}(x)\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-c_{k}^{\ast})\right|\sim 2^{1-n}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\log w_{0}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx\right\}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, Bernstein obtains (
1.5
) from (
1.7
). Consequently, Theorem
1.1
is verified in this case. Proceeding from this, Bernstein extends the analysis by allowing for vanishing factors of the form
|x-b_{k}|^{s_{k}}
to be introduced to the weight where
b_{k}\in[-1,1]
and
s_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, see
[
9
]
. To show this, he uses a clever technique of bounding the factors
|x-b_{k}|^{s_{k}}
from above and below. However, the connection to the maximal deviation of the corresponding orthogonal polynomials as in (
1.7
) gets lost.

To illustrate why this connection becomes more delicate when zeros are added to the weight function, he provides the explicit example of the monic Jacobi polynomials. Following
[
41
, Chapter IV]
, we let
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
denote the classical Jacobi polynomials with parameters
\alpha,\beta>-1.
These are uniquely defined by the property that
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is a polynomial of exact degree
n
and

(1.8)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}P_{m}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)dx=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}}{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)n!}\delta_{nm},

where
\delta_{nm}
is the Kronecker delta.
Since all zeros of the Jacobi polynomials reside in
[-1,1]
and since

P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=2^{-n}{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}}x^{n}+\mbox{lower order terms},

we find that

(1.9)

2^{n}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\Big/{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})

for some values
\psi_{k}^{\ast}\in[0,\pi]
. Bernstein’s analysis provides the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Bernstein
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\rho_{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4}
and
\rho_{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}
, and let
\psi_{k}^{\ast}
be associated with
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
as in (
1.9
).

(1)

If

(1.10)

0\leq\rho_{\alpha}\leq 1/2\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq\rho_{\beta}\leq 1/2

both hold, then

(1.11)

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\sim 2^{1-n-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(2)

If one of the conditions in (
1.10
) fails to hold, then so does (
1.11
).

Note that by replacing
w_{0}
with
(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}
in (
1.6
), the corresponding weight function, to which the orthogonal polynomials are associated, is nothing but

(1.12)

\frac{[(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}]^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}.

It is a simple matter to verify that

(1.13)

2^{1-n}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\log[(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}]}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx\right\}=2^{1-n-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}},

see for instance
[
13
]
where also a translation of the proof of Theorem
1.1
appears. As such, we see that the monic Jacobi polynomials defined in (
1.9
) are close to minimal with respect to

(1.14)

\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\right|

precisely when (
1.10
) holds.

As a partial result, we wish to carry out a detailed analysis of the quantity

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|

for the parameters satisfying (
1.10
). By carefully manipulating a Bernstein-type inequality due to Chow et al.
[
12
]
, we will establish the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\rho_{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4}
and
\rho_{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}
, and let
\psi_{k}^{\ast}
be as in (
1.9
). If (
1.10
) holds, then

(1.15)

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\leq 2^{1-n-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

for every
n
with equality if and only if
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
.

By combining Theorem
1.3
with Theorem
1.2
, we see that

(1.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}2^{n}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\\
=\sup_{n}2^{n}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}2(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}\end{split}

which shows that the convergence is from below.
The question of obtaining upper bounds for the quantity

(1.17)

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{a}(1+x)^{b}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\right|

for different values of
a,b,\alpha,\beta
is well studied, see for instance
[
19
,
12
,
27
]
and has applications to e.g. representation theory and the study of Schrödinger operators. Our main interest is the applications that such a result has to the corresponding Chebyshev problem (
1.14
). To state our main result, we introduce the Widom factor
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
defined by

(1.18)

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}):=2^{n}\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\right|.

The choice of naming in honour of H. Widom – who in
[
44
]
considered Chebyshev polynomials corresponding to a plethora of compact sets in the complex plane – stems from
[
24
]
. The considerations of Widom factors in the literature are plentiful, see for instance
[
14
,

,

,

,

37
,

. In this article we wish to carry out a detailed study of the behavior of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
for different parameter values.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any value of the parameters
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 0
, we have that

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\sim 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore:

(1)

If
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
, then the quantity
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
is constant.

(2)

If
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in[0,1/2]
, then

(1.19)

\sup_{n}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}.

(3)

If
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0\}\cup[1/2,\infty)
, then

(1.20)

\inf_{n}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}},

(1.21)

\sup_{n}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq\left(\frac{2\rho_{\alpha}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\rho_{\beta}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\beta}},

and
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
decreases monotonically as
n
increases.

The case of
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
is classical and if
\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
and
\rho_{\alpha}\geq 1/2

\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 1/2
. This method originates with

2.
The case of
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}

We proceed by delving into the proof of Theorem
1.4
, split into three cases. The first case is very simple. When
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
, the minimizers of (
1.18
) are simply the Chebyshev polynomials of the 1
{}^{\text{st}}
to 4
{}^{\text{th}}
kind, see e.g.
[
36
]
. Using basic trigonometry, it follows that if
\theta\in[0,\pi]
then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\cos\frac{\theta}{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sin\frac{\theta}{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}}.

Through the change of variables
x=\cos\theta
, the trigonometric functions

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)=2^{1-n}\cos n\theta,

\displaystyle\quad U_{n}(x)=2^{-n}\frac{\sin(n+1)\theta}{\sin\theta},

\displaystyle V_{n}(x)=2^{-n}\frac{\cos\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\theta}{\cos\frac{\theta}{2}},

\displaystyle\quad W_{n}(x)=2^{-n}\frac{\sin\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\theta}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}},

all define monic polynomials in
x
of degree
n
. These are precisely, in order, the monic Chebyshev polynomials of the 1
{}^{\text{st}}
, 2
{}^{\text{nd}}
, 3
{}^{\text{rd}}
and 4
{}^{\text{th}}
kind. It is a straightforward matter to verify that they all satisfy the alternating property from (
1.4
) upon multiplication with the weight functions

\displaystyle 1,\quad

\displaystyle\sin\theta=\sqrt{1-x^{2}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}=\sqrt{1+x},\quad

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}=\sqrt{1-x},

respectively. As a result,
T_{n},U_{n},V_{n}
, and
W_{n}

are the minimal configurations sought after in (
1.18
) for the weight parameters
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
. It is evident that
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
in all these cases and this shows the first part of Theorem
1.4
.

3.
The case of
0\leq\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\leq 1/2

Having settled the first case of Theorem
1.4
, we turn toward the verification of the second case. For parameter values
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in[0,1/2]
, we want to show that

(3.1)

\sup_{n}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}.

These parameter values correspond to the lower left square in Figure
1
. Observe that if
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
then this follows from the first case of Theorem
1.4
since then the sequence
\{{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\}
is constant. Our approach will consist of first verifying Theorem
1.3
. For this reason we remind the reader of our definition of
\psi_{k}^{\ast}\in[0,\pi]
through the formula

(3.2)

\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})=2^{n}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\Big/{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}},

where
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
denotes the Jacobi polynomial with parameters
\alpha,\beta
and the angles
\psi_{k}^{\ast}
are those for which
\cos\psi_{1}^{\ast},\dotsc,\cos\psi_{n}^{\ast}
enumerate its zeros. We also recall the relation between the
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
parameters

(3.3)

\begin{cases}\rho_{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4},\\
\rho_{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}.\end{cases}

If we can show the validity of (
1.15
), then we trivially obtain

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq 2^{n}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\leq 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

from which (
3.1
), the second case of Theorem
1.4
follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\sim 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The key to verifying (
1.15
) is the following result.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

If
-1/2\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 1/2
and
\theta\in[0,\pi]
, then

(3.4)

\left(\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\beta+1/2}\left|P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\cos\theta)\right|\leq\frac{\Gamma(q+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{{n+q}\choose{n}}\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)^{-q-\frac{1}{2}}

where
q=\max(\alpha,\beta)
.

This result provides a Bernstein-type inequality for Jacobi polynomials and sharpens a previous result of Baratella
[
6
]
who had shown (
3.4
) with the larger factor
2.821
in place of
\Gamma(q+1)/\Gamma(1/2)
. In the case where
|\alpha|=|\beta|=1/2
, the inequality in (
3.4
) is saturated. For arbitrary
-1/2\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 1/2
the constant
\Gamma(q+1)/\Gamma(1/2)
is asymptotically sharp as can be seen from an argument using Stirling’s formula which we will provide in the proof of Lemma
3.2
. The type of inequality exemplified in (
3.4
) originate with Bernstein who showed in
[
9
]
that the Legendre polynomials
P_{n}:=P_{n}^{(0,0)}
satisfy

(3.5)

\left(\sin\theta\right)^{1/2}|P_{n}(\cos\theta)|\leq\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}n^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\theta\in[0,\pi].

Antonov and Holšhevnikov
[
5
]
sharpened (
3.5
) and later Lorch
[
32
]
extended such an inequality to Gegenbauer polynomials. In terms of Jacobi polynomials, this provides a Bernstein-type inequality for
P_{n}^{(\lambda,\lambda)}
with
-1/2\leq\lambda\leq 1/2
. Theorem
3.1
contains all these inequalities as special cases. In a different direction, the Erdélyi–Magnus–Nevai Conjecture
[
19
]
proposes that if
\hat{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
denotes the orthonormal Jacobi polynomial then for any parameter values
\alpha,\beta\geq-1/2
,

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\alpha+1/2}(1+x)^{\beta+1/2}(\hat{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x))^{2}=O(\max[1,(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})^{1/4}]).

Theorem
3.1
can be used to verify this conjecture in the case where
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 1/2
. This is shown in
[
23
]
where an extensive consideration concerning the sharpness of Theorem
3.1
is studied numerically for different parameter values.

We consider transferring the trigonometric weights to algebraic weights as in the statement of Theorem
1.3
. Letting
x=\cos\theta
, we find that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left(\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}=2^{-\rho_{\alpha}}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^{\beta+1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+x}{2}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}=2^{-\rho_{\beta}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}},

with the parameter relation stated in (
3.3
).
Together with (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.2
), the change of variables transforms (
3.4
) into the statement that for
x\in[-1,1]
,

(3.8)

(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\leq\frac{\Gamma(q+1)2^{n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}}},

where
q=\max(\alpha,\beta)
. From (
3.3
) we see that this implies that

(3.9)

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\left(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\right)\leq 2^{n}\frac{\Gamma(q+1)2^{n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}}}

for
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in[0,1/2]
. In order to determine the upper bound of (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we are ultimately led to consider the quantity

(3.10)

M_{n}(\alpha,\beta):=\frac{\Gamma(q+1)2^{2n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{\sqrt{\pi}\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in[-1/2,1/2]
. Then
M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
, defined in (
3.10
), increases monotonically to
2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The proof we provide constitutes a lengthy computation. We have therefore chosen to illustrate the simpler case where
\alpha=\beta=q
in the proof below while postponing most of the remaining computations to the appendix. The asymptotical behavior
M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\sim 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
merits attention since this shows that the constant
\Gamma(q+1)/\Gamma(1/2)
is sharp in (
3.4
). To see this, note that

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq 2^{n}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\leq M_{n}(\alpha,\beta).

The left-hand side converges to
2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and consequently

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\geq 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}.

On the other hand, as Lemma
3.2
shows,
M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\sim 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and therefore the constant factor in (
3.4
) cannot be improved.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

We rewrite the binomial coefficients using Gamma functions so that

(3.11)

{{n+q}\choose{n}}=\frac{\Gamma(n+q+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(q+1)},

(3.12)

{{2n+\alpha+\beta}\choose{n}}=\frac{\Gamma(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}.

The Legendre duplication formula implies that

(3.13)

\Gamma(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)=\frac{2^{2n+\alpha+\beta}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1\right).

The combination of (
3.10
), (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) yields

\displaystyle M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}\Gamma(n+q+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2})^{q+1/2}\Gamma(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(n+q+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}{(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2})^{q+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2})\Gamma(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1)}.

The Gamma function satisfies that
\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(n+b)\sim n^{a-b}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and we may conclude that

\displaystyle M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\sim 2^{\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{2}}\frac{n^{q+1}n^{\alpha+\beta+1}}{n^{q+\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}n^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1}}=2^{\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{2}}=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We are left to determine the monotonicity of
M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
. For this reason we consider the quotient

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{M_{n+1}(\alpha,\beta)}{M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)}&=\frac{(n+q+1)(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}+1\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2})(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1)}\\
&=\frac{(n+q+1)(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}\right)^{q-\frac{1}{2}}}{\left(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}+1\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}(n+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1)}.\end{split}

Whether this quotient is bigger than or smaller than
1
determines the monotonicity of the sequence
\{M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\}
. To simplify, we begin by considering the case
\alpha=\beta=q
. Writing
M_{n}(q):=M_{n}(q,q)
, the quotient (
3.14
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{M_{n+1}(q)}{M_{n}(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1+q)(n+2q+1)}{(n+q+\frac{1}{2})(n+q+1)}\left(\frac{n+q+\frac{1}{2}}{n+q+\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{q+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(n+2q+1)\frac{(n+q+\frac{1}{2})^{q-\frac{1}{2}}}{(n+q+\frac{3}{2})^{q+\frac{1}{2}}}.

We introduce the function

f(x)=(x+2q+1)\frac{(x+q+\frac{1}{2})^{q-\frac{1}{2}}}{(x+q+\frac{3}{2})^{q+\frac{1}{2}}},

and claim that
f^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x>0
. From the fact that
f(x)\rightarrow 1
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, this will imply that
f(x)\geq 1
for every
x>0
. As a consequence we see that
M_{n+1}(q)\geq f(n)M_{n}(q)\geq M_{n}(q)
.

Taking the logarithmic derivative of
f
generates

\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{q^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{(x+2q+1)(x+q+\frac{1}{2})(x+q+\frac{3}{2})}.

If
-1/2<q<1/2
, then it is clear that

\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}<0

and as a consequence
f^{\prime}(x)<0
. We conclude that
M_{n+1}(q)\geq M_{n}(q)
and this settles the case where
\alpha=\beta=q
.

If
\alpha\neq\beta
, then the problem is more delicate. We again need to show that the quotient (
3.14
) is strictly greater than
1
. In order to show this, we reintroduce the function
f
by defining it as

(3.15)

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{(x+q+1)(x+\alpha+\beta+1)}{(x+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1)}\frac{(x+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2})^{q-1/2}}{(x+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}+1)^{q+1/2}}.

In a completely analogous manner, we only need to show that
f^{\prime}(x)<0
for every
x\in(0,\infty)
in order to conclude that
f(x)\geq 1
and consequently
M_{n+1}(\alpha,\beta)\geq f(n)M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\geq M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
. Since
M_{n}
is symmetric with respect to its variables and
q=\max(\alpha,\beta)
, it is enough to consider the parameter values
-1/2\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
.

The logarithmic derivative of
f
is given by

\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{1}{x+q+1}+\frac{1}{x+\alpha+\beta+1}+\frac{q-1/2}{x+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}}-\frac{1}{x+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1}-\frac{q+1/2}{x+\frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{2}+1}.

Written over common denominator and utilizing that
\alpha=q
, this expression becomes

(3.16)

\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)x^{2}+c_{1}(\alpha,\beta)x+c_{0}(\alpha,\beta)}{(x+\alpha+1)(x+\alpha+\beta+1)(x+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2})(x+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+1)(x+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+\frac{3}{2})},

where

(3.17)

\displaystyle c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{4},

(3.18)

\displaystyle c_{1}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\alpha^{3}}{4}+\frac{(4\beta+8)\alpha^{2}}{8}+\frac{(\beta^{2}-1)\alpha}{4}+\frac{\beta^{3}}{2}+\beta^{2}-\frac{\beta}{4}-\frac{1}{2},

(3.19)

\displaystyle c_{0}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{4}}{4}+\frac{(3\beta+6)\alpha^{3}}{8}+\frac{(\beta+2)^{2}\alpha^{2}}{8}+\frac{(\beta^{2}-1)(\beta+2)\alpha}{8}+\frac{\beta^{4}}{8}+\frac{\beta^{3}}{2}+\frac{3\beta^{2}}{8}-\frac{\beta}{4}-\frac{1}{4}.

Now it is immediately clear that
c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)<0
unless
|\alpha|=|\beta|=1/2
. We claim that the same is true for the remaining coefficients
c_{1}(\alpha,\beta)
and
c_{0}(\alpha,\beta)
. Namely,
c_{k}(\alpha,\beta)<0
for
k=0,1,2
unless
|\alpha|=|\beta|=1/2
. A consequence of these inequalities is that
f^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x>0
which is precisely the sought after inequality. The proof of this is provided in Lemma
6.1
in the appendix. As a result, we obtain that
f(n)\geq 1
and therefore

M_{n+1}(\alpha,\beta)=f(n)M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\geq M_{n}(\alpha,\beta).

In conclusion,
M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
converges monotonically to
2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
from below. ∎

An immediate consequence of Lemma
3.2
together with (
3.8
) is that

(3.20)

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq 2^{n}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\psi_{k}^{\ast})\right|\leq M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\leq 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}.

This simultaneously shows that Theorem
1.3
holds and that

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

is valid for every
n
. On the other hand, (
1.13
) implies that these quantities are all asymptotically equal as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This is possible only if

\sup_{n}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

and hence the second case of Theorem
1.4
is shown. From the fact that
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
, we gather the following corollary to Lemma
3.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
0\leq\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\leq 1/2
and
r=2\max(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
, then

(3.21)

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq\frac{\Gamma(r+1/2)2^{2n+\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}{{n+r-1/2}\choose{n}}}{\sqrt{\pi}(n+\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta})^{r}{{2n+2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}-1}\choose{n}}}\leq 2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}

and the rightmost inequality is strict unless
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\in\{0,1/2\}
.

4.
The case of
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 1/2

We are left to prove the third and final case of Theorem
1.4
. Our approach to proving this revolves around relating Jacobi weighted Chebyshev polynomials to weighted Chebyshev polynomials on the unit circle. These ideas have previously been investigated in
[
30
,
7
]
for certain choices of the parameters. We will provide a general argument which works for any choice of parameters
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 1/2
.

The approach first studied in
[
30
]
hinges upon the Erdős–Lax inequality, see
[
31
]
. If
P
is a polynomial of degree
n
which does not vanish anywhere on
{\mathbb{D}}
, then

(4.1)

\max_{|z|=1}|P^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{n}{2}\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|.

This result relates the maximum modulus of a polynomial with its derivative. Furthermore, equality occurs in (
4.1
) if and only if all zeros of
P
lie on the unit circle. A recent extension of (
4.1
) to the case of generalized polynomials having all zeros situated on the unit circle is the following.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
s_{k}\geq 1
and
\theta_{k}\in[0,2\pi)
for
k=1,\dotsc,n
. Then

\max_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{d}{dz}\left\{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}})^{s_{k}}\right\}\right|=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}}{2}\max_{|z|=1}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}})^{s_{k}}\right|.

Given a polynomial with zeros in the unit disk, there is a procedure of constructing a related polynomial whose zeros all lie on the unit circle.

Lemma 4.2

(Pólya and Szegő
[
38
]
)
.

Let
|a_{k}|\leq 1
for
k=1,\dotsc,n
and suppose that
m\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

z^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-a_{k})+e^{i\theta}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\overline{a_{k}}z)

does not vanish away from
|z|=1
.

Utilizing these two results, we obtain the following theorem which “opens up” the interval to the circle.

Theorem 4.3
.

Given
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 1/2
, let
\theta_{1}^{\ast},\dotsc,\theta_{n}^{\ast}\in[0,2\pi)
be the unique minimizing data of the expression

(4.2)

I_{n}:=\min_{\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{n}}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k})\right|.

In that case,

\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}+2n}\frac{d}{dz}\left\{(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}^{\ast}})(z-e^{-i\theta_{k}^{\ast}})\right\}

is the unique minimizer of

(4.3)

C_{n}:=\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2n+1}}\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k})\right|.

Furthermore, we have that

(4.4)

C_{n}=2^{n+\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}-1}I_{n}.

Proof.

We will prove this result in the opposite direction as it is stated by first analyzing the minimizer of (
4.3
). Assume that the points
a_{k}^{\ast}
are uniquely chosen to satisfy

\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})\right|=C_{n}.

A result of Fejér
[
21
]
says that the zeros of a complex Chebyshev polynomial corresponding to a compact set
{\mathsf{E}}
are always situated in the convex hull of
{\mathsf{E}}
. In our setting, a similar reasoning ensures us that
|a_{k}^{\ast}|\leq 1
holds for
k=1,\dotsc 2n+1
. It is actually possible to show that this inequality is strict, see
[
30
]
for details. We form the combination

(4.5)

P(z)=z\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})-\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(1-\overline{a_{k}^{\ast}}z).

It is immediate that
P
is a monic polynomial of degree
2n+2
and by Lemma
4.2
we conclude that
P
has all its zeros on the unit circle. Since the minimizer of (
4.3
) is unique, all
a_{k}^{\ast}
must come in conjugate pairs with one possible exception. Due to symmetry of the weight function, this exceptional zero must always be real.
Therefore,

P(z)=z\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})-\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(1-a_{k}^{\ast}z)

and as a consequence,

\displaystyle P(1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(1-a_{k}^{\ast})-\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(1-a_{k}^{\ast})=0,

\displaystyle P(-1)

\displaystyle=-\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(-1-a_{k}^{\ast})-\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(1+a_{k}^{\ast})=0.

We conclude that

(4.6)

P(z)=(z-1)(z+1)\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}^{\ast}})

for some values
\theta_{k}^{\ast}\in[0,2\pi)
.

Now let
\varphi_{k}\in[0,2\pi)
be chosen such that

(4.7)

\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right|=\min_{\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{2n}}\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}})\right|.

From the fact that

\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}\left|1-a_{k}^{\ast}z\right|=\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}|z-a_{k}^{\ast}|

for
|z|=1
together with the representation in (
4.5
), we conclude from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right|\leq\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}P(z)\right|

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})\right|=2C_{n}.

On the other hand, the expression

\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}+2n}\frac{d}{dz}(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})

is of the form

(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-b_{k})

for some values of
b_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. From the Gauss–Lucas Theorem we can actually conclude that
|b_{k}|\leq 1
. As it turns out, this derivative is a candidate for a minimizer of (
4.3
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\max_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}+2n}\frac{d}{dz}\left\{(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right\}\right|

(4.9)

\displaystyle\geq\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})\right|

and Theorem
4.1
immediately gives us that

\displaystyle 2\max_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}+2n}\frac{d}{dz}\left\{(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right\}\right|

(4.10)

\displaystyle=\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right|.

By combining (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we conclude that equality must hold in all the inequalities. In particular,

\displaystyle\max_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}+2n}\frac{d}{dz}\left\{(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right\}\right|

(4.11)

\displaystyle=\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})\right|

and

(4.12)

\displaystyle\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right|=\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}P(z)\right|.

As a consequence of (
4.11
) together with uniqueness of the minimizer, we have that

\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}+2n}\frac{d}{dz}\left\{(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\varphi_{k}})\right\}=(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})

and so the minimizing points
\varphi_{k}
, which were chosen to satisfy (
4.7
), are determined from the point set
\{a_{k}^{\ast}\}
. Incidentally we have established that the solution to (
4.7
) is unique and as a consequence all nodes
e^{i\varphi_{k}}
come in conjugate pairs. Furthermore, (
4.12
) implies that after a possible rearrangement, it holds that
\theta_{k}^{\ast}=\varphi_{k}
for
k=1,\dotsc,2n
.

We now let
x=(z+z^{-1})/2
and recall that
x\in[-1,1]
when
|z|=1
. Under this transformation,

(z-e^{i\theta_{k}})(z-e^{-i\theta_{k}})=2z(x-\cos\theta_{k})

so if we arrange the angles
\theta_{k}^{\ast}
in such a way that
\theta_{k+n}^{\ast}=\theta_{k}^{\ast}+\pi
for
k=1,\dotsc,n
, then

(4.13)

(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}\ast})=(2z)^{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}+n}(x-1)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(x+1)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}^{\ast}).

In conclusion, to each expression of the form

(x-1)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(x+1)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}),

there corresponds a function

(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}})(z-e^{-i\theta_{k}})

and their absolute values are related through multiplication with the constant
2^{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}+n}
. Since the left-hand side of (
4.13
) is minimal in terms of maximal modulus on the unit circle, we conclude that

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|(x-1)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(x+1)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}^{\ast})\right|=I_{n}.

The result now follows from the uniqueness of the minimizer of (
4.2
). ∎

We end this section by listing some consequences of Theorem
4.3
.

Corollary 4.4
.

When
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 1/2
, there is a unique configuration
\theta_{1}^{\ast},\dotsc,\theta_{2n}^{\ast}\in[0,2\pi)
such that

(4.14)

\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}^{\ast}})\right|=\min_{\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{2n}}\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(z-e^{i\theta_{k}})\right|.

Our main conclusion is the following monotonicity result which constitutes the final puzzle piece to proving the third case in Theorem
1.4
.

Corollary 4.5
.

If
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}\geq 1/2
, then
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
decays monotonically to
2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and for every
n
, we have that

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})\leq\left(\frac{2\rho_{\alpha}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\rho_{\beta}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\beta}}.

Proof.

We begin by verifying the monotonicity of the sequence of Widom factors. From (
1.18
) and Theorem
4.3
we know that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})=2^{n}\min_{\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{n}}\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k})\right|

\displaystyle=2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2n+1}}\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k})\right|.

Clearly the right-hand side decreases monotonically in
n
since multiplying by
z^{2}
does not change the absolute value and

\max_{|z|=1}\left|z^{2}(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}(z-a_{k}^{\ast})\right|\\
\geq\min_{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2(n+1)+1}}\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{2(n+1)+1}(z-a_{k})\right|.

Hence monotonicity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
follows.

To show the upper bound, we note that Theorem
4.3
is still valid if
n=0
meaning that the expression

(4.15)

\frac{1}{2\rho_{\alpha}+2\rho_{\beta}}\frac{d}{dz}\left\{(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}}\right\}=(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}(z-a^{\ast})

minimizes (
4.3
) in the case where
n=0
. Consequently,

(4.16)

2^{1-\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}\min_{a}\max_{|z|=1}\left|(z-1)^{2\rho_{\alpha}-1}(z+1)^{2\rho_{\beta}-1}(z-a)\right|=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\right|\\
=\left(1+\frac{\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\alpha}}\left(1-\frac{\rho_{\alpha}-\rho_{\beta}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\beta}}=\left(\frac{2\rho_{\alpha}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\rho_{\beta}}{\rho_{\alpha}+\rho_{\beta}}\right)^{\rho_{\beta}}

and the upper bound now follows from the monotonicity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
. ∎

Proposition 5.1

For fixed

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
is continuous in

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{\ast}
and
\theta_{k}^{\ast}
denote the minimizing nodes associated with the parameters
(\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha},\tilde{\rho}_{\beta})
and
(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})

\displaystyle\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{\ast})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}^{\ast})\Big|

and vice versa when the roles of the parameters
(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
and
(\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha},\tilde{\rho}_{\beta})
are interchanged.

Given
\varepsilon>0
it is always possible to choose
(\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha},\tilde{\rho}_{\beta})
close enough to
(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
in a manner that, independently of the choice of
a_{k}\in[-1,1]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\Big|-\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-a_{k})\Big|\right|\leq\varepsilon.

We thus find that

\displaystyle\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{\ast})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}^{\ast})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}^{\ast})\Big|+\varepsilon

and therefore

\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\tilde{\theta}_{k}^{\ast})\Big|-\max_{x\in[-1,1]}\Big|(1-x)^{\rho_{\alpha}}(1+x)^{\rho_{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\cos\theta_{k}^{\ast})\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

By symmetry, we may also deduce that the negative of the left-hand side is
\leq\varepsilon

when
(\tilde{\rho}_{\alpha},\tilde{\rho}_{\beta})
is sufficiently close to
(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
. This implies the desired continuity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta})
with respect to the parameters
\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\beta}
.

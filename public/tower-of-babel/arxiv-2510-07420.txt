Newton-Okounkov bodies for nested Hilbert schemes
Ian Cavey, Eugene Gorsky, Alexei Oblomkov, Joshua P. Turner

Abstract
. We study sections of line bundles on the nested Hilbert scheme of points on the affine plane. We describe the spaces of sections in terms of certain ideals introduced by Haiman, and find explicit bases for them by analyzing the trailing terms in some monomial order. As a consequence, we compute the Newton-Okounkov bodies for nested Hilbert schemes.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study line bundles on the nested Hilbert schemes of points on the affine plane, and their spaces of sections.

Recall that the Hilbert scheme of
n
points
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is the moduli space of ideals
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{C}[x,y]
such that
\dim\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\mathcal{I}=n
(or, equivalently,
0
-dimensional subschemes
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
of length
n
). It carries the natural tautological bundle
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\mathcal{I}
, the natural line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)=\wedge^{n}\mathcal{T}
and its powers
\mathcal{O}(m)
.

The space of global sections of
\mathcal{O}(m)
was studied by Haiman
[
10
,
11
]
who proved, among other things, that for
m\geq 0
it agrees with
\mathrm{sgn}(m)
-component of the ideal
J^{m}\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{n}]
where

(1.1)

J=\bigcap_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j},y_{i}-y_{j}).

Here
\mathrm{sgn}(m)=\mathrm{sgn}^{\otimes m}
denotes the one-dimensional representation of
S_{n}
which is trivial for
m
even and sign for
m
odd.
In
[
3
]
the first named author described bases for these spaces of sections by characterizing the set of all trailing term exponents of these polynomials. The asymptotic version of this result as
m\to\infty
is a Newton-Okounkov body computation for
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, also described in
[
3
]
.

The main object of this paper is the
nested Hilbert scheme

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
defined as the moduli space of pairs of ideals

\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{C}[x,y]

such that
\dim\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\mathcal{I}=n
and
\dim\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\mathcal{J}=n+1.
Equivalently, it is the space of pairs of subschemes
(Z,Z^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
such that
Z\subset Z^{\prime}
. It is known
[
4
,
17
]
that
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}\mathbb{C}^{2}
is smooth of dimension
2n+2
. We have natural projections

(1.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hilb}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n+1}}

which send a pair
(\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{I})
respectively to
\mathcal{I},\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{J})
and
\mathcal{J}
.
Define line bundles

\mathcal{O}(m,k)=\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(m)\otimes\pi_{n+1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k)

on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Here is our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
m,k\geq 0
the global sections of
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
are identified with the
\mathrm{sgn}(m+k)
-component of
J^{m+k}\cap I^{k}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n},x,y]
, where the ideal
J
is given by (
1.1
) and

I=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x,y_{i}-y).

To prove this result, we first observe that

(1.3)

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

where
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})=\pi^{-1}(0)
. We describe the explicit isomorphism in Lemma
2.2
, which allows us to focus on line bundles on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Next, we consider the Hilbert scheme of points of
\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
, the blowup of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
at the origin. Let
E
be the exceptional divisor in the blowup. Our second main result identifies the sections of line bundles on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is an isomorphism

H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m,k)\right)=H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m+k)\otimes\mathcal{O}(kE)_{n}\right)

where
\mathcal{O}(E)_{n}
denotes the line bundle obtained by pulling back the symmetrization of
\mathcal{O}(E)
from the symmetric power of
\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
essentially follows from a geometric argument: we show that the complements of certain codimension 2 subsets in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
are isomorphic, see Proposition
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
.

The spaces of sections of line bundles on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
also admit a succinct description.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
be the space of antisymmetric polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. Then

H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m+k)\otimes\mathcal{O}(kE)_{n}\right)

is isomorphic to the subspace of polynomials
f
in
A^{m+k}
such that each monomial term
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
of
f
satisfies
a_{i}+b_{i}\geq k
for all
i
.

We deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
by means of a certain isomorphism
\phi
inspired by (
1.3
), see Section
3
for all details.

Next, we describe the explicit bases for these spaces of sections. We use lexicographic order on monomials such that
x<y<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}
. A basis element is characterized by its trailing term with respect to this order.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
m,k\geq 0
, a monomial
x^{a}y^{b}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
is the trailing term of some polynomial
g\in H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m,k)\right)
if and only if:

(1)

a,b\geq 0

(2)

0\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
,

(3)

for any
j=1,\dots,n-1
for which
a_{j}=a_{j+1}
, we have
b_{j+1}\geq b_{j}+m+k
, and

(4)

for each
j=1,\dots,n,
we have
b_{j}\geq\max\{k-a_{j},0\}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\max\{m+k-(a_{j}-a_{i}),0\}.

As a consequence of our proof of Theorem
1.4
, we obtain in Corollary
4.11
new surjectivity results for a certain ring of global sections of line bundles
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

All of the above ideals are homogeneous, with
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
grading given by
\deg(x_{i})=\deg(x)=q
and
\deg(y_{i})=\deg(y)=t
. Geometrically, this grading corresponds to the action of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
(resp.
\mathrm{Bl}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
), and on the corresponding Hilbert schemes and spaces of sections.
We can use Theorem
1.4
to compute their Hilbert series.

Corollary 1.5
.

The Hilbert series of
H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m,k)\right)
is given by

H_{m,k}(q,t)=\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t)}\sum_{\mathcal{P}(m+k,k)}q^{a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}}t^{b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}}

where
\mathcal{P}(m+k,k)
is the subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2n}
defined by the inequalities (2)-(4) above.

Alternatively, for
k,m>0
one can use localization formula (
2.2
) to compute this Hilbert series, see Corollary
2.8
for more details. The equality between the two formulas leads to surprising combinatorial identities which we explore in Section
4.2
.

Finally, we describe the Newton-Okounkov bodies on the nested Hilbert scheme with respect to the corresponding valuation. Let
\Delta(m+k,k)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
denote the polyhedron defined by conditions (2) and (4) in Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

The Newton-Okounkov body of
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
with respect to the valuation described in Section
4.3
is
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}\times\Delta(m+k,k)
.

We expect that the combinatorics of these polytopes is related to the birational geometry of the nested Hilbert scheme from wall-crossing studied by Nakajima and Yoshioka
[
14
]
.

2.
Birational relation

In this section we relate the geometries of the nested Hilbert scheme on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and the Hilbert scheme of the blow up
\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

2.1.
Nested Hilbert schemes

Let
S
be a surface, we denote by
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)
the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
S
and by
U\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)\times S
the universal scheme. We have two projections.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{S}}

and the scheme-theoretic fiber of
p_{n}
over
\mathcal{I}
is
\mathrm{Spec}\ \mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. There is a tautological rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{T}=p_{n*}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
and the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)=\det\mathcal{T}
. More generally, given a divisor
E
on
S
there is
rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{O}(E)^{[n]}=p_{n*}(\mathcal{O}_{U}\otimes p_{S}^{*}(\mathcal{O}(E))

and the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(E)_{n}=\det(\mathcal{O}(E)^{[n]})
.
If
E\subset S
is a smooth irreducible curve then
the latter has a geometric description as the subsheaf of functions vanishing on the locus

E_{n}\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)
where
E_{n}
is the locus of ideal sheaves
\mathcal{I}
such that

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I})\cap E\neq\emptyset
.
Then
E_{n}
is Cartier divisor and
\mathcal{O}(E)_{n}=\mathcal{O}(E_{n})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
8
]

The Picard group of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)
is freely generated by
\mathcal{O}(1)
and the divisors
E_{n}
for
E\in\mathrm{Pic}(S)
, so

\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S))\simeq\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathcal{O}(1)\rangle\oplus\mathrm{Pic}(S).

In particular, for
S=\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
the Picard group of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
is freely generated by
\mathcal{O}(1)
and
E_{n}
where
E\subset\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the exceptional divisor.

Next, we consider the nested Hilbert schemes and the diagram (
1.2
).
Consider the natural projection
\pi:\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
sending a pair of subschemes
(Z,Z^{\prime})
to the extra support point of
Z^{\prime}
, and let
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})=\pi^{-1}(0)
. The following is well known but we provide the proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Proof.

Given
v=(v_{1},v_{2})
, we can define the translation

\tau_{v}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2},\ \tau_{v}(x,y)=(x+v_{1},y+v_{2}).

This translation extends to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
where we also denote it by
\tau_{v}
. We can define the maps

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\ (Z,Z^{\prime})\mapsto(\pi(Z,Z^{\prime}),\tau_{-\pi(Z,Z^{\prime})}(Z,Z^{\prime}))

and

\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\ (v,(Z,Z^{\prime}))\mapsto\tau_{v}(Z,Z^{\prime}).

Clearly, these are inverse to each other and the result folows.
∎

We set
\delta\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the divisor consisting of pairs
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
such that

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I})
contains
0
. Let
\partial=\partial_{n}\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
,
\partial_{n+1}\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

be the big diagonal divisors, i.e. the loci of nonreduced subschemes in each. It is well-known that on these Hilbert schemes we have
\mathcal{O}(\partial_{n})=\mathcal{O}(-2)
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial_{n+1})=\mathcal{O}(-2)
. Then we have
\delta=\pi_{n+1}^{-1}(\partial_{n+1})\setminus\pi_{n}^{-1}(\partial_{n})
and may define the line bundle

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(-\delta/2)=\pi_{n}^{*}(\mathcal{O}(-1))\otimes\pi_{n+1}^{*}(\mathcal{O}(1)).

More generally, we may consider line bundles

\ \mathcal{O}(m,k)=\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(m)\otimes\pi_{n+1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k)

In particular, we have

(2.1)

\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(m)\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k}=\mathcal{O}(m-k,k).

By
[
16
]
,
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is spanned by
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
.

2.2.
Birational relation

We claim that
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
are isomorphic outside of a codimension 2 locus.
In particular, we construct a graph of the birational map

\beta_{n}:\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}).

First, we observe that
\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
are naturally isomorphic and set
\beta_{1}
to be this isomorphism.
As above, let
\pi_{n}:\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the natural projection, and
\partial\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is the big diagonal divisor.

Then
we can define a regular map
\beta_{n}^{\circ}:\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\setminus\pi_{n}^{-1}(\partial)\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
by taking
n
-th symmetric power of
the map
\beta_{1}
. More precisely, the complement of
\pi_{n}^{-1}(\partial)
consists of unordered
n
-tuples of distinct points
Z=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
and another subscheme
Z^{\prime}
such that
Z\subset Z^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{supp}(Z^{\prime})=\mathrm{supp}(Z)\cup 0
. If all of
P_{i}
are distinct from
0
, then
Z^{\prime}
is uniquely determined by
Z
and we just send
(Z,Z^{\prime})
to the corresponding
n
-tuple of points on
\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}
. If
P_{i}=0
for exactly one
i
then we can interpret
(Z,Z^{\prime})
as a point in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus 0)\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{1,2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and use the map
\beta_{1}
to define its image.

Respectively, we define
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
to be the closure of
graph of the map
\beta_{n}^{\circ}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f:\Gamma\to\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
s:\Gamma\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the natural projections. Then
the birational morphism
\beta_{n}=s\circ f^{-1}
is regular in codimension
2
. This map yields an isomorphism
of the Picard groups:

\beta_{n}(\partial)=\partial,\quad\beta_{n}(\delta)=E_{n},\quad\beta_{n}(\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1))=\mathcal{O}(1).

Proof.

Let
V\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}
be the open locus of pairs of subschemes
(Z,Z^{\prime})
such that
Z
has multiplicity at most
1
at the origin.
Clearly,
\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\setminus\pi_{n}^{-1}(\partial)\subset V
and
by the above discussion the map
\beta_{n}^{\circ}
extends to
V
.

Let us prove that the complement of
V
has codimension
2
. Indeed, the locus in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}
where
Z
has multiplicity
k\geq 2
at the origin is locally modeled on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n-k}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus 0)\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{k,k+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
and has codimension

2n-2(n-k)-k=k.

Here we used the fact that
\dim\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})=2n
(this follows, say, from Lemma
2.2
) and
\dim\mathrm{Hilb}^{k,k+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)=k

[
4
]
.

The image
U=\beta_{n}(V)
is the locus in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
consisting of subschemes
Z^{\prime\prime}
such that the total multiplicity of
Z^{\prime\prime}
on the exceptional divisor
E
is at most
1
. We claim that the complement of
U
also has codimension
2
. Indeed, consider the locus in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
where
Z^{\prime\prime}
has
s
points with multiplicities
k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}
on
E
. It is locally modeled on

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n-k_{1}-\ldots-k_{s}}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus E)\times\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{Hilb}^{k_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\times E^{s}

and has codimension

2n-2(n-k_{1}-\ldots-k_{s})-\sum_{i=1}^{s}(k_{i}-1)-s=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}.

The statement about Picard groups follows since
\beta^{\circ}_{n}(\partial\cap V)
and
\beta^{\circ}_{n}(\delta\cap V)
are open sets inside irreducible
divisors
\partial
and
E_{n}
, and
\mathcal{O}(1)=-\frac{1}{2}\partial
.
∎

Since the varieties
\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
are smooth and hence normal we obtain an important statement

.

For all

, there is an identification

denotes the line bundle obtained by pulling back the symmetrization of

from the symmetric power of

.

The fact that
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
are isomorphic outside of codimension

almost immediately

.

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

\mathrm{Bl}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

is Frobenius split by

. Moreover from the previous proof we see that there is an open subset

that is isomorphic to a dense

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Since

the

.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

For any
m>0,k>0
we have
H^{i}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m,k))=0
for all

.

Proof.

Consider the nested Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
as an open subset of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. For fixed
m,k>0
, the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(m,k)\otimes\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(\ell H)_{n}
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
is ample for
\ell
sufficiently large, where

is the line at infinity
[
16
]
. The restriction of
\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(\ell H)_{n}
to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is trivial, so we have that
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
is ample on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
for all

. By Lemma
2.2
,
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
is ample on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
as well. The Frobenius splitting of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}_{0}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
therefore implies that the higher cohomology of
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
vanishes
[
1
]
, as desired.
∎

2.3.
Localization formulas

One can use the Atiyah-Bott localization formula to compute the holomorphic Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
. Recall that the natural action of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
extends to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

The fixed points in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
correspond to monomial ideals
I_{\lambda}
which are labeled by Young diagrams
\lambda
of size
n
. The fixed points in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
correspond to nested pairs of monomial ideals, labeled by pairs of Young diagrams
\lambda\subset\mu
,
|\lambda|=n
and
|\mu|=n+1
. We denote the box
\mu\setminus\lambda
by
\blacksquare
.

The
(q,t)
-character of the cotangent space to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

3.
Global Sections

We start with some definitions. Let
\mathrm{sgn}(m)=\mathrm{sgn}^{\otimes m}
denote the one-dimensional representation of
S_{n}
which is trivial for
m
even and sign for
m
odd. Similarly, if
V
is a representation of
S_{n}
then the
\mathrm{sgn}(m)
component of
V
is
V^{S_{n}}
if
m
is even and
V^{\mathrm{sgn}}
if
m
is odd. Consider the diagonal action of
S_{n}
on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
which permutes
x_{i},y_{i}
simultaneously. We will also consider the action of
S_{n}
on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},x,y]
which permutes
x_{i},y_{i}
simultaneously and fixes the variables
x
and
y
. We define the bigrading on these polynomial rings by
\deg(x_{i})=\deg(x)=q
and
\deg(y_{i})=\deg(y)=t
, and note that the action of
S_{n}
preserves the grading. We will simply refer to
\deg
as to degree.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be the subspace of antisymmetric polynomials. We define
J
as the ideal generated by
A
.

Note that the space of antisymmetric polynomials in

\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},x,y]
with respect to the above action is
A[x,y]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}
be an
n
-element subset. We will always assume without loss of generality that the elements of
S
are labeled in increasing lexicographic order:
S=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})\}
, and define
\Delta_{S}=\det(x_{i}^{a_{j}}y_{i}^{b_{j}})
.

Note that a different choice of ordering for elements of
S
yields the same
\Delta_{S}
up to sign. One can think of
\Delta_{S}
as antisymmetrization of a monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
, which implies that
\Delta_{S}
form a homogeneous basis of
A
.

Definition 3.3
.

We define the integer powers of
A
and
J
as follows. If
m\leq 0
then

J^{m}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

and
A^{m}
is the
\mathrm{sgn}(m)
component of
J^{m}
. If
m>0
then
J^{m}
(resp.
A^{m}
) is the span of products of
m
-tuples of elements of
J
(resp. A).

In particular,
A^{0}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{S_{n}}
. The following result of Haiman (see also
[
3
, Lemma 3.12]
) describes some important properties of
J^{m}
and
A^{m}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
10
,
11
]

For all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
one has

H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m))=A^{m}.

Furthermore, we have

J^{m}=\bigcap_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j},y_{i}-y_{j})^{m}

and the
\mathrm{sgn}(m)
component of
J^{m}
coincides with
A^{m}
.

This description of global sections of line bundles on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
can be generalized to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(X)
for any smooth toric surface
X
. This was done in
[
3
]
in the case
X
is projective, but we state here the more general result and give a self-contained proof. We will then transport the description of sections of line bundles on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
the nested Hilbert scheme using Corollary
2.4
.

The generalization of Theorem
3.4
to toric surfaces
X
with
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
is easily obtained by localization.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the open set defined by
x\neq 0
and
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
the open set defined by
xy\neq 0
. We have open subsets
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C})\subseteq\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, and the sections of
\mathcal{O}(m)
over these open subsets are given by the localizations

H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{O}(m))=(A^{m})_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}],

and

H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m))=(A^{m})_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1},y_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,y_{n}^{\pm 1}].

Moreover, each of these localizations is given by the
\mathrm{sgn}(m)
components of the ideal
\bigcap_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j},y_{i}-y_{j})^{m}
considered as an ideal in the corresponding Laurent polynomial ring.

We define
\widetilde{A^{m}}=(A^{m})_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}
to be the space of sections of
\mathcal{O}(m)
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}).

Now we use this local description to establish the generalization of Theorem
3.4
to arbitrary smooth toric
X
. For background on toric varieties, see
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
D
be a torus-invariant divisor on a smooth toric surface
X
with corresponding polygon
P_{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The global sections
H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(X),\mathcal{O}(D)_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m))
are the elements
f\in\widetilde{A^{m}}
such that
f
, considered as a Laurent polynomial in each pair
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
, is supported on
P_{D}
.

Proof.

Let
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}\subseteq X
denote the open torus. Since
\mathcal{O}(D)
restricts trivially to
T
, we also have that the restriction of
\mathcal{O}(D)_{n}
from
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(X)
to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(T)
is trivial. By Corollary
3.5
, the sections of
\mathcal{O}(m)\otimes\mathcal{O}(D)_{n}
over
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(T)
are therefore identified with
\widetilde{A^{m}}
.

Now we will again use Corollary
3.5
to determine which of these extend to global sections. Write
D=\sum c_{j}D_{j}
where the
D_{j}\subseteq X
are torus-invariant curves. Each
D_{j}
corresponds to a ray in the fan corresponding to
X
, and we let
(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})
denote the corresponding ray generator. We have
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\simeq U_{j}\subseteq X
, where
U_{j}
denotes the open subset obtained by removing all
D_{k}
for
k\neq j
from
X
. The restriction of
\mathcal{O}(D)
to
U_{j}
coincides with that of
\mathcal{O}(c_{j}D_{j})
, and the curve
D_{j}\cap U_{j}\subseteq U_{j}
is defined by
x^{\gamma_{j}}y^{\delta_{j}}=0
where
(\gamma_{j},\delta_{j})
is any integer point such that
\alpha_{j}\gamma_{j}+\beta_{j}\delta_{j}=1
. The sections of
\mathcal{O}
over
U_{j}
are the Laurent polynomials
f(x,y)
such that every term
x^{a}y^{b}
appearing in
f
satisfies
\alpha_{j}a+\beta_{j}b\geq 0
. The sections of
\mathcal{O}(c_{j}D_{j})
over
U_{j}
are obtained by multiplying the sections of
\mathcal{O}
by
(x^{\gamma_{j}}y^{\delta_{j}})^{c_{j}}
, after which the support constraint becomes
\alpha_{j}a+\beta_{j}b\geq c_{j}
.

After a change of coordinates, Corollary
3.5
says that the sections of
\mathcal{O}(m)
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(T)
that extend to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
are those elements
f\in\widetilde{A^{m}}
such that in each pair of variables
x_{i}
,
y_{i}
, any term
x_{i}^{a}y_{i}^{b}
that appears in
f
satisfies
\alpha_{j}a+\beta_{j}b\geq 0
. Sections of
\mathcal{O}(c_{i}D_{i})_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m)
over
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
are obtained by multiplying these by the local equation
(x_{1}^{\gamma_{j}}\cdots x_{n}^{\gamma_{j}}y_{1}^{\delta_{j}}\cdots y_{n}^{\delta_{j}})^{c_{j}}
, after which the support constraint becomes
\alpha_{j}a+\beta_{j}b\geq c_{j}
for the exponents appearing on each set of variables.

Consider the restriction of
\mathcal{O}(D)_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m)
to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
. This line bundle coincides with the restriction of
\mathcal{O}(c_{j}D_{j})_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m)
. Therefore, the sections of the restriction of
\mathcal{O}(D)_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m)
over
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(T)
that extend to
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
are the elements
f\in\widetilde{A^{m}}
that satisfy the support constraint corresponding to
\mathcal{O}(c_{j}D_{j})
in each pair of variables
x_{i}
,
y_{i}
.

Finally, we observe that the open sets
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
cover
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(X)
in codimension
1
. Indeed, the complement of the open set
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(T)\subseteq\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(X)
is the union of the divisors consisting of ideals
\mathcal{I}
meeting one of the curves
D_{j}\subseteq X
, and each of these divisors meets
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
. Thus the global sections of
\mathcal{O}(D)_{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m)
are the same as those sections over
\bigcup\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(U_{j})
, which we have shown above is as claimed.
∎

In particular, we have the following.

Proposition 3.7
.

The global sections of
H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m)\otimes\mathcal{O}(kE)_{n}\right)
consist of polynomials
f
in
A^{m}
such that each monomial term
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
of

satisfies
a_{i}+b_{i}\geq k
for all
i
.

We denote the set of polynomials in
A^{m}
satisfying the condition in Proposition
3.7
by
A^{m}_{\geq k}
.
To realize these as sections on the nested Hilbert scheme, we substitute

x_{i}-x
for
x_{i}
and
y_{i}-y
for
y_{i}
,
which corresponds to the change of coordinates that shifts the distinguished point
(x,y)
to the origin.

Definition 3.8
.

We define the ring homomorphism

\phi:\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n},x,y]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n},x,y]

x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}-x,\,y_{i}\mapsto y_{i}-y,

x\mapsto x,\,y\mapsto y.

Remark 3.9
.

We think of
\phi
as an algebraic incarnation of the isomorphism in Lemma
2.2
. Indeed,
(x_{i},y_{i})
are support points of the subscheme
Z
while
(x,y)=\pi(Z,Z^{\prime})
is the extra support point of
Z^{\prime}
for
(Z,Z^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Lemma 3.10
.

a) Suppose
f\in A^{m}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
q^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}
, then

(3.1)

\phi(f)=\\
f(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{n})+\sum_{a\leq d_{1},b\leq d_{2},(a,b)\neq(d_{1},d_{2})}x^{a}y^{b}g_{a,b}(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{n})

where
g_{a,b}\in A^{m}
is homogeneous of degree
q^{d_{1}-a}t^{d_{2}-b}
.

b) For all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
the map
\phi
maps
A^{m}[x,y]
isomorphically to itself.

Proof.

a) Observe that the map
\phi
preserves the degree and commutes with the action of
S_{n}
. If
f\in A
is a homogeneous antisymmetric polynomial of degree
q^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}
then
\phi(f)
is antisymmetric and belongs to
A[x,y]
, so
g_{a,b}\in A
. Similarly, if
f
is symmetric then all
g_{a,b}
are symmetric, this proves the statement for
m\leq 1
.

For
m>1
, observe that
\phi(f_{1}\cdots f_{m})=\phi(f_{1})\cdots\phi(f_{m})
. If
f_{i}\in A
for each
i
then we can expand
\phi(f_{i})
as a polynomial in
x,y
using (
3.1
), and all coefficients belong to
A
. By multiplying such expansions, we get an expansion of
\phi(f_{1}\cdots f_{m})
where all coefficients are in
A^{m}
. Since the coefficients
g_{a,b}
are uniquely determined by
f
, the result follows.

b) If we choose a homogeneous basis
\{f_{\alpha}\}
of
A^{m}
then
f_{\alpha}x^{i}y^{j}
is a homogeneous basis of
A^{m}[x,y]
. We introduce a partial order on the latter by

f_{\alpha}x^{i}y^{j}\prec f_{\beta}x^{i^{\prime}}y^{j^{\prime}}\quad\mathrm{if}\ i\leq i^{\prime},j\leq j^{\prime},(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}).

We claim that
\phi(f_{\alpha})x^{i}y^{j}
is another basis of
A^{m}[x,y]
. Indeed, by (
3.1
) it is related to
f_{\alpha}x^{i}y^{j}
by a unitriangular matrix, therefore
\phi
maps
A^{m}[x,y]
isomorphically to itself.
∎

Definition 3.11
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},x,y]
be the ideal

I=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x,y_{i}-y).

Lemma 3.12
.

We have that

I^{k}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x,y_{i}-y)^{k}.

Furthermore,

f\in\mathrm{span}\left(x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}\mid a_{i}+b_{i}\geq k\quad\mathrm{for\ all}\ i\right)

if and only if
\phi(f)\in I^{k}
.

Proof.

First, observe that the monomial ideal

I^{\prime}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i},y_{i})

is spanned by the monomials
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
such that
a_{i}+b_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
. Similarly,

(I^{\prime})^{k}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i},y_{i})^{k}

is spanned by the monomials
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}

a_{i}+b_{i}\geq k
for all

. It remains to notice that
\phi(I^{\prime})=I
and

\phi((I^{\prime})^{k})=I^{k}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x,y_{i}-y)^{k}.

∎

Lemma 3.13
.

The map

\phi:A^{m+k}_{\geq k}[x,y]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n},x,y]

is injective with image
A^{m+k}[x,y]\cap I^{k}

tells us that

.

We have an isomorphism

4.
Trailing Terms of Global Sections

4.1.
Trailing Terms Analysis

In this section we describe bases for the global sections of line bundles on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathrm{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
by characterizing the sets of trailing term exponents of the corresponding polynomials. Throughout, we use the lexicographic term order with
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}
. Here the
trailing term
of
f
is defined to be the term
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{b^{n}}
such that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
is lexicographically minimal among all the terms of
f
.

Example 4.1
.

For
m=0
,
A^{0}_{\geq k}
is the set of symmetric polynomials with respect to the diagonal
S_{n}
-action on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}]
satisfying the support constraint. There is a simple basis for this space consisting of polynomials of the form

x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{b_{n}}+(\text{symmetric terms}),

where
(a_{1},b_{1})\leq\cdots\leq(a_{n},b_{n})
are integer points with
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 0
and
a_{i}+b_{i}\geq k
. With the points
(a_{i},b_{i})
ordered in non-decreasing lexicographic order as above,
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{b_{n}}
is the trailing term of this polynomial. One can show that the trailing term of any element of
A^{0}_{\geq k}
is of this form.

Example 4.2
.

For
m=1
,
A^{1}_{\geq k}
is the set of anti-symmetric polynomials with respect to the diagonal
S_{n}
-action on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}]
satisfying the support constraint. There is again a simple basis for this space, the determinants
\Delta_{S}
where
S=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},b_{n})\}
is a collection of distinct integer points
(a_{1},b_{1})<\cdots<(a_{n},b_{n})
with
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 0
and
a_{i}+b_{i}\geq k
. Again, we order
(a_{i},b_{i})
in increasing lexicographic order as above so that
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{b_{n}}
is the trailing term of this polynomial. Similarly, one can show that the trailing term of any element of
A^{1}_{\geq k}
is of this form.

Characterizing the trailing terms of
A^{m}_{\geq k}
for
m>1
is more difficult as there are no longer obvious bases to work with. As we will show, the trailing term exponents of
A^{m}_{\geq k}
are characterized by the following explicitly defined set.

Definition 4.3
.

For any integers
m,k\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{P}(m,k)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2n}
be the subset defined by:

(1)

0\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
,

(2)

for any
j=1,\dots,n-1
for which
a_{j}=a_{j+1}
, we have
b_{j+1}\geq b_{j}+m
, and

(3)

for each
j=1,\dots,n,
we have
b_{j}\geq\max\{k-a_{j},0\}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\max\{m-(a_{j}-a_{i}),0\}.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
m,k\geq 0
, a monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
is the trailing term of some polynomial
f\in A^{m}_{\geq k}
if and only if
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)
.
For
m>0
, these are exactly the monomials that appear as the trailing term of an
m
-fold product of determinants
\Delta_{S^{(1)}}\cdots\Delta_{S^{(m)}}
for some
n
-element subsets

such that the product
\Delta_{S^{(1)}}\cdots\Delta_{S^{(m)}}
satisfies the support condition defining
A^{m}_{\geq k}
.

is given below after some preparatory results.

.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

. For any integer
k\geq 0
, there exist

with
\sum_{i}k_{i}=k
such that

. Furthermore, we may choose the

are those for

.

We will not need the final condition in the previous lemma for its applications, but including it in the statement helps to simplify the proof.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
with the trivial
k=0
base case. Given
\ell_{k}(S)=\ell_{k_{1}}(S^{(1)})+\cdots+\ell_{k_{m}}(S^{(m)})
with
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}
as in the statement of the lemma, we will show that one of the
k_{i}
’s can be replaced by
k_{i}+1
to obtain such a decomposition for
\ell_{k+1}(S)
. Writing
\ell_{k}(S)=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},b_{n})\}
, we have
\ell_{k+1}(S)=\{(a_{1},b_{1}^{\prime}),\dots,(a_{n},b_{n}^{\prime})\}
, where

b_{j}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}b_{j}+1&\text{if }a_{j}\leq k,\\
b_{j}&\text{if }a_{j}>k.\end{cases}

If
k\geq a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, then all the points in
\ell_{k}(S)
are shifted up by one to obtain the corresponding points in
\ell_{k+1}(S)
. In this case, we may replace any one of the
k_{i}
’s with
k_{i}+1
. Indeed, by hypothesis we have
a^{(i)}\leq k_{i}
for all
i,j
, so replacing
\ell_{k_{i}}(S^{(i)})
by
\ell_{k_{i}+1}(S^{(i)})
in the decomposition also shifts all the points up by one as desired.

Otherwise let
j
be the smallest index such that
a_{j}>k
. In other words,
(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{j-1},b_{j-1})
are the points of
\ell_{k}(S)
that must be shifted up to obtain the corresponding points of
\ell_{k+1}(S)
, while
(a_{j},b_{j}),\dots,(a_{n},b_{n})
are unchanged. Since
a_{j}=a_{j}^{(1)}+\cdots+a_{j}^{(m)}
and

, there is some index
i
such that
a_{j}^{(i)}>k_{i}
. By our choice of

we have
a_{1},\dots,a_{j-1}\leq k
so by the final induction hypothesis we also have
a_{1}^{(i)},\dots,a_{j-1}^{(i)}\leq k_{i}
. Therefore, replacing
\ell_{k_{i}}(S^{(i)})
by
\ell_{k_{i}+1}(S^{(i)})
in the decomposition also shifts up exactly the points
(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{j-1},b_{j-1})
by one, as desired.

Lastly, we check that the final induction hypothesis is preserved by this procedure. In each step, for any index

such that

(i.e.
j^{\prime}>j
in the notation of the previous paragraph), we are increasing one of the
k_{i}
’s that has
a_{j^{\prime}}^{(i)}\geq a_{j}^{(i)}>k_{i}
. It follows that for any index
j^{\prime}
such that
a_{j^{\prime}}\leq k
, we must have
a_{j^{\prime}}^{(i)}\leq k_{i}
for all

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

First we show that for
f\in A^{m}
a polynomial with trailing term
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
we must have
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathcal{P}(m,k)
. We argue directly that
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
is satisfied. Indeed, since
f
is either symmetric or anti-symmetric, terms corresponding to the permutations of these pairs of exponents also appear in
f
. For
x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
to be the trailing term in this lexicographic term order, it must in particular be the last term among these permutations, which implies
0\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
.

To see that conditions (2) and (3) in the definition of
\mathcal{P}(m,k)
are necessary, we apply some intermediate results of
[
3
]
. Specifically, since
f\in A^{m}
we may apply Proposition 3.5 of
[
3
]
, noting that in the notation of this proposition the hypothesis
f\in\overline{A}^{m}
is satisfied since by Corollary 3.10 of
[
3
]
we have
A^{m}=\overline{A}^{m}
. This proposition directly states that our condition (2) is satisfied, and also that for any
j=1,\dots,n
, the Newton polytope of
f
contains some integer point corresponding to exponents
a_{j}
and
b_{j}^{\prime}:=b_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\max\{m-(a_{j}-a_{i}),0\}
on
x_{j}
and
y_{j}
respectively. But we are assuming that
f\in A^{m}_{\geq k}
, so these integer points
(a_{j},b_{j}^{\prime})
must satisfy the inequality restriction on the support of
f
. In other words, we have
b_{j}^{\prime}\geq\max\{k-a_{j}^{\prime},0\}
, which is equivalent to the inequality asserted in condition (3). This shows that conditions (1), (2), and (3) are necessary for trailing term exponents of elements of
A^{m}_{\geq k}
.

For the other direction, as well as the additional claims, recall that we have observed that every element of
\mathcal{P}(m,k)
is of the form
\ell_{k}(S)
for some set
S=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},b_{n})\}
corresponding to a point of
\mathcal{P}(m,0)
. By Theorem
[
3
]
Propositions 3.7 and 3.9 we can decompose
S=S^{(1)}+\cdots+S^{(m)}
for some sets
S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(m)}
, and by Lemma
4.9
there are some
k_{1},\dots,k_{m}
with
\sum k_{i}=k
such that
\ell_{k}(S)=\ell_{k_{1}}(S^{(1)})+\cdots+\ell_{k_{m}}(S^{(m)})
. This means that
\ell_{k}(S)
is obtained as the trailing term exponent of the product

f=\Delta_{\ell_{k_{1}}(S^{(1)})}\cdots\Delta_{\ell_{k_{m}}(S^{(m)})}.

The final observation is that
\Delta_{\ell_{k_{i}}(S^{(i)})}\in A^{1}_{\geq k_{i}}
for each
i=1,\dots,m
, since by the definition of the lifting operator every point
(a,b)\in\ell_{k_{i}}(S^{(i)})
satisfies
a+b\geq k
. This implies that their product
f
is in
A^{m}_{\geq k}
, exhibiting
\ell_{k}(S)
as the trailing term exponent of a product as claimed.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

For
m,k>0
, the natural multiplication map

\bigoplus_{k_{1}+\ldots+k_{m}=k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(1-k_{i},k_{i}))\to H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m-k,k))

is surjective. In other words, the bigraded algebra

\bigoplus_{k,m=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(m-k,k))=\bigoplus_{k,m=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\pi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}(m)\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k})

is generated by the components of
m
-degree 1.

Using Theorem
4.4
, we can determine the trailing terms of
A^{m+k}[x,y]\cap I^{k}
with respect to the extended lexicographic term order with
x<y<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}
. Indeed, combining (
3.1
) with Theorem
4.4
we obtain the following:

Corollary 4.12
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
m,k\geq 0
, a monomial
x^{a}y^{b}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{n}^{b_{n}}
is the trailing term of some polynomial

4.2.

Corollary 4.13

The Hilbert series of

4.3.
Newton-Okounkov Bodies

Newton-Okounkov bodies are asymptotic invariants of divisors/line bundles usually considered only on projective varieties. They were originally constructed by Okounkov
[
15
]
, and their theory was later developed by Kaveh and Khovanskii
[
12
]
and by Lazarsfeld and Mustaţă
[
13
]
. In this section we show that the construction works for the non-projective variety
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and characterize the resulting sets.

We first recall the basic construction
[
12
,
13
]
. For a variety
X
with line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, we have a graded ring of sections

\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d}).

The Newton-Okounkov body depends on valuation-like function
\nu:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d})\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\dim(X)}
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For any nonzero constant
c
and nonzero section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d})
we have
\nu(cs)=\nu(s).

(2)

Ordering
\mathbb{Z}^{\dim(X)}
lexicographically, we have
\nu(s_{1}+s_{2})\geq\min\{\nu(s_{1}),\nu(s_{2})\}
for any two nonzero sections
s_{1},s_{2}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d}).

(3)

For nonzero sections
s_{1}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d_{1}})
and
s_{2}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d_{2}})
we have
\nu(s_{1}\otimes s_{2})=\nu(s_{1})+\nu(s_{2})
.

(4)

The valuation
\nu
has one-dimensional leaves. In other words, for every
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\dim(x)}
the quotient of the vector space
\{s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d})|\nu(s)\geq\mathbf{v}\})\cup\{0\}
by
\{s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d})|\nu(s)>\mathbf{v}\})\cup\{0\}
is at most one-dimensional.

Definition 4.15
.

The Newton-Okounkov body of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
\nu
, denoted
\Delta(\mathcal{L})
or
\Delta_{\nu}(\mathcal{L})
is the closed convex hull in
\mathbb{R}^{\dim(X)}
of the set

\bigcup_{d\geq 1}\frac{1}{d}\cdot\text{Im}(\nu:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d})\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\dim(X)}).

We will take
X=\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(m,k)
. In this case we have
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d}=\mathcal{O}(dm,dk)
, and by Theorem
3.14
we know the sections
H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(dm,dk))\simeq A^{dm+dk}[x,y]\cap I^{dk}
. Let
\nu
be the valuation that takes a nonzero polynomial
f\in A^{dm+dk}[x,y]\cap I^{dk}
to its lexicographic trailing term exponent
(a,b,a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
with variables ordered as in the previous section. One can check that it satisfies the properties (1)-(4) above.

Definition 4.16
.

Let
\Delta(m,k)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be the set of points
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
such that:

(1)

0\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
, and

(2)

for each
j=1,\dots,n,
we have
b_{j}\geq\max\{k-a_{j},0\}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\max\{m+k-(a_{j}-a_{i}),0\}.

Note that
\Delta(m,k)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is a polyhedron since condition (2) above can be broken up into separate linear inequalities among the coordinates.

Lemma 4.17
.

\overline{\bigcup_{d\geq 1}\frac{1}{d}\cdot\mathcal{P}(dm,dk)}=\Delta(m,k).

Proof.

We first observe that
\mathcal{P}(m,k)\subseteq\Delta(m,k)
is a subset that contains every integer point in the strict interior of
\Delta(m,k)
. Indeed, for any interior point we have
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}
, so condition (2) in the definition of
\mathcal{P}(m,k)
never applies, and the remaining conditions in the definition of
\mathcal{P}(m,k)
are exactly the defining equations of
\Delta(m,k)
. By the same argument, we have the same conclusion for
\mathcal{P}(dm,dk)\subseteq\Delta(dm,dk)
for any
d\geq 1
. Scaling both sets by a factor of
\frac{1}{d}
, we have that
\frac{1}{d}\cdot\mathcal{P}(dm,dk)\subseteq\frac{1}{d}\cdot\Delta(dm,dk)
is a subset containing every interior point with
\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}
-coordinates of
\frac{1}{d}\cdot\Delta(dm,dk)
.

By the homogeneity of the defining inequalities we have
d\cdot\Delta(m,k)=\Delta(dm,dk)
for any
d\geq 1
, and hence
\frac{1}{d}\cdot\Delta(dm,dk)=\Delta(m,k)
. Thus, we have shown that

\bigcup_{d\geq 1}\frac{1}{d}\cdot\mathcal{P}(dm,dk)\subseteq\Delta(m,k)

is a subset containing every rational point in the strict interior of
\Delta(m,k)
. One easily checks that
\Delta(m,k)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is a full-dimensional polyhedron (for example, it contains every point with
0\ll a_{1}\ll a_{2}\ll\cdots\ll a_{n}
and
b_{i}\geq 0
), so closure of this subset is exactly
\Delta(m,k)
as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

The Newton-Okounkov body of
\mathcal{O}(m,k)
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}\mathbb{C}^{2}
with respect to
\nu
is the polyhedron
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}\times\Delta(m+k,k)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.12
, we have

\text{Im}(\nu:H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n,n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathcal{O}(dm,dk))\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{2n+2})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}\times\mathcal{P}(dm+dk,dk).

With this characterization, the Newton-Okounkov body is the closed convex hull of the set

\bigcup_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d}\cdot\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}\times\mathcal{P}(dm+dk,dk)\right).

By Lemma
4.17
, this is exactly
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}\times\Delta(m+k,k)
, as desired.
∎

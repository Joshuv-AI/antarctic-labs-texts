Planar Bilipschitz Extension from Separated Nets
Michael Dymond School of Mathematics University of Birmingham Birmingham B15 2TT, UK 0000-0002-1900-3549, Vojtěch Kaluža Institute of Science and Technology Austria Am Campus 1 3400 Klosterneuburg, Austria 0000-0002-2512-8698

Abstract
We prove that every L L -bilipschitz mapping ℤ 2 → ℝ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2} can be extended to a C ⁡ ( L ) C(L) -bilipschitz mapping ℝ 2 → ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2} and provide a polynomial upper bound for C ⁡ ( L ) C(L) . Moreover, we extend the result to every separated net in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} instead of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} , with the upper bound gaining a polynomial dependence on the separation and net constants associated to the given separated net. This answers an Oberwolfach question of Navas [ 1 ] from 2015 and is also a positive solution of the two-dimensional form of a decades old open (in all dimensions at least two) problem due to Alestalo, Trotsenko and Väisälä [ 2 ] .

Introduction.

A classical theorem of Kirszbraun
[
9
]
says that any
L
-Lipschitz mapping defined on
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be extended to an
L
-Lipschitz mapping
\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is one of the fundamental results of Lipschitz analysis with a wide range of applications.

While there is no obstruction for the extendability of Lipschitz mappings in the realm of euclidean spaces, the story is very different in the case of bilipschitz mappings. Every bilipschitz
f\colon A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an embedding of
A
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any bilipschitz mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a homeomorphism. Thus, if
f
does not preserve some topological invariant of the pair
(\mathbb{R}^{d},A)
, then there is no hope to extend
f
to a homeomorphism
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let alone to a bilipschitz one. As an illustrative example, let
A
be a unit circle in the plane together with its centre and consider
f
that is equal to the identity on the circle, but maps its centre to a point outside the circle. Then there is no homeomorphic extension of
f
to the whole plane, since every homeomorphism of the plane maps the inside of the circle onto the inside of the image of the circle.

To add to the subtlety of the picture, even if there is a homeomorphic extension to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of a bilipschitz
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there still might not be a bilipschitz one; for an example, see
[
19
]
.

Another difference to the Lipschitz case stems from the fact that even if we
know
that for
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
every bilipschitz
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be extended to a bilipschitz mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it might not be possible for an extension to have the same bilipschitz constant as
f
; see, e.g.,
[
10
, Ex. 5.2]
. Controlling the bilipschitz constant of extensions (when they exist) therefore emerges as an additional facet to extension problems; see for example
[
5
,
10
,
11
]
.

For convenience in the continuing discussion, we fix some terminology:

The bilipschitz extension problem for a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the task of determining whether every bilipschitz mapping
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits a bilipschitz extension
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Naturally, investigation of the bilipschitz extension problem for
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
begins with the simplest forms of sets
A
. From a linear structure point of view these are lines and from a metric topology point of view, these are uniformly discrete, where the most basic example, beyond finite sets, is the integer lattice
A=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Whilst the bilipschitz extension problem for lines has a positive solution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
18
]
and a negative solution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
12
, 3.10]
,
[
17
, 14]
, bilipschitz extension from the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has remained elusive. The bilipschitz extension problem for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, or, more generally, for separated nets

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is therefore fundamental for advancing understanding of bilipschitz extendability.

Accordingly, the bilipschitz extension problem for separated nets

of

has received significant exposure and attention. Formally it dates back to 2003: Alestalo, Trotsenko and Väisälä
[

question being distinguished by Navas as one of the open problems

considered by the Arbeitsgemeinschaft on Mathematical Quasicrystals, held in Oberwolfach in 2015. The problem has further appeared explicitly in

In the present article, we provide a positive solution for the two dimensional bilipschitz extension problem for separated nets. In particular, this is a complete positive solution of Navas’ problem
[
1
, Problem 2.6.1]
at Oberwolfach. It is also a positive solution of Alestalo, Trotsenko and Väisälä’s problems
[

Whenever the bilipschitz extension problem for a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
has a positive solution, this opens up a further question of the optimal bilipschitz constant with which an extension can be found. For applications, it is crucial to have a bound on the bilipschitz constant of the extension depending only on the bilipschitz constant of
f
, the set
A
and the dimension
d
. Alestalo, Trotsenko and Väisälä
[
2
, 4.4(a)–(b)]
specifically ask for this in their formulation of the bilipschitz extension problem for separated nets.

In addition to extending bilipschitz mappings of separated nets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we will provide an explicit bound on the bilipschitz constant of our extension, determined by the bilipschitz constant of the initial mapping and the separation and net constants of the separated net.

Most positive results on bilipschitz extension occur in dimension two. In two key settings, progress has started with a solution to a bilipschitz extension problem and then developed further to describe and improve on the control of the bilipschitz constant of the extension. Given a bilipschitz mapping to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, defined on a boundary of a square or on a line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there is a bilipschitz extension of the mapping to the whole square or to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
respectively. Both of these results were first proven by Tukia
[
17
,
18
]
, however, without providing any explicit dependence between the bilipschitz constant of the extension and that of the original mapping. An explicit bound in the case of the boundary of a square was first obtained by Daneri and Pratelli
[
5
]
. Currently, the best dependence, due to Kovalev
[
11
]
, extends any
L
-bilipschitz mapping of the unit circle to a
2\cdot 10^{14}L
-bilipschitz mapping of the whole unit disk. An explicit bound has also been established in the case of the line: Kovalev
[
10
]
proves that any
L
-bilipschitz mapping of a line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be extended to a
2000L
-bilipschitz mapping of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The absence of positive results and significant additional difficulty of bilipschitz extension in dimensions higher than two is perhaps best demonstrated by the situation surrounding the Jordan–Schoenflies Theorem (see e.g.
[
16
, Thm. 3.1]
). The Jordan–Schoenflies Theorem states that any embedding of the circle in the plane extends to a homeomorphism of the plane. However, its analogue fails for embeddings of
S^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all higher dimensions
d\geq 3

[
13
, Thm. 3.7]
, even if such an embedding, known as a wild sphere, is additionally assumed to be bilipschitz!
[
7
, Thm. 7.2.1]
We discuss the higher dimensional situation in greater detail in a companion article
[
6
]
. The main results of
[
6
]
, as well as the positive extension results discussed above, for the boundary of a square and for a line, play an important role in the present work.

There is a justifiable potential for the methodology and strategy introduced in the present solution of the two-dimensional bilipschitz extension problem for separated nets, to form a basis for the future research objective of achieving a solution in general higher dimension. This is substantiated in our companion article
[
6
]
. In particular, in
[
6
, Theorem 1.5]
we reduce the higher dimensional problem to two conjectures concerning bilipschitz extension to or from
(d-1)
-dimensional hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The proof of these two conjectures is completed in the present work in dimension
d=2
; significant and powerful new tools would need to be added in, in order to implement our strategies in higher dimensions.

Main Results and Strategy.

The main result of the present article extends any
L
-bilipschitz mapping
f\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
to a
p(L)
-bilipschitz mapping
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
p
is a polynomial.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a polynomial function
p\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any
L\geq 1
and any
L
-bilipschitz mapping
f\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
there is a
p(L)
-bilipschitz mapping
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
F|_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}=f
.

Although above we do not determine the polynomial
p(L)
, we later state a version of the result, in Theorem
1.4
, where this polynomial is given explicitly. In our companion article
[
6
]
we prove, in all finite dimensions
d\geq 2
, that the bilipschitz extension problem for separated nets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is equivalent to the bilipschitz extension problem for the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. This allows us to replace
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in Theorem
1.1
with an arbitrary separated net
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, gaining a dependence on the separation and net constants of
X
in the expression bounding the bilipschitz constant of the extension
F
. More precisely, Theorem
1.1
and
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
admit the following immediate corollary:

Corollary 1
.

Let the polynomial function
p\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by Theorem
1.1
. Then, for any
L\geq 1
, any
r,R>0
, any
r
-separated
R
-net
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any
L
-bilipschitz mapping
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there is a bilipschitz mapping
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
F|_{X}=f
and

\operatorname{bilip}(F)\leq Kp\left(50R^{2}K^{3}L\right),

where
K:=16\max\left\{\frac{6}{r},1\right\}
.

In what follows, we set out the main extension tools which we develop in the paper and explain how they combine to prove our main result Theorem
1.1
.

One key tool in the proof of Theorem
1.1
comes from our companion article
[
6
]

into strips. Crucially

Theorem 1.2
.

[

For any

(i)

There exists a bilipschitz mapping

.

(ii)

There exist

\widetilde{F}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cup\left(\mathbb{R}\times\left(T\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\to\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad\widetilde{F}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\\

Preliminaries.

Basic notation and facts.

We use largely a common notation in the present article and its companion article
[
6
]
. We write
:=
to signify a definition by equality. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
the notation
[n]
refers to
\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. Moreover, for
P\in\mathbb{R}
, the notation
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq P}
(e.g.) stands for
\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\colon i\geq P\right\}
. We write
e_{1},e_{2}
for the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The orthogonal projection to the
i
-th coordinate in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is denoted by
\proj_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Given two sets
A,A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
we let

\dist(A,A^{\prime}):=\inf\left\{\left\|a-a^{\prime}\right\|\colon a\in A,\,a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\right\}.

In the case that
A=\left\{a\right\}
is a singleton we just write
\dist(a,A^{\prime})
instead of
\dist(\left\{a\right\},A^{\prime})
.
The expression
\diam(A)
stands for the diameter of the set
A
.

Given
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
r>0
, we say that
A
is
r
-separated
if
\left\|a-a^{\prime}\right\|\geq r
for every
a,a^{\prime}\in A,a\neq a^{\prime}
. We say that
A
is an
r
-net
if
\dist(x,A)\leq r
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If there are
r,s>0
such that
A
is an
s
-separated
r
-net, we call
A
a
separated net
.

We write
B(x,r)
and
\overline{B}(x,r)
respectively for the open and closed
euclidean
balls with centre
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and radius
r\geq 0
. Moreover, we use the same notation for neighbourhoods of sets, i.e,
B(A,r):=\bigcup_{x\in A}B(x,r)
, where
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and similarly for
\overline{B}(A,r)
. The letter
B
is reserved for balls generally. Occasionally, we use also balls with respect to the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm; these are denoted by
B_{\infty}
(or
\overline{B}_{\infty}
for the closed version).

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, by
[x,y]
we mean the closed line segment with endpoints
x
and
y
.

Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, by
\partial A,\inter A
and
\overline{A}
, we denote the boundary, interior and closure of
A
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, respectively. For a mapping
f\colon A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
, we write
f|_{A}
for the restriction of
f
to
A
. Given a second mapping
g\colon A^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we write
f\equiv g
if
f(x)=g(x)
for every
x\in A^{\prime}
. The notation
\dom(f),\img(f)
refer to the domain and the image of
f
, respectively. We say that
f
is a
homeomorphism
if
f
is injective, continuous and that its inverse mapping
f^{-1}\colon f(A^{\prime})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is also continuous (sometimes the term
embedding
is used instead in the literature).

Lipschitz and bilipschitz mappings.

For a subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a mapping
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
we let

\operatorname{Lip}(f):=\sup\left\{\frac{\left\|f(y)-f(x)\right\|}{\left\|y-x\right\|}\colon x,y\in A,x\neq y\right\}.

In the case that
f
is injective, we further define

\operatorname{bilip}(f):=\max\left\{\operatorname{Lip}(f),\operatorname{Lip}(f^{-1})\right\}.

Given
L\geq 1
, we say that
f
is
L
-Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leq L
and that it is
L
-bilipschitz
if
\operatorname{bilip}(f)\leq L
. Further, we say that
f
is
Lipschitz
(
bilipschitz
) if there is
L<\infty
such that
f
is
L
-Lipschitz (
L
-bilipschitz).

Throughout the paper, we will construct many polygonal curves as images of piecewise linear mappings defined on
\mathbb{R}
. The following criterion, providing sufficient conditions for such a mapping to be bilipschitz, is going to be used several times.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a mapping,
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a strictly increasing double-sided sequence of real numbers and
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
be such that for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the restriction
\gamma|_{[p_{n},p_{n+1}]}
is affine and the angle at
\gamma(p_{n})
in the (possibly degenerate) triangle with vertices
\gamma(p_{n-1})
,
\gamma(p_{n})
and
\gamma(p_{n+1})
is at least
\alpha
. Let
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
be constants satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\frac{1}{C_{1}}\leq\left\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right\|\leq C_{1}
for almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

For any
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\left|n-m\right|\geq 2
we have that

\dist\left(\left[\gamma(p_{n-1}),\gamma(p_{n})\right],\left[\gamma(p_{m-1}),\gamma(p_{m})\right]\right)\geq C_{2}.

(c)

(i)

For any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|\geq C_{3}
we have
\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|\geq\frac{1}{C_{4}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|
,
or

(ii)

\gamma|_{\left\{p_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}}
is
C_{1}
-bilipschitz and
\left\|\gamma(p_{n})-\gamma(p_{n-1})\right\|=C_{3}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Then
\gamma
is
\max\left\{\frac{4C_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\cos\alpha}},C_{4},\frac{C_{3}}{C_{2}}\right\}
-bilipschitz if (
ci
) holds and
\gamma
is
\max\left\{\frac{4C_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\cos\alpha}},\frac{10C_{1}C_{3}}{C_{2}}\right\}
-bilipschitz if (
cii
) holds.

Proof.

The fact that
\gamma
is piecewise affine and the second inequality in (
a
) imply that
\gamma
is
C_{1}
-Lipschitz. It remains to check the lower bound in the bilipschitz condition. Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
and let
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
be the indices for which
t_{i}\in[p_{n_{i}-1},p_{n_{i}}]
. We will bound the quotient
\frac{\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|}{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}
below by a constant depending only on
C_{1},\ldots,C_{4}
and
\alpha
. We distinguish three cases:
n_{1}=n_{2}
,
n_{2}=n_{1}+1
and
n_{2}\geq n_{1}+2
. In the first case,
n_{1}=n_{2}
, we may apply the first inequality of (
a
) to deduce
\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|\geq\frac{1}{C_{1}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|
. In the second case,
n_{2}=n_{1}+1
we have, using (
a
) again, that

\frac{1}{C_{1}}\left|t_{i}-p_{n_{1}}\right|\leq\left\|\gamma(t_{i})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|,\qquad\text{for }i=1,2.

Hence
\left\|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|+\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\geq\frac{1}{C_{1}}\left(\left|t_{1}-p_{n_{1}}\right|+\left|t_{2}-p_{n_{1}}\right|\right)=\frac{1}{C_{1}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\geq\left\|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|
so that
\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\geq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|
. If
\left\|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|
we get

\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|\geq\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|-\left\|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\geq\frac{1}{4C_{1}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|.

Otherwise,
\frac{1}{4C_{1}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\leq\left\|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\leq\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|
and
\gamma(t_{1})
,
\gamma(p_{n_{1}})
and
\gamma(t_{2})
are vertices of a triangle where the two sides meeting at
\gamma(p_{n_{1}})
have lengths at least
\frac{1}{4C_{1}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|
and the angle at which they meet is at least
\alpha
, by hypothesis. This information implies a lower bound on the length of the side opposite
\gamma(p_{n_{1}})
, namely

\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|^{2}\geq 2\left\|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|(1-\cos\alpha)\geq\frac{1-\cos\alpha}{8C_{1}^{2}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2}.

In the last remaining case,
n_{2}\geq n_{1}+2
, we will obtain different bounds depending on whether we assume (
ci
) or (
cii
). First, we assume (
ci
). This provides the lower bound of
1/C_{4}
for the specified quotient when
\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|\geq C_{3}
. On the other hand, if
\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|\leq C_{3}
, we may apply (
b
) to get

\frac{\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|}{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}\geq\frac{C_{2}}{C_{3}}.

Now suppose that instead of (
ci
) we have (
cii
). We will make use of the following bounds:

\displaystyle\left|t_{1}-p_{n_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right|\leq C_{1}\left|\gamma(p_{n_{1}})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}-1})\right|=C_{1}C_{3},\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\left|t_{2}-p_{n_{2}-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{3}\quad\text{(similarly).}

We distinguish two cases:
If
\left|p_{n_{1}}-p_{n_{2}-1}\right|\geq 8C_{1}C_{3}
, then
\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|\geq 8C_{1}C_{3}
and we deduce

\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|\geq\left\|\gamma(p_{n_{2}-1})-\gamma(p_{n_{1}})\right\|-2C_{3}\geq\frac{\left|p_{n_{2}-1}-p_{n_{1}}\right|}{C_{1}}-2C_{3}\\
\geq\frac{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}{C_{1}}-4C_{3}\geq\frac{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}{2C_{1}}.

If
\left|p_{n_{1}}-p_{n_{2}-1}\right|<8C_{1}C_{3}
, then
\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|\leq 10C_{1}C_{3}
and, from (
b
), we get

\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|\geq C_{2}\geq\frac{C_{2}}{10C_{1}C_{3}}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|.

It remains to note that all the lower bounds we obtained for
\frac{\left\|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{1})\right\|}{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}
over all cases are at least
\left(\max\left\{\frac{4C_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\cos\alpha}},C_{4},\frac{C_{3}}{C_{2}}\right\}\right)^{-1}
, assuming (
ci
), and at least,
\left(\max\left\{\frac{4C_{1}}{\sqrt{1-\cos\alpha}},\frac{10C_{1}C_{3}}{C_{2}}\right\}\right)^{-1}
, assuming (
cii
) instead of (
ci
).
∎

Extensions.

Kovalev’s bilipschitz extension results
[
10
,
11
]
, from the circle to the disc and from the line to the plane, will have an important role in the present paper. Of course there is no meaningful difference between bilipschitz extension from the circle and from the square; the next lemma is a bridge between them.

Lemma 3
.

There is a
10
-bilipschitz bijection between
\overline{B}(0,1)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
[0,1]^{2}
.

Proof.

We will show that the mapping
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by
\Phi(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\left\|x\right\|}{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}}x&\text{if }x\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }x=0\end{cases}
is
2(1+\sqrt{2})
-bilipschitz. Since
\Phi|_{\overline{B}(0,1)}
is a bijection from
\overline{B}(0,1)
to
[-1,1]^{2}
and the latter set is clearly equivalent via a
2
-bilipschitz bijection to
[0,1]^{2}
, the result then follows.

We first show that
\Phi
is
2(1+\sqrt{2})
-Lipschitz. Since
\Phi
is continuous, it suffices to derive, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, the following

\left\|\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)\right\|=\left\|\frac{\left\|y\right\|}{\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}}(y-x)+x\left\|y\right\|\left(\frac{1}{\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}}\right)+\frac{x}{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}}\left(\left\|y\right\|-\left\|x\right\|\right)\right\|\\
\leq\frac{\left\|y\right\|}{\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}}\left\|y-x\right\|+\frac{\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|}{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}}\left|\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}-\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}\right|+\frac{\left\|x\right\|}{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}}\left|\left\|y\right\|-\left\|x\right\|\right|\\
\leq\left(\sqrt{2}+2+\sqrt{2}\right)\left\|y-x\right\|.

Since
\Phi^{-1}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}}{\left\|x\right\|}x&\text{if }x\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }x=0\end{cases}
, a symmetric argument, interchanging the norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
, shows that
\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi^{-1})\leq 2\sqrt{2}+1
.
∎

It will be convenient for us to work with the next restatement of Kovalev’s bilipschitz extension
[
11
, Thm. 1.1]
, formulated for the square rather than the circle using Lemma
3
.

Theorem 2.1

(Kovalev
[
11
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let
f\colon\partial[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an
L
-bilipschitz mapping. Then there is a
2\cdot 10^{16}L
-bilipschitz mapping
F\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
extending
f
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\colon\overline{B}(0,1)\to[0,1]^{2}
be a
10
-bilipschitz bijection given by Lemma
3
. Then,
[
11
, Thm 1.1]
provides a
2\cdot 10^{14}\cdot 10L
-bilipschitz extension
G\colon\overline{B}(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the
10L
-bilipschitz mapping
f\circ\Phi|_{S^{1}}
. The desired extension of
f
is now given by
G\circ\Phi^{-1}
.
∎

Similarly, we formulate Kovalev’s bilipschitz extension from the line to the plane in the form most convenient for our use. We also add some extra details about connected components to the statement.

Theorem 2.2

(Kovalev
[
10
, Thm 1.2]
)
.

Let
L\geq 1
,
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an
L
-bilipschitz mapping and
W_{-}
,
W_{+}
denote the two connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\xi(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a
2000L
-bilipschitz mapping
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Phi\circ\xi(t)=(t,0)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Phi\left(W_{-}\right)=\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,0)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi\left(W_{+}\right)=\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty).

Proof.

By
[
10
, Thm 1.2]
there exists a
2000L
-bilipschitz mapping
\Delta\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Delta(t,0)=\xi(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\Delta^{-1}\circ\xi(t)=(t,0)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, as a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\Delta^{-1}
maps the two connected components
W_{-}
and
W_{+}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\xi(\mathbb{R})
onto the two connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta^{-1}\circ\xi(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0\right\}\right)
. Accordingly we have that
\Delta^{-1}(W_{+})
is either equal to
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
or to
\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,0)
. In the former case we take
\Phi=\Delta^{-1}
and in the latter case we take
\Phi=R_{0}\circ\Delta^{-1}
where

denotes the reflection in the line
\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0\right\}
.
∎

Gluing of maps.

We will often build a mapping on a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
from two or more mappings defined on subsets of
A
. Assuming the latter are Lipschitz or bilipschitz, we usually want then to provide guarantees of the same type for the new mapping.

We refer to the operation of putting several mappings together as
gluing
. We introduce this terminology formally below together with a special notation for it:

Definition 2.3

(Gluing of mappings.)
.

Given two mappings
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2},i\in\left\{1,2\right\},
we say that
f_{1}\cup f_{2}
is
well-defined
if
f_{1}|_{A_{1}\cap A_{2}}\equiv f_{2}|_{A_{1}\cap A_{2}}
.
In this case
f_{1}\cup f_{2}
denotes the mapping

f_{1}\cup f_{2}\colon A_{1}\cup A_{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

defined as
(f_{1}\cup f_{2})(x):=f_{i}(x)
whenever
x\in A_{i}
for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
.

More generally, if
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
is a collection of mappings
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2},i\in\mathcal{I}
, such that for every
i,j\in\mathcal{I},i\neq j
the mapping
f_{i}\cup f_{j}
is well-defined, then the gluing operation yields also a well-defined mapping

\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}f_{i}\colon\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}A_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

setting
\left(\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}f_{i}\right)(x):=f_{i}(x)
whenever
x\in A_{i}
for
i\in\mathcal{I}
.

The following easy observation about gluing of Lipschitz mappings is a restatement of
[
12
, Lem. 2.22]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, be two
L
-Lipschitz mappings such that
f_{1}\cup f_{2}
is well-defined. If there is
C>0
such that for every
x\in A_{1}\setminus A_{2},y\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}
there is
z(x,y)\in A_{1}\cap A_{2}
satisfying
C\left\|x-y\right\|\geq\left\|x-z(x,y)\right\|+\left\|y-z(x,y)\right\|
, then
f_{1}\cup f_{2}
is
CL
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

For each
x\in A_{1}\setminus A_{2}
and
y\in A_{2}\setminus A_{1}

\displaystyle\left\|(f_{1}\cup f_{2})(x)-(f_{1}\cup f_{2})(y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(z(x,y))+f_{2}(z(x,y))-f_{2}(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|x-z(x,y)\right\|+L\left\|z(x,y)-y\right\|\leq CL\left\|x-y\right\|.\qed

We will use the following consequence of Lemma
4
to glue bilipschitz mappings in a situation where there is an obvious choice of
z(\cdot,\cdot)
making
C=1
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, be two
L
-bilipschitz mappings such that
f_{1}\cup f_{2}
is well-defined. If
\partial A_{1}\subseteq A_{1}\cap A_{2}
and
f_{1}(A_{1})\cap f_{2}(A_{2})=f_{1}(A_{1}\cap A_{2})
, then

3
First approximation of the shore.

To place the present section in context, recall that the overarching aim of the paper is to construct, for a given bilipschitz mapping
f\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a bilipschitz extension
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The objective of this section is to construct what may be thought of as a first approximation of the extension on a horizontal line, more precisely, of the mapping
F|_{\mathbb{R}\times\left\{i+\frac{1}{2}\right\}}
, for some fixed integer
i
. The latter can be thought of as a bilipschitz curve loosely following the points
f(x,i)
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}
in the correct order. To get such a curve, we may start with a Lipschitz curve
\gamma
going from
f(x,i)
to
f(x,i+1)
along straight line segments and then modify it in steps. In the present section, we show that such a Lipschitz curve
\gamma
admits a relatively close by bilipschitz curve, which will later be modified further to become
F|_{\mathbb{R}\times\left\{i+\frac{1}{2}\right\}}
. The title of the present section comes from our imagination of the set
f(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq i+1})
as a sea and the curve
F|_{\mathbb{R}\times\left\{i+\frac{1}{2}\right\}}
as defining its coastline.

is attained at

, making

and

(and

and

\displaystyle\qquad Y:=\Upsilon\circ f(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})\cap\left(\mathbb{R}\times[-R,R]\right).

We obtain a bilipschitz mapping
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{bilip}(\gamma)\leq\frac{1200\cdot 2R}{\frac{2}{10^{7}L^{2}}}=2400\cdot 10^{14}L^{10}\leq\frac{1}{4}10^{18}L^{10},

\displaystyle\left|\gamma_{1}(t)-t\right|\leq\frac{s}{4}=\frac{1}{2\cdot 10^{7}L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\gamma_{2}(t)\right|\leq R=2\cdot 10^{7}L^{8}\quad\text{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$,}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\dist(\Upsilon\circ f(x),\gamma(\mathbb{R}))\geq\frac{2s}{100}=\frac{4}{10^{9}L^{2}}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

The last stated property for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
comes from the conclusion of Theorem
1
, if
\Upsilon\circ f(x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\left[-R,R\right]
, and from (
34
), otherwise. Moreover, the mapping
\gamma
we get from Theorem
1
has one further property: letting
W_{+}
and
W_{-}
denote the connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\gamma(\mathbb{R})
containing
\mathbb{R}\times(R,\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,-R)
respectively, we have

\Upsilon\circ f(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})\cap\left(\mathbb{R}\times[-R,R]\right)\subseteq W_{-}\quad\text{and}\quad\Upsilon\circ f(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})\cap\left(\mathbb{R}\times[-R,R]\right)\subseteq W_{+}.

(35)

Define
F\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cup\left(\mathbb{R}\times\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

F(x)=f(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad F(t,1/2)=\Upsilon^{-1}\circ\gamma(t)\quad\text{whenever }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Combining (
35
), (
32
) and (
33
), we deduce that
f(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})\subseteq\Upsilon^{-1}(W_{-})
and
f(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})\subseteq\Upsilon^{-1}(W_{+})
.
Finally, the inequalities

\displaystyle\operatorname{bilip}(\Upsilon^{-1}\circ\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{2}\cdot 10^{5}L\cdot\frac{1}{4}10^{18}L^{10}\leq 10^{24}L^{11}=:M,

\displaystyle\dist(f(x),\Upsilon^{-1}\circ\gamma(\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\operatorname{bilip}(\Upsilon)}\dist\left(\Upsilon\circ f(x),\gamma(\mathbb{R})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4^{2}\cdot 10^{5}L}\cdot\frac{4}{10^{9}L^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{10^{15}L^{3}}=:\theta\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$,}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\left\|\Upsilon^{-1}\circ\gamma(i)-f(i,0)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{bilip}(\Upsilon)\left\|\gamma(i)-\Upsilon\circ\xi(i)\right\|+\left\|\xi(i)-f(i,0)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{bilip}(\Upsilon)\left(\left|\gamma_{1}(i)-i\right|+\left|\gamma_{2}(i)\right|\right)+\left\|\xi(i)-f(i,0)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 4^{2}\cdot 10^{5}L\cdot\left(1+2\cdot 10^{7}L^{8}\right)+8L^{5}\leq 10^{14}L^{9}=:K\quad\text{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$,}

allow us to apply Lemma

. We also get
\operatorname{bilip}\left(F|_{\mathbb{R}\times\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}}\right)=\operatorname{bilip}(\Upsilon^{-1}\circ\gamma)\leq 10^{24}L^{11}

∎

6
Bilipschitz mappings on a strip.

The aim of the present section is to build upon Theorem
2.1
to show that bilipschitz mappings can be extended from a boundary of a strip to the whole strip in the plane:

\restateexttostrip

As a preparation for the proof of Theorem
1
, we note that while Theorem
2.1
is stated for mappings on the unit square, we can readily extend it to any domain of the form
T([0,1]^{2})
, where
T
is a bilipschitz mapping
[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, by composing the relevant mappings with
T
or
T^{-1}
. For convenience, we state it formally.

Proposition 10
.

Let
K,L\geq 1
and
T\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f\colon\partial\left(T\left([0,1]^{2}\right)\right)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be
K
-bilipschitz and
L
-bilipschitz mappings respectively. Then there is a
2\cdot 10^{16}K^{2}L
-bilipschitz mapping
F\colon T\left([0,1]^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
extending
f
.

Proof.

We define
g(x):=f(T(x))
, which is a
KL
-bilipschitz mapping
\partial[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By Theorem
2.1
we get its
2\cdot 10^{16}KL
-bilipschitz extension
G
to
[0,1]^{2}
. Setting
F(x):=G(T^{-1}(x))
we obtain the desired extension of
f
.
∎

We also recall the gluing operation from Definition
2.3
. Lemma
4
about gluing of Lipschitz mappings will be used frequently, and thus, in the present section the letter
z
always refers to
z(\cdot,\cdot)
from its statement.

While it is clear that every trapezium is a bilipschitz image of
[0,1]^{2}
, the purpose of the following lemma is to record an explicit bound on the bilipschitz constant of such a bijection.

Lemma 11
.

Let
P
be a trapezium of height
h
, with bases of length
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, with
b_{1}\leq b_{2}
,
and legs of lengths
l_{1},l_{2}
, where
l_{1}\leq l_{2}
.
Then there is a bilipschitz bijection
T\colon[0,1]^{2}\to P
with

\operatorname{Lip}(T)\leq b_{2}+l_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Lip}\left(T^{-1}\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{b_{2}^{2}+\frac{3l_{2}^{2}}{2}}}{b_{1}h}.

Proof.

Up to a rotation, we may assume that the bases of
P
are horizontal.
The mapping
T
is then defined to map the vertices of
[0,1]^{2}
onto the vertices of
P
in the natural order (i.e., the left bottom vertex of
[0,1]^{2}
is mapped onto the left bottom vertex of
P
, and so on). We split
[0,1]^{2}
into two triangles
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
using one of the diagonals of
[0,1]^{2}
. Now we define
T
as the unique affine extension of
T
to each of these two triangles. We denote the length of the common side of
T(\Delta_{1})
and
T(\Delta_{2})
by
d
. Note that this side is a diagonal of
P
. The indices are chosen in such a way that
T(\Delta_{1})
has sides of lengths
b_{1},l_{1},d
.

It is enough to bound the bilipschitz constants of
T|_{\Delta_{1}}
and
T|_{\Delta_{2}}
separately, since these mappings and their inverses satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
4
with
C=1
; to see this, we just note that for every
x_{i}\in\Delta_{i}
and
y_{i}\in T(\Delta_{i}),i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, it suffices to take
z(x_{1},x_{2})
as the intersection of
[x_{1},x_{2}]
with the common side of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, and similarly,
z(y_{1},y_{2})
as the intersection of
[y_{1},y_{2}]
with the common side of
T(\Delta_{1})
and
T(\Delta_{2})
. We estimate the bilipschitz constant of
T
on
\Delta_{2}
; the other case is the same. Up to a reflection, we may assume that the chosen diagonal goes from the top left vertex of
[0,1]^{2}
to the bottom right vertex and that
\Delta_{2}
is beneath it. Up to a translation, we may also assume that
T(0,0)=(0,0)
and
T(1,0)=(b_{2},0)
. Moreover, we write
T(0,1)
as
(a_{1}b_{2},a_{2}b_{2})
, with appropriately chosen
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, we can view
T
as a composition of a scaling by
b_{2}
and a linear mapping
\widetilde{T}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a_{1}\\
0&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. The defining relations for
a_{1},a_{2}
are
a_{2}=\frac{h}{b_{2}}>0
and
a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}=\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{b_{2}^{2}}
, as
h
is the height of
T(\Delta_{2})
and
l_{2}
the length of
T(0,1)
. It remains to bound
\left\|\widetilde{T}\right\|_{\text{op}}
and
\left\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\text{op}}
.

We fix
x,y
such that
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
and bound
\left\|\widetilde{T}(x,y)\right\|
from above:

\left\|\widetilde{T}(x,y)\right\|^{2}=(x+a_{1}y)^{2}+a_{2}^{2}y^{2}=x^{2}+2a_{1}xy+(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})y^{2}\leq 1+\left|a_{1}\right|+\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{b_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{b_{2}^{2}+b_{2}l_{2}+l_{2}^{2}}{b_{2}^{2}},

where we used that
2xy\leq x^{2}+y^{2}=1
, that
x^{2},y^{2}\leq 1
and also that
a_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{l_{2}^{2}}{b_{2}^{2}}
. We conclude that
\operatorname{Lip}\left(T|_{\Delta_{2}}\right)=b_{2}\left\|\widetilde{T}\right\|_{\text{op}}\leq\sqrt{b_{2}^{2}+b_{2}l_{2}+l_{2}^{2}}<b_{2}+l_{2}
.

By a direct calculation, we see that
\widetilde{T}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\\
0&\frac{1}{a_{2}}\end{pmatrix}
. The same way as above, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{-1}(x,y)\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{a_{2}^{2}}\left((a_{2}x-a_{1}y)^{2}+y^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{2}^{2}+\left|a_{1}a_{2}\right|+a_{1}^{2}+1}{a_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\frac{3}{2}(a_{2}^{2}+a_{1}^{2})}{a_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{b_{2}^{2}+\frac{3l_{2}^{2}}{2}}{h^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain that
\operatorname{Lip}\left(T^{-1}|_{T(\Delta_{2})}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{b_{2}^{2}+\frac{3l_{2}^{2}}{2}}{b_{2}^{2}h^{2}}}
.

By an analogous computation for
\Delta_{1}
, we get that
\operatorname{Lip}(T|_{\Delta_{1}})\leq b_{1}+l_{1}
and
\operatorname{Lip}\left(T^{-1}|_{T(\Delta_{1})}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{b_{1}^{2}+\frac{3l_{1}^{2}}{2}}{b_{1}^{2}h^{2}}}
. Clearly, the bounds for
\operatorname{Lip}(T)
and
\operatorname{Lip}(T^{-1})
in the statement of the lemma are upper bounds for
\operatorname{Lip}\left(T|_{\Delta_{i}}\right)
and
\operatorname{Lip}\left(T^{-1}|_{T(\Delta_{i})}\right)
,
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, respectively.
∎

Let
a,w,{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
a\neq{p}
and
w\neq{p}
. We denote by
\gamma(w,{p},a)
the size of the angle at
{p}
in the (possibly degenerate) triangle
a{p}w
. Obviously,
\gamma
is a continuous function. We will need a lower bound on
\gamma(w,{p},a)
on triples
a,w,{p}
such that
w,{p}
lie in a certain compact convex set
\mathcal{K}
and
a\notin\inter\mathcal{K}
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a compact convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
w,{p}\in\mathcal{K},w\neq{p}
such that the open line segment
(w,{p})
lies in
\inter\mathcal{K}
. Then
\gamma(w,{p},\cdot)
constrained to the set

A_{w{p}}:=\left\{a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left(\inter\mathcal{K}\cup\left\{{p}\right\}\right)\colon\left\|a-{p}\right\|\leq\left\|a-w\right\|\right\}

attains its minimum at an intersection of
\partial\mathcal{K}
with the bisector of
[w,{p}]
.

Proof.

By the convexity of
\mathcal{K}
and the assumptions on
w,{p}
, the bisector of
[w,{p}]
intersects
\partial\mathcal{K}
at exactly two points
a_{1},a_{2}
. As
a_{1},a_{2}\neq{p}
, we see that
a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{w{p}}
. Now we take
a\in A_{w{p}}
and assume for contradiction that

\gamma(w,{p},a)<\min\left\{\gamma(w,{p},a_{1}),\gamma(w,{p},a_{2})\right\}.

(36)

This means that
a
lies in the interior of the convex cone containing
w
and bounded by the lines
a_{1}{p}
,
a_{2}{p}
; this cone together with the bisector of
[w,{p}]
defines a triangle
T
. By our assumptions,
a\in T
. On the other hand,
T\subset\mathcal{K}
by the convexity of
\mathcal{K}
. As
a\in T\cap A_{w{p}}\subseteq\partial T
and since
(a_{1},a_{2})\cap A_{w{p}}=\emptyset
, we have
a\in[a_{1},{p}]\cup[a_{2},{p}]
, which contradicts (
36
).
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
B
be an open disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
w,{p}\in\overline{B},w\neq{p}
. We write
B_{w{p}}
for the open disk with diameter
(w,{p})
. Let
a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus{\left(\left(B\cap B_{w{p}}\right)\cup\left\{{p}\right\}\right)}
such that
\left\|a-{p}\right\|\leq\left\|a-w\right\|
and
\gamma(w,{p},a)\leq\pi/2
. Then

\sin\gamma(w,{p},a)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\frac{\left\|w-{p}\right\|}{\diam B}.

Proof.

Let
A_{w{p}}
be the set from Lemma
12
applied to
w,{p}
and
\mathcal{K}=\overline{B}\cap\overline{B}_{w{p}}
and let
a_{w{p}}\in A_{w{p}}
be the minimiser provided there. Note that, by the assumptions,
a\in A_{w{p}}
as well, and thus,
\gamma(w,{p},a)\geq\gamma(w,{p},a_{w{p}})
. Therefore, since
\gamma(w,{p},a)\leq\pi/2
, to lower bound
\sin\gamma(w,{p},a)
, we only need to bound
\gamma(w,{p},a_{w{p}})
below.

If
a_{w{p}}\notin\partial B
, then by Lemma
12
,
a_{w{p}}\in\partial B_{w{p}}
and
\gamma(w,{p},a)\geq\pi/4=\gamma(w,{p},a_{w{p}})
. Thus,

\sin\gamma(w,{p},a)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}>\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\frac{\left\|w-{p}\right\|}{\diam B}.

We can now assume that
a_{w{p}}\in\partial B
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
B
is centred at
\mathbf{0}
. Let
b
stand for the midpoint of
[w,{p}]
,
d:=\left\|w-{p}\right\|
and
r:=\diam B/2
. By the reverse triangle inequality, we see that
\left\|b-a_{w{p}}\right\|\geq r-\left\|b\right\|\geq r-\sqrt{r^{2}-(d/2)^{2}}
.

On the other hand,

\sin\gamma(w,{p},a_{w{p}})=\frac{\left\|b-a_{w{p}}\right\|}{\left\|{p}-a_{w{p}}\right\|}=\frac{\left\|b-a_{w{p}}\right\|}{\sqrt{\left\|b-a_{w{p}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{2}}}.

Since the function
t\mapsto\frac{t}{\sqrt{t^{2}+\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{2}}}
is monotone increasing on
(0,\infty)
, we may apply the lower bound
\left\|b-a_{w{p}}\right\|\geq r-\sqrt{r^{2}-(d/2)^{2}}
to get

\displaystyle\sin\gamma(w,{p},a_{w{p}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r-\sqrt{r^{2}-\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{2}}}{\sqrt{\left(r-\sqrt{r^{2}-\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{2}}}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right)^{2}}}{\sqrt{\left(1-\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right)^{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\left(1-\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right)^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right)^{2}}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\frac{d}{2r},

where we used that for every
\zeta\in[0,1]
it holds that
1-\sqrt{\zeta}\leq\sqrt{1-\zeta}\leq 1-\frac{\zeta}{2}
.
∎

Now we are ready for the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

First we argue that we may assume
h=1
: Suppose the theorem holds if
h=1
and we are given
L\geq 1
,
h>0
and an
L
-bilipschitz mapping
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0,h\right\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then let
g\colon\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0,1\right\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be defined by
g(x)=\frac{1}{h}f(hx)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0,1\right\}
. The version of the theorem for
h=1
provides a
10^{19}L^{7}
-bilipschitz extension
G\colon\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0,1\right\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
g
. Then the mapping
F\colon\left\{0,h\right\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by
F(x)=hG\left(\frac{x}{h}\right)
is a bilipschitz extension of
f
, verifying Theorem
1
.

We may now assume that
h=1
and fix a polynomial
q(L)
, whose precise value will be specified later. We also write
\mathfrak{L}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\times\left\{0\right\}
and
\mathfrak{L_{1}}:=\mathbb{R}\times\left\{1\right\}
. We start by selecting a sequence
\left(\bar{x}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathfrak{L}_{0}
so that the points are increasing in their first coordinate and with distance exactly
q(L)
between consecutive points. Our aim now is to come up with two sequences
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathfrak{L}_{0}
and
\left(y_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathfrak{L}_{1}
such that the segments
[x_{k},y_{k}]
are pairwise non-crossing, and moreover, so that we can extend
f
to each of these segments in a bilipschitz way simply by interpolating from
f(x_{k})
to
f(y_{k})
linearly, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

To this end, we first define
B_{k}:=B(f(\bar{x}_{k}),L)
. We want to make sure that these disks are pairwise disjoint, so we require that

q(L)>2L^{2}.

(37)

Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and observe that
\overline{B}_{k}
intersects
f(\mathfrak{L}_{1})
. By compactness, there are two points
x_{k}\in\mathfrak{L}_{0}
and
y_{k}\in\mathfrak{L}_{1}
such that
f(x_{k}),f(y_{k})\in\overline{B}_{k}
and

\left\|f(x_{k})-f(y_{k})\right\|=\dist(f(\mathfrak{L}_{0})\cap\overline{B}_{k},f(\mathfrak{L}_{1})\cap\overline{B}_{k})\leq L.

(38)

As a consequence,
[f(x_{k}),f(y_{k})]
meets
\img(f)
only at the endpoints.
This allows us to define an injective mapping
F_{k}\colon\mathfrak{L}_{0}\cup\mathfrak{L}_{1}\cup[x_{k},y_{k}]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
extending
f
by interpolating linearly between
f(x_{k})
and
f(y_{k})
along
[x_{k},y_{k}]
.
For a later use, we also record that

\left\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\right\|,\left\|y_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\right\|,\left\|x_{k}-y_{k}\right\|\leq L^{2}.

(39)

Claim 13.1
.

The mapping
F_{k}
satisfies
\operatorname{Lip}(F_{k})\leq 2L^{3}
and
\operatorname{Lip}(F_{k}^{-1})\leq 12L^{3}
.

Proof.

The points
x_{k},y_{k}
lie on two parallel lines
\mathfrak{L}_{0},\mathfrak{L}_{1}
with distance
1
. Therefore, letting
\alpha_{k}
denote the smallest of the angles between
[x_{k},y_{k}]
and
\mathfrak{L}_{0}
(or
\mathfrak{L}_{1}
), by (
39
) we have

\sin\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{\left\|x_{k}-y_{k}\right\|}\geq\frac{1}{L^{2}}.

(40)

The mapping
F_{k}|_{[x_{k},y_{k}]}
is affine and thus its bilipschitz constants are realised on the pair
x_{k},y_{k}
, where
F_{k}
is equal to
f
. Because
f
is
L
-bilipschitz, we have that
F_{k}|_{[x_{k},y_{k}]}
is
L
-bilipschitz as well.

Since we know that
F_{k}|_{\mathfrak{L}_{0}\cup\mathfrak{L}_{1}}
is
L
-bilipschitz, it is enough to bound the bilipschitz constants of
F_{k}|_{\mathfrak{L}_{i}\cup[x_{k},y_{k}]}
,
i\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, separately. We discuss only the case
i=0
, as the other one is the same.

We again use Lemma
4
. To this end, given
x\in\mathfrak{L}_{0}
and
y\in[x_{k},y_{k}]
, we set
z(x,y):=x_{k}
. By the sine theorem and (
40
), we get that

\left\|x-z(x,y)\right\|+\left\|y-z(x,y)\right\|\leq\frac{2\left\|x-y\right\|}{\sin\alpha_{k}}\leq 2L^{2}\left\|x-y\right\|,

(41)

which proves the first part of the claim.

Now we are given
a\in f(\mathfrak{L}_{0})
and
b\in[f(x_{k}),f(y_{k})]
. We can assume that
b\neq f(x_{k}),f(y_{k})
. We set
z(a,b):=f(x_{k})
if
\left\|a-f(x_{k})\right\|\leq\left\|a-f(y_{k})\right\|
; in the opposite case, we set
z(a,b):=f(y_{k})
.

We denote the angle in the triangle
z(a,b)ab
at the vertex
z(a,b)
by
\gamma_{z}
. If
\gamma_{z}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
\left\|a-b\right\|
is the longest side of the triangle, and hence,
\left\|a-z(a,b)\right\|+\left\|b-z(a,b)\right\|\leq 2\left\|a-b\right\|
.

When
\gamma_{z}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, we set
z:=z(a,b)=f(x_{k})
and
w:=f(y_{k})
(or the other way around) and note that
\gamma_{z}
is also the angle at
z(a,b)
in the triangle
af(x_{k})f(y_{k})
. Moreover, we observe that
a\notin B_{k}\cap B_{wz}
, as otherwise
a
would be a point of
f(\mathfrak{L}_{0})\cap B_{k}
closer to
f(y_{k})\in f(\mathfrak{L}_{1})\cap\overline{B}_{k}
than
f(x_{k})
, contradicting the definition of
x_{k}
and
y_{k}
; see (
38
). Thus, we can apply Lemma
13
to
a,w,p=z
and
B=B_{k}
and obtain that

\sin\gamma_{z}=\sin\gamma(w,z,a)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\frac{\left\|f(x_{k})-f(y_{k})\right\|}{\diam B_{k}}.

(42)

Now we use again the sine theorem and deduce
\frac{2\left\|a-b\right\|}{\sin\gamma_{z}}\geq\left\|a-z(a,b)\right\|+\left\|b-z(a,b)\right\|
. By the bilipschitz property of
f
,
\frac{\left\|f(x_{k})-f(y_{k})\right\|}{\diam B_{k}}\geq\frac{1/L}{2L}=1/2L^{2}
. Combining it with (
42
), we have shown that given any
a\in f(\mathfrak{L}_{0})
and
b\in[f(x_{k}),f(y_{k})]
,

\left\|a-z(a,b)\right\|+\left\|b-z(a,b)\right\|\leq 8\sqrt{2}L^{2}\left\|a-b\right\|\leq 12L^{2}\left\|a-b\right\|.

By Lemma
4
, we conclude that
\operatorname{Lip}(F_{k}^{-1})\leq 12L^{3}
.
∎

By definition,
\left\|\overline{x}_{k}-\overline{x}_{k+1}\right\|=q(L)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and thus, by (
37
) and (

), the relative order of both
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\left(y_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
along
\mathfrak{L}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{1}

\left(\bar{x}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, the segments
[x_{k},y_{k}]
are pairwise non-crossing.

Next, we would like to use Lemma
4
to show that the mappings
\left(F_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
combine to give a bilipschitz mapping.

Claim 13.2
.

Let
G\colon\mathfrak{L}_{0}\cup\mathfrak{L}_{1}\cup\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[x_{k},y_{k}]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the mapping extending
F_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
\operatorname{Lip}(G)\leq 4L^{3}
and
\operatorname{Lip}(G^{-1})\leq 24L^{3}
.

Proof.

We note that
F_{k}\cup F_{l}
and
F_{k}^{-1}\cup F_{l}^{-1}
are well-defined according to Definition
2.3
for every
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. As
G
is
L^{\prime}
-bilipschitz if and only if each
F_{k}\cup F_{l}
is
L^{\prime}
-bilipschitz for every
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, it is enough to argue that there is
C>0
such that for every
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
the mappings
F_{k},F_{l}
as well as the mappings
F^{-1}_{k},F^{-1}_{l}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
4
with the constant
C
.
In fact, to obtain the claimed Lipschitz constants, in light of Claim
13.1
it suffices to show that we can choose
C=2
. For this, we additionally assume

q(L)\geq 4L^{2}.

(43)

We fix
k<l,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p_{k}\in[x_{k},y_{k}]
and
p_{l}\in[x_{l},y_{l}]
. We set
z(p_{k},p_{l}):=\bar{x}_{k}
and argue that this choice satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4
for
F_{k}
and
F_{l}
.

By the triangle inequality, we can write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|p_{k}-z(p_{k},p_{l})\right\|+\left\|p_{l}-z(p_{k},p_{l})\right\|&\leq\left\|p_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\right\|+\left\|p_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\right\|+\left\|p_{k}-p_{l}\right\|.\end{split}

(44)

On the other hand, by (
39
), (
43
) and the definition of
\left(\bar{x}_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
(applied in this order), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\left\|p_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\right\|\leq 2L^{2}\leq q(L)-2L^{2}\leq\left\|p_{k}-p_{l}\right\|.\end{split}

(45)

Combining (
44
) and (
45
), we directly obtain that

\left\|p_{k}-z(p_{k},p_{l})\right\|+\left\|p_{l}-z(p_{k},p_{l})\right\|\leq 2\left\|p_{k}-p_{l}\right\|.

For
F_{k}^{-1}
and

to show that the assumptions of Lemma

are satisfied with

. Thus, we write only the relevant inequalities without further justification:

\left\|f(p_{k})-z\right\|+\left\|f(p_{l})-z\right\|\leq\left\|f(p_{k})-f(\bar{x}_{k})\right\|+\left\|f(p_{k})-f(\bar{x}_{k})\right\|+\left\|f(p_{k})-f(p_{l})\right\|.

Instead of (
39

) and the bilipschitz property of

:

2\left\|f(p_{k})-f(\bar{x}_{k})\right\|\leq 2L\leq\frac{q(L)}{L}-2L\leq\left\|f(p_{k})-f(p_{l})\right\|.

Altogether, we get that

\displaystyle\left\|f(p_{k})-z\right\|+\left\|f(p_{l})-z\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|f(p_{k})-f(p_{l})\right\|.\qed

We summarise where we currently stand. For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have a trapezium
P_{k}
with vertices

and

Claim 13.3
.

For every

,
there is a bilipschitz bijection

with
\operatorname{bilip}(T_{k})\leq\frac{7q(L)}{4}

Proof.

We fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}

11
, we call by
b_{1}\leq b_{2}
the lengths of the bases of
P_{k}
and by
l_{1}\leq l_{2}
the lengths of the legs of
P_{k}

. By the definition of
\left(\bar{x}_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

q(L)-2L^{2}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq q(L)+2L^{2}

and the legs of
P_{k}
satisfy

By Lemma
11
there is a bilipschitz bijection
T_{k}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to P_{k}
satisfying some explicitly stated bounds on
\operatorname{Lip}(T_{k})
and
\operatorname{Lip}(T_{k}^{-1})
in terms of the quantities

. From the bound on
\operatorname{Lip}(T_{k})

\operatorname{Lip}(T_{k})\leq q(L)+2L^{2}+L^{2}\leq\frac{7q(L)}{4}.

Next we show that
\operatorname{Lip}(T_{k}^{-1})\leq 4
. This will complete the proof, since
\frac{7q(L)}{4}\geq 7L^{2}\geq 7
by (
43
). In view of the bound on
\operatorname{Lip}(T_{k}^{-1})
from Lemma
11
, it suffices to argue that

4b_{1}\geq\sqrt{b_{2}^{2}+\frac{3l_{2}^{2}}{2}}.

By the upper bounds on

and

∎

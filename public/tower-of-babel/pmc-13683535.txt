Efficacy and safety of a novel glaucoma drainage tube with a porous outer structure (GDTI-50H) in rabbit eyes
Authors: Wanting Wu, Suping Yu, Nanjin Li, Shan Liang, Jinchuan Zhao, Xiaoyu Wang, Junhua Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We developed a novel glaucoma drainage tube, designated as GDTI-50H. The outer opening end of the drainage tube is provided with multiple drainage holes, which allows for normal drainage of aqueous humor through the remaining holes when one of the drainage holes becomes blocked. It is potentially enhancing the sustained drainage efficiency. Fourteen New Zealand white rabbits were randomly assigned to receive GDTI-50H implantation in either the right or left eye as the test eye, with the contralateral eye serving as its own control (undergoing sham surgery). Intraocular pressure(IOP) was continuously monitored within 12 weeks postoperatively, combined with slit-lamp microscopy, anterior segment optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging, Trypan Blue patency test, and histopathological examination. Results showed that IOP in the test group was significantly lower than that in the control group at 12 weeks postoperatively (all differences were statistically significant, p < 0.05). Anterior segment OCT showed that drainage tube was accurately positioned within the anterior chamber angle without displacement or detachment. The Trypan Blue patency test demonstrated that at 12 weeks postoperatively, Trypan Blue dye could still drain from the anterior chamber into the subconjunctival space in the test group. Hematoxylin and Eosin staining images showed no significant inflammatory cell infiltration around drainage tube. This study demonstrated that after implantation of drainage tube into the anterior chamber of rabbit eyes, and it has certain efficacy and safety in controlling IOP.

Introduction

Glaucoma is the leading irreversible cause of blindness worldwide, and the resulting optic nerve damage and visual field defect are irreversible [1]. In 2020, there were already 76 million glaucoma patients globally, and this number is projected to increase to 111.8 million by 2040 (among individuals aged 40–80 years), with Asia and Africa accounting for a significant proportion [2]. In 2020, the number of patients with glaucoma in China reached 21 million, among whom the number of blind cases was 8.6 million [3–5].

The primary approach to glaucoma treatment is the reduction of intraocular pressure (IOP) [6], which is mainly achieved through medication, laser, and surgery. According to clinical studies, approximately 10–30% of glaucoma patients still require surgical treatment after medication and laser therapy [7]. Surgery, as an important method in glaucoma treatment, is continuously evolving. Drainage valve implantation surgery is the preferred procedure for refractory glaucoma filtration surgery [8]. Recently, Minimally Invasive Glaucoma Surgery (MIGS) has been rapidly developing [9, 10]. The XEN gel stent, as a type of minimally invasive glaucoma drainage tube, has been successfully implanted in over 100,000 cases worldwide, with approximately 70% occurring in Europe and the United States [11]. According to literature, the advantages of XEN gel stent include the use of biocompatible materials, implantation through a micro-incision to reduce tissue trauma, and a significant reduction in the risk of bleb infection and hypotony. It has clinical significance for patients with refractory glaucoma who respond poorly to conventional surgery or those with open-angle glaucoma inadequately controlled by medication [11–13]. However, the external opening of the currently clinically used XEN gel stent is prone to encapsulation, which affects the long-term efficiency of aqueous humor drainage.

Building upon the structural features of the XEN gel stent, we optimized the structural design of the drainage tube and successfully developed a novel glaucoma drainage tube, designated as GDTI-50H. This drainage tube features an inner diameter of 50 μm and employs state-of-the-art Micron-level Laser Drilling Technology to fabricate multiple drainage apertures on its distal end. The unique design of this drainage tube enables sustained aqueous humor drainage through the remaining apertures in the event of obstruction of the primary drainage apertures, thereby holding promise for improving the overall long-term aqueous humor drainage efficiency. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of GDTI-50H using animal experimental models.

Methods

Experimental animals

Fourteen New Zealand rabbits without specific pathogens, aged 3–4 months and weighing 2.0–3.0 kg each, were used in this study. The experimental animals were purchased from EastPoint Innovation Center Co, Ltd. This study complies with the provisions of GB/T 16886.2-2011 “Biological Evaluation of Medical Devices Part 2: Animal Welfare Requirements” and other relevant requirements for the welfare of experimental animals. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the Regulations for the Administration of Laboratory Animals issued by the National Science Council of China. The fourteen New Zealand rabbits were individually housed in standard cages (60 cm2), with labels indicating the experiment number, sex, animal number, and date of the experiment. They were fed with clean water and standard laboratory animal maintenance feed. The experimental animal certificate number is SCXK(Zhejiang)2022-0001, and the ethical number is No.2024053.Fourteen New Zealand rabbits were randomly assigned to receive GDTI-50H implantation in either the right or left eye as the test eye, with the contralateral eye serving as its own control (undergoing sham surgery, all surgical procedures identical to the test eye except without drainage tube implantation).

The device

The GDTI-50H system consists of two parts: the drainage tube and the injector. The drainage tube is composed of glutaraldehyde-crosslinked gelatin and pre-loaded into the injector. The inner diameter of GDTI-50H is 50 μm, the outer diameter is 150 μm, and the total length is 6 mm (Fig. 1A, B). This biomaterial exhibits favorable flexibility and softness following hydration, enabling smooth insertion and good tissue compatibility during minimally invasive implantation. The injector serves as a tool for implantation, as shown in Fig. 1C. The drainage tube is pre-loaded in the injector and is delivered through a 1 mm corneal incision to the target angle of the anterior chamber, where the drainage tube is punctured and implanted into the subconjunctival space. The core innovation of GDTI-50H lies in the application of micron-level laser punching technology to construct a porous structure at the outer end of the drainage tube. This design can not only improve the efficiency of aqueous humor drainage but also reduce the risk of tissue encapsulation at the outer end. When a single drainage hole is blocked due to tissue encapsulation, the remaining channels can still facilitate aqueous humor drainage, thereby effectively ensuring the stability of the drainage function. After hydration, the porcine skin gelatin material exhibits an “S”-shaped flexible conformity to the ocular surface tissue, with excellent biocompatibility, significantly reducing the risk of conjunctival erosion and drainage tube exposure, and causing no immune rejection reaction. It can form a minimally invasive permanent aqueous humor outflow channel. Surgical procedure

After general anesthesia and disinfection of the eye area of the experimental rabbits, 0.2 mg/mL Mitomycin C (MMC) 0.1 mL was injected under the conjunctiva above the nasal area. A clear corneal incision approximately 1 mm in length was made in the inferotemporal region, and viscoelastic was injected into the anterior chamber. Then removed the yellow stopper of the injector and confirmed no abnormality of the trabecular meshwork structure at the puncture site by gonioscopy. The puncture needle was inserted through the clear corneal incision, passed through the anterior chamber, and punctured into the nasal Trabecular meshwork, sequentially traversing the Schlemm’s canal, sclera, and Tenon’s capsule until reaching the subconjunctival space. At this point, the push button was pressed to advance the distal end of the GDTI-50H into the subconjunctival space. The proximal end of the drainage tube was positioned approximately 1–2 mm within the anterior chamber, the distal end 2–3 mm within the subconjunctival space, and the middle 2 mm traversed the scleral stroma. The anterior chamber was flushed with Balanced Salt Solution (BSS) to replace the viscoelastic, and allowed the filtering bleb to protrude mildly, then closed the corneal incision with sutures. The control eye underwent sham surgery in the same manner (all surgical steps were identical to the test eye, except that no implantation of the GDTI-50H drainage tube was performed). At the end of the surgery, 0.1% tobramycin dexamethasone eye ointment was applied to the eye, and the experimental rabbits were returned to the feeding cage after awakening. After surgery, all animals (including both the experimental group and the control group) received identical postoperative care: 0.1% tobramycin dexamethasone ophthalmic ointment and 0.5% levofloxacin eye drops, four times daily for 3 days, followed by once daily for 2 weeks. The medication regimen, frequency, and duration were exactly the same for both groups.

GDTI-50H implantation position determination

The position of GDTI-50H was observed four weeks and twelve weeks after the operation through the anterior ocular OCT to determine whether the position of the drainage tube is stable. The anterior OCT was used to perform a high-resolution scan perpendicular to the direction of the tube, with an axial resolution of about 10 μm, and the cornea, iris, and drainage tube structures were clearly displayed. Focus on the contact between the nozzle (the opening at the end of the drain) and the angle and iris. The position of the orifice was manually marked in the OCT image, and the distance between the orifice and the anterior chamber angle at 4 weeks and 12 weeks after surgery was observed and whether there was fibrotic wrapping in the surrounding tissues. If the position of the tube is fixed and the tube body does not touch the cornea or iris, the nozzle position is considered to be stable, and the nozzle is considered to be unobstructed if there is no tissue around the nozzle. Two experienced ophthalmologists independently label the orifice, cornea, and iris in OCT images.

The patency of the aqueous humor drainage channel test

Four weeks and 12 weeks after the GDTI-50H eye implantation, a single-use 1 mL syringe was slowly inserted at a 45° angle into the anterior chamber at the limbus, and 0.1 mL of 0.4% trypan blue was injected. Ten minutes later, the morphology of trypan blue leakage under the conjunctiva, the spread range, and the dynamic formation of filtering blebs were observed. When completely unobstructed, trypan blue will continue to and steadily overflow from the opening of the drainage tube in a linear or columnar shape, and spread to the subconjunctiva along the tissues around the drainage tube. The diffusion range under the conjunctiva is wide, and it can spread to the entire conjunctival sac in a short time. A slightly raised translucent vesicle-like structure appears under the conjunctiva, which gradually increases over time and has a clear boundary. When partially unobstructed, the dye spillover may be fine or intermittent. The diffusion speed is slow, the diffusion range is limited, only a small area around the drainage tube, the formation of the filter vesicle is slow, and it may appear 10 min or even more after injection. The shape is irregular and the size is different. When there is no patency, no dye overflows, or the dye is confined to the opening of the drainage tube, there is no obvious diffusion, and no filter vesicle is formed. Based on the results of the above observations, we define complete patency as the ability of trypan blue dye to successfully overflow from the drain opening and diffuse widely under the conjunctiva to form a clear filtration vesicle, indicating that GDTI-50H effectively drains. Partial patency is defined as poor dye overflow, thin lines or discontinuities, limited diffusion range, slow formation of filter vesicles and irregular morphology, indicating that GDTI-50H drainage is partially limited. If it is not patency, it is manifested as no dye overflow, no diffusion under the conjunctiva and the formation of filter vesicles, indicating that GDTI-50H is blocked.

Histology and immunofluorescent analysis

At 12 weeks post-surgery, the experimental rabbits were euthanized using the method of intravenous air injection. After confirming that the rabbits were completely deceased, sterile surgical instruments were used to incise along the eyelid margins, separate the surrounding tissues, and completely enucleate the eyes. The eyes were then immediately placed in pre-cooled phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for washing to remove surface bloodstains and impurities. The washed eyes were quickly placed into pre-cooled 4% paraformaldehyde fixative, with the volume of fixative being at least 5 times the volume of the eyes to ensure thorough fixation. After the fixation is completed, the fixation solution on the surface of the eyeball is first rinsed with running water, and the gradient dehydration is carried out with different concentrations of ethanol. The dehydrated sample is treated transparently. After the sample is completely transparent, it is immersed in a pre-melted paraffin solution. Finally, the wax-immersed eyeball sample is placed in a paraffin embedding mold, and an appropriate amount of molten paraffin solution is injected. After the sample is cooled and solidified, a paraffin-embedded block is formed. The paraffin-embedded block is fixed to the slicer, and slices of the cornea, iris, sclera and surrounding tissues are continuously cut. GDTI-50H. After gently flattening the slices in a water bath, pick them up with a slide and place them in a constant temperature drying oven to dry. After that, the slices were soaked in xylene twice in succession, then in different concentrations of ethanol for hydration, and finally rinsed with distilled water for H&E staining treatment.

Statistical analysis

For each rabbit, either the right eye or the left eye was randomly selected as the experimental eye (implanted with GDTI 50H), and the contralateral eye served as the control eye (sham surgery). Since the two eyes of each rabbit naturally formed a paired design, statistical analysis was performed using a paired design with each rabbit as an independent unit. IOP data at each time point are presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). Normality of the data in each group was first assessed using the Shapiro Wilk test. For data following a normal distribution, a paired t test was used to compare IOP differences between the experimental and control eyes; for data not following a normal distribution, the Wilcoxon signed rank test was applied. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The generalized estimating equation (GEE) was used to adequately account for the longitudinal correlation of IOP measurements at different time points. IOP values at postoperative time points (weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12) were taken as outcome variables, and group (experimental vs. control) was taken as the independent variable. A GEE model with an independent correlation structure was constructed to account for the longitudinal correlation of IOP measurements at different time points. Results are presented as β (95% CI) and p-value, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (IBM Corp. Version 24.0, Armonk, NY, USA).

In Vitro Biocompatibility Evaluation

According to the GB/T 16886 series of standards, a systematic in vitro biocompatibility evaluation of the GDTI-50H drainage system was conducted. The cytotoxicity test (MTT assay) showed that the relative survival rate of L‑929 cells in 100% sample extract was 79.3%, indicating no potential cytotoxicity. In the eye irritation test, after instillation of the sample extract into the conjunctival sac of New Zealand white rabbits, no abnormal eye irritation reactions were observed at any observation time point (1 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h). The eye irritation score was 0 at all time points, and the sample was determined to be non‑irritating to the eyes. In the skin sensitization test (GPMT method), the skin reaction grade in the test group was less than 1, the positive sensitization rate was 0%, the negative control group also showed 0%, and the positive control group (DNCB) showed 100%, indicating that the sample extract did not cause skin sensitization reactions in guinea pigs. In the acute systemic toxicity test, after a single injection via the tail vein (polar extract) or intraperitoneally (non‑polar extract) in mice, no abnormal clinical symptoms were observed in any animal, and body weight increased normally. The sample was determined to have no acute systemic toxicity. In the pyrogen test, after injection of the sample extract into the marginal ear vein of rabbits, the temperature rise in all three rabbits was less than 0.6 °C, and the sum of temperature rises was less than 1.3 °C, indicating no potential pyrogenic reaction(Report Nos.: SZB23100859A-R1-C2, SZB23100859B-R1-CN). In summary, the GDTI-50H drainage system exhibits good in vitro biocompatibility.

Fluid dynamics analysis

To evaluate the fluid dynamic performance of the porous structure (no more than 10 pores) at the outer end of the GDTI-50H drainage tube, finite element analysis (FEA) was used to conduct numerical simulations of fluid dynamics for the GDTI-50H. The inlet pressure was set to 20 mmHg, 40 mmHg, and 60 mmHg, simulating normal to severe ocular hypertension; the outlet pressure was set to 0 mmHg (simulating the subconjunctival space). Aqueous humor was considered a Newtonian fluid with a viscosity of 0.0007 Pa·s, and a steady-state laminar flow model was adopted.

The simulation results showed that the porous design of the GDTI-50H exhibited good drainage efficiency under both normal IOP (20 mmHg) and high IOP(40–60 mmHg) conditions. By comparing the pressure drop and pressure difference before and after reaching steady state, it was confirmed that the porous structure effectively reduces local flow resistance. These results suggest that the multiple channels distribute the aqueous humor flow to several outlets, resulting in a more uniform pressure gradient distribution at the outlet, thereby avoiding the local high‑pressure zone and vortex energy loss commonly seen in single‑hole outlets. Discussion

Ex-PRESS mini drainage device and XEN gel stent are commonly used in glaucoma drainage. The patency of external drainage is affected by factors such as blockage of internal and external openings, obstruction of the lumen, and wrapping of the external opening. When the inner and outer openings and lumen of the drainage tube are unobstructed, whether the outer opening is wrapped or not is one of the key factors to determine the success rate of surgery. Studies have shown that the incidence of early bleb encapsulation and scarring after Ex-PRESS mini-drainage is 16.7–25% [14, 15], and the reason is that the stainless steel material, due to its high hardness and rigidity, is prone to triggering local inflammatory reactions, thereby increasing the risk of scar formation [16]. The XEN gel stent is made of hydrophilic porcine gelatin material. The filtration vesicle encapsulation rate in the early postoperative period is about 10–15.3% [17–20], and the wrapping probability is slightly lower than that of Ex-PRESS micro drainage. However, it is still high. The XEN gel stent only has one drainage orifice. Once the drainage of the outer mouth is blocked, it will lead to the obstruction of aqueous humor drainage, and the IOP will increase again [12, 13]. To address the shortcomings of existing glaucoma drainage devices, the GDTI-50H drainage tube in this study employs a proprietary gelatin material with a specially designed porous structure at its outer opening. Even if a single channel becomes blocked, the remaining channels can continue to drain aqueous humor. It addresses the issues of the material-driven inflammatory mechanism of Ex-PRESS and the single-point failure mechanism of XEN, both of which lead to reduced drainage efficiency [14, 18]. This dual optimization of material properties and structural design aims to reduce postoperative encapsulation and enhance the efficiency of aqueous humor outflow.

This animal experiment systematically evaluated the effectiveness and safety of the drainage tube in reducing IOP in rabbits. A total of 14 New Zealand rabbits received unilateral implantation with drainage tubes, and IOP slit lamp assessment, anterior segment OCT, trypan blue patency test, and pathological analysis were performed after surgery. Conjunctival hyperemia, corneal opacity, and anterior chamber inflammation were not found in the surgical eye during slit lamp examination, and the lens and vitreous were transparent, which preliminarily verified the biological safety of drainage tube implantation. IOP in test eyes that were implanted with GDTI-50H was significantly lower than that in contralateral control eyes that underwent sham surgery. The IOP in test eyes was reduced by 21.44% compared to contralateral control eyes at 12 weeks after surgery. The IOP-lowering effect remained stable during the 12-week observation period, which preliminarily confirmed that the GDTI-50H drainage tube had a significant effect in reducing IOP. This result is comparable to the effect of glaucoma drainage devices on IOP reduction in similar studies. In the rabbit eye experiment by Lin Xie et al. [21] on HA-Mg drainage plates, the IOP of the experimental group decreased by 21.88% compared to the control group 21 weeks after surgery. Ruixue Yin et al. [22] monitored IOP in rabbit experiments with the diverging drainage stent (the diverging drainage stent) for 7 days post-surgery, and at the end of the experiment, the experimental group showed an approximately 19% reduction in IOP compared to baseline. Notably, both the experimental eyes and the control eyes received treatment with 0.1% tobramycin dexamethasone ophthalmic ointment and 0.5% levofloxacin eye drops for two weeks after surgery. Glucocorticoid‑induced ocular hypertension typically occurs after long‑term use (weeks to months), and the risk of inducing significant IOP elevation with short‑term use (≤ 3 weeks) is low. Therefore, the potential impact of medication on IOP is comparable between the two groups and relatively minor, and the comparative results still reflect the IOP‑lowering effect of the drainage tube itself.

The anterior segment OCT of this study showed that the drain was stably positioned within the corner of the rabbit’s eye, and no displacement or dislocation of the drain was observed. In the trypan blue patency test at 4 weeks and 12 weeks after surgery, 12 and 13 test eyes observed that trypan blue dye flowed smoothly into the subconjunctival space, with a wide diffusion range, and formed a slightly raised translucent bubble-like structure, indicating that the two ends of the GDTI-50H drainage tube were located in the anterior chamber and the subconjunctival space respectively, and the drainage tube was unobstructed, which could effectively drain the aqueous humor. For the remaining test eyes, which did not observe the efflux of indigo stain from the conjunctiva, we analyzed that the reason may be that the waiting time (10 min) reduced the visibility of the dye. The pathological results are shown in Fig. 6. There is no inflammatory cell infiltration and fibrosis reaction in the drainage tube implantation area and surrounding tissues, and its natural biomaterial characteristics reduce the risk of immune rejection, and have good biocompatibility and safety with intraocular tissues. During the whole process, the experimental rabbits had a normal diet, water, and excretion, and there were no systemic complications.

The experimental results of the Ahmed drainage valve by Joo Yeon Kim et al. [23] showed that the incidence of corneal decompensation and corneal edema in the three months after surgery was about 8.6%. The observation period of this study lasted for 12 weeks, and no corneal decompensation or corneal edema appeared in the test eyes. We believe that GDT-50H has a smaller size, a larger distance between the inner end of the mouth and the corneal endothelium, and has been optimized in terms of materials, resulting in a lower incidence of corneal decompensation and corneal edema after surgery.

This study confirmed through fluid dynamics FEA simulation that the GDTI-50H maintains high drainage efficiency even under high IOP ( > 40 mmHg). The underlying mechanisms are as follows: (1) the porous structure increases the total outflow cross-sectional area, reducing local flow velocity and shear stress; (2) multiple channels share the fluid pressure, resulting in a more uniform pressure gradient distribution and reducing vortex formation and energy loss; (3) when one channel gradually narrows due to tissue encapsulation or protein deposition, the other channels can compensate by increasing flow, thereby delaying the decline in total drainage. These fluid dynamics results indicate that in patients who may experience high IOP fluctuations (> 40 mmHg) in the early postoperative period, the GDTI-50H can more efficiently drain aqueous humor to the subconjunctival space, thereby lowering IOP to a safe range more quickly, and the porous structure contributes to maintaining long‑term IOP control. Although the porous design increases the surface area for protein deposition, functional redundancy makes the probability of simultaneous multi-pore occlusion significantly lower than that of single-outlet failure, resulting in superior overall drainage reliability. Its long-term mechanical stability remains unclear and requires in vivo observation beyond one year. Future studies will focus on structural integrity and drainage function maintenance after longer implantation periods.

Additionally, as the first-in-animal study of the GDTI-50H drainage tube, the 12-week observation period was designed to capture the critical window for early safety assessment of MIGS devices, covering events such as tube migration, corneal decompensation, anterior chamber inflammation, and bleb encapsulation. However, long-term material degradation, mechanical stability, and maintenance of drainage efficiency beyond one year require further extended observation in subsequent studies. Meanwhile, non-human primates exhibit ocular anatomical structures—particularly the anterior chamber angle and trabecular meshwork morphology—that more closely resemble human anatomy, making them an indispensable preclinical model before advancing to clinical translation.

This animal experiment still has certain limitations. (1) Micro-CT three-dimensional imaging was not performed in this study. As alternatives, we comprehensively evaluated the tube position and patency using anterior segment OCT, trypan blue patency testing, and histopathology. Future studies will incorporate Micro-CT to more comprehensively characterize the three-dimensional structural changes of the drainage tube. (2) This study used healthy rabbit eyes with normal IOP rather than an ocular hypertension model, which cannot fully replicate the pathophysiological state of glaucoma patients. In future studies, GDTI-50H will be evaluated in hypertensive animal models to validate its efficacy under conditions of pathological aqueous outflow resistance. Concurrently, extended observation over one year will be conducted to assess the long-term material stability and bleb evolution.

Conclusions

This study systematically evaluated the effectiveness and safety of the GDTI-50H drainage tube with a porous outer end design in reducing IOP through a rabbit eye control experiment. The preliminary results showed that the GDTI-50H drainage tube could effectively drain aqueous humor out of the eye and reduce IOP. The lumen remained unobstructed during the follow-up period, and it had good biocompatibility and safety with intraocular tissues. These results provide a strong experimental basis for its future clinical application and provide a new choice for the development of clinical glaucoma drainage devices with high efficiency and safety.

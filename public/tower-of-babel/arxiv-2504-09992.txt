Weighted inequality of integral operators induced by Hardy kernels
Zipeng Wang, Kenan Zhang

Abstract
. For doubling weights, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition such that the one weighted inequality of the integral operator induced by Hardy kernels on the unit disk holds. This confirms a conjecture by Guo and Wang in such situations.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
47G10, 42A50.

1.
Introduction

Let
\alpha>0
and
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disk in the complex plane. Cheng-Fang-Wang-Yu
[
1
]
considered the following integral operators on the unit disk

(K_{\alpha}f)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\lambda)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)dA(\lambda),

where

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)=\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}.

The intergal kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is a typical reproducking kernel, in particular unitary invariance kernels
[
3
]
, for certain Hilbert spaces of holomorphic functions on the unit disk. For example, the operator
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is the Bergman projection and
\mathcal{K}_{1}
is an operator induced by the Hardy kernel. Cheng-Fang-Wang-Yu in
[
1
]
descripted the range of
(p,q)\in[1,+\infty]\times[1,+\infty]
such that
K_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{D})
.

A weight
\omega
is a positive integrable function on the unit disk. For
p\geq 1
, the weighted space
L^{p}(\omega)
is the Banach space

\{f:f\mbox{ is a complex-valued function on $\mathbb{D}$ with }\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}:=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dA(z)\bigg)^{1/p}

and
dA(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
is the normalized area measure.

The one weight inequality for
K_{\alpha}
is to characterize the weight
\omega
such that

K_{\alpha}:L^{p}(\omega)\to L^{p}(\omega)

is bounded. When
\alpha=2
, the corresponding one weight inequality is the one weight problem of Bergman projection, and is well understood by the remarkable Bekollé-Bonami weight
[
2
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, except the Bergman projection, all the other one weight inequality problems of the integral operator
K_{\alpha}
remain open.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{T}
be an interval on the unit circle. The associated Carleson box
Q_{I}
in the unit disk is

Q_{I}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:z/|z|\in I,1-|I|<|z|<1\},

where
|I|
is the normalized arc length of the interval
I
.

Let
|Q_{I}|=\int_{Q_{I}}dA
and
|Q_{I}|_{\omega}=\int_{Q_{I}}\omega(z)dA(z)
.
In 2015, Guo-Wang
[
4
]
proved that if a weight
\omega
on the unit disk satisfies

\sup_{I:I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\omega^{-1}}}{|Q_{I}|^{3/2}}<\infty,

then
K_{1}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\omega)
. They also conjectured that the necessary and sufficient condition for the boundedness of
K_{1}
on
L^{2}(\omega)
is

\sup_{I:I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\omega^{-1}}}{|Q_{I}|}<\infty.

In this note, under the assumption on the doubling condition, we confirm Guo-Wang’s conjecture. Moreover, for any
\alpha>1
we characterize the one weight inequality for
K_{\alpha}
on the weighted space
L^{p}(\omega)
for
1<p<\infty
.

Recall a measure
\mu
on the unit disk satisfies the doubling conditions if there extists a constant
C>0
such that
\mu(B(z,2r))\leq C\mu(B(z,r))
for any
z\in\mathbb{D},r>0
, where
B(z,r)
is the Euclidean disk on the complex plane with center
z
and radius
r
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
1<p,p^{\prime}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.
Let
\omega
be a weight on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and
\sigma=\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}
be its dual weight.
Suppose that both
\omega
and
\sigma
are doubling weights. Then the operator
K_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega)
if and only if

[\omega]_{p,\alpha}:=\sup\limits_{I\subset\mathbb{T},\ interval}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{p-1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{p\alpha}{2}}}<\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

The only if part holds without requiring the doubling conditions. The if part remains true under the reverse doubling conditions (
2.3
), which are slightly weaker than the standard doubling condition.

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.
The necessity

Assume that
K_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega)
.
By Sawyer’s duality trick
[
6
]
, the operator

(K_{\alpha}f)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\lambda)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)dA(\lambda)

is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega)
if and only if

(K_{\alpha,\sigma}f)(z):=(K_{\alpha}(\sigma f))(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\lambda)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)

is bounded from
L^{p}(\sigma)
to
L^{p}(\omega)
. Let

A=\|K_{\alpha,\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)\to L^{p}(\omega)}.

Then the following weak-type inequality holds

(2.1)

|\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|(K_{\alpha,\sigma}f)(z)|>\gamma\}|_{\omega}\leqslant\frac{A^{p}}{\gamma^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\sigma(z)dA(z).

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\alpha>0
, there exist intervals
I,J\in\mathbb{T}
such that
|J|=|I|
, and for each
g\geqslant 0
supported in
Q_{I}
and each
z\in Q_{J}
, we have

|(K_{\alpha}g)(z)|\geqslant\frac{C_{1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(\lambda)dA(\lambda),

where
C_{1}
depentent only on
\alpha
.

Proof.

Denote the center of
Q_{I}
by
c
.
Then for fixed
g\geqslant 0
supporting on
Q_{I}
, we have

\displaystyle(K_{\alpha}g)(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(\lambda)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{c})^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(\lambda)dA(\lambda)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{c})^{\alpha}}\right)g(\lambda)dA(\lambda).

Consider the bracketed part in the latter term, by the mean value theorem

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{c})^{\alpha}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{c})^{\alpha}}\right|\left|\frac{(1-z\overline{c})^{\alpha}-(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}{(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|1-z\overline{c}|^{\alpha}}\frac{\alpha|z\overline{c}-z\overline{\lambda}||1-z\overline{\xi}|^{\alpha-1}}{|1-z\overline{\lambda}|^{\alpha}},

where
\xi
is a point on the segment connecting
c
and
\lambda
.

Here we estimate the second term in the last equation. We identify the circle
\mathbb{T}
with the interval
[0,2\pi)
.
Let
I=(0,\theta)
and
J=(d\theta,(d+1)\theta)
for some
d>2
and
0<(d+1)\theta<\pi/2
.
Assume
z=r_{1}e^{i\xi_{1}}\in Q_{J}
and
\lambda=r_{2}e^{i\xi_{2}}\in Q_{I}
, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle|1-z\overline{\lambda}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|1-r_{1}r_{2}e^{i(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1+r_{1}^{2}r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=2r_{1}r_{2}(1-\cos(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}))+(1-r_{1}r_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=4r_{1}r_{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}{2}\right)+(1-r_{1}r_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{4}{\pi}(1-\theta)^{2}(d-1)^{2}\theta^{2}

Since
d>2
and
0<(d+1)\theta<\pi/2
, we have

1-\theta\geqslant 1-\frac{\pi}{2(d+1)}\geqslant 1-\frac{\pi}{6}.

Let the constant
a=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(1-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)
, we have

|1-z\overline{\lambda}|\geqslant a(d-1)\theta.

Besides,

\displaystyle|c-\lambda|^{2}

\displaystyle=|c|^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2|c|r_{2}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\xi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2|c|r_{2}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\xi_{2}\right)\right)+(|c|-r_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=4|c|r_{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\xi_{2}\right)+(|c|-r_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\theta^{2}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{4}\theta^{2},

Then

|c-\lambda|\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\theta.

Since

\displaystyle|1-z\overline{\xi}|

\displaystyle\leqslant|1-z\overline{\lambda}|+|z||\overline{\xi}-\overline{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\leqslant|1-z\overline{\lambda}|+|c-\lambda|,

we have

\displaystyle\frac{|1-z\overline{\xi}|}{|1-z\overline{\lambda}|}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\frac{|c-\lambda|}{|1-z\overline{\lambda}|}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2a(d-1)}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2a}.

Denote the constan
b=1+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2a}
. Then we can choose
d
large enough such that

\frac{\alpha|z\overline{c}-z\overline{\lambda}||1-z\overline{\xi}|^{\alpha-1}}{|1-z\overline{\lambda}|^{\alpha}}\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{5}b^{\alpha-1}\alpha}{2a(d-1)}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Hence,

\left|\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{c})^{\alpha}}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|1-z\overline{c}|^{\alpha}}.

Then by (
2.2
), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
depentent only on
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle|(K_{\alpha}g)(z)|

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|1-z\overline{c}|^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(\lambda)dA(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{C_{1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(\lambda)dA(\lambda).

∎

Let
Q_{I}
be a Carleson box on
\mathbb{D}
small enough and
g(\lambda)=\sigma(\lambda)\chi_{Q_{I}}(\lambda)
.
Then by the weak-type estimate (
2.1
) there exists a Carleson box
Q_{J}
such that for all
z\in Q_{J}

|(K_{\alpha}g)(z)|\geqslant\frac{C_{1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(\lambda)dA(\lambda)=C_{1}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Let
f(z)=\chi_{Q_{J}}(z)
, then
K_{\alpha,\sigma}f=K_{\alpha}g
and by (
2.1
), we have

|Q_{J}|_{\omega}\leqslant\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|(K_{\alpha,\sigma}f)(z)|>C_{1}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\right\}\right|_{\omega}\leqslant\left(\frac{A}{C_{1}}\right)^{p}\frac{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{p\alpha}{2}}}{|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{p}}\int_{Q_{I}}\sigma(z)dA(z).

Denote
C_{2}=\left(\frac{A}{C_{1}}\right)^{p}
, then we have

\frac{|Q_{J}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{p-1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{p\alpha}{2}}}\leqslant C_{2}.

Since
|I|=|J|
by Lemma
2.1
,

\sup\limits_{I\subset\mathbb{T},\ interval}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{p-1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{p\alpha}{2}}}\leqslant C_{2}<\infty.

This completes the proof of the necessary part.

2.2.
The Sufficienty

We consider the following dyadic systems on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
:

\mathcal{D}^{0}:=\left\{\left[\frac{2\pi k}{2^{j}},\frac{2\pi(k+1)}{2^{j}}\right):j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},0\leqslant k\leqslant 2^{j}-1\right\},

and

\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{3}}:=\left\{\left[\frac{2\pi k}{2^{j}}+\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{2\pi(k+1)}{2^{j}}+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right):j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},0\leqslant k\leqslant 2^{j}-1\right\}.

Denote the corresponding Carleson box system on the unit disk by

\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}:=\{Q_{I}:I\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}\},\quad\beta\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}.

For each
\beta\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}
, we define an operator
T_{\alpha}^{\beta}
on
L^{1}(\mathbb{D})
by

(T_{\alpha}^{\beta}f)(z)=\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}}\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{Q_{I}}f(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\chi_{Q_{I}}(z),

where
\chi_{Q_{I}}(z)
is the characteristic function of
Q_{I}
.

The following lemma illustrates that
K_{\alpha}
can be pointwise controlled by
T_{\alpha}^{\beta},\beta\in\{0,1/3\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that for any
f\geqslant 0
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

|K_{\alpha}f(z)|\leqslant C_{3}\left((T_{\alpha}^{0}f)(z)+(T_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{3}}f)(z)\right).

Proof.

Fixed
\beta\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}
.
Let
K_{\alpha}^{\beta}
be the integral kernel of
T_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, i.e. for
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}

K_{\alpha}^{\beta}(z,\lambda)=\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}\frac{\chi_{Q_{I}}(z)\chi_{Q_{I}}(\lambda)}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Hence it suffices to prove that for any
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
only depending on
\alpha
such that

|K_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)|\leqslant C_{3}\left(K_{\alpha}^{0}(z,\lambda)+K_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{3}}(z,\lambda)\right).

Recall the known relation between Bergman-type kernels and Carleson boxes, there exists a constant
C_{4}
such that for any
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, there exists a Carleson box
Q_{I}
such that
z,\lambda\in Q_{I}
and

\frac{1}{C_{4}}|Q_{I}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant|1-z\overline{\lambda}|\leqslant C_{4}|Q_{I}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then we have

|K_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)|=\bigg|\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{\lambda})^{\alpha}}\bigg|\leqslant\frac{C_{4}^{\alpha}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Since

|Q_{I}|=\left\{\begin{aligned} &|I|^{2}-\frac{|I|^{3}}{2},\quad&&0<|I|\leqslant 1\\
&\frac{|I|}{2},\quad&&1<|I|\leqslant 2\pi\end{aligned}\right.,

we have

\frac{1}{4\pi}|I|^{2}\leqslant|Q_{I}|\leqslant|I|^{2}.

Applying Mei’s elegant result
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
, we see that for any interval
I\in\mathbb{T}
there exists an interval
J\in\mathcal{D}^{0}\cup\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{3}}
such that
I\in J
and
|J|\leqslant 6|I|
.
Hence, for any
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle|K_{\alpha}(z,\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{4}^{\alpha}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(2\sqrt{\pi}C_{4})^{\alpha}}{|I|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(12\sqrt{\pi}C_{4})^{\alpha}}{|J|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(12\sqrt{\pi}C_{4})^{\alpha}}{|Q_{J}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}\left(K_{\alpha}^{0}(z,\lambda)+K_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{3}}(z,\lambda)\right).

Here
C_{3}=(12\sqrt{\pi}C_{4})^{\alpha}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

For a weight
\omega
on
\mathbb{D}
, we also consider the maximal operator

(M_{\omega}^{\beta}f)(z)=\sup\limits_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}}\frac{\chi_{Q_{I}}(z)}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}}\int_{Q_{I}}|f(\lambda)|\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda).

By
[
7
, Theorem 5.7]
, we have

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega
be a weight on
\mathbb{D}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{5}
independent of the weight
\omega
and the dyadic systen
\mathcal{D}^{\beta}
such that

||M_{\omega}^{\beta}f||_{L^{p}(\omega)}\leqslant C_{5}||f||_{L^{p}(\omega)}.

By
[
7
, Theorem 5.8]
,
[
2
, Lemma 10]
and Lemma
2.3
, we have the following Carleson embedding results.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\omega
be a weight on
\mathbb{D}
with the reverse doubling property and let
\mathcal{Q}^{\beta},\beta\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}
be a Carleson system over
\mathbb{D}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{6}>0
such that for all
g\in L^{p}(\omega)
, we have

\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}|Q_{I}|_{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}}\int_{Q_{I}}g(\lambda)\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)^{p}\leqslant C_{6}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(\lambda)|^{p}\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda).

Here we recall the definition of the reverse doubling property.
Given a Carleson box
Q_{I}
, set

B_{I}:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\frac{z}{|z|}\in I,1-\frac{|I|}{2}<|z|<1\},

a weight
\omega
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
has the reverse doubling property if there exists a constant
\delta<1
such that

(2.3)

\frac{|B_{I}|_{\omega}}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}}<\delta

for all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{T}
.
By
[
2
, Lemma 6]
, a doubling weight on
\mathbb{D}
has the reverse doubling property.

Now, we are ready to prove the sufficiency of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
and
\omega
be a weight on
\mathbb{D}
, and
\sigma=\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}

Assume both
\omega
and
\sigma
have the reverse doubling property and

[\omega]_{p,\alpha}<\infty.

By Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to prove
T_{\alpha}^{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega)
for each
\beta\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}
. By Sawyer’s dual arguments
[
6
]
, this is equivalent to proving

T^{\beta}_{\alpha,\sigma}:L^{p}(\sigma)\longrightarrow L^{p}(\omega)

is bounded where
\sigma=\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}
and

(T^{\beta}_{\alpha,\sigma}f)(z):=\left(T^{\beta}_{\alpha}(\sigma f)\right)(z)=\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{Q_{I}}f(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\chi_{Q_{I}}(z).

Next, we assume that
f
and
g
are non-negative functions on
\mathbb{D}
. For all
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\langle T^{\beta}_{\alpha,\sigma}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\left(\int_{Q_{I}}f(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)\left(\int_{Q_{I}}g(\lambda)\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}\int_{Q_{I}}f(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}}\int_{Q_{I}}g(\lambda)\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right).

Here

\displaystyle\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{p}}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\left(\frac{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{p-1}}{|Q_{I}|^{\frac{p\alpha}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant[\omega]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\alpha}|Q_{I}|_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle T^{\beta}_{\alpha,\sigma}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle[\omega]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\alpha}\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}\left[|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}\int_{Q_{I}}f(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)\right]\left[|Q_{I}|_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}}\int_{Q_{I}}g(\lambda)\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle[\omega]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\alpha}\left[\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\sigma}}\int_{Q_{I}}f(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{Q}^{\beta}}|Q_{I}|_{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\omega}}\int_{Q_{I}}g(\lambda)\omega(\lambda)dA(\lambda)\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

Since
\sigma
and
\omega
have the reverse doubling property, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\langle T^{\beta}_{\alpha,\sigma}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leqslant C_{6}[\omega]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\alpha}||f||_{L^{p}(\sigma)}||g||_{L^{p}(\omega)}.

This completes the proof of the sufficient part, and completes the whole proof of Theorem
1.1
.

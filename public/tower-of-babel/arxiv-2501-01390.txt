On higher order isolas of unstable Stokes waves
Massimiliano Berti, Livia Corsi, Alberto Maspero, Paolo Ventura

Abstract




Abstract.
We overview the recent result
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
about the high-frequency
instability of Stokes waves subject to longitudinal perturbations. The
spectral bands of unstable eigenvalues away from the origin form a sequence of

isolas
parameterized by an integer
\mathtt{p}\geq 2
for any value of the depth
\mathtt{h}>0
such that an explicit analytic
function
\beta_{1}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})
is not zero.
In
[
3
]
it is proved that the map
\mathtt{h}\mapsto\beta_{1}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})
is not identically zero for any

\mathtt{p}\geq 2

by showing that
\lim_{\mathtt{h}\to 0^{+}}\beta_{1}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})=-\infty
.
In this manuscript we compute the asymptotic expansion
of
\beta_{1}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})

in the deep-water limit
\mathtt{h}\to+\infty
–it vanishes exponentially fast to zero–
for
\mathtt{p}=2
,
3
,
4
.

Introduction

Stokes waves are among the most renowned global in time solutions of the gravity water waves equations,
discovered in the pioneering work

[
33
]
of Stokes in 1847. The water waves equations govern
the time evolution of
a bidimensional, incompressible, perfect
fluid occupying the time dependent region

{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}:=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\;:\;-\mathtt{h}<y<\eta(t,x)\big\}\,,\quad\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\,,

under the action of gravity.
The dynamics of the fluid is
determined by the Euler equations inside
{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}
and
by two boundary conditions at the free surface.
The first is that the fluid particles remain, along the evolution, on the free surface
(kinematic boundary condition),
and the second one is that the pressure of the fluid
is equal, at the free surface, to the constant atmospheric pressure
(dynamic boundary condition).
As shown
by Zhakarov
[
38
]
these equations form an infinite dimensional Hamiltonian system.

The Stokes waves
are space and time
periodic traveling solutions of the form

\eta_{\epsilon}(x-c_{\epsilon}t)
with a periodic profile

\eta_{\epsilon}(\cdot)
.
The first mathematically rigorous proof of their existence was given by
[
34
,
24
,
30
]
almost one century ago.

A question of fundamental physical importance concerns their stability/instability.

In the sixties
Benjamin and Feir
[
2
,
1
]
, Whitham
[
35
]
, Lighthill
[
25
]
and Zakharov
[
37
,
39
]
discovered,
through experiments and formal arguments, that small amplitude Stokes waves in sufficiently deep water are unstable
when subject to long-wave perturbations. More precisely, their works predicted
that
2
\pi
-periodic Stokes waves evolving in a tank of depth

\mathtt{h}>1.363...
and length
2\pi/\mu
, with
\mu\ll 1
, are unstable.
This phenomenon is nowadays called “Benjamin-Feir” –or modulational– instability.
It is supported by an enormous amount of
physical observations and numerical simulations, see e.g.
[
16
,
26
]
,
and detected in several dispersive fluid PDE models, such as KdV, gKdV, NLS and the Whitham equation,
see e.g.
[
36
,
32
,
17
,
18
,
10
,
23
,
20
,
9
,
21
,
27
]
.

In the eighties other mechanisms of instability
-called nowadays high frequency instabilities-
were detected by McLean
[
28
,
29
]
.
In order to explain properly these phenomena
we formulate the
problem in rigorous mathematical terms.

Mathematical formulation.

Consider
a
2\pi
-periodic Stokes wave
with amplitude
0<\epsilon\ll 1

in an ocean with depth
\mathtt{h}>0
.
The linearized water waves equations at the Stokes waves
are, in the inertial reference frame moving with the speed
c_{\epsilon}
of the wave,
a linear time-independent system of partial pseudo-differential equations

\partial_{t}h(t,x)=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathtt{h})\,[h(t,x)]\,.

(1.1)

Using the Zakharov Hamiltonian formulation
[
38
]

of the water waves
equation (and after some changes of variables)
the linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\mathtt{h})

turns out to be the Hamiltonian and reversible real operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\mathtt{h}):=\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathrm{Id}\\
-\mathrm{Id}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1+a_{\epsilon}(x)&-({\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}+p_{\epsilon}(x))\partial_{x}\\
\partial_{x}\circ({\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}+p_{\epsilon}(x))&|D|\tanh((\mathtt{h}+\mathtt{f}_{\epsilon})|D|)\end{bmatrix}

(1.2)

where

p_{\epsilon}(x),a_{\epsilon}(x)
are real even analytic functions and
\mathtt{f}_{\epsilon}
is a real number.
Both
p_{\epsilon}(x),a_{\epsilon}(x)
and
\mathtt{f}_{\epsilon}
depend analytically with respect to
\epsilon
. We refer to
[
31
,
4
,
5
]
for details.

The dynamics
of (
1.1
) for
h(t,\cdot)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is fully determined by the
spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathtt{h})
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let us summarize what is known about it.
The first mathematically rigorous proof
of local branches of Benjamin-Feir spectrum was obtained by Bridges-Mielke
[
8
]
in finite depth, for
\mathtt{h}>1.363...
, see also
[
22
]
, and recently by Nguyen-Strauss
[
31
]

in deep water.
Very recently Berti-Maspero-Ventura
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
proved that these local branches can be globally continued to form a complete figure “8”, a fact that
had been observed numerically in
[
16
]
. The papers
[
4
]
, resp.
[
5
]
, proved the existence of the figure “8” in deep water, resp. in finite depth
\mathtt{h}>1.363...
; finally
[
6
]

proved that also at
the critical depth
\mathtt{h}=1.363...
, and also slightly below it, the Stokes waves are modulationally unstable, answering to a question that had been controversial for long times.
This settles the question about the shape of the spectrum close to the origin.

In the eighties McLean
[

the existence of unstable spectrum away from the origin. Later on
Deconinck and Oliveras obtained in
[

Deconinck and Oliveras
conjectured the existence of infinitely many isolas, parametrized by integers
\mathtt{p}\geq 2
, going to infinity along the imaginary axis and shrinking as

Bloch-Floquet expansion.

Since the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}:={\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\mathtt{h})
in
(
1.2
) has
2\pi
-periodic coefficients, its
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
spectrum is conveniently described
by the Bloch-Floquet decomposition

\sigma_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big({\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\big)=\bigcup_{\mu\in\big[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}\sigma_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\big({\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}\big)\,\qquad\textup{where}\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}:={\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}(\mathtt{h}):=e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\mu x}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\,\mu x}

(1.3)

is
the complex
Hamiltonian
and
reversible
pseudo-differential operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathrm{Id}\\
-\mathrm{Id}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1+a_{\epsilon}(x)&-({\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}+p_{\epsilon}(x))(\partial_{x}+\mathrm{i}\,\mu)\\
(\partial_{x}+\mathrm{i}\,\mu)\circ({\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}+p_{\epsilon}(x))&|D+\mu|\tanh\big((\mathtt{h}+\mathtt{f}_{\epsilon})|D+\mu|\big)\end{bmatrix}\,,

(1.4)

with
domain
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}):=H^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{2})
and range
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}):=L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

The spectrum of

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}

on

L^{2}(\mathbb{T})

is discrete and its eigenvalues span, as
\mu
varies, the continuous spectral bands of
\sigma_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big({\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\big)
.
The parameter

\mu
is usually referred to as the Floquet exponent.
A direct consequence of the Bloch-Floquet decomposition is that any
solution
of (
1.1
) can be decomposed as a linear superposition of Bloch-waves

h(t,x)=e^{\lambda t}e^{\mathrm{i}\,\mu x}v(x)

(1.5)

where
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}

with associated eigenvector

v(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.
If
\lambda
is unstable, i.e. has positive real part, then the
solution
(
1.5
)
grows exponentially fast in time.

We remark that the spectrum

\sigma_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}({\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon})

is a set which is

1
-periodic in
\mu
, and thus
it is sufficient to consider

\mu
in the first zone of Brillouin
[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})
. Furthermore by the reality
of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
the spectrum
\sigma_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}({\mathcal{L}}_{-\mu,\epsilon})

is the complex conjugated of

\sigma_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}({\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon})

and we can restrict
\mu
to the interval
[0,\tfrac{1}{2})
. From a physical point of view, the spectrum of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}
encodes the linear dynamics of any long-wave perturbation of the Stokes waves of amplitude
\epsilon

evolving inside a tank of length
2\pi\mu^{-1}
.

The Hamiltonian nature of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,\epsilon}
constrains the eigenvalues with nonzero real part to arise, for
small
\epsilon>0
, as perturbation of
multiple
eigenvalues of

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,0}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}(\partial_{x}+\mathrm{i}\,\mu)&|D+\mu|\tanh(\mathtt{h}|D+\mu|)\\
-1&{\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}(\partial_{x}+\mathrm{i}\,\mu)\end{bmatrix}\,.

(1.6)

The spectrum of the
Fourier multiplier matrix operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,0}(\mathtt{h})

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{2})

is given by the purely imaginary numbers

\lambda_{j}^{\sigma}(\mu,\mathtt{h}):=\mathrm{i}\,\omega^{\sigma}(j+\mu,\mathtt{h})={\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}(j+\mu)-\sigma\Omega(j+\mu,\mathtt{h})\,,\ \forall\sigma=\pm\,,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(1.7)

where

\omega^{\sigma}(\varphi,\mathtt{h}):={\mathtt{c}}_{\mathtt{h}}\varphi-\sigma\Omega(\varphi,\mathtt{h})\,,\qquad\Omega(\varphi,\mathtt{h}):=\sqrt{\varphi\tanh(\mathtt{h}\varphi)}\,,\qquad\varphi\in\mathbb{R}\,,\ \ \sigma=\pm\,.

(1.8)

Note that
\omega^{+}(-\varphi,\mathtt{h})=-\omega^{-}(\varphi,\mathtt{h})
. Sometimes

\sigma
is called the Krein signature of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}^{\sigma}(\mu,\mathtt{h})
.
The spectrum of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,0}(\mathtt{h})
is purely imaginary.
As stated in
[
3
]
[Lemma 2.9], for any

\mathtt{h}>0
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,0}(\mathtt{h})
possesses, away from
0
, only simple or double eigenvalues. Its double eigenvalues
form a diverging sequence

\{\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_{*}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})\}_{\mathtt{p}=2,3,\dots}

where each
\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_{*}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})
lies in the spectrum of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\underline{\mu},0}(\mathtt{h})
only for
one
particular real value of the Floquet exponent

\underline{\mu}=\underline{\varphi}(\mathtt{p},\mathtt{h})>0

and its integer shifts
\underline{\mu}+k
,

k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
see
[
3
]
[Lemma 2.7].
In other words at
\underline{\mu}=\underline{\varphi}(\mathtt{p},\mathtt{h})
,
mod
1
, the unperturbed spectral bands of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu,0}(\mathtt{h})
intersect.
The values
\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_{*}^{(\mathtt{p})}(\mathtt{h})
are the branching points of the
\mathtt{p}
-th isolas stated in Theorem
2.1
below.

In this paper we complement the above results

Before stating the main result, let us introduce the following standard notation.

Notation
. Let
f,g:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}

be two real functions with
f
strictly positive, and let
x_{0}\in[0,+\infty]
. We denote

•

x\to x_{0}

|g(x)|\leq Cf(x)
for any
x
near
x_{0}
;

•

g=o(f)
as
x\to x_{0}
if
\lim\limits_{x\to x_{0}}\dfrac{g(x)}{f(x)}=0
.

The next result provides the sharp asymptotic expansion of

Theorem 2.1
.

The coefficients
\beta_{1}^{(p)}(\mathtt{h})
,

\mathtt{p}=2,3,4

\mathtt{h}\to+\infty

the asymptotic expansions

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\mathtt{h})=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{64}e^{-\frac{\mathtt{h}}{2}}+O\big(e^{-\frac{3}{4}\mathtt{h}}\big)\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{(3)}(\mathtt{h})=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}e^{-2\mathtt{h}}+O\big(e^{-3\mathtt{h}}\big)\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{(4)}(\mathtt{h})=-\frac{5\sqrt{5}}{8\sqrt{3}}e^{-2\mathtt{h}}+O\big(e^{-4\mathtt{h}}\big)\,.

(2.3)

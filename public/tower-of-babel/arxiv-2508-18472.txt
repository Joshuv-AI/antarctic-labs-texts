Abstract

Let
M
be a closed orientable hypersurface of dimension
n
, with nonwhere vanishing mean curvature
H
, immersed into a Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold
{\mathcal{Z}}
carrying a parallel spinor field. The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}({\not\hskip-2.84544ptD})
(with the least absolute value) of the induced Dirac operator
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
of
M
satisfies the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
Bär inequality
[
6
,
32
,
35
]
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}({\not\hskip-2.84544ptD})\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4\ \mathrm{vol}(M)}\int_{M}H^{2}dV,

where
\mathrm{vol}(M)
is the volume of
M
and
dV
is the volume form
of the manifold
M
. In this paper, we classify hypersurfaces
M
that satisfy the equality case in the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
Bär inequality when
{\mathcal{Z}}=(0,+\infty)\times P
is the cone over a Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold
P
carrying a real Killing spinor, under two conditions: one being a Ricci condition on
{\mathcal{Z}}
, and the second one the curvature of the auxiliary line bundle associated with the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure on
{\mathcal{Z}}
. More precisely, we prove that
M
are the slices
\{s\}\times P
, where
s\in(0,+\infty)
. In the special case, when
{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, i.e., the cone over the sphere, which is a Spin manifold with a parallel spinor, the classification result was previously obtained by Hijazi and Montiel
[
21
]
.

Key words
:
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structures, Dirac operator, Eigenvalues, The Bär inequality, Hypersurfaces.

Introduction

In recent years, researchers in Submanifold Theory and experts in Riemannian and Spin Geometry have increasingly studied Dirac operators naturally associated with the extrinsic geometry of hypersurfaces, exploring their spectral properties to gain deeper insights into the geometry and topology of these hypersurfaces (see
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
36
]
and related references). These Spin Geometry techniques have led to significant advances in extrinsic geometry, including the study of constant mean curvature (CMC) or minimal surfaces in homogeneous
3
-spaces from Thurston’s classification of 3-dimensional geometries, the Willmore conjecture, the Willmore functional and Alexandrov-type theorems. Remarkably, Spin Geometry tools —especially special spinor fields and the Dirac operator—have played a crucial role in many of these developments, inspiring further research directions.

When shifting from classical Spin geometry to
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
geometry, the framework becomes more general, giving rise to a range of challenges and obstacles. This is because the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure depends not only on the geometry of the manifold but also on the connection (and hence the curvature) of the auxiliary line bundle associated with the chosen
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure. From a physical point of view, spinors model fermions while
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
spinors can be
interpreted as fermions coupled to an electromagnetic field. Extending the use of Spin methods in submanifold studies to the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
setting allows for the exploration of a broader range of ambient geometric structures. Indeed, every Spin manifold is trivially
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
and manifolds such as CR, Kähler, complex, and Sasaki manifolds are naturally
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
, often with special spinor fields. For instance, from an extrinsic viewpoint, O. Hijazi, S. Montiel, and F. Urbano constructed
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structures on Kähler-Einstein manifolds with positive scalar curvature, carrying Kählerian Killing spinors
[
22
]
. When restricted to minimal Lagrangian submanifolds, these spinors impose topological and geometric constraints on the submanifolds. Further applications of
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
geometry in extrinsic geometry can be found in
[
32
,
34
]
. We will now highlight an example of such results that is particularly relevant to the problem we will address in this paper.

Problem Setting:
Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed oriented hypersurface, isometrically immersed in a
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold
{\mathcal{Z}}
, as is typical for many ambient spaces in Submanifold Theory. Under these conditions,
M
inherits an induced

\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure. We will denote the spinor bundle on
M
by
{\not\hskip-2.84544pt\Sigma}M
and the Dirac operator by
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
. This situation occurs, for example, when the ambient space
{\mathcal{Z}}
is
\mathbb{CP}^{n+1}
, which is always a
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold—Spin when
n+1
is odd and non-Spin when
n+1
is even. In fact, it carries a canonical
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure endowed with a parallel spinor. More generally, a complete, simply connected manifold admits a parallel
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
spinor if and only if it is isometric to the Riemannian product of a simply connected Kähler manifold (with its canonical or anti-canonical
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure) and a simply connected Spin manifold with a parallel spinor
[
31
,
14
]
. Examples of such Riemannian Spin manifolds admitting non-trivial parallel spinor fields include Euclidean spaces, Calabi-Yau manifolds, hyper-Kähler manifolds, certain
7
- and
8
-dimensional special Riemannian manifolds, and their Riemannian products
[
37
]
.

Let
\psi
be a parallel spinor field on
{\mathcal{Z}}
, which we can normalize to have unit constant length. We also denote by
\psi
the restriction of this parallel spinor to the hypersurface
M
. Similar to the approach used by Bleecker and Weiner for the Laplacian, Bär
[
6
]
used this spinor field
\psi
as a test spinor in the variational characterization (Rayleigh quotient) of the lowest eigenvalues of the Spin Dirac operator on
M
, deriving an upper bound for the first eigenvalue of the Spin Dirac operator
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
. This inequality, known as the Bär inequality, was subsequently extended to the eigenvalues of the hypersurface
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
Dirac operator
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
on
M

[
32
,
35
]
. More precisely, we have the following result:

Theorem 1.1
(
[
6
,
32
,
35
]
.
)

Let
M
be a compact orientable hypersurface immersed into a Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold
{\mathcal{Z}}
carrying a parallel spinor.
Let
\lambda_{1}({\not\hskip-2.84544ptD})
be the first eigenvalue (with the least absolute value) of the
induced Dirac operator
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
of
M
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}({\not\hskip-2.84544ptD})\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4\ \mathrm{vol}(M)}\int_{M}H^{2}dV,

(1)

where
\mathrm{vol}(M)
is the volume of
M
,
dV
is the volume form
of the manifold
M
and
H
the mean curvature of
M
.

Problem Statement:

Can we characterize hypersurfaces
M
that satisfy the equality case in the Bär Inequality (
1
) ?

For several years, it was an open problem to determine when equality holds in the Bär inequality (
1
), even for the Spin Dirac operator. Indeed, equality directly implies that the test spinor field
\psi
must be an eigenspinor of
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
. It is evident that this condition implies that the mean curvature
H
is constant. However, this information alone is insufficient unless the ambient manifold is
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and the hypersurface
M
is embedded. In this case, we can apply the well-known Alexandrov theorem
[
2
]
, which states:
Any closed hypersurface embedded in Euclidean space with constant mean curvature must be a hypersphere
. As a result of this theorem, during the 1960s and 1970s, many geometers studying submanifolds believed that spheres might be the only hypersurfaces immersed in Euclidean space with constant mean curvature (see
[
24
]
). However, since 1983, Hsiang et al.
[
25
]
have constructed many examples of such hypersurfaces that are not round spheres, particularly in even dimensions of the ambient space, while Wente
[
38
]
and Kapouleas
[
26
,
27
]
have provided examples in three-dimensional space.

In summary, if equality holds in (
1
), the mean curvature
H
of the hypersurface
M
is constant. Consequently, as previously noted, the restriction of the parallel spinor field
\psi
becomes an eigenspinor of the Dirac operator
{\not\hskip-2.84544ptD}
, corresponding to one of the eigenvalues
\pm\frac{n}{2}H
and this eigenvalue must be the one with the least absolute value. Recently, hypersurfaces
M
that achieve equality in the Bär inequality have been classified when
{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

[
21
]
, revealing that
M
must be a hypersphere. A careful examination of the proofs indicates that the use of the position vector field
x
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is crucial. In other words, a key ingredient is the existence of a vector field
x\in T(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
such that
\nabla_{X}x=X
for all
X\in T(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. But the existence of such a vector field is a characteristic feature of the cone over an arbitrary
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
P
, that is the
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
{\mathcal{Z}}
defined as
{\mathcal{Z}}=(0,+\infty)\times P
endowed with the following warped product metric:

dr\otimes dr+r^{2}\left\langle.,.\right\rangle_{P},\ \ \ \ \forall r\in(0,+\infty).

In fact, the vector field
x
given by

x=r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}

plays the role of the vector position field in the Euclidean space, that is
\nabla_{X}x=X
for all

X\in T{\mathcal{Z}}
. Note that for
P=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we have
{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and this is the only case where
the singularity at the vertex of the cone can be removed.

Bär made an insightful observation (see
[
5
]
) that the cone
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a Riemannian Spin manifold with a non-trivial parallel spinor if the factor
P
is a Riemannian Spin manifold equipped with a non-trivial real Killing spinor. Assuming for simplicity that
P
is simply connected, and aside from the basic case
P=\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
P
could be either an Einstein-Sasaki Riemannian manifold, a
7
-dimensional Spin(7)-manifold, or a
6
-dimensional nearly-Kähler manifold of constant type 1 (see
[
5
]
). For any of these choices of
P
, the corresponding cone
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a Riemannian Spin manifold endowed with a non-trivial parallel spinor and a position vector field. In the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
case, a similar result holds: the cone
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold with a non-trivial parallel spinor if
P
is taken to be a Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold endowed with a non-trivial real Killing spinor
[
30
]
. However, despite the existence of a position vector field, and as pointed previously, several challenges arise since the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure depends not only on the geometry of the manifold but also on the connection (and thus the curvature) of the auxiliary line bundle associated with the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure. For instance, Spin manifolds with real Killing spinors (resp. parallel spinors) are Einstein (resp. Ricci flat) but
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifolds with real Killing spinors (resp. parallel spinors) are not necessarily Einstein (resp. Ricci flat). We will answer the previously stated problem in the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
context, and indeed, we will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2

Let
P^{n}
be a closed Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold of dimension
n
carrying a Killing spinor. Consider the cone
{\mathcal{Z}}=(0,+\infty)\times P
over
P
and assume that

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}^{\mathcal{Z}}\geq c_{n+1}|\Omega^{\mathcal{Z}}|,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathcal{Z}}(X,X)\geq 0,\ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \ \ X\in\Gamma(T{\mathcal{Z}})

(3)

where
\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathcal{Z}}
is the Ricci of
{\mathcal{Z}}
,
\mathrm{R}^{\mathcal{Z}}
denotes the scalar curvature of
{\mathcal{Z}}
,
i\Omega^{\mathcal{Z}}
the curvature
2
-form of the auxiliary line bundle associated with the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure (
\Omega^{\mathcal{Z}}
is a real
2
-form on
M
), and
c_{n+1}=2[\frac{n+1}{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Here
[\frac{n+1}{2}]
is the integer part of
\frac{n+1}{2}
. Let
M
be a closed hypersurface immersed into
{\mathcal{Z}}
satisfying the equality case of the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
Bär type inequality (
1
). Then,
M
is a slice
\{s\}\times P
, where
s\in(0,\infty)
. We shall say that the only compact constant mean curvature hypersurfaces of
{\mathcal{Z}}=(0,+\infty)\times P
immersed by first
eigenspinors are slices
\{s\}\times P
.

If
P
is a
\mathrm{Spin}
manifold with a real Killing spinor then condition (
2
) is always satisfied because the manifold is Einstein with positive scalar curvature. Moreover, the cone carries a parallel
spinor so it is Ricci flat, and the scalar curvature is zero. Hence, the condition
0=\mathrm{R}^{\mathcal{Z}}\geq c_{n+1}|\Omega|^{\mathcal{Z}}=0
is also satisfied.
Again, in the very special case is when
P=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and thus
{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the result was proved by Hijazi and Montiel in
[
21
]
. Two non-
\mathrm{Spin}
examples are provided in Section
6
. In fact, we prove that the only compact hypersurfaces of
{\mathcal{Z}}=(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{B}^{3}
, for some Berger Spheres
\mathbb{B}^{3}
, that satisfy the equality case in (
1
) are slices
\{s\}\times\mathbb{B}^{3}
. Berger Spheres are
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifolds with a real Killing spinor. They are also Spin but without real Killing spinors. Also, we construct explicitly a
5
-dimensional non-
\mathrm{Spin}
, non-Einstein Sasakian manifold such that its cone
{\mathcal{Z}}
fullfills the two conditions of Theorem
1.2
.

As a consequence of the Theorem
1.2
, we can prove the following Alexandrov-type theorem:

Theorem 1.3
(Alexandrov-type Theorem)

Let
P
be a compact Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold of dimension
n
carrying a real Killing spinor.
Consider
{\mathcal{Z}}=(0,+\infty)\times P
the cone over
P
and assume that conditions (
2
) and (
3
) are satisfied. Then, the only compact embedded hypersurfaces into
{\mathcal{Z}}
of constant positive mean curvature are slices
\{s\}\times P
.

The Dirac operator on Spin
c
manifolds.
Let
({\mathcal{Z}}^{n+1},g=\left\langle.,.\right\rangle)
be a Riemannian
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
manifold of dimension
n+1
without boundary. On such a manifold, we have a Hermitian complex vector bundle
\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}}
endowed with a natural scalar product (denoted also by
\left\langle.,.\right\rangle
) and a connection
\nabla
that parallelizes the metric. That is,

\displaystyle X\left\langle\psi,\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla_{X}\psi,\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle\psi,\nabla_{X}\varphi\right\rangle,

(4)

for any
\psi,\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}})
and
X\in\Gamma(T{\mathcal{Z}})
. This complex vector bundle, called the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
bundle, is endowed with a Clifford multiplication denoted by ”
\gamma
”,
\gamma\colon T{\mathcal{Z}}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}})
, such that at every point
x\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, it defines an irreducible representation of the corresponding Clifford algebra. Hence, the complex rank of
\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}}
is
2^{[\frac{n+1}{2}]}
. The Hermitian scalar product
\left\langle.,.\right\rangle
is compatible with this Clifford multiplication and the spinorial connection
\nabla
. That is,

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma(X)\psi,\gamma(X)\psi\right\rangle=|X|^{2}\left\langle\psi,\varphi\right\rangle,

(5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}(\gamma(Y)\psi)=\gamma(\nabla_{X}Y)\psi+\gamma(Y)\nabla_{X}\psi,

(6)

for any
\psi,\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}})
and
X,Y\in\Gamma(T{\mathcal{Z}})
.

Given a
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure on
({\mathcal{Z}}^{n+1},g)
, one can prove that the determinant line bundle
\mathrm{det}(\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}})
has a root of index
2^{[\frac{n+1}{2}]-1}
. We denote
by
L
this root line bundle over
{\mathcal{Z}}
and it is called the auxiliary line bundle associated with the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure. Locally, a
\mathrm{Spin}
structure always exists. We denote by
\Sigma^{\prime}{\mathcal{Z}}
the (possibly globally non-existent)
spinor bundle. Moreover, the square root of the auxiliary line bundle
L

always exists locally. But,
\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}}=\Sigma^{\prime}{\mathcal{Z}}\otimes{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}
exists globally. This essentially means that, while
the spinor bundle and
{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}

may not exist globally, their tensor product (the
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
bundle) is
defined globally. Thus, the connection
\nabla
on
\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}}
is the twisted connection of the one on the
spinor bundle (coming from the Levi-Civita connection) and a fixed connection on
L
.

We may now define the Dirac operator
D
acting on the space of smooth
sections of
\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}}
by the composition of the metric connection and
the Clifford multiplication. In local coordinates this reads as

\displaystyle D=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\gamma(e_{j})\nabla_{e_{j}},

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+1}\}
is a local oriented orthonormal tangent frame. It is a first order elliptic operator, formally self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-scalar product and satisfies the Schrödinger-Lichnerowicz formula

\displaystyle D^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{R}^{{\mathcal{Z}}}\mathrm{Id}_{\Gamma(\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}})}+\frac{i}{2}\gamma(\Omega^{\mathcal{Z}}),

(7)

where
\nabla^{*}
is the adjoint of
\nabla
with respect to the

L^{2}
-scalar product and
\gamma(\Omega^{{\mathcal{Z}}})
is the extension of the Clifford multiplication to differential forms.
By definition, the
function
\left\langle D\psi,\psi\right\rangle
is nothing but the trace of a symmetric two-tensor T
ψ
,
associated with the spinor field
\psi
, the so-called energy-momentum
tensor which is defined as follows

\displaystyle T^{\psi}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\gamma(X)\nabla_{Y}\psi+\gamma(Y)\nabla_{X}\psi,\psi\right\rangle,

(8)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma({\mathcal{Z}})
.

Every almost complex manifold
({\mathcal{Z}}^{2m=n+1},g,J)

of complex dimension
m
has a canonical
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure. In fact, the complexified cotangent bundle

T^{*}{\mathcal{Z}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}=\Lambda^{1,0}{\mathcal{Z}}\oplus\Lambda^{0,1}{\mathcal{Z}}

decomposes into the
\pm i
-eigenbundles of the complex linear extension of the complex structure. Thus, the spinor bundle of the canonical
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure is given by

\Sigma{\mathcal{Z}}=\Lambda^{0,*}{\mathcal{Z}}=\oplus_{r=0}^{m}\Lambda^{0,r}{\mathcal{Z}},

where
\Lambda^{0,r}{\mathcal{Z}}=\Lambda^{r}(\Lambda^{0,1}{\mathcal{Z}})
is the bundle of
r
-forms of type
(0,1)
. The auxiliary
line bundle of this canonical
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure is given by
L=(K_{\mathcal{Z}})^{-1}=\Lambda^{m}(\Lambda^{0,1}{\mathcal{Z}})
,
where
K_{\mathcal{Z}}
is the canonical bundle of
{\mathcal{Z}}

[
11
,
29
,
32
]
. Let
\ltimes

be the Kähler form defined by the complex structure
J
, i.e.
\ltimes(X,Y)=g(X,JY)
for all vector
fields
X,Y\in\Gamma(T{\mathcal{Z}}).
The auxiliary line bundle
L=(K_{\mathcal{Z}})^{-1}
has a canonical holomorphic
connection induced from the Levi-Civita connection whose curvature form is given
by
i\Omega^{\mathcal{Z}}=i\rho^{\mathcal{Z}}
, where
\rho
is the Ricci
2
-form given by
\rho(X,Y)=\mathrm{Ric}(X,JY)
. For the canonical
\mathrm{Spin^{c}}
structure and when

is a Kähler manifold, admits parallel spinors (constant functions)
lying in
\Lambda^{0,0}{\mathcal{Z}}
[
31

\mathrm{Spin^{c}}

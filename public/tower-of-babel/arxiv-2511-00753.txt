Abstract
. We show, using the techniques developed in [ DEST25 , DET23 ] , that dagger algebras and Tate algebras in the sense of Berkovich in prime characteristic p > 0 p>0 have intersection flat Frobenius. Equivalently, if S S is such a ring, then S 1 / p S^{1/p} is a flat and Mittag-Leffler S S -module. As a consequence, we deduce that any ideal-adic completion of a reduced ring that is essentially of finite type over a dagger algebra or a Berkovich Tate algebra in prime characteristic has big test elements from tight closure theory.



1.
Introduction

A long-standing conjecture in prime characteristic commutative algebra is the existence of
big test elements
for an excellent reduced ring
R
of prime characteristic
p>0

[
Hun96
]
. Big test elements are uniform multipliers in
R
that can be used to ‘test’ when an element of an arbitrary
R
-module
M
is in the tight closure
N_{M}^{*}
of any submodule
N
. These special multipliers exist for many commonly occurring classes of excellent rings such as the class of reduced Noetherian rings with finite Frobenius (
F
-finite
rings) and the class of reduced rings that are essentially of finite type over an excellent local ring of characteristic
p

[
HH90
,
HH94
]
. In dimension
\leq 2
, big test elements are known to exist in full generality
[
Abe93
]
. In addition, big test elements also exist for excellent rings with universally injective Frobenius
[
Sha10
]
, commonly known as
F
-pure rings
.

The main result of this paper establishes the existence of big test elements for excellent rings
arising in the theory of Berkovich spaces
[
Ber90
,
Ber93
]
and dagger
spaces
[
GK00
,
Stu22
]
; see
Theorem 1.1
for a precise statement. We first provide some context and motivation for our main result. For a non-Archimedean field
(k,|\cdot|)
of characteristic
p
, reduced affinoid algebras over
k
are excellent
[
Kie69
]
but not
F
-finite, essentially of finite type over an excellent local ring, or
F
-pure in general. Thus, it is natural to ask whether such rings have big test elements. The known proofs of the existence of big test elements for reduced
F
-finite Noetherian rings and reduced rings essentially of finite type over an excellent local ring rely on the existence of non-zero
R
-linear maps
R^{1/p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }R

[
HH90
,
HH94
]
. In fact, work of Aberbach implies that any excellent reduced
R
with a non-zero
R
linear map
R^{1/p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }R
has test elements
[
Abe93
]
. However, in
[
DM20
]
, the first author and Murayama give examples of non-Archimedean fields
k
of characteristic
p>0
for which the Tate algebra
T_{n}(k)
has no non-zero
T_{n}(k)
-linear maps
T_{n}(k)^{1/p}\xrightarrow{}T_{n}(k)
for all integers
n>0
. Consequently, some of the previous strategies that were used to show the existence of test elements do not appear to apply to homomorphic images of
T_{n}(k)
, i.e., the
affinoid algebras
.

A different path forward is suggested by the work of Sharp
[
Sha12
]
, who shows that if a reduced
R
is a homomorphic image of an excellent regular ring
S
of characteristic
p
such that
S^{1/p}
as an
S
-module satisfies a technical property known as
intersection flatness
, then
R
has big test elements. For the expert, we note that the intersection flatness condition is equivalent to
S^{1/p}
being a flat and Mittag-Leffler
S
-module (see
[
DEST25
,
DET23
]
). In
[
DEST25
]
, the first and third authors, Epstein and Schwede use Sharp’s result to show that
T_{n}(k)^{1/p}
is intersection flat over
T_{n}(k)
for any non-Archimedean field
k
of characteristic
p
, thereby establishing that reduced affinoid algebras of prime characteristic have big test elements. Their method uses functional analytic properties of non-Archimedean fields, topological properties of Tate algebras and a robust theory of intersection flatness developed by the first and third authors and Epstein
[
DET23
]
, building on and unifying results obtained in
[
RG71
,
OR72
,
HJ21
]
.

The affinoid algebras alluded to above occur in Tate’s theory of
rigid analytic spaces

[
Tat71
]
.
However, there are other theories of analytic geometry that give rise to excellent rings that are not affinoid in the sense of Tate. First, there is the theory of
Berkovich Spaces
, in which Berkovich defines a variant
T_{n,\rho}(k)
of the Tate algebra, for any
n
-tuple of positive real numbers
\rho=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{n})

[
Ber90
,
Ber93
]
. If
\rho\in(\Gamma_{k})_{>0}^{n}
, where
\Gamma_{k}
is the value group of the non-Archimedean field
k
, then
T_{n,\rho}(k)
is affinoid in the sense of Tate. Moreover, if
\rho=(1,\dots,1)
, then
T_{n,\rho}(k)=T_{n}(k)
, the classical Tate algebra. However, if
\rho\notin\Gamma_{k}^{n}
, then
T_{n,\rho}(k)
is a new type of excellent
[
Duc09
]
regular ring. We will call homomorphic images of
T_{n,\rho}(k)

affinoid algebras in the sense of Berkovich
, or simply
Berkovich affinoid algebras
. Berkovich spaces have witnessed many application in rigid geometry, including the proof of the local Langlands theorem
[
HTT01
]
, applications to resolution of singularities
[
Tem13
]
, and an early version of perfectoid geometry
[
KL15
]
.

Secondly, related to the theory of Berkovich spaces is the theory of
dagger spaces
, or analytic spaces with overconvergent structure sheaves. The algebraic building blocks of dagger spaces are the rings
T_{n}(k)^{\dagger}\coloneqq\colim_{r\in(\Gamma_{k})_{>1}}T_{n,\rho_{r}}(k)
, where
\rho_{r}=(r,\dots,r)
. These rings appear to have been first considered by Güntzer
[
Gün67
]
and can be interpreted as rings of analytic functions which converge on a polydisk of some radius larger than
1
. Homomorphic images of
T_{n}(k)^{\dagger}
are called
dagger affinoid algebras
. Unlike
T_{n}(k)
and
T_{n,\rho}(k)
,
T_{n}(k)^{\dagger}
is not a Banach
k
-algebra since it is not complete with respect to the canonical Gauss norm that one can define on it. Nevertheless, one can use dagger affinoid algebras to mimic other constructions in non-Archimedean analytic geometry. For example, Grosse-Klönne describes
a version of the theory of Tate’s rigid analytic
spaces in terms of dagger affinoid algebras
[
GK00
]

. Dagger affinoid algebras have been used in the theory of rigid cohomology
[
LS07
,
Stu22
]
, and for computing zeta functions of varieties of finite fields
[
Ked01
]
.

Our main result shows that big test elements exist for reduced Berkovich affinoids and reduced dagger affinoids. In fact, we obtain:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
Theorem 3.5
,
Section 3
,
Section 4
)

Let
(k,|\cdot|)
be a non-Archimedean field of characteristic
p>0
. Then for all integers
n>0
, and indeterminates
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
and
\underline{y}=y_{1},\dots,y_{s}
we have:

(a)

(T_{n,\rho}(k)[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket)^{1/p}
is an intersection flat
T_{n,\rho}(k)[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket
-algebra.

(b)

(T_{n}(k)^{\dagger}[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket)^{1/p}
is an intersection flat
T_{n}(k)^{\dagger}[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket
-algebra.

Consequently, if
R
is a reduced ring that is essentially of finite type over
T_{n,\rho}(k)
or
T_{n}(k)^{\dagger}
and
I
is any ideal of
R
(including
I=0
), then the
I
-adic completion
\widehat{R}^{I}
has big test elements.

For the first case, we adapt the methods of
[
DEST25
]
,
which establishes the intersection flatness of
T_{n}(k)[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket^{1/p}
over
T_{n}(k)[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket
, to the setting of
T_{n,\rho}(k)[\underline{x}]\llbracket\underline{y}\rrbracket
. For the second case, we use a descent argument. Along the way, we give a detailed proof of the excellence of dagger algebras for the reader’s convenience, since this fact is claimed without proof in
[
GK00
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

For a ring
A
, an ideal
I
and an
A
-module
M
, we always let
\widehat{M}^{I}
denote the
I
-adic completion of
M
. If
A
is Noetherian, then it is well-known that the canonical map
A\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{A}^{I}
is flat. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
A
containing
I
, then
\mathfrak{p}\widehat{A}^{I}
is a prime ideal of
\widehat{A}^{I}
(containing
I\widehat{A}^{I}
) such that
\widehat{A}^{I}/\mathfrak{p}\widehat{A}^{I}\cong A/\mathfrak{p}
. Indeed, this follows upon applying
\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}^{I}
to the exact sequence of finitely generated
A
-modules
0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathfrak{p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }A\xrightarrow{\ \ }A/\mathfrak{p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0
, and noting that
A/\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}^{I}\cong\widehat{A/\mathfrak{p}}^{I}\cong A/\mathfrak{p}
. Conversely, if
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(\widehat{A}^{I})
such that
\mathfrak{q}\supset I\widehat{A}^{I}
, then since the induced map
A/I\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{A}^{I}/I\widehat{A}^{I}
is an isomorphism, it follows that
\mathfrak{q}
is expanded from a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
that contains
I
, and hence,
\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\cong\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

2.1.
Regular ring maps and excellent rings

Definition 2.1
.

[
Sta
,
Tag 07BZ
]

A ring homomophism
A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
is
regular
if the map is flat and if for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(A)
, the fiber
B\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a Noetherian geometrically regular
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra.

We will frequently use Popescu’s desingularization theorem for regular maps of Noetherian rings.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Pop85
,
Pop86
,
Pop90
]

Let
\varphi\colon A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
be a flat map of Noetherian rings. Then
\varphi
is regular if and only if
B
can be expressed as a filtered colimit of smooth
A
-algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring. We say
A
is

(a)

a
G
-ring
or a
Grothendieck ring
if for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(A)
, the canonical completion map
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is regular.

(b)

a
quasi-excellent ring
if
A
is a
G
-ring and if every finite type
A
-algebra has an open regular locus.

(c)

an
excellent ring
if
A
is quasi-excellent and universally catenary.

Remark 2.4
.

Since Cohen-Macaulay rings are universally catenary
[
Sta
,
Tag 00NM
]
, a quasi-excellent ring that is a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay ring is automatically excellent.

The property of being geometrically regular can be tested at maximal ideals provided that the domain is a
G
-ring. This is known as the
Grothendieck localization problem
for geometric regularity
[
And74
]
. The precise statement is:

Theorem 2.5
.

[
And74
, Thm. on Pg 297]

Let
\varphi\colon(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A},\kappa_{A})\xrightarrow{\ \ }(B,\mathfrak{m}_{B},\kappa_{B})
be a flat local homomorphism of Noetherian local rings such that
B/\mathfrak{m}_{A}B
is a geometrically regular
\kappa_{A}
-algebra. If
A
is a
G
-ring then
\varphi
is a regular map.

A non-local corollary of the above result that will be useful for us is:

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
be a flat map of Noetherian rings. Suppose for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
B
, if
\mathfrak{p}\coloneqq\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{m})
, then
B\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a geometrically regular
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra. If
A
is a
G
-ring, then
\varphi
is a regular map.

Proof.

Since
B
is Noetherian, by
[
Sta
,
Tag 07C0, part (3)
]
it suffices to show that the induced local homomorphism
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }B_{\mathfrak{m}}
is regular. Note that
B_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a geometrically regular
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra because it is a further localization of
B\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
. Thus, since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a
G
-ring, we get
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }B_{\mathfrak{m}}
is regular by
Theorem 2.5
.
∎

We record an application of the previous corollary we will need later.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
be a regular map of Noetherian rings such that
A
is a
G
-ring. If
I
is an ideal of
A
, then the induced map on
I
-adic completion
\widehat{\varphi}^{I}\colon\widehat{A}^{I}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{B}^{IB}

Proof.

Since
B
is
A
-flat,
\widehat{B}^{IB}
is
\widehat{A}^{I}
-flat by
[
Sta
,
Tag 0AGW
]
. By
Section 2.1
it suffices to show that if
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
\widehat{B}^{IB}
and
\mathfrak{p}\coloneqq\mathfrak{m}\cap\widehat{A}^{I}
, then
\widehat{B}^{IB}\otimes_{\widehat{A}^{I}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a geometrically regular
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra. Note that
I\widehat{B}^{IB}
is contained in the Jacobson radical of
\widehat{B}^{IB}

[
Sta

Tag 05GI

\mathfrak{m}\supset I\widehat{B}^{IB}
, and hence,
\mathfrak{p}\supset I\widehat{A}^{I}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{p}
must be expanded from a prime ideal

of

\widehat{B}^{IB}\otimes_{\widehat{A}^{I}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong(\widehat{B}^{IB}\otimes_{\widehat{A}^{I}}\widehat{A}^{I}/I\widehat{A}^{I})\otimes_{\widehat{A}^{I}/I\widehat{A}^{I}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong B/IB\otimes_{A/I}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\cong B\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})

Remark 2.8
.

If
\varphi\colon A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
is a faithfully flat map of Noetherian rings and

is an ideal of

, then
\widehat{\varphi}^{I}\colon\widehat{A}^{I}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{B}^{IB}
is faithfully flat as well. Indeed, as above, flatness of
\widehat{\varphi}^{I}
follows by
[

,
Tag 0AGW

\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
\widehat{A}^{I}

\mathfrak{m}\widehat{B}^{IB}\neq\widehat{B}^{IB}
. But

is contained in the Jacobson radical of
\widehat{A}^{I}
, so
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\widehat{A}^{I}

\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is a maximal ideal of

such that
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\supset I
. Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(B)

,
Tag 0GIM

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(I,\leq)
be a directed poset. Let

and

Since smoothness is preserved under arbitrary base change, for all

-algebras. Now,

Hence we have reduced the lemma to showing that if

is a ring and

is a system of rings indexed by

such that each

is a filtered colimit of smooth

-algebras, then

We record a descent result for the (quasi)-excellence property. A proof is included for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
be a faithfully flat regular map of rings. If
B
is quasi-excellent, then
A
is quasi-excellent. If
B
is quasi-excellent and Cohen-Macaulay, then

is excellent.

Proof.

The property of being Noetherian satisfies faithfully flat descent. Thus, quasi-excellence of
B
implies

is Noetherian. We first show that the property of being a
G
-ring satisfies faithfully flat descent. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(A)
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(B)
such that
\mathfrak{q}\cap A=\mathfrak{p}
. The induced map
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }B_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular by
[
Sta
,
Tag 07C0
]
. Then consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{\mathfrak{q}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{B_{\mathfrak{q}}}},}

where the bottom horizontal arrow is the induced map on completions. If
B
is a
G
-ring then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{B_{\mathfrak{q}}}
is a regular ring map by
[
Sta
,
Tag 07QI
]
. Since
\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\widehat{B_{\mathfrak{q}}}
is faithfully flat,

must be regular by
[
Sta
,
Tag 07NT
]
. Thus,

is a
G
-ring.

Next note that if
A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B
is a faithfully flat regular map of Noetherian rings, then the image of
\Reg(B)
under the induced map on
\Spec
is
\Reg(A)
. Indeed, since the property of being regular satisfies faithfully flat descent, it is clear that
\Spec(\varphi)(\Reg(B))\subset\Reg(A)
. Conversely, let
\mathfrak{p}\in\Reg(A)
. Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(B)
such that

. Then as above,

Let

is also regular

,

2.2.
Tate algebras and generalizations

Recall that a
non-Archimedean norm
on a field
k
is a function

|\cdot|\colon k\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

that satisfies the following properties:

\bullet

|x|=0
if and only if
x=0
,

\bullet

|xy|=|x||y|
, and

\bullet

|x+y|\leq\max\{|x|,|y|\}
.

In other words,
|\cdot|
is a non-Archimedean multiplicative valuation of
k
of rank
1
.
A field
k
equipped with a non-Archimedean norm
|\cdot|
is called a
real-valued field
and is denoted
(k,|\cdot|)
.

The valuation ring of
k
is the subring
k^{\circ}\coloneqq\{x\in k\colon|x|\leq 1\}
. This is a local ring with maximal ideal
k^{\circ\circ}\coloneqq\{x\in k\colon|x|<1\}
. Note that
k^{\circ}
has Krull dimension
1
.

Definition 2.13
.

A
non-Archimedean
(abbrev. NA) field is a real-valued field
(k,|\cdot|)
such that
k
is complete with respect to the metric
|x-y|
that is induced by
|\cdot|
and such that
|k^{\times}|\neq{1}
, that is,
k
is non-trivially valued.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
(k,|\cdot|)
be a NA field. For every positive integer
n
, the
Tate algebra
in
n
indeterminates over
k
, denoted,
T_{n}(k)
, is the
k
-subalgebra of the formal power series ring
k\llbracket X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rrbracket
consisting of those power series

\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{\nu}X^{\nu}

(written in multi-index notation) such that
|a_{\nu}|\xrightarrow{\ \ }0
as
\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\infty
. An element of
T_{n}(k)
is called a
restricted power series
. A homomorphic image of
T_{n}(k)
is called a
strictly affinoid
k
-algebra
or
strictly
k
-affinoid
.

Remark 2.15
.

What we call “strictly affinoid
k
-algebras” are often simply called
affinoid
k
-algebras
. The term “strictly” tends to be used in the literature on Berkovich spaces
[
Ber90
,
Ber93
]
. On the other hand, when dealing with other theories of
p
-adic analytic geometry, like Huber’s adic spaces, it is more common to use the terminology ”affinoid
k
-algebra”. Since affinoid
k
-algebras in the sense of Berkovich will play a prominent role in this paper, we will follow Berkovich’s practice of calling homomorphic images of
T_{n}(k)
strictly
k
-affinoid.

The Tate algebra is a
k
-Banach algebra (i.e. a complete normed
k
-algebra) with respect to the
Gauss norm
, which is defined as follows: for all

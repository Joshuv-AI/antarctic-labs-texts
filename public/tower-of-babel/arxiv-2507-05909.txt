Universal Coacting Hopf Algebra of a finite-dimensional Algebra over an Operad
Saikat Goswami, Satyendra Kumar Mishra, Suman Pattanayak

Abstract
. A. L. Agore and G. Militaru constructed a new invariant (a “universal coacting Hopf algebra”) for some finite-dimensional binary quadratic algebras such as Lie/Leibniz algebras, associative algebras, and Poisson algebras with prominent applications. In our recent work, we extended their construction from the binary case to Lie-Yamaguti algebras (an algebra with a binary and a ternary bracket). In this paper, we give a construction of universal coacting bi/Hopf algebra for any finite-dimensional algebra over a symmetric operad 𝒫 \mathcal{P} . Precisely, we construct a universal algebra 𝒞 ⁡ ( 𝔞 ) \mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}) for a finite-dimensional 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -algebra 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . Furthermore, we show that the category of finite dimensional 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -algebras is enriched over the dual category of commutative algebras. This enrichment gives a unique bialgebra structure on the universal algebra 𝒞 ⁡ ( 𝔞 ) \mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}) , making it a universal coacting bialgebra of the 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -algebra 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . Subsequently, we obtain a universal coacting Hopf algebra of the 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -algebra 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . We also show that universal coacting Hopf algebra constructed here coincides with the existing cases of Lie/Leibniz, Poisson, and associative algebras. Furthermore, our operadic approach helps us construct a universal coacting algebra for algebras over a graded symmetric operad (graded algebras with finite-dimensional homogeneous components). This allows us to discuss the universal constructions for k k -ary quadratic algebras and graded algebras like graded Leibniz, graded Poisson algebras, Gerstenhaber algebras, BV algebras, etc. In the end, we characterize 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -algebra automorphisms in terms of the invertible group-like elements of the finite dual bialgebra 𝒞 ⁡ ( 𝔞 ) \mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}) . We also give a characterization of the abelian group gradings of finite dimensional 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -algebras.

1.
Introduction

Operads are structures that formalize the concept of operations with multiple inputs. They provide a framework to study and encode various types of algebras. The term “operad” was first introduced by J. P. May
[
18
]
, though its origins trace back to Lazard’s work
[
13
]
. Operads have played a key role in the study of loop spaces, with notable contributions from topologists such as M. Boardman and R. Vogt
[
8
]
, J. P. May
[
17
]
, and J. Stasheff
[
21
]
. Beyond topology, operads have found applications in differential geometry, quantum field theory, category theory, string topology, and combinatorics
[
18
,
19
,
25
]
.
There are multiple equivalent ways to define an operad, but the monoidal definition is particularly straightforward to state. Let
\mathrm{Vect}
denote the category of vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathrm{Func_{Vect}}
the category of endofunctors on
\mathrm{Vect}
. An algebraic operad
\mathcal{P}
is a monoid
(\mathcal{P},\gamma,\eta)
in the category
\mathrm{Func_{Vect}}
. The other equivalent descriptions of operads, can be found in
[
14
, Chapter 5]
. In this paper, we focus on symmetric (graded) operads, which we will explicitly define in the next section. Representations of an operad
\mathcal{P}
are called algebras over
\mathcal{P}
or
\mathcal{P}
-algebras. Classical algebras such as associative, Lie, and commutative algebras can be defined as algebras over operads
\mathcal{A}ss,~\mathcal{C}om,
and
\mathcal{L}ie
, respectively. Other operads, such as those encoding pre-Lie, Leibniz, Zinbiel, perm, and Poisson algebras can be found in
[
14
, Chapter 13]
. The operadic approach offers new insights, even for classical algebras, by treating different types of algebras within a unified framework.

Given two unital associative algebras
A
,
B
over a field
\mathbb{K}
, a coalgebra measuring from
A
to
B
is a pair
(C,\phi)
, where
C
is a coalgebra over
\mathbb{K}
and
\phi:C\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map such that

\phi(c)(ab)~=~\sum\phi(c_{(1)})(a)\cdot\phi(c_{(2)})(b)\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(c)(1_{A})~=~\varepsilon(c)1_{B}\quad\mbox{for all }c\in C,

where
a,b\in A,~\Delta(c)=\sum c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
. A coalgebra measuring
(C,\phi)
can be seen as a generalization of algebra homomorphisms from
A
to
B
, since for any group-like element
c\in C
,
\phi(c):A\rightarrow B
is an algebra homomorphism. M. E. Sweedler
[
22
]
first introduced a universal measuring coalgebra for associative algebras. This universal construction leads to an enrichment of the category of associative algebras over the monoidal category of cocommutative coalgebras and finally, associates a natural universal acting Hopf algebra to an algebra (see
[
22
, Chapter 7]
). Recent articles
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
studied measurings between monoids, algebras over operads, Hopf algebroids and discussed the associated enrichments and other applications. The dual notion of an existing notion is equally important in Hopf algebra theory
[
22
]
. Yu. I. Manin
[
15
]
and D. Tambara
[
23
]
dualised Sweedler’s universal construction and called it ‘universal coacting bi/Hopf algebra’. These objects have many applications in non-commutative algebraic geometry
[
20
]
. The universal coacting algebra exists only for finite-dimensional algebras.
In last five years, there are several successful attempts to explicitly construct a universal coacting bi/Hopf algebra for associative algebras
[
16
]
, Lie and Leibniz algebras
[
1
]
, Poisson algebras
[
3
]
, and Lie-Yamaguti algebras
[
9
]
. These constructions provided tools to solve hard and complex problems in the literature such as: 1. characterizing the group of automorphisms of an algebra, and 2. characterizing the abelian group gradings on an algebra. We also refer the reader to a construction of universal (co)acting objects with generalized duality results in the case of
\Omega
-algebras
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we consider finite-dimensional algebras over a symmetric operad and construct a universal coacting algebra unifying the different constructions
[
1
,
3
,
9
,
16
]
in literature. We show that the category of finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebras is enriched over the dual category of commutative algebras. Consequently, we obtain a universal coacting bi/Hopf algebra for any finite-dimensional algebra over
\mathcal{P}
. We also consider the notion of graded operads and algebras over a graded operad to construct a universal algebra for graded algebras. We explicitly show that our universal coacting bi/Hopf algebra coincides with the universal coacting bi/Hopf algebra of Lie/Leibniz algebras
[
1
]
, Poisson algebras
[
3
]
, and associative algebras
[
16
]
when the underlying operad
\mathcal{P}
is
\mathcal{L}ie/\mathcal{L}eib
,
\mathcal{P}ois
, and
\mathcal{A}ss
respectively. In a sequel, we also discuss the universal constructions in the special cases of some (graded) quadratic algebras such as Zinbiel algebras, pre-Lie algebras, perm algebras,
k
-ary quadratic algebras, graded Lie algebras, graded Poisson algebras, Gerstenhaber algebras, Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras, and Loday type algebras. In the end, we give a complete description of automorphism group of a finite dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra and characterize the abelian group gradings on any finite dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra.

2.
Preliminaries

In the section, we recall the all the necessary definitions and notions related to operads and graded operads, that we use throughout this article.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\mathbb{S}
-module
over
\mathbb{K}
is a family

\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}(0),\mathcal{P}(1),\ldots,\mathcal{P}(n),\ldots)
of right
\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{S}_{n}]
-modules
\mathcal{P}(n)
. The
tensor product
of two
\mathbb{S}
-modules
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
is given by

\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{Q}(n):=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}\mathcal{P}(i)\otimes\mathcal{Q}(j)\otimes\mathbb{K}[Sh(i,j)]

where
Sh(i,j)
denotes the set of all
(i,j)
-shuffles of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
. The category of
\mathbb{S}
-modules, denoted by
\mathbb{S}
-mod, forms a monoidal category given by the following
composition product

\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{Q}(n):=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{P}(k)\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_{k}}\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes k}

The notation
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes k}
denotes the tensor product of
k
copies of the
\mathbb{S}
-module
\mathcal{Q}
. The unit for the composition is given by
\mathrm{I}=(0,\mathbb{K},0,\cdots)
.
See
[
14
, Section 5.1, pg 123]
for further details regarding
\mathbb{S}
-modules and their products.

Definition 2.2

(
Monoidal Definition).

An
operad

\mathcal{P}
is a monoid in the monoidal category of
\mathbb{S}
-modules. More precisely, it is an
\mathbb{S}
-module
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
endowed with a morphisms of
\mathbb{S}
-module

\gamma:\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta:\mathrm{I}\to\mathcal{P}

called composition map and unit map respectively, which makes
\mathcal{P}
into a monoid.
For a more detailed description of the monoidal definition we refer the readers to
[
14
, Section 5.2.1, pg 131]
.

There are several other equivalent definitions of an operad, including the classical, combinatorial, and partial definitions. We describe the partial definition below, while the classical and combinatorial definitions can be found in
[
14
, pg 142 and pg 161]
, respectively.

Definition 2.3

(
Partial Definition).

An
operad

\mathcal{P}
is a sequence
\{\mathcal{P}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of vector spaces, together with partial composition maps

\circ_{i}:\mathcal{P}(m)\times\mathcal{P}(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(m-1+n)

for
1\leq i\leq m
and
n\geq 0
, along with a unit element
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathcal{P}(1)
satisfying the following conditions

•

(Associativity) for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(m)
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(n)
, and
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(r)

(2.1)

(\mu\circ_{i}\nu)\circ_{j}\eta=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mu\circ_{j}\eta)\circ_{i+j-1}\nu&1\leq j\leq i-1,\\
\mu\circ_{i}(\nu\circ_{j-i+1}\eta)&i\leq j\leq m+i-1,\\
(\mu\circ_{j-m+1}\eta)\circ_{i}\nu&m+i\leq j\leq m-1+n.\end{array}\end{cases}

•

(Equivariance) Each
\mathcal{P}(n)
has a right action of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
,
\cdot:\mathcal{P}(n)\times\mathbb{S}_{n}\to\mathcal{P}(n)
, denoted by
(\mu,\sigma)\mapsto\mu\cdot\sigma
, such that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(n)
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(m)
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{m}

(2.2)

(\mu\cdot\sigma)\circ_{i}(\nu\cdot\tau)=(\mu\circ_{i}\nu)\cdot(\sigma\circ_{i}\tau)

where
\sigma\circ_{i}\tau
is the block permutation, with the
i^{th}
entry of
\sigma
being replaced by the entire block
\tau
.

•

(Unitality) There exists a unique element
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathcal{P}(1)
such that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(n)

(\text{with}~n\geq 1)
, and
1\leq i\leq n
,

(2.3)

\mu\circ_{i}\mathbbm{1}~=~\mu~=~\mathbbm{1}\circ_{1}\mu.

A pictorial description of the above mentioned conditions can be found in
[
14
, Section 5.3.4, pg 146]
.

Let us consider a fundamental example to illustrate the basic idea of operads: the endomorphism operad, associated with a given vector space serves as a key example to understand the operadic composition.

Example 2.4

(
Endomorphism operad).

For any vector space
\mathfrak{a}
, the endomorphism operad
\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is given by

(2.4)

\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{a}}(n):=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes n},\mathfrak{a})\quad\text{for all}~n\geq 0,

where
\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes n}
denotes the
n
-fold tensor product of
\mathfrak{a}
, and by convention
\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes 0}=\mathbb{K}
. The partial composition maps given by
\circ_{i}:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes n},\mathfrak{a})\times\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes m},\mathfrak{a})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes m-1+n},\mathfrak{a})
are defined as follows:

(2.5)

f\circ_{i}g(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1+n}):=f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+m-1}),a_{i+m},\ldots,a_{m-1+n})

for all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1+n}\in\mathfrak{a}
. The right action
\cdot:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes n},\mathfrak{a})\times\mathbb{S}_{n}\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes n},\mathfrak{a})
is given by

(2.6)

(f\cdot\sigma)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}):=f(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(n)})

and the unit element
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity map
\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{a}}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{a}}(1)
.

Example 2.5
.

Consider a constant sequence of the field
\mathbb{K}
as follows:

Com(n):=\mathbb{K}\quad\text{for all }n\geq 0.

together with partial compositions,
\mathbb{S}_{n}
right action, and unit element described below.

Partial composition:
For any
a\in Com(m)=\mathbb{K}
and
b\in Com(n)=\mathbb{K}
define

(the usual multiplication of

The associativity of the partial composition maps follows from the associativity and commutativity of the multiplication in
\mathbb{K}

\mathbb{S}_{n}

The right action of
\mathbb{S}_{n}

Unit element:

For any

With this notation

\mathcal{C}om

Example 2.6
.

Let

.

Consider a sequence of vector spaces over

Remark 2.10

Any algebra over the operad

5.3.
Algebras over non-symmetric operads

Any given non-symmetric operad
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}(0),\mathcal{P}(1),\ldots)
, one can construct a symmetric operad
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
given as follows:

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(n):=\mathcal{P}(n)\otimes\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{S}_{n}],

where the action of the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(n)
is given by the regular representation
\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{S}_{n}]
. The association
\mathcal{P}\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
is refer to as “symmetrization”. Furthermore, we note that the category of algebras over the non-symmetric operad
\mathcal{P}
and over its associated symmetric operad
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
are the same. See
[
14
, Section 5.9.11]
for further details. Thus, using symmetrization our universal constructions also holds for algebras over non-symmetric operads.

We refer the readers to
[
7
]
for examples of non-symmetric operads that encode various Loday-type algebras such as dialgebras, associative trialgebras, dendriform algebras, dendriform trialgebras, quadri algebras, and ennea algebras. Consequently, using symmetrization to obtain the associated symmetric operads for the operads encoding Loday-type algebras, we can construct the universal algebras for these Loday-type algebras using the results obtained in Section
3
.

5.4.
Algebras over graded operads

Here, we consider examples of graded algebras and operads encoding them. Precisely, we give the construction of universal coacting algebras for graded Lie/Leibniz algebras, graded Poisson algebras, Garstenhaber algebras, and Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras.

5.4.1.
Graded Leibniz algebras

A graded Leibniz algebra is a graded vector space
\mathfrak{H}=\oplus_{p\geq 0}\mathfrak{H_{p}}
equipped with a degree
0
binary bracket “
[\cdot,\cdot]
”, called the graded Leibniz bracket. It satisfies the following identity

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,[x,z]],

where
|x|
and
|y|
denotes the degree of the homogeneous elements
x
and
y
, respectively. This identity is known as the graded Leibniz identity.

The operad
\mathcal{L}eib
encodes the category of graded Leibniz algebras. Note that we can consider the operad
\mathcal{L}eib
as a graded operad concentrated at degree zero in every arity. Then, a graded Leibniz algebra structure on a graded vector space
\mathfrak{H}=\oplus_{p\geq 0}\mathfrak{H_{p}}
is given by a graded operad morphism
\gamma:\mathcal{L}eib\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{H}}
.

The following corollary to Theorem
3.9
naturally extends the universal construction of A. L. Agore and G. Militaru
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
to the graded context.

Corollary 5.20
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}=\oplus_{p\geq 0}\mathfrak{H}_{p}
be a graded Leibniz algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Then the functor
\mathfrak{H}\otimes-:\mathrm{g}\text{-}\mathrm{ComAlg}\to\mathrm{g}\text{-}\mathrm{Leib}
admits a left adjoint if and only if each component
\mathfrak{H}_{p}
of
\mathfrak{H}
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

(
\implies
)
Since each component
\mathfrak{H}_{p}
of
\mathfrak{H}=\oplus_{p\geq 0}\mathfrak{H}_{p}
is finite-dimensional, let

\{a_{01},\ldots,a_{0d_{0}};a_{11},\ldots,a_{1d_{1}};\ldots;a_{p1},\ldots,a_{pd_{p}};\ldots\}

be a vector space basis of
\mathfrak{H}
, where
\{a_{p1},\ldots,a_{pd_{p}}\}
denotes a vector space basis of the
p
-degree component
\mathfrak{H}_{p}
. The structure constants of
\mathfrak{H}
are obtained as follows: for any
p,q\geq 0

(5.25)

[a_{pi},a_{qj}]=\sum_{s=1}^{d_{p+q}}\alpha_{pi,qj}^{\theta,s}~a_{(p+q)s}

where
\theta=p+q
,
1\leq i\leq d_{p}
, and
1\leq j\leq d_{q}
. Consequently, the set of scalars given below yields the structure constants of
\mathfrak{H}
:

\bigcup_{p,q\geq 0}\{\alpha_{pi,qj}^{\theta,s}~|~\theta=p+1;~1\leq i\leq d_{p};~1\leq j\leq d_{q};~1\leq s\leq d_{\theta}\}.

Now, for any object
\mathfrak{G}\in\mathrm{g}\text{-}\mathrm{Leib}
we shall construct a graded commutative algebra
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G})
.
Let
\{b_{pi}:p\geq 0,i\in I_{p}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{G}
. Then, for any
p,q\geq 0
,
i\in I_{p}
, and
j\in I_{q}
there exists a finite set
B_{pi,qj}\subset I_{p+q}
for which

(5.26)

[b_{pi},b_{qj}]~=\sum_{u\in B_{pi,qj}}\beta_{pi,qj}^{\theta,u}~b_{(p+q)u},

where
\theta=p+q
.
Let
\mathbb{K}\big[X_{si}^{(\pi)}:s,i,\pi\geq 0\big]
be the usual graded commutative polynomial algebra with indeterminants
X^{(\pi)}_{si}
of cohomological degree
\pi
. We define

(5.27)

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G}):=\mathbb{K}\big[X_{si}^{(\pi)}:s,i,\pi\geq 0\big]\big/J

where
J
is the ideal generated by the polynomials of the following form: for any

5.4.2.

5.4.5.
Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras

The notion of Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) algebras appear in diverse places of mathematics, such as they appear in differential geometry of polyvector fields, on the Hochschild cohomology of unital cyclic associative algebras, on the homology of free loop spaces, on vertex operator algebras, etc. A BV algebra structure is given by a data
(\mathfrak{G},\cdot,\Delta)
where
\mathfrak{G}
is a graded vector space equipped with a graded commutative product
``\cdot"
of degree
0
, and a unary operation
\Delta
of degree
1
satisfying
\Delta\circ\Delta=0
. Furthermore, these two operation satisfy the following compatibility relation:

\Delta(x\cdot y\cdot z)-\Delta(x\cdot y)\cdot z-(-1)^{|y|(|x|+|z|)}\Delta(z\cdot x)\cdot y-(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}\Delta(y\cdot z)\cdot x\\
+\Delta(a)\cdot b\cdot c+(-1)^{|y|(|x|+|z|)}\Delta(z)\cdot x\cdot y+(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|)}\Delta(y)\cdot z\cdot x=0,

for any homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{G}
. Note that, the data of a BV algebra structure
(\mathfrak{G},\cdot,\Delta)
is equivalent to that of a Garstenhaber algebra structure
(\mathfrak{G},\cdot,[-,-])
endowed with a square zero unary operation
\Delta
of degree
1
such that the following equality holds:

[-,-]=\Delta(-\cdot-)-\big(\Delta(-)\cdot-\big)-\big(-\cdot\Delta(-)\big).

The operad corresponding the category of all BV algebras is denoted by
\mathcal{BV}:=\mathbb{T}(E_{\mathcal{BV}})/(R_{\mathcal{BV}})
. The graded
\mathbb{S}
-module is given as follows,
E_{\mathcal{BV}}:=(0,\mathbb{K}\Delta,\mathbb{K}m,\ldots)
, where
\mathbb{K}\Delta
is a one-dimensional graded vector space (
\mathbb{S}_{1}
-module) concentrated in degree
1
and
\mathbb{K}m
is a one-dimensional graded vector space (
\mathbb{S}_{2}
-module) concentrated in degree
0
. The space of relations
R_{\mathcal{BV}}
is the
\mathbb{K}[\mathbb{S}_{3}]
-module generated by

\begin{cases}m\circ_{1}m-m\circ_{2}m,~\Delta\circ_{1}\Delta,\\
\Delta\circ_{1}m\circ_{1}m-\big((\Delta\circ_{1}m)\circ_{1}m\big)^{\mathrm{id}+(123)+(132)}+\big(\Delta\circ_{1}(m\circ_{1}m)\big)^{\mathrm{id}+(123)+(132)}.\end{cases}

Finally, as a corollary of Theorem
3.9
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 5.25
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}=\oplus_{p\geq 0}\mathfrak{G}_{p}
be a BV algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Then the functor
\mathfrak{G}\otimes-:\mathrm{g}\text{-}\mathrm{ComAlg}\to\mathcal{BV}
admits a left adjoint if and only if each component
\mathfrak{G}_{p}
of
\mathfrak{G}
is finite-dimensional.

Remark 5.26
.

The existence and description of universal (co)acting bialgebras and Hopf algebras, within the category of unital associative algebras, have been studied in the setting of
\Omega
-algebras in
[
4
]
. While
\mathcal{P}
-algebras considered in this work can be seen as a special case of
\Omega
-algebras (by forgetting the symmetries of the generating operations and the S-module structure spanned by the generating operations of a
\mathcal{P}
-algebra), the existence of the universal algebra
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
, associated to two
\mathcal{P}
-algebras
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
as introduced here, does not follow from the results of
[
4
]
.
This is primarily because the algebra
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is required to be universal within the category of commutative algebras. Our focus on this category is motivated by its significance in understanding the automorphism groups of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras and in the classification of abelian group gradings on such algebras, as discussed in Section
6

The structured framework of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras is also useful to accommodate a wide range of important examples, including graded Lie and Leibniz algebras, graded Poisson algebras, Gerstenhaber algebras, and Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) algebras by using graded operads instead of operads.

6.1.

\mathcal{P}

Let

The set

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
be the finite dual bialgebra of the bialgebra

[

, Theorem 3.1]
for finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebras.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra and
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})
be its universal coacting bialgebra. Then, the group of
\mathcal{P}
-algebra automorphisms of
\mathfrak{a}
is isomorphic to the group of all invertible group-like elements
U(G(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})^{\times}))
.

Proof.

It follows that the set
G(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})^{\times})
is the same as the set of algebra homomorphisms from
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})
to
\mathbb{K}
.
The bijection given in Equation (
3.17
) implies that we have a bijection

\Theta:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{P}\text{-Alg}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}),\quad\mbox{defined by }\Theta(\Phi)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)\circ\eta_{\mathfrak{a}}.

The vector space
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K})
is a monoid with respect to the composition of maps. A monoid structure on
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K})=G(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})^{\times})
is given by the convolution product, i.e.,

\Phi_{1}\star\Phi_{2}(x_{ij}):=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\Phi_{1}(x_{is})\Phi_{2}(x_{sj}).

It immediately follows that
\Theta
is an isomorphism of monoids and thus it induces an isomorphism between the invertible elements in the monoids. If
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\}
is a basis of the vector space
\mathfrak{a}
, then the isomorphism between the invertible elements in the monoids is given by the map

\zeta:U(G(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})^{\times}))\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{P}\text{-Alg}}(\mathfrak{a}),\quad\zeta(\Phi)(a_{i}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(x_{ji})a_{j}.

Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.

\mathcal{P}\text{-algebra }\mathfrak{a}.\end{array}}

Proposition 6.3
.

There exists a bijection between the set of all
G
-gradings on the finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra
\mathfrak{a}
and the set
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComBiAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K}[G])
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the bijections given by (
6.1
) and (
6.3
).
∎

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{\sigma\in G}\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{\sigma\in G}\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
be two
G
-gradings on the finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra
\mathfrak{a}
. Then the two gradings are said to be isomorphic if we have a
\mathcal{P}
-algebra automorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}
such that
\phi(\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma})=\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
.

Next, we define a equivalence relation on the set
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComBiAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K}[G])
.

Definition 6.5
.

The morphisms
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComBiAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K}[G])
are called conjugates if there exists an element
g\in U(G(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})^{\times}))
such that
\Phi_{2}=g\star\Phi_{1}\star g^{-1}
.
Note that
here
``\star"
is the convolution product in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComBiAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K}[G])
, given by
\Phi_{1}\star\Phi_{2}(x)=\sum\Phi_{1}(x_{(1)})\Phi_{2}(x_{(2)})
.

The conjugacy defined in the Definition
6.5
gives an equivalence relation on the set of bialgebra morphisms from
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a})
to
\mathbb{K}[G]
. Let us denote the set of equivalence classes by
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComBiAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K}[G])/\sim
. Then, using a similar argument as in the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
, we can conclude the following result:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and
\mathfrak{a}
be a finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra. Then, the isomorphism classes of
G
-gradings on
\mathfrak{a}
bijectively corresponds to the equivalence classes in the set
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{ComBiAlg}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{a}),\mathbb{K}[G])/\sim
.

Conclusion

In this paper, we gave an explicit construction of a universal coacting algebra for any finite-dimensional
\mathcal{P}
-algebra. Using the operadic approach, we also extended the constructions to the graded context. Our approach is also prudent to address the following problems.

I.

A universal module construction and functors between module categories.

In
[
2
]
, A. L. Agore defined functors between the category of Lie algebra modules and the category of associated universal algebra modules. The results in
[
2
]
leads to a representation-theoretic counterpart of Manin-Tambara’s universal coacting objects
[
15
,
23
]
. Later on, for other quadratic algebras such as Poisson algebras and Lie-Yamaguti algebras, the representation theoretic universal constructions have been addressed in
[
3
,
9
]
. We already discussed here how the operadic approach unifies the universal constructions in
[
1
,
3
,
9
,
16
]
. In a separate work, by considering modules over
\mathcal{P}
-algebras for a symmetric (graded) operad
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain a universal module construction to define functors between module categories which not only unifies the results in
[
2
,
3
,
9
]
but also yield the explicit constructions and related results for different (graded) quadratic algebras.

II.

Construction of a universal algebra for finite-dimensional coalgebras over (graded) Operads

For any vector space
\mathfrak{a}
, the coendomorphism operad
\mathrm{coEnd}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is given by

\mathrm{coEnd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(n):=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes n})\quad\text{for all }n\geq 0,

with the partial composition maps dual to the maps in
\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{a}}
(Example
2.4
). A
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra structure on a vector space
V
is an operad morphism from
\mathcal{P}
to
\mathrm{coEnd}(V)
. The construction in Section
3
can be dualised to obtain a universal coacting algebra for finite-dimensional coalgebras over (graded) Operads.

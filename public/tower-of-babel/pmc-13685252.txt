Deleted in lymphocytic leukemia 2 contributes to lung fibroblast activation and pulmonary fibrosis by interacting with and triggering focal adhesion kinase phosphorylation
Authors: Honglan Zhu, Junjie Lan, Xiao Wu, Ya Gao, Xingfen Ran, Runrun He, Zhixiang Luo, Yiju Cheng, Zifeng Yang, Daishun Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe etiology of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) remains unknown, and therapeutic interventions are currently restricted. The activation of lung fibroblasts and the focal adhesion kinase (FAK) signaling pathway are crucial to the pathogenesis of IPF, yet the exact molecular mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Our study shows that the deleted in lymphocytic leukemia 2 (DLEU2) is elevated in the peripheral blood of patients with IPF, as well as in the lung tissues of mice with pulmonary fibrosis. Functional assays revealed that overexpression of DLEU2 facilitated fibrogenesis, whereas its suppression attenuated lung fibrosis in vivo and impeded fibroblast activation in vitro, predominantly through the regulation of FAK phosphorylation. Mechanistically, our findings reveal that DLEU2 regulates lung fibroblast activation by targeting miR-29a-3p. Conversely, inhibition of miR-29a-3p reversed the anti-fibrotic effects observed upon DLEU2 knockdown. Furthermore, DLEU2 was shown to directly interact with FAK, enhancing its phosphorylation and thereby facilitating fibrogenesis; inhibition of FAK phosphorylation counteracted the pro-fibrotic effects induced by DLEU2 overexpression. Taken together, these findings clarify the function of DLEU2 in facilitating FAK phosphorylation via its interactions with miR-29a-3p and FAK. Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a fatal interstitial lung disease of unknown cause, characterized histopathologically by usual interstitial pneumonia and clinically by a steady decline in lung function. The condition mainly impacts individuals over 50, with a higher prevalence in males. Conservative epidemiological estimates report a prevalence of 3 to 9 cases per 100,000 individuals, with incidence rates demonstrating an upward trend [1]. The median survival following diagnosis ranges from approximately 2.5 to 3.5 years [2]. Approved pharmacological treatments for IPF, pirfenidone and nintedanib, have demonstrated efficacy in decelerating disease progression and decreasing mortality rates. Nonetheless, these treatments do not offer a definitive cure for the disorder [3, 4]. Pathophysiologically, the persistent activation of pulmonary fibroblasts leads to an abnormal accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM). This process is critically influenced by the dysregulation of the focal adhesion kinase (FAK) signaling pathway, which plays a key role in the activation of fibroblasts [5–14]. Notably, phosphorylation of FAK constitutes a critical event in the initiation of downstream signaling cascades. Despite this, the upstream regulatory mechanisms controlling FAK activation remain inadequately understood. Elucidating these upstream signaling pathways may facilitate the identification of novel clinical biomarkers and therapeutic targets for IPF.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) demonstrate substantial biological roles despite their limited capacity for protein coding. Emerging evidence indicates that lncRNAs are integral to the activation of lung fibroblasts, thereby impacting the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis. lncITPF associates with heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein L (hnRNP L) to promote histone H3 and H4 acetylation at the ITGBL1 gene promoter. This epigenetic modification enhances lung fibroblast differentiation, proliferation, and migration, thus promoting fibrotic processes [15]. DNM3OS enhances the expression of miR-199a-5p, miR-199a-3p, and miR-214-3p, promoting lung fibroblast differentiation via canonical TGF-β/Smad and non-Smad pathways, thereby worsening pulmonary fibrosis [16]. Research on lncRNAs in IPF pathophysiology is still in early stages, with their regulatory roles and mechanisms not yet fully understood.

Mechanistically, they modulate gene expression through diverse pathways such as direct protein interactions, alteration of histone modifications, interference with mRNA splicing, and functioning as molecular sponges for microRNAs [17]. The lncRNA deleted in lymphocytic leukemia 2 (DLEU2) was initially identified for its regulatory role in leukemia. Subsequent studies have implicated DLEU2 in various solid tumors, including breast, endometrial, esophageal, pancreatic, gastric cancers, glioma, and non-small cell lung cancer [18]. In chronic lymphocytic leukemia, DLEU2 overexpression upregulates miR-15a and miR-16-1, which suppress cyclins E1 and D1, inhibiting tumor cell proliferation and colony formation [19]. Additionally, DLEU2 contributes to cancer progression by sponging miR-30a-5p to regulate ETS2 in gastric cancer [20] and interacting with EZH2 in hepatocellular carcinoma [21]. Collectively, these findings underscore the significant regulatory functions and translational potential of DLEU2 in oncological contexts. However, investigations into the role of DLEU2 in non-neoplastic diseases remain limited, and its involvement in lung fibroblast activation and associated molecular mechanisms requires further exploration.

This study revealed a significant upregulation of DLEU2 expression in the peripheral blood of patients with IPF as well as in the lung tissues of bleomycin (BLM)-induced mouse models, suggesting a potentially pivotal role for DLEU2 in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis. The inhibition of DLEU2 expression substantially suppressed lung fibroblast activation and mitigated pulmonary fibrosis in vivo. Mechanistic investigations indicated that DLEU2 promotes lung fibroblast activation by regulating FAK phosphorylation, mediated through its modulation of miR-29a-3p and direct interaction with FAK. These findings offer novel insights that may contribute to the development of diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for IPF.

Materials and methods

Mouse model of pulmonary fibrosis

Male C57BL/6 mice, aged 6 to 9 weeks, were procured from Spiffy (Beijing) Biotechnology Co. Ltd. (SCXK (Jing) 2019-0010, China). A lung fibrosis model was established through the administration of a single intratracheal dose of BLM at 5 mg/kg (MedChemExpress, Zhejiang, China) under anesthesia. Fibrotic changes were assessed 28 days post-administration. Control subjects were treated with saline. Following the principle of random allocation, mice were assigned to six experimental groups: (1) Saline + Lentivirus 3 (LV3)-short hairpin RNA (shRNA) negative control (NC) (LV3-sh-NC), (2) Saline + LV3-sh-DLEU2, (3) BLM + LV3-sh-NC, (4) BLM + LV3-sh-DLEU2, (5) BLM + LV3-sh-DLEU2 + Adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV9)-FAK, and (6) BLM + Nintedanib. LV3-sh-DLEU2 or LV3-sh-NC (GenePharma Technology, Shanghai, China) was administered intratracheally at a dose of 3 × 106 transducing units per mouse on days 7, 14, and 21. On day 7, AAV9-FAK (JK Gene Technology, Shanghai, China) was administered intratracheally at a dose of 2.57 × 1011 viral genome copies per animal. For the BLM + Nintedanib group, nintedanib was administered via daily oral gavage at a dose of 30 mg/kg (McLean, Shanghai, China) from day 1 through day 28 following bleomycin administration. All animal procedures adhered to protocols approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Guizhou Medical University (Approval No. 2304180).

Cell lines, cell induction, transfection and lentivirus infection

Human fetal lung fibroblast cell line MRC-5 (CCL-171), embryonic mouse fibroblast 3T3 cells (NIH-3T3, CRL-1658) and HEK-293T cells (CRL-3216) were sourced from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Lung fibroblast activation was induced by treating NIH-3T3 cells and MRC-5 cells, cultured to approximately 60% confluency, with 10 ng/mL of TGF-β1 for a period of 48 h.HEK-293T cells were employed in a dual-luciferase reporter assay to examine the interaction between DLEU2 and miR-29a-3p. MRC-5 cells and NIH-3T3 cells in the exponential growth phase were plated into 60 mm culture dishes or 6-well plates and incubated for a duration of 24 h. Cells were transfected with either siRNA-DLEU2, the plasmid of lncRNA DLEU2, miR-29a-3p mimic, or miR-29a-3p inhibitor using Lipofectamine® 3000 (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. To achieve stable knockdown of DLEU2 in NIH-3T3 cells, recombinant lentivirus 3 harboring shRNAs targeting DLEU2 (LV3-sh-DLEU2-1, -2, and -3) were generated. A non-targeting shRNA (LV3-sh-NC) served as the negative control. For DLEU2 overexpression, recombinant lentivirus 5 containing the full-length coding sequence of DLEU2 were employed, with an empty lentiviral vector used as the corresponding control. Puromycin is employed as a selective agent to isolate stable transfectant cell lines. All lentiviral constructs, controls, mimics, and inhibitors were procured from GenePharma Technology (Shanghai, China). Transfection and infection efficiencies were assessed via quantitative real-time PCR. Detailed sequences of all constructs are provided in Table 1.

Clinical samples

Peripheral blood samples were collected from patients with IPF and healthy controls at the Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University. Participants with significant comorbidities, including malignant tumors, immunodeficiency disorders, connective tissue diseases, and hematological conditions, were excluded from the study. IPF diagnosis followed the 2022 updated international clinical guidelines [22]. The Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University approved the collection and use of human specimens and all experimental protocols, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (1989) (Approval No. 163 of 2023 Lunshen). Before the collection of samples, written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Before the extraction of total RNA, whole blood samples underwent treatment with a red blood cell lysis buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China).

Histological identification

The left lung of the mouse was surgically excised, with meticulous removal of adjacent tissues and adipose matter. The lung specimens were subsequently fixed at ambient temperature in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for a duration of 48 to 72 h. Following fixation, the tissues were subjected to dehydration through a graded ethanol series of increasing concentrations, then cleared using xylene, and finally embedded in paraffin. The paraffin-embedded tissue blocks were sectioned into slices measuring 3–5 μm in thickness for subsequent hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining as well as Masson’s trichrome staining. Pulmonary fibrosis was evaluated using the ashcroft scoring system. Three pathologists independently conducted blinded assessments of the pathological sections, with each sample analyzed by randomly selecting 30 fields of view on the HE-stained slides.

Immunohistochemistry

Lung tissue samples embedded in paraffin were rehydrated using a graded ethanol series and underwent antigen retrieval by incubating in EDTA repair solution for 2.5 min. The specimens were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide at room temperature for 25 min to suppress endogenous peroxidase activity. Following this, the tissues were incubated with bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min to block nonspecific binding sites. Primary antibodies targeting α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) at a dilution of 1:320 and collagen I at a dilution of 1:200, both obtained from Cell Signaling Technology, were applied and incubated overnight at 4 °C. Post-washing, sections were treated with an anti-mouse IgG horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (1:100; Proteintech) at room temperature for 50 min, then stained with diaminobenzidine (DAB). Lung tissue sections were counterstained with hematoxylin at room temperature for 3 min and subsequently mounted using neutral balsam. Staining extent and intensity were quantitatively evaluated with Image Pro Plus version 6.0.

Measurement of hydroxyproline content

Hydroxyproline concentrations in lung tissue were determined utilizing a hydroxyproline assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bio Co., Nanjing, China). In brief, 30–50 mg of mouse lung tissue was accurately weighed and transferred into a 15 mL glass tube, to which 1 mL of hydrolysis solution was added. The samples underwent hydrolysis in a water bath maintained at 95 °C for 20 min. Following hydrolysis, the tubes were cooled under running water, and 10 µL of indicator solution was introduced and thoroughly mixed. Subsequently, 1 mL of pH-adjusting solution A was added, resulting in a red-colored solution upon mixing. Thereafter, pH-adjusting solution B was carefully added dropwise, with continuous mixing after each addition, until the solution color transitioned from red to yellow-green, indicating a pH range of approximately 6.0 to 6.8. Additional reagents were then incorporated in accordance with the kit protocol, and the mixture was incubated at 60 °C for 15 min. Finally, the absorbance of each sample was measured at 550 nm. Hydroxyproline content within the lung tissue was calculated based on the manufacturer’s guidelines.

Quantitative real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction(qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was isolated from blood samples, lung tissues, and cells utilizing TRIzol reagent (Takara, Japan). For the analysis of lncRNA and mRNA, reverse transcription was performed employing the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Japan). miRNA detection was carried out through reverse transcription using the First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (TOYOBO, Japan). qRT-PCR was conducted with Takara SYBR Premix Ex Taq II on an ABI 7500 Fast Real-time PCR system. Expression levels of lncRNA and mRNA were normalized against GAPDH, while miRNA expression was normalized to U6. Primer efficiency was assessed via serial dilution assays, and primer specificity was validated through dissociation curve analysis. Primer sequences are provided in Table 2. Gene expression quantification was performed using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Protein isolation and western blot analysis

Protein samples were isolated from cellular and pulmonary tissues utilizing the BeyoLytic™ mammalian active protein extraction reagent supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). The protein concentrations in the resulting lysates were determined via the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). Proteins were resolved on 8% or 10% SDS-polyacrylamide gels and subsequently transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBS-T) buffer to prevent nonspecific binding, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies diluted at 1:1000, except for GAPDH, which was used at a 1:10,000 dilution (refer to Table 3 for antibody specifications). After incubation, membranes underwent five washes of 5 min each with TBS-T at room temperature, then were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for one hour. This was followed by an additional five washes of 5 min each with TBS-T. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents and quantified employing Gel-Pro analyzer software.

Immunofluorescence staining

NIH-3T3 cells were cultured on circular glass coverslips measuring 14 mm in diameter, which were placed within 12-well culture plates. Following either transfection or treatment with TGF-β1, the coverslips were rinsed three times with PBS for 5 min per wash and subsequently fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at ambient temperature for 15 min. After fixation, the cells were permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 10 min and then blocked with 5% BSA in PBS for 30 min. Samples were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary antibody targeting α-SMA at a dilution of 1:2000 (Huabio, China). On the following day, cells were washed three times with PBS and incubated in the dark with a fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:1000, Proteintech, China). Nuclear counterstaining was performed using DAPI (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China) at a 1:200 dilution for 30 min. Fluorescent images were subsequently captured using confocal laser scanning microscopy.

ELISA

The mouse CTGF and PDGF ELISA Kit (Jingmei Biotechnology, Jiangsu, China) was used to measure CTGF and PDGF levels in NIH-3T3 cell supernatants and mouse bronchial alveolar lavage fluid (BALF) following the specified protocols.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

GenePharma Technology (Shanghai, China) constructed psi-CHECK2 vectors containing either the wild-type or mutated sequences of DLEU2, referred to as Wt-DLEU2 and Mut-DLEU2, respectively. HEK 293T cells were plated in 24-well plates and co-transfected with the psiCHECK-2 plasmid constructs along with either a miR-29a-3p mimic or inhibitor using Lipofectamine 3000. Following transfection, the cells were incubated for 48 h. Subsequently, an equal volume of Dual-Glo® Luciferase Reagent was added to each well and mixed thoroughly. After a 30-minute incubation, firefly luciferase activity was quantified using a multifunctional microplate reader. Thereafter, an equal volume of Dual-Glo® Stop & Glo® Reagent was introduced to each well, mixed, and incubated for a minimum of 30 min prior to measuring Renilla luciferase activity with the same instrument. The ratio of firefly to Renilla luciferase luminescence was then calculated according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Promega, Madison, USA).

RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP)

This assay was conducted utilizing a commercially available kit from Bersinbio (China), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Initially, NIH-3T3 cells treated with TGF-β1 were lysed in polysome lysis buffer supplemented with protease and RNase inhibitors. The lysates were then subjected to DNA removal and subsequently incubated with either rabbit immunoglobulin G or an anti-FAK antibody (Cell Signaling Technology) at 4 °C for 16 h. Protein A/G beads were added to the mixture, which was further incubated at 4 °C for one hour to enable immunoprecipitation. Following three washes, RNA-protein complexes were eluted using polysome elution buffer and treated with proteinase K. RNA was extracted employing Trizol reagent, chloroform, and isopropanol. Reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Japan). PCR amplification was carried out with 2× Taq Master Mix (Dye Plus). Subsequently, 3–5 µL of the PCR products were analyzed by electrophoresis on a 3% agarose gel. The gel was initially run at a constant voltage of 80 V until the dye front reached one-third of the gel length, after which the voltage was increased to 120 V until the bands migrated to approximately four-fifths of the gel. The electrophoretic results were recorded using a chemiluminescent gel imaging system.

RNA pull down

This assay was conducted utilizing the RNA Pull-Down Kit from BersinBio, China. Protein extracts were prepared from at least 1 × 107 NIH-3T3 cells treated with TGF-β1, following the manufacturer’s protocol. These extracts were then incubated with 50 pmol of a biotinylated DLEU2 probe (GenePharma Technology, Shanghai, China) and streptavidin-coated magnetic beads under continuous rotation. Post-incubation, the beads were subjected to washing with the provided wash buffer and isolated using a magnetic rack. Proteins bound to the beads were then eluted using biotin elution buffer and analyzed via western blotting.

Wound healing assay

NIH-3T3 cells were seeded into 6-well culture plates and subjected to transfection with or without designated modifications. Upon reaching approximately 90% confluence, a linear scratch was introduced into the cell monolayer utilizing the tip of a 200 µL plastic micropipette. Subsequently, the cells were maintained in basal culture medium for an additional 24-hour incubation period. Images were acquired immediately following the scratch (0 h) and at 24 h post-scratch to assess the wound healing dynamics. Migration distance was calculated as [(initial wound area - wound area after 24 h) / initial wound area] × 100%.

Fluorescent In Situ Hybridization (FISH)

A FISH kit (GenePharma Technology, Shanghai, China) was employed to determine the subcellular localization of DLEU2. NIH-3T3 cells were cultured on circular glass coverslips measuring 9 mm in diameter, which were placed within 24-well culture plates, and subsequently treated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 for a duration of 48 h. Post-treatment, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at ambient temperature for 15 min, followed by permeabilization with 0.2% Triton X-100 for an additional 15 min. Subsequently, cells were blocked with 5% BSA at 37 °C for 30 min. The DLEU2 probe was pre-incubated with streptavidin-fluorescein amidite (SA-FAM) in a 37 °C water bath for 30 min, after which hybridization buffer was added and thoroughly mixed to prepare the probe solution. This mixture was then applied to the cells, which were incubated overnight at 37 °C in a dark, humidified chamber to facilitate hybridization. Following hybridization, cells were subjected to a series of washes under specified conditions, and nuclear counterstaining was performed using DAPI for 30 min. Fluorescent images were acquired using a Zeiss LSM 710 inverted confocal microscope equipped with a high-resolution AiryScan module. Image acquisition and analysis were conducted utilizing Zen software (Zeiss).

Subcellular fractionation

The Cytoplasmic and Nuclear RNA Purification Kit (Norgen Biotek, NGB-21000) was utilized to isolate and purify nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA fractions from NIH-3T3 cells, following the manufacturer’s protocol. RT-qPCR was subsequently performed to assess the expression levels of DLEU2 in both nuclear and cytoplasmic compartments, employing U6 and GAPDH as internal reference controls for the nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions, respectively.

Transwel assay

Initially, NIH-3T3 cells underwent transfection procedures, either incorporating specific modifications or without them. Subsequently, the cells were resuspended in 200 µL of serum-free medium and seeded into the upper chambers of a Transwell system. The lower chambers were filled with 500 µL of DMEM supplemented with 20% FBS. After incubation for 48 h at 37 °C, the media from both chambers were discarded. The chambers were then rinsed with PBS, and cells remaining on the upper surface of the membrane were carefully removed using a cotton swab. Following two additional PBS washes, the cells were fixed with 500 µL of 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and subsequently stained with crystal violet solution for 20 min. Finally, the number of migrated NIH-3T3 cells was quantified via observation under an inverted light microscope.

Statistics and analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 10.0 and SPSS 27.0. For normally distributed data, descriptive statistics are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). For comparisons between two groups with equal variances, an independent samples t-test is utilized. One-way ANOVA is used for comparing multiple groups. For pairwise comparisons among groups, the least significant difference (LSD) test is applied when variances are equal, whereas the Tamhane test is employed if variances are unequal. For data not following a normal distribution, descriptive statistics are presented as the median with interquartile range (25th and 75th percentiles), and nonparametric tests are applied. For linear correlation analyses, Pearson’s correlation coefficient is calculated when data are normally distributed; otherwise, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient is used. A p-value below 0.05 signifies statistical significance.

Results

DLEU2 was elevated in pulmonary fibrosis

The IPF datasets GSE21369 and GSE24206 were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to identify differentially expressed lncRNAs, applying criteria of log fold change (logFC) greater than 1 and adjusted p-value (P.adj) less than 0.05 (Fig. 1A, B). Among the upregulated lncRNAs consistently identified across both datasets, DLEU2 and CARMN were notable (Fig. 1C). Following a 48-hour treatment of NIH-3T3 cells with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1, a significant upregulation of Dleu2 expression was observed relative to the control group, whereas Carmn expression exhibited no significant difference between groups (Fig. 1D). Similarly, in MRC5 cells treated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1, DLEU2 expression was also elevated relative to the control group (Fig. 1E). Elevated DLEU2 expression in the IPF group was corroborated in both GSE21369 and GSE24206 datasets (Fig. 1F).

In the GSE21369 dataset, DLEU2 expression was significantly upregulated in lung tissues from patients classified in the forced vital capacity (FVC) 3 group (FVC < 50%) relative to both healthy controls and patients in the FVC 2 group (50% < FVC < 80%). Moreover, DLEU2 expression positively correlated with FVC stratification (R = 0.641, P = 0.006) (Fig. 1G), suggesting that reduced pulmonary function corresponds to elevated DLEU2 levels in lung tissue. DLEU2 facilitates TGF-β1-induced fibrogenic responses in lung fibroblasts

To elucidate the role of DLEU2 in the TGF-β1-mediated activation of lung fibroblasts, both knockdown and overexpression approaches were employed. Three short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting DLEU2 were designed to assess their silencing efficiency, with shRNA1 and shRNA2 selected for further experimentation (Fig. 2A). Stable knockdown and overexpression of DLEU2 were subsequently established in immortalized NIH-3T3 cells via lentiviral vector transduction (Figs. 2B, C). Functional analyses demonstrated that DLEU2 knockdown significantly decreased the expression of fibrotic markers, including collagen type I alpha 1 chain (col1a1) and fibronectin 1 (fn1), while its overexpression amplified TGF-β1-induced upregulation of these genes (Fig. 2D). ELISA results indicated that DLEU2 silencing decreased, whereas its overexpression increased, the secretion of profibrotic factors ctgf and pdgf in the culture supernatant of TGF-β1-stimulated NIH-3T3 cells (Fig. 2E). Moreover, TGF-β1 treatment elevated α-SMA, collagen I, and fibronectin 1 (FN1) protein levels in NIH-3T3 cells. DLEU2 depletion significantly reduced these effects, while its overexpression enhanced them (Fig. 2F-G). Consistently, in MRC5 cells, silencing DLEU2 expression suppressed the TGF-β1-induced upregulation of α-SMA, collagen I, and FN1 protein levels (Fig. 2H, J), while overexpression of DLEU2 facilitated the enhanced expression of these proteins (Fig. 2I, K). Immunofluorescence staining demonstrated that DLEU2 knockdown inhibited fibroblast activation in NIH-3T3 cells, as indicated by reduced α-SMA fluorescence, whereas overexpression led to increased α-SMA intensity (Fig. 2L). Additionally, DLEU2 silencing significantly impaired TGF-β1-induced cell migration, while its overexpression facilitated this migratory response (Figs. 2M, N). These results indicate that DLEU2 is critically involved in the regulation of lung fibroblast activation mediated by TGF-β1.

Reduced expression of DLEU2 attenuates experimental lung fibrosis

To investigate the role of DLEU2 in pulmonary fibrosis, an animal model was established through intratracheal administration of BLM. As illustrated in Fig. 3A, LV3-sh-DLEU2 administration effectively reduced DLEU2 expression in murine lung tissue. Western blotting and immunohistochemical analysis further confirmed that DLEU2 silencing abrogated the BLM-mediated increase in fibrosis-associated proteins, including α-SMA, collagen I, and FN1 (Figs. 3G-I). ELISA results demonstrated that DLEU2 knockdown reduced the secretion of profibrotic factors ctgf and pdgf in BALF from mice with BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis (Fig. 3J). The findings suggest that the inhibition of DLEU2 can effectively hinder the advancement of lung fibrosis.

DLEU2 promotes FAK phosphorylation

Subsequently, we explored the mechanistic basis through which depletion of DLEU2 attenuates lung fibroblast activation and experimental pulmonary fibrosis in murine models. TGF-β1 treatment of NIH-3T3 cells and MRC5 cells significantly increased phosphorylation of FAK at tyrosine 397 and extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) (Fig. 4A - D). Notably, knockdown of DLEU2 markedly diminished these phosphorylation events (Fig. 4A B), whereas overexpression of DLEU2 further potentiated them (Fig. 4C D). In parallel, administration of BLM in C57BL/6 mice elevated FAK phosphorylation at tyrosine 397 and ERK activation in lung tissue, effects that were substantially mitigated following DLEU2 depletion (Fig. 4E). Given that lncRNAs modulate gene expression through transcriptional and post-transcriptional mechanisms-processes predominantly occurring in the nucleus and cytoplasm, respectively-we assessed the subcellular localization of DLEU2 via RNA FISH (Fig. 4F) and Subcellular fractionation detection (Fig. 4G, H). The findings revealed that DLEU2 is present in both nuclear and cytoplasmic compartments of control cells, with a predominance in the nucleus. However, upon TGF-β1 stimulation, DLEU2 partially translocated from the nucleus to the cytoplasm. These observations suggest that DLEU2 may regulate FAK phosphorylation through multiple, potentially distinct mechanisms.

DLEU2 facilitates the phosphorylation of FAK and fibrogenesis through its interaction with miR-29a-3p

Increasing evidence suggests that lncRNAs act as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) by binding to microRNAs (miRNAs) and inhibiting their activity [23]. A bioinformatics analysis was conducted utilizing the lncRNA database (http://regrna.mbc.nctu.edu.tw/html/prediction.html) to identify miRNAs that are dysregulated in lung fibrosis and potentially interact with DLEU2. This investigation aimed to elucidate the involvement of these miRNAs in mediating anti-fibrotic effects and regulating FAK phosphorylation within our experimental models. This approach yielded 90 miRNAs containing putative binding sites for DLEU2. Among these, 88 miRNAs were found to be downregulated in the IPF miRNA expression dataset (GEO: GSE32538), which comprises 106 IPF samples and 50 non-diseased controls (Fig. 5A). Concurrently, 99 miRNAs associated with the FAK pathway were identified through the miRPathDB database (http://tools4mirs.org/software/mirna_databases/mirpathdb/). Intersection of the miRNAs obtained from these three analyses resulted in 4 candidate miRNAs: miR-29a-3p, miR-15a-5p, let-7b-5p, and miR-130b-3p (Fig. 5B). Notably, miR-29a-3p and miR-15a-5p exhibited the strongest associations with the FAK signaling pathway (Table 5) and were significantly downregulated in lung tissues from patients with IPF within the GSE32538 dataset (Figs. 5C). Treatment of NIH-3T3 cells with TGF-β1 significantly reduced miR-29a-3p expression, while miR-15a-5p expression exhibited a slight, non-significant decrease (Fig. 5D). Similarly, treatment of MRC5 cells with TGF-β1 led to a significant reduction in miR-29a-3p expression (Fig. 5E).In a murine model of pulmonary fibrosis induced by BLM, miR-29a-3p expression in lung tissue was significantly lower 28 days after BLM administration compared to controls (Fig. 5F), and it showed a negative correlation with hydroxyproline expression levels (R = − 0.915, P = 0.01) (Fig. 5G). Subsequent analyses revealed reduced miR-29a-3p levels in the peripheral blood of patients with IPF relative to healthy controls (Fig. 5H), and a strong inverse correlation between miR-29a-3p and DLEU2 expression was identified in human peripheral blood samples (R = − 0.902, P < 0.001) (Fig. 5I). Additionally, knockdown of DLEU2 in TGF-β1-treated NIH-3T3 cells (Fig. 5J) and MRC5 cells (Fig. 5K) restored miR-29a-3p expression. A luciferase reporter assay was conducted with constructs containing either wild-type or mutated DLEU2 fragments to directly demonstrate the interaction with miR-29a-3p (Fig. 5L).As demonstrated in Fig. 5M, transfection with a miR-29a-3p mimic significantly reduced luciferase activity in HEK-293T cells with the wild-type DLEU2 construct, but this suppression was absent in the mutant construct missing the miR-29a-3p binding site. Conversely, a miR-29a-3p inhibitor enhanced luciferase activity in cells expressing wild-type DLEU2 but had no effect on the mutant construct (Fig. 5N). These findings collectively support a functional interaction between DLEU2 and miR-29a-3p, implicating this axis in the regulation of pulmonary fibrosis via the FAK signaling pathway.

DLEU2 interacts with FAK to modulate its functional activity

The subcellular localization of lncRNAs is a critical determinant of their functional mechanisms. The study found that DLEU2 is present in both the cytoplasm and nucleus, primarily localizing in the nucleus under normal conditions (Fig. 3F, G). Recent research has demonstrated that lncRNAs can directly interact with proteins, thereby modulating protein expression and activity [24]. Notably, FAK has been reported to translocate from focal adhesions to the nucleus in response to cellular stress [25]. Utilizing the RPISeq database (http://pridb.gdcb.iastate.edu/RPISeq/), which employs random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM) classifiers, we predicted a potential interaction between DLEU2 and FAK, suggesting that DLEU2 may bind to FAK (Fig. 8A). To empirically validate this interaction, we conducted an RNA pulldown assay, which confirmed the presence of FAK protein in RNA-protein complexes via western blot analysis (Fig. 8B, C). Furthermore, RIP assays revealed a significant enrichment of DLEU2 in complexes precipitated with an anti-FAK antibody compared to IgG controls (Fig. 8D, E). Additionally, we employed PF-573,228, a specific inhibitor of FAK phosphorylation, to investigate the functional consequences of this interaction (Fig. 8F, G). In TGF-β1-treated NIH-3T3 cells and MRC5 cells, DLEU2 overexpression elevated phosphorylated FAK (p-FAK) and ERK (p-ERK) levels. However, co-treatment with PF-573,228 effectively suppressed the phosphorylation of both proteins (Fig. 8H, I). These findings suggest that DLEU2 directly associates with FAK and influences its phosphorylation state.

Phosphorylation of FAK is essential for mediating the profibrotic effects induced by DLEU2

To explore if DLEU2 influences lung fibroblasts activation by modulating FAK phosphorylation, NIH-3T3 cells with stable DLEU2 overexpression via lentiviral transduction were treated with the FAK phosphorylation inhibitor PF-573,228. At the mRNA level, PF-573,228 attenuated the upregulation of col1a1 and fn1 induced by DLEU2 overexpression (Fig. 9A, B). Similarly, NIH-3T3 cells treated with PF-573,228 exhibited reduced expression of profibrotic factors ctgf and pdgf compared to cells overexpressing DLEU2 alone (Fig. 9C, D). Moreover, treatment with PF-573,228 induced a reduction in the protein expression levels of α-SMA, Collagen I, and FN1 (Fig. 9E). In MRC5 cells transfected with a DLEU2 overexpression plasmid, simultaneous administration of PF-573,228 to inhibit FAK phosphorylation resulted in decreased protein expression of α-SMA, Collagen I, and FN1 relative to cells overexpressing DLEU2 alone (Fig. 9F). These findings suggest that inhibition of FAK phosphorylation mitigates the pro-fibrotic and collagen-accumulating effects driven by DLEU2. PF-573,228 inhibited DLEU2-induced cell migration and fibroblast differentiation into myofibroblasts under TGF-β1 stimulation in NIH-3T3 cells (Figs. 9G-I). These findings suggest that FAK phosphorylation serves as a downstream effector of DLEU2, mediating its profibrotic function.

Overexpression of FAK attenuates the anti-fibrotic efficacy of DLEU2 knockdown in an established experimental murine model of lung fibrosis

To further investigate the role of the interaction between DLEU2 and FAK in pulmonary fibrosis in a murine model, we administered LV3-sh-DLEU2 and AAV9-FAK following BLM induction, with mice sacrificed 28 days post intratracheal BLM administration (Fig. 10A). Mice receiving intratracheal BLM instillation and treated with 30 mg/kg nintedanib via gavage served as the positive control group. Overexpression of FAK in the lungs of AAV9-FAK-treated mice was confirmed (Fig. 10B). Knockdown of DLEU2 alone significantly inhibited BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis, as evidenced by reduced collagen-related protein deposition in lung tissue and decreased expression of ctgf and pdgf in BALF. This inhibitory effect was similar with nintedanib treatment, with no significant statistical difference between them. Notably, combined knockdown of DLEU2 and overexpression of FAK abrogated the antifibrotic effect of DLEU2 knockdown alone, restoring pulmonary fibrosis development (Fig. 10C, D). Masson’s trichrome staining and hydroxyproline assays demonstrated exacerbated collagen deposition in lung tissue following combined DLEU2 knockdown and FAK overexpression (Fig. 10E, F). Furthermore, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that the combination counteracted the DLEU2 knockdown-induced suppression of col1a1 and fn1 mRNA expression (Fig. 10G, H). ELISA measurements of ctgf and pdgf levels in BALF exhibited a similar pattern of increased expression (Fig. 10I, J). Western blot analysis confirmed elevated protein expression of α-SMA, Collagen I, and FN1 in lung tissue (Fig. 10K, L). Immunohistochemical staining of lung sections consistently demonstrated increased expression of α-SMA and Collagen I when DLEU2 was knocked down and FAK was overexpressed (Fig. 10M-O). These findings suggest that DLEU2 suppression alleviates BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice, while FAK overexpression negates the protective benefits of DLEU2 knockdown. These findings underscore the profibrotic role of the DLEU2 / FAK signaling axis.

Discussion

The study found increased DLEU2 expression in both the peripheral blood of patients with IPF and BLM-treated mice. Silencing DLEU2 in BLM-treated mice resulted in the attenuation of pulmonary fibrosis. Furthermore, DLEU2 expression levels positively correlated with disease severity in patients with IPF. Mechanistic analyses revealed that DLEU2 interacts with miR-29a-3p, facilitating the phosphorylation of FAK and ERK proteins, which in turn promotes lung fibroblasts differentiation and ECM accumulation. Complementary in vivo and in vitro studies demonstrated that DLEU2 directly binds to FAK, augmenting the phosphorylation of FAK / ERK signaling components, thereby enhancing lung fibroblasts activation and contributing to the progression of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice. These results indicate that DLEU2 may serve as a viable therapeutic target for the management of IPF. Additionally, DLEU2 holds potential as a biomarker for the diagnosis of IPF.

FAK is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase with an approximate molecular weight of 120 kDa. It is localized to focal adhesions, intercellular adhesion junctions, endosomes, and the nucleus, where it regulates critical cellular functions such as adhesion, migration, survival, proliferation, and the activation of integrin recycling [26]. FAK possesses six tyrosine phosphorylation sites—Y397, Y407, Y576, Y577, Y861, and Y925—with phosphorylation at Y397 being particularly critical for FAK-mediated signal transduction. Moreover, FAK contains both a nuclear export signal (NES) and a nuclear localization signal (NLS). Accumulating evidence implicated FAK as a pivotal factor in the pathogenesis of IPF. Extracellular stimuli such as growth factors (e.g., TGF-β and PDGF), extracellular matrix components like fibronectin 1, alterations in matrix stiffness, and hypoxic conditions have been shown to enhance FAK phosphorylation. Once activated, FAK facilitates lung fibroblasts proliferation, migration, invasion, differentiation, and resistance to apoptosis via signaling cascades including FAK/PI3K/Akt, FAK/Fas, and FAK/MAPK/ERK pathways. These events contribute to ECM accumulation, aberrant lung injury repair, and ultimately the development of pulmonary fibrosis [5–13]. Our investigations have demonstrated that the interaction between FAK and integrin β1 promotes the migration of human fetal lung fibroblast 1 (HFL-1) cells and participates in pulmonary fibrosis progression in murine models. Pharmacological inhibition of FAK using PF573,228 effectively attenuates these processes [27]. Furthermore, we identified that the FAK/ERK/S100A4 signaling axis regulates HFL-1 cell differentiation, migration, ECM deposition, and pulmonary fibrosis formation in mice, with nintedanib exerting therapeutic effects by suppressing FAK signaling [14]. Despite these insights, the upstream regulatory mechanisms governing FAK activation in IPF remain to be fully elucidated. Our current study reveals that the lncRNA DLEU2 promotes phosphorylation of FAK and ERK through dual mechanisms: by targeting and binding miR-29a-3p and through direct interaction with FAK. This dual regulation leads to lung fibroblasts activation and contributes to the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis.

Recent investigations have elucidated the key role of lncRNAs in the pathogenesis of IPF. Specifically, the extranuclear exosome complex protein Rbm7 interacts with lncNEAT1, facilitating its degradation, which subsequently impairs DNA repair mechanisms and induces apoptosis in alveolar epithelial cells [28]. Additionally, lncRNA-uc.77 and 2700086A05Rik have been shown to promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) by binding to the transcription factors ZEB1 and Hoxa3, thereby contributing to the progression of pulmonary fibrosis [29]. Furthermore, lnc-PCF acts as a ceRNA by sponging miR-344a-5p, resulting in the upregulation of Map3k11, which enhances alveolar epithelial cell proliferation and exacerbates pulmonary fibrosis [30]. In the present study, we observed a significant upregulation of DLEU2 in the lung tissues of mice subjected to experimental lung fibrosis as well as in TGF-β1-induced fibrotic fibroblasts. Notably, silencing of DLEU2 attenuated TGF-β1-induced activation of lung fibroblasts and mitigated BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in murine models.

The functional roles of lncRNAs are largely dependent on their subcellular localization. In this study, we observed that DLEU2 is expressed not only in the nucleus of NIH-3T3 cells but also shows significant cytoplasmic localization following treatment with TGF-β1. Previous studies have established that cytoplasmic lncRNAs can function through a ceRNA mechanism by sponging miRNAs. In breast cancer, the lncRNA SEMA3B-AS1 regulates KLLN expression by competitively binding to miR-3940-3p, thereby inhibiting tumor progression [31]. Through bioinformatics analyses, we identified miR-29a-3p as a candidate miRNA with a binding site on DLEU2 and exhibiting the strongest correlation with the FAK signaling pathway. Subsequent experimental validation confirmed that DLEU2 promotes FAK phosphorylation by targeting and binding miR-29a-3p. It is well recognized that lncRNAs exert their functions not only through interactions with RNA and DNA but also via direct protein binding [32, 33]. lncRNA DACH1 suppresses pulmonary fibrosis by binding to SRSF1 and inhibiting CTNNB1 aggregation [34], while lncRNA00941 regulates autophagy and facilitates myofibroblast differentiation through interaction with ELAVL1 [35]. Upon binding to proteins, lncRNAs can influence post-translational modifications such as acetylation, methylation, ubiquitination, and phosphorylation. Notably, lncRNA GAS5 interacts with YAP, inducing its phosphorylation, which subsequently promotes ubiquitination and degradation, thereby inhibiting YAP signaling and exerting anti-colorectal cancer effects [24]. In the present study, we demonstrated that DLEU2 directly binds to FAK, enhancing its phosphorylation, which ultimately activates lung fibroblasts and contributes to the pathogenesis and progression of pulmonary fibrosis.

DLEU2 expression is upregulated in the peripheral blood of patients with IPF; however, its expression within immune cells of IPF patients has not yet been reported in the literature. In the context of autoimmune encephalomyelitis, the peptide Dleu2-17aa, encoded by DLEU2, promotes the generation of inducible regulatory T (iTreg) cells by interacting with SMAD family member 3 and enhancing its binding to the conserved non-coding sequence 1 region of Foxp3 [36]. Single-cell RNA sequencing analyses of esophageal carcinoma have demonstrated that DLEU2 expression is predominantly localized in T cells, especially within the exhausted T cell subset; furthermore, DLEU2 modulates tumor necrosis factor signaling through the TNFRSF1A/B pathway, thereby facilitating immune evasion and correlating with poor prognosis [37]. Immune cells are critically involved in the pathogenesis and progression of IPF. Reviews have indicated that regulatory T cells suppress Th1 cell activation, thereby modulating the Th1/Th2 balance within the lung microenvironment. Th2 cell-derived cytokines, interleukin-4 and interleukin-13, promote the polarization of alveolar macrophages toward an M2 profibrotic phenotype and drive the differentiation of circulating fibrocytes into fibroblasts. Additionally, interleukin-17 produced by Th17 cells enhances fibroblast activation. CD8+ T cells and tissue-resident memory CD4+ T cells appear to exert protective effects against fibrosis, although the precise mechanisms remain to be elucidated [38]. Within the context of IPF, it remains unclear which immune cell populations predominantly express DLEU2 and whether DLEU2 contributes to pulmonary fibrosis through interactions between immune cells and fibroblasts. Future investigations could initially employ single-cell sequencing of peripheral blood samples from IPF patients to delineate the cellular distribution of DLEU2, followed by mechanistic studies exploring the interactions among DLEU2, immune cells, and pulmonary fibroblasts.

It is now well recognized that miRNAs predominantly exert their regulatory effects by binding to the 3’untranslated region (3’UTR) of target mRNAs, thereby either suppressing the translation of these mRNAs into proteins or facilitating their degradation. To enhance the understanding of miRNA functions, the miRPathDB database integrates analyses of functional categories derived from Gene Ontology alongside signaling pathways from KEGG, Reactome, and WikiPathways, enabling the identification of associations between specific miRNAs and distinct biological processes or signaling pathways [39, 40]. In the context of clear cell renal cell carcinoma, initial investigations revealed a downregulation of miR-18a-5p expression in affected patients. Subsequent analyses utilizing the miRPathDB database demonstrated a significant correlation between miR-18a-5p and the HIF-1α signaling pathway, thereby substantiating the role of miR-18a-5p in modulating tumor prognosis via this pathway [41]. Similarly, our analyses using miRPathDB identified a notable association between miR-29a-3p and the FAK signaling pathway. Prior studies have implicated miR-29a-3p in the pathogenesis of various malignancies; for example, in endometrial cancer, miR-29a-3p is downregulated and functions to inhibit tumor progression through the VEGFA/CDC42/PAK1 signaling axis [42]. In prostate cancer, miR-29a-3p is sequestered by the lncRNA LINC00852, which modulates the expression of the target gene JARID2, thereby facilitating tumor cell proliferation and invasion [43]. Our current study demonstrates that miR-29a-3p expression is diminished in the peripheral blood of patients with IPF, as well as in lung fibroblasts treated with TGF-β1 and in lung tissues from mice subjected to BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis. Functionally, miR-29a-3p inhibits lung fibroblasts activation and FAK phosphorylation. Moreover, rescue assays conducted through the simultaneous suppression of DLEU2 and miR-29a-3p in NIH-3T3 cells and MRC5 cells substantiated that DLEU2 facilitates lung fibroblasts activation and enhances FAK phosphorylation via its interaction with miR-29a-3p.

This study presents several limitations: (1) the clinical sample size utilized was relatively limited; (2) the specific binding sites of DLEU2 in its interaction with FAK were not definitively characterized; (3) the potential influence of FAK phosphorylation on other post-translational modifications of FAK, including ubiquitination and acetylation, was not examined; and (4) the RNA modifications of DLEU2, as well as its upstream regulatory factors, were not investigated. These issues merit comprehensive and detailed exploration in subsequent studies.

Conclusion

As illustrated in Fig. 11, our research reveals that DLEU2 facilitates the phosphorylation of FAK through its interaction with miR-29a-3p or FAK itself, thereby enhancing the activation of lung fibroblasts and the deposition of extracellular matrix components. This mechanism contributes to the progression of lung fibrosis. Consequently, findings from both in vitro and in vivo studies suggest that targeting DLEU2 expression could represent a promising therapeutic approach for the prevention and treatment of lung fibrosis.

A conceptual framework illustrating the regulatory mechanisms of DLEU2 in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis. DLEU2 promotes the phosphorylation of FAK by interacting with miR-29a-3p or FAK directly, thereby enhancing the migratory capacity of fibroblasts. This interaction also facilitates the differentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts and stimulates the secretion of profibrotic factors, ultimately resulting in the accumulation of extracellular matrix. Collectively, these processes contribute to the progression of pulmonary fibrosis

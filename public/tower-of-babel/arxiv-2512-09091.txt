Local Banach Space Theoretic Approach to Bohr’s Theorem for Vector Valued Holomorphic and Pluriharmonic Functions
Himadri Halder

Abstract
. We study Bohr’s theorem for vector valued holomorphic and operator valued pluriharmonic functions on complete Reinhardt domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . Using invariants from local Banach space theory, we show that the associated Bohr radius is always strictly positive and obtain its asymptotic behavior separately in the finite- and infinite-dimensional settings. Our analysis provides asymptotic estimates for this constant on convex as well as non-convex complete Reinhardt domains. The framework developed here includes the classical Minkowski-space setting as a special case and applies to a wide class of Banach sequence spaces, including mixed Minkowski, Lorentz, and Orlicz spaces. We further establish a coefficient-type Schwarz–Pick lemma for operator valued pluriharmonic maps on complete Reinhardt domains.

1.
Introduction and the main results

This paper aims to contribute to the study of Bohr’s theorem for vector valued holomorphic and operator valued pluriharmonic functions through the framework of local Banach space theory, an area not previously explored.
The framework and unified approach developed here provide a powerful tool for studying both vector valued holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions in broad classes of Banach sequence spaces, representing one of the most significant applications of our results in functional and complex analysis.
Over the last three decades, Bohr’s theorem and its applications have occupied a central position in geometric function theory, connecting ideas from several complex variables, functional analysis, and operator theory. For a given complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Bohr radius
K_{n}(\Omega)
of
\Omega
is defined to be the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that for each holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
we have

(1.1)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}|\leq\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left|f(z)\right|.

Note that with this notation, Bohr’s celebrated power series theorem states that
K_{1}(\mathbb{D})=1/3
(see
[
11
]
). One of the most striking aspects of Bohr’s theorem arises in multidimensional settings, where the situation becomes significantly more complex and many problems remain open. In particular, there is still limited knowledge about the behavior of the constant
K_{n}(\Omega)
. A key challenge lies in determining the constant
K_{n}(\Omega)
, whose exact value remains unknown for
n>1
, even in the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{D}^{n}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{j}|<1\,\mbox{for all}\,1\leq j\leq n\}
. To advance this direction, researchers have focused on bounding
K_{n}(\Omega)
and analyzing its asymptotic behavior on general domains, and in particular on key domains such as the unit ball of Minkowski space and finite-dimensional complex Banach spaces. Beginning with the work of Dineen and Timoney
[
27
]
, further developed by Boas and Khavinson
[
9
,
10
]
, and later extended by Aizenberg
[
2
]
and by Defant and Frerick
[
22
]
, it is known that for every
1\leq q\leq\infty
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist constants
C,D>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}\leq K_{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})\leq D\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}},

where
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}:=\left\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<1\right\}
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}:=\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

A class of holomorphic functions
f
, defined in a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, is said to exhibit the Bohr phenomenon on
\Omega
if there exists a universal constant
r=r_{0}\in(0,1]
, called the Bohr radius of
\Omega
with respect to that class, such that the inequality (
1.1
) holds for all functions in that class. Since not every class of functions exhibits the Bohr phenomenon (see
[
3
,
6
,
8
]
), it is of interest to determine when a given class does. One of the main aims of this note is to address this question for vector valued functions within the framework of local Banach space theory, by establishing conditions under which the Bohr phenomenon can be verified in this setting. With this goal in mind, we first recall key facts about Banach space valued holomorphic functions and, for this purpose, introduce the Bohr radius for this class. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator between two complex Banach spaces and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
, denoted by
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for all holomorphic functions
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}:\Omega\rightarrow X
we have

(1.2)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{\alpha}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X},

where
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}
. Set
K(\Omega,p,U):=K_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
K(\Omega,p,X):=K_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
,
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
K(\Omega,p):=K_{1}(\Omega,p)
. Note that with this notations,
K(\Omega,1)
coincides with
K_{n}(\Omega)
, Bohr radius for scalar valued case defined by (
1.1
). The above definition is inspired by Defant
et al.

[
24
]
, extending their case
p=1
and
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n}
to a broader setting, thereby enabling the study of the Bohr phenomenon for more general domains and function classes. Bohr’s theorem for operator valued holomorphic functions defined in the unit disc was first investigated by Paulsen and Singh
[
29
]
.

The constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,1)
was first studied by Bombieri
[
12
]
, who determined its exact value for
\lambda\in[1,\sqrt{2}]
. Later, Bombieri and Bourgain
[
13
]
have investigated its exact asymptotic behavior as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Following these results, Defant
et al.
have introduced the more general constant
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1,U)
in
[
24
]
and established its asymptotic estimates in both finite and infinite dimensional Banach spaces
X
. On a related note, the constants
K(\mathbb{D},p)
and
K(\mathbb{D}^{n},p)
were first examined by Djakov and Ramanujan
[
26
]
, and later developed further by Bénéteau, Dahlner, Khavinson, Das
[
6
]
. In the Banach space valued setting, Blasco
[
8
]
has shown that
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)=0
for
p\in[1,2)
, while
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)>0
if and only if
X
is
p
-uniformly
\mathbb{C}
-convex for
2\geq p<\infty
.
These results demonstrate that
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)
is not necessarily nonzero for all Banach spaces
X
. This limitation motivated a refinement of its definition to ensure non-vanishing behavior for all
X
, which led to the introduction of the parameter
\lambda
in
[
24
, Definition 1.1]
. With these developments in mind, it is natural to ask the following question.

Question 1.1
.

For a given complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, can the the constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
be studied in detail? If so, does it always remain nonzero, and what can be said about its asymptotic behavior?

We answer affirmatively to this question in this paper. We now focus on complex-valued and operator-valued pluriharmonic functions defined on domains
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A twice continuously differentiable function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be plurharmonic if its restriction to every complex line is harmonic. Moreover, if
\Omega
is a simply connected domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin, then
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is pluriharmonic if it admits a representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h,g
are holomorphic on
\Omega
with
g(0)=0
. Similarly, for a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, a continuous function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is pluriharmonic if and only if
f
can be expressed as

(1.3)

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha},

where
h(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
and
g(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha}b_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
are
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
. Here
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the algebra of all bounded linear operators on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. For a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain, let
\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
denote the set of all bounded
X
valued pluriharmonic functions on
\Omega
, where
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Here bounded means
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}<\infty
for
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
.
The Bohr radius problem for complex valued harmonic functions was first explored in
[
1
]
, later extended to operator valued functions in
[
7
]
, and more recently to pluriharmonic Hilbert space valued functions in
[
17
]
. These advances naturally lead to the following question for operator-valued pluriharmonic functions.

Question 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain. Can we study the Bohr radius problem for operator valued pluriharmonic functions analogously to the inequality (
1.2
) and the constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
?

In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to this question, and to this end, we first introduce the notion of the powered Bohr radius for operator-valued pluriharmonic functions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y
be any complex Banach space. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
, denoted by
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
of the form (
1.3
) we have

(1.4)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}.

Set
R(\Omega,p,U):=R_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
R(\Omega,p,X):=R_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
R(\Omega,p):=R_{1}(\Omega,p)
. The asymptotic behaviour of the constants
R(\Omega,1)
and
R(\Omega,p)
have been studied in
[
16
,
18
]
whenever
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
.

We now proceed to present our main results. To the best of our knowledge, no attempt has yet been made to study operator-valued analogues of Bohr’s theorem for pluriharmonic functions in the framework of Definition
1.1
, not even in the simplest cases such as
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
p=1
,
\lambda=1
, and for the classical domains such as the unit balls of Minkowski spaces. This gap motivates us to pose the following question.

Question 1.3
.

For a given simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, can the the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
be studied in detail? If so, does it always remain nonzero, and what can be said about its asymptotic behavior?

In this paper, we address this question and begin by examining the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
through invariants from local Banach space theory, such as unconditional bases and projection constants. Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be an
n
-dimensional Banach space for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis (or equivalently, the open unit ball
B_{Z}
is a complete Reinhardt domain). There is a one-to-one correspondence between bounded convex complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the open unit balls of norms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. Indeed, the Minkowski functional
p_{\Omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
of
\Omega
defined by

p_{\Omega}(z):=\inf\left\{t>0:z/t\in\Omega\right\}

is a norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
\Omega
coincides with the open unit ball of
(\mathbb{C}^{n},p_{\Omega})
. Conversely, if
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
is a Banach space whose canonical basis is normalized
1
-unconditional, then its open unit ball
B_{Z}
is clearly a bounded convex complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Thus, the study of the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for the unit balls
\Omega=B_{Z}
of finite dimensional complex Banach spaces
(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
with the normalized
1
-unconditional canonical bases, is equivalent to studying
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
over bounded complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
In the following result, we first establish that
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is strictly positive whenever
\Omega=B_{Z}
. Combining this with the above correspondence and Lemma
4.3
, we conclude that
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is strictly positive for every bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, even in the non-convex case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for
\lambda>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq D.\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}
, where

D=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right\}\,&\text{for $\left\lVert U\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}$},\\[8.53581pt]
\max\left\{\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}&\text{for $0<\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}$}.\end{cases}

In particular, we obtain the following lower bound of the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius for operator-valued pluriharmonic functions whenever
U
is the identity operator on
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
1<\lambda
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p)\geq\frac{1}{2^{2+\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}.

Remark 1.1
.

One might ask whether Theorem
1.1
remains valid when
\left\lVert U\right\rVert=\lambda
. The following example shows that in this case the constant
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
may not be strictly positive. Thus the condition
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
is indeed necessary.

Example 1.1
.

For a bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

F_{k}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

F_{k}(z)=(i\,\cos\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}+(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}z_{1}+(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}\overline{z_{1}}

for
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
, where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
U=\lambda\,I
, with
I
the identity on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
F_{k}(0)=(i\,\cos\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Clearly,
F_{k}\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\left\lVert F_{k}\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
. Assume, for contradiction, that there exists
r_{0}>0
such that (
1.4
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
for all
z\in r_{0}\,\Omega
. Applying this to
F_{k}
yields,

\lambda^{p}\,|\cos\frac{1}{k}|^{p}+\lambda^{p}\,|\sin\frac{1}{k}|^{p}\,|z_{1}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}

all
z\in r_{0}\,\Omega
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. However, this inequality fails for sufficiently large
k
, since
\cos(1/k)\rightarrow 1
and
\sin(1/k)\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,while
z

can be chosen in
r_{0}\,\Omega
such that
z_{1}\neq 0
. This contradiction shows that the condition
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
in Theorem
1.1
is necessary.

1.3

R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)

\lambda

[

\lambda
-Bohr radius for operator valued pluriharmonic functions on any complete Reinhardt domains. The investigation separates naturally into two cases, according to whether
X
is finite and infinite dimensional. We first address the finite dimensional case, where the following theorem describes the exact asymptotic behavior of the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
when
\Omega=B_{Z}
and
U
is the identity operator on
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Recall that a Schauder basis
\{w_{k}\}
of a Banach space

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a Banach space such that

where

2
-convex, we have

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z_{n}},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},\,\frac{1}{e\,\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{2}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{(\sqrt{n})^{1-\theta}},\,\frac{1}{e^{(1-\theta)}\,\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}^{1-\theta}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $1<p<2$}.\end{cases}

and

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq d\,\lambda^{2/\log\,n}\,\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}\,\,n^{-1/p}\,\,\sqrt{\log\,n}.

The constants
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
, and
d
are same as in Theorem
1.2
.

To obtain the exact asymptotic behavior of the constant
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
in the case where
X
is infinite dimensional, we shall make use of Lemma
4.3
. This lemma reduces the problem to studying the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)
for a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, where the task of estimating bounds becomes considerably simpler. The most natural examples of such domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are the unit balls
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
of the Minkowski spaces
\ell^{n}_{q}
, with
1\leq q\leq\infty
. We therefore first analyze
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)
when
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. For finite dimensional
X
, Corollary
1.2
shows that the asymptotics of
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p)
involve a logarithmic term. In contrast, in the infinite dimensional case, the following theorem demonstrates that this logarithmic factor disappears, and the asymptotic decay of
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p)
is instead determined by the geometry of the Banach space
X
, namely its optimal cotype
Cot(X)
(see Section
2
for the definition).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an infinite dimensional complex Banach space and
\lambda>1
. Then

R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq\Psi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q):=\begin{cases}E_{5}\,\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}&\text{for $p>\min\{Cot(X),q\}$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{6}\,\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}\,\,n^{{\frac{1}{\min\{t,q\}}}-\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p\leq\min\{Cot(X),q\}$},\end{cases}

if
X
has a finite cotype
t
. Here
E_{5}>0
and
E_{6}>0
are constant independent of
X
and
n
.
On the other hand, we have

R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq\Psi_{2}(n,\lambda,p,q):=\begin{cases}2\,\lambda\,n^{\frac{1}{q}\,-\,\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $q\leq Cot(X)$},\\[5.69054pt]
2\,\lambda\,n^{\frac{1}{Cot(X)}\,-\,\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $q>Cot(X)$}.\end{cases}

We are now in a stage to give the bounds of the Bohr radius
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
for the unit ball
B_{Z}
of
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
whenever
X
is a infinite dimensional complex Banach space. In view of Theorem
1.4
and Lemma
4.3
, we obtain the following result. Before stating the result, we introduce the following standard notation. For bounded complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}):=\inf\left\{s>0:\Omega_{1}\subset s\,\Omega_{2}\right\}
. Recall that if
Z
and
W
are Banach sequence spaces then
S(B_{Z},B_{W})=\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow W\right\rVert
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space such that
\chi(\{e_{k}\}^{n}_{k=1})=1
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be infinite dimensional, and
\Psi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)
and
\Psi_{2}(n,\lambda,p,q)
be as in Theorem
1.4
. For
\lambda>1
,

\frac{\Psi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)}{S(B_{Z},B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})S(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{Z})}\,\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq S(B_{Z},B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})S(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{Z})\,\Psi_{2}(n,\lambda,p,q).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
Z
be a Banach sequence space and
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be infinite dimensional. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Z_{n}
be the linear span of
\{e_{k},\,k=1,\ldots,n\}
. Then for
\lambda>1

(1)

if
Z
is subset of
\ell_{2}
, we have

\frac{\Psi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)}{S(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{Z_{n}})}\,\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z_{n}},p,X)\leq S(B_{\ell^{n}_{2}},B_{Z_{n}})\,\Psi_{2}(n,\lambda,p,q);

(2)

if
Z
is symmetric and
2
-convex, we have

\frac{\sqrt{n}\Psi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)}{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}\,\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z_{n}},p,X)\leq\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}{\sqrt{n}}\,\Psi_{2}(n,\lambda,p,q).

Remark 1.2
.

We have thus far determined the asymptotic behavior of
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)
in the case
\Omega=B_{Z}
, where
B_{Z}
is the open unit ball of the Banach space
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
whose canonical basis is normalized
1
-unconditional. This framework also covers the bounded convex simply connected complete Reinhardt domains (see the correspondence preceding Theorem
1.1
). In light of Lemma
4.3
, Theorem
1.2
, and Theorem
1.5
, these asymptotic results extend to every bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain, without requiring convexity.

We now turn our attention to answering Question
1.1
. To begin with, it is useful to point out some important differences between the study of Bohr’s theorem for vector-valued holomorphic functions and that for pluriharmonic functions. First, in the pluriharmonic setting we restrict ourselves to simply connected complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. This restriction is necessary because the series expansion (
1.3
) is valid only on such domains. Second, in this framework we focus exclusively on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued pluriharmonic functions, and correspondingly restrict the operator
U
to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The reason is that the notion of complex conjugation is well defined in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, which is essential for our analysis. On the other hand, in the study of Bohr’s theorem for vector-valued holomorphic functions, the situation is somewhat simpler. Here, it suffices to consider complete Reinhardt domains (without the additional requirement of simply connectedness) because the domain of convergence of multivariable power series is complete Reinhardt domain and within its domain of convergence it defines a holomorphic function. Moreover, since complex conjugation does not play any role in this context, we are not constrained to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued functions. Instead, we can work with holomorphic functions taking values in any complex Banach space. Therefore, in addressing Question
1.1
, we adopt the following framework: we consider bounded holomorphic functions with values in an arbitrary complex Banach space
X
, defined on complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In the following theorem, we first establish that the constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is nonzero whenever
\Omega=B_{Z}
, which serves as the holomorphic analogue of Theorem
1.1
. By applying the same arguments as in the pluriharmonic case (see the discussion preceding Theorem
1.1
), it then follows that
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is nonzero for every bounded complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
and
Y
be any complex Banach spaces and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for
\lambda>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{C}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}},

where

C=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right\}\,&\text{for $\left\lVert U\right\rVert\geq 1$},\\[8.53581pt]
\max\left\{\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}&\text{for $0<\left\lVert U\right\rVert<1$}.\end{cases}

For
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the bounds of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
in Theorems
1.2
–
1.6
extend to
K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
for any complex Banach space
X
with different constants, ensuring that the asymptotic behaviors of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
and
K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
coincide for
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The proofs are analogous and omitted.

2.
Application

This section presents several applications of our results to various classes of sequence spaces. We begin with the family of mixed Minkowski spaces. Recall the definition of mixed Minkowski spaces:

\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t}):=\{(z_{k})^{m}_{k=1}:z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

with
\left\lVert(z_{k})^{m}_{k=1}\right\rVert_{s,t}:=(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lVert z_{k}\right\rVert^{s}_{t})^{1/s}
. When
s=t
, the spaces
\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t})
reduces exactly to the classical Minkowski spaces
\ell^{mn}_{s}
. Earlier studies such as those in
[
16
,
24
]
focused primarily on classical Minkowski spaces. The framework developed in this article incorporates that setting as a particular case while at the same time treating a substantially broader family of sequence spaces. This unified approach enables us to study both holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions in general sequence spaces.

Example 2.1

(
Mixed spaces).

Let

. Then

(1)

Let

(2)

If

is infinite dimensional then

\frac{\Psi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,2)}{m^{1/2-1/s}\,n^{1/2-1/t}}\,\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq m^{1/2-1/s}\,n^{1/2-1/t}\,\Psi_{2}(n,\lambda,p,2).

Here, the constants
E_{1}(X)

,

are as in Theorem

.

.

,
1.5
, and
1.6
, together with the preceding observations. The proof is complete.

3.
Preliminaries

We use standard notation and notions from Banach space theory. All Banach spaces
W
are assumed to be complex, their topological duals are denoted by
W^{*}
and their open unit balls by
B_{W}
. A Banach space
W
is said to have cotype
t\in[0,\infty]
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for arbitrarily chosen vectors
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W
we have

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert w_{k}\right\rVert^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{n}(s)w_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
r_{n}
it the
n
-th Rademacher function on
[0,1]
and we write

Cot(W):=\inf\{2\leq t\leq\infty:W\,\mbox{has cotype}\,t\}.

It is worth mentioning that every Banach space
W
has cotype
\infty
, and whenever
Cot(W)=\infty
we denote
1/Cot(W)=0
. A Schauder basis
\{w_{k}\}
of a Banach space
W
is said to be unconditional if there is a constant
c\geq 1
such that
\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\epsilon_{j}\mu_{j}w_{j}\right\rVert\leq c\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}w_{j}\right\rVert

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, all
\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and all
\epsilon_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\epsilon_{j}|\leq 1
, and in this case the best constant is denoted by
\chi(\{w_{k}\})
, called unconditional basis constant of
\{w_{k}\}
. We say that
\{w_{k}\}
is
1
-unconditional basis whenever
\chi(\{w_{k}\})=1
. Moreover, the unconditional basis constant of
W
is defined to be
\chi(W):=\inf\chi(\{w_{k}\})\in[1,\infty]
, the infimum taken over all unconditional basis
\{w_{k}\}
of
W
. A Banach space
Z
for which
\ell_{1}\subset Z\subset c_{0}
(with normal inclusions) is said to be a Banach sequence space if the canonical sequence
\{e_{k}\}
forms a
1
-unconditional basis. Recall that a Banach lattice
Z
is said to be
2
-convex if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in Z
. Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space and
Y
be any Banach space, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)
the space of all
m
-homogeneous polynomials
Q:Z\rightarrow Y
of the form
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
, together with the norm
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)}:=\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}|Q(z)|
. By
\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y))
we denote the unconditional basis constant of the monomials
z^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
.
Set
X:=\mathcal{B}(H)
, the Banach space of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
H
. Denote by
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,X)
the space of all
m
-homogeneous pluriharmonic polynomials
P:Z\rightarrow X
of the form

(3.1)

P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}

and set
\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,X)}:=\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}|||P(z)||
.

A bounded linear operator
U:W\rightarrow Y
between two Banach spaces
W
and
Y
is called
(r,t)
-summing,
r,t\in[1,\infty)
, if there is a constant
C>0
such that for each choice of finitely many
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W
we have that

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert U(w_{k})\right\rVert^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq C\,\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\phi(w_{k})|^{t}\right)^{1/t}.

The best such
C
is usually denoted by
\pi_{r,t}(U)
. In the case
r=t
we call
U
is
r
- summing and write the best constant as
\pi_{r}(U)
.

4.
Key Properties and Supporting Lemmas

In the scalar case, a standard approach to deriving nontrivial estimates for Bohr radii of holomorphic functions is to first analyze
m
-homogeneous polynomials, as this reduction often isolates the essential difficulties while providing sharper insight into the general framework. The following definition introduces the
m
-homogeneous analogue of Definition
1.1
, which will serve as the basis for our further developments. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
with respect to
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X)
, denoted by
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for every pluriharmonic polynomials
P:\Omega\rightarrow X
of the form (
3.1
) we have

(4.1)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}.

Here
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}
. Set
R^{m}(\Omega,p,U):=R^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
R^{m}(\Omega,p,X):=R^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
,
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
R^{m}(\Omega,p):=R^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p)
. It is straightforward that
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=\lambda^{1/m}\,R^{m}(\Omega,p,U)
. Similarly as above, we can define the constant
K^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for the class
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m}(W)

i.e.
, for any Banach space
W
valued
m
-homogeneous holomorphic polynomial
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
. Clearly, we have
K^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=\lambda^{1/m}\,K^{m}(\Omega,p,U)
. In light of Bohnenblust-Hille inequality, it is known that the constant
K^{m}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1)
is hypercontractive due to Defant
et al.

[
23
]
.

Schwarz–Pick type lemmas and distortion estimates for holomorphic and harmonic functions constitute an important direction of research in geometric function theory. Coefficient-type Schwarz–Pick estimates for complex-valued harmonic functions on the unit disc were obtained in
[
1
,
15
]
, and subsequently extended to complex-valued pluriharmonic functions on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
in
[
16
, Theorem 2.7]
.
In analogy with
[
16
, Theorem 2.7]
, we now extend these estimates to the operator valued setting by establishing a coefficient-type Schwarz–Pick lemma for pluriharmonic functions defined on general complete Reinhardt domains. This lemma constitutes a fundamental ingredient in the proofs of several main results of this article.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{PH}(Z,X)
be of the form (
1.3
). Then for all
|\alpha|=m\geq 1
, we have
\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}\pm b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 4\,\left\lVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\,I-{\operatorname{Re}\,}(a_{0})\right\rVert
. Moreover, if
B_{Z}=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
then
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\,\rho_{\alpha}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
,
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\,\rho_{\alpha}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
, where
\rho_{\alpha}:=\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{1/q}
.

Proof
.
For any fixed
z\in B_{Z}
, we consider the function
g:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
by
g(\xi)=f(\xi z)
for
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
g
is a
\mathcal{B}(H)
-valued harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D},X}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
. Observe that
g
has the following expansion

g(\xi)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right)\,\xi^{m}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right)^{*}\,\bar{\xi}^{m}

for
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
, and hence from the
[
7
, (2.12)]
we conclude
\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 4\left\lVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D},X}\,I-{\operatorname{Re}\,}(a_{0})\right\rVert\leq 4\left\lVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\,I-{\operatorname{Re}\,}(a_{0})\right\rVert
.
As
h=-if\in\mathcal{PH}(Z,X)
and
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
, the desired estimate for
\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
follows by applying the preceding inequality to
h
. Now assume that
B_{Z}=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. We consider the operator-valued holomorphic function
\tilde{h}(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}
on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. For any
\phi\in X^{*}
with
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert\leq 1
,
\tilde{g}=\phi\circ\tilde{h}
is a complex-valued holomorphic function on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
with
\left\lVert\tilde{g}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}\leq\left\lVert\tilde{h}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}
. Moreover,
\tilde{g}(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\phi(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}
for
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. Observe that
\tilde{g}
is also a complex-valued pluriharmonic function, and hence from
[
16
, Theorem 2.6]
, we have for
|\alpha|=m\geq 1
,

\left\lVert a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\right\rVert=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}|\phi(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\lVert\tilde{g}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\lVert\tilde{h}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}.

Finally, the bound of
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\right\rVert
follows by considering the function
\tilde{P}=-i\tilde{h}
.
∎

The following result constitutes the basic link between the Bohr radius for pluriharmonic functions and the Bohr radius for homogeneous pluriharmonic functions. It plays a crucial role in the proofs of our main results. This lemma can be regarded as the pluriharmonic analogue of
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
, which concerns the Minkowski spaces in the context of the powered Bohr inequality. The proof follows along similar lines as that of
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
, and therefore, we omit the details.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y
any complex Banach space, and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty),q\in[1,\infty]
,
\lambda>1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)

(2)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\lambda\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Analogous results of Lemma
4.2
hold for
K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
and
K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
.

The following lemma compares the Bohr radius constants for two complete Reinhardt domains. Its proof is similar to that of
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
and is therefore omitted.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two bounded complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then we have

\frac{R_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U)}{S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})}\,\leq R_{\lambda}(\Omega_{1},p,U)\leq S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})\,R_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U).

As an immediate application, we observe that the Bohr radius
R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)
serves as a universal lower bound for the Bohr radius
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
of every complete Reinhardt domain.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, we have

R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\geq R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U).

We use the following remarkable result by Maurey and Pisier to prove the desired upper bound in the case of infintedimensional complex Banach space.

Theorem A
.

[
25
, Theorem 14.5]

Given any infinite dimensional complex Banach space
X
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}/2\leq\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}z_{j}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{Cot(X)}
for every choice of
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Clearly, setting
z=e_{j}
, gives
\left\lVert x_{j}\right\rVert\geq 1/2
, where
e_{j}
is the
j
-th canonical basis vector of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

5.
Proof of the main results

We now begin the proofs of the main results. Let
f(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
be an
X
valued function. For any
p\geq 1
, we write
M_{f,p}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}
, which will be used throughout the proofs.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\beta|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,X)
. Consider the polynomial
H(z):=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}
. Applying the definition of the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius for holomorphic functions to the function
H
and then from Lemma
4.1
, for
z\in B_{Z}
, we obtain

M_{H,p}\leq\frac{\lambda^{p}}{\left(K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\right)^{pm}}\,\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}\leq\frac{2^{p}\lambda^{p}}{\left(K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\right)^{pm}}\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}.

Similarly, considering
G(z):=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}
,
we obtain

M_{G,p}\leq\frac{2^{p}\lambda^{p}}{\left(K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\right)^{pm}}\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}

for
z\in B_{Z}
. Now, for
p\geq 1
, using these inequalities, for
z\in B_{Z}
we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(M_{H,p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(M_{G,p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\frac{4\lambda}{\left(K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\right)^{m}}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}.

One can verify that the inequality (
5.1
) remains true when the left-hand side is replaced by
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
for
z\in B_{Z}
.
By making use of these inequalities, for
z\in B_{Z}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}\right)|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\left(\frac{K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)}{2^{\frac{1}{pm}}(4\lambda)^{\frac{1}{m}}}\right)^{-pm}\lambda^{p}\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X},

which immediately shows that

(5.2)

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)}{2^{\frac{1}{pm}}(4\lambda)^{\frac{1}{m}}}.

According to (
5.2
), it is enough to focus on finding a lower bound for
K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
, as this directly leads to a lower bound for
R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
. Let
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,X)
. For any
z\in B_{Z}
we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z}}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z}}(|z_{1}|^{p}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{p})^{m},

which is less than or equals to
\lambda^{p}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z}}\left(\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}\right)^{pm}
.
Therefore,

K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}
,
which together (
5.2
) and Lemma
4.2
(1) yields

(5.3)

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}.

On the other hand, for any
z\in B_{Z}
, we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z}}\left(\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}\right)^{pm}.

Thus,
K^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}.

Using this from Lemma
4.2
(2) and (
5.2
), we deduce that

(5.4)

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}},\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\left\lVert U\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}

and

(5.5)

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}},\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,0<\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Finally, the conclusion follows from (
5.3
), (
5.4
), and (
5.5
). This completes the proof.
∎

To simplify computations in the proofs of the remaining theorems, we may assume without loss of generality that all pluriharmonic functions
f\in\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)
satisfy
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1
, where
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. In other words, we restrict attention to the class

\mathcal{BPH}(B_{Z},X):=\{f:f\in\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)\,\mbox{ with}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1\}.

This is justified since any
f\in\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)
with
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}>1
can be rescaled by refining the coefficients. Indeed, if
f
has the form (
1.3
) with
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}>1
, then defining
F
by
F(z)=f(z)/\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
yields
\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}=1
with coefficients
a_{\alpha}
,
b_{\alpha}
replaced by
a_{\alpha}/\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
and
b_{\alpha}/\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
in (
1.3
). In the same reasoning, we consider

\mathcal{BPH}(^{m}B_{Z},X):=\{P:P\in\mathcal{PH}(^{m}B_{Z},X)\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1\}.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We want to first obtain a lower bound of
R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
when
X
is finite dimensional. For all
m\geq 1
, let
P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\beta|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{BPH}(^{m}B_{Z},X)
. The proof is presented in three separate cases, depending on the values of
p
:
p=1
,
p\geq 2
, and
1<p<2
. Let
H
and
G
be the functions as defined in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Case
p=1
:

For each
\phi\in B_{X^{*}}
, we now consider the holomorphic polynomial
\tilde{H}:=\phi\circ H:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\tilde{H}(z)=\phi(H(z))=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}
.
Clearly,
|\tilde{H}(z)|\leq\left\lVert H(z)\right\rVert
for each
z
. Since
X
is finite dimensional, the identity operator
I_{X}
is
1
-summing. Then for all
z\in B_{Z}
, we have

M_{H,1}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right|\right),

which is again less than or equals to

\pi_{1}(I_{X})\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right|z^{\alpha}\right|.

This quantity by the definition of
\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))
is less than or equals to

\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right|.

Thus,

M_{H,1}\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}|\tilde{H}(z)|\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}.

By considering the holomorphic polynomial
\tilde{G}:=\phi\circ G:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, proceeding as above we obtain
M_{G,1}\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\,\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
for all
z\in B_{Z}
. By making use of these inequalities, and then from Lemma
4.1
, for all
z\in B_{Z}
, a simple computation shows that

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\right)\leq 4\,\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)).

Similarly, for all
z\in B_{Z}
, we have
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq 4\,\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))
.
The last two inequalities give

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert)|z^{\alpha}|\leq 8\,\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)),

which yields

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq E_{1}(X)\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\left(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\right)^{-\frac{1}{m}},

where
E_{1}(X)
is a constant depending on
X
. Plugging the estimates from
[
19
, (4.5), (4.6), pp. 187]
in the above inequality, we obtain

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq E_{1}(X)\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{1}{\left(\frac{m^{m}}{m!}\right)^{1/m}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}\right\},

and hence the desired lower bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from Lemma
4.2
(1) and using the fact
\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(m^{m}/m!)^{1/m}=e
. This completes the proof for
p=1
.

Case
p\geq 2
:
Let
p\geq 2
. Note that

M_{H,p}=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

for any
z\in B_{Z}
. Hence, by the fact
\left\lVert(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})/2\right\rVert=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}|\phi((a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})/2)|
and the estimate in
[
19
, p. 187]
, we obtain

M_{H,p}\leq\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2m}\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{2}_{B_{Z},X}
.
Similarly, we have
M_{G,p}\leq\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2m}\left\lVert G\right\rVert^{2}_{B_{Z},X}
.

Then these two inequalities together with Lemma
4.1
yield

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{\frac{2m}{p}}2^{\frac{2}{p}}.

This inequality remains valid if the left hand quantity replaced by
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
.
A little calculation using these inequalities shows

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq 2^{p+3}\,\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2m},

and as a consequence,

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq 2^{-\frac{p+3}{pm}}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\frac{2}{p}}
.
Finally, the desired lower bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from Lemma
4.2
(1).

Case
1<p<2
:
For any
1<p<2
, using Hölder’s inequality we obtain

M_{H,p}\leq\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\right)^{2-p}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{p-1}.

Lemma
4.1
, combined with the bounds of
M_{H,1}
and
M_{H,2}
from the above two cases, yields

M_{H,p}\leq 2^{p}\,(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{2-p}\,(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{2-p}\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow X\right\rVert^{2(p-1)m}.

This last inequality remains valid if we replace the left-hand side by
M_{G,p}
as well. Thus, we obtain

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq 4\,(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{(2-p)/p}\,(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)))^{(2-p)/p}\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow X\right\rVert^{2(p-1)/(pm)}.

This inequality remains valid if the left-hand side is replaced by
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
. Using these, a simple calculation shows that

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq 2^{2p+1}(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{2-p}\,(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)))^{2-p}\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow X\right\rVert^{2(p-1)m},

and thus,

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq E_{2}(X)\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{-\frac{(2-p)}{pm}}\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow X\right\rVert^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{p}}
, where
E_{2}(X)
is a constant depending on
X
and
p
. Plugging the estimates from
[
19
, (4.5), (4.6), pp. 187]
in the last inequality and letting
\theta=(2(p-1))/p
, we obtain

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq E_{2}(X)\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{(\sqrt{n})^{1-\theta}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\theta}}{\left(\frac{m^{m}}{m!}\right)^{(1-\theta)/m}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert^{1-\theta}}\right\},

and hence the desired lower bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from Lemma
4.2
(1) and using the fact
\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(m^{m}/m!)^{1/m}=e
. We now want to find the desired upper bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
. In view of Lemma
4.3
, it is enough to know the upper bound of
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)
for a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
. For
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, from
[
4
]
, it is known that

K_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq d\,\lambda^{\frac{2}{\log\,n}}\,n^{1/q}\,\frac{\left(\log n\right)^{1-\left(1/\min\{q,2\}\right)}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}}=d\,\lambda^{\frac{2}{\log\,n}}\,n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\left(1/\min\{q,2\}\right)}

for some constant
d>0
. Now the desired upper bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from the fact
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq K_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
and from Lemma
4.3
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We first recall the following well-known observations: if
Y=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
is a Banach space for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis then
(i)

\left\lVert Id:Y\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{z\in B_{Y}}(\sum_{m=1}^{n}|z_{m}|^{2})^{1/2}
,
(ii)

\left\lVert Id:Y\rightarrow\ell_{1}^{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{z\in B_{Y}}\sum_{m=1}^{n}|z_{m}|=\left\lVert\sum_{m=1}^{n}e^{*}_{m}\right\rVert_{Y^{*}}
for all
z\in Y
. We now come to the proof of this theorem.

Proof of part
(1)
:
Since
Z\subset\ell_{2}
, we may assume that the inclusion map
Z\subset\ell_{2}
has norm
1
. The desired conclusion then follows from Theorem
1.2
, together with the preceding facts
(i)
and
(ii)
.

Proof of part
(2)
:
In view of
[
28
, Theorem 1.d.5]
, we may assume that
M^{(2)}(X)=1
. Consequently,
\left\lVert Id:\ell_{2}^{n}\rightarrow Z_{n}\right\rVert=1
. The proof now follows from Theorem
1.2
, the above facts
(i)
and
(ii)
, and
[
19
, (5.3), p. 191]
.This completes the proof. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We first want to find the lower bound. We split the proof into the following cases. Let
H
and
G
be the functions as defined in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Let
P
be as in Theorem
1.2
.

Case
p>q
:
Since
p>q
, we have
M_{H,p}\leq M_{H,q}
for
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
. So, estimate in
[
21
, (3
.7), p. 24]
give

\displaystyle M_{H,p}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(e^{\frac{m}{q}}\left(\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}\right)^{q}|z^{\alpha}|^{q}=e^{m}\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{q}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{q}\big)^{m}\leq e^{m}\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{q}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}

for any
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
.
Similarly,
M_{G,p}\leq e^{m}\left\lVert G\right\rVert^{q}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}
. By Lemma
4.1
, using the last two inequalities we deduce that
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq 2\,e^{\frac{m}{p}}\,2^{\frac{q}{p}}
. This inequality remains valid if the left-hand side is replaced by
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
.
By making use of these inequalities, we obtain

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq 2^{p+1}\,e^{m}\,2^{q}
,
which shows that

R^{m}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq 2^{-\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{1}{m}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,2^{-\frac{q}{pm}}
. Finally, in view of Lemma
4.2
(2), we deduce that

R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}\,2^{-\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)}\,e^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,2^{-\frac{q}{p}}.

Case
p>Cot(X)
:
In view of Lemma
4.4
, to find a lower bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
it is enough to find that of
R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,X)
. Let
f\in\mathcal{BPH}(\mathbb{D}^{n},X)
be of the form (
1.3
). For any complex Banach space
X
with a finite cotype
t
, by making use of
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
to the function
H
we have

(5.6)

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq c^{m}\,\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left\lVert H(z)\right\rVert^{t}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq c^{m}\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}^{n},X}

for some constant
c>0
. Now, for any
p>Cot(X)
, there exists
t\in[Cot(X),p)
such that
X
has cotype
t
, and hence
X
has cotype
p
. Using (
5.6
), we have
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\right)/2\right\rVert^{p}\leq c^{mp}\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{p}_{\mathbb{D}^{n},X}
.
Similarly, by considering the function
G
, there exists constant
d>0
such that
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\right)/2\right\rVert^{p}\leq d^{mp}\left\lVert G\right\rVert^{p}_{\mathbb{D}^{n},X}
.
For any
p\geq 1
, using last two inequalities from Lemma
4.1
we deduce that
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p}(c^{m}+d^{m})^{p}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p}(c^{m}+d^{m})^{p}
. By these inequalities, for
z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, we obtain

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)\leq 2^{p+1}\,(c^{m}+d^{m})^{p},

which shows

R^{m}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,X)\geq E_{5}\,2^{-\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{1}{m}}
,
where
E_{5}>0
is a constant independent of
n
. In view of Lemma
4.2
(2) and Lemma
4.4
we get

R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,X)\geq E_{5}\,2^{-\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)}\,\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}.

Case
p\leq\min\{Cot(X),q\}
:
Let
p\leq\min\{Cot(X),q\}
, then
p\leq t
if
X
has cotype
t
. Let
\beta:=\min\{t,q\}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{BPH}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
be of the form (
1.3
). Then from the above two cases, for any
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, there exists constant
c>0
such that

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{\beta}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{\beta})|(\delta\,z)^{\alpha}|^{\beta}\leq\lambda^{\beta}
,
where
\delta=c\,(\lambda^{p}-1)^{1/p}/\lambda
. Using this inequality together with Hölder’s inequality, and letting
r=\gamma\,\delta\in(0,1)
, we obtain
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p})|(r\,z)^{\alpha}|^{p}
is less than or equals to

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{pm}((\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}(\delta z)\right\rVert^{\beta})^{p/\beta}+(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}(\delta z)\right\rVert^{\beta})^{p/\beta})(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1)^{1-p/\beta}\leq\lambda^{p}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\big(c_{1}\gamma n^{\left(1/p-1/\beta\right)}\big)^{pm},

for some constant
c_{1}>0
, independent of
n
. Now the last quantity is less than or equals to
\lambda^{p}
if
\gamma=c_{2}\,n^{\left(1/p-1/\beta\right)}
, where
c_{2}
is a constant, independent of
n
. Hence, we conclude that

R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq E_{6}\,\,\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{1/p}}{\lambda}\,\,n^{\left({\frac{1}{\beta}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}

for some constant
E_{6}>0
, independent of
n
.
Now, we want to find the upper bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
. To prove this, we shall make use of Theorem
A
. By considering
z=e_{j}
in Theorem
A
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in X
such that
1/2\leq\left\lVert a_{j}\right\rVert
for each
1\leq j\leq n
. Using this inequality and considering the
1
-homogeneous polynomial
F\in\mathcal{PH}(^{1}B_{Z},X)
of the form
F(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}a_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a^{*}_{j}\bar{z_{j}}
, from Theorem
A
and the definition of
R^{1}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p)
, a simple computation shows that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{2\,n}{2^{p}\,n^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\left\lVert a_{j}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert a_{j}\right\rVert^{p})n^{-\frac{p}{q}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X}}{(R^{1}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X))^{p}}\leq\frac{2}{(R^{1}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X))^{p}}\,\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{p}_{Cot(X)}.

For
q\leq Cot(X)
, we have
\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{Cot(X)}\leq 1
, which by (
5.7
) give

R^{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq 2\,n^{1/q\,-\,1/p}.
On the other hand, for
q>Cot(X)
,
\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{Cot(X)}\leq n^{1/Cot(X)\,-\,1/p}
, and so from (
5.7
) we obtain
R^{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p)\leq 2\,n^{1/Cot(X)\,-\,1/p}
. It is known that
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq R^{1}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)=\lambda\,R^{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
, and hence the upper bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
immediately follows. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we have shown that for
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
K^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}
and
K^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}
, and the same reasoning extends to any complex Banach space
X
. The desired bound of
K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
then follows from Remark
4.1
, completing the proof.
∎

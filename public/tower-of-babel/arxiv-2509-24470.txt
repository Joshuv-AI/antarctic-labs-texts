A Source Identification Problem for the Bi-Parabolic Equation Containing a Poly-harmonic Operator
Dang Duc Trong, Bui Thanh Duy, Nguyen Dang Minh

Abstract
In this paper, we address the source identification problem for the bi-parabolic equation involving a operator. Specifically, we investigate the equation ( ∂ t + 𝔄 ) 2 u ( t ) = ψ ( t ) f \displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathfrak{A})^{2}u(t)=\psi(t)f , where 𝔄 \displaystyle\mathfrak{A} denotes a poly-harmonic operator. Given the perturbed data of ψ \displaystyle\psi and u ⁡ ( T ) \displaystyle u(T) (where T > 0 \displaystyle T>0 ), our objective is to determine f \displaystyle f . Although several scientific publications have explored regularization techniques for bi-parabolic problems, the existing literature remains limited. By relaxing certain conditions on the function ψ \displaystyle\psi and employing a truncation regularization method while considering the problem on an unbounded domain, we believe our results provide valuable insights.

2
The ill-posed nature of the problem

Using the Fourier transform, we get from (
1
) and (
2
) the equation

\widehat{u}_{tt}(\mathbf{z},t)+2|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}\widehat{u}_{t}(\mathbf{z},t)+|\mathbf{z}|^{4\sigma}\widehat{u}(\mathbf{z},t)=\psi(t)\widehat{f}({\mathbf{z}}).

Here, we note in
[
5
,
3
]
that

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u}(\mathbf{z})=|\mathbf{z}|^{\gamma}\widehat{u}(\mathbf{z}).

We deduce from (
2
) that
\displaystyle\widehat{u}(\mathbf{z},0)=\widehat{u}_{t}(\mathbf{z},0)=0
and the solution of the above differential equation with respect to
\displaystyle t
is given explicitly by

\widehat{u}(\mathbf{z},t)=\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})e^{-|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}(t-s)}\left(t-s\right)\psi(s)ds.

At
\displaystyle t=T
, we have

\widehat{u}(\mathbf{z},T)=\int_{0}^{T}\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})e^{-|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}(T-s)}\left(T-s\right)\psi(s)ds.

This is the equation (
4
).

For all
\displaystyle f
in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if we define

\mathbf{K}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})\,\widehat{f}\big),

then equation (
4
) can be rewritten in the form

\mathbf{K}f=h.

In the sense of Hadamard, a problem is said to be
ill-posed
if it fails to satisfy at least one of the following three conditions:

(i)

the existence of a solution,

(ii)

the uniqueness of that solution, and

(iii)

the stability of the solution with respect to perturbations in the data.
The first two conditions are clear; the third will be explained in more detail below.

The problem
\displaystyle\mathbf{K}f=h
is said to be unstable if there exists a sequence

\displaystyle\{f_{n}\}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\mathbf{K}f_{n}\to\mathbf{K}f\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

but

f_{n}\not\to f\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We now investigate whether our problem is ill-posed in the sense of Hadamard.

This will be clearly demonstrated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\displaystyle\psi\in L^{1}(0,T)
.

(a)

Denote

\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}:=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})=0}\right\}.

Equation (
4
) has a solution in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

if and only if

\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z})=0\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}}\left|\frac{\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z})}{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\right|^{2}\,d\mathbf{z}<\infty.

(b)

Denote

N_{\psi}:=\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:\psi(\nu)\neq 0\}.

If
\displaystyle m(N_{\psi})>0
, then
\displaystyle m_{d}(\mathcal{Z}_{\psi})=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})\neq 0
for almost every
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and equation (
4
) has at most one solution

\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(c)

For every
\displaystyle\beta>0
, define

\pi_{\psi}(\beta):=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant\beta\right\}.

If
\displaystyle m(N_{\psi})>0
, then
\displaystyle m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(\beta))>0
for every
\displaystyle\beta>0
,
and the problem is unstable; that is, we can find a sequence

\displaystyle\{f_{n}\}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\mathbf{K}f_{n}\to 0\quad\text{but}\quad f_{n}\not\to 0.

(a)

Let
\displaystyle f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(F)
, where

F(\mathbf{z})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z})}{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})},&\mathbf{z}\notin\mathcal{Z}_{\psi},\\[8.00003pt]
0,&\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to verify that
\displaystyle f
is a solution of (
4
).

(b)

We show that
\displaystyle H_{\psi}(\nu)\not\equiv 0
for
\displaystyle\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.
Assume, to the contrary, that
\displaystyle H_{\psi}(\nu)=0
for every
\displaystyle\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

{H_{\psi}(\nu)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\nu^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{T}(T-s)^{n+1}\,\psi(s)\,ds,\quad\forall\nu\in\mathbb{R}.}

It follows that

{\int_{0}^{T}t^{n+1}\psi(T-t)\,dt=0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},}

which implies

\int_{0}^{T}P(t)t\psi(T-t)\,dt=0

for every polynomial
\displaystyle P
. Since the set of polynomials is dense in
\displaystyle C[0,T]
, we deduce that
\displaystyle\psi=0
a.e., contradicting the assumption
\displaystyle m(N_{\psi})>0
. Hence
\displaystyle H_{\psi}\not\equiv 0
.

Moreover, since
\displaystyle H_{\psi}
is analytic, its set of real zeros

Z_{\psi}=\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:\,\nu\geqslant 0,\ H_{\psi}(\nu)=0\}

consists of isolated and at most countably many points.
Let
\displaystyle\nu_{j}\in Z_{\psi}
,
\displaystyle j\in J\subset\mathbb{N}
, satisfy

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\dots
. Then

\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}=\bigcup_{j\in J}\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}=\nu_{j}\}.

Therefore
\displaystyle m_{d}(\mathcal{Z}_{\psi})=0
.

Now, suppose
\displaystyle f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})\,\widehat{f}_{k}(\mathbf{z})=\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z}),\quad k=1,2.

This implies

H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})\,\widehat{f}_{1}(\mathbf{z})=H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})\,\widehat{f}_{2}(\mathbf{z}).

For
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}\notin\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{1}(\mathbf{z})=\widehat{f}_{2}(\mathbf{z})
.
Since
\displaystyle m_{d}(\mathcal{Z}_{\psi})=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{1}=\widehat{f}_{2}
a.e., and thus
\displaystyle f_{1}=f_{2}
a.e.

(c)

The proof will consist of two steps.

Step 1. Prove that
\displaystyle m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(\beta))>0
for every
\displaystyle\beta>0
.

If
\displaystyle m(N_{\psi})>0
then the function
\displaystyle H_{\psi}(\nu)
is
analytic with respect to
\displaystyle\nu
and
\displaystyle H_{\psi}(\nu)\not\equiv 0
. On the other hand, using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem yields

\lim_{\nu\to\infty}H_{\psi}(\nu)=\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(T-s)\nu}(T-s)\psi(s)\,ds=0.

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{\nu\to\infty}H_{\psi}(\nu)=0
, for any
\displaystyle\beta>0
there exists
\displaystyle r>0
such that

|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\leqslant\beta,\quad\forall\nu\geqslant r.

Therefore

[r,\infty)\subset\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\leqslant\beta\},

which implies that the set
\displaystyle\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\leqslant\beta\}
has positive Lebesgue measure. It means

m\big(\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:\penalty\ |H_{\psi}(\nu)|\leqslant\beta\}\big)>0.

Next, we have

m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(\beta))=\int_{\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ |H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant\beta\}}d\mathbf{z}.

Using spherical coordinates
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}=\rho\mathbf{w}
with
\displaystyle\rho=|\mathbf{z}|\geqslant 0
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{w}=\frac{\mathbf{z}}{|\mathbf{z}|}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, where

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \big|\ |\mathbf{x}|=1\right\}

is the unit sphere in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\displaystyle d\Sigma(\mathbf{w})
denotes its surface measure, we have

d\mathbf{z}=\rho^{d-1}\,d\rho\,d\Sigma(\mathbf{w}),

so that

\displaystyle m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(\beta))

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\rho\geqslant 0:\ |H_{\psi}(\rho^{2\sigma})|\leqslant\beta\}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\rho^{\,d-1}\,d\Sigma(\mathbf{w})\right)d\rho=\omega_{d}\int_{\{\rho\geqslant 0:\ |H_{\psi}(\rho^{2\sigma})|\leqslant\beta\}}\rho^{\,d-1}\,d\rho,

where
\displaystyle\omega_{d}:=\Sigma(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=\dfrac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}
is the surface area of the unit sphere in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now change variables
\displaystyle\nu=\rho^{2\sigma}
(so
\displaystyle\rho=\nu^{1/(2\sigma)}
and

\displaystyle d\rho=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\nu^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}-1}\,d\nu
). Then

\rho^{\,d-1}\,d\rho=\nu^{\frac{d-1}{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sigma}\nu^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}-1}\,d\nu=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\nu^{\frac{d-2}{2\sigma}}\,d\nu.

Hence

m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(\beta))=\frac{\omega_{d}}{2\sigma}\int_{\{\nu\geqslant 0:\ |H_{\psi}(\nu)|\leqslant\beta\}}\nu^{\frac{d-2}{2\sigma}}\,d\nu.

Since the set
\displaystyle\{\nu\geqslant 0:|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\leqslant\beta\}
has positive Lebesgue measure (as argued above), and the function inside the integral,
\displaystyle\nu^{\frac{d-2}{2\sigma}}
, is strictly positive on a set of positive measure, it follows that
\displaystyle m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(\beta))>0
for every
\displaystyle\beta>0
.

Step 2. Prove that the problem is unstable.

For
\displaystyle n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
we denote

A_{n,m}=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\penalty\ |H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant\frac{1}{n},\ |\mathbf{z}|\leqslant m\right\}.

We have

\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{n,m}=\pi_{\psi}(1/n),\quad A_{n,m}\subset A_{n,m+1}.

Hence, from the fundamental properties of measure,

\lim_{m\to\infty}m_{d}(A_{n,m})=m_{d}(\pi_{\psi}(1/n))>0\quad\text{for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, we can find a number
\displaystyle m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

0<m_{d}(A_{n,m_{n}})<\infty.

We choose
\displaystyle f=h=0
, and set
\displaystyle\mathbf{K}f_{n}=h_{n}
where

\widehat{f}_{n}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{\chi_{A_{n,m_{n}}}(\mathbf{z})}{\sqrt{|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\,m_{d}(A_{n,m_{n}})}},\quad\widehat{h}_{n}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{\sqrt{|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|}}{\sqrt{m_{d}(A_{n,m_{n}})}}\chi_{A_{n,m_{n}}}(\mathbf{z}).

We have

|\widehat{f}_{n}(\mathbf{z})|\geqslant\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{m_{d}(A_{n,m_{n}})}}\,\chi_{A_{n,m_{n}}}(\mathbf{z}),

which implies

\|\widehat{f}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{m_{d}(A_{n,m_{n}})}}\chi_{A_{n,m_{n}}}(\mathbf{z})\right)^{2}d\mathbf{z}=n.

On the other hand,

\|\widehat{h}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\,m_{d}(A_{n,m_{n}})}}\chi_{A_{n,m_{n}}}(\mathbf{z})\right)^{2}d\mathbf{z}=\frac{1}{n}.

It follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|h_{n}-h\|=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|=\infty.

Hence, our problem is unstable.

3
Modified truncated regularization

Assuming that
\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let us consider Problem (
1
)-(
2
).
If it has a solution

u\in C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)\cap C^{2}\big(0,T;D({\mathfrak{A}})\big),

then Theorem
2.1
implies that

\displaystyle H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})\not=0
almost everywhere, and

\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z})}{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(7)

In (
7
), the term
\displaystyle H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})
is a source of numerical instability, especially when it approaches zero. There are several ways to handle this instability.
According to regularization theory, one can choose filter functions
\displaystyle g_{\alpha}(\lambda)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}g_{\alpha}(\lambda)=1/\lambda
. Then, the term

\displaystyle 1/H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})
can be replaced by
\displaystyle g_{\alpha}(H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}))
.

For example, one can take

Theorem 3.1

(b) If

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

4
Convergence of truncated regularization

In this section, our focus is on the convergence of the modified truncated regularization as
\displaystyle\alpha\to 0^{+}
, rather than on its convergence rate. Consequently, the required conditions are not overly restrictive. We now present the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
,
\displaystyle s\geqslant 0
, and let
\displaystyle\psi,\widetilde{\psi}\in L^{1}(0,T)
,

\displaystyle h,\widetilde{h}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be functions satisfying (
3
), with

\displaystyle\psi\not\equiv 0
in
\displaystyle L^{1}(0,T)
.
Assume that the equation (
4
) admits a unique solution

\displaystyle f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as stated in Theorem
2.1
.

If there exists a function
\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\alpha(\varepsilon)=0,\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon}{[\alpha(\varepsilon)]^{1+s/{2\sigma}}}=0,

then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|f-f_{\alpha(\varepsilon),\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

Remark 4.1
.

(i)

If we consider the problem in the
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

setting (i.e.,
\displaystyle s=0
), then we can obtain convergence under the conditions

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\alpha(\varepsilon)=0,\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha(\varepsilon)}=0.

(ii)

Assume that
\displaystyle\varepsilon=o(\alpha(\varepsilon))
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\alpha(\varepsilon)\pm C\varepsilon}{\alpha(\varepsilon)}=1.

Hence,
\displaystyle\alpha(\varepsilon)\pm C\varepsilon\asymp\alpha(\varepsilon)
as
\displaystyle\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Let
\displaystyle\alpha>0
and
\displaystyle\vartheta\in L^{1}(0,T)
. From (
9
), we have

\omega_{\vartheta}(\alpha):=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|H_{\vartheta}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|>\alpha\}.

and clearly, we obtain
\displaystyle\omega_{\vartheta}(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\pi_{\vartheta}(\alpha)
.

From the regularization solution (
10
) and the exact solution (
7
), we have

f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}(\mathbf{z})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{\widehat{\widetilde{h}}(\mathbf{z})}{H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\chi_{\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|>\alpha\}}\Bigg),\quad\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z})}{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}.

To estimate the error
\displaystyle\|f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we first split

f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}=(f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h})+(f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}),

where
\displaystyle f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}
is the regularized solution with the exact data
\displaystyle h
.

Applying the inequality
\displaystyle(a+b)^{2}\leqslant 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
in
\displaystyle H^{s}
, we get

\|f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant 2\|f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+2\|f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Next, we decompose the first term according to the set

\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha):=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|>\alpha\},\quad\pi_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha):=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha),

so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\Big|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})-\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})\frac{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}{H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\Big|^{2}d\mathbf{z}+\int_{\pi_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2},

and we denote

I_{3}:=\|f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Finally, combining the above, we obtain

\|f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant 2(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}),

(11)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})-\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})\frac{H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}{H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z},

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{\pi_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}d\mathbf{z},

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=I_{1}+I_{2}.

(12)

Step 1. Prove
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{1}=0
.

We have, for every
\displaystyle\psi,\vartheta\in L^{1}(0,T)
,

|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})-H_{\vartheta}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant T\|\psi-\vartheta\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}.

(13)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})-H_{\vartheta}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}(T-s)}(T-s)(\psi(s)-\vartheta(s))ds\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}(T-s)|\psi(s)-\vartheta(s)|ds\leq T\|\psi-\vartheta\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}\left|\frac{H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})-H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}{H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{T^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}d\mathbf{z}\leqslant\frac{T^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

(14)

and thus, since
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\alpha(\varepsilon)=0,\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon}{[\alpha(\varepsilon)]^{1+s/a}}=0
, we deduce
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{1}=0
.

Step 2. Estimate
\displaystyle I_{2}
and show
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{2}=0
.

By definition, for every
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}\in\pi_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)
, we have

|H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant\alpha.

Using (
13
), it follows that

Hence, we have the inclusion

which gives

I_{2}\leqslant\int_{\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}d\mathbf{z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)}(\mathbf{z})(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z}.

Next, from the definition

\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})=0\},

by Theorem
2.1
,
\displaystyle m(\mathcal{Z}_{\psi})=0
. We split the integral over
\displaystyle\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)
as

\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)=\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}\,\cup\,\big(\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}\big).

On the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}
, the integral is zero since
\displaystyle m(\mathcal{Z}_{\psi})=0
. On the set
\displaystyle\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}
, for each
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}\notin\mathcal{Z}_{\psi}
, the characteristic function satisfies

\chi_{\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)}(\mathbf{z})\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Moreover, the function

is dominated by
\displaystyle(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is independent of
\displaystyle\varepsilon
.
Therefore, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{\pi_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)}(\mathbf{z})(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z}=0.

Hence

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{2}=0.

Step 3. Prove
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{3}=0
.

We first prove that there exist
\displaystyle\lambda_{0},z_{1},a>0
such that

|\mathbf{z}|^{a}|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant 2\lambda_{0}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ |\mathbf{z}|\geqslant z_{1}.

(15)

In fact, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}e^{-|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}(T-s)}|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}(T-s)|\psi(s)|ds.

Using the inequality
\displaystyle ye^{-y}\leqslant e^{-1}
for every
\displaystyle y\geqslant 0
we obtain

|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma}|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant e^{-1}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}.

So we can choose
\displaystyle a=2\sigma
. However, in the proof, we still use the general constant
\displaystyle a
because there are many constants
\displaystyle a
that satisfy (
15
).
We have

I_{3}=\int_{\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\left|\frac{\widehat{\widetilde{h}}(\mathbf{z})-\widehat{h}(\mathbf{z})}{H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})}\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z}.

(16)

If
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}\in\omega_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)
then

|H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\geqslant|H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|-|H_{\widetilde{\psi}}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})-H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\geqslant\alpha-\varepsilon T.

Hence for
\displaystyle|\mathbf{z}|\geqslant z_{1}
we have

(\alpha-\varepsilon T)|\mathbf{z}|^{a}\leqslant|\mathbf{z}|^{a}H_{\psi}(|\mathbf{z}|^{2\sigma})|\leqslant 2\lambda_{0}.

It follows that

|\mathbf{z}|\leqslant\left(\frac{2\lambda_{0}}{\alpha-\varepsilon T}\right)^{1/a}.

Hence, from (
16

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\alpha(\varepsilon)=0,\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon}{[\alpha(\varepsilon)]^{1+s/a}}=0
, we deduce

I_{3}\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\left[1+\left(\frac{2\lambda_{0}}{\alpha-\varepsilon T}\right)^{2/a}\right]^{s}\|\widehat{\widetilde{h}}-\widehat{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant\frac{C\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2(1+s/a)}}.

(17)

Combining Steps 1–3 and (
12

∎

5
Convergence rate of the regularization

This section is devoted to a rigorous analysis of the convergence rate of the truncated regularization method.
To obtain estimates that improve upon those presented in the previous section, a detailed study of the properties of the function
\displaystyle H_{\psi}
is required.
Using the definition of
\displaystyle H_{\psi}
in (
5
), we first establish a lemma concerning the set of its zeros.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle P>0,\theta<2
,
\displaystyle p_{T}\not=0
. If
\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\tau^{\theta}\psi(T-\tau)=p_{T},|\tau^{\theta}\psi(T-\tau)|\leqslant P
then we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{-\theta+2}H_{\psi}(x)=p_{T}\Gamma(-\theta+2),

where
\displaystyle\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}dt
is the Gamma function.

Remark 5.1
.

(i)

We note that the set of
\displaystyle\psi
is not small, for example:
\displaystyle\psi(t)=(T-t)^{-\theta}[b_{0}+(T-t)\xi(t)]
where
\displaystyle b_{0}\neq 0,\theta<1
, and
\displaystyle\xi\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
.

(ii)

From Lemma
5.1
, we can put
\displaystyle a=2\sigma(2-\theta)
to obtain (
15
).

By putting
\displaystyle y=x(T-s)
, we obtain

H(x)=\int_{0}^{xT}e^{-y}\frac{y}{x}\psi\left(T-\frac{y}{x}\right)\frac{1}{x}dy=\int_{0}^{xT}e^{-y}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{-\theta+1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\theta}\psi\left(T-\frac{y}{x}\right)\frac{1}{x}dy.

Hence

x^{2-\theta}H(x)=\int_{0}^{xT}e^{-y}y^{-\theta+1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\theta}\psi\left(T-\frac{y}{x}\right)dy.

Using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem gives

\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{-\theta+2}H(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}y^{(-\theta+2)-1}p_{T}dy=p_{T}\Gamma(-\theta+2),

where
\displaystyle\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}dt
is the Gamma function.

∎

Lemma 5.2

\displaystyle h_{0}>0

(a)

\displaystyle|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\not=0

\displaystyle\delta_{k}=\frac{\rho^{\frac{1}{2p_{k}}}}{M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2p_{k}}}L^{\frac{P_{N}-2p_{k}}{2p_{k}}}}

\displaystyle\nu_{k}+\delta_{k}\leqslant\nu\leqslant\nu_{k+1}-\delta_{k+1}
, we have

|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\geqslant M_{0}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left|\nu-\nu_{k}\right|^{p_{k}}\geqslant M_{0}\delta_{k}^{p_{k}}\delta_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}L^{\mathbb{P}_{k}}=\rho.

Here,
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k}=P_{N}-p_{k}-p_{k+1}
.

Case 2.

\displaystyle\nu_{N}+\delta_{N}<\nu
, we have

|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\geqslant M_{0}L^{P_{N}-p_{N}}\delta_{N}^{p_{N}}

and by the choice
\displaystyle\delta_{N}=\left(\frac{\rho L^{2p_{N}}}{L^{P_{N}}M_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p_{N}}}
with
\displaystyle\rho\leqslant M_{0}L^{P_{N}}
, we get
\displaystyle|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\geqslant\rho
.

Case 3.
If
\displaystyle 0\leqslant\nu<\nu_{1}-\delta_{1}
, similarly,

|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\geqslant M_{0}L^{P_{N}-p_{1}}\delta_{1}^{p_{1}}

and for
\displaystyle\delta_{1}=\left(\frac{\rho L^{2p_{N}}}{L^{P_{N}}M_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p_{1}}}
, we obtain that
\displaystyle|H_{\psi}(\nu)|\geqslant\rho
.

Therefore,

B_{\psi}(\rho)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}V_{k}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where}\penalty\ V_{k}=(\nu_{k}-\delta_{k},\nu_{k}+\delta_{k}).

Next, as shown in
[
14
]
, the volume of a ball of radius
\displaystyle\rho
is given by
\displaystyle V_{d}(\rho)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{d}{2}+1\right)}\,\rho^{d},
where
\displaystyle\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function. So, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle m_{d}[B_{\psi}(R)]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}[(\nu_{k}+\delta_{k})^{d}-(\nu_{k}-\delta_{k})^{d}]=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(\nu_{k}+\delta_{k})^{d-1-j}(\nu_{k}-\delta_{k})^{j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2d\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{k}(\nu_{k}+\delta_{k})^{d-1}\leqslant\frac{2d\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{k}\left(\max_{k=\overline{1,N}}\nu_{k}+\delta_{k}\right)^{d-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2d\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{k}(\nu_{k}+\delta_{k})^{d-1}\leqslant\frac{2d\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{d-1}\\
{j}\end{array}\right)\widetilde{\nu}^{d-1-j}\delta_{k}^{j+1}.

Here,
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}=\max_{k=\overline{1,N}}\nu_{k}
. Choose

\rho_{0}=\min\left\{M_{0}L^{P_{N}},\frac{1}{2}\right\},\beta=\min_{k=\overline{1,N},j=\overline{0,d-1}}\frac{j+1}{2m_{s}},

then for
\displaystyle\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, we obtain
\displaystyle\mu[B_{\psi}(\rho)]\leqslant C_{0}\rho^{\beta}
where

C_{0}=\frac{2d\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{d-1}\\
{j}\end{array}\right)\frac{\widetilde{\nu}^{d-1-j}}{\left(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2p_{k}}}L^{\frac{P_{N}-2p_{k}}{2p_{k}}}\right)^{j+1}}.

and the proof of this lemma is finished.

∎

Using the lemmas above, we obtain the result on the convergence rate of truncated regularization. For the convenience of the readers, we explicitly state the assumptions that were made in the previous results.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle d\geqslant 1
,
\displaystyle\sigma>0
,
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
,
\displaystyle s\geqslant 0
, and let

\displaystyle\psi\in L^{1}(0,T)
,
\displaystyle h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\displaystyle\psi\not\equiv 0
in
\displaystyle L^{1}(0,T)
.
Let
\displaystyle\beta>0
be as in Lemma
5.2
(c). Assume that:

(a)

There exist
\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}\in L^{1}(0,T)
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
depending on
\displaystyle\varepsilon
such that

\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}+\|h-\widetilde{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{for all sufficiently small }\varepsilon>0.

(b)

There exist
\displaystyle p\in(1,2)
and
\displaystyle\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; denote by
\displaystyle q=\frac{p}{p-1}
the conjugate exponent of
\displaystyle p
.

(c)

There exist
\displaystyle P>0
,
\displaystyle\theta<2
, and
\displaystyle p_{T}\neq 0
such that

\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\tau^{\theta}\psi(T-\tau)=p_{T},\qquad\sup_{0<\tau<T}\big|\tau^{\theta}\psi(T-\tau)\big|\leqslant P.

Then, for any
\displaystyle s\in[0,\gamma]
, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C>0
independent of
\displaystyle\varepsilon
such that

\|f-f_{\alpha(\varepsilon),\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\,\varepsilon^{\frac{2b}{\,1+\frac{s}{2-\theta}+b\,}},

where

\alpha:=\alpha(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{s}{2-\theta}+b}}

and

b=\min\left\{\frac{\beta(q-2)}{2q},\ \frac{\gamma-s}{2\sigma(2-\theta)}\right\}.

From (
11
), we have

\|f-f_{\varepsilon,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=2(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}).

As shown in (
4
), we have

I_{1}\leqslant\frac{T^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

We estimate
\displaystyle I_{2}
. From Lemma
5.2
, we have

I_{2}=I_{in}+I_{out}

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{in}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z},

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{out}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{C_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s}\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{z}.

Since
\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
\displaystyle 1<p<2
, we obtain in view of Hausdorff-Young’s theorem that
\displaystyle\widehat{f}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\displaystyle q>2
,
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
\displaystyle\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
Using this inequality together with Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{in}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant(1+h_{0}^{1/\sigma})^{s}(m_{d}(B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)))^{(q-2)/q}\left(\int_{B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{q}d\mathbf{z}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant(1+h_{0}^{1/\sigma})^{s}(m_{d}(B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)))^{(q-2)/q}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Next, in order to handle the measure of the set
\displaystyle B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)
, we note that

B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)\subset B_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T).

(19)

From Lemma
5.2
we obtain

m_{d}(B_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha))\leqslant m_{d}(B_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T))\leqslant C(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{\beta}.

It follows that

I_{in}\leqslant C(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{\frac{\beta(q-2)}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(20)

To compute
\displaystyle I_{out}
, we consider the set
\displaystyle C_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)
. Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{z}\in C_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)
and
\displaystyle|\mathbf{z}|>h_{0}
. Using Lemma
5.2
and proceeding as in (
19
), we obtain

C_{\widetilde{\psi}}(\alpha)\subset C_{\psi}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)\subset\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\mathbf{z}|>C_{1}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma(2-\theta)}}\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{out}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\{|\mathbf{z}|>C_{1}(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma(2-\theta)}}\}}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{s-\gamma}(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{2})^{\gamma}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{z})|^{2}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant C(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{\frac{\gamma-s}{\sigma(2-\theta)}}\|f\|_{H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(21)

Finally, we estimate
\displaystyle I_{3}
. Using (
17
) with

\displaystyle a=2-\theta
yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},h}-f_{\alpha,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant C\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2(1+s/(2-\theta))}}.

(22)

Combining (
4
), (
20
),
(
21
) and (
22
) we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f-f_{\varepsilon,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant 2\frac{T^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{\frac{\beta(q-2)}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{\frac{\gamma-s}{\sigma(2-\theta)}}\|f\|_{H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2(1+s/(2-\theta))}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant C\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2(1+s/(2-\theta))}}\left(1+\|f\|_{H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)+C(\alpha+\varepsilon T)^{2b}\left(\|f\|_{H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)

where
\displaystyle b=\min\left\{\frac{\beta(q-2)}{2q},\frac{\gamma-s}{2\sigma(2-\theta)}\right\}
.

We choose
\displaystyle\alpha
by balancing

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\alpha^{2(1+s/(2-\theta))}}=\alpha^{2b}

which gives

\alpha=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{1+\frac{s}{2-\theta}+b}}

and

\|f-f_{\varepsilon,\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{h}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{\frac{2b}{1+\frac{s}{2-\theta}+b}}.

∎

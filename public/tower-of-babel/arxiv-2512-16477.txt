A note on S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 | n ) SU(1,1|n) and O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 6 | 2 ) OSp(6|2) superconformal mechanics


Abstract
In this article we consider the construction of the superconformal mechanics that realize S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 | n ) SU(1,1|n) and O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 6 | 2 ) OSp(6|2) symmetries and involve interactions with non-Abelian bosonic currents. If is shown that for N > 4 N>4 supersymmetries the currents involved have to satisfy the algebraic equations. General considerations on methods of solving these equations are given. In the obtained particular solutions currents are expressed in terms of semi-dynamical variables (harmonics), and, on one instance, coordinates and momenta.

\displaystyle\big\{Q^{\alpha\beta},Q^{\mu\nu}\big\}=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\left({\rm i}p_{r}^{2}-{\rm i}\frac{a^{2}}{4r^{2}}J_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}\psi_{\sigma\lambda}\psi{}^{\rho\lambda}\right)+\frac{{\rm i}}{r^{2}}\psi^{\alpha\beta}J_{\rho}{}^{[\mu}\psi{}^{\nu]\rho}\left(-a-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)+\frac{{\rm i}}{r^{2}}\psi^{\mu\nu}J_{\rho}{}^{[\alpha}\psi{}^{\beta]\rho}\left(-a-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)-

Solving algebraic equation on currents

Both

J_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}J_{\mu}{}^{\beta}-J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}J_{\mu}{}^{\mu}=\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}A

The second equation turns out to be simpler to analyze. As

J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=U_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}D_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{\nu}{}^{\beta},\;\;U_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{\mu}{}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta},\;\;D=\mbox{diag}\big(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots\big).

In the particular case of

4\times 4

D=\mbox{diag}\big(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}\big)

Substituting (
28

27

due to relation

U_{\rho}{}^{\lambda}U_{\sigma}{}^{\tau}\epsilon^{\beta\nu\rho\sigma}=\det U\,\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{\rho}^{\beta}\,\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{\sigma}^{\mu}\,\epsilon^{\lambda\tau\rho\sigma},

(29)

which follows from unitarity of

\displaystyle-D_{\rho}{}^{\alpha}D_{\sigma}{}^{\mu}\epsilon^{\beta\nu\rho\sigma}+D_{\rho}{}^{\beta}D_{\sigma}{}^{\mu}\epsilon^{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}+D_{\rho}{}^{\alpha}D_{\sigma}{}^{\nu}\epsilon^{\beta\mu\rho\sigma}-D_{\rho}{}^{\beta}D_{\sigma}{}^{\nu}\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\rho\sigma}=B\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\;\;\Rightarrow

\displaystyle-B=\big(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\big)^{2}=\big(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}\big)^{2}=\big(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\big)^{2}.

(30)

The latter equations can also be written as

\displaystyle\big(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\big)\big(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+2\lambda_{3}\big)=0,

\displaystyle\big(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3}\big)\big(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}+2\lambda_{2}\big)=0,

(31)

\displaystyle\big(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}\big)\big(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}+2\lambda_{1}\big)=0.

Assuming all right multiples are zero results in
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0

\lambda

\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}

\big(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\big)\big(\lambda_{1}+3\lambda_{2}\big)=0.

(32)

Assuming the first multiple is zero leads to
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}

\lambda_{1}=-3\lambda_{2}

due to

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}{}^{1}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{1}{}^{\beta}+U_{\alpha}{}^{2}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{2}{}^{\beta}+U_{\alpha}{}^{3}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{3}{}^{\beta}+U_{\alpha}{}^{4}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{4}{}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=-4\lambda_{1}U_{\alpha}{}^{4}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{4}{}^{\beta}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

(33)

Formally identifying
v_{\alpha}=\sqrt{-2\lambda_{1}}U_{\alpha}{}^{4}
,
\bar{v}{}^{\alpha}=\sqrt{-2\lambda_{1}}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{4}{}^{\alpha}
, one obtains solution (
18
)

J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=v_{\alpha}\,\bar{v}{}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}v_{\gamma}\,\bar{v}{}^{\gamma}.

(34)

The generators (
34
) form
su(4)\sim so(6)
algebra if one assumes that
\big\{v_{\alpha},\bar{v}{}^{\beta}\big\}=-{\rm i}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
.

The solution of (
26
) is more subtle. It is easy to find a solution in the particular case

J_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}J_{\mu}{}^{\beta}-J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}J_{\mu}{}^{\mu}=0.

(35)

Substituting
J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
as (
28
), one again factorizes unitary matrices and obtains equation

D_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}D_{\mu}{}^{\beta}=D_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}D_{\mu}{}^{\mu}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\lambda_{i}^{2}=\lambda_{i}\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}.

(36)

Therefore, either
\lambda_{i}=0
or
\lambda_{i}=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}
, and only one of
\lambda
can be different from zero; without loss of generality, one can assume that
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
. Therefore,

J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=U_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}D_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{\nu}{}^{\beta}=\lambda_{1}U_{\alpha}{}^{1}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{1}{}^{\beta}=v_{\alpha}{}\bar{v}{}^{\beta},\;\;v_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}U_{\alpha}{}^{1},\;\;\bar{v}{}^{\alpha}=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\big(U^{\dagger}\big)_{1}{}^{\alpha}=\big(v_{\alpha}\big)^{\dagger}.

(37)

Harmonic structure that appears in the solutions (
34
), (
36
) is already familiar and is frequently encountered in the superfield approach. Equations (
35
) and (
27
), therefore, provide an algebraic explanation behind this structure.

If
A\neq 0
in (
26
), one can substitute
L_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}J_{\gamma}{}^{\gamma}
into (
26
) to find that
L_{\alpha}{}^{\gamma}L_{\gamma}{}^{\beta}\sim\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
. Therefore,
L_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
has two eigenvalues with opposite signs. If one of these eigenvalues is encountered only once,
L_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
, and, consequently,
J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
have to be expressed in terms of one set of harmonics, and one obtains a solution extending (
37
)

J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=v_{\alpha}{}\bar{v}{}^{\beta}+k\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}v_{\mu}\bar{v}{}^{\mu}.

(38)

If the lowest multiplicity among the eigenvalues is greater than one, similarly greater number of harmonics
v_{\alpha}{}^{i}
,
\bar{v}_{j}^{\beta}
appear. They have to satisfy
v_{\alpha}{}^{i}\bar{v}_{j}^{\alpha}\sim\delta_{j}^{i}
, as they originate from the unitary matrix.

The solution with many harmonics is not satisfactory from different point of view. As
J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
have to produce
u(n)
algebra with respect to the Dirac bracket, one has to define brackets of
v_{\alpha}{}^{i}
,
\bar{v}_{j}^{\beta}
that result in the proper algebra of
J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
, satisfy Jacobi identity and respect the relation
v_{\alpha}{}^{i}\bar{v}_{j}^{\alpha}\sim\delta_{j}^{i}
. One can expect these brackets to take form

\big\{v^{i}_{\alpha},v^{j}_{\beta}\big\}=f_{1}\big(v^{i}_{\alpha}v^{j}_{\beta}-v^{j}_{\alpha}v^{i}_{\beta}\big),\;\;\big\{v^{i}_{\alpha},{\bar{v}}_{j}^{\beta}\big\}=f_{2}\delta^{i}_{j}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}+f_{3}\delta^{i}_{j}v^{k}_{\alpha}{\bar{v}}_{k}^{\beta}+f_{4}v^{i}_{\alpha}{\bar{v}}_{j}^{\beta},

(39)

where
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
,
f_{3}
,
f_{4}
are assumed to be functions of
v^{k}_{\gamma}{\bar{v}}_{k}^{\gamma}
. However, it is impossible to satisfy all three demands simultaneously.

While it may seem that (
38
) is the most general solution to (
26
), in at least one particular case exists a substantially different solution. For
n=4
, one can check that

J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}={\rm i}\left(z_{[\alpha\mu]}p^{[\beta\mu]}-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}z_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu\nu}\right)

(40)

satisfies equation (
26
) with nontrivial righthand side

J_{\alpha}{}^{\gamma}J_{\gamma}{}^{\beta}=\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\,J_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\,J_{\nu}{}^{\mu}\;\;\rightarrow\;\;c_{1}=\frac{1}{4},c_{2}=0

(41)

and has proper brackets provided that

\big\{p^{\mu\nu},z_{\alpha\beta}\big\}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\mu}\delta_{\beta}^{\nu}-\delta_{\alpha}^{\nu}\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}.

(42)

The
u(1)
part of the
u(4)
algebra in this case is fermionic, thus reproducing one of solutions found in
[
14
]
. Only for this particular algebra it is possible to make
z_{\alpha\beta}
and
p^{\mu\nu}
real, as one can assume that

\big(z_{\alpha\beta}\big)^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}z_{\mu\nu},\;\;\big(p^{\mu\nu}\big)^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha\beta}.

(43)

To show that (
40
) satisfies equation (
26
), one should note that due to properties of the
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
-symbol

z_{\alpha\gamma}p^{\beta\gamma}=-z^{\beta\gamma}p_{\alpha\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}z_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu\nu},\;\;z_{\alpha\gamma}z^{\beta\gamma}=\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}z_{\mu\nu}z^{\mu\nu}.

(44)

Let us also note that using fermionic
su(4)
generator
{\widehat{J}}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\psi_{\alpha}\,{\bar{\psi}}{}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\psi_{\mu}{\bar{\psi}}{}^{\mu}
and the sum
{\widetilde{J}}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=J_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}+{\widehat{J}}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
, one can write the appropriate Hamiltonian (
2
) using the Casimir operators of each algebra

H=\frac{1}{2}p_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{2}C_{su(4),bos}-C_{su(4)}+\frac{3}{5}C_{su(4),ferm}\right),

(45)

where
C_{su(4),bos}=J_{\mu}{}^{\nu}J_{\nu}{}^{\mu}
,
C_{su(4),ferm}={\widehat{J}}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{\widehat{J}}_{\nu}{}^{\mu}
and
C_{su(4)}={\widetilde{J}}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{\widetilde{J}}_{\nu}{}^{\mu}
.
It is worth noting that coefficients in (
45
) coincide with ones found in
[
14
]
up to normalization of generators.

Therefore, the list of solutions presented here may be not exhaustive and other solutions may exist for particular algebras.

5
Conclusion

We constructed versions of superconformal mechanics with
SU(1,1|n)
and
OSp(6|2)
superconformal symmetries that involve interactions with bosonic non-Abelian currents. It was shown that for
N>4
supersymmetry these currents have to satisfy algebraic equations. As in all the considered cases the currents are Hermitean, solutions to these equations can be found by representing the current as a diagonal matrix multiplied by a unitary matrix and its inverse. In all the considered cases unitary matrices factorize, and equations effectively constrain eigenvalues of the diagonal matrix. In the
OSp(6|2)
case, the current is a
su(4)
matrix with eigenvalues
(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda,-3\lambda)
and can be expressed in terms of one row of a unitary matrix and one column of its conjugate, which after assigning proper Dirac brackets can be treated as harmonics. The situation in the
SU(1,1|n)
case is more complicated. In general,
u(n)
current has two unequal eigenvalues. If one of them has multiplicity
1
, the solution can be expressed in terms of harmonics similarly to
OSp(6|2)
case. If lowest multiplicity among the eigenvalues is two or greater, algebraic equation allows for a solution with two or greater sets of harmonics, but they are themselves algebraically constrained and no proper Dirac brackets can be assigned to them.

It is notable that obtained list of solutions is not exhaustive. For example, in the
SU(1,1|4)
case an entirely different solution exists that does not involve harmonics but usual coordinates and momenta. This was obtained in a different representation in
[
14
]
, but can not be explicitly found by the methods used in this article, suggesting that for particular algebras other solutions can be found. This suggestion is further strengthened by the existence of solutions of
10
and
11
dimensional supergravities that preserve some supersymmetry and are products of
AdS_{2}
and some compact manifold. For any such solution one can expect existence of the superconformal mechanics, despite the fact that solutions found in this article (with exception of (
40
)) do not support such a geometric interpretation. Another related but unsolved question is the construction of the general solution for currents in
OSp(n|2)
systems.

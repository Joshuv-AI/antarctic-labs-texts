Efficacy and Safety of SPH3127 Tablet, a Novel Direct Renin Inhibitor, in Patients with Mild-to-Moderate Essential Hypertension: A Phase III Randomised Trial
Authors: Qiang Lv, Changsheng Ma, Fang Wang, Lipeng Li, Gang Xiong, Zhanquan Li, Yanling Qu, Wenyue Pang, Wenliang Xiao, Hongyun Ruan, Liping Wei, Gang Liu, Jun Zhang, Qiong Liu, Ling Lin, Mingzhi Long, Huanming Li, Hong Yuan, Ling Liu, Yue Gao, Peijing Liu, Cuntai Zhang, Zhirong Wang, Jianhong Xie, Jiguang Wang, Xin Zhang, Yongyi Lu, Kai Huang, Min Liu, Jiyan Chen, Xiaohong Gao, Dongfang Wang, Xueping Ma, Xiaoping Chen, Yuhui Li, Yue Zhang, Xiangqing Kong, Fang Chen, Xiaowei Zhang, Jie Qi

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of sitokiren (SPH3127) tablet in patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension in comparison to valsartan capsule.MethodsThis multicentre, randomised, double-blind, parallel Phase III trial was designed in 2 stages. In the 1st stage, eligible patients were randomised to receive 50 mg, 100 mg or 200 mg of SPH3127 tablet or 80 mg of valsartan capsule once daily (QD) for 12 consecutive weeks. In the 2nd stage, eligible patients were randomised to receive assigned dose of SPH3127 tablet based on the results from the 1st stage or valsartan 80 mg QD for 12 consecutive weeks. Primary outcome was the change from baseline in mean sitting diastolic blood pressure (msDBP) at Week 12. Safety outcome measures included any adverse events. Exploratory outcomes included plasma concentration of SPH3127, as well as the assessment of the correlation between SPH3127 exposure with the level of renin inhibition, clinical efficacy and occurrence of adverse events.ResultsThe 1st stage enrolled 189 patients, of which 129 eligible patients were randomised. High plasma renin activity (PRA) inhibitory effect (83%) and the biggest reduction of msDBP at Week 12 from baseline (− 8.17 ± 5.90 mmHg) was detected in SPH3127 group at the dosage of 100 mg, which was determined for the subsequent stage 2 of this trial. The 2nd stage screened 1260 patients, of which 828 eligible patients were randomised to receive SPH3127 tablet 100 mg (N = 413) and valsartan capsule 80 mg (N = 415), QD. Main analysis based on the treatment policy strategy indicated 5.97 (0.41) mmHg [least-square mean (LSM) and standard error (SE)] of the msDBP changes at Week 12 from baseline in the SPH3127 and 6.32 (0.41) in valsartan groups, with inter-group difference of − 0.35 mmHg (95% CI: − 1.48, 0.78, p = 0.005). Main analysis based on hypothetical strategy showed 5.95 (0.41) mmHg of the msDBP changes at Week 12 from baseline in the SPH3127 and 6.31 (0.42) mmHg in valsartan groups, with inter-group difference of − 0.36 mmHg (95% CI: − 1.51, 0.78, p = 0.006). During the 12-week treatment period, 250 (61.1%) patients in SPH3127 group and 231 (56.2%) patients in valsartan group reported treatment emergent adverse events (TEAEs). Adverse drug reaction (ADR) was reported by 34 (8.3%) and 41 (10.0%) patients in SPH3127 and valsartan group, respectively. The serious adverse events (SAEs) reported by 10 patients were considered not related to the investigational drugs. Death was reported in 1 patient in the valsartan group due to myocardial infarction, which was considered not related to the study drug.ConclusionsOral SPH3127 tablet 100 mg QD is effective and safe for adult patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension.Trial RegistrationClinicaltrials.gov Identifier number NCT05359068.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40265-026-02315-z.

Key Points

The SPH3127 tablet 100 mg once daily (QD) showed most favourable effects in inhibiting plasma renin activity (PRA) and reducing mean sitting diastolic blood pressure (msDBP) in comparison with 80 mg of valsartan capsule and 50 mg or 200 mg of SPH3127 tablet during the 12-week treatment period.The SPH3127 tablet 100 mg QD tablets exerts stable and significant antihypertensive efficacy in patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension, with consistent therapeutic effects observed across different patient subgroups over the 12-week treatment period.The SPH3127 tablet 100 mg QD exhibits a favourable safety profile in the treatment of mild-to-moderate essential hypertension, with no serious adverse drug reactions or grade ≥ 3 Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) reported.

Introduction

Hypertension represents a paramount global public health challenge, affecting approximately 1.3 billion adults aged ≥ 30 years worldwide, with a prevalence of 31.1%; its global burden is projected to rise to 1.56 billion by 2025 [1]. In China, 23.2% of adults (around 245 million) are living with hypertension, accompanied by suboptimal rates of 51.6% for awareness, 45.8% for treatment, and 16.8% for disease control [2]. As a major modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, chronic kidney disease (CKD), and more than 10 million annual deaths worldwide, hypertension urgently requires the development of novel, safe, and effective antihypertensive agents [3]. International guidelines recommend five first-line antihypertensive drug classes: angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), calcium channel blockers (CCBs), thiazide diuretics, and beta-blockers [4]. Although ACEIs and ARBs are widely used for cardioprotection, they act downstream in the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), leading to compensatory elevations in plasma renin activity and incomplete pathway inhibition. In contrast, CCBs, diuretics, and beta-blockers are associated with specific adverse effect profiles [5]. Direct renin inhibitors (DRIs) target the initial rate-limiting step of RAAS activation, enabling complete and sustained blockade without compensatory renin elevation [6].

Sitokiren (SPH3127) tablet, a novel best-in-class potential DRI discovered and developed by Shanghai Pharmaceuticals Holding Co., Ltd (SPH), was approved by the National Medical Products Administration for clinical trials in 2016 as a category 1.1 oral antihypertensive drug. It shares a similar mechanism of action with aliskiren, which confers distinct mechanistic advantages over ACEIs and ARBs in RAAS modulation [7]. Compared with aliskiren, SPH3127 possesses a unique chemical structure, lower molecular weight, and more potent renin inhibitory activity [8]. Animal models showed effective anti-hypertensive effects and the effective dose was lower than that of liskiren [9]. The results of Phase I clinical trials revealed that SPH3127 exposure was increased with both single ascending dose (SAD) and multiple ascending doses (MADs), with up to 90% inhibition of renin activity until 24 h of last dose administration. The drug was well tolerated in Phase I trials [10]. A Phase II, multicentre, randomised double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial showed that SPH3127 tablet was effective and safe in treating adult patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension in a dosage of 50, 100, and 200 mg once daily (QD) [11]. Efficacy and safety between SPH3127 and valsartan were compared in this Phase III study in patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension.

Methods/Design

Study Design

This is a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, double-dummy, positive drug controlled, parallel Phase III trial with a 2-stage design. The 1st stage was conducted across 14 centres in China from June 2021 to December 2021. The 2nd stage was conducted in 43 centres in China, where screening, induction and double-blind treatment periods were from March 2022 to February 2023. The long-term safety observational period of the stage 2 is ongoing, and the results will be reported separately. The trial was conducted in adherence to the guidelines set forth by the International Conference for Harmonization (ICH), as well as relevant national laws and regulations. The general ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki were also followed. The ethics committee and institution review board of each institution participating in the study approved the trial protocol. All participants signed the written informed consent form before screening. This trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05359068).

Inclusion Criteria

We included adult patients aged ≥ 18 years, diagnosed with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension and who understood the study and voluntarily participated in the present study. Exclusion criteria included patients with secondary hypertension, suspected grade 3 hypertension, acute hypertension, at risk when current antihypertensive therapy is discontinued, severe cardiovascular disease within 6 months, severe or malignant retinopathy. The trial excluded patients whose diabetes was not well controlled, had a history of malignant tumour or psychiatric disorder, were planning to use or were using other antihypertensive drugs or other drugs that may affect blood pressure during the trial. Patients with substance use disorder in the past 2 years and pregnant or lactating women were also excluded from the trial. Further situations deemed not suitable for the study as assessed by investigators.

Treatment and Randomisation

In stage 1, eligible patients were randomised at a ratio of 1:1:1:1 to receive 50 mg, 100 mg or 200 mg of SPH3127 tablet or 80 mg of valsartan capsule QD for a period of 12 weeks (OSM, Resource 3). In stage 2, eligible patients were randomised at a ratio of 1:1 to receive an assigned dose of SPH3127 tablet or valsartan capsule 80 mg QD for 12 consecutive weeks. Block randomisation was adopted by an independent biostatistician to generate random numbers using PROC PLAN process of SAS software. Participants who benefited from the 12-week treatment with either SPH3127 tablet or valsartan capsule, as assessed by investigators, and agreed to continue in the trial, were eligible for the subsequent long-term safety observation period in stage 2. Study Procedure

Sitting blood pressure measurement was performed in the examination room in accordance with the standards specified in the Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Hypertension (Revised 2018). Briefly, after seated rest for at least 5 min, blood pressure was measured using a calibrated electronic sphygmomanometer with the upper arm at heart level. Two readings were taken 1–2 min apart; if systolic blood pressure (SBP) and/or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) differed by > 5 mmHg, a third measurement was obtained. The average of two or three readings was recorded [12]. The measurement was conducted for each patient between 8 am to 12 am before treatment and repeated every 2 weeks throughout the 12-week treatment period. Collections of blood sample for pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) were performed separately. Blood samples for PK were collected within 4 h before and 0.5–2 h after administration of the SPH3127 tablet or a valsartan capsule at visit 4 (Week 4 after treatment) and visit 6 (Week 8 after treatment), respectively. Blood samples for PD were collected at baseline (visit 2) and treatment period of visit 4 (Week 4 after treatment), 6 (Week 8 after treatment), and 8 (Week 12 after treatment), respectively. Participants who were included in the long-term safety observation period underwent assessment via telephone follow-up every 4 weeks and re-examination every 12 weeks, with a final interval of 16 weeks. The adverse events observed during the study were documented using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) 25.1. These adverse events were categorised according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 throughout the study period. All study results, including safety and efficacy analyses, were uniformly conducted after unblinding of the trial.

Outcome Assessment

The primary outcome was the change from baseline in mean sitting diastolic blood pressure (msDBP) at Week 12. Secondary outcomes included changes from baseline in msDBP at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 after treatment; changes from baseline in mean sitting systolic blood pressure (msSBP) at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 after treatment; overall effective rate at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 after treatment; and rate of reaching target blood pressure (defined as normal blood pressure with mean diastolic pressure < 90 mmHg and mean systolic pressure < 140 mmHg) at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 after treatment. Safety outcomes were the number of participants who reported any adverse events that occurred after the administration of the investigational drugs. Safety outcomes were the number of participants who reported any adverse events that occurred after the administration of the investigational drugs, including the definitions as follows: treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE) was defined as any adverse event that occurred or worsened in severity after the first dose of the study drug until the end of the 12-week treatment period. Serious adverse event (SAE) was defined as any adverse event that resulted in death, was life-threatening, required inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, resulted in persistent or significant disability/incapacity, or was a congenital anomaly/birth defect, in accordance with the ICH guidelines. Plasma concentration of SPH3127, as well as the assessment of the correlation between SPH3127 exposure and the level of renin inhibition, clinical efficacy as well as occurrence of adverse events, were considered as exploratory outcomes.

Study Populations

Full analysis set (FAS) comprised all randomised participants who received at least 1 dose of investigational drugs and had a post-baseline measurement of blood pressure. The FAS was used for analysis of baseline characteristics and efficacy outcomes in stage 1 of the study. Intention-to-treat set (ITTs) comprised all randomised participants and was used for analyses of baseline characteristics and efficacy outcomes in stage 2 of the study. Safety set (SS) comprised all patients who received at least 1 dose of investigational drugs and was used for safety evaluation. The PK/PD analysis set (PKPDAS) consisted of all participants who were randomised and administered at least 1 dose of study drug as well as having at least 1 pair of valid PK/PD data.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were carried out using SAS 9.4. A two-sided test with a 95% confidence level (CI) was adopted at a significance level of α = 0.05. Continuous variables were displayed using case number, missing number, mean, median, lower and upper quartile, standard deviation (SD), minimum and maximum. Categorical variables were expressed as number and percentage. Paired t test and independent-sample t test was used to compare changes of blood pressure before and after treatment within the same group and between groups, respectively. Fisher exact probability was adopted to compare the total effective rate and rate of reaching target blood pressure between groups.

The main analysis of the primary outcome in stage 2 was conducted using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) after multiple imputations based on treatment policy strategy and hypothetical strategy, respectively. The corrected least-square mean (LSM) and 95% CI of the inter-group difference in the change of msDBP from baseline were computed by utilising the changes of msDBP at Week 12 from baseline as the outcome variable, the treatment groups as fixed effect, and blood pressure at baseline as covariate. A non-inferiority test was conducted if lower limit of 95% CI was > − 1.83 mmHg, which was prespecified as the non-inferiority margin.

Sensitivity analyses of the primary outcome in stage 2 were carried out using mixed-effect models for repeated measures (MMRM). Analysis of covariance was applied after the implementation of worst observation carried forward (WOCF) imputation and multiple imputation, which utilised msDBP at baseline, stratification on availability of renin activity/PK blood sample collection and central random effect as covariates. Subgroup analyses of the primary outcome were performed based on age (< 65 and ≥ 65 years), gender and baseline msDBP (< 100 mmHg and ≥ 100 mmHg). Fisher exact probability was adopted to analyse the total effective rate and rate of reaching target blood pressure between groups.

Results

Outcomes of Stage 1

A total of 189 patients were initially screened at stage 1 of this study, 129 of whom were included and randomised to receive SPH3127 tablet 50 mg (n = 33), SPH3127 tablet 100 mg (n = 29), SPH3127 tablet 200 mg (n = 33), and valsartan capsule 80 mg (n = 34) QD for 12 weeks, respectively. A total of 124 patients were enrolled in Stage 1 of this trial, and 30, 29, 32 and 33 patients were allocated into SPH3127 tablet 50 mg, SPH3127 tablet 100 mg, SPH3127 tablet 200 mg, and valsartan capsule 80 mg group, respectively (OSM, Resource 3).

The primary outcomes (change of msDBP at Week 12 from baseline) were − 6.23 ± 6.41 mmHg in the valsartan 80-mg group, and − 4.76 ± 6.47, − 8.17 ± 5.90 mmHg as well as − 6.39 ± 8.64 mmHg in SPH3127 50-mg, 100-mg and 200-mg groups, respectively (Table 2). The reduction in msDBP at Week 12 was greater in the SPH3127 100-mg group than that in valsartan 80-mg group, as well as in SPH3127 50-mg and 200-mg groups (Table 2). In summary, SPH3127 showed a comparable efficacy and safety profile to the positive control group (valsartan capsule 80 mg) at all investigation doses, with 100 mg displaying optimal antihypertensive effect. In addition, the median of plasma renin activity (PRA) was reduced > 80% in SPH3127 100-mg and 200-mg groups (83% and 89%, respectively), suggesting a dose of ≥ 100 mg provided good PRA inhibitory effect (OSM, Resource 4). Based on the results of efficacy, safety and PK/PD analyses from the stage 1 and a Phase IIa trial (manuscript in preparation), the dosage regime of SPH3127 tablet 100-mg QD was recommended for the subsequent stage 2 of this trial.

Outcomes of Stage 2

Of a total 1260 patients who were initially screened at stage 2, 828 were eligible and were randomly allocated to receive SPH3127 tablet 100 mg, QD (n = 413) and valsartan capsule 80 mg, QD (n = 415) (Fig. 2). Among these participants, 8 individuals did not receive investigational drugs after randomisation (4 patients each in SPH3127 and valsartan group, respectively). A total of 51 participants did not complete the first 12 weeks of treatment (22 and 29 patients in SPH3127 and valsartan group, respectively) and 8 individuals were lost to the follow-up visit (4 participants each in SPH3127 and valsartan group, respectively). Analyses of secondary outcomes revealed a similar trend from baseline between SPH3127 and valsartan groups in msDBP at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10; msSBP at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12; overall effective rate at Week 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12; and rate of reaching target blood pressure at Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 (OSM, Resource 6).

A total of 820 participants were randomised to take at least a dose of study drugs and included in SS (409 in the SPH3127 group and 411 in the valsartan group). During the treatment for 12 consecutive weeks, there were 250 (61.1% of SPH3127 group) and 231 (56.2% of valsartan group) patients who reported TEAEs, respectively. The incidence of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) in the SPH3127 group (8.3%, n = 34) was slightly lower than in the valsartan group (10.0%, n = 41). Both groups demonstrated a favourable safety profile, as evidenced by neither serious ADRs nor ADRs of CTCAE ≥ 3. The SAEs reported by 10 patients (1.2%, n = 5 in each group) and AEs of CTCAE ≥ 3 reported by 19 patients (2.0%, n = 8 and 2.7%, n = 11 in SPH3127 and valsartan group, respectively) were considered not related to the investigational drugs. No suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction (SUSAR) was identified in either group. There was no reported ADR leading to treatment withdrawal and/or study withdrawal in the SPH3127 group. However, 1 (0.2%) patient in valsartan group withdrew from the treatment and study due to abnormal examination results of liver function, which was considered related to valsartan. Common AEs (≥ 5% of frequency) in both groups were COVID-19, urinary tract infection, hyperuricemia, hyperlipidaemia and fever. One ADR (0.2%) of hyperkalaemia occurred in the SPH3127 group and 2 cases (0.5%) in the valsartan group. Discussion

Treatment with SPH3127 tablet 100 mg QD was shown to have similar antihypertensive effects to valsartan 80 mg QD in Chinese patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension in this study. With regard to the safety profile, no significant differences on frequency and severity of ADRs among the 3 tested doses of SPH3127 tablet and valsartan capsule 80 mg were observed during the study at stage 1. However, the frequency of ADRs in SPH3127 tablet 100-mg group was slightly lower than valsartan capsule 80-mg group in stage 2, indicating that SPH3127 tablet 100 mg was well tolerated and safe in the treatment of mild-to-moderate essential hypertension. Notably, hyperkalaemia is a common safety consideration with RAAS inhibitors. Its incidence did not show an increased trend in SPH3127-treated patients compared with those receiving valsartan. This finding aligns with the Phase IIa trial data and further supports the favourable safety profile of SPH3127 [11]. The few SAEs that were reported in the SPH3127 and valsartan groups, were considered not related to the investigational drugs.

Hypertension remains a major global health concern and causes serious health outcomes including mortality, aneurysm, stroke, chronic kidney disease, eye damage, and cardiovascular disease [3, 13]. Despite modest improvements regarding awareness, treatment, and control of hypertension, its prevalence remains high [1]. Reducing the blood pressure to a level of less than 130 mmHg by antihypertensive medications was demonstrated to prevent hypertension-related complications [14]. The RAAS could regulate blood pressure and fluid homeostasis; thus, playing a major role in the pathophysiology of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease and chronic heart failure [9, 10]. The ACEI and ARBs that target the key components of RAAS have attracted significant attention in treating hypertension [9, 15]. Therapeutic potential for ACEI and ARBs are limited due to their incomplete blockade of RAAS, resulting in increased plasma renin activity [16]. Since renin is the first step and rate-limiting enzyme in RAAS, DRIs should be likely to block this cascade completely even in situations of elevated circulating or tissue renin activity [17]. Aliskiren, the first approved DRI that inhibits renin enzyme from the top of RAAS cascade was developed and shown to have similar efficacy to ARBs and ACEI [18, 19]. However, several issues like low oral bioavailability, long synthesis route and severe side effects remained a challenge for clinical practice of aliskiren [20–22].

From the stage 1 of the present trial, SPH3127 tablet 100 mg was considered as the optimal dose for stage 2 of the trial. During the randomised treatment period of 12 weeks at stage 2, results of main analysis revealed that the reduction of msDBP from baseline in the SPH3127 100-mg group was comparable to that in valsartan 80-mg group.

In this study, the non-inferiority margin was defined based on a meta-analysis of the inter-group difference (95% CI) for antihypertensive effect between valsartan 80-mg and placebo groups, which was 4.55 (3.65, 5.44) mmHg [23]. According to boundary immobilisation method adopted from Guideline on Non-Inferiority Clinical Trials for Drugs released by the Center for Drug Evaluation of China [24], f-value should be less than half of the 95% CI lower limit (i.e., 1.83 mmHg). The lower limit of 95% CI for the difference between the SPH3127 100-mg and valsartan group, as determined by both treatment policy strategy and hypothetical strategy, exceeded − 1.83 mmHg, indicating non-inferiority of antihypertensive efficacy between SPH3127 tablet 100 mg QD and valsartan capsule 80 mg QD on treating patients with mild-to-moderate hypertension. Furthermore, a similar decrease of msDBP at Week 12 from baseline was shown in SPH3127 and valsartan groups by subgroup analyses for the primary outcome, based on age, gender and baseline levels of msDBP using treatment policy strategy and hypothetical strategy, indicating patients from all these subgroups could benefit from a 12-week treatment with SPH3127 and valsartan.

A multicentre, prospective, open-label, single arm study (Val-Perfect) was conducted to investigate the efficacy and tolerability of once-daily valsartan monotherapy (80 mg for two weeks, followed by 160 mg for eight weeks) in 195 Chinese patients with mild-to-moderate hypertension. Significant reductions (p < 0.0001) were observed for msSBP (15.6 ± 12.3 mmHg) and msDBP (11.1 ± 8.6 mmHg), from baseline to at Week 10, respectively [25]. In stage 2 of this study, similar reductions of msSBP (11.1 ± 15.0 mmHg and 11.2 ± 15.5 mmHg in SPH3127 100-mg and valsartan 80-mg groups, respectively) and msDBP (7.4 ± 8.0 mmHg and 7.7 ± 8.6 mmHg in SPH3127 100-mg and valsartan 80-mg groups, respectively) were observed at Week 10 from baseline. The difference of msDBP and msSBP reduction between valsartan 80-mg and 160-mg groups at Week 10 from baseline was due to insufficient number of study samples or a dose-dependent antihypertensive effect of valsartan, needs to be further clarified [26]. It is important to acknowledge that the administration of SPH3127 tablet at a dosage of 200 mg did not exhibit superior antihypertensive efficacy compared to the 100-mg dosage during stage 1 of this trial. This finding aligns with the conclusion drawn from the Phase II study [11].

Limitations

Despite the robust two-stage, randomised, double-blind study design, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study was conducted only in Chinese patients, and the efficacy and safety results might not be generalisable to other geographic regions and ethnicities. Second, the observation period was short. The long-term follow up study is ongoing, which is expected to provide further insights into the efficacy and safety of the SPH3127 tablet in mild-to-moderate essential hypertension patients. Third, our study focused on SPH3127 at the 100-mg QD dose, which was identified as the optimal dose from the prior dose-finding studies of SPH3127, and did not explore doses beyond this range. The observed noninferiority to valsartan 80 mg QD may reflect a potential ceiling effect of this dose. Further studies are planned to characterise the dose-response relationship of SPH3127, which may help to optimise its clinical application in diverse patient populations.

Conclusion

This Phase III trial confirmed that the 100-mg SPH3127 tablet QD exerts significant and stable antihypertensive efficacy in adult patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension, with consistent therapeutic effects observed across subgroups stratified by age, gender and baseline msDBP. Also, SPH3127 exhibited a favourable safety profile, with a slightly lower incidence of ADRs than valsartan, no SADRs or CTCAE ≥ 3 ADRs reported, and a low incidence of hyperkalaemia. As a novel DRI targeting the rate-limiting step of the RAAS pathway, SPH3127 100 mg QD provides a new and safe monotherapy option for the clinical management of mild-to-moderate essential hypertension in Chinese patients.

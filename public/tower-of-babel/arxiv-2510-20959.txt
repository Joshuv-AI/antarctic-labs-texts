L 2 L^{2} -torsion of automorphisms
Sam Hughes, Wolfgang Lück

Abstract
. We develop the theory of L 2 L^{2} -torsion of an automorphism of a group and compute it for every automorphism of a group which is hyperbolic and one-ended relative to a finite collection of virtually polycyclic groups. We also prove a combination formula for the L 2 L^{2} -torsion of a group in terms of the L 2 L^{2} -torsion of its stabilisers of a sufficiently nice action on a contractible space. We apply it to compute the L 2 L^{2} -torsion of a selection of CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \operatorname{CAT}(0) lattices, of many relatively hyperbolic groups and their automorphisms, of higher dimensional graph manifolds, and of handlebody groups.

In this section we use

7.2.
Higher dimensional graph manifolds

Higher dimensional graph manifolds were introduced in
[
16
]

and studied from the point of view of the Borel Conjecture and quasi-isometric rigidity.
We now recall the construction:

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Gamma
be a finite graph. For each vertex

of
\Gamma

be a compact manifold of dimension

whose interior is a complete hyperbolic
manifold of finite volume, and hence has toral cusps. The boundary of

is a
collection of tori, where

, where

denotes the
\ell
-torus. We call each

collection of

-dimensional tori. An

is any

diffeomorphisms such that each edge of
\Gamma
corresponds to some gluing. For a piece

N_{v}
of

, then we call

a

. We denote the

Recall that the

-torsion of a closed hyperbolic manifold of odd dimension
(2d+1)

has been computed by Hess–Schick
[
24
]
and of a compact manifold of
odd dimension

whose interior is a complete hyperbolic manifold of finite volume
by Lück–Schick
[
39

dimension constant

to its volume.

We may decomposes a graph manifold as a graph of spaces: each vertex space
M_{v}
is a
piece

and each edge space
M_{e}
is the collar of the torus boundary that is obtained
by gluing two geometric pieces via an affine diffeomorphism. Note that on the level of
fundamental groups this decomposes

as a graph of groups with vertex groups

\pi_{1}M_{v}=\pi_{1}N_{i}
and edge groups
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let

be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional extended graph manifold with hyperbolic pieces

. Then,
\pi_{1}(M)
is sofic, satisfies the Determinant Conjecture, and

Proof.

We first establish that
\pi_{1}(M)
is sofic satisfies the Determinant Conjecture. We
have that
\pi_{1}M
admits a decomposition as a graph of groups such that: Every edge
group

is
\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, and every vertex group

splits as a direct product

where

is a (possibly trivial) abelian group and

is the

whose interior is
a complete hyperbolic manifold of finite volume. In the case that

is trivial, we
have

. It follows that every edge group is amenable and every vertex
group is residually finite. Thus,

is sofic
by
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
and so satisfies the Determinant
Conjecture by
[
14

. Note that this implies the stabiliser
of any vertex or edge satisfies Determinant Conjecture.

Note that

is a cocompact
\pi_{1}(M)
-space. In the graph
of groups decomposition for
\pi_{1}(M)
we have that every vertex group either contains
an infinite finitely generated abelian normal subgroup and is

-acyclic
by
[
34
, Theorem 7.4(1),(2)]
, or is the fundamental group of a compact
manifold of odd dimension whose interior is a complete hyperbolic manifold of finite

-acyclic by
[
39
, Corollary 6.5]
.

Now, we have that
\rho^{(2)}(\widetilde{M})=\rho^{(2)}(\pi_{1}M)
. For an edge

[

]
. Each vertex group

for some

-torsion of

vanishes

[

. The result follows from
Theorem
3.6

(vii)
.
∎

Let

such that
|\Phi^{n}(g)|<Cn^{d}+C

For a conjugacy class

denote the length of a shortest
representative. We say
\Phi\in\Out(G)
is
polynomially growing
if for each
conjugacy class
[g]
of
G
there is a constant

such that
||\Phi^{n}([g])||<Cn^{d}+C

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We remark that for any two finite generating sets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
the corresponding word
metrics on

are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. It follows that the two definitions of growth
are independent of the choice of a finite generating set.

Throughout the next few subsections we will prove vanishing of

-torsion for various
polynomially growing automorphisms. We collect these results in
Theorem
1
and remark that many of the arguments here are
inspired by arguments in
[

]
proving vanishing of
torsion homology growth using the cheap rebuilding
property
[
1
]
.

We say that the pair
(G,\mathcal{F})
is a
sporadic free product
if one of the following
holds:

(i)

k=0
and
G=\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

k=1
and
G=G_{1}
or
G=G_{1}\ast\mathbb{Z}
; or

(iii)

k=2
and
G=G_{1}\ast G_{2}
.

The key point for us is that automorphisms of sporadic free products have a canonical

G
-tree that they preserve. We recall this result here noting that it built on work of
Guirardel and Horbez
[
21
]

Proposition 7.11
.

[
3
, Proposition
2.1]
Let
(G,\mathcal{F})

be a free product and let
\Phi\in\Out(G,\mathcal{F})
be polynomially growing. There is a
free factor system
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of
(G,\mathcal{F})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Phi^{k}
preserves

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
(G,\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is sporadic. In particular,
\Phi^{k}
preserves a Bass–Serre
tree associated to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

We are now ready to prove a vanishing result for the
L^{2}
-torsion of polynomially growing
automorphisms of free products.

Proposition 7.12
.

Let
G=G_{1}\ast\ldots\ast G_{k}\ast F_{N}
be a free product of groups satisfying
condition (DFJ), let
\Phi
be a polynomially-growing automorphism of
G
which
preserves the conjugacy classes of the factors
G_{i}
, and let
\Phi_{i}\colon G_{i}\to G_{i}

be the appropriate restriction of
\Phi
up to conjugacy. Suppose that for every

i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we have
\rho^{(2)}(\Phi_{i})=0
, then
\rho^{(2)}(\Phi)=0
.

Proof.

This proof follows a similar structure
to
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. We proceed by induction on
the Grushko rank
k+N
. If
k=1
and
N=0
, then
\rho^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0
, since

\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}
by hypothesis. If
k=0
and
N=1
, then
\Gamma
has finite index
subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, in particular,
\rho^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0
. Suppose now

k+N\geq 2
. Let
\Phi
be the image of
\Phi
in
\Out(G)
and let
\mathcal{F}
be the
sporadic free factor system given by Proposition
7.11
. Let

\mathcal{T}
be the Bass–Serre tree of
G
associated to the free factor system
\mathcal{F}
and
note that there is a positive power of
\Phi
which preserves
\mathcal{T}
. The vertex
stabilisers of
G
acting on
\mathcal{T}
are proper free factors of
G
and hence have
smaller Grushko rank than
G
and the edge stabilisers are trivial. It follows that a
finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
acts on
\mathcal{T}
with vertex stabilisers of
the form
\Gamma^{\prime}_{v}=G_{v}\rtimes_{\Phi^{n}|_{G_{v}}}\mathbb{Z}
and with infinite cyclic edge
stabilisers. Now,
\Phi^{n}
is det-finite and
\rho^{(2)}(\Phi^{n}_{v})=0
by the inductive
hypothesis and
\rho^{(2)}(\mathbb{Z})=0
. Thus, the result follows from
Example
5.20
and
Theorem
5.3
(ii)
.
∎

We remark that in the special case of free-by-cyclic groups this recovers a result of
Clay
[
13
]
.

Corollary 7.13
.

If
\Phi\in\Aut(F_{N})
is polynomially growing, then

\rho^{(2)}(F_{N}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z})=0
.

We first deal with the one-ended case.

\Gamma=G\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}

5.3
(ii)

Proposition 7.15
.

Let
G
be a group hyperbolic relative to a finite collection

7.5.3.
Right-angled Artin and Coxeter groups

Let
L
be a flag complex. The
right-angled Artin group
(RAAG)
A_{L}
is defined to
be the group with presentation

\langle L^{(0)}\ |\ [v,w]\text{ if }\{v,w\}\in L^{(1)}\rangle.

The
right-angled Coxeter group
(RACG) is the group

W_{L}=A_{L}/\langle\langle v^{2}\ |\ v\in L^{(0)}\rangle\rangle.

We define a number of
automorphisms of RAAGs and RACGs:

(i)

graph automorphisms
, that is automorphism induced from
L
;

(ii)

inversions
, which send
v\mapsto v^{-1}
and
u\mapsto u
for
u\neq v
and

u,v\in L^{(0)}
;

(iii)

partial conjugations

k_{W,C}
for
w\in L^{(0)}
and a connected component

C
of
L\setminus\mathrm{st}(w)
, which are defined by
k_{w,C}(u)=w^{-1}uw
if

u\in C^{(0)}
and
k_{w,C}(u)=u
if
u\in L^{(0)}\setminus C
;

(iv)

folds

t_{v,w}
for any
v,w\in L^{(0)}
with

\mathrm{lk}(v)\subseteq\mathrm{lk}(w)
, which are defined by
t_{v,w}(v)=vw
and

t_{v,w}(u)=u
for all
u\in L^{(0)}\setminus\{v\}
.

We say an automorphism of
A_{L}
(resp.
W_{L}
) is
untwisted
if it is contained in
the subgroup
U(A_{L})\leqslant\Aut(A_{L})
(resp.
U(W_{L})\leqslant\Aut(W_{L})
) which is
generated by the graph automorphisms, inversions, partial conjugations, and folds.

By
[
15
, Proposition A(3)]
, the untwisted automorphisms of
A_{L}
are
exactly the automorphism which preserve the standard coarse median structure on
A_{L}
.
By
[
45
]
, the subgroup of untwisted automorphisms of
\Aut(W_{L})
has
finite index.

Proposition 7.16
.

Let
L
be a flag complex on
[m]
and let
\Gamma=A_{L}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}
. If
\Phi
is
an untwisted and polynomially growing automorphism of

We note that the following argument is structurally very similar to the proof
of
[

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

. The base case, when

, implies

is

as

Surveillance of enterovirus infection and genotypes/subgenotypes of enteroviruses in Dak Lak, Vietnam in 2022-2024

Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The risk of hand, foot, and mouth disease remains high in Dak Lak.•The genetic diversity of the virus, with major outbreaks driven by subgenotype.•A shift in circulating subgenotypes warns Hand, foot, and mouth disease epidemics.•The continued threat of Hand, foot, and mouth disease epidemics highlights the need for timely molecular surveillance.Keywords: Hand, foot, and mouth disease; Enterovirus; Subgenotype; Dak Lak

Introduction

Enteroviruses (EVs), a family of single-stranded positive-sense ribonucleic acid (RNA) viruses, are classified under the family of Picornaviridae, a family of many different pathogens of different genetic and antigenological compositions. Besides nonhuman EVs and rhinoviruses, over 100 serotypes have been identified and characterized as human EV species, classified under human EV species, that is, from Enterovirus A to Enterovirus D [1]. In total, there are 116 serotypes of these human EVs. In most cases, typing is carried out for the amplification and sequencing of capsid protein VP1, as it is responsible for maximum antigenic variation and defines the serotypes of these human EVs. Human EV infections lead to a wide range of clinical manifestations

The EV infections can bring about serious neurological and systemic disorders, including sepsis, acute flaccid paralysis, myocarditis, encephalitis, and acute flaccid myelitis, despite the fact that most infections are mild in nature [2,3]. In the Western Pacific, in China, Japan, and Taiwan, hand, foot, and mouth diseases (HFMDs) have had a major negative impact on the poor health and economic status of people due to frequent and large-scale outbreaks in this region [4]. EV-A71 is particularly of great concern as it is significantly linked with complications and deaths. In addition to this, Coxsackievirus A16 (CVA16), A6 (CVA6), and A10 (CVA10) are major contributors to the overall epidemic activities in this region as well [[3], [4], [5]]. It can be clearly implied that EV-A71 has survived long enough in Dak Lak region of Vietnam in relation to the evolutionary process based on the major genetic changes that have taken place in this period of time in this region. Molecular analysis in the Dak Lak province in Dak Lak region of Vietnam revealed genotype change in the period ranging from 2011 to 2016 [6].

There have been few recent studies describing the strains that are currently in circulation. Without recent molecular surveillance results, it makes it harder to assess risks, put in place early warning systems, and prepare for possible outbreaks. But the continued threat of HFMD epidemics highlights the need for timely molecular surveillance. The high pathogenicity of EV-A71 makes it a pressing need. To determine the genotypes and subgenotypes that are in circulation, we conducted molecular surveillance of EVs isolates in Dak Lak of Vietnam in 2022-2024. In this study, there is the added perspective of the phylogenetic relationship of regional epidemic strains.

Methods

Patients and sampling

Dak Lak province, with an estimated population of approximately 1.9 million, is located between 12°09′-13°25′ N latitude and 107°28′-108°59′ E longitude, bordering Cambodia. The terrain is predominantly flat highland, with an average elevation of approximately 500 m above sea level. The province has a tropical monsoon climate characterized by two distinct seasons: a rainy season from May to October and a dry season from November to April. The rainy season accounts for approximately 90% of the annual precipitation. The average annual rainfall is around 2000 mm, and the mean annual temperature ranges from 23°C to 24°C. A total of 167 inpatients admitted in the district/provincial hospitals in Dak Lak who satisfied the predetermined eligibility requirements were included in the study between 2022 and 2024. According to the Ministry of Health of Viet Nam guidelines, a suspected case of HFMD is defined as a brief febrile illness accompanied by a typical skin rash, with or without oral ulcers [7].

Each participant provided a specimen, such as a stool sample or a throat swab, which was sent to the Tay Nguyen Institute of Hygiene and Epidemiology’s Department of Microbiology and Immunology in Dak Lak, Vietnam, for laboratory verification of viral infection. Age, sex, and sampling date were among the pertinent patient data that were methodically recorded for later examination.

RNA extraction and virus detection

Viral RNA was isolated from stool and throat swab specimens using the QIAamp Viral RNA Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany) following the manufacturer’s recommendations. EV RNA was detected by real-time reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) with primer–probe sets specific for the conserved 5′ untranslated region of the genome [4].

Virus sequencing

Samples with positive real-time RT-PCR results with Ct value under 25 were chosen for VP1 sequence analysis. As mentioned above, RNA extraction was carried out. RT-PCR was used to amplify the entire VP1 coding region using primer pairs that had been previously reported [8]. The complete VP1 gene (891 bp) was sequenced using the primers 2349F, 2757F (5′-GCHAAYTGGGAYATAGACATAAC-3′), 2780R (5′- CCCTRTATCTGTATTGDCC-3′), and 3393R. a thermal cycler for 45 minutes at 48°C, 2 minutes at 94°C, 35 cycles of 94°C for 10 seconds, 50°C for 10 seconds, 65°C for 1 minute, and then at 65°C for 5 minutes. The partial VP1 gene was performed using the primers as previous described [9]. Following purification using the Wizard SV Gel and PCR Clean-Up System (Promega), amplicons were confirmed by electrophoresis on a 1% agarose gel. Using an ABI 3130XL Genetic Analyzer (Applied Biosystems), purified products underwent bidirectional sequencing. The resulting nucleotide sequences were submitted to the GenBank database.

Phylogenetic analysis

EV-A71 VP1 nucleotide sequences were aligned using ClustalX version 2.0. For comparative analysis, reference sequences that represented entire VP1 regions were obtained from the GenBank database. Using the Kimura two-parameter substitution model and the maximum-likelihood method in molecular evolutionary genetics analysis (MEGA) version 11, phylogenetic relationships were deduced [10]. Using 1000 iterations of bootstrap resampling, the statistical support for tree topology was evaluated.

Statistical analysis

The chi-square (χ²) test was used to compare the incidence of HFMD stratified by age group and sex; a two-tailed P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.

Nucleotide sequence accession numbers

The complete VP1 sequences reported in this study were deposited in the GenBank sequence database under the accession numbers PP309822 to PP309830.

Ethical approval

All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee and with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards.

Phylogenetic analysis on the VP1 gene region of 16 EV-A71 isolates from Dak Lak, Vietnam, in 2022-2024 showed that the main subgenotype was B5 which made up 15 out of 16 isolates. One isolate EV71_28. 23_DakLak_2023 belonged to subgenotype C4. The subgenotype B5 strains from Dak Lak were closely related to subgenotype B5 strains from Ho Chi Minh City in 2023 (Figure 5). All 7/7 CVA6 strains circulating in Dak Lak during 2022-2024 belonged to subgenogroup D3 and were closely related to strains circulating in Dong Thap in 2013 and Australia in 2017 (Figure 6). Meanwhile, all CVA16 strains belonged to subgenogroup B1a and were genetically closely related to strains identified in Ho Chi Minh City in 2015, Hai Phong in 2017, Thailand in 2014, and Japan in 2010 (Figure 7). Phylogenetic analysis of CVA10 isolates also showed that all 5/5 CVA10 strains belonged to subgenogroup D3 and were closely related to strains circulating in China in 2012-2015 (Figure 8).

Discussion

The infection of children with EV is a major concern for children’s health because the occurrence of recurrent large-scale outbreaks has been demonstrated in various Asian countries. Although the infection is generally limited in scope, some cases will lead to serious neurocognitive and systemic infections, in which a small percentage of the cases will lead to death [4]. The global distribution of the outbreak patterns with respect to EV has not been fully developed to be consistent with each other; however, there have been considerable variations in the forms of infection that have been observed in the world in the last decade [11].

There was a total of 96 positive cases of HFMD in Dak Lak of Vietnam from 2022 to 2024. Molecular characterization indicated that EV-A71 and CVA6 was the major etiological agent responsible for 56.2% of the cases. CVA16 and CVA10 were present in smaller proportions of 16.7% and 10.4%, respectively. In addition, 10.4% of the cases were positive for EV, with no further typing being possible. This result confirms previous findings on EV in the same region [12].

In the Northern region of Vietnam, surveillance data from 2008 to 2018 found that out of 2603 specimens that were positive for EV, EV-A71 made up 35.04%, followed by CVA6 at 28.00%, CVA16 at 19.32%, and CVA10 at 2.61%, while the rest were <1% [13].

A seasonal analysis for EV infections indicated that the highest cases of EV infections occurred during the dry season, that is, from July to November. In addition, the highest cases of EV infections occurred in September and November. The results obtained in this study for EV infections are consistent with the results of an earlier periodicity report for HFMD studies conducted in Southern of Vietnam [14)]. Age-stratified analysis indicated that children aged 1-5 years old have the highest specimens submitted and the highest proportion of EV infections. Earlier studies have indicated that children in the age group of 1-5 years are highly susceptible to EV infections. For instance, long-term studies conducted in South Korea from 2011 to 2020 [15] and studies conducted in the southern province of Vietnam [14] have indicated that children in the 1-5 years age group have the highest proportion of EV infections.

Although previous studies found differences in the susceptibility to EV infections in relation to sex, which may be due to differences in the immune response among the population [16], in this study, no significant differences in the detection rate of EV between the sexes were found.

(EV-A71 is identified as one of the major causative pathogens of HFMD and has often been linked with outbreaks of large magnitude and serious clinical consequences and increased mortality rates among young children [17]. The first case of EV-A71 infection identified in Vietnam was recorded between 1989 and 1990 [18]. In recent years, molecular surveillance of EV-A71 based on genotyping of the virus has been increasingly employed in different countries to enable timely detection of emerging strains of EV-A71, with different subgenotypes identified annually [15,[19], [20], [21]].

CVA6 identified as the second most frequently detected pathogen in the present study, is increasingly being identified as one of the major causative pathogens of HFMD outbreaks across Asia. In fact, in recent years, it has partially replaced EV-A71 and CVA16 as major circulating pathogens in these regions [15,22].

Phylogenetic analysis showed that all isolates of EV-A71 identified in this study were classified as subgenotype B5 and were closely related to each other. These isolates had a high degree of genetic relatedness with those of EV-A71 B5 identified as circulating in southern Vietnam during 2023, suggesting regional co-circulation and possible epidemiological linkage.

There are some publications [14,[23], [24], [25]], which present the results of some long-term observations of patients suffering from HFMD and their contact persons, who lived in the Southern provinces in Vietnam. The authors proved the role of EV-A71 virus as the main etiological factor for HFMD, which is the emerging infection with the highest incidence in Vietnam (2013-2018). At the same time, together with EV-A71 virus, some other strains from the EV A species, such as CVA10, CVA6, and CVA16, also circulated in these provinces. Co-circulation in Vietnam in 2018 of EV-A71 viruses belonging to two different subgenotypes, namely, C4 and B5, has been described. These data coincide with our research results. The results of our research, conducted in the province in Dak Lak of Vietnam, also showed that in 2011-2013, the predominant subgenotype was C4, and then this was replaced by the B5 subgenotype in 2014 [6]. Viruses of these subgenotypes have been mainly detected during outbreaks of EV infections, which have been registered during the same period of time in China and Taiwan. The peak incidence of HFMD in Vietnam was recorded in 2011, and the largest number of cases was reported in the Southern provinces. In 2011, the Ministry of Health of Vietnam recognized HFMD as a severe infectious disease (infectious disease of class B), which has high epidemic potential, and included it in the list of infectious diseases for weekly reporting [25]. The highest incidence of EV infection and the highest number of deaths have been reported from EV-A71. Circulation of EV-A71 subgenotypes C1, C4, C5, and B5 was recorded at different periods of time in Vietnam [16,17,25].

Several longitudinal studies have been carried out to examine the occurrence of HFMD among patients and their families in southern Vietnam, and this has helped in gathering useful information on the pattern of EV circulation in Vietnam [14,[23], [24], [25]]. In all the studies, EV-A71 was consistently reported as the dominant virus responsible for HFMD, with clear evidence of its emergence as an etiologic agent with high incidence rates in the period spanning 2013 to 2018. In addition to EV-A71, other EV A genus strains that were reported to be simultaneously circulating in HFMD-endemic areas included CVA10, CVA6, and CVA16.

Interestingly, simultaneous circulation of EV-A71 subgenotypes B5 and C4 in Vietnam occurred during 2018. This is consistent with the molecular characteristics identified in this current study. Similarly, earlier state surveillance in Dak Lak province of Dak Lak demonstrated a highly dynamic pattern of genotype replacement; subgenotype C4 was the most common from 2011 through 2013 and was replaced by subgenotype B5 beginning in 2014 [6]. Virus isolates from both of these subgenotypes were also commonly associated with the reports of EV outbreaks in other countries during the same period, such as China and Taiwan, which suggests that there are broader regional transmission dynamics.

In Vietnam, there was a major outbreak of HFMD in 2011. Following this outbreak, it was reported that there was a great deal of disease present in the southern provinces of Vietnam. As a result, the Ministry of Health officially classified it as a class B infectious disease with a large potential for major epidemics and added it to the national notifiable weekly infectious disease monitoring program [25]. In all instances where there was a surveillance program for infectious diseases, it was consistently shown that EV-71 had the highest rates of morbidity and mortality. It has been shown that there are a number of subgenotypes of EV-71 present in Vietnam at different times, namely C1, C4, C5, and B5 [16,17,25].

The results of this study indicated that 100% of the CVA16-positive samples collected in Dak Lak during 2022-2024 belonged to subgenogroup B1a. This finding is consistent with our previous studies on CVA16 strains isolated in Dak Lak in 2019 and circulating CVA16 strains in Ho Chi Minh City in 2016, and a study conducted in southern Vietnam using samples collected during 2011-2017 [26]. In Thailand, a study published in 2018 using samples collected in 2013 showed that circulating CVA16 strains belonged to subgenogroups B1a and B1b, with B1a being predominant [27]. A study conducted across 22 provinces in China in 2020 revealed the circulation of three subgenogroups, B1a, B1b, and B1c, of which B1b remained the predominant subgenogroup. All CVA16 strains identified in Beijing during 2010-2019 belonged to subgenogroup B1, with B1b being the dominant lineage [28]. Previous studies have consistently demonstrated that all CVA16 strains circulating in Vietnam from 2005 to 2021 belonged to subgenogroup B1a, suggesting that the genetic diversity of CVA16 is lower than that of EV-A71 [13,25,26]. Similarly, CVA16 subgenogroup B1 was also the predominant lineage among HFMD cases in China from 2000 to 2018 [28,29].

In this study, phylogenetic analysis showed that all CVA6 strains identified in Dak Lak during 2022-2024 belonged to subgenogroup D3 and clustered with strains circulating in Australia and Dong Thap during 2013-2021. Since 2008, subgenogroup D3 has become the predominant lineage in many countries worldwide [30,31] Therefore, the spread of subgenogroup D3 may have contributed to the prolonged global outbreaks of HFMD associated with CVA6. Molecular epidemiological studies of CVA6 have demonstrated a shift from subgenogroup D2 to D3 in China and France [30,32]. Furthermore, since 2010, CVA6/D3 strains have caused major HFMD outbreaks in several countries, including Brazil, India, Japan, and Vietnam [13].

Phylogenetic data obtained from Vietnamese EV isolates indicate that there have been repeated introductions of the virus from neighboring countries, followed by extensive local transmission. These repeated importations, in addition to the local transmission, have probably contributed to the continued burden HFMD in Vietnam. HFMD, therefore, remains a serious public health concern in the country. To address this concern, there is a national surveillance system in Vietnam that seeks to improve early outbreak detection and epidemiological trend analysis to inform early public health interventions.

There are limitations in our study. Clinical data of HFMD cases are not available to be included in this study, therefore, the valuation on the clinical severity and disease outcomes according to genotype is lacked. Secondly, partial sequencing of the VP1 gene region was performed, and the obtained sequences varied in nucleotide length. Therefore, nucleotide and amino acid sequence homology between viruses circulating in Dak Lak and those circulating in other regions of Vietnam could not be comprehensively analyzed, and amino acid substitutions associated with antigenic determinants or viral virulence could not be identified. In addition, the study was conducted with a relatively small sample size, which may not fully represent the circulating viral subgenotypes.

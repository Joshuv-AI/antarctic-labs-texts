Optimized super-twisting active disturbance rejection control based on nonlinear extended state observer for aircraft attitude stabilization
Authors: FatemehSadat Yazdiniya, GholamReza Ansarifar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, a novel control strategy for the attitude stabilization of a nonlinear aircraft model is proposed, integrating active disturbance rejection control (ADRC) with the smart super-twisting sliding mode control (ST-SMC) algorithm. The primary objective of this study is to enhance the control system’s robustness against disturbances while improving tracking accuracy. The proposed method leverages a nonlinear extended state observer (NESO) to accurately estimate the lumped disturbances and system uncertainties. These estimated disturbances are actively compensated within the control law, thereby transforming the uncertain nonlinear system into a simplified compensated system. To ensure robust tracking and effective disturbance rejection, a high-order sliding mode control law based on the super-twisting algorithm is implemented, where the gains of the super-twisting controller are tuned using the reinforcement learning (RL) method. The stability of the nonlinear extended state observer and the super-twisting ADRC is theoretically analyzed, and closed-loop stability is proved. Extensive simulations are conducted on a nonlinear aircraft model under various disturbance scenarios, including actuator faults, aerodynamic uncertainties, and atmospheric disturbances. The results demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method over traditional control strategies, such as Linear ADRC and classical ST-SMC, in terms of convergence speed, robustness, and disturbance rejection performance. This study contributes significantly to flight control systems, offering a robust and efficient solution for modern aerospace applications.Keywords: Nonlinear aircraft model, Active disturbance rejection control (ADRC), Nonlinear extended state observer (NESO), Super-Twisting sliding algorithm (STA), Reinforcement learning (RL)Subject terms: Engineering, Aerospace engineering, Applied mathematics

Introduction

Aircraft flying at high altitudes encounter significant variability in their dynamic parameters, exhibiting heightened sensitivity to flight conditions, physical properties, and atmospheric disturbances. In such environments, achieving precise attitude control becomes essential, necessitating continuous adjustments throughout the flight. Consequently, designing a robust control system capable of compensating for external disturbances and accommodating changes in the system’s physical parameters is a fundamental aspect of modern flight control research.

In systems characterized by complex non-linear dynamics, traditional control methods such as proportional, integral, and derivative (PID) controllers and linear quadratic regulator (LQR) controllers often fail to track the desired path under conditions of uncertainty and strong disturbances. To address this challenge, active disturbance rejection control (ADRC) was introduced by Han1. This proposal by Gao et al.2,3 was introduced in the English language. ADRC is an advanced control strategy that estimates the disturbances of the entire system, including external disturbances, uncertainties, and unmodeled dynamics, using an extended state observer (ESO). Due to its robust performance and simplicity, ADRC has found widespread applications in numerous practical scenarios4–8.

ADRC consists of two essential components: an Extended State Observer (ESO) and a suitable control law for disturbance rejection. The ESO9 is the core of ADRC because it estimates unmodeled dynamics, uncertainties, and external disturbances considered as total the disturbance. Also, it provides estimations of the system states. The ESO has been widely adopted since its initial introduction by Han9 primarily due to two key advantages10: First, when system dynamics are unknown, the ESO can estimate dynamics and disturbances, while guaranteeing an upper bound on the estimation error that decreases with increasing observer bandwidth. Second, when system dynamics are explicitly defined, the observer’s error dynamics exhibit asymptotic stability.

Considering the inherent challenges of flight vehicles, a suitable control strategy to be integrated with the extended state observer can be drawn from the class of robust control techniques. Among the various non-linear robust control methods, sliding mode control has maintained a prominent position over the past decades due to its high robustness and simplicity of design. However, a major drawback of SMC is the chattering phenomenon caused by the switching action near the sliding surface. To mitigate this issue, high-order sliding mode control (HOSMC) techniques have emerged as an effective solution. These methods minimize chattering by concealing the high-frequency switching term within higher derivatives of the sliding variable. The super-twisting sliding mode control (ST-SMC), known as a second-order sliding mode technique significantly, reduces chattering while enhancing efficiency, convergence speed, and robustness compared to conventional methods11,12.

One of the main practical challenges lies in tuning multiple control gains, which have a direct impact on system stability, convergence rate, and disturbance rejection capability13. Manual adjustment of these gains is often time-consuming and may even result in suboptimal performance, particularly in complex, multidimensional, and nonlinear flight systems14. With the rapid progress of machine learning and artificial intelligence, data-driven approaches have been increasingly employed to address this limitation. These methods provide the ability to automatically adapt control parameters to varying operating conditions, thereby reducing reliance on manual trial-and-error tuning and improving overall performance. In particular, reinforcement learning offers a powerful framework for online and adaptive gain adjustment, ensuring robustness and adaptability of flight control systems under highly nonlinear dynamics and uncertain environments.

This paper presents a novel active disturbance rejection control (ADRC) framework for nonlinear model aircraft attitude stabilization, integrating a reinforcement learning tuned super-twisting sliding mode controller with a nonlinear extended state observer. The proposed method enhances robustness and adaptability against actuator dynamics, aerodynamic uncertainties, sensor bias, and external disturbances. Key contributions include: (i) development of a super-twisting ADRC structure with reinforcement learning, (ii) design of a nonlinear ESO for disturbance estimation and compensation, and (iii) implementation of a nonlinear tracking differentiator to improve convergence and reduce oscillations. In this paper, in Sect. “Mathematical model”, the nonlinear aircraft model and actuators’ dynamics are described. Then, in Sect. “Controller methodology”, the non-linear extended state observer for estimating unknown variables is presented. The non-linear active disturbance rejection controller based on a smart super-twisting algorithm is designed to ensure that the closed-loop state variables match the reference state. In the following, the stability of non-linear ESO and ADRC is theoretically analyzed and proved. In Sect. “Stability analysis of closed-loop dynamics”, the attitude control of the aircraft is simulated, taking into account the limitations of the actuators and the types of disturbances. In Sect. “Simulation result”, the summary and conclusions about this work are discussed and, suggestions for future work are presented.

The main objective of the paper is attitude control, control of three rotational DOFs (ϕ,θ,ψ), with the three aerodynamic actuators (aileron, elevator, rudder), i.e., a 3-input/3-output design problem. Indeed, although the full model is 6-DOF, the control objective is attitude regulation (ϕ,θ,ψ), achieved through the three control inputs . In other words, the problem is formulated as a three-input/three-output (MIMO) control design, which is standard in aircraft attitude control practice. The main objective of this study is to apply attitude control to the six-degree-of-freedom model. The obtained results confirm the capability of the proposed approach in achieving stable and reliable system performance.

Super twisting algorithm

Sliding mode control (SMC), also known as variable structure control, is a robust control methodology characterized by high-speed switching control laws. This technique directs system state variables toward a predefined sliding surface within the state space. The SMC control law consists of two distinct components: one that guides the system states to the sliding surface (reaching mode) and another that keeps them on this surface (sliding mode).

SMC offers significant advantages, including robustness against external disturbances and uncertainties, and rapid transient response. However, a major drawback of conventional SMC is the chattering phenomenon, which arises from the high-frequency switching of control signals. To address this issue, higher-order sliding mode control (HOSMC) has been developed as an effective solution. HOSMC represents a powerful alternative to classical SMC, leveraging the same measurement information to mitigate or eliminate chattering while providing enhanced robustness and control efficiency in the presence of uncertainties and external disturbances17. Among the most prominent HOSMC algorithms is the super-twisting control law, which combines a discontinuous time derivative term with a continuous function of the sliding variable. Controller gain tuning via RL-DDPG

As a subset of machine learning, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has been successfully applied across various domains. As an unsupervised learning approach, DRL is capable of establishing a direct mapping between observations and desired outputs using the maximum likelihood method. Through continuous interaction with the environment guided by policy strategies and a reward function it can iteratively discover and refine this mapping19. Two main classical DRL algorithms are the Deep Q-Network (DQN)20 and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG)21. While DQN is limited to discrete state systems, such as chess, DDPG extends it to handle continuous states and actions. Later improvements, such as Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3) and Asynchronous Advantage Actor–Critic (A3C), have been developed to address specific shortcomings of the original methods, though they introduce their own limitations.

In this work, DDPG is employed to determine the STA-ADRC controller parameters. The essence of DDPG lies in obtaining the optimal policy through continuous interaction between the environment and the agent. This loop continues until an optimization convergence condition is checked. Specifically, a penalty term is included for large error derivatives to suppress oscillatory responses. Training is conducted using the DDPG algorithm in a continuous action space, enabling smooth parameter updates. To ensure closed-loop stability during training, the output gains from the agent are passed through a saturation function, constraining them to a predefined positive range before application to the controller. This approach allowed the RL agent to autonomously discover optimal gain values for the nine controller parameters, enhancing robustness and reducing dependency on manual tuning. Disturbance modeling

In the design and analysis of aircraft control systems, it is essential to account for various sources of disturbances and uncertainties that may degrade performance and compromise stability. This section presents a comprehensive model of disturbances, encompassing multiple sources of errors and uncertainties that can impact the aircraft’s flight dynamics and control system performance.

1) Actuator Dynamics: The first source of error considered is the imperfect performance of actuators. Actuator dynamics can introduce disturbances in the control inputs, potentially leading to deviations from the desired control signals. This disturbance is modeled as an additive term to the control inputs, representing the discrepancy between the commanded and actual actuator outputs.

2) Model Uncertainties: The second source of error stems from the simplifying assumptions made in deriving the equations of motion. Several flight parameters, such as mass properties, moments of inertia, and thrust forces, are typically assumed constant for simplification. These uncertainties are modeled as percentage variations in the respective parameters.

These uncertainties are modeled as percentage changes in the respective parameters, allowing for both increases and decreases in their values.

3) Sensors Bias: Sensor outputs, which serve as inputs to the controller and observer, can be subject to noise and errors. This source of disturbance is modeled as sensor bias with a constant noise of . The bias is applied to the feedback of Euler angles and their rates, representing systematic errors in the sensor measurements.

4) Atmospheric Disturbances: During flight, an aircraft is subjected to wind and atmospheric turbulence, which can significantly disrupt the attitude control system. To accurately model these atmospheric disturbances, the Dryden wind model is utilized. This model is derived from atmospheric turbulence velocity spectra and their gradients, forming a transfer function that converts white noise signals into colored noise. This conversion effectively simulates realistic wind patterns. The parameters of the Dryden model are tuned according to the MIL-F-8785 C standard, ensuring that the simulated atmospheric disturbances closely match real-world conditions. As illustrated in Fig. 7, the implemented Dryden wind model is designed based on the aircraft’s flight altitude, assuming light turbulence conditions. The resulting wind velocities are added to the aircraft’s velocities in all three directions.

Firstly, the controller performance is evaluated against two reference methods: a standalone super-twisting sliding mode controller and a linear ADRC scheme, where the super-twisting controller is combined with a linear extended state observer (LESO). The latter is referred to as linear ADRC due to the linear nature of its observer. Then, the estimation capabilities of the nonlinear extended state observer are benchmarked against those of a linear extended state observer, providing a clear demonstration of its enhanced performance. These comprehensive comparisons clearly demonstrate the effectiveness and advantages of the proposed method, establishing it as a robust and efficient solution for attitude control in nonlinear systems. Conclusion

In this paper, a robust and efficient control strategy for attitude stabilization of a nonlinear aircraft model is presented by integrating active disturbance rejection control (ADRC) and an Optimezd super-twisting sliding mode control (ST-SMC) algorithm. The proposed control method effectively combines the disturbance estimation capability of the nonlinear extended state observer (NESO) with the robustness of the super-twisting algorithm and reinforcement learning to achieve precise attitude control in the presence of significant uncertainties and external disturbances. The stability analysis, performed using Lyapunov theory, confirms the asymptotic stability of the closed-loop system. Extensive simulation results, conducted on a Learjet 24 model considering the actuators model under various disturbance scenarios, validate the superior performance of the proposed method in terms of convergence speed, robustness, and disturbance rejection capability when compared with the linear ADRC scheme (based on STC + LESO) and the classical ST-SMC technique. The results demonstrate the proposed controller’s capability to maintain stability even under severe disturbances and actuator faults, while achieving low tracking errors and high disturbance rejection efficiency. Furthermore, the comparative analysis between nonlinear and linear extended state observers (NESO and LESO) highlights the superior accuracy and faster convergence of the NESO. This research significantly contributes to modern flight control systems by providing a reliable and resilient approach to attitude stabilization, which can be effectively applied to both military and civilian aerospace applications.

Future research could focus on leveraging data-driven approaches for observer design, such as employing deep learning, which could significantly improve disturbance estimation accuracy and adaptive dynamics modeling. These methods can complement the NESO by learning complex and time-varying dynamics from data, thus enhancing the robustness of the control system.

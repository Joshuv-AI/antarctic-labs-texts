A novel hybrid metaheuristic algorithm and response surface methodology approach for predictive modeling and optimization of cementitious compressive strength
Authors: Rawa Saman Maaroof, Akhterkhan Saber Hamad, Mohammad Mahmood Faqe Hussein
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCompressive strength is a fundamental property for evaluating cementitious materials, yet its prediction and optimization remain challenging due to the complex and nonlinear interactions of chemical composition, curing time, and processing conditions. This study presents a hybrid framework that integrates Response Surface Methodology (RSM) with the Genetic Algorithm (GA) to enhance predictive modeling and optimize cement mixtures. RSM was used to construct polynomial models—linear, linear + square, linear + interaction, and full quadratic—to capture the relationships between eight oxide compositions (Silo₂, Allow₃, Foo₃, Cano, Mg, SO₃, K₂O, NaoSiO2,Al2O3,Fe2O3,CaO,MgO,SO3 ,K2O,Na2O) and compressive strength at 2, 7, and 28 days. The Genetic Algorithm was employed to explore this high-dimensional solution space and identify optimal compositions. Experimental data from a regional cement producer /laboratory were used for model training and validation. Results indicate that the full Quadratic model consistently outperformed other RSM models, achieving the lowest RMSE and highest R² across all curing periods. GA optimization further improved performance, with maximum predicted compressive strengths increasing by 83% at 2 days, 48% at 7 days, and 43% at 28 days compared to RSM-only predictions. These findings highlight the robustness of the hybrid GA + RSM approach in capturing nonlinearities, improving accuracy, and achieving superior optimization outcomes. Introduction

Compressive strength is one of the fundamental mechanical properties used to evaluate the quality and durability of cementitious materials in modern construction. This property is influenced by several interrelated factors, including the water-to-cement ratio, curing time, and the incorporation of supplementary cementing materials. In addition, the chemical composition of the cement, particularly the oxide contents such as SiO₂, Al₂O₃, Fe₂O₃, CaO, MgO, SO₃, K₂O, and Na₂O, plays a critical role in determining compressive strength, as these components directly affect hydration reactions and microstructural development. Complex and nonlinear interactions among these variables often result in significant variations in final performance. However, conventional mix design methods frequently fail to account for such interactions, limiting their effectiveness in optimizing desired properties.

In recent years, considerable efforts have been devoted to improving the predictive accuracy and optimization of cementitious materials using advanced modeling techniques1,2. demonstrated that mathematical modeling and regression-based methods can effectively predict compressive strength when supplementary materials such as waste-glass powders are incorporated. Similarly3,4 investigated linear, nonlinear, and artificial neural network (ANN) models to predict the strength of innovative cementitious and geopolymer systems enhanced with pozzolanic materials. Additionally5, applied machine learning methods to estimate bond strength between modified concrete and reinforcing steel, highlighting the growing trend toward data-centric modeling in civil engineering materials. While these studies provide strong predictive capabilities, most focus primarily on prediction, with limited attention to optimization.

Previous studies have predominantly applied either Response Surface Methodology (RSM) or optimization techniques, such as conventional Genetic Algorithms (GA), in isolation. However, only a limited number of studies have attempted to integrate these approaches into a unified framework capable of simultaneously providing accurate predictive modeling and global optimization of cementitious mixtures.

Response Surface Methodology (RSM) has become an attractive alternative due to its ability to systematically design experiments, quantify factor interactions, and construct accurate second-order polynomial models using relatively small datasets. Notably6, applied a hybrid RSM–Genetic Algorithm (GA) approach to enhance permeate flux and reduce energy consumption in microfiltration processes, demonstrating the effectiveness of combining modeling with global optimization. Similarly7, employed RSM with Box-Behnken Design to optimize hydrolysis conditions for producing gelatin hydrolysate from Barramundi scales, achieving highly efficient outcomes. In mining applications8, utilized a hybrid chaos-genetic algorithm coupled with RSM to optimize stope dimensions, resulting in accurate predictive models and multiple Pareto-optimal solutions. These studies collectively highlight the potential of integrating RSM with optimization techniques, such as GA, to achieve both predictive accuracy and practical optimization in complex systems.

Motivated by these advancements and the identified research gap, the present study aims to develop an integrated predictive modeling and optimization framework that combines Response Surface Methodology (RSM) with the Genetic Algorithm (GA), specifically the Genetic Algorithm (GA), to comprehensively enhance the compressive strength of cementitious mixtures. RSM is employed to design the experiments and construct a second-order polynomial model that captures the relationships between influential variables and the response, while the Genetic Algorithm is utilized to efficiently explore the solution space and identify the optimal combinations of input factors. This hybrid framework enhances the capacity for optimal prediction and mechanical-performance improvement of cementitious materials, contributing to the development of high-efficiency and sustainable construction mixtures.

Data collection

Experimental data for this study were gathered at the Kasen Cement Production Laboratory located in the Sulaymaniyah Governorate. The data collection process spanned the entire year of 2024, with daily measurements recorded to ensure comprehensive coverage and temporal consistency. The dataset comprises:

Eight continuous independent variables representing the primary chemical components of the cement mixture: SiO₂, Al₂O₃, Fe₂O₃, CaO, MgO, SO₃, K₂O, and Na₂O.

Three response variables corresponding to compressive strength measured at different curing periods:2-day, 7-day, and 28-day strength tests.

All samples were based on Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) and adhered to the technical specifications and quality standards approved for cement production in Iraq.

Conclusions and recommendations

This section provides a summary of the most important findings from the study, in addition to suggestions for enhancing the accuracy of the models and expanding the applications of the proposed approach in the future.

Conclusions

Model Performance: The Full Quadratic RSM model proved superior across all curing ages, capturing complex nonlinearities and chemical interactions more effectively than linear or partially quadratic models.

Optimization Power of GA: The Genetic Algorithm consistently converged to near-optimal solutions, enhancing predictive performance and significantly improving compressive strength estimates beyond RSM alone.

Chemical Insights: Key oxides (SiO₂, Al₂O₃, Fe₂O₃, CaO) reduced compressive strength, while MgO, SO₃, K₂O, and Na₂O showed variable impacts, and underlined the necessity of advanced models to capture context-dependent effects.

Practical Value: The hybrid GA + RSM framework offers a robust tool for predictive modeling and optimization, supporting real-world cement mix design and advancing the pursuit of high-performance, sustainable materials.

Methodology

Metaheuristic algorithms

Metaheuristic algorithms are advanced stochastic optimization techniques increasingly applied in statistics and Response Surface Methodology (RSM) to solve complex, nonlinear, and multi-modal problems9–11. Unlike traditional gradient-based methods, metaheuristics operate independently of the mathematical structure of the problem and are capable of avoiding local optima by thoroughly exploring the solution space12_13. These algorithms, inspired by natural and physical phenomena—such as Genetic Algorithms (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Simulated Annealing (SA)—have proven effective in optimizing response surfaces, especially when classical RSM techniques struggle with irregular or high-dimensional design spaces9–11,14–16, In practice, metaheuristic algorithms enable researchers to find globally optimal parameter settings without requiring derivative information, making them suitable for improving model fitting, parameter estimation, and prediction accuracy in complex experimental designs9,10,14,17,18.

Eminent metaheuristic algorithms

A wide range of metaheuristic algorithms has been introduced in recent decades, each offering unique advantages and facing specific limitations. The following are some of the most widely recognized metaheuristic techniques, along with concise explanations of their operational principles15,16.

Genetic algorithms

(GA): are computational methods inspired by the principles of natural selection and genetics. The process begins with the random generation of an initial population of potential solutions. This population then undergoes iterative improvement through the application of genetic operators such as selection, crossover, and mutation. These mechanisms emulate the evolutionary process, whereby individuals with higher fitness are more likely to reproduce and transmit their advantageous traits to subsequent generations18_16. GA has been extensively employed in optimization tasks, particularly those requiring the identification of optimal combinations of parameters or features17.

Particle swarm optimization

(PSO): is a population-based metaheuristic algorithm inspired by the collective behavior observed in social organisms, such as bird flocks or fish schools. The algorithm begins by initializing a swarm of particles, where each particle represents a candidate solution to the optimization problem. Particles navigate the search space by updating their positions and velocities based on their individual experiences and the globally best-known position discovered by the swarm. PSO has been effectively applied to a wide range of optimization tasks, particularly in determining optimal weight configurations in neural networks and other machine learning models9–11,14,17,1815,16.

Simulated annealing

(SA): is a stochastic optimization technique inspired by the annealing process in metallurgy, where controlled cooling of a material leads to a stable crystalline structure. The algorithm begins with a randomly generated initial solution and iteratively explores the solution space while gradually decreasing a control parameter analogous to temperature. During the early stages, the algorithm permits occasional acceptance of inferior solutions, enabling it to escape local optima. As the temperature decreases, the likelihood of accepting such solutions diminishes, allowing convergence toward an optimal or near-optimal solution. SA has been successfully applied to a variety of complex optimization problems, particularly those involving parameter tuning in intricate models and simulations9–11,14,17,1815,16.

Tabu search

(TS): is a metaheuristic optimization technique inspired by the concept of adaptive memory in human decision-making processes. The algorithm initiates with a randomly generated solution and explores its neighborhood by applying local search operators, such as swaps or reversals. To prevent cycling and promote exploration of new areas in the solution space, TS employs a tabu list— a short-term memory structure that records recently visited solutions or moves and temporarily prohibits their reconsideration. Tabu Search has proven effective in solving complex combinatorial optimization problems, particularly those involving sequencing and decision-making tasks such as scheduling and routing9–11,14,17,1815,16.

Ant colony optimization

(ACO) : is a swarm intelligence-based algorithm inspired by the foraging behavior of ants, particularly their ability to discover the shortest path between a food source and their nest. The algorithm begins by initializing a set of pheromone trails, which encode the relative quality of previously explored solutions. As artificial ants traverse the search space, they probabilistically select paths influenced by the intensity of existing pheromone trails and the quality of newly discovered solutions. Over time, the collective behavior reinforces promising routes through increased pheromone concentration, guiding subsequent ants toward optimal or near-optimal solutions. ACO has been extensively applied to a variety of combinatorial optimization problems, especially those involving routing and pathfinding in logistics and transportation systems9–11,14,17,1815,16,.

Genetic algorithm (GA): theory and formulas

Genetic algorithm

GA is a population-based metaheuristic optimization technique inspired by the principles of natural selection, evolution, and genetics. It simulates biological processes such as selection, crossover, and mutation to iteratively evolve a population of candidate solutions toward optimality.

GAs are particularly effective in tackling complex, nonlinear, and multi-dimensional optimization problems, especially where traditional gradient-based methods are ineffective, infeasible, or prone to local optima. Their stochastic nature and global search capability make them robust for exploring vast solution spaces in engineering, machine learning, and material design18.

Response surface methodology (RSM)

It is a collection of mathematical and statistical techniques useful for developing, improving, and optimizing processes in which a response of interest is influenced by several variables, such as, 5_6The primary goal is to model and maximize (or minimize) this response. The surface generated through these methods is referred to as a response surface11.

RSM is extensively applied in the industrial sector, particularly in scenarios involving multiple input factors that influence product or process performance, such as quality characteristics or efficiency metrics. These performance measures are referred to as responses, while the input factors are known as independent variables.

Compare the performance of different models

To compare the performance of different models (like RSM models: Linear, Linear + Square, Interaction, Full Quadratic) for predictive modeling (e.g., cement compressive strength), the following performance metrics are commonly used:

R² – Coefficient of determination

The R² value quantifies how well a regression model captures the variance in the observed data. It essentially measures the proportion of the total variation in the dependent variable that is explained by the independent variables.

Range:

R² = 1

The model explains 100% of the variance — a perfect fit.

R² = 0

The model explains none of the variance — equivalent to using the mean as a prediction.

RMSE – root mean square error

RMSE quantifies the average magnitude of the prediction errors, expressing how far the predicted values deviate from the actual values. It is widely used due to its interpretability—it is reported in the same units as the dependent variable.

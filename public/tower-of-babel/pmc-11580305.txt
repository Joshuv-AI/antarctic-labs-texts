Enablers and barriers to the implementation of breast self-examination (BSE) education programmes among adolescent girls in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA): an integrative systematic review protocol
Authors: Benedictus Atsu Hlormenu, Emmanuel Osei Bonsu, Caleb Boadi, William Dormechele, Mercy Oseiwah Adams, Bright Boatey Bossman, Stephen Kwakye Addo, Isaac Yeboah Addo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer is emerging as a leading cause of mortality among women and adolescent girls in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA). However, there is a lack of clarity on the enablers and barriers associated with the implementation of preventive strategies, such as breast self-examination (BSE), particularly among adolescent girls.ObjectiveThis paper presents a protocol for conducting a systematic review aiming to synthesise studies on the enablers and barriers to implementing BSE education programmes among adolescent girls in SSA.Methods and analysisThe systematic review will be conducted from December 2024 to August 2025, following the predefined methodological framework outlined in the PRISMA guidelines. To gather relevant studies, a comprehensive search will be conducted across four main online databases, namely PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library and EMBASE, along with other relevant grey literature sources, such as Google Scholar, to identify pertinent literature on this topic. The screening process will involve an initial review of titles and abstracts to quickly identify relevant studies, followed by a thorough assessment of the full-text versions of selected studies. Two reviewers, working independently, will extract data and evaluate the risk of bias in the included studies using either the Joana Briggs Institute’s Critical Appraisal tool or Cochrane’s Risk of Bias tool V.2 (RoB 2). The focus of the included studies will be on BSE among adolescents aged 10 to 19 years of age. Depending on the results of the literature search and the characteristics of the findings in the selected studies, we will conduct a meta-analysis if possible. Alternatively, a narrative synthesis will be conducted. The review will provide empirical evidence to inform health promoters and policymakers in advocating for BSE, particularly, among adolescent girls in SSA, with the ultimate goal of mitigating the risk of breast cancer.Ethics and disseminationSince this study is based on the analysis of already published research articles, ethical approval is not required. The results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed academic publications and presentations, informing further research and practice regarding BSE education programmes.

Introduction

Breast cancer is widely considered the most prevalent life-threatening cancer affecting women.1 Evidence shows that a significant proportion of breast cancer cases in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) are diagnosed at advanced stages, primarily attributable to a lack of disease awareness.2 For instance, a systematic review underscored the insufficient awareness of early treatment options for breast cancer among women in SSA.3 In numerous sub-regions of SSA, breast cancer constitutes the primary cancer type among women and it is linked to several unfavourable outcomes when diagnosed at advanced stages, such as inoperable tumours, metastasis to other organs, limited access to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, inadequate palliative care, compromised immune systems, treatment non-compliance, increased psychosocial and physical complications, and even deaths.4 5 Therefore, early detection plays a crucial role in enhancing long-term survival rates, and the practice of regular Breast Self-Examination (BSE) has emerged as an innovative approach for early detection.1

Breast cancer screening involves a variety of methods aimed at early detection. These methods commonly include mammography, clinical breast examination, breast ultrasound, MRI and BSE.6 While the selection of a specific screening method depends on several factors, such as age, individual risk factors and healthcare provider recommendations, BSE is widely regarded as the most accessible, cost-free, and promising approach, especially in resource-constrained settings, as it allows individuals to take an active role in their own health regardless of resource limitations.7 Moreover, BSE aligns with the objectives outlined in the Sustainable Development Goal 3.4, which aims to reduce premature mortality from non-communicable diseases through disease prevention and health promotion.8 The Global Breast Cancer Institute, established by the WHO, supports and emphasises the importance of BSE as an essential component of health promotion strategies.8

BSE is crucial for adolescent girls as it empowers them with the knowledge and skills to monitor their breast health and detect any abnormalities early, potentially leading to early diagnosis and improved outcomes in breast cancer cases.9 Adolescent girls are recognised as a vulnerable demographic experiencing critical physiological and psychological development, thus requiring specific healthcare considerations.10 This transitional phase of rapid transformation presents an opportune time for imparting health-related knowledge and fostering behaviours that can have lasting effects into adulthood.10 For instance, a recent study conducted by9 showed that about 73% of the respondents had ever practised BSE and students with good knowledge and perception of BSE were more likely to practice BSE.9 These factors, along with others, underscore the importance of BSE for adolescent girls.

Research shows that adolescent girls in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) may have a notable lack of knowledge regarding BSE, which can be attributed to various factors, such as limited access to health information due to long distance to health facilities, poor communication with health providers, long waiting periods in healthcare centres leading to limited time for health education, limited or no counselling at diagnosis, lack of formal education, poor exposure to media messages on health, among others.11

A systematic review by Srinath et al12 has shown that lack of awareness, cost of screening service, and distance to screening centres act as major obstacles to breast screening for females. The review also found how feelings of embarrassment, fear and cultural factors such as lack of spousal or family support become obstacles to breast screening. Another review article by Iss et al13 on Breast Cancer Awareness in Nigeria established that breast cancer awareness was variable, depending on the population being evaluated, with wide differences between city and rural community dwellers. Identified barriers to breast cancer awareness included financial, political, religious, traditional and cultural factors.13 Akuoko et al3 reviewed barriers to early presentation and diagnosis of breast cancer among African women living in SSA. The review found a lack of awareness of early detection treatment, poor perception of breast cancer and sociocultural factors such as beliefs, traditions and fear as factors impacting SSA women’s breast cancer screening behaviour. On the other hand, some enabling factors can be identified, such as dependable social support, routine access to health education or traning, the availability of early detection services and friendly caregivers.11 Despite the existence of several studies 1423 assessing knowledge of BSE in female adolescents in SSA, there is a noticeable absence of a comprehensive review evaluating and synthesising the available evidence. A systematic review is essential to consolidate the fragmented body of evidence, providing a comprehensive assessment of knowledge levels, identifying regional disparities, and offering evidence-driven insights to inform targeted interventions and shape public health strategies aimed at enhancing early breast cancer detection in this understudied population.

This present systematic review protocol is important as it will provide a systematic and rigorous approach to identify, evaluate and synthesise all relevant studies, thereby facilitating a deeper understanding of the factors influencing the implementation of BSE education programmes in SSA and even beyond. By consolidating the findings of existing studies, this review will help identify gaps in the current evidence base and highlight areas where further research is needed. Ultimately, the findings of this review will be invaluable in informing the development of effective interventions and policies aimed at improving breast health outcomes among adolescent girls in SSA. This systematic review protocol therefore aims to guide a review of relevant existing evidence to delineate strategies for implementing education programmes that are effective and accessible to adolescent girls in SSA to enhance their knowledge of BSE techniques.

Objectives and relevance

This protocol aims to guide a systematic review that will comprehensively identify the key facilitators and barriers associated with implementing educational programmes on BSE among adolescent girls in SSA. By synthesising existing literature, the review will also identify potential gaps and areas for improvement in this context. Furthermore, it intends to delve into the factors that contribute to these facilitators and barriers, offering a nuanced understanding of the challenges and opportunities surrounding BSE education programmes in SSA.

The findings of this review are expected to be valuable for researchers, practitioners, policymakers and other stakeholders involved in the implementation of BSE programmes among adolescent girls in SSA, providing evidence-based insights to inform programme development and implementation strategies. Ultimately, the review aims to contribute to a more equitable, accessible and effective health promotion related to breast health in SSA, potentially leading to improved health outcomes for adolescent girls in the region.

Methods and analysis

The development of this research will adhere to the PRISMA framework. The PRISMA framework offers a systematic and standardised approach for conducting systematic reviews, ensuring transparency and completeness throughout the research process.

By following the PRISMA guidelines, this study aims to enhance the methodological rigour and reporting quality of the systematic review. Considering the systematic nature of this study without a specific hypothesis, an integrative systematic review is deemed appropriate, as it allows for the inclusion of relevant literature from various study modalities, enabling a comprehensive understanding of the topic. This systematic review is scheduled to initiate in December 2024 and conclude in August 2025.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted using databases such as PubMed, Embase, Scopus and the Cochrane Library. The search will specifically target English language studies that provide information on barriers to implementing BSE education programmes among adolescent girls in SSA.

The search strategy will include the following keywords, their synonyms, and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH): “Adolescent” OR “Teen*” OR “Youth*” OR “Girl*” AND “SSA” OR “SSAn*” AND “BSE” OR “Breast self-exam*” OR “BSE” AND “Education” OR “Programme*” OR “Intervention*” OR “Training” AND “Barrier*” OR “Challenge*” OR “Obstacle*” OR “Facilitator*” OR “Enabler*”. This comprehensive search strategy aims to capture relevant studies and ensure the inclusion of pertinent literature for the systematic review. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

For this review, we will include articles written in English to maintain consistency in language and facilitate data extraction and synthesis while excluding studies in languages other than English to ensure clarity and accuracy. We will consider publications up to December 2024 to capture the most recent evidence, excluding studies focusing on cancer screening methods other than BSE to maintain focus and relevance. Participants in the studies should be aged from 10 to 19 years to target the adolescent population, with exclusion criteria applied to opinion papers, reviews, commentaries, editorial reports, newsletters and studies lacking data on this age group to ensure relevance to our research questions. All types of research designs will be considered for inclusion to capture a wide range of evidence and provide a comprehensive review of the literature on BSE education programmes among adolescent girls in SSA.

Study selection

The selection of publications will be carried out in three steps. First, the titles and abstracts will be thoroughly examined based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Second, publications that meet the criteria in the first screening step will proceed to the second screening step, where the full text will be evaluated. Finally, in the third screening step, the references of the selected literature will be reviewed to identify any additional publications that may have been missed during the initial database search, using the same set of inclusion criteria.

Data extraction

The data extraction process will be conducted systematically using standardised Data Extraction Forms (DEFs) developed by the research team. Separate DEFs will be employed for qualitative and quantitative studies that meet the inclusion criteria of this systematic review. This approach will ensure that data extraction is tailored to the specific characteristics of each study type, facilitating a comprehensive and structured review process. During data extraction, key elements such as adolescent girls aged between 10 and 19, residing in both rural and urban areas, and the BSE education programmes will be carefully considered. These elements are crucial to the research questions of this review, as they focus on the target population and intervention of interest.

To promote the accuracy and reliability of the extracted data, it will be reviewed independently by other authors. This step will help mitigate the risk of bias and errors in data extraction, enhancing the overall quality of the review. In cases where disparities in prevalence data are identified, consensus-based discussions among the authors will be conducted to resolve any discrepancies. This collaborative approach will ensure that the data extraction process is rigorous and transparent, contributing to the robustness of the systematic review findings.

Quality assessment

The data extraction and risk of bias assessment will be conducted by two independent reviewers. They will use either the Joana Briggs Institute’s Critical Appraisal tool or Cochrane’s Risk of Bias tool V.2 (RoB 2) to evaluate the included studies. If the literature search results and characteristics of the selected studies allow, a meta-analysis will be conducted. In case a meta-analysis is not feasible, a narrative synthesis will be performed.

In addition to the data analysis, an implication for policy report will be prepared to communicate the main findings and implications of the review to policymakers and health practitioners. Several criteria will be considered when evaluating the quality of the review, including the clarity of objectives, adequacy of the search strategy, appropriateness of inclusion and exclusion criteria, approach to assessing the quality of the included studies, and the strategy for data extraction and analysis.

Both DEFs will cover various aspects of the included studies, including the aims of the study, sampling procedure, data collection methods, analysis approach and limitations. The qualitative extraction form will also assess the level of critical self-reflection about biases and the transferability of the findings to other settings or groups. The quantitative extraction form will focus on evaluating the reliability, validity and generalisability of the quantitative papers.

Data synthesis and analysis

The findings from both qualitative and quantitative articles will be integrated and analysed using a thematic synthesis approach. In this study, we will employ a comprehensive and rigorous approach to analysing qualitative studies within the PRISMA systematic review framework.24 Our approach will begin with identifying and organising themes that emerge from the qualitative studies included in the review. This will involve closely reading and coding the primary study texts to identify recurring ideas, concepts or patterns. Subsequently, we will establish clear connections between the conclusions drawn in the primary studies and the themes identified, ensuring that our synthesis of the qualitative data is firmly rooted in the original study findings.

Additionally, we will conduct a thorough discussion of the content of the articles, critically evaluating the quality of the studies, the relevance of the findings and their overall contribution to our review’s objectives. Following this, we will extract and synthesise the results of the qualitative studies in an analytical manner, summarising key findings, comparing and contrasting results across studies, and identifying overarching themes or patterns. Through this process, we aim to achieve a robust and insightful analysis of the qualitative data, providing a comprehensive and reliable synthesis of the qualitative evidence within the PRISMA systematic review framework.24

The findings from the quantitative papers will be incorporated within the identified themes using the multisource synthesis method. This method enhances transparency in synthesising quantitative and contextual data, allowing for meaningful comparisons between studies.

The subsequent sections of this review will adhere to the structure outlined in the PRISMA 2020 statement.24 This ensures that the reporting is transparent, complete and accurate. The sections will include the identification of relevant studies, the screening process and the criteria used for inclusion in the review. Following the PRISMA guidelines will help maintain the quality and integrity of the review process.

Implementation summary

In addressing the research questions, our study will adopt a comprehensive approach. First, we will conduct a systematic review of existing literature to identify the key factors contributing to the successful implementation of BSE education programmes. This will involve analysing studies that have reported successful outcomes and identifying common themes and strategies.

Second, we will explore the main obstacles or barriers that impede the implementation of BSE education programmes. Through a thorough review of the literature, we will identify challenges faced by programmes in SSA and examine how these barriers have been addressed or overcome in previous studies.

Third, we will investigate how cultural and societal factors influence the implementation of BSE education programmes among adolescent girls in SSA. This will involve examining cultural beliefs, practices and norms related to breast health and cancer screening and how these factors impact the uptake and effectiveness of BSE education programmes.

Finally, we will explore strategies and approaches that can be employed to overcome barriers and enhance the implementation of BSE education programmes. This will include identifying best practices from successful programmes, as well as innovative approaches that have been proposed or tested in the literature.

Ethics and dissemination

This study does not require ethical approval since it solely relies on the analysis of published research articles. The results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed academic publications and presentations, providing guidance for future research and practice in BSE education programmes.

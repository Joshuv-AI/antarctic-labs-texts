Duality covariant curvatures for the heterotic string
Falk Hassler, David Osten, and Yuho Sakatani

Abstract
Duality covariant curvature and torsion tensors in double field theory/generalized geometry are central in analyzing consistent truncations, generalized dualities, and related integrable σ \sigma -models. They are constructed systematically with the help of a larger, auxiliary space in a procedure inspired by Cartan geometry originally proposed by Poláček and Siegel for bosonic strings. It pivots around a maximally isotropic group that captures the generalized structure group of the physical space. We show how dropping the isotropy condition on this group allows us to describe heterotic/type I strings. As an immediate application, we construct a new family of heterotic backgrounds that interpolates between the two-dimensional cigar and trumpet backgrounds.



1
Introduction

Generalized geometry and the closely related double/exceptional field theories provide a unified framework to analyze the dynamics of strings, membranes and their low-energy effective supergravity limit. They extend fundamental objects from differential geometry and seek a physical interpretation for them. Important examples are the generalized metric and the related generalized frame. Like the metric in general relativity, they aim to unify all physical fields of more complicated theory with gravity. Depending on the implemented duality group, these fields include besides the metric various form fields and even fermions for supergroups. In contrast to general relativity, it has proven very difficult to define a curvature tensor in this setup. While a generalized version of the torsion tensor can be constructed from the generalized Lie derivative, the generalized Riemann tensor is more elusive. A first naive guess – the Riemann tensor of standard differential geometry – does not transform covariantly. It has to be further modified and projected. While these steps eventually provides a result, its geometric interpretation is not clear and a more natural way to define curvature is desirable. One might argue that this is a purely mathematical problem because to describe a physical system an action which is invariant under all symmetries of the theory is sufficient. However, recently it became clear that a more refined understanding of curvatures in generalized geometry is central in the exploration of dualities and consistent truncations

One encounters two relevant symmetries in this approach: generalized diffeomorphisms that unify diffeomorphisms with gauge transformations (and even supersymmetry transformations for super-duality groups), and generalized Lorentz transformations – encoding the transformation of fermions. Algebra

We already explained that the Poláček-Siegel construction utilizes the mega-space to geometrize generalized diffeomorphisms and local gauge transformations on the physical space

3.1
Highest weight representations

After the algebra that underlies the Poláček-Siegel construction is fixed, we will focus on its representations. In particular, we are interested in the Verma modules of the fundamental representation, which is characterized by the highest weight state
|^{\alpha}\rangle
. Accordingly,

and

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\tfrac{1}{n}R_{\alpha}^{A}|^{\alpha}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{n}\langle_{\alpha}|\widetilde{R}^{\alpha}_{A}\,,

\displaystyle|_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{n-1}R_{\alpha\beta}|^{\beta}\rangle\,,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\langle^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{n-1}\langle_{\beta}|\widetilde{R}^{\beta\alpha}\,.

\beta
are well-known for
\mathrm{O}(d,d)
and the exceptional duality groups
[
20
]
. Note that
I(R_{1})
is the Dynkin index of the
R_{1}=\text{fundamental}
representation and is given by

I(R)=\frac{\dim R}{2\dim G}\left(\lambda(R),\lambda(R)+2\rho\right)\,.

(92)

Remarkably, for all duality groups
\alpha=2I(R_{1})
and one therefore finds the simple relation

\mathbf{Z}=-\mathbf{C}+\beta\,1\otimes 1\,.

(93)

Finally,
\beta=0
for
\mathrm{O}(d,d)
, and the relation simplifies further to

\mathbf{Z}=-\mathbf{C}\,.

(94)

By using the states defined in section
3.1
, one can verify that

\mathbf{Z}^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}_{\hat{C}\hat{D}}=\langle_{\hat{D}}|\langle_{\hat{C}}|\mathbf{Z}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle|^{\hat{B}}\rangle=\eta^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}\eta_{\hat{C}\hat{D}}-\delta^{\hat{A}}_{\hat{D}}\delta^{\hat{B}}_{\hat{C}}

(95)

holds. This is exactly the relation we derived in (
82
), but in flat instead of curved indices. As we will discuss in section
5
, these two index types are related by the generalized frame

\widehat{E}_{\hat{A}}{}^{\hat{I}}=\langle_{\hat{A}}|\widehat{E}|^{\hat{I}}\rangle\,,\qquad\text{and its inverse}\qquad(\widehat{E}^{-1})_{\hat{I}}{}^{\hat{A}}=\langle_{\hat{I}}|\widehat{E}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle\,.

(96)

Because it is an element of the duality group
\mathrm{O}(d+n,d+n)
, one can simply swap flat for curved indices in (
95
) to obtain (
82
). A quick crosscheck of this result can be done by recovering the quadratic Casimir operator
\mathbf{C}
from
\mathbf{Z}
as

\langle_{\hat{A}}|\mathbf{C}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle=-\langle_{\hat{C}}|\langle_{\hat{A}}|\mathbf{Z}|^{\hat{C}}\rangle|^{\hat{B}}\rangle=-\mathbf{Z}^{\hat{C}\hat{B}}_{\hat{A}\hat{C}}=\left[2(d+n)-1\right]\delta_{\hat{A}}^{\hat{B}}\,,

(97)

which matches the expectation set by (
86
).

Finally, we want to rewrite
\mathbf{Z}
in the heterotic basis introduced in section
3.2
. Doing so give rise to

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{K}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\odot\mathcal{K}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}+2\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\odot\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}+2\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{A}}\odot\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}^{\mathcal{A}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\odot\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}

(98)

\displaystyle-2\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}\odot\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}-2\mathcal{K}_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\odot\mathcal{K}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}^{\tilde{\alpha}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\tilde{\beta}}_{\mathcal{A}}\odot\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{\beta}}^{\mathcal{A}}\,.

In the first line, we collected all contributions from
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}
generators, while the second line only contains generators from the set
\mathfrak{p}
. This particular decomposition will prove very useful in the next section.

5
Generalized mega-frame

After understanding how generalized diffeomorphsims act on the mega-space, we proceed with the generalized frame that underlies the Poláček-Siegel construction. Following previous work, we choose it to have the form
[
1
,
2
]

\widehat{E}=\widetilde{M}\mathcal{E}\widetilde{V}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathcal{E}\in G_{\mathrm{PS}}\,.

(99)

Each of the components on the right-hand side is an element of the duality group
\mathrm{O}(d+n,d+n)
, while
\mathcal{E}
is further restricted to be in the subgroup
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
generated by (
26
). Moreover, a tilde over a quantity emphasizes that it depends on coordinates that parameterize auxiliary directions in the mega-space. At the end of the day, we want to remove these directions to obtain the physical space. Therefore, it is in our interest to keep track of them. The objective of this section is to fix
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{V}
completely by imposing that the mega-space torsion, which is computed with the generalized Lie derivative (
78
) by

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle_{\hat{A}}|\widehat{E}}\widehat{E}\widehat{E}^{-1}=\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}}\,,

(100)

has certain constant components.

5.1
Fixing
\widetilde{M}

As a first step, we want to go from curved to flat indices. This is done by
\widetilde{V}
with the components

\widetilde{V}_{\hat{A}}{}^{\hat{I}}=\langle_{\hat{A}}|\widetilde{V}|^{\hat{I}}\rangle

(101)

that are further restricted by

\widetilde{V}_{\hat{A}}{}^{\hat{I}}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}&0&\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\mu}\\
0&\widetilde{V}_{A}{}^{I}&0\\
0&0&\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\end{pmatrix},

(102)

with
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and
\widetilde{V}_{(\alpha}{}^{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\beta)\mu}=-\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
arising from the requirement
\widetilde{V}\in\mathrm{O}(d+n,d+n)
, namely

\eta^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}\widetilde{V}_{\hat{A}}{}^{\hat{I}}\widetilde{V}_{\hat{B}}{}^{\hat{J}}=\eta^{\hat{I}\hat{J}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\\
0&\eta^{IJ}&0\\
\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(103)

Applying it to the partial derivative in the definition (
78
) gives rise to a new derivative

\widetilde{D}=\widetilde{V}\partial\,.

(104)

On the mega-space, we use the solution

\partial^{\mu}\,\cdot\,=0\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{I}\,\cdot\,\partial^{I}\,\cdot\,=0

(105)

of the section condition which is required for generalized diffeomorphisms to close. Taking into account the chosen restrictions on
\widetilde{V}
in (
102
), we equivalently have

\widetilde{D}^{\alpha}\,\cdot\,=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{D}_{A}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{D}^{A}\,\cdot\,=0\,.

(106)

For the tilded quantities
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{V}
these two equations are solved by

\widetilde{D}^{\alpha}\widetilde{M}=\widetilde{D}^{\alpha}\widetilde{V}=0\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{D}_{A}\widetilde{M}=\widetilde{D}_{A}\widetilde{V}=0\,.

(107)

Moreover, we take

\widetilde{D}^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}=0\,,\qquad\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}=0\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{D}_{A}\mathcal{E}\,\widetilde{D}^{A}\mathcal{E}=0\,,

(108)

hinting that
\mathcal{E}
contains all information about the physical space, while
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{V}
are auxiliary quantities required to tune certain components of the generalized torsion
\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}}
. An inconvenient property of the latter is that it still depends on the auxiliary coordinates of the mega-space. However, we can introduce the twisted torsion
T_{\hat{A}}
by

\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}}=\widetilde{M}_{\hat{A}}{}^{\hat{B}}(\widetilde{M}T_{\hat{B}}\widetilde{M}^{-1})\,,

(109)

for which this dependence will drop out – assuming of course an appropriate choice of
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{V}
. Combining this equation with (
100
) and (
78
) gives rise to the compact expression

\boxed{T_{\hat{A}}=\langle_{\hat{A}}|\mathcal{E}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle\Theta_{\hat{B}}+\langle_{\hat{A}}|\Theta_{\hat{B}}\mathbf{Z}\mathcal{E}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle}

(110)

for the twisted torsion with the Maurer-Cartan form

\Theta_{\hat{A}}=\widetilde{M}^{-1}\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\widetilde{M}+\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{-1}+\mathcal{E}\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{-1}\,.

(111)

Taking into account the restrictions (
107
) and (
108
), one finds that the only non-vanishing components of
\Theta_{\hat{A}}
are

\displaystyle\Theta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{M}^{-1}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\widetilde{M}+\mathcal{E}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{-1}\,,\qquad\text{and}

(112)

\displaystyle\Theta_{A}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{D}_{A}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{-1}\,.

Eventually, we want to fix them such that the lowest components of the twisted generalized torsion become the generators of the structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
we discussed in section
3.3
. This property lies at the heart of the Poláček-Siegel construction and is realized by imposing

\boxed{T_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}\,.}

(113)

For this relation to hold, we first seed
\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
by choosing

\widetilde{M}^{-1}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\widetilde{M}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}

(114)

which is equivalent to identifying
\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
with the left-invariant Maurer-Cartan form

\widetilde{M}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{M}=\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\widehat{t}_{\alpha}\,.

(115)

Consequentially,
\widetilde{M}
has to be an element of the generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
.

5.2
Fixing
\widetilde{V}

What is left to check is whether the remaining component
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\mu}
of
\widetilde{V}
can be fixed such that (
113
) holds. The central point of this computation is to make sure that the twisted torsion components
T_{\alpha}
are independent of the choice of
\mathcal{E}\in G_{\mathrm{PS}}
. To show that
\mathcal{E}
decouples, we use its property

\langle_{\alpha}|\mathcal{E}=\langle_{\alpha}|\,

(116)

and rewrite (
110
) as

\mathcal{E}^{-1}T_{\hat{A}}\mathcal{E}=\langle_{\hat{A}}|\mathcal{E}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle\overline{\Theta}_{\hat{B}}+\langle_{\hat{A}}|\mathcal{E}\overline{\Theta}_{\hat{B}}\mathbf{Z}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle,

(117)

with

\overline{\Theta}_{\hat{A}}=\mathcal{E}^{-1}\Theta_{\hat{A}}\mathcal{E}

(118)

to fully exploit it. Now, we can simplify the expression of
T_{\alpha}
to

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\alpha}+\mathcal{E}\langle_{\alpha}|\overline{\Theta}_{\beta}\mathbf{Z}|^{\beta}\rangle\mathcal{E}^{-1}

(119)

\displaystyle=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{E}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}|}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}+\langle_{\alpha}|\widehat{t}_{\beta}\mathbf{Z}|^{\beta}\rangle\right)\mathcal{E}^{-1}

(120)

and therefore fix

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}|}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}=-\langle_{\alpha}|\widehat{t}_{\beta}\mathbf{Z}|^{\beta}\rangle\,.

(121)

It is straightforward to evaluate the right-hand side; All one needs is given in section
3
. Similarly for the left-hand side, but this time one also has to take into account the restrictions on
\widetilde{V}
given in (
102
). Without loss of generality, they give rise to

\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}=w_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}K_{\gamma}^{\beta}-\tfrac{1}{2}w_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\widetilde{R}^{\gamma\beta},

(122)

with

\displaystyle w_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\beta}{}^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\nu}{}^{\gamma}\,,\qquad\text{and}

(123)

\displaystyle w_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{[\beta|\nu}\widetilde{V}_{|\gamma]}{}^{\nu}-w_{\alpha[\beta|}{}^{\delta}\widetilde{V}_{\delta\mu}\widetilde{V}_{|\gamma]}{}^{\mu}\,,

(124)

under the assumption that
V_{A}{}^{I}
is constant and therefore does not contribute. For (
121
) to hold, we only need the components to satisfy

\displaystyle w_{[\alpha\beta]}{}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}\qquad\text{and}

(125)

\displaystyle w_{[\alpha\beta\gamma]}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\tfrac{1}{6}f_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,,

(126)

with

f_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\delta}\kappa_{\delta\gamma}\,.

(127)

This tensor is totally antisymmetric because we required (
59
) before. As we have already fixed
\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}
(and with it its inverse
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}
) by (
115
), the first constraint does not provide any new information. Of course it still holds. Hence, the last component of
\widetilde{V}
that has to be determined is
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\mu}
. With out loss of generality, it can be rewritten as

\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\mu}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\alpha\beta}\widetilde{V}^{\beta}{}_{\mu}+\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\nu}\widetilde{B}_{\nu\mu}

(128)

to obtain

w_{[\alpha\beta\gamma]}=\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\beta}{}^{\nu}\widetilde{V}_{\gamma}{}^{\rho}\partial_{[\mu}\widetilde{B}_{\nu\rho]}\,.

(129)

Because the two-form
\widetilde{B}_{\mu\nu}
is not invariant under generalized diffeomorphisms on the mega-space, we instead use the corresponding
H
-flux

\widetilde{H}_{\mu\nu\rho}=3\partial_{[\mu}\widetilde{B}_{\nu\rho]}\,.

(130)

It is constrained by (
126
) to

\widetilde{H}_{\mu\nu\rho}=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\widetilde{V}_{\nu}{}^{\beta}\widetilde{V}_{\rho}{}^{\gamma}

(131)

and leaves us with the final question, if there exists a
B
-field which gives rise to this specific
H
-flux. To answer this question in the affirmative, it is sufficient to check for

4\partial_{[\mu}\widetilde{H}_{\nu\rho\lambda]}=0\,.

(132)

This equation indeed holds, due to the Jacobi identity of the structure constants
f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}
. Finally, we have to fix the constant
V_{A}{}^{I}
. All constant
\mathrm{O}(d,d)
elements are admissible, but a particular simple choice is the identity. An immediate consequence is that

|^{A}\rangle\widetilde{D}_{A}=|^{I}\rangle\partial_{I}\,.

(133)

This completes the first major objective in exploring the twisted Poláček-Siegel construction: We have constructed the generalized frame
\widehat{E}
on the mega-space, such that:

1.

Both
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{V}
are completely fixed by requiring that
\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
arises as the component
T_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
of the twisted generalized torsion.

2.

The inner frame
\mathcal{E}
contains all information about the physical space. It does not depend on the auxiliary coordinates and is an element of
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
.

3.

The twisted generalized torsion
T_{\hat{A}}
does not depend on the auxiliary coordinates. This is the reason why we do not use the mega-space torsion
\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}}
but instead its twisted version
T_{\hat{A}}
which we defined in (
109
).

5.3
Twisted generalized torsion

By design, the twisted generalized torsion satisfies
T_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
. Because it is not a completely free tensor, this choice also affects the other components,
T_{A}
,
T^{\alpha}
and therefore suggests the splitting into an intrinsic and extrinsic part according to

T_{\hat{A}}=\mathring{T}_{\hat{A}}+\check{T}_{\hat{A}}\,.

(134)

As the name implies, the intrinsic part is automatically built into the mega-space frame. It arises when we set the frame
\mathcal{E}
to the identity. Taking into account the equations from the last section, we therefore obtain

\mathring{T}_{\hat{A}}=\mathring{\Theta}_{\hat{A}}+\langle_{\hat{A}}|\mathring{\Theta}_{\beta}\mathbf{Z}|^{\beta}\rangle

(135)

with the only non-vanishing component

\mathring{\Theta}_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}K_{\gamma}^{\beta}-\tfrac{1}{12}f_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\widetilde{R}^{\beta\gamma}\,.

(136)

of
\mathring{\Theta}_{\hat{A}}
. Besides the already known
\mathring{T}_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
, the remaining components
\mathring{T}_{A}
and
\mathring{T}^{\alpha}
fix all contributions to the respective components of
T_{\hat{A}}
for the generators
K^{\alpha}_{\beta}
,
\widetilde{R}^{\alpha}_{B}
and
\widetilde{R}^{\alpha\beta}
. Therefore the remaining, free components have to go to the extrinsic torsion with
\check{T}_{\alpha}=0
to not conflict with
T_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
. We parameterize it in the heterotic basis by

\displaystyle\check{T}_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}+f_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}^{\mathcal{B}}+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\,,\qquad\text{and}

(137)

\displaystyle\check{T}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}+f_{\mathcal{B}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}+\tfrac{1}{2}f^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\,,

where the different
f
-tensors encode torsions and curvatures of the physical space. Therefore, we will streamline the way
\check{T}
is computed in the following. Doing so, we will in particular show later that several of its components can be fixed by choosing
\mathcal{E}
appropriately.

Because all the generators that appear in the extrinsic torsion belong to
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}
, we can isolate them by considering the twisted torsion components
\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{B}}|T_{\hat{A}}|^{\mathds{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
. The same holds for the index
\hat{A}
, because we have already seen that
\check{T}_{\alpha}=0
. Hence, instead of
T_{\hat{A}}
, we rather work with

\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle\left.T_{\hat{B}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}

(138)

in the heterotic basis discussed above. The projection to
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}
on the right-hand side is performed such that

\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|X|_{\mathds{B}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|X|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}|_{\mathds{B}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}

(139)

holds. Practically, this means that we remove all tilded generators and
K_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
except for

\displaystyle\left.K_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}^{\tilde{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}

\displaystyle=R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,

\displaystyle\left.K_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{\tilde{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}R_{\tilde{\alpha}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,

(140)

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{R}^{\tilde{\alpha}}_{B}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}

\displaystyle=R^{\tilde{\alpha}}_{B}\,,

and

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{R}^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}

\displaystyle=R^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}\,.

(141)

The resulting tensor, called the restricted twisted torsion, is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}
. Again, it decomposes into an intrinsic and extrinsic part according to

\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}=\mathring{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathds{A}}+\check{T}_{\mathds{A}}\,.

(142)

Remarkably, the form of the extrinsic part (
137
) is preserved. The advantage of this form is that it can be easier computed than
T_{\hat{A}}
because only the generators of
G_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}
are involved. Hence, similar to the restriction in (
138
), we furthermore define
X|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}
to replace the generators

\displaystyle\left.K_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}^{\tilde{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,

\displaystyle\left.K_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\widetilde{R}_{\tilde{\alpha}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\,,

\displaystyle\left.K_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{\tilde{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}R_{\tilde{\alpha}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,

(143)

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{R}^{\tilde{\alpha}}_{B}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=R^{\tilde{\alpha}}_{B}\,,

and

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{R}^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=R^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}

(144)

while keeping all the others.

To get
\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}
starting from the original expression in (
110
), we first need some auxiliary quantities. Most important is the torsion introduced by
\widetilde{V}
which is

T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\hat{A}}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle\widetilde{V}_{\hat{A}}|}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}

(145)

with the components

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}K_{\gamma}^{\beta}-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\widetilde{R}^{\beta\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}\,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\alpha}\widetilde{R}^{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{A}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(146)

In particular, we are interested in the restricted components

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-f_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}K_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\widetilde{R}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}-f_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,

(147)

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\tilde{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-f_{\tilde{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}K_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\widetilde{R}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}-f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}\,\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\widetilde{R}^{\beta\gamma}-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\widetilde{R}^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}R^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}\,\tilde{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}f^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R_{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\,.

In the same vein, we define

\uptau_{\alpha}=\widehat{t}_{\alpha}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}

(148)

with the only non-vanishing component

\uptau_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}=t_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}+f_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}{}^{\tilde{\beta}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\uptau_{\tilde{\alpha}}=t_{\tilde{\alpha}}-f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}R^{\tilde{\beta}}{}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\,.

(149)

After these preparations, we eventually obtain

\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathds{A}}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\mathcal{E}|^{\beta}\rangle\uptau_{\beta}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\uptau_{\beta}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{E}|^{\beta}\rangle\,.

(150)

Several new quantities are appearing here; We explain them going from the left to the right: First, we have the generalized fluxes obtained from the twisted generalized Lie derivative

\mathcal{L}_{\scalebox{0.75}{\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}}U|}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U|\partial V\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U|\mathcal{Z}|\partial U\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U|^{\hat{A}}\rangle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\hat{A}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

(151)

with the heterotic
\mathbf{Z}
-operator ,

\mathcal{Z}=-\mathcal{K}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\odot\mathcal{K}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}+2\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\odot\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}+2\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}_{\mathcal{A}}\odot\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}^{\mathcal{A}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\odot\mathcal{R}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\,,

(152)

given by term on the first line of (
98
). With it, we compute the generalized fluxes

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathds{A}}=\mathcal{L}_{\scalebox{0.75}{\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}}_{\mathds{A}}|\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{-1}\,.

(153)

Note that
\mathcal{Z}
arises from
\mathbf{Z}
because all generators of
\mathfrak{p}
are removed through the projection
|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}
.

The last, in (
151
) describes a generalized torsion. To make contact with the heterotic string, this torsion has to have a particular form, namely

\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\hat{A}}|\mathcal{E}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\hat{B}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\hat{A}}|\mathcal{E}|_{\tilde{\beta}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\tfrac{1}{2}f^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}\tilde{\delta}}R_{\tilde{\gamma}\tilde{\delta}}\,.

(154)

This only happens, if we impose the restriction

f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}=0\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{\tilde{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}=0\,,

(155)

Rendering the structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}=G_{\mathrm{H}}\times G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}
into a direct product of the heterotic gauge group
G_{\mathrm{H}}
, generated by
t_{\tilde{\alpha}}
, and the residual structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}
whose Lie algebra is spanned by
t_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}
. In this case,
\widetilde{V}
’s torsion components in (
147
) simplify to

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-f_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}K_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\,,

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}\,\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\widetilde{R}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\,,

(156)

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}}_{\tilde{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad

\displaystyle T^{\widetilde{V}\,\tilde{\alpha}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}f^{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R_{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\,.

All components on the second line give rise to (
154
). Combined with the second term of
\mathcal{Z}
in (
152
), it forms the generalized Lie derivative of heterotic double field theory with gauge group
G_{\mathrm{H}}
. The terms on the first line of (
156
) are of the same form as one gets for the duality group O(
d
,
d
) in the standard Poláček-Siegel construction. In fact one can reuse all the expressions computed there after a slight adjustment of the indices. Another way to obtain this result is to remember that because of the product form of
G_{\mathrm{S}}
, one can do the reduction from the mega-space to the physical space in two steps: First we eliminate the auxiliary coordinates of the heterotic gauge group
G_{\mathrm{H}}
with the Poláček-Siegel construction for a non-degenerate
\kappa_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}
presented here. The resulting heterotic frame is further reduced by the standard Poláček-Siegel construction which indices
\alpha
substituted by
\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}
and
A
by
\mathcal{A}
.

We see that the mega-space frame
\mathcal{E}
contributes in two different ways to the restricted twisted torsion in (
150
): All terms coming from the generalized Lie derivative, except for the torsion, contain one derivative. Whereas the frame enters through the last two terms algebraically. The form of the differential part is very similar to the O(
d
,
d
) case. Additionally, the algebraic part gives an interesting glimpse at how to fix additional components of the mega-space frame which go beyond the frame on the physical space. This is not of immediate concern when discussing generalized dualities because they use the extended space as starting point. However, one might also want to turn the argument around and try to reconstruct the mega-space for general backgrounds. In this case, one needs to impose constraints on the restrict generalized torsion in such a way that they ideally only leave the physical frame
\mathcal{E}
as a free parameters.

Assuming the factorization of the generalized structure group implied by (
155
), we should think more about how the generators
\widehat{t}_{\alpha}
should be chosen. The simplest possibly is to just set

\uptau_{\tilde{\alpha}}=0

(157)

which in combination with (
155
) implies

\widehat{t}_{\tilde{\alpha}}=K_{\tilde{\alpha}}+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}=f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\,,

(158)

and is therefore compatible with (
57
). This can be easily seen by remembering that the generators
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\beta\gamma}
introduced in (
60
) generate O(
p
,
q
). Therefore, the heterotic gauge group
G_{\mathrm{H}}
is embedded into this group by the structure constants
f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\tilde{\gamma}}
. In this case, the components of the reduced, twisted torsion are

\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}\mathds{B}\mathds{C}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{B}}|\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}|_{\mathds{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathds{A}\mathds{B}\mathds{C}}+3\mathcal{E}_{[\mathds{A}|}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\delta}}(\uptau_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\delta}})_{|\mathds{B}\mathds{C}]},

(159)

with
\mathcal{F}_{\mathds{A}\mathds{B}\mathds{C}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{B}}|\mathcal{F}_{\mathds{A}}|_{\mathds{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathds{A}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{E}|^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
, and
(\tau_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}})_{\mathds{B}\mathds{C}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{B}}|\tau_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}|_{\mathds{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
. Remarkably, (
159
) has the same form as it would have in the Poláček-Siegel-construction for O(
d
,
d
). It would be desirable to keep this from after transitioning to more complicated realizations for
\uptau_{\tilde{\alpha}}
. This possible by shifting the additional term in (
159
) into the heterotic generalized Lie derivative on the physical space. However, it is usually preferable to work with untwisted generalized Lie derivatives because they describe the standard symmetries of the physical fields. Hence, one aims to shift the twist from the Lie derivative to the frame. We will show how this works for the example in section
7
.

Fixing at least parts of components
\mathcal{E}_{\mathds{A}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}
systematically requires more information about the structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}
. However, one important lesson which can be already learned here is that the inner frame
\mathcal{E}
should be split according to

\boxed{G_{\mathrm{PS}}\ni\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{A}E\qquad\text{with}\qquad E\in\mathrm{O}(d+p,d+q)}

(160)

into two parts. The first part
\mathcal{A}
, is valued in the coset
G_{\mathrm{PS}}/\mathrm{O}(d+p,d+q)
and should be constraint through torsion constraints such that ideally only the physical frame
E
– which is generated by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}
– survives as it contains all the physical degrees of freedom. This works because by construction
E|^{\alpha}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=|^{\alpha}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
holds and we therefore are left with

\mathcal{E}_{\mathds{A}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\mathcal{E}|^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\mathcal{A}|^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathds{A}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\,.

(161)

Hence, it is possible to replace
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{A}
in (
159
). We will introduce an explicit parameterization of
\mathcal{A}
later and then compute the relevant components of the restricted twisted torsion.

5.4
Dilatonic torsion

Last but not least, we have to lift the dilaton to the mega-space. It is captured by the dilatonic torsion

|\widehat{T}\rangle=2\widehat{E}|\partial\hat{\Phi}\rangle-\partial_{\hat{I}}\widehat{E}|^{\hat{I}}\rangle\,,

(162)

which corresponds to the one-index generalized flux of double field theory’s flux formulation
[
19
]
. Like before, we twist it to remove all dependence on auxiliary coordinates and obtain

|T\rangle=\widetilde{M}^{-1}|\widehat{T}\rangle=2\mathcal{E}|\widetilde{D}\hat{\Phi}\rangle-\widetilde{M}^{-1}\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}(\widetilde{M}\mathcal{E}\widetilde{V})\widetilde{V}^{-1}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle,

(163)

with the derivative

|\widetilde{D}\hat{\Phi}\rangle=\widetilde{D}_{A}\hat{\Phi}|^{A}\rangle+\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\hat{\Phi}|^{\alpha}\rangle

(164)

of the generalized dilaton
\hat{\Phi}
. Although Poláček and Siegel did not discuss the dilaton in
[
3
]
, we follow the same idea
[
4
,
1
]
and define the Poláček-Siegel form of the mega-space dilaton

\hat{\Phi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{m}+\log\det(\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu})\right)+\Phi

(165)

in terms of the generalized dilaton
\Phi
on the physical space. The first term on the right hand side only depends on the auxiliary coordinates. Its
\widetilde{m}
will be chosen to fix

\boxed{\langle_{\alpha}|T\rangle=-\langle_{\alpha}|\widehat{t}_{\beta}|^{\beta}\rangle=f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}\,,}

(166)

the dilatonic version of (
113
). It will source the intrinsic dilatonic torsion in analogy to the discussion of the last subsection. Let us see how this works out by first computing

\langle_{\alpha}|T\rangle=2\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\hat{\Phi}-f_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\beta}-\langle_{\alpha}|\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle\,.

(167)

To evaluate the last term on the right-hand side, we remember (
122
) and find

\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{-1}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle=w_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\beta}|^{\alpha}\rangle\,,

(168)

which eventually gives rise to

\langle_{\alpha}|T\rangle=2\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\hat{\Phi}-f_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\beta}-w_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\beta}=2\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\hat{\Phi}-w_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}\,,

(169)

where (
125
) was used in the last step. For the mega-space dilaton in (
165
), the right-hand side becomes

\langle_{\alpha}|T\rangle=\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\widetilde{m}=f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}\,,

(170)

due to

\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\log\det(\widetilde{V}_{\beta}{}^{\nu})=\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\beta}{}^{\nu}\widetilde{V}_{\nu}{}^{\beta}=w_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}

(171)

and
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\Phi=0
. As result, we obtain the differential equation

\mathrm{d}\widetilde{m}=\widetilde{V}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\,.

(172)

It can be integrated at least locally (and this is all we need) due to the Jacobi identity for the structure constants
f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}
. With this result, we are able to bring (
163
) it into its final form,

|T\rangle=F_{\hat{A}}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle-\widehat{t}_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}|^{\alpha}\rangle\,,

(173)

which further takes into account the parameterization of
\mathcal{E}
in (
160
) and makes use of the one-index generalized flux

F_{\hat{A}}=2\mathcal{E}_{\hat{A}}{}^{I}\partial_{I}\Phi-\partial_{I}\mathcal{E}_{\hat{A}}{}^{I}

(174)

on the physical space.

Already without going to the heterotic basis, (
173
) has the same form as (
150
) from the previous section. The first term on its right-hand side vanishes if we take
\mathcal{E}
to be constant, for example as the identity. Hence, it only contributes to the extrinsic dilatonic torsion. As before, we obtain the physical content of the dilatonic torsion by projecting it to
\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|
, giving rise to

\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|T\rangle=F_{\mathds{A}}-\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\uptau_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}|^{\alpha}\rangle\,,

(175)

with the one-index generalized flux

F_{\mathds{A}}=2\mathcal{E}_{\mathds{A}}{}^{I}\partial_{I}\Phi-\partial_{I}\mathcal{E}_{\mathds{A}}{}^{I}

(176)

on the mega-space. Assuming moreover that the generalized structure group decomposes into the direct product
G_{\mathrm{S}}=G_{\mathrm{H}}\times G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}
and (
157
) holds, (
175
) simplifies further to

\mathcal{T}_{\mathds{A}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|T\rangle=F_{\mathds{A}}-\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\uptau_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\mathcal{E}|^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\,.

(177)

5.5
Generalized structure group transformations

A key feature of the Poláček-Siegel construction is that the twisted torsion we discussed above provides various covariant tensors under the action of
G_{\mathrm{S}}
and generalized diffeomorphisms on the physical space. These tensors will become crucial in constructing an invariant action in section
6
. In this section, we discuss the corresponding transformations.

More specifically, we are interested in generalized diffeomorphisms

\delta_{\xi}\widehat{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle\widehat{\xi}|}\widehat{E}

(178)

on the mega-space that preserve the form of
\widehat{E}
introduced in (
99
). Therefore,
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{V}
should not be affected by them. To see which generalized diffeomorphisms are admissible, a convenient quantity to analyze is the twisted shift of the frame

\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\widetilde{M}^{-1}(\delta_{\xi}\widehat{E})\widetilde{V}^{-1}E^{-1}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{A}+\delta_{\xi}EE^{-1}

(179)

because it only contains the dynamic parts on the right-hand side. Using the definition of the generalized Lie derivative (
78
) together with (
178
), one finds that it is given by the expression

\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=\xi^{\hat{A}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Theta_{\hat{A}}\mathcal{A}+\langle\xi|\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}(E\widetilde{V})(E\widetilde{V})^{-1}\mathbf{Z}|^{\hat{A}}\rangle+\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\xi^{\hat{B}}\langle_{\hat{B}}|\mathbf{Z}E|^{\hat{A}}\rangle,

(180)

with

\langle\xi|=\xi^{\alpha}\langle_{\alpha}|+\xi^{A}\langle_{A}|+\xi_{\alpha}\langle^{\alpha}|\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\widehat{\xi}|=\langle\xi|E\widetilde{V}\,.

(181)

This equation can be simplified to

\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle\widehat{\xi}|}(E\widetilde{V})(E\widetilde{V})^{-1}+\xi^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\widehat{t}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}+\xi^{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}D_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}+\widetilde{D}_{\hat{A}}\xi^{\hat{B}}\langle_{\hat{B}}|\mathbf{Z}E|^{\hat{A}}\rangle\,.

(182)

Note that we have introduced the flat derivative on the physical space,

D_{\mathcal{A}}=E_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{I}\partial_{I}\qquad\text{with}\qquad E_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{I}=\langle_{\mathcal{A}}|E|^{I}\rangle\,

(183)

in this equation. By definition,
\delta\mathbb{E}
can only contain generators of
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
. This is already true for the two terms in the middle of (
182
), while the first and the last term require additional care. To eliminate any contributions from
K_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
\widetilde{R}^{\alpha}_{A}
and
\widetilde{R}^{\alpha\beta}
introduced by them, we require

\xi_{\alpha}=\kappa_{\alpha\beta}\xi^{\beta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\xi^{\hat{B}}=0\,,

(184)

resulting in

\langle\xi|=\xi^{\alpha}\left(\langle_{\alpha}|+\kappa_{\alpha\beta}\langle^{\beta}|\right)+\xi^{A}\langle_{A}|

(185)

or written in the heterotic basis

\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}\xi|=\xi^{\mathcal{A}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|+\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}|\,.

(186)

We can now further simplify the right-hand side of (
182
) to get

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=

\displaystyle\left(2D_{[\mathcal{B}}\xi_{\mathcal{A}]}+\xi^{\mathcal{C}}F_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\right)\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}+\xi^{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}D_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}

(187)

\displaystyle-D_{\mathcal{A}}\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}^{\mathcal{A}}-\xi^{\alpha}\left(K_{\alpha}+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\right)+\xi^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\widehat{t}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,,

with the generalized fluxes from (
11
). To further split the gauge indices in the last two terms, we assume a product generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}=G_{\mathrm{H}}\times G_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}
and (
158
). Taking the latter into account, we see that
\mathcal{A}
commutes with
\widehat{t}_{\tilde{\alpha}}
and therefore

\xi^{\tilde{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\widehat{t}_{\tilde{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}-\xi^{\tilde{\alpha}}\left(K_{\tilde{\alpha}}+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\right)=\xi^{\tilde{\alpha}}\widehat{t}_{\tilde{\alpha}}-\xi^{\tilde{\alpha}}\left(K_{\tilde{\alpha}}+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}R^{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}\right)=0

(188)

vanishes. Hence we eventually find

\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=\left(2D_{[\mathcal{B}}\xi_{\mathcal{A}]}+\xi^{\mathcal{C}}F_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\right)\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}+\xi^{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}D_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}-D_{\mathcal{A}}\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}R_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}^{\mathcal{A}}-\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}K_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}+\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}\,.

(189)

The first term of this transformation captures undeformed generalized diffeomorphisms on the physical space. To see why, note that

\langle_{\mathcal{A}}|\delta_{\xi}EE^{-1}|_{\mathcal{B}}\rangle=\delta_{\xi}E_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\mathcal{I}}E_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{B}}=\mathbb{L}_{\xi}E_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\mathcal{I}}E_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{I}}=2D_{[\mathcal{B}}\xi_{\mathcal{A}]}+3\xi^{\mathcal{C}}F_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}

(190)

holds if we only focus on the contribution from
\xi^{\mathcal{A}}
while setting
\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}=0
. Thus, the physical frame
E_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\mathcal{I}}
transforms exactly as it should in heterotic double field theory with the gauge group
G_{\mathrm{H}}
specified by the structure constants
f_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}{}^{\tilde{\gamma}}
. Moreover, the second term of (
189
) tells us that all the other fields, no matter how they particularly enter into
\mathcal{A}
, have to transform as (generalized) scalars. This makes perfect sense because neither of them has any curved indices. We conclude that the action of generalized diffeomorphisms is generated by the component
\xi^{\mathcal{A}}
. It is not modified and therefore does not require further attention. The situation is more involved for structure group transformations, generated by
\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}}
. To see their action in more detail, one can choose an explicit parameterization for
\mathcal{A}
as we will do in the next section.

The same logic, we have to follow for the generalized dilaton on the mega-space. In analogy with (
178
), we have now

\delta_{\xi}\hat{\Phi}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\langle\widehat{\xi}|}\hat{\Phi}=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}+\xi^{I}\partial_{I}\Phi-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{I}\xi^{I}\,.

(191)

Remarkably, the first two terms cancel for the generalized dilaton we have chosen. This can be seen from

\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}=\xi^{\alpha}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}\hat{\Phi}-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{V}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}=\xi^{\alpha}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}+\tfrac{1}{2}w_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\beta}-\tfrac{1}{2}w_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\beta}\right)=0\,.

(192)

Consequentially,
\Phi
indeed transforms as

\delta_{\xi}\Phi=\xi^{I}\partial_{I}\Phi-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{I}\xi^{I}\,,

(193)

the standard transformation of the generalized dilaton on the physical space.

There are two other fundamental objects for which we would like to derive the transformation, the twisted torsion
T_{\mathcal{A}}
and its dilatonic counterpart
|T\rangle
. Let us begin with the former whose transformation is given by

\delta_{\xi}T_{\hat{A}}=\xi^{B}D_{B}T_{\hat{A}}+\xi^{\gamma}\langle_{\hat{A}}|\widehat{t}_{\gamma}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle T_{\hat{B}}+\xi^{\gamma}[\widehat{t}_{\gamma},T_{\hat{A}}]\,.

(194)

It is not surprising that the first term again describes generalized diffeomorphisms for a scalar on the physical space. Much more interesting are the second and third terms. Hence, we restrict the discussion to
\xi^{\mathcal{A}}=0
and deal with

\delta_{\xi}T_{\hat{A}}=\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\langle_{\hat{A}}|\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle T_{\hat{B}}+\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}[\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}},T_{\hat{A}}]\qquad\text{for}\qquad\xi^{\mathcal{A}}=0\,.

(195)

As we have seen in section
5.3
,
T_{\hat{A}}
splits into two contributions, the intrinsic and the extrinsic torsion. Under the transformation (
195
) the latter, which contains all information about the physical space, transforms into itself. Saying that if we start from a torsion contribution in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}
, it will stay in this subspace after the transformation. For the intrinsic torsion, the situation is more subtle because it might bleed into
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}
and therefore mix with the extrinsic torsion. Such a mixing results in a shift symmetry which usually is not desirable as it competes with torsion constraints. To avoid it, one imposes

\delta_{\xi}\mathring{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathds{A}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}=\xi^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\left(\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}|^{\mathcal{B}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\widehat{t}_{\mathcal{B}}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}|^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\beta}}+[\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}},\widehat{t}_{\mathds{A}}]\right)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}=0\,.

(196)

Here, we have introduced the new generators
\widehat{t}_{\hat{A}}
by

\widehat{t}_{\mathds{A}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathds{A}}|^{\hat{B}}\rangle\mathring{T}_{\hat{B}}

(197)

in analogy with
\widehat{t}_{\alpha}=\mathring{T}_{\alpha}
. This constraint is obviously solved by

[\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}},\widehat{t}_{\mathds{B}}]|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}=-(t_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}})_{\mathds{B}}{}^{\mathcal{C}}\widehat{t}_{\mathcal{C}}-(t_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}})_{\mathds{B}}{}^{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}\widehat{t}_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\gamma}}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}}\,.

(198)

For the dilatonic torsion, the situation is similar. The mega-space transformation takes the simple form

\delta_{\xi}|T\rangle=\xi^{A}D_{A}|T\rangle+\xi^{\alpha}\widehat{t}_{\alpha}|T\rangle\,.

(199)

6
Invariant action and parameterization of
\mathcal{A}

After understanding how the symmetries of the physical theory arise from the mega-space, we proceed with the construction of an invariant action. Ideally it should be formulated on the mega-space because all symmetries are manifest there. In principle, any combination of invariant terms are allowed. Even when we restrict the discussion to expressions with only two derivatives, there are still various possibilities. To construct a unique two derivative action we should remember that we have introduced auxiliary fields contained in
\mathcal{A}
which in principle should not appear as physical degrees of freedom. The mega-space action should be constructed such that they either do not appear at all or can be integrated out. In particular, this implies that their equations of motion should be algebraic.

To this end, consider the action

\displaystyle S=\int\mathrm{d}^{d+n}x\,e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\Bigl(

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}\hat{D}_{\hat{A}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{B}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{B}}-\tfrac{1}{4}\mathcal{H}^{\hat{A}\hat{D}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}\hat{B}\hat{C}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{D}}{}^{\hat{B}\hat{C}}

(200)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{12}\mathcal{H}^{\hat{A}\hat{B}}\mathcal{H}^{\hat{C}\hat{D}}\mathcal{H}^{\hat{E}\hat{F}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{A}\hat{C}\hat{E}}\widehat{T}_{\hat{B}\hat{D}\hat{F}}\Bigr)\,.

From the cigar to the trumpet

To illustrate the construction we describe in this paper, let us consider an example based on the coset mega-space SU(2)
\times
SU(2)/SU(2)
diag
. Despite its minimalistic character, this setup is sufficient to demonstrate most of what was discussed in the previous sections. Moreover, it is a role model on how to obtain a large class of new generalized dualities for heterotic/type I strings. As it is common in this context, we start with fixing the algebra and from there construct the physical fields.

Generators of each factor in SU(2)
\times
SU(2) are denoted by
t_{\underline{a}}
and
t_{\overline{a}}
respectively. They are governed by the commutation relations

(209)

where
\epsilon
is the totally antisymmetric tensor with
\epsilon_{12}{}^{3}=1
. Moreover, we have the invariant pairing

\langle\!\langle t_{\underline{a}},t_{\underline{b}}\rangle\!\rangle=\delta_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\,,\qquad\langle\!\langle t_{\overline{a}},t_{\overline{b}}\rangle\!\rangle=-\delta_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\!\langle t_{\underline{a}},t_{\overline{b}}\rangle\!\rangle=0\,.

(210)

As the generalized structure group, we choose a U(1) generated by

t_{\tilde{1}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(t_{\underline{1}}+\lambda t_{\overline{1}}\right)

(211)

which gives rise to

\langle\!\langle t_{\tilde{1}},t_{\tilde{1}}\rangle\!\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\lambda^{2})=-\kappa_{\tilde{1}\tilde{1}}\,.

(212)

\lambda
has been introduced to be able to tune the
\kappa_{\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}}
. There are two special points, namely
\lambda=\pm 1
, where the invariant metric vanishes and one recovers the original Poláček-Siegel construction. Furthermore, we choose

t^{\tilde{1}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1\pm\lambda}\left(t^{\underline{1}}\mp t^{\overline{1}}\right)

(213)

to guarantee the additional pairings

\langle\!\langle t^{\tilde{1}},t_{\tilde{1}}\rangle\!\rangle=1\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\!\langle t^{\tilde{1}},t^{\tilde{1}}\rangle\!\rangle=0\,.

(214)

Note that according to the sign choice in (
213
) corresponds
\lambda=\pm 1
are excluded. By treating these two cases separately the parameter

covers the full real line. Finally, we fix

t_{a}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(t_{\underline{a}}-t_{\overline{a}}\right)\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad t^{a}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(t_{\underline{a}}+t_{\overline{a}}\right)

(215)

to have a full set of generators and with them all algebraic information fixed.

On the mega-space, we now construct a SU(2)
\times

8
Conclusions

Starting point for this article has been the question if it is possible to drop the isotropy of the generalized structure group for the Poláček-Siegel construction. Considering the relation between the latter and symplectic reductions of Courant algebroids, it was expected that the construction should work even without this constraint and then should relate to heterotic/type I strings whose gauge structure is governed by transitive Courant algebroids. This expectation has been confirmed and we furthermore witnessed that a versatile structure emerges from an at the first glance quite innocent extension.

An important application of the presented construction is to obtain backgrounds which solve the field equations of the respective leading-order, low-energy effective actions as illustrated by the example in section
7
. Because their underlying algebraic structure is manifest, the analysis of generalized dualities and consistent truncations for heterotic/type I strings, whose exploration only begun recently
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
, is simplified considerably. A distinguished subclass in the relevant family of generalized homogenous spaces leads to integrable
\sigma
-models. Again, they have been mostly studied for bosonic strings and type II superstrings while only few results have been reported in the heterotic sector. A major advantage in our approach is that their mega-space is always a generalized group manifold (generalized parallelizable space) for the duality group O(
D
,
D
). These space have been extensively studied over the last years and we understand their structure very well. Going from O(
d
,
d
) to O(
d
,
d+q
) with gauge group
G_{\mathrm{H}}
comes with challenges like the requirement of section condition violating twists that have to be introduced by hand. From the new perspective we take here, all the additional ingredients arise automatically from the reduction of the mega-space.

A second driving force behind the presented results is the construction of higher-derivative corrections in generalized geometry. At the moment, the most sophisticated technique to do so is called the generalized Bergshoeff-de Roo (gBdR) identification
[
25
,
26
]
. It builds on the original idea of Bergshoeff and de Roo
[
27
]
to extend the gauge group of the heterotic string by the Lorentz group and then identify the additional connection components with the spin connection. Thereby parts of the kinetic term of the gauge field generate a Riemann square term. Remarkably, the gBdR identification generates not only the leading (4-derivative) corrections but an infinite tower of higher derivatives. Unfortunately at the moment, these still lack the well established geometrical interpretation of the leading order, ruling out any application which relies on them – like generalized duality or consistent truncations. Moreover, it makes it very hard to understand the underlying reasons for recent no-go theorems suggesting that
\xi(3)
-corrections cannot be captured in generalized geometry
[
28
]
. The heterotic version of the Poláček-Siegel construction presented here, is the key to gaining a better understanding of the mathematical structures that underlie the gBdR identification
[
29
]
. From a more geometrical perspective in terms of torsion constraints and partial gauge fixing, both are concepts which already appear in general relativity and gauge theories.
